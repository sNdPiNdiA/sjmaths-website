23. Ogives and Histograms Previous Year Questions

Core Formula: Plot Ogive at upper limits, Mode from intersecting diagonals of tallest histogram bar

CISCE Examiner Observation & Common Deduction Pitfall

Do not join ogive points with a ruler. Ogive must be a smooth freehand S-curve. Also state scale explicitly (e.g. 2 cm = 10 units).

ICSE 2026 Board Q5(i) Histogram & Mode Graphical
4 Marks
The histogram drawn on the graph represents the number of students of different heights. (a) State the modal class. (b) Find the mode of the distribution graphically from the histogram.
Histogram with intersecting modal diagonals determining Mode = 137 cm
View Verified Solution & Marking
Part (a) Modal Class The bar with the highest frequency (f = 14) is for height 130–140 cm.
Hence, Modal Class = 130–140 cm. [1 Mark]

Part (b) Mode from Graph Join the top-left vertex of the modal bar to the top-left vertex of the right adjacent bar, and top-right of modal to top-right of left adjacent bar.
From their point of intersection, draw a vertical line down to the x-axis.
The reading on the x-axis gives Mode = 137 cm (± 0.5 cm). [3 Marks]
ICSE 2026 Board Q7(ii) Ogive Graph Paper
6 Marks
Using a graph paper, draw an ogive for the following distribution showing records of weight of 100 students (Scale: 2 cm = 5 kg on x-axis, 2 cm = 10 students on y-axis):
Weight: 35–40 (f=4), 40–45 (f=6), 45–50 (f=10), 50–55 (f=24), 55–60 (f=26), 60–65 (f=17), 65–70 (f=8), 70–75 (f=5).
Use your ogive to estimate the:
(a) median weight of the students.
(b) percentage of students whose weight is 60 kg or more.
Ogive (Cumulative Frequency) curve with Median ≈ 56.5 kg and ≥60 kg estimate = 30%
View Verified Solution & Marking
Cumulative Frequencies
(40, 4), (45, 10), (50, 20), (55, 44), (60, 70), (65, 87), (70, 95), (75, 100). [1.5 Marks]
Ogive Plot Plotted points joined smoothly with a free-hand S-curve starting from (35, 0). [1.5 Marks]

Part (a) Median Total N = 100. Median position = N/2 = 50th student.
From y = 50, project horizontally to the curve and down to the x-axis.
Median Weight ≈ 56.5 kg (± 0.5 kg). [1.5 Marks]

Part (b) ≥ 60 kg From x = 60 kg, draw vertical line to meet curve at y = 70.
Number of students weighing 60 kg or more = 100 − 70 = 30 students.
Percentage = (30 / 100) × 100 = 30%. [1.5 Marks]

ICSE 2025 Board Q10(iii) Ogive Salary Curve Interpretation
4 Marks
Using the salary ogive (points plotted at 2k, 4k, 6k, 8k, 10k, 12k with cf 10, 20, 35, 55, 70, 75 up to N=80), find:
(a) total number of workers.
(b) median class.
(c) lower-quartile class.
(d) workers earning ≥ ₹6,000 but < ₹10,000.
View Official Marking Scheme Solution
(a) Total number of workers = 80 workers. [1 Mark]
(b) Median = N/2 = 40th worker ⇒ lies in ₹6,000 – ₹8,000 class. [1 Mark]
(c) Lower quartile Q1 = N/4 = 20th worker ⇒ ₹2,000 – ₹4,000 class. [1 Mark]
(d) Number of workers = cf(10,000) − cf(6,000) = 70 − 35 = 35 workers. [1 Mark]

ICSE 2023 Board Q6(i) Histogram & Graphical Mode Determination
3 Marks
The following distribution gives the daily wages of 60 workers of a factory:
Daily income in ₹ 200–300300–400400–500500–600600–700700–800
Number of workers ($f$) 6101416104
Use graph paper to answer this question. Take 2 cm = ₹100 along one axis and 2 cm = 2 workers along the other axis. Draw a histogram and hence find the mode of the given distribution.
Figure: Histogram with modal class 500–600 and intersecting diagonals giving Mode = ₹525
View Verified Solution & Marking
Step 1 Highest frequency bar corresponds to class 500–600 (frequency 16).
Draw diagonals connecting upper corners of the modal bar to the adjacent bars.1.5 Marks

Step 2 Drop perpendicular from intersection point to horizontal axis.
Reading from the graph: $\mathbf{\text{Mode} = ₹525}$ (acceptable range ₹520 to ₹530).
Formula Check: $\text{Mode} = L + \frac{f_1 - f_0}{2f_1 - f_0 - f_2} \times h = 500 + \frac{16 - 14}{32 - 14 - 10} \times 100 = 500 + \frac{2}{8} \times 100 = 500 + 25 = ₹525$.1.5 Marks
ICSE 2023 Board Q10(ii) Ogive Construction & Estimation (Median, Upper Quartile)
6 Marks
Use graph paper to answer this question.
During a medical checkup of 60 students in a school, weights were recorded as follows:
Weight (in kg) 28–3030–3232–3434–3636–3838–4040–4242–44
Number of Students 24101315952
Taking 2 cm = 2 kg along one axis and 2 cm = 10 students along the other axis, draw an ogive. Use your graph to find the:
  1. median
  2. upper quartile
  3. number of students whose weight is above 37 kg.
Figure: Cumulative frequency ogive showing Median reading at $N/2=30$ and $Q_3$ at $3N/4=45$
View Verified Solution & Marking
Cumulative Frequency Table
Points to plot (Upper Boundary, $cf$): $(28, 0), (30, 2), (32, 6), (34, 16), (36, 29), (38, 44), (40, 53), (42, 58), (44, 60)$.
Total number of students $N = 60$.2 Marks

(a) Median $\frac{N}{2} = \frac{60}{2} = 30^\text{th}$ student.
Locate $cf = 30$ on vertical axis, draw horizontal line to curve and drop perpendicular to weight axis.
Median $\approx 36.1\text{ kg}$ (acceptable range $36.0\text{ to } 36.3\text{ kg}$).1.5 Marks

(b) Upper Quartile $Q_3$ $\frac{3N}{4} = \frac{3 \times 60}{4} = 45^\text{th}$ student.
From $cf = 45$, drop perpendicular from curve to weight axis.
Upper Quartile $Q_3 \approx 38.2\text{ kg}$ (acceptable range $38.0\text{ to } 38.4\text{ kg}$).1.5 Marks

(c) Students Weighing Above 37 kg
From weight $= 37\text{ kg}$ on horizontal axis, draw vertical line to ogive curve.
Corresponding cumulative frequency reading $\approx 36.5$ (approx 36 or 37 students weigh $\le 37\text{ kg}$).
Number of students weighing above $37\text{ kg} = 60 - 36.5 \approx \mathbf{23\text{ to } 24\text{ students}}$.1 Mark
