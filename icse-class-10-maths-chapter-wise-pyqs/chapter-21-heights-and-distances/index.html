21. Heights and Distances Previous Year Questions

Core Formula: tanθ = Opposite / Adjacent, Exact table values or natural tables

CISCE Examiner Observation & Common Deduction Pitfall

Do not approximate √3 as 1.73 if the question explicitly gives √3 = 1.732. Use the given value.

ICSE 2026 Board Q1 Word Problem with Flagpole
5 Marks
A flagpole is erected at the top of a building. The angle of elevation of the top and foot of the flagpole from a point 100 m away, on the same level as that of the foot of the building, are 33° and 31° respectively. Find the height of the flagpole. Give your answer correct to the nearest metre. (Use Mathematical Tables: tan 31° = 0.6009, tan 33° = 0.6494)
View Verified Solution & Marking
Vertical flagpole CD on building BC with elevation angles 31° and 33° from 100 m away
Geometry Let building height BC = h, flagpole CD = x, ground distance AB = 100 m.
In right-angled ΔABC: tan 31° = BC / AB = h / 100
⇒ h = 100 × tan 31° = 100 × 0.6009 = 60.09 m. [2 Marks]

Total Height In right-angled ΔABD: tan 33° = BD / AB = (h + x) / 100
⇒ h + x = 100 × tan 33° = 100 × 0.6494 = 64.94 m. [2 Marks]

Height of Flagpole x = (h + x) − h = 64.94 − 60.09 = 4.85 m.
Rounding to the nearest metre: x = 5 metres. [1 Mark]
ICSE 2025 Board Q2 Heights & Distances
5 Marks
AB is vertical tower 100 m away from foot of 30-storied building CD. Angles of depression from C and E (midpoint of CD) are 35° and 14° respectively. Find height of: (a) tower AB, (b) building CD.
View Verified Solution & Marking
In ΔAPC: CP = 100 tan 35° = 100(0.7002) = 70.02 m. [1.5 Marks]
In ΔAPE: EP = 100 tan 14° = 100(0.2493) = 24.93 m. [1.5 Marks]
CE = CP − EP = 70.02 − 24.93 = 45.09 m.
Since E is midpoint of CD: Height of building CD = 2 × 45.09 = 90.18 m. [1 Mark]
Tower AB = PD = ED − EP = 45.09 − 24.93 = 20.16 m. [1 Mark]
ICSE 2024 Board Q3 Trigonometry • Heights and Distances
5 Marks
The angle of elevation of the top of a 100 m high tree from two points A and B on opposite sides of the tree are 52° and 45° respectively. Find the distance AB, to the nearest metre.
View Verified Solution & Marking
In right ΔCDA: tan 52° = 100 / AD ⇒ AD = 100 / 1.2799 ≈ 78.13 m. [2 Marks]
In right ΔCDB: tan 45° = 100 / BD ⇒ BD = 100 m. [1.5 Marks]
AB = AD + BD = 78.13 + 100 = 178.13 m ≈ 178 m. [1.5 Marks]
ICSE 2023 Board Q4 Heights & Distances (Depression Angles to Ships on Opposite Sides)
4 Marks
From the top of a tower 100 m high a man observes the angles of depression of two ships $A$ and $B$, on opposite sides of the tower as $45^\circ$ and $38^\circ$ respectively. If the foot of the tower and the ships are in the same horizontal line, find the distance between the two ships $A$ and $B$ to the nearest metre.
(Use Mathematical Tables for this question.)
Figure: Tower $CD = 100\text{ m}$ with depression angles $45^\circ$ and $38^\circ$ to ships $A$ and $B$
View Verified Solution & Marking
Let $CD$ be the tower of height 100 m, with foot $C$.
By alternate interior angles, angle of elevation at ship $A$ is $\angle CAD = 45^\circ$, and at ship $B$ is $\angle CBD = 38^\circ$.

Distance AC In right-angled $\triangle ACD$:
$\tan 45^\circ = \frac{CD}{AC} \implies 1 = \frac{100}{AC} \implies AC = 100\text{ m}$.1.5 Marks

Distance BC In right-angled $\triangle BCD$:
$\tan 38^\circ = \frac{CD}{BC} \implies BC = \frac{100}{\tan 38^\circ}$.
From natural tangents tables: $\tan 38^\circ \approx 0.7813$.
$BC = \frac{100}{0.7813} \approx 127.99\text{ m}$.1.5 Marks

Total Distance AB
$AB = AC + BC = 100 + 127.99 = 227.99\text{ m} \approx \mathbf{228\text{ m}}$ (to the nearest metre).1 Mark
ICSE 2020 Board Q5 Heights & Distances (Cliff & 20m Tower)
4 Marks
From the top of a cliff, the angle of depression of the top and bottom of a tower are observed to be $45^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ respectively. If the height of the tower is 20 m, find:
(i) the height of the cliff
(ii) the distance between the cliff and the tower.
View Verified Solution & Marking
Figure: Cliff $AB$ (height $H$) and tower $CD$ (height $20\text{ m}$) separated by distance $d$
Step 1 Let cliff height be $H$ ($AB = H$) and distance between them be $d$ ($BD = EC = d$).
Height of tower $CD = EB = 20\text{ m} \implies AE = H - 20$.
In right $\triangle AEC$: $\tan 45^\circ = \frac{AE}{EC} = \frac{H - 20}{d} \implies 1 = \frac{H - 20}{d} \implies \mathbf{H = d + 20}$. [1.5 Marks]
Step 2 In right $\triangle ABD$: $\tan 60^\circ = \frac{AB}{BD} = \frac{H}{d} \implies \sqrt{3} = \frac{H}{d} \implies \mathbf{H = d\sqrt{3}}$. [1 Mark]
Step 3 Equating expressions for $H$:
$$d\sqrt{3} = d + 20 \implies d(\sqrt{3} - 1) = 20 \implies d = \frac{20}{\sqrt{3} - 1} = \frac{20(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{2} = 10(1.732 + 1) = \mathbf{27.32\text{ m}}$$
Then cliff height $H = d + 20 = 27.32 + 20 = \mathbf{47.32\text{ m}}$.
Answer: (i) Height of cliff $= 47.32\text{ m}$, (ii) Distance $= 27.32\text{ m}$. [1.5 Marks]
ICSE 2019 Board Q6 Heights and Distances: Tower with Approaching Observer
4 Marks
A man observes the angle of elevation of the top of the tower to be $45^\circ$. He walks towards it in a horizontal line through its base. On covering $20\text{ m}$ the angle of elevation changes to $60^\circ$. Find the height of the tower correct to 2 significant figures.
View Verified Solution & Marking
Vector Diagram: Elevation angles 45° and 60° to tower top with 20 m baseline approach
Step 1 Let $AB = h$ be the height of the vertical tower and $B$ be its base.
Let observer start at $C$ ($\angle ACB = 45^\circ$) and walk $20\text{ m}$ to $D$ ($\angle ADB = 60^\circ$).
Let $DB = x$, then $CB = x + 20$. [1 Mark]
Step 2 In right triangle $\Delta ABC$:
$\tan 45^\circ = \frac{AB}{CB} \implies 1 = \frac{h}{x + 20} \implies x + 20 = h \implies x = h - 20$. [1 Mark]
Step 3 In right triangle $\Delta ABD$:
$\tan 60^\circ = \frac{AB}{DB} \implies \sqrt{3} = \frac{h}{x} \implies h = x\sqrt{3}$.
Substitute $x = h - 20$:
$h = (h - 20)\sqrt{3} \implies h = h\sqrt{3} - 20\sqrt{3} \implies h(\sqrt{3} - 1) = 20\sqrt{3}$.
$h = \frac{20\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} - 1} = \frac{20\sqrt{3}(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{3 - 1} = 10\sqrt{3}(\sqrt{3} + 1) = 10(3 + \sqrt{3})$. [1 Mark]
Step 4 Evaluate numerically with $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$:
$h = 10(3 + 1.732) = 10(4.732) = 47.32\text{ m}$.
Rounding to 2 significant figures: $\mathbf{h \approx 47\text{ m}}$.
Height of tower = 47 m. [1 Mark]
ICSE 2026 Imp Q7 Heights & Distances: Lighthouse Approaching Ship
5 Marks
As observed from the top of a light house, $80\text{ m}$ above sea level, the angle of depression of a ship, sailing directly towards it, changes from $35^\circ$ to $55^\circ$.
  1. Draw a free hand sketch representing the given information.
  2. Find the distance travelled by the ship during the period of observation. Give your answer to the nearest meter.
(Use mathematical tables for this question)
View Verified Solution & Marking
Vector Diagram: Elevation/depression geometry of ship approaching 80 m lighthouse
Step 1 (a) Freehand sketch with angles of depression $35^\circ$ and $55^\circ$ converting to alternate angles of elevation. [1.5 Marks]
Step 2 (b) In right triangle $\Delta ABD$ ($\angle ADB = 55^\circ$):
$\tan 55^\circ = \frac{AB}{BD} \implies BD = \frac{80}{\tan 55^\circ}$.
Using mathematical tables: $\tan 55^\circ \approx 1.4281 \implies BD = \frac{80}{1.4281} \approx 56.02\text{ m}$. [1.5 Marks]
Step 3 In right triangle $\Delta ABC$ ($\angle ACB = 35^\circ$):
$\tan 35^\circ = \frac{AB}{BC} \implies BC = \frac{80}{\tan 35^\circ}$.
Using mathematical tables: $\tan 35^\circ \approx 0.7002 \implies BC = \frac{80}{0.7002} \approx 114.25\text{ m}$. [1 Mark]
Step 4 Distance travelled by ship ($CD$):
$CD = BC - BD = 114.25 - 56.02 = 58.23\text{ m}$.
Rounding to nearest meter: $\mathbf{CD \approx 58\text{ m}}$. [1 Mark]
ICSE 2018 Board Q8 Heights and Distances - Two Towers
4 Marks
The angle of elevation from a point P of the top of a tower QR, 50m high is $60^\circ$ and that of the tower PT from a point Q is $30^\circ$. Find the height of the tower PT, correct to the nearest metre.
View Verified Solution & Marking
Step 1 Let distance between the two towers $PQ = d$, and height of tower $PT = h$.
In right $\triangle PQR$ ($\angle PQR = 90^\circ$):
$$\tan 60^\circ = \frac{QR}{PQ} = \frac{50}{d}$$ $$\sqrt{3} = \frac{50}{d} \implies d = \frac{50}{\sqrt{3}}\text{ m}$$ [1.5 Marks]
Step 2 In right $\triangle QPT$ ($\angle QPT = 90^\circ$):
$$\tan 30^\circ = \frac{PT}{PQ} = \frac{h}{d}$$ $$\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{h}{d} \implies h = \frac{d}{\sqrt{3}}$$ [1.5 Marks]
Step 3 Substitute $d = \frac{50}{\sqrt{3}}$:
$$h = \frac{\frac{50}{\sqrt{3}}}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{50}{3} = 16.67\text{ m}$$ [0.5 Mark]
Rounding to the nearest metre:
$$h \approx \mathbf{17\text{ metres}}$$ [0.5 Mark]
Answer: The height of the tower $PT$ is 17 metres.
ICSE 2017 Board Q9 Heights and Distances - Opposite Ships
4 Marks
The angles of depression of two ships A and B as observed from the top of a light house 60 m high are $60^\circ$ and $45^\circ$ respectively. If the two ships are on the opposite sides of the light house, find the distance between the two ships. Give your answer correct to the nearest whole number.
View Verified Solution & Marking
Step 1 Let $CD = 60\text{ m}$ be the lighthouse. $A$ and $B$ are on opposite sides of base $D$.
Angle of depression $60^\circ \implies \angle CAD = 60^\circ$.
Angle of depression $45^\circ \implies \angle CBD = 45^\circ$.
Step 2: Distance AD In right $\triangle CDA$ ($\angle D = 90^\circ$):
$$\tan 60^\circ = \frac{CD}{AD} \implies \sqrt{3} = \frac{60}{AD} \implies AD = \frac{60}{\sqrt{3}} = 20\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$$ [1.5 Marks]
Step 3: Distance BD In right $\triangle CDB$ ($\angle D = 90^\circ$):
$$\tan 45^\circ = \frac{CD}{BD} \implies 1 = \frac{60}{BD} \implies BD = 60\text{ m}$$ [1 Mark]
Step 4: Total Distance AB
$$AB = AD + BD = 20\sqrt{3} + 60 = 20(1.732) + 60 = 34.64 + 60 = 94.64\text{ m}$$ To the nearest whole number: $\mathbf{95\text{ metres}}$ [1.5 Marks]
Answer: The distance between the two ships is 95 metres.
