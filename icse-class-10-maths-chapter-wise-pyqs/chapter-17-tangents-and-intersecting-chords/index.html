17. Tangents and Intersecting Chords Previous Year Questions

Core Formula: Alternate Segment Theorem: ∠ between tangent & chord = ∠ in alternate segment

CISCE Examiner Observation & Common Deduction Pitfall

Do not assume radius is perpendicular to chord. Radius is perpendicular ONLY to the tangent line at the point of contact.

ICSE 2026 Board Q10(iii) Complex Circle Angles Proof
4 Marks
In the given figure, O is the centre of the circle. PQ is a tangent to the circle at B and AB = AC. If ∠CBQ = 40°, find the unknown angles x, y, z and w.
Circle with tangent PQ at B, AB = AC, diameter BOE, and ∠CBQ = 40°
View Verified Solution & Marking
Finding x By Alternate Segment Theorem, angle between tangent BQ and chord BC equals angle subtended by chord BC in the alternate segment: ∠BAC = ∠CBQ ⇒ x = 40°. [1 Mark]

Finding y Angle subtended at the centre O by arc BC is twice the angle subtended at the circumference: y = ∠BOC = 2 × ∠BAC = 2 × 40° ⇒ y = 80°. [1 Mark]

Finding z In cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, opposite angles are supplementary:
∠ABC + ∠ADC = 180°. Since AB = AC, ΔABC is isosceles ⇒ ∠ABC = ∠ACB = (180° − 40°)/2 = 70°.
⇒ z = ∠ADC = 180° − 70° ⇒ z = 110°. [1 Mark]

Finding w In right triangle with tangent PBQ: Radius OB ⊥ tangent PQ ⇒ ∠OBQ = 90°.
∠OBC = ∠OBQ − ∠CBQ = 90° − 40° = 50°.
Since ∠ABC = 70°, we have w = ∠ABO = ∠ABC − ∠OBC = 70° − 50° ⇒ w = 20°. [1 Mark]
ICSE 2025 Board Q9(iii) Circle Tangents & Alternate Segment Theorem
4 Marks
X, Y, Z and C are points on circle with centre O. AB is tangent at X and ZY = XY. Given ∠OBX = 32° and ∠AXZ = 66°. Find: (a) ∠BOX, (b) ∠CYX, (c) ∠ZYX, (d) ∠OXY.
View Official Marking Scheme Solution
(a) In right ΔOBX (∠OXB = 90°): ∠BOX = 90° − 32° = 58°. [1 Mark]
(b) By Alternate Segment Theorem, angle between tangent AX and chord XZ equals angle in alternate segment: ∠XYZ = ∠AXZ = 66°. [1 Mark]
(c) In ΔXYZ, ZY = XY ⇒ ∠XZY = ∠ZXY = (180° − 66°)/2 = 57°. Hence ∠ZYX = 66°. [1 Mark]
(d) Since OX ⊥ AB, ∠AXO = 90°. ∠OXY = ∠AXZ − ∠AXO = 66° − 90° or in ΔOXY, ∠OXY = 33°. [1 Mark]
ICSE 2025 Board Q10(ii) Tangent-Secant Theorem Proof
3 Marks
Chord SR produced meets tangent XTP at P.
(a) Prove ΔPTR ∼ ΔPST.
(b) Prove PT² = PR × PS.
(c) If PR = 4 cm and PS = 16 cm, find length of PT.
View Official Marking Scheme Solution
(a) In ΔPTR and ΔPST: ∠P = ∠P (Common), ∠PTR = ∠PST (Angle in alternate segment theorem). By AA criterion, ΔPTR ∼ ΔPST. [1 Mark]
(b) Proportional sides: PT/PS = PR/PT ⇒ PT² = PR × PS. [1 Mark]
(c) PT² = 4 × 16 = 64 ⇒ PT = 8 cm. [1 Mark]

ICSE 2024 Board Q9(i) Geometry • Tangent-Secant Theorem
3 Marks
The figure shows a circle of radius 9 cm with O as centre. Diameter AB produced meets tangent PQ at P. If PA = 24 cm, find the length of tangent PQ.
View Official Marking Scheme Solution
Radius = 9 cm ⇒ Diameter AB = 18 cm. PB = PA − AB = 24 − 18 = 6 cm. [1 Mark]
By Tangent-Secant Theorem: PQ2 = PA × PB = 24 × 6 = 144. [1 Mark]
PQ = √144 = 12 cm. [1 Mark]
ICSE 2023 Board Q1(iii) Multiple Choice Question
1 Mark
In the given diagram, $RT$ is a tangent touching the circle at $S$. If $\angle PST = 30^\circ$ and $\angle SPQ = 60^\circ$, then $\angle PSQ$ is equal to:
Figure: Tangent $RT$ touches at $S$, chord $SP$, $\triangle PQS$ inscribed
(a) 40°
(b) 30°
(c) 60°
(d) 90°
View Verified Solution & Marking
Step 1 By the Alternate Segment Theorem, the angle between the tangent $ST$ and chord $SP$ equals the angle subtended by $SP$ in the alternate segment:
$\angle PQS = \angle PST = 30^\circ$.
Step 2 In $\triangle PQS$, the sum of interior angles is $180^\circ$:
$\angle PSQ = 180^\circ - (\angle SPQ + \angle PQS) = 180^\circ - (60^\circ + 30^\circ) = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ$.
Correct Option: (d) [1 Mark]
ICSE 2023 Board Q2(iii) Circle Angle Properties & Alternate Segment
4 Marks
In the given figure, $O$ is the centre of the circle. $CE$ is a tangent to the circle at $A$. If $\angle ABD = 26^\circ$, then find:
  1. $\angle BDA$
  2. $\angle BAD$
  3. $\angle CAD$
  4. $\angle ODB$
Figure: Diameter $AB$, tangent $CE$ touching at $A$, chord $BD$ with $\angle ABD = 26^\circ$
View Verified Solution & Marking
Since segment $AB$ passes through the centre $O$, $AB$ is a diameter.

(a) $\angle BDA$ Angle in a semicircle is a right angle: $\angle BDA = 90^\circ$.1 Mark

(b) $\angle BAD$ In right-angled $\triangle ABD$, the acute angles are complementary:
$\angle BAD = 90^\circ - \angle ABD = 90^\circ - 26^\circ = \mathbf{64^\circ}$.1 Mark

(c) $\angle CAD$ By the Alternate Segment Theorem, the angle between tangent $AC$ and chord $AD$ is equal to the angle subtended by chord $AD$ in the alternate segment:
$\angle CAD = \angle ABD = \mathbf{26^\circ}$.
(Alternatively: radius $OA \perp CE \implies \angle CAO = 90^\circ$. Thus $\angle CAD = 90^\circ - 64^\circ = 26^\circ$).1 Mark

(d) $\angle ODB$ In $\triangle OBD$, $OB = OD$ (radii of the same circle).
Therefore, $\triangle OBD$ is isosceles with $\angle ODB = \angle OBD = \mathbf{26^\circ}$.1 Mark
ICSE 2023 Board Q4(iii) Tangent-Secant Theorem & Circle Geometry
4 Marks
In the given figure, $O$ is the centre of the circle. $PQ$ is a tangent to the circle at $T$. Chord $AB$ produced meets the tangent at $P$.
$AB = 9\text{ cm}, BP = 16\text{ cm}, \angle PTB = 50^\circ, \angle OBA = 45^\circ$. Find:
  1. length of $PT$
  2. $\angle BAT$
  3. $\angle BOT$
  4. $\angle ABT$
Figure: Tangent $PT$ and secant $PBA$ with chord $BT$
View Verified Solution & Marking
(a) Length of PT By the Tangent-Secant Theorem, $PT^2 = PA \times PB$.
Here $PA = AB + BP = 9 + 16 = 25\text{ cm}$, and $PB = 16\text{ cm}$.
$PT^2 = 25 \times 16 = 400 \implies PT = \sqrt{400} = \mathbf{20\text{ cm}}$.1 Mark

(b) $\angle BAT$ By the Alternate Segment Theorem, the angle between tangent $PT$ and chord $BT$ equals the angle subtended by $BT$ in the alternate segment:
$\angle BAT = \angle PTB = \mathbf{50^\circ}$.1 Mark

(c) $\angle BOT$ The angle subtended by arc $BT$ at the centre is twice the angle subtended by it at any point on the remaining part of the circle:
$\angle BOT = 2 \times \angle BAT = 2 \times 50^\circ = \mathbf{100^\circ}$.1 Mark

(d) $\angle ABT$ In $\triangle OBT$, $OB = OT$ (radii). Thus $\triangle OBT$ is isosceles:
$\angle OBT = \frac{180^\circ - \angle BOT}{2} = \frac{180^\circ - 100^\circ}{2} = 40^\circ$.
Radius $OB$ divides angle $\angle ABT$ into $\angle OBA$ and $\angle OBT$:
$\angle ABT = \angle OBA + \angle OBT = 45^\circ + 40^\circ = \mathbf{85^\circ}$.1 Mark
