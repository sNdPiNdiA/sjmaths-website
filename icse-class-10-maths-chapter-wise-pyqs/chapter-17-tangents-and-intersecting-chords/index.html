17. Tangents and Intersecting Chords Previous Year Questions

Core Formula: Alternate Segment Theorem: ∠ between tangent & chord = ∠ in alternate segment

CISCE Examiner Observation & Common Deduction Pitfall

Do not assume radius is perpendicular to chord. Radius is perpendicular ONLY to the tangent line at the point of contact.

ICSE 2026 Board Q1 Complex Circle Angles Proof
4 Marks
In the given figure, O is the centre of the circle. PQ is a tangent to the circle at B and AB = AC. If ∠CBQ = 40°, find the unknown angles x, y, z and w.
Circle with tangent PQ at B, AB = AC, diameter BOE, and ∠CBQ = 40°
View Verified Solution & Marking
Finding x By Alternate Segment Theorem, angle between tangent BQ and chord BC equals angle subtended by chord BC in the alternate segment: ∠BAC = ∠CBQ ⇒ x = 40°. [1 Mark]

Finding y Angle subtended at the centre O by arc BC is twice the angle subtended at the circumference: y = ∠BOC = 2 × ∠BAC = 2 × 40° ⇒ y = 80°. [1 Mark]

Finding z In cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, opposite angles are supplementary:
∠ABC + ∠ADC = 180°. Since AB = AC, ΔABC is isosceles ⇒ ∠ABC = ∠ACB = (180° − 40°)/2 = 70°.
⇒ z = ∠ADC = 180° − 70° ⇒ z = 110°. [1 Mark]

Finding w In right triangle with tangent PBQ: Radius OB ⊥ tangent PQ ⇒ ∠OBQ = 90°.
∠OBC = ∠OBQ − ∠CBQ = 90° − 40° = 50°.
Since ∠ABC = 70°, we have w = ∠ABO = ∠ABC − ∠OBC = 70° − 50° ⇒ w = 20°. [1 Mark]
ICSE 2025 Board Q2 Circle Tangents & Alternate Segment Theorem
4 Marks
X, Y, Z and C are points on circle with centre O. AB is tangent at X and ZY = XY. Given ∠OBX = 32° and ∠AXZ = 66°. Find: (a) ∠BOX, (b) ∠CYX, (c) ∠ZYX, (d) ∠OXY.
View Verified Solution & Marking
(a) In right ΔOBX (∠OXB = 90°): ∠BOX = 90° − 32° = 58°. [1 Mark]
(b) By Alternate Segment Theorem, angle between tangent AX and chord XZ equals angle in alternate segment: ∠XYZ = ∠AXZ = 66°. [1 Mark]
(c) In ΔXYZ, ZY = XY ⇒ ∠XZY = ∠ZXY = (180° − 66°)/2 = 57°. Hence ∠ZYX = 66°. [1 Mark]
(d) Since OX ⊥ AB, ∠AXO = 90°. ∠OXY = ∠AXZ − ∠AXO = 66° − 90° or in ΔOXY, ∠OXY = 33°. [1 Mark]
ICSE 2025 Board Q3 Tangent-Secant Theorem Proof
3 Marks
Chord SR produced meets tangent XTP at P.
(a) Prove ΔPTR ∼ ΔPST.
(b) Prove PT² = PR × PS.
(c) If PR = 4 cm and PS = 16 cm, find length of PT.
View Verified Solution & Marking
(a) In ΔPTR and ΔPST: ∠P = ∠P (Common), ∠PTR = ∠PST (Angle in alternate segment theorem). By AA criterion, ΔPTR ∼ ΔPST. [1 Mark]
(b) Proportional sides: PT/PS = PR/PT ⇒ PT² = PR × PS. [1 Mark]
(c) PT² = 4 × 16 = 64 ⇒ PT = 8 cm. [1 Mark]

ICSE 2024 Board Q6 Geometry • Tangent-Secant Theorem
3 Marks
The figure shows a circle of radius 9 cm with O as centre. Diameter AB produced meets tangent PQ at P. If PA = 24 cm, find the length of tangent PQ.
View Verified Solution & Marking
Radius = 9 cm ⇒ Diameter AB = 18 cm. PB = PA − AB = 24 − 18 = 6 cm. [1 Mark]
By Tangent-Secant Theorem: PQ2 = PA × PB = 24 × 6 = 144. [1 Mark]
PQ = √144 = 12 cm. [1 Mark]
ICSE 2023 Board Q7 Multiple Choice Question
1 Mark
In the given diagram, $RT$ is a tangent touching the circle at $S$. If $\angle PST = 30^\circ$ and $\angle SPQ = 60^\circ$, then $\angle PSQ$ is equal to:
Figure: Tangent $RT$ touches at $S$, chord $SP$, $\triangle PQS$ inscribed
(a) 40°
(b) 30°
(c) 60°
(d) 90°
View Verified Solution & Marking
Step 1 By the Alternate Segment Theorem, the angle between the tangent $ST$ and chord $SP$ equals the angle subtended by $SP$ in the alternate segment:
$\angle PQS = \angle PST = 30^\circ$.
Step 2 In $\triangle PQS$, the sum of interior angles is $180^\circ$:
$\angle PSQ = 180^\circ - (\angle SPQ + \angle PQS) = 180^\circ - (60^\circ + 30^\circ) = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ$.
Correct Option: (d) [1 Mark]
ICSE 2023 Board Q8 Circle Angle Properties & Alternate Segment
4 Marks
In the given figure, $O$ is the centre of the circle. $CE$ is a tangent to the circle at $A$. If $\angle ABD = 26^\circ$, then find:
  1. $\angle BDA$
  2. $\angle BAD$
  3. $\angle CAD$
  4. $\angle ODB$
Figure: Diameter $AB$, tangent $CE$ touching at $A$, chord $BD$ with $\angle ABD = 26^\circ$
View Verified Solution & Marking
Since segment $AB$ passes through the centre $O$, $AB$ is a diameter.

(a) $\angle BDA$ Angle in a semicircle is a right angle: $\angle BDA = 90^\circ$.1 Mark

(b) $\angle BAD$ In right-angled $\triangle ABD$, the acute angles are complementary:
$\angle BAD = 90^\circ - \angle ABD = 90^\circ - 26^\circ = \mathbf{64^\circ}$.1 Mark

(c) $\angle CAD$ By the Alternate Segment Theorem, the angle between tangent $AC$ and chord $AD$ is equal to the angle subtended by chord $AD$ in the alternate segment:
$\angle CAD = \angle ABD = \mathbf{26^\circ}$.
(Alternatively: radius $OA \perp CE \implies \angle CAO = 90^\circ$. Thus $\angle CAD = 90^\circ - 64^\circ = 26^\circ$).1 Mark

(d) $\angle ODB$ In $\triangle OBD$, $OB = OD$ (radii of the same circle).
Therefore, $\triangle OBD$ is isosceles with $\angle ODB = \angle OBD = \mathbf{26^\circ}$.1 Mark
ICSE 2023 Board Q9 Tangent-Secant Theorem & Circle Geometry
4 Marks
In the given figure, $O$ is the centre of the circle. $PQ$ is a tangent to the circle at $T$. Chord $AB$ produced meets the tangent at $P$.
$AB = 9\text{ cm}, BP = 16\text{ cm}, \angle PTB = 50^\circ, \angle OBA = 45^\circ$. Find:
  1. length of $PT$
  2. $\angle BAT$
  3. $\angle BOT$
  4. $\angle ABT$
Figure: Tangent $PT$ and secant $PBA$ with chord $BT$
View Verified Solution & Marking
(a) Length of PT By the Tangent-Secant Theorem, $PT^2 = PA \times PB$.
Here $PA = AB + BP = 9 + 16 = 25\text{ cm}$, and $PB = 16\text{ cm}$.
$PT^2 = 25 \times 16 = 400 \implies PT = \sqrt{400} = \mathbf{20\text{ cm}}$.1 Mark

(b) $\angle BAT$ By the Alternate Segment Theorem, the angle between tangent $PT$ and chord $BT$ equals the angle subtended by $BT$ in the alternate segment:
$\angle BAT = \angle PTB = \mathbf{50^\circ}$.1 Mark

(c) $\angle BOT$ The angle subtended by arc $BT$ at the centre is twice the angle subtended by it at any point on the remaining part of the circle:
$\angle BOT = 2 \times \angle BAT = 2 \times 50^\circ = \mathbf{100^\circ}$.1 Mark

(d) $\angle ABT$ In $\triangle OBT$, $OB = OT$ (radii). Thus $\triangle OBT$ is isosceles:
$\angle OBT = \frac{180^\circ - \angle BOT}{2} = \frac{180^\circ - 100^\circ}{2} = 40^\circ$.
Radius $OB$ divides angle $\angle ABT$ into $\angle OBA$ and $\angle OBT$:
$\angle ABT = \angle OBA + \angle OBT = 45^\circ + 40^\circ = \mathbf{85^\circ}$.1 Mark
ICSE 2020 Board Q10 Tangents from External Point & Inscribed Angles
4 Marks
In the given figure $TP$ and $TQ$ are two tangents to the circle with centre $O$, touching at $A$ and $C$ respectively. If $\angle BCQ = 55^\circ$ and $\angle BAP = 60^\circ$, find:
(i) $\angle OBA$ and $\angle OBC$   (ii) $\angle AOC$   (iii) $\angle ATC$
Figure: Circle with tangents $TP$ and $TQ$ touching at $A$ and $C$
View Verified Solution & Marking
Tangent radius theorem: $OA \perp TP \implies \angle OAP = 90^\circ$ and $OC \perp TQ \implies \angle OCQ = 90^\circ$.
(i) In $\triangle OAB$: $OA = OB$ (radii).
$\angle OAB = 90^\circ - \angle BAP = 90^\circ - 60^\circ = 30^\circ \implies \mathbf{\angle OBA = 30^\circ}$.
In $\triangle OBC$: $OC = OB$ (radii).
$\angle OCB = 90^\circ - \angle BCQ = 90^\circ - 55^\circ = 35^\circ \implies \mathbf{\angle OBC = 35^\circ}$. [1.5 Marks]
(ii) Total angle $\angle ABC = \angle OBA + \angle OBC = 30^\circ + 35^\circ = 65^\circ$.
Angle at centre is double the angle at the circumference:
$\mathbf{\angle AOC = 2 \times \angle ABC = 2 \times 65^\circ = 130^\circ}$. [1.5 Marks]
(iii) In tangent quadrilateral $OATC$: $\angle OAT = \angle OCT = 90^\circ$.
$\mathbf{\angle ATC = 180^\circ - \angle AOC = 180^\circ - 130^\circ = 50^\circ}$. [1 Mark]
ICSE 2019 Board Q11 Circle Tangent, Inscribed Angles & Unknowns x, y
3 Marks
In the given figure $AC$ is a tangent to the circle with centre $O$. If $\angle ADB = 55^\circ$, find $x$ and $y$. Give reasons for your answers.
Vector Diagram: Circle with tangent AC, radius OA, secant BC, and marked angles x, y
View Verified Solution & Marking
Step 1 Find $\angle B$ using properties of circle and tangent:
$AC$ is tangent at $A$ and $OA$ is radius $\implies OA \perp AC \implies \angle OAC = 90^\circ$.
In $\Delta ABD$, angle in semicircle / tangent property gives $\angle A = 90^\circ$.
In $\Delta ABD$: $\angle B = 180^\circ - (\angle A + \angle ADB) = 180^\circ - (90^\circ + 55^\circ) = 180^\circ - 145^\circ = 35^\circ$.
Since $OB = OE$ (radii of circle), in $\Delta OBE$, $\angle OEB = \angle B = 35^\circ$. [1 Mark]
Step 2 Find $x$ using $\Delta EDC$:
$\angle DEC = \angle OEB = 35^\circ$ (vertically opposite angles).
Since $\angle ADE$ and $\angle EDC$ form a linear pair on line $AD$:
$\angle EDC = 180^\circ - 55^\circ = 125^\circ$.
In $\Delta EDC$: the sum of angles is $180^\circ$:
$x + \angle DEC + \angle EDC = 180^\circ \implies x + 35^\circ + 125^\circ = 180^\circ \implies x + 160^\circ = 180^\circ \implies \mathbf{x = 20^\circ}$. [1 Mark]
Step 3 Find $y$ using right triangle $\Delta OAC$:
In $\Delta OAC$, $\angle OAC = 90^\circ$ and $\angle ACO = x = 20^\circ$.
$y + \angle OAC + x = 180^\circ \implies y + 90^\circ + 20^\circ = 180^\circ \implies y + 110^\circ = 180^\circ \implies \mathbf{y = 70^\circ}$.
Answer: $x = 20^\circ$ and $y = 70^\circ$. [1 Mark]
ICSE 2026 Imp Q12 Tangent-Secant Theorem & Triangle Similarity
4 Marks
In the given diagram, $PT$ is a tangent to the circle and $QT$ is a secant meeting the circle at $R$, such that $QR = 9\text{ cm}$ and $RT = 16\text{ cm}$.
  1. Prove that: $\Delta PTR \sim \Delta QTP$.
  2. Find the length of $PT$.
Vector Diagram: Tangent PT and secant QTR illustrating PT² = TR × TQ
View Verified Solution & Marking
Step 1 (a) Prove $\Delta PTR \sim \Delta QTP$:
In $\Delta PTR$ and $\Delta QTP$:
$\angle T = \angle T$ (Common angle)
$\angle TPR = \angle TQP$ (Angles in alternate segment by Alternate Segment Theorem)
$\therefore \mathbf{\Delta PTR \sim \Delta QTP}$ by **AA Similarity Criterion**. [2 Marks]
Step 2 (b) Determine length of $PT$ using ratio of corresponding sides:
$\frac{PT}{QT} = \frac{RT}{PT} \implies PT^2 = QT \times RT$.
Given $QR = 9\text{ cm}$ and $RT = 16\text{ cm} \implies QT = QR + RT = 9 + 16 = 25\text{ cm}$.
$PT^2 = 25 \times 16 = 400 \implies PT = \sqrt{400} = \mathbf{20\text{ cm}}$. [2 Marks]
ICSE 2017 Board Q13 Circle Tangents and Bisectors
3 Marks
In the given figure $PQ$ is a tangent to the circle at $A$. $AB$ and $AD$ are bisectors of $\angle CAQ$ and $\angle PAC$. If $\angle BAQ = 30^\circ$, prove that:
(i) $BD$ is a diameter of the circle.
(ii) $ABC$ is an isosceles triangle.
View Verified Solution & Marking
(i) Prove BD is Diameter
Since $AB$ bisects $\angle CAQ$: $\angle CAB = \angle BAQ = 30^\circ \implies \angle CAQ = 60^\circ$.
Since $PAQ$ is a straight tangent line: $\angle PAC = 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ$.
$AD$ bisects $\angle PAC \implies \angle DAC = \frac{120^\circ}{2} = 60^\circ$.
$$\angle DAB = \angle DAC + \angle CAB = 60^\circ + 30^\circ = 90^\circ$$ Since $\angle DAB = 90^\circ$ is an inscribed angle in the circle, chord $BD$ must subtend a right angle at the circumference.
$\therefore \mathbf{BD\text{ is a diameter of the circle}}$. [1.5 Marks]
(ii) Prove ABC is Isosceles
By Alternate Segment Theorem: Angle between tangent $AQ$ and chord $AB$ equals the angle in the alternate segment:
$$\angle ACB = \angle BAQ = 30^\circ$$ In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle CAB = 30^\circ$ and $\angle ACB = 30^\circ$.
Since two angles are equal, opposite sides $AB = BC$.
$\therefore \mathbf{\triangle ABC\text{ is an isosceles triangle}}$. [1.5 Marks]
Hence Proved.
