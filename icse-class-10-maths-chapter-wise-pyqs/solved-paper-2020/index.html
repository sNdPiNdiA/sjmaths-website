In the figure given below, $O$ is the centre of the circle and $AB$ is a diameter. If $AC = BD$ and $\angle AOC = 72^\circ$. Find:
(i) $\angle ABC$   (ii) $\angle BAD$   (iii) $\angle ABD$
Figure: Circle with diameter $AB$, centre $O$, and equal chords $AC = BD$
View Stepwise Solution & Rubric
(i) Arc $AC$ subtends $\angle AOC = 72^\circ$ at the centre and $\angle ABC$ at the circumference.
$\angle ABC = \frac{1}{2} \angle AOC = \frac{1}{2} \times 72^\circ = \mathbf{36^\circ}$. (Angle at circumference is half angle at centre). [1 Mark]
(ii) Given chord $AC = \text{chord } BD$. Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre:
$\angle BOD = \angle AOC = 72^\circ$.
Therefore, $\angle BAD = \frac{1}{2} \angle BOD = \frac{1}{2} \times 72^\circ = \mathbf{36^\circ}$. [1 Mark]
(iii) Since $AB$ is a diameter, $\angle ADB = 90^\circ$ (Angle in a semicircle is a right angle).

(b) Trigonometrical Identity Proof [3 Marks] Ch 20: Trig Identities
Prove that: $$\frac{\sin A}{1 + \cot A} - \frac{\cos A}{1 + \tan A} = \sin A - \cos A$$
View Stepwise Solution & Rubric
Step 1 Express $\cot A$ and $\tan A$ in terms of $\sin A$ and $\cos A$:
$$\text{LHS} = \frac{\sin A}{1 + \frac{\cos A}{\sin A}} - \frac{\cos A}{1 + \frac{\sin A}{\cos A}} = \frac{\sin A}{\frac{\sin A + \cos A}{\sin A}} - \frac{\cos A}{\frac{\cos A + \sin A}{\cos A}}$$ [1 Mark]
Step 2 Simplify the compound fractions:
$$= \frac{\sin^2 A}{\sin A + \cos A} - \frac{\cos^2 A}{\sin A + \cos A} = \frac{\sin^2 A - \cos^2 A}{\sin A + \cos A}$$ [1 Mark]
Step 3 Factor the difference of squares in the numerator:
$$= \frac{(\sin A - \cos A)(\sin A + \cos A)}{\sin A + \cos A} = \sin A - \cos A = \text{RHS}$$
Hence Proved. [1 Mark]
(c) Section Formula (Division by Y-Axis) [4 Marks] Ch 12: Section & Midpoint
In what ratio is the line joining $P(5, 3)$ and $Q(-5, 3)$ divided by the y-axis? Also find the coordinates of the point of intersection.
View Stepwise Solution & Rubric
Step 1 Let the y-axis divide the segment joining $P(5, 3)$ and $Q(-5, 3)$ in the ratio $k : 1$ at point $R(0, y)$ (since any point on the y-axis has $x = 0$). [1 Mark]
Step 2 By section formula for the x-coordinate:
$$x = \frac{k(-5) + 1(5)}{k + 1} \implies 0 = \frac{-5k + 5}{k + 1} \implies -5k + 5 = 0 \implies 5k = 5 \implies k = 1$$
Thus, the required ratio is $1 : 1$ (the segment is bisected by the y-axis). [1.5 Marks]
Step 3 By section formula for the y-coordinate:
$$y = \frac{1(3) + 1(3)}{1 + 1} = \frac{6}{2} = 3$$
Answer: Ratio = $1 : 1$, Point of intersection = $(0, 3)$. [1.5 Marks]

(a) Solid Mensuration (Melting Sphere into Marbles) [3 Marks] Ch 19: Mensuration
A solid spherical ball of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into 64 identical spherical marbles. Find the radius of each marble.
View Stepwise Solution & Rubric
Step 1 Radius of big spherical ball $R = 6\text{ cm}$.
Volume of big sphere $V = \frac{4}{3} \pi R^3 = \frac{4}{3} \pi (6)^3\text{ cm}^3$. [1 Mark]
Step 2 Let $r$ be the radius of each small spherical marble.
Total volume of 64 marbles $= 64 \times \left(\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3\right)$.
By conservation of volume during recasting:
$$\frac{4}{3} \pi (6)^3 = 64 \times \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \implies 216 = 64 r^3$$ [1 Mark]
Step 3 Solving for $r$:
$$r^3 = \frac{216}{64} = \frac{27}{8} \implies r = \sqrt[3]{\frac{27}{8}} = \frac{3}{2} = \mathbf{1.5\text{ cm}}$$
Answer: The radius of each marble is $1.5\text{ cm}$. [1 Mark]

(b) Arithmetic Mean (Find Missing Frequency 'f') [3 Marks] Ch 22: Central Tendency
The mean of the following data is 16. Calculate the value of $f$.
Marks ($x$): 5, 10, 15, 20, 25
No. of Students ($f$): 3, 7, $f$, 9, 6
View Stepwise Solution & Rubric
Step 1 Calculate $\sum f_i$ and $\sum f_i x_i$:
$\sum f_i = 3 + 7 + f + 9 + 6 = 25 + f$. [1 Mark]
$\sum f_i x_i = (5 \times 3) + (10 \times 7) + (15 \times f) + (20 \times 9) + (25 \times 6)$

(c) Geometric Progression (Terms & Common Ratio) [4 Marks] Ch 9: Geometric Progression
The 4th, 6th and the last term of a geometric progression are 10, 40 and 640 respectively. If the common ratio is positive, find the first term, common ratio and the number of terms of the series.
View Stepwise Solution & Rubric
Step 1 Let the first term be $a$ and common ratio be $r$ ($r > 0$).
$T_4 = a r^3 = 10$
$T_6 = a r^5 = 40$

Step 2 Substitute $x = -\frac{1}{2}$ into $P(x)$:
$$P\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = 2\left(-\frac{1}{8}\right) - 3\left(\frac{1}{4}\right) - 8\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) + k = -\frac{1}{4} - \frac{3}{4} + 4 + k = -1 + 4 + k = 3 + k$$ [1 Mark]
Step 3 Equating remainder to 10:
$$3 + k = 10 \implies \mathbf{k = 7}$$
Answer: 7 must be added. [1 Mark]

(b) Componendo & Dividendo Radical Proof [4 Marks] Ch 10: Ratio & Proportion
If $x = \frac{\sqrt{2a + 1} + \sqrt{2a - 1}}{\sqrt{2a + 1} - \sqrt{2a - 1}}$, prove that $x^2 - 4ax + 1 = 0$.
View Stepwise Solution & Rubric
Step 1 Express $x$ as $\frac{x}{1}$ and apply Componendo and Dividendo:
$$\frac{x + 1}{x - 1} = \frac{(\sqrt{2a + 1} + \sqrt{2a - 1}) + (\sqrt{2a + 1} - \sqrt{2a - 1})}{(\sqrt{2a + 1} + \sqrt{2a - 1}) - (\sqrt{2a + 1} - \sqrt{2a - 1})} = \frac{2\sqrt{2a + 1}}{2\sqrt{2a - 1}} = \frac{\sqrt{2a + 1}}{\sqrt{2a - 1}}$$ [1.5 Marks]
Step 2 Squaring both sides:
$$\frac{(x + 1)^2}{(x - 1)^2} = \frac{2a + 1}{2a - 1} \implies \frac{x^2 + 2x + 1}{x^2 - 2x + 1} = \frac{2a + 1}{2a - 1}$$ [1 Mark]
Step 3 Applying Componendo and Dividendo again:
$$\frac{(x^2 + 2x + 1) + (x^2 - 2x + 1)}{(x^2 + 2x + 1) - (x^2 - 2x + 1)} = \frac{(2a + 1) + (2a - 1)}{(2a + 1) - (2a - 1)}$$
$$\frac{2(x^2 + 1)}{4x} = \frac{4a}{2} \implies \frac{x^2 + 1}{2x} = 2a \implies x^2 + 1 = 4ax \implies \mathbf{x^2 - 4ax + 1 = 0}$$
Hence Proved. [1.5 Marks]

From a solid wooden cylinder of height 28 cm and diameter 6 cm, two conical cavities are hollowed out. The diameters of the cones are also of 6 cm and height 10.5 cm. Taking $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$, find the volume of the remaining solid.

(c) Trigonometric Identity Proof [4 Marks] Ch 20: Trig Identities
Prove the identity: $$\left(\frac{1 - \tan \theta}{1 - \cot \theta}\right)^2 = \tan^2 \theta$$
View Stepwise Solution & Rubric
Step 1 Express $\cot \theta$ as $\frac{1}{\tan \theta}$:
$$\text{LHS} = \left(\frac{1 - \tan \theta}{1 - \frac{1}{\tan \theta}}\right)^2$$ [1.5 Marks]
Step 2 Simplify the denominator fraction:
$$1 - \frac{1}{\tan \theta} = \frac{\tan \theta - 1}{\tan \theta} = -\frac{1 - \tan \theta}{\tan \theta}$$ [1.5 Marks]
Step 3 Substitute back into LHS:
$$\text{LHS} = \left(\frac{1 - \tan \theta}{-\frac{1 - \tan \theta}{\tan \theta}}\right)^2 = \left(-\tan \theta\right)^2 = \mathbf{\tan^2 \theta} = \text{RHS}$$
Hence Proved. [1 Mark]
