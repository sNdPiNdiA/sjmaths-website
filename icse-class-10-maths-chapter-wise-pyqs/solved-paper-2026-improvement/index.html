(ii) Mensuration: Ratio of Volumes of Two Cones [1 Mark] Ch 19: Mensuration
Two right circular cones, $C_1$ and $C_2$, have their heights in the ratio $2 : 1$ and radii in the ratio $1 : 2$. The ratio of their volumes is:
(a) 1 : 4
(b) 1 : 2
(c) 2 : 1
(d) 4 : 1
View Stepwise Solution & Rubric
Solution Given: $\frac{h_1}{h_2} = \frac{2}{1}$ and $\frac{r_1}{r_2} = \frac{1}{2}$.
$\text{Ratio of volumes } \frac{V_1}{V_2} = \frac{\frac{1}{3}\pi r_1^2 h_1}{\frac{1}{3}\pi r_2^2 h_2} = \left(\frac{r_1}{r_2}\right)^2 \times \left(\frac{h_1}{h_2}\right) = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 \times \left(\frac{2}{1}\right) = \frac{1}{4} \times 2 = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}}$.
Correct Answer: (b) 1 : 2 [1 Mark]

(xiii) Trigonometric Assertion & Reason: Secant Identity [1 Mark] Ch 20: Trigonometrical Identities
Assertion (A): $\sec\theta (1 + \sin\theta)(\sec\theta - \tan\theta) = 1$
Reason (R): $\sec^2\theta - \tan^2\theta = 1$, for any valid value of $\theta$.
(a) (A) is true, (R) is false.
(b) (A) is false, (R) is true.
(c) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(d) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
View Stepwise Solution & Rubric

Hence Assertion (A) is true, Reason (R) is true, and (R) is the exact justification for (A).
Correct Answer: (c) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A). [1 Mark]

Vector Diagram: Solid sphere fitting completely inside cylindrical container of height 7 cm
View Stepwise Solution & Rubric

(ii) Quadratic Word Problem: Car Speed and Journey Time [4 Marks] Ch 5: Quadratic Equations
A car covers a distance of $400\text{ km}$ at a certain speed. Had the speed been $12\text{ km/h}$ more, the time taken for the journey would have been 1 hour 40 minutes less. Find the:
  1. original speed of the car.
  2. time taken with the increased speed.
View Stepwise Solution & Rubric
Step 1 Let the original speed of the car be $x\text{ km/h}$.
Original time $t_1 = \frac{400}{x}\text{ hours}$.
Increased speed $= (x + 12)\text{ km/h}$.
New time $t_2 = \frac{400}{x + 12}\text{ hours}$.
Time difference $= 1\text{ hr } 40\text{ min} = 1 + \frac{40}{60} = 1 + \frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{3}\text{ hours}$. [1 Mark]
Step 2 Formulate and solve quadratic equation:
$\frac{400}{x} - \frac{400}{x + 12} = \frac{5}{3} \implies 400 \left[\frac{x + 12 - x}{x(x + 12)}\right] = \frac{5}{3}$
$\frac{4800}{x^2 + 12x} = \frac{5}{3} \implies 5(x^2 + 12x) = 14400 \implies x^2 + 12x - 2880 = 0$. [1.5 Marks]
Step 3 Factorise quadratic equation:
$(x + 60)(x - 48) = 0 \implies x = 48 \text{ or } x = -60$.
Since speed cannot be negative, $x = 48\text{ km/h}$.
(a) $\mathbf{\text{Original speed} = 48\text{ km/h}}$. [1 Mark]
Step 4 (b) Time taken with increased speed ($48 + 12 = 60\text{ km/h}$):
$\text{Time} = \frac{400}{60} = \frac{20}{3}\text{ hours} = \mathbf{6\text{ hours } 40\text{ minutes}}$. [0.5 Mark]

(ii) Surd Arithmetic Progression & Sum of 10 Terms [4 Marks] Ch 8: Arithmetic Progression
$\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{8}, \sqrt{18}, \dots$ forms a progression.
  1. Identify the type of progression.
  2. The sum of its first 10 terms is $p\sqrt{2}$. Find the value of $p$.
View Stepwise Solution & Rubric
Step 1 (a) Simplify surd terms:
$T_1 = \sqrt{2}$, $T_2 = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}$, $T_3 = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}$.
$T_2 - T_1 = 2\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2}$, and $T_3 - T_2 = 3\sqrt{2} - 2\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2}$.

Step 2 (b) Calculate sum of first 10 terms ($S_{10}$):
$S_{10} = \frac{10}{2}[2a + (10 - 1)d] = 5[2\sqrt{2} + 9\sqrt{2}] = 5(11\sqrt{2}) = 55\sqrt{2}$.
Given $S_{10} = p\sqrt{2} \implies \mathbf{p = 55}$. [2 Marks]

(iii) Trigonometrical Identity: Radical Proof [4 Marks] Ch 20: Trigonometrical Identities
Prove that: $$\sqrt{\frac{\sec\theta + 1}{\sec\theta - 1}} + \sqrt{\frac{\sec\theta - 1}{\sec\theta + 1}} = 2\text{ cosec}\,\theta$$
View Stepwise Solution & Rubric
Step 1 Combine the fractions by taking the common denominator:
$\text{LHS} = \frac{\left(\sqrt{\sec\theta + 1}\right)^2 + \left(\sqrt{\sec\theta - 1}\right)^2}{\sqrt{(\sec\theta - 1)(\sec\theta + 1)}} = \frac{(\sec\theta + 1) + (\sec\theta - 1)}{\sqrt{\sec^2\theta - 1}}$. [1.5 Marks]
Step 2 Simplify numerator and denominator:
$\text{Numerator} = 2\sec\theta$.
Using identity $\sec^2\theta - 1 = \tan^2\theta$, $\sqrt{\sec^2\theta - 1} = \tan\theta$.
$\text{LHS} = \frac{2\sec\theta}{\tan\theta}$. [1.5 Marks]
Step 3 Express in terms of sine and cosine:
$\frac{2\sec\theta}{\tan\theta} = 2 \times \frac{1/\cos\theta}{\sin\theta/\cos\theta} = \frac{2}{\sin\theta} = \mathbf{2\text{ cosec}\,\theta} = \text{RHS}$.
Hence Proved. [1 Mark]
