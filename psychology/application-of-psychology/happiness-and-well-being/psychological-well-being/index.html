Psychological Well-Being

Psychological well-being (PWB) is a multifaceted construct reflecting optimal human functioning, personal growth, and existential life fulfillment, moving beyond mere absence of mental illness.

UP PGT Psychology • Code 13 UNIT X • Application of Psychology

1. Definitional Framework and Foundational Theories

Psychological well-being (PWB) is fundamentally divided into two major philosophical and empirical traditions: Hedonic well-being (often equated with subjective well-being or SWB, pioneered by Ed Diener), which focuses on happiness, pleasure maximization, and pain avoidance; and Eudaimonic well-being, which traces its roots back to Aristotle's concept of eudaimonia (human flourishing and realizing one's true potential).

  • Ryff's Multidimensional Model of PWB (1989, 1995): Carol Ryff synthesized diverse life-span developmental and clinical theories (including Carl Rogers' fully functioning person, Abraham Maslow's self-actualization, Gordon Allport's maturity, and Erik Erikson's psychosocial stages) into a six-factor model of psychological well-being: Autonomy, Environmental Mastery, Personal Growth, Positive Relations with Others, Purpose in Life, and Self-Acceptance.
  • Self-Determination Theory (SDT) by Deci & Ryan (1985, 2000): Posits that psychological well-being depends upon the fulfillment of three basic psychological needs: Autonomy (feeling in control of one's actions), Competence (feeling effective in one's interactions with the social environment), and Relatedness (feeling a sense of belonging and connectedness with others).
  • Diener’s Subjective Well-Being (SWB) Paradigm: Comprises three components: high positive affect, low negative affect, and cognitive life satisfaction. While distinct from eudaimonic PWB, both interact dynamically to constitute holistic mental health.

2. Neurocognitive / Empirical Mechanisms & Methodological Paradigms

Empirical investigations into psychological well-being integrate neurobiological markers with psychometric evaluations. Neuroimaging studies (e.g., Richard Davidson's affective neuroscience lab) demonstrate that sustained eudaimonic well-being and positive affect are correlated with greater activation in the left prefrontal cortex (PFC) and lower baseline levels of salivary cortisol and systemic inflammatory markers (such as interleukin-6).

Methodologically, PWB is assessed using both self-report psychometric scales—such as Ryff's Psychological Well-Being Scales (PWBS), the Satisfaction with Life Scale (SWLS) by Diener, and the Flourishing Scale—and ecological momentary assessment (EMA) to track real-time emotional states and daily meaning-making.

3. Clinical, Educational & Practical Applications

In clinical and educational domains, fostering psychological well-being shifts the objective from psychopathology remediation to asset-building and resilience enhancement. In school psychology and teacher training (central to UP PGT PGT mandates), implementing positive psychology curricula enhances student engagement, lowers burnout rates, and improves academic persistence.

Therapeutically, approaches like Well-Being Therapy (Fava) and Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) actively target Ryff's dimensions to help clients overcome existential distress, cultivate mindfulness, and restructure maladaptive cognitions that obstruct personal growth and autonomy.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Psychology

  • Carol Ryff proposed the six-factor model of Psychological Well-Being in 1989, integrating developmental and humanistic theories.
  • Subjective Well-Being (SWB) pioneered by Ed Diener consists of cognitive life satisfaction and emotional affect (positive and negative).
  • Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan) highlights Autonomy, Competence, and Relatedness as universal psychological needs.
  • Neuroscientifically, sustained positive affect and well-being correlate with higher relative left prefrontal cortical activation.
  • Ryff's PWBS comes in various item lengths (e.g., 84, 54, 42, and 18-item versions) assessing the six distinct dimensions.
  • Eudaimonia emphasizes human flourishing, realization of potentials, and deep meaning rather than momentary hedonic pleasure.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Confusing Subjective Well-Being (SWB) with Psychological Well-Being (PWB). SWB is hedonic (happiness/pleasure/life satisfaction), whereas PWB is eudaimonic (growth/purpose/mastery).
  • Misconception 2: Assuming psychological well-being is merely the absence of clinical depression or anxiety. Mental health is a dual-continua model where high PWB can coexist with mild distress, and low PWB can exist without clinical illness.

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Test your conceptual understanding. Select an answer and check the detailed pedagogical rationale.

Q1 Who formulated the six-factor multidimensional model of Psychological Well-Being consisting of autonomy, environmental mastery, personal growth, positive relations, purpose in life, and self-acceptance?
  • A Ed Diener
  • B Carol Ryff
  • C Martin Seligman
  • D Abraham Maslow
Correct Option: B (Carol Ryff)

Carol Ryff (1989) developed the six-factor model of psychological well-being based on humanistic and developmental theories. Ed Diener is known for Subjective Well-Being; Martin Seligman for PERMA / Positive Psychology; and Abraham Maslow for the Hierarchy of Needs.

Q2 Which of the following best characterizes 'Eudaimonic' well-being?
  • A Maximizing sensory pleasure and avoiding pain
  • B Achieving high scores on positive affect and low on negative affect
  • C Realizing one's true human potential, purpose, and personal growth
  • D Experiencing material wealth and social status
Correct Option: C (Realizing one's true human potential, purpose, and personal growth)

Eudaimonic well-being focuses on human flourishing, self-actualization, and existential meaning (Ryff's model), whereas hedonic well-being focuses on pleasure maximization and affect balance.

Q3 According to Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan, 1985), what are the three basic psychological needs essential for psychological well-being?
  • A Food, Water, Shelter
  • B Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness
  • C Safety, Esteem, Self-Actualization
  • D Pleasure, Comfort, Success
Correct Option: B (Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness)

Deci and Ryan's Self-Determination Theory posits that Autonomy, Competence, and Relatedness are the innate, fundamental psychological needs required for autonomous motivation and psychological growth.

Q4 Which neurobiological asymmetry has been linked in affective neuroscience research (e.g., Richard Davidson) to greater psychological resilience and positive affect?
  • A Right prefrontal cortical activation
  • B Left prefrontal cortical activation
  • C Cerebellar hyperactivation
  • D Occipital lobe dominant EEG synchronization
Correct Option: B (Left prefrontal cortical activation)

Research by Richard Davidson and colleagues consistently demonstrates that greater relative left prefrontal activation is associated with positive affect, approach motivation, and psychological well-being.

Q5 The Satisfaction with Life Scale (SWLS) developed by Ed Diener is primarily a measure of which construct?
  • A Eudaimonic well-being
  • B Subjective well-being (Cognitive component)
  • C Psychological distress
  • D Environmental mastery
Correct Option: B (Subjective well-being (Cognitive component))

The SWLS is a widely used 5-item self-report instrument designed to measure the cognitive-judgmental aspect of Subjective Well-Being (life satisfaction).

Q6 In the context of Ryff's dimensions of PWB, 'Environmental Mastery' refers to:
  • A The ability to manipulate others for personal gain
  • B A sense of mastery and competence in managing the surrounding environment
  • C Physical fitness and ecological awareness
  • D Financial independence
Correct Option: B (A sense of mastery and competence in managing the surrounding environment)

Environmental Mastery refers to the individual's ability to choose or create environments suitable to their psychical conditions and effectively manage complex external contexts.

Q7 Which therapeutic approach specifically integrates Ryff's model of psychological well-being to promote personal growth and overcome demoralization?
  • A Classic Psychoanalysis
  • B Well-Being Therapy (Fava)
  • C Systematic Desensitization
  • D Aversion Therapy
Correct Option: B (Well-Being Therapy (Fava))

Well-Being Therapy (WBT), developed by Giovanni Fava, is a short-term psychotherapy specifically structured around tracking and enhancing the six dimensions of Ryff's psychological well-being.

Q8 What is the primary distinction between hedonic and eudaimonic approaches to happiness?
  • A Hedonic focuses on feeling good; eudaimonic focuses on functioning well and finding meaning.
  • B Hedonic focuses on animals; eudaimonic focuses exclusively on humans.
  • C Hedonic is unscientific; eudaimonic is purely biological.
  • D There is no theoretical distinction.
Correct Option: A (Hedonic focuses on feeling good; eudaimonic focuses on functioning well and finding meaning.)

Hedonic psychology studies happiness as pleasure and affect balance ('feeling good'), whereas eudaimonic psychology studies meaning, virtue, and self-realization ('functioning well').

Q9 Which of the following is NOT one of the six dimensions in Carol Ryff's Psychological Well-Being scale?
  • A Autonomy
  • B Financial Security
  • C Purpose in Life
  • D Self-Acceptance
Correct Option: B (Financial Security)

Financial security is not part of Ryff's model. The six dimensions are Autonomy, Environmental Mastery, Personal Growth, Positive Relations with Others, Purpose in Life, and Self-Acceptance.

Q10 The dual-continua model of mental health suggests that:
  • A Mental illness and mental health are two opposite poles of a single continuum.
  • B Mental illness and mental health (well-being) are distinct, correlated axes.
  • C Well-being is entirely genetically determined and unchangeable.
  • D Clinical diagnosis completely predicts an individual's psychological well-being score.
Correct Option: B (Mental illness and mental health (well-being) are distinct, correlated axes.)

The dual-continua model (Keyes) posits that mental health (flourishing/well-being) and mental illness (psychopathology) operate along two separate yet related continuums, meaning an individual can have low mental illness symptoms yet also low well-being.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between subjective well-being and psychological well-being?

Subjective Well-Being (SWB) is rooted in the hedonic tradition, focusing on high positive affect, low negative affect, and cognitive life satisfaction. Psychological Well-Being (PWB) is rooted in the eudaimonic tradition, focusing on personal growth, life purpose, autonomy, and self-actualization as operationalized by Carol Ryff.

Why is Carol Ryff's model important for UP PGT Psychology examinations?

Ryff's model is a canonical syllabus topic under Applied Psychology and Positive Psychology. Questions frequently test the exact six dimensions, historical antecedents, and distinctions between hedonic and eudaimonic frameworks.

Can psychological well-being be improved through psychological interventions?

Yes. Positive psychology interventions (PPIs), Well-Being Therapy (WBT), and mindfulness-based programs have been empirically demonstrated to significantly enhance eudaimonic well-being, increase left prefrontal activation, and build psychological resilience.

How does Self-Determination Theory relate to psychological well-being?

Self-Determination Theory (SDT) posits that psychological well-being is contingent upon satisfying three innate psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness. When these needs are met, individuals experience intrinsic motivation and optimal psychological functioning.
