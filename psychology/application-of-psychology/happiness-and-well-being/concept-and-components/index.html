Concept and Components of Well-Being

The study of well-being occupies a central position in positive psychology, shifting the clinical focus from mere pathology reduction to human flourishing.

UP PGT Psychology • Code 13 UNIT X • Application of Psychology

1. Definitional Framework and Foundational Theories

Well-being is a multifaceted construct encompassing optimal functioning, positive emotional experiences, and life fulfillment. Historically, psychology bifurcated into two major philosophical and empirical traditions regarding the nature of happiness: Hedonism and Eudaimonism.

  • Hedonic Well-Being: Grounded in the philosophy of Aristippus, hedonism equates well-being with the maximization of pleasure and the minimization of pain. In contemporary psychology, this is operationalized as Subjective Well-Being (SWB), extensively researched by Ed Diener. SWB comprises three components: frequent positive affect, infrequent negative affect, and cognitive life satisfaction.
  • Eudaimonic Well-Being: Rooted in Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics, eudaimonism posits that true happiness is derived from self-realization, personal growth, and fulfilling one's true potential (daimon). Carol Ryff operationalized this into the Six-Factor Model of Psychological Well-Being, moving beyond mere pleasure to deeper existential meaning.
  • Key Theorists: Ed Diener (Subjective Well-Being, 1984; PANAS scale), Carol Ryff (Psychological Well-Being, 1989), Martin Seligman (PERMA model, 2011), and Abraham Maslow (Self-Actualization hierarchy).

2. Neurocognitive / Empirical Mechanisms & Methodological Paradigms

Empirical investigations into well-being examine both its neurobiological correlates and cognitive appraisals. Affective neuroscience highlights the role of the prefrontal cortex (PFC)—particularly the left dorsolateral and ventromedial regions—in mediating positive affect and approach motivation, while the amygdala governs negative emotional reactivity.

  • Neurotransmitters and Hormones: Dopamine pathways (mesolimbic reward system) facilitate anticipation and pleasure, whereas serotonin and oxytocin regulate mood stability, social bonding, and stress resilience.
  • The Hedonic Treadmill (Brickman & Campbell, 1971): A psychological phenomenon suggesting humans rapidly return to a relatively stable level of happiness despite major positive or negative life events or shifts in socioeconomic status.
  • Broaden-and-Build Theory (Fredrickson, 1998, 2001): Unlike negative emotions which narrow behavioral repertoires (fight-or-flight), positive emotions (joy, interest, contentment) broaden momentary thought-action repertoires and build enduring physical, intellectual, and social resources.

3. Clinical, Educational & Practical Applications

Well-being frameworks are widely applied across clinical psychology, educational settings, and organizational behavior to foster resilience and performance.

  • Clinical Interventions: Positive Psychology Interventions (PPIs) such as gratitude journaling, the 'Three Good Things' exercise, and mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT) are utilized to buffer against depressive symptoms and clinical anxiety.
  • Educational Integration: Fostering student eudaimonic well-being through autonomy-supportive teaching styles enhances intrinsic motivation, academic engagement, and psychological resilience.
  • Organizational Psychology: Job-demands resources (JD-R) model links employee well-being directly with organizational productivity, mitigating burnout and occupational fatigue.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Psychology

  • Ed Diener is universally recognized as the pioneer of Subjective Well-Being (SWB) research, introducing the Satisfaction with Life Scale (SWLS) in 1985.
  • Carol Ryff's model contains precisely six dimensions: Autonomy, Environmental Mastery, Personal Growth, Positive Relations with Others, Purpose in Life, and Self-Acceptance.
  • Brickman and Campbell (1971) formulated the 'Hedonic Treadmill' hypothesis, explaining adaptation level theory in emotional happiness.
  • Barbara Fredrickson's 'Broaden-and-Build Theory' asserts that positive emotions expand cognitive scope and build long-term psychological resources.
  • Martin Seligman expanded positive psychology into the PERMA framework in his 2011 book 'Flourish'.
  • Left prefrontal cortical activation is consistently associated with higher resting levels of positive affect and approach motivation.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Students often confuse subjective well-being with momentary mood. Accurate principle: SWB includes both affective components (mood) and a stable cognitive evaluation component (life satisfaction).
  • Misconception 2: Assuming eudaimonic and hedonic well-being are mutually exclusive. Accurate principle: They are distinct but moderately correlated constructs that complement a holistic view of human flourishing.

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Test your conceptual understanding. Select an answer and check the detailed pedagogical rationale.

Q1 Who among the following is credited with pioneering the concept of Subjective Well-Being (SWB) and developing the Satisfaction with Life Scale (SWLS)?
  • A Martin Seligman
  • B Carol Ryff
  • C Ed Diener
  • D Abraham Maslow
Correct Option: C (Ed Diener)

Ed Diener is the foundational researcher of Subjective Well-Being (SWB), comprising life satisfaction and affect balance, and developed the SWLS in 1985. Seligman developed PERMA, Ryff developed Psychological Well-Being, and Maslow focused on the hierarchy of needs.

Q2 Which of the following is NOT one of the six dimensions in Carol Ryff's model of Psychological Well-Being?
  • A Environmental Mastery
  • B Personal Growth
  • C Hedonic Balance
  • D Purpose in Life
Correct Option: C (Hedonic Balance)

Hedonic Balance belongs to Ed Diener's Subjective Well-Being model, not Carol Ryff's eudaimonic model. Ryff's six dimensions are Autonomy, Environmental Mastery, Personal Growth, Positive Relations, Purpose in Life, and Self-Acceptance.

Q3 The 'Hedonic Treadmill' theory, suggesting that people quickly return to a baseline level of happiness after major positive or negative events, was proposed by:
  • A Brickman and Campbell
  • B Fredrickson and Losada
  • C Diener and Emmons
  • D Seligman and Csikszentmihalyi
Correct Option: A (Brickman and Campbell)

Philip Brickman and Donald Campbell introduced the term 'hedonic treadmill' in 1971 to describe adaptation level theory applied to happiness.

Q4 Barbara Fredrickson's Broaden-and-Build Theory primarily posits that positive emotions:
  • A Narrow attentional focus to facilitate immediate survival
  • B Broaden momentary thought-action repertoires and build personal resources
  • C Eliminate all forms of negative psychological distress instantly
  • D Reduce metabolic activity in the prefrontal cortex
Correct Option: B (Broaden momentary thought-action repertoires and build personal resources)

Fredrickson's theory states that whereas negative emotions narrow attention (fight-or-flight), positive emotions broaden cognitive scope and build long-lasting intellectual, social, and physical resources.

Q5 In Martin Seligman's PERMA model of well-being, what does the letter 'E' stand for?
  • A Empathy
  • B Engagement
  • C Energy
  • D Environment
Correct Option: B (Engagement)

In the PERMA model, 'E' stands for Engagement (flow states), while 'P' stands for Positive Emotion, 'R' for Relationships, 'M' for Meaning, and 'A' for Accomplishment.

Q6 Eudaimonic well-being is philosophically rooted in the traditions of:
  • A Aristippus and hedonism
  • B Aristotle and virtue ethics
  • C Sigmund Freud and psychoanalysis
  • D John B. Watson and behaviorism
Correct Option: B (Aristotle and virtue ethics)

Eudaimonia translates to human flourishing and self-realization, tracing back to Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics.

Q7 Which brain region is consistently implicated in affective neuroscience research regarding resting positive affect and approach motivation?
  • A Right temporal lobe
  • B Left prefrontal cortex
  • C Occipital lobe
  • D Cerebellar vermis
Correct Option: B (Left prefrontal cortex)

EEG and neuroimaging studies show greater relative left prefrontal cortical activation is associated with positive affect and approach-oriented emotional states.

Q8 Which of the following psychometric instruments is specifically designed to measure Subjective Well-Being by evaluating cognitive life satisfaction?
  • A PANAS (Positive and Negative Affect Schedule)
  • B SWLS (Satisfaction with Life Scale)
  • C MMPI-2
  • D TAT (Thematic Apperception Test)
Correct Option: B (SWLS (Satisfaction with Life Scale))

The Satisfaction with Life Scale (SWLS), developed by Diener et al. (1985), assesses global cognitive judgments of one's life satisfaction.

Q9 According to positive psychology, flow states—characterized by complete absorption in an activity—are most closely aligned with which component of the PERMA model?
  • A Positive Emotion
  • B Engagement
  • C Meaning
  • D Accomplishment
Correct Option: B (Engagement)

Engagement in the PERMA model refers to flow experiences where individuals become entirely absorbed and lose self-consciousness during challenging tasks.

Q10 How does Subjective Well-Being (SWB) differ fundamentally from Psychological Well-Being (PWB)?
  • A SWB focuses on emotional balance and life satisfaction, whereas PWB focuses on human potential and existential growth.
  • B SWB is a clinical diagnostic tool, whereas PWB is purely biological.
  • C SWB ignores emotional states entirely.
  • D There is no theoretical difference between the two.
Correct Option: A (SWB focuses on emotional balance and life satisfaction, whereas PWB focuses on human potential and existential growth.)

SWB is hedonic (evaluating pleasure, positive/negative affect, and life satisfaction), whereas PWB is eudaimonic (evaluating self-actualization, personal growth, and existential purpose).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the primary difference between hedonic and eudaimonic well-being?

Hedonic well-being focuses on subjective happiness, pleasure maximization, and emotional balance (Subjective Well-Being). Eudaimonic well-being focuses on human potential, meaning, self-realization, and personal growth (Psychological Well-Being).

Who developed the Six-Factor Model of Psychological Well-Being?

Carol Ryff developed the model in 1989, encompassing autonomy, environmental mastery, personal growth, positive relations with others, purpose in life, and self-acceptance.

What does the Hedonic Treadmill imply for long-term happiness?

The Hedonic Treadmill theory suggests that humans quickly adapt to positive or negative life changes (such as winning the lottery or experiencing trauma), returning to a genetically and temperamentally determined baseline level of happiness.

How is Subjective Well-Being measured in psychological research?

It is typically measured using self-report questionnaires such as Ed Diener's Satisfaction with Life Scale (SWLS) for the cognitive component, and the Positive and Negative Affect Schedule (PANAS) for the emotional/affective component.
