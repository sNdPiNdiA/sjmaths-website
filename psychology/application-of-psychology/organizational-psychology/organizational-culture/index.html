Organizational Culture

Organizational culture encompasses the shared values, beliefs, assumptions, and norms that govern how people behave in organizations.

UP PGT Psychology • Code 13 UNIT X • Application of Psychology

1. Definitional Framework and Foundational Theories

Organizational Culture refers to the system of shared meaning held by members that distinguishes the organization from other organizations (Robbins, Judge, & Sanghi). It represents the unwritten rules, psychological climate, historical traditions, and collective mindsets of a workplace.

  • Edgar Schein’s Three-Level Model (1985, 2010): Schein is the preeminent theorist on organizational culture. He conceptualized culture as having three distinct levels: Artifacts (visible structures, processes, and observable behavior), Espoused Beliefs and Values (strategies, goals, and philosophies explicitly stated by management), and Basic Underlying Assumptions (unconscious, taken-for-granted beliefs, perceptions, and thoughts that are ultimate sources of values and action).
  • Competing Values Framework (CVF) by Quinn and Cameron (1983, 2011): This model categorizes organizational cultures along two main axes: flexibility/discretion vs. stability/control, and internal focus/integration vs. external focus/differentiation. This yields four culture types: Clan, Adhocracy, Market, and Hierarchy.
  • Harrison and Handy’s Typology of Culture (1972/1993): Categorizes cultures into Power Culture (web-like, centralized control), Role Culture (bureaucratic, structured by function), Task Culture (matrix-oriented, project-focused), and Person Culture (individualistic, serving individual needs).

2. Neurocognitive / Empirical Mechanisms & Methodological Paradigms

Understanding organizational culture requires examining how cognitive schemata, social identity theory, and neuroplastic adaptation operate within collective environments.

  • Social Identity Theory (Tajfel & Turner, 1979): Employees internalize organizational culture into their social identity, leading to in-group favoritism, organizational citizenship behavior (OCB), and reduced turnover intentions when value congruence (person-organization fit) is high.
  • Sensemaking Theory (Weick, 1995): Culture acts as a cognitive lens through which employees interpret ambiguous organizational events, crises, and strategic changes.
  • Assessment Methodologies: Culture is assessed via both qualitative approaches (ethnographic observation, deep interviews, content analysis of corporate artifacts) and quantitative surveys (e.g., Organizational Culture Assessment Instrument [OCAI] based on CVF, Organizational Culture Profile [OCP] by O'Reilly, Chatman, & Caldwell).

3. Clinical, Educational & Practical Applications

In applied psychological consulting, diagnosing and changing organizational culture is crucial for organizational development (OD) and mental health.

  • Mergers and Acquisitions (M&A): Cultural friction is the leading cause of M&A failure. Industrial psychologists perform cultural audits to bridge value gaps between merging entities.
  • Psychological Safety (Edmondson, 1999): A culture that fosters psychological safety encourages interpersonal risk-taking, whistleblowing, innovation, and error-reporting without fear of punitive repercussions.
  • Change Management: Kurt Lewin’s 3-stage model (Unfreezing, Changing, Refreezing) heavily relies on shifting underlying cultural assumptions to embed permanent behavioral modifications.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Psychology

  • Edgar Schein proposed the seminal Three-Level Model of Organizational Culture: Artifacts, Espoused Values, and Basic Assumptions.
  • The Organizational Culture Assessment Instrument (OCAI) developed by Kim Cameron and Robert Quinn operationalizes the Competing Values Framework (CVF).
  • Harrison and Handy classified organizational cultures into four types: Power, Role, Task, and Person.
  • Person-Organization (P-O) fit measures the congruence between an individual's personal values and the prevailing organizational culture.
  • Amy Edmondson defined 'Psychological Safety' as a shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking.
  • Kurt Lewin's Change Model involves three phases: Unfreezing, Changing (Moving), and Refreezing.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Students often confuse 'Climate' and 'Culture'. Organizational Climate refers to temporary, surface-level perceptions of work environment policies and procedures, whereas Culture refers to deep-seated, enduring values, assumptions, and historical meanings.
  • Misconception 2: Assuming espoused values always reflect actual behavioral practice. In reality, espoused values often diverge significantly from enacted values due to systemic pressures.

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Test your conceptual understanding. Select an answer and check the detailed pedagogical rationale.

Q1 According to Edgar Schein's model of organizational culture, which of the following represents the deepest and least conscious level of culture?
  • A Artifacts
  • B Espoused Values
  • C Basic Underlying Assumptions
  • D Observed Behavioral Norms
Correct Option: C (Basic Underlying Assumptions)

Edgar Schein identified Basic Underlying Assumptions as the deepest level of culture. These are unconscious, taken-for-granted beliefs and perceptions that determine behavior, thought, and feeling.

Q2 Which framework developed by Kim Cameron and Robert Quinn evaluates organizational culture along the dimensions of flexibility vs. stability and internal vs. external focus?
  • A Competing Values Framework (CVF)
  • B Grid-Group Cultural Theory
  • C Big Five Personality Taxonomy
  • D Path-Goal Leadership Model
Correct Option: A (Competing Values Framework (CVF))

The Competing Values Framework (CVF) uses flexibility versus stability and internal versus external focus to classify cultures into Clan, Adhocracy, Market, and Hierarchy.

Q3 Who introduced the four-fold culture typology consisting of Power, Role, Task, and Person cultures?
  • A Edgar Schein
  • B Charles Handy (adapted from Roger Harrison)
  • C Fred Fiedler
  • D Douglas McGregor
Correct Option: B (Charles Handy (adapted from Roger Harrison))

Charles Handy popularized the typology originally proposed by Roger Harrison, dividing organizational cultures into Power, Role, Task, and Person types.

Q4 What does 'Person-Organization (P-O) Fit' primarily measure in industrial psychology?
  • A The physical ergonomic fit of an employee's desk
  • B The match between an individual's skills and job requirements
  • C The compatibility between an individual's values and the organization's culture
  • D The financial compensation equity across job grades
Correct Option: C (The compatibility between an individual's values and the organization's culture)

P-O fit assesses how well an individual's personal values, goals, and personality align with the overarching culture and values of the organization.

Q5 In the Competing Values Framework, an organization focused heavily on stability, control, efficiency, and formal rules exhibits which culture type?
  • A Clan
  • B Adhocracy
  • C Market
  • D Hierarchy
Correct Option: D (Hierarchy)

A Hierarchy culture emphasizes stability, control, formal rules, clear lines of authority, and structural efficiency.

Q6 What term did Amy Edmondson coin to describe a shared belief that a workplace is safe for interpersonal risk-taking and speaking up?
  • A Psychological Safety
  • B Emotional Labor
  • C Cognitive Dissonance
  • D Transformational Leadership
Correct Option: A (Psychological Safety)

Psychological safety, popularized by Amy Edmondson, represents a climate where employees feel secure taking risks, asking questions, and admitting mistakes.

Q7 Which assessment instrument is specifically designed to measure organizational culture using the Competing Values Framework?
  • A Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory (MMPI)
  • B Organizational Culture Assessment Instrument (OCAI)
  • C Thematic Apperception Test (TAT)
  • D Job Descriptive Index (JDI)
Correct Option: B (Organizational Culture Assessment Instrument (OCAI))

The OCAI (Organizational Culture Assessment Instrument) is a validated psychometric ipsative tool created by Cameron and Quinn to profile organizational culture based on CVF.

Q8 According to Kurt Lewin's classic change model, which phase involves disrupting existing cognitive and cultural equilibria to prepare employees for new ways of working?
  • A Refreezing
  • B Transitioning
  • C Unfreezing
  • D Benchmarking
Correct Option: C (Unfreezing)

Unfreezing involves dismantling old habits, assumptions, and cultural practices to create motivation for change.

Q9 Which type of organizational culture, according to Harrison and Handy, is characterized by a central power source (web-like structure) with power radiating outward from a dominant leader?
  • A Role Culture
  • B Power Culture
  • C Task Culture
  • D Person Culture
Correct Option: B (Power Culture)

Power culture resembles a web with a spider at the center, where control is centralized and decisions are made swiftly by key leaders.

Q10 How do artifacts function within Edgar Schein's three levels of organizational culture?
  • A As unconscious hidden assumptions
  • B As deeply held ethical creeds
  • C As visible, tangible manifestations of culture such as dress code, layout, and rituals
  • D As statistical measures of staff turnover
Correct Option: C (As visible, tangible manifestations of culture such as dress code, layout, and rituals)

Artifacts are the most visible and accessible level of culture, including architecture, dress codes, annual reports, awards, myths, and observable rituals.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between organizational culture and organizational climate?

Organizational culture refers to deep-seated, enduring values, assumptions, and historical traditions. Organizational climate represents employees' immediate, surface-level perceptions of the work environment, policies, and management style at a given point in time.

How is organizational culture measured in psychological research?

Culture is measured through qualitative methods (ethnography, interviews, artifact analysis) and quantitative surveys, most notably the Organizational Culture Assessment Instrument (OCAI) and the Organizational Culture Profile (OCP).

Can organizational culture be intentionally changed?

Yes, but it requires sustained leadership commitment, aligning reward systems with new values, altering physical artifacts, and applying structured change models like Kurt Lewin's unfreezing-change-refreezing process.

Why is Edgar Schein's model important for UP PGT and UGC NET exams?

Schein's model is foundational because it provides the definitive tripartite structure of culture (Artifacts, Espoused Values, Basic Assumptions), which is frequently tested in multiple-choice and conceptual questions.
