Process of Organizational Communication

The process of organizational communication encompasses the systematic flow of information, ideas, and attitudes within a structured network, serving as the neural network of any corporate or institutional body.

UP PGT Psychology • Code 13 UNIT X • Application of Psychology

1. Definitional Framework and Foundational Theories

Organizational communication is defined as the sending and receiving of messages among interrelated individuals within a specified environment or setting to achieve individual and common organizational goals. Pioneers like Claude Shannon and Warren Weaver (1949) initially established the mathematical model of communication, which was later expanded by David Berlo (1960) into the SMCR Model (Source, Message, Channel, Receiver), emphasizing psychological filters such as attitudes, knowledge, and socio-cultural systems.

  • Classical Management Perspective: Fayol’s Administrative Theory emphasizes formal, hierarchical communication channels (gangplank / passerelle principle for horizontal communication).
  • Human Relations Movement: Hawthorne Studies led by Elton Mayo (1930s) highlighted the critical role of the informal communication network, commonly known as the 'grapevine', in determining employee morale and productivity.
  • Systems Theory Approach: Katz and Kahn (1966) conceptualized organizations as open social systems that depend on dynamic information exchange with their external and internal environments to maintain negative entropy.

2. Channels, Directionality, and Barriers in Organizational Communication

Communication flow within organizations operates across multiple vectors: downward (directives, performance feedback), upward (grievances, status reports), horizontal (peer coordination), and diagonal (cross-functional problem solving). However, information distortion frequently occurs due to several psychological and structural barriers.

  • Psychological Barriers: Perceptual defense, status differentials, cognitive overload, emotional states, and selective attention.
  • Semantic Barriers: Jargon, ambiguous terminology, and differential decoding of symbols and corporate vernacular.
  • Network Structures: Research by Alex Bavelas and Harold Leavitt (1951) identified structural communication networks—Wheel, Chain, Y, Circle, and All-Channel (Comcon)—demonstrating that centralized networks (Wheel) are faster for simple tasks, whereas decentralized networks (All-Channel) yield higher satisfaction and accuracy for complex, creative tasks.

3. Clinical, Educational & Practical Applications

In applied organizational psychology, understanding the communication process facilitates structured interventions such as sensitivity training (T-groups), executive coaching, and organizational development (OD) audits using communication network analysis (CNA). Industrial psychologists utilize these frameworks to mitigate burnout, reduce turnover intentions through transparent upward feedback channels, and implement effective crisis management protocols.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Psychology

  • Shannon and Weaver (1949) introduced the concept of 'noise' in communication channels, representing any disturbance that degrades message fidelity.
  • Harold Leavitt (1951) proved that centralized networks (like the Wheel) are optimal for simple tasks, while decentralized networks (All-Channel) excel in complex tasks.
  • The 'Grapevine' operates primarily through four distinct patterns: Single Strand, Gossip, Probability, and Cluster (Keith Davis).
  • Downward communication is frequently distorted by serial transmission errors, famously demonstrated in Bartlett’s schema and rumor transmission studies.
  • Katz and Kahn (1966) viewed organizations as open thermodynamic systems requiring continuous informational inputs.
  • Downward communication failure is the primary contributor to role ambiguity and job dissatisfaction in industrial settings.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Students often believe the 'grapevine' is entirely detrimental and non-functional. Scientifically, organizational psychologists note that the grapevine satisfies social needs, provides quick feedback, and often acts as a safety valve during crises.
  • Misconception 2: Confusing 'horizontal' communication with 'diagonal' communication. Horizontal occurs between peers at the same hierarchical level, whereas diagonal occurs across different levels and departments without direct reporting relationships.

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Test your conceptual understanding. Select an answer and check the detailed pedagogical rationale.

Q1 Which classical communication network structure was found by Harold Leavitt (1951) to produce the highest member satisfaction and accuracy on complex problem-solving tasks?
  • A The Wheel Network
  • B The Chain Network
  • C The All-Channel (Comcon) Network
  • D The Y Network
Correct Option: C (The All-Channel (Comcon) Network)

Leavitt's experimental research demonstrated that decentralized networks, specifically the All-Channel network, allow all members to communicate freely, resulting in higher satisfaction and better performance on complex tasks compared to centralized structures like the Wheel.

Q2 In the context of organizational communication barriers, 'status differential' refers to:
  • A Physical distance between offices
  • B Discrepancies in technological access
  • C Psychological hesitation or filtering of messages due to hierarchical power gaps
  • D Differences in semantic understanding of corporate terminology
Correct Option: C (Psychological hesitation or filtering of messages due to hierarchical power gaps)

Status differential is a psychological barrier where subordinates filter upward communication to please superiors or hesitate to report errors due to fear of negative evaluation or reprisal.

Q3 Who formulated the foundational mathematical model of communication featuring a sender, encoder, channel, decoder, and receiver with the inclusion of 'noise'?
  • A David Berlo
  • B Claude Shannon and Warren Weaver
  • C Katz and Kahn
  • D Keith Davis
Correct Option: B (Claude Shannon and Warren Weaver)

Shannon and Weaver (1949) developed the mathematical model of communication, introducing key concepts like signal, transmitter, noise, and receiver.

Q4 According to Keith Davis, which grapevine pattern involves an individual randomly selecting acquaintances to pass along information?
  • A Single Strand Chain
  • B Probability Chain
  • C Gossip Chain
  • D Cluster Chain
Correct Option: B (Probability Chain)

In the probability chain, individuals communicate randomly with others according to the laws of probability, as classified by Keith Davis in his study of informal organizational communication.

Q5 Communication that flows from subordinates to managers, such as grievance reports and performance feedback, is known as:
  • A Downward communication
  • B Horizontal communication
  • C Upward communication
  • D Diagonal communication
Correct Option: C (Upward communication)

Upward communication travels from lower levels of an organizational hierarchy to upper levels, serving to provide feedback, report problems, and express grievances.

Q6 The 'gangplank' (passerelle) principle introduced by Henri Fayol primarily facilitates which type of organizational communication?
  • A Downward communication
  • B Horizontal / Lateral communication
  • C Upward communication
  • D External communication
Correct Option: B (Horizontal / Lateral communication)

Fayol introduced the gangplank principle to allow employees at the same hierarchical level in different departments to communicate directly, bypassing rigid scalar chains when necessary for efficiency.

Q7 Which of the following is NOT considered a semantic barrier to effective organizational communication?
  • A Technical jargon
  • B Use of ambiguous acronyms
  • C Status anxiety and fear of reprimand
  • D Differing interpretations of company policy wording
Correct Option: C (Status anxiety and fear of reprimand)

Status anxiety and fear of reprimand are psychological or emotional barriers, not semantic ones. Semantic barriers deal specifically with language, symbols, and meaning.

Q8 In Berlo’s SMCR model of communication, what do the letters stand for?
  • A Source, Message, Channel, Receiver
  • B Sender, Medium, Cognition, Response
  • C System, Meaning, Code, Report
  • D Stimulus, Memory, Control, Reaction
Correct Option: A (Source, Message, Channel, Receiver)

Berlo’s 1960 SMCR model stands for Source, Message, Channel, and Receiver, emphasizing psychological factors influencing each component.

Q9 An open systems perspective of organizations, emphasizing dynamic information exchange with the environment, was prominently advanced by:
  • A Frederick Taylor
  • B Elton Mayo
  • C Katz and Kahn
  • D Max Weber
Correct Option: C (Katz and Kahn)

Daniel Katz and Robert Kahn (1966) applied open-systems theory to organizations, conceptualizing them as social systems dependent on energy and information exchanges with their environment.

Q10 What is the primary psychological consequence of excessive serial transmission in downward communication?
  • A Enhanced message accuracy
  • B Information leveling, sharpening, and assimilation
  • C Elimination of noise
  • D Immediate feedback loops
Correct Option: B (Information leveling, sharpening, and assimilation)

Serial transmission (passing messages through multiple people) results in errors similar to Bartlett's memory studies: leveling (dropping details), sharpening (exaggerating specific parts), and assimilation (distorting details to fit personal expectations).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between formal and informal communication networks in organizations?

Formal communication networks follow the official organizational chart and hierarchical channels to convey task-related directives, policies, and reports. Informal networks (the grapevine) emerge organically based on social relationships, bypassing official channels to satisfy social needs and disseminate rapid, though sometimes distorted, information.

How does 'noise' affect the communication process in industrial settings?

In organizational psychology, 'noise' is not merely acoustic sound; it represents any psychological, semantic, physiological, or environmental interference that distorts, obscures, or alters the intended message during transmission between sender and receiver.

Why is upward communication often blocked in hierarchical organizations?

Upward communication is frequently hindered by psychological barriers such as fear of reprisal, status differentials, hierarchical distance, and the 'Mum effect'—the tendency of subordinates to withhold negative feedback or bad news to avoid displeasing management.

What did Harold Leavitt's experiments demonstrate about communication network structures?

Leavitt demonstrated that centralized networks (such as the Wheel) process simple tasks faster and with fewer errors because a central leader coordinates all information. However, decentralized networks (such as the All-Channel) yield higher morale, satisfaction, and accuracy when tackling complex, ambiguous, or creative problem-solving tasks.
