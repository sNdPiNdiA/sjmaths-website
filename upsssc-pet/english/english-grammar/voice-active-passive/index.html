Core Rules & Detailed Explanations

Rule 1: The Universal SVO Transformation Formula & Mnemonic

• Active Structure: Subject (S) + Auxiliary Verb + Main Verb + Object (O)

Passive Structure: Object (New S) + Auxiliary Verb (Be-form) + V3 (Past Participle) + Preposition (by/to/with/at) + Subject (New O)
Step 1: Identify S, V, and O in the Active sentence.
Step 2: Swap positions of S and O.
Step 3: Change the main verb into V3.
Step 4: Add appropriate Be-verb according to the tense.
Example 1: Active: She writes a letter. | Passive: A letter is written by her.
Example 2: Active: Raman built this house. | Passive: This house was built by Raman.
Example 3: Active: They are playing football. | Passive: Football is being played by them.
Shortcut Mnemonic: SVO → O-Be-V3-By-S (Object + Be Verb + V3 + By + Subject).

Rule 2: Tense-Wise Auxiliary Verb Conversion Matrix

• Simple Present (V1/V5): Active: `do/does + V1` → Passive: `is/am/are + V3`

Present Continuous (is/am/are + V4): Active: `is/am/are + V4` → Passive: `is/am/are + being + V3`
Present Perfect (has/have + V3): Active: `has/have + V3` → Passive: `has/have + been + V3`
Simple Past (V2): Active: `V2 / did + V1` → Passive: `was/were + V3`
Past Continuous (was/were + V4): Active: `was/were + V4` → Passive: `was/were + being + V3`
Past Perfect (had + V3): Active: `had + V3` → Passive: `had + been + V3`
Simple Future (will/shall + V1): Active: `will/shall + V1` → Passive: `will/shall + be + V3`
Future Perfect (will/shall + have + V3): Active: `will/shall + have + V3` → Passive: `will/shall + have + been + V3`
Example 1: Active: He has finished the task. | Passive: The task has been finished by him.
Example 2: Active: They were watching a movie. | Passive: A movie was being watched by them.

Rule 3: Imperative Sentences (Commands, Requests, Advice & Orders)

• Command / Order: Active: `V1 + Object` → Passive: `Let + Object + be + V3` OR `You are ordered to + V1...`

Request: Active: `Please / Kindly + V1 + Object` → Passive: `You are requested to + V1 + Object`
Advice / Suggestion: Active: `V1 + Object` → Passive: `Object + should + be + V3` OR `You are advised to + V1...`
Moral Duty / Obligation: Active: `Help the poor.` → Passive: `The poor should be helped.`
Example 1 (Command): Active: Shut the door. | Passive: Let the door be shut.
Example 2 (Request): Active: Please give me a pen. | Passive: You are requested to give me a pen.
Example 3 (Advice): Active: Obey your parents. | Passive: Your parents should be obeyed.
Tip: Remove 'Please' or 'Kindly' when adding 'You are requested to'.

Rule 4: Interrogative Sentences Transformation Rules

• Auxiliary Verb Questions (Yes/No): Keep the auxiliary verb at the beginning matching the new subject.

Formula: Active: `Do/Does/Did + Sub + V1 + Obj?` → Passive: `Is/Am/Are/Was/Were + Obj + V3 + by + Sub?`
'Who' Rule: Active: `Who + Verb + Object?` → Passive: `By whom + Helping Verb + Object + V3?`
'Whom' Rule: Active: `Whom + Helping Verb + Subject + V1?` → Passive: `Who + Helping Verb + V3 + by + Subject?`
'Which/What' Rule: Active: `Which book do you like?` → Passive: `Which book is liked by you?`
Example 1: Active: Did you clean the room? | Passive: Was the room cleaned by you?
Example 2: Active: Who broke this glass? | Passive: By whom was this glass broken?
Example 3: Active: Whom did the manager call? | Passive: Who was called by the manager?

Rule 5: Modal Auxiliaries, Infinitives & Quasi-Passive Verbs

• Modal Auxiliaries (Can/Could/May/Might/Must/Should/Would): Active: `Modal + V1` → Passive: `Modal + be + V3`

Modal Perfect: Active: `Modal + have + V3` → Passive: `Modal + have + been + V3`
Infinitive Structures: Active: `To + V1` → Passive: `To + be + V3`
Quasi-Passive Verbs (Sensory Verbs): Active: `Subject + Verb + Adjective` → Passive: `Subject + Verb + Adjective + when + it/they + is/are + V3`
Example 1 (Modal): Active: You must take this medicine. | Passive: This medicine must be taken by you.
Example 2 (Infinitive): Active: I have to complete this project. | Passive: This project has to be completed by me.
Example 3 (Quasi-Passive): Active: Quinine tastes bitter. | Passive: Quinine is bitter when it is tasted.

Rule 6: Fixed Prepositions (Omission of 'By')

• Certain verbs do NOT take the preposition 'by' in Passive Voice; they take fixed prepositions like 'to', 'at', 'with', 'in', 'of'.

Known / Obliged: Active: `I know him.` → Passive: He is known to me. (NOT by me)
Surprised / Shocked / Astonished: Take 'at' for actions/news, 'with' for persons.
Satisfied / Pleased / Displeased / Disgusted: Take 'with'.
Interested / Contained / Embodied: Take 'in'.
Example 1: Active: His behavior surprised me. | Passive: I was surprised at his behavior.
Example 2: Active: This bottle contains milk. | Passive: Milk is contained in this bottle.

Tricks & Shortcuts to Remember

  • Trick 1: The 'V3 Fixed Rule':

    Regardless of tense, sentence type, or length, the main verb in EVERY Passive Voice sentence must be in Past Participle (V3) form. If an option does not contain V3, eliminate it immediately! Example: A letter is written (V3) by him.

  • Trick 2: The 'Continuous = Being' Rule:

    In all continuous tenses (Present Continuous, Past Continuous), look for the keyword 'being' followed by V3. Formula: is/am/are/was/were + BEING + V3. Example: Active: She is singing. → Passive: A song is being sung.

  • Trick 3: The 'Perfect = Been' Rule:

    In all perfect tenses (Present Perfect, Past Perfect, Future Perfect), look for the keyword 'been' followed by V3. Formula: has/have/had/will have + BEEN + V3. Example: Active: He had done it. → Passive: It had been done by him.

  • Trick 4: The 4-Tense Elimination Rule:

    Instantly mark 'No Passive Voice' or eliminate choices that attempt to convert these 4 tenses: Present Perfect Continuous, Past Perfect Continuous, Future Continuous, and Future Perfect Continuous.

  • Trick 5: The 'Who → By Whom' Swap:

    If active question starts with 'Who', passive question MUST start with 'By whom'. If active starts with 'Whom', passive starts with 'Who'. Example: Who wrote Hamlet? → By whom was Hamlet written?

  • Trick 6: Imperative Shortcut (Please / Kindly):

    Whenever you see 'Please' or 'Kindly', immediately replace it with 'You are requested to' and drop the word 'Please'. Example: Please help me → You are requested to help me.

  • Trick 7: Intransitive Verb Elimination:

    Verbs without an object (e.g., go, come, sleep, die, laugh, run, sit) CANNOT be converted into passive voice directly. Example: He sleeps. → No Passive Voice possible.

  • Trick 8: Retaining Phrasal Prepositions:

    Never drop the preposition attached to a phrasal verb during transformation! Example: Active: She looked after the baby. → Passive: The baby was looked after by her. (Dropping 'after' is a common exam trap).

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mistake 1: Changing Tense during Voice Conversion:

    Candidates often confuse Voice with Direct-Indirect Speech and change Simple Present into Simple Past. Rule: Tense NEVER changes in Voice! Incorrect: Present Simple → Past Simple. Correct: Present Simple → Present Simple (is/am/are + V3).

  • Mistake 2: Blindly Using 'By' for All Verbs:

    Using 'by' after verbs like 'know', 'surprise', 'satisfy', or 'contain'. Incorrect: He is known by me. Correct: He is known to me.

  • Mistake 3: Subject-Verb Agreement Errors in Passive:

    Failing to match the auxiliary verb with the NEW Subject (Passive Subject). Incorrect: He gave me some books. → Some books was given to me. Correct: Some books were given to me.

  • Mistake 4: Retaining Active Word Order in Questions:

    In interrogatives, putting the subject before the helping verb in passive. Incorrect: Was this book written by whom? Correct: By whom was this book written?

  • Mistake 5: Retaining 'Please' in Request Conversions:

    Keeping the word 'Please' even after adding 'You are requested to'. Incorrect: You are requested to please open the door. Correct: You are requested to open the door.

  • Mistake 6: Confusing 'Being' and 'Been':

    Using 'been' in continuous tenses or 'being' in perfect tenses. Continuous requires BEING (`is being V3`). Perfect requires BEEN (`has been V3`).

  • Mistake 7: Omitting Prepositions in Phrasal Verbs:

    Forgetting the prepositions associated with verbs like laugh at, look after, break into. Incorrect: The beggar was laughed by them. Correct: The beggar was laughed at by them.

Point-wise Detailed Summary

  • 1. Voice Concept Summary:

    Active Voice emphasizes the Doer/Subject. Passive Voice emphasizes the Action/Object.

  • 2. Main Verb Rule:

    The main verb in passive voice is ALWAYS in V3 (Past Participle) form.

  • 3. Transitive Verb Requirement:

    Only sentences containing Transitive Verbs (सकर्मक क्रिया) can be converted into passive.

  • 4. Non-Convertible Tenses:

    4 Tenses cannot be transformed: Present Perfect Continuous, Past Perfect Continuous, Future Continuous, Future Perfect Continuous.

  • 5. Pronoun Changes:

    Subject Pronouns change to Object Pronouns: I→me, We→us, He→him, She→her, They→them, Who→whom.

  • 6. Continuous Tense Indicator:

    All continuous tenses add 'being' before V3 in passive voice.

  • 7. Perfect Tense Indicator:

    All perfect tenses add 'been' before V3 in passive voice.

  • 8. Simple Future Passive Formula:

    Active: `will/shall + V1` → Passive: `will/shall + be + V3`.

  • 9. Imperative Command Formula:

    Active: `V1 + Obj` → Passive: `Let + Obj + be + V3`.

  • 10. Imperative Request Formula:

    Active: `Please + V1 + Obj` → Passive: `You are requested to + V1 + Obj`.

  • 11. 'Who' Question Formula:

    Active: `Who + Verb + Obj?` → Passive: `By whom + Helping Verb + Obj + V3?`.

  • 12. Special Preposition 'Known To':

    Verb 'Know' takes 'to' instead of 'by' in passive voice (`Known to`).

  • 13. Special Preposition 'Surprised At':

    Verb 'Surprise' takes 'at' for actions/news (`Surprised at`).

  • 14. Special Preposition 'Pleased With':

    Verb 'Please' takes 'with' for persons/conduct (`Pleased with`).

  • 15. Final Exam Elimination Strategy:

    Check Verb V3 → Check Auxiliary Verb according to Tense → Check New Subject-Verb Agreement → Eliminate wrong options.

UPSC Notes & InsightsUPSC नोट्स और अंतर्दृष्टि

UPSSSC PET Exam Strategy: Use option elimination method! First check if the main verb in options is V3. Next check if the tense is unchanged. This eliminates 80% of wrong choices in under 10 seconds.

UPSSSC PET Frequently Tests: 'Known by me' vs 'Known to me'. Always remember that 'know' and 'oblige' take 'to' in passive voice, not 'by'.

Imperative Trap: Options often include 'Please' along with 'You are requested to'. Ensure 'Please' or 'Kindly' is completely removed when using 'requested to'.

Double Object Trick: If a sentence has both direct and indirect objects, prioritizing the indirect object (person) as the new subject usually forms the most natural and preferred answer in PET MCQs.

Phrasal Verb Trap: Options frequently omit prepositions from phrasal verbs (e.g., changing 'looked after by' to 'looked by'). Never drop the verb's original preposition!

Key Takeawaysमुख्य बातें

  • Passive voice ALWAYS uses the V3 (Past Participle) form of the main verb.
  • Tense NEVER changes during Voice transformation in English grammar.
  • Only Transitive Verbs (verbs with an object) can be converted to Passive Voice.
  • Continuous tenses take 'being' (is/am/are/was/were + being + V3).
  • Perfect tenses take 'been' (has/have/had + been + V3).
  • Four tenses cannot be converted into Passive Voice (3 Perfect Continuous tenses + Future Continuous).
  • Certain verbs take fixed prepositions instead of 'by' (e.g., known to, surprised at, pleased with, contained in).
