14.1

What waves carry

A wave carries a disturbance, energy and information through space or a medium.

  • A wave is a disturbance that propagates and transports energy without transporting matter as a whole. Medium particles oscillate about equilibrium; they do not travel with the wave.
  • Mechanical waves need an elastic material medium. Electromagnetic waves need no medium and travel in vacuum at c=2.99792458×10⁸ m s⁻¹. Matter waves are associated with particles. This chapter treats mechanical waves.
  • Coupled oscillators pass a disturbance to neighbours. In sound, a vibrating source creates compressions and rarefactions that move through the medium.
  • Wave types: transverse (particle displacement ⟂ propagation) and longitudinal (particle displacement ∥ propagation). Solids support both; fluids support longitudinal bulk waves. Surface water waves combine vertical and horizontal particle motion.
  • Short-wavelength capillary waves are restored mainly by surface tension; longer gravity waves by gravity.
NCERT Example 14.1

Classifying wave motion

Question. For each, state whether the wave motion is transverse, longitudinal or a combination: (a) a kink in a longitudinal spring when one end is displaced sideways; (b) waves in a liquid cylinder when its piston moves back and forth; (c) waves produced by a motorboat; (d) ultrasonic waves in air from a vibrating quartz crystal.

  1. Step 1. (a) The sideways kink and along-spring compression give transverse and longitudinal components
  2. Step 2. (b) Piston motion creates longitudinal compressions
  3. Step 3. (c) Water-surface motion combines transverse and longitudinal components
  4. Step 4. (d) Sound in air is longitudinal.
14.2

Progressive wave and its equation

A progressive wave shifts the same disturbance along the medium while each particle oscillates.

  • A sinusoidal wave travelling in +x is y(x,t)=a sin(kx−ωt+φ); in −x use kx+ωt (or x+vt). Here a is amplitude, φ initial phase, k wave number and ω angular frequency.
  • At fixed t, the shape varies sinusoidally with x; at fixed x, each medium particle executes SHM with angular frequency ω. A crest/trough is a maximum/minimum transverse displacement. Longitudinal displacement can be written s(x,t)=a sin(kx−ωt+φ).
  • Wavelength λ is the shortest distance between points in the same phase: k=2π/λ. Period T=2π/ω; frequency ν=1/T=ω/(2π). Phase at (x,t) is kx−ωt+φ.
  • Derive speed: follow a fixed phase kx−ωt+φ=C. Differentiating gives k dx/dt−ω=0; hence v=ω/k=λν=λ/T. The medium sets wave speed; source frequency then fixes λ.
  • A general pulse has y=f(x−vt) for +x, or f(x+vt) for −x. The profile translates unchanged at speed v.
NCERT Example 14.2

Read a harmonic wave

Question. For y=0.005 sin(80x−3t) (SI units), find amplitude, wavelength, period, frequency, and y at x=0.30 m,t=20 s.

  1. Step 1. Compare with a sin(kx−ωt): a=.005 m, k=80 m⁻¹, ω=3 s⁻¹
  2. Step 2. λ=2π/k=.0785 m; T=2π/ω=2.09 s; ν=ω/2π=.477 Hz
  3. Step 3. At the stated point, phase=24−60=−36 rad, so y=.005 sin(−36)≈0.00496 m.
14.3

Wave speed in a string and in sound

Wave speed follows from the medium’s restoring elasticity and inertia.

  • For a stretched string, linear density μ=m/L and tension F give v=√(F/μ). Dimensional analysis: [F/μ]=L²T⁻², so v=C√(F/μ); the transverse-force derivation gives C=1.
  • For a longitudinal wave in a medium, v=√(B/ρ), where B is bulk modulus and ρ density. In a thin solid rod use Young’s modulus Y: v=√(Y/ρ).
  • Bulk modulus B=−ΔP/(ΔV/V). In an isothermal ideal gas B=P (Newton’s estimate v=√(P/ρ), about 280 m s⁻¹ at STP, too low). Sound compression/rarefaction is rapid and approximately adiabatic: B=γP, so Laplace’s result v=√(γP/ρ); γ=7/5 for air gives about 331.3 m s⁻¹ at STP.
  • For an ideal gas, P/ρ=RT/M, hence v=√(γRT/M): independent of pressure at fixed temperature, increases with absolute temperature. Humid air is less dense at equal P,T and sound travels faster. Greater elastic modulus generally makes sound faster; density opposes it.
NCERT Example 14.3

Wave speed on a steel wire

Question. A steel wire 0.72 m long has mass 5.0×10⁻³ kg and tension 60 N. Find transverse wave speed.

  1. Step 1. μ=m/L=0.005/0.72=6.94×10⁻³ kg m⁻¹
  2. Step 2. v=√(F/μ)=√(60/0.00694)≈93.0 m s⁻¹.
NCERT Example 14.4

Newton and Laplace sound speeds

Question. Estimate sound speed in air at STP using Newton’s isothermal model, then Laplace’s correction.

  1. Step 1. Newton: B=P, v=√(P/ρ)≈280 m s⁻¹
  2. Step 2. Sound changes are adiabatic, so B=γP
  3. Step 3. For air γ=1.4, v=√γ times Newton’s result ≈331.3 m s⁻¹.
14.4

Superposition and interference

When waves overlap in a linear medium, their displacements add algebraically.

  • Principle of superposition: y=Σfᵢ(x±vᵢt). It applies while disturbances overlap; each continues through the other. It underlies interference.
  • For equal-amplitude waves a with same k and ω travelling in +x, y₁=a sin(kx−ωt), y₂=a sin(kx−ωt+δ). Using sin A+sin B gives y=2a cos(δ/2) sin(kx−ωt+δ/2).
  • The resultant keeps the same frequency and wavelength; amplitude depends on phase difference. δ=0 gives constructive interference, amplitude 2a. δ=π gives destructive interference, zero resultant displacement.
14.5

Reflection, standing waves and normal modes

Boundary reflections can combine with incident waves to form fixed vibration patterns.

  • At a rigid end, the reflected pulse has phase change π so displacement at the boundary remains zero. At an ideal free/open end there is no phase change. At an interface, part reflects and part transmits; oblique reflection/refraction follow their usual laws. Echo is reflection.
  • Equal waves travelling in opposite directions y₁=a sin(kx−ωt), y₂=a sin(kx+ωt) give y=2a sin kx cosωt. Space and time factors separate: this is a standing wave, with no net propagation of the pattern.
  • Amplitude at position x is 2a|sin kx|. Nodes: x=nλ/2; antinodes: x=(n+½)λ/2. Successive nodes (or antinodes) are λ/2 apart; adjacent loops oscillate oppositely. Every non-node element has the same frequency; ideal standing-wave elements are in phase within one loop and π out of phase in adjacent loops.
  • String fixed at both ends has nodes at x=0,L. L=nλ/2, so λₙ=2L/n and νₙ=nv/(2L), n=1,2,… . These natural modes are harmonics; the lowest is the fundamental.
  • Open pipe: displacement antinodes at both ends, all harmonics νₙ=nv/(2L). Closed-open pipe: displacement node at closed end, antinode at open end, λ=4L/(2n+1), ν=(2n+1)v/(4L); only odd harmonics. Pressure nodes and antinodes are reversed relative to displacement.
  • Forced vibration near a natural frequency gives resonance. A clamped circular membrane has two-dimensional normal modes; the circumference is a node. Sitar/violin timbre depends on the mix of modes.
14.6

Sound in pipes and resonance

Boundary conditions select the allowed wavelengths of an air column.

  • At a closed pipe end air displacement is zero (node) and pressure variation is maximum (antinode). At an open end displacement is maximum (antinode) and pressure variation is minimum (node).
  • Geometry gives the modes: open-open ends are displacement antinodes, so L=nλ/2 and νₙ=nv/(2L). For closed-open, node-to-antinode spans an odd number of quarter wavelengths: L=(2n+1)λ/4 and νₙ=(2n+1)v/(4L). Hence a closed pipe has no even harmonics. End corrections can shift real resonances; neglect them only when the question says so.
  • A resonant mode is driven strongly when source frequency matches a natural frequency. Consecutive resonance lengths in a closed pipe differ by λ/2, which lets speed be determined without the end correction.
NCERT Example 14.5

Open and closed pipe modes

Question. A 30 cm pipe is open at both ends. Does a 1.1 kHz source resonate? Would it resonate if one end were closed? Take v=330 m s⁻¹.

  1. Step 1. Open pipe fundamental ν₁=v/(2L)=330/.60=550 Hz; 1100 Hz=2ν₁, so second harmonic resonates
  2. Step 2. Closed pipe fundamental is v/(4L)=275 Hz and allowed frequencies are 275,825,1375… Hz; 1100 Hz is absent, so no resonance.
14.7

Beats

Two close frequencies alternately reinforce and cancel, producing a periodic loudness change.

  • At a fixed point, use cos A+cos B=2cos((A−B)/2)cos((A+B)/2): s=a cosω₁t+a cosω₂t=2a cos(ωᵦt)cos(ωₐt), where ωₐ=(ω₁+ω₂)/2 and ωᵦ=(ω₁−ω₂)/2.
  • The rapid factor cos(ωₐt) is the audible oscillation; the slowly varying envelope sets loudness. Beat frequency is νᵦ=|ν₁−ν₂| (beats per second).
  • Musicians tune instruments by reducing the beat frequency. Increasing string tension increases frequency; decreasing tension lowers it.
NCERT Example 14.6

Sitar strings and beats

Question. Two sitar strings A and B playing “Dha” produce 5 Hz beats. The tension of B is slightly increased and the beat frequency decreases to 3 Hz. If νA=427 Hz, find the original frequency of B.

  1. Step 1. Increasing B tension raises νB
  2. Step 2. If B were above A, that increase would widen the beat gap
  3. Step 3. Since the gap shrinks, B must initially be below A
  4. Step 4. Thus νB=427−5.

NCERT 14.1

A string of mass 2.50 kg is under a tension of 200 N. How long would a transverse wave take to travel the length of the string, 20.0 m?

Review related concept

  1. Step 1. μ=2.50/20=.125 kg m⁻¹; v=√(200/.125)=40 m s⁻¹.
  2. Step 2. t=L/v=20/40=0.50 s.
NCERT 14.2

A stone is dropped from the top of a tower 300 m high. When will the splash be heard at the top? Speed of sound=340 m s⁻¹; g=9.8 m s⁻².

Review related concept

  1. Step 1. Fall time t₁=√(2h/g)=√(600/9.8)=7.82 s. Sound return t₂=300/340=0.882 s.
  2. Step 2. Total time ≈8.70 s after release.
NCERT 14.3

A steel wire 12.0 m long has mass 2.10 kg. What tension gives transverse waves the same speed as sound in dry air (343 m s⁻¹)?

Review related concept

  1. Step 1. μ=2.10/12=.175 kg m⁻¹. Set √(F/μ)=343.
  2. Step 2. F=μv²=.175(343)²≈2.06×10⁴ N.
NCERT 14.4

Using v=√(γP/ρ), explain why sound speed in air is (a) independent of pressure, (b) increases with temperature, (c) increases with humidity.

Review related concept

  1. Step 1. Ideal gas: ρ=PM/(RT), so γP/ρ=γRT/M.
  2. Step 2. At fixed T, P cancels. Higher absolute T raises v. Humid air has lower mean molar mass M at the same P,T, so v rises.
NCERT 14.5

Which functions represent travelling waves? (a) (x−vt)²; (b) log[(x+vt)/x₀]; (c) 1/(x+vt).

Review related concept

  1. Step 1. A travelling profile must depend on x and t through x−vt or x+vt only. (a) and (c) do; in (b), x₀ is constant scale, so the argument still depends only on x+vt.
  2. Step 2. All three are travelling forms (where defined); (a),(c) travel +x/−x respectively? f(x−vt) moves +x; f(x+vt) moves −x. (b) also moves −x.
NCERT 14.6

A bat emits ultrasound of frequency 1000 kHz into water. Find wavelengths in (a) reflected sound in air and (b) transmitted sound in water; speeds 340 and 1486 m s⁻¹.

Review related concept

  1. Step 1. Frequency remains 1.00×10⁶ Hz at the interface. λair=340/10⁶; λwater=1486/10⁶.
  2. Step 2. Reflected in air: 0.340 mm. Transmitted in water: 1.486 mm.
NCERT 14.7

A medical ultrasound travels through tissue at 1.7 km s⁻¹. Find its wavelength at 4.2 MHz.

Review related concept

  1. Step 1. λ=v/ν=1700/(4.2×10⁶).
  2. Step 2. λ=4.05×10⁻⁴ m=0.405 mm.
NCERT 14.8

For y=3 sin(36t+0.018x+π/4), x,y in cm and t in s, identify wave type, speed/direction, amplitude, frequency, phase at origin and least crest separation.

  1. Step 1. The phase is kx+ωt+φ: it travels −x. a=3 cm; k=.018 rad cm⁻¹; ω=36 rad s⁻¹.
  2. Step 2. v=ω/k=2000 cm s⁻¹=20 m s⁻¹; ν=36/(2π)=5.73 Hz; origin phase=π/4; λ=2π/.018=349 cm=3.49 m.

Classify each displacement: (a) y=2cos(3x)sin(10t); (b) y=2√(x−vt); (c) y=3sin(5x−.5t)+4cos(5x−.5t); (d) y=cos x sin t+cos2x sin2t.

NCERT 14.16

A steel rod 100 cm long is clamped at its middle. Its fundamental longitudinal frequency is 2.53 kHz. Find sound speed in steel.

Review related concept

NCERT 14.17

A 20 cm pipe is closed at one end. Which harmonic resonates at 430 Hz? Would the same source resonate if both ends were open? Take sound speed 340 m s⁻¹.

Review related concept

  1. Step 1. Closed-pipe fundamental=340/(4×.20)=425 Hz; 430 Hz is approximately the fundamental (first odd harmonic). Open-pipe fundamental=340/(2×.20)=850 Hz.
  2. Step 2. Closed pipe: fundamental resonance (within given approximation). Open pipe: no, 430 Hz is not an integer harmonic of 850 Hz.
NCERT 14.18

Two sitar strings A and B playing “Ga” produce 6 Hz beats. Slightly reducing A tension reduces beats to 3 Hz. If A originally has frequency 324 Hz, find B.

Review related concept

  1. Step 1. Reducing A tension lowers νA. Since the beat gap shrinks, A was initially above B.
  2. Step 2. νB=324−6=318 Hz.
NCERT 14.19

Explain: (a) sound displacement node is pressure antinode and vice versa; (b) bats use echoes to locate obstacles; (c) violin and sitar can share pitch but sound different; (d) solids support transverse and longitudinal waves but gases only longitudinal; (e) a pulse distorts in a dispersive medium.

Review related concept

  1. Step 1. (a) Pressure variation is proportional to the negative spatial gradient of displacement: where displacement is zero by a node, its gradient is largest. (b) Echo delay and direction, strength and spectrum give distance, direction, size and surface clues. (c) The harmonic mixture and amplitudes (timbre) differ although fundamental frequency (pitch) is equal. (d) Solids have shear and bulk elasticity; fluids at rest have no shear restoring modulus. (e) In a dispersive medium speed depends on frequency, so pulse components separate in phase.
  2. Step 2. These follow from boundary/constitutive properties, superposition and frequency-dependent phase speed.
POINT-WISE SUMMARY

Revision

Definitions, equations, assumptions and key results by concept.

14.1 · What waves carry

Review notes
  • Waves transport energy and a pattern, not bulk medium.
  • Mechanical waves require a medium; EM waves can travel in vacuum; matter waves accompany particles.
  • Transverse: displacement perpendicular to propagation. Longitudinal: parallel.
  • Surface water waves combine both; capillary/gravity waves have different restoring forces.

14.2 · Progressive wave and its equation

Review notes
  • +x: f(x−vt); −x: f(x+vt).
  • Harmonic wave: a sin(kx∓ωt+φ), k=2π/λ, ω=2πν.
  • Phase constant gives v=ω/k=λν. Frequency comes from source; speed from medium.

14.3 · Wave speed in a string and in sound

Review notes
  • String: v=√(F/μ), μ=m/L.
  • Fluid/gas: v=√(B/ρ); rod: v=√(Y/ρ).
  • Newton isothermal gas result underestimates speed; adiabatic Laplace correction uses B=γP.
  • Ideal gas v=√(γRT/M): independent of P, increases with T and humidity (at fixed P,T).

14.4 · Superposition and interference

Review notes
  • Overlapping displacements add: y=Σyᵢ.
  • Equal waves: resultant amplitude 2a cos(δ/2).
  • δ=0 constructive (2a); δ=π destructive (0).

14.5 · Reflection, standing waves and normal modes

Review notes
  • Rigid reflection phase reversal π; free/open reflection no reversal.
  • Standing wave: 2a sin kx cosωt; nodes x=nλ/2, antinodes x=(2n+1)λ/4.
  • Fixed string/open pipe: all harmonics νₙ=nv/(2L). Closed-open pipe: odd harmonics only.
  • Sound displacement node ↔ pressure antinode; resonance occurs near a natural frequency.

14.6 · Sound in pipes and resonance

Review notes
  • Open end: displacement antinode; closed end: displacement node. Pressure pattern is opposite.
  • Open-open: all harmonics. Closed-open: odd harmonics.
  • Adjacent closed-pipe resonance lengths differ by λ/2.

14.7 · Beats

Review notes
  • Beat frequency νbeat=|ν₁−ν₂|.
  • Mean frequency sets the rapid tone; half-difference sets the envelope factor.
  • Tune until beats disappear (frequencies match).
