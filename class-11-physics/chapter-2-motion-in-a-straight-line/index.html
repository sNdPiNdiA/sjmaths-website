2.1

Introduction

CONCEPT

Motion & Position

An object is in motion when its position changes with time. For one-dimensional motion, select an origin and a positive direction; the signed coordinate x then specifies position.

Point-object approximation: An extended body may be represented by a point when its size is small compared with the distance it travels in the time interval relevant to the question. Criterion: size of object ≪ distance travelled Point-object cue: Use the approximation for translational motion when shape, rotation and orientation do not matter.

x=x(t)
Key observation: Always state the origin and positive direction before assigning signs.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example

A particle is at x = 3 m at t = 2 s and x = 11 m at t = 6 s. Find its average velocity.

Given / identifyGiven: x₁ = 3 m, t₁ = 2 s; x₂ = 11 m, t₂ = 6 s.

v̄ = (x₂−x₁)/(t₂−t₁) = (11−3)/(6−2) = 2 m s⁻¹.

Answer: average velocity = 2 m s⁻¹.
2.2

Instantaneous Velocity and Speed

CONCEPT

Average Velocity

Average velocity measures net change in position per unit time. It depends on displacement, not total path length.

v¯=ΔxΔt
Key observation: A return to the starting point gives zero average velocity even though the particle travelled a non-zero path.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example

A particle moves from x = 4 m at t = 2 s to x = 16 m at t = 5 s. Find the average velocity.

Given / identifyΔx = 16−4 = 12 m; Δt = 5−2 = 3 s.

v̄ = 12/3 = 4 m s⁻¹.

Answer: 4 m s⁻¹.
CONCEPT

Instantaneous Velocity

Instantaneous velocity is the limiting value of average velocity as the time interval becomes infinitesimally small. It is the slope of the tangent to the x–t curve.

v=limΔxΔt=dxdt
Key observation: On an x–t graph, draw a tangent at the required instant and read its slope.
EXAMPLE
NCERT Example 2.1

The position of an object moving along x-axis is given by x = a + bt² where a = 8.5 m, b = 2.5 m s⁻² and t is measured in seconds. What is its velocity at t = 0 s and t = 2.0 s. What is the average velocity between t = 2.0 s and t = 4.0 s ?

Given / what is askedGiven: x=a+bt², a=8.5 m, b=2.5 m s⁻².

Velocity is v=dx/dt=2bt=5t m s⁻¹. Therefore v(0)=0 and v(2)=10 m s⁻¹.

Average velocity from 2 to 4 s: x(2)=18.5 m, x(4)=48.5 m, so v̄=(48.5−18.5)/2=15 m s⁻¹.

Answer: see the worked steps above.
CONCEPT

Instantaneous Speed

Instantaneous speed is the magnitude of instantaneous velocity. A velocity can be positive or negative according to direction; speed is non-negative.

speed=|v|
Key observation: Do not confuse average speed with magnitude of average velocity.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example

At an instant a particle has velocity −24 m s⁻¹. What is its instantaneous speed?

Given / identifyspeed = |−24| m s⁻¹ = 24 m s⁻¹.
Answer: 24 m s⁻¹.
CONCEPT

Position–Time Graph

The slope of an x–t graph represents velocity. A positive slope means positive velocity; a negative slope means negative velocity; a horizontal segment means zero velocity.

slope x–t=dxdt=v
Key observation: Do not interpret the shape of an x–t graph as the particle's spatial trajectory.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example

An x–t graph is a straight line joining (2 s, 4 m) and (6 s, 16 m). Find the velocity.

Given / identifyThe slope is (16−4)/(6−2)=12/4=3 m s⁻¹.
Answer: constant velocity = 3 m s⁻¹.
2.3

Acceleration

CONCEPT

Average & Instantaneous Acceleration

Acceleration describes how velocity changes with time. Average acceleration uses a finite interval; instantaneous acceleration is the limit as the interval shrinks to zero.

a¯=ΔvΔt;a=dvdt
Key observation: Acceleration is a signed quantity. Its sign alone does not tell whether the particle is speeding up.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example

Velocity changes from 5 m s⁻¹ at 2 s to 17 m s⁻¹ at 5 s. Find the average acceleration.

Given / identifyā = (17−5)/(5−2) = 12/3 = 4 m s⁻².

In the limiting case, a=dv/dt.

Answer: 4 m s⁻².
CONCEPT

Sign Convention & Speeding Up

The sign of v and a is determined by the chosen positive direction. In one-dimensional motion, velocity and acceleration with the same sign increase speed; opposite signs decrease speed.

same sign → speeding up; opposite signs → slowing down
Key observation: A negative acceleration does not automatically mean slowing down.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example

A ball moves upward with v>0 while gravity gives a<0. Is the ball speeding up or slowing down?

Given / identifyVelocity and acceleration have opposite signs.

Therefore the speed decreases during the upward motion.

Answer: slowing down.
CONCEPT

Velocity–Time Graph

The slope of a v–t graph gives acceleration. The signed area under a v–t curve gives displacement.

slope = a; area under v–t = displacement
Key observation: For negative velocity, area below the time axis contributes negative displacement.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example

A particle moves with constant velocity 6 m s⁻¹ for 5 s. What is the area under its v–t graph?

Given / identifyThe graph is a rectangle of height 6 and width 5.

Area = 6×5 = 30 m, which equals displacement.

Answer: displacement = 30 m.
2.4

Kinematic Equations for Uniformly Accelerated Motion

CONCEPT

Uniformly Accelerated Motion

The elementary kinematic equations apply when acceleration remains constant over the interval. This is uniformly accelerated motion.

a=constant
Key observation: Check the constant-acceleration condition before using the four standard equations.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example

A particle starts with v₀=6 m s⁻¹ and has constant acceleration 2 m s⁻² for 4 s. Find its final velocity.

Given / identifyConstant acceleration is given.

v = v₀ + at = 6 + 2×4 = 14 m s⁻¹.

Answer: 14 m s⁻¹.
CONCEPT

First Equation of Motion

The first equation connects final velocity with initial velocity, constant acceleration and elapsed time.

v=v0+at
Key observation: Use signed values of v₀ and a after choosing the positive direction.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example

A car has initial velocity 10 m s⁻¹ and accelerates uniformly at 2 m s⁻² for 4 s. Find its velocity.

Given / identifyGiven: v₀=10 m s⁻¹, a=2 m s⁻², t=4 s.

v=10+2×4=18 m s⁻¹.

Answer: 18 m s⁻¹.
DERIVATION
Derivation: First equation of motion

Statement: Derive the relation between final velocity, initial velocity, acceleration and time for constant acceleration.

Given/conditions: initial velocity v₀ at t=0; final velocity v at time t; constant acceleration a.

Step 1 — Start from definition

a=v−v0t

Step 2 — Rearrange

at=v−v0

Step 3 — Result

v = v₀ + at

Meaning: Under constant acceleration, the change in velocity in time t is at.

CONCEPT

Displacement Equation

For constant acceleration, displacement can be expressed using initial velocity, acceleration and time.

Δx=v0t+12at2
Key observation: This equation is especially useful when time is known.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example

A particle has v₀=5 m s⁻¹, a=3 m s⁻² and moves for 4 s. Find the displacement.

Given / identifyΔx=v₀t+½at² = 5×4 + ½×3×16 = 44 m.
Answer: 44 m.
DERIVATION
Derivation: Displacement equation

Statement: Derive displacement for uniformly accelerated motion.

Given/conditions: initial velocity v₀, acceleration a and time t.

Step 1 — Area under the v–t graph

Δx=v0t+12v−v0t

Step 2 — Use v−v₀ = at

Δx=v0t+12at2

Step 3 — General form

x−x0=v0t+12at2

Meaning: displacement equals the initial-velocity contribution plus the acceleration contribution.

CONCEPT

Average Velocity Relation

For uniform acceleration, average velocity equals the arithmetic mean of initial and final velocities.

v¯=v0+v2
Key observation: This relation is not generally valid for arbitrary non-uniform acceleration.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example

A particle changes velocity uniformly from 8 m s⁻¹ to 20 m s⁻¹. Find the average velocity.

Given / identifyBecause acceleration is uniform, v̄=(8+20)/2=14 m s⁻¹.
Answer: 14 m s⁻¹.
DERIVATION
Derivation: Average velocity

Statement: Show that average velocity equals the arithmetic mean of v₀ and v for constant acceleration.

Step 1: Start from Δx=((v₀+v)/2)t.

Δxt=v0+v2

Step 2 — Identify average velocity

v¯=v0+v2

Meaning: For uniform acceleration, the average value of velocity over the interval is the midpoint of the initial and final velocities.

CONCEPT

Velocity–Displacement Equation

This equation relates velocity and displacement without explicitly involving time.

v2=v02+2aΔx
Key observation: Use it when time is not given or is easier to eliminate.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example

A car changes speed from 10 m s⁻¹ to 20 m s⁻¹ under constant acceleration 5 m s⁻². Find the displacement.

Given / identifyv²−v₀²=20²−10²=300.

Δx=300/(2×5)=30 m.

Answer: 30 m.
DERIVATION
Derivation: Velocity–displacement equation

Statement: Eliminate time from the kinematic equations.

Step 1: From v=v₀+at, t=(v−v₀)/a.

Step 2: Substitute in Δx=((v₀+v)/2)t.

Δx = ((v₀+v)/2) ((v−v₀)/a)

Step 3 — Simplify

2aΔx=v2−v02

Step 4 — Result

v² = v₀² + 2aΔx
CONCEPT

Calculus Derivation of the Equations

The chapter derives the equations of uniformly accelerated motion directly from the definitions a=dv/dt and v=dx/dt. This method also points toward non-uniform acceleration.

a=dvdt,v=dxdt
Key observation: Keep the integration limits consistent with the initial conditions.
EXAMPLE
NCERT Example 2.2

Obtain equations of motion for constant acceleration using method of calculus.

Given / what is askedGiven/conditions: constant a, v(0)=v₀, x(0)=x₀.

From dv=a dt and integration: v=v₀+at.

From dx=v dt and integration: x−x₀=v₀t+½at².

Using a=v(dv/dx): v²=v₀²+2a(x−x₀).

Result: derived equations of motion for constant acceleration.
DERIVATION
Derivation: Calculus method

Statement: Obtain the equations of motion for constant acceleration using calculus.

Given/conditions: a is constant; at t=0, v=v₀ and x=x₀.

Step 1 — Integrate acceleration

a=dv/dt → dv=a dt → ∫dv = ∫a dt
v=v₀+at

Step 2 — Integrate velocity

dx=v dt=(v₀+at)dt
x−x₀=v₀t+½at²

Step 3 — Eliminate time

a=(dv/dx)(dx/dt)=v(dv/dx)
v2=v02+2a(x−x0)

Meaning: the calculus route starts from definitions instead of assuming the kinematic equations.

CONCEPT

Free Fall

An object near Earth's surface is in free fall when air resistance is neglected. For a height small compared with Earth's radius, g is treated as constant. With upward positive, a=−g.

a=−g;v=−gt;y=−12gt2
Key observation: Free fall is a special case of uniformly accelerated motion.
EXAMPLES
NCERT Example 2.3

A ball is thrown vertically upwards with a velocity of 20 m s⁻¹ from the top of a multistorey building. The height of the point from where the ball is thrown is 25.0 m from the ground. (a) How high will the ball rise? and (b) how long will it be before the ball hits the ground? Take g = 10 m s⁻².

Given / what is askedInitial velocity, launch height and g are given; find maximum rise and time to ground.

At maximum height v=0: 0=20²−20(y−25), so y−25=20 m.

For the total time: 0=25+20t−5t², whose positive root is t=5 s.

Answer: rise above launch point = 20 m; total time = 5 s.
NCERT Example 2.4

Free-fall : Discuss the motion of an object under free fall. Neglect air resistance.

Given / conditionsAir resistance neglected; upward direction positive; released from rest.

Given/conditions: air resistance neglected; upward positive.

Then a=−g≈−9.8 m s⁻². If released from rest, v₀=0.

Therefore v=−gt, y=−½gt² and v²=−2gy.

Key result: free fall is uniformly accelerated motion with downward acceleration g.
DERIVATION
Specialisation of the kinematic equations

Statement: Obtain the free-fall equations for an object released from rest.

Given/conditions: v₀=0; a=−g; upward y positive.

v=−gt
y=−½gt²
v²=−2gy

Meaning: all three are direct special cases of uniformly accelerated motion.

CONCEPT

Galileo's Odd-Number Law

For a body falling from rest, the distances covered during successive equal intervals of time are in the ratio 1:3:5:7:… .

successive distances ∝ 1:3:5:7:…
Key observation: The result follows from the quadratic dependence y∝t².
EXAMPLE
NCERT Example 2.5

Galileo’s law of odd numbers: “The distances traversed, during equal intervals of time, by a body falling from rest, stand to one another in the same ratio as the odd numbers beginning with unity [namely, 1: 3: 5: 7…...].” Prove it.

Given / what is askedGiven/conditions: free fall from rest and equal intervals τ.

Position after nτ varies as n², so the distance in the nth interval is proportional to n²−(n−1)²=2n−1.

Hence the successive distances are 1:3:5:7:… .

Answer: see the worked steps above.
DERIVATION
Proof

Statement: Prove Galileo's law of odd numbers for a body falling from rest.

Step 1: After time nτ, the distance from the starting point is proportional to n².

y_n ∝ n²

Step 2: Distance covered in the nth interval is the difference of two successive positions.

s_n ∝ n²−(n−1)²

Step 3: Simplify.

s_n ∝ 2n−1

Meaning: for equal time intervals, the distances form the odd-number sequence.

CONCEPT

Stopping Distance

Stopping distance is the distance travelled after brakes are applied until the vehicle comes to rest. For a fixed retardation magnitude, it depends on the square of initial speed.

ds=v022a
Key observation: Doubling initial speed makes stopping distance four times as large for the same retardation.
EXAMPLE
NCERT Example 2.6

Stopping distance of vehicles : When brakes are applied to a moving vehicle, the distance it travels before stopping is called stopping distance. It is an important factor for road safety and depends on the initial velocity (v₀) and the braking capacity, or deceleration, −a that is caused by the braking. Derive an expression for stopping distance of a vehicle in terms of v₀ and a.

Given / what is askedGiven: v₀, final v=0 and retardation magnitude a.

Use 0=v₀²−2adₛ.

Therefore dₛ=v₀²/(2a), showing dₛ∝v₀².

Answer: see the worked steps above.
DERIVATION
Derivation

Statement: Derive stopping distance in terms of initial velocity and retardation magnitude.

Given/conditions: initial speed v₀, final speed v=0, acceleration=−a.

Step 1:

0 = v₀² + 2(−a)dₛ

Step 2: Rearrange.

ds=v022a

Meaning: stopping distance is proportional to the square of the initial speed for fixed retardation.

CONCEPT

Reaction Time

Reaction time is the time taken to observe a situation, think and act. The ruler-drop experiment estimates it using free-fall displacement.

tr=2dg
Key observation: This is an application of the free-fall equation d=½gtᵣ².
EXAMPLE
NCERT Example 2.7

Reaction time : When a situation demands our immediate action, it takes some time before we really respond. Reaction time is the time a person takes to observe, think and act. For example, if a person is driving and suddenly a boy appears on the road, then the time elapsed before he slams the brakes of the car is the reaction time. Estimate reaction time using the ruler-drop experiment, for which d = 21.0 cm.

Given / what is askedGiven: d=21.0 cm=0.210 m and g=9.8 m s⁻².

The ruler is in free fall: d=½gtᵣ².

Therefore tᵣ=√(2d/g)=√(0.420/9.8)≈0.207 s.

Answer: see the worked steps above.
2.5

Relative Velocity

CONCEPT

Relative Velocity

Relative velocity is the velocity of one object as measured from another object. In one dimension, subtract the reference object's algebraic velocity from the object's velocity.

vA/B=vA−vB
Key observation: Identify the reference object first. In vector form, v→A/B=v→A−v→B.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example

A moves at +20 m s⁻¹ and B at +12 m s⁻¹ in the same direction. Find the velocity of A relative to B.

Given / identifyv_A/B = 20−12 = +8 m s⁻¹.
Answer: +8 m s⁻¹.

Read each statement below carefully and state with reasons and examples, if it is true or false; A particle in one-dimensional motion

(a)
with zero speed at an instant may have non-zero acceleration at that instant
(b)
with zero speed may have non-zero velocity,
(c)
with constant speed must have zero acceleration,
(d)
with positive value of acceleration must be speeding up.

Solution
(a) True

At the highest point of a vertical throw, speed is zero instantaneously while acceleration remains g downward.

(b) False

Speed is the magnitude of velocity, so zero speed means zero velocity.

(c) False

Constant speed can occur with changing direction in general. In strictly one-dimensional motion without reversal, constant speed gives zero acceleration.

(d) False

The sign of acceleration depends on the chosen positive direction. Speed increases when velocity and acceleration have the same sign.

Solution
Interpretation
