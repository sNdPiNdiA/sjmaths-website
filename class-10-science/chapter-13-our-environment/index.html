Concepts & Visual Theory

Core concepts, definitions, and key exam principles

01

Ecosystem and Its Components

An ecosystem consists of all the living organisms (biotic factors) in an area together with the non-living physical environment (abiotic factors) interacting with each other.

Biotic Components: Plants, animals, bacteria, and fungi.
Abiotic Components: Sunlight, temperature, rainfall, soil, and minerals.
02

Producers, Consumers, and Decomposers

Organisms are classified based on how they obtain sustenance:

  • Producers (Autotrophs): Green plants and blue-green algae that synthesise organic food from inorganic substances using photosynthesis.
  • Consumers (Heterotrophs): Organisms that depend on producers directly or indirectly (Herbivores, Carnivores, Omnivores, and Parasites).
  • Decomposers: Microorganisms (bacteria & fungi) that break down dead remains and waste products of organisms into simple inorganic substances, replenishing the soil.
03

Food Chain & 10% Law of Energy Flow

A food chain is a series of organisms each dependent on the next as a source of food. Energy flows unidirectionally from the sun to producers, and then to higher trophic levels.

Lindeman's 10% Law: Only about 10% of the energy entering a trophic level is stored in biomass and available to the next trophic level. 90% is lost as heat to the environment or consumed in life processes (respiration, digestion, reproduction).
04

Biological Magnification

Biological Magnification refers to the phenomenon where the concentration of harmful, non-biodegradable chemicals (like pesticides, DDT) increases progressively at each successive trophic level of a food chain.

Crucial Exam Note: Since humans occupy the top position in many food chains, the maximum concentration of toxic chemicals accumulates in human bodies.
05

Ozone Layer Depletion & Waste Disposal

Ozone ($O_3$): A molecule formed by 3 oxygen atoms. It resides in the stratosphere and shields Earth's surface from harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation.

Depletion: Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) used in refrigerants and fire extinguishers release free chlorine atoms under UV light, catalytic dismantling of ozone molecules.

Biodegradable Waste: Decomposed naturally by bacteria (vegetable peels, paper).
Non-Biodegradable Waste: Cannot be broken down naturally and persists for years (plastics, synthetic polymers).

NCERT Questions & Solutions

Textbook solutions for CBSE Board preparation

Q1

What are trophic levels? Give an example of a food chain and state the different trophic levels in it.

Each step or level of a food chain forms a trophic level:

  1. First trophic level ($T_1$): Producers — Grass
  2. Second trophic level ($T_2$): Primary consumers (herbivores) — Deer
  3. Third trophic level ($T_3$): Secondary consumers (carnivores) — Tiger
Q2

What is the role of decomposers in the ecosystem?

Decomposers break down complex organic matter of dead plants and animals into simpler inorganic substances that return to the soil, water, and air, maintaining the natural nutrient cycle and keeping the environment clean.

Quick Revision Points

Fast recall summary for quick revision before exams

  • Energy Flow: Unidirectional (Sun → Autotrophs → Heterotrophs). Energy cannot revert back.
  • 10% Law: 10% energy transferred to next level; limits food chains to 3–4 trophic levels.
  • Bio-magnification: Max pesticide/toxin accumulation occurs at the top trophic level (Humans).
  • Ozone Formation: $O_2 \xrightarrow{UV} O + O$; then $O + O_2 \to O_3$. Protects against skin cancer and cataracts.
  • Montreal Protocol (1987): International agreement to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels.
