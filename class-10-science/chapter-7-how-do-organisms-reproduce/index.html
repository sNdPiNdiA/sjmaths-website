Concepts & Visual Theory

30 structured concept blocks

01

Why do organisms reproduce?

Continuity of a species

Reproduction is not necessary to maintain the life of an individual in the way nutrition, respiration and excretion are. It creates new individuals and therefore helps maintain populations and species.

Key: Individual survives; species continues.
02

DNA copying — the basic event

DNA copying and cell formation

Chromosomes contain DNA, which carries inherited information and is the information source for making proteins. A basic event in reproduction is creation of a DNA copy, accompanied by additional cellular apparatus before the copies separate into cells.

Key: DNA copy → cellular apparatus → separation → new cells.
03

Why offspring are similar but not identical

Accurate copying + small copying changes

DNA-copying reactions are not absolutely reliable. Variations can arise. Some may be drastic and harmful, while others allow the new cells to survive. The surviving cells are therefore similar to, but subtly different from, one another.

Key: Variation during reproduction is the basis for evolution in the chapter.
04

Importance of variation

Changing environment and survival

Populations occupy niches. Stable DNA copying helps maintain body designs suited to those niches, while variation can help a few individuals survive if the niche changes, such as when temperature or water conditions change.

Key: Stable conditions favour existing designs; changing conditions may favour variants.
05

Asexual reproduction

Six asexual modes

The chapter's single-individual modes are asexual reproduction: fission, fragmentation, regeneration, budding, vegetative propagation and spore formation.

Key: F-B-F-R-V-S = Fission, Budding, Fragmentation, Regeneration, Vegetative propagation, Spore formation.
06

Binary fission

Binary fission — one cell becomes two

Many bacteria and protozoa split into two daughter cells. In Amoeba the split may occur in any plane. Leishmania divides in a definite orientation related to its whip-like structure.

Key: Binary = two.
07

Multiple fission

Multiple fission — Plasmodium

Plasmodium divides into many daughter cells simultaneously.

Key: Multiple = many; example: Plasmodium.
08

Budding

Budding — yeast / Hydra concept

Yeast produces small buds that separate and grow. In Hydra a bud forms by repeated cell division at one site, matures and detaches as an independent individual.

Key: Bud grows → matures → detaches.
09

Fragmentation

Fragmentation — Spirogyra

Spirogyra can break into smaller pieces upon maturation, and the fragments grow into new individuals. More complex organisms cannot generally reproduce by simple cell-by-cell division because specialised cells form organised tissues and organs.

Key: Fragment → grow → new individual.
10

Regeneration

Regeneration — body pieces can reorganise

Hydra and Planaria can give rise to complete organisms from body pieces. Specialised cells proliferate and then differentiate into cell types and tissues through organised development. The chapter distinguishes regeneration from normal reproduction.

Key: Regeneration is not automatically the same as reproduction.
11

Vegetative propagation

Vegetative propagation — plant part → new plant

Roots, stems and leaves of some plants can form new plants. The chapter describes layering/grafting, examples such as sugarcane, roses and grapes, and Bryophyllum leaf buds. It can allow earlier flowering/fruiting and propagation of plants that have lost seed-producing capacity.

Key: Plant part → new plant.
12

Tissue culture

Tissue culture sequence

Tissue or cells from a growing tip are placed in artificial medium, divide rapidly into a callus, then are transferred to a hormone-containing medium for growth and differentiation. Plantlets are finally transferred to soil.

Key: Tissue → callus → hormone medium → plantlet → soil.
13

Spore formation

Rhizopus — sporangium and spores

In Rhizopus, hyphae are not the reproductive parts. Sporangia contain spores. Thick spore walls protect them until they reach a moist surface where growth can begin.

Key: Sporangium stores; spore spreads.
14

Why sexual reproduction?

Combining DNA from two individuals

Sexual reproduction combines DNA from different individuals. Because individuals carry different accumulated variations, their combination creates novel combinations and generates variation more effectively than gradual copying errors alone.

Key: Sexual = combination of existing variations.
15

Meiosis and chromosome number

Meiosis halves; fertilisation restores

Sexual reproduction would otherwise double DNA each generation. Meiosis produces germ-cells with half the chromosome number and half the DNA amount of ordinary body cells; fusion restores the number in the new generation.

Key: Meiosis halves; fertilisation restores.
16

Male and female gametes

Gamete specialisation

As organisms become more complex, germ-cells specialise. One is larger and contains food stores; the other is smaller and motile. The motile one is conventionally called the male gamete and the food-containing one the female gamete.

Key: Large food-containing gamete vs smaller motile gamete.
17

Flower reproductive parts

Simplified flower — reproductive structures

Stamen is the male reproductive part and produces pollen. Pistil is the female reproductive part and consists of stigma, style and ovary. Ovary contains ovules, and each ovule has an egg cell.

Key: Pistil = S-S-O: stigma, style, ovary.
18

Unisexual and bisexual flowers

Unisexual vs bisexual

Unisexual flowers contain either stamens or pistil; the source examples are papaya and watermelon. Bisexual flowers contain both; examples are Hibiscus and mustard.

Key: Uni = one reproductive type; bi = both.
19

Pollination

Pollination — pollen reaches stigma

Pollination is transfer of pollen from stamen/anther to stigma. Same-flower transfer is self-pollination; transfer from one flower to another is cross-pollination. Wind, water and animals can act as agents.

Key: Pollination = pollen reaches stigma.
20

Fertilisation and seed/fruit formation

Flowering plant reproduction sequence

A pollen tube grows through the style to the ovary. Male and female germ-cells fuse to form a zygote. The zygote becomes an embryo; the ovule develops into a seed and the ovary ripens into a fruit. Germination is development of a seedling under suitable conditions.

Key: Pollination → pollen tube → fertilisation → zygote → embryo → seed; ovary → fruit.
21

Puberty and sexual maturation

Adolescence → puberty → reproductive maturity

During adolescence, reproductive tissues mature as general body growth begins to slow. Changes occur gradually and at different rates. The source describes body-hair changes, oily skin/pimples, breast development and menstruation in girls, and facial hair, voice changes and genital changes in boys.

Key: Puberty = reproductive maturation during adolescence.
22

Male reproductive system

Male reproductive system — simplified pathway

Testes produce sperms and testosterone and are located in the scrotum because sperm formation requires a lower temperature. Vas deferens transports sperm. Seminal vesicles and prostate add secretions that aid transport and nutrition. Urethra is a common passage for sperm and urine.

Key: Testes → sperm/testosterone; vas deferens → transport.
23

Female reproductive system

Female reproductive system — simplified pathway

Ovaries produce eggs and some hormones. After puberty, one egg is released each month by one ovary as described in the source. The fallopian tube carries the egg and is the site where sperm may encounter it. The uterus supports embryo/foetus development; cervix opens into vagina.

Key: Ovary → oviduct → uterus → cervix → vagina.
24

Fertilisation, implantation and placenta

Human reproduction — from fertilisation to foetus

Sperm enter through the vagina and travel to the oviduct. Fertilisation forms a zygote, which divides into an embryo. The embryo implants in the uterine lining and develops into a foetus. Placenta provides a large exchange surface for glucose and oxygen to pass to the embryo and wastes to pass to the mother's blood.

Key: Fertilisation → zygote → embryo → implantation → foetus.
25

When the egg is not fertilised

Menstrual cycle concept

The uterus prepares a thick lining every month. If fertilisation does not occur, the lining is no longer needed and breaks down, leaving through the vagina as blood and mucus. This is menstruation, usually lasting about two to eight days according to the source.

Key: No fertilisation → lining sheds → menstruation.
26

Reproductive health and STIs

Reproductive health — informed choice + protection

The chapter states that sexual maturation does not necessarily mean physical or mental readiness for sexual activity or parenthood. It lists gonorrhoea, syphilis, warts and HIV-AIDS as sexually transmitted infections and states that condom use helps prevent transmission of many such infections to some extent.

Key: Reproductive health includes informed choices and infection prevention.
27

Contraception

Contraception — four broad categories

Methods described include mechanical barriers such as condoms, hormonal oral pills, intrauterine devices such as copper-T, and surgical blocking of the vas deferens or fallopian tubes. Pills can have side effects because they alter hormonal balance; IUDs can cause side effects due to uterine irritation.

Key: Barrier → hormonal → IUD → surgical.
28

Copper-T and STI protection

Pregnancy prevention vs STI protection

Copper-T is presented as a contraceptive device to prevent pregnancy. The chapter associates condom use with helping to prevent transmission of many sexually transmitted infections. Therefore copper-T should not be written as an STI-protection method.

Key: Copper-T prevents pregnancy; condom is the chapter's STI-protection example.
29

Population and social responsibility

Population size and reproductive choices

Birth and death rates determine population size. The chapter also discusses social and health consequences around reproductive choices and states that prenatal sex determination has been prohibited by law and condemns illegal sex-selective abortion.

Key: Reproductive health has biological and social dimensions.
30

Master concept map

Key: MASTER CHAIN: DNA → variation → reproduction → gametes → fertilisation → development → reproductive health.

NCERT Questions & Answers

Questions appearing in the uploaded Chapter 7 source

7.1 Q1

What is the importance of DNA copying in reproduction?

Answer: DNA carries inherited information and is the information source for making proteins. Copying DNA passes this information to new cells/offspring; the copying process can also introduce variations.
7.1 Q2

Why is variation beneficial to the species but not necessarily for the individual?

Answer: A variation can be neutral or harmful to an individual, but some variants may survive when environmental conditions change, helping the species persist.
7.2 Q1

How does binary fission differ from multiple fission?

Answer: Binary fission produces two daughter cells; multiple fission produces many daughter cells simultaneously. The chapter gives Plasmodium as the multiple-fission example.
7.2 Q2

How will an organism be benefited if it reproduces through spores?

Answer: Spores have thick protective walls and can remain protected until they reach a moist surface suitable for growth.
7.2 Q3

Why can more complex organisms not give rise to new individuals through regeneration?

Answer: Their specialised cells form organised tissues and organs in definite positions, so they are not simply collections of interchangeable cells.
7.2 Q4

Why is vegetative propagation practised for growing some types of plants?

Answer: It can give earlier flowering/fruiting in the situations described, propagate plants that have lost seed-producing capacity and produce plants genetically similar enough to retain parental characteristics.
7.2 Q5

Why is DNA copying an essential part of reproduction?

Answer: DNA contains the information needed for inherited features and body design, so its copying is essential for passing this information into new cells/offspring.
Final Q1

How is pollination different from fertilisation?

Answer: Pollination is pollen transfer to the stigma. Fertilisation is fusion of male and female germ-cells to form a zygote.
Final Q2

What is the role of seminal vesicles and prostate gland?

Answer: They add secretions to sperm, making transport easier and providing nutrition.
Final Q3

What are the changes seen in girls at puberty?

Answer: The source describes breast development, darkening of nipple skin and beginning of menstruation, along with general changes such as body hair, oily skin and pimples.
Final Q4

How does the embryo get nourishment inside the mother's body?

Answer: Through the placenta, which provides an exchange surface for glucose and oxygen to reach the embryo and wastes to pass to the mother's blood.
Final Q5

If a woman is using a copper-T, will it help protect her from sexually transmitted diseases?

Answer: No. Copper-T is described as a contraceptive device. The chapter associates condom use with helping to prevent transmission of many STIs.
Exercise 1

Asexual reproduction takes place through budding in: Amoeba, Yeast, Plasmodium or Leishmania?

Answer: Yeast.
Exercise 2

Which is not part of the female reproductive system: ovary, uterus, vas deferens or fallopian tube?

Answer: Vas deferens.
Exercise 3

The anther contains sepals, ovules, pistil or pollen grains?

Answer: Pollen grains.
Exercise 4

What are the advantages of sexual reproduction over asexual reproduction?

Answer: It combines DNA from two different individuals and creates novel combinations of accumulated variations, generating variation more effectively.
Exercise 5

What are the functions performed by the testis?

Answer: Testes produce sperms and secrete testosterone; testosterone regulates sperm formation and puberty-related changes described in the chapter.
Exercise 6

Why does menstruation occur?

Answer: If fertilisation does not occur, the thick uterine lining prepared for an embryo is no longer needed and breaks down and exits as blood and mucus.
Exercise 7

Draw a labelled diagram of the longitudinal section of a flower.

Answer: Label the major structures, especially stamen/anther/pollen and pistil/stigma/style/ovary/ovule.
Exercise 8

What are the different methods of contraception?

Answer: Mechanical barriers, hormonal oral pills, copper-T/other intrauterine devices and surgical methods blocking the vas deferens or fallopian tubes.
Exercise 9

How are reproductive modes different in unicellular and multicellular organisms?

Answer: In unicellular organisms, cell division itself can produce new individuals. More complex multicellular organisms require more organised reproductive mechanisms because their cells form specialised tissues and organs.
Exercise 10

How does reproduction help provide stability to populations?

Answer: Reproduction maintains populations, while consistent DNA copying maintains body designs suited to niches. Variation can help some individuals survive environmental change.
Exercise 11

What could be reasons for adopting contraceptive methods?

Answer: To avoid or plan pregnancy when individuals are not ready for the physical and mental demands of pregnancy and parenthood; the chapter also discusses reproductive health.
