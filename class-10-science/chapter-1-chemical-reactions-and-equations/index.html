Concepts & Theories

Short • Exam-ready • High scoring

01
What is a Chemical Reaction?

A chemical reaction occurs when substances undergo a chemical change and new substances are formed.

Chemical reaction → old substances change → new substances form.
💡 Board tip: If asked how you know a reaction occurred, write at least two observations.
🧠 Mnemonic: SCGT = State • Colour • Gas • Temperature
02
Signs of a Chemical Reaction

A chemical reaction may be indicated by:

Change in state • Change in colour • Evolution of gas • Change in temperature

Example: magnesium burns with a dazzling white flame and forms white magnesium oxide.

03
Word Equation

A word equation represents a reaction using names of reactants and products.

Magnesium + Oxygen → Magnesium oxide Reactants → Product
✍️ Exam tip: Reactants are written on the left; products on the right.
04
Chemical Equation

A chemical equation represents a chemical reaction using chemical formulae.

Mg + O₂ → MgO Skeleton equation

An equation is balanced when the number of atoms of every element is equal on both sides.

Never change subscripts while balancing. Change only coefficients.
05
Law of Conservation of Mass

Mass can neither be created nor destroyed during a chemical reaction.

Therefore, the number of atoms of each element must remain the same before and after the reaction.
🧠 Remember: Atoms don't disappear — they rearrange.
06
How to Balance an Equation

Use the hit-and-trial method.

1. Write correct formulae.
2. Count atoms on both sides.
3. Balance one element at a time.
4. Use smallest whole-number coefficients.
5. Recheck every atom.

3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂
⚠️ Common mistake: Never turn H₂O into H₂O₄. Put coefficient 4 before H₂O.
07
Physical State Symbols
(s) = solid   |   (l) = liquid   |   (g) = gas   |   (aq) = aqueous solution
3Fe(s) + 4H₂O(g) → Fe₃O₄(s) + 4H₂(g)
💡 Use state symbols when the question specifically asks for a chemical equation with state symbols.

Types of Chemical Reactions

Very important for MCQs + 2/3 markers

Combination

Two or more substances form one product.

A + B → AB

Decomposition

One substance breaks into two or more products.

AB → A + B

Displacement

A more reactive element displaces another element.

A + BC → AC + B

Double Displacement

Ions/groups exchange between two compounds.

AB + CD → AD + CB
08
Exothermic vs Endothermic
Exothermic → heat released 🔥

Example: respiration releases energy.

Endothermic → energy absorbed ❄️

Decomposition reactions may require heat, light or electricity.

🧠 EXO = energy EXits
🧠 ENDO = energy ENters
09
Oxidation & Reduction

Oxidation is gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen.

Reduction is loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen.

CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O
CuO loses oxygen → reduced
H₂ gains oxygen → oxidised
🧠 OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss; Reduction Is Gain.
10
Corrosion

Corrosion is the gradual attack of metals by substances in their surroundings such as moisture and acids.

Rusting of iron is a common example.

Iron → reddish-brown rust
💡 Why paint iron? Paint prevents contact of iron with air/moisture and therefore helps prevent corrosion.
11
Rancidity

Fats and oils undergo oxidation and develop unpleasant smell and taste.

Oxidation of fats/oils → Rancidity

Antioxidants, airtight containers and nitrogen flushing help slow oxidation.

🎯 Board favourite: Chips packets are flushed with nitrogen to prevent oxidation of oils/fats.

Board Answer Formula

Use this structure for theory questions

Definition → Explanation → Chemical Equation → Observation/Example → Conclusion

For a 3-mark question, avoid one-line answers. Give the definition first, then the scientific reason, followed by the relevant equation/example.

🧠 D-E-E-C = Definition → Explanation → Equation → Conclusion

NCERT Exercises

Questions + board-ready answers

1. Which statements about 2PbO(s) + C(s) → 2Pb(s) + CO₂(g) are incorrect?
Answer
(a) Lead is getting reduced.
(b) Carbon dioxide is getting oxidised.
(c) Carbon is getting oxidised.
(d) Lead oxide is getting reduced.

Carbon gains oxygen to form CO₂, so carbon is oxidised. PbO loses oxygen and forms Pb, so PbO is reduced. Therefore, the incorrect statement is (b).

2. Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe is an example of which reaction?
Answer

Displacement reaction. Aluminium displaces iron from iron(III) oxide.

3. What happens when dilute hydrochloric acid is added to iron filings?
Answer

Hydrogen gas and iron chloride are produced.

Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂
4. What is a balanced chemical equation? Why should equations be balanced?
Board Answer

A balanced chemical equation has an equal number of atoms of each element on both sides.

Equations are balanced to obey the law of conservation of mass.

5. Translate and balance: Hydrogen gas combines with nitrogen to form ammonia.
Answer
N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃
6. Balance the following: HNO₃ + Ca(OH)₂ → Ca(NO₃)₂ + H₂O
Answer
2HNO₃ + Ca(OH)₂ → Ca(NO₃)₂ + 2H₂O
7. Write and balance: Calcium hydroxide + Carbon dioxide → Calcium carbonate + Water
Answer
Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O
8. Identify the reaction type: Hydrogen + Chlorine → Hydrogen chloride.
Answer

It is a combination reaction because two reactants combine to form one product.

H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl
9. What are exothermic and endothermic reactions?
Answer

Exothermic: reactions in which heat is released.

Endothermic: reactions in which energy is absorbed.

Example of exothermic reaction: respiration.

10. Why is respiration considered an exothermic reaction?
Answer

During respiration, glucose reacts with oxygen and releases energy.

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy
11. Why are decomposition reactions called the opposite of combination reactions?
Answer

In combination, two or more substances form a single product. In decomposition, one substance breaks into two or more products.

12. Write decomposition equations using heat, light and electricity.
Answer
CaCO₃ ──Heat──→ CaO + CO₂
2AgCl ──Sunlight──→ 2Ag + Cl₂
2H₂O ──Electricity──→ 2H₂ + O₂
13. Difference between displacement and double displacement.
Answer

Displacement: one element displaces another element from its compound.

Double displacement: ions/groups are exchanged between two compounds.

Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu
Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄ + 2NaCl
14. Write the reaction involved in recovery of silver from silver nitrate using copper.
Answer
Cu + 2AgNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2Ag

Copper displaces silver from silver nitrate.

15. What is a precipitation reaction?
Answer

A reaction in which an insoluble solid is formed from solutions is called a precipitation reaction.

Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + 2NaCl

BaSO₄ is the white insoluble precipitate.

16. Explain oxidation and reduction in terms of oxygen.
Answer

Oxidation: gain of oxygen.

Reduction: loss of oxygen.

2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO
17. A shiny brown element X becomes black on heating in air. Identify X and the black compound.
Answer

X = Copper (Cu)
Black compound = Copper(II) oxide (CuO)

2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO
18. Why is paint applied on iron articles?
Answer

Paint forms a protective layer and prevents iron from coming into contact with air and moisture, thereby reducing corrosion.

19. Why are oil- and fat-containing food items flushed with nitrogen?
Answer

Nitrogen reduces contact with oxygen and therefore slows oxidation of fats and oils, preventing rancidity.

20. Explain corrosion and rancidity with one example each.
Answer

Corrosion: gradual deterioration of a metal due to environmental substances. Example: rusting of iron.

Rancidity: oxidation of fats/oils causing unpleasant smell and taste.

PYQs

Board-style high-frequency questions

1 MARK

What type of reaction occurs when a single substance breaks down into two or more products?

Answer: Decomposition reaction.

1 MARK

What is observed when copper powder is heated in air?

Answer: Copper develops a black coating of copper(II) oxide.

2 MARKS

Explain why AgCl turns grey in sunlight.

AgCl decomposes in sunlight to form silver and chlorine.

2AgCl ──Sunlight──→ 2Ag + Cl₂
2 MARKS

Why is the reaction between iron and copper sulphate called a displacement reaction?

Iron is more reactive than copper and displaces copper from copper sulphate.

Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu
3 MARKS

Explain oxidation and reduction using CuO and H₂.

CuO loses oxygen and is reduced, while H₂ gains oxygen and is oxidised.

CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O
3 MARKS

Differentiate between combination, decomposition, displacement and double displacement reactions.

Write the general pattern and one example for each. This is a high-value classification question.

3 MARKS

Explain corrosion and two methods of preventing it.

Focus on the definition, rusting example and protective methods.

5 MARKS

Balance the given equations and identify their reaction types.

Always show the balanced equation clearly before naming the reaction type.

Rapid Revision

⚡ Definitions

  • Chemical reaction → chemical change producing new substances.
  • Combination → many reactants, one product.
  • Decomposition → one reactant, many products.
  • Displacement → one element displaces another.
  • Double displacement → exchange of ions/groups.
  • Oxidation → gain O / loss H.
  • Reduction → loss O / gain H.
  • Corrosion → deterioration of metals by surroundings.
  • Rancidity → oxidation of fats/oils.

🧪 Must-Know Equations

  • 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO
  • CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + Heat
  • CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
  • 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl₂
  • Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu
  • Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄ + 2NaCl
  • CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O
  • C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy

🧠 Mnemonics

  • SCGT → State, Colour, Gas, Temperature
  • EXO → Energy EXits
  • ENDO → Energy ENters
  • OIL RIG → Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain
  • A+B→AB → Combination
  • AB→A+B → Decomposition

  • Use state symbols when specifically required.
