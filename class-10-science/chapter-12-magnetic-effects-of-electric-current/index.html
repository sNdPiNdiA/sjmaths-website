01
Magnetic Field & Field Lines

A compass needle is a small magnet. The region around a magnet in which its magnetic force can be detected is called a magnetic field. Magnetic field lines represent the magnetic field. Outside a bar magnet they emerge from the north pole and enter the south pole; inside the magnet they go from south to north, forming closed curves.

  • Field lines show both direction and relative strength.
  • Closer field lines represent a stronger magnetic field.
  • No two magnetic field lines intersect because a compass needle cannot point in two directions at one point.
  • The tangent to a field line at a point gives the direction of the magnetic field there.
Oersted's observation: a current-carrying conductor can deflect a compass needle, showing the link between electricity and magnetism.
NCERT Activities 12.1–12.3: compass deflection demonstrates the magnetic effect of current; iron filings reveal the magnetic-field pattern around a bar magnet; and a compass can be moved step-by-step to trace magnetic field lines.
NCERT In-text Question 1 — Why does a compass needle get deflected when brought near a bar magnet?
Because the compass needle is itself a small magnet and the magnetic field of the bar magnet exerts a magnetic influence on it.
NCERT Questions
1. Draw magnetic field lines around a bar magnet.
Answer / Diagram guidance: Draw closed curves emerging from N and entering S outside the magnet, with arrows showing N→S outside and S→N inside. Make the lines denser near the poles.
2. List the properties of magnetic field lines.
Answer: They form closed curves; outside a magnet their direction is N→S and inside S→N; field strength is indicated by line density; and no two field lines intersect.
3. Why don’t two magnetic field lines intersect each other?
Answer: At an intersection the compass needle would have to point in two different directions at the same point, which is impossible.

02
Magnetic Field Due to a Current-Carrying Straight Conductor

A current-carrying straight wire produces magnetic field lines in the form of concentric circles around the conductor. The magnitude of the field at a point increases when current increases and decreases when the point is moved farther from the conductor.

Right-hand thumb rule: hold the conductor in the right hand with the thumb pointing in the direction of current; the curled fingers give the direction of the magnetic field. This is also called Maxwell's corkscrew rule.
Straight conductorConcentric magnetic field lines
Key ideaCurrent in the straight wire creates circular magnetic field lines. Reverse the current to reverse the field direction.
NCERT Activities 12.4–12.5: reversing the current reverses the compass deflection; increasing current increases the deflection; moving the compass farther away decreases the deflection.
Solved Example 12.1
Question: A current through a horizontal power line flows in east to west direction. What is the direction of magnetic field at a point directly below it and at a point directly above it?
Solution: Applying the right-hand thumb rule, the magnetic field at any point below or above the wire turns clockwise in a plane perpendicular to the wire when viewed from the east end, and anti-clockwise when viewed from the west end.
Related NCERT Exercise

03
Magnetic Field Due to a Current-Carrying Circular Loop

When a straight conductor is bent into a circular loop and current is passed through it, the magnetic field contributions from different parts of the loop add in the same direction at the centre. Near the centre, the circular arcs of the field pattern appear as approximately straight, parallel lines.

For a coil of n turns, the field is n times that of a single turn under the same conditions because the field due to each turn adds.

Answer guidance: Use the right-hand rule with the curled fingers following the clockwise current. The thumb gives the direction of the magnetic field through the centre, perpendicular to the plane of the loop; outside the loop the field lines curve back in the opposite direction to complete closed loops.

04
Magnetic Field Due to a Solenoid & Electromagnets

A solenoid is a coil of many circular turns of insulated wire wrapped closely in the shape of a cylinder. The magnetic field pattern around a current-carrying solenoid is similar to that of a bar magnet. One end behaves as a north pole and the other as a south pole.

Inside a long solenoid the field lines are parallel and equally spaced, indicating an approximately uniform magnetic field. A soft iron core placed inside the coil becomes magnetised strongly and produces an electromagnet.

SolenoidUniform interior field
Key ideaEach turn adds its field to the next. Inside a long solenoid, the lines become parallel and nearly uniform.

05
Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor & Fleming's Left-Hand Rule

A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force. The direction of the force depends on the direction of current and magnetic field. The force is greatest when current and magnetic field are at right angles. Reversing either the current or the field reverses the force direction.

Fleming's left-hand rule: stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the left hand mutually perpendicular. First finger → magnetic field; second finger → current; thumb → force/motion.
Solved Example 12.2
Question: An electron enters a magnetic field at right angles to it, as shown in Fig. 12.14. The direction of force acting on the electron will be (a) to the right. (b) to the left. (c) out of the page. (d) into the page.
Solution: Answer is option (d). The force is perpendicular to the direction of magnetic field and current as given by Fleming’s left-hand rule. The direction of current is opposite to the direction of motion of electrons, so the force is directed into the page.

06
Electric Motor

An electric motor converts electrical energy into mechanical energy. A current-carrying coil in a magnetic field experiences forces on its two sides that form a turning effect. The split-ring commutator reverses current after every half rotation so the rotation continues in the same direction; carbon brushes maintain contact.

Electric motorForce → torque → rotation
Key ideaThe magnetic field pushes opposite sides of the coil in opposite directions, producing torque and rotation.
07
Electromagnetic Induction

A changing magnetic field associated with a coil can induce current in the coil. A galvanometer detects the induced current. No induced current is observed when the magnetic flux remains unchanged.

08
Electric Generator

An electric generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy through electromagnetic induction. In an AC generator a rotating coil in a magnetic field produces an alternating output. Slip rings and carbon brushes provide electrical connection to the rotating coil.

AC generatorMechanical rotation → induced AC
Key ideaRotating the coil changes magnetic flux. That changing flux induces current; reverse rotation reverses the output.
09
Domestic Electric Circuits, Fuse, Earthing & Safety

Domestic supply uses live, neutral and earth connections. In the supplied NCERT text, the potential difference is 220 V; the chapter summary also states an AC supply frequency of 50 Hz. Separate circuits may be used for higher-power and lower-power appliances. Appliances are connected in parallel so they receive the same potential difference and can be operated independently.

The earth wire provides a low-resistance path for leakage current from metallic appliance bodies and reduces electric-shock risk. A fuse protects a circuit against excessive current. In short-circuiting, the current can rise abruptly; Joule heating melts the fuse and breaks the circuit. Overloading can also result from connecting too many appliances to one socket or from an accidental rise in supply voltage.

NCERT In-text Questions
1. Name two safety measures commonly used in electric circuits and appliances.
2. An electric oven of 2 kW power rating is operated in a domestic electric circuit (220 V) that has a current rating of 5 A. What result do you expect? Explain.
Solution: The oven current is I = P/V = 2000/220 ≈ 9.09 A, which is greater than the circuit rating of 5 A. The circuit is therefore overloaded; the protective fuse/device should operate to prevent damage.

Revision — One Screen Before the Exam

Rules, diagrams and high-frequency relationships

R
Magnetic field essentials
  • Field lines outside a bar magnet: North → South; inside: South → North.
  • Field-line crowding indicates stronger magnetic field.
  • No two magnetic field lines intersect.
  • Straight current-carrying conductor: concentric circular field lines.
  • Right-hand thumb rule gives field direction.
M
Motor + generator
Motor: electrical energy → mechanical energy. Principle: force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. Split ring reverses current every half rotation.
Generator: mechanical energy → electrical energy. Principle: electromagnetic induction. AC generator uses slip rings and produces alternating current.
S
Domestic circuit safety
  • Parallel connection lets appliances operate independently.
  • Fuse is placed in the live wire.
  • Earthing provides a safe path for leakage current.
  • Do not overload sockets or bypass protective devices.
