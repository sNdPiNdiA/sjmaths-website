Concepts & Visual Theory

30 concepts • dedicated SVG visual for every concept

01

Variation

WHERE VARIATION COMES FROM

Reproduction creates individuals that are similar but subtly different. In later generations, inherited differences can combine with newly created differences.

Remember: Inherited + new variation → diversity.
02

Accumulation of variation

OVER SUCCESSIVE GENERATIONS

Asexual reproduction can produce minor differences through small inaccuracies in DNA copying; sexual reproduction can generate greater diversity.

Remember: More generations → more variation.
03

Variation and survival

ENVIRONMENT CAN CHANGE THE ADVANTAGE

Different variants have different survival chances in different environments. A useful variation can improve survival.

Remember: Environment can favour a variant.
04

Heredity

HEREDITY = TRANSMISSION

Heredity is the process by which traits and characteristics are reliably inherited from parents to offspring.

Remember: Heredity = transmission of traits.
05

Inherited traits

MENDEL'S CONTRASTING PEA CHARACTERS

Both father and mother contribute genetic material, so a trait can be influenced by paternal and maternal DNA.

Remember: Two parental contributions.
06

Mendel's experiment

P → F₁

Mendel used contrasting pea characters such as tall/short, round/wrinkled and white/violet, crossed plants and counted offspring.

Remember: Cross + count + compare.
07

F1 generation

CONCEPT MAP

Tall × short pea plants produced all tall F1 plants; there were no medium-height plants.

Remember: F1: all tall.
08

F2 generation

WHAT DOMINANT / RECESSIVE MEANS HERE

Self-pollination of F1 tall plants produced F2 offspring including short plants; about one quarter were short.

Remember: F2: 3 tall : 1 short.
09

Genes

GENE = A SECTION OF DNA

Mendel's hereditary factors are now called genes. Two copies controlling a trait are present in sexually reproducing organisms.

Remember: Two gene copies.
10

Dominant trait

GENOTYPE → PHENOTYPE

In the pea example, T is dominant because one copy is enough for tallness.

Remember: TT and Tt → tall.
11

Recessive trait

CONCEPT MAP

In the pea example, t is recessive because shortness appears when both copies are t.

Remember: tt → short.
12

Monohybrid cross

HOW A GENE CAN AFFECT PLANT HEIGHT

Tt × Tt produces TT, Tt, Tt and tt, giving a 1:2:1 genotype ratio and 3:1 phenotype ratio.

Remember: Genotype 1:2:1; phenotype 3:1.
13

Gene expression

TWO PARENTS, TWO SOURCES OF GENETIC MATERIAL

DNA provides information for proteins. Proteins and enzymes can influence biochemical pathways and therefore characteristics.

Remember: DNA → protein → trait.
14

Plant height example

A CHROMOSOME PAIR

Efficient enzyme action can produce more growth hormone and tallness; less efficient action can produce less hormone and shortness.

Remember: Gene → enzyme → hormone → height.
15

Equal genetic contribution

WHY GAMETES CARRY ONE FROM EACH PAIR

Both parents contribute practically equal amounts of genetic material through sexual reproduction.

Remember: Father + mother ≈ equal.
16

Chromosomes

TWO TRAITS CAN RECOMBINE

Genes are organised on separate DNA pieces called chromosomes. Cells have two copies of each chromosome, one from each parent.

Remember: Chromosomes carry genes.
17

Germ cells

TWO TRAITS CAN RECOMBINE

Each germ cell takes one chromosome from each pair. Fusion restores the normal chromosome number.

Remember: One from each pair → fusion restores.
18

Independent inheritance

A TRAIT CAN BE PRESENT BUT NOT EXPRESSED

Mendel's two-trait experiment produced new combinations of seed shape and colour in F2, showing independent inheritance.

Remember: Two traits can recombine.
19

Dihybrid cross

ASEXUAL REPRODUCTION

Tall round × short wrinkled produced F1 tall round plants; F2 contained parental and new combinations.

Remember: 9:3:3:1 phenotype ratio.
20

Sex determination in species

SEXUAL REPRODUCTION

Different organisms use different mechanisms; some reptiles use temperature and some snails can change sex.

Remember: Mechanism differs by species.
21

Human sex chromosomes

KEEP THE TERMS DISTINCT

Humans have 22 chromosome pairs plus a sex-chromosome pair. Women are XX and men are XY in the source.

Remember: XX = female; XY = male.
22

Father's X/Y contribution

HUMAN SEX CHROMOSOMES

The mother contributes X to every child. The father contributes X or Y, so the paternal chromosome determines XX or XY.

Remember: Mother X fixed; father X/Y.
23

1:1 expectation

EVERY EGG CARRIES X

The human cross gives approximately half XX and half XY outcomes.

Remember: ≈ 50% : 50%.
24

Variation over generations

SPERM CAN CARRY X OR Y

Each generation inherits differences and can acquire additional differences during reproduction.

Remember: Inherited + new.
25

Environmental selection

EXPECTED OUTCOME

The chapter uses heat-resistant bacteria to show that some variants survive better when conditions change.

Remember: Useful variation → survival.
26

Blood-group caution

ENVIRONMENT CAN CHANGE THE ADVANTAGE

One family observation is not enough to establish dominance; an appropriate inheritance pattern is needed.

Remember: One observation ≠ proof.
27

Genotype vs phenotype

KEEP THE TERMS DISTINCT

Genotype is the genetic combination such as TT, Tt or tt. Phenotype is the expressed characteristic such as tall or short.

Remember: Genotype ≠ phenotype.
28

Dominant vs recessive

KEEP THE TERMS DISTINCT

Dominant describes an expressed version in the Mendelian example; it does not mean biologically superior.

Remember: Dominant ≠ better.
29

Chapter synthesis

KEEP THE TERMS DISTINCT

Variation can be inherited; Mendel explains dominance and independent inheritance; genes are DNA sections; chromosomes organise gene sets; human sex depends on paternal X/Y contribution.

Remember: Variation → heredity → genes → chromosomes → XX/XY.

Exam cross method

Remember: P-G-O-R-C.

NCERT Questions & Answers

Questions explicitly present in the uploaded Chapter 8 source.

Chapter Q1

If trait A is in 10% and trait B in 60% of an asexually reproducing species, which likely arose earlier?

Answer: Trait B is more likely to have arisen earlier because it occurs in a larger proportion of the population, assuming it has been inherited through successive generations.
Chapter Q2

How does creation of variations promote survival?

Answer: Some variations can provide an advantage in particular environmental conditions, allowing those individuals to survive and reproduce more successfully.
Chapter Q3

How do Mendel's experiments show dominance and recessiveness?

Answer: Tall × short produced all tall F1 plants, while short plants reappeared in F2. Tallness was expressed and shortness was recessive in the example.
Chapter Q4

How do Mendel's experiments show independent inheritance?

Answer: F2 included new combinations of seed shape and seed colour, showing that the two traits can be inherited independently.
Chapter Q5

Is an A-blood-group man, O-blood-group woman and O-blood-group daughter enough to decide dominance?

Answer: No. One family observation is not enough to establish dominance; appropriate inheritance patterns and genotypes must be considered.
Chapter Q6

How is sex determined in human beings?

Answer: The mother contributes X to every child. The father contributes X or Y. XX gives a girl and XY gives a boy.
