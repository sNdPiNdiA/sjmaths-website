Social Control

Social Control is a fundamental sociological concept examining the mechanisms, norms, and sanctions through which society maintains social order, regulates individual behavior, and prevents deviance. Crucial for UP PGT Sociology and UGC NET, it spans classical macro-level integration theories by Émile Durkheim and Talcott Parsons to micro-level compliance studied by Indian sociologists within institutional frameworks like caste, kinship, and religion.

UP PGT Sociology • Code 16 Section 8

1. Definitional Framework, Etymology & Classical Foundations

The term Social Control (from Latin socialis meaning companion/society, and controllum from medieval Latin contra-rotulum referring to checking accounts) was first popularized in American sociology by Edward Alsworth Ross in his seminal 1901 work Social Control: A Survey of the Foundations of Order. Ross argued that belief systems, public opinion, suggestion, and religion are far more potent in maintaining social order than physical coercion by the state.

  • Émile Durkheim & Solidarity: Durkheim examined social control through the lens of collective conscience (conscience collective) and moral regulation. In The Division of Labour in Society (1893), he contrasted mechanical solidarity (repressive laws, high collective consciousness) with organic solidarity (restitutive laws, interdependence, specialized division of labor). Deviance is defined as an infraction against the collective conscience.
  • Max Weber & Legitimate Domination: Weber analyzed social control through power (Macht) and authority (Herrschaft). He established three ideal types of legitimate domination: Traditional, Charismatic, and Legal-Rational authority, which form the bedrock of institutionalized compliance and bureaucratic control.
  • Talcott Parsons & Structural Functionalism: In The Social System (1951), Parsons treated social control as a core functional prerequisite. Control mechanisms operate through socialization (internalization of norms) and institutionalized sanctions (rewards and punishments) to counteract deviance and restore equilibrium.

2. Structural Concepts, Empirical Processes & Indian Sociological Context

Sociologists classify social control into primary and secondary modalities. Formal social control is codified, enacted by authorized agents of the state (law, police, judiciary, bureaucracy), whereas informal social control relies on unwritten customs, folkways, mores, gossip, ostracism, and public opinion.

  • G.S. Ghurye & Caste Regulation: Ghurye's indological and historical approach in Caste and Race in India (1932) highlighted how endogamy, commensal restrictions, and Brahmanical hegemony functioned as rigid indigenous mechanisms of social control in traditional Hindu society.
  • M.N. Srinivas & Dominant Caste: In his studies of Rampura village and works like Religion and Society Among the Coorgs of South India (1952), Srinivas demonstrated how the 'dominant caste' exercises local socio-political and economic control, enforcing village morality and caste panchayat decrees.
  • Radhakamal Mukerjee & Social Values: Mukerjee argued in The Social Structure of Values (1949) that institutionalized human values provide the ultimate internalized framework of social control, bridging biological drives with societal ideals.

3. Contemporary Dynamics, Social Policy & Applied Dimensions

In modern industrial and democratic societies, traditional informal controls (kinship, caste councils, village elders) undergo transformation or conflict with formal legal frameworks. The rise of constitutionalism, fundamental rights, and democratic legislation in India has sought to dismantle oppressive informal controls (such as untouchability, honor killings by Khap Panchayats, and child marriage) while instituting modern protective legislation.

  • State Legislation as Social Control: Acts such as the Untouchability Offences Act (1955, later Protection of Civil Rights Act), Dowry Prohibition Act (1961), and the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act (2005) represent formal state-sponsored social control aimed at social reform.
  • Deviance and Social Pathology: Sociological theories of deviance—such as Robert K. Merton's Strain Theory (Anomie), Edwin Sutherland's Differential Association Theory, and Howard Becker's Labeling Theory—explain how breakdowns in social control lead to deviant subcultures and criminal behavior.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Sociology

  • Edward Alsworth Ross coined the term 'Social Control' in his 1901 book 'Social Control: A Survey of the Foundations of Order'.
  • Émile Durkheim differentiated between mechanical solidarity (repressive law/control) and organic solidarity (restitutive law/control) in 'The Division of Labour in Society' (1893).
  • G.S. Ghurye outlined six cardinal features of caste society acting as structural controls in 'Caste and Race in India' (1932).
  • M.N. Srinivas introduced the concepts of 'Dominant Caste' and Sanskritization, explaining how local social control is mediated through ritual and economic power.
  • Talcott Parsons viewed social control as a core imperative of the social system alongside adaptation, goal attainment, and latency (AGIL schema).
  • Informal controls rely on folkways and mores, terms originally formulated by William Graham Sumner in his 1906 classic 'Folkways'.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Confusing E.A. Ross with Herbert Spencer as the coiner of 'Social Control'. Spencer is famous for social evolution and organic analogy, whereas Ross coined social control.
  • Assuming social control is always negative or coercive. Sociologically, social control includes positive sanctions (rewards, praise, honors) as well as negative sanctions (punishments, ostracism).

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Test your conceptual understanding. Select an answer and check the detailed pedagogical rationale.

Q1 Who among the following American sociologists first used and popularized the term 'Social Control' in 1901?
  • A Talcott Parsons
  • B Edward Alsworth Ross
  • C William Graham Sumner
  • D Robert K. Merton
Correct Option: B (Edward Alsworth Ross)

E.A. Ross formally introduced the concept of social control in his 1901 book 'Social Control: A Survey of the Foundations of Order', arguing that non-rational psychological factors like belief and public opinion maintain order.

Q2 Which theorist associated mechanical solidarity with repressive laws and penal sanctions as a mechanism of social control?
  • A Max Weber
  • B Karl Marx
  • C Émile Durkheim
  • D Vilfredo Pareto
Correct Option: C (Émile Durkheim)

Émile Durkheim, in 'The Division of Labour in Society' (1893), argued that mechanical solidarity is maintained by repressive laws that punish infractions against a strong collective conscience.

Q3 What distinguishes formal social control from informal social control?
  • A Formal control is unwritten and based on custom.
  • B Formal control is enforced by codified laws and designated state agencies.
  • C Formal control operates exclusively in primitive hunting and gathering tribes.
  • D Formal control relies entirely on gossip and ridicule.
Correct Option: B (Formal control is enforced by codified laws and designated state agencies.)

Formal social control is characterized by explicit rules, written laws, and enforcement by specialized state agencies like police, courts, and administrative bodies.

Q4 Who authored the 1906 sociological classic 'Folkways', which detailed the folkways and mores that serve as primary instruments of informal social control?
  • A Kingsley Davis
  • B William Graham Sumner
  • C Robert Redfield
  • D Herbert Spencer
Correct Option: B (William Graham Sumner)

William Graham Sumner wrote 'Folkways' (1906), establishing the foundational distinction between folkways (habits/customs) and mores (moral imperatives) as informal controls.

Q5 In Talcott Parsons' structural functionalist framework, social control operates primarily to:
  • A Promote violent revolution and class struggle
  • B Counteract deviance and restore equilibrium to the social system
  • C Abolish all legal institutions in favor of pure individualism
  • D Maintain absolute economic inequality without normative regulation
Correct Option: B (Counteract deviance and restore equilibrium to the social system)

For Talcott Parsons, social control mechanisms are institutionalized processes designed to curb deviance, manage strain, and preserve systemic equilibrium.

Q6 Which Indian sociologist extensively analyzed caste endogamy, commensal restrictions, and Brahmanical supremacy as institutional controls in traditional Indian society?
  • A M.N. Srinivas
  • B D.P. Mukerji
  • C G.S. Ghurye
  • D Radhakamal Mukerjee
Correct Option: C (G.S. Ghurye)

G.S. Ghurye, in 'Caste and Race in India' (1932), provided the definitive indological analysis of the six structural features of the caste system that functioned as rigorous mechanisms of social control.

Q7 According to Max Weber, which type of authority forms the foundation of legal-rational social control in modern bureaucracies?
  • A Traditional Authority
  • B Charismatic Authority
  • C Legal-Rational Authority
  • D Patriarchal Authority
Correct Option: C (Legal-Rational Authority)

Legal-rational authority, based on enacted rules and laws, is the cornerstone of modern bureaucratic systems of social control as analyzed by Max Weber.

Q8 Which of the following is classified as an informal sanction used for social control?
  • A Imprisonment
  • B Capital punishment
  • C Public gossip and social ridicule
  • D A statutory fine imposed by a magistrate
Correct Option: C (Public gossip and social ridicule)

Public gossip, ridicule, and social ostracism are informal sanctions because they are uncodified and enforced by primary groups rather than judicial courts.

Q9 Robert K. Merton's theory of Anomie and Strain deals primarily with which sociological phenomenon related to social control?
  • A The biological evolution of primate hierarchy
  • B Deviant behavior arising when cultural goals are blocked by institutional means
  • C The ceremonial rituals of Totemism in Australia
  • D The circulation of political elites in democratic states
Correct Option: B (Deviant behavior arising when cultural goals are blocked by institutional means)

Merton's strain theory explains how structural limitations on legitimate means to achieve cultural goals produce deviant adaptations (innovation, ritualism, retreatism, rebellion).

Q10 What is the primary role of Khap Panchayats in certain regions of North India when analyzing informal social control?
  • A Acting as official High Court branch offices
  • B Operating as traditional caste/community councils enforcing customary norms against inter-caste marriages
  • C Implementing formal central government fiscal policy
  • D Conducting democratic panchayati raj elections
Correct Option: B (Operating as traditional caste/community councils enforcing customary norms against inter-caste marriages)

Khap panchayats function as traditional, extra-constitutional caste councils that exert informal social control, often enforcing customary norms and endogamy through severe extralegal diktats.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who coined the term 'Social Control' and in which book?

The term was coined by American sociologist Edward Alsworth Ross in his 1901 book 'Social Control: A Survey of the Foundations of Order'.

What is the core difference between formal and informal social control?

Formal social control is deliberate, codified, and enforced by official state institutions (laws, police, courts). Informal social control is unwritten, spontaneous, and enforced by primary groups through customs, folkways, gossip, and moral pressure.

How does Émile Durkheim relate social control to division of labour?

Durkheim argued that mechanical solidarity relies on repressive laws and strong collective conscience to control deviance, whereas organic solidarity relies on restitutive laws and functional interdependence to maintain social cohesion.

Why is the study of social control important for UP PGT Sociology examinations?

It bridges classical theory (Durkheim, Weber, Parsons) with Indian sociological realities (caste panchayats, kinship rules, state legislation, and social change), making it a high-yield topic frequently tested through conceptual and applied MCQs.
