Section 13 • Changes in Indian Society

Globalization

Globalization refers to the compression of the world and the intensification of worldwide social relations, linking distant localities in such a way that local happenings are shaped by events occurring miles away. In the context of UP PGT Sociology and Indian society, it represents a profound structural shift extending beyond economic liberalization (1991) to cultural homogenization, hybridization, and the reconfiguration of traditional institutions like caste, family, and religion.

1. Definitional Framework, Etymology & Classical Foundations

Etymologically derived from the root word global (combining the Latin globus meaning spherical mass with the suffix -ization indicating a process), globalization denotes the acceleration and intensification of global interconnectedness. While often perceived as a contemporary economic phenomenon, its sociological roots trace back to classical paradigms:

  • Karl Marx & Friedrich Engels (1848): In The Communist Manifesto, Marx anticipated globalization by highlighting how the need for a constantly expanding market chases the bourgeoisie over the entire surface of the globe, tearing down national barriers and universalizing production and consumption.
  • Émile Durkheim: Explored global interdependence through his concept of the expanding Division of Labour (1893), where planetary scale specialization deepens organic solidarity across societies.
  • Max Weber: Addressed global rationalization, disenchantment, and the worldwide spread of the 'Iron Cage' of bureaucratic capitalism and instrumental rationality.
  • Contemporary Theorists: Roland Robertson coined the term glocalization to describe the interpenetration of the global and the local; Anthony Giddens defined it as 'time-space distanciation'; and David Harvey analyzed 'time-space compression'.

2. Structural Concepts, Empirical Processes & Indian Sociological Context

The impact of globalization on Indian society has been scrutinized extensively by Indian sociologists who examine how exogenous economic forces interact with endogenous traditions:

  • Yogendra Singh (1986, Modernization of Indian Tradition): Argued that globalization accelerates the modernization of Indian tradition, shifting cultural patterns toward universalism, achievement orientation, and secularization without entirely erasing primordial identities.
  • M.N. Srinivas: Explored how Westernization and modernization under globalization provide new vectors of mobility, altering the traditional dynamics of the Dominant Caste and Sanskritization.
  • Economic & Agrarian Transformations: Post-1991 New Economic Policy (LPG: Liberalization, Privatization, Globalization) led to agrarian crises, farmer suicides, corporate farming, and the growth of the informal sector and urban gig economies.
  • Cultural Dimensions: Arjun Appadurai's dimensions of global cultural flow—ethnoscapes, mediascapes, technoscapes, financescapes, and ideoscapes—provide the theoretical lens to understand hybrid cultural identities in urban India.

3. Contemporary Dynamics, Social Policy & Applied Dimensions

Globalization generates intense dialectical tensions between homogenization and cultural resistance, manifested in contemporary social policy and collective behavior:

  • Identity Politics & Fundamentalism: As local cultures feel threatened by Western cultural hegemony and consumerism, there is often a resurgence of religious nationalism, communalism, and ethnocentric revivalism.
  • Gender and Labor: The feminization of labor in export-processing zones, call centers, and domestic service coexists with patriarchal backlashes and new forms of exploitation.
  • State Sovereignty & Welfare: Neoliberal globalization has recalibrated the welfare state, leading to the dismantling of public sector monopolies, deregulation, and the rise of public-private partnerships (PPPs) in education, health, and infrastructure.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Sociology

  • Roland Robertson coined and popularized the sociological concept of 'Glocalization' in the early 1990s.
  • George Ritzer authored The McDonaldization of Society in 1993, emphasizing calculability, predictability, efficiency, and control.
  • India officially initiated structural adjustment programs and the LPG (Liberalization, Privatization, Globalization) model in July 1991 under Prime Minister P.V. Narasimha Rao and Finance Minister Manmohan Singh.
  • Arjun Appadurai conceptualized global cultural flows through five 'scapes': ethnoscapes, mediascapes, technoscapes, financescapes, and ideoscapes in Modernity at Large (1996).
  • Yogendra Singh's seminal work Modernization of Indian Tradition (1986) analyzes how exogenous cultural forces interact with indigenous social structures.
  • Anthony Giddens defines globalization as 'time-space distanciation'—the intensification of worldwide social relations which link distant localities.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Students often assume globalization is purely an economic phenomenon starting in 1991. Sociologically, it is a multidimensional process involving cultural, political, and technological dimensions with historical roots stretching back to colonialism and early capitalism.
  • Misconception 2: Confusing cultural imperialism with complete homogenization. Sociological analysis shows that receptor cultures actively resist, negotiate, or indigenize global influences rather than passively absorbing them.

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Test your conceptual understanding. Select an answer and check the detailed pedagogical rationale.

Q1 Who among the following sociologists coined the term 'Glocalization' to describe the blending of global and local realities?
  • A Anthony Giddens
  • B Roland Robertson
  • C David Harvey
  • D George Ritzer
Correct Option: B (Roland Robertson)

Roland Robertson introduced the term 'glocalization' into sociological discourse to signify how global forces are adapted to fit local conditions, borrowing from the Japanese word 'dotojoka'.

Q2 What does George Ritzer's concept of 'McDonaldization' primarily signify?
  • A The worldwide spread of democratic governance
  • B The application of principles of fast-food restaurants to all spheres of social life
  • C The revival of indigenous agricultural practices
  • D The decline of multinational corporations
Correct Option: B (The application of principles of fast-food restaurants to all spheres of social life)

George Ritzer's McDonaldization refers to the process by which the principles of the fast-food restaurant are coming to dominate more and more sectors of American society as well as the rest of the world, characterized by efficiency, calculability, predictability, and control.

Q3 Arjun Appadurai's concept of 'scapes' includes which of the following dimensions?
  • A Ethnoscapes, Technoscapes, Financescapes, Mediascapes, Ideoscapes
  • B Geoscapes, Bioscapes, Stratoscapes, Econoscapes, Politicoscapes
  • C Demoscapes, Agro-scapes, Industro-scapes, Cyber-scapes, Ethno-scapes
  • D Socioscapes, Physioscapes, Psychoscapes, Technoscapes, Geospaces
Correct Option: A (Ethnoscapes, Technoscapes, Financescapes, Mediascapes, Ideoscapes)

In 'Modernity at Large' (1996), Arjun Appadurai proposes five dimensions of global cultural flow: ethnoscapes, mediascapes, technoscapes, financescapes, and ideoscapes.

Q4 Which year marked the formal introduction of the New Economic Policy (LPG model) in India?
  • A 1984
  • B 1991
  • C 1996
  • D 2000
Correct Option: B (1991)

India introduced sweeping economic reforms under the New Economic Policy in July 1991, dismantling the License Raj and opening the economy to Liberalization, Privatization, and Globalization.

Q5 Who authored the book 'Modernization of Indian Tradition' (1986)?
  • A M.N. Srinivas
  • B D.P. Mukerji
  • C Yogendra Singh
  • D S.C. Dube
Correct Option: C (Yogendra Singh)

Yogendra Singh wrote 'Modernization of Indian Tradition' (1986), a foundational text in Indian sociology examining the structural and cultural changes brought about by modernization and globalization.

Q6 According to Anthony Giddens, globalization is best understood as:
  • A A purely economic conspiracy by Western nations
  • B Time-space distanciation
  • C The complete erasure of national borders
  • D The biological evolution of human societies
Correct Option: B (Time-space distanciation)

Anthony Giddens defines globalization as 'time-space distanciation,' meaning the acceleration and intensification of social relations across vast geographic distances, linking local and global events.

Q7 Which sociological perspective views globalization primarily as a form of cultural imperialism and capitalist expansion?
  • A Structural Functionalism
  • B Symbolic Interactionism
  • C Conflict / Marxist Perspective
  • D Ethnomethodology
Correct Option: C (Conflict / Marxist Perspective)

Conflict theorists and Marxist sociologists view globalization as the expansion of global capitalism, leading to cultural imperialism, exploitation of peripheral labor, and widening global inequalities.

Q8 The term 'time-space compression', denoting how globalization accelerates communication and transport, was popularized by:
  • A David Harvey
  • B Max Weber
  • C Émile Durkheim
  • D Auguste Comte
Correct Option: A (David Harvey)

Geographer and social theorist David Harvey coined 'time-space compression' in 'The Condition of Postmodernity' (1989) to describe processes that make the world feel smaller and distances shorter.

Q9 How has globalization impacted the traditional Indian caste system?
  • A It completely eradicated caste identities across rural India
  • B It created new avenues of secular mobility, professional stratification, and political mobilization alongside traditional caste solidarities
  • C It strictly reinforced ritual purity and pollution without any economic shifts
  • D It replaced caste exclusively with religious fundamentalism
Correct Option: B (It created new avenues of secular mobility, professional stratification, and political mobilization alongside traditional caste solidarities)

Sociological studies show that while globalization opens up non-traditional occupations and urban mobility, caste identities are simultaneously repurposed for political mobilization and corporate networking.

Q10 Hyperglobalizers argue that globalization represents:
  • A A passing political fad with little structural consequence
  • B A new era in which national governments and borders are increasingly irrelevant
  • C A return to 19th-century mercantilism
  • D An entirely localized phenomenon confined to North America
Correct Option: B (A new era in which national governments and borders are increasingly irrelevant)

Hyperglobalizers believe that globalization is a real, irreversible phenomenon creating a single global market where nation-states lose their sovereign authority and economic control.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Westernization and Globalization in Indian Sociology?

M.N. Srinivas defined Westernization as the changes brought about in Indian society due to over 150 years of British rule, focusing on technology, dress, values, and education. Globalization is a broader, contemporary planetary process involving multidirectional flows of capital, culture, technology, and media well beyond Western dominance, though Western cultural hegemony remains a significant component.

Why is Yogendra Singh's work crucial for understanding globalization in UP PGT exams?

Yogendra Singh provides the quintessential sociological framework for analyzing how indigenous Indian traditions absorb modernization and globalization. Rather than simple Western imitation, Indian tradition undergoes structural adaptation, which is a frequently tested topic in competitive sociological examinations.
