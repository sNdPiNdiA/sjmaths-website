Universalization in Indian Society

Universalization is a foundational concept in Indian sociology formulated by McKim Marriott to explain the upward vertical spread and transformation of little tradition elements into the great tradition. Essential for UP PGT Sociology and UGC NET, it captures the dynamic cultural integration between local folk practices and pan-Indian classical traditions.

UP PGT Sociology • Code 16 Section 13

1. Definitional Framework, Etymology & Classical Foundations

The concept of Universalization originates from Robert Redfield's seminal civilizational framework distinguishing between the Little Tradition (folk, peasant, unreflective local culture) and the Great Tradition (reflective, classical, elite literate culture). McKim Marriott operationalized and expanded this dichotomy through his intensive empirical study of Kishan Garhi village in Uttar Pradesh, published in his classic essay 'Little Communities in an Indigenous Civilization' (1955).

  • Etymological Root: Derived from the Latin universalis (belonging to all, general), referring to the process whereby particular local traits become incorporated into the broader, all-encompassing institutional network of a civilization.
  • Marriott's Formulation: Universalization is defined as a two-way process of cultural transmission where elements of the local (little) tradition are communicated upwards, accepted, and reinterpreted into the pan-Indian great tradition.
  • Complementary Process: It operates dialectically alongside Parochialization—the downward process where great tradition elements are localized, filtered, and indigenized by local communities. Together, Marriott terms these twin dynamics Cultural-Social Interaction.

2. Structural Concepts, Empirical Processes & Indian Sociological Context

In the sociology of Indian traditions, universalization explains how localized deities, rituals, and kinship practices acquire pan-Hindu or national validity. For instance, a village deity (Gramadevata) like Naugaja or local ghosts and spirits may become identified with classical Puranic deities such as Shiva, Vishnu, or Devi through the reinterpretation of local myths.

  • Yogendra Singh's Perspective: In Modernisation of Indian Tradition (1973), Singh incorporates universalization into the cumulative structural changes of Indian society, noting that cultural universalization facilitates structural integration and national consensus.
  • M.N. Srinivas & Sanskritization Link: While Sanskritization focuses on emulation of upper-caste behavioral patterns by lower castes, Universalization focuses on the structural transformation of cultural contents across levels of civilization as conceptualized by Redfield and Marriott.
  • D.P. Mukerji's Dialectics: Emphasized that Indian traditions are not static; the interaction between local and classical traditions constitutes the very dialectic of Indian social history (Modern Indian Culture, 1942).

3. Contemporary Dynamics, Social Policy & Applied Dimensions

In contemporary India, the process of universalization is vastly accelerated by mass media, electronic communication, constitutional integration, and commercialized religious festivals. Regional folk festivals like Durga Puja in West Bengal, Ganesh Utsav in Maharashtra, and Chhath Puja in Bihar have undergone massive universalization, transforming into pan-national or global spectacles.

  • Globalization & Mass Culture: Globalization acts as a hyper-universalizing agency, blending local indigenous knowledge systems (IKT) with global consumer markets.
  • Policy Implications: State-sponsored cultural integration policies and tourism boards utilize universalization to promote national unity and cultural heritage tourism.

Memory Shortcut / Mnemonic
The Marriott-Redfield Twin Processes
UP = Upward Universalization / DOWN = Downward Parochialization

Remember that Universalization (U) always moves UP from the Little Tradition to the Great Tradition, whereas Parochialization (P) moves down from the Great to the Little tradition.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Sociology

  • McKim Marriott coined the concepts of Universalization and Parochialization in his essay 'Little Communities in an Indigenous Civilization' (1955).
  • Robert Redfield formulated the foundational dichotomy of 'Little Tradition' and 'Great Tradition' based on his study of Mesoamerican civilizations.
  • Universalization is a bottom-up process where local cultural elements are incorporated into the pan-Indian civilization.
  • Parochialization is the complementary top-down process where classical elements are localized.
  • Yogendra Singh integrated these civilizational concepts into his comprehensive work 'Modernisation of Indian Tradition' (1973).
  • McKim Marriott conducted his famous empirical village study in Kishan Garhi, Uttar Pradesh.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Students often confuse Universalization with Westernization. Universalization is an indigenous civilizational process occurring between local and classical traditions, whereas Westernization denotes the impact of Western (British/modern) values and technology on Indian society.
  • Misconception 2: Confusing Universalization with Parochialization in exam questions. Always check the direction of cultural flow: upward vs. downward.

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Test your conceptual understanding. Select an answer and check the detailed pedagogical rationale.

Q1 Who among the following sociologists introduced the concepts of 'Universalization' and 'Parochialization'?
  • A M.N. Srinivas
  • B McKim Marriott
  • C S.C. Dube
  • D Milton Singer
Correct Option: B (McKim Marriott)

McKim Marriott introduced the dual concepts of Universalization and Parochialization in his 1955 study of Kishan Garhi village, building upon Robert Redfield's 'Little and Great Traditions' framework. Therefore, Option 1 is correct.

Q2 In which village did McKim Marriott conduct his intensive ethnographic fieldwork to formulate his theories on Indian traditions?
  • A Rampura
  • B Shamirpet
  • C Kishan Garhi
  • D Sripuram
Correct Option: C (Kishan Garhi)

McKim Marriott conducted his fieldwork in Kishan Garhi, a village in Aligarh district, Uttar Pradesh. Rampura was studied by M.N. Srinivas, and Shamirpet by S.C. Dube.

Q3 Universalization is best described as which of the following cultural processes?
  • A Downward movement from Great Tradition to Little Tradition
  • B Upward movement from Little Tradition to Great Tradition
  • C Total replacement of indigenous culture by Western culture
  • D Isolation of tribal communities from mainstream civilization
Correct Option: B (Upward movement from Little Tradition to Great Tradition)

Universalization refers to the upward transmission and incorporation of local (little tradition) elements into the pan-Indian (great tradition). Downward movement is Parochialization.

Q4 Who originally formulated the theoretical concepts of 'Little Tradition' and 'Great Tradition' before Marriott applied them to India?
  • A Louis Dumont
  • B Robert Redfield
  • C Milton Singer
  • D G.S. Ghurye
Correct Option: B (Robert Redfield)

Robert Redfield developed the dichotomy of Little and Great Traditions during his anthropological studies in Mexico, which McKim Marriott and Milton Singer later applied to Indian civilization.

Q5 Which sociologist authored the influential book 'Modernisation of Indian Tradition' (1973)?
  • A D.P. Mukerji
  • B Yogendra Singh
  • C A.R. Desai
  • D T.N. Madan
Correct Option: B (Yogendra Singh)

Yogendra Singh authored 'Modernisation of Indian Tradition' (1973), where he systematically analyzed the structural and cultural changes in India, including Sanskritization, Westernization, and Universalization.

Q6 Which of the following pairs is correctly matched regarding civilizational studies in India?
  • A McKim Marriott - Kishan Garhi
  • B M.N. Srinivas - Shamirpet
  • C S.C. Dube - Rampura
  • D D.P. Mukerji - Sripuram
Correct Option: A (McKim Marriott - Kishan Garhi)

McKim Marriott studied Kishan Garhi (UP). M.N. Srinivas studied Rampura (Mysore), S.C. Dube studied Shamirpet (Andhra Pradesh), and Andre Beteille studied Sripuram (Tamil Nadu).

Q7 When a local village deity is reinterpreted and assimilated into the pan-Indian Puranic pantheon, this process exemplifies:
  • A Parochialization
  • B Universalization
  • C Secularization
  • D Industrialization
Correct Option: B (Universalization)

The elevation of a local deity to the status of a great classical deity represents Universalization, as local cultural elements move upwards into the great tradition.

Q8 According to McKim Marriott, Universalization and Parochialization are parts of what broader sociological phenomenon?
  • A Class Struggle
  • B Cultural-Social Interaction
  • C Social Darwinism
  • D Bureaucratic Rationalization
Correct Option: B (Cultural-Social Interaction)

Marriott viewed Universalization and Parochialization as dual, complementary aspects of continuous cultural-social interaction within an indigenous civilization.

Q9 Which concept coined by M.N. Srinivas shares a conceptual overlap with upward cultural mobility, though focused primarily on caste emulation?
  • A Westernization
  • B Sanskritization
  • C Secularization
  • D Universalization
Correct Option: B (Sanskritization)

Sanskritization refers to the process by which lower castes emulate the customs, rituals, and lifestyle of upper castes to elevate their ritual status, sharing an upward mobility dynamic with Universalization.

Q10 What is the primary methodological approach used by McKim Marriott and Robert Redfield in studying civilizations?
  • A Purely statistical survey method
  • B Civilizational and ethnographic approach
  • C Experimental laboratory method
  • D Strictly historical archival research
Correct Option: B (Civilizational and ethnographic approach)

Marriott and Redfield utilized civilizational frameworks combined with intensive micro-ethnographic fieldwork to study the interplay between folk and classical traditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Universalization and Sanskritization?

Sanskritization (coined by M.N. Srinivas) is primarily concerned with caste emulation and the adoption of upper-caste lifestyle, rituals, and values by lower castes. Universalization (coined by McKim Marriott) is a civilizational concept focusing on how local (little tradition) cultural elements, rituals, and deities move upward to become part of the pan-Indian (great tradition).

Who coined the terms Universalization and Parochialization?

American anthropologist McKim Marriott coined both terms in his 1955 essay 'Little Communities in an Indigenous Civilization', building upon Robert Redfield's framework of Little and Great Traditions.

Is Universalization a one-way process?

No. Universalization is part of a two-way dynamic process of cultural interaction. While Universalization is the upward flow from Little to Great Tradition, its counterpart, Parochialization, is the downward flow from Great to Little Tradition.

Why is Universalization important for UP PGT and UGC NET Sociology examinations?

It represents a core pillar of Indian sociology's civilizational approach. Questions frequently test the author of the concept (McKim Marriott), the village where fieldwork was conducted (Kishan Garhi), and its distinction from Sanskritization and Parochialization.
