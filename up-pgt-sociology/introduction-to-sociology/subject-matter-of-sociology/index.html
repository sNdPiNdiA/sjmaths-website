Section 1 • Introduction to Sociology

Subject Matter of Sociology

The subject matter of sociology encompasses the systematic study of human social life, groups, institutions, and societies, focusing on the interplay between individual action and structural forces. For UP PGT Sociology and UGC NET examinations, mastering this topic requires an in-depth understanding of the formalist and synthetic schools of thought, classical paradigms, and Indian sociological perspectives.

UP PGT Sociology • Code 16 Section 1

1. Definitional Framework, Etymology & Classical Foundations

Sociology as an academic discipline emerged in the 19th century amidst profound industrial, political, and intellectual upheavals in Europe. Etymologically, the term is a hybrid coinage combining the Latin socius (companion, associate) and the Greek logos (study, reason, discourse), originally coined by French philosopher Auguste Comte in 1838 in his seminal work Cours de Philosophie Positive (Course in Positive Philosophy).

  • Auguste Comte's Positivism: Comte envisioned sociology as the 'queen of the sciences,' applying the empirical methods of natural sciences to study human society, governed by the Law of Three Stages (Theological, Metaphysical, and Positive/Scientific).
  • Émile Durkheim and Social Facts: In The Rules of Sociological Method (1895), Durkheim defined the subject matter of sociology as the study of social facts (faits sociaux)—ways of acting, thinking, and feeling that are external to the individual and endowed with a coercive power by which they control them.
  • Max Weber's Verstehen and Social Action: Weber emphasized interpretive sociology (Verstehen), defining sociology in Economy and Society (1922) as a science which attempts the interpretive understanding of social action in order to arrive at a causal explanation of its course and effects.
  • Formal vs. Synthetic Schools: The debate over the subject matter of sociology is historically divided into the Formal School (Georg Simmel, Tonnies, von Wiese), which focuses on the abstract forms of social relationships independent of their content, and the Synthetic School (Durkheim, Hobhouse, Sorokin), which views sociology as a synthesis of all social sciences, studying society as an integrated whole.

2. Structural Concepts, Empirical Processes & Indian Sociological Context

Moving beyond Western paradigms, the subject matter of sociology in India integrates classical Indological perspectives with structural-functional and Marxist frameworks. Indian sociologists have meticulously mapped the complexities of Indian social stratification, village communities, and institutional structures.

  • G.S. Ghurye and the Indological Approach: Known as the father of Indian sociology, Ghurye analyzed Indian society through classical texts and historical documents, particularly in Caste and Race in India (1932), identifying the six cardinal features of the caste system (segmental division, hierarchy, civil and religious disabilities, restriction on feeding and social intercourse, lack of unrestricted choice of occupation, and endogamy).
  • M.N. Srinivas and Field-View Sociology: Emphasizing micro-empirical studies, Srinivas introduced monumental concepts such as Sanskritization, Westernization, and the Dominant Caste through his seminal village ethnography Religion and Society Among the Coorgs of South India (1952) and The Remembered Village (1976).
  • Radhakamal Mukerjee and Social Values: Mukerjee pioneered ecological and institutional sociology, emphasizing that sociology must study human values, morals, and economic behavior as a unified socio-ecological whole, as articulated in The Social Structure of Values (1949).
  • D.P. Mukerji and Dialectics of Tradition: Adopting a Marxian-Indological synthesis, D.P. Mukerji argued in Modern Indian Culture (1942) that Indian sociology must begin with the study of Indian traditions (Purana, Itihasa) and their dialectical encounters with modernity.

3. Contemporary Dynamics, Social Policy & Applied Dimensions

In contemporary academic discourse and competitive examinations like UP PGT Sociology, the subject matter extends to applied sociology, addressing structural inequalities, social movements, globalization, and developmental policies in modern India.

  • Social Stratification and Mobility: Analyzing how class, caste, and gender intersect in contemporary India, drawing upon Andre Beteille's Caste, Class, and Power (1965) and Yogendra Singh's Modernization of Indian Tradition (1973).
  • Rural and Urban Transformation: Studying the impact of the Community Development Programme (1952), Panchayati Raj (73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts), and rapid urbanization on traditional joint family and kinship structures.
  • Applied Social Problems: Investigating persistent and emergent social pathologies such as poverty, unemployment, agrarian distress, dowry, communalism, and domestic violence through sociological intervention frameworks and constitutional mandates.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Sociology

  • Auguste Comte coined the term 'Sociology' in 1838 in his work 'Cours de Philosophie Positive', replacing his earlier term 'Social Physics'.
  • Émile Durkheim formally established sociology as an independent academic discipline with distinct subject matter ('Social Facts') in 'The Rules of Sociological Method' (1895).
  • Max Weber defined sociology as the science of 'Social Action' requiring interpretive understanding or 'Verstehen' in 'Economy and Society' (1922).
  • G.S. Ghurye is widely regarded as the 'Father of Indian Sociology' and established the Department of Sociology at the University of Bombay.
  • M.N. Srinivas introduced the concepts of 'Sanskritization', 'Westernization', and 'Dominant Caste' through his intensive anthropological study of Coorgs and Rampura village.
  • The Formal School of sociology focuses on the 'forms' of social relationships (advocated by Georg Simmel), whereas the Synthetic School views sociology as a general synthesis of social life (advocated by Émile Durkheim).

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Students often confuse the Formal School with qualitative research methods. Fact: The Formal School refers to the theoretical delimitation of sociology's subject matter to abstract 'forms' of social interaction, not methodology.
  • Misconception 2: Assuming M.N. Srinivas studied only caste mobility. Fact: While Sanskritization explains cultural mobility, Srinivas also extensively studied village social structure, factionalism, secularization, and agrarian relations.

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Q1 Who among the following proponents is strongly associated with the 'Formal School' of sociology?
  • A Émile Durkheim
  • B Georg Simmel
  • C Pitirim Sorokin
  • D L.T. Hobhouse
Correct Option: B (Georg Simmel)

Georg Simmel is the principal architect of the Formal School of sociology, which argues that sociology should study the abstract forms of social interaction (such as subordination, competition, conflict) rather than their historical contents. Durkheim, Sorokin, and Hobhouse belong to the Synthetic School.

Q2 In which book did Émile Durkheim formally define 'Social Facts' as the subject matter of sociology?
  • A The Division of Labour in Society (1893)
  • B Suicide (1897)
  • C The Rules of Sociological Method (1895)
  • D The Elementary Forms of the Religious Life (1912)
Correct Option: C (The Rules of Sociological Method (1895))

Émile Durkheim explicitly established the methodological foundation and subject matter of sociology as 'social facts' in his 1895 publication 'The Rules of Sociological Method'.

Q3 Who coined the term 'Sociology' in the year 1838?
  • A Herbert Spencer
  • B Max Weber
  • C Karl Marx
  • D Auguste Comte
Correct Option: D (Auguste Comte)

Auguste Comte coined the term 'Sociology' in 1838 in Volume 4 of his 'Cours de Philosophie Positive' by combining Latin and Greek roots.

Q4 Which Indian sociologist systematically outlined the six cardinal features of the caste system in India in 'Caste and Race in India' (1932)?
  • A M.N. Srinivas
  • B G.S. Ghurye
  • C D.P. Mukerji
  • D Radhakamal Mukerjee
Correct Option: B (G.S. Ghurye)

G.S. Ghurye formulated the six classical features of the caste system in his seminal 1932 work 'Caste and Race in India', marking the foundation of Indological sociology in India.

Q5 Max Weber defined sociology as a science which attempts the interpretive understanding of:
  • A Social Facts
  • B Class Struggle
  • C Social Action
  • D Collective Conscience
Correct Option: C (Social Action)

Max Weber defined sociology as the interpretive understanding (Verstehen) of 'social action' in order to arrive at a causal explanation of its course and effects.

Q6 What is the primary premise of the 'Synthetic School' of sociology?
  • A Sociology should study only isolated forms of interaction.
  • B Sociology is a general science that synthesizes data from all social sciences to study society as an integrated whole.
  • C Sociology must reject historical analysis entirely in favor of mathematics.
  • D Sociology is identical to formal economics and political science.
Correct Option: B (Sociology is a general science that synthesizes data from all social sciences to study society as an integrated whole.)

The Synthetic School, championed by thinkers like Durkheim and Hobhouse, views sociology as a broad, generalizing science that coordinates and synthesizes findings from various specialized social sciences.

Q7 Which sociological concept was introduced by M.N. Srinivas to explain the process by which lower castes adopt the cultural practices of upper castes to raise their social status?
  • A Westernization
  • B Secularization
  • C Sanskritization
  • D Modernization
Correct Option: C (Sanskritization)

Sanskritization is the concept coined by M.N. Srinivas to denote the process of mobility wherein lower castes emulate the lifestyle, rituals, and beliefs of Brahmins or other higher twice-born castes.

Q8 Who among the following championed the dialectical approach to the study of Indian traditions, arguing that traditions are dynamic and encounter modernity?
  • A G.S. Ghurye
  • B D.P. Mukerji
  • C B.R. Ambedkar
  • D Irawati Karve
Correct Option: B (D.P. Mukerji)

D.P. Mukerji emphasized the dialectical study of Indian traditions and culture, arguing that Indian social reality cannot be understood without analyzing the encounter between tradition and modernity.

Q9 The etymological roots of the word 'Sociology' are derived from:
  • A Greek 'Socius' and Latin 'Logos'
  • B Latin 'Socius' and Greek 'Logos'
  • C Latin 'Societas' and Greek 'Nomos'
  • D French 'Societe' and Latin 'Scientia'
Correct Option: B (Latin 'Socius' and Greek 'Logos')

Sociology is etymologically hybrid, derived from the Latin word 'socius' (companion or associate) and the Greek word 'logos' (study or speech/reason).

Q10 Which school of thought argues that the subject matter of sociology should be restricted exclusively to the study of the pure forms of human relationships?
  • A Synthetic School
  • B Formal School
  • C Functionalist School
  • D Conflict School
Correct Option: B (Formal School)

The Formal School (associated with Georg Simmel, Alfred von Wiese, etc.) maintains that sociology should isolate the 'forms' of social relationships from their concrete contents, akin to geometry.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the core subject matter of sociology according to classical theorists?

Classical theorists defined the subject matter differently: Auguste Comte focused on social order and progress via positivism; Émile Durkheim focused on objective 'social facts'; Max Weber emphasized meaningful 'social action'; and Karl Marx focused on material conditions and class struggle.

What is the difference between the Formal School and the Synthetic School?

The Formal School (Simmel, Weber) argues that sociology is a special science studying distinct forms of social interaction. The Synthetic School (Durkheim, Hobhouse) argues that sociology is a general science that integrates insights from all social sciences to understand society as a holistic entity.

Why is the study of Indian social thinkers crucial for the UP PGT Sociology examination?

UP PGT Sociology extensively tests Indian sociological traditions, village studies, and stratification models pioneered by G.S. Ghurye, M.N. Srinivas, D.P. Mukerji, and Radhakamal Mukerjee, reflecting the socio-cultural realities of Indian society.

How does M.N. Srinivas's approach differ from G.S. Ghurye's approach?

While G.S. Ghurye primarily employed an Indological approach utilizing classical texts, historical literature, and scriptural sources, M.N. Srinivas pioneered the field-view approach through intensive micro-ethnographic fieldwork in villages like Rampura and Coorg.
