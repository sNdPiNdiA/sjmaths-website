Section 1 • Introduction to Sociology

Meaning of Sociology

Sociology is the scientific study of human society, social relationships, institutions, and the complex interplay between individual agency and social structures. For the UP PGT Sociology and UGC NET examinations, mastering the meaning of sociology requires understanding its dual etymological roots, classical sociological paradigms, and its evolution into a distinct empirical science of social order and social change.

UP PGT Sociology • Code 16 Section 1

1. Definitional Framework, Etymology & Classical Foundations

The term Sociology is a linguistic hybrid, coined by the French philosopher Auguste Comte in 1838. It derives from the Latin word 'Socius' (meaning companion, associate, or society) and the Greek word 'Logos' (meaning study, science, or reasoned discourse). Literally translated, sociology is the 'science of society'. Prior to 1838, Comte referred to the discipline as 'Social Physics' (Physique Sociale) in his seminal work 'Course in Positive Philosophy' (Cours de Philosophie Positive, 6 vols., 1830–1842), before renaming it to avoid intellectual plagiarism with Belgian statistician Adolphe Quetelet.

  • Auguste Comte's Positivism: Established sociology as the queen of the sciences, governed by the Law of Three Stages (Theological, Metaphysical, and Positive/Scientific).
  • Émile Durkheim's Definition: In 'The Rules of Sociological Method' (1895), Durkheim defined sociology as the study of Social Facts—external ways of acting, thinking, and feeling that exert a coercive power over the individual. He famously asserted: 'Consider social facts as things.'
  • Max Weber's Verstehen Approach: Defined sociology in 'Economy and Society' (Wirtschaft und Gesellschaft, 1922) as 'a science which attempts the interpretive understanding of social action in order thereby to arrive at a causal explanation of its course and effects.'
  • Formal vs. Synthetic Schools: The formal school, led by Georg Simmel, Ferdinand Tönnies, and Max Weber, argued that sociology should study the forms of social relationships abstracted from their content. The synthetic school, championed by Émile Durkheim, L.T. Hobhouse, and Morris Ginsberg, viewed sociology as a comprehensive, synthesizing discipline combining all specialized social sciences.

2. Structural Concepts, Empirical Processes & Indian Sociological Context

Moving beyond general definitions, sociology examines social structure, stratification, culture, and socialization. In the Indian sociological tradition, the discipline assumes a unique character, blending universal sociological categories with indigenous civilizational realities.

  • G.S. Ghurye (1893–1983): The father of Indian sociology, operating from Bombay University. In 'Caste and Race in India' (1932), he outlined the six structural features of the caste system (segmental division, hierarchy, civil and religious disabilities, restriction on feeding and social intercourse, lack of unrestricted choice of occupation, and endogamy).
  • M.N. Srinivas (1916–1999): Introduced pivotal field-view concepts based on his Rampura village study in 'Religion and Society Among the Coorgs of South India' (1952) and 'Social Change in Modern India' (1966), including Sanskritization, Westernization, and Dominant Caste.
  • D.P. Mukerji (1894–1953): Advocated for a dialectical, Marxian-Indological synthesis in 'Modern Indian Culture' (1942), arguing that understanding Indian society requires analyzing the traditions (Tradition and Modernity, 1958).
  • Radhakamal Mukerjee (1889–1968): Pioneered institutional and ecological economics, propounding a distinct theory of social values in 'The Social Structure of Values' (1949).

3. Contemporary Dynamics, Social Policy & Applied Dimensions

In contemporary times, sociology serves as both a pure academic discipline and an applied science for social engineering, policy formulation, and welfare administration.

  • Social Legislation and Constitutional Mandates: Sociological research underpins protective discrimination policies, Untouchability Offenses Act (1955, later Protection of Civil Rights Act), Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act (1989), and the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts (1992) for decentralized rural and urban local self-government.
  • Development and Planning: Evaluating Community Development Programmes (1952), National Extension Services, and poverty alleviation schemes through sociological lenses ensures structural adequacy rather than merely technical inputs.
  • Addressing Social Problems: Applying functionalist and conflict perspectives to analyze structural inequalities, regionalism, communalism, domestic violence, and juvenile delinquency in modern India.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Sociology

  • Auguste Comte coined the term 'Sociology' in 1838, shifting the terminology from 'Social Physics'.
  • Émile Durkheim established sociology as an independent academic discipline with empirical methodology in 'The Rules of Sociological Method' (1895).
  • Max Weber defined sociology as the interpretive understanding (Verstehen) of social action in 'Economy and Society' (1922).
  • G.S. Ghurye detailed the structural characteristics of the Indian caste system in 'Caste and Race in India' (1932).
  • M.N. Srinivas introduced the concepts of Sanskritization and Dominant Caste in his studies of Indian village communities.
  • MacIver and Page defined sociology in 'Society: An Introductory Analysis' (1949) as the study of social relationships, calling society 'a web of social relationships'.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Students often believe sociology studies only abnormal behavior or social problems. Scientifically, sociology studies normative, routine, and institutionalized social interaction alongside structural pathology.
  • Misconception 2: Confusing Auguste Comte's coining of sociology in 1838 with the formal institutionalization of university departments (which occurred later under Durkheim in France and Albion Small in the US).

Q1 Who among the following originally referred to sociology as 'Social Physics' before renaming it in 1838?
  • A Herbert Spencer
  • B Auguste Comte
  • C Émile Durkheim
  • D Max Weber
Correct Option: B (Auguste Comte)

Auguste Comte initially used the term 'Social Physics' (Physique Sociale) in his work 'Course in Positive Philosophy' (1830-1842). He later coined the term 'Sociology' in 1838. Therefore, option 1 is correct.

Q2 In which book did Émile Durkheim formally outline the methodological rule that 'social facts must be treated as things'?
  • A The Division of Labour in Society
  • B Suicide
  • C The Rules of Sociological Method
  • D The Elementary Forms of the Religious Life
Correct Option: C (The Rules of Sociological Method)

Published in 1895, 'The Rules of Sociological Method' is Durkheim's foundational methodological treatise where he establishes social facts as the subject matter of sociology and asserts they must be treated as things. Option 2 is correct.

Q3 Which of the following sociologists belongs to the 'Formal School' of sociological thought?
  • A Émile Durkheim
  • B Georg Simmel
  • C Morris Ginsberg
  • D L.T. Hobhouse
Correct Option: B (Georg Simmel)

Georg Simmel, along with Max Weber, Ferdinand Tönnies, and Alfred Vierkandt, belongs to the formal or specialized school of sociology, which emphasizes the study of forms of social interaction rather than its content. Option 1 is correct.

Q4 Who defined society as 'a web of social relationships'?
  • A Kingsley Davis
  • B MacIver and Page
  • C Morris Ginsberg
  • D Max Weber
Correct Option: B (MacIver and Page)

R.M. MacIver and C.H. Page, in their classic textbook 'Society: An Introductory Analysis' (1949), defined society explicitly as a web of social relationships which is ever-changing. Option 1 is correct.

Max Weber emphasized 'Verstehen' (interpretive understanding) as the epistemological foundation for studying subjective meanings attached to social action. Option 1 is correct.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the exact etymological meaning of Sociology?

Sociology is derived from the Latin word 'Socius' (society, companion, or associate) and the Greek word 'Logos' (study, science, or reason). Etymologically, it signifies the scientific study or discourse of society.

Why did Auguste Comte change the name from 'Social Physics' to 'Sociology'?

Comte originally used 'Social Physics' in his positive philosophy lectures. However, Belgian statistician Adolphe Quetelet began using 'social physics' for statistical demographic research and data collection. To maintain intellectual distinction for his master science of society, Comte coined the hybrid term 'Sociology' in 1838.

What is the difference between the Formal School and Synthetic School in sociology?

The Formal School (led by Georg Simmel and Max Weber) argues that sociology should be a specialized science studying the distinct forms of social relationships independently of their content. The Synthetic School (led by Émile Durkheim and Morris Ginsberg) argues that sociology is a general, synthesizing discipline that brings together findings from all specialized social sciences to study society as an organic whole.

How is Indian sociology distinct from Western sociology?

While Western sociology emerged out of industrialization, capitalism, and Enlightenment individualism, Indian sociology (pioneered by G.S. Ghurye, M.N. Srinivas, and D.P. Mukerji) is deeply rooted in civilizational structures, textual indology, religious traditions, caste stratification, and rural-agrarian realities.
