Section 12 • Rural Change & Development

Panchayati Raj

Panchayati Raj represents the institutionalization of local self-governance at the rural grassroots level in India, transforming traditional village councils into statutory democratic units. From a sociological perspective, it serves as a critical mechanism for decentralization of power, political socialization, and the renegotiation of caste, class, and gender hierarchies as analyzed by pioneers like M.N. Srinivas and Andre Beteille.

1. Definitional Framework, Etymology & Classical Foundations

The term Panchayati Raj is derived from Sanskrit roots: Pancha (meaning five) and Ayatana (meaning assembly or abode), historically denoting a council of five elders resolving disputes within Indian village communities. Sociologically, it bridges traditional communal structures with modern bureaucratic-legal rationality, aligning with Max Weber's formulations of legal-rational authority and democratic decentralization.

  • Classical Sociological Viewpoint: Early colonial and nationalist sociologists, including Radhakamal Mukerjee and D.P. Mukerji, viewed the Indian village not merely as an economic unit of peasant production, but as a moral community bound by reciprocal obligations, caste interdependence (Jajmani system), and customary panchayats.
  • Statutory Evolution: Moving from traditional caste/village panchayats (informal, ascribed authority) to statutory local bodies established through colonial Acts, culminating in post-Independence community development initiatives and the historic 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act of 1992.

2. Structural Concepts, Empirical Processes & Indian Sociological Context

The operationalization of Panchayati Raj in rural India has been profoundly shaped by stratification systems, notably caste, class, and power. M.N. Srinivas's concept of the Dominant Caste illustrates how numeric strength, economic power (landownership), and ritual status enable specific castes to capture leadership positions within local democratic institutions, often subverting egalitarian constitutional mandates.

Empirical village studies—such as Andre Beteille's study of Sripuram in Tamil Nadu and S.C. Dube's study of Shamirpet—demonstrated that structural changes (caste, class, and power becoming disaggregated) do not occur uniformly. While the 73rd Amendment introduced mandatory reservations for Scheduled Castes (SCs), Scheduled Tribes (STs), and Women (not less than one-third), sociological analyses by scholars like Yogendra Singh (Modernization of Indian Tradition, 1973) reveal a complex interplay between primordial loyalties and structural democratization.

3. Contemporary Dynamics, Social Policy & Applied Dimensions

The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 (effective April 24, 1993) added Part IX to the Constitution of India, institutionalizing a mandatory three-tier structure: Gram Panchayat (village level), Panchayat Samiti (intermediate/block level), and Zilla Parishad (district level). It established State Election Commissions and State Finance Commissions to ensure regular elections and fiscal devolution.

Sociologically, the reservation of seats and leadership positions (Sarpanch/Pradhan) for women has triggered significant social transformation, often termed 'grassroots feminism' or 'silent revolution', challenging patriarchal hegemony in rural public spheres despite instances of proxy representation (e.g., Sarpanch Pati phenomenon). Furthermore, the implementation of welfare schemes like MGNREGA through Gram Panchayats has altered rural wage labor dynamics and patron-client relationships.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Sociology

  • The Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) first recommended the establishment of a three-tier Panchayati Raj system in India.
  • Panchayati Raj was inaugurated for the first time in independent India on October 2, 1959, in Nagaur district of Rajasthan by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru.
  • The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 added Part IX and the Eleventh Schedule (containing 29 functional items) to the Constitution of India.
  • Ashok Mehta Committee (1978) recommended a two-tier system (Zilla Parishad and Mandal Panchayat) instead of the three-tier model.
  • L.M. Singhvi Committee (1986) first recommended giving constitutional status to Panchayati Raj institutions.
  • Article 243G of the Indian Constitution empowers Panchayats with powers, authority, and responsibilities regarding economic development and social justice.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Students often confuse the committee that recommended constitutional status (L.M. Singhvi Committee, 1986) with the committee that recommended the initial creation of the three-tier system (Balwant Rai Mehta Committee, 1957).
  • Misconception 2: Assuming traditional caste panchayats are identical to statutory Gram Panchayats. Traditional panchayats were informal, caste-based, and non-constitutional, whereas statutory panchayats are legally mandated, democratic, and secular institutions of local self-government.

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Q1 Which committee recommended the constitutional recognition of Panchayati Raj institutions in India for the first time?
  • A Balwant Rai Mehta Committee
  • B Ashok Mehta Committee
  • C L.M. Singhvi Committee
  • D G.V.K. Rao Committee
Correct Option: C (L.M. Singhvi Committee)

The L.M. Singhvi Committee, appointed in 1986 by the Rajiv Gandhi government, recommended for the first time that Panchayati Raj institutions should receive constitutional recognition and protection to ensure regular and fair elections. Therefore, option 2 is correct, while options 0, 1, and 3 refer to committees with different primary mandates.

Q2 The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 added which Schedule to the Constitution of India?
  • A Ninth Schedule
  • B Tenth Schedule
  • C Eleventh Schedule
  • D Twelfth Schedule
Correct Option: C (Eleventh Schedule)

The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act added the Eleventh Schedule to the Constitution, containing 29 functional items devolved to Panchayats. The Twelfth Schedule was added by the 74th Amendment for Municipalities. Option 2 is correct.

Q3 In which state and district was Panchayati Raj inaugurated for the first time in India on October 2, 1959?
  • A Sitapur, Uttar Pradesh
  • B Nagaur, Rajasthan
  • C Anantapur, Andhra Pradesh
  • D Gandhinagar, Gujarat
Correct Option: B (Nagaur, Rajasthan)

Panchayati Raj was first inaugurated by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru on October 2, 1959, in Nagaur district of Rajasthan. Option 1 is correct.

Q4 Who authored the seminal sociological work 'Caste, Class, and Power' (1965), which examines local power structures and factionalism in a Tanjore village?
  • A M.N. Srinivas
  • B Andre Beteille
  • C S.C. Dube
  • D D.P. Mukerji
Correct Option: B (Andre Beteille)

Andre Beteille authored 'Caste, Class, and Power: Changing Patterns of Stratification in a Tanjore Village' (1965), studying Sripuram village and showing how traditional caste stratification intersects with modern political and economic power. Option 1 is correct.

Q5 Which of the following tiers constitutes the apex body of the three-tier Panchayati Raj system?
  • A Gram Panchayat
  • B Panchayat Samiti
  • C Zilla Parishad
  • D Gram Sabha
Correct Option: C (Zilla Parishad)

The three tiers are Gram Panchayat (village level), Panchayat Samiti (block level), and Zilla Parishad (district level). The Zilla Parishad operates at the apex district level. Option 2 is correct.

Q6 Article 243A of the Indian Constitution pertains to which of the following?
  • A Constitution of Panchayats
  • B Gram Sabha
  • C Reservation of seats
  • D Duration of Panchayats
Correct Option: B (Gram Sabha)

Article 243A deals with the Gram Sabha, defining it as a body consisting of persons registered in the electoral rolls relating to a village within the area of a Panchayat at the village level. Option 1 is correct.

Q7 The Ashok Mehta Committee (1978) recommended which of the following models for Panchayati Raj?
  • A Three-tier system
  • B Four-tier system
  • C Two-tier system
  • D Single-tier system
Correct Option: C (Two-tier system)

The Ashok Mehta Committee recommended replacing the three-tier system with a two-tier system consisting of Zilla Parishad at the district level and Mandal Panchayat comprising a group of villages. Option 2 is correct.

Q8 Which sociological concept developed by M.N. Srinivas is most useful for understanding the capture of local political power in rural India?
  • A Sanskritization
  • B Dominant Caste
  • C Westernization
  • D Secularization
Correct Option: B (Dominant Caste)

M.N. Srinivas's concept of 'Dominant Caste' (characterized by numerical strength, economic power through landownership, and political influence) explains leadership patterns and power dynamics in local bodies like Panchayats. Option 1 is correct.

Q9 What proportion of total seats is mandatorily reserved for women at all tiers of Panchayati Raj under the 73rd Amendment?
  • A One-fourth (25%)
  • B One-third (33.3%)
  • C One-half (50%)
  • D Two-thirds (66%)
Correct Option: B (One-third (33.3%))

Article 243D of the Constitution mandates that not less than one-third (33.3%) of the total number of seats to be filled by direct election in every Panchayat shall be reserved for women. Option 1 is correct.

Q10 Which committee was appointed in 1985 to review administrative arrangements for rural development and poverty alleviation schemes, known as the 'CAARD' committee?
  • A G.V.K. Rao Committee
  • B Sarkaria Commission
  • C Balwant Rai Mehta Committee
  • D V.T. Krishnamachari Committee
Correct Option: A (G.V.K. Rao Committee)

The G.V.K. Rao Committee was appointed by the Planning Commission in 1985 (often referred to as the Committee to Review Administrative Arrangements for Rural Development, CAARD) and recommended strengthening district-level planning through Zilla Parishads. Option 0 is correct.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the sociological significance of the 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act?

Sociologically, the 73rd Amendment transformed rural power structures by institutionalizing local democracy, mandating reservations for historically marginalized groups (SCs, STs, and women), and shifting authority from traditional ascriptive elites to democratically elected representatives, thereby altering caste and gender dynamics.

How does M.N. Srinivas's concept of Dominant Caste apply to Panchayati Raj?

Srinivas argued that economic power (landownership) combined with numerical strength enables certain intermediate or upper castes to dominate rural politics. In Panchayati Raj elections, members of the dominant caste frequently capture key positions such as Pradhan or Sarpanch, maintaining localized hegemony despite statutory democratic reforms.

What is the difference between a Gram Sabha and a Gram Panchayat?

The Gram Sabha is the general body comprising all registered voters in a village, functioning as direct democracy at the grassroots level. The Gram Panchayat is the executive committee elected by the Gram Sabha to carry out day-to-day administrative and developmental functions.

Why is the reservation of women in Panchayati Raj considered a watershed moment?

Mandating at least one-third reservation for women forced patriarchal rural structures to accommodate female leadership. Sociological studies show that despite initial proxy representation ('Sarpanch Pati'), women leaders have gained political efficacy, altered development priorities (focusing on drinking water, sanitation, and education), and challenged traditional gender roles.
