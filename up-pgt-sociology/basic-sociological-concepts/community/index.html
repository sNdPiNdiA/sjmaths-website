Section 7 • Basic Sociological Concepts

Community

The concept of 'community' is fundamental to sociological inquiry, denoting a group of people sharing a common geographic territory, social structure, and a sense of belonging or 'we-feeling'. In UP PGT Sociology and UGC NET examinations, understanding community entails analyzing Ferdinand Tönnies' monumental dichotomy of Gemeinschaft and Gesellschaft, MacIver and Page's criteria of community, and the transformation of rural communities into modern civic entities.

UP PGT Sociology • Code 16 Section 7

1. Definitional Framework, Etymology & Classical Foundations

Etymologically, the term community is derived from the Latin roots 'com' (together) and 'munus' (to serve, or gift/duty), implying shared service, common life, and reciprocal obligation among individuals. In sociological literature, community is defined not merely as a collection of individuals residing in a specific locality, but as a social unit characterized by a sense of identity, shared values, and psychological interdependence.

  • MacIver and Page's Formulation: In their classic treatise Society: An Introductory Analysis (1949), Robert M. MacIver and Charles H. Page define a community as 'an area of social living marked by some degree of social coherence.' They emphasize two primary criteria: locality (a territorial base) and community sentiment (we-feeling, role-feeling, and dependency-feeling).
  • Ferdinand Tönnies' Dichotomy: In Gemeinschaft und Gesellschaft (1887), Tönnies established the foundational paradigm of human grouping. Gemeinschaft (Community) is rooted in organic, natural will (Wesenwille), kinship, neighborhood, friendship, and shared traditions, exemplified by traditional rural villages. Gesellschaft (Society/Association) is based on rational, calculating will (Kürwille), contractual relationships, individualism, and urban-industrial existence.
  • Emile Durkheim's Solidarity: Durkheim explored community cohesion through the evolution of solidarity. In The Division of Labour in Society (1893), he contrasted mechanical solidarity (based on resemblance, collective conscience, and repressive law in primitive/traditional communities) with organic solidarity (based on functional interdependence, specialized division of labour, and restitutive law in modern industrial societies).

2. Structural Concepts, Empirical Processes & Indian Sociological Context

In the study of Indian society, the concept of community takes on unique structural dimensions due to the historical significance of the village community, caste networks, and religious pluralism. Indian sociologists have rigorously examined how communities operate beyond mere territorial boundaries.

  • The Indian Village Community: Classical British administrators like Charles Metcalfe famously described Indian villages as 'little republics' due to their self-sufficiency and autonomy. However, subsequent empirical studies by Indian and Western sociologists debunked this myth of isolation and unchanging stability.
  • S.C. Dube's Village Studies: In Indian Village (1955), Dube studied Shamirpet village, demonstrating that Indian villages are intrinsically linked to wider regional, economic, and political networks, stratified heavily by caste, class, and factionalism.
  • M.N. Srinivas and Rampura: Srinivas utilized intensive field studies in Rampura (documented in The Remembered Village, 1976) to analyze how the 'dominant caste' acts as a pivot of community power, integration, and conflict resolution across different religious and economic groups within the rural locality.
  • Radhakamal Mukerjee and Social Ecology: Mukerjee pioneered regional sociology and human ecology in India (The Regional Balance of Man, 1938), arguing that community is an ecological grouping where human communities adapt to their natural and social environments through symbiotic relationships.

3. Contemporary Dynamics, Social Policy & Applied Dimensions

In contemporary society, traditional definitions of community based strictly on physical propinquity are challenged by globalization, digital networks, and urban migration. The sociological understanding of community now encompasses virtual communities, transnational diasporas, and planned community development.

  • Community Development Programme (CDP, 1952): Launched in post-independence India, the CDP was the first major state-led initiative aimed at the holistic socio-economic transformation of rural communities through participatory planning, agriculture extension, and infrastructural development.
  • Panchayati Raj Institutions (73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments, 1992): Institutionalized local self-government, devolving political power directly to rural (Gram Panchayat) and urban communities, ensuring democratic decentralization and reservation for marginalized sections (SC, ST, and women).
  • Urbanization and Urbanism: Louis Wirth's classic essay 'Urbanism as a Way of Life' (1938) conceptualized how urban communities experience anonymity, secondary relations, and spatial segregation, contrasting sharply with rural gemeinschaft.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Sociology

  • Ferdinand Tönnies coined the terms Gemeinschaft and Gesellschaft in his 1887 seminal work 'Gemeinschaft und Gesellschaft'.
  • MacIver and Page defined community in 'Society: An Introductory Analysis' (1949) emphasizing locality and community sentiment.
  • Emile Durkheim differentiated mechanical solidarity (based on resemblance) from organic solidarity (based on division of labour) in 1893.
  • Charles Metcalfe famously characterized Indian village communities as 'little republics' possessing self-sufficiency.
  • Louis Wirth published his influential essay 'Urbanism as a Way of Life' in the American Journal of Sociology in 1938.
  • The Community Development Programme (CDP) was inaugurated in India on October 2, 1952, to promote rural community development.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Confusing 'Community' with 'Society'. Society is a web of social relationships which is abstract and lacks a specific territorial boundary, whereas a community always requires a definite territorial locality and a concrete group of people.
  • Misconception 2: Assuming Tönnies viewed Gemeinschaft as purely good and Gesellschaft as purely bad. Tönnies used them as sociological ideal types (analytical tools) to describe historical structural shifts rather than moral judgments.

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Test your conceptual understanding. Select an answer and check the detailed pedagogical rationale.

Q1 Who among the following sociologists introduced the classic sociological distinction between 'Gemeinschaft' and 'Gesellschaft'?
  • A Emile Durkheim
  • B Ferdinand Tönnies
  • C Max Weber
  • D Robert M. MacIver
Correct Option: B (Ferdinand Tönnies)

Ferdinand Tönnies introduced the concepts of Gemeinschaft (community) and Gesellschaft (society) in his 1887 book 'Gemeinschaft und Gesellschaft' to explain the structural transformation from traditional to modern society.

Q2 According to R.M. MacIver and C.H. Page, what are the two essential bases of a community?
  • A Class and Status
  • B Locality and Community Sentiment
  • C Division of Labour and Solidarity
  • D Custom and Law
Correct Option: B (Locality and Community Sentiment)

MacIver and Page explicitly state that a community requires a territorial base (locality) and a psychological bond of belonging (community sentiment or 'we-feeling').

Q3 Which British administrator famously described the Indian village communities as 'little republics'?
  • A Sir Henry Maine
  • B Charles Metcalfe
  • C Baden-Powell
  • D William Crooke
Correct Option: B (Charles Metcalfe)

Sir Charles Metcalfe, in his minute of 1830, referred to Indian village communities as little republics that seemed to endure independently of changes in dynasties and political turmoil.

Q4 In which book did Emile Durkheim elaborate upon the concepts of mechanical and organic solidarity?
  • A The Rules of Sociological Method
  • B Suicide
  • C The Division of Labour in Society
  • D The Elementary Forms of the Religious Life
Correct Option: C (The Division of Labour in Society)

Durkheim's doctoral dissertation, 'The Division of Labour in Society' (1893), focuses on social solidarity, contrasting mechanical solidarity in traditional societies with organic solidarity in modern industrial societies.

Q5 Who authored the famous ethnographic study of Shamirpet village titled 'Indian Village' (1955)?
  • A M.N. Srinivas
  • B S.C. Dube
  • C D.P. Mukerji
  • D A.R. Desai
Correct Option: B (S.C. Dube)

S.C. Dube conducted an intensive anthropological study of Shamirpet village near Hyderabad, published as 'Indian Village' in 1955, making a foundational contribution to Indian rural sociology.

Q6 What does the Latin root 'munus' in the etymology of 'community' signify?
  • A Territory
  • B Duty, gift, or service
  • C Blood relation
  • D Contractual agreement
Correct Option: B (Duty, gift, or service)

Etymologically, community derives from 'com' (together) and 'munus' (service, duty, or gift), reflecting shared obligations and mutual aid.

Q7 Louis Wirth's essay 'Urbanism as a Way of Life' (1938) highlights which of the following characteristics of urban communities?
  • A Intimate kinship ties
  • B Primacy of primary group relations
  • C Anonymity, heterogeneity, and secondary contacts
  • D Complete economic self-sufficiency
Correct Option: C (Anonymity, heterogeneity, and secondary contacts)

Louis Wirth defined urbanism in terms of population size, density, and heterogeneity, leading to social heterogeneity, anonymity, and impersonal secondary relationships.

Q8 When was the Community Development Programme (CDP) officially launched in independent India?
  • A August 15, 1947
  • B January 26, 1950
  • C October 2, 1952
  • D October 2, 1959
Correct Option: C (October 2, 1952)

The Community Development Programme (CDP) was launched on Mahatma Gandhi's birthday, October 2, 1952, to spearhead rural development through community participation.

Q9 Which constitutional amendment institutionalized Panchayati Raj institutions in India, formally recognizing local self-governing village communities?
  • A 42nd Amendment
  • B 44th Amendment
  • C 73rd Amendment
  • D 86th Amendment
Correct Option: C (73rd Amendment)

The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992, added Part IX to the Constitution of India, providing constitutional status to Panchayati Raj institutions at the village, intermediate, and district levels.

Q10 How does 'Society' differ fundamentally from 'Community' in sociological analysis?
  • A Society has a fixed geographical boundary while community does not.
  • B Society is a web of social relationships and is abstract, whereas community requires a definite territorial locality.
  • C Community is based on formal contracts while society is based on kinship.
  • D There is no theoretical difference between society and community.
Correct Option: B (Society is a web of social relationships and is abstract, whereas community requires a definite territorial locality.)

MacIver and Page emphasize that society is abstract (a web of social relationships), whereas a community is concrete, requiring a specific territorial locality and shared community sentiment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the primary difference between community and society according to MacIver and Page?

According to MacIver and Page, a community is a concrete group of people living in a specific locality sharing a common way of life and 'we-feeling'. In contrast, society is an abstract web of social relationships, institutions, and customs that does not require a specific territorial boundary.

Why is Ferdinand Tönnies' concept of Gemeinschaft important for UP PGT Sociology?

Tönnies' concept of Gemeinschaft provides the foundational typology for understanding traditional, face-to-face, kin-based community structures. Questions frequently appear on how industrialization transforms Gemeinschaft into Gesellschaft.

Are Indian villages still considered self-sufficient 'little republics' today?

No. Modern sociological and anthropological studies (such as those by S.C. Dube, M.N. Srinivas, and A.R. Desai) have conclusively proven that Indian villages are intricately connected to wider regional, national, and global economic, political, and cultural networks.

How does virtual community challenge traditional sociological definitions of community?

Traditional sociological definitions (like MacIver's) insist on physical 'locality' as a mandatory criterion. Virtual communities created via the internet challenge this by demonstrating that 'community sentiment' and intense social cohesion can exist entirely independently of geographic propinquity.
