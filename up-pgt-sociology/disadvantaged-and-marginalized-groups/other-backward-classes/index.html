Section 16 • Disadvantaged & Marginalized Groups

Other Backward Classes

The Other Backward Classes (OBCs) represent a crucial intermediate stratum in India's social stratification system, situated between the upper castes and the Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes. Sociologically, OBCs encapsulate a heterogeneous category of agrarian, artisanal, and pastoral castes characterized by historical social, educational, and economic backwardness, serving as a primary focal point for state-sponsored affirmative action and identity politics.

1. Sociological Foundations, Etymology & Conceptual Genesis

The term 'Other Backward Classes' (OBCs) emerged through constitutional provisions and administrative commissions in post-independent India, though its sociological roots lie deep within the traditional Varna-Jati framework. Etymologically, the term denotes collectivities that suffered historical disadvantages distinct from the ritual pollution associated with Untouchability (Scheduled Castes) or the geographical and cultural isolation of tribal communities (Scheduled Tribes).

  • Constitutional & Legal Mandate: Article 340 of the Indian Constitution empowers the President to appoint a Commission to investigate the conditions of socially and educationally backward classes. This led to the establishment of the First Backward Classes Commission (Kaka Kalelkar Commission, 1953) and the Second Backward Classes Commission (Mandal Commission, 1979).
  • Weberian Perspective on Stratification: Max Weber’s multidimensional approach to social stratification—comprising class (economic position), status (social honor/lifestyle), and party (power)—is vital for analyzing OBCs. OBCs frequently experience moderate economic standing (often as peasant proprietors or tenants) and varying social status, but historically lacked commensurate political and ritual power compared to the 'twice-born' (Dvija) upper castes.
  • André Béteille's Intersectionality: Béteille, in his seminal study Caste, Class, and Power (1965) conducted in Sripuram village, demonstrated how caste, class, and power do not always overlap perfectly. The rise of OBCs exemplifies the growing divergence between ritual status and secular class-power in rural India.

2. Structural Dynamics, Commissions & Indian Sociological Perspectives

The sociological analysis of OBCs is deeply intertwined with the transformation of the caste system, land reforms, and the emergence of the 'dominant caste' phenomenon formulated by M.N. Srinivas.

  • M.N. Srinivas and the Dominant Caste: Many communities that constitute the OBCs today overlap significantly with what Srinivas termed 'Dominant Castes' (e.g., Vokkaligas, Lingayats, Yadavs, Jats). Possession of numerical strength, economic power (land ownership), and political clout allowed these intermediate castes to assert themselves, driving processes of Sanskritization and political mobilization.
  • The Mandal Commission (1979–1980): Officially known as the Second Backward Classes Commission, chaired by B.P. Mandal, it identified 3,743 castes as socially and educationally backward, recommending a 27% reservation in central government services. The implementation of the Mandal recommendations in 1990 triggered profound sociological transformations, shifting the locus of Indian politics from upper-caste hegemony to 'backward caste' assertiveness (often theorized by Yogendra Singh and Christophe Jaffrelot).
  • Yogendra Singh's Modernization of Indian Tradition (1973): Singh argues that modernization in India does not lead to the destruction of tradition; rather, tradition and modernity dialectically interact. The mobilization of OBCs through caste associations and political parties (such as the Yadav-led political formations in Northern India) represents the structural adaptation of traditional primordial identities to democratic, modern political institutions.

3. Contemporary Transformations, Class Formation & Social Policy

In contemporary India, OBCs are no longer a monolithic sociological category. The stratification within OBCs has given rise to profound internal differentiation, leading to critical policy debates and sociological inquiries.

  • Creamy Layer Distinction: Following the landmark Supreme Court judgment in Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992), the concept of the 'Creamy Layer' was introduced to exclude economically advanced sections among OBCs from reservation benefits, aligning sociological criteria with economic class mobility.
  • Sub-categorization of OBCs: The Rohini Commission (established under Article 340 in 2017) highlighted that a minuscule fraction of dominant OBC castes captured the lion's share of reservation benefits, sparking sociological debates on equity, intra-category inequality, and the necessity of splitting the 27% quota into distinct tiers.
  • Agrarian Distress and Proletarianization: Many OBC communities historically dependent on agriculture face structural crises due to land fragmentation, declining profitability in farming, and sluggish industrial job growth, leading to demands for reclassification and inclusion under alternative reservation quotas (e.g., Patidars, Marathas, Jats).

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Sociology

  • Article 340 of the Indian Constitution provides for the appointment of a Commission to investigate the conditions of socially and educationally backward classes.
  • The First Backward Classes Commission was headed by Kaka Kalelkar and submitted its report in 1955.
  • The Second Backward Classes Commission (Mandal Commission) was appointed in 1979 and submitted its report in 1980, recommending 27% reservation for OBCs.
  • The landmark Supreme Court judgment addressing the Mandal Commission recommendations and introducing the 'Creamy Layer' concept is Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992).
  • André Béteille's study 'Caste, Class, and Power' (1965) in Sripuram village analyzes the changing intersection of caste, class, and political power among agrarian communities.
  • Yogendra Singh's 'Modernization of Indian Tradition' (1973) explains how primordial identities like caste adapt to democratic and bureaucratic structures.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Students often confuse the constitutional criteria for SCs/STs with OBCs. SC/ST categorization historically hinged upon 'Untouchability' and tribal isolation respectively, whereas OBC categorization hinges on 'social and educational backwardness' under Articles 15(4) and 16(4).
  • Misconception 2: Assuming OBCs are a homogenous class. Sociologically, OBCs span a vast continuum from prosperous peasant proprietors (dominant castes) to impoverished artisan groups, necessitating the sociological study of internal differentiation and the 'Creamy Layer'.

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Q1 Who was the chairman of the Second Backward Classes Commission appointed in 1979?
  • A Kaka Kalelkar
  • B B.P. Mandal
  • C B.K. Roy Burman
  • D M.N. Srinivas
Correct Option: B (B.P. Mandal)

B.P. Mandal chaired the Second Backward Classes Commission (popularly known as the Mandal Commission), which recommended 27% reservation for Other Backward Classes in central government posts.

Q2 Which constitutional article empowers the President of India to appoint a Commission to investigate the conditions of socially and educationally backward classes?
  • A Article 330
  • B Article 338
  • C Article 340
  • D Article 356
Correct Option: C (Article 340)

Article 340 of the Constitution of India specifically deals with the appointment of a Commission to investigate the conditions of backward classes.

Q3 In which landmark Supreme Court case was the concept of the 'Creamy Layer' introduced regarding OBC reservations?
  • A Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala
  • B Indra Sawhney v. Union of India
  • C Champakam Dorairajan v. State of Madras
  • D Minerva Mills v. Union of India
Correct Option: B (Indra Sawhney v. Union of India)

The nine-judge bench in Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992) upheld the 27% reservation for OBCs but mandated the exclusion of the economically advanced section, termed the 'Creamy Layer'.

Q4 André Béteille's classical sociological study 'Caste, Class, and Power' (1965) was conducted in which state?
  • A Uttar Pradesh
  • B Bihar
  • C Tamil Nadu
  • D Maharashtra
Correct Option: C (Tamil Nadu)

André Béteille conducted his empirical village study in Sripuram, located in the Tanjore district of Tamil Nadu, analyzing the disjunction and overlap between caste, class, and power.

Q5 Which sociological concept developed by M.N. Srinivas explains the upward mobility and political assertion of many intermediate and OBC castes?
  • A Westernization
  • B Dominant Caste
  • C Little Tradition
  • D Sacred Complex
Correct Option: B (Dominant Caste)

The 'Dominant Caste' concept formulated by M.N. Srinivas refers to castes that hold numerical strength, economic power (land), and political clout, characteristics typical of many politically active OBC groups.

Q6 What was the primary criterion used by the Mandal Commission to determine social and educational backwardness?
  • A Purely religious denomination
  • B Eleven indicators grouped under social, educational, and economic criteria
  • C Strictly per capita income levels
  • D Urban residency status
Correct Option: B (Eleven indicators grouped under social, educational, and economic criteria)

The Mandal Commission utilized 11 indicators divided into social (backwardness of caste, manual labor of women, etc.), educational (school dropouts, literacy rates), and economic (value of family assets, sources of drinking water) categories.

Q7 Which book authored by Yogendra Singh explores the transformation of Indian society through the lens of structural modernization and tradition?
  • A Indian Village
  • B Modernization of Indian Tradition
  • C Social Change in Modern India
  • D Caste and Race in India
Correct Option: B (Modernization of Indian Tradition)

'Modernization of Indian Tradition' (1973) was authored by Yogendra Singh, providing a comprehensive sociological analysis of how Indian traditions adapt to modern institutional frameworks.

Q8 The Rohini Commission, constituted in 2017, deals with which of the following issues?
  • A Sub-categorization of Other Backward Classes (OBCs)
  • B Delimitation of parliamentary constituencies
  • C Abolition of zamindari system
  • D Formulation of tribal panchsheel
Correct Option: A (Sub-categorization of Other Backward Classes (OBCs))

The Rohini Commission was established under Article 340 to examine the sub-categorization of OBCs to ensure equitable distribution of reservation benefits among all backward communities.

Q9 According to Max Weber's theory of stratification, social classes are primarily formed on the basis of:
  • A Purely ritual purity and pollution
  • B Market situation and economic power
  • C Kinship and gotra exogamy
  • D Magico-religious rituals
Correct Option: B (Market situation and economic power)

Max Weber defined class in terms of economic position and life chances determined by the market situation (ownership of goods and services).

Q10 Which Articles of the Indian Constitution specifically enable the state to make special provisions for the advancement of socially and educationally backward classes (OBCs) regarding admission to educational institutions?
  • A Article 14 and 21
  • B Article 15(4) and 16(4)
  • C Article 32 and 226
  • D Article 48 and 50
Correct Option: B (Article 15(4) and 16(4))

Article 15(4) and Article 16(4) inserted by the First Constitutional Amendment (1951) empower the state to make special provisions for the advancement of any socially and educationally backward classes of citizens.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are OBCs sociologically distinguished from Scheduled Castes (SCs)?

Sociologically, SCs experienced historical social exclusion rooted in ritual pollution and 'Untouchability'. OBCs comprise intermediate agrarian, pastoral, and artisanal castes that faced social and educational backwardness and lack of political power, but did not suffer from ritual untouchability.

What is the sociological significance of the 'Creamy Layer'?

The 'Creamy Layer' criterion introduced in the 1992 Supreme Court Indra Sawhney judgment prevents economically advanced families within OBC groups from availing reservation benefits, ensuring that affirmative action reaches genuinely marginalized individuals within those communities.

How does M.N. Srinivas's concept of 'Dominant Caste' apply to OBCs?

Many OBC communities (such as Yadavs, Jats, Vokkaligas, and Lingayats) possess numerical strength and land ownership in rural areas, fitting Srinivas's definition of a dominant caste, which enables them to wield considerable political influence and drive social mobility.

Why is the Mandal Commission considered a watershed moment in Indian sociology and politics?

The Mandal Commission's implementation in 1990 catalyzed the rise of subaltern political parties, shifted power dynamics away from upper-caste dominance, and institutionalized caste as a primary axis of democratic mobilization and distributive justice in India.
