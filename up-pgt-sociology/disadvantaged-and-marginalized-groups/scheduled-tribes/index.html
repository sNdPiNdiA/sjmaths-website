Section 16 • Disadvantaged & Marginalized Groups

Scheduled Tribes

Scheduled Tribes (Adivasis) represent indigenous communities historically isolated from mainstream caste-stratified society, possessing distinct cultural traits, dialects, and socio-economic vulnerabilities. In UP PGT Sociology and UGC NET, the study of tribes involves examining their transition from 'isolated primitives' to 'assimilated/integrated citizens' through constitutional safeguards, sanskritization, and de-peasantization.

1. Definitional Framework, Etymology & Classical Foundations

The term 'Scheduled Tribes' (STs) derives its administrative validity from Article 366(25) of the Constitution of India, which refers to such tribes or tribal communities or parts of groups within tribes as deemed under Article 342. Etymologically, the word 'tribe' comes from the Latin tribus, originally denoting the three original divisions of the Roman people (Ramnes, Turies, Luceres).

Sociologically, classical theorists like Lewis Henry Morgan (Ancient Society, 1877) classified human social evolution into stages of Savagery, Barbarism, and Civilization, identifying tribal societies primarily with the lower/middle rungs of barbarism based on subsistence technology (hunting-gathering, shifting cultivation). Émile Durkheim, in The Elementary Forms of Religious Life (1912), used Australian aboriginal totemic clans as the foundational archetype to study the elementary forms of religion, social solidarity (mechanical solidarity), and collective conscience.

  • Key Theoretical Postulate: Tribal societies are characterized by segmentary social structures, kinship-based political organization, absence of complex state apparatuses, generalized reciprocity, and a low degree of economic differentiation.
  • G.S. Ghurye's Perspective: In his seminal work The Aborigines-'So-Called'-and Their Future (1943), Ghurye categorized Indian tribes as 'backward Hindus', arguing that they were gradually assimilated into the lower strata of the Hindu caste system through continuous cultural contact rather than remaining entirely distinct.
  • Verrier Elwin's Position: Contrasting Ghurye, Elwin (The Baiga, 1939; A Philosophy for NEFA, 1959) advocated a policy of 'national park' or isolation/protection for tribal communities, fearing exploitative contact with plainsmen would destroy their rich cultural fabric.

2. Structural Concepts, Empirical Processes & Indian Sociological Context

Indian sociology views tribes not as static or isolated entities, but as dynamic groups undergoing continuous interaction with the peasant and urban economies. N.K. Bose coined the term 'Hindu Method of Tribal Absorption' to explain how tribal groups entered the Hindu caste matrix as specialized occupational jatis. Surajit Sinha further elaborated on the 'tribe-caste continuum', demonstrating how state-formation processes in central India transformed tribal chiefs into Kshatriya kings and entire tribal aggregates into Hindu castes.

M.N. Srinivas observed that while Sanskritization operates powerfully among tribes seeking upward mobility, the process often leads to internal stratification and cultural loss. B.H. Mehta and L.P. Vidyarthi provided extensive empirical field studies on central Indian tribes (such as the Munda, Oraon, and Birhor). Vidyarthi propounded the 'Nature-Man-Spirit Complex' to explain the symbiotic worldview of simple forest-dwelling tribes.

  • The Isolation vs. Assimilation vs. Integration Debate: Jawaharlal Nehru, influenced by Verrier Elwin and Ghurye, steered Indian policy towards Tribal Panchsheel—respect for tribal rights in land, forest, culture, self-governance, and avoidance of over-administration.
  • Economic Stratification: Differentiation within tribes due to land alienation, migration, industrialization, and the emergence of a tribal middle class (elite capture of reservation benefits).

3. Contemporary Dynamics, Social Policy & Applied Dimensions

Post-independence India established robust constitutional provisions for the protection and development of Scheduled Tribes. These include Article 342 (Presidential notification of STs), Articles 15(4) and 16(4) (educational and public employment reservations), Article 275(1) (Grants-in-Aid for tribal welfare), Fifth Schedule (administration of tribal areas in states outside the Northeast), and Sixth Schedule (Autonomous District Councils in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram).

Landmark legislative milestones include the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (PESA), which extends Panchayati Raj to Fifth Schedule areas recognizing customary law and traditional governance systems, and the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 (FRA), which rectifies historical injustices by granting individual and community forest rights.

Contemporary sociological concerns revolve around displacement caused by mega-dams, mining, and industrial corridors (e.g., in Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, and Odisha), leading to forced proletarianization, cultural alienation, and extremist insurgencies (Naxalism/Left-Wing Extremism), as theorized through subaltern and Marxist paradigms.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Sociology

  • Article 342 of the Indian Constitution empowers the President to specify the tribes or tribal communities deemed to be Scheduled Tribes.
  • G.S. Ghurye authored 'The Aborigines-'So-Called'-and Their Future' (1943), terming tribes 'backward Hindus'.
  • Verrier Elwin advocated the 'National Park' policy in his early anthropological writings like 'The Baiga' (1939).
  • N.K. Bose introduced the concept of the 'Hindu Method of Tribal Absorption'.
  • L.P. Vidyarthi propounded the 'Nature-Man-Spirit Complex' in his study of the Maler tribe of Bihar.
  • The Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act (PESA) was enacted in 1996 to empower Gram Sabhas in Fifth Schedule areas.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Believing that all tribes in India speak Indo-Aryan or Dravidian languages. Fact: Indian tribes belong to four distinct linguistic families—Austro-Asiatic, Tibeto-Burman, Dravidian, and Indo-Aryan.
  • Misconception 2: Confusing the Fifth Schedule with the Sixth Schedule. Fact: Fifth Schedule applies to tribal areas in 10 mainland states with Governor's rule and Tribes Advisory Councils, whereas Sixth Schedule applies to tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram with Autonomous District Councils.

Q1 Who among the following Indian sociologists characterized Scheduled Tribes as 'backward Hindus' and argued for their gradual assimilation into mainstream society?
  • A Verrier Elwin
  • B G.S. Ghurye
  • C N.K. Bose
  • D M.N. Srinivas
Correct Option: B (G.S. Ghurye)

G.S. Ghurye in his 1943 book 'The Aborigines-'So-Called'-and Their Future' argued that Indian tribes are essentially backward Hindus who have been imperfectly integrated into Hindu society. Option 0 (Elwin) advocated initial isolation; Option 2 (Bose) spoke of the Hindu method of absorption; Option 3 (Srinivas) is famous for Sanskritization.

Q2 The concept 'Nature-Man-Spirit Complex' was propounded by which Indian anthropologist/sociologist?
  • A S.C. Dube
  • B L.P. Vidyarthi
  • C D.N. Majumdar
  • D N.K. Bose
Correct Option: B (L.P. Vidyarthi)

L.P. Vidyarthi propounded the 'Nature-Man-Spirit Complex' in his classic study of the Maler of the Santal Parganas, highlighting the deep ecological and spiritual integration of tribal life.

Q3 Which article of the Indian Constitution contains the provision for the specification of Scheduled Tribes?
  • A Article 330
  • B Article 338A
  • C Article 342
  • D Article 356
Correct Option: C (Article 342)

Article 342 empowers the President of India to public-notify tribes or tribal communities as Scheduled Tribes after consulting the Governor of the state.

Q4 The Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act was passed in which year?
  • A 1992
  • B 1994
  • C 1996
  • D 2001
Correct Option: C (1996)

The PESA Act was enacted in 1996 to extend the provisions of Panchasayats to Fifth Schedule areas, giving sweeping powers of self-governance to Gram Sabhas.

Q5 Who wrote the famous book 'The Baiga' (1939) advocating the protection of tribal culture from harmful external influences?
  • A W.H.R. Rivers
  • B Verrier Elwin
  • C S.C. Roy
  • D Ghanshyam Shah
Correct Option: B (Verrier Elwin)

Verrier Elwin authored 'The Baiga' (1939), wherein he passionately argued for the protection of Baiga tribal culture, famously suggesting an isolationist policy that later evolved into integrated tribal development.

Q6 The Fifth Schedule of the Constitution applies to tribal areas in which of the following?
  • A Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram
  • B States other than Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram with tribal populations
  • C Only Union Territories
  • D Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh
Correct Option: B (States other than Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram with tribal populations)

The Fifth Schedule applies to tribal-dominant areas in states outside the Northeast (Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram are covered under the Sixth Schedule).

Q7 N.K. Bose is well-known in Indian sociology for which of the following concepts?
  • A Sanskritization
  • B The Hindu Method of Tribal Absorption
  • C Dominant Caste
  • D Circulation of Elites
Correct Option: B (The Hindu Method of Tribal Absorption)

N.K. Bose propounded the 'Hindu Method of Tribal Absorption', explaining how various tribes entered Hindu caste society as functional occupational groups over centuries.

Q8 Which linguistic family has the largest concentration of tribal population in India?
  • A Indo-Aryan
  • B Dravidian
  • C Austro-Asiatic (Austro-asiatic/Munda)
  • D Tibeto-Burman
Correct Option: C (Austro-Asiatic (Austro-asiatic/Munda))

While Bhils and Gonds (Indo-Aryan/Dravidian speakers numerically dominate certain regions), Austro-Asiatic (Munda/Mon-Khmer) languages form a core indigenous stock across central India, and overall Scheduled Tribe linguistic distribution includes major groups under Austro-Asiatic, Dravidian, and Tibeto-Burman.

Q9 What was the primary recommendation of Jawaharlal Nehru's 'Tribal Panchsheel'?
  • A Forced conversion to mainstream religions
  • B Rapid industrialization of forest zones
  • C Development along lines of their own genius without external imposition
  • D Complete geographical isolation with no administrative contact
Correct Option: C (Development along lines of their own genius without external imposition)

Nehru's Tribal Panchsheel emphasized development along the lines of tribal people's own genius, respecting land rights, forest culture, and avoiding over-administration.

Q10 The Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act was enacted in:
  • A 1988
  • B 1996
  • C 2006
  • D 2013
Correct Option: C (2006)

The Forest Rights Act (FRA) was enacted in 2006 to recognize and vest forest rights and occupation in forest land in forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes and traditional forest dwellers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between the Fifth and Sixth Schedules of the Indian Constitution?

The Fifth Schedule deals with the administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes in states other than Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram, featuring Tribes Advisory Councils. The Sixth Schedule specifically provides for autonomous administrative governance through Autonomous District Councils (ADCs) in the tribal-majority areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram.

How do sociologists define the 'tribe-caste continuum'?

Popularized by scholars like Surajit Sinha and N.K. Bose, the tribe-caste continuum posits that Indian tribes and castes are not mutually exclusive polar opposites. Instead, through historical processes of acculturation, Hinduization, and economic integration, many tribes have gradually transitioned into lower-caste jatis, demonstrating a continuous fluid spectrum.

What are the key provisions of the PESA Act 1996?

The Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 extends Part IX of the Constitution to Fifth Schedule areas. It grants extensive powers to Gram Sabhas, including mandatory consultation on land acquisition, control over minor forest produce, management of minor water bodies, prevention of land alienation, and enforcement of traditional customary laws.

Why is G.S. Ghurye's approach to tribes termed 'assimilationist'?vibes

G.S. Ghurye viewed tribes as imperfectly integrated Hindus or 'backward Hindus' who had lagged behind in civilization. He advocated that their ultimate future lay in closer cultural contact and assimilation with mainstream Hindu society rather than artificial isolation.
