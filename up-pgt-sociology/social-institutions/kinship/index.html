Section 5 • Major Social Institutions

Kinship

Kinship represents one of the foundational social institutions in human society, regulating descent, marriage, alliance, and inheritance. In the context of UP PGT Sociology and UGC NET examinations, an exhaustive mastery of kinship systems, linguistic zones, terminologies, and Indian sociological contributions—most notably by Irawati Karve—is critical for structural-functional and descent-based analyses.

UP PGT Sociology • Code 16 Section 5

1. Definitional Framework, Etymology & Classical Foundations

The term kinship derives from the Old English word cyn (meaning family, tribe, or race) combined with scipe (denoting condition or state). Sociologically and anthropologically, kinship refers to a system of social relationships based on blood ties (consanguinity) and marital ties (affinity). It constitutes the primary web of social organization in pre-industrial, tribal, and agrarian societies, and continues to influence modern social structure.

  • A.R. Radcliffe-Brown's Formulation: In his seminal work African Systems of Kinship and Marriage (1950), Radcliffe-Brown defined the kinship system as a part of the social structure that links individuals into definitive networks of rights, obligations, and mutual dependencies based on biological and social recognition.
  • W.H.R. Rivers and Lewis Henry Morgan: Morgan's monumental work Systems of Consanguinity and Affinity of the Human Family (1871) established the distinction between descriptive and classificatory kinship terminologies, a framework essential for comparative sociological analysis.

2. Structural Concepts, Empirical Processes & Indian Sociological Context

Kinship in India exhibits extraordinary regional, linguistic, and cultural variation. The pioneering work of Irawati Karve in her classic text Kinship Organisation in India (1953) divided the country into four distinct kinship zones based on language families, marriage rules, and family structures:

  • Northern Zone (Indo-Aryan): Characterized by gotra and sapinda exogamy, village exogamy (marrying outside the village), patrilocal residence, and strict avoidance rules between specific kin members.
  • Southern Zone (Dravidian): Characterized by preferential cross-cousin marriage (mother's brother's daughter or father's sister's daughter), uncle-niece marriage, matrilocal or patrilocal options, and a classificatory terminology system that emphasizes alliance over strict genealogical distance.
  • Central Zone: A transitional zone exhibiting overlapping features of northern and southern systems, such as limited cross-cousin marriage alongside patrilineal dominance.
  • Eastern Zone: Dominated by Austro-Asiatic and Tibeto-Burman language-speaking tribal communities, featuring matrilineal systems (e.g., Khasi, Garo) and distinct clan structures.

Furthermore, structural anthropologists distinguish between descent (unilineal—patrilineal and matrilineal, double, and cognatic/bilateral) and alliance theory, propounded by Claude Lévi-Strauss in The Elementary Structures of Kinship (1949), which views marriage and exchange as the fundamental basis of social cohesion.

3. Contemporary Dynamics, Social Policy & Applied Dimensions

In contemporary Indian society, kinship networks undergo rapid transformation due to industrialization, urbanization, geographic mobility, and legal reforms. While traditional caste-endogamous and village-exogamous norms persist in rural interiors, urban settings witness an increase in nuclear families and bilateral kinship networks.

  • Legal Interventions: Legislation such as the Hindu Marriage Act (1955), Hindu Succession Act (1956, amended in 2005 granting coparcenary rights to daughters), and the Special Marriage Act (1954) have drastically altered traditional kinship obligations, inheritance rights, and gender hierarchies.
  • Kinship Usages: Practices such as avoidance (keeping physical or verbal distance between certain kin, e.g., father-in-law and daughter-in-law), joking relationships (permitted banter and sexual teasing between specific relatives, e.g., jija-sali), teknonomy (referring to parents through their children), avunculate (special importance attached to the maternal uncle), and amitate (special role of the paternal aunt) continue to regulate interpersonal behavior and maintain social equilibrium.

Memory Shortcut / Mnemonic
Irawati Karve's Four Kinship Zones of India
NESC (North, East, South, Central)

To remember Irawati Karve's geographical classification of kinship in India ('Kinship Organisation in India', 1953), recall the compass directions: North (Indo-Aryan, village exogamy), East (Tribal, matrilineal/patrilineal mix), South (Dravidian, cross-cousin marriage), and Central (Transitional zone).

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Sociology

  • Irawati Karve authored 'Kinship Organisation in India' in 1953, dividing India into four distinct kinship zones.
  • Lewis Henry Morgan established the fundamental distinction between 'descriptive' and 'classificatory' kinship in his 1871 classic 'Systems of Consanguinity and Affinity of the Human Family'.
  • Claude Lévi-Strauss formulated 'Alliance Theory' in 'The Elementary Structures of Kinship' (1949), emphasizing exchange and marriage over unilineal descent.
  • A.R. Radcliffe-Brown defined kinship in 'African Systems of Kinship and Marriage' (1950) as a network of social relationships based on consanguinity and affinity.
  • Avoidance, joking relationships, teknonomy, avunculate, and amitate are classical 'kinship usages' that regulate social distance and solidarity.
  • The Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act 2005 granted equal coparcenary rights to daughters in ancestral joint family property, directly altering traditional patrilineal inheritance.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Students often confuse 'consanguineal kin' with 'affinal kin'. Consanguineal kin are blood relatives, whereas affinal kin are relatives acquired through marriage.
  • Misconception 2: Many candidates assume cross-cousin marriage is universally practiced across India. In reality, it is strictly prohibited in the Northern zone due to sapinda and gotra exogamy rules, though it is culturally preferred in the Southern zone.

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Test your conceptual understanding. Select an answer and check the detailed pedagogical rationale.

Q1 Who authored the monumental sociological study 'Kinship Organisation in India' (1953)?
  • A M.N. Srinivas
  • B Irawati Karve
  • C G.S. Ghurye
  • D S.C. Dube
Correct Option: B (Irawati Karve)

Irawati Karve authored 'Kinship Organisation in India' (1953), providing the foundational regional classification of Indian kinship systems into Northern, Southern, Central, and Eastern zones. M.N. Srinivas is famous for 'Religion and Society among the Coorgs of South India' and the concepts of Sanskritization and Dominant Caste.

Q2 Which anthropologist is primarily associated with 'Alliance Theory' and published 'The Elementary Structures of Kinship' in 1949?
  • A A.R. Radcliffe-Brown
  • B Bronislaw Malinowski
  • C Claude Lévi-Strauss
  • D Lewis Henry Morgan
Correct Option: C (Claude Lévi-Strauss)

Claude Lévi-Strauss is the father of structural anthropology and formulated Alliance Theory in 'The Elementary Structures of Kinship' (1949), arguing that the incest taboo drives the exchange of women between groups, forming the basis of social structure.

Q3 What is the term used in kinship studies when a person is addressed or referred to through their child (e.g., 'Father of X')?
  • A Avunculate
  • B Teknonymy
  • C Amitate
  • D Levirate
Correct Option: B (Teknonymy)

Teknonymy is the kinship usage where adults are addressed or referred to by the names of their children. Avunculate refers to the special role of the maternal uncle, amitate to the paternal aunt, and levirate to marriage with a deceased brother's wife.

Q4 In the context of Indian kinship, which region is characterized by preferential cross-cousin and uncle-niece marriage?
  • A Northern Zone
  • B Central Zone
  • C Southern Zone
  • D Eastern Zone
Correct Option: C (Southern Zone)

The Southern (Dravidian) kinship zone is characterized by preferential cross-cousin marriage and uncle-niece marriage, in sharp contrast to the Northern zone which enforces strict gotra, sapinda, and village exogamy.

Q5 Who made the fundamental distinction between 'descriptive' and 'classificatory' kinship systems in 1871?
  • A E.B. Tylor
  • B Lewis Henry Morgan
  • C J.J. Bachofen
  • D Henry Maine
Correct Option: B (Lewis Henry Morgan)

Lewis Henry Morgan introduced the distinction between descriptive and classificatory kinship systems in his 1871 landmark publication 'Systems of Consanguinity and Affinity of the Human Family'.

Q6 Which kinship usage prescribes a special, privileged, and authoritative role to the maternal uncle (mother's brother)?
  • A Couvade
  • B Avunculate
  • C Amitate
  • D Sororate
Correct Option: B (Avunculate)

Avunculate refers to the institutionalized importance of the maternal uncle in many matrilineal and patrilineal societies. Couvade refers to ritual customs practiced by the father during pregnancy.

Q7 A kinship system where descent is traced exclusively through the father's line is known as:
  • A Matrilineal descent
  • B Patrilineal descent
  • C Bilateral descent
  • D Double descent
Correct Option: B (Patrilineal descent)

Patrilineal descent (agnatic descent) traces lineage, inheritance, and succession exclusively through male links from father to son.

Q8 Which amendment to Indian civil law significantly strengthened women's property rights by making daughters coparceners in Hindu Undivided Family property by birth?
  • A Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005
  • B Hindu Marriage Act, 1955
  • C Special Marriage Act, 1954
  • D Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961
Correct Option: A (Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005)

The Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 amended Section 6 of the 1956 Act, granting daughters equal coparcenary rights by birth in ancestral joint family property, transforming traditional patrilineal inheritance norms.

Q9 What does 'sapinda' exogamy imply in traditional Hindu kinship and marriage rules?
  • A Prohibition of marriage within the same village
  • B Prohibition of marriage between persons sharing common blood ancestors within specified generations
  • C Mandatory marriage between maternal cousins
  • D Obligatory marriage outside one's linguistic group
Correct Option: B (Prohibition of marriage between persons sharing common blood ancestors within specified generations)

Sapinda exogamy prohibits marriage between individuals who share common ancestors within specified genealogical limits (typically up to five generations on the mother's side and seven on the father's side).

Q10 Which sociological theorist defined kinship systems as part of the social structure maintaining solidarity through rights and obligations in 'African Systems of Kinship and Marriage' (1950)?
  • A Talcott Parsons
  • B A.R. Radcliffe-Brown
  • C Max Weber
  • D Émile Durkheim
Correct Option: B (A.R. Radcliffe-Brown)

A.R. Radcliffe-Brown, alongside Daryll Forde, edited 'African Systems of Kinship and Marriage' (1950), which remains a structural-functional benchmark for analyzing kinship as a mechanism of social integration.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between consanguineal and affinal kin in sociology?

Consanguineal kin are relatives connected by blood ties (e.g., parents, siblings, children), whereas affinal kin are relatives connected through marriage (e.g., spouses, in-laws).

How did Irawati Karve classify kinship in India?

Irawati Karve classified Indian kinship into four distinct regional zones—Northern, Southern, Central, and Eastern—based on language families, marriage rules, family structures, and inheritance customs.

What are kinship usages and why are they important?

Kinship usages are socially prescribed patterns of behavior, etiquette, and institutionalized interaction between specific relatives—such as avoidance, joking relationships, teknonomy, and avunculate—that maintain social order, resolve structural tensions, and regulate interpersonal conduct.

What is the significance of Lewis Henry Morgan's work on kinship?

Lewis Henry Morgan introduced the fundamental distinction between descriptive and classificatory kinship systems in 1871, laying the empirical and theoretical foundation for anthropological and sociological kinship studies worldwide.
