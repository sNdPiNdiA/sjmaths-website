Section 11 • Caste, Class & Rural Power

Caste System

The caste system represents a unique, hereditary, endogamous, and hierarchical form of social stratification deeply embedded in Indian society and Hindu social organization. This module examines its classical sociological definitions, structural foundations, theoretical paradigms, and contemporary empirical transformations vital for the UP PGT Sociology and UGC NET examinations.

UP PGT Sociology • Code 16 Section 11

1. Definitional Framework, Etymology & Classical Foundations

The term 'caste' derives from the Portuguese word 'casta', meaning breed, race, or lineage, which itself traces back to the Latin 'castus' (pure or chaste). Early European travelers used it to describe the complex socio-religious divisions observed in India. Sociologically, the caste system is conceptualized as an extreme form of closed social stratification where social status is ascribed at birth and is immutable throughout life.

  • G.S. Ghurye's Sixfold Characteristics: In his seminal work 'Caste and Race in India' (1932), Govind Sadashiv Ghurye outlined six structural features of the caste system: (1) Segmental division of society, (2) Hierarchy, (3) Civil and religious disabilities and privileges of different sections, (4) Lack of unrestricted choice of occupation, (5) Restriction on feeding and social intercourse, and (6) Endogamy (marriage within the caste).
  • Louis Dumont's Structuralist Approach: In 'Homo Hierarchicus' (1966), Dumont argued that the caste system cannot be understood through Western individualistic paradigms of power or class. Instead, it is governed by the overarching ideological principle of purity and pollution, where the opposition between the pure (Brahmin) and the impure (Shudra/Untouchable) forms the axis of hierarchy.
  • Max Weber on Caste and Status: Weber distinguished caste from class, noting that castes are status groups based on social honor and ritual purity rather than purely economic ownership of the means of production. He analyzed caste in relation to Hinduism, karma, and samsara in 'The Religion of India: The Sociology of Hinduism and Buddhism' (1916).

2. Structural Concepts, Empirical Processes & Indian Sociological Context

Understanding the operational reality of the caste system requires examining the distinction between Varna and Jati, alongside classical and modern empirical field studies conducted by Indian sociologists.

  • Varna vs. Jati: Varna refers to the pan-Indian theoretical fourfold division (Brahmin, Kshatriya Vaishya, Shudra) plus the Panchamas (outcastes), derived from the Purusha Sukta of the Rig Veda. Jati refers to the thousands of actual endogamous, localized occupational groups that constitute the empirical reality of the social structure.
  • M.N. Srinivas and Dominant Caste: Srinivas introduced the concepts of Sanskritization (the process by which lower castes adopt the customs, rituals, and lifestyle of upper castes to raise their ritual status) and Dominant Caste (a caste that wields numerical strength, economic power, and political influence in a local region, such as the Vokkaligas and Lingayats in Karnataka or Reddys and Kammas in Andhra Pradesh). Reference: 'Religion and Society Among the Coorgs of South India' (1952).
  • B.R. Ambedkar's Radical Critique: Ambedkar rejected the functionalist justification of caste, viewing it as an anti-social, divisive division of laborers rather than a rational division of labor. In his monumental, undelivered presidential address 'Annihilation of Caste' (1936), he argued that caste destroys public spirit, prevents national integration, and must be dismantled through inter-caste dining and inter-caste marriages.
  • Andre Beteille's Sripuram Study: In 'Caste, Class, and Power' (1965), Beteille demonstrated how the traditional overlapping hierarchy of caste, class, and power in a Tanjore village was undergoing disjunction due to modernization, adult franchise, and secular education.

3. Contemporary Dynamics, Social Policy & Applied Dimensions

In contemporary India, the caste system has undergone profound transformation rather than outright disappearance, shifting from ritual stratification to political mobilization and identity politics.

  • Yogendra Singh's Modernization of Indian Tradition (1973): Singh analyzed how structural changes such as industrialization, urbanization, legal reforms, and democratic decentralization have altered traditional stratification, leading to greater social mobility and the emergence of caste associations as interest groups.
  • Constitutional & Legislative Safeguards: Article 15 prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth; Article 17 abolishes 'Untouchability' (enforced via the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955, and the SC/ST Prevention of Atrocities Act, 1989); and Articles 15(4) and 16(4) provide for protective discrimination and affirmative action (reservations in educational institutions and public employment).
  • Caste and Politics: Rudolph and Rudolph ('The Modernity of Tradition', 1967) highlighted how traditional institutions like caste adapt to modern democratic processes through 'vertical mobilization' and 'horizontal mobilization', transforming caste into political pressure groups.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Sociology

  • The term 'caste' is derived from the Portuguese word 'casta', meaning breed, race, or purity of blood.
  • G.S. Ghurye published 'Caste and Race in India' in 1932, providing the first systematic sociological analysis of caste in India.
  • Louis Dumont's 'Homo Hierarchicus' (1966) posits that the caste system is founded on the ideological core of 'purity and pollution'.
  • M.N. Srinivas coined the concepts of 'Sanskritization' and 'Dominant Caste' in his study of the Coorgs of South India (1952).
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar wrote 'Annihilation of Caste' in 1936, offering a scathing critique of the Hindu social order.
  • Andre Beteille's 'Caste, Class, and Power' (1965) examined stratification in the Tanjore village of Sripuram.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Students often confuse 'Varna' and 'Jati' as identical terms. Sociologically, Varna is the pan-Indian theoretical fourfold stratification, whereas Jati is the empirical, localized, endogamous group.
  • Misconception 2: Assuming that Sanskritization results in structural change for the entire lower caste. Srinivas clarified that Sanskritization leads to positional change within the existing hierarchical framework, not structural/systemic change.

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Test your conceptual understanding. Select an answer and check the detailed pedagogical rationale.

Q1 Who among the following sociologists authored the seminal work 'Homo Hierarchicus: The Caste System and Its Implications'?
  • A G.S. Ghurye
  • B Louis Dumont
  • C M.N. Srinivas
  • D Andre Beteille
Correct Option: B (Louis Dumont)

'Homo Hierarchicus' was written by French sociologist Louis Dumont in 1966. He analyzed the Indian caste system through a structuralist lens, arguing that it is governed by the ideological principle of purity and pollution.

Q2 Which sociologist outlined the 'sixfold characteristics' of the caste system in India?
  • A D.P. Mukerji
  • B S.C. Dube
  • C G.S. Ghurye
  • D Yogendra Singh
Correct Option: C (G.S. Ghurye)

G.S. Ghurye outlined the six characteristics of caste (segmental division, hierarchy, civil/religious disabilities, restrictions on food/marriage, and hereditary occupation) in his 1932 book 'Caste and Race in India'.

Q3 The concept of 'Dominant Caste' was introduced by M.N. Srinivas based on his empirical study of which region/community?
  • A The Coorgs of Karnataka
  • B Rampura village in Mysore
  • C Sripuram in Tanjore
  • D Shamirpet in Andhra Pradesh
Correct Option: A (The Coorgs of Karnataka)

M.N. Srinivas introduced the concept of 'Dominant Caste' in his work 'Religion and Society Among the Coorgs of South India' (1952), later elaborating it in his studies of Rampura village.

Q4 Who is the author of the radical text 'Annihilation of Caste' (1936)?
  • A Mahatma Gandhi
  • B Jyotirao Phule
  • C B.R. Ambedkar
  • D Periyar E.V. Ramasamy
Correct Option: C (B.R. Ambedkar)

'Annihilation of Caste' was written by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar as a prepared speech for the Jat-Pat-Todak Mandal of Lahore in 1936, which was later published independently due to its radical critique of the Hindu caste structure.

Q5 According to Louis Dumont, what is the fundamental underlying ideology of the caste system?
  • A Economic exploitation and surplus value
  • B Purity and pollution
  • C Political power and coercion
  • D Racial supremacy and colonization
Correct Option: B (Purity and pollution)

Louis Dumont argued that the caste system is primarily religious and ideological, built squarely upon the opposition between purity and pollution.

Q6 Which of the following books was authored by Andre Beteille examining the intersection of caste, class, and power?
  • A Indian Village
  • B Caste and Race in India
  • C Caste, Class, and Power
  • D Modernization of Indian Tradition
Correct Option: C (Caste, Class, and Power)

'Caste, Class, and Power: Changing Patterns of Stratification in a Tanjore Village' (1965) was authored by Andre Beteille, studying the village of Sripuram.

Q7 What does 'Sanskritization' primarily imply in Indian sociology?
  • A Economic upward mobility through industrial employment
  • B Positional change in hierarchy by adopting upper-caste customs
  • C Complete abolition of caste inequalities through legal action
  • D Adoption of Western education and democratic values
Correct Option: B (Positional change in hierarchy by adopting upper-caste customs)

M.N. Srinivas defined Sanskritization as a process whereby a lower caste or tribe adopts the customs, rituals, and beliefs of Brahmins or other high castes to achieve a higher position in the caste hierarchy.

Q8 Which constitutional article in India explicitly abolishes 'Untouchability' and forbids its practice in any form?
  • A Article 14
  • B Article 15
  • C Article 16
  • D Article 17
Correct Option: D (Article 17)

Article 17 of the Indian Constitution abolishes 'Untouchability' and makes its practice in any form a punishable offense under the law.

Q9 Who among the following scholars analyzed caste as a status group based on social honor rather than purely economic class?
  • A Karl Marx
  • B Max Weber
  • C Emile Durkheim
  • D Auguste Comte
Correct Option: B (Max Weber)

Max Weber analyzed social stratification through a multidimensional approach (Class, Status, Party), associating caste primarily with status groups characterized by specific social honor and ritual lifestyles.

Q10 The term 'Jati' refers to:
  • A The pan-Indian fourfold theoretical stratification
  • B The localized, endogamous, hereditary occupational group
  • C The modern political mobilization of backward classes
  • D The constitutional category for scheduled castes
Correct Option: B (The localized, endogamous, hereditary occupational group)

While 'Varna' is the pan-Indian theoretical framework, 'Jati' refers to the actual, empirical, localized, endogamous kinship and occupational groups that form the everyday reality of the Indian caste system.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fundamental difference between Varna and Jati in Indian sociology?

Varna is a pan-Indian, theoretical, normative fourfold classification (plus outcastes) found in classical texts, whereas Jati refers to the thousands of actual, localized, endogamous, and hereditary occupational groups that constitute the empirical reality of the caste system.

How did M.N. Srinivas define 'Dominant Caste'?

M.N. Srinivas defined a dominant caste as one that possesses numerical strength, economic power (land ownership), political influence, and ritual status within a local region, allowing it to exert decisive control over other local groups.

What is Louis Dumont's central thesis in 'Homo Hierarchicus'?

Louis Dumont argued that the Indian caste system is an integrated religious system founded on the ideological opposition between 'purity and pollution', rejecting economic or political reductionism.

How has the caste system transformed in contemporary democratic India?

In contemporary India, caste has shifted from ritual stratification to identity politics and competitive democratic mobilization. Through 'horizontal mobilization', caste groups function as political pressure groups and interest associations to secure resource distribution and state reservations.
