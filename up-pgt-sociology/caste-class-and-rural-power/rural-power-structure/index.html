Section 11 • Caste, Class & Rural Power

Rural Power Structure

The study of rural power structure in Indian sociology examines the distribution of authority, influence, and dominance within agrarian communities. It explores how traditional axes of caste and land ownership intersect with modern democratic institutions, transforming local politics and leadership patterns.

UP PGT Sociology • Code 16 Section 11

1. Definitional Framework, Etymology & Classical Foundations

Power in sociology, following Max Weber, is defined as the probability that an actor within a social relationship will be in a position to carry out his own will despite resistance. In the agrarian context of India, this power is institutionalized through economic control, ritual status, and numerical strength. Classical and modern sociologists study rural power not merely as a political phenomenon, but as a total social fact intertwined with stratification systems.

  • Weberian Tri-partite Stratification: Max Weber separates stratification into Class (economic order), Status (social/ritual order), and Party (power/political order). In rural India, these three dimensions historically converged in the upper-caste, landowning elite, but modern democratic processes have caused a disjuncture among them.
  • Elite Theories: Vilfredo Pareto and Gaetano Mosca explain power through the circulation of elites and organized minority rule. In rural settings, power transitions from traditional agrarian oligarchies to neo-rich intermediaries and democratic power brokers.

2. Structural Concepts, Empirical Processes & Indian Sociological Context

Indian sociology has generated rich empirical literature regarding rural power through village studies. M.N. Srinivas introduced the concept of the Dominant Caste in his study of Rampura village (published in The Social System of a Mysore Village, 1955), noting that a caste is dominant when it wields numerical strength, economic power (land ownership), and political backing, regardless of its ritual hierarchy.

André Béteille's classical monograph Caste, Class, and Power (1965), based on his study of Sripuram village in Tamil Nadu, demonstrated how caste, class, and power—historically congruent—were becoming increasingly differentiated with the advent of modern education, adult franchise, and statutory panchayats.

Other key contributors include S.C. Dube (Indian Village, 1955, examining Shamirpet), who highlighted factionalism and leadership patterns, and Ramakrishna Mukherjee, who analyzed rural class structures through a Marxian framework.

3. Contemporary Dynamics, Social Policy & Applied Dimensions

Post-independence land reforms (Zamindari abolition), the Green Revolution, and the 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act (1992)—which institutionalized the three-tier Panchayati Raj system with mandatory reservations for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Women—have radically altered the rural power matrix.

While traditional feudal lords have lost absolute sway, new rural elites have emerged through control over cooperatives, credit societies, educational institutions, and political party networks. The rise of 'subaltern assertiveness' and Bahujan politics has further contested traditional upper-caste hegemony in rural North India, leading to intense local factionalism and caste-based political mobilization.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP PGT Sociology

  • M.N. Srinivas coined the term 'Dominant Caste' in his seminal paper 'The Dominant Caste in Rampura' (1959).
  • André Béteille authored 'Caste, Class, and Power: Changing Patterns of Stratification in a Tanjore Village' in 1965.
  • The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act (1992) gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj institutions, mandating 33% reservation for women.
  • S.C. Dube's monograph 'Indian Village' (1955) is based on his intensive field study of Shamirpet village near Hyderabad.
  • Max Weber analyzed power as the probability of imposing one's will within a social relationship, distinct from authority (Herrschaft) which implies legitimacy.
  • Daniel Thorner categorized the agrarian class structure in India into Malik, Kisan, and Mazdoor.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Students often equate 'Dominant Caste' exclusively with the highest ritual status (Brahmin). Sociologically, a dominant caste is often a middle or intermediate peasant caste possessing land and numerical strength (e.g., Vokkaligas, Lingayats, Jats, Reddys).
  • Misconception 2: Assuming the Jajmani system was purely economic. It is a traditional socio-economic-ritual network of reciprocal obligations among castes in rural India.

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Test your conceptual understanding. Select an answer and check the detailed pedagogical rationale.

Q1 Who among the following sociologists authored the classic study 'Caste, Class, and Power' based on Sripuram village?
  • A M.N. Srinivas
  • B André Béteille
  • C S.C. Dube
  • D D.P. Mukerji
Correct Option: B (André Béteille)

André Béteille conducted his fieldwork in Sripuram village in Thanjavur district, Tamil Nadu, resulting in his landmark publication 'Caste, Class, and Power' (1965), which examined the changing patterns of social stratification.

Q2 The concept of 'Dominant Caste' was first explicitly formulated by M.N. Srinivas in relation to which village?
  • A Shamirpet
  • B Sripuram
  • C Rampura
  • D Kishangarh
Correct Option: C (Rampura)

M.N. Srinivas formulated the concept of the Dominant Caste based on his extensive anthropological fieldwork in Rampura village in Mysore (Karnataka).

Q3 Which Constitutional Amendment Act gave statutory status and local self-government powers to Panchayati Raj Institutions in rural India?
  • A 42nd Amendment Act
  • B 44th Amendment Act
  • C 73rd Amendment Act
  • D 74th Amendment Act
Correct Option: C (73rd Amendment Act)

The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992, added Part IX to the Constitution of India, providing constitutional status and mandatory institutional structure to rural Panchayati Raj bodies.

Q4 According to Max Weber, power is primarily situated within which order of social stratification?
  • A Economic order (Class)
  • B Social/Ritual order (Status)
  • C Political order (Party)
  • D Cultural order (Ideology)
Correct Option: C (Political order (Party))

In Weber's multidimensional theory, class belongs to the economic order, status to the social/cultural order, and 'party' (power) explicitly pertains to the political order and organizational capability.

Q5 Who classified the agrarian population of India into 'Malik', 'Kisan', and 'Mazdoor'?
  • A A.R. Desai
  • B Daniel Thorner
  • C Radhakamal Mukerjee
  • D B.R. Ambedkar
Correct Option: B (Daniel Thorner)

Daniel Thorner, in 'The Agrarian Prospect in India' (1956), utilized the categories of Malik (property owners), Kisan (working peasants/tenants), and Mazdoor (agricultural laborers) to analyze rural class structure.

Q6 Which of the following is NOT a necessary criterion for a caste to be considered 'Dominant' according to M.N. Srinivas?
  • A Numerical strength
  • B Economic power (control over land)
  • C Political representation
  • D Highest position in ritual purity (Varna hierarchy)
Correct Option: D (Highest position in ritual purity (Varna hierarchy))

Srinivas explicitly noted that a dominant caste need not occupy the highest ritual position in the Varna hierarchy; intermediate or even lower castes can be dominant if they possess land, numbers, and political muscle.

Q7 S.C. Dube's pioneering village study 'Indian Village' (1955) was conducted in which state?
  • A Uttar Pradesh
  • B Andhra Pradesh
  • C Karnataka
  • D Tamil Nadu
Correct Option: B (Andhra Pradesh)

S.C. Dube conducted his sociological study of Shamirpet, which formed the basis of 'Indian Village' (1955), located in the state of Andhra Pradesh (formerly Hyderabad state).

Q8 In the context of rural power structures, what does the term 'Jajmani System' primarily denote?
  • A A system of cooperative farming
  • B A hereditary system of patron-client socio-economic and ritual obligations
  • C A democratic electoral process in village panchayats
  • D A land revenue collection system introduced by the British
Correct Option: B (A hereditary system of patron-client socio-economic and ritual obligations)

The Jajmani system is a traditional rural socio-economic institution where lower-caste service groups (Kamin) provide customary services to upper-caste landowning patrons (Jajman) in exchange for fixed shares of harvest.

Q9 Which sociological approach emphasizes that rural power structures cannot be understood without examining historical colonial interventions and land revenue settlements?
  • A Functionalist approach
  • B Structural-Functional approach
  • C Historical-Marxian approach
  • D Indological approach
Correct Option: C (Historical-Marxian approach)

The Historical-Marxian approach, championed by scholars like A.R. Desai and D.P. Mukerji, stresses the impact of colonial policies (such as Permanent Settlement and Ryotwari) in reshaping rural class and power equations.

Q10 What has been the general impact of the Green Revolution on rural power dynamics in India?
  • A Complete eradication of caste inequalities
  • B Consolidation of power among middle peasant proprietors (neo-rich/kulaks)
  • C Total empowerment of landless agricultural laborers
  • D Return of feudal landlordism
Correct Option: B (Consolidation of power among middle peasant proprietors (neo-rich/kulaks))

The Green Revolution economically strengthened the middle peasant castes who owned land (such as Jats, Patidars, Reddys), converting economic gains into assertive regional political power and challenging traditional upper-caste dominance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is meant by the term 'Rural Power Structure' in Indian Sociology?

Rural power structure refers to the institutionalized pattern of authority, dominance, and decision-making within agrarian communities. Historically based on caste hierarchy, ritual status, and land ownership, it has evolved due to democratic decentralization, universal adult franchise, and land reforms.

How does M.N. Srinivas define a 'Dominant Caste'?

According to M.N. Srinivas, a caste is dominant when it wields numerical strength, economic power (particularly land ownership), political influence, and a relatively high ritual status, allowing it to control local village affairs.

Why is André Béteille's study of Sripuram significant for PGT exams?

Béteille's 'Caste, Class, and Power' (1965) is crucial because it empirically proved that caste, class, and power—which were overlapping and congruent in traditional agrarian settings—were becoming structurally differentiated in modern India.

What role did the 73rd Constitutional Amendment play in rural power dynamics?

The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act (1992) institutionalized the Panchayati Raj system, introducing mandatory reservations for women and marginalized castes (SCs/STs). This decentralized power and challenged traditional elite monopolies by opening leadership roles to new social groups.
