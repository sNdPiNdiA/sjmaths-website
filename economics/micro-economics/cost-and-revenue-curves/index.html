Micro Economics

Cost and Revenue Analysis: Short-Run and Long-Run Curves

Cost and Revenue Analysis forms the bedrock of microeconomic theory of the firm, governing profit-maximizing output decisions across varying time horizons. Mastery of short-run and long-run cost curves alongside average and marginal revenue relations is vital for high-stakes competitive examinations like UP PGT Economics and UGC-NET.

🏛️ Key Economists & Landmark Publications

Adam Smith
1776, 'The Wealth of Nations'

Introduced the foundational concepts of division of labor and specialization, which underpin economies of scale in long-run cost structures.

Alfred Marshall
1890, 'Principles of Economics'

Formulated the distinction between the short-run (fixed plant size) and long-run (variable plant size), and introduced external and internal economies of scale.

Jacob Viner
1931, 'Cost Curves and Supply Curves'

Highlighted the geometric error in drawing the long-run average cost curve as the lower envelope of short-run average cost curves, clarifying tangency points.

Edward Chamberlin and Joan Robinson
1933, Monopolistic and Imperfect Competition Treatises

Developed modern revenue analysis under imperfect competition, establishing the relationship between Average Revenue (AR), Marginal Revenue (MR), and Price Elasticity of Demand.

⚖️ Fundamental Assumptions & Hypotheses

  • Profit Maximization: The firm's singular objective is to maximize economic profit ($\pi = TR - TC$), guiding all output decisions.
  • Rational Behavior: Entrepreneurs and managers possess complete information and act rationally to minimize costs for any given output level.
  • Homogeneous Inputs: Factors of production are assumed to be perfectly divisible and uniform in quality within each category.
  • Constant Factor Prices: Input prices remain invariant regardless of the quantity purchased by the individual firm (perfectly elastic factor supply).
  • Short-Run Fixed Plant: In the short run, at least one factor of production (typically capital, $K$) is fixed, while labor ($L$) remains variable.
  • Long-Run Variable Scale: In the long run, all factors of production, including plant size and capital equipment, are completely variable.

1. Short-Run Cost Curves: Total, Average, and Marginal Dimensions

In the short run, Total Cost ($TC$) is divided into Total Fixed Cost ($TFC$) and Total Variable Cost ($TVC$). Correspondingly, Average Cost ($AC$) comprises Average Fixed Cost ($AFC$) and Average Variable Cost ($AVC$). $AFC$ is a rectangular hyperbola continuously falling as output increases. $AVC$, $ATC$, and Marginal Cost ($MC$) exhibit U-shapes driven by the Law of Variable Proportions (diminishing marginal returns). The $MC$ curve intersects both $AVC$ and $ATC$ at their respective minimum points.

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • AFC curve is asymptotic to both axes, representing a rectangular hyperbola.
  • MC curve intersects AVC and ATC strictly at their minimum points.
  • When MC < AC, AC is falling; when MC > AC, AC is rising.

2. Long-Run Cost Curves: The Envelope Curve and Scale Economies

The Long-Run Average Cost ($LRAC$) curve is derived as the lower envelope of an infinite family of Short-Run Average Cost ($SRAC$) curves. It displays a saucer or U-shape, driven by economies of scale (decreasing costs), constant returns to scale (constant costs), and diseconomies of scale (increasing costs). Viner's realization clarified that the $LRAC$ is tangent to $SRAC$ curves at their minimum points only at the minimum point of the $LRAC$ itself; for falling $LRAC$, it touches $SRAC$ to the left of the $SRAC$ minimum, and to the right for rising $LRAC$.

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • LRAC is known as the envelope curve or planning curve of the firm.
  • LRAC touches SRAC at its minimum only at the minimum point of LRAC (optimum scale of plant).
  • Long-run marginal cost (LRMC) intersects LRAC from below at the minimum of LRAC.

3. Revenue Analysis: Total, Average, and Marginal Revenue Framework

Revenue curves define receipts received by a firm from selling output. Average Revenue ($AR$) is total revenue divided by quantity, identical to the demand curve facing the firm. Marginal Revenue ($MR$) is the change in total revenue resulting from selling an additional unit. Under perfect competition, price is constant, making $AR = MR = P$ (horizontal demand curve). Under imperfect competition (monopoly, monopolistic competition), the demand curve slopes downward, causing $MR$ to lie below $AR$ and fall at twice the rate of $AR$.

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • AR curve is identically the firm's demand curve.
  • Under monopoly, MR is positive when demand is elastic, zero when unitary elastic, and negative when inelastic.
  • AR = P always, regardless of market structure.

4. Elasticity of Demand and Marginal Revenue Relationship

A fundamental identity links price, marginal revenue, and price elasticity of demand ($e_p$): $MR = P \cdot (1 - \frac{1}{e_p})$. This formula establishes that when demand is elastic ($e_p > 1$), $MR$ is positive. When demand is unitary elastic ($e_p = 1$), $MR$ is zero, and Total Revenue ($TR$) is at its maximum. When demand is inelastic ($e_p < 1$), $MR$ is negative, meaning the firm can increase $TR$ by reducing output and raising price.

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • At maximum Total Revenue, Marginal Revenue is exactly equal to zero ($MR = 0$).
  • MR is negative when price elasticity of demand is less than unity ($e_p < 1$).
  • The formula $MR = P(1 - 1/e_p)$ is a recurring favorite in UGC-NET numerical questions.

📐 Mathematical Formulations & Equilibrium Conditions

Short-Run Cost Identity
TC = TFC + TVC ⇒ ATC = AFC + AVC = TFCQ + TVCQ

Deconstructs total expenditure into fixed overheads and variable operating costs per unit of output.

Marginal Cost Definition
MC = d(TC)dQ = d(TVC)dQ

Measures the instantaneous rate of change in total cost with respect to output; TFC drops out because its derivative with respect to Q is zero.

Relationship between AR, MR, and Elasticity
MR = AR · 1 - 1ep

Proves mathematically how the gap between AR and MR depends entirely on the price elasticity of demand ($e_p$).

Profit Maximization Condition
d(π)dQ = MR - MC = 0 ⇒ MR = MC

First-order condition for profit maximization; second-order condition requires $\frac{d(MC)}{dQ} > \frac{d(MR)}{dQ}$ (MC must be rising or intersecting MR from below).

Output Elasticity of Cost
EC = \% Δ TC\% Δ Q = dTC/TCdQ/Q = MCAC

Measures scale economies: $E_C < 1$ indicates economies of scale, $E_C = 1$ constant returns, and $E_C > 1$ diseconomies of scale.

🧠 Exam Tips, Tricks & Mnemonics (10-Second Shortcuts)

💡 Elasticity and MR Shortcut E-U-I Rule for MR (Elastic = Positive, Unitary = Zero, Inelastic = Negative)
Remember: e > 1 -> MR > 0; e = 1 -> MR = 0; e < 1 -> MR < 0.

Exam Application: Instantly solves questions asking about Total Revenue behavior when a monopolist changes price in different elasticity zones.

💡 MC Intersection Rule Scissors Blade Rule (Marshallian)
MC always cuts AC and AVC from below at their absolute minimum points.

Exam Application: Eliminate incorrect graph options in UP PGT exams where MC cuts AC while AC is still falling.

💡 Cost Elasticity Scale Test MC/AC Ratio
If MC < AC, economies of scale prevail; if MC > AC, diseconomies prevail.

Exam Application: Fast calculation in numericals where AC and MC are given to determine returns to scale.

⚠️ Economic Paradoxes & Boundary Exceptions

Exception / Paradox Viner's Tangency Controversy

Conditions: Jacob Viner instructed his draftsman to draw an LRAC curve passing through the minimum points of all SRAC curves, which geometrically proved impossible.

Diagrammatic Behavior: Draftsman's error showed LRAC intersecting SRAC at their minimums, whereas mathematically LRAC can only touch the minimum of the optimum SRAC.

Exception / Paradox Shut-Down Point in Short-Run

Conditions: When price falls below Average Variable Cost ($P < AVC$), the firm minimizes losses by shutting down production immediately.

Diagrammatic Behavior: The supply curve of the firm in the short run is the portion of the MC curve lying at or above the minimum point of AVC.

Exception / Paradox L-Shaped Long-Run Average Cost Curve (Stigler / Johnston)

Conditions: Empirical studies reveal that LRAC does not rise at higher outputs due to diseconomies, but rather flattens out at a minimum plateau.

Diagrammatic Behavior: LRAC falls sharply due to initial scale economies and then becomes horizontal (L-shaped) instead of U-shaped, invalidating traditional rising diseconomies.

⚡ Must-Remember Economic Identities & Laws

  • Law of Variable Proportions: As more units of a variable input are applied to a fixed input, the marginal product of the variable input eventually declines, causing marginal cost to eventually rise.
  • MR and Price Elasticity Identity: MR = P * (1 - 1/|ED|), demonstrating that MR is positive when demand is elastic (|ED| > 1), zero when unit elastic (|ED| = 1), and negative when inelastic (|ED| < 1).
  • Cost-Productivity Relationship: MC = w / MPL (where w is the wage rate and MPL is marginal product of labor), proving that MC is at its minimum when MPL is at its maximum.
  • AVC-APL Relationship: AVC = w / APL (where APL is average product of labor), proving that AVC is at its minimum when APL is at its maximum.
  • Expansion Path Tangency Condition: Long-run equilibrium requires MR = LMC and tangency of the firm's isoquant and isocost lines where MR = LAC at the minimum point of LAC only under constant returns to scale.
  • Relationship between ATC and MC: When MC < ATC, ATC is falling; when MC > ATC, ATC is rising; when MC = ATC, ATC is at its minimum point.
  • Euler's Theorem on Product Exhaustion: If the production function exhibits constant returns to scale, total output is exactly exhausted when each factor is paid the value of its marginal product.

🚫 Common Analytical Confusions (Contrast Table)

Short-Run Average Cost Minimum vs. Long-Run Average Cost Minimum VS Long-Run Average Cost Tangency

SAC minimum points are not generally tangent to the LAC curve except at the single output level where LAC is at its absolute minimum (optimum scale of plant). On the falling portion of LAC, SAC touches LAC to the right of SAC's minimum; on the rising portion of LAC, SAC touches LAC to the left of SAC's minimum.

Average Revenue Curve vs. Marginal Revenue Curve VS Demand Curve for a Firm

Under perfect competition, AR, MR, and Demand are identical horizontal lines (P = AR = MR). Under imperfect competition (monopoly/monopolistic competition), AR is the downward-sloping demand curve, and the MR curve lies strictly below the AR curve with twice the slope.

Internal Economies of Scale VS External Economies of Scale

Internal economies depend exclusively on the firm's own scale of operations and shift its individual LAC downward. External economies depend on industry-wide growth and development, shifting the LAC curves of all firms in the industry downward.

Shut-down Point in Short-Run VS Break-even Point in Short-Run

The short-run shut-down point occurs where price equals minimum Average Variable Cost (P = AVC); below this, the firm cannot cover variable costs and stops production. The break-even point occurs where price equals minimum Average Total Cost (P = ATC), where economic profit is zero.

📚 Complete Concept-by-Concept Glossary

Every fundamental term, curve, and mechanism covered in this chapter.

Total Fixed Cost (TFC): Costs that do not vary with the level of output in the short run; expenditures on fixed factors such as plant, land, and top management.
Total Variable Cost (TVC): Costs that change directly with output in the short run; expenditures on variable inputs such as raw materials, direct labor, and power.
Average Fixed Cost (AFC): Total fixed cost divided by the quantity of output produced (TFC/Q); it declines continuously as output increases, forming a rectangular hyperbola.
Average Variable Cost (AVC): Total variable cost divided by the quantity of output produced (TVC/Q); initially falls, reaches a minimum, and then rises, forming a U-shape.
Average Total Cost (ATC): Total cost divided by output (TC/Q or AFC + AVC); represents cost per unit of output and reaches its minimum at a higher output level than AVC.
Marginal Cost (MC): The addition to total cost resulting from producing one additional unit of output (dTC/dQ); intersects AVC and ATC at their respective minimum points.
Planning Curve (Long-Run Average Cost - LAC): The envelope curve tangent to a series of short-run average cost (SAC) curves, representing the lowest possible average cost for any output level when all factors are variable.
Economies of Scale: Cost advantages that enterprises obtain due to size, output, or scale of operation, with cost per unit of output generally decreasing with increasing scale.
Diseconomies of Scale: Cost disadvantages that occur when a firm grows too large, leading to managerial inefficiencies, communication breakdowns, and rising long-run average costs.
Total Revenue (TR): The total amount of money a firm receives from the sale of its output, calculated as price multiplied by quantity (P × Q).
Average Revenue (AR): Revenue generated per unit of output sold (TR/Q); identically equal to the price of the product under all market structures.
Marginal Revenue (MR): The change in total revenue resulting from the sale of one additional unit of output (dTR/dQ); reflects the slope of the total revenue curve.
Lerner Index: A measure of monopoly power calculated as (P - MC)/P, which is inversely related to the price elasticity of demand (|ED|).
Envelope Curve: A curve that is tangent to a family of curves (such as LAC tangent to SACs); in the long run, LAC is the lower envelope of all SAC curves.

Question 1 of 18

If the total cost function is given by TC = 100 + 10Q - 4Q^2 + Q^3, what is the equation for the Average Variable Cost (AVC)?

Explanation: Total Cost is TC = TFC + TVC. Here TFC = 100 and TVC = 10Q - 4Q^2 + Q^3. AVC is TVC divided by Q, which equals (10Q - 4Q^2 + Q^3)/Q = 10 - 4Q + Q^2.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following curves is U-shaped due to the operation of the law of variable proportions?

Explanation: The Average Variable Cost (AVC) curve is U-shaped because of the law of variable proportions (increasing returns followed by diminishing returns to the variable factor). AFC is a rectangular hyperbola, and TFC is a horizontal straight line.

Question 3 of 18

The long-run average cost (LAC) curve is often called an envelope curve because:

Explanation: The LAC curve is tangent to a series of short-run average cost (SAC) curves, each representing a different plant size. Hence, it forms an 'envelope' over the SAC curves.

Question 4 of 18

When the price elasticity of demand (|ED|) for a product is equal to 1, Marginal Revenue (MR) is:

Explanation: Using the formula MR = P(1 - 1/|ED|), if |ED| = 1, then 1 - 1/1 = 0. Therefore, MR = 0 when demand is unitary elastic.

Question 5 of 18

At the minimum point of the Long-Run Average Cost (LAC) curve:

Explanation: At the minimum point of the U-shaped LAC curve, LAC equals LMC, and it is tangent to the minimum point of the corresponding SAC curve that represents the optimum scale of plant.

Question 6 of 18

If the marginal cost curve lies above the average total cost curve, then:

Explanation: Whenever marginal cost is greater than average total cost (MC > ATC), it pulls the average up, meaning ATC is rising. When MC < ATC, ATC is falling; when MC = ATC, ATC is at its minimum.

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following represents the relationship between Average Revenue (AR), Marginal Revenue (MR), and Price Elasticity of Demand (|ED|)?

Explanation: Since AR equals Price (P), the identity is MR = P (1 - 1/|ED|) or MR = AR (1 - 1/|ED|).

Question 8 of 18

Internal economies of scale include all of the following EXCEPT:

Explanation: A pooled labor market resulting in skilled labor availability for all firms in an industrial cluster is an external economy of scale, not an internal one.

Question 9 of 18

What is the shape of the Average Fixed Cost (AFC) curve?

Explanation: AFC = TFC / Q. Since TFC is constant, AFC varies inversely with Q, forming a rectangular hyperbola asymptotic to both axes.

Question 10 of 18

A firm shuts down production in the short run if the price is:

Explanation: In the short run, if P < min AVC, the firm cannot even cover its variable costs. Producing anything would increase its losses beyond total fixed costs, so it shuts down.

Question 11 of 18

If total revenue increases as output increases, marginal revenue must be:

Explanation: Marginal revenue is the slope of the total revenue curve (dTR/dQ). If total revenue is increasing with output, the slope is positive, hence MR > 0.

Question 12 of 18

The plant curve refers to:

Explanation: Each Short-Run Average Cost (SAC) curve is associated with a specific fixed plant size, hence SAC is often termed the 'plant curve'.

Question 13 of 18

Economies of scale occur primarily because:

Explanation: Economies of scale arise from specialization, division of labor, spreading of lumpy overheads/fixed costs, and technical efficiencies associated with large-scale production.

Question 14 of 18

If the demand curve is linear and downward sloping, the marginal revenue curve:

Explanation: If P = a - bQ, then TR = P*Q = aQ - bQ^2, and MR = dTR/dQ = a - 2bQ. Thus, MR starts at the same vertical intercept 'a' and declines twice as fast (slope -2b versus -b).

Question 15 of 18

In the long-run equilibrium of a perfectly competitive industry, firms operate at:

Explanation: Under long-run competitive equilibrium, free entry and exit ensure that price equals minimum LAC and minimum SAC, earning zero economic profit.

Question 16 of 18

What is the relationship between Marginal Cost (MC) and Average Product of Labor (APL), given a constant wage rate (w)?

Explanation: AVC = TVC / Q = (w * L) / Q = w / (Q / L) = w / APL. Thus, AVC varies inversely with APL, and minimum AVC corresponds to maximum APL.

Question 17 of 18

If a firm operates on the falling portion of its Long-Run Average Cost curve, it is experiencing:

Explanation: When LAC is falling as output expands, the firm experiences economies of scale.

Question 18 of 18

A monopolistic firm will never operate on which section of its demand curve?

Explanation: A profit-maximizing monopolist operates where MR = MC. Since MC > 0, MR must be positive. MR is positive only when demand is elastic (|ED| > 1). Thus, a profit-maximizing monopolist will never operate on the inelastic or unit elastic portions of its demand curve.

Previous Year Pattern • Q4

If price elasticity of demand for a commodity is 2, what is the ratio of Price to Marginal Revenue (P/MR)?

A. 1 : 2
B. 2 : 1
C. 1 : 1
D. 4 : 1
Solution: Using the identity: MR = P * (1 - 1/|ED|). Given |ED| = 2: MR = P (1 - 1/2) = P (0.5) = P / 2. Therefore, P / MR = 2 / 1, or 2:1.

Test Item 1 of 10

If a firm's production function exhibits constant returns to scale throughout, what is the shape of its Long-Run Average Cost (LAC) curve?

Rationale: Constant returns to scale mean that proportional increases in inputs lead to an identical proportional increase in output, implying that average cost remains constant at all output levels. Thus, the LAC is a horizontal straight line.

Test Item 2 of 10

For a linear demand curve P = a - bQ, at what output level is Total Revenue (TR) maximized?

Rationale: TR is maximized where Marginal Revenue (MR) equals zero. For P = a - bQ, MR = a - 2bQ. Setting MR = 0 gives a - 2bQ = 0, which yields Q = a / (2b).

Test Item 3 of 10

Which of the following statements about cost curves is INCORRECT?

Rationale: The minimum point of AVC occurs at a smaller output level than the minimum point of ATC because ATC = AFC + AVC, and AFC is continuously falling. Thus, AVC reaches its minimum to the left of ATC's minimum, making statement C incorrect.

Test Item 4 of 10

If the price of a good is 20 and the price elasticity of demand is 1.5, what is the Marginal Revenue?

Rationale: Using MR = P * (1 - 1/|ED|): MR = 20 (1 - 1/1.5) = 20 (1 - 2/3) = 20 * (1/3) = 6.67.

Test Item 5 of 10

The expansion path in the long run represents:

Rationale: The long-run expansion path connects the tangency points of isoquants and isocosts, showing how the optimal mix of inputs changes as output increases while relative factor prices remain constant.

Test Item 6 of 10

If AVC = 10 + 2Q, what is the total variable cost when output is 5 units?

Rationale: AVC = TVC / Q, therefore TVC = AVC * Q. Given AVC = 10 + 2(5) = 20. TVC = 20 * 5 = 100.

Test Item 7 of 10

Why does the Short-Run Average Cost (SAC) curve lie above or touch the Long-Run Average Cost (LAC) curve, but never lie below it?

Rationale: By definition, the long run provides flexibility to adjust plant size and all inputs, so the lowest attainable cost for any output cannot exceed the short-run cost. Thus, LAC envelopes SAC from below.

Test Item 8 of 10

If the Marginal Cost of producing the 10th unit is 50, and the Average Total Cost of 9 units is 45, what happens to the Average Total Cost of 10 units?

Rationale: Since the Marginal Cost of the 10th unit (50) is greater than the Average Total Cost of 9 units (45), the addition of the 10th unit pulls the average up. Thus, ATC increases.

Test Item 9 of 10

Which market form exhibits AR = MR at all levels of output?

Rationale: Under perfect competition, a firm is a price taker, meaning price (P = AR) is constant regardless of output. Consequently, selling an extra unit adds exactly the market price to total revenue, making AR = MR.

Test Item 10 of 10

According to the traditional theory of costs, why is the Long-Run Average Cost (LAC) curve U-shaped?

Rationale: The U-shape of the LAC curve is explained by economies of scale (specialization, indivisibility) dominating at lower outputs, and diseconomies of scale (managerial coordination challenges) dominating at higher outputs.
