Indian Economy

Industrial Sector of Indian Economy

The industrial sector of the Indian economy encompasses structural evolution, regulatory frameworks, public versus private sector dynamics, and policy shifts from import substitution to liberalisation. Mastery of this domain is crucial for competitive examinations such as UP PGT Economics and UGC-NET, bridging historical industrial policy resolutions with contemporary manufacturing indices.

🏛️ Key Economists & Landmark Publications

P.C. Mahalanobis
1955, The Second Five-Year Plan Draft Frame

Formulated the Feldman-Mahalanobis two-sector and four-sector growth models prioritizing heavy capital goods industries for rapid self-reliant industrialization.

Jawaharlal Nehru & S.K. Dey
1948, Industrial Policy Resolution (IPR 1948)

Established the foundation of a mixed economy framework, demarcating spheres for state monopoly, joint sector, and private enterprise.

Montek Singh Ahluwalia
1991, Economic Reforms and Industrial Deregulation Framework

Spearheaded the structural adjustment policies, dismantling industrial licensing (except 18, later reduced to 4) and promoting foreign direct investment.

Jagdish Bhagwati & Padma Desai
1970, India: Planning for Industrialization

Critiqued India's License-Permit-Quota Raj, highlighting the inefficiencies of inward-looking trade policies and excessive industrial controls.

⚖️ Fundamental Assumptions & Hypotheses

  • Assumption of State Primacy in Early Plans: Assumed that the private sector lacked sufficient capital and risk appetite for heavy capital goods industries, necessitating direct state entrepreneurship.
  • Assumption of Import Substitution: Assumed that domestic industrialization could be best accelerated by artificially shielding indigenous producers from international competition through high tariffs and quantitative restrictions.
  • Assumption of Trickle-Down Industrialization: Assumed that capital-intensive heavy industry growth would automatically generate forward and backward linkages, eventually absorbing surplus agrarian labor.
  • Assumption of Public Sector Social Efficiency: Assumed that public sector undertakings (PSUs) would act as social benefactors, subserving developmental goals over profit maximization.
  • Assumption of Market Failure in Credit Allocation: Assumed that commercial banking networks required state direction and priority sector lending targets to finance industrial and infrastructural expansion.

1. Industrial Policy Resolutions and Evolution (1948, 1956, 1991)

The evolution of India's industrial policy traces a trajectory from statist command-and-control to market-driven liberalization. IPR 1948 outlined the mixed economy. IPR 1956 (the 'Economic Constitution' of India) classified industries into Schedule A (state monopoly), Schedule B (state-led with private participation), and Schedule C (private sector). The New Industrial Policy (NIP) of July 1991 dismantled industrial licensing, abolished MRTP limits, welcomed foreign equity up to 51% (later expanded), and initiated public sector disinvestments.

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • IPR 1956 classified industries into 3 schedules: A (17 industries), B (12 industries), and C (remaining).
  • NIP 1991 abolished industrial licensing except for a short negative list of strategic/environmental sectors.
  • MRTP Act 1969 was replaced by the Competition Act 2002 following the Raghavan Committee recommendations.

2. Small Scale Industries (SSIs), MSMEs, and Reservation Policy

Small Scale Industries and modern Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) form the backbone of employment generation, export promotion, and regional balance in India. Historically, product reservation (protecting certain items exclusively for SSI production) was utilized, but this was systematically phased out post-2000 under the recommendations of the Abid Hussain Committee. Current definitions depend on composite criteria of annual turnover and investment in plant and machinery.

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • MSME classification uses investment and annual turnover criteria (e.g., Micro: investment up to ₹1 crore, turnover up to ₹5 crores).
  • Abid Hussain Committee (1997) recommended replacing reservation with technology upgradation and cluster development.
  • MSMEs contribute nearly 30% to India's GDP and around 45% to total manufacturing exports.

3. Industrial Sickness, Exit Policy, and Public Sector Reforms

Industrial sickness—chronic operational losses and erosion of net worth—plagued both public and private sectors due to obsolete technology, power shortages, labor rigidities, and mismanaged credit. The Sick Industrial Companies Act (SICA) 1985 and the Board for Industrial and Financial Reconstruction (BIFR) were established to diagnose and rehabilitate sick units, though often criticized for delaying liquidation. Reforms introduced Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) systems for Navratna/Maharatna PSUs to grant operational autonomy.

4. Index of Industrial Production (IIP) and Annual Survey of Industries (ASI)

Quantitative measurement of industrial performance in India relies heavily on the Index of Industrial Production (IIP) and the Annual Survey of Industries (ASI). Compiled monthly by the National Statistical Office (NSO), IIP measures short-term changes in the volume of production across Mining, Manufacturing, and Electricity sectors, categorized further into use-based sectors. The ASI is the primary source of detailed organized manufacturing statistics covering capital, employment, and value added.

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • Current base year for IIP is 2011-12.
  • Manufacturing has the highest weight in IIP (~77.8%), followed by Mining (~14.4%) and Electricity (~7.9%).
  • Core Industries (8 core sectors) comprise over 40% of the weight of the IIP basket.

📐 Mathematical Formulations & Equilibrium Conditions

Mahalanobis Two-Sector Growth Model (Investment Allocation)
λk = IkI

Represents the proportion of total investment allocated to the capital goods sector ($\lambda_k$), which dictates the long-term industrial growth capacity of the economy.

Index of Industrial Production (IIP) Weighted Arithmetic Mean
IIP = \frac{\sum (\frac{Qit}{Qi0}) × Wi}{\sum Wi}

Laspeyres-type formula where $Q_{it}/Q_{i0}$ is the production relative of item $i$ in current period $t$ compared to base period $0$, weighted by value-added weights $W_i$.

Capital-Output Ratio (ICOR)
ICOR = Δ KΔ Y = Ig

Incremental Capital-Output Ratio measures the units of capital investment ($\Delta K$ or rate $I$) required to generate an additional unit of output ($\Delta Y$ or growth rate $g$). Lower ICOR indicates higher industrial efficiency.

Total Factor Productivity (TFP) Growth (Solow Residual)
\.\!AA = \.\!YY - α \.\!LL - (1-α)\.\!KK

Measures technological progress in the industrial sector by subtracting the weighted growth rates of labor ($L$) and capital ($K$) from total output growth ($Y$).

⚠️ Economic Paradoxes & Boundary Exceptions

Exception / Paradox Jobless Growth in Organized Manufacturing

Conditions: Occurs when industrial output expands rapidly due to capital-intensive automation, but employment in the organized manufacturing sector remains stagnant or declines.

Diagrammatic Behavior: Diverging trajectories where output growth curve slopes steeply upward while the organized labor absorption curve remains flat near the horizontal axis.

Exception / Paradox Sickness Amidst Aggregate Growth

Conditions: The coexistence of high industrial growth rates in sunrise sectors (IT, pharmaceuticals) alongside widespread structural sickness in traditional sunset industries (textiles, jute).

Diagrammatic Behavior: Bimodal distribution of firm performance: high profitability spikes for large conglomerates contrasted with persistent negative net worth for legacy units.

Exception / Paradox Backwardness of the Capital Goods Sector Post-Liberalization

Conditions: Despite initial protectionist focus on heavy machinery, trade openness sometimes caused domestic capital goods producers to suffer import competition, reversing Mahalanobis priorities.

Diagrammatic Behavior: Downward shift in domestic capital goods production share within IIP while consumer durable imports surge.

⚡ Must-Remember Economic Identities & Laws

  • MSME Classification Formula (2020 Revision): Micro (Investment <= ₹1 Cr, Turnover <= ₹5 Cr); Small (Investment <= ₹10 Cr, Turnover <= ₹5 Cr); Medium (Investment <= ₹50 Cr, Turnover <= ₹250 Cr).
  • IIP Weighting Structure: WMining + WManufacturing + WElectricity = 100, where manufacturing constitutes roughly 77.8% of the total index weight.
  • Index Formula (Laspeyres-type used in IIP): IIP = \frac{\sum (Qi / Q0i) × Wi}{\sum Wi}
  • SICA 1985 Trigger Criterion: A company is deemed sick if its accumulated losses at the end of any financial year equal or exceed 100% of its peak net worth during the preceding 4 financial years.
  • MRTP Threshold Rule: Prior to 1991 amendments, any firm with assets exceeding ₹100 crore fell under the purview of the MRTP Commission for pre-entry clearance.
  • Public Sector Monopoly Reduction: IPR 1956 had 17 industries in Schedule A (exclusive state domain), which was drastically reduced to 2 (Atomic Energy and Railways) in the NIP 1991.

🚫 Common Analytical Confusions (Contrast Table)

IPR 1956 Schedule A vs Schedule B VS IPR 1956 Schedule C

Schedule A comprised exclusive state-owned monopolies (17 industries). Schedule B comprised mixed sectors where state established new units but private sector could supplement (12 industries). Schedule C left all remaining industries to the private sector.

Industrial Licensing VS Industrial Reservation (SSIs)

Licensing regulated who could start production and how much capacity could be installed across large industries. Reservation restricted the type of product to be manufactured exclusively by small-scale enterprises.

Strategic Disinvestment VS Minority Disinvestment

Minority disinvestment retains government control (>51% equity) with sale of a fraction of shares. Strategic disinvestment transfers management control and majority stake (>=51%) to a strategic private partner.

Index of Industrial Production (IIP) VS Annual Survey of Industries (ASI)

IIP is a high-frequency (monthly) indicator measuring short-term physical volume changes across registered and unregistered/factory sectors. ASI is an annual, comprehensive census-cum-sample survey capturing detailed structural variables (value added, capital, employment) for registered factories.

SICA 1985 VS Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) 2016

SICA focused on rehabilitation and protection of sick industrial companies through BIFR, often plagued by delays. IBC 2016 replaced SICA, shifting focus from rehabilitation to time-bound corporate insolvency resolution and asset maximization.

📚 Complete Concept-by-Concept Glossary

Every fundamental term, curve, and mechanism covered in this chapter.

Industrial Policy Resolution (IPR) 1948: The first comprehensive industrial policy of independent India that laid down the foundation of a mixed economy, dividing industries into four distinct categories.
Industrial Policy Resolution (IPR) 1956: Often termed the 'Economic Constitution' of India, it placed commanding heights of the economy in the hands of the public sector through Schedules A, B, and C.
New Industrial Policy (NIP) 1991: A paradigm shift marked by LPG (Liberalisation, Privatisation, and Globalisation), abolishing industrial licensing for most sectors and welcoming FDI.
Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) Act 2006: Legislation that formally classified manufacturing and service enterprises based on investment ceilings, later amended in 2020 to include turnover criteria.
Industrial Sickness: A condition where an industrial firm incurs continuous cash losses, eroding its net worth entirely and leading to potential closure or restructuring.
Board for Industrial and Financial Reconstruction (BIFR): A statutory body established under the Sick Industrial Companies Act (SICA) 1985 to detect sickness in sick industrial units and determine remedial measures.
Navratna and Maharatna Status: Special financial and operational autonomy granted to high-performing Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) to compete globally.
Index of Industrial Production (IIP): A composite indicator that measures the short-term changes in the volume of production of a basket of industrial products during a given period with respect to a base year.
Annual Survey of Industries (ASI): The principal source of industrial statistics in India, providing detailed data on manufacturing units registered under the Factories Act 1948.
MRTP Act 1969: Monopolies and Restrictive Trade Practices Act designed to control concentration of economic power; repealed and replaced by the Competition Act 2002.
Exit Policy: Regulations governing the closure, liquidation, or retrenchment of unviable industrial undertakings, historically constrained by Chapter VB of the Industrial Disputes Act.
Reservation Policy for SSIs: A protective policy introduced in 1967 earmarking specific products for exclusive production by small-scale sector units, systematically phased out post-1991.
Disinvestment: The sale of government equity in Public Sector Undertakings to private entities, aimed at improving efficiency and raising fiscal resources.
Manufacturing Sector Weights in IIP: The dominant component of the IIP basket holding the highest weight (~77.8%), followed by Mining and Electricity.

Question 1 of 18

Which Industrial Policy Resolution is correctly referred to as the 'Economic Constitution of India'?

Explanation: IPR 1956 formed the basis of the Second Five-Year Plan and regulated industrial development through distinct schedules, earning it the title 'Economic Constitution of India'.

Question 2 of 18

Under the MSME classification revised in 2020, what are the investment and turnover limits for a 'Small' enterprise?

Explanation: According to the MSME revised norms (effective July 2020), a small enterprise is defined as one where investment does not exceed ₹10 crore and annual turnover does not exceed ₹50 crore.

Question 3 of 18

How many industries were kept exclusively reserved for the public sector under the New Industrial Policy of 1991?

Explanation: NIP 1991 drastically reduced public sector monopoly to only 2 industries: atomic energy and railway transport (later defense production was also opened up).

Question 4 of 18

What is the highest weighted sector in the computation of the Index of Industrial Production (IIP) in India?

Explanation: Manufacturing holds the highest weight in IIP at approximately 77.8%, followed by Mining (~14.4%) and Electricity (~7.9%).

Question 5 of 18

The Sick Industrial Companies Act (SICA) led to the creation of which institutional body?

Explanation: SICA (1985) established the Board for Industrial and Financial Reconstruction (BIFR) to deal with industrial sickness in India.

Question 6 of 18

Which of the following committees recommended the abolition of industrial licensing in 1991?

Explanation: The structural reforms and dismantling of license raj in 1991 were shaped by committees and policy advisors like Montek Singh Ahluwalia, alongside the Narasimham Committee for financial sectors.

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following best defines 'Strategic Disinvestment'?

Explanation: Strategic disinvestment involves the sale of a substantial portion of government shareholding (usually 50% or more) along with transfer of management control.

Question 8 of 18

The Annual Survey of Industries (ASI) primarily covers factories registered under which legislative act?

Explanation: ASI is the comprehensive annual survey covering all factories registered under sections 2m(i) and 2m(ii) of the Factories Act, 1948.

Question 9 of 18

Assertion (A): IPR 1956 laid heavy emphasis on the establishment of basic and heavy capital goods industries in the public sector. Reason (R): Private sector lacked adequate capital and willingness to invest in long-gestation infrastructure projects during the Second Five-Year Plan.

Explanation: The Mahalanobis strategy of the Second Plan required heavy industries, which private capitalists could not afford due to long gestation periods, making public sector intervention imperative.

Question 10 of 18

What was the primary objective of the MRTP Act, 1969?

Explanation: The Monopolies and Restrictive Trade Practices Act aimed to curb monopolistic tendencies and prevent economic power concentration.

Question 11 of 18

Which base year is currently used for calculating the Index of Industrial Production (IIP) in India?

Explanation: The base year for IIP in India was revised to 2011-12 effective May 2017.

Question 12 of 18

Which committee recommended the policy of reservation of items for the Small Scale Industries (SSI) sector?

Explanation: The Village and Small Scale Industries Committee, headed by D.G. Karve (1955), laid the framework for promoting and protecting small-scale units.

Question 13 of 18

What does 'Navratna' status granted to PSUs signify?

Explanation: Navratna status allows PSUs to incur capital expenditure without prior government approval up to specified limits, enhancing operational efficiency.

Question 14 of 18

Under the New Industrial Policy 1991, automatic approval for Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) was granted up to what percentage in high-priority industries?

Explanation: NIP 1991 permitted automatic approval for foreign equity up to 51% in 34 high-priority industries.

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following is NOT a core sector included in the Index of Eight Core Industries?

Explanation: The Eight Core Industries are Coal, Crude Oil, Natural Gas, Refinery Products, Fertilizers, Steel, Cement, and Electricity. Automobiles is not among them.

Question 16 of 18

What led to the abolition of the BIFR?

Explanation: BIFR was wound up with the enactment of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) 2016, which streamlined corporate restructuring and exit mechanisms.

Question 17 of 18

Which industrial policy first introduced the concept of 'Joint Sector' in India?

Explanation: The Industrial Licensing Policy Inquiry Committee (Dutt Committee) recommended the 'Joint Sector' (partnership between state and private enterprise), which was formally endorsed in 1973.

Question 18 of 18

What is the primary significance of the 'Use-based' classification of IIP?

Explanation: The use-based classification divides IIP items into primary goods, capital goods, intermediate goods, infrastructure/construction goods, consumer durables, and consumer non-durables.
