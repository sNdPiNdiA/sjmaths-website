Money and Banking

NABARD

The National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD) is the apex development financial institution in India, established in 1982 to promote sustainable agriculture and rural prosperity through credit, institution-building, and refinancing. It forms a cornerstone of monetary and banking policy frameworks tested rigorously in UP PGT, TGT Social Science, and UGC-NET competitive examinations.

🏛️ Key Economists & Landmark Publications

B. Shivaraman Committee
1979-1981 (Committee to Review Arrangements for Institutional Credit for Agriculture and Rural Development)

Recommended the creation of an apex institution by merging the Agricultural Credit Department and Rural Planning and Credit Cell of RBI with Agricultural Refinance and Development Corporation (ARDC).

Indira Gandhi / Government of India
1982 (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development Act, 1981)

Enacted parliamentary legislation to officially bring NABARD into operation on July 12, 1982.

M. Narasimham
1991 (Report of the Committee on the Financial System)

Evaluated rural credit structures and recommended capital adequacy frameworks, supervisory autonomy, and restructuring of RRBs under NABARD supervision.

Y. H. Malegam Committee
2011 (Committee on Microfinance Institutions)

Advocated for regulatory frameworks over microfinance institutions (MFIs), heavily influencing NABARD's Self-Help Group (SHG) bank linkage model norms.

⚖️ Fundamental Assumptions & Hypotheses

  • Institutional credit inadequacy assumption: Rural capital markets suffer from information asymmetries, high transaction costs, and collateral deficiencies, necessitating state-sponsored apex intervention.
  • Trickle-down and linkage hypothesis: Providing wholesale refinancing to Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), Cooperative Banks, and Commercial Banks optimizes grassroots credit delivery.
  • Priority sector mandate assumption: Agricultural and allied sector investments yield social returns higher than private marginal returns, justifying subsidized credit flows.
  • Cooperative federalism in banking: State Cooperative Agriculture and Rural Development Banks (SCARDBs) and Primary Urban Cooperative Banks form vital links that require federal financial backing.
  • Integrated rural development framework: Credit alone is insufficient; agricultural growth requires concurrent infrastructure support via the Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF).

1. Establishment and Historical Evolution of NABARD

NABARD was established on July 12, 1982, pursuant to Parliament's National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development Act, 1981. It implemented the core recommendations of the B. Shivaraman Committee. Initially owned jointly by the Government of India and the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), complete ownership was transferred to the Government of India in 2019.

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • Date of establishment: July 12, 1982.
  • Statutory basis: NABARD Act, 1981.
  • Committee recommendation: B. Shivaraman Committee (1979-81).

2. Core Functions: Refinancing, Credit, and Supervisory Role

NABARD operates as a refinancing agency rather than a direct retail lender. It provides short-term, medium-term, and long-term credit to State Cooperative Banks (StCBs), Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), and Land Development Banks (LDBs). Additionally, it conducts statutory inspections of RRBs and State/District Central Cooperative Banks under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • Primary role is wholesale refinancing, not direct retail banking.
  • Inspects RRBs and Cooperative Banks under Section 35(6) of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.
  • Provides Short-Term Refinance for crop production and Long-Term Refinance for capital formation in agriculture.

3. Developmental, Promotional, and Supervisory Mandates

Beyond refinancing, NABARD manages flagship developmental funds like the Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF), established in 1995-96 to finance ongoing/incomplete state rural infrastructure projects. It spearheads financial inclusion through the SHG-Bank Linkage Programme (started as a pilot in 1992) and promotes micro-irrigation, watershed development, and tribal development funds.

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • RIDF was established in 1995-96 out of shortfall in priority sector lending by commercial banks.
  • SHG-Bank Linkage Project launched in 1992 is the world's largest microfinance initiative.
  • Administers various funds including the Warehouse Development Fund and Food Processing Fund.

4. Organizational Structure, Capitalization, and Recent Amendments

NABARD's apex management vests in a Board of Directors appointed by the Central Government. The authorized capital was enhanced significantly through the NABARD (Amendment) Act, 2018, empowering the Central Government to increase authorized capital up to Rs. 30,000 crore. RBI holds a residual stake or transferred full ownership depending on legislative amendments (Government holds 100% equity since 2019).

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • Headquarters: Mumbai, Maharashtra.
  • NABARD Amendment Act 2018 raised authorized capital limit to Rs. 30,000 crore.
  • Government of India holds 100% equity stake post-2019 RBI share transfer.

⚠️ Economic Paradoxes & Boundary Exceptions

Exception / Paradox Cooperative Credit Paradox

Conditions: Stated objective is to democratize rural credit, yet several State Cooperative Banks suffer from high Non-Performing Assets (NPAs), dual control by State Governments and RBI/NABARD, and severe politicization.

Diagrammatic Behavior: Upward divergence between credit infusion and actual agricultural asset formation due to leakage and interest-subvention diversion.

Exception / Paradox Regional Disparity in Credit Absorption

Conditions: Credit-refinance mechanisms favor agriculturally advanced states (e.g., Punjab, Haryana) over credit-starved eastern states (e.g., Bihar, eastern UP) due to weak cooperative infrastructure.

Diagrammatic Behavior: Asymmetrical Lorenz curve showing high concentration of institutional refinance in progressive agrarian clusters.

Exception / Paradox Interest Subvention Deadweight Loss

Conditions: Deeply subsidized short-term crop loans (at 4% net effective rates) intended to boost productivity often induce moral hazard, encouraging large farmers to divert low-cost institutional credit to non-agricultural speculative lending.

Diagrammatic Behavior: Discontinuity in credit demand curves where subsidized segments exhaust allocations instantly, creating rationing equilibria.

⚡ Must-Remember Economic Identities & Laws

  • NABARD Act, 1981: Statutory enactment providing the legal framework for the establishment and operations of NABARD.
  • Section 35 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949: Empowers NABARD to conduct statutory inspections of RRBs and Cooperative Banks.
  • Priority Sector Lending (PSL) Mandate: Domestic commercial banks must allocate 40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) to priority sectors, with specific sub-targets for agriculture and weaker sections.
  • RIDF Contribution Formula: Shortfall in Priority Sector Lending for agriculture by commercial banks is mandatorily deposited into RIDF at varying interest rates based on shortfall magnitude.
  • Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) for RRBs and Cooperatives: Enforced under NABARD's supervisory guidelines to ensure financial stability, typically pegged at a minimum of 9% or higher based on RBI directives.
  • NABARD Authorized Capital Limit: Post-2018 Amendment, the authorized capital stands at Rs 30,000 crore, ensuring enhanced credit absorption capacity.

🚫 Common Analytical Confusions (Contrast Table)

RBI (Reserve Bank of India) VS NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development)

RBI is the apex central banking institution regulating commercial banks and monetary policy, whereas NABARD is a specialized development bank focusing exclusively on agriculture, SSI, cottage industries, and rural development refinancing and supervision of RRBs/Cooperatives.

Short-Term Refinance VS Long-Term Refinance

Short-term refinance is for seasonal agricultural operations (crop loans) and working capital up to 18 months, whereas long-term refinance is for capital investments like tractors, minor irrigation, and rural infrastructure extending from 3 to 15 years.

RIDF (Rural Infrastructure Development Fund) VS FSPF (Farm Sector Promotion Fund)

RIDF finances medium-to-large incomplete public infrastructure projects of state governments (irrigation, rural roads), while FSPF supports micro-level innovations, action research, and skill development in the farm sector.

RRBs (Regional Rural Banks) VS Cooperative Banks (StCBs/DCCBs)

RRBs are sponsored commercial banks jointly owned by Central Govt (50%), State Govt (15%), and Sponsor Bank (35%) regulated by RBI/NABARD, whereas Cooperative Banks are federal member-owned cooperative societies operating on cooperative principles.

📚 Complete Concept-by-Concept Glossary

Every fundamental term, curve, and mechanism covered in this chapter.

NABARD: National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development; established on July 12, 1982, as an apex development bank in India for agriculture and rural development.
B. Sivaraman Committee: The Committee to Review Arrangements for Institutional Credit for Agriculture and Rural Development (CRAFICARD), which recommended the creation of NABARD.
Refinancing: Providing financial assistance and liquidity to primary lending institutions (RRBs, Cooperative Banks, Commercial Banks) against loans disbursed to the rural sector.
Short-Term Refinance: Provided to State Cooperative Banks (StCBs) and Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) for crop production, marketing of crops, and working capital requirements.
Medium and Long-Term Refinance: Provided for capital formation in agriculture, minor irrigation, farm mechanization, animal husbandry, and rural non-farm sector.
Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF): Established in 1995-96 within NABARD with allocations from scheduled commercial banks failing to meet priority sector lending targets, aimed at funding ongoing state infrastructure projects.
SHG-Bank Linkage Programme (SBLP): Pioneered by NABARD in 1992, it is the world's largest microfinance project, linking Self-Help Groups with the formal banking system.
Supervisory Role: Empowered under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, NABARD inspects Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) and State/District Central Cooperative Banks (StCBs/DCCBs).
Kisan Credit Card (KCC): Introduced in 1998-99 on the recommendations of the R.V. Gupta Committee, designed and promoted by NABARD to provide timely and adequate credit to farmers.
Tribal Development Fund (TDF): A dedicated fund managed by NABARD to promote sustainable orchard-based livelihoods for tribal families (Wadi model).
Farm Sector Promotion Fund (FSPF): NABARD's fund aimed at supporting action research, technology transfer, and innovation in the farm sector.
NABARD Amendment Act, 2018: Increased the authorized capital of NABARD from Rs 5,000 crore to Rs 30,000 crore and transferred the entire 1% equity held by the RBI to the Central Government.
Climate Change Fund (CCF): NABARD's dedicated fund to build adaptive capacity and mitigate climate change impacts in vulnerable rural sectors.

Question 1 of 18

When was NABARD established, and on whose recommendation?

Explanation: NABARD was established on July 12, 1982, by implementing the National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development Act, 1981, based on the recommendations of the Committee to Review Arrangements for Institutional Credit for Agriculture and Rural Development (CRAFICARD) chaired by B. Sivaraman.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following institutions was merged into NABARD at the time of its inception?

Explanation: NABARD took over the agricultural credit functions of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and the refinancing functions of the Agricultural Refinance and Development Corporation (ARDC).

Question 3 of 18

What is the primary function of the Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF) managed by NABARD?

Explanation: RIDF was established in 1995-96 to provide loans to state governments and state-owned corporations to complete ongoing rural infrastructure projects (irrigation, soil conservation, rural roads), funded by contributions from commercial banks failing to meet priority sector lending targets.

Question 4 of 18

The Kisan Credit Card (KCC) scheme, promoted by NABARD, was introduced in which year based on whose recommendation?

Explanation: The Kisan Credit Card (KCC) scheme was introduced in 1998-99 on the recommendations of the R.V. Gupta Committee to provide adequate and timely timely credit support from the banking system to farmers.

Question 5 of 18

Under the NABARD Amendment Act, 2018, the authorized capital of NABARD was increased to:

Explanation: The NABARD (Amendment) Act, 2018 enabled the Central Government to increase the authorized capital of NABARD from Rs 5,000 crore to Rs 30,000 crore.

Question 6 of 18

Which of the following institutions are statutorily inspected by NABARD under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949?

Explanation: NABARD has been entrusted with statutory inspection of Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) and State Cooperative Banks (StCBs) / District Central Cooperative Banks (DCCBs) under Section 35 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.

Question 7 of 18

What was the ownership structure of NABARD prior to the 2018 Amendment Act?

Explanation: Originally, NABARD's equity was held jointly by the Government of India and the RBI. Prior to 2018, the Central Government held 99% and the RBI held 1%. The 2018 amendment transferred the RBI's 1% stake entirely to the Central Government, making it 100% government-owned.

Question 8 of 18

The Self-Help Group-Bank Linkage Programme (SBLP), launched by NABARD in 1992, primarily aims to:

Explanation: SBLP was pioneered by NABARD in 1992 as a cost-effective mechanism for providing financial services to the unreached and underserved rural poor, particularly women, through thrift and credit groups.

Question 9 of 18

Which fund was set up by NABARD to support sustainable orchard-based farming for tribal communities?

Explanation: The Tribal Development Fund (TDF) focuses on the 'Wadi' model, creating sustainable orchards and livelihood opportunities for tribal families.

Question 10 of 18

NABARD's refinancing operations are classified into:

Explanation: NABARD provides short-term refinance for seasonal agricultural operations and working capital, and medium/long-term refinance for capital formation and asset creation in rural sectors.

Question 11 of 18

Assertion (A): NABARD acts as the apex refinancing agency for agricultural and rural development in India. Reason (R): NABARD directly lends to individual farmers for crop cultivation at retail counters.

Explanation: NABARD is an apex refinancing agency; it does not directly lend to individual retail farmers. It provides refinance to banks (RRBs, cooperatives, commercial banks) which in turn lend to farmers.

Question 12 of 18

Which committee recommended the formation of Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), which are supervised by NABARD?

Explanation: The Narasimham Working Group (1975) recommended establishing RRBs to cater to rural credit needs, and their statutory supervision was later vested in NABARD.

Question 13 of 18

Which of the following is NOT a direct developmental mandate of NABARD?

Explanation: Formulating monetary policy and setting repo rates are core functions of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), not NABARD.

Question 14 of 18

What role does NABARD play in relation to Cooperative Banks in India?

Explanation: NABARD provides financial refinancing to State and District Central Cooperative Banks and inspects them under Section 35 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.

Question 15 of 18

The 'ESDM' and skill development initiatives supported by NABARD in rural areas are meant to:

Explanation: Skill development programs supported by NABARD aim to promote rural non-farm sector employment, entrepreneurship, and sustainable livelihoods.

Question 16 of 18

Match List I (Funds/Schemes) with List II (Associated Institution / Purpose): 1. RIDF - A. Rural Infrastructure via Bank PSL Shortfall 2. SBLP - B. Microfinance via Self-Help Groups 3. TDF - C. Tribal Orchard Development (Wadi Model) 4. KCC - D. Timely Crop Credit to Farmers

Explanation: All pairings are correct: RIDF relates to infrastructure funding from PSL shortfalls, SBLP to microfinance, TDF to tribal wadi development, and KCC to farmer credit.

Question 17 of 18

Under the priority sector lending norms, what percentage of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) is generally stipulated for agriculture overall by domestic commercial banks?

Explanation: Domestic commercial banks are mandated to allocate 18% of their ANBC to agriculture, out of the overall 40% priority sector lending target.

Question 18 of 18

Which organization works as a wholly owned subsidiary of NABARD providing consultancy services in agriculture and rural development?

Explanation: NABARD Consultancy Services (NABCONS) is a wholly owned subsidiary of NABARD offering consultancy services in agriculture, rural development, and allied sectors.
