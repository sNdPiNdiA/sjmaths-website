International Trade

Balance of Payments, Foreign Exchange Determination, and Trade Policy

This module provides authoritative theoretical study notes on the Balance of Payments accounting framework, theories of foreign exchange rate determination, and modern trade policy instruments. It is meticulously structured for advanced competitive examinations including UP PGT Economics, UGC-NET, and State Assistant Professor tests.

David Hume

Gustav Cassel

Formalized and popularized the Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) theory of exchange rate determination.

Abba P. Lerner & Joan Robinson

Robert Mundell & Marcus Fleming

Demonstrated how adverse terms-of-trade shifts in a growing open economy can result in lower overall national welfare.

⚖️ Fundamental Assumptions & Hypotheses

  • Perfect Capital Mobility: Capital flows freely across international borders in response to nominal interest rate differentials (core assumption in Mundell-Fleming model).
  • Constant Terms of Trade in the Short Run: Prices of traded goods are assumed sticky or given when analyzing short-term exchange rate fluctuations and elasticity conditions.
  • Passthrough Completeness: Importers and exporters fully pass foreign exchange rate changes to final consumer prices (relaxed in pricing-to-market models).
  • Absence of Transportation Costs and Trade Barriers: Standard assumption in classical and neoclassical trade policy models unless tariffs/quotas are explicitly introduced.
  • Macroeconomic Equilibrium: Domestic aggregate demand equals domestic aggregate supply plus net exports ($Y = C + I + G + NX$).
  • Static Expectations vs. Adaptive/Rational Expectations: Foreign exchange market participants hold specific expectation formation structures regarding future spot rates.

1. Balance of Payments (BOP) Accounting Framework and Equilibrium

The Balance of Payments is a systematic record of all economic transactions between residents of a reporting country and the rest of the world over a specified period. It is broadly divided into the Current Account (visible trade, invisible trade, and unilateral transfers), the Capital and Financial Account (foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, and official reserves), and Errors and Omissions. Accounting-wise, BOP always balances to zero ($CA + KA + Official Reserves = 0$). However, economic disequilibrium occurs when autonomous receipts differ from autonomous payments, requiring accommodating transactions or adjustments in foreign exchange reserves.

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • A deficit in the current account must be financed by a surplus in the capital/financial account or depletion of reserves.
  • Unilateral transfers (remittances, foreign aid) are recorded under current account invisibles.
  • Errors and Omissions act as a balancing item to correct statistical discrepancies in data collection.

2. Foreign Exchange Rate Determination: Purchasing Power Parity and Interest Rate Parity

Exchange rate determination is analyzed through multiple economic paradigms. Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) asserts that exchange rates between currencies are in equilibrium when their purchasing power is the same in each of the two countries (Absolute PPP: $E = P/P^$; Relative PPP relates percentage changes in exchange rates to inflation differentials: $hat{E} = pi - pi^$). Interest Rate Parity (IRP) links spot and forward exchange rates with interest rate differentials. Covered Interest Rate Parity (CIRP) holds due to arbitrage using forward contracts ($F/S = (1+i)/(1+i^*)$), while Uncovered Interest Rate Parity (UIRP) relies on investor risk neutrality where expected future spot rate equals the forward rate.

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • Relative PPP implies that countries with higher inflation experience currency depreciation.
  • Covered Interest Rate Parity (CIRP) is an arbitrage-free condition that holds almost continuously in modern liquid financial markets.
  • Uncovered Interest Rate Parity (UIRP) implies higher domestic interest rate currencies are expected to depreciate.

3. Elasticity Approach and Absorption Approach to Balance of Payments Adjustment

The Elasticity Approach (Marshall-Lerner Condition) examines how exchange rate devaluation affects the trade balance through relative price changes of exports and imports. It requires the sum of the price elasticities of demand for exports and imports to exceed unity ($eta_x + eta_m > 1$) for a devaluation to improve the trade balance. The J-Curve effect demonstrates that the trade balance initially worsens following devaluation before improving due to time lags in volume adjustments. Alternatively, Alexander's Absorption Approach focuses on domestic income and expenditure, asserting that a trade balance improvement requires domestic output (production) to increase relative to domestic absorption (spending): $B = Y - A$.

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • Marshall-Lerner condition assumes infinite export and import supply elasticities.
  • The J-curve phenomenon illustrates a temporary worsening of the current account post-devaluation.
  • Absorption approach emphasizes macroeconomic policies (fiscal/monetary contraction) alongside exchange rate changes.

4. Trade Policy Instruments: Tariffs, Quotas, and Strategic Trade Policy

Trade policy encompasses government interventions altering international trade flows. Tariffs (taxes on imports) create wedge distortions, generating government revenue, deadweight loss, production loss, and consumption loss. In a large country, an optimal tariff exists where the terms-of-trade gain exceeds the deadweight welfare loss, though retaliatory tariffs can lead to suboptimal trade wars. Quotas impose direct quantitative limits on imports, rents of which can accrue to domestic or foreign holders (licensing/VERs). Strategic Trade Policy (Brander-Spencer model) shows that governments can use subsidies or tariffs in imperfectly competitive oligopolistic markets to capture monopoly rents from foreign rivals.

Key Exam Takeaways:
  • Small country tariffs cause zero terms-of-trade gain; large country tariffs can improve terms of trade.
  • Tariffs and quotas are equivalent under perfect competition, but quotas become non-equivalent and monopolistic under domestic market distortions.
  • The Metzler Paradox occurs when a tariff lowers the domestic relative price of the imported good.

📐 Mathematical Formulations & Equilibrium Conditions

Marshall-Lerner Condition for Trade Balance Improvement
\etax + \etam > 1

States that currency depreciation improves the trade balance if the sum of absolute values of export demand elasticity ($\eta_x$) and import demand elasticity ($\eta_m$) exceeds one.

Relative Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)
Δ EE = πd - πf

The percentage change in the exchange rate equals the inflation differential between the domestic country ($\pi_d$) and the foreign country ($\pi_f$).

Covered Interest Rate Parity (CIRP)
F = S 1 + id1 + if

Arbitrage condition linking Spot rate ($S$), Forward rate ($F$), domestic interest rate ($i_d$), and foreign interest rate ($i_f$).

Metzler Tariff Welfare Formula / Effective Rate of Protection (ERP)
g = t - ai ti1 - ai

Measures effective protection ($g$) of a domestic industry given nominal tariff on final good ($t$), nominal tariff on imported inputs ($t_i$), and share of imported input in free-trade value ($a_i$).

Mundell-Fleming IS Curve Equilibrium Condition
Y = C(Y - T) + I(i) + G + NX(E, Y, Y^*)

Open economy goods market equilibrium incorporating exchange rate ($E$) effects on net exports alongside domestic income and interest rate.

🧠 Exam Tips, Tricks & Mnemonics (10-Second Shortcuts)

💡 Marshall-Lerner Inequality Trick M-L > 1 (Sum Greater than One)
Remember: Devaluation works if the sum of elasticities ($\eta_x + \eta_m$) is GREATER than 1. If sum equals 1, trade balance in value terms remains unchanged.

Exam Application: Instantly eliminate answer choices in UGC-NET that state depreciation improves trade balance when sum of elasticities is less than 1.

💡 Mundell-Fleming Trilemma Mnemonic IMPOSSIBLE TRINITY: F-M-C
Fixed exchange rate (F), Monetary policy independence (M), and Capital mobility (C). You can pick any TWO, but never all THREE simultaneously.

Exam Application: Quickly answer questions regarding pegged exchange rates and capital controls in open-economy macro.

💡 Effective Rate of Protection (ERP) Shortcut ERP > Nominal Tariff if Input Tariff is Zero
If raw material imports enter duty-free ($t_i = 0$), then ERP is simply $t / (1 - a_i)$, which is strictly greater than the nominal tariff $t$.

Exam Application: Fast mental calculation for UP PGT numerical problems on effective protection rates.

⚠️ Economic Paradoxes & Boundary Exceptions

Exception / Paradox Metzler Paradox

Conditions: When a country imposes an import tariff, and the foreign export supply curve is highly inelastic.

Diagrammatic Behavior: The imposition of a tariff lowers the domestic relative price of the imported good instead of raising it, counter to standard Lerner-symmetry expectations.

Exception / Paradox J-Curve Effect

Conditions: Short-run price and volume rigidities immediately following currency devaluation or depreciation.

Diagrammatic Behavior: The trade balance graph dips below the horizontal axis (worsening deficit) before rising upward into a surplus over the medium to long term as elasticities catch up.

Exception / Paradox Immiserizing Growth

Conditions: Export-biased growth in a large country facing inelastic foreign demand, coupled with worsening terms of trade.

Diagrammatic Behavior: Real national income and welfare decrease despite an expansion in physical output and export volume.

Exception / Paradox Transfer Problem Paradox (Keynes-Ohlin Debate)

Conditions: International capital transfer (reparations) where marginal propensities to consume diverge.

Diagrammatic Behavior: The terms-of-trade movement can be so extreme that the transferring country's trade balance improves rather than worsens, challenging classical transfer assumptions.

⚡ Must-Remember Economic Identities & Laws

  • BOP Identity: Current Account + Capital Account + Financial Account + Net Errors and Omissions = 0
  • Absolute PPP: e = P / P*
  • Relative PPP: \hat{e} = π - π^*
  • Covered Interest Rate Parity: (1 + i) = FS(1 + i^*)
  • Marshall-Lerner Condition: |Ex + Em| > 1
  • Absorption Identity: Y = C + I + G + (X - M) ⇒ Y = A + B ⇒ B = Y - A
  • Effective Rate of Protection: ERP = \frac{tf - aijtm}{1 - aij}

🚫 Common Analytical Confusions (Contrast Table)

Absolute PPP VS Relative PPP

Absolute PPP states that exchange rates equal price level ratios across countries (e = P/P*), requiring identical price baskets and no transportation costs. Relative PPP states that the percentage change in the exchange rate equals the inflation differential between the two countries.

Covered Interest Rate Parity (CIRP) VS Uncovered Interest Rate Parity (UIRP)

CIRP involves forward contracts to eliminate exchange rate risk, holding true empirically due to active arbitrage. UIRP involves expected future spot rates and does not hedge exchange rate risk, frequently failing empirically due to risk premia.

Elasticity Approach VS Absorption Approach

The elasticity approach is a partial equilibrium analysis focusing on relative price changes and price elasticities of export and import demand (Marshall-Lerner). The absorption approach is a macroeconomic general equilibrium analysis focusing on national income (Y) versus total domestic spending (A).

Tariff VS Import Quota

A tariff is a price mechanism that generates government revenue and allows imports to expand if foreign supply prices drop. A quota is a quantitative restriction that does not generate direct government revenue unless auctioned (yielding quota rents).

Lerner Symmetry Theorem VS Metzler Paradox

Lerner Symmetry Theorem states that an import tariff has the same real effects as an export tax. Metzler Paradox shows that under specific elasticity conditions, a tariff can lower the domestic relative price of the imported good.

📚 Complete Concept-by-Concept Glossary

Every fundamental term, curve, and mechanism covered in this chapter.

Balance of Payments (BOP): A systematic record of all economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world over a specific period, categorized into current, capital, and financial accounts.
Current Account: The component of the BOP that records trade in goods and services, primary income (investment income), and secondary income (unilateral transfers like remittances).
Capital and Financial Account: The section of the BOP tracking capital transfers, acquisition/disposal of non-produced non-financial assets, and financial transactions such as foreign direct investment (FDI), portfolio investment, and reserve assets.
Official Settlement Balance: The net balance of all transactions in the current and capital/financial accounts excluding official reserve transactions, which must be financed by changes in official reserve assets.
Purchasing Power Parity (PPP): A theory of exchange rate determination stating that the exchange rate between two currencies equals the ratio of their price levels, equalizing the purchasing power of both currencies.
Covered Interest Rate Parity (CIRP): An arbitrage condition where the difference in interest rates between two countries is equal to the forward discount or premium of one currency relative to the other, with exchange rate risk hedged.
Uncovered Interest Rate Parity (UIRP): A parity condition stating that the difference in nominal interest rates between two countries must equal the expected percentage change in exchange rates, leaving investors indifferent between domestic and foreign bonds without forward hedging.
Marshall-Lerner Condition: The condition stating that a devaluation or depreciation of a country's currency will improve its trade balance if the sum of the absolute price elasticities of demand for exports and imports is greater than one (|Ex + Em| > 1).
J-Curve Effect: The empirical phenomenon where a country's trade balance initially deteriorates following a currency depreciation before eventually improving as trade volumes adjust to new relative prices.
Absorption Approach: Sidney Alexander's macroeconomic framework analyzing BOP adjustments through national income and domestic absorption (spending), where a trade deficit implies domestic absorption exceeds national output (A > Y).
Tariff: A tax or duty imposed by a government on imported goods and services, serving as a trade policy instrument to protect domestic industries and generate revenue.
Import Quota: A direct quantitative restriction limiting the physical volume or value of a specific good that can be imported during a given period.
Effective Rate of Protection (ERP): The percentage increase in domestic value added made possible by a tariff structure on both final goods and imported intermediate inputs, calculated as (VA' - VA) / VA.
Strategic Trade Policy: Government policies using subsidies, tariffs, or R&D support in imperfectly competitive markets (oligopolies) to shift excess returns or rents from foreign firms to domestic firms.
Metzler Paradox: A theoretical finding where the imposition of an import tariff by a large country can actually lower the internal relative price of the imported good, thereby reducing the domestic price rather than raising it.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following items is recorded under the Current Account of the Balance of Payments?

Explanation: Unilateral transfers (gifts, remittances, grants) are recorded in the current account (secondary income). FDI, external borrowings, and portfolio bond purchases belong to the capital and financial account.

Question 2 of 19

According to Absolute Purchasing Power Parity, if the price of a consumption basket in the US is $100 and the price of the identical basket in India is ₹8,000, what is the equilibrium exchange rate (₹ per $)?

Explanation: Absolute PPP dictates e = P / P* = 8000 / 100 = 80 ₹/$.

Question 3 of 19

Covered Interest Rate Parity (CIRP) relies crucially on which of the following mechanisms?

Explanation: CIRP holds through covered interest arbitrage, where investors use forward contracts to lock in future exchange rates and eliminate currency risk.

Question 4 of 19

The Marshall-Lerner condition assumes which of the following initial conditions for the trade balance?

Explanation: The standard derivation of the Marshall-Lerner condition (|Ex + Em| > 1) assumes that trade is initially balanced, meaning export revenue equals import expenditure.

Question 5 of 19

Why does the J-Curve effect occur following a currency devaluation?

Explanation: Initially, trade volumes are sticky, so a depreciation makes imports more expensive in domestic currency, widening the trade deficit. Over time, volumes adjust and the trade balance improves.

Question 6 of 19

In Sidney Alexander's absorption approach, what condition must hold for a devaluation to improve the trade balance (B)?

Explanation: B = Y - A. For devaluation to improve B, income (Y) must rise relative to absorption (A). This requires that the marginal propensity to absorb (a = dA/dY) is less than 1.

Question 7 of 19

What does the Effective Rate of Protection (ERP) measure when a tariff is applied to imported inputs used in domestic production?

Explanation: ERP measures the net protection of value added in domestic manufacturing by accounting for tariffs on both final goods and imported intermediate inputs.

Question 8 of 19

Which of the following trade policy instruments creates quota rents?

Explanation: An import quota limits quantity, creating a wedge between domestic and foreign prices. The resulting windfall gain is known as quota rent, captured by quota license holders or foreign exporters.

Question 9 of 19

The Metzler Paradox occurs under which circumstance?

Explanation: Metzler Paradox occurs when foreign demand for the importing country's exports is extremely inelastic, causing the imposition of a tariff to lower the internal relative price of the import.

Question 10 of 19

According to the Mundell-Fleming model under fixed exchange rates, what is the efficacy of monetary policy?

Explanation: Under fixed exchange rates, the central bank must intervene to maintain the exchange rate peg, forfeiting independent control over the money supply. Thus, monetary policy is ineffective.

Question 11 of 19

Which term describes a deliberate downward adjustment of the official exchange rate in a fixed exchange rate regime?

Explanation: Devaluation is an official policy action reducing the value of a currency under a fixed exchange rate system, whereas depreciation occurs in a floating exchange rate system.

Question 12 of 19

In the Stolper-Samuelson theorem, an increase in the relative price of a commodity (e.g., via a tariff on the import-competing good) leads to:

Explanation: The Stolper-Samuelson theorem states that raising the price of a good increases the real return to the factor used intensively in its production (the scarce factor in an import-competing sector).

Question 13 of 19

If Uncovered Interest Rate Parity holds, and the domestic interest rate is 6% while the foreign interest rate is 2%, what is expected of the foreign currency?

Explanation: UIRP: i - i* = expected depreciation of domestic currency (\hat{e}). Here, 6% - 2% = 4%, meaning the domestic currency depreciates by 4% and the foreign currency appreciates by 4%.

Question 14 of 19

What is the primary objective of strategic trade policy?

Explanation: Strategic trade policy uses government intervention to shift economic rents or profits from foreign competitors to domestic firms in imperfectly competitive global markets.

Question 15 of 19

The Lerner Symmetry Theorem establishes that:

Explanation: Abba Lerner proved that taxing imports has the exact same relative price and resource allocation effects as taxing exports.

Question 16 of 19

Under a flexible exchange rate regime, an expansionary monetary policy leads to:

Explanation: Expansionary monetary policy lowers domestic interest rates, causing capital outflows, currency depreciation, and an increase in net exports.

Question 17 of 19

Net Errors and Omissions in the Balance of Payments account exist primarily to:

Explanation: Due to disparate data sources and timing discrepancies, global BOP accounts do not sum to zero. Net Errors and Omissions is the balancing item added to reconcile discrepancies.

Question 18 of 19

According to the monetary approach to the balance of payments, a BOP deficit is fundamentally a:

Explanation: The monetary approach views BOP deficits as a monetary disequilibrium where domestic money supply exceeds money demand, leading residents to spend excess cash balances abroad.

Question 19 of 19

Which of the following represents a non-tariff barrier (NTB) to trade?

Explanation: A Voluntary Export Restraint (VER) is a quota on trade imposed from the exporting country's side at the import country's request, classifying it as a non-tariff barrier.
