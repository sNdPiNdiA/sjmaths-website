Practical Communication in Agricultural Extension

1. Fundamentals and Models of Communication in Agricultural Extension

Communication is the two-way process of exchanging messages, ideas, and agricultural innovations between change agents (extension workers) and farmers. In agricultural extension, effective communication bridges the gap between agricultural research stations and farmers' fields.

  • Aristotle's Model: Focuses on speaker, speech, occasion, audience, and effect. It is a linear model emphasizing persuasion.
  • Berlo's SMCR Model: Stands for Source, Message, Channel, and Receiver. Communication fidelity is heavily influenced by the communication skills, attitudes, knowledge, and social system of both source and receiver.
  • Shannon and Weaver Model: Introduces the concept of 'noise' (barriers to communication) in a linear transmission system.
  • Leagans' Model: Specifically designed for extension education, comprising communicator, message, channel, treatment, audience, audience response, and feedback.

2. Classification of Communication Channels and Methods

Extension teaching methods are categorized based on their form, use, and social contact level to ensure maximum diffusion and adoption of agricultural technologies.

  • Individual Contact Methods: High cost per farmer but highest persuasion and feedback. Examples include farm and home visits, personal letters, telephone calls, and result demonstrations.
  • Group Contact Methods: Suitable for intermediate audience sizes. Examples include method demonstrations, farmers' training camps (Krishak Goshthi), result demonstrations, group discussions, and field days.
  • Mass Contact Methods: Reaches large, heterogeneous audiences rapidly with low cost per capita but limited feedback. Examples include radio broadcasts, television programs, agricultural exhibitions, printed bulletins, leaflets, newspapers, and social media campaigns.

3. Audio-Visual Aids: Principles of Selection and Utilization

Audio-visual aids enhance learning efficiency by engaging multiple human senses. According to Edgar Dale's Cone of Experience, learners retain 10% of what they read, 20% of what they hear, 30% of what they see, 50% of what they see and hear, and 90% of what they do.

  • Audio Aids: Radio, public address systems, and audio cassettes. Best for rapid dissemination of weather advisories and market rates.
  • Visual Aids: Charts, posters, flashcards, flannel graphs, and specimen boards. Highly effective for illiterate or semi-literate farming communities.
  • Audio-Visual Aids: Sound films, video documentaries, television, and multimedia projectors. Combines sight and sound for maximum retention and comprehension.

4. Barriers to Communication in Rural Extension

Successful agricultural technology transfer is frequently hindered by various psychological, physical, cultural, and semantic barriers that impede message fidelity.

  • Semantic Barriers: Use of complex technical jargon by scientists that rural farmers cannot comprehend.
  • Psychological Barriers: Prejudices, skepticism toward new high-yielding varieties, and fear of crop failure.
  • Physical and Environmental Barriers: Distances, poor connectivity, power outages, and background noise during field demonstrations.
  • Sociocultural Barriers: Caste dynamics, gender inequalities in farming decision-making, and traditional dogmas regarding agricultural practices.

5. Modern ICT Tools and Diffusion of Innovations

Information and Communication Technology (ICT) has revolutionized rural communication. E-governance portals and mobile apps provide real-time agricultural advisories.

  • m-Agriculture: Initiatives like mKisan, Kisan Call Centers (1800-180-1551), and SMS-based weather and pest alerts.
  • Diffusion Process: The spread of a new idea from source to adopter involves awareness, interest, evaluation, trial, and adoption.
  • Adopter Categories: Innovators (2.5%), Early Adopters (13.5%), Early Majority (34%), Late Majority (34%), and Laggards (16%).

  • Kisan Call Center toll-free number is 1800-180-1551, operational in 22 regional languages.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing method demonstration with result demonstration; remember that method shows 'how' while result proves 'why' or 'value'.
  • Assuming mass media provides immediate two-way feedback, whereas mass media feedback is notoriously delayed and indirect.

Concept 1

Communication Process and Models

Communication is dynamic and cyclical.

  • SMCR Model: Berlo's model focusing on Source, Message, Channel, and Receiver.
  • Noise: Any factor that distorts or interferes with message transmission.
Concept 2

Audio-Visual Aids Hierarchy

Categorized by sensory engagement.

  • Cone of Experience: Edgar Dale's visual representation of learning retention.
  • Projected Aids: Slide projectors, LCDs, and educational films combining sight and motion.
Concept 3

Extension Teaching Methods Matrix

Tailoring methods to audience characteristics.

  • Individual Contact: Best for personal persuasion and adoption of complex technologies.
  • Mass Contact: Essential for creating rapid awareness across large geographical tracts.
Concept 4

Adoption and Diffusion Process

The journey from ignorance to institutionalization.

  • Innovators: Venturesome risk-takers who introduce new ideas from outside the community.
  • Laggards: Traditional, localized individuals who are the last to adopt innovations.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Communication (Communication Process)
The two-way process of sharing information, ideas, and attitudes between a sender and a receiver.
Feedback (Audience Response / Feedback)
The reaction of the receiver back to the sender, ensuring mutual understanding.
Method Demonstration (Skill Training Method)
A short-time demonstration given to a group to show how to carry out a new skill or practice.
Result Demonstration (Comparative Field Test)
A method conducted on a farmer's field to prove the superiority of a recommended practice over local practice.
Diffusion (Diffusion of Innovations)
The process by which an innovation is communicated through certain channels over time among members of a social system.
Adoption (Adoption Process)
The mental process through which an individual passes from first hearing about an innovation to final utilization.
Opinion Leader (Formal/Informal Leader)
An individual who informally influences other people's attitudes and overt behavior in a desired way with relative frequency.
Audio-Visual Aid (Multisensory Teaching Device)
Any device that appeals to both the sense of hearing and sight to facilitate learning.
Noise (Communication Barrier)
Any unwanted disturbance or barrier that interferes with the clear transmission of a message.
mKisan (Mobile-based Agro Advisory)
Government of India portal for sending targeted SMS advisories directly to registered farmers.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Method Demonstration VS Result Demonstration

Method demonstration shows skill execution, whereas result demonstration proves comparative economic and agronomic superiority on a farmer's field over time.
