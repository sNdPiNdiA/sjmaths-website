Types of News in Agricultural Journalism and Extension

Introduction to Agricultural News and Journalism

Agricultural journalism is a specialized branch of mass communication that involves gathering, processing, and disseminating information related to agriculture, animal husbandry, horticulture, and rural development. News in agricultural extension serves as the primary medium for transferring farm technologies from research laboratories to farmers' fields (Lab-to-Land)

  • News values in agriculture include timeliness, proximity, impact, prominence, and human interest.
  • Effective agricultural reporting bridges the knowledge gap between agricultural scientists, extension workers, and farming communities.

Classification of News Based on Subject Matter and Scope

News in agricultural extension can be categorized based on its thematic content, geographical coverage, and temporal relevance. Understanding these categories helps extension personnel tailor information packages for different target audiences.

  • Production News: Focuses on crop husbandry, high-yielding varieties (e.g., Triticum aestivum variety HD 2967), agronomic practices, fertilizer management, and irrigation schedules.
  • Market and Economic News: Covers minimum support prices (MSP), mandi rates, market arrivals, export-import policies, and agricultural credit schemes.
  • Regulatory and Policy News: Informs farmers about government subsidies, land tenure reforms, crop insurance schemes (e.g., PMFBY), and pest quarantine regulations.
  • Scientific and Research News: Highlights breakthrough innovations, transgenic crops, biotechnology applications, and adaptive research trial results.

Hard News versus Soft News in Agricultural Extension

Agricultural news is broadly divided into hard news and soft news based on its urgency and treatment style. Both types are essential for comprehensive farm broadcasting and print journalism.

  • Hard News: Timely, factual reporting of immediate events such as pest outbreaks (e.g., Desert Locust Schistocerca gregaria invasions), sudden unseasonal rainfall, sudden revision of fertilizer subsidies, or emergency disease warnings.
  • Soft News: Human interest stories, success stories of progressive farmers, profile features on organic farming pioneers, traditional indigenous technical knowledge (ITK), and seasonal festival features related to agriculture (e.g., Makar Sankranti, Bihu, Pongal).

News Values and Selection Criteria in Farm Broadcasting

Selecting appropriate news items for rural audiences requires strict adherence to professional journalistic criteria adapted to agrarian settings.

  • Timeliness: Information regarding sowing windows (e.g., optimal sowing time for wheat is second fortnight of November) must be published before the event.
  • Proximity: Localized news regarding regional soil types, district weather forecasts, and local cooperative societies has higher utility than distant national news.
  • Utility/Actionability: News that provides direct economic benefits or problem-solving methods (e.g., weedicide dosage for Phalaris minor in wheat) is ranked highest.

Role of Mass Media and News Channels in Technology Transfer

Mass media channels play a pivotal role in creating awareness and diffusing innovations across heterogeneous rural populations according to the Diffusion of Innovations theory.

  • Print media (farm magazines, newspapers, extension bulletins) provides detailed, referenceable technical data.
  • Electronic media (radio, television, Krishi Darshan programs) overcomes literacy barriers and delivers real-time weather advisories and market intelligence.
  • New media and mobile applications (e.g., Kisan Suvidha, mKisan) enable personalized, location-specific agricultural advisory services.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Agricultural journalism primarily aims at accelerating the adoption rate of recommended farm technologies.
  • The inverted pyramid structure in news writing places the most critical facts (Who, What, Where, When, Why) at the very beginning.
  • Success stories of progressive farmers fall under the category of soft news or human interest features.
  • Market intelligence regarding Minimum Support Price (MSP) and mandi arrivals is classified as economic and market news.
  • Timeliness is the most vital news value when reporting pest and disease outbreaks such as wheat yellow rust (caused by Puccinia striiformis).
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing hard news with soft news: Hard news is urgent and factual, whereas soft news is feature-oriented and evergreen.
  • Assuming agricultural news is only about crop production; it encompasses animal husbandry, fisheries, agricultural economics, and rural sociology as well.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Types of News in Agricultural Journalism and Extension

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Definition and Scope of Agricultural Journalism

Agricultural journalism is the specialized reporting of agricultural sciences, rural development, and agribusiness.

  • Agricultural Journalism: The systematic gathering, writing, editing, and publishing of agricultural information through print, electronic, and digital media.
Concept 2

Classification Based on Content

News is categorized based on whether it addresses production, economics, research, or policy.

  • Production News: Focuses on agronomic practices, crop protection, and yield maximization.
  • Economic News: Focuses on pricing, marketing, credit, and trade.
Concept 3

Hard vs. Soft News Dynamics

The distinction lies in immediacy and emotional appeal.

  • Hard News: Factual reports on urgent events requiring immediate attention.
  • Soft News: Feature articles focusing on human interest, inspiration, and lifestyle.
Concept 4

News Values in Extension

Criteria that determine newsworthiness in rural communication.

  • Proximity: Geographical or cultural closeness of the news to the reader.
  • Impact: The magnitude of consequence the information has on farmers' livelihoods.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Agricultural Journalism (Specialized Mass Communication in Agriculture)
The reporting and dissemination of agricultural and rural development news across print, electronic, and digital media.
Hard News (Urgent Factual Reporting)
Time-sensitive news reports covering immediate events such as pest outbreaks, market crashes, and weather advisories.
Soft News (Feature and Human Interest News)
Evergreen articles focusing on farmer profiles, success stories, and traditional farming practices.
Inverted Pyramid (News Writing Structure)
A journalistic writing format where the most important information is presented first, followed by supporting details in decreasing order of importance.
News Value (Newsworthiness Criteria)
Attributes such as timeliness, proximity, impact, and prominence that determine whether an event is reported.
Proximity (Geographical Relevance)
The nearness of an event to the audience, which heightens reader interest and utility.
Timeliness (Temporal Relevance)
The currency of information, crucial for time-bound agricultural operations like sowing and spraying.
Krishi Darshan (Agricultural Television Broadcast)
India's longest-running television program for farmers, broadcast by Doordarshan for technology dissemination.
ITK (Indigenous Technical Knowledge)
Traditional local knowledge systems practiced by farmers over generations, often featured in soft news.
Diffusion of Innovations (Everett Rogers' Communication Model)
The process by which an innovation is communicated through certain channels over time among members of a social system.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Success stories belong to soft news.
  • Market rates and MSP announcements are economic news.

  • Core news values include timeliness, proximity, impact, and utility.
  • The inverted pyramid structure is universally used in hard news reporting.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following best describes 'Hard News' in agricultural journalism?

Explanation: Hard news refers to timely, factual reports of urgent events such as pest outbreaks, unseasonal rains, or sudden market policy changes that require immediate attention.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary structural format used in writing hard news stories?

Explanation: The inverted pyramid structure places the most critical and vital information at the beginning of the news story, followed by supporting details in decreasing order of importance.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which category of news does a detailed feature on a progressive organic farmer belong to?

Explanation: Feature articles, success stories, and human-interest pieces are classified as soft news because they are evergreen and focus on emotional or inspirational appeal rather than immediate urgency.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which news value refers to the geographical or psychological nearness of the event to the target audience?

Explanation: Proximity denotes how close an event is to the audience. Farmers are more interested in local district weather and mandi prices than distant national events.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Information regarding the announcement of Minimum Support Price (MSP) for wheat (Triticum aestivum) falls under which news category?

Explanation: MSP, mandi rates, and market arrivals are classified under economic and market news, which directly impact farmers' financial decision-making.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which mass media program initiated by Doordarshan is famously dedicated to farmers in India?

Explanation: Krishi Darshan was launched on January 26, 1967, and remains a cornerstone television initiative for broadcasting agricultural technology news to rural India.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why is 'Timeliness' considered the most crucial news value in reporting crop pest infestations?

Explanation: Timeliness is critical because pest outbreaks (e.g., brown plant hopper in rice or yellow rust in wheat) require immediate plant protection measures to prevent severe economic crop losses.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is NOT considered a standard news value in agricultural journalism?

Explanation: Fictional creativity is not a news value. Journalistic news must be based on verified facts, whereas core news values include timeliness, proximity, impact, prominence, and utility.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main objective of publishing 'Production News' in agricultural magazines?

Explanation: Production news focuses on crop husbandry, input recommendations, seed rates, sowing times, and plant protection, helping farmers optimize crop yields.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

According to Everett Rogers' Diffusion of Innovations, mass media channels are most effective at which stage of the adoption process?

Explanation: Mass media channels (news broadcasts, farm magazines, newspapers) are most efficient in creating initial awareness and knowledge about new agricultural technologies among large audiences.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

An emergency advisory regarding unseasonal hailstorms damaging standing mustard (Brassica juncea) crops is classified as:

Explanation: Weather warnings and disaster advisories are urgent hard news items that enable farmers to undertake emergency mitigation practices.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which communication channel combines audio-visual elements and overcomes literacy barriers most effectively in rural areas?

Explanation: Television and video broadcasts bridge the literacy gap by demonstrating practical agricultural operations visually and audibly to rural viewers.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary characteristic of evergreen agricultural news?

Explanation: Evergreen news (often soft news features on soil health management, success stories, or ITK) does not have a strict time shelf-life and remains useful indefinitely.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which body or organization in India is primarily responsible for coordinating agricultural research and educational news dissemination across SAUs?

Explanation: ICAR coordinates agricultural research, extension education, and the dissemination of scientific news across national research institutes and State Agricultural Universities (SAUs).

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

When an agricultural journalist writes a news story, the 'Lead' paragraph should ideally answer how many Ws and one H?

Explanation: The traditional journalistic lead answers Who, What, Where, When, Why, and How (5 Ws and 1 H) to give readers a complete summary in the opening paragraph.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

How does 'Utility' function as a news value in farm journalism?

Explanation: Utility measures the practical value of information—such as a specific weedicide dosage or fertilizer recommendation—that farmers can directly apply to increase efficiency or profit.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which type of agricultural publication is best suited for publishing comprehensive, peer-reviewed research findings for extension scientists?

Explanation: Scientific journals and bulletins provide the rigorous, peer-reviewed platform necessary for publishing empirical research data and technical recommendations.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What distinguishes agricultural journalism from general political journalism?

Explanation: Agricultural journalism requires domain-specific knowledge of crop science, animal husbandry, soil science, agricultural economics, and extension methodologies.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which mobile-based advisory service launched by the Government of India provides personalized agro-advisories to farmers?

Explanation: mKisan and Kisan Suvidha portals provide real-time, location-specific weather, market prices, and agro-advisories via SMS and mobile apps to Indian farmers.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In the context of agricultural news evaluation, 'Prominence' refers to:

Explanation: Prominence means that news involving high-profile personalities (e.g., award-winning progressive farmers, eminent agricultural scientists) or premier institutions (e.g., IARI, ICAR) attracts greater reader interest.

A news report detailing an unexpected outbreak of Fall Armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda) in maize fields across western Uttar Pradesh with recommended chemical control measures is an example of:

Solution & Rationale: Pest outbreak reports requiring immediate action are classified as hard news with high production advisory value.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Why is the inverted pyramid structure considered advantageous in agricultural newspaper reporting?

Solution & Rationale: The inverted pyramid places key facts at the top, enabling editors to trim paragraphs from the bottom during layout adjustments without sacrificing critical information.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following best differentiates 'Prominence' from 'Proximity' as news values?

Solution & Rationale: Prominence focuses on the status or fame of subjects, while proximity focuses on spatial or cultural nearness to the target audience.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

In agricultural extension communication, what is the primary limitation of relying solely on print media news for marginal and illiterate farmers?

Solution & Rationale: Low literacy rates in rural areas restrict the direct adoption of print-based agricultural news, necessitating radio, television, and interpersonal channels.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

How do market and economic news reports assist farmers in strategic farm planning?

Solution & Rationale: Economic news provides critical market intelligence that helps farmers decide when and where to sell their produce for maximum net returns.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What distinguishes agricultural journalism from general journalism in terms of ethical reporting?

Solution & Rationale: Inaccurate agronomic advice in farm news can lead to severe crop loss and financial ruin for farmers, making scientific verification paramount.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Which communication model aligns most closely with the top-down dissemination of hard agricultural news from research institutes to farmers?

Solution & Rationale: Traditional hard news reporting in extension follows a linear sender-message-channel-receiver (SMCR) paradigm.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

When reporting a success story of a farmer achieving record wheat yield using zero-tillage technology, what journalistic element makes it effective?

Solution & Rationale: Combining verifiable agronomic data with relatable human success inspires peer adoption and validates the technology.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What role do 'Letters to the Editor' and 'Feedback columns' play in agricultural newspapers?

Solution & Rationale: Feedback columns enable two-way communication, giving voice to farming communities and helping journalists address grassroots issues.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which factor is most critical when translating complex agricultural research news into a readable newspaper article for farmers?

Solution & Rationale: Agricultural journalists must translate scientific terminology into simple, comprehensible terms while maintaining scientific fidelity.
