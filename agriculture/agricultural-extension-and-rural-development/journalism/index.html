Introduction to Agricultural Journalism

Agricultural journalism is a specialized branch of journalism that focuses on reporting, writing, editing, and broadcasting information related to agriculture, animal husbandry, horticulture, forestry, and rural development. It serves as a vital bridge between agricultural scientists, extension workers, and farming communities.

  • Primary Objective: To translate complex scientific research findings into simple, actionable, and readable language for farmers.
  • Target Audience: Farmers, rural youth, rural women, input dealers, agri-entrepreneurs, and policy makers.
  • Role in Extension: Acts as an institutional mass media channel for rapid dissemination of agricultural innovations.

Print Media in Agriculture

Print media remains one of the most reliable and enduring forms of agricultural communication, providing permanent records and detailed technical guidance.

  • Farm Magazines & Periodicals: Specialized magazines such as 'Indian Farming' and 'Kheti' published by ICAR provide monthly updates on seasonal crops, plant protection, and animal care.
  • Newspapers: Daily newspapers feature agricultural columns, market intelligence, mandi prices, and weather forecasts.
  • Bulletins & Leaflets: State Agricultural Universities (SAUs) and Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs) publish localized extension literature for distribution during farmer fairs (Kisan Melas).

Electronic Media and Broadcasting

Electronic media bridges the literacy barrier, making agricultural information accessible to illiterate and semi-literate farming communities.

  • Radio: Programs like 'Krishi Vani' broadcasted via All India Radio (AIR) provide timely weather advisories and market rates. Community Radio Stations (CRS) empower local farmers with hyper-local programming.
  • Television: Dedicated agricultural channels like DD Kisan, along with specialized agricultural programs on Doordarshan and private networks, utilize visual demonstrations to explain agronomic practices, machinery operation, and pest management.
  • Video Documentation: Short documentary films prepared by media centers are screened during village camps and training sessions.

Information and Communication Technologies (ICTs) in Extension

Modern agricultural journalism heavily relies on digital platforms and ICT tools to achieve real-time, two-way communication.

  • Kisan Call Centers (KCC): Toll-free number (1800-180-1551) providing instant expert advice to farmers across India in their regional languages.
  • Mobile Apps & Portals: Platforms like mKisan, Meghdoot for weather forecasting, and the National Agriculture Market (e-NAM) portal empower farmers digitally.
  • Social Media & Web Portals: Agricultural journalists utilize YouTube channels, WhatsApp groups, and specialized web portals to disseminate instant pest alerts and market trends.

Principles and Ethics of Farm Reporting

Effective agricultural journalism requires adherence to professional ethics and specialized writing techniques tailored to rural audiences.

  • Clarity and Simplicity: Avoid technical jargon; use local terminology and relatable analogies.
  • Timeliness: Information must reach farmers before or during the critical crop growth stage (e.g., weedicide application timing or irrigation scheduling).
  • Accuracy: Data regarding fertilizer dosages, pesticide dilutions, and seed rates must be verified by agronomists to prevent crop failure or economic loss.

Concept 1

Scope and Definition of Agricultural Journalism

Agricultural journalism is the specialized reporting of agricultural science and rural affairs.

  • Definition: Writing and broadcasting agricultural news and information for rural stakeholders.
  • Objective: Accelerate technology adoption through effective communication channels.
Concept 2

Role of Print Publications in Technology Transfer

Print media provides durable, detailed technical information for progressive farmers and extension personnel.

  • ICAR Periodicals: 'Indian Farming', 'Indian Journal of Agricultural Sciences'.
  • Advantage: Permanent reference value and detailed step-by-step instructions.
Concept 3

Broadcasting and Telecasting in Rural Areas

Radio and television overcome geographical and literacy barriers to deliver timely advisories.

  • Radio: Highly portable, inexpensive, and effective for weather and market updates.
  • Television: Combines sight and sound for effective skill-based training demonstrations.
Concept 4

ICT Integration in Agricultural Extension

Modern digital interventions enable real-time problem solving and market integration.

  • Kisan Call Center: Toll-free advisory service operationalized in local dialects.
  • mKisan: SMS-based advisory system for registered farmers.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Agricultural Journalism (Agri-Journalism)
The specialized practice of gathering, writing, editing, and publishing agricultural news and features.
Kisan Call Center (KCC)
A toll-free telephone advisory service (1800-180-1551) providing agricultural guidance to farmers.
Community Radio (CRS)
A low-power broadcasting service catering to local community needs and rural development.
DD Kisan (Doordarshan Kisan Channel)
India's 24-hour dedicated television channel for agriculture and rural development.
Extension Literature (Printed Advisory Material)
Leaflets, bulletins, and folders distributed by KVKs and SAUs for farmer education.
Farm Magazine (Agricultural Periodical)
A periodic publication containing articles on crop production, animal husbandry, and farm economics.
Mass Media (Mass Communication Channels)
Technological tools designed to reach a vast, heterogeneous audience simultaneously.
ICT in Agriculture (Information and Communication Technology)
Digital technologies used to handle information and facilitate communication in farming.
mKisan (Mobile Kisan Portal)
An SMS portal used by researchers and officers to send targeted advisories to farmers.
Editorial Policy (Publication Guidelines)
The guiding principles and standards established by a media house for content selection and tone.
Feedback Mechanism (Audience Response System)
The process by which media producers gauge audience comprehension and reaction.
Market Intelligence (Mandi Price Reporting)
Timely broadcast of commodity prices across various agricultural produce markets.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Print Media VS Electronic Media

Print media requires reading literacy and provides permanent records, whereas electronic media requires no reading literacy and provides audio-visual broadcasts.
