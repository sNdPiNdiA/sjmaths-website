Khatauni: Rural Land Records, Legal Structure, and Revenue Administration in Uttar Pradesh

Khatauni is a foundational legal land record of Uttar Pradesh maintained under the UP Land Revenue Act, detailing landholdings, ownership, cultivation rights, and tenancy structures. It is an indispensable revenue document evaluated extensively in competitive agricultural and administrative examinations like UP TGT and PGT Agriculture.

Introduction and Legal Definition of Khatauni

Khatauni is an official legal land register maintained by the revenue department of Uttar Pradesh under the provisions of the Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006 (superseding older sections of the UP Land Revenue Act, 1901). It acts as a comprehensive record of holdings that documents all land parcels held by a specific tenure-holder, landholder, or cultivator within a revenue village (Mauza).

  • Primary Purpose: Establishes the connection between the landholder (Khatedar) and the specific plots of land they cultivate or possess, along with their respective shareholding proportions.
  • Administrative Unit: Compiled at the village level and updated regularly to reflect real-time changes in ownership, inheritance, partitioning, or transfer of agricultural lands.
  • Legal Validity: While Khatauni serves as prima facie evidence of possession and cultivation rights, it is not a direct document of absolute title ownership (which is established by the Khate ki Nakal or Title Deed, though Khatauni provides the operational legal status of land use).

Compilation and Maintenance Authority

The preparation, maintenance, and regular updating of the Khatauni are statutory responsibilities managed by the district revenue administration under the supervision of the District Collector (District Magistrate).

    Lekhpal (Village Accountant): The primary field-level functionary who maintains the village registers, records mutations (Dakhil-Kharij), notes agricultural changes, and submits periodic reports to the revenue inspectors.
  • Kanoongo / Revenue Inspector: Supervises the work of multiple Lekhpals within a designated circle, verifies field entries, and conducts preliminary checks on partition and inheritance applications.
  • Tehsildar: The quasi-judicial authority at the tehsil level who holds the power to sanction mutations, resolve minor boundary disputes, and officially command updates in the record of rights.
  • Revision Cycle: Under standard administrative rules, the Khatauni is revised and republished every six years (known as the periodic Khatauni revision) to incorporate all accumulated amendments, court decrees, and succession entries.

Key Information Contained in a Khatauni Document

A standard Khatauni extract (Khatauni Nakal) contains specific columns and coded numerical data essential for agricultural planning, rural credit acquisition, and legal verification.

  • Khata Number (Account Number): A unique numerical identifier assigned to a family or joint tenure-holder group owning or cultivating a set of land plots.
  • Name of Tenure-Holder (Khatedar): The legal name of the landowner, bhumidhar with transferable rights, bhumidhar with non-transferable rights, or government lessee.
  • Gata Number (Plot Number / Khasra Number): The specific cadastral survey number assigned to each discrete parcel of agricultural land.
  • Area of Land: Expressed in standard metric units (hectares, Ares, or square meters) replacing traditional local units like Bigha, Biswa, and Dhur (though regional conversions remain in practical rural use).
  • Encumbrances and Liabilities: Notes regarding institutional agricultural loans, Kisan Credit Card (KCC) charges, government dues, or legal attachments on the specific holding.

Classification of Tenure-Holders in Uttar Pradesh

The Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006 streamlined land tenure categories, defining distinct classes of rights recorded within the rural land documentation system:

  • Bhumidhar with Transferable Rights: The highest category of agricultural landholders enjoying permanent,heritable, and fully transferable rights, including the right to mortgage or sell land for agricultural and permitted non-agricultural purposes.
  • Bhumidhar with Non-Transferable Rights: Tenure-holders who possess heritable rights but face statutory restrictions on selling or transferring their land without prior clearance from revenue authorities or completion of specific statutory holding periods (frequently allotted to marginalized or landless farmers under state welfare schemes).
  • Asami (Tenant): Occupants holding land under specific lease arrangements from gram panchayats or institutional bodies, holding limited cultivation rights without permanent ownership status.
  • Government Lessees and Encroachers: Special categories where state-owned land or public utility land (Gram Sabha property) is leased out or illegally occupied, requiring distinct tracking in revenue records.

Significance in Agriculture, Banking, and Rural Development

Khatauni is much more than a tax record; it is the cornerstone of agricultural economics and rural governance in Uttar Pradesh.

  • Institutional Credit Access: Commercial banks, Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), and cooperative societies mandate an updated Khatauni extract to sanction crop loans, term loans for farm machinery (tractors, threshers, pump sets), and Kisan Credit Card (KCC) limits.
  • Subsidy and Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) Verification: State and central agricultural schemes—such as PM-KISAN, seed subsidies, fertilizer distribution checks, and calamity compensation (crop failure relief due to unseasonal rainfall or drought)—verify land ownership and holding size through Khatauni data.
  • Land Consolidation (Chakbandi): Serves as the benchmark document during consolidation operations to merge fragmented landholdings into unified operational blocks, enhancing farm mechanization efficiency.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Khatauni is officially maintained under the provisions of the Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006.
  • The primary village-level revenue functionary responsible for maintaining land records is the Lekhpal.
  • Khatauni is legally revised and republished in Uttar Pradesh once every six years.
  • The unique number assigned to a landholder's collective holding in a Khatauni is called the Khata Number.
  • Bhumidhar with transferable rights represents the highest category of agricultural land tenure in Uttar Pradesh.
  • Institutional agricultural loans and Kisan Credit Card (KCC) limits are sanctioned based on verified Khatauni extracts.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Khatauni with Khasra: Remember that Khatauni records WHO owns or holds the land (rights and accounts), whereas Khasra records WHAT is growing on the land plot-by-plot and season-by-season.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Khatauni: Rural Land Records, Legal Structure, and Revenue Administration in Uttar Pradesh

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Legal Basis and Definition

Khatauni is the statutory Record of Rights (RoR) defining landholding shares in rural Uttar Pradesh.

  • Statutory Basis: Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006.
  • Scope: Covers all land parcels under a tenure-holder in a revenue village.
Concept 2

Administrative Hierarchy

The maintenance and updating of Khatauni involve a structured chain of revenue officers.

  • Lekhpal: Field-level custodian of village revenue registers and mutation entries.
  • Tehsildar: Quasi-judicial authority empowered to approve mutations and corrections.
Concept 3

Tenure Classification

Landholders in Uttar Pradesh are categorized based on their statutory rights of transfer and inheritance.

  • Bhumidhar: Tenure-holder with transferable or non-transferable rights.
  • Asami: Lessee cultivator holding temporary tenancy under institutional or statutory bodies.
Concept 4

Agricultural and Financial Integration

Khatauni links land administration directly with agricultural credit and state welfare disbursement.

  • Credit Linkage: Mandatory for securing institutional loans and Kisan Credit Card facilities.
  • DBT Verification: Used to verify landholding limits for PM-KISAN and crop calamity compensation.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Khatauni (Record of Rights (RoR))
A legal revenue document detailing landholdings, ownership shares, and cultivator rights within a village.
Khasra (Cadstral Plot Register)
A plot-wise field book detailing crop type, soil classification, irrigation source, and seasonal agricultural status.
Lekhpal (Village Revenue Accountant)
The primary grassroots revenue official responsible for maintaining Khasra, Khatauni, and recording mutations.
Khata Number (Account Number)
A unique numerical code grouping all land plots held by a specific individual or joint family in a village.
Gata Number (Cadstral Plot Number)
A unique survey number assigned to a distinct parcel of agricultural land on the village map.
Bhumidhar (Primary Landholder)
A statutory tenure-holder possessing permanent, heritable agricultural land rights in Uttar Pradesh.
Asami (Institutional Tenant)
A secondary cultivator holding temporary lease rights over specific public or gram sabha lands.
Mutation (Dakhil-Kharij)
The official process of transferring and updating land ownership records in the Khatauni following sale or inheritance.
Tehsildar (Tehsil Revenue Officer)
The executive and quasi-judicial officer heading a tehsil revenue administration and sanctioning mutations.
Mauza (Revenue Village)
The standard geographical unit for revenue administration and land record compilation in India.
Chakbandi (Land Consolidation)
The administrative process of amalgamating fragmented agricultural land parcels into single contiguous holdings.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Khatauni records ownership and rights; Khasra records physical crop and soil status.
  • The primary unit of land account in Khatauni is the Khata Number.
  • Bhumidhar with transferable rights represents absolute agricultural tenure in UP.
  • Mutation (Dakhil-Kharij) is legally required to update Khatauni after property transfer.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Khatauni is the legal Record of Rights in Uttar Pradesh under the UP Revenue Code, 2006.
  • Maintained by the Lekhpal and supervised by revenue inspectors and tehsildars.
  • Revised every six years to incorporate mutations, inheritance, and partitioning.
  • Essential for securing agricultural bank loans, Kisan Credit Cards, and government subsidies.

Khatauni VS Khasra

Question 1 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Under which legislative act is the Khatauni primarily governed and maintained in Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: The Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006 consolidated and amended laws relating to land revenue, tenure-holders, and maintenance of rural land records including Khatauni in Uttar Pradesh.

Question 2 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Who is the primary grassroots revenue functionary responsible for maintaining the Khatauni and Khasra at the village level?

Explanation: The Lekhpal is the primary administrative functionary at the village level tasked with maintaining land records, recording agricultural changes, and processing mutation entries.

Question 3 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

How often is the periodic revision and republication of the Khatauni statutorily scheduled in Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: Under standard revenue rules in Uttar Pradesh, the Khatauni is comprehensively revised and republished every six years to clear accumulated mutations and update succession entries.

Question 4 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What does a Khata Number represent in a Khatauni document?

Explanation: A Khata Number is an account number that groups all discrete land plots (Gata numbers) owned or cultivated by a specific individual or joint tenure-holder family within a village.

Question 5 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following land tenure categories represents the highest order of agricultural landholding rights in Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: Bhumidhar with transferable rights possesses full legal ownership, heritability, and unrestricted rights to sell, mortgage, or transfer agricultural land under UP land laws.

Question 6 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the administrative term used for the official transfer and updating of land ownership records in the Khatauni following a sale or inheritance?

Explanation: Dakhil-Kharij or mutation is the legal revenue procedure through which the name of the previous landowner is removed and the name of the new rightful owner or legal heir is entered into the Khatauni.

Question 7 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which revenue officer possesses the primary quasi-judicial authority at the tehsil level to approve mutations and correct entries in the Khatauni?

Explanation: The Tehsildar is empowered with quasi-judicial powers under the UP Revenue Code to hear objections, sanction mutations, and direct corrections in the Khatauni records.

Question 8 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary difference between Khatauni and Khasra?

Explanation: Khatauni is a Record of Rights focusing on who owns the land, whereas Khasra is a field book focusing on crop acreage, soil type, irrigation sources, and seasonal cultivation status plot by plot.

Question 9 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Why is an updated Khatauni extract mandatory for farmers approaching commercial banks or cooperative societies?

Explanation: Banks require a verified Khatauni extract as primary legal proof of landholding and ownership to establish collateral charge and sanction agricultural loans or Kisan Credit Cards.

Question 10 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard geographical revenue unit for which a separate Khatauni is compiled?

Explanation: Khatauni is compiled and maintained separately for each revenue village (Mauza) within a tehsil district administrative structure.

Question 11 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which term designates an individual holding temporary lease or cultivation rights over specific gram sabha or institutional lands without permanent ownership status?

Explanation: An Asami is a tenure-holder or tenant who cultivates land leased from a Bhumidhar, Gram Panchayat, or institutional body under specific temporary terms without acquiring permanent ownership rights.

Question 12 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the significance of the Gata Number found inside a Khatauni extract?

Explanation: The Gata Number (or Khasra plot number) is the unique cadastral survey identifier of a distinct parcel of land, allowing spatial mapping and cross-referencing between Khatauni and village maps.

Question 13 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

In the context of UP land records, what does the term 'Chakbandi' refer to?

Explanation: Chakbandi (Land Consolidation) is the administrative process of pooling scattered agricultural plots of farmers into a single contiguous block to facilitate farm mechanization and efficiency.

Question 14 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which official supervises the revenue work and field inspections of multiple Lekhpals within a designated revenue circle?

Explanation: The Kanungo (Revenue Inspector) supervises the work of Lekhpals within a designated revenue circle, verifying plot entries, crop inspections, and preliminary mutation reports.

Question 15 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which central and state welfare verification processes heavily rely on Khatauni landholding data?

Explanation: Schemes like PM-KISAN and state drought or unseasonal rain compensation depend entirely on verified Khatauni land records to confirm beneficiary landholding eligibility and extent.

Question 16 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What administrative status is assigned to a Bhumidhar who receives land under state welfare schemes with statutory restrictions on sale?

Explanation: Bhumidhar with non-transferable rights holds heritable cultivation rights over land allotted by the government, but cannot sell or transfer the land without statutory clearance.

Question 17 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

How are traditional land area units like Bigha and Biswa officially recorded in modern computerized Khatauni documents in Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: Modern computerized revenue records in Uttar Pradesh express land areas in standard metric units (Hectares, Ares, and square meters) to maintain uniformity across administrative boundaries.

Question 18 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary legal nature of Khatauni as a revenue document?

Explanation: Khatauni serves as prima facie (on the face of it) evidence of possession, cultivation rights, and landholding shares, though ultimate title ownership can sometimes be contested in specialized revenue or civil courts.

Evaluate the following statements regarding the Khatauni document in Uttar Pradesh: 1. It is maintained under the Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006. 2. It serves as a plot-wise register of daily seasonal crop cultivation. 3. It is officially revised and republished once every six years. Which of the above statements are correct?

Solution & Rationale: Statement 1 is correct (governed by UP Revenue Code 2006). Statement 2 is incorrect because plot-wise seasonal crop cultivation is recorded in the Khasra, not the Khatauni. Statement 3 is correct (periodic revision every 6 years).

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

In the administrative hierarchy of UP land revenue management, what is the correct downward sequence of officials involved in Khatauni supervision and maintenance?

Solution & Rationale: The administrative hierarchy flows from the Tehsildar (tehsil level) down to the Kanungo (revenue circle inspector) and finally to the Lekhpal (grassroots village accountant).

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

A farmer in Uttar Pradesh purchases agricultural land from another tenure-holder. What is the mandatory legal procedure required to reflect this change in the village Khatauni?

Solution & Rationale: Dakhil-Kharij (Mutation) is the mandatory legal process for updating the ownership entries in the Khatauni following a sale, gift, or inheritance.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following tenure categories under the UP Revenue Code possesses both heritability and unrestricted transferability rights for agricultural land?

Solution & Rationale: Bhumidhar with transferable rights enjoys permanent, heritable, and fully transferable rights, allowing sale, mortgage, or transfer of agricultural land.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

Consider the following attributes: - Grouping of land parcels held by an owner - Identified by a unique Khata Number - Revised every six years Which revenue document do these attributes describe?

Solution & Rationale: These attributes specifically define the Khatauni (Record of Rights), which groups land accounts by Khata number and undergoes periodic six-year revision.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary reason commercial banks and agricultural cooperatives insist on examining a verified Khatauni extract before sanctioning crop loans or Kisan Credit Cards (KCC)?

Solution & Rationale: Banks require a Khatauni extract to legally establish ownership and create a formal charge or mortgage over the agricultural land as collateral security for loans.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Which statement accurately distinguishes between Gata Number and Khata Number in revenue records?

Solution & Rationale: A Gata Number identifies a specific physical land parcel (survey plot), whereas a Khata Number groups all plots owned or cultivated by a specific tenure-holder.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

If a dispute arises regarding the actual physical crop grown in a particular field during the Rabi season, which document should be consulted instead of the Khatauni?

Solution & Rationale: The Khasra (Field Book) records seasonal plot-wise crop cultivation, irrigation sources, and soil status, making it the correct document for crop-specific disputes.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

What role does the Tehsildar play regarding mutation entries in the Khatauni?

Solution & Rationale: The Tehsildar functions as a quasi-judicial revenue officer at the tehsil level with statutory powers to sanction or reject mutation applications after reviewing Lekhpal reports.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

Which category of landholder represents an individual cultivating land leased from a Gram Panchayat or institutional body under temporary terms without permanent ownership?

Solution & Rationale: An Asami is a tenant or cultivator holding temporary lease rights over gram sabha or institutional land without acquiring permanent bhumidhari rights.
