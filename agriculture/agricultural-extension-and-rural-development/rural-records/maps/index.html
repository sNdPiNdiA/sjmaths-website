Introduction to Agricultural Mapping and Land Records

Agricultural mapping forms the foundational database for rural development, land revenue administration, and farm planning in India. In the context of Uttar Pradesh land administration, various maps are maintained at the village, tehsil, and district levels to record ownership, land use, and physical boundaries.

  • Cadastral Maps: Detailed legal maps showing property boundaries, survey numbers (khasras), and land parcels within a village.
  • Topographical Maps: Produced by the Survey of India, these maps depict natural and man-made features including contours, elevation, water bodies, and forests.
  • Thematic Maps: Specialized maps representing specific agricultural parameters such as soil types, agro-climatic zones, irrigation canals, and groundwater potential.

Cadastral Maps and Village Mapping (Shajra and Shajra Kishtwar)

The village map, locally known as the Shajra, is a legal and geographical document prepared during land settlements. It depicts every individual field or land parcel (Khasra) along with its shape, dimensions, and adjoining plots.

  • Shajra Plan: The master tracing cloth or digital map of a village showing all field boundaries drawn to a standard scale (commonly 1 inch = 1 chain or 1:3960).
  • Khasra Number: A unique parcel identification number assigned to every piece of agricultural land in a village, corresponding to entries in the Record of Rights (Khatauni).
  • Ameen and Lekhpal Role: Village revenue officials utilize cadastral maps for field inspections, mutation entries, demarcation of boundaries, and disaster damage assessments.

Topographical Maps and Contour Interpretation

Topographical maps are essential tools for soil and water conservation planning, watershed management, and farm layout design. Published by the Survey of India, they use contour lines to represent relief features on a two-dimensional plane.

  • Contour Lines: Imaginary lines joining points of equal elevation above mean sea level. Closely spaced contours indicate steep slopes, whereas widely spaced contours indicate gentle or flat terrain.
  • Scale and Grid Systems: Standard topographical sheets are available at scales of 1:50,000 and 1:250,000, utilizing national grid reference systems.
  • Relief Representation: Essential for planning contour bunds, terracing, farm ponds, and gravity-fed irrigation channels.

Modern Geomatics: GIS, GPS, and Remote Sensing in Agriculture

Modern agricultural extension increasingly relies on digital geomatics, combining Geographic Information Systems (GIS), Global Positioning Systems (GPS), and Remote Sensing (RS) satellite data for precise farm management.

  • Remote Sensing: Utilization of multi-spectral satellite imagery (e.g., Resourcesat, Landsat, Sentinel) to monitor crop health, estimate crop acreage, and detect moisture stress using indices like NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index).
  • GPS Technology: Real-time kinematic (RTK) GPS enables precision farming applications such as variable rate fertilizer application, tractor autosteering, and accurate boundary mapping.
  • Digital India Land Records Modernization Programme (DILRMP): An initiative to digitize cadastral maps, integrate textual records (Khatauni) with spatial maps (Shajra), and provide conclusive land titling.

Agro-Climatic Zoning and Thematic Agricultural Mapping

The planning commission and the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) have divided India into distinct agro-climatic zones based on soil characteristics, rainfall patterns, temperature regimes, and water availability, which are visually communicated through thematic maps.

  • Uttar Pradesh Agro-Climatic Zones: UP is divided into 9 distinct agro-climatic zones (e.g., Northwestern Plain Zone, Bundelkhand Zone, Eastern Plain Zone) mapped to guide regional crop planning.
  • Soil Mapping: National Bureau of Soil Survey and Land Use Planning (ICAR-NBSS&LUP) prepares detailed soil association maps at state and district levels.
  • Irrigation Command Area Maps: Maps depicting canal networks, distributaries, minors, and chak boundaries for efficient water management.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The village map showing individual field parcels and survey numbers is known as the Shajra.
  • Khasra is a legal document containing plot-wise details of land area, ownership, and crop grown in a village.
  • Khatauni is the Record of Rights (RoR) that lists all landholdings belonging to an individual landholder in a village.
  • Cadastral maps in Uttar Pradesh are traditionally drawn to the scale of 1 inch = 1 chain (1:3960).
  • Topographical maps in India are surveyed, published, and maintained exclusively by the Survey of India, Dehradun.
  • NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index) ranges from -1 to +1, where higher positive values indicate dense, healthy green vegetation.
  • Uttar Pradesh is officially classified into 9 agro-climatic zones by the Planning Commission of India.
  • DILRMP stands for Digital India Land Records Modernization Programme, integrating spatial and textual land data.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Khasra with Khatauni: Khasra is a plot-wise field register, whereas Khatauni is an owner-wise landholding register.
  • Assuming cadastral maps show elevation contours: Cadastral maps are 2D property boundary maps and do not show contour elevations like topographical maps.
  • Misunderstanding map scale: A larger representative fraction (RF) denominator indicates a smaller scale map, but cadastral maps use very large scales to show individual small land parcels.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Agricultural Maps and Cadastral Surveying in Rural Development

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Cadastral Surveying and Village Maps

Cadastral surveys establish legal boundaries of land ownership for taxation and administrative control.

  • Shajra: Legal map of a village indicating individual agricultural fields and parcel boundaries.
  • Khasra Number: Unique survey plot number used by revenue officials.
Concept 2

Topographical Relief Mapping

Topographical maps represent the three-dimensional terrain on a flat surface using contour lines.

  • Contour Interval: Vertical distance between successive contour lines on a topographical map.
  • Spot Height: Exact surveyed elevation point marked on a topo sheet.
Concept 3

Remote Sensing and GIS in Agriculture

Space-borne sensors and geographic software provide spatial datasets for crop monitoring and yield estimation.

  • NDVI: Spectral index calculated using Near-Infrared (NIR) and Red bands to assess canopy vigor.
  • Georeferencing: Assigning spatial coordinates to image pixels or scanned paper maps.
Concept 4

Land Revenue Records in Uttar Pradesh

UP land records system operates under the UP Revenue Code, maintaining meticulous spatial and tabular databases.

  • Lekhpal: Village-level revenue official responsible for maintaining Khasra and Khatauni updates.
  • Mutation (Dakhil-Kharij): Transfer of land ownership title in revenue records following sale or inheritance.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Cadastral Map (Shajra Plan)
A large-scale map showing property boundaries, survey numbers, and ownership parcels within a village.
Khasra (Field Plot Register)
A legal land revenue document detailing plot-wise area, soil type, irrigation source, and crop grown.
Khatauni (Record of Rights (RoR))
An owner-wise ledger maintained in revenue records detailing total landholdings of a person in a village.
Contour Line (Isohyps)
An imaginary line on a map connecting points of equal elevation above mean sea level.
Representative Fraction (RF Scale)
The ratio of map distance to ground distance expressed as a dimensionless numerical fraction (e.g., 1:50,000).
Remote Sensing (Earth Observation)
Acquiring information about objects or areas from a distance, typically using aircraft or satellite sensors.
NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index)
A spectral vegetation index used to quantify green biomass and crop canopy health.
Geographic Information System (GIS)
A computer system designed to capture, store, manipulate, analyze, and display spatial geographic data.
Ameen (Revenue Surveyor)
A revenue department official tasked with land measurement, field demarcation, and boundary verification.
Mutation (Dakhil-Kharij)
The official recording of a change in land ownership title in revenue registers.
Agro-Climatic Zone (ACZ)
A land unit homogeneous in agro-climatic conditions for optimal crop planning and production.
DILRMP (Digital India Land Records Modernization Programme)
Central government scheme aiming to create a modern, transparent land titling and digital mapping system.

  • NDVI utilizes Near-Infrared and Red spectral bands.

  • Topographical maps prepared by the Survey of India display physical relief and contours.
  • Khasra is a plot register, while Khatauni is an owner-wise Record of Rights in Uttar Pradesh.

  • Uttar Pradesh is divided into 9 agro-climatic zones for regional agricultural planning.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Khasra VS Khatauni

Khasra is a plot-wise agricultural survey number register, whereas Khatauni is an owner-wise landholding register.

Cadastral Map VS Topographic Map

Cadastral maps focus on legal property boundaries and ownership, while topographic maps focus on natural and physical elevation contours.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which type of map is primarily utilized for land revenue administration and showing individual property boundaries in a village?

Explanation: Cadastral maps (Shajra) are large-scale maps depicting individual property boundaries, survey numbers, and land parcels for revenue and legal administration.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the official local term used in Uttar Pradesh for the village map showing individual agricultural fields?

Explanation: Shajra refers to the village cadastral map drawn to scale showing all field boundaries and parcel numbers.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which government organization is officially responsible for publishing topographical maps of India?

Explanation: The Survey of India (SOI) is the national geodetic and mapping organization under the Ministry of Science and Technology, responsible for topographic surveys and maps.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does a contour line on a topographical map represent?

Explanation: Contour lines are imaginary lines connecting points of equal elevation (altitude) above mean sea level on topographical maps.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In Uttar Pradesh land records, what does 'Khatauni' represent?

Explanation: Khatauni is the legal Record of Rights (RoR) that provides a comprehensive list of land parcels owned by an individual or family in a village.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard traditional scale of cadastral maps maintained in many parts of northern India including UP?

Explanation: Traditionally, Indian cadastral maps were prepared on a scale of 1 inch to 1 chain, which corresponds to a representative fraction of 1:3960.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which spectral index is most widely used in satellite remote sensing to monitor crop canopy vigor and biomass?

Explanation: NDVI uses reflectance in the Near-Infrared (NIR) and Red spectral bands to quantify green vegetation health and density.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

How many agro-climatic zones are officially designated in the state of Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: Uttar Pradesh is divided into 9 agro-climatic zones by the Planning Commission, ranging from the Tarim region to the Bundelkhand plateau.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary duty of a village 'Lekhpal' regarding land records?

Explanation: The Lekhpal is responsible for maintaining and updating village land revenue records, including crop inspections in Khasra and ownership in Khatauni.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does the term 'Mutation' (Dakhil-Kharij) signify in agricultural land administration?

Explanation: Mutation (Dakhil-Kharij) is the official process of updating land ownership records in the revenue registers following a transfer of title.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which satellite program is operated by ISRO specifically dedicated to natural resource monitoring and cartography?

Explanation: ISRO's Cartosat and Resourcesat satellite series provide high-resolution spatial imagery used for cadastral mapping, agricultural monitoring, and land use planning.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does 'DILRMP' stand for in the context of Indian agricultural land administration?

Explanation: DILRMP stands for Digital India Land Records Modernization Programme, aimed at creating a transparent and integrated land information system.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

On a topographical map, what do closely spaced contour lines indicate?

Explanation: Closely spaced contour lines indicate steep terrain or rapid changes in elevation, whereas widely spaced contours indicate gentle slopes.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which agency in India is mandated for systematic soil survey, soil mapping, and land use planning?

Explanation: ICAR-NBSS&LUP (National Bureau of Soil Survey and Land Use Planning) is the premier institute for soil mapping and land resource database management in India.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main purpose of agricultural land consolidation (Chakbandi)?

Explanation: Chakbandi (Consolidation of holdings) reorganizes scattered farm parcels into a single contiguous block to improve agricultural operational efficiency.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which GPS segment comprises the constellation of satellites orbiting the Earth?

Explanation: The GPS architecture consists of three segments: Space segment (satellites), Control segment (ground monitoring stations), and User segment (receivers).

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does a representative fraction (RF) scale of 1:50,000 mean?

Explanation: An RF scale of 1:50,000 indicates that one unit of measurement on the map (such as 1 centimeter) equals 50,000 of the same units on the ground.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which revenue officer at the tehsil level in Uttar Pradesh is principally responsible for supervising land records and supervising Lekhpals?

Explanation: The Revenue Inspector (Kanungo) supervises the work of village Lekhpals, and reports to the Tehsildar at the tehsil administrative level.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary advantage of integrating GIS with spatial agricultural maps?

Explanation: GIS allows farmers and planners to overlay multiple thematic layers (soil, slope, water, crop type) for advanced spatial analysis and agricultural planning.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which satellite remote sensing band combination is commonly used to generate False Color Composites (FCC) where vegetation appears bright red?

Explanation: In standard False Color Composites (FCC), Near-Infrared (NIR) is assigned to the red channel, making healthy green vegetation appear in bright red hues.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

In cadastral surveying, what is the fundamental difference between a baseline and a tie line?

Solution & Rationale: The baseline is the primary longest line through the center of the survey area upon which the framework is built, whereas tie lines connect subsidiary detail points.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

When converting a map scale from 1:25,000 to 1:50,000, how does the area representation of a farm parcel change on the map?

Solution & Rationale: Since the linear scale factor is reduced by half (1:2 to 1:4 in area ratio), the area on the map is reduced to (1/2)^2 = 1/4th of its original size.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

Which specific satellite data product is most appropriate for cadastral map overlay and property boundary updating in rural GIS?

Solution & Rationale: Very High Resolution (VHR) imagery with spatial resolution under 1 meter is required to discern individual field boundaries and rural property lines.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary function of a 'Kanungo' (Revenue Inspector) in the land records administration of Uttar Pradesh?

Solution & Rationale: A Kanungo acts as an intermediary revenue inspector supervising multiple Lekhpals within a revenue circle in UP.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

In topographic mapping, what does a 'Hachure' represent?

Solution & Rationale: Hachures are traditional cartographic symbols consisting of short hatched lines indicating relief and slope gradient on older maps.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What does an Orthophoto map differ from a standard aerial photograph?

Solution & Rationale: An orthophoto is an aerial photograph geometrically corrected such that the scale is uniform, removing tilt and relief distortion.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Under the DILRMP framework, what is the ultimate objective of linking textual records (Khatauni) with spatial maps (Shajra)?

Solution & Rationale: The integration of spatial cadastral maps with textual RoR ensures conclusive land titling and transparent property transactions.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

Which projection system is universally used for global mapping and topographic quad sheets such as UTM (Universal Transverse Mercator)?

Solution & Rationale: UTM is based on the Transverse Mercator cylindrical projection system, dividing the Earth into 60 zones.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What does a spot height on a topographical map signify?

Solution & Rationale: A spot height is a surveyed point on a map whose exact elevation above sea level is explicitly labeled with a dot and number.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

In agricultural remote sensing, what is the main purpose of atmospheric correction of satellite imagery?

Solution & Rationale: Atmospheric correction removes the distorting effects of the atmosphere to obtain true surface reflectance values for accurate vegetation monitoring.
