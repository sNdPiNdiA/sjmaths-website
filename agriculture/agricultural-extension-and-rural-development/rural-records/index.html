Introduction to Land Revenue Administration and Rural Records

Rural records form the bedrock of land revenue administration, agricultural planning, and rural sociology in India. In the state of Uttar Pradesh, land records are maintained meticulously at the village level under the framework of the Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006 (which replaced several colonial acts like the UP Land Revenue Act, 1901). These records are vital for tracking land ownership, tenancy rights, cropping patterns, and land-use transformations over agricultural seasons.

  • Administrative Hierarchy: The district is headed by the District Magistrate (Collector), assisted by Additional District Magistrates (ADM), Sub-Divisional Magistrates (SDM), Tehsildars, Naib Tehsildars, Revenue Inspectors (Kanungos), and Lekhpals (Village Accountants).
  • Primary Custodian: The Lekhpal is the primary custodian of village-level land and agricultural records in Uttar Pradesh, maintaining nearly 14 core registers under statutory provisions.

Key Village Records Maintained by Lekhpal

The land records maintained at the village level by the Lekhpal provide exhaustive information regarding plots, ownership, cultivation status, and disaster assessments.

  • Khasra (Record of Plots): Also known as the Village Map Register, it contains plot-wise details of land area, soil type, irrigation source, crop grown, seed variety, and yield status across three agricultural seasons (Kharif, Rabi, and Zaid).
  • Khatauni (Record of Rights): A legal document detailing land ownership, shareholding, encumbrances, and transfer of rights through succession, sale, or gift. It is updated periodically through mutation (Dakhil-Kharij).
  • Khasra Map (Shajra): The scaled spatial map of the village showing individual survey numbers (khasra plots), boundaries, water channels, and public utility lands.
  • Kisan Credit Card (KCC) Register and Village Census Register: Registers tracking institutional agricultural credit disbursement and socio-economic demographic parameters of rural households.

Agricultural Seasons and Crop Accounting in Rural Records

Crop accounting in the Khasra is synchronized with the three distinct agricultural seasons recognized in the Indo-Gangetic plains and Uttar Pradesh agro-climatic zones.

  • Kharif Season (Monsoon Crop): Sowing from June-July and harvesting in October-November. Primary crops recorded include Oryza sativa (paddy), Zea mays (maize), and Pennisetum glaucum (pearl millet).
  • Rabi Season (Winter Crop): Sowing from October-November and harvesting in March-April. Primary crops recorded include Triticum aestivum (wheat), Cicer arietinum (chickpea), and Brassica juncea (mustard).
  • Zaid Season (Summer Crop): Sowing from March to June. Primary crops recorded include cucurbits, Vigna radiata (green gram), and fodder crops.

Land Utilization Statistics and Classification in India

Land utilization statistics published by the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare, Government of India, categorize total geographical area (TGA) into standard nine-fold land-use classifications based on village records.

  • Forests: All lands classified as forest under any legal enactment.
  • Land put to Non-Agricultural Uses: Land occupied by buildings, roads, railways, water bodies, and industrial sites.
  • Barren and Unculturable Land: Wastelands like mountains, deserts, and rocky terrain that cannot be brought under cultivation at an economic cost.
  • Permanent Pastures and Other Grazing Lands: All grazing land, whether permanent pasture or village meadows.
  • Land under Miscellaneous Tree Crops and Groves: Land not included in the net sown area but put to some agricultural use (orchards and bamboo groves).
  • Culturable Waste Land: Land available for cultivation but abandoned for more than five consecutive years.
  • Fallow Lands other than Current Fallow: Land left uncultivated for a period of 1 to 5 agricultural years.
  • Current Fallow: Land kept uncultivated during the current agricultural year to restore soil fertility.
  • Net Sown Area (NSA): The physical extent of land sown with crops and orchards at least once in an agricultural year.

Modernization of Land Records (DILRMP)

To eliminate corruption, reduce litigation, and provide transparent land administration, the Government of India launched the Digital India Land Records Modernization Programme (DILRMP), merging the erstwhile National Land Records Modernization Programme (NLRMP).

  • Core Components: Computerization of all land records (Khasra, Khatauni), digitization of cadastral maps (Shajra), integration of textual and spatial data, and establishment of modern record rooms at tehsil and district levels.
  • Unique Land Parcel Identification Number (ULPIN): A 14-digit alpha-numeric unique ID assigned to every land parcel based on spatial coordinates, often described as the 'Aadhaar for land'.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The primary village-level functionary responsible for maintaining Khasra and Khatauni in Uttar Pradesh is the Lekhpal.
  • Khasra is maintained on a seasonal basis (Kharif, Rabi, and Zaid), whereas Khatauni is a legal register of land ownership rights.
  • The process of recording a change in land ownership due to sale, gift, or inheritance in the Khatauni is known as Mutation (Dakhil-Kharij).
  • Net Sown Area (NSA) refers to the physical area sown with crops in a village or country, excluding multiple cropping overlap.
  • Cropping Intensity is calculated as (Gross Cropped Area / Net Sown Area) × 100.
  • Shajra refers to the village cadastral map showing plot boundaries and identification numbers.
  • The Digital India Land Records Modernization Programme (DILRMP) utilizes ULPIN (Unique Land Parcel Identification Number), a 14-digit alphanumeric code.
  • Land kept uncultivated for more than 1 year and up to 5 years is classified under Fallow Lands other than Current Fallow.
  • Land left uncultivated for only one agricultural year to regain fertility is termed Current Fallow.
  • Under the UP Revenue Code, 2006, land records maintenance and revenue administration are supervised at the tehsil level by the Tehsildar.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Khasra with Khatauni: Remember that Khasra records physical plot characteristics and crops season-wise, while Khatauni records legal ownership and tenure rights.
  • Confusing Net Sown Area with Gross Cropped Area: Net Sown Area counts physical land area only once, whereas Gross Cropped Area accounts for area cropped multiple times in a year.
  • Assuming Lekhpal maintains records at the district level instead of the village/circle level.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Rural Records in Agricultural Extension and Revenue Administration

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Role and Statutory Duties of Lekhpal in Rural Records

The Lekhpal is the fundamental administrative functionary at the grass-roots level in Uttar Pradesh responsible for maintaining agricultural and land revenue statistics.

  • Lekhpal Records: Maintains 14 core registers including Khasra, Khatauni, Milan Khasra, and village crop inspection diaries.
Concept 2

Distinction Between Khasra and Khatauni

Land records are broadly bifurcated into plot-wise agricultural logs and ownership-wise tenure registers.

  • Khasra: Contains 22 columns detailing crop, soil, irrigation, and area for every plot in Kharif, Rabi, and Zaid.
  • Khatauni: Legal record of rights establishing who owns or holds tenure rights over specific khatas (holding numbers).
Concept 3

Nine-Fold Land Utilization Classification

India classifies its total geographical area into nine mutually exclusive land-use categories for agricultural planning and statistical reporting.

  • Net Sown Area: The core agricultural footprint representing arable land utilized for crop production during the agricultural year.
Concept 4

Digital Modernization of Land Administration (DILRMP & ULPIN)

Technological integration has transformed traditional paper-based record keeping into transparent digital geodatabases.

  • ULPIN: 14-digit unique identification number assigned to land parcels to prevent fraud and streamline property transactions.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Khasra (Village Plot Register)
A legal and statistical document containing plot-wise details of crops, soil, irrigation, and area for every agricultural season.
Khatauni (Record of Rights)
A register recording land ownership, tenure status, and shareholdings of cultivators in a village.
Shajra (Cadstral Map)
The official scaled map of a village depicting individual plot boundaries, survey numbers, and geographical features.
Lekhpal (Village Accountant)
The grass-roots government functionary responsible for maintaining land and agricultural records in Uttar Pradesh.
Net Sown Area (NSA)
The physical extent of land sown with crops and orchards, counted only once in an agricultural year regardless of cropping frequency.
Gross Cropped Area (GCA)
The total area sown under various crops in a year, counting land cropped multiple times across seasons separately.
Mutation (Dakhil-Kharij)
The recording of a change in land ownership or title in the Khatauni register following sale, inheritance, or gift.
Cropping Intensity (CI Formula: (GCA / NSA) × 100)
The percentage ratio indicating how many times crops are grown on the net sown area in an agricultural year.
Current Fallow (Current Fallow Land)
Arable land left uncultivated during the current agricultural year to restore soil fertility.
ULPIN (Unique Land Parcel Identification Number)
A 14-digit alpha-numeric spatial coordinate based unique ID for land parcels under DILRMP.
Tehsildar (Executive Revenue Officer)
The administrative head of a tehsil responsible for revenue collection and land record administration.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Khasra contains 22 columns of agricultural statistics per plot.
  • ULPIN stands for Unique Land Parcel Identification Number (14 digits).
  • Mutation is known locally as Dakhil-Kharij.
  • The primary revenue administrative unit below a district is the Tehsil.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Lekhpal maintains Khasra and Khatauni at the village level in UP.
  • Khasra tracks seasonal crops and plot attributes; Khatauni tracks ownership rights.
  • India's land utilization is classified into 9 standard categories.
  • Cropping Intensity equals (Gross Cropped Area / Net Sown Area) × 100.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Khasra VS Khatauni

Khasra is a seasonal plot-wise agricultural register, whereas Khatauni is a legal ownership register of tenure rights.

Net Sown Area VS Gross Cropped Area

Net Sown Area counts physical sown land once; Gross Cropped Area counts land multiple times if cropped in multiple seasons.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which official at the village level in Uttar Pradesh is primarily responsible for maintaining the Khasra and Khatauni?

Explanation: The Lekhpal (Village Accountant) is the primary revenue functionary responsible for maintaining village-level land records, Khasra, and Khatauni in Uttar Pradesh.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does Khasra primarily represent in Indian land revenue administration?

Explanation: Khasra is a plot-wise agricultural register that records details of crops grown, soil type, irrigation source, and area for every agricultural season (Kharif, Rabi, Zaid).

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the legal register of land ownership and tenure rights known as in Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: Khatauni is the official Record of Rights that details land ownership, tenure holders, and share percentages of individuals in village land holdings.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the process of updating land ownership in the Khatauni following a sale or inheritance called?

Explanation: Mutation, locally known as Dakhil-Kharij, is the legal process of recording changes in land ownership in the Khatauni following sale, gift, or succession.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does Shajra refer to in village revenue records?

Explanation: Shajra is the cadastral map of a village that provides a scaled spatial layout of all survey plots (khasra numbers), boundaries, and public land.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

How many standard land-use categories are recognized in the official land utilization statistics of India?

Explanation: India officially classifies its total geographical area into nine-fold land utilization categories, ranging from forests and non-agricultural uses to net sown area and fallows.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is 'Net Sown Area' (NSA) defined as?

Explanation: Net Sown Area represents the physical land area sown with crops at least once during an agricultural year, regardless of how many crops are harvested from it.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

If the Gross Cropped Area is 160 hectares and the Net Sown Area is 100 hectares, what is the Cropping Intensity?

Explanation: Cropping Intensity = (160 / 100) × 100 = 160%. This indicates a high degree of multiple cropping in the agricultural holding.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the duration for land classified under 'Fallow Lands other than Current Fallow'?

Explanation: Fallow lands other than current fallow include land left uncultivated for a period ranging from 1 to 5 agricultural years to regain fertility.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is ULPIN, introduced under the Digital India Land Records Modernization Programme (DILRMP)?

Explanation: ULPIN stands for Unique Land Parcel Identification Number, a 14-digit alpha-numeric spatial identifier assigned to every land parcel under DILRMP.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which agricultural season in Uttar Pradesh spans from June-July to October-November?

Explanation: The Kharif season is the monsoon crop season in India, with sowing occurring in June-July and harvesting in October-November.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the administrative revenue officer heading a Tehsil in Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: The Tehsildar is the administrative head of a tehsil, responsible for revenue administration, land records maintenance, and statutory clearances.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Land available for cultivation but not cultivated for more than five consecutive years is classified as:

Explanation: Culturable waste land refers to land available for cultivation but left uncultivated for more than five consecutive agricultural years.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the supervisory revenue officer positioned directly above the Lekhpal in Uttar Pradesh revenue administration?

Explanation: The Revenue Inspector, traditionally called Kanungo, supervises the work of Lekhpals within a designated revenue circle.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is NOT included in the nine-fold land utilization classification?

Explanation: Industrial Export Zones are commercial/industrial planning designations, whereas Forests, Net Sown Area, and Non-Agricultural Uses are standard categories in the nine-fold land classification.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of maintaining the Khatauni register?

Explanation: The primary objective of Khatauni is to maintain a legal Record of Rights that clearly establishes land ownership, co-sharers, and encumbrances.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Under which statutory code are land records and revenue administration currently governed in Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: The UP Revenue Code, 2006 consolidated and amended laws relating to land tenure, land revenue, and revenue administration in Uttar Pradesh.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does 'Milan Khasra' maintained by revenue officials signify?

Explanation: Milan Khasra is a comparative statement of village area under various land-use classifications between the current year and the preceding year.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which agency at the national level compiles and publishes the comprehensive land use statistics of India?

Explanation: The Directorate of Economics and Statistics (DES) under the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare compiles and publishes agricultural statistics and land-use data for India.

Test Question 1 of 10 TGT Standard

In the context of UP land revenue administration, match the administrative officer with their correct jurisdiction hierarchy from lowest to highest:

Solution & Rationale: The hierarchy starts at the village level with the Lekhpal, supervised by the Revenue Inspector (Kanungo), managed at the tehsil level by the Tehsildar, overseen by the SDM, and headed at the district level by the District Magistrate (Collector).

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

If a farmer leaves a parcel of arable land uncultivated for two consecutive agricultural years due to lack of irrigation water, under which land utilization category must the Lekhpal record this plot?

Solution & Rationale: Land left uncultivated for a period of 1 to 5 agricultural years is classified under 'Fallow Lands other than Current Fallow'. Current fallow is strictly for one agricultural year.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the mathematical relationship between Gross Cropped Area (GCA), Net Sown Area (NSA), and Area Sown More Than Once (ASMO)?

Solution & Rationale: Gross Cropped Area is the sum of Net Sown Area (physical unique land area) and the Area Sown More Than Once during different agricultural seasons within the same year.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following statements regarding the Khasra and Khatauni is scientifically and legally accurate in Uttar Pradesh?

Solution & Rationale: Khasra is a seasonal plot-wise agricultural document, whereas Khatauni is the legal Record of Rights detailing land tenure and ownership.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary significance of ULPIN in modern land revenue management?

Solution & Rationale: ULPIN (Unique Land Parcel Identification Number) assigns a standardized 14-digit geo-referenced ID to land parcels to streamline administration and curb land fraud.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

In the nine-fold land classification, land under orchards, bamboo groves, and miscellaneous tree crops that are not included in the Net Sown Area is categorized under:

Solution & Rationale: Land under fruit trees, orchards, and groves not accounted for in Net Sown Area constitutes the specific land-use category 'Land under Miscellaneous Tree Crops and Groves'.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

During which crop season does the Lekhpal record the sowing of Rabi crops such as wheat and chickpea in the Khasra?

Solution & Rationale: Rabi crops are sown at the onset of winter during October-November and harvested in March-April.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

What does the term 'Mutation' (Dakhil-Kharij) signify in agricultural land revenue terminology?

Solution & Rationale: Mutation (Dakhil-Kharij) refers to updating the ownership records in the Khatauni when a property changes hands through sale, gift, or inheritance.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

If a village has a Total Geographical Area of 1000 hectares, where 600 hectares is Net Sown Area, 100 hectares is Forest, 200 hectares is Non-Agricultural Use, and 100 hectares is Barren Land, what is the proportion of arable land utilization?

Solution & Rationale: Net Sown Area is 600 hectares out of a Total Geographical Area of 1000 hectares, which equals 60%.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following bodies is responsible for implementing the Digital India Land Records Modernization Programme (DILRMP) nationally?

Solution & Rationale: DILRMP is implemented by the Department of Land Resources under the Ministry of Rural Development, Government of India.
