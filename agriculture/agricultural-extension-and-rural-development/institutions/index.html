Agricultural Institutions and Extension Systems

This comprehensive module covers the structural framework of agricultural institutions, research councils, extension organizations, and rural development agencies vital for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture examinations. It details the operational hierarchy from national councils like ICAR down to grassroots extension delivery systems.

Evolution and National Framework of Agricultural Research

Agricultural research and education in India underwent a major transformation with the establishment of the Imperial (now Indian) Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) on July 16, 1929, acting on the recommendations of the Royal Commission on Agriculture. ICAR is the apex body coordinating agricultural research, education, and extension in India.

  • Apex Research Body: Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) functioning under the Department of Agricultural Research and Education (DARE), Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare.
  • State Agricultural Universities (SAUs): The first SAU was established in 1960 at Pantnagar (Govind Ballabh Pant University of Agriculture and Technology), modeled on the US Land-Grant college system.
  • Deemed Universities: Premier institutions like Indian Agricultural Research Institute (IARI, New Delhi), National Dairy Research Institute (NDRI, Karnal), Indian Veterinary Research Institute (IVRI, Izatnagar), and Central Institute of Fisheries Education (CIFE, Mumbai) hold deemed university status under ICAR.

Agricultural Extension Systems and Grassroots Delivery

Transfer of technology from laboratory to land depends on structured institutional mechanisms. The national extension architecture bridges scientific discoveries and farming communities.

  • National Extension Service (NES): Launched on October 2, 1953, following the Community Development Programme (CDP, 1952) to provide intensive extension coverage.
  • Training and Visit (T&V) System: Introduced in 1974 with World Bank assistance, focusing on single-line command, regular training of extension personnel, and fortnightly visits to contact farmers.
  • Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK): First KVK was established in 1974 at Puducherry (Pondicherry) under TNAU, based on the recommendations of the Mohan Singh Mehta Committee. KVKs serve as single-window districts-level resource and training centers.
  • Agricultural Technology Management Agency (ATMA): A registered society at the district level operationalized under NATP/NAIP to integrate research-extension-farmer linkages and decentralize decision-making.

Specialized National Institutes and Commodity Boards

India maintains a network of specialized Central Agricultural Research Institutes (CARIs), National Research Centres (NRCs), and Commodity Boards to address specific crop, livestock, and socio-economic challenges.

  • Central Research Institutes: Examples include Central Rice Research Institute (CRRI, Cuttack), Indian Institute of Sugarcane Research (IISR, Lucknow), and Central Arid Zone Research Institute (CAZRI, Jodhpur).
  • Commodity Boards: Statutory bodies such as the Tea Board, Coffee Board, Rubber Board, Spices Board, and Tobacco Board regulate production, processing, and export of major commercial crops.
  • National Demonstration Projects: Initiated by ICAR in 1964 to demonstrate the biological potential of high-yielding crop varieties directly on farmers' fields under scientist supervision.

Rural Development Programs and Institutional Credit Agencies

Rural development institutions provide financial backing, infrastructure, and livelihood security to farming households across rural India.

  • NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development): Established on July 12, 1982, based on the recommendations of the B. Sivaraman Committee, serving as the apex refinancing institution for agricultural and rural credit.
  • Lead Bank Scheme: Introduced in 1969 following the Gadgil Study Group recommendations, assigning specific districts to commercial banks to spearhead banking development and credit flow.
  • Regional Rural Banks (RRBs): Established in 1975 under the RRB Act based on the Narasimham Working Group to cater specifically to small and marginal farmers, agricultural laborers, and rural artisans.
  • Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs): Constitutionalized through the 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act (1992), establishing a three-tier local self-government system (Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zilla Parishad) for decentralized rural planning.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the establishment year of ICAR (1929) with IARI (established in 1905 at Pusa, Bihar, and shifted to New Delhi in 1936 after the 1934 earthquake).
  • Mistaking the first KVK location as New Delhi instead of Puducherry (Pondicherry).
  • Believing NABARD is a direct lending institution to individual farmers rather than an apex refinancing agency.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Agricultural Institutions and Extension Systems

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Apex Research and Educational Infrastructure

ICAR operates as an autonomous organization under DARE, managing over 100 institutes and 70 agricultural universities across India.

  • DARE: Department of Agricultural Research and Education, created in 1973 in the Ministry of Agriculture.
  • SAUs: State Agricultural Universities funded jointly by ICAR and respective state governments following land-grant principles.
Concept 2

Frontline Extension Delivery via Krishi Vigyan Kendras

KVKs function as grassroots science centers conducting on-farm testing, frontline demonstrations, and vocational skill training.

  • Mandate: Technology assessment and refinement through On-Farm Testing (OFT) and Frontline Demonstrations (FLD).
  • Funding: 100% centrally funded by ICAR.
Concept 3

Agricultural Credit Architecture

Rural credit is channeled through a multi-agency approach comprising commercial banks, RRBs, cooperative banks, and apex refinancing bodies.

  • NABARD: Apex bank providing refinance to cooperative banks, RRBs, and commercial banks for term loans and crop loans.
  • Lead Bank Scheme: District-level credit planning initiated in 1969.
Concept 4

Decentralized Governance and Panchayati Raj

Local agricultural development plans are routed through democratically elected rural local bodies.

  • 73rd Amendment: Constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions in 1992.
  • Three Tiers: Gram Panchayat (village), Panchayat Samiti (block), and Zilla Parishad (district).

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK (Farm Science Centre))
District-level ICAR institution designed to impart vocational training and conduct frontline agricultural technology demonstrations.
NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development)
Apex development bank in India providing refinancing facilities for agriculture and rural development activities.
ATMA (Agricultural Technology Management Agency)
Autonomous district-level institution promoting decentralized, farmer-driven agricultural extension management.
T&V System (Training and Visit System)
World Bank-assisted extension system based on professional extension personnel, single-line command, and strict fortnightly visitation schedules.
DARE (Department of Agricultural Research and Education)
Government department under the Ministry of Agriculture facilitating linkages between central government and ICAR.
IARI (Indian Agricultural Research Institute)
India's premier agricultural research and higher education institution, colloquially known as Pusa Institute.
Lead Bank Scheme (LBS)
Banking scheme assigning specific districts to designated commercial banks to lead credit expansion and financial inclusion.
RRB (Regional Rural Bank)
Locally-focused commercial banks established in 1975 to provide credit to small and marginal farmers and rural laborers.
NATP (National Agricultural Technology Project)
ICAR project initiated with World Bank support to restructure and strengthen agricultural research and extension in India.
Lab-to-Land (Lab-to-Land Programme)
Extension program launched by ICAR in 1979 during its golden jubilee to transfer proven technologies from research stations to small farmers.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

ICAR Establishment VS IARI Establishment

ICAR was established in 1929 in New Delhi; IARI was established earlier in 1905 at Pusa, Bihar, and later shifted to New Delhi in 1936.

First KVK Location VS First SAU Location

The first KVK was established in Puducherry (1974), whereas the first SAU was established in Pantnagar, Uttarakhand (1960).
