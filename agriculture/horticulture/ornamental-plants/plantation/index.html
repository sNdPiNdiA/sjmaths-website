Plantation Crops: Agronomy, Pomology, and Management

Comprehensive study material on major plantation crops covering botanical taxonomy, propagation, agro-climatic requirements, nutrient management, and crop protection, tailored for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture examinations.

Introduction and Botanical Taxonomy of Plantation Crops

Plantation crops refer to those perennial crops cultivated on an extensive scale in a contiguous area, managed as an enterprise, and yielding high-value commercial produce requiring primary processing before final consumption. In the context of Indian agriculture and ICAR classification, major plantation crops include coconut,arecanut, oil palm, cashew, tea, coffee, rubber, and cocoa.

  • Coconut (Cocos nucifera): Family Arecaceae, origin Indo-Malaya or South America, chromosome number 2n = 32. It is often termed the 'Kalpavriksha' or tree of heaven due to its multifaceted utility.
  • Arecanut (Areca catechu): Family Arecaceae, origin Malaysia or tropical Asia, chromosome number 2n = 32. Known as the betel nut palm.
  • Cashew (Anacrhadium occidentale): Family Anacardiaceae, origin Northeastern Brazil, chromosome number 2n = 42. Introduced to India by the Portuguese in the 16th century primarily for soil conservation.
  • Tea (Camellia sinensis): Family Theaceae, origin China/Assam, chromosome number 2n = 30. An evergreen perennial shrub grown for its aromatic young leaves and flush.

Agro-Climatic Requirements, Soil, and Propagation Methods

Plantation crops have specific climatic and edaphic requirements due to their perennial nature and deep root systems. Tropical crops like coconut, arecanut, and oil palm thrive in humid tropical conditions with high relative humidity and well-distributed rainfall ranging from 2000 mm to 3000 mm annually.

  • Soil Requirements: Deep, well-drained soils are mandatory. Coconut prefers coastal sandy loams or laterite soils with good drainage and a water table within 1.5 to 2.0 meters. Tea and coffee require acidic soils (pH 4.5 to 5.5 for tea, 6.0 to 6.5 for Arabica coffee) rich in organic matter.
  • Propagation Techniques: Coconut is exclusively propagated through seed nuts selected from high-yielding mother palms aged 20 to 40 years. Arecanut is propagated through selected seed nuts sprouted in shaded nurseries. Tea is propagated vegetatively through single-node apical cuttings (green wood cuttings) in polythene sleeves under mist chambers. Cashew is commercially propagated through soft-wood grafting and air layering.
  • Spacing Geometry: Coconut is planted at a standard spacing of 7.5 m × 7.5 m or 8 m × 8 m accommodating about 160 to 175 palms per hectare. Tea is planted in double-hedge systems with a spacing of 120 cm × 60 cm × 45 cm.

Nutrient Management and Fertilizer Calculations in Plantation Crops

Perennial plantation crops extract substantial quantities of macro and micronutrients from the soil over decades. Balanced fertilization is essential for sustained economic yields. Fertilizers are generally applied in split doses coinciding with the onset of monsoons to maximize nutrient use efficiency and minimize leaching losses.

  • Nutrient Recommendation for Coconut (Adult Palm/Year): Standard recommendation is 500 g N, 320 g P2O5, and 1200 g K2O per palm per year, applied in two split doses (1/3rd in May-June and 2/3rd in September-October).
  • Fertilizer Calculation Example: To supply 500 g N using Urea (containing 46% N), the quantity of urea required per palm is calculated as: (500 / 46) × 100 = 1086.9 g of urea per palm annually.
  • Micronutrient Deficiencies: Boron deficiency in coconut causes button shedding and deformed nuts. Magnesium deficiency causes 'orange-leaf' or yellowing of older outer leaves. Zinc deficiency in tea results in 'little leaf' or rosette formation.

Irrigation, Weed Management, and Intercultural Operations

Although many plantation crops are grown under rainfed conditions in high-rainfall tracts, supplemental irrigation during dry summer months significantly boosts productivity and prevents premature fruit drop.

  • Irrigation Methods: Basin irrigation, drip irrigation, and micro-sprinklers are highly recommended. Drip irrigation saves up to 50% water and ensures optimal soil moisture tension. Coconut requires about 200 liters of water per palm once in 4 days under basin irrigation.
  • Weed Flora and Control: Common weeds in plantation orchards include Imperata cylindrica, Cyperus rotundus, and Borreria hispida. Integrated weed management involves manual slashing, mulching with organic crop residues (e.g., coconut husk), and application of systemic non-selective herbicides like glyphosate at 1.0 kg a.i./ha during active weed growth.
  • Cover Cropping: Leguminous cover crops like Calopogonium mucunoides, Pueraria phaseoloides, and Mucuna bracteata are cultivated in rubber and oil palm plantations to fix atmospheric nitrogen, check soil erosion, and suppress weed growth.

Major Pests, Diseases, and Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

Plantation crops are vulnerable to several destructive insect pests and fungal/viral diseases that can cause devastating crop losses if not managed proactively.

  • Pests of Coconut: Rhinoceros beetle (Oryctes rhinoceros) bores into unopened fronds; managed by field sanitation, application of hook, and releasing the baculovirus Oryctes. Red palm weevil (Rhynchophorus ferrugineus) is the most destructive internal tissue borer; managed using pheromone traps and curative injection of 1% chlorpyrifos.
  • Diseases of Coconut: Root wilt (mycoplasma-like organism vectored by tingid bug Stephanitis typica), Bud rot (Phytophthora palmivora) controlled by prophylactic spraying of 1% Bordeaux mixture.
  • Pests and Diseases of Tea: Tea mosquito bug (Helopeltis theivora) damages young shoots. Blister blight of tea caused by Exobasidium vexans is controlled by copper oxychloride or hexaconazole sprays during monsoon seasons.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Coconut botanical name is Cocos nucifera, family Arecaceae, chromosome number 2n = 32.
  • The most destructive internal pest of coconut palm is the Red Palm Weevil (Rhynchophorus ferrugineus).
  • Tea requires strongly acidic soils with a pH range of 4.5 to 5.5 for optimal growth and quality.
  • Commercial propagation of cashew is predominantly achieved through Softwood Grafting.
  • Bud rot of coconut is caused by the fungal pathogen Phytophthora palmivora and is effectively controlled by 1% Bordeaux mixture.
  • Cover crops like Calopogonium mucunoides are grown in rubber plantations to fix nitrogen and prevent soil erosion.
  • The economic part of tea is the 'two leaves and a bud' harvested during flushes.
  • Bacteriological control of rhinoceros beetle in coconut is achieved using the fungal pathogen Metarhizium anisopliae.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Students often confuse the propagation method of coconut (seed nuts) with tea, which is propagated vegetatively via stem cuttings.
  • Confusing the causal organism of bud rot of coconut (Phytophthora palmivora) with root wilt (phytoplasma vectored by Stephanitis typica).
  • Assuming cashew belongs to the family Arecaceae due to tropical association, whereas it belongs to Anacardiaceae.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Plantation Crops: Agronomy, Pomology, and Management

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Taxonomy and Commercial Significance

Plantation crops form the backbone of tropical agriculture, earning substantial foreign exchange.

  • Kalpavriksha: Coconut palm, signifying that every part of the tree is economically useful.
  • Commodity Boards: Statutory bodies in India such as Tea Board, Coffee Board, Rubber Board, and Spices Board regulating production and export.
Concept 2

Propagation and Nursery Management

Standardized propagation protocols ensure high genetic purity and early bearing in perennial plantation crops.

  • Softwood Grafting: Primary clonal propagation method for cashew ensuring true-to-type plants.
  • Mist Chamber: Essential greenhouse structure for rooting tea stem cuttings under high humidity.
Concept 3

Nutrient and Water Management

Perennial crops require deep-root fertilization and efficient irrigation scheduling to sustain yields over decades.

  • Split Application: Dividing fertilizer doses into pre-monsoon and post-monsoon applications to minimize nutrient leaching.
  • Drip Irrigation: Modern micro-irrigation technique that maximizes water use efficiency in coconut and oil palm.
Concept 4

Plant Protection and IPM

Integrated pest management combines cultural, biological, and chemical methods to combat pests and diseases.

  • Pheromone Traps: Utilized for trapping male adults of the red palm weevil in coconut plantations.
  • Bordeaux Mixture: Traditional copper fungicide (copper sulfate, lime, and water) used widely against fungal pathogens like Phytophthora.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Kalpavriksha (Cocos nucifera)
The mythological and practical tree of heaven referring to the coconut palm due to its versatility.
Copra (Dried coconut endosperm)
The dried kernel of coconut from which commercial coconut oil is extracted.
Flush (Tender shoot growth)
The periodic active vegetative growth of young shoots and leaves harvested in tea.
Softwood Grafting (Epicotyl / Wedge grafting)
A horticultural propagation technique used extensively in cashew using semi-hardwood scion sticks.
Bordeaux Mixture (CuSO4 + Ca(OH)2 + H2O)
A classic broad-spectrum protective fungicide discovered by Millardet in France.
Rhinoceros Beetle (Oryctes rhinoceros)
A major coleopteran pest boring into unopened fronds of coconut and oil palm.
Red Palm Weevil (Rhynchophorus ferrugineus)
A lethal internal tissue-boring pest of palms causing internal rotting and crown topple.
Cover Crop (Calopogonium mucunoides)
A leguminous creeping plant grown in plantations to suppress weeds and prevent soil erosion.
Button Shedding (Premature female flower drop)
A physiological or pathological disorder in coconut caused by nutrient deficiency or moisture stress.
Blister Blight (Exobasidium vexans)
A destructive fungal foliar disease of tea prevalent in high humidity and misty weather.
Commodity Board (Statutory Apex Body)
Government organization responsible for the overall development and export promotion of specific crops.
Double Hedge System (High-density planting geometry)
A specialized planting layout used in tea to accommodate higher plant population per unit area.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Plantation crops are perennial, high-value commercial crops cultivated on an extensive scale requiring primary processing.
  • Coconut (Cocos nucifera) is propagated through selected seed nuts and is prone to red palm weevil and bud rot.
  • Tea demands acidic soils (pH 4.5-5.5) and is vegetatively propagated through single-node stem cuttings.
  • Cashew is successfully propagated via softwood grafting and produces valuable kernels and cashew apples.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Rhinoceros Beetle VS Red Palm Weevil

Rhinoceros beetle feeds externally on unopened fronds causing V-shaped cuts, whereas Red Palm Weevil is an internal borer completing its life cycle inside the trunk.

Bud Rot of Coconut VS Root Wilt of Coconut

Bud rot is caused by Phytophthora palmivora (fungus), while root wilt is a phytoplasmal disease vectored by the tingid bug Stephanitis typica.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the botanical name and family of the coconut palm?

Explanation: Coconut is scientifically named Cocos nucifera and belongs to the family Arecaceae (formerly Palmae).

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest is considered the most destructive internal tissue borer of coconut palms?

Explanation: Red Palm Weevil (Rhynchophorus ferrugineus) is the most lethal internal borer, completing its lifecycle inside the trunk of coconut palms.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the ideal soil pH range required for successful tea cultivation?

Explanation: Tea thrives best in strongly acidic soils with a pH ranging between 4.5 and 5.5, which facilitates optimal uptake of nutrients like iron and manganese.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is the commercial method of vegetative propagation in Cashew?

Explanation: Softwood grafting is the standard and most successful commercial method for propagating high-yielding varieties of cashew true-to-type.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Bud rot of coconut is caused by which of the following pathogens?

Explanation: Bud rot of coconut is caused by the oomycete/fungal pathogen Phytophthora palmivora, leading to rotting of the central spindle leaf.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard chromosome number (2n) of the coconut palm?

Explanation: Both coconut (Cocos nucifera) and arecanut (Areca catechu) have a somatic chromosome number of 2n = 32.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which leguminous cover crop is commonly grown in rubber plantations to suppress weeds and prevent soil erosion?

Explanation: Calopogonium mucunoides, along with Pueraria phaseoloides, is extensively used as a cover crop in rubber and oil palm plantations.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Blister blight of tea is caused by which causal organism?

Explanation: Blister blight of tea is caused by the fungus Exobasidium vexans, causing translucent spots turning into white blisters on young leaves.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended annual N:P:K fertilizer dose for an adult bearing coconut palm under standard management?

Explanation: The standard fertilizer recommendation for an adult bearing coconut palm is 500 g N, 320 g P2O5, and 1200 g K2O per year.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which statutory board in India regulates the production, development, and export of coffee?

Explanation: The Coffee Board of India is a statutory body constituted under the Coffee Act VII of 1942, functioning under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary harvesting standard for high-quality tea manufacture?

Explanation: Plucking 'two leaves and a bud' is the universal standard for harvesting tea to ensure high quality and fine tea manufacture.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which pest causes characteristic 'V-shaped' cuts on unopened fronds of coconut palms?

Explanation: Rhinoceros beetle (Oryctes rhinoceros) damages emerging fronds, resulting in characteristic triangular or V-shaped cuts when the fronds open.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the botanical family of tea (Camellia sinensis)?

Explanation: Tea belongs to the family Theaceae, comprising acid-loving evergreen woody perennials.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following fungicides is most commonly used as a prophylactic spray against coconut bud rot?

Explanation: 1% Bordeaux mixture is the most effective and traditional prophylactic spray applied to the crown of coconut palms to prevent bud rot infection.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the origin and family of Cashew (Anacardium occidentale)?

Explanation: Cashew originates from Northeastern Brazil and belongs to the family Anacardiaceae.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which vector transmits the phytoplasma causing root wilt disease in coconut?

Explanation: The tingid lace bug Stephanitis typica acts as the insect vector transmitting the phytoplasma responsible for coconut root wilt disease.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the economic part of Arecanut (Areca catechu)?

Explanation: The endosperm (kernel) of the arecanut fruit is the economic part, processed into raw, boiled, or scented supari.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which biological agent is widely used for the microbial control of rhinoceros beetle larvae in manure pits?

Explanation: The entomopathogenic fungus Metarhizium anisopliae is successfully utilized to infect and kill grubs of the rhinoceros beetle in organic manure pits.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosome number (2n) of tea (Camellia sinensis)?

Explanation: The somatic chromosome number of tea (Camellia sinensis) is 2n = 30.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which agro-climatic condition is most suitable for the cultivation of rubber (Hevea brasiliensis)?

Explanation: Rubber requires a humid tropical climate with temperatures between 20°C to 35°C and well-distributed annual rainfall of over 2000 mm.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate the quantity of urea (46% N) required to supply the recommended nitrogen dose of 500 g per palm for 100 coconut palms in a year.

Solution & Rationale: For 1 palm: (500 / 46) × 100 = 1086.9 g. For 100 palms: 1086.9 g × 100 = 108,690 g = 108.69 kg (approx 108.7 kg).

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following pairs of Plantation Crop and its Family is INCORRECTLY matched?

Solution & Rationale: Rubber (Hevea brasiliensis) belongs to the family Euphorbiaceae, not Rubiaceae (which includes Coffee).

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

In tea plantations, what is the primary purpose of 'skiffing'?

Solution & Rationale: Skiffing is a light pruning operation in tea performed to level the bush canopy, remove damaged growth, and facilitate easy plucking.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following is an effective pheromone trap lure used for monitoring and mass trapping the Red Palm Weevil?

Solution & Rationale: Ferrugineol is the aggregation pheromone specifically synthesized and utilized to attract both male and female red palm weevils.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the standard spacing adopted for square system planting of coconut palms?

Solution & Rationale: The standard recommended spacing for coconut under rainfed conditions in a square system is 7.5 m × 7.5 m.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Which disorder in coconut is characterized by premature shedding of female flowers and is often associated with boron deficiency or moisture stress?

Solution & Rationale: Button shedding refers to the heavy premature dropping of female flowers (buttons) in coconut due to nutritional imbalances or drought.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

The process of tapping latex in rubber trees (Hevea brasiliensis) involves incision of which plant tissue?

Solution & Rationale: Latex is harvested by making a tangential cut on the trunk tapping the laticiferous vessels located in the secondary phloem/bark without damaging the cambium.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following plantation crops is propagated commercially through 'supple shoots' or 'stump planting'?

Solution & Rationale: Rubber is commonly established in the field using green budding or brown budded stumps derived from clonal nurseries.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the active ingredient and function of Bordeaux mixture in plantation protection?

Solution & Rationale: Bordeaux mixture is prepared by mixing copper sulfate (CuSO4) and lime (Ca(OH)2) in water, serving as a classic broad-spectrum protective fungicide.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

Which research station serves as the headquarters of the ICAR-Central Plantation Crops Research Institute (CPCRI)?

Solution & Rationale: ICAR-CPCRI is headquartered at Kasaragod in Kerala, serving as the premier apex research institution for coconut, arecanut, and cocoa.
