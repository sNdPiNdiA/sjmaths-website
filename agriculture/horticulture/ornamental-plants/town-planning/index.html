Introduction to Town Planning and Environmental Horticulture

Town planning in the context of agricultural and horticultural sciences encompasses the systematic arrangement of residential, industrial, commercial, and recreational zones integrated with functional green infrastructure. As per urban forestry and arboriculture standards, urban green spaces (UGSs) are vital for mitigating the urban heat island effect, reducing air pollution, and enhancing aesthetic quality.

  • Green Infrastructure: Comprises street trees, public parks, botanical gardens, institutional lawns, and median plantings.
  • Air Quality Management: Specific ornamental species act as bio-filters, absorbing particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10) and gaseous pollutants like sulfur dioxide (SO2) and nitrogen oxides (NOx).
  • Aesthetic and Psychological Harmony: Utilizes principles of line, form, color, and texture to create balanced landscapes that improve human well-being.

Basic Styles of Gardening and Landscape Design

Landscape design and town planning layouts draw heavily from historical gardening styles categorized into formal, informal, and wild styles. Understanding these frameworks is essential for designing institutional campuses, public parks, and highway planting schemes.

  • Formal Style (Geometrical Style): Characterized by strict symmetry, geometric beds, straight pathways, and clipped hedges/topiaries. Examples include Mughal gardens, Persian gardens, and Italian Renaissance gardens.
  • Informal Style (Natural Style): Imitates nature with curving paths, irregular water bodies, free-growing trees, and undulating lawns. Examples include Japanese gardens and English landscape gardens.
  • Free Style: A modern blend where functional requirements dictate design, combining elements of both formal and informal styles to suit urban architectural needs.

Arboriculture: Selection, Planting, and Maintenance of Street Trees

Street tree planting is a critical component of town planning. Trees planted along urban thoroughfares must possess specific morphological and physiological traits to withstand urban stress, soil compaction, and atmospheric pollution.

  • Desirable Traits for Avenue Trees: Deep root systems (preventing sidewalk disruption), sturdy branching habits, moderate growth rate, pest and disease resistance, and pollution tolerance.
  • Recommended Avenue Trees in India: Polyalthia longifolia (Mast tree), Azadirachta indica (Neem), Delonix regia (Gulmohar), Cassia fistula (Amaltas), and Millingtonia hortensis (Indian Cork Tree).
  • Planting Geometry: Standard spacing depends on the canopy spread at maturity, typically ranging from 8 to 12 meters apart along arterial roads. Pit dimensions for urban tree planting should be at least 1m × 1m × 1m filled with a balanced mixture of garden soil, farmyard manure (FYM), and coarse sand.

Turfing, Lawns, and Ground Covers in Urban Landscapes

Lawns form the green carpet foundation of any landscape design, offering recreational spaces and preventing soil erosion in urban settings. Establishing a dense, wear-tolerant turf requires careful grass species selection and maintenance.

  • Popular Turf Grasses: Cynodon dactylon (Doob grass - drought and wear tolerant, ideal for tropical plains), Zoysia japonica (Japanese lawn grass - slow-growing, dense turf), Paspalum vaginatum (Seashore paspalum - tolerant to salinity and waterlogging), and Stenotaphrum secundatum (Buffalo grass - shade tolerant).
  • Establishment Methods: Turfing (direct laying of turf pieces), turf plastering (dibbling runners), turf seeding, and sodding.
  • Maintenance Practices: Regular mowing (keeping height between 3 to 5 cm), vertical mowing (scarification), aeration, balanced nitrogen fertilization, and weed control using selective herbicides.

Features of Historic and Institutional Gardens

Historic garden traditions provide foundational blueprints for modern town planning and public park design. Key features must be recognized for agricultural examinations.

  • Mughal Gardens: Characterized by running water channels (nahars), terraces, central baradaris, and the incorporation of flowering and fragrant trees. Prominent examples include Shalimar Bagh, Nishat Bagh, and Pinjore Gardens.
  • Japanese Gardens: Symbolize nature in miniature, focusing on five principal elements: rocks, water, plants, bridges, and lanterns/pagodas.
  • English Gardens: Features expansive rolling lawns, picturesque tree groupings, herbaceous borders, and naturalistic lakes.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Polyalthia longifolia is commonly known as the Mast tree or False Ashoka and is widely used for vertical accent in avenue planting.
  • Cynodon dactylon (Doob grass) is the most drought-resistant and commonly used turf grass in the Indo-Gangetic plains.
  • Mughal gardens are famous for their terraced design, running water channels, and central Baradari pavilions.
  • The standard pit size recommended for planting large avenue trees in urban town planning is 1m × 1m × 1m.
  • Zoysia japonica exhibits high tolerance to foot traffic and low temperature, making it suitable for institutional lawns.
  • Windbreaks and shelterbelts in town planning are established perpendicular to the direction of prevailing strong winds.
  • Topiary is the art of clipping and training shrubs and trees into ornamental geometric or animal shapes.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing formal gardens with naturalistic informal gardens; remember Mughal gardens are strictly formal while Japanese gardens are informal.
  • Assuming all turf grasses perform equally well in heavy shade; species like Stenotaphrum secundatum tolerate shade better than Cynodon dactylon.
  • Neglecting the root architecture of avenue trees, which can cause severe damage to underground utilities and pedestrian pavements if taproot systems are absent.

Concept 1

Urban Green Infrastructure Principles

Urban green infrastructure integrates ecological principles into town planning to provide ecosystem services.

  • Bioclimatic Control: Trees and green belts reduce urban ambient temperature through evapotranspiration.
  • Noise Abatement: Multi-tiered planting of dense shrubs and tall trees acts as a physical sound barrier along highways.
Concept 2

Classification of Landscape Plants

Landscape plant materials are categorized based on their functional utility in town planning layouts.

  • Avenue Trees: Tall, upright trees providing shade and aesthetic alignment along roads.
  • Hedges and Edges: Shrubs planted closely to form living fences or boundary markers.
Concept 3

Lawn Establishment and Management

Establishing a permanent green turf requires precise land preparation, weed eradication, and appropriate grass propagation.

  • Turf Plastering: A common Indian method involving dibbling stolons in prepared soil moistened for rapid root development.
Concept 4

Historical Garden Archetypes

Landscape architecture relies on historical archetypes to establish thematic public spaces.

  • Mughal Paradigm: Symmetrical chahar-bagh layout emphasizing water and shade.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Hedge VS Edge

A hedge is a tall living fence (over 1 meter) used for boundary or screening, whereas an edge is a low-growing plant (under 30 cm) used to outline flower beds and pathways.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

In the context of landscape arboriculture, what does the term 'thatch' refer to in a lawn?

Solution & Rationale: Thatch is the intermingled layer of dead and living organic turf shoots and roots that accumulates above the soil surface.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Why are windbreaks planted perpendicular to the prevailing wind direction in regional planning?

Solution & Rationale: Windbreaks placed perpendicular reduce wind velocity and create a protected leeward zone extending downwind.
