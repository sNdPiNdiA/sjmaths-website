1. Introduction, Botanical Taxonomy, and Classification of Ornamental Plants

Ornamental horticulture involves the cultivation, propagation, and marketing of plants grown for aesthetic and environmental purposes. Based on their growth habits, morphology, and landscape utility, ornamental plants are broadly classified into annuals, biennials, perennials, bulbs, climbers, shrubs, and trees.

  • Annuals: Complete their life cycle in one growing season. Examples include Tagetes erecta (African marigold), Petunia hybrida (Petunia), and Antirrhinum majus (Snapdragon). They are categorized into winter annuals (grown in rabi) and summer annuals (grown in kharif).
  • Perennials and Woody Ornamentals: Plants that persist for multiple years. Shrubs like Rosa indica (Rose), Hibiscus rosa-sinensis (Chinese hibiscus), and trees like Delonix regia (Gulmohar) form the structural backbone of landscaping.
  • Bulbous Ornamentals: Plants possessing underground storage organs such as bulbs, corms, tubers, and rhizomes. Important examples include Gladiolus grandifloras (Gladiolus), Polianthes tuberosa (Tuberose), and Lilium longiflorum (Lily).

2. Soil, Climate, and Propagation Techniques in Floriculture

Successful production of ornamental crops depends heavily on precise edaphic and climatic parameters. Well-drained, fertile sandy loam to clay loam soils rich in organic matter with a pH range of 6.0 to 7.5 are optimal for most flowers and shrubs.

  • Climate and Photoperiodism: Many ornamental plants are sensitive to day length. Short-day plants like Chrysanthemum indicum require critical night lengths for floral induction, whereas long-day plants like Matthiola incana require extended photoperiods.
  • Propagation Methods: Propagation occurs via seeds (annuals), cutting (hardwood/semi-hardwood in Rosa and Hibiscus), layering (air layering or gootee in jasmine), division (chrysanthemum suckers, canna rhizomes), and micropropagation (tissue culture for disease-free orchids and gerbera).
  • Seed Rate and Spacing: Seed rates vary significantly; for instance, African marigold requires 1.0 to 1.5 kg seeds per hectare, planted at a spacing of 40 cm x 30 cm.

3. Nutrient Management, Irrigation, and Cultural Practices

Nutrient management in floriculture requires balanced fertilization tailored to the vegetative and reproductive phases. High-value cut flowers demand intensive fertigation.

  • Fertilizer Recommendations: For commercial rose and gladiolus cultivation, a general N:P:K recommendation of 200:100:100 kg/ha is standard. Split applications of nitrogen reduce leaching losses and promote continuous bloom.
  • Irrigation Scheduling: Critical moisture stress during bud differentiation and flowering causes bud drop and reduced stem length. Drip irrigation coupled with mulching is recommended to maintain optimum soil matric potential.
  • Pinching and Disbudding: Pinching (removal of growing tips) encourages lateral branching in Chrysanthemum and Tagetes, while disbudding eliminates side buds to produce a single large exhibition-quality bloom in Gladiolus and Chrysanthemum.

4. Plant Protection: Major Pests, Diseases, and Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

Ornamental plants are vulnerable to various insect pests, fungal pathogens, and viral vectors that mar their aesthetic value and marketability.

  • Insect Pests: Red spider mite (Tetranychus urticae) on roses, aphids (Aphis gossypii) on chrysanthemums, and Helicoverpa pod borer on marigolds. Control involves spraying dimethoate (0.05%) or abamectin.
  • Fungal Diseases: Powdery mildew caused by Podosphaera pannosa in roses and wilt caused by Fusarium oxysporum in gladiolus. Preventative spraying with wettable sulfur (0.2%) or carbendazim (0.1%) is effective.
  • Viral Diseases: Cucumber Mosaic Virus (CMV) and Tomato Spotted Wilt Virus (TSWV) transmitted by insect vectors like thrips and aphids. Eradication of infected host plants and vector control form the cornerstone of IPM.

5. Harvesting Indices, Post-Harvest Handling, and Commercial Yields

Post-harvest physiology dictates the vase life and market value of cut flowers.

  • Harvesting Indices: Roses are harvested at the tight bud stage when sepals reflex and color is visible. Gladiolus is harvested when the bottom 1-2 florets show color. Tuberose spikes are harvested when 1-2 pair of flowers open.
  • Vase Life Enhancement: Use of chemical pulsing solutions containing silver thiosulfate (STS) and sucrose (2-4%) extends vase life by preventing vascular blockage and ethylene synthesis.
  • Average Yields: Commercial marigold yields 12-15 tonnes of loose flowers per hectare; gladiolus produces 150,000 to 200,000 spikes per hectare.

  • Pinching is a critical cultural operation in Chrysanthemum to encourage lateral branching and enhance flower yield.
  • Botandical name of African marigold is Tagetes erecta, having a chromosome number of 2n = 24.
  • Silver thiosulfate (STS) is widely used as an anti-ethylene agent to increase the vase life of cut flowers like carnations and roses.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing bulbs and corms: Gladiolus produces corms, whereas Tuberose produces true bulbs.
  • Assuming marigold can only be grown as a winter annual; modern hybrid marigolds can be cultivated year-round under optimum irrigation.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Comprehensive Study Material on Ornamental Plants for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Taxonomical and Botanical Foundation

Understanding plant taxonomy and correct binomial nomenclature is fundamental for competitive examinations.

  • Binomial Nomenclature: Standard scientific naming formulated by Linnaeus, italicized in print.
  • Family Groupings: Asteraceae for composite flower heads, Iridaceae for sword-shaped foliage plants.
Concept 2

Propagation and Nursery Management

Mastering asexual and sexual propagation methods ensures high survival rates and true-to-type progeny.

  • Cuttings & Layers: Used extensively for woody perennials like roses and jasmine.
  • Tissue Culture: Essential for virus-free commercial production of orchids, gerbera, and carnation.
Concept 3

Cultural Operations and Canopy Management

Modifying plant architecture enhances floral quality and commercial yield.

  • Pinching: Removal of apical dominance to stimulate lateral bud breaks.
  • Disbudding: Selective removal of side buds for exhibition-sized solitary blooms.
Concept 4

Post-Harvest Physiology and Marketing

Post-harvest management minimizes qualitative and quantitative losses in perishable cut flowers.

  • Vase Life: Extended via germicides, acidifiers, and ethylene inhibitors.
  • Cold Chain: Storage at 2-4 degrees Celsius preserves market readiness.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Pinching (Apical Bud Removal)
The removal of the growing tip of a shoot to break apical dominance and promote lateral branching.
Disbudding (Lateral Bud Elimination)
The practice of removing side flower buds to channel all plant nutrients into producing a single large terminal bloom.
Corm (Modified Underground Stem)
A short, vertical, swollen underground plant stem that serves as a storage organ in plants like gladiolus and crocus.
Vase Life (Post-Harvest Longevity)
The duration cut flowers retain their visual appeal and freshness after harvesting.
Photoperiodism (Day-Length Response)
The physiological reaction of organisms to the length of night or day, critical for floral induction.
Geophyte (Perennial Plant with Underground Storage)
Plants that survive adverse environmental conditions by underground organs like bulbs, tubers, or corms.
Rugging (Rouging)
The removal of off-type, diseased, or undesirable plants from a seed production or commercial plot.
Deadheading (Spent Flower Removal)
The removal of dead or fading flowers from a plant to encourage further blooming and prevent seed set.
Gootee (Air Layering)
A propagation method where rooting is induced on an aerial stem while still attached to the parent plant.
Pulsing (High-Concentration Chemical Treatment)
Treating freshly cut flowers with high concentrations of sugar and germicides for a short duration prior to storage or transit.
Heading Back (Pruning Technique)
Pruning branch tips back to a bud or side branch to stimulate vigorous new growth.
Perennial (Multi-year Plant)
A plant that lives for more than two years, persisting through seasonal dieback of vegetative parts.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Gladiolus is propagated via corms; Tuberose is propagated via bulbs.
  • Chrysanthemum is a classic short-day plant requiring controlled photoperiods for commercial greenhouse forcing.
  • African marigold scientific name is Tagetes erecta (2n = 24), belonging to family Asteraceae.
  • Silver thiosulfate (STS) acts as an effective anti-ethylene treatment for ethylene-sensitive cut flowers.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Ornamental plants encompass annuals, perennials, bulbs, shrubs, and trees cultivated for aesthetic and landscaping value.
  • Scientific propagation relies on seeds for annuals and vegetative means (cuttings, corms, bulbs) for perennials.
  • Cultural practices like pinching and disbudding are vital for improving flower quality and yield.
  • Post-harvest longevity is significantly improved through correct harvesting stage and chemical pulsing agents.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Bulb VS Corm

A bulb consists of modified leaf scales attached to a short stem plate (e.g., onion, tuberose), whereas a corm is a solid, swollen underground stem base (e.g., gladiolus).

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following ornamental plants is commercially propagated through underground corms?

Explanation: Gladiolus is commercially propagated using corms. Polianthes tuberosa uses bulbs, Rosa uses hardwood cuttings, and Jasminum uses layered stems or cuttings.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of pinching in Chrysanthemum cultivation?

Explanation: Pinching removes apical dominance, stimulating lateral buds to grow, which increases the number of flowering stems and overall flower yield in chrysanthemums.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the botanical family of African marigold (Tagetes erecta)?

Explanation: African marigold belongs to the family Asteraceae (formerly Compositae), characterized by capitulum inflorescence.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical compound is widely used as an anti-ethylene agent to extend the vase life of cut flowers?

Explanation: Silver thiosulfate (STS) blocks ethylene action and receptors, substantially increasing vase life in sensitive cut flowers.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

At what stage should Gladiolus spikes be harvested for commercial marketing and long-distance transport?

Explanation: Gladiolus spikes are harvested when the bottom 1 to 2 florets show color, ensuring they will open successfully indoors and withstand transit.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a short-day ornamental plant requiring specific night lengths for floral induction?

Explanation: Chrysanthemum indicum is a classic short-day plant that initiates flowering when day lengths shorten below a critical threshold.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical commercial propagation material for Polianthes tuberosa (Tuberose)?

Explanation: Tuberose is commercially propagated using healthy, medium-to-large single bulbs weighing around 25-30 grams.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended seed rate for raising African marigold (Tagetes erecta) to plant one hectare?

Explanation: A seed rate of 1.0 to 1.5 kg per hectare is recommended to raise sufficient healthy seedlings for transplanting marigold.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest is notorious for causing severe leaf silvering and transmitting viral diseases in ornamental crops?

Explanation: Thrips are rasping-sucking insects that cause silvery scarring on foliage and petals, and act as primary vectors for tospoviruses.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does the cultural practice of 'disbudding' involve in ornamental horticulture?

Explanation: Disbudding involves removing lateral flower buds so that the plant's energy is channeled into developing one large, high-quality terminal bloom.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which plant family does the rose (Rosa indica) belong to?

Explanation: The rose belongs to the family Rosaceae, which includes many economically important fruit and ornamental species.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which method of propagation is most commonly utilized for commercial production of hybrid tea roses?

Explanation: Hybrid tea roses are commercially propagated using T-budding or shield budding onto hardy rootstocks like Rosa multiflora or Rosa indica var. odorata.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary symptom of powdery mildew (Podosphaera pannosa) infection in roses?

Explanation: Powdery mildew appears as a white, powdery fungal mycelium covering young leaves, stems, and flower buds, impairing photosynthesis and aesthetics.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a summer annual flower grown in Indian gardens?

Explanation: Zinnia elegans is a popular summer annual that thrives in warm weather, unlike petunia, phlox, and sweet pea which are winter (rabi) annuals.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the optimal soil pH range for cultivating the majority of ornamental and flowering plants?

Explanation: A pH range of 6.0 to 7.5 is optimal for most ornamental plants, ensuring balanced availability of macro and micronutrients.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which plant hormone is predominantly applied to stimulate rooting in ornamental stem cuttings?

Explanation: Indole-3-butyric acid (IBA) is the most effective and widely used auxin for promoting adventitious root development in hardwood and semi-hardwood cuttings.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the term for removing spent or dead flowers from ornamental plants to prolong blooming?

Explanation: Deadheading is the removal of faded flowers before seed formation occurs, redirecting plant energy into producing new flower buds.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which fungal pathogen is the primary causal agent of Fusarium wilt in gladiolus and carnations?

Explanation: Fusarium oxysporum causes vascular wilt in gladiolus and carnations, plugging xylem vessels and leading to wilting and plant death.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical vase life of well-managed standard cut roses kept in preservative solutions?

Explanation: With proper harvesting, cold chain management, and preservative pulsing, standard cut roses achieve a vase life of 7 to 14 days.

In commercial floriculture, what is the significance of using Silver Thiosulfate (STS) pulsing solutions?

Solution & Rationale: STS is an effective anti-ethylene agent that prevents premature petal abscission and senescence in ethylene-sensitive flowers.

Test Question 2 of 10 Página/TGT Standard

What distinguishes a bulb from a corm in geophytic ornamental plants?

Solution & Rationale: Morphologically, a bulb is composed largely of modified leaf scales (e.g., onion, tuberose), while a corm is a solid, swollen stem base (e.g., gladiolus, crocus).

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Agriculture Standard

A commercial grower observes yellowing of leaves with green veins (interveinal chlorosis) in rose plants grown in calcareous soil. Which corrective measure should be recommended?

Solution & Rationale: Interveinal chlorosis in alkaline/calcareous soils indicates iron deficiency, which is rapidly corrected via foliar application of ferrous sulfate.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Agriculture Standard

Why is 'pinching' an indispensable cultural operation in commercial Chrysanthemum production?

Solution & Rationale: Pinching removes the apical bud, reducing auxin concentration and allowing lateral buds to develop into numerous flowering shoots.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Agriculture Standard

Which of the following binomial combinations and families is incorrectly matched?

Solution & Rationale: Rosa indica belongs to the family Rosaceae, not Solanaceae (which includes tomato, potato, and brinjal).

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Agriculture Standard

Under what environmental condition is flower induction successfully triggered in short-day plants like Chrysanthemum?

Solution & Rationale: Short-day plants require continuous darkness exceeding a critical length (night length greater than day length) for floral bud initiation.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Agriculture Standard

What is the recommended propagation method and rootstock utilization for commercial Hybrid Tea roses?

Solution & Rationale: Hybrid tea roses are commercially produced by T-budding scion cultivars onto hardy, disease-resistant rootstocks.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Agriculture Standard

What is the primary role of gibberellic acid (GA3) when applied in specific ornamental floriculture operations?

Solution & Rationale: Gibberellic acid is a plant growth regulator known for promoting internodal elongation, breaking bud dormancy, and enlarging flower size.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Agriculture Standard

Which of the following symptoms characterizes infection by Cucumber Mosaic Virus (CMV) in ornamental annuals?

Solution & Rationale: Viral infections like CMV characteristically cause foliar mosaic mottling, leaf distortion, and stunted plant development.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Agriculture Standard

What is the correct harvesting stage for Tuberose (Polianthes tuberosa) spikes destined for local loose flower markets versus distant cut-flower markets?

Solution & Rationale: For loose flower extraction, individual open flowers are hand-picked, whereas for cut spikes, harvesting occurs when the bottom 1-2 pairs of florets open.
