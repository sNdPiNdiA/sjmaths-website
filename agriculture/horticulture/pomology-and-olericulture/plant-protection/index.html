Principles of Plant Protection and Economic Threshold Level (ETL)

Plant protection encompasses the study and management of biotic stresses—namely insect pests, plant pathogens (fungi, bacteria, viruses, phytoplasmas, nematodes), and weeds—that negatively impact horticultural productivity. Understanding population dynamics is vital for executing timely interventions.

  • Economic Injury Level (EIL): The lowest pest population density that will cause economic damage, where the cost of artificial control equals the value of the yield saved.
  • Economic Threshold Level (ETL): The pest density at which control measures should be applied to prevent the pest population from reaching the EIL.
  • Biannual/Seasonal Monitoring: Systematic scouting, pheromone trapping, and light trapping ensure early detection and prevent catastrophic crop loss in high-value horticultural systems.

Major Insect Pests of Fruit Crops (Pomology) and Management

Fruit crops are vulnerable to a wide array of borers, sap-sucking insects, and defoliators. Precise identification of life stages and injury symptoms guides effective Integrated Pest Management (IPM).

  • Mango Hopper (Idioscopus clypealis and Amritodus atkinsoni): Nymphs and adults suck cell sap from tender panicles, leaves, and shoots, leading to severe flower drop and secretion of honeydew that promotes sooty mold (Capnodium sp.).
  • Mango Stem Borer (Batocera rufomaculata): Grubs bore into the trunk and branches, feeding on internal sapwood and causing structural weakening and gum exudation. Controlled by cleaning holes and applying kerosene or dichlorvos.
  • Citrus Psylla (Diaphorina citri): Piercing-sucking pest acting as the primary vector for the devastating bacterial greening disease, Huanglongbing (HLB) caused by Candidatus Liberibacter asiaticus.
  • Fruit Fly (Bactrocera dorsalis): Females oviposit into ripening fruits; maggots feed internally, causing premature fruit drop and rot. Managed using methyl eugenol traps.

Major Insect Pests of Vegetable Crops (Olericulture) and Management

Intensively cultivated vegetable crops experience heavy pressure from polyphagous lepidopteran borers and sap-sucking vectors.

  • Brinjal Shoot and Fruit Borer (Leucinodes orbonalis): Larvae bore into tender shoots (causing 'dead hearts') and later into developing fruits, completely ruining marketability. Management involves clipping infested shoots and installing pheromone traps.
  • Tomato Fruit Borer (Helicoverpa armigera): Polyphagous pest; caterpillars make circular holes in green and ripening fruits while thrusting their anterior half inside.
  • Diamondback Moth (Plutella xylostella) on Cole Crops: Larvae feed on leaf epidermis, creating 'shot-hole' appearances on cabbage and cauliflower leaves. Known for developing rapid resistance to conventional insecticides.
  • Red Pumpkin Beetle (Aulacophora foveicollis): Adults skeletonize leaves and damage roots/stems of cucurbits, especially at the seedling stage.

Fungal, Bacterial, and Viral Diseases in Horticultural Crops

Pathogenic infections cause severe quantitative and qualitative losses in horticultural ecosystems, requiring exact diagnostic and prophylactic strategies.

  • Powdery Mildew of Mango and Cucurbits (Oidium mangiferae / Erysiphe cichoracearum): White, powdery fungal mycelium covers leaves, inflorescences, and young fruits. Controlled effectively by wettable sulfur sprays.
  • Citrus Canker (Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. citri): Bacterial disease causing raised, corky, necrotic lesions on leaves, twigs, and fruits. Spread via wind-driven rain; managed through pruning and streptomycin sulfate sprays.
  • Tomato Leaf Curl Virus (ToLCV): Transmitted by the whitefly vector Bemisia tabaci, causing severe upward rolling, puckering, and stunting of plants with drastic yield reduction.
  • Panama Wilt of Banana (Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. cubense): Soil-borne fungal pathogen invading the vascular system, causing yellowing and longitudinal splitting of leaf sheaths.

Pesticide Formulation, Toxicology, and Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

Safe, judicious application of agrochemicals combined with biological control agents ensures sustainable pest suppression without disrupting natural ecological balances.

  • Systemic vs. Contact Insecticides: Contact chemicals kill upon external touch (e.g., Cypermethrin), whereas systemic chemicals are absorbed and translocated throughout plant tissues to target sap-sucking insects (e.g., Imidacloprid, Dimethoate).
  • Fungicides: Protective (preventative) fungicides like Mancozeb and Chlorothalonil versus systemic (curative) fungicides like Carbendazim and Hexaconazole.
  • Biological Control: Utilization of natural predators and parasitoids such as Trichogramma chilonis (egg parasitoid) and entomopathogenic fungi like Beauveria bassiana.
  • Safe Handling: Strict adherence to the Waiting Period (Pre-Harvest Interval - PHI) to eliminate hazardous chemical residues in consumable fruits and vegetables.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Economic Threshold Level (ETL) is always lower than Economic Injury Level (EIL) to allow time for control measures before economic damage occurs.
  • Methyl eugenol traps are specifically deployed for monitoring and mass trapping of the male fruit fly (Bactrocera dorsalis).
  • Citrus canker is caused by a bacterium named Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. citri, characterized by raised corky lesions on fruit rinds.
  • Panama wilt of banana is caused by the soil-borne fungus Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. cubense, which persists in soil for decades.
  • Imidacloprid is a neonicotinoid systemic insecticide widely used against sap-sucking insect vectors like aphids, jassids, and whiteflies.
  • Trichogramma chilonis is a widely used egg parasitoid released in biological control programs against lepidopteran borers.
  • Powdery mildew on cucurbits and mango panicles is effectively managed using wettable sulfur formulations.
  • The vector responsible for transmitting Tomato Leaf Curl Virus (ToLCV) is the whitefly Bemisia tabaci.
  • Waiting period refers to the mandatory time interval between the last pesticide application and the safe harvest of the crop.
  • Bordeaux mixture (Copper sulfate + Lime + Water in 1:1:10 ratio) discovered by Millardet is a classic protective fungicide.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Students often confuse Economic Injury Level (EIL) with Economic Threshold Level (ETL); remember EIL is the damage level, while ETL is the action trigger point.
  • Confusing contact fungicides (like Mancozeb, which only protect the surface) with systemic fungicides (like Carbendazim, which cure internal infections).
  • Assuming all insect pests are controlled by systemic insecticides; chewing insects and borers often require contact or stomach poisons.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Plant Protection in Horticultural Crops: Principles, Entomology, Plant Pathology, and Integrated Management

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Economic Threshold Level and Pest Management Economics

Plant protection economics relies on calculating when pest damage costs exceed control costs.

  • EIL: The lowest pest density that causes economic damage equal to control costs.
  • ETL: The tactical action threshold below EIL where intervention must begin.
Concept 2

Insect Pest Dynamics in Fruit Orchards

Orchard pests require multi-tiered management involving mechanical cleaning, pheromone trapping, and targeted chemical rotation.

  • Stem Borers: Require physical extraction and insertion of fumigants into bore holes.
  • Fruit Flies: Managed efficiently via male annihilation technique using methyl eugenol.
Concept 3

Vegetable Pest Complex and Damage Symptoms

Vegetables sustain high pressure from borers that destroy marketable produce.

  • Shoot and Fruit Borer: Causes dead hearts in brinjal shoots and ruined bored fruits.
  • Diamondback Moth: Defoliates cole crops and rapidly develops insecticide resistance.
Concept 4

Phytopathological Etiology and Disease Vectors

Fungal, bacterial, and viral pathogens require distinct management protocols based on their transmission mode.

  • Vector-Borne Viruses: Controlled primarily by managing insect vectors like whiteflies and aphids.
  • Soil-Borne Fungi: Managed through crop rotation, soil solarization, and resistant rootstocks.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Economic Injury Level (EIL)
The lowest pest population density that causes economic damage.
Economic Threshold Level (ETL)
The pest density at which control measures should be initiated.
Systemic Pesticide (Systemic Agrochemical)
A chemical absorbed by the plant and translocated internally to protect against pests.
Contact Pesticide (Contact Agrochemical)
A chemical that kills pests upon direct external physical contact.
Vector (Biological Transmitting Agent)
An organism, typically an insect, that transmits disease-causing pathogens from plant to plant.
Waiting Period (Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI))
The required time gap between pesticide application and safe harvest of edible produce.
Bordeaux Mixture (Copper Sulfate + Quicklime + Water)
A classic broad-spectrum protective fungicide formulated in 1:1:10 ratio.
Parasitoid (Entomophagous Insect)
An insect whose larvae develop on or inside other arthropod hosts, eventually killing them.
Sooty Mold (Capnodium spp.)
A fungus growing on honeydew secretions of sap-sucking insects, hindering photosynthesis.
Dead Heart (Borer Injury Symptom)
Withered, dried central growing shoot caused by larval boring activity.

  • Fruit flies are effectively trapped using methyl eugenol lures.
  • Sap-sucking vectors transmit devastating viral and bacterial diseases like citrus greening and leaf curl.
  • Systemic fungicides and insecticides offer internal protection, unlike contact formulations.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

EIL VS ETL

EIL is the damage breaking point, while ETL is the action trigger point set below EIL.

Contact Fungicide VS Systemic Fungicide

Contact fungicides stay on the plant surface; systemic fungicides enter and move through plant vascular tissues.

What does the term 'Waiting Period' (Pre-Harvest Interval) signify in pesticide application?

Explanation: The waiting period or pre-harvest interval is the mandatory time gap required for pesticide residues to degrade safely below harmful thresholds prior to harvest.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard ratio of ingredients in traditional Bordeaux mixture?

Explanation: Traditional Bordeaux mixture is formulated using 1 part copper sulfate, 1 part quicklime, and 10 parts water (1:1:10).

Which insect pest causes severe flower drop in mango trees by sucking cell sap from panicles and excreting honeydew?

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of Integrated Pest Management (IPM)?

Explanation: IPM aims to maintain pest populations below economic injury levels through economically and environmentally sound combination of tactics.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the mode of action of contact insecticides?

Explanation: Contact insecticides act externally by penetrating the insect's body wall or cuticle when physical contact occurs.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ
