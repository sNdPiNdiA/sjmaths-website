Principles of Soil-Water-Plant Relationships

Understanding the dynamic interactions between soil moisture constants, plant water uptake mechanisms, and atmospheric evaporative demand is fundamental for efficient irrigation management in horticulture. Water is held within the soil matrix by capillary and adsorptive forces, characterized quantitatively by energy states known as soil water potential.

  • Saturation Percentage: Occurs when all soil pores are filled with water, corresponding to a soil water potential of approximately zero bars (0 kPa).
  • Field Capacity (FC): The amount of soil moisture retained after excess gravitational water has drained away and the rate of downward movement has drastically decreased, typically occurring at -0.33 bars (-33 kPa) for loamy soils.
  • Permanent Wilting Point (PWP): The soil moisture content at which plants can no longer extract water and remain permanently wilted, corresponding to a soil water potential of -15 bars (-1500 kPa).
  • Available Water Capacity (AWC): The range of moisture available to plants, calculated mathematically as the numerical difference in moisture content between Field Capacity and Permanent Wilting Point (AWC = FC - PWP).

Irrigation Scheduling and Critical Stages in Horticultural Crops

Irrigation scheduling determines the precise quantity of water to apply and the optimal timing of application to maximize crop yield per drop while preventing water stress or waterlogging. Scheduling approaches rely on soil moisture depletion limits, climatological water balance, and physiological critical growth stages.

  • Critical Stages: Periods during crop ontogeny when moisture stress causes irreversible yield losses or quality deterioration. For instance, in solanaceous vegetables like tomato (Solanum lycopersicum), flowering and fruit set are extremely sensitive stages.
  • Pan Evaporation Approach (CPE): Scheduling based on the ratio of irrigation water depth (IW) to Cumulative Pan Evaporation (CPE). An IW/CPE ratio of 0.8 or 1.0 is standard for many vegetable and fruit crops under semi-arid conditions.
  • Soil Moisture Tension: Using tensiometers to trigger irrigation when tension reaches specific thresholds, typically 20 to 30 kPa for shallow-rooted leafy vegetables and 50 to 70 kPa for deep-rooted fruit trees.

Surface, Sprinkler, and Micro-Irrigation Systems

Selection of the appropriate irrigation method depends on topography, soil texture, water availability, crop geometry, and economic viability. Modern horticultural practices increasingly emphasize pressurized micro-irrigation systems to maximize water use efficiency (WUE).

  • Surface Irrigation: Basin, border strip, and furrow methods. Highly prone to deep percolation and evaporation losses, with field application efficiencies ranging from 50% to 60%.
  • Sprinkler Irrigation: Overhead application simulating rainfall, suitable for undulating topographies and close-growing crops. Field efficiencies range from 75% to 85%.
  • Drip (Trickle) Irrigation: Application of water drop-by-drop directly to the root zone via emitters at low operating pressures (1.0 to 2.5 bar). Achieves the highest application efficiency (90% to 95%) and enables fertigation.
  • Sub-surface Drip Irrigation (SDI): Emitters buried below the soil surface, minimizing evaporation and weed germination while protecting components from mechanical damage.

Water Requirement Calculations and Irrigation Efficiency

Accurate quantification of crop water requirements ensures optimal sizing of irrigation infrastructure and prevents over-irrigation. Crop evapotranspiration (ET_c) is derived from reference crop evapotranspiration (ET_0) multiplied by the crop coefficient (K_c).

  • Formula: ET_c = ET_0 × K_c
  • Net Irrigation Requirement (NIR): NIR = ET_c - P_e - Δ GW, where P_e is effective rainfall and Δ GW is groundwater contribution.
  • Gross Irrigation Requirement (GIR): GIR = NIRE_a, where E_a is the decimal fraction of irrigation efficiency.
  • Duty and Delta: Delta (Δ) represents the total depth of water (in cm or meters) required by a crop from sowing to harvest, while Duty (D) is the area of land (in hectares) irrigated by a continuous supply of one cubic meter per second of water.

Drainage and Management of Salinity Hazards in Irrigated Agriculture

Improper irrigation without adequate drainage leads to secondary soil salinization, waterlogging, and sodicity, severely impairing agricultural productivity in canal-command areas.

  • Leaching Requirement (LR): The fraction of irrigation water that must pass through the root zone to maintain soil salinity below the threshold tolerance level of the crop: LR = ECiwECed, where ECiw is electrical conductivity of irrigation water and ECed is electrical conductivity of drainage water.
  • Surface Drainage: Removal of excess surface water via field ditches and lateral drains to prevent root suffocation.
  • <Sub-surface Drainage: Installation of perforated corrugated plastic pipes or tile drains at predetermined depths and spacing to lower high water tables and leach out accumulated salts.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Field capacity is measured at -0.33 bars (-33 kPa) soil water tension.
  • Permanent wilting point occurs at -15 bars (-1500 kPa) soil water tension.
  • Drip irrigation achieves the highest water use efficiency, ranging between 90% and 95%.
  • Crop evapotranspiration (ET_c) is calculated as the product of reference crop evapotranspiration (ET_0) and crop coefficient (K_c).
  • Tensiometers are best suited for scheduling irrigation in coarse-textured soils operating within the 0 to 85 kPa tension range.
  • Leaching requirement formula is LR = ECiw / ECed to manage secondary soil salinization.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing field capacity (-0.33 bar) with permanent wilting point (-15 bar); remember that lower negative pressure means higher water retention.
  • Assuming Gross Irrigation Requirement is less than Net Irrigation Requirement; GIR is always higher due to system inefficiencies.
  • Using tensiometers in heavy clay soils where moisture tension exceeds 85 kPa, causing the instrument to lose hydraulic continuity.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Advanced Irrigation Engineering and Water Management in Horticulture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Soil Water Constants and Energy State

Soil moisture constants define the energetic state of water in the root zone.

  • Field Capacity: Upper limit of available water held at -0.33 bar tension.
  • Wilting Point: Lower limit of available water held at -15.0 bar tension.
Concept 2

Evapotranspiration and Crop Coefficients

Crop water demand fluctuates according to meteorological parameters and phenological stages.

  • ET_0: Reference crop evapotranspiration calculated via the Penman-Monteith equation.
  • K_c: Empirical crop coefficient varying across initial, mid-season, and maturity stages.
Concept 3

Irrigation Efficiency Metrics

Quantifying water losses across conveyance, application, and storage phases.

  • Application Efficiency: Ratio of water stored in root zone to water delivered to the field.
  • Water Use Efficiency: Economic yield produced per unit volume of water consumed (kg/m^3).
Concept 4

Micro-Irrigation and Fertigation Integration

Applying water and soluble fertilizers simultaneously through pressurized networks.

  • Venturi Injector: Differential pressure device used for suction and mixing of fertilizer solution.
  • Emitters: Precision discharge devices operating at specific flow rates (e.g., 2 LPH, 4 LPH).

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Field Capacity (Maximum Retained Moisture (-0.33 bar))
Soil moisture content after gravitational drainage ceases.
Permanent Wilting Point (Lower Limit of Availability (-15 bar))
Moisture content where plants undergo irreversible wilting.
Available Water (AWC = FC - PWP)
Water retained between field capacity and wilting point.
Evapotranspiration (ET = Evaporation + Transpiration)
Total water loss from soil surface and plant canopy.
Crop Coefficient (Kc = ETc / ET0)
Ratio relating crop water demand to reference evapotranspiration.
Tensiometer (Soil Moisture Suction Gauge)
Instrument measuring soil water matric potential in unsaturated soils.
Leaching Requirement (LR = ECiw / ECed)
Fraction of irrigation water required to control root zone salinity.
Gross Irrigation Requirement (GIR = NIR / Ea)
Total water required including application and conveyance losses.
Fertigation (Nutrient Application via Irrigation)
Simultaneous application of water and dissolved fertilizers.
Duty of Water (Hectares per Cumec)
Area of crop irrigated by continuous flow of 1 m3/s during maturity.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • FC is -0.33 bar; PWP is -15 bar.
  • GIR equals NIR divided by application efficiency.
  • Penman-Monteith method is the standard for estimating reference evapotranspiration (ET_0).
  • Tensiometers operate effectively up to 85 kPa.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Soil water energy states govern water availability to horticultural crops.
  • Drip irrigation achieves over 90% application efficiency and allows precise fertigation.
  • Scheduling based on CPE ratios optimizes water productivity in vegetables and fruits.
  • Leaching requirement prevents salt accumulation in arid and semi-arid command areas.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Net Irrigation Requirement VS Gross Irrigation Requirement

NIR is the exact water needed by the crop, whereas GIR accounts for system efficiency losses.

Field Capacity VS Saturation

Saturation fills all soil pores (0 bar), while Field Capacity represents retention after gravitational drainage (-0.33 bar).

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

In the Penman-Monteith equation for estimating reference evapotranspiration (ET0), which parameters are explicitly integrated?

Test Question 4 of 10 PGR Standard

What is the theoretical tension limit beyond which standard mercury tensiometers lose hydraulic contact with the soil?

Solution & Rationale: Tensiometers measure matric suction up to approximately 85 kPa (0.85 bar), beyond which cavitation occurs inside the water column.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

If the electrical conductivity of irrigation water (ECiw) is 1.5 dS/m and the maximum tolerable electrical conductivity of drainage water (ECed) is 10.0 dS/m, what is the Leaching Requirement (LR)?

Which scheduling parameter uses the ratio of Irrigation Water (IW) to Cumulative Pan Evaporation (CPE)?

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary consequence of continuous over-irrigation without adequate drainage in canal command areas?
