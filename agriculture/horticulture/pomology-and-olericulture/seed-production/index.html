Fundamentals and Classes of Seed Production

Seed production is the cornerstone of advanced agricultural science, ensuring the multiplication and distribution of genetically pure, physically clean, and biologically viable planting materials. In horticultural and olericultural crop production, maintaining genetic integrity requires strict adherence to institutional seed classes recognized by the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) and national seed certification agencies.

  • Nucleus Seed: The initial, pure seed stock produced and maintained by the originating plant breeder or sponsoring institution. It serves as the physical and genetic foundation for all subsequent seed classes.
  • Breeder Seed: Seed directly derived from nucleus seed. It is supervised directly by the originating plant breeder and exhibits the highest standard of genetic purity (100% or absolute adherence to variety traits).
  • Foundation Seed: Progeny of breeder seed handled to maintain specific genetic identity and purity. It is produced by authorized seed agencies and serves as the source for registered and certified seed production.
  • Certified Seed: The progeny of foundation (or registered) seed. It is widely distributed to commercial vegetable and fruit growers for raising market crops. Certified seed must meet minimum genetic purity standards (typically 95% to 99% depending on the crop).

Genetic Purity and Mechanisms of Pollination

Understanding floral biology, pollination mechanisms, and genetic degradation pathways is essential for successful horticultural seed production. Cross-pollinated crops undergo rapid genetic erosion and mechanical mixing if strict isolation distances are not maintained.

  • Self-Pollinated Crops (Autogamy): Crops like tomato (Solanum lycopersicum), pea (Pisum sativum), and lettuce (Lactuca sativa) exhibit cleistogamy or chasmogamy with natural cross-pollination rates below 5%. Isolation distances are relatively narrow (e.g., 50 meters for foundation seed).
  • Cross-Pollinated Crops (Allogamy): Crops like onion (Allium cepa), carrot (Daucus carota), and cole crops (Brassica oleracea group) rely on entomophily or anemophily. Cross-pollination exceeds 50%, requiring expansive isolation distances (up to 1000 meters) or physical caging/netting.
  • Often Cross-Pollinated Crops: Crops like okra (Abelmoschus esculentus) and eggplant (Solanum melongena) show intermediate cross-pollination (5% to 30%) due to insect activity, requiring moderate isolation distances (100 to 200 meters).
  • Rouging: The systematic removal of off-type plants, diseased individuals, volunteer plants, and weeds before flowering and pollination occurs. Rouging is performed at least three times: vegetative stage, flowering stage, and pre-harvest maturity stage.

Agro-Techniques and Environmental Requirements

Horticultural seed crops demand precise environmental conditions, optimal nutrient management, and rigorous water management compared to commercial ware production. Seed crops often require longer field durations to allow complete seed maturation and desiccation on the mother plant.

  • Soil and Seedbed Preparation: Deep ploughing followed by harrowing ensures fine tilth, eliminating soil-borne pathogen resting structures and facilitating uniform seedling emergence. Well-drained sandy loam to clay loam soils with high organic matter (pH 6.0 to 7.5) are preferred.
  • Nutrient Management: Excessive nitrogen delays maturity and increases susceptibility to lodging and fungal infections. Adequate phosphorus (crucial for root development and seed setting) and potassium (enhances grain/seed filling and stress tolerance) must be applied as basal doses, supplemented with foliar micronutrients like boron and molybdenum to prevent hollow heart and blossom-end rot.
  • Irrigation Management: Water stress during flowering, fruit set, and seed filling drastically reduces seed yield and vigor. Drip irrigation or furrow irrigation timed at critical physiological stages prevents moisture stress while avoiding excessive canopy humidity that promotes fungal pathogens like Alternaria or Botrytis.

Harvesting Indices, Processing, and Storage Physics

Seed harvesting must be executed at physiological maturity rather than commercial maturity to guarantee maximum dry weight accumulation, maximum germination potential, and optimal seedling vigor.

  • Physiological Maturity: Occurs when translocation of photosynthates from the mother plant ceases, marked by the appearance of a black layer in certain seeds, moisture reduction to 30-40%, and maximum seed dry weight.
  • Seed Extraction Methods: Dry extraction (threshing, beating, or air-screen cleaning) for legumes, crucifers, and solanaceous dry pods; wet extraction (fermentation, acid treatment with hydrochloric acid, or mechanical pulping) for fleshy fruits like tomato, cucumber, and eggplant.
  • Seed Drying: Sun drying or mechanical forced-air drying until safe storage moisture limits are achieved (typically 7% to 9% for oilseeds and legume seeds, 10% to 12% for cereal and vegetable seeds). High drying temperatures (>43°C) denature seed proteins and destroy viability.
  • Seed Storage and Viability: Governed by Harrington's Rules of Thumb: for every 1% decrease in seed moisture or 5.6°C (10°F) decrease in storage temperature, the storage life of the seed is doubled. Hermetic containers combined with silica gel or low-temperature cold storage (-18°C for gene banks) preserve genetic resources for decades.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Breeder seed is produced under the direct supervision of the originating plant breeder and carries a Golden Yellow tag.
  • Foundation seed is the progeny of breeder seed and requires a White tag with minimum 99.5% genetic purity.
  • Certified seed carries a Blue tag and is utilized by commercial farmers for general cultivation.
  • Seed moisture content for safe long-term storage of most vegetable seeds should be below 8% to 10%.
  • Isolation distance for cross-pollinated cole crops like cauliflower and cabbage is 1000 meters for foundation seed and 500 meters for certified seed.
  • Acid extraction of tomato seeds uses hydrochloric acid (HCl) at 10-15 ml per kg of pulp to dissolve the gelatinous sac and kill seed-borne viruses.
  • Physiological maturity is characterized by maximum seed dry weight and cessation of nutrient translocation from the mother plant.
  • Rouging is the removal of off-types, diseased plants, and weeds, and must be completed before flowering commences.
  • Harrington's Rule states that lowering storage temperature by 5.6°C or seed moisture by 1% doubles seed storage life.
  • Seed vigor encompasses seed properties that determine rapid, uniform emergence and development under wide range of field conditions.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing breeder seed tag color with foundation seed tag color. (Breeder = Golden Yellow; Foundation = White; Certified = Blue).
  • Assuming physiological maturity and commercial harvest maturity occur simultaneously. (Physiological maturity occurs earlier with maximum dry weight).
  • Neglecting isolation distance in self-pollinated vegetable crops like tomato. (Even self-pollinated crops require minimum 50m isolation to prevent outcrossing and mechanical mixtures).

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Seed Production Technology in Horticultural Crops

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Hierarchical Seed Chain and Certification Standards

Seed multiplication follows a strict generational sequence to prevent genetic degeneration and maintain varietal authenticity.

  • Breeder Seed: 100% genetic purity, gold tag, direct plant breeder control.
  • Foundation Seed: 99.5% genetic purity, white tag, bulk multiplication source.
  • Certified Seed: 99.0% genetic purity, blue tag, distributed to farming community.
Concept 2

Pollination Control and Isolation Distances

Genetic purity in cross-pollinated horticultural crops depends entirely on spatial isolation or physical barriers.

  • Autogamous Crops: Require minimal isolation (50 m) due to cleistogamy.
  • Allogamous Crops: Require extensive isolation (up to 1000 m) to prevent foreign pollen introgression via insect vectors (entomophily).
Concept 3

Seed Maturation and Physiological Indices

Harvesting at the correct developmental stage ensures maximum enzymatic activity and germination potential.

  • Physiological Maturity: Maximum dry weight, black layer formation, high moisture content.
  • Harvest Maturity: Moisture level drops to safe levels for mechanical combining and threshing.
Concept 4

Seed Extraction and Drying Technologies

Post-harvest handling techniques vary based on fruit morphology to prevent mechanical injury and seed-borne disease transmission.

  • Wet Extraction: Fermentation or HCl treatment for solanaceous and cucurbitaceous pulpy fruits.
  • Dry Extraction: Threshing and winnowing for dry pods, capsules, and siliquas.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Nucleus Seed (Initial Breeder Stock)
The initial sample of seed of a newly developed variety maintained by the originating breeder.
Rouging (Off-type Elimination)
The rogueing or removal of undesirable, off-type, diseased, or wild plants from a seed crop field.
Isolation Distance (Spatial Separation)
The minimum physical distance maintained between two different varieties of the same crop to prevent cross-pollination.
Physiological Maturity (Maximum Dry Weight Stage)
The developmental stage when a seed attains maximum dry weight and nutrient accumulation from the mother plant.
Seed Vigor (Germination Potential & Endurance)
The sum total of properties that permit active, uniform emergence and seedling development under diverse environmental conditions.
Certified Seed (Commercial Planting Material)
The progeny of foundation seed produced according to certification standards with a blue tag.
Entomophily (Insect Pollination)
Pollination mediated predominantly by insects, requiring strict isolation distances in seed production fields.
Cleistogamy (Closed Flower Fertilization)
A type of automatic self-pollination where flowers remain closed, ensuring absolute genetic selfing.
Seed Health (Phytosanitary Status)
The presence or absence of disease-causing pathogens, insect pests, and weed seeds in a seed lot.
Seed Certification (Legally Enforced Quality Control)
A legally sanctioned system for quality control of seed multiplication and distribution.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Breeder Seed VS Foundation Seed

Breeder seed is handled directly by the plant breeder with 100% purity and a golden yellow tag, whereas foundation seed is the progeny of breeder seed with 99.5% purity and a white tag.

Physiological Maturity VS Harvest Maturity

Physiological maturity is when seed achieves maximum dry weight, while harvest maturity is when the seed moisture drops to a safe level for physical harvesting.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the genetic purity standard required for Foundation seed of self-pollinated vegetable crops?

Explanation: Foundation seed requires a minimum genetic purity of 99.5% as per national seed certification standards, whereas breeder seed is 100% pure and certified seed is typically 99.0% pure.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which color tag is attached to Breeder seed bags according to Indian Seed Certification standards?

Explanation: Breeder seed bags carry a Golden Yellow tag, Foundation seed carries a White tag, and Certified seed carries a Blue tag.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended isolation distance for Foundation seed production of cabbage (Brassica oleracea var. capitata)?

Explanation: Cabbage is a cross-pollinated crop belonging to the family Brassicaceae, requiring a foundation seed isolation distance of 1000 meters (and 500 meters for certified seed) to prevent cross-contamination.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical is commonly used for wet extraction and surface disinfection of tomato seeds to eliminate seed-borne viruses?

Explanation: Hydrochloric acid (HCl) is routinely used in wet extraction of tomato seeds to dissolve the gelatinous pulp sac surrounding the seed and inactivate plant viruses like Tobacco Mosaic Virus (TMV).

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

At what developmental stage should seeds be harvested for maximum seed quality and vigor?

Explanation: Physiological maturity is the optimum stage for seed harvest because the seed has attained its maximum dry weight, maximum germination capacity, and highest vigor.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of rouging in seed production plots?

Explanation: Rouging involves the systematic rogueing or removal of off-type plants, diseased individuals, and weeds to maintain genetic purity and prevent seed-borne pathogen transmission.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

According to Harrington's Rule of Thumb in seed storage, what happens to seed storage life when moisture content is reduced by 1%?

Explanation: Harrington's Rules state that for every 1% decrease in seed moisture or 5.6°C decrease in temperature, the storage life of the seed is doubled.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the isolation distance required for foundation seed production of self-pollinated tomato (Solanum lycopersicum)?

Explanation: Because tomato is an autogamous (self-pollinated) crop, the required isolation distance for foundation seed is only 50 meters (and 25 meters for certified seed).

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which seed class is the direct progeny of breeder seed?

Explanation: Foundation seed is the direct progeny of breeder seed and is produced under the supervision of authorized certification agencies.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the safe moisture content for long-term storage of orthodox vegetable seeds like onion and carrot?

Explanation: Orthodox seeds must be dried down to 7% to 9% moisture content (5% to 8% for oily seeds) to prevent respiration, insect damage, and fungal proliferation during storage.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which term describes seeds that can withstand desiccation and low-temperature storage without losing viability?

Explanation: Orthodox seeds tolerate drying to low moisture levels (5-10%) and freezing temperatures, making them suitable for long-term gene bank storage.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which vegetable crop is classified as an often cross-pollinated crop due to moderate insect activity?

Explanation: Okra (Abelmoschus esculentus) exhibits often cross-pollinated behavior driven by bees and other insects, requiring a foundation seed isolation distance of 200 meters.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the physical marker indicating physiological maturity in many cereal and vegetable seeds?

Explanation: Black layer formation at the base or placental attachment of seeds signals the cessation of nutrient flow, denoting physiological maturity.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which agency in India is legally empowered to enforce seed certification standards and issue certification tags?

Explanation: State Seed Certification Agencies (SSCAs) established under Section 8 of the Seeds Act (1966) are responsible for field inspections, seed testing, and tag issuance.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the minimum genetic purity standard required for Certified seed of major vegetable crops?

Explanation: Certified seed requires a minimum genetic purity of 99.0% (varying slightly by crop species) to ensure high yield and trueness-to-type for commercial growers.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which meteorological factor during seed crop maturity is most damaging to seed quality in vegetable seed production?

Explanation: Rainfall and high humidity during maturity cause pre-harvest sprouting, seed discoloration, fungal invasion (e.g., Alternaria blights), and rapid loss of seed viability.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is seed vigor?

Explanation: Seed vigor encompasses those seed properties that determine the potential for rapid, uniform emergence and development of normal seedlings across a wide range of field environments.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which horticultural crop seed extraction involves the fermentation method?

Explanation: Tomato seed extraction traditionally involves natural fermentation of pulp for 24-48 hours to dissolve the mucilaginous sheath surrounding the seed.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Under the Seeds Act of India (1966), which aspect is legally compulsory for notified seed varieties?

Explanation: Under the Seeds Act 1966, truthful labeling of seed containers regarding germination, physical purity, and variety name is mandatory for all notified varieties.

Calculate the physical seed rate (in kg/ha) for a vegetable crop if the recommended plant population is 50,000 plants/ha, the laboratory germination is 90%, physical purity is 95%, and expected field establishment success is 80%.

Solution & Rationale: Formula: Seed Rate = (Target Plant Population * 100 * 100 * 100) / (Germination % * Purity % * Field Establishment %). Assuming average seed weight (e.g., 4g/1000 seeds for calculation), detailed proportional seed adjustment accounts for sub-optimal field emergence. Calculation: 50,000 / (0.90 * 0.95 * 0.80) adjusted by test weight yields approximately 4.6 kg/ha depending on thousand seed weight.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following botanical crop pairs and their corresponding isolation distances for Foundation seed are correctly matched?

Solution & Rationale: Cabbage is a cole crop requiring 1000 meters for foundation seed. Tomato requires 50 meters, okra requires 200 meters, and onion requires 1000 meters.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGR Standard

What is the physiological consequence of exceeding drying temperatures above 43°C during mechanical seed drying of vegetable crops?

Solution & Rationale: High drying temperatures (>43°C) cause thermal coagulation of seed storage proteins and denaturation of enzymes, resulting in severe loss of germination capacity and vigor.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Why is rouging performed prior to flowering in cross-pollinated vegetable seed production fields?

Solution & Rationale: In cross-pollinated crops, off-type plants must be removed before flowering to prevent them from contributing undesirable pollen to the gene pool of true-to-type plants.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

In the context of seed testing, what does TZ test (Tetrazolium test) evaluate?

Solution & Rationale: The 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (TZ) test is a rapid biochemical test that evaluates seed viability by staining respiring living tissues red due to the formation of formazan.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Which legislation governs the protection of plant varieties and farmers' rights in India, complementing seed production acts?

Solution & Rationale: The Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights (PPV&FR) Act, 2001 provides an effective system for protecting plant varieties, farmers' rights, and plant breeders' rights in India.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary reason for maintaining a higher isolation distance for Foundation seed compared to Certified seed?

Solution & Rationale: Foundation seed is closer to the breeder seed stage and serves as the source for all subsequent multiplication; hence, stricter isolation distances ensure minimal genetic drift.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What happens to seed respiration rate and lipid peroxidation when seed moisture content increases from 8% to 16% in storage?

Solution & Rationale: High moisture content activates metabolic enzymes and microbial respiration, generating heat and free radicals that accelerate lipid peroxidation and rapid loss of viability.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which class of seed is multiplied to produce Foundation seed?

Solution & Rationale: Breeder seed is the direct source used to produce Foundation seed under the supervision of plant breeders and certification agencies.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the main advantage of wet seed extraction using hydrochloric acid in tomato seed production?

Solution & Rationale: Acid treatment efficiently removes the gelatinous pulp surrounding tomato seeds and destroys pathogenic viruses like TMV and bacterial pathogens present on the seed coat.
