Sowing and Transplanting in Horticultural Crops

Master the principles of sowing and transplanting in horticulture, focusing on seed viability, nursery management, seed rates, depth, and spacing geometry for optimal yield in UP TGT and PGT Agriculture examinations.

UP PGT Agriculture Tracker →

Principles of Seed Selection and Viability

Seed selection forms the foundational step in successful crop establishment. High-quality seeds must possess high genetic and physical purity, high germination percentage, and adequate vigor. Seed testing involves determining moisture content, which should ideally be between 8% and 12% for safe storage to prevent microbial infestation and preserve enzymatic activity.

  • Physical Purity Test: Determines the percentage of pure seeds free from inert matter, weed seeds, and other crop seeds.
  • Germination Test: Standard laboratory tests evaluate the proportion of seeds producing normal seedlings under optimal temperature and moisture conditions.
  • Tetrazolium Test (TZ Test): A biochemical test using 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride to assess seed viability rapidly by staining living tissues red.

Nursery Raising and Pro-Tray Technology

Vegetable crops such as tomato, brinjal, chilli, and cauliflower require rigorous nursery management due to their minute seed size and delicate early-stage growth. Modern horticulture relies heavily on soil-less media in plastic pro-trays (plug trays) to produce disease-free, vigorous seedlings.

    Media Composition: A sterile mixture of cocopeat, perlite, and vermiculite in a 3:1:1 ratio ensures optimal aeration, drainage, and water retention.
  • Seedling Hardening: Gradually reducing irrigation and exposing seedlings to direct sunlight 7 to 10 days before transplanting helps them withstand field stress.
  • Damping-Off Management: Caused by Pythium spp. and Rhizoctonia solani; managed by treating seeds with Trichoderma viride or Captan at 2 g/kg of seed.

Direct Sowing Methods and Machinery

Direct seeding (direct drilling) is practiced for crops with large seeds or those that poorly tolerate root disturbance, such as cucurbits, peas, beans, okra, and root vegetables (carrot and radish). Sowing methods vary based on topography, soil texture, and moisture availability.

  • Broadcasting: Manual scattering of seeds across the seedbed; used for small-seeded crops but results in uneven plant stands.
  • Line Sowing (Drilling): Sowing seeds in furrows at uniform depth and spacing using seed drills or manual ploughs, facilitating inter-cultivation and weeding.
  • Dibbling: Placing seeds at specified intervals in pre-marked holes or hills; highly efficient for hybrid seeds and large-seeded crops like cucurbits.

Transplanting Techniques and Establishment

Transplanting involves moving young seedlings from a nursery bed or pro-tray to the main field. Success depends on minimizing root shock, maintaining appropriate planting depth, and ensuring immediate post-transplanting irrigation.

  • Optimum Time: Late afternoon or cloudy days are preferred to reduce transpiration stress and wilting.
  • Root Dip Treatment: Dipping seedling roots in bio-fungicides like Pseudomonas fluorescens or azospirillum suspension protects against soil-borne pathogens.
  • Planting Depth: Seedlings should be planted at the same depth they grew in the nursery; deep planting causes stem rot, while shallow planting leads to lodging.

Seed Rate, Spacing Geometry, and Plant Population Calculations

Determining the precise seed rate and spacing geometry is critical for maximizing photosynthetic efficiency and avoiding intra-specific competition. Plant population per hectare is calculated using the formula: \(Plant Population = 10,000 m^2Spacing (Row-to-Row in m) × Plant-to-Plant in m)\).

  • Seed Rate Factors: Test weight (1000-seed weight), germination percentage, purity percentage, and desired plant population density.
  • Square Planting: Facilitates two-way inter-cultivation, mechanized weeding, and uniform light interception.
  • Rectangular Planting: Standard configuration for most vegetable crops, optimizing intra-row space for bush or vining habits.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The ideal sowing depth for most medium-sized vegetable seeds is 3 to 4 times the smallest diameter of the seed.
  • Seed viability is tested rapidly and accurately using 1% 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride solution.
  • Damping-off in solanaceous nurseries is primarily caused by Pythium debaryanum and Rhizoctonia solani.
  • Plug-tray nursery raising uses a sterile media mixture of cocopeat, perlite, and vermiculite in a 3:1:1 ratio.
  • Transplanting during late afternoon minimizes transplanting shock by reducing transpiration losses.
  • Seed rate formula incorporates physical purity, germination percentage, and desired plant population.
  • Square planting geometry allows bidirectional inter-cultivation and uniform light interception.
  • Cucurbitaceous crops are traditionally direct-seeded in raised beds or pits (basin planting).
  • Hardening off involves withholding nitrogen fertilizers and reducing irrigation 7-10 days prior to uprooting.
  • The tetrazolium test stains living, respiring embryonic tissues red due to the reduction of colorless tetrazolium salt into formazan.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing seed viability with seed vigor; viability refers to whether a seed is alive, while vigor determines its potential for rapid, uniform emergence under varied field conditions.
  • Assuming deep sowing improves moisture absorption; excessive depth exhausts seed food reserves before emergence.
  • Neglecting seed treatment in direct-seeded crops, leading to severe pre-emergence damping-off and poor plant stands.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Sowing and Transplanting in Horticultural Crops

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Seed Quality and Testing Parameters

Seed quality encompasses genetic purity, physical purity, germination capacity, and sanitary condition.

  • Tetrazolium Test: Biochemical assay evaluating seed viability by staining viable embryos red.
  • Germination Percentage: Proportion of seeds producing normal seedlings under controlled conditions.
Concept 2

Nursery Management and Media Preparation

Nursery raising provides a controlled environment for delicate seedlings before shifting to the main field.

  • Pro-Tray Technology: Utilizes plug trays filled with soil-less media (cocopeat, perlite, vermiculite) to ensure disease-free root balls.
  • Seedling Hardening: Physiological conditioning through moisture stress to improve field survival.
Concept 3

Sowing Methods and Depth Mechanics

Placement of seeds at correct depths and spacings ensures optimal seedling emergence and resource utilization.

  • Rule of Thumb: Sowing depth should be 3-4 times the seed diameter.
  • Dibbling: Precise placement of single seeds in pre-marked spots, ideal for hybrid cucurbits and solanaceous crops.
Concept 4

Transplanting Physiology and Agronomy

Transplanting bridges nursery production and main field establishment, requiring careful handling to prevent transplant shock.

  • Transplant Shock: Temporary cessation of growth due to root damage; mitigated by planting in late evening and root-dip treatments.
  • Root Dip: Application of bio-inoculants like Pseudomonas fluorescens to protect against soil pathogens.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Seed Viability (Capacity of seed to germinate and produce normal seedling)
The capability of a seed to germinate and develop into a normal seedling under favorable environmental conditions.
Seed Vigor (Sum total of seed properties determining potential for rapid uniform emergence)
Properties determining the potential for rapid, uniform emergence and development of normal seedlings under a wide range of field environments.
Tetrazolium Test (2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride viability assay)
A biochemical test measuring dehydrogenase enzyme activity in living embryos to determine seed viability.
Hardening Off (Physiological conditioning of nursery seedlings)
The practice of conditioning seedlings to withstand adverse environmental conditions by withholding water and nutrients.
Dibbling (Manual or mechanical hill dropping of seeds)
Placing seeds at fixed intervals in pre-made holes to economize expensive hybrid seeds and ensure uniform spacing.
Pro-Tray (Plastic plug tray for nursery raising)
Multi-celled plastic trays used for raising individual seedlings in soil-less media, facilitating clean root systems.
Damping-Off (Fungal seedling rot caused by Pythium and Rhizoctonia)
A fungal disease affecting germinating seeds and young seedlings, causing pre- or post-emergence collapse.
Transplant Shock (Post-transplantation stress and growth arrest)
Temporary wilting and cessation of growth following root disturbance during transplanting into the main field.
Seed Rate (Mass of seed required per unit area)
The quantity of seed (in kg or g) required to sow a unit area (hectare or acre) to achieve optimum plant population.
Plant Geometry (Arrangement of plants in the field (Row x Plant spacing))
The spatial arrangement of plants in a field designed to optimize interception of solar radiation and root foraging.
Pure Live Seed (PLS) (Percentage of viable pure seed)
Product of percentage purity and percentage germination divided by 100, indicating effective seed quality.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Tetrazolium test uses 1% 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride solution.
  • Cocopeat, perlite, and vermiculite are mixed in a 3:1:1 ratio for pro-tray nursery media.
  • Damping-off is controlled using bio-fungicide seed treatments like Trichoderma viride.
  • Plant population formula: 10,000 / (Row spacing x Plant spacing in meters).

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Seed selection and viability testing ensure high germination and vigorous crop establishment.
  • Modern horticulture utilizes pro-trays with soil-less media for disease-free nursery raising.
  • Sowing depth should generally be 3 to 4 times the seed diameter to guarantee adequate moisture and oxygen.
  • Transplanting should be conducted during late afternoon or cloudy weather to minimize transplant shock.
  • Seed rate calculations account for purity, germination percentage, and test weight.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Seed Viability VS Seed Vigor

Viability indicates if the seed is alive, whereas vigor indicates the speed and uniformity of seedling emergence under stress.

Broadcasting VS Dibbling

Broadcasting scatters seeds randomly with high seed waste, while dibbling places individual seeds precisely at specified grid intervals.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical reagent is universally used in the tetrazolium test to determine seed viability?

Explanation: 2,3,5-Triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (TZ) is reduced by respiring tissues in living embryos, forming a red-colored formazan compound, indicating seed viability.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard volume ratio of cocopeat, perlite, and vermiculite used in commercial pro-tray nursery media?

Explanation: The standard soil-less media mixture for pro-tray nursery raising is 3 parts cocopeat, 1 part perlite, and 1 part vermiculite to ensure optimal water retention and aeration.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the general rule of thumb for determining the optimum sowing depth of a seed?

Explanation: The general agronomic rule is to sow seeds at a depth of 3 to 4 times their smallest diameter to ensure adequate moisture contact without exhausting food reserves before emergence.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which fungal pathogens are primarily responsible for damping-off disease in vegetable nurseries?

Explanation: Damping-off in solanaceous and cucurbitaceous nurseries is chiefly caused by soil-borne fungi Pythium species and Rhizoctonia solani, which attack seedling hypocotyls.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of seedling hardening off before transplanting?

Explanation: Hardening off hardens cell walls and increases dry matter accumulation, enabling seedlings to withstand wind, sun, and moisture stress in the main field.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Calculate the plant population per hectare for a vegetable crop spaced at 50 cm x 50 cm.

Explanation: Plant population = 10,000 / (0.5 x 0.5) = 10,000 / 0.25 = 40,000 plants per hectare.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Why is late afternoon preferred for transplanting vegetable seedlings?

Explanation: Transplanting in the late afternoon provides the entire night for seedlings to recover from transplant shock and establish root contact with soil moisture before intense daytime sunlight.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following crops is best suited for direct dibbling rather than nursery transplanting?

Explanation: Cucurbits possess fragile root systems and poor regenerative capacity after root disturbance, making direct seeding or hill planting preferable over transplanting.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does Pure Live Seed (PLS) percentage mathematically represent?

Explanation: PLS indicates the actual percentage of pure, viable seeds in a lot, calculated as (Physical Purity % × Germination %) / 100.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which root-dip treatment is commonly recommended for solanaceous vegetable seedlings to protect against bacterial wilt?

Explanation: Dipping seedling roots in a suspension of Pseudomonas fluorescens before transplanting provides biological protection against soil-borne bacterial and fungal pathogens.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary disadvantage of the broadcasting method of sowing?

Explanation: Broadcasting leads to clumped or sparse patches, making mechanical weeding and intercultural operations difficult.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which physiological parameter distinguishes seed vigor from seed viability?

Explanation: Seed vigor reflects the energy and performance capacity of a seed lot, dictating rapid and uniform emergence even under suboptimal field conditions.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is square planting geometry primarily advantageous for?

Explanation: Square planting ensures equal foraging area for root and shoot systems in all directions and permits mechanical weeding in two perpendicular directions.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following describes 'dibbling'?

Explanation: Dibbling involves manual or mechanical placement of seeds at predetermined points, saving seed material and ensuring exact spacing.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the ideal moisture content for safe long-term seed storage in most vegetable crops?

Explanation: Keeping seed moisture between 8% and 12% inhibits fungal growth, insect activity, and enzymatic respiration, thus preserving high viability during storage.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main purpose of pelleting small seeds (like onion or carrot) before sowing?

Explanation: Pelleting enlarges and regularizes minute seeds, making them easier to handle and sow precisely using mechanical seed drills.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which symptom characterizes pre-emergence damping-off in a nursery bed?

Explanation: Pre-emergence damping-off involves the decay of seeds and hypocotyls before emergence, resulting in missing patches in the nursery bed.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In calculating seed rate, if the physical purity is 95% and germination is 80%, what is the Pure Live Seed (PLS) factor?

Explanation: PLS = (95 × 80) / 100 = 7600 / 100 = 76.0%.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended corrective action if nursery seedlings become 'leggy' due to over-crowding and excess shade?

Explanation: Leggy seedlings result from excessive shade and moisture. Corrective action includes reducing water, improving light penetration, and thinning.

If a vegetable crop requires a plant population of 50,000 plants per hectare and the row-to-row spacing is 50 cm, what should be the plant-to-plant spacing?

Solution & Rationale: Plant Population = 10,000 / (Row Spacing in m × Plant Spacing in m). 50,000 = 10,000 / (0.5 × Plant Spacing). Plant Spacing = 10,000 / (50,000 × 0.5) = 10,000 / 25,000 = 0.4 m or 40 cm? Wait: 10,000 / (0.5 * x) = 50000 => 0.5 * x = 10000 / 50000 = 0.2 m = 20 cm.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Which test measures membrane integrity and seed vigor by quantifying electrolytes leaked into water by seeds?

Solution & Rationale: The electrical conductivity test measures solute leakage from seeds soaked in water; higher leakage indicates deteriorated cell membranes and low vigor.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the recommended practice for hardening nursery seedlings 7 to 10 days prior to transplanting?

Solution & Rationale: Withholding nitrogen and reducing watering slows succulent vegetative growth, thickens cell walls, and increases carbohydrate accumulation for stress tolerance.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

In seed technology, what does 'Accelerated Aging Test' evaluate?

Solution & Rationale: The accelerated aging test exposes seeds to high temperature (41-45°C) and near 100% relative humidity to artificially stress them, evaluating their storability and vigor.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which sowing method is most appropriate for small-seeded vegetable crops like onion and carrot when high precision and uniform stand are required?

Solution & Rationale: Line sowing ensures uniform seed distribution, precise depth control, and facilitates weeding and intercultural operations.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the biological mechanism behind the positive reaction in the tetrazolium test?

Solution & Rationale: Living cells contain active dehydrogenase enzymes during respiration that hydrogenate the colorless tetrazolium solution into insoluble red formazan.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following factors does NOT directly influence the optimum seed rate of a crop?

Solution & Rationale: Market price dictates economic returns but has no direct agronomic bearing on biological seed rate calculations.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary risk associated with sowing seeds deeper than their optimum agronomic depth?

Solution & Rationale: Excessive depth forces the seedling to expend its stored reserves before reaching the soil surface, resulting in weak emergence or total failure.
