Introduction to Pomology and Orchard Establishment

Pomology is the branch of horticulture that deals with the scientific study and cultivation of fruit crops. Orchard establishment is a long-term capital-intensive venture requiring meticulous planning regarding site selection, soil testing, climatic adaptation, and layout design.

  • Site Selection: Proximity to markets, perennial water source, and well-drained, fertile soil with a pH range of 6.5 to 7.5 are mandatory for most fruit trees.
  • Orchard Layout Systems: Square system (simplest and easiest for interculture), Rectangular system, Quincunx system (temporary filler tree in the center, increasing plant population by 78-100%), Hexagonal system (accommodates 15% more trees than square system), and Contour system for hilly terrains.
  • Windbreaks and Shelterbelts: Planting tall, dense evergreen trees (e.g., Casuarina equisetifolia or Eucalyptus tereticornis) on the windward side to protect tender fruit crops from mechanical damage and high evapo-transpiration.

Botanical Taxonomy and Propagation in Fruit Crops

Understanding botanical families, chromosome numbers, and commercial propagation methods is fundamental for answering competitive examination questions in horticulture.

  • Mango (Mangifera indica): Family Anacardiaceae, 2n = 40. Commercial propagation is predominantly done through veneer grafting and epicotyl (stone) grafting.
  • Banana (Musa paradisiaca): Family Musaceae, 2n = 33 (triploid). Commercial propagation is achieved using sword suckers weighing 500-750g or tissue culture-raised plants.
  • Citrus (Citrus spp.): Family Rutaceae, 2n = 18. Propagated via T-budding or chip budding on rootstocks like trifoliate orange (Poncirus trifoliata) or rough lemon (Citrus jambhiri).
  • Guava (Psidium guajava): Family Myrtaceae, 2n = 22. Commercial propagation relies on air layering (gootee) and wedge grafting.

Olericulture: Classification, Nursery Management, and Sowing Systems

Olericulture encompasses the science of vegetable growing, dealing with herbaceous plants cultivated for edible parts such as roots, stems, leaves, flowers, or fruits. Nursery raising is critical for solanaceous and cruciferous crops.

  • Botanical Classification: Solanaceae (Solanum lycopersicum, Solanum melongena, Capsicum annuum), Cucurbitaceae (Cucumis sativus, Lagenaria siceraria), Cruciferae / Brassicaceae (Brassica oleracea var. capitata).
  • Nursery Bed Preparation: Raised beds of 10-15 cm height, 1 m width, and convenient length are prepared to prevent water stagnation and damping-off disease. Soil solarization using transparent polyethylene sheets is recommended for pathogen eradication.
  • Hardening-Off: Gradually reducing irrigation and withholding shade 7-10 days prior to transplanting to enable seedlings to withstand environmental stress in the main field.

Nutrient Management, Irrigation, and Physiological Disorders

Precise fertilizer application and moisture management dictate fruit quality, yield, and marketability, while physiological disorders often arise from nutritional or environmental imbalances.

  • Nutrient Management: Heavy feeders like banana require up to 200g N, 60g P2O5, and 300g K2O per mat per year. Micronutrient sprays (e.g., Borax at 0.5% and Zinc sulfate at 0.5%) prevent fruit cracking and little leaf in fruit crops.
  • Irrigation Scheduling: Micro-irrigation systems (drip and sprinkler) optimize water use efficiency. Critical stages include flowering, fruit setting, and rapid fruit development stages.
  • Physiological Disorders: Spongy tissue in Mango (internal breakdown due to high mesocarp temperature); Blossom-end rot in Tomato (Calcium deficiency coupled with irregular irrigation); Hen and Chicken disorder in Grapes (Boron deficiency leading to small, seedless berries mixed with normal ones).

Plant Protection: Major Pests, Diseases, and Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

Horticultural crops are susceptible to various insect pests, fungal, bacterial, and viral pathogens that cause severe economic losses if not managed proactively.

  • Mango Hopper (Idioscopus spp.): Sucks sap from tender inflorescences and secretes honeydew, leading to sooty mold. Managed by spraying imidacloprid 17.8% SL @ 0.3 ml/L at the panicle emergence stage.
  • Fruit Fly (Bactrocera dorsalis): Attacks guava, mango, and cucurbits, causing oviposition punctures and fruit rotting. Managed using methyl eugenol pheromone traps @ 10-15 traps/hectare and bait sprays.
  • Damping-off in Vegetable Nurseries: Caused by Pythium spp. and Rhizoctonia solani. Seed treatment with Trichoderma viride @ 4-5 g/kg seed or captan @ 2.5 g/kg seed effectively controls the pathogen.
  • Yellow Vein Mosaic Virus (YVMV) in Okra: Transmitted by whitefly (Bemisia tabaci). Controlled by vector management using yellow sticky traps and spraying dimethoate 30 EC @ 1.5 ml/L.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The national fruit of India is Mango (Mangifera indica), belonging to the family Anacardiaceae with chromosome number 2n = 40.
  • Papaya (Carica papaya) is strictly propagated through seeds, and being dioecious, requires male trees to be planted at 10% ratio for pollination.
  • Banana is a monocarpic, triploid (2n = 33) fruit crop propagated commercially via sword suckers weighing 500-750 grams.
  • The edible part of Litchi (Litchi chinensis) is the succulent aril, which develops from the outer integument.
  • Guava variety 'Allahabad Safeda' is renowned for its high ascorbic acid content and dessert quality.
  • Tomato fruit color red is due to the pigment Lycopene, which develops best at temperatures between 21°C and 24°C.
  • Cauliflower curd discoloration is prevented by buttoning prevention (avoiding nitrogen starvation and delayed transplanting) and browning prevention (applying Borax).
  • Cucurbits are monoecious plants; application of Ethephon (250 ppm) at the 2-4 leaf stage increases the number of female flowers and enhances early yield.
  • Citrus tristeza virus (CTV) is primarily transmitted by the brown citrus aphid (Toxoptera citricida).
  • Pusa Sawani is a popular variety of Okra (Abelmoschus esculentus) bred for resistance against Yellow Vein Mosaic Virus.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the edible portion of Apple (fleshy thalamus) with that of Mango (mesocarp). Remember, apple is a pome where the thalamus becomes fleshy, whereas mango is a drupe with a fleshy mesocarp.
  • Assuming Papaya can be successfully propagated through stem cuttings commercially; however, viral transmission and poor root systems make seed propagation mandatory.
  • Confusing Buttoning in Cauliflower (caused by nitrogen deficiency) with Whiptail (caused by molybdenum deficiency).

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Pomology and Olericulture: Comprehensive Agronomic Study Material for UP TGT/PGT Agriculture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Orchard Layout and Planting Systems

Orchard systems dictate the spatial arrangement of trees to maximize sunlight interception and ease interculture operations.

  • Square System: Trees planted at four corners of a square; simple and allows cross-cultivation.
  • Quincunx System: Square system with an additional filler tree in the center; increases plant density by 78-100% during early years.
Concept 2

Botanical Families and Edible Parts

Taxonomic identification is a cornerstone of horticultural examinations.

  • Rutaceae: Citrus family; modified berry called hesperidium.
  • Musaceae: Banana family; edible part is the mesocarp and endocarp.
Concept 3

Nursery Techniques in Olericulture

Raising healthy vegetable seedlings ensures strong field establishment and higher yields.

  • Damping-off: Major fungal nursery disease controlled by seed dressing with Trichoderma viride.
  • Hardening-off: Conditioning seedlings to withstand environmental shock prior to transplanting.
Concept 4

Physiological Disorders and Nutritional Management

Nutrient imbalances trigger severe morphological abnormalities in fruits and vegetables.

  • Blossom-End Rot: Calcium deficiency in tomato fruit tissue.
  • Internal Necrosis: Boron deficiency in aonla fruits.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Pomology (Pomology (Latin 'pomum' + Greek 'logia'))
The branch of science that deals with the cultivation, production, and scientific study of fruit crops.
Olericulture (Olericulture)
The science of vegetable growing, dealing with the culture of non-woody herbaceous plants.
Hesperidium (Hesperidium Berry)
A specialized berry characteristic of the Rutaceae family (Citrus) with a leathery rind and juice sacs.
Drupe (Drupe (Stone Fruit))
A fleshy fruit containing a hard stone enclosing a single seed, e.g., Mango, Peach, Plum.
Gootee (Air Layering)
A vegetative propagation method where roots are induced on an aerial stem wrapped with moist rooting medium.
Clonal Rootstock (Clonal Rootstock)
A rootstock propagated vegetatively to confer disease resistance, dwarfing, or soil adaptability to the scion.
Parthenocarpy (Parthenocarpic Development)
The development of fruit without prior fertilization, resulting in seedless fruits like commercial banana.
Climacteric Fruit (Climacteric Fruit)
Fruits that exhibit a surge in respiration and ethylene production during ripening, e.g., Mango, Banana, Guava.
Non-climacteric Fruit (Non-climacteric Fruit)
Fruits that do not show a sudden rise in respiration after harvest, e.g., Citrus, Grape, Pomegranate.
Sucker (Vegetative Sucker)
A shoot arising from the underground rhizome or stem base, used extensively in banana propagation.
Vector (Insect Vector)
An organism, typically an insect, that transmits plant pathogens from diseased to healthy plants.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Climacteric Fruit VS Non-climacteric Fruit

Climacteric fruits continue to ripen after harvest with a respiration burst (Mango, Banana), whereas non-climacteric fruits do not ripen further off the plant (Citrus, Grapes).

Buttoning in Cauliflower VS Whiptail in Cauliflower

Buttoning is the formation of small curds due to nitrogen deficiency, while Whiptail is strap-like leaf development caused by molybdenum deficiency.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which system of orchard layout accommodates approximately 15% more trees compared to the square system?

Explanation: The hexagonal (or triangular) system allows 15% more trees per unit area than the square system because trees are placed at the vertices of equilateral triangles.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the commercial method of vegetative propagation for Mango (Mangifera indica) in India?

Explanation: Veneer grafting and epicotyl (stone) grafting are the standard commercial methods for propagating mango in India due to high success rates and uniformity.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The edible portion of Litchi (Litchi chinensis) is botanically known as:

Explanation: In litchi, the edible fleshy part is the aril, which grows outward from the base of the seed and covers it.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Blossom-end rot in Tomato is primarily caused due to the deficiency of:

Explanation: Blossom-end rot is a physiological disorder caused by local calcium deficiency in the distal end of the fruit, often exacerbated by moisture stress.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a climacteric fruit?

Explanation: Banana is a classic climacteric fruit that can be harvested mature-green and ripened artificially using ethylene.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosome number (2n) of cultivated Banana (Musa paradisiaca)?

Explanation: Most edible bananas are triploids (3n = 33), having three sets of chromosomes derived from wild diploid progenitors.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Whiptail disorder in Cauliflower is caused by the deficiency of:

Explanation: Whiptail in cauliflower is caused by Molybdenum deficiency, resulting in narrow, strap-like leaves and impaired curd formation.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect vector is responsible for transmitting Citrus Tristeza Virus (CTV)?

Explanation: The brown citrus aphid (Toxoptera citricida) is the most efficient vector transmitting the closterovirus causing tristeza in citrus.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Pusa Sawani variety of Okra is specifically bred for resistance against which disease?

Explanation: Pusa Sawani was historically bred for resistance against Yellow Vein Mosaic Virus (YVMV), though resistance breakdown has occurred over time.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In the Quincunx system of orchard planting, what is the purpose of the central filler tree?

Explanation: The central filler tree (such as papaya or peach in a mango orchard) utilizes vacant central space and yields income until permanent trees occupy the canopy.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the family of Papaya (Carica papaya)?

Explanation: Papaya belongs to the family Caricaceae and is grown commercially for papain extraction and fresh fruit consumption.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which plant growth regulator is commonly applied to induce female flowers in cucurbits?

Explanation: Ethephon releases ethylene, which promotes femaleness and reduces node number for the first female flower in monoecious cucurbits.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The botanical name of Guava is:

Explanation: Guava is scientifically known as Psidium guajava, belonging to the Myrtaceae family.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Spongy tissue is a major physiological disorder prevalent in which fruit crop?

Explanation: Spongy tissue is a severe internal ripening disorder in Alphonso mangoes caused by solar heat pre-harvest, preventing normal starch hydrolysis.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which propagation unit is standard for commercial banana cultivation?

Explanation: Sword suckers weighing 500-750g and certified disease-free tissue culture plantlets are the recommended planting material for banana.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of 'hardening-off' vegetable nursery seedlings?

Explanation: Hardening-off thickens cuticle layers and reduces succulent growth, enabling seedlings to survive field conditions such as wind, sun, and drought.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which fruit crop is propagated commercially through air layering (gootee)?

Explanation: Guava responds exceptionally well to air layering (gootee), making it a popular commercial multiplication technique in addition to wedge grafting.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What pigment is responsible for the yellow color in ripe Papaya?

Explanation: Carotenoids, particularly cryptoxanthin and violaxanthin, impart the characteristic yellow-orange coloration to ripe papaya pulp.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following vegetables is a cole crop belonging to Brassicaceae?

Explanation: Cole crops include cabbage, cauliflower, broccoli, and knol-khol, all belonging to the family Brassicaceae (Brassica oleracea group).

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended seed rate for tomato per hectare when raising seedlings in a nursery?

Explanation: A seed rate of 400 to 500 grams is adequate to raise sufficient seedlings for planting one hectare of indeterminate or determinate tomato crops.

Test Question 1 of 10 TGT Standard

Calculate or determine the plant population per hectare for a square system of orchard layout with a spacing of 10 m × 10 m:

Solution & Rationale: Area of 1 hectare = 10,000 m^2. Spacing = 10 m × 10 m = 100 m^2. Plant population = 10,000 / 100 = 100 trees.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Which fruit crop is exceptionally sensitive to waterlogging and readily succumbs to collar rot caused by Phytophthora spp.?

Solution & Rationale: Papaya has a soft, succulent stem and shallow root system highly susceptible to root rot and collar rot under waterlogged soil conditions.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

The phenomenon of apomixis in mango results in:

Solution & Rationale: Polyembryonic mango varieties produce nucellar seedlings through apomixis, which are genetically uniform and true-to-type, mimicking vegetative propagation.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Which chemical is widely recommended for artificial ripening of climacteric fruits like mango and banana post-harvest?

Solution & Rationale: Ethephon is legally approved and safe for releasing ethylene gas to induce uniform ripening, whereas calcium carbide is banned due to toxic arsenic/phosphorus impurities.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary causal organism of damping-off in vegetable nurseries?

Solution & Rationale: Soil-borne fungi Pythium spp., Phytophthora spp., and Rhizoctonia solani attack tender hypocotyls at the soil line, causing seedling collapse.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

In citrus propagation, rootstocks are selected primarily to confer resistance against:

Solution & Rationale: Rootstocks like trifoliate orange or rough lemon provide tolerance or resistance against soil-borne pathogens like Phytophthora and burrowing nematodes.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Which vegetable crop is categorized as a day-neutral plant regarding flowering response?

Solution & Rationale: Tomato is day-neutral, meaning its flowering is independent of day length, whereas spinach is long-day and certain brassicas are cold-requiring biennial types.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the optimum temperature range for red color development (lycopene synthesis) in Tomato fruits?

Solution & Rationale: Lycopene synthesis is optimal between 21°C and 24°C. Temperatures above 30°C inhibit lycopene formation, resulting in yellow or orange fruit shoulders (carotene accumulation).

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Which training system is predominantly practiced in commercial grape (Vitis vinifera) cultivation for ease of pruning and maximum sunlight exposure?

Solution & Rationale: The bower (or pandal) training system is widely used for grapes and cucurbitaceous vegetables to support heavy vine growth and provide excellent canopy ventilation.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the function of methyl eugenol traps in fruit crop IPM?

Solution & Rationale: Methyl eugenol is a powerful male sex pheromone mimic that attracts male fruit flies, effectively disrupting mating and suppressing populations.
