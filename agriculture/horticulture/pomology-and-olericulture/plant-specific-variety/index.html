Botanical Taxonomy and Variety Classification in Horticultural Crops

Understanding plant-specific varieties requires a rigorous foundation in botanical taxonomy, ploidy levels, center of origin, and mode of pollination. Horticultural crops span across multiple families such as Solanaceae, Cucurbitaceae, Rutaceae, and Anacardiaceae.

  • Mango (Mangifera indica): Family Anacardiaceae, chromosome number 2n = 40. Indo-Chinese vs. South Indian types exhibit distinct polyembryony.
  • Citrus (Citrus spp.): Family Rutaceae, chromosome number 2n = 18. Highly prone to nucellar embryony yielding true-to-type seedlings.
  • Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum): Family Solanaceae, chromosome number 2n = 24. Self-pollinated crop with high degree of autogamy.

Agro-Climatic Requirements, Soil Preferences, and Propagation Protocols

Horticultural crop success is fundamentally governed by temperature thresholds, photoperiodic sensitivity, soil drainage, and specific vegetative propagation techniques.

  • Soil Health: Most fruit trees and vegetable crops thrive in well-drained, sandy loam to clay loam soils with a pH range of 6.0 to 7.5. Waterlogging induces root rot and nutrient lockout.
  • Propagation Methods: Wedge grafting and stone grafting are standard for mango; tongue and whip grafting for temperate pome fruits; softwood grafting for guava; and true-to-type seed selection or tissue culture for banana (Musa spp.).
  • Temperature Regulates Flowering: Chilling hours are mandatory for temperate fruit crops like apple (Malus domestica) requiring 800 to 1200 hours below 7°C.

Nutrient Management, Fertilization, and Irrigation Scheduling

Precision nutrient application and critical irrigation stages dictate optimal yield and quality parameters in intensive horticultural production systems.

  • Macronutrients: High-feeding vegetable crops like tomato and potato require intensive N:P:K basal and top-dressing schedules. Fruit trees require balanced annual applications based on canopy age and soil testing.
  • Micronutrient Deficiencies: Zinc deficiency causes little leaf in mango and citrus; boron deficiency causes cracking in tomato and pomegranate; molybdenum deficiency causes whip-tail in cauliflower.
  • Critical Irrigation Stages: Moisture stress during flowering and fruit set in fruit trees and tuber bulking in root crops leads to severe flower drop and yield reduction.

Pest, Disease, and Physiological Disorder Management

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) and scientific diagnostic protocols are essential for mitigating biotic and abiotic stresses in horticultural ecosystems.

  • Major Insect Pests: Fruit borer (Helicoverpa armigera) in tomato, fruit fly (Bactrocera dorsalis) in mango and cucurbits, and shoot and fruit borer (Leucinodes orbonalis) in brinjal.
  • Major Diseases: Powdery mildew (Oidium mangiferae) in mango, bacterial wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum) in solanaceous vegetables, and citrus canker (Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. citri).
  • Physiological Disorders: Spongy tissue in 'Alphonso' mango, blossom end rot in tomato due to calcium deficiency, and black heart in potato due to sub-optimal oxygen diffusion during storage.

High-Yielding Varieties, Hybrids, and Post-Harvest Technology

Commercial viability relies on adopting state-certified high-yielding cultivars, hybrids, and adhering to strict harvesting indices and cold-chain storage parameters.

  • Notable Fruit Varieties: 'Amrapali' (Dashehari x Neelum) planted at ultra-high density (1600 plants/ha); 'Mallika' (Neelum x Dashehari); 'Pusa Arunima' and 'Pusa Surya' in mango.
  • Notable Vegetable Varieties: 'Pusa Ruby', 'Pusa Rohini', and 'Arka Vikas' in tomato; 'Pusa Kranti' in brinjal; 'Pusa Sadabahar' in chilli.
  • Harvesting & Storage: Optimum harvest maturity is determined by Total Soluble Solids (TSS) to acid ratio, firmness, and skin color transition. Cold storage extends shelf-life at 0°C to 4°C with 85-90% relative humidity.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Amrapali mango hybrid is bred from Dashehari x Neelum with a planting geometry of 2.5m x 2.5m accommodating 1600 plants per hectare.
  • Spongy tissue in Alphonso mango is a physiological disorder caused by excessive internal heat from solar radiation during fruit development.
  • Botryodiplodia rot and powdery mildew (Oidium mangiferae) are major fungal threats managed effectively by wettable sulfur sprays.
  • Blossom End Rot (BER) in tomato is caused by localized calcium deficiency combined with irregular moisture fluctuations.
  • Black heart of potato is an abiotic storage disorder resulting from insufficient oxygen diffusion inside poorly ventilated piles or warehouses.
  • Citrus canker is caused by the bacterium Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. citri and is spread by the citrus leaf miner (Phyllocnistis citrella).
  • Botanical name of onion is Allium cepa, family Alliaceae, and its pungent flavor is attributed to allyl propyl disulphide.
  • Pusa Sadabahar is a renowned virus-resistant variety of chilli developed by IARI, New Delhi.
  • Wedge grafting (Inarching / Epicotyl grafting) is the commercially recommended propagation technique for rapid mango nursery production.
  • Clonal selection is the primary breeding method utilized in vegetatively propagated perennial fruit crops.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the parentage of Amrapali and Mallika. Accurate fact: Amrapali = Dashehari x Neelum; Mallika = Neelum x Dashehari.
  • Attributing spongy tissue to a fungal pathogen. Accurate fact: Spongy tissue is a physiological disorder driven by internal fruit pulp temperature.
  • Confusing alternate bearing with regular bearing cultivars. Accurate fact: Amrapali is a regular bearer, whereas Dashehari is a pronounced alternate bearer.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Plant Specific Variety in Horticulture, Pomology, and Olericulture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Taxonomic Nomenclature and Ploidy in Horticultural Species

All commercial horticultural species possess distinct botanical taxonomy, somatic chromosome numbers, and floral biology.

  • Mango Taxonomy: Mangifera indica, 2n = 40, family Anacardiaceae.
  • Tomato Taxonomy: Solanum lycopersicum, 2n = 24, family Solanaceae.
Concept 2

High-Density Planting (HDP) and Ultra-HDP Systems

Modern orcharding relies on high-density planting to maximize early yields and input use efficiency per unit area.

  • Amrapali Geometry: Planted at 2.5m x 2.5m (1600 plants/ha).
  • Meadow Orchard: Ultra-high density system in guava or apple achieving over 5000 plants/ha.
Concept 3

Physiological Disorders and Nutritional Management

Abiotic stresses cause profound economic losses if corrective foliar sprays and irrigation management are neglected.

  • Blossom End Rot: Corrected by timely calcium nitrate sprays.
  • Mango Malformation: Managed via pruning infected shoots and application of naphthalene acetic acid (NAA).
Concept 4

Propagation Protocols and Nursery Standards

True-to-type establishment requires vegetative propagation using certified rootstocks and scion combinations.

  • Stone Grafting: Popular in coastal and humid regions for mango during early monsoon.
  • Budding Techniques: Shield (T) budding is standard in citrus and rose.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Polyembryony (Polyembryony (Multiple Embryos))
The occurrence of more than one embryo in a seed, common in polyembryonic mango and citrus cultivars yielding nucellar seedlings.
Alternate Bearing (Biennial Bearing)
A physiological phenomenon where a fruit tree yields a heavy crop in one year (ON year) and little to no crop the following year (OFF year).
Spongy Tissue (Internal Pulp Breakdown)
A physiological disorder in Alphonso mango characterized by a spongy, yellowish patch of mesocarp near the stone.
High Density Planting (HDP / Mehanized Orcharding)
Accommodating a significantly higher number of plants per unit area than conventional spacing to increase early economic returns.
Blossom End Rot (Calcium Deficiency Disorder)
Water-soaked sunken black lesions at the distal end of tomato and bell pepper fruits caused by localized calcium deficiency.
Clonal Selection (Asexual Progeny Selection)
Selecting superior individual plants from a vegetatively propagated population to establish a uniform true-to-type cultivar.
Chilling Hours (Thermal Requirement for Dormancy Break)
The cumulative number of hours below 7°C required by temperate fruit trees to break winter dormancy and initiate floral bud break.
Fruit Drop (Abscission Layer Formation)
Premature shedding of flowers and young fruits triggered by hormonal imbalance, moisture stress, or nutritional deficiency.
Parthenocarpy (Stenospermocarpy / Seedless Fruit Development)
Development of fruit without prior fertilization, resulting in seedless fruits such as in commercial banana and seedless grapes.
Curing (Post-Harvest Moisture Reduction)
The process of drying root crops and bulbs (like onion and potato) to heal wounds, harden skin, and prevent microbial decay.
TSS (Total Soluble Solids)
Measurement of sugar content and dissolved solutes in fruit juice expressed in degrees Brix (°Bx) using a refractometer.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Amrapali VS Mallika

Amrapali is a regular bearer with dwarf stature suitable for HDP (Dashehari x Neelum), while Mallika is a vigorous tree with late maturity (Neelum x Dashehari).

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the exact parental cross responsible for developing the 'Amrapali' mango hybrid?

Explanation: Amrapali is a hybrid developed from the cross between Dashehari (female) and Neelum (male) at IARI, New Delhi, released in 1978.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended high-density planting (HDP) spacing for 'Amrapali' mango orchards?

Explanation: Amrapali's dwarf canopy allows a spacing of 2.5m x 2.5m, accommodating 1600 plants per hectare, maximizing early yield.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which physiological disorder in Alphonso mango is characterized by internal yellowish spongy pulp near the stone?

Explanation: Spongy tissue is an internal physiological disorder unique to Alphonso mango caused by solar heat radiation during fruit development.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary cause of Blossom End Rot (BER) in tomato fruits?

Explanation: Blossom End Rot is caused by localized calcium deficiency in the distal end of the fruit, exacerbated by irregular watering schedules.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which bacterium is the causal organism of Citrus Canker?

Explanation: Citrus canker is a contagious bacterial disease caused by Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. citri, disseminated by wind-driven rain and leaf miners.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the somatic chromosome number (2n) of cultivated mango (Mangifera indica)?

Explanation: Cultivated mango is diploid with 2n = 40 chromosomes.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which vegetable variety is known as 'Pusa Sadabahar' and possesses resistance to Leaf Curl Virus?

Explanation: Pusa Sadabahar is a popular virus-resistant chilli variety developed by IARI, New Delhi.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary vector transmitting Tomato Leaf Curl Virus (ToLCV)?

Explanation: The whitefly Bemisia tabaci is the exclusive vector transmitting Tomato Leaf Curl Virus in tropical and subtropical regions.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which propagation method is most commonly utilized for rapid commercial mango nursery production in North India?

Explanation: Wedge grafting and stone grafting have largely replaced inarching due to higher success rates, ease of handling, and vigorous growth.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What causes 'Black Heart' disorder in stored potato tubers?

Explanation: Black heart is a physiological disorder caused by lack of oxygen and accumulation of carbon dioxide in poorly ventilated storage structures.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which fruit crop requires chilling hours below 7°C for successful flower bud differentiation?

Explanation: Apple is a temperate fruit crop requiring 800 to 1200 chilling hours to break winter rest and initiate normal flowering.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the parentage of the mango hybrid 'Mallika'?

Explanation: Mallika is produced from the cross between Neelum (female) and Dashehari (male), resulting in large-sized fruits with superior keeping quality.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which nutrient deficiency causes 'Little Leaf' disorder in mango and citrus crops?

Explanation: Zinc deficiency leads to shortened internodes and small, narrow, rosetted leaves, commonly known as little leaf disorder.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the botanical family of tomato (Solanum lycopersicum)?

Explanation: Tomato belongs to the family Solanaceae, characterized by gamopetalous corolla and alternate leaves.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical is commonly used as a plant growth regulator to induce flowering and prevent fruit drop in mango?

Explanation: Auxins like NAA or low concentrations of 2,4-D are sprayed to prevent premature flower and fruit drop by inhibiting abscission layer formation.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of 'curing' in onion and garlic bulbs prior to storage?

Explanation: Curing reduces moisture content in outer scales, tightens the neck, and heals physical wounds to prevent post-harvest microbial decay.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following mango varieties exhibits pronounced alternate bearing behavior?

Explanation: Dashehari is notorious for alternate or biennial bearing, whereas Amrapali and Neelum are regular bearers.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does Total Soluble Solids (TSS) measure in horticultural produce?

Explanation: TSS measures the concentration of sugars, organic acids, and dissolved solids in fruit juice, expressed in degrees Brix (°Bx).

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest is universally recognized for causing severe damage to tomato fruit by boring circular holes?

Explanation: Helicoverpa armigera (tomato fruit borer) is a polyphagous pest that causes heavy economic loss by damaging developing fruits.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the ideal harvest maturity stage for tomato destined for long-distance transport and distant markets?

Explanation: Harvesting tomatoes at the breaker or turning stage ensures adequate shelf-life and firmness during transit without sacrificing ripening quality.

Which physiological disorder in Alphonso mango is triggered by high internal fruit pulp temperatures, and how is it managed?

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

Compare the parentage of Amrapali and Mallika. Which statement is scientifically correct?

Solution & Rationale: Amrapali and Mallika are reciprocal crosses from the same parentage (Dashehari and Neelum) but with reversed maternal and paternal roles.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary biochemical and agronomic reason for Blossom End Rot in solanaceous crops like tomato?

Solution & Rationale: Blossom End Rot is a physiological disorder driven by inadequate calcium translocation to the distal end of the fruit during rapid cell division and moisture fluctuation.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following pairs of plant-specific variety and resistance trait is correctly matched?

Solution & Rationale: Pusa Kranti brinjal variety exhibits notable tolerance and field resistance to little leaf disease caused by phytoplasma.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

In citrus orcharding, what is the significance of nucellar embryony?

Solution & Rationale: Nucellar embryony arises from maternal nucellar tissue surrounding the embryo sac, producing asexual nucellar seedlings that are genetically uniform and disease-free.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the optimum temperature range and chilling hour requirement for successful commercial apple cultivation?

Solution & Rationale: Temperate fruit crops like apple require cold winters with 800 to 1200 chilling hours below 7°C to break dormancy and ensure proper fruit setting.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Why is 'curing' considered an indispensable post-harvest operation for onion bulbs?

Solution & Rationale: Curing removes excess moisture from the neck and outer scales, forming a protective barrier against fungal pathogens like Aspergillus niger during storage.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

What distinguishes alternate bearing from regular bearing in fruit trees, and which cultural practices help mitigate it?

Solution & Rationale: Alternate bearing (biennial bearing) exhausts tree reserves in ON years; manual de-blossoming in ON years and adequate nutrition help restore a regular bearing cycle.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

How does wedge grafting superiorly replace traditional inarching in mango nursery management?

Solution & Rationale: Wedge grafting (including stone/epicotyl grafting) utilizes young germinating stones or seedlings, significantly shortening nursery rearing time and making transport economical.
