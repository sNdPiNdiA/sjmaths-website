Nutritional Science in Horticultural Crops: Pomology and Olericulture

This chapter delivers comprehensive study material for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture examinations, focusing on the essential mineral nutrition, physiological functions, deficiency symptoms, fertilizer calculation methodologies, and nutrient management in fruit crops (Pomology) and vegetable crops (Olericulture).

Fundamentals of Essential Plant Nutrients in Horticulture

Plant nutrition is the study of chemical elements and compounds necessary for plant growth, metabolism, and completion of their life cycle. According to the criteria of essentiality established by Arnon and Stout (1939), an element is essential if a plant cannot complete its vegetative or reproductive phase in its absence, the action cannot be replaced by another element, and the element is directly involved in plant metabolism.

  • Macronutrients: Required in larger quantities (measured in percentage or g/kg dry matter). They include Primary Nutrients (Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium) and Secondary Nutrients (Calcium, Magnesium, Sulfur).
  • Micronutrients (Trace Elements): Required in minute quantities (measured in mg/kg or ppm dry matter). They include Iron, Manganese, Zinc, Copper, Boron, Molybdenum, Chlorine, and Nickel.
  • Beneficial Elements: Elements such as Sodium (Na), Silicon (Si), Cobalt (Co), and Vanadium (V) that promote growth in specific plant species under certain environmental conditions.

Physiological Roles and Mobility of Nutrients in Fruit and Vegetable Crops

Understanding nutrient mobility within the vascular system (xylem and phloem) is vital for diagnosing nutritional disorders and deficiency symptoms in horticultural plantations.

  • Mobile Nutrients (N, P, K, Mg): Translocated rapidly from older, mature leaves to actively growing meristematic tissues and developing fruits. Consequently, deficiency symptoms manifest first on older, lower leaves (e.g., chlorosis in nitrogen deficiency).
  • Immobile Nutrients (Ca, B, Fe, Zn, Mn, Cu): Cannot be readily mobilized from older tissues. Deficiency symptoms appear primarily on younger leaves, terminal buds, and growing root tips (e.g., blossom-end rot in tomato due to Calcium deficiency, internal cork in apple due to Boron deficiency).
  • Nitrogen (N): Integral component of amino acids, proteins, nucleic acids, and chlorophyll. Essential for vegetative vigor and fruit size.
  • Phosphorus (P): Energy currency (ATP/ADP), root development, flowering, and nucleic acid synthesis.
  • Potassium (K): Enzyme activation, stomatal regulation, osmoregulation, translocation of assimilates, and fruit quality enhancement.

Deficiency Symptoms and Physiological Disorders in Pomology and Olericulture

Horticultural crops are highly sensitive to micronutrient imbalances and specific macro-element deficiencies, which lead to distinct physiological disorders affecting yield and marketability.

  • Tomato Blossom-End Rot (BER): Caused by local calcium deficiency coupled with irregular water supply; affects the distal end of the fruit, causing sunken, dark leathery lesions.
  • Cauliflower Whiptail: Caused by Molybdenum deficiency, leading to narrow, distorted, whip-like leaf blades and failure of curd formation.
  • Cauliflower Buttoning: Premature formation of small curds caused by nitrogen starvation or restricted root growth.
  • Apple Internal Cork and Bitter Pit: Boron and Calcium deficiencies respectively, causing corky tissue development and bitter, sunken spots on the fruit peel.
  • Mango Internal Breakdown/Spongy Tissue: Physiological disorder influenced by high temperature, solar radiation, and orchard nutrition management.

Fertilizer Calculation, Application Methods, and Fertigation

Precise nutrient management requires calculating fertilizer quantities based on recommended nutrient doses (RDF) and fertilizer grades.

  • Formula for Urea Calculation: $Urea required (kg) = Recommended N (kg) × 100Percentage of N in Urea (46%) For instance, to apply 100 kg N, Urea needed = (100 × 100) / 46 = 217.39 kg.
  • Formula for SSP (Single Super Phosphate) Calculation: SSP required (kg) = Recommended P_2O_5 (kg) × 100Percentage of P_2O_5 in SSP (16%)
  • Formula for MOP (Muriate of Potash) Calculation: MOP required (kg) = Recommended K_2O (kg) × 100Percentage of K_2O in MOP (60%)
  • Fertigation: Application of water-soluble fertilizers through drip irrigation systems, ensuring high nutrient use efficiency (NUE) and reduced leaching losses in orchards and greenhouse vegetables.

Foliar Nutrition and Soil Testing Protocols in Orchards

Foliar feeding is an effective technique for correcting acute micronutrient deficiencies rapidly during critical phenological stages, bypassing soil fixation problems.

  • Foliar Spray of Zinc: 0.5% Zinc Sulfate (ZnSO_4) combined with lime is commonly sprayed in citrus and guava orchards to correct 'little leaf' or 'mottle leaf'.
  • Foliar Spray of Boron: 0.2% to 0.3% Borax application during pre-bloom stage enhances fruit set and prevents fruit cracking in pomegranate and litchi.
  • Soil Testing: Collecting representative core samples from orchard root zones (0–30 cm and 30–60 cm depth) to determine available N, P, K, organic carbon, and electrical conductivity (EC), guiding site-specific nutrient recommendations.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Arnon and Stout proposed the criteria of essentiality for plant nutrients in 1939.
  • Urea contains 46% Nitrogen, SSP contains 16% Phosphorus pentoxide (P_2O_5), and MOP contains 60% Potassium oxide (K_2O).
  • Blossom-end rot in tomato and capsicum is primarily caused by calcium deficiency.
  • Whiptail in cauliflower is a classic symptom of Molybdenum deficiency.
  • Zinc deficiency in fruit trees causes 'little leaf' or 'rosetting' disorder.
  • Boron is vital for pollen germination, pollen tube growth, and fruit set in horticultural crops.
  • Nickel (Ni) was the last element added to the list of essential plant nutrients by Brown et al. in 1987.
  • Chloride (Cl^-) is essential for the oxygen-evolving complex in Photosystem II during photosynthesis.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing mobile and immobile nutrient deficiency symptoms: Remember, mobile nutrient deficiencies start on older leaves, while immobile nutrient deficiencies start on younger leaves.
  • Assuming Urea has 50% Nitrogen; the exact standard fertilizer grade percentage for agricultural Urea is 46% N.
  • Confusing cauliflower whiptail (due to Molybdenum) with buttoning (due to Nitrogen deficiency).

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Nutritional Science in Horticultural Crops: Pomology and Olericulture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Criteria of Essentiality

Essential plant nutrients must satisfy strict physiological criteria established by D.I. Arnon and P.A. Stout in 1939.

  • Direct Involvement: The element must be directly involved in plant nutrition and metabolism.
  • Irreplaceability: Deficiency cannot be corrected by supplying another chemical analog.
Concept 2

Nutrient Mobility and Diagnosis

Phloem mobility dictates the spatial distribution of deficiency symptoms across plant organs.

  • Mobile Elements: Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium, Magnesium. Symptoms appear on older leaves.
  • Immobile Elements: Calcium, Boron, Iron, Zinc. Symptoms appear on younger apex leaves and meristems.
Concept 3

Fertilizer Stoichiometry and Calculations

Agronomic fertilizer calculations require precise application of nutrient percentage formulas.

  • Nutrient Mass Formula: Fertilizer (kg) = [Requirement (kg) × 100] / Grade percentage(%).
Concept 4

Micronutrient Disorders in Horticulture

Horticultural crops exhibit specific visual marker symptoms under micronutrient stress.

  • Boron: Internal cork in apple, fruit cracking in pomegranate.
  • Zinc: Little leaf in citrus and mango.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Essential Element (Arnon-Stout Criteria Element)
An inorganic chemical element required by plants to complete their life cycle.
Macronutrient (Major Plant Nutrient)
Nutrient required in large concentrations (> 0.1% dry weight), e.g., N, P, K.
Micronutrient (Trace Element)
Nutrient required in micro-concentrations (< 100 ppm), e.g., Zn, B, Fe.
Blossom-End Rot (Calcium Deficiency Necrosis)
Physiological disorder in solanaceous vegetables causing sunken black lesions at fruit distal ends.
Whiptail (Molybdenum Deficiency Symptom)
Malformation of leaf blades into narrow whip-like structures in crucifers.
Fertigation (Fertilizer Irrigation)
Application of dissolved fertilizers through pressurized irrigation systems.
Chlorosis (Loss of Chlorophyll)
Yellowing of green plant tissues due to impaired chlorophyll synthesis or destruction.
Necrosis (Tissue Death)
Localized death of plant cells and tissues, appearing brown or black.
Little Leaf (Rosette Disorder)
Stunting of leaf expansion resulting from Zinc deficiency.
Muriate of Potash (Potassium Chloride (KCl))
Standard potassic fertilizer containing 60% K_2O equivalent.
Single Super Phosphate (SSP Fertilizer)
Phosphatic fertilizer containing 16% P_2O_5 and 12% Sulfur.
Foliar Nutrition (Extra-Root Feeding)
Application of dilute nutrient solutions directly onto plant foliage for rapid absorption.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Buttoning in Cauliflower VS Whiptail in Cauliflower

Buttoning is caused by nitrogen starvation resulting in small curds, whereas Whiptail is caused by molybdenum deficiency leading to narrow, distorted leaves.

Mobile Nutrients VS Immobile Nutrients

Mobile nutrients show deficiency symptoms on older leaves; immobile nutrients show symptoms on younger growing shoots.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Who proposed the classic criteria for the essentiality of plant mineral nutrients in 1939?

Explanation: D.I. Arnon and P.A. Stout formulated the modern criteria of essentiality in 1939, stating that a plant must be unable to complete its life cycle without the element, the element cannot be substituted by another, and it must be directly involved in plant metabolism.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is an immobile plant nutrient in horticultural crops?

Explanation: Calcium is an immobile nutrient in plants. Because it is bound in cell walls as calcium pectate, it cannot be translocated from older tissues to younger growing regions during deficiency.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the exact percentage of Nitrogen present in commercial agricultural grade Urea?

Explanation: Commercial Urea [CO(NH_2)_2] contains 46% Nitrogen by weight, making it the most concentrated solid nitrogenous fertilizer.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Blossom-End Rot (BER) in tomato fruit is primarily caused by the deficiency of which element?

Explanation: Blossom-end rot is a physiological disorder caused by localized calcium deficiency in the distal tissues of developing tomato fruits, often aggravated by irregular irrigation.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Whiptail disease in cauliflower is specifically caused by the deficiency of:

Explanation: Whiptail in cauliflower is caused by Molybdenum deficiency, which impairs nitrate reduction and protein synthesis, leading to distorted, whip-like leaf blades.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Little leaf or rosetting in citrus and fruit trees is corrected by the application of:

Explanation: Little leaf is caused by Zinc deficiency, which disrupts tryptophan synthesis and auxin production. It is successfully corrected by foliar sprays of Zinc Sulfate.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

How much Single Super Phosphate (SSP) is required to supply 32 kg of P_2O_5?

Explanation: Calculation: ( ext{Required } P_2O_5 imes 100) / 16 = (32 imes 100) / 16 = 3200 / 16 = 200 kg of SSP.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which nutrient element was added as the 17th essential plant nutrient by Brown et al. in 1987?

Explanation: Nickel (Ni) was recognized as the 17th essential plant nutrient by Brown, Welch, and Cary in 1987 because it is a vital constituent of the enzyme urease.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Internal cork and fruit cracking in apples and pomegranates are characteristic symptoms of:

Explanation: Boron is essential for cell wall structure, sugar translocation, and pollination. Its deficiency leads to corky tissue formation and fruit splitting.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the nutrient percentage of Potassium (K_2O) in Muriate of Potash (MOP)?

Explanation: Muriate of Potash (Potassium Chloride, KCl) contains 60% potassium expressed as water-soluble K_2O.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following macronutrients acts as the 'energy currency' of the plant cell?

Explanation: Phosphorus is a core component of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), nucleic acids, and phospholipids, governing energy storage and transfer in cellular metabolism.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Deficiency symptoms of mobile nutrients (like Nitrogen and Potassium) first appear on:

Explanation: Mobile nutrients are translocated from older tissues to active growing points during deficiency, causing chlorosis or necrosis to appear first on older, lower leaves.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Buttoning in cauliflower is primarily caused by:

Explanation: Buttoning is the formation of premature, undersized curds in cauliflower caused by restricted vegetative growth due to nitrogen deficiency or transplanting aged seedlings.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Chlorosis between leaf veins while veins remain green (interveinal chlorosis) is a classic symptom of:

Explanation: Interveinal chlorosis occurs when iron or magnesium is deficient, impairing chlorophyll synthesis while vascular veins retain some green pigmentation.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary advantage of fertigation in modern horticulture?

Explanation: Fertigation delivers water-soluble fertilizers directly to the active root zone in precise doses, vastly improving nutrient use efficiency and reducing leaching losses.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which element is a crucial component of the enzyme nitrate reductase and nitrogenase?

Explanation: Molybdenum is a metallic micronutrient that acts as a cofactor for nitrate reductase and nitrogenase enzymes, facilitating nitrogen assimilation.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Bitter pit in apples is a physiological disorder associated with:

Explanation: Bitter pit is caused by localized calcium deficiency in the outer flesh of apple fruits, often linked to imbalances in calcium-to-nitrogen ratios.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which fertilizer is both a source of phosphorus and sulfur?

Explanation: Single Super Phosphate (SSP) contains 16% P_2O_5, 19% Calcium, and 12% Sulfur, making it a dual-nutrient fertilizer.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Chloride (Cl^-) is essential in plant physiology primarily for:

Explanation: Chloride plays a vital role in the photolysis of water and oxygen evolution in Photosystem II, as well as in turgor regulation and osmotic balance.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following pairs of physiological disorders and their causal nutrient elements is INCORRECTLY matched?

Solution & Rationale: Little leaf in citrus is caused by Zinc (Zn) deficiency, not Iron (Fe) deficiency. Iron deficiency causes interveinal chlorosis of young leaves.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

In soil science and plant nutrition, what defines a 'beneficial element'?

Solution & Rationale: Beneficial elements (e.g., Sodium, Silicon, Cobalt) are not strictly essential for all plants but promote growth or substitute for essential elements in specific plant taxa.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary visual symptom of Potassium deficiency in most horticultural crops?

Solution & Rationale: Potassium is highly mobile. Its deficiency causes marginal chlorosis followed by necrosis (scorching or firing) starting at the tips and margins of older leaves.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

Which instrument or method is standard for determining available potassium in orchard soils?

Solution & Rationale: Available potassium in soils is extracted using neutral normal ammonium acetate (NH_4OAc) and quantified using a Flame Photometer.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary role of Boron in horticultural crop nutrition during the reproductive stage?

Solution & Rationale: Boron is critical for successful pollination and fertilization because it facilitates pollen germination and guides pollen tube elongation down the style.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

If an orchard soil test indicates severe acidity (pH < 5.0), the availability of which micronutrient can increase to toxic levels?

Solution & Rationale: In strongly acidic soils, iron and manganese become highly soluble and available, often leading to iron/manganese toxicity, whereas molybdenum availability decreases.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which chemical compound is commonly used as a foliar spray at 0.5% concentration to correct Zinc deficiency in fruit crops?

Solution & Rationale: Zinc Sulfate (ZnSO_4 · 7H_2O) is the standard, cost-effective salt utilized for foliar application in horticulture, often neutralized with lime to prevent leaf scorching.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the chemical formula and nutrient content of Muriate of Potash?

Solution & Rationale: Muriate of Potash is Potassium Chloride (KCl), containing 60% potassium expressed as K_2O$.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Why does Calcium deficiency manifest first on younger leaves and growing points rather than older leaves?

Solution & Rationale: Calcium is transported solely via the xylem and is structurally bound in cell walls (calcium pectate). Being immobile in the phloem, it cannot be redistributed from older to younger tissues.
