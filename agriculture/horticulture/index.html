Introduction and Branches of Horticulture

Horticulture is derived from the Latin words hortus (garden) and cultura (cultivation). It is broadly divided into major branches: Pomology (fruit cultivation), Olericulture (vegetable production), Floriculture (flower cultivation), Post-Harvest Technology, Landscape Architecture, and Spices, Plantation, and Medicinal Crops.

  • Pomology: Study and cultivation of fruit crops.
  • Olericulture: Production of herbaceous vegetable crops for human consumption.
  • Floriculture: Commercial cultivation and arrangement of ornamental and flowering plants.

Climate, Soil, and Propagation Techniques in Fruit Crops

Fruit crops require specific agro-climatic conditions. Tropical fruits like Mangifera indica and Carica papaya thrive in frost-free, warm climates, whereas temperate fruits like Malus domestica require specific chilling hours. Propagation methods include sexual (seeds) and asexual (vegetative) techniques such as grafting, budding, layering, and tissue culture to maintain genetic purity.

  • Mango (Mangifera indica): Propagated via veneer grafting or stone grafting.
  • Citrus species: Propagated primarily through T-budding or patch budding.
  • Guava (Psidium guajava): Air layering (gootee) and patch budding are standard practices.

Olericulture: Classification and Production Technologies of Major Vegetables

Vegetables are classified based on their climatic requirements (warm-season vs. cool-season), botanical families, and edible plant parts. Solanaceous vegetables (Tomato, Brinjal, Chilli) require warm temperatures, while cole crops (Cabbage, Cauliflower) prefer cooler climes.

  • Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum): Self-pollinated crop, sensitive to high temperatures and frost; indeterminate and determinate growth habits.
  • Cauliflower (Brassica oleracea var. botrytis): Requires cool, moist weather; curd development is highly sensitive to boron and molybdenum availability.

Nutrient Management, Irrigation, and Physiological Disorders

Horticultural crops demand balanced application of macro and micronutrients. Drip irrigation and fertigation maximize water-use efficiency. Several physiological disorders arise from nutritional imbalances or climatic stress.

  • Spongy Tissue in Mango: Physiological disorder prevalent in 'Alphonso' caused by high internal fruit temperatures pre-harvest.
  • Black Heart in Potato: Caused by oxygen deficiency and high temperature during storage.
  • Blossom-End Rot in Tomato: Caused by calcium deficiency coupled with irregular water supply.

Plant Protection: Major Pests, Diseases, and Management

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) is critical for managing insect pests and phytopathogenic diseases in horticultural ecosystems.

  • Fruit Fly (Bactrocera dorsalis): Attacks mango and guava; managed using methyl eugenol traps and bait sprays.
  • Powdery Mildew (Oidium mangiferae): Major fungal disease in mango controlled by wettable sulfur sprays.
  • Damping-Off in Nursery: Caused by Pythium spp.; managed through seed treatment with Trichoderma viride or captan.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Mango is India's national fruit and is considered the king of fruits, with 'Alphonso' being the leading export variety.
  • The edible portion of banana is the mesocarp and endocarp, and it is a parthenocarpic fruit propagated via sword suckers.
  • Papaya sex expression is highly diverse; gynodioecious varieties like 'Pusa Dwarf' and 'Pusa Majesty' are preferred for commercial cultivation.
  • Citrus tristeza virus (CTV) is transmitted by the brown citrus aphid (Toxoptera citricidus).
  • Bortrytis rot and black heart in potato can be mitigated by maintaining adequate ventilation and optimal storage temperatures.
  • The National Horticulture Board (NHB) is headquartered in Gurugram, Haryana.
  • Central Institute for Subtropical Horticulture (CISH) is located in Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the propagation method of Banana (sword suckers or tissue culture) with seed propagation.
  • Attributing spongy tissue in mango to a fungal or bacterial pathogen, when it is actually a physiological disorder linked to internal fruit heat.
  • Mistaking parthenocarpy in banana for apomixis; parthenocarpy is fruit development without fertilization, whereas apomixis relates to seed formation without sexual fusion.

Concept 1

Pomology and Fruit Tree Architecture

Pomology encompasses orchard establishment, training, and pruning systems.

  • Training: Giving a desirable shape to the young fruit tree.
  • Pruning: Selective removal of plant parts to balance vegetative and reproductive growth.
Concept 2

Olericulture Systems and Nursery Management

Vegetable production relies heavily on raising healthy seedlings in protected nurseries.

  • Nursery Bed Soil Solarization: Using transparent polyethylene sheets to kill soil-borne pathogens and weed seeds prior to sowing.
Concept 3

Protected Cultivation and Greenhouse Technology

Growing high-value vegetable and floral crops under controlled environmental conditions like net houses and polyhouses.

  • Ventilation: Crucial for regulating relative humidity and preventing fungal epidemics inside greenhouses.
Concept 4

Post-Harvest Handling of Horticultural Produce

Minimizing post-harvest losses through proper grading, sorting, waxing, and cold-chain logistics.

  • Climacteric Fruits: Fruits that exhibit a sharp rise in respiration rate post-harvest (e.g., Mango, Banana, Tomato).

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Pomology (Branch of horticulture dealing with fruit crops)
The scientific study and cultivation of fruit-bearing plants.
Olericulture (Branch of horticulture dealing with vegetables)
The science and practice of growing herbaceous vegetable crops.
Floriculture (Branch of horticulture dealing with flowers)
The cultivation, processing, and marketing of ornamental and flowering plants.
Parthenocarpy (Fruit development without fertilization)
Production of seedless fruits naturally or via chemical induction.
Climacteric Fruit (Ripening fruits with high respiration surge)
Fruits that can ripen after harvest accompanied by an ethylene burst (e.g., banana, mango).
Non-Climacteric Fruit (Ripening fruits without respiration surge)
Fruits that ripen only while attached to the parent plant (e.g., citrus, grapes, litchi).
Veneer Grafting (Standard method of mango propagation)
A common vegetative propagation technique used in Indian nurseries for mango saplings.
Tip Burn (Physiological disorder in lettuce/cabbage)
Calcium deficiency disorder leading to necrosis of leaf margins under high transpiration rates.
Blossom-End Rot (Calcium disorder in solanaceous crops)
Water-soaked sunken lesions at the distal end of tomato and bell pepper fruits.
Gootee (Air layering technique)
A traditional method of layering where root initiation is induced on an aerial stem.
Curing (Post-harvest process for onion and garlic)
Drying the outer skin of bulbs to reduce moisture and prevent microbial infection.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Horticulture is divided into Pomology, Olericulture, and Floriculture.
  • Vegetative propagation preserves genetic integrity in fruit trees.
  • Climacteric fruits exhibit an ethylene and respiration burst during ripening.
  • Nutrient deficiencies manifest as distinct physiological disorders across horticultural crops.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Climacteric Fruits VS Non-Climacteric Fruits

Climacteric fruits ripen post-harvest with a respiration peak, while non-climacteric fruits do not ripen once detached from the plant.

Training VS Pruning

Training is done to give structural framework to the plant, whereas pruning is the removal of plant parts to improve yield and quality.
