Introduction to Post-Harvest Physiology and Maturity Indices

Post-harvest horticulture deals with the technology and science of preserving horticultural produce from harvest through handling, storage, and processing. Understanding maturity indices is critical to minimize post-harvest losses, which can exceed 30% in developing nations.

  • Maturity Indices: Physiological maturity refers to the stage when a plant or plant part has completed its ontogeny and is capable of continuing its development even if detached. Horticultural maturity is the stage of development when a plant part possesses the prerequisites for utilization by consumers for a particular purpose.
  • Climacteric vs. Non-Climacteric Fruits: Climacteric fruits exhibit a sharp rise in respiration and ethylene production at the onset of ripening (e.g., Mangifera indica, Musa paradisiaca, Lycopersicon esculentum). Non-climacteric fruits show a gradual decrease or steady rate of respiration during ripening without an ethylene peak (e.g., Citrus sinensis, Vitis vinifera, Ananas comosus).

Ethylene Action, Ripening Regulations, and Storage Technologies

Ethylene (C2H4) is a natural plant hormone that acts as the primary master regulator of ripening in climacteric fruits. Managing ethylene production and sensitivity is vital for shelf-life extension.

  • Ethylene Scavengers and Inhibitors: Potassium permanganate (KMnO4) acts as an ethylene scrubber by oxidizing it. 1-Methylcyclopropene (1-MCP) is a potent gaseous ethylene action inhibitor that blocks ethylene receptors.
  • Cold Storage and Controlled Atmosphere (CA): Lowering storage temperatures reduces respiration and metabolic rates in accordance with the Q10 temperature coefficient. CA storage involves modifying oxygen (O2) and carbon dioxide (CO2) concentrations relative to ambient air to suppress respiration and ethylene synthesis. Modified Atmosphere Packaging (MAP) creates a passive or active modified atmosphere using polymeric films.

Pre-Treatments, Curing, and Degreening Practices

Various physical and chemical pre-treatments are applied immediately after harvest to heal wounds, prevent microbial invasion, and improve visual appeal.

  • Curing: Essential for root and tuber crops (e.g., Dioscorea spp., Ipomoea batatas) and bulbs (e.g., Allium cepa, Allium sativum) to dry neck tissues, heal wounds, and prevent pathogen entry.
  • Degreening: Applied primarily to citrus fruits (Citrus spp.) to destroy green chlorophyll pigments and reveal natural carotenoid colors using low concentrations of exogenous ethylene gas under controlled temperature and humidity conditions.
  • Hot Water Treatments (HWT): Immersing fruits in hot water (typically 48°C to 52°C for specific durations) to disinfest fruit fly larvae and fungal spores such as Colletotrichum gloeosporioides in mangoes.

Physiological Disorders, Post-Harvest Pathogens, and Control

Post-harvest losses are heavily driven by physiological breakdowns and fungal/bacterial infections during transit and storage.

  • Major Physiological Disorders: Bitter pit in apples (calcium deficiency), chilling injury in tropical fruits stored at low non-freezing temperatures (e.g., black heart in Solanum tuberosum, internal breakdown in mangoes), and spongy tissue in Alphonso mangoes.
  • Major Post-Harvest Fungi: Green mold (Penicillium digitatum) and blue mold (Penicillium italicum) in citrus; soft rot caused by Erwinia carotovora in vegetables; and anthracnose caused by Colletotrichum gloeosporioides in mangoes and papayas. Controlled by proper sanitation, fungicides, and low-temperature storage.

Value Addition, Food Processing, and Preservation Techniques

Processing perishable horticultural crops into shelf-stable products ensures remunerative returns to farmers and prevents distress sales during peak harvest seasons.

  • Preservation Methods: Use of high sugar concentration (jam, jelly, marmalade), high salt and oil (pickles), dehydration/drying, and thermal processing (canning, bottling).
  • FPO and FSSAI Standards: Legal and quality standards governing fruit products. Jelly requires pectin, sugar, and acid (tartaric or citric acid) to set properly, forming a rigid gel structure when cooled.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • 1-Methylcyclopropene (1-MCP) is a potent inhibitor of ethylene action by competitively binding to ethylene receptors.
  • Potassium permanganate acts as an ethylene scrubber by oxidizing ethylene gas to carbon dioxide and water.
  • Alphonso mango is highly susceptible to spongy tissue, a physiological disorder linked to internal heat buildup.
  • Bitter pit in apples is primarily caused by localized calcium deficiency within the fruit flesh.
  • The optimum temperature for curing onions (Allium cepa) is 35°C to 38°C with low relative humidity.
  • Jelly setting is dependent on the precise balance of pectin (0.5-1.0%), acid (0.5-1.0%), and sugar (60-65%).
  • Chilling injury occurs in tropical and subtropical crops when stored at temperatures above freezing but below a critical threshold (typically 10-15°C).
  • Degreening of citrus fruits is achieved by exposing them to 10-20 ppm ethylene gas at 28-30°C and 85-90% relative humidity.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing climacteric and non-climacteric fruits: Citrus is non-climacteric, whereas mango and papaya are climacteric.
  • Assuming chilling injury only happens below 0°C: Chilling injury occurs above freezing temperatures (e.g., 10-13°C for bananas).
  • Confusing curing with degreening: Curing heals wounds in roots, tubers, and bulbs, whereas degreening removes green chlorophyll in citrus.

Concept 1

Maturity Indices and Harvest Standards

Establishing the correct harvest window ensures optimal eating quality, nutritional value, and storage potential.

  • Total Soluble Solids (TSS): Measured in Brix degrees using a refractometer, indicating sugar concentration.
  • Pressure Test: Measured with a fruit penetrometer to gauge flesh firmness as ripening proceeds.
Concept 2

Respiration and Transpiration Dynamics

Post-harvest commodities remain living tissues that consume oxygen and release carbon dioxide, heat, and water vapor.

  • Transpiration: Loss of water vapor leading to weight loss, wilting, and loss of crispness.
  • Q10 Coefficient: For every 10°C rise in temperature, respiration rate increases 2 to 3 times.
Concept 3

Controlled Atmosphere (CA) and Modified Atmosphere (MA) Storage

Environmental modification inside storage chambers or packaging extends storage life significantly.

  • CA Storage: Precise regulation of oxygen (down to 1-3%) and carbon dioxide (up to 2-5%).
  • MAP: Passive or active modification through permeable packaging films respiring with the produce.
Concept 4

Value Addition and Preservation Principles

Processing raw produce into jams, jellies, juices, and pickles prevents gluts and stabilizes farm incomes.

  • Pectin Gel Test: Alcohol test or jelmeter test used to determine pectin adequacy before boiling jelly batches.
  • Enzymatic Browning: Inhibition achieved through blanching, citric acid addition, or sulfiting agents.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Climacteric Fruit (Climacteric Fruit)
Fruits that exhibit an upsurge in respiration and ethylene production during the ripening phase.
Non-Climacteric Fruit (Non-Climacteric Fruit)
Fruits that ripen without any marked rise in respiration rate or autocatalytic ethylene production.
1-MCP (1-Methylcyclopropene)
A synthetic cyclic olefin that blocks ethylene receptors, effectively delaying fruit ripening.
Curing (Post-Harvest Curing)
Drying and healing outer tissues of root crops, tubers, and bulbs to prevent microbial entry and water loss.
Degreening (Chlorophyll Degradation)
Destruction of green chlorophyll pigments in citrus fruits using trace ethylene to reveal carotenoid colors.
Chilling Injury (Low Temperature Physiological Disorder)
Physiological breakdown in tropical and subtropical crops exposed to temperatures above freezing but below threshold.
Spongy Tissue (Internal Fruit Breakdown)
A major physiological disorder rendering the mesocarp of Alphonso mango spongy and yellow-white.
TSS (Total Soluble Solids)
Concentration of sugars, acids, and soluble minerals in fruit juice expressed as degrees Brix.
MAP (Modified Atmosphere Packaging)
Packaging perishable produce in plastic films where the respiration creates a beneficial low O2 and high CO2 atmosphere.
Penetrometer (Fruit Firmness Tester)
Mechanical instrument used to measure fruit flesh firmness as an index of maturity.
Hot Water Treatment (Thermal Disinfestation)
Dipping fruits in heated water to eradicate fungal spores and insect eggs prior to storage.
Blanching (Thermal Enzyme Inactivation)
Brief heat treatment of vegetables using steam or hot water to destroy oxidative enzymes prior to freezing or drying.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Ethylene biosynthesis pathway: Methionine -> S-Adenosylmethionine (SAM) -> 1-Aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylic acid (ACC) -> Ethylene.
  • Optimum storage temperature for most temperate fruits is near 0°C to 4°C.
  • Alphonso mango spongy tissue cannot be detected externally.
  • Jelly requires correct sugar, pectin, and acid balance to form a firm gel.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Maturity indices govern the post-harvest storage and eating quality of fruits and vegetables.
  • Climacteric fruits ripen with an ethylene and respiration surge, whereas non-climacteric fruits do not.
  • Refrigeration and cold chains are the single most effective tool for lowering respiration and slowing senescence.
  • Controlled atmosphere storage extends shelf-life by reducing oxygen and elevating carbon dioxide.
  • Processing and value addition turn surplus perishable produce into stable commercial products.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Climacteric Fruit VS Non-Climacteric Fruit

Climacteric fruits can be harvested mature-green and ripened off the tree using ethylene; non-climacteric fruits must ripen on the plant for optimum quality.

Curing VS Blanching

Curing heals wounds in harvested bulbs and tubers in ambient conditions; blanching inactivates enzymes in vegetables using hot water or steam.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a climacteric fruit?

Explanation: Mango (Mangifera indica) is a classic climacteric fruit showing a pronounced respiration and ethylene peak. Citrus, grapes, and pineapples are non-climacteric.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of 1-Methylcyclopropene (1-MCP) in post-harvest management?

Explanation: 1-MCP binds strongly to ethylene receptors in plant tissues, blocking ethylene action and significantly delaying ripening and senescence.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical compound is widely used as an ethylene scrubber in fruit storage containers?

Explanation: Potassium permanganate (KMnO4) oxidizes ethylene gas into carbon dioxide and water, removing it from storage atmospheres.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which physiological disorder is characterized by internal spongy and yellowish-white tissue in Alphonso mangoes?

Explanation: Spongy tissue is a major physiological disorder in Alphonso mangoes caused by heat-induced starch accumulation and enzymatic failure.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of post-harvest curing in onions and garlic?

Explanation: Curing dries the outer scales and neck of bulbs like onions and garlic, closing entry points for pathogens and reducing moisture loss.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which fruit is non-climacteric among the following?

Explanation: Sweet orange (Citrus sinensis) is non-climacteric, whereas banana, papaya, and tomato are climacteric fruits.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical concentration range of sugar required for a proper jelly set?

Explanation: A sugar concentration of 60% to 65% is essential in jelly making to bind water and form a stable pectin gel matrix.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Bitter pit in apples is a physiological disorder caused by the deficiency of which nutrient?

Explanation: Bitter pit is caused by localized deficiency of calcium in the fruit flesh, leading to cellular breakdown and sunken necrotic spots.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does TSS stand for in horticultural post-harvest evaluation?

Explanation: TSS stands for Total Soluble Solids, representing sugars, organic acids, and soluble minerals dissolved in fruit juice.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Chilling injury in tropical and subtropical fruits typically occurs when they are stored at:

Explanation: Chilling injury occurs when sensitive crops are exposed to low non-freezing temperatures, causing metabolic disruption and membrane dysfunction.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a post-harvest fungal pathogen responsible for green mold in citrus?

Explanation: Penicillium digitatum is the causal organism of green mold, the most destructive post-harvest disease of citrus fruits.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of degreening in citrus fruits?

Explanation: Degreening removes the green chlorophyll pigments from citrus fruit peel using trace ethylene gas, exposing the underlying carotenoid pigments.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Modified Atmosphere Packaging (MAP) primarily alters which two gases inside the package?

Explanation: MAP works by reducing oxygen (O2) and increasing carbon dioxide (CO2) concentrations inside sealed polymeric packages.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the instrument used to measure fruit flesh firmness during maturity determination?

Explanation: A fruit penetrometer measures firmness by recording the force required to push a plunger tip of specified diameter into the fruit flesh.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Black heart in potato tubers stored under poorly ventilated conditions is caused by:

Explanation: Black heart occurs when potato tubers experience oxygen deficiency combined with elevated temperatures, causing internal tissue death and blackening.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which acid is commonly added to fruit jams and jellies to assist in pectin gel formation?

Explanation: Citric acid is added to lower pH (optimally 2.8 to 3.2), neutralizing negative charges on pectin molecules so they can form a gel network.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

The Q10 temperature coefficient for respiration in most horticultural produce states that for every 10°C rise in temperature, respiration rate increases by:

Explanation: The Q10 value for post-harvest respiration typically ranges from 2 to 3, meaning respiration doubles or triples with every 10°C temperature increase.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main purpose of blanching vegetables before freezing?

Explanation: Blanching inactivates enzymes such as peroxidase and polyphenol oxidase, preventing undesirable color changes, flavor loss, and nutrient degradation during freezing.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which stage of maturity indicates that a fruit has completed its ontogeny and is ready for harvest?

Explanation: Physiological maturity refers to the developmental stage when a fruit has completed its growth and is capable of ripening successfully.

Explain the role of Controlled Atmosphere (CA) storage in extending the post-harvest life of climacteric fruits.

Solution & Rationale: CA storage maintains low O2 and elevated CO2 levels, effectively depressing the respiration rate and delaying the ethylene climacteric rise.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which physiological disorder in potato is caused by inadequate oxygen diffusion during storage?

Solution & Rationale: Oxygen starvation inside poorly ventilated potato stores causes internal cellular asphyxiation and blackening known as black heart.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the precise biochemical mechanism of 1-Methylcyclopropene (1-MCP)?

Solution & Rationale: 1-MCP binds irreversibly or semi-permanently to plant ethylene receptors, preventing downstream ethylene signal transduction.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Why is curing important for bulb crops like onion and garlic prior to long-term storage?

Solution & Rationale: Curing dries neck tissues and outer scales, preventing fungal and bacterial infection and reducing water loss during storage.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

What are the three essential components required for a successful jelly set?

Solution & Rationale: Jelly formation relies on the precise balance of pectin (0.5-1.0%), sugar (60-65%), and acid (pH 2.8-3.2).

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following describes chilling injury symptoms in bananas?

Solution & Rationale: Bananas stored below 13°C suffer chilling injury resulting in sub-surface peel browning, graying, and failure to ripen normally.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary function of potassium permanganate in post-harvest handling?

Solution & Rationale: Potassium permanganate acts as an oxidizing agent that scrubs ethylene gas from storage environments.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

In citrus fruit management, what is degreening?

Solution & Rationale: Degreening involves applying low levels of ethylene gas to break down green chlorophyll pigments in citrus peel.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the main physiological consequence of high transpiration rates in harvested horticultural produce?

Solution & Rationale: Transpiration causes water loss leading to tissue dehydration, shriveling, loss of crispness, and direct marketable weight loss.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

How does waxing assist in post-harvest preservation of fruits and vegetables?

Solution & Rationale: Food-grade waxes act as a modified atmosphere barrier, reducing moisture loss and slowing gas exchange without causing anaerobiosis.
