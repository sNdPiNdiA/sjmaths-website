Introduction to Pomology and Botanical Classification of Fruit Crops

Pomology is the branch of horticulture that deals with the science and cultivation of fruit crops. Understanding the exact botanical taxonomy, family, origin, and chromosomal constitution is crucial for competitive examinations such as UP TGT and PGT Agriculture.

  • Mango: Mangifera indica, Family: Anacardiaceae, Origin: Indo-Burma, Chromosome Number: 2n = 40.
  • Banana: Musa paradisiaca / Musa acuminata × Musa balbisiana, Family: Musaceae, Origin: South-East Asia, Chromosome Number: 2n = 3x = 33 (Triploid, seedless through parthenocarpy).
  • Guava: Psidium guajava, Family: Myrtaceae, Origin: Tropical America, Chromosome Number: 2n = 22.
  • Citrus (Mandarin/Sweet Orange): Citrus reticulata / Citrus sinensis, Family: Rutaceae, Origin: South-East Asia, Chromosome Number: 2n = 18.
  • Papaya: Carica papaya, Family: Caricaceae, Origin: Tropical America, Chromosome Number: 2n = 18 (Dioecious fruit crop).
  • Apple: Malus domestica, Family: Rosaceae, Origin: South-West Asia, Chromosome Number: 2n = 34.

Orchard Establishment, Planting Systems, and Layout

Establishing a permanent orchard requires meticulous planning, site selection, soil testing, and selection of appropriate planting systems to maximize solar interception and yield efficiency.

  • Site Selection: Preference for deep, well-drained loamy soils with a pH range of 6.0 to 7.5, away from frost pockets, and with assured irrigation facilities.
  • Planting Systems: Square system is the most common and easiest to layout. Triangular system accommodates 15% more trees than the square system. Quincunx system accommodates a temporary filler tree in the center of the square, maximizing land use during early years. Hexagonal system accommodates 15% more trees than the square system and arranges trees equidistant from each other. Rectangular system allows intercultural operations more easily in one direction. High-Density Planting (HDP) involves narrow spacing combined with dwarfing rootstocks or pruning management (e.g., Mango variety Amrapali planted at 2.5 m × 2.5 m yielding 1600 plants/ha).

Training and Pruning in Fruit Crops

Training refers to the structural shaping of a young fruit tree to develop a strong scaffold framework, whereas pruning involves the selective removal of plant parts (branches, buds, roots) to regulate growth, flowering, and fruiting.

  • Training Systems: Central Leader system (strong trunk, but blocks sunlight penetration), Open Centre system (low spreading canopy, excellent light interception, prone to sun-scald), and Modified Leader system (compromise between central and open centre, widely adopted in deciduous and tropical fruits).
  • Pruning Objectives: To remove dead, diseased, or interlocking branches, maintain optimal leaf-to-fruit ratio, enhance light distribution within the canopy, and prevent alternate bearing.
  • Special Horticultural Practices: Ringing, girdling, notching, and bending are practised to check vegetative growth and promote floral bud differentiation (e.g., Paclobutrazol application in mango to induce regular bearing).

Nutrient and Water Management in Orchards

Precision nutrient and irrigation management ensures optimal vegetative growth, flowering, fruit set, and quality maintenance.

  • Fertilizer Application: Generally applied during pre-monsoon and post-harvest periods in circular trenches or basins around the tree canopy drip line. For example, a fully grown bearing mango tree typically receives 1000g N, 500g P2O5, and 1000g K2O per year, supplemented with well-rotted Farm Yard Manure (FYM).
  • Irrigation Methods: Basin method, ring method, furrow method, and modern micro-irrigation techniques such as drip and micro-sprinklers which save water and enhance nutrient use efficiency (fertigation). Critical stages for irrigation include flowering, fruit setting, and rapid fruit development stages.

Major Pests, Diseases, and Physiological Disorders

Fruit crops are vulnerable to various insect pests, fungal pathogens, and abiotic physiological disorders that severely diminish marketable yield.

  • Insect Pests: Mango Hopper (Idioscopus clypealis) damages inflorescence by sucking sap and secreting honeydew leading to sooty mould; Fruit Fly (Bactrocera dorsalis) causes premature fruit drop and rotting.
  • Diseases: Mango Malformation (causal agent: Fusarium mangiferae); Citrus Canker (bacterial disease caused by Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. citri); Papaya Ring Spot Virus (PRSV) transmitted by aphids.
  • Physiological Disorders: Spongy tissue in mango (internal breakdown of pulp near the stone, temperature-related); Black tip of mango (due to brick-kiln smoke containing SO2, CO, and ethylene); Fruit cracking in pomegranate and litchi (due to boron deficiency or erratic moisture fluctuations); Biennial bearing (alternate bearing) in mango (e.g., 'Chausa' and 'Langra' are alternate bearers, whereas 'Amrapali' is a regular bearer).

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Mango variety 'Amrapali' is suitable for High-Density Planting (HDP) at a spacing of 2.5 m × 2.5 m.
  • Papaya is strictly propagated through seeds and is a dioecious fruit crop, though gynodioecious varieties like Pusa Delicious and Pusa Nanha are widely cultivated.
  • Citrus canker is a bacterial disease caused by Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. citri and is managed using Streptocycline and copper fungicides.
  • Spongy tissue is a major internal physiological disorder of the mango variety 'Alphonso'.
  • Black tip of mango is caused by gaseous pollutants (SO2, ethylene) emitted from brick kilns and can be prevented by spraying Borax (0.6%).
  • Banana is a triploid (2n = 33) monocotyledonous fruit crop propagated commercially through healthy sword suckers weighing 500-750g or tissue culture plants.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Students often confuse the parents of mango hybrid 'Amrapali' and 'Mallika'. Remember: Amrapali = Dashehari × Neelum, whereas Mallika = Neelum × Dashehari.
  • Confusing the chromosome number of banana: Banana is triploid with 2n = 3x = 33, not a diploid.
  • Assuming papaya is propagated vegetatively; commercial papaya propagation is exclusively via seeds or tissue culture, never by cuttings or grafting.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Comprehensive Fruit Production in Horticulture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Taxonomy and Chromosome Constitution

Accurate identification of botanical binomials, families, and ploidy levels is fundamental for pomological classification.

  • Botanical Nomenclature: Must follow ICBN rules with binomial italicized.
  • Ploidy Levels: Most cultivated fruits are diploid (2n = 18, 22, 40), while banana is triploid (2n = 3x = 33).
Concept 2

Orchard Layout and Planting Systems

Proper orchard planning ensures efficient land utilization and maximum photosynthetic efficiency.

  • Hexagonal System: Accommodates 15% more plants than the square system.
  • Quincunx System: Utilizes temporary filler trees in the center of the square.
Concept 3

Training and Pruning Mechanics

Structural management of fruit trees directly impacts canopy microclimate and yield potential.

  • Training: Framework development during early vegetative phase.
  • Pruning: Maintenance practice to balance vegetative and reproductive growth.
Concept 4

Physiological Disorders and Plant Protection

Abiotic stresses and biotic pathogens require integrated management approaches.

  • Black Tip: Abiotic disorder due to brick kiln fumes mitigated by Borax.
  • Mango Malformation: Fungal disorder managed by pruning affected parts and applying NAA.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Pomology (Branch of Horticulture dealing with fruit crops)
The science and cultivation of fruits, encompassing propagation, orchard management, and post-harvest handling.
Parthenocarpy (Development of fruit without fertilization)
The natural or induced production of seedless fruits, as seen in commercial banana varieties.
High-Density Planting (HDP) (Ultra-high-density orcharding)
A planting system where a large number of dwarf fruit trees are accommodated per unit area to increase early yield.
Spongy Tissue (Internal fruit disorder of Alphonso mango)
A physiological breakdown of mesocarp tissue near the stone, rendering the pulp spongy and unpalatable.
Biennial Bearing (Alternate bearing habit)
The tendency of fruit trees (e.g., mango) to bear heavy crop in 'on' year and little to no crop in 'off' year.
Quincunx System (Diagonal planting system)
A modification of the square system where an additional temporary filler tree is planted in the center.
Veneer Grafting (Modified whip grafting method)
A popular commercial propagation method used for mango in India.
Sword Sucker (Pruned planting material in banana)
A vigorous banana sucker having narrow sword-like leaves and a broad base, ideal for planting.
Citrus Canker (Bacterial phytopathogen infection)
A destructive bacterial disease affecting citrus caused by Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. citri.
Paclobutrazol (Triazole plant growth retardant)
A chemical PGR used in mango to inhibit vegetative growth and induce early floral bud differentiation.
Dioecious Crop (Separate male and female plants)
A plant species where male and female flowers are borne on separate individual plants, such as papaya and date palm.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosome number (2n) of cultivated Mango (Mangifera indica)?

Explanation: Cultivated mango (Mangifera indica) is a diploid species with a somatic chromosome number of 2n = 40.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following mango hybrids is a cross between Dashehari and Neelum?

Explanation: Amrapali is an ICAR hybrid resulting from the cross between Dashehari (female) and Neelum (male), released in 1978.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the planting spacing recommended for High-Density Planting (HDP) of mango variety 'Amrapali'?

Explanation: Amrapali is a dwarf variety with regular bearing habit, suitable for high-density planting at 2.5 m × 2.5 m accommodating 1600 plants per hectare.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which planting system accommodates approximately 15% more trees compared to the square system?

Explanation: The hexagonal system (equilateral triangle layout) accommodates about 15% more plants per unit area compared to the square system.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the causal organism of Citrus Canker?

Explanation: Citrus canker is a bacterial plant disease caused by Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. citri, leading to raised necrotic lesions on citrus leaves, twigs, and fruits.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which fruit crop is commercially propagated through sword suckers or tissue culture?

Explanation: Banana (Musa spp.) is commercially propagated using vigorous sword suckers or micropropagated tissue culture plants, as seeded fruits are undesirable.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Black tip of mango is an abiotic physiological disorder caused by:

Explanation: Black tip of mango is caused by toxic pollutant gases like sulfur dioxide (SO2), carbon monoxide, and ethylene released from nearby brick kilns.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended chemical spray to prevent black tip disorder in mango?

Explanation: Spraying Borax at 0.6% at the marble stage of fruit development helps prevent black tip of mango.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Spongy tissue is a severe physiological disorder characteristic of which mango variety?

Explanation: Spongy tissue is an internal physiological disorder specific to Alphonso mango, caused by internal heat build-up pre-harvest.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosome number (2n) of Banana?

Explanation: Most commercial edible bananas are triploid with a somatic chromosome number of 2n = 3x = 33.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Papaya (Carica papaya) belongs to which botanical family?

Explanation: Papaya belongs to the family Caricaceae, order Brassicales, and is native to tropical America.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which plant growth regulator is commonly used in mango to induce regular bearing and suppress vegetative flush?

Explanation: Paclobutrazol is widely applied as a soil drench in mango orchards to control excessive vegetative vigor and promote heavy flowering and regular bearing.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary method of commercial propagation for Guava (Psidium guajava)?

Explanation: Guava is commercially propagated through vegetative methods, predominantly air layering (gootee), stooling (mound layering), and patch or wedge grafting.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In the quincunx planting system, what purpose does the central tree serve?

Explanation: In the quincunx system, a temporary filler tree (such as papaya or peach) is planted in the center of the square, providing early returns before main trees expand.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Mango Malformation is caused by:

Explanation: Mango malformation is a major fungal disorder caused by Fusarium mangiferae (formerly F. subglutinans), distorting floral panicles into leafy vegetative masses.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which fruit crop is known as the 'Queen of Fruits'?

Explanation: Mangosteen (Garcinia mangostana) is globally acclaimed as the 'Queen of Fruits' due to its exquisite flavor and delicate pericarp.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the edible part of a Banana?

Explanation: In banana, the edible portion comprises the fleshy mesocarp and endocarp, along with placentae.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which mango variety is a hybrid of Neelum × Dashehari?

Explanation: Mallika is an ICAR hybrid bred from Neelum (female) × Dashehari (male), known for large oblong fruits and excellent keeping quality.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended seed rate per hectare for direct sowing or nursery raising of Papaya?

Explanation: Approximately 400 to 500 grams of viable papaya seeds are required to raise sufficient seedlings for planting one hectare of land.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Which training system is characterized by a central main trunk running right to the top with lateral branches radiating around it, though often resulting in poor light penetration inside the canopy?

Solution & Rationale: The Central Leader system allows uninterrupted vertical growth of the main trunk, creating a strong framework, but shades the lower interior canopy.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Stenospermocarpy refers to:

Solution & Rationale: Stenospermocarpy is a form of seedlessness where pollination and fertilization occur normally, but the embryo aborts early, leaving small vestigial seeds (e.g., in certain grapes).

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following mango hybrids is seedless or possesses a very thin stone ('stone less')?

Solution & Rationale: Sindhu is a hybrid of Ratna × Alphonso, notable for having an extremely thin, spongy stone weighing less than 6 grams (stenospermocarpic trait).

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the critical temperature threshold below which frost causes severe damage to subtropical and tropical fruit orchards?

Solution & Rationale: Temperatures dropping to 0 °C and below cause ice crystal formation within plant cell walls, leading to cell rupture and severe frost injury.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

In citrus orcharding, what is 'Tristeza'?

Solution & Rationale: Citrus Tristeza Virus (CTV) is a destructive viral pathogen vectored principally by the brown citrus aphid, causing decline and death of trees on sour orange rootstock.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary objective of girdling fruit tree branches?

Solution & Rationale: Girdling removes a ring of phloem tissue, temporarily halting carbohydrate transport to the roots and accumulating sugars above the ring to promote flowering.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which fruit crop is classified as climacteric?

Solution & Rationale: Mango exhibits a distinct rise in respiration and ethylene production post-harvest (climacteric), whereas citrus and pineapple are non-climacteric.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the ideal pH range of soil for optimum growth and nutrient availability in tropical fruit orchards?

Solution & Rationale: A slightly acidic to neutral soil pH (6.0 to 7.5) ensures maximum availability of macro and micronutrients without toxicity or fixation.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following is a dioecious fruit species requiring male trees in an orchard ratio (e.g., 10%) for effective pollination?

Solution & Rationale: Papaya is dioecious (separate male and female plants), requiring careful male-to-female tree proportion in seed-propagated orchards.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What does the term 'Alternate Bearing' signify in perennial fruit trees?

Solution & Rationale: Alternate or biennial bearing is the physiological tendency of a tree to produce an abundant crop ('on' year) followed by a sparse or zero crop ('off' year).
