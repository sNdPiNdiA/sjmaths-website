Vegetable Production: Principles, Practices, and Crop Management

Master the principles of vegetable production, including botanical taxonomy, nursery management, agronomic practices, and plant protection for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture examinations.

Taxonomy, Origin, and Cytogenetics of Major Vegetables

Understanding the botanical classification, geographical origin, and chromosomal constitution of vegetable crops is fundamental for advanced agronomic studies and competitive exams.

  • Solanaceae Family: Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum, 2n=24, South America), Brinjal or Eggplant (Solanum melongena, 2n=24, Indo-China), Chilli (Capsicum annuum, 2n=24, South America).
  • Cucurbitaceae Family: Bottle gourd (Lagenaria siceraria, 2n=22, Africa), Cucumber (Cucumis sativus, 2n=14, India), Bitter gourd (Momordica charantia, 2n=22, Tropics of Old World).
  • Cruciferae (Brassicaceae) Family: Cabbage (Brassica oleracea var. capitata, 2n=18, Mediterranean), Cauliflower (Brassica oleracea var. botrytis, 2n=18, Europe), Knol-khol (Brassica oleracea var. gongylodes, 2n=18, Mediterranean).
  • Malvaceae Family: Okra or Lady's finger (Abelmoschus esculentus, 2n=130, Africa).

Nursery Raising and Protected Cultivation Technologies

Many vegetable crops with tiny seeds require specialized nursery raising techniques to ensure robust seedling establishment and uniform field stands.

  • Raised Bed Preparation: Beds should be 10-15 cm high, 1.0 m wide, and of convenient length to facilitate drainage and intercultural operations.
  • Soil Solarization: Spreading transparent polythene sheets over moist soil for 4-6 weeks during peak summer to kill soil-borne pathogens, weed seeds, and nematodes.
  • Chemical Soil Sterilization: Treatment with formaldehyde (4%) or use of captan/carbendazim drenches to prevent damping-off disease caused by Pythium spp. and Rhizoctonia spp.
  • Protected Structures: Use of net houses, walk-in tunnels, and naturally ventilated polyhouses to protect crops from adverse weather, insect vectors, and heavy rainfall.

Nutrient Management and Fertilizer Calculations

Precision nutrient management maximizes vegetable yields and prevents physiological disorders. Recommendations are typically based on soil test values and crop removal patterns.

  • Macronutrients: Nitrogen (N) promotes vegetative growth and leaf expansion; Phosphorus (P) enhances root development and flowering; Potassium (K) improves fruit quality, disease resistance, and osmotic regulation.
  • Micronutrient Disorders: Boron deficiency causes hollow stem in cauliflower and internal necrosis in tomato; Molybdenum deficiency causes whiptail in cauliflower; Calcium deficiency leads to blossom-end rot in tomato and tip burn in cabbage.
  • Fertilizer Calculation Example: To apply 100 kg N per hectare using Urea (containing 46% N), the quantity of urea required is calculated as: 10046 × 100 = 217.39 kg/ha.

Insect Pests, Diseases, and Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

Vegetable crops are highly susceptible to diverse pests and diseases due to their succulent nature. Integrated Pest Management (IPM) combines cultural, biological, and chemical methods.

  • Insect Pests: Fruit borer (Helicoverpa armigera) in tomato and okra; Shoot and fruit borer (Leucinodes orbonalis) in brinjal; Diamondback moth (Plutella xylostella) in cole crops.
  • Diseases: Early blight (Alternaria solani) and late blight (Phytophthora infestans) in tomato/potato; Damping-off (Pythium debaryanum) in nurseries; Powdery mildew (Erysiphe cichoracearum) in cucurbits.
  • IPM Strategies: Use of resistant/tolerant varieties, yellow sticky traps (10-15 per hectare) for whiteflies and aphids, pheromone traps (5-6 per hectare) for borers, and need-based application of biopesticides like Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) and neem-based formulations.

Physiological Disorders and Post-Harvest Management

Post-harvest losses in vegetables range from 20 to 30% in India, making proper harvesting indices and storage conditions critical.

  • Physiological Disorders: Puffiness in tomato (improper pollination/fertilization), catface in tomato (low temperatures during flowering), buttoning in cauliflower (nitrogen deficiency or transplanting aged seedlings).
  • Harvesting Indices: Tomato for local market harvested at pink stage; tomato for distant market harvested at mature green stage; okra harvested when pods are tender and snap easily.
  • Storage Conditions: Leafy vegetables require low temperatures near 0°C and 90-95% relative humidity; root crops and solanaceous crops store best at 8-12°C depending on chilling sensitivity.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Tomato fruit type is a berry and the edible portion is the pericarp and placenta.
  • Okra (Abelmoschus esculentus) belongs to the Malvaceae family and has a high chromosome number (2n=130, amphidiploid).
  • Blossom-end rot in tomato is caused by calcium deficiency and irregular irrigation schedules.
  • Botrytis cinerea causes grey mold, whereas Phytophthora infestans causes late blight of potato and tomato.
  • The seed rate of hybrid tomato is 125-150 g/ha, whereas for open-pollinated varieties it is 400-500 g/ha.
  • Cucurbits are predominantly monoecious, requiring insect pollination (bees) for effective fruit set.
  • Ethephon (chloroethyl phosphonic acid) is commonly applied in cucurbits to increase the number of female flowers.
  • The critical stage of irrigation in potato is stolon formation and tuber bulking stages.
  • Pusa Sawani is a famous yellow vein mosaic virus (YVMV) resistant variety of okra.
  • Brinjal shoot and fruit borer (Leucinodes orbonalis) larvae bore into shoots and developing fruits, causing 'dead hearts'.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Students often confuse fruit types: tomato is a berry, while cucumber and bottle gourd are pepos.
  • Mistaking physiological disorders for pathogenic diseases (e.g., blossom-end rot is due to Ca deficiency, not fungal infection).
  • Confusing seed rates of hybrid vs. open-pollinated vegetable varieties.
  • Assuming all solanaceous crops are strictly self-pollinated (chilli exhibits considerable cross-pollination).

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Vegetable Production: Principles, Practices, and Crop Management

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Botanical Classification and Family Traits

Vegetable crops belong to diverse botanical families with distinct floral and vegetative morphologies.

  • Solanaceae: Alternating leaves, bicarpellate syncarpous ovary, superior ovary, berry fruit.
  • Cucurbitaceae: Tendril climbers, unisexual flowers, inferior ovary, pepo fruit.
Concept 2

Nursery Management and Hardening

Healthy seedlings are vital for successful vegetable establishment.

  • Damping-off: Prevented by seed dressing with Thiram or Captan @ 2.5 g/kg seed.
  • Hardening: Gradual reduction of irrigation and shade 7-10 days prior to transplanting to withstand field stress.
Concept 3

Nutrient Deficiencies and Physiological Disorders

Nutrient imbalances lead to specific macroscopic symptoms on leaves and fruits.

  • Whiptail: Caused by Molybdenum deficiency in cauliflower.
  • Hollow Stem: Caused by excess nitrogen and Boron deficiency.
Concept 4

Insect Pest and Disease Management

Integrated approaches combining cultural practices, resistant cultivars, and safe chemicals.

  • Fruit Borer: Controlled using pheromone traps and NPV (Nuclear Polyhedrosis Virus).
  • Late Blight: Managed preventively with Mancozeb @ 2.5 g/L of water.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Pepo (Specialized berry fruit characteristic of Cucurbitaceae with a hard rind.)
A fleshy, many-seeded fruit with a tough rind, typical of cucumbers, melons, and gourds.
Blossom-End Rot (Physiological disorder due to Calcium (Ca) deficiency)
Water-soaked sunken patches appearing at the distal end of tomato fruits.
Monoecious (Unisexual flowers (male and female) on the same plant)
Floral arrangement common in Cucurbitaceae crops ensuring cross-pollination.
Damping-off (Fungal seedling disease caused by Pythium spp.)
Pre-emergence or post-emergence collapse of seedlings at the soil line.
Catface (Physiological fruit disorder in tomato)
Distortion and scarring at the blossom end of tomato fruits caused by low temperatures.
Hollow Stem (Internal stem cavitation in cauliflower and broccoli)
Physiological disorder caused by rapid growth due to excess nitrogen or boron deficiency.
Buttoning (Premature formation of small curd in cauliflower)
Caused by nitrogen starvation in the nursery or planting aged seedlings.
Parthenocarpy (Development of fruit without fertilization)
Resulting in seedless fruits, naturally occurring in some cucumbers and induced by plant growth regulators.
Soil Solarization (Thermal disinfestation using solar energy)
Covering moist soil with transparent polyethylene film to raise soil temperature and kill pathogens.
Curd (Edible inflorescence of cauliflower)
Compact mass of shortened, fleshy floral branches harvested before opening.
Tip Burn (Calcium translocation disorder in leafy vegetables)
Necrosis of leaf margins in cabbage and lettuce caused by localized calcium deficiency.
YVMV (Yellow Vein Mosaic Virus)
Severe viral disease of okra transmitted by whitefly (Bemisia tabaci).

  • Okra family: Malvaceae, 2n=130.
  • Cauliflower whiptail is due to Molybdenum deficiency.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Vegetable crops are classified based on taxonomy, climatic adaptation, and edible plant parts.
  • Solanaceous and cucurbitaceous crops form the backbone of warm-season vegetable production in India.
  • Nursery raising requires strict sanitation, proper seed treatment, and controlled irrigation.
  • Nutrient management must balance N, P, K with micronutrients to prevent physiological disorders.
  • IPM strategies integrate cultural, biological, and chemical methods to suppress pests and diseases.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Buttoning in Cauliflower VS Blindness in Cauliflower

Buttoning refers to the formation of prematurely small curds due to nitrogen stress, whereas blindness refers to the absence of a terminal growing bud.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosomal number (2n) of cultivated tomato (Solanum lycopersicum)?

Explanation: The diploid chromosome number (2n) of tomato is 24, which is typical for most species in the genus Solanum.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following physiological disorders in cauliflower is caused by Molybdenum deficiency?

Explanation: Whiptail in cauliflower is caused by a deficiency of Molybdenum, which impairs nitrogen metabolism in the plant.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the edible plant part of cauliflower?

Explanation: The edible portion of cauliflower is the curd, which consists of a mass of aborted, hypertrophied floral branches.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest is the major vector of Yellow Vein Mosaic Virus (YVMV) in Okra?

Explanation: Yellow Vein Mosaic Virus in okra is transmitted by the whitefly (Bemisia tabaci) in a persistent manner.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the fruit type of Cucurbitaceae family vegetables such as cucumber and bottle gourd?

Explanation: The fruit type characteristic of Cucurbitaceae is a pepo, which is a specialized inferior-ovary berry with a hard rind.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Blossom-end rot in tomato is primarily caused by the deficiency of which nutrient?

Explanation: Blossom-end rot is a physiological disorder caused by inadequate calcium uptake or erratic watering during fruit development.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended seed rate for hybrid tomato per hectare?

Explanation: The seed rate for hybrid tomato is 125-150 g/ha, whereas open-pollinated varieties require 400-500 g/ha.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical is commonly used for soil solarization in vegetable nurseries?

Explanation: Soil solarization utilizes transparent polyethylene sheets laid over moist soil during summer months to trap heat and kill soil-borne pests.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does 'Buttoning' in cauliflower refer to?

Explanation: Buttoning is the development of prematurely small curds in cauliflower caused by restricted vegetative growth due to nutrient stress.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which plant growth regulator is frequently applied in cucurbits to promote female flower production?

Explanation: Ethephon (chloroethyl phosphonic acid) releases ethylene, which shifts the sex ratio towards more female flowers in monoecious cucurbits.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the family of Okra (Abelmoschus esculentus)?

Explanation: Okra belongs to the Malvaceae family, characterized by monadelphous stamens and mucilaginous sap.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which pest is notorious for causing 'dead hearts' in Brinjal crops?

Explanation: The larva of Leucinodes orbonalis bores into young shoots, causing them to wither and dry up, resulting in 'dead hearts'.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

At what stage should tomato be harvested if fruits are destined for distant markets?

Explanation: For distant markets, tomatoes are harvested at the mature green stage as they ripen gradually during transit.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the purpose of hardening vegetable nursery seedlings before transplanting?

Explanation: Hardening conditions seedlings to withstand wind, high temperatures, and moisture stress after transplanting.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which vegetable crop is known to have a very high chromosome number (2n=130)?

Explanation: Cultivated okra (Abelmoschus esculentus) is an allotetraploid or amphidiploid with 2n=130 chromosomes.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What causes hollow stem in cauliflower and broccoli?

Explanation: Hollow stem occurs when plants grow too rapidly due to high nitrogen levels, often exacerbated by a lack of boron.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which fungus causes Damping-off disease in vegetable nurseries?

Explanation: Damping-off is primarily caused by soil-borne fungi such as Pythium species and Rhizoctonia solani.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the edible portion of Cabbage (Brassica oleracea var. capitata)?

Explanation: The edible portion of cabbage is the head, which is composed of tightly overlapping enlarged leaves.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a systemic insecticide commonly used in vegetable IPM?

Explanation: Imidacloprid is a systemic chloronicotinyl insecticide absorbed by roots and foliage to control sucking pests.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary mode of pollination in chilli (Capsicum annuum)?

Explanation: Although self-pollination occurs, chilli is often classified as a frequently cross-pollinated crop due to insect activity.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGR Standard

Calculate the quantity of Urea (46% N) required to supply 120 kg Nitrogen per hectare for a cabbage crop.

Solution & Rationale: Formula: (Target N / % N in fertilizer) * 100 = (120 / 46) * 100 = 260.86 kg/ha.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which botanical variety represents Kohl-rabi or Knol-khol?

Solution & Rationale: Brassica oleracea var. gongylodes represents knol-khol, characterized by an edible turnip-like swollen stem.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the typical seed rate per hectare for open-pollinated tomato varieties?

Solution & Rationale: Open-pollinated tomato varieties require 400 to 500 grams of seed per hectare, whereas hybrids require 125-150 g.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Which physiological disorder in tomato is characterized by concentric or radial cracking around the stem scar due to erratic watering?

Solution & Rationale: Fruit cracking occurs when heavy irrigation or rainfall follows a dry spell, causing rapid internal expansion exceeding skin elasticity.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

Match the following vegetables with their respective family: A. Tomato (Solanaceae), B. Bitter Gourd (Cucurbitaceae), C. Okra (Malvaceae), D. Cabbage (Brassicaceae). Which combination is correct?

Solution & Rationale: All listed pairings of vegetable crops with their respective botanical families are scientifically accurate.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGR Standard

Which insect pest lays eggs in clusters on the lower surface of leaves, and whose larvae feed gregariously on chlorophyll, later skeletonizing cole crop leaves?

Solution & Rationale: Cabbage butterfly (Pieris brassicae) larvae feed gregariously and can completely skeletonize leaves of brassica crops.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the causal organism of Early Blight of Potato and Tomato?

Solution & Rationale: Early blight is caused by the fungus Alternaria solani, producing characteristic target-board concentric leaf spots.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

Which plant growth regulator is effectively used to induce parthenocarpy in glasshouse cucumber production?

Solution & Rationale: Gibberellic acid applications can stimulate fruit set without fertilization, producing seedless (parthenocarpic) cucumbers.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGR Standard

What is the primary objective of growing trap crops like marigold alongside tomato fields?

Solution & Rationale: Marigold acts as a preferred host for Helicoverpa armigera, diverting pests away from the primary tomato crop.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following describes 'Puffiness' in tomato fruits?

Solution & Rationale: Puffiness occurs due to incomplete pollination or fertilization, leading to hollow locules inside the tomato fruit.
