Introduction and Taxonomic Classification of Plant Parasitic Nematodes

Plant parasitic nematodes are microscopic, unsegmented, pseudocoelomate roundworms belonging to the phylum Nematoda. Often referred to as 'eelworms', they possess a specialized hollow, protrusible stylet (or stomatostyle) used to pierce plant cell walls, inject digestive enzymes, and ingest plant sap. In agricultural ecosystems, they function as hidden enemies of crops, causing severe root dysfunction and predisposing plants to secondary fungal and bacterial infections.

  • Phylum: Nematoda
  • Class: Secernentea (Phasmida) and Adenophorea (Aphasmida)
  • Major Agricultural Orders: Tylenchida, Dorylaimida, and Aphelenchida
  • Morphological Characteristics: Vermiform body shape (except sedentary females like Meloidogyne spp. which become pyriform or saccate), bilateral symmetry, complete digestive tract, lack of respiratory and circulatory systems.

Major Plant Parasitic Nematodes and Economic Crop Losses

Understanding specific genera and species of plant parasitic nematodes is critical for diagnosis and management in field crops, vegetables, and horticultural systems. The feeding habits vary from ectoparasitic to endoparasitic, leading to characteristic diagnostic symptoms.

  • Root-Knot Nematode: Meloidogyne spp. (e.g., Meloidogyne incognita, Meloidogyne javanica). Induces giant cells (syncytia/nurse cells) and distinctive root galls or knots. Polyphagous pest attacking solanaceous crops, cucurbits, and pulses.
  • Cyst Nematode: Heterodera spp. (e.g., Heterodera avenae in cereal cyst nematode, Globodera rostochiensis in potato golden nematode). Females form protective leathery cysts enclosing eggs in soil.
  • Lesion Nematode: Pratylenchus spp. Migratory endoparasites causing dark necrotic lesions on roots, impairing water and nutrient uptake.
  • Reniform Nematode: Rotylenchulus reniformis. Kidney-shaped mature females; important pest of cotton, castor, and vegetable crops.
  • Stalk and Bulb / Stem Nematode: Ditylenchus dipsaci. Attacks stem and bulb tissues, causing twisting, swelling, and stem breaking in onion and garlic.

Symptoms of Nematode Damage and Plant Interaction

Nematode damage manifests both above-ground and below-ground. Because symptoms often mimic moisture stress, nutrient deficiencies (such as nitrogen or iron chlorosis), and root rot fungal complexes, microscopic soil and root analysis is vital for accurate diagnosis.

  • Above-Ground Symptoms: Stunting, patchiness in fields, wilting during hot mid-day hours despite adequate soil moisture, chlorosis (yellowing of leaves), premature defoliation, and reduced yield quantity and quality.
  • Below-Ground Symptoms: Root galling or knotting (characteristic of Meloidogyne), excessive root branching (root beard), stubby and coarse roots, root necrosis, dark brown lesions, and complete rotting of root systems due to secondary pathogen invasion.
  • Disease Complexes: Nematodes interact synergistically with soil-borne fungi like Fusarium oxysporum and Rhizoctonia solani, breaking plant resistance and intensifying wilt complexes.

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) Strategies for Nematodes

Management of plant parasitic nematodes relies heavily on preventative and cultural practices because complete eradication from field soil is practically impossible once established. An integrated approach combines agronomic, biological, and chemical methods.

  • Cultural Practices: Deep summer ploughing exposes nematodes to solar radiation and desiccation; crop rotation with non-host crops (e.g., marigold, Tagetes spp., or cereal crops); green manuring with sunn hemp (Crotalaria juncea).
  • Biological Control: Utilization of antagonistic rhizobacteria (Pseudomonas fluorescens, Bacillus subtilis) and fungal antagonists like Purpureocillium lilacinum (formerly Paecilomyces lilacinus) and Trichoderma viride that parasitize nematode eggs and females.
  • Host Plant Resistance: Cultivation of resistant crop varieties bred specifically against Meloidogyne incognita and Globodera rostochiensis.
  • Chemical Control: Application of nematicides such as systemic carbamates (carbofuran) and organophosphates (fosthiazate) or soil fumigants (dichloropropene, metam sodium) where economically viable.

Laboratory Extraction Techniques and Soil Sampling Methodology

Accurate estimation of nematode population density (population threshold levels) requires systematic soil and root sampling across a field followed by standard extraction methodologies in laboratory settings.

  • Soil Sampling Procedure: Collect zigzag core samples from the root zone (rhizosphere) at a depth of 15 to 30 cm. Composite samples of approximately 500g to 1kg of soil are placed in polythene bags, labeled, and kept cool during transport.
  • Baermann Funnel Technique: Used for extracting active, mobile vermiform nematodes from soil or root samples using water film migration principles.
  • Cobb's Decanting and Sieving Method: Utilizes a series of fine wire-mesh sieves (e.g., 100-mesh to 400-mesh) based on particle size and nematode body dimensions to separate nematodes from heavy soil particles.
  • Root Incubation Method: Chopping infected roots and incubating them in water to extract migrating endoparasitic juveniles and males.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The hollow, protrusible feeding organ in plant parasitic nematodes is called a stylet or stomatostyle.
  • Root-knot nematode belongs to the genus Meloidogyne and is characterized by the formation of multinucleate giant cells.
  • Golden nematode of potato is caused by Globodera rostochiensis, a major quarantine pest regulated globally.
  • Baermann funnel technique is standard laboratory procedure used for extracting live, active nematodes from soil and plant tissues.
  • Marigold (Tagetes patula / Tagetes erecta) acts as a trap crop and secretes alpha-terthienyl, which suppresses soil nematode populations.
  • Purpureocillium lilacinum is a prominent fungal biological control agent used effectively against root-knot nematode eggs.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Error: Believing nematodes are insects because they cause wilting and stunting. Scientific fact: Nematodes are non-segmented roundworms belonging to Phylum Nematoda, lacking jointed appendages and exoskeletons.
  • Error: Assuming all nematodes are harmful parasites. Scientific fact: The vast majority of soil nematodes are free-living bacterivores, fungivores, or predators that play beneficial roles in nutrient cycling and soil food webs.

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Taxonomy and Morphology

Plant parasitic nematodes are microscopic, unsegmented pseudocoelomates characterized by bilateral symmetry and a muscular oesophagus containing a median bulb and glands.

  • Stomatostyle: Specialized hollow piercing spear derived from the stoma used to puncture host plant cell walls.
Concept 2

Feeding Habits and Parasitism

Nematodes exhibit diverse feeding strategies ranging from ectoparasites remaining outside root surfaces to sedentary endoparasites establishing permanent feeding sites.

  • Syncytium: Specialized multinucleate nurse cells formed by cell wall dissolution under the chemical influence of sedentary nematode secretions.
Concept 3

Symptomatology and Field Diagnosis

Symptoms appear as patches of stunted, chlorotic, and wilted plants accompanied by characteristic underground root deformities.

  • Root Galling: Swelling and hypertrophy of root cortical tissues induced by Meloidogyne species feeding.
Concept 4

Integrated Management Practices

Sustainable management relies on combining resistant cultivars, bio-agents, and eco-friendly cultural methods to maintain nematode populations below economic injury levels.

  • Solarization: Covering moist soil with transparent polythene sheets during hot summer months to trap solar heat and kill soil-borne pathogens and nematodes.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Stomatostyle (Hollow protrusible stylet)
A specialized piercing organ formed in the stoma of plant-parasitic nematodes used for puncturing host plant cells.
Vermiform (Worm-shaped body form)
Elongated, cylindrical, and slender body morphology typical of mobile nematode life stages and males.
Cyst (Encysted female body)
The hardened, dead body wall of female Heterodera or Globodera species containing dormant eggs that withstand adverse environmental conditions.
Giant Cell (Multinucleate nurse cell)
Enlarged plant root cells induced by root-knot nematodes to supply continuous nutrients during feeding.
Baermann Funnel (Extraction apparatus)
A laboratory apparatus utilizing a water-filled funnel and mesh screen to extract active motile nematodes from soil samples.
Trap Crop (Agro-ecological decoy crop)
A crop planted to attract nematodes but preventing their successful reproduction, effectively lowering soil infestation.
Nematicide (Chemical pesticide for nematodes)
Chemical formulations categorized into fumigants and non-fumigants used to suppress or eradicate nematode populations.
Endoparasite (Internal parasite)
A nematode that penetrates and feeds from inside the root tissues of host plants.
Ectoparasite (External parasite)
A nematode that remains outside the root surface and feeds on epidermal and cortical cells using its long stylet.
Parthenogenesis (Asexual reproduction)
Reproduction from an ovum without fertilization, common in many plant-parasitic nematode species ensuring rapid population buildup.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Phylum: Nematoda; Class: Secernentea and Adenophorea.
  • Diagnostic feeding organ: Stomatostyle.
  • Biological control fungus: Purpureocillium lilacinum.
  • Trap crop for root-knot nematode suppression: Marigold (Tagetes patula).

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Plant parasitic nematodes possess a diagnostic stylet for piercing and sucking plant cell contents.
  • Root-knot nematode (Meloidogyne spp.) is the most economically destructive genus globally, causing distinctive root galls.
  • Golden nematode of potato (Globodera rostochiensis) is a major regulated quarantine pest in India.
  • Integrated nematode management utilizes summer ploughing, organic amendments, bio-control agents, and resistant cultivars.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Endoparasitic Nematode VS Ectoparasitic Nematode

Endoparasites enter and feed inside root tissues (e.g., Pratylenchus), whereas ectoparasites remain entirely in the rhizosphere soil and feed externally using their long stylets.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which specialized feeding structure is characteristic of plant-parasitic nematodes?

Explanation: Plant-parasitic nematodes possess a hollow, protrusible stylet known as a stomatostyle used for piercing cell walls and ingesting nutrients.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Root-knot nematode belongs to which genus?

Explanation: Meloidogyne spp. are well-known sedentary endoparasites that induce gall formation (root-knots) on a wide range of agricultural crops.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

The golden nematode of potato is scientifically known as:

Explanation: Globodera rostochiensis is the potato golden nematode, famous for its persistent encysted stage in soil.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which laboratory technique is most commonly used for extracting active, motile nematodes from soil samples?

Explanation: The Baermann funnel technique uses a water film and gravity to separate active, mobile vermiform nematodes from soil or root debris.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop is widely cultivated as a trap crop to control root-knot nematodes due to root exudates containing alpha-terthienyl?

Explanation: Marigold (Tagetes species) releases nematicidal compounds like alpha-terthienyl from its roots, which actively suppress nematode populations.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which fungal bio-control agent is extensively utilized for parasitizing nematode eggs?

Explanation: Purpureocillium lilacinum is an efficient egg-parasitic fungus used in biological management of root-knot and cyst nematodes.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Multinucleate giant cells formed inside host roots during feeding by Meloidogyne species are known as:

Explanation: Root-knot nematodes induce the formation of giant cells or syncytia, which act as nutrient sinks for the developing nematode.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Cereal cyst nematode is caused by which species?

Explanation: Heterodera avenae is the causal organism of cereal cyst nematode (often known as 'malady disease of cereals').

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which nematode life stage is typically infective for most plant-parasitic species?

Explanation: The second-stage juvenile (J2) is universally recognized as the primary infective stage that enters host plant roots.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Rotylenchulus reniformis is commonly known as the:

Explanation: Rotylenchulus reniformis is called the reniform nematode because mature females assume a characteristic kidney (reniform) shape.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary above-ground symptom of heavy nematode infestation in field crops?

Explanation: Damaged root systems restrict water and nutrient uptake, causing patchy stunting, chlorosis, and severe wilting during hot weather.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical group includes systemic nematicides such as carbofuran?

Explanation: Carbofuran is a well-known carbamate nematicide/insecticide that inhibits acetylcholinesterase in target pests.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Solarization of soil for nematode management is best achieved during which season in northern India?

Explanation: Soil solarization is highly effective during hot summer months (Zaid) when transparent polythene traps solar energy, raising soil temperatures.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which plant parasitic nematode is classified as a migratory endoparasite?

Explanation: Pratylenchus species are migratory endoparasites that move back and forth through root cortex tissues, causing necrotic lesions.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

The phylum Nematoda is characterized by which type of body cavity?

Explanation: Nematodes are pseudocoelomates, possessing a fluid-filled body cavity between the digestive tract and body wall that lacks a true mesodermal peritoneum lining.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Green manuring with which crop helps suppress soil nematode populations due to toxic root breakdown products?

Explanation: Sunn hemp (Crotalaria juncea) is widely used as a green manure crop that releases nematicidal compounds (like monocrotaline) upon incorporation.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which nematode species causes 'ufra' disease in deepwater rice?

Explanation: Ditylenchus angustus is an ectoparasitic nematode causing 'ufra' disease in deepwater and lowland rice ecosystems.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

The transmission of tobacco ringspot virus is mediated by which nematode vector?

Explanation: Xiphinema americanum (dagger nematode) acts as a known vector for plant nepoviruses including tobacco ringspot virus.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which agronomic practice exposes dormant nematode cysts and eggs to ultraviolet rays and desiccation?

Explanation: Deep summer ploughing turns over soil layers, exposing hidden nematodes, egg masses, and cysts to intense solar heat and desiccation.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

In nematology, the term 'polyphagous' describes plant-parasitic nematodes that:

Solution & Rationale: Polyphagous nematodes, such as Meloidogyne incognita, have a broad host range spanning multiple plant families.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which diagnostic symptom distinguishes damage caused by root-knot nematodes from bacterial wilt in solanaceous crops?

Solution & Rationale: Root galls (knots) are uniquely diagnostic for root-knot nematode attack, whereas bacterial wilt shows vascular browning without gall formation.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary mechanism by which crop rotation reduces plant-parasitic nematode populations?

Solution & Rationale: Crop rotation with non-host crops starves hatched juveniles because they cannot complete their life cycle or reproduce.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Which group of nematodes is known as 'dagger nematodes' and serves as vectors for plant viruses?

Solution & Rationale: Xiphinema species (dagger nematodes) are ectoparasites known to transmit nepoviruses.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

What happens to the female Heterodera nematode after fertilization?

Solution & Rationale: The female Heterodera becomes lemon-shaped, and her body wall tans and hardens into a protective cyst enclosing eggs upon death.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following is an advantage of using organic amendments (like neem cake) in nematode management?

Solution & Rationale: Neem cake decomposition releases toxic fatty acids and phenolics while boosting populations of beneficial antagonistic microorganisms.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

The economic threshold level (ETL) for plant-parasitic nematodes refers to:

Solution & Rationale: ETL is the population density at which the cost of artificial control equals the economic loss caused by the pest.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which genus of nematodes is responsible for causing 'root lesion disease' in cereals and fruit trees?

Solution & Rationale: Pratylenchus species are migratory endoparasites commonly called lesion nematodes.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Why are soil fumigants generally restricted in modern sustainable agriculture despite high efficacy against nematodes?

Solution & Rationale: Soil fumigants are hazardous, expensive, and environmentally restricted due to toxicity and soil ecosystem disruption.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the role of the median bulb in the oesophagus of plant-parasitic nematodes?

Solution & Rationale: The muscular median oesophageal bulb acts as a pump to draw food and secrete digestive enzymes through the stylet.
