Botanical Taxonomy, Origin, and Cytogenetics of Major Field Crops

A precise understanding of the botanical classification, origin center, family, and chromosomal constitution (ploidy level) of major field crops forms the foundational basis for competitive agricultural examinations such as UP TGT and PGT Agriculture. Crops are broadly classified into cereals, pulses, oilseeds, and commercial crops.

  • Rice (Paddy): Classified as Oryza sativa, belonging to the family Poaceae (Gramineae). Originating in South/Southeast Asia (Indo-Burma region), it possesses a diploid chromosome number of 2n = 24.
  • Wheat: Common bread wheat is Triticum aestivum (hexaploid, 2n = 6x = 42), originating in South-West Asia (Near East/Turkey). Durum wheat is Triticum durum (tetraploid, 2n = 4x = 28), and emmer wheat is Triticum dicoccum.
  • Maize: Known as the 'Queen of Cereals', Zea mays belongs to family Poaceae with 2n = 20, originating in Central America (Mexico).
  • Chickpea (Gram): Cicer arietinum is a self-pollinated pulse crop belonging to family Fabaceae (Leguminosae) with a diploid chromosome number of 2n = 16, originating in South-Western Asia.
  • Mustard (Indian Mustard): Brassica juncea belongs to family Brassicaceae (Cruciferae) with 2n = 36, representing an amphidiploid species derived from Brassica nigra and Brassica rapa.

Climatic Requirements, Seed Rate, Spacing, and Sowing Geometry

Successful crop establishment dictates yield potential. Agronomic parameters including optimum thermal regimes, seed rates, sowing depths, and crop geometry must be meticulously memorized for competitive examinations.

  • Wheat Sowing Geometry: Requires a seed rate of 100 kg/ha for timely sown conditions, increased to 125 kg/ha for late sowing. Optimal row-to-row spacing is 22.5 cm with a sowing depth of 5 cm.
  • Rice Nursery Management: For the conventional wet bed method, 40-50 kg of seeds are required for one hectare of main field planting. For the System of Rice Intensification (SRI), seed rate is drastically reduced to 5 kg/ha using single seedlings aged 8-12 days at a wide spacing of 25 cm × 25 cm.
  • Maize Plant Population: Optimum plant density is maintained at 65,000 to 75,000 plants per hectare, using a seed rate of 20 kg/ha for grain and 40 kg/ha for fodder, adopting a spacing of 60 cm × 20 cm.
  • Soybean Agronomy: Glycine max requires 75 kg/ha seed rate with a spacing of 45 cm × 5 cm, sown at a depth of 3-4 cm in well-drained loamy soils.

Nutrient Management and Fertilizer Calculations

Balanced fertilization ensures optimal vegetative growth and reproductive efficiency. Calculations regarding recommended doses of Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P₂O₅), and Potassium (K₂O) using commercial fertilizers (Urea, Di-Ammonium Phosphate, Muriate of Potash) are frequently tested in UP PGT examinations.

  • Recommended N:P:K for High Yielding Wheat: 120 kg N, 60 kg P₂O₅, and 40 kg K₂O per hectare. Split application of Nitrogen is mandatory: 50% basal at sowing, 25% at Crown Root Initiation (CRI) stage, and 25% at the tillering/jointing stage.
  • Fertilizer Calculation Formula: To calculate the quantity of commercial fertilizer required, use the formula:
    Quantity of Fertilizer = (Recommended Nutrient Dose / Percentage of Nutrient in Fertilizer) × 100.
  • Urea Calculation Example: If 120 kg N is required per hectare, and Urea contains 46% N, then
    Quantity of Urea = (120 / 46) × 100 = 260.8 kg per hectare.
  • DAP Calculation: If 60 kg P₂O₅ is required using DAP (18% N, 46% P₂O₅), then
    Quantity of DAP = (60 / 46) × 100 = 130.4 kg per hectare. This application also supplies 23.4 kg N (130.4 × 0.18), which must be subtracted from the total nitrogen requirement.

Irrigation Scheduling and Critical Stages of Crop Water Requirement

Water management is critical for grain filling and preventing physiological stress. Irrigation scheduling based on critical growth stages ensures maximum water use efficiency (WUE).

  • Wheat Critical Stages: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) occurring 20-25 days after sowing (DAS) is the most critical stage. Missing irrigation at CRI results in a yield loss of up to 30-40%. Other critical stages include Tillering, Late Jointing, Flowering, Milk stage, and Dough stage (Total 6 irrigations under optimal conditions).
  • Rice Critical Stages: Rice is a semi-aquatic plant requiring continuous submergence (5±2 cm) during active vegetative phases, specifically tillering to flowering. Panicle initiation and flowering are most sensitive to moisture stress.
  • Maize Sensitivity: Tasseling and silking stages are extremely sensitive to moisture stress. Water deficit during silking leads to poor pollination and barren cobs.
  • Chickpea Irrigation: Requires 1-2 light irrigations: first at pre-flowering (branching) and second at pod development stage. Heavy irrigation induces excessive vegetative growth and reduces yield.

Major Insect Pests, Diseases, and Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

Crop protection entails identifying causal organisms, vectors, economic threshold levels (ETL), and applying IPM strategies integrating cultural, mechanical, biological, and chemical controls.

  • Rice Blast: Caused by the fungus Magnaporthe oryzae (anamorph Pyricularia oryzae), characterized by spindle-shaped lesions with grey centers and brown margins. Controlled by seed treatment with Tricyclazole 75% WP @ 0.6 g/kg seed.
  • Brown Planthopper (BPH) in Rice: Nilaparvata lugens damages the crop by sucking sap from the phloem, causing 'hopper burn'. Natural predators include spiders and microvelia. ETL is 5-10 hoppers per hill.
  • Wheat Yellow Rust (Stripe Rust): Caused by Puccinia striiformis, producing yellow pustules in linear rows along leaf veins. Managed through resistant varieties (e.g., PBW 343, DBW 187) and propiconazole sprays.
  • Gram Pod Borer: Helicoverpa armigera is a polyphagous pest attacking chickpea, pigeonpea, and tomato. Larvae feed on foliage and bore directly into pods. Managed using Pheromone traps (5 traps/ha) and NPV (Nuclear Polyhedrosis Virus) @ 250 LE/ha.

Harvesting Indices, Storage Standards, and Post-Harvest Technology

Determining maturity indices prevents grain shattering and qualitative loss during storage. Proper grain moisture content is vital for safe storage and preventing fungal proliferation.

  • Wheat Harvesting Index: Harvested when grain moisture drops to 20-22%, but safe storage moisture content is strictly 10-12%. Yellowing of spikes and hard grains that cannot be dented with a fingernail indicate physiological maturity.
  • Rice Harvesting: Harvested when 80-85% of panicles turn straw-colored and grain moisture is around 20-24%. Safe storage moisture is 12-13% for milled rice and 10-11% for seeds.
  • Maize Maturity: Indicated by the appearance of a black layer at the base of the grain (placental layer). Safe storage moisture is 12%.
  • Storage Pests: Major pests of stored grains include Rice Weevil (Sitophilus oryzae), Khapra Beetle (Trogoderma granarium), and Angoumois Grain Moth (Sitotroga cerealella). Fumigation with Aluminium phosphide (AlP) is standard practice under hermetic storage conditions.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Crown Root Initiation (CRI) in wheat occurs exactly 20-25 days after sowing and is the most critical irrigation stage.
  • The most critical weed competitor in wheat fields is Phalaris minor (known as 'Gulabdanda' or little seed canary grass), controlled by Isoproturon or Clodinafop-propargyl.
  • System of Rice Intensification (SRI) originated in Madagascar by Father Henri de Laulanié in 1961.
  • Paddy crop utilizes Nitrogen predominantly in Ammoniacal (NH₄⁺) form due to submerged anaerobic soil conditions, whereas upland crops prefer Nitrate (NO₃⁻) form.
  • Helicoverpa armigera is a polyphagous pest managed effectively using Nucleopolyhedrovirus (NPV) and HaNPV formulations.
  • Safe moisture content for long-term grain storage of cereals is 10 to 12 percent.
  • Protein content in wheat grain is known as Gluten, which determines baking quality and dough elasticity.
  • Triticale is the world's first man-made cereal, developed by crossing Wheat (Triticum durum) and Rye (Secale cereale).
  • Bt cotton and Bt maize express insecticidal crystal (Cry) proteins derived from the soil bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis.
  • Zinc deficiency in rice causes 'Khaira disease', characterized by chlorotic patches turning brown on older leaves, first reported by Y.N. Nene at Pantnagar.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the botanical family of Wheat and Rice: Both belong to Poaceae, not Fabaceae.
  • Applying all Nitrogen as a basal dose in wheat: Nitrogen must be split across basal, CRI, and tillering stages to prevent leaching losses.
  • Assuming rice prefers Nitrate nitrogen: Rice roots absorb Ammoniacal nitrogen in flooded paddy fields because nitrification is suppressed under anaerobic conditions.

Concept 1

Botanical and Cytogenetic Basis of Crops

Taxonomic classification provides the fundamental identification framework for agronomic species.

  • Ploidy Level: Hexaploid wheat (Triticum aestivum) has 2n = 42 chromosomes, providing superior buffering capacity and adaptability across diverse agro-climatic zones.
Concept 2

Seed Rate and Crop Geometry Optimization

Proper plant population density prevents intra-specific competition and maximizes solar radiation interception.

  • Plant Density: Maintaining 65,000 to 75,000 plants per hectare in maize ensures optimal cob development and grain weight.
Concept 3

Nutrient Stoichiometry and Fertilizer Equivalency

Precise calculation of fertilizer quantities based on nutrient percentage prevents nutrient toxicity and soil degradation.

  • Urea Equivalent: Urea contains 46% Nitrogen, requiring stoichiometric adjustments when substituting organic or complex inorganic sources.
Concept 4

Critical Irrigation Timing and Water Use Efficiency

Targeting irrigation at physiological transition phases guarantees maximum yield per drop of water.

  • CRI Stage: Crown Root Initiation in wheat is the non-negotiable watering window at 21 DAS.
Concept 5

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) Frameworks

Combining cultural, biological, and chemical methods suppresses pest populations below economic injury levels.

  • ETL: Economic Threshold Level dictates the precise pest population density where chemical intervention becomes economically viable.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Amphidiploid (Allopolyploid)
An organism containing two complete sets of chromosomes from each of two different species, such as Indian mustard.
Crown Root Initiation (CRI Stage in Wheat)
The critical physiological phase when permanent crown roots develop at 20-25 days after wheat sowing.
Economic Threshold Level (ETL)
The pest density at which control measures should be initiated to prevent economic damage.
Khaira Disease (Zinc Deficiency in Rice)
A physiological disorder in rice caused by Zinc deficiency, identified by chlorotic and necrotic leaf spots.
Triticale (Triticum × Secale)
The first synthetic cereal created by crossing wheat and rye.
Phalaris minor (Little Seed Canary Grass)
A noxious weed mimicking wheat plants during early vegetative stages, developing resistance to isoproturon.
System of Rice Intensification (SRI Method)
An agronomic methodology for increasing rice yields using young single seedlings, wider spacing, and intermittent irrigation.
Gluten (Prolamin Protein Complex)
The storage protein composite found in wheat flour responsible for dough elasticity and baking quality.
Black Layer Maturity (Placental Black Layer)
The physiological maturity marker in maize grain indicating cessation of dry matter accumulation.
Hopper Burn (Phloem Sap Depletion)
The destructive symptom caused by heavy infestation of Brown Planthopper (Nilaparvata lugens) in rice fields.
Nucleopolyhedrovirus (NPV Biopesticide)
A naturally occurring baculovirus used specifically for controlling lepidopteran pests like Helicoverpa armigera.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Rice botanical name: Oryza sativa (2n = 24, Diploid).

  • CRI stage in wheat occurs at 20-25 Days After Sowing.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Wheat and rice are primary cereal staples with strict adherence to critical irrigation windows like CRI in wheat.
  • Fertilizer calculations require stoichiometric conversion based on nutrient percentage in Urea, DAP, and MOP.
  • IPM strategies integrate resistant cultivars, biological agents (NPV, Trichoderma), and judicious chemical application.
  • Safe storage moisture content across major cereals must be maintained at 10-12% to prevent microbial and insect proliferation.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

C3 Plants VS C4 Plants

C3 plants (wheat, rice) undergo photorespiration and have lower water use efficiency compared to C4 plants (maize, sugarcane).

Diploid Wheat VS Hexaploid Wheat

Einkorn is diploid (2n=14), whereas modern bread wheat (Triticum aestivum) is hexaploid (2n=42).

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosomal number (2n) of common bread wheat (Triticum aestivum)?

Explanation: Common bread wheat (Triticum aestivum) is a hexaploid species with 2n = 6x = 42 chromosomes. Einkorn is diploid (2n=14) and Durum wheat is tetraploid (2n=28).

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is the most critical stage for irrigation in wheat?

Explanation: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) occurring 20-25 DAS is the absolute most critical irrigation stage in wheat. Missing water here causes severe yield reduction.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Khaira disease in rice is caused by the deficiency of which nutrient?

Explanation: Khaira disease in rice is caused by Zinc deficiency, characterized by chlorotic patches turning brown on older leaves. It is corrected by applying ZnSO₄.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard seed rate of timely sown wheat under normal irrigated conditions?

Explanation: The recommended seed rate for timely sown wheat is 100 kg/ha. For late sown conditions, it is increased to 125 kg/ha due to reduced tillering time.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which weed is known as a serious mimicry weed in wheat fields?

Explanation: Phalaris minor is a classic mimicry weed in wheat during early vegetative stages because its morphology closely resembles wheat seedlings.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the origin center of maize (Zea mays)?

Explanation: Maize originated in Central America, specifically Mexico, from its wild ancestor teosinte (Zea mays ssp. parviglumis).

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended N:P:K fertilizer dose for high-yielding dwarf wheat varieties in India?

Explanation: The standard recommended fertilizer dose for high-yielding dwarf wheat varieties is 120 kg N, 60 kg P₂O₅, and 40 kg K₂O per hectare.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

How much quantity of Urea (containing 46% N) is required to supply 120 kg of pure Nitrogen per hectare?

Explanation: Calculation: (120 / 46) × 100 = 260.86 kg of Urea per hectare.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the causal organism of Rice Blast disease?

Explanation: Rice blast is caused by the fungus Magnaporthe oryzae (anamorph Pyricularia oryzae), one of the most destructive fungal diseases in rice cultivation worldwide.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the safe moisture content for long-term grain storage of cereal crops?

Explanation: Safe storage moisture content for cereals like wheat, rice, and maize is 10 to 12 percent. Higher moisture leads to mold growth and insect infestation.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest causes 'hopper burn' in rice crops?

Explanation: Brown Planthopper (BPH), Nilaparvata lugens, sucks sap from rice stems, causing plants to dry up rapidly, a condition known as 'hopper burn'.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the seed rate required in the System of Rice Intensification (SRI) method per hectare?

Explanation: In the System of Rice Intensification (SRI), only 5 kg of seed is required per hectare because single, young seedlings (8-12 days old) are planted at wide spacing (25 cm × 25 cm).

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which physiological indicator determines the maturity of maize grain for harvesting?

Explanation: The appearance of a placental black layer at the base of the maize kernel indicates physiological maturity and cessation of nutrient flow.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the family of Indian Mustard (Brassica juncea)?

Explanation: Indian Mustard belongs to the family Brassicaceae (formerly Cruciferae). It is an amphidiploid species.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which biopesticide is extensively used for the management of Helicoverpa armigera?

Explanation: Nuclear Polyhedrosis Virus (HaNPV) is a highly specific bio-control agent used effectively against the gram pod borer (Helicoverpa armigera).

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What type of photosynthetic pathway is exhibited by Maize?

Explanation: Maize is a C4 plant exhibiting high photosynthetic efficiency, higher optimum temperatures, and superior water use efficiency compared to C3 cereals.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical is commonly used for fumigation of stored grains against insect pests like Khapra beetle?

Explanation: Aluminium phosphide tablets release phosphine (PH₃) gas upon reacting with moisture in sealed silos or godowns, effectively killing all stages of stored grain pests.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the critical stage of irrigation in Maize crop?

Explanation: Tasseling and silking stages in maize are extremely sensitive to water stress. Moisture deficit here prevents pollen shedding and silk receptivity, causing barrenness.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Triticale is an intergeneric hybrid obtained by crossing which two crops?

Explanation: Triticale is the world's first man-made cereal, developed by crossing Wheat (Triticum species) and Rye (Secale cereale).

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate the exact quantity of DAP (18% N, 46% P₂O₅) and Urea (46% N) required to supply 60 kg N and 60 kg P₂O₅ per hectare, accounting for Nitrogen supplied by DAP.

Solution & Rationale: Step 1: DAP required = (60 / 46) × 100 = 130.4 kg. Step 2: N supplied by 130.4 kg DAP = 130.4 × 0.18 = 23.4 kg N. Step 3: Remaining N required = 60 - 23.4 = 36.6 kg N. Step 4: Urea required = (36.6 / 46) × 100 = 79.5 kg (approx 78.3 kg based on exact fractions).

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT/PGT Standard

Why is Ammoniacal (NH₄⁺) nitrogen preferred over Nitrate (NO₃⁻) nitrogen in lowland flooded paddy soils?

Solution & Rationale: In flooded paddy fields, the submerged soil layer is anaerobic. Applied nitrates are rapidly reduced to nitrogen gas via denitrification and lost to the atmosphere. Thus, ammoniacal fertilizers (like Ammonium sulphate) are retained effectively.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

Match the following fungal diseases with their respective causal organisms: I. Wheat Yellow Rust - A. Magnaporthe oryzae II. Rice Blast - B. Puccinia striiformis III. Loose Smut of Wheat - C. Ustilago tritici

Solution & Rationale: Wheat Yellow Rust is caused by Puccinia striiformis, Rice Blast is caused by Magnaporthe oryzae, and Loose Smut of Wheat is caused by Ustilago tritici (an internally seed-borne fungal pathogen).

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary objective of practicing 'stale seedbed technique' in weed management?

Solution & Rationale: The stale seedbed technique involves irrigating land to induce weed seed germination, followed by shallow tillage or non-selective herbicide application (e.g., Paraquat) to eradicate weeds before sowing the main crop.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following agronomic practices characterizes the System of Rice Intensification (SRI)?

Solution & Rationale: SRI involves alternate wetting and drying (AWD) rather than continuous submergence, planting young single seedlings (8-12 days old), wider square spacing, and using cono-weeders to aerate the soil.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the economic threshold level (ETL) significance in Integrated Pest Management (IPM)?

Solution & Rationale: ETL is the population density of a pest at which control measures should be initiated to prevent the pest population from reaching the Economic Injury Level (EIL).

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Which cytogenetic event led to the evolution of hexaploid bread wheat (Triticum aestivum)?

Solution & Rationale: Hexaploid bread wheat (AABBDD, 2n=42) evolved through natural hybridization between tetraploid wheat (AABB, Triticum dicoccoides/durum) and the wild goat grass Aegilops tauschii (DD), followed by chromosome doubling.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Identify the correct statement regarding hybrid vigor (heterosis) in maize:

Solution & Rationale: Single cross hybrids (A × B) exhibit maximum uniformity, vigor, and yield potential. However, seeds harvested from hybrid crops cannot be saved for subsequent planting because heterosis declines in the F2 generation due to genetic segregation.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the mode of action of Isoproturon herbicide commonly used in wheat?

Solution & Rationale: Isoproturon is a selective systemic herbicide belonging to the urea family that inhibits Photosystem II, disrupting photosynthetic electron transport in susceptible grass and broadleaf weeds.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary reason for lodging in cereal crops subjected to heavy nitrogen fertilization and wind storms?

Solution & Rationale: Excessive nitrogen application stimulates rapid internodal elongation and lush vegetative growth, which results in thin culm walls, top-heavy plants, and inadequate mechanical stem strength, leading to lodging during wind and rain storms.
