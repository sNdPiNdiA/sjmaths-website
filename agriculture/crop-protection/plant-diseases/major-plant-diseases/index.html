Introduction to Plant Pathology and Disease Taxonomy

Plant pathology (Fitopatologia) is the scientific study of diseases in plants caused by infectious agents (pathogens) and physiological factors (abiotic stresses). Understanding the taxonomy, morphology, and life cycles of plant pathogens is paramount for devising effective crop protection regimes in Indian agriculture.

  • Fungi: Eukaryotic, spore-bearing organisms lacking chlorophyll. Cell walls are primarily composed of chitin and \beta-glucans. Reproduction occurs both sexually and asexually. Examples include Puccinia graminis f. sp. tritici and Magnaporthe oryzae.
  • Bacteria: Prokaryotic, unicellular microorganisms lacking a distinct nuclear membrane. Most plant-pathogenic bacteria are rod-shaped (bacilli) and possess polar or peritrichous flagella. Examples include Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae and Ralstonia solanacearum.
  • Viruses: Sub-microscopic, obligate intracellular parasites composed of nucleic acid (DNA or RNA) enclosed in a protein coat (capsid). They depend entirely on host cellular machinery for replication and are frequently transmitted by arthropod vectors (aphids, whiteflies, leafhoppers).
  • Phytoplasmas: Wall-less, pleomorphic prokaryotes belonging to the class Mollicutes, inhabiting the phloem sieve elements of plants and typically transmitted by leafhoppers.

Cereal Crop Diseases: Paddy, Wheat, and Maize

Cereals form the backbone of Indian food security. Diseases affecting these crops lead to substantial economic losses if not managed proactively through resistant varieties, cultural practices, and timely fungicidal applications.

  • Rice Blast: Caused by the fungus Magnaporthe oryzae (anamorph Pyricularia oryzae). Symptoms include spindle-shaped lesions on leaves with grey centers and brown margins, neck rot, and node infection. Favored by high relative humidity (90-95%), intermittent rainfall, and excessive nitrogenous fertilization. Managed by seed treatment with tricyclazole @ 1 g/kg and foliar spray of tricyclazole 75% WP @ 0.6 g/L.
  • Wheat Rusts: Three distinct types affect wheat: Black or Stem Rust (Puccinia graminis f. sp. tritici), Brown or Leaf Rust (Puccinia triticina), and Yellow or Stripe Rust (Puccinia striiformis). Stem rust produces dark reddish-brown telia/uredinia on stems and leaves; stripe rust forms linear yellow pustules along leaf veins. Managed through resistant varieties (e.g., PBW 343, WH 1105) and propiconazole 25% EC spray @ 1 mL/L.
  • Bunt and Smuts of Wheat: Karnal Bunt is caused by Tilletia indica (syn. Neovossia indica), imparting a characteristic fishy odor due to trimethylamine. Covered smut of barley and loose smut of wheat (Ustilago tritici) are internally seed-borne, controlled via hot water treatment or systemic seed treatment with carboxin 75% WP @ 2.5 g/kg seed.

Pulse and Oilseed Crop Diseases: Gram, Pigeonpea, Mustard, and Groundnut

Pulses and oilseeds are susceptible to destructive soil-borne and foliar pathogens that severely restrict yield potential under fluctuating climatic conditions.

  • Fusarium Wilt of Pigeonpea and Chickpea: Caused by the soil-borne fungus Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. ciceri (in chickpea) and F. oxysporum f. sp. udum (in pigeonpea). Symptoms include sudden yellowing, drooping, and vascular browning. Managed through deep summer ploughing, crop rotation with cereals, bio-priming seeds with Trichoderma viride @ 10 g/kg, and cultivating resistant varieties (e.g., Pusa 362, IPC 2004-52).
  • White Rust and Alternaria Blight of Mustard: White rust is caused by Albugo candida, producing white chalky pustules on leaves and inflorescence (staghead formation). Alternaria blight is caused by Alternaria brassicae, causing concentric ring lesions. Managed by seed dressing with metalaxyl @ 6 g/kg and spraying mancozeb 75% WP @ 2 kg/ha.
  • Tikka Disease of Groundnut: Caused by two distinct fungal species: Early leaf spot (Cercospora arachidicola) producing dark brown spots with yellow halos on the upper leaf surface, and Late leaf spot (Nidula arachidis / Cercosporidium personatum) producing smaller, darker spots restricted to the lower leaf surface. Managed using chlorothalonil 75% WP @ 2 g/L or carbendazim 50% WP @ 1 g/L.

Cash Crop and Horticultural Diseases: Sugarcane, Potato, and Tomato

Horticultural and cash crops require intensive plant protection due to high economic returns and vulnerability to vascular and viral diseases.

  • Red Rot of Sugarcane: Caused by the ascomycetous fungus Colletotrichum falcatum (teleomorph Glomerella tucumanensis). Symptoms include drooping of leaves, internal pith reddening interspersed with white transverse patches, and a characteristic alcoholic smell. Managed by selecting disease-free seed sets from certified nurseries, hot water treatment of setts at 52°C for 30 minutes, and crop rotation.
  • Late Blight of Potato: Caused by the oomycete Phytophthora infestans. Famous for causing the Irish Famine (1845). Symptoms include water-soaked dark brown lesions on leaves and stems with white downy fungal growth on the lower leaf surface during humid weather. Managed proactively by spraying preventive fungicides like mancozeb @ 2.5 kg/ha followed by curative metalaxyl-M based formulations.
  • Tomato Leaf Curl Virus (ToLCV): A begomovirus transmitted by the whitefly vector Bemisia tabaci. Symptoms include severe leaf curling, puckering, yellowing, and severe stunting with drastic reduction in fruit yield. Managed by vector control using imidacloprid 17.8% SL @ 0.3 mL/L, raising nursery under insect-proof nets, and growing resistant varieties (e.g., Pusa Sadabahar).

Epidemiology, Disease Triangle, and Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

Epidemiology studies the factors influencing disease development in plant populations over time and space. Successful plant disease management relies on understanding the interaction of components within the disease framework.

  • The Disease Triangle: Interaction between three essential components: a susceptible Host Plant, a Virulent Pathogen, and a Favorable Environment. Eliminating any one component prevents disease development. Adding Time transforms the triangle into a Disease Tetrahedron.
  • Disease Forecasting: Utilizing meteorological data (temperature, relative humidity, rainfall duration) to predict disease outbreaks, particularly for late blight of potato (using Beaumont scale or Smith period) and wheat rusts.
  • Integrated Disease Management (IDM): Combining regulatory (quarantine), cultural (crop sanitation, summer ploughing, crop rotation), physical (solarization, hot water seed treatment), biological (Trichoderma spp., Pseudomonas fluorescens), and chemical (systemic and contact fungicides) methods in an ecologically sound and economically viable manner.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the causal organism of late blight of potato (Phytophthora infestans - Oomycete) with early blight (Alternaria solani - True fungus).
  • Mistaking Karnal bunt (Tilletia indica, partial bunt affecting individual grains) with loose smut of wheat (Ustilago tritici, destroying the entire ear head into black spore mass).
  • Assuming phytoplasmas can be cultured on artificial nutrient agar media; phytoplasmas are obligate biotrophic prokaryotes inhabiting plant phloem.
  • Attributing viral diseases to fungal or bacterial pathogens; remember that viruses require living host cells and insect vectors for transmission.

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Fungal Plant Pathology & Pathogenesis

Fungi constitute the largest group of plant pathogens. Pathogenesis involves spore germination, appressorium formation, penetration via natural openings or wounds, and colonization of host tissues.

  • Appressorium: Specialized swelling on a germ tube or hypha that aids in attaching to the host surface and puncturing the cuticle.
  • Haustorium: Specialized absorptive outgrowth of fungal hyphae penetrating host plant cells to absorb nutrients without killing them immediately.
Concept 2

Bacterial and Phytoplasmal Plant Diseases

Bacterial plant pathogens enter plants through hydathodes, stomata, or wounds. They produce extracellular polysaccharides (EPS) and cell-wall degrading enzymes (pectinases) leading to tissue maceration and wilting.

  • Kresek Phase: The severe seedling wilt phase of bacterial blight of rice caused by Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae.
  • Phytoplasma: Wall-less prokaryotes residing in phloem sieve tubes, causing symptoms like phyllody, witches' broom, and little leaf.
Concept 3

Viral Diseases and Vector Dynamics

Plant viruses lack cellular structure and rely on vectors for transmission across fields. Vector management is often the most critical component of viral disease control.

  • Begomovirus: Largest genus of plant-infecting geminiviruses possessing circular ssDNA genomes, transmitted exclusively by whiteflies (Bemisia tabaci).
  • Persistent Transmission: Pathogen circulates through the vector's body (hemolymph and salivary glands) before inoculation into a new host plant.
Concept 4

Disease Epidemiology and Management Strategies

Epidemiology governs disease forecasting and the timing of prophylactic plant protection measures. Integration of biological agents, resistant cultivars, and judicious chemical application ensures sustainable disease control.

  • Disease Triangle: The simultaneous presence of a susceptible host, virulent pathogen, and favorable environment required for disease outbreak.
  • Biological Control: Use of antagonistic microorganisms such as Trichoderma viride and Pseudomonas fluorescens to suppress soil-borne pathogens.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Biotroph (Obligate Parasite)
An organism that obtains nutrients only from living host cells without causing immediate host death.
Necrotroph (Facultative Parasite / Saprophyte)
A pathogen that kills host cells before or during colonization and feeds on dead tissue.
Urediniospore (Repeat Spore Stage)
The repeating asexual dikaryotic spore stage of rust fungi responsible for rapid secondary spread.
Teliospore (Overwintering Spore)
The resting or overwintering spore stage in rust and smut fungi where karyogamy occurs.
Chlorosis (Yellowing of Green Tissue)
The abnormal yellowing of plant leaves resulting from chlorophyll destruction or suppression.
Necrosis (Tissue Death)
The localized death of plant cells and tissues, appearing brown or black.
Hyperplasia (Cell Proliferation)
An abnormal increase in the rate of cell division resulting in galls or swellings.
Hypertrophy (Cell Enlargement)
Abnormal enlargement of plant cells leading to structural distortion.
Systemic Infection (Generalized Pathogen Spread)
Infection where the pathogen spreads throughout the entire plant vascular system.
Hypersensitive Reaction (Rapid Host Cell Death)
A rapid defense response where host cells surrounding the infection site die quickly to starve the pathogen.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Fungal pathogens possess chitinous cell walls and reproduce via diverse spores (conidia, ascospores, basidiospores).
  • Bacteria are prokaryotic rods causing wilts, leaf spots, and cankers, controlled by bactericides like streptomycin.
  • Viruses and phytoplasmas rely heavily on insect vectors such as whiteflies, aphids, and leafhoppers for dissemination.
  • The disease triangle comprises host, pathogen, and environment; modifying any side prevents disease.
  • Integrated Pest Management (IPM) combines cultural, biological, physical, and chemical tactics for sustainable crop health.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Covered Smut VS Loose Smut

Covered smut spores remain enclosed in membranes until threshed, whereas loose smut spores are powdery and disperse freely in wind before harvest.

Systemic Fungicide VS Contact Fungicide

Systemic fungicides are absorbed and translocated inside plant tissues, whereas contact fungicides remain on the plant surface and act protectively.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following plant diseases is historically associated with the Great Irish Famine of 1845?

Explanation: The Great Irish Famine of 1845 was triggered by late blight of potato, caused by the oomycete pathogen Phytophthora infestans, which devastated potato crops across Ireland.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The causal organism of Karnal bunt of wheat is:

Explanation: Karnal bunt of wheat is caused by Tilletia indica (syn. Neovossia indica), which partially infects wheat grains and releases trimethylamine, creating a fishy smell.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary vector responsible for transmitting Tomato Leaf Curl Virus (ToLCV)?

Explanation: Tomato Leaf Curl Virus is a begomovirus transmitted persistently by the silverleaf whitefly, Bemisia tabaci.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Sugarcane red rot is caused by which of the following fungal species?

Explanation: Sugarcane red rot is caused by Colletotrichum falcatum. It is characterized by internal reddening of pith broken by transverse white patches.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The 'Kresek' phase is a devastating symptom associated with which crop disease?

Explanation: The kresek phase refers to the rapid wilting and drying of young rice seedlings caused by systemic infection of Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Citrus canker, characterized by raised corky lesions on leaves and fruits, is caused by:

Explanation: Citrus canker is a bacterial disease caused by Xanthomonas citri, spreading via wind-driven rain and leaf miner wounds.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Little leaf of brinjal is a classic example of a plant disease caused by:

Explanation: Little leaf of brinjal is caused by a phytoplasma (wall-less prokaryote) transmitted by the leafhopper Hishimonus phycitis.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which fungicide is specifically and highly effective against Rice Blast disease caused by Magnaporthe oryzae?

Explanation: Tricyclazole is a systemic fungicide widely recommended for controlling rice blast by inhibiting melanin biosynthesis in the appressorium.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Tikka disease of groundnut involves two distinct fungal species causing leaf spots. They are:

Explanation: Tikka disease is caused by Cercospora arachidicola (early leaf spot with yellow halos) and Cercosporidium personatum (late leaf spot).

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The three components of the classic plant disease triangle are:

Explanation: The disease triangle consists of a Susceptible Host, a Virulent Pathogen, and a Favorable Environment.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Staghead formation in cruciferous crops like mustard is a characteristic symptom of:

Explanation: White rust caused by Albugo candida causes severe hypertrophy and malformation of mustard inflorescences, known as 'staghead'.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following cereal rusts produces dark reddish-brown telia/uredinia predominantly on stems?

Explanation: Puccinia graminis f. sp. tritici causes black stem rust of wheat, producing dark reddish-brown pustules on stems and leaf sheaths.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which bio-control agent is extensively utilized for the management of soil-borne fungal pathogens such as Fusarium and Pythium?

Explanation: Trichoderma viride is a widely used fungal bio-control agent that antagonizes soil-borne plant pathogens through mycoparasitism and antibiosis.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The Bengal Famine of 1943, which devastated rice crops, was caused by:

Explanation: The Bengal Famine was precipitated by an epiphytotic of brown spot of rice caused by Bipolaris oryzae (syn. Helminthosporium oryzae).

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which plant pathogen group lacks a cell wall and has a pleomorphic cellular structure?

Explanation: Phytoplasmas belong to Mollicutes and lack rigid cell walls, rendering them pleomorphic.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary resting or overwintering spore stage in many rust fungi?

Explanation: Teliospores are dark, thick-walled resting or overwintering spores produced by rust fungi.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical formulation is standard for seed treatment to control loose smut of wheat?

Explanation: Carboxin (Vitavax) is a systemic fungicide widely used for seed treatment against internally seed-borne loose smut of wheat.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the principal mode of reproduction in asexual plant-pathogenic fungi?

Explanation: Conidia are asexual spores produced by fungi that facilitate rapid dissemination during the growing season.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which environmental condition most strongly favors the development of potato late blight?

Explanation: Phytophthora infestans thrives in wet, cool, and highly humid environments with persistent cloud cover and intermittent rain.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Yellow rust or stripe rust of wheat is caused by:

Explanation: Puccinia striiformis causes yellow or stripe rust, manifesting as striking yellow pustules arranged linearly along wheat leaf veins.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Analyze the following statements regarding wheat rusts in India: 1. Black stem rust is caused by Puccinia graminis f. sp. tritici. 2. Brown rust is favored by cooler temperatures than yellow rust. 3. Yellow rust produces linear pustules on leaves. Which of the above statements are correct?

Solution & Rationale: Statement 1 is correct (black rust is caused by P. graminis). Statement 3 is correct (yellow rust produces linear stripe pustules). Statement 2 is incorrect because yellow rust requires cooler temperatures (10-15°C) than brown rust (15-20°C).

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Match the following crop diseases with their respective causal oomycete/fungal agents: A. Potato Late Blight - 1. Albugo candida, B. Mustard White Rust - 2. Phytophthora infestans, C. Rice Blast - 3. Magnaporthe oryzae, D. Sugarcane Red Rot - 4. Colletotrichum falcatum. Correct matching code:

Solution & Rationale: Potato late blight is caused by Phytophthora infestans (A-2); Mustard white rust by Albugo candida (B-1); Rice blast by Magnaporthe oryzae (C-3); Sugarcane red rot by Colletotrichum falcatum (D-4).

Test Question 3 of 10 PGR Standard

Which of the following systemic fungicides inhibits ergosterol biosynthesis in fungal cell membranes and is widely recommended for rust and powdery mildew control?

Solution & Rationale: Propiconazole is a DMI (demethylation inhibitor) systemic fungicide that interferes with ergosterol biosynthesis in fungi, effectively controlling rusts and powdery mildews.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary mechanism of action of Trichoderma spp. in controlling phytopathogenic fungi in soil?

Solution & Rationale: Trichoderma species suppress plant pathogens through multiple mechanisms including mycoparasitism (coiling and digesting hyphae), production of antibiotics (viridin, gliotoxin), and intense competition.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

Which vector transmits Rice Tungro Virus (RTV), a combination of Tungro bacilliform virus and Tungro spherical virus?

Solution & Rationale: Rice Tungro Virus is transmitted in a semi-persistent manner by green leafhoppers, particularly Nephotettix virescens.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

In integrated disease management, the term 'Disease Triangle' can be expanded into a 'Disease Tetrahedron' by the addition of which factor?

Solution & Rationale: Adding 'Time' to the host-pathogen-environment triangle transforms it into a tetrahedron, accounting for the duration and timing of disease interactions.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following plant diseases is characterized by internal pith reddening interspersed with white transverse bands and an alcoholic odor?

Solution & Rationale: Sugarcane red rot (Colletotrichum falcatum) exhibits internal reddening of pith with characteristic white cross-bands and an acidic/alcoholic odor.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which bactericide is commonly tank-mixed with copper oxychloride for effective management of bacterial plant diseases such as citrus canker and bacterial blight?

Solution & Rationale: Streptocycline (a combination of streptomycin sulfate and tetracycline hydrochloride) is a standard agricultural bactericide used alongside copper oxychloride.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Which symptom distinguishes early leaf spot (Cercospora arachidicola) from late leaf spot (Cercosporidium personatum) in groundnut?

Solution & Rationale: Early leaf spot (Cercospora arachidicola) produces larger spots with yellow halos visible on the upper leaf surface, whereas late leaf spot (Cercosporidium personatum) produces darker spots prominent on the lower surface.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the optimal temperature range for the germination of urediniospores of wheat stem rust (Puccinia graminis)?

Solution & Rationale: Urediniospores of Puccinia graminis germinates best at relatively warm temperatures of 20-25°C, unlike yellow rust which favors cooler conditions.
