Introduction to Agricultural Entomology and Insect Classification

Agricultural entomology deals with the study of insects that affect crop production, stored products, and livestock. Insects belong to the phylum Arthropoda, class Insecta, characterized by a segmented body divided into three distinct regions: head, thorax, and abdomen. They possess three pairs of jointed legs and typically one or two pairs of wings.

  • Phylum: Arthropoda
  • Class: Insecta
  • Key Orders in Agriculture: Lepidoptera (moths and butterflies), Coleoptera (beetles), Hemiptera (true bugs), Homoptera (aphids, jassids, whiteflies), Thysanoptera (thrips), and Diptera (flies).
  • Economic Threshold Level (ETL): The population density of a pest at which control measures must be applied to prevent the pest population from reaching the Economic Injury Level (EIL).
  • Economic Injury Level (EIL): The lowest population density that causes economic damage, where the cost of artificial control equals the value of crop damage prevented.

Major Insect Pests of Cereal Crops (Rice, Wheat, and Maize)

Cereal staple crops are heavily attacked by a complex of borers, sap-suckers, and defoliators throughout their phenological stages. Understanding their larval habits and vector status is vital for competitive exams.

  • Rice Stem Borer (Scirpophaga incertulas): Monophagous pest of rice. The larva bores into the central shoot, causing the drying of the central whorl known as 'dead heart' during the vegetative stage and 'white earhead' at the reproductive stage.
  • Brown Plant Hopper (BPH) (Nilaparvata lugens): A sap-sucking pest that causes 'hopper burn' where patches of the crop turn brown and dry up. It also acts as a natural vector for viral diseases such as ragged stunt and grassy stunt.
  • Rice Gundhi Bug (Leptocorisa oratorius): Emits an offensive odor. Adults and nymphs suck the milky sap from developing grains, leading to empty or chalky, shriveled grains.
  • Wheat Termite (Microtermes obesus): A subterranean pest that attacks wheat roots and stems, leading to the drying of plants which easily pull out of the soil, causing patchy crop stands.
  • Maize Stem Borer (Chilo partellus): The caterpillar bores into the stem, causing dead hearts in young maize and sorghum plants and leaving shot-holes in leaves.

Major Insect Pests of Pulse and Oilseed Crops

Pulses and oilseeds are susceptible to pod borers, leaf miners, and aphids, which directly impact protein and oil-rich reproductive structures.

  • Gram Pod Borer (Helicoverpa armigera): A polyphagous pest attacking chickpea, pigeonpea, tomato, and cotton. Larvae feed on foliage and bore into pods, inserting their anterior half inside while the posterior remains outside, feeding on developing grains.
  • Spotted Pod Borer (Maruca vitrata): Infests pulses like pigeonpea and cowpea, webbing flowers and pods together and feeding within.
  • Mustard Aphid (Lipaphis erysimi): Both nymphs and adults suck sap from tender leaves, inflorescences, and pods of cruciferous oilseeds (mustard, toria), causing curling of leaves, stunted growth, and sooty mold development due to honeydew excretion.
  • Painted Bug (Bagrada hilaris): Sucks sap from mustard leaves and pods, injecting toxic saliva that causes wilting and premature drying of plants.

Major Insect Pests of Cash Crops (Sugarcane and Cotton)

Cash crops require intensive pest management due to high economic returns and susceptibility to specialized monophagous borers and sucking complexes.

  • Sugarcane Early Shoot Borer (Chilo infuscatellus): Attacks sugarcane during the tillering phase, causing dead hearts that can be easily pulled out, characterized by a foul smell.
  • Sugarcane Top Borer (Scirpophaga excerptalis): Attacks the growing tip of mature sugarcane, causing bunchy top appearance and mid-rib tunneling.
  • Cotton Pink Bollworm (Pectinophora gossypiella): The larvae feed on developing cotton bolls and seeds, causing rosettes flowers and damaged lint. Diapause is a key feature of its life cycle.
  • Tobacco Caterpillar / Armyworm (Spodoptera litura): Polyphagous defoliator; young larvae feed gregariously on chlorophyll, skeletonizing leaves, while older larvae act as nocturnal solitary cutworms.

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) Strategies in Field Crops

IPM is an ecosystem-based strategy that focuses on long-term prevention of pests through a combination of techniques such as biological control, habitat manipulation, modification of cultural practices, and use of resistant varieties.

  • Cultural Practices: Crop rotation, deep summer plowing to expose pupae, rogueing of infested plant parts, and adjusting sowing dates.
  • Mechanical Control: Hand picking of egg masses and larvae, setting up light traps, pheromone traps for mass trapping and monitoring (e.g., Helicoverpa and Spodoptera lures @ 5 traps/ha).
  • Biological Control: Conservation and release of parasitoids like Trichogramma chilonis against lepidopteran borers, predators like ladybird beetles (Coccinella septempunctata) against aphids, and microbial insecticides such as Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) and NPV (Nuclear Polyhedrosis Virus).
  • Chemical Control: Targeted application of safe, selective insecticides only when pest populations exceed the Economic Threshold Level (ETL), minimizing hazards to beneficial fauna.

ETL vs EIL Memory Rule
ETL is the Early Trigger Level; EIL is the Economic Injury Level.

ETL always comes before EIL. ETL is the warning bell that triggers insecticide application before the pest population reaches the damaging EIL threshold.

  • The yellow stem borer of rice (Scirpophaga incertulas) causes 'dead hearts' at the vegetative stage and 'white earheads' at the reproductive stage.
  • Brown Plant Hopper (Nilaparvata lugens) causes 'hopper burn' symptoms in rice fields and transmits grassy stunt and ragged stunt viruses.
  • The gram pod borer (Helicoverpa armigera) is polyphagous and exhibits cannibalistic behavior during larval development.
  • Sugarcane early shoot borer (Chilo infuscatellus) causes dead hearts that can be easily pulled out, emitting a characteristic foul smell.
  • Mustard aphid (Lipaphis erysimi) is the most destructive pest of rapeseed and mustard, multiplying rapidly during cloudy and humid weather.
  • Economic Threshold Level (ETL) is always lower than the Economic Injury Level (EIL) to allow time for control measures before economic loss occurs.
  • Pheromone traps are extensively used for monitoring and mass trapping of lepidopteran pests using specific sex pheromones.
  • Cotton pink bollworm (Pectinophora gossypiella) overwinters as fully grown larvae in a state of diapause inside double seeds or soil debris.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Error: Confusing 'dead heart' in rice with 'dead heart' in sugarcane.
    Correction: Rice dead heart is caused by Scirpophaga incertulas, whereas sugarcane early shoot borer dead heart is caused by Chilo infuscatellus.
  • Error: Assuming ETL and EIL are synonymous.
    Correction: ETL is the action threshold (lower population density), while EIL is the lowest population density that causes economic loss (higher population density).

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Major Insect Pests of Field Crops: Biology, Damage Symptoms, and Integrated Pest Management

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Economic Threshold Level and Economic Injury Level

Economic decision-making in pest management relies on quantitative thresholds established through pest-crop loss relationship studies.

  • ETL (Economic Threshold Level): The pest density at which control action should be initiated to prevent the pest population from reaching the EIL.
  • EIL (Economic Injury Level): The lowest pest population density that will cause economic damage equal to the cost of artificial control measures.
Concept 2

Morphological Damage Symptoms in Cereals

Cereal borers and hoppers inflict specific, identifiable visual symptoms on host plants.

  • Dead Heart: Drying of the central shoot caused by stem borer larval feeding during the pre-flowering stage.
  • White Earhead: Completely chaffy, erect, white panicles devoid of grain setting due to stem borer attack at panicle initiation.
  • Hopper Burn: Circular patches of dried, straw-colored, dead rice plants caused by heavy sap-sucking by BPH.
Concept 3

Categories of Pests Based on Host Range

Insect pests are classified according to their host specificity and feeding habits in agroecosystems.

  • Monophagous Pests: Feed on a single plant species or closely related genera (e.g., Rice stem borer).
  • Oligophagous Pests: Feed on a narrow range of plants within a specific botanical family (e.g., Mustard aphid on Cruciferae).
  • Polyphagous Pests: Feed on a wide variety of host plants across multiple families (e.g., Helicoverpa armigera).
Concept 4

Principles of Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

IPM integrates multiple control tactics compatibly to keep pest populations below economic injury levels while safeguarding environmental health.

  • Cultural Control: Manipulating the environment through crop rotation, deep plowing, and sanitation.
  • Biological Control: Utilization of natural enemies, predators, parasitoids, and pathogens.
  • Chemical Control: Judicious and need-based application of synthetic or botanical pesticides.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Economic Threshold Level (ETL)
The pest population density at which control measures must be applied to prevent economic loss.
Economic Injury Level (EIL)
The lowest pest population density that causes economic damage equal to the cost of control.
Dead Heart (Central shoot necrosis)
Symptoms of stem borer larval feeding where the central shoot dries up and turns brown.
White Earhead (Chaffy panicle)
Empty, white panicles in rice caused by stem borer attack at the panicle emergence stage.
Hopper Burn (Severe sap depletion)
Drying and browning of plant patches in rice caused by massive feeding of Brown Plant Hopper.
Diapause (Insect dormancy)
A period of suspended physiological development in insects during unfavorable environmental conditions.
Monophagous (Single-host feeder)
An insect pest that feeds exclusively on a single plant species or genus.
Polyphagous (Multi-host feeder)
An insect pest capable of feeding on diverse host plant species across multiple families.
Parasitoid (Insect parasite)
An insect whose larvae consume and kill their host insect (e.g., Trichogramma species).
Pheromone Trap (Sex attractant trap)
A monitoring and control device using synthetic sex hormones to attract and trap male moths.
Sooty Mold (Capnodium species growth)
Black fungal growth developing on honeydew excreted by sap-sucking insects like aphids and whiteflies.

  • Major insect pests cause severe economic losses in cereal, pulse, oilseed, and cash crops.
  • Stem borers in rice and sugarcane produce characteristic 'dead heart' and 'white earhead' symptoms.
  • Sucking pests like aphids and brown plant hoppers deplete plant sap and can vector viral diseases.

  • IPM emphasizes the harmonization of cultural, mechanical, biological, and chemical methods.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Dead Heart in Rice VS Dead Heart in Sugarcane

Rice dead heart is caused by Scirpophaga incertulas, whereas sugarcane early shoot borer dead heart is caused by Chilo infuscatellus.

ETL VS EIL

ETL is the action threshold level triggering pest management intervention, whereas EIL is the break-even economic damage level.

Question 1 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following insect pests causes 'white earhead' symptoms in rice?

Explanation: The yellow stem borer of rice, Scirpophaga incertulas, causes 'dead hearts' during the vegetative stage and 'white earheads' (empty, white panicles) during the reproductive/panicle emergence stage.

Question 2 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

The phenomenon known as 'hopper burn' in rice fields is caused by the feeding activity of:

Explanation: Brown Plant Hopper (BPH), Nilaparvata lugens, feeds voraciously at the base of rice plants, sucking plant sap and causing circular patches of dried, straw-colored plants known as 'hopper burn'.

Question 3 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the relationship between Economic Threshold Level (ETL) and Economic Injury Level (EIL)?

Explanation: ETL is set lower than EIL to provide a safety margin so that control measures can be applied before the pest population reaches the density that causes economic injury.

Question 4 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

The gram pod borer, Helicoverpa armigera, exhibits which of the following characteristic larval behaviors?

Explanation: Helicoverpa armigera larvae are known for their cannibalistic behavior; when two or more larvae meet inside a pod or on a plant, the stronger larva frequently consumes the other.

Question 5 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Sugarcane early shoot borer (Chilo infuscatellus) is most active and destructive during which crop phase?

Explanation: Sugarcane early shoot borer attacks sugarcane primarily during the early vegetative stage (tillering phase, 0 to 120 days after planting), causing dead hearts.

Question 6 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Mustard aphid (Lipaphis erysimi) damage is most severe under which weather conditions?

Explanation: Mustard aphid populations multiply rapidly during cloudy, humid, and relatively cool winter weather conditions, leading to severe crop infestation.

Question 7 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a polyphagous insect pest?

Explanation: Helicoverpa armigera is a classic polyphagous pest, feeding on numerous host plants across diverse botanical families including Fabaceae, Solanaceae, and Malvaceae.

Question 8 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

The presence of sooty mold on mustard leaves attacked by aphids is due to:

Explanation: Aphids excrete sugary honeydew onto plant surfaces, providing a substrate for the growth of black saprophytic fungi known as sooty mold, which reduces photosynthetic capacity.

Question 9 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Cotton pink bollworm (Pectinophora gossypiella) overwinters primarily in which stage?

Explanation: Pink bollworm survives unfavorable winter conditions through larval diapause, commonly inside fused or double cotton seeds and plant debris.

Question 10 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which biological control agent is widely used against lepidopteran stem borers?

Explanation: Trichogramma chilonis is an efficient egg parasitoid widely mass-produced and released in crop fields for the biological control of lepidopteran stem borers and fruit borers.

Question 11 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

The offensive odor associated with the rice gundhi bug (Leptocorisa oratorius) is produced by:

Explanation: Gundhi bugs possess thoracic stink glands that secrete volatile chemical compounds emitting a distinct, pungent odor when disturbed.

Question 12 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest acts as a natural vector for Rice Tungro Virus?

Explanation: The green leafhopper, Nephotettix virescens, is the principal vector transmitting Rice Tungro Bacilliform Virus (RTBV) and Rice Tungro Spherical Virus (RTSV).

Question 13 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary damage symptom caused by maize stem borer (Chilo partellus) on young maize plants?

Explanation: Maize stem borer larvae feed on young whorl leaves, creating characteristic transverse 'shot-holes' and subsequently boring into the stem to cause dead hearts.

Question 14 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Deep summer plowing is recommended as an effective cultural practice primarily to:

Explanation: Deep summer plowing exposes hidden pupae, larvae, and eggs of soil-inhabiting pests (such as cutworms, borers, and heliothis pupae) to solar heat and natural predators like birds.

Question 15 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect order includes beetles and weevils characterized by hardened forewings called elytra?

Explanation: Order Coleoptera comprises beetles and weevils, distinguished by their heavily sclerotized forewings known as elytra, which protect the membranous hindwings and abdomen.

Question 16 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

The painted bug (Bagrada hilaris) attacks which group of crops predominantly?

Explanation: Painted bug (Bagrada hilaris) is a notorious pest of cruciferous crops (Mustard, Cabbage, Cauliflower, Radish), sucking sap from leaves and pods.

Question 17 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary mode of action of Nuclear Polyhedrosis Virus (NPV) when used as a bio-pesticide?

Explanation: NPV must be ingested by the target caterpillar; once inside the gut, viral occlusion bodies dissolve, infecting tissues and causing systemic breakdown and larval death.

Question 18 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following parameters defines the Economic Injury Level (EIL)?

Explanation: EIL is defined mathematically and economically as the lowest pest density that causes economic damage, where the cost of control equals the monetary value of crop yield saved.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

A farmer observes circular patches of straw-colored, dried plants in a paddy field accompanied by heavy basal infestation of small brown insects. Identify the pest and the symptom.

Solution & Rationale: Brown Plant Hopper (Nilaparvata lugens) feeds at the base of rice plants, resulting in circular patches of dried plants known as hopper burn.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

In Integrated Pest Management, which sequence represents the correct logical progression of decision-making?

Solution & Rationale: IPM begins with monitoring pest populations, comparing observations against ETL, selecting appropriate cultural, biological, or chemical tactics, and evaluating outcomes.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

Match the following insect pests with their respective target crops correctly: 1. Scirpophaga incertulas 2. Chilo infuscatellus 3. Lipaphis erysimi 4. Pectinophora gossypiella

Solution & Rationale: Scirpophaga incertulas is the rice yellow stem borer; Chilo infuscatellus is the sugarcane early shoot borer; Lipaphis erysimi is the mustard aphid; Pectinophora gossypiella is the cotton pink bollworm.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Why is Economic Threshold Level (ETL) maintained significantly below the Economic Injury Level (EIL) in crop protection?

Solution & Rationale: ETL serves as an early operational action trigger, giving farmers sufficient time to arrange and apply control measures before the pest population reaches the damaging EIL.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following insect orders is characterized by piercing-sucking mouthparts forming a rostrum or beak?

Solution & Rationale: True bugs belonging to the order Hemiptera possess specialized piercing-sucking mouthparts adapted for extracting plant sap or animal fluids.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

The primary symptom of attack by sugarcane top borer (Scirpophaga excerptalis) during later crop growth stages is:

Solution & Rationale: Sugarcane top borer attacks the growing apex during elongation stages, resulting in mid-rib tunneling, shot-holes in emerging leaves, and a characteristic bunchy top appearance.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary objective of mass trapping using sex pheromone traps in pest management?

Solution & Rationale: Pheromone traps are deployed for surveillance and mass trapping of male moths, thereby lowering mating frequency and reducing subsequent larval populations.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which developmental stage of Helicoverpa armigera is responsible for direct economic damage to chickpea and pigeonpea crops?

Solution & Rationale: The larval stage of Helicoverpa armigera feeds aggressively on foliage, flowers, and developing pods, causing direct economic yield loss.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

In the context of insect biology, define 'diapause'.

Solution & Rationale: Diapause is a physiological dormancy state enabling insects to survive unfavorable environmental conditions such as extreme cold or drought.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following cultural practices helps minimize subterranean termite damage in wheat crops?

Solution & Rationale: Using well-decomposed FYM prevents termite attraction, whereas un-decomposed organic matter attracts subterranean termites (Microtermes obesus) to the field.
