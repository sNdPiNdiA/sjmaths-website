Principles and Scope of Weed Management

Weeds are defined as plants growing out of place or unwanted vegetation that interfere with agricultural operations, compete for essential environmental resources, and diminish crop yield and quality. JETHRO TULL coined the term 'weed' in his 1731 publication 'Horse-hoeing Husbandry' where he also introduced the concept of horse-hoeing to control weeds through inter-cultivation.

  • Yield Losses: Weeds cause the highest proportion of potential agricultural losses globally compared to insects, pathogens, and rodents combined. In India, annual crop losses due to weeds alone are estimated at 33-37%.
  • Competition Mechanisms: Weeds compete intensely with cultivated crops for solar radiation, soil moisture, space, and available plant nutrients (especially nitrogen and phosphorus), often exhibiting higher nutrient-absorption capacity in early developmental stages.
  • Allelopathy: Many weed species exude secondary metabolites (allelochemicals) from roots, leaves, or decaying residues that inhibit germination and growth of adjacent crop plants. Examples include Parthenium hysterophorus and Cyperus rotundus.

Classification and Biology of Agricultural Weeds

A sound understanding of weed taxonomy, life cycles, and reproductive strategies is vital for designing effective control measures. Weeds are classified based on life span, botanical affinity, habitat, and cotyledonary structure.

  • Annual Weeds: Complete their life cycle within one year or season. Examples include Phalaris minor (winter annual) and Echinochloa crus-galli (summer annual).
  • Biennial Weeds: Require two years to complete their life cycle, growing vegetatively in the first year and flowering/seeding in the second. Example: Cichorium intybus.
  • Perennial Weeds: Persist for more than two years, propagating through seeds as well as vegetative structures like tubers, rhizomes, bulbs, and stolons. Examples include Cyperus rotundus (purple nutsedge) and Cynodon dactylon (Bermuda grass).
  • Botany and Taxonomy: Weeds are broadly divided into Grasses (Monocots with narrow leaves, e.g., Dactyloctenium aegyptium), Sedges (Solid triangular stems, absence of ligules, e.g., Cyperus esculentus), and Broad-leaved weeds (Dicots with net-like venation, e.g., Chenopodium album, Amaranthus viridis).

Herbicides: Chemistry, Classification, and Selectivity

Herbicides are chemical substances utilized to kill or inhibit the growth of target weed species. They are classified based on their application timing, mode of action, and selectivity.

  • Selectivity: Selective herbicides destroy specific weed flora without causing phytotoxic damage to the main crop (e.g., 2,4-D in wheat). Non-selective herbicides destroy all green vegetation indiscriminately (e.g., Glyphosate, Paraquat).
  • Application Timing: Pre-plant incorporation (PPI) applied before sowing and mixed into soil (e.g., Fluchloralin); Pre-emergence (PE) applied after sowing but before crop and weed emergence (e.g., Pendimethalin, Atrazine); Post-emergence (PoE) applied after both crop and weeds have emerged (e.g., Bispyribac-sodium, Metsulfuron-methyl).
  • Mode of Action: Divided into systemic (translocated throughout plant tissues via symplast/apoplast, e.g., Glyphosate) and contact (destroying only contacted plant parts, e.g., Paraquat). Photosynthesis inhibitors include triazines (Atrazine), while cell division inhibitors include dinitroanilines (Pendimethalin).

Parasitic Weeds and Mimicry Weeds in Indian Agriculture

Parasitic weeds depend partially or completely on host crop plants for water and nutrients through specialized feeding structures known as haustoria. They are categorized based on their site of attachment and chlorophyll content.

  • Root Parasites: Obligate or facultative parasites attached to host roots. Total root parasite: Orobanche spp. (Broomrape) affecting Solanaceous crops (tobacco, tomato, potato). Semi-root parasite: Striga spp. (Witchweed) attacking cereals (sorghum, maize, sugarcane).
  • Stem Parasites: Parasitize aerial plant parts. Total stem parasite: Ccuta spp. (Dodder) attacking lucerne, niger, and various ornamentals. Semi-stem parasite: Loranthus spp. attacking fruit trees and forest canopy.
  • Weed Mimicry: Certain weeds evolve morphological similarities to cultivated crops, making manual rogueing difficult during early growth stages. Classic example: Phalaris minor (Pharaoh's grass / Canary grass) mimicking wheat (Triticum aestivum) in morphology and seed size.

Integrated Weed Management (IWM) and Economic Threshold Level

Integrated Weed Management combines cultural, mechanical, biological, and chemical methods in a harmonious manner to maintain weed population densities below levels that cause economic injury.

  • Cultural Methods: Crop rotation, summer deep ploughing, stale seedbed technique, competitive crop varieties, proper plant geometry, and adequate fertilizer management.
  • Mechanical and Physical Methods: Hand weeding, hoeing, inter-cultivation with weeders, mowing, burning, and soil solarization using transparent polyethylene sheets.
  • Biological Methods: Utilization of natural enemies (insects, pathogens) to control specific weed populations. Notable success: Control of Parthenium hysterophorus using the leaf-feeding beetle Zygogramma bicolorata, and Salvinia molesta using the weevil Cyrtobagus salviniae.
  • Economic Threshold Level (ETL): The weed population density at which the cost of weed control equals the monetary value of yield gain. Managing weeds beyond ETL prevents economic loss and herbicide resistance.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Jethro Tull coined the term 'weed' in his book 'Horse-hoeing Husbandry' published in 1731.
  • Phalaris minor is a classic example of crop mimicry in wheat fields, often controlled using Clodinafop-propargyl or Sulfosulfuron.
  • Parthenium hysterophorus (Congress grass / Carrot grass) is controlled biologically by the beetle Zygogramma bicolorata.
  • Cuscuta reflexa is a total stem parasite that reproduces profusely and attaches to host plants via haustoria.
  • Stale seedbed technique involves germinating and destroying early flushes of weeds before planting the main crop.
  • Atrazine is a selective pre-emergence herbicide commonly used in maize and sugarcane crops.
  • The critical period of crop-weed competition in most field crops is generally the first 30 to 45 days after sowing (DAS).
  • Fluchloralin is applied as a pre-plant incorporation (PPI) herbicide in soybean and groundnut.
  • Bispyribac-sodium is a broad-spectrum post-emergence herbicide used for weed control in transplanted and direct-seeded rice.
  • Striga is a semi-root parasite that secretes germination stimulants (strigolactones) from host roots.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Cuscuta as a root parasite; Cuscuta is actually a total stem parasite.
  • Assuming 2,4-D is a non-selective herbicide; 2,4-D is a selective systemic herbicide used against broad-leaved weeds in cereal crops.
  • Believing that weeding can be delayed until flowering without yield penalty; the critical weed-free period requires intervention in early vegetative stages (30-45 DAS).
  • Classifying Phalaris minor as a dicot; it is a monocot grass weed.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Advanced Weed Management in Field Crops

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Definition and Impact of Weeds

Weeds are unwanted plants that cause severe economic damage by competing for growth factors, harboring pests, and lowering produce quality.

  • Yield Loss: Average annual crop yield reduction due to weeds in India is estimated between 33% and 37%.
  • Allelopathy: Biochemical inhibition of crop plants by weed exudates.
Concept 2

Classification of Weeds

Weeds are categorized based on life span (annual, biennial, perennial) and botanical features (grasses, sedges, broad-leaved).

  • Perennial Weeds: Propagate via vegetative structures like rhizomes and tubers (e.g., Cyperus rotundus).
  • Sedges: Characterized by solid triangular stems and absence of ligules.
Concept 3

Parasitic Weeds

Parasites obtain water and nutrients from host crops through haustoria, divided into total/semi and root/stem categories.

  • Total Root Parasite: Orobanche species attacking Solanaceae family crops.
  • Total Stem Parasite: Cuscuta species attacking lucerne and niger.
Concept 4

Herbicide Classification and Application

Herbicides are chemical compounds classified by application time (PPI, PE, PoE) and selectivity (selective vs. non-selective).

  • Pre-Emergence: Applied after sowing but prior to crop emergence (e.g., Pendimethalin).
  • Systemic Herbicide: Translocated throughout the plant vascular system (e.g., Glyphosate).

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Weed (Plant out of place)
Any plant that interferes with agricultural objectives, human activities, or natural ecosystems.
Allelopathy (Biochemical inhibition)
Detrimental effect of one plant species on another through the release of chemical compounds into the environment.
Haustorium (Parasitic root structure)
Specialized absorptive organ of a parasitic plant that penetrates host tissues to extract water and nutrients.
Stale Seedbed (Bursts of weed germination)
Agronomic technique where weed seeds are allowed to germinate with irrigation/rain and subsequently destroyed prior to crop planting.
Critical Period (Critical weed-free period)
The specific time span in crop growth cycle when weed competition causes maximum yield reduction.
Herbicide Selectivity (Differential toxicity)
The ability of a chemical to kill target weed species without causing phytotoxic damage to the crop.
Pre-Emergence Herbicide (PE Application)
Herbicide applied after crop planting but before the emergence of crop seedlings and weeds.
Systemic Herbicide (Translocated Herbicide)
Chemical absorbed by plant foliage or roots and translocated throughout the plant vascular system.
Contact Herbicide (Non-translocated chemical)
Herbicide that destroys only the plant tissues with which it comes into direct physical contact.
Economic Threshold Level (ETL in Weed Science)
Weed density at which cost of control equals the value of the yield saved.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Cuscuta VS Orobanche

Cuscuta is a total stem parasite, whereas Orobanche is a total root parasite.

Pendimethalin VS Paraquat

Pendimethalin is a selective pre-emergence herbicide, whereas Paraquat is a non-selective contact herbicide.

Who coined the term 'weed' and introduced horse-hoeing husbandry?

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a total root parasite?

Explanation: Orobanche cernua is a total root parasite. Cuscuta is a total stem parasite, Striga is a semi-root parasite, and Loranthus is a semi-stem parasite.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the critical period of crop-weed competition in most annual field crops?

Explanation: The first 30 to 45 days after sowing (DAS) is universally recognized as the critical period of crop-weed competition during which weed interference inflicts maximum yield penalty.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Phalaris minor in wheat fields is a classic example of:

Explanation: Phalaris minor exhibits strong morphological mimicry to wheat, making manual identification difficult before earhead emergence.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect is successfully utilized for the biological control of Parthenium hysterophorus?

Explanation: Zygogramma bicolorata (Mexican leaf-feeding beetle) is employed in biological control programs against Parthenium hysterophorus.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Fluchloralin is typically applied as which type of herbicide application?

Explanation: Fluchloralin is a dinitroaniline herbicide applied as a pre-plant incorporation (PPI) to prevent volatilization and photochemical decomposition.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which herbicide is widely used as a selective pre-emergence application in maize?

Explanation: Atrazine is a selective pre-emergence herbicide extensively used in maize and sugarcane.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Cuscuta reflexa is known botanically as a:

Explanation: Cuscuta reflexa is a total stem parasite lacking chlorophyll, depending entirely on host stem tissues via haustoria.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical is classified as a non-selective contact herbicide?

Explanation: Paraquat is a non-selective contact herbicide that acts rapidly in the presence of light, destroying green plant tissues.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of the 'stale seedbed' technique?

Explanation: The stale seedbed technique reduces the weed seed bank in the topsoil by flushing out weeds prior to crop sowing.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Striga asiatica is a semi-root parasite that parasitizes which of the following crops?

Explanation: Striga asiatica (Witchweed) is a semi-root parasite that attacks tropical cereals like sorghum, maize, sugarcane, and millets.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Sedges are distinguished from grasses and broad-leaved weeds by having:

Explanation: Sedges belong to the family Cyperaceae and are characterized by solid triangular stems and lack of ligules.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which term describes the harmful biochemical effect of one plant on another?

Explanation: Allelopathy refers to the chemical inhibition of one species by another through secondary metabolites.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Bispyribac-sodium is highly effective as a post-emergence herbicide in which crop?

Explanation: Bispyribac-sodium is a broad-spectrum post-emergence herbicide used for controlling grasses, sedges, and broad-leaved weeds in rice.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does ETL stand for in modern weed management?

Explanation: ETL stands for Economic Threshold Level, representing the weed density at which control measures prevent economic loss exceeding treatment costs.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following weeds reproduces primarily through underground tubers and bulbs?

Explanation: Cyperus rotundus produces extensive underground tuber chains connected by rhizomes, rendering mechanical pulling ineffective.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which herbicide category is applied after crop sowing but prior to weed and crop emergence?

Explanation: Pre-emergence herbicides are applied after planting but before seedlings emerge, targeting germinating weed seeds.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Chenopodium album (Bathua) belongs to which botanical family?

Explanation: Chenopodium album belongs to the family Chenopodiaceae (now often subsumed under Amaranthaceae in modern APG systems, but classically Chenopodiaceae).

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which method involves covering moist soil with transparent polyethylene sheets to kill weed seeds using solar heat?

Explanation: Soil solarization utilizes transparent polyethylene mulch during hot summer months to elevate soil temperatures and kill weed seeds and soil pathogens.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Glyphosate is classified as a:

Explanation: Glyphosate is a non-selective systemic herbicide translocated throughout the plant to destroy perennial and annual weeds.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Match the herbicides with their respective modes of application: A. Fluchloralin - 1. Post-emergence B. Pendimethalin - 2. Pre-plant incorporation C. Bispyribac-sodium - 3. Pre-emergence Correct matching code is:

Solution & Rationale: Fluchloralin is applied as Pre-plant incorporation (PPI), Pendimethalin as Pre-emergence (PE), and Bispyribac-sodium as Post-emergence (PoE).

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

Why is deep summer ploughing considered an effective cultural weed management practice in northern plains of India?

Solution & Rationale: Deep summer ploughing exposes weed seeds, rhizomes, and tubers to scorching summer temperatures, desiccating and destroying them.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following weed species is recognized as a total stem parasite on crops like Lucerne and Niger?

Solution & Rationale: Cuscuta reflexa wraps around host stems forming haustoria, functioning as a total stem parasite.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

In the context of herbicide selectivity, what biochemical mechanism prevents injury to maize when treated with Atrazine?

Solution & Rationale: Maize possesses high levels of glutathione S-transferase enzymes that detoxify Atrazine rapidly through glutathione conjugation, conferring selectivity.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary reason why Phalaris minor has developed widespread resistance against certain herbicides like Isoproturon in Indo-Gangetic plains?

Solution & Rationale: Continuous monocropping and reliance on Isoproturon selected for resistant biotypes of Phalaris minor across northwestern India.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which weed is effectively controlled by the bio-control agent Zygogramma bicolorata?

Solution & Rationale: Zygogramma bicolorata is a host-specific leaf-feeding beetle imported for biological suppression of Parthenium hysterophorus.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which among the following is a classic example of a sedge weed possessing triangular stems?

Solution & Rationale: Cyperus rotundus (purple nutsedge) is a sedge featuring solid triangular stems.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What characterizes the action of a contact herbicide like Paraquat?

Solution & Rationale: Contact herbicides like Paraquat destroy cell membranes upon direct contact but do not translocate significantly through vascular tissues.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

The critical period of weed competition in transplanted rice typically occurs during which interval?

Solution & Rationale: In transplanted rice, keeping the field weed-free during the first 30 to 45 days after transplanting (DAT) prevents significant grain yield reduction.
