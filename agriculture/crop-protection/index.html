Introduction to Crop Protection and Economic Threshold Level

Crop protection is a multi-disciplinary science dealing with the management of insect pests, plant pathogens, nematodes, and weeds that constrain crop yields. In modern agronomy, chemical, biological, physical, and cultural methods are integrated under the umbrella of Integrated Pest Management (IPM).

  • Economic Injury Level (EIL): The lowest population density of a pest that will cause economic damage, where the cost of artificial control equals the value of damage prevented.
  • Economic Threshold Level (ETL): The pest density at which control measures should be applied to prevent an increasing pest population from reaching the Economic Injury Level (EIL).
  • General Equilibrium Position (GEP): The average population density of a pest over a long period, around which the pest population fluctuates, independent of temporary control measures.

Major Insect Pests of Cereal and Pulse Crops

Insect pests inflict heavy losses during vegetative and reproductive stages of crops. Correct identification of life cycles, mouthparts, and damage symptoms is vital for UP TGT/PGT examinations.

  • Rice Stem Borer (Scirpophaga incertulas): Causes 'dead heart' symptoms during the vegetative stage and 'white ear head' during the reproductive stage. Larvae are monophagous on rice.
  • Gram Pod Borer (Helicoverpa armigera): A polyphagous pest attacking chickpea, pigeonpea, cotton, and tomato. Larvae bore into developing pods and feed on grains by inserting the anterior half of their body.
  • Brown Planthopper (Nilaparvata lugens): Sucks sap from the base of rice plants, causing 'hopper burn'. It also acts as a vector for viral diseases such as ragged stunt and grassy stunt.

Fungal, Bacterial, and Viral Plant Pathogens

Plant pathology investigates causal agents, disease cycles, and epidemiological factors influencing epidemics in field crops.

  • Rice Blast (Magnaporthe oryzae): Characterized by spindle-shaped lesions with grey centers and brown margins on leaves, nodes, and panicles. Favored by high relative humidity and intermittent rainfall.
  • Wheat Rusts: Caused by Puccinia species (Puccinia graminis tritici for stem rust, Puccinia striiformis for stripe rust, and Puccinia triticina for leaf rust). These are heteroecious macrocyclic fungi requiring alternate hosts like Berberis or Thalictrum.
  • Citrus Canker: Caused by the bacterium Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. citri. Symptoms include raised corky lesions on leaves, twigs, and fruits, spread by citrus leaf miner and splashing rain.

Principles of Integrated Pest and Disease Management (IPDM)

IPDM emphasizes the harmonious use of multiple tactics to keep pest populations below the Economic Injury Level while minimizing environmental risks.

  • Cultural Practices: Deep summer ploughing to expose pupae, crop rotation with non-host crops, adjusted sowing dates, and rogueing of infected plants.
  • Biological Control: Conservation and augmentation of natural enemies such as parasitoids (Trichogramma spp.) and predators (ladybird beetles, chrysopids), alongside microbial biopesticides like Bacillus thuringiensis and Beauveria bassiana.
  • Chemical Control: Rational application of fungicides, insecticides, and bactericides. Strict adherence to waiting periods and pre-harvest intervals (PHI) is mandatory.

Weed Science and Herbicide Classifications

Weeds compete with crops for light, water, nutrients, and space, often acting as alternate hosts for insect pests and plant pathogens.

  • Classification by Life Cycle: Annuals (e.g., Phalaris minor), biennials (e.g., Wild carrot), and perennials (e.g., Cyperus rotundus).
  • Selectivity: Selective herbicides (e.g., 2,4-D, Pendimethalin) target specific weed flora without harming the main crop, whereas non-selective herbicides (e.g., Glyphosate, Paraquat) destroy all green vegetation.
  • Mode of Action: Contact herbicides destroy plant tissue at the site of application, while systemic herbicides are absorbed and translocated throughout the vascular system of the weed.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Economic Threshold Level (ETL) is always lower than the Economic Injury Level (EIL) to allow time for control measures before economic loss occurs.
  • Dead heart and white ear head in rice are characteristic damage symptoms caused by Scirpophaga incertulas (Yellow Stem Borer).
  • Puccinia graminis tritici is a heteroecious macrocyclic fungus completing its sexual cycle on Berberis vulgaris as an alternate host.
  • Hopper burn in rice fields is caused by heavy infestation and feeding by the Brown Planthopper (Nilaparvata lugens).
  • Citrus canker is caused by the bacterium Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. citri and is effectively managed by pruning infected twigs and spraying streptomycin sulfate.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Economic Injury Level (EIL) with Economic Threshold Level (ETL); remember EIL is the damage level, while ETL is the action threshold before reaching EIL.
  • Assuming all plant pathogenic fungi are homoecious; rust fungi like Puccinia are heteroecious, requiring two different host species to complete their life cycle.

Concept 1

Economic Threshold Level and Pest Dynamics

Understanding pest population dynamics ensures timely intervention before economic damage occurs.

  • ETL: Action threshold population density triggering pest control implementation.
  • EIL: Lowest pest population density that causes economic damage equal to control costs.
Concept 2

Insect Pests and Damage Symptoms

Correct diagnostic association of insect pests with their characteristic feeding injury is critical for field agronomy.

  • Dead Heart: Vegetative stage damage in rice caused by stem borer larvae.
  • Hopper Burn: Rapid drying and yellowing of rice clumps due to heavy sap-sucking by planthoppers.
Concept 3

Fungal Rust Pathogens and Host Specificity

Rust diseases of cereals exhibit complex life cycles involving multiple spore stages and alternate hosts.

  • Heteroecious Rust: Requires two distinct host species to complete its five spore stages (e.g., Puccinia graminis).
  • Urediniospores: Repeating asexual spores responsible for rapid secondary spread of rust epidemics.
Concept 4

Integrated Weed Management Systems

Combining cultural, mechanical, and chemical approaches maintains weed population below competitive thresholds.

  • Selective Herbicide: Chemical agent targeting specific weed species without injuring the primary crop.
  • Critical Period of Crop-Weed Competition: Initial 30 to 45 days after sowing when weed interference causes maximum yield reduction.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Economic Threshold Level (ETL)
The pest population density at which control measures must be applied to prevent economic injury.
Economic Injury Level (EIL)
The lowest pest population density that causes economic damage equal to the cost of control.
Heteroecious Fungus (Heteroecium Pathogen)
A parasitic fungus that requires two different host species to complete its life cycle.
Macrocyclic Rust (Macrocyclic Puccinia)
A rust fungus producing up to five distinct spore stages during its life cycle.
Hopper Burn (Planthopper Phytotoxemia)
Complete drying and browning of crop patches caused by heavy sap removal by planthoppers.
Dead Heart (Central Shoot Necrosis)
Symptoms of stem borer attack where the central leaf whorl dries up and turns brown.
Systemic Pesticide (Systemic Agrochemical)
A chemical absorbed by the plant and translocated internally through xylem and phloem.
Contact Pesticide (Contact Agrochemical)
A pesticide that controls pests upon direct physical contact without internal plant translocation.
Bio-pesticide (Microbial Control Agent)
Naturally occurring organisms or their metabolites used to suppress pest populations.
Rogueing (Pathogen Eradication)
The removal and destruction of diseased or off-type plants from a crop field.

  • Puccinia graminis tritici utilizes Berberis vulgaris as its alternate host.
  • Yellow planthopper causes hopper burn in paddy fields.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

ETL VS EIL

ETL is the action trigger level, whereas EIL is the break-even damage level.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does the Economic Threshold Level (ETL) represent in pest management?

Explanation: ETL is defined as the pest density at which control measures must be initiated to prevent the population from rising to the Economic Injury Level (EIL).

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which symptom is characteristic of rice stem borer attack during the vegetative stage?

Explanation: During the vegetative stage, larvae of Scirpophaga incertulas feed inside the central shoot, causing it to dry up and form a 'dead heart'.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which organism serves as the alternate host for wheat stem rust (Puccinia graminis tritici)?

Explanation: Puccinia graminis tritici is a heteroecious fungus that completes its pycnial and aecial stages on Berberis vulgaris (barberry).

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Hopper burn in paddy crops is primarily caused by the attack of:

Explanation: Nilaparvata lugens (Brown Planthopper) sucks sap from the plant base in large numbers, resulting in the rapid drying of crop patches known as hopper burn.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Citrus canker is caused by which type of phytopathogen?

Explanation: Citrus canker is caused by the bacterium Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. citri, producing raised corky lesions on plant tissues.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following herbicides is classified as a selective herbicide?

Explanation: 2,4-D is a selective systemic herbicide used to control broad-leaf weeds in cereal crops without damaging monocot crops.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary role of urediniospores in rust fungi?

Explanation: Urediniospores are repeating asexual spores that spread rapidly via wind, initiating secondary infection cycles during the crop season.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The critical period of crop-weed competition in most annual field crops generally corresponds to:

Explanation: The initial 30 to 45 days after crop emergence is the critical period when weed interference causes maximum reduction in yield.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest is notorious for boring into chickpea pods and consuming developing grains?

Explanation: Helicoverpa armigera larvae bore circular holes into chickpea and pigeonpea pods, inserting their anterior body while feeding on seeds.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does GEP stand for in insect population ecology?

Explanation: General Equilibrium Position (GEP) refers to the average population density around which a pest population fluctuates over long periods.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which pathogen causes blast disease in rice?

Explanation: Rice blast is caused by the ascomycete fungus Magnaporthe oryzae, characterized by spindle-shaped leaf lesions.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical is widely used as a seed treatment fungicide against loose smut of wheat?

Explanation: Carbendazim is a systemic fungicide effective against internally seed-borne pathogens such as loose smut of wheat (Ustilago tritici).

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the nature of damage caused by white ear head in rice?

Explanation: When stem borers attack rice at the panicle emergence stage, the panicle turns white and becomes chaffy, known as white ear head.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which plant parasitic nematode causes root-knot disease in vegetables?

Explanation: Meloidogyne species (root-knot nematodes) induce characteristic galls or knots on the roots of susceptible host crops.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which type of pesticide application is designed to be absorbed by roots and translocated throughout the plant?

Explanation: Soil drenching with systemic chemicals allows roots to absorb the active ingredient and transport it acropetally to protect new growth.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a microbial biopesticide derived from a bacterium?

Explanation: Bacillus thuringiensis is a soil-dwelling bacterium that produces crystal proteins toxic to lepidopteran and dipteran insect pests.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary diagnostic symptom of loose smut of wheat?

Explanation: Loose smut replaces the entire grain and chaff with a powdery mass of black teliospores that disperse in the wind.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which factor primarily influences the rapid spread of airborne fungal spores like rusts and blast?

Explanation: Fungal pathogens causing airborne diseases require free moisture, high relative humidity, and wind currents for spore dissemination and germination.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does the term 'Rogueing' signify in crop protection and seed production?

Explanation: Rogueing is the systematic removal and destruction of infected, weak, or off-type plants to maintain crop purity and prevent disease spread.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which agency in India recommends national standards for pesticide registration and residue limits?

Explanation: The CIBRC regulates the manufacture, import, sale, transport, distribution, and use of insecticides under the Insecticides Act, 1968.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

If the Economic Injury Level (EIL) is calculated at 25 insects per square meter and the cost-benefit ratio dictates early action, at what approximate percentage should the Economic Threshold Level (ETL) typically be set?

Solution & Rationale: ETL is maintained below EIL (usually 60-80% of EIL) to provide adequate lead time for applying control measures before the population reaches economic damage levels.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

A heteroecious macrocyclic rust fungus completes its dikaryotic stages on the primary cereal host and its monokaryotic pycnial/aecial stages on an alternate host. Which combination is correct?

Solution & Rationale: Puccinia graminis tritici is a classic heteroecious macrocyclic rust utilizing wheat as the primary host and Berberis vulgaris as the alternate host.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following insect-pest and symptom pairs is incorrectly matched?

Solution & Rationale: Leptocorisa acuta is the rice gundhi bug, which feeds on milky grains, not dead heart in wheat. Dead heart is caused by stem borers in paddy/maize.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary mode of action of systemic fungicides like Carbendazim?

Solution & Rationale: Carbendazim is a systemic benzimidazole fungicide that enters plant tissues and interferes with fungal microtubule formation during mitosis.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which agronomic practice is most effective in breaking the life cycle of soil-borne pupae and perennial weeds?

Solution & Rationale: Deep summer ploughing exposes hidden insect pupae, weed tubers, and pathogen propagules to solar radiation and predatory birds, effectively reducing initial inoculum.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What characterizes the damage caused by brown planthopper (Nilaparvata lugens) in rice fields?

Solution & Rationale: Brown planthopper feeding causes circular dying patches (hopper burn) as plants lose turgor and lodge due to heavy sap extraction.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Which spore stage of Puccinia graminis is responsible for over-summering in the plains of India?

Solution & Rationale: Teliospores are thick-walled resting spores that help the fungus survive unfavorable hot summer conditions in the plains of India.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which weed is considered a noxious grassy weed infesting wheat fields globally and mimicking wheat plants during early vegetative growth?

Solution & Rationale: Phalaris minor (littleseed canary grass) is a major grassy weed in wheat whose morphological similarity to wheat during early vegetative stages makes manual weeding difficult.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

In Integrated Pest Management (IPM), the use of pheromone traps primarily serves which function?

Solution & Rationale: Pheromone traps use species-specific sex pheromones to monitor pest arrival, assess population density, and assist in mass trapping male adults.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which bacterial disease of fruit crops is characterized by raised corky cankers on fruits and leaves, transmitted by rain splash and leaf miners?

Solution & Rationale: Citrus canker is caused by Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. citri, forming characteristic necrotic corky lesions on citrus foliage and fruit surfaces.
