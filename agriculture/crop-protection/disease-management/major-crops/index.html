Botanical Taxonomy, Origin, and Cytogenetics of Major Field Crops

A precise understanding of botanical nomenclature, family classification, chromosomal constitution (ploidy level), and geographical origin is fundamental for answering taxonomic questions in UP TGT/PGT Agriculture examinations.

  • Wheat (Triticum aestivum): Belongs to family Poaceae (Gramineae), tribe Triticeae. Hexaploid species with 2n = 6x = 42, originating in South-Western Asia (Near East / Fertile Crescent).
  • Paddy / Rice (Oryza sativa): Family Poaceae, subfamily Ehrhartoideae. Diploid species with 2n = 2x = 24, originating in Tropical and Subtropical South-Eastern Asia.
  • Maize (Zea mays): Family Poaceae, tribe Maydeae. Diploid species with 2n = 2x = 20, originating in Central America (Mexico). It is a classic cross-pollinated C4 plant.
  • Chickpea (Cicer arietinum): Family Fabaceae (Leguminosae), subfamily Papilionoideae. Diploid species with 2n = 2x = 16, originating in South-Western Asia (Turkey / Mediterranean region).
  • Mustard / Indian Mustard (Brassica juncea): Family Brassicaceae (Cruciferae). Amphidiploid species formed by natural hybridization between Brassica nigra (2n=16) and Brassica oleracea (2n=18), resulting in 2n = 4x = 36. Originates in Central Asia / China.

Soil, Agro-Climatic Requirements, and Temperature Thresholds

Crop success depends on optimal soil physical-chemical properties and temperature thresholds during phenological phases.

  • Wheat: Thrives best in well-drained fertile loamy to clay-loam soils with neutral pH (6.5 to 7.5). It is a cool-season (rabi) crop requiring 20 to 25°C at germination and tillering, and 25 to 30°C at grain filling. Terminal heat stress occurs if temperatures exceed 32°C during grain maturation.
  • Rice: Adapted to heavy clay or clay-loam soils capable of retaining standing water due to low percolation rates. Optimal temperature is 21 to 37°C. Rice is a short-day plant in tropical ecotypes, though day-neutral varieties exist.
  • Maize: Prefers deep, well-drained alluvial or sandy-loam soils rich in organic matter. Sensitive to water stagnation. Optimal temperature is 21 to 27°C. Freezing temperatures destroy the crop.
  • Chickpea: Grown on sandy-loam to clay soils with good aeration. Requires mild cold winters and moderate warmth during pod maturation. Optimum temperature ranges from 15 to 25°C.

Agronomic Practices: Field Preparation, Sowing Geometry, and Nutrient Management

Scientific execution of seed rate, spacing, and balanced fertilizer application ensures high economic yield and nutrient use efficiency.

  • Seed Rate & Spacing:
    • Wheat: 100 kg/ha for timely sown (spacing 20 to 22.5 cm row-to-row); 125 kg/ha for late sowing.
    • Rice (Transplanted): 20 to 25 kg/ha; spacing 20 × 15 cm. Direct Seeded Rice (DSR): 30 to 35 kg/ha.
    • Maize: 20 to 25 kg/ha; spacing 60 × 20 cm.
    • Chickpea: 75 to 80 kg/ha for desi types; 100 kg/ha for kabuli types. Spacing 30 × 10 cm.
  • Fertilizer Recommendation (N:P:K in kg/ha):
    • High-yielding Wheat: 120:60:40 NPK. Split application: 50% N + full P & K as basal, remaining 50% N top-dressed in two equal splits at CRI and tillering.
    • Hybrid Rice: 120:60:60 NPK. Nitrogen applied in 3 splits (50% basal, 25% active tillering, 25% panicle initiation).
    • Maize: 120:60:40 NPK.
    • Chickpea: 20:40:20 NPK (starter dose, as it fixes atmospheric nitrogen via Rhizobium symbiosis). Seed inoculation with Rhizobium leguminosarum is mandatory.

Critical Irrigation Stages and Water Management

Moisture stress at critical phenological stages leads to catastrophic yield reductions. Efficient scheduling is paramount.

  • Wheat Critical Stages: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) at 20–25 DAS is the single most critical stage. Subsequent critical stages include Tillering (40–45 DAS), Late Jointing (60–65 DAS), Flowering (85–90 DAS), Milk stage (105–110 DAS), and Dough stage (120–125 DAS).
  • Rice Critical Stages: Tillering, Panicle Initiation (PI), Flowering, and Milk stages. Continuous submergence of 5 cm standing water is maintained from transplanting until 10 days before harvest, except during weeding and top-dressing.
  • Maize Critical Stages: Tasseling and Silking. Water stress during silking drastically reduces grain set (poor fertilization), leading to barren cobs.
  • Chickpea Critical Stages: Pre-flowering (branching) and pod development. Excess irrigation causes vegetative overgrowth and increases susceptibility to pod borer and collar rot.

Crop Protection: Weeds, Major Insect Pests, and Fungal Diseases

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) combines cultural, mechanical, biological, and chemical methods to suppress pests below Economic Injury Levels (EIL).

  • Major Weeds & Herbicides:
    • Phalaris minor (Little seed canary grass) in wheat: Controlled by Clodinafop-propargyl (0.06 kg/ha) or Sulfosulfuron (0.025 kg/ha) post-emergence at 30-35 DAS.
    • Echinochloa crus-galli (Barnyard grass) in rice: Controlled by Pretilachlor (1.0 kg/ha) pre-emergence.
  • Insect Pests:
    • Stem Borer (Scirpophaga incertulas) in rice: Causes 'dead heart' at vegetative stage and 'white ear' at reproductive stage. Controlled by Cartap hydrochloride 4G at 25 kg/ha.
    • Gram Pod Borer (Helicoverpa armigera) in chickpea: Larvae feed on foliage and bore into developing pods, eating grains. Controlled by installing pheromone traps (5/ha) and spraying Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC.
  • Plant Diseases:
    • Yellow Rust of Wheat: Caused by Puccinia striiformis. Symptoms include bright yellow pustules arranged in linear stripes on leaves.
    • Blast of Rice: Caused by Magnaporthe oryzae. Symptoms include spindle-shaped lesions with grey centers and dark margins on leaves and nodes.
    • Udbatta disease of rice: Caused by Ephelis oryzae.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Crown Root Initiation (CRI) in wheat occurs precisely 20-25 days after sowing (DAS).
  • Phalaris minor is known as 'wheat's mimic weed' or 'little seed canary grass', exhibiting herbicide resistance to Isoproturon.
  • Amphidiploid nature of Indian Mustard (Brassica juncea) has 2n = 4x = 36 chromosomes.
  • Bt cotton and Bt maize express Cry genes derived from the soil bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis for insect protection.
  • Economic Threshold Level (ETL) is the pest density at which control measures must be applied to prevent economic damage.
  • Zinc deficiency in paddy causes 'Khaira disease', correctable by spraying 0.5% zinc sulfate along with lime.
  • Sugarcane 'Red Rot' is caused by the fungal pathogen Colletotrichum falcatum.
  • Silking and tasseling in maize are extremely sensitive to drought stress, causing complete cob barrenness.
  • Rhizobium leguminosarum inoculation is mandatory for chickpea seed to ensure biological nitrogen fixation.
  • Triticale is the first man-made cereal, produced by crossing wheat (Triticum) and rye (Secale).
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the critical irrigation stage of wheat (CRI at 20-25 DAS) with tillering (40-45 DAS). CRI is the most critical stage.
  • Mistakenly assuming rice is a C4 plant; rice is a C3 plant with typical photorespiration, unlike maize and sorghum which are C4.
  • Applying high nitrogenous fertilizer doses to pulses; pulses require minimal starter nitrogen (20 kg/ha) as they fix atmospheric nitrogen.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Comprehensive Agronomy and Crop Protection of Major Field Crops for UP TGT/PGT Agriculture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Taxonomic Classification and Cytogenetics

Precise knowledge of family, chromosome numbers, and ploidy levels is critical for taxonomic identification.

  • Ploidy Level: Hexaploid wheat (2n=42), Diploid rice (2n=24), Amphidiploid mustard (2n=36).
Concept 2

Agro-Ecological and Climatic Adaptations

Temperature and photoperiod govern vegetative growth, floral initiation, and grain filling.

  • Thermal Regime: Wheat requires cool winters and moderate spring; rice requires warm tropical submerged environments.
Concept 3

Precision Agronomy and Nutrient Management

Balancing seed rates, geometry, and NPK fertilizer split applications maximizes nutrient recovery and yield.

  • Split Application: Nitrogen is applied in splits to prevent leaching losses, particularly in flooded rice and sandy soils.
Concept 4

Integrated Pest and Disease Management (IPM)

Combining resistant varieties, cultural practices, and need-based chemical control keeps pest populations below Economic Injury Levels.

  • ETL Principle: Treatment is initiated only when pest population reaches the Economic Threshold Level.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

CRI Stage (Crown Root Initiation)
The most critical irrigation stage in wheat occurring 20-25 days after sowing when permanent crown roots develop.
Amphidiploid (Allotetraploid)
An organism having two sets of chromosomes from each of two different parental species, such as Brassica juncea.
Dead Heart (Vegetative Stem Borer Symptom)
Dry, central dead shoot in cereal crops caused by larval feeding of stem borers like Scirpophaga incertulas.
White Ear (Reproductive Stem Borer Symptom)
Blanched, empty panicles in rice caused by stem borer feeding at panicle emergence.
Khaira Disease (Zinc Deficiency in Rice)
Physiological disorder characterized by chlorotic patches turning brown on rice leaves due to zinc deficiency.
ETL (Economic Threshold Level)
Pest population density at which control measures should be initiated to prevent economic injury.
EIL (Economic Injury Level)
The lowest pest population density that causes economic damage equal to the cost of artificial control.
Triticale (Triticum x Secale)
The first successful cereal hybrid produced by crossing wheat and rye.
DSR (Direct Seeded Rice)
Crop establishment method where rice seeds are sown directly into dry or wet soil without nursery raising and transplanting.
Ratooning (Regrowth Crop Management)
Cultivation practice of harvesting a crop and allowing the stubble to regrow for a second harvest, common in sugarcane.
Lodging (Stem Fallover)
Permanent displacement of cereal stems from the vertical position due to heavy top growth or weak root anchorage.
Silking (Maize Flowering Phase)
Emergence of style and stigma (silk) from female maize inflorescence (cob), critical for pollination.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • CRI stage in wheat = 20-25 DAS.
  • Khaira disease of rice = Zinc deficiency.
  • Brassica juncea = Amphidiploid (2n=36).
  • Yellow rust of wheat = Puccinia striiformis.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Wheat is a hexaploid rabi cereal with 2n=42, requiring CRI irrigation at 20-25 DAS.
  • Rice is a diploid hydrophytic C3 cereal requiring standing water and split nitrogen applications.
  • Chickpea is a winter pulse crop requiring Rhizobium seed inoculation and sensitive to excess moisture.
  • Integrated Pest Management relies on combining host plant resistance, cultural practices, biological agents, and selective chemicals.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

CRI Stage VS Tillering Stage

CRI occurs at 20-25 DAS and is the primary irrigation bottleneck; tillering occurs later at 40-45 DAS.

Dead Heart VS White Ear

Dead heart occurs during vegetative stage; white ear occurs during reproductive panicle emergence stage.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosomal number (2n) of hexaploid wheat (Triticum aestivum)?

Explanation: Bread wheat (Triticum aestivum) is a hexaploid with 2n = 6x = 42 chromosomes, derived from three diploid ancestral species.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is the most critical irrigation stage in wheat?

Explanation: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) at 20-25 DAS is the most critical stage; moisture stress here causes irreversible yield reduction.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Khaira disease of rice is caused by the deficiency of which nutrient element?

Explanation: Khaira disease in rice is caused by zinc deficiency, characterized by chlorotic patches that turn brown on lower leaves.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the botanical family of Indian Mustard (Brassica juncea)?

Explanation: Brassica juncea belongs to family Brassicaceae (Cruciferae), characterized by tetradynamous stamens and siliqua fruits.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which physiological stage in maize is extremely sensitive to moisture stress, leading to poor cob setting?

Explanation: Water stress during silking and tasseling causes poor pollination and fertilization, resulting in barren cobs.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the causal organism of Yellow Rust in wheat?

Explanation: Yellow rust or stripe rust of wheat is caused by Puccinia striiformis, producing bright yellow pustules in linear stripes.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which microorganism is used as a biofertilizer seed inoculant for chickpea cultivation?

Explanation: Chickpea, being a pulse crop, forms a symbiotic association with Rhizobium leguminosarum for biological nitrogen fixation.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended seed rate for transplanted paddy under normal conditions?

Explanation: For transplanted rice, 20-25 kg of quality seed is sufficient to raise a nursery for one hectare of main field.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which weed is known as 'wheat's mimic weed' due to morphological similarity in early vegetative stages?

Explanation: Phalaris minor mimics wheat plants during early vegetative growth stages, making manual weeding difficult.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the synthetic amphidiploid chromosome number (2n) of Indian Mustard (Brassica juncea)?

Explanation: Brassica juncea is an amphidiploid with 2n = 4x = 36 chromosomes, combining genomes of B. nigra and B. oleracea.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest causes 'dead heart' symptoms in rice during the vegetative stage?

Explanation: The yellow stem borer (Scirpophaga incertulas) larvae feed inside stems, causing central shoot necrosis known as 'dead heart'.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended N:P:K fertilizer ratio for high-yielding wheat varieties?

Explanation: The standard fertilizer recommendation for high-yielding HYV wheat is 120:60:40 kg NPK per hectare.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What type of photosynthetic pathway is exhibited by rice and wheat?

Explanation: Both rice and wheat are C3 plants with the Calvin cycle for carbon dioxide fixation.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical is commonly used as a pre-emergence herbicide in direct-seeded rice?

Explanation: Pretilachlor is a selective pre-emergence herbicide used effectively to control grassy and broad-leaved weeds in rice.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the origin center of cultivated maize (Zea mays)?

Explanation: Maize originated in Central America, specifically Mexico, where it was domesticated from its wild relative teosinte.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the optimum temperature range for wheat seed germination?

Explanation: Wheat seeds germinate best at 20 to 25 °C, while higher temperatures inhibit germination vigour.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which disease of rice is caused by the fungus Magnaporthe oryzae?

Explanation: Rice blast, one of the most destructive fungal diseases of paddy, is caused by Magnaporthe oryzae.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the seed rate of desi chickpea per hectare under rainfed conditions?

Explanation: The recommended seed rate for desi chickpea is 75-80 kg/ha to achieve optimal plant stand under rainfed conditions.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the economic part of sugarcane used for commercial planting?

Explanation: Sugarcane is propagated commercially using stem cuttings known as 'setts', typically having 2 to 3 healthy buds.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of top-dressing nitrogen in wheat at the CRI stage?

Explanation: Top-dressing nitrogen at the CRI stage provides essential nutrients for active tillering and robust vegetative growth.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the critical weed competition period (in DAS) for direct-seeded rice?

Solution & Rationale: The first 15 to 45 days after sowing represent the critical period of crop-weed competition in direct-seeded rice, requiring strict weed management.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

In hybridization, what does an amphidiploid plant possess?

Solution & Rationale: An amphidiploid contains complete diploid sets of chromosomes from two different species, behaving as a stable allotetraploid.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which herbicide is specifically recommended for controlling Phalaris minor in wheat fields?

Solution & Rationale: Clodinafop-propargyl is a post-emergence graminicide used effectively against Phalaris minor in wheat.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the optimum planting depth for wheat sowing under normal soil moisture conditions?

Solution & Rationale: Optimum planting depth for wheat is 4 to 5 cm to ensure proper placement in moist soil without hindering seedling emergence.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which insect pest of chickpea feeds voraciously on foliage and bores into developing pods?

Solution & Rationale: Helicoverpa armigera (Gram pod borer) is a notorious polyphagous pest damaging chickpea pods and grains.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary objective of puddling in rice field preparation?

Solution & Rationale: Puddling destroys soil aggregation, reduces percolation losses, controls weeds, and provides a soft planting medium.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Which nutrient deficiency causes interveinal chlorosis in older leaves of cereal crops?

Solution & Rationale: Nitrogen is a mobile nutrient; its deficiency first manifests as general chlorosis and yellowing of older leaves.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the scientific name and family of Chickpea?

Solution & Rationale: Chickpea is scientifically known as Cicer arietinum and belongs to the Fabaceae family.
