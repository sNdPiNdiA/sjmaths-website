Plant Physiology and Seed Science

Seed Types in Crop Rotations

An authoritative agronomic guide detailing seed types, classes of seeds, and their strategic integration into intensive and sustainable crop rotations for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture examinations.

UP TGT Agriculture Tracker →

1. Introduction to Seed Classes and Agronomic Significance

Seeds are the fundamental biological unit of agricultural production, determining the genetic potential, purity, and ultimate yield of crops. In systematic crop rotations, the choice of seed type, class, and variety dictates soil health restoration, weed suppression, and economic viability. According to the Indian Minimum Seed Certification Standards (IMSCS), seeds are classified into distinct generation classes maintained through strict pedigree maintenance systems.

  • Nucleus Seed: The initial, genetically pure seed stock produced and maintained by the originating plant breeder or sponsoring institution. It serves as the physical source for all subsequent seed classes.
  • Breeder Seed: The progeny of nucleus seed, supervised directly by the originating plant breeder or institution. It is packed with high genetic purity and forms the base for Foundation seed production. It is usually tagged with a golden-yellow label.
  • Foundation Seed: The progeny of breeder seed handled to maintain specific genetic identity and purity. It is produced by authorized seed agencies and certified by state seed certification agencies under white-colored tags.
  • Certified Seed: The progeny of foundation seed (or occasionally registered seed) that is commercially distributed to farming communities for general crop production. It carries a blue-colored tag.

2. Role of Seed Types in Diversified Crop Rotations

A crop rotation is the practice of growing different types of crops in the same area in sequenced seasons. Integrating appropriate seed types and functional crop categories—such as cereals, pulses, oilseeds, and green manure crops—breaks pest cycles and optimizes nutrient utilization.

  • Cereal-Legume Alternation: Alternating heavy-feeding cereal crops like Triticum aestivum (wheat) or Oryza sativa (rice) with symbiotic nitrogen-fixing legumes like Cicer arietinum (chickpea) or Vigna radiata (mung bean) replenishes soil available nitrogen through biological fixation (BNF) via Rhizobium symbiosis.
  • Root Depth Diversity: Alternating shallow-rooted crops with deep-rooted taproot crops prevents hardpan formation and utilizes nutrients from different soil profiles.
  • Green Manuring Integration: Incorporating in-situ green manure seed types such as Sesbania aculeata (Daincha) or Crotalaria juncea (sunn hemp) adds substantial organic matter and mineral nitrogen before main Kharif cereal sowing.

3. Seed Quality Parameters, Testing, and Priming Techniques

High-quality seeds guarantee rapid field emergence, uniform crop stand, and resilience against biotic and abiotic stresses. Seed testing evaluates physical purity, germination percentage, moisture content, and health status.

  • Germination Percentage Formula: Germination % = (Number of normal seedlings germinatedTotal seeds tested) × 100. For certified wheat and paddy seeds, the minimum prescribed germination standard is 85%.
  • Physical Purity Analysis: Determines the percentage by weight of pure seed, other crop seeds, weed seeds, and inert matter. Pure seed standard for most field crops is ≥ 98%.
  • Seed Priming: Hydro-priming, osmo-priming (using polyethylene glycol or solutions of KNO_3), and bio-priming with plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR) like Pseudomonas fluorescens or Trichoderma viride enhance seedling vigor under moisture stress conditions.

4. Seed Treatment Protocols and Integrated Pest Management in Rotations

Seed treatment is an economical pre-sowing application of biological, chemical, or physical agents that protect seeds and emerging seedlings from seed-borne and soil-borne pathogens and pests.

  • Fungicidal Seed Treatment: Treatment with systemic or contact fungicides such as Carbendazim @ 2.0 g/kg of seed or Thiram @ 2.5 g/kg of seed prevents seedling blights and loose smut of wheat caused by Ustilago tritici.
  • Inoculation with Bio-fertilizers: Legume seeds are inoculated with specific Rhizobium strains, while non-legumes like cereals are treated with Azotobacter or Azospirillum to enhance biological nitrogen fixation and phosphorus solubilization (via Bacillus megaterium).
  • Breaking Monoculture Pests: Rotating host crops with non-host seed types starves specialized soil-borne nematodes like Meloidogyne incognita and soil fungi like Fusarium oxysporum, drastically reducing chemical pesticide dependency.

5. Seed Standards, Certification Agencies, and Seed Legislation in India

Regulatory frameworks govern seed production, quality control, and distribution across India to safeguard farmers' interests and maintain genetic integrity.

  • Seeds Act of 1966: The principal legislative enactment providing for the regulation of the quality of seeds for sale and matters connected therewith.
  • Seed Rules (1968) and Seed (Control) Order (1983): Outline operational guidelines for seed inspectors, labeling norms, and licensing of seed dealers.
  • State Seed Certification Agency (SSCA): Autonomous bodies established in each state to inspect seed production plots, verify genetic and physical purity, conduct laboratory seed testing, and issue certification tags.
  • National Seeds Corporation (NSC) and State Farms Corporation of India (SFCI): Apex public sector organizations responsible for foundation and certified seed production of superior high-yielding varieties (HYVs) and hybrids.

  • Certified seed tag color is blue with a minimum genetic purity of 99.0%.
  • The Seeds Act was enacted in India in the year 1966.
  • Minimum germination percentage for certified wheat and paddy seeds is 85%.
  • Loose smut of wheat (Ustilago tritici) is an internally seed-borne fungal disease controlled by systemic fungicides like Carboxin or Carbendazim.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Breeder seed genetic purity (99.9%) with Foundation seed genetic purity (99.5%) and Certified seed (99.0%).
  • Assuming Nucleus seed has a commercial certification tag; nucleus seed is maintained exclusively by the plant breeder without standard commercial tags.
  • Neglecting seed moisture content standards during storage; storing cereal seeds above 12-14% moisture invites storage fungi like Aspergillus and Rhizopertha dominica infestation.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Seed Types in Crop Rotations

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Pedigree Maintenance and Seed Generations

Seed classes follow a strict generational chain ensuring genetic deterioration is prevented over multiplication cycles.

  • Nucleus Seed: 100% pure stock directly from the breeder.
  • Breeder Seed: Direct progeny of nucleus seed with 99.9% purity and golden-yellow tag.
Concept 2

Agronomic Role of Crop Rotations and Seed Diversity

Diversifying seed types in a rotational sequence breaks host-specific pathogen and weed cycles while optimizing soil nutrient uptake.

  • Biological Nitrogen Fixation: Legumes fix atmospheric nitrogen, reducing synthetic fertilizer dependence for subsequent cereal crops.
  • Root Architecture Alternation: Alternating taproot and fibrous root systems improves subsoil aeration and moisture absorption.
Concept 3

Seed Quality Parameters and Testing Standards

Seed certification relies on rigorous laboratory and field inspections establishing physical purity, germination capacity, and freedom from weed seeds.

  • Germination Standard: Minimum 85% for most major cereals and pulses.
  • Physical Purity: Minimum 98% pure seed weight ratio.
Concept 4

Seed Treatment and Phytosanitary Protection

Pre-sowing seed treatments safeguard vulnerable germinating seedlings from soil-borne fungi, seed-borne viruses, and insect pests.

  • Fungicidal Dressing: Carbendazim @ 2 g/kg seed controls smut and blight pathogens.
  • Inoculation: Rhizobium and PSB cultures enhance nutrient availability.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Nucleus Seed (Initial Breeder Stock)
The genetically pure seed stock directly produced and maintained by the originating plant breeder.
Breeder Seed (Progeny of Nucleus Seed)
Seed class with 99.9% genetic purity identified by a golden-yellow certification tag.
Foundation Seed (Parent Seed Stock)
Progeny of breeder seed produced by authorized agencies carrying a white certification tag.
Certified Seed (Commercial Farm Seed)
Progeny of foundation seed distributed for general agricultural production with a blue tag.
Seed Germination (Radicle Emergence Standard)
The emergence and development from the seed embryo of those essential structures which indicate its ability to produce a normal plant under favorable conditions.
Seed Priming (Controlled Hydration Treatment)
Pre-sowing hydration technique that initiates metabolic processes without radical protrusion, improving stress tolerance.
Crop Rotation (Sequenced Agronomic Cropping)
The practice of growing different crops sequentially on the same land to improve soil health and disrupt pest cycles.
Green Manuring (In-situ Organic Enrichment)
Ploughing under of young, succulent plant tissues (e.g., Sesbania aculeata) to enrich soil organic matter and nitrogen.
Seed Viability (Tetrazolium / Living Embryo Test)
The capability of a seed to germinate and develop into a normal seedling under optimal environmental conditions.
Roguing (Off-type Elimination)
The removal of diseased, damaged, or genetically off-type plants from a seed production plot to maintain genetic purity.
Seed Health (Pathogen-Free Status)
The absence or presence of disease-causing microorganisms, insects, or nematodes on or within the seed structure.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Breeder Seed VS Foundation Seed

Breeder seed is the direct progeny of nucleus seed supervised by breeders (Golden-Yellow tag), whereas Foundation seed is the progeny of breeder seed produced by authorized agencies (White tag).

Physical Purity VS Genetic Purity

Physical purity refers to the percentage by weight of clean, pure seeds of the crop versus inert matter and weed seeds, whereas genetic purity refers to the percentage of seeds true to the specific variety type.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the prescribed minimum genetic purity percentage for Foundation seed in India according to minimum seed certification standards?

Explanation: According to Indian Minimum Seed Certification Standards (IMSCS), the minimum genetic purity for Foundation seed is 99.5%, while Breeder seed is 99.9% and Certified seed is 99.0%.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which seed class is characterized by a golden-yellow certification tag?

Explanation: Breeder seed is packed with high genetic purity and carries a golden-yellow color tag. Foundation seed uses a white tag, and certified seed uses a blue tag.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In which year was the Seeds Act enacted in India?

Explanation: The Seeds Act was enacted by the Parliament of India in 1966 to regulate the quality of seeds for sale and establish seed certification agencies.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the minimum germination percentage prescribed for certified wheat and paddy seeds under Indian seed standards?

Explanation: The minimum germination percentage for certified seeds of most cereals, including wheat and paddy, is established at 85% to ensure adequate crop stand.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following fungal diseases is internally seed-borne in wheat and effectively controlled by systemic seed treatment with Carboxin or Carbendazim?

Explanation: Loose smut of wheat caused by Ustilago tritici is internally seed-borne. Systemic fungicides like Carboxin or Carbendazim penetrate the seed tissues to eradicate the internal mycelium.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which seed class serves as the direct physical source for the production of Foundation seed?

Explanation: Foundation seed is the progeny of breeder seed. Breeder seed is itself the progeny of nucleus seed.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary agronomic benefit of incorporating a legume crop seed in a cereal-based crop rotation?

Explanation: Legumes form a symbiotic association with Rhizobium bacteria in root nodules, fixing atmospheric nitrogen and enriching the soil for subsequent cereal crops.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What color is the certification tag assigned to Certified seed distributed for commercial cultivation?

Explanation: Certified seed, which is the progeny of foundation seed sold to farmers for commercial grain production, carries a blue-colored certification tag.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of 'Roguing' in seed production plots?

Explanation: Roguing is the systematic removal of off-type plants, diseased plants, and other crop plants from a seed production plot to maintain high genetic and physical purity.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which organization acts as the apex public sector undertaking for foundation and certified seed production and distribution in India?

Explanation: National Seeds Corporation (NSC), established in 1963, is a premier public sector undertaking under the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare engaged in foundation and certified seed production.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard moisture content limit recommended for safe storage of cereal seeds to prevent fungal infestation and maintain viability?

Explanation: For safe storage of cereal seeds, moisture content should be maintained between 12% and 14% (lower for oilseeds, around 7-9%) to prevent mold growth, insect activity, and rapid loss of viability.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which seed priming technique involves hydrating seeds in an osmotic solution (like PEG) to control water uptake without permitting radical emergence?

Explanation: Osmo-priming involves soaking seeds in osmotic solutions such as polyethylene glycol (PEG) or inorganic salts to regulate water absorption, ensuring uniform germination under stress.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the minimum physical purity percentage required for certified cereal seeds under Indian Minimum Seed Certification Standards?

Explanation: The minimum physical purity standard for most field crop certified seeds is 98%, meaning inert matter, weed seeds, and other crop seeds combined cannot exceed 2%.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop is most commonly utilized for in-situ green manuring prior to paddy cultivation in northern India?

Explanation: Sesbania aculeata (Daincha) is a fast-growing, succulent green manure crop tolerant to waterlogging, widely incorporated into soil before rice transplanting to add organic matter and nitrogen.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does the term 'Seed Viability' signify in seed science?

Explanation: Seed viability refers to the physiological status of the seed indicating whether it is alive and capable of germinating and producing a normal seedling under optimal conditions.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which bacterium is commonly used as a bio-fertilizer seed inoculant for non-legume crops like rice, wheat, and maize to fix atmospheric nitrogen?

Explanation: Azotobacter chroococcum is a free-living, non-symbiotic diazotroph used for seed inoculation in non-leguminous cereal crops to supply fixed nitrogen and growth-promoting substances.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of acid delinting in cotton seeds before sowing?

Explanation: Acid delinting using concentrated sulfuric acid removes fuzz fibers from cotton seeds, facilitating smooth mechanical sowing and destroying surface-borne fungal/bacterial pathogens.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which administrative body is responsible for issuing seed certification in Indian states?

Explanation: State Seed Certification Agencies (SSCA) are designated autonomous bodies responsible for field inspections, seed testing, and issuing certification tags within their respective states.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is meant by 'Certified Seed' being the progeny of 'Foundation Seed'?

Explanation: In pedigree seed multiplication, foundation seed serves as the immediate parent stock to produce certified seed, ensuring genetic purity is maintained across generations.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

How does diversifying seed types in crop rotations assist in integrated weed management (IWM)?

Explanation: Rotating crops with contrasting growth habits, canopy architectures, and tillage requirements prevents the buildup of specialized weed species (such as Phalaris minor in wheat monoculture).

A seed lot of wheat shows a physical purity of 98%, other crop seeds 0.5%, inert matter 1.0%, and weed seeds 0.5%. If the germination test yields 88% normal seedlings, what is the Real Value (RV) of the seed lot?

Solution & Rationale: Real Value (RV) = (Purity % * Germination %) / 100. Here, Purity = 98%, Germination = 88%. RV = (98 * 88) / 100 = 8624 / 100 = 86.24% approx 86.2%.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following statements regarding Breeder seed is scientifically correct?

Solution & Rationale: Breeder seed is the progeny of nucleus seed produced directly by or under the supervision of the originating plant breeder. It carries a golden-yellow tag and 99.9% purity.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

In crop rotations, why is the inclusion of a green manure crop like Sesbania aculeata considered superior to chemical nitrogen application alone in sustainable agriculture?

Solution & Rationale: Green manuring adds active organic matter, improves soil aggregation, enhances microbial activity, increases CEC, and supplies organic nitrogen without the leaching risks of raw chemical fertilizers.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary function of Tetrazolium (TZ) test conducted on seed samples?

Solution & Rationale: The Tetrazolium test is a biochemical test used to evaluate seed viability. Living tissues containing active dehydrogenases reduce colorless tetrazolium chloride to red formazan.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

According to Indian Seed Certification standards, what is the isolation distance required for foundation seed production of self-pollinated crops like wheat and barley to prevent genetic contamination?

Solution & Rationale: For self-pollinated crops like wheat, barley, and rice, the isolation distance is kept minimal (typically 3 meters) because outcrossing rates are extremely low.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

What distinguishes Foundation seed from Certified seed in terms of pedigree generation and tag labeling?

Solution & Rationale: Foundation seed is the direct progeny of breeder seed and carries a white tag. Certified seed is produced from foundation seed and carries a blue tag.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

How does a well-designed crop rotation suppress soil-borne plant-parasitic nematodes such as Meloidogyne incognita?

Solution & Rationale: Plant-parasitic nematodes often have narrow host ranges. Rotating susceptible host crops with non-host crops (e.g., marigold or certain grasses) starves the nematodes, reducing population density.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the seed rate per hectare recommended for normal sown wheat (Triticum aestivum) under irrigated timely-sown conditions in India?

Solution & Rationale: The standard recommended seed rate for timely-sown irrigated wheat in India is 100 kg per hectare, ensuring an optimum plant population of approximately 400-500 ears per square meter.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

In seed technology, what does 'Hard Seeds' refer to in leguminous seed lots?

Solution & Rationale: Hard seeds are viable seeds in legume crops that possess a water-impermeable palisade layer in the seed coat, preventing water imbibition and germination until scarified.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

Which statutory order empowers state governments and seed inspectors to enforce seed quality control, inspect premises, and draw seed samples for testing?

Solution & Rationale: The Seeds Act (1966) along with the Seed (Control) Order (1983) provides the legal framework for licensing seed dealers, regulating seed quality, and empowering seed inspectors.
