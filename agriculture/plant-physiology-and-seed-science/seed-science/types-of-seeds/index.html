1. Introduction to Seed Science and Classes of Seeds

Seeds are the fundamental biological input in crop production, serving as the primary vehicle for genetic improvement and high-yielding technology. In formal seed systems, seed multiplication follows a strict generational cascade to maintain genetic purity, physical identity, and physiological vigor. According to the Indian Seeds Act (1966) and the guidelines established by the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) and national seed certification agencies, seeds are categorized into four distinct classes.

  • Nucleus Seed: The initial, pure seed stock produced directly by the originating breeder or institution, serving as the source for all subsequent classes.
  • Breeder Seed: Directly controlled by the originating breeder or sponsoring institution, possessing maximum genetic purity (100%) and tagged with a yellow label.
  • Foundation Seed: The progeny of breeder seed, handled to maintain specific genetic identity and purity, supervised by certification agencies, and issued with a white label.
  • Certified Seed: The progeny of foundation seed (or occasionally registered seed), commercially distributed to farmers, carrying a blue label.

2. Detailed Agronomy of Seed Production and Generation System

Successful seed production requires rigorous adherence to agronomic and regulatory protocols to prevent genetic drift, mechanical mixture, and weed seed contamination. Isolation distance is a critical agronomic parameter required to prevent cross-pollination in out-crossed crops like maize (Zea mays) and pearl millet (Pennisetum glaucum).

  • Isolation Distance: Varies by crop pollination behavior. Foundation seed plots require significantly larger isolation distances than certified seed plots to ensure absolute purity.
  • Rogueing: The physical removal of off-type plants, diseased plants, and distinct weed species prior to flowering and pollination.
  • Seed Rate and Sowing Geometry: Precision sowing ensures optimal plant population, adequate aeration, and uniform light interception. For instance, foundation wheat (Triticum aestivum) seed is sown at 100 kg/ha with a row-to-row spacing of 22.5 cm.
  • Nutrient Management: Balanced fertilization (N:P:K) is mandatory. Excessive nitrogen delays maturity and increases lodging risk, whereas adequate phosphorus promotes root development and seed setting.

3. Seed Physiology, Viability, Vigor, and Testing

Seed physiology determines how a seed develops, matures, and transitions into a healthy seedling. Understanding physiological parameters is essential for high-quality seed production and storage.

  • Seed Viability: The capacity of a seed to germinate and produce a normal seedling under favorable conditions, commonly assessed via the Tetrazolium (TZ) test using 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride.
  • Seed Vigor: The sum total of structural and functional properties determining the potential level of activity and performance of the seed lot during germination and emergence.
  • Seed Germination Test: Conducted under controlled laboratory conditions (e.g., between paper or top of paper methods at 20°C to 30°C) to determine the percentage of normal seedlings.
  • Physical Purity Analysis: Determines the percentage composition by weight of pure seed, other crop seed, weed seed, and inert matter.

4. Seed Treatment, Processing, and Storage Technology

Post-harvest management of seeds safeguards them against biotic and abiotic stresses during storage and transit. Seed treatment involves the application of fungicides, insecticides, or biological agents to protect seeds from soil- and seed-borne pathogens.

  • Fungicidal Treatment: Application of carbendazim or thiram at standard doses (e.g., 2.5 g/kg of seed) to control smuts, bunts, and damping-off fungi.
  • Seed Processing: Involves cleaning, grading, and sizing using air-screen cleaners, specific gravity separators, and indent cylinder separators based on physical characteristics.
  • Safe Moisture Content: Orthodox seeds must be dried to critical moisture levels before storage—typically below 10-12% for cereals and 8-9% for oilseeds—to prevent respiration heating and fungal invasion.
  • Storage Environment: Cool and dry conditions (low relative humidity and low temperature) maintain high seed vigor and extend longevity according to Harrington's Rules of thumb for seed storage.

5. Legal Framework: The Seeds Act, 1966, and Seed Rules

The regulatory framework governing the seed industry in India ensures that farmers receive high-quality planting material with guaranteed genetic and physical purity.

  • The Seeds Act (1966): Enacted to regulate the quality of seeds for sale and matters connected therewith. It provides for the establishment of the Central Seed Committee and Central Seed Certification Board.
  • Seed Certification Agency: An autonomous body functioning under state governments responsible for verifying seed plots through field inspections, sample testing, and issuing certification tags.
  • Truthfully Labeled (TL) Seeds: Seeds that do not fall under compulsory certification crop notification but whose producers declare minimum standards of germination and purity on a label attached to the container.
  • Offenses and Penalties: Legal provisions against the sale of substandard, misbranded, or spurious seeds under the Essential Commodities Act and Seed Rules.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Breeder seed is produced under the direct supervision of the plant breeder and carries a golden yellow tag with 100% genetic purity.
  • Foundation seed is the progeny of breeder seed and carries a white tag with a minimum genetic purity of 99.5%.
  • Certified seed is commercially distributed to farmers and carries a blue tag with a minimum genetic purity of 99.0%.
  • The Seeds Act was enacted in India in the year 1966 and came into effect fully in October 1969.
  • The Tetrazolium (TZ) test uses 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride to rapidly evaluate seed viability based on living respiring tissues turning red.
  • Foundation and certified seed production plots require strict isolation distances to prevent genetic contamination via pollen drift.
  • Truthfully Labeled (TL) seeds comply with minimum standards of germination and purity but are not subjected to official state seed certification field inspections.
  • Orthodox seeds can be dried to low moisture levels and stored for long periods, unlike recalcitrant seeds such as mango and jackfruit which lose viability upon drying.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Students often confuse foundation seed tag color (White) with certified seed tag color (Blue). Remember: W for White = Foundation, B for Blue = Certified.
  • Assuming breeder seed is sold directly to farmers for commercial grain production; breeder seed is exclusively used to produce foundation seed.
  • Confusing seed viability with seed vigor; viability measures whether a seed is alive and can germinate, whereas vigor measures the overall performance potential under stress.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Comprehensive Study Material on Types of Seeds in Agriculture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Generation System of Seed Multiplication

The step-by-step multiplication of seeds ensures that genetic traits bred into a variety are successfully passed down to commercial farmers without deterioration.

  • Generational Cascade: Nucleus -> Breeder -> Foundation -> Certified. Each step multiplies volume while maintaining strict purity thresholds.
Concept 2

Seed Certification and Quality Control Standards

Seed certification is a legally sanctioned system for quality control of seed multiplication and distribution.

  • Phases of Certification: Receipt of application, verification of seed source, field inspection during critical crop growth stages, seed sampling, laboratory testing, and tagging/sealing.
Concept 3

Seed Physiology and Storage Physics

Seed longevity is governed by initial seed quality, moisture content, storage temperature, and relative humidity.

  • Critical Moisture: Moisture levels must be kept below hygroscopic equilibrium thresholds to halt insect activity and microbial respiration.
Concept 4

Seed Health and Pathology

Seed-borne pathogens can be internal or external, necessitating rigorous seed health testing and chemical/biological seed dressing treatments.

  • Seed Treatment: Essential preventive agronomic practice to eliminate seed-borne fungi, bacteria, and viruses before sowing.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Nucleus Seed (Nucleus Seed Stock)
The initial pure seed stock derived from a selected plant or breeder's material, maintained directly by the originating breeder.
Breeder Seed (Breeder Seed (Yellow Tag))
Seed produced directly from nucleus seed, possessing 100% genetic purity and serving as the source for foundation seed.
Foundation Seed (Foundation Seed (White Tag))
The progeny of breeder seed handled to maintain specific genetic identity and purity under certification agency supervision.
Certified Seed (Certified Seed (Blue Tag))
The progeny of foundation seed produced by registered growers for commercial crop production by farmers.
Truthfully Labeled Seed (TL Seed)
Seeds of notified or non-notified varieties sold in containers bearing a label with minimum quality declarations made by the producer.
Isolation Distance (Spatial Isolation)
The minimum physical distance maintained between two different varieties of the same crop to prevent cross-pollination.
Rogueing (Off-type Removal)
The systematic removal of undesirable, diseased, or off-type plants from a seed production plot to maintain genetic purity.
Seed Viability (Physiological Viability)
The capacity of a seed to germinate and develop into a normal seedling under favorable environmental conditions.
Seed Vigor (Seed Vigor Index)
The sum of seed properties that determine rapid, uniform emergence and development under a wide range of field environments.
Tetrazolium Test (TZ Test)
A biochemical test using 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride to stain respiring living tissues red, assessing seed viability.
Orthodox Seeds (Dehydration-tolerant Seeds)
Seeds that can be dried to low moisture content (5-10%) and successfully stored for long periods at low temperatures.
Recalcitrant Seeds (Dehydration-sensitive Seeds)
Seeds that lose viability rapidly when dried below relatively high moisture thresholds and cannot be stored conventionally.

  • Seed multiplication follows a precise four-tier generation system: Nucleus, Breeder, Foundation, and Certified.
  • Genetic purity requirements are strictest for breeder seed (100%) and progressively lower for foundation (99.5%) and certified seed (99.0%).
  • Tag colors serve as instant identifiers: Golden Yellow for Breeder, White for Foundation, and Blue for Certified.
  • The Seeds Act, 1966 provides the legal backing for seed quality control and certification in India.
  • Isolation distances and timely rogueing are mandatory agronomic operations in seed production plots.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Foundation Seed VS Certified Seed

Foundation seed has 99.5% genetic purity and a white tag, whereas certified seed has 99.0% genetic purity and a blue tag.

Orthodox Seeds VS Recalcitrant Seeds

Orthodox seeds tolerate desiccation and low-temperature storage, whereas recalcitrant seeds lose viability upon drying.
