Seed Maintenance, Vigor, and Viability in Crop Science

Introduction to Seed Maintenance and Physiological Quality

Seed maintenance refers to the systematic preservation of genetic and physical purity, high viability, and maximum vigor of seed lots from harvest until sowing. High-quality seed is the cornerstone of sustainable crop production, influencing uniform seedling emergence, stress tolerance, and final economic yield. According to ICAR standards, seed quality comprises genetic purity, physical purity, physiological quality (germination and vigor), and health status (freedom from seed-borne pathogens).

  • Genetic Integrity: Preventing mechanical mixtures, genetic drift, and natural outcrossing during seed production cycles.
  • Physiological Deterioration: Inevitable age-related decline in seed viability governed by lipid peroxidation, enzyme denaturation, and chromosomal aberrations.
  • Moisture Equilibrium: The single most critical environmental factor governing seed longevity during commercial and genebank storage.

Mechanisms of Seed Deterioration and Aging

Seed aging is an irreversible degenerative process that begins immediately after physiological maturity. As seeds age, their biochemical efficiency diminishes, leading to delayed emergence, abnormal seedlings, and total loss of viability. Understanding these pathways is essential for managing seed stocks in tropical and subtropical Indian conditions.

  • Lipid Peroxidation: Free radical-mediated attack on unsaturated fatty acids in cellular membranes, destroying semi-permeability and causing leachate leakage.
  • Enzymatic Decline: Progressive inactivation of vital metabolic enzymes such as catalase, peroxidase, glutamic acid decarboxylase (GAD), and cytochrome oxidase.
  • DNA Damage: Single- and double-strand breaks in nucleic acids accompanied by depurination and loss of chromosomal integrity during prolonged storage.
  • Accumulation of Toxic Metabolites: Buildup of secondary metabolites and aldehydes that inhibit subsequent germination processes.

Factors Affecting Seed Viability and Longevity

Seed longevity is determined by both hereditary characteristics of the species and environmental conditions during harvest, processing, and storage. Harrington's Rules of Thumb provide the foundational empirical framework for safe seed storage.

  • Harrington's Rules: (1) Each 1% decrease in seed moisture doubles storage life; (2) Each 5°C reduction in storage temperature doubles storage life; (3) The sum of percentage relative humidity (%) and storage temperature (°F) should not exceed 100 for safe storage.
  • Initial Seed Quality: Mechanically damaged, weathered, or immature seeds deteriorate significantly faster than sound, fully mature seeds.
  • Storage Environment: Relative humidity (RH) and temperature are paramount. Airtight hermetic sealing prevents moisture readsorption in high-humidity zones.
  • Storage Fungi: Proliferation of field fungi (e.g., Fusarium spp., Alternaria spp.) and storage fungi (e.g., Aspergillus spp., Penicillium spp.) accelerates respiration and heating.

Seed Vigor Testing and Physiological Evaluation

While standard germination tests measure the maximum potential under optimum laboratory conditions, vigor tests evaluate the ability of seeds to perform successfully under wide-ranging field environments. Vigor encompasses rapid uniform emergence and stress tolerance.

  • Accelerated Aging (AA) Test: Exposing seeds to high temperature (41°C) and high relative humidity (100%) for a specified duration to simulate severe storage conditions.
  • Electrical Conductivity Test: Measuring the amount of electrolytes leaked into soak water from deteriorated cell membranes; higher leakage indicates lower vigor.
  • Tetrazolium (TZ) Test: Biochemical staining using 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride to evaluate viable respiring tissues based on red formazan dye formation.
  • Cold Test: Particularly critical for maize (Zea mays) and soybean (Glycine max), evaluating emergence under cold, wet soil conditions conducive to pathogen attack.

Maintenance of Genetic Purity in Certified Seed Production

Maintaining genetic purity requires rigorous isolation distances, rouging of off-type plants, and prevention of mechanical contamination during threshing, processing, and bagging. Certified seed classes must conform to strict certification standards prescribed by the National Seeds Corporation (NSC) and State Seed Certification Agencies.

  • Isolation Distance: Spatial or temporal separation required between different varieties or crops to prevent cross-pollination by wind or insects (e.g., Foundation seed of hybrid maize requires at least 400 meters).
  • Rouging: Systematic removal of diseased plants, weed plants, volunteer plants, and off-types (differing in morphological traits such as pigmentation, heading date, or plant height) prior to flowering and harvesting.
  • Post-Harvest Handling: Thorough cleaning of combines, threshers, elevators, and processing plants (air-screen cleaners, gravity separators) between different varieties to avoid mechanical mixing.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Harrington's rule states that each 1% reduction in seed moisture doubles the storage life of orthodox seeds.
  • The sum of relative humidity (%) and storage temperature (°F) must not exceed 100 for safe, unsealed seed storage.
  • Tetrazolium (TZ) test utilizes 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride, which stains living respiring tissues red due to formazan production.
  • Accelerated aging test is conducted typically at 41°C and 100% relative humidity for 48 to 72 hours depending on the crop species.
  • Electrical conductivity test measures electrolyte leakage caused by damaged cell membranes during seed aging.
  • Orthodox seeds can be dried to low moisture contents (5-8%) and stored at sub-zero temperatures without losing viability.
  • Recalcitrant seeds (e.g., mango, jackfruit, cacao) cannot tolerate drying below critical moisture thresholds (20-30%) and cannot be stored for long periods.
  • Foundation seed is produced from Nucleus or Breeder seed and is supervised by certification agencies to maintain genetic identity.
  • Isolation distance for certified seed production of hybrid maize (Zea mays) is 200 meters, while for foundation seed it is 400 meters.
  • Glutamic acid decarboxylase (GAD) activity is biochemical marker used to assess the vigor and aging status of cereal seeds.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing seed viability with seed vigor: Viability measures whether a seed can germinate under optimal conditions, whereas vigor measures performance and stress tolerance in the field.
  • Assuming recalcitrant seeds can be dried like orthodox seeds: Drying recalcitrant seeds below 20-30% moisture causes lethal desiccation injury.
  • Believing that standard germination test reflects field emergence under adverse weather conditions: Vigor tests are necessary because standard germination overestimates field emergence.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Seed Maintenance, Vigor, and Viability in Crop Science

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Seed Deterioration Kinetics

Seed aging is an inexorable, cumulative biochemical deterioration governed by environmental storage parameters and genetic makeup.

  • Lipid Peroxidation: Free radical destruction of cellular membranes resulting in solute leakage.
  • Enzyme Inactivation: Loss of catalase, GAD, and superoxide dismutase activity.
Concept 2

Harrington's Storage Principles

Empirical rules dictating safe storage limits for orthodox crop seeds.

  • Moisture Rule: 1% decrease doubles storage longevity.
  • Temperature Rule: 5°C decrease doubles storage longevity.
Concept 3

Orthodox vs. Recalcitrant Seeds

Classification based on desiccation and freezing tolerance during storage.

  • Orthodox Seeds: Tolerant to desiccation (5-8% moisture) and low temperatures (e.g., wheat, rice, pulses).
  • Recalcitrant Seeds: Intolerant to drying and freezing; lose viability rapidly (e.g., rubber, mango, cocoa).
Concept 4

Genetic Purity Maintenance

Protocols designed to prevent genetic drift, mechanical mixing, and outcrossing.

  • Isolation Distance: Spatial buffer zone separating seed crops from contaminating pollen sources.
  • Rouging: Removal of off-types, diseased, and volunteer plants prior to pollination.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Seed Viability (Physiological Germinability)
The capability of a seed to germinate and produce a normal seedling under favorable environmental conditions.
Seed Vigor (Vigor Index / Performance Potential)
The sum total of seed properties determining rapid, uniform emergence and development under diverse field conditions.
Orthodox Seed (Desiccation-Tolerant Seed)
Seeds that can be dried to low moisture contents (5-8%) and successfully stored at sub-zero temperatures for long periods.
Recalcitrant Seed (Desiccation-Sensitive Seed)
Seeds that cannot survive drying below high critical moisture thresholds (20-30%) and cannot be stored for extended durations.
Accelerated Aging (AA Test)
A vigor test exposing seeds to high temperature (41°C) and 100% relative humidity to simulate adverse storage aging.
Tetrazolium Test (TZ Test)
A biochemical test using 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride to stain living respiring tissues red for viability assessment.
Electrical Conductivity (EC Leachate Assay)
A test measuring electrolyte leakage from deteriorated cell membranes into seed soak water.
Rouging (Off-Type Removal)
The systematic removal of diseased, weed, and off-type plants from a seed production plot to preserve genetic purity.
Isolation Distance (Spatial Pollination Barrier)
The minimum physical distance required between two different varieties or crops to prevent cross-pollination.
Breeder Seed (Nucleus-Derived Elite Seed)
Seed directly controlled by the originating plant breeder, possessing the highest genetic purity.
Foundation Seed (Registered Progeny Seed)
The progeny of breeder seed produced to maintain high genetic purity and supply registered or certified seed growers.
Certified Seed (Commercial Farm Seed)
The progeny of foundation or registered seed distributed for general commercial crop production by farmers.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Harrington's rule: RH (%) + Temperature (°F) < 100 for safe unsealed storage.
  • Tetrazolium test stains viable tissues red due to formazan formation.
  • Recalcitrant seeds die if dried below 20-30% moisture.
  • Breeder seed tag is Golden Yellow; Foundation seed tag is White; Certified seed tag is Blue.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Seed maintenance ensures retention of genetic purity, physical purity, viability, and vigor.
  • Seed deterioration is driven by lipid peroxidation, membrane damage, enzyme inactivation, and DNA breakage.
  • Harrington's rules state that halving moisture or lowering temperature by 5°C doubles orthodox seed storage life.
  • Vigor tests (accelerated aging, electrical conductivity, TZ test) evaluate stress tolerance and field emergence potential.
  • Genetic purity is maintained via strict isolation distances, meticulous rouging, and prevention of mechanical mixing.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Foundation Seed Tag VS Certified Seed Tag

Foundation seed utilizes a White tag, whereas Certified seed utilizes a Blue tag.

Orthodox Seeds VS Recalcitrant Seeds

Orthodox seeds withstand drying and low-temperature storage; recalcitrant seeds suffer lethal desiccation injury when dried.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following environmental factors has the most profound influence on the longevity of orthodox seeds during storage?

Explanation: Seed moisture content and storage temperature are the two primary parameters determining seed longevity. Harrington's rules establish that lowering moisture and temperature exponentially increases storage life.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

According to Harrington's empirical rules of seed storage, what happens to the storage life of orthodox seeds when the seed moisture content is reduced by 1%?

Explanation: According to Harrington's first rule, each 1% decrease in seed moisture content doubles the storage life of orthodox seeds (within practical moisture limits of 5% to 14%).

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary chemical reaction responsible for the staining of viable tissues in the Tetrazolium (TZ) test?

Explanation: Living cells with active dehydrogenase enzymes reduce the colorless 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride into a non-diffusible red-colored compound called formazan, indicating active respiration and viability.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Seeds that cannot tolerate desiccation below a moisture content of 20-30% and lose viability rapidly when dried are classified as:

Explanation: Recalcitrant seeds (e.g., mango, jackfruit, rubber) are desiccation-sensitive and suffer lethal injury when dried below critical moisture thresholds, making long-term conventional storage extremely difficult.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which seed vigor test evaluates membrane integrity by measuring the quantity of electrolytes leaked into soak water?

Explanation: The electrical conductivity test measures ions (electrolytes) leaking from compromised cell membranes into deionized water; higher electrical conductivity indicates deteriorated membranes and low vigor.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the color of the certification tag attached to Foundation seed bags according to Indian seed certification standards?

Explanation: Foundation seed tags are White, Breeder seed tags are Golden Yellow, and Certified seed tags are Blue.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

During seed aging and deterioration, which biochemical parameter characteristically declines rapidly?

Explanation: Enzyme activity such as Glutamic acid decarboxylase (GAD) declines progressively as seeds age and deteriorate, making it a reliable biochemical indicator of seed vigor.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the maximum sum of relative humidity (%) and storage temperature (°F) recommended by Harrington for safe, unsealed seed storage?

Explanation: Harrington's third rule states that the sum of relative humidity percentage and storage temperature in degrees Fahrenheit should not exceed 100 for safe, unsealed seed storage.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following best defines seed vigor?

Explanation: Seed vigor encompasses those seed properties that determine the potential for rapid, uniform emergence and development of normal seedlings across a wide range of field stress conditions.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of rouging in seed production plots?

Explanation: Rouging is the rogue removal of off-types, mutant plants, diseased individuals, and other crop varieties from seed production fields to maintain high genetic purity standards.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following crops produces orthodox seeds that can be successfully dried and stored for decades at sub-zero temperatures?

Explanation: Wheat (Triticum aestivum) produces orthodox seeds that tolerate desiccation and freezing temperatures, unlike mango, cocoa, and rubber which produce recalcitrant seeds.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The accelerated aging test evaluates seed vigor by subjecting seeds to which of the following extreme conditions?

Explanation: The accelerated aging test exposes seeds to high temperature (typically 41°C) and 100% relative humidity to artificially age them rapidly and evaluate their potential storage longevity and vigor.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended color of the tag for Certified seed bags?

Explanation: Certified seed, which is distributed to farmers for commercial crop production, is identified by a Blue tag.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following enzymes is primarily responsible for membrane lipid peroxidation during seed deterioration?

Explanation: Lipoxygenase and non-enzymatic free radical reactions attack unsaturated fatty acids in seed cell membranes, causing lipid peroxidation and loss of membrane semi-permeability during seed aging.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why are orthodox seeds dried to low moisture contents before hermetic sealing in genebanks?

Explanation: Drying orthodox seeds to 5-8% moisture reduces cellular metabolism and prevents ice crystal formation during sub-zero genebank conservation, thereby preserving viability for decades.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which test is specifically recommended for evaluating the cold tolerance and seedling emergence vigor of maize seeds?

Explanation: The cold test is designed specifically for maize and certain other crops to simulate adverse, cold, and wet seedbed conditions where soil pathogens attack weakened seeds.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary reason why recalcitrant seeds lose viability when subjected to drying?

Explanation: Removal of water from recalcitrant seeds causes mechanical stress, membrane phase transitions, and irreversible collapse of cellular ultrastructure, leading to rapid death.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does a high electrical conductivity reading of seed soak water indicate about a seed lot?

Explanation: High electrical conductivity of seed soak water indicates high electrolyte leakage resulting from damaged cell membranes, which is a hallmark of low seed vigor and advanced deterioration.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In the context of seed certification, what does 'isolation distance' prevent?

Explanation: Isolation distance ensures that pollen from other varieties or wild relatives does not contaminate the seed crop via wind or insect pollination, thereby maintaining genetic purity.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following classes of seed has the highest level of genetic purity and is directly handled by the plant breeder?

Explanation: Breeder seed is directly produced or controlled by the originating plant breeder or institution and possesses the highest standard of genetic purity.
