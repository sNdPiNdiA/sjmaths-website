Introduction to Seed Testing and International Standards

Seed testing is a scientific procedure designed to evaluate the planting value of a seed lot. It forms the backbone of quality seed control systems, ensuring that farmers receive high-performing seeds that meet legal thresholds for purity and germination. The International Seed Testing Association (ISTA), established in 1924, standardizes seed testing protocols globally, while the Indian Minimum Seed Certification Standards (IMSCS) regulate domestic seed production.

  • Primary Objectives: Assessing planting quality, determining seed lot uniformity, detecting seed-borne pathogens, and estimating commercial value.
  • ISTA Rules: Provide uniform methods for sampling, purity analysis, moisture determination, and germination testing.
  • Representative Sampling: The integrity of any seed test depends entirely on obtaining a truly representative sample from the bulk seed lot using designated triers and sampling probes.

Seed Sampling and Working Sample Size

To evaluate a seed lot accurately, a small yet representative working sample must be drawn from the composite sample. The size of the submitted sample and the working sample is strictly governed by the seed size and species, as outlined by ISTA guidelines.

  • Primary Sample: Small portions taken from different positions in the seed lot (top, middle, bottom of bags).
  • Composite Sample: Combining all primary samples and thoroughly mixing them.
  • Submitted Sample: The representative sample sent to the seed testing laboratory (e.g., Triticum aestivum: 1000 g; Oryza sativa: 700 g; Brassica juncea: 400 g).
  • Working Sample: The reduced sample weighed out directly for a specific test (e.g., purity analysis working sample for wheat is 120 g).

Physical Purity Analysis

Physical purity analysis determines the composition of the seed lot by weight, separating it into three distinct components: Pure Seed, Other Crop Seeds, and Inert Matter. This test ensures the seed lot is free from weed seeds and inert debris.

  • Pure Seed: Includes mature, unbroken seeds of the designated crop, as well as undersized, shrivelled, cracked, or diseased seeds provided they can be positively identified.
  • Other Crop Seeds: Seeds of any plant species other than the crop being tested found in the working sample.
  • Inert Matter: Non-seed materials, broken seed units one-half or less of the original size, stones, sand, dust, chaff, and stems.

Seed Germination and Viability Testing

Seed germination is defined as the emergence and development from the seed embryo of those essential structures which indicate the ability to produce a normal plant under favorable conditions. Standard germination tests are conducted using specialized substrates like Top of Paper (TP), Between Paper (BP), or Sand (S).

    Normal Seedlings: Possess well-developed root and shoot systems, normal cotyledons, and adequate vigor.
  • Abnormal Seedlings: Damaged, deformed, decayed, or lacking essential structures due to physiological or pathological defects.
  • Hard Seeds: Seeds (common in Fabaceae family) that do not absorb water during the test period due to an impermeable seed coat.
  • Fresh Ungerminated Seeds: Seeds that remain firm and viable at the end of the test period but fail to germinate due to dormancy.
  • Tetrazolium Test (TZ Test): A rapid biochemical test using 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride to evaluate seed viability by staining living tissues red due to respiring dehydrogenase enzymes.

Seed Vigor, Moisture Content, and Seed Health Testing

Beyond standard germination, seed vigor reflects the sum total of properties that determine the potential for rapid, uniform emergence across a wide range of field environments. Moisture content is another critical parameter that dictates safe storage longevity.

  • Vigor Tests: Accelerated Aging (AA) test, Conductivity test, Cool test, and Seedling Vigor Classification.
  • Moisture Determination: High-constant-temperature oven method (130^\circC for 1-2 hours) or low-constant-temperature oven method (103^\circC for 17 hours). Safe storage moisture for cereals is generally 10–12%, and for oilseeds 7–9%.
  • Seed Health Testing: Blotter test and agar plate method to detect seed-borne fungi (e.g., Bipolaris oryzae in rice, Ustilago tritici in wheat) and bacteria.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • ISTA was established in the year 1924 with its headquarters in Switzerland.
  • The working sample size for physical purity analysis of wheat (Triticum aestivum) is 120 grams.
  • The chemical used in the tetrazolium viability test is 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (or bromide), which turns red in living tissues.
  • Seed moisture content for safe storage of cereal grains is recommended below 12 percent.
  • Broken seed units that are one-half or less of the original size are classified as inert matter during purity analysis.
  • Seed vigor tests include the accelerated aging test, electrical conductivity test, and seedling growth rate test.
  • Hard seeds are characteristically found in the family Fabaceae due to an impermeable waxy cuticle and palisade layer.
  • The standard temperature maintained for germination testing of most temperate crops is 20^\circC to 25^\circC / 30^\circC.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing seed viability with seed vigor; viability simply means whether the seed is alive, whereas vigor encompasses performance capacity under stress.
  • Mistaking other crop seeds for weed seeds; other crop seeds belong to cultivated crop species, whereas weed seeds belong to uncultivated or noxious weed flora.
  • Assuming tetrazolium test measures actual field emergence; TZ measures enzymatic activity and structural integrity, not field emergence under stress.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Seed Testing: Principles, Protocols, and Quality Control

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

International Seed Testing Association (ISTA) and Standards

ISTA provides standard international protocols for seed sampling, purity, germination, and vigor testing. Compliance with ISTN standards facilitates international seed trade and ensures uniform quality grading.

  • ISTA Foundation: Established in 1924 to harmonize seed testing procedures globally.
  • Working Sample: Fraction obtained from submitted sample by reduction for specific laboratory tests.
Concept 2

Physical Purity Components

Physical purity analysis breaks down a seed sample into three core constituents to evaluate physical contamination and seed cleanliness.

  • Pure Seed: Specified crop species, including shrivelled or damaged variants.
  • Inert Matter: Non-seed debris, sand, stones, and broken seed fragments less than half size.
  • Other Crop Seeds: Seeds of cultivated plants other than the primary crop.
Concept 3

Seed Germination and Seedling Evaluation

Germination testing evaluates the potential of a seed lot to produce normal, healthy seedlings under optimum laboratory conditions.

  • Normal Seedling: Possesses well-developed root and shoot structures.
  • Abnormal Seedling: Defective, deformed, or decayed seedlings incapable of continued growth.
Concept 4

Viability and Vigor Testing

Evaluating physiological potential through biochemical and stress tests ensures reliable crop stands.

  • TZ Test: Biochemical test utilizing tetrazolium salt to stain active living tissues red.
  • Accelerated Aging: Vigor test exposing seeds to high temperature (41^\circC) and high relative humidity (100%) before germination testing.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

ISTA (International Seed Testing Association)
Global regulatory body that standardizes seed sampling and testing procedures.
Pure Seed (Pure Seed Fraction)
Proportion of working sample belonging to the designated crop variety by weight.
Inert Matter (Non-viable Debris)
Stones, soil, chaff, and broken seed fragments less than half the original size.
Tetrazolium Test (2,3,5-Triphenyl Tetrazolium Chloride Test)
Rapid biochemical assay used to evaluate seed viability based on cellular respiration.
Hard Seeds (Impermeable Seed Coat Seeds)
Viable seeds that fail to absorb water due to a water-impermeable seed coat.
Fresh Ungerminated Seeds (Dormant Viable Seeds)
Seeds that remain firm and alive at the end of a germination test but do not sprout due to dormancy.
Seed Vigor (Physiological Vigor Potential)
Sum total of seed properties determining rapid, uniform emergence under diverse field stress.
Submitted Sample (Laboratory Sample)
Representative seed sample submitted to the testing laboratory for quality evaluation.
Working Sample (Test Sub-sample)
Reduced sample weighed out from the submitted sample for specific purity or germination tests.
Moisture Content (Seed Water Content Percentage)
Percentage of water weight relative to total seed weight, crucial for safe storage.
Accelerated Aging Test (AA Vigor Test)
Stress test subjecting seeds to high heat and humidity to estimate storage potential and vigor.
Normal Seedling (Fully Developed Seedling)
Seedling possessing complete essential structures indicating potential for normal plant development.

  • Tetrazolium salt turns red in living, respiring seed tissues due to formazan production.
  • Broken seeds smaller than half the original size are classified as inert matter.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Seed testing evaluates planting quality, purity, germination, and health.
  • ISTA establishes international guidelines for seed sampling and analysis.
  • Physical purity divides samples into pure seed, other crop seeds, and inert matter.
  • Germination tests classify seedlings into normal, abnormal, dead, hard, or fresh ungerminated.
  • Tetrazolium (TZ) test is a rapid biochemical test for seed viability.
  • Seed moisture must be maintained at safe levels (typically below 12%) to prevent storage deterioration.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Seed Viability VS Seed Vigor

Viability indicates whether a seed is alive and capable of germinating under optimal conditions, whereas vigor measures its performance under stress and storage potential.

Hard Seeds VS Fresh Ungerminated Seeds

Hard seeds fail to germinate because their seed coat prevents water uptake, whereas fresh ungerminated seeds take up water but remain dormant due to internal physiological blocks.

Question 1 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which international organization is responsible for standardizing seed testing methods globally?

Explanation: The International Seed Testing Association (ISTA), established in 1924, sets global standards for seed testing, sampling, and analysis.

Question 2 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

During physical purity analysis, broken seed units that are what fraction or less of the original size are classified as inert matter?

Explanation: According to ISTA rules, broken seed units that are one-half or less of the original size are classified as inert matter, while those larger than half are treated as pure seed.

Question 3 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chemical name of the salt used in the tetrazolium viability test?

Explanation: 2,3,5-Triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (or bromide) is used in the TZ test, where living respiring tissues reduce the salt to form a red formazan compound.

Question 4 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Hard seeds are predominantly found in which plant family due to an impermeable seed coat?

Explanation: Hard seeds are a common characteristic of the Fabaceae (legume) family, where a waxy cuticle and palisade layer prevent water absorption.

Question 5 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of an accelerated aging test in seed science?

Explanation: The accelerated aging test evaluates seed vigor and storability by subjecting seeds to high stress (high temperature and 100% relative humidity) before germination testing.

Question 6 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which fraction in physical purity analysis includes seeds of any cultivated plant species other than the crop being tested?

Explanation: Other crop seeds refer to seeds of any cultivated crop species found in the sample other than the main crop being analyzed.

Question 7 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended safe moisture content for long-term storage of cereal grains like wheat and rice?

Explanation: For safe storage of cereal grains, moisture content must be maintained between 10% and 12% to prevent insect activity, fungal growth, and rapid respiration.

Question 8 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

In a standard germination test, how are seedlings that possess well-developed essential structures classified?

Explanation: Seedlings that show balanced, complete development of essential structures (roots and shoots) are classified as normal seedlings.

Question 9 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What color is produced in viable respiring seed tissues during a tetrazolium test?

Explanation: Living respiring cells produce dehydrogenase enzymes that reduce tetrazolium chloride into an insoluble red formazan compound, indicating viability.

Question 10 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following describes 'fresh ungerminated seeds' at the end of a germination test?

Explanation: Fresh ungerminated seeds are viable, firm, and healthy at the termination of the test period but do not germinate due to physiological dormancy.

Question 11 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the working sample size required for physical purity analysis of wheat (Triticum aestivum) according to ISTA rules?

Explanation: Under ISTA guidelines, the working sample size for physical purity analysis of wheat is 120 grams.

Question 12 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What does the abbreviation ISTA stand for in seed science?

Explanation: ISTA stands for International Seed Testing Association, founded in 1924.

Question 13 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which seed health testing method is commonly used to detect seed-borne fungi by placing seeds on moistened filter paper?

Explanation: The blotter test is a standard seed health testing method where seeds are placed on moistened blotter paper to encourage fungal growth and identification.

Question 14 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Electrical conductivity of seed leachate is a measure of what seed property?

Explanation: Electrical conductivity measures the leakage of electrolytes from seeds into water; higher leakage indicates damaged cell membranes and lower seed vigor.

Question 15 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of pre-washing or pre-conditioning seeds prior to a tetrazolium test?

Explanation: Pre-conditioning seeds in water softens the seed coat, facilitates easy dissection, and reactivates enzymes necessary for the tetrazolium staining reaction.

Question 16 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is considered inert matter in a seed purity test?

Explanation: Stones, sand, dust, soil particles, and broken seed units less than half the original size are classified as inert matter.

Question 17 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Why are seed vigor tests increasingly important in modern agriculture alongside standard germination tests?

Explanation: Standard germination tests are conducted under optimum laboratory conditions, whereas seed vigor tests predict field emergence across diverse environmental stresses.

Question 18 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What type of seedling is produced when essential structures are deformed, decayed, or unbalanced?

Explanation: Seedlings exhibiting structural defects, necrosis, or severe stunting are classified as abnormal seedlings in germination evaluations.

In seed testing laboratory practice, what is the exact relationship between the submitted sample and the working sample?

Which of the following enzymatic groups is primarily responsible for the reduction of tetrazolium chloride into red formazan in viable seeds?

Solution & Rationale: Dehydrogenase enzymes associated with active cellular respiration reduce colorless tetrazolium salt into red, water-insoluble formazan.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

When conducting a physical purity analysis on a seed lot, what classification is given to a broken seed unit that is exactly 60% of the original seed size?

Solution & Rationale: According to ISTA rules, any broken seed unit larger than 50% (one-half) of the original size is classified as pure seed.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following crops is well known for possessing physical dormancy due to hard, impermeable seed coats requiring scarification?

Solution & Rationale: Legumes like soybean (Glycine max) frequently exhibit hard-seededness due to impermeable seed coat characteristics.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary physiological consequence of seed deterioration during prolonged storage under high humidity and temperature?

Solution & Rationale: Deterioration causes lipid peroxidation and membrane breakdown, leading to increased electrolyte leakage and loss of viability.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

In the context of seed health testing, what does the blotter test primarily screen for?

Solution & Rationale: The blotter method is widely used to detect and identify seed-borne fungal pathogens growing from seeds placed on moistened blotter paper.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the principal reason for maintaining low moisture content (below 12%) in seeds during storage?

Solution & Rationale: Low moisture content restricts respiration, suppresses fungal growth, and prevents insect proliferation during storage.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

Which test provides the most comprehensive evaluation of a seed lot's ability to emerge rapidly and uniformly under adverse field conditions?

Solution & Rationale: Vigor tests like accelerated aging are specifically designed to evaluate emergence potential under environmental stress.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

How are dormant seeds categorized if they fail to germinate during a standard germination test but remain alive and firm?

Solution & Rationale: Fresh ungerminated seeds are viable seeds that do not germinate during the standard test period due to dormancy.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the standard temperature range specified by ISTA for conducting germination tests on most agricultural crop seeds?

Solution & Rationale: ISTA guidelines prescribe alternating or constant temperatures typically between 20^\circC and 30^\circC for the germination testing of most agricultural crops.
