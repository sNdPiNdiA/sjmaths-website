Introduction to Seed Science and Seed Classifications

Seed science is the foundational discipline dealing with the production, processing, testing, storage, and certification of agricultural seeds. A true botanical seed is a fertilized mature ovule comprising an embryo, stored food reserves (endosperm or cotyledons), and a protective seed coat (testa). In agronomy, any plant propagation material used for sowing—including vegetative propagules like tubers, bulbs, and rhizomes—is legally and commercially termed as seed.

  • Nucleus Seed: The initial amount of pure seed derived from the selected breeder's plant, maintained by the originating plant breeder or institution.
  • Breeder Seed: Directly controlled by the originating breeder or sponsoring institution. It provides the source for the initial and recurring increase of foundation seed. It possesses 100% genetic purity and is tagged with a golden-yellow label.
  • Foundation Seed: The progeny of breeder seed, handled to maintain specific genetic identity and purity. It is produced by designated seed agencies and tagged with a white label.
  • Certified Seed: The progeny of foundation seed or registered seed. It is widely distributed to farmers for commercial crop production and is tagged with an azure-blue label.

Seed Production, Physiological Maturity, and Harvesting

High-quality seed production demands strict adherence to isolation distances to prevent genetic contamination via cross-pollination or mechanical mixing. Physiological maturity marks the developmental stage when the seed accumulates maximum dry weight, after which moisture content declines naturally. Harvesting at optimum moisture prevents mechanical damage and seed deterioration.

  • Isolation Distance: Varies by crop pollination mode; self-pollinated crops require shorter isolation distances (e.g., 3 meters for wheat and paddy), whereas cross-pollinated crops like maize require 400 meters for foundation seed and 200 meters for certified seed.
  • Physiological Maturity Marker: Characterized by the appearance of the black layer at the placental attachment point in cereal grains such as Zea mays.
  • Optimum Harvest Moisture: Grains should be harvested when kernel moisture drops to 18-20% to prevent field weathering, fungal infection, and physiological aging.
  • Rouging: Systematic removal of off-types, diseased plants, and weed plants before flowering and harvesting to maintain genetic purity standards.

Seed Processing, Conditioning, and Upgrading

Seed processing involves harvesting, pre-cleaning, drying, cleaning, grading, treating, and packaging. The primary objective is to separate pure seeds from weed seeds, inert matter, and other crop seeds, while upgrading physical and physiological quality.

  • Air-Screen Cleaner: The fundamental machine in seed processing plants that utilizes a combination of scalping screens, grading screens, and a controlled air blast to separate seeds based on size, width, and weight.
  • Gravity Separator: Upgrades seed lots by separating seeds of identical size but differing specific gravity, effectively removing insect-damaged, cracked, or shrivelled seeds.
  • Seed Treatment: Application of fungicides (e.g., Carbendazim or Thiram at 2.5 g/kg seed) and biofertilizers (e.g., Rhizobium japonicum for Glycine max) to protect against seed-borne pathogens and enhance seedling establishment.
  • Seed Drying: Lowers seed moisture content to safe storage limits (usually below 12% for cereals and 8-9% for oilseeds) using natural sun drying or forced-air mechanical driers where drying air temperature does not exceed 43°C for cereal seeds.

Seed Testing Methodologies and Quality Control

Seed testing follows standardized International Seed Testing Association (ISTA) protocols to evaluate planting value. Key physical and physiological parameters determine whether a seed lot meets national certification standards.

  • Purity Analysis: Determines the percentage weight composition of pure seed, other crop seeds, weed seeds, and inert matter in a representative sample.
  • Standard Germination Test: Conducted under optimal temperature, light, and moisture conditions to determine the percentage of normal seedlings capable of developing into robust plants.
  • Tetrazolium (TZ) Test: A rapid biochemical viability test using 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride. Viable living tissues stain red due to the reduction of colorless tetrazolium salt by active dehydrogenase enzymes into formazan.
  • Seed Vigor: The sum total of properties that permit rapid, uniform emergence and development of normal seedlings under a wide range of environmental conditions.

Seed Storage, Deterioration, and Legislation in India

Seed storage longevity is governed by Harrington's Rule of Thumb, which states that for every 1% decrease in seed moisture or 5°C decrease in storage temperature, the storage life of the seed is doubled. High humidity and high temperature drastically accelerate seed deterioration through lipid peroxidation and enzyme denaturation.

  • Seed Act of India: Enacted in 1966 and enforced from October 2, 1969, to regulate the quality of seeds sold for agricultural purposes across India.
  • Seed Rules: Framed in 1968, followed by the landmark Seed Control Order of 1983 under the Essential Commodities Act.
  • National Seeds Corporation (NSC): Established in 1963 as a premier public sector undertaking for foundation seed production and distribution.
  • State Seed Certification Agencies (SSCAs): Autonomous bodies responsible for inspecting seed production plots, conducting field counts, and issuing certification tags.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing breeder seed tag color with foundation seed tag color. (Breeder = Golden Yellow, Foundation = White, Certified = Blue).
  • Assuming vegetative propagation material (tubers, cuttings) is excluded from seed legislation. (Legally, all propagules used for sowing are classified as seeds under the Seeds Act 1966).
  • Believing that seed moisture content for oilseeds should be the same as cereals. (Oilseeds require lower moisture content around 7-8% due to high lipid oxidation risk).

Concept 1

Seed Generation System and Genetic Purity

The Indian seed multiplication chain ensures sustained availability of genetically pure seeds through systematic generation phases.

  • Nucleus Seed: 100% pure seed maintained by the originating breeder.
  • Breeder Seed: Direct progeny of nucleus seed with 100% genetic purity (Golden Yellow tag).
  • Foundation Seed: Progeny of breeder seed with 99.5% genetic purity (White tag).
  • Certified Seed: Progeny of foundation seed with 99.0% genetic purity (Azure Blue tag).
Concept 2

Physiological Maturity and Seed Vigor

Seed quality is heavily dependent on the timing of harvest and post-harvest handling.

  • Physiological Maturity: Maximum dry weight accumulation point marked by cessation of translocation from the mother plant.
  • Harvest Maturity: Stage when seed moisture drops to safe handling levels for mechanical threshing.
  • Seed Vigor: Comprehensive physiological attribute determining rapid, uniform emergence under stress conditions.
Concept 3

Seed Testing Principles and ISTA Standards

Standardized laboratory evaluations ensure compliance with national seed standards.

  • Purity Analysis: Gravimetric determination of pure seed, weed seed, and inert matter percentages.
  • Standard Germination: Percentage of normal seedlings produced under optimum environmental conditions.
  • Tetrazolium Test: Rapid enzymatic assay assessing living embryo respiration via red formazan staining.
Concept 4

Seed Storage and Longevity Dynamics

Seed storage preservation relies on controlling environmental factors that accelerate aging.

  • Orthodox Seeds: Seeds that withstand desiccation and low-temperature storage (e.g., most field crops).
  • Recalcitrant Seeds: Seeds that lose viability rapidly when dried below critical moisture thresholds (e.g., mango, jackfruit, rubber).
  • Harrington's Rule: Quantitative rule linking moisture and temperature reductions to doubled storage life.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Breeder Seed VS Foundation Seed

Breeder seed is directly produced by the plant breeder (Golden Yellow tag, 100% purity), whereas Foundation seed is the progeny of breeder seed produced by designated agencies (White tag, 99.5% purity).

Orthodox Seeds VS Recalcitrant Seeds

Orthodox seeds tolerate desiccation and low-temperature storage, while recalcitrant seeds lose viability rapidly when dried below critical moisture levels.
