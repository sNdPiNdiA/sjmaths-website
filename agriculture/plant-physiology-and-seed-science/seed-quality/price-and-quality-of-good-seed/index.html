Price and Quality of Good Seed in Agronomic Production

Economic Value and Cost-Benefit Dynamics of Quality Seeds

High-quality seed acts as the catalyst for agricultural productivity, amplifying the responsiveness of crops to applied agro-inputs such as fertilizers, irrigation, and plant protection chemicals. Although quality seeds command a higher initial purchase price relative to farmer-saved grain, their incremental cost is negligible when amortized across total crop yield and economic returns.

  • Investment Multiplier: Seed is a primary, non-divisible technology input; a superior seed lot guarantees higher germination percentages, eliminating gap-filling costs and reducing required seed rates.
  • Input Efficiency: Certified seeds of high-yielding varieties (HYVs) and hybrids exhibit uniform emergence and vigour, maximizing nutrient uptake efficiency from applied fertilizers like urea and diammonium phosphate (DAP).
  • Risk Mitigation: Premium pricing reflects rigorous seed production standards, rouging, field inspections, and laboratory testing, which collectively protect farmers from crop failure due to seed-borne pathogens or weed seed contamination.

Seed Quality Parameters: Physical, Physiological, and Genetic Purity

Seed quality is a multidimensional attribute evaluated through distinct biological and physical parameters governed by the Seed Act (1966) and Minimum Seed Certification Standards (Indian Minimum Seed Certification Standards - IMSCS).

  • Physical Purity (%): Measures the proportion of pure seed of the designated crop species in a sample, free from inert matter, weed seeds, and other crop seeds. Minimum physical purity for most cereals is maintained at 98%.
  • Germination Percentage (%): The emergence and development from the seed embryo of those essential structures which indicate its ability to produce a normal plant under favourable field conditions. For Triticum aestivum (wheat) and Oryza sativa (paddy), minimum germination is 85%.
  • Genetic Purity (%): The true-to-type nature of the seed lot, ensuring that offspring retain the exact phenotypic and genotypic characteristics of the cultivar. Maintained through strict isolation distances and rouging of off-types during flowering.
  • Seed Moisture Content (%): Crucial for safe storage. Safe moisture levels for orthodox seeds like cereals range between 10-12%, while oilseeds like Glycine max (soybean) and Arachis hypogaea (groundnut) require 7-8% to prevent lipid peroxidation and fungal infestation.

Seed Classes and Generation System of Multiplication

Seed multiplication follows a structured hierarchical system to maintain genetic identity and purity while scaling up volume for commercial distribution to farming communities.

  • Nucleus Seed: The initial quantum of seed derived from a single plant or plant selection, maintained by the originating plant breeder or institution. It has 100% genetic purity and no tag.
  • Breeder Seed: Seed produced directly from nucleus seed under the supervision of the plant breeder. It is supervised by monitoring committees and carries a Golden Yellow tag.
  • Foundation Seed: Progeny of breeder seed handled to maintain specific genetic identity and purity. It is produced by state seed corporations, agricultural universities, or designated agencies and carries a White tag.
  • Certified Seed: Progeny of foundation seed (or occasionally registered seed) handled to maintain satisfactory genetic identity and purity, approved and certified by the State Seed Certification Agency. It carries an Azure Blue tag for commercial sale to farmers.

Seed Viability, Vigour, and Physiological Deterioration

Seed viability refers to whether a seed is alive and capable of germinating under optimal conditions, whereas seed vigour encompasses the sum total of properties that determine the potential level of activity and performance of the seed or seed lot during germination and seedling emergence.

  • Tetrazolium (TZ) Test: A rapid biochemical test utilizing 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride to evaluate seed viability by staining living tissues red due to the reduction of tetrazolium salt by respiring dehydrogenase enzymes.
  • Accelerated Ageing Test: A stress test used to evaluate seed vigour by exposing seeds to high temperature (41-45°C) and high relative humidity (nearly 100%) for a short duration before standard germination testing.
  • Causes of Deterioration: Mechanical damage during threshing (especially in legumes like Cicer arietinum and soybean), insect damage by Callosobruchus chinensis, high storage humidity leading to fungal invasion by Aspergillus and Penicillium species, and natural lipid auto-oxidation.

Seed Enhancement Techniques and Seed Treatment Protocols

Pre-sowing seed treatments enhance field emergence, protect against soil-borne and seed-borne pathogens, and stimulate early seedling vigour.

  • Fungicidal Seed Dressing: Application of systemic and contact fungicides such as Carbendazim or Thiram at 2.0 to 2.5 g per kg of seed to control seed-borne fungal pathogens like Ustilago nuda (loose smut of wheat).
  • Inoculation with Biofertilizers: Coating legume seeds with Rhizobium strains and cereal seeds with Azospirillum or Azotobacter cultures using jaggery slurry to enhance biological nitrogen fixation.
  • Seed Priming: Soaking seeds in water or osmotic solutions (hydropriming, halopriming) for predefined durations to initiate early metabolic events of germination without radicle emergence, ensuring synchronized field emergence.

  • Certified seed, sold commercially to farmers, carries an Azure Blue tag.
  • The Tetrazolium test is a biochemical assay used to rapidly determine seed viability without waiting for germination.
  • Seed moisture content for safe storage of orthodox cereal seeds should be kept below 12%.
  • Loose smut of wheat (Ustilago nuda) is an internal seed-borne pathogen controlled effectively by systemic fungicides like Carboxin or Carbendazim.

  • Seed Act in India was enacted in the year 1966, and Seed Rules were framed in 1968.
  • National Seeds Corporation (NSC) was established in the year 1963.
  • Isolation distance is mandatory in seed production plots to prevent genetic contamination via cross-pollination.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing breeder seed tag color with foundation seed tag color; breeder seed is Golden Yellow while foundation seed is White.
  • Assuming seed viability and seed vigour are synonymous terms; viability measures if the seed is alive, while vigour measures its performance capacity under stress.
  • Believing that seed moisture content can be measured accurately by hand touch; precise moisture determination requires hot-air oven drying methods or electronic moisture meters.
  • Confusing certified seed with truthfully labelled (TL) seed; certified seed requires compulsory certification by an agency, whereas TL seed is the responsibility of the producer.

Economic Rationale of Seed Pricing

Seed pricing reflects the R&D investment, rigorous field inspection, rouging, processing, packaging, and quality testing involved in production.

Concept 2

Seed Quality Certification Framework

Seed certification is a legally sanctioned system for quality control of seed multiplication and distribution, ensuring varietal purity and health.

  • Seed Act 1966: Legislative framework regulating the quality of seeds sold in India.
Concept 3

Generation System of Seed Multiplication

Controlled multiplication ensures genetic degradation is minimized across generations from nucleus to certified seed.

  • Multiplication Ratio: Dictates the speed at which a superior cultivar can be scaled up for commercial farming.
Concept 4

Physiological Seed Testing Parameters

Laboratory testing evaluates germination potential, moisture content, and vigour to predict field emergence success.

  • Tetrazolium Test: Vital biochemical test assessing living tissue respiration via dehydrogenase activity.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Breeder Seed (Primary Genetic Stock)
Seed produced directly by the plant breeder, possessing 100% genetic purity and carrying a Golden Yellow tag.
Foundation Seed (Secondary Multiplication Stock)
Progeny of breeder seed handled to maintain genetic identity, carrying a White tag.
Certified Seed (Commercial Planting Stock)
Progeny of foundation seed certified by State Seed Certification Agency, carrying an Azure Blue tag.
Seed Viability (Live Embryo Capacity)
The capability of a seed to germinate and produce a normal seedling under favourable conditions.
Seed Vigour (Comprehensive Performance Potential)
The sum total of seed properties determining activity level and performance during germination and emergence.
Physical Purity (Clean Seed Percentage)
The percentage by weight of pure seed relative to inert matter, weed seeds, and other crop seeds in a sample.
Genetic Purity (True-to-Type Percentage)
The percentage of seeds belonging to the exact designated cultivar, free from off-types.
Isolation Distance (Spatial Buffer Barrier)
Minimum physical separation maintained between two different crop varieties to prevent cross-pollination.
Rouging (Off-Type Removal)
The process of identifying and removing diseased, off-type, or weed plants from a seed production field.
Tetrazolium Test (TTC Biochemical Assay)
A rapid biochemical test using 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride to evaluate seed viability.
Truthfully Labelled Seed (TL Seed)
Seeds that do not mandatorily require state certification but must meet minimum standards declared by the producer on the label.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which seed class has 100% genetic purity and is produced under the direct supervision of the plant breeder?

Explanation: Breeder seed is the progeny of nucleus seed produced directly by the originating plant breeder or institution. It has 100% genetic purity and carries a Golden Yellow tag.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the official tag color of Certified Seed in India?

Explanation: Certified seed, which is the progeny of foundation seed sold to farmers for commercial cultivation, carries an Azure Blue tag.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In which year was the Seeds Act enacted in India?

Explanation: The Seeds Act was enacted by the Indian Parliament in 1966 to regulate the quality of seeds for sale and matters connected therewith.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chemical reagent used in the Tetrazolium test to evaluate seed viability?

Explanation: The Tetrazolium test utilizes 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (TTC), which reacts with hydrogen ions released by respiring living cells to form a red-colored formazan precipitate.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended maximum seed moisture content for safe storage of orthodox cereal seeds like wheat and paddy?

Explanation: Orthodox cereal seeds like wheat and paddy can be safely stored without rapid deterioration or fungal attack when moisture content is maintained between 10-12%.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which institution was established in 1963 to undertake production of foundation and certified seeds in India?

Explanation: National Seeds Corporation (NSC) was incorporated in 1963 as a public sector undertaking under the Ministry of Agriculture to promote seed production and quality control.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the minimum germination percentage standard prescribed for certified wheat seed under IMSCS?

Explanation: According to the Indian Minimum Seed Certification Standards (IMSCS), certified wheat seed requires a minimum germination percentage of 85%.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the tag color associated with Foundation Seed?

Explanation: Foundation seed is the progeny of breeder seed and carries a White tag to indicate its intermediate generation status in seed multiplication.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which term describes the removal of off-types, diseased plants, and weeds from a seed production plot?

Explanation: Rouging is the critical agronomic operation of identifying and removing off-type plants, weeds, and diseased plants from a seed crop to prevent genetic contamination.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does seed vigour primarily measure?

Explanation: Seed vigour encompasses the sum total of seed properties that determine the potential level of activity and performance of the seed lot during germination and seedling emergence under diverse environmental conditions.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following oilseed crops requires the lowest storage moisture content due to high lipid oxidation risk?

Explanation: Soybean and other oilseeds contain high levels of polyunsaturated fatty acids, necessitating strict storage moisture levels of 7-8% to prevent rapid physiological deterioration.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of maintaining isolation distance in seed production plots?

Explanation: Isolation distance provides a physical spatial barrier between different varieties of the same crop species to prevent cross-pollination by wind or insects, thereby preserving genetic purity.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which fungus is responsible for loose smut of wheat, an internally seed-borne pathogen controlled by seed treatment?

Explanation: Ustilago nuda causes loose smut of wheat. Since the mycelium resides inside the seed embryo, systemic fungicides like Carboxin or Carbendazim are required for effective seed treatment.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is Truthfully Labelled (TL) seed?

Explanation: Truthfully Labelled (TL) seeds are not compulsorily required to be certified by the state seed certification agency, but the producer must ensure and declare minimum quality standards on the label.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which test subjects seeds to high temperature and high humidity conditions to evaluate storage potential and vigour?

Explanation: The accelerated ageing test exposes seeds to stress conditions (approx. 41-45°C and nearly 100% relative humidity) for a specified period, after which germination is tested to estimate vigour and storability.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the minimum physical purity percentage required for certified cereal seeds under Indian standards?

Explanation: Under IMSCS, the minimum physical purity for certified cereal seeds is 98%, ensuring that weed seeds and inert matter are kept to minimal thresholds.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why is high-quality seed considered a non-divisible technology input in agriculture?

Explanation: Seed is termed a non-divisible input because its intrinsic genetic and physiological potential must be accepted as a whole unit; its benefits cannot be fractionated or substituted by other inputs.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which enzyme group in living seed tissues reduces tetrazolium chloride to form red formazan during the viability test?

Explanation: Dehydrogenase enzymes associated with active cellular respiration in living tissues reduce the colorless tetrazolium salt into a red, water-insoluble compound called formazan.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical seed multiplication ratio for paddy (rice)?

Explanation: The seed multiplication ratio for paddy generally ranges around 1:80 to 1:100, meaning one unit weight of seed sown yields eighty to one hundred unit weights of harvestable seed.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary risk associated with mechanical damage during threshing of grain legumes like soybean and chickpea?

Explanation: Mechanical damage during threshing causes cotyledon cracking and seed coat rupture in legumes, making them highly susceptible to pathogen invasion and rapid loss of viability.

Analyze the hierarchy of seed classes. Which sequence accurately represents the flow of seed multiplication from elite stock to commercial farming?

Solution & Rationale: The correct sequence begins with Nucleus seed (single plant selection), which is multiplied into Breeder seed (Golden Yellow tag), then Foundation seed (White tag), and finally Certified seed (Azure Blue tag) for commercial farmers.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

A seed lot of wheat shows 90% physical purity and 85% germination. According to Indian Minimum Seed Certification Standards (IMSCS), why would this seed lot fail certification?

Solution & Rationale: Under IMSCS, certified wheat seed requires a minimum physical purity of 98%. Since the sample has 90% physical purity, it fails certification despite having acceptable germination (85%).

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

In the Tetrazolium (TZ) viability test, what does an entirely unstained, white embryo indicate?

Solution & Rationale: An unstained, milky-white embryo in a TZ test indicates dead tissue, as non-viable cells lack active dehydrogenase enzymes to reduce tetrazolium chloride into red formazan.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Why are oilseed crops like soybean dried to much lower moisture levels (7-8%) compared to cereal crops (10-12%) for safe storage?

Solution & Rationale: Oilseeds contain high concentrations of triglycerides and unsaturated fatty acids. Higher moisture levels accelerate lipid peroxidation and microbial growth, necessitating stricter moisture limits of 7-8%.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary agronomic advantage of paying a higher price for certified seed compared to using farm-saved grain?

Solution & Rationale: Certified seed guarantees genetic purity, high physical purity, and rigorous germination standards, which translate to uniform emergence, vigorous early growth, and maximum response to fertilizers.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following statements regarding Breeder Seed is scientifically correct?

Solution & Rationale: Breeder seed is the direct progeny of nucleus seed, supervised by the originating breeder, maintains 100% genetic purity, and is identified by a Golden Yellow tag.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the role of isolation distance in seed production fields?

Solution & Rationale: Isolation distance prevents pollen contamination from adjacent fields growing different varieties or species, thereby preserving the genetic purity of the seed crop.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What does the Accelerated Ageing Test measure in seed science?

Solution & Rationale: The accelerated ageing test evaluates seed vigour by artificially stressing seeds under high temperature and humidity before germination testing, revealing how well the seed will store and emerge under adverse field conditions.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which agency in India is legally empowered to issue certification for foundation and certified seeds?

Solution & Rationale: State Seed Certification Agencies (SSCA), established under Section 8 of the Seeds Act 1966, are statutory bodies responsible for field inspections and seed certification.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Why is mechanical injury during threshing particularly detrimental to grain legume seeds like chickpea and soybean?

Solution & Rationale: Grain legumes have large epigeal cotyledons and delicate embryonic axes situated near the seed surface. Threshing trauma cracks the seed coat and damages cotyledons, allowing pathogen entry and rapid loss of viability.
