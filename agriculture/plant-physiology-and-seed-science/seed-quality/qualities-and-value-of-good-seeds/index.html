Qualities and Value of Good Seeds in Crop Production

Introduction to Seed Science and Quality Concept

A seed is a fertilized mature ovule possessing an embryonic plant, stored food reserves, and a protective seed coat, capable of propagating its species. In modern agronomy, a seed is considered the basic, vital, and least expensive input for sustainable agricultural production. According to J.R. Harlan, quality seed is the catalyst that maximizes the genetic potential of a crop variety.

  • Definition: A good quality seed must be genetically pure, physically clean, biologically viable, and physiologically vigorous.
  • Role in Agriculture: It ensures rapid and uniform field emergence, optimal plant stand, robust root and shoot development, and high tolerance to biotic and abiotic stresses.
  • Economic Impact: High-quality seeds increase fertilizer use efficiency, reduce replanting costs, and guarantee higher marketable yield per unit area.

Core Attributes of Good Quality Seeds

The agronomic value of a seed lot is determined by four principal quality parameters recognized by the Indian Minimum Seed Certification Standards (IMSCS):

  • Genetic Purity: True-to-type status of the seed lot representing the specific cultivar without mechanical or genetic contamination from off-types.
  • Physical Purity: Freedom from inert matter, weed seeds, other crop seeds, and damaged or shrivelled seeds, expressed as a weight percentage.
  • Physiological Quality: High seed germination percentage and seedling vigor index, ensuring robust field emergence under variable soil temperatures and moisture.
  • Health Status: Complete absence of seed-borne fungal, bacterial, viral pathogens, and insect infestations (e.g., loose smut in Triticum aestivum).

Classes of Seeds in Seed Multiplication Chain

Seed production follows a strict hierarchical multiplication system to maintain genetic purity and prevent genetic drift:

  • Nucleus Seed: The initial, highly pure seed stock produced and maintained by the original plant breeder or originating institution. Tag color: None (Handled by breeder).
  • Breeder Seed: Progeny of nucleus seed, supervised by the plant breeder. It has 100% genetic purity. Tag color: Golden Yellow.
  • Foundation Seed: Progeny of breeder seed, produced by state agricultural universities, national seeds corporation, and authorized farms. Tag color: White.
  • Certified Seed: Progeny of foundation seed, distributed to farmers for commercial grain and fiber production. Tag color: Azure Blue.

Seed Testing, Sampling, and Viability Determination

Seed testing is conducted as per International Seed Testing Association (ISTA) rules to evaluate planting value:

  • Sampling: Representative samples are drawn using spear triphers from composite bags following standard protocols.
  • Germination Test: Conducted using Between Paper (BP), Top of Paper (TP), or Sand method at optimal temperatures (e.g., 20°C to 30°C).
  • Tetrazolium (TZ) Test: A rapid biochemical viability test using 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride. Living tissues stain red due to respiring living cells reducing the colorless salt to formazan.
  • Vigor Testing: Measured via accelerated aging, electrical conductivity, and cold test to assess field emergence potential under stress.

Seed Processing, Drying, and Storage Management

Raw harvested seeds undergo rigorous post-harvest processing before bagging:

  • Seed Drying: Safe storage moisture content varies by crop; cereals like Triticum aestivum and Oryza sativa require safe moisture below 12%, while oilseeds like Glycine max and Arachis hypogaea require 8% to 9%.
  • Cleaning and Grading: Air-screen cleaners separate inert matter by size and specific gravity using gravity separators.
  • Seed Treatment: Application of fungicides (e.g., Carbendazim, Thiram) and biofertilizers (Azotobacter, Rhizobium) to protect against seed-borne pathogens and enhance early vigor.
  • Storage Conditions: Low temperature and low relative humidity (RH) rule (e.g., Sum of temperature in °F and RH in % should not exceed 100) are maintained in modern warehouses.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Foundation seed tag color with Certified seed tag color; Foundation is White while Certified is Azure Blue.
  • Assuming seed viability and seed vigor are synonymous; viability indicates living status, while vigor denotes performance under stress.
  • Believing that nucleus seed has a certification tag; nucleus seed is unlabelled and handled strictly by breeders.
  • Treating oilseed crops with high storage moisture; oilseeds require lower moisture (8-9%) than cereals (12%) to prevent rancidity.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Qualities and Value of Good Seeds in Crop Production

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Seed Quality Parameters

Seed quality encompasses multiple biological and physical traits that dictate crop performance.

  • Genetic Purity: Exact conformity to variety description without off-types.
  • Physical Purity: Freedom from inert matter, weed seeds, and broken kernels.
  • Physiological Quality: High germination percentage and rapid seedling vigor.
Concept 2

Seed Multiplication Chain

The controlled generation system maintains genetic integrity from breeder to commercial farmer.

  • Nucleus Seed: Breeder's initial stock.
  • Breeder Seed: Golden Yellow tag, 100% purity.
  • Foundation Seed: White tag, 99.5% purity.
  • Certified Seed: Azure Blue tag, 99.0% purity.
Concept 3

Seed Testing and Viability

Laboratory testing ensures sowing value conforms to statutory standards.

  • Tetrazolium Test: Biochemical test turning living embryo tissue red.
  • Germination Test: Standard blotter or sand method measuring normal seedlings.
  • Vigor Index: Product of germination % and total seedling length.
Concept 4

Seed Storage and Moisture Management

Proper storage prevents deterioration, insect damage, and loss of viability.

  • Safe Moisture: 10-12% for cereals, 8-9% for oilseeds.
  • Harrington's Rule: Sum of temperature in °F and RH in % should be below 100 for safe open storage.
  • Seed Treatment: Fungicidal dusting and inoculation with bio-fertilizers.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Nucleus Seed (Breeder Maintained Initial Stock)
The initial stock of a variety produced by the originating plant breeder, serving as the basis for all further seed multiplication.
Breeder Seed (Progeny of Nucleus Seed)
Seed produced directly by the plant breeder with 100% genetic purity, bearing a golden yellow tag.
Foundation Seed (Progeny of Breeder Seed)
The primary source for certified seed production, handled to maintain specific genetic identity and purity (white tag).
Certified Seed (Commercial Farm Seed)
Progeny of foundation seed produced by registered growers, bearing an azure blue tag for commercial crop production.
Genetic Purity (True-to-Type Percentage)
The percentage of seeds belonging to the specific cultivar being evaluated, free from off-types and mutations.
Physical Purity (Clean Seed Percentage)
The proportion by weight of pure seed relative to inert matter, weed seeds, and other crop seeds in a sample.
Tetrazolium Test (2,3,5-Triphenyl Tetrazolium Chloride Test)
A rapid biochemical viability assay where respiring living tissues reduce colorless tetrazolium salt into red formazan.
Seed Vigor (Physiological Potential under Stress)
The sum total of properties that determine the potential for rapid, uniform emergence and development of normal seedlings under diverse field conditions.
Inert Matter (Non-seed Components)
All matter in a seed sample that is not true seed, including chaff, stones, dirt, and broken seed fragments less than half size.
Seed Certification (Legally Controlled Quality Assurance)
A legally sanctioned system for quality control of seed increase and production, ensuring high genetic purity and physical standards.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Breeder seed genetic purity is 100%; Foundation is 99.5%; Certified is 99.0%.
  • Harrington's rule: Storage Temp (°F) + Relative Humidity (%) < 100.
  • ISTA rules govern international seed testing procedures.
  • Loose smut of wheat is a internal seed-borne disease controlled by systemic fungicide Carboxin.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Good quality seed is characterized by genetic purity, physical purity, high germination, and disease-free health.
  • The seed multiplication chain follows: Nucleus -> Breeder (Golden Yellow) -> Foundation (White) -> Certified (Azure Blue).
  • Tetrazolium test determines seed viability biochemically without waiting for germination.
  • Safe storage moisture is lower for oilseeds (8-9%) compared to cereals (10-12%).
  • Seed vigor index combines germination percentage and seedling length.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Seed Viability VS Seed Vigor

Viability is the capacity of a seed to germinate under favorable conditions, whereas vigor includes the ability to germinate rapidly and uniformly under wide environmental stress conditions.

Foundation Seed VS Certified Seed

Foundation seed is produced from breeder seed under strict breeder supervision with a white tag (99.5% purity), while certified seed is produced from foundation seed with an azure blue tag (99.0% purity).

What is the official tag color of Breeder Seed?

Question 2 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical is used in the Tetrazolium test for determining seed viability?

Explanation: 2,3,5-Triphenyl tetrazolium chloride is reduced by respiring living cells to form red formazan, identifying viable embryos.

Question 3 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the minimum genetic purity standard required for Foundation Seed?

Explanation: Foundation seed requires a minimum genetic purity of 99.5% as per Indian Minimum Seed Certification Standards.

Question 4 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Harrington's rule for safe seed storage states that the sum of storage temperature in Fahrenheit and relative humidity in percentage should not exceed:

Explanation: Harrington's rule states that Temperature (°F) + Relative Humidity (%) should be less than 100 for safe open seed storage.

Question 5 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the safe moisture content for storing soybean seeds?

Explanation: Soybean being an oilseed contains high fat content and must be stored at 8 to 9% moisture to prevent rapid lipid oxidation and loss of viability.

Question 6 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the tag color of Certified Seed?

Explanation: Certified seed bags carry an Azure Blue tag issued by the State Seed Certification Agency.

Question 7 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Seed vigor index is calculated as:

Explanation: Seed vigor index = Germination percentage × Total seedling length (Root length + Shoot length).

Question 8 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is an internal seed-borne pathogen controlled by systemic fungicides?

Explanation: Ustilago nuda tritici causes loose smut of wheat and is internal seed-borne, requiring systemic fungicides like Carboxin for eradication.

Question 9 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the minimum physical purity percentage required for certified cereal seeds?

Explanation: Minimum physical purity for most field crops like cereals is 98%, limiting inert matter and weed seeds.

Question 10 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Who maintains the Nucleus Seed?

Explanation: Nucleus seed is the initial stock maintained solely by the originating plant breeder or institution.

Question 11 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of seed processing?

Explanation: Seed processing involves cleaning, drying, and grading to eliminate physical impurities and upgrade seed lot quality.

Question 12 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which international body standardizes seed testing procedures globally?

Explanation: ISTA (International Seed Testing Association) formulates uniform rules for seed testing across the world.

Question 13 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What does 'off-type' mean in seed production fields?

Explanation: Off-types are plants within a crop stand that deviate in morphological traits from the true description of the variety and must be rogued out.

Question 14 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following crop seeds has epigeal germination?

Explanation: Soybean exhibits epigeal germination where the hypocotyl elongates, lifting cotyledons above the ground, unlike hypogeal germination in wheat and pea.

Question 15 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the genetic purity percentage of Foundation seed?

Explanation: Foundation seed has a minimum genetic purity standard of 99.5%.

Question 16 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the purpose of seed scarification?

Explanation: Scarification mechanically or chemically abrades hard seed coats to make them permeable to water and oxygen, breaking physical dormancy.

Question 17 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

In seed sampling, what is a composite sample?

Explanation: A composite sample is formed by thoroughly mixing all the primary samples drawn from various containers in a seed lot.

Question 18 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the critical seed moisture content for attack by storage insects like rice weevil?

Explanation: Storage insects and fungi proliferate rapidly when seed moisture content rises above 10% to 12%.

Test Question 1 of 10 TGT Standard

In the seed multiplication chain, which seed class is the direct progeny of Foundation seed?

Solution & Rationale: The sequence is Nucleus -> Breeder -> Foundation -> Certified. Therefore, Certified seed is the direct progeny of Foundation seed.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the minimum germination percentage prescribed for certified wheat seeds under Indian Minimum Seed Certification Standards?

Solution & Rationale: Certified seeds of wheat (Triticum aestivum) must meet a minimum germination standard of 85%.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

Which seed quality parameter is evaluated using the accelerated aging test?

Solution & Rationale: The accelerated aging test exposes seeds to high temperature (41-45°C) and near 100% relative humidity to evaluate seed vigor potential.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

What color is the certification tag for Foundation seed?

Solution & Rationale: Foundation seed tags are White, Breeder seeds are Golden Yellow, and Certified seeds are Azure Blue.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary reason for maintaining isolation distance in seed production plots?

Solution & Rationale: Isolation distance prevents foreign pollen contamination (cross-pollination) and mechanical mixing from adjacent crop fields.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

During tetrazolium testing, what indicates a non-viable seed embryo?

Solution & Rationale: Living respiring cells reduce the salt to red formazan. Dead tissues lack respiring enzymes and remain unstained (white/colorless).

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following crop varieties requires the highest isolation distance for certified seed production due to cross-pollination?

Solution & Rationale: Maize is cross-pollinated primarily by wind and requires larger isolation distances (e.g., 200-400 meters) compared to self-pollinated wheat and rice.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the term used for the removal of diseased, off-type, and undesirable plants from a seed production plot?

Solution & Rationale: Roguing is the systematic removal and destruction of off-types, weeds, and diseased plants to maintain genetic purity.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the safe moisture limit for long-term hermetic seed storage of vegetable seeds belonging to family Solanaceae?

Solution & Rationale: Vegetable seeds require very low moisture content (6-7%) for safe long-term storage to preserve enzyme activity and prevent respiration loss.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

Which agency is legally empowered to issue seed certification in Indian states?

Solution & Rationale: State Seed Certification Agencies (SSCAs), established under the Seeds Act 1966, are legally mandated to perform seed certification.
