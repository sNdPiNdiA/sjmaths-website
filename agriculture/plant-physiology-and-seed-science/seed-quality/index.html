Introduction to Seed Quality and Its Attributes

Seed quality is the cornerstone of modern agricultural productivity. High-quality seed acts as a catalyst for realizing the full genetic potential of a crop variety, ensuring uniform crop stand, enhanced seedling vigor, and higher resistance to biotic and abiotic stresses. According to ICAR and international seed testing standards (ISTA), seed quality comprises multiple interdependent attributes:

  • Genetic Purity: The true-to-type nature of the seed, ensuring that it possesses the specific genetic constitution of the variety without mechanical or out-crossing contamination.
  • Physical Purity: The proportion of pure seed of the designated crop relative to inert matter, weed seeds, and other crop seeds, expressed as a percentage by weight.
  • Physiological Quality: Encompasses seed germination capacity, seedling vigor, and viability. It determines how rapidly and uniformly seeds will emerge under field conditions.
  • Sanitary Quality: The freedom of the seed lot from seed-borne pathogens (fungi, bacteria, viruses, nematodes) and insect pests.

Seed Generation System and Classes of Seeds

To maintain genetic purity and identity, seed production follows a strict, well-defined generation system governed by national certification agencies (e.g., State Seed Certification Agencies under the Seeds Act, 1966). The recognized classes of seeds are:

  • Nucleus Seed: The initial, highly pure seed stock produced and maintained by the originating plant breeder or institution. It serves as the source for breeder seed and has 100% genetic purity.
  • Breeder Seed: Seed directly controlled by the originating plant breeder. It is produced with supervision and used to multiply foundation seed. Tag color: Golden Yellow.
  • Foundation Seed: The progeny of breeder seed, handled to maintain specific genetic identity and purity. It is the source of certified or registered seed, produced by state seed corporations or authorized agencies. Tag color: White.
  • Registered Seed: The progeny of foundation seed handled to maintain satisfactory genetic purity and identity, serving as the source for certified seed (optional class in many Indian states). Tag color: Purple.
  • Certified Seed: The progeny of foundation or registered seed. It is widely distributed to farmers for commercial crop production. Tag color: Azure Blue.

Seed Sampling, Testing, and ISTA Standards

Seed testing is conducted in accredited laboratories following International Seed Testing Association (ISTA) rules to evaluate planting value. Key parameters assessed include:

  • Seed Sampling: Drawing representative samples from seed lots using triphala or spear samplers following strict randomization across containers.
  • Purity Analysis (%): Separation of a working sample into pure seed, other crop seeds, weed seeds, and inert matter.
  • Germination Test (%): Evaluation under optimal moisture, temperature, and light conditions (e.g., between paper or top of paper methods) to count normal seedlings. Minimum seed germination standards for cereals like Triticum aestivum and Oryza sativa is generally 80%.
  • Moisture Content Determination: Measured using the high-temperature or low-temperature oven method. Safe storage moisture content varies by crop (e.g., cereals: 10-12%, oilseeds: 7-9%).
  • Vigor Testing: Accelerated aging test, electrical conductivity test, and cold test to measure physiological stamina beyond standard germination.

Seed Processing, Drying, and Treatment Operations

Seed processing involves preparing harvested seed for marketing through cleaning, upgrading, and drying. Essential mechanical operations include:

  • Seed Drying: Reducing seed moisture to safe levels without damaging embryo viability. Natural sun drying or forced-air mechanical drying (temperatures not exceeding 43°C for most crops) is utilized.
  • Air-Screen Cleaner: Separates seeds based on size and width using wire mesh screens and airflow (aspiration).
  • Gravity Separator: Separates seeds of the same size but differing in specific weight or density (removing weathered, insect-damaged, or lightweight weed seeds).
  • Seed Treatment: Application of fungicides (e.g., Carbendazim, Thiram) and insecticides to protect seeds from soil-borne and seed-borne pathogens. Inoculation with biofertilizers like Rhizobium or Azotobacter is done last.

Seed Certification, Legislation, and Institutional Framework

Regulatory frameworks ensure that farmers receive high-quality planting material. In India, key legislative acts and institutions include:

  • The Seeds Act, 1966: Enacted to regulate the quality of seeds for sale and establish Central and State Seed Committees.
  • The Seeds Rules, 1968: Detailed operational guidelines for seed testing, labeling, and certification standards.
  • Seed Control Order, 1983: Issued under the Essential Commodities Act to regulate licensing, distribution, and price control of seeds.
  • Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights (PPV&FR) Act, 2001: Established to protect plant breeders' intellectual property and recognize farmers' traditional rights in conserving landraces and traditional varieties.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Breeder seed is produced under the direct supervision of the originating plant breeder and has a golden yellow tag.
  • Certified seed is the progeny of foundation seed and bears an azure blue certification tag.
  • The Seeds Act in India was enacted in the year 1966 and came into force fully in 1969.
  • Minimum seed germination standard for hybrid cotton is 65%, while for wheat and paddy it is 85% and 80% respectively.
  • Seed moisture content for safe orthodox seed storage should generally be kept below 10-12%.
  • Air-screen machine separates seeds primarily on the basis of size and width differences.
  • Gravity separator works on the principle of seed specific gravity (density).
  • PPV&FR Authority was established under the Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001.
  • Tetrazolium test (TZ test) is a rapid biochemical test used to determine seed viability using 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride.
  • Foundation seed is produced from breeder seed and has a minimum genetic purity requirement of 99.5%.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing breeder seed tag color with foundation seed tag color: Breeder seed tag is golden yellow, whereas foundation seed tag is white.
  • Assuming seed viability and seed vigor are synonymous: Viability refers to whether a seed is alive, while vigor refers to its performance potential under stress conditions.
  • Believing that certified seed can be endlessly re-saved by farmers without losing genetic purity: Certified seed purity degrades due to mechanical mixing and out-crossing over generations.

Concept 1

Seed Quality Parameters

Seed quality is multi-dimensional, combining genetic, physical, physiological, and health attributes.

  • Genetic Purity: True-to-type status maintained by rouging and isolation distances.
  • Physical Purity: Freedom from inert matter and weed seeds determined in seed testing laboratories.
Concept 2

Seed Generation Chain

The orderly multiplication of seed stocks ensures commercial availability without genetic deterioration.

  • Nucleus to Certified: Progressive multiplication through Breeder and Foundation stages.
Concept 3

Seed Testing and ISTA Protocols

Laboratory procedures standardize quality evaluation worldwide.

  • Germination %: Standard percentage of normal seedlings produced under optimal conditions.
Concept 4

Seed Processing Machinery

Mechanical separation techniques upgrade raw harvested seed into certified marketable lots.

  • Air-Screen Cleaner: Primary cleaning machine utilizing scalping, grading, and aspiration.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Breeder Seed (Nucleus Progeny Stock)
Seed produced directly by the originating breeder with golden yellow tag and 99.9% genetic purity.
Foundation Seed (Progeny of Breeder Seed)
Source of registered and certified seed produced by authorized agencies with a white tag.
Certified Seed (Commercial Planting Seed)
Progeny of foundation seed sold to farmers for commercial cultivation with an azure blue tag.
Germination Percentage (Standard Viability Index)
Proportion of seeds in a test sample that produce normal seedlings under specified conditions.
Seed Vigor (Physiological Stamina)
Sum total of properties that determine the potential for rapid, uniform emergence across diverse field conditions.
Inert Matter (Non-Seed Component)
All matter in a seed sample that is not true seed, including chaff, stones, dust, and broken seed units.
Rouging (Off-Type Removal)
The removal of diseased, off-type, or weed plants from a seed production plot prior to flowering.
Isolation Distance (Spatial Separation)
Minimum geographical distance maintained between two different varieties of the same crop to prevent cross-pollination.
Tetrazolium Test (TZ Viability Assay)
A biochemical test utilizing 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride to stain respiring living embryo tissues red.
Seed Dormancy (Temporarily Arrested Development)
A physiological condition in which viable seeds fail to germinate even when provided favorable environmental conditions.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Seed Viability VS Seed Vigor

Viability is the biological capacity of a seed to germinate, whereas vigor indicates the level of activity and performance under sub-optimal stress conditions.

In the seed production chain, which class serves as the immediate precursor for foundation seed multiplication?

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the recommended isolation distance for producing certified seed of hybrid maize to prevent contamination?

Solution & Rationale: For hybrid maize seed production, an isolation distance of 200-400 meters (typically 400m for foundation and 200m for certified) is maintained against foreign pollen sources.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

Which test evaluates the physiological stamina and potential field emergence of a seed lot under simulated stress conditions?

Solution & Rationale: The accelerated aging test exposes seeds to high temperature (41-45°C) and high relative humidity (nearly 100%) for a short duration to measure vigor.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Under the Seeds Act 1966, what legal provision makes labeling mandatory for seed containers sold in the market?

Solution & Rationale: Section 7 of the Seeds Act, 1966 regulates the sale of notified seeds and mandates container labeling for truth-in-labeling.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following describes 'Orthodox' seeds?

Solution & Rationale: Orthodox seeds (e.g., most cereals and pulses) withstand drying and low-temperature storage, unlike recalcitrant seeds.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the maximum permissible weed seed count per kg in certified wheat seed according to Indian seed certification standards?

Solution & Rationale: Maximum permissible other crop seeds and weed seeds are stringently restricted; for wheat certified seed, objectionable and total weed seeds have strict low limits (e.g., max 5 weed seeds per kg).

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Which enzyme system is primarily responsible for reducing tetrazolium chloride to red formazan in living plant tissues?

Solution & Rationale: Dehydrogenase enzymes active in living cells' respiration reduce colorless tetrazolium salt into insoluble red formazan.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

What is 'Hard Seed' phenomenon commonly observed in Fabaceae (Leguminosae) family crops?

Solution & Rationale: Hard seeds have impermeable palisade cell layers in the seed coat that prevent water entry, causing delayed germination.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Which organization coordinates seed certification work across various states in India at the national level?

Solution & Rationale: The Central Seed Certification Board advises the central and state governments on all matters pertaining to seed certification.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary function of a magnetic seed separator?

Solution & Rationale: Magnetic separators remove rough weed seeds (like dodder) from smooth crop seeds by mixing iron powder that adheres to rough surfaces and is removed by a magnet.
