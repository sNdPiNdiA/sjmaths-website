Introduction to Agricultural Meteorology and Weather Dynamics

Agricultural Meteorology (Agrometeorology) is a specialized branch of applied meteorology that investigates the responses of biological production systems—crops and livestock—to weather and climatic environments. Weather describes the instantaneous state of the atmosphere at a specific location over a short temporal scale (hours to days), characterized by variables such as air temperature, relative humidity, barometric pressure, wind velocity, cloud cover, and precipitation.

  • Atmospheric Layers: The troposphere contains approximately 75% of atmospheric mass and 99% of its water vapor, serving as the core layer where all major weather phenomena and agricultural microclimate modifications occur.
  • Solar Radiation: Serves as the fundamental energy engine driving photosynthesis, evapotranspiration, and thermal accumulation in crop canopies. Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) ranges between 400 nm and 700 nm.
  • Microclimate: The localized atmospheric condition within a crop canopy, differing significantly from the macroclimate due to transpiration, shading, and soil moisture gradients.

Indian Crop Seasons and Agro-Climatic Zones

Indian agriculture is traditionally structured around distinct seasonal crop-growing windows governed by the South-West (Summer) Monsoon and North-East (Winter) Monsoon regimes. The National Commission on Agriculture (NCA) and the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) have delineated India into 15 broad agro-climatic zones based on soil type, rainfall, temperature, and water availability.

  • Kharif Season (Rainy/Monsoon Season): Spans from July to October. Crops are sown with the onset of the monsoon in June-July and harvested in October-November. Representative crops include Oryza sativa (rice), Zea mays (maize), Glycine max (soybean), and Cajanus cajan (pigeonpea).
  • Rabi Season (Winter Season): Spans from October to March. Characterized by low temperatures, short photoperiods, and high residual soil moisture or supplemental irrigation. Representative crops include Triticum aestivum (wheat), Cicer arietinum (chickpea), Brassica juncea (mustard), and Hordeum vulgare (barley).
  • Zaid Season (Summer Season): Spans from March to June. Short-duration, irrigated crops cultivated between Rabi harvest and Kharif sowing. Representative crops include Cucumis sativus (cucumber), Cucurbita pepo (pumpkin), Vigna radiata (mung bean), and fodder maize.

Thermal Regimes and Growing Degree Days (GDD)

Thermal energy dictates the rate of phenological development in plants. Crop phenology is intrinsically linked to accumulated heat units rather than sheer chronological time, a concept quantified through Growing Degree Days (GDD) or thermal units.

  • Formula for GDD: GDD = Tmax + Tmin2 - Tbase where Tmax is maximum daily temperature, Tmin is minimum daily temperature, and Tbase (base temperature) is the threshold temperature below which physiological development ceases (typically 10°C for temperate crops like wheat and 15°C for tropical crops like rice and maize).
  • Photoperiodism: Plants are classified based on their day-length requirements for floral induction into short-day plants (e.g., rice, soybean), long-day plants (e.g., wheat, barley), and day-neutral plants (e.g., tomato, cotton).
  • Heat Stress: Extreme temperatures during critical phases such as anthesis and grain filling lead to pollen sterility, reduced dry matter accumulation, and premature senescence.

Precipitation, Drought Indices, and Irrigation Scheduling

Precipitation is the primary determinant of rainfed (barani) agriculture in India. Spatial and temporal variability in monsoon rainfall frequently induces agricultural droughts, necessitating precise meteorological monitoring and drought indices.

  • Agricultural Drought: A shortage of soil moisture resulting from inadequate precipitation and high evapotranspiration, leading to impaired crop development and wilting.
  • Aridity Index (AI): Calculated as AI = P - PETPET × 100, where P is precipitation and PET is potential evapotranspiration.
  • Evaporation Measurement: Class A Pan Evaporimeter is the standard instrument used by agrometeorological observatories to estimate daily potential water loss, guiding irrigation scheduling based on the IW/CPE ratio (Irrigation Water to Cumulative Pan Evaporation).

Extreme Weather Events and Climate Adaptation Strategies

Climate change has amplified the frequency of extreme weather events such as unseasonal hail storms, terminal heat waves, cold waves, erratic cloudbursts, and prolonged dry spells during the monsoon season.

  • Frost Management: In cold-sensitive crops, frost damage is mitigated via overhead irrigation, smoke generation, shelterbelt establishment, and application of anti-transpirants or potassium nitrate.
  • Resilient Varieties: Adoption of drought-tolerant and heat-tolerant genotypes (e.g., terminal heat-tolerant wheat cultivars like HD 2967 or DBW 187) and short-duration, photoperiod-insensitive hybrids.
  • Contingency Crop Planning: Maintaining seed banks for alternate crops (e.g., sowing Pennisetum glaucum or Vigna mungo if early monsoon fails) to stabilize farm incomes under aberrant weather scenarios.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The troposphere is the lowest layer of the atmosphere where all weather and cloud formations occur.
  • Base temperature (Tbase) for temperate cereals like wheat is universally taken as 5°C to 10°C, whereas for tropical crops like rice and maize it is 10°C to 15°C.
  • Class A Pan Evaporimeter has a standard diameter of 120.7 cm and a depth of 25 cm.
  • India is divided into 15 broad agro-climatic zones according to the Planning Commission / ICAR classification.
  • Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) wavelength band is strictly 400 nm to 700 nm.
  • The instrument used to record bright sunshine hours continuously is the Campbell-Stokes Sunshine Recorder.
  • Relative humidity is measured using a Wet and Dry Bulb Hygrometer housed inside a Stevenson Screen.
  • Atmospheric pressure is measured in millibars (mb) or hectopascals (hPa) using a Aneroid Barometer or Fortin's Barometer.
  • Zaid season crops are essentially short-duration crops cultivated between the harvest of Rabi crops and the sowing of Kharif crops.
  • The IW/CPE ratio approach for irrigation scheduling relies on cumulative pan evaporation data.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing base temperature thresholds: students often apply temperate crop base temperatures (10°C) to tropical C4 grasses like maize and sorghum, which require 15°C.
  • Mistaking Potential Evapotranspiration (PET) with actual evapotranspiration; PET assumes unlimited soil moisture availability.
  • Believing that Zaid season crops constitute a major monsoon cropping period, whereas Zaid crops are strictly summer crops dependent on supplemental irrigation.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Seasons and Weather in Agricultural Meteorology

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Atmospheric Structure and Weather Fundamentals

The atmosphere is layered thermally into troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, and thermosphere. The troposphere contains the entire weather machine.

  • Troposphere: Extends up to 8 km at poles and 18 km at equator; temperature decreases with altitude at normal lapse rate of 6.5°C per kilometer.
  • Weather vs Climate: Weather is instantaneous atmospheric state; climate is statistical aggregate of weather over 30 years or more.
Concept 2

Agro-Climatic Zoning in India

India's diverse physiography is categorized into 15 agro-climatic zones by the Planning Commission to formulate localized regional agricultural planning.

  • Zone 1: Western Himalayan Region characterized by cold, hilly terrain and temperate horticulture.
  • Zone 4: Middle Gangetic Plains Region characterized by high rainfall, alluvial soils, and intensive rice-wheat cropping systems.
Concept 3

Thermal Energy and Crop Phenology

Plant development rate is governed by thermal time units expressed as Degree Days.

  • Growing Degree Days (GDD): Numerical integration of daily mean temperature above base temperature.
  • Helio-Thermal Units (HTU): Product of GDD and actual daily bright sunshine hours, reflecting radiation interception.
Concept 4

Drought Classification and Meteorological Indices

Droughts are categorized based on meteorological, hydrological, and agricultural impacts.

  • Meteorological Drought: Situation when annual precipitation is significantly below normal climatological mean (exceeding 25% deficit).
  • Aridity Index: Quantitative ratio assessing the severity of moisture deficit in arid and semi-arid tracts.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Agrometeorology (Agricultural Meteorology)
The branch of meteorology that investigates meteorological and hydrological factors affecting agricultural production.
Stevenson Screen (Meteorological Shelter Box)
A double-louvered wooden box painted white, mounted 1.25 meters above ground to house thermometers and hygrometers safely.
Growing Degree Days (GDD)
Heat unit index measuring thermal accumulation required for crop phenological milestones.
Photoperiodism (Photoperiodic Response)
The physiological reaction of organisms to the length of day or night, governing flowering and bulb formation.
Potential Evapotranspiration (PET)
The maximum quantity of water evaporated and transpired from a vegetated surface under unlimited soil moisture conditions.
Anemometer (Cup Anemometer)
An instrument designed to measure horizontal wind speed, usually calibrated in kilometers per hour or meters per second.
Albedo (Surface Reflectivity)
The ratio of reflected solar radiation to incident solar radiation on a surface, expressed as a percentage.
Normal Lapse Rate (Vertical Temperature Gradient)
The decline in atmospheric temperature with increasing elevation in the troposphere, averaging 6.5°C per 1,000 meters.
Aridity Index (AI Formula Index)
An index quantifying the degree of water deficiency calculated from precipitation and potential evapotranspiration.
Microclimate (Canopy Climate)
The atmospheric conditions within and immediately surrounding a crop canopy, distinct from regional macroclimate.
Symons Rain Gauge (Standard Non-Recording Rain Gauge)
A standard meteorological instrument used to measure daily cumulative precipitation depth in millimeters.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Troposphere holds 75% of atmospheric mass and is the seat of all agricultural weather.
  • PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation) wavelength band spans 400 to 700 nm.
  • India has 15 agro-climatic zones as per Planning Commission / ICAR classifications.
  • Base temperature for wheat GDD calculation is commonly taken as 4.5°C to 5°C.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Agrometeorology links atmospheric science with crop production to optimize yield and mitigate weather risks.
  • India's cropping calendar is divided into Kharif, Rabi, and Zaid seasons aligned with monsoon regimes.
  • Growing Degree Days (GDD) provide a robust quantitative measure of thermal accumulation for predicting crop harvest timing.
  • Instrumentation like Stevenson Screen, Class A Pan, and Anemometer form the backbone of meteorological observatories.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Weather VS Climate

Weather is instantaneous atmospheric condition at a given place and time; climate is the long-term synthesis of weather patterns over at least 30 years.

GDD VS HTU

GDD measures pure thermal heat units using temperature alone; HTU (Helio-Thermal Units) incorporates solar radiation via sunshine hours.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which atmospheric layer contains approximately 75% of the total atmospheric mass and is the site of all weather phenomena?

Explanation: The troposphere is the lowest layer of the atmosphere, extending up to 8-18 km, containing 75% of atmospheric mass and nearly all water vapor, making it the zone of weather occurrence.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard wavelength range of Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) utilized by green plants?

Explanation: Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) occupies the spectral band between 400 and 700 nanometers, which chlorophyll pigments absorb efficiently for photosynthesis.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

How many broad agro-climatic zones has the Planning Commission and ICAR delineated in India?

Explanation: The Planning Commission (now NITI Aayog) and ICAR divided India into 15 broad agro-climatic zones to facilitate regional agricultural research and planning.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which instrument is universally utilized for recording bright sunshine hours at meteorological observatories?

Explanation: The Campbell-Stokes Sunshine Recorder utilizes a solid glass sphere that focuses solar rays to burn a trace on a graduated card, recording bright sunshine duration.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the formula for calculating Growing Degree Days (GDD)?

Explanation: GDD is calculated by taking the arithmetic mean of maximum and minimum daily temperatures and subtracting the base temperature (Tbase) below which crop growth halts.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

During which season are Kharif crops predominantly sown in India?

Explanation: Kharif crops are monsoon-season crops sown with the onset of the South-West monsoon in June-July and harvested in September-October.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard diameter of the Class A Pan Evaporimeter used for estimating water evaporation?

Explanation: The standard US Weather Bureau Class A Pan Evaporimeter has a diameter of 120.7 cm and a depth of 25 cm, installed on a wooden platform.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop season in India falls between the Rabi harvest and Kharif sowing, characterized by high temperatures and irrigation?

Explanation: Zaid is the summer cropping season spanning March to June, featuring short-duration crops like cucurbits and green gram cultivated under irrigation.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the normal lapse rate of temperature in the troposphere?

Explanation: The normal lapse rate in the troposphere is 6.5°C drop in temperature per 1,000 meters (1 km) increase in altitude.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a short-day plant (SDP)?

Explanation: Rice (Oryza sativa) is a classic short-day plant that flowers when day lengths are shorter than its critical photoperiod.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does an Aridity Index (AI) evaluate in agricultural meteorology?

Explanation: The Aridity Index measures the degree of water deficiency by comparing precipitation (P) with potential evapotranspiration (PET).eks

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Inside which structure are meteorological thermometers (maximum, minimum, dry bulb, wet bulb) housed for protection?

Explanation: The Stevenson Screen is a specially designed louvered wooden box painted white to shield meteorological instruments from direct solar radiation and rain while permitting free air circulation.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which meteorological phenomenon describes the sudden drop in temperature causing ice crystal formation on plant tissues?

Explanation: Frost occurs when ambient air temperatures drop below 0°C, causing ice nucleation inside or outside plant cells, resulting in severe cellular damage.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of establishing shelterbelts and windbreaks in agricultural fields?

Explanation: Shelterbelts and windbreaks reduce wind velocity, decrease mechanical lodging, lower evapotranspiration losses, and protect topsoil from wind erosion.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop is most susceptible to terminal heat stress during grain filling when temperatures spike above 35°C in March?

Explanation: Wheat (Triticum aestivum) experiences terminal heat stress when high temperatures during March-April accelerate grain filling and reduce test weight (1000-grain weight).

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What type of drought occurs when meteorological drought persists for a prolonged period, depleting surface and groundwater reservoirs?

Explanation: Hydrological drought is characterized by deficiencies in surface and subsurface water supplies (streamflow, lake levels, and groundwater tables) following extended meteorological drought.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is albedo?

Explanation: Albedo is the proportion of incident solar radiation that is reflected back by a surface, varying from high for snow to low for dense green forest and wet soil.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which instrument is used to measure atmospheric pressure in meteorological observatories?

Explanation: Fortin's Barometer and Aneroid Barometers are precision instruments used to measure atmospheric pressure in millibars or hectopascals.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the term for a wind system that reverses its direction seasonally, bringing heavy rainfall to the Indian subcontinent?

Explanation: Monsoon refers to seasonal reversing winds accompanied by corresponding changes in precipitation, most notably the South-West monsoon bringing rain to India.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does Helio-Thermal Unit (HTU) incorporate that standard Growing Degree Days (GDD) do not?

Explanation: Helio-Thermal Units (HTU) are calculated by multiplying GDD by the actual bright sunshine hours (n), integrating thermal energy and solar radiation.
