Fundamental Concepts of Weather and Climatology

Agricultural meteorology (Agrometeorology) is a branch of applied meteorology that investigates the responses of agricultural crops and livestock to physical processes of the lower atmosphere. It integrates atmospheric science with agronomy to optimize crop productivity and mitigate climatic risks.

  • Weather: The instantaneous or short-term state of the atmosphere at a given place and time, characterized by variables such as temperature, atmospheric pressure, wind velocity, humidity, and precipitation.
  • Climate: The generalized synthesis of weather conditions over a long geographic duration, typically spanning 30 years, reflecting statistical properties including mean values, extremes, and frequencies of meteorological phenomena.
  • Significance in Agriculture: Weather directly dictates daily farm operations such as tillage, sowing, pesticide application, irrigation scheduling, and harvesting, whereas climate determines the macro-distribution of cropping patterns, crop zonation, and varietal selection across distinct agro-ecological regions.

Atmospheric Layers and Solar Radiation Dynamics

The Earth's atmosphere is vertically stratified into distinct thermal layers that dictate global energy balances, weather formation, and the transmission of solar radiation critical for photosynthesis in major crops like Triticum aestivum and Oryza sativa.

  • Troposphere: The lowest layer extending from the surface up to 8–18 km, containing 75% of atmospheric mass and almost all water vapor. It is characterized by a negative lapse rate where temperature decreases with altitude at an average rate of 6.5°C per 1,000 meters.
  • Stratosphere: Extending from the tropopause to approximately 50 km, housing the ozone layer which absorbs harmful ultraviolet-B and ultraviolet-C radiation. Temperatures increase with altitude due to ozone absorption.
  • Solar Radiation & Albedo: Solar radiation reaching the Earth's surface powers the hydrological cycle and drives photosynthetic active radiation (PAR). Albedo is the reflection coefficient of surfaces; for instance, fresh snow has an albedo of 75–95%, whereas dark moist soils exhibit an albedo of 10–15%.

Agro-Climatic Zones of India and Uttar Pradesh

The Planning Commission of India delineated the country into 15 distinct Agro-Climatic Zones based on physiography, soils, geological formation, climate, rainfall, and irrigation availability. Uttar Pradesh spans multiple key agro-climatic zones defined by the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR).

  • UP Agro-Climatic Zones: Uttar Pradesh is broadly divided into 9 agro-climatic zones including the Western Plain Zone, Central Plain Zone, Bundelkhand Zone, Eastern Plain Zone, and North-Eastern Plain Zone.
  • Climatic Typology: The state primarily experiences a humid to sub-humid subtropical climate characterized by hot summers, moderate monsoons, and cool winters, highly favorable for double and triple cropping systems involving Triticum aestivum, Zea mays, and Brassica juncea.
  • Agro-Ecological Regions: India is categorized into 20 agro-ecological regions based on soil groups, physiography, and bioclimatic types, serving as the scientific basis for land-use planning.

Agrometeorological Hazards and Disaster Management

Extreme weather events pose severe threats to agricultural economies, necessitating robust forecasting, crop insurance schemes, and climate-smart agronomic interventions.

  • Drought: Categorized into meteorological (rainfall deficiency), hydrological (depleted water bodies), agricultural (soil moisture stress leading to wilting), and socio-economic droughts.
  • Frost and Cold Stress: Occurs when ambient temperatures drop below 0°C, causing intracellular ice crystal formation that ruptures cell walls in sensitive crops like Cicer arietinum and Solanum tuberosum. Countermeasures include smoke generation, overhead sprinkling, and wind machines.
  • Hailstorms and Cyclonic Winds: Cause physical shredding of leaves and lodging in cereals such as Zea mays and Sorghum bicolor, mitigated through windbreaks, shelterbelts, and timely weather advisories issued by the India Meteorological Department (IMD).

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Weather is the instantaneous state of the atmosphere, whereas climate is the statistical average of weather conditions over a minimum of 30 years.
  • The normal environmental lapse rate in the troposphere is 6.5°C decrease per 1,000 meters of altitude.
  • Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) spans the wavelength band of 400 to 700 nanometers.
  • India is divided into 15 Agro-Climatic Zones as per the Planning Commission and 20 Agro-Ecological Regions as per ICAR.
  • Albedo is highest for fresh snow (75-95%) and lowest for deep water surfaces under perpendicular sun rays (3-5%).
  • Agrometeorological advisories in India are coordinated nationwide by the India Meteorological Department (IMD).
  • PET stands for Potential Evapotranspiration, which represents water loss under an unlimited moisture supply condition.
  • Uttar Pradesh is officially divided into 9 distinct Agro-Climatic Zones for targeted agricultural planning.
  • Anemometer is the standard meteorological instrument used for measuring wind speed.
  • Stevenson Screen is a specially designed wooden louvred box used to house thermometers and hygrometers at a standard height of 1.25 meters above ground.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing weather with climate by considering seasonal variations as climatic shifts; remember climate requires a 30-year temporal baseline.
  • Assuming the stratosphere has a decreasing temperature profile; temperature actually increases in the stratosphere due to solar UV absorption by ozone.
  • Treating agro-climatic zones and agro-ecological regions as identical; agro-climatic zones focus on rainfall and cropping patterns, whereas agro-ecological regions integrate soils and physiography.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Weather and Climatology in Agricultural Meteorology

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Scope and Definition of Agricultural Meteorology

Agricultural meteorology evaluates the direct and indirect impacts of weather and climate on agricultural production. It helps optimize resource use efficiency.

  • Agrometeorology: The study of meteorological and hydrological factors affecting agricultural operations and crop yields.
  • Microclimate: The atmospheric condition prevailing in the immediate vicinity of the crop canopy, differing significantly from macroclimate.
Concept 2

Radiation and Energy Balance in Cropping Systems

Solar radiation is the primary energy engine driving crop growth, transpiration, and dry matter accumulation.

  • PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation): The 400–700 nm waveband utilized by green plants for carbohydrate synthesis.
  • Net Radiation: The difference between incoming total solar/sky radiation and outgoing terrestrial radiation.
Concept 3

Atmospheric Pressure and Wind Systems

Pressure gradients generate wind movement, which dictates moisture transport, cloud formation, and precipitation patterns across Indian agricultural belts.

  • Coriolis Force: An apparent force resulting from Earth's rotation that deflects winds to the right in the Northern Hemisphere.
  • Monsoon: A large-scale seasonal wind reversal driven by differential heating of land and ocean masses.
Concept 4

Agro-Climatic Zonation of India

Zonation enables localized agricultural research, technology transfer, and optimal cropping system design.

  • Planning Commission Zones: 15 zones based on topography, soils, and rainfall.
  • ICAR Zones: 120 agro-climatic sub-zones providing fine-scale regional recommendations.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Agrometeorology (Agricultural Meteorology)
Branch of applied meteorology studying weather and climatic interactions with crop production.
Lapse Rate (Environmental Lapse Rate)
Rate of temperature decrease with increasing altitude in the troposphere, standard being 6.5°C/km.
Albedo (Surface Reflectivity)
Ratio of reflected radiation from a surface to the total incident solar radiation upon it.
Stevenson Screen (Meteorological Shelter)
A louvred wooden box painted white, shielding meteorological instruments from direct radiation.
Evapotranspiration (ET (Evaporation + Transpiration))
Combined loss of water vapor from the soil surface via evaporation and from plant tissues via transpiration.
PET (Potential Evapotranspiration)
Maximum possible water loss from a well-watered continuous green crop cover under given weather conditions.
Monsoon (Seasonal Wind System)
Periodic wind reversal characterized by heavy summer rainfall and dry winter conditions in South Asia.
Isobar (Line of Equal Pressure)
Contour line on a weather map connecting points of equal atmospheric pressure.
Isotherm (Line of Equal Temperature)
Cartographic line connecting geographic points sharing identical mean temperature values.
Hyetograph (Recording Rain Gauge)
Continuous self-recording instrument used to measure rainfall amount and intensity over time.
Microclimate (Canopy Climate)
Climate of a very small specific area, such as within a crop canopy, differing from the surrounding regional climate.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Evaporation VS Transpiration

Evaporation is water loss from free water or soil surfaces, whereas transpiration is water loss specifically through plant stomata.

Weather VS Climate

Weather changes hourly or daily; climate represents long-term statistical averages over decades.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard environmental lapse rate in the troposphere?

Explanation: The environmental lapse rate in the troposphere averages a temperature decrease of 6.5°C for every 1,000 meters increase in altitude.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) falls within which wavelength range?

Explanation: PAR corresponds exactly to the visible light spectrum from 400 to 700 nanometers, which is absorbed by chlorophyll pigments.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

How many Agro-Climatic Zones were delineated by the Planning Commission of India?

Explanation: The Planning Commission of India divided the country into 15 major agro-climatic zones based on soil, climate, rainfall, and irrigation parameters.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which atmospheric layer contains the ozone layer that absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation?

Explanation: The stratosphere houses the ozone layer (ozonosphere) between 15 and 35 km altitude, absorbing harmful UV-B and UV-C rays.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard height of the rim of a ordinary rain gauge above the ground surface?

Explanation: The rim of a standard rain gauge is placed exactly 30 cm above the ground surface to avoid splash errors from raindrops bouncing off the soil.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which instrument is used to measure wind speed?

Explanation: An anemometer measures wind velocity or speed, whereas a wind vane indicates wind direction.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is albedo?

Explanation: Albedo is the proportion of incident light or radiation that is reflected by a surface, expressed as a percentage.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

How many agro-climatic zones are present in the state of Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: Uttar Pradesh is officially categorized into 9 distinct agro-climatic zones ranging from the Western Plain Zone to the Vindhyan Zone.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What force causes winds to deflect to the right in the Northern Hemisphere?

Explanation: The Coriolis force deflects moving objects and winds to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does PET stand for in agricultural meteorology?

Explanation: PET stands for Potential Evapotranspiration, representing water loss from soil and plant surfaces when water is not limiting.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which meteorological instrument is housed inside a Stevenson Screen?

Explanation: A Stevenson Screen houses dry-bulb, wet-bulb, maximum, and minimum thermometers along with hygrometers.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What type of drought occurs due to prolonged meteorological drought leading to the depletion of surface and groundwater reserves?

Explanation: Hydrological drought is characterized by deficiencies in surface and subsurface water supplies (rivers, lakes, groundwater aquifers).

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which radiation sensor is used to measure sunshine duration?

Explanation: The Campbell-Stokes recorder uses a glass sphere to focus sunlight and burn a trace on a calibrated card to record bright sunshine hours.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the dry adiabatic lapse rate in the atmosphere approximately equal to?

Explanation: The dry adiabatic lapse rate is approximately 9.8°C per 1,000 meters (1°C per 100 meters).

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary driver of the Indian Summer Monsoon?

Explanation: The Indian Summer Monsoon is driven by intense low-pressure development over the heated Tibetan Plateau and Asian landmass, drawing moisture-laden winds from the high-pressure Indian Ocean.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop is most sensitive to frost damage during winter in Northern India?

Explanation: Chickpea (Cicer arietinum) is highly susceptible to frost, which causes flower drop, pod abortion, and malic acid secretion on foliage.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Lines connecting points of equal atmospheric pressure on a weather map are called:

Explanation: Isobars are lines on weather maps connecting geographic locations with identical atmospheric pressure values.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the term for lines connecting points of equal rainfall on a map?

Explanation: Isohyets are cartographic contour lines representing points receiving equal amounts of precipitation.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of shelterbelts and windbreaks in agriculture?

Explanation: Windbreaks and shelterbelts reduce wind speed, minimizing mechanical damage, lodging, and moisture loss through evaporation in crops.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which climatic factor has the most immediate influence on the rate of plant transpiration?

Explanation: Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD) represents the difference between the saturation vapor pressure and actual vapor pressure, acting as the primary aerodynamic driving force for transpiration.

What color is a Stevenson Screen painted, and why?

Solution & Rationale: Stevenson Screens are painted bright white to maximize solar reflection and prevent internal heating by direct radiation.
