Introduction and Scope of Agricultural Meteorology

Agricultural meteorology (Agrometeorology) is a branch of applied meteorology that investigates the responses of crops and livestock to weather and climatic conditions. Its primary objective is to optimize agricultural production by applying meteorological knowledge to agricultural planning and operations.

  • Definition: The science investigating weather and climate interactions with agricultural systems.
  • Scope: Crop selection, scheduling irrigation, disease and pest forecasting, fertilizer application timing, and disaster management.
  • Significance in India: Indian agriculture remains heavily dependent on the monsoon; hence, agrometeorological advisories directly influence national food security and farm incomes.

Atmospheric Layers and Solar Radiation

The Earth's atmosphere is divided into distinct vertical layers based on temperature gradients: Troposphere, Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Thermosphere, and Exosphere. Weather phenomena occur almost exclusively in the troposphere.

  • Troposphere: Lowest layer containing 75% of atmospheric mass and water vapor; temperature decreases with altitude (lapse rate ~ 6.5°C per km).
  • Stratosphere: Contains the ozone layer which absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation; temperature increases with altitude.
  • Solar Radiation: The ultimate source of energy for crop photosynthesis; measured in terms of sunshine hours, photosynthetically active radiation (PAR, 400-700 nm), and net radiation balance.

Agro-Climatic Zones and Weather Forecasting

India has been classified into diverse agro-climatic zones by the Planning Commission and the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) to facilitate regional agricultural planning.

  • Planning Commission Classification: Divides India into 15 broad agro-climatic zones based on soil type, climate, rainfall, and water resources.
  • NARS / ICAR Classification: Further refines zones into 127 agro-climatic sub-zones for localized research and extension.
  • Weather Forecasting Types: Nowcasting (up to 24 hours), Short-range (1 to 3 days), Medium-range (3 to 10 days, crucial for farm operations), and Long-range (seasonal, e.g., monsoon outlook).

Microclimate Modification and Weather Hazards

Crops experience a microclimate within the plant canopy that differs significantly from macro-weather. Modifying this microclimate helps protect crops from adverse weather hazards.

  • Windbreaks and Shelterbelts: Rows of trees or shrubs planted to reduce wind speed, mechanical damage, and excessive evapotranspiration.
  • Mulching: Application of organic or synthetic materials to conserve soil moisture and moderate soil temperature.
  • Weather Hazards: Frost, drought, hailstorms, heat waves, and cyclones managed through contingency planning and crop insurance (e.g., PMFBY).

Climate Change Adaptation and Mitigation in Agriculture

Climate change poses severe threats to global food security through rising temperatures, erratic precipitation, and increased frequency of extreme weather events.

  • Adaptation Strategies: Adopting climate-resilient crop varieties (e.g., drought-tolerant, submergence-tolerant like Oryza sativa Swarna-Sub1), altering planting dates, and practicing conservation agriculture.
  • Mitigation Strategies: Reducing greenhouse gas emissions (methane from paddy fields, nitrous oxide from nitrogenous fertilizers) through efficient nutrient management, direct seeded rice (DSR), and agroforestry.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Troposphere is the lowest atmospheric layer where all weather phenomena occur, with a normal lapse rate of 6.5°C per kilometer.
  • Planning Commission of India classified the country into 15 agro-climatic zones.
  • Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) ranges between 400 to 700 nanometers wavelength.
  • Medium-range weather forecast (3 to 10 days) is most useful for day-to-day farm operations like irrigation and plant protection.
  • Evapotranspiration is measured using instruments like Pan Evaporimeter and Lysimeter.
  • Instrument used to measure atmospheric pressure is the Barometer, and wind direction is measured by Wind Vane.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing short-range forecasts with medium-range forecasts; remember medium-range covers 3 to 10 days and is standard for agrometeorological advisories.
  • Assuming weather and climate are identical; weather is instantaneous state of the atmosphere, whereas climate is the statistical synthesis of weather over 30 years or more.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Agrometeorology (Agricultural Meteorology)
Study of meteorological and hydrological factors affecting agricultural production.
Lapse Rate (Temperature Lapse Rate)
Rate of decrease of temperature with an increase in elevation in the troposphere.
PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation)
Spectral range of solar radiation from 400 to 700 nm used by plants for photosynthesis.
Albedo (Surface Albedo)
Proportion of incident solar radiation reflected by a surface, expressed as a percentage.
Evapotranspiration (ET0 / ETc)
Combined loss of water from soil surface through evaporation and from plant tissues through transpiration.
Shelterbelt (Windbreak Agroforestry)
Line of trees or shrubs planted to protect an area from high winds and reduce soil erosion.
Microclimate (Canopy Microclimate)
Climate of a very small, specific area, differing from the surrounding macroclimate.
Inversion (Thermal Inversion)
Reversal of normal temperature behavior in the troposphere, where temperature increases with altitude.
Barometer (Mercury/Aneroid Barometer)
Scientific instrument used to measure atmospheric pressure.
Anemometer (Cup Anemometer)
Instrument used for measuring wind speed.
Lysimeter (Weighing/Non-weighing Lysimeter)
Device used to measure actual evapotranspiration of crops.
ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation)
Agency providing satellite data for weather monitoring and disaster warning in India.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Weather VS Climate

Weather is short-term atmospheric condition at a specific time and place; climate is long-term average pattern over 30 years or more.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which atmospheric layer contains approximately 75% of the total atmospheric mass and is responsible for all weather phenomena?

Explanation: The troposphere is the lowest layer of the atmosphere, containing most of the water vapor, dust, and 75% of atmospheric mass where weather phenomena take place.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the normal lapse rate in the troposphere?

Explanation: The normal temperature lapse rate in the troposphere is approximately 6.5°C decrease in temperature per 1000 meters increase in altitude.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Into how many agro-climatic zones was India divided by the Planning Commission?

Explanation: The Planning Commission of India classified the country into 15 broad agro-climatic zones based on topography, soils, and climatic parameters.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) falls within which wavelength range?

Explanation: PAR spans the 400 to 700 nanometer wavelength band, which plants utilize for photosynthetic carbon fixation.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which type of weather forecast is most critical for scheduling immediate farm operations like irrigation and fertilizer application?

Explanation: Medium-range forecasts (3 to 10 days) provide sufficient advance notice for farmers to plan irrigation, spraying, and harvesting operations effectively.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What instrument is used to measure sunshine duration?

Explanation: The Campbell-Stokes Sunshine Recorder uses a heavy glass sphere that focuses sunlight to burn a temporal trace on a recording card.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which instrument measures actual evapotranspiration directly?

Explanation: A lysimeter is a weighing or non-weighing device used to measure water loss from a soil-crop system, specifically actual evapotranspiration.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is thermal inversion?

Explanation: Thermal inversion is a meteorological phenomenon where temperature increases with altitude rather than decreasing, often trapping pollutants and cold air near the surface.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which gas in the stratosphere absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation?

Explanation: Stratospheric ozone (O3) absorbs high-energy ultraviolet (UV-B and UV-C) radiation from the sun, protecting living organisms on Earth.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of planting shelterbelts around agricultural fields?

Explanation: Shelterbelts act as windbreaks, reducing wind speed, minimizing mechanical damage to crops, and lowering excessive evaporative demand.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which organization in India coordinates the Integrated Agromet Advisory Services (IAAS)?

Explanation: The India Meteorological Department (IMD) under the Ministry of Earth Sciences coordinates the IAAS in collaboration with ICAR and SAUs.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does albedo refer to in agricultural meteorology?

Explanation: Albedo is the reflectivity of a surface, expressed as the percentage of incoming solar radiation that is reflected back into the atmosphere.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which weather parameter is measured using a cup anemometer?

Explanation: A cup anemometer measures horizontal wind speed, typically expressed in kilometers per hour or meters per second.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is microclimate?

Explanation: Microclimate refers to the atmospheric conditions near the ground surface and within the crop canopy, differing markedly from macroclimate.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which weather phenomenon is associated with the warming of sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean?

Explanation: El Nino involves anomalous warming of eastern Pacific waters, frequently disrupting normal monsoon rainfall patterns across the Indian subcontinent.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary greenhouse gas emitted from flooded paddy fields?

Explanation: Anaerobic conditions in submerged rice paddies favor methanogenic bacteria, resulting in significant methane emissions.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which instrument is used to measure relative humidity?

Explanation: A psychrometer containing wet-bulb and dry-bulb thermometers is used to determine atmospheric humidity levels.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the time frame for a short-range weather forecast?

Explanation: Short-range weather forecasts cover time horizons of 1 to 3 days, utilized for immediate tactical farming decisions.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which adaptation strategy helps mitigate the impact of terminal heat stress in wheat crops?

Explanation: Early sowing using heat-tolerant wheat cultivars enables the crop to complete grain filling before high temperatures set in during late spring.

Test Question 1 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following describes the relationship between altitude and atmospheric pressure?

Solution & Rationale: Atmospheric pressure decreases exponentially with increasing altitude because the overlying mass of air diminishes rapidly.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary cause of the Indian Summer Monsoon?

Solution & Rationale: The Indian Summer Monsoon is driven by differential heating and cooling of the Asian landmass and the surrounding Indian Ocean, creating a low-pressure trough over the subcontinent.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

Which agrometeorological index is widely used to predict the developmental stages of insect pests and crops?

Solution & Rationale: Growing Degree Days (GDD) quantify thermal accumulation required for phenological development in crops and insect pests.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

How is Growing Degree Days (GDD) calculated for a given day?

Solution & Rationale: GDD is computed as the mean daily temperature minus the base temperature (threshold temperature below which crop growth ceases).

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the significance of the base temperature in GDD calculations for crops like wheat?

Solution & Rationale: For cool-season crops like wheat and barley, 4.5°C is commonly adopted as the biological base temperature.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Which instrument is used for measuring total global solar radiation?

Solution & Rationale: A pyranometer measures broadband solar irradiance on a planar surface from a 180-degree field of view.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the main purpose of shelterbelts in arid and semi-arid regions?

Solution & Rationale: Shelterbelts break the force of winds, lowering wind speed and decreasing moisture loss through evapotranspiration.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

Which climatic phenomenon is characterized by abnormal cooling of sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific?

Solution & Rationale: La Nina represents the cool phase of the El Nino-Southern Oscillation (ENSO), often associated with above-average monsoon rainfall in India.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which type of drought occurs due to prolonged meteorological dry spells resulting in depleted soil moisture and crop failure?

Solution & Rationale: Agricultural drought occurs when soil moisture and rainfall are inadequate to support healthy crop growth, leading to stressed or failed yields.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the term used for the ratio of actual evapotranspiration to potential evapotranspiration?

Solution & Rationale: The Moisture Availability Index evaluates the sufficiency of precipitation and stored soil moisture relative to potential water demand.
