Measurement Of Weather Elements in Agricultural Meteorology

This chapter covers comprehensive measurement techniques for weather elements including temperature, atmospheric pressure, relative humidity, precipitation, wind velocity, and solar radiation, vital for agricultural planning and crop modeling in UP TGT and PGT Agriculture examinations.

Introduction to Agricultural Meteorological Instruments

Agricultural meteorology relies heavily on precise, standardized measurement of micro-climatic and macro-climatic parameters that govern plant growth, development, and phenology. The instruments used must meet International Meteorological Organization (IMO) and World Meteorological Organization (WMO) standards. Standard observations are typically recorded at designated synoptic hours inside a properly oriented Stevenson Screen, which protects thermometers from direct solar radiation and precipitation while ensuring free ventilation.

  • Stevenson Screen Construction: Double-louvered wooden box painted white to reflect sunlight, mounted 1.25 to 2.0 meters above short green grass.
  • Exposure Standards: Rain gauges must be placed on level ground free from wind eddies caused by adjacent trees or buildings.
  • Data Logging: Modern automated weather stations (AWS) integrate electronic sensors for real-time recording of agrometeorological indices.

Measurement of Atmospheric Temperature and Thermal Regimes

Air and soil temperatures dictate physiological reaction rates, enzymatic activity, and developmental milestones in crops such as Triticum aestivum and Oryza sativa. Temperature is quantified using various specialized thermometers housed in the Stevenson Screen.

  • Maximum Thermometer: Utilizes a constriction near the bulb that allows mercury to expand with rising temperature but prevents it from returning on cooling until manually reset by swinging.
  • Minimum Thermometer: Contains alcohol with a tiny dumb-bell shaped index. As temperature drops, the meniscus drags the index downward; as temperature rises, the alcohol flows past the index, recording the lowest temperature.
  • Soil Temperature: Measured at various depths (5 cm, 10 cm, 20 cm, 30 cm) using bent-stem thermometers or electrical resistance blocks to monitor seed germination and root zone dynamics.

Measurement of Atmospheric Moisture and Humidity

Moisture parameters including relative humidity (RH), vapor pressure deficit (VPD), and dew point temperature govern transpiration rates, stomatal conductance, and fungal pathogen proliferation like Alternaria solani and Puccinia graminis.

  • Psychrometer (Dry and Wet Bulb Hygrometer): Operates on the principle of evaporative cooling. The depression of the wet-bulb temperature relative to the dry-bulb indicates relative humidity using psychrometric tables.
  • Hair Hygrometer: Relies on the property of degreased human hair expanding when humidity increases and contracting when humidity decreases.
  • Sling Psychrometer: Rotated manually to ensure adequate ventilation (minimum 2.5 m/s air speed) across the wet wick for accurate readings.

Measurement of Precipitation and Evapotranspiration

Precipitation is the primary input in rainfed and irrigated agroecosystems. Water balance calculations require accurate quantification of both rainfall input and evaporative water loss.

  • Non-Recording Rain Gauge (Symons' Rain Gauge): Measures cumulative daily precipitation (typically read at 08:30 hours IST) using a graduated measuring cylinder.
  • Recording Rain Gauges: Tipping-bucket rain gauges and weighing-bucket rain gauges provide continuous temporal distribution and intensity of rainfall events.
  • Evaporation Measurement: Class A Open Pan Evaporimeter measures free-water surface evaporation, corrected by a pan coefficient to estimate crop evapotranspiration (ET_0).

Measurement of Wind Velocity, Direction, and Solar Radiation

Wind speed and solar radiation drive boundary layer heat exchange, carbon assimilation, and lodging risks in cereal crops.

  • Cup Anemometer: Measures horizontal wind run and average wind speed in kilometers per hour or meters per second.
  • Wind Vane: Indicates wind direction based on the point of the compass from which the wind blows.
  • Campbell-Stokes Sunshine Recorder: Uses a glass sphere to focus solar rays onto a chemically treated card, burning a trace that records bright sunshine hours.
  • Pyranometer: Measures total incoming global solar radiation (direct plus diffuse) on a horizontal surface.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The standard height of the thermometer bulb inside a Stevenson Screen above ground level is 1.25 to 2.0 meters.
  • Symons' rain gauge has a standard collection funnel diameter of 127 mm (5 inches) with a receiving bottle inside a metal casing.
  • Maximum thermometer uses mercury because of its high boiling point and uniform thermal expansion, while minimum thermometer uses alcohol due to its low freezing point (-114°C).
  • Class A evaporation pan has a standard diameter of 120.7 cm and a depth of 25 cm, installed on a wooden platform 15 cm above ground.
  • Campbell-Stokes recorder uses a solid glass sphere of 10 cm diameter acting as a plano-convex lens.
  • Relative humidity is expressed as a percentage and is calculated as the ratio of actual mixing ratio to saturation mixing ratio at the same temperature.
  • Anemometer cups rotate around a vertical axis, and wind speed is integrated over 24 hours to obtain daily wind run.
  • The wind vane points towards the direction *from* which the wind blows, consistent with meteorological convention.
  • A pyranometer measures global solar radiation across the wavelength spectrum from 0.3 to 3.0 micrometers.
  • A psychrometer requires an airflow velocity of at least 2.5 m/s across the wet wick to achieve thermodynamic equilibrium.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Error: Confusing wind direction terminology with navigation. Correction: Meteorological wind direction refers to the direction *from* which the wind blows, whereas oceanographic currents are named by the direction they flow *towards*.
  • Error: Assuming maximum thermometer can be reset by shaking downward like a clinical thermometer. Correction: Maximum thermometer has a constriction and must be swung in an arc or centrifuged back, whereas clinical thermometers use a downward jerk.
  • Error: Placing rain gauges close to farm boundary trees. Correction: Rain gauges must be placed at a distance at least equal to 2 to 4 times the height of nearby obstructions to prevent wind turbulence and undercatch.

Concept 1

Stevenson Screen Design and Microclimatic Siting

The Stevenson screen is the cornerstone enclosure for meteorological sensors.

  • Screen Color: White titanium dioxide paint to maximize albedo and minimize solar heat absorption.
  • Ventilation: Double-louvered side walls inclined downwards and inwards to prevent entry of direct sun rays and rain while permitting free lateral airflow.
Concept 2

Thermometric Principles in Agrometeorology

Thermal measurement governs growing degree day (GDD) calculations.

  • Maximum Thermometer: Incorporates a glass constriction preventing mercury recession without manual centrifugal force.
  • Minimum Thermometer: Filled with pure ethyl alcohol and a dark index pin pulled by surface tension of the meniscus.
Concept 3

Psychrometry and Humidity Indices

Humidity quantification determines crop water stress and disease forecasting.

  • Depression: Dry bulb temperature minus wet bulb temperature (T_d - T_w).
  • Saturation Vapor Pressure: Increases exponentially with temperature following the Clausius-Clapeyron relation.
Concept 4

Precipitation Catchment and Evaporation Pan Physics

Water balance inputs and outputs require standardized gauges and pans.

  • Catchment Error: Wind eddies reduce rain catch efficiency; rim height must be leveled precisely.
  • Pan Coefficient (K_p): Empirically derived factor (approx. 0.70 to 0.80) to convert open pan evaporation to reference crop evapotranspiration (ET_0).

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Stevenson Screen (Meteorological Instrument Enclosure)
A double-louvered wooden shelter painted white, designed to protect thermometers and hygrometers from direct radiation while permitting free air circulation.
Psychrometer (Dry and Wet Bulb Hygrometer)
An instrument containing two identical thermometers (dry and wet) used to determine atmospheric humidity through evaporative cooling.
Symons' Rain Gauge (Standard Non-Recording Rain Gauge)
A standard meteorological gauge with a 127 mm collector funnel used to measure daily cumulative rainfall.
Cup Anemometer (Wind Speed Sensor)
An instrument with rotating hemispherical cups used to measure horizontal wind velocity and cumulative wind run.
Wind Vane (Wind Direction Indicator)
A pivoting asymmetric arrow pointing into the wind to indicate directional origin.
Campbell-Stokes Recorder (Sunshine Duration Recorder)
An optical device using a glass sphere to focus sun rays and burn a temporal trace on a graduated card.
Pyranometer (Global Solar Radiation Sensor)
A radiometer designed to measure broadband solar irradiance from a flat 180-degree hemispherical view.
Class A Pan Evaporimeter (Open Water Evaporation Tank)
A standardized circular pan of 120.7 cm diameter used to quantify free-water surface evaporation.
Dew Point Temperature (Condensation Threshold Temperature)
The temperature to which air must be cooled at constant pressure to reach saturation.
Albedo (Surface Reflectivity)
The ratio of reflected solar radiation to incident radiation on a surface, expressed as a percentage.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Stevenson Screen is painted white to maximize solar reflection and maintain internal ambient temperature equilibrium.
  • Standard height of temperature sensors is 1.25 to 2.0 meters above short turf grass.
  • Symons' rain gauge funnel diameter is 127 mm (5 inches).
  • Wind vane indicates the direction *from* which the wind originates.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Weather elements are systematically recorded inside a Stevenson screen at standard agrometeorological observatories.
  • Maximum and minimum thermometers use mercury and alcohol respectively due to their distinct physical properties.
  • Humidity is quantified via psychrometric depression, hair hygrometers, or electronic capacitance sensors.
  • Rainfall measurement requires a level non-recording or recording gauge free from aerodynamic wind shadows.
  • Solar radiation and sunshine hours are vital inputs for estimating crop photosynthesis and reference evapotranspiration.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Maximum Thermometer VS Minimum Thermometer

Maximum thermometer uses mercury with a constriction to hold peak temperature; minimum thermometer uses alcohol with an index pin to mark lowest temperature.

Dry Bulb Temperature VS Dew Point Temperature

Dry bulb is actual ambient air temperature; dew point is the temperature where air becomes fully saturated and condensation begins.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following liquids is exclusively used in a standard minimum agrometeorological thermometer?

Explanation: Ethyl alcohol has a very low freezing point (-114°C), making it ideal for minimum thermometers where sub-zero winter temperatures might freeze mercury.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard height of the thermometer bulb inside a Stevenson Screen above the ground surface?

Explanation: WMO and ICAR standards specify that thermometers in a Stevenson Screen must be mounted with their bulbs at 1.25 to 2.0 meters above short, well-watered grass.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The diameter of the collecting funnel in a standard Symons' non-recording rain gauge is:

Explanation: Symons' rain gauge features a brass or copper funnel with a standard standard aperture diameter of 127 mm (5 inches).

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why is the Stevenson Screen painted white on its outer surfaces?

Explanation: White paint has a high albedo, reflecting incoming solar radiation and preventing the wooden box from heating up, thereby recording true ambient air temperature.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which meteorological instrument is utilized for recording the duration of bright sunshine hours?

Explanation: The Campbell-Stokes recorder uses a glass sphere lens to focus solar rays, burning a distinct temporal trace on a graduated card to measure bright sunshine hours.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In a psychrometer, what does a large wet-bulb depression indicate about the surrounding air mass?

Explanation: A large wet-bulb depression means rapid evaporation from the wet wick, which lowers the wet-bulb temperature significantly, indicating dry air with low relative humidity.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary working principle behind the operation of a maximum thermometer?

Explanation: A maximum thermometer has a narrow constriction near the bulb; mercury passes through it during expansion but cannot return on cooling until manually swung.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The Class A Evaporation Pan has a standard diameter of approximately:

Explanation: The standard Class A open pan evaporimeter has a diameter of 120.7 cm (4 feet) and a depth of 25 cm, mounted on an open wooden frame.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Wind direction reported as 'North-West' by a meteorological wind vane implies that the wind is blowing:

Explanation: Meteorological wind direction always specifies the direction *from* which the wind originates. A NW wind blows from NW to SE.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Global solar radiation (direct plus diffuse) on a horizontal plane is measured using a:

Explanation: A pyranometer is a type of actinometer used to measure broad-spectrum solar irradiance from a flat 180-degree hemispherical field of view.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Atmospheric pressure is measured in standard meteorological practice using which primary instrument?

Explanation: Fortin's Mercurial Barometer is the standard precision instrument used for measuring absolute atmospheric pressure in agrometeorological observatories.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the minimum recommended airflow velocity across the wet bulb wick of a psychrometer to ensure accurate humidity readings?

Explanation: An airflow of at least 2.5 m/s is required across the wet-bulb wick to ensure complete ventilation and accurate psychrometric depression.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which instrument is classified as a recording rain gauge?

Explanation: Weighing bucket and tipping bucket rain gauges are recording rain gauges that automatically record rainfall amount against time on a rotating chart or data logger.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The constant factor used to multiply Class A Pan evaporation to estimate reference crop evapotranspiration (ET_0) is called:

Explanation: The pan coefficient (K_p) corrects the water loss from an elevated metal pan to match that of an extensive green crop canopy surface.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Soil temperature at various depths is routinely measured in agrometeorological observatories using:

Explanation: Bent-stem soil thermometers are installed at specific depths (5, 10, 20 cm) with stems bent at right angles so the scale remains visible above ground.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of double louvers in a Stevenson screen?

Explanation: Double louvers permit continuous lateral airflow to keep internal temperatures equal to ambient air while shielding instruments from solar radiation and rain.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which physical property makes mercury ideal for maximum thermometers?

Explanation: Mercury has a uniform coefficient of expansion, high boiling point (356.7°C), and does not wet glass, making it ideal for accurate high-temperature measurement.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Relative humidity is defined mathematically as:

Explanation: Relative humidity is the ratio of the partial pressure of water vapor to the equilibrium vapor pressure of water at the same temperature, multiplied by 100.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the unit of wind run measured by a cup anemometer over a 24-hour period?

Explanation: Wind run is the cumulative linear distance traveled by the wind past the anemometer over a given period, typically expressed in kilometers per day.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In agro-meteorological station layout, the grass plot surrounding the enclosure should ideally be kept:

Explanation: The observatory enclosure and surrounding area must be maintained with short, well-watered grass to represent standard natural surface albedo and roughness.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

A hydrometeorological observation shows a dry bulb temperature of 30°C and a wet bulb temperature of 20°C. Which of the following best describes this condition?

Solution & Rationale: A wet bulb depression of 10°C (30°C - 20°C) indicates substantial evaporation potential and relatively low relative humidity.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

During winter frost monitoring, why is the minimum thermometer mounted horizontally rather than vertically?

Solution & Rationale: Horizontal mounting ensures the index pin remains correctly suspended and dragged by the meniscus of the contracting alcohol column without gravitational distortion.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

In designing an agrometeorological station, what is the mandatory minimum distance rule for positioning rain gauges relative to surrounding trees or structures?

Solution & Rationale: To prevent wind eddies and undercatch errors, rain gauges must be placed away from obstacles by a horizontal distance at least 2 to 4 times the height of the obstacle.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Which radiation instrument utilizes blackened thermopile junctions exposed to solar radiation under a glass dome?

Solution & Rationale: A pyranometer uses thermopile sensors under glass domes to measure broadband global radiation by converting thermal gradients into electrical voltage.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary reason why mercury is unsuitable for minimum thermometers?

Solution & Rationale: Mercury freezes at -39°C. In regions experiencing frost or sub-zero agricultural temperatures, mercury would solidify, rendering the minimum thermometer inoperable.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the function of the check-nut in Fortin's Mercurial Barometer?

Solution & Rationale: The leather bag at the base of Fortin's barometer can be raised using the thumb-screw and secured with a check-nut to fill the cistern with mercury during transport, preventing air entry.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

How does a tipping-bucket rain gauge quantify precipitation amount?

Solution & Rationale: A tipping-bucket rain gauge uses a balanced two-chamber bucket that tips each time a calibrated volume of water (typically 0.1 mm or 0.2 mm) accumulates, sending an electrical pulse.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the theoretical lower limit of relative humidity in natural outdoor agricultural atmospheres?

Solution & Rationale: While theoretical 0% is possible in closed laboratory desiccators, natural outdoor air rarely drops below 5% to 10% even in hyper-arid desert agricultural zones due to background moisture.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

In a Campbell-Stokes sunshine recorder, what causes the burn trace on the calibrated card?

Solution & Rationale: The solid glass sphere concentrates direct solar radiation onto the chemically treated cardboard strip, charring a dark trace whenever the sun shines brightly.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Why must the Class A Evaporation Pan be installed on a wooden grill or platform raised 15 cm above the ground?

Solution & Rationale: Raising the pan 15 cm on an open wooden frame allows air to circulate underneath, maintaining thermal equilibrium between the pan bottom and ambient air.
