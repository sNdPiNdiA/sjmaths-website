Introduction to Agricultural Meteorology and Observation Networks

Agricultural Meteorology (Agrometeorology) studies the response of biological systems—specifically crops, livestock, and forests—to weather and climate parameters. Precise measurement of microclimatic and macroclimatic variables forms the backbone of crop modeling, irrigation scheduling, and pest forecasting. Observatories established by the India Meteorological Department (IMD) deploy standardized instruments housed within specific enclosures to maintain spatial and temporal data consistency.

  • Standard Enclosure: The Stevenson Screen is a double-louvered wooden box painted white to reflect solar radiation, positioned 1.25 to 2.0 meters above short green grass to house temperature and humidity sensors free from direct radiative interference.
  • Microclimate Sensors: Canopy temperature, soil heat flux, and photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) sensors record the immediate physical environment of cultivated crops like Triticum aestivum and Oryza sativa.

Measurement of Atmospheric Temperature and Thermal Regimes

Thermal properties dictate phenological development, germination rates, and heat unit accumulations (Growing Degree Days - GDD). Temperature measurements require strict calibration using liquid-in-glass thermometers or electrical resistance transducers.

  • Maximum Thermometer: Utilizes mercury with a constriction near the bulb that prevents the mercury column from retreating as the temperature falls, recording the peak daily thermal maximum.
  • Minimum Thermometer: Utilizes alcohol as the thermometric liquid containing a small dumbbell-shaped index. As temperature drops, the meniscus drags the index down; as temperature rises, the alcohol flows past the index, leaving it at the lowest recorded thermal point.
  • Soil Thermometers: Bent-stem or encasement thermometers installed at standard depths (5 cm, 10 cm, 20 cm, 30 cm) to monitor thermal transfer into the root zone of crops.

Measurement of Atmospheric Humidity and Vapor Pressure Deficit

Atmospheric moisture profoundly influences transpiration rates, stomatal conductance, and fungal spore germination in pathogens like Magnaporthe oryzae. Humidity is quantified using absolute, relative, and dew-point parameters.

  • Dry and Wet Bulb Hygrometer (Psychrometer): Consists of two matched mercury thermometers. The wet bulb is covered by a muslin wick dipped in distilled water. Ventilation lowers the wet-bulb temperature via evaporative cooling; the depression is used alongside psychrometric tables to determine relative humidity and dew point.
  • Hair Hygrometer: Operates on the principle that degreased human or animal hair expands with increasing relative humidity and contracts when drier. Changes in length are magnified through a mechanical lever system onto a graduated dial or drum.

Precipitation Measurement and Rain Gauge Mechanics

Rainfall is the dominant hydrological input in rainfed agricultural systems across India. Accurating quantifying depth, intensity, and temporal distribution is vital for watershed management and drought mitigation.

  • Symons' Non-Recording Rain Gauge: The standard manual gauge adopted by the IMD. It consists of a metal funnel with a 127 mm (5 inches) collecting diameter leading into a receiver bottle. Rainfall depth is measured daily at 08:30 hours IST using a graduated measuring glass.
  • Self-Recording Rain Gauges: Includes tipping-bucket and siphon-type (Natural Siphon) rain gauges that continuously record rainfall amount against time on a clockwork drum chart, essential for calculating storm intensity and runoff potential.

Wind Dynamics and Solar Radiation Instruments

Wind speed, direction, and solar energy input govern evapotranspiration (ET_o) rates, lodging risks, and photosynthetic efficiency in agricultural fields.

  • Cup Anemometer: Measures horizontal wind run and speed using three or four hemispherical cups mounted on a vertical axis. Wind speed is integrated over time and recorded in kilometers or miles of wind run.
  • Wind Vane: A balanced asymmetrical arrow or plate pivoting on a vertical shaft that indicates instantaneous wind direction relative to cardinal points.
  • Campbell-Stokes Sunshine Recorder: Utilizes a solid glass sphere that focuses direct solar beam radiation onto a chemically treated, graduated card, charring a trace line corresponding to bright sunshine hours.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The standard collecting diameter of Symons' non-recording rain gauge used by the IMD is 127 mm (5 inches).
  • A Stevenson screen is painted white with specialized high-reflectance paint to prevent direct solar absorption and maintain internal air temperature equilibrium.
  • The thermometric liquid used inside a maximum thermometer is mercury, whereas a minimum thermometer uses alcohol.
  • Class A evaporation pan is constructed of galvanized iron (GI) sheet with a standard diameter of 120.7 cm and a depth of 25 cm.
  • Relative humidity is calculated using dry-bulb and wet-bulb depression values applied alongside standard psychrometric tables.
  • Campbell-Stokes sunshine recorder uses a solid glass sphere to burn a trace on a graduated card, measuring bright sunshine hours.
  • Wind speed in agricultural meteorology is traditionally expressed in kilometers per hour (km/h) or meters per second (m/s) using a cup anemometer.
  • Soil temperatures are recorded at shallower depths (5 cm and 10 cm) to monitor seed germination and deeper depths (20 cm and 30 cm) for root zone thermal tracking.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the thermometric liquids: believing minimum thermometers use mercury instead of alcohol (alcohol remains liquid at much lower freezing temperatures than mercury).
  • Assuming Symons' rain gauge records rainfall intensity continuously, whereas it is strictly a manual cumulative daily measuring device.
  • Placing a rain gauge directly on the ground surface, ignoring the splash error. Standard gauges must be mounted with the rim exactly 30 cm above ground level.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Meteorological Instruments in Agricultural Meteorology

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

The Stevenson Screen and Ambient Thermal Shielding

The Stevenson screen is the international standard shelter designed to protect meteorological sensors from direct solar radiation, precipitation, and wind turbulence while ensuring adequate ventilation.

  • Height Requirement: Base positioned 1.25m to 2.0m above ground level.
  • Design Feature: Double-louvered sides painted white to maximize albedo and minimize thermal conduction.
Concept 2

Precipitation Measurement Mechanics

Accurate precipitation data is crucial for crop water requirement calculations and irrigation scheduling.

  • Symons' Gauge: Non-recording manual gauge read every 24 hours at 08:30 IST.
  • Rim Height: Installed precisely 30 cm above ground level to eliminate soil splash errors.
Concept 3

Atmospheric Humidity Quantification

Water vapor content in the lower troposphere dictates plant transpiration and disease epidemics.

  • Psychrometer: Measures dry and wet bulb depression.
  • Hair Hygrometer: Utilizes organic hair strands that expand or contract proportionally with relative humidity changes.
Concept 4

Wind and Solar Radiation Sensors

Dynamic atmospheric forces and radiant energy drive crop photosynthesis and water loss.

  • Cup Anemometer: Integrates wind run and speed over time.
  • Sunshine Recorder: Focuses solar beams via a glass sphere onto a calibrated card.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Stevenson Screen (Meteorological Instrument Shelter)
A double-louvered wooden shelter painted white, housing thermometers and hygrometers at a standard height of 1.25 to 2.0 meters.
Symons' Rain Gauge (Non-recording Precipitation Gauge)
A manual rain gauge with a 127 mm funnel collector used for measuring 24-hour cumulative rainfall depth.
Psychrometer (Dry and Wet Bulb Hygrometer)
An instrument comprising paired thermometers used to determine relative humidity and dew point through evaporative cooling.
Cup Anemometer (Wind Speed Recorder)
An electromechanical device with rotating cups that measures horizontal wind run and speed.
Campbell-Stokes Recorder (Sunshine Duration Instrument)
An optical device utilizing a glass sphere to focus direct sunlight and burn a record on a graduated card.
Maximum Thermometer (Mercury-in-glass Peak Thermometer)
A thermometer featuring a constriction near the bulb that retains the highest recorded temperature of the day.
Minimum Thermometer (Alcohol-in-glass Low Thermometer)
A thermometer utilizing alcohol and an internal index pin to record the lowest daily ambient temperature.
Class A Evaporation Pan (Open Water Evaporimeter)
A standardized galvanized iron pan (120.7 cm diameter) used to measure free water surface evaporation for agronomic scheduling.
Wind Vane (Wind Direction Indicator)
A pivoted asymmetrical arrow indicating instantaneous wind direction relative to geographical coordinates.
Soil Thermometer (Subsurface Thermal Probe)
An encapsulated bent-stem thermometer inserted at standard soil depths to monitor thermal regimes in root zones.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Symons' rain gauge aperture diameter is 127 mm (5 inches).
  • Stevenson screen height above ground level is 1.25 to 2.0 meters.
  • Rain gauge rim height above ground level to prevent splash is 30 cm.
  • Class A evaporation pan diameter is 120.7 cm and depth is 25 cm.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Meteorological instruments provide vital baseline data for agricultural planning, irrigation scheduling, and crop modeling.
  • The Stevenson screen protects temperature and humidity instruments from direct solar radiation and rain.
  • Symons' rain gauge is the standard non-recording precipitation gauge utilized across Indian meteorological stations.
  • Maximum thermometers use mercury with a constriction, while minimum thermometers use alcohol with an index pin.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Maximum Thermometer VS Minimum Thermometer

Maximum thermometer uses mercury and a constriction; minimum thermometer uses alcohol and a dumbbell-shaped index pin.

Recording Rain Gauge VS Non-recording Rain Gauge

Recording gauges (tipping-bucket/siphon) document rainfall against time automatically; non-recording gauges (Symons') measure 24-hour cumulative totals manually.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard height of the Stevenson Screen base above the ground surface in an agrometeorological observatory?

Explanation: The Stevenson screen is mounted on a wooden or metal stand such that the floor of the screen is maintained between 1.25 and 2.0 meters above short green turf to measure ambient air representative of the crop canopy environment.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard collecting diameter of the Symons' non-recording rain gauge adopted by the India Meteorological Department (IMD)?

Explanation: Symons' rain gauge features a standard circular brass funnel with an inner diameter of exactly 127 mm (5 inches) connected to a receiver bottle.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which thermometric liquid is utilized inside a standard maximum thermometer?

Explanation: Mercury is used in maximum thermometers because it allows a constriction near the bulb to hold the maximum reading when temperature falls.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why is the Stevenson Screen painted white on its outer surfaces?

Explanation: Painting the Stevenson screen white maximizes its albedo, reflecting incoming solar radiation and preventing internal air temperatures from rising above ambient levels.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard rim height of a rain gauge above the ground surface to prevent splash error?

Explanation: The rim of a rain gauge must be installed exactly 30 cm above ground level to prevent raindrops splashing from the surrounding soil surface into the funnel.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which instrument is specifically utilized to record the duration of bright sunshine hours during a day?

Explanation: The Campbell-Stokes sunshine recorder focuses direct solar rays through a glass sphere onto a chemically treated card to char a trace line corresponding to bright sunshine hours.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary thermometric liquid used in a minimum thermometer?

Explanation: Alcohol is used in minimum thermometers because its freezing point (-114°C) is much lower than mercury's (-39°C), preventing freezing during cold winter nights.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In a dry and wet bulb psychrometer, what causes the temperature drop in the wet bulb thermometer?

Explanation: Water evaporating from the muslin wick surrounding the wet bulb absorbs latent heat from the thermometer bulb, causing a drop in temperature known as wet-bulb depression.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does a cup anemometer measure in an agricultural meteorological station?

Explanation: A cup anemometer measures horizontal wind speed and total wind run over a specific time interval, usually recorded in km/h.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard diameter of the Class A Evaporation Pan used for open pan evaporation measurements?

Explanation: The standard Class A Evaporation Pan has a diameter of 120.7 cm (4 feet) and a depth of 25 cm, constructed of unpainted galvanized iron.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

At what standard soil depths are soil thermometers typically installed in an agrometeorological observatory?

Explanation: Standard soil thermometer depths monitored in agrometeorology are 5 cm, 10 cm, 20 cm, and 30 cm to capture thermal profiles across the upper root zone.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which instrument is used to determine instantaneous wind direction relative to cardinal points?

Explanation: A wind vane (weather vane) is an asymmetrical pivoted mechanical pointer that indicates instantaneous wind direction.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the role of the dumbbell-shaped index pin inside a minimum thermometer?

Explanation: The meniscus of the alcohol column drags the index pin downward as temperature drops; when temperature rises, alcohol flows past the pin, leaving its lower end at the minimum temperature.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which meteorological parameter is expressed in millibars (mb) or hectopascals (hPa)?

Explanation: Atmospheric pressure is the force exerted by the weight of the air column above and is standardly expressed in millibars (mb) or hectopascals (hPa).

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of the double louvers on a Stevenson Screen?

Explanation: Double-louvered slats permit ambient air to circulate freely through the shelter while blocking direct solar radiation and preventing external radiant heating of the enclosed instruments.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a self-recording rain gauge?

Explanation: Tipping-bucket and natural siphon rain gauges are self-recording instruments that continuously chart precipitation against time, unlike Symons' manual gauge.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main purpose of measuring open pan evaporation in agriculture?

Explanation: Open pan evaporation data is multiplied by a pan coefficient (K_p) to estimate reference crop evapotranspiration (ET_o), forming the basis for scientific irrigation water management.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What specific feature distinguishes a maximum thermometer from an ordinary thermometer?

Explanation: A maximum thermometer has a narrow constriction in the mercury capillary just above the bulb, which prevents mercury from retreating when temperatures decline until manually reset.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard unit for reporting rainfall depth measured by a rain gauge in India?

Explanation: Rainfall depth is universally reported in millimeters (mm), where 1 mm of rainfall corresponds to 1 liter of water uniformly distributed over 1 square meter of surface area.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which instrument uses human or animal hair to measure moisture changes in the atmosphere?

Explanation: A hair hygrometer utilizes degreased human hair whose length varies predictably with relative humidity, driving a mechanical lever to indicate humidity percentages.

In an agrometeorological observatory, what is the exact vertical distance maintained between the ground surface and the rim of Symons' rain gauge?

Solution & Rationale: The rim of Symons' rain gauge must be placed exactly 30 cm above the ground surface to prevent soil splash water from entering the funnel while avoiding aerodynamic wind eddy distortions.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following instruments is calibrated to measure potential evapotranspiration (PET) directly in meteorological stations?

Solution & Rationale: The Class A Evaporation Pan measures free water surface evaporation, which is subsequently adjusted using pan coefficients to estimate potential evapotranspiration.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

What structural characteristic of the Stevenson Screen ensures that internal instruments are shielded from direct thermal radiation?

Solution & Rationale: Double-louvered wooden walls painted white provide optimal thermal reflection and airflow ventilation, maintaining internal air temperatures identical to the ambient environment.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

How is a maximum thermometer reset after recording daily peak temperatures?

Solution & Rationale: A maximum thermometer is reset by holding it firmly by the top and swinging or whirling it in a downward arc so centrifugal force pushes the mercury column past the constriction back into the bulb.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

What principle governs the operation of the Campbell-Stokes Sunshine Recorder?

Solution & Rationale: The solid glass sphere acts as a convex lens that concentrates direct solar beam radiation onto a treated card, burning a carbonized track as the sun traverses the sky.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

Why is alcohol preferred over mercury in minimum thermometers used in cold agricultural regions?

Solution & Rationale: Mercury solidifies at approximately -39°C, making it unsuitable for freezing winter nights where minimum temperatures drop lower, whereas alcohol freezes at -114°C.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary function of the wet-bulb thermometer in a psychrometer when evaluating crop microclimates?

Solution & Rationale: The wet-bulb thermometer measures the temperature depression caused by water evaporation from its moist muslin cover, which correlates directly with ambient relative humidity and vapor pressure deficit.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

In wind measurement instrumentation, what does 'wind run' recorded by a cup anemometer signify?

Solution & Rationale: Wind run represents the cumulative horizontal distance the wind has traveled past the instrument, expressed in kilometers, calculated by multiplying average wind speed by time.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which soil depth represents the shallowest standard measurement interval for soil thermometers in agricultural observatories?

Solution & Rationale: Soil thermometers are standardly installed at 5 cm, 10 cm, 20 cm, and 30 cm depths, with 5 cm representing the shallowest layer monitoring immediate seedbed thermal conditions.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the standard color specification for painting meteorological instrument enclosures (Stevenson Screens) globally?

Solution & Rationale: Bright white paint is mandatory for Stevenson screens to maximize solar reflectance (albedo) and prevent radiative absorption that would falsely elevate internal temperature recordings.
