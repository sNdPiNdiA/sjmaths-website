Environmental Factors Affecting Crop Growth

Introduction to Agro-Ecological and Environmental Determinants

Crop growth and development are the net manifestation of the interaction between the plant's genetic makeup (genotype) and the surrounding environment (phenotype = genotype + environment). In agricultural meteorology and agronomy, the environment is broadly classified into climatic (above-ground atmospheric conditions), edaphic (below-ground soil properties), biotic (living organisms), and physiographic (topographical features) factors.

  • Climatic Factors: Include solar radiation, temperature, precipitation, atmospheric humidity, and wind velocity.
  • Edaphic Factors: Encompass soil moisture, soil air, soil temperature, soil reaction (pH), and available plant nutrients.
  • Biotic Factors: Involve beneficial and harmful interactions with insects, pathogens, weeds, and soil microorganisms such as Rhizobium and Azotobacter.

Solar Radiation and Photoperiodism in Crop Plants

Solar radiation provides the ultimate source of energy for photosynthesis and dry matter accumulation. Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) ranges between 400 to 700 nanometers. Crop response to the duration of light, known as photoperiodism, dictates floral initiation and geographical adaptation.

  • Short-Day Plants (SDP): Flower when day length is shorter than a critical photoperiod (e.g., Oryza sativa, Glycine max, Sorghum bicolor).
  • Long-Day Plants (LDP): Require day lengths exceeding a critical threshold (e.g., Triticum aestivum, Hordeum vulgare, Pisum sativum).
  • Day-Neutral Plants (DNP): Flowering is unaffected by day length (e.g., Zea mays, Lycopersicon esculentum, Gossypium hirsutum).

Thermal Regimes: Cardinal Temperatures and GDD

Temperature dictates the rate of biochemical reactions, enzyme kinetics, and phenological development. Every crop species possesses specific cardinal temperatures: minimum (below which metabolic activity ceases), optimum (at which growth rate is maximal), and maximum (above which proteins denature).

  • Growing Degree Days (GDD): Also known as thermal units, calculated as GDD = Tmax + Tmin2 - Tbase, where base temperature is typically 10°C for warm-season crops and 4°C to 5°C for cool-season crops.
  • Vernalization: Low-temperature requirement for the initiation of flowering in winter cereals like Secale cereale and winter varieties of Triticum aestivum.

Atmospheric Moisture, Precipitation, and Drought Dynamics

Water serves as the universal solvent, reactant in photosynthesis, and coolant via transpiration. Precipitation distribution and atmospheric humidity dictate whether a region requires supplemental irrigation or rainfed management.

  • Relative Humidity (RH): High RH (>80%) favors fungal spore germination such as Erysiphe graminis in wheat, whereas low RH accelerates transpiration and induces water stress.
  • Vapour Pressure Deficit (VPD): The driving force for transpiration; high VPD increases water loss and can cause stomatal closure, limiting carbon assimilation.

Edaphic and Physiographic Control on Crop Productivity

Soil acts as a physical anchor and reservoir of water and essential mineral ions. Edaphic constraints such as salinity, sodicity, waterlogging, and extreme pH directly impair root elongation and nutrient uptake.

  • Soil Aeration: Adequate oxygen diffusion rate (ODR) is mandatory for root respiration; hypoxia induced by waterlogging restricts active ion uptake and induces lodging.
  • Physiographic Effects: Aspect (direction of slope) alters solar insolation; southern slopes in the Northern Hemisphere receive higher thermal energy compared to northern slopes.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) wavelength range is 400 to 700 nanometers.
  • Growing Degree Days (GDD) formula uses base temperatures of 10°C for warm-season crops and 4.5°C for cool-season crops.
  • Vernalization promotes early flowering in winter wheat by exposure to low temperatures (0°C to 5°C).
  • Relative humidity exceeding 85% combined with cloudy weather severely aggravates the incidence of blast disease in rice caused by Magnaporthe oryzae.
  • Transpiration ratio is the mass of water transpired by a plant per unit mass of dry matter produced.
  • Excess carbon dioxide enrichment in protected cultivation enhances net photosynthetic rates in C3 crops like Solanum lycopersicum.
  • Soil pH range of 6.0 to 7.5 provides optimum availability for most macro and micronutrients.
  • Water use efficiency (WUE) is highest in C4 crops such as Zea mays and Sorghum bicolor due to the absence of photorespiration.
  • Hypoxia in waterlogged soils causes accumulation of toxic organic acids and reduction in iron and manganese.
  • Windbreaks and shelterbelts planted perpendicular to prevailing winds reduce wind velocity and mechanical damage to standing crops.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Day-Neutral plants with Long-Day plants; remember that maize flowers irrespective of day length.
  • Assuming higher temperature always increases crop yield; temperatures above the optimum threshold accelerate senescence and reduce grain-filling duration.
  • Treating GDD calculation as absolute without accounting for base temperature specific to the crop species.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Environmental Factors Affecting Crop Growth

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Climatic Determinants of Crop Growth

Climatic factors govern the macro-environment of agricultural production. Solar radiation drives photosynthesis, thermal units dictate phenology, and atmospheric precipitation satisfies evaporative demand.

  • PAR Fraction: Approximately 45-50% of total incoming solar radiation falls within the 400-700 nm PAR spectrum.
  • Photoperiod: Controls floral induction through phytochrome pigment regulation (Pr and Pfr forms).
Concept 2

Thermal Regimes and Phenological Development

Crop development rate is linearly related to temperature within specific biological limits. Heat unit concepts enable prediction of harvest maturity across diverse agro-climatic zones.

  • Thermal Unit: Measured in Degree Days (°C-days) accumulated daily above a threshold base temperature.
  • Cold Injury: Chilling injury occurs above freezing (0-15°C) in tropical crops like rice, while freezing injury occurs below 0°C.
Concept 3

Edaphic Environment and Root Function

The rhizosphere environment dictates nutrient acquisition and root respiration. Soil physical properties directly influence root elongation rates and microbial activity.

  • Soil Aeration: Soil air should contain at least 10-12% oxygen by volume for normal root metabolism.
  • Bulk Density: Values exceeding 1.6 g/cm³ in clay soils mechanically restrict root penetration.
Concept 4

Biotic and Anthropogenic Environmental Factors

Interactions with weeds, pests, diseases, and human-induced atmospheric modifications strongly alter agricultural yields.

  • Microclimate Modification: Practices like mulching, irrigation, and shading alter the immediate crop boundary layer.
  • Allelopathy: Chemical inhibition of one plant species by another through secondary metabolites released into the environment.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR (400-700 nm))
Solar radiation waveband utilized by green plants for photosynthetic carbon fixation.
Photoperiodism (Photoperiodic response)
Physiological reaction of organisms to the length of day or night, governing flowering.
Growing Degree Days (GDD (Thermal Units))
A heuristic measure of thermal energy used to predict plant and pest development rates.
Vernalization (Low-temperature induction)
The induction of a plant's flowering process by exposure to prolonged cold winter temperatures.
Cardinal Temperatures (Min, Opt, Max temperatures)
The three temperature limits (minimum, optimum, maximum) that define the range of metabolic activity.
Vapour Pressure Deficit (VPD)
The difference between the saturation vapour pressure and actual vapour pressure at a given temperature.
Edaphic Factors (Soil environmental properties)
Physical, chemical, and biological properties of soil that influence plant growth.
Hypoxia (Oxygen deficiency)
Low oxygen condition in waterlogged soils that restricts root respiration and nutrient uptake.
Allelopathy (Biochemical inhibition)
Biological phenomenon where an organism produces biochemicals that influence germination and growth.
Transpiration Ratio (Water requirement ratio)
Mass of water transpired per unit dry matter produced by the crop canopy.
Shelterbelt (Windbreak barrier)
Rows of trees or shrubs planted to protect an area from wind and reduce soil erosion.
Boundary Layer (Micro-climatic layer)
The thin layer of air adhering directly to the plant leaf surface where temperature and humidity differ from bulk air.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Rice and soybean are classic Short-Day Plants.
  • Wheat and barley are classic Long-Day Plants.
  • Maize and tomato are Day-Neutral Plants.
  • Water Use Efficiency is higher in C4 plants than C3 plants.
  • Optimum soil pH range for most field crops is 6.0 to 7.5.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Environment consists of climatic, edaphic, biotic, and physiographic components.
  • PAR (400-700 nm) is essential for dry matter production via photosynthesis.
  • Crops are classified into Short-Day, Long-Day, and Day-Neutral based on photoperiodic requirements.
  • GDD calculation tracks thermal accumulation to forecast crop maturity stages.
  • Soil aeration and moisture status dictate root respiration and nutrient availability.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Short-Day Plants VS Long-Day Plants

SDP flower when day length is shorter than a critical limit, whereas LDP flower when day length exceeds a critical limit.

Chilling Injury VS Freezing Injury

Chilling injury occurs at low positive temperatures (0-15°C) in tropical crops, while freezing injury involves ice crystal formation below 0°C.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the waveband range of Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) utilized by crops?

Explanation: Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) spans the visible light spectrum from 400 to 700 nanometers, which is absorbed by chlorophyll pigments.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following crop species is classified as a Short-Day Plant (SDP)?

Explanation: Rice (Oryza sativa) is a short-day plant, whereas wheat, pea, and barley are long-day plants.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the formula for calculating Growing Degree Days (GDD)?

Explanation: GDD is calculated as the mean daily temperature minus the base temperature: GDD = [(Tmax + Tmin) / 2] - Tbase.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which physiological process is induced by exposure to low temperatures in winter cereals?

Explanation: Vernalization is the promotion of a plant's flowering phase by exposure to the cold temperatures of winter.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop category exhibits higher Water Use Efficiency (WUE) under high temperatures?

Explanation: C4 plants possess PEP carboxylase and Kranz anatomy, minimizing photorespiration and yielding higher WUE.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical optimum soil pH range for the cultivation of most field crops?

Explanation: A soil pH range of 6.0 to 7.5 maximizes the availability of essential macro and micronutrients.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a classic example of a Day-Neutral Plant (DNP)?

Explanation: Maize (Zea mays) is day-neutral, whereas soybean and tobacco are short-day and sugar beet is long-day.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What term describes the low oxygen condition in waterlogged soils that restricts root respiration?

Explanation: Hypoxia refers to reduced oxygen levels in waterlogged soils, impairing root metabolism and ion absorption.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the biological significance of shelterbelts in agricultural meteorology?

Explanation: Shelterbelts and windbreaks reduce wind speed, minimizing soil erosion and physical damage to crops.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the driving force for transpiration in plant canopies?

Explanation: Vapour Pressure Deficit (VPD) represents the drying power of air, serving as the primary physical driving force for transpiration.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which phytochrome pigment form absorbs red light (660 nm) and is physiologically inactive?

Explanation: Pr (phytochrome red) absorbs red light at 660 nm and converts to Pfr, which absorbs far-red light at 730 nm.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which climatic factor most directly affects the rate of biochemical reactions in plants?

Explanation: Temperature governs enzyme kinetics and metabolic rates within plant tissues.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What type of injury occurs when tropical crops are exposed to low positive temperatures between 0°C and 15°C?

Explanation: Chilling injury occurs in warm-season crops (e.g., rice, cotton) when exposed to temperatures between 0°C and 15°C.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the ratio of water transpired to dry matter produced called?

Explanation: Transpiration ratio (or transpiration coefficient) is the mass of water transpired per unit dry matter produced.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is categorized as an edaphic factor affecting crop growth?

Explanation: Edaphic factors pertain to soil properties, including soil moisture, air, temperature, pH, and nutrient status.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

How does high relative humidity (>85%) impact fungal plant pathogens like Magnaporthe oryzae?

Explanation: High relative humidity and leaf wetness provide free moisture necessary for fungal spore germination and infection.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is allelopathy?

Explanation: Allelopathy involves the release of allelochemicals that inhibit or stimulate the growth of neighboring plant species.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Why is adequate soil aeration critical for crop root systems?

Explanation: Oxygen is mandatory for root respiration, generating ATP required for active nutrient uptake and cell division.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which physiographic factor influences solar insolation received by mountain slopes in the Northern Hemisphere?

Explanation: Slope aspect determines the angle and duration of solar radiation incidence, affecting microclimate and soil temperature.

Calculate the Growing Degree Days (GDD) for a maize crop on a day where Tmax is 32°C and Tmin is 18°C, assuming a base temperature of 10°C.

Solution & Rationale: Mean temperature = (32 + 18) / 2 = 25°C. GDD = 25 - 10 = 15°C-days. Wait, calculation: (32+18)/2 = 25; 25 - 10 = 15. Let's correct option: Option A is 15 °C-days.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following pairings of crop and photoperiodic response is correct?

Solution & Rationale: Rice is a classic short-day plant. Wheat is long-day, soybean is short-day, and maize is day-neutral.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary physiological consequence of high Vapour Pressure Deficit (VPD) on plant leaves?

Solution & Rationale: High VPD increases the transpiration demand, often leading to stomatal closure to conserve plant water status.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Which cardinal temperature limit marks the point where plant metabolic enzymes undergo irreversible thermal denaturation?

Solution & Rationale: Temperatures exceeding the maximum cardinal limit cause protein denaturation and cessation of growth.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

In edaphic environments, what is the critical oxygen diffusion rate (ODR) below which crop root elongation is severely inhibited?

Solution & Rationale: An oxygen diffusion rate below 30-40 x 10^-8 g/cm²/min limits root respiration and nutrient uptake in most agricultural crops.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Which pigment system perceives photoperiodic signals and controls flowering in plants?

Solution & Rationale: Phytochrome is the photo-receptive pigment responsible for sensing day length and regulating flowering responses.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the effect of vernalization treatment on winter wheat varieties sown in spring?

Solution & Rationale: Winter wheat requires cold induction (vernalization); spring sowing without cold treatment prevents floral initiation, keeping the plant vegetative.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following environmental factors is classified as a biotic growth determinant?

Solution & Rationale: Biotic factors involve living interactions, such as nitrogen-fixing symbiosis between Rhizobium and legume roots.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

How does carbon dioxide enrichment in greenhouses affect C3 crop species?

Solution & Rationale: Elevated CO2 suppresses oxygenase activity of Rubisco, reducing photorespiration and enhancing net photosynthesis in C3 crops.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary agronomic purpose of establishing windbreaks around agricultural fields in arid zones?

Solution & Rationale: Windbreaks reduce wind velocity, decreasing evapotranspiration and protecting soil and crops from wind damage.
