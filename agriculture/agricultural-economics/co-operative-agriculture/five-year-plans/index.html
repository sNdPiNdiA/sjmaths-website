Introduction to Economic Planning and Agricultural Strategy in India

Economic planning in independent India derives its genesis from the socialist-leaning centralized planning model adopted from the erstwhile USSR under the guidance of Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru. The Planning Commission of India was established on March 15, 1950, with the Prime Minister acting as its ex-officio Chairman. The primary objective of economic planning was to maximize national income, attain rapid industrialization, achieve self-reliance, and alleviate rural poverty through targeted allocation of scarce national resources.

  • Genesis: Adoption of five-year economic cycles to systematically prioritize capital allocation across agriculture, industry, infrastructure, and social services.
  • Shift to NITI Aayog: The Planning Commission was dissolved and replaced by the National Institution for Transforming India (NITI Aayog) on January 1, 2015, shifting the planning paradigm from centralized resource command to cooperative federalism.
  • Primary Focus on Agriculture: Given that over 70% of India's population depended on agriculture at independence, initial plans focused heavily on agrarian restructuring, institutional land reforms, and large-scale irrigation infrastructure to overcome chronic food deficits.

First Three Five-Year Plans (1951-1966): Foundation and Crisis

The foundational phase of Indian planning was marked by an aggressive push towards agricultural self-sufficiency, institutional reforms, and establishment of heavy irrigation networks, culminating in severe macroeconomic shocks and food security crises.

  • First Five-Year Plan (1951-1956): Based on the Harrod-Domar model, it allocated top priority to agriculture and irrigation (nearly 44.6% of total outlay). Major achievements included the launch of the Community Development Programme (CDP) in 1952, initiation of major river valley projects (Bhakra Nangal, Hirakud, Damodar Valley), and laying the groundwork for abolishing the archaic Zamindari system. Target growth rate of 2.1% was surpassed, achieving an actual GDP growth of 3.6%.
  • Second Five-Year Plan (1956-1961): Based on the Mahalanobis model, it shifted focus toward rapid industrialization and heavy industries. Agriculture received a lower direct allocation, though community development blocks continued expansion.
  • Third Five-Year Plan (1961-1966): Emphasized achieving self-reliance in agricultural food grain production and industrial base consolidation. However, this plan suffered catastrophic setbacks due to the Sino-Indian War (1962), Indo-Pakistani War (1965), and severe consecutive droughts in 1965-66. This crisis necessitated the implementation of three Annual Plans (Plan Holidays from 1966 to 1969) and the initiation of the Green Revolution with High-Yielding Varieties (HYVs) of dwarf wheat (Triticum aestivum) and rice (Oryza sativa).

Mid-Term Plans and the Green Revolution Era (1969-1985): Fourth to Sixth Plans

This period witnessed the structural transformation of traditional farming into intensive, science-led commercial agriculture through high-yielding varieties, chemical fertilizers, expanded irrigation, and institutional credit support.

  • Fourth Five-Year Plan (1969-1974): Focused on 'Growth with Stability' and progressive achievement of self-reliance. It introduced the High Yielding Varieties Programme (HYVP), Small Farmers Development Agency (SFDA), and Marginal Farmers and Agricultural Labourers (MFAL) schemes to address agrarian disparities.
  • Fifth Five-Year Plan (1974-1979): Emphasized 'Removal of Poverty' (Garibi Hatao) and attainment of self-reliance. The Minimum Needs Programme (MNP) was launched. Command Area Development Authority (CADA) was established in 1974-75 to bridge the gap between irrigation potential created and utilized.
  • Sixth Five-Year Plan (1980-1985): Marked the actual beginning of economic liberalization and direct poverty alleviation via Integrated Rural Development Programme (IRDP) launched pan-India in 1980. National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD) was established on July 12, 1982, on the recommendation of the B. Sivaraman Committee to streamline agricultural credit.

Modernization, Liberalization, and Inclusive Growth (1985-2012): Seventh to Eleventh Plans

As India transitioned into a market-driven economy following the 1991 Balance of Payments crisis, agricultural planning pivoted toward diversification, agro-export promotion, watershed management, and ecological sustainability.

  • Seventh Five-Year Plan (1985-1990): Emphasized food, work, and productivity, focusing on eastern India's agricultural potential and dryland farming technologies.
  • Eighth Five-Year Plan (1992-1997): Post-liberalization plan focusing on human resource development, employment generation, and commercialization of agriculture.
  • Ninth Five-Year Plan (1997-2002): Focused on 'Growth with Social Justice and Equity'. Emphasized watershed development and wasteland reclamation.
  • Tenth Five-Year Plan (2002-2007): Targeted 8% GDP growth, aiming to reduce regional disparities and poverty. Launch of National Horticulture Mission (NHM) in 2005-06.
  • Eleventh Five-Year Plan (2007-2012): Focused on 'Faster and More Inclusive Growth'. Targeted 4% annual growth in agriculture through Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana (RKVY) launched in 2007 and National Food Security Mission (NFSM) in 2007-08.

Twelfth Plan, NITI Aayog, and Contemporary Agricultural Policy Framework

The final five-year plan period shifted focus to sustainable, green growth before transitioning to strategic policy formulation through NITI Aayog.

  • Twelfth Five-Year Plan (2012-2017): Focused on 'Faster, Sustainable, and More Inclusive Growth', targeting a 4% growth rate in agriculture. Addressed climate resilience, soil health card schemes, and agricultural marketing reforms.
  • Shift to Vision Documents: Post-2017, India adopted 15-year vision documents, 7-year strategy papers, and 3-year action agendas under NITI Aayog to maintain dynamic long-term planning.
  • Key Contemporary Initiatives: Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY, 2016), Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY, 2015), and Soil Health Card Scheme to maintain macro-agronomic stability and farm income security.

Memory Trick / Mnemonic
Mnemonic for the Focuses of the First Three Five-Year Plans
A - I - H (Agriculture, Industry, Heavy Agriculture/Self-reliance)

A stands for Agriculture (First Plan prioritised agrarian reform and irrigation); I stands for Industry (Second Plan Mahalanobis model prioritized heavy industries); H stands for Heavy agricultural push and self-reliance (Third Plan aimed at food grain self-sufficiency).

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the First Five-Year Plan model with the Second Plan model: The First Plan used the Harrod-Domar model focused on savings and capital accumulation in agriculture, whereas the Second Plan used the Mahalanobis model focused on heavy industrialization.
  • Assuming the Green Revolution started during a Five-Year Plan: It actually began during the 'Plan Holidays' (1966-1969) between the Third and Fourth Five-Year Plans due to severe food crises.
  • Misattributing the establishment of NABARD: NABARD was established in 1982 during the Sixth Five-Year Plan, not the Fifth Plan.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Five-Year Plans in Indian Agriculture: Economic Planning and Structural Reforms

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Foundational Planning and Agrarian Reform (1951-1961)

The initial phase of Indian planning focused on rectifying structural defects in agrarian relations inherited from the colonial era. Abolition of intermediary tenures (Zamindari) and tenancy reforms formed the core.

  • Harrod-Domar Model: Economic growth model utilized in the First Plan emphasizing investment rate and capital-output ratio.
  • Community Development Programme: Launched in 1952 to promote multi-dimensional rural development across agriculture, animal husbandry, and rural health.
Concept 2

Green Revolution and Agricultural Modernization (1966-1980)

The food crisis of the mid-1960s forced a technological breakthrough in agriculture, transitioning India from a food-deficient nation to a self-sufficient grain surplus economy.

  • HYVP: High Yielding Varieties Programme introduced dwarf semi-dwarf wheat and rice varieties responsive to chemical fertilizers and controlled irrigation.
  • CADA: Command Area Development Authority established to bridge the gap between irrigation potential created and actual farm-level utilization.
Concept 3

Institutional Credit and Rural Development (1980-1990)

The Sixth and Seventh Plans emphasized institutional credit restructuring and direct poverty alleviation programs to support small and marginal farmers.

  • NABARD: National apex development bank established on July 12, 1982, for agricultural refinancing and rural credit supervision.
  • IRDP: Integrated Rural Development Programme designed to provide income-generating assets to rural poor below the poverty line.
Concept 4

Contemporary Agricultural Policy and NITI Aayog (2007-Present)

Recent planning cycles target inclusive agricultural growth, climate resilience, and decentralized state-level agricultural planning.

  • RKVY: Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana providing flexible financial autonomy to states to boost agricultural GDP growth.
  • NITI Aayog: Policy think-tank replacing the Planning Commission, fostering cooperative federalism and bottom-up economic strategies.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Planning Commission (Central Planning Agency of India)
Body established in March 1950 to formulate India's Five-Year Plans for economic development.
Harrod-Domar Model (Keynesian Economic Growth Model)
Economic model used in the First Five-Year Plan emphasizing savings rate and capital-output ratio.
Mahalanobis Model (Two-Sector / Four-Sector Growth Model)
Economic strategy developed by P.C. Mahalanobis emphasizing heavy industrialization in the Second Five-Year Plan.
Plan Holiday (Inter-Plan Annual Planning Period)
Period from 1966 to 1969 when regular five-year planning was suspended due to economic crisis and wars.
HYVP (High Yielding Varieties Programme)
Agricultural modernization program launched in 1966-67 introducing dwarf responsive crop varieties.
CADA (Command Area Development Authority)
Program initiated in 1974-75 to maximize water use efficiency and bridge irrigation gaps.
NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development)
Apex refinancing institution established on July 12, 1982, for agricultural and rural credit.
IRDP (Integrated Rural Development Programme)
Poverty alleviation program launched in 1980 providing productive assets to rural households.
RKVY (Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana)
State-incentive agricultural development scheme launched in 2007 during the Eleventh Plan.
NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India)
Policy think-tank established on January 1, 2015, replacing the Planning Commission.
NFSM (National Food Security Mission)
Central sponsored scheme launched in 2007-08 to increase production of rice, wheat, and pulses.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

First Five-Year Plan VS Second Five-Year Plan

First Plan prioritized agriculture and irrigation using the Harrod-Domar model; Second Plan prioritized heavy industries using the Mahalanobis model.

Planning Commission VS NITI Aayog

Planning Commission exercised top-down resource allocation, whereas NITI Aayog functions as a cooperative federalist think-tank without financial allocation powers.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

When was the Planning Commission of India formally established?

Explanation: The Planning Commission was established by a resolution of the Government of India on March 15, 1950, with Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru as Chairman.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which economic model formed the basis of the First Five-Year Plan in India?

Explanation: The First Five-Year Plan was based on the Harrod-Domar growth model, which emphasizes that economic growth depends on net savings and capital-output ratio.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What was the primary focus of the First Five-Year Plan (1951-1956)?

Explanation: The First Plan allocated top priority to agriculture, irrigation, and power, dedicating nearly 44.6% of its total outlay to overcome food grain deficits.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The Community Development Programme (CDP) was launched in India on which date?

Explanation: The Community Development Programme (CDP) was launched on October 2, 1952, aiming for comprehensive rural upliftment across agriculture, animal husbandry, and infrastructure.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which Five-Year Plan was based on the P.C. Mahalanobis strategy emphasizing heavy industries?

Explanation: The Second Five-Year Plan (1956-1961), often called the Mahalanobis Plan, focused on rapid industrialization with a heavy emphasis on capital goods industries.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

During which period were the 'Plan Holidays' implemented in Indian economic history?

Explanation: Three Annual Plans spanning 1966 to 1969 are referred to as 'Plan Holidays' because regular five-year planning was temporarily suspended due to economic distress.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

When was NABARD established, and under which Five-Year Plan?

Explanation: NABARD was established on July 12, 1982, during the Sixth Five-Year Plan, acting as the apex refinancing institution for agricultural and rural development.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The Command Area Development Authority (CADA) was established in which year?

Explanation: CADA was established in 1974-75 during the Fifth Five-Year Plan to ensure optimum utilization of created irrigation potential through on-development works.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

When did NITI Aayog replace the Planning Commission?

Explanation: NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) was established on January 1, 2015, replacing the 65-year-old Planning Commission.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana (RKVY) was launched during which Five-Year Plan?

Explanation: RKVY was launched in 2007-08 during the Eleventh Five-Year Plan as an umbrella scheme to ensure holistic agricultural development.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What was the main economic objective of the Fifth Five-Year Plan (1974-1979)?

Explanation: The Fifth Five-Year Plan emphasized the 'Removal of Poverty' (Garibi Hatao) and attainment of self-reliance through targeted minimum needs programs.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Who chaired the committee that recommended the establishment of NABARD?

Explanation: NABARD was established upon the recommendation of the Committee to Review Arrangements for Institutional Credit for Agriculture and Rural Development (CRAFICARD) headed by B. Sivaraman.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

National Food Security Mission (NFSM) was launched in which academic/fiscal year?

Explanation: NFSM was launched in Rabi 2007-08 (Eleventh Plan) to enhance the production of rice, wheat, and pulses through area expansion and productivity enhancement.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which Five-Year Plan experienced the lowest agricultural growth rate due to severe droughts and wars?

Explanation: The Third Five-Year Plan (1961-1966) recorded severely negative or stagnant agricultural growth due to wars (1962, 1965) and consecutive droughts.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Integrated Rural Development Programme (IRDP) was launched nationwide in which year?

Explanation: IRDP was launched nationwide on October 2, 1980 (Sixth Five-Year Plan) to provide self-employment opportunities to rural families below the poverty line.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary role of NITI Aayog compared to the former Planning Commission?

Explanation: NITI Aayog acts as the premier policy think-tank of the Government of India, providing directional and policy inputs while fostering cooperative federalism without financial allocation powers.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which Five-Year Plan introduced the concept of 'Growth with Social Justice and Equity'?

Explanation: The Ninth Five-Year Plan (1997-2002) explicitly focused on 'Growth with Social Justice and Equity', emphasizing basic minimum services and regional balance.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The Green Revolution in India successfully introduced high-yielding varieties of which two crops initially?

Explanation: The Green Revolution initially centered around dwarf Mexican wheat (Triticum aestivum) and semi-dwarf rice (Oryza sativa) varieties developed by CIMMYT and IRRI.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which Five-Year Plan targeted an 8% GDP growth rate for the first time?

Explanation: The Tenth Five-Year Plan (2002-2007) targeted an 8% average GDP growth rate, aiming to improve quality of life and reduce poverty.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Who acts as the Chairperson of NITI Aayog?

Explanation: The Prime Minister of India acts as the ex-officio Chairperson of NITI Aayog, just as was the case with the Planning Commission.
