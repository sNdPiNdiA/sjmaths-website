Introduction to Tillage and Land Preparation Machinery

Tillage is the mechanical manipulation of soil to provide favorable conditions for seed germination, root development, and weed control. Land preparation machinery is broadly classified into primary tillage implements and secondary tillage implements.

  • Primary Tillage Implements: Designed to open and break hard soil crusts, invert the soil to bury crop residues, weeds, and organic manures. Examples include mouldboard ploughs, disc ploughs, chisel ploughs, and subsoilers.
  • Secondary Tillage Implements: Follow primary tillage to pulverize clods, level the seedbed, conserve moisture, and prepare a fine tilth for seeding. Examples include disc harrows, cultivators, rotavators, plankers, and rollers.

Primary Tillage Implements: Mouldboard and Disc Ploughs

Primary tillage machinery forms the foundation of deep soil conditioning. The selection of implement depends on soil texture, moisture status, and tractor drawbar power.

  • Mouldboard (MB) Plough: The most efficient implement for complete inversion of soil. Key components include the share, mouldboard, landside, frog, and shank. It is classified into animal-drawn and tractor-drawn (mounted, semi-mounted, or trailed) types.
  • Disc Plough: Consists of heavy steel concave discs (usually 500 to 700 mm diameter) set at an angle to the direction of travel. It is ideal for stony, root-infested, dry, and sticky soils where mouldboard ploughs fail to scour or penetrate.
  • Draft and Mechanics: Draft is the total horizontal component of pull exerted by a tractor or animal. Soil resistance, width of cut, depth of cut, and speed directly influence the draft requirements of ploughs.

Secondary Tillage Implements: Harrows, Cultivators, and Rotavators

Secondary tillage refines the soil surface, destroys emerging weed seedlings, and incorporates fertilizers prior to sowing.

  • Disc Harrow: Comprises a series of rotating concave discs mounted on a common shaft (gang). Single-action, double-action (tandem), and offset disc harrows are widely used. Tandem harrows ensure complete soil coverage by throwing soil in opposite directions.
  • Cultivator (Tine-type): Equipped with spring-loaded or rigid tines fitted with shovel or sweep points. It stirs the soil without complete inversion, making it indispensable for inter-cultivation and seedbed preparation.
  • Rotavator (Rotary Tiller): Powered directly by the tractor Power Take-Off (PTO) shaft. It combines primary and secondary tillage operations in a single pass by intensely pulverizing soil using L-shaped or C-shaped blades mounted on a rotating rotor.

Puddling and Wetland Preparation Equipment

Puddling is a specialized land preparation technique performed in standing water for wetland paddy (Oryza sativa) cultivation to destroy soil aggregation, reduce percolation losses, and facilitate easy transplanting.

  • Puddler: Traditional blade-type or cage-wheel puddlers churn saturated soils, creating an impermeable sub-surface pan.
  • Cage Wheel: Steel cleated wheels mounted on tractors replacing pneumatic tires for paddy field operations to prevent sinking and ensure high traction.
  • Rotary Puddler / Wetland Cultivator: High-speed rotor assemblies designed specifically for wet land tillage, producing a fine mud slurry required for rice seedling establishment.

Draft, Power, and Field Capacity Calculations in Tillage

Understanding machinery performance parameters is vital for agricultural engineering numerical questions in PGT examinations.

  • Theoretical Field Capacity (TFC): Calculated as TFC = S × W10, where S is speed in km/h and W is width of implement in meters (Result in hectares per hour, ha/h).
  • Effective Field Capacity (EFC): The actual rate of land coverage, accounting for field efficiencies (turnaround time, clogging, etc.): EFC = TFC × Field Efficiency (%)100.
  • Specific Draft: Force required per unit cross-sectional area of the furrow slice (kN/m^2).

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The mouldboard plough provides complete soil inversion and buries crop residues effectively.
  • Theoretical Field Capacity formula is TFC = S × W10 where S is speed in km/h and W is width in meters.
  • Disc ploughs are best suited for stony, root-infested, and hard dry soils where mouldboard ploughs fail to scour.
  • Puddling is essential in wetland paddy (Oryza sativa) cultivation to reduce deep percolation losses and suppress weeds.
  • A tandem disc harrow has two or more gangs where soil is thrown outward by front gangs and inward by rear gangs.
  • Rotavator operates directly via tractor PTO power, combining primary and secondary tillage in a single pass.
  • Specific draft of a plough is expressed in kiloNewtons per square meter (kN/m^2) of furrow cross-section.
  • Cage wheels are attached to tractors during wetland puddling to prevent sinkage and improve tractive efficiency.
  • Field efficiency of tillage machinery is generally between 70% to 85% under normal field conditions.
  • Subsoilers are heavy-duty primary tillage implements used specifically to break hardpans located deep in the soil profile.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing primary tillage with secondary tillage: Remember mouldboard and disc ploughs are primary, while harrows and cultivators are secondary.
  • Misinterpreting theoretical vs. effective field capacity: Effective capacity always accounts for field efficiency losses like turning and adjustments.
  • Assuming rotavators are animal-drawn: Rotavators require high torque from tractor PTO shafts and are strictly tractor-operated.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Land Preparation Machinery And Implements in Agricultural Engineering

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Classification of Tillage Operations

Tillage is divided into primary and secondary phases based on operational depth and timing.

  • Primary Tillage: Deep cutting and soil inversion (15-30 cm depth).
  • Secondary Tillage: Shallow surface refinement and weed control (<15 cm depth).
Concept 2

Mouldboard Plough Mechanics

The MB plough relies on share, mouldboard, and landside to cut and invert the furrow slice cleanly.

  • Suction: Vertical and horizontal suction ensures proper penetration and width control.
Concept 3

Disc Implement Dynamics

Disc harrows and ploughs utilize concave rotating steel discs to handle abrasive, stony, and dry soils.

  • Disc Angle: Angle of disc to the direction of travel determines penetration and soil throw.
Concept 4

Puddling Machinery

Wetland preparation requires specialized equipment to destroy capillary pores in rice fields.

  • Cage Wheel: Increases traction and churns saturated soil efficiently.
Concept 5

Field Capacity Calculations

Machinery performance metrics are evaluated using field capacity formulas.

  • TFC Formula: TFC = S × W10 (ha/h).

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Primary Tillage (Primary Tillage Operation)
Initial major soil working operation designed to open hard soils and invert furrow slices.
Secondary Tillage (Secondary Tillage Operation)
Subsequent shallow operations to pulverize clods, level land, and prepare fine seedbeds.
Draft (Draft Force)
Total horizontal resistance of an implement parallel to the line of motion.
Mouldboard Plough (Mouldboard Plough (MB Plough))
Primary tillage implement that cuts, lifts, and completely inverts the soil.
Disc Plough (Disc Plough)
Primary tillage implement utilizing concave rotating discs for hard, stony, or dry soils.
Disc Harrow (Disc Harrow)
Secondary tillage implement with gangs of concave discs for clod crushing and weed destruction.
Rotavator (Rotary Tiller / Rotavator)
PTO-driven tractor implement with rotating blades for intensive soil pulverization.
Puddling (Wetland Puddling)
Stirring of saturated soil in standing water for paddy cultivation to prevent water percolation.
Cage Wheel (Cage Wheel)
Open-structured iron wheel replacing tractor pneumatic tires during wetland puddling.
Field Capacity (Theoretical / Effective Field Capacity)
Rate of field area processed by agricultural machinery per unit time (ha/h).
Vertical Suction (Vertical Suction (Plough))
Downward pull of the plough share to ensure proper soil penetration depth.
Horizontal Suction (Horizontal Suction (Plough))
Inward pull of the plough share toward the unploughed land wall.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Rotavator operates via tractor PTO shaft power.
  • Tandem disc harrows throw soil outward with front gangs and inward with rear gangs.
  • Vertical and horizontal suctions are key adjustment features in animal-drawn mouldboard ploughs.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Tillage machinery is divided into primary (inversion/deep) and secondary (pulverization/shallow) implements.
  • Mouldboard ploughs give complete inversion; disc ploughs excel in stony and dry soils.
  • Field capacity formula: TFC = S × W10 (ha/h).
  • Puddling is critical for rice (Oryza sativa) to minimize percolation losses using cage wheels and puddlers.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Theoretical Field Capacity VS Effective Field Capacity

Theoretical assumes 100% time utilization without turns or losses; effective includes operational field efficiencies.

Primary Tillage VS Secondary Tillage

Primary tillage involves deep soil opening and inversion, while secondary tillage involves shallow seedbed refinement.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a primary tillage implement?

Explanation: Mouldboard plough is a primary tillage implement used for cutting, lifting, and completely inverting the soil. Disc harrows, cultivators, and plankers are secondary tillage implements.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the theoretical field capacity (TFC) of a cultivator having a working width of 2.0 meters operating at a speed of 5.0 km/h?

Explanation: TFC = (5.0 km/h * 2.0 m) / 10 = 10.0 / 10 = 1.0 hectare per hour (ha/h).

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which implement is best suited for sticky, stony, and dry unploughed soils where mouldboard ploughs fail to scour?

Explanation: Disc ploughs use rotating concave discs which prevent soil sticking (scouring issues) and easily roll over stones and roots where fixed mouldboard ploughs fail.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of puddling in wetland rice (Oryza sativa) cultivation?

Explanation: Puddling destroys soil macropores, creates a compacted sub-surface layer (hardpan) to reduce deep percolation of water, and submerges weed seeds.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which tractor component directly drives a rotavator?

Explanation: The rotavator is powered directly by the tractor Power Take-Off (PTO) shaft, which transfers engine rotation to the rotor blades.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In a tandem disc harrow, how do the front and rear gangs throw the soil?

Explanation: In a tandem disc harrow, front gangs throw soil outward, and rear gangs throw soil inward, ensuring complete coverage and a level seedbed without leaving ridges.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does 'vertical suction' in an animal-drawn mouldboard plough ensure?

Explanation: Vertical suction is the downward clearance of the plough share tip below the landside sole, ensuring the plough penetrates and maintains the desired working depth.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following implements is classified as a secondary tillage tool?

Explanation: Disc harrows are secondary tillage implements used for clod crushing, levelling, and smoothing the seedbed after primary ploughing.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why are cage wheels used in agricultural tractors during wetland operations?

Explanation: Cage wheels replace standard pneumatic rubber tires in flooded paddy fields to provide high traction and prevent deep sinkage of the tractor.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The total horizontal component of pull exerted by a tractor on a tillage implement is known as:

Explanation: Draft is defined as the total horizontal component of the force parallel to the line of motion required to pull an implement through the soil.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical field efficiency range for primary tillage operations like mouldboard ploughing?

Explanation: Field efficiency for primary tillage implements typically ranges from 70% to 85%, accounting for turning at headlands, adjustments, and cleaning.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which implement is specifically designed to shatter hardpans located deep in the soil profile without inverting the topsoil?

Explanation: A subsoiler is a heavy-duty primary tillage implement designed to break up hardpans and compacted subsoil layers at depths exceeding 40-60 cm without bringing sterile subsoil to the surface.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the function of the landside in a mouldboard plough?

Explanation: The landside slides along the furrow wall, absorbing side pressure exerted by the mouldboard and stabilizing the ploughing operation.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which tillage system involves minimum soil disturbance and retains at least 30% crop residue on the soil surface?

Explanation: Conservation tillage is any tillage and planting system that leaves at least 30% of the soil surface covered by plant residue after planting to reduce soil erosion and moisture loss.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does horizontal suction in a plough ensure?

Explanation: Horizontal suction (or heel clearance) ensures that the plough takes a uniform width of cut by pulling toward the unploughed land.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Calculate the effective field capacity of a disc harrow with an effective width of 3 meters operating at 4 km/h, given a field efficiency of 75%.

Explanation: TFC = (3 * 4) / 10 = 1.2 ha/h. Effective Field Capacity (EFC) = 1.2 * 0.75 = 0.9 * 0.75 = 0.9? Wait: 1.2 * 0.75 = 0.90 ha/h. Let's check correct index: 0.9 ha/h is option 0.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is an advantage of using a rotavator over conventional ploughing and harrowing?

Explanation: Rotavators intensely pulverize soil and combine primary and secondary tillage into a single pass, saving time and operational costs despite higher PTO power demand.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The angle at which the disc of a disc plough is set relative to the direction of travel is called:

Explanation: Disc angle is the angle that the plane of the cutting edge of the disc makes with the direction of travel, typically ranging from 42 to 45 degrees.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of a planker or roller used in secondary tillage?

Explanation: Plankers and rollers crush remaining clods, level the soil surface, and pack the soil slightly around seeds to ensure good seed-soil contact for germination.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which soil property significantly increases the draft requirement of primary tillage implements?

Explanation: High clay content, high soil compaction, and low moisture (dry hard soil) significantly increase soil shear strength and cohesion, thereby increasing the draft requirement.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

A tractor-drawn mouldboard plough has 3 bottoms, each cutting a width of 30 cm. If the operating speed is 6 km/h and field efficiency is 80%, what is the effective field capacity?

Solution & Rationale: Total width W = 3 * 0.3 m = 0.9 m. Speed S = 6 km/h. TFC = (6 * 0.9) / 10 = 0.54 ha/h. EFC = 0.54 * 0.80 = 0.432 ha/h.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following implement combinations represents a complete primary followed by secondary tillage sequence?

Solution & Rationale: Primary tillage is performed first by Mouldboard Plough for deep inversion, followed by secondary tillage using a Disc Harrow for seedbed pulverization.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

The angle of tilt in a disc plough refers to:

Solution & Rationale: Tilt angle is the angle that the plane of the cutting edge of the disc makes with the vertical. It assists in disc penetration in hard soils.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary reason for incorporating a breakaway mechanism in tractor cultivator tines?

Solution & Rationale: Spring-loaded cultivator tines feature a release mechanism allowing them to deflect backward over rocks or roots and automatically return to position, preventing structural damage.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

In wetland paddy preparation, why is puddling considered superior to dry ploughing alone?

Solution & Rationale: Puddling destroys soil structure and forms a compacted layer (hardpan) below the plow layer, drastically reducing percolation water losses and keeping nutrients available in the root zone.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

If the specific draft of a soil is 50 kN/m² and the furrow cross-sectional area is 0.08 m², what is the total draft of the implement?

Solution & Rationale: Total Draft = Specific Draft * Cross-sectional Area = 50 kN/m² * 0.08 m² = 4.0 kN.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which secondary tillage implement utilizes power directly from the tractor engine via PTO and does not rely solely on drawbar pull?

Solution & Rationale: The rotavator is driven by the tractor PTO shaft, using rotational mechanical power rather than drawbar pull to pulverize soil.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary role of scrapers in disc ploughs and disc harrows?

Solution & Rationale: Scrapers are mounted close to the concave surface of discs to continuously clean off adhering sticky soil during operation.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following factors decreases the draft of a mouldboard plough?

Solution & Rationale: As soil moisture increases up to the optimum plastic limit, soil cohesion and shear strength decrease, thereby lowering the draft required by ploughs.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

Offset disc harrows are particularly advantageous for:

Solution & Rationale: Offset disc harrows can be offset to either side of the tractor drawbar, enabling cultivation close to tree trunks in orchards without damaging low-hanging branches.
