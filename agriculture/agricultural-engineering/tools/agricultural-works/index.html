Introduction to Agricultural Works and Field Operations

Agricultural works encompass all mechanical, manual, and biological operations performed on the soil, crop canopy, and harvested produce to optimize crop establishment, growth, and yield. In the context of Indian agriculture and UPSESSB TGT/PGT syllabi, these operations are broadly categorized into primary tillage, secondary tillage, inter-culture operations, earth-working, and post-harvest management.

  • Definition: Agricultural works refer to the systematic application of energy—human, animal, mechanical, or electrical—to manipulate soil physical properties, manage weeds, and ensure precise seed-soil contact.
  • Objectives: To create a desirable tilth, eradicate weeds, incorporate crop residues and organic manures, ensure optimal soil aeration, and enhance moisture retention capacity.
  • Energy Requirements: Field operations are evaluated based on draft requirements, specific resistance of the soil (measured in ext{kN/m}^2), and power utilization efficiency of tractors and animal-drawn implements.

Classification of Tillage and Primary Agricultural Works

Tillage is the fundamental mechanical manipulation of soil. Primary agricultural works initiate the preparation of the seedbed by opening, cutting, shattering, and inverting the compacted soil mass.

  • Primary Tillage Implements: Moldboard plow (MB plow), disc plow, subsoiler, and chisel plow. The MB plow is designed for complete soil inversion, burying surface trash and weed seeds.
  • Depth of Operation: Primary tillage typically operates at a deeper depth ranging from 15 cm to 30 cm, whereas subsoilers penetrate deeper (45 cm to 60 cm) to break hard pans.
  • Draft and Power: Draft (D) is the horizontal component of pull parallel to the line of motion. It is calculated using the formula: D = S imes A, where S is specific soil resistance and A is the cross-sectional area of the furrow slice.

Secondary Agricultural Works and Seedbed Preparation

Secondary tillage operations follow primary tillage to pulverize clods, level the field, conserve moisture, and prepare a fine, firm seedbed conducive to uniform seed germination.

  • Secondary Implements: Cultivators, harrows (disc harrows, spike-tooth harrows, spring-tine harrows), rollers, plankers, and weeders.
  • Puddling: A specialized secondary tillage operation performed in standing water for wetland paddy (Oryza sativa) cultivation. It destroys soil aggregation, reduces percolation losses of water, and creates an impermeable layer.
  • Surface Levelling: Laser land levelers are modern implements that ensure high-precision micro-topography, improving irrigation water use efficiency by 25% to 30%.

Inter-culture, Earth-Working, and Weed Management Operations

Inter-cultivation involves shallow tillage operations conducted in the inter-row spaces of standing crops after seed emergence to control weeds, aerate the root zone, and conserve soil moisture through dust mulch formation.

  • Earth-Working Implements: Ridgers, bund formers, ditchers, and potato diggers are specialized implements designed to move soil and form ridges or furrows.
  • Critical Period of Weed Competition: Generally occurs during the first one-third to one-fourth of the crop growth cycle (e.g., 20 to 45 days after sowing in maize and soybean).
  • Mechanical vs Chemical: While hand hoeing and animal-drawn cultivators provide effective mechanical weed control, timely application of selective herbicides such as atrazine for maize or pendimethalin for soybean complements inter-culture operations.

Earth-Moving, Land Development, and Soil Conservation Works

Large-scale agricultural engineering works involve soil excavation, shaping, and terracing to prevent soil erosion and manage watershed hydrology.

  • Implements Used: Bulldozers, scrapers, graders, and buck scrapers.
  • Terracing and Bunding: Contour bunding is practiced in areas with rainfall less than 750 mm and slopes up to 6-10%, while graded bunds are constructed in high rainfall zones (>750 mm).
  • Drainage Works: Surface drainage (open ditches) and subsurface drainage (tile drains, perforated pipes) prevent waterlogging and soil salinity buildup, maintaining root zone aeration.

  • Moldboard plow provides complete inversion of soil, burying crop residues and weed seeds deep into the furrow.
  • Puddling is a specialized secondary tillage operation performed in standing water to reduce deep percolation losses in rice fields.
  • Subsoiler is used to break subsurface compaction and hard pans at depths ranging from 45 cm to 60 cm.
  • Laser land leveler improves irrigation water use efficiency by 25% to 30% through precise micro-topography management.
  • The critical period of weed competition in most field crops is the first 20 to 45 days after sowing.
  • Contour bunding is recommended in areas with annual rainfall less than 750 mm and land slopes up to 6-10%.
  • Disc harrow is classified as a secondary tillage implement that operates without complete soil inversion.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing primary tillage with secondary tillage: Remember that primary tillage involves deep soil opening and inversion, whereas secondary tillage pulverizes and levels.
  • Assuming puddling is done in dry soil: Puddling is strictly performed in saturated or flooded soil conditions for wetland paddy cultivation.
  • Treating subsoilers as standard cultivators: Subsoilers operate much deeper (up to 60 cm) specifically to shatter hardpans, requiring massive tractor drawbar power.

Concept 1

Mechanics of Tillage and Draft Force

Tillage mechanics evaluates forces acting on agricultural implements during soil cutting and displacement.

  • Draft (D): Total horizontal force parallel to the line of motion required to pull an implement, calculated as D = S imes A.
  • Specific Resistance: Force required per unit cross-sectional area of furrow slice, varying with soil texture (clay > loam > sandy).
Concept 2

Primary Tillage Implements and Soil Inversion

Primary tillage breaks the cohesive forces of soil masses to a depth of 15-30 cm.

  • Moldboard Plow: Consists of share, moldboard, landside, frog, and tailpiece; ensures complete inversion.
  • Disc Plow: Consists of concave steel discs inclined at an angle; highly effective in stony, sticky, or root-infested soils.
Concept 3

Secondary Tillage and Seedbed Refinement

Secondary tillage prepares a fine tilth and firm seedbed for planting.

  • Disc Harrow: Uses gangs of rotating discs to cut clods and incorporate stubble.
  • Cultivator: Equipped with shovel or sweep tines for shallow weed eradication and soil stirring.
Concept 4

Specialized Earth-Working and Water Management Works

Earth-working implements shape the soil surface for water management and crop geometry.

  • Ridger: Moves soil to form raised beds and furrows for row crops like sugarcane.
  • Bund Former: Compresses soil into embankments to impound water in basins.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Draft (Draft (D))
The horizontal component of pull parallel to the direction of travel required to operate an implement.
Specific Soil Resistance (Specific Resistance (S))
The resistance offered by the soil per unit cross-sectional area of the furrow slice, expressed in ext{kN/m}^2.
Puddling (Wet Tillage / Puddling)
The tillage of soil in standing water to destroy soil aggregates and reduce percolation losses for rice cultivation.
Moldboard Plow (Moldboard Plow)
A primary tillage implement equipped with a curved plate that cuts, lifts, and completely inverts the furrow slice.
Subsoiler (Subsoil Plow)
A heavy-duty chisel-type implement designed to fracture compacted subsoil layers without bringing them to the surface.
Inter-culture (Inter-cultivation)
Shallow tillage operations performed between crop rows during the growing season for weed control and aeration.
Dust Mulch (Soil Mulch)
A thin layer of dry soil created by surface harrowing to break capillary action and reduce soil moisture evaporation.
Contour Bunding (Contour Embankment)
Earth embankments constructed along contour lines in sloppy lands to conserve moisture and prevent soil erosion.
Laser Leveller (Laser-Guided Land Leveller)
A grading machine guided by a laser transmitter to create a perfectly flat or uniformly sloped field surface.
Furrow (Furrow Slice)
The trench opened by a plow or the space between adjacent ridges in a planted field.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Draft formula: D = S \times A, where S is specific resistance and A is cross-sectional area.
  • Subsoiler operating depth is 45-60 cm to break hard pans.
  • Contour bunding is suited for rainfall < 750 mm and slopes up to 6-10%.
  • Laser land levelers save irrigation water by 25% to 30%.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Agricultural works are categorized into primary tillage, secondary tillage, inter-culture, and earth-moving operations.
  • Primary tillage implements like moldboard and disc plows operate at depths of 15-30 cm with soil inversion.
  • Secondary tillage implements like harrows and cultivators refine the seedbed and destroy emerging weeds.
  • Puddling is unique to wetland rice culture, lowering percolation losses and easing seedling transplanting.
  • Earth-working implements like ridgers and bund formers shape land topography for efficient irrigation.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Moldboard Plow VS Disc Harrow

Moldboard plow inverts soil deeply as a primary tillage tool; Disc harrow operates shallower without full inversion as a secondary tillage tool.

Ridger VS Bund Former

Ridger creates alternating ridges and furrows for row crops; Bund former constructs small perimeter walls to hold standing water in plots.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following implements is classified strictly as a primary tillage implement?

Explanation: The moldboard plow is a primary tillage implement that cuts and completely inverts the furrow slice, burying trash. Disc harrows, cultivators, and spike-tooth harrows are secondary tillage implements.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of performing puddling in rice (Oryza sativa) fields?

Explanation: Puddling breaks down soil aggregates, creates a hard pan, and significantly reduces percolation losses of water and nutrients, maintaining standing water for paddy cultivation.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which implement is specifically designed to shatter hardpans in the subsoil without bringing infertile soil to the surface?

Explanation: A subsoiler is a heavy-duty tillage tool designed to break compacted subsoil layers (hardpans) at depths of 45-60 cm without inverting or bringing subsoil to the top.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

The horizontal component of pull parallel to the line of motion required to operate an agricultural implement is known as:

Explanation: Draft is defined as the horizontal pull required to pull an implement through the soil. It depends on soil texture, speed, and implement geometry.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Contour bunding is generally recommended for areas where annual rainfall is less than:

Explanation: Contour bunding is practiced in low to medium rainfall regions receiving less than 750 mm of annual rainfall on lands with slopes up to 6-10%.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which agricultural implement is best suited for constructing small embankments to impound irrigation water in flat fields?

Explanation: A bund former is designed to collect soil from both sides and press it into small bunds or dikes, dividing fields into irrigation plots.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of secondary tillage operations?

Explanation: Secondary tillage operations follow primary plowing to break clods, level the soil surface, eradicate weeds, and prepare a firm, fine seedbed for planting.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which soil physical property dictates the specific soil resistance (S) during tillage operations?

Explanation: Specific soil resistance depends directly on soil texture, moisture content, bulk density, and cohesion. Heavy clay soils offer much higher resistance than sandy loam soils.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In the formula for draft (D = S imes A), what does 'A' represent?

Explanation: In draft calculations, A represents the cross-sectional area of the furrow slice, calculated as width of cut multiplied by depth of cut.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary benefit of using a laser land leveler in crop production?

Explanation: Laser land levelers eliminate high and low spots in fields, ensuring uniform water spread, reducing irrigation time, and enhancing water use efficiency by 25% to 30%.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which implement creates V-shaped furrows and ridges simultaneously, commonly used in potato and sugarcane planting?

Explanation: A ridger features two moldboards facing away from each other, which throws soil to both sides to form ridges and furrows.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Inter-cultivation operations in standing row crops are primarily performed to achieve:

Explanation: Inter-culture operations destroy emerging weed seedlings, break soil crusts, and create a dust mulch that conserves soil moisture by breaking capillary pores.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is NOT a component of a standard moldboard plow?

Explanation: A gang bolt is an assembly rod that holds multiple discs together in disc harrows or disc plows. A moldboard plow consists of a share, moldboard, landside, frog, and standard.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Why is the first 20 to 45 days after sowing considered critical in crop management?

Explanation: The critical period of weed competition occurs during the early vegetative growth phase (typically 20-45 DAS), where uncontrolled weed growth severely reduces final crop yields.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What type of soil tillage is puddling categorized under?

Explanation: Puddling is a specialized form of secondary tillage performed in saturated or flooded soil conditions, primarily for rice cultivation.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which implement is categorized as a passive tillage implement because its working parts are not driven by the tractor PTO?

Explanation: A moldboard plow is a passive implement whose soil-working action relies entirely on the forward draft pull of the tractor. Rotavators and power harrows are active implements driven by the PTO.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of a planker or levelling board used after harrowing?

Explanation: A planker is a secondary tillage tool used to smooth the soil surface, crush large clods, compact light soils slightly, and ensure good seed-soil contact.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which factor has the greatest influence on the draft of a tillage implement?

Explanation: Soil texture, bulk density, moisture content, and implement speed are the primary determinants of soil resistance and overall draft requirement.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is zero tillage (no-till farming)?

Explanation: Zero tillage is a conservation agriculture practice where seeds are planted directly into soil with no prior primary or secondary tillage, relying on herbicides for weed control.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate the draft of an MB plow operating at a depth of 20 cm and width of 30 cm, given that the specific resistance of the soil is 50 \, ext{kN/m}^2.

Solution & Rationale: Width (W) = 0.3 m, Depth (D) = 0.2 m. Area (A) = 0.3 imes 0.2 = 0.06 \, ext{m}^2. Draft = Specific Resistance (S) imes A = 50 imes 0.06 = 3.0 \, ext{kN}.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following implements relies on rotating concave steel discs for primary soil cutting and inversion?

Solution & Rationale: A disc plow uses individually mounted concave steel discs that rotate as the plow moves forward, cutting and partially inverting the soil, making it ideal for hard or stony soils.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

In wetland rice cultivation, what is the specific mechanical objective of puddling regarding soil hydraulic properties?

Solution & Rationale: Puddling destroys macro-pores and soil aggregation, creating a dense, impermeable compacted layer (hard pan) that minimizes downward percolation of water.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

What distinguishes a chisel plow from a standard moldboard plow?

Solution & Rationale: A chisel plow uses narrow rigid tines to fracture compacted soil layers deeply without bringing subsoil to the surface, unlike the complete inversion action of a moldboard plow.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Why are secondary tillage operations performed after primary tillage?

Solution & Rationale: Secondary tillage breaks down large clods left by primary plowing, levels the field, kills emerging weeds, and creates the fine tilth necessary for optimal seed germination.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

Which soil conservation structure is most appropriate for rolling topography with slopes between 2% and 10% in high rainfall zones (>750 mm)?

Solution & Rationale: Graded bunds (or channel terracing) are used in high rainfall areas to safely conduct excess runoff water along a graded channel without causing soil erosion.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary function of the landside component in a moldboard plow?

Solution & Rationale: The landside is a flat plate that presses against the unplowed furrow wall, absorbing side pressure and stabilizing the plow during operation.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

How does inter-cultivation contribute to soil moisture conservation?

Solution & Rationale: Shallow inter-culture operations create a loose layer of soil (dust mulch) that interrupts soil capillaries, preventing moisture from rising and evaporating into the atmosphere.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following is an active tillage implement driven directly by the tractor Power Take-Off (PTO)?

Solution & Rationale: A rotavator utilizes rotating blades powered by the tractor's PTO shaft, actively cutting, mixing, and pulverizing the soil in a single pass.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary agronomic advantage of zero-tillage over conventional tillage systems?

Solution & Rationale: Zero tillage conserves soil moisture, prevents wind and water erosion, reduces tractor fuel and labor costs, and preserves soil organic matter.
