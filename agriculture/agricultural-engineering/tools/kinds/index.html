Classification and Kinds of Agricultural Implements

Agricultural implements and machinery are broadly classified based on their primary function in crop production, source of power required for operation, and the unit operations they perform in the field. Understanding these classifications is fundamental for agricultural engineering and crop production.

  • Primary Tillage Implements: Implements used for initial major soil working, cutting, and inversion, such as mouldboard ploughs, disc ploughs, chisel ploughs, and subsoilers.
  • Secondary Tillage Implements: Implements used for pulverizing, leveling, and firming the seedbed after primary tillage, including disc harrows, cultivators, rotavators, plankers, and rollers.
  • Sowing and Planting Equipment: Seed drills, seed-cum-fertilizer drills, planters, transplanting machinery, and dibblers designed for precise seed placement and depth control.
  • Inter-cultivation and Plant Protection Equipment: Weeders, hoes, knapsack sprayers, power sprayers, and dusters used for post-sowing crop management and pest control.
  • Harvesting and Threshing Machinery: Sickles, reapers, combine harvesters, threshers, and shellers designed for crop separation and grain recovery.

Power Sources in Agriculture

Agricultural operations rely on diverse power sources to drive implements of varying draft requirements and capacities.

  • Human Power: The fundamental source of energy for small-scale tools, hand hoes, sprayers, and manual weeders. Average human power output is approximately 0.075 kW (0.1 hp) continuously.
  • Animal Power: Draft animals such as bullocks, camels, horses, and donkeys are utilized for traditional ploughing, harrowing, and carting. A pair of bullocks typically develops about 0.75 kW (1.0 hp) of draft power.
  • Mechanical Power: Internal combustion engines fueled by diesel or gasoline, utilized in tractors, power tillers, and self-propelled harvesters, providing high torque and speed versatility.
  • Electrical Power: Stationary electric motors employed in irrigation pumping units, threshers, feed grinders, and grading sheds due to high operational efficiency and low maintenance.
  • Renewable Energy Sources: Solar photovoltaic pumps, wind mills, and biomass gasifiers gaining prominence in sustainable farm mechanization.

Tillage Implements: Mechanics and Operation

Tillage is the mechanical manipulation of soil to provide a favorable condition for seed germination and root growth. Implements are engineered to overcome soil resistance, shear strength, and friction.

  • Mouldboard Plough (MB Plough): Consists of a share, mouldboard, landside, frog, and tailpiece. It performs cutting, lifting, pulverizing, and complete inversion of the soil furrow slice.
  • Disc Plough: Features individually rotating concave steel discs tilted at an angle to the direction of motion. Highly suitable for stony, hard, sticky, and root-infested soils where MB ploughs fail to scour.
  • Cultivators and Tines: Equipped with rigid or spring-loaded tines to stir the soil without complete inversion, widely used for seedbed preparation and secondary tillage.
  • Rotavator (Rotary Tiller): Powered by the tractor's Power Take-Off (PTO) shaft, utilizing L-shaped blades to intensely chop, mix, and pulverize the soil in a single pass.

Sowing, Planting, and Fertilization Machinery

Precise seed and fertilizer placement ensures optimal plant population and nutrient use efficiency.

  • Seed Drill Mechanics: Comprises a seed box, metering mechanism (fluted roller, cup-feed, or internal double-run), delivery tubes, and furrow openers (shoe type, disc type, or hoe type).
  • Seed-Cum-Fertilizer Drills: Deliver seeds and granular chemical fertilizers simultaneously into separate bands at targeted depths below and to the side of the seed.
  • Planters: Utilize precise plate-type or pneumatic metering mechanisms to drop single seeds at exact intra-row spacings, crucial for wide-row crops like maize, cotton, and soybean.
  • Transplanters: Semi-automatic or automatic mechanical devices that plant vegetable seedlings or pre-germinated paddy mat-type seedlings into puddled fields.

Plant Protection and Harvesting Equipment

Safeguarding crops from biotic stresses and gathering produce efficiently requires specialized machinery.

  • Sprayers and Dusters: Convert liquid agrochemicals into fine droplets or dust particles. Key types include pneumatic knapsack sprayers, hydraulic power sprayers, and ultra-low volume (ULV) sprayers.
  • Reapers and Mowers: Mechanical devices equipped with reciprocating knife cutter bars or rotating discs to harvest cereal crops, forage, and grasses.
  • Combine Harvester: A multi-functional machine that performs harvesting, threshing, separating, and cleaning of grains in a single continuous forward pass across the field.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The power of a standard pair of bullocks is approximately 0.75 kW (1.0 hp).
  • Mouldboard plough performs four distinct actions: cutting, lifting, pulverizing, and inversion.
  • Disc plough is preferred in sticky, stony, and hard dry soils where mouldboard plough fails to scour.
  • Fluted roller is the most common seed metering mechanism used in standard Indian seed drills.
  • Rotavator is driven directly by the tractor Power Take-Off (PTO) shaft, running at standard speeds of 540 rpm or 1000 rpm.
  • Combine harvester performs four simultaneous operations: harvesting, threshing, separating, and cleaning.
  • Subsoiler is a heavy-duty primary tillage implement designed to break hardpans beneath the plough layer at depths exceeding 45-60 cm.
  • The angle at which the disc of a disc plough is set relative to the direction of travel is called the disc angle (usually 42° to 45°).
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing primary tillage with secondary tillage: Remember that primary tillage involves deep soil cutting and inversion, whereas secondary tillage involves seedbed smoothing, leveling, and pulverization.
  • Assuming rotavators are pulled by draft alone: Rotavators are active implements driven by the tractor PTO shaft, not passive implements like disc harrows.
  • Mixing up seed drill and planter metering mechanisms: Seed drills use fluted rollers for continuous sowing, whereas planters use cell feed plates or pneumatic discs for hill/precision dropping.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Classification, Kinds and Mechanics of Agricultural Implements and Machinery

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Mechanics of Tillage Implements

Tillage implements overcome soil mechanical impedance by applying cutting, shearing, and bending forces.

  • Draft: Total horizontal force required to pull an implement through the soil, measured in kiloNewtons (kN).
  • Specific Resistance: Draft per unit cross-sectional area of the furrow slice, varying with soil texture and moisture content.
Concept 2

Seed Metering Mechanisms

Accurate seed distribution depends on the internal mechanism of the seed hopper.

  • Fluted Roller: Adjustable sliding cylinder with external grooves for volumetric seed delivery in cereal drills.
  • Pneumatic Metering: Uses air suction pressure against a rotating disc with calibrated holes to pick up and drop individual seeds precisely.
Concept 3

Tractor Power Transmission

Tractors deliver engine power through multiple systems to accomplish field tasks.

  • PTO Shaft: Transfers rotational engine power directly to stationary or mobile active implements like rotavators and threshers.
  • Drawbar Power: The effective pulling power delivered at the tractor drawbar to haul trailers and passive tillage implements.
Concept 4

Harvesting and Threshing Efficiency

Modern harvesting machinery minimizes grain losses and maintains high throughput capacities.

  • Threshing Cylinder: Spike-tooth, rasp-bar, or wire-loop drums that detach grains from panicles or cobs through impact and friction.
  • Concave Clearance: Adjustable gap between the threshing cylinder and concave grate that prevents grain damage and unthreshed head loss.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Draft (Draft Force (kN))
The horizontal component of pull parallel to the line of motion required to operate an implement.
Scouring (Soil Shedding Action)
The natural self-cleaning ability of a mouldboard or disc surface to shed sticky soil during ploughing.
PTO (Power Take-Off)
A splined rotating shaft on a tractor that transmits engine power to attached implements.
Disc Angle (Angle of Disc to Direction of Motion)
The angle between the plane of the cutting edge of a disc plough and the direction of travel, typically 42-45 degrees.
Tilt Angle (Disc Vertical Tilt)
The angle between the vertical plane and the face of the disc blade, aiding soil penetration in hard soils.
Fluted Roller (Volumetric Seed Metering Element)
A grooved cylindrical roller that meters seeds continuously based on exposed effective length.
Subsoiling (Deep Tillage (>45 cm))
Breaking compacted subsoil layers or hardpans without bringing inert subsoil to the surface.
Field Capacity (Effective Area Coverage (ha/hr))
The actual rate of land area covered by an implement per unit of time under field operating conditions.
Hitch System (Three-Point Linkage)
Standardized mechanical linkage connecting mounted implements to a tractor via two lower links and one top link.
Threshing (Grain Detachment)
The mechanical separation of cereal grains from chaff, ears, or pods.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Rotavator is a PTO-driven active secondary tillage implement.
  • Disc plough disc angle is normally maintained between 42° and 45°.
  • Fluted roller is standard for cereal seed drilling.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Agricultural implements are classified into primary tillage, secondary tillage, sowing, plant protection, and harvesting machinery.
  • Mouldboard ploughs provide complete soil inversion, whereas disc ploughs operate effectively in stony and sticky soils.
  • Tractors supply power via drawbar pull, hydraulic lift, and PTO shaft rotation.
  • Seed drills utilize fluted roller mechanisms, whereas precision planters use cell plates or pneumatic suction.
  • Combine harvesters integrate cutting, threshing, separating, and cleaning into a single operation.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Active Implement VS Passive Implement

Active implements (e.g., rotavators) receive direct power from the tractor PTO shaft, while passive implements (e.g., harrows, cultivators) rely entirely on drawbar pull.

Primary Tillage VS Secondary Tillage

Primary tillage involves deep soil cutting and inversion (ploughs), while secondary tillage follows to pulverize and level the seedbed (harrows, cultivators).

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a primary tillage implement?

Explanation: Mouldboard (MB) ploughs are primary tillage implements used for deep cutting and inversion of the soil furrow slice. Disc harrows, cultivators, and rotavators are secondary tillage implements.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the approximate power output of a standard pair of bullocks in India?

Explanation: A standard pair of medium-heavy draft bullocks develops approximately 0.75 kW (1.0 horsepower) of draft power under normal working conditions.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which implement is specifically recommended for ploughing hard, dry, sticky, and stony soils where standard mouldboard ploughs fail to scour?

Explanation: Disc ploughs are designed with rotating concave steel discs that handle hard, dry, sticky, and stony soils effectively, providing self-cleaning rolling action (scouring) where sliding mouldboards fail.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The seed metering mechanism most commonly found in conventional cereal seed drills is:

Explanation: The fluted roller mechanism is widely used in Indian seed drills for continuous volumetric metering of wheat, barley, and paddy seeds.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard tractor PTO shaft rotation speed specified by international and Indian standards?

Explanation: Standard tractor Power Take-Off (PTO) speeds are 540 rpm (and 1000 rpm for higher horsepower tractors) to ensure compatibility with active implements like rotavators and threshers.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of a subsoiler?

Explanation: Subsoilers are heavy-duty primary tillage implements designed to shatter hardpans, plough pans, and compacted subsoil layers without bringing inert soil to the surface.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following describes an 'active' agricultural implement?

Explanation: Active implements (such as rotavators and power tillers) receive direct mechanical power from the tractor engine via the PTO shaft, unlike passive implements which rely solely on drawbar pull.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The disc angle in a standard disc plough is typically maintained between:

Explanation: The disc angle of a disc plough is normally set between 42° and 45° relative to the direction of travel to ensure optimum soil penetration and inversion.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which component of a mouldboard plough actually cuts the soil horizontally beneath the furrow slice?

Explanation: The share is the cutting edge of the mouldboard plough bottom that penetrates the soil and makes the horizontal cut at the bottom of the furrow.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary advantage of a planter over a standard seed drill?

Explanation: Planters use specialized metering devices (plates or pneumatic discs) to drop single seeds at precise intervals, ensuring exact plant population and uniform spacing.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which machine combines the four operations of harvesting, threshing, separating, and cleaning into one continuous process?

Explanation: A combine harvester is a complex self-propelled machine that performs cutting, threshing, straw-grain separation, and grain cleaning simultaneously.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In a seed drill, what is the purpose of the furrow opener?

Explanation: Furrow openers (hoe, shoe, or disc type) cut open a narrow trench at the desired depth into which seeds are deposited from the metering unit.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary action of a disc harrow used in secondary tillage?

Explanation: Disc harrows chop crop residues, shatter clods, and mix fertilizers into the top layer of soil during secondary tillage without deep inversion.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Draft of a tillage implement is measured in which units?

Explanation: Draft is a force required to pull an implement through soil, expressed in kiloNewtons (kN) or kilograms-force (kgf).

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which tillage system involves leaving crop residues on the soil surface and minimizing soil disturbance?

Explanation: Conservation and zero tillage systems minimize mechanical soil manipulation and retain surface crop residues to reduce erosion and conserve moisture.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The three-point linkage system for mounting implements on tractors was standardized primarily by:

Explanation: Irish engineer Harry Ferguson patented and popularized the three-point hitch linkage system, which integrates tractor and mounted implement into a single functional unit.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a stationary machine used exclusively for separating grains from harvested crop heads?

Explanation: A thresher is a stationary machine powered by a tractor PTO or electric motor that beats or rubs harvested crop ears/panicles to detach grains.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does 'field capacity' of an agricultural machine refer to?

Explanation: Field capacity is the rate at which a machine accomplishes its work, typically expressed in hectares per hour (effective or theoretical field capacity).

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which type of seed drill mechanism uses cups attached to a rotating ring to scoop and drop seeds?

Explanation: The cup-feed mechanism uses circular cups mounted on a rotating ring that lift and drop seeds gently, minimizing mechanical damage to delicate seeds.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main purpose of packing or planking after sowing?

Explanation: Planking or packing firms the soil over sown seeds, eliminating air pockets and promoting capillary moisture movement toward the seed for rapid germination.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

In agricultural machinery management, effective field capacity is always less than theoretical field capacity primarily due to:

Solution & Rationale: Effective field capacity accounts for downtime such as headland turns, filling seed/fertilizer boxes, cleaning clogged tines, and field overlap, making it lower than theoretical capacity.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following implements is best suited for puddling in wet paddy cultivation?

Solution & Rationale: Wetland puddlers and cage wheels are specially designed to churn saturated soils, destroy soil aggregation, reduce percolation losses, and prepare a soft mud bed for paddy transplanting.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary function of the landside in a mouldboard plough?

Solution & Rationale: The landside is a flat plate that slides against the undisturbed furrow wall, counteracting lateral side pressures generated by soil inversion.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Why are pneumatic planters preferred over mechanical plate planters for high-speed precision sowing of maize and soybean?

Solution & Rationale: Pneumatic planters use positive or negative air pressure against calibrated holes on a rotating disc, ensuring accurate single-seed singulation even at higher forward operating speeds.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate the theoretical field capacity (in ha/hr) of a cultivator with a working width of 2.0 meters operating at a speed of 5.0 km/h.

Solution & Rationale: Theoretical Field Capacity (TFC) = (Width in meters × Speed in km/h) / 10 = (2.0 × 5.0) / 10 = 10 / 10 = 1.0 ha/hr.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Which tillage practice leaves rough, cloddy soil surfaces over winter to weather and break down natural soil aggregates?

Solution & Rationale: Fall or rough tillage exposes deep soil clods to freezing, thawing, and wetting cycles, improving physical structure for spring planting.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the specific purpose of the concave grate in a threshing cylinder?

Solution & Rationale: The concave is a curved wire-mesh grate fitted beneath the threshing drum; grains detached by impact pass through its openings while straw is discharged backward.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

Which hitch point on a three-point linkage carries the primary draft load of mounted implements?

Solution & Rationale: The two lower links of the three-point linkage transmit the main draft pull from the tractor to the implement, while the top link provides stability and depth control.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

In a disc plough, increasing the tilt angle improves:

Solution & Rationale: The tilt angle tilts the disc blade slightly backward from vertical, enhancing aggressive soil penetration in hard, dry, and compacted soil conditions.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following implements is classified as a passive secondary tillage tool?

Solution & Rationale: Spring-tine cultivators are passive implements relying on drawbar pull, whereas rotavators, power harrows, and puddling rotors are active PTO-driven implements.
