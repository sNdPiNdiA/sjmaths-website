Machinery and Implements in Agricultural Engineering

Classification and Principles of Tillage Implements

Tillage is the mechanical manipulation of soil to provide a favorable condition for seed germination and plant growth. Tillage implements are broadly classified into primary and secondary tillage implements based on the depth and intensity of soil inversion and pulverization.

  • Primary Tillage Implements: Designed for deeper soil penetration, cutting, and inversion. Examples include mouldboard ploughs, disc ploughs, and subsoilers.
  • Secondary Tillage Implements: Used after primary tillage to refine the seedbed, level the soil, destroy weeds, and conserve moisture. Examples include cultivators, harrows (disc, tooth, spring-tine), plankers, and rollers.
  • Mouldboard Plough (MB Plough): The most important primary tillage implement that performs four main functions: cutting, lifting, shattering, and inversion of the furrow slice.
  • Disc Plough: Consists of heavy steel concave discs that rotate under the action of soil resistance, highly suitable for stony, sticky, or dry hard soils where mouldboard ploughs fail to scour.

Sowing, Planting, and Fertilizer Application Machinery

Precision in seed placement and fertilizer application directly impacts crop stand, nutrient-use efficiency, and ultimate grain yield. Modern agricultural engineering provides specialized planting equipment for different cropping systems.

  • Seed Drill: An implement used for placing seeds in a continuous stream in furrows at a uniform depth and seed rate, followed by soil covering and compaction.
  • Seed-cum-Fertilizer Drill: Equipped with dual boxes allowing simultaneous placement of seeds and granular fertilizers (such as diammonium phosphate) at appropriate depths below or beside the seed line, preventing fertilizer seedling burn.
  • Planter: Precision planting machine used for large-seeded crops (e.g., Zea mays, Glycine max) to drop single seeds at pre-determined exact hill spacings within rows.
  • Transplanters: Mechanical devices or tractor-mounted units used for transplanting nursery-grown seedlings, commonly utilized in puddled lowland rice (Oryza sativa) cultivation.

Plant Protection Equipment and Spraying Mechanics

Plant protection machinery is vital for the timely and uniform application of chemical insecticides, fungicides, and herbicides to manage biotic stresses efficiently and minimize environmental drift.

  • Knapsack Sprayer: A manually operated or battery-powered sprayer carried on the operator's back, generating hydraulic pressure through a hand pump or electric diaphragm pump.
  • Power Sprayer: Engine-operated high-pressure sprayer capable of delivering large volumes of wash per unit time, suitable for orchards and extensive field crops.
  • Dusting Equipment: Dusters distribute dry pesticide formulations using an air blast generated by a fan or blower mechanism.
  • Nozzle Types: Hollow cone nozzles are preferred for insecticides and fungicides (fine droplet coverage), while flat-fan nozzles are standard for systemic and contact herbicide application.

Harvesting and Threshing Machinery

Timely harvesting minimizes pre-harvest shattering losses and weather damage. Harvesting and threshing technology ranges from simple manual sickles to sophisticated self-propelled combine harvesters.

  • Reapers: Mechanical machines designed to cut standing cereal crops and lay them in a windrow for easy manual or mechanical collection.
  • Threshers: Stationary machines driven by electric motors or tractor power take-off (PTO) shafts to separate grains from panicles or pods through impact, rubbing, and shearing action.
  • Combine Harvester: A multi-functional self-propelled machine that performs reaping, threshing, cleaning, and grain separation simultaneously in a single pass through the field.
  • Cylinder Types in Threshers: Spike-tooth, rasp-bar, and hammer-mill types; rasp-bar cylinders are most common for wheat and paddy due to minimal grain breakage.

Tractor Systems and Power Transmission in Agriculture

The agricultural tractor is the primary mobile power source on modern mechanized farms, providing drawbar pull, power take-off (PTO) drive, and hydraulic lift capacity.

  • Power Take-Off (PTO): A rotating shaft spline at the rear of the tractor that transmits mechanical engine power directly to stationary or trailing implements like threshers, sprayers, and rotary tillers. Standard PTO speeds are 540 rpm and 1000 rpm.
  • Drawbar Power: The horizontal pulling force exerted by the tractor to haul tillage implements and trailers across agricultural fields.
  • Hydraulic System: Uses fluid power under pressure to lift, lower, and precisely position three-point hitch mounted implements (Category I, II, and III).
  • Engine Classification: Internal combustion engines operating on diesel fuel operating on four-stroke cycle principles are universally standard in agricultural tractors due to high torque output and fuel economy.

DPH - Drawbar, PTO, Hydraulics

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The standard speed of a tractor Power Take-Off (PTO) shaft for Indian agricultural machinery is 540 rpm.
  • Mouldboard plough performs four distinct functions: cutting, lifting, shattering, and inversion of the soil furrow slice.
  • Seed-cum-fertilizer drills place fertilizer slightly below and to the side of the seed to prevent seedling root burn.
  • Rasp-bar type threshing cylinders are most widely used in cereal threshers to minimize grain breakage.
  • Hollow cone nozzles are standard for applying insecticides and fungicides, whereas flat-fan nozzles are used for herbicides.
  • Subsoilers are specialized deep primary tillage implements used specifically for breaking hardpans located in subsoil layers.
  • A combine harvester performs four operations simultaneously: harvesting, threshing, cleaning, and separating.
  • The draft of a mouldboard plough is directly proportional to the depth of ploughing and the specific resistance of the soil.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing primary tillage with secondary tillage: Remember that primary tillage involves deep soil inversion (e.g., MB plough), while secondary tillage involves seedbed refinement (e.g., cultivator).
  • Assuming seed drills drop individual seeds at fixed spacing like planters; seed drills drop seeds in a continuous stream.
  • Confusing hollow-fan nozzles with flat-fan nozzles for herbicide application; flat-fan nozzles ensure uniform strip coverage for weedicides.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Machinery and Implements in Agricultural Engineering

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Tillage and Implement Classification

Tillage implements form the mechanical foundation of seedbed preparation.

  • Primary Tillage: Deep cutting and inversion of soil using mouldboard and disc ploughs.
  • Secondary Tillage: Seedbed finishing, weed eradication, and moisture conservation using harrows and cultivators.
Concept 2

Sowing and Metering Mechanisms

Accurate seed and fertilizer distribution ensures optimal plant population density.

  • Fluted Roller: Standard metering device used in seed drills for cereal crops.
  • Plate Mechanism: Used in planters for precise single-seed dropping in row crops.
Concept 3

Tractor Power Outlets

Tractors deliver power through three primary pathways to perform farm operations.

  • PTO Shaft: Delivers rotary power at standard 540 rpm.
  • Drawbar: Provides pull force for trailing implements.
Concept 4

Harvesting Technology

Advanced machinery reduces labor bottlenecks during peak harvesting periods.

  • Combine Harvester: Integrates cutting, threshing, and cleaning into one continuous process.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Mouldboard Plough (Primary Tillage Implement)
An implement designed to cut, lift, invert, and shatter soil slices.
Subsoiler (Deep Tillage Implement)
A heavy-duty primary tillage tool used to fracture compacted hardpans below the normal plough layer.
Power Take-Off (PTO) (Tractor Power Shaft)
A splined rotating shaft on a tractor that transmits mechanical power to driven implements.
Seed Drill (Sowing Implement)
A machine that opens furrows, drops seeds at uniform depths, and covers them with soil.
Planter (Precision Sowing Machine)
A machine that places single seeds at precise intervals within rows for large-seeded crops.
Disc Harrow (Secondary Tillage Implement)
A secondary tillage implement consisting of steel discs arranged in gangs to chop stubble and pulverize clods.
Draft (Tractor Pull Resistance)
The total horizontal pulling force required to pull an implement through the soil.
Combine Harvester (Multi-Crop Harvester)
A self-propelled machine that harvests, threshes, cleans, and separates grain in one operation.
Hollow Cone Nozzle (Sprayer Component)
A hydraulic nozzle that produces a ring-shaped spray pattern ideal for insecticide and fungicide application.
Flat Fan Nozzle (Herbicide Spray Nozzle)
A nozzle that produces a fan-shaped spray pattern designed for uniform herbicide application.
Scraper (Plough Attachment)
A device fitted on a mouldboard plough or disc plough to remove adhering soil from the disc or mouldboard.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Standard tractor PTO speed is 540 rpm.
  • Mouldboard plough performs four functions: cutting, lifting, shattering, and inversion.
  • Rasp-bar cylinders minimize grain breakage during threshing.
  • Flat-fan nozzles are used for herbicide application.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Primary tillage implements perform deep soil inversion, while secondary tillage implements prepare the seedbed.
  • Tractors transmit power via Drawbar, PTO, and hydraulic systems.
  • Seed drills use fluted rollers for continuous seeding, whereas planters use seed plates for precise hill dropping.
  • Combine harvesters integrate cutting, threshing, and cleaning into a single pass.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Seed Drill VS Planter

Seed drills drop seeds in a continuous stream for small grains, whereas planters drop individual seeds at precise intra-row intervals.

Primary Tillage VS Secondary Tillage

Primary tillage involves deep soil inversion and breaking hard layers, whereas secondary tillage focuses on surface leveling and pulverization.

Which implement is specifically recommended for breaking hardpans located in subsoil layers?

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which metering mechanism is commonly used in seed drills for cereal crops like wheat?

Explanation: The fluted roller mechanism is widely utilized in seed drills for metering cereal seeds continuously into the delivery tubes.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What type of spray nozzle is most appropriate for applying systemic and contact herbicides?

Explanation: Flat fan nozzles produce a uniform fan-shaped spray pattern, making them the standard choice for herbicide application.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which operation is NOT performed by a combine harvester?

Explanation: Ploughing is a primary tillage operation performed before planting, whereas a combine harvester performs reaping, threshing, and cleaning.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of a mouldboard plough?

Explanation: The mouldboard plough cuts, lifts, shatters, and completely inverts the soil furrow slice.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which threshing cylinder type is most commonly used for wheat and paddy to minimize grain damage?

Explanation: Rasp-bar cylinders use a rubbing action that effectively threshes grains while minimizing mechanical breakage.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which implement is classified as a secondary tillage implement?

Explanation: Disc harrows are secondary tillage implements used for clod crushing, stubble incorporation, and seedbed preparation.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of a seed-cum-fertilizer drill?

Explanation: A seed-cum-fertilizer drill places both seeds and granular fertilizers at desired depths simultaneously in a single pass.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which component is attached to a mouldboard plough to remove adhering sticky soil?

Explanation: A scraper prevents soil from sticking to the mouldboard or disc, ensuring consistent scouring.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does the term 'draft' refer to in agricultural engineering?

Explanation: Draft is the horizontal component of the pull parallel to the line of motion required to pull an implement.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which machine is best suited for precise single-seed planting of maize or soybean?

Explanation: Precision planters regulate exact seed spacing and depth for large-seeded row crops.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main advantage of disc ploughs over mouldboard ploughs?

Explanation: Disc ploughs are capable of rotating and rolling over stones and roots, making them ideal for difficult soils where MB ploughs fail to scour.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which nozzle type is recommended for insecticide and fungicide application to achieve fine droplet coverage?

Explanation: Hollow cone nozzles create fine droplets and circular spray patterns ideal for canopy penetration in pest and disease control.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the function of the landside in a mouldboard plough?

Explanation: The landside slides against the furrow wall to counteract lateral side pressures exerted on the mouldboard plough.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which category of tractor three-point hitch is standard for medium-hp agricultural tractors in India?

Explanation: Category II three-point linkage is standard for medium-sized agricultural tractors operating in India.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of harrowing after ploughing?

Explanation: Harrowing pulverizes clods, levels the soil surface, destroys emerging weeds, and conserves soil moisture before sowing.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In a four-stroke tractor diesel engine, when is power generated?

Explanation: Power is generated exclusively during the power (expansion) stroke when expanding combustion gases force the piston downward.

Test Question 1 of 10 TGT Standard

Calculate or determine the theoretical field capacity of a cultivator with a working width of 2.0 meters operating at a speed of 5.0 km/h, assuming 100% field efficiency.

Solution & Rationale: Theoretical Field Capacity (TFC) = (Width in meters * Speed in km/h) / 10 = (2.0 * 5.0) / 10 = 1.0 hectare per hour.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following tillage implements inverts the soil completely and cuts the furrow slice vertically and horizontally?

Solution & Rationale: The mouldboard plough is specifically designed to cut the furrow slice vertically with the coulter and horizontally with the share, then invert it completely.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary function of the differential unit in an agricultural tractor?

Solution & Rationale: The differential allows drive wheels to rotate at different speeds during turns, preventing wheel scuffing and transmission wind-up.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Which metering mechanism is characteristic of precision corn and cotton planters?

Solution & Rationale: Precision planters use seed plates with specific cell sizes to select and drop single seeds at precise intervals.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Why is a subsoiler classified as a primary tillage implement rather than a secondary tillage implement?

Solution & Rationale: Subsoilers operate at deep soil levels to fracture dense subsoil layers, categorizing them as deep primary tillage implements.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary reason for using a seed-cum-fertilizer drill instead of broadcasting fertilizer separately?

Solution & Rationale: Placement of fertilizer close to the seed band increases nutrient use efficiency and prevents fixation or weed stimulation.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Which tractor parameter defines its ability to pull trailing implements across an agricultural field?

Solution & Rationale: Drawbar power is the measure of the tractor's pulling capacity available at the drawbar hitch point.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the function of the scouring action in a disc plough?

Solution & Rationale: Scouring is the self-cleaning action where soil slides off the polished face of the disc or mouldboard during ploughing.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

In spray application technology, what does a smaller droplet size (VMD) primarily affect?

Solution & Rationale: Smaller droplets provide superior coverage and canopy penetration but are highly susceptible to wind drift.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following combine components is primarily responsible for separating grain from straw through centrifugal force and agitation?

Solution & Rationale: Straw walkers shake and separate remaining grains from the bulk straw discharged from the threshing cylinder.
