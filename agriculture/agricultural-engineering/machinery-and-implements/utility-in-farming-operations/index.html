Classification and Principles of Agricultural Implements

Agricultural implements are broadly classified based on their source of power (manual, animal-drawn, and tractor-drawn) and the specific agricultural operations they perform. Understanding the mechanics, draft requirements, and specific utility of each implement is vital for efficient farm power management.

  • Primary Tillage Implements: Designed for deep soil manipulation, inversion, and breaking hardpans. Examples include mouldboard ploughs, disc ploughs, and subsoilers.
  • Secondary Tillage Implements: Used for seedbed preparation, clod crushing, leveling, and weed eradication. Examples include cultivators, harrows (disc, spike-tooth, spring-tooth), and plankers.
  • Power Source Optimization: Matching the implement size and draft requirement with the tractor or animal power prevents engine overload and optimizes fuel consumption.

Tillage Operations and Soil Manipulation Machinery

Tillage is the mechanical manipulation of soil to provide a favorable condition for seed germination, root growth, and weed control. Tillage machinery is categorized into primary and secondary units based on the depth of cut and soil inversion extent.

  • Mouldboard Plough (MB Plough): The most efficient primary tillage tool for complete inversion of soil, burying crop residues, and exposing weed seeds to solar heat. Consists of a share, mouldboard, landside, frog, and tailpiece.
  • Disc Plough: Utilized in stony, sticky, or dry hard soils where mouldboard ploughs fail to penetrate. It rolls over obstacles rather than sliding against them.
  • Cultivators and Harrows: Rigid or spring-loaded tines that stir the soil without complete inversion, preserving soil moisture and preparing a fine seedbed.

Sowing, Planting, and Fertilization Equipment

Precise placement of seeds and fertilizers at optimal depth and spacing ensures uniform crop stand, efficient resource utilization, and maximum yield potential across cereal, pulse, and oilseed crops.

  • Seed Drills and Seed-Cum-Fertilizer Drills: Mechanically meter seeds and deposit them in furrows at predetermined depths and rates, simultaneously placing fertilizers below or to the side of the seed zone to prevent fertilizer burn.
  • Planters: Precision machines used for spaced-row crops like maize, cotton, and soybean, utilizing cell-feed mechanisms or vacuum plates for single-seed delivery.
  • Puddled Field Seeders and Transplanters: Specialized machinery such as drum seeders for direct-seeded rice in puddled fields and mechanical paddy transplanters to maintain optimal plant population density.

Intercultural and Plant Protection Machinery

Intercultural operations involve post-sowing soil management, weed control, and earth-up operations, while plant protection equipment ensures timely delivery of agrochemicals to control insect pests, fungal pathogens, and weeds.

  • Cultivators and Hoes: Animal-drawn or tractor-mounted sweeps and duckfoot sweeps used between crop rows for weed eradication and soil aeration without damaging crop root systems.
  • Sprayers: Hydraulic, knapsack, power, and ultra-low volume (ULV) sprayers atomize liquid agrochemicals into droplets for uniform foliar deposition. Proper nozzle selection (hollow cone, flat fan, flood jet) dictates spray quality and drift reduction.
  • Dusters: Apply insecticides and fungicides in dry powder formulation using a fan-driven air blast.

Harvesting, Threshing, and Post-Harvest Processing Equipment

Timely harvesting and efficient threshing minimize grain shattering losses and mechanical damage, preserving grain quality and safe moisture thresholds for storage.

  • Reapers and Mowers: Cut and bundle cereal crops or forage grasses mechanically, reducing labor dependency during peak harvest windows.
  • Combines (Combine Harvesters): Self-propelled or tractor-drawn multi-operation machines that simultaneously reap, thresh, separate grain from straw, and clean the produce in a single pass.
  • Threshers: Spike-tooth, rasp-bar, and axial-flow threshers designed to separate grains from panicles or pods at adjustable cylinder speeds to prevent grain cracking.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The mouldboard plough is the principal implement used for complete soil inversion and burial of organic trash.
  • A disc plough is best suited for stony, sticky, and dry hard soils where mouldboard ploughs cannot penetrate effectively.
  • Seed-cum-fertilizer drills place fertilizer slightly below and to the side of the seed to prevent chemical root burn.
  • Combine harvesters perform three primary operations simultaneously: harvesting, threshing, and cleaning.
  • Subsoilers are heavy-duty primary tillage implements designed to break impermeable subsoil layers (hardpans) below normal ploughing depth.
  • Hollow cone nozzles in sprayers are predominantly used for applying insecticides and fungicides to ensure uniform foliar coverage.
  • Flat fan nozzles are specifically recommended for broadcasting herbicide sprays across the soil surface or crop canopy.
  • Axial-flow threshers cause less grain breakage compared to traditional tangential-flow threshers due to longitudinal material flow.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing primary tillage with secondary tillage: Remember that primary tillage involves deep soil inversion (e.g., MB plough), whereas secondary tillage prepares the seedbed without deep inversion (e.g., cultivator).
  • Assuming seed drills apply fertilizers simultaneously: Standard seed drills only drop seeds; a seed-cum-fertilizer drill is required for simultaneous fertilizer placement.
  • Incorrect nozzle selection: Using hollow cone nozzles for herbicide application instead of flat fan nozzles leads to uneven weedicide distribution and crop damage.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Utility In Farming Operations

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Tillage Machinery Classification

Tillage implements are categorized based on their functional depth and soil-working action.

  • Primary Tillage: Implements operating deeper than 15 cm for soil inversion and heavy clod breaking.
  • Secondary Tillage: Lighter implements for clod pulverization, weed destruction, and surface leveling prior to sowing.
Concept 2

Mouldboard Plough Anatomy

The mouldboard plough is an engineered tool consisting of key functional units.

  • Share: Penetrates the soil and makes the horizontal cut.
  • Mouldboard: Lifts, twists, and inverts the soil slice.
  • Landside: Absorbs lateral side-draft pressure against the furrow wall.
Concept 3

Sowing and Metering Mechanisms

Modern sowing machinery relies on mechanical metering devices.

  • Fluted Roller: Commonly used in seed drills for continuous drilling of cereal seeds like wheat and paddy.
  • Cup Feed Mechanism: Used for graded seeds like gram and peas to minimize mechanical seed damage.
Concept 4

Plant Protection Equipment Mechanics

Spray equipment atomizes liquids into droplets for effective pest control.

  • Hydraulic Sprayers: Pressure is generated by a pump to force liquid through a nozzle orifice.
  • Nozzle Types: Hollow cone for insecticides/fungicides; flat fan for herbicides.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Draft (Draft Force (kN))
The total horizontal resistance of an implement against the direction of travel when pulled by a tractor or animal.
Mouldboard (Mouldboard Assembly)
The curved plate of a plough that lifts, twists, and inverts the soil slice cut by the share.
Subsoiler (Subsoil Plough)
A heavy-duty chisel-type implement designed to shatter hardpan layers below 45 cm depth without bringing subsoil to the surface.
Disc Harrow (Disc Harrow Implement)
A secondary tillage implement consisting of a series of concave steel discs arranged on one or more axles to pulverize soil and chop residues.
Seed Drill (Mechanical Seed Drill)
An implement that opens furrows, drops seeds at uniform depth and spacing, and covers them with soil.
Fluted Roller (Fluted Feed Mechanism)
A seed metering device with adjustable fluted cylinders that regulate seed discharge rate based on exposure length.
Combine Harvester (Multi-Crop Combine)
A self-propelled machine that integrates harvesting, threshing, separating, and cleaning into a single continuous operation.
Thresher (Grain Thresher)
A machine used to separate grains from panicles or pods through mechanical impact and rubbing action inside a concavity.
Planker (Soil Leveller / Beam)
A flat wooden or metallic beam dragged across the field to crush clods, smooth the surface, and compact soil to conserve moisture.
Nozzle (Atomizing Orifice)
The terminal component of a sprayer that breaks liquid into fine droplets and distributes them in a specific spray pattern.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Share, mouldboard, landside, frog, and tailpiece are the five main components of an MB plough.
  • Subsoilers are used specifically to break impermeable subsoil hardpans.
  • Fluted rollers are standard metering devices for cereal seeds in seed drills.
  • Axial-flow threshers minimize grain cracking compared to traditional units.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Tillage implements are divided into primary tillage (deep inversion) and secondary tillage (seedbed refinement).
  • Mouldboard ploughs provide complete soil inversion, whereas disc ploughs operate well in stony or hard dry soils.
  • Seed-cum-fertilizer drills place seeds and fertilizers in separate bands simultaneously to maximize nutrient uptake.
  • Combines perform harvesting, threshing, and cleaning in one pass.
  • Flat fan nozzles are standard for herbicide application, while hollow cone nozzles are used for insecticides and fungicides.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Primary Tillage VS Secondary Tillage

Primary tillage cuts and inverts soil deeply (>15 cm), while secondary tillage operates at shallow depths (<15 cm) for seedbed finishing.

Seed Drill VS Seed-Cum-Fertilizer Drill

Seed drill handles seeds only; seed-cum-fertilizer drill features dual hoppers to deposit both seeds and fertilizers simultaneously.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a primary tillage implement?

Explanation: Mouldboard (MB) ploughs are primary tillage implements designed for deep cutting and complete inversion of the soil slice.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which component of a mouldboard plough absorbs the lateral side-draft pressure against the furrow wall?

Explanation: The landside is the flat, vertical plate that bears the side-draft pressure exerted by the furrow slice against the unplowed wall.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Under which soil condition is a disc plough preferred over a mouldboard plough?

Explanation: Disc ploughs are specifically designed for stony, sticky, or dry hard soils where MB ploughs fail to penetrate or experience excessive sliding friction.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of a subsoiler?

Explanation: Subsoilers are heavy-duty primary tillage implements designed to fracture and shatter impermeable subsoil hardpans without bringing sterile subsoil to the surface.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which metering mechanism is most commonly used in seed drills for continuous sowing of cereal seeds like wheat?

Explanation: The fluted roller mechanism is widely used in seed drills for cereal crops because it provides a steady, continuous volumetric flow of seeds.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why is a seed-cum-fertilizer drill preferred over a standard seed drill?

Explanation: Seed-cum-fertilizer drills feature separate hoppers and delivery tubes to place fertilizer slightly below and beside the seed, ensuring efficient nutrient uptake and avoiding chemical root burn.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which nozzle type is standard for applying herbicide sprays uniformly across a field?

Explanation: Flat fan nozzles produce a tapered-edge spray pattern designed to overlap perfectly on boom sprayers, ensuring uniform herbicide distribution.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What are the three primary operations performed simultaneously by a combine harvester?

Explanation: A combine harvester integrates harvesting (reaping), threshing (separating grain from panicles), and cleaning (removing chaff) into one pass.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which secondary tillage implement consists of a series of concave steel discs mounted on a common shaft?

Explanation: A disc harrow utilizes concave steel discs arranged in gangs to pulverize soil clods, chop surface residue, and prepare the seedbed.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is 'draft' in agricultural machinery terminology?

Explanation: Draft is defined as the total horizontal pull required to draw an implement through the soil at a uniform speed.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which part of a mouldboard plough makes the initial horizontal cut at the bottom of the furrow slice?

Explanation: The share is the wedge-shaped cutting edge responsible for making the horizontal cut beneath the soil slice.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of a planker in seedbed preparation?

Explanation: A planker is used for smoothing the soil surface, crushing residual clods, and lightly packing the seedbed to improve seed-soil contact and reduce moisture evaporation.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which sprayer component is responsible for breaking liquid agrochemicals into fine droplets?

Explanation: The nozzle atomizes the pressurized liquid chemical into droplets of specific sizes and distributes them in a defined spray pattern.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why are axial-flow threshers preferred over traditional tangential-flow threshers?

Explanation: Axial-flow threshers allow crop material to move longitudinally along the rotor axis, resulting in gentlest threshing action and minimal grain cracking.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which implement is classified as a secondary tillage tool?

Explanation: Cultivators are secondary tillage implements used for shallow stirring, weed eradication, and seedbed preparation.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the function of the 'frog' in a mouldboard plough assembly?

Explanation: The frog is the structural foundation or backbone of the plough bottom to which the share, mouldboard, and landside are securely attached.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which precision planting machine is ideally suited for wider-spaced crops like maize and cotton?

Explanation: Precision row crop planters use cell-feed plates or pneumatic mechanisms to place individual seeds at exact intra-row spacings for crops like maize, cotton, and soybean.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What type of nozzle is recommended for applying systemic or contact insecticides and fungicides?

Explanation: Hollow cone nozzles produce fine droplets that circulate and penetrate dense crop canopies, providing thorough coverage for insect and disease control.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which factor primarily determines the draft of an agricultural implement?

Explanation: Draft is directly influenced by soil texture, soil moisture status, depth of operation, implement geometry, and forward speed.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main advantage of using a spring-loaded cultivator over a rigid-tine cultivator?

Explanation: Spring-loaded cultivators feature flexible tines that yield and spring back upon striking underground obstacles like stones or roots, preventing damage to the implement frame and tines.

Test Question 1 of 10 TGT Standard

A farmer observes that soil inversion is incomplete and trash burial is poor after primary tillage. Which implement adjustment or selection was likely incorrect?

Solution & Rationale: Complete soil inversion and trash burial require primary tillage implements like the mouldboard plough, whereas cultivators only stir the soil without inversion.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate or evaluate the function of a seed-cum-fertilizer drill: Why is fertilizer placed in a band below and beside the seed rather than mixed directly with the seeds?

Solution & Rationale: Band placement slightly below and to the side of the seed prevents high salt concentrations from damaging germinating seeds while ensuring early nutrient availability.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

Which implement combination represents a complete sequence of primary tillage, secondary tillage, and sowing?

Solution & Rationale: The correct sequence starts with primary tillage (Mouldboard Plough), followed by secondary tillage/seedbed preparation (Disc Harrow), and culminates in sowing (Seed-Cum-Fertilizer Drill).

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

In spray application technology, if a field operator switches from a flat fan nozzle to a hollow cone nozzle while spraying a pre-emergence herbicide, what is the likely agronomic consequence?

Solution & Rationale: Flat fan nozzles are engineered for uniform horizontal distribution over a flat surface, whereas hollow cone nozzles concentrate droplets in a circular ring, leading to uneven herbicide coverage.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

During ploughing operations, excessive side-draft is experienced. Which component of the mouldboard plough should be inspected for wear and alignment?

Solution & Rationale: Excessive side-draft occurs when the landside is worn out or misaligned, failing to counteract the lateral forces exerted by the furrow slice.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary mechanical difference between tangential-flow threshers and axial-flow threshers regarding material movement?

Solution & Rationale: In tangential-flow threshers, crop material passes perpendicular across the rotating cylinder, whereas in axial-flow threshers, material flows parallel to the rotor axis for a longer and gentler threshing action.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Why are disc harrows preferred over spike-tooth harrows in heavy, trash-infested soils?

Solution & Rationale: The rolling action of concave discs in a disc harrow effectively cuts and mixes crop residues into the soil without clogging, unlike rigid spike teeth.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

A farmer wishes to break an impermeable clay pan (hardpan) located 40 cm below the surface without mixing infertile subsoil with topsoil. Which implement should be deployed?

Solution & Rationale: Subsoilers penetrate deep below normal ploughing depth to shatter hardpans without turning over or bringing infertile subsoil layers to the surface.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

In a fluted roller seed metering mechanism, how is the seed rate per hectare primarily adjusted?

Solution & Rationale: The seed discharge rate in a fluted roller mechanism is adjusted by shifting the roller axially to increase or decrease the active fluted length exposed to the seed hopper.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary agronomic benefit of incorporating a packer or planker immediately after secondary tillage operations?

Solution & Rationale: Planking or packing firms the seedbed, crushes large air pockets, reduces moisture evaporation, and ensures intimate seed-to-soil contact for rapid germination.
