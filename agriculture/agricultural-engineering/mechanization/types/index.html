Agricultural Engineering and Implements

Types of Agricultural Machinery, Implements, and Power Sources

An authoritative overview of agricultural engineering classifications, machinery types, tillage implements, and power sources designed specifically for UP TGT and UP PGT Agriculture competitive examinations.

UP PGT Agriculture Tracker →

Classification of Agricultural Power Sources

Agricultural mechanization relies on various sources of power to operate stationary and mobile farm equipment. Efficient farm management requires a balanced utilization of animate and mechanical power.

  • Human Power: The primary source of power for minor farm operations, harvesting, weeding, and seed grading. Average continuous output of a healthy human worker is approximately 0.075 kW (0.1 hp).
  • Animal Power: Utilized extensively for light tillage, sowing, and inter-cultivation. Draft animals (bullocks, buffaloes, camels, horses, and mules) deliver a draft power of roughly 10% to 12% of their body weight at an average speed of 2.5 to 3.6 km/h.
  • Mechanical Power: Comprises internal combustion (IC) engines (diesel and petrol tractors, power tillers) and electric motors. Tractors provide high drawbar and PTO (Power Take-Off) power for heavy primary and secondary tillage, sowing, and transport.
  • Electrical Power: Highly efficient and reliable for stationary operations such as irrigation pumping, threshing, chaff cutting, and cold storage processing.
  • Renewable Energy Sources: Solar photovoltaic pumps, biomass gasifiers, and wind mills gaining prominence in modern sustainable agricultural engineering frameworks.

Classification of Tillage Implements

Tillage implements are broadly classified into primary tillage and secondary tillage equipment based on the depth of operation, soil inversion requirements, and seedbed preparation stages.

  • Primary Tillage Implements: Designed to cut, shatter, and invert the soil deeply (usually 15 to 30 cm) to bury crop residues, weeds, and manure. Examples include moldboard plows, disc plows, chisel plows, and subsoilers.
  • Secondary Tillage Implements: Shallower operations (5 to 15 cm) that pulverize, level, and firm the seedbed, moisture conservation, and weed suppression. Examples include cultivators, harrows (disc, tooth, spring-tine), rotavators, and plankers.
  • Animal-Drawn vs. Tractor-Drawn: Implements are also categorized by their draft requirement; indigenous wooden plows and iron inversion plows are common in animal draft, while mounted, semi-mounted, and trailed disc/moldboard plows operate via tractor three-point linkages.
  • Rotary Tillage: Rotavators use power from the tractor PTO to rotate blades, providing intensive pulverization and mixing in a single pass.

Sowing, Planting, and Fertilizing Machinery

Precision placement of seeds and fertilizers is vital for optimal crop stand establishment, uniform germination, and high harvest indices.

  • Seed Drills: Mechanical devices that drop seeds in furrows at uniform depth and regulated seed rates, followed by soil compaction. Modern seed drills are equipped with fertilizer attachment mechanisms (seed-cum-fertilizer drills).
  • Planters: Used for crops requiring precise hill-dropping or check-row planting (e.g., maize, cotton, soybean, sugarcane). They utilize seed plates, pneumatic pickup, or finger-pick mechanisms to ensure single-seed spacing.
  • Transplanters: Semi-automatic or fully automatic machines used for transplanting paddy seedlings (mat-type nurseries) and vegetable plug seedlings (tomato, chilli, cabbage).
  • Fertilizer Distributors and Broadcasters: Equipment designed for uniform application of granular fertilizers, chemical pesticides, and organic manures across the field.

Plant Protection and Irrigation Equipment

Safeguarding crops from biotic stresses (weeds, insect pests, and fungal/bacterial pathogens) requires specialized plant protection machinery alongside efficient irrigation delivery systems.

  • Sprayers: Classified into manual (knapsack, foot, rocker) and power-operated (engine-driven, tractor-mounted hydraulic boom sprayers, and orchard sprayers). They break spray liquids into fine droplets to ensure thorough foliar coverage.
  • Dusters: Used for applying powder formulations (dusts) of insecticides or fungicides.
  • Irrigation Pumps: Centrifugal pumps, submersible pumps, and turbine pumps driven by electric motors or diesel engines for lifting irrigation water from surface and groundwater sources.
  • Micro-Irrigation Systems: Drip and sprinkler irrigation equipment consisting of control heads, filtration units, fertilizer injectors, PVC/HDPE mainlines, sub-mains, and emitters/sprinkler heads for water-use efficiency.

Harvesting, Threshing, and Post-Harvest Machinery

Timely harvesting and efficient post-harvest processing minimize grain shatter losses and maintain grain quality during storage.

  • Reapers and Mowers: Mechanical devices for cutting cereal crops and forage grasses close to the ground level, laying them in windrows.
  • Combine Harvesters: Self-propelled or tractor-drawn heavy machines that perform multiple operations simultaneously: cutting, threshing, separating grain from straw, and cleaning grain.
  • Threshers: Specialized stationary or tractor-operated machines (axial-flow, spike-tooth, or rasp-bar cylinders) used to detach grains from panicles or pods (wheat, paddy, pulses, oilseeds).
  • Shellers and Cleaners: Maize shellers, winnowers, and grading screens that remove foreign matter and grade agricultural produce according to size and density.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Average power output of a healthy human labourer is approximately 0.075 kW (0.1 hp).
  • Draft of an animal is normally 10% to 12% of its body weight at a normal walking speed of 2.5 to 3.6 km/h.
  • Moldboard (MB) plow is the standard primary tillage implement used for complete soil inversion and weed burial.
  • Subsoiler is used to break hardpans in subsoil layers without bringing infertile soil to the surface, operating deeper than 45 cm.
  • Rotavator operates directly through tractor PTO drive, combining primary and secondary tillage into a single pass.
  • Seed-cum-fertilizer drills place seeds and fertilizers simultaneously at predetermined depths in separate bands.
  • Combine harvester performs three principal operations simultaneously: harvesting (cutting), threshing, and cleaning.
  • Axial-flow threshers minimize grain damage and separation losses in paddy and wheat threshing operations.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing primary tillage with secondary tillage: Cultivators and harrows are secondary tillage tools, whereas moldboard and disc plows are primary tillage tools.
  • Assuming rotavators are purely secondary tillage implements: While often used for seedbed preparation, they can perform primary tillage in lighter soils.
  • Incorrectly calculating animal draft power as a fixed percentage of speed instead of body weight percentage.

Concept 1

Agricultural Power Sources and Mechanics

Farm power availability is a direct indicator of agricultural productivity and mechanization level in Indian agriculture.

  • Human Power: 0.075 kW output.
  • Animal Draft: 10-12% of animal body weight.
  • Tractor PTO: Delivers high rotational energy for threshers, rotavators, and spray pumps.
Concept 2

Classification and Mechanics of Tillage Implements

Tillage alters soil physical properties, aerates root zones, and manages weed flora.

  • Primary Tillage: Deep cutting and inversion (Moldboard Plow, Disc Plow).
  • Secondary Tillage: Pulverization and leveling (Cultivator, Harrow, Plank).
Concept 3

Sowing and Planting Mechanisms

Seed drills and planters ensure precise depth control and plant population density.

  • Fluted Roller: Used in seed drills for volumetric metering of small grains.
  • Pneumatic Metering: Uses vacuum suction for precise single-seed singulation in planters.
Concept 4

Plant Protection and Harvesting Machinery

High-efficiency sprayers and combines reduce harvest losses and protect crops.

  • Hydraulic Sprayers: Pressure atomization of liquids.
  • Combine Harvester: Integrates cutting, threshing, and cleaning mechanisms.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Draft (Horizontal Resistance Force)
The horizontal component of pull exerted by an animal or tractor to pull an implement through the soil.
Moldboard Plow (Inversion Tillage Implement)
A primary tillage implement equipped with a share and moldboard that cuts, lifts, turns, and inverts the furrow slice.
Disc Plow (Spherical Disc Tillage Tool)
A primary tillage implement using inclined concave steel discs that roll over stony or sticky soils without clogging.
Cultivator (Secondary Tillage Implement)
An implement with rigid or spring tines used for weed destruction, soil stirring, and secondary seedbed preparation.
Rotavator (Rotary Tillage Implement)
A tractor PTO-driven tillage machine with L-shaped blades that thoroughly pulverizes and mixes soil in a single pass.
Seed Drill (Mechanical Sowing Machine)
A machine that opens furrows, drops seeds at uniform depths and rates, and covers them with soil.
Planter (Precision Sowing Machine)
A machine designed for accurate hill-dropping or check-row planting of bold-seeded crops at exact spacing.
Subsoiler (Deep Tillage Implement)
A heavy-duty chisel-type implement designed to break impermeable subsoil hardpans at depths exceeding 45 cm.
Power Take-Off (PTO) (Tractor Shaft Power Output)
A rotating shaft spline at the rear of a tractor that transmits engine power directly to mounted or trailed implements.
Combine Harvester (Multi-Operation Harvesting Machine)
A self-propelled machine that simultaneously reaps, threshes, separates, and cleans grain crops.
Thresher (Grain Detachment Machine)
A machine utilizing rotating drums and concaves to detach grains from panicles or pods through impact and friction.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Farm power sources include human, animal, mechanical, electrical, and renewable energy.
  • Primary tillage involves deep soil inversion (Moldboard and Disc plows).
  • Secondary tillage prepares fine seedbeds (Cultivators, Harrows, Rotavators).
  • Seed drills use fluted rollers while planters use precision plate or pneumatic mechanisms.
  • Combine harvesters integrate cutting, threshing, and cleaning operations.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Primary Tillage VS Secondary Tillage

Primary tillage inverts deep soil layers (15-30 cm); secondary tillage shallowly pulverizes and levels the seedbed (5-15 cm).

Seed Drill VS Planter

Seed drills deposit continuous streams of seeds for close-spaced crops; planters drop individual seeds at precise intervals for wide-row crops.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the average continuous power output of a healthy human labourer?

Explanation: A healthy human labourer can deliver an average continuous power output of approximately 0.075 kW (equivalent to 0.1 horsepower) over an ordinary working day.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The draft of a normal draft animal is typically what percentage of its total body weight?

Explanation: Draft animals (bullocks, horses) comfortably exert a draft power equivalent to 10% to 12% of their body weight while working at a normal speed of 2.5 to 3.6 km/h.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a primary tillage implement?

Explanation: The moldboard plow is a classic primary tillage implement designed to cut, lift, turn, and invert the soil slice completely.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of a subsoiler in agricultural engineering?

Explanation: A subsoiler is a heavy tillage implement designed to break hardpans and compacted subsoil layers below the normal plow layer without bringing sterile subsoil to the surface.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which implement combines both primary and secondary tillage operations in a single pass using tractor PTO power?

Explanation: A rotavator utilizes power from the tractor's Power Take-Off (PTO) shaft to rotate blades that intensely cut, mix, and pulverize the soil in a single pass.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the seed metering mechanism commonly found in standard cereal seed drills?

Explanation: The fluted roller mechanism is the most widely used seed metering device in conventional seed drills for volumetric feeding of cereal seeds.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which machine is best suited for precise hill-dropping or check-row planting of maize or cotton?

Explanation: Planters use specialized cellular plates, pneumatic suction, or mechanical fingers to singulate and place bold seeds at exact spatial intervals.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What are the three principal operations performed simultaneously by a combine harvester?

Explanation: A combine harvester integrates three core harvest operations: cutting (reaping) the crop, threshing grain from the panicle, and cleaning chaff from the grain.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of a tractor's Power Take-Off (PTO) shaft?

Explanation: The PTO shaft is a splined mechanical power outlet on the tractor that transmits engine rotational energy directly to operate driven implements.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which implement is classified as a secondary tillage tool?

Explanation: Cultivators are secondary tillage implements used to stir soil, eradicate weeds, and prepare fine seedbeds following primary plowing.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main advantage of using a seed-cum-fertilizer drill over a simple seed drill?

Explanation: A seed-cum-fertilizer drill is equipped with dual hopper compartments to place seeds and granular fertilizers at precise depths in adjacent bands during sowing.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which type of plow is most effective in stony, stumpy, or sticky soils without getting choked?

Explanation: Disc plows utilize rotating concave discs that roll over stones, roots, and obstacles, making them highly effective in difficult or sticky soil conditions.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical depth range for primary tillage operations?

Explanation: Primary tillage implements like moldboard and disc plows operate at depths ranging from 15 cm to 30 cm to invert and shatter soil layers.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which mechanism is used in pneumatic planters for precise single-seed singulation?

Explanation: Pneumatic planters utilize a rotating perforated disc with vacuum suction to pick up and hold individual seeds until release at the furrow.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which farm power source contributes the highest proportion of stationary mechanical power for irrigation pumping in India?

Explanation: Electric motors and diesel engines provide the primary mechanical and electrical power for lifting irrigation water through centrifugal and submersible pumps.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the purpose of a planker or levler attached behind a cultivator?

Explanation: A planker is a secondary tillage leveling tool used to crush remaining soil clods, compact the seedbed slightly, and smooth the surface.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In animal-drawn implements, what determines the line of pull and depth adjustment?

Explanation: The hitch beam and clevis assembly connect animal-drawn implements to the yoke, allowing adjustments in depth and line of draft.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which classification of tillage includes harrowing and planking?

Explanation: Harrowing, cultivating, and planking are standard secondary tillage operations designed to refine seedbeds after primary plowing.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary advantage of minimum tillage systems?

Explanation: Minimum tillage reduces tillage operations to the barest minimum required for crop establishment, saving fuel, reducing labor, and conserving soil structure.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which instrument or parameter measures the draft resistance of a tillage implement?

Explanation: A dynamometer is an instrumentation device used to measure the draft (pulling force) exerted by tractors or animals on tillage implements.

Calculate the approximate draft of a 400 kg bullock when working at maximum efficiency where draft is 12% of body weight.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which primary tillage implement provides complete inversion of the furrow slice and is equipped with a share, moldboard, and landside?

Solution & Rationale: The moldboard plow features a share for cutting, a moldboard for turning and inverting, and a landside for lateral stability.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

What type of seed metering device utilizes a rotating cylinder with peripheral grooves to meter small seeds volumetrically?

Solution & Rationale: The fluted roller mechanism uses adjustable fluted cylinders to control seed flow volumetrically in grain seed drills.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Which agricultural machine is specifically engineered to cut standing sugarcane or cereal crops and lay them in orderly windrows?

Solution & Rationale: A reaper is a harvesting machine designed solely to cut crops and discharge them in side windrows.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

In tractor-drawn implements, what connects the implement to the tractor and consists of two lower link arms and one upper top link?

Solution & Rationale: The three-point hitch linkage standardizes tractor-implement connection for mounted and semi-mounted implements.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

Why are disc plows preferred over moldboard plows in dry, hard, or abrasive soils?

Solution & Rationale: Disc plows rotate as they move through the soil, converting sliding friction into rolling friction and reducing component wear in abrasive soils.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary function of the concave and cylinder assembly in a grain thresher?

Solution & Rationale: The rotating cylinder and stationary concave create impact and rubbing action to detach grains from the earheads or pods.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which machine uses a high-velocity air stream generated by a fan to separate lighter chaff and straw from heavier threshed grains?

Solution & Rationale: Winnowers and cleaning mechanisms utilize centrifugal blower fans to blow away lighter impurities while clean grain falls through.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary distinction between zero tillage and conventional tillage?

Solution & Rationale: Zero tillage (no-till) places seeds directly into unplowed soil using specialized disk planters, retaining crop residues on the surface.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which power transmission component on a tractor allows implements to be operated at varying speeds independent of the tractor's forward ground speed?

Solution & Rationale: An independent PTO has its own clutch control, allowing the PTO shaft speed to remain constant regardless of tractor travel speed.
