Production Under Different Heads in Agronomy and Field Crops

Classification of Crop Production Systems Based on Utility and Purpose

Crop production under different heads refers to the systematic classification and agronomic management of field crops based on their economic use, botanical families, life cycles, and specific ecological or industrial requirements. This classification forms the foundation of applied agronomy.

  • Cereals and Millets: Cultivated primarily for their starchy, edible grains belonging to the Poaceae (Gramineae) family (e.g., Triticum aestivum, Oryza sativa, Zea mays).
  • Pulses and Grain Legumes: Grown for high-protein seed production belonging to the Fabaceae (Leguminosae) family, possessing symbiotic nitrogen-fixing root nodules (e.g., Cicer arietinum, Cajanus cajan, Lens culinaris).
  • Oilseed Crops: Cultivated for vegetable oil extraction from seeds or kernels (e.g., Brassica juncea, Arachis hypogaea, Glycine max, Helianthus annuus).
  • Fiber Crops: Grown for spinning, weaving, and industrial textile production, extracting fibers from seed, stem, or leaves (e.g., Gossypium hirsutum, Corchorus capsularis).
  • Sugar and Starch Crops: Cultivated for sucrose or industrial starch extraction (e.g., Saccharum officinarum, Beta vulgaris, Solanum tuberosum).

Cereal and Cereal-Equivalent Crop Production Management

Cereals constitute the staple food supply globally and in India, anchoring national food security. Agronomic management requires precision in land preparation, seed rate, nutrient scheduling, and critical irrigation.

  • Wheat (Triticum aestivum): Requires a cool, moist growing season and warm, bright ripening period. Optimum temperature for germination is 20-25°C. Critical irrigation stage is Crown Root Initiation (CRI), occurring 20-25 days after sowing. Standard seed rate is 100 kg/ha with a spacing of 22.5 cm between rows.
  • Rice (Oryza sativa): Thrives under submerged or saturated soil conditions in tropical and subtropical humid climates. Nursery raising methods include Wet Bed, Dry Bed, and Dapog methods. Recommended N:P:K ratio for high-yielding varieties is typically 120:60:40 kg/ha.
  • Maize (Zea mays): A C4 plant highly sensitive to both water stress and waterlogging. Sown with a seed rate of 20 kg/ha for grain and 40 kg/ha for fodder at a spacing of 60 cm x 20 cm.

Pulse and Oilseed Production Technologies

Pulses and oilseeds are vital for nutritional security, soil fertility restoration through biological nitrogen fixation, and dryland farming sustainability.

  • Gram / Chickpea (Cicer arietinum): The premier pulse crop of India. Requires a moderate to cool climate with 600-900 mm annual rainfall. Inoculation with Rhizobium leguminosarum strain is mandatory. Nipping (removal of terminal buds) is practiced at 30-40 days after sowing to encourage branching and prevent excessive vegetative growth.
  • Mustard / Indian Mustard (Brassica juncea): A rabi oilseed crop requiring cool, dry weather during flowering and pod filling. Sulfur application is critical for oil synthesis and protein quality, usually applied @ 20-40 kg S/ha through gypsum or elemental sulfur.
  • Groundnut (Arachis hypogaea): An underground pod-forming oilseed legume requiring sandy loam soils. Gypsum is applied @ 250-500 kg/ha at flowering (peg formation stage) to supply calcium and sulfur, essential for proper pod filling and reducing 'pops' (empty pods).

Commercial, Fiber, and Sugar Crop Production

Industrial and commercial crops demand high capital investment, precise nutrient scheduling, and intensive plant protection measures.

  • Sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum): A long-duration C4 tropical grass requiring 1000-1500 mm water. Planted using 3-budded setts with a seed rate of 35,000 to 40,000 setts per hectare (approx. 60-75 quintals/ha). Earthing up is performed at the grand growth stage (90-120 days after planting) to prevent lodging.
  • Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum): Requires frost-free days with warm temperatures (21-30°C) and moderate rainfall (500-1000 mm). Hand picking of seed cotton is standard. Bt cotton hybrids are widely adopted to manage the pink bollworm (Pectinophora gossypiella).

Agronomic Calculations: Seed Rate, Fertilizer, and Plant Population

UP TGT and PGT Agriculture examinations frequently test numerical problem-solving skills related to crop production inputs.

  • Plant Population Calculation: Formula: Plant Population per hectare = 10^4Row spacing (m) × Plant spacing (m). For 20 cm x 10 cm spacing in rice, population = 100000.20 × 0.10 = 5,00,000 plants/ha.
  • Pure Live Seed (PLS) Formula: PLS = Purity % × Germination %100. Used for adjusting field seed rate when seed lot quality is sub-optimal.
  • Fertilizer Nutrient to Commercial Fertilizer Conversion: To apply 120 kg N/ha using Urea (containing 46% N), required urea quantity = 120 × 10046 = 260.8 kg/ha.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Crown Root Initiation (CRI) in wheat occurs exactly 20-25 days after sowing and is the most critical irrigation stage.
  • Nipping in chickpea (gram) is practiced to remove apical dominance, thereby encouraging lateral branching and increasing pod number.
  • Sugarcane planting is predominantly done using 3-budded setts requiring approximately 35,000 to 40,000 setts per hectare.
  • Gypsum application @ 250-500 kg/ha in groundnut at pegging stage provides calcium and sulfur, preventing empty pods (pops).
  • Bt cotton utilizes genes from Bacillus thuringiensis to confer resistance against lepidopteran pests like the American bollworm and pink bollworm.
  • Dapog method of rice nursery raising produces seedlings ready for transplanting within 11-14 days without soil.
  • Pure Live Seed (PLS) percentage is calculated as (Purity % x Germination %) / 100.
  • Urea contains 46% nitrogen, whereas Di-ammonium Phosphate (DAP) contains 18% nitrogen and 46% phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5).
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the C3 photosynthetic pathway of wheat and rice with the C4 pathway of maize and sugarcane.
  • Assuming CRI stage in wheat occurs at germination rather than 20-25 days after sowing when nodal roots initiate.
  • Applying gypsum to groundnut at sowing instead of the critical pegging stage (30-45 DAS) for pod development.
  • Mixing up seed rates of broadcasted rice versus transplanted lowland rice.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Production Under Different Heads in Agronomy and Field Crops

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Agronomic Crop Classification Systems

Crops are classified based on utility, life cycle (annual, biennial, perennial), and botanical affinity.

  • Utility Classification: Grouping crops into food, feed, fiber, oil, sugar, and medicinal heads.
  • Botanical Classification: Grouping based on floral and vegetative characteristics (Poaceae, Fabaceae, Brassicaceae).
Concept 2

Cereal Crop Production and Water Management

Cereals form the bedrock of agronomic production systems requiring precise water management.

  • CRI Stage: The most sensitive moisture stress window in wheat occurring at 20-25 DAS.
  • Submergence: Rice requires continuous or intermittent ponding of 5 cm water depth during active vegetative growth.
Concept 3

Legume and Oilseed Agronomy

Legumes and oilseeds contribute to nitrogen economy and lipid-rich human diets.

  • Symbiotic Fixation: Rhizobium association fixes atmospheric nitrogen in root nodules.
  • Nipping: Vegetative pruning in chickpea to enhance harvest index.
Concept 4

Input Quantification and Fertilizer Calculations

Agronomists must calculate exact fertilizer and seed requirements to optimize input use efficiency.

  • Nutrient Conversion: Dividing desired nutrient dose by nutrient percentage in fertilizer multiplied by 100.
  • Plant Density: Inverse product of row and intra-row plant spacing.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Agronomy (Agros (field) + Nomos (management))
The branch of agricultural science concerned with the principles and practices of crop production and field management.
CRI Stage (Crown Root Initiation)
The most critical irrigation stage in wheat occurring 20-25 days after sowing when permanent nodal roots develop.
Nipping (Terminal Bud Removal)
The manual or chemical removal of apical buds in chickpea to stimulate lateral branching and grain yield.
Pegging (Geocarpy Initiation)
The downward movement of fertilized ovaries (pegs) into the soil in groundnut where pods subsequently develop.
Dapog Method (Mat Nursery Technique)
A soil-less rice nursery raising technique originating in the Philippines producing ready seedlings in 11-14 days.
Earthing Up (Ridge Formation around Stalks)
The agronomic practice of heaping soil around the base of crops like sugarcane and potato to prevent lodging and tuber exposure.
Pure Live Seed (PLS) (Viable Seed Quality Index)
The product of seed purity percentage and germination percentage divided by 100.
C4 Plant (Dicarboxylic Acid Pathway Plant)
Plants like maize, sugarcane, and sorghum possessing higher photosynthetic efficiency under high light and temperature.
Ratooning (Stubble Crop Cultivation)
Growing a second crop from the stubbles or roots of the harvested main crop, commonly practiced in sugarcane.
Bt Cotton (Bacillus thuringiensis Transgenic Cotton)
Genetically modified cotton expressing insecticidal crystal proteins toxic to bollworms.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Urea contains 46% Nitrogen.
  • DAP contains 18% N and 46% P2O5.
  • CRI in wheat is the universal benchmark for single irrigation availability.
  • Groundnut requires calcium at peg formation to prevent empty pods.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Crop production heads categorize crops into cereals, pulses, oilseeds, fibers, and sugar crops based on utility.
  • Wheat requires CRI stage irrigation at 20-25 DAS and a standard seed rate of 100 kg/ha.
  • Chickpea undergoes nipping to boost branching and prevent vegetative lodging.
  • Groundnut requires gypsum application at pegging to supply calcium for proper kernel filling.
  • Fertilizer dose calculations depend on percentage nutrient composition of commercial carriers like urea and DAP.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

CRI Stage in Wheat VS Tillering Stage in Wheat

CRI occurs at 20-25 DAS at the crown root zone, whereas tillering occurs later around 40-45 DAS.

Pegging in Groundnut VS Flowering in Groundnut

Flowering occurs above ground, whereas pegging involves the downward entry of fertilized flower stalks into the soil.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the most critical irrigation stage in wheat crop production?

Explanation: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) is universally recognized as the most critical irrigation stage in wheat, occurring 20-25 days after sowing. Water stress at this stage severely impairs crown root development and reduces final grain yield.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which agronomic practice is exclusively performed in chickpea (gram) to enhance branching and pod yield?

Explanation: Nipping (removal of terminal buds) in chickpea breaks apical dominance, promoting lateral branching, which directly increases flower and pod bearing sites.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the nitrogen percentage present in commercial agricultural grade Urea?

Explanation: Urea is an organic amide fertilizer containing 46% nitrogen by weight, making it the most concentrated solid nitrogenous fertilizer used globally.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In groundnut cultivation, why is gypsum applied at the pegging stage?

Explanation: Gypsum supplies calcium (Ca) and sulfur (S). Calcium is directly absorbed by developing pods (pegs) in the soil zone, preventing empty pods ('pops') and ensuring kernel development.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a C4 photosynthetic crop?

Explanation: Maize, sugarcane, and sorghum are C4 plants possessing Kranz anatomy and higher photosynthetic efficiency under high light intensity and temperature compared to C3 crops like wheat and rice.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard seed rate recommended for wheat cultivation under timely sown irrigated conditions?

Explanation: The recommended seed rate for timely sown irrigated wheat is 100 kg/ha, ensuring an optimum plant population of approximately 300-400 plants per square meter.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The Dapog method of nursery raising is associated with which crop?

Explanation: The Dapog method is a specialized, soil-less nursery technique for rice where seedlings are raised on banana leaves or plastic sheets and ready for transplanting in 11-14 days.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

How much quantity of Urea is required to supply 60 kg of actual Nitrogen?

Explanation: Required Urea = (60 / 46) * 100 = 130.43 kg. Therefore, approximately 130.4 kg of urea is required to supply 60 kg of pure nitrogen.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of earthing up in sugarcane production?

Explanation: Earthing up provides physical support to tall sugarcane stalks, preventing lodging during heavy winds, while also creating furrows for irrigation and drainage.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which botanical family does Indian Mustard (Brassica juncea) belong to?

Explanation: Indian Mustard belongs to the Brassicaceae (Cruciferae) family, characterized by tetramerous flowers and siliqua type fruits.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does 'PLS' stand for in seed technology and agronomy?

Explanation: Pure Live Seed (PLS) represents the percentage of seed in a lot that is both pure and viable, calculated as (Purity % x Germination %) / 100.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which nutrient is specifically required for oil synthesis and protein quality in oilseed crops like mustard?

Explanation: Sulfur is essential for oilseed crops because it is a key constituent of sulfur-containing amino acids (methionine, cysteine) and is vital for glucosinolate and oil synthesis.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the optimum planting geometry (spacing) generally recommended for hybrid maize?

Explanation: Maize is planted with a standard spacing of 60 cm between rows and 20 cm between plants within the row, ensuring an ideal population of approximately 83,333 plants/ha.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In which crop is 'detasseling' a mandatory agronomic practice?

Explanation: Detasseling (removal of male tassels) is practiced in hybrid maize seed production to prevent self-pollination and ensure cross-pollination by desired male parent rows.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the average storage moisture content safe for long-term storage of cereal grains?

Explanation: Safe moisture content for long-term storage of cereal grains (wheat, rice, maize) is 10-12% to prevent mold growth, insect activity, and grain respiration loss.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest is targeted by transgenic Bt cotton technology?

Explanation: Bt cotton expresses Cry proteins derived from Bacillus thuringiensis, which are specifically toxic to lepidopteran pests such as the American bollworm (Helicoverpa armigera) and pink bollworm (Pectinophora gossypiella).

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the botanical name of Pigeonpea, an important grain legume of India?

Explanation: Pigeonpea is scientifically named Cajanus cajan (family Fabaceae), serving as a major Kharif pulse crop in Indian cropping systems.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary cause of 'Pops' in groundnut production?

Explanation: 'Pops' refer to empty or unfilled pods in groundnut, primarily caused by a deficiency of calcium in the pegging zone during pod development.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is ratoon cropping in sugarcane?

Explanation: Ratoon cropping involves allowing the stubbles of harvested sugarcane to sprout and produce a subsequent crop without fresh planting, saving operational costs.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Calculate the plant population per hectare if the spacing is 30 cm x 10 cm.

Explanation: Population = 10,000 / (0.30 * 0.10) = 10,000 / 0.03 = 3,33,333 plants/ha.
