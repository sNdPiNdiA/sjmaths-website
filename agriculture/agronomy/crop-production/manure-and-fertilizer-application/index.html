Manure and Fertilizer Application in Agronomy

Classification and Characteristics of Manures and Fertilizers

Plant nutrients are supplied through organic and inorganic sources. Organic manures include Farmyard Manure (FYM), compost, vermicompost, and green manures, which improve soil physical properties, water-holding capacity, and microbial activity in addition to supplying macro and micronutrients. Chemical fertilizers are concentrated inorganic salts manufactured industrially to supply essential plant nutrients rapidly.

  • Farmyard Manure (FYM): A well-decomposed mixture of cattle dung, urine, and litter. On average, standard FYM contains 0.5% N, 0.2% P2O5, and 0.5% K2O.
  • Compost: Produced by aerobic or anaerobic decomposition of farm and urban organic wastes. Indore and Bangalore methods are classical aerobic and anaerobic composting techniques.
  • Green Manuring: Incorporating green plant tissues (preferably legumes such as Crotalaria juncea or Sesbania aculeata) into the soil to enrich it with organic matter and biological nitrogen fixation.
  • Nitrogenous Fertilizers: Include urea (46% N), ammonium sulphate (20.6% N), and calcium ammonium nitrate (25% N). Urea is the most widely used amide fertilizer in India.
  • Phosphatic Fertilizers: Include Single Super Phosphate (SSP containing 16% P2O5, 12% S, and 19.5% Ca) and Di-ammonium Phosphate (DAP containing 18% N and 46% P2O5).
  • Potassic Fertilizers: Muriate of Potash (MOP / Potassium chloride containing 60% K2O) and Potassium sulphate (50% K2O).

Methods of Fertilizer Application

The method of fertilizer application determines nutrient use efficiency (NUE) and minimizes fixation, volatilization, leaching, and runoff losses. Selection depends on crop root system, soil type, mobility of nutrient in soil, and stage of crop growth.

  • Broadcasting: Uniform scattering of fertilizers over the soil surface. Used mainly for basal dressing in dense-seeded crops like wheat and rice before sowing or transplanting.
  • Placement: Placing fertilizers close to the seed or plant root zone in bands or pockets. Examples include plow-sole placement and deep placement of urea supergranules (USG) in flooded paddy fields.
  • Band Placement: Applying fertilizer in bands either below or to the side of the seed row (starter solutions or row placement). Highly efficient for phosphatic fertilizers to reduce soil contact and fixation.
  • Pellet Application: Pressing nitrogenous fertilizers (especially ammonium fertilizers) into mudballs or pellets and placing them deep in reduced soil zones (e.g., lowland rice) to prevent nitrification-denitrification losses.
  • Foliar Application: Spraying dilute solutions of water-soluble fertilizers (e.g., 2% urea or 1% potassium nitrate) directly onto plant leaves to correct acute micronutrient or macronutrient deficiencies rapidly.
  • Fertigation: Applying dissolved fertilizers through irrigation systems (drip or sprinkler irrigation). It ensures high fertilizer use efficiency, uniform distribution, and precise nutrient delivery directly to the active root zone.

Nutrient Use Efficiency (NUE) and Loss Pathways

Nutrient Use Efficiency represents the proportion of applied nutrients taken up and utilized by the crop. Low NUE causes economic loss and environmental degradation such as eutrophication and groundwater contamination.

  • Nitrogen Losses: Nitrogen is lost primarily through ammonia volatilization (high pH soils), denitrification (anaerobic waterlogged soils), nitrate leaching (coarse-textured sandy soils), and surface runoff.
  • Phosphorus Fixation: Phosphorus reacts with iron and aluminum oxides in acidic soils, and with calcium carbonates in alkaline soils, forming insoluble complexes that reduce availability.
  • Potassium Fixation: Potassium can become fixed within the lattice structure of expanding 2:1 clay minerals such as illite and vermiculite.
  • Enhancing NUE: Use of nitrification inhibitors (e.g., nitrapyrin, neem-coated urea), urease inhibitors, split applications of nitrogen, balanced fertilization, and localized band placement.

Nutrient Requirement Calculations and Fertilizer Equivalents

Agronomists and farm managers must calculate exact commercial fertilizer quantities based on recommended nutrient doses (N:P2O5:K2O) and fertilizer grades.

  • Formula for Fertilizer Quantity:
    Quantity of Fertilizer (kg) = [Recommended Nutrient Dose (kg/ha) × 100] / Percentage of Nutrient in Fertilizer
  • Urea Calculation Example: To apply 120 kg N per hectare using Urea (46% N):
    Quantity of Urea = (120 × 100) / 46 = 260.87 kg/ha
  • DAP and Urea Combination for N and P: When applying DAP (18% N, 46% P2O5), the nitrogen supplied by DAP must be subtracted from the total recommended nitrogen before calculating the remaining urea requirement.
  • Sulphur and Calcium in SSP: Single Super Phosphate provides secondary nutrients (12% sulphur and 19.5% calcium), making it superior to DAP in sulphur-deficient soils.

Integrated Nutrient Management (INM)

Integrated Nutrient Management is the maintenance or adjustment of soil fertility and plant nutrient supply at an optimum level for sustaining crop productivity through the optimized use of all available organic, inorganic, and biological sources in an integrated manner.

  • Objectives: To maintain long-term soil health, maximize economic yield, minimize environmental pollution, and reduce dependence on expensive chemical fertilizers.
  • Components: Chemical fertilizers, organic manures, crop residues, green manures, and biofertilizers (e.g., Rhizobium, Azotobacter, Azospirillum, and Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria like Bacillus megaterium).
  • Biofertilizers: Living microorganisms that colonize the rhizosphere or interior of plants and promote growth by increasing the supply or availability of primary nutrients to the host plant.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Urea contains 46% Nitrogen and is an amide fertilizer.
  • Single Super Phosphate (SSP) contains 16% P2O5, 12% Sulphur, and 19.5% Calcium.
  • Muriate of Potash (MOP) contains 60% K2O and should be avoided in chlorine-sensitive crops like tobacco and potato.
  • Neem-coated urea slows down nitrification and reduces nitrogen loss through leaching and volatilization.
  • DAP contains 18% Nitrogen and 46% Phosphorus (P2O5).
  • Fertigation is the application of water-soluble fertilizers through micro-irrigation systems.
  • Indore method of composting is an aerobic process, while Bangalore method is anaerobic.
  • Biofertilizer Rhizobium is specific to legume crops for symbiotic nitrogen fixation.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the nutrient content of SSP (16% P2O5) with DAP (46% P2O5).
  • Assuming MOP contains 50% potassium when it actually contains 60% K2O.
  • Believing that organic manures can replace chemical fertilizers in high-yielding intensive cropping systems without substantial bulk application.
  • Forgetting to subtract nitrogen supplied by DAP when calculating total urea requirements for recommended N:P doses.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Manure and Fertilizer Application in Agronomy

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Nutrient Classification and Essentiality

Essential plant nutrients are classified into macronutrients (primary: N, P, K; secondary: Ca, Mg, S) and micronutrients (Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B, Mo, Cl, Ni) based on plant tissue concentration.

  • Criteria of Essentiality: Proposed by Arnon and Stout (1939), stating that a plant must be unable to complete its life cycle without the element, and the element must act directly in plant metabolism.
Concept 2

Organic Manures versus Inorganic Fertilizers

Organic manures improve soil structure, microbial population, and cation exchange capacity (CEC), whereas inorganic fertilizers provide immediate, concentrated, and readily available nutrient ions.

  • C:N Ratio: Ideal C:N ratio of mature compost is around 10:1 to 12:1; higher ratios cause temporary microbial immobilization of soil nitrogen.
Concept 3

Fertilizer Calculation and Doses

Calculating fertilizer weights accurately prevents crop toxicity and nutrient waste. Always convert nutrient recommendations (N, P2O5, K2O) using the percentage composition of commercial fertilizers.

  • Nutrient Conversion: P to P2O5 multiplier is 2.29; K to K2O multiplier is 1.20.
Concept 4

Methods of Application and Efficiency

Choosing the correct application method (broadcasting, banding, fertigation, foliar spray) maximizes nutrient uptake and minimizes environmental losses such as volatilization and leaching.

  • Fertigation: Achieves nutrient use efficiencies exceeding 85% by delivering nutrients directly to the root zone via drip lines.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Farmyard Manure (FYM) (Decomposed cattle dung, urine, and litter)
Traditional organic manure containing roughly 0.5% N, 0.2% P2O5, and 0.5% K2O.
Green Manuring (In-situ incorporation of succulent green legume crops)
Practiced to add organic matter and biologically fixed nitrogen into the soil (e.g., Crotalaria juncea).
Nitrification Inhibitor (Chemical compound suppressing Nitrosomonas bacteria)
Used to delay the conversion of ammonium to nitrate, reducing leaching and denitrification losses.
Fertigation (Fertilizer application through irrigation water)
High-efficiency method delivering soluble fertilizers directly to plant root zones via drip or sprinkler systems.
C:N Ratio (Carbon to Nitrogen Ratio)
Proportion of organic carbon to total nitrogen in organic residues, governing decomposition rate and nitrogen availability.
Broadcasting (Uniform surface scattering of fertilizers)
Basic method of fertilizer application used prior to sowing or for close-growing crops.
Band Placement (Placement of fertilizer in bands near seed rows)
Minimizes soil-fertilizer contact and reduces phosphorus fixation.
Top Dressing (Surface application of fertilizers to growing crops)
Commonly practiced for split applications of nitrogenous fertilizers like urea.
Biofertilizer (Microbial inoculant for nutrient supply)
Living formulations of beneficial microorganisms like Rhizobium, Azotobacter, and PSB that enhance nutrient availability.
Nutrient Use Efficiency (NUE) (Crop uptake / Applied nutrient ratio)
Percentage of applied plant nutrients actually absorbed and utilized by the target crop.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • P to P2O5 conversion factor is 2.29.
  • K to K2O conversion factor is 1.20.
  • Neem coating of urea prevents rapid hydrolysis and nitrification.
  • Chlorine-sensitive crops like tobacco and potato should not receive MOP (Muriate of Potash).

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Manures improve soil physical and biological properties; fertilizers provide concentrated nutrients.
  • Urea contains 46% N; DAP contains 18% N and 46% P2O5; MOP contains 60% K2O.
  • Placement and fertigation significantly improve nutrient use efficiency over broadcast methods.
  • Integrated Nutrient Management (INM) combines organic, inorganic, and biological sources for sustainable agriculture.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

SSP (Single Super Phosphate) VS DAP (Di-ammonium Phosphate)

SSP contains 16% P2O5 along with 12% Sulphur and 19.5% Calcium; DAP contains 18% N and 46% P2O5 without sulphur.

MOP (Muriate of Potash) VS SOP (Sulphate of Potash)

MOP contains 60% K2O with chloride (unsuitable for sensitive crops); SOP contains 50% K2O with sulphur (safe for sensitive crops).

What is the average nitrogen percentage present in commercial commercial urea fertilizer?

Explanation: Commercial urea (CO(NH2)2) contains 46% nitrogen by weight, making it the most concentrated solid nitrogenous fertilizer used globally.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following fertilizers contains 18% Nitrogen and 46% Phosphorus (P2O5)?

Explanation: Di-ammonium Phosphate (DAP) contains 18% N and 46% P2O5.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary nutrient composition of Muriate of Potash (MOP)?

Explanation: Muriate of Potash (Potassium chloride, KCl) contains 60% K2O.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which method of fertilizer application is best suited to minimize phosphorus fixation in acidic and alkaline soils?

Explanation: Band placement concentrates phosphorus in a localized zone, reducing contact with soil particles and thereby lowering fixation rates.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of coating urea with neem oil?

Explanation: Neem-coated urea slows down the hydrolysis of urea and subsequent nitrification, reducing leaching and volatilization losses.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which secondary nutrient is abundantly present in Single Super Phosphate (SSP) besides phosphorus?

Explanation: SSP contains 16% P2O5, 12% Sulphur, and 19.5% Calcium.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the ideal C:N ratio for mature compost?

Explanation: A C:N ratio of 10:1 to 12:1 indicates stable compost where nitrogen is neither excessively immobilized nor leached.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Application of fertilizers through irrigation water is known as:

Explanation: Fertigation is the injection of fertilizers, used with micro-irrigation systems like drip or sprinklers.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a biofertilizer used for symbiotic nitrogen fixation in legume crops?

Explanation: Rhizobium forms a symbiotic relationship with legume roots to fix atmospheric nitrogen.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What conversion factor is used to convert elemental Phosphorus (P) to Phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5)?

Explanation: To convert P to P2O5, multiply by 2.29 (molecular weight of P2O5 [142] divided by 2P [62]).

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which potassic fertilizer is recommended for chlorine-sensitive crops like tobacco and potato?

Explanation: Potassium Sulphate (SOP) is free from harmful chloride ions and is safe for chlorine-sensitive crops.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the average nitrogen and phosphorus content in standard Farmyard Manure (FYM)?

Explanation: Standard well-decomposed FYM contains approximately 0.5% N, 0.2% P2O5, and 0.5% K2O.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Indore method of composting is a:

Explanation: The Indore method is an aerobic composting process involving periodic turning of organic waste heaps.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary loss pathway of nitrogen in flooded lowland rice soils?

Explanation: In flooded rice soils, applied ammonium nitrogen undergoes nitrification in the oxidized surface layer and denitrification in the underlying reduced layer, resulting in nitrogen loss.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is categorized as a straight nitrogenous fertilizer?

Explanation: Urea supplies only nitrogen, making it a straight nitrogenous fertilizer.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the active microorganism in the Bangalore method of composting?

Explanation: The Bangalore method is primarily an anaerobic composting process conducted in closed trenches.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which element's deficiency causes chlorosis initially on older leaves due to its high mobility in plants?

Explanation: Nitrogen is mobile in plants; deficiency symptoms appear first on older leaves as general yellowing (chlorosis).

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the formula to calculate the exact quantity of urea needed for a specific nitrogen dose?

Explanation: Quantity = (Recommended N dose in kg/ha × 100) / 46.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following terms describes the capacity of soil to hold and exchange nutrient cations?

Explanation: Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC) represents the total capacity of a soil to hold exchangeable cations, vital for nutrient retention.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Integrated Nutrient Management (INM) aims to:

Explanation: INM optimizes the benefits of chemical fertilizers, organic manures, and biofertilizers for sustainable crop production.
