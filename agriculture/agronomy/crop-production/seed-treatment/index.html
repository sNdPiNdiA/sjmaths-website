Seed Treatment in Field Crops: Principles, Practices, and Agronomic Applications

Seed treatment is a critical pre-sowing agronomic practice involving the application of biological, chemical, or physical agents to seeds to suppress, control, or repel pathogens, insects, and other pests, thereby improving germination, seedling vigor, and final crop establishment.

Introduction and Objectives of Seed Treatment

Seed treatment represents the cornerstone of modern precision agronomy and integrated crop management. It involves applying specialized chemical fungicides, bactericides, insecticides, biological agents (such as Rhizobium, Azotobacter, and Trichoderma viride), or micronutrients directly to the seed surface before planting.

  • Primary Objectives: To eradicate seed-borne and soil-borne pathogens, protect emerging seedlings from early-season insect pests, enhance germination uniformity, and inoculate legumes with specific symbiotic nitrogen-fixing bacteria.
  • Economic Efficiency: Chemical economy is achieved because very small quantities of active ingredients are applied per unit area compared to broad-field soil applications, thereby reducing environmental chemical loading.
  • Stress Mitigation: Advanced seed treatments, including polymer coating and bio-priming, improve seedling tolerance against abiotic stresses such as moisture deficit and low soil temperatures.

Types and Categories of Seed Treatments

Agronomic seed treatments are broadly classified into biological, chemical, and physical methods based on the nature of the active agent applied and the specific objective of the treatment.

  • Seed Disinfection: Eradicates pathogens that have infected the interior seed tissues or are present on the seed coat (e.g., hot water treatment for loose smut of wheat caused by Ustilago tritici).
  • Seed Disinfestation: Destroys fungal spores and bacterial cells adhering to the outer surface of the seed coat (e.g., using vitavax or carbendazim).
  • Seed Protection: Protects the seed and young seedling from soil-inhabiting pathogenic fungi and insects during the vulnerable germination phase (e.g., imidacloprid for sucking pests in cotton).
  • Seed Inoculation: Coating legume seeds with effective strains of Bradyrhizobium or cereal seeds with Azospirillum to enhance biological nitrogen fixation and nutrient solubilization.
  • Seed Priming and Bio-priming: Hydration of seeds to a level that permits pre-germination metabolic activity without actual radicle protrusion, often combined with beneficial microorganisms.

Chemical Seed Treatments and Recommended Dosages

Chemical seed dressings are formulated as dry powders (DS), flowable concentrates (FS), or wettable powders (WS). Strict adherence to recommended dosages is vital to prevent phytotoxicity and reduction in seed viability.

  • Fungicidal Treatment: Carbendazim 50% WP or Thiram 75% WP applied at 2.0 to 2.5 g per kilogram of seeds is standard for controlling damping-off diseases and seed rot fungi like Rhizoctonia solani and Pythium species.
  • Insecticidal Treatment: Imidacloprid 600 FS applied at 5.0 ml per kg of seed protects crops like cotton, maize, and soybean against early-season jassids, aphids, and whiteflies. Chlorpyriphos 20 EC is used for soil-dwelling termites in wheat and chickpea.
  • Systemic versus Contact: Systemic fungicides (e.g., carboxin, tebuconazole) penetrate the seed coat and translocate into developing seedlings, providing prolonged protection, whereas contact fungicides (e.g., captan, thiram) remain on the seed surface to form a protective barrier against soil pathogens.

Biological Seed Treatments and Bio-Inoculants

Bio-priming and microbial seed coating integrate eco-friendly disease suppression with enhanced plant nutrition, aligning with sustainable and organic agriculture standards.

  • Fungal Antagonists: Trichoderma viride or Trichoderma harzianum applied at 4 to 10 g per kg of seed effectively controls root rot and wilt pathogens like Fusarium oxysporum through mycoparasitism and antibiosis.
  • Rhizobial Inoculation: Essential for pulse crops (e.g., chickpea, pigeonpea, lentil). Peat-based or liquid inoculants of Rhizobium species are mixed with a sticky solution (jaggery or sugar solution at 10%) to ensure uniform coating at 200 g per 10 kg of seeds.
  • Phosphate Solubilizing Microorganisms (PSM): Inoculation with Bacillus megaterium or Pseudomonas putida solubilizes insoluble inorganic soil phosphates, increasing phosphorus availability in the rhizosphere.

Operational Precautions, Safety, and Storage Standards

Executing seed treatments requires adherence to strict safety protocols and environmental safety regulations to protect farm workers and maintain seed longevity.

  • Sequence of Application: When multiple treatments are applied, insecticides and fungicides should be applied first, followed by microbial inoculants after the chemical layer has completely dried to prevent the deactivation of beneficial bacteria.
  • Moisture Management: Treated seeds must be dried in the shade to bring moisture content down to safe storage limits (generally below 10-12% depending on the crop) to prevent heating and fungal proliferation during storage.
  • Safety Gear: Operators must wear nitrile gloves, protective masks, and goggles during chemical seed dressing. Treated seeds are strictly unfit for human consumption or animal feed and must be dyed distinctively with non-toxic colorants.

Memory Trick / Mnemonic
Sequence of Seed Treatment Application: F.I.B.E.R.
F - Fungicide, I - Insecticide, B - Bio-inoculant (Rhizobium/Trichoderma), E - Drying, R - Sowing.

Follow this exact sequence to ensure chemical fungicides and insecticides do not kill or deactivate the living beneficial microorganisms applied during bio-inoculation.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Hot water seed treatment at 52°C to 54°C for 10 minutes is universally recommended for eradicating loose smut of wheat (Ustilago tritici).
  • Carbendazim 50% WP is applied at a standard rate of 2.0 g per kg of seeds for broad-spectrum fungal control.
  • Imidacloprid 600 FS seed treatment at 5 ml/kg of seed effectively protects cotton seedlings from sucking pests up to 30-45 days after sowing.
  • Rhizobium inoculation for pulses requires a 10% jaggery solution as an adhesive agent to ensure uniform slurry coating on seeds.
  • Trichoderma viride is a potent fungal antagonist used at 4-10 g per kg of seeds against Fusarium wilt in chickpea and pigeonpea.
  • Safe moisture content for storing treated crop seeds is generally between 10% and 12% to prevent mold growth and loss of viability.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Error: Applying Rhizobium inoculant immediately after mixing chemical fungicides on the same seed batch without drying. Fact: Chemicals can kill living rhizobial bacteria; fungicides must dry completely or be applied hours before microbial inoculation.

Concept 1

Definition and Scope of Seed Treatment

Seed treatment encompasses all physical, chemical, and biological procedures applied to seeds from harvest to planting to improve seedling establishment and crop health.

  • Seed Disinfection: Eradication of deep-seated seed-borne pathogens using systemic chemicals or hot water treatment.
  • Seed Protection: Shielding seeds and seedlings from external soil pathogens and insects during germination.
Concept 2

Fungicidal and Insecticidal Dressings

Application of chemical formulations to suppress seed- and soil-borne fungi and early-season insect pests.

  • Carbendazim: Systematic benzimidazole fungicide applied at 2 g/kg seed.
  • Imidacloprid: Neonicotinoid seed dressing insecticide for sucking pest management.
Concept 3

Biological Seed Inoculation

Utilization of beneficial microorganisms to enhance nutrient availability and suppress phytopathogenic fungi.

  • Rhizobium: Symbiotic nitrogen-fixing bacteria specific to legume crops.
  • Trichoderma: Free-living mycoparasitic fungi used for biological control of root rots.
Concept 4

Safety, Precautions, and Storage Standards

Operational guidelines to ensure worker safety, environmental protection, and maintenance of seed germinability.

  • Poison Coloring: Treated seeds must be colored with non-toxic dyes to prevent accidental human consumption.
  • Moisture Limit: Seeds must be dried to below 12% moisture post-treatment to prevent heating and vigor loss.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Seed Treatment (Pre-sowing Seed Dressing)
The application of chemical, physical, or biological agents to seeds to protect against pests and enhance germination.
Seed Disinfection (Eradication of Internal Pathogens)
Destruction of pathogens that have penetrated beneath the seed coat into internal seed tissues.
Seed Disinfestation (Surface Spore Destruction)
Elimination of fungal spores or bacterial cells adhering to the outer surface of the seed coat.
Bio-priming (Controlled Hydration with Microbes)
Hydrating seeds in a suspension of beneficial microorganisms to accelerate germination and induce disease resistance.
Mycoparasitism (Fungal Parasitism of Fungi)
The phenomenon where antagonistic fungi like Trichoderma parasitize and destroy phytopathogenic fungi.
Systemic Fungicide (Translocating Fungicide)
A chemical that is absorbed by the seed and translocated upward into growing plant tissues.
Contact Fungicide (Protective Surface Fungicide)
A chemical that remains on the seed surface, creating a protective chemical barrier against soil pathogens.
Phytotoxicity (Chemical Crop Injury)
Toxic injury to seeds or plants caused by the improper application or excessive dosage of agricultural chemicals.
Rhizobial Inoculation (Legume Bacterial Coating)
Coating legume seeds with symbiotic bacteria to facilitate biological nitrogen fixation.
Loose Smut of Wheat (Ustilago tritici Infection)
An internal seed-borne fungal disease controlled effectively by hot water treatment or systemic carboxin.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Hot water treatment temperature for loose smut of wheat: 52-54°C for 10 minutes.
  • Standard Carbendazim 50% WP dosage for seed dressing: 2.0 g per kg of seed.
  • Imidacloprid 600 FS seed treatment dosage: 5.0 ml per kg of seed.
  • Safe storage moisture content for treated seeds: Below 12%.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Seed treatment is essential for eliminating seed-borne infections and protecting early-season seedlings.
  • Fungicides like Carbendazim and Thiram are standard for controlling damping-off and seed rot.
  • Imidacloprid 600 FS provides reliable protection against early-season sucking insect pests.
  • Bio-inoculants like Rhizobium and Trichoderma promote sustainable nutrition and disease biocontrol.
  • Correct sequencing (Chemicals first, then Bio-inoculants after drying) is critical for treatment efficacy.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Seed Disinfection VS Seed Disinfestation

Disinfection eliminates pathogens inside seed tissues, whereas disinfestation destroys pathogens residing on the outer seed coat surface.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following treatments is specifically used to eradicate internal seed-borne loose smut of wheat (Ustilago tritici)?

Explanation: Hot water treatment at 52-54°C for 10 minutes penetrates the seed coat and kills the dormant mycelium of Ustilago tritici inside wheat grains without destroying embryo germinability.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard recommended dosage of Carbendazim 50% WP for general fungicidal seed treatment per kilogram of seeds?

Explanation: Carbendazim 50% WP is universally recommended at 2.0 to 2.5 grams per kilogram of seeds to effectively control seed- and soil-borne fungal pathogens.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

When applying both chemical seed dressings and bio-inoculants (such as Rhizobium), what is the correct operational sequence?

Explanation: Chemical fungicides and insecticides must be applied and dried first to prevent their toxic action from killing the living bacterial cells in Rhizobium inoculants applied subsequently.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which insecticidal chemical formulation is widely used as a seed treatment at 5 ml/kg of seed to protect cotton seedlings against sucking pests?

Explanation: Imidacloprid 600 FS is a systemic neonicotinoid formulated specifically for seed treatment, providing long-lasting protection against aphids, jassids, and whiteflies in cotton and maize.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary mode of action of Trichoderma viride when used as a biological seed treatment against root rot fungi?

Explanation: Trichoderma viride acts as a biological control agent through mycoparasitism (parasitizing pathogenic fungi), intense competition for space and nutrients, and production of secondary antibiotic metabolites.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the maximum safe moisture content percentage for storing chemically treated seeds to prevent vigor loss and fungal growth?

Explanation: Seeds treated with chemicals or bio-agents must be dried down to a safe storage moisture content of 10% to 12% to prevent respiration heating, mold proliferation, and loss of viability.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which adhesive agent is commonly recommended at 10% concentration for preparing a slurry to coat pulse seeds with Rhizobium inoculant?

Explanation: A 10% jaggery (gur) or sugar solution is used as a sticker to ensure that the peat-based Rhizobium inoculant adheres uniformly to the seed coat without clumping.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What distinguishes seed disinfestation from seed disinfection?

Explanation: Seed disinfestation refers to the destruction of fungal spores and bacteria adhering to the outer surface of the seed, whereas seed disinfection refers to eradicating pathogens that have infected internal seed tissues.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why are treated seeds mandatorily colored with non-toxic dyes before distribution to farmers?

Explanation: Treating chemicals are highly toxic; hence, distinct coloration serves as a visual safety warning to ensure treated seeds are never mistakenly used for human consumption or animal feed.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following fungal pathogens is effectively controlled by seed treatment with Captan or Thiram at 2.5 g/kg seed?

Explanation: Captan and Thiram are contact protective fungicides highly effective against seed rot and damping-off fungi such as Pythium, Rhizoctonia, and Fusarium species.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is 'bio-priming' of seeds?

Explanation: Bio-priming combines seed hydration (priming) with inoculation by beneficial fungi or bacteria, promoting rapid, uniform germination while establishing protective microbes on the spermosphere.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical is classified as a systemic seed-dressing fungicide that protects crops against smuts and bunts?

Explanation: Carboxin is a systemic fungicide renowned for its high efficacy against loose smut and covered smut diseases in cereal crops when applied as a seed treatment.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is phytotoxicity in the context of seed treatment?

Explanation: Phytotoxicity occurs when chemical seed treatments are applied at excessive rates or when seeds have mechanical cracks, leading to reduced germination, abnormal seedling growth, or seed death.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which group of bacteria is universally inoculated onto soybean, chickpea, and lentil seeds via seed treatment?

Explanation: Leguminous crops establish symbiotic relationships with specific Rhizobium or Bradyrhizobium strains, which fix atmospheric nitrogen in root nodules, making seed inoculation standard agronomic practice.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary advantage of seed pelleting over standard seed dressing?

Explanation: Seed pelleting encases the seed in an inert coating material along with nutrients and pesticides, modifying seed geometry for accurate mechanical single-seed placement in precision drills.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which disease of pearl millet (bajra) is managed effectively using systemic metalaxyl seed treatment?

Explanation: Downy mildew of pearl millet, caused by Sclerospora graminicola, is effectively managed by seed treatment with Metalaxyl 35 SD at 6 g per kg of seeds.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What role do Phosphate Solubilizing Microorganisms (PSM) play when applied as seed inoculants?

Explanation: PSMs like Bacillus megaterium secrete organic acids and enzymes that convert insoluble tricalcium phosphates in the soil into plant-available monocalcium phosphate forms.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why is it important to dry treated seeds in the shade rather than under direct scorching sunlight?

Explanation: Direct exposure to high solar radiation and excessive heat can damage embryo viability and destroy living biological agents like Rhizobium or Trichoderma present on the seed coat.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which UP PGT Agriculture exam concept relates to treating sugarcane setts with fungicides before planting?

Explanation: Sugarcane setts are treated with fungicidal solutions (e.g., Carbendazim) before planting to prevent seed- and soil-borne fungal infections, especially red rot caused by Colletotrichum falcatum.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary agronomic benefit of polymer seed coating in modern field crop production?

Explanation: Polymer seed coating binds active chemicals firmly to the seed coat, preventing dust-off during handling, improving flowability in precision planters, and controlling moisture exchange.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What primary agronomic role does Trichoderma harzianum play when applied as a seed bio-priming agent in vegetable and pulse crops?

Solution & Rationale: Trichoderma species are premier biological control agents that colonize the spermosphere and rhizosphere, protecting emerging roots from damping-off fungi via mycoparasitism and ISR.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the critical moisture threshold that must be attained after wet seed treatment or bio-priming before seeds can be safely bagged for storage?

Solution & Rationale: Seeds must be dried back to safe storage moisture limits (10-12%) to prevent respiration heating, microbial spoilage, and loss of seed viability during warehouse storage.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which agronomic term describes the phenomenon where chemical seed treatment causes stunted germination, abnormal root swelling, or seed necrosis due to excessive chemical absorption?

Solution & Rationale: Phytotoxicity refers to the toxic adverse effects of chemical over-application or improper formulation on seed germination and seedling development.
