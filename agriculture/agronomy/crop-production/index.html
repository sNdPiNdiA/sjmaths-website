Agronomy and Field Crops

Comprehensive Agronomy of Field Crop Production for UP TGT/PGT Agriculture

1. Botanical Taxonomy, Origin, and Cytogenetics of Major Field Crops

Crop production science begins with accurate taxonomic identification and genetic understanding of field crops. The Poaceae (Gramineae) family dominates cereal production, while Fabaceae (Leguminosae) governs pulse and oilseed crop systems through symbiotic nitrogen fixation.

  • Rice (Oryza sativa): Family Poaceae, Chromosome number 2n = 2x = 24, South-East Asia origin. Diploid species with AA genome constitution.
  • Wheat (Triticum aestivum): Family Poaceae, Chromosome number 2n = 6x = 42 (Hexaploid), South-West Asia origin. Complex amphidiploid combining genomes of A, B, and D sets.
  • Maize (Zea mays): Family Poaceae, Chromosome number 2n = 2x = 20, Central America (Mexico) origin. Cross-pollinated monoecious cereal with protandry.
  • Chickpea (Cicer arietinum): Family Fabaceae, Chromosome number 2n = 2x = 16, South-West Asia origin. Self-pollinated principal pulse crop of semi-arid tropical regions.

2. Agro-Climatic Zones and Environmental Requirements

Climatic factors such as temperature, photoperiod, and relative humidity dictate the physiological development and geographical distribution of agricultural crops. India is divided into 15 agro-climatic zones by the Planning Commission, with Uttar Pradesh falling under the Upper Gangetic Plain, Middle Gangetic Plain, and Central Plateau and Hills zones.

  • Temperature Thresholds: Most tropical and subtropical crops (C4 plants like maize, sorghum, sugarcane) exhibit optimum photosynthetic efficiency at 30°C to 35°C, whereas temperate crops (C3 plants like wheat and barley) thrive at 20°C to 25°C.
  • Photoperiodism: Crops are classified as short-day plants (rice, soybean), long-day plants (wheat, barley, oat), or day-neutral plants (maize, cotton, sunflower).
  • Growing Degree Days (GDD): Thermal time concept used to predict phenological development stages in cereal crops based on base temperature thresholds.

3. Tillage, Seedbed Preparation, and Sowing Geometry

Proper tillage creates an optimal physical environment for seed germination, root proliferation, and moisture conservation. Conservation tillage and zero tillage are increasingly adopted in rice-wheat cropping systems of Northern India.

  • Tillage Operations: Primary tillage (plowing with moldboard plow or disc plow to invert soil) followed by secondary tillage (harrowing and planking to pulverize clods and level seedbeds).
  • Seed Rate and Spacing: High-yielding wheat varieties require 100 kg/ha seed rate with a standard row spacing of 20 to 22.5 cm. Hybrid rice requires 15 kg/ha, while inbred rice requires 100 kg/ha under direct seeding and 40-50 kg/ha for nursery raising.
  • Seed Treatment: Bio-priming and seed dressing with fungicides like Carbendazim @ 2 g/kg seed or bio-fertilizers such as Rhizobium cultures for legumes and Azotobacter for cereals.

4. Nutrient Management and Fertilizer Calculations

Integrated Nutrient Management (INM) combines inorganic chemical fertilizers, organic manures, and bio-fertilizers to sustain soil health and crop productivity. Balanced fertilization adheres to the 4Rs: Right source, Right rate, Right time, and Right place.

  • Recommended N:P:K Dosages: High-yielding wheat typically demands 120:60:40 kg N:P2O5:K2O per hectare. Rice requires 100:50:50 kg N:P2O5:K2O per hectare.
  • Fertilizer Calculation Math: To apply 120 kg N using Urea (containing 46% N), the quantity of urea required is calculated as: (120 × 100) / 46 = 260.8 kg of urea per hectare. Similarly, for 60 kg P2O5 using DAP (containing 18% N and 46% P2O5), the quantity of DAP required is (60 × 100) / 46 = 130.4 kg per hectare.
  • Splitting of Nitrogen: Nitrogen is applied in splits to prevent leaching and volatilization losses (e.g., basal, crown root initiation, and booting stages in wheat).

5. Irrigation Water Management and Critical Stages

Water management involves optimizing irrigation scheduling based on critical crop growth stages, soil moisture tension, and climatological approaches such as the IW/CPE ratio (Irrigation Water to Cumulative Pan Evaporation).

  • Critical Stages in Wheat: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) at 21-25 days after sowing (DAS) is the most critical stage. Other stages include tillering, jointing, flowering, milk, and dough stages.
  • Critical Stages in Rice: Tillering, Panicle Initiation (PI), Flowering, and Milking stages. Rice requires continuous submergence (5 cm standing water) except during tillering and drainage before harvest.
  • Irrigation Efficiency: Methods include surface irrigation (border strip, check basin, furrow), sprinkler irrigation, and micro-irrigation (drip) to maximize water use efficiency (WUE).

6. Weed Management, Plant Protection, and Crop Economics

Weeds compete with crops for light, water, nutrients, and space, causing significant yield reductions. Integrated Weed Management (IWM) integrates cultural, mechanical, biological, and chemical methods.

  • Weed Flora: Mimetic weeds (Phalaris minor in wheat), parasitic weeds (Striga in sorghum, Orobanche in mustard, Cuscuta in berseem), and broad-leaved weeds (Chenopodium album).
  • Herbicides: Selective herbicides like Sulfosulfuron or Clodinafop-propargyl for grass weeds in wheat; Butachlor or Pretilachlor as pre-emergence herbicides in transplanted rice.
  • Plant Protection: Monitoring Economic Threshold Levels (ETL) for insect pests like stem borers (Scirpophaga incertulas) and diseases like wheat yellow rust (Puccinia striiformis) and rice blast (Magnaporthe oryzae).

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Crown Root Initiation (CRI) stage in wheat occurs exactly 21 to 25 days after sowing and is the most critical irrigation stage.
  • Phalaris minor is known as a mimetic weed of wheat due to morphological similarity in vegetative stages.
  • Urea contains 46% nitrogen, whereas Di-ammonium Phosphate (DAP) contains 18% nitrogen and 46% phosphorus (P2O5).
  • Rice is a typical C3 aquatic/semi-aquatic plant that prefers ammonium (NH4+) form of nitrogen uptake.
  • Maize is a C4 plant exhibiting protandry (male flowers mature before female flowers) to prevent self-pollination.
  • Botany of chickpea (Cicer arietinum) reveals glandular hairs secreting malic and oxalic acid on leaves.
  • Atrazine is a selective pre-emergence herbicide widely used in maize and sugarcane cultivation.
  • The optimum storage moisture grain limit for cereals like wheat and rice is 10% to 12% to prevent fungal infestation.
  • Economic Threshold Level (ETL) refers to the pest population density at which artificial control measures must be initiated to prevent economic injury.
  • Parbhani Kranti is a famous yellow vein mosaic resistant variety of okra developed through interspecific hybridization.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing C3 and C4 photosynthetic pathways: Rice and wheat are C3 plants with lower water use efficiency, while maize and sugarcane are C4 plants with high photosynthetic efficiency under high temperatures.
  • Miscalculating fertilizer quantities: Students often forget to divide the nutrient requirement by the percentage active ingredient (e.g., dividing by 0.46 for urea) when calculating commercial fertilizer weights.
  • Confusing pre-emergence and post-emergence herbicide application timing: Pre-emergence herbicides like Butachlor are applied before crop and weed emergence, whereas Sulfosulfuron is applied post-emergence in standing wheat crops.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Comprehensive Agronomy of Field Crop Production for UP TGT/PGT Agriculture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Taxonomic Classification and Plant Breeding Fundamentals

Understanding the phylogenetic relationships and cytogenetic makeup of crops provides the foundation for hybridization and selection programs.

  • Ploidy Level: Hexaploid wheat (2n=6x=42) possesses AABBDD genomes derived from wild ancestral grasses.
  • Pollination Mechanism: Most cereals are self-pollinated (rice, wheat), whereas maize is cross-pollinated due to separate male and female inflorescences on the same plant.
Concept 2

Tillage Dynamics and Soil Physical Health

Tillage alters bulk density, porosity, and hydraulic conductivity of agricultural soils to optimize root development.

  • Minimum Tillage: Reduces soil erosion and energy consumption by limiting operations to seedbed zone only.
  • Zero Tillage: Direct drilling of seeds into unplowed stubble-mulched soil, highly successful in rice-wheat cropping zones.
Concept 3

Nutrient Stoichiometry and Fertilizer Chemistry

Macronutrients (N, P, K) and secondary/micronutrients govern biochemical pathways, enzymatic activation, and grain filling.

  • DAP Analysis: 18% N and 46% P2O5; provides basal phosphorus requirements for root elongation.
  • MOP (Muriate of Potash): KCl containing 60% K2O, essential for osmoregulation and lodging resistance.
Concept 4

Irrigation Scheduling and Moisture Stress Physiology

Water deficit during critical phenophases causes irreversible yield loss, whereas optimal scheduling maximizes water productivity.

  • CRI Stage: Critical root initiation phase in wheat where nodal roots develop.
  • IW/CPE Ratio: Scientific scheduling index comparing irrigation water depth to cumulative pan evaporation.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Agronomy (Agro-nomos (Greek))
Branch of agricultural science dealing with field crop production and soil management.
CRI Stage (Crown Root Initiation)
Most critical irrigation stage in wheat occurring 21-25 days after sowing.
Protandry (Protandrous Maturation)
Condition where male reproductive organs (anthers) mature before female organs (stigmas), promoting cross-pollination.
Mimetic Weed (Vavilovian Mimicry)
Weeds that mimic cultivated crops in vegetative morphology, such as Phalaris minor in wheat fields.
ETL (Economic Threshold Level)
Pest population density at which control measures must be applied to prevent economic damage.
GDD (Growing Degree Days)
Thermal heat unit index used to estimate phenological development of crops.
Amphidiploid (Allopolyploid)
Polyploid organism containing two or more distinct sets of chromosomes derived from different species.
Inceptisol (USDA Soil Taxonomy Order)
Dominant young alluvial soil order found across the Indo-Gangetic plains of Uttar Pradesh.
PDI (Percent Disease Index)
Quantification metric used in plant pathology to measure disease severity in field crops.
Bulk Density (Dry Soil Mass per Unit Volume)
Indicator of soil compaction, typically ranging between 1.30 to 1.45 g/cm³ in fertile loamy soils.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Clodinafop-propargyl VS Pendimethalin

Clodinafop is a post-emergence grass herbicide for wheat, whereas Pendimethalin is a pre-emergence broad-spectrum herbicide.

Protandry VS Protogyny

Protandry is male organ maturation first (e.g., maize), while protogyny is female organ maturation first (e.g., pearl millet).

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is the most critical irrigation stage for wheat crop production?

Explanation: The Crown Root Initiation (CRI) stage is universally recognized as the most critical period for wheat irrigation. Water stress at this stage severely impairs tillering and root development, leading to significant grain yield reduction.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosome number (2n) of common bread wheat (Triticum aestivum)?

Explanation: Triticum aestivum is an allohexaploid with 6 sets of 7 chromosomes, giving a somatic chromosome number of 2n = 6x = 42.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

How much pure nitrogen is supplied by 260.8 kg of urea fertilizer?

Explanation: Using the formula: (Quantity of Urea × % N) / 100 = (260.8 × 46) / 100 = 120 kg of Nitrogen.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which weed is known as a classic example of a mimetic weed in wheat fields?

Explanation: Phalaris minor (littleseed canarygrass) exhibits Vavilovian mimicry, morphologically resembling wheat plants during early vegetative growth stages.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the photosynthetic pathway operating in maize plants?

Explanation: Maize is a C4 plant characterized by high photosynthetic efficiency, lack of photorespiration, and PEP carboxylase enzyme activity.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended seed rate for normal-sown wheat under irrigated conditions?

Explanation: The standard recommended seed rate for timely sown wheat (Triticum aestivum) is 100 kg/ha to maintain an optimum plant population of 400-500 plants per square meter.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which nutrient element deficiency causes 'Khaira disease' in rice?

Explanation: Khaira disease of rice is caused by Zinc deficiency, characterized by chlorotic patches on lower leaves turning brown/rust-colored.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the origin center of rice (Oryza sativa)?

Explanation: South-East Asia (including India and Myanmar) is widely accepted as the primary center of origin and domestication for cultivated rice.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical herbicide is selectively used for broad-leaved weed control in wheat?

Explanation: 2,4-D (2,4-Dichlorophenoxyacetic acid) is a selective post-emergence herbicide used to control broad-leaved weeds in cereal crops like wheat and maize.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the nutrient composition of Di-ammonium Phosphate (DAP)?

Explanation: Di-ammonium Phosphate (DAP) contains 18% Nitrogen and 46% Phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5).

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which phenomenon describes male flowers maturing before female flowers in maize?

Explanation: Protandry is the maturation of male tassels before female silks in maize, promoting cross-pollination and hybrid vigor.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the average recommended seed rate for hybrid rice cultivation?

Explanation: Hybrid rice seed rate is approximately 15 kg/ha because hybrid seeds possess high vigor and tillering capacity.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which soil order is most prevalent across the Indo-Gangetic alluvial plains of Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: Inceptisols are the dominant soil order of the fertile Indo-Gangetic plains, characterized by recent alluvial deposition.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the ideal safe moisture percentage for long-term grain storage of cereals?

Explanation: Safe storage moisture content for cereals like wheat, paddy, and maize is 10% to 12% to prevent mold growth and insect infestations.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which herbicide is classified as a non-selective total weed killer?

Explanation: Glyphosate is a broad-spectrum, non-selective systemic post-emergence herbicide that destroys all green plant tissues.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the botanical family of Chickpea (Cicer arietinum)?

Explanation: Chickpea belongs to the Fabaceae (Leguminosae) family, sub-family Papilionoideae.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does ETL stand for in modern Integrated Pest Management (IPM)?

Explanation: ETL (Economic Threshold Level) is the pest population density at which control measures must be applied to prevent the pest population from reaching the Economic Injury Level (EIL).

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which climate type is best suited for wheat cultivation?

Explanation: Wheat is primarily a temperate crop that requires cool, moist weather during vegetative growth and warm, bright sunshine during grain filling.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of primary tillage operations like moldboard plowing?

Explanation: Primary tillage aims to open up compacted soil, invert the soil slice to bury weeds and crop residues, and improve aeration.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which micro-organism is used as a bio-fertilizer for non-leguminous cereal crops like rice and wheat?

Explanation: Azotobacter is a free-living diazotrophic bacterium commonly applied as a bio-fertilizer for cereals, vegetables, and cotton crops.

Calculate the quantity of Muriate of Potash (MOP, containing 60% K2O) required to supply 40 kg of K2O per hectare.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following cropping systems exhibits maximum biological nitrogen fixation efficiency under Indian conditions?

Solution & Rationale: Soybean is a legume that fixes substantial atmospheric nitrogen via symbiosis with Bradyrhizobium japonicum, benefiting succeeding wheat crops.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

In scientific agronomy, what is meant by 'Critical Period of Crop-Weed Competition'?

Solution & Rationale: The critical period of crop-weed competition is the time span (usually the first one-third of the crop's life cycle) during which weeds must be kept controlled to prevent yield reduction.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Which specific plant hormone or synthetic growth regulator is commercially utilized to induce uniform ripening and boll opening in cotton?

Solution & Rationale: Ethephon is an ethylene-releasing compound used to accelerate fruit ripening and synchronize boll opening in cotton.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary causal organism of Yellow Rust of wheat?

Solution & Rationale: Yellow stripe rust of wheat is caused by Puccinia striiformis, which produces characteristic linear yellow pustules on leaves.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following sowing methods in rice reduces water requirement and labor dependency significantly compared to traditional puddled transplanting?

Solution & Rationale: Direct Seeded Rice (DSR) saves water, energy, and labor by eliminating nursery raising, puddling, and manual transplanting.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the genomic constitution of hexaploid wheat (Triticum aestivum)?

Solution & Rationale: Hexaploid bread wheat has 42 chromosomes combining genomes from three diploid ancestral species (AABBDD).

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

Which weed species exhibits complete root parasitism on crops like tobacco and tomato?

Solution & Rationale: Orobanche (broomrape) is a total root parasite attacking solanaceous crops like tobacco, tomato, and potato.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary purpose of 'Earthing up' agronomic practice in sugarcane and potato production?

Solution & Rationale: Earthing up provides physical support against lodging, covers tubers/stalks, and creates furrow channels for irrigation.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which climatic phenomenon is measured using Growing Degree Days (GDD)?

Solution & Rationale: Growing Degree Days (GDD) quantify thermal time accumulation required by crops to transition between developmental stages.
