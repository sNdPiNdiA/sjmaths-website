Botanical Taxonomy, Origin, and Cytogenetics of Major Field Crops

Understanding the taxonomic classification, center of origin, and ploidy level (chromosomal complement) of field crops is fundamental for plant production agronomy and competitive examinations like UP TGT/PGT Agriculture.

  • Rice (Oryza sativa): Family Poaceae (Gramineae), diploid with 2n = 2x = 24, originating in South/Southeast Asia.
  • Wheat (Triticum aestivum): Family Poaceae, hexaploid (2n = 6x = 42) with genome formula AABBDD, originating in Southwestern Asia (Levant/Fertile Crescent).
  • Maize (Zea mays): Family Poaceae, diploid (2n = 2x = 20), originating in Central America (Mexico).
  • Chickpea (Cicer arietinum): Family Fabaceae (Leguminosae), diploid (2n = 2x = 16), originating in Southwestern Asia (Turkey/Syria).
  • Mustard (Brassica juncea): Family Brassicaceae (Cruciferae), amphidiploid (2n = 4x = 36, AABB), originating in Central Asia/China.

Soil, Agro-Climatic Requirements, and Tillage Operations

Crop establishment relies heavily on optimum edaphic and climatic conditions. Tillage prepares the seedbed to ensure adequate aeration, moisture retention, and weed suppression.

  • Soil Reaction (pH): Most field crops perform best in a neutral to slightly acidic pH range of 6.0 to 7.5. Salt-affected soils require amendments like gypsum for sodic soils and agricultural lime for acidic soils.
  • Temperature Thresholds: Germination and vegetative phases have cardinal temperatures (minimum, optimum, maximum). For instance, wheat requires an optimum temperature of 20°C to 25°C for grain filling, whereas tropical crops like rice and maize thrive at 30°C to 35°C.
  • Primary and Secondary Tillage: Mouldboard ploughs and disc ploughs are utilized for primary inversion tillage, followed by disc harrows, cultivators, and plankers for secondary tillage to achieve a fine crumb structure and proper seed-soil contact.

Seed Rate, Sowing Geometry, and Seed Treatment Protocols

Precision in seed rate and planting geometry avoids inter- and intra-row competition, optimizing solar radiation interception and nutrient uptake.

  • Seed Rate Standards:
    • Wheat: 100 kg/ha (delayed sowing: 125 kg/ha).
    • Rice (Transplanted): 40-50 kg/ha for nursery.
    • Hybrid Maize: 20-25 kg/ha.
    • Soybean (Glycine max): 75-80 kg/ha.
  • Plant Geometry: Spacing configuration directly dictates plant population density. For example, wheat is sown at 20-22.5 cm row spacing, while maize utilizes 60 cm × 20 cm or 75 cm × 20 cm.
  • Seed Treatments: Fungicidal dressing with Carbendazim @ 2.5 g/kg seed suppresses seed-borne pathogens. Rhizobium and Azotobacter inoculations are mandatory for legume and cereal seeds respectively to enhance biological nitrogen fixation.

Nutrient Management and Fertilizer Recommendation Calculations

Balanced fertilization involves the application of macro-nutrients (N, P, K) and micro-nutrients based on soil testing and crop demand.

  • Recommended N:P:K Doses: High-yielding wheat varieties typically require 120:60:40 kg N:P₂O₅:K₂O per ha.
  • Urea Calculation Example: To supply 120 kg N per hectare using Urea (containing 46% N), the required mass of urea is calculated as: (120 / 46) × 100 = 260.87 kg/ha.
  • DAP Calculation Example: To supply 60 kg P₂O₅ per hectare using Di-ammonium Phosphate (containing 18% N and 46% P₂O₅), the required mass of DAP is: (60 / 46) × 100 = 130.43 kg/ha. The nitrogen supplied through DAP must be subtracted from the total N requirement before calculating the urea dose.
  • Split Application: Nitrogen is applied in splits (e.g., basal, tillering, and flowering stages in cereals) to minimize leaching and volatilization losses.

Crop Protection: Weeds, Insect Pests, Diseases, and IPM

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) combines cultural, mechanical, biological, and chemical methods to maintain pest populations below the Economic Threshold Level (ETL).

  • Weed Management: Grassy weeds like Phalaris minor in wheat are managed using herbicides such as Clodinafop-propargyl or Sulfosulfuron. Broad-leaved weeds are controlled using 2,4-D.
  • Insect Pests: Stem borers (Chilo partellus in maize) and pod borers (Helicoverpa armigera in chickpea) cause substantial yield reductions. Pheromone traps and Bacillus thuringiensis applications form key components of IPM.
  • Major Diseases:
    • Wheat Yellow Rust: Caused by Puccinia striiformis.
    • Rice Blast: Caused by Magnaporthe oryzae.
    • Loose Smut of Wheat: Caused by Ustilago tritici (an internally seed-borne fungus controlled by hot water treatment or systemic fungicides like Carboxin).

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The most critical irrigation stage in wheat is Crown Root Initiation (CRI), occurring at 21 Days After Sowing (DAS).
  • Rice grain is botanically termed as a caryopsis, and the inflorescence is a panicle.
  • Phalaris minor is a major mimicry weed found in wheat fields, belonging to family Poaceae.
  • Urea contains 46% Nitrogen, while Single Super Phosphate (SSP) contains 16% P₂O₅ and 12% Sulfur.
  • Botanical name of Indian mustard is Brassica juncea with chromosome number 2n = 36.
  • Loose smut of wheat (Ustilago tritici) is an internally seed-borne disease.
  • The Economic Threshold Level (ETL) is the pest population density at which control measures must be initiated to prevent economic injury.
  • Safe moisture content for safe storage of cereal grains is 10% to 12%.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the critical irrigation stage of wheat (CRI at 21 DAS) with flowering stage; while flowering is also sensitive, CRI is universally the most critical.
  • Assuming urea contains 50% Nitrogen instead of the exact chemical standard of 46% N.
  • Treating loose smut of wheat with contact fungicides; because it is internally seed-borne, it requires systemic fungicides like Carboxin or hot water treatment.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Caryopsis (Dry indehiscent fruit of Poaceae)
A dry, one-seeded fruit in which the seed coat is fused with the pericarp wall, typical of cereal crops.
CRI Stage (Crown Root Initiation)
The most critical stage for irrigation in wheat occurring approximately 21 days after sowing.
Mimicry Weed (Vavilovian mimic)
A weed that shares morphological and physiological similarities with a cultivated crop, such as Phalaris minor in wheat.
ETL (Economic Threshold Level)
The pest density at which control action must be taken to prevent economic damage.
Amphidiploid (Allotetraploid)
An organism having two complete sets of chromosomes derived from two different species, such as Brassica juncea.
Panicle (Branched racemose inflorescence)
The characteristic inflorescence structure found in rice and sorghum.
Tillage (Mechanical manipulation of soil)
The agricultural preparation of soil by mechanical agitation of various types, such as digging, stirring, and overturning.
Seed Rate (Mass of seeds per unit area)
The quantity of seeds required to be sown per hectare to obtain optimum plant population density.
Nitrification Inhibitor (Biological/Chemical compound)
Substances like Neem oil or Nitrapyrin that retard the oxidation of ammonium to nitrate in soil.
Storage Moisture (Safe grain moisture percentage)
The moisture content (usually 10-12%) at which grains can be stored safely without fungal or insect infestation.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Internal Seed-Borne Disease VS External Seed-Borne Disease

Internal seed-borne pathogens (e.g., loose smut of wheat) reside inside the embryo and require systemic fungicides or hot water, whereas external pathogens reside on the seed coat and are managed by surface seed dressings.

Which herbicide is classified as a selective grass killer (graminicide) used against Phalaris minor in wheat?

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the genome constitution of hexaploid wheat (Triticum aestivum)?

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

Why is loose smut of wheat managed using systemic fungicides rather than surface contact fungicides?

Solution & Rationale: Loose smut (Ustilago tritici) resides inside the seed embryo, requiring systemic chemicals (like Carboxin) that translocate into plant tissues.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Solution & Rationale: Phalaris minor mimics wheat morphologically during early vegetative stages, making manual weeding difficult.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary purpose of establishing an Economic Threshold Level (ETL) in IPM?

Solution & Rationale: ETL ensures pesticide application occurs only when the cost of damage exceeds the cost of chemical control.
