Introduction and Agronomic Significance of Seed Rate

Seed rate refers to the exact weight or number of seeds required to sow a unit area of land (typically expressed in kg ha-1 or number of seeds per square meter) to achieve optimum plant population and maximum economic yield.

  • Optimum Plant Population: Ensures efficient utilization of solar radiation, soil moisture, and applied nutrients without excessive intra-crop competition.
  • Economic Yield: An excessively high seed rate leads to mutual shading, lodging, skinny culms, and increased susceptibility to pests and diseases. Conversely, a sub-optimal seed rate results in vacant patches, poor weed suppression, and reduced final harvest.
  • Standard Metrics: Seed rate calculations depend directly on test weight (thousand grain weight), desired plant population, germination percentage, and purity percentage.

Mathematical Formulation of Seed Rate

Agronomists and agricultural scientists use standard mathematical formulas to compute actual field seed requirements based on laboratory parameters.

  • General Formula:
    Seed Rate (kg/ha) = [Target Plant Population per m2 × Test Weight (g) × 100] / [Germination (%) × Purity (%) × 1000]
  • Correction for Germination and Purity: Real seed value (RSV) must always be accounted for.
    Real Seed Value (%) = [Germination (%) × Purity (%)] / 100
  • Adjustment for Hybrid vs. Open-Pollinated Crops: Hybrid seeds are often expensive and sown at lower seed rates with wider spacing, whereas broadcasted small-seeded cereals (e.g., Triticum aestivum, Oryza sativa) require higher seed rates.

Crop-Specific Seed Rate Standards (ICAR & SAUs)

Different sowing methods dictate varying seed rates for major field crops under Indian agro-climatic conditions.

  • Cereals: Wheat (Triticum aestivum) normal sowing requires 100 kg ha-1; late-sown wheat requires 125 kg ha-1. Rice (Oryza sativa) transplanting needs 40–50 kg ha-1 (wet nursery), while direct-seeded rice (DSR) requires 80–100 kg ha-1. Hybrid rice demands only 15 kg ha-1.
  • Pulses: Chickpea (Cicer arietinum) desi varieties require 75–80 kg ha-1 (small seeded) and 100 kg ha-1 (bold seeded); kabuli varieties require 100–120 kg ha-1. Pigeonpea (Cajanus cajan) sole cropping requires 15–20 kg ha-1.
  • Oilseeds: Mustard (Brassica juncea) requires 4–5 kg ha-1. Soybean (Glycine max) requires 75–80 kg ha-1. Groundnut (Arachis hypogaea) requires 100 kg ha-1 (kernels) or 120–140 kg ha-1 (pods).

Factors Influencing Seed Rate Selection

Agronomist must adjust baseline seed rates depending on several biotic and abiotic field variables.

  • Test Weight (Test Size): Bold-seeded varieties naturally require higher weight-based seed rates compared to fine-seeded varieties to attain the same number of individual viable seeds.
  • Time of Sowing: Delayed sowing generally requires a 20-25% increase in seed rate to compensate for reduced tillering capacity and lower thermal units.
  • Method of Sowing: Line sowing and dibbling conserve seed and lower seed rates compared to broadcasting, which suffers from uneven depth and high mortality.
  • Seed Viability: Low germination percentages demand proportional upward adjustments in seed rates to maintain required plant geometry.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The standard seed rate for normal-sown wheat (Triticum aestivum) is 100 kg/ha, which is increased to 125 kg/ha under late-sown conditions.
  • Hybrid rice (Oryza sativa) requires an exceptionally low seed rate of 15 kg/ha due to high tillering potential and single-seed seedling transplanting.
  • Kabuli chickpea (Cicer arietinum) requires a higher seed rate (100–120 kg/ha) compared to desi chickpea (75–80 kg/ha) due to larger seed size and lower test weight.
  • Groundnut kernel seed rate is 100 kg/ha, whereas pod seed rate is 120–140 kg/ha.
  • Real Seed Value (RSV) formula is (Germination % × Purity %) / 100.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the seed rate of bold-seeded and small-seeded legumes; bold seeds require higher weight per hectare despite lower individual counts.
  • Assuming seed rate remains constant regardless of sowing delay; late sowing always demands an increased seed rate to offset poor vegetative growth duration.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Seed Rate (Mass or count per unit area)
The total quantity of seed sown per unit land area to establish an optimum crop canopy.
Test Weight (Thousand Grain Weight (g))
The weight of 1000 seeds expressed in grams, vital for seed rate calculations.
Real Seed Value (RSV (%))
The percentage of pure, viable seeds present in a seed lot.
Plant Population (Stand Density)
Number of plants established per unit ground area (usually per square meter or hectare).
Broadcasting (Random Scatter Sowing)
Manual scattering of seeds across the soil surface resulting in highest seed rate requirement.
Dibbling (Precision Hole Sowing)
Placing seeds manually or mechanically at precise depths and spacings, requiring minimum seed rate.
Germination Percentage (Viability Index)
Proportion of seeds that produce normal seedlings under favorable laboratory conditions.
Seed Purity (Physical Purity (%))
Percentage by weight of pure crop seeds relative to inert matter, weed seeds, and other crop seeds.
CRI Stage (Crown Root Initiation)
Critical irrigation stage in wheat occurring 20-25 days after sowing.
Lodging (Stem Fallover)
Displacement of crop stems from their vertical upright position, often caused by high seed rates and excess nitrogen.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended seed rate for normal-sown wheat (Triticum aestivum) under irrigated conditions in India?

Explanation: The standard seed rate for timely sown wheat under irrigated conditions is 100 kg/ha to maintain an optimum plant population of around 400-500 spikes per square meter.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Why is the seed rate increased by 20-25% under late-sown wheat conditions?

Explanation: In late-sown wheat, the vegetative phase is compressed, reducing individual tillering capacity. Therefore, a higher seed rate (125 kg/ha) is necessary to ensure adequate final spike density.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the seed rate requirement for hybrid rice cultivation?

Explanation: Hybrid rice seed rate is only 15 kg/ha because hybrid seeds have high vigor, greater tillering capacity, and are typically transplanted as single seedlings per hill.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Calculate the Real Seed Value (RSV) given that Germination is 90% and Purity is 95%.

Explanation: RSV = (90 × 95) / 100 = 8550 / 100 = 85.5%.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following pulses requires a higher seed rate for Kabuli varieties compared to Desi varieties?

Explanation: Kabuli chickpea has a higher test weight (larger seed size) and therefore requires 100-120 kg/ha compared to 75-80 kg/ha for desi chickpea.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended seed rate for Indian Mustard (Brassica juncea)?

Explanation: Indian mustard requires 4-5 kg/ha when sown in lines spaced 30 cm apart.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which sowing method generally requires the highest seed rate due to high seedling mortality and uneven depth?

Explanation: Broadcasting results in uneven seed depth, bird damage, and high seedling mortality, thus requiring a higher seed rate than line sowing or dibbling.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the groundnut (Arachis hypogaea) kernel seed rate per hectare?

Explanation: The seed rate for groundnut kernels is 100 kg/ha, whereas when using unshelled pods, it is 120-140 kg/ha.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which factor does NOT directly affect the calculation of crop seed rate?

Explanation: Soil pH affects nutrient availability and crop establishment, but it is not a direct variable in the mathematical seed rate formula.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended seed rate for Soybean (Glycine max)?

Explanation: Soybean requires 75-80 kg/ha to maintain a proper plant population in rows spaced 45 cm apart.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the seed rate for pearl millet (Pennisetum glaucum) under normal rainfed conditions?

Explanation: Pearl millet requires 4-5 kg/ha of seed for optimum crop stand in arid and semi-arid tracts.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary consequence of using an excessively high seed rate in cereal crops?

Explanation: Excessive seed rates lead to intense competition for light and nutrients, causing weak, thin stems that easily lodge and favor fungal infections.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the seed rate for Berseem (Trifolium alexandrinum), a major winter forage crop?

Explanation: Berseem requires 25-30 kg/ha of seed to establish a dense, productive forage canopy.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which formula correctly represents Real Seed Value (RSV)?

Explanation: Real Seed Value reflects the percentage of pure, viable seeds in a lot and is calculated as (Germination % × Purity %) / 100.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the seed rate for Sugarcane sett planting (three-budded setts) per hectare?

Explanation: Sugarcane requires about 35,000 to 40,000 three-budded setts (or 60,000 two-budded setts), weighing roughly 35-40 quintals per hectare.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the seed rate for Sorghum (Sorghum bicolor) grown for grain production?

Explanation: Sorghum for grain production requires 9-12 kg/ha, while fodder sorghum requires 40-50 kg/ha for high green biomass.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

How does seed size impact the seed rate when calculated on a weight basis?

Explanation: Because bolder seeds have a higher test weight (fewer seeds per unit weight), a greater weight of seed is required to achieve the same target plant population.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the seed rate for Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum) using delinted seeds?

Explanation: Delinted cotton seeds require 12-15 kg/ha, whereas fuzzy seeds require 18-20 kg/ha.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following crops has the lowest seed rate per hectare?

Explanation: Transplanted tomato requires only 400-500 grams of seed per hectare for nursery raising, making its field seed equivalent the lowest among field crops.

Test Question 1 of 10 TGT Standard

If a wheat seed lot has a germination of 80% and purity of 90%, what is its Real Seed Value (RSV)?

Solution & Rationale: RSV = (80 × 90) / 100 = 7200 / 100 = 72%.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following agronomic practices necessitates an increase in the recommended seed rate?

Solution & Rationale: Delayed sowing shortens growth duration and tillering, requiring a 20-25% increase in seed rate.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the standard seed rate for transplanted Rice (Oryza sativa) using wet nursery?

Solution & Rationale: Conventional transplanted rice requires 40-50 kg/ha of seed for nursery raising to plant one hectare.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Why do hybrid crops generally require lower seed rates than open-pollinated varieties?

Solution & Rationale: Hybrid vigor (heterosis) produces robust plants that require wider spacing and fewer individual seeds.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the seed rate for Sunflower (Helianthus annuus)?

Solution & Rationale: Sunflower requires 8-10 kg/ha of seed to establish a population of around 55,000-60,000 plants per hectare.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

In calculating adjusted seed rate for suboptimal germination, if the recommended seed rate is 100 kg/ha at 100% germination, what is the adjusted seed rate if germination drops to 80%?

Solution & Rationale: Adjusted Seed Rate = (Recommended Seed Rate × 100) / Actual Germination % = (100 × 100) / 80 = 125 kg/ha.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Which pulse crop has the highest seed rate requirement among major grain legumes?

Solution & Rationale: Kabuli chickpea requires 100-120 kg/ha, which is higher than most other grain legumes due to large seed mass.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the seed rate of Potato (Solanum tuberosum) tubers per hectare for commercial planting?

Solution & Rationale: Potato tuber seed rate is 25-30 quintals (2500-3000 kg) per hectare using whole or cut tubers weighing 25-50 g.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the seed rate for fodder Sorghum when sown for high green forage yield?

Solution & Rationale: Forage sorghum is sown at a high seed rate of 40-50 kg/ha (broadcasting or close drilling) to suppress branching and produce fine, succulent stems.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following statements regarding seed rate and plant population is scientifically accurate?

Solution & Rationale: An optimum plant population ensures maximum utilization of light, water, and nutrients, avoiding both under-utilization and overcrowding stress.
