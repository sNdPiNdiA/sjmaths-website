Botanical Classification, Taxonomy, and Cytogenetics of Major Food Grains

Food grains primarily comprise cereals and millets belonging to the family Poaceae (Gramineae). Understanding their exact botanical nomenclature, chromosomal constitution, and center of origin forms the bedrock of agronomic science tested in competitive exams.

  • Rice (Paddy): Oryza sativa, 2n = 24, diploid, native to Tropical and Subtropical Asia.
  • Wheat: Triticum aestivum (Hexaploid, bread wheat, 2n = 42), Triticum durum (Tetraploid, macaroni wheat, 2n = 28), and Triticum dicoccum (Emmer wheat, 2n = 28). Native to South-Western Asia (Levant/Fertile Crescent).
  • Maize: Zea mays, 2n = 20, cross-pollinated C4 plant, native to Central America (Mexico).
  • Pearl Millet (Bajra): Pennisetum glaucum (syn. Pennisetum typhoides), 2n = 14, protogynous cross-pollinated crop, native to Tropical Africa.
  • Sorghum (Jowar): Sorghum bicolor, 2n = 20, native to Central and East Africa.

Soil, Climate, and Agro-Ecological Requirements

Food grain productivity is strictly governed by edaphic and meteorological parameters. Temperature thresholds and soil reactions dictate crop selection across the Indo-Gangetic plains and plateau regions of Uttar Pradesh.

  • Rice: Thrives in clay to clay-loam soils with high water retention capacity. Optimum temperature ranges from 21°C to 37°C. It is a typical short-day plant requiring standing water during vegetative growth.
  • Wheat: Prefers well-drained fertile alluvial and loam soils with neutral pH (6.5 to 7.5). Cool, moist weather during vegetative growth and warm, bright sunshine during grain filling (maturity) are essential. Optimum sowing temperature is 20°C to 22°C.
  • Maize: Requires deep, fertile, well-drained soils rich in organic matter. Intolerant to waterlogging. Optimum temperature ranges from 21°C to 27°C.
  • Millets (Bajra and Sorghum): Highly drought-tolerant, grown predominantly in light sandy to medium-textured soils under rainfed conditions. Sorghum requires 26°C to 30°C for optimum germination and growth.

Cultural Operations, Seed Rate, Spacing, and Sowing Geometry

Precise crop establishment ensures optimal plant population and resource utilization. Seed rate and geometry are high-yield areas for UP TGT/PGT questions.

  • Rice (Transplanted): Seed rate is 40-50 kg/ha for conventional nurseries, and 20 kg/ha for Hybrid rice. Nursery area required is 1/10th of the main field (1000 m^2 per hectare). Spacing: 20 × 10 cm or 22.5 × 10 cm.
  • Wheat: Standard seed rate is 100 kg/ha for timely sowing. For late-sown conditions or broad-bed furrow planting, seed rate is increased to 125 kg/ha. Spacing: Row-to-row distance of 20-22.5 cm. Depth of sowing: 4-5 cm.
  • Maize: Seed rate is 20-25 kg/ha for grain purpose and 40 kg/ha for fodder. Spacing: 60 × 20 cm or 75 × 20 cm.
  • Seed Treatment: Essential prophylactic step. Wheat seeds treated with Vitavax (carboxin) @ 2.5 g/kg seed against loose smut. Rice seeds soaked in 0.1% Carbendazim + 2% saltwater for specific gravity separation.

Nutrient Management and Fertilizer Recommendation Calculations

Macronutrients (N, P, K) and micronutrients (Zinc, Iron) dictate yield ceilings. Split applications prevent leaching and volatilization losses.

  • Rice Nutrient Schedule: General recommendation is 100:60:40 kg NPK/ha. Nitrogen is applied in 3 splits: 50% basal, 25% at tillering, and 25% at panicle initiation. Zinc deficiency causes 'Khaira disease of rice', corrected by a foliar spray of 0.5% Zinc Sulfate + 0.25% Lime at 15-20 days after transplanting (DAT).
  • Wheat Nutrient Schedule: Standard recommendation is 120:60:40 kg NPK/ha. Full P and K and 1/3rd N are applied as basal. The remaining 2/3rd N is top-dressed at Crown Root Initiation (CRI) and tillering stages.
  • Fertilizer Calculation Math: To apply 120 kg N using Urea (containing 46% N), the quantity of urea required is calculated as: (120 / 46) × 100 = 260.86 kg/ha. Similarly, for 60 kg P_2O_5 using Di-ammonium Phosphate (DAP containing 18% N and 46% P_2O_5), DAP required is (60 / 46) × 100 = 130.43 kg/ha.

Critical Irrigation Stages and Water Management

Moisture stress at critical physiological phases causes irreversible yield penalties. Irrigation scheduling based on IW:CPE ratio and critical stages is vital.

  • Wheat Critical Stages: Crown Root Initiation (CRI - 20-25 Days After Sowing [DAS]), Tillering (40-45 DAS), Late Jointing (60-65 DAS), Flowering (85-90 DAS), Milk stage (100-105 DAS), and Dough stage (120 DAS). CRI is the most critical stage.
  • Rice Critical Stages: Tillering, Panicle Initiation (PI), Flowering, and Soft Dough stage. Submergence of 5 cm standing water is maintained from transplanting up to 10 days before harvest.
  • Maize Critical Stages: Tasseling and Silking stages are extremely sensitive to moisture stress; water stress during silking causes poor pollination and barren cobs.

Crop Protection: Weeds, Pests, Diseases, and IPM

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) combines cultural, mechanical, biological, and chemical methods to keep pests below Economic Threshold Levels (ETL).

  • Weed Management in Wheat: Phalaris minor (Little seed canary grass, a mimicry weed) is controlled using Isoproturon @ 0.75-1.0 kg a.i./ha or Clodinafop-propargyl @ 60 g a.i./ha post-emergence (30-35 DAS). Broad-leaved weeds like Chenopodium album are controlled using 2,4-D @ 0.5 kg a.i./ha.
  • Pests of Rice: Stem borer (Scirpophaga incertulas) causing dead hearts and white heads; Brown Planthopper (Nilaparvata lugens) causing hopper burn. Controlled by Carbofuran 3G or Imidacloprid.
  • Diseases of Wheat: Yellow rust (Puccinia striiformis), Brown rust (Puccinia triticina), Black rust (Puccinia graminis tritici), and Loose smut of wheat (Ustilago tritici - an internal seed-borne fungus).
  • Diseases of Rice: Blast (Magnaporthe oryzae), Bacterial Leaf Blight (Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae), and Sheath blight (Rhizoctonia solani).

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Khaira disease in rice is caused by Zinc (Zn) deficiency, discovered by Y.L. Nene at Pantnagar in 1966.
  • Crown Root Initiation (CRI) in wheat occurs at 20-25 Days After Sowing and is the single most critical stage for irrigation.
  • Loose smut of wheat (Ustilago tritici) is an internally seed-borne fungus and cannot be controlled by contact fungicides.
  • Phalaris minor is a classic example of a weed showing mimicry to wheat plants during the vegetative stage.
  • Pearl millet (Bajra) is highly protogynous, meaning female organs mature before male organs to promote cross-pollination.
  • Maize is a monoecious plant possessing separate male inflorescence (tassel) and female inflorescence (cob) on the same plant.
  • The most critical moisture-sensitive stages in maize are Tasseling and Silking.
  • Rice grain is botanically a caryopsis, and the edible milled rice is primarily endosperm.
  • DAP fertilizer contains 18% Nitrogen and 46% Phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5).
  • Isoproturon is a selective systemic herbicide widely used in wheat for controlling grassy weeds like Phalaris minor.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the critical irrigation stage of wheat: CRI occurs at 20-25 DAS, NOT at tillering (45 DAS) or flowering (90 DAS).
  • Assuming loose smut of wheat is externally seed-borne; in reality, it is internally seed-borne, necessitating systemic fungicides like Carboxin.
  • Confusing maize pollination types: Maize is monoecious with cross-pollination due to protandry (tassels shed pollen before silks emerge), NOT cleistogamous.
  • Mistaking Khaira disease of rice as a fungal or bacterial pathogen; it is a physiological/nutritional disorder due to Zinc deficiency.

Concept 1

Botanical and Cytogenetic Foundations

Cereals belong to Poaceae and exhibit specialized inflorescences. Hexaploid bread wheat possesses 42 chromosomes, whereas diploid rice has 24 chromosomes.

  • Ploidy Level: Hexaploid (2n=6x=42) for bread wheat.
  • Inflorescence: Panicle in rice, Spike in wheat, Tassel/Cob in maize.
Concept 2

Seed Rate and Planting Geometry Standards

Establishing the correct plant population requires calculating exact seed rates, germination percentages, and spacing dimensions.

  • Wheat Seed Rate: 100 kg/ha for timely sowing.
  • Rice Nursery Area: 1/10th of total main field area (1000 m^2/ha).
Concept 3

Nutrient Stoichiometry and Fertilizer Calculations

Balanced fertilization utilizing primary NPK fertilizers based on active nutrient percentage formulations.

  • Urea Grade: Contains 46% Nitrogen.
  • DAP Grade: Contains 18% Nitrogen and 46% P_2O_5.
Concept 4

Irrigation Scheduling and Critical Phenophases

Water management must align with critical crop stages to prevent terminal drought stress and sterility.

  • CRI Stage: 20-25 DAS in wheat.
  • Silking Stage: Most sensitive water-demand phase in maize.
Concept 5

Phytosanitary and Weed Management

Control of noxious weed flora like Phalaris minor and management of epidemics like wheat rusts and rice blast.

  • Mimicry Weed: Phalaris minor in wheat fields.
  • Khaira Correction: 0.5% Zinc Sulfate + 0.25% Lime spray.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Caryopsis (Dry indehiscent fruit of Poaceae)
A dry, one-seeded fruit in which the seed coat is fused with the pericarp wall.
CRI Stage (Crown Root Initiation)
The most critical irrigation stage in wheat occurring 20-25 days after sowing where nodal roots emerge near soil surface.
Protandry (Proterandrous flower maturation)
A condition in monoecious plants like maize where male organs (tassels) mature and shed pollen before female organs (silks) become receptive.
Protogyny (Proterogynous flower maturation)
A condition where female reproductive organs mature before male organs, prominent in pearl millet to ensure cross-pollination.
Hopper Burn (Insect feeding phytotoxicity symptom)
Drying and browning of rice plants in circular patches caused by heavy feeding of sap by Brown Planthoppers.
Dead Heart (Stem borer larval damage symptom)
Sympton in cereals where central shoot withers and dries up due to stem borer larval tunneling.
White Ear (Reproductive phase stem borer damage)
Empty, bleached white panicles in rice caused by stem borer attack during the heading stage.
Khaira Disease (Zinc deficiency disorder in Oryza sativa)
Physiological disorder characterized by chlorotic patches turning brown/rust-colored on lower leaves of rice seedlings.
Mimicry Weed (Vavilovian mimicry in agriculture)
Weeds that closely resemble cultivated crops in vegetative morphology, such as Phalaris minor in wheat fields.
Tasseling (Male inflorescence emergence in Zea mays)
The emergence and opening of the tassel (male flower) in maize plant marking the onset of flowering.
Silking (Female flower stigma emergence)
The emergence of styles and stigmas from the maize husk, extremely sensitive to moisture stress.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Cereals belong to Poaceae family, possessing caryopsis fruit and high carbohydrate content.
  • Wheat is hexaploid (2n=42), rice is diploid (2n=24), and maize is diploid (2n=20).
  • CRI stage in wheat and Panicle Initiation in rice are non-negotiable critical irrigation stages.
  • Khaira disease of rice is caused by Zinc deficiency and rectified using Zinc Sulfate spray.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Loose Smut of Wheat VS Flag Smut of Wheat

Loose smut is internally seed-borne (Ustilago tritici), whereas flag smut is soil and seed-borne (Urocystis agropyri).

Protandry VS Protogyny

Protandry means male organs mature first (Maize), while protogyny means female organs mature first (Pearl Millet).

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the somatic chromosome number (2n) of hexaploid bread wheat (Triticum aestivum)?

Explanation: Bread wheat (Triticum aestivum) is an allohexaploid with 6 sets of 7 chromosomes, giving a somatic chromosome number of 2n = 6x = 42. Durum wheat is tetraploid (2n=28) and diploid einkorn is 2n=14.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is the most critical irrigation stage in wheat crop?

Explanation: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) occurring 20-25 DAS is the most critical stage for wheat irrigation. Missing irrigation at this stage causes drastic reduction in tillering and final grain yield.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Khaira disease of rice, characterized by rusting and chlorosis of leaves, is caused by:

Explanation: Khaira disease in rice is a physiological disorder caused by Zinc (Zn) deficiency in soils, corrected by foliar application of Zinc Sulfate and lime.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the percentage of nitrogen present in commercial Urea fertilizer?

Explanation: Pure commercial grade urea contains 46% nitrogen by weight in amide form.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Phalaris minor, a major problematic weed in wheat fields, is classified as a:

Explanation: Phalaris minor (Little seed canary grass) exhibits Vavilovian mimicry as its morphological features resemble wheat seedlings during early vegetative phases.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop is known as the 'Queen of Cereals' due to its high genetic yield potential and multiple uses?

Explanation: Maize (Zea mays) is globally recognized as the 'Queen of Cereals' due to its highest genetic yield potential among cereals and versatility as food, feed, and industrial raw material.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard recommended seed rate for timely-sown wheat per hectare?

Explanation: The standard seed rate for timely sown wheat under normal conditions is 100 kg/ha to achieve a target plant population of 400-50ers/m².

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Loose smut of wheat (Ustilago tritici) is transmitted through:

Explanation: Loose smut of wheat is an internally seed-borne disease where the fungus resides inside the seed embryo, requiring systemic fungicides like carboxin for control.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the optimum nursery area required for transplanting one hectare of conventional rice field?

Explanation: The standard nursery area required for one hectare of transplanted rice is 1000 square meters (1/10th of a hectare).

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which plant hormone or synthetic chemical is used for eradicating broad-leaved weeds in wheat crops without harming cereals?

Explanation: 2,4-D (2,4-Dichlorophenoxyacetic acid) is a selective systemic post-emergence herbicide used to control broad-leaved weeds in monocot cereal crops.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

The floral biology of Pearl Millet (Bajra) is characterized by:

Explanation: Pearl millet exhibits protogyny, where stigmas emerge and become receptive days before anthers shed pollen, ensuring cross-pollination.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What symptom does the Brown Planthopper (Nilaparvata lugens) cause in rice crops under severe infestation?

Explanation: Heavy feeding by Brown Planthoppers extracts phloem sap, resulting in drying and browning of plants in circular patches known as 'hopper burn'.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the photosynthetic pathway of Maize (Zea mays)?

Explanation: Maize is a C4 plant possessing Kranz anatomy, higher photosynthetic efficiency, and negligible photorespiration under high temperatures.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

How much Phosphorus (P_2O_5) is present in Di-ammonium Phosphate (DAP) fertilizer?

Explanation: DAP contains 18% Nitrogen and 46% Phosphorus pentoxide (P_2O_5).

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following insect pests causes 'Dead Heart' and 'White Ear' symptoms in rice?

Explanation: Yellow Stem Borer (Scirpophaga incertulas) larvae bore into the stem, severing the growing point during vegetative stage (causing dead heart) and panicle base during reproductive stage (causing white ear).

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the optimum soil pH range for successful wheat cultivation?

Explanation: Wheat prefers well-drained alluvial and loam soils with a neutral pH range of 6.5 to 7.5.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which weed killer was specifically introduced to combat Isoproturon-resistant Phalaris minor in wheat fields?

Explanation: When Phalaris minor developed resistance to Isoproturon, alternative herbicides like Clodinafop-propargyl and Sulfosulfuron were successfully introduced.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the botanical name of Pearl Millet?

Explanation: The accepted modern botanical name for pearl millet is Pennisetum glaucum (syn. Pennisetum typhoides).

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of 'System of Rice Intensification' (SRI)?

Explanation: SRI involves transplanting very young seedlings (8-12 days old) singly at wider spacing (25 × 25 cm) with intermittent irrigation to promote root growth.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate the exact quantity of Urea and DAP required to supply 120 kg Nitrogen and 60 kg P_2O_5 per hectare, assuming only DAP is used for supplying Phosphorus. (Urea contains 46% N; DAP contains 18% N and 46% P_2O_5)

Solution & Rationale: Step 1: DAP required for 60 kg P2O5 = (60 / 46) * 100 = 130.43 kg. Step 2: N supplied through 130.43 kg DAP = 130.43 * 0.18 = 23.48 kg N. Step 3: Remaining N required = 120 - 23.48 = 96.52 kg N. Step 4: Urea required = (96.52 / 46) * 100 = 209.8 kg (approx 210 kg, or standard calculation check: total N from 260.8 kg urea = 120 kg N; total DAP = 130 kg).

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Match the following cereal crops with their respective inflorescence types: 1. Rice, 2. Wheat, 3. Maize (Male). Choose the correct code:

Solution & Rationale: Rice inflorescence is a panicle, wheat is a spike (or ear), and male maize inflorescence is a tassel.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

Which agronomic practice is recommended to overcome Iron chlorosis in lowland rice nurseries?

Solution & Rationale: Iron deficiency or chlorosis in upland or poorly managed nurseries is corrected by draining excess water to aerate soil and foliar spraying of 0.5% FeSO4.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

In hybrid seed production of maize, what is the recommended row ratio of female (seed parent) to male (pollinator parent) lines?

Solution & Rationale: Standard row ratio for maize hybrid seed production is 4 female rows to 2 male rows (4:2) to ensure adequate pollen load.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following wheat diseases is caused by a fungus that produces smut sori replacing the entire ear head with black powdery spores?

Solution & Rationale: Loose smut (Ustilago tritici) destroys spikelets completely, replacing them with black powdery spore masses.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the seed rate required for hybrid rice cultivation per hectare?

Solution & Rationale: Due to high cost and vigor of hybrid seeds, a lower seed rate of 15-20 kg/ha is sufficient.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which weed is recognized as an absolute weed and a primary parasite or associate of wheat crops across Northern India?

Solution & Rationale: Phalaris minor is the premier annual grassy weed associated with wheat in the Indo-Gangetic plains.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the critical stage of irrigation in maize where water stress leads to barrenness and severe grain yield reduction?

Solution & Rationale: Tasseling and silking coincide with flowering; moisture stress at this time desynchronizes pollen shedding and silk receptivity, causing barren cobs.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Which chemical and concentration is recommended for seed bio-priming or seed treatment against Bakanae disease of rice (caused by Fusarium moniliforme)?

Solution & Rationale: Seed treatment with Carbendazim @ 2 g/kg of seed effectively controls seed-borne Fusarium moniliforme causing Bakanae disease (foolish seedling disease of rice).

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the botanical family to which all true cereal food grains belong?

Solution & Rationale: All true cereal crops and millets belong to the grass family Poaceae (Gramineae).
