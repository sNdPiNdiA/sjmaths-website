Taxonomy, Botanical Classification, and Origin of Major Fodder Crops

Fodder crops form the backbone of dairy and livestock production systems in Indian agriculture, providing the necessary roughage and nutrients. Botanically, cultivated forages belong principally to the families Poaceae (grasses) and Fabaceae (legumes).

  • Berseem (Egyptian Clover): Trifolium alexandrinum, Family: Fabaceae, Origin: Egypt. Chromosome number: 2n = 16. It is considered the 'King of Rabi Fodder' due to its high protein content (17-20%) and multi-cut nature.
  • Lucerne (Alfalfa): Medicago sativa, Family: Fabaceae, Origin: South-Western Asia. Chromosome number: 2n = 32. Known as the 'Queen of Forages' on account of its deep root system, extreme drought tolerance, and perennial persistence.
  • Oat: Avena sativa, Family: Poaceae, Origin: Central Asia/Europe. Chromosome number: 2n = 42. A premier cereal rabi fodder crop prized for high palatability and digestible dry matter.
  • Sorghum (Jowar): Sorghum bicolor, Family: Poaceae, Origin: North-Eastern Africa. Chromosome number: 2n = 20. The leading kharif cereal fodder, though young plants contain toxic cyanogenic glucosides (dhurrin).
  • Pearl Millet (Bajra): Pennisetum glaucum (syn. Cenchrus americanus), Family: Poaceae, Origin: Tropical Africa. Chromosome number: 2n = 14. Highly drought-hardy kharif fodder that does not accumulate toxic prussic acid.

Agro-Climatic Requirements, Soil Preferences, and Field Preparation

Successful establishment of forage crops depends heavily on fine seedbed preparation and favorable temperature ranges corresponding to their seasonal adaptation.

  • Rabi Forages (Berseem, Lucerne, Oats): Require cool, dry winters with optimum temperatures between 15°C and 25°C. Berseem thrives in heavy clay to loam soils with good water-retention capacity, as it requires frequent irrigation. Lucerne prefers deep, well-drained loamy soils and is highly sensitive to waterlogging. Oats are widely adaptable across sandy loam to clay loam soils.
  • Kharif Forages (Sorghum, Maize, Cowpea, Cluster Bean): Warm-season crops requiring temperatures of 25°C to 35°C, high sunshine hours, and adequate monsoon rainfall or irrigation. Maize (Zea mays) requires fertile, well-drained alluvial soils rich in organic matter.
  • Tillage Operations: Deep ploughing followed by 2-3 harrowings and planking to achieve a fine, weed-free seedbed. For small-seeded legumes like berseem, leveling is critical to ensure uniform flood irrigation without water stagnation.

Seed Rate, Seed Treatment, Sowing Geometry, and Inoculation Technology

Optimized plant population density is vital for maximizing green forage yield and quality.

  • Seed Rates: Berseem requires 25-30 kg/ha; Lucerne requires 20-25 kg/ha; Oats require 80-100 kg/ha; Single-cut Sorghum requires 40-50 kg/ha (multicut: 15-20 kg/ha); Maize requires 40-50 kg/ha.
  • Seed Treatment & Inoculation: Legume seeds (berseem, lucerne, cowpea) must be inoculated with specific Rhizobium strains (e.g., Rhizobium trifolii for berseem) using a jaggery (gur) slurry to enhance biological nitrogen fixation. Berseem seeds are often mixed with chicory (Cichorium intybus) weed seeds; screening in 10% common salt solution floats away the lighter weed seeds.
  • Sowing Time and Spacing: Rabi forages are sown from mid-October to mid-November. Kharif forages are sown with the onset of the monsoon (June-July). Sowing is typically done in lines spaced at 25-30 cm for cereals and 20-25 cm for legumes using broadcasting or drill methods.

Nutrient Management, Fertilizer Calculations, and Irrigation Scheduling

Forage crops remove large quantities of macro- and micronutrients due to their heavy vegetative biomass production.

  • Fertilizer Recommendations: Legumes require low nitrogen (starter dose of 20 kg N/ha) and high phosphorus (60-80 kg ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5/ha) to stimulate root development and nodulation. Non-legumes (oats, sorghum, maize) require heavy nitrogen fertilization (80-120 kg N/ha) applied in split doses (basal + top dressing after each cutting).
  • Fertilizer Dose Calculation Example: To apply 100 kg N/ha using Urea (containing 46% N), the required quantity of Urea is calculated as: (100 / 46) imes 100 = 217.39 ext{ kg/ha}. Similarly, for 60 kg ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5/ha using Single Super Phosphate (SSP, containing 16% ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5): (60 / 16) imes 100 = 375 ext{ kg/ha}.
  • Irrigation Management: Berseem requires frequent irrigations every 10-12 days in winter and 6-8 days in summer. Total irrigations range from 8 to 12. Sorghum and maize need moisture during active vegetative growth and knee-high stages, avoiding moisture stress during grand growth periods.

Crop Protection: Weeds, Insect Pests, Diseases, and Physiological Disorders

Effective pest management ensures high forage quality free from chemical residues and toxins.

  • Major Weeds and Control: Chicory (Cichorium intybus) is a noxious weed in berseem fields. Hand weeding and sowing certified, weed-free seed are essential. Fluchloralin or pendimethalin can be used as pre-emergence herbicides where recommended.
  • Insect Pests: Shoot fly (Atherigona soccata) in sorghum and maize; stem borer (Chilo partellus); aphid (Aphis craccivora) in berseem and lucerne. Management involves seed treatment with imidacloprid and releasing natural predators.
  • Diseases: Root rot and stem rot (Rhizoctonia spp.) in berseem; powdery mildew (Erysiphe polygoni) in lucerne; red rot and leaf blight in sorghum. Seed treatment with carbendazim (2 g/kg seed) prevents seedling blights.
  • Toxicity Hazards: Hydrocyanic acid (HCN) or prussic acid poisoning occurs when hungry animals graze young, drought-stressed sorghum or sudan grass below 45-50 cm height. Nitrate poisoning can occur in heavily nitrogen-fertilized oats and maize under cloudy weather.

Harvesting Indices, Multiple Cutting Management, and Yield Economics

Timing of harvest dictates the trade-off between dry matter yield and nutritional quality (crude protein and crude fiber).

  • Harvesting Stage: Cereal forages (oats, sorghum, maize) are best harvested at the 50% flowering to early milky stage. Legumes (berseem, lucerne) are harvested at the pre-flowering or 10% flowering stage to maximize protein content and palatability while ensuring quick regeneration of shoots.
  • Multi-Cut Management: Berseem yields 4 to 6 cuts at intervals of 30-35 days. The first cut is taken at 50-60 days after sowing. Multicut sorghum hybrids yield 3 to 4 cuts if stubble height is maintained at 10-15 cm above ground level.
  • Green Forage Yields: Berseem yields 800-1000 quintals/ha; Oats yield 400-500 quintals/ha; Sorghum yields 400-600 quintals/ha. Integration of legume-cereal intercropping (e.g., Berseem + Oats) balances the carbon-nitrogen ratio and boosts total forage productivity.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing berseem as a cereal grass; remember berseem is a leguminous clover belonging to Fabaceae.
  • Applying high nitrogen doses to legume fodders; legumes fix their own nitrogen and only require a small starter dose.
  • Grazing sorghum at early vegetative stages (<50 cm height), which leads to fatal HCN poisoning in cattle.
  • Assuming lucerne is an annual crop; lucerne is a perennial forage legume lasting 3-4 years in the field.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Comprehensive Agronomy of Fodder Crops for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Botanical and Family Classification

Fodder crops are broadly divided into legumes (improving soil nitrogen and protein content) and grasses/cereals (providing bulk carbohydrates and energy).

  • Leguminous Forages: Berseem, Lucerne, Cowpea, Cluster bean (Family Fabaceae).
  • Graminaceous Forages: Sorghum, Maize, Bajra, Oats, Napier grass (Family Poaceae).
Concept 2

Nutritional Quality Parameters

Forage nutritive value is determined by chemical constituents and digestibility.

  • Crude Protein (CP): Legumes contain 18-24% CP, whereas grasses contain 8-12% CP.
  • Dry Matter Digestibility (DMD): Declines sharply as lignification increases past the flowering stage.
Concept 3

Anti-Nutritional Factors in Forages

Presence of secondary plant metabolites can cause toxicity or metabolic disorders in livestock.

  • Dhurrin (HCN): Found in young sorghum; hydrolyzes into toxic hydrocyanic acid.
  • Saponins & Tannins: Found in lucerne and berseem, causing bloat (tympanites) in ruminants.
Concept 4

Seed Management and Inoculation

Ensuring high seedling vigor and symbiotic efficiency requires strict seed protocols.

  • Rhizobium Inoculation: Increases biological ext{N}_2 fixation by 15-25%.
  • Salt Flotation: Effective technique to remove weed seeds like chicory from berseem lots.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

King of Rabi Fodder (Trifolium alexandrinum)
Berseem, valued for its high palatability, multi-cut capacity, and rich protein content during winter.
Queen of Forages (Medicago sativa)
Lucerne or Alfalfa, a perennial deep-rooted legume forage known for drought hardiness and persistence.
Prussic Acid (Hydrocyanic Acid (HCN))
Toxic cyanogenic glucoside compound found in young sorghum plants capable of causing rapid animal death.
Multi-Cut System (Regenerative Forage Harvesting)
Agronomic practice of harvesting forage crops multiple times during their growth cycle from basal shoots.
Chicory (Cichorium intybus)
A major weed infesting berseem seed lots whose seeds closely mimic berseem in size and shape.
Rhizobium Inoculation (Symbiotic Bacterial Seed Dressing)
Coating legume seeds with live nitrogen-fixing bacteria to enhance nodulation and atmospheric nitrogen fixation.
Dry Matter Yield (DMY (Quintals/Hectare))
The total weight of forage biomass after complete moisture removal, representing true nutritive bulk.
Bloat (Rumen Tympany)
A metabolic disorder in ruminants caused by gas accumulation due to rapid fermentation of lush legume forages.
Stubble Height (Residual Stem Height)
The height of plant stem left above the ground after harvesting to facilitate successful regrowth in multi-cut crops.
Silage (Ensilage / Fermented Forage)
Preserved green succulent forage produced through anaerobic lactic acid fermentation in silos.
Hay (Sun-dried Forage)
Forage conserved by drying to a moisture content below 15-20% for storage during lean periods.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Berseem and oats dominate rabi forage production, while sorghum and maize lead kharif production.
  • Legumes fix atmospheric nitrogen, requiring phosphorus-rich fertilizers (SSP) rather than heavy nitrogen inputs.
  • Harvesting at 50% flowering ensures an optimal balance between total dry matter yield and crude protein percentage.
  • Prussic acid (HCN) toxicity in sorghum requires grazing restriction until plants exceed 50 cm in height.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Silage VS Hay

Silage is fermented green fodder stored anaerobically with high moisture (65-70%), whereas hay is sun-dried forage stored at low moisture (<20%).

Dhurrin VS Saponin

Dhurrin is the cyanogenic glucoside in sorghum causing HCN poisoning, while saponins are compounds in legumes associated with ruminant bloat.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop is universally recognized as the 'King of Rabi Fodder' in Indian agriculture?

Explanation: Berseem (Trifolium alexandrinum) is known as the King of Rabi Fodder due to its high yield, excellent nutritive value, palatability, and multi-cut capability (4-6 cuts).

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the somatic chromosome number (2n) of Berseem (Trifolium alexandrinum)?

Explanation: The somatic chromosome number of berseem is 2n = 16. For comparison, maize is 20, sorghum is 20, and oats is 42.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which anti-nutritional factor is associated with young, drought-stressed Sorghum plants?

Explanation: Young, water-stressed sorghum plants contain high levels of dhurrin, which breaks down into Hydrocyanic acid (HCN) or prussic acid, inhibiting cellular respiration in animals.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended seed rate for Berseem (Trifolium alexandrinum) per hectare?

Explanation: The standard recommended seed rate for berseem is 25 to 30 kg/ha to ensure a dense, uniform canopy capable of high multi-cut biomass production.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which notorious weed seed is frequently found contaminant in Berseem seed lots and can be separated using 10% salt solution?

Explanation: Chicory (Cichorium intybus), locally known as kasni, produces seeds similar in size and shape to berseem. Immersion in 10% salt solution causes chicory seeds to float due to lower specific gravity.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which Rhizobium species is specific for seed inoculation in Berseem?

Explanation: Rhizobium trifolii is the specific nitrogen-fixing bacterial strain used for inoculating berseem seeds to enhance nodulation and biological nitrogen fixation.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Why is Single Super Phosphate (SSP) preferred over other phosphatic fertilizers for leguminous forage crops?

Explanation: SSP contains approximately 16% P2O5 and 11-12% Sulphur. Sulphur is essential for synthesizing sulfur-containing amino acids (methionine, cysteine) and enhancing nodulation in legumes.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the optimum stage of maturity for harvesting cereal fodder crops like Oats and Sorghum to maximize nutrient yield?

Explanation: Harvesting cereal forages at 50% flowering to early milky stage provides the best balance between dry matter accumulation, crude protein content, and digestibility.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which forage crop is known as the 'Queen of Forages' due to its perennial nature and deep root system?

Explanation: Medicago sativa (Lucerne or Alfalfa) is known as the Queen of Forages because it is a perennial legume that persists for several years and possesses a deep taproot system providing drought tolerance.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary cause of 'Bloat' (Rumen Tympany) in cattle grazing heavily on lush legume forages?

Explanation: Bloat occurs when soluble proteins and saponins in lush legumes (like berseem and lucerne) stabilize fermentation gases in the rumen into a persistent foam, preventing eructation (belching).

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosome number (2n) of Cultivated Oats (Avena sativa)?

Explanation: Cultivated oats (Avena sativa) is a hexaploid species with a somatic chromosome number of 2n = 6x = 42.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which kharif legume fodder crop is exceptionally drought-resistant and suitable for sandy soils?

Explanation: Cluster bean (Cyamopsis tetragonoloba), commonly known as Guar, is a deep-rooted, highly drought-tolerant kharif legume forage well adapted to arid and semi-arid sandy soils.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

How many successful cuts are typically obtained from a healthy Berseem crop under optimal management?

Explanation: A well-managed berseem crop yields 4 to 6 cuts at intervals of 30-35 days, beginning about 50-60 days after sowing.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard recommended seed rate for single-cut Sorghum fodder per hectare?

Explanation: For single-cut sorghum fodder, a seed rate of 40-50 kg/ha is recommended to ensure optimal plant density and fine stem quality, whereas multicut types require 15-20 kg/ha.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which gas is trapped during the ensilage process to convert green fodder into high-quality silage?

Explanation: Ensilage relies on anaerobic conditions where lactic acid bacteria ferment water-soluble carbohydrates into lactic acid, lowering pH and preserving the forage.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the safe moisture content threshold for storing Hay without fungal spoilage?

Explanation: Hay must be dried to a moisture content below 15-20% before baling and storage to prevent heating, microbial decomposition, and spontaneous combustion.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a dual-purpose crop grown for both grain and fodder?

Explanation: Oats (Avena sativa) can be utilized as a dual-purpose crop, allowing a single cutting for green forage in mid-winter followed by grain recovery in spring.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the optimum temperature range for the germination and vegetative growth of Rabi fodder crops?

Explanation: Rabi forages like berseem, oats, and lucerne thrive best under temperate to cool conditions with an optimum temperature range of 15°C to 25°C.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which mineral deficiency in soils commonly limits the growth and nitrogen fixation of leguminous fodder crops?

Explanation: Phosphorus is critical for leguminous forages because it stimulates root proliferation, enhances nodulation, and drives energy transfer reactions in biological nitrogen fixation.

Calculate the quantity of Urea (46% N) required to supply 80 kg N per hectare for an Oat fodder crop.

Solution & Rationale: Formula: ( ext{Target N} / \% ext{N in fertilizer}) imes 100 = (80 / 46) imes 100 = 173.91 ext{ kg/ha}.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

A farmer wishes to apply 60 kg ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5/ha using Single Super Phosphate (SSP containing 16% ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5). What is the required quantity of SSP?

Solution & Rationale: Formula: (60 / 16) imes 100 = 3.75 imes 100 = 375 ext{ kg/ha}.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

Which chemical test or plant physiological condition is most critical to avoid acute animal mortality when utilizing Sorghum fodder?

Solution & Rationale: Young or drought-stunted sorghum plants (<50 cm) accumulate lethal levels of cyanogenic glucosides (dhurrin), releasing hydrocyanic acid (HCN) upon ingestion.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Match the following fodder crops with their respective families: I. Berseem, II. Oats, III. Cowpea, IV. Sorghum.

Solution & Rationale: Berseem and Cowpea belong to Fabaceae (legumes); Oats and Sorghum belong to Poaceae (cereals/grasses).

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary objective of maintaining a stubble height of 10-15 cm while harvesting multicut sorghum or berseem?

Solution & Rationale: Leaving a 10-15 cm stubble preserves lower nodes containing dormant meristematic buds necessary for quick ratooning and subsequent cuts.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Which agronomic intervention effectively eliminates Chicory (Cichorium intybus) contamination from Berseem seed lots prior to sowing?

Solution & Rationale: Because chicory seeds have a lower specific gravity than berseem seeds, immersing the seed lot in a 10% sodium chloride solution causes chicory seeds to float for easy removal.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Why are leguminous forages generally lower in soluble carbohydrate content compared to cereal grasses, requiring additives during ensilage?

Solution & Rationale: Legumes possess high buffering capacity and low water-soluble carbohydrates (WSC), making direct silage fermentation difficult unless molasses or cereal fodder is blended in.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the typical green forage yield range of a well-managed Berseem crop across 4 to 6 cuts?

Solution & Rationale: Under optimum agronomic management, high-yielding berseem varieties produce between 800 to 1000 quintals of green fodder per hectare over the entire season.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

In what way does Lucerne differ fundamentally from Berseem in terms of crop life cycle?

Solution & Rationale: Lucerne (Medicago sativa) is a perennial forage legume persisting for 3 to 4 years, whereas Berseem (Trifolium alexandrinum) is a winter annual clover.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the ideal sowing time window for Rabi fodder crops in the Indo-Gangetic plains of Uttar Pradesh?

Solution & Rationale: Mid-October to mid-November provides optimum temperatures for germination and establishment of rabi forages before severe winter cold sets in.
