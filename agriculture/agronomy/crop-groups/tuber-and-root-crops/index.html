Tuber and Root Crops: Comprehensive Agronomy and Crop Production

An authoritative agronomic guide covering botanical classification, cultivation practices, nutrient management, and crop protection of major tropical and temperate tuber and root crops for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture examinations.

Introduction and Botanical Taxonomy of Tuber and Root Crops

Tuber and root crops are specialized underground storage organs that accumulate vast amounts of carbohydrates, predominantly starch, serving as vital staple foods and industrial raw materials globally and across India.

  • Potato (Solanum tuberosum): Belongs to the family Solanaceae, native to the Andean region of South America. Chromosome number is 2n = 48 (tetraploid). It is a modified stem tuber characterized by 'eyes' or buds.
  • Sweet Potato (Ipomoea batatas): Belongs to the family Convolvulaceae, native to Central or South America. Chromosome number is 2n = 90 (hexaploid). It is a true modified root tuber.
  • Cassava / Tapioca (Manihot esculenta): Belongs to the family Euphorbiaceae, native to South America. Chromosome number is 2n = 36 (diploid). Highly drought-tolerant root crop.
  • Elephant Foot Yam (Amorphophallus paeoniifolius): Belongs to the family Araceae, native to tropical Asia. Chromosome number is 2n = 26 or 28. It forms a large underground corm.

Soil, Climate, and Land Preparation Requirements

Successful production of root and tuber crops depends heavily on loose, friable soil texture that permits unrestricted underground expansion of tubers and corms without mechanical impedance.

  • Soil Texture: Well-drained sandy loam to loam soils rich in organic matter with a pH range of 5.5 to 6.5 are ideal. Heavy clays cause tuber malformation and harvesting difficulties, while sandy soils leach nutrients rapidly.
  • Climatic Adaptation: Potato is a cool-season (temperate) crop requiring optimal tuberization temperatures between 15°C and 20°C. Tropical root crops like cassava, sweet potato, and elephant foot yam thrive under warm, humid conditions with temperatures between 25°C and 35°C.
  • Land Preparation: Deep ploughing followed by 2 to 3 harrowing operations is necessary to achieve a fine tilth. Formation of ridges and furrows or raised beds is standard practice to prevent water stagnation and tuber rotting.

Seed Selection, Sowing Geometry, and Propagation Methods

Propagation techniques vary distinctly among tuber and root crops, relying on vegetative parts, stem cuttings, or whole tubers.

  • Potato Seed Management: Utilizes whole or cut tubers weighing 25–30 g, possessing at least 2 to 3 active eyes. Seed rate ranges from 2.5 to 3.0 tonnes per hectare. Spacing is maintained at 50-60 cm × 20-25 cm. Seed tubers are treated with boric acid (3%) or carbendazim to prevent fungal and bacterial surface infections.
  • Sweet Potato Propagation: Propagated using terminal vine cuttings of 20–30 cm length, planted at a spacing of 60 cm × 20 cm. Vine cutting prevents pest transmission and ensures higher yields than small roots.
  • Cassava Propagation: Stem cuttings of 15–20 cm length taken from mature, healthy stems are planted vertically or inclined at a spacing of 90 cm × 90 cm.

Nutrient Management and Fertilizer Recommendations

Root and heavy-yielding tuber crops exhibit high nutrient removal capacity, particularly for potassium and nitrogen, which govern starch accumulation and vegetative expansion.

  • Potato Fertilizer Dose: Generally requires an N:P:K recommendation of 120:80:100 kg/ha for high-yielding varieties. Full dose of phosphorus and potassium along with half dose of nitrogen is applied basal at planting, while the remaining nitrogen is top-dressed during earthing-up.
  • Sweet Potato & Cassava: Typically responds well to 75:50:75 kg NPK/ha and 100:50:100 kg NPK/ha respectively. Farm Yard Manure (FYM) @ 10–15 t/ha is incorporated during land preparation to improve soil structure and microbial activity.
  • Micronutrients: Boron deficiency causes hollow heart in potatoes and internal brown spot; soil application of borax @ 10 kg/ha corrects this disorder.

Water Management and Weed Control Practices

Moisture stress at critical crop growth stages drastically reduces tuber yield and market quality.

  • Critical Irrigation Stages in Potato: Stoloni-genesis and tuber initiation stage, and tuber bulking stage are extremely sensitive to water stress. Total 6 to 8 irrigations are required depending on soil type.
  • Weed Management: Initial 30 to 45 days represent the critical period of crop-weed competition. Pre-emergence application of Pendimethalin @ 1.0 kg a.i./ha combined with manual earthing-up at 30-35 days after planting (DAP) provides effective weed control.
  • Earthing-up: Essential agronomic operation in potato and elephant foot yam to prevent greening of tubers exposed to sunlight and to facilitate root zone aeration.

Plant Protection: Major Pests, Diseases, and Physiological Disorders

Pest and disease management is critical for sustaining commercial yields in tuber crops, especially against fungal blights and insect vectors.

  • Late Blight of Potato: Caused by Phytophthora infestans, producing water-soaked lesions on leaves and stems under cool, wet conditions. Controlled by prophylactic spraying of Mancozeb @ 2.5 g/L or curative sprays of Cymoxanil + Mancozeb.
  • Early Blight of Potato: Caused by Alternaria solani, characterized by concentric ring spots on older foliage.
  • Potato Tuber Moth (Phthorimaea operculella): Larve mine leaves and bore into tubers both in field and storage. Managed through deep planting, frequent earthing-up, and pheromone traps.
  • Physiological Disorders: Black heart in potato occurs due to oxygen deficiency and high storage temperatures (>35°C), leading to internal tissue necrosis.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing sweet potato as a modified stem: Sweet potato is a modified root tuber, whereas potato is a modified stem tuber.
  • Assuming cassava is propagated via tubers: Cassava is exclusively propagated via woody stem cuttings.
  • Mixing up early and late blight causal organisms: Early blight is caused by Alternaria solani, while late blight is caused by Phytophthora infestans.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Tuber and Root Crops: Comprehensive Agronomy and Crop Production

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Botanical Classification and Morphological Nature

Understanding underground storage structures is fundamental for agronomic classification.

  • Potato Stem Tuber: Swollen underground stem axis with axillary buds (eyes).
  • Sweet Potato Root Tuber: Adventitious root modification without nodes or internodes.
Concept 2

Propagation and Seed Standards

Seed quality and planting geometry dictate plant population and final marketable yield.

  • Seed Rate: 2.5 - 3.0 t/ha for whole/cut seed tubers weighing 25-30g.
  • Cutting Treatment: Dipping seed pieces in boric acid (3%) prevents common scab and bacterial rot.
Concept 3

Nutrient and Irrigation Dynamics

Balanced fertilization and timely irrigation management ensure high starch accumulation.

  • NPK Ratio: 120:80:100 kg/ha for potato, emphasizing high potassium requirement.
  • Critical Irrigation: Tuber initiation phase is drought-sensitive.
Concept 4

Disease and Disorder Management

Protection against fungal pathogens and physiological stress ensures safe storage and high grade.

  • Phytophthora infestans: Causal agent of late blight, managed via Mancozeb.
  • Black Heart: Internal tissue necrosis from inadequate oxygen in storage.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Tuber (Modified Underground Storage Stem or Root)
A thickened underground plant organ that stores nutrients for plant perennation and propagation.
Stolon (Underground Stem Runner)
A horizontal stem branch from which potato tubers develop at the apex.
Earthing-up (Soil Mounding)
The agronomic practice of heaping soil around the base of the plant to cover tubers and support stems.
Black Heart (Physiological Storage Disorder)
Internal necrosis of potato tuber tissue caused by oxygen starvation during storage.
Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans Infection)
A devastating fungal-like oomycete disease affecting foliage and tubers under cool, wet weather.
Green Tuber (Solanine Accumulation)
Development of green color and toxic glycoalkaloid solanine when tubers are exposed to light.
True Potato Seed (TPS) (Botanical Seed of Potato)
True botanical seeds produced through sexual reproduction, used to eliminate seed-borne viral diseases.
Corm (Solid Underground Stem Swelling)
A short, vertical, swollen underground plant stem that serves as a storage organ in crops like elephant foot yam.
Solanine (Glycoalkaloid Poison)
A toxic chemical compound formed in green potatoes rendering them bitter and unpalatable.
Hollow Heart (Internal Tuber Cavity Disorder)
A physiological cavity inside large potato tubers caused by rapid fluctuating growth rates.

  • Sweet potato is a root tuber belonging to Convolvulaceae, propagated through vine cuttings.
  • Late blight caused by Phytophthora infestans is the most destructive disease of potato.

  • Potassium is the key nutrient governing starch synthesis and tuber bulking.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Stem Tuber VS Root Tuber

Stem tubers possess nodes, scales, and buds (e.g., potato), while root tubers are modified adventitious roots lacking nodes (e.g., sweet potato).

Early Blight VS Late Blight

Early blight is caused by Alternaria solani with concentric rings; late blight is caused by Phytophthora infestans producing water-soaked dark lesions.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following tuber crops is botanically classified as a modified stem?

Explanation: Potato (Solanum tuberosum) is a modified underground stem tuber possessing nodes and buds ('eyes'), whereas sweet potato and cassava are modified roots.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosome number (2n) of the cultivated potato (Solanum tuberosum)?

Explanation: Cultivated potato is a tetraploid (2n = 4q = 48). Sweet potato is hexaploid (2n = 90), and cassava is diploid (2n = 36).

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Late blight of potato is caused by which of the following pathogens?

Explanation: Late blight is caused by Phytophthora infestans, an oomycete thriving in cool, wet weather. Alternaria solani causes early blight.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended seed rate for planting one hectare of potato using standard whole or cut tubers?

Explanation: The standard seed rate for potato cultivation is 2.5 to 3.0 tonnes per hectare using seed tubers weighing 25-30g each.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which physiological disorder in potato storage is caused by an inadequate supply of oxygen and high temperature?

Explanation: Black heart occurs when interior tuber tissues suffer from oxygen starvation and excessive heat during storage, causing cell death and blackening.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of performing earthing-up in potato cultivation?

Explanation: Earthing-up covers developing tubers with soil, preventing exposure to sunlight which causes greening and accumulation of toxic solanine.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Propagation in sweet potato is commercially and successfully achieved through:

Explanation: Sweet potato is commercially propagated using terminal vine cuttings (20-30 cm long), which establish rapidly and yield better than small root tubers.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical is commonly recommended for treating potato seed tubers before planting to prevent fungal infections?

Explanation: Dipping potato tubers in 3% boric acid solution for 15-20 minutes before planting effectively controls surface-borne pathogens like common scab and black scurf.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Cassava (Tapioca) belongs to which botanical family?

Explanation: Cassava (Manihot esculenta) belongs to the family Euphorbiaceae. Potato belongs to Solanaceae, and sweet potato to Convolvulaceae.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is the most critical stage for irrigation in potato crop to avoid severe yield loss?

Explanation: Stolon formation and tuber initiation represent the most water-sensitive critical stage in potato; moisture stress here impairs tuber set.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What causes the greening of potato tubers when exposed to light?

Explanation: Exposure to light induces chlorophyll development (making tubers green) and simultaneous synthesis of toxic solanine, making them bitter and unsafe.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Elephant foot yam belongs to which family and propagates through which plant part?

Explanation: Elephant foot yam (Amorphophallus paeoniifolius) belongs to the family Araceae and is propagated using corm pieces weighing 500-750g.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended N:P:K fertilizer dosage for high-yielding potato varieties under standard Indian conditions?

Explanation: The standard general recommendation for high-yielding potato varieties is 120 kg N, 80 kg P2O5, and 100 kg K2O per hectare.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest is a major threat to potato crops both in the field and in traditional storage structures?

Explanation: Potato tuber moth (Phthorimaea operculella) is a notorious pest whose larvae damage foliage in the field and tunnel into tubers during storage.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

True Potato Seed (TPS) technology was developed primarily to overcome which major agricultural challenge?

Explanation: TPS technology bypasses bulky, virus-infected seed tubers by utilizing true botanical seeds, significantly reducing seed cost and disease transmission.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which herbicide is commonly applied as a pre-emergence treatment in potato fields?

Explanation: Pendimethalin @ 1.0 kg a.i./ha applied pre-emergence effectively controls annual grass and broad-leaved weeds in potato fields.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the optimum temperature range required for successful tuberization in potato?

Explanation: Potato is a cool-season crop; optimal tuberization and carbohydrate translocation occur between 15°C and 20°C.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Sweet potato is sensitive to which environmental factor during its root enlargement phase?

Explanation: Sweet potato requires well-drained soils; water logging causes rotting of tuberous roots and hampers starch accumulation.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What causes hollow heart in potato tubers?

Explanation: Hollow heart is a physiological disorder occurring when large tubers grow too rapidly due to sudden fluctuations in soil moisture and nutrient availability.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a key adaptation of Cassava (Tapioca) making it popular in marginal farming systems?

Explanation: Cassava is renowned for its outstanding drought tolerance and capability to produce economic yields on impoverished, acidic soils where other crops fail.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

In potato cultivation, what is the scientific rationale behind dehaulming prior to harvesting?

Solution & Rationale: Dehaulming (killing or cutting vines 10-15 days before harvest) hardens the periderm of tubers, prevents skin bruising during digging, and stops late-season viral infection.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which vector is primarily responsible for transmitting Potato Leaf Roll Virus (PLRV) and Potato Virus Y (PVY)?

Solution & Rationale: Myzus persicae (green peach aphid) is the principal insect vector responsible for transmitting persistent and non-persistent viral diseases in potato crops.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

What distinguishes root tubers of sweet potato from stem tubers of potato anatomically?

Solution & Rationale: Stem tubers (potato) are modified stems bearing nodes, buds ('eyes'), and scale leaves. Root tubers (sweet potato) are modified adventitious roots lacking buds and nodes.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the recommended spacing geometry for commercial potato planting in standard ridges and furrows?

Solution & Rationale: Standard recommended spacing for potato is 50 to 60 cm between ridges and 20 to 25 cm between seed tubers within the row.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

Which micro-element deficiency in sweet potato leads to internal brown spots and corky tissue development?

Solution & Rationale: Boron is essential for cell wall integrity and translocation of sugars; its deficiency causes internal brown spots, hollow heart, and corky skin textures in root crops.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the optimum storage temperature range for commercial table potato to minimize sprouting and weight loss?

Solution & Rationale: Table potatoes are best stored at 2°C to 4°C with high relative humidity (85-90%) to suppress sprouting and minimize respiration losses without inducing low-temperature sweetening.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following describes the multiplication method of Elephant Foot Yam?

Solution & Rationale: Elephant foot yam is propagated using corm split pieces or whole small corms weighing 500-750g, treated with cow dung slurry and ash or fungicides.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary toxic constituent present in cassava tubers that necessitates proper processing before consumption?

Solution & Rationale: Cassava contains cyanogenic glucosides (predominantly linamarin), which release toxic hydrogen cyanide upon enzymatic hydrolysis if tubers are improperly processed.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which fungal pathogen causes early blight in potato, characterized by concentric dark brown rings on older leaves?

Solution & Rationale: Alternaria solani is the causal agent of early blight, distinguished by target-board or concentric ring symptoms on aging foliage under warm, humid conditions.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What role does Potassium play specifically in tuber and root crops?

Solution & Rationale: Potassium acts as an activator for numerous enzymes involved in photosynthesis and is vital for translocating sugars from leaves to underground storage sinks.
