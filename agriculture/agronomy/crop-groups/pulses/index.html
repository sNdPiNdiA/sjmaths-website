Taxonomy, Origin, and Chromosomal Configuration of Major Pulses

Pulses, or grain legumes belonging to the family Fabaceae (subfamily Papilionoideae), are vital sources of dietary plant protein, restorative green manure, and atmospheric nitrogen fixation. Understanding their taxonomic hierarchy, center of origin, and cytogenetics is essential for UP TGT/PGT Agriculture aspirants.

  • Chickpea (Gram): Scientifically known as Cicer arietinum, family Fabaceae, origin in South-Western Asia (Afghanistan/Persia). Diploid chromosome number is 2n = 2x = 16. It is further divided into two main botanical types: Kabuli (white flowers, large beige seeds, 2n=16) and Desi (pink/purple flowers, small coloured seeds, 2n=16).
  • Pigeonpea (Red Gram / Tur): Cajanus cajan, origin in Africa (specifically India/Africa region), 2n = 22. It is a perennial shrub often cultivated as an annual crop.
  • Mungbean (Green Gram): Vigna radiata, origin in India, 2n = 22.
  • Urdbean (Black Gram): Vigna mungo, origin in India, 2n = 22.
  • Lentil (Masoor): Lens culinaris, origin in Near East / Mediterranean, 2n = 14.
  • Field Pea: Pisum sativum var. arvense, origin in Mediterranean / Central Asia, 2n = 14.

Agro-Climatic Requirements, Soil, and Field Preparation

Grain legumes thrive under specific environmental conditions. Excess moisture or heavy waterlogging is universally fatal to pulse crops due to oxygen starvation in the root zone, which inhibits root respiration and deactivates nitrogenase enzyme activity in root nodules.

  • Temperature Ranges: Cool-season pulses (Chickpea, Lentil, Field Pea) require an optimum temperature range of 15°C to 25°C for vegetative growth and 25°C to 30°C for grain filling. Warm-season pulses (Mungbean, Urdbean, Pigeonpea) require 25°C to 35°C.
  • Soil Type: Well-drained sandy loam to clay loam soils with a neutral pH range of 6.5 to 7.5 are ideal. Highly acidic soils require lime application, while sodic soils require gypsum amendment.
  • Tillage Operations: One deep ploughing followed by 2 to 3 harrowings and planking to achieve a fine, weed-free, and firm seedbed. Conservation agriculture practices like zero-tillage sowing are increasingly popular in rice-fallow systems.

Seed Rate, Seed Treatment, Sowing Geometry, and Inoculation

Agronomic management of seed and sowing ensures optimum plant population density and vigorous early establishment, which directly influences yield realization.

  • Seed Treatment: Seeds must be treated with Carbendazim or Thiram at 2.5 g/kg of seed to suppress soil-borne pathogens like Fusarium oxysporum and Rhizoctonia species.
  • Biofertilizer Inoculation: Crucial step involving Rhizobium cultures (specific to each pulse species, e.g., Rhizobium leguminosarum for lentil and pea; Bradyrhizobium sp. for pigeonpea and mungbean) along with Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria (PSB like Bacillus megaterium) at 20-25 g/kg seed.
  • Seed Rates and Spacing: Chickpea (Desi: 75-80 kg/ha, Kabuli: 100 kg/ha; Spacing: 30-40 cm × 10 cm); Pigeonpea (Straight: 15-20 kg/ha; Spacing: 50-60 cm × 20 cm); Mungbean (20-25 kg/ha; Spacing: 30 cm × 10 cm); Urdbean (20-25 kg/ha; Spacing: 30 cm × 10 cm).

Nutrient Management and Biological Nitrogen Fixation (BNF)

Although pulses fix atmospheric nitrogen via symbiotic association with Rhizobium bacteria housed in root nodules, they require a starter dose of nitrogen and adequate phosphorus for root proliferation.

  • Starter Nitrogen Dose: An application of 15-20 kg N/ha is recommended to kickstart early vegetative growth before functional root nodules are established.
  • Phosphorus Requirement: Phosphorus is the master nutrient for pulses, essential for root growth, nodulation, and energy transfer. Applied entirely as basal dressing through Single Super Phosphate (SSP) at 40-60 kg ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5/ha. SSP also supplies essential Calcium (19%) and Sulphur (12%).
  • Sulphur and Micronutrients: Application of 20 kg S/ha enhances protein synthesis (methionine and cysteine amino acids). Foliar spray of Boron and Molybdenum aids in flower retention and nitrogenase enzyme activity.

Water Management and Critical Stages of Irrigation

Pulses are predominantly grown under rainfed conditions (approximately 85% area), but providing supplemental irrigation at critical moisture-sensitive stages drastically boosts grain yield.

  • Critical Stages in Chickpea: Pre-flowering stage (30-35 DAS) and Pod formation / grain filling stage (75-80 DAS).
  • Critical Stages in Pigeonpea: Branching and flowering stage, and pod development stage. Water stagnation must be strictly prevented by providing lateral surface drainage channels.
  • Critical Stages in Summer Mungbean/Urdbean: Irrigation at 10-12 days intervals, specifically at pre-flowering and pod filling stages. Avoid irrigation during peak flowering to prevent flower drop.

Crop Protection: Major Pests, Diseases, and Integrated Management (IPM)

Plant protection in pulses is challenging due to overlapping vegetative and reproductive phases that attract lepidopteran borers and fungal wilts.

  • Insect Pests: Gram Pod Borer (Helicoverpa armigera) is the most destructive pest attacking chickpea and pigeonpea. Larvae bore into pods and feed on developing grains. Management: Pheromone traps at 5 traps/ha, installation of bird perches (50/ha), and spraying of Indoxacarb 14.5% SC or Nucleopolyhedrovirus (NPV). Leafhopper (Amarasca biguttula biguttula) and Whitefly (Bemisia tabaci) act as vectors for Yellow Mosaic Virus (YMV) in mungbean and urdbean.
  • Fungal Diseases: Wilt caused by Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. ciceri in chickpea is a soil-borne vascular disease causing sudden drying of plants. Management: Deep summer ploughing, crop rotation, and use of resistant varieties (e.g., Avrodhi). Ascochyta Blight (Ascochyta rabiei) and Sterility Mosaic Virus (vector: Aceria cajani) in pigeonpea.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Chickpea is also known as Bengal gram, Gram, or Chana, and belongs to the family Fabaceae with chromosome number 2n = 16.
  • Pigeonpea is a cross-pollinated crop facilitated by insects (often cross-pollinated up to 20-70%).
  • Single Super SSP is preferred in pulse fertilization because it supplies 16% ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5, 19% Calcium, and 12% Sulphur.
  • Yellow Mosaic Virus (YMV) in mungbean and urdbean is transmitted by the whitefly vector Bemisia tabaci.
  • Nipping (removal of apical buds) is a unique cultural practice in chickpea performed at 30-45 DAS to encourage lateral branching and increase pod yield.
  • The most destructive insect pest of chickpea and pigeonpea is the gram pod borer, Helicoverpa armigera.
  • Rhizobium inoculation should always be done in the shade to prevent ultraviolet ray damage to living bacteria.
  • Protein content in pulses generally ranges from 20% to 25%, with soybean exceptionally having 40% protein (though botanically an oilseed-pulse).
  • Avrodhi is a wilt-resistant variety of chickpea recommended for cultivation in Uttar Pradesh.
  • Optimum storage moisture content for safe storage of pulse grains is 9% to 10%.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Error: Confusing Kabuli chickpea seed rate with Desi chickpea seed rate. Scientific Fact: Kabuli chickpea has larger seeds and requires 100 kg/ha seed rate, whereas Desi chickpea requires 75-80 kg/ha.
  • Error: Applying heavy nitrogenous fertilization to boost pulse growth. Scientific Fact: Excessive nitrogen inhibits root nodulation and nitrogenase activity; only a starter dose of 15-20 kg N/ha is recommended.
  • Error: Assuming pulses can withstand waterlogging. Scientific Fact: Pulses are extremely susceptible to waterlogging; poor aeration leads to root rot and mortality.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Comprehensive Agronomy of Pulses (Grain Legumes) for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Botanical Taxonomy and Cytogenetics of Grain Legumes

Pulses belong to the family Fabaceae and are characterized by papilionaceous corollas, diadelphous stamens, and root nodules housing symbiotic Rhizobium bacteria.

  • Chromosome Number: Chickpea (2n=16), Pigeonpea (2n=22), Lentil (2n=14), Field Pea (2n=14).
  • Symbiosis: Atmospheric nitrogen fixation converts inert ext{N}_2 gas into ammonia via the nitrogenase enzyme complex.
Concept 2

Seed Technology and Inoculation Protocols

Rigorous seed selection, chemical dressing, and microbial inoculation guarantee robust crop establishment.

  • Fungicide Dressing: Carbendazim @ 2.5 g/kg seed controls seed-borne and early soil-borne fungi.
  • Biofertilizers: Rhizobium + PSB co-inoculation maximizes phosphorus availability and biological nitrogen fixation.
Concept 3

Nutrient and Water Management Strategies

Balanced fertilization and precise irrigation scheduling prevent vegetative over-growth and enhance harvest index.

  • Phosphorus Role: Basal application of SSP promotes extensive root architecture and pod setting.
  • Critical Irrigation: Restricting water stress during pre-flowering and pod filling phases safeguards grain yield.
Concept 4

Integrated Pest and Disease Management (IPM)

Managing biotic stresses requires combining cultural practices, resistant varieties, and judicious chemical interventions.

  • Helicoverpa armigera: Managed through pheromone traps, NPV sprays, and bird perches.
  • Fusarium Wilt: Soil-borne pathogen controlled via resistant cultivars like Avrodhi and deep summer ploughing.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Nipping (Apical bud removal)
The manual or chemical removal of apical growing tips in chickpea to promote lateral branching and increase flower production.
Rhizobium (Symbiotic diazotroph)
A genus of soil bacteria that fix atmospheric nitrogen within root nodules of legumes.
Diadelphous (Staminal tube arrangement (9+1))
An androecium condition where stamens are fused into two bundles, characteristic of the subfamily Papilionoideae.
Harvest Index (HI = (Economic Yield / Biological Yield) × 100)
The ratio of economic grain yield to total biological plant dry matter yield.
Ascochyta Blight (Causal organism: Ascochyta rabiei)
A devastating fungal disease in chickpea causing concentric rings on leaves and pods during humid weather.
Sterility Mosaic (Pigeonpea viral disease)
A viral disease of pigeonpea transmitted by the eriophyid mite vector Aceria cajani, causing chlorosis and cessation of pod formation.
Nitrogenase (Metalloenzyme complex (Mo-Fe protein))
The key enzyme responsible for biological reduction of atmospheric nitrogen to ammonia in root nodules.
Kabuli Gram (Cicer arietinum var. kabuli)
Chickpea type characterized by white flowers, large beige-coloured seeds, and smooth seed coats.
Desi Gram (Cicer arietinum var. inum / desi)
Chickpea type characterized by coloured flowers, small angular seeds, and high fiber content.
YMV (Yellow Mosaic Virus)
A viral disease affecting mungbean and urdbean, causing severe yellow mottling of leaves, transmitted by Bemisia tabaci.
Symbiosis (Mutualistic biological association)
A mutually beneficial relationship between legume roots and Rhizobium bacteria where plants provide carbohydrates and bacteria provide fixed nitrogen.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Kabuli Chickpea VS Desi Chickpea

Kabuli has 2n=16, white flowers, large bold seeds, and lower acreage in India; Desi has 2n=16, coloured flowers, smaller angular seeds, and accounts for ~85% of Indian chickpea production.

Mungbean VS Urdbean

Mungbean is scientifically Vigna radiata (green seeds), whereas Urdbean is Vigna mungo (black seeds and mucilaginous seed coat).

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the diploid chromosome number (2n) of chickpea (gram)?

Explanation: Chickpea (Cicer arietinum) has a diploid chromosome number of 2n = 2x = 16. Lentil and field pea have 2n = 14, while pigeonpea, mungbean, and urdbean have 2n = 22.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which cultural operation involves the removal of apical buds in chickpea to encourage lateral branching?

Explanation: Nipping is the manual or chemical removal of terminal growing tips (apical buds) in chickpea. This breaks apical dominance, promotes profuse lateral branching, and ultimately increases flower and pod production.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is the most critical irrigation stage for chickpea?

Explanation: The two critical stages of irrigation in chickpea are the pre-flowering stage (30-35 DAS) and the pod formation / grain filling stage (75-80 DAS). (Note: CRI is specific to wheat).

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended seed rate for Kabuli chickpea compared to Desi chickpea?

Explanation: Due to the bolder and larger seed size of Kabuli chickpea (higher test weight), a higher seed rate of approximately 100 kg/ha is required, whereas Desi chickpea requires 75-80 kg/ha.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which bacterium is responsible for symbiotic nitrogen fixation in pigeonpea and mungbean root nodules?

Explanation: Pigeonpea, mungbean, and urdbean are nodulated by Bradyrhizobium species (cowpea miscellany group), while Rhizobium leguminosarum specifically infects lentil, pea, and vetch.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which fertilizer is most ideal for supplying phosphorus, calcium, and sulphur simultaneously to pulse crops?

Explanation: Single Super Phosphate (SSP) is highly recommended for pulses because in addition to 16% phosphorus, it provides 19% calcium and 12% sulphur, both of which are essential for nodulation and protein synthesis.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the causal organism of wilt in chickpea?

Explanation: Chickpea wilt is caused by the soil-borne fungus Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. ciceri. It blocks xylem vessels, causing wilting and drooping of foliage without chlorosis.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Yellow Mosaic Virus (YMV) in mungbean and urdbean is transmitted by which insect vector?

Explanation: Yellow Mosaic Virus (YMV) is a major viral constraint in pulse crops like mungbean, urdbean, and soybean, and is efficiently transmitted by the whitefly vector Bemisia tabaci.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the safe moisture percentage for long-term safe storage of pulse grains?

Explanation: Pulse grains must be dried to 9% to 10% moisture content before storage to prevent bruchid beetle infestation (Callosobruchus chinensis) and fungal mold growth.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following pulses has a chromosome number of 2n = 22?

Explanation: Pigeonpea (Cajanus cajan), mungbean (Vigna radiata), and urdbean (Vigna mungo) all possess a diploid chromosome number of 2n = 22.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Avrodhi is a popular, wilt-resistant variety of which pulse crop?

Explanation: Avrodhi is a well-known, high-yielding, and Fusarium wilt-resistant variety of chickpea (gram) widely recommended for cultivation across Uttar Pradesh.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of seed inoculation with Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria (PSB) alongside Rhizobium?

Explanation: PSB (such as Bacillus megaterium) secrete organic acids that solubilize fixed and insoluble inorganic phosphorus compounds in the soil, making ext{P} readily available for root uptake and nodule development.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest is universally referred to as the gram pod borer?

Explanation: Helicoverpa armigera (American bollworm / Gram pod borer) is a highly destructive polyphagous pest that bores into developing pods of chickpea, pigeonpea, and tomato, consuming the grain inside.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the botanical name of black gram (urdbean)?

Explanation: Black gram (urdbean) is scientifically named Vigna mungo (2n = 22), whereas green gram (mungbean) is Vigna radiata.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Why is heavy nitrogenous fertilizer application discouraged in pulse cultivation?

Explanation: When abundant mineral nitrogen is present in the soil solution, legume plants do not invest energy in forming symbiotic root nodules with Rhizobium, drastically reducing biological nitrogen fixation.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Sterility mosaic disease of pigeonpea is vectored by:

Explanation: Sterility mosaic disease, characterized by mosaic symptoms and complete cessation of flower/pod production in pigeonpea, is transmitted by the microscopic eriophyid mite vector Aceria cajani.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical protein content found in most pulse crops?

Explanation: Pulse grains generally contain between 20% and 25% protein on a dry weight basis, which is roughly double the protein content found in cereal grains.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which state is the leading producer of pulses in India?

Explanation: Madhya Pradesh is the leading producer of pulses in India, followed by Maharashtra, Rajasthan, and Uttar Pradesh, contributing significantly to national pulse basket production.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a Rabi pulse crop?

Explanation: Lentil (Lens culinaris) is a prominent Rabi season pulse crop, whereas pigeonpea, mungbean, and urdbean are primarily grown during the Kharif or summer seasons.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Analyze the statement: 'Nipping in chickpea increases seed yield by promoting vegetative canopy over reproductive sinks.' Is this statement scientifically accurate?

Solution & Rationale: Nipping removes apical dominance, redirecting energy and nutrients from excessive vegetative stem elongation toward lateral branches, flowers, and pod development, thereby increasing economic yield.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

In a fertilizer calculation for chickpea, if the recommended dose of nitrogen is 20 kg N/ha and phosphorus is 40 kg ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5/ha, how much Single Super Phosphate (SSP containing 16% ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5) is required per hectare?

Solution & Rationale: Formula: (Required ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5 / % Nutrient in fertilizer) × 100 = (40 / 16) imes 100 = 2.5 imes 100 = 250 kg of SSP per hectare.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

Why is seed inoculation with Rhizobium ineffective when soils are highly acidic (pH < 5.5)?

Solution & Rationale: Low pH (high acidity) impairs Rhizobium survival in soil, inhibits root hair curling (the infection process), and disrupts the stability of the nitrogenase enzyme complex. Liming is required beforehand.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Which agronomic practice is most effective in mitigating the adverse effects of terminal drought in rainfed Rabi chickpea?

Solution & Rationale: Under terminal moisture stress (drought during grain filling), foliar application of 2% Urea or ext{KNO}_3 sustains leaf turgor, prevents premature senescence, and aids grain filling.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

What distinguishes Kabuli chickpea from Desi chickpea cytologically and morphologically?

Solution & Rationale: Both Kabuli and Desi types belong to Cicer arietinum and share the same chromosome number (2n=16), but differ in flower color, seed coat color, and seed size.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary reason for low harvest index in indeterminate pigeonpea varieties when grown under high fertility conditions?

Solution & Rationale: Indeterminate pigeonpea continues vegetative growth under high moisture and nitrogen, leading to luxuriant foliage growth (source-sink imbalance) and reduced harvest index.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following pairs of Causal Organism and Disease in pulses is correctly matched?

Solution & Rationale: Chickpea wilt is caused by Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. ciceri. Ascochyta blight is caused by Ascochyta rabiei, and sterility mosaic affects pigeonpea (vectored by Aceria cajani).

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

How does intercropping cereals with pulses (e.g., Pearl Millet + Pigeonpea) improve land equivalent ratio (LER)?

Solution & Rationale: Intercropping cereals and legumes utilizes temporal and spatial niche differentiation—deep-rooted pigeonpea taps lower soil strata while shallow-rooted cereals utilize upper soil layers, yielding an LER > 1.0.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary function of leghemoglobin found in active root nodules of pulse crops?

Solution & Rationale: Leghemoglobin is an oxygen-binding protein in root nodules that buffers oxygen concentration, keeping free oxygen low enough to protect the oxygen-sensitive nitrogenase enzyme while supplying enough oxygen for bacterial respiration.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

Under integrated pest management (IPM) for Helicoverpa armigera in chickpea, what is the role of installing bird perches at 50 per hectare?

Solution & Rationale: Bird perches attract insectivorous birds (such as mynahs and drongos) which actively predate upon Helicoverpa armigera larvae, serving as an effective biological control component in IPM.
