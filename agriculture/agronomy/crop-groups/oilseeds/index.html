Botanical Taxonomy, Origin, and Economic Classification of Oilseed Crops

Oilseed crops are predominantly cultivated for their edible and industrial oils extracted from seeds. Based on their life cycle and agro-climatic adaptability, they are categorized into Rabi and Kharif oilseeds.

  • Groundnut (Peanut): Arachis hypogaea, family Fabaceae, origin South America (Brazil). Diploid/Tetraploid (2n = 40). Known as the 'King of oilseeds' in India.
  • Rapeseed-Mustard: Includes Indian mustard (Brassica juncea, 2n = 36), Toria (Brassica rapa var. toria, 2n = 20), and Yellow sarson (Brassica rapa var. yellow sarson, 2n = 20), family Brassicaceae, origin Central Asia/China.
  • Soybean: Glycine max, family Fabaceae, origin China (2n = 40). Dual-purpose crop (oil and protein).
  • Sunflower: Helianthus annuus, family Asteraceae, origin North America (2n = 34). Highly photo-insensitive, cross-pollinated crop.
  • Sesame (Til): Sesamum indicum, family Pedaliaceae, origin Africa (2n = 26). Known as the oldest oilseed crop known to humanity.

Soil, Agro-Climatic Requirements, and Field Preparation

Oilseed crops require well-drained soils since they are highly sensitive to waterlogging, particularly at germination and flowering stages.

  • Groundnut: Requires light sandy loam to sandy soils. Heavy clay causes pod deformation and harvesting difficulties. Optimum pH is 6.0 to 6.5.
  • Rapeseed-Mustard: Thrives best in medium to heavy loams with neutral pH (6.5 to 7.5). Requires cool, dry winters with bright sunshine.
  • Soybean: Well-drained fertile loam soils with pH 6.0 to 7.5. Avoid fields with poor drainage to prevent root rot diseases.
  • Field Preparation: Fine tilth is essential for tiny-seeded oilseeds like mustard and sesame to ensure proper seed-soil contact. Deep ploughing followed by 2-3 harrowings and planking is standard practice.

Seed Rate, Sowing Geometry, and Seed Treatment Protocols

Proper plant population and seed health management form the foundation of high-yielding oilseed production systems.

  • Groundnut: Seed rate is 100-120 kg/ha (kernels) for bunch types and 80-90 kg/ha for spreading types. Spacing: 30 × 10 cm (bunch) and 45 × 15 cm (spreading). Seed treatment with Rhizobium and Trichoderma viride @ 4-5 g/kg seed is mandatory.
  • Mustard: Seed rate is 4-5 kg/ha. Spacing: 30 × 10 cm. Seed treatment with Metalaxyl @ 6 g/kg seed prevents white rust and downy mildew.
  • Soybean: Seed rate is 75-80 kg/ha. Spacing: 45 × 5 cm. Inoculation with Bradyrhizobium japonicum is essential for biological nitrogen fixation.
  • Sunflower: Seed rate is 8-10 kg/ha. Spacing: 60 × 30 cm. Hand pollination or bee pollination enhancement is recommended for hybrid seed sets.

Nutrient Management and Fertilizer Recommendation Calculations

Oilseeds have high energy requirements for lipid synthesis, demanding balanced fertilization of Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potassium (K), and especially Sulphur (S).

  • Sulphur Requirement: Sulphur is indispensable for oilseed crops as it is a constituent of amino acids (methionine, cysteine) and enhances oil content. Application of gypsum @ 200-250 kg/ha is a standard agronomic recommendation.
  • Groundnut (N:P:K): Recommended dose is 20:60:40 kg NPK/ha along with gypsum. Calcium is critical for pod filling ('pegging').
  • Mustard (N:P:K): Recommended dose is 80:40:40 kg NPK/ha + 40 kg S/ha. Half N and full P, K, and S are applied basal, remaining N is top-dressed at first irrigation.
  • Soybean (N:P:K): Recommended dose is 20:60:40 kg NPK/ha. Starter dose of nitrogen is applied since biological nitrogen fixation starts only after 12-15 days of germination.

Critical Stages of Irrigation and Weed Management

Moisture stress during critical developmental phases leads to drastic yield penalties in oilseed production.

  • Critical Irrigation Stages: Groundnut: Flower initiation, pegging, and pod development. Mustard: Pre-flowering (30-35 DAS) and pod filling (60-65 DAS). Soybean: Flowering and pod formation. Sunflower: Budding, flowering, and seed filling.
  • Weed Management: Crop-weed competition is severe during the first 30-45 DAS (critical period of crop-weed competition).
  • Herbicides: Pendimethalin @ 1.0 kg a.i./ha applied as pre-emergence controls annual grasses and broad-leaved weeds in soybean and groundnut. Fluchloralin is incorporated pre-plant for mustard.

Insect Pests, Diseases, and Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

Plant protection requires continuous monitoring and adherence to economic threshold levels (ETL) to minimize chemical residues.

  • Major Pests: Aphids (Lipaphis erysimi) on mustard; Whitefly (Bemisia tabaci) acting as a vector for yellow mosaic virus in soybean; Leaf miner (Aproaerema modicella) in groundnut; Heliothis pod borer (Helicoverpa armigera) in sunflower and chickpea/oilseed cropping systems.
  • Major Diseases: Alternaria blight (Alternaria brassicae) in mustard; White rust (Albugo candida) in mustard; Collar rot / Stem rot (Sclerotium rolfsii) in groundnut; Charcoal rot (Macrophomina phaseolina) in soybean.
  • IPM Strategies: Deep summer ploughing to expose pupae, use of resistant varieties, seed treatment with biological agents, installation of yellow sticky traps @ 10-12/ha for sucking pests, and need-based application of quinalphos or imidacloprid.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Groundnut is a self-pollinated legume crop exhibiting geocarpy (underground pod development).
  • Sulphur is crucial for oilseeds because it increases oil content and protein synthesis via sulfur-containing amino acids.
  • Albugo candida is the causal organism of white rust in mustard, characterized by white creamy pustules on leaves and inflorescence.
  • Bradyrhizobium japonicum is the specific bacterium used for soybean seed inoculation to fix atmospheric nitrogen.
  • Pendimethalin is a widely used pre-emergence selective herbicide in kharif oilseeds like soybean and groundnut.
  • The 'pegging' stage in groundnut refers to the downward movement of fertilized ovaries into the soil where pods develop.
  • Sunflower is a cross-pollinated crop where capitulum (head) positioning follows heliotropism before flowering.
  • Toria (Brassica rapa var. toria) is a short-duration catch crop grown in multi-cropping systems during the Rabi season.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Error: Confusing geocarpy of groundnut with cleistogamy. Scientific fact: Groundnut exhibits cleistogamy (flowers pollinated before opening) followed by geocarpy (pods maturing underground), whereas crops like wheat exhibit cleistogamy without underground pod formation.
  • Error: Assuming nitrogen is the most critical nutrient for oil percentage. Scientific fact: Sulphur (S) is the quality-determining nutrient for oilseeds, not nitrogen, although nitrogen is required for vegetative growth.
  • Error: Applying post-emergence broadleaf herbicides in legume oilseeds without crop safety caution. Scientific fact: Most broadleaf herbicides injure legumes; selective pre-emergence herbicides like pendimethalin or cultural hand weeding are preferred.

Concept 1

Botanical and Cytogenetic Basis of Oilseeds

Understanding the chromosome number and ploidy level is vital for plant breeding and hybrid seed production in oilseeds.

  • Tri-genome Concept: U's Triangle explains the genomic relationships among cultivated Brassica species (B. oleracea, B. nigra, B. rapa, B. napus, B. juncea, B. carinata).
  • Geocarpy: Unique subterranean pod development seen in groundnut where gynophores (pegs) penetrate the soil.
Concept 2

Nutritional Role of Sulphur and Rhizobium Inoculation

Oilseed production technology relies heavily on biochemical enhancements through specialized fertilizers and biofertilizers.

  • Gypsum Application: Supplies both calcium (preventing 'pops' or empty pods in groundnut) and sulphur.
  • Symbiotic Fixation: Rhizobium and Bradyrhizobium strains reduce atmospheric dinitrogen into ammonia within root nodules of Fabaceae oilseeds.
Concept 3

Critical Stages and Irrigation Efficiency

Managing water stress during sensitive reproductive stages prevents flower drop and incomplete seed setting.

  • CRI Equivalent in Mustard: Pre-flowering (30-35 DAS) is the most sensitive physiological stage for moisture stress.
  • Transpiration Ratio: High in oilseeds due to lipid synthesis energy demand; excess moisture stagnation causes root asphyxiation.
Concept 4

Integrated Pest and Disease Management (IPM)

Management of biotic stresses requires combining cultural, biological, and chemical methods.

  • ETL (Economic Threshold Level): Pest population density at which control measures must be initiated to prevent economic damage.
  • Vector Management: Controlling whitefly (Bemisia tabaci) is essential to prevent yellow mosaic virus epidemics in soybean.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Geocarpy (Subterranean Pod Maturation)
The botanical phenomenon where fertilized flowers penetrate the soil and develop into underground pods, characteristic of groundnut (Arachis hypogaea).
Peg (Gynophore)
The stalk-like structure carrying the fertilized ovule downward into the soil in groundnut plants.
U's Triangle (Brassica Genomic Triangle)
Cytogenetic model demonstrating genomic relationships and inter-specific hybridization among six cultivated Brassica species.
Glucosinolates (Thioglycosides)
Secondary plant metabolites found in Brassicaceae crops responsible for pungency in mustard oil and meal.
Canola (Double Zero Brassica)
Rapeseed cultivars bred to have very low levels of erucic acid in oil and low glucosinolates in meal.
Heliotropism (Solar Tracking)
The directional movement of plant organs (such as sunflower heads) in response to the sun's position.
Pop Pods (Empty Groundnut Shells)
Physiological disorder in groundnut caused by calcium deficiency resulting in pods without kernels.
CRI Stage (Crown Root Initiation)
Critical irrigation milestone, though primarily cereal terminology, analogous to pre-flowering critical moisture stages in mustard.
Bradyrhizobium (Bradyrhizobium japonicum)
Symbiotic bacterium used as inoculant for soybean seeds to enhance atmospheric nitrogen fixation.
Albugo candida (White Rust Pathogen)
Oomycete fungal pathogen causing white blister-like pustules on mustard leaves and inflorescences.
Capitulum (Composite Flower Head)
Inflorescence structure characteristic of Asteraceae family crops like sunflower.
Pendimethalin (Dinitroaniline Herbicide)
Selective pre-emergence herbicide used for weed control in kharif oilseed crops like soybean and groundnut.

  • Indian mustard botanical name: Brassica juncea (2n = 36).
  • Soybean origin: China; Groundnut origin: Brazil/South America.
  • White rust of mustard is caused by Albugo candida.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Oilseeds are rich sources of energy, essential fatty acids, and proteins, categorized into Kharif (groundnut, soybean, sesame, sunflower) and Rabi (mustard, toria, linseed) groups.
  • Groundnut exhibits geocarpy and requires adequate calcium and sulphur during the pegging stage.
  • Sulphur fertilization (via gypsum or elemental sulfur) is indispensable for maximizing oil recovery and protein quality in Brassicaceae crops.
  • Soybean requires specific inoculation with Bradyrhizobium japonicum for efficient nitrogen fixation.
  • Integrated Pest Management combining resistant varieties, seed treatment, and economic threshold monitoring ensures sustainable oilseed production.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Geocarpy VS Cleistogamy

Geocarpy is underground pod development, whereas cleistogamy is pollination occurring within unopened flowers.

Canola VS Toria

Canola refers to low-erucic acid, low-glucosinolate rapeseed varieties, whereas Toria is a distinct short-duration Brassica species (Brassica rapa var. toria).

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following oilseed crops exhibits the unique botanical phenomenon of geocarpy?

Explanation: Groundnut (Arachis hypogaea) exhibits geocarpy, where fertilized flowers produce pegs that bend downward and penetrate the soil where pods mature.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosome number (2n) of Indian mustard (Brassica juncea)?

Explanation: Indian mustard (Brassica juncea) is an amphidiploid with 2n = 36, combining genomes from B. nigra (2n = 16) and B. rapa (2n = 20).

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which nutrient is considered the 'quality-determining element' for enhancing oil content and protein synthesis in oilseeds?

Explanation: Sulphur is essential for oilseed crops because it is a key component of sulphur-containing amino acids and is directly involved in lipid biosynthesis.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the causal organism of white rust in rapeseed-mustard?

Explanation: Albugo candida is the causal organism of white rust in mustard, producing prominent white pustules on foliar surfaces and floral parts.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which specific biofertilizer is recommended for seed inoculation in soybean to ensure effective biological nitrogen fixation?

Explanation: Bradyrhizobium japonicum is the specific root-nodule bacterium used for inoculating soybean seeds to optimize nitrogen fixation.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended seed rate (kernels) for bunch type groundnut per hectare?

Explanation: The optimum seed rate for bunch-type groundnut kernels is 100-120 kg/ha, while spreading types require 80-90 kg/ha.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which herbicide is commonly applied as a pre-emergence selective weed killer in soybean and groundnut?

Explanation: Pendimethalin is widely used as a pre-emergence herbicide in kharif oilseeds like soybean and groundnut to control weeds during the critical early crop-weed competition phase.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary reason for 'pop' (empty pods) formation in groundnut cultivation?

Explanation: Calcium deficiency in the pod zone leads to unfilled or hollow pods ('pops') in groundnut, corrected by applying gypsum.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following oilseed crops is a cross-pollinated crop belonging to the family Asteraceae?

Explanation: Sunflower (Helianthus annuus) belongs to the family Asteraceae and is primarily cross-pollinated by insects.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

U's Triangle demonstrates the cytogenetic relationships among cultivated species of which genus?

Explanation: U's Triangle (proposed by Dr. Woo Jang-keun) explains the genomic evolution and inter-specific hybridization among six cultivated Brassica species.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the critical stage of irrigation for mustard crop to avoid moisture stress and yield reduction?

Explanation: Pre-flowering (30-35 DAS) and pod filling stages are the most critical irrigation stages for mustard.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is 'Canola' quality oilseed primarily characterized by?

Explanation: Canola refers to rapeseed varieties bred to have less than 2% erucic acid in the oil and less than 30 micromoles of glucosinolates per gram in the meal.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest acts as a vector for Yellow Mosaic Virus in soybean?

Explanation: Whitefly (Bemisia tabaci) transmits the Gemini virus responsible for Yellow Mosaic Virus in soybean and mungbean.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the origin of Groundnut (Arachis hypogaea)?

Explanation: Groundnut originates from South America, specifically the region covering Brazil, Bolivia, and Paraguay.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is an oilseed crop often referred to as 'Til' and belongs to the family Pedaliaceae?

Explanation: Sesame (Sesamum indicum), commonly known as Til, belongs to the family Pedaliaceae.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended fertilizer NPK dose for Indian mustard under general irrigated conditions?

Explanation: The general recommended NPK dose for irrigated Indian mustard is 80 kg N, 40 kg P2O5, and 40 kg K2O per hectare.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which pathogen causes Alternaria blight in mustard, forming concentric rings on leaves and siliquae?

Explanation: Alternaria brassicae causes Alternaria blight in mustard, characterized by dark brown spots with characteristic concentric rings.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of applying Gypsum @ 200-250 kg/ha in groundnut at flowering?

Explanation: Gypsum application supplies readily available calcium for pod filling and sulphur for oil synthesis in groundnut.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop is known as the 'Miracle Crop' due to its dual utility as a high-protein food and vegetable oil source?

Explanation: Soybean (Glycine max) is termed the 'Miracle Crop' because of its high nutritive value, rich protein content (~40%), and edible oil content (~20%).

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the optimum temperature range for germination and vegetative growth of rapeseed-mustard?

Explanation: Rapeseed-mustard is a cool-season Rabi crop requiring an optimum temperature range of 18-25 °C for robust growth and development.

In U's Triangle, which species represents the amphidiploid resulting from the hybridization of Brassica oleracea and Brassica rapa?

Solution & Rationale: Brassica napus (rapeseed/canola) is an amphidiploid (2n = 38, AACC genome) derived from the interspecific cross between Brassica oleracea (2n = 18, CC) and Brassica rapa (2n = 20, AA).

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Analyze the following statements regarding groundnut agronomy: 1. Groundnut is a short-day plant with self-pollination. 2. Pegging is negatively affected by calcium deficiency in the pod zone. 3. Spreading varieties require a higher seed rate than bunch varieties. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Solution & Rationale: Statement 1 is correct (groundnut is self-pollinated and short-day). Statement 2 is correct (calcium is vital for pegging and pod filling). Statement 3 is incorrect because bunch types require higher seed rates (100-120 kg/ha) than spreading types (80-90 kg/ha) due to closer planting geometry.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the economic threshold level (ETL) and primary damage symptom caused by the mustard aphid (Lipaphis erysimi)?

Solution & Rationale: Lipaphis erysimi is a serious sap-sucking pest of mustard. The ETL is 10-15 aphids per 10 cm terminal shoot, causing curling of leaves, stunted growth, and sooty mold development on honeydew.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Why is sulphur fertilization specifically emphasized in oilseed production over cereals?

Solution & Rationale: Sulphur is vital for oilseeds because it is required for synthesizing sulphur-containing amino acids (methionine, cysteine) and plays a direct enzymatic role in converting carbohydrates into lipids.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Match the oilseed crop with its respective family: A. Groundnut - Asteraceae B. Sunflower - Pedaliaceae C. Sesame - Brassicaceae D. Mustard - Brassicaceae Which of the matched pairs is correct?

Solution & Rationale: Groundnut belongs to Fabaceae, Sunflower to Asteraceae, Sesame to Pedaliaceae, and Mustard to Brassicaceae. Therefore, only pairing D (Mustard - Brassicaceae) is correct.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Which physiological disorder in groundnut is directly mitigated by the application of gypsum at the pegging stage?

Solution & Rationale: Calcium deficiency in the fruiting zone causes hollow or empty pods known as 'pops', which is corrected by applying gypsum.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the chromosomal number (2n) and genome constitution of cultivated groundnut (Arachis hypogaea)?

Solution & Rationale: Cultivated groundnut (Arachis hypogaea) is an allotetraploid with 2n = 40, possessing an AABB genome derived from wild species like A. duranensis and A. ipaensis.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

In soybean cultivation, why is a starter dose of nitrogen recommended along with Rhizobium inoculation?

Solution & Rationale: Although soybean is a legume, symbiotic nitrogen fixation does not begin until root nodules are formed and functional (about 12-15 days after germination); hence, a small starter dose of nitrogen is necessary.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following herbicides is classified as a pre-plant incorporation (PPI) herbicide used in mustard cultivation?

Solution & Rationale: Fluchloralin is typically applied as a pre-plant incorporation (PPI) herbicide in mustard and other oilseed crops to control annual grasses and weeds effectively.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary harvesting index criterion for mustard crops?

Solution & Rationale: Mustard is ready for harvest when 75% of the siliquae turn yellowish-brown and seed moisture content drops to 20-25% before complete drying to prevent shattering.
