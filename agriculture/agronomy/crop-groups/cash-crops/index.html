Cash Crops: Agronomy, Production Technology and Management

Master the comprehensive production technology, botanical classifications, nutrient calculations, and protection measures of major commercial cash crops tailored for Uttar Pradesh TGT and PGT Agriculture competitive examinations.

1. Botanical Taxonomy, Origin and Chromosomal Profile of Major Cash Crops

Cash crops or commercial crops are cultivated primarily for sale in domestic and international markets rather than for direct family consumption. A thorough understanding of their botanical taxonomy, family, origin, and cytogenetics is essential for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture examinations.

  • Sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum): Belongs to the family Poaceae (Gramineae), tribe Andropogoneae. Origin: New Guinea. Chromosomal number: 2n = 8x = 80 (aneuploid/polyploid series). It is a classic C4 long-duration perennial grass cultivated as an annual.
  • Cotton (Gossypium spp.): Belongs to the family Malvaceae. Origin: India (for Gossypium arboreum and Gossypium herbaceum) and Central America/South America (for New World tetraploids Gossypium hirsutum and Gossypium barbadense). Chromosomal number: 2n = 26 (Diploids: G. arboreum, G. herbaceum) and 2n = 52 (Tetraploids: G. hirsutum, G. barbadense).
  • Jute (Corchorus spp.): Belongs to the family Malvaceae (formerly Tiliaceae). Origin: Indo-Burma region (Corchorus capsularis - White jute) and Africa (Corchorus olitorius - Tossa jute). Chromosomal number: 2n = 14.
  • Tobacco (Nicotiana tabacum): Belongs to the family Solanaceae. Origin: Tropical America (South America). Chromosomal number: 2n = 48 (Amphidiploid).

2. Agro-Climatic Requirements, Soil and Field Preparation

Successful establishment and yield maximization of cash crops depend on precise matching of agro-climatic conditions and meticulous seedbed preparation.

  • Sugarcane: Thrives in tropical and subtropical climates with a temperature range of 20°C to 35°C and bright sunshine. Requires deep, well-drained loamy to clay-loam soils with a pH of 6.5 to 7.5. Field preparation involves 3 to 4 ploughings followed by harrowing and planking to achieve a fine tilth, creating ridges and furrows for planting setts.
  • Cotton: Requires a warm, sunny climate with a frost-free period of at least 200 days and an optimum temperature of 21°C to 30°C. Deep black cotton soils (Vertisols) with high moisture-retention capacity are ideal, though alluvial and red soils with good drainage also support cultivation. Deep ploughing followed by disc harrowing and leveling is mandatory.
  • Jute: Prefers a hot, humid climate with high rainfall (1000–1500 mm) and temperatures ranging from 24°C to 38°C. Alluvial loamy to clay-loam soils renewed by annual river silt deposition are best. Land is prepared by repeated ploughing and laddering before the pre-monsoon showers.

3. Seed Rate, Planting Geometry, and Propagation Methods

Plant population density and spatial arrangement dictate light interception, inter-row competition, and final commercial yield.

  • Sugarcane Propagation: Vegetative propagation using three-budded setts. Seed rate is 35,000 to 40,000 setts per hectare (approx. 60–75 quintals of setts). Spacing is maintained at 75 cm to 90 cm between rows. Sett treatment with Carbendazim (0.1%) is critical before planting.
  • Cotton Planting: Propagated via seeds. Seed rate depends on the species: Desi cotton (G. arboreum) requires 12–15 kg/ha; American cotton (G. hirsutum) requires 15–20 kg/ha; Hybrid cotton requires 1.5–2.5 kg/ha. Spacing: 60–90 cm between rows and 30–45 cm between plants.
  • Jute Sowing: Broadcasted or line-sown. Seed rate is 6–8 kg/ha for line sowing and 10 kg/ha for broadcasting. Line spacing is maintained at 25 cm with a plant-to-plant distance of 5–7 cm after thinning.

4. Nutrient Management and Fertilizer Calculation

High biomass and commercial yield extraction require heavy nutrient application, balanced between macro- and micronutrients.

  • Sugarcane Nutrient Recommendation: General recommendation is 150–200 kg N, 60–80 kg P₂O₅, and 60–80 kg K₂O per hectare. Nitrogen is applied in three split doses (basal, tillering, and grand growth phase). Phosphorus is applied entirely as a basal dose, while potassium is split between basal and earthing-up stages.
  • Cotton Nutrient Recommendation: Recommended dose is 100–120 kg N, 50–60 kg P₂O₅, and 50–60 kg K₂O per hectare for hybrids. Nitrogen is split into 4 equal installments: basal, squaring, flowering, and boll development stages.
  • Fertilizer Calculation Example: To supply 120 kg N using Urea (containing 46% N), the quantity of urea required is calculated as: \((120 imes 100) / 46 = 260.86\) kg of urea per hectare.

5. Irrigation Dynamics, Critical Stages, and Weed Management

Water management and weed control during early growth phases prevent severe economic yield reductions in cash crops.

  • Sugarcane Irrigation: Requires 20–25 irrigations depending on the season. Critical stages include germination phase, tillering phase, and grand growth phase (most critical). Drought during grand growth drastically reduces cane length and weight. Weed control is achieved by pre-emergence application of Atrazine at 1.0–1.5 kg a.i./ha coupled with intercultural operations during earthing-up.
  • Cotton Irrigation: Requires 5–7 irrigations. Critical stages are square formation, flowering, and boll development. Water stress during flowering causes heavy shedding of squares and bolls. Weeds are managed by pre-emergence application of Pendimethalin at 1.0 kg a.i./ha followed by hand weeding or inter-cultivation at 30 and 45 DAS.

6. Crop Protection: Pests, Diseases, and Economic Threshold Levels (ETL)

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) is vital for managing destructive insect pests and phytopathogenic fungi in commercial crops.

  • Sugarcane Pests and Diseases: Major pest is the Early Shoot Borer (Chilo infuscatellus), controlled by releasing parasitoid Trichogramma chilonis and applying Chlorpyriphos. Major disease is Red Rot of sugarcane caused by the fungus Colletotrichum falcatum, managed by using disease-free setts and resistant varieties like CoS 767.
  • Cotton Pests and Diseases: Major insect pests include the American Bollworm (Helicoverpa armigera) and Pink Bollworm (Pectinophora gossypiella), managed by installing pheromone traps (5/ha) and need-based application of Quinalphos or Spinosad. Major disease is Bacterial Blight caused by Xanthomonas citri pv. malvacearum, controlled by seed treatment with Streptocycline.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Red rot of sugarcane is caused by the fungus Colletotrichum falcatum, often called the 'cancer of sugarcane'.
  • The most critical stage for irrigation in sugarcane is the grand growth phase.
  • American cotton belongs to the species Gossypium hirsutum with a chromosome number of 2n = 52.
  • Ribboning is the process of fiber extraction associated with jute processing.
  • Atrazine is widely used as a selective pre-emergence herbicide in sugarcane cultivation.
  • Topping and detoppting are essential cultural operations performed in tobacco cultivation to enhance leaf size and quality.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Students often confuse the family of Jute; Jute belongs to Malvaceae (formerly Tiliaceae), not Tiliaceae alone in modern classification.
  • Confusing the chromosome number of diploid cotton (2n=26) with tetraploid American cotton (2n=52).
  • Assuming sugarcane can be successfully grown in waterlogged heavy clay soils; sugarcane requires well-drained soils despite high water requirements.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Cash Crops: Agronomy, Production Technology and Management

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Taxonomical and Cytogenetic Basis of Cash Crops

Cash crops exhibit diverse taxonomic backgrounds ranging from monocot Poaceae (sugarcane) to dicot Malvaceae (cotton and jute) and Solanaceae (tobacco).

  • Ploidy Variation: Ranging from diploid wild cottons (2n=26) to allopolyploid cultivated American upland cotton (2n=52).
Concept 2

Establishment and Planting Technology

Planting geometry and vegetative propagation techniques govern initial stand establishment and final economic yield.

  • Sett Selection: Sugarcane requires disease-free 3-budded setts treated with fungicides to prevent sett rot.
Concept 3

Nutrient and Water Management Precision

Commercial crops demand intensive nutrient budgeting and precise irrigation scheduling at moisture-sensitive physiological phases.

  • Grand Growth Phase: Peak nutrient uptake and water demand period in sugarcane.
Concept 4

Plant Protection and Integrated Pest Management

Bollworms in cotton and borers/fungal rots in sugarcane demand strict ETL monitoring and biological-chemical integration.

  • ETL Monitoring: Essential for preventing indiscriminate pesticide application and avoiding resistance development.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Sett (Vegetative propagule of Saccharum officinarum)
A short section of sugarcane stalk containing one or more buds used for vegetative planting.
Ratooning (Ratoon crop management)
Cultivation of a crop from the stubbs or roots of the harvested previous crop, highly prevalent in sugarcane.
Lint (Gossypium seed hair fiber)
The long, white, spinnable epidermal hair attached to cotton seeds after ginning.
Ginning Percentage (Lint-to-seedcotton ratio)
The percentage weight of lint obtained from a given weight of seed cotton ( kapas ).
Retting (Microbial decomposition of plant stems)
A microbiological process utilizing water immersion to loosen bast fibers from the woody core in jute processing.
Ribboning (Mechanical fiber stripping)
The extraction of ribbon-like bark strips containing bast fibers from green jute stalks prior to retting.
Topping (Terminal bud removal)
Manual removal of the apical growing tip in tobacco plants to divert nutrient flow toward leaf development.
Curing (Post-harvest biochemical processing)
A specialized drying and fermentation process applied to harvested tobacco leaves to develop desired aroma, color, and texture.
Square (Floral bud of Gossypium)
The triangular flower bud of the cotton plant enclosed by bracts, appearing prior to blooming.
Earthing-up (Soil mounding around crop base)
Agronomic practice of heaping soil around the base of crop plants (sugarcane, potato) to prevent lodging and support root development.
Economic Threshold Level (ETL) (Pest population density threshold)
The pest density at which control measures must be initiated to prevent economic damage.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Colletotrichum falcatum causes Red Rot of sugarcane.
  • Gossypium hirsutum accounts for over 90% of global cotton production.
  • Atrazine is a standard pre-emergence herbicide in sugarcane.
  • Jute retting temperature optimum is around 34°C.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Cash crops are high-value commercial commodities grown for market exchange.
  • Sugarcane is propagated via setts and requires warm tropical-subtropical conditions.
  • Cotton fiber quality is determined by staple length, strength, and micronaire value.
  • Jute fiber extraction relies heavily on microbial retting in slow-running water bodies.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

White Jute (Corchorus capsularis) VS Tossa Jute (Corchorus olitorius)

White jute can tolerate water stagnation and has round pods, whereas Tossa jute cannot tolerate waterlogging and has elongated cylindrical pods.

Desi Cotton VS American Cotton

Desi cotton comprises diploid species (G. arboreum, G. herbaceum) with 2n=26; American cotton comprises tetraploid species (G. hirsutum, G. barbadense) with 2n=52.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following cash crops belongs to the family Poaceae?

Explanation: Sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum) belongs to the family Poaceae (Gramineae). Cotton and jute belong to Malvaceae, and tobacco belongs to Solanaceae.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosomal number (2n) of cultivated American Upland Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum)?

Explanation: Gossypium hirsutum is an allotetraploid with 2n = 4x = 52 chromosomes. Diploid desi cottons have 2n = 26.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which disease is popularly known as the 'cancer of sugarcane'?

Explanation: Red rot of sugarcane caused by Colletotrichum falcatum is the most destructive fungal disease, often referred to as the cancer of sugarcane.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the most critical stage for irrigation in sugarcane?

Explanation: The grand growth phase is the most critical stage where water stress severely penalizes cane elongation, girth, and final yield.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical is standardly recommended as a pre-emergence selective herbicide in sugarcane?

Explanation: Atrazine is widely used as a pre-emergence herbicide in sugarcane fields at 1.0 to 1.5 kg a.i./ha.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the process of loosening bast fibers from the woody stalk of jute through microbial action called?

Explanation: Retting is the microbiological process where pectin-degrading bacteria dissolve gummy substances holding the bast fibers to the woody core.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following species represents Tossa Jute?

Explanation: Corchorus olitorius is known as Tossa Jute, whereas Corchorus capsularis is White Jute.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the seed rate requirement for hybrid cotton per hectare?

Explanation: Hybrid cotton requires only 1.5 to 2.5 kg/ha of seed due to wider spacing (e.g., 90 x 60 cm) and high cost of hybrid seeds.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The removal of terminal apical buds in tobacco cultivation is known as:

Explanation: Topping is the removal of the terminal flower head to enhance leaf expansion, thickness, and nicotine accumulation.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the economic portion of the cotton plant?

Explanation: The economic produce of cotton consists of lint (seed hair) and seed (yielding cotton seed oil and cake).

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest is a major threat during the early vegetative phase of sugarcane?

Explanation: Early shoot borer (Chilo infuscatellus) attacks young sugarcane tillers, causing characteristic deadhearts.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the optimum temperature range for jute cultivation?

Explanation: Jute thrives under hot and humid conditions with temperatures ranging between 24°C and 38°C and high relative humidity.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is an allopolyploid cash crop?

Explanation: Nicotiana tabacum is an amphidiploid (allopolyploid) with 2n = 48, derived from ancestral diploid species.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard fertilizer dose of Nitrogen recommended for high-yielding sugarcane?

Explanation: The recommended nitrogen dose for sugarcane is generally 150 to 200 kg N per hectare applied in splits.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does ginning percentage signify in cotton?

Explanation: Ginning percentage is the weight of lint obtained from 100 parts of seed cotton (kapas) expressed as a percentage.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which micro-organism group is primarily responsible for biological retting of jute?

Explanation: Pectinolytic bacteria such as Clostridium butyricum and Bacillus spp. break down pectic compounds during retting.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the botanical family of Tobacco?

Explanation: Tobacco (Nicotiana tabacum) belongs to the Solanaceae family.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which agronomic practice helps prevent lodging in sugarcane and supports lateral roots?

Explanation: Earthing-up provides mechanical support to tall sugarcane stalks, preventing lodging and promoting root aeration.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a New World diploid cotton species?

Explanation: Gossypium arboreum and Gossypium herbaceum are Old World diploid species (Asian cottons), whereas New World species are tetraploid.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the ideal soil type for growing cotton successfully?

Explanation: Vertisols (black cotton soils) are ideal due to high moisture retention capacity, montmorillonite clay mineralogy, and neutral to alkaline reaction.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following botanical classification pairs is mismatched?

Solution & Rationale: Cotton belongs to the family Malvaceae, not Leguminosae (Fabaceae).

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary objective of 'curing' in tobacco production?

Solution & Rationale: Curing is a post-harvest biochemical process that removes moisture while altering chemical constituents to create commercial leaf quality.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Under Integrated Pest Management in cotton, what is the significance of installing pheromone traps?

Solution & Rationale: Pheromone traps monitor insect pest incidence (like Helicoverpa armigera and Pectinophora gossypiella) and guide pesticide application thresholds.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following setts selection criteria is vital for sugarcane planting?

Solution & Rationale: 3-budded setts from disease-free seed nurseries ensure high germination percentage and freedom from red rot and smut.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

Why is basal application of Phosphorus recommended in cash crops like cotton and sugarcane?

Solution & Rationale: Phosphorus is immobile in soil and critical for early root system architecture, making basal placement near the root zone essential.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

What differentiates Corchorus capsularis from Corchorus olitorius?

Solution & Rationale: Corchorus capsularis (White jute) can withstand water stagnation, whereas Corchorus olitorius (Tossa jute) requires well-drained upland soils.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following weed management practices in cotton combines mechanical and chemical approaches?

Solution & Rationale: Integrated weed management combines pre-emergence herbicides (Pendimethalin) with subsequent mechanical inter-cultivation for broad-spectrum weed control.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the typical seed rate per hectare for line sowing of Jute?

Solution & Rationale: Line sowing of jute requires 6 to 8 kg of seeds per hectare, whereas broadcasting requires around 10 kg/ha.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which physiological disorder or symptom is associated with water stress during the flowering and boll formation stage in cotton?

Solution & Rationale: Moisture stress during reproductive development in cotton causes severe shedding of squares and young bolls, heavily reducing seed-cotton yield.
