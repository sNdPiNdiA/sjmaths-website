Taxonomic and Agronomic Classification of Field Crops

Field crops are classified based on various parameters including botanical family, life cycle, economic utility, and seasonal adaptation. Understanding these groupings is fundamental for agronomic management and competitive examinations.

  • Botanical Classification: Based on morphological and floral characteristics. Major families include Poaceae (cereals and grasses), Fabaceae (legumes/pulses), Brassicaceae (oilseeds), and Solanaceae (tubers/solanaceous crops).
  • Agronomic/Economic Groups: Grouping based on utility such as cereals, millets, pulses, oilseeds, fibre crops, sugar crops, forage/fodder crops, and green manure crops.
  • Life Cycle Classification: Annuals (complete life cycle in one season, e.g., Triticum aestivum), Biennials (two seasons, e.g., Beta vulgaris), and Perennials (live for several years, e.g., Saccharum officinarum).
  • Climatic Adaptation: Kharif (monsoon/rainfed crops sown in June-July) and Rabi (winter/temperate crops sown in October-November).

Cereals and Millets: Agronomy, Production, and Protection

Cereals belong predominantly to the family Poaceae and form the staple food supply globally. Millets are hardy, drought-tolerant cereal counterparts grown under marginal environments.

  • Major Cereals: Rice (Oryza sativa), Wheat (Triticum aestivum), Maize (Zea mays), Barley (Hordeum vulgare).
  • Millets: Sorghum (Sorghum bicolor), Pearl Millet (Pennisetum glaucum), Finger Millet (Eleusine coracana).
  • Critical Irrigation Stages: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) in wheat (20-25 DAS); Panicle Initiation and Flowering in rice; Silking and Tasselling in maize.
  • Weed Management: Use of Butachlor at 1.5 kg a.i./ha in rice as pre-emergence; Pendimethalin at 1.0 kg a.i./ha in wheat and maize.
  • Pests and Diseases: Stem borer (Chilo partellus) in maize and sorghum; Brown spot (Bipolaris oryzae) and Blast (Magnaporthe oryzae) in rice; Yellow rust (Puccinia striiformis) in wheat.

Pulses and Oilseeds: Biological Nitrogen Fixation and Lipid Accumulation

Pulses and oilseeds are vital protein and energy sources in human diets and play critical roles in sustainable cropping systems through biological nitrogen fixation and crop diversification.

  • Major Pulses (Fabaceae): Chickpea (Cicer arietinum), Pigeonpea (Cajanus cajan), Lentil (Lens culinaris), Blackgram (Vigna mungo).
  • Major Oilseeds: Mustard/Rapeseed (Brassica juncea), Groundnut (Arachis hypogaea), Soybean (Glycine max), Sunflower (Helianthus annuus).
  • Symbiotic Nitrogen Fixation: Rhizobium inoculation (e.g., Rhizobium leguminosarum for pea, Bradyrhizobium for soybean) at 200 g per 10 kg seed.
  • Critical Stages for Irrigation: Pre-flowering and pod formation stages in pulses; flowering and pegging stages in groundnut.
  • Diseases: Wilt (Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. ciceris) in chickpea; Alternaria blight (Alternaria brassicae) in mustard.

Commercial, Fibre, Sugar, and Forage Crop Groups

Commercial crops provide raw materials for agro-based industries, generate export revenue, and supply high-energy animal feeds.

  • Fibre Crops: Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum, Malvaceae), Jute (Corchorus capsularis, Tiliaceae). Cotton requires a frost-free period with 180-200 frost-free days.
  • Sugar Crops: Sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum, Poaceae) propagated through three-budded setts treated with mercuric fungicides or hot water treatment.
  • Forage Crops: Berseem (Trifolium alexandrinum), Lucerne (Medicago sativa), Napier-Bajra hybrid. Provide continuous green fodder during lean winter periods.
  • Nutrient Management: Sugarcane responds well to high nitrogen application (150-250 kg N/ha) split into three doses; balanced application of phosphorus and potassium.

Nutrient Management, Fertilizer Calculations, and Crop Protection IPM

Efficient nutrient stewardship and integrated pest management (IPM) maximize fertilizer use efficiency (FUE) and minimize environmental degradation.

  • Fertilizer Calculations: To apply 120 kg N, 60 kg P2O5, and 40 kg K2O per hectare using Urea (46% N), DAP (18% N, 46% P2O5), and Muriate of Potash (60% K2O).
  • DAP Calculation: 60 kg P2O5 requires (60 / 0.46) = 130.4 kg DAP supplying (130.4 * 0.18) = 23.47 kg N. Remaining N = 120 - 23.47 = 96.53 kg N.
  • Urea Calculation: 96.53 kg N requires (96.53 / 0.46) = 209.8 kg Urea. MOP calculation: 40 / 0.60 = 66.6 kg MOP.
  • IPM Strategies: Deep summer ploughing to expose pupae, use of pheromone traps (5-6 traps/ha), biological control agents like Trichogramma chilonis, and need-based application of safer insecticides/fungicides following Economic Threshold Levels (ETL).

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Botanical name of bread wheat is Triticum aestivum (hexaploid, 2n=6x=42).
  • Crown Root Initiation (CRI) stage in wheat occurs at 20-25 days after sowing.
  • Botany of chickpea (gram) is Cicer arietinum belonging to family Fabaceae.
  • Sugarcane is planted using three-budded setts and is a C4 plant.
  • Soybean (Glycine max) contains approximately 40-42% protein and 20% oil.
  • Ricinus communis (castor) belongs to family Euphorbiaceae.
  • Berseem (Trifolium alexandrinum) is known as the king of Egyptian clover/winter forages.
  • Cotton fibre is composed of nearly 90% pure cellulose.
  • Seed rate of wheat under normal sown conditions is 100 kg/ha.
  • Pre-emergence herbicide Pendimethalin is commonly applied at 1.0 kg a.i./ha in pulses and oilseeds.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the ploidy level of bread wheat (hexaploid, 42 chromosomes) with durum wheat (tetraploid, 28 chromosomes).
  • Assuming legumes require heavy nitrogenous fertilization, whereas they only need a starter dose (20-25 kg N/ha) prior to nodulation.
  • Confusing the C4 photosynthetic pathway of maize and sugarcane with the C3 pathway of rice and wheat.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Comprehensive Study Material on Crop Groups for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Botanical Taxonomy and Plant Families

Agronomic crop groups are systematically arranged based on floral morphology and phylogenetic relationships.

  • Poaceae: Monocotyledonous cereal and grass family with hollow jointed stems and caryopsis fruit type.
  • Fabaceae: Dicotyledonous legume family characterized by papilionaceous corolla, root nodules, and pod/legume fruit.
Concept 2

Physiological Crop Grouping (C3 vs C4 Plants)

Crops are classified by their photosynthetic carbon fixation pathways which dictate water and light use efficiency.

  • C3 Crops: Rice, wheat, soybean, pulses; lower water use efficiency and photorespiration present.
  • C4 Crops: Maize, sugarcane, sorghum, pearl millet; higher photosynthetic rate and no detectable photorespiration.
Concept 3

Nutrient Dynamics in Crop Groups

Nutrient uptake varies significantly across groups, influencing fertilizer schedules and rotations.

  • Cereals: High demand for nitrogen; require split applications.
  • Pulses: Biological nitrogen fixation via Rhizobium mutualism reduces synthetic N dependency.
Concept 4

Integrated Crop Protection Principles

Combining cultural, biological, and chemical methods ensures sustainable crop health.

  • ETL (Economic Threshold Level): Pest population density at which control measures must be initiated to prevent economic damage.
  • IPM: Multi-tactic approach emphasizing biological control and resistant varieties.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Caryopsis (Dry indehiscent single-seeded fruit)
The fruit type characteristic of cereal crops in family Poaceae where the pericarp is fused with the seed coat.
Legume / Pod (Dehiscent dry fruit)
Fruit type of Fabaceae crops that splits along two sutures to release seeds.
CRI Stage (Crown Root Initiation)
The most critical irrigation stage in wheat occurring 20-25 days after sowing.
Biological N Fixation (Symbiotic N2 reduction)
Conversion of atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia by Rhizobium bacteria in legume root nodules.
Photoperiodism (Day-length response)
Physiological reaction of organisms to the length of night or day (Short-day, Long-day, Day-neutral plants).
Ratoon Crop (Regrowth from stubble)
A crop harvested by allowing the stubble to sprout new stems, common in sugarcane and sorghum.
Seed Rate (Mass of seed per unit area)
Quantity of seeds required to sow one hectare of land to achieve optimum plant population.
Ploidy Level (Number of chromosome sets)
The number of complete sets of chromosomes in a cell (e.g., hexaploid wheat has 6x=42).
Pre-emergence Herbicide (Soil-applied weedicide)
Herbicides applied to the soil after sowing but before crop and weed emergence (e.g., Pendimethalin).
ETL (Economic Threshold Level)
Pest density at which control intervention prevents economic injury level.
C4 Pathway (Hatch-Slack pathway)
Photosynthetic pathway characterized by superior water use efficiency in tropical grasses.

  • Bread wheat: Triticum aestivum (2n=42).
  • Soybean protein content: 40-42%.
  • Sugarcane planting material: Three-budded setts.
  • Cotton family: Malvaceae.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Field crops are categorized taxonomically by family and agronomically by economic utility.
  • Cereals (Poaceae) provide energy, while pulses (Fabaceae) supply dietary protein and enrich soil via N fixation.
  • Oilseeds store high lipid reserves and require balanced sulfur and phosphorus nutrition.
  • Commercial and fibre crops demand specific agro-climatic conditions and intensive management.
  • Precise fertilizer calculations and adherence to critical irrigation stages are vital for maximizing yields.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Short Day Plants (SDP) VS Long Day Plants (LDP)

SDP flower when day length is shorter than a critical photoperiod (e.g., Rice, Soybean), whereas LDP flower when day length exceeds a critical limit (e.g., Wheat, Barley).

C3 Photosynthesis VS C4 Photosynthesis

C3 plants fix carbon into 3-carbon molecules with higher photorespiration, while C4 plants utilize a 4-carbon acid intermediate with minimal photorespiration.

What is the standard planting material used for commercial propagation of sugarcane?

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate the quantity of Urea required to supply 120 kg of Nitrogen, assuming DAP supplies part of the nitrogen requirement (60 kg P2O5 total requirement using DAP with 18% N and 46% P2O5).

Solution & Rationale: 60 kg P2O5 requires 130.4 kg DAP. DAP supplies (130.4 * 0.18) = 23.47 kg N. Remaining N = 120 - 23.47 = 96.53 kg N. Urea required = 96.53 / 0.46 = 209.8 kg.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following crop groups exhibits hypogeal seed germination?

Solution & Rationale: In hypogeal germination (seen in gram, pea, maize), the epicotyl elongates while the cotyledons remain below the soil surface.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the optimum temperature range for grain filling in wheat?

Solution & Rationale: Optimum temperature for grain filling and test weight accumulation in wheat is 20-25 °C. High temperatures (>30 °C) cause forced maturity and shriveled grains.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Which botanical family is characterized by tetradynamous stamens and siliqua fruit type?

Solution & Rationale: Brassicaceae (mustard family) is distinguished by 4 petals, 6 stamens (4 long, 2 short - tetradynamous), and siliqua fruits.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the typical seed rate for bold-seeded chickpea varieties?

Solution & Rationale: Bold-seeded kabuli chickpea varieties require a higher seed rate of 100-120 kg/ha compared to desi varieties (75-80 kg/ha).

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Which weed is considered a noxious grassy weed specifically associated with wheat fields?

Solution & Rationale: Phalaris minor (littlereseed canary grass) is a major grassy weed mimicking wheat morphologically.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary objective of 'Earthing-up' in sugarcane cultivation?

Solution & Rationale: Earthing-up provides mechanical support to sugarcane stools, preventing lodging during high winds and heavy vegetative growth.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following is a quantitative short-day plant?

Solution & Rationale: Soybean flowers earlier under short day conditions, classifying it as a quantitative short-day plant.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the typical oil percentage found in mustard seeds (Brassica juncea)?

Solution & Rationale: Indian mustard (Brassica juncea) seeds contain approximately 38-42% oil.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which causal organism is responsible for loose smut of wheat?

Solution & Rationale: Loose smut of wheat is caused by Ustilago tritici, an internally seed-borne fungal pathogen.
