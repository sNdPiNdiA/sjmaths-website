Objectives of Tillage and Primary Agronomic Operations

Master the fundamental objectives of tillage, seedbed preparation, soil physical manipulation, and weed management tailored for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture competitive examinations.

1. Definition and Fundamental Objectives of Tillage

Tillage is the mechanical manipulation of soil to provide a desirable condition for seed germination, root growth, and weed control. Coined by Jethro Tull, who mistakenly believed that fine soil particles were consumed by plant roots (tillage is manure), modern agronomy views tillage through the lens of soil physics and crop ecology.

  • Seedbed Preparation: Creating a fine, firm, and level seedbed to ensure intimate seed-to-soil contact for optimum imbibition and germination.
  • Moisture Conservation: Breaking capillary pores in the soil surface to reduce evaporative water loss and enhancing infiltration capacity during precipitation.
  • Weed Eradication: Destroying obnoxious weed flora, perennial weeds, and their propagules (rhizomes, tubers) by exposing them to solar desiccation.
  • Stubble Incorporation: Incorporating crop residues, green manures, and organic fertilizers into the active root zone to enhance microbial activity and humification.

2. Classification of Tillage Operations

Tillage operations are classified based on the timing, depth, and intensity of soil manipulation during the cropping cycle.

  • Primary Tillage: Deeper soil manipulation (15–30 cm depth) performed immediately after harvesting the preceding crop. Implements used include moldboard plow, disc plow, chisel plow, and subsoiler.
  • Secondary Tillage: Lighter and shallower operations (5–10 cm depth) following primary tillage to pulverize clods, level the soil, and prepare the final seedbed. Implements include harrows, cultivators, rotavators, planks, and rollers.
  • Special Tillage Operations: Subsoiling (breaking hardpans at 45–60 cm depth), contour tillage (preventing soil erosion on slopes), and blind tillage (weeding crop rows before emergence).

3. Modern Tillage Concepts: Conservation and Zero Tillage

Traditional intensive tillage often leads to soil degradation, loss of organic carbon, and structural breakdown. Modern agronomy emphasizes conservation tillage systems.

  • Zero Tillage (No-Till): Direct seeding into unplowed soil where weeds are controlled using non-selective systemic herbicides such as glyphosate or paraquat. It saves energy and preserves soil structure.
  • Minimum Tillage: Reducing tillage operations to the bare minimum necessary for a good seedbed, saving fuel, labor, and time while minimizing soil erosion.
  • Stubble Mulch Tillage: Leaving crop residues on the soil surface and performing tillage with sweep or blade implements that do not invert the soil.

4. Effects of Tillage on Soil Properties and Crop Growth

Tillage profoundly alters the physical, chemical, and biological properties of the soil ecosystem.

  • Bulk Density and Porosity: Initial tillage decreases bulk density and increases total porosity. However, excessive tillage destroys soil aggregates, leading to surface crusting and subsoil compaction (plow pan).
  • Soil Aeration: Enhances gaseous exchange between the root zone and the atmosphere, facilitating aerobic respiration of roots and beneficial nitrifying bacteria.
  • Soil Temperature: Darkened, loosened soils absorb solar radiation more rapidly, influencing germination rates and early seedling vigor.
  • Microbial Population: Temporary flush of microbial activity occurs due to aeration and release of easily oxidizable organic substrates.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Jethro Tull is recognized as the father of weed science and introduced the concept of tillage in his 1731 treatise 'Horse-hoeing Husbandry'.
  • Subsoiling is a specialized primary tillage operation designed to break impermeable subsoil hardpans at depths exceeding 45 cm.
  • Blind tillage is commonly practiced in sugarcane and potato crops to destroy early emerging weed seedlings without damaging the main crop shoots.
  • Bulk density of normal mineral soils typically ranges from 1.30 to 1.40 g/cm³, whereas compacted soils exceed 1.60 g/cm³.
  • Paraquat is a contact, non-selective herbicide widely used in zero-tillage systems for burndown of existing weed flora.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing primary tillage with secondary tillage implements; remember that moldboard and disc plows are primary, while cultivators and harrows are secondary.
  • Assuming zero tillage eliminates all weed problems; actually, it often increases reliance on chemical herbicides like glyphosate.
  • Believing that more tillage always increases crop yield; excessive tillage destroys soil aggregates, increases moisture loss, and accelerates organic matter oxidation.

Concept 1

Definition and Scope of Tillage

Tillage is the foundational agronomic practice involving mechanical manipulation of soil to establish an optimum environment for seed germination, root proliferation, and weed suppression.

  • Jethro Tull: Father of tillage who hypothesized that fine soil particles serve as plant nutrition.
  • Agronomic Goal: Modification of soil impedance, aeration, and water-holding capacity.
Concept 2

Primary versus Secondary Tillage

Classification based on depth and sequence of field operations.

  • Primary Tillage: Deep cutting and soil inversion using moldboard or disc plows.
  • Secondary Tillage: Shallow refinement, clod crushing, and leveling using harrows and cultivators.
Concept 3

Conservation Agriculture and Zero Tillage

Modern paradigms aimed at mitigating soil degradation, erosion, and high energy costs.

  • Zero Tillage: Sowing crops directly into unplowed land with chemical weed control.
  • Residue Retention: Maintaining surface soil cover to enhance infiltration and reduce evaporation.
Concept 4

Soil Physical Modifications through Tillage

Evaluating changes in soil structure, density, and porosity.

  • Bulk Density: Decreases temporarily post-tillage but rises with subsequent compaction.
  • Infiltration Rate: Enhanced initially by creating macro-porosity and surface roughness.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Tillage (Mechanical Soil Manipulation)
The practice of modifying soil physical condition through mechanical implements for crop production.
Primary Tillage (Deep Soil Inversion Tillage)
Initial deep plowing (15–30 cm) performed to shatter soil and bury residues.
Secondary Tillage (Shallow Seedbed Preparation)
Shallow operations (5–10 cm) following primary tillage to pulverize clods and level land.
Subsoiling (Hardpan Shattering Tillage)
Deep tillage breaking subsurface compacted layers without soil inversion.
Zero Tillage (No-Till System)
Establishing crops without primary or secondary tillage, relying on herbicides for weed control.
Blind Tillage (Post-Planting Pre-Emergence Tillage)
Tillage operation performed after sowing but before crop emergence to destroy weed seedlings.
Bulk Density (Dry Mass per Unit Bulk Volume)
The ratio of mass of dry soil to its total bulk volume, expressed in g/cm³.
Plow Pan (Subsurface Compacted Layer)
A dense soil layer formed directly below the depth of normal plowing due to implement pressure.
Minimum Tillage (Reduced Tillage Intensity)
System minimizing tillage operations to only those absolutely necessary for crop establishment.
Stubble Mulch (Residue Surface Management)
Leaving crop residues on the soil surface during tillage to protect against erosion.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Father of Tillage: Jethro Tull (1731).
  • Subsoiling breaks hardpans at depths > 45 cm.
  • Paraquat and glyphosate are essential non-selective herbicides in zero tillage.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Tillage objectives include seedbed preparation, weed control, moisture conservation, and residue incorporation.
  • Primary tillage involves deep soil inversion, whereas secondary tillage focuses on seedbed refinement.
  • Conservation tillage reduces soil erosion, preserves organic matter, and saves fossil fuel energy.
  • Excessive tillage leads to structural breakdown, humus oxidation, and plow pan formation.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Primary Tillage VS Secondary Tillage

Primary tillage involves deep soil opening and inversion (15-30 cm), while secondary tillage involves shallow seedbed preparation (5-10 cm).

Zero Tillage VS Minimum Tillage

Zero tillage eliminates all mechanical seedbed preparation, whereas minimum tillage reduces the number of tillage passes.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Who is acknowledged as the father of tillage and weed science?

Explanation: Jethro Tull introduced systematic horse-drawn hoeing and tillage concepts, earning him the title of father of tillage.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a primary tillage implement?

Explanation: Moldboard (MB) plow is a classic primary tillage implement designed for deep soil inversion.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical depth range for primary tillage operations?

Explanation: Primary tillage is typically performed at a depth of 15 to 30 cm to break compacted soil and bury residues.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which specialized tillage operation is performed to break subsoil hardpans?

Explanation: Subsoiling is executed using subsoilers to shatter impenetrable subsoil hardpans or plow pans.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is 'blind tillage' primarily used for?

Explanation: Blind tillage is carried out after sowing but before crop emergence to kill newly emerged weed flushes, commonly in sugarcane and potato.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical is most commonly used for weed knockdown in zero-tillage systems?

Explanation: Glyphosate is a broad-spectrum, non-selective systemic herbicide widely utilized in zero-tillage systems for burndown.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the normal bulk density range of a fertile mineral soil?

Explanation: Normal mineral soils have a bulk density between 1.30 and 1.40 g/cm³, whereas values above 1.60 g/cm³ indicate compaction.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following implements is used for secondary tillage?

Explanation: Disc harrows are secondary tillage implements used to break clods and refine the seedbed after primary plowing.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of secondary tillage?

Explanation: Secondary tillage focuses on smoothing the soil surface, pulverizing clods, and creating a firm seedbed.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Excessive and intensive tillage over many years typically leads to:

Explanation: Intensive tillage accelerates microbial oxidation of soil organic matter, destroys aggregates, and causes crusting.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In conservation agriculture, what percentage of soil surface should ideally be covered with crop residues?

Explanation: Conservation agriculture requires a minimum of 30% soil surface coverage with crop residues to protect against erosion.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which property of soil is temporarily improved immediately after primary tillage?

Explanation: Primary tillage loosens the soil matrix, thereby decreasing bulk density and significantly increasing total porosity.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is NOT a primary objective of tillage?

Explanation: Increasing chemical fertilizer runoff is detrimental; tillage aims to enhance infiltration and nutrient retention, not runoff.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does minimum tillage aim to achieve?

Explanation: Minimum tillage reduces tillage passes to the bare minimum required, saving fuel, labor, and machinery wear.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What causes the formation of a 'plow pan'?

Explanation: Plow pans develop due to the shearing action and weight of tillage implements operating at a constant depth year after year.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which implement is best suited for puddling in wetland rice cultivation?

Explanation: Paddy puddlers and cage wheels are specialized implements used to churn saturated soil, destroying percolation channels for rice.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Stubble mulch tillage is primarily designed to:

Explanation: Stubble mulch tillage utilizes sweep implements that undercut weeds without inverting the soil, leaving residues on top.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following terms describes the weight of dry soil per unit volume including pore space?

Explanation: Bulk density is defined as the mass of dry soil divided by its total bulk volume (solids plus pores).

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical particle density of common mineral soils?

Explanation: Particle density of most mineral soils is relatively constant at approximately 2.65 g/cm³.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary reason for performing puddling in lowland rice cultivation?

Solution & Rationale: Puddling destroys soil aggregation, creates a compacted layer that reduces percolation losses, and provides a soft mud for easy transplanting.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

Why is excessive secondary tillage discouraged in dryland agriculture?

Solution & Rationale: Excessive harrowing in dryland farming exposes moist soil to the atmosphere, accelerating moisture loss and breaking down fragile aggregates.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary agronomic advantage of conservation tillage over conventional tillage?

Solution & Rationale: Conservation tillage minimizes soil disturbance and retains crop residues, which substantially reduces erosion and builds organic carbon.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

A soil sample has a dry mass of 130 g and a total bulk volume of 100 cm³. What is its bulk density?

Solution & Rationale: Bulk density = Mass of dry soil / Total volume = 130 g / 100 cm³ = 1.30 g/cm³.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following implements is specifically designed to cut and lift soil without inversion, leaving crop residues on the surface?

Solution & Rationale: Sweep and blade implements operate horizontally beneath the surface, cutting weed roots without inverting the soil, ideal for stubble mulch farming.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

In the context of soil physical properties, what does an increase in total soil porosity signify?

Solution & Rationale: Porosity represents the percentage of soil volume occupied by pore space; higher porosity means more pore volume relative to solids.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following practices is considered a special tillage operation?

Solution & Rationale: Contour tillage, subsoiling, and blind tillage are categorized as special tillage operations performed for specific soil and water management needs.
