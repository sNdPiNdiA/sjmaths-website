Introduction and Classification of Tillage Methods

Tillage is the manipulation of soil physical conditions to create a favorable seedbed for seed germination, root proliferation, and weed management. Developed by Jethro Tull, who advocated for intensive soil pulverization, modern agronomy now classifies tillage into primary tillage, secondary tillage, and specialized conservation methods.

  • Primary Tillage: Initial, deeper soil manipulation (depth 15–30 cm) performed immediately after harvesting the preceding crop. Implements include moldboard plows, disc plows, and subsoilers designed to invert, shatter, and aerate the soil profile.
  • Secondary Tillage: Lighter, shallower operations (depth 5–10 cm) aimed at clod pulverization, leveling, seedbed refinement, and incorporation of basal fertilizers and herbicides. Implements include harrows, cultivators, rotavators, and plankers.
  • Zero Tillage and Conservation Tillage: Practices that minimize soil disturbance and retain crop residues on the soil surface to conserve moisture, prevent wind and water erosion, and enhance soil organic carbon. Paraquat or glyphosate is frequently used as a knock-down herbicide prior to direct drilling.

Sowing and Planting Methods for Major Field Crops

The choice of planting method dictates plant population, spatial distribution, light interception, and root competition. Accurate seed placement depth and seed-to-soil contact are vital for uniform emergence.

  • Broadcasting: Manual or mechanical scattering of seeds uniformly over the soil surface, followed by harrowing or planking. Used for small-seeded crops like sesame (Sesamum indicum) and finger millet (Eleusine coracana), though it results in uneven depth and higher seed rates.
  • Line Sowing (Drilling): Depositing seeds in continuous furrows at a controlled depth using seed drills or bullock-drawn/tractor-drawn multi-crop planters. This ensures optimal plant geometry, facilitates intercultivation, and reduces weed pressure.
  • Dibbling: Manual placement of seeds at pre-determined spacing in holes made with a dibbler or peg. Widely adopted for hybrid crops, vegetables, and high-value species like maize (Zea mays) and cotton (Gossypium hirsutum) to minimize seed wastage.
  • Transplanting: Raising seedlings in specialized nurseries followed by uprooting and resetting them in the main field. Essential for paddy rice (Oryza sativa), tobacco (Nicotiana tabacum), and various solanaceous vegetables.

Seed Rate, Seed Treatment, and Sowing Geometry Calculations

Agronomic calculations regarding seed rates, plant populations, and fertilizer dosages are fundamental for UP TGT and PGT examinations. Proper seed treatment safeguards young seedlings against soil-borne and seed-borne phytopathogens.

  • Plant Population Formula: Target Plant Population per Hectare = 10^4Row-to-Row Spacing (m) × Plant-to-Plant Spacing (m). Adjustments must be made based on expected field emergence percentage.
  • Seed Rate Calculation: Seed Rate (kg/ha) = Target Plant Population × Test Weight (g)10^3 × Germination Percentage × Purity Percentage.
  • Fungicidal and Biological Seed Treatment: Treatment with Carbendazim or Thiram at 2.5 g/kg of seed controls smut and blight pathogens. Inoculation with Rhizobium or Azotobacter cultures at 20–25 g/kg of seed enhances biological nitrogen fixation in legumes and cereals.

Specialized Planting Systems and Cropping Geometries

Maximizing solar radiation interception and nutrient uptake requires specialized planting configurations, especially in intercropping and wider-row perennial crops.

  • Paired Row Planting: Planting crops in pairs of rows with a reduced inter-row distance and a wider gap between pairs. This accommodates intercrops such as mustard (Brassica juncea) or chickpea (Cicer arietinum) within wide-spaced autumn-planted sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum).
  • Bed Planting System: Raising beds and forming furrows simultaneously. Irrigation water is applied exclusively through furrows, preventing waterlogging, improving aeration, and saving up to 30–40% irrigation water in wheat (Triticum aestivum) and maize cultivation.
  • System of Rice Intensification (SRI): Involves transplanting young 8-to-12-day-old single seedlings at wide spacing (25 cm × 25 cm) under intermittent wetting and drying (IWD) irrigation, significantly reducing seed rates and water footprints while boosting tillering.

Weed Management and Intercultural Operations in Crop Establishment

Mechanical and chemical weed control methods integrated with appropriate tillage and sowing techniques form the cornerstone of effective weed management.

  • Critical Period of Crop-Weed Competition: Generally the first one-third to one-fourth of the crop life cycle (e.g., 30–45 days after sowing in wheat and soybean, 15–30 days in transplanted rice).
  • Mechanical Intercultivation: Using hand hoes, wheel hoes, or power weeders to disrupt weed root systems, earth up crop bases, and break soil crusts to enhance soil aeration.
  • Herbicide Application Methods: Pre-plant incorporation (PPI) like trifluralin, pre-emergence application (PE) like pendimethalin or atrazine within 2–3 days of sowing, and post-emergence application (POE) like 2,4-D or sulfosulfuron after crop and weed emergence.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Jethro Tull is acclaimed as the father of tillage and authored 'Horse Hoeing Husbandry' in 1731.
  • The optimum sowing depth for most cereal crops like wheat (Triticum aestivum) and barley (Hordeum vulgare) is 4 to 5 cm.
  • Zero tillage technology in India was pioneered and popularized for the rice-wheat cropping system of the Indo-Gangetic Plains.
  • Pendimethalin is a widely used selective pre-emergence herbicide applied within 3 days of sowing.
  • System of Rice Intensification (SRI) originates from Madagascar and uses single young seedlings (8-12 days old) with wide spacing (25 cm × 25 cm).
  • Paraquat is a non-selective, contact, quaternary ammonium herbicide used as a knock-down agent in zero-tillage systems.
  • Seed viability is tested using the Tetrazolium test (using 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride), which stains living tissues red.
  • The critical period of crop-weed competition in transplanted rice is generally 30 to 45 days after transplanting.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing pre-plant incorporation (PPI) with pre-emergence (PE) herbicide application; PPI is incorporated into the soil before sowing, whereas PE is sprayed on the soil surface after sowing but before crop emergence.
  • Assuming zero tillage eliminates the need for weed control; zero tillage actually requires non-selective knock-down herbicides followed by selective pre-emergence chemicals because weed seeds are not buried by plowing.
  • Calculating plant populations using cm instead of meters in spatial formula denominators, leading to magnitude errors of 10,000.

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Mechanics of Primary and Secondary Tillage

Tillage alters bulk density, porosity, and hydraulic conductivity. Primary tillage breaks cohesive soil forces, while secondary tillage prepares the seedbed matrix.

  • Moldboard Plow: Inverts soil slice completely, burying weeds and crop residues efficiently.
  • Rotavator: Powered secondary tillage implement driven by tractor PTO shaft that pulverizes soil in a single pass.
Concept 2

Seed Sowing Methods and Spatial Geometry

Spatial geometry dictates resource capture (light, water, nutrients). Line sowing and dibbling ensure optimum leaf area index (LAI) development.

  • Drilling: Continuous seed flow in furrows at uniform depth.
  • Dibbling: Spot placement ensuring exact plant-to-plant and row-to-row spacing with zero seed wastage.
Concept 3

Conservation Agriculture and Zero Tillage Principles

Conservation agriculture rests on three pillars: minimum soil disturbance, permanent organic soil cover, and diversified crop rotations.

  • Happy Seeder: Tractor-mounted machine that sows wheat directly into heavy paddy stubble loads without prior burning.
  • Knock-down Herbicide: Rapidly desynchronizes green weed tissue contact without leaving active soil residues harmful to sprouting seedlings.
Concept 4

Seed Quality Parameters and Germination Testing

High-quality certified seed must meet rigorous physical purity, germination percentage, and sanitary health standards.

  • Test Weight: Weight of 1000 seeds expressed in grams, vital for seed rate calculations.
  • Seed Index: Weight of 100 seeds expressed in grams.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Primary Tillage (Deep Soil Manipulation (15-30 cm))
Initial mechanical soil cutting and inversion performed immediately after harvest to break hardpans and bury residues.
Secondary Tillage (Shallow Seedbed Preparation (5-10 cm))
Lighter tillage operations focused on clod pulverization, surface leveling, and weed eradication.
Zero Tillage (No-Till Crop Establishment)
Planting crops directly into unplowed land where previous crop residue is retained and weeds are controlled chemically.
Dibbling (Spot Sowing Method)
Manual or mechanical placement of single seeds at exact intervals in pre-marked grid points.
Test Weight (1000-Seed Weight (g))
Standard gravimetric expression of one thousand representative seeds used for seed rate computation.
Happy Seeder (In-situ Residue Management Seeder)
Specialized tractor implement that cuts standing rice stubble, mulches it, and drills wheat simultaneously.
Pre-emergence Herbicide (PE Herbicidal Application)
Chemical weedkiller applied to the soil surface after crop sowing but prior to the emergence of crop and weed shoots.
Critical Period (Critical Crop-Weed Competition Window)
The specific growth window during which weed presence causes maximum irreversible yield loss.
Tetrazolium Test (TTC Viability Assay)
Biochemical test utilizing 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride to evaluate seed embryo respiration and viability.
Rotavator (Power Rotary Tillage Implement)
PTO-driven tractor implement equipped with L-shaped blades that intensely churn and pulverize soil.
Planking (Surface Smoothing and Compacting)
Secondary operation using a heavy wooden beam (patela) to crush clods, level seedbeds, and improve seed-soil contact.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Tillage operations are broadly divided into primary tillage (deep soil inversion) and secondary tillage (seedbed refinement).
  • Sowing methods include broadcasting, line sowing (drilling), dibbling, and transplanting.
  • Zero tillage conserves soil moisture and structure while curbing production costs in cereal rotations.
  • Precise seed rate calculation depends on plant population formulas, test weight, purity, and germination percentage.
  • Integrated weed management combines mechanical intercultivation with timely pre-plant, pre-emergence, and post-emergence herbicides.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Test Weight VS Seed Index

Test weight is the weight of 1000 seeds in grams, whereas seed index is the weight of 100 seeds in grams.

Broadcasting VS Drilling

Broadcasting scatters seeds randomly with uneven depth and high seed rates; drilling places seeds in furrows at uniform depth with controlled seed rates.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Who is universally recognized as the Father of Tillage and author of 'Horse Hoeing Husbandry'?

Explanation: Jethro Tull published 'Horse Hoeing Husbandry' in 1731 and is celebrated as the father of tillage and mechanical seed drilling.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which implement is classified as a primary tillage tool designed for complete soil inversion?

Explanation: The moldboard (MB) plow is the premier primary tillage implement for soil inversion and burial of stubborn weeds and crop residues.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard recommended sowing depth for common wheat (Triticum aestivum)?

Explanation: The optimal sowing depth for wheat is 4 to 5 cm to ensure adequate moisture access and successful coleoptile emergence.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical herbicide is predominantly used as a non-selective knock-down agent in zero-tillage systems?

Explanation: Paraquat is a fast-acting, non-selective contact herbicide widely used in zero-tillage systems to kill existing weeds prior to direct seeding.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the weight of 1000 seeds expressed in grams termed as in agronomy?

Explanation: The weight of 1000 seeds in grams is known as the test weight, whereas the weight of 100 seeds is the seed index.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The System of Rice Intensification (SRI), which advocates single young seedling transplanting, originated in which country?

Explanation: SRI was developed in Madagascar in the early 1980s and focuses on wide spacing, young seedlings, and intermittent irrigation.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which sowing method consumes the highest seed rate per hectare due to uneven depth and bird damage?

Explanation: Broadcasting requires a higher seed rate (often 120-150% of recommended) because seeds are exposed to birds and germinate unevenly.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Pendimethalin is categorized under which class of herbicide application timing?

Explanation: Pendimethalin is a selective pre-emergence herbicide applied within 2-3 days of crop sowing to inhibit emerging weed seedlings.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of secondary tillage operations?

Explanation: Secondary tillage refines the seedbed by breaking clods, leveling the soil surface, and ensuring good seed-to-soil contact.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Calculate the theoretical plant population per hectare for a crop planted at 20 cm × 10 cm spacing.

Explanation: Population = 10,000 / (0.2 × 0.1) = 10,000 / 0.02 = 500,000 plants per hectare.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which biochemical test is standard for determining seed embryo viability?

Explanation: The Tetrazolium (TTC) test measures dehydrogenase enzyme activity in living cells, staining viable embryos red.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does conservation agriculture specifically avoid to protect soil structural integrity?

Explanation: Conservation agriculture minimizes soil disturbance by eliminating intensive plowing, preserving soil aggregates and organic carbon.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which implement is specifically engineered to drill wheat directly into loose rice residue without burning?

Explanation: The Turbo Happy Seeder cuts standing or loose paddy straw, mulches it over the seed line, and drills wheat simultaneously.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the critical period of crop-weed competition in transplanted rice?

Explanation: The critical period of weed competition in transplanted rice is generally 30 to 45 days after transplanting.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which planting geometry allows intercropping of mustard or chickpea within wide-spaced autumn sugarcane?

Explanation: Paired row planting in sugarcane leaves wide inter-row spaces, facilitating successful intercropping with short-duration rabi crops.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main purpose of planking after harrowing during seedbed preparation?

Explanation: Planking smooths the surface, crushes remaining clods, and packs soil lightly around seeds to enhance capillary moisture movement.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which tillage system involves minimum soil disturbance with seeds placed in narrow slots?

Explanation: Zero tillage restricts soil disturbance to narrow planting slits opened by special disk or punch planters.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary advantage of dibbling over broadcasting?

Explanation: Dibbling provides exact plant spacing, uniform crop growth, and saves substantial quantities of expensive hybrid seeds.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which herbicide is classified as a post-emergence systemic weedkiller used in cereal crops?

Explanation: 2,4-D (2,4-Dichlorophenoxyacetic acid) is a systemic post-emergence herbicide targeting broad-leaved weeds in cereal crops.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does the term 'test weight' of cereal grains signify in seed technology?

Explanation: Test weight in Indian agronomy specifically denotes the weight of 1000 seeds expressed in grams, essential for seed rate calculations.

Calculate the exact seed rate (kg/ha) for wheat given: Target plant population = 4,000,000 plants/ha, Test weight = 40 g, Germination = 85%, and Purity = 95%.

Solution & Rationale: Seed Rate = (Target Population × Test Weight in g) / (10^3 × Germination % × Purity %) = (4,000,000 × 40) / (1000 × 0.85 × 0.95) = 160,000,000 / 807.5 = 197.3 kg/ha.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary agronomic advantage of adopting bed planting over flat-bed sowing in wheat cultivation?

Solution & Rationale: Bed planting applies irrigation water only in furrows, saving water, improving aeration, and preventing lodging.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Which type of herbicide is Trifluoromethyl-dinitroaniline compound Fluchloralin when applied before crop sowing and mixed into soil?

Solution & Rationale: Fluchloralin is a classic pre-plant incorporation (PPI) herbicide mixed into the soil prior to sowing crops like soybean and groundnut.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

In the System of Rice Intensification (SRI), what age of seedlings is recommended for transplanting to maximize tillering capacity?

Solution & Rationale: SRI mandates very young seedlings (8-12 days old, 2-leaf stage) before they experience transplanting shock.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Which tillage practice involves cutting narrow tilled slots for seed placement without disturbing the inter-row soil area?

Solution & Rationale: Zero tillage (no-till) restricts soil disturbance solely to narrow furrows opened by disk or tine openers.

What is the recommended seed treatment dosage of Carbendazim 50% WP per kg of seed to control seed-borne fungal diseases?

Solution & Rationale: Carbendazim is standardly applied at 2.0 to 2.5 grams per kg of seed for effective prophylactic disease control.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

Why is deep plowing (>30 cm depth) performed occasionally in semi-arid soils?

Solution & Rationale: Deep plowing using chisel plows or subsoilers breaks impenetrable subsoil hardpans, facilitating deep root growth and rainwater percolation.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the defining characteristic of a selective herbicide?

Solution & Rationale: Selective herbicides target specific physiological pathways in weeds without causing phytotoxicity to the main crop.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

Solution & Rationale: Market price dictates economic returns but has no agronomic bearing on physical seed rate calculations.
