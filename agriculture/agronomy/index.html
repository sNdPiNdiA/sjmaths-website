1. Introduction, Scope, and Principles of Agronomy

Agronomy is a specialized branch of agricultural sciences that focuses on the principles and practices of crop production and soil management. Derived from the Greek words 'agros' (meaning field) and 'nomos' (meaning to manage), agronomy bridges fundamental sciences and practical farming to maximize crop yield and economic returns while conserving natural resources.

  • Definition: Agronomy is the science and art of producing and dealing with field crops by producing food, feed, fiber, fuel, and bioproducts.
  • Scope: Encompasses crop ecology, agro-meteorology, tillage systems, weed science, irrigation agronomy, soil fertility, and sustainable farming systems.
  • Role of an Agronomist: Integrating soil-water-plant-atmosphere continuum dynamics to enhance Water Use Efficiency (WUE) and Nutrient Use Efficiency (NUE).

2. Botanical Taxonomy, Origin, and Cytogenetics of Major Field Crops

A sound understanding of botanical classification, geographical origin, and ploidy level is fundamental for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture aspirants. Field crops belong predominantly to the families Poaceae (Gramineae), Fabaceae (Leguminosae), Brassicaceae, and Malvaceae.

  • Triticum aestivum (Bread Wheat): Family Poaceae, Origin South-West Asia, Hexaploid (2n = 6x = 42).
  • Oryza sativa (Rice): Family Poaceae, Origin Indo-Burma region, Diploid (2n = 2x = 24).
  • Zea mays (Maize): Family Poaceae, Origin Central America (Mexico), Diploid (2n = 2x = 20).
  • Cicer arietinum (Chickpea): Family Fabaceae, Origin South-Western Asia (Turkey), Diploid (2n = 2x = 16).

3. Soil, Climate, Tillage, and Crop Establishment

Crop establishment is dictated by edaphic and climatic factors. Tillage operations modify soil physical properties, ensuring optimal aeration, moisture retention, and seedling emergence.

  • Tillage Systems: Conventional tillage, minimum tillage, zero tillage (direct drilling), and conservation agriculture based on permanent organic soil cover and crop diversification.
  • Seed Rate & Spacing: Precise seed rate calculation depends on test weight (1000-grain weight), germination percentage, and desired plant population density.
  • Seed Treatment: Rhizobium inoculation for legumes, Azotobacter/Azospirillum for cereals, and seed dressing with fungicides like Carbendazim @ 2.5 g/kg seed to prevent seed-borne pathogens.

4. Nutrient Management and Fertilizer Calculations

Balanced nutrition is paramount for optimal crop growth. Essential plant nutrients are categorized into macro-nutrients (Primary: N, P, K; Secondary: Ca, Mg, S) and micro-nutrients (Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B, Mo, Cl, Ni).

  • Fertilizer Calculation Formula: Quantity of fertilizer = ( ext{Recommended Nutrient Dose} imes 100) / (\% ext{ Nutrient Content in Fertilizer}).
  • Urea Calculation: To apply 120 kg N/ha, amount of urea needed = (120 imes 100) / 46 = 260.8 kg/ha (since urea contains 46% N).
  • DAP Calculation: To apply 60 kg ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5/ha, amount of DAP needed = (60 imes 100) / 46 = 130.4 kg/ha (DAP contains 18% N and 46% ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5).

5. Irrigation Agronomy and Critical Stages of Water Requirement

Water management entails scheduling irrigation based on critical physiological growth stages where moisture stress causes irreversible yield penalties.

  • Wheat: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) stage at 20-25 days after sowing (DAS) is the most critical stage. Other stages include tillering, jointing, flowering, milk, and dough stages.
  • Rice: Panicle initiation, flowering, and milking stages are extremely sensitive to moisture stress. Continuous submergence of 5 cm is maintained except during weeding and tillering.
  • Maize: Tasseling and silking stages are extremely sensitive to moisture stress, which causes tassel-silking synchrony failure and barren cobs.

6. Weed Science, Crop Protection, and Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

Weeds compete with crops for light, water, nutrients, and space, often causing heavy economic losses. Chemical weed control combined with cultural practices forms the bedrock of modern weed management.

  • Major Weeds: Phalaris minor (Little seed canary grass) in wheat; Echinochloa crus-galli (Barnyard grass) in rice; Cyperus rotundus (Purple nutsedge) as a perennial weed.
  • Herbicides: Selective pre-emergence herbicides like Pendimethalin; post-emergence herbicides like Sulfosulfuron in wheat and Bispyribac-sodium in rice.
  • Plant Protection: Management of insect pests like Helicoverpa armigera in chickpea and diseases like Magnaporthe oryzae (Rice blast) using Tricyclazole.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Crown Root Initiation (CRI) stage in wheat occurs at 20-25 Days After Sowing (DAS) and is the single most critical irrigation stage.
  • Phalaris minor is a major weed in wheat fields, known as 'Mimicry weed' or 'Little seed canary grass', controlled efficiently by Sulfosulfuron.
  • Urea contains 46% Nitrogen, whereas Diammonium Phosphate (DAP) contains 18% Nitrogen and 46% Phosphorus ( ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5).
  • Rice is a typical C3 aquatic/semi-aquatic plant, whereas maize is a highly efficient C4 tropical crop.
  • The most critical weed-free period for most kharif crops is the first 30 to 45 days after sowing.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the chromosome number of hexaploid wheat (2n=42) with tetraploid durum wheat (2n=28).
  • Assuming herbicides like 2,4-D can be safely applied to broad-leaved crops (legumes); 2,4-D is strictly a selective herbicide for narrow-leaved crops (cereals).
  • Confusing CRI stage timing with tillering stage in wheat; CRI occurs much earlier at 20-25 DAS.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Comprehensive Agronomy and Field Crops Masterclass

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Fundamentals of Crop Ecology and Agronomic Principles

Agronomy relies heavily on manipulating the crop environment to maximize solar radiation interception and nutrient uptake efficiency.

  • Agro-meteorology: Study of weather and climate parameters influencing crop growth and development.
  • PAR: Photosynthetically Active Radiation (400-700 nm) utilized by green plants for photosynthesis.
Concept 2

Tillage and Seedbed Preparation Dynamics

Tillage creates an ideal physical condition for seed germination and root proliferation.

  • Zero Tillage: Direct sowing of seeds without primary or secondary tillage, conserving soil moisture and organic carbon.
  • Bulky Organic Manures: Farmyard Manure (FYM) and compost supplying essential humus and macro-nutrients.
Concept 3

Integrated Nutrient Management (INM)

Combining chemical fertilizers, organic manures, biofertilizers, and green manuring to sustain soil fertility.

  • Biofertilizers: Microbial inoculants like Rhizobium, Azotobacter, and Azospirillum fixing atmospheric nitrogen.
  • Green Manuring: Incorporation of green succulent plant tissues (e.g., Sesbania aculeata) into the soil to add nitrogen and organic matter.
Concept 4

Integrated Weed Management (IWM)

Integration of cultural, mechanical, biological, and chemical methods to keep weed population below the Economic Threshold Level (ETL).

  • Selective Herbicide: Chemical that kills specific weed flora without harming the main cultivated crop (e.g., Atrazine in maize).
  • Critical Period of Crop-Weed Competition: The timeframe during which crops must be kept weed-free to prevent yield loss (usually 1/3rd to 1/4th of crop life span).

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Agronomy (Agros + Nomos)
The branch of agricultural science dealing with field crop production and soil management.
CRI Stage (Crown Root Initiation)
The most critical irrigation stage in wheat occurring 20-25 days after sowing.
Mimicry Weed (Phalaris minor)
A noxious weed morphologically resembling wheat plants during vegetative stages.
C4 Plant (Hatch-Slack Pathway)
Plants possessing high photosynthetic efficiency with zero photorespiration (e.g., Maize, Sugarcane).
Biofertilizer (Microbial Inoculant)
Living microorganisms that enhance nutrient availability in soil through nitrogen fixation or phosphorus solubilization.
Green Manuring (In-situ Incorporation)
Growing and ploughing under of succulent green crops to enrich soil fertility and organic matter.
Water Use Efficiency (WUE)
Ratio of crop yield or dry matter produced to the total amount of water consumed by evapotranspiration.
Test Weight (Weight of 1000 Seeds)
The weight in grams of 1000 seeds, used for seed rate calculations and grading.
Etl (Economic Threshold Level)
Pest population density at which control measures must be initiated to prevent economic injury level.
Ploidy Level (Chromosome Set Count)
Number of complete sets of chromosomes in a biological cell (e.g., Hexaploid wheat 6x=42).
Pendimethalin (Dinitroaniline Herbicide)
A selective pre-emergence herbicide widely used in various field crops to control annual grasses and broad-leaved weeds.
Ratooning (Regrowth Management)
Practice of harvesting a crop and allowing the stubbles to sprout for a second harvest (e.g., Sugarcane).

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Pre-emergence Herbicide VS Post-emergence Herbicide

Pre-emergence herbicides are applied after sowing but before crop/weed emergence; post-emergence herbicides are applied after both crop and weeds have emerged.

Macro-nutrients VS Micro-nutrients

Macro-nutrients (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S) are required in large quantities by plants, whereas micro-nutrients (Fe, Zn, Cu, etc.) are required in trace quantities.

Which among the following is recognized as the most critical irrigation stage in wheat?

What is the actual percentage of Nitrogen present in commercial Urea fertilizer?

Which weed is known as the classic 'mimicry weed' in wheat fields?

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosome number (2n) of cultivated rice (Oryza sativa)?

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which photosynthetic pathway is exhibited by Maize (Zea mays)?

How much quantity of DAP is required to supply 46 kg of ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5?

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which biofertilizer is specifically recommended for legume crops like Chickpea?

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the origin center of cultivated Maize (Zea mays)?

Explanation: Maize originated in Central America (Mexico) with its wild relative being Teosinte.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which herbicide is widely used as a selective pre-emergence weedicide in various field crops?

Explanation: Pendimethalin is a standard selective pre-emergence herbicide used to control annual grasses and broad-leaved weeds.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the geographical origin of cultivated rice (Oryza sativa)?

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does ETL stand for in pest management?

Explanation: ETL stands for Economic Threshold Level, the pest density at which control measures should be applied to prevent economic injury.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which nutrient deficiency causes 'Khaira disease' in rice?

Explanation: Khaira disease in rice is caused by Zinc deficiency, characterized by chlorotic patches turning brown on older leaves.

What is the chemical formula and nitrogen content of DAP?

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the term used for green manuring crop commonly grown and incorporated in-situ in rice fields?

Explanation: Sesbania aculeata (Daincha) is widely cultivated and incorporated as green manure in paddy fields.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a non-selective, post-emergence contact herbicide?

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following crop rotations exhibits highest land use efficiency and soil fertility restoration?

Solution & Rationale: Legume integration like Mungbean in Maize-Wheat-Mungbean rotation enhances nitrogen status and maximizes cropping intensity.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary objective of applying a pre-emergence herbicide?

Solution & Rationale: Pre-emergence herbicides act in the soil zone to kill weed seeds and emerging seedlings during early crop establishment.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

In transgenic Bt cotton, what is the mode of action against insect pests?

Solution & Rationale: Bt genes encode crystal (Cry) proteins that dissolve in the alkaline midgut of lepidopteran pests, causing paralysis and death.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which climatic factor is primarily responsible for inducing photoperiodism in short-day plants?

Solution & Rationale: Photoperiodism in short-day plants is regulated by the duration of continuous darkness (night length).

Test Question 6 of 10 PGR Standard

What is the critical period of weed competition in direct-seeded rice?

Solution & Rationale: The first 30 to 45 days represent the peak critical period where weed competition causes maximum yield reduction in rice.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following represents the correct formula for cropping intensity?

Solution & Rationale: Cropping intensity is calculated as the ratio of Gross Cropped Area to Net Area Sown expressed as a percentage.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary causal organism of Rice Blast disease?

Solution & Rationale: Rice blast, a devastating fungal disease, is caused by Magnaporthe oryzae (formerly Pyricularia oryzae).

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which secondary tillage implement is best suited for breaking soil clods and leveling the seedbed?

Solution & Rationale: Harrows are secondary tillage tools specifically designed for pulverizing clods, leveling, and finishing the seedbed.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the physiological role of Potassium (K) in higher plants?

Solution & Rationale: Potassium plays a vital role in osmoregulation, controlling stomatal opening/closing, and activating numerous key enzymes.
