Introduction to Agronomy and Scope of Field Crops

Agronomy is a specialized branch of agricultural sciences that deals with the principles and practices of field crop production and soil management. Derived from the Greek words 'agros' (field) and 'nomos' (to manage), agronomy integrates knowledge from various basic sciences to optimize crop yield and resource use efficiency.

  • Definition: The science and art of producing plants for food, fuel, fiber, and land reclamation while maintaining environmental quality.
  • Scope: Encompasses tillage operations, seed technology, nutrient management, water management, weed control, and cropping systems.
  • Role of Agronomist: To bridge the gap between basic laboratory research and practical farmer implementation to achieve maximum economic yield (MEY).

Tillage and Tilth Dynamics in Crop Production

Tillage refers to the mechanical manipulation of soil to provide a favorable physical environment for seed germination, root growth, and weed suppression. The physical condition of the soil resulting from tillage is known as tilth.

  • Primary Tillage: Deep soil manipulation involving ploughing, subsoiling, or chiseling (e.g., using moldboard plough or disc plough) to break hardpans and invert soil.
  • Secondary Tillage: Lighter operations such as harrowing, disking, cultivating, and planking aimed at pulverizing clods and leveling the seedbed.
  • Conservation Tillage: Practices like minimum tillage and zero tillage that leave at least 30% crop residue on the soil surface to conserve moisture and prevent soil erosion.

Cropping Systems and Intensification Indices

Cropping system refers to the pattern of crops taken up over a given period from a given area of land, including management practices and interactions with the environment.

  • Monoculture: Growing the same crop on the same land year after year.
  • Crop Rotation: Growing different crops in a planned sequence on the same land to maintain soil fertility and break pest cycles.
  • Multiple Cropping: Growing two or more crops on the same field in a single year (includes sequential cropping and intercropping).
  • Cropping Intensity Index (CII): Calculated as (Total cropped area / Total net sown area) × 100, reflecting the intensity of land use.

Principles of Weed Science and Integrated Weed Management (IWM)

Weeds are unwanted, uninvited, and harmful plants that compete with cultivated crops for light, water, nutrients, and space. Weed management is critical to prevent significant economic yield losses.

  • Critical Period of Crop-Weed Competition: Usually the first one-third to one-fourth of the crop growth cycle (e.g., 30 to 45 days after sowing in cereals).
  • Herbicides: Chemical agents used to kill or inhibit weed growth. Classified based on application time as pre-plant, pre-emergence (e.g., Atrazine, Pendimethalin), and post-emergence (e.g., 2,4-D, Glyphosate).
  • Integrated Weed Management (IWM): Combining cultural, mechanical, biological, and chemical methods for sustainable and economical weed control.

Nutrient Management and Fertilizer Calculations

Nutrient management involves supplying essential plant nutrients in adequate quantities and balanced proportions to maximize crop productivity without degrading soil health.

  • Essential Elements: 17 essential plant nutrients required for growth, including macronutrients (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S) and micronutrients (Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B, Mo, Cl, Ni).
  • Fertilizer Calculation: Determining the exact quantity of commercial fertilizers (like Urea, DAP, MOP) required to supply recommended nutrient doses based on nutrient percentages (e.g., Urea contains 46% N; DAP contains 18% N and 46% P₂O₅).
  • Integrated Nutrient Management (INM): Harmonious use of chemical fertilizers, organic manures, biofertilizers (such as Rhizobium, Azotobacter, PSB), and green manuring.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Agronomy is derived from Greek words 'Agros' (field) and 'Nomos' (to manage).
  • The critical period of crop-weed competition in most cereal crops is the first 30 to 45 days after sowing.
  • Urea contains 46% nitrogen, while Di-ammonium Phosphate (DAP) contains 18% nitrogen and 46% phosphorus pentoxide (P₂O₅).
  • Zero tillage technology eliminates primary and secondary tillage completely, placing seeds directly into unploughed soil slots.
  • Cropping intensity for a single crop grown once a year on a field is 100%.
  • Pendimethalin is a widely used pre-emergence selective herbicide in wheat, paddy, and soybean.
  • Integrated Pest Management (IPM) combines cultural, mechanical, biological, and chemical methods to keep pest populations below the Economic Threshold Level (ETL).
  • Petro-crops and bio-diesel crops like Jatropha curcas represent modern diversification in agronomy.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing pre-emergence herbicides with post-emergence herbicides; remember pre-emergence is applied after sowing but before crop/weed emergence, while post-emergence is applied after crop and weed emergence.
  • Mistaking cropping intensity formula as (Net sown area / Total cropped area) × 100 instead of the correct (Total cropped area / Net sown area) × 100.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Comprehensive Agronomy Concepts and Field Crop Production Principles

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Scope and Definition of Agronomy

Agronomy forms the core of agricultural production sciences, integrating crop science, soil science, and agricultural meteorology.

  • Agronomy: Scientific management of land and field crops to maximize productivity and profitability.
  • MEY: Maximum Economic Yield attained through optimized agronomic inputs.
Concept 2

Tillage and Seedbed Preparation

Tillage alters soil physical properties to favor seed germination and crop establishment.

  • Tilth: Physical condition of soil resulting from tillage operations.
  • Zero Tillage: Ultimate conservation tillage practice utilizing herbicides for weed control prior to direct drilling.
Concept 3

Cropping Systems and Indices

Maximizing land productivity through intensive multi-crop and rotation strategies.

  • Cropping Intensity: Metric indicating how many times a field is cropped in a agricultural year.
  • Intercropping: Growing companion crops in definite row proportions to enhance land equivalent ratio (LER).
Concept 4

Weed Management Principles

Effective weed management relies on preventing weed seed entry, cultural practices, and judicious herbicide application.

  • Critical Period: Duration during which weed competition causes irreversible yield reduction.
  • Selective Herbicide: Chemical that kills weeds without injuring the main crop.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Agronomy (Agronomy (Agros + Nomos))
The branch of agriculture dealing with field crop production and soil management.
Tilth (Soil Tilth Condition)
The physical condition of soil relative to its ease of tillage and suitability for seed germination.
Zero Tillage (No-Till System)
A crop production system where seeds are planted directly into undisturbed soil previously treated with herbicides.
Cropping Intensity (CII Formula)
The percentage ratio of total cropped area to net sown area in a agricultural year.
Intercropping (Multiple Row Cropping)
Growing two or more crops simultaneously in distinct strip or row arrangements on the same land.
Herbicide (Chemical Weed Killer)
A chemical substance used to destroy or suppress undesirable vegetation or weeds.
Pre-emergence Herbicide (Pre-emergence Application)
Herbicide applied after crop planting but before crop or weed seedlings emerge above the soil surface.
Critical Period (Critical Weed-Free Period)
The specific time frame in the crop lifecycle when weed competition causes maximum yield loss.
INM (Integrated Nutrient Management)
The maintenance of soil fertility and plant nutrient supply at an optimum level for sustaining desired crop productivity through organic and inorganic sources.
MEY (Maximum Economic Yield)
The highest yield level that maximizes net economic returns per unit of land.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Urea contains 46% N; DAP contains 18% N and 46% P₂O₅.
  • Critical period of weed competition in cereals is generally 30-45 DAS.
  • Cropping intensity formula: (Total Cropped Area / Net Sown Area) × 100.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Agronomy integrates plant, soil, and environmental sciences for sustainable crop production.
  • Tillage creates favorable seedbeds; modern trends favor conservation and zero tillage.
  • Cropping intensity and multi-cropping increase agricultural productivity per unit area per unit time.
  • Weeds cause severe yield losses; timely weeding and pre-emergence herbicides are vital.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Mixed Cropping VS Intercropping

Mixed cropping involves sowing mixed seeds without row arrangement mainly for subsistence, whereas intercropping involves distinct row arrangements to maximize synergy and total yield.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate the quantity of Urea required to supply 120 kg of Nitrogen per hectare, given that Urea contains 46% N.

Solution & Rationale: Urea required = (Recommended N / % N in fertilizer) × 100 = (120 / 46) × 100 = 260.87 kg.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the cropping intensity for a farm with a net sown area of 200 hectares and a total cropped area of 300 hectares?

Solution & Rationale: Cropping Intensity = (Total Cropped Area / Net Sown Area) × 100 = (300 / 200) × 100 = 150%.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

Which agronomic practice involves sowing seeds directly into untilled soil where weeds have been knocked down by non-selective herbicides?

Solution & Rationale: Zero tillage involves direct drilling into unploughed soil utilizing herbicides for initial weed management.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following herbicides is specifically classified as a post-emergence herbicide for broadleaf weed control in wheat?

Solution & Rationale: 2,4-D is a selective systemic post-emergence herbicide used against broadleaf weeds in cereal crops.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

What does the Economic Threshold Level (ETL) signify in crop protection?

Solution & Rationale: ETL is the pest density at which management action must be taken to prevent pest population from reaching the Economic Injury Level (EIL).

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

In a fertilizer recommendation of N:P:K at 120:60:40 kg/ha, how much DAP is required if DAP provides 18% N and 46% P₂O₅?

Solution & Rationale: P₂O₅ required is 60 kg. DAP contains 46% P₂O₅. DAP required = (60 / 46) × 100 = 130.43 kg.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following terms describes growing two or more crops simultaneously in alternate rows to increase land use efficiency?

Solution & Rationale: Intercropping involves growing multiple crops in distinct row arrangements.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary reason for practicing crop rotation in sustainable agronomy?

Solution & Rationale: Crop rotation restores soil fertility, reduces fertilizer costs, and interrupts disease and weed life cycles.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which element is a micronutrient essential for plant growth?

Solution & Rationale: Zinc (Zn) is an essential micronutrient, whereas K, S, and Ca are macronutrients.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following is an example of a legume green manure crop used to enrich soil nitrogen?

Solution & Rationale: Sesbania aculeata (Daincha) is widely used as a green manure crop in Indian agriculture.
