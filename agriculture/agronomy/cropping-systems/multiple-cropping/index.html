Introduction and Fundamental Concepts of Multiple Cropping

Multiple cropping is defined as the practice of growing more than one crop on the same piece of land during one agricultural calendar year. It represents an advanced intensification strategy designed to maximize land, solar radiation, water, and nutrient utilization efficiency.

  • Temporal Intensification: Involves growing crops sequentially (sequential cropping), where the succeeding crop is planted only after the preceding crop has been harvested.
  • Spatial Intensification: Involves growing two or more crops simultaneously during all or part of their life cycle (intercropping or mixed cropping).
  • Cropping Intensity Index (CII): Calculated as the total gross cropped area divided by the net sown area, multiplied by 100. High multiple cropping index signifies superior agro-ecosystem productivity.

Classification and Types of Multiple Cropping Systems

Agronomically, multiple cropping systems are categorized based on spatial and temporal overlaps into distinct functional groups:

  • Sequential Cropping: Growing crops in quick succession within a year. Examples include Oryza sativa (Rice) followed by Triticum aestivum (Wheat) followed by Vigna radiata (Green gram).
  • Intercropping: Growing two or more crops simultaneously in distinct row arrangements. Sub-types include row intercropping, strip intercropping, relay intercropping, and mixed intercropping.
  • Relay Cropping: A special form of sequential intercropping where the succeeding crop is planted before the harvesting of the preceding crop (e.g., planting lentil or soybean into standing rice crop).
  • Ratooning: Raising a crop from the regrowths coming out of the roots or stalks after harvest of the main crop, widely practiced in Saccharum officinarum (Sugarcane) and Sorghum bicolor (Sorghum).

Indices of Competition and Efficiency in Multiple Cropping

Quantifying the biological and economic advantage of multiple cropping over sole cropping requires specialized mathematical and agronomic indices:

  • Land Equivalent Ratio (LER): Measures the relative land area under sole crops required to produce the yields achieved in intercropping. LER > 1.0 indicates yield advantage. Formula: ext{LER} = rac{Y_{ab}}{Y_{aa}} + rac{Y_{ba}}{Y_{bb}} where Y_{ab} is yield of crop A in association with B.
  • Competitive Ratio (CR): Indicates the degree of competition between intercropped species and calculates which crop is more competitive.
  • Aggressivity (A): Determines how much the relative increase in yield of component crop 'A' is greater than that of component crop 'B'.
  • Monetary Advantage Index (MAI): Evaluates the economic profit of the cropping system by incorporating economic value and LER.

Nutrient, Water, and Weed Management in Intensified Systems

Multiple cropping increases nutrient and water demands, requiring precise agronomic interventions:

  • Nutrient Management: Legume-cereal systems reduce nitrogen fertilizer requirements due to biological nitrogen fixation (BNF) by Rhizobium species. Fertilizer recommendations must account for residual nutrient effects.
  • Water Management: Synchronizing critical irrigation stages (e.g., CRI stage in wheat, flowering in legumes) prevents moisture stress in intercropped setups. Micro-irrigation systems are highly advantageous.
  • Weed Suppression: Dense canopy closure in multiple and intercropping systems smothers weed emergence, significantly reducing weed dry matter and minimizing herbicide dependency.
  • Allele-Chemical Interactions: Certain crops release allelochemicals that inhibit weed growth or affect companion crops, necessitating careful species selection.

Socio-Economic Importance and Constraints in UP Agriculture

Adopting multiple cropping systems in the Indo-Gangetic plains of Uttar Pradesh offers profound socio-economic benefits alongside notable challenges:

  • Employment Generation: Continuous cropping operations increase annual labor utilization and reduce seasonal unemployment.
  • Risk Mitigation: Diversified cropping buffers against total crop failure caused by aberrant weather or pest outbreaks.
  • Machinery Constraints: Timely turnaround between sequential crops (e.g., rice-wheat rotation) requires robust mechanization, such as zero-till seed drills and happy seeders.
  • Soil Health Maintenance: Continuous intensive tillage without organic recycling can cause structural degradation, demanding integrated nutrient management (INM) and residue retention.

  • Relay cropping involves planting the succeeding crop before harvesting the preceding crop, such as sowing lentil in standing paddy fields.
  • Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) greater than 1.0 indicates a biological advantage of intercropping over sole cropping.
  • The Rice-Wheat cropping system is the predominant sequential cropping system of the Indo-Gangetic Plains in Uttar Pradesh.
  • Ratooning is commercially practiced in Sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum) and Napier grass (Pennisetum purpureum).
  • Biological Nitrogen Fixation (BNF) in legume-cereal intercropping systems significantly reduces inorganic nitrogen fertilizer requirements.
  • Competitive Ratio (CR) measures the ratio of individual LERs of two crops and reflects their competitive ability.
  • Allelopathy refers to the chemical inhibition of one plant species by another through the release of secondary metabolites into the environment.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing mixed cropping with intercropping: Mixed cropping involves sowing seeds without distinct row arrangements, whereas intercropping involves definite row proportions.
  • Assuming LER measures economic profit directly: LER measures biological efficiency; Monetary Advantage Index (MAI) or Net Returns must be used for economic evaluation.
  • Treating relay cropping as sequential cropping: Relay cropping has temporal overlap between crops, whereas sequential cropping has zero overlap.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Multiple Cropping Systems and Intensification in Agronomy

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Principles of Cropping Systems Intensification

Intensification in agronomy refers to increasing the productivity per unit of land and time through judicious management of environmental factors.

  • Temporal Intensity: Maximizing days of the year when photosynthetically active radiation is captured by green canopies.
  • Spatial Intensity: Utilizing inter-row spaces and root zone stratification efficiently.
Concept 2

Sequential Cropping Dynamics

Sequential cropping relies heavily on rapid turnaround time and moisture availability between crop cycles.

  • Turnaround Time: The interval between harvesting one crop and establishing the next; minimized using zero-tillage technology.
  • Residual Fertility: Factoring nutrient carryover from heavily fertilized preceding crops to subsequent crops.
Concept 3

Intercropping Geometry and Competition

Spatial arrangement dictates the degree of complementarity versus competition.

  • Additive Series: Intercrop is added to the base crop population without altering base crop density.
  • Replacement Series: Base crop and intercrop populations are reduced and proportioned to share standard area.
Concept 4

Biological and Economic Efficiency Indices

Quantification of intercropping success involves standardized biometric formulas.

  • LER: Standardized relative land requirement index where ext{LER} > 1 proves intercropping superiority.
  • MAI: Translates biological advantage into monetary terms using market prices.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Mixed Cropping VS Intercropping

Mixed cropping has no row arrangement and seeds are mixed; intercropping features definite row geometries.

Sequential Cropping VS Relay Cropping

Sequential cropping has zero crop overlap; relay cropping involves planting the second crop before harvesting the first.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does a Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) value of 1.25 signify?

Explanation: An LER of 1.25 means that growing sole crops would require 25% more land to produce the same combined yield achieved by the intercropping system.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following represents a classic example of relay cropping?

Explanation: Sowing lentil into standing paddy fields before rice harvest exemplifies relay cropping, as the two crops overlap for a brief period.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The Cropping Intensity Index (CII) is calculated using which formula?

Explanation: Cropping Intensity Index is calculated as (Gross Cropped Area / Net Sown Area) × 100, reflecting the number of times crops are grown on the same land per year.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In an additive intercropping series:

Explanation: In an additive intercropping series, the base crop is maintained at its full recommended plant population, and the intercrop is added into the inter-row spaces.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which agricultural practice involves growing a crop from the regrowths of harvested plant roots or stems?

Explanation: Ratooning is the agricultural practice of cultivating a crop from the stubble or root regrowths after the harvest of the plant crop.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of introducing legumes into cereal-based intercropping systems?

Explanation: Legumes harbor symbiotic Rhizobium bacteria in root nodules that fix atmospheric nitrogen, supplementing soil fertility for companion cereals.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which term describes the detrimental biochemical effect of one plant species on another?

Explanation: Allelopathy is the biological phenomenon where an organism produces biochemicals that influence the growth, survival, and reproduction of other organisms.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

When the Competitive Ratio (CR) of crop A is greater than crop B in an intercropping system, it indicates that:

Explanation: A higher CR value for crop A indicates that crop A is more competitive and dominates resource acquisition compared to crop B.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does 'Sequential Cropping' strictly imply regarding crop overlap?

Explanation: Sequential cropping involves growing crops in sequence with zero temporal overlap; sowing of the next crop occurs only after harvesting the preceding crop.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which cropping system index measures the economic profitability of intercropping relative to sole cropping?

Explanation: Monetary Advantage Index (MAI) evaluates intercropping systems by combining biological efficiency (LER) with market economics and valuation.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which state in India is widely known for intensive Rice-Wheat sequential cropping systems?

Explanation: Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, and Haryana form the core Indo-Gangetic region where the Rice-Wheat sequential cropping system dominates.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In replacement series intercropping:

Explanation: In replacement series intercropping, component crops substitute each other in specific proportions (e.g., 2:1 or 1:1 row ratios) within a shared land area.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is a major agronomic challenge associated with intensive sequential cropping like rice-wheat?

Explanation: Intensive sequential cropping creates a tight turnaround window, requiring rapid stubble management and mechanization (e.g., zero-till drills) to sow the next crop on time.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does Aggressivity (A) equal to zero signify in an intercropping system?

Explanation: An aggressivity value of zero indicates that both crops are equally competitive, with neither crop demonstrating dominance over the other.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop is most suitable for relay cropping in northern India during the Rabi season transition?

Explanation: Lentil (Lens culinaris) or Lathyrus is commonly broadcast into standing paddy fields prior to harvest to utilize residual soil moisture.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary advantage of multiple cropping over sole cropping regarding environmental risk?

Explanation: Crop diversification in multiple cropping buffers against complete economic loss if aberrant weather or specific pest attacks damage one component crop.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does 'Multi-Storey Cropping' refer to in agronomy?

Explanation: Multi-storey cropping involves growing plant species with different vertical canopy structures and light requirements simultaneously (e.g., coconut, pepper, and pineapple).

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which index evaluates the actual yield of an intercrop relative to its expected yield based on sown proportion?

Explanation: Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) evaluates intercrop yields relative to sole crop performance across proportional land equivalents.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why is zero-tillage strongly recommended in sequential rice-wheat cropping systems?

Explanation: Zero-tillage allows immediate wheat sowing directly into rice harvested fields without intensive primary tillage, saving time, energy, and moisture.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the definition of Mixed Cropping?

Explanation: Mixed cropping involves sowing seeds of two or more crops mixed together without any distinct row pattern, traditionally practiced as an insurance against crop failure.

Test Question 1 of 10 TGT Standard

Calculate the Cropping Intensity Index if a farmer cultivates a Net Sown Area of 5 hectares and achieves a Gross Cropped Area of 10 hectares.

Solution & Rationale: CII = ( ext{Gross Cropped Area} / ext{Net Sown Area}) imes 100 = (10 / 5) imes 100 = 200\%.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

In an intercropping experiment, if Crop A sole yield is 2000 kg/ha and intercrop yield is 1500 kg/ha (with sole crop B yield 1000 kg/ha and intercrop yield 500 kg/ha), what is the LER?

Solution & Rationale: LER = (1500/2000) + (500/1000) = 0.75 + 0.50 = 1.25.

Which of the following cropping systems exemplifies spatial intensification without temporal separation?

In replacement intercropping series, if wheat and mustard are sown in a 3:1 ratio, what does this ratio represent?

Solution & Rationale: The 3:1 ratio denotes three rows of the base crop (wheat) for every one row of the intercrop (mustard).

Solution & Rationale: Monetary Advantage Index (MAI) integrates LER and market value to determine economic profitability.

What is the primary difference between mixed cropping and intercropping?

Solution & Rationale: Mixed cropping involves random sowing of mixed seeds, whereas intercropping utilizes distinct row geometries.
