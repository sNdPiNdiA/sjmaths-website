Intercropping Systems in Agronomy: Principles, Indices, and Management

Introduction and Fundamental Concepts of Intercropping

Intercropping is defined as the agricultural practice of cultivating two or more crop species simultaneously in the same field during a growing season. Unlike mixed cropping, where seeds are randomly mixed and sown without distinct row arrangements, intercropping involves a definite spatial arrangement or geometry. The primary objective is to maximize crop productivity per unit area and time through the efficient utilization of natural resources such as solar radiation, water, and soil nutrients.

  • Base Crop: The main crop planted at its optimal sole-crop population, usually having a longer duration and higher competitive ability.
  • Intercrop: The component crop planted alongside the base crop, typically of shorter duration, lower stature, and complementary growth habit (e.g., legumes paired with cereals).
  • Principal Benefits: Enhanced biological yield, risk insurance against weather aberrations, better weed suppression, and improved soil fertility through biological nitrogen fixation (BNF).

Classification and Spatial-Temporal Arrangements

Based on the spatial arrangement and temporal overlap of component crops, intercropping systems are classified into distinct agronomic configurations:

  • Row Intercropping: Growing two or more crops simultaneously where at least one crop is planted in rows (e.g., maize alternating with soybean).
  • Strip Intercropping: Growing two or more crops in wide strips wide enough to permit independent machine cultivation while preventing inter-crop competition at the strip borders.
  • Relay Intercropping: Planting a second crop into an existing standing crop before harvesting the first crop, minimizing temporal gaps (e.g., sowing lentil or pea into standing late-season rice).
  • Ratoon Intercropping: Utilizing the regrowth of the harvested base crop along with an intercrop planted in the inter-row spaces.

Quantitative Indices for Evaluating Intercropping Efficiency

Agronomists employ mathematical indices to measure the biological and economic superiority of intercropping over sole cropping systems:

  • Land Equivalent Ratio (LER): The relative area of sole crops required to produce the yields achieved in intercropping. Calculated as LER = (Y_{ab}/Y_{aa}) + (Y_{ba}/Y_{bb}), where Y_{aa} and Y_{bb} are sole crop yields, and Y_{ab} and Y_{ba} are intercrop yields. An LER greater than 1.0 indicates yield advantage.
  • Competitive Ratio (CR): Measures the degree of competition between component crops and indicates which crop is more competitive.
  • Aggressivity (A): Denotes how much the relative yield increase of component 'A' is greater than that of component 'B'.
  • Monetary Advantage Index (MAI): Evaluates economic profitability by multiplying the LER value by the total value of combined crop yields.

Nutrient and Water Management in Intercropping

Nutrient management requires tailored fertilization strategies to balance the demands of both component crops without inducing severe inter-specific competition.

  • Nitrogen Dynamics: Legume-cereal intercrops derive significant nitrogen benefits via rhizobial fixation. However, base cereal crops (e.g., Zea mays) require targeted top-dressing, while the legume component (e.g., Glycine max or Cajanus cajan) benefits from basal phosphorus application (e.g., single superphosphate at 40-60 kg P_2O_5 ha^{-1}).
  • Water Use Efficiency (WUE): Intercropping canopy stratification reduces direct soil evaporation and increases transpiration efficiency. Irrigation scheduling must cater to the critical moisture stages of the more sensitive base crop.

Crop Protection, Weed Suppression, and Agronomic Constraints

Diverse canopies create physical and microclimatic barriers that alter pest behavior and weed seed germination.

  • Weed Management: Rapid canopy closure in spatial intercropping suppresses weed emergence during early vegetative stages, reducing the critical period of crop-weed competition. Pre-emergence selective herbicides must be chosen carefully to ensure safety for both crops.
  • Pest and Disease Dynamics: Non-host plants act as physical barriers, reducing the spread of airborne spores and insect vectors. For instance, growing Cicer arietinum with Brassica juncea reduces gram pod borer (Helicoverpa armigera) incidence.
  • Agronomic Constraints: Mechanized harvesting becomes difficult, allelopathic interactions may suppress minor crop growth, and competition for light or moisture can occur if geometry is mismanaged.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) greater than 1.0 indicates biological advantage and higher land-use efficiency in intercropping.
  • The classic companion cropping combination is Maize (Zea mays) + Soybean (Glycine max) in a 1:2 or 2:2 row proportion.
  • Relay cropping is exemplified by sowing lentil (Lens culinaris) into standing rice (Oryza sativa) fields prior to harvest.
  • Aggressivity value of zero indicates equal competitive ability between two intercropped species.
  • Biological nitrogen fixation in legume-cereal intercropping reduces synthetic nitrogen fertilizer requirements by 25-30% for the cereal.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing mixed cropping with intercropping; remember that intercropping strictly requires a definite row or spatial geometric arrangement.
  • Assuming LER measures economic profit directly; MAI (Monetary Advantage Index) must be calculated alongside LER for economic evaluation.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Intercropping Systems in Agronomy: Principles, Indices, and Management

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Spatial Geometry and Plant Population

Intercropping requires precise spatial adjustments. The base crop is usually maintained at 100% of its recommended sole crop population, while the intercrop population is adjusted between 50% to 100% depending on competitive indices.

  • Additive Series: Intercrop is added to the full population of the base crop.
  • Replacement Series: Base and intercrop populations share the total area by replacing sole crop proportions.
Concept 2

Biological Nitrogen Fixation (BNF) Synergy

Legume-cereal combinations optimize nitrogen dynamics through rhizobial symbiosis. Rhizobium bacteria fix atmospheric nitrogen, part of which becomes available to the associated cereal crop through root exudates and nodule sloughing.

  • Transfer Rate: Up to 15-20% of fixed nitrogen is directly transferred to the companion cereal during co-cultivation.
Concept 3

Quantitative Evaluation Indices

Agronomic efficiency cannot be judged by yield alone. Indices such as LER, CR, and A provide mathematical rigor to determine competitive dominance and land-use advantage.

  • LER Calculation: LER = (Y_{ab}/Y_{aa}) + (Y_{ba}/Y_{bb})
Concept 4

Microclimate Modification and Pest Reduction

Multi-tier canopies alter light interception, relative humidity, and air temperature, creating unfavorable conditions for specific insect pests and reducing wind erosion.

  • Barrier Effect: Physical disruption of host-seeking flight paths in monophagous insect pests.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Intercropping (Multi-species simultaneous cultivation)
Growing two or more crop species simultaneously in alternate rows or strips on the same land during a single growing season.
Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) (Relative land productivity index)
The relative land area required under sole cropping to produce the yields achieved in an intercropping system.
Relay Cropping (Overlapping sequential cropping)
Planting a succeeding crop into a standing mature crop before the primary crop is harvested.
Additive Series (Population addition method)
An intercropping design where the base crop population is maintained at 100% and the intercrop is added in between rows.
Replacement Series (Population substitution method)
An intercropping design where base and intercrop plants share the available land by replacing sole crop proportions.
Aggressivity (Competitive dominance index)
An index indicating how much the relative yield increase of one crop is greater than that of the companion crop.
Competitive Ratio (CR) (Competitive ability ratio)
A measure of the exact degree of competition between two crop species in an intercropping system.
Monetary Advantage Index (MAI) (Economic profitability index)
An index that converts agronomic LER advantage into monetary value to assess net economic returns.
Complementarity (Resource use efficiency)
The differential utilization of environmental resources (light, water, nutrients) by component crops in time and space.
Companion Crop (Beneficial secondary crop)
An intercrop planted to assist the establishment, protection, or yield of the primary base crop.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • LER is dimensionless and independent of market prices.
  • Maize + Soybean is the standard model for additive row intercropping.
  • Aggressivity value greater than zero indicates the dominant species.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Intercropping involves definite row or strip geometry to enhance resource use efficiency.
  • LER > 1.0 demonstrates biological yield superiority over sole cropping.
  • Legume-cereal systems optimize nitrogen nutrition through biological nitrogen fixation.
  • Relay cropping eliminates turnaround time between sequential crops.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Intercropping VS Mixed Cropping

Intercropping uses definite row geometry, whereas mixed cropping involves random mixing of seeds without row arrangement.

Relay Cropping VS Double Cropping

Relay cropping overlaps the growth cycle of two crops before harvest, whereas double cropping is strictly sequential.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does a Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) value of 1.25 signify in an intercropping system?

Explanation: An LER of 1.25 means that 25% more land would be required under sole cropping to produce the same combined yield obtained from one hectare of intercropping.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which intercropping system involves planting a second crop into a standing mature crop before harvesting the first?

Explanation: Relay cropping is a temporal arrangement where the succeeding crop is sown into the standing primary crop prior to its harvest (e.g., lentil sown in standing rice).

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In an additive intercropping series:

Explanation: In an additive series, the base crop population is kept at its recommended sole-crop density, and the intercrop is added into the inter-row spaces.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary agronomic benefit of pairing a legume with a cereal in intercropping?

Explanation: Legume-cereal intercropping improves nitrogen economy through biological nitrogen fixation and utilizes different strata of soil and light efficiently.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which mathematical index evaluates the exact economic profitability of an intercropping system?

Explanation: Monetary Advantage Index (MAI) calculates economic profitability by factoring in both biological LER and market price valuations of component crops.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

If the Aggressivity value of crop A is positive (+0.4) in an intercropping system with crop B (-0.4), what does this indicate?

Explanation: A positive aggressivity value indicates that crop A is the dominant competitor, while the negative value for crop B shows it is the dominated species.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following crop combinations is a classic example of cereal-legume intercropping in India?

Explanation: Maize (Zea mays) and Soybean (Glycine max) represent an ideal cereal-legume intercropping system with complementary canopy structures.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does LER = 1.0 indicate?

Explanation: An LER of exactly 1.0 indicates that intercropping has the exact same productivity as sole cropping, showing neither advantage nor disadvantage.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Strip intercropping differs from row intercropping primarily because:

Explanation: Strip intercropping involves growing crops in wider bands (strips) that allow independent tractor or machinery operation across each strip without damaging adjacent crops.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which agronomic factor is minimized during successful temporal relay intercropping?

Explanation: Relay cropping eliminates the fallow turnaround gap between harvesting one crop and sowing the next, maintaining continuous living root cover and reducing soil erosion.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In a replacement series intercropping design:

Explanation: In a replacement series, the total plant population is partitioned between component crops by substituting percentages of sole crop densities (e.g., 50:50 or 75:25).

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary reason for reduced insect pest incidence in diverse intercropping systems?

Explanation: Intercropping increases biodiversity, sheltering predators and parasatoids while creating a complex canopy that disrupts host plant location by specialized insect pests.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following parameters is NOT required to calculate Land Equivalent Ratio (LER)?

Explanation: Market prices are required for monetary indices like MAI, but LER is strictly a biological yield ratio independent of market prices.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the biological significance of root exudates in legume-cereal intercropping?

Explanation: Root exudates from legumes stimulate beneficial rhizosphere microorganisms and assist in mobilizing sparingly soluble soil nutrients like phosphorus.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which management practice is crucial when applying pre-emergence herbicides in intercropping systems?

Explanation: Since two different crop species grow close together, pre-emergence herbicides must be selective to both crops or applied via shielded sprayers to prevent phytotoxicity.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What characterizes temporal complementarity in intercropping systems?

Explanation: Temporal complementarity occurs when component crops have staggered growth peaks, reducing peak competition for water, light, and nutrients.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which term describes the total monetary value gained from an intercropping system relative to sole cropping?

Explanation: Monetary Advantage Index (MAI) evaluates the economic net return by multiplying LER economic value.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Why is canopy stratification important in successful intercropping systems?

Explanation: Canopy stratification allows taller, sun-loving base crops (e.g., maize) and shorter, shade-tolerant intercrops (e.g., cowpea) to share light energy without mutual shading stress.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a primary limitation of intercropping in intensive mechanized farming?

Explanation: Mechanized harvesting machinery is typically designed for sole-crop monocultures; multi-row intercropping complicates mechanical weeding and combine harvesting.

Calculate the Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) if the sole crop yield of Maize is 40 q/ha and Soybean is 20 q/ha, while in intercropping their respective yields are 30 q/ha and 10 q/ha.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which intercropping index measures the competitive dominance of crop A over crop B and accounts for their respective relative yield totals?

Solution & Rationale: Aggressivity (A) specifically denotes the difference between the relative yield of component crops, indicating competitive dominance.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

In a replacement series where Maize and Green Gram are sown in a 2:2 row ratio, what does the 2:2 notation represent?

Solution & Rationale: The notation 2:2 represents row proportion geometry: two consecutive rows of the base crop followed by two rows of the intercrop.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Which physiological mechanism enables certain intercrops to thrive under the shade of taller base crops?

Solution & Rationale: Shade-tolerant intercrops adapt via structural modifications in chloroplasts and higher chlorophyll b content to efficiently capture diffuse light.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the principal agronomic disadvantage of excessive competition for water in a dryland intercropping system?

Solution & Rationale: In dryland conditions, high plant density and root competition can rapidly deplete soil moisture, resulting in severe water stress and yield reduction.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

Which crop combination exemplifies 'multistorey cropping' (a specialized form of intercropping practiced in plantations)?

Solution & Rationale: Coconut palms form the upper tier, black pepper vines climb the trunk (middle tier), and pineapple occupies the ground level (lower tier).

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

What does a Competitive Ratio (CR) greater than 1 for crop A and less than 1 for crop B indicate?

Solution & Rationale: A CR value greater than 1 indicates higher competitive ability for that specific crop within the intercropping system.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which agronomic term describes growing a short-duration vegetable intercrop between rows of a slow-growing wide-spaced perennial base crop?

Solution & Rationale: Companion cropping utilizes the vacant inter-row spaces of wide-spaced crops (like sugarcane or orchards) during their slow early growth phase.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

How does intercropping affect the incidence of soil-borne plant pathogens compared to sole cropping?

Solution & Rationale: Increasing the distance between susceptible host plants dilutes inoculum potential and slows down the dispersal of soil-borne and foliar pathogens.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary objective of calculating the Actual Yield Loss (AYL) index in intercropping?

Solution & Rationale: AYL gives accurate insight into competition by comparing the actual yield of each intercrop with its expected yield based on its sown proportion.
