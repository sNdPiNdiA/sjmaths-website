Crop Sequence and Cropping Systems in Agronomy

Master the principles of crop sequencing, intensity indices, biological soil fertility replenishment, and agronomic management required for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture examinations.

1. Fundamentals of Crop Sequence and Cropping Systems

Crop sequence refers to the order in which different crops are grown on a specific piece of land over a fixed period, typically one agricultural year. It forms the core of an intensive cropping system aimed at maximizing productivity per unit area per unit time while conserving soil health.

  • Cropping Intensity: Calculated as the total cropped area divided by the total net cultivated area multiplied by 100. A standard intensive rotation achieves over 200%.
  • Monoculture vs. Poly-culture: Growing the exact same crop species continuously leads to a severe decline in soil fertility, buildup of soil-borne pathogens, and weed proliferation. Crop sequences break these cycles.
  • Rotational Principle: Deep-rooted crops like Cajanus cajan or Gossypium hirsutum should alternate with shallow-rooted cereals such as Triticum aestivum or Oryza sativa to tap nutrients from varying soil strata.

2. Agronomic Classification and Types of Crop Sequences

Crop sequences are classified based on duration, intensity, and the specific ecological requirements of the agro-climatic zone. In northern India (Uttar Pradesh plains), standard sequences revolve around the Kharif, Rabi, and Zaid seasons.

  • Rice-Wheat Sequence (Oryza sativa - Triticum aestivum): The predominant cropping sequence of the Indo-Gangetic Plains, covering millions of hectares.
  • Legume Integration: Alternating non-legumes with grain legumes like Cicer arietinum, Vigna radiata, or Lens culinaris helps fix atmospheric nitrogen via Rhizobium symbiosis, reducing inorganic N requirements by 30 to 50 kg/ha.
  • Green Manuring Sequences: Incorporating Sesbania aculeata (Dhaincha) before rice transplantation adds vital organic matter and suppresses weed emergence.

3. Soil Fertility, Nutrient Management, and Fertilizer Economics

Continuous cropping places immense pressure on macro and micronutrients. A sound crop sequence balances nutrient extraction rates between heavy feeders and restorative crops.

  • Nutrient Balance: Cereals deplete Nitrogen (N) and Phosphorus (P), whereas legumes fix N and utilize deeper potassium reserves.
  • Fertilizer Calculation Math: To apply 120 kg N/ha using Urea (containing 46% N), the total fertilizer required is: \((120 / 46) \times 100 = 260.8\) kg of urea per hectare.
  • Residual Effect: P applied to a preceding pulse crop (e.g., Cicer arietinum) shows a high residual availability for the succeeding cereal crop.

4. Weed Dynamics, Insect Pests, and Disease Management in Sequences

Crop sequencing acts as a natural biological filter to break pest and disease cycles.

  • Weed Suppression: Alternating dry-seeded crops with flooded transplanted rice (Oryza sativa) eliminates obligate weed species like Phalaris minor that infest wheat fields.
  • Soil-Borne Pathogens: Continuous growing of solanaceous crops like Solanum lycopersicum promotes bacterial wilt caused by Ralstonia solanacearum. Rotating with graminaceous crops disrupts the pathogen host cycle.
  • Integrated Pest Management (IPM): Using trap crops and non-host rotational crops reduces reliance on broad-spectrum synthetic pesticides.

5. Agro-Climatic Adaptations and High-Yielding Crop Rotations for Uttar Pradesh

Uttar Pradesh spans multiple agro-climatic zones ranging from the Western Plains to the Bundelkhand region, necessitating tailored crop sequences.

  • Western UP (High Irrigation): Maize (Zea mays) - Potato (Solanum tuberosum) - Wheat (Triticum aestivum) yielding ultra-high cropping intensity.
  • Eastern UP (Alluvial Plains): Rice (Oryza sativa) - Lentil (Lens culinaris) - Green gram (Vigna radiata).
  • Bundelkhand (Rainfed/Semi-arid): Sorghum (Sorghum bicolor) - Chickpea (Cicer arietinum) or Blackgram (Vigna mungo) - Mustard (Brassica juncea).

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Cropping intensity formula: (Total Cropped Area / Net Cultivated Area) * 100.
  • The Rice-Wheat sequence covers the largest agricultural area in the Indo-Gangetic Plains of India.
  • Sesbania aculeata (Dhaincha) is the most widely recommended green manure crop for reclamation of sodic soils before rice cultivation.
  • Continuous monoculturing of wheat leads to severe infestation of Phalaris minor (Gulli danda).
  • Urea contains 46% nitrogen; DAP contains 18% N and 46% P2O5.
  • Leguminous crops in a sequence reduce the inorganic nitrogen fertilizer requirement of succeeding cereal crops by 30-50 kg/ha.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing cropping intensity with crop rotation length; cropping intensity is expressed as a percentage per agricultural year, whereas rotation length is measured in years.
  • Assuming all legumes fix equal amounts of nitrogen; grain legumes fix lesser amounts compared to green manure legumes like Sesbania or Sunnhemp (Crotalaria juncea).

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Crop Sequence and Cropping Systems in Agronomy

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Definition and Objectives of Crop Sequence

Crop sequence is the systematic arrangement of different crops grown successively on the same land to maintain soil fertility and maximize net economic returns.

  • Crop Sequence: Temporal ordering of crops.
  • Objective: Minimizing pest buildup and optimizing nutrient use efficiency.
Concept 2

Cropping Intensity Index Calculations

The mathematical evaluation of land utilization efficiency within an agricultural calendar year.

  • Formula: (Gross Cropped Area / Net Sown Area) * 100.
  • Standard Intensive System: Exceeds 200% under perennial irrigation.
Concept 3

Role of Legumes in Crop Sequences

Biological nitrogen fixation through symbiosis between Rhizobium bacteria and legume root nodules.

  • Nutrient Economy: Saves significant chemical fertilizer input costs.
  • Soil Physical Condition: Improves crumb structure and organic matter.
Concept 4

Weed and Pest Disruption via Rotation

Changing host crops breaks the life cycle of specialized weeds and soil-borne pathogens.

  • Allele Disruption: Prevents buildup of obligate parasitic weeds like Striga or Orobanche.
  • Pathogen Control: Suppresses nematode populations and fungal wilts.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Crop Sequence (Temporal Crop Ordering)
The sequential order in which crops are cultivated on a specific plot of land over a defined time period.
Cropping Intensity (Land Utilization Index)
Percentage ratio of gross cropped area to net sown area in a single agricultural year.
Green Manuring (In-situ Biomass Incorporation)
Growing and incorporating fresh plant biomass (e.g., Sesbania aculeata) into the soil to improve fertility and organic matter.
Monoculture (Continuous Single Species Cropping)
Cultivation of the exact same crop species on the same field year after year without rotation.
Residual Effect (Nutrient Carryover)
The availability of unutilized nutrients applied to a preceding crop for the benefit of the succeeding crop.
Phalaris minor (Little Seed Canary Grass)
A major weed species mimicking wheat plants, commonly controlled by crop rotation and specialized herbicides.
Rhizobium (Symbiotic Nitrogen Fixing Bacteria)
Soil bacteria forming nodules on legume roots to fix atmospheric nitrogen gas.
Gross Cropped Area (Total Annual Sown Area)
Sum of area under cultivation across all crop seasons (Kharif, Rabi, Zaid) in one year.
Net Sown Area (Physical Cultivated Land Area)
The actual unique physical area of land sown with crops at least once in a year.
Trap Crop (Pest Lure Crop)
A crop planted alongside or in sequence to attract pests away from the primary cash crop.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Crop Rotation VS Crop Sequence

Crop rotation implies a recurring cyclical order over years, whereas crop sequence is the chronological order of crops grown in a specific timeframe.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the cropping intensity of a field where the net sown area is 100 hectares and the gross cropped area is 200 hectares?

Explanation: Cropping intensity = (Gross Cropped Area / Net Sown Area) * 100 = (200 / 100) * 100 = 200%.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following green manure crops is most suitable for reclaiming sodic and saline soils prior to rice cultivation?

Explanation: Sesbania aculeata (Dhaincha) is highly tolerant to alkalinity and waterlogging, making it the premier green manure crop for sodic soils.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Continuous cultivation of which crop sequence is primarily responsible for the severe infestation of Phalaris minor in Northern India?

Explanation: The Rice-Wheat sequence creates ecological conditions favoring Phalaris minor (little seed canary grass) due to similar growing seasons and tillage practices.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

How many kilograms of urea are required to supply 120 kg of pure nitrogen?

Explanation: Calculation: (120 / 46) * 100 = 2.6086 * 100 = 260.8 kg of urea.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary biological advantage of including a grain legume in a cereal-based crop sequence?

Explanation: Grain legumes fix atmospheric nitrogen via Rhizobium bacteria, enriching soil fertility and reducing subsequent nitrogen fertilizer requirements.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which term describes the cultivation of the exact same crop species continuously on the same land without rotation?

Explanation: Monoculture refers to growing a single crop species repeatedly on the same land, which often leads to soil fatigue and pest outbreaks.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In the calculation of cropping intensity, what does the denominator represent?

Explanation: Net Sown Area is the physical land area sown, serving as the denominator in the cropping intensity formula.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a deep-rooted crop suitable for rotation with shallow-rooted cereals?

Explanation: Cajanus cajan (Pigeonpea) develops a deep taproot system capable of extracting moisture and nutrients from lower soil profiles.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the nitrogen percentage present in Di-ammonium Phosphate (DAP)?

Explanation: DAP contains 18% Nitrogen and 46% Phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5).

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which agro-climatic region of Uttar Pradesh is best known for the Sorghum-Chickpea or Pearl Millet-Mustard rainfed cropping sequences?

Explanation: The Bundelkhand region features semi-arid conditions and rainfed agriculture, making drought-tolerant crops like sorghum and chickpea ideal.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the term for growing two or more crops simultaneously in distinct row arrangements without intercrop competition?

Explanation: Intercropping involves growing two or more crops simultaneously in definite row patterns on the same field during part of their life cycle.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following best defines 'Residual Effect' in crop sequences?

Explanation: Residual effect refers to nutrients applied to or fixed by a preceding crop that remain available in the soil for the succeeding crop.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which plant nutrient is primarily conserved and added to the soil by leguminous green manure crops?

Explanation: Leguminous green manure crops fix atmospheric nitrogen gas, significantly increasing soil total nitrogen content upon incorporation.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the maximum theoretical cropping intensity achievable in a year if four distinct short-duration crops are grown sequentially?

Explanation: Growing four sequential crops in one agricultural year yields a maximum cropping intensity of 400%.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest is effectively managed by rotating solanaceous crops with non-host graminaceous crops?

Explanation: Root-knot nematodes (Meloidogyne spp.) have wide host ranges among solanaceous crops but their populations decline when non-host cereal crops are grown in sequence.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following crops is classified as a heavy feeder of nutrients?

Explanation: Hybrid Maize (Zea mays) demands large amounts of nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium, making it a classic heavy feeder crop.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of introducing a break-crop in a crop sequence?

Explanation: Break-crops interrupt the life cycles of specialized weeds, insects, and soil-borne pathogens by replacing the host species.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which index measures the diversity and rotation frequency of crops grown over a specific cycle?

Explanation: The Crop Diversification Index evaluates the shift from monoculture to multiple crop species within a farming system.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Why is deep ploughed summer fallow or inter-season tillage recommended in intensive crop sequences?

Explanation: Summer ploughing exposes dormant weed seeds, insect pupae, and pathogen resting structures to solar heat and desiccation.

Calculate the exact quantity of Single Super Phosphate (SSP) required to supply 60 kg of P2O5 per hectare, given that SSP contains 16% P2O5.

Solution & Rationale: Formula: (Required P2O5 / % P2O5 in SSP) * 100 = (60 / 16) * 100 = 3.75 * 100 = 375 kg of SSP per hectare.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

In an intensive crop rotation of Rice-Wheat-Green Gram, what is the theoretical cropping intensity percentage if all three crops are successfully harvested in one agricultural year?

Solution & Rationale: Three distinct crop seasons utilized in a single year result in a gross cropped area three times the net sown area, yielding 300% intensity.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

Which biological characteristic makes Sesbania aculeata superior for green manuring in sodic soils compared to Crotalaria juncea?

Solution & Rationale: Sesbania aculeata exhibits high tolerance to soil alkalinity, high pH, and standing water, making it ideal for sodic soil reclamation.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following crop sequences exemplifies a restorative rotation that actively improves soil fertility without chemical nitrogen supplementation?

Solution & Rationale: Pigeonpea (Cajanus cajan) is a deep-rooted legume that fixes atmospheric nitrogen and drops nutrient-rich leaf litter, enhancing soil fertility for succeeding wheat.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary agronomic reason for alternating shallow-rooted cereal crops with deep-rooted taproot crops in a rotation?

Solution & Rationale: Alternating rooting depths prevents localized nutrient and moisture depletion from a single soil layer.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

If a farmer applies 150 kg N, 60 kg P2O5, and 40 kg K2O using Urea, DAP, and Muriate of Potash (MOP, 60% K2O), how much MOP is required?

Solution & Rationale: MOP contains 60% K2O. Formula: (Required K2O / 60) * 100 = (40 / 60) * 100 = 66.67 kg of MOP.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which weed is specifically known as a parasitic weed associated with solanaceous and brassica crops in sequence?

Solution & Rationale: Orobanche (broomrape) is a root parasite affecting crops like mustard, tomato, and potato.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary advantage of relay cropping over standard sequential cropping?

Solution & Rationale: Relay cropping overlaps crop cycles, saving time and utilizing residual soil moisture efficiently.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which scientific binomial refers to Dhaincha, the premier green manuring crop?

Solution & Rationale: Sesbania aculeata is the scientific name for Dhaincha.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

In nutrient budgeting for a crop sequence, what is meant by 'nutrient mining'?

Solution & Rationale: Nutrient mining occurs when crop extraction exceeds nutrient replenishment, leading to long-term soil fertility depletion.
