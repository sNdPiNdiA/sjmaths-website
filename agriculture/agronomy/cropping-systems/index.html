Cropping Systems and Intensification: Agronomic Principles and Management

1. Introduction and Definitions in Cropping Systems

A cropping system represents the pattern of crops taking up a given area of land over a specified period. It includes all components required for the production of a particular crop set and the interrelationships between them and farm environments. Understanding these systems is vital for maximizing resource utilization efficiency under Indian agro-climatic conditions.

  • Cropping Pattern: The yearly sequence and spatial arrangement of crops or crops and fallow on a given land area. For instance, the traditional Oryza sativa - Triticum aestivum (Rice-Wheat) cropping pattern dominant in the Indo-Gangetic Plains.
  • Cropping Intensity: The number of crops cultivated on the same field over one agricultural year, expressed as a percentage. Calculated as:
    Cropping Intensity (%) = (Total Gross Cropped Area / Net Sown Area) × 100.
  • Farming System: A complex matrix of soils, plants, animals, implements, workers, laborers, and other inputs controlled by the farmer and influenced by external institutional, physical, and socio-economic factors.

2. Classification of Cropping Systems

Cropping systems are broadly categorized based on spatial and temporal arrangements of crop species, water availability, and intensity of input use:

  • Monoculture (Monocropping): The repetitive growing of the exact same crop species on the same piece of land year after year. Example: Continuous cultivation of Triticum aestivum without rotational breaks, leading to severe soil fatigue and weed buildup.
  • Multiple Cropping: Growing two or more crops on the same field in one calendar year. This includes intercropping, mixed cropping, sequential cropping, and relay cropping.
  • Intercropping: Growing two or more crops simultaneously in distinct row arrangements on the same land. Examples include maize (Zea mays) with soybean (Glycine max).
  • Sequential Cropping: Growing crops in quick succession in the same field per year; the succeeding crop is planted after the preceding crop has been harvested. Example: Kharif Paddy → Rabi Wheat → Zaid Green Gram (Vigna radiata).
  • Relay Cropping: Planting a succeeding crop before harvesting the preceding mature crop. Example: Sowing lentils (Lens culinaris) in standing rice fields a few weeks before harvest.

3. Quantitative Indices in Intercropping Systems

Agronomic evaluation of intercropping systems requires precise mathematical indices to quantify biological efficiency and economic returns compared to sole cropping.

  • Land Equivalent Ratio (LER): The relative land area under sole crops required to produce the yields achieved in intercropping. LER = (Y_{ab}/Y_{aa}) + (Y_{ba}/Y_{bb}), where Y_{aa} and Y_{bb} are sole crop yields, and Y_{ab} and Y_{ba} are intercrop yields. An LER greater than 1.0 indicates yield advantages.
  • Competitive Ratio (CR): Measures the exact degree of competition between intercropped species. CR values indicate how much one crop is competing with another.
  • Aggressiveness: Indicates how much the relative increase in yield of component crop 'a' is greater than that of component crop 'b'.
  • Crop Index / Harvest Index (HI): Ratio of economic yield to biological yield expressed as a percentage.

4. Multi-Tier Cropping and Alley Cropping

Advanced intensification techniques maximize vertical space utilization and solar radiation interception:

  • Multi-Tier Cropping (Multistrata Systems): Cultivating crops of varying heights simultaneously on the same land. Common in tropical perennial plantations (e.g., Coconut canopy + Banana tier + Pineapple ground cover).
  • Alley Cropping (Agrisilviculture): Growing herbaceous crops between hedgerows of fast-growing woody shrubs or trees (e.g., Leucaena leucocephala). Hedgerows are pruned periodically to prevent shading and provide green leaf manure (nitrogen-rich biomass) to companion field crops.
  • Integrated Farming System (IFS): Combines crop husbandry with dairy, poultry, fishery, apiculture, or sericulture to ensure nutrient recycling, zero-waste generation, and steady farm income streams.

5. Agronomic Management and Sustainability Challenges

Intensive cropping systems present specific agronomic challenges that require scientific mitigation:

  • Nutrient Depletion: High cropping intensity exhausts macro- and micro-nutrients. Integrated Nutrient Management (INM) combining chemical fertilizers, farmyard manure (FYM), and biofertilizers (e.g., Azotobacter, Rhizobium) is critical.
  • Soil Health and Structure: Continuous puddling for rice breaks soil aggregates and forms hardpans. Inclusion of leguminous green manure crops like Sesbania aculeata (Dhaincha) restores organic carbon and fixes atmospheric nitrogen.
  • Weed and Pest Dynamics: Monocultures encourage specialized weed flora (e.g., Phalaris minor in wheat) and endemic pests. Crop rotation breaks weed-pest cycles naturally.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Cropping intensity formula: (Gross Cropped Area / Net Sown Area) × 100.
  • Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) > 1.0 signifies biological advantage of intercropping over sole cropping.
  • Rice-Wheat cropping system is the most dominant cropping system in the Indo-Gangetic Plains of India.
  • Alley cropping involves growing agricultural crops between hedgerows of nitrogen-fixing trees or shrubs.
  • Relay cropping involves planting the succeeding crop before the harvesting of the preceding crop (e.g., Lendl in standing rice).
  • Multistrata or multi-tier cropping utilizes vertical space stratification to optimize solar radiation capture.
  • Green manuring with Sesbania aculeata (Dhaincha) adds substantial organic matter and biological nitrogen to the soil.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing mixed cropping with intercropping: Mixed cropping has no distinct row arrangement and seeds are sown mixed, whereas intercropping involves definite row proportions.

Concept 1

Cropping Intensity Index

Quantifies land utilization frequency within a 12-month agricultural cycle.

  • Formula: (Gross Cropped Area ÷ Net Sown Area) × 100. Standard intensive systems exceed 200%.
Concept 2

Land Equivalent Ratio (LER)

The standard index for measuring intercropping biological efficiency.

  • Threshold: LER > 1 indicates yield advantage; LER < 1 indicates yield disadvantage.
Concept 3

Sequential Cropping Dynamics

Intensive temporal management where turnaround time between harvest and sowing is minimized.

  • Example: Kharif Paddy - Rabi Wheat - Zaid Green Gram.
Concept 4

Integrated Farming Systems (IFS)

Holistic agricultural production linking crop husbandry with allied agricultural enterprises.

  • Objective: Waste recycling, risk reduction, and nutritional security.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Cropping Intensity (Gross Cropped Area / Net Sown Area × 100)
The percentage ratio indicating how many times crops are grown on a given net area in one agricultural year.
Intercropping (Simultaneous Multi-crop Production)
Growing two or more crop species simultaneously in distinct row configurations on the same field.
Mixed Cropping (Broadcast Mixed Sowing)
Cultivation of two or more crops simultaneously with no distinct spatial row arrangement.
Relay Cropping (Overlap Sequential Sowing)
Sowing a succeeding crop into a standing mature crop before its harvest.
Alley Cropping (Agrisilvicultural Hedgerow System)
Growing annual food crops in alleys formed by hedgerows of perennial trees or shrubs.
Land Equivalent Ratio (LER Index)
Relative land area under sole crops required to equal the yield of an intercropping system.
Monoculture (Sole Monocropping)
Repetitive cultivation of a single crop species on the same field without rotational breaks.
Crop Rotation (Temporal Crop Sequencing)
Changing the type of crop grown on a specific field in successive seasons or years.
Multi-tier Cropping (Multistrata Canopy System)
Simultaneous cultivation of crops with different vertical heights and shade tolerances.
Net Sown Area (Physical Cultivated Area)
The physical land area sown with crops, counted only once regardless of crops grown within the year.
Gross Cropped Area (Total Cumulative Sown Area)
The total area sown once and/or multiple times in a year; sum of net sown area and area sown more than once.
Green Manuring (In-situ Biomass Incorporation)
Ploughing undecomposed green plant tissues (e.g., Sesbania aculeata) into the soil to enrich organic matter and nitrogen.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Formula for Cropping Intensity: (GCA / NSA) × 100.
  • LER > 1 means intercropping is superior to sole cropping.
  • Relay cropping minimizes turnaround time between two successive crops.
  • Rice-Wheat is the leading sequential cropping system in northern India.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Cropping systems determine spatial and temporal resource distribution in agriculture.
  • Cropping intensity reflects the frequency of crop cultivation on the net sown area.
  • Intercropping requires LER calculation to verify biological efficiency.
  • Sequential cropping maximizes annual production under assured irrigation.
  • Integrated Farming Systems ensure sustainable resource recycling and steady farm income.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Intercropping VS Mixed Cropping

Intercropping has definite row arrangements for efficient management, whereas mixed cropping involves random seed mixing without rows.

Sequential Cropping VS Relay Cropping

Sequential cropping has zero temporal overlap between crops, while relay cropping involves sowing the next crop before harvesting the previous one.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the formula for calculating cropping intensity in agronomy?

Explanation: Cropping intensity is expressed as a percentage of gross cropped area divided by net sown area multiplied by 100.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

If the Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) of an intercropping system is 1.25, what does it signify?

Explanation: An LER of 1.25 means 25% more land would be required under sole cropping to produce the same yield obtained from the intercropping system.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following describes relay cropping?

Explanation: Relay cropping involves overlapping crop cycles where the second crop is sown before harvesting the first crop.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary distinguishing feature of mixed cropping compared to intercropping?

Explanation: In mixed cropping, seeds of different crops are mixed together and sown without any distinct row pattern.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop is most commonly used for green manuring in Indian paddy-wheat cropping systems?

Explanation: Sesbania aculeata (Dhaincha) is widely used for green manuring due to its rapid biomass accumulation and nitrogen-fixing ability.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

The continuous cultivation of the same crop species on the same field year after year is known as:

Explanation: Monoculture refers to growing the exact same crop species continuously on the same land without rotation.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is alley cropping?

Explanation: Alley cropping is an agrisilvicultural practice where food crops are grown in the alleys between hedgerows of trees or shrubs.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which cropping system is characterized by the highest cropping intensity?

Explanation: Growing four crops sequentially within a year maximizes the gross cropped area relative to the net sown area, yielding the highest intensity.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main objective of adopting crop rotation?

Explanation: Crop rotation restores soil health, prevents nutrient depletion, and naturally suppresses specialized weeds and pests.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In the formula for Land Equivalent Ratio (LER), what do Y_{aa} and Y_{bb} represent?

Explanation: Y_{aa} and Y_{bb} represent the yields of sole crops 'a' and 'b' grown individually.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which index measures the competitive dominance of one crop over another in intercropping?

Explanation: Competitive Ratio and Aggressiveness quantify how strongly component crops compete for resources in an intercrop system.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does Integrated Farming System (IFS) primarily promote?

Explanation: IFS integrates crop production with allied enterprises like dairy and poultry to recycle wastes and optimize farm income.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which agro-climatic zone in India is synonymous with the Rice-Wheat cropping system?

Explanation: The Indo-Gangetic Plains spanning Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, and Bihar are the core area of the Rice-Wheat cropping system.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary advantage of multi-tier cropping systems?

Explanation: Multi-tier cropping exploits different canopy strata, maximizing solar radiation interception and total biomass production per unit area.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which term denotes the total area sown in a year, including areas sown more than once?

Explanation: Gross Cropped Area (GCA) represents the total area sown once or multiple times across agricultural seasons in a year.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Why are leguminous crops frequently included in crop rotations?

Explanation: Leguminous crops host Rhizobium bacteria in their root nodules, fixing atmospheric nitrogen and enriching soil fertility.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is strip cropping?

Explanation: Strip cropping involves planting strips of erosion-permitting crops and erosion-resisting crops alternately across slopes to check soil erosion.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is an example of sequential cropping?

Explanation: Rice → Wheat → Green gram represents sequential cropping where crops occupy the land one after another without temporal overlap.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the net sown area of a farm?

Explanation: Net Sown Area is the physical land area cultivated with crops during the year, counted once regardless of multiple cropping.

What does an LER value equal to 1.0 indicate?

Which intercropping system involves growing a main crop and an intercrop where the intercrop is planted after the main crop has established?

In Integrated Farming Systems (IFS), what is the primary benefit of linking fish culture with poultry farming?

Which management practice is most effective in breaking the life cycle of specialized weeds like Phalaris minor in wheat fields?

Solution & Rationale: Crop rotation breaks weed-pest cycles by altering cultural practices and competitive environments.

What is the key distinction between crop rotation and monoculture?
