Agronomy and Field Crops

Principles of Agronomy, Seed Technology, Soil Tillage, and Crop Production Dynamics

This module systematically covers foundational principles of agronomy, seed biology, tillage mechanics, crop nutrition, water management, weed dynamics, and cropping systems aligned with ICAR and UPSESSB standards.

1. Fundamental Principles of Agronomy, Historical Milestones, and Agro-Climatic Zoning

Agronomy is derived from the Greek words agros (field) and nomos (to manage). Pietro de' Crescenzi (1230–1307 AD) is widely regarded as the 'Father of Agronomy' for compiling early scientific farming methods in his treatise Opus Ruralium Commodorum. Modern agronomy integrates plant physiology, soil science, crop management, and agricultural meteorology to maximize yield per unit area, time, and input.

Key principles governing crop production include:

  • Agro-Climatic Adaptation: Selecting crops matched to thermal, photoperiodic, and moisture regimes. Planning based on Planning Commission classification (15 Agro-Climatic Zones in India) and NARP (127 Agro-Ecological Sub-zones).
  • Seedbed Optimization: Creating ideal seed-soil contact, aeration, and moisture retention for uniform germination and seedling establishment.
  • Nutrient Mass-Balance: Supplying essential macronutrients and micronutrients at optimal growth stages (Law of Minimum by Justus von Liebig, 1840).
  • Plant Population Dynamics: Managing optimum plant density to minimize intraspecific competition for light, carbon dioxide, water, and nutrients.
  • Integrated Pest and Weed Management: Minimizing pest and weed pressure below Economic Threshold Levels (ETL) through chemical, biological, cultural, and mechanical means.

2. Tillage Mechanics, Seed Physiology, and Geometry of Crop Stand

Tillage refers to the physical manipulation of soil to establish optimal physical conditions for seed germination, root growth, and weed suppression. Jethro Tull (1674–1741) is recognized as the 'Father of Tillage' and author of Horse-Hoeing Husbandry.

Types and Mechanics of Tillage:

  • Primary Tillage: Initial major soil-turning operation breaking hard pan and opening uncultivated soil (e.g., moldboard plow, disc plow, chisel plow). Target depth: 15–30 cm.
  • Secondary Tillage: Follows primary tillage to refine seedbed, destroy weeds, and level soil surface (e.g., harrows, cultivators, rotavators, plankers). Target depth: 5–10 cm.
  • Conservation Tillage: Leaves at least 30% crop residue on soil surface to minimize erosion and conserve soil moisture. Includes Zero Tillage (No-Till), Strip Tillage, and Minimum Tillage.

Seed Quality, Viability, and Planting Geometry:

Seed viability is measured using chemical tests like the 2,3,5-Triphenyl Tetrazolium Chloride (TTC) test, where living embryonic tissues convert colorless TTC into insoluble red formazan via dehydrogenase activity.

Plant population (P) formula based on spacing geometry:

$Plant Population (plants/ha) = 10,000Row-to-Row spacing (m) × Plant-to-Plant spacing (m)

3. Crop Nutrition Dynamics and Fertilizer Dose Computation

Plants require 17 essential elements for growth (Arnon and Stout, 1939; refined by Nicholas, 1961). Essential non-mineral elements: C, H, O. Primary macronutrients: N, P, K. Secondary macronutrients: Ca, Mg, S. Micronutrients: Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B, Mo, Cl, Ni.

Fertilizer Dosing Calculations:

To calculate the amount of commercial fertilizer required for a given dose of nutrients ( ext{kg/ha}):

Fertilizer Requirement (kg/ha) = Recommended Dose of Nutrient (kg/ha)Percentage of Nutrient in Commercial Fertilizer × 100

Example: To apply 120 kg N/ha using Urea (46% N):

Urea Required = 12046 × 100 = 260.87 kg/ha

If Single Superphosphate (SSP, 16% P_2O_5) is used to provide 60 kg P_2O_5/ha:

SSP Required = 6016 × 100 = 375 kg/ha

4. Irrigation Agronomy, Critical Growth Stages, and Water Use Efficiency

Irrigation scheduling is critical to avoid moisture stress at sensitive developmental stages. Crop Water Requirement (WR) is defined as:

WR = ET_c + Application Losses + Special Requirements (e.g., puddling, leaching)

Critical Stages for Moisture Stress in Major Crops:

  • Wheat (Triticum aestivum): Crown Root Initiation (CRI) at 21 DAS (most critical), Jointing, Booting, Flowering, Milk, and Dough stages.
  • Rice (Oryza sativa): Panicle Initiation (PI) and Flowering stage.
  • Maize (Zea mays): Tasseling and Silking stages.
  • Groundnut (Arachis hypogaea): Flowering and Pegging stages.
  • Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum): Square formation and Flowering/Boll development.

Water Use Efficiency (WUE):

Crop WUE (kg/ha-mm) = Economic Yield (kg/ha)Crop Evapotranspiration, ET_c (mm)

5. Weed Biology, Classification, Herbicide Physiology, and IWM

Weeds are unwanted, uncultivated plants that cause greater annual economic loss to Indian agriculture than pests and diseases combined. Jethro Tull coined the word 'Weed'.

Classification of Weeds Based on Life Cycle and Morphology:

  • Monocot / Narrow-leaf Weeds (Poaceae, Cyperaceae): Phalaris minor (Canary grass), Echinochloa crus-galli (Barnyard grass), Cyperus rotundus (Nut sedge - triangular stem, non-grass).
  • Dicot / Broad-leaf Weeds: Chenopodium album (Bathua), Convolvulus arvensis (Field bindweed), Parthenium hysterophorus (Carrot grass).
  • Parasitic Weeds:
    • Total Stem Parasite: Cuscuta spp. (Dodder on Lucerne/Dodder)
    • Partial Stem Parasite: Loranthus spp. (Dendrophthoe on fruit trees)
    • Total Root Parasite: Orobanche spp. (Broomrape on Mustard/Tobacco)
    • Partial Root Parasite: Striga spp. (Witchweed on Sorghum/Sugarcane)

Herbicides and Application Geometry:

Pre-emergence herbicides (e.g., Atrazine, Pendimethalin) are applied 1–3 days after sowing before weeds emerge. Post-emergence herbicides (e.g., Sulfosulfuron, Clodinafop-propargyl, 2,4-D) are applied active on emerged weed foliage (21–35 DAS).

6. Cropping Systems, Index Calculations, and Farming Systems Research

Understanding cropping systems and land utilization metrics is vital for agrarian economics and intensification analysis.

Cropping Intensity (CI):

Cropping Intensity (%) = Total Cropped AreaNet Sown Area × 100

Land Equivalent Ratio (LER): Evaluates yield advantage of intercropping over monocropping.

LER = (YabYaa) + (YbaYbb)

Where Yab is intercrop yield of crop a, Yaa is pure crop yield of crop a, Yba is intercrop yield of crop b, and Ybb is pure crop yield of crop b. If LER > 1.0, intercropping yields a higher productivity advantage than monoculture.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Jethro Tull is the Father of Weed Science and Father of Tillage; Pietro de' Crescenzi is the Father of Agronomy.
  • Critical Stage for Irrigation in Wheat is Crown Root Initiation (CRI), occurring 21 days after sowing (DAS).
  • Urea contains 46% Nitrogen, Single Superphosphate (SSP) contains 16% P2O5 and 12% Sulphur, and Muriate of Potash (MOP) contains 60% K2O.
  • 2,3,5-Triphenyl Tetrazolium Chloride (TTC) test measures seed viability by staining living embryo tissue red (formazan formation).
  • Herbicide 2,4-D is a selective, systemic hormone-type herbicide used to control broadleaf weeds in cereal crops.
  • Phalaris minor in wheat developed resistance against Isoproturon; controlled now using Sulfosulfuron (25 g/ha), Clodinafop (60 g/ha), or Pinoxaden.
  • Kranz Anatomy is a characteristic anatomical feature of C4 plants (e.g., Maize, Sorghum, Sugarcane).
  • Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) > 1.0 indicates yield advantage in intercropping over monocropping.
  • The Law of Minimum was formulated by Justus von Liebig in 1840.
  • The optimum temperature for germination of wheat seed is 20°C to 25°C.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Total Root Parasite with Partial Root Parasite: Orobanche is Total Root Parasite (Mustard/Tobacco), whereas Striga is Partial Root Parasite (Sorghum/Sugarcane).
  • Confusing Fertilizer Nitrogen content: Ammonium Sulphate contains 20.6% N and 24% S, Calcium Ammonium Nitrate (CAN) contains 25% N, and Urea contains 46% N.
  • Assuming Rotavator is a primary tillage implement: Rotavator is a secondary tillage tool (though often used for single-pass seedbed preparation).
  • Miscalculating LER: LER is calculated by dividing intercrop yields by MONOCROP yields, not total area yield.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Principles of Agronomy, Seed Technology, Soil Tillage, and Crop Production Dynamics

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Principles of Tillage and Seedbed Dynamics

Tillage alters soil bulk density, porosity, aeration, and thermal conductivity. Tilth is the physical condition of soil in relation to plant growth. Good tilth exhibits aggregated, crumbly soil structure with equal proportion of micro and macro pores (50% total pore space). Conservation tillage maintains continuous organic soil cover minimizing soil erosion below the T-value threshold.

Concept 2

Seed Science, Classes of Seed, and Viability Standards

Seed propagation involves four recognized generations: Nucleus Seed (100% pure, produced by breeder), Breeder Seed (Golden Yellow tag, 100% genetic purity), Foundation Seed (White tag, 99% purity), and Certified Seed (Azul/Blue tag, 98% purity, issued for commercial cultivation).

Concept 3

Plant Nutrition and Mineral Absorption Pathways

Nutrient uptake occurs via mass flow, diffusion, and root interception. Nitrogen is absorbed as NO_3^- and NH_4^+. Phosphorus is absorbed as H_2PO_4^- and HPO_42-. Mobilization in plant tissue: N, P, K, Mg are highly mobile (deficiency symptoms appear first on older lower leaves), whereas Ca and B are immobile (deficiency symptoms appear at the terminal growing tip).

Concept 4

Irrigation Agronomy and Crop Water Metrics

Soil water available to plants ranges between Field Capacity (-0.33 bar) and Permanent Wilting Point (-15 bar). Micro-irrigation systems (Drip/Trickle) achieve highest application efficiency (90–95%), while Overhead Sprinkler achieves 70–80% efficiency.

Concept 5

Weed Physiology, Herbicidal Selectivity, and Resistance Mechanics

Weeds compete via allelopathy (excretion of biochemical secondary metabolites like juglone or sorgoleone) and high reproductive capacity. Herbicide modes of action include ALS inhibitors (Sulfonylureas), Photosystem II inhibitors (Atrazine, Simazine), and EPSP Synthase inhibitors (Glyphosate).

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Agronomy (Agronomy)
A branch of agricultural science dealing with field crop management and soil principles for sustainable crop production.
Tilth (Soil Tilth)
The physical condition of soil in respect to its fitness for planting and growth of crops.
Blind Tillage (Blind Tillage)
Tillage performed after sowing crops but before crop seedling emergence to kill young germinating weed seedlings.
Real Value of Seed (Real Value (%) = (Purity % x Germination %) / 100)
A percentage index reflecting both physical purity and germination potential of a seed lot.
Economic Threshold Level (ETL) (Economic Threshold Level)
The pest population density at which control measures should be applied to prevent reaching the Economic Injury Level (EIL).
Allelopathy (Allelopathy)
The direct or indirect harmful or beneficial effect of one plant on another through the release of chemical compounds into the environment.
Field Capacity (Field Capacity (-0.33 bar / -33 kPa))
The amount of soil moisture or water content held in soil after excess gravitational water has drained away.
Permanent Wilting Point (PWP) (PWP (-15 bar / -1500 kPa))
The minimal soil moisture level at which plants wilt and fail to recover turgidity even when placed in a saturated atmosphere.
Cropping Intensity (Cropping Intensity Index)
The ratio of total cropped area to net sown area expressed as a percentage.
Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) (Land Equivalent Ratio)
The relative land area under sole crops required to produce yields equivalent to those obtained under intercropping systems.
Consumptive Use (CU) (Consumptive Use = ET + Water used in plant tissue)
The total quantity of water used by plants in evapotranspiration and metabolic plant tissue development.
Critical Period of Weed Competition (CPWC)
The duration during crop growth cycle when weed growth causes maximum reduction in crop yields if unmanaged.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Foundation Seed Tag VS Certified Seed Tag

Foundation seed carries a White certification tag, whereas Certified seed carries an Azure Blue certification tag.

Cuscuta VS Orobanche

Cuscuta is a Total Stem Parasite affecting crops like Lucerne, whereas Orobanche is a Total Root Parasite affecting Solanaceous and Cruciferous crops.

Cropping Intensity VS Harvest Index

Cropping Intensity measures land usage over time [(Total Cropped Area / Net Sown Area) x 100], whereas Harvest Index measures economic efficiency [(Economic Yield / Biological Yield) x 100].

Synergism VS Allelopathy

Synergism refers to mutually cooperative positive interactions, whereas Allelopathy typically refers to biochemical inhibition exerted by one plant species on another.

Question 1 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Who is universally regarded as the 'Father of Agronomy'?

Explanation: Pietro de' Crescenzi is called the Father of Agronomy for his comprehensive compilation of agricultural practices. Jethro Tull is the Father of Tillage and Weed Science.

Question 2 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which tag color is officially allotted to 'Breeder Seed' containers in India?

Explanation: Breeder seed containers are issued with a Golden Yellow tag. Foundation seed carries a White tag, and Certified seed carries an Azure Blue tag.

Question 3 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical compound is utilized in seed testing laboratories to determine embryo seed viability?

Explanation: The Tetrazolium (TTC) test measures respiration in living embryonic cells. Dehydrogenase enzymes reduce TTC to red-colored insoluble triphenylformazan.

Question 4 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

If a farmer needs to apply 115 kg of pure Nitrogen per hectare using Urea, how much Urea fertilizer must be applied per hectare?

Explanation: Urea dose = (115 / 46) * 100 = 250 kg/ha.

Question 5 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which stage is considered the most critical moisture-sensitive stage for irrigation in Wheat (Triticum aestivum)?

Explanation: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) occurring at 21 DAS is the absolute most critical stage for wheat irrigation; stress at CRI causes irreversible yield reduction.

Question 6 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

The parasitic weed Orobanche spp. (Broomrape) is classified as a:

Explanation: Orobanche is a Total Root Parasite that mainly attacks Solanaceous crops, Tobacco, and Mustard.

Question 7 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Kranz anatomy, absence of photorespiration, and high photosynthetic efficiency are key characteristics of:

Explanation: C4 plants possess Kranz leaf anatomy (specialized bundle sheath cells surrounded by mesophyll cells), allowing efficient carbon fixation with negligible photorespiration.

Question 8 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

In soil water terminology, the moisture tension at 'Field Capacity' is approximately equal to:

Explanation: Field capacity is held at -0.33 bar (-33 kPa or pF 2.54). Permanent Wilting Point occurs at -15 bar.

Question 9 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following implements is strictly categorized as a Primary Tillage implement?

Explanation: Moldboard (MB) Plow is a primary tillage implement designed to break, lift, and invert soil slices. Harrows and rotavators are secondary tillage equipment.

Question 10 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the Cropping Intensity of a farm where Net Sown Area is 10 hectares and Total Cropped Area in a year is 25 hectares?

Explanation: Cropping Intensity = (25 / 10) * 100 = 250%.

Question 11 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which essential plant nutrient is an integral central atom of the Chlorophyll molecule?

Explanation: Magnesium (Mg2+) forms the central atom of the porphyrin ring in chlorophyll molecules.

Question 12 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Phalaris minor (Canary grass) is a prominent mimicry weed in which crop field?

Explanation: Phalaris minor is a major narrow-leaf annual weed infesting wheat crops across Indo-Gangetic plains.

Question 13 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

The Law of Minimum, which states that crop yield is limited by the scarcest nutrient resource, was propounded by:

Explanation: Justus von Liebig proposed the Law of Minimum in 1840. Arnon and Stout proposed criteria of essentiality in 1939.

Question 14 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Blind tillage is typically recommended in which crop to break soil crust and destroy early weeds before emergence?

Explanation: Blind tillage is performed in Sugarcane and Potato after planting but before shoot emergence to crush soil crust and destroy emerging weed seedlings.

Question 15 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) if pure crop yields of Crop A and Crop B are 4 t/ha and 2 t/ha, while their intercropped yields are 3 t/ha and 1.5 t/ha respectively?

Explanation: LER = (3 / 4) + (1.5 / 2) = 0.75 + 0.75 = 1.50. This indicates a 50% yield advantage over monocropping.

Question 16 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Deficiency symptoms of Calcium (Ca) and Boron (B) first appear on which part of the plant?

Explanation: Calcium and Boron are highly immobile inside plant vascular tissues; therefore, deficiency symptoms appear at the terminal apical bud and growing tips.

Question 17 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Atrazine and Simazine belong to which chemical group of selective herbicides?

Explanation: Atrazine and Simazine belong to the Triazine group of herbicides, which inhibit Photosystem II electron transport.

Question 18 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which formula calculates the Real Value of a seed lot?

Explanation: Real Value of Seed (%) = (Physical Purity % × Germination %) / 100.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Match the following selective herbicides with their target crop: 1. Pendimethalin -> A. Broad-spectrum Pre-emergence 2. Atrazine -> B. Maize & Sugarcane 3. Sulfosulfuron -> C. Wheat 4. Glyphosate -> D. Non-selective systemic

Solution & Rationale: Pendimethalin is broad-spectrum pre-emergence, Atrazine is selective pre-emergence for Maize/Sugarcane, Sulfosulfuron is post-emergence for Wheat weeds, and Glyphosate is non-selective systemic.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

Which form of soil moisture is most readily absorbed by plant root hair systems?

Solution & Rationale: Capillary water held in soil micropores between -0.33 bar and -15 bar tension is the main pool of available soil moisture for roots.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Striga spp. (Witchweed) acts as a semi/partial root parasite primarily attacking which host crops?

Solution & Rationale: Striga species are partial root parasites that infect C4 cereal crops like Sorghum, Sugarcane, Maize, and Pearl Millet.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

Single Superphosphate (SSP) fertilizer contains which percentage proportion of phosphorus (P_2O_5) and sulfur (S) respectively?

Solution & Rationale: SSP contains 16% citrate-soluble P_2O_5$, 12% Sulfur, and 19–21% Calcium.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the Harvest Index (HI) of a pulse crop yielding 2,000 kg/ha economic seed yield and 6,000 kg/ha total biological yield?

Solution & Rationale: Harvest Index (%) = (Economic Yield / Biological Yield) x 100 = (2000 / 6000) x 100 = 33.33%.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following is a non-selective, systemic, post-emergence herbicide used for total vegetation management?

Solution & Rationale: Glyphosate is non-selective, systemic, post-emergence. Paraquat is non-selective but contact in action.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

Intercropping of Chickpea with Mustard in 6:1 row ratio is a classic example of:

Solution & Rationale: Chickpea + Mustard (6:1) is an additive series intercropping practice popular in rainfed northern India.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Which enzyme converts colorless 2,3,5-Triphenyl Tetrazolium Chloride into red triphenylformazan during viability testing?

Solution & Rationale: Active respiratory dehydrogenase enzymes in viable plant embryonic tissues reduce TTC to insoluble red formazan.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

Zero Tillage practice combined with retention of crop residues on the soil surface is called:

Solution & Rationale: Conservation Agriculture (CA) rests on three pillars: minimal soil disturbance (Zero Tillage), permanent organic soil cover (residue mulch), and crop diversification.
