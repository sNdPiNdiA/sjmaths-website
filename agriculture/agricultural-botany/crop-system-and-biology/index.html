1. Botanical Taxonomy, Origin, and Chromosomal Constitution of Major Crops

Understanding the botanical classification, centre of origin, family, and ploidy level of major agronomic crops forms the foundation of Agricultural Botany for competitive examinations like UP PGT/TGT Agriculture. Taxonomy dictates reproductive behavior, breeding strategies, and inheritance patterns.

  • Rice (Oryza sativa): Belongs to the family Poaceae (Gramineae), subfamily Pooideae/Oryzoideae, tribe Oryzeae. Origin: Indo-Burma region or South China. Chromosome number: 2n = 2x = 24 (Diploid).
  • Wheat (Triticum aestivum): Family Poaceae, tribe Triticeae. Origin: South-Western Asia (Near East). It is a hexaploid species with 2n = 6x = 42, possessing three genomes (AABBDD).
  • Maize (Zea mays): Family Poaceae, tribe Maydeae. Origin: Central America (Mexico). Chromosome number: 2n = 2x = 20. It is a monoecious cross-pollinated C4 plant.
  • Pigeonpea (Cajanus cajan): Family Fabaceae (Leguminosae), subfamily Papilionoideae. Origin: Africa/India. Chromosome number: 2n = 2x = 22.
  • Mustard (Brassica juncea): Family Brassicaceae (Cruciferae). Origin: Central Asia/China. Amphidiploid with 2n = 4x = 36 (AABB genomes).

2. Crop Growth Stages, Phenology, and Critical Irrigation Periods

Crop phenology tracks morphological development over time in response to thermal units (Growing Degree Days - GDD) and photoperiod. Identifying critical growth stages is vital for irrigation scheduling and fertilizer top-dressing to prevent yield penalties.

  • Crown Root Initiation (CRI) in Wheat: Occurs 20-25 days after sowing (DAS). This is the most critical moisture-sensitive stage where the permanent root system establishes below the soil surface.
  • Panicle Initiation and Flowering in Rice: Reproductive phase is extremely sensitive to moisture stress. Water deficit during microsporogenesis causes sterility and chaffy grains.
  • Silking and Tasseling in Maize: Asynchrony between pollen shedding and silk emergence due to drought severely reduces grain setting. Optimal moisture is essential 10 days before and after silking.
  • Branching and Pod Filling in Pulses: Moisture stress during pod development results in flower drop and shrivelled seeds, drastically lowering harvest index.

3. Fertilizer Calculation Mathematics and Nutrient Management

Agronomic calculations involving nutrient conversions from commercial fertilizers to pure nutrient equivalents (N, P_2O_5, K_2O) are frequently tested in UP PGT examinations. Mastery of these stoichiometric equations ensures precise field application.

  • Urea Calculation Formula: Urea contains 46% Nitrogen (N). To apply 100 kg N ha-1, the physical weight of urea required = 1000.46 = 217.39 kg.
  • DAP Calculation Formula: Diammonium Phosphate (DAP) contains 18% N and 46% P_2O_5. To supply 60 kg P_2O_5 ha-1, the physical DAP required = 600.46 = 130.43 kg. This application simultaneously supplies 130.43 × 0.18 = 23.48 kg N.
  • Muriate of Potash (MOP) Calculation: MOP contains 60% K_2O. To apply 40 kg K_2O ha-1, physical MOP required = 400.60 = 66.67 kg.
  • Integrated Nutrient Management (INM): Combines chemical fertilizers, farmyard manure (FYM), green manuring (e.g., Sesbania aculeata), and biofertilizers (Rhizobium, Azotobacter, Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria - PSBs) to maintain soil health and biological equilibrium.

4. Crop Protection: Weeds, Pests, Diseases, and Economic Threshold Levels

Crop biology is inextricably linked with biotic stress management. Understanding causal organisms, vectors, weed-crop competition windows, and chemical controls is essential for maximizing crop productivity.

  • Weed Management: Phalaris minor in wheat is a major grassy weed controlled effectively by Clodinafop-propargyl or Sulfosulfuron. Striga is a root parasitic weed affecting sorghum and maize. Cuscuta is a total stem parasite on Lucerne (Medicago sativa).
  • Insect Pests: Helicoverpa armigera (gram pod borer) is a polyphagous pest attacking pulses and cotton. Scirpophaga incertulas (yellow stem borer) causes 'dead heart' in vegetative rice and 'white ear' at flowering.
  • Diseases: Karnal bunt of wheat is caused by Tilletia indica (partial smut, emitting a fishy odour due to trimethylamine). Rice blast is caused by Magnaporthe oryzae. Loose smut of wheat is caused by Ustilago tritici (internally seed-borne).
  • Economic Threshold Level (ETL): The population density of a pest at which control measures must be initiated to prevent the pest population from reaching the Economic Injury Level (EIL).

5. Harvesting Indices, Post-Harvest Technology, and Storage Standards

Harvest maturity is determined using physiological markers, grain moisture content, and Harvest Index (HI), which is the ratio of economic yield to biological yield expressed as a percentage.

  • Harvest Index (HI): HI = (Economic YieldBiological Yield) × 100. Modern dwarf cereal varieties exhibit high HI values ranging from 0.40 to 0.50 (40% - 50%).
  • Safe Grain Storage Moisture: Cereal grains (wheat, rice, maize) must be stored at ≤ 12% moisture content. Oilseeds (mustard, groundnut, soybean) require safer storage moisture thresholds of ≤ 8% - 9% to prevent fungal proliferation (e.g., Aspergillus flavus producing aflatoxins).
  • Physiological Maturity Indicator: Disappearance of the black layer at the placental scar of maize kernels or hard dough stage in wheat kernels indicates cessation of dry matter translocation from source to sink.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing C3 and C4 plants: Remember that rice and wheat are C3 plants, whereas maize, sorghum, and sugarcane are C4 plants.
  • Miscalculating fertilizer physical weight: Always divide the required nutrient quantity by the percentage concentration expressed as a decimal (e.g., 50 kg N using Urea = 50 / 0.46 = 108.7 kg urea).
  • Confusing wheat smut types: Loose smut of wheat (Ustilago tritici) is internally seed-borne, whereas covered/karnal bunt is partially seed and soil-borne.
  • Assuming all pulses have identical chromosome numbers: Pigeonpea has 2n = 22, whereas chickpea has 2n = 16.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Crop System and Biology in Agricultural Botany

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Botanical Nomenclature and Classification

The foundation of crop science relies on binomial nomenclature established by Carl Linnaeus. Every crop has a genus and species name written in italics, accompanied by family grouping according to ICBN rules.

  • Binomial Nomenclature: Genus capitalized, species in lowercase, italicized.
  • Ploidy Level: Defines the number of complete sets of chromosomes (2n).
Concept 2

Crop Phenology and Thermal Units

Crop development is driven by temperature accumulation known as Growing Degree Days (GDD). Phenological stages dictate irrigation, weeding, and nutrient schedules.

  • GDD Formula: GDD = Tmax + Tmin2 - Tbase
  • Base Temperature: Threshold below which crop growth ceases (10°C for most temperate cereals).
Concept 3

Stoichiometry of Fertilizer Application

Accurate fertilizer calculation requires converting recommended pure nutrient doses into commercial fertilizer formulations based on guaranteed nutrient grades.

  • Nutrient Grade: Percentage composition of N, P_2O_5, and K_2O by weight.
  • Conversion Factor: Physical Weight = Recommended NutrientPercentage Grade (decimal).
Concept 4

Integrated Crop Protection and Economic Threshold

Managing weeds, insects, and plant pathogens requires understanding biological thresholds and ecological interactions to ensure sustainable agricultural production.

  • ETL: Pest density threshold triggering chemical or biological intervention.
  • Parasitic Weeds: Total root parasite (Orobanche), Total stem parasite (Cuscuta), Semi-root parasite (Striga).

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Harvest Index (Harvest Index (HI))
The ratio of economic yield to total biological yield expressed as a percentage.
Protandry (Protandric Development)
Condition where male reproductive organs (anthers/tassels) mature before female organs (stigmas/silks).
CRI Stage (Crown Root Initiation)
Critical irrigation stage in wheat occurring 20-25 days after sowing when permanent roots establish.
Photoperiodism (Photoperiodic Response)
Physiological reaction of organisms to the length of day or night (Short-day, Long-day, Day-neutral plants).
Amphidiploid (Allopolyploid)
An allotetraploid containing two complete diploid sets of chromosomes from two different species.
Photorespiration (C2 Cycle)
Light-dependent uptake of oxygen and release of carbon dioxide by photosynthetic tissue, lowering C3 efficiency.
ETL (Economic Threshold Level)
Pest population density at which control measures should be applied to prevent economic injury.
Dead Heart (Stem Borer Symptom)
Central shoot drying up and turning brown in vegetative tillering stage of cereals due to borer feeding.
Biofertilizer (Microbial Inoculant)
Substances containing living microorganisms that colonize the rhizosphere and promote growth.
Climacteric Fruit (Ripening Respiration Surge)
Fruits that continue to ripen after harvest accompanied by an increase in respiration rate (e.g., banana, tomato).

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Loose Smut of Wheat VS Karnal Bunt of Wheat

Loose smut (Ustilago tritici) destroys the entire earhead leaving only the rachis internally seed-borne, whereas Karnal bunt (Tilletia indica) infects only a few grains partially, leaving embryos intact.

C3 Plants VS C4 Plants

C3 plants use RuBisCO and produce 3-PGA, having high photorespiration; C4 plants use PEP carboxylase initially and store carbon in 4-carbon acids with negligible photorespiration.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a C4 photosynthetic crop?

Explanation: Maize (Zea mays) is a classical C4 plant with high photosynthetic efficiency and optimal temperature requirements between 30°C and 47°C.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

How much physical urea (46% N) is required to supply 92 kg of pure nitrogen?

Explanation: Physical urea required = 920.46 = 200 kg. Urea contains 46% nitrogen by weight.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the most critical irrigation stage for wheat crop?

Explanation: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) occurs 20-25 DAS and is the critical moisture-sensitive stage determining root system establishment and final tillering.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Karnal bunt of wheat is caused by which causal organism?

Explanation: Karnal bunt is caused by Tilletia indica (Neovossia indica), which infects individual grains partially and produces a characteristic fishy odour.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the center of origin of rice (Oryza sativa)?

Explanation: The primary center of origin for Asian rice (Oryza sativa) is the Indo-Burma region, characterized by high genetic diversity.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Phalaris minor is a major weed commonly associated with which crop?

Explanation: Phalaris minor is a troublesome grassy weed in wheat fields, often exhibiting resistance to substituted urea herbicides.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the safe moisture content threshold for safe storage of cereal grains like wheat and rice?

Explanation: Cereal grains must be stored at or below 12% moisture content to prevent fungal growth and grain deterioration.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which enzyme acts as the primary carbon dioxide acceptor in C4 plants during initial fixation?

Explanation: Phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP) carboxylase is the primary carboxylating enzyme in C4 mesophyll cells, fixing CO2 into oxaloacetate.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does Harvest Index (HI) measure?

Explanation: Harvest Index represents the efficiency of dry matter partitioning towards the economically useful plant parts.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosome number of chickpea (Cicer arietinum)?

Explanation: Chickpea (Cicer arietinum) is a diploid pulse crop with 2n = 2x = 16 chromosomes.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which insect pest causes 'dead heart' symptoms in rice?

Explanation: Yellow stem borer (Scirpophaga incertulas) larvae feed inside rice stems, cutting off growing points and causing dead hearts.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the percentage of phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5) in single superphosphate (SSP)?

Explanation: SSP contains 16% P2O5, alongside 12% sulfur and 19-20% calcium.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which parasitic weed is a total root parasite on crops like tobacco and tomato?

Explanation: Orobanche (broomrape) is a total root parasite (holoparasite) that severely damages solanaceous crops.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is protandry in crop plants?

Explanation: Protandry ensures cross-pollination by shedding pollen before stigmas become receptive, as seen in maize.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is an amphidiploid crop species?

Explanation: Indian mustard (Brassica juncea) is an amphidiploid (2n = 36) derived from interspecific hybridization between Brassica nigra and Brassica rapa.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the Economic Threshold Level (ETL) in pest management?

Explanation: ETL is the population level of a pest where intervention must occur to prevent the pest population from reaching the Economic Injury Level.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which gas is responsible for the fishy odour in Karnal bunt infected wheat grains?

Explanation: Trimethylamine is produced by the spores of Tilletia indica, imparting a distinct foul fishy odour to infected wheat lots.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of biofertilizers containing Rhizobium?

Explanation: Rhizobium bacteria form symbiotic associations with legume roots to fix atmospheric nitrogen directly into plant-available forms.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which term describes plants that flower only when day length is shorter than a critical photoperiod?

Explanation: Short-day plants (SDPs) require long nights/short days to initiate flowering, typical of tropical origin crops.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

In C4 plants, the primary carbon dioxide fixation occurs in which cells before being transferred to bundle sheath cells?

Solution & Rationale: Mesophyll cells contain PEP carboxylase which initially fixes CO2 into C4 acids, subsequently transported to bundle sheath cells for Calvin cycle integration.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the economic significance of the 'black layer' formation in maize grain development?

Solution & Rationale: The black layer at the placental scar signifies physiological maturity, after which no further dry matter is translocated into the kernel.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following fungal pathogens is internally seed-borne and causes loose smut of wheat?

Solution & Rationale: Ustilago tritici is internally seed-borne, infecting the embryo and manifesting as black spore masses replacing wheat earheads.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the chromosome number (2n) and ploidy level of cultivated rice (Oryza sativa)?

Solution & Rationale: Cultivated rice is a diploid species with 2n = 2x = 24 chromosomes.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Which herbicide is classified as a selective systemic post-emergence herbicide commonly used against broadleaf weeds in wheat?

Solution & Rationale: 2,4-D (2,4-Dichlorophenoxyacetic acid) is a selective systemic auxin-type herbicide used post-emergence for broadleaf weed control in cereal crops.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

What does the term 'protogyny' signify in floral biology?

Solution & Rationale: Protogyny is the maturation of female floral organs (stigma) prior to male organs (anthers), preventing self-pollination.

Solution & Rationale: India is divided into 15 broad agro-climatic zones based on soil, climate, topography, and water availability.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary mode of action of Rhizobium in legume symbiosis?

Solution & Rationale: Rhizobium bacteria fix atmospheric nitrogen gas into ammonia within specialized root nodules through nitrogenase enzyme activity.
