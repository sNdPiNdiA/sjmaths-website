Introduction to Veterinary Epidemiology and Disease Classification

Animal diseases constitute the single largest economic bottleneck in livestock production, impacting milk yield, draft power, reproduction efficiency, and carcass quality. Understanding the etiology and epidemiological triangle—host, agent, and environment—is fundamental for veterinary prophylaxis.

  • Infectious Diseases: Caused by pathogenic microorganisms including viruses, bacteria, rickettsia, fungi, and protozoa that can be horizontally or vertically transmitted.
  • Contagious Diseases: A subset of infectious diseases that spread rapidly through direct contact, fomites, or aerosol droplets (e.g., Foot and Mouth Disease, Rinderpest).
  • Non-Infectious / Metabolic Diseases: Caused by nutritional deficiencies, physiological stress, hormonal imbalances, or toxicities (e.g., Milk Fever, Ketosis, Bloat).
  • Zoonotic Diseases: Infections transmissible between animals and humans, posing severe public health hazards (e.g., Brucellosis, Anthrax, Rabies).

Major Viral Diseases of Livestock

Viral infections in livestock are highly contagious, causing systemic morbidity and significant mortality unless controlled via strict biosecurity and vaccination protocols.

  • Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD): Caused by an Aphthovirus (Picornaviridae family) with 7 distinct serotypes (O, A, C, SAT1, SAT2, SAT3, Asia-1). Characterized by high fever, excessive salivation, and vesicular lesions on the oral mucosa, interdigital spaces, and coronary bands.
  • Rinderpest (Cattle Plague): An acute, highly fatal viral disease caused by a Morbillivirus. Officially eradicated globally in 2011 through concerted FAO/OIE vaccination campaigns.
  • Rabies (Hydrophobia): Caused by a Lyssavirus (Rhabdoviridae family) affecting the central nervous system of all warm-blooded animals and humans via bite wounds containing infected saliva.
  • Blue Tongue: An insect-borne viral disease of sheep and goats transmitted by Culicoides midges, characterized by cyanosis of the tongue, edema, and lameness.

Major Bacterial and Zoonotic Diseases

Bacterial pathogens cause severe systemic infections, reproductive failures, and sudden mortalities in ruminants and equines.

  • Anthrax: Caused by the spore-forming bacterium Bacillus anthracis. It is peracute, causing sudden death with dark, tarry, non-clotting blood oozing from natural orifices. Carcasses must not be opened to prevent spore formation.
  • Black Quarter (BQ): Caused by Clostridium chauvoei, affecting young cattle (6-24 months). Characterized by acute, painful, crepitant swelling in heavy muscle groups (hindquarters).
  • Haemorrhagic Septicaemia (HS): Caused by Pasteurella multocida (Serotype B:2 or E:2), commonly known as 'Pasteurellosis' or 'Ghotwa'. Highly fatal in cattle and buffaloes during the rainy season, causing respiratory distress and severe edema in the throat region.
  • Brucellosis (Contagious Abortion): Caused by Brucella abortus in cattle, B. melitensis in sheep/goats, and B. suis in swine. Characterized by third-trimester abortions, retained placenta, and orchitis in bulls. Zoonotic (undulant fever in humans).

Metabolic and Nutritional Deficiency Disorders

Metabolic diseases occur due to imbalances in dietary nutrients, mineral homeostasis, or endocrine dysregulation during high production phases (e.g., peak lactation).

  • Milk Fever (Parturient Paresis): Hypocalcemia in high-yielding dairy cows around parturition due to sudden calcium drain into colostrum exceeding dietary uptake and mobilization from bone. Treated with intravenous calcium borogluconate.
  • Ketosis (Acetonemia): Hypoglycemia and hyperketonemia occurring in early lactation when energy intake cannot meet the massive demands of milk synthesis, leading to mobilization of body fat and accumulation of ketone bodies.
  • Bloat (Tympanites): Acute ruminal distension caused by the accumulation of fermentation gases trapped in a stable foam (legume bloat) or due to physical obstruction of the esophagus (free-gas bloat).
  • Pica: Depraved appetite caused by phosphorus deficiency, leading animals to chew bones, wood, soil, and rags.

Parasitic Infestations and Vector-Borne Diseases

Endoparasites and ectoparasites cause chronic debility, reduced feed conversion efficiency, and anemia.

  • Theileriosis: Protozoan disease caused by Theileria annulata, transmitted by the brown ear tick Hyalomma anatolicum. Characterized by high fever, enlarged superficial lymph nodes, and generalized weakness.
  • Babesiosis (Redwater Fever): Caused by intra-erythrocytic protozoa Babesia bovis or B. bigemina, transmitted by ticks (Boophilus microplus), leading to hemoglobinuria (red urine) and severe anemia.
  • Fascioliasis (Liver Fluke Infestation): Caused by the trematode Fasciola hepatica or F. gigantica, utilizing freshwater snails (Lymnaea species) as intermediate hosts. Causes chronic weight loss, bottle jaw, and liver cirrhosis.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD) is caused by an Aphthovirus possessing 7 immunologically distinct serotypes.
  • Anthrax is a peracute zoonotic bacterial disease caused by Bacillus anthracis where carcass necropsy is strictly prohibited.
  • Milk Fever (Parturient Paresis) is treated effectively via intravenous administration of Calcium Borogluconate.
  • Haemorrhagic Septicaemia (HS) outbreaks peak during the rainy season and are caused by Pasteurella multocida (Serotype B:2).
  • The intermediate host for the liver fluke Fasciola gigantica is the aquatic snail belonging to the genus Lymnaea.
  • Rinderpest (Cattle Plague) was globally eradicated in 2011 through massive international immunization campaigns.
  • The vector for bovine tropical theileriosis (Theileria annulata) is the hard tick Hyalomma anatolicum.
  • Brucellosis causes infectious abortion in the third trimester of pregnancy and is zoonotic, causing undulant fever in humans.
  • Ketosis in high-yielding dairy cows is characterized by hypoglycemia, hypoglycemia-induced fat mobilization, and sweet-smelling breath due to acetone.
  • Black Quarter (BQ) primarily affects young, well-nourished cattle between 6 to 24 months of age, presenting crepitant swellings.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the causal organism of Black Quarter (Clostridium chauvoei) with Anthrax (Bacillus anthracis).
  • Opening the carcass of an animal suspected of dying from Anthrax—doing so exposes vegetative bacteria to oxygen, forming highly resistant spores that contaminate soil for decades.
  • Assuming Milk Fever is caused by a dietary infection rather than a physiological, acute homeostatic failure of calcium mobilization at parturition.
  • Treating viral diseases like FMD with antibiotics—antibiotics only prevent secondary bacterial infections, not the primary viral pathogen.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Animal Diseases and Veterinary Science for UP TGT & PGT Agriculture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Etiology and Classification of Animal Diseases

Animal diseases are broadly categorized based on etiology into infectious, non-infectious, parasitic, and metabolic disorders.

  • Infectious Agent: Pathogenic entities capable of replication and horizontal/vertical transmission.
  • Zoonoses: Diseases naturally transmissible from vertebrate animals to humans.
Concept 2

Viral Infections and Control Measures

Viral diseases spread via aerosols, direct contact, and vectors, requiring stringent quarantine and vaccination protocols.

  • FMD Serotypes: 7 global serotypes with no cross-immunity.
  • Rabies Neurotropism: Travel of virus via peripheral nerves to the central nervous system.
Concept 3

Bacterial Pathology and Carcass Management

Bacterial pathogens form spores or toxins that cause rapid systemic failure and death.

  • Anthrax Prohibition: Never perform post-mortem on suspected anthrax carcasses to prevent soil spore contamination.
  • HS Seasonality: High incidence in monsoon due to stress and high humidity.
Concept 4

Metabolic Disorders and Mineral Homeostasis

High milk production places immense physiological stress on mineral and energy reserves.

  • Milk Fever: Hypocalcemia treated with calcium borogluconate IV.
  • Ketosis: Energy deficit leading to ketone body accumulation in blood and milk.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Epizootic (Epizooticus)
An outbreak of disease affecting many animals of the same kind at the same time in a specific region (equivalent to an epidemic in humans).
Zoonosis (Zoonosis)
Any disease or infection that is naturally transmissible from vertebrate animals to humans.
Crepitant (Crepitatio)
A crackling sound or feeling under the skin caused by gas accumulation, typical of Black Quarter lesions.
Hemoglobinuria (Haemoglobinuria)
The presence of free hemoglobin in the urine, characteristic of blood parasite infections like Babesiosis.
Parturient Paresis (Hypocalcaemia puerperalis)
Metabolic paralysis occurring around calving due to severe blood calcium depletion (Milk Fever).
Ketosis (Acetonemia)
Metabolic disorder characterized by elevated ketone bodies in body fluids resulting from negative energy balance.
Vesicle (Vesicula)
A small fluid-filled blister on skin or mucous membrane, hallmark of FMD.
Vector (Vector biologicus)
An organism, typically a biting arthropod, that transmits a disease-causing pathogen from one host to another.
Fomite (Inanimate carrier)
Inanimate objects or materials (such as bedding, feeding troughs, boots) that are capable of carrying infectious agents.
Pica (Allocasia / Depraved Appetite)
Behavioral disorder where animals ingest non-food items due to mineral (phosphorus) deficiency.
Endemic (Enzootic)
A disease that is continually present in a particular animal community or geographic region.
Quarantine (Quarantena)
Strict isolation imposed on animals to prevent the spread of infectious disease.

  • FMD is caused by an Aphthovirus with 7 serotypes and presents vesicular lesions on feet and mouth.
  • Anthrax (Bacillus anthracis) causes sudden death with uncoagulated tarry blood; autopsies are prohibited.
  • Black Quarter (Clostridium chauvoei) affects young cattle with crepitant muscular swellings.
  • Milk Fever is caused by acute hypocalcemia at parturition and is treated with intravenous calcium borogluconate.
  • Haemorrhagic Septicaemia (Pasteurella multocida) causes acute respiratory distress and throat edema during monsoons.
  • Liver fluke (Fasciola) utilizes freshwater snails of the genus Lymnaea as intermediate hosts.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Haemorrhagic Septicaemia VS Black Quarter

HS is caused by a bacterium (Pasteurella multocida) affecting the respiratory system and throat; BQ is caused by a clostridial spore former affecting muscle groups with crepitant gas swellings.

Milk Fever VS Ketosis

Milk Fever is calcium deficiency (hypocalcemia) around parturition; Ketosis is energy/glucose deficiency (hypoglycemia) in early lactation.

Question 1 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following viral diseases of livestock was officially declared globally eradicated in 2011?

Explanation: Rinderpest was officially declared eradicated globally in May 2011 by the FAO and OIE, making it only the second animal disease (after rinderpest in humans/smallpox in humans) to be eradicated.

Question 2 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

The causal organism of Black Quarter (BQ) in cattle is:

Explanation: Black Quarter is an acute infectious bacterial disease caused by Clostridium chauvoei, characterized by gas-filled, crepitant swellings in heavy muscle groups.

Question 3 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Why is post-mortem examination strictly prohibited in an animal suspected of dying from Anthrax?

Explanation: Opening an anthrax carcass exposes Bacillus anthracis bacilli to oxygen, triggering spore formation. These spores persist in the soil for decades, contaminating pastures indefinitely.

Question 4 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Haemorrhagic Septicaemia (HS) outbreaks in cattle and buffaloes in India are most prevalent during which season?

Explanation: Haemorrhagic Septicaemia, caused by Pasteurella multocida, peaks during the monsoon season due to high humidity, stress, and favorable environmental conditions.

Question 5 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Milk Fever in dairy cows is primarily caused by a deficiency of which mineral in the blood plasma around calving?

Explanation: Milk Fever (Parturient Paresis) is a metabolic disorder caused by acute hypocalcemia (low blood calcium) occurring when calcium demand for colostrum exceeds dietary intake and bone mobilization.

Question 6 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

The vector responsible for the transmission of Bovine Theileriosis (Theileria annulata) is:

Explanation: Hyalomma anatolicum (brown ear tick) is the primary biological vector for transmitting Theileria annulata, the protozoan parasite causing tropical theileriosis in cattle.

Question 7 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

How many distinct immunological serotypes of the Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD) virus exist globally?

Explanation: FMD virus exists in 7 distinct serotypes: O, A, C, SAT1, SAT2, SAT3, and Asia-1, with no cross-immunity between them.

Question 8 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following diseases is characterized by third-trimester abortions and retained placenta in cows?

Explanation: Brucellosis, caused by Brucella abortus, has a strong tropism for placental trophoblasts, leading to late-term abortions and retained fetal membranes.

Question 9 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Ketosis in high-yielding dairy cows during early lactation is primarily a disorder of:

Explanation: Ketosis (Acetonemia) arises from a severe negative energy balance where glucose demand for lactose synthesis exceeds hepatic gluconeogenesis, causing fat mobilization and ketone body accumulation.

Question 10 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

The intermediate host for the liver fluke Fasciola gigantica is:

Explanation: Fascioliasis requires an aquatic or semi-aquatic intermediate snail host belonging to the genus Lymnaea to complete its complex larval life cycle.

Question 11 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which specific treatment is administered intravenously for acute Milk Fever in dairy cattle?

Explanation: Intravenous infusion of Calcium Borogluconate solution is the standard, life-saving therapeutic intervention for cows down with Milk Fever.

Question 12 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Rabies virus belongs to which viral family?

Explanation: Rabies is caused by a Lyssavirus belonging to the family Rhabdoviridae, characterized by its bullet-shaped morphology and neurotropic properties.

Question 13 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Pica in farm animals is a behavioral symptom primarily associated with the deficiency of:

Explanation: Pica (depraved appetite) in ruminants is a classic sign of phosphorus deficiency, driving animals to chew inedible objects like bones and soil.

Question 14 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which bacterial disease is commonly known as 'Ghotwa' or throat edema in Indian veterinary practice?

Explanation: Haemorrhagic Septicaemia is locally known as 'Ghotwa' due to the characteristic severe edematous swelling of the throat and neck region causing asphyxiation.

Question 15 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Blue Tongue disease in sheep is transmitted by:

Explanation: Blue Tongue is a non-contagious viral disease of sheep transmitted exclusively by biting midges of the genus Culicoides.

Question 16 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a zoonotic bacterial disease transmissible via unpasteurized milk from infected cows?

Explanation: Brucella abortus is excreted in milk and can infect humans consuming unpasteurized dairy products, causing undulant fever (Brucellosis).

Question 17 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

The vaccine strain commonly used for immunization against Brucellosis in young female calves is:

Explanation: Brucella abortus Strain 19 is a live attenuated vaccine widely used globally and in India to immunize female calves against brucellosis.

Question 18 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary pathological sign of Babesiosis (Redwater Fever) in cattle?

Explanation: Babesiosis causes intravascular hemolysis, releasing large amounts of hemoglobin into the bloodstream, which is excreted in urine, causing redwater fever.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Match the following animal diseases with their respective causal agents: 1. Anthrax - A. Clostridium chauvoei, 2. Black Quarter - B. Bacillus anthracis, 3. Haemorrhagic Septicaemia - C. Pasteurella multocida. Choose the correct combination:

Solution & Rationale: Anthrax is caused by Bacillus anthracis (1-B), Black Quarter by Clostridium chauvoei (2-A), and Haemorrhagic Septicaemia by Pasteurella multocida (3-C).

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following statements regarding Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD) is scientifically INCORRECT?

Solution & Rationale: Although FMD has 7 serotypes, infection or vaccination with one serotype does NOT confer cross-immunity to the other serotypes.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

A dairy cow in its third lactation exhibits staggering, sternal recumbency, cold extremities, and subnormal body temperature within 24 hours of parturition. The most probable diagnosis and immediate treatment are:

Solution & Rationale: These are classic clinical signs of parturient paresis (Milk Fever) caused by hypocalcemia, requiring immediate IV calcium borogluconate.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

In the epidemiological triangle of animal diseases, which component represents the vector or fomite?

Solution & Rationale: Environmental factors encompass all external conditions, vectors, fomites, and housing systems that facilitate or impede pathogen transmission.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which bacterial pathogen produces extremely resilient endospores that survive in soil for decades and is responsible for sudden death with uncoagulated blood in ruminants?

Solution & Rationale: Bacillus anthracis forms heat- and desiccation-resistant endospores that remain viable in soil for decades, causing peracute anthrax.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

The causative agent of tropical theileriosis in cattle, transmitted by Hyalomma anatolicum, belongs to which group of organisms?

Solution & Rationale: Theileria annulata is an intra-erythrocytic and lymphocytic protozoan parasite belonging to the phylum Apicomplexa.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which metabolic disease of ruminants is characterized by a sweet, fruity odor of acetone on the breath and milk?

Solution & Rationale: Ketosis results in elevated ketone bodies (acetoacetate, beta-hydroxybutyrate, and acetone), giving breath and milk a characteristic sweet, fruity acetone odor.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary objective of prophylactic vaccination against Haemorrhagic Septicaemia (HS) just before the onset of monsoon?

Solution & Rationale: Pre-monsoon vaccination ensures peak humoral immunity when Pasteurella multocida challenge is highest due to rains and seasonal stress.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which parasitic trematode requires freshwater snails of the genus Lymnaea as an obligatory intermediate host to complete its life cycle?

Solution & Rationale: Fasciola gigantica (liver fluke) utilizes aquatic Lymnaea snails for asexual reproduction and development of cercariae.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following describes a 'contagious' disease as distinct from a purely 'infectious' disease?

Solution & Rationale: While all contagious diseases are infectious, contagious diseases are specifically distinguished by their high rate of direct or indirect transmission between animals.
