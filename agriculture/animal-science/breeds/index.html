Introduction to Livestock Breeds and Taxonomic Classification

Animal husbandry forms a vital component of the agricultural economy in India. A breed is defined as a homogenous group of domestic animals within a species that shares common ancestry, distinct morphological features, and uniform physiological characteristics developed through deliberate selection or geographic isolation.

  • Cattle (Bos indicus and Bos taurus): Indigenous Zebu cattle (Bos indicus) possess a prominent hump, drooling dewlap, and high heat tolerance, whereas exotic European cattle (Bos taurus) lack a hump and are specialized for high milk production under temperate climates.
  • Water Buffalo (Bubalus bubalis): Divided into riverine types (found in India, Pakistan, and Western Asia, with 50 chromosomes) and swamp types (found in Southeast Asia and China, with 48 chromosomes).
  • Classification based on Utility: Livestock breeds are broadly categorized into dairy (milch), draught (working), dual-purpose (both milk and meat/draught), and mutton/fiber/pelt-producing breeds.

Indigenous and Exotic Cattle Breeds of India

India possesses 53 recognized indigenous cattle breeds adapted to diverse agro-climatic conditions. Understanding their specific traits is crucial for competitive examinations.

  • Milch Breeds: High milk yielders with pendulous udders and long teats. Examples include Gir (Gujarat, red and white spotted coat, convex forehead), Sahiwal (Punjab/Haryana, reddish-brown coat, considered the best milch breed in India), and Red Sindhi (Sindh origin, deep red coat).
  • Draught Breeds: Bullocks are strong, active, and heat-resistant; milk yield is generally low. Examples include Nagori (Rajasthan, fast-trotting white bullocks), Amritmahal (Karnataka, grey coat, sharp horns), Kangayam (Tamil Nadu), and Khillari (Maharashtra).
  • Dual-Purpose Breeds: Animals yield moderate milk and possess good draught capability. Examples include Hariana (Haryana/UP, white-grey coat), Kankrej (Gujarat, lyre-shaped horns, known as 'Wadhiyar' in movement), Ongole (Andhra Pradesh), and Tharparkar (Rajasthan, white coat resistant to harsh drought).
  • Exotic Breeds: High milk-producing breeds introduced from temperate regions. Holstein-Friesian (highest milk yield, black and white patches), Jersey (high butterfat percentage, fawn-colored coat, smallest body size among dairy exotics), and Brown Swiss.

Buffalo Breeds of India and Their Economic Traits

India holds the world's highest buffalo population, contributing over 55% of the total milk production in the country. Buffaloes are hardy, possess superior feed conversion efficiency for coarse roughages, and exhibit wallowing behavior.

  • Murrah: Originating in Rohtak and Jind districts of Haryana. Known as the 'Black Gold' of India. Features tightly curved horns, jet black coat with white markings on the tail switch, and high milk yield (1500–2500 kg per lactation).
  • Nili-Ravi: Found in the Sutlej river valley. Characterized by wall eyes (white iris), white markings on the forehead, face, legs, and tail switch ( പഞ്ചകല്യാണി / Panchakallani).
  • Mehsana: Found in Gujarat, resulting from a cross between Murrah and Surti breeds. Milk has a high fat percentage.
  • Surti: Originating in Kaira and Baroda districts of Gujarat. Sickle-shaped horns, brown or grey coat, and high butterfat content (up to 8-10%).
  • Jaffarabadi: Found in Saurashtra region of Gujarat. Heaviest and largest Indian buffalo breed with very heavy, drooping horns.
  • Bhadawari: Found in Agra and Etawah districts of Uttar Pradesh. Copper-colored body coat with a distinct high fat content (up to 12-14%).

Sheep and Goat Breeds: Small Ruminant Production

Sheep and goats are termed 'poor man's cow' due to their adaptability, low maintenance cost, and multiple-product yield (meat, milk, wool, manure, and skin).

  • Sheep Breeds: Mainly reared for wool and mutton. Nali (Rajasthan, fine carpet wool), Chokla (Rajasthan, known as the 'Merino of India' due to fine fleece), Gaddi (Hilly tracts of Himachal Pradesh, fine white wool), Deccani (South India, mixed black/grey fleece for coarse blankets), and Nellore (Andhra Pradesh, tall, hair sheep reared exclusively for mutton).
  • Goat Breeds: Classified for meat, milk, and dual purpose. Jamunapari (Etawah district, Uttar Pradesh, largest Indian goat breed with roman nose and pendulous ears, high milk producer), Beetal (Punjab, similar to Jamunapari but smaller), Barbari (Agra/Mathura, dwarf goat with short erect ears, highly prolific twins/triplets), Black Bengal (West Bengal, premium quality meat and skin, high prolificacy), and Boer (South African meat breed used for crossbreeding).

Swine (Pig) Breeds and Poultry Breeds Classification

Swine husbandry offers rapid economic return due to high prolificacy, short generation interval, and high dressing percentage (70-80%).

  • Exotic Pig Breeds: Large White Yorkshire (most popular exotic breed in India, white coat, dish face, excellent mothering ability), Landrace (long body, extra ribs, excellent bacon producer), and Duroc (reddish coat, excellent growth rate and meat quality).
  • Indigenous Pig Breeds: Desi pigs found scavenging across rural India, characterized by slow growth and small litter size, but possessing high disease resistance.
  • Poultry Breeds: Classified into American (Plymouth Rock, New Hampshire), Asiatic (Brahma, Cochin - heavy breeds with feathered shanks), Mediterranean (Leghorn - top egg layer), and Indian/Indigenous (Aseel - renowned for grit, stamina, and cockfighting; Kadaknath - famous for completely black meat and high medicinal value).

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Sahiwal is widely recognized as the best indigenous dairy cattle breed of India, originating from the Montgomery district.
  • Murrah buffaloes are known as 'Black Gold' due to their jet black coat and high milk-producing capacity with tightly curved spiral horns.
  • Bhadawari buffalo milk contains the highest butterfat percentage among all dairy species, ranging from 12% to 14%.
  • Chokla is known as the 'Merino of India' because it produces superior quality fine carpet wool.
  • Jamunapari is the largest Indian goat breed, distinguished by its roman nose and long pendulous ears.
  • Barbari goat breed is famous for its prolific breeding habit, frequently producing twins and triplets.
  • Kadaknath poultry breed, indigenous to Madhya Pradesh, possesses completely black melanin-pigmented meat and holds a GI tag.
  • Large White Yorkshire is the most widely distributed exotic pig breed in India known for excellent mothering ability.
  • Nili-Ravi buffaloes possess characteristic 'wall eyes' and white markings on their extremities (Panchakallani).
  • Aseel is a famous indigenous chicken breed celebrated for its exceptional fighting stamina, gameness, and dense meat texture.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Sahiwal (milch cattle breed originating in Punjab) with Sahiwal sheep or other species. Sahiwal is exclusively a cattle breed.
  • Believing that Bhadawari buffaloes yield the highest total volume of milk; in reality, they yield low volume but the highest fat percentage (12-14%).
  • Mistaking Surti for a sheep breed; Surti is an important dairy buffalo breed from Gujarat known for sickle-shaped horns.
  • Assuming exotic dairy cattle like Holstein-Friesian thrive without heat stress management in tropical Indian plains.

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Taxonomic and Utility Classification of Livestock

Livestock breeds are systematically classified based on their anatomical features and primary economic purpose. The categorization into milch, draught, and dual-purpose ensures targeted breeding programs under the National Gokul Mission.

  • Milch Breeds: Specialized for high milk production with feminine wedge-shaped bodies and well-developed udders.
  • Draught Breeds: Sturdy bullocks designed for heavy field labor with compact bodies and strong legs.
Concept 2

Indigenous Dairy Excellence: Sahiwal and Gir

Indigenous Zebu cattle are naturally adapted to tropical parasites and disease pressures. Sahiwal and Gir represent the pinnacle of milk production among Indian breeds.

  • Sahiwal: Known for tick resistance, docile temperament, and lactation yield averaging 2000–2500 kg.
  • Gir: Features a prominent convex forehead (dome-shaped) and long pendulous ears that protect eyes from flies.
Concept 3

Superiority of Indian Riverine Buffaloes

India's dairy industry relies heavily on riverine buffaloes for liquid milk supply due to their high fat content and efficient conversion of coarse crop residues.

  • Murrah: Recognized globally for genetic superiority and export value for dairy improvement.
  • Bhadawari: Unique copper-colored coat adapted to ravines of the Chambal river basin.
Concept 4

Small Ruminant Production: Sheep and Goats

Small ruminants provide livelihood security to marginal farmers and landless laborers across arid and semi-arid zones.

  • Chokla Sheep: Produces the finest carpet wool in India.
  • Jamunapari Goat: The premier dairy goat breed of Northern India with high daily milk yield.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Breed (Homogenous Livestock Population)
A group of domestic animals having common origin and uniform characteristics that distinguish them from other groups within the same species.
Zebu Cattle (Bos indicus)
Humped cattle species indigenous to South Asia, possessing high tolerance to heat and tropical ectoparasites.
Riverine Buffalo (Bubalus bubalis (River type))
Buffaloes with 50 chromosomes preferring clean running water and deep rivers, specialized for milk and meat.
Dual-Purpose Breed (Milch & Draught Utility)
Breeds where females are fairly good milk producers and males are useful for agricultural draught operations (e.g., Hariana, Kankrej).
Lactation Yield (Total Milk Production per Lactation)
The total volume of milk produced by a female animal from calving to weaning or dry period, typically measured over 305 days.
Panchakallani (Five White Points)
A term applied to Nili-Ravi buffaloes having white markings on the forehead, face, four legs, and tail switch.
Wallowing (Mud Bath Behavior)
A thermoregulatory behavior exhibited by buffaloes to lower body temperature through evaporative cooling in water or mud.
Prolificacy (High Litter Size)
The reproductive capacity to produce multiple offspring per parturition, as seen in Barbari and Black Bengal goats.
Fleece (Sheep Wool Coat)
The coat of wool shorn from a sheep, evaluated based on fiber diameter, crimp, and tensile strength.
Exotic Breed (Introduced Foreign Breed)
Livestock breeds developed outside India, typically in temperate zones, known for high production traits (e.g., Holstein-Friesian, Jersey).

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Sahiwal Cattle VS Sindhi Cattle

Sahiwal has a reddish-brown coat and is heavier; Red Sindhi has a deep red coat and smaller body size.

Murrah Buffalo VS Nili-Ravi Buffalo

Murrah has tightly curled spiral horns and jet black coat; Nili-Ravi has loosely curled horns, wall eyes, and white markings on extremities.

Question 1 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following indigenous cattle breeds is considered the best milch breed in India?

Explanation: Sahiwal is universally acclaimed as the best indigenous milch cattle breed in India, known for high milk yield and rich flavor.

Question 2 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosome number of riverine buffaloes (Bubalus bubalis)?

Explanation: Riverine buffaloes possess 2n = 50 chromosomes, while cattle (Bos indicus) possess 2n = 60 chromosomes.

Question 3 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which buffalo breed is famous for having the highest butterfat percentage in its milk (12-14%)?

Explanation: Bhadawari buffalo milk contains exceptionally high fat, ranging between 12% and 14%, making it distinct among all dairy breeds.

Question 4 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which sheep breed is popularly known as the 'Merino of India' due to its fine carpet wool?

Explanation: Chokla sheep, found primarily in Rajasthan, produces the finest quality wool in India, earning it the title 'Merino of India'.

Question 5 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is the largest Indian goat breed with a Roman nose and long pendulous ears?

Explanation: Jamunapari is the tallest and largest goat breed in India, highly valued for milk production and characterized by its distinct Roman nose.

Question 6 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the characteristic horn shape of the Murrah buffalo?

Explanation: Murrah buffaloes are distinguished by their short, tightly curved horns forming a spiral shape.

Question 7 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which exotic dairy cattle breed holds the record for the highest milk production per lactation?

Explanation: Holstein-Friesian (HF) is the highest milk-producing exotic dairy cattle breed in the world.

Question 8 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which indigenous chicken breed of Madhya Pradesh is famous for its black meat and internal organs due to melanin pigmentation?

Explanation: Kadaknath is renowned for its completely black meat, blood, and bones, exhibiting high protein and low fat content.

Question 9 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a dual-purpose cattle breed?

Explanation: Hariana is a prominent dual-purpose Indian cattle breed found extensively in Haryana and Western Uttar Pradesh.

Question 10 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosome number of domestic pigs (Sus domesticus)?

Explanation: The diploid chromosome number of swine is 38 (2n = 38).

Question 11 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Question 12 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which exotic pig breed is most popular and widely distributed across India for commercial pork production?

Explanation: Large White Yorkshire is the most successful and widely adapted exotic pig breed in India.

Question 13 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the term 'Panchakallani' associated with?

Explanation: Nili-Ravi buffaloes are called Panchakallani when they possess white markings on the forehead, face, four legs, and tail tip.

Question 14 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which Indian cattle breed is characterized by lyre-shaped horns and white-grey coat, originating in Gujarat?

Explanation: Kankrej is a dual-purpose cattle breed from Gujarat featuring large, lyre-shaped horns and a distinct gait known as 'Sawai'.

Question 15 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which poultry breed is famous in India for its unbeatable fighting stamina and game qualities?

Explanation: Aseel is an indigenous Indian chicken breed renowned for its pugnacity, high stamina, and majestic posture.

Question 16 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a draught-purpose cattle breed?

Explanation: Nagori is a specialized draught cattle breed known for its fleet-footed bullocks.

Question 17 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the average gestation period in buffaloes?

Explanation: The gestation period in buffaloes is longer than in cattle, averaging around 310 days (range 300 to 320 days).

Question 18 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Explanation: Nellore is a tall, hair sheep breed of Southern India reared exclusively for meat production.
