Species Of Animals And Birds: Classification, Characteristics, and Economic Significance

This comprehensive study material delves into the scientific classification, key characteristics, and economic importance of various animal and bird species crucial for agriculture. Mastering this topic is fundamental for UP TGT and UP PGT Agriculture aspirants, as it forms the bedrock of animal husbandry and veterinary science knowledge.

1. Introduction to Animal Classification and Taxonomy

Animal classification is the scientific process of arranging organisms into groups based on shared characteristics, reflecting evolutionary relationships. This systematic approach, known as taxonomy, helps in understanding biodiversity, managing genetic resources, and improving animal production. The hierarchical system typically includes Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, and Species.

  • Kingdom: Animalia (all animals)
  • Phylum: Chordata (animals with a notochord)
  • Subphylum: Vertebrata (animals with a backbone)
  • Class: Mammalia (mammals), Aves (birds), Pisces (fish), etc.
  • Order, Family, Genus, Species: Further specific groupings.
  • The scientific name of an animal consists of its Genus and Species, written in italics (e.g., Bos indicus).

2. Major Livestock Species: Taxonomy and Characteristics

Understanding the specific characteristics and taxonomic details of major livestock is crucial for their efficient management and breeding.

2.1. Cattle (Bovine)

  • Scientific Name: Bos indicus (Zebu/Humped cattle), Bos taurus (Taurine/Humpless cattle)
  • Chromosome Number (2n): 60
  • Origin: Bos indicus from South Asia, Bos taurus from Europe/Middle East.
  • Key Characteristics: Ruminant, cloven-hoofed, primary source of milk, meat (beef), and draft power. Indian breeds are known for heat tolerance and disease resistance.
  • Economic Importance: Dairy (milk, butter, cheese), Meat, Leather, Draft power, Dung (manure, biogas).

2.2. Buffalo (Bubaline)

  • Scientific Name: Bubalus bubalis (Domestic water buffalo)
  • Chromosome Number (2n): River buffalo (e.g., Murrah) 50; Swamp buffalo (e.g., Carabao) 48
  • Origin: Asia.
  • Key Characteristics: Ruminant, adapted to hot and humid climates, known for rich milk fat content.
  • Economic Importance: Milk (higher fat content than cow milk), Meat (carabeef), Draft power, Leather.

2.3. Sheep (Ovine)

  • Scientific Name: Ovis aries
  • Chromosome Number (2n): 54
  • Origin: Central Asia.
  • Key Characteristics: Ruminant, woolly coat, adaptable to various climates, known for grazing efficiency.
  • Economic Importance: Wool, Meat (mutton, lamb), Milk, Skin.

2.4. Goat (Caprine)

  • Scientific Name: Capra hircus
  • Chromosome Number (2n): 60
  • Origin: Western Asia.
  • Key Characteristics: Ruminant, browsers, highly adaptable, often called 'poor man's cow' due to low input requirements.
  • Economic Importance: Milk, Meat (chevon), Fiber (cashmere, mohair), Skin.

2.5. Pig (Porcine)

  • Scientific Name: Sus scrofa domesticus
  • Chromosome Number (2n): 38
  • Origin: Eurasia.
  • Key Characteristics: Non-ruminant (monogastric), omnivorous, high prolificacy, rapid growth rate, high feed conversion efficiency.
  • Economic Importance: Meat (pork, bacon, ham), Lard, Leather, Bristles.

3. Important Poultry Species: Taxonomy and Production Traits

Poultry farming is a significant component of animal agriculture, providing protein-rich food products.

3.1. Chicken (Galline)

  • Scientific Name: Gallus gallus domesticus
  • Chromosome Number (2n): 78
  • Origin: Southeast Asia.
  • Key Characteristics: Most common poultry, high egg and meat production, short generation interval.
  • Economic Importance: Eggs, Meat (broiler, culled layers), Feathers, Manure.

3.2. Duck (Anatine)

  • Scientific Name: Anas platyrhynchos domesticus
  • Chromosome Number (2n): 80
  • Origin: Asia.
  • Key Characteristics: Waterfowl, hardy, resistant to common poultry diseases, good foragers, known for large eggs and fatty meat.
  • Economic Importance: Eggs, Meat, Feathers (down), Pest control in rice fields.

3.3. Turkey (Meleagrine)

  • Scientific Name: Meleagris gallopavo
  • Chromosome Number (2n): 82
  • Origin: North America.
  • Key Characteristics: Largest domestic fowl, known for lean meat, slower growth than chicken.
  • Economic Importance: Meat (especially for festive occasions), Feathers.

3.4. Quail (Coturnix)

  • Scientific Name: Coturnix coturnix japonica (Japanese quail)
  • Chromosome Number (2n): 78
  • Origin: Asia.
  • Key Characteristics: Smallest domestic fowl, rapid growth, early maturity, high egg production, low space requirement.
  • Economic Importance: Eggs (small, spotted), Meat (delicacy).

4. Other Economically Important Animals

Beyond major livestock and poultry, several other species contribute significantly to agriculture and rural livelihoods.

4.1. Camel (Camelidae)

  • Scientific Name: Camelus dromedarius (Dromedary/One-humped), Camelus bactrianus (Bactrian/Two-humped)
  • Chromosome Number (2n): 74
  • Origin: North Africa, Middle East, Central Asia.
  • Key Characteristics: Adapted to arid and semi-arid regions, known for endurance and ability to withstand water scarcity.
  • Economic Importance: Draft power (transport, ploughing), Milk, Meat, Wool/Hair.

4.2. Horse (Equine)

  • Scientific Name: Equus caballus
  • Chromosome Number (2n): 64
  • Origin: North America, Eurasia.
  • Key Characteristics: Non-ruminant herbivore, known for speed, strength, and intelligence.
  • Economic Importance: Draft power, Transport, Sports, Recreation, Meat (limited in India).

4.3. Rabbit (Lagomorpha)

  • Scientific Name: Oryctolagus cuniculus
  • Chromosome Number (2n): 44
  • Origin: Europe.
  • Key Characteristics: Herbivore, high prolificacy, rapid growth, efficient feed conversion, low space requirement.
  • Economic Importance: Meat, Fur, Wool (Angora), Laboratory animals.

4.4. Fish (Aquaculture)

  • Scientific Name: Varies widely (e.g., Labeo rohita for Rohu, Catla catla for Catla)
  • Origin: Aquatic environments globally.
  • Key Characteristics: Cold-blooded vertebrates, diverse species adapted to fresh, brackish, or marine waters.
  • Economic Importance: Primary source of protein, Omega-3 fatty acids, ornamental trade, livelihood for fishing communities.

5. Domestication, Breed Development, and Genetic Resources

Domestication is the process by which humans selectively breed animals for specific traits, leading to distinct breeds. A breed is a group of animals within a species that have been selected by humans for specific characteristics and are maintained by controlled breeding.

  • Domestication: Began thousands of years ago, transforming wild species into livestock and poultry suitable for human needs.
  • Breed Development: Involves selection for traits like high milk yield, rapid growth, disease resistance, specific wool quality, or draft power.
  • Indigenous Breeds: Often well-adapted to local agro-climatic conditions, disease-resistant, and require less intensive management (e.g., Sahiwal cattle, Murrah buffalo, Nali sheep, Jamunapari goat).
  • Exotic Breeds: Known for high production potential under intensive management (e.g., Holstein Friesian cattle, Large White Yorkshire pig, White Leghorn chicken).
  • Crossbreeding: Mating animals of different breeds to combine desirable traits (hybrid vigor or heterosis).
  • Conservation of Animal Genetic Resources: Essential to preserve biodiversity, adapt to future challenges, and maintain genetic variability. Methods include in situ (on-farm) and ex situ (gene banks, cryopreservation) conservation.

6. Economic Significance and Contribution to Agriculture

Livestock and poultry play a multifaceted role in the agricultural economy, particularly in India.

  • Food Security: Provide essential protein (milk, meat, eggs) and fats.
  • Income Generation: Primary source of livelihood for millions of small and marginal farmers.
  • Draft Power: Crucial for ploughing, carting, and other farm operations in many regions.
  • Manure: Organic fertilizer, improving soil fertility and structure, and a source for biogas production.
  • By-products: Leather, wool, hair, bones, bristles, and pharmaceutical products.
  • Weed and Pest Control: Ducks and geese can control weeds and pests in certain cropping systems.
  • Risk Mitigation: Act as a living bank, providing financial security during crop failures or emergencies.
  • Employment: Creates employment opportunities in farming, processing, marketing, and veterinary services.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the chromosome numbers of different species: While cattle and goats both have 2n=60, sheep have 2n=54, and pigs have 2n=38. Memorize these specific numbers.
  • Misidentifying the origin of major livestock: Bos indicus is from South Asia, Bos taurus from Europe/Middle East, and domestic chicken from Southeast Asia.
  • Incorrectly classifying digestive systems: Pigs are monogastric (non-ruminant), while cattle, buffalo, sheep, and goats are ruminants. Horses are non-ruminant herbivores with a large cecum.
  • Attributing high fat milk to cows: Buffalo milk consistently has a higher fat percentage than cow milk, making it preferred for certain dairy products.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Species Of Animals And Birds: Classification, Characteristics, and Economic Significance

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Taxonomic Hierarchy and Scientific Nomenclature

The classification of animals follows a hierarchical system from Kingdom to Species. Each level groups organisms based on shared evolutionary traits. The scientific name, a binomial nomenclature, consists of the genus and species, providing a universal and unambiguous identity for each animal. For example, Bos indicus specifically refers to Zebu cattle, distinguishing it from Bos taurus (Taurine cattle).

  • Binomial Nomenclature: A two-part naming system (Genus species) used to identify organisms, always italicized.
  • Species: The most specific taxonomic rank, representing a group of organisms capable of interbreeding and producing fertile offspring.
Concept 2

Major Livestock Species: Characteristics and Chromosome Numbers

Key livestock species like cattle, buffalo, sheep, goats, and pigs form the backbone of animal agriculture. Each species possesses unique characteristics, including specific chromosome numbers, digestive systems (ruminant vs. monogastric), and adaptations to various environments. For instance, cattle (Bos indicus/Bos taurus) and goats (Capra hircus) share a chromosome number of 2n=60, while sheep (Ovis aries) have 2n=54, and pigs (Sus scrofa domesticus) have 2n=38. These genetic differences underpin their distinct physiological and production traits.

  • Ruminant: Animals with a four-compartment stomach (rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum) capable of digesting fibrous plant material (e.g., cattle, sheep, goats, buffalo).
  • Monogastric: Animals with a single-chambered stomach (e.g., pigs, poultry, horses).
Concept 3

Poultry Species: Production Traits and Economic Value

Poultry, primarily chickens, ducks, turkeys, and quails, are vital for egg and meat production. Chickens (Gallus gallus domesticus) are the most common, known for rapid growth and high egg laying. Ducks (Anas platyrhynchos domesticus) are hardy and provide fatty meat and large eggs. Turkeys (Meleagris gallopavo) are valued for lean meat, and quails (Coturnix coturnix japonica) for their small size, quick maturity, and high prolificacy. Each species contributes uniquely to dietary protein and farmer income.

  • Broiler: Chicken breeds specifically selected and reared for meat production, characterized by rapid growth.
  • Layer: Chicken breeds specifically selected for high egg production.
Concept 4

Domestication, Breed Development, and Genetic Conservation

Domestication is the long-term process of selective breeding by humans to adapt animals for specific purposes, leading to the development of distinct breeds. A breed is a subgroup within a species with common characteristics, maintained through controlled breeding. Indigenous breeds are often well-adapted to local conditions, while exotic breeds offer high production potential. Crossbreeding combines desirable traits from different breeds. Conservation of animal genetic resources, through in situ and ex situ methods, is critical for maintaining biodiversity and ensuring future adaptability.

  • Breed: A group of animals within a species that share common characteristics and are maintained through selective breeding.
  • Crossbreeding: Mating of animals from different breeds to produce offspring with improved traits (heterosis).
Concept 5

Economic and Socio-Cultural Role of Animals in Agriculture

Animals and birds are integral to the agricultural economy, particularly in developing countries like India. They provide essential food products (milk, meat, eggs), draft power for farm operations, organic manure for soil fertility, and valuable by-products like leather and wool. Beyond direct economic contributions, livestock act as a living bank, offering financial security, and play significant socio-cultural roles in rural communities. Their contribution extends to employment generation across the value chain.

  • Draft Power: The use of animals (e.g., bullocks, buffaloes, camels, horses) for farm operations like ploughing, carting, and irrigation.
  • Manure: Animal excreta used as organic fertilizer, rich in nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium, improving soil health.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Binomial Nomenclature (Scientific Naming)
A two-part naming system (Genus species) used to identify organisms, providing a universal and unambiguous name.
Ruminant (Ruminant Digestive System)
Mammals with a four-compartment stomach (rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum) adapted for digesting fibrous plant material through microbial fermentation.
Monogastric (Simple Stomach)
Animals possessing a single-chambered stomach, unable to digest large amounts of fibrous feed efficiently (e.g., pigs, poultry).
Zebu Cattle (Bos indicus)
Humped cattle breeds, primarily found in tropical regions, known for heat tolerance and disease resistance.
Taurine Cattle (Bos taurus)
Humpless cattle breeds, primarily found in temperate regions, known for high milk and meat production.
Carabeef (Buffalo Meat)
Meat derived from domestic water buffalo, known for its leanness and often darker color.
Chevon (Goat Meat)
Meat derived from domestic goats, popular for its distinct flavor and leanness.
Prolificacy (Reproductive Rate)
The ability of an animal to produce a large number of offspring, often measured by litter size or number of eggs laid.
Draft Animal (Work Animal)
Animals used for pulling loads, ploughing fields, or other farm operations requiring physical strength.
Crossbreeding (Hybridization)
The mating of animals from different breeds to combine desirable traits and often achieve hybrid vigor (heterosis).
Indigenous Breeds (Native Breeds)
Animal breeds that originated and evolved in a specific geographical region, adapted to local environmental conditions.
Exotic Breeds (Foreign Breeds)
Animal breeds introduced from other geographical regions, often for their high production potential under intensive management.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Bos indicus VS Bos taurus

Bos indicus refers to humped cattle (Zebu), adapted to tropical climates, while Bos taurus refers to humpless cattle (Taurine), adapted to temperate climates.

River Buffalo VS Swamp Buffalo

River buffalo (e.g., Murrah) have 2n=50 chromosomes, are primarily dairy animals, and prefer deep water. Swamp buffalo (e.g., Carabao) have 2n=48 chromosomes, are mainly draft animals, and prefer wallowing in shallow muddy water.

Broiler VS Layer

Broilers are chicken breeds specifically selected for rapid meat production, while layers are breeds selected for high egg production.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following domestic animals is characterized by a chromosome number (2n) of 54?

Explanation: Sheep (Ovis aries) have a diploid chromosome number of 2n=54. Cattle (Bos indicus/Bos taurus) and goats (Capra hircus) both have 2n=60, while pigs (Sus scrofa domesticus) have 2n=38.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The scientific name Bubalus bubalis refers to which domestic animal?

Explanation: Bubalus bubalis is the scientific name for the domestic water buffalo. Domestic cattle are Bos indicus or Bos taurus, yak is Bos grunniens, and camel is Camelus dromedarius or Camelus bactrianus.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following poultry species has the highest chromosome number (2n)?

Explanation: Turkey (Meleagris gallopavo) has 2n=82 chromosomes, which is the highest among the given options. Chicken and quail have 2n=78, and duck has 2n=80.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The term 'chevon' is used to refer to the meat of which animal?

Explanation: 'Chevon' is the culinary term for goat meat. Beef is from cattle, carabeef from buffalo, and mutton/lamb from sheep.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a monogastric animal?

Explanation: Pigs (Sus scrofa domesticus) are monogastric animals, meaning they have a single-chambered stomach. Goats, cattle, and sheep are all ruminants with a four-compartment stomach.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Bos indicus cattle are primarily characterized by:

Explanation: Bos indicus, or Zebu cattle, are characterized by a prominent hump, a large dewlap, and are well-adapted to tropical and subtropical climates due to their heat tolerance.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is an example of an 'exotic' cattle breed known for high milk production?

Explanation: Holstein Friesian is an exotic breed originating from the Netherlands, renowned globally for its exceptionally high milk production. Sahiwal, Gir, and Red Sindhi are indigenous Indian dairy breeds.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The primary product for which Angora rabbits are reared is:

Explanation: Angora rabbits are primarily reared for their long, fine wool, which is highly valued in the textile industry. While rabbits can be used for meat and research, Angora is specific for wool.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which domestic animal is often referred to as the 'poor man's cow' due to its low input requirements and adaptability?

Explanation: Goats are often called the 'poor man's cow' because they are hardy, adaptable, require less space and feed compared to cattle, and can thrive on marginal lands, providing milk, meat, and fiber.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the diploid chromosome number (2n) for domestic pigs (Sus scrofa domesticus)?

Explanation: The domestic pig (Sus scrofa domesticus) has a diploid chromosome number of 2n=38.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a characteristic feature of River Buffalo (e.g., Murrah) compared to Swamp Buffalo?

Explanation: River buffalo, such as Murrah, are primarily dairy animals and have 2n=50 chromosomes. Swamp buffalo have 2n=48 chromosomes and are mainly used for draft power, preferring shallow muddy wallows.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The process of selective breeding by humans to adapt animals for specific purposes is known as:

Explanation: Domestication is the long-term process by which humans selectively breed animals for desired traits, leading to their adaptation to human environments and needs. Hybridization is crossbreeding, genetic engineering is direct gene manipulation, and natural selection occurs in wild populations.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which poultry species is known for its high resistance to common poultry diseases and good foraging ability?

Explanation: Ducks are generally more hardy and resistant to common poultry diseases than chickens, and they are excellent foragers, making them suitable for extensive and semi-intensive systems.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary economic importance of fish in aquaculture?

Explanation: Fish in aquaculture are primarily cultivated as a vital source of protein and essential nutrients for human consumption, contributing significantly to food security.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The scientific name Gallus gallus domesticus refers to:

Explanation: Gallus gallus domesticus is the scientific name for the domestic chicken. Ducks are Anas platyrhynchos domesticus, and turkeys are Meleagris gallopavo.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is NOT a ruminant animal?

Explanation: Horses are non-ruminant herbivores, possessing a simple stomach and a large cecum for hindgut fermentation. Cattle, buffalo, and goats are all ruminants.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chromosome number (2n) for domestic ducks (Anas platyrhynchos domesticus)?

Explanation: Domestic ducks (Anas platyrhynchos domesticus) have a diploid chromosome number of 2n=80.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which animal by-product is commonly used as an organic fertilizer and for biogas production?

Explanation: Animal manure is widely used as an organic fertilizer to enrich soil fertility and is also a primary feedstock for biogas plants, producing renewable energy.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

A breed is defined as a group of animals within a species that:

Explanation: A breed is a specific group of animals within a species that has been developed and maintained by humans through selective breeding for particular traits, distinguishing them from other groups within the same species.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is an indigenous Indian dairy cattle breed known for its heat tolerance and disease resistance?

Explanation: Sahiwal is a prominent indigenous Indian dairy cattle breed, highly valued for its good milk yield, heat tolerance, and resistance to various tropical diseases. Jersey, Holstein Friesian, and Brown Swiss are exotic breeds.

Identify the correct match between the animal and its diploid chromosome number (2n):

Solution & Rationale: The correct match is Pig - 38. Cattle and goats have 2n=60, while sheep have 2n=54. River buffalo have 2n=50.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following statements about buffalo milk is correct?

Solution & Rationale: Buffalo milk is renowned for its significantly higher fat content (typically 7-8%) compared to cow milk (3.5-5%), making it preferred for products like paneer and khoa. River buffalo are the primary dairy type. It is highly suitable for cheese making.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

The scientific name Oryctolagus cuniculus refers to an animal primarily reared for:

Solution & Rationale: Oryctolagus cuniculus is the scientific name for the domestic rabbit. Rabbits are primarily reared for their meat, and certain breeds like Angora are specifically for wool, while others for fur.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following is a characteristic that distinguishes Bos indicus from Bos taurus cattle?

Solution & Rationale: Bos indicus (Zebu cattle) are characterized by a prominent hump, a large dewlap, and superior adaptation to tropical climates, including heat tolerance and disease resistance. Bos taurus (Taurine cattle) are humpless, generally have higher milk production potential, and are adapted to temperate climates.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

In poultry farming, 'layers' are primarily raised for:

Solution & Rationale: Layers are specific breeds of chickens (or other poultry) that have been selectively bred and are managed primarily for high egg production. Broilers are raised for meat.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following statements accurately describes the digestive system of a horse?

Solution & Rationale: Horses are non-ruminant herbivores. They have a simple, single-chambered stomach but possess a very large and functional cecum where microbial fermentation of fibrous feed occurs (hindgut fermentation).

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

The conservation strategy that involves maintaining animal populations in their natural habitats or on farms is known as:

Solution & Rationale: In situ conservation refers to the conservation of species in their natural habitats or on-farm, allowing them to continue evolving under natural selection pressures. Ex situ conservation involves preserving genetic material outside the natural habitat, such as in gene banks or through cryopreservation.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following poultry species is known for its rapid growth, early maturity, and high prolificacy, making it suitable for small-scale commercial farming?

Solution & Rationale: Japanese quail (Coturnix coturnix japonica) are highly valued for their rapid growth rate, early sexual maturity (around 6-7 weeks), and high egg-laying capacity, requiring minimal space, which makes them ideal for small-scale and backyard farming.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary contribution of livestock to soil fertility in traditional agricultural systems?

Solution & Rationale: Livestock primarily contribute to soil fertility through the production of organic manure (dung and urine), which enriches the soil with essential nutrients (N, P, K) and improves its physical structure and water-holding capacity.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

A farmer wants to crossbreed his indigenous cattle with an exotic breed to improve milk yield while retaining some disease resistance. This practice is an example of:

Solution & Rationale: Crossbreeding is the mating of animals from different breeds to combine desirable traits from both parents, such as the high milk yield of an exotic breed and the disease resistance of an indigenous breed. Inbreeding and linebreeding involve mating related individuals, while grading up is a specific form of crossbreeding to replace an indigenous breed with an exotic one over generations.
