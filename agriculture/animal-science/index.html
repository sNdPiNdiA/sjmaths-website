Introduction to Livestock Production and Dairy Science

Animal husbandry forms the backbone of mixed farming systems in India, contributing substantially to agricultural GDP, rural employment, and nutritional security. Livestock production encompasses breeding, feeding, management, and health care of domesticated animals such as cattle, buffaloes, sheep, goats, pigs, and poultry.

  • Significance: Provides year-round employment, organic manure, and draft power alongside milk, meat, and fiber.
  • Dairying Industry: India ranks first globally in milk production, largely driven by indigenous and crossbred cattle and elite buffalo breeds.
  • Animal Genetics: Selection and breeding methodologies aim to optimize productivity traits such as lactation yield, growth rate, and disease resistance.

Classification and Characteristics of Cattle and Buffalo Breeds

Bovine classification in the Indian subcontinent is categorized based on utility and geographic origin into dairy, draught, dual-purpose, and exotic breeds. Buffaloes belong to the genus Bubalus bubalis and are categorized into riverine and swamp types.

  • Milch Breeds: High milk yielders with docile temperament; e.g., Sahiwal, Red Sindhi, Gir, and Deoni among cattle; Murrah and Nili-Ravi among buffaloes.
  • Draught Breeds: Bullocks possess robust physical stamina for agricultural operations; e.g., Kangayam, Amritmahal, Malvi, and Khillari.
  • Dual-Purpose Breeds: Moderate milk production and efficient draught capacity; e.g., Hariana, Tharparkar, and Kankrej.
  • Exotic Dairy Breeds: High genetic potential for milk production introduced for crossbreeding programs; e.g., Bos taurus species like Holstein-Friesian, Jersey, and Brown Swiss.

Principles of Animal Nutrition and Ration Formulation

Scientific feeding is critical for expressing the genetic potential of livestock. Animal feed is broadly categorized into roughages (green and dry) and concentrates (grains, oilcakes, agro-industrial byproducts).

  • Nutrients: Water, carbohydrates, proteins, fats, minerals, and vitamins are essential for maintenance, growth, reproduction, and lactation.
  • Roughages: High in crude fiber (>18%) and low in total digestible nutrients (TDN); e.g., berseem, lucerne, napier grass, and paddy straw.
  • Concentrates: Low in fiber (<18%) and high in energy and proteins; e.g., mustard cake, groundnut cake, wheat bran, and maize grain.
  • Balanced Ration: A feed formulation that provides all essential nutrients in appropriate proportions and quantities over a 24-hour period.

Dairy Technology, Milk Hygiene, and Processing

Milk is an exceptionally nutritious food containing proteins (casein), lactose, lipids, minerals, and vitamins. Hygienic milk production and processing prevent spoilage and pathogen transmission.

  • Composition: Normal cow milk contains approximately 87.5% water and 12.5% total solids (SNF and fat). Buffalo milk typically has higher fat content (~6.5-8.0%).
  • Pasteurization: Thermal processing to destroy pathogenic microorganisms without altering nutritional quality significantly. Methods include Low-Temperature Long-Time (LTLT: 63°C for 30 minutes) and High-Temperature Short-Time (HTST: 72°C for 15 seconds).
  • Quality Control: Lactometer reading (specific gravity), Gerber test (fat estimation), and clot-on-boiling test ensure milk purity and safety.
  • By-products: Cream, butter, ghee, paneer, skim milk, and khoa are manufactured through standard dairy unit operations.

Veterinary Health Care, Diseases, and Biosecurity

Livestock health management focuses on disease prevention, vaccination protocols, parasite control, and immediate therapeutic interventions.

  • Viral Diseases: Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD) caused by aphthovirus, Rinderpest (eradicated), and Pox infections characterized by vesicular lesions, fever, and drop in milk yield.
  • Bacterial Diseases: Hemorrhagic Septicemia (HS) caused by Pasteurella multocida, Black Quarter (BQ) caused by Clostridium chauvoei, and Anthrax caused by Bacillus anthracis.
  • Parasitic Infestations: Endoparasites (flukes, roundworms) and ectoparasites (ticks, mites, lice) controlled via regular deworming and acaricide dips.
  • Biosecurity: Strict quarantine measures, isolation of sick animals, and regular disinfection of barns and equipment.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • India ranks 1st in global milk production, largely contributed by buffaloes and cattle.
  • Murrah is recognized as the queen of Indian buffalo breeds, native to Rohtak and Jind districts of Haryana.
  • The gestation period in cattle is approximately 280-285 days (9 months and 9 days).
  • The gestation period in buffaloes is longer, averaging 310 days.
  • Pasteurization of milk by HTST method involves heating at 72°C for 15 seconds followed by rapid cooling.
  • Colostrum is the first milk secreted after parturition; it is rich in immunoglobulins and provides passive immunity to the newborn.
  • The scientific name of Indian humped cattle is Bos indicus, while exotic cattle belong to Bos taurus.
  • Bhanur and Karn Swiss are notable synthetic dairy cattle strains developed at NDRI Karnal.
  • Rinderpest, a viral disease of cattle, was officially declared eradicated globally in 2011.
  • The Gerber method is standard for estimating fat percentage in milk samples.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the origin of Jersey cattle (United Kingdom/Jersey Island) with Holstein-Friesian (Netherlands).
  • Assuming buffalo milk has lower fat content than cow milk, whereas buffalo milk typically contains significantly higher fat (~7%).
  • Mistaking Black Quarter for a viral infection when it is actually caused by the bacterium Clostridium chauvoei.

Concept 1

Taxonomic Classification of Farm Animals

Understanding the zoological hierarchy of farm livestock is essential for breed characterization and evolutionary studies.

  • Bos indicus: Humped cattle indigenous to tropical regions, possessing high heat tolerance.
  • Bubalus bubalis: Water buffalo species domesticated extensively in South Asia for milk and draught power.
Concept 2

Reproductive Physiology in Bovines

Managing reproduction parameters efficiently ensures optimal herd productivity and generational turnover.

  • Estrus Cycle: The recurring physiological cycle in cattle lasting approximately 21 days with an estrus duration of 12-24 hours.
  • Artificial Insemination (AI): Technique of depositing semen into the female reproductive tract using specialized instrumentation for genetic improvement.
Concept 3

Milk Composition and Physio-Chemical Properties

Milk quality parameters govern processing standards and commercial pricing structures.

  • SNF (Solids-Not-Fat): Comprises proteins, lactose, minerals, and vitamins, standardly maintained above 8.5% in cow milk.
  • Specific Gravity: Normal cow milk specific gravity ranges between 1.028 and 1.032 at 15°C.
Concept 4

Veterinary Prophylaxis and Vaccination

Preventive veterinary medicine protects livestock populations from epidemic outbreaks and production losses.

  • Ring Vaccination: Vaccination strategy applied in concentric circles around an outbreak epicenter to contain viral spread.
  • Acaricides: Chemical agents utilized for controlling tick and mite infestations on livestock hide and housing structures.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Colostrum (First lacteal secretion)
Nutrient-rich first milk containing high levels of immunoglobulins essential for newborn immunity.
Lactation Period (Milking phase)
The duration spanning from calving to the cessation of milk production, ideally lasting 305 days.
Dry Period (Non-lactating interval)
The resting phase between consecutive lactations necessary for mammary gland regeneration and fetal growth.
Ruminant (Polygastric herbivore)
An animal with a four-chambered stomach (rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum) capable of microbial fiber fermentation.
Flushing (Nutritional management)
Providing high-energy, high-protein feed to breeding females prior to mating to increase ovulation rates.
Freemartin (Intersex heifer)
A sterile female calf born twin to a male, resulting from vascular anastomosis and hormonal transfer in utero.
Culling (Herd sanitization)
The removal of unproductive, diseased, or aged animals from the herd to maintain profitability.
Homogenization (Mechanical fat dispersion)
A high-pressure process that reduces fat globule size in milk to prevent cream layer separation.
SNF (Solids-Not-Fat)
Total milk solids excluding fat, comprising casein, whey proteins, lactose, and ash.
Zoonosis (Cross-species infection)
Diseases communicable between animals and humans, such as brucellosis, anthrax, and tuberculosis.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Bos indicus VS Bos taurus

Bos indicus represents humped tropical cattle, whereas Bos taurus represents humpless temperate exotic cattle.

Rumen VS Abomasum

Rumen is the fermentation vat housing symbiotic microbes, while abomasum is the true glandular stomach secreting digestive enzymes.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is considered the best milch breed of cattle in India, native to Punjab and Pakistan?

Explanation: Sahiwal is the premier indigenous dairy breed of cattle, originating in the Montgomery district (now in Pakistan) and well-adapted to Indian climatic conditions.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the average gestation period of a buffalo?

Explanation: The average gestation period in buffaloes is approximately 310 days (ranging from 300 to 320 days), compared to 280 days in cattle.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which viral disease affects cloven-hoofed animals and is characterized by excessive salivation and blisters on feet and tongue?

Explanation: Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD) is an acute viral disease affecting cloven-hoofed animals, marked by fever, vesicular lesions in the mouth and interdigital spaces, and severe lameness.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard time-temperature combination for High-Temperature Short-Time (HTST) milk pasteurization?

Explanation: HTST pasteurization involves heating milk to at least 72°C for 15 seconds, effectively destroying pathogens while preserving sensory and nutritional attributes.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Black Quarter in cattle is caused by which of the following pathogens?

Explanation: Black Quarter (BQ) is an acute infectious bacterial disease caused by Clostridium chauvoei, characterized by gas accumulation and muscle necrosis in hindquarters.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which indigenous buffalo breed is famous for its tightly curved horns and highest milk production potential?

Explanation: Murrah is the premier buffalo breed of India, native to Haryana, renowned for its jet-black coat, tightly curled horns, and exceptional dairy performance.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The first milk secreted by the mother immediately after calving is called:

Explanation: Colostrum is the initial lacteal secretion packed with antibodies (immunoglobulins) that provide passive immunity to newborn calves against various infections.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the scientific name of humped Indian cattle?

Explanation: Indian zebu cattle belong to the species Bos indicus, characterized by a prominent hump over the shoulder, loose skin, and high heat tolerance.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Hemorrhagic Septicemia (HS) in cattle is primarily caused by:

Explanation: Hemorrhagic Septicemia is an acute, fatal septicemic disease caused by Pasteurella multocida, frequently occurring during rainy and humid seasons.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a dual-purpose cattle breed in India?

Explanation: Hariana is a prominent dual-purpose breed where cows are fair milkers and bullocks are efficient for agricultural draft operations.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The process of removing unproductive or diseased animals from a livestock herd is known as:

Explanation: Culling is the systematic elimination of low-yielding, sterile, or diseased animals from the herd to maintain overall economic viability.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which instrument is used to determine the specific gravity of milk for detecting water adulteration?

Explanation: A lactometer is used to measure the specific gravity of milk, helping identify adulteration such as watering or skimming.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which exotic dairy cattle breed holds the record for highest absolute volume of milk production globally?

Explanation: Holstein-Friesian (HF) is the highest milk-producing dairy breed globally, originating from the Netherlands and North Germany.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the normal body temperature of cattle in Fahrenheit?

Explanation: The normal rectal temperature of healthy adult cattle is approximately 101.5°F (38.6°C).

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which acid is predominantly formed during normal souring and fermentation of milk by lactic acid bacteria?

Explanation: Lactic acid is produced when bacteria ferment lactose in milk, leading to curdling and souring when pH drops.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The practice of feeding extra concentrates to pregnant animals during the last phase of gestation is called:

Explanation: Steaming up involves feeding pregnant dams additional concentrates 4 to 6 weeks before calving to support fetal growth and milk secretion.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following diseases is a major zoonotic bacterial infection transmitted from cattle to humans?

Explanation: Brucellosis (contagious abortion) is caused by Brucella abortus and is a significant zoonotic disease transmissible to humans through unpasteurized milk and handling.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the average duration of the estrus cycle in cattle?

Explanation: The normal estrus cycle in cows and heifers lasts approximately 21 days, with heat duration spanning 12 to 24 hours.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which vitamin is synthesized in adequate quantities by rumen microflora in ruminants?

Explanation: Rumen microflora synthesize all B-complex vitamins and vitamin K in sufficient quantities to meet the metabolic requirements of adult ruminants.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The Gerber test is universally employed in dairy plants for the estimation of:

Explanation: The Gerber method is a rapid and accurate volumetric chemical test used to determine the fat content in milk and milk products.

In dairy herd management, what is the primary purpose of 'Steaming Up'?

Solution & Rationale: Steaming up provides supplementary nutrition during late gestation to build body reserves and ensure high milk yield post-calving.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following cattle breeds is an exotic dual-purpose or dairy breed known for highest butterfat percentage in milk?

Solution & Rationale: Jersey cattle produce milk with the highest fat percentage among major exotic dairy breeds, averaging around 4.5% to 5.0%.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

Which micro-organism is the causative agent of Anthrax in livestock?

Solution & Rationale: Anthrax is a peracute, fatal bacterial disease of livestock caused by the spore-forming bacterium Bacillus anthracis.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary objective of keeping the dry period around 60 days in dairy cows?

Solution & Rationale: A 60-day dry period is essential for resting the udder tissue, restoring secretory cells, and ensuring optimal birth weight of the subsequent calf.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

Which chemical reagent is used along with sulfuric acid in the Gerber test for milk fat estimation?

Solution & Rationale: Amyl alcohol is added in the Gerber test to facilitate clean separation of the fat column from the digested milk mixture.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What does the term 'Freemartin' denote in veterinary science?

Solution & Rationale: A freemartin is an infertile female bovine born as a twin to a male, experiencing hormonal and cellular interchange in utero that causes reproductive tract malformation.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which buffalo breed is indigenous to the Kutch region of Gujarat and is characterized by heavy body and spiral horns?

Solution & Rationale: Jaffarabadi is a massive dairy buffalo breed native to the Gir forest region of Gujarat, known for high milk and fat yield.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the recommended temperature and holding time for Low-Temperature Long-Time (LTLT) pasteurization?

Solution & Rationale: LTLT pasteurization (batch method) requires holding milk at a minimum temperature of 63°C for 30 minutes.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following is a symptom of Rinderpest (cattle plague), now eradicated globally?

Solution & Rationale: Rinderpest was a highly contagious viral disease marked by fever, necrotic lesions in the mucous membranes of the digestive tract, and severe diarrhea leading to death.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the function of the omasum in the ruminant stomach?

Solution & Rationale: The omasum acts as a pump filtering ingesta, absorbing water and volatile fatty acids, and reducing particle size before reaching the abomasum.
