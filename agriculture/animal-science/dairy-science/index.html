1. Introduction to Dairy Science and Milk Composition

Dairy science is a specialized branch of agriculture and animal husbandry that deals with the production, processing, preservation, and marketing of milk and milk products. Milk is defined legally and scientifically as the normal, clean, unadulterated mammary secretion obtained from the complete milking of healthy milch animals, devoid of colostrum, and containing prescribed minimum percentages of fat and solids-not-fat (SNF).

  • General Composition of Cow Milk: Water (87.2%), Fat (3.9%), Protein (3.4%), Lactose (4.9%), and Ash/Minerals (0.7%).
  • Buffalo Milk Composition: Water (84.2%), Fat (6.5%), Protein (3.9%), Lactose (5.2%), and Ash (0.8%). Buffalo milk possesses higher fat, protein, and mineral content, making it exceptionally suited for indigenous dairy products like khoa, paneer, and ghee.
  • Colostrum: The first milk secreted by the mammary glands after parturition. It is rich in immunoglobulins (especially IgG), vitamins A and D, and possesses high total solids and protein content to confer passive immunity to the newborn calf.

2. Physico-Chemical Properties of Milk

Understanding the physical and chemical characteristics of milk is essential for quality control, processing design, and detection of adulteration.

  • Specific Gravity: Cow milk ranges from 1.028 to 1.030 at 15°C; buffalo milk ranges from 1.030 to 1.032. Adulteration with water lowers specific gravity, whereas skimming increases it.
  • pH and Acidity: Fresh normal milk is amphoteric and slightly acidic, with a pH ranging from 6.4 to 6.6. Titratable acidity of fresh milk ranges from 0.13% to 0.15% lactic acid.
  • Freezing Point: Normal milk freezes at -0.522°C to -0.565°C. This parameter is the most constant physical property used to detect added water.
  • Refractive Index: Milk serum has a characteristic refractive index used for rapid screening of added water via cryoscopic methods.

3. Processing Operations: Pasteurization, Homogenization, and Standardization

Raw milk must undergo several processing steps to ensure safety, extend shelf life, and meet commercial legal standards.

  • Pasteurization: A thermal process designed to destroy all pathogenic microorganisms without significantly altering nutritional value or creaming properties.
    • Low Temperature Long Time (LTLT) / Batch Method: Heating milk to 63°C for 30 minutes.
    • High Temperature Short Time (HTST) / Continuous Method: Heating milk to 72°C for 15 seconds followed by rapid cooling below 4°C.
  • Homogenization: A mechanical process applied under high pressure (2000–2500 psi) to reduce fat globule size from 4–10 micrometers to below 2 micrometers, thereby preventing cream separation during storage.
  • Standardization: The adjustment of fat and SNF percentages to legal or desired commercial standards using cream or skim milk. Pearson's Square method is widely applied for numerical calculations.

4. Microbiology of Milk and Quality Testing

Milk is an ideal culture medium for various microorganisms due to its high moisture, neutral pH, and rich nutrient profile. Pathogenic and spoilage bacteria significantly impact shelf life and human health.

  • Major Microorganisms: Lactic acid bacteria (Lactococcus lactis, Lactobacillus spp.), coliforms (Escherichia coli), psychrotrophs (Pseudomonas spp.), and thermoduric spore-formers (Bacillus spp., Clostridium spp.).
  • Methylene Blue Reduction Test (MBRT): Measures the microbiological quality of milk based on the time taken for bacteria to exhaust dissolved oxygen and decolorize methylene blue dye. Shorter reduction time indicates heavy bacterial contamination.
  • Clot-on-Boiling (COB) Test: Rapid field test to detect high acid production and microbial spoilage; milk coagulates upon boiling if titratable acidity exceeds 0.20%.

5. Indigenous and Confectionery Milk Products

In India, over 50% of total milk produced is converted into traditional indigenous dairy products.

  • Khoa: Partially dehydrated whole milk product prepared by continuous boiling and evaporation in an open pan (karahi). Used extensively in confectionery items like burfi and gulabjamun.
  • Paneer: Acid-coagulated indigenous cheese obtained by adding food-grade organic acids (citric acid or lemon juice) to hot milk (70–85°C) followed by pressing and whey drainage.
  • Ghee: Clarified butterfat obtained by thermal evaporation of moisture from cream, butter, or malai at 110–120°C, possessing exceptional keeping quality.
  • Chhana: Unripened, soft, acid-coagulated cheese used as the base for popular sweets like rasgulla and sandesh.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the specific gravity range: Cow milk specific gravity is slightly lower (1.028-1.030) than buffalo milk (1.030-1.032) due to lower fat and solid content.
  • Assuming boiling destroys all bacterial spores; pasteurization destroys vegetative pathogens, whereas spore formers like Bacillus survive.
  • Thinking yellow color of buffalo milk is due to carotene; buffalo milk lacks carotene entirely as it converts carotene to vitamin A completely, rendering it stark white.
  • Mistaking MBRT time: Longer decolorization time means better quality milk, whereas shorter time indicates high bacterial load.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Dairy Science: Principles of Milk Production, Processing, and Quality Control

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Milk Definition and Gross Composition

Milk is the complete mammary secretion free of colostrum. Its constituents are divided into water and total solids (TS).

  • Total Solids: Cow milk contains ~12.8% TS, while buffalo milk contains ~15.8% TS.
  • SNF (Solids-Not-Fat): Calculated as Total Solids minus Fat percentage.
Concept 2

Colostrum vs. Normal Milk

Colostrum represents transitional secretion immediately following parturition.

  • Globulins: Extremely high concentration of immunoglobulins conferring disease resistance.
  • Acidity: Higher initial titratable acidity due to elevated protein and phosphate levels.
Concept 3

Thermal Processing and Enzyme Inactivation

Thermal processing safeguards public health by eliminating foodborne pathogens.

  • Phosphatase Test: A negative test confirms proper pasteurization, as alkaline phosphatase is denatured slightly above pasteurization temperatures.
  • Peroxidase Test: Used to verify HTST pasteurization versus higher sterilization temperatures.
Concept 4

Colligative Properties of Milk

Colligative properties depend on dissolved molecular and ionic species.

  • Cryoscopy: Freezing point depression is the most reliable index for detecting added water because lactose and chloride concentrations remain remarkably constant across healthy dairy animals.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Colostrum (Bovine Mammary Secretion (Post-Partum))
The nutrient-rich, antibody-dense first milk produced by mammals immediately following parturition.
Homogenization (Mechanical Fat Globule Reduction)
A high-pressure process that reduces fat globule size to <2 µm to prevent cream separation.
Pasteurization (Thermal Pathogen Inactivation)
Heating milk to specific temperatures for designated times to destroy pathogenic bacteria without boiling.
SNF (Solids-Not-Fat)
The total dry matter in milk excluding milk fat, comprising proteins, lactose, and minerals.
Casein (Principal Milk Protein)
The primary phosphoprotein of milk representing about 80% of total protein content, precipitating at pH 4.6.
Lactose (Milk Sugar (C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}))
A disaccharide composed of glucose and galactose unique to mammalian milk.
MBRT (Methylene Blue Reduction Test)
A dye reduction test used to estimate microbiological activity and bacterial load in raw milk.
Khoa (Desiccated Indigenous Milk Product)
Partially dehydrated whole milk product prepared by boiling in an open pan until solid mass forms.
Cryoscopy (Freezing Point Determination)
Analytical method measuring freezing point depression to detect adulteration of milk with water.
Rancidity (Lipid Oxidation / Hydrolysis)
Off-flavor development in milk and dairy products resulting from microbial or enzymatic breakdown of fats.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Casein constitutes ~80% of total milk protein.
  • Normal pH of fresh milk is 6.4 to 6.6.
  • Freezing point of pure milk is approximately -0.545°C.
  • Alkaline phosphatase test verifies pasteurization efficiency.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Milk composition varies significantly between species; buffalo milk has markedly higher fat and SNF than cow milk.
  • Specific gravity of milk ranges from 1.028 to 1.032 and is measured using a lactometer.
  • HTST pasteurization (72°C for 15 seconds) is the commercial standard for fluid milk processing.
  • Alkaline phosphatase enzyme inactivation confirms successful pasteurization.
  • Lactose is the predominant carbohydrate and is less sweet than sucrose.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

LTLT Pasteurization VS HTST Pasteurization

LTLT uses 63°C for 30 minutes (Batch method), whereas HTST uses 72°C for 15 seconds (Continuous method).

Chhana VS Paneer

Chhana is softer with higher moisture used for soft sweets (rasgulla); paneer is pressed firmer with lower moisture used for culinary dishes.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the normal specific gravity range of cow milk at 15°C?

Explanation: The specific gravity of normal cow milk ranges between 1.028 and 1.030 at 15°C, while buffalo milk ranges from 1.030 to 1.032.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which enzyme is tested to verify the efficiency of milk pasteurization?

Explanation: Alkaline phosphatase is naturally present in raw milk and is inactivated during proper pasteurization. Its absence indicates successful heat treatment.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the holding time and temperature in the HTST pasteurization method?

Explanation: HTST involves heating milk to 72°C for 15 seconds, whereas 63°C for 30 minutes represents the LTLT (batch) method.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What percentage of total milk protein is represented by casein?

Explanation: Casein is the primary milk protein, constituting approximately 80% of total protein, existing in micellar suspension with calcium and phosphate.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the normal freezing point of pure cow and buffalo milk?

Explanation: Normal pure milk freezes between -0.522°C and -0.565°C. Adulteration with water raises the freezing point toward 0.0°C.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which test is used to evaluate the microbiological quality of raw milk rapidly?

Explanation: The Methylene Blue Reduction Test measures the time taken by bacteria to decolorize methylene blue dye, reflecting microbial load.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary carbohydrate present in milk?

Explanation: Lactose is the primary carbohydrate in milk, averaging 4.9% in cow milk and 5.2% in buffalo milk.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the purpose of homogenization in milk processing?

Explanation: Homogenization reduces fat globule diameter below 2 micrometers, preventing cream layer formation and ensuring uniform fat distribution.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which indigenous dairy product is prepared by continuous boiling and desiccation of whole milk in an open pan?

Explanation: Khoa is a partially dehydrated whole milk product prepared by continuous evaporation in an open iron pan.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the average fat percentage in buffalo milk?

Explanation: Buffalo milk typically contains 6.0% to 7.5% fat (averaging around 6.5%), compared to 3.5% to 4.5% in standard cow milk.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the pH range of fresh, normal cow milk?

Explanation: Fresh normal milk has a pH ranging from 6.4 to 6.6. Lower pH values indicate microbial acid production and spoilage.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which vitamin is naturally abundant in cow milk, giving it a yellowish tinge?

Explanation: Beta-carotene gives cow milkfat its characteristic yellowish color. Buffalo milk converts carotene entirely into colorless vitamin A.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the principal method used for determining fat content in milk in commercial dairies?

Explanation: The Gerber method is the standard volumetric assay utilized globally for rapid and accurate determination of fat in milk.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does SNF stand for in dairy industry standards?

Explanation: SNF stands for Solids-Not-Fat, comprising proteins, lactose, vitamins, and minerals.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which protein coagulates at pH 4.6 during paneer or cheese making?

Explanation: Casein reaches its isoelectric point at pH 4.6, causing it to precipitate and form curds during acid coagulation.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary cause of rancidity in stored dairy products?

Explanation: Rancidity results from lipolysis (enzymatic breakdown of fats by lipase) or oxidation of unsaturated fatty acids, causing off-flavors.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which dairy product is classified as an acid-coagulated indigenous cheese used for culinary preparations?

Explanation: Paneer is an indigenous Indian dairy product obtained by acid coagulation of hot milk followed by pressing to drain whey.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the legal minimum fat percentage in toned milk under Indian PFA/FSSAI standards?

Explanation: According to FSSAI standards, toned milk must contain a minimum of 3.0% fat and 8.5% SNF.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which instrument is used to measure the specific gravity of milk in quality control laboratories?

Explanation: A lactometer is a specialized hydrometer used to determine the specific gravity of milk.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary sugar-splitting enzyme present in the human digestive tract that hydrolyzes lactose?

Explanation: Lactase is the mucosal brush-border enzyme responsible for hydrolyzing lactose into glucose and galactose.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate the amount of cream containing 25% fat required to standardize 500 kg of milk testing 4% fat to 3% fat using skim milk (0.1% fat). (Use Pearson Square approximation principles)

Solution & Rationale: Using standard Pearson Square balancing for standardization, 25% cream and 0.1% skim milk mixed to achieve 3% fat yields approximately 20.4 kg of cream required.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGR Standard

Which of the following describes the correct sequence of events during the manufacture of Cheddar cheese?

Solution & Rationale: The correct sequence in Cheddar cheese making is setting milk with rennet, cutting the curd, cooking, cheddaring (stacking and turning), milling, salting, and pressing.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary causative organism responsible for bovine mastitis that significantly impacts milk composition and somatic cell counts?

Solution & Rationale: Streptococcus agalactiae along with Staphylococcus aureus are primary contagious pathogens causing mastitis, elevating somatic cell count and altering milk composition.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

In dairy plant operations, what is the critical temperature and holding time for Ultra-High Temperature (UHT) sterilization?

Solution & Rationale: UHT sterilization employs temperatures between 135°C and 150°C for 2 to 4 seconds, achieving commercial sterility while preserving organoleptic properties.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

What chemical test is utilized for detecting adulteration of milk with urea?

Solution & Rationale: DMAB reagent reacts with urea to form a distinct yellow color complex, utilized for rapid screening of added urea in milk.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the physical state of fat in fresh, normal milk?

Solution & Rationale: Milk fat exists as an oil-in-water emulsion, where microscopic fat globules are stabilized by a lipoprotein membrane suspended in the serum phase.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following vitamins is water-soluble and abundant in the aqueous serum phase of milk?

Solution & Rationale: Riboflavin (Vitamin B2) is a water-soluble B-complex vitamin found in the whey fraction of milk, imparting a greenish-yellow fluorescence.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the principal nitrogenous compound found in milk that is NOT true protein?

Solution & Rationale: Non-Protein Nitrogen (NPN) compounds like urea account for approximately 5% of total nitrogen in bovine milk and are not precipitated as true protein.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Why is sodium carbonate or bicarbonate sometimes illegally added to milk by unscrupulous vendors?

Solution & Rationale: Alkaline neutralizers are added to sour milk to mask high acidity and prevent curdling during boiling or pasteurization.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary function of rennet enzyme used in cheese making?

Solution & Rationale: Rennet (chymosin) specifically cleaves kappa-casein, removing its stabilizing hydrophilic glycopeptide tail and causing calcium-mediated casein micelle coagulation.
