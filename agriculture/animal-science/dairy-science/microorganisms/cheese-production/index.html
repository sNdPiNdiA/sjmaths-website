Cheese Production: Microbiology, Biochemistry, and Processing Technology

Introduction and Classification of Cheese

Cheese is a consolidated dairy product derived from milk by coagulation of milk protein casein, separating the whey, and ripening the resulting curd with specific microorganisms and enzymes. It serves as an essential method of preserving surplus milk solids, rich in proteins, fats, calcium, and fat-soluble vitamins.

  • Classification Based on Moisture Content: Soft cheeses (Cottage, Camembert, moisture > 55%), Semi-hard cheeses (Gouda, Edam, moisture 39-50%), Hard cheeses (Cheddar, Swiss, moisture 34-39%), and Very hard cheeses (Parmesan, Romano, moisture < 34%).
  • Classification Based on Ripening Agent: Unripened (fresh curd products like Paneer, Mozzarella) and Ripened (bacteria-ripened or mold-ripened).

Microbiology and Starter Cultures in Cheese Making

Starter cultures are vital microbial inoculants added to milk to ferment lactose into lactic acid, lowering pH and assisting in curd formation, syneresis, and pathogen inhibition.

  • Mesophilic Cultures: Optimum growth temperature between 20°C and 30°C. Includes Lactococcus lactis subsp. lactis, Lactococcus lactis subsp. cremoris, and Leuconostoc species used for Cheddar and Gouda.
  • Thermophilic Cultures: Optimum growth temperature between 42°C and 45°C. Includes Streptococcus thermophilus, Lactobacillus helveticus, and Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus used for Swiss and Italian hard cheeses.
  • Secondary Flora: Propionibacterium freudenreichii subsp. shermanii for eye formation and flavor in Swiss cheese; Penicillium camemberti and Penicillium roqueforti for surface and interior mold-ripened cheeses respectively.

Biochemistry of Coagulation and Rennet Action

Coagulation is the primary step converting liquid milk into a gel matrix. It is achieved via enzymatic action (rennet) or acid precipitation.

  • Rennet Coagulation: Chymosin (rennin), extracted from the abomasum of unweaned calves or produced via recombinant DNA technology, specifically cleaves the peptide bond between phenylalanine (105) and methionine (106) in kappa-casein.
  • Glycomacropeptide Release: The hydrophilic C-terminal portion (kappa-casein glycomacropeptide) is cleaved off and released into the whey, destabilizing the casein micelle steric stabilization.
  • Calcium-Mediated Aggregation: In the presence of divalent calcium ions (Ca2+) and temperatures around 30°C to 32°C, destabilized para-kappa-casein micelles aggregate to form a continuous three-dimensional network (curd).

Step-by-Step Cheese Manufacturing Process

The standardized industrial manufacture of hard and semi-hard cheeses like Cheddar involves sequential unit operations.

  • Standardization and Pasteurization: Adjusting the Fat-to-Solids-Not-Fat (SNF) ratio and destroying pathogenic microorganisms via HTST pasteurization (72°C for 15 seconds).
  • Inoculation and Ripening: Addition of starter cultures to condition the milk and develop acidity.
  • Renneting and Cutting: Addition of rennet, setting the curd, and cutting into uniform cubes to facilitate whey drainage (syneresis).
  • Cooking, Salting, and Hooping: Raising temperature to shrink curd particles, draining whey, dry salting or brine immersion, and pressing into molds (hoops) to expel remaining moisture.

Ripening (Curing) and Storage Technology

Ripening transforms raw, rubbery curd into a flavorful, digestible cheese with distinct textural characteristics through biochemical breakdown of proteins, fats, and residual lactose.

  • Glycolysis: Fermentation of residual lactose into lactic acid, propionic acid, acetic acid, and carbon dioxide.
  • Proteolysis: Breakdown of casein proteins into large peptides, small peptides, and free amino acids by residual rennet and microbial proteases/peptidases.
  • Lipolysis: Hydrolysis of milk fat triglycerides into free fatty acids, aldehydes, and ketones, which contribute significantly to the characteristic aroma and flavor profiles of aged cheeses.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Chymosin is the primary proteolytic enzyme found in rennet that specifically cleaves the Phe105-Met106 bond of kappa-casein.
  • The process of piling and turning curd blocks during Cheddar cheese manufacture is known as 'Cheddaring'.
  • Propionibacterium freudenreichii subsp. shermanii is responsible for the characteristic eyes and sweet, nutty flavor in Swiss Emmental cheese.
  • Syneresis refers to the spontaneous contraction of a coagulated milk gel and the expulsion of whey.
  • Moisture content in hard cheese such as Parmesan is below 34%, whereas soft cheese such as Cottage cheese exceeds 55%.
  • Pasta filata is a unique stretching and kneading manufacturing technique used for Mozzarella and Provolone cheeses.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing mesophilic and thermophilic starter cultures; mesophilic cultures grow best at 20-30°C (Cheddar), while thermophilic grow at 42-45°C (Swiss/Italian).
  • Assuming all cheese undergoes microbial ripening; fresh unripened cheeses like Cottage and Paneer do not undergo curing.
  • Believing that rennet acts directly on calcium caseinate without prior enzymatic cleavage of kappa-casein.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Cheese Production: Microbiology, Biochemistry, and Processing Technology

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Milk Coagulation Mechanism

Enzymatic coagulation occurs when chymosin attacks kappa-casein, removing its hydrophilic protective coating.

  • Kappa-Casein: Forms the hairy outer layer of casein micelles, providing steric stability.
  • Para-Kappa-Casein: Insoluble residue remaining on the micelle after chymosin cleavage, leading to clot formation in the presence of calcium.
Concept 2

Starter Culture Fermentation

Starter cultures convert lactose into lactic acid, dropping pH and preventing spoilage organisms.

  • Lactic Acid Bacteria: Essential flora including Lactococcus, Streptococcus, and Lactobacillus species.
  • Acid Development: Crucial for syneresis, curd contraction, and texture development.
Concept 3

Biochemical Changes During Ripening

Ripening involves complex biochemical pathways breaking down core milk constituents into volatile aroma compounds.

  • Proteolysis: Breakdown of proteins into bitter and savory amino acids and peptides.
  • Lipolysis: Hydrolysis of fats yielding free fatty acids responsible for sharp cheese flavors.
Concept 4

Specialty Processing Technologies

Different regional traditions yield distinct physical and chemical structures.

  • Cheddaring: Acidification and fusion of curd blocks in Cheddar production.
  • Pasta Filata: Hot-water stretching of Mozzarella curds to align protein fibers.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Chymosin (Rennet Protease Enzyme)
Aspartic proteinase enzyme extracted from the fourth stomach of ruminants used to coagulate milk.
Syneresis (Whey Expulsion)
The shrinkage of a gel matrix resulting in the extrusion of liquid whey.
Cheddaring (Curd Piling and Milling)
A unique processing step in Cheddar cheese making where curd slabs are piled, drained, and milled.
Pasta Filata (Stretched Curd)
A stretched-curd manufacturing technique characteristic of Italian cheeses like Mozzarella.
Mesophilic (Moderate Temperature Microbes)
Bacteria that grow optimally at moderate temperatures ranging from 20°C to 30°C.
Thermophilic (Heat-Loving Microbes)
Bacteria that thrive at higher temperatures ranging from 42°C to 45°C.
Glycomacropeptide (GMP Fragment)
The hydrophilic peptide cleaved from kappa-casein during rennet action and released into the whey.
Proteolysis (Protein Degradation)
The enzymatic breakdown of proteins into smaller peptides and amino acids during cheese ripening.
Lipolysis (Fat Breakdown)
The hydrolysis of triglycerides into free fatty acids by lipases during cheese aging.
Brine Salting (Sodium Chloride Immersion)
Submerging pressed cheese blocks in a concentrated salt solution to control moisture and microbial growth.
Primary Flora (Starter Lactic Acid Bacteria)
Microorganisms added primarily for acid production during early cheese manufacturing.
Secondary Flora (Ripening Microorganisms)
Molds, yeasts, or ripening bacteria added for flavor and texture development during curing.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Chymosin cleaves the Phe105-Met106 bond in kappa-casein.
  • Swiss cheese eyes are produced by carbon dioxide gas generated by Propionibacterium.
  • Hard cheeses have moisture content below 39%, while soft cheeses exceed 55%.
  • Pasta filata refers to the stretching process used in Mozzarella production.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Cheese production converts perishable milk into stable protein and fat concentrates via coagulation and ripening.
  • Rennet enzyme chymosin specifically cleaves kappa-casein to initiate milk gel formation.
  • Starter cultures provide necessary acidity, flavor compounds, and inhibit pathogenic bacteria.
  • Ripening involves complex microbiological and biochemical breakdown of proteins, fats, and carbohydrates.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Mesophilic Culture VS Thermophilic Culture

Mesophilic cultures operate at 20-30°C (Cheddar), while thermophilic cultures operate at 42-45°C (Swiss).

Syneresis VS Proteolysis

Syneresis is the physical expulsion of whey from the curd, whereas proteolysis is the biochemical breakdown of proteins during ripening.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which specific peptide bond in kappa-casein is cleaved by chymosin during rennet coagulation?

Explanation: Chymosin specifically targets and hydrolyzes the peptide bond between phenylalanine at position 105 and methionine at position 106 in kappa-casein, releasing glycomacropeptide.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of starter cultures added during cheese manufacturing?

Explanation: Starter cultures ferment lactose into lactic acid, lowering the pH, which aids in curd syneresis, controls undesirable bacteria, and assists rennet action.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which microorganism is responsible for the characteristic 'eyes' (holes) and sweet flavor in Swiss Emmental cheese?

Explanation: Propionibacterium freudenreichii subsp. shermanii ferments lactic acid into propionic acid, acetic acid, and carbon dioxide gas, forming the characteristic holes in Swiss cheese.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a very hard cheese with moisture content below 34%?

Explanation: Parmesan is a very hard, aged Italian cheese with moisture content typically below 34%, heavily pressed and aged for long periods.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the term for the process of piling and turning curd blocks during Cheddar cheese production?

Explanation: Cheddaring is a unique mechanical process where curd slabs are piled on top of each other and periodically turned to expel whey and knit the curd.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which temperature range characterizes thermophilic starter cultures used in Swiss and Italian cheeses?

Explanation: Thermophilic lactic acid bacteria such as Streptococcus thermophilus and Lactobacillus helveticus have optimum growth temperatures between 42°C and 45°C.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What happens to the glycomacropeptide portion of kappa-casein during enzymatic renneting?

Explanation: The hydrophilic glycomacropeptide fragment is cleaved from kappa-casein and lost in the whey, leaving insoluble para-kappa-casein in the curd.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Pasta filata is a manufacturing technique primarily used for which cheese variety?

Explanation: Pasta filata ('spun paste') is a stretching and kneading technique used in making Mozzarella and Provolone to align protein fibers.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which mold is specifically inoculated to produce internal blue-veined cheese such as Roquefort or Gorgonzola?

Explanation: Penicillium roqueforti is the specialized mold pierced into blue cheeses to grow aerobically and impart sharp, peppery flavors.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is syneresis in the context of cheese production?

Explanation: Syneresis is the shrinkage of the protein network accompanied by the expulsion of liquid whey.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following cheeses is classified as an unripened, fresh variety?

Explanation: Cottage cheese and Paneer are unripened fresh cheeses consumed shortly after manufacture without a curing process.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Proteolysis during cheese ripening primarily results in the breakdown of which milk component?

Explanation: Proteolysis is the enzymatic degradation of casein proteins into peptides and free amino acids, contributing to flavor and texture.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the optimal pH range of milk for standard rennet coagulation?

Explanation: Rennet acts effectively near the normal pH of fresh milk, which is around 6.5 to 6.7.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which enzyme group is responsible for lipolysis during cheese ripening?

Explanation: Lipases hydrolyze milk fat triglycerides into free fatty acids, imparting characteristic sharp flavors in aged cheeses.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of salting cheese curds?

Explanation: Salting controls bacterial growth, regulates moisture loss through osmosis, and enhances flavor.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which bacteria are classified as mesophilic starter cultures?

Explanation: Lactococcus lactis subsp. cremoris and Lactococcus lactis subsp. lactis are typical mesophilic starter cultures.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the average moisture content range for semi-hard cheeses like Gouda and Edam?

Explanation: Semi-hard cheeses generally contain between 39% and 50% moisture.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why is milk pasteurized prior to cheese making in industrial settings?

Explanation: Pasteurization destroys pathogenic and spoilage microorganisms, ensuring safety and standardizing microbial baseline for starter cultures.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Camembert cheese obtains its characteristic white velvety surface mold from which organism?

Explanation: Penicillium camemberti grows on the surface of soft cheeses like Camembert and Brie, forming a white mycelial rind.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which factor most significantly accelerates the rate of rennet coagulation in milk?

Explanation: Adding calcium chloride increases calcium ion concentration, enhancing aggregation of rennet-altered casein micelles and firming the curd.
