Classification and Definition of Milk Products

Milk products encompass all marketable commodities derived from the processing of raw milk, including skimming, creaming, churning, concentration, drying, and microbial fermentation. According to Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) guidelines, these products are categorized based on their moisture content, fat-to-solids-not-fat (SNF) ratio, and processing technology.

  • Liquid Milk & Concentrated Products: Evaporated milk and sweetened condensed milk prepared by thermal water removal.
  • Fat-Rich Products: Butter, ghee, and malai derived through phase inversion or skimming.
  • Fermented & Coagulated Products: Curd (dahi), paneer, chhena, shrikhand, and cheese produced via enzymatic or lactic acid coagulation.
  • Desiccated Indigenous Sweets: Khoa (mava) obtained by open-pan thermal dehydration.

Technology of Indigenous Milk Products (Khoa, Paneer, Chhena, Ghee)

Indigenous milk products constitute nearly 50% of total milk utilization in India. Their production relies heavily on traditional artisan techniques now scaled up via industrial mechanized vats.

  • Khoa: Prepared by continuous boiling and moisture evaporation of milk in a shallow iron pan (karahi) until solid content reaches 65-70%. Three major types exist: Pindi (smooth texture, high fat), Dhap (granular, high moisture), and Buranu (coarse grain).
  • Paneer and Chhena: Acid-coagulated products. Paneer uses hot milk (70-85°C) coagulated with citric or lactic acid (1-2% solution), pressed to 50-55% moisture. Chhena is unpressed, softer curd used for rosogolla and sandesh.
  • Ghee: Clarified butterfat obtained by direct boiling of cream, butter, or indigenous dahi-butter at 110-120°C, ensuring complete moisture removal (<0.5%) and characteristic nutty flavor development via carbonyl compounds.

Fermented Dairy Products and Starter Cultures

Fermentation extends milk shelf-life and enhances digestibility and nutritional value through microbial action.

  • Dahi (Curd): Produced using mixed-strain starter cultures containing Lactococcus lactis subsp. lactis, Lactococcus lactis subsp. cremoris, Lactococcus lactis subsp. diacetylactis, and Leuconostoc species at an incubation temperature of 22-30°C or 37-42°C (thermophilic).
  • Yogurt: Symbiotic culture of Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus incubated at 42-45°C.
  • Shrikhand: Prepared by draining whey from dahi (chakka) and blending it with sugar, cardamom, and saffron. Chakka yield typically ranges from 45% to 50% of the original dahi weight.

Butter, Cream, and Ice Cream Processing

High-fat and frozen dairy products require precise temperature control, standardization, and homogenization.

  • Cream: Separation of whole milk into skim milk and cream using centrifugal cream separators based on Stokes' Law. Standard market creams include light cream (18-30% fat), whipping cream (30-40% fat), and plastic cream (80% fat).
  • Butter: Water-in-oil emulsion produced by churning cream (phase inversion). Cream is neutralized, pasteurized, aged (to promote fat crystallization), churned, washed, worked, and salted. Standard butter must contain a minimum of 80% milk fat and a maximum of 16% moisture.
  • Ice Cream: Complex frozen emulsion containing milk fat (10-12%), SNF (10-11%), sugar (12-16%), and stabilizer/emulsifier (0.2-0.5%). Air incorporation during freezing is termed 'overrun', ideally maintained between 70% and 100%.

Evaporated and Dried Milk Products

Dehydration techniques prolong the shelf life of surplus milk for transport and seasonal balancing.

  • Evaporated Milk: Whole milk concentrated in vacuum evaporators to roughly 2.1 times its original solids, then homogenized, canned, and sterilized at 115-118°C for 15 minutes.
  • Sweetened Condensed Milk: Concentrated milk preserved by adding 40-45% sucrose rather than thermal sterilization, yielding a final moisture level of about 25-27%.
  • Milk Powder: Produced mainly via two methods: Roller Drying (drum drying, which imparts a cooked flavor) and Spray Drying (atomizing milk into a hot air chamber at 150-200°C), yielding superior solubility and nutritional retention. Standard dry whole milk contains max 4% moisture and min 26% fat.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Khoa yield from buffalo milk is approximately 20-22%, whereas from cow milk it is 16-18% due to higher total solids and fat in buffalo milk.
  • The standard bacterial culture for commercial yogurt manufacturing consists of Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus in a 1:1 ratio.
  • Overrun in ice cream is defined as the volume increase of ice cream over the volume of the milk mix, expressed as a percentage (standard target: 70-100%).
  • Ghee must contain not less than 99.5% fat and moisture content must not exceed 0.5% under FSSAI rules.
  • Chakka is the drained curd mass used for making Shrikhand, and its acidity should be between 1.0% and 1.2% lactic acid.
  • Spray drying operates at inlet air temperatures of 150-200°C and outlet temperatures of 70-90°C to prevent protein denaturation.
  • The optimum temperature for coagulating milk to make paneer is 70-85°C, ensuring a smooth texture and firm curd matrix.
  • Browning in Khoa and sweetened condensed milk is primarily caused by the non-enzymatic Maillard reaction between lactose (reducing sugar) and milk proteins (amino acids).
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Chhena with Paneer: Chhena is unpressed soft acid curd used for soft sweets, while Paneer is pressed to remove moisture and used for savory dishes.
  • Assuming butter can contain up to 25% water; statutory limits strictly cap moisture in butter at 16%.
  • Believing ghee crystallization is a defect; granular texture in ghee is a desirable physical property caused by high-melting triglyceride fractions.
  • Mistaking roller-dried milk powder for spray-dried powder regarding solubility and flavor; roller-dried powder often exhibits scorched particles and reduced solubility.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Milk Products and Dairy Processing Technology for UP TGT/PGT Agriculture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Thermal Processing and Desiccation (Khoa)

Desiccation involves removing water by boiling milk in open pans, resulting in structural changes in milk proteins and fat globule aggregation.

  • Maillard Reaction: Chemical reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars producing brown pigments and characteristic cooked flavor in Khoa.
  • Pindi Khoa: High-fat, smooth textured khoa ideal for round sweets like Gulab Jamun.
Concept 2

Acid Coagulation Technology (Paneer and Chhena)

Acid coagulation destabilizes casein micelles by lowering pH to their isoelectric point (~4.6) at elevated temperatures.

  • Isoelectric Point: pH 4.6 where casein carries net zero charge, causing immediate precipitation.
  • Coagulation Temperature: Optimal range of 70-85°C for paneer to achieve optimal moisture retention and elasticity.
Concept 3

Phase Inversion and Butter Making

Butter production requires converting oil-in-water emulsion (cream) into water-in-oil emulsion (butter) through mechanical agitation and phase inversion.

  • Churning: Mechanical agitation that breaks fat globule membranes, causing fat globules to coalesce into butter granules.
  • Working: Kneading butter granules to incorporate moisture uniformly and regulate salt distribution.
Concept 4

Dehydration and Drying Technologies

Conversion of liquid milk into stable powder form for extended shelf life without refrigeration.

  • Spray Drying: Atomizing milk into fine droplets inside a drying chamber with hot air, yielding superior dispersibility.
  • Roller Drying: Passing milk over heated rotating drums; causes greater heat damage and lower solubility.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Khoa (Desiccated whole milk product)
An indigenous milk product obtained by partial evaporation of milk moisture in an open pan until solids reach 65-70%.
Paneer (Pressed acid-coagulated product)
An indigenous soft cheese variety prepared by acid coagulation of hot milk followed by pressing.
Chhena (Unpressed acid-coagulated curd)
An unpressed intermediate milk product used as the base for Bengali sweets like Rosogolla and Sandesh.
Ghee (Clarified butterfat)
Exclusively clarified butterfat obtained from milk, cream, or butter by direct thermal evaporation of moisture.
Overrun (Percentage volume increase in ice cream)
The increase in volume of ice cream over the volume of the liquid mix due to air incorporation during freezing.
Chakka (Drained dahi curd)
Solid mass obtained by draining whey from dahi through muslin cloth, serving as the base for Shrikhand.
Maillard Reaction (Non-enzymatic browning reaction)
Reaction between reducing sugars (lactose) and amino groups of proteins during heat treatment of milk products.
Standardized Milk (Standardized milk composition)
Milk adjusted to specified fat and SNF percentages, typically 4.5% fat and 8.5% SNF in India.
Phase Inversion (Emulsion conversion in butter)
The conversion of oil-in-water emulsion (cream) to water-in-oil emulsion (butter) during churning.
Neutralizer (Cream acidity regulator)
Chemical agent (such as sodium bicarbonate or sodium hydroxide) added to high-acid cream before butter making.
Spray Drying (Atomized thermal dehydration)
Method of milk powder production where atomized droplets meet hot air in a drying chamber.
Recombined Milk (Reconstituted dairy product)
Product made by mixing milk powder, anhydrous milk fat, and water to match normal milk composition.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Khoa moisture content: 15-25%; Paneer moisture content: 50-55%.
  • Minimum milk fat in Ghee: 99.5%; maximum moisture: 0.5%.
  • Yogurt culture: Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus.
  • Isoelectric point of casein: pH 4.6.
  • Maximum moisture in spray-dried whole milk powder: 4.0%.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Indigenous milk products comprise Khoa, Paneer, Chhena, Ghee, Dahi, and Shrikhand, accounting for bulk dairy consumption in India.
  • Buffalo milk is preferred over cow milk for Khoa and Paneer manufacture due to its higher total solids, fat, and mineral content.
  • Butter must meet statutory standards of minimum 80% fat and maximum 16% moisture.
  • Spray drying is superior to roller drying for milk powder as it preserves solubility, color, and nutritional value.
  • Overrun in ice cream is essential for texture, mouthfeel, and commercial yield, maintained between 70% and 100%.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Paneer VS Chhena

Paneer is pressed to remove moisture and has firm texture, whereas Chhena is unpressed, soft, and crumbly.

Evaporated Milk VS Sweetened Condensed Milk

Evaporated milk is sterilized by heat without sugar addition; condensed milk is preserved by adding 40-45% sugar without in-can sterilization.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the approximate average moisture content of standard Pindi Khoa?

Explanation: Pindi Khoa typically has a moisture content ranging from 20% to 25% (often cited between 15% and 25% depending on type), making it firm and smooth.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following bacterial species is a key component of the commercial yogurt starter culture?

Explanation: Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus paired with Streptococcus thermophilus forms the standard symbiotic starter culture for yogurt.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the statutory minimum milk fat percentage required in commercial table butter in India?

Explanation: According to FSSAI standards, butter must contain a minimum of 80.0% milk fat and a maximum of 16.0% moisture.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

The phase inversion process during butter manufacturing converts which type of emulsion?

Explanation: During churning, cream (an oil-in-water emulsion) undergoes phase inversion to become butter (a water-in-oil emulsion).

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

At what approximate pH does casein precipitation occur during the manufacture of paneer or chhena?

Explanation: Casein has an isoelectric point at pH 4.6, where proteins lose their hydration shell and electrostatic repulsion, leading to immediate coagulation.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the maximum permissible moisture limit in high-grade Ghee according to FSSAI standards?

Explanation: FSSAI standards stipulate that the moisture content in ghee must not exceed 0.5% to ensure long shelf life without refrigeration.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does 'overrun' refer to in ice cream manufacturing?

Explanation: Overrun is the percentage increase in the volume of ice cream over the volume of the liquid mix caused by whipped air during freezing.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Chkka, used for making Shrikhand, is obtained from which dairy product?

Explanation: Chakka is prepared by hanging and draining whey from set dahi through a cloth bag until moisture drops to roughly 60-70%.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following milk products is preserved primarily by high concentration of added sucrose rather than thermal sterilization?

Explanation: Sweetened condensed milk uses 40-45% sucrose to create high osmotic pressure that prevents microbial spoilage without requiring heat sterilization.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Why is buffalo milk preferred over cow milk for making Khoa and Paneer?

Explanation: Buffalo milk has significantly higher fat, SNF, and colloidal calcium phosphate, yielding firmer paneer and higher weight recovery of Khoa.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary cause of browning (Maillard reaction) in Khoa and Dulce de Leche?

Explanation: The Maillard reaction is a non-enzymatic browning reaction between the reducing sugar lactose and free amino groups of milk proteins during prolonged heating.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which drying method produces milk powder with the highest solubility and minimal heat damage?

Explanation: Spray drying exposes atomized milk droplets to hot air for a fraction of a second, resulting in rapid moisture evaporation and minimal protein denaturation.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended optimum coagulation temperature for producing high-quality Paneer?

Explanation: Coagulating milk at 70-85°C ensures proper curd tension, moisture retention, and a smooth, non-crumbly texture in paneer.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which enzyme is traditionally used in cheese making to coagulate milk protein?

Explanation: Rennet, containing the active enzyme chymosin, is the classic coagulant used to destabilize casein and form firm curds in cheese making.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the maximum moisture content allowed in spray-dried whole milk powder under standard specifications?

Explanation: Standard spray-dried whole milk powder must contain a maximum of 4.0% moisture to ensure storage stability.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which milk product is categorized as a fermented indigenous milk beverage resembling buttermilk?

Explanation: Chhaas is a refreshing fermented dairy drink obtained as a byproduct during churning of dahi into butter or prepared by diluting dahi with water.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main function of ripening cream prior to churning in butter manufacture?

Explanation: Ripening cream with lactic cultures produces diacetyl (giving butter its characteristic aroma) and allows controlled fat crystallization for efficient churning.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical fat percentage in standard whipping cream?

Explanation: Whipping cream typically contains between 30% and 40% milk fat, allowing it to trap air and form a stable foam when whipped.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is an example of an acid-heat coagulated indigenous milk product?

Explanation: Paneer is produced by combining heat treatment (70-85°C) and acid coagulation, followed by pressing to remove whey.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate the approximate yield of Khoa from 100 kg of buffalo milk testing 6% fat and 9% SNF, assuming an average yield of 21%.

Solution & Rationale: Buffalo milk yields approximately 20-22% Khoa due to higher total solids. For 100 kg milk at 21% average yield, the expected output is 21 kg.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

During the manufacture of Cheddar cheese, what is the primary role of starter cultures?

Solution & Rationale: Starter bacteria convert lactose to lactic acid, creating the necessary acidic environment for whey expulsion and cheese maturation.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary function of homogenization in market milk and ice cream mix processing?

Solution & Rationale: Homogenization forces milk under high pressure through microscopic valves, reducing fat globule diameter below 2 µm and preventing cream separation.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Which microorganism is predominantly responsible for the characteristic eyes (holes) in Swiss-type cheese?

Solution & Rationale: Propionibacteria ferment lactic acid into propionic acid, acetic acid, and carbon dioxide gas, forming the characteristic holes in Swiss cheese.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the legal minimum milk fat percentage for standard commercial Ghee in India under FSSAI rules?

Solution & Rationale: FSSAI regulations mandate that ghee must have a minimum fat content of 99.5% and maximum moisture of 0.5%.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

In ice cream formulation, what is the recommended range for total solids (TS) to ensure desirable body and texture?

Solution & Rationale: Total solids in a quality ice cream mix normally range between 36% and 42% to balance freezing point depression, texture, and overrun.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary defect known as 'sandiness' in sweetened condensed milk or ice cream?

Solution & Rationale: Sandiness is caused by the growth of large lactose crystals (>15 µm) that impart a gritty, sand-like texture on the tongue.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which test is used to detect adulteration of ghee with vegetable oils (such as vanaspati or hydrogenated oils)?

Solution & Rationale: The Baudouin test detects sesame oil, which is legally mandated to be added to vanaspati, thus revealing vanaspati adulteration in ghee.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the typical recovery percentage of Chhena from cow milk?

Solution & Rationale: Cow milk typically yields about 15% to 18% chhena due to its lower total solids content compared to buffalo milk.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary purpose of adding neutralizers to high-acid cream prior to churning into butter?

Solution & Rationale: Neutralizing high-acidity cream prevents curdling during pasteurization and reduces fat loss in the buttermilk during churning.
