Introduction and Classification of Organic Manures

Organic manures are partially decomposed plant and animal residues applied to the soil to supply plant nutrients, improve soil physical properties, enhance water retention capacity, and stimulate beneficial microbial populations. According to ICAR classification standards, manures are broadly classified into two major categories based on their bulk density and nutrient concentration:

  • Bulky Organic Manures: These are characterized by a low percentage of primary plant nutrients (N, P2O5, K2O) and are applied in large quantities. Major examples include Farmyard Manure (FYM), compost (rural and urban), vermicompost, and green manures.
  • Concentrated Organic Manures: These possess a higher percentage of total nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium compared to bulky manures and are derived from plant or animal tissues. Major examples include oilcakes (edible and non-edible), blood meal, bone meal, meat meal, and fish meal.

Farmyard Manure (FYM) and Compost Production Dynamics

Farmyard Manure (FYM) is a heterogeneous mixture of cattle dung, urine, and litter (bedding material such as straw or chopped crop residues). Well-decomposed FYM typically contains approximately 0.5% N, 0.2% P2O5, and 0.5% K2O. Proper storage and composting methods are critical to prevent nutrient losses through leaching and volatilization:

  • Nutrient Losses Prevention: Urine contains nearly half of the total nitrogen and most of the potassium present in excreta; therefore, absorption using bedding material and protected storage trenches is essential. Lignin and cellulose present in straw regulate decomposition rates.
  • Composting Methods: Traditional methods include the Bangalore method (closed trench system, anaerobic decomposition) and the Indore method (aerobic heap system developed by Howard and Wad). The Indore method involves alternating layers of organic wastes, night soil, and wood ash, taking about 3 to 4 months for complete maturity.

Green Manuring and Biological Nitrogen Fixation

Green manuring involves growing un-decomposed green plant tissue—usually leguminous crops—and incorporating it into the soil while still green to improve soil fertility and structure. It serves as an in-situ biological nitrogen factory:

  • Popular Green Manuring Crops: Sunnhemp (Crotalaria juncea), Dhaincha (Sesbania aculeata), Cowpea (Vigna unguiculata), and Cluster bean (Cyamopsis tetragonoloba). Sesbania aculeata is exceptionally tolerant to waterlogging, salinity, and alkalinity.
  • Agronomic Benefits: Green manuring adds 60 to 90 kg N ha-1, increases organic carbon content, enhances cation exchange capacity (CEC), and mobilizes insoluble soil phosphorus through organic acid exudation. The optimum time of incorporation is usually at the flowering stage to ensure tender tissues and a favorable C:N ratio.

Vermicomposting and Nutrient Mineralization

Vermicomposting is the biotechnological process of composting organic residues utilizing earthworm species to accelerate decomposition and produce a nutrient-rich organic amendment:

  • Earthworm Species: Epigeic species such as Eisenia fetida (Red Worm), Eudrilus eugeniae (African Nightcrawler), and Perionyx excavatus are predominantly used due to their high ingestion and reproductive rates.
  • Nutrient Profile: Vermicompost generally exhibits higher nutrient availability than traditional FYM, typically containing 1.5% to 2.5% N, 0.9% to 1.5% P2O5, and 1.2% to 1.8% K2O, along with plant growth regulators, humic acids, and beneficial microflora.
  • C:N Ratio Dynamics: Microorganisms require carbon for energy and nitrogen for protein synthesis. The critical C:N ratio for rapid decomposition is approximately 20:1 to 30:1. A wider C:N ratio (>30:1) results in microbial immobilization of soil available nitrogen, whereas a narrow ratio (<20:1) promotes rapid net mineralization and release of inorganic nitrogen into the soil solution.

Concentrated Organic Manures and Oilcakes

Concentrated organic manures are rich in nutrient content and are widely utilized in high-value horticulture and organic field crop production:

  • Edible Oilcakes: Neem (Azadirachta indica), Karanja (Pongamia pinnata), Mustard, and Groundnut cake. Mustard cake contains approximately 5.1% N, 1.8% P2O5, and 1.5% K2O.
  • Non-Edible Oilcakes: Castor cake, Mahua cake, and Karanja cake. These are typically toxic or unpalatable to livestock and are used directly as organic fertilizers or nitrification inhibitors. For instance, neem cake contains triterpenoids and limonoids that inhibit soil nitrification, reducing nitrate leaching losses.
  • Bone Meal: Steamed bone meal is a rich source of calcium and phosphorus, containing about 20% to 25% total P2O5 (mostly tricalcium phosphate) and 3% to 4% N, highly effective in acidic soils.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Farmyard Manure (FYM) standard nutrient composition is 0.5% N, 0.2% P2O5, and 0.5% K2O.
  • The Indore composting method relies on aerobic decomposition, whereas the Bangalore method utilizes anaerobic fermentation.
  • The critical C:N ratio for rapid decomposition and prevention of nitrogen immobilization in soil is 20:1 to 30:1.
  • Sesbania aculeata (Dhaincha) is the most preferred green manuring crop for sodic and waterlogged soils.
  • Vermicomposting utilizes epigeic earthworm species such as Eisenia fetida and Eudrilus eugeniae.
  • Neem cake acts as a natural nitrification inhibitor due to the presence of active compounds like azadirachtin and nimbin.
  • Urine fraction of cattle excreta contains the major share of total potassium and half of the total nitrogen.
  • Steamed bone meal is classified as a concentrated organic manure exceptionally rich in insoluble phosphorus.
  • Green manuring in-situ typically fixes 60 to 90 kg N ha-1 within 45 to 60 days of crop growth.
  • Hume and organic acids produced during manure decomposition solubilize native soil phosphorus and micronutrients.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the C:N ratio of straw (>80:1) with that of well-decomposed FYM (20:1 to 30:1), leading to errors in questions regarding nitrogen immobilization.
  • Believing that cattle urine is poorer in nutrients than dung; in reality, urine contains higher concentrations of potassium and immediate plant-available nitrogen.
  • Assuming the Bangalore composting method is aerobic when it is strictly an anaerobic pit process.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Comprehensive Study Material on Manures for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Definition and Classification of Manures

Manures are organic substances of plant, animal, or human origin that supply essential plant nutrients and ameliorate soil physical, chemical, and biological properties.

  • Bulky Organic Manures: Low nutrient concentration, applied in large quantities (FYM, compost, green manure).
  • Concentrated Organic Manures: High nutrient concentration, derived from plant/animal processing (oilcakes, bone meal).
Concept 2

Nutrient Dynamics and C:N Ratio

The carbon-to-nitrogen ratio governs the biological breakdown of organic residues and regulates nitrogen availability to crops.

  • Mineralization: Release of inorganic ions from organic combination during microbial breakdown.
  • Immobilization: Temporary assimilation of soil mineral nitrogen by microbes when high C:N ratio materials (>30:1) are added.
Concept 3

Composting Technologies

Composting transforms raw organic waste into stable, sanitized, and nutrient-enhanced humus.

  • Indore Method: Aerobic process involving turned heaps of organic waste and soil/ash.
  • Bangalore Method: Anaerobic process utilizing sealed trenches to conserve carbon and nitrogen.
Concept 4

Green Manuring Principles

In-situ incorporation of succulent legume crops to enrich soil fertility and structure.

  • Optimum Stage: Flowering stage, balancing succulence, maximum biomass, and nitrogen accumulation.
  • Key Species: Sesbania aculeata and Crotalaria juncea.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Humus (Amorphous Dark Colloidal Organic Matter)
The stable, highly decomposed fraction of soil organic matter that resists rapid microbial degradation and imparts high cation exchange capacity.
Mineralization (Biological Conversion to Inorganic Ions)
The microbial conversion of organic nitrogen and phosphorus compounds into plant-available inorganic ions such as ammonium and phosphate.
Immobilization (Microbial Assimilation of Inorganic Nutrients)
The conversion of inorganic soil nutrients into organic cellular components by heterotrophic microbes decomposing high C:N ratio substrates.
C:N Ratio (Carbon to Nitrogen Ratio)
The mass ratio of total organic carbon to total nitrogen in organic residues or soils, dictating decomposition rate and nutrient availability.
Epigeic Earthworms (Litter-Dwelling Earthworm Guild)
Surface-dwelling earthworm species such as Eisenia fetida that consume organic litter and accelerate vermicomposting.
Nitrification Inhibitor (Biological or Chemical Nitrification Suppressor)
Substances like neem compounds that retard the biological oxidation of ammonium to nitrate, reducing leaching and denitrification losses.
Green Manuring (In-Situ Legume Incorporation)
The agricultural practice of growing and burying succulent plant tissues into the soil to boost organic matter and nitrogen status.
Indore Method (Aerobic Composting Technique)
A standard pit-or-heap composting method developed by Howard and Wad utilizing aerobic decomposition with turned layers.
Bangalore Method (Anaerobic Composting Technique)
A traditional composting process carried out in closed trenches under anaerobic conditions to minimize nitrogen loss.
Farmyard Manure (FYM (Cattle Dung, Urine, and Litter))
A bulky organic manure composed of weathered cattle dung, urine, and bedding straw containing roughly 0.5% N, 0.2% P2O5, and 0.5% K2O.
Bone Meal (Steamed Bone Phosphate Manure)
A concentrated organic phosphorus source produced by steaming and grinding animal bones, rich in tricalcium phosphate.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Cattle urine contains higher K and immediate available N than cattle dung.
  • Critical C:N ratio for avoiding nitrogen starvation is 20:1 to 30:1.
  • Neem cake contains active nitrification-inhibiting principles.
  • Optimum stage for green manure incorporation is flowering.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Manures are divided into bulky (FYM, compost) and concentrated (oilcakes, bone meal) organic types.
  • FYM standard composition stands at 0.5% N, 0.2% P2O5, and 0.5% K2O with a C:N ratio of 20:1 to 30:1.
  • Indore composting is aerobic, while Bangalore composting is anaerobic.
  • Green manuring with Sesbania aculeata adds 60 to 90 kg N ha-1 and corrects soil sodicity.
  • Vermicomposting relies on epigeic earthworms like Eisenia fetida for rapid, nutrient-dense organic breakdown.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Indore Method VS Bangalore Method

Indore method is aerobic and involves frequent turning of heaps, whereas Bangalore method is anaerobic and performed in sealed pits without turning.

Mineralization VS Immobilization

Mineralization releases plant-available inorganic nutrients from organic matter, whereas immobilization ties up inorganic nutrients into microbial biomass.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which composting method is characterized by aerobic decomposition in layered heaps?

Explanation: The Indore method is an aerobic composting process where organic waste and soil are arranged in layers and turned periodically.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the critical C:N ratio below which net mineralization of nitrogen occurs without immobilization?

Explanation: When the C:N ratio is below 20:1 to 30:1, soil microbes find sufficient nitrogen for their tissue synthesis, resulting in net mineralization.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which green manuring crop is exceptionally tolerant to waterlogging, salinity, and sodicity?

Explanation: Sesbania aculeata (Dhaincha) thrives under waterlogged and sodic soil conditions, making it ideal for reclamation of problematic soils.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which earthworm species is predominantly utilized for epigeic vermicomposting in commercial production?

Explanation: Eisenia fetida is an epigeic earthworm species with high reproductive and organic waste ingestion rates, ideal for vermicomposting.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which component of cattle excreta contains the higher share of total potassium?

Explanation: Cattle urine contains nearly all of the potassium and half of the total nitrogen excreted by cattle, making its conservation critical.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary action of neem cake when applied alongside chemical nitrogen fertilizers?

Explanation: Neem cake acts as a natural nitrification inhibitor by suppressing Nitrosomonas bacteria, thereby reducing nitrate leaching and denitrification.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard C:N ratio of cereal straw or fresh crop residues?

Explanation: Cereal straws have a very wide C:N ratio exceeding 80:1, which causes severe nitrogen immobilization upon incorporation into the soil.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which organic manure is classified as a concentrated organic manure derived from animal slaughterhouse by-products?

Explanation: Bone meal is a concentrated organic manure produced from animal bones, rich in tricalcium phosphate.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following describes the Bangalore method of composting?

Explanation: The Bangalore method is an anaerobic composting process conducted in closed trenches to prevent loss of carbon dioxide and nitrogen.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the ideal crop growth stage for in-situ incorporation of green manure crops?

Explanation: Green manure crops should be incorporated at the flowering stage because tissues are tender, decompose rapidly, and have optimal nitrogen accumulation.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which oilcake is edible and commonly used as cattle feed as well as an organic manure?

Explanation: Groundnut cake is an edible oilcake with high protein content, used both as livestock feed and high-grade organic manure.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical nitrogen percentage found in mustard cake?

Explanation: Mustard cake contains approximately 5.1% N, 1.8% P2O5, and 1.5% K2O.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which parameter is most crucial in determining the rate of decomposition of organic residues?

Explanation: The C:N ratio is the primary biochemical index governing microbial multiplication and organic matter breakdown rate.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the term for the biological transformation of organic nitrogen into ammonium ions?

Explanation: Ammonification is the microbial breakdown of organic nitrogenous compounds into ammonia or ammonium ions.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is NOT an objective of applying bulky organic manures?

Explanation: Organic manures buffer soil pH and improve physical conditions; they do not aim to make soils extremely alkaline.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the approximate nutrient composition of vermicompost in terms of N-P2O5-K2O?

Explanation: Vermicompost typically contains 1.5% to 2.5% N, 1.0% to 1.5% P2O5, and 1.2% to 1.8% K2O.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which state of India is historically associated with the development of the Indore composting method?

Explanation: The Indore method was developed in Madhya Pradesh by Sir Albert Howard and Y.D. Wad.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What happens to available soil nitrogen when organic residues with a C:N ratio of 60:1 are incorporated?

Explanation: Residues with a wide C:N ratio induce nitrogen immobilization as soil microbes consume available inorganic nitrogen to build biomass.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following non-edible oilcakes is known for containing toxic saponins and is primarily used in rice fields to control pests?

Explanation: Mahua cake contains saponins (mahuanin) which are toxic to earthworms and fish, and it is used to manage pests in turf and fisheries.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Evaluate the following statements regarding Farmyard Manure (FYM): 1. FYM supplies macro and micro nutrients. 2. Urine has higher potassium than dung. 3. Fresh FYM has a C:N ratio of 10:1. Which of the above statements are correct?

Solution & Rationale: Statement 1 is correct (FYM supplies macro/micro nutrients). Statement 2 is correct (Urine is richer in potassium). Statement 3 is incorrect because fresh FYM or straw-mixed dung has a wide C:N ratio around 30:1 to 40:1, which narrows down to 20:1 upon decomposition.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary biochemical distinction between the Indore and Bangalore composting methods?

Solution & Rationale: The Indore method is an aerobic process requiring periodic turning of heaps, whereas the Bangalore method is an anaerobic process conducted in closed trenches.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

A farmer incorporates rice straw with a C:N ratio of 75:1 into a sandy loam soil. What agronomic consequence is expected in the immediate crop cycle?

Solution & Rationale: Incorporating high C:N ratio rice straw stimulates microbial proliferation, leading to the scavenging and immobilization of soil mineral nitrogen, causing temporary nitrogen deficiency in crops.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Why is Sesbania aculeata specifically recommended for green manuring in sodic soils of Uttar Pradesh?

Solution & Rationale: Sesbania aculeata (Dhaincha) possesses high tolerance to soil sodicity, alkalinity, and waterlogging, making it ideal for reclamation trials in the Indo-Gangetic plains.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following oilcakes contains active compounds that inhibit nitrification, thereby improving nitrogen use efficiency in cereals?

Solution & Rationale: Neem cake contains triterpenoids (azadirachtin, nimbin) that inhibit the activity of Nitrosomonas bacteria, slowing nitrification.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the typical mechanism by which earthworms enhance the nutrient status of vermicompost compared to raw feedstocks?

Solution & Rationale: Earthworms ingest organic waste along with microflora; enzymatic digestion and microbial action in their gut concentrate nutrients, rendering them more available.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

If 10 tonnes of FYM containing 0.5% N is applied to a field, what is the total absolute quantity of Nitrogen added?

Solution & Rationale: Calculation: 0.5% of 10,000 kg (10 tonnes) = (0.5 / 100) * 10,000 = 50 kg of Nitrogen.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

In the context of organic manures, what does 'mineralization' specifically entail?

Solution & Rationale: Mineralization is the microbial conversion of organically bound nutrients into soluble inorganic forms (e.g., NH4+, NO3-, H2PO4-).

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following organic manures has the highest concentration of total nitrogen?

Solution & Rationale: Concentrated organic manures like oilcakes (castor, mustard) contain much higher nitrogen (4-6%) compared to bulky manures like FYM (0.5%).

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary reason for incorporating green manure crops at the flowering stage rather than at full maturity?

Solution & Rationale: At the flowering stage, plant tissues are succulent, possess low lignin content, and have a favorable C:N ratio, ensuring rapid decomposition and nutrient release.
