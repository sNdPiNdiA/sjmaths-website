Introduction and Fundamental Classification of Plant Nutrient Sources

In agricultural science and plant nutrition, nutrient sources supplied to the soil-plant continuum are broadly classified into two major categories: Manures (organic sources) and Fertilizers (inorganic or synthetic sources). This fundamental classification is based on their origin, chemical nature, nutrient concentration, and speed of nutrient release in the soil solution.

  • Organic Manures: Bulky and concentrated organic substances derived from plant and animal residues that improve soil physical, chemical, and biological properties in addition to supplying macro- and micronutrients.
  • Inorganic Fertilizers: Chemical compounds manufactured industrially containing high concentrations of plant nutrients, designed for rapid dissolution and immediate availability to crop plants.

Classification of Organic Manures

Organic manures are classified into two distinct groups based on their bulk density, nutrient density, and origin: Bulky Organic Manures and Concentrated Organic Manures.

  • Bulky Organic Manures: Characterized by a low percentage of nutrients (N:P2O5:K2O) and are applied in large quantities. Examples include Farmyard Manure (FYM), Compost (rural and urban), Vermicompost, and Green Manuring crops (e.g., Crotalaria juncea, Sesbania aculeata).
  • Concentrated Organic Manures: Possess a higher nutrient content compared to bulky manures. They are derived from plant or animal tissues. Examples include oilcakes (non-edible like Azadirachta indica cake, edible like mustard cake), blood meal, bone meal, and fish meal.

Classification of Inorganic Fertilizers

Chemical fertilizers are systematically categorized according to the primary nutrient they supply (Straight vs. Complex), chemical form, and solubility:

  • Nitrogenous Fertilizers: Supply primary macronutrient Nitrogen (N). Sub-classified into Nitrate fertilizers (e.g., Sodium Nitrate), Ammoniacal fertilizers (e.g., Ammonium Sulphate), Ammonium-Nitrate fertilizers (e.g., Calcium Ammonium Nitrate - CAN), and Amide fertilizers (e.g., Urea, CO(NH2)2).
  • Phosphatic Fertilizers: Supply Phosphorus (P2O5). Sub-classified based on water solubility into Water-soluble (e.g., Single Super Phosphate - SSP, Di-ammonium Phosphate - DAP), Citric acid-soluble (e.g., Basic Slag, Dicalcium Phosphate), and Insoluble phosphatic fertilizers (e.g., Rock Phosphate).
  • Potassic Fertilizers: Supply Potassium (K2O). Examples include Muriate of Potash (MOP or Potassium Chloride, KCl) and Potassium Sulphate (K2SO4).
  • Complex Fertilizers: Contain two or more primary major nutrients manufactured chemically, such as NPK complexes (e.g., 12:32:16, 10:26:26).
  • Micronutrient Fertilizers: Supply essential trace elements like Zinc (Zinc Sulphate, ZnSO4), Boron (Borax), Iron, Manganese, Copper, and Molybdenum.

Nutrient Dynamics and Soil Interaction

The interaction of applied manures and fertilizers with the soil complex dictates nutrient recovery efficiency and crop growth dynamics.

  • Mineralization and Immobilization: Organic nitrogen undergoes ammonification and nitrification. The C:N ratio dictates whether mineralization or microbial immobilization occurs (critical C:N ratio is approximately 20:1 to 30:1).
  • Fixation Reactions: Phosphorus applied via fertilizers readily fixes with iron and aluminum in acidic soils, and with calcium in alkaline/calcareous soils, necessitating placement methods like band placement or pellet application.
  • Leaching and Volatilization Losses: Nitrate-N is prone to leaching in sandy soils, while urea is susceptible to ammonia volatilization under high soil pH conditions.

Agronomic Calculation and Fertilizer Recommendation Math

Agronomists must calculate exact commercial fertilizer quantities based on recommended nutrient doses (RDF - Recommended Dose of Fertilizers) expressed in terms of N, P2O5, and K2O per hectare.

  • Formula for Fertilizer Quantity: Fertilizer Required = Recommended Nutrient Dose% Nutrient in Fertilizer × 100
  • Example Calculation: To apply 120 kg N using Urea (containing 46% N): Urea = 12046 × 100 = 260.87 kg/ha.
  • DAP Calculation (Dual Nutrient): DAP contains 18% N and 46% P2O5. If applying 60 kg P2O5, DAP required = 6046 × 100 = 130.43 kg/ha (which also supplies 130.43 × 0.18 = 23.48 kg N).

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Farmyard Manure (FYM) standard average nutrient composition is 0.5% N, 0.2% P2O5, and 0.5% K2O.
  • Urea contains 46% Nitrogen and is an amide form of nitrogenous fertilizer.
  • Single Super SSP contains 16% Water-soluble P2O5, 12% Sulphur, and 19.5% Calcium.
  • Muriate of Potash (MOP) contains 60% K2O and is chloride-based, making it unsuitable for chloride-sensitive crops like tobacco and potato.
  • Critical C:N ratio for decomposition of organic residues without causing nitrogen starvation is 20:1 to 30:1.
  • DAP (Di-ammonium Phosphate) contains 18% Nitrogen and 46% Phosphorus (P2O5).
  • Green manuring in-situ is commonly practiced using sunnhemp (Crotalaria juncea) or dhaincha (Sesbania aculeata).
  • Calcium Ammonium Nitrate (CAN) is neutral in soil reaction and contains 25% Nitrogen (half nitrate and half ammoniacal).
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Assuming organic manures supply only nitrogen; in reality, they supply all macro and micronutrients along with humic substances.
  • Confusing water-soluble phosphatic fertilizers (SSP, DAP) with citrate-soluble or insoluble ones (Rock phosphate).
  • Forgetting that MOP contains chloride, which can impair starch quality in potato tubers.
  • Calculating fertilizer requirement without adjusting for the nutrient percentage present in the commercial grade compound.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Classification of Manures and Fertilizers in Soil Science and Agricultural Chemistry

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Primary Classification Framework

Nutrient sources are divided into organic (manures) and inorganic (fertilizers). Manures improve biological and physical soil health, while fertilizers provide high-analysis mineral ions.

  • Bulky Organic Manures: FYM, compost, and green manures with low nutrient percentages.
  • Concentrated Manures: Oilcakes and bone meal with higher nutrient density.
Concept 2

Nitrogenous Fertilizer Classification

Nitrogen fertilizers are categorized by chemical binding form.

  • Ammoniacal: Ammonium sulphate, ammonium chloride.
  • Nitrate: Sodium nitrate, calcium nitrate.
  • Amide: Urea (CO(NH2)2).
Concept 3

Phosphatic Fertilizer Solubility Sub-groups

Phosphorus availability depends directly on water and citrate solubility.

  • Water Soluble: SSP, Triple Super Phosphate (TSP), DAP.
  • Citrate Soluble: Dicalcium phosphate, basic slag.
  • Insoluble: Rock phosphate.
Concept 4

Potassic and Secondary Nutrient Sources

Potassium and secondary elements complete the balanced fertilization paradigm.

  • Potassic: MOP (KCl, 60% K2O) and SOP (K2SO4, 50% K2O).
  • Secondary: Gypsum for Calcium and Sulphur supply.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Farmyard Manure (FYM) (Mixed excreta of cattle, urine, and litter)
A bulky organic manure with average composition of 0.5% N, 0.2% P2O5, and 0.5% K2O.
Green Manuring (In-situ incorporation of green plant tissues)
The practice of growing green manure crops like Crotalaria juncea and ploughing them into the soil to enrich organic matter and nitrogen.
Urea (CO(NH2)2)
An amide nitrogenous fertilizer containing 46% nitrogen, widely used in Indian agriculture.
Single Super Phosphate (SSP)
A phosphatic fertilizer containing 16% water-soluble P2O5, 12% sulphur, and 19.5% calcium.
Muriate of Potash (Potassium Chloride (KCl))
The principal potassic fertilizer containing 60% K2O.
C:N Ratio (Carbon to Nitrogen Ratio)
The ratio of the mass of organic carbon to nitrogen in soil or organic residues, determining decomposition rate.
Mineralization (Microbial conversion of organic nutrients to inorganic ionic forms)
The biological transformation of organic nitrogen into ammonium and nitrate ions available to plants.
Immobilization (Biological assimilation of inorganic ions by soil microbes)
The conversion of inorganic soil nutrients into organic microbial biomass, temporarily rendering them unavailable.
Complex Fertilizer (Multi-nutrient chemical fertilizer)
A fertilizer containing two or more primary major plant nutrients produced by chemical reaction (e.g., DAP, NPK grades).
Biofertilizer (Microbial inoculant)
Preparations containing living or latent cells of efficient strains of microorganisms that augment nutrient availability.
Nutrient Use Efficiency (NUE)
The proportion of applied nutrient that is absorbed and utilized by the crop plant.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • FYM composition: 0.5:0.2:0.5% N:P:K.
  • Urea N content: 46%.
  • DAP N and P content: 18% N and 46% P2O5.
  • SSP P content: 16% P2O5.
  • MOP K content: 60% K2O.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Manures are bulky organic sources with wide C:N ratios and low nutrient concentrations.
  • Fertilizers are concentrated, high-analysis synthetic chemical compounds.
  • Nitrogenous fertilizers exist in ammoniacal, nitrate, amide, and ammoniacal-nitrate forms.
  • Phosphatic fertilizers are classified based on water and citrate solubility.
  • Muriate of Potash is the most common potassic fertilizer containing 60% K2O.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Bulky Organic Manures VS Concentrated Organic Manures

Bulky manures have low nutrient percentage and are applied in large volumes (FYM, compost); concentrated manures have higher nutrient percentages (oilcakes, bone meal).

Straight Fertilizers VS Complex Fertilizers

Straight fertilizers supply only one primary nutrient (e.g., Urea, MOP), whereas complex fertilizers supply two or more primary nutrients (e.g., DAP, NPK mixtures).

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the average percentage of Nitrogen present in standard Farmyard Manure (FYM)?

Explanation: Standard Farmyard Manure (FYM) contains approximately 0.5% N, 0.2% P2O5, and 0.5% K2O.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as an amide nitrogenous fertilizer?

Explanation: Urea is an amide fertilizer containing 46% nitrogen. Ammonium sulphate is ammoniacal, and sodium nitrate is a nitrate fertilizer.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the nutrient content of Nitrogen and Phosphorus (P2O5) in Di-ammonium Phosphate (DAP)?

Explanation: DAP contains 18% Nitrogen and 46% Phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5).

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Single Super Phosphate (SSP) is valued not only for phosphorus but also supplies which secondary nutrient?

Explanation: SSP contains 16% water-soluble P2O5, along with approximately 19.5% Calcium and 12% Sulphur.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary potassium oxide (K2O) content in Muriate of Potash (MOP)?

Explanation: Muriate of Potash (Potassium Chloride, KCl) contains 60% K2O.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which C:N ratio threshold marks the dividing line between net mineralization and net immobilization of soil nitrogen?

Explanation: When the C:N ratio of organic residue is wider than 20:1 to 30:1, soil microbes immobilize available nitrogen, causing temporary deficiency.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following green manuring crops is commonly used for reclaiming alkali (sodic) soils?

Explanation: Sesbania aculeata (Dhaincha) is widely adapted and tolerant to sodic and waterlogged conditions, making it ideal for reclaiming alkali soils.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which nitrogenous fertilizer is neutral in its soil reaction and contains equal parts of ammoniacal and nitrate nitrogen?

Explanation: Calcium Ammonium Nitrate (CAN) contains 25% N (50% in nitrate form and 50% in ammoniacal form) and is neutral in soil reaction.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Concentrated organic manures differ from bulky organic manures because they:

Explanation: Concentrated organic manures (such as oilcakes, fish meal, and blood meal) have a higher concentration of nutrients than bulky organic manures like FYM or compost.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the chemical formula and potassium content of Potassium Sulphate (SOP)?

Explanation: Potassium Sulphate (K2SO4) contains 50% K2O and 18% Sulphur, and is free of harmful chloride ions.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which fertilizer is strictly prohibited for use in tobacco and potato crops due to negative quality effects caused by chloride ions?

Explanation: Muriate of Potash contains high levels of chloride, which impairs the burning quality of tobacco and reduces starch accumulation in potato tubers.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which category of phosphatic fertilizers is insoluble in water but soluble in citric acid?

Explanation: Citric-acid soluble phosphatic fertilizers include basic slag and dicalcium phosphate, suitable for slightly acidic soils.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of bulky organic manures in agricultural soils?

Explanation: Bulky organic manures primarily improve soil aggregation, water holding capacity, microbial activity, and cation exchange capacity.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

How many kilograms of Urea are theoretically required to supply 120 kg of Nitrogen?

Explanation: Calculation: (120 / 46) * 100 = 260.87 kg of urea.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which micro-organism is primarily responsible for symbiotic nitrogen fixation in leguminous green manure crops?

Explanation: Rhizobium bacteria form a symbiotic association with roots of leguminous crops, fixing atmospheric nitrogen.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which form of nitrogen is most susceptible to leaching losses in sandy soils?

Explanation: Nitrate (NO3-) is an anion that does not adsorb onto soil colloids and readily leaches down with percolating water.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Composting process where earthworms are utilized to decompose organic wastes is known as:

Explanation: Vermicomposting is the bio-oxidation and stabilization of organic material through the joint action of earthworms and microorganisms.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the principal reason for the reversion or fixation of water-soluble phosphorus in acidic soils?

Explanation: In acidic soils, water-soluble phosphorus reacts with iron and aluminum oxides to form insoluble iron and aluminum phosphates, causing fixation.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which fertilizers contain two or more primary major nutrients chemically combined?

Explanation: Complex fertilizers supply two or more primary nutrients (N, P, K) manufactured through chemical combination.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate the exact quantity of DAP required to supply 60 kg of P2O5 per hectare, given that DAP contains 18% N and 46% P2O5.

Solution & Rationale: DAP required = (Required P2O5 / % P2O5 in DAP) * 100 = (60 / 46) * 100 = 130.43 kg/ha.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which classification group does Basic Slag belong to among phosphatic fertilizers?

Solution & Rationale: Basic slag is a byproduct of the steel industry and is classified as a citrate-soluble phosphatic fertilizer.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

What happens to the availability of soil nitrogen when organic residues with a C:N ratio of 40:1 are incorporated into the soil?

Solution & Rationale: A wide C:N ratio (40:1) exceeds the critical threshold (20-30:1), leading microbial populations to scavenge available inorganic nitrogen, causing immobilization.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following oilcakes is categorized as an edible oilcake useful for both animal feed and organic manuring?

Solution & Rationale: Mustard cake is an edible oilcake with high nitrogen content, whereas neem, castor, and mahua cakes are non-edible.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the typical nutrient percentage of Nitrogen, Phosphorus, and Potassium in Vermicompost?

Solution & Rationale: Vermicompost has a higher nutrient concentration than FYM, typically containing 1.5-3.0% N, 1.0-2.0% P2O5, and 1.5-2.0% K2O.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Which chemical form of nitrogenous fertilizer leaves a residual acidic effect in the soil upon continuous application?

Solution & Rationale: Ammonium sulphate is an acid-forming fertilizer due to nitrification of the ammonium ion releasing hydrogen ions.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Rock phosphate is primarily recommended for application in which type of soil?

Solution & Rationale: Rock phosphate is insoluble in water and requires acidic soil conditions (low pH) to dissolve and release available phosphorus.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

What distinguishes straight fertilizers from complex fertilizers?

Solution & Rationale: Straight fertilizers supply a single primary nutrient (N, P, or K), whereas complex fertilizers contain two or more primary nutrients.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following secondary nutrients is supplied by Gypsum (CaSO4.2H2O)?

Solution & Rationale: Gypsum is a mineral source supplying Calcium (Ca) and Sulphur (S), and is also used for the reclamation of sodic soils.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary agronomic advantage of green manuring in crop production?

Solution & Rationale: Green manuring adds substantial organic matter, improves soil structure, and fixes biological nitrogen via leguminous crops.
