Introduction and Classification of Fertilizers

Fertilizers are commercially manufactured chemical or natural substances containing one or more essential plant nutrient elements supplied to the soil to augment natural soil fertility, correct nutrient deficiencies, and maximize crop productivity. Based on the primary nutrient supplied, fertilizers are broadly classified into straight fertilizers, complex fertilizers, and mixed fertilizers.

  • Straight Fertilizers: Contain only one primary major nutrient (N, P, or K), such as urea [CO(NH2)2], single superphosphate (SSP), and muriate of potash (KCl).
  • Complex Fertilizers: Contain at least two or three primary nutrients produced through chemical reactions, such as diammonium phosphate (DAP) containing 18% N and 46% P2O5.
  • Mixed Fertilizers: Physical mixtures of two or more straight fertilizers blended mechanically without chemical interaction.
  • Secondary and Micronutrient Fertilizers: Supply calcium, magnesium, sulfur, iron, zinc, manganese, copper, boron, molybdenum, and chlorine.

Nitrogenous Fertilizers: Chemistry and Transformation in Soil

Nitrogenous fertilizers supply elemental nitrogen, which is vital for vegetative growth, chlorophyll synthesis, and protein formation. They are categorized based on the chemical form in which nitrogen is present: nitrate fertilizers, ammoniacal fertilizers, ammonium-nitrate fertilizers, and amide fertilizers.

  • Ammoniacal Fertilizers: Contain nitrogen in the ammonium ion ( ext{NH}_4^+) form, e.g., Ammonium Sulfate [(NH_4)_2SO_4]. Highly resistant to leaching in fine-textured soils because ext{NH}_4^+ is adsorbed on soil colloidal complexes.
  • Nitrate Fertilizers: Contain nitrogen in the nitrate ion ( ext{NO}_3^-) form, e.g., Sodium Nitrate (NaNO_3). Highly mobile in soil solution, prone to leaching losses, and induces an alkaline residual effect.
  • Amide Fertilizers: Contain nitrogen in the amide group (- ext{CONH}_2), e.g., Urea [CO(NH2)2], containing 46% N. Upon soil application, it undergoes enzymatic hydrolysis via urease enzyme to form ammonium carbonate.
  • Urea Hydrolysis and Transformation: ext{CO(NH}_2)_2 + 2 ext{H}_2 ext{O} \xrightarrow{ ext{Urease}} ( ext{NH}_4)_2 ext{CO}_3 ightarrow 2 ext{NH}_4^+ + ext{CO}_2 + 2 ext{OH}^-, followed by nitrification into nitrites and nitrates.

Phosphatic Fertilizers: Classification, Solubility, and Fixation

Phosphorus plays an indispensable role in energy transfer (ATP, ADP), root development, nucleic acid synthesis, and early crop maturity. Phosphatic fertilizers are classified based on their solubility in water, neutral ammonium citrate, or mineral acids.

  • Water-Soluble Phosphatic Fertilizers: Monocalcium phosphate based. Includes Single Superphosphate (SSP, 16% ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5), Triple Superphosphate (TSP, 46% ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5), and Ammonium Phosphates. Highly effective in neutral and slightly acidic soils.
  • Citrate-Soluble Phosphatic Fertilizers: Dicalcium phosphate based. Includes Dicalcium Phosphate and Basic Slag (14–18% ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5). Suitable for slightly acidic soils.
  • Insoluble Phosphatic Fertilizers: Tricalcium phosphate based. Includes Rock Phosphate (20–40% ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5). Highly effective in strongly acidic soils due to dissolution by organic and mineral acids.
  • Phosphorus Fixation: In acidic soils, phosphorus is fixed by iron and aluminum oxides/hydroxides as insoluble Fe-phosphate and Al-phosphate. In alkaline and calcareous soils, it is fixed by calcium as insoluble calcium phosphates.

Potassic Fertilizers and Secondary/Micronutrient Fertilizers

Potassium serves as an activator for numerous enzymatic systems, regulates stomatal opening and closing, enhances translocation of assimilates, and imparts resistance against lodging, drought, and diseases.

  • Potassium Chloride (Muriate of Potash - MOP): Contains 60% ext{K}_2 ext{O}. Most common and economical potassic fertilizer. Contains chloride, making it unsuitable for chloride-sensitive crops like tobacco, potato, and grapes.
  • Potassium Sulfate (SOP): Contains 50% ext{K}_2 ext{O} and 17.5% Sulfur. Ideal for chloride-sensitive crops and high-value horticulture.
  • Secondary Nutrients: Gypsum ( ext{CaSO}_4\cdot2 ext{H}_2 ext{O}) supplies calcium and sulfur, widely used in sodic soil reclamation and oilseed fertilization.
  • Micronutrients: Zinc Sulfate ( ext{ZnSO}_4\cdot7 ext{H}_2 ext{O}, 21% Zn or 33% monohydrate) is the most critical micronutrient fertilizer applied in zinc-deficient Indian soils, particularly in intensive rice-wheat cropping systems.

Fertilizer Calculation Mathematics and Application Methods

Agronomic management requires precise calculation of fertilizer quantities based on recommended nutrient doses (N:P:K) and commercial fertilizer grades.

  • Basic Formula for Straight Fertilizer: ext{Quantity of Fertilizer} = rac{ ext{Recommended Dose of Nutrient (kg/ha)}}{\% ext{ Nutrient in Fertilizer} imes 100} imes 100
  • Urea Calculation Example: To apply 120 kg N/ha using urea (46% N): ext{Urea} = rac{120}{46} imes 100 = 260.86 ext{ kg/ha}.
  • DAP Calculation Example: To apply 60 kg ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5/ha using DAP (18% N, 46% ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5): ext{DAP} = rac{60}{46} imes 100 = 130.43 ext{ kg/ha}. Note: This DAP application also supplies 130.43 imes 0.18 = 23.47 ext{ kg N/ha}. Remaining N must be adjusted using urea.
  • Application Methods: Broadcasting, band placement, furrow placement, pellet application, foliar spraying, and fertigation through drip irrigation systems.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Urea is an organic amide fertilizer containing the highest percentage of solid nitrogen (46%).
  • Single Superphosphate (SSP) contains 16% P2O5, 12% Sulfur, and 19.5% Calcium.
  • Muriate of Potash (MOP) contains 60% K2O and is chemically known as Potassium Chloride (KCl).
  • Diammonium Phosphate (DAP) contains 18% Nitrogen and 46% Phosphorus (P2O5).
  • Biuret toxicity in crops (especially citrus) can occur if biuret content in urea exceeds 1.5%.
  • Anhydrous ammonia contains the highest concentration of nitrogen among all nitrogenous fertilizers (82%).
  • Rock phosphate is completely insoluble in water but soluble in weak organic acids, making it suitable for acidic soils.
  • Potassium sulfate (SOP) is preferred over MOP for chloride-sensitive crops like potato and tobacco.
  • Zinc sulfate (ZnSO4·7H2O) contains 21% zinc and is the most common fertilizer used to correct zinc deficiency in paddy fields.
  • Inflorescence and grain setting failure in crops is primarily linked to Boron deficiency.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing DAP's composition (18-46-0) with NPK complex fertilizers: DAP contains only N and P, no potassium.
  • Assuming rock phosphate is water-soluble: Rock phosphate is completely water-insoluble and requires acidic soil conditions for plant availability.
  • Believing MOP can be safely applied to all crops without restriction: MOP contains chloride ions which harm chloride-sensitive crops like potato, tobacco, and grapes.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Fertilizers: Classification, Chemistry, Nutrient Dynamics, and Agronomic Application

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Primary Nutrient Classification

Primary fertilizers supply nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium, which are required in large quantities by crops for optimal physiological functioning and yield formation.

  • Nitrogen: Essential for amino acids, proteins, and chlorophyll synthesis.
  • Phosphorus: Vital for root proliferation, energy storage (ATP), and nucleic acids.
  • Potassium: Crucial for enzyme activation, osmoregulation, and translocation of sugars.
Concept 2

Nitrogen Transformation and Loss Pathways

Nitrogen applied via fertilizers undergoes complex microbiological and chemical transformations in the soil environment.

  • Ureolysis: Enzymatic conversion of urea to ammonium carbonate via urease.
  • Nitrification: Two-step microbial oxidation of ammonium to nitrite (by Nitrosomonas) and nitrate (by Nitrobacter).
  • Denitrification: Reduction of nitrates to gaseous nitrogen ( ext{N}_2 or ext{N}_2 ext{O}) under anaerobic waterlogged conditions.
Concept 3

Phosphorus Fixation Dynamics

Phosphorus availability is heavily governed by soil pH due to precipitation and adsorption reactions.

  • Acidic Soils: P is fixed by iron ( ext{Fe}) and aluminum ( ext{Al}) oxides as insoluble phosphates.
  • Alkaline Soils: P is fixed by calcium ( ext{Ca}) as dicalcium and tricalcium phosphates.
  • Optimal Availability: Maximum phosphorus availability occurs in the pH range of 6.0 to 7.0.
Concept 4

Fertilizer Calculation Methodology

Agronomic nutrient management requires strict mathematical calculation to convert crop nutrient recommendations into actual commercial fertilizer quantities.

  • Formula: ext{Fertilizer Weight} = rac{ ext{Recommended Nutrient Rate}}{\% ext{ Nutrient in Fertilizer}} imes 100.
  • Multiple Nutrient Adjustment: When using complex fertilizers like DAP, calculate P requirement first, then determine the residual N supplied by DAP and top-dress the balance using straight nitrogenous fertilizers like urea.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Straight Fertilizer (Single Nutrient Fertilizer)
A fertilizer containing only one primary major plant nutrient (N, P, or K).
Complex Fertilizer (Multi-Nutrient Chemical Fertilizer)
A fertilizer containing two or three primary nutrients produced through chemical reaction.
Biuret (Carbamylurea ( ext{C}_2 ext{H}_5 ext{N}_3 ext{O}_2))
A toxic impurity formed during industrial manufacturing of urea that causes foliar chlorosis and necrosis if concentration exceeds 1.5%.
Nitrification (Biological Oxidation of Ammonium)
Microbial conversion of ext{NH}_4^+ to ext{NO}_2^- and subsequently to ext{NO}_3^- by autotrophic bacteria.
Denitrification (Microbial Reduction of Nitrate)
Reduction of soil nitrates to gaseous nitrogen forms under anaerobic waterlogged conditions.
Phosphorus Fixation (P Adsorption and Precipitation)
Conversion of soluble orthophosphate ions into insoluble compounds with Fe, Al, or Ca in soil.
Muriate of Potash (Potassium Chloride ( ext{KCl}))
The most common potassic fertilizer containing 60% ext{K}_2 ext{O} and high chloride content.
Single Superphosphate (SSP ( ext{Ca(H}_2 ext{PO}_4)_2 + ext{CaSO}_4))
A water-soluble phosphatic fertilizer containing 16% ext{P}_2 ext{O}_5, 12% sulfur, and 19.5% calcium.
Fertigation (Application of Fertilizers via Irrigation)
The injection of water-soluble fertilizers into irrigation water systems (drip or sprinkler).
Anhydrous Ammonia (Liquefied Ammonia Gas ( ext{NH}_3))
The most concentrated nitrogenous fertilizer containing 82% elemental nitrogen.

  • SSP P2O5 content: 16% (plus 12% Sulfur)
  • MOP K2O content: 60%
  • Maximum safe biuret limit in urea: 1.5%

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Fertilizers are classified into straight, complex, and mixed types based on nutrient composition.
  • Urea contains 46% nitrogen in amide form and requires urease enzyme hydrolysis.
  • Phosphatic fertilizers are categorized by solubility into water-soluble, citrate-soluble, and insoluble forms.
  • Potassium chloride (MOP) supplies 60% K2O but is unsuitable for chloride-sensitive crops.
  • Accurate fertilizer dose calculation requires accounting for the exact nutrient percentage in the commercial product.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Ammoniacal Fertilizers VS Nitrate Fertilizers

Ammoniacal fertilizers contain NH4+ which resists leaching due to soil colloid adsorption, whereas nitrate fertilizers contain NO3- which is highly mobile and prone to leaching.

Single Superphosphate VS Triple Superphosphate

SSP contains 16% P2O5 along with calcium and sulfur, whereas TSP contains 46% P2O5 with no sulfur.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the percentage of elemental nitrogen present in commercial grade urea?

Explanation: Commercial grade urea [CO(NH2)2] contains exactly 46% elemental nitrogen by weight. 20.6% is found in ammonium sulfate, and 82% in anhydrous ammonia.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which bacterium is primarily responsible for converting nitrite to nitrate during soil nitrification?

Explanation: Nitrification occurs in two steps: Nitrosomonas oxidizes ammonium (NH4+) to nitrite (NO2-), and Nitrobacter oxidizes nitrite (NO2-) to nitrate (NO3-).

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which phosphatic fertilizer contains 16% P2O5 and approximately 12% Sulfur?

Explanation: Single Superphosphate (SSP) contains 16% P2O5, 19.5% Calcium, and 12% Sulfur because sulfuric acid is used in its manufacturing process.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the maximum permissible limit of biuret in urea fertilizer to avoid crop toxicity?

Explanation: The maximum permissible biuret limit in agricultural grade urea is 1.5%. Higher concentrations cause phytotoxicity, particularly during foliar application.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following potassic fertilizers is strictly recommended for chloride-sensitive crops like potato and tobacco?

Explanation: Potassium Sulfate (SOP) contains no chloride and provides sulfur, making it ideal for chloride-sensitive crops like tobacco, potato, and grapes.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the nutrient composition (N-P2O5-K2O) of Diammonium Phosphate (DAP)?

Explanation: Diammonium Phosphate (DAP) contains 18% Nitrogen and 46% Phosphorus (P2O5) with 0% Potassium, expressed as 18-46-0.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In strongly acidic soils, phosphorus fixation is predominantly caused by which elements?

Explanation: In acidic soils (pH < 5.5), soluble phosphorus reacts with iron (Fe) and aluminum (Al) to form highly insoluble iron and aluminum phosphates.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which nitrogenous fertilizer leaves a basic (alkaline) residual effect in the soil?

Explanation: Sodium Nitrate (Chilean saltpeter) leaves a basic residual effect because plant uptake of nitrate ions exceeds sodium uptake, leaving behind basic hydroxyl or sodium residues.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

How much quantity of urea is required to supply 60 kg of actual Nitrogen?

Explanation: Calculation: (60 / 46) * 100 = 130.43 kg of urea.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which fertilizer contains the highest concentration of nitrogen among all commercial nitrogenous fertilizers?

Explanation: Anhydrous ammonia is a pressurized gas containing 82% elemental nitrogen, the highest among all nitrogenous fertilizers.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Rock phosphate is best suited for application in which type of soils?

Explanation: Rock phosphate is water-insoluble. It dissolves effectively only in strongly acidic soils where soil acids release the bound phosphorus.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of potassium in plant physiological processes?

Explanation: Potassium regulates stomatal opening and closing (osmoregulation), activates over 60 enzymes, and improves disease resistance and lodging tolerance.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical compound is formed when urea undergoes hydrolysis in the soil?

Explanation: Urea reacts with water in the presence of urease enzyme to form ammonium carbonate [(NH4)2CO3], which subsequently releases ammonium ions and carbon dioxide.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which secondary nutrient is supplied in substantial amounts by Single Superphosphate (SSP)?

Explanation: SSP contains 19.5% calcium and 12% sulfur derived from the sulfuric acid treatment of rock phosphate during manufacturing.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the K2O percentage in Muriate of Potash (MOP)?

Explanation: Muriate of Potash (Potassium Chloride, KCl) contains 60% potassium oxide equivalent (K2O).

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which fertilizer application method is most efficient for sandy soils prone to heavy leaching losses of nitrogen?

Explanation: Split application of nitrogenous fertilizers ensures that nutrients match crop demand phases, minimizing leaching losses in sandy soils.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which micronutrient fertilizer is most commonly applied to correct 'Khaira' disease in rice?

Explanation: Khaira disease in rice is caused by Zinc deficiency and is successfully corrected by applying Zinc Sulfate (ZnSO4).

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary loss pathway of nitrogen under waterlogged anaerobic paddy field conditions?

Explanation: Under waterlogged anaerobic conditions, nitrates are reduced by denitrifying bacteria into nitrogen gases (N2 and N2O), which escape into the atmosphere.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which fertilizer is classified as a citrate-soluble phosphatic fertilizer?

Explanation: Dicalcium phosphate and basic slag are citrate-soluble phosphatic fertilizers, making them suitable for slightly acidic soils.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does the term 'grade' of a fertilizer signify on commercial bags?

Explanation: The fertilizer grade represents the guaranteed minimum percentage of total nitrogen (N), available phosphorus (P2O5), and soluble potassium (K2O) in that order.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following transformations represents the correct sequence of nitrogen transformations in flooded rice soils when urea is applied as a basal dose?

Solution & Rationale: Urea (amide) is first hydrolyzed to ammonium in the soil. Under aerobic surface layers, ammonium is nitrified to nitrate. When nitrate moves down to the anaerobic sub-surface layer, it undergoes denitrification into nitrogen gas.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

Why is Single Superphosphate (SSP) preferred over Diammonium Phosphate (DAP) in sulfur-deficient soils?

Solution & Rationale: SSP contains 12% sulfur and 19.5% calcium, making it an excellent fertilizer for correcting sulfur deficiencies in oilseeds and pulses, whereas DAP contains no sulfur.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary agronomic consequence of continuous application of ammonium sulfate without lime amendment?

Solution & Rationale: Nitrification of ammonium from ammonium sulfate releases hydrogen ions (H+), leading to progressive soil acidification and depletion of basic cations.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

Which fertilizer is classified as a straight, water-soluble, neutral potassium fertilizer containing sulfur?

Solution & Rationale: Potassium Sulfate (SOP) is a straight, water-soluble potassium fertilizer that contains 50% K2O and 17.5% Sulfur, leaving a neutral residual soil reaction.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What happens to the availability of phosphorus when applied to highly calcareous soils with high pH (> 8.0)?

Solution & Rationale: In calcareous soils with high pH, applied soluble phosphate reacts rapidly with calcium ions (Ca2+) to precipitate as relatively insoluble dicalcium and tricalcium phosphates, reducing P availability.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following fertilizers is hygroscopic and requires special moisture-proof packaging?

Solution & Rationale: Ammonium nitrate is highly hygroscopic, readily absorbing moisture from the atmosphere and caking, which requires specialized moisture-proof bags and coating agents.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

In fertigation systems (drip irrigation), why are water-soluble fertilizers specifically required?

Solution & Rationale: Insoluble or partially soluble fertilizers cause physical blockage of micro-irrigation emitters and pipes; hence, fully water-soluble fertilizers are mandatory in fertigation.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Which test or analytical parameter is used to determine the neutralizing value of basic slag or liming materials used alongside fertilizers?

Solution & Rationale: Calcium Carbonate Equivalent (CCE) measures the acid-neutralizing capacity of liming materials and basic slag relative to pure calcium carbonate.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which fertilizer property is denoted by the term 'Equivalent Acidity'?

Solution & Rationale: Equivalent acidity is defined as the number of parts by weight of calcium carbonate required to neutralize the acidity produced by 100 parts by weight of the fertilizer.
