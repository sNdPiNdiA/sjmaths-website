Introduction to Plant Nutrients and Manurial Classification

Soil fertility management is the cornerstone of modern agronomy and crop production economics. Plant nutrients are categorized based on their quantitative requirement into essential macro-nutrients (Primary: Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium; Secondary: Calcium, Magnesium, Sulfur) and micro-nutrients or trace elements (Iron, Manganese, Zinc, Copper, Boron, Molybdenum, Chlorine, Nickel). To maintain optimal soil physical, chemical, and biological properties, soil amendments are divided into organic manures and inorganic (mineral) fertilizers.

  • Bulky Organic Manures: Farmyard Manure (FYM), compost, and green manures characterized by low percentage of nutrients and large bulk volumes.
  • Concentrated Organic Manures: Oilcakes (castor, mustard, groundnut), blood meal, and bone meal with relatively higher nutrient concentrations.
  • Inorganic Fertilizers: Synthetic chemical compounds supplying specific primary or secondary nutrients with high solubility and rapid availability.

Bulky Organic Manures: Composition, Preparation, and Nutrient Dynamics

Bulky organic manures improve soil structure, water retention capacity, cation exchange capacity (CEC), and microbial population. Farmyard Manure (FYM) is a decomposed mixture of cattle dung, urine, and litter (bedding material). Ideal well-rotted FYM contains on average 0.5% Nitrogen (N), 0.2% Phosphorus pentoxide (P_2O_5), and 0.5% Potassium oxide (K_2O).

  • Nutrient Losses: Substantial losses of nitrogen occur through volatilization of ammonia (NH_3) and leaching if manure pits are exposed to sun and rain. Use of superphosphate or gypsum as chemical preservatives reduces nitrogen loss.
  • Composting Methods: The Indore method (aerobic decomposition taking 4 to 5 months) and the Bangalore method (anaerobic decomposition taking 6 to 8 months) are standard scientific composting techniques.
  • Green Manuring: Incorporation of green plant tissues grown in-situ or brought from outside to enrich soil nitrogen and organic matter. Common green manure crops include Crotalaria juncea (sunn hemp), Sesbania aculeata (dhaincha), and Vigna unguiculata (cowpea).

Inorganic Fertilizers: Classification, Chemistry, and Nutrient Analysis

Inorganic fertilizers supply high concentrations of available plant nutrients instantly. They are classified into straight fertilizers (supplying one primary nutrient) and complex fertilizers (supplying two or three primary nutrients simultaneously).

  • Nitrogenous Fertilizers: Urea (CO(NH_2)_2) contains 46% N, Ammonium Sulfate ((NH_4)_2SO_4) contains 21% N and 24% S, and Calcium Ammonium Nitrate (CAN) contains 25% N. Urea undergoes enzymatic hydrolysis by urease to form ammonium carbonate, which can volatilize into ammonia if surface-applied on alkaline soils.
  • Phosphatic Fertilizers: Single Superphosphate (SSP) contains 16% P_2O_5, 12% Sulfur, and 19.5% Calcium. Diammonium Phosphate (DAP) contains 18% N and 46% P_2O_5. Rock phosphate is insoluble and used primarily in acidic soils.
  • Potassic Fertilizers: Muriate of Potash (MOP or Potassium Chloride, KCl) contains 60% K_2O, and Potassium Sulfate (K_2SO_4) contains 50% K_2O along with 18% Sulfur, preferred for chloride-sensitive crops like potato and tobacco.

Fertilizer Calculations and Integrated Nutrient Management (INM)

Agronomists must calculate exact commercial fertilizer quantities based on recommended nutrient doses (N:P_2O_5:K_2O kg/ha). The standard formula for fertilizer calculation is:

Fertilizer Quantity (kg) = (Recommended Nutrient Dose (kg/ha) / Percentage of Nutrient in Fertilizer) × 100

  • Urea Requirement: To supply 100 kg N/ha, Urea required = (100 / 46) \times 100 = 217.39 kg/ha.
  • DAP and Urea Split: When DAP is used to supply phosphorus, it also supplies nitrogen. This nitrogen contribution must be deducted from the total recommended nitrogen dose before calculating the remaining urea requirement.
  • Integrated Nutrient Management (INM): The maintenance of soil fertility and plant nutrient supply at an optimum level for sustaining the desired crop productivity through the optimization of the benefits from all possible sources of organic, inorganic, and biological components in an integrated manner.

Nutrient Transformations, Fixation, and Losses in Soils

Once applied to the soil, fertilizers undergo complex chemical and biological transformations that dictate nutrient availability and environmental impact.

  • Nitrogen Transformations: Ammonification converts organic nitrogen to ammonium (NH_4^+). Nitrification mediated by Nitrosomonas converts NH_4^+ to nitrite (NO_2^-), and Nitrobacter converts NO_2^- to nitrate (NO_3^-). Nitrate is prone to leaching loss and denitrification under waterlogged anaerobic conditions.
  • Phosphorus Fixation: In acidic soils, soluble orthophosphates react with iron (Fe) and aluminum (Al) to form insoluble iron and aluminum phosphates. In alkaline calcareous soils, phosphorus reacts with calcium (Ca) to form insoluble tricalcium phosphate.
  • Potassium Dynamics: Potassium exists in soil as readily available exchangeable K, non-exchangeable fixed K in clay mineral interlayers, and structural K in primary minerals.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Assuming organic manures supply instantaneous high nutrient concentrations like chemical fertilizers, whereas organic manures release nutrients slowly over time through mineralization.
  • Confusing the N percentage of Urea (46%) with Ammonium Sulfate (21%), leading to erroneous field calculation doses.
  • Believing Indore composting method is anaerobic; Indore is strictly aerobic requiring periodic turning of organic mass.

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Classification and Role of Soil Amendments

Soil amendments are vital inputs for crop production that supply essential plant nutrients and ameliorate unfavorable soil physical and chemical environments.

  • Macro-nutrients: Essential elements required in large quantities, divided into primary (N, P, K) and secondary (Ca, Mg, S).
  • Organic Manures: Bulky or concentrated carbon-based materials that improve soil organic matter, microbial activity, and physical structure.
Concept 2

Composting Technology and Nutrient Conservation

Composting converts raw organic wastes into stabilized humus-like substances through controlled microbial decomposition.

  • Indore Method: Aerobic process utilizing alternate layers of animal dung, crop residues, and soil, taking 4 to 5 months.
  • Bangalore Method: Trench-based anaerobic process taking 6 to 8 months with high nitrogen conservation.
Concept 3

Inorganic Fertilizer Chemistry and Grades

Inorganic fertilizers provide concentrated, water-soluble plant nutrients for immediate crop uptake and rapid yield response.

  • Urea: Amide fertilizer containing 46% N, hydrolyzing rapidly in soil to ammonium carbonate.
  • SSP: Phosphatic fertilizer providing 16% P2O5 alongside essential sulfur and calcium.
Concept 4

Nutrient Cycling and Loss Pathways

Nutrient transformations dictate fertilizer efficiency and environmental safety in agricultural ecosystems.

  • Nitrification: Two-step biological oxidation of ammonium to nitrate via Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter.
  • Fixation: Chemical binding of soluble phosphorus with iron, aluminum, or calcium, rendering it temporarily unavailable in soils.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Farmyard Manure (FYM)
A decomposed mixture of cattle dung, urine, and bedding straw containing roughly 0.5% N, 0.2% P2O5, and 0.5% K2O.
Green Manuring (In-situ green plant incorporation)
The practice of ploughing undecomposed green plant tissues into the soil to enrich organic matter and nitrogen.
Nitrification (Biological oxidation of ammonium to nitrate)
Microbial process where ammonium is converted to nitrite by Nitrosomonas and further to nitrate by Nitrobacter.
Denitrification (Microbial reduction of nitrate to nitrogen gas)
Anaerobic microbial reduction of soil nitrates into nitrous oxide and nitrogen gas, causing nutrient loss.
C/N Ratio (Carbon to Nitrogen Ratio)
Proportion of organic carbon to total nitrogen in organic residues, critical for determining decomposition rate.
Mineralization (Biological conversion of organic to inorganic form)
The microbial conversion of organically bound plant nutrients into plant-available inorganic mineral ions.
Immobilization (Microbial assimilation of inorganic nutrients)
The conversion of inorganic soil nutrients into organic microbial biomass, temporarily rendering them unavailable to crops.
Single Superphosphate (SSP)
A straight phosphatic fertilizer containing 16% P2O5, 12% sulfur, and 19.5% calcium.
Muriate of Potash (Potassium Chloride (KCl))
The most widely used potassic fertilizer containing 60% K2O.
Integrated Nutrient Management (INM)
The holistic management of soil fertility combining organic manures, biofertilizers, and chemical fertilizers.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Average FYM nutrient status: 0.5% N, 0.2% P2O5, 0.5% K2O.
  • Urea nitrogen content is exactly 46%.
  • MOP potassium oxide content is exactly 60%.
  • Indore method takes 4-5 months; Bangalore method takes 6-8 months.
  • C/N ratio of well-decomposed farm compost is around 10:1 to 12:1.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Bulky organic manures supply low nutrient percentages but vastly improve soil physical conditions.
  • Indore composting method is aerobic, while Bangalore composting method is anaerobic.
  • Urea is the most prominent nitrogenous fertilizer containing 46% N in amide form.
  • Single Superphosphate supplies phosphorus, sulfur, and calcium simultaneously.
  • Nitrification is executed by Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter bacteria in aerated soils.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Mineralization VS Immobilization

Mineralization releases inorganic nutrients from organic matter, whereas immobilization ties up inorganic nutrients into microbial cells.

Indore Method VS Bangalore Method

Indore method is aerobic and requires frequent turning; Bangalore method is anaerobic and requires no turning.

Which composting method developed in India operates under strictly anaerobic conditions in trenches?

What is the exact percentage of nitrogen contained in commercial Urea fertilizer?

Explanation: Urea contains 46% nitrogen in amide form, making it the most concentrated dry solid nitrogenous fertilizer.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which bacterium is primarily responsible for converting ammonium into nitrite during nitrification?

Explanation: Nitrosomonas bacteria oxidize ammonium (NH_4^+) to nitrite (NO_2^-), which is subsequently converted to nitrate by Nitrobacter.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Single Superphosphate (SSP) supplies which secondary nutrient along with phosphorus?

Explanation: SSP contains 16% P2O5, 12% Sulfur, and 19.5% Calcium, making it a multi-nutrient carrier.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the potassium oxide (K2O) content in Muriate of Potash (MOP)?

Explanation: Muriate of Potash (KCl) contains 60% K2O equivalent and is the principal potassic fertilizer used globally.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a leguminous green manure crop commonly used in Indian agriculture?

Explanation: Sesbania aculeata (dhaincha) is a prominent leguminous green manure crop capable of fixing atmospheric nitrogen and adding organic matter.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the C/N ratio of microbial-stabilized humus in agricultural soils?

Explanation: The C/N ratio of stable soil organic matter (humus) is approximately 10:1, reflecting equilibrium between soil microbes and organic carbon.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical compound is commonly added to manure pits to prevent nitrogen loss through ammonia volatilization?

Explanation: Gypsum acts as a chemical preservative in FYM by reacting with volatile ammonium carbonate to form non-volatile ammonium sulfate.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does INM stand for in modern agronomic nutrient management?

Explanation: INM stands for Integrated Nutrient Management, combining organic manures, crop residues, biofertilizers, and chemical fertilizers for sustainable fertility.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In which soil condition is phosphorus fixation most pronounced due to iron and aluminum precipitation?

Explanation: In acidic soils, soluble orthophosphates precipitate with active iron and aluminum to form insoluble iron and aluminum phosphates.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary nutrient supplied by bone meal, a concentrated organic manure?

Explanation: Steamed bone meal is a concentrated organic manure valued primarily for its high phosphorus content (20-25% P2O5).

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which bacteria complete the second step of nitrification converting nitrite into nitrate?

Explanation: Nitrobacter oxidizes nitrite (NO_2^-) into nitrate (NO_3^-), which is readily absorbed by plant roots.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended quantity of Urea required to supply 100 kg of pure Nitrogen per hectare?

Explanation: Urea contains 46% N. Therefore, Urea required = (100 / 46) \times 100 = 217.39 kg/ha.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a complex fertilizer supplying more than one primary nutrient?

Explanation: Diammonium Phosphate (DAP) contains 18% N and 46% P2O5, making it a prominent complex fertilizer.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary cause of nitrogen loss under waterlogged anaerobic paddy field conditions?

Explanation: Denitrification occurs when anaerobic bacteria reduce soil nitrates (NO_3^-) to gaseous nitrogen (N_2), which escapes into the atmosphere.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Who developed the Indore method of aerobic composting?

Explanation: Sir Albert Howard and Y.D. Wad developed the Indore method of aerobic composting at the Institute of Plant Industry, Indore.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which fertilizer is strictly prohibited or discouraged for chloride-sensitive crops like tobacco and potato?

Explanation: Muriate of Potash contains high chloride levels, which adversely affect the quality and burning properties of tobacco and starch content in potatoes.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the biological process where soil microorganisms convert inorganic nutrient ions into organic forms within their biomass?

Explanation: Immobilization is the microbial assimilation of inorganic soil nutrients into organic compounds, making them temporarily unavailable to plants.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Why is Potassium Sulfate (K_2SO_4) preferred over Muriate of Potash (KCl) for potato cultivation in saline-prone soils?

Solution & Rationale: Potassium Sulfate supplies 50% K2O and 18% Sulfur while avoiding chloride toxicity, which impairs starch accumulation in tubers.

Test Question 3 of 10 PST Standard

In the context of organic matter decomposition, what happens when crop residues with a C/N ratio greater than 30:1 are incorporated into the soil?

Solution & Rationale: Residues with high C/N ratios (>30:1) cause soil microbes to scavenge available inorganic nitrogen, leading to temporary nitrogen immobilization.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

Which chemical reaction characterizes phosphorus fixation in calcareous (alkaline) soils?

Solution & Rationale: In high pH calcareous soils, soluble phosphates react with calcium ions to form relatively insoluble tricalcium phosphate.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary distinction between the Indore and Bangalore composting methods?

Solution & Rationale: Indore method is aerobic and involves periodic turning, whereas Bangalore method is anaerobic and trench-based without turning.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

Which nitrogenous fertilizer leaves an acidic residual effect on agricultural soils upon continuous long-term application?

Solution & Rationale: Ammonium Sulfate leaves an acidic residual effect due to nitrification of the ammonium ion producing hydrogen ions (H^+).

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the theoretical nitrogen contribution of Diammonium Phosphate (DAP) when applying 100 kg of P2O5 per hectare?

Solution & Rationale: DAP contains 18% N and 46% P2O5. For 100 kg P2O5, DAP required = (100 / 46) \times 100 = 217.39 kg. Nitrogen supplied = 217.39 \times 0.18 = 39.1 kg N.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which stage of the nitrogen cycle is directly inhibited by soil waterlogging and anaerobic conditions?

Solution & Rationale: Nitrification is an aerobic biological oxidation process requiring oxygen; hence, waterlogging halts nitrification by depriving nitrifying bacteria of oxygen.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary agronomic benefit of green manuring with leguminous crops before paddy cultivation?

Solution & Rationale: Leguminous green manures fix atmospheric nitrogen via root nodules and enrich soil organic matter, improving physical structure and fertility for subsequent crops.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following organic manures has the highest nitrogen concentration among oilcakes?

Solution & Rationale: Groundnut cake is a concentrated organic manure with higher nitrogen content (~7.9% N) compared to castor (~4.3%) and neem (~5.2%).
