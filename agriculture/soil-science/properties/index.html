Soil Properties: Physical, Chemical, and Biological Characteristics

Physical Properties of Soil

Soil physical properties dictate root penetration, water movement, aeration, and mechanical resistance. Understanding these parameters is essential for evaluating soil suitability for intensive crop production.

  • Soil Texture: The relative proportion of sand (2.0 - 0.05 mm), silt (0.05 - 0.002 mm), and clay (< 0.002 mm) particles as defined by the USDA particle size classification system. Textural classes are determined using the textural triangle.
  • Soil Structure: The spatial arrangement and aggregation of primary soil particles into secondary units called peds. Major structural types include granular, crumb, platy, prismatic, columnar, and blocky. Granular structures are most ideal for agricultural crop production due to high macro-porosity.
  • Bulk Density ( ho_b): Mass of dry soil per unit bulk volume, typically ranging from 1.1 to 1.6 Mg m-3 in mineral soils. Compacted soils exhibit higher bulk density, restricting root elongation.
  • Particle Density ( ho_s): Density of the solid soil particles alone, averaging approximately 2.65 Mg m-3 for quartz-dominated mineral soils.
  • Total Porosity (f): Calculated via the formula f = \left(1 - ρ_bρ_s\right) × 100, representing the fraction of soil volume occupied by air and water.

Chemical Properties and Soil Reaction

Chemical properties govern nutrient availability, buffering capacity, and toxic ion concentrations in the rhizosphere. Soil reaction (pH) is the master variable influencing all chemical equilibria.

  • Soil pH: Negative logarithm of hydrogen ion activity (-\log[H^+]). Acidic soils (pH < 6.5) often suffer from aluminum and manganese toxicity alongside phosphorus fixation by iron and aluminum oxides. Alkaline soils (pH > 7.5) face micronutrient deficiencies (Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu) and sodium hazard.
  • Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC): The total quantity of exchangeable cations a soil can adsorb, expressed in centimoles of charge per kilogram of dry soil (cmol(p^+)/kg). It is primarily a function of clay mineralogy and soil organic matter content.
  • Base Saturation Percentage: The percentage of CEC occupied by basic cations (Ca^{2+}, Mg^{2+}, K^+, Na^+) rather than acidic cations (H^+, Al^{3+}).
  • Electrical Conductivity (EC): Measured in dS/m at 25°C, representing the total soluble salt concentration in the soil saturation extract. Values above 4.0 dS/m indicate salinity stress.

Clay Mineralogy and Ion Exchange Dynamics

The colloidal fraction of soil is dominated by layer silicates (phyllosilicates) and hydrous oxides of iron and aluminum. The nature of these colloids dictates CEC and swelling-shrinking behavior.

  • 1:1 Type Silicates (e.g., Kaolinite): Composed of one tetrahedral silica sheet and one octahedral alumina sheet. Hydrogen bonding holds layers together, preventing expansion. CEC is very low (3–15 cmol(p^+)/kg).
  • 2:1 Expanding Type Silicates (e.g., Montmorillonite): Composed of two tetrahedral sheets sandwiching one octahedral sheet. Isomorphic substitution generates permanent negative charge. Water enters between layers, causing severe swelling and shrinking. CEC is high (80–150 cmol(p^+)/kg).
  • 2:1 Non-Expanding Type Silicates (e.g., Illite): Similar to expanding 2:1 clays, but potassium ions are fixed in the interlayer spaces, preventing expansion and maintaining a moderate CEC (20–40 cmol(p^+)/kg).
  • Isomorphic Substitution: The replacement of one ion by another of similar size within the crystal lattice without changing the crystal structure (e.g., Al^{3+} replacing Si^{4+} in tetrahedral sheets), creating permanent negative charge.

Soil Organic Matter and Biological Properties

Soil organic matter (SOM) and biological activity form the cornerstone of soil health, nutrient cycling, and structural stability.

  • Composition of SOM: Comprises living biomass, fresh plant residues, active fraction detritus, and highly stable humic substances (humic acid, fulvic acid, and humin).
  • Carbon-to-Nitrogen (C:N) Ratio: Microbial decomposition efficiency depends on the C:N ratio. An optimum C:N ratio of approximately 10:1 to 12:1 matches microbial demand. Wide C:N ratios (> 30:1) induce temporary nitrogen immobilization.
  • Soil Microbial Biomass: Bacteria, actinomycetes, fungi, algae, and protozoa drive biogeochemical cycles including nitrification, denitrification, biological nitrogen fixation (e.g., Rhizobium, Azotobacter), and sulfur oxidation.
  • Enzymatic Activity: Soil enzymes such as urease, phosphatase, dehydrogenase, and beta-glucosidase serve as sensitive bio-indicators of soil metabolic health and fertility status.

Problem Soils and Management Interventions

Abnormal soil chemical and physical properties require specialized reclamation technologies to restore agricultural productivity.

  • Saline Soils: Characterized by EC > 4.0\ dS/m, exchangeable sodium percentage (ESP) < 15, and pH < 8.5. Reclaimed primarily through leaching excess soluble salts using good quality irrigation water coupled with adequate subsurface drainage.
  • Sodic (Alkali) Soils: Characterized by EC < 4.0\ dS/m, ESP > 15, and pH > 8.5 due to sodium carbonate and bicarbonate dominance. Reclaimed using chemical amendments such as agricultural gypsum (CaSO_4 · 2H_2O) to replace exchangeable sodium with calcium.
  • Acid Soils: Reclaimed through the application of liming materials such as calcitic limestone (CaCO_3), dolomitic limestone (CaCO_3 · MgCO_3), or basic slag to neutralize hydrogen and aluminum ions, raising pH to the optimum range of 6.5–7.0.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Ideal bulk density of agricultural mineral soil ranges between 1.1 to 1.4 Mg m-3.
  • Particle density of mineral soil is universally assumed to be constant at 2.65 Mg m-3 (density of quartz).
  • Montmorillonite clay exhibits the highest swelling and shrinking behavior due to internal surface expansion and water adsorption.
  • Gypsum requirement formula for sodic soils is calculated based on Exchangeable Sodium Percentage (ESP) reduction and gypsum purity.
  • Soil pH is measured in a 1:2.5 soil-to-water suspension using a glass electrode pH meter.
  • Cation exchange capacity (CEC) is expressed in centimoles of charge per kilogram of dry soil (cmol(p^+)/kg).
  • Kaolinite is a 1:1 type non-expanding clay mineral held together by strong hydrogen bonding.
  • A C:N ratio wider than 30:1 leads to microbial immobilization of available soil nitrogen.
  • Electrical Conductivity (EC) of saturation extract at 25°C is the standard index of soil salinity.
  • Agricultural lime (CaCO_3) is the standard amendment used for reclaiming acidic soils.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing particle density with bulk density: Particle density only considers solid mineral volume (2.65 Mg m-3), whereas bulk density includes pore spaces.
  • Assuming sodic soils have low pH: Sodic soils possess high pH (> 8.5) due to sodium carbonate hydrolysis, unlike saline soils which have pH < 8.5.
  • Confusing permanent charge with pH-dependent charge: Permanent charge arises from isomorphic substitution in silicate clays, whereas variable charge arises from exposed hydroxyl groups on edges of clays and organic matter.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Soil Properties: Physical, Chemical, and Biological Characteristics

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Physical Architecture and Porosity

Soil physical characteristics define the root environment. Total porosity is inversely proportional to bulk density.

  • Bulk Density: Mass of dry soil per unit bulk volume (Mg\ m^{-3}).
  • Porosity: Volume fraction of soil not occupied by solids, typically 50% in ideal loam soils.
Concept 2

Colloidal Chemistry and Ion Exchange

Colloids possess negative charges that attract plant nutrients. Clay minerals and humus form the colloidal complex.

  • CEC: Capacity of soil to hold and exchange cations with soil solution.
  • Isomorphic Substitution: Substitution within crystal lattices creating permanent negative charge.
Concept 3

Soil Reaction and Buffering

Soil pH regulates nutrient availability and microbial populations. Most crops perform best in neutral to slightly acidic pH (6.5–7.5).

  • Buffering Capacity: The ability of soil to resist sudden changes in pH upon addition of acids or bases.
Concept 4

Problem Soils and Reclamation

Saline, sodic, and acid soils require specific chemical and physical amendments to restore fertility.

  • Gypsum (CaSO_4 · 2H_2O): Primary chemical amendment for reclaiming sodic soils.
  • Liming: Essential intervention for neutralizing active acidity in acid soils.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Bulk Density (Mass per unit bulk volume ( ho_b))
The dry mass of soil divided by its total volume (including pore space), expressed in Mg\ m^{-3} or g\ cm^{-3}.
Particle Density (Density of solid soil particles ( ho_s))
The mass of solid soil particles divided by the solid volume, averaging 2.65\ Mg\ m^{-3}.
Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC)
The total sum of exchangeable cations that a soil can adsorb, expressed in cmol(p^+)/kg.
Electrical Conductivity (EC)
A measure of ionic concentration in soil solution, used to quantify soil salinity (dS/m at 25°C).
Exchangeable Sodium Percentage (ESP)
The percentage of cation exchange capacity occupied by sodium ions.
Isomorphic Substitution (Lattice ion replacement)
Replacement of an ion by another of similar ionic radius in crystal lattices without altering crystal structure.
Soil Structure (Ped aggregation)
The combination or arrangement of primary soil particles into secondary structural units called peds.
Soil Texture (Mechanical composition)
The relative proportions of sand, silt, and clay particles in a given soil mass.
Humus (Amorphous colloidal SOM)
Complex, dark-colored, highly stable amorphous organic compounds resistant to rapid microbial decomposition.
Base Saturation (Percentage basic cations on exchange complex)
Proportion of CEC saturated with exchangeable basic cations (Ca^{2+}, Mg^{2+}, K^+, Na^+).

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Standard particle density of mineral soil is 2.65 Mg m^-3.
  • Montmorillonite clay has the highest cation exchange capacity (80–150 cmol(+)/kg).
  • Gypsum is the universally recommended amendment for sodic soil reclamation.
  • Optimum C:N ratio for rapid microbial decomposition without nitrogen immobilization is 10:1 to 12:1.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Soil physical properties include texture, structure, bulk density, particle density, and porosity.
  • Chemical properties are governed by soil pH, CEC, base saturation, and electrical conductivity.
  • Silicate clays are categorized into 1:1 (kaolinite) and 2:1 types (montmorillonite, illite).
  • Saline soils have EC > 4.0 dS/m, ESP < 15, and pH < 8.5; sodic soils have EC < 4.0 dS/m, ESP > 15, and pH > 8.5.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Saline Soil VS Sodic Soil

Saline soils have excess neutral soluble salts with EC > 4 dS/m and pH < 8.5, whereas sodic soils have excess exchangeable sodium with ESP > 15 and pH > 8.5.

Bulk Density VS Particle Density

Bulk density includes pore spaces in volume calculations, whereas particle density considers only solid mineral volume.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What causes the permanent negative charge on silicate clay minerals?

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

If a soil has a bulk density of 1.35 Mg m^-3 and a particle density of 2.65 Mg m^-3, what is its total percent porosity?

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

Which silicate clay mineral possesses the highest specific surface area and swelling capacity due to interlayer expansion?

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary reason why P fixation occurs in strongly acidic soils?

Solution & Rationale: In acidic soils, soluble phosphate ions react with active iron and aluminum to form insoluble iron and aluminum phosphates, causing P fixation.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following describes the mechanism of gypsum application in sodic soil reclamation?

Solution & Rationale: Gypsum (CaSO_4 · 2H_2O) supplies Ca^{2+} which exchanges with Na^+ on the exchange complex, forming soluble Na_2SO_4 which is then leached out.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What factor primarily dictates the permanent negative charge of 2:1 layer silicate clays?

Solution & Rationale: Isomorphic substitution of lower valence cations for higher valence cations within the crystal lattice generates permanent negative charge.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Why is the C:N ratio of organic residues critical in determining nitrogen availability in soil?

Solution & Rationale: A C:N ratio > 30:1 forces soil microbes to immobilize available inorganic nitrogen to build microbial biomass.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which textural class exhibits intermediate properties of sand, silt, and clay, making it most desirable for general agriculture?

Solution & Rationale: Loam soils contain balanced proportions of sand, silt, and clay, offering optimal water retention, aeration, and workability.

What is the chemical formula and mineral name of agricultural limestone used for acid soil amelioration?

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

How does an increase in soil organic matter typically affect the bulk density of a mineral soil?

Solution & Rationale: Organic matter has a low particle density and promotes aggregation, thereby increasing porosity and decreasing bulk density.
