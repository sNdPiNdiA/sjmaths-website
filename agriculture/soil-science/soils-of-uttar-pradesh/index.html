Pedogenesis and Physiographic Divisions of Uttar Pradesh Soils

The soils of Uttar Pradesh have evolved through complex pedogenic processes dictated by the Indo-Gangetic geomorphology, climate variations, parent material depositions by Himalayan river systems, and native vegetation. Uttar Pradesh is broadly divided into three main physiographic zones: the Himalayan region in the north, the vast Indo-Gangetic alluvial plain (central and eastern expanse), and the southern plateau/bundelkhand region.

  • Alluvial Soils (Bangar and Khadar): Covering nearly three-fourths of the state, these soils are formed by the continuous deposition of sediments brought down by the Ganga, Yamuna, Ghaghra, and Gandak rivers. Bangar represents the older alluvial upland soils which are less fertile, heavier in texture, and occasionally contain calcium carbonate nodules known as kankar. Khadar represents newer alluvial flood plain soils, sandy to loamy in texture, renewed annually by seasonal river floods, and highly fertile.
  • Bundelkhand Soils (Southern Plateau): Developed in the southern districts (Lalitpur, Jhansi, Banda, Hamirpur, Jalaun) from ancient gneiss, granite, and Vindhyan rocks. These are categorized into distinct local types: Parwa (light sandy-loam), Mar (heavy black clay), Kabar (heavy clay-loam), and Rakar (sloping, eroded red soils).
  • Terai and Bhabar Soils: Located along the foothills of the Himalayas (Shivalik range). Bhabar soils are coarse, gravelly, and excessively drained where rivers disappear underground. Terai soils are moist, swampy, fine-textured, rich in organic matter, and naturally supporting tall grass and high-water-demanding cropping systems.

Taxonomic Classification and Physico-Chemical Properties

Under USDA Soil Taxonomy, the soils of Uttar Pradesh predominantly belong to the orders Inceptisols, Entisols, Alfisols, and Vertisols, reflecting their stage of weathering and mineralogical composition.

  • Inceptisols: The most dominant soil order across the central Gangetic plains. These are young yet moderately developed soils with cambic subsurface horizons, neutral to mildly alkaline pH, and good base saturation.
  • Entisols: Young, weakly developed soils found mainly in active floodplains (Khadar tracts) and sandy tracts (Bhurs) with little or no pedogenic horizon differentiation.
  • Alfisols: Found in areas of higher effective precipitation or older uplands, characterized by argillic horizons with illuvial clay accumulation and slightly acidic to neutral reaction.
  • Vertisols: Occurring in the southern Bundelkhand zone (Mar and Kabar formations), these are shrink-swell clay soils rich in smectite minerals, exhibiting deep vertical cracks during dry summer months and high plasticity when wet.
  • Nutrient Status: Most UP soils are deficient in total nitrogen (N) and available zinc (Zn). Phosphorus (P) availability ranges from low to medium, whereas potassium (K) is generally medium to high due to illite-dominated clay mineralogy.

Problem Soils of Uttar Pradesh: Saline, Sodic, and Ravine Lands

Uttar Pradesh possesses significant tracts of degraded and problem soils that constrain agricultural productivity unless scientifically managed.

  • Sodic (Alkaline/Usar) Soils: Extensively distributed in districts like Rae Bareli, Sultanpur, Unnao, Pratapgarh, Kanpur, Aligarh, and Etah. Characterized by high exchangeable sodium percentage (ESP > 15), high pH (> 8.5), and sodium carbonate/bicarbonate dominance, causing dispersion of clay particles and poor hydraulic conductivity.
  • Reclamation and Management of Sodic Soils: Gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O) is applied as the primary chemical amendment to replace exchangeable sodium with calcium. Leaching of salts, green manuring with Sesbania aculeata (dhaincha), and cultivation of salt-tolerant rice varieties (e.g., CSR-36) are standard agronomic practices.
  • Ravine and Eroded Soils: Found along the Chambal, Yamuna, and Betwa river basins in western and south-western UP. Accelerated gully erosion has stripped the topsoil, necessitating contour bunding, gully plugging, and afforestation with species like Acacia nilotica and Dalbergia sissoo.

Agro-Climatic Zones and Soil-Crop Suitability

Uttar Pradesh is partitioned into nine distinct agro-climatic zones by the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) based on rainfall, temperature, topography, and soil characteristics, ensuring targeted crop zoning.

  • Western Plain Zone: Loamy to sandy-loam soils; highly suitable for intensive wheat (Triticum aestivum), sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum), and basmati rice (Oryza sativa) cultivation.
  • Central Plain Zone: Deep alluvial soils supporting diversified cropping systems including maize (Zea mays), potato (Solanum tuberosum), and mustard (Brassica juncea).
  • Bundelkhand Zone: Mixed red and black soils suited for pulses like chickpea (Cicer arietinum), pigeonpea (Cajanus cajan), and oilseeds under rainfed conditions.
  • Eastern Plain and North-Eastern Plain Zones: High moisture regimes and fine alluvial soils ideal for high-intensity lowland rice and wheat rotations, alongside jute (Corchorus olitorius) and vegetables.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Bangar soils represent older alluvium containing calcium carbonate nodules known locally as kankar.
  • Khadar soils represent newer alluvium deposited annually by floodwaters, possessing high natural fertility.
  • The southern plateau region of Uttar Pradesh is known as the Bundelkhand region, featuring Mar, Kabar, Parwa, and Rakar soil types.
  • Sodic (Usar) soils of UP are reclaimed using agricultural grade gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O) as a chemical amendment.
  • Bhabar soils are coarse, gravelly, and excessively drained soils located immediately south of the Shivalik Himalayan foothills.
  • Terai soils are moist, swampy, fine-textured alluvial soils characterized by high organic matter content.
  • Under USDA Soil Taxonomy, the majority of Indo-Gangetic alluvial soils in Uttar Pradesh belong to the Inceptisols order.
  • Mar and Kabar soils of Bundelkhand exhibit high shrink-swell properties due to the dominance of smectite clay minerals.
  • Zinc (Zn) and Nitrogen (N) are the most universally deficient plant nutrients across Uttar Pradesh soils.
  • The nine agro-climatic zones of Uttar Pradesh were designated by ICAR to formulate localized agricultural research and extension strategies.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Bangar with Khadar: Bangar is older alluvium situated on uplands, whereas Khadar is newer alluvium in active flood plains.
  • Assuming Bundelkhand soils are purely alluvial: Bundelkhand soils are residual soils derived from granites and gneisses, distinct from Indo-Gangetic alluvium.
  • Treating sodic soils with lime: Sodic soils have high pH and sodium; they require gypsum (CaSO4), whereas acidic soils require agricultural lime (CaCO3).

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Soils of Uttar Pradesh: Comprehensive Agronomic & Pedological Study

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Alluvial Pedogenesis in the Gangetic Plains

Alluvial soils occupy the largest geographic expanse in Uttar Pradesh, formed through successive fluvial deposits of the Ganga river system over millennia.

  • Bangar: Older alluvium, elevated topography, non-flooded, contains kankar nodules.
  • Khadar: Newer alluvium, low-lying flood plains, annually rejuvenated, highly fertile.
Concept 2

Taxonomic Profile and Mineralogy

Pedological classification of UP soils highlights active weathering profiles under subtropical monsoonal climates.

  • Inceptisols: Dominant order with cambic horizons across the central Gangetic plains.
  • Vertisols: Smectite-rich shrinking and swelling clay soils localized in Bundelkhand.
Concept 3

Problem Soils and Reclamation Dynamics

Degraded lands require precise chemical and biological interventions to restore crop productivity.

  • Sodic Soils: High pH (>8.5), high ESP (>15), reclaimed via gypsum application and green manuring.
  • Ravine Lands: Severe gully erosion along river channels requiring watershed management and afforestation.
Concept 4

Agro-Climatic Zonation

UP's division into nine agro-climatic zones aligns soil capability with optimum cropping patterns.

  • Western Zone: Intensive sugarcane-wheat belt on fertile loamy soils.
  • Bundelkhand Zone: Rainfed pulse and oilseed belt on Mar and Parwa soils.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Bangar (Older Alluvium)
Older alluvial soil found on higher terraces of the Gangetic plain, containing kankar nodules and lower organic fertility.
Khadar (Newer Alluvium)
Recent alluvial soil deposited annually along river floodplains, characterized by high fertility and sandy-loam texture.
Bhabar (Piedmont Gravelly Soil)
Coarse, gravelly, highly porous soil zone located along the Himalayan foothills where rivers sub-surface.
Terai (Sub-montane Moist Soil)
Fine-textured, poorly drained, high-moisture soil belt running parallel to the Bhabar zone with rich organic carbon.
Mar (Black Clay Vertisol)
Heavy dark-colored clay soil of Bundelkhand dominated by smectite minerals with high shrink-swell capacity.
Kabar (Heavy Clay-Loam Soil)
Dark brown to black heavy soil of Bundelkhand intermediate between Mar and Parwa.
Parwa (Reddish Sandy-Loam Soil)
Light-textured upland soil of Bundelkhand suited for millets and pulses under rainfed farming.
Rakar (Eroded Sloping Soil)
Sloping, eroded, coarse-textured marginal soil of Bundelkhand with low fertility and moisture retention.
Usar (Sodic / Alkali Soil)
Wasteland soil characterized by high sodium saturation, high pH, and poor structural stability.
Bhur (Sandy Eolian Upland)
Elevated sandy tract formed by wind action along river valleys in the Gangetic plains.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Kankar nodules are calcium carbonate segregations found in Bangar alluvial profiles.
  • Smectite is the principal expanding clay mineral in Mar and Kabar soils of Bundelkhand.
  • Zinc is the most widespread micronutrient deficiency across all Uttar Pradesh soil zones.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Alluvial soils cover over 75% of Uttar Pradesh and are divided into Bangar (older) and Khadar (newer).
  • Bundelkhand soils consist of Mar, Kabar, Parwa, and Rakar formed from ancient crystalline rocks.
  • Inceptisols represent the most widespread USDA soil order in Uttar Pradesh.
  • Sodic soils require gypsum application for successful chemical reclamation and crop establishment.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Bhabar Soil VS Terai Soil

Bhabar is coarse, stony, and excessively drained at the foothills; Terai is fine-textured, marshy, and moist immediately south of Bhabar.

Mar Soil VS Rakar Soil

Mar is a heavy, fertile, expanding black clay; Rakar is a coarse, sloping, eroded red soil of low fertility.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following soil orders dominates the Indo-Gangetic alluvial plains of Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: The majority of the Indo-Gangetic alluvial soils in Uttar Pradesh are classified under Inceptisols due to moderate pedogenic development.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the local term used for older alluvial soils located on higher uplands in Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: Bangar refers to older alluvium situated above flood levels, often containing calcium carbonate kankar nodules.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which soil amendment is primarily recommended for the reclamation of sodic (alkaline) soils in Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: Gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O) supplies calcium, which replaces exchangeable sodium in sodic soils, lowering exchangeable sodium percentage and pH.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which physiographic zone in Uttar Pradesh is characterized by coarse gravelly deposits where rivers disappear underground?

Explanation: Bhabar soils are porous, coarse, and gravelly zones where Himalayan streams descend and temporarily flow underground.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Mar soil, found in the Bundelkhand region of Uttar Pradesh, is characterized by the dominance of which clay mineral?

Explanation: Mar soils are heavy black clay Vertisols dominated by smectite clay minerals exhibiting pronounced plasticity and stickiness.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary cause of ravine formation along the Chambal and Yamuna river valleys in Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: Ravines are formed by severe accelerated gully erosion caused by runoff water cutting through deep alluvial deposits along major river courses.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which micronutrient deficiency is most universally prevalent across the agricultural soils of Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: Zinc deficiency is widespread in Indo-Gangetic alluvial soils due to high pH, calcareousness, and low organic matter.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Khadar soils differ from Bangar soils because Khadar soils are:

Explanation: Khadar soils occupy lower floodplains and receive fresh silt deposits every year during monsoon floods, maintaining high fertility.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

How many distinct agro-climatic zones has the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) demarcated in Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: Uttar Pradesh is officially classified into 9 agro-climatic zones by ICAR to facilitate localized agricultural planning and research.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which soil type in the Bundelkhand region is described as a light, reddish-brown sandy-loam easily eroded by water?

Explanation: Parwa is a lighter sandy-loam to loam soil in Bundelkhand with a reddish tinge, requiring supplemental irrigation.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Under USDA Soil Taxonomy, what order do active floodplain (Khadar) soils typically belong to?

Explanation: Entisols are young, weakly developed soils found in active floodplains and shifting sand dunes where pedogenesis is minimal.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the characteristic pH range of sodic (usar) soils found in districts like Rae Bareli and Aligarh?

Explanation: Sodic soils possess a high pH exceeding 8.5 due to the hydrolysis of sodium carbonate and bicarbonate salts.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Rakar soil in the Bundelkhand region is best defined as:

Explanation: Rakar soils are sloping, eroded, coarse-textured marginal soils with low fertility, often unsuited for intensive farming without conservation.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which clay mineral is predominant in the alluvial soils of the Indo-Gangetic plain, contributing to high potassium availability?

Explanation: Illite is the dominant clay mineral in Gangetic alluvial soils, providing a natural reserve of potassium for crop uptake.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The Terai soil zone is situated geographically between which two regions?

Explanation: Terai soils lie immediately south of the gravelly Bhabar belt, forming a moist, fine-textured boundary before the central alluvial plains.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary agronomic role of growing Sesbania aculeata (dhaincha) in sodic soil management?

Explanation: Dhaincha green manuring adds organic matter, releases organic acids upon decomposition that solubilize calcium carbonate, and helps lower sodic soil pH.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which district in Uttar Pradesh is most notable for extensive ravine lands along the Yamuna and Chambal rivers?

Explanation: Etawah and surrounding districts along the Yamuna-Chambal confluence feature extensive, deep ravine topography.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical organic carbon status of virgin Terai soils?

Explanation: Terai soils are naturally rich in organic carbon due to high vegetative biomass production and restricted microbial decomposition under moist conditions.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following describes Kabar soil in Bundelkhand?

Explanation: Kabar is a heavy clay-loam soil with dark brown to black coloration, possessing moderate to high moisture retention.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary reason for low phosphorus availability in many neutral to alkaline UP soils?

Explanation: In alkaline and calcareous soils, applied phosphorus reacts with calcium to form insoluble calcium phosphate complexes, reducing availability.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Analyze the pedological evolution of Indo-Gangetic alluvium: Which factor differentiates Bangar from Khadar formations most fundamentally?

Solution & Rationale: Bangar and Khadar share similar parent sediments from Himalayan rivers, but differ fundamentally in geological age and elevation, resulting in annual flood renewal for Khadar versus stability and kankar formation in older Bangar terraces.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

In the chemical reclamation of sodic soils using gypsum, what is the exact chemical reaction mechanism that reduces soil pH and ESP?

Solution & Rationale: Applied gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O) dissociates, and divalent calcium ions exchange with monovalent sodium ions on the soil exchange complex. Released sodium forms soluble sodium sulfate, which is subsequently leached out of the root zone.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

Why do Mar soils exhibit profound vertical cracking during the summer season in the Bundelkhand agro-climatic zone?

Solution & Rationale: Mar soils are Vertisols dominated by smectite (montmorillonite) clay, which undergoes drastic volume changes—swelling when wet and shrinking severely to form deep wide cracks upon drying.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

What pedogenic process is primarily responsible for the development of Alfisols in older upland regions of Uttar Pradesh?

Solution & Rationale: Alfisols develop through lessivage, which is the mechanical migration of suspended clay particles from eluvial surface horizons (E horizon) to illuvial subsurface horizons (Argillic Bt horizon).

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which agronomic practice is least effective for soil moisture conservation in rainfed Parwa soils of Bundelkhand?

Solution & Rationale: Leaving fields fallow without weeding accelerates moisture loss through weed transpiration and soil evaporation, whereas summer ploughing, mulching, and contour bunding conserve moisture effectively.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

In terms of potassium dynamics, why do UP alluvial soils generally test medium to high in available potassium despite continuous cropping?

Solution & Rationale: Gangetic alluvium is inherently rich in illite and mica derived from Himalayan parent rocks, which weather and release non-exchangeable potassium into available pools.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

What distinguishes the Bhabar soil zone from the Terai soil zone in terms of hydrological behavior?

Solution & Rationale: Bhabar consists of coarse gravel and boulders where streams sink underground (excessive drainage), whereas Terai is where these waters reappear, creating a moist, poorly drained swampy zone.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

A farmer in Aligarh district has a sodic soil with a pH of 9.2 and an Exchangeable Sodium Percentage (ESP) of 28. Assuming a root zone depth of 30 cm and bulk density of 1.4 g/cm³, what is the primary factor determining the gypsum requirement?

Solution & Rationale: Gypsum requirement calculations are directly based on neutralizing excess exchangeable sodium present on the soil exchange complex to bring ESP down to a safe threshold (usually <10).

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which specific soil property is primarily responsible for the sticky and plastic consistency of Kabar soils when wetted?

Solution & Rationale: The presence of 2:1 lattice expanding clay minerals (smectite/illite mix) in Kabar soils imparts high plasticity, cohesion, and stickiness when moist.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

In the context of Uttar Pradesh soil taxonomy, why are most Gangetic alluvial soils classified as Inceptisols rather than Entisols?

Solution & Rationale: Inceptisols represent soils that have undergone moderate weathering and show beginning horizon formation (such as a cambic B horizon), distinguishing them from young, featureless Entisols.
