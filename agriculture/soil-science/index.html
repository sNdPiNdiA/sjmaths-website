Introduction to Soil Science: Pedology and Edaphology

Soil science is broadly divided into two main branches: Pedology (the study of soil in its natural environment, focusing on genesis, morphology, and classification) and Edaphology (the study of soil as a medium for plant growth, focusing on productivity and management). According to V.V. Dokuchaev, the Father of Soil Science, soil is a natural body formed by the combined action of climate and living organisms acting upon parent material over time.

  • Active Soil Factors: Climate and Biosphere (organisms) that supply energy and matter to the soil system.
  • Passive Soil Factors: Parent material, Topography (relief), and Time that provide the substrate and context for soil development.
  • Soil Profile: A vertical cross-section of soil exposing distinct horizontal layers known as master horizons: O, A, E, B, C, and R.

Physical Properties of Soil: Texture, Structure, and Density

Soil physical properties dictate root penetration, water retention, and aeration. Soil texture refers to the relative proportion of sand (2.0 - 0.05 mm), silt (0.05 - 0.002 mm), and clay (< 0.002 mm) particles as defined by the USDA classification system.

  • Bulk Density (D_b): Mass of dry soil per unit bulk volume, typically ranging from 1.1 to 1.6 g/cm³ in mineral soils. High compaction increases D_b and restricts root elongation.
  • Particle Density (D_s): Density of solid soil particles alone, averaging 2.65 g/cm³ for quartz-dominated mineral soils.
  • Soil Porosity (f): Calculated as f = \left(1 - D_bD_s\right) × 100. Determines water storage capacity and gaseous exchange.

Soil Chemistry: CEC, Base Saturation, and Soil Reaction (pH)

Soil chemical behavior revolves around surface charges, cation exchange capacity (CEC), and hydrogen ion activity (pH). Soil CEC measures the total capacity of a soil to hold exchangeable cations, expressed in cmol(p^+)/kg of dry soil. Humus and 2:1 expanding clay minerals exhibit high CEC.

  • Soil pH: Negative logarithm of hydrogen ion activity (-\log[H^+]). Acidic soils (pH < 6.5) dominate high-rainfall humid regions, whereas alkaline soils (pH > 7.5) characterize arid and semi-arid tracts.
  • Base Saturation: Percentage of the CEC occupied by basic cations (Ca^{2+}, Mg^{2+}, K^+, Na^+).
  • Saline and Sodic Soils: Saline soils have electrical conductivity (EC > 4 dS/m), exchangeable sodium percentage (ESP < 15), and pH < 8.5. Sodic (alkaline) soils have EC < 4 dS/m, ESP > 15, and pH > 8.5.

Soil Microbiology and Biochemistry: Nitrogen and Phosphorus Cycles

Soil fertility is heavily driven by microbial transformations. Biological nitrogen fixation (BNF) converts atmospheric dinitrogen into plant-available ammonia via the enzyme nitrogenase found in symbiotic bacteria like Rhizobium, Bradyrhizobium, and free-living forms like Azotobacter and Azospirillum.

  • Nitrification: Two-step biological oxidation where ammonium (NH_4^+) is oxidized to nitrite (NO_2^-) by Nitrosomonas, followed by oxidation of nitrite to nitrate (NO_3^-) by Nitrobacter.
  • Ammonification: Breakdown of organic nitrogenous compounds into ammonia by heterotrophic bacteria and fungi.
  • Phosphorus Fixation: In acidic soils, soluble orthophosphates fix with iron and aluminum oxides, whereas in calcareous soils, they precipitate with calcium compounds, rendering P largely unavailable.

Manures, Fertilizers, and Nutrient Management Calculations

Efficient nutrient management requires balancing crop removals with fertilizer applications. Straight fertilizers supply a single primary nutrient, while complex fertilizers supply two or more.

  • Urea Composition: Contains 46% nitrogen. To calculate the quantity of urea required to supply 100 kg N: 1000.46 = 217.39 kg of urea.
  • DAP Composition: Diammonium Phosphate contains 18% N and 46% P_2O_5.
  • Integrated Nutrient Management (INM): Harmonious combination of organic manures, green manuring, biofertilizers, and chemical fertilizers to sustain soil health and crop productivity.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Father of Soil Science and pioneer of genetic soil classification: V.V. Dokuchaev.
  • Ideal Bulk Density (D_b) for mineral soils supporting root growth is approximately 1.33 g/cm³.
  • Particle density (D_s) of most mineral soils is universally taken as 2.65 g/cm³.
  • Urea contains 46% N, Anhydrous ammonia contains 82% N (highest among nitrogenous fertilizers).
  • Single Super Phosphate (SSP) contains 16% P_2O_5 and 12% Sulfur.
  • Gypsum (CaSO_4 · 2H_2O) is the primary chemical amendment used for reclaiming sodic (alkaline) soils.
  • Leaching requirement (LR) is used to manage salt accumulation in saline soils.
  • Nitrosomonas oxidizes ammonium to nitrite, while Nitrobacter oxidizes nitrite to nitrate.
  • Vermiculite and Montmorillonite are 2:1 expanding type clay minerals with high CEC.
  • Kaolinite is a 1:1 non-expanding type clay mineral predominant in red and laterite soils.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing bulk density with particle density: Bulk density includes pore space volume, whereas particle density considers only solid mineral volume.
  • Confusing saline and sodic soil reclamation: Saline soils require leaching, whereas sodic soils require gypsum replacement of exchangeable sodium.
  • Assuming all nitrogen fertilizers leave an acidic residual effect: Sodium nitrate leaves a basic residue, whereas ammonium sulfate leaves an acidic residue.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Soil Science and Agricultural Chemistry for UP TGT/PGT Agriculture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Soil Genesis and Factors of Formation

Soil formation proceeds through weathering of parent rocks governed by Dokuchaev's equation.

  • Active Factors: Climate and biological activity driving weathering energy.
  • Passive Factors: Parent rock, topography, and duration of weathering.
Concept 2

Soil Physical Architecture

Physical properties dictate water retention and aeration status.

  • Texture: Sand, silt, and clay proportions determining soil class.
  • Porosity: Percentage pore volume inversely proportional to bulk density.
Concept 3

Soil Colloids and Ion Exchange

Colloidal fractions (humus and clay) govern nutrient retention capacity.

  • CEC: Total exchangeable cations held per unit mass of soil.
  • Base Saturation: Proportion of exchange sites occupied by basic cations.
Concept 4

Problem Soils and Reclamation

Chemical imbalances require specific amelioration strategies.

  • Saline Soils: High EC (>4 dS/m), reclaimed via leaching.
  • Sodic Soils: High ESP (>15), reclaimed via gypsum application.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Pedology (Pedologia)
The scientific study of soils in their natural setting, including genesis, morphology, and classification.
Edaphology (Edaphologia)
The study of soil as a medium for plant growth and agricultural productivity.
Bulk Density (Db = Mass / Bulk Volume)
The mass of dry soil per unit bulk volume, including pore spaces, expressed in g/cm³.
Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC)
The maximum quantity of total cations adsorbed that a soil can hold at a specific pH, expressed in cmol(+)/kg.
Nitrification (Biological oxidation of NH₄⁺ to NO₃⁻)
Microbial oxidation of ammonium to nitrite by Nitrosomonas and subsequently to nitrate by Nitrobacter.
Gypsum Requirement (GR for Sodic Soils)
The quantity of agricultural gypsum required to replace exchangeable sodium in sodic soils.
Kaolinite (Al₂Si₂O₅(OH)₄)
A 1:1 non-expanding aluminosilicate clay mineral characterized by low plasticity and low CEC.
Montmorillonite (Smectite group)
A 2:1 expanding type clay mineral known for high swelling, shrinking, and high CEC.
Rhizobium (Rhizobium leguminosarum)
A symbiotic soil bacterium that fixes atmospheric nitrogen inside root nodules of leguminous crops.
Electrical Conductivity (EC (dS/m))
A measure of the concentration of soluble salts in a soil water extract.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Kaolinite is 1:1 type; Montmorillonite is 2:1 expanding type.
  • Nitrosomonas converts ammonium to nitrite; Nitrobacter converts nitrite to nitrate.
  • ESP greater than 15 defines sodic soils.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Dokuchaev is recognized as the Father of Soil Science.
  • Soil bulk density typically averages 1.33 g/cm³, while particle density averages 2.65 g/cm³.
  • Urea supplies 46% nitrogen; DAP supplies 18% N and 46% P₂O₅.
  • Saline soils are reclaimed by leaching; sodic soils are reclaimed using gypsum.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Saline Soil VS Sodic Soil

Saline soils have high soluble salts (EC >4) and pH <8.5; sodic soils have high exchangeable sodium (ESP >15) and pH >8.5.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Who is acknowledged as the Father of Soil Science?

Explanation: V.V. Dokuchaev formulated the foundational principles of pedology and genetic soil classification, earning him the title Father of Soil Science.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the average particle density of mineral soils?

Explanation: The particle density (solid mineral density) of most agricultural soils is remarkably constant at approximately 2.65 g/cm³.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the nitrogen percentage present in commercial urea fertilizer?

Explanation: Urea is an organic amide fertilizer containing 46% nitrogen.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which bacteria is responsible for converting nitrite to nitrate in soil?

Explanation: Nitrobacter oxidizes nitrite (NO_2^-) into nitrate (NO_3^-), completing the second stage of nitrification.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What chemical amendment is recommended for the reclamation of sodic (alkaline) soils?

Explanation: Gypsum (CaSO_4 · 2H_2O) supplies calcium ions (Ca^{2+}) which replace exchangeable sodium on the exchange complex.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What type of clay mineral is Kaolinite?

Explanation: Kaolinite is a 1:1 type silicate clay mineral with fixed interlayer spacing, making it non-expanding with low CEC.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the electrical conductivity (EC) threshold defining saline soils?

Explanation: Saline soils are officially classified by an electrical conductivity greater than 4 dS/m at 25°C.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the Exchangeable Sodium Percentage (ESP) threshold for sodic soils?

Explanation: Sodic soils exhibit an Exchangeable Sodium Percentage greater than 15, leading to dispersion and high pH.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a 2:1 expanding type clay mineral?

Explanation: Montmorillonite is a smectite group 2:1 expanding clay mineral that swells when wet and shrinks when dry.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

How much quantity of urea is required to supply 50 kg of actual Nitrogen?

Explanation: Calculation: 50 / 0.46 = 108.69 kg of urea.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a secondary plant nutrient?

Explanation: Calcium, magnesium, and sulfur are classified as secondary macronutrients.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary mechanism of phosphorus fixation in acidic soils?

Explanation: In acidic soils, soluble phosphate ions react with iron and aluminum oxides/hydroxides, forming insoluble compounds.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which soil order represents organic soils formed in wet environments?

Explanation: Histosols are organic soils (peat and muck soils) containing high organic matter accumulated under waterlogged conditions.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the bulk density range of normal mineral agricultural soils?

Explanation: Bulk density of mineral soils generally varies between 1.1 and 1.6 g/cm³ depending on compaction and organic matter.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which microorganism is responsible for symbiotic nitrogen fixation in soybean?

Explanation: Bradyrhizobium japonicum (formerly Rhizobium japonicum) forms a symbiotic nitrogen-fixing association with soybean.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does soil porosity represent?

Explanation: Soil porosity refers to the percentage of soil volume occupied by pore spaces containing water and air.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which element is essential for nitrate reduction inside plant tissues?

Explanation: Molybdenum is a crucial micro-nutrient functioning as a metallic component in nitrate reductase and nitrogenase enzymes.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the percentage of P₂O₅ in Single Super Phosphate (SSP)?

Explanation: SSP contains 16% water-soluble phosphorus pentoxide (P_2O_5).

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which master horizon in a soil profile represents the zone of eluviation (leaching)?

Explanation: The E horizon is the eluvial horizon characterized by maximum loss of clay, iron, and aluminum oxides.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main cause of soil acidity in high-rainfall humid regions?

Explanation: Heavy rainfall leaches soluble basic cations (Ca^{2+}, Mg^{2+}, K+, Na^+) out of the root zone, leaving acidic H^+ and Al^{3+} ions.

Test Question 1 of 10 TGT Standard

Calculate the bulk density of a soil sample having a dry soil mass of 130 g and a total bulk volume of 100 cm³.

Solution & Rationale: Bulk Density = Mass of dry soil / Total volume = 130 g / 100 cm³ = 1.3 g/cm³.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

A soil has a bulk density of 1.25 g/cm³ and a particle density of 2.50 g/cm³. What is the percent porosity of this soil?

Solution & Rationale: Porosity (%) = [1 - (Db / Ds)] * 100 = [1 - (1.25 / 2.50)] * 100 = [1 - 0.5] * 100 = 50%.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following micronutrients is most likely to be deficient in calcareous alkaline soils of India?

Solution & Rationale: Zinc availability decreases sharply in high pH calcareous soils due to adsorption on calcium carbonate surfaces and precipitation.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary chemical reaction involved when gypsum is applied to sodic soils?

Solution & Rationale: Gypsum supplies calcium ions that precipitate toxic sodium carbonate as relatively insoluble calcium carbonate, releasing sodium sulfate which is leached out.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which soil order is characterized by shrinking and swelling clays (Vertic properties), leading to wide deep cracks during dry periods?

Solution & Rationale: Vertisols contain high amounts of expanding 2:1 smectite clays, causing pronounced volume changes with moisture fluctuations.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the C:N ratio of microbial biomass in typical agricultural soils?

Solution & Rationale: Soil microbes maintain a relatively narrow C:N ratio ranging between 8:1 and 12:1.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following fertilizers leaves an alkaline (basic) residual effect in the soil?

Solution & Rationale: Sodium nitrate leaves a basic residue because plants absorb the nitrate anion, leaving sodium cations that form alkaline sodium carbonate in soil solution.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the critical limit of available soil Phosphorus (Olsen-P) in kg/ha below which crop response is expected?

Solution & Rationale: Olsen-P values below 10 kg P/ha generally indicate low soil phosphorus status requiring immediate phosphate fertilization.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which master soil horizon is characterized by the accumulation of illuvial silicate clay, iron, aluminum, or humus?

Solution & Rationale: The B horizon is the subsurface illuvial horizon where materials washed down from upper horizons accumulate.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the base saturation percentage range for neutral to slightly alkaline fertile soils?

Solution & Rationale: Fertile neutral to alkaline soils exhibit high base saturation (often >80% to nearly 100%), dominated by basic cations.
