Introduction to Agricultural Hydrology and Runoff

Runoff refers to the portion of precipitation, snowmelt, or irrigation water that flows over the land surface, ultimately reaching streams, rivers, and water bodies after satisfying all initial abstractions such as interception, depression storage, and infiltration.

  • Hydrological Cycle: Runoff is a fundamental component of the terrestrial phase of the hydrological cycle, dictating watershed yield and soil erosion dynamics.
  • Significance in Agronomy: Excessive runoff leads to severe topsoil erosion, loss of plant nutrients (such as nitrogen and phosphorus), and downstream flooding, whereas insufficient runoff in rainfed agriculture indicates poor moisture harvesting efficiency.
  • Watershed Approach: For effective agricultural planning, runoff must be analyzed at the micro-watershed level to design appropriate soil and water conservation structures.

Classification and Components of Runoff

Runoff is classified based on the time lag between rainfall occurrence and runoff appearance, as well as the pathway the water takes to reach the drainage channel.

  • Surface Runoff (Overland Flow): Water that flows directly over the soil surface during or immediately after a high-intensity rainfall event when the rainfall rate exceeds the infiltration capacity of the soil (Hortonian overland flow).
  • Sub-surface Runoff (Interflow): Infiltration water that moves laterally through the upper soil horizons without joining the water table and eventually emerges into streams or root zones.
  • Base Flow (Groundwater Flow): Deep percolated water that reaches the water table and slowly feeds streams over extended periods, sustaining perennial flow during dry seasons.
  • Factors Affecting Runoff: Meteorological factors (rainfall intensity, duration, distribution) and physiographic factors (soil type, slope steepness, land use, vegetative cover, and catchment size).

Runoff Estimation Methods

Accurate estimation of peak runoff rate and total runoff volume is vital for designing hydraulic structures such as farm ponds, terraces, contour bunds, and spillways.

  • Rational Formula: Used primarily for small agricultural watersheds (less than 50 km²) to estimate peak runoff rate (Q = CIA360, where Q is peak discharge in m³/s, C is runoff coefficient, I is rainfall intensity in mm/h, and A is catchment area in hectares).
  • SCS Curve Number (CN) Method: Developed by the USDA Soil Conservation Service, this method estimates direct runoff volume from storm rainfall based on soil hydrologic groups (A, B, C, D) and antecedent moisture conditions (AMC).
  • Empirical Formulae: Regional equations such as Khosla's formula or Strange's tables used in Indian agro-climatic zones for estimating seasonal water yields.

Soil Hydrologic Groups and Antecedent Moisture Condition (AMC)

The USDA curve number method relies heavily on classifying soils into specific hydrologic groups based on their minimum infiltration rates.

  • Hydrologic Group A: Deep, well-drained sands or gravels with high infiltration rates and low runoff potential.
  • Hydrologic Group B: Moderately deep to deep, moderately fine to moderately coarse textures with moderate infiltration rates.
  • Hydrologic Group C: Soils with slower infiltration rates, comprising moderately fine to fine textures with a layer that impedes downward movement of water.
  • Hydrologic Group D: Heavy clay soils, swelling clays (such as Vertisols), or soils with a high water table, exhibiting very low infiltration rates and maximum runoff potential.
  • AMC States: AMC-I (dry soil condition), AMC-II (average moisture condition), and AMC-III (wet soil condition due to heavy rainfall in preceding 5 days).

Runoff Measurement Techniques in Agricultural Watersheds

Measuring runoff helps in validating hydrological models and managing irrigation water supplies effectively.

  • Area-Velocity Method: Measures cross-sectional area of the stream and the velocity of water flow using current meters or floats (Q = A × V).
  • Weirs and Flumes: Artificial control structures installed in open channels. V-notch weirs and Parshall flumes are widely used in agricultural field channels for precise discharge measurement.
  • Water Level Recorders: Continuous stage recorders linked with rating curves to convert water level height into discharge rates over time.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The Rational Formula Q = CIA360 requires rainfall intensity (I) to be expressed in mm/h and catchment area (A) in hectares.
  • USDA soil hydrologic Group D exhibits the highest runoff potential due to heavy clay texture and very low infiltration rates.
  • Antecedent Moisture Condition (AMC) considers the total precipitation amount occurring in the 5-day period preceding the design storm.
  • Parshall flume is a fixed hydraulic structure used for measuring open-channel water flow without causing excessive head loss.
  • Hortonian overland flow occurs when rainfall intensity exceeds the steady-state infiltration capacity of the soil surface.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the Rational Formula with SCS Curve Number method: Rational formula calculates peak discharge, whereas SCS calculates total runoff volume.
  • Assuming Soil Group A has high runoff potential: Group A actually has high infiltration and lowest runoff potential.
  • Forgetting unit conversions in the Rational Formula when area is given in square meters instead of hectares.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Runoff Management and Hydrological Principles in Agriculture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Fundamentals of Agricultural Runoff

Runoff is the residual precipitation after initial abstractions are met. It dictates soil erosion rates and watershed hydrology.

  • Initial Abstraction: Comprises interception by plant canopy, depression storage, and initial infiltration before overland flow begins.
Concept 2

Rational Formula Application

Used for estimating peak runoff rate for small catchments and hydraulic structure sizing.

  • Constant 360: Conversion factor used when area is in hectares and intensity in mm/h to yield discharge in m³/s.
Concept 3

SCS-CN Method Dynamics

Empirical method linking soil characteristics, land use management, and antecedent moisture.

  • Curve Number Range: Varies from 0 to 100, where higher numbers indicate impervious surfaces and maximum runoff.
Concept 4

Hydrological Soil Group Classification

Categorization of soils into A, B, C, and D groups based on transmission rates and profile characteristics.

  • Group D Soils: Dominated by expanding 2:1 clay minerals like montmorillonite with restricted internal drainage.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Runoff (Surface and Subsurface Flow)
The portion of precipitation that makes its way towards stream channels after meeting evaporative and infiltration losses.
Rational Formula (Q = CIA / 360)
Empirical equation used to calculate peak runoff rate for small agricultural watersheds.
Curve Number (SCS-CN Index)
An empirical parameter ranging from 0 to 100 used to predict direct runoff based on soil type and land use.
Antecedent Moisture Condition (AMC)
The degree of wetness in a watershed at the start of a rainfall-runoff event.
Parshall Flume (Venturi Flume)
A calibrated device used for measuring water flow rates in open channels and irrigation canals.
Hortonian Overland Flow (Infiltration Excess Runoff)
Runoff generated when rainfall intensity exceeds the infiltration capacity of the soil.
Interflow (Subsurface Stormflow)
Water that infiltrates the soil and moves laterally through upper soil horizons towards stream channels.
Hydrologic Soil Group (HSG (A, B, C, D))
Classification of soils based on their runoff potential and minimum infiltration rates.
Initial Abstraction (Ia)
All rainfall losses occurring before surface runoff starts, including depression storage and canopy interception.
Time of Concentration (Tc)
The time required for a drop of water to travel from the most hydraulically remote point in the watershed to the outlet.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Rational formula denominator is 360 for metric units.
  • Group D soils have minimum infiltration rates below 1.3 mm/h.
  • AMC-III represents saturated soil conditions prior to rainfall.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Runoff is categorized into surface runoff, interflow, and base flow based on pathways.
  • The Rational formula is restricted to catchments under 50 hectares for peak rate estimation.
  • USDA soil groups range from A (lowest runoff) to D (highest runoff).
  • Curve Number values increase with urbanization, soil compaction, and poor vegetation cover.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Surface Runoff VS Base Flow

Surface runoff occurs rapidly over the soil surface, while base flow is delayed groundwater discharge sustaining streams.

Rational Formula VS SCS Curve Number Method

Rational formula gives peak discharge rate, whereas SCS-CN calculates cumulative runoff volume.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary output calculated using the Rational Formula in agricultural hydrology?

Explanation: The Rational Formula is specifically designed to estimate the peak rate of runoff (discharge) for small agricultural watersheds for designing drainage structures.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which USDA hydrological soil group exhibits the highest runoff potential and lowest infiltration rate?

Explanation: Hydrologic Group D soils consist of heavy clays or soils with a high water table, having very low infiltration rates (< 1.3 mm/h) and maximum runoff potential.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In the Rational Formula Q = CIA / 360, what unit must rainfall intensity (I) be expressed in?

Explanation: When using the metric version of the Rational Formula with 360 as the denominator, intensity (I) must be in mm/h and area (A) in hectares.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does Antecedent Moisture Condition (AMC-III) signify in the SCS curve number method?

Explanation: AMC-III represents wet soil conditions resulting from heavy rainfall or high antecedent precipitation during the 5-day period preceding the storm event.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which type of overland flow occurs when rainfall intensity exceeds the steady-state infiltration capacity of the soil?

Explanation: Hortonian overland flow (or infiltration excess runoff) occurs when the rainfall rate surpasses the rate at which soil can absorb water.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the upper catchment area limit for which the Rational Formula is generally considered reliable?

Explanation: The Rational Formula assumes rainfall is uniform across the entire watershed, making it reliable only for small catchments generally under 50 hectares.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which measuring device is a fixed structure used in open channels to measure irrigation water and runoff without moving parts?

Explanation: A Parshall flume is an established standard hydraulic structure utilized for accurate open-channel flow measurement with minimal head loss.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is represented by 'Ia' in the USDA SCS Curve Number runoff equation?

Explanation: Initial abstraction (Ia) includes all water losses due to surface storage, canopy interception, and early infiltration before runoff commences.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which soil hydrologic group is characterized by deep, well-drained sandy soils with high infiltration rates?

Explanation: Hydrologic Group A soils have high infiltration rates (> 7.6 mm/h) when thoroughly wetted, consisting chiefly of deep sands or gravels.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the range of curve number (CN) values in the SCS method?

Explanation: Curve numbers range theoretically from 0 (representing porous surfaces with infinite abstraction) to 100 (representing impermeable water surfaces or pavement).

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does 'Time of Concentration (Tc)' measure in watershed hydrology?

Explanation: Time of concentration is defined as the time needed for a parcel of water to flow from the hydraulically most remote point in the drainage basin to the outlet.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following factors decreases surface runoff in an agricultural watershed?

Explanation: Dense vegetative cover and organic mulches intercept raindrops, reduce surface sealing, improve soil aggregation, and increase infiltration, thereby decreasing runoff.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is interflow in the context of hydrological pathways?

Explanation: Interflow (subsurface stormflow) is infiltrated water that moves laterally through the upper permeable soil strata and eventually reaches stream channels.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In soil conservation engineering, what is the primary purpose of constructing farm ponds?

Explanation: Farm ponds capture and store surface runoff during peak rainfall events to provide life-saving supplemental irrigation during dry spells.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which formula is commonly used in India for estimating runoff volume from catchments based on rainfall and regional coefficients?

Explanation: Strange's tables and Khosla's empirical formula are traditional Indian methods used to estimate runoff yield from monsoon rainfall.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

How does soil compaction due to heavy machinery affect surface runoff?

Explanation: Soil compaction reduces total porosity and destroys macropores, leading to lowered infiltration capacity and significantly higher surface runoff.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of contour bunds in sloping agricultural lands?

Explanation: Contour bunds break the length of the slope, retain surface runoff behind the bund, and provide more time for water to infiltrate into the soil profile.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which parameter in the Rational Formula represents the ratio of peak runoff rate to rainfall intensity?

Explanation: The runoff coefficient (C) is a dimensionless factor representing the proportion of rainfall that is converted into direct surface runoff.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does base flow represent in a hydrograph analysis?

Explanation: Base flow is the delayed subsurface contribution from groundwater aquifers that sustains streamflow long after direct surface runoff has ceased.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate the peak runoff rate using the Rational Formula for a watershed of 25 hectares, given a runoff coefficient of 0.4 and a design rainfall intensity of 72 mm/h.

Solution & Rationale: Using Q = (C * I * A) / 360 -> Q = (0.4 * 72 * 25) / 360 = 720 / 360 = 2.00 m³/s.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

A soil profile classified under USDA Hydrologic Group C possesses which of the following infiltration characteristics?

Solution & Rationale: Hydrologic Group C soils are characterized by slow infiltration rates ranging between 1.3 and 3.8 mm/h due to impeding clay-rich subsoil layers.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

In the context of the SCS curve number method, what happens to the Curve Number (CN) when a watershed transitions from dense forest cover to urban pavement?

Solution & Rationale: Urban paving increases impermeability and runoff potential, driving the Curve Number higher toward 100.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Which runoff estimation method is specifically recommended for small agricultural catchments where rainfall intensity data is available?

Solution & Rationale: The Rational Formula is universally applied for estimating peak discharge in small agricultural watersheds (< 50 ha).

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary effect of adopting conservation tillage (such as zero-tillage with crop stubble retention) on surface runoff?

Solution & Rationale: Conservation tillage leaves crop residue on the surface, which cushions raindrop impact, prevents crusting, and increases infiltration, thereby reducing surface runoff.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following conditions represents AMC-I in the USDA Curve Number procedure?

Solution & Rationale: AMC-I represents dry soil conditions where the watershed has experienced low rainfall in the 5 days prior to the design storm.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary reason for measuring water velocity using a current meter in an open agricultural channel?

Solution & Rationale: The area-velocity method requires measuring cross-sectional area (A) and flow velocity (V) via current meters to calculate discharge (Q = A * V).

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

How does an increase in watershed slope steepness influence the time of concentration (Tc)?

Solution & Rationale: Steeper slopes cause water to travel faster across the catchment, thereby shortening the time of concentration (Tc).

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which soil textural class is most likely to be categorized under USDA Hydrologic Group A?

Solution & Rationale: Deep coarse sands possess high permeability and rapid infiltration, placing them in Hydrologic Group A.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary limitation of applying the Rational Formula to large river basins?

Solution & Rationale: The Rational Formula assumes uniform rainfall over the entire catchment simultaneously, an assumption that fails in large river basins.
