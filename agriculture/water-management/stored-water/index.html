Introduction to Stored Water and Hydrological Principles

Water management in Indian agriculture is heavily reliant on both surface water and groundwater storage systems to mitigate spatial and temporal variabilities in monsoon precipitation. Stored water refers to the accumulation of surface runoff, river discharges, or groundwater in natural and artificial reservoirs, tanks, ponds, farm ponds, and check dams for subsequent agricultural utilization.

  • Importance in Agronomy: Ensures supplemental irrigation during dry spells (intermittent droughts), enabling protective and life-saving irrigation for high-value field crops like Triticum aestivum (wheat) and Oryza sativa (rice).
  • Hydrological Balance: Governed by the fundamental water balance equation: P + I = ET + R + D \pm \Delta S, where P is precipitation, I is irrigation, ET is evapotranspiration, R is surface runoff, D is deep percolation, and \Delta S is the change in stored water.
  • Reservoir Classification: Based on capacity and purpose, reservoirs are categorized into major (>10,000 ha m), medium (1,000 to 10,000 ha m), and minor (<1,000 ha m) irrigation projects, alongside micro-water harvesting structures.

Types of Water Storage Structures and Farm Ponds

Farm ponds and community tanks form the backbone of decentralized water storage in rainfed agro-ecosystems. Proper site selection, geometric design, and seepage control dictate the storage efficiency of these structures.

  • Excavated (Dugout) Ponds: Constructed by excavating soil in relatively flat topography; ideal for capturing local surface runoff and shallow groundwater seepage.
  • Embankment (Barrier) Ponds: Formed by constructing an earthen bund across a natural drainage line or stream valley; highly efficient for rolling and undulating topographies.
  • Seepage Control Measures: Unlined earthen ponds suffer heavy conveyance and storage losses through percolation. Lining materials include low-density polyethylene (LDPE) films (250 to 500 microns), bentonite clay, soil-cement stabilization (5:1 ratio), and brick masonry.
  • Evaporation Suppression: In arid and semi-arid regions, up to 30-40% of stored water is lost via evaporation. Chemical evaporation retardants such as cetyl alcohol (hexadecanol) and stearyl alcohol form monomolecular films that reduce evaporation losses by 20-30%.

Irrigation Efficiencies and Water Application Economics

Maximizing the agronomic productivity per unit volume of stored water requires rigorous management of conveyance, distribution, and field application efficiencies.

  • Conveyance Efficiency (E_c): Defined as the ratio of water delivered to the farm gate to the water diverted from the storage source. Minimized by adopting underground pipeline systems or lined channels.
  • Water Application Efficiency (E_a): The ratio of water stored in the root zone to the water delivered to the field. Improved significantly through pressurized micro-irrigation systems (drip and sprinkler).
  • Water Use Efficiency (WUE): Expressed as marketable crop yield per unit of water consumed (kg/ha/mm or kg/m^3). Can be substantially enhanced by deficit irrigation strategies during non-critical crop growth stages.
  • Conjunctive Use: The harmonized and simultaneous utilization of surface stored water and groundwater to prevent waterlogging, manage salinity build-up, and optimize overall irrigation scheduling.

Command Area Development and On-Farm Water Management

Command Area Development (CAD) programs bridge the gap between irrigation potential created and potential utilized through structural and agronomic interventions.

  • On-Farm Development (OFD) Works: Includes land leveling, shaping, construction of field channels (fahss), field drains, and creation of participatory water user associations (WUAs).
  • Warabandi System: A rotational water distribution system practiced extensively in northern India (Punjab, Haryana, Western Uttar Pradesh) ensuring equitable and volumetric allocation of stored canal water to farmers based on landholding size.
  • Drainage Integration: Stored water application without adequate subsurface drainage often triggers secondary salinization and sodification, particularly in arid and semi-arid canal commands. Installation of horizontal subsurface drainage or vertical drainage tubewells is imperative.

Agronomic Practices for Enhancing Stored Water Productivity

Agronomic management directly influences soil moisture conservation and crop water productivity under stored water regimes.

  • Mulching: Application of organic crop residues (e.g., paddy straw at 5 ext{ t/ha}) or synthetic polyethylene sheets to suppress soil evaporation and regulate soil temperatures.
  • Antitranspirants: Foliar application of stomatal closing types (phenylmercuric acetate, abscisic acid), reflection-reducing types (kaolin at 5%), and film-forming types (silicone oils, wax emulsions) to reduce transpirational water loss during severe moisture stress.
  • Conservation Tillage: Minimum tillage and zero-tillage systems (e.g., in Triticum aestivum following Oryza sativa) enhance soil organic matter, aggregate stability, and infiltration capacity while reducing evaporative moisture loss.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Water Use Efficiency (WUE) is highest under drip irrigation systems, reaching up to 90-95% compared to 30-40% in traditional flood irrigation.
  • Cetyl alcohol (hexadecanol) is the most widely recommended chemical compound for suppressing evaporation losses from open water storage reservoirs.
  • LDPE (Low-Density Polyethylene) film of 250 to 500 microns thickness is standard for lining farm ponds to control heavy seepage losses.
  • Warabandi is a rotational water distribution system practiced extensively in northern India for equitable allocation of canal-stored water.
  • Kaolin spray at 5% concentration acts as an antitranspirant by increasing leaf albedo and reducing leaf temperature under moisture stress.
  • The basic water balance equation for a reservoir is P + I = ET + R + D \pm \Delta S, where \Delta S represents the change in stored volume.
  • Subsurface drainage is vital in canal command areas to prevent secondary salinization and waterlogging caused by unlined stored water seepage.
  • Micro-watershed farm ponds are designed based on peak runoff rates calculated using the rational formula Q = CIA / 360.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Water Use Efficiency (WUE) with Water Application Efficiency (E_a). WUE relates crop yield to total water consumed (kg/m^3), whereas E_a relates root zone storage to water delivered to the field.
  • Assuming unlined farm ponds have negligible water losses; in sandy and loamy soils, seepage losses can exceed 50% of the total stored volume without synthetic or clay lining.
  • Believing antitranspirants increase crop yield under normal irrigated conditions; antitranspirants are exclusively beneficial under severe moisture stress conditions by restricting transpiration.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Stored Water Management and Conservation Agronomy

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Stored Water Dynamics and Hydrological Balance

Stored water management encompasses surface and subsurface accumulation mechanisms designed to bridge temporal water deficits in agriculture.

  • Water Balance: Fundamental accounting of inflows and outflows governing storage volume fluctuations.
  • Runoff Harvesting: Capturing excess precipitation in artificial reservoirs and farm ponds.
Concept 2

Seepage and Evaporation Loss Mitigation

Minimizing physical losses from storage structures is critical for maintaining high water availability during critical crop growth stages.

  • Seepage Control: Application of LDPE liners, bentonite, and soil-cement stabilization.
  • Evaporation Control: Use of monomolecular films like cetyl alcohol and physical floating covers.
Concept 3

Irrigation Efficiencies in Stored Water Utilization

Quantifying system performance through conveyance, application, and storage efficiencies ensures optimal agronomic returns.

  • Conveyance Efficiency: Minimized through closed underground pipe distribution networks.
  • Application Efficiency: Maximized by adopting pressurized micro-irrigation technologies.
Concept 4

Command Area and On-Farm Water Management

Integrating water storage projects with command area development ensures equitable distribution and environmental sustainability.

  • Warabandi System: Rotational volumetric water allocation guaranteeing reliable turn timings.
  • Conjunctive Use: Simultaneous deployment of surface stored water and groundwater.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Stored Water (Surface and Subsurface Reservoir Storage)
Accumulated water in natural or artificial structures utilized for supplementary agricultural irrigation during dry spells.
Water Use Efficiency (WUE (kg/m^3))
The economic yield of harvested crop produced per unit volume of water transpired or consumed by evapotranspiration.
Conveyance Efficiency (E_c = (W_f / W_d) imes 100)
The percentage of diverted water that successfully reaches the farm gate without conveyance losses.
Application Efficiency (E_a = (W_s / W_f) imes 100)
The percentage of delivered irrigation water that is effectively stored in the crop root zone.
Cetyl Alcohol (Hexadecanol (CH_3(CH_2)_{15}OH))
A chemical compound used to create monomolecular films on open water reservoirs to suppress evaporation.
Farm Pond (Excavated or Embankment Storage Structure)
A decentralized micro-water harvesting structure constructed on-farm to capture surface runoff for life-saving irrigation.
Warabandi (Rotational Water Distribution Schedule)
A fixed rotational schedule of water supply from canal outlets to farmers based on landholding size.
Antitranspirant (Stomatal and Reflection Regulators)
Chemical formulations applied to crop foliage to reduce transpirational water loss under moisture stress.
Conjunctive Use (Integrated Surface-Groundwater Utilization)
The coordinated management of surface stored water and groundwater to maximize water security and prevent salinization.
LDPE Lining (Low-Density Polyethylene Membrane)
Flexible plastic sheeting installed in farm ponds and canals to prevent excessive seepage percolation.
Command Area Development (CAD Programme)
An institutional framework designed to bridge the gap between irrigation potential created and utilized.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Conveyance Efficiency VS Application Efficiency

Conveyance efficiency measures water transport losses from source to farm gate, whereas application efficiency measures root zone storage losses at the field level.

Water Storage Efficiency VS Water Use Efficiency

Storage efficiency evaluates how well irrigation replenishes root zone moisture deficit, while water use efficiency evaluates crop yield output per unit water consumed.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical compound is most commonly utilized as a monomolecular film to suppress evaporation from open water storage reservoirs?

Explanation: Cetyl alcohol (hexadecanol) spreads across open water surfaces to form a microscopic monomolecular film, significantly reducing vapor pressure gradients and cutting evaporation losses by 20-30%.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard recommended thickness of Low-Density Polyethylene (LDPE) film used for lining farm ponds to prevent seepage losses?

Explanation: LDPE films of 250 to 500 microns thickness provide an optimal balance of mechanical strength, puncture resistance, and cost-effectiveness for lining earthen farm ponds.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

How is Water Use Efficiency (WUE) typically expressed in agronomic studies?

Explanation: Water Use Efficiency is defined as the economic yield of a crop produced per unit volume of water consumed, commonly expressed in kg/m^3 or kg/ha/mm.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which formula correctly represents Water Conveyance Efficiency (E_c)?

Explanation: E_c = (W_f / W_d) imes 100, where W_f is water delivered to the farm gate and W_d is water diverted from the storage source.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary operational objective of the 'Warabandi' water distribution system?

Explanation: Warabandi is a rotational water distribution system that ensures equitable distribution of canal-stored water among farmers proportional to their landholding size.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which category of antitranspirant does Kaolin (5%) belong to?

Explanation: Kaolin acts as a reflection-reducing antitranspirant (albedo type) that increases leaf reflectance, lowers canopy temperature, and reduces transpiration under moisture stress.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does the term \Delta S represent in the hydrological water balance equation for a reservoir?

Explanation: In the water balance equation (P + I = ET + R + D \pm \Delta S), \Delta S represents the net change in water storage within the reservoir over the given accounting period.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which irrigation method achieves the highest Water Application Efficiency (E_a)?

Explanation: Drip irrigation achieves the highest application efficiency (85-95%) because water is applied directly to the root zone with minimal evaporation, runoff, and deep percolation losses.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of On-Farm Development (OFD) works in command area management?

Explanation: OFD works including land leveling, field channels, and drainage ensure that water from storage projects reaches every farm efficiently, reducing the gap between potential created and utilized.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which agronomic practice is best suited for reducing soil evaporation and conserving stored soil moisture in rainfed crops?

Explanation: Mulching with organic crop residues (e.g., paddy straw) creates a physical barrier that intercepts solar radiation, suppresses weed growth, and drastically reduces soil evaporation.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is conjunctive use in agricultural water management?

Explanation: Conjunctive use involves the harmonized utilization of surface stored water and groundwater to maximize irrigation reliability, prevent waterlogging, and mitigate drought risks.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is classified as a stomatal-closing type antitranspirant?

Explanation: Abscisic acid (ABA) and phenylmercuric acetate act as stomatal-closing antitranspirants that induce stomatal closure, restricting transpirational water loss.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main hazard associated with unlined stored water irrigation in arid and semi-arid canal commands without proper drainage?

Explanation: Continuous unlined irrigation raises the groundwater table, leading to capillary rise of saline groundwater and subsequent secondary soil salinization and sodification.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In farm pond design, which type of pond is constructed by digging soil in relatively flat terrain?

Explanation: Excavated or dugout ponds are constructed by digging earth below natural ground level in flat topographies, primarily fed by surface runoff collection or shallow groundwater.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does Water Storage Efficiency (E_s) measure?

Explanation: Water Storage Efficiency evaluates how completely the water required in the root zone prior to irrigation is stored during the irrigation event.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which meteorological factor has the most dominant influence on the evaporation rate from open stored water bodies?

Explanation: Vapor pressure deficit (VPD), temperature, solar radiation, and wind velocity are the primary meteorological drivers governing evaporation rates from open water storage.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of a check dam in watershed management?

Explanation: Check dams are small barrier structures constructed across gullies and streams to reduce water velocity, trap sediment, and enhance localized groundwater recharge.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a surface-film forming antitranspirant?

Explanation: Wax emulsions, silicone oils, and plastic films act as surface-film forming antitranspirants by coating leaf surfaces with a thin hydrophobic layer that impedes water vapor escape.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why is deficit irrigation often practiced when utilizing stored water supplies during drought years?

Explanation: Deficit irrigation restricts water application to non-critical growth stages, spreading limited stored water over a larger area to maximize total agricultural productivity per unit volume.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of installing subsurface drainage in canal command areas?

Explanation: Subsurface drainage systems control rising water tables resulting from stored water irrigation seepage, preventing waterlogging and facilitating the leaching of harmful salts.

Which of the following management strategies best optimizes Water Use Efficiency (WUE) under limited stored water availability?

Solution & Rationale: Scheduling irrigations at critical growth stages (e.g., CRI in wheat, flowering in grain legumes) ensures maximum yield returns per unit of stored water consumed.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

What distinguishes an embankment farm pond from a dugout farm pond?

Solution & Rationale: Embankment ponds utilize natural topography by blocking a drainage line with an earthen bund, whereas dugout ponds are created by excavating soil on flat terrain.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

In the evaluation of irrigation project performance, which efficiency metric directly accounts for losses occurring between the storage reservoir and the individual farm boundary?

Solution & Rationale: Conveyance efficiency (E_c) evaluates water delivery performance from the storage source to the farm gate, capturing canal seepage and evaporation losses.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which antitranspirant group functions by physically blocking stomatal pores with a thin hydrophobic layer?

Solution & Rationale: Surface-film forming antitranspirants (wax emulsions, silicone oils) form a physical barrier over stomata, restricting water vapor diffusion out of the leaf.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary agronomic consequence of poor On-Farm Development (OFD) in a newly constructed stored water command area?

Solution & Rationale: Without OFD works (proper field channels and land leveling), head-end farmers over-irrigate leading to waterlogging, while tail-end farmers suffer severe moisture stress.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

How does the application of cetyl alcohol on a farm reservoir reduce evaporation losses?

Solution & Rationale: Cetyl alcohol forms a microscopic monomolecular film on the water surface, creating a barrier that impedes water molecules from escaping into the atmosphere as vapor.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

In the context of conjunctive use, what is the strategic role of groundwater pumping during peak irrigation demand periods when canal stored water is insufficient?

Solution & Rationale: Conjunctive use utilizes groundwater during periods of surface water shortage, which simultaneously helps lower high water tables and prevents waterlogged conditions.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which parameter is most critical when calculating the required storage capacity of a micro-watershed farm pond?

Solution & Rationale: Farm pond sizing is determined by hydrological calculations of catchment area, rainfall intensity, runoff coefficient, and total volumetric water yield.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary limitation of using plastic sheeting (LDPE) for lining farm ponds in high rodent-infested soils?

Solution & Rationale: Rodents and burrowing animals can puncture unprotected plastic liners, causing localized leakage and structural seepage failure unless covered with a protective soil-cement or clay layer.
