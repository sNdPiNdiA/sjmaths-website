Irrigation Sources: Hydrological Classification, Distribution, and Utilization in Indian Agriculture

Classification and Overview of Irrigation Sources in India

Irrigation sources in Indian agriculture are fundamentally divided into surface water sources and groundwater sources, dictated by regional topography, hydrogeology, and climatological regimes. Surface irrigation includes flow and lift irrigation from rivers, streams, and artificial storage reservoirs (dams and canals). Groundwater irrigation relies on deep tube-wells, shallow tube-wells, open dug wells, and artesian wells tapping into saturated unconfined and confined aquifers.

  • Surface Water Dominance: Historically dominated canal networks in Northern alluvial plains (e.g., Gangetic plains of Uttar Pradesh).
  • Groundwater Dominance: Tube-wells and pump sets constitute over 60% of net irrigated area in India due to on-demand availability and high water-use efficiency compared to unlined gravity canals.
  • Agro-Climatic Relevance: Tank irrigation is prominent in peninsular India (Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka) owing to undulating rocky terrain that facilitates natural or artificial impoundments.

Surface Irrigation Sources: Canals, Rivers, and Reservoirs

Surface water irrigation systems transport water from perennial or non-perennial rivers through diversion structures, barrages, and intricate canal distribution hierarchies. Canals are classified into perennial canals (drawing sustained supply via storage dams) and inundation canals (operating only during seasonal high river flows).

  • Canal Hierarchy: Main Canal → Branch Canal → Major Distributary → Minor Distributary → Watercourse (Command Area Development).
  • Command Area: Gross Command Area (GCA), Culturable Command Area (CCA), and Net Irrigated Area dictate the design capacity and water delivery schedules of irrigation projects.
  • UP Context: Uttar Pradesh possesses one of the largest canal networks in India, including the Upper Ganga Canal, Lower Ganga Canal, Sarda Canal system, and Gandak Canal, irrigating millions of hectares of major crops like Triticum aestivum (wheat) and Oryza sativa (rice).

Groundwater Irrigation Sources: Wells, Tube-Wells, and Aquifers

Groundwater extraction represents the backbone of intensive agriculture in the Indo-Gangetic alluvial belt. Water is abstracted from subsurface geological formations called aquifers using mechanical centrifugal or submersible pumpsets powered by electricity or diesel engines.

  • Open Dug Wells: Traditional masonry-lined pits tapping unconfined shallow phreatic aquifers, common in hard-rock regions of central and southern India.
  • Tube-Wells: Bored vertical holes lined with slotted pipes equipped with gravel packs to prevent sand pumping, categorized into shallow (<50 m) and deep (>100 m) commercial tube-wells.
  • Conjunctive Use: Scientific integration of surface water and groundwater to prevent waterlogging, soil salinization, and excessive groundwater table depletion.

Tank Irrigation and Traditional Water Harvesting Structures

Tank irrigation involves artificial or natural storage basins formed by constructing earthen bunds across small streams and drainage channels. It is extensively utilized in the crystalline rock formations of the Deccan Plateau where canal construction is unfeasible.

  • Earthen Embankments: Capture and store monsoon runoff for delayed application during dry spells (kharif supplementation or rabi pre-sowing).
  • Socio-Agronomic Significance: Traditional systems such as tanks (Kalyanis, Eri), Johads, and Ahars (in Bihar and eastern UP) serve as decentralized decentralized water security lifelines.
  • Limitation: High evaporation losses, siltation accumulation, and vulnerability to complete failure during consecutive meteorological droughts.

Hydrological Parameters and Irrigation Water Quality Metrics

Assessing the suitability of irrigation sources requires rigorous chemical and physical water quality analysis. Poor quality water leads to soil sodicity, salinity, and plant toxicity.

  • Electrical Conductivity (EC): Measures total dissolved solids (TDS) and salinity hazard, expressed in dS/m at 25°C.
  • Sodium Adsorption Ratio (SAR): Evaluates sodium hazard relative to calcium and magnesium concentrations, calculated as SAR = Na^+√(Ca2+ + Mg2+)/2. High SAR causes clay dispersion and soil structural degradation.
  • Residual Sodium Carbonate (RSC): Assesses carbonate and bicarbonate hazards: RSC = (CO_32- + HCO_3^-) - (Ca2+ + Mg2+).

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Uttar Pradesh has the largest canal irrigation network in India, led by the Sarda Canal system.
  • Electrical Conductivity (EC) measures the total dissolved salts in irrigation water and is expressed in dS/m.
  • Sodium Adsorption Ratio (SAR) formula evaluates sodium hazard against calcium and magnesium ions.
  • Tank irrigation is most predominant in the southern peninsular states, particularly Tamil Nadu and Karnataka.
  • Groundwater accounts for more than 60 percent of the total net irrigated area in contemporary Indian agriculture.
  • Gross Command Area (GCA) represents the total area that can be potentially irrigated by an irrigation project under optimal supply conditions.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Gross Command Area (GCA) with Culturable Command Area (CCA); remember that GCA includes uncultivable patches like roads and residential land within the boundary.
  • Assuming canal water has higher mineral solute concentration than deep tube-well water; deep groundwater often contains higher soluble salts and fluoride/arsenic contaminants.
  • Treating Electrical Conductivity (EC) as a measure of acidity rather than total dissolved ionic concentration.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Irrigation Sources: Hydrological Classification, Distribution, and Utilization in Indian Agriculture

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Classification of Irrigation Sources

Irrigation sources are categorized into surface and sub-surface systems based on their origin and mode of capture.

  • Surface Water: Includes flow and lift irrigation utilizing rivers, streams, and reservoir storage networks.
  • Groundwater: Comprises phreatic and deep confined aquifers tapped via open wells and modern tube-wells.
Concept 2

Canal Irrigation Engineering and Hierarchy

Canals operate through gravity flow from diversion works to farm gates.

  • Canal Hierarchy: Main canal feeds branch canals, which supply major and minor distributaries terminating at watercourses.
  • CCA: Culturable Command Area represents the actual cultivable portion within the irrigation project boundary.
Concept 3

Groundwater Dynamics and Tube-Wells

Subsurface water extraction enables precise and timely irrigation scheduling.

  • Aquifer: Porous geological formation capable of storing and yielding significant quantities of groundwater.
  • Conjunctive Use: Synchronized utilization of surface and groundwater supplies to optimize basin-wide water productivity.
Concept 4

Tank Irrigation Systems

Decentralized rainwater storage in undulating peninsular topography.

  • Tank Bunds: Earthen barriers constructed across drainage lines to impound monsoon runoff.
  • Eri / Ahar: Traditional regional nomenclature for tank irrigation structures in southern and eastern India.
Concept 5

Water Quality Indices

Chemical criteria dictating whether a water source is safe for sustained crop production.

  • SAR: Sodium Adsorption Ratio quantifying soil sodicity risks.
  • RSC: Residual Sodium Carbonate indicating carbonate precipitation potential in soils.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Gross Command Area (GCA)
The total geographical area bounded by an irrigation project that can be potentially irrigated, including both cultivable and uncultivable land.
Culturable Command Area (CCA)
The portion of the Gross Command Area that is actually cultivable and fit for agricultural crop production.
Perennial Canal (Perennial Irrigation Canal)
A canal designed to supply water throughout the year, fed by perennial rivers or large storage reservoirs.
Inundation Canal (Seasonal Inundation Canal)
A canal that draws water from rivers only during periods of high seasonal river flow or monsoon floods.
Aquifer (Subsurface Hydrological Reservoir)
A saturated geological formation of permeable rock, gravel, or sand that yields economic quantities of water to wells.
Electrical Conductivity (EC (dS/m))
The numerical measure of a solution's electrical current carrying capacity, directly proportional to total dissolved salts.
Sodium Adsorption Ratio (SAR Formula)
A mathematical index estimating the proportion of sodium ions relative to calcium and magnesium in irrigation water.
Residual Sodium Carbonate (RSC Index)
An analytical measure evaluating carbonate and bicarbonate hazards in relation to calcium and magnesium levels.
Tank Irrigation (Reservoir Impoundment System)
An irrigation method utilizing small artificial reservoirs formed by earthen embankments across seasonal streams.
Conjunctive Use (Integrated Water Management)
The coordinated management and use of surface water and groundwater resources to maximize water security.
Phreatic Aquifer (Unconfined Aquifer)
An aquifer having a water table under atmospheric pressure, freely fluctuating with recharge and discharge.
Duty of Water (Irrigation Duty)
The area of land that can be irrigated for a crop throughout its maturity by a continuous supply of one cubic meter per second.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Gross Command Area (GCA) VS Culturable Command Area (CCA)

GCA includes all land within project boundaries including barren and residential plots, whereas CCA includes only cultivable agricultural land.

Unconfined Aquifer VS Confined Aquifer

Unconfined aquifers have a free water table open to atmospheric pressure, while confined aquifers are sandwiched between impermeable strata under high hydrostatic pressure.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which state in India possesses the largest canal irrigation network?

Explanation: Uttar Pradesh has the largest canal irrigation network in India, spanning thousands of kilometers through major river systems like the Ganga, Sarda, and Yamuna.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does GCA stand for in irrigation project management?

Explanation: GCA stands for Gross Command Area, which encompasses the total geographical area that can potentially be irrigated by an irrigation project, including both cultivable and uncultivable patches.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which irrigation source is most dominant in the peninsular region of India (e.g., Tamil Nadu)?

Explanation: Tank irrigation is predominant in peninsular India due to undulating rocky topography and hard-rock geology which facilitates the construction of earthen storage bunds.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of calculating the Sodium Adsorption Ratio (SAR) of irrigation water?

Explanation: SAR evaluates the sodium hazard in irrigation water. High SAR values cause calcium and magnesium to be replaced by sodium on exchange complexes, leading to soil dispersion and structural breakdown.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which unit is standard for expressing Electrical Conductivity (EC) of irrigation water?

Explanation: Electrical Conductivity is expressed in deciSiemens per meter (dS/m) at 25 degrees Celsius, indicating the total concentration of dissolved ionized salts in the water sample.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the correct hierarchical order of canal water distribution from source to field?

Explanation: The correct hierarchy of canal distribution is Main Canal → Branch Canal → Distributary (Major/Minor) → Minor → Watercourse.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

An aquifer in which the water table serves as the upper boundary under atmospheric pressure is known as:

Explanation: An unconfined or phreatic aquifer has its upper surface bounded by a water table exposed directly to atmospheric pressure.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does RSC stand for in irrigation water quality analysis?

Explanation: RSC stands for Residual Sodium Carbonate, which evaluates the excess of carbonate and bicarbonate over calcium and magnesium in irrigation water.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following irrigation sources provides the highest level of on-demand water control to farmers?

Explanation: Tube-wells provide high on-demand control because farmers can operate pumps independently according to critical crop water requirements, unlike rigid canal delivery rotations.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is Culturable Command Area (CCA)?

Explanation: Culturable Command Area (CCA) represents the actual cultivable portion within the Gross Command Area where agricultural operations can take place.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a major disadvantage of tank irrigation?

Explanation: Tank irrigation suffers from very high water losses due to large surface areas exposed to solar evaporation and high seepage rates through earthen bunds.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In the formula for SAR, which ions are placed in the denominator?

Explanation: The denominator of the SAR formula contains the square root of the average concentration of calcium and magnesium ions: \sqrt{(Ca2+ + Mg2+)/2}.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Canals that operate only during periods of high river flow, particularly during the monsoon season, are called:

Explanation: Inundation canals take off from rivers without any diversion weir or barrage and operate only when the parent river is in flood or high stage.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary ecological risk associated with excessive groundwater extraction in alluvial plains?

Explanation: Over-extraction of groundwater leads to declining water tables, increased pumping costs, and potential land subsidence or saltwater intrusion in coastal tracts.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What term describes the integrated use of surface water and groundwater to maximize irrigation efficiency?

Explanation: Conjunctive use refers to the coordinated and scientific management of surface water and groundwater supplies to optimize irrigation water availability and prevent environmental degradation.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which geological formation acts as an aquiclude?

Explanation: An aquiclude is a solid, impermeable geological formation (such as heavy clay) that contains water but does not transmit it readily to wells.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following parameters is critical when evaluating the salinity hazard of irrigation water?

Explanation: Electrical Conductivity (EC) is the primary index used to evaluate the overall salinity hazard of irrigation water.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical depth range of shallow tube-wells commonly installed in Indian alluvial regions?

Explanation: Shallow tube-wells typically extend to depths of less than 50 meters, tapping upper phreatic aquifers using surface-mounted centrifugal pumps.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which traditional water harvesting structure is prominent in the state of Bihar and eastern Uttar Pradesh?

Explanation: Ahars are traditional floodwater harvesting systems comprising rectangular embankments constructed in undulating terrain, particularly prominent in Bihar and eastern Uttar Pradesh.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate the Sodium Adsorption Ratio (SAR) if sodium concentration is 20 meq/L, calcium is 4 meq/L, and magnesium is 2 meq/L.

Solution & Rationale: Using formula SAR = Na√(Ca + Mg)/2 = 20√(4 + 2)/2 = 20√3 = 201.732 ≈ 11.55.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following distinguishes a confined aquifer from an unconfined aquifer?

Solution & Rationale: A confined aquifer is sandwiched between two impermeable layers (aquicludes or aquitards) and water is under hydrostatic pressure, whereas unconfined aquifers have a free water table.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

In canal irrigation terminology, what does a 'Distributary' specifically feed?

Solution & Rationale: Distributaries branch off from major canals or branch canals and supply water to minors and eventually to watercourses for farm-level application.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

If an irrigation water sample has an Electrical Conductivity (EC) of 2.5 dS/m, how is its salinity hazard classified according to US Salinity Laboratory standards?

Solution & Rationale: USSL classification: C1 (0.1-0.25 dS/m), C2 (0.25-0.75 dS/m), C3 (0.75-2.25 dS/m or up to 3.0 dS/m classified as high C3), where 2.5 dS/m falls under high salinity hazard (C3).

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary agronomic consequence of high Residual Sodium Carbonate (RSC) in irrigation water?

Solution & Rationale: High RSC causes calcium and magnesium to precipitate as carbonates, increasing the relative proportion of sodium, which results in sodic (black alkali) soil formation.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Which type of well is constructed by digging a wide-diameter pit through soil down to the water table and lining it with brick or stone masonry?

Solution & Rationale: Open dug wells are traditional large-diameter unconfined wells lined with masonry to prevent collapse, utilized extensively for lift irrigation.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

What does the term 'Duty' of irrigation water represent?

Solution & Rationale: Duty is defined as the relationship between the volume of water and the area of crop it can mature, expressed as hectares per cumec of continuous flow.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Why are unlined canals prone to massive water losses?

Solution & Rationale: Unlined earthen canals suffer huge conveyance losses due to percolation and seepage through permeable perimeter soils, alongside evaporative losses.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Which region in India is historically most famous for 'Ahars' and 'Pynes' as indigenous irrigation and flood-water harvesting systems?

Solution & Rationale: Ahars and Pynes are traditional indigenous irrigation systems developed in the floodplains and undulating tracts of south Bihar and eastern Uttar Pradesh.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary role of gravel packing in tube-well construction?

Solution & Rationale: Gravel packing placed around the slotted well screen acts as a natural hydraulic filter that prevents fine sand from entering the pump while allowing clear water to flow freely.
