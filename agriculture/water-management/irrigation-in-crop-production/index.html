Fundamentals of Irrigation and Water Requirement

Irrigation is the artificial application of water to the soil to supplement natural precipitation and ensure optimum crop growth. Water requirement (WR) of a crop refers to the total quantity of water needed for crop production from sowing to harvesting, including consumptive use (evapotranspiration, ET), metabolic needs, and water losses during application (such as deep percolation and run-off).

  • Consumptive Use (CU): The quantity of water used by the vegetative growth of a crop through transpiration and evaporation from adjacent soil surfaces, expressed in depth units (mm or cm).
  • Duty of Water: The area of land that can be irrigated by a continuous supply of one cusec (cubic foot per second) of water throughout the base period of the crop, expressed in hectares per cusec (ha/cusec).
  • Delta of Water (\Delta): The total depth of water (in cm or meters) required by a crop from sowing to harvest, distributed across all irrigation events.
  • Relationship between Duty (D), Delta (\Delta), and Base period (B):
    \Delta (meters) = 8.64 * B (days) / D (hectares/cusec)

Critical Stages of Irrigation in Major Field Crops

Applying irrigation at moisture-sensitive physiological stages is critical for maximizing crop yield and water-use efficiency (WUE). Moisture stress during these periods causes irreversible yield reduction.

  • Wheat (Triticum aestivum): Crown Root Initiation (CRI) at 20-25 days after sowing (DAS) is the most critical stage. Other stages include Tillering (40-45 DAS), Late Jointing (60-65 DAS), Flowering (80-85 DAS), Milk stage (105 DAS), and Dough stage (120 DAS).
  • Maize (Zea mays): Tasseling and Silking are the most critical stages; moisture stress here drastically reduces grain setting.
  • Rice (Oryza sativa): Panicle Initiation (PI), Flowering, and Milking stages are highly sensitive to water stress. Continuous submergence of 5 cm depth is generally maintained from transplanting to 10 days before harvest.
  • Chickpea (Cicer arietinum): Pre-flowering and Pod-formation stages are critical.
  • Sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum): Formative phase (tillering stage) is the most critical; drought during this phase halts cane elongation.

Irrigation Methods and Water Application Efficiency

Selection of an appropriate irrigation method depends on topography, soil texture, water availability, and crop type.

  • Surface Irrigation Methods: Includes flooding, check basin, border strip, and furrow methods. Best suited for medium- to heavy-textured soils with gentle slopes.
  • Sprinkler Irrigation: Overhead application mimicking natural rainfall. Highly efficient for undulating topography, sandy soils, and closely spaced crops like wheat, pulses, and oilseeds. Saves 30-50% water compared to surface methods.
  • Drip (Trickle) Irrigation: Application of water directly to the root zone drop by drop. Achieves the highest water-use efficiency (80-95%) and is ideal for wide-row horticultural and vegetable crops (e.g., tomato, Lycopersicon esculentum; banana, Musa paradisiaca).
  • Sub-surface Drainage and Irrigation: Controlled water table management used in waterlogged or heavy clay soils.

Water Measurement and Irrigation Scheduling Criteria

Accurate measurement of irrigation water is essential for efficient farm water management. Common devices include Parshall flumes, V-notch weirs, and rectangular weirs.

  • Soil Moisture Tension: Tensiometers are used to schedule irrigation in sandy to medium soils at low tension (0 to 0.8 bar), while resistance blocks are used for higher tensions.
  • IW/CPE Ratio: Irrigation Water (IW) to Cumulative Pan Evaporation (CPE) ratio is widely used for scientific irrigation scheduling. An IW/CPE ratio of 0.75 or 1.0 means irrigation is applied when cumulative pan evaporation equals 1.33 or 1.0 times the depth of irrigation water applied (usually 50 mm).
  • Depletion of Available Soil Moisture (ASM): Irrigation is typically scheduled when 50% of the Available Soil Moisture is depleted in the root zone.

Drainage in Crop Production and Salinity Management

Excess irrigation or poor natural drainage leads to waterlogging, soil salinization, and sodicity, severely impairing crop growth.

  • Waterlogging Effects: Depletion of oxygen in the root zone, accumulation of toxic carbon dioxide, reduction in nutrient uptake (especially nitrogen and phosphorus), and shallow root development.
  • Surface Drainage: Removal of excess surface water through field ditches, land grading, and bed-and-furrow systems.
  • Sub-surface Drainage: Installation of perforated pipes or tile drains at predefined depths to lower the high water table and leach out excess soluble salts.
  • Reclamation of Saline-Sodic Soils: Use of gypsum ( ext{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2 ext{H}_2 ext{O}) as an amendment to replace exchangeable sodium with calcium, followed by heavy leaching of salts through ponding.

  • Drip irrigation achieves the highest water use efficiency (up to 90-95%) among all irrigation systems.
  • IW/CPE ratio of 0.75 to 1.0 is standard for scheduling irrigations in wheat under semi-arid conditions.
  • Tensiometer measures soil moisture tension and works best in sandy soils up to 0.8 bar tension.
  • Gypsum ( ext{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2 ext{H}_2 ext{O}) is the primary chemical amendment used for reclaiming sodic (alkali) soils.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing CRI stage with tillering stage in wheat; CRI occurs earlier (20-25 DAS) and affects crown root development, whereas tillering occurs around 40-45 DAS.
  • Assuming duty and delta are independent parameters; they are inversely proportional for a given base period.
  • Confusing field capacity (0.33 bar) with permanent wilting point (15 bar) regarding soil moisture tension limits.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Irrigation In Crop Production

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Soil-Water-Plant Relationships

Understanding soil moisture constants is vital for determining irrigation schedules and net irrigation depths.

  • Field Capacity: Soil moisture content held against gravity at -0.33 bar tension.
  • Wilting Point: Soil moisture tension at -15.0 bar where plants can no longer extract water.
  • Available Water: The water retained between field capacity and permanent wilting point available for plant uptake.
Concept 2

Irrigation Scheduling Criteria

Methods used to determine when to irrigate crops to prevent moisture stress and optimize water productivity.

  • IW/CPE Approach: Ratio of irrigation water depth to cumulative pan evaporation.
  • Critical Crop Stages: Physiological phases where water stress causes irreversible yield penalties.
  • Soil Moisture Depletion: Replenishing water when 50% of available soil moisture is depleted.
Concept 3

Modern Irrigation Efficiency

Maximizing agronomic output per drop of water through advanced micro-irrigation technologies.

  • Sprinkler System: Overhead spraying suitable for undulating terrain and sandy soils.
  • Drip System: Point-source application delivering water directly to the plant root zone.
  • Water Use Efficiency: Ratio of marketable crop yield to total water consumed.
Concept 4

Drainage and Salinity Control

Preventing land degradation caused by waterlogging and excess salt accumulation.

  • Surface Drainage: Removal of ponded water via surface channels and land smoothing.
  • Subsurface Drainage: Lowering water table using perforated buried pipes.
  • Leaching Fraction: Fraction of applied water that percolates below the root zone to remove salts.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Consumptive Use (Evapotranspiration (ET))
Total water transpired by crop plants and evaporated from adjacent soil surfaces.
Duty of Water (Irrigation Duty (D))
Area of land in hectares irrigated by 1 cusec of continuous water flow over the base period.
Delta of Water (Total Water Depth (\Delta))
Total depth of irrigation water required by a crop from sowing to harvest.
CRI Stage (Crown Root Initiation)
Most critical irrigation stage in wheat occurring 20-25 days after sowing.
Field Capacity (Maximum Soil Retention)
Soil moisture content held at -0.33 bar tension after gravitational drainage ceases.
Permanent Wilting Point (PWP (-15 bar))
Soil moisture tension at which plants permanently wilt and cannot recover.
IW/CPE Ratio (Irrigation Water / Pan Evaporation)
Index used for scientific scheduling of irrigations based on atmospheric evaporative demand.
Tensiometer (Soil Moisture Tension Meter)
Instrument used to measure soil moisture suction in unsaturated soils.
Parshall Flume (Open Channel Flow Meter)
Standard calibrated device used for measuring irrigation water discharge in open channels.
Gypsum Requirement (Sodic Soil Amendment ( ext{CaSO}_4))
Quantity of agricultural gypsum required to replace exchangeable sodium in sodic soils.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Saline Soil VS Sodic (Alkali) Soil

Saline soils have high EC (>4 dS/m) and low ESP (<15), whereas sodic soils have low EC (<4 dS/m) and high ESP (>15) with high pH (>8.5).

Duty VS Delta

Duty is the area irrigated per unit water flow rate (ha/cusec), while Delta is the total depth of water applied (cm or meters).

Which of the following is the most critical stage for irrigation in wheat (Triticum aestivum)?

Explanation: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) at 20-25 DAS is the most critical moisture-sensitive stage in wheat. Moisture stress here causes severe yield reduction.

Question 2 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the soil moisture tension at Permanent Wilting Point (PWP)?

Explanation: Permanent Wilting Point corresponds to a soil moisture tension of 15.0 bars (1.5 MPa), beyond which roots cannot extract water.

Question 3 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which irrigation method achieves the highest water-use efficiency?

Explanation: Drip irrigation achieves the highest water-use efficiency (85-95%) by minimizing evaporation, runoff, and deep percolation losses.

Question 4 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What does 'Delta' of water refer to in agronomy?

Explanation: Delta (\Delta) represents the total depth of water (in cm or m) required by a crop during its entire base period.

Question 5 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical amendment is universally recommended for reclaiming sodic (alkali) soils?

Explanation: Agricultural gypsum ( ext{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2 ext{H}_2 ext{O}) provides ext{Ca}^{2+} ions which replace excess ext{Na}^{+} on soil colloids in sodic soils.

Question 6 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the soil moisture tension at Field Capacity?

Explanation: Field capacity represents the water content retained in soil after gravitational drainage has ceased, typically at -0.33 bar tension.

Question 7 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which instrument is best suited for measuring soil moisture tension in sandy soils?

Explanation: Tensiometers measure soil water suction (tension) up to 0.8 bar and are very effective in sandy to medium-textured soils.

Question 8 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of scheduling irrigation using the IW/CPE ratio?

Explanation: The IW/CPE ratio links irrigation scheduling directly to open-pan evaporation, reflecting real-time climatic demand.

Question 9 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop is most sensitive to water stagnation during early vegetative growth?

Explanation: Soybean (Glycine max) suffers severe root asphyxiation and yield reduction under waterlogged conditions during early growth.

Question 10 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the relationship between Duty (D), Delta (\Delta), and Base period (B)?

Explanation: The standard hydrological formula is \Delta = 8.64 B / D, where \Delta is in meters, B in days, and D in ha/cusec.

Question 11 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which device is commonly used for measuring open channel irrigation water discharge?

Explanation: Parshall flumes and V-notch weirs are standard hydraulic structures utilized for measuring water flow rates in open channels.

Question 12 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is consumptive use of water primarily composed of?

Explanation: Consumptive use is the sum of water lost through plant transpiration and soil evaporation, plus the minor amount retained in plant tissues.

Question 13 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which irrigation method is best suited for undulating topography and sandy soils?

Explanation: Sprinkler irrigation is ideal for undulating topography and light sandy soils where leveling is impractical and infiltration is high.

Question 14 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which critical stage of maize is extremely sensitive to moisture stress?

Explanation: Tasseling and silking in maize are highly sensitive; water stress during this window causes poor pollination and barren cobs.

Question 15 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What causes permanent wilting point in plants?

Explanation: At 15 bar tension, soil moisture is held so tightly by soil particles that plant roots cannot exert enough suction to absorb water.

Question 16 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended water management practice in lowland transplanted rice?

Explanation: Lowland rice requires continuous submergence of about 5 cm depth from transplanting until 10 days before harvest for optimal growth.

Question 17 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary effect of waterlogging on crop plants?

Explanation: Waterlogging displaces soil air, depleting oxygen and causing root asphyxiation, nutrient deficiency, and stunted growth.

Question 18 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

How is Water Use Efficiency (WUE) generally expressed?

Explanation: Agronomic water use efficiency is expressed as kg/ha-mm or kg/m³ of water consumed by the crop.
