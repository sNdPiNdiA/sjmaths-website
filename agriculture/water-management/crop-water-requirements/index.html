Introduction to Crop Water Requirements and Terminology

Crop water requirement (CWR) is defined as the total water depth needed by a crop from planting to harvest for optimal growth, development, and yield under field conditions. It includes water consumed by the crop through evapotranspiration (ET), water lost through deep percolation, and water required for special operations such as land preparation, puddling, and leaching of salts.

  • Consumptive Use (CU): The quantity of water used by the vegetative growth of a crop for transpiration and building of plant tissues, plus the evaporated water from the adjacent soil surface in a specified time.
  • Evapotranspiration (ET): The combined process of evaporation from soil and plant surfaces and transpiration from plant tissues.
  • Potential Evapotranspiration (PET): The rate at which evapotranspiration occurs from an extended surface of short green crop, completely covering the ground, of uniform height, and adequately supplied with water.

Estimation of Reference Evapotranspiration (ET0)

The estimation of reference evapotranspiration (ET0) forms the baseline for calculating specific crop water needs. Several empirical and physics-based formulas are utilized in agronomy:

  • Penman-Monteith Equation: Recommended by the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO-56) as the standard method for determining ET0, as it incorporates radiation, air temperature, air humidity, and wind speed.
  • Modified Pan Evaporimeter Method: Uses a Class A evaporation pan where evaporation (E_{pan}) is multiplied by a pan coefficient (K_{pan}) to estimate ET0.
  • Blaney-Criddle Method: Relies on mean daily temperature and percentage of daytime hours, widely used in arid regions with limited meteorological data.
  • Hargreaves Method: Temperature-based method requiring maximum, minimum, and mean temperatures along with extraterrestrial radiation.

Crop Coefficient (Kc) and Effective Rainfall

Crop water requirements are not static throughout the growing season; they vary with crop phenology. The relationship between crop water requirement and reference evapotranspiration is expressed via the crop coefficient (K_c).

  • Crop Coefficient (K_c): Defined as the ratio of crop evapotranspiration (ET_c) to reference evapotranspiration (ET_0). Mathematically: ET_c = K_c imes ET_0.
  • Stages of K_c Variation: Initial stage (low K_c due to sparse canopy), crop development stage (increasing K_c), mid-season stage (peak K_c at full canopy cover), and late-season stage (declining K_c as senescence sets in).
  • Effective Rainfall (P_e): That portion of total precipitation that is effectively used by the crop to meet its consumptive use requirements, excluding surface runoff, deep percolation, and canopy interception.

Irrigation Water Requirement (IWR) and Net Irrigation Requirement (NIR)

Translating field-level consumption into practical irrigation scheduling requires accounting for moisture deficits and system efficiencies.

  • Net Irrigation Requirement (NIR): The depth of water required to bring the soil moisture content in the root zone up to field capacity. Calculated as: NIR = ET_c - P_e - G_e + WL (where G_e is groundwater contribution and WL is water loss).
  • Gross Irrigation Requirement (GIR): The total amount of water diverted from the source, accounting for application and conveyance losses. Calculated as: GIR = rac{NIR}{ ext{Field Irrigation Efficiency } (\eta_f)}.
  • Water Use Efficiency (WUE): The economic or crop yield produced per unit of water consumed or applied. Expressed in kg/ha-mm or tonnes/ha-cm.

Critical Stages of Irrigation for Major Field Crops

Moisture stress at critical phenological stages causes irreversible yield reductions. Agronomists must schedule irrigations precisely:

  • Wheat (Triticum aestivum): Crown Root Initiation (CRI) stage (20-25 Days After Sowing) is the absolute most critical stage. Other stages include tillering, flowering, and milking.
  • Rice (Oryza sativa): Panicle initiation, flowering, and milking stages are extremely sensitive to moisture stress. Continuous submergence of 5 cm is maintained except during weeding and tillering drainage.
  • Maize (Zea mays): Tasseling and silking stages are extremely critical; moisture stress causes pollination failure and barren cobs.
  • Gram / Chickpea (Cicer arietinum): Pre-flowering and pod-filling stages.

Formula for Gross Irrigation Requirement (GIR)

Gross equals Net divided by Efficiency. Since application and conveyance losses always exist, GIR is always greater than NIR.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • The FAO-56 Penman-Monteith method is internationally accepted as the standard for calculating reference evapotranspiration (ET0).
  • Crown Root Initiation (CRI) in wheat occurs approximately 20-25 days after sowing and is the single most critical irrigation stage.
  • Crop coefficient (Kc) varies across crop growth stages, reaching its peak value during the mid-season stage.
  • Water Use Efficiency (WUE) is expressed in kg/ha-mm and is highest under drip and sprinkler irrigation systems.
  • Consumptive use includes evaporation from adjacent soil surfaces and plant transpiration.
  • Effective rainfall is the fraction of total rainfall utilized by the crop to meet its consumptive use requirements.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Net Irrigation Requirement (NIR) with Gross Irrigation Requirement (GIR). Remember that GIR includes irrigation efficiency losses.
  • Assuming crop water requirements remain constant throughout the crop season; in reality, Kc changes dynamically across phenological stages.
  • Equating potential evapotranspiration (PET) with actual evapotranspiration (AET) under drought conditions; AET is lower than PET when soil moisture is limiting.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Crop Water Requirements in Agronomy and Irrigation Management

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Consumptive Use and Evapotranspiration Fundamentals

Water consumed by crops is partitioned into evaporation from soil and transpiration through stomata.

  • Consumptive Use: Total water loss through ET plus metabolic water retained in plant tissues.
  • Lysimeter: Precision instrument used for direct measurement of evapotranspiration under field conditions.
Concept 2

FAO Penman-Monteith and ET0 Estimation

Reference crop evapotranspiration (ET0) represents the evaporative demand of the atmosphere independently of crop type.

  • ET0 Standard: Defined for a hypothetical grass reference crop with an assumed height of 0.12 m, surface resistance of 70 s/m, and albedo of 0.23.
Concept 3

Crop Coefficient Dynamics

The crop coefficient integrates plant canopy characteristics with atmospheric demand.

  • Kc Peak: Occurs during the mid-season phase when leaf area index (LAI) is maximum.
Concept 4

Irrigation Requirement Calculations

Translating crop water demand into field application depths requires accurate efficiency factors.

  • Field Efficiency: Ratio of water stored in root zone to water applied in the field.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Wheat critical irrigation stage: CRI (20-25 DAS).
  • Maize critical irrigation stage: Tasseling and Silking.
  • Formula: GIR = NIR / Efficiency.
  • ETc = Kc * ET0.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Crop water requirement equals evapotranspiration plus water needed for special operations and deep percolation.
  • FAO Penman-Monteith method is the standard for estimating ET0.
  • Crop coefficient (Kc) varies across four distinct growth stages.
  • Gross irrigation requirement is always higher than net irrigation requirement due to irrigation efficiencies.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Net Irrigation Requirement VS Gross Irrigation Requirement

NIR is the exact moisture deficit in the root zone, whereas GIR includes system water losses.

Potential ET VS Actual ET

PET assumes unlimited water supply, while AET reflects actual moisture availability in soil.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary method recommended by FAO-56 for estimating reference evapotranspiration (ET0)?

Explanation: The FAO-56 Penman-Monteith equation is internationally accepted as the standard method for estimating reference crop evapotranspiration due to its high physical rigor.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following equations correctly represents crop evapotranspiration (ETc)?

Explanation: Crop evapotranspiration (ETc) is calculated by multiplying the reference evapotranspiration (ET0) by the crop coefficient (Kc).

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which is the most critical irrigation stage in wheat (Triticum aestivum)?

Explanation: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) occurring 20-25 DAS is the most critical stage for wheat irrigation; moisture stress here causes severe yield loss.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

How is Gross Irrigation Requirement (GIR) related to Net Irrigation Requirement (NIR)?

Explanation: GIR is calculated by dividing NIR by the field irrigation efficiency to account for conveyance and application losses.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does Lysimeter measure in agronomic research?

Explanation: A lysimeter is a device used to measure the quantity of water lost through evapotranspiration and deep percolation from a bounded soil volume.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

At which crop growth stage is the crop coefficient (Kc) typically at its maximum value?

Explanation: The mid-season stage exhibits the highest Kc value because the crop has full canopy cover and maximum transpiration rates.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Effective rainfall refers to:

Explanation: Effective rainfall is that portion of total rainfall that is effectively utilized by the crop for its consumptive use.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following crops is extremely sensitive to moisture stress at the silking and tasseling stage?

Explanation: Maize (Zea mays) is highly sensitive during tasseling and silking; moisture stress results in poor pollination and reduced grain set.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Water Use Efficiency (WUE) is mathematically expressed in which of the following units?

Explanation: Water Use Efficiency is expressed as kg of harvested yield per hectare-millimeter (kg/ha-mm) of water consumed or applied.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is Potential Evapotranspiration (PET)?

Explanation: Potential evapotranspiration is the rate of ET from an extended surface of short green crop, completely covering the ground, with unlimited water supply.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In sprinkler irrigation systems, what is the typical range of field irrigation efficiency?

Explanation: Sprinkler irrigation efficiency generally ranges between 60% and 75%, whereas surface irrigation is 40-50% and drip is 85-90%.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What happens to the crop coefficient (Kc) during the late-season or ripening stage?

Explanation: During the late-season stage, Kc decreases due to crop senescence, leaf drying, and reduced transpiration demand.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is NOT a component of consumptive use (CU)?

Explanation: Consumptive use comprises plant transpiration, soil evaporation, and metabolic water in tissue. Deep percolation is considered a loss, not CU.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which irrigation scheduling approach uses the depletion of Available Soil Moisture (ASM)?

Explanation: Scheduling based on soil moisture depletion uses Management Allowed Depletion (MAD) of available soil moisture to trigger irrigation.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the full form of IW/CPE ratio used in irrigation scheduling?

Explanation: IW/CPE stands for Irrigation Water to Cumulative Pan Evaporation ratio, pioneered by DR Bhumbla and researchers in India for scientific irrigation scheduling.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop requires continuous submergence (puddling) for optimum growth?

Explanation: Rice (Oryza sativa) is traditionally grown under flooded conditions with standing water of 5 cm to suppress weeds and maintain anaerobic root zone conditions.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why is deep percolation considered inefficient in crop water management?

Explanation: Deep percolation moves water and dissolved nutrients (especially nitrogen) below the effective root zone, leading to nutrient leaching and water wastage.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following irrigation methods achieves the highest water use efficiency?

Explanation: Drip (trickle) irrigation achieves efficiencies of 85-90%, minimizing evaporation and deep percolation losses.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is Management Allowed Depletion (MAD)?

Explanation: MAD represents the fraction of available soil moisture that is allowed to be depleted before the next irrigation is applied to prevent yield reduction.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In the context of crop water requirements, what does 'advection' refer to?

Explanation: Advection is the horizontal transfer of sensible heat from dry surrounding areas into an irrigated field, increasing local evapotranspiration.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGR Standard

Calculate the Gross Irrigation Requirement (GIR) if the Net Irrigation Requirement (NIR) is 60 mm and the field irrigation efficiency is 75%.

Solution & Rationale: GIR = NIR / Efficiency = 60 / 0.75 = 80 mm.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

If reference evapotranspiration (ET0) is 5.0 mm/day and the crop coefficient (Kc) at mid-season is 1.2, what is the crop evapotranspiration (ETc)?

Solution & Rationale: ETc = Kc * ET0 = 1.2 * 5.0 = 6.0 mm/day.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGR Standard

Which of the following phenological stages is the most sensitive to water deficit in chickpea (Cicer arietinum)?

Solution & Rationale: Pre-flowering and pod-filling stages are critical in chickpea; water stress during pod filling leads to shriveled grains and drastic yield reduction.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary objective of applying the IW/CPE ratio in irrigation scheduling?

Solution & Rationale: The IW/CPE approach triggers irrigation when cumulative pan evaporation reaches a specific value relative to the irrigation water depth applied.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which instrument is specifically designed to measure soil moisture tension for irrigation scheduling?

Solution & Rationale: A tensiometer measures soil water suction (tension) in centibars, guiding irrigation scheduling in coarse to medium-textured soils.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGR Standard

What is the typical water requirement range for rice (Oryza sativa) from transplanting to harvest?

Solution & Rationale: Rice requires a high amount of water, typically ranging between 900 mm and 2500 mm depending on percolation rates, climate, and duration.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which term describes the loss of water vapor specifically from plant stomata?

Solution & Rationale: Transpiration is the physiological process where water is absorbed by roots and released as vapor primarily through stomata in leaves.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGR Standard

In arid regions, why is actual evapotranspiration (AET) often less than potential evapotranspiration (PET)?

Solution & Rationale: PET assumes unlimited water availability; when soil dries out, actual ET drops below PET due to moisture stress.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which irrigation system has the highest conveyance efficiency because water is transported through closed pipes?

Solution & Rationale: Drip and sprinkler systems use pressurized closed pipelines, eliminating open-channel conveyance losses such as seepage and evaporation.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGR Standard

What is the consequence of over-irrigation on crop production and soil health?

Solution & Rationale: Over-irrigation induces waterlogging, suffocates roots, leaches nitrates, and causes secondary salinization as groundwater tables rise.
