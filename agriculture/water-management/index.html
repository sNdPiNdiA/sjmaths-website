Fundamentals of Soil-Water-Plant Relationships

Understanding the dynamic interactions between soil, water, and plants is paramount for effective irrigation management. Soil moisture constants define the availability of water for crop uptake and dictate irrigation scheduling parameters.

  • Saturation: All soil pores are completely filled with water, and the soil water potential is approximately zero (0 bars).
  • Field Capacity (FC): The amount of soil moisture retained after excess gravitational water has drained away (usually 24 to 48 hours after heavy rainfall or saturation), corresponding to a matric potential of -1/3 bar (-33 kPa) for loamy soils.
  • Permanent Wilting Point (PWP): The soil moisture content at which plants permanently wilt and cannot recover even when placed in a saturated atmosphere, corresponding to a soil water potential of -15 bars (-1500 kPa).
  • Available Water Capacity (AWC): The range of water storable in the soil that can be extracted by plants, calculated mathematically as the difference between Field Capacity and Permanent Wilting Point (AWC = FC - PWP).

Classification and Measurement of Irrigation Water

Quantifying irrigation water application and evaluating water quality are critical agronomic competencies. Water measurement units vary from volumetric to rate-based metrics.

  • Units of Measurement: One hectare-centimeter (ha-cm) equals 100,000 liters of water. One hectare-meter (ha-m) equals 10,000 cubic meters (10^4 m^3).
  • Measurement Devices: Parshall flumes, V-notch weirs, and rectangular weirs are standard hydraulic structures utilized in open channels to measure discharge rates.
  • Water Quality Parameters: Salinity hazard is evaluated using Electrical Conductivity (EC); sodicity hazard is measured by the Sodium Adsorption Ratio (SAR); and residual sodium carbonate (RSC) indicates bicarbonate hazards.

Methods of Irrigation

Selecting the appropriate irrigation method depends on soil texture, topography, water availability, crop type, and initial capital investment.

  • Surface Irrigation: Border strip, check basin, and furrow methods. Highly efficient for medium to fine-textured soils with level topography, though prone to deep percolation and evaporation losses.
  • Sprinkler Irrigation: Simulates natural rainfall through pressurized nozzles. Ideal for undulating terrains, sandy soils, and high-value crops, saving 30-50% water compared to surface methods. Not suitable for heavy clay soils or strong windy conditions.
  • Drip (Trickle) Irrigation: Applies water directly to the root zone drop by drop through emitters. Offers the highest water use efficiency (85-95%) and enables fertigation.

Water Use Efficiency and Irrigation Scheduling

Optimizing water productivity requires precise calculation of crop water requirements and strategic scheduling based on physiological critical stages.

  • Water Use Efficiency (WUE): Defined as crop yield per unit of water consumed (kg/ha-cm). Improved through mulching, weed control, and micro-irrigation.
  • Critical Stages of Irrigation: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) in Triticum aestivum (20-25 DAS), flowering and pod development in Cicer arietinum, and panicle initiation and flowering in Oryza sativa.
  • IW/CPE Ratio: Irrigation scheduling based on the ratio of Irrigation Water (IW) to Cumulative Pan Evaporation (CPE) is widely adopted in research and field agronomy.

Agricultural Drainage Systems

Excess water logging leads to root asphyxiation, nutrient leaching, and soil salinization. Drainage removes surplus gravitational water from the root zone.

  • Surface Drainage: Open ditches and field drains used to remove surface ponding resulting from heavy downpours on flat lands.
  • Sub-surface Drainage: Tile drains or perforated pipe networks installed below the soil surface to lower high water tables and leach harmful salts from saline-sodic soils.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Field Capacity (-1/3 bar) with Permanent Wilting Point (-15 bars) potential values.
  • Assuming surface irrigation methods have higher water use efficiency than micro-irrigation systems.
  • Forgetting that CRI stage in wheat stands for Crown Root Initiation, occurring strictly at the adventitious root formation phase.

Concept 1

Soil Water Energy Concepts

Soil water potential measures the free energy status of soil water relative to pure free water. It dictates the direction and rate of water movement in soil-plant-atmosphere continua.

  • Matric Potential: Adsorption forces of soil solids on water molecules, always negative in unsaturated soils.
  • Osmotic Potential: Due to dissolved solutes in soil solution, reducing water availability.
Concept 2

Irrigation Scheduling Criteria

Determining when and how much to irrigate based on soil, plant, and meteorological indicators.

  • Soil Moisture Depletion (SMD): Irrigating when a fixed percentage (e.g., 50%) of available water is depleted.
  • IW/CPE Approach: Applying irrigation when cumulative pan evaporation reaches a predetermined threshold relative to irrigation water depth.
Concept 3

Duty and Delta of Irrigation Water

Fundamental irrigation engineering parameters linking water volume to crop land area.

  • Duty (D): Area of crop irrigated (in hectares) by a continuous supply of 1 cumec of water throughout its base period.
  • Delta (Δ): Total depth of water (in cm or meters) required by a crop from sowing to harvest.
Concept 4

Water Quality Indices

Chemical criteria determining the suitability of groundwater or surface water for sustained agricultural production.

  • EC: Measures total dissolved salts (dS m-1).
  • SAR: Calculated as Na^+/\sqrt{(Ca2+ + Mg2+)/2}.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Field Capacity (Maximum Retained Soil Moisture)
Soil moisture content remaining in soil after gravitational drainage has ceased (-1/3 bar).
Permanent Wilting Point (Lower Limit of Available Water)
Soil moisture content at which plants suffer irreversible wilting (-15 bars).
Available Water (Available Water Capacity (AWC))
Water retained between Field Capacity and Permanent Wilting Point.
Duty (Irrigation Duty)
Hectares of land irrigated per cumec of continuous water discharge.
Delta (Total Water Depth)
Total depth of irrigation water required by a crop during its entire growing season.
Consumptive Use (Evapotranspiration (ET))
Total water transpired by crop plants plus water evaporated from adjacent soil.
CRI Stage (Crown Root Initiation)
Most critical irrigation stage in wheat occurring 20-25 days after sowing.
Parshall Flume (Open Channel Flow Meter)
Venturi-type measuring flume used to measure irrigation water discharge.
SAR (Sodium Adsorption Ratio)
Index reflecting the sodicity hazard of irrigation water.
Fertigation (Fertilizer Application via Irrigation)
Applying dissolved fertilizers through micro-irrigation systems.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • 1 ha-cm = 100,000 liters of water.
  • Field capacity potential = -1/3 bar; Wilting point potential = -15 bars.
  • CRI in wheat is the gold standard exam question for critical irrigation stages.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Soil moisture constants govern plant water availability and irrigation scheduling intervals.
  • Micro-irrigation (drip and sprinkler) significantly enhances water use efficiency compared to surface methods.
  • Critical crop growth stages must never experience water stress to avoid severe yield penalties.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Duty VS Delta

Duty represents land area irrigated per unit water flow, whereas Delta represents total water depth applied over the crop period.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the soil water potential at Permanent Wilting Point (PWP) in standard agricultural soils?

Explanation: Permanent Wilting Point corresponds to a soil matric potential of -15 bars (-1500 kPa), at which point plants can no longer extract soil moisture and permanently wilt.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following irrigation methods achieves the highest water use efficiency?

Explanation: Drip irrigation achieves the highest water use efficiency (85-95%) by minimizing evaporation, runoff, and deep percolation losses.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the most critical irrigation stage in wheat (Triticum aestivum)?

Explanation: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) is the most critical stage for wheat irrigation, occurring at 20-25 DAS, vital for tillering and establishment.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

One hectare-centimeter (ha-cm) of water is equivalent to how many liters?

Explanation: 1 ha-cm = 10,000 m² * 0.01 m = 100 m³. Since 1 m³ = 1,000 liters, 100 m³ = 100,000 liters.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which hydraulic structure is commonly used for measuring open channel irrigation water discharge?

Explanation: Parshall flumes are widely used open-channel flow measurement devices designed to measure irrigation water streams accurately.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does SAR stand for in irrigation water quality analysis?

Explanation: SAR stands for Sodium Adsorption Ratio, an index used to assess the sodicity hazard of irrigation water.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Available Water Capacity (AWC) is mathematically defined as:

Explanation: Available Water Capacity is the water retained in soil between Field Capacity (upper limit) and Permanent Wilting Point (lower limit).

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the soil matric potential at Field Capacity in loamy soils?

Explanation: Field Capacity is conventionally defined at a soil water matric potential of -1/3 bar (-33 kPa).

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which term describes the total depth of water required by a crop during its entire growing season?

Explanation: Delta represents the total depth of water required by a crop from sowing to harvest.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary advantage of sub-surface drainage?

Explanation: Sub-surface drainage systems lower high water tables, preventing waterlogging and facilitating the leaching of harmful salts.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What instrument is used to measure soil moisture tension in situ?

Explanation: Tensiometers measure soil water suction (tension) up to 0.85 bars, useful for scheduling irrigation in moist soils.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the term for applying fertilizers through irrigation water systems?

Explanation: Fertigation is the practice of delivering dissolved fertilizers to crops via micro-irrigation systems like drip or sprinkler.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In irrigation scheduling, what does CPE stand for?

Explanation: CPE stands for Cumulative Pan Evaporation, used alongside irrigation water depth in the IW/CPE scheduling approach.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which soil texture exhibits the highest Available Water Capacity (AWC)?

Explanation: Loam soils possess optimal pore size distribution, yielding the highest available water capacity compared to sands and heavy clays.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the base period of a crop?

Explanation: Base period is defined as the total number of days between the first watering for crop land preparation/sowing and the last irrigation prior to harvest.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop is extremely sensitive to water stagnation during early vegetative growth?

Explanation: Soybean is highly susceptible to waterlogging, leading to oxygen depletion in the root zone and severe yield reduction, unlike rice which tolerates standing water.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does a Lysimeter measure?

Explanation: Lysimeters are precision devices used to measure actual evapotranspiration and deep percolation losses from soil-crop systems.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a surface irrigation method?

Explanation: Border strip is a classical surface irrigation method where fields are divided into long sloping strips.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary cause of secondary salinization in irrigated agricultural lands?

Explanation: Poor drainage coupled with high evaporation rates causes groundwater to rise, bringing dissolved salts to the surface where they accumulate upon evaporation.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate the delta (Δ) in centimeters for a crop if its duty is 1000 hectares per cumec and the base period is 100 days.

Solution & Rationale: Formula: Δ = 8.64 × B / D, where B is base period in days (100) and D is duty in ha/cumec (1000). Δ = (8.64 × 100) / 1000 = 86.4 cm.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

At what soil water suction value is Field Capacity conventionally measured in standard laboratory pressure plate apparatus?

Solution & Rationale: Field Capacity represents soil moisture retained at -1/3 bar (-33 kPa) matric potential after gravitational drainage.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following chemical formulas represents the Sodium Adsorption Ratio (SAR) calculation?

Solution & Rationale: SAR is calculated as the square root of the average of calcium and magnesium concentrations divided into the sodium concentration: Na^+/\sqrt{(Ca2++Mg2+)/2} with ion concentrations in meq/L.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the critical irrigation stage for chickpea (Cicer arietinum)?

Solution & Rationale: Chickpea requires critical irrigations at pre-flowering (branching) and pod development stages to ensure optimal grain filling.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

How many liters of water are contained in 2.5 hectare-centimeters (ha-cm)?

Solution & Rationale: 1 ha-cm = 100,000 liters. Therefore, 2.5 ha-cm = 2.5 × 100,000 = 250,000 liters.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

Which irrigation scheduling parameter utilizes the ratio of Irrigation Water depth to Cumulative Pan Evaporation?

Solution & Rationale: The IW/CPE ratio approach schedules irrigation when cumulative pan evaporation reaches a specific value proportional to the irrigation depth applied.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

What primary limitation prevents sprinkler irrigation from being effective in heavy clay soils?

Solution & Rationale: Heavy clay soils have low infiltration rates. High application rates from sprinklers exceed infiltration capacity, causing surface ponding and runoff.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which type of soil water is held by surface tension forces in capillary pores and is readily available to plant roots?

Solution & Rationale: Capillary water is held in soil micropores between -1/3 bar and -15 bars, forming the bulk of available water for plant uptake.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary function of a tensiometer in agronomic research?

Solution & Rationale: Tensiometers measure the tension with which water is held by soil particles, indicating soil wetness and moisture stress.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

In saline soils reclamation, which drainage system combined with leaching is most effective?

Solution & Rationale: Sub-surface drainage effectively lowers the water table and flushes out dissolved salts through the soil profile when coupled with leaching irrigation.
