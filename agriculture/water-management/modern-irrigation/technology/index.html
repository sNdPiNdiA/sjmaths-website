Introduction to Modern Irrigation Technology

Modern irrigation technology encompasses advanced engineering and agronomic methods designed to apply water to crops with high distribution uniformity, minimal conveyance losses, and optimal nutrient integration. Traditional surface irrigation methods often suffer from low water use efficiency (WUE), ranging from 35% to 50%, due to deep percolation, runoff, and evaporation. Modern pressure-based micro-irrigation systems elevate WUE up to 85%–95%.

  • Water Use Efficiency (WUE): Defined as the ratio of crop water use (evapotranspiration) to the total water applied or depleted.
  • Conveyance Efficiency: Significantly enhanced in pressurized piping networks compared to unlined earthen channels.
  • Application Efficiency: Minimized loss of water beneath the root zone by matching irrigation frequency precisely with crop root zone storage capacity.

Micro-Irrigation Systems: Drip and Sprinkler Technology

Micro-irrigation is divided into drip (trickle) irrigation and sprinkler irrigation. Drip irrigation applies water slowly directly to the soil root zone through emitters, reducing evaporation and weed growth between rows. Sprinkler irrigation simulates natural rainfall, distributing water through overhead nozzles under pressure.

  • Drip Irrigation Components: Head control unit (filters, pumps, fertilizer injectors), main line, sub-main lines, laterals, and emitters (online, inline, pressure-compensating).
  • Sprinkler Types: Rotating head, perforated pipe, micro-sprinklers, and center-pivot systems.
  • Operating Pressures: Drip systems typically operate at low pressure (1.0 to 2.5 kg/cm²), while standard sprinklers require medium to high pressure (2.0 to 4.5 kg/cm²).

Automation and Sensor-Based Irrigation Systems

Automation removes human error from irrigation scheduling by integrating electronic controllers, solenoid valves, sensors, and telemetry. Sensor-based systems monitor soil moisture tension, climatic parameters, or plant water status in real time to trigger irrigation cycles autonomously.

  • Soil Moisture Sensors: Tensiometers, electrical capacitance probes, and frequency domain reflectometry (FDR) sensors.
  • Climatic Sensors: Automatic weather stations measuring pan evaporation, temperature, relative humidity, and solar radiation to compute reference crop evapotranspiration (ET_0).
  • Controllers: Time-based, volume-based, and closed-loop feedback controllers utilizing IoT platforms and GSM/GPRS modules for remote smartphone operation.

Fertigation and Chemigation Dynamics

Fertigation is the practice of applying water-soluble fertilizers and agrochemicals through pressurized irrigation systems. This approach ensures precise placement of nutrients directly in the active root zone, significantly enhancing nutrient use efficiency (NUE) and reducing leaching losses.

  • Injectors: Venturi injectors, piston/diaphragm pumps, and fertilizer tanks (differential pressure tanks).
  • Sequence of Operation: Pre-irrigation with clean water to establish moisture, fertilizer injection phase, and post-irrigation flushing of lines to prevent salt accumulation and biological clogging.
  • Compatibility: Avoid injecting calcium-based fertilizers with sulfate or phosphate sources to prevent gypsum or calcium phosphate precipitation.

System Maintenance, Filtration, and Water Quality Management

Maintenance of modern irrigation networks requires rigorous management of suspended solids, chemical precipitates, and biological growths that cause emitter clogging. Filtration and chemical treatments are essential for system longevity.

  • Filters: Screen filters (for inorganic suspended solids), disc filters (stacked grooved discs), and media/sand filters (for organic and biological matter).
  • Biological Clogging: Controlled using chlorine injection (sodium hypochlorite) or hydrogen peroxide to eliminate algae and bacterial slime.
  • Chemical Clogging: Acid treatment (hydrochloric or nitric acid injection) to dissolve calcium and magnesium carbonate precipitates when pH exceeds 7.5.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Drip irrigation achieves the highest water use efficiency, reaching up to 95%.
  • Venturi injector operates on the Bernoulli principle utilizing pressure differential across a constriction.
  • Tensiometers measure soil matric potential and are best suited for coarse-textured soils operating in the range of 0 to 85 kPa.
  • Sand (media) filters are primarily used to remove algae, organic matter, and biological impurities from surface water sources.
  • Screen filters have mesh sizes typically measured in 'Mesh' (number of openings per linear inch); 120-mesh is standard for drip systems.
  • Critical stage irrigation in wheat coincides with Crown Root Initiation (CRI), occurring 20–25 days after sowing.
  • Chemigation involves applying agricultural chemicals such as fertilizers, herbicides, and nematicides through irrigation water.
  • Water-soluble fertilizers like urea, ammonium nitrate, mono-ammonium phosphate (MAP), and potassium nitrate are ideal for fertigation.
  • Acid injection (such as phosphoric or nitric acid) is used to clear calcium carbonate precipitation and maintain emitter discharge rates.
  • Evapotranspiration (ET_0) estimation using the Penman-Monteith equation is recommended by FAO-56 as the global standard.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing screen filters with media filters: Screen filters remove inorganic sand/silt, whereas media filters remove biological/organic matter.
  • Applying calcium nitrate simultaneously with sulfate-rich fertilizers, leading to insoluble calcium sulfate precipitation and clogged emitters.
  • Assuming sprinklers operate at lower pressure than drip systems, whereas sprinklers generally require higher operating pressures.
  • Neglecting line flushing after fertigation, which results in bacterial slime growth and fertilizer salt encrustation inside laterals.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Modern Irrigation Technology and Automation

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Micro-Irrigation Fundamentals

Micro-irrigation delivers water frequently and in small quantities directly to the root zone.

  • Distribution Uniformity (DU): The measure of how evenly water is applied across a field, expected to be above 90% in well-designed drip systems.
  • Emitters: Devices that dissipate pressure and discharge water at controlled rates ranging from 2 to 8 liters per hour.
Concept 2

Irrigation Automation & Sensors

Automation integrates electronic hardware to eliminate manual valve operation.

  • Tensiometer: Measures soil water suction (tension) directly in centibars (kPa).
  • FDR Sensors: Measure soil dielectric permittivity to calculate volumetric water content rapidly.
Concept 3

Fertigation Principles

Nutrient delivery through irrigation water maximizes uptake efficiency.

  • Venturi Principle: Pressure drop created by fluid acceleration through a throat draws fertilizer into the main flow.
  • NUE: Nutrient Use Efficiency is enhanced by split applications matching plant growth stages.
Concept 4

Filtration and Water Quality

Water quality determines filtration requirements and chemical treatment protocols.

  • Disc Filter: Combines the benefits of screen and depth filtration using grooved plastic rings.
  • Shock Chlorination: Periodic application of 10–20 ppm chlorine to eradicate biofilm and bacterial slime.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Water Use Efficiency (WUE)
Ratio of crop water consumed through evapotranspiration to total water supplied by irrigation and rainfall.
Venturi Injector (Venturi Tube)
A hydraulic device utilizing pressure differential based on Bernoulli's principle to draw liquid fertilizer into an irrigation pipeline.
Tensiometer (Soil Moisture Tension Meter)
A sealed water-filled tube with a porous ceramic cup used to measure soil matric suction up to 85 kPa.
Media Filter (Sand Filter)
A tank filled with graded sand and gravel used to filter organic matter, algae, and suspended solids from irrigation water.
Emitters (Tricklers / Drippers)
Mechanical devices attached to laterals that dissipate pressure and discharge water drop-by-drop.
Fertigation (Fertilizer Irrigation)
The application of water-soluble fertilizers through pressurized irrigation systems directly to the root zone.
Chemigation (Chemical Irrigation)
The application of pesticides, herbicides, fungicides, and fertilizers via irrigation systems.
Pressure Compensating Emitter (PC Emitter)
An emitter designed to deliver a constant discharge rate across varying operating water pressures.
Evapotranspiration (ET)
Combined water loss from soil surface evaporation and plant transpiration.
Screen Filter (Mesh Filter)
A cylindrical wire mesh or plastic screen used to filter inorganic suspended particles from pressurized water.
Distribution Uniformity (DU)
An index expressing the uniformity of water application across an entire irrigated field.
Solenoid Valve (Electromagnetic Valve)
An electromechanically operated valve used in automated irrigation systems to control water flow.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Drip systems operate at low pressure (1.0 - 2.5 kg/cm²).
  • Screen filter mesh size of 120-mesh is standard for drip irrigation systems.
  • CRI stage in wheat occurs 20-25 days after sowing and is the most critical for irrigation.
  • Calcium nitrate must not be mixed with sulfate fertilizers to prevent precipitation.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Micro-irrigation achieves up to 95% water use efficiency by minimizing deep percolation and evaporation.
  • Venturi injectors operate without external power using pressure drops across a constriction.
  • Tensiometers measure soil matric potential in kPa and work best in coarse-textured soils.
  • Sand/media filters are essential for removing organic impurities and algae from surface water sources.
  • Fertigation improves nutrient use efficiency and minimizes leaching losses by split application.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Screen Filter VS Media Filter

Screen filters trap inorganic solid particles on a wire mesh, whereas media filters trap organic matter and algae using graded sand beds.

Drip Irrigation VS Sprinkler Irrigation

Drip applies water directly to the soil root zone at low pressure, while sprinkler sprays water through the air like rainfall at higher pressure.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical water use efficiency (WUE) achieved under a well-managed drip irrigation system?

Explanation: Drip irrigation applies water directly to the plant root zone in small, frequent amounts, minimizing deep percolation, runoff, and evaporation losses, thus achieving 85% to 95% water use efficiency.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which hydraulic principle is utilized by a Venturi injector during fertigation?

Explanation: A Venturi injector operates on Bernoulli's principle. As water accelerates through a constricted throat, its velocity increases and static pressure drops, creating a vacuum that draws fertilizer solution into the main stream.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which sensor is specifically used to measure soil matric potential or tension in the root zone?

Explanation: A tensiometer measures soil matric suction (tension) directly in centibars (kPa) and is highly effective in coarse-textured soils for scheduling irrigation.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What type of filter is primarily recommended for removing algae and organic matter from surface water sources?

Explanation: Media filters, commonly filled with graded sand and gravel, provide depth filtration capable of trapping suspended organic impurities, algae, and biological slimes present in open reservoirs or canal water.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which operating pressure range is standard for drip irrigation systems?

Explanation: Drip irrigation systems are low-pressure systems typically operating between 1.0 to 2.5 kg/cm² to maintain steady flow through emitters without bursting lateral lines.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary operational sequence recommended during a fertigation event?

Explanation: The proper sequence is: first run clean water to wet the soil and stabilize operating pressure, then inject the fertilizer solution, and finally flush the lines with clean water to prevent salt encrustation and bacterial growth.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which chemical treatment is recommended to remove calcium carbonate precipitates and unclog dripper emitters?

Explanation: Acid injection (such as hydrochloric or nitric acid) lowers irrigation water pH to dissolve calcium and magnesium carbonate precipitates that cause chemical clogging in emitters.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the standard mesh size of a screen filter recommended for typical agricultural drip irrigation setups?

Explanation: A 120-mesh screen filter is the industry standard for drip irrigation systems to effectively filter out fine sand and inorganic suspended particles that could otherwise clog 1–2 mm emitter pathways.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which fertilizer combination should be avoided during fertigation to prevent chemical precipitation?

Explanation: Mixing calcium-bearing fertilizers (like calcium nitrate) with sulfate- or phosphate-bearing fertilizers leads to the formation of insoluble calcium sulfate or calcium phosphate precipitates, causing severe emitter clogging.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does the term 'Distribution Uniformity (DU)' evaluate in micro-irrigation systems?

Explanation: Distribution Uniformity (DU) measures how evenly water is discharged by emitters across an irrigated field, indicating system design quality and maintenance status.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which type of emitter automatically maintains a nearly constant discharge rate despite fluctuations in operating pressure?

Explanation: Pressure-compensating (PC) emitters use an elastomeric diaphragm that deforms under higher pressures to restrict flow area, ensuring uniform discharge across varying field elevations and pressures.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary purpose of shock chlorination in micro-irrigation systems?

Explanation: Shock chlorination involves injecting high concentrations of chlorine (10–20 ppm) to kill algae, iron bacteria, and slime-forming microorganisms that cause biological clogging in drip lines.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which parameter is measured by Frequency Domain Reflectometry (FDR) sensors in automated irrigation?

Explanation: FDR sensors measure the dielectric permittivity of the soil-water-air matrix to rapidly and accurately determine volumetric soil water content for automated irrigation controllers.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which irrigation system is most suitable for undulated topography and shallow soils where land leveling is difficult?

Explanation: Drip and micro-irrigation systems convey water through closed pipe networks, making them ideal for undulating terrain and shallow soils where surface irrigation methods would cause severe erosion and uneven distribution.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of a pressure regulator installed at the head control unit?

Explanation: Pressure regulators reduce and stabilize fluctuating upstream pressures to the specific operating pressure required by drip laterals or micro-sprinklers.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which fertilizer is completely water-soluble and highly suitable for supplying both nitrogen and phosphorus via fertigation?

Explanation: Mono-ammonium Phosphate (MAP) is completely water-soluble, supplying both ammoniacal nitrogen and phosphorus without leaving insoluble residues that clog emitters.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main limitation of sprinkler irrigation compared to drip irrigation?

Explanation: Sprinklers spray water into the air, making them highly susceptible to wind distortion and significant evaporative losses in hot, dry, windy climates compared to subsurface or surface drip irrigation.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What role do automatic weather stations play in smart irrigation management?

Explanation: Automatic weather stations record climatic data to compute reference evapotranspiration (ET_0), enabling smart irrigation controllers to adjust watering schedules dynamically based on atmospheric demand.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which component is essential at the main control head unit of a drip system to prevent backflow of chemical-laden water into the drinking water supply?

Explanation: A non-return valve (check valve) is a mandatory safety device installed at the head unit during chemigation and fertigation to prevent contaminated water from flowing back into the primary water source.

Test Question 1 of 10 PGT Standard

In a drip irrigation design, what is the maximum recommended pressure variation across the entire lateral line to ensure acceptable emission uniformity?

Solution & Rationale: To maintain high distribution uniformity, pressure variation within a drip sub-main and lateral assembly should not exceed 20% of the design operating pressure.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

Calculate the volume of liquid fertilizer required per irrigation if the fertigation dose is 50 kg N/ha, using urea (46% N), applied over an area of 2 hectares.

Solution & Rationale: Total N required = 50 kg/ha * 2 ha = 100 kg N. Urea contains 46% N. Total urea = (100 / 0.46) = 217.39 kg, approximately 217.4 kg.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

Which soil physical property dictates the upper limit of readily available soil moisture for plant uptake following drip irrigation?

Solution & Rationale: Field Capacity represents the upper limit of available water held in the soil root zone after gravitational drainage ceases (typically at -33 kPa matric potential).

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary advantage of subsurface drip irrigation (SDI) over surface drip irrigation?

Solution & Rationale: Subsurface drip irrigation places lateral lines below the soil surface, keeping the surface dry, which prevents weed seed germination and drastically reduces surface evaporation.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

Which formula is internationally recommended by FAO-56 for estimating reference crop evapotranspiration (ET0)?

Solution & Rationale: The FAO Penman-Monteith equation is the standard method for determining reference evapotranspiration, accounting for radiation, temperature, humidity, and wind speed.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary hazard associated with continuous over-irrigation under micro-irrigation in poorly drained heavy clay soils?

Solution & Rationale: In heavy clay soils with poor internal drainage, excessive drip application can cause localized waterlogging and accumulation of salts around the wetted perimeter.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

In automatic irrigation controllers, what does a closed-loop feedback system signify?

Solution & Rationale: Closed-loop automated systems use real-time data from soil moisture sensors or weather stations to make autonomous decisions regarding irrigation timing and duration.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Which device is used to measure instantaneous flow rate and cumulative water volume passing through the main irrigation pipeline?

Solution & Rationale: Propeller, electromagnetic, or ultrasonic water meters are installed in mainlines to measure flow rate and total water volume delivered to fields.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Why is an air release valve installed at high points in a pressurized irrigation mainline network?

Solution & Rationale: Air release valves vent trapped air during system startup and allow air to enter during shutdown, preventing vacuum collapse and damaging water hammer waves.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary agronomic benefit of pulse irrigation enabled by automated controllers in high-frequency drip systems?

Solution & Rationale: Pulse irrigation applies water in multiple short cycles per day, keeping root zone moisture near field capacity without exceeding infiltration rates or causing deep percolation.
