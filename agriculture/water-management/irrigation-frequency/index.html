Irrigation Frequency: Principles, Determinants, and Crop-Specific Management

Master the agronomic principles of irrigation frequency, soil-water-plant relationships, critical crop stages, and scheduling techniques essential for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture examinations.

1. Fundamentals of Irrigation Frequency and Soil-Water-Plant Continuum

Irrigation frequency refers to the time interval (in days) between successive irrigations under specified field conditions. Determining the correct frequency depends heavily on the soil's water holding capacity, atmospheric evaporative demand, crop rooting depth, and allowable depletion of available soil moisture.

  • Available Water Capacity (AWC): The amount of water retained in the soil between Field Capacity (FC, -0.33 bar) and Permanent Wilting Point (PWP, -15.0 bar).
  • Management Allowed Depletion (MAD): The fraction of available soil water that can be depleted from the root zone before the next irrigation is applied without causing water stress.
  • Root zone depth expands as the crop matures, increasing the total volume of available water and generally extending the irrigation interval later in the season.
  • Fine-textured clay soils hold more total water than coarse-textured sandy soils, yet sandy soils require more frequent irrigation due to lower water holding capacity and lower matric suction.

2. Methods of Determining Irrigation Frequency

Agronomists employ several empirical, physical, and physiological approaches to schedule irrigations accurately and prevent yield losses.

  • Soil Moisture Tension Approach: Utilizing tensiometers or electrical resistance blocks to trigger irrigation when soil matric potential reaches crop-specific thresholds (e.g., -0.5 bar for vegetables, -1.5 bar for field crops).
  • Climatic Approach (IW/CPE Ratio): Irrigation Water (IW) to Cumulative Pan Evaporation (CPE) ratio is widely used in Indian agriculture (ICAR standards). An IW/CPE ratio of 0.75 or 1.0 dictates when to apply a fixed depth of irrigation water (e.g., 50 mm).
  • Plant-Based Indicators: Canopy temperature measurement using infrared thermometry, leaf water potential, and visual deficiency symptoms (wilting, rolling of leaves in cereals).
  • Calendar Method: Fixed-interval scheduling based on historical weather data; simple to apply but inefficient during erratic rainfall events.

3. Critical Stages of Irrigation in Major Field Crops

Applying water at moisture-sensitive physiological phases is crucial to maximize harvest index and water use efficiency (WUE).

  • Wheat (Triticum aestivum): Crown Root Initiation (CRI) at 20-25 days after sowing (DAS) is the most critical stage. Other stages include Tillering, Jointing, Flowering, Milk, and Dough stages.
  • Rice (Oryza sativa): Panicle Initiation (PI), Flowering, and Milking stages are extremely sensitive to moisture stress. Continuous submergence of 5±2 cm is maintained from transplanting to 10 days before harvest.
  • Maize (Zea mays): Tasseling and Silking are the most critical stages; moisture stress during silking drastically reduces grain setting (barrenness).
  • Soybean (Glycine max): Flower initiation and Pod development (pod filling) stages require optimum moisture.

4. Factors Influencing Irrigation Frequency

Environmental, edaphic, and agronomic factors dynamically alter water consumption rates and irrigation intervals.

  • Atmospheric Demand: High temperature, low relative humidity, strong winds, and high sunshine hours accelerate evapotranspiration (ET), shortening irrigation intervals.
  • Crop Characteristics: C4 plants (e.g., maize, sorghum) and deep-rooted perennials have different water extraction patterns compared to shallow-rooted vegetable crops (e.g., onion, potato).
  • Soil Physical Properties: Infiltration rate, bulk density, and structural stability dictate how quickly water drains beyond the root zone or evaporates from the surface.
  • Agronomic Management: Mulching reduces soil evaporation, thereby extending irrigation frequency; tillage practices modify soil porosity and root proliferation.

5. Water Use Efficiency and Irrigation Scheduling Metrics

Optimizing irrigation frequency directly enhances Water Use Efficiency (WUE), minimizing deep percolation and runoff losses.

  • Field Water Use Efficiency (FWUE): Ratio of marketable crop yield (Y) to total water applied (W), expressed as kg/ha-mm.
  • Transpiration Ratio: Mass of water transpired by the plant per unit mass of dry matter produced.
  • Deficit Irrigation: Deliberately allowing crop water stress during less sensitive growth stages to save water without incurring severe economic yield penalties.
  • Precision scheduling ensures that root-zone soil moisture remains between field capacity and the management allowed depletion threshold.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Crown Root Initiation (CRI) stage in wheat occurs at 20-25 days after sowing and is the most sensitive stage to moisture stress.
  • The IW/CPE ratio method for scheduling irrigation was popularized in India by Dr. S.D. Rajput and ICAR scientists.
  • Field capacity of soil is measured at a soil matric potential of -0.33 bar (or -33 kPa).
  • Permanent wilting point (PWP) of soil is defined at a soil matric potential of -15.0 bar (or -1500 kPa).
  • In sugarcane, the formative phase (60-130 days after planting) is the most critical period requiring frequent irrigation.
  • Panicle initiation is the most critical physiological growth stage for moisture in lowland rice (Oryza sativa).
  • Management Allowed Depletion (MAD) for sensitive vegetable crops generally ranges from 30% to 40% of available water.
  • Tensiometers are best suited for scheduling irrigation in coarse-textured soils operating within the 0 to 0.85 bar tension range.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Field Capacity (-0.33 bar) with Permanent Wilting Point (-15.0 bar); always remember FC holds maximum available water while PWP represents zero available water for plant uptake.
  • Believing sandy soils require longer irrigation intervals; sandy soils have low water holding capacity and therefore require frequent, light irrigations.
  • Assuming CRI stage in wheat occurs at heading; CRI is the crown root initiation stage occurring at 20-25 DAS right after crown root development.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Irrigation Frequency: Principles, Determinants, and Crop-Specific Management

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Soil-Water Energy Status and Availability

Soil water retention is quantified using total soil water potential, comprising matric, osmotic, pressure, and gravitational potentials. Plants extract water easily from saturation down to permanent wilting point.

  • Field Capacity: Soil moisture content after free gravitational drainage ceases (-0.33 bar).
  • Available Water: The water held between -0.33 bar and -15.0 bar suction levels.
Concept 2

IW/CPE Ratio Scheduling Concept

The IW/CPE ratio approach integrates daily meteorological evaporative demand into irrigation scheduling. When cumulative pan evaporation reaches a calculated threshold relative to the fixed irrigation water depth, irrigation is applied.

  • IW/CPE 1.0: Irrigation water applied equals cumulative pan evaporation since the last irrigation.
  • Water Economy: Lowering the ratio to 0.6 or 0.7 imposes mild deficit irrigation suitable for drought-tolerant pulse crops.
Concept 3

Critical Stages of Crop Water Requirement

Water stress during vegetative growth can stunt plants, but moisture stress during reproductive phases (flowering, pollination, seed set) causes irreversible yield penalties and barrenness.

  • Sensitive Phases: Flowering and grain filling are universally critical across cereals, pulses, and oilseeds.
  • CRI in Wheat: 20-25 DAS marking nodal root emergence.
Concept 4

Measurement Instruments for Irrigation Scheduling

Precise scheduling relies on field instruments that measure soil moisture tension or volumetric water content directly.

  • Tensiometer: Measures soil matric suction up to 0.85 bar; ideal for coarse-textured soils and drip irrigation.
  • Neutron Probe: Measures volumetric water content based on fast neutron thermalization.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Available Water Capacity (Available Water Capacity (AWC))
The range of soil water between field capacity (-0.33 bar) and permanent wilting point (-15.0 bar) available for plant root uptake.
Field Capacity (Field Capacity (FC))
The amount of soil moisture retained in the soil after excess gravitational water has drained away, typically at -0.33 bar potential.
Permanent Wilting Point (Permanent Wilting Point (PWP))
The minimal soil moisture level at which plants can no longer extract water, resulting in permanent turgor loss (-15.0 bar).
Management Allowed Depletion (Management Allowed Depletion (MAD))
The fraction of available soil water permitted to be depleted from the root zone before initiating the next irrigation event.
IW/CPE Ratio (IW / CPE Ratio)
Ratio of irrigation water depth applied to cumulative pan evaporation, serving as a scientific basis for irrigation scheduling in India.
Crown Root Initiation (Crown Root Initiation (CRI))
The most critical irrigation stage in wheat occurring 20-25 days after sowing when permanent crown roots emerge.
Evapotranspiration (Evapotranspiration (ET))
Combined loss of water from the soil surface through evaporation and from plant tissues through transpiration.
Water Use Efficiency (Water Use Efficiency (WUE))
The ratio of crop economic yield produced per unit volume of water consumed or applied.
Tensiometer (Soil Moisture Tensiometer)
An instrument measuring soil matric suction or tension, used for scheduling irrigation in high-value vegetable crops.
Deficit Irrigation (Regulated Deficit Irrigation)
An irrigation practice where water is applied below full crop water requirement during less sensitive stages to conserve water.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Field capacity potential = -0.33 bar
  • Permanent wilting point potential = -15.0 bar
  • CRI wheat stage timing = 20-25 DAS
  • Tensiometer effective working range = 0 to 0.85 bar

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Irrigation frequency is governed by soil texture, atmospheric evaporative demand, and crop rooting depth.
  • Sandy soils require frequent, low-depth irrigations; clay soils require infrequent, high-depth irrigations.
  • CRI stage in wheat (20-25 DAS) and Tasseling/Silking in maize are non-negotiable critical irrigation stages.
  • IW/CPE ratio links irrigation timing directly with meteorological pan evaporation data.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Field Capacity VS Permanent Wilting Point

Field capacity is upper limit of available water (-0.33 bar), whereas permanent wilting point is lower limit (-15.0 bar).

IW/CPE Ratio VS SAR (Sodium Adsorption Ratio)

IW/CPE ratio guides irrigation frequency based on evaporation, while SAR measures irrigation water sodicity hazard.

Question 1 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the soil matric potential at Permanent Wilting Point (PWP) for agricultural soils?

Explanation: Permanent Wilting Point is universally standardized at -15.0 bar (-1500 kPa), beyond which plant roots cannot overcome soil matric suction to extract water.

Question 2 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is the most critical stage for irrigation in wheat (Triticum aestivum)?

Explanation: CRI stage is the most critical irrigation period in wheat. Missing irrigation here results in severe reduction in tillering and up to 40% yield loss.

Question 3 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

The IW/CPE ratio method for irrigation scheduling utilizes Cumulative Pan Evaporation measured from which standard US Weather Bureau class?

Explanation: The IW/CPE ratio method developed by ICAR utilizes evaporation data recorded from the standard Class A Pan Evaporimeter.

Question 4 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Why do sandy soils require more frequent irrigation than clay soils?

Explanation: Sandy soils have low available water capacity and high percolation rates, meaning they store less water and dry out faster, requiring frequent, light irrigations.

Question 5 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the effective operating tension range of a soil moisture tensiometer?

Explanation: Tensiometers work effectively in the range of 0 to 0.85 bar (0 to 85 kPa). Beyond 0.85 bar, air enters the ceramic cup and breaks the water column continuum.

Question 6 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the most critical stage for irrigation in maize (Zea mays) to prevent cob barrenness?

Explanation: Tasseling and Silking represent the peak critical period in maize. Moisture stress at this stage disrupts pollination and causes barren cobs.

Question 7 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What does MAD stand for in irrigation management terminology?

Explanation: MAD stands for Management Allowed Depletion, representing the threshold percentage of available soil water that can be depleted before scheduling the next irrigation.

Question 8 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

At what soil matric potential is Field Capacity (FC) typically measured in loamy soils?

Explanation: Field Capacity is universally defined at -0.33 bar (-33 kPa) for most loamy and clayey soils, where gravitational drainage has nearly ceased.

Question 9 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following crops is most sensitive to moisture stress during the panicle initiation stage?

Explanation: Rice (Oryza sativa) is extremely sensitive to water deficit during panicle initiation and flowering, which directly affects spikelet sterility and grain yield.

Question 10 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of Deficit Irrigation?

Explanation: Deficit irrigation deliberately exposes the crop to mild water stress during drought-tolerant growth stages to maximize water use efficiency (WUE) without severe yield penalties.

Question 11 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

In sugarcane, which growth phase requires the highest frequency and volume of irrigation water?

Explanation: The formative phase (tillering and rapid vegetative growth) is the most critical period for sugarcane, requiring frequent irrigation to ensure maximum stalk elongation.

Question 12 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

How is Available Water Capacity (AWC) calculated?

Explanation: AWC = Moisture content at Field Capacity (-0.33 bar) minus Moisture content at Permanent Wilting Point (-15.0 bar).

Question 13 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which instrument is used to measure volumetric water content through neutron thermalization?

Explanation: The Neutron Probe (Neutron Moisture Meter) measures soil water content by slowing down fast neutrons emitted from a radioactive source when colliding with hydrogen atoms in soil water.

Question 14 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary disadvantage of scheduling irrigation purely by the calendar method?

Explanation: The calendar method relies on fixed intervals, ignoring sudden rainfall or high evaporation days, which can lead to over-irrigation or under-irrigation.

Question 15 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which pulse crop is particularly sensitive to irrigation during the pre-flowering and pod-formation stages?

Explanation: Chickpea (Cicer arietinum) requires critical irrigations at pre-flowering and pod development stages; excess vegetative irrigation can cause excessive vegetative growth (lodging).

Question 16 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What does a higher IW/CPE ratio indicate regarding irrigation frequency?

Explanation: A higher IW/CPE ratio (e.g., 1.2 vs 0.6) means irrigation is triggered after a smaller amount of pan evaporation, resulting in more frequent irrigation.

Question 17 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following describes Field Water Use Efficiency (FWUE)?

Explanation: Field Water Use Efficiency is defined as the economic or marketable yield (Y) divided by the total depth of water applied (W) to the field.

Question 18 of 18 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical indicator of moisture stress in cereal crops like sorghum and maize during early vegetative growth?

Explanation: Leaf rolling and wilting are classic visual indicators of moisture stress in cereals, serving as a farmer-friendly guide for scheduling emergency irrigations.

Calculate the Management Allowed Depletion (MAD) depth in mm for a root zone depth of 100 cm, given that Available Water Capacity (AWC) is 1.5 mm/cm and the permissible MAD fraction is 50%.

Solution & Rationale: Total AWC in root zone = Root depth (100 cm) * AWC (1.5 mm/cm) = 150 mm. MAD depth = Total AWC * MAD fraction (0.50) = 150 * 0.50 = 75 mm.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which soil physical property dictates the maximum amount of water a soil can retain against gravity for crop utilization?

Solution & Rationale: Field Capacity represents the maximum water content a soil retains after gravitational drainage ceases, serving as the upper limit of available water for plants.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

In the IW/CPE scheduling approach, if the IW is fixed at 50 mm and the IW/CPE ratio is 0.80, at what Cumulative Pan Evaporation (CPE) should irrigation be applied?

Solution & Rationale: IW/CPE = 0.80 -> CPE = IW / 0.80 = 50 / 0.80 = 62.5 mm. Irrigation is scheduled when cumulative pan evaporation reaches 62.5 mm.

Test Question 4 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary physiological consequence of moisture stress during the silking stage in maize?

Solution & Rationale: Moisture stress during silking causes desiccation of silks before pollen shedding, resulting in incomplete fertilization and barren cobs.

Test Question 5 of 10 TGT Standard

Which instrument uses electrical resistance blocks made of gypsum to measure soil moisture tension indirectly?

Solution & Rationale: Bouyoucos blocks (gypsum blocks) measure electrical resistance, which varies inversely with soil moisture tension, suitable for drier soil ranges.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

How does increasing organic matter content in agricultural soils affect irrigation frequency?

Solution & Rationale: Organic matter improves soil aggregation, infiltration, and water holding capacity, thereby storing more water and extending the interval between irrigations.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the critical stage of irrigation for pearl millet (Pennisetum glaucum)?

Solution & Rationale: Flowering and grain filling (booting stage) are the most moisture-sensitive stages in pearl millet, where stress causes severe floret sterility.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Under deficit irrigation management, when is water restriction applied to minimize negative impacts on economic yield?

Solution & Rationale: Deficit irrigation restricts water supply specifically during less sensitive stages (e.g., early vegetative or late ripening) to optimize water productivity.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following factors shortens irrigation frequency during summer vegetable cultivation?

Solution & Rationale: High temperature, low humidity, and strong winds elevate atmospheric vapor pressure deficit (VPD), accelerating evapotranspiration and requiring frequent irrigation.

Test Question 10 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the typical rooting depth considered for scheduling irrigation in standard wheat crops during peak vegetative growth?

Solution & Rationale: Wheat roots typically explore a soil depth of 60 to 90 cm under favorable soil conditions, defining the effective root zone for irrigation water depth calculations.
