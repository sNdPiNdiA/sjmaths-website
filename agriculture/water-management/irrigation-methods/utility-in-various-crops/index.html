Principles of Crop Water Requirement and Consumptive Use

Crop water requirement (ET_crop) encompasses the total water needed for crop growth, metabolic processes, and evapotranspiration under field conditions. Understanding water dynamics is critical for optimizing irrigation efficiency and maximizing water use efficiency (WUE) in Indian agriculture.

  • Evapotranspiration (ET): The combined process of evaporation from soil surface and transpiration from plant tissues, measured in millimeters per day (mm/day).
  • Consumptive Use (CU): The quantity of water used by vegetative growth of a crop area in transpiration and building of plant tissues, plus evaporation from soil and intercepted water.
  • Water Use Efficiency (WUE): Defined as the ratio of crop yield (Y) to the amount of water depleted by the crop in evapotranspiration (ET), expressed in kg/ha-mm.
  • Effective Rainfall: That portion of total precipitation that is effectively utilized by the crop for its growth needs, excluding runoff and deep percolation losses.

Cereal Crops: Irrigation Dynamics and Critical Stages

Cereals form the foundation of Indian food security, with wheat, rice, and maize requiring precise irrigation schedules aligned with phenological development phases.

  • Wheat (Triticum aestivum): Requires 450–650 mm of water across 4 to 6 irrigations. The most critical stage is Crown Root Initiation (CRI), occurring 20–25 days after sowing (DAS). Other critical stages include tillering, jointing, flowering, milk stage, and dough stage.
  • Rice (Oryza sativa): Demands 900–1500 mm of water. Continuous submergence of 5 cm is practiced, except during tillering drainage and 10 days before harvesting. Critical stages are panicle initiation, booting, flowering, and milking.
  • Maize (Zea mays): Consumes 500–800 mm of water. Highly sensitive to moisture stress during tasseling and silking stages; water stress here causes severe yield reduction due to barren cobs.
  • Pearl Millet (Pennisetum glaucum): Drought-tolerant crop requiring 250–350 mm. Critical stages are tillering and flowering.

Pulses and Oilseeds: Moisture Management and Stress Tolerance

Pulses and oilseeds are predominantly grown under rainfed (barani) conditions or with limited irrigation. Excess moisture during early growth is as detrimental as drought during reproductive phases.

  • Chickpea (Cicer arietinum): Requires 200–300 mm of water. Two critical irrigations—one at pre-flowering (branching) and another at pod development—significantly boost yields. Excess water causes excessive vegetative growth and flower drop.
  • Pigeonpea (Cajanus cajan): Highly sensitive to waterlogging. Requires well-drained soils; critical stages are pre-flowering and pod filling.
  • Rapeseed and Mustard (Brassica juncea): Requires 200–250 mm. Critical irrigation stages are rosette growth (branching) and siliqua development.
  • Groundnut (Arachis hypogaea): Demands 500–700 mm. Pegging and pod formation stages are extremely sensitive to water stress; moisture deficit during pegging halts peg penetration into the soil.

Commercial and Cash Crops: Intensive Water Regimes

Commercial crops like sugarcane, cotton, and potato require high capital investment in irrigation infrastructure due to their long duration and high biomass production.

  • Sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum): Consumes 1500–2500 mm of water across 15–20 irrigations. Critical periods include germination, tillering, grand growth phase (highest water demand), and maturity. Water stress during the grand growth period severely reduces cane yield and sugar recovery.
  • Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum): Requires 700–1300 mm. Flowering and boll formation stages are critical. Irrigation must be managed carefully to prevent vegetative overgrowth and shedding of squares and bolls.
  • Potato (Solanum tuberosum): Requires 500–700 mm. Tubers are sensitive to both drought and waterlogging. Regular light irrigations maintaining soil moisture at 50% available soil moisture (ASM) prevent tuber malformation and common scab.

Advanced Irrigation Methods and Water Saving Technologies

To combat depleting groundwater tables and enhance water productivity, micro-irrigation systems and precision scheduling tools are vital.

  • Drip Irrigation (Trickle Irrigation): Applies water drop by drop directly to the root zone through emitters. Achieves 90–95% water use efficiency, prevents weed growth, and enables fertigation.
  • Sprinkler Irrigation: Simulates natural rainfall through overhead nozzles under pressure. Ideal for undulating topography and sandy soils where surface irrigation causes heavy percolation losses.
  • Check Basin and Border Strip Methods: Traditional surface irrigation methods optimized with proper sizing to reduce tail-water runoff and deep percolation.
  • Irrigation Scheduling Parameters: Utilizing IW/CPE ratio (Irrigation Water to Cumulative Pan Evaporation), typically set at 0.75 to 1.0 for optimal field crop scheduling.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Crown Root Initiation (CRI) in wheat occurs exactly 20-25 days after sowing and is the most critical stage where water stress reduces yield by up to 30%.
  • Sugarcane requires the highest quantity of water among common field crops, ranging between 1500 mm to 2500 mm.
  • The IW/CPE ratio approach uses Irrigation Water depth divided by Cumulative Pan Evaporation, commonly kept at 0.75 for general field crops.
  • Groundnut pegging stage is extremely sensitive to moisture stress; dry soil prevents pegs from entering the ground, leading to zero pod formation.
  • Drip irrigation achieves the highest water use efficiency of 90-95% by minimizing evaporation and deep percolation losses.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing the CRI stage timing in wheat: students often state 40-45 DAS, but true CRI occurs at 20-25 DAS when crown roots emerge.
  • Believing rice needs standing water throughout its life cycle; drainage is essential at maximum tillering and 10-14 days before harvest.
  • Assuming pulses do not need irrigation; while they are drought-tolerant, one or two strategic irrigations at branching and pod filling double the yield.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Utility of Irrigation Systems and Water Management in Various Major Crops

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Consumptive Use and Evapotranspiration Dynamics

Water dynamics in agricultural fields are governed by meteorological demand and plant physiological traits.

  • ET_0: Reference crop evapotranspiration calculated via Penman-Monteith equation.
  • CU Formula: Consumptive Use = Transpiration + Evaporation from plant and soil surfaces.
  • Management: Timely irrigation scheduling prevents terminal drought stress and nutrient leaching.
Concept 2

Critical Stages of Water Demand in Cereals

Cereals exhibit distinct phases of high water sensitivity during reproductive transitions.

  • Wheat CRI: 20-25 DAS, vital for root system establishment.
  • Rice Submergence: 5 cm standing water maintained from transplanting to hard dough stage.
  • Maize Silking: Synchrony of pollen shed and silk receptivity requires absolute optimum moisture.
Concept 3

Moisture Management in Pulses and Oilseeds

Legumes and oilseeds require balanced moisture regimes to avoid vegetative rank growth and root diseases.

  • Chickpea Branching: First irrigation at pre-flowering induces profuse branching.
  • Groundnut Pegging: Soil must be friable and moist for successful peg penetration.
  • Mustard Siliqua: Irrigation during siliqua filling increases seed weight and oil content.
Concept 4

Commercial Crops and Irrigation Efficiency

High-value cash crops demand precision irrigation infrastructure and scheduling.

  • Sugarcane Grand Growth: Period of maximum water uptake and dry matter accumulation.
  • Micro-irrigation: Drip and sprinkler systems drastically reduce conveyance and application losses.
  • Fertigation: Applying water-soluble fertilizers directly through irrigation systems maximizes nutrient use efficiency.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Consumptive Use (CU (mm))
The total depth of water used by plant vegetative growth and evaporation from adjacent soil.
Water Use Efficiency (WUE (kg/ha-mm))
The ratio of marketable crop yield to total water depleted through evapotranspiration.
Crown Root Initiation (CRI Stage)
The most critical irrigation stage in wheat occurring 20-25 days after sowing.
IW/CPE Ratio (Irrigation Water / Cumulative Pan Evaporation)
An index used for scientific irrigation scheduling based on meteorological evaporation.
Pegging Stage (Geocarpy in Groundnut)
The stage in groundnut where fertilized ovaries develop downward into pods; highly moisture-sensitive.
Grand Growth Period (Sugarcane Vegetative Phase)
The phase of rapid elongation in sugarcane requiring maximum water and nutrient inputs.
Effective Rainfall (Effective Precipitation)
That portion of total rainfall directly available to meet crop evapotranspiration demand.
Fertigation (Nutrient Application via Irrigation)
The injection of water-soluble fertilizers through pressurized irrigation systems like drip.
Available Soil Moisture (ASM (Field Capacity to Wilting Point))
The range of soil water held between field capacity (-0.33 bar) and permanent wilting point (-15 bars).
Pan Evaporimeter (Class A Evaporation Pan)
An instrument used to measure free water evaporation to compute cumulative pan evaporation.
Tasseling Stage (Male Inflorescence Emergence in Maize)
The critical reproductive phase in maize where pollen is shed, highly sensitive to moisture stress.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Groundnut pegging stage moisture stress leads to complete crop failure.
  • IW/CPE ratio of 0.75 is standard for irrigating cereal crops.
  • Sugarcane grand growth phase demands peak irrigation intervals.
  • Drip irrigation achieves 90-95% irrigation efficiency.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Wheat requires 450-650 mm of water with CRI at 20-25 DAS as the absolute critical stage.
  • Rice demands 900-1500 mm with continuous submergence except during tillering and pre-harvest drainage.
  • Maize is extremely sensitive to water stress during tasseling and silking stages.

  • Drip and sprinkler irrigation offer the highest water use efficiency and are vital for modern resource conservation.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Consumptive Use VS Evapotranspiration

Consumptive use includes water used in plant metabolic tissue building along with ET, though often used interchangeably in agronomy.

Field Capacity VS Permanent Wilting Point

Field capacity is upper limit of available water (-0.33 bar), while permanent wilting point is lower limit (-15 bars) where plants permanently wilt.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is the most critical irrigation stage in wheat (Triticum aestivum)?

Explanation: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) occurring at 20-25 DAS is the most critical stage in wheat. Water stress at this phase causes permanent reduction in tiller formation and up to 30% yield loss.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the total water requirement of sugarcane under Indian agro-climatic conditions?

Explanation: Sugarcane requires 1500 to 2500 mm of water across 15-20 irrigations, making it one of the highest water-consuming field crops.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which physiological stage in maize (Zea mays) is extremely sensitive to moisture stress?

Explanation: Tasseling and silking stages are extremely critical in maize. Moisture stress during this period causes desiccation of pollen and non-receptivity of silks, resulting in barren cobs.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the typical water use efficiency range achieved under drip irrigation systems?

Explanation: Drip irrigation applies water directly to the plant root zone, achieving very high water use efficiencies of 90 to 95%.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which stage in groundnut (Arachis hypogaea) is most critical for irrigation?

Explanation: Pegging and pod formation is the critical irrigation stage in groundnut. Dry soil during pegging prevents pegs from penetrating the soil, resulting in zero pod setting.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does the IW/CPE ratio stand for in scientific irrigation scheduling?

Explanation: IW/CPE stands for Irrigation Water to Cumulative Pan Evaporation, a standard meteorological index used to schedule irrigations efficiently.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which crop requires continuous submergence of about 5 cm of water during most of its vegetative phase?

Explanation: Rice requires continuous submergence of 5 cm from transplanting up to the hard dough stage, except during tillering drainage and pre-harvest drying.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the average water requirement of chickpea (Cicer arietinum)?

Explanation: Chickpea requires 200 to 300 mm of water, typically supplied through 1 or 2 strategic irrigations at branching and pod development.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is NOT a critical irrigation stage for wheat?

Explanation: Tasseling is specific to maize (Zea mays). Wheat critical stages include CRI, tillering, jointing, flowering, milk, and dough stages.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary disadvantage of surface flood irrigation in heavy clay soils?

Explanation: Flood irrigation in heavy soils often causes waterlogging, depleting soil oxygen and restricting root respiration, coupled with deep percolation losses.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which index is defined as crop yield divided by water depleted through evapotranspiration?

Explanation: Water Use Efficiency (WUE) measures the mass of crop yield produced per unit volume of water consumed via evapotranspiration.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Why is drainage required in rice fields prior to harvesting?

Explanation: Draining the rice field 10-14 days before harvest promotes uniform grain ripening and hardens the soil for smooth mechanical or manual harvesting.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following crops is most sensitive to waterlogging throughout its growth period?

Explanation: Pigeonpea is extremely susceptible to waterlogging. Even 24 to 48 hours of stagnant water causes root rot and sudden death of the crop.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main advantage of sprinkler irrigation over surface irrigation?

Explanation: Sprinkler irrigation is ideal for uneven topography as it does not require land leveling and ensures uniform water application.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

At what soil moisture tension is Permanent Wilting Point (PWP) typically defined?

Explanation: Permanent Wilting Point occurs at a soil moisture tension of -1.5 MPa (-15 bars), where plants can no longer extract water and wilt permanently.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which period in sugarcane represents the peak water requirement phase?

Explanation: The grand growth period of sugarcane (120-250 days after planting) is characterized by rapid internode elongation and maximum water consumption.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the recommended IW/CPE ratio for scheduling irrigation in wheat under normal conditions?

Explanation: An IW/CPE ratio of 0.75 is widely recommended for scheduling irrigations in wheat, ensuring adequate moisture without waterlogging.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which oilseed crop requires critical irrigation during rosette (branching) and siliqua development?

Explanation: Rapeseed and mustard require critical irrigations at branching (rosette stage) and siliqua (pod) development for optimum seed setting and oil content.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is effective rainfall in agricultural water management?

Explanation: Effective rainfall is that portion of total precipitation that is actually utilized by the crop to meet its evapotranspirative demand, excluding runoff and deep percolation.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which term describes the application of fertilizers along with irrigation water through micro-irrigation?

Explanation: Fertigation is the practice of dissolving and injecting fertilizers directly into the irrigation water stream, commonly used in drip systems.

Test Question 1 of 10 PAGES Standard

Calculate the total depth of irrigation water required if 5 irrigations of 7 cm each are applied to a wheat crop. Express in millimeters.

Solution & Rationale: Total water = 5 irrigations × 7 cm = 35 cm. Since 1 cm = 10 mm, 35 cm = 350 mm.

Test Question 2 of 10 TGT Standard

Which agronomic practice is most effective in reducing evaporation losses from the soil surface in wide-row crops like maize and sugarcane?

Solution & Rationale: Mulching creates a physical barrier that intercepts solar radiation and drastically curtails direct soil evaporation.

Test Question 3 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the primary physiological consequence of moisture stress during the flowering stage in pulses?

Solution & Rationale: Water stress during flowering in pulses disrupts pollination and triggers abscission hormone synthesis, leading to heavy flower drop.

Test Question 4 of 10 PAGES Standard

In a field trial, a crop consumes 500 mm of water through evapotranspiration and yields 2500 kg/ha of grain. What is its Water Use Efficiency (WUE)?

Solution & Rationale: WUE = Yield / ET = 2500 kg/ha / 500 mm = 5.0 kg/ha-mm.

Test Question 5 of 10 PAGES Standard

Which of the following irrigation scheduling methods is based on soil matric potential sensors?

Solution & Rationale: Tensiometers measure soil matric suction (tension) directly in centibars, guiding irrigation scheduling based on soil moisture status.

Test Question 6 of 10 TGT Standard

Why is excessive irrigation harmful during the ripening stage of potato tubers?

Solution & Rationale: Excessive moisture late in potato development causes tuber cracking, susceptibility to fungal rots, and secondary sprouting (knobbing).

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Which micro-irrigation system is characterized by operating pressures between 1.0 to 2.5 kg/cm² and rotating sprinkler heads?

Solution & Rationale: Sprinkler systems utilize moderate pressure pumps (1.0-2.5 kg/cm²) to distribute water via rotating nozzles resembling natural rainfall.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the critical moisture sensitive stage for cotton in relation to boll development?

Solution & Rationale: Flowering and boll formation are critical stages in cotton; moisture stress here leads to shedding of squares and young bolls.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

If the Cumulative Pan Evaporation (CPE) is 50 mm and the IW/CPE ratio is set at 0.75, at what depth of evaporation should irrigation (IW) be applied?

Solution & Rationale: IW = IW/CPE × CPE = 0.75 × 50 mm = 37.5 mm depth of irrigation.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

Which agronomic adaptation is critical when cultivating crops on sloping lands to prevent soil erosion and improve water infiltration?

Solution & Rationale: Contour farming and bunding trap runoff water, increasing infiltration time and preventing topsoil erosion on slopes.
