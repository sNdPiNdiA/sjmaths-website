Introduction and Principles of Irrigation Water Management

Water management in Indian agriculture is critical given the vagaries of the monsoon and the finite availability of surface and groundwater resources. Irrigation is the artificial application of water to the soil to supplement natural rainfall and capillary rise, ensuring optimum crop growth, root development, and physiological functioning across various agro-climatic zones.

  • Water Use Efficiency (WUE): The ratio of water used by the crop (evapotranspiration) to the amount of water applied through irrigation. Modern pressurized systems maximize WUE compared to traditional gravity methods.
  • Hydraulic Head and Distribution Uniformity: Key engineering parameters determining the uniformity coefficient (UC) and distribution uniformity (DU) in field application networks.
  • Agronomic Context: Proper irrigation scheduling prevents moisture stress during critical crop growth stages such as crown root initiation (CRI) in Triticum aestivum and flowering in Glycine max.

Surface Irrigation Methods: Classification and Hydraulics

Surface irrigation involves applying water directly to the soil surface from an open ditch or pipeline system, utilizing gravity flow. It remains the most widely practiced method in the Indo-Gangetic plains due to low initial capital investment, though it exhibits lower water-use efficiency (40-50%).

  • Border Strip Irrigation: The field is divided into long, narrow strips bordered by low ridges. Suitable for deep, moderately permeable soils and close-growing field crops like Oryza sativa and Triticum aestivum.
  • Basin Irrigation: The field is enclosed by dikes or levees to form flat basins, allowing water to pond rapidly. Ideal for paddy cultivation, orchards, and flat terrain.
  • Furrow Irrigation: Water flows in small parallel channels (furrows) between crop rows. Highly efficient for wide-row crops such as Zea mays, Sorghum bicolor, and Gossypium hirsutum.

Pressurized Irrigation: Sprinkler Irrigation Systems

Sprinkler irrigation simulates natural rainfall by spraying water under pressure into the air through nozzles, breaking it into small droplets that fall uniformly across the crop canopy. Water application efficiency ranges from 70% to 85%.

  • System Components: Pumping unit, mainlines, sub-mains, lateral lines, risers, and rotating sprinkler heads or impact sprinklers.
  • Agro-Climatic Suitability: Highly recommended for undulating topography, shallow soils, sandy soils with high infiltration rates, and water-scarce regions.
  • Crops Benefited: Oilseeds (Brassica juncea), pulses (Cicer arietinum), fodder crops, and vegetables. Not ideal for high wind velocities or heavy clay soils with low infiltration rates.

Micro-Irrigation: Drip and Sub-Surface Systems

Micro-irrigation (trickle or drip irrigation) applies water slowly and directly to the root zone of plants through emitters or drippers at frequent intervals. It achieves the highest water application efficiency (90-95%) and nutrient-use efficiency through fertigation.

  • Key Components: Head control unit (filters, fertilizer tanks, pressure regulators), mainlines, manifolds, laterals, and online/inline emitters.
  • Favorable Conditions: Water-scarce zones, high-value horticultural crops (Solanum lycopersicum, Capsicum annuum, Mangifera indica), and saline water usage.
  • Advantages: Minimized weed growth, reduced evaporation losses, elimination of runoff, and precise fertilizer management.

Selection Criteria, Economics, and Government Schemes

Choosing the appropriate irrigation method requires a multi-criteria evaluation of soil texture, topography, water quality, crop root depth, energy availability, and capital investment capacity.

  • Soil Considerations: Sandy soils demand frequent, low-volume applications (drip/sprinkler), whereas clayey soils can store larger quantities of water suitable for basin or border methods.
  • Economic Appraisal: Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR) and Internal Rate of Return (IRR) are evaluated for pressurized systems against conventional surface methods.
  • Schemes such as Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY) promote 'More Crop Per Drop' through micro-irrigation subsidies across Indian states.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Drip irrigation achieves the highest water use efficiency ranging from 90% to 95%.
  • Border strip method is best suited for close-growing field crops like wheat and barley on medium to heavy textured soils.
  • Furrow irrigation is the standard method for wide-row crops such as maize, sugarcane, and cotton.
  • Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY) targets 'Har Khet ko Paani' and 'More Crop Per Drop'.
  • Sprinkler irrigation is unsuitable in high wind velocity areas as it disrupts distribution uniformity.
  • Fertigation is primarily practiced through micro-irrigation systems to maximize nutrient uptake.
  • Consumptive use of water refers to the quantity of water transpired by plants plus evaporated from adjacent soil.
  • Distribution Uniformity (DU) evaluates how evenly water is applied across an irrigated field.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Assuming sprinkler irrigation is efficient on heavy clay soils with very low infiltration rates.
  • Confusing surface irrigation water use efficiency (40-50%) with drip irrigation efficiency (90-95%).
  • Believing that furrow irrigation is designed for flooded paddy fields instead of row crops.

Concept 1

Classification of Irrigation Systems

Irrigation methods are broadly categorized based on how water is delivered to the field.

  • Surface Irrigation: Gravity flow over soil surface (Border, Basin, Furrow).
  • Pressurized Irrigation: Application under pressure (Sprinkler and Drip).
Concept 2

Water Use Efficiency (WUE)

WUE measures the effectiveness of water application and crop conversion.

  • Field WUE: Ratio of crop water requirement to total water applied. Drip has maximum efficiency.
Concept 3

Hydraulics of Surface Irrigation

Governed by open channel flow dynamics and infiltration characteristics.

  • Advance Phase: Time taken for water front to reach the lower end of the border or furrow.
Concept 4

Micro-Irrigation Components

Precision engineering units designed for root-zone application.

  • Emitters: Devices that dissipate pressure and discharge water at a constant low rate (e.g., 2-4 LPH).

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Border Strip (Border Irrigation)
A surface irrigation method where the field is divided into strips separated by low ridges.
Basin Irrigation (Level Basin Method)
Method where flat plots are enclosed by dikes for rapid ponding of water.
Furrow Irrigation (Rill Irrigation)
Water application through small parallel channels for row crops.
Sprinkler Irrigation (Overhead Irrigation)
Applying water in the form of artificial rainfall under pressure.
Drip Irrigation (Trickle Irrigation)
Slow, precise application of water drop-by-drop to the root zone.
Fertigation (Nutrient Injection)
Application of water-soluble fertilizers through micro-irrigation systems.
Distribution Uniformity (DU Parameter)
A measure of how evenly water is distributed across an irrigated area.
Consumptive Use (Evapotranspiration (ET))
Total water used by plant metabolism and evaporated from soil.
Infiltration Rate (Infiltration Capacity)
Downward entry rate of water into the soil matrix.
PMKSY (Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana)
National flagship scheme for expanding irrigation coverage and water efficiency.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Drip WUE = 90-95%.
  • Surface irrigation efficiency = 40-50%.
  • Border strip is best for close-growing crops.
  • Furrow is best for wide-row crops like maize and sugarcane.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Surface methods have low initial cost but low WUE (40-50%).
  • Sprinkler systems are ideal for undulating topography and sandy soils.
  • Drip irrigation achieves maximum efficiency (90-95%) through point-source application.
  • Fertigation enhances nutrient uptake while saving fertilizer quantities.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Border Irrigation VS Basin Irrigation

Border strips are long, sloping channels open at the lower end, whereas basins are flat, level plots completely enclosed by dikes.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which irrigation method exhibits the highest water use efficiency?

Explanation: Drip irrigation applies water directly to the plant root zone with minimal evaporation and runoff losses, achieving 90-95% efficiency.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Border strip irrigation is best suited for which of the following crop types?

Explanation: Border strips are ideal for close-growing field crops such as wheat, barley, and alfalfa on medium to heavy soils.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the approximate water use efficiency of traditional surface irrigation methods?

Explanation: Traditional surface methods generally operate at 40-50% efficiency due to conveyance, runoff, and deep percolation losses.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which irrigation method is most suitable for undulating topography and sandy soils?

Explanation: Sprinkler irrigation is well-suited for undulating land and sandy soils where leveling is impractical and infiltration is high.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The simultaneous application of water and fertilizers through a pressurized irrigation system is known as:

Explanation: Fertigation delivers water-soluble fertilizers directly to the root zone via drip or sprinkler systems, enhancing nutrient-use efficiency.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Furrow irrigation is primarily recommended for which category of crops?

Explanation: Furrow irrigation is designed for wide-row crops such as maize, sugarcane, cotton, and potato.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of the Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY)?

Explanation: PMKSY aims to expand cultivable area under assured irrigation ('Har Khet ko Paani') and improve water use efficiency ('More Crop Per Drop').

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which factor severely limits the performance of a sprinkler irrigation system?

Explanation: High wind velocity distorts the spray pattern and reduces distribution uniformity in sprinkler irrigation.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Basin irrigation is widely adopted for which of the following cropping systems?

Explanation: Basin irrigation involves enclosing flat land with dikes to pond water, making it standard for lowland paddy cultivation and orchards.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

In micro-irrigation, emitters are designed to deliver water at:

Explanation: Emitters dissipate pressure and discharge water at low rates (typically 2 to 4 liters per hour) directly to the root zone.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does Distribution Uniformity (DU) measure in an irrigation system?

Explanation: DU is an index indicating how evenly water is applied across the irrigated area, ensuring crops receive uniform moisture.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following soils is least suitable for surface irrigation methods without extensive land leveling?

Explanation: Steeply sloping sandy soils cause severe erosion and rapid percolation under surface gravity irrigation, requiring pressurized systems.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Consumptive use of water by a crop refers to:

Explanation: Consumptive use is synonymous with evapotranspiration (ET), combining plant transpiration and evaporation from adjacent soil surfaces.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which component is essential in a drip irrigation head control unit to prevent emitter clogging?

Explanation: Filters (screen, disc, or media filters) are critical in drip systems to remove suspended impurities and prevent clogging of micro-emitters.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The ratio of crop water requirement to the total water diverted from the source is termed as:

Explanation: Project water use efficiency encompasses conveyance, distribution, and field application efficiencies across the entire irrigation project.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which irrigation system eliminates conveyance losses entirely?

Explanation: Pressurized systems transport water through closed pipes under pressure, eliminating evaporation and seepage conveyance losses.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary agronomic advantage of alternate furrow irrigation?

Explanation: Alternate furrow irrigation applies water to alternate furrows in successive irrigations, saving up to 30-50% water with minimal yield reduction.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which soil textural class has the highest water holding capacity but lowest drainage rate?

Explanation: Clay soils possess maximum field capacity and wilting point due to high surface area and micropores, retaining water tightly.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

The depth of water required to bring the root zone soil moisture up to field capacity is calculated based on:

Explanation: Net irrigation requirement is determined by soil bulk density, field capacity, permanent wilting point, and effective root zone depth.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Under micro-irrigation, what device is commonly used to inject fertilizers into the pressurized water stream?

Explanation: Venturi injectors and fertilizer tanks utilize pressure differentials to accurately draw liquid fertilizers into the main irrigation line.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Which Indian national initiative integrates micro-irrigation subsidies under the motto 'More Crop Per Drop'?

In furrow irrigation, what is the agronomic benefit of alternate furrow irrigation?

Solution & Rationale: Alternate furrow irrigation reduces applied water volume by irrigating alternate channels.

Test Question 7 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following parameters directly dictates the net irrigation requirement (NIR) of a crop?

Solution & Rationale: NIR is calculated based on soil moisture deficit between field capacity and current moisture across the effective root zone.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

Why is surface irrigation generally unsuited for undulating topography without heavy land leveling?

Solution & Rationale: Gravity flow requires uniform slopes; undulating land leads to pooling in low spots and dry elevated patches.

Test Question 9 of 10 TGT Standard

What term describes the total water lost through plant transpiration and soil evaporation combined?

Solution & Rationale: Consumptive use is equivalent to crop evapotranspiration (ET).
