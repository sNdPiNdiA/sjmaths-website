Natural and Organic Farming Systems: Key Features and Agronomic Principles

Introduction and Core Philosophy of Natural Farming

Natural farming is an ecological farming approach that relies heavily on natural biodiversity and micro-climate management without the application of synthetic fertilizers, chemical pesticides, or intensive soil tillage. Pioneered globally by Masanobu Fukuoka and advanced in India by Subhash Palekar (Zero Budget Natural Farming / Zero Budget Natural Farming - ZBNF, currently promoted as Bharatiya Prakritik Krishi Paddhati - BPKPP), natural farming aims to restore soil health and revive native soil microbiota.

  • Elimination of all synthetic external inputs such as chemical nitrogenous and phosphatic fertilizers, systemic pesticides, and synthetic growth regulators.
  • Complete reliance on indigenous on-farm inputs such as cow dung, cow urine, jaggery, pulses flour, and local soil.
  • Minimum or zero tillage to preserve soil aggregate stability, earthworm burrows, and soil moisture.
  • Permanent soil cover using crop residues, green manures, or live mulches to suppress weed germination and prevent thermal fluctuations.

Pillars of Natural Farming (Palekar Model / BPKPP)

The framework of natural farming in India is built upon distinct pillars designed to activate native soil microbiology and create a self-sustaining nutrient cycle within the agro-ecosystem.

  • Jeevamrutha: A microbial culture prepared using 200 liters of water, 10 kg local cow dung, 10 liters cow urine, 2 kg jaggery, 2 kg pulse flour, and a handful of virgin forest soil. Fermented for 48 hours, it supplies billions of beneficial microorganisms (bacteria, fungi, actinomycetes) that solubilize native soil nutrients.
  • Beejamrutha: A seed and seedling treatment concoction made from cow dung, cow urine, lime, and forest soil, protecting germinating seeds from seed-borne and soil-borne fungal pathogens.
  • Acchadana (Mulching): Soil mulching categorized into straw mulch (crop residues), live mulch (intercropping legumes and cover crops), and soil mulch (shallow inter-cultivation to break capillary rise of water).
  • Whapasa (Moisture-Air Balance): Ensuring the ideal balance of water vapor and air in the soil root zone, significantly reducing irrigation water requirements by up to 50-80%.

Principles and Certification Standards of Organic Farming

Organic farming is a holistic production management system that promotes and enhances agro-ecosystem health, including biodiversity, biological cycles, and soil biological activity. It is governed by rigorous national and international standards such as the National Programme for Organic Production (NPOP) in India.

  • Principle of Health: Sustaining and enhancing the health of soil, plant, animal, human, and planet as one and indivisible.
  • Principle of Ecology: Working through living ecological systems and cycles, emulating them, and helping to sustain them.
  • Principle of Fairness: Building relationships that ensure fairness with regard to the common environment and life opportunities.
  • Principle of Care: Management in a precautionary and responsible manner to protect the health and well-being of current and future generations and the environment.

Soil Fertility Management and Biological Nitrogen Fixation

Nutrient supply in natural and organic farming relies on biological activity, organic matter mineralization, and symbiotic or free-living microbial fixation.

  • Symbiotic Fixation: Utilization of Rhizobium species in leguminous crops, Frankia in non-leguminous trees, and Anabaena azollae in water fern Azolla.
  • Asymbiotic/Free-living Fixation: Activity of Azotobacter chroococcum, Beijerinckia, and blue-green algae (Cyanobacteria such as Nostoc and Anabaena).
  • Phosphate Solubilization: Deployment of phosphate-solubilizing bacteria (PSB) such as Bacillus megaterium var. phosphaticum and fungi such as Penicillium species to convert insoluble tricalcium phosphates into plant-available orthophosphates.
  • Vesicular-Arbuscular Mycorrhiza (VAM): Fungal hyphae of genera like Glomus enhancing the uptake of immobile nutrients such as phosphorus, zinc, and copper.

Plant Protection and Weed Management in Natural Systems

Plant protection in chemical-free farming systems shifts from curative chemical eradication to preventative ecological management and biological control.

  • Botanical Extracts: Utilization of fermented neem (Azadirachta indica) leaf extracts, dashparni ark, and agniastra for insect pest deterrence and management.
  • Biological Control Agents: Release of predators and parasitoids such as Trichogramma species, Chrysoperla carnea, and entomopathogenic nematodes against lepidopteran pests like Helicoverpa armigera.
  • Microbial Biopesticides: Application of Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt), Beauveria bassiana, Metarhizium anisopliae, and Nucleopolyhedrovirus (NPV).
  • Weed Management: Hand weeding, mechanical hoeing, pseudo-seedbed preparation, cover cropping, and allelopathic crop rotations to suppress weed emergence.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Masanobu Fukuoka is internationally recognized as the pioneer of natural farming and author of 'One-Straw Revolution'.
  • Subhash Palekar popularized Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF) in India, later renamed as Bharatiya Prakritik Krishi Paddhati (BPKPP).
  • Jeevamrutha is fermented for 48 hours and contains trillions of beneficial soil microbes per milliliter.
  • Beejamrutha protects seeds against seed-borne and soil-borne fungal pathogens using cow dung, cow urine, and lime.
  • Whapasa refers to the ideal condition where both water molecules and air molecules are present in adequate proportions in soil pore spaces.
  • Acchadana (mulching) prevents soil moisture evaporation, suppresses weed seed germination, and moderates soil temperature.
  • NPOP stands for National Programme for Organic Production, launched in India under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry.
  • PGS stands for Participatory Guarantee Systems, a peer-review organic certification mechanism suited for domestic markets.
  • Vesicular-Arbuscular Mycorrhiza (VAM) fungi like Glomus enhance the uptake of immobile phosphorus in organic soils.
  • Azotobacter chroococcum is a free-living nitrogen-fixing bacterium commonly inoculated in non-leguminous crops like wheat and rice.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing natural farming with organic farming: Organic farming allows the purchase and application of external certified organic inputs (like rock phosphate and vermicompost), whereas strict natural farming permits zero external inputs.
  • Assuming zero tillage means no soil disturbance ever: Zero tillage means planting crops directly into unplowed soil with minimal surface disruption, unlike no-till which might still involve shallow slit opening for seed placement.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Natural and Organic Farming Systems: Key Features and Agronomic Principles

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Philosophy of Natural Farming

Natural farming rejects human interference in natural ecological loops, treating the farm as a self-regulating forest ecosystem.

  • Fukuoka Method: Do-nothing farming philosophy emphasizing non-cultivation, no chemical fertilizers, no weeding, and no pruning.
Concept 2

Microbial Activation via Jeevamrutha

Jeevamrutha acts as a catalytic inoculant rather than a nutrient source, awakening dormant native soil microorganisms.

  • Composition: 200 L water, 10 kg cow dung, 10 L cow urine, 2 kg jaggery, 2 kg pulse flour, and forest soil.
Concept 3

Soil Moisture and Air Dynamics (Whapasa)

Whapasa ensures roots obtain adequate oxygen along with capillary moisture, reducing irrigation frequency.

  • Root Respiration: Prevents root hypoxia and encourages deep root penetration.
Concept 4

Organic Certification Frameworks

Quality assurance in organic agriculture requires legal standards and traceability from farm gate to consumer plate.

  • NPOP: Apex Indian standard for organic export and domestic trade overseen by APEDA.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Jeevamrutha (Microbial Inoculant Solution)
A fermented liquid formulation of cow dung, urine, jaggery, and pulse flour used to stimulate soil microbial activity.
Beejamrutha (Seed Treatment Concoction)
A biological seed dressing paste made from cow dung, urine, and lime to protect against fungal infections.
Acchadana (Soil Mulching)
The practice of covering soil with organic residues, living plants, or dust mulch to conserve moisture and suppress weeds.
Whapasa (Moisture-Air Equilibrium)
The optimal soil condition containing balanced proportions of water vapor and air in pore spaces.
NPOP (National Programme for Organic Production)
Indian regulatory framework providing standards and procedures for organic certification.
PGS (Participatory Guarantee System)
A peer-review certification process where local farmers evaluate and guarantee organic integrity.
VAM (Vesicular-Arbuscular Mycorrhiza)
Symbiotic endophytic fungi forming arbuscules within root cortex cells to improve phosphorus absorption.
Azotobacter (Azotobacter chroococcum)
A free-living aerobic nitrogen-fixing bacterium common in neutral to alkaline soils.
Dashparni Ark (Botanical Pest Repellent)
A fermented botanical extract prepared using leaves of ten different plants combined with cow products.
Green Manuring (In-situ Biomass Incorporation)
Growing leguminous crops like Crotalaria juncea and incorporating them green into soil to add organic matter and nitrogen.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Masanobu Fukuoka authored 'One-Straw Revolution'.
  • Jeevamrutha is fermented for 48 hours before field application.
  • APEDA is the nodal agency for NPOP implementation in India.
  • Whapasa ensures optimal root aeration and moisture balance.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Natural farming eliminates all synthetic external inputs and operates on 100% on-farm biological cycles.
  • The four pillars of the Palekar model are Jeevamrutha, Beejamrutha, Acchadana, and Whapasa.
  • Organic farming is regulated under NPOP in India and requires formal third-party or PGS certification.
  • Biological nitrogen fixation and VAM fungal inoculation are central to nutrient availability in organic systems.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Natural Farming VS Organic Farming

Natural farming prohibits all purchased external inputs (even organic composts), whereas organic farming permits certified organic external amendments.

Question 1 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Who is internationally recognized as the pioneer of natural farming and author of 'One-Straw Revolution'?

Explanation: Masanobu Fukuoka pioneered natural farming in Japan, emphasizing non-cultivation and non-intervention in natural ecological processes.

Question 2 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is NOT one of the four main pillars of natural farming according to the Palekar model?

Explanation: Chemical urea is a synthetic nitrogenous fertilizer and is completely prohibited in natural farming systems.

Question 3 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

For how many hours is Jeevamrutha typically fermented before application in the field?

Explanation: Jeevamrutha is brewed and fermented for 48 hours, during which beneficial bacterial populations multiply exponentially.

Question 4 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What does the term 'Whapasa' signify in natural farming systems?

Explanation: Whapasa refers to the condition where soil pore spaces contain adequate moisture vapor and oxygen for optimal root respiration.

Question 5 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which nodal agency oversees the implementation of the National Programme for Organic Production (NPOP) in India?

Explanation: APEDA functions as the apex secretariat for NPOP accreditation and certification in India.

Question 6 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Beejamrutha is primarily used for which of the following agricultural operations?

Explanation: Beejamrutha is a biological seed treatment concoction using cow dung, urine, and lime to protect seeds from fungal pathogens.

Question 7 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of VAM (Vesicular-Arbuscular Mycorrhiza) fungi in organic soils?

Explanation: VAM fungi form symbiotic associations with plant roots, extending hyphae to absorb and transport immobile phosphorus to the host plant.

Question 8 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Participatory Guarantee Systems (PGS) in organic agriculture are best described as:

Explanation: PGS is a decentralized organic quality assurance system based on peer review, trust, and local participation.

Question 9 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is a free-living aerobic nitrogen-fixing bacterium commonly used as biofertilizer?

Explanation: Azotobacter chroococcum is a free-living diazotroph that fixes atmospheric nitrogen in non-leguminous crop rhizospheres.

Question 10 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is 'Acchadana' in the context of natural farming?

Explanation: Acchadana refers to mulching using straw, live cover crops, or soil mulch to conserve moisture and suppress weeds.

Question 11 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which organic manure has the highest average nitrogen content?

Explanation: Poultry manure contains approximately 1.5% to 3.0% N, which is significantly higher than FYM or standard compost.

Question 12 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Biodynamic agriculture, a precursor to modern organic farming, was founded by:

Explanation: Rudolf Steiner delivered a series of agricultural lectures in 1924 that laid the foundation for biodynamic farming.

Question 13 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which botanical extract is widely utilized in organic farming for managing sucking insect pests?

Explanation: Neem oil and azadirachtin-based botanical formulations act as effective anti-feedants and insect growth regulators against sucking pests.

Question 14 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary distinction between natural farming and organic farming regarding external inputs?

Explanation: Natural farming relies exclusively on on-farm generated biological inputs (ZBNF model), whereas organic farming permits purchased certified organic amendments.

Question 15 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which microbe is specifically associated with symbiotic nitrogen fixation in leguminous root nodules?

Explanation: Rhizobium species form mutually beneficial symbiotic relationships with legume roots, fixing atmospheric nitrogen inside root nodules.

Question 16 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the role of jaggery in the preparation of Jeevamrutha?

Explanation: Jaggery supplies sugars that serve as a readily available energy source for rapid proliferation of beneficial microorganisms during fermentation.

Question 17 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which weed management practice in natural farming involves shallow inter-cultivation to disrupt soil capillaries?

Explanation: Soil mulch involves shallow surface stirring to break capillary rise, conserving moisture and destroying emerging weed seedlings.

Question 18 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Azolla is used as a biofertilizer primarily in which crop ecosystem?

Explanation: Azolla fern harbors cyanobacteria Anabaena azollae within its leaf cavities, fixing nitrogen effectively in flooded lowland rice fields.

Question 19 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

Which certification mark represents organic products under the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) and NPOP?

Explanation: The 'India Organic' logo is the mandatory certification mark for all organic products certified under NPOP in India.

Question 20 of 20 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary agronomic advantage of minimum tillage in organic and natural farming systems?

Explanation: Minimum tillage reduces soil carbon oxidation, preserves macro-pore networks, and protects earthworm populations.

In the formulation of 200 liters of Jeevamrutha, what are the standard recommended quantities of cow dung and cow urine?

Which of the following biofertilizers is classified as a free-living photosynthetic cyanobacterium capable of fixing atmospheric nitrogen in wetland rice?

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary role of Beejamrutha prior to seed sowing?

Solution & Rationale: Beejamrutha coats seeds with beneficial antagonistic microorganisms from cow dung and urine, protecting seedlings from soil- and seed-borne fungal pathogens.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following organic amendments has the highest C:N ratio, leading to slower mineralization and potential temporary nitrogen immobilization?

Solution & Rationale: Wheat straw has a very high C:N ratio (around 80:1 to 100:1), which causes soil microbes to immobilize available mineral nitrogen during decomposition.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

In organic crop production, phosphate solubilization is prominently mediated by which genus of soil bacteria?

Solution & Rationale: Phosphate-solubilizing bacteria (PSB) such as Bacillus megaterium secrete organic acids that solubilize insoluble soil phosphates.

Test Question 8 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary objective of introducing 'Live Mulch' in natural farming cropping systems?

Solution & Rationale: Live mulches (such as intercropped legumes) cover the soil surface, suppress weed growth, contribute biological nitrogen, and support beneficial soil fauna.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Solution & Rationale: Both statements are correct. Third-party certification is mandatory for export under NPOP, while PGS is ideal for domestic direct marketing.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

Which of the following botanical insecticides contains azadirachtin as its principal active constituent?

Solution & Rationale: Azadirachtin is the primary limonoid triterpenoid extracted from neem (Azadirachta indica) seeds, acting as an antifeedant and insect growth disruptor.
