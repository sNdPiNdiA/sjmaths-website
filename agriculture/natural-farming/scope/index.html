Natural and Organic Farming Systems

Scope and Principles of Natural and Organic Farming Systems in India

Explore the multi-dimensional scope, historical development, core principles, and socioeconomic relevance of natural and organic farming systems in India, tailored for UP TGT and PGT Agriculture examinations.

Historical Evolution and Paradigm Shift in Indian Agriculture

Indian agriculture has undergone a massive transformation from traditional, low-external-input sustenance farming to high-input intensive chemical farming during the Green Revolution era (mid-1960s). While the introduction of high-yielding dwarf varieties of wheat (Triticum aestivum) and rice (Oryza sativa) along with synthetic fertilizers and chemical pesticides made India self-sufficient in food grain production, subsequent decades revealed severe ecological and agronomic repercussions.

  • Second-Generation Problems: Intensive chemical cultivation led to multi-nutrient deficiencies (especially sulfur, zinc, and boron), declining organic carbon levels in soils (often dropping below 0.3%), groundwater depletion, and pesticide residue accumulation in the food chain.
  • Rise of Alternative Farming: To counteract environmental degradation, soil health deterioration, and escalating input costs, modern agrarian science has redirected focus toward Natural Farming (Prakritik Krishi) and Certified Organic Farming. These systems harness ecological loops, biological nitrogen fixation, and indigenous microorganisms (IMOs).

Core Principles and Conceptual Framework of Organic Farming

Organic farming is a holistic production management system that promotes and enhances agro-ecosystem health, including biodiversity, biological cycles, and soil biological activity. It is governed by four overarching principles defined by the International Federation of Organic Agriculture Movements (IFOAM):

  • Principle of Health: Sustaining and enhancing the health of soil, plant, animal, human, and planet as one indivisible whole. Healthy soils produce healthy crops that foster health in animals and humans.
  • Principle of Ecology: Basing agricultural systems on living ecological systems and cycles, working with them, mimicking them, and helping to sustain them.
  • Principle of Fairness: Building relationships that ensure equity with regard to the common environment and life opportunities, providing a good quality of life and food sovereignty.
  • Principle of Care: Managing agriculture in a precautionary and responsible manner to protect the health and well-being of current and future generations and the environment.

Natural Farming Systems: Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF) / Subhash Palekar Natural Farming (SPNF)

Natural farming, popularly popularized in India as Subhash Palekar Natural Farming (SPNF) and previously known as Zero Budget Natural Farming, operates on the foundational premise of eliminating chemical inputs and credit-based external purchases. It relies exclusively on indigenous on-farm biological inputs.

  • Jeevamrutha: A fermented microbial culture made from cow dung, cow urine, jaggery, pulse flour, live soil, and water. It acts as a catalytic inoculant that revives dormant soil macro- and micro-organisms.
  • Bijamrutha: A seed-treatment formulation utilizing cow dung, cow urine, lime, and forest soil to protect germinating seeds from soil- and seed-borne fungal pathogens such as Fusarium oxysporum and Rhizoctonia solani.
  • Acchadana (Mulching): Soil mulching using crop residues, live cover crops, or dead organic matter to conserve soil moisture, suppress weed emergence, and regulate soil thermal regimes.
  • Whapasa: Maintaining the optimal balance of air molecules and water vapor molecules within the root zone to prevent root asphyxiation and enhance moisture retention.

Agronomic Management, Nutrient Cycling, and Plant Protection

Managing soil fertility and crop protection in organic and natural systems requires proactive, preventive, and systemic approaches rather than curative chemical applications.

  • Biological Nutrient Supply: Utilization of farmyard manure (FYM), vermicompost, green manure crops like sunnhemp (Crotalaria juncea) and dhaincha (Sesbania aculeata), and biofertilizers such as Rhizobium, Azotobacter, Azospirillum, and Phosphorus Solubilizing Bacteria (PSB) like Bacillus megaterium.
  • Integrated Pest Management (IPM) & Biopesticides: Deployment of botanical extracts (neem oil containing azadirachtin), entomopathogenic fungi (Beauveria bassiana, Metarhizobium anisopliae), and bacterial bio-agents like Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) to suppress lepidopteran pests like Helicoverpa armigera.
  • Weed Management: Hand weeding, mechanical hoeing, stale seedbed techniques, competitive intercropping, and allelopathic cover crops instead of synthetic herbicides like glyphosate or pendimethalin.

National Schemes, Certification, and Economic Scope in India

The institutional scope of organic and natural farming in India is backed by robust national policies, certification frameworks, and market mechanisms.

  • PKVY (Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana): Promotes organic farming through cluster-based approaches, participatory guarantee systems (PGS), and financial assistance for input procurement and marketing.
  • NMOOP / Mission Organic Value Chain Development for North Eastern Region (MOVCDNER): Focuses on developing end-to-end organic value chains, processing, branding, and export facilitation.
  • Certification Systems: Third-party certification (e.g., NPOP - National Programme for Organic Production) for export markets and Participatory Guarantee Systems (PGS-India) for domestic markets through peer-review mechanisms.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • India ranks first globally in terms of organic farmers and ninth in terms of certified organic agricultural land (FiBL & IFOAM statistics).
  • Sikkim became the world's first fully organic state in the year 2016.
  • Jeevamrutha requires fresh cow dung (approx. 10 kg) and cow urine (5-10 liters) per 200 liters of water for one acre.
  • Participatory Guarantee Systems (PGS-India) operates under the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare for domestic organic assurance.
  • Azadirachtin, the principal active limonoid extracted from neem (Azadirachta indica), acts as an effective antifeedant and growth disruptor in organic pest management.
  • Green manure crops like sunnhemp (Crotalaria juncea) accumulate 80-100 kg N/ha within 45-60 days of growth.
  • The National Programme for Organic Production (NPOP) standards are officially notified under the Foreign Trade (Development and Regulation) Act by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry.
  • Whapasa refers to the coexistence of equal proportions of water molecules and air spaces within soil micro- and macro-pores.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Natural Farming with Organic Farming: Organic farming still permits certified commercial organic inputs and mined minerals, whereas Natural Farming (SPNF) strictly prohibits all external inputs, even organic fertilizers bought from outside the farm.
  • Assuming organic farming yields drop permanently: While there is a temporary yield reduction during the 2-3 year transition phase, yields stabilize and often surpass conventional systems as soil biological health recovers.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Scope and Principles of Natural and Organic Farming Systems in India

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Agro-ecosystem Health and Soil Microbiome

Natural and organic farming systems prioritize soil biological vitality over mere chemical fertility.

  • Soil Organic Carbon (SOC): The cornerstone of organic fertility, targeted to be maintained above 1.0% for resilient crop production.
  • Rhizosphere Dynamics: Enhanced by exudates from diverse cropping systems that feed mycorrhizal fungi and plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR).
Concept 2

Microbial Inoculants and Fermented Formulations

Harnessing indigenous microorganisms (IMOs) through localized fermentation technologies.

  • Jeevamrutha: Supplies billions of native soil bacteria and actinomycetes that mineralize unavailable soil nutrients.
  • Bijamrutha: Forms a protective biofilm around seeds, preventing seedling blights and root rots caused by soil fungi.
Concept 3

Certification and Regulatory Frameworks

Ensuring authenticity and consumer trust through rigorous verification standards.

  • NPOP: Governs export quality and international market standards.
  • PGS-India: Decentralized, peer-appraisal certification system tailored for domestic farmer groups.
Concept 4

Economic Resilience and Risk Mitigation

Insulating farmers from debt traps caused by high-cost chemical fertilizers and proprietary hybrid seeds.

  • Input Cost Reduction: Elimination of purchased chemical inputs drastically lowers the operational cost of cultivation.
  • Price Premium: Certified chemical-free produce commands higher market value, enhancing net farm income.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Organic Farming (Holistic Production Management System)
A production system that sustains the health of soils, ecosystems, and people, relying on ecological processes rather than synthetic inputs.
Natural Farming (Prakritik Krishi / SPNF)
A chemical-free farming system based on agroecology, utilizing on-farm natural inputs and eliminating external purchases.
Jeevamrutha (Fermented Microbial Liquid Formulation)
A concoction of cow dung, urine, jaggery, and pulse flour used to multiply soil microbes and enhance nutrient availability.
Bijamrutha (Seed Treatment Concoction)
An antimicrobial seed coating solution made from cow dung, urine, and lime to protect seeds against soil-borne pathogens.
Acchadana (Soil Mulching)
Covering the soil surface with crop residues or live vegetation to conserve moisture and suppress weed growth.
Whapasa (Soil Moisture-Air Equilibrium)
The ideal soil moisture condition where water vapor and air molecules coexist in equal proportions within root zones.
NPOP (National Programme for Organic Production)
The Indian statutory framework regulating organic certification and export standards under the Ministry of Commerce.
PGS-India (Participatory Guarantee System)
A peer-review certification mechanism operating locally for domestic organic marketing and verification.
Vermicompost (Earthworm-Processed Organic Manure)
Nutrient-rich organic fertilizer produced through the breakdown of organic wastes by epigeic earthworms like Eisenia fetida.
Azadirachtin (Limonoid Botanical Pesticide)
The primary active insecticidal compound derived from neem seeds (Azadirachta indica) used in organic crop protection.
Biofertilizer (Living Microbial Inoculant)
Preparations containing active strains of microorganisms that fix atmospheric nitrogen or solubilize phosphorus in the soil.

🎯 Must-Remember High-Frequency Facts

  • Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF) was renamed and structured academically as Subhash Palekar Natural Farming (SPNF).
  • Jeevamrutha is applied to the soil to activate soil fauna, not sprayed directly as a foliar nutrient for plant uptake.
  • NPOP certification is mandatory for exporting organic produce from India to international markets like the EU and USA.
  • Azadirachtin acts primarily as an antifeedant, insect growth regulator, and repellent rather than a direct knockdown neurotoxin.

📌 Quick Recap Bullet Points

  • Natural and organic farming systems restore soil biological activity, reverse degradation caused by Green Revolution technologies, and ensure long-term agricultural sustainability.
  • The four core principles of IFOAM are Health, Ecology, Fairness, and Care.
  • Subhash Palekar Natural Farming (SPNF) rests on four pillars: Jeevamrutha, Bijamrutha, Acchadana, and Whapasa.
  • Sikkim is recognized as the first 100% organic state in the world, achieving complete transition in 2016.
  • National schemes like Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana (PKVY) actively promote organic clusters and PGS certification in India.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Organic Farming VS Natural Farming

Organic farming permits external certified organic inputs, rock dust, and specific biopesticides; Natural Farming strictly prohibits any external inputs, relying 100% on internal biological cycles.

NPOP Certification VS PGS-India Certification

NPOP is a third-party mandatory certification for export and high-end retail; PGS-India is a decentralized peer-review group certification for domestic markets.

Question 1 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which state in India achieved the status of becoming the world's first fully organic state in 2016?

Explanation: Sikkim was officially declared the world's first 100% organic state by Prime Minister Narendra Modi in January 2016, phasing out chemical fertilizers and pesticides completely.

Question 2 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary function of 'Jeevamrutha' in Natural Farming systems?

Explanation: Jeevamrutha is a potent microbial culture that introduces millions of beneficial soil microbes to kickstart nutrient mineralization and biological activity in depleted soils.

Question 3 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Who is the pioneer associated with the conceptualization of Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF), later known as SPNF?

Explanation: Subhash Palekar developed ZBNF (now SPNF) as an alternative to input-intensive chemical farming, emphasizing zero external credit and zero chemical usage.

Question 4 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is NOT one of the four cardinal pillars of Subhash Palekar Natural Farming (SPNF)?

Explanation: Urea top-dressing is a hallmark of conventional chemical farming. The four pillars of SPNF are Jeevamrutha, Bijamrutha, Acchadana, and Whapasa.

Question 5 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What does the term 'Whapasa' signify in natural farming terminology?

Explanation: Whapasa refers to the ideal soil moisture condition where both water molecules and air pockets exist in balanced proportions, promoting root respiration and preventing plant stress.

Question 6 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which international body defines the four overarching principles of organic agriculture (Health, Ecology, Fairness, and Care)?

Explanation: IFOAM (International Federation of Organic Agriculture Movements) formulated the four core ethical principles governing organic agriculture globally.

Question 7 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Under which Indian ministry does the National Programme for Organic Production (NPOP) operate for export certification?

Explanation: NPOP standards are notified under the Foreign Trade (Development and Regulation) Act by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, Government of India.

Question 8 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary role of 'Bijamrutha' in crop establishment?

Explanation: Bijamrutha is a fermented concoction of cow dung, cow urine, lime, and soil used to coat seeds, protecting them from fungal diseases during germination.

Question 9 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is 'Acchadana' in the context of natural farming systems?

Explanation: Acchadana refers to mulching (soil cover), which suppresses weed emergence, moderates soil temperature, and conserves soil moisture.

Question 10 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which certification system in India is specifically designed for domestic organic markets through decentralized peer appraisal?

Explanation: PGS-India operates on trust and peer review among farmer groups, making organic certification accessible and affordable for domestic smallholders.

Question 11 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which botanical compound extracted from neem (Azadirachta indica) is widely used as an organic biopesticide?

Explanation: Azadirachtin is the primary active tetranortriterpenoid compound in neem that acts as an antifeedant, insect growth regulator, and repellent.

Question 12 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary objective of the Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana (PKVY) in India?

Explanation: PKVY is a flagship sub-mission under the National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture (NMSA) aimed at promoting organic farming clusters and chemical-free food production.

Question 13 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Why is deep tillage generally discouraged in Natural Farming systems?

Explanation: Deep inversion tillage disrupts natural soil micro-environments, exposes organic carbon to rapid microbial oxidation, and destroys earthworm networks.

Question 14 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which microorganisms are primarily responsible for symbiotic biological nitrogen fixation in legume root nodules?

Explanation: Bacteria belonging to the genus Rhizobium form symbiotic associations with legume roots, fixing atmospheric nitrogen into plant-usable ammonium.

Question 15 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the main advantage of incorporating green manure crops like Crotalaria juncea (sunnhemp) into agricultural soils?

Explanation: Green manuring with leguminous crops adds substantial organic biomass and fixes atmospheric nitrogen, improving soil structure and fertility.

Question 16 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which of the following is an entomopathogenic fungus commonly used in organic pest management against insect larvae?

Explanation: Beauveria bassiana is a soil-borne entomopathogenic fungus that infects and kills various insect pests upon direct contact.

Question 17 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

What is the primary limitation faced by farmers during the conversion phase from conventional to certified organic farming?

Explanation: During the conversion period (usually 2-3 years), soils readjust their biological equilibria, often resulting in temporary yield dips before organic equilibrium is achieved.

Question 18 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

In the context of organic nutrient management, what do Phosphorus Solubilizing Bacteria (PSB) such as Bacillus megaterium do?

Explanation: PSB secrete organic acids that dissolve bound or insoluble soil phosphates, making orthophosphate ions readily available for plant uptake.

Question 19 of 19 Conceptual MCQ

Which organization manages the standards for Participatory Guarantee Systems (PGS-India) at the national level?

Explanation: PGS-India is implemented and overseen at the national level by the National Centre of Organic Farming (NCOF) under the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare.

Analyze the conceptual difference between Certified Organic Farming and Natural Farming (SPNF). Which statement is scientifically accurate?

Solution & Rationale: SPNF strictly enforces zero external inputs (no purchase of even organic fertilizers), while certified organic farming permits approved external organic soil amendments and pest control substances.

Test Question 2 of 10 PGT Standard

During the preparation of Jeevamrutha, why are fresh cow dung and cow urine combined with jaggery and pulse flour?

Solution & Rationale: Jaggery supplies sugars (carbon) and pulse flour provides proteins and amino acids, serving as an ideal growth medium to multiply the microbial populations present in cow dung.

Test Question 3 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the primary agronomic significance of maintaining 'Whapasa' in natural farming crop production?

Solution & Rationale: Whapasa maintains the delicate balance of air and water in soil pores, which is vital for aerobic respiration of plant roots and beneficial soil microbes.

Test Question 4 of 10 TGT Standard

Which regulatory framework governs the export of organic agricultural commodities from India to international markets like the European Union?

Solution & Rationale: NPOP provides the institutional framework for third-party export certification, ensuring equivalence with international organic standards.

Test Question 5 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the role of green manuring crops like Sesbania aculeata (dhaincha) in organic cropping systems?

Solution & Rationale: Green manuring crops add rich organic carbon, fix nitrogen, and often release root exudates that suppress harmful soil nematodes.

Test Question 6 of 10 PGT Standard

How do biofertilizers such as Azotobacter contribute to plant nutrition in organic agriculture?

Solution & Rationale: Azotobacter is a free-living diazotroph that fixes atmospheric nitrogen in non-leguminous plant rhizospheres and synthesizes auxins and gibberellins.

Test Question 7 of 10 TGT Standard

Why is third-party certification under NPOP often considered less accessible for marginal smallholder farmers in India compared to PGS?

Solution & Rationale: Third-party certification involves high recurring costs and rigorous documentation, making PGS (Participatory Guarantee System) a more viable alternative for local and domestic markets.

Test Question 8 of 10 PGT Standard

What is the scientific mechanism of action of entomopathogenic nematodes (EPNs) like Steinernema carpocapsae in organic pest control?

Solution & Rationale: EPNs seek out soil-dwelling insect pests, enter their natural body openings, and release symbiotic bacteria that kill the insect within 24-48 hours.

Test Question 9 of 10 PGT Standard

Which of the following best explains the phenomenon of soil sickness often mitigated by organic management practices?

Solution & Rationale: Soil sickness arises from continuous chemical use and monoculturing, which depletes microbial diversity and allows specific pathogens to dominate. Organic management restores microbial equilibrium.

Test Question 10 of 10 TGT Standard

What is the core philosophy behind 'Acchadana' (mulching) in natural farming regarding weed management?

Solution & Rationale: Mulching creates a physical barrier that intercepts light, preventing photosynthetic activity and emergence of weed seedlings without chemical herbicides.
