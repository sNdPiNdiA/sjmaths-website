Natural Farming Systems in Agronomy

Natural farming is an ecologically driven, chemical-free agricultural production system pioneered by Masanobu Fukuoka and advanced in India by Subhash Palekar as Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF) / Subhash Palekar Natural Farming (SPNF), focusing on soil microbiology, indigenous inputs, and biodiversity conservation.

Principles and Philosophy of Natural Farming

Natural farming operates on the core principle of working in harmony with natural ecosystems rather than subduing them with synthetic chemicals, deep tillage, and external energy inputs. It relies entirely on self-regulating biological processes within the soil-plant-atmosphere continuum.

  • No Tillage (Zero Tillage): The soil is not plowed, turned, or disturbed, preserving natural soil stratification, earthworm burrows, and fungal mycelial networks (e.g., Glomus species).
  • No Chemical Fertilizers or Pesticides: Complete elimination of synthetic urea, diammonium phosphate (DAP), muriate of potash (MOP), and broad-spectrum neurotoxic pesticides.
  • No Weeding by Tillage: Weeds are controlled through mulching and cover cropping rather than physical or chemical eradication.
  • No Pruning: Plants are allowed to grow in their natural architectural forms, enhancing natural disease resistance.

Core Pillars of Subhash Palekar Natural Farming (SPNF)

In the Indian context, Natural Farming has been popularized under the framework of SPNF (formerly ZBNF), which rests on four foundational pillars designed to eliminate the cost of external credit and chemical inputs.

  • Jeevamrutha: A fermented microbial culture made from fresh indigenous cow dung (Bos indicus), cow urine, jaggery, pulse flour, and living soil, applied to soil to stimulate manifold microbial activity.
  • Bijamrutha: A seed treatment formulation consisting of cow dung, cow urine, lime, and forest soil, protecting germinating seedlings from seed-borne and soil-borne fungal pathogens.
  • Acchadana (Mulching): Covering topsoil with crop residues, live cover crops, or mixed mulches to conserve soil moisture, regulate soil temperature, and suppress weed emergence.
  • Whapasa (Moisture-Air Balance): The condition where soil contains both moisture and air molecules in optimal balance, achieved by minimizing irrigation frequency and enhancing soil organic carbon.

Preparation and Application of Botanical Formulations

Natural farming substitutes synthetic agrochemicals with potent fermented botanicals and indigenous livestock co-products that act as plant growth promoters and biopesticides.

  • Jeevamrutha Preparation: 200 kg local cow dung, 20 liters local cow urine, 2 kg jaggery, 2 kg pulse flour (gram flour), and a handful of virgin bund soil are mixed in 200 liters of water. Fermented for 48 hours under shade with constant stirring, it is applied per acre through irrigation water or direct soil drenching.
  • Agniastra: A potent bio-pesticide formulated by boiling crushed leaves of neem (Azadirachta indica), green chili (Capsicum annuum), tobacco (Nicotiana tabacum), and garlic (Allium sativum) in cow urine, effective against chewing and sucking insect pests.
  • Neemastra: Prepared using 10 kg neem leaves, 10 liters cow urine, and 2 kg cow dung fermented for 48 hours, targeting soft-bodied sucking pests like aphids (Aphis gossypii) and whiteflies (Bemisia tabaci).

Soil Microbiology and Nutrient Cycling in Natural Farming

Soil health in natural farming is evaluated through biological activity rather than merely chemical nutrient status. The addition of Jeevamrutha introduces billions of native soil microbes, including Plant Growth-Promoting Rhizobacteria (PGPR) like Azotobacter chroococcum, Bacillus subtilis, and Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria (PSB) such as Pseudomonas putida.

  • Mycorrhizal Symbiosis: Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi (AMF) form mutualistic associations with plant roots, vastly expanding the absorptive surface area for immobile phosphorus (P) and zinc (Zn) uptake.
  • Organic Carbon Accumulation: Continuous root exudation and surface mulching elevate Soil Organic Carbon (SOC), improving cation exchange capacity (CEC) and aggregate stability.
  • Nitrogen Fixation: Asymbiotic and associative biological nitrogen fixation (BNF) meet a substantial fraction of crop nitrogen requirements without synthetic fertilizer leaching.

Crop Protection, Intercropping, and Biodiversity Management

Pest and disease management in natural farming relies on ecological pest suppression (EPS) through diversity rather than eradication via chemical toxins.

  • Trap Cropping: Planting marigold (Tagetes erecta) around tomato fields to trap tomato fruit borer (Helicoverpa armigera) or castor (Ricinus communis) for Spodoptera litura.
  • Multi-tier Cropping (Babu/Mixed Cropping): Growing crops of varying canopy heights simultaneously (e.g., coconut, banana, black pepper, and pineapple) to maximize solar interception and confuse insect host-search behavior.
  • Bird Perches: Installing T-shaped wooden perches across fields to invite predatory birds that consume lepidopteran larvae.

⚡ High-Yield Exam Points (UP TGT / PGT Focus)
  • Masanobu Fukuoka is the global father of Natural Farming, author of 'One-Straw Revolution'.
  • Subhash Palekar popularized Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF), now designated as Subhash Palekar Natural Farming (SPNF) in India.
  • Jeevamrutha supplies approximately 200–500 billion beneficial soil microorganisms per application per acre.
  • Bijamrutha is primarily used as a seed treatment to protect against soil-borne fungal pathogens like Fusarium oxysporum.
  • Whapasa refers to the ideal soil physical condition containing 50% moisture and 50% air pore space.
  • Acchadana (Mulching) reduces soil evaporation by up to 50–70% and suppresses weed germination by cutting off light.
  • Agniastra and Brahmastra are traditional fermented botanical formulations used for controlling lepidopteran and sucking pests.
  • Indigenous cow (Bos indicus) dung and urine are scientifically preferred in SPNF due to higher microbial richness compared to exotic dairy cattle.
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Exam Pitfalls
  • Confusing Natural Farming with Organic Farming: Organic farming still permits mined minerals, imported organic fertilizers, and heavy tractor tillage, whereas Natural Farming strictly forbids tillage and external inputs.
  • Assuming Jeevamrutha is a direct plant nutrient source: Jeevamrutha acts primarily as a biological catalyst and microbial inoculant rather than a bulk chemical fertilizer.

⚡ Chapter Master Summary: Natural Farming Systems in Agronomy

Systematic concept review designed for quick retention and last-minute pre-exam revision.

Concept 1

Philosophy of Zero Tillage

Zero tillage forms the bedrock of natural farming, preventing the oxidation of soil organic matter and safeguarding soil macrofauna.

  • Mycelial Preservation: Avoiding plowing protects delicate fungal hyphae that facilitate nutrient transfer.
  • Soil Aggregation: Glomalin secreted by mycorrhizae stabilizes soil aggregates against water erosion.
Concept 2

Microbial Inoculation via Jeevamrutha

Jeevamrutha bridges the gap between dormant native soil microflora and active nutrient mobilization.

  • Fermentation Window: Optimum microbial population peaks between 48 to 72 hours of aerobic fermentation.
  • Enzyme Activation: Provides cellulolytic and proteolytic enzymes that break down native soil organic residues.
Concept 3

Moisture-Air Equilibrium (Whapasa)

Whapasa ensures roots do not suffer from hypoxia (waterlogging) or drought stress.

  • Capillary Action: Enhanced soil organic carbon improves upward water movement during dry spells.
  • Root Respiration: Adequate macropores supply continuous oxygen for root metabolic respiration.
Concept 4

Botanical Pest Management

Botanicals like Azadirachtin from neem disrupt insect metamorphosis and feeding behavior without causing secondary pest outbreaks.

  • Anti-feedant Property: Deters herbivorous insects from consuming treated foliage.
  • Selective Toxicity: Harmless to beneficial parasitoids and predatory insects.

📚 Technical Terms Glossary

Natural Farming (Fukuoka System / Shizen Nōhō)
An ecological farming system that requires no human intervention in the form of tillage, synthetic chemicals, weeding, or pruning.
SPNF (Subhash Palekar Natural Farming)
The official Indian adaptation of zero-budget natural farming focusing on four main pillars.
Jeevamrutha (Microbial Bio-Inoculant Culture)
A fermented liquid formulation of cow dung, urine, jaggery, and flour used to activate soil microflora.
Bijamrutha (Botanical Seed Treatment)
A protective coating mixture applied to seeds before sowing to prevent seed-borne fungal infections.
Acchadana (Soil Mulching)
The practice of covering soil surfaces with organic residues or live plants to conserve moisture and suppress weeds.
Whapasa (Moisture-Air Balance)
The ideal soil state where water molecules and air pockets coexist in equal proportion in root zones.
Agniastra (Fermented Bio-Pesticide)
A concoction of tobacco, chili, garlic, and neem boiled in cow urine to control chewing insect pests.
Neemastra (Botanical Sucking Pest Deterrent)
A botanical spray made from neem leaves and cow urine targeted at aphids and whiteflies.
Glomus (Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi Genus)
A genus of fungi forming symbiotic relationships with plant roots to enhance phosphorus uptake.
Trap Crop (Allele-Attractant Companion Crop)
A secondary crop planted alongside main crops to lure away destructive insect pests.
Bos indicus (Humped Zebu Cattle)
The indigenous Indian cattle species whose dung and urine are mandatory for SPNF formulations.
One-Straw Revolution (Fukuokan Agrarian Manifesto)
The seminal book authored by Masanobu Fukuoka outlining the philosophy of natural farming.

⚖️ Concepts Often Confused in Examinations

Natural Farming VS Organic Farming

Natural farming prohibits all tillage and external commercial organic inputs, whereas organic farming allows tillage and purchased organic manures.

Jeevamrutha VS Bijamrutha

Jeevamrutha is applied to the soil as a general microbial stimulant, while Bijamrutha is specifically used as a seed treatment before sowing.
