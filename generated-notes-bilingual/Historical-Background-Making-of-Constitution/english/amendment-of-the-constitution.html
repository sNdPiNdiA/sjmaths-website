Amendment of the Constitution

UPSC Study Notes - Brief and Comprehensive

ENGLISH

Constitutional Provisions

  • Article 368 (Part XX) deals with the power of Parliament to amend the Constitution and its procedure.
  • The Constitution is a blend of rigidity (special majority) and flexibility (simple majority).

Types of Amendments

The Constitution provides for three modes of amendment:

  • Simple Majority: Amendments by a simple majority of Parliament (like ordinary legislation). Examples: Admission of new states, alteration of boundaries, creation of Legislative Councils.
  • Special Majority: Amendments by a majority of the total membership of each House and a majority of not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting. Used for Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles.
  • Special Majority + Ratification: Requires special majority plus ratification by at least half of the state legislatures by a simple majority. Used for federal provisions (e.g., Election of President, Supreme Court/High Courts, GST Council).

Procedure for Amendment

  • Initiation: Bill can be introduced in either House of Parliament, not in State Legislatures.
  • Introduction: Can be introduced by a Minister or a private member; no prior permission of the President is required.
  • Passage: Must be passed in each House by the prescribed majority.
  • Deadlock: There is no provision for a joint sitting to resolve a deadlock between the two Houses.
  • Presidential Assent: The President must give assent (24th Amendment Act, 1971 made it mandatory). The President cannot withhold or return the bill.

Key Judicial Doctrines

  • Shankari Prasad Case (1951): Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution, including Fundamental Rights.
  • Golaknath Case (1967): Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights; they are "sacrosanct."
  • Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973): Parliament can amend any part, but cannot alter the "Basic Structure" of the Constitution.
  • Minerva Mills Case (1980): Reaffirmed the Basic Structure doctrine and limited the amending power of Parliament.

Important Amendments for Prelims

  • 1st Amendment (1951): Added 9th Schedule to protect land reform laws.
  • 24th Amendment (1971): Made it compulsory for the President to assent to Constitutional Amendment Bills.
  • 42nd Amendment (1976): Known as the "Mini-Constitution"; added 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity' to the Preamble.
  • 44th Amendment (1978): Restored some powers of the Judiciary; removed 'Right to Property' from Fundamental Rights.
  • 61st Amendment (1989): Reduced voting age from 21 to 18 years.
  • 101st Amendment (2016): Introduced Goods and Services Tax (GST).
  • 103rd Amendment (2019): Provided 10% reservation for EWS.
