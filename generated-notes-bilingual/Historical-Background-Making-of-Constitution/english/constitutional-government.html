Constitutional Government

UPSC Study Notes - Brief and Comprehensive

ENGLISH

Definition and Core Concept

  • Constitutional Government refers to a system where the authority of the government is derived from and limited by a body of fundamental law (the Constitution).
  • It is the antithesis of arbitrary power; it ensures that the state operates within defined legal boundaries.
  • Key principle: Limited Government—the state exists to serve the citizens, not to exercise absolute control.

Essential Features

  • Rule of Law: All individuals, including government officials, are subject to the law. No one is above the Constitution.
  • Separation of Powers: Division of state responsibilities into distinct branches (Legislature, Executive, Judiciary) to prevent concentration of power.
  • Fundamental Rights: Guaranteed protections for citizens against state encroachment, ensuring individual liberty.
  • Independent Judiciary: Acts as the guardian of the Constitution, possessing the power of Judicial Review to strike down unconstitutional acts.
  • Accountability and Transparency: Mechanisms like elections, parliamentary oversight, and Right to Information (RTI) ensure the government remains answerable to the people.

Constitutionalism vs. Constitution

  • Constitution: A document or set of rules defining the structure of government.
  • Constitutionalism: The political philosophy that the government should be legally limited in its powers and its authority depends on its observance of these limitations.
  • A country can have a Constitution (e.g., authoritarian regimes) but lack Constitutionalism if the government ignores legal constraints.

Relevance in Indian Context

  • Preamble: Declares India a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic, setting the tone for constitutional governance.
  • Part III (Fundamental Rights): Acts as a check on legislative and executive actions.
  • Article 13: Explicitly empowers the Judiciary to declare laws void if they contravene Fundamental Rights.
  • Federalism: Distribution of powers between the Union and States prevents centralization of authority.
  • Checks and Balances: The system of 'Checks and Balances' ensures that no single organ of the state becomes supreme.

UPSC Perspective: Key Takeaways

  • Limited Government: The most important feature of a constitutional government.
  • Constitutional Morality: The commitment to the inclusive and democratic values enshrined in the Constitution, beyond just the letter of the law.
  • Dynamic Nature: The Constitution is a 'living document', capable of evolving through amendments to meet changing societal needs while maintaining its core structure (Basic Structure Doctrine).
