Charter Act of 1853

The Charter Act of 1853 was the last of the series of Charter Acts passed by the British Parliament between 1793 and 1853. It was a significant constitutional landmark that laid the foundation of the modern parliamentary form of government in India.

Key Legislative Provisions

  • Separation of Functions: For the first time, the legislative and executive functions of the Governor-General’s Council were separated.
  • Legislative Council: A separate Governor-General’s Legislative Council (also known as the Indian Central Legislative Council) was established, consisting of 6 new members called Legislative Councillors.
  • Mini-Parliament: The legislative wing functioned as a mini-parliament, adopting procedures similar to the British Parliament. Legislation was treated as a special function of the government requiring special machinery and process.

Civil Services Reforms

  • Open Competition: The Act introduced an open competition system for the selection and recruitment of civil servants.
  • Macaulay Committee: The Macaulay Committee (Committee on the Indian Civil Service) was appointed in 1854 to implement this system, effectively ending the patronage system of the East India Company.

Administrative and Territorial Provisions

  • Company's Rule Extended: Unlike previous acts, this Act did not specify any particular period for the continuation of the Company's rule. This was a clear indication that the Company's control could be terminated by the British Parliament at any time.
  • Local Representation: Out of the 6 new legislative members, 4 were appointed by the local (provincial) governments of Madras, Bombay, Bengal, and Agra.
  • Law Member: The Law Member was made a full-time member of the Governor-General’s Executive Council.

Significance for UPSC

  • Constitutional Precedent: It established the principle that legislation is a distinct function of the government, separate from administration.
  • Indian Participation: Although limited, it introduced the concept of local representation in the central legislative process.
  • End of Patronage: The shift toward merit-based recruitment (Civil Services) marked the beginning of the professionalization of the Indian administration.
