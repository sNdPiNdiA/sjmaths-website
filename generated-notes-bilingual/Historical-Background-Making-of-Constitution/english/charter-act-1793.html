Charter Act of 1793

The Charter Act of 1793, also known as the East India Company Act 1793, was passed by the British Parliament to renew the East India Company's (EIC) charter for another 20 years.

Key Provisions

  • Extension of Monopoly: The EIC’s trade monopoly in India was extended for a further period of 20 years.
  • Governor-General’s Powers: The Governor-General of Bengal was given more power over the presidencies of Bombay and Madras. He could override his council’s decisions in matters concerning peace, security, or the interests of the British Empire in India.
  • Commander-in-Chief: The Commander-in-Chief was no longer automatically a member of the Governor-General’s Council, unless specifically appointed by the Governor-General.
  • Payment of Staff: The salaries of the members of the Board of Control and their staff were to be paid out of the Indian revenues, a practice that continued until 1919.
  • Seniority Rule: It was mandated that all members of the Governor-General’s Council must have resided in India for at least 12 years.
  • Legislative Framework: The regulations framed by the government were now required to be registered in the law courts, giving them the force of law.
  • Separation of Functions: The Act initiated the separation of revenue administration from judicial functions, effectively ending the magisterial powers of the Collectors.

Significance for UPSC

  • Consolidation: It did not introduce major structural changes but consolidated the existing administrative framework established by the Pitt’s India Act of 1784.
  • Financial Burden: By charging the salaries of the Board of Control to Indian revenues, it marked the beginning of the "Home Charges", a major point of criticism by early Indian nationalists.
  • Centralization: It reinforced the trend of centralization of power in the hands of the Governor-General of Bengal over the other presidencies.
