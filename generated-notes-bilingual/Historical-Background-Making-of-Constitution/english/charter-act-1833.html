Charter Act of 1833 (Saint Helena Act)

The Charter Act of 1833 was a significant step towards the centralization of administration in British India. It served as the final step in the gradual process of British constitutional development in India.

Key Provisions

  • Centralization of Power: The Governor-General of Bengal was redesignated as the Governor-General of India, vested with all civil and military powers. Lord William Bentinck became the first Governor-General of India.
  • Legislative Supremacy: The Governors of Bombay and Madras were stripped of their legislative powers. The Governor-General of India was given exclusive legislative powers for the entire British India.
  • End of Commercial Activities: The East India Company was transformed into a purely administrative body. It was required to close its commercial activities entirely.
  • Retention of Territories: The Company’s territories in India were held by it "in trust for His Majesty, his heirs and successors."
  • Law Commission: The Act provided for the codification of laws. A Law Commission was constituted under the chairmanship of Lord Macaulay to codify all Indian laws.
  • Open Competition: It attempted to introduce a system of open competition for the selection of civil servants. It stated that Indians should not be debarred from holding any place, office, or employment under the Company (though this was later negated by the Court of Directors).
  • Slavery: The Act directed the Governor-General-in-Council to take measures to mitigate the state of slavery and ultimately abolish it (leading to the Slavery Abolition Act of 1843).

Significance for UPSC

  • Final Centralization: It marked the zenith of centralization in British India, which began with the Regulating Act of 1773.
  • Legislative Council: The Governor-General's Council was enlarged for legislative purposes by the addition of a fourth member (Law Member), who could participate only in legislative proceedings.
  • Administrative Shift: By ending the Company's commercial monopoly, the Act aligned the British governance model in India with the principles of Laissez-faire.
