Parliamentary System: Indian vs. British Model

India adopted the British Parliamentary system (Westminster model) but with significant modifications to suit its republican and federal nature.

Key Differences

  • Head of State:
    • British Model: Monarch (Hereditary position).
    • Indian Model: President (Elected indirectly for a fixed term).
  • Sovereignty of Parliament:
    • British Model: Parliament is supreme; there is no judicial review of primary legislation.
    • Indian Model: Parliament is not supreme; it functions within the limits of a written Constitution, subject to Judicial Review.
  • Nature of Constitution:
    • British Model: Unwritten Constitution; relies on conventions, statutes, and judicial decisions.
    • Indian Model: Written Constitution; the supreme law of the land.
  • Membership of Ministers:
    • British Model: Only members of Parliament can be appointed as ministers.
    • Indian Model: A person can be appointed as a minister for six months without being a member of either House of Parliament.
  • Legal Responsibility of Ministers:
    • British Model: Ministers are not legally responsible for the official acts of the Head of State (countersignature is required).
    • Indian Model: There is no such system of legal responsibility; courts are barred from inquiring into the nature of advice tendered by ministers to the President (Article 74).
  • Shadow Cabinet:
    • British Model: Unique institution formed by the opposition to balance the ruling cabinet.
    • Indian Model: No such formal institution exists.

Commonalities

  • Presence of the Executive as part of the Legislature.
  • Collective Responsibility of the executive to the legislature.
  • Political Homogeneity (members of the ruling party share the same ideology).
  • Double Membership (ministers are members of both the legislature and executive).
  • Leadership of the Prime Minister.
  • Dissolution of the Lower House (Lok Sabha/House of Commons).

Significance for UPSC

The Indian model is a synthesis of the British Parliamentary system and the American Presidential system (e.g., Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review). While India follows the British principle of Parliamentary Sovereignty, it is balanced by the Constitutional Supremacy required for a diverse federal state.
