Amending Act of 1781 (The Declaratory Act)

The Amending Act of 1781 was enacted by the British Parliament to rectify the defects of the Regulating Act of 1773. It is also known as the Declaratory Act.

Key Objectives

  • To remove the ambiguities and administrative friction created by the Regulating Act of 1773.
  • To resolve the conflict of jurisdiction between the Supreme Court at Calcutta and the Governor-General-in-Council.

Major Provisions

  • Exemption for Government Servants: It exempted the Governor-General and the Council from the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court for acts done in their official capacity.
  • Jurisdiction Limitation: The Supreme Court’s jurisdiction was restricted to Calcutta only. It was mandated to administer the personal law of the defendants (Hindus according to Hindu law, Muslims according to Mohammedan law).
  • Revenue Matters: The Supreme Court was stripped of its jurisdiction over matters concerning revenue collection or acts ordered in the collection of revenue.
  • Appellate Authority: It mandated that appeals from the Provincial Courts were to be taken to the Governor-General-in-Council, not the Supreme Court.
  • Legislative Power: The Governor-General-in-Council was empowered to frame regulations for the Provincial Courts and Councils, effectively strengthening the executive over the judiciary.

Significance for UPSC

  • Shift in Power: It marked a significant step in curbing the judicial activism of the Supreme Court and asserting the supremacy of the Executive (Governor-General-in-Council).
  • Legal Pluralism: By mandating the application of personal laws, it acknowledged the necessity of respecting indigenous legal customs in the British judicial system.
  • Precedent: It established the pattern of "amending" previous constitutional acts, a practice that continued throughout the British colonial period.
