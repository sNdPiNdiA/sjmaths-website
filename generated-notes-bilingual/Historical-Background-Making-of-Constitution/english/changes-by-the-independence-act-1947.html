UPSC Study Notes - Brief and Comprehensive

ENGLISH

Indian Independence Act, 1947

Enacted by the British Parliament on July 18, 1947, based on the Mountbatten Plan (June 3 Plan), this Act ended British rule in India and established two independent dominions.

Key Provisions

  • End of British Rule: Declared India as an independent and sovereign state from August 15, 1947.
  • Partition of India: Provided for the creation of two independent dominions—India and Pakistan—with the right to secede from the British Commonwealth.
  • Abolition of Viceroy: Abolished the office of the Viceroy and provided for a Governor-General for each dominion, to be appointed by the British King on the advice of the dominion cabinet.
  • Constituent Assemblies: Empowered the Constituent Assemblies of the two dominions to frame and adopt any constitution and to repeal any act of the British Parliament, including the Independence Act itself.
  • Legislative Sovereignty: Until the new constitutions were framed, the existing Constituent Assemblies were empowered to legislate for their respective dominions. No Act of the British Parliament passed after August 15, 1947, would extend to either dominion unless extended by a law of the legislature of the dominion.
  • Termination of British Suzerainty: Lapsed the paramountcy of the British Crown over the Indian princely states. They were free to join either India or Pakistan or remain independent.
  • Abolition of Secretary of State: Abolished the office of the Secretary of State for India and transferred his functions to the Secretary of State for Commonwealth Affairs.
  • Civil Services: Discontinued the appointment to civil services and reservation of posts by the Secretary of State. However, those already in service were entitled to receive the same benefits as before.

Important Facts for Prelims

  • Royal Titles: Dropped the title of "Emperor of India" from the royal titles of the British King.
  • Interim Arrangement: The Act provided that the Government of India Act, 1935 would continue to govern the dominions until the new constitutions were framed, with necessary modifications.
  • First Governor-Generals: Lord Mountbatten became the first Governor-General of the new Dominion of India; Muhammad Ali Jinnah became the first Governor-General of Pakistan.
