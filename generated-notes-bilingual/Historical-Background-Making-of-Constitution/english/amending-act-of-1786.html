Amending Act of 1786

The Amending Act of 1786 was enacted to facilitate the appointment of Lord Cornwallis as the Governor-General of Bengal. Cornwallis insisted on specific powers before accepting the position, leading to this legislative intervention.

Key Provisions

  • Veto Power: The Governor-General was empowered to override the decisions of his Council in special cases affecting the safety, peace, or interests of the British Empire in India.
  • Dual Role: The Act combined the offices of the Governor-General of Bengal and the Commander-in-Chief.
  • Precedent: These provisions were specifically designed to ensure that Lord Cornwallis had absolute authority over his subordinates and the military.

Significance for UPSC

  • Centralization: This Act marked a significant step toward the centralization of power, shifting authority from the Council to the individual Governor-General.
  • Precedent for Future: The power to override the Council became a standard feature of the Governor-General's authority in subsequent Charter Acts.
  • Context: It reflects the British Parliament's attempt to balance the administrative needs of the East India Company with the necessity of strong, decisive leadership in India.
