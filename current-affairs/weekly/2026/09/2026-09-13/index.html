National Affairs: Kishau Project, UCC, PM Vishwakarma and New Policy Updates

NationalHigh importance13–19 September 2026

Key Facts & Highlights

  • India hosted the 18th BRICS Summit in New Delhi on 12–13 September 2026. India holds the 2026 chairship under the theme “Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability”; initiatives include an infectious-disease early-warning system, logistics supply-chain framework, incubator network, Startup Innovation Fund, MSME Portal, smart-grid and energy-storage centre, Urban Mobility Hub and agro-ecology centres.
  • India and China held their first Corps Commander-level meeting in the Arunachal Pradesh sector at the Wacha and Damai border meeting points as part of military communication for peace and stability along the LAC.
  • Six states and Delhi signed an MoU on 15 September for the Kishau Multipurpose Project on the Tons River, a major Yamuna tributary. The project is estimated at about ₹15,000 crore, with a 232.6-metre dam, about 1,476 million units of hydropower and irrigation potential of about 97,076 hectares; the site connects Sirmaur in Himachal Pradesh with Dehradun in Uttarakhand.
  • Union Home Minister Amit Shah stated on 13 September that the BJP-NDA-ruled 21 states would implement a Uniform Civil Code before the 2029 Lok Sabha election. Article 44 directs the State to endeavour to secure a Uniform Civil Code for citizens.
  • The Ministry of Information and Broadcasting revised CBFC film-certification guidelines, including statutory warnings for drug or psychotropic-substance scenes and UA age markers of UA 7+, UA 13+ and UA 16+ under the Cinematograph Rules, 2024.
  • The Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Act, 2026 is to take effect from 1 October 2026. Normal registration is expected within 21 days; delay beyond one year and up to two years requires DM, SDM or authorised Executive Magistrate approval, while delay beyond two years requires a Judicial Magistrate First Class order.
  • PM Vishwakarma completed three years on 17 September with about 30 lakh artisans registered. The scheme supports traditional artisans through skill training, modern tools, affordable credit, digital support and market access.
  • PM-Kisan Maandhan Yojana completed seven years in September 2026. About 24.96 lakh farmers were enrolled by February 2026; eligible small and marginal farmers aged 18–40 years with cultivable holdings up to two hectares make voluntary contributions for an assured pension after age 60.
  • The West Bengal Government approved a proposal for a DRDO weapon and missile-testing range at Junput in Purba Medinipur. It is intended to add a third major missile-testing hub for ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, air-defence systems and BMD systems.
  • A three-day Colombo Security Conclave Table-Top Exercise was held in Visakhapatnam on maritime terrorism, narcotics and human trafficking, IUU fishing and maritime security. Members include India, Sri Lanka, Maldives, Mauritius, Bangladesh and Seychelles.

The week combined BRICS diplomacy, the Kishau inter-state river project, constitutional policy debate, certification and civil-registration reforms, artisan and farmer schemes, defence infrastructure and maritime security.

Exam angle: Remember the BRICS theme and initiatives, Kishau River and measurements, Article 44, CBFC age markers, delayed-registration authorities, PM Vishwakarma, PM-KMY and CSC members.

Remember: Kishau: Tons River; dam: 232.6 m; hydropower: 1,476 million units; irrigation: 97,076 ha; RBD Act: 1 October 2026.

International Affairs: India–Vietnam, MERCOSUR, Space Cooperation and West Asia

InternationalHigh importance13–19 September 2026

Key Facts & Highlights

  • India and Vietnam agreed to observe 2027 as the Year of India–Vietnam Friendship, marking 55 years of diplomatic relations, with a bilateral trade target of US$25 billion by 2030 and cooperation in defence, trade, supply chains, connectivity, space, nuclear energy and culture.
  • India and MERCOSUR signed the First Additional Protocol to their Preferential Trade Agreement to recognise electronic Certificates of Origin and modernise customs and trade procedures. Authorised electronic signatures give e-CoO the legal validity of paper certificates.
  • The first International Space Summit 2026 was held in Paris on 9–10 September with representatives from about 90 countries. It focused on space cooperation, the space economy, science and exploration, space security and sustainability; 51 announcements were valued at about €20 billion, and India–France agreements covered launches, surveillance and microsatellites.
  • The Indian and Malaysian navies held the fourth edition of Exercise Samudra Laksamana at Lumut, Malaysia, with harbour and sea phases including cross-deck visits, subject-matter-expert exchanges, professional discussions and joint maritime training.
  • US President Donald Trump signed the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 on 18 September. It expands sanctions and authorises tariffs on countries buying Russian oil or gas, making it relevant to India’s energy imports.
  • A UN fact-finding mission stated on 18 September that it found reasonable grounds for possible international humanitarian-law violations connected with US attacks on a school and sports centre in Iran; the Geneva Conventions of 1949 are central to civilian protection and treatment of prisoners in armed conflict.
  • India summoned Pakistan’s Charge d’Affaires after a collision between a Pakistani vessel and an Indian Navy vessel on 15 September, referring to the India–Pakistan agreement of 1991.

International developments covered India’s partnerships with Vietnam and MERCOSUR, space diplomacy, maritime cooperation, sanctions, humanitarian law and India–Pakistan maritime relations.

Exam angle: Focus on the 2027 friendship year, US$25 billion trade target, e-CoO, Paris Space Summit, Samudra Laksamana, sanctions legislation and the Geneva Conventions.

Environment and Ecology: Himalayan Monal, Black Carbon and New Species

EnvironmentHigh importance13–19 September 2026

Key Facts & Highlights

  • The Himalayan Monal is Uttarakhand’s state bird. Researchers studied climate-change effects on its habitat and observed changes in calls associated with tourism and traffic noise; its IUCN status is Least Concern.
  • The A Resilient Himalaya report highlighted black carbon’s contribution to Himalayan glacier mass loss. South Asian black carbon was associated with about one-third of glacier mass-loss influence; brick kilns are major anthropogenic contributors, and black carbon is a short-lived climate pollutant with strong warming impact.
  • Researchers from the Wildlife Institute of India and Uluberia Botanical Institute Herbarium recorded Ophiopogon japonicus in India for the first time in West Kameng, Arunachal Pradesh, an Eastern Himalayan botanical discovery.
  • Two new frog species were reported from Mizoram: Ingerana aizawl from the Hratdawng Stream area of Aizawl and Duttaphrynus phawngpui from Phawngpui National Park. The research appeared in Zootaxa.
  • Dryland Congress 2026 was held in New Delhi under the theme “Transforming Dryland Agriculture with South–South Cooperation”. The Delhi Declaration on Drylands (3D) was adopted; ICAR and ICRISAT organised the event, marking 50 years of their partnership.

Environmental coverage linked species conservation, climate pollutants, Himalayan ecology, new biodiversity records and dryland agriculture cooperation.

Exam angle: Remember Himalayan Monal status and state, black-carbon sources, West Kameng, both frog species, Zootaxa, 3D Declaration and ICAR–ICRISAT.
