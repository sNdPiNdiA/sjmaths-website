National Affairs: PM-JAY, Make in India, UDAN and Governance Updates

NationalHigh importance20–26 September 2026

Key Facts & Highlights

  • Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY completed eight years on 23 September 2026. More than 60 crore beneficiaries have been covered; the scheme was launched at Ranchi on 23 September 2018. Arogya Manthan 2026 launched the National Healthcare Providers Registry Mobile App, Secure Data Environment and Auto-Adjudication Framework, while ABDM completed five years.
  • Make in India completed 12 years on 25 September. Launched in 2014, it now covers manufacturing, electronics, automobiles, pharmaceuticals, defence, steel, solar manufacturing and startups, with policy emphasis moving from assembly to domestic value chains, design and innovation.
  • The National Single Window System provides investors with a single digital interface for approvals and clearances across departments and governments, reducing the need to approach multiple agencies.
  • The next phase of UDAN was launched with MoUs involving 22 States and UTs. A Challenge Mode Portal for aerodrome development allows nominations of airstrips and helipads, after which airlines bid for routes.
  • The President amended the Inter-State Council Order, 1990 under Article 263. During Article 356 President’s Rule, a State Governor may be invited to Inter-State Council meetings; for Delhi, the Lieutenant Governor may be invited when Article 239AB applies.
  • DILRMP integrates land records, cadastral maps, registration and GIS through unique land identification; the 14-digit ULPIN or Bhu-Aadhaar remains important for Prelims.
  • The National Steering Committee approved a ₹320 crore Strategic Investment Plan for the Kurukshetra ITI Cluster under PM-SETU. It integrates seven government ITIs, with Jindal Naveen Avsar Limited as anchor partner; cumulative PM-SETU investment reached about ₹2,491 crore across 10 clusters.
  • The 100 Weeks Nasha Mukt Yuva Abhiyan was launched through MY Bharat and NSS. The Rashtriya Sankalp Samaroh began on 18 September, mobilising over four lakh participants across more than 1,000 locations under Prevent, Protect, Recover and Lead.
  • Swachh Seva Aankalan was launched as an annual digital self-assessment framework for rural sanitation under SBM-Grameen 2.0. Gram Panchayats assess ODF sustainability, visual cleanliness, waste management and sanitation assets, with Gram Sabha validation.
  • A Joint Parliamentary Committee began examining the Foreign Contribution Regulation Amendment Bill, 2026, covering NGO regulation, internal security, transparency and civil-society financing.
  • The 72nd National Film Awards were presented during the week; veteran Kannada actor Anant Nag received the Dadasaheb Phalke Award for 2024, India’s highest cinema honour.

The week covered health and digital-health milestones, manufacturing policy, investor clearances, regional aviation, federal institutions, land records, skilling, de-addiction, sanitation, FCRA oversight and cinema honours.

Exam angle: Remember PM-JAY dates and coverage, Make in India, Articles 263, 356 and 239AB, UDAN’s 22 MoUs, Kurukshetra PM-SETU, the four Nasha Mukt pillars and Swachh Seva Aankalan.

Remember: PM-JAY: 60+ crore beneficiaries; PM-SETU Kurukshetra: ₹320 crore; ULPIN: 14 digits; National Film Awards: Anant Nag.

Science and Technology: Quantum Cryogenics, NETRA, AI and New Materials

Science & TechnologyHigh importance20–26 September 2026

Key Facts & Highlights

  • DRDO signed its first high-value deep-tech Technology Development Fund agreement with Zero mK India to develop an indigenous 20 millikelvin dilution refrigerator, approximately 0.02 Kelvin, for quantum-computing hardware and reduced dependence on imported infrastructure.
  • ISRO and ESA extended their cooperation agreement until 8 January 2032, expanding work in human and robotic exploration, space weather, sustainable outer-space activities, Earth observation, space science and navigation.
  • Assam laid the foundation for ISRO’s ISTRAC Advanced Radar Research Facility at Chandrapur, Kamrup Metropolitan, under NETRA. The facility will improve Space Situational Awareness; Assam provided about 70 acres and the first radar is expected around February 2027.
  • The Technology Development Board agreed to provide GalaxEye about ₹63.84 crore for next-generation multisensor satellite technology for Earth observation and remote sensing.
  • The Technology Development Board provided about ₹285 crore RDI support to Ubifly Technologies for next-generation air-mobility technologies and indigenous advanced aviation systems.
  • Raman Research Institute researchers measured a quantum measure of about 1.17, close to a predicted 1.18. It relates to Quantum Measure Theory and does not mean photon-detection probability exceeds one; the effect arises from quantum interference.
  • IIEST Shibpur and TCS signed an MoU for an AI Centre of Excellence focused on NLP, computer vision, image processing, data analytics, AI research and startup innovation.
  • The government highlighted commercial bio-based biodegradable plastic under the BioE3 initiative to reduce dependence on petroleum-based plastics; food-safety regulation, testing, traceability and consumer protection also remained important themes.

Science and technology developments linked quantum hardware, space cooperation and radar-based SSA with multisensor satellites, air mobility, AI and sustainable materials.

Environment and Ecology: Circular Economy, Soil Carbon and Great Nicobar Minerals

EnvironmentHigh importance20–26 September 2026

Key Facts & Highlights

  • World Circular Economy Forum 2026 was held at Gandhinagar, Gujarat, making India the first South Asian host. Its theme was “Circular Economy: Transition for People and Prosperity”, with resource efficiency, waste reduction, recycling, reuse and Extended Producer Responsibility as focus areas.
  • India initiated its first farm-level soil-carbon payments in Punjab. About 2,550 smallholder farmers from Punjab and Haryana were to receive more than ₹2.9 crore for Direct Seeded Rice, reduced tillage and crop-residue management linked to verified carbon credits.
  • Begonia neelamorum, a new flowering plant, was discovered in Keyi Panyor district, Arunachal Pradesh, around 1,000 metres elevation. It was provisionally classified Data Deficient under IUCN criteria and named after the Neelam clan of the Nyishi community.
  • Researchers documented 3,261 flowering plant species in the Agasthyamalai Biosphere Reserve, reinforcing the Western Ghats’ status as a biodiversity hotspot.
  • The Ministry of Mines notified two offshore mineral blocks near Great Nicobar: West Sewell Ridge-01 of 1,000 sq km and Sewell Rise-01 of 632 sq km, together 1,632 sq km. Target minerals include polymetallic nodules and crusts containing nickel, cobalt, manganese, copper and rare-earth elements, raising blue-economy, biodiversity, tribal-rights and marine-conservation questions.
  • BRIC-NIPGR’s SPROUT facility was highlighted for climate-resilient crop research and varieties better suited to climate stresses.

Environmental coverage connected circular economy, verified farm carbon credits, biodiversity discoveries, Western Ghats conservation, deep-ocean minerals and climate-resilient crops.
