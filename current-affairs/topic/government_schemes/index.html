The Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA)

Context: The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare commenced nationwide celebrations to mark the 10th anniversary of the Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA) under the theme 10 Years of PMSMA – A Decade of Care. About The Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA): Launched In: The scheme was officially launched on June 9, 2016, by Prime Minister Narendra Modi. Ministry Involved: The program is designed, financed, and executed by the Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (MoHFW), Government of India. Aim: PMSMA seeks to reduce preventable maternal and infant deaths through early identification and management of high-risk pregnancies, ensuring every pregnant woman receives at least one specialist antenatal check-up in the second or third trimester. 9th of the Month Rule: On the 9th day of every month, all designated government health facilities conduct special PMSMA sessions, ensuring predictable and universal access to antenatal care for pregnant women.

Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyaan: A Decade of Inclusive Maternal Healthcare

GS Paper II – Social Justice (Health) | GS Paper I – Society (Women) Maternal Health; Antenatal Care; High-Risk Pregnancy; Institutional Deliveries; SDG 3 Launched in 2016, the Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyaan (PMSMA) provides free antenatal care and early risk detection for pregnant women. Over the past decade, it has served more than 7.5 crore women and contributed to improved maternal health outcomes, reflected in the decline of India’s Maternal Mortality Ratio from 130 to 87 per lakh live births. Complementary Maternal Health Initiatives Tenth Anniversary Commemoration (June 2026) Over the past decade, PMSMA has strengthened maternal healthcare through free antenatal services, early identification of high-risk pregnancies, and improved follow-up care. Supported by complementary maternal and nutrition schemes, it has contributed to a significant decline in maternal mortality, advancing the goal of safe motherhood and inclusive healthcare. https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2270486®=3&lang=1

India’s Free Trade Agreement Paradox

This editorial is based on “Four challenges that demand attention in India’s FTAs” published in The Indian Express on 08/06/2026. The article brings into focus India's rapidly expanding FTA network, which is set to cover 69 countries and nearly 75% of India's exports, underscoring its growing role in trade policy. For Prelims: Free Trade Agreement (FTA), Trade Deficit, India–EU FTA, UNCTAD, China Plus One, Rules of Origin (RoO), Most-Favored-Nation (MFN), Inverted Duty Structure, EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, MSMEs, Production-Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme, Mutual Recognition Agreements (MRAs), Export Promotion Councils (EPCs). For Mains: Key facts regarding FTAs and reasons behind India moving towards FTAs, Key issues associated with India's FTAs and measures needed for best utilisation of FTAs. As India aggressively expands its Free Trade Agreement (FTA) network, now covering 15 agreements with 27 countries, it faces critical structural challenges that threaten to undermine domestic manufacturing and widen trade deficits. Failure to harmonize input duties with FTA commitments risks weakening domestic value addition and industrial capacity, potentially turning FTAs into a gateway for increased imports rather than export-led growth.

Sustainable Agriculture in India

This editorial is based on “Right time to focus on sustainable agriculture” published in The Hindu Business Line on 12/06/2026. The article emphasizes that India must transition to a climate-resilient, sustainable agricultural framework. It highlights the urgent need to realign support policies, scale R&D funding, and incentivize ecosystem services to secure food stability amidst climate change. For Prelims: Sustainable Agriculture, GMO Seeds, Crop Rotation, Carbon Sink, Drip Irrigation, Dead Zone, Food Chain, Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana, Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchai Yojana, Soil Health Card Scheme, NAFCC, NABARD, National Agriculture Market (e-NAM), Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs), Carbon Sequestration, Participatory Guarantee System, PM-KUSUM, Seed Bank. For Mains: Key Facts Regarding Sustainable Agriculture, Key Steps Taken to Promote its Adoption in India, Persisting Issues in Adoption of Sustainable Agriculture in India. To address the multifaceted challenges posed by climate change and resource-intensive practices, India must urgently transition toward a resilient agricultural framework that prioritizes sustainability and productivity. There is an urgent need to scale up public and private (CSR-like) funding for R&D and improve technology transfer from "lab to fields." Furthermore, the integration of AI-based weather forecasting models for real-time crop advisory is essential.
