India’s Free Trade Agreement Paradox

This editorial is based on “Four challenges that demand attention in India’s FTAs” published in The Indian Express on 08/06/2026. The article brings into focus India's rapidly expanding FTA network, which is set to cover 69 countries and nearly 75% of India's exports, underscoring its growing role in trade policy. For Prelims: Free Trade Agreement (FTA), Trade Deficit, India–EU FTA, UNCTAD, China Plus One, Rules of Origin (RoO), Most-Favored-Nation (MFN), Inverted Duty Structure, EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, MSMEs, Production-Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme, Mutual Recognition Agreements (MRAs), Export Promotion Councils (EPCs). For Mains: Key facts regarding FTAs and reasons behind India moving towards FTAs, Key issues associated with India's FTAs and measures needed for best utilisation of FTAs. As India aggressively expands its Free Trade Agreement (FTA) network, now covering 15 agreements with 27 countries, it faces critical structural challenges that threaten to undermine domestic manufacturing and widen trade deficits. Failure to harmonize input duties with FTA commitments risks weakening domestic value addition and industrial capacity, potentially turning FTAs into a gateway for increased imports rather than export-led growth.

R&D Underspending in India: Systemic and Cultural Causes

GS Paper III – Economy (Industrial Policy; Innovation) | GS Paper III – Science & Technology Research & Development (R&D) Investment; Innovation Ecosystem; Private Sector Behaviour Indian businesses underinvest in R&D due to a combination of structural and historical factors. Limited manufacturing depth, financialisation, and historically commerce-oriented business practices have reduced incentives for innovation. As a result, India’s R&D intensity remains below the level needed to support its long-term economic and technological ambitions. The Long Shadow of Colonial Deindustrialisation Empirical Evidence from Developed Markets (Lazonick, HBR 2014 – US S&P 500 companies, 2003-2012): Public vs. Private Companies (Asker, Farre-Mensa, Ljungqvist, 2015): Democracy, Uncertainty, and the Discount Rate Indian firms underinvest in R&D due to interacting structural and institutional factors. Protected domestic markets, colonial-era commercial orientation, and premature financialisation have weakened manufacturing depth and innovation incentives. Global evidence shows similar trends, with firms prioritising buybacks and dividends over long-term R&D due to short-term market pressures and high discount rates. In India, these forces collectively result in rational but insufficient investment in long-horizon technological capabilities critical for growth. https://indianexpress.com/article/opinion/columns/rd-underspending-in-india-has-no-one-cause-its-systemic-as-well-as-cultural-10735416/
