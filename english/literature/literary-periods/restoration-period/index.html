Restoration Period: Comprehensive Literary & Grammatical Analysis

High-yield conceptual breakdown and foundational study notes of Restoration Period tailored for competitive exam mastery.

UP PGT English UP TGT English B.A. / M.A. Level

The Restoration Period (1660–1700) marks the reestablishment of the Stuart monarchy under Charles II following the interregnum of Oliver Cromwell. This era witnessed a profound cultural shift from Puritan austerity to neoclassical refinement, wit, and social satire. Literature became heavily urban, court-centric, and polite, heavily influenced by French classical models observed during the exiled court's stay in France. The period saw the reopening of theaters, giving birth to Restoration comedy of manners and heroic tragedy, alongside the rise of empirical philosophy, journalism, and modern English prose. For aspirants of UP TGT/PGT and UGC-NET, this period is a high-yield zone characterized by foundational figures such as John Dryden, William Congreve, William Wycherley, Aphra Behn, and John Bunyan. Examiners frequently target questions on the reopening of theatres (1660), Dryden's critical prefaces and heroic couplets, the witty repartees of Comedy of Manners, and the political allegories embedded in satire like Dryden's 'Absalom and Achitophel'.

1. Historical Matrix and Cultural Transition

Shift from Puritan Commonwealth to Royalist Restoration and Neoclassicism.

  • Monarchy restored in 1660 with the return of Charles II from French exile.
  • Rejection of Puritan zeal in favor of reason, moderation, order, and decorum.
  • Establishment of the Royal Society in 1662, fostering scientific empiricism and plain prose style.
  • Dominance of Neoclassicism: emulation of ancient Greek and Roman writers mediated through French critics like Boileau and Corneille.
Comparative Analysis: Unlike the boundless imagination and metaphysical complexity of the Jacobean period, the Restoration prioritized wit, urbanity, formal regularity, and social satire.
  • Key Dates: 1660 (Restoration), 1662 (Royal Society founded), 1688 (Glorious Revolution).
  • Core Aesthetic: Reason, order, wit, and classical restraint.

2. Restoration Drama: Comedy of Manners and Heroic Tragedy

The theatrical renaissance following the 1642 Puritan ban on public playhouses.

  • Theatres reopened in 1660; introduction of actresses on the English stage (e.g., Nell Gwyn).
  • Comedy of Manners (Wycherley, Congreve, Etherege): focused on the artificiality, sexual intrigue, and witty repartee of high London society.
  • Heroic Tragedy (Dryden): written in heroic couplets, dealing with epic themes of love, honor, and imperial power.
  • William Wycherley's 'The Country Wife' and William Congreve's 'The Way of the World' as peak comedic achievements.
Comparative Analysis: Contrasts sharply with Elizabethan drama by shifting focus from cosmic, tragic heroes to witty, cynical aristocrats navigating complex social codes of urban life.
  • First professional female playwright: Aphra Behn ('Oroonoko', 'The Rover').
  • Jeremy Collier's 1698 tract 'A Short View of the Immorality and Profaneness of the English Stage' marked the decline of Restoration comedy.

3. Poetry and Prose: Satire, Allegory, and Empirical Realism

The triumph of the heroic couplet and the development of journalistic, plain prose.

  • John Dryden established as the literary dictator ('Father of English Criticism') and master of neoclassical satire.
  • Dryden's 'Absalom and Achitophel' (1681): political allegory attacking the Earl of Shaftesbury.
  • John Bunyan's 'The Pilgrim's Progress' (1678): religious allegory written in vernacular, plain prose.
  • John Locke's 'An Essay Concerning Human Understanding' (1690) revolutionized epistemology and philosophy.
Comparative Analysis: Dryden's mastery of the heroic couplet set the standard for 18th-century verse, later perfected by Alexander Pope.
  • Dryden coined the term 'metaphysical poets' in a pejorative sense regarding Donne.
  • Bunyan's prose style represents the bridge between Puritan spiritual autobiography and the modern English novel.

Literary Periods & Regional Context

🌐 Global/Historical Context

The Restoration mirrors European Classicism and the Age of Enlightenment (French Age of Louis XIV), prioritizing rationalism, academies, and strict literary rules over Elizabethan romanticism.

🇮🇳 Indian Literature in English

Directly precedes the British colonial consolidation in India; however, literary works of this period rarely reference India, though travel writing and trade corporation journals began noting Eastern geographies.

📐 Key Literary Devices & Grammar Rules
The Heroic Couplet
aa bb cc (Rhyming iambic pentameters)
Parameters: Closed structure, often featuring balanced caesura and antithesis, used extensively in Dryden's epic verse and satire.
Exam Focus: Frequently tested in UP PGT/UGC-NET regarding versification shifts from blank verse to closed couplets.
Comedy of Manners Plot Structure
Intrigue + Wit Combat + Exposed Hypocrisy + Resolution of Estates
Parameters: Stock character types (fop, rake, witty heroine, jealous husband) revolving around marriage plots and financial settlements.
Exam Focus: Essential for matching authors to characters in UGC-NET matching-type questions.

Mnemonics, Memory Shortcuts & Exam Traps

💡 Major Restoration Playwrights
W.W.C.E.B. (Wife Wants Congreve's Etherege Beauties)

W = Wycherley, W = Congreve (William), E = Etherege, B = Behn. Represents the big four dramatists of the Restoration.

⚠️ Exam Trap: Do not confuse Restoration playwrights with Jacobean dramatists like Webster or Ford; remember Restoration plays emphasize social satire and wit over bloody revenge.
💡 Dryden's Major Works
A.M.A.D. (Absalom, Mac Flecknoe, All for Love, Dryden)

A = Absalom and Achitophel, M = Mac Flecknoe, A = All for Love, D = Dryden.

⚠️ Exam Trap: Remember 'All for Love' is Dryden's neoclassical rewriting of Shakespeare's 'Antony and Cleopatra' strictly following unities.

📚 High-Yield Literary & Grammatical Terminology

Comedy of Manners UP PGT / UGC-NET

A form of drama that satirizes the manners and affectations of contemporary society and questions societal standards, popularized by playwrights like William Congreve and George Etherege.

Heroic Couplet UP TGT

A traditional poetic form consisting of a rhyming pair of lines in iambic pentameter, which became the dominant verse form of the Restoration, perfected by John Dryden and later Alexander Pope.

The Royal Society UGC-NET

Founded in 1660, it advocated for empirical science and a plain, unadorned style of prose writing, significantly influencing the development of English journalism and literature.

Patents of 1662 UP PGT

Royal decrees issued by Charles II that granted exclusive rights to Thomas Killigrew and Sir William Davenant to form two theatrical companies, effectively reopening the London public theatres.

Breeches Role UP PGT

A theatrical part in which an actress appeared in male costume, such as tight-fitting trousers or breeches, which became a prominent feature of Restoration comedy.

Restoration Comedy UP TGT / PGT

English comedies written and performed in the Restoration period characterized by sexual cynicism, witty dialogue, and intricate plots involving intrigue and illicit affairs.

Tory Satire UGC-NET

Political and social satire aligned with the conservative Tory faction, which defended the monarchy, the Church of England, and traditional social hierarchies.

Whig Historiography UGC-NET

An approach to historiography that presents the past as an inevitable progression toward liberty and enlightenment, reflecting the rise of Whig political power post-1688.

Sentimental Comedy UP PGT

An 18th-century dramatic genre that emerged in reaction to the perceived immorality of Restoration comedy, emphasizing middle-class virtue and eliciting tears rather than laughter.

Mock-Heroic / Mock-Epic UP TGT

A genre of poetry that uses the lofty style and conventions of epic poetry to deal with trivial or mundane subjects, creating satirical contrast.

The Glorious Revolution UGC-NET

The overthrow of King James II in 1688 by a union of English Parliamentarians with the Dutch stadtholder William of Orange, establishing a constitutional monarchy.

Coffeehouse Culture UP PGT

A social phenomenon in Restoration and 18th-century London where intellectuals, politicians, and writers gathered to discuss literature, philosophy, and politics.

Aestheticism UGC-NET

A late 19th-century movement, but its critical roots in debates over art for art's sake trace back to neoclassical discussions of form versus content initiated in the Restoration.

🧭 Prescribed Authors, Critics & Rules

John Dryden (1668 ('Of Dramatic Poesy'))

Formulated early English literary criticism through a dialogue defending neoclassical unities, rhyme in drama, and the adaptation of French models while championing native Elizabethan vigor.

Thomas Hobbes (1651 ('Leviathan'))

Argued that human life in a state of nature is 'solitary, poor, nasty, brutish, and short,' necessitating an absolute sovereign to maintain social order and prevent civil war.

John Locke (1689 ('Two Treatises of Government'))

Proposed the theory of the social contract based on natural rights (life, liberty, property) and argued that government derives its legitimacy from the consent of the governed.

Jeremy Collier (1698 ('A Short View of the Immorality and Profaneness of the English Stage'))

Launched a fierce moral attack on Restoration drama, accusing playwrights like Congreve and Vanbrugh of blasphemy and obscenity, which catalyzed the shift toward sentimentalism.

Question 1 of 18
Which year marks the official beginning of the Restoration period in English literature?

Rationale: The Restoration period began in 1660 with the restoration of the monarchy under King Charles II, following the Interregnum.

Question 2 of 18
Who wrote the landmark critical essay 'Of Dramatic Poesy' (1668)?

Rationale: John Dryden wrote 'Of Dramatic Poesy', a dialogue that defends English drama against French neoclassical strictures.

Question 3 of 18
Which of the following plays was written by William Congreve?

Rationale: 'The Way of the World' (1700) is Congreve's masterpiece and one of the finest examples of Restoration Comedy of Manners.

Question 4 of 18
The Licensing Act of 1737 was enacted primarily to control and censor what form of literature?

Rationale: The Stage Licensing Act of 1737 was introduced by Robert Walpole to censor political satire in theatrical performances.

Question 5 of 18
Which poetic form became the dominant vehicle for satire and verse during the Restoration and Augustan periods?

Rationale: The heroic couplet, characterized by rhymed iambic pentameter lines, was the preferred verse form for serious poetry and satire.

Question 6 of 18
What historical event in 1688 ended the reign of James II and established a constitutional monarchy?

Rationale: The Glorious Revolution of 1688 bloodlessly deposed James II and brought William and Mary to the throne.

Question 7 of 18
Who wrote 'Leviathan' (1651), providing a philosophical justification for absolute sovereignty?

Rationale: Thomas Hobbes authored 'Leviathan', arguing for a strong central authority to avoid the chaos of the state of nature.

Question 8 of 18
Which female playwright of the Restoration is famous for writing 'The Rover'?

Rationale: Aphra Behn was a pioneer female professional writer, best known for her play 'The Rover' (1677) and the prose fiction 'Oronooko'.

Question 9 of 18
Jeremy Collier's 1698 pamphlet attacked English drama primarily on what grounds?

Rationale: Collier's 'A Short View' attacked the stage for impiety, indecency, and undermining public morals.

Question 10 of 18
Which of the following was NOT a prominent feature of Restoration Comedy?

Rationale: Restoration comedies were set in contemporary urban high society (primarily London), not in idealized pastoral settings.

Question 11 of 18
Who is considered the author of 'Absalom and Achitophel', a masterwork of political satire?

Rationale: John Dryden used biblical allegory in 'Absalom and Achitophel' (1681) to satirize the Earl of Shaftesbury and the Exclusion Crisis.

Question 12 of 18
The re-opening of the theatres in England occurred in which year?

Rationale: The theatres, closed by the Puritans in 1642, were officially reopened in 1660 following the restoration of Charles II.

Question 13 of 18
What is the primary subject of Samuel Butler's 'Hudibras'?

Rationale: Samuel Butler's 'Hudibras' is a long satirical mock-heroic poem ridiculing Puritans and Presbyterian zealotry.

Question 14 of 18
In what year was the Royal Society of London for Improving Natural Knowledge founded?

Rationale: The Royal Society was founded in 1660 and received its royal charter in 1662, championing empirical science and clear prose.

Question 15 of 18
Which prose work by John Bunyan is an allegory of Christian salvation written during the late 17th century?

Rationale: John Bunyan's 'The Pilgrim's Progress' (1678) is a foundational Christian allegory of spiritual pilgrimage.

Question 16 of 18
Which Restoration dramatist wrote 'The Country Wife', known for its famous 'china closet' scene?

Rationale: William Wycherley wrote 'The Country Wife' (1675), a biting satire on London marital hypocrisy and urban sophistication.

Question 17 of 18
What term refers to a play in which women actors first appeared on the English public stage?

Rationale: The Restoration era marked the official introduction of professional actresses on the English stage, replacing boy actors.

Question 18 of 18
Which monarch's reign immediately followed the Restoration period, ushering in the Augustan Age?

Rationale: Queen Anne's reign (1702–1714) bridges the late Restoration and the height of the Augustan Age.

The term 'Restoration' in English literary history refers to the restoration of which monarch to the English throne?

Official Benchmark Explanation: The Restoration refers to the return of Charles II in 1660 from exile in France, restoring the Stuart monarchy after the Commonwealth.

UGC-NET English
Which of Dryden's poems is a satire directed against Thomas Shadwell and the Mac Flecknoe lineage?

Official Benchmark Explanation: 'Mac Flecknoe' (1682) is a personal mock-heroic satire by John Dryden targeting rival playwright Thomas Shadwell.

UP PGT English
Which 17th-century philosophical text argues that the mind is a 'tabula rasa' at birth, heavily influencing Enlightenment thought?

Official Benchmark Explanation: John Locke's 'An Essay Concerning Human Understanding' (1690) famously posited the 'tabula rasa' (blank slate) theory of human knowledge.

Question 1 of 10
In John Dryden's 'Of Dramatic Poesy', the character Neander represents the views of whom?

Solution: Neander (meaning 'new man') acts as Dryden's own mouthpiece in the dialogue, advocating for modern English drama and the use of rhyme.

Question 2 of 10
Which Restoration sub-genre of drama dealt primarily with grand heroic conflicts between love and duty, often written in heroic couplets?

Solution: Heroic Tragedy, pioneered by Dryden and others, focused on epic themes of love and honor in formal heroic couplets.

Question 3 of 10
What political crisis did John Dryden's 'Absalom and Achitophel' allegorize?

Solution: The poem allegorizes the Exclusion Crisis (1679–1681), where the Earl of Shaftesbury attempted to exclude the Catholic James II from the throne.

Question 4 of 10
Which Restoration writer is best known for the philosophical diary chronicling the Great Plague and the Great Fire of London?

Solution: Samuel Pepys kept an extraordinary personal diary from 1660 to 1669, providing vivid eyewitness accounts of major Restoration events.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following plays is an example of Restoration 'She-Tragedy', a genre focusing on the sufferings of virtuous heroines?

Solution: Thomas Otway's 'The Orphan' (1680) and 'Venice Preserv'd' are prime examples of pathetic tragedy or 'she-tragedy'.

Question 6 of 10
The closure of the English public theatres by the Puritan Parliament occurred in what year?

Solution: The Long Parliament issued an ordinance closing all public theatres in September 1642, initiating an 18-year theatrical ban.

Question 7 of 10
Which critic and dramatist wrote 'The Rehearsal' (1671), a play designed to burlesque the bombastic conventions of heroic drama?

Solution: George Villiers, the Duke of Buckingham, penned 'The Rehearsal', satirizing the absurdities of Dryden and heroic tragedy.

Question 8 of 10
What was the main stylistic recommendation of the Royal Society regarding English prose?

Solution: The Royal Society urged members to use a plain style, favoring mathematical clarity over metaphorical embellishment.

Question 9 of 10
Which work by William Congreve contains the memorable wit of characters like Mirabell and Millamant?

Solution: 'The Way of the World' (1700) features the legendary verbal sparring between Mirabell and Millamant regarding their prenuptial agreement.

Question 10 of 10
In the context of Restoration theater, what innovative architectural feature became prominent on the proscenium stage?

Solution: Restoration theaters introduced indoor proscenium arches, deep aprons, and perspective-painted scenery shifted via sliding wings.
