Longinus: Comprehensive Literary & Grammatical Analysis

High-yield conceptual breakdown and foundational study notes of Longinus tailored for competitive exam mastery.

UP PGT English UP TGT English B.A. / M.A. Level

Longinus, traditionally credited as the author of the monumental ancient treatise 'Peri Hpsous' (On the Sublime), stands as one of the most influential classical critics in Western literature alongside Aristotle and Horace. Though the precise identity of the author remains a matter of historical debate—often referred to as 'Pseudo-Longinus' due to scholarly consensus pointing to an unknown writer of the 1st century AD rather than Cassius Longinus of the 3rd century—his work marks a pivotal shift from strict rhetorical rules to the psychological and emotional transport of the reader. For aspirants of UP TGT/PGT English and UGC-NET, Longinus is quintessential for understanding classical aesthetics, the genesis of comparative literary criticism, and the historical elevation of style above mere mechanical correctness. The treatise itself is celebrated for its democratization of literary greatness, asserting that true sublimity arises from noble thoughts, grand passions, and majestic diction rather than elaborate ornamentation. Longinus categorizes the sources of the sublime into five distinct fountains, placing structural emphasis on the interaction between innate genius and acquired art. His critical approach bridges classical rhetoric and Romantic sensibility, heavily influencing subsequent theorists like Boileau, John Dryden, and Edmund Burke. Examination questions frequently probe his five sources, his critique of bathos (or 'tumidity'), and his famous comparative analysis of Homer and Sappho.

1. Authorship, Date, and Textual History

Historical identity debate surrounding 'Pseudo-Longinus' and the survival of the treatise.

  • The text is traditionally attributed to Cassius Longinus, a 3rd-century rhetorician and philosopher.
  • Modern scholarship designates the author as 'Pseudo-Longinus' due to linguistic and historical clues placing the text in the 1st century AD (approx. 1st century AD under the Roman Empire).
  • The manuscript was first discovered and published by Robortello in Basel in 1554 as 'Peri Hpsous'.
  • Nicolas Boileau's 1674 French translation ('Traité du Sublime') popularized the text across Europe and sparked the neoclassical debate on sublimity.
  • The surviving manuscript is famously mutilated, missing approximately one-third of its original text, including sections on public speaking and specific stylistic figures.
Comparative Analysis: Unlike Aristotle's systematic categorization of tragedy in the 'Poetics', Longinus focuses on the psychological impact of literature on the audience, prefiguring modern reader-response criticism and Romantic critical theory.
  • Author known as Pseudo-Longinus.
  • Dated to the 1st century AD.
  • First translated into English by John Hall (1652) and famously popularized by Boileau (1674).

2. The Five Sources of the Sublime

The foundational structural framework of literary greatness outlined in 'On the Sublime'.

  • 1. Grandeur of Thought (Magnanimity/Elevation of Mind): The capacity for noble conceptions and innate greatness of soul.
  • 2. Capacity for Strong and Inspired Emotion (Pathos): The powerful, passionate transport that stirs the reader's soul.
  • 3. Appropriate Use of Figures: Masterful deployment of rhetorical devices like asyndeton, hyperbaton, and periphrasis, provided they appear natural rather than artificial.
  • 4. Noble Diction: Choice of impressive, choice words and metaphors that give body and dignity to thought.
  • 5. Dignified and Elevated Composition (Word Arrangement): Harmonious arrangement and structural synthesis of words to produce rhythmic melody and grandeur.
Comparative Analysis: While Aristotle emphasizes plot and character action in drama, Longinus prioritizes elevated diction, emotional intensity, and grandeur of thought across poetry and prose alike.
  • The first two sources (thought and emotion) are largely innate.
  • The last three sources (figures, diction, composition) depend upon technical skill and artistic training.

3. Flaws in Style: The Anti-Sublime

Longinus's critical taxonomy of stylistic errors that destroy sublimity.

  • Tumidity (Turgidity): Striving for grandeur through inflated, bombastic expressions that lead to grandiloquent nonsense.
  • Puerility (Childishness): Academic pedantry characterized by cold, over-refined, and affected conceits that freeze rather than warm the audience.
  • Parenthesis (Ill-timed Passion): Displaying emotional intensity where no emotion is demanded by the context (false pathos).
  • The treatise warns against over-elaboration, equating excessive ornamentation with a lack of true artistic maturity.
  • True sublimity echoes a great soul, whereas false sublimity is merely superficial glitter.
Comparative Analysis: Longinus's critique of tumidity parallels modern stylistic warnings against purple prose and Victorian excess, mirroring Horace's strictures against literary disproportion.
  • Tumidity and puerility are the primary obstacles to achieving true sublimity.
  • Sublimity is an echo of a great soul.

4. Comparative Literary Analysis: Homer and Sappho

Empirical demonstration of sublimity through classical textual exegesis.

  • Longinus compares the Iliad (representing the ocean at its height, vast and sublime) with the Odyssey (representing the setting sun, calm yet grand).
  • He cites Sappho's Ode to Anactoria ('Phainetai moi kenos') as the supreme example of the sublime of emotion (pathos).
  • Sappho achieves sublimity by selecting the most striking physical symptoms of jealousy and love, fusing them into an organic whole.
  • He uses these examples to prove that sublimity is a universal aesthetic standard transcending mere mechanical rule-following.
Comparative Analysis: Longinus's text-based practical criticism anticipates the Close Reading methods of the 20th-century New Critics.
  • Iliad linked to stormy grandeur; Odyssey linked to narrative ebb.
  • Sappho's ode is celebrated as the pinnacle of emotional sublimity.

Literary Periods & Regional Context

🌐 Global/Historical Context

Classical antiquity (Greco-Roman literary criticism), profoundly revived during the 17th-century French Neoclassical period via Boileau, and deeply influential on 18th-century English Romanticism and Edmund Burke's theories of the sublime.

🇮🇳 Indian Literature in English

Longinus's concepts show remarkable convergence with Sanskrit Poetics, specifically the 'Dhvani' (suggestion) theory propounded by Anandavardhana and the 'Rasa' (aesthetic flavor) theory of Bharata Muni, where emotional resonance supersedes explicit statement.

📐 Key Literary Devices & Grammar Rules
The Five Sources of the Sublime (Longinian Formula)
Sublimity = Innate Genius (Thought + Emotion) + Acquired Art (Figures + Diction + Composition)
Parameters: Balanced integration of natural grandeur and technical mastery; absence of tumidity or puerility.
Exam Focus: Frequently tested in multiple-choice questions regarding which sources are innate versus acquired.
The Test of True Sublimity
Universal Appeal + Permanent Repetition = True Sublimity
Parameters: If a work pleases all men at all times across different cultures and ages, it possesses genuine, unshakeable sublimity.
Exam Focus: Appears in UGC-NET questions concerning Longinus's criteria for universal aesthetic evaluation.

Mnemonics, Memory Shortcuts & Exam Traps

💡 Mnemonic for the Five Sources of the Sublime
TEF-DC

T - Thought (Grandeur of), E - Emotion (Strong/Inspired Pathos), F - Figures (Use of), D - Diction (Noble), C - Composition (Dignified Arrangement).

⚠️ Exam Trap: Examiners often trap students by listing 'Plot' or 'Character' as Longinian sources (these belong to Aristotle, not Longinus).
💡 Authorship Verification Trap
P-L = Pseudo-Longinus / 1st Century AD

Never attribute 'On the Sublime' definitively to Cassius Longinus of the 3rd century; always acknowledge the scholarly consensus of 'Pseudo-Longinus' from the 1st century AD.

⚠️ Exam Trap: UP PGT and UGC-NET often include 'Cassius Longinus (3rd Century)' as a distractor option against 'An Unknown Writer of the 1st Century'.

📚 High-Yield Literary & Grammatical Terminology

Sublime UGC-NET Core Concept

That which elevates human thought and speech above the ordinary, creating an ecstatic transport (ekstasis) in the audience.

Peri Hypsous UP PGT Fact

The original Greek title of Longinus's treatise, translated as 'On the Sublime'.

Ekstasis Critical Terminology

The psychological state of transport, rapture, or being carried out of oneself caused by true sublimity.

Puerile Diction Literary Flaw

A fault of style characterized by inflated, pompous, and cold language that fails to achieve true sublimity.

Parenthesis Rhetorical Device

A rhetorical figure discussed by Longinus involving the sudden interruption of thought to express intense emotion.

Asyndeton Stylistic Figure

The omission of conjunctions between clauses, creating a rapid and forceful effect praised by Longinus.

Hyperbaton Syntactic Device

A figure of speech involving inversion of normal word order to express agitated mental states.

Amplification Discourse Technique

A rhetorical strategy of accumulating all the details and arguments associated with a subject to give it massive weight.

Pseudo-Sublimity Critical Pitfall

False grandeur characterized by bombast, excessive ornament, and emotional over-expression (frigidity).

Bathos Comparative Term

An unintentional descent from the sublime to the ridiculous in writing or speech.

Canon of the Sublime High-Yield Framework

Longinus's formulation of the five sources of sublimity: grandeur of thought, capacity for strong emotion, figures of thought/speech, noble diction, and dignified composition.

Anonymus Textual History

The designation often given to the author of 'On the Sublime' due to scholarly debates surrounding its true authorship.

Dionysius Longinus Authorship Debate

The historical figure to whom Renaissance manuscripts attributed 'On the Sublime', though modern scholarship dates the work to the 1st century AD.

Boileau Reception History

The 17th-century French critic who translated 'On the Sublime' in 1674, sparking the modern European revival of Longinian criticism.

🧭 Prescribed Authors, Critics & Rules

Pseudo-Longinus (1st Century AD) (Classical Antiquity / Roman Imperial Period)

Sublimity is the echo of a great soul. It is the highest excellence in discourse, producing not persuasion, but ecstasy and transport in the hearer.

The Five Sources of Sublimity (Longinus) (c. 1st Century AD)

The first two sources (Grandeur of thought and Strong emotion) are inborn, while the remaining three (Noble figures, Noble diction, and Dignified composition) are art-dependent.

Nicolas Boileau-Despréaux (1674 (French Translation))

Popularized Longinus in Europe through his translation 'Traité du Sublime', shifting critical focus from Aristotelian rules to aesthetic emotion and genius.

John Dryden and Alexander Pope (Late 17th to 18th Century England)

Integrated Longinian aesthetics into English neoclassical criticism, balancing rule-bound art with the concept of unprompted genius and soaring imagination.

Interactive Concept Practice

Instant evaluation with detailed explanations for each question.

Question 1 of 19
What is the literal meaning of the Greek title 'Peri Hypsous'?

Rationale: 'Peri Hypsous' translates directly to 'On the Sublime' or 'On Elevation' in English.

Question 2 of 19
According to Longinus, how many sources of sublimity are there?

Rationale: Longinus outlines five sources of the sublime: grandeur of thought, strong emotion, appropriate use of figures, noble diction, and dignified composition.

Question 3 of 19
Which of the five sources of sublimity are considered inborn rather than acquired through art?

Rationale: Grandeur of thought and strong emotion are innate qualities of a great soul, whereas the other three depend on technical art.

Question 4 of 19
Who translated 'On the Sublime' into French in 1674, immensely reviving interest in Longinus?

Rationale: Boileau's 1674 translation 'Traité du Sublime' brought Longinus to the forefront of modern European literary criticism.

Question 5 of 19
What is the primary effect of the sublime on an audience, according to Longinus?

Rationale: Unlike rhetoric which persuades, the sublime produces 'ekstasis' (transport) and rapture in the listener.

Question 6 of 19
Which critic is famous for defining the sublime as 'the echo of a great soul'?

Rationale: Longinus famously stated that 'sublimity is the echo of a great soul' (megalophrosynes echema).

Question 7 of 19
What is the chief flaw opposed to sublimity that involves inflated and pretentious language?

Rationale: Frigidity or puerility is the vice of over-elaboration and bombast that results in false sublimity.

Question 8 of 19
In which century is 'On the Sublime' generally believed to have been written?

Rationale: Modern scholarship generally dates 'On the Sublime' to the 1st century AD, though its exact authorship remains disputed.

Question 9 of 19
Which of the following is NOT one of the vices of the sublime identified by Longinus?

Rationale: Parenthesis is a rhetorical figure, not a vice. The three vices listed by Longinus are frigidity, parenthyrson (ill-timed emotion), and amphibolos (childishness/puerility).

Question 10 of 19
Longinus compares true sublimity to what natural phenomenon?

Rationale: Longinus describes true sublimity as shattering everything like a thunderbolt or flash of lightning.

Question 11 of 19
How does Longinus's critical approach differ primarily from Aristotle's 'Poetics'?

Rationale: Longinus is concerned with the psychological impact and elevation of the soul, whereas Aristotle focuses on plot, structure, and catharsis.

Question 12 of 19
What role do rhetorical figures play in Longinus's theory?

Rationale: Figures reinforce sublimity, but only when they appear natural and are backed by noble thoughts and deep passion.

Question 13 of 19
Which English Romantic poet and critic was deeply influenced by Longinus's concept of overwhelming aesthetic experience?

Rationale: The Romantics, including Wordsworth and Coleridge, revived Longinian ideas of genius, imagination, and the sublime in nature and art.

Question 14 of 19
What does Longinus mean by 'dignified composition'?

Rationale: Dignified composition refers to word-order, rhythm, and structural harmony that blend elements into a unified, majestic whole.

Question 15 of 19
Why is the author of 'On the Sublime' often referred to as 'Pseudo-Longinus'?

Rationale: Scholars proved the treatise could not have been written by the 3rd-century rhetorician Cassius Longinus, hence the prefix 'Pseudo'.

Question 16 of 19
Which biblical quotation does Longinus famously cite as an example of sublime writing in Genesis?

Rationale: Longinus (in traditional accounts or scholarly commentary associated with him) praised the Jewish lawgiver's depiction of creation as an instance of true sublimity.

Question 17 of 19
Which rhetorical device involves posing a question not to elicit an answer, but to create dramatic intensity?

Rationale: Longinus notes that rhetorical questions simulate agitation and emotional immediacy.

Question 18 of 19
According to Longinus, what is the relationship between art and nature in achieving sublimity?

Rationale: Longinus views art and nature as interdependent: nature supplies the raw genius, while art provides method and discipline.

Question 19 of 19
What does the term 'Parenthyrson' signify in Longinus's critique?

Rationale: Parenthyrson is emotional display that is out of place, cold, or exaggerated where no emotion is required.

Question 1 of 10
In the context of Longinus's treatise, what distinguishes true sublimity from false grandiosity?

Solution: Longinus establishes a pragmatic test for the sublime: that which pleases all men at all times, standing the test of repeated examination.

Question 2 of 10
How does Longinus treat the use of rhetorical figures in achieving the sublime?

Solution: Longinus argues that figures are most effective when they appear natural, hiding their artistry behind intense emotion.

Question 3 of 10
Which literary period in England experienced the most intense revival of interest in Longinian aesthetics?

Solution: Following Boileau's translation, 18th-century English critics and poets like Dennis, Addison, Pope, and Burke intensely debated and expanded upon the sublime.

Question 4 of 10
What is meant by Longinus's concept of 'Amplification' (Auxesis)?

Solution: Amplification builds up a topic incrementally, piling point upon point to elevate the emotional intensity and grandeur.

Question 5 of 10
Why is the exact authorship of 'On the Sublime' a matter of ongoing academic debate?

Solution: Manuscript evidence points to 'Dionysius or Longinus', but linguistic and historical clues suggest an anonymous author writing in the 1st century AD.

Question 6 of 10
Which of Longinus's five sources of sublimity relates directly to vocabulary and word choice?

Solution: Noble diction encompasses the choice of striking, elevated, and appropriate words to convey elevated ideas.

Question 7 of 10
How does Longinus define the relationship between persuasion and sublimity?

Solution: Longinus differentiates the two: persuasion convinces the judgment, but the sublime exercises an irresistible dominion and ecstasy.

Question 8 of 10
What critical error does Longinus label as 'Puerility'?

Solution: Puerility is intellectual childisness—striving for grand effects with petty, over-ornamented, or trivial ideas.

Question 9 of 10
In what way did Longinus influence Edmund Burke's later philosophical treatise on the sublime ('A Philosophical Enquiry into the Origin of Our Ideas of the Sublime and Beautiful', 1757)?

Solution: While Longinus applied the sublime primarily to literature, Burke expanded it into aesthetics and psychology, linking it to terror, awe, and infinity.

Question 10 of 10
Which statement best captures Longinus's ultimate legacy in literary criticism?

Solution: Longinus is celebrated for introducing psychological criticism and championing genius and transport over rigid neoclassical rules.
