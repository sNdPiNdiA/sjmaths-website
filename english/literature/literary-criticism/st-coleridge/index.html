St Coleridge: Comprehensive Literary & Grammatical Analysis

High-yield conceptual breakdown and foundational study notes of Samuel Taylor Coleridge tailored for competitive exam mastery in UP TGT/PGT English and UGC-NET.

UP PGT English UP TGT English B.A. / M.A. Level

Samuel Taylor Coleridge (1772–1834) stands as a foundational titan of English Romanticism, operating at the crossroads of visionary poetry, transcendental philosophy, and pioneering literary criticism. Alongside William Wordsworth, he inaugurated the Romantic age with the publication of 'Lyrical Ballads' (1798), introducing a manifesto that revolutionized the thematic and linguistic scope of English verse. Beyond his iconic supernatural poems such as 'The Rime of the Ancient Mariner' and 'Kubla Khan', Coleridge is celebrated for establishing English practical and philosophical criticism through his monumental prose work, 'Biographia Literaria' (1817). In this text, he systematically dismantles eighteenth-century neoclassical poetics while formulating radical definitions of the poetic imagination, fancy, and the organic unity of art. For aspirants of the UP TGT/PGT and UGC-NET examinations, Coleridge represents a high-frequency testing zone. Examiners frequently probe his critical terminology (Primary Imagination, Secondary Imagination, Fancy, Willing Suspension of Disbelief), the specific provenance and publication history of 'Lyrical Ballads', and the philosophical underpinnings he borrowed from German idealist thinkers like Immanuel Kant and Friedrich Schelling. Mastery over his critical vocabulary, autobiographical milestones, and structural contributions to English verse form an indispensable prerequisite for competitive success.

1. Biographical Milestones and Major Poetic Works

The chronological trajectory and thematic maturation of Coleridge's poetic corpus from revolutionary zeal to transcendental introspection.

  • Born in Ottery St Mary, Devonshire; educated at Christ's Hospital where he formed a lifelong friendship with Charles Lamb.
  • Attended Jesus College, Cambridge; heavily influenced by radical politics and unitarian theology, planning a utopian community called 'Pantisocracy' with Robert Southey on the banks of the Susquehanna River.
  • Partnered with William Wordsworth to publish 'Lyrical Ballads' (1798), which opened with Coleridge's masterpiece 'The Rime of the Ancient Mariner'.
  • Authored 'Kubla Khan; or, A Vision in a Dream: A Fragment' (composed 1797, published 1816), famously interrupted by a 'person from Porlock'.
  • Wrote 'Christabel' (parts published in 1816), establishing the 'four-beat' accentual meter that later influenced Walter Scott and Lord Byron.
  • Composed 'Dejection: An Ode' (1802), addressing his emotional estrangement, failing health, opium addiction, and the drying up of his poetic genius.
Comparative Analysis: Unlike Wordsworth, who focused on the 'actual' and the rustic mundane to elevate ordinary life, Coleridge specialized in the supernatural and the uncanny, naturalizing the miraculous through psychological realism.
  • Lyrical Ballads (1798) marks the official commencement of English Romanticism.
  • The Rime of the Ancient Mariner is the opening poem of Lyrical Ballads.
  • Kubla Khan is a supreme example of dream-vision poetry and organic fragmentism.

2. Literary Criticism: Biographia Literaria and Theoretical Frameworks

Coleridge's monumental contribution to practical criticism, aesthetic philosophy, and the psychology of poetic creation.

  • Published 'Biographia Literaria' in 1817, serving as a philosophical autobiography and a critique of Wordsworth's poetic theories.
  • Critiqued Wordsworth's assertion that the proper language of poetry is the real language of men in a middling and rustic life.
  • Formulated the seminal distinction between the Primary Imagination, Secondary Imagination, and Fancy.
  • Defined Primary Imagination as the living Power and prime Agent of all human Perception, a repetition in the finite mind of the eternal act of creation.
  • Defined Secondary Imagination as an echo of the primary, coexisting with the conscious will, yet dissolving, diffusing, dissipating in order to recreate.
  • Defined Fancy as a mode of memory emancipated from the order of time and space, operating on fixed and dead materials through association.
  • Coinage of the phrase 'Willing suspension of disbelief for the moment, which constitutes poetic faith'.
Comparative Analysis: While Wordsworth viewed imagination as an extension of observation and memory, Coleridge elevated it to a divine, metaphysical, and transcendental faculty akin to German transcendentalism.
  • Primary Imagination is universal; Secondary Imagination is exclusive to the artistic genius.
  • Fancy is mechanical and associative; Imagination is organic and creative.
  • Biographia Literaria Chapter XIV contains the core manifesto of Romantic criticism.

3. Philosophical Influences, Prose, and Later Years

Coleridge's transition from radical political philosopher and Unitarian preacher to conservative Anglican thinker and literary sage.

  • Deeply influenced by German idealist philosophers including Immanuel Kant, F.W.J. Schelling, and A.W. Schlegel.
  • Authored 'The Friend' (1809–1810), a periodical essay collection dealing with morals, politics, and religion.
  • Wrote 'Aids to Reflection' (1825), a work of religious philosophy heavily utilized by Victorian intellectuals and theologians.
  • Delivered celebrated lectures on Shakespeare and other dramatists, establishing modern psychological character criticism.
  • Spent his final years residing in Highgate under the medical care of Dr. James Gillman, acting as a literary oracle to young Victorian writers like Thomas Carlyle.
  • Passed away on July 25, 1834, leaving behind an immense legacy of marginalia and uncollected notebooks.
Comparative Analysis: Coleridge's shift from Jacobin radicalism to Tory conservatism mirrors the trajectory of his fellow Lake Poets, Wordsworth and Southey.
  • Coleridge introduced German transcendental philosophy to British intellectual discourse.
  • His Highgate drawing-room sessions cemented his reputation as England's premier literary philosopher.
  • His Shakespearean lectures rescued Elizabethan drama from neoclassical condemnation.

Literary Periods & Regional Context

🌐 Global/Historical Context

Rooted firmly in the British Romantic Movement, specifically the Lake District circle, while absorbing Continental European idealism from German Romanticism (Jena set) and French revolutionary philosophy.

🇮🇳 Indian Literature in English

Coleridge's concepts of organic unity, aesthetic intuition, and the vitalist imagination share deep philosophical parallels with ancient Indian aesthetic theories of 'Rasa' and 'Dhwani' as propounded by Anandavardhana and Abhinavagupta, frequently featured in comparative literature sections of UGC-NET.

📐 Key Literary Devices & Grammar Rules
The Rime of the Ancient Mariner Stanzaic Structure
Ballad Stanza: Quatrains rhyming ABCB (or ABAB) in alternating iambic tetrameter and iambic trimeter
Parameters: Lines 1 & 3: Iambic tetrameter (8 syllables); Lines 2 & 4: Iambic trimeter (6 syllables)
Exam Focus: Frequently tested in UP PGT for metrical identification and rhyme scheme analysis of Romantic narrative verse.
Coleridgean Faculty Trichotomy
Primary Imagination (Universal/Unconscious) + Secondary Imagination (Artistic/Will) vs. Fancy (Mechanical/Associative)
Parameters: Primary = Perception mirror; Secondary = Creator/Re-creator; Fancy = Combiner of fixed historical/perceptual data
Exam Focus: A staple question category in UGC-NET matching lists and assertion-reasoning items regarding Biographia Literaria.

Mnemonics, Memory Shortcuts & Exam Traps

💡 Coleridge's Critical Faculties (PSF)
P S F — 'Please Save Fancy'

P = Primary Imagination, S = Secondary Imagination, F = Fancy. Helps recall the three mental faculties analyzed in Biographia Literaria chapter XIII.

⚠️ Exam Trap: Examiners often trick candidates by stating Fancy is higher than Secondary Imagination. Remember: Imagination is organic and superior; Fancy is mechanical and inferior.
💡 Major Works Chronology (L-R-K-C-B)
L R K C B — 'Let Readers Know Coleridge's Books'

L = Lyrical Ballads (1798), R = The Rime of the Ancient Mariner, K = Kubla Khan (1816), C = Christabel (1816), B = Biographia Literaria (1817).

⚠️ Exam Trap: Watch out for composition dates versus publication dates: Kubla Khan was written in 1797 but published much later in 1816.

📚 High-Yield Literary & Grammatical Terminology

Biographia Literaria UGC-NET Essential

Coleridge's seminal 1817 critical autobiography containing his profound theories on imagination, fancy, and Wordsworth's poetry.

Primary Imagination UP PGT Core

The living Power and prime Agent of all human Perception, a repetition in the finite mind of the eternal act of creation in the infinite I AM.

Secondary Imagination UP TGT Standard

An echo of the primary, co-existing with the conscious will, yet identical with the primary in the kind of its agency, and differing only in degree and in the mode of its operation.

Fancy UGC-NET Concept

A mode of memory emancipated from the order of time and space, blending with other faculties, but operating with fixities and definites rather than creating anew.

Willing Suspension of Disbelief Universal Literary Term

A poetic faith whereby the reader momentarily accepts the impossible or supernatural elements of a work as true for the sake of emotional and imaginative engagement.

The Rime of the Ancient Mariner Textual Knowledge

Coleridge's masterpiece published in Lyrical Ballads (1798), exploring sin, penance, and the interconnection of all living things.

Kubla Khan UP PGT Text

A visionary poem composed in an opium dream, exemplifying the sublime and the fragmented nature of romantic inspiration.

Dejection: An Ode UGC-NET Reference

An autobiographical poem addressed to Sara Hutchinson, lamenting the loss of his imaginative power and philosophical joy.

Shakespearean Criticism Criticism Standard

Coleridge's lectures and notes that revolutionized Shakespearean scholarship by treating characters as real psychological entities rather than mere dramatic plots.

Table Talk Biographical Fact

A collection of Coleridge's informal conversations and opinions recorded by his nephew Henry Nelson Coleridge.

Lyrical Ballads Foundational

The 1798 landmark collection co-authored by Coleridge and Wordsworth that launched the Romantic Movement in English literature.

Friend, The Prose Work

A periodical essay series written and published by Coleridge, dealing with morals, politics, and literature.

Aids to Reflection Philosophical Text

A philosophical prose work by Coleridge designed to assist in the formation of moral and religious principles.

Pantisocracy Historical Context

A utopian scheme proposed by Coleridge and Robert Southey in 1794 to establish a model community on the banks of the Susquehanna River.

Stream of Fancy Critical Distinction

Coleridge's distinction of mere associative mental wandering from the purposive and unified agency of the secondary imagination.

🧭 Prescribed Authors, Critics & Rules

Samuel Taylor Coleridge (Imagination vs. Fancy) (1817 (Biographia Literaria))

Imagination is the shaping, unifying, and creative faculty that dissolves, diffuses, and dissipates in order to recreate; Fancy is merely a logical mode of memory combining fixed and definite data without transforming them.

Coleridge (Primary vs. Secondary Imagination) (1817 (Biographia Literaria, Chapter XIII))

Primary Imagination is universal and unconscious perception; Secondary Imagination is conscious, operates with the will, and is the exclusive domain of the true artist or poet.

Coleridge (Theory of Poetic Unity and Organic Form) (Romantic Period / Shakespearean Lectures)

A true poem is an organic whole where form and content are inseparable; 'A legitimate poem may be defined as that species of composition, which is opposed to works of science, by proposing for its immediate object pleasure, not truth.'

Coleridge & Wordsworth (Joint Literary Manifesto) (1798 / 1800 (Preface to Lyrical Ballads))

Coleridge's domain in Lyrical Ballads was to impart a human interest and a semblance of truth sufficient to procure for these shadows of imagination that willing suspension of disbelief for the moment.

Question 1 of 19
In which chapter of Biographia Literaria does Coleridge famously formulate his distinction between Imagination and Fancy?

Rationale: Chapter XIII of Biographia Literaria contains Coleridge's famous philosophical distinction between Primary Imagination, Secondary Imagination, and Fancy.

Question 2 of 19
How does Coleridge define Primary Imagination?

Rationale: Coleridge defines Primary Imagination as 'the living Power and prime Agent of all human Perception, and as a repetition in the finite mind of the eternal act of creation in the infinite I AM.'

Question 3 of 19
Which critical term was coined by Coleridge to describe the reader's acceptance of supernatural elements in literature?

Rationale: 'Willing suspension of disbelief for the moment, which constitutes poetic faith,' was introduced by Coleridge in Biographia Literaria.

Question 4 of 19
According to Coleridge, what differentiates Fancy from Imagination?

Rationale: Coleridge states that Fancy 'has no other counters to play with, but fixities and definites' and is 'indeed no other than a mode of Memory emancipated from the order of time and space.'

Question 5 of 19
Which poem by Coleridge contains the famous line: 'Water, water, everywhere, nor any drop to drink'?

Rationale: This iconic line appears in Part II of Coleridge's masterpiece, The Rime of the Ancient Mariner.

Question 6 of 19
What is the subtitle of Coleridge's poem 'Kubla Khan'?

Rationale: 'Kubla Khan: Or, a Vision in a Dream. A Fragment' is the complete title/subtitle provided by Coleridge upon its publication in 1816.

Question 7 of 19
In 'Dejection: An Ode', Coleridge laments the loss of which faculty?

Rationale: In 'Dejection: An Ode', Coleridge expresses deep grief over his inability to feel and his loss of the creative 'shaping spirit of Imagination'.

Question 8 of 19
Coleridge's critical approach to Shakespeare was groundbreaking because he:

Rationale: Coleridge treated Shakespeare's characters as real human beings with complex psychological motivations, shifting focus from rigid neoclassical rules to organic character analysis.

Question 9 of 19
Which utopian community did Coleridge plan to establish with Robert Southey in America?

Rationale: Pantisocracy was the equal-rule utopian scheme devised by Coleridge and Southey in 1794, though it was never realized.

Question 10 of 19
Which of the following works was NOT written by S.T. Coleridge?

Rationale: 'A Defence of Poetry' is an essay written by Percy Bysshe Shelley in 1821.

Question 11 of 19
Coleridge defined poetry as being opposed to science by having what as its immediate object?

Rationale: Coleridge stated that a legitimate poem proposes for its immediate object pleasure, not truth, distinguishing it from works of science.

Question 12 of 19
Who recorded Coleridge's conversational remarks published as 'Table Talk'?

Rationale: Henry Nelson Coleridge, his nephew, collected and published his uncle's remarks as 'Specimens of the Table Talk of Samuel Taylor Coleridge'.

Question 13 of 19
In the division of labor for Lyrical Ballads (1798), what was Coleridge's specific assignment?

Rationale: As Wordsworth explained in the Preface, Coleridge's task was to direct his endeavors to persons and characters supernatural, or at least romantic, to evoke a willing suspension of disbelief.

Question 14 of 19
What kind of poem is 'Christabel'?

Rationale: 'Christabel' is an atmospheric, unfinished gothic poem written in an irregular meter based on accent rather than syllable count.

Question 15 of 19
According to Coleridge, Secondary Imagination differs from Primary Imagination in:

Rationale: Coleridge explicitly notes that Secondary Imagination is 'identical with the primary in the kind of its agency, and differing only in degree and in the mode of its operation'.

Question 16 of 19
Which critical concept of Coleridge highlights the unity of a work where the parts reflect the whole and the whole reflects the parts?

Rationale: Organic form, a cornerstone of Romantic criticism championed by Coleridge, dictates that a work grows from within like a living plant.

Question 17 of 19
Coleridge's lectures on Shakespeare delivered in 1811-1812 were heavily indebted to the critical insights of which German critic?

Rationale: Coleridge drew extensively upon August Wilhelm Schlegel's lectures on dramatic art and literature, though Coleridge brought his own unique psychological depth to English criticism.

Question 18 of 19
In 'Biographia Literaria', Coleridge heavily criticizes Wordsworth's poetic theory regarding:

Rationale: Coleridge dedicates several chapters of Biographia Literaria to analyzing and critiquing Wordsworth's theory of poetic diction and his definition of a poet.

Question 19 of 19
Which philosophical thinker had the most profound impact on Coleridge's later critical and metaphysical theories?

Rationale: Coleridge played a pivotal role in introducing German transcendentalism and idealism (Kant, Schelling) into English literary thought.

Question 1 of 10
According to Coleridge, what is the fundamental difference between the agency of Secondary Imagination and Fancy?

Solution: Coleridge asserts that secondary imagination dissolves, diffuses, in order to recreate, whereas fancy must receive all its materials ready made from the law of association.

Question 2 of 10
In which chapter of Biographia Literaria does Coleridge undertake a detailed critique of Wordsworth's theory of poetic diction?

Solution: Chapters XIV through XXII of Biographia Literaria contain Coleridge's famous assessment and critique of Wordsworth's specific assertions regarding the language of real life used by rustics.

Question 3 of 10
What does Coleridge mean by describing Secondary Imagination as an 'echo' of the primary imagination?

Solution: The secondary imagination echoes the primary (which repeats the divine act of creation) by co-existing with the conscious will and bringing about artistic creation.

Question 4 of 10
Which poem by Coleridge is structured as a conversation poem addressed to his infant son Hartley?

Solution: 'Frost at Midnight' is Coleridge's masterly conversation poem where he meditates in silence while watching his sleeping infant son, Hartley.

Question 5 of 10
Coleridge's criticism of Wordsworth's assertion that the language of poetry should be the actual language of men includes the argument that:

Solution: Coleridge argued against Wordsworth that a rustic's language is often bound by narrow confines and that a composite language of educated men is more suited for profound poetry.

Question 6 of 10
How does Coleridge characterize the operation of Fancy in relation to the mind?

Solution: Fancy is defined by Coleridge as a mode of memory emancipated from time and space, operating purely through association rather than organic transformation.

Question 7 of 10
In his Shakespearean criticism, Coleridge introduced the concept of 'imitation' as opposed to:

Solution: Coleridge distinguished 'imitation' from mere 'copy', arguing that true art imitates the creative spirit of nature rather than slavishly copying its surface appearances.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following best summarizes Coleridge's view on poetic meter?

Solution: Coleridge argued that meter produces a superadded tone of feeling and helps balance the emotional excitement of the poem.

Question 9 of 10
What is the primary theme of Coleridge's philosophical prose treatise 'Aids to Reflection' (1825)?

Solution: 'Aids to Reflection' aims to assist readers in moral and spiritual growth by distinguishing between understanding (Verstand) and reason (Vernunft).

Question 10 of 10
According to Coleridge's distinction between Reason and Understanding, which faculty deals with higher spiritual truths and intuitive apprehension?

Solution: Following German idealists, Coleridge classified 'Reason' (Vernunft) as the intuitive faculty grasping spiritual truth, while 'Understanding' (Verstand) handles empirical data and scientific categorization.
