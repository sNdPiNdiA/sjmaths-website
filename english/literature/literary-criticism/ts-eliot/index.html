Literature — Literary Criticism

Ts Eliot: Comprehensive Literary & Grammatical Analysis

High-yield conceptual breakdown and foundational study notes of T.S. Eliot as a literary critic, tailored for competitive exam mastery in UP TGT/PGT English and UGC-NET.

UP PGT English UP TGT English B.A. / M.A. Level

Thomas Stearns Eliot stands as the preeminent architectural force of 20th-century Anglo-American literary criticism, shifting the critical paradigm away from romantic subjectivism and Victorian moralism toward an objective, formalist, and impersonal evaluation of art. In foundational essays such as 'Tradition and the Individual Talent' (1919), 'Hamlet and His Problems' (1919), and 'The Metaphysical Poets' (1921), Eliot weaponized rigorous aesthetic criteria, introducing seminal concepts like the 'objective correlative,' the 'dissociation of sensibility,' and the doctrine of impersonality. For aspirants of the UP TGT/PGT and UGC-NET examinations, mastery of Eliot's critical vocabulary, historical lineages, and polemical stances against romanticism and impressionism is non-negotiable, as examiners routinely test both direct terminology and his theoretical applications to canonical texts.

1. Tradition and the Individual Talent: The Impersonal Theory of Poetry

Reconceptualization of poetic tradition, historical sense, and the extirpation of the authorial personality.

  • Tradition is not inherited; it must be obtained by immense labor involving the historical sense.
  • The historical sense compels a writer to write not merely with their own generation in bones, but with a simultaneous awareness of the entire literature of Europe from Homer.
  • Poetry is not a turning loose of emotion, but an escape from emotion; it is not the expression of personality, but an escape from personality.
  • The mind of the mature poet is a passive 'catalyst' that unifies disparate experiences, remaining fundamentally unchanged during the chemical fusion.
  • Progress in art is not human creation of goodness, but a continual self-sacrifice and extinction of personality.
Comparative Analysis: Contrasts sharply with William Wordsworth's Romantic definition of poetry as 'the spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings' originating from 'emotion recollected in tranquility.'
  • Coined the 'Impersonal Theory of Poetry.'
  • Viewed tradition as a dynamic, ever-altering organic whole.
  • Positioned the artist's mind as a catalytic agent.

2. Objective Correlative and Hamlet and His Problems

The structural requirement of finding physical symbols for abstract emotions and the famous critique of Shakespeare's dramatic failure.

  • Definition: A set of objects, a situation, a chain of events serving as the formula for a particular emotion, evoking that exact emotion instantly.
  • Artistic inevitability is achieved when the external facts are adequate to the internal sensory and emotional experience.
  • In 'Hamlet and His Problems,' Eliot famously pronounces Shakespeare's masterpiece an 'artistic failure.'
  • The play fails because Hamlet's revulsion is an emotion 'exceeding the facts,' directed against a mother whose guilt is unprovable and opaque to the protagonist.
  • Gertrude is a negative character; her inadequacy as a focal point prevents Shakespeare from finding an objective correlative for Hamlet's specific neurosis.
Comparative Analysis: Directly opposes A.C. Bradley's character-analysis approach to Shakespearean tragedy, shifting focus strictly onto textual and structural mechanics.
  • Term 'Objective Correlative' originally coined by Washington Allston, popularized by Eliot.
  • Classified *Hamlet* as an artistic failure due to emotional excess over objective triggers.

3. The Metaphysical Poets and the Dissociation of Sensibility

Historical bifurcation of English poetic thought, unification of sensibility, and the defense of 17th-century metaphysical wit.

  • Eliot praised the Metaphysical poets (John Donne, Andrew Marvell) for their 'telescoping of images' and ability to devour any kind of experience.
  • Unified Sensibility: The direct, seamless capacity to devour experience and feel their thought as immediately as the odor of a rose.
  • Dissociation of Sensibility: A historical rupture occurring in the 17th century (post-Milton and Dryden) where thought and feeling became permanently split.
  • After the dissociation, poets could either feel or think, but they could not feel their thought as a unified mental act.
  • Wit in metaphysical poetry is not mere cleverness, but a tough reasonableness beneath a slight superficial flippancy.
Comparative Analysis: Reverses Samuel Johnson's initial neoclassical critique of metaphysical conceits ('yoking heterogeneous ideas by violence') into a supreme literary virtue.
  • Identified the 17th century as the historical turning point of English thought.
  • Elevated John Donne and metaphysical wit against Victorian and Romantic sweetness.

Literary Periods & Regional Context

🌐 Global/Historical Context

Eliot's critical framework dominated the mid-20th-century Anglo-American landscape, serving as the foundational manifesto for the New Criticism movement spearheaded by Cleanth Brooks, John Crowe Ransom, and I.A. Richards, emphasizing close textual reading over biographical context.

🇮🇳 Indian Literature in English

Eliot's deep fascination with Sanskrit texts (particularly *The Waste Land* incorporating the *Brihadaranyaka Upanishad*) profoundly influenced modern Indian English poetry, while his critical impersonality resonated with the ancient Indian theory of *Rasa* and *Dhvani* regarding universalized emotion (*Sadharanikaran*).

📐 Key Literary Devices & Grammar Rules
The Equation of the Objective Correlative
External Objects + Situation + Chain of Events = Exact Equivalence of Internal Emotion
Parameters: Requires absolute precision where sensory inputs mathematically generate psychological states without didactic authorial intervention.
Exam Focus: Frequently tested in UP PGT and UGC-NET via matching questions pairing critical terms with their exact textual definitions or source essays.
Historical Progression of Sensibility
Unified Sensibility (Pre-17th C) + Historical Rupture (Milton/Dryden) = Dissociation of Sensibility (Post-17th C Onward)
Parameters: Traces the structural decline of holistic human perception into bifurcated intellectualism and sentimentalism.
Exam Focus: Core focus of multiple-choice questions regarding Eliot's essay 'The Metaphysical Poets' (1921).

📚 High-Yield Literary & Grammatical Terminology

Objective Correlative UGC-NET Favorite

A set of objects, a situation, a chain of events which shall be the formula of that particular emotion; such that when the external facts, which must terminate in sensory experience, are given, the emotion is immediately evoked.

Dissociation of Sensibility UP PGT Core

A historical split between thought and feeling in English poetry that Eliot argued began after the seventeenth-century metaphysical poets, leading poets to either feel their thought or think their feelings, but not both simultaneously.

Impersonal Theory of Poetry UP TGT & PGT

Eliot's critical view that poetry is not a turning loose of emotion, but an escape from emotion; it is not the expression of personality, but an escape from personality.

Tradition and the Individual Talent UGC-NET Conceptual

An essay arguing that art never improves, but that the material of art is never the same. It emphasizes the historical sense, which involves a perception, not only of the pastness of the past, but of its presence.

The Waste Land Masterwork Identification

A 434-line modernist poem published in 1922 that employs radical fragmentation, shifting perspectives, and extensive mythological allusions to portray the spiritual sterility of post-WWI Western civilization.

Tradition Critical Term

A matter of much wider significance than mere inheritance; it involves the 'historical sense' and compels a writer to write not merely with his own generation in his bones, but with a feeling that the whole of the literature of Europe from Homer has a simultaneous existence.

The Metaphysical Poets UP PGT History of English Literature

An essay by Eliot (1921) where he coined the term 'dissociation of sensibility' and praised Donne, Cowley, and Crashaw for their unified sensibility and integration of disparate experiences.

Auden Generation Modernist Context

A term for the leftist poets of the 1930s influenced heavily by Eliot's early modernist techniques and social critiques, though they moved towards overt political engagement.

Gerontion Poetic Analysis

A 1920 modernist poem featuring an elderly, disillusioned speaker in a dry month, often viewed as a companion piece or prelude to the psychological landscape of 'The Waste Land'.

Ash Wednesday Late Modernism

Eliot's long poem published in 1930, marking his formal conversion to Anglo-Catholicism, characterized by themes of spiritual renunciation, repentance, and the search for grace.

Four Quartets Major Text

A sequence of four interlinked poems ('Burnt Norton', 'East Coker', 'The Dry Salvages', 'Little Gidding') published between 1935 and 1942, exploring time, eternity, and Christian redemption.

The Hollow Men Famous Quotation

A 1925 poem presenting a vision of spiritual emptiness and despair, concluding with the famous lines: 'This is the way the world ends / Not with a bang but a whimper.'

Excision and Revision Literary History

The editorial process famously demonstrated by Ezra Pound in cutting down the initial draft of 'The Waste Land' to roughly half its original length, transforming it into a high-modernist masterpiece.

Auditory Imagination Critical Concept

A phrase used by Eliot to describe the feeling for syllable and rhythm, penetrating below the conscious levels of thought and feeling, and fusing the old and the modern.

🧭 Prescribed Authors, Critics & Rules

T.S. Eliot (Tradition and the Individual Talent) (1919 (Published in The Egoist))

Art never improves, but the material of art is never the same. Progress of an artist is a continual self-sacrifice, a continual extinction of personality.

T.S. Eliot (The Objective Correlative) (1919 (Hamlet and His Problems))

The only way of expressing emotion in the form of art is by finding an 'objective correlative'; in other words, a set of objects, a situation, a chain of events which shall be the formula of that particular emotion.

T.S. Eliot (Dissociation of Sensibility) (1921 (The Metaphysical Poets))

A dissociation of sensibility set in, from which we have never recovered; and this split is apparent in the separation of thought from feeling, intellect from emotion.

T.S. Eliot (Function of Criticism) (1923)

The critical function is 'the elucidation of work of art and the correction of taste.' Criticism is as inevitable as breathing.

Interactive Concept Practice

Instant evaluation with detailed explanations for each question.

Question 1 of 19
In which essay does T.S. Eliot introduce the concept of the 'objective correlative'?

Rationale: Eliot first introduces and defines the term 'objective correlative' in his 1919 essay 'Hamlet and His Problems', where he criticizes Shakespeare's play as an artistic failure because Hamlet's emotions lack an adequate objective correlative.

Question 2 of 19
Complete T.S. Eliot's famous dictum from 'Tradition and the Individual Talent': 'Poetry is not a turning loose of emotion, but an escape from emotion; it is not the expression of personality, but...'

Rationale: The core of Eliot's impersonal theory of poetry states that poetry is 'an escape from personality,' emphasizing that the poet's mind is a passive catalyst rather than a repository of personal feelings.

Question 3 of 19
According to T.S. Eliot, when did the 'dissociation of sensibility' occur in English poetry?

Rationale: Eliot argues in 'The Metaphysical Poets' that a dissociation of sensibility set in after the generation of seventeenth-century poets like Donne and Herbert, permanently splitting thought from feeling.

Question 4 of 19
Which poem by T.S. Eliot concludes with the lines: 'This is the way the world ends / Not with a bang but a whimper'?

Rationale: 'The Hollow Men' (1925) ends with this iconic despairing couplet, reflecting the spiritual vacuity and paralysis of post-war modern humanity.

Question 5 of 19
What is the title of T.S. Eliot's long poem published in 1930 that marks his conversion to the Anglo-Catholic Church?

Rationale: 'Ash Wednesday' (1930) is Eliot's first major long poem following his conversion to Anglo-Catholicism, characterized by penitence and the search for spiritual rebirth.

Question 6 of 19
Who helped T.S. Eliot heavily edit 'The Waste Land', cutting it down to roughly half its original length?

Rationale: Ezra Pound acted as the critical midwife for 'The Waste Land', drastically trimming the manuscript and earning Eliot's famous dedication: 'il miglior fabbro' (the better craftsman).

Question 7 of 19
Which of the following phrases was coined or popularized by T.S. Eliot to describe the poetic capacity to integrate sound, rhythm, and subterranean thought?

Rationale: Eliot uses the term 'auditory imagination' in 'The Use of Poetry and the Use of Criticism' (1933) to refer to the feeling for syllable and rhythm that plunges below conscious thought.

Question 8 of 19
In 'Tradition and the Individual Talent', Eliot compares the mind of the mature poet to which of the following scientific agents?

Rationale: Eliot uses the analogy of a platinum catalyst in a chemical reaction containing oxygen and sulphur dioxide; it facilitates the combination of feelings into a new artistic whole without itself being altered.

Question 9 of 19
Which sequence of four poems by T.S. Eliot explores the mystery of time, eternity, and Christian redemption?

Rationale: 'Four Quartets' ('Burnt Norton', 'East Coker', 'The Dry Salvages', 'Little Gidding') represents the culmination of Eliot's poetic career, meditating deeply on time and Christian spirituality.

Question 10 of 19
In 'Hamlet and His Problems', Eliot calls Shakespeare's 'Hamlet':

Rationale: Eliot provocatively declares that 'Hamlet (the play) is the 'Monna Lisa' of literature' and asserts that it is 'an artistic failure' because Hamlet's madness and grief lack an objective correlative.

Question 11 of 19
What is the opening line of T.S. Eliot's famous poem 'The Waste Land'?

Rationale: The opening lines of 'The Waste Land' ('April is the cruellest month...') subvert Chaucerian spring traditions by presenting spring as a painful reawakening for a sterile land.

Question 12 of 19
Which critic is primarily associated with the New Critical formalist framework that drew heavy inspiration from T.S. Eliot's impersonal theory?

Rationale: Both American New Critics (like Cleanth Brooks) and British Scrutiny critics (like F.R. Leavis) built their formalist methodologies directly upon Eliot's critical tenets of textual autonomy and close reading.

Question 13 of 19
What does Eliot mean by the 'historical sense' in 'Tradition and the Individual Talent'?

Rationale: The historical sense involves a perception, not only of the pastness of the past, but of its presence; it compels a writer to write with the whole of literature of Europe from Homer simultaneous in their consciousness.

Question 14 of 19
Which poetic drama by T.S. Eliot deals with the assassination of Archbishop Thomas Becket at Canterbury Cathedral?

Rationale: 'Murder in the Cathedral' (1935) is Eliot's verse drama dramatizing the martyrdom of Thomas Becket, commissioned for the Canterbury Festival.

Question 15 of 19
Which of the following early poems features the refrain 'In the room the women come and go / Talking of Michelangelo'?

Rationale: This famous refrain is from 'The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock' (1915), highlighting the shallow, pretentious social milieu in which Prufrock feels paralyzed.

Question 16 of 19
According to Eliot in 'The Function of Criticism', what are the two primary tasks of criticism?

Rationale: Eliot explicitly defines the two primary dimensions of critical activity as 'the elucidation of works of art and the correction of taste' in his 1923 essay.

Question 17 of 19
Which section of 'The Waste Land' is titled after a famous tarot card, though the reader encounters Madame Sosostris?

Rationale: In 'The Burial of the Dead', Madame Sosostris consults her tarot pack, referencing cards like 'the drowned Phoenician Sailor' and 'Belladonna, the Lady of the Rocks'.

Question 18 of 19
What is the underlying theme of T.S. Eliot's essay 'Ulysses, Order and Myth' (1923)?

Rationale: Eliot praised Joyce's use of the 'mythical method' in *Ulysses*, arguing it was a crucial step toward finding order in the chaotic aftermath of WWI.

Question 19 of 19
Which of the following anthologies or critical works solidified T.S. Eliot's status as a dominant critical voice in Britain during the 1920s?

Rationale: 'The Sacred Wood' (1920) contains many of Eliot's foundational essays, including 'Tradition and the Individual Talent' and 'Hamlet and His Problems'.

UP PGT English (Previous Year Pattern)
Who among the following critics formulated the phrase 'dissociation of sensibility'?

Official Benchmark Explanation: T.S. Eliot coined 'dissociation of sensibility' in his 1921 essay 'The Metaphysical Poets' to describe the historical split between intellect and emotion in English poetry.

UGC-NET English (Previous Year Pattern)
Match the works with their authors: (a) The Sacred Wood i. I.A. Richards (b) Practical Criticism ii. T.S. Eliot (c) The Use of Poetry and the Use of Criticism iii. F.R. Leavis (d) New Bearings in English Poetry iv. T.S. Eliot Correct code:

Official Benchmark Explanation: 'The Sacred Wood' (1920) and 'The Use of Poetry and the Use of Criticism' (1933) are by T.S. Eliot. 'Practical Criticism' (1929) is by I.A. Richards. 'New Bearings in English Poetry' (1932) is by F.R. Leavis.

UP TGT English (Previous Year Pattern)
Which poem by T.S. Eliot opens with the line: 'Let us go then, you and I, / When the evening is spread out against the sky / Like a patient etherized upon a table'?

Official Benchmark Explanation: This is the world-famous opening simile of 'The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock', introducing the psychological alienation and decadent urban landscape typical of early Eliot.

UGC-NET English (Previous Year Pattern)
In which of T.S. Eliot's critical essays does the statement 'The progress of an artist is a continual self-sacrifice, a continual extinction of personality' appear?

Official Benchmark Explanation: This quote is the cornerstone of Eliot's impersonal theory of poetry, articulated in 'Tradition and the Individual Talent' (1919).

UP PGT English (Previous Year Pattern)
What is the subtitle of T.S. Eliot's verse drama 'Murder in the Cathedral'?

Official Benchmark Explanation: While often published simply as a verse drama, Eliot's 'Murder in the Cathedral' was specifically written for performance at the Canterbury Festival in 1935 without a formal subtitle.

Question 1 of 10
How does T.S. Eliot define 'Tradition' in relation to the individual artist in his seminal 1919 essay?

Solution: Eliot insists that tradition cannot be inherited; if you want it you must obtain it by great labour. It involves the historical sense, viewing the past as a simultaneous whole.

Question 2 of 10
Why does T.S. Eliot argue that Shakespeare's play 'Hamlet' is an 'artistic failure'?

Solution: Eliot argues that Gertrude's guilt is a feeling which inundates Shakespeare's art, but Shakespeare fails to find an objective correlative to render this specific emotion artistically coherent.

Question 3 of 10
What is the critical significance of Eliot's critique of Milton in his later lectures?

Solution: In essays like 'Milton I and II', Eliot suggested that Milton's linguistic artifice and Latinate syntax caused a stilted diction in later English poetry, though he later softened his critique.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following best describes Eliot's view of poetic 'originality'?

Solution: Eliot famously asserts in 'Tradition and the Individual Talent' that the most individual parts of a poet's work may be those in which the dead poets, his ancestors, assert their immortality most vigorously.

Question 5 of 10
In 'The Waste Land', the section 'The Fire Sermon' prominently features allusions to which religious and literary traditions?

Solution: 'The Fire Sermon' borrows its title from the Buddha's fire sermon (urging detachment from sensory burning), features St. Augustine's fall into Carthage, and incorporates Eastern asceticism.

Question 6 of 10
What does Eliot mean by the 'mythical method' in his 1923 essay on James Joyce's *Ulysses*?

Solution: The mythical method is Joyce's technique of mapping Homeric parallel structures onto modern Dublin life to impart narrative order onto an otherwise fragmented world.

Question 7 of 10
How did T.S. Eliot's literary criticism shift during his later career (post-1930s)?

Solution: In his later phase (e.g., *After Strange Gods*, *Notes Towards the Definition of Culture*), Eliot's criticism became overtly religious and cultural, arguing that literary greatness must be judged by moral and theological standards.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following statements about Eliot's impersonal theory is FALSE?

Solution: Statement 2 is false because Eliot explicitly argues that poetry is *not* a transcription of private trauma or autobiographical confession; 'the man who suffers and the mind which creates' are strictly separate.

Question 9 of 10
What is the thematic function of the Grail Legend in T.S. Eliot's 'The Waste Land'?

Solution: Eliot explicitly acknowledges in his notes that the Fisher King and the Grail legends (drawn from Jessie Weston's *From Ritual to Romance*) provide the overarching mythic skeleton representing spiritual drought.

Question 10 of 10
Which critic's work on practical criticism and affective psychology was intellectually parallel yet distinct from T.S. Eliot's formalist project?

Solution: I.A. Richards, working alongside Eliot in Cambridge-centered modernism, formulated practical criticism and psychological theories of value that helped shape modern critical analysis.
