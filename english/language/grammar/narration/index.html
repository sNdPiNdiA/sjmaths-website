Narration: Comprehensive Literary & Grammatical Analysis

High-yield conceptual breakdown and foundational study notes of Narration tailored for competitive exam mastery.

UP PGT English UP TGT English B.A. / M.A. Level

Narration, broadly defined in linguistics as the transference of speech from direct quotation to indirect reporting, involves intricate shifts in tense, pronoun alignment, spatio-temporal markers, and mood. In the context of English grammar exams such as UP TGT, UP PGT, and UGC-NET, mastery of narration requires a rigorous structural understanding of reporting verbs, declarative interrogative imperative and exclamatory sentence conversions, and exception clauses. Historically evolved from classical rhetorical delivery styles into standardized structural transformations in Modern English, direct and indirect speech forms serve as fundamental litmus tests for syntactic command, semantic fidelity, and morphological adjustment in competitive assessments.

1. Fundamentals of Direct and Indirect Speech Conversion

Mechanisms governing the transition from exact quotation (Direct) to reported utterance (Indirect).

  • Reporting verb tense dictates the tense transformation of the reported speech clause.
  • When the reporting verb is in the Present or Future tense, the tense of the reported speech remains unchanged.
  • When the reporting verb is in the Past tense, the reported speech undergoes backshift (past tense transformations).
  • Universal truths, habitual actions, and scientific facts remain in the simple present tense even if the reporting verb is past.
  • Quotation marks and commas are eliminated in indirect speech, usually replaced by conjunctions like 'that', 'if', or 'whether'.
Comparative Analysis: Direct speech preserves the pragmatic autonomy and deictic anchor of the original speaker, whereas indirect speech integrates the utterance into the syntactically subordinate clause structure governed by the reporter.
  • Past reporting verb triggers backshift of tenses.
  • Universal truths never undergo backshift.
  • Punctuation marks are dropped during conversion.

2. Pronoun, Deictic, and Spatio-Temporal Adverb Shifts

The transformation of personal pronouns and indicators of time and space in reported discourse.

  • First-person pronouns in reported speech shift according to the subject of the reporting verb.
  • Second-person pronouns in reported speech shift according to the object of the reporting verb.
  • Third-person pronouns remain unchanged (Rule of 123: 1=Subject, 2=Object, 3=No change).
  • Adverbs of time shift: 'now' becomes 'then', 'today' becomes 'that day', 'yesterday' becomes 'the previous day'.
  • Adverbs of place shift: 'here' transforms into 'there', and demonstratives like 'this' become 'that'.
Comparative Analysis: Unlike tense shifts which depend strictly on the reporting verb, pronoun shifts depend on the pragmatic participant roles (speaker, addressee, third party) inherent in the communicative matrix.
  • Apply the 123 rule for pronouns: 1st person to Subject, 2nd to Object, 3rd unchanged.
  • Spatial and temporal markers shift to reflect psychological and physical distance.
  • Demonstrative 'this' changes to 'that' unless the object is physically present.

3. Interrogative, Imperative, and Exclamatory Transformations

Advanced syntactic restructuring for non-declarative sentence types in reported speech.

  • Interrogative sentences drop auxiliary inversion, converting into affirmative word order.
  • Yes/No questions use 'if' or 'whether'; Wh- questions retain the original wh-word as a linking connector.
  • Reporting verbs for interrogatives shift to 'asked', 'inquired', or 'queried'.
  • Imperative sentences transform reporting verbs to 'ordered', 'requested', 'advised', or 'commanded', using to-infinitives.
  • Exclamatory sentences convert to assertive statements, using reporting verbs like 'exclaimed with joy/sorrow' and removing interjections.
Comparative Analysis: Declarative sentences rely on 'that', whereas interrogatives shift the clause type from interrogative to declarative subordinate structure, erasing question marks entirely.
  • Interrogatives lose auxiliary inversion and question marks in indirect forms.
  • Imperatives use to-infinitives instead of conjunctions.
  • Exclamatories shift to statements with emotion-denoting reporting phrases.

Literary Periods & Regional Context

🌐 Global/Historical Context

Standard British and American English share core narration transformation rules, though stylistic preferences in journalism favor indirect discourse to maintain editorial objectivity.

🇮🇳 Indian Literature in English

In Indian academic curricula (UP TGT/PGT), narration testing rigorously emphasizes traditional prescriptive grammar rules, exceptions to universal truths, and complex mixed-sentence dialogues.

📐 Key Literary Devices & Grammar Rules
The Tense Backshift Rule
Direct (Present/Future) -> Indirect (No Shift) / Direct (Past) -> Indirect (Corresponding Past)
Parameters: Simple Present -> Simple Past; Present Continuous -> Past Continuous; Simple Past -> Past Perfect
Exam Focus: Crucial for identifying distractor options in multiple-choice questions where backshift is incorrectly applied to universal facts.
Pronoun Transformation Formula (1-2-3 Rule)
1st Person -> Subject of Reporting Verb | 2nd Person -> Object of Reporting Verb | 3rd Person -> No Change
Parameters: Applies universally across declarative, interrogative, and imperative speech conversions.
Exam Focus: Frequently tested in complex sentences containing multiple pronouns to trap careless candidates.

Mnemonics, Memory Shortcuts & Exam Traps

💡 SON: Pronoun Conversion Rule
SON (1 = Subject, 2 = Object, 3 = N/A)

1st person aligns with Subject, 2nd person aligns with Object, 3rd person remains No change.

⚠️ Exam Trap: Watch out for sentences where the reporting verb lacks an explicit object for 2nd person pronouns; supply an implicit object logically.
💡 STOP: Tense Exception Check
STOP for Universal Truths

Always check if the reported clause is a Scientific truth, Temporal habit, Or Proverb (STOP), which forbids tense backshift.

⚠️ Exam Trap: Examiners love changing 'The teacher said, "The earth revolves round the sun"' into 'revolved' to trap candidates.

📚 High-Yield Literary & Grammatical Terminology

Direct Speech UP TGT/PGT Basic Narration

The exact words spoken by a person, enclosed within quotation marks, without any grammatical modification by the reporter.

Indirect Speech UGC-NET Syntax & Grammar

The reporting of what someone said without using their exact words, involving shifts in tense, pronouns, and time/place markers.

Reporting Verb UP TGT Core Concept

The verb in the main clause (e.g., 'said', 'told', 'inquired') that introduces the direct or indirect speech.

Reported Speech UP PGT Grammar

The subordinate clause containing the actual message or statement conveyed by the speaker.

Backshifting UGC-NET Advanced Rule

The backward shift of verb tenses in indirect speech when the reporting verb is in the past tense.

Universal Truth UP TGT Exception Rule

A permanent fact or general validity whose tense remains unchanged in indirect speech even if the reporting verb is in the past.

Historical Present UP PGT Exception Rule

A stylistic device describing past events in the present tense; remains unshifted in narrative structures.

Vocative Case UGC-NET Transformation

Direct address of a person within the reported clause, converted to 'addressing someone as' or placed as the object of 'told'.

Imperative Sentence UP TGT Standard Pattern

Commands, requests, or advice converted in indirect speech using infinitives (to / not to) and appropriate reporting verbs like 'ordered' or 'requested'.

Interrogative Transformation UP PGT Transformation

Questions changed to statements in indirect speech, removing auxiliary inversion and using 'if/whether' for yes/no questions.

Exclamatory Conversion UP TGT Grammar

Exclamations expressing sudden emotion transformed into assertive statements introduced by 'exclaimed with joy/sorrow/wonder'.

Optative Sentence UP PGT Advanced

Expressions of prayer, wish, or curse converted using reporting verbs like 'prayed' or 'wished' with 'might' in the reported clause.

Proximity Markers UP TGT Basic Rule

Words denoting nearness in time or place (e.g., 'now', 'here', 'this') that shift to distance markers ('then', 'there', 'that') in indirect speech.

First Person Pronoun Shift UP TGT Pronoun Rule

Pronouns like 'I', 'we', 'my' in the reported speech change according to the subject of the reporting verb.

Second Person Pronoun Shift UP TGT Pronoun Rule

Pronouns like 'you', 'your' in the reported speech change according to the object of the reporting verb.

🧭 Prescribed Authors, Critics & Rules

Rule of Backshifting (Sequential Tense Concord) (Traditional English Grammar)

When the reporting verb stands in the past tense, all present tenses in the direct speech shift correspondingly to their past equivalents (Present Indefinite to Past Indefinite, Present Continuous to Past Continuous, etc.).

The Universal Truth Exception Rule (Standard Grammatical Axiom)

Tense backshifting is suspended if the reported speech expresses a habitual action, scientific principle, geographical fact, or historical constant.

Rule of Interrogative Word Order Neutralization (Syntactic Transformation Law)

In indirect narration, interrogative structures must lose their question form (subject-auxiliary inversion) and assume standard declarative word order (Subject + Verb).

Reporting Verb Tense Invariance Principle (Modern English Syntax)

If the reporting verb is in the Present or Future tense, no backshifting of tenses occurs in the reported clause, though pronouns and proximity markers still undergo necessary transformations.

Question 1 of 18
Choose the correct indirect form of: He said, 'I am writing a letter now.'

Rationale: The reporting verb 'said' is in the past tense, so 'am writing' (Present Continuous) shifts to 'was writing' (Past Continuous), and 'now' changes to 'then'.

Question 2 of 18
Convert into indirect speech: She said to me, 'Where do you live?'

Rationale: Wh- questions retain the question word, drop the auxiliary 'do', and convert to declarative word order ('I lived'). 'Inquired' requires 'of' before the object, making 'asked' more direct.

Question 3 of 18
Identify the correct indirect narration for: The teacher said, 'The earth revolves around the sun.'

Rationale: Since 'The earth revolves around the sun' is a universal truth, the tense does not change even though the reporting verb is in the past tense.

Question 4 of 18
Transform into indirect speech: He said, 'Alas! I am undone.'

Rationale: Exclamations of grief ('Alas!') convert using 'exclaimed with sorrow', and 'am' shifts to 'was'.

Question 5 of 18
Choose the correct indirect form: The captain said to the soldiers, 'March forward!'

Rationale: Military commands take the reporting verb 'ordered' followed by an infinitive ('to march forward').

Question 6 of 18
Change into indirect speech: Ram said to Sita, 'Will you help me tomorrow?'

Rationale: Yes/No questions use 'if' or 'whether'. 'Will' changes to 'would', 'me' changes to 'him' (referring to Ram), and 'tomorrow' changes to 'the next day'.

Question 7 of 18
Select the correct indirect version: He said, 'May God bless you, my son!'

Rationale: Optative sentences use 'prayed', 'might' replaces 'may', and vocative 'my son' shifts to the possessive modifier within the object structure.

Question 8 of 18
Convert: She says, 'I am ready to go.'

Rationale: Because the reporting verb 'says' is in the present tense, the tense inside the reported speech remains unchanged ('is').

Question 9 of 18
Choose the correct indirect narration for: He said to me, 'Thank you.'

Rationale: Direct expressions of gratitude like 'Thank you' convert directly into the past tense verb 'thanked' with the object as the direct recipient.

Question 10 of 18
Find the correct indirect form of: 'Don't touch this wire,' the electrician said to the boy.

Rationale: Negative imperatives with 'Don't' can be elegantly converted using the verb 'forbade', which absorbs the negation, followed by 'to touch that wire'.

Question 11 of 18
Convert into indirect speech: He said, 'What a beautiful painting!'

Rationale: Wait, let's look at option A vs B. Option A provides the complete grammatical conversion into indirect speech ('He exclaimed with wonder that it was a very beautiful painting'). Correct index is 0.

Question 12 of 18
Choose the correct transformation: He said, 'If I were rich, I would help the poor.'

Rationale: Subjunctive conditionals expressing hypothetical states in past/present ('If I were') generally remain unchanged in indirect speech to preserve mood.

Question 13 of 18
Identify the correct indirect speech: 'Who is the author of this book?' she asked.

Rationale: Wh- question word serves as the connector, verb 'is' shifts to 'was', and 'this' changes to 'that' with standard subject-verb order.

Question 14 of 18
Convert: He said, 'Let us go for a walk.'

Rationale: 'Let us' suggestions convert using 'proposed' or 'suggested' followed by 'that they should'.

Question 15 of 18
Choose the correct indirect form of: He said, 'I must finish this assignment today.'

Rationale: Modal 'must' can either remain unchanged (permanent rule/moral obligation) or change to 'had to' (external obligation), making both A and B contextually valid.

Question 16 of 18
Convert into indirect speech: He said, 'Good morning, my friends.'

Rationale: Salutations like 'Good morning' convert using the verb 'wished' followed by the object and the greeting.

Question 17 of 18
Identify the correct indirect sentence: The teacher said to Mohan, 'You have done well.'

Rationale: Second person 'You' changes to 'he' (matching object Mohan), and 'have done' (Present Perfect) changes to 'had done' (Past Perfect).

Question 18 of 18
Choose the correct indirect form: He said, 'Hush! Do not make a noise.'

Rationale: Interjections like 'Hush!' translating to quietness are integrated into the command structure ('told them to be quiet and not to make a noise').

Previous Years Questions & Exam Benchmarks

Real and benchmark questions aligned with UP PGT, TGT, and National Eligibility tests.

UP PGT English (Previous Year Pattern)
Choose the correct indirect speech of: He said to her, 'Why don't you read this book?'

Official Benchmark Explanation: Interrogative negative sentence: reporting verb becomes 'asked', wh- word 'Why' is retained, 'don't' changes to 'didnt' (past), 'this' changes to 'that', and word order becomes declarative ('she didn't read').

UP TGT English (Previous Year Pattern)
The indirect speech of: He said, 'I have been living here for two years.' is:

Official Benchmark Explanation: Present Perfect Continuous ('have been living') shifts to Past Perfect Continuous ('had been living'), 'here' changes to 'there'.

UGC-NET English (Grammar & Syntax)
Identify the grammatically correct indirect transformation of: She said, 'I saw him yesterday.'

Official Benchmark Explanation: Past Indefinite ('saw') changes to Past Perfect ('had seen'), and 'yesterday' changes to 'the previous day' or 'the day before'.

UP PGT English (Previous Year Pattern)
Change into indirect: The principal said, 'Let the boy enter the room.'

Official Benchmark Explanation: Imperative sentences starting with 'Let' denoting permission or official command convert using 'ordered that ... should' or 'allowed'.

UP TGT English (Previous Year Pattern)
Choose the correct direct speech for the indirect sentence: He asked me if I knew the way to the station.

Official Benchmark Explanation: Reverse transformation: 'if' indicates a Yes/No question in direct speech using auxiliary 'Do' with present tense 'know', and pronoun 'I' changes back to 'you'.

UGC-NET English (Grammar)
Which of the following sentences maintains an incorrect tense shift in indirect narration?

Official Benchmark Explanation: If the reporting verb is in the past tense ('She said'), 'will' must backshift to 'would', and 'tomorrow' must change, making option B incorrect in its raw form.
