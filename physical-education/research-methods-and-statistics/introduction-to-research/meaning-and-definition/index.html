Meaning & Definition of Research

Research in Physical Education and Sports Sciences is a systematic, controlled, empirical, and critical investigation into natural phenomena guided by theory and hypotheses. For aspirants of the UP TGT and PGT Physical Education examinations, mastering the foundational definitions, scientific methodologies, and pedagogical applications of research is crucial for tackling conceptual and factual multiple-choice questions.

UP PGT Physical Education PGT Unit 8

1. Definitional Framework and Theoretical Foundations

The term research is derived from the French word 'rechercher' (meaning to travel through or survey, and 'cerchier' meaning to search). In academic and scientific parlance, it represents a structured inquiry designed to add to the body of organized knowledge. According to Best and Kahn, research is considered the formal, systematic, and intensive process of carrying on a scientific method of analysis.

  • Empirical Nature: Relies on direct observation, measurement, and experimentation in laboratory or field settings.
  • Systematic and Controlled: Eliminates extraneous variables to establish cause-and-effect relationships in exercise physiology, biomechanics, or sports psychology.
  • Critical and Objective: Minimizes researcher bias through standardized testing protocols, reliable instruments (e.g., goniometers, force plates, spirometers), and valid statistical analyses.

2. Scientific Paradigm and Characteristics of Sports Research

Physical education research bridges biological sciences, social sciences, and pedagogical practices. Grounded in rigorous methodologies, research in sports sciences adheres to distinct operational parameters:

  • Problem-Solving Orientation: Seeks solutions to practical coaching dilemmas, such as optimizing a sprinter's start phase or preventing ACL tears in female athletes.
  • Replicability: Detailed methodological descriptions allow independent investigators to repeat studies and verify findings.
  • Cyclical Process: Starts with an observation or research problem, moves through data collection and statistical evaluation (e.g., ANOVA, t-test, chi-square), and concludes with new questions.

Classic sports research scholars such as Clarke and Clarke emphasize that physical education research encompasses historical, descriptive, experimental, and philosophical methodologies tailored to human movement.

3. Application in Sports Performance, Coaching & Pedagogy

Applied research directly transforms athletic training, motor learning, and physical education curricula. Coaches utilize physiological research on VO2 max thresholds, lactate accumulation, and periodization models (e.g., Matveyev's linear model) to peak athletes for major competitions such as the Khelo India Games, Asian Games, or Olympic events.

In school pedagogy, action research allows physical education teachers to evaluate classroom behavior, motor skill acquisition among primary school children, and the effectiveness of inclusive physical education modules.

S - E - C - O - R (Secor)

S = Systematic, E = Empirical, C = Controlled, O = Objective, R = Replicable. Use this mnemonic to quickly recall the core hallmarks of scientific research during UP TGT/PGT exams.

High-Yield Exam Points for UP TGT & PGT

  • The word research originates from the French word 'rechercher', meaning to search closely or investigate thoroughly.
  • Best and Kahn defined research as the formal, systematic, and intensive application of scientific methods.
  • Fundamental (basic) research aims to discover general principles without immediate practical application.
  • Applied research directly addresses practical problems in physical education and sports coaching.
  • Objectivity in research ensures that findings are independent of the researcher's personal biases or subjective opinions.
  • Empirical research is strictly based on observable, measurable evidence gathered through experiments or field tests.

Common Misconceptions & Exam Traps

  • Misconception 1: Students often confuse 'Applied Research' with 'Action Research'. While both solve practical problems, action research is conducted by practitioners (teachers/coaches) for immediate local improvement, whereas applied research seeks broader applications across similar populations.
  • Misconception 2: Assuming library work or reading textbooks constitutes 'research'. True research requires systematic data collection, analysis, and interpretation to discover new facts or verify existing theories.

Practice Examination Questions (10 MCQs)

Test your conceptual understanding. Select an answer and check the detailed pedagogical rationale.

Q1 The term 'research' is derived from which language and original word?
  • A Latin - Researcus
  • B French - Rechercher
  • C Greek - Methodos
  • D German - Forschung
Correct Option: B (French - Rechercher)

Research is derived from the old French word 'rechercher', which means 'to go about seeking' or 'to search closely'. This is a frequent direct factual question in UPSESSB examinations.

Q2 Which type of research is primarily designed to add to the organized body of scientific knowledge without immediate practical utility?
  • A Action Research
  • B Applied Research
  • C Basic (Pure/Fundamental) Research
  • D Historical Research
Correct Option: C (Basic (Pure/Fundamental) Research)

Basic or pure research is driven by intellectual curiosity to expand knowledge and formulate theories, rather than solving an immediate practical problem.

Q3 According to Best and Kahn, research is best defined as:
  • A A random collection of sports statistics
  • B The formal, systematic, and intensive process of carrying on a scientific method of analysis
  • C A subjective evaluation of athletic performance by expert coaches
  • D A historical compilation of Olympic medal counts
Correct Option: B (The formal, systematic, and intensive process of carrying on a scientific method of analysis)

Best and Kahn defined research formally as the systematic and intensive application of the scientific method to problem-solving.

Q4 What is the primary characteristic of empirical research?
  • A It relies on philosophical arguments and logical deductions.
  • B It is based on direct observation, testing, and measurable evidence.
  • C It is based entirely on the personal experience of veteran coaches.
  • D It focuses exclusively on ancient sports history.
Correct Option: B (It is based on direct observation, testing, and measurable evidence.)

Empirical research relies on tangible, observable data gathered through rigorous measurement instruments and controlled trials.

Q5 If a physical education teacher conducts a study to solve an immediate discipline or learning problem in their specific school gymnasium, what type of research is it?
  • A Basic Research
  • B Action Research
  • C Cross-cultural Research
  • D Ethnographic Research
Correct Option: B (Action Research)

Action research is undertaken by practitioners (teachers or coaches) to solve immediate localized problems in their specific instructional settings.

Q6 Which of the following best describes 'objectivity' in scientific research?
  • A The researcher's personal belief dictates the study outcome.
  • B Findings are free from personal bias, prejudice, and subjective judgment.
  • C The study can only be performed by one specific expert.
  • D Data collection is done without any instruments.
Correct Option: B (Findings are free from personal bias, prejudice, and subjective judgment.)

Objectivity ensures that independent researchers using identical methods will arrive at the same conclusions, free from researcher bias.

Q7 Which research methodology studies past events, documents, and records to understand current physical education trends?
  • A Experimental Research
  • B Historical Research
  • C Biomechanical Analysis
  • D Survey Research
Correct Option: B (Historical Research)

Historical research involves investigating past records, artifacts, and literature to reconstruct the past and understand its impact on modern physical education.

Q8 In the context of sports sciences, what does 'replicability' mean?
  • A The ability to copy another researcher's thesis without citation.
  • B The capability of a study to be repeated by other investigators to verify results.
  • C The duplication of sports equipment in a laboratory.
  • D The repetition of training loads without rest.
Correct Option: B (The capability of a study to be repeated by other investigators to verify results.)

Replicability is a cornerstone of the scientific method, ensuring that research procedures are transparent enough for independent verification.

Q9 Which of the following represents an application of applied research in sports?
  • A Studying the molecular structure of muscle proteins in vitro.
  • B Testing the effect of a 6-week high-intensity interval training (HIIT) protocol on basketball players' agility.
  • C Reading philosophical treatises on ancient Greek Olympics.
  • D Cataloging the rules of football from 1863.
Correct Option: B (Testing the effect of a 6-week high-intensity interval training (HIIT) protocol on basketball players' agility.)

Testing specific training protocols on athletes to improve performance is classic applied research.

Q10 Why is research methodology considered essential for UP PGT Physical Education candidates?
  • A It helps in designing decorative school banners.
  • B It provides evidence-based approaches to training, injury prevention, and curriculum evaluation.
  • C It is solely required for writing sports journalism.
  • D It replaces practical ground training.
Correct Option: B (It provides evidence-based approaches to training, injury prevention, and curriculum evaluation.)

Research methodology empowers educators and coaches to use scientific evidence rather than unverified myths for enhancing training and teaching.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the exact definition of research in physical education?

Research in physical education is a systematic, controlled, empirical, and critical investigation of hypothetical propositions concerning presumed relations among natural phenomena in human movement, exercise physiology, and sports pedagogy.

How does basic research differ from applied research in sports sciences?

Basic research expands theoretical knowledge in controlled laboratory settings without immediate practical application, whereas applied research solves specific, practical problems in coaching, training, or physical education classrooms.

Why is objectivity critical in physical education research?

Objectivity ensures that measurements (such as timing a sprint, measuring VO2 max, or scoring a skill test) are free from personal bias, ensuring valid and reliable scientific conclusions.

What are the key traits remembered by the SECOR mnemonic?

SECOR stands for Systematic, Empirical, Controlled, Objective, and Replicable—the five indispensable characteristics of scientific research.
