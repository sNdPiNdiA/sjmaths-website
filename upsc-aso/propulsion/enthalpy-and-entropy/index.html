Enthalpy & entropy

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Engine pressure ratio (EPR) indicators and Exhaust Gas Temperature (EGT) gauges directly monitor enthalpy drops and rises across turbine stages.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: High ambient entropy and temperature reduce compressor efficiency and mass flow, directly degrading take-off thrust margins on hot days.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 mandate rigorous engine performance monitoring to track compressor degradation and thermal fatigue.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers analyze FDR enthalpy and EGT exceedance logs during uncontained turbine failure or compressor stall investigations.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Enthalpy is the measure of total energy in a thermodynamic system ($H = U + pV$), whereas entropy is a measure of thermal energy unavailability per unit temperature ($dS = \frac{dQ_{rev}}{T}$). These state functions govern the work extraction and irreversibility losses in gas turbine compression and expansion processes.
  • Physical Meaning: Enthalpy represents the total heat content of a working fluid, combining internal thermal energy and flow work. Entropy quantifies microscopic disorder and lost work within gas turbine components.
  • Governing Law: Governed by the First and Second Laws of Thermodynamics, dictating energy conservation and the directional degradation of energy quality.
  • Continuum Boundary: Assumed valid within the continuum fluid mechanics domain where thermodynamic equilibrium properties are defined for control volumes.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $H$ = Total Enthalpy ($\text{J}$)
  • $h$ = Specific Enthalpy ($\text{J/kg}$)
  • $U$ = Internal Energy ($\text{J}$)
  • $p$ = Static Pressure ($\text{Pa}$)
  • $V$ = Volume ($\text{m}^3$)
  • $S$ = Total Entropy ($\text{J/K}$)
  • $s$ = Specific Entropy ($\text{J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$)
  • $T$ = Absolute Temperature ($\text{K}$)
  • $Q$ = Heat Transfer ($\text{J}$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Gas molecules possess translational, rotational, and vibrational kinetic energies that directly establish the macroscopic internal energy and static enthalpy of the fluid parcel.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Pressure and temperature gradients across compressor blades and turbine passages drive enthalpy conversion into kinetic energy and shaft work.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Viscous friction, shock waves, and flow separation generate internal irreversibilities, causing entropy generation and reducing stagnation pressure recovery across aerodynamic channels.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Combine the first law of thermodynamics with boundary work for an open flow system.

$$dq = dh - v dp$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Introduce the definition of entropy for a reversible thermodynamic process.

$$ds = \frac{dq_{rev}}{T}$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Substitute the reversible heat transfer relation into the combined property relation.

$$ds = \frac{dh - v dp}{T}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Express specific enthalpy change for an ideal gas with constant specific heat.

$$dh = c_p dT$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Specific enthalpy has dimensions $[L^2 T^{-2}]$ and specific entropy has dimensions $[L^2 T^{-2} \Theta^{-1}]$.
  • SI Unit: Specific enthalpy is measured in Joules per kilogram ($\text{J/kg}$ or $\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$); specific entropy is measured in Joules per kilogram Kelvin ($\text{J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$).
  • CGS Unit: Specific enthalpy in ergs per gram; specific entropy in ergs per gram degree Celsius.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: British Thermal Units per pound ($\text{BTU/lb}$) for enthalpy, and $\text{BTU/(lb}\cdot\text{°R)}$ for specific entropy.

    Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2 Series 'B' Part VII requires strict adherence to engine operating limits, while ICAO Annex 6 mandates continuous airworthiness tracking of propulsion thermal margins.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: FADEC algorithms rely on real-time enthalpy computations to schedule fuel flow, variable stator vanes, and bleed-air extraction to prevent engine surges.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: British Airways Flight 009 encountered volcanic ash over Mt. Galunggung, choking airflow, spiking turbine entry enthalpy, and causing multi-engine flameout. Root safety cause: High particulate ingestion altering gas path thermodynamics and exceeding turbine thermal limits.

    • Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

    Trap 1: Isentropic Process Equals Zero Enthalpy Change

    ❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming that an isentropic expansion or compression implies zero change in specific enthalpy.

    ✅ UPSC Truth: Isentropic means constant entropy ($ds=0$), but enthalpy changes significantly as work is extracted or added ($dh = v dp$).

    Trap 2: Entropy Can Decrease in Adiabatic Flow

    ❌ Examiner Trap: Believing that adiabatic flow through a real turbine blade passage maintains constant entropy.

    ✅ UPSC Truth: Friction and shock waves create irreversibilities, meaning entropy always increases in real adiabatic flow ($ds > 0$).

    Trap 3: Total Enthalpy Changes in Inter-Blade Ducting

    ❌ Examiner Trap: Stating that stagnation enthalpy changes across stationary nozzle guide vanes.

    ✅ UPSC Truth: No work is exchanged in stationary ducts, so stagnation enthalpy remains constant even as static enthalpy drops and kinetic energy rises.

    Trap 4: Specific Heat is Constant at High Mach Numbers

    ❌ Examiner Trap: Using constant $c_p$ values for high-temperature hypersonic scramjet combustion calculations.

    ✅ UPSC Truth: At elevated temperatures inside turbine burners, gas dissociation causes specific heat $c_p$ to vary significantly with temperature.

    Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

    Realistic Aircraft Scenario

    An aircraft gas turbine compressor receives air at a stagnation temperature of $300\text{ K}$ and compresses it to a pressure ratio of $10:1$ with an isentropic efficiency of $85\%$. Calculate the actual exit specific enthalpy assuming constant specific heat $c_p = 1005\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$ and ratio of specific heats $\gamma = 1.4$.

    Given Flight Parameters:
    Stagnation temperature $T_{01} = 300\text{ K}$, Pressure ratio $\pi_c = 10$, Isentropic efficiency $\eta_c = 0.85$, $c_p = 1005\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$, $\gamma = 1.4$
    Governing Formula:
    $$T_{02s} = T_{01} \cdot \pi_c^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}, \quad \eta_c = \frac{h_{02s} - h_{01}}{h_{02a} - h_{01}} = \frac{T_{02s} - T_{01}}{T_{02a} - T_{01}}$$
    Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
    Step 1: Compute ideal exit temperature: $T_{02s} = 300 \cdot (10)^{\frac{1.4-1}{1.4}} = 300 \cdot (10)^{0.2857} = 300 \cdot 1.9307 = 579.2\text{ K}$. Step 2: Compute actual exit temperature using efficiency: $T_{02a} = T_{01} + \frac{T_{02s} - T_{01}}{\eta_c} = 300 + \frac{579.2 - 300}{0.85} = 300 + \frac{279.2}{0.85} = 628.47\text{ K}$. Step 3: Calculate actual specific enthalpy change: $\Delta h_0 = c_p (T_{02a} - T_{01}) = 1005 \cdot (628.47 - 300) = 330,612\text{ J/kg}$ or $330.61\text{ kJ/kg}$.
    Final Answer: Actual specific enthalpy change $\Delta h_0 = 330.61\text{ kJ/kg}$ (Actual exit temperature $628.47\text{ K}$)
    Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

    Accurate enthalpy rise calculations ensure compressor discharge temperatures remain within metallurgical limits, preventing creep failure and uncontained engine separation during high-power operations.

    Question 1 • conceptual
    Which of the following thermodynamic properties remains constant during an ideal, frictionless adiabatic process?
    A Enthalpy
    B Entropy
    C Temperature
    D Pressure
    Solution & Technical Rationale:
    An ideal adiabatic process is isentropic, meaning entropy remains constant ($ds = 0$), whereas enthalpy, temperature, and pressure vary.
    Question 2 • statement
    Consider the following statements regarding stagnation enthalpy in a gas turbine engine: 1. Stagnation enthalpy remains constant across stationary diffuser passages. 2. Stagnation enthalpy decreases across a turbine rotor stage. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
    A 1 only
    B 2 only
    C Both 1 and 2
    D Neither 1 nor 2
    Solution & Technical Rationale:
    Statement 1 is correct because no work is done in stationary diffusers ($h_0$ is constant). Statement 2 is correct because turbines extract shaft work, reducing stagnation enthalpy.
    Question 3 • numerical
    Air flows through an adiabatic compressor with an inlet stagnation temperature of $288\text{ K}$ and an exit stagnation temperature of $550\text{ K}$. If $c_p = 1005\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$, what is the specific enthalpy change across the compressor?
    A $263.3\text{ kJ/kg}$
    B $294.5\text{ kJ/kg}$
    C $552.7\text{ kJ/kg}$
    D $838.0\text{ kJ/kg}$
    Solution & Technical Rationale:
    Specific enthalpy change $\Delta h_0 = c_p (T_{02} - T_{01}) = 1005 \times (550 - 288) = 1005 \times 262 = 263,310\text{ J/kg} = 263.3\text{ kJ/kg}$.
    Question 4 • assertion
    Assertion (A): Entropy increases in all real gas turbine flow processes. Reason (R): Viscosity, friction, and shock waves introduce thermodynamic irreversibilities.
    A Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
    B Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
    C (A) is true, but (R) is false.
    D (A) is false, but (R) is true.
    Solution & Technical Rationale:
    According to the Second Law of Thermodynamics, real fluid flows involve irreversibilities such as viscous shear and shock waves, leading to entropy generation ($ds > 0$).
    Question 5 • conceptual
    What is the physical significance of the T-ds relation $T ds = dh - v dp$ in compressible gas dynamics?
    A It relates heat transfer and irreversibilities to changes in enthalpy and pressure.
    B It proves that enthalpy is independent of pressure for ideal gases.
    C It defines the polytropic efficiency of a centrifugal pump.
    D It calculates the absolute velocity of a supersonic jet.
    Solution & Technical Rationale:
    The Gibbs equation ($T ds = dh - v dp$) links entropy changes to enthalpy and pressure variations, forming the foundation of compressible flow analysis.
    Question 6 • statement
    Which of the following statements about specific heat at constant pressure ($c_p$) are correct? 1. It equals the rate of change of enthalpy with respect to temperature at constant pressure. 2. For calorically perfect gases, $c_p$ varies widely with pressure. Select the correct answer using the code below:
    A 1 only
    B 2 only
    C Both 1 and 2
    D Neither 1 nor 2
    Solution & Technical Rationale:
    Statement 1 is the definition of $c_p = (\partial h / \partial T)_p$. Statement 2 is false because calorically perfect gases have constant $c_p$ independent of temperature and pressure.
    Question 7 • numerical
    In an adiabatic expansion process through a turbine, the inlet enthalpy is $1200\text{ kJ/kg}$ and the exit enthalpy is $800\text{ kJ/kg}$. If the mass flow rate is $50\text{ kg/s}$, what is the power output of the turbine?
    A $10\text{ MW}$
    B $20\text{ MW}$
    C $40\text{ MW}$
    D $60\text{ MW}$
    Solution & Technical Rationale:
    Power output $P = \dot{m}(h_{in} - h_{out}) = 50\text{ kg/s} \times (1200 - 800)\text{ kJ/kg} = 50 \times 400 = 20,000\text{ kW} = 20\text{ MW}$.
    Question 8 • conceptual
    How does an increase in ambient air temperature affect the takeoff performance of a turbofan engine?
    A Increases air density and specific thrust
    B Reduces air density, increases specific entropy, and decreases mass flow rate and thrust
    C Decreases entropy generation and increases compressor surge margin
    D Has no effect on compressor thermodynamics
    Solution & Technical Rationale:
    Higher ambient temperature decreases air density and increases initial entropy state, reducing engine mass flow rate and available takeoff thrust.
    Question 9 • statement
    Consider statements on polytropic efficiency: 1. Polytropic efficiency accounts for the cumulative thermodynamic losses in multi-stage compressors. 2. Polytropic efficiency is always equal to isentropic efficiency regardless of pressure ratio. Which statements are correct?
    A 1 only
    B 2 only
    C Both 1 and 2
    D Neither 1 nor 2
    Solution & Technical Rationale:
    Statement 1 is correct. Statement 2 is false because isentropic efficiency depends heavily on the overall pressure ratio due to the stage-stacking effect, unlike polytropic efficiency.
    Question 10 • assertion
    Assertion (A): Throttling an ideal gas through a porous plug is an isenthalpic process. Reason (R): The pressure drop is accompanied by an equivalent increase in kinetic energy without external heat or work transfer.
    A Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
    B Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
    C (A) is true, but (R) is false.
    D (A) is false, but (R) is true.
    Solution & Technical Rationale:
    Assertion (A) is true (throttling is isenthalpic, $h_1 = h_2$). Reason (R) is false because kinetic energy changes are negligible in porous plug throttling; enthalpy remains constant due to $Q=0$ and $W=0$.
    Question 11 • numerical
    Calculate the specific entropy change of an ideal gas when its temperature increases from $300\text{ K}$ to $600\text{ K}$ at constant pressure. Given $c_p = 1005\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$.
    A $296.8\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$
    B $696.6\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$
    C $1005.0\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$
    D $1250.4\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$
    Solution & Technical Rationale:
    At constant pressure ($dp = 0$), $ds = c_p \ln(T_2 / T_1) = 1005 \times \ln(600 / 300) = 1005 \times \ln(2) = 1005 \times 0.69315 = 696.6\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$... Wait, let's recalculate: $1005 \times 0.693147 = 696.6$. Correct option is B.
    Question 12 • conceptual
    Why is the Mollier diagram ($h-s$ diagram) preferred over the T-s diagram for steam and gas turbine analysis?
    A Constant pressure lines are vertical on an h-s diagram.
    B Vertical distances on an h-s diagram directly represent enthalpy drops or work done.
    C Entropy is held constant across all coordinates.
    D It eliminates the need for specific heat values.
    Solution & Technical Rationale:
    On an $h-s$ (Mollier) diagram, vertical distances directly measure enthalpy change ($\Delta h$), which equals work in adiabatic turbines and compressors.

    Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
    In an irreversible adiabatic process, entropy change ($ds$) is:
    A Equal to zero
    B Less than zero
    C Greater than zero
    D Negative infinity
    Rationale:
    The Second Law dictates that entropy increases ($ds > 0$) for any real, irreversible adiabatic process.
    Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
    For an ideal gas, specific enthalpy depends exclusively on:
    A Pressure
    B Temperature
    C Volume
    D Entropy
    Rationale:
    Joule's law proves that internal energy and enthalpy of an ideal gas are functions of temperature alone.
    Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
    The slope of an isobar on a T-s diagram is equal to:
    A $c_v / T$
    B $c_p / T$
    C $T / c_p$
    D $R / T$
    Rationale:
    From $T ds = c_p dT - v dp$, along an isobar ($dp=0$), the slope is $\left(\frac{dT}{ds}\right)_p = \frac{T}{c_p}$, hence slope inverse is $c_p/T$.
    Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
    A compressor operating with an isentropic efficiency of $80\%$ compared to $100\%$ will result in:
    A Lower exit temperature and lower entropy generation
    B Higher exit temperature and higher entropy generation
    C No change in exit temperature
    D Lower exit pressure
    Rationale:
    Lower efficiency means higher irreversibility, resulting in greater entropy generation and a higher actual discharge temperature.
    Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
    What is the unit of specific entropy in SI units?
    A $\text{J/K}$
    B $\text{J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$
    C $\text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$
    D $\text{kg/(m}\cdot\text{s)}$
    Rationale:
    Specific entropy is entropy per unit mass, yielding $\text{J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$.
    Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
    The process inside an ideal rocket nozzle expansion is assumed to be:
    A Isothermal
    B Isobaric
    C Isentropic
    D Isochoric
    Rationale:
    Ideal expansion nozzles convert thermal enthalpy into kinetic energy reversibly and adiabatically, which is isentropic.
    Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
    Which of the following thermodynamic relations is known as the first Gibbs equation?
    A $T ds = du + p dv$
    B $T ds = dh - v dp$
    C $dh = c_p dT$
    D $du = c_v dT$
    Rationale:
    The first Gibbs relation is $T ds = du + p dv$.
    Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
    In a gas turbine combustion chamber, pressure drops slightly while heat is added. This process approximates:
    A Isothermal heating
    B Rayleigh flow with heat addition
    C Adiabatic throttling
    D Isentropic compression
    Rationale:
    Combustion in a constant-area or constant-area duct with heat addition and friction is modeled as Rayleigh flow.
    Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
    An Air Safety Officer reviewing an engine failure notes a high EGT reading. This directly indicates an abnormal rise in:
    A Compressor efficiency
    B Turbine exit enthalpy and thermal stress
    C Ambient air density
    D Fan bypass ratio
    Rationale:
    High Exhaust Gas Temperature (EGT) indicates excessive turbine entry or exit enthalpy, signaling poor turbine efficiency or fuel control unit malfunction.
