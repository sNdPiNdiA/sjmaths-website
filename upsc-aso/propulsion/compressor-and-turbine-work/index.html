Compressor & turbine work

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Cockpit Engine Indicating and Crew Alerting System (EICAS) monitors Interstage Turbine Temperature (ITT) and compressor rotor speeds to safeguard mechanical work limits.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: High turbine work requirements during hot-and-high airfield operations restrict maximum takeoff weights and limit climb thrust margins.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Compliance with DGCA CAR Section 2 Series 'B' Part VII mandates strict recording of turbine gas temperature (TGT) exceedances and compressor surge events.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air safety officers analyze FDR parameters for compressor stall signatures and turbine over-temperature spikes during uncontained engine failure investigations.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Compressor and turbine work represent the specific enthalpy changes per unit mass extracted from or added to a working fluid across gas turbine engine rotating stages: $$w = h_2 - h_1$$.
  • Physical Meaning: Compressor work is shaft input energy raising static pressure and enthalpy, whereas turbine work is expansion-driven energy extraction supplying shaft torque.
  • Governing Law: Governed by the Steady Flow Energy Equation (SFEE) and the First Law of Thermodynamics for an open adiabatic system without external heat transfer.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid under continuous fluid flow domains where Knudsen numbers remain below $0.01$, treating blade-passage fluids as compressible continua.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $w$ = Specific work transfer per unit mass ($J/kg$)
  • $h$ = Specific enthalpy ($J/kg$)
  • $c_p$ = Specific heat at constant pressure ($J/(kg \cdot K)$)
  • $T$ = Static temperature ($K$)
  • $T_0$ = Total or stagnation temperature ($K$)
  • $\dot{m}$ = Mass flow rate ($kg/s$)
  • $W$ = Total power (Watts or $J/s$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: High-velocity gas parcels strike rotating turbine blades, transferring kinetic and thermal momentum to mechanical shaft rotation.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Compressor blades exert boundary-layer shear and pressure forces on the fluid, doing work to raise static pressure and temperature gradients.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Total enthalpy changes directly reflect energy coupling between shaft torque and fluid stagnation state variables across the blading rows.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Apply the SFEE across a control volume with zero heat transfer and no potential energy change.

$$\dot{Q} - \dot{W}_s = \dot{m}\left(h_{02} - h_{01}\right)$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Express specific stagnation enthalpy in terms of constant specific heat and total temperature.

$$h_0 = c_p T_0$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Substitute enthalpy relations into the specific work expression for adiabatic compressor and turbine processes.

$$w_c = c_p (T_{02} - T_{01}) \quad \text{and} \quad w_t = c_p (T_{03} - T_{04})$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Incorporate Euler's turbine equation utilizing tangential velocity components at rotor inlet and exit.

$$w = U_2 V_{\theta 2} - U_1 V_{\theta 1}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Specific work has dimensions of $[L^2 T^{-2}]$ derived from force times distance per unit mass.
  • SI Unit: Joules per kilogram ($J/kg$), equivalent to $m^2/s^2$ or $W/(kg/s)$.
  • CGS Unit: Ergs per gram ($erg/g$), where $1 \, J/kg = 10^4 \, erg/g$.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: British Thermal Units per pound ($BTU/lb$), where $1 \, BTU/lb = 2326 \, J/kg$. Horsepower-hours per pound are also utilized in specific fuel consumption metrics.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Governed by DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'C' and ICAO Annex 8 Airworthiness of Aircraft, requiring rigorous engine rotor integrity audits.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Directly influences Full Authority Digital Engine Control (FADEC) scheduling, bleed air pressure regulation, and high-pressure turbine blade cooling flows.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: British Airways Flight 38 suffered dual engine flameout due to fuel icing restricting fuel-oil heat exchanger work balance; root safety cause led to updated fuel system certification standards.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Isothermal Compression Assumption

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming compressor work calculations use isothermal temperature ratios instead of polytropic or isentropic relations.

✅ UPSC Truth: Compressor and turbine processes in gas turbines operate adiabatically, making stagnation temperature changes proportional to work input/output ($w = c_p \Delta T_0$).

Trap 2: Work Sign Conventions

❌ Examiner Trap: Treating compressor work as positive output energy because it raises fluid pressure.

✅ UPSC Truth: Work input to a compressor is negative from a thermodynamic system perspective ($W_{in} < 0$), whereas turbine work output is positive ($W_{out} > 0$).

Trap 3: Ignoring Efficiency Losses

❌ Examiner Trap: Calculating real compressor work directly from ideal pressure ratios without applying isentropic efficiency.

✅ UPSC Truth: Real compressor work requires dividing ideal enthalpy change by isentropic efficiency ($w_{actual} = w_{ideal} / \eta_c$).

Trap 4: Euler Work Equation Velocity Triangles

❌ Examiner Trap: Using axial velocity instead of tangential swirl velocity in Euler's pump and turbine work equation.

✅ UPSC Truth: Specific work is exclusively driven by the change in angular momentum, determined by tangential velocity components ($U \cdot V_\theta$).

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An axial compressor processes $50 \, kg/s$ of air with an inlet stagnation temperature of $288 \, K$. If the stagnation temperature at the compressor exit is $550 \, K$ and specific heat $c_p = 1005 \, J/(kg \cdot K)$, determine the specific compressor work and total power input.

Given Flight Parameters:
Mass flow rate $\dot{m} = 50 \, kg/s$, Inlet stagnation temperature $T_{01} = 288 \, K$, Exit stagnation temperature $T_{02} = 550 \, K$, $c_p = 1005 \, J/(kg \cdot K)$.
Governing Formula:
$w_c = c_p (T_{02} - T_{01}) \quad \text{and} \quad \dot{W}_c = \dot{m} w_c$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute stagnation temperature difference $\Delta T_0 = 550 - 288 = 262 \, K$. Step 2: Calculate specific compressor work $w_c = 1005 \times 262 = 263,310 \, J/kg$. Step 3: Multiply by mass flow rate for total power $\dot{W}_c = 50 \times 263,310 = 13,165,500 \, W$ or $13.166 \, MW$.
Final Answer: Specific Work = $263.31 \, kJ/kg$, Total Power Input = $13.166 \, MW$.
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate calculation of compressor power demand is vital for verifying starter motor torque ratings and preventing thermal overloading during engine ground starts.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary thermodynamic classification of flow work across an ideal adiabatic gas turbine compressor?
A Isothermal compression
B Isentropic compression
C Polytropic expansion
D Constant volume heating
Solution & Technical Rationale:
An ideal compressor in a gas turbine analysis assumes reversible adiabatic, i.e., isentropic compression.
Question 2 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding compressor and turbine work: 1. Compressor work input increases stagnation enthalpy. 2. Turbine work output decreases total temperature. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. Compression adds mechanical energy raising $T_0$, while turbine expansion extracts energy reducing $T_0$.
Question 3 • numerical
An air compressor has an inlet stagnation temperature of $300 \, K$ and exit stagnation temperature of $600 \, K$. Taking $c_p = 1005 \, J/(kg \cdot K)$, what is the specific work input?
A $150.75 \, kJ/kg$
B $301.50 \, kJ/kg$
C $603.00 \, kJ/kg$
D $904.50 \, kJ/kg$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $w = c_p (T_{02} - T_{01}) = 1005 \times (600 - 300) = 301,500 \, J/kg$ or $301.50 \, kJ/kg$.
Question 4 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Turbine work output is positive in thermodynamic sign convention. Reason (R): Work is done by the working fluid on the turbine rotor blades.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Work output from a system is defined as positive. Since the fluid does work on the turbine blades, the turbine work output is positive.
Question 5 • conceptual
Euler's turbine work equation expresses specific work primarily as a function of which parameter?
A Static pressure drop across the stator
B Change in tangential velocity (swirl) across the rotor
C Radial velocity components at shroud tip
D Boundary layer friction coefficient
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Euler's pump and turbine equation states $w = U_2 V_{\theta 2} - U_1 V_{\theta 1}$, highlighting the change in angular momentum via tangential velocity.
Question 6 • statement
Which of the following affect compressor work requirements for a given mass flow rate? 1. Inlet stagnation temperature 2. Compressor pressure ratio 3. Isentropic efficiency Select the correct answer using the code below:
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2, and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
All three parameters directly determine the temperature rise and enthalpy change, hence governing actual compressor work.
Question 7 • numerical
A turbine expands gas from an inlet total temperature of $1200 \, K$ to an exit total temperature of $900 \, K$. If $c_p = 1150 \, J/(kg \cdot K)$, what is the specific work extracted?
A $115 \, kJ/kg$
B $230 \, kJ/kg$
C $345 \, kJ/kg$
D $460 \, kJ/kg$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Specific turbine work $w_t = c_p (T_{03} - T_{04}) = 1150 \times (1200 - 900) = 345,000 \, J/kg = 345 \, kJ/kg$.
Question 8 • conceptual
What is the physical significance of compressor isentropic efficiency?
A Ratio of actual work input to ideal work input
B Ratio of ideal work input to actual work input
C Ratio of actual pressure ratio to ideal temperature ratio
D Ratio of exit kinetic energy to inlet enthalpy
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Isentropic efficiency for a compressor is defined as $\eta_c = w_{ideal} / w_{actual}$ because real work is higher than ideal work due to losses.
Question 9 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Compressor surge represents an unstable operating limit where work input fails to overcome adverse pressure gradients. Reason (R): Reverse flow occurs when downstream static pressure exceeds compressor pressure delivery capability.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Surge is an aerodynamic instability caused by severe adverse pressure gradients leading to flow reversal, fully explained by reason R.
Question 10 • conceptual
In a dual-spool turbofan engine, what drives the low-pressure compressor (LPC)?
A High-pressure turbine via a concentric outer shaft
B Low-pressure turbine via an inner concentric shaft
C Accessory gear box directly
D Ram air turbine (RAT)
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The low-pressure turbine extracts energy to drive the low-pressure compressor and fan via the inner coaxial shaft.
Question 11 • numerical
If a compressor consumes $2 \, MW$ of power at a mass flow rate of $10 \, kg/s$, what is the specific work input?
A $20 \, kJ/kg$
B $200 \, kJ/kg$
C $2000 \, kJ/kg$
D $20,000 \, kJ/kg$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
$w = \dot{W} / \dot{m} = 2,000,000 \, W / 10 \, kg/s = 200,000 \, J/kg = 200 \, kJ/kg$.
Question 12 • statement
Which factors contribute to aerodynamic losses reducing turbine stage work output? 1. Blade tip clearance leakage 2. Profile drag and boundary layer separation 3. Shock wave losses in transonic/supersonic blading Select the correct answer using the code below:
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2, and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Tip leakage, profile boundary layer friction, and shock waves all contribute to irreversibilities, reducing actual turbine work extraction.

For an adiabatic compressor, how does the actual work input compare to the ideal isentropic work input?
A Actual work is equal to ideal work
B Actual work is less than ideal work
C Actual work is greater than ideal work due to aerodynamic irreversibilities

Rationale:
Friction and turbulence require more work input to achieve the same pressure ratio, making actual work greater than ideal work.
Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
The power produced by a gas turbine is primarily determined by:
A The difference between turbine work output and compressor work input
B The sum of compressor and turbine work
C The fuel lower heating value alone
D The ambient static pressure
Rationale:
Net work output of a gas turbine Brayton cycle is $W_{net} = W_{turbine} - W_{compressor}$.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
What happens to the stagnation temperature of a gas across an adiabatic turbine stage?
A Increases
B Decreases
C Remains constant
D Drops to absolute zero
Rationale:
Because work is extracted from the fluid by the turbine, enthalpy and stagnation temperature decrease.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
In Euler's turbine equation, if the swirl velocity $V_\theta$ increases across a rotor, the work interaction is:
A Positive work output (turbine action)
B Negative work input (compressor action)
C Zero work
D Infinite work
Rationale:
An increase in tangential angular momentum across a rotor signifies energy being added to the fluid, characteristic of a compressor.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the typical range of isentropic efficiency for modern aircraft gas turbine compressors?
A $50\% - 60\%$
B $70\% - 75\%$
C $85\% - 92\%$
D $98\% - 100\%$
Rationale:
Modern multi-stage axial compressors achieve high aerodynamic refinement with isentropic efficiencies between $85\%$ and $92\%$.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
How does an increase in ambient inlet temperature affect compressor work required for a fixed corrected mass flow?
A Decreases compressor work
B Increases compressor work for the same pressure ratio
C Has no effect on specific work
D Causes immediate compressor stall
Rationale:
Since $w = c_p (T_{02} - T_{01})$, a higher inlet temperature $T_{01}$ at the same temperature ratio requires higher absolute specific work.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
Which non-dimensional parameter relates rotor speed to inlet temperature in compressor performance mapping?
A Mach number
B Corrected speed ($N / \sqrt{\theta}$)
C Reynolds number
D Specific heat ratio
Rationale:
Corrected rotor speed scales rotational speed by the square root of temperature ratio $\theta = T_0 / T_{ref}$ to maintain dynamic similarity.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
Why are multi-stage compressors utilized in high-bypass turbofan engines instead of single-stage centrifugal compressors?
A To achieve high overall pressure ratios efficiently without excessive Mach number shock losses
B To reduce engine weight to zero
C To eliminate the need for turbine work
D To increase fuel viscosity
Rationale:
Multi-stage axial compressors handle high mass flows and achieve high overall pressure ratios efficiently, crucial for modern turbofans.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
In a gas turbine engine, the high-pressure turbine directly drives which component?
A The fan
B The high-pressure compressor
C The accessory gearbox
D The propeller
Rationale:
The high-pressure turbine (HPT) is coupled to the high-pressure compressor (HPC) via the outer coaxial spool.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
What thermodynamic property remains constant across an ideal throttling valve, distinguishing it from compressor work?
A Entropy
B Stagnation enthalpy
C Static pressure
D Temperature
Rationale:
Throttling is an isenthalpic process ($h_1 = h_2$) with zero work, whereas compressors involve explicit shaft work transfer.
