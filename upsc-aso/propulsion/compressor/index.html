Compressor

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Cockpit Engine Indicating and Crew Alerting System (EICAS) monitors compressor speed ($N_1$, $N_2$), Interstage Turbine Temperature (ITT), and compressor discharge pressure ($P_{3}$).
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Compressor surge and stall margins dictate takeoff thrust limitations, maximum altitude dispatch, and engine handling under crosswinds.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 mandate rigorous borescope inspections, cyclic life limits, and non-destructive testing of compressor blades.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers analyze FDR compressor vibration spikes, surge signatures, and blade foreign object damage (FOD) during uncontained engine failure investigations.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

An axial or centrifugal gas turbine compressor is a continuous-flow aerodynamic machine designed to raise the stagnation pressure and temperature of working fluid air through mechanical work input: $W_{in} = \dot{m}(h_{02} - h_{01})$.
  • Physical Meaning: It converts shaft work from the turbine into kinetic and potential energy of the gas stream.
  • Governing Law: Conservation of angular momentum (Euler's turbine equation) and First Law of Thermodynamics for open systems.
  • Continuum Boundary: Operates strictly within the continuum fluid mechanics domain where Knudsen number $Kn \ll 0.01$.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\dot{m}$ = Mass flow rate ($kg/s$)
  • $h_0$ = Stagnation enthalpy ($J/kg$)
  • $P_0$ = Stagnation pressure ($Pa$)
  • $T_0$ = Stagnation temperature ($K$)
  • $\pi_c$ = Compressor total-to-total pressure ratio (Dimensionless)
  • $\eta_{is}$ = Isentropic efficiency (Dimensionless)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: High-speed rotor blades impact air molecules, transferring kinetic energy and increasing absolute molecular velocity and random thermal motion.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Stator blade passages act as diffuser channels, converting high kinetic energy into static pressure via adverse pressure gradients.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Progressive stages compound pressure ratios, establishing the dense high-pressure air required for optimal combustion chamber stoichiometry.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Euler Work Equation

Relates shaft work to angular momentum changes across rotor blades.

$$W_c = \dot{m}(U_2 V_{\theta2} - U_1 V_{\theta1})$$
Step 2: Thermodynamic Enthalpy Relation

Equates work input to stagnation enthalpy rise across the stage.

$$h_{02} - h_{01} = U_2 V_{\theta2} - U_1 V_{\theta1}$$
Step 3: Isentropic Efficiency Formulation

Compares actual enthalpy change to ideal isentropic enthalpy change.

$$\eta_{is} = \frac{h_{02s} - h_{01}}{h_{02} - h_{01}} = \frac{T_{02s} - T_{01}}{T_{02} - T_{01}}$$
Step 4: Final Pressure Ratio Law

Expresses compressor pressure ratio as a function of temperature and efficiency.

$$\pi_c = \left[1 + \eta_{is}\left(\frac{T_{02}}{T_{01}} - 1\right)\right]^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Pressure ratio is dimensionless $[M^0 L^0 T^0]$. Mass flow rate has dimensions $[M L^{-1} T^{-1}]$.
  • SI Unit: Pressure ratio is dimensionless. Compressor work per unit mass is measured in Joules per kilogram ($J/kg$ or $m^2/s^2$).
  • CGS Unit: Enthalpy change in ergs per gram ($erg/g$). Pressure in baryes ($dyn/cm^2$).
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Pressure in pounds per square inch ($psi$). Mass flow in pounds per second ($lb/s$). Temperature in Rankine ($^\circ R$).

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'C' and ICAO Annex 8 Airworthiness of Aircraft govern compressor overhaul schedules and life-limited parts tracking.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Compressor bleed air systems supply vital pneumatic pressure for cabin pressurization, wing anti-icing, and air-conditioning packs.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: Uncontained compressor rotor failure leading to severed hydraulic lines and loss of flight controls, prompting mandatory high-pressure compressor (HPC) inspection ADs.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Isentropic vs Polytropic Efficiency

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming compressor isentropic efficiency remains constant regardless of overall pressure ratio.

✅ UPSC Truth: Polytropic efficiency accounts for stage efficiency independently of pressure ratio, whereas stage isentropic efficiency decreases as overall pressure ratio increases due to reheating.

Trap 2: Compressor Surge vs Stator Stall

❌ Examiner Trap: Equating local blade stall with full engine compressor surge.

✅ UPSC Truth: Blade stall is a localized flow separation on individual airfoil sections, whereas compressor surge is a complete breakdown of compressor flow resulting in flow reversal and loud detonations.

Trap 3: Work Input Sign Convention

❌ Examiner Trap: Treating compressor work as positive output work like a turbine.

✅ UPSC Truth: Compressors require external shaft work input; hence work is supplied to the system ($W_{in} > 0$), increasing stagnation enthalpy.

Trap 4: Mach Number Limits in Axial Compressors

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming axial compressor relative inlet velocities are strictly subsonic throughout all stages.

✅ UPSC Truth: Modern transonic and supersonic compressors deliberately incorporate supersonic relative inlet velocities in front stages to achieve higher pressure ratios per stage.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An axial compressor operating with ambient air at ISA sea level conditions processes air at a total pressure ratio of $12:1$. If the isentropic efficiency of the compressor is $85\%$, calculate the compressor exit stagnation temperature.

Given Flight Parameters:
Inlet stagnation temperature $T_{01} = 288.15 \, K$, Pressure ratio $\pi_c = 12$, Isentropic efficiency $\eta_{is} = 0.85$, Ratio of specific heats $\gamma = 1.4$.
Governing Formula:
$$T_{02s} = T_{01} \cdot \pi_c^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}}, \quad T_{02} = T_{01} + \frac{T_{02s} - T_{01}}{\eta_{is}}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute isentropic exit temperature: $T_{02s} = 288.15 \cdot (12)^{\frac{1.4 - 1}{1.4}} = 288.15 \cdot (12)^{0.2857} = 288.15 \cdot 2.071 = 596.77 \, K$. Step 2: Apply isentropic efficiency to find actual exit temperature: $T_{02} = 288.15 + \frac{596.77 - 288.15}{0.85} = 288.15 + \frac{308.62}{0.85} = 288.15 + 363.08 = 651.23 \, K$. Step 3: Verify temperature rise across compressor stages: $\Delta T_0 = 651.23 - 288.15 = 363.08 \, K$.
Final Answer: $651.23 \, K$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

High compressor exit temperatures directly impact turbine blade thermal loading; monitoring $T_03$ prevents over-temperature creep failures and catastrophic turbine distress.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary aerodynamic function of stator blades in an axial flow compressor?
A To accelerate the air and impart angular momentum
B To diffuse the air and convert kinetic energy into static pressure
C To extract work from the gas stream to drive the fan
D To provide structural rigidity without influencing airflow
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Stator blades act as stationary diffuser passages that slow down the airflow exiting the rotating blades, converting dynamic head into static pressure rise.
Question 2 • conceptual
Which phenomenon represents complete breakdown of stable compressor operation accompanied by flow reversal?
A Boundary layer suction
B Compressor surge
C Choked mass flow
D Supersonic diffusion
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Compressor surge is a system-level instability where the entire compressor can no longer sustain the downstream pressure, resulting in violent flow reversal and loud bangs.
Question 3 • conceptual
In a centrifugal compressor, where does the majority of static pressure rise occur?
A Inlet guide vanes
B Impeller eye
C Impeller vanes and diffuser
D Turbine nozzle guide vanes
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The centrifugal impeller imparts high kinetic energy via centrifugal force, which is subsequently converted into static pressure in the diffuser section.
Question 4 • conceptual
How does Bleed Air extraction affect the operating line of an axial compressor?
A Moves the operating line closer to the surge line
B Moves the operating line away from the surge line
C Has no effect on surge margin
D Completely eliminates compressor choking
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Bleeding air from intermediate stages reduces mass flow through rear stages, lowering their incidence angle and shifting the operating line away from the surge boundary.
Question 5 • conceptual
What is the purpose of Variable Inlet Guide Vanes (VIGVs) in modern multi-stage axial compressors?
A To increase fuel injection pressure
B To optimize the angle of attack on front stage rotor blades across different RPMs
C To cool the combustion chamber liners
D To bypass air around the core engine
Solution & Technical Rationale:
VIGVs modulate the swirl angle entering the first rotor stage, preventing off-design stall and maintaining high compressor efficiency across throttle settings.
Question 6 • conceptual
Why do modern high-bypass turbofans utilize dual-spool (twin-spool) compressors?
A To allow low-pressure and high-pressure spools to rotate at their optimal aerodynamic speeds
B To reduce the overall engine weight to zero
C To eliminate the need for fuel pumps
D To prevent turbine blade cooling requirements
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Twin-spool designs decouple the fan/LP compressor from the HP compressor, allowing each to operate at its ideal rotational speed without aerodynamic compromise.
Question 7 • conceptual
What occurs when an axial compressor stage reaches 'choked' condition?
A Mass flow rate reaches a maximum value limited by sonic velocity in throat areas
B Airflow velocity drops to zero instantly
C Pressure ratio drops to unity
D Compressor efficiency increases to 100%
Solution & Technical Rationale:
When blade passage throats reach Mach 1, mass flow chokes, meaning further downstream pressure increases cannot augment ingested mass flow.
Question 8 • statement-based
Consider the following statements regarding gas turbine compressors: 1. Polytropic efficiency is independent of overall pressure ratio. 2. Isentropic efficiency of a stage decreases as overall pressure ratio increases due to reheating. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct aero-thermodynamic principles. Polytropic efficiency evaluates infinitesimal stage efficiency, whereas cumulative isentropic efficiency suffers from reheat effects.
Question 9 • statement-based
Consider the following statements about centrifugal vs axial compressors: 1. Centrifugal compressors offer higher pressure ratio per single stage. 2. Axial compressors provide greater total mass flow handling capability for a given frontal area. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Centrifugal compressors achieve high pressure ratios (4:1 to 10:1) per stage due to centrifugal radius change, while axial compressors excel in high continuous mass flow throughput.
Question 10 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Compressor blade stall typically originates at the blade tips or root junctions due to adverse pressure gradients. Reason (R): Boundary layer separation is promoted by strong adverse pressure gradients combined with centrifugal secondary flows.
A Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true, but R is false
D A is false, but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Adverse pressure gradients in diffusers slow boundary layers, and secondary flows at hub/tip corners trigger flow separation and blade stall.
Question 11 • numerical
An axial compressor has an inlet stagnation temperature of $300 \, K$ and an exit stagnation temperature of $600 \, K$. If $\gamma = 1.4$ and isentropic efficiency is $0.80$, what is the actual pressure ratio?
A 8.12
B 11.31
C 14.52
D 18.20
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $\Delta T_{is} = \eta_{is} \Delta T = 0.80 \times (600 - 300) = 240 \, K$, $T_{02s} = 540 \, K$. Pressure ratio $\pi_c = (540/300)^{\frac{1.4}{0.4}} = (1.8)^{3.5} = 11.31$.
Question 12 • numerical
Calculate the work input per unit mass ($J/kg$) for an axial compressor where stagnation enthalpy rises from $h_{01} = 290 \, kJ/kg$ to $h_{02} = 580 \, kJ/kg$.
A 145 kJ/kg
B 290 kJ/kg
C 435 kJ/kg
D 870 kJ/kg
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Work input $W_c = h_{02} - h_{01} = 580 - 290 = 290 \, kJ/kg = 290,000 \, J/kg$.

What is the primary operational hazard associated with operating a gas turbine compressor deep within the stall regime?
A Excessive fuel flow to the burners
B Severe combustion chamber acoustic resonance and rapid turbine overheating
C Increased bypass ratio
D Excessive generator electrical output
Rationale:
Stall and surge disrupt airflow to the combustor, causing flameouts, severe EGT spikes, and structural thermal distress.
Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
In compressor performance maps, what does the 'surge line' separate?
A Stable operating regions from unstable surge regions
B Subsonic flow from supersonic flow regions
C Wet bulb temperature from dry bulb temperature
D Takeoff power from idle power settings
Rationale:
The surge line is the locus of operating points where compressor instability begins; operating to the left of this line induces surge.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
How do Inlet Guide Vanes (IGVs) pre-swirl the incoming air into an axial compressor rotor?
A In the direction of rotor rotation, reducing relative Mach number
B Opposite to rotor rotation, increasing aerodynamic loading
C Perpendicular to rotor rotation
D IGVs do not affect air swirl
Rationale:
Pre-swirling air in the direction of rotor rotation reduces the relative inlet Mach number and blade incidence angle at high corrected speeds.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
What thermodynamic parameter remains constant across an ideal, frictionless, adiabatic compressor stage?
A Stagnation Entropy
B Static Pressure
C Stagnation Temperature
D Static Temperature
Rationale:
An ideal isentropic process is reversible and adiabatic, meaning stagnation entropy remains constant ($\Delta s = 0$).
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
Why are centrifugal compressors predominantly used in small auxiliary power units (APUs) and helicopter turboshafts rather than large commercial turbofans?
A They provide lower pressure ratios per stage
B They have a larger frontal area relative to mass flow capacity
C They cannot ingest air
D They require excessive turbine work
Rationale:
Centrifugal compressors have a large radial diameter (frontal area) relative to mass flow, making them impractical for high-thrust commercial turbofans.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
What is 'reheat factor' in multi-stage axial compressors?
A The ratio of cumulative isentropic enthalpy change to ideal overall enthalpy change
B The ratio of fuel burned in the compressor
C The ratio of static pressure to dynamic pressure
D The ratio of compressor RPM to turbine RPM
Rationale:
Due to inefficiencies, air temperature rises more than isentropically; cumulative stage enthalpy drops exceed overall enthalpy drop, giving a reheat factor greater than 1.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
During a compressor stall, what immediate cockpit indication is typically observed on multi-engine transport aircraft?
A Rapid rise in EGT, vibration spike, and momentary thrust loss
B Immediate engine shutdown and fire bottle discharge
C Increase in hydraulic pressure
D Drop in cabin altitude
Rationale:
Surge and stall manifest as sharp vibration, loud popping noises, exhaust gas temperature (EGT) spikes, and loss of thrust.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
Which aerodynamic design feature on compressor blades helps delay boundary layer separation under adverse pressure gradients?
A Boundary layer suction or active flow control slots
B Polished mirror finishes with zero camber
C Increasing blade thickness to maximum limits
D Removing all stator vanes
Rationale:
Active flow control and boundary layer suction remove retarded low-energy fluid near blade surfaces, preventing separation.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the primary function of interstage bleed valves on high-pressure compressors during engine start and low-power transients?
A To bleed off excess air and prevent front-stage choke and rear-stage stall
B To supply fuel to the afterburner
C To cool the engine cowl
D To lubricate main shaft bearings
Rationale:
Bleeding air from middle stages adjusts mass flow matching between front and rear stages during off-design startup conditions, avoiding surge.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
If a compressor's rotational speed is increased while maintaining constant inlet temperature, how does corrected mass flow behave?
A Increases until choking occurs
B Decreases to zero
C Remains strictly constant
D Oscillates sinusoidally
Rationale:
Corrected mass flow increases with RPM until the throat chokes, after which it remains constant (choked mass flow).
