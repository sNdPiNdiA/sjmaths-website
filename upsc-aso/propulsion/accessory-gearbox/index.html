Accessory gearbox

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Drives the Integrated Drive Generator (IDG), Permanent Magnet Alternator (PMA), hydraulic pumps, and engine fuel metering units.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Failure of the accessory gearbox causes immediate loss of primary electrical generation and hydraulic pressure, triggering emergency checklist protocols.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Governed by DGCA CAR Section 2 Series 'B' Part VII and ICAO Annex 8 regarding continued airworthiness and engine accessory mounting pad security.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Investigators inspect magnetic chip detectors, scavenge screens, and spur gear tooth pitting during uncontained engine failure root-cause analysis.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

The accessory gearbox (AGB) is a mechanical gear train driven by the high-pressure (HP) compressor rotor via a radial driveshaft, providing mechanical power extraction for essential aircraft accessories. The mechanical power transfer is governed by the relation $P_{AGB} = \tau \cdot \omega$, where $\tau$ is the transmitted torque and $\omega$ is the rotational speed.
  • Physical Meaning: It steps down high turbine rotational speeds to safe, usable torque limits required by mounted accessories.
  • Governing Law: Conservation of mechanical energy and gear train velocity ratios ($N_1/N_2 = D_2/D_1 = T_2/T_1$).
  • Continuum Boundary: Operates under boundary and hydrodynamic lubrication regimes within a sealed magnesium or aluminum alloy housing.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $P_{AGB}$ = Power extracted by the accessory gearbox (Watts or horsepower)
  • $\tau$ = Gear shaft transmitted torque (Newton-meters, $N\cdot m$)
  • $\omega$ = Angular velocity of the driven accessory pad (Radians per second, $rad/s$)
  • $N$ = Rotational speed of the gear wheel (Revolutions per minute, $RPM$)
  • $GR$ = Gear ratio of the internal gear train (Dimensionless)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Mechanical Power Extraction: A bevel gear mounted on the engine HP spool (N2 shaft) meshes with a radial drive shaft transferring rotational kinetic energy outward through the nacelle core.
2. Speed Reduction & Torque Conversion: Internal spur and helical gear sets systematically alter angular velocity to match specific design RPM limits for fuel pumps, generators, and hydraulic units.
3. Accessory Drive Distribution: Multiple dynamic mounting pads split the mechanical torque across various independent accessory shafts operating at synchronized gear ratios.
4. Lubrication & Thermal Dissipation: Internal oil jet nozzles continuously spray pressurized oil to cool gear tooth contacts and flush wear debris toward scavenging magnetic chip detectors.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

The fundamental power balance equating turbine core extraction to accessory load demands plus internal frictional losses.

$$P_{extracted} = \sum P_{accessories} + P_{friction}$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Relating angular speed and torque for any individual gear stage within the accessory gearbox housing train.

$$P_{in} = \tau_{in} \omega_{in} = \tau_{out} \omega_{out} \cdot \eta_{mech}$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Expressing the output pad speed using the mechanical gear ratio ($GR$) defined by tooth count ($Z$).

$$N_{pad} = \frac{N_{HP}}{GR} = N_{HP} \left( \frac{Z_{driver}}{Z_{driven}} \right)$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Calculating total accessory drag torque imposed back onto the main engine high-pressure compressor rotor.

$$\tau_{engine} = \sum \left( \frac{\tau_{acc, i}}{\eta_{mech, i} \cdot GR_i} ight)$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Power has dimensions $[M L^2 T^{-3}]$, torque has $[M L^2 T^{-2}]$, and rotational speed has $[T^{-1}]$.
  • SI Unit: Power is measured in Watts ($W$), torque in Newton-meters ($N\cdot m$), and rotational speed in radians per second ($rad/s$ or $RPM$).
  • CGS Unit: Power in Ergs per second ($erg/s$), torque in Dyne-centimeters ($dyne\cdot cm$).
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Mechanical horsepower ($hp$), foot-pounds ($ft\cdot lb$), and revolutions per minute ($RPM$) are universally used in aviation maintenance manuals.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Mandates rigorous non-destructive testing (NDT) of internal gears and radial driveshafts per FAA/DGCA approved Airworthiness Limitations Sections (ALS).
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Directly supports FADEC power via PMAs, flight control actuation via engine-driven hydraulic pumps, and fuel pressurization.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: British Airways Flight 38 experienced dual engine rollbacks due to ice accumulation in the fuel-oil heat exchanger, highlighting vulnerabilities in accessory-driven fuel systems.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: AGB Driven by Low-Pressure Spool

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming the accessory gearbox is driven by the low-pressure (N1) compressor shaft.

✅ UPSC Truth: The AGB is driven exclusively by the high-pressure (N2 or N3) compressor spool to maintain adequate accessory idle speeds during low N1 ground operations.

Trap 2: Constant Speed Output

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing that accessory output speed remains constant regardless of engine throttle setting.

✅ UPSC Truth: AGB accessory speeds vary directly with engine core RPM (N2), requiring Constant Speed Drives (CSDs) only for electrical generators.

Trap 3: Lubrication Integration

❌ Examiner Trap: Thinking the AGB utilizes a completely independent fluid lubrication tank separate from the engine.

✅ UPSC Truth: Modern turbofan AGBs typically share a common or scavenged circulating lubrication system with the main engine bearing compartments.

Trap 4: Radial Drive Shaft Function

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming the radial driveshaft transfers thrust forces from the turbine blades to the airframe.

✅ UPSC Truth: The radial driveshaft transmits pure torsional mechanical power outward through the compressor casing, bearing zero axial thrust loads.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A turbofan engine high-pressure spool rotates at $12,000 RPM$. The accessory gearbox uses a bevel gear ratio of $2:1$ from the tower shaft to the main gear train, and a spur gear set with a ratio of $3:1$ driving a hydraulic pump. Calculate the operating speed of the hydraulic pump.

Given Flight Parameters:
High-pressure spool speed ($N_{HP}$) = $12,000 RPM$; Bevel gear ratio ($GR_1$) = $2:1$ (step-down); Spur gear ratio ($GR_2$) = $3:1$ (step-down).
Governing Formula:
$N_{pump} = \frac{N_{HP}}{GR_1 \cdot GR_2}$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute intermediate tower shaft speed: $N_{tower} = \frac{12000}{2} = 6000 RPM$. Step 2: Compute final hydraulic pump speed: $N_{pump} = \frac{6000}{3} = 2000 RPM$. Step 3: Verify dimensional consistency and mechanical limits.
Final Answer: $2,000 RPM$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Ensuring proper gear reduction ratios prevents hydraulic pump cavitation and overheating during high-power takeoff conditions.

Question 1 • conceptual
Which engine component directly drives the accessory gearbox radial driveshaft in a modern high-bypass turbofan?
A Low-pressure turbine (LPT) shaft
B High-pressure (HP) compressor rotor spool
C Fan spinner cone assembly
D Accessory driven starter motor
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The AGB is mechanically coupled to the high-pressure compressor rotor (N2/N3) to ensure adequate accessory speeds during ground idle operations.
Question 2 • conceptual
What is the primary purpose of magnetic chip detectors installed within the accessory gearbox scavenge line?
A To filter out fuel impurities
B To generate electrical power for the FADEC
C To trap ferrous wear particles indicating gear or bearing distress
D To regulate oil temperature prior to entering the cooler
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Magnetic chip detectors attract ferrous metallic debris suspended in the lubricating oil, serving as an early indicator of gear or bearing spalling.
Question 3 • statement-based
Consider the following statements regarding the accessory gearbox: 1. It extracts mechanical power from the engine core. 2. All mounted accessories rotate at the exact same RPM. 3. It houses the engine lube and scavenge pumps. Which of the statements given above are correct?
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2, and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 and 3 are correct. Statement 2 is false because different accessories require specific gear ratios and operate at varied rotational speeds.
Question 4 • numerical
If an engine HP spool operates at $15,000 RPM$ and the total gear reduction ratio between the HP spool and the fuel pump pad is $4:1$, what is the fuel pump operating speed?
A $60,000 RPM$
B $3,750 RPM$
C $7,500 RPM$
D $3,000 RPM$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Pump speed = $15,000 / 4 = 3,750 RPM$.
Question 5 • assertion-reason
Assertion (A): The accessory gearbox is mounted on the fan casing or core cowl rather than directly on the hot turbine section. Reason (R): Mounting on cooler external casing areas protects sensitive accessory components and lubricants from extreme turbine section thermal degradation.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct and R provides the correct physical justification for placing the AGB away from hot turbine sections.
Question 6 • conceptual
Which of the following accessories is typically NOT mounted on the accessory gearbox pad?
A Integrated Drive Generator (IDG)
B Engine oil scavenge pump
C Low-pressure compressor booster stage
D Permanent Magnet Alternator (PMA)
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The LP compressor booster stage is an internal aerodynamic component of the core compressor, not an accessory mounted on the external gearbox.
Question 7 • conceptual
What safety mechanism prevents catastrophic damage to the engine core if an accessory (such as a hydraulic pump) suffers a mechanical seizure?
A A thermal circuit breaker in the flight deck
B A shear section built into the accessory drive shaft
C An automatic fuel shutoff valve
D A bypass check valve in the hydraulic reservoir
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A sacrificial shear neck on the accessory drive shaft is designed to snap cleanly under excessive torque, decoupling the seized accessory from the running engine core.
Question 8 • numerical
An accessory gearbox transmits a total mechanical power of $120 kW$ from the engine core. If internal gear train mechanical efficiency is $95\%$, what is the total power delivered to the mounted accessories?
A $126.3 kW$
B $120.0 kW$
C $114.0 kW$
D $100.0 kW$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Delivered power = $120 kW \times 0.95 = 114.0 kW$.
Question 9 • statement-based
Consider the following statements regarding AGB lubrication: 1. Lubrication oil is supplied under pressure from the engine oil tank. 2. Scavenge pumps return oil from the AGB sump back to the main tank. 3. Grease packed bearings are used exclusively for all high-speed AGB gear shafts. Which of the statements given above are correct?
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2, and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is false because high-speed AGB bearings require continuous pressure oil jet lubrication, not sealed grease packing.
Question 10 • conceptual
Why do constant speed drives (CSDs) or IDGs require integrated oil coolers connected to the fuel or air system?
A To pre-heat fuel before combustion
B To dissipate thermal energy generated by hydraulic slip and mechanical gear friction
C To prevent the hydraulic fluid from freezing at sea level
D To pressurize the aircraft cabin bleed air
Solution & Technical Rationale:
CSDs and IDGs generate significant heat due to mechanical power conversion losses, requiring dedicated fuel-oil or air-oil heat exchangers.
Question 11 • assertion-reason
Assertion (A): A failure of the accessory gearbox radial driveshaft results in an immediate engine flameout. Reason (R): The radial driveshaft transmits core rotational energy to the fuel control unit and fuel pumps mounted on the AGB.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. A radial driveshaft failure cuts off fuel delivery from the AGB-mounted fuel pump, starving the combustion chambers.
Question 12 • numerical
An accessory drive shaft transmits $50 kW$ of power at a rotational speed of $3,000 RPM$. Calculate the transmitted torque in Newton-meters ($N\cdot m$).
A $106.1 N\cdot m$
B $159.2 N\cdot m$
C $318.4 N\cdot m$
D $500.0 N\cdot m$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Angular speed $\omega = 3000 \times 2\pi / 60 = 314.16 rad/s$. Torque $\tau = P / \omega = 50,000 / 314.16 = 159.2 N\cdot m$.

Rationale:
Synthetic turbine oils designed for high-temperature stability lubricate both the engine bearings and the accessory gearbox gears.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
If an accessory gearbox experiences total loss of lubrication during flight, what is the immediate primary hazard?
A Immediate fuel freezing
B Extreme gear tooth friction, rapid temperature spike, and possible shaft seizure

Rationale:
Loss of lubrication causes severe frictional heating, gear welding, and potential uncontained mechanical failure.

A To determine fuel octane ratings

Inspecting chip detectors reveals whether mechanical wear debris contributed to the failure chain.
