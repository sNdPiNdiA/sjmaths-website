Axial & centrifugal compressors

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Cockpit Engine Indicating and Crew Alerting System (EICAS) monitors compressor speed ($N_1, N_2$), inter-turbine temperature, and compressor discharge pressure ($P_{3}$).
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Uncontained compressor failures or severe surge events compromise aircraft pressurization, bleed air supply, and total thrust generation during critical climb phases.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 Series 'B' Part VII and ICAO Annex 8 mandate strict borescope inspections and life-limited parts tracking for compressor discs.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: ASO investigators examine compressor blade metallurgic fatigue, FOD (Foreign Object Damage) impacts, and bleed valve sequencing during engine incident probes.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Axial and centrifugal compressors are dynamic continuous-flow turbomachines that impart mechanical energy to a working fluid, raising its static pressure and temperature prior to combustion: $W_c = \dot{m}(h_{02} - h_{01})$.
  • Physical Meaning: They continuously convert shaft rotational energy into fluid enthalpy and kinetic energy through rotating blades (rotors) and stationary passages (stators/diffusers).
  • Governing Law: Conservation of angular momentum (Euler's turbine equation) and the steady-flow energy equation dictate pressure and velocity transformations across stages.
  • Continuum Boundary: Fluid behavior operates entirely within the continuum hypothesis, where molecular mean free path is extremely small compared to blade passage dimensions ($Kn \ll 0.01$).

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\dot{m}$ = Mass flow rate through the compressor ($kg/s$)
  • $h_0$ = Stagnation enthalpy ($J/kg$)
  • $U$ = Blade tangential velocity ($m/s$)
  • $V$ = Absolute fluid velocity ($m/s$)
  • $W$ = Relative fluid velocity ($m/s$)
  • $\Pi_c$ = Total pressure ratio ($P_{02}/P_{01}$)
  • $\eta_is$ = Isentropic compressor efficiency (Dimensionless)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: High-speed rotor blades impact air molecules, transferring kinetic energy and sharply increasing both translational velocity and internal thermal agitation.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Divergent blade passages and static diffuser vanes decelerate the high-speed flow, converting dynamic head into adverse static pressure gradients via the Bernoulli principle.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Boundary layer growth on blade suction surfaces must be controlled via proper diffusion limits to prevent flow separation, stall, and catastrophic surge.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Euler's Turbine Equation

Relates torque to the change in angular momentum across the rotor.

$$\tau = \dot{m}(r_2 V_{\theta 2} - r_1 V_{\theta 1})$$
Step 2: Specific Work Input

Multiplying torque by angular velocity $\omega$ yields specific work per unit mass.

$$w_c = \omega (r_2 V_{\theta 2} - r_1 V_{\theta 1}) = U_2 V_{\theta 2} - U_1 V_{\theta 1}$$
Step 3: Enthalpy Stagnation Change

Relating velocity triangles to static and stagnation enthalpy changes.

$$h_{02} - h_{01} = \frac{1}{2}(U_2^2 - U_1^2) + \frac{1}{2}(W_1^2 - W_2^2) + \frac{1}{2}(V_2^2 - V_1^2)$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Expressing ideal total pressure ratio using isentropic relations.

$$\Pi_c = \left[1 + \eta_{is} \left(\frac{T_{02s} - T_{01}}{T_{01}}\right)\right]^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Mass flow rate $[M T^{-1}]$, Pressure ratio $[M^0 L^0 T^0]$, Rotational speed $[T^{-1}]$, Work per unit mass $[L^2 T^{-2}]$.
  • SI Unit: Pressure in Pascal ($Pa = kg / (m \cdot s^2)$), Mass flow in $kg/s$, Specific work in $J/kg$.
  • CGS Unit: Pressure in $dyn/cm^2$, Mass flow in $g/s$, Specific work in $erg/g$.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Pressure in $psi$ ($lb/in^2$), Mass flow in $slugs/s$ or $lb/s$, RPM in $rev/min$.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2 Series C mandates approved maintenance schedules, while ICAO Annex 8 sets international airworthiness standards for turbine engine containment.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Compressor bleed air feeds environmental control systems (ECS), wing anti-ice, and engine cowl anti-ice valves; failure risks unmetered hot air leaks.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: British Airways Flight 38 (Boeing 777) experienced dual engine rollbacks due to fuel icing in the fuel-oil heat exchangers, halting fuel flow to the compressor face during final approach.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Axial vs. Centrifugal Pressure Rise

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming a single axial stage achieves a higher pressure ratio than a centrifugal stage.

✅ UPSC Truth: A single centrifugal stage can achieve pressure ratios up to 10:1 due to strong centrifugal acceleration, whereas an axial stage is limited to 1.3:1 due to boundary layer limits.

Trap 2: Surge vs. Stagnation

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing compressor surge with localized blade stall.

✅ UPSC Truth: Blade stall is localized flow separation on individual airfoils; surge is a complete breakdown of compressor operation causing flow reversal and violent engine rumble.

Trap 3: Work Input Sign Conventions

❌ Examiner Trap: Treating compressor work as positive output work like a turbine.

✅ UPSC Truth: Compressors require external shaft work input; hence work done on the fluid is negative in standard thermodynamic control volume energy balances unless explicitly defined as work input ($W_{in}$).

Trap 4: Mach Number Limits

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming axial compressor relative inlet velocities are strictly subsonic.

✅ UPSC Truth: Modern transonic and supersonic axial compressors purposefully utilize supersonic relative velocities at outer blade spans to maximize mass flow and pressure ratio.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An axial compressor stage handles air at an inlet stagnation temperature of $T_{01} = 288 K$ with an isentropic total pressure ratio of $\Pi_c = 1.35$. If the isentropic efficiency is $\eta_{is} = 0.88$ and ratio of specific heats $\gamma = 1.4$, calculate the actual exit stagnation temperature $T_{02}$.

Given Flight Parameters:
Inlet stagnation temperature $T_{01} = 288 K$, Pressure ratio $\Pi_c = 1.35$, Efficiency $\eta_{is} = 0.88$, $\gamma = 1.4$.
Governing Formula:
$T_{02s} = T_{01} \cdot \Pi_c^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}}$ and $T_{02} = T_{01} + \frac{T_{02s} - T_{01}}{\eta_{is}}$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Calculate isentropic exit temperature: $T_{02s} = 288 \times (1.35)^{\frac{0.4}{1.4}} = 288 \times (1.35)^{0.2857} = 288 \times 1.0931 = 314.81 K$. Step 2: Apply isentropic efficiency to find actual temperature rise: $\Delta T_{actual} = \frac{314.81 - 288}{0.88} = \frac{26.81}{0.88} = 30.47 K$. Step 3: Compute actual exit stagnation temperature: $T_{02} = 288 + 30.47 = 318.47 K$.
Final Answer: $318.47 K$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate calculation of compressor discharge temperature is vital for predicting turbine thermal stress limits and preventing over-temperature trips during high-thrust operations.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary aerodynamic mechanism responsible for pressure rise in a centrifugal compressor impeller?
A Pure diffusion in stationary stator channels
B Combined centrifugal acceleration and diffusion in rotating impeller passages
C Supersonic shock wave compression at the inlet inducer
D Frictional viscous drag across closely spaced cascade blades
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A centrifugal compressor utilizes both rotational centrifugal body force and diffusion within the impeller channels to achieve high pressure ratios.
Question 2 • statement-based
Consider the following statements regarding axial and centrifugal compressors: 1. Axial compressors handle significantly higher mass flow rates for a given frontal area. 2. Centrifugal compressors provide higher pressure ratio capability per individual stage. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Axial compressors excel in high mass flow for small frontal area (ideal for large turbofans), while centrifugal compressors achieve high single-stage pressure ratios.
Question 3 • numerical
An axial compressor stage has a stagnation temperature rise of $25 K$. If the entering stagnation temperature is $300 K$ and $\gamma = 1.4$, what is the ideal isentropic temperature rise for a stage efficiency of $0.85$?
A 21.25 K
B 25.00 K
C 29.41 K
D 31.25 K
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Isentropic efficiency is defined as $\eta_{is} = \Delta T_{is} / \Delta T_{actual}$. Therefore, $\Delta T_{is} = 0.85 \times 25 K = 21.25 K$.
Question 4 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Compressor surge is a catastrophic operational instability involving flow reversal. Reason (R): Surge occurs when the compressor operates beyond its positive stall limit line due to excessive adverse pressure gradients.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Surge represents complete breakdown of stable compressor operation caused by excessive adverse pressure gradients pushing the operating point past the surge line.
Question 5 • conceptual
Which component in a centrifugal compressor is responsible for converting high kinetic energy leaving the impeller into static pressure?
A Inducer
B Diffuser and vollet/collector
C Bleed valve
D Inlet guide vanes
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The diffuser (vaned or vaneless) downstream of the impeller decelerates the flow, converting velocity head into static pressure.
Question 6 • conceptual
What is the primary function of Variable Inlet Guide Vanes (VIGVs) in an axial compressor?
A To filter foreign object debris from entering the core
B To impart swirl and optimize the angle of attack on first-stage rotor blades across varying RPMs
C To extract bleed air for cabin pressurization
D To cool the turbine blades downstream
Solution & Technical Rationale:
VIGVs modulate inlet air angle to prevent blade stall and maintain optimum aerodynamic incidence angles at off-design speeds.
Question 7 • numerical
Calculate the work done per unit mass for an axial compressor stage where $U_2 V_{\theta 2} = 35000 m^2/s^2$ and $U_1 V_{\theta 1} = 5000 m^2/s^2$.
A 5000 J/kg
B 30000 J/kg
C 35000 J/kg
D 40000 J/kg
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Euler's work equation states $w_c = U_2 V_{\theta 2} - U_1 V_{\theta 1} = 35000 - 5000 = 30000 J/kg$.
Question 8 • statement-based
Which of the following phenomena characterize compressor stall? 1. Detached boundary layer flow on compressor blades. 2. Propagation of rotating stall cells around the annulus. 3. Instantaneous reverse flow through the entire engine core. Select the correct answer:
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2, and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Rotating stall involves localized blade separation and stall cells moving around the annulus, whereas complete reverse flow characterizes full surge.
Question 9 • conceptual
Why are centrifugal compressors rarely used in high-bypass commercial turbofans?
A They have extremely low pressure ratios
B Their large radial diameter creates an excessive frontal area and aerodynamic drag
C They cannot ingest air at supersonic flight speeds
D They require cryogenic cooling
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The large radial envelope of centrifugal compressors increases nacelle drag, making them unsuitable for high-thrust multi-spool turbofan engines.
Question 10 • numerical
If a compressor has a total-to-total isentropic efficiency of $0.85$ and consumes $250 kJ/kg$ of ideal work, what is the actual work input required?
A 212.5 kJ/kg
B 250.0 kJ/kg
C 294.1 kJ/kg
D 312.5 kJ/kg
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Actual work is greater than ideal work: $W_{actual} = W_{ideal} / \eta_{is} = 250 / 0.85 = 294.1 kJ/kg$.
Question 11 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Bleed valves are opened during engine startup and low-throttle transients. Reason (R): Bleed valves vent intermediate stage air to prevent rear-stage choking and front-stage stall.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Bleed valves bleed off excess air during transients to keep front stages operating at positive incidence and rear stages from choking.
Question 12 • conceptual
What defines the 'choke' condition in a compressor stage?
A Fluid velocity reaches Mach 1 in the blade passages, limiting maximum mass flow
B The compressor stops rotating completely
C Fuel flow exceeds the stoichiometric ratio
D Oil pressure drops to zero
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Choke occurs when flow reaches sonic velocity ($M=1$) at the minimum flow area (throat), fixing the maximum mass flow rate.

The slope of the compressor operating line on a map of pressure ratio versus corrected mass flow must remain to the right of which critical boundary?
A Turbine turbine nozzle line
B Compressor surge / stall line
C Choke mass flow limit

Rationale:
Operating too close to or crossing the surge line leads to aerodynamic instability and engine surge.
Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
In an axial compressor stage, the diffusion factor (DF) is used primarily to evaluate:
A Bearing lubrication effectiveness
B Blade boundary layer health and risk of flow separation
C Turbine blade cooling efficiency
D Fuel atomization quality
Rationale:
The diffusion factor quantifies blade loading and deceleration rate to ensure boundary layers do not separate.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the primary advantage of a multi-spool (dual or triple spool) axial compressor configuration?
A Elimination of all gearbox requirements
B Allows each compressor spool to rotate at its optimum aerodynamic speed, widening the operating surge margin
C Reduces total engine weight to zero
D Permits operation without intake cowlings
Rationale:
Multi-spool designs decouple low and high-pressure compressors, allowing each to rotate at peak efficiency and avoiding off-design stall.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
If the rotational speed of a centrifugal compressor is doubled, the theoretical ideal pressure ratio increases by approximately what factor?
A Factor of 2
B Factor of 4
C Factor of 8
D Factor of 16
Rationale:
Centrifugal head is proportional to $U_2^2$. Doubling tip speed $U_2$ quadruples the pressure head.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
During an air safety investigation following an uncontained engine failure, metal splatter on compressor blades indicates:
A Normal operation at cruise altitude
B Upstream ingestion of foreign object debris (FOD)
C Downstream turbine material failure melting and migrating forward
D Excessive lubricating oil pressure
Rationale:
Forward migration of melted turbine material (like single-crystal superalloys) points conclusively to downstream turbine failure.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the purpose of the inducer section in a centrifugal compressor?
A To inject fuel directly into the eye of the compressor
B To smoothly turn incoming axial flow into the radial impeller blades with minimum shock losses
C To decelerate air prior to the combustion chamber
D To bleed high-pressure air for wing anti-ice
Rationale:
The inducer curves the axial flow into the radial blades smoothly, minimizing incidence losses and flow separation.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
Which thermodynamic parameter remains constant across an ideal adiabatic reversible (isentropic) compressor stage?
A Static entropy
B Stagnation entropy
C Static temperature
D Stagnation pressure
Rationale:
An ideal isentropic process is reversible and adiabatic, meaning stagnation entropy remains constant ($ds = 0$). Note that static pressure and temperature increase.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
In aircraft gas turbine condition monitoring, an unexplained rise in exhaust gas temperature (EGT) combined with a drop in compressor discharge pressure ($P_3$) typically indicates:
A Compressor fouling or blade degradation
B Excessive fuel pump pressure
C Blocked fuel nozzles
D Over-efficiency of the compressor
Rationale:
Compressor fouling reduces efficiency and pressure ratio ($P_3$), forcing the turbine to work harder, which raises EGT.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
The degree of reaction in an axial compressor stage is defined as the ratio of:
A Static enthalpy rise in the rotor to total static enthalpy rise in the stage
B Kinetic energy to potential energy
C Actual work to ideal work
D Inlet velocity to exit velocity
Rationale:
Degree of reaction measures the proportion of static pressure rise achieved across the rotating blades versus stationary stators.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
According to DGCA airworthiness guidelines, compressor blade inspections using borescopy are mandatory when:
A The aircraft completes exactly 10 flight hours
B There is an incident report of hard landing, severe turbulence, or suspected FOD ingestion
C The cabin altitude exceeds 10,000 feet
D Refueling with Jet B instead of Jet A-1 occurs
Rationale:
Borescope inspections are triggered by events like FOD, bird strikes, hard landings, or abnormal vibration parameters.
