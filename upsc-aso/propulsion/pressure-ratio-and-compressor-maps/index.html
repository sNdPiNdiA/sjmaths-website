Pressure ratio & compressor maps

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Engine Indicating and Crew Alerturing System (EICAS) displays EPR (Engine Pressure Ratio) or $N_1$/$N_2$ speeds directly reflecting compressor performance.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Exceeding compressor operational limits near the surge line leads to catastrophic flameouts or uncontained engine rotor failures.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 Series 'B' Part VII and ICAO Annex 8 govern turbine engine type certification and operational safety limits.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: ASOs inspect compressor maps and FDR traces for stall signatures, FOD damage patterns, and bleed valve malfunctions during incident audits.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

The compressor total pressure ratio $\pi_c$ is defined as the ratio of compressor discharge total pressure ($P_{t3}$) to compressor inlet total pressure ($P_{t2}$), given by $\pi_c = \frac{P_{t3}}{P_{t2}}$. Compressor maps are graphical performance curves relating corrected mass flow, corrected speed, pressure ratio, and polytropic efficiency.
  • Physical Meaning: Maps characterize unsteady and steady aerodynamic operation across shifting spool speeds.
  • Governing Law: Conservation of mass, momentum, and energy across blade rows combined with gas dynamics.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid down to Knudsen numbers where slip flow regimes begin, typically above high-altitude stagnation.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\pi_c$ = Compressor Total Pressure Ratio (Dimensionless)
  • $P_{t2}$ = Compressor Inlet Stagnation Pressure ($Pa$)
  • $P_{t3}$ = Compressor Discharge Stagnation Pressure ($Pa$)
  • $\dot{m}_{corr}$ = Corrected Mass Flow Rate ($kg/s$)
  • $N_{corr}$ = Corrected Rotor Speed ($RPM$)
  • $\eta_c$ = Compressor Isentropic Efficiency (Dimensionless)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Air parcels enter rotating blade passages where relative kinetic energy transforms into static enthalpy and pressure.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Adverse pressure gradients develop along the suction surfaces of stator and rotor airfoils due to flow deceleration.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Excessive pressure ratios without matching rotational speed trigger boundary layer separation, stall, and surge.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Define isentropic stagnation temperature-pressure relation across the compression process.

$$\frac{T_{t3s}}{T_{t2}} = \left(\frac{P_{t3}}{P_{t2}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}}$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Introduce isentropic efficiency $\eta_c$ to relate actual work to ideal work.

$$\eta_c = \frac{T_{t3s} - T_{t2}}{T_{t3} - T_{t2}}$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Substitute ideal temperature ratio into the efficiency expression.

$$T_{t3} = T_{t2} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{\eta_c} \left[ \left(\frac{P_{t3}}{P_{t2}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}} - 1 \right] \right)$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Express compressor work per unit mass as a function of pressure ratio and efficiency.

$$W_c = C_p T_{t2} \left\{ \frac{1}{\eta_c} \left[ \pi_c^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}} - 1 \right] \right\}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Pressure ratio is dimensionless $[M^0 L^0 T^0 \theta^0]$. Corrected mass flow has dimensions of mass per time $[M T^{-1}]$.
  • SI Unit: Pressure ratio is dimensionless; corrected mass flow is expressed in kilograms per second ($kg/s$).
  • CGS Unit: Pressure ratio is dimensionless; corrected mass flow is expressed in grams per second ($g/s$).
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Corrected speed in $RPM / \sqrt{\theta}$, mass flow in $lbm/sec$, pressure in $psia$.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2, Airworthiness Requirements, mandates strict compliance with engine operating envelopes defined in the Type Certificate Data Sheet (TCDS). ICAO Annex 8 details continuing airworthiness of aircraft engines.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Compressor map integrity directly affects FADEC scheduling, variable geometry stator (VGS) actuators, bleed valves, and cabin pressurization bleed air purity.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: British Airways Flight 038 (Boeing 777-236ER) experienced dual engine power rollback on final approach due to ice accretion in the fuel-oil heat exchangers, disrupting fuel flow and compressor operating lines.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Corrected Speed Formula

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming corrected speed uses static temperature instead of total temperature.

✅ UPSC Truth: Corrected speed must utilize total temperature ratio $\theta = T_{t2} / T_{ref}$ to account for ram compression at the inlet.

Trap 2: Surge Line Location

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing the surge line lies on the right-hand side of the compressor map.

✅ UPSC Truth: The surge line is located on the extreme left boundary of the compressor map at low mass flows and high pressure ratios.

Trap 3: Polytropic vs Isentropic Efficiency

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming isentropic efficiency remains constant regardless of the stage pressure ratio.

✅ UPSC Truth: Polytropic efficiency applies to infinitesimal stages and differs from overall isentropic efficiency across multi-stage compressors.

Trap 4: Choke Flow Condition

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming corrected mass flow increases indefinitely with higher pressure ratios.

✅ UPSC Truth: At high pressure ratios, the compressor throat chokes, rendering corrected mass flow independent of downstream pressure.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A multi-stage axial compressor operates with an inlet total temperature of $288.15 \, K$ and an inlet total pressure of $101.325 \, kPa$. The compressor discharge total pressure is $1520 \, kPa$, and the isentropic efficiency is $0.85$. Calculate the compressor total pressure ratio and the compressor discharge total temperature assuming $\gamma = 1.4$.

Given Flight Parameters:
$P_{t2} = 101.325 \, kPa$, $P_{t3} = 1520 \, kPa$, $T_{t2} = 288.15 \, K$, $\eta_c = 0.85$, $\gamma = 1.4$
Governing Formula:
$\pi_c = \frac{P_{t3}}{P_{t2}} \quad \text{and} \quad T_{t3} = T_{t2} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{\eta_c} \left[ \pi_c^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}} - 1 \right] \right)$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute total pressure ratio $\pi_c = \frac{1520}{101.325} = 15.0$. Step 2: Compute isentropic temperature term $\pi_c^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}} = 15.0^{\frac{0.4}{1.4}} = 15.0^{0.2857} = 2.404$. Step 3: Compute actual discharge temperature $T_{t3} = 288.15 \left( 1 + \frac{1}{0.85} [2.404 - 1] \right) = 288.15 \left( 1 + 1.6518 \right) = 763.78 \, K$.
Final Answer: Pressure Ratio $\pi_c = 15.0$ and Discharge Temperature $T_{t3} = 763.78 \, K$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurately predicting compressor discharge temperature is vital for preventing thermal degradation of turbine blades and ensuring bleed air system safety during high-power takeoff.

Question 1 • conceptual
What does the surge line on a compressor map represent?
A The boundary beyond which flow separates and violent flow reversal occurs
B The maximum corrected mass flow rate achievable at choked conditions
C The locus of points representing 100% polytropic efficiency
D The operating line for optimal cruise specific fuel consumption
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The surge line represents the operational limit where adverse pressure gradients cause massive boundary layer separation, leading to flow reversal and engine surge.
Question 2 • conceptual
In a compressor map, what parameters are typically plotted on the axes?
A Corrected mass flow versus pressure ratio for various corrected speeds
B Thrust versus fuel flow rate
C Rotor speed versus turbine inlet temperature
D Mach number versus angle of attack
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Standard compressor maps plot corrected mass flow on the abscissa and total pressure ratio on the ordinate, parameterized by lines of constant corrected speed and efficiency.
Question 3 • conceptual
How does choking affect the compressor map at high corrected speeds?
A Corrected mass flow becomes independent of pressure ratio, forming a vertical line on the map
B Pressure ratio drops to zero
C Corrected speed drops to zero
D Efficiency reaches 100%
Solution & Technical Rationale:
When the compressor throat chokes (Mach number equals 1), the mass flow reaches its maximum corrected value, making the speed lines vertical on the compressor map.
Question 4 • conceptual
Why are non-dimensional or corrected parameters used for compressor maps?
A To render performance data independent of ambient atmospheric temperature and pressure variations
B To eliminate the need for accounting of rotational speed
C To simplify calculations by ignoring gas compressibility
D To ensure constant isentropic efficiency across all flight regimes
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Correcting parameters ($N/\sqrt{\theta}$ and $\dot{m}\sqrt{\theta}/\delta$) allows a single set of compressor performance curves to be used across varying altitude and temperature conditions.
Question 5 • statement-based
Consider the following statements regarding compressor maps: 1. The operating line lies safely to the right of the surge line. 2. Compressor efficiency islands peak near the center of the working envelope. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A Both 1 and 2
B 1 only
C 2 only
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. The operating line must maintain a surge margin to the left (surge line), and efficiency islands ('islands of high efficiency') peak within the design operating envelope.
Question 6 • statement-based
Consider the following assertions: 1. Increasing altitude decreases ambient temperature, which increases corrected rotational speed for a given physical RPM. 2. A higher pressure ratio always guarantees higher compressor isentropic efficiency. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is correct because $\theta = T_t/T_{ref}$ decreases with altitude, increasing corrected speed $N/\sqrt{\theta}$. Statement 2 is false because excessive pressure ratios can push operation toward the surge line, severely degrading efficiency.
Question 7 • numerical
A compressor operates with an inlet total temperature of $300 \, K$. If the reference temperature is $288.15 \, K$, what is the temperature correction factor $\theta$?
A 1.041
B 0.960
C 1.102
D 0.895
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The temperature correction factor is $\theta = \frac{T_{t2}}{T_{ref}} = \frac{300}{288.15} = 1.0411$.
Question 8 • numerical
An axial compressor has a total pressure ratio of $12.0$ and an isentropic efficiency of $0.88$. Given $\gamma = 1.4$, what is the value of $\pi_c^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}$?
A 2.227
B 1.856
C 2.541
D 1.414
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Evaluating $\pi_c^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma} = 12.0^{0.4/1.4} = 12.0^{0.2857} = 2.227$.
Question 9 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Modern gas turbine engines employ variable stator vanes (VSVs). Reason (R): VSVs alter the stagger angle of compressor blades to maintain optimum incidence angles and avoid surge across wide operating ranges.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both assertion and reason are true. VSVs are critical active control devices used to prevent compressor surge by adjusting blade incidence angles at off-design rotational speeds.
Question 10 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Compressor surge is accompanied by loud rumbling noises and rapid turbine gas temperature spikes. Reason (R): Flow reversal during surge causes hot combustion gases to flow backward through the compressor.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both assertion and reason are true. Compressor surge results in flow breakdown and reverse flow of hot gases, causing loud noises and rapid thermal spikes.
Question 11 • conceptual
What is the primary purpose of maintaining a surge margin in aircraft gas turbine engine design?
A To ensure safe operational separation between the steady-state operating line and the surge line during transients
B To maximize the maximum possible pressure ratio at sea level static conditions
C To reduce overall engine weight by eliminating unnecessary blade rows
D To maintain a constant fuel-air ratio across all flight Mach numbers
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Surge margin provides a buffer so that rapid throttle transients, gust ingestion, or inlet distortion do not push the compressor across the surge line.
Question 12 • conceptual
Which system actively manages compressor operating lines to prevent surge during rapid aircraft accelerations?
A Full Authority Digital Engine Control (FADEC) operating variable bleed valves and VSVs
B Cabin pressurization outflow valve
C Pitot-static probe heating circuit
D Fuel tank inerting nitrogen generation system
Solution & Technical Rationale:
FADEC continuously modulates variable stator vanes and bleed valves to shift the operating line and maintain adequate surge margin.

Question 1 of 10 +3 / -1
The compressor total pressure ratio $\pi_c$ is defined as:
A $P_{t3} / P_{t2}$
B $P_{t2} / P_{t3}$
C $P_{s3} / P_{s2}$
D $T_{t3} / T_{t2}$
Rationale:
Compressor total pressure ratio is defined as compressor discharge stagnation pressure divided by compressor inlet stagnation pressure ($P_{t3} / P_{t2}$).
Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
On a standard compressor map, the surge line separates:
A Stable operation region from unstable surge/stall region
B Choked flow region from unchoked flow region
C Subsonic flow region from supersonic flow region
D High efficiency region from low efficiency region
Rationale:
The surge line is the boundary dividing stable operating points to the right from unstable stall and surge regimes to the left.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
Corrected mass flow rate for a compressor is proportional to:
A $\dot{m} \sqrt{\theta} / \delta$
B $\dot{m} \delta / \sqrt{\theta}$
C $\dot{m} \theta / \delta^2$
D $\dot{m} / (\theta \delta)$
Rationale:
Corrected mass flow is given by $\dot{m}_{corr} = \frac{\dot{m} \sqrt{T_{t2}/T_{ref}}}{P_{t2}/P_{ref}} = \frac{\dot{m} \sqrt{\theta}}{\delta}$.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
What happens to the corrected mass flow line on a compressor map when the compressor chokes?
A It becomes nearly vertical
B It becomes horizontal
C It disappears completely
D It slopes downward steeply
Rationale:
At choking, mass flow reaches its maximum limit regardless of downstream pressure changes, making the speed lines vertical.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
Which DGCA CAR section primarily governs airworthiness requirements for turbine engines?
A CAR Section 2
B CAR Section 5
C CAR Section 8
D CAR Section 11
Rationale:
DGCA CAR Section 2 covers Airworthiness requirements, including engine design, testing, and type certification standards.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
In an axial compressor stage, diffusion of air across rotor and stator blades results in:
A Adverse pressure gradients that can lead to boundary layer separation
B Favorable pressure gradients that accelerate the flow
C Isothermal expansion across the blade passage
D Zero static pressure rise
Rationale:
Compressor blades decelerate the flow to convert kinetic energy into static pressure, creating adverse pressure gradients prone to separation.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
If inlet total temperature increases while physical RPM remains constant, corrected speed will:
A Decrease
B Increase
C Remain exactly constant
D Become zero
Rationale:
Corrected speed is $N / \sqrt{\theta}$. An increase in $T_t$ increases $\theta$, thereby decreasing the corrected rotational speed.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the primary indicator on the flight deck of compressor pressure ratio in older turbojet/turbofan installations?
A EPR (Engine Pressure Ratio) gauge
B Fuel flow meter
C Oil temperature gauge
D Vibration monitoring indicator
Rationale:
Engine Pressure Ratio (EPR) gauges display turbine discharge total pressure divided by compressor inlet total pressure as a primary thrust indicator.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
During compressor stall, the immediate effect on engine operation is:
A Loss of thrust, rising exhaust gas temperature (EGT), and loud banging noises
B Immediate overspeed of the N1 spool and fuel starvation
C Sudden drop in EGT and surge in thrust output
D Complete cessation of fuel flow by the FADEC
Rationale:
Compressor stall disrupts airflow, causing thrust loss, rapid EGT spikes due to uncooled combustion gases, and characteristic loud bangs.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
Polytropic efficiency differs from isentropic efficiency because polytropic efficiency:
A Applies to an infinitesimal stage, whereas isentropic efficiency applies to the overall finite compression process
B Ignores frictional losses entirely
C Is only valid for centrifugal compressors
D Assumes isothermal compression
Rationale:
Polytropic efficiency characterizes the efficiency of an elementary stage, avoiding the pressure-ratio bias inherent in overall isentropic efficiency.
