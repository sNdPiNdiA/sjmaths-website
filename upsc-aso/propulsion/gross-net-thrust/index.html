Gross/net thrust

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Engine Pressure Ratio (EPR) indicators and Cockpit Display Engine Indicating and Crew Alerting Systems (EICAS) rely directly on gross and net thrust computations.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Takeoff field length calculations and V-speed determinations depend strictly on accurate net thrust availability under runway temperature limits.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 mandate strict thrust verification protocols during engine overhaul testing and test-cell calibration.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers investigate runway overruns by auditing FADEC recorded thrust traces versus expected net thrust margins.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Gross thrust ($F_g$) is the total force generated by an airbreathing jet engine before subtracting free-stream momentum, defined as $F_g = \dot{m}_e v_e - \dot{m}_0 v_0 + (p_e - p_0)A_e$, while net thrust ($F_n$) represents the propulsive force available to propel the aircraft forward.
  • Physical Meaning: Gross thrust accounts for total momentum and pressure thrust at the nozzle exit plane, whereas net thrust represents the usable surplus force overcoming external airframe drag.
  • Governing Law: Derived directly from Euler's control volume momentum conservation theorem applied to an internal flow control volume enclosing the engine.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid under continuous fluid flow assumptions from the engine intake face through the core and exhaust convergent-divergent supersonic nozzle.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $F_g$ = Gross Thrust (Newtons, N)
  • $F_n$ = Net Thrust (Newtons, N)
  • $\dot{m}_e$ = Exhaust Mass Flow Rate ($kg/s$)
  • $\dot{m}_0$ = Free-stream Intake Mass Flow Rate ($kg/s$)
  • $v_e$ = Exhaust Gas Exit Velocity ($m/s$)
  • $v_0$ = Flight Free-Stream Velocity ($m/s$)
  • $p_e$ = Exhaust Nozzle Static Pressure ($Pa$)
  • $p_0$ = Ambient Free-Stream Static Pressure ($Pa$)
  • $A_e$ = Nozzle Exit Cross-Sectional Area ($m^2$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: High-energy combustion gas molecules inside the turbine discharge expand rapidly toward the nozzle throat and exit plane.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Static pressure forces and momentum fluxes interact across control volume boundaries to produce unbalanced directional forces.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: The net resultant force propels the aircraft forward by overcoming aerodynamic drag and sustaining steady-state flight conditions.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Apply Reynolds Transport Theorem for momentum conservation over an engine control volume.

$$\sum \vec{F} = \oint_{CS} \vec{V} (\rho \vec{V} \cdot d\vec{A}) + \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \int_{CV} \rho \vec{V} dV$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Evaluate intake face (0) and exhaust exit plane (e) under steady-state conditions.

$$\sum F_x = \dot{m}_e v_e - \dot{m}_0 v_0 + (p_e - p_0)A_e$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Define gross thrust by grouping internal exhaust momentum and pressure area terms together.

$$F_g = \dot{m}_e v_e + (p_e - p_0)A_e$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Subtract free-stream ram drag to yield the final net propulsive thrust equation.

$$F_n = F_g - \dot{m}_0 v_0 = (\dot{m}_e v_e - \dot{m}_0 v_0) + (p_e - p_0)A_e$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: $[M^1 L^1 T^{-2}]$ derived via force equals mass times acceleration ($[M][L][T^{-2}]$).
  • SI Unit: Newton ($N$), defined as $kg \cdot m / s^2$.
  • CGS Unit: Dyne, defined as $g \cdot cm / s^2$ with a conversion factor of $1 N = 10^5$ dynes.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Pounds-force ($lbf$), where $1 lbf \approx 4.44822 N$, frequently used in FAA and legacy flight crew operations manuals.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'B', Part VII and ICAO Annex 8 govern turbine engine thrust certification and performance standards.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Confusing Gross and Net Thrust

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming gross thrust equals net thrust when an aircraft is stationary on the ground.

✅ UPSC Truth: When flight velocity $v_0 = 0$, free-stream ram drag is zero, making net thrust equal to gross thrust ($F_n = F_g$).

Trap 2: Neglecting Pressure Area Term

❌ Examiner Trap: Ignoring the static pressure differential term $(p_e - p_0)A_e$ in unchoked nozzles.

✅ UPSC Truth: The pressure thrust term must always be included unless the exhaust nozzle is perfectly expanded to ambient pressure ($p_e = p_0$).

Trap 3: Sign Convention Errors for Ram Drag

❌ Examiner Trap: Adding free-stream momentum instead of subtracting it when computing net thrust.

✅ UPSC Truth: Free-stream momentum ($\dot{m}_0 v_0$) acts as ram drag opposing forward motion and must always be subtracted from gross thrust.

Trap 4: Mass Flow Rate Discrepancy

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming intake mass flow equals exhaust mass flow under fuel addition.

✅ UPSC Truth: Exhaust mass flow equals intake mass flow plus injected fuel mass flow rate ($\dot{m}_e = \dot{m}_0 + \dot{m}_f$).

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A twin-engine turbofan aircraft is operating at a cruising altitude where free-stream velocity is $240 \, m/s$ and ambient static pressure is $30 \, kPa$. The engine ingests air at a rate of $150 \, kg/s$ with a fuel flow rate of $3 \, kg/s$, discharging exhaust gases at $550 \, m/s$ through an exit area of $0.5 \, m^2$ where static pressure is $35 \, kPa$. Calculate the gross and net thrust of the engine.

Given Flight Parameters:
$\dot{m}_0 = 150 \, kg/s$, $\dot{m}_f = 3 \, kg/s$, $\dot{m}_e = 153 \, kg/s$, $v_0 = 240 \, m/s$, $v_e = 550 \, m/s$, $p_0 = 30,000 \, Pa$, $p_e = 35,000 \, Pa$, $A_e = 0.5 \, m^2$
Governing Formula:
$F_g = \dot{m}_e v_e + (p_e - p_0)A_e$ and $F_n = F_g - \dot{m}_0 v_0$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute exhaust momentum flux: $\dot{m}_e v_e = 153 \times 550 = 84,150 \, N$. Step 2: Compute pressure thrust term: $(p_e - p_0)A_e = (35,000 - 30,000) \times 0.5 = 5,000 \times 0.5 = 2,500 \, N$. Step 3: Calculate gross thrust $F_g = 84,150 + 2,500 = 86,650 \, N$, and subtract ram drag $\dot{m}_0 v_0 = 150 \times 240 = 36,000 \, N$ to find net thrust $F_n = 86,650 - 36,000 = 50,650 \, N$.
Final Answer: Gross Thrust ($F_g$) = 86,650 N; Net Thrust ($F_n$) = 50,650 N
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate calculation of net thrust ensures proper performance margins during critical climb and high-altitude cruise segments, directly preventing thrust-limited emergencies.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary physical distinction between gross thrust and net thrust in a turbojet engine?
A Gross thrust includes ram drag whereas net thrust excludes it.
B Net thrust includes ram drag whereas gross thrust excludes it.
C Gross thrust accounts for free-stream velocity, while net thrust does not.
D Net thrust includes fuel mass flow rate, while gross thrust does not.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Gross thrust is the total thrust generated by the engine internals without subtracting intake momentum (ram drag), while net thrust subtracts ram drag ($F_n = F_g - \dot{m}_0 v_0$). Thus, gross thrust includes ram drag effects conceptually when comparing total momentum changes.
Question 2 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding gross and net thrust: 1. When an aircraft is stationary on the run-up bay, gross thrust equals net thrust. 2. Pressure thrust is zero if the exhaust nozzle is perfectly expanded to ambient pressure. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is correct because zero flight velocity ($v_0 = 0$) results in zero ram drag, making $F_n = F_g$. Statement 2 is correct because when $p_e = p_0$, the term $(p_e - p_0)A_e$ vanishes entirely.
Question 3 • numerical
An engine discharges exhaust at $600 \, m/s$ with a total mass flow of $100 \, kg/s$. If the flight speed is $200 \, m/s$ and pressure thrust is negligible, what is the net thrust?
A 40,000 N
B 60,000 N
C 20,000 N
D 80,000 N
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Net thrust $F_n = \dot{m}_e v_e - \dot{m}_0 v_0$. Assuming $\dot{m}_e \approx \dot{m}_0 = 100 \, kg/s$, $F_n = 100(600 - 200) = 100 \times 400 = 40,000 \, N$.
Question 4 • assertion
Assertion (A): Net thrust decreases as flight Mach number increases at constant throttle setting in a turbojet engine. Reason (R): Ram drag increases linearly with flight velocity, reducing the usable propulsive force.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
As flight speed increases, ram drag ($\dot{m}_0 v_0$) increases, which directly subtracts from gross thrust and reduces net thrust in standard turbojets. Both assertion and reason are correct with proper causal linkage.
Question 5 • conceptual
Under what nozzle operating condition does the static pressure term $(p_e - p_0)A_e$ become negative?
A Over-expanded nozzle where $p_e < p_0$
B Under-expanded nozzle where $p_e > p_0$
C Perfectly expanded nozzle where $p_e = p_0$
D Choked nozzle where $p_e = critical pressure$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
If the exhaust static pressure $p_e$ is less than ambient pressure $p_0$ (over-expanded nozzle), the term $(p_e - p_0)$ is negative, resulting in a negative pressure thrust contribution.
Question 6 • statement
Which factors directly influence the gross thrust of a gas turbine engine? 1. Exhaust gas velocity 2. Fuel mass flow rate 3. Free-stream aircraft velocity Select the correct answer using the code below:
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2 and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Gross thrust depends on exhaust velocity and exhaust mass flow (which includes fuel flow rate). Free-stream velocity affects ram drag, which governs net thrust rather than gross thrust.
Question 7 • numerical
Calculate the pressure thrust component given $p_e = 120 \, kPa$, $p_0 = 100 \, kPa$, and nozzle exit area $A_e = 0.2 \, m^2$.
A 4,000 N
B 2,000 N
C 44,000 N
D 24,000 N
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Pressure thrust = $(p_e - p_0)A_e = (120,000 - 100,000) \times 0.2 = 20,000 \times 0.2 = 4,000 \, N$.
Question 8 • conceptual
What happens to propulsive efficiency as flight speed approaches exhaust velocity in a turbojet?
A Propulsive efficiency approaches 100%
B Propulsive efficiency drops to zero
C Propulsive efficiency reaches a local minimum
D Propulsive efficiency becomes undefined
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Propulsive efficiency is maximized when flight speed equals exhaust velocity ($v_0 = v_e$), approaching 100% theoretically because kinetic energy wasted in the exhaust wake is minimized.
Question 9 • assertion
Assertion (A): Turbofan engines maintain higher propulsive efficiency at subsonic speeds compared to turbojets. Reason (R): Turbofans bypass a large mass of air at lower exhaust velocities, better matching flight velocity.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Lower exhaust velocities in the bypass stream reduce kinetic energy loss in the wake, yielding higher propulsive efficiency matching subsonic flight speeds. Both statements are correct with direct causation.
Question 10 • statement
Which of the following equations correctly expresses net thrust ($F_n$) incorporating fuel mass flow?
A $F_n = (\dot{m}_0 + \dot{m}_f)v_e - \dot{m}_0 v_0 + (p_e - p_0)A_e$
B $F_n = \dot{m}_0(v_e - v_0)$
C $F_n = \dot{m}_f v_e - (p_e - p_0)A_e$
D $F_n = \dot{m}_0 v_e + \dot{m}_f v_0$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Exhaust mass flow $\dot{m}_e$ is equal to intake air mass flow $\dot{m}_0$ plus fuel mass flow $\dot{m}_f$. Substituting this into the momentum equation gives option A.
Question 11 • numerical
An engine has a gross thrust of $50,000 \, N$ and an intake mass flow of $120 \, kg/s$ at a flight speed of $150 \, m/s$. What is the net thrust?
A 32,000 N
B 50,000 N
C 68,000 N
D 18,000 N
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Ram drag = $\dot{m}_0 v_0 = 120 \times 150 = 18,000 \, N$. Net thrust $F_n = F_g - \text{Ram Drag} = 50,000 - 18,000 = 32,000 \, N$.
Question 12 • conceptual
In a convergent-divergent nozzle operating in an under-expanded regime, what is the direction of the pressure thrust force?
A Forward, aiding net thrust
B Backward, opposing net thrust
C Perpendicular to the centerline
D Zero
Solution & Technical Rationale:
In an under-expanded nozzle, $p_e > p_0$, making $(p_e - p_0)A_e$ positive, which acts in the forward direction and adds to net propulsive thrust.

A The pressure area term $(p_e - p_0)A_e$ becomes zero

Rationale:
When $p_e = p_0$, the pressure thrust term $(p_e - p_0)A_e$ equals zero, leaving only momentum terms.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
Calculate net thrust given $\dot{m}_e = 200 \, kg/s$, $v_e = 500 \, m/s$, $\dot{m}_0 = 195 \, kg/s$, $v_0 = 300 \, m/s$, and $p_e = p_0$.
A 41,000 N
B 100,000 N
C 58,500 N
D 40,000 N
Rationale:
$F_n = (200 \times 500) - (195 \times 300) = 100,000 - 58,500 = 41,000 \, N$.

A A net loss in total thrust due to negative pressure thrust
B An increase in propulsive efficiency
C Elimination of all shock waves
D Doubling of mass flow rate
Rationale:
Over-expansion creates a negative pressure thrust term $(p_e - p_0)A_e$, reducing total net thrust.

Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
How does an increase in altitude affect net thrust for a standard un-augmented turbojet at constant throttle?
A Net thrust decreases due to declining air density reducing mass flow
B Net thrust increases due to lower ambient temperature
C Net thrust remains completely constant
D Net thrust increases exponentially
Rationale:
Higher altitude reduces air density ($\rho_0$), decreasing intake mass flow rate ($\dot{m}_0$), which directly reduces net thrust.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
What term represents the momentum penalty incurred by capturing and accelerating ambient air into the engine?
A Ram Drag
B Scrubbing Drag
C Induced Drag
D Wave Drag
Rationale:
Ram drag is the momentum flux of the incoming free-stream air ($\dot{m}_0 v_0$) that must be overcome by gross thrust.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
In FADEC equipped aircraft, what parameter is primarily monitored as an indirect proxy for net thrust?
A Engine Pressure Ratio (EPR) or Fan Speed ($N_1$)
B Oil temperature
C Hydraulic pressure

FADEC systems use EPR or low-pressure spool speed ($N_1$) to monitor and control net thrust delivery.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the physical unit of specific thrust (thrust per unit mass flow rate)?
A $m/s$ (meters per second)
B $N / (kg \cdot s)$
C $kg / N$
D $N \cdot m$
Rationale:
Specific thrust is $F_n / \dot{m}_0$, having units of $N / (kg/s)$, which simplifies dimensionally to velocity units ($m/s$).
