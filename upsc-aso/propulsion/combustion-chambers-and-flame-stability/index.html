Combustion chambers & flame stability

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Cockpit displays rely on Exhaust Gas Temperature (EGT) thermocouples and Interstage Turbine Temperature (ITT) probes to monitor combustion chamber health.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Combustor lean blowout and rich extinction limits define the high-altitude operational envelope and relight boundaries for safe dispatch.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 mandate strict cyclic life limits and borescope inspection intervals for combustor liners.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers investigate flameout events by analyzing engine trend monitoring data, fuel nozzle coking patterns, and igniter plug wear.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Combustion chambers in gas turbines convert chemical fuel energy into high-enthalpy thermal energy through continuous, stable burning at high velocities, governed by the mass conservation law $\nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{V}) = \dot{w}_{fuel}$.
  • Physical Meaning: It sustains a continuous flame in a high-speed, highly turbulent air stream by anchoring a reaction zone.
  • Governing Law: Conservation of chemical species and energy coupled with finite-rate Arrhenius chemical kinetics.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid down to Knudsen numbers where molecular collision frequency exceeds macroscopic flow residence times.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\dot{m}_a$ = Air mass flow rate into combustor ($kg/s$)
  • $\dot{m}_f$ = Fuel mass flow rate ($kg/s$)
  • $f$ = Fuel-air ratio, $\dot{m}_f / \dot{m}_a$ (Dimensionless)
  • $\phi$ = Equivalence ratio, $(f/f_{stoic})$ (Dimensionless)
  • $T_t$ = Total or stagnation temperature ($K$)
  • $P_t$ = Total or stagnation pressure ($Pa$)
  • $\eta_b$ = Combustion efficiency (Dimensionless)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Atomized fuel droplets vaporize, mix with turbulent air molecules, and undergo chain-branching radical reactions via high-energy binary collisions.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Strong pressure and temperature gradients drive counter-flow recirculation zones that supply continuous heat to incoming reactants.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Swirlers create vortex breakdown bubbles, lowering axial velocity locally so flame speed matches local flow velocity to prevent blowout.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Apply conservation of energy across the control volume of the combustion chamber.

$$\sum \dot{m}_{in} h_{t,in} + \dot{Q} - \dot{W} = \sum \dot{m}_{out} h_{t,out}$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Express total enthalpy using specific heat and heat of reaction.

$$h_t = c_p T + \frac{V^2}{2}$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Incorporate combustion efficiency $\eta_b$ and lower heating value $LHV$.

$$(\dot{m}_a + \dot{m}_f) c_{p,out} T_{t,out} - \dot{m}_a c_{p,in} T_{t,in} = \eta_b \dot{m}_f LHV$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Solve explicitly for the exit total temperature $T_{t,out}$.

$$T_{t,out} = \frac{\dot{m}_a c_{p,in} T_{t,in} + \eta_b \dot{m}_f LHV}{(\dot{m}_a + \dot{m}_f) c_{p,out}}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Combustor pressure drop and heat release utilize mass, length, time, and temperature $[M^1 L^{-1} T^{-2}]$ and $[M^1 L^2 T^{-2}]$.
  • SI Unit: Pressure drop in Pascals ($Pa$ or $N/m^2$); Heat release rate in Watts ($W$ or $J/s$).
  • CGS Unit: Pressure in baryes ($dyn/cm^2$); Energy release rate in ergs per second ($erg/s$).
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Pressure in pounds per square inch ($psi$); Mass flow in pounds per second ($lb/s$); Temperature in Rankine ($^\circ R$).

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Series 'C' and ICAO Annex 8 mandate rigorous airworthiness type certification testing for combustor altitude relight and structural integrity.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Combustor performance directly impacts FADEC scheduling, fuel metering units, ignition exciters, and bleed air extraction temperatures.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: British Airways Flight 38 suffered dual engine flameout due to ice crystal accretion in the fuel-oil heat exchangers blocking fuel flow to the combustion chambers.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Flame Stabilization Mechanism

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming the main combustion zone is stabilized purely by continuous spark ignition from the igniter plugs.

✅ UPSC Truth: Igniters are only used for engine start; combustion is sustained entirely by aerodynamic flame holders and recirculation zones.

Trap 2: Equivalence Ratio at Turbine Entry

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing that the overall stoichiometric fuel-air ratio is fed directly into the turbine blades.

✅ UPSC Truth: Primary zone operates near stoichiometric for stability, but dilution air is added downstream to lower turbine entry temperatures.

Trap 3: Combustor Pressure Loss Origin

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming total pressure loss in a combustion chamber is caused solely by wall skin friction.

✅ UPSC Truth: The dominant contribution is fundamental momentum pressure loss driven by continuous heat addition (Rayleigh flow).

Trap 4: Lean Blowout at High Altitude

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming combustor blowout occurs because fuel freezes at high altitude.

✅ UPSC Truth: It occurs because low ambient pressure and temperature reduce chemical reaction rates below the flow residence rate.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A gas turbine combustion chamber receives air at $T_{t,in} = 600 \, K$ with a mass flow rate of $\dot{m}_a = 40 \, kg/s$. Fuel (Jet A-1 with $LHV = 43,000 \, kJ/kg$) is injected at $\dot{m}_f = 0.8 \, kg/s$. Assuming combustion efficiency is $\eta_b = 0.98$ and mean specific heat is $c_p = 1.15 \, kJ/(kg \cdot K)$, calculate the exit total temperature.

Given Flight Parameters:
$T_{t,in} = 600 \, K$, $\dot{m}_a = 40 \, kg/s$, $\dot{m}_f = 0.8 \, kg/s$, $LHV = 43,000 \, kJ/kg$, $\eta_b = 0.98$, $c_p = 1.15 \, kJ/(kg \cdot K)$
Governing Formula:
$T_{t,out} = \frac{\dot{m}_a c_p T_{t,in} + \eta_b \dot{m}_f LHV}{(\dot{m}_a + \dot{m}_f) c_p}$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute total chemical energy release: $\dot{Q}_{in} = \eta_b \dot{m}_f LHV = 0.98 \times 0.8 \times 43000 = 33,712 \, kW$. Step 2: Compute sensible enthalpy entering the combustor: $H_{in} = \dot{m}_a c_p T_{t,in} = 40 \times 1.15 \times 600 = 27,600 \, kW$. Step 3: Compute exit temperature: $T_{t,out} = \frac{27600 + 33712}{(40 + 0.8) \times 1.15} = \frac{61312}{46.92} = 1306.73 \, K$.
Final Answer: $1306.73 K$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate calculation of combustor exit temperature ensures turbine blade metallurgical limits are not exceeded, preventing catastrophic turbine blade failure during high-thrust operations.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary function of the primary zone in a gas turbine combustion chamber?
A Dilution of exhaust gases
B Atomization and stable flame anchoring
C Cooling of the turbine blades
D Increasing overall mass flow rate
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The primary zone is designed to provide stable flame anchoring through recirculation zones and proper fuel-air mixing near stoichiometric ratios.
Question 2 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding combustion chamber pressure losses: 1. Friction accounts for the majority of total pressure loss. 2. Heat addition (Rayleigh flow) is a major contributor to total pressure drop. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Frictional pressure loss is relatively small compared to momentum pressure loss caused by heat addition in a constant-area or variable-area combustor.
Question 3 • numerical
If air enters a combustor at $500 \, K$ and fuel energy addition increases the enthalpy by $300 \, kJ/kg$ (with $c_p = 1.0 \, kJ/kg\cdot K$), what is the ideal temperature rise?
A $150 \, K$
B $300 \, K$
C $500 \, K$
D $800 \, K$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Temperature rise $\Delta T = \Delta h / c_p = 300 / 1.0 = 300 \, K$.
Question 4 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Combustion chambers operate with overall lean mixtures. Reason (R): Turbine inlet temperature limits require dilution air to be mixed with combustion products.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
While the primary zone is near stoichiometric, massive dilution air is added downstream, resulting in an overall lean air-fuel ratio.
Question 5 • conceptual
Which phenomenon limits high-altitude operation of a gas turbine combustor?
A Choked nozzle flow
B Lean blowout due to slow reaction rates
C Compressor surge
D Turbine blade creep
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Low pressure and temperature at high altitude slow down chemical reaction rates, leading to lean blowout if residence time is too short.
Question 6 • conceptual
What is the purpose of secondary and dilution air holes in a combustor liner?
A To provide engine thrust directly
B To cool the casing and reduce turbine entry temperature
C To increase fuel atomization
D To act as secondary ignition sources
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Secondary air completes combustion, and dilution air lowers the gas temperature to acceptable limits for the turbine.
Question 7 • statement
Which of the following combustor types are commonly used in modern aircraft gas turbines? 1. Can-annular 2. Annular 3. Ram-jet can
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1, 2 and 3
D 1 only
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Can-annular and annular combustors are standard in modern turbofans and turboprops; ram-jet cans are not standard gas turbine layouts.
Question 8 • numerical
If the fuel-air ratio $f$ is $0.02$ and stoichiometric fuel-air ratio $f_{stoic}$ is $0.067$, what is the equivalence ratio $\phi$?
A $0.298$
B $0.333$
C $3.350$
D $1.334$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Equivalence ratio $\phi = f / f_{stoic} = 0.02 / 0.067 \approx 0.298$.
Question 9 • conceptual
Why are swirlers incorporated around the fuel nozzles in a combustion chamber?
A To increase axial flow velocity
B To generate a central recirculation zone for flame stabilization
C To atomize fuel via centrifugal force
D To cool the fuel nozzle tip
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Swirlers impart angular momentum, creating an adverse pressure gradient that forms a toroidal recirculation zone.
Question 10 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Combustor walls require film cooling. Reason (R): Flame temperatures exceed the melting point of superalloy liner materials.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Stoichiometric flame temperatures reach 2200-2400 K, well above metallurgical limits, necessitating advanced film cooling.
Question 11 • conceptual
What is 'coking' in a gas turbine combustion chamber?
A Ice crystal accumulation on fuel nozzles
B Carbon deposit buildup from incomplete fuel vaporization
C Mechanical cracking of liner walls
D Corrosion due to sulfur in fuel
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Coking is the thermal breakdown of liquid fuel into solid carbon deposits on nozzles and walls.
Question 12 • numerical
A combustor has an inlet total pressure of $1.2 \, MPa$ and an exit total pressure of $1.14 \, MPa$. What is the pressure recovery factor?
A $0.05$
B $0.95$
C $1.05$
D $0.90$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Pressure recovery factor $P_{t,out} / P_{t,in} = 1.14 / 1.20 = 0.95$.

A Lower manufacturing cost
B Uniform temperature distribution and lower weight/length
C Elimination of igniter plugs
D Higher pressure drop tolerance

Annular combustors provide a continuous annular space, reducing surface area, weight, and thermal hot spots.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
Which phenomenon occurs when local flow velocity exceeds flame propagation speed?
A Flashback
B Lean Blowout
C Detonation
D Thermal choke
Rationale:
When flow velocity outpaces flame speed, the flame is swept downstream and blows out.

What is the purpose of igniter plugs in a gas turbine engine?
A To continuously sustain combustion throughout flight
B To initiate combustion during engine start and restart envelopes
C To regulate fuel flow rate
D To measure exhaust gas temperature
Rationale:
Igniters provide high-energy sparks only during startup or in-flight relight procedures.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
Film cooling in combustor liners is achieved by:
A Circulating fuel through internal passages
B Bleeding compressor air over the inner surface to form a cool insulating layer
C Water injection into the primary zone
D Radiation shielding using ceramic tiles
Rationale:
Cool air from the compressor is ducted through small louvers to form a protective film along the liner wall.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
Combustion efficiency in modern gas turbine engines at cruise conditions is typically:
A 50% - 60%
B 75% - 85%
C 98% - 99%+
D Exactly 100%
Rationale:
Modern combustors achieve near-complete combustion efficiency exceeding 98-99% under normal operating conditions.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
Which airworthiness authority standard governs gas turbine combustor structural integrity and containment?
A FAA FAR Part 33 / EASA CS-E
B ICAO Annex 1
C FAA Part 91
D CAR Section 5
Rationale:
FAR Part 33 and EASA CS-E establish airworthiness standards for aircraft engines, including combustors.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the primary indicator on the flight deck of combustion instability or lean blowout?
A Oil pressure drop
B Rapid EGT decrease and rotor speed rundown
C Hydraulic fluid contamination
D Pitot-static blockage warning
Rationale:
Flameout results in an immediate drop in Exhaust Gas Temperature and subsequent spool-down.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
The reference Mach number in aircraft gas turbine combustion chambers is typically in what range?
A $0.05$ to $0.12$

Combustor inlet and internal velocities are kept low (Mach 0.05 to 0.12) to prevent blowout and manage pressure losses.
