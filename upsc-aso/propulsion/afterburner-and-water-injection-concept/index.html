Afterburner & water injection (concept)

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Cockpit gauges monitor Exhaust Gas Temperature (EGT), Fuel Flow transducers, and Nozzle Position Indication (NPI) during afterburner lighting.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Incorrect afterburner blow-out or asymmetric light-off during carrier-borne catapult launches or interceptor scrambles introduces severe yaw moments.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 Series 'I' and ICAO Annex 8 govern military and civil supersonic type certification, combustion stability, and thermal fatigue limits.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Investigators analyze Flight Data Recorders (FDR) for uncommanded afterburner hangups, fuel manifold coking, or catastrophic uncontained turbine failures.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Afterburner (reheat) injects auxiliary fuel downstream of the turbine to yield up to 50% thrust augmentation, while water injection injects H2O into the compressor or combustion chamber to restore mass flow and suppress turbine inlet temperatures. Governing thrust increase equation: $$F = \dot{m}_e v_e - \dot{m}_a v_0 + A_e(p_e - p_0)$$
  • Physical Meaning: Reheat capitalizes on unburnt core turbine exhaust oxygen to release latent chemical energy at the cost of catastrophic Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC).
  • Governing Law: Conservation of linear momentum applied to a variable area convergent-divergent propulsive exhaust nozzle.
  • Continuum Boundary: Operates strictly in supersonic continuum flow regimes where chemical reaction rates exceed characteristic fluid transit times.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $F$ = Total net thrust generated by turbojet/turbofan engine ($N$)
  • $\dot{m}_e$ = Total exhaust gas mass flow rate ($kg/s$)
  • $v_e$ = Effective exhaust jet velocity ($m/s$)
  • $\dot{m}_a$ = Ingested core air mass flow rate ($kg/s$)
  • $v_0$ = Free-stream flight velocity ($m/s$)
  • $A_e$ = Exhaust nozzle exit cross-sectional area ($m^2$)
  • $p_e$ = Static pressure at exhaust nozzle exit plane ($Pa$)
  • $p_0$ = Free-stream ambient atmospheric static pressure ($Pa$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: High enthalpy exhaust gases contain 12-16% unburnt atmospheric oxygen molecules possessing high translational kinetic energy.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Secondary fuel spray bars atomize and vaporize instantly in the high-temperature subsonic duct, creating localized exothermic combustion pressure spikes.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: The massive volumetric expansion requires an actively opening variable convergent-divergent nozzle to accelerate gases to supersonic velocities.
4. Water Injection Dynamics: Evaporative latent heat absorption cools the compressor core, increasing air density and enabling higher turbine entry temperatures without thermal failure.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Momentum balance across the afterburner duct including added fuel mass flow rate.

$$\dot{m}_e = \dot{m}_a + \dot{m}_{f,ab}$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Energy balance incorporating lower heating value ($LHV$) and burner efficiency ($\eta_{ab}$).

$$\dot{m}_a C_{p,t} T_{t4} + \dot{m}_{f,ab} LHV \cdot \eta_{ab} = (\dot{m}_a + \dot{m}_{f,ab}) C_{p,e} T_{t,ab}$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Exhaust velocity derived from total temperature expansion through choked C-D nozzle.

$$v_e = \sqrt{2 C_{p,e} T_{t,ab} \left[1 - \left(\frac{p_0}{p_{t,ab}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}}\right]}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Net thrust augmentation ratio as a function of temperature ratio across the afterburner.

$$\frac{F_{ab}}{F_{dry}} \approx \sqrt{\frac{T_{t,ab}}{T_{t,4}}}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Thrust $[M L T^{-2}]$, Mass flow rate $[M T^{-1}]$, Specific fuel consumption $[M N^{-1} T^{-1}]$.
  • SI Unit: Newtons ($N$) for thrust, kilograms per second ($kg/s$) for mass flow, kilograms per Newton-second ($kg/(N\cdot s)$) for TSFC.
  • CGS Unit: Dynes for force ($1\,N = 10^5\,dynes$), grams per second ($g/s$) for mass flow.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Pounds-force ($lbf$) for thrust ($1\,lbf = 4.44822\,N$), pounds per horsepower-hour ($lb/(hp\cdot hr)$) for specific fuel consumption.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Governed by DGCA CAR Section 2, Airworthiness, covering gas turbine engine standards, flameout margins, and thermal endurance limits.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Directly interacts with FADEC logic, fuel shut-off valves, hydraulic actuators for variable geometry nozzles, and fire detection loops.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: Concorde operational experience mandated rigorous afterburner ignition sequencing and variable intake ramp controls to prevent unstart phenomena and engine surging.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Afterburner Fuel Efficiency

❌ Examiner Trap: Candidates assume afterburner improves overall engine thermal and propulsive efficiency.

✅ UPSC Truth: Afterburner drastically increases thrust at the expense of doubling or tripling Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC) due to poor combustion efficiency in high-speed ducts.

Trap 2: Water Injection Purpose

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming water injection is primarily used to generate additional combustible chemical energy.

✅ UPSC Truth: Water is inert; its purpose is evaporative cooling, which increases air density, mass flow rate, and compressor surge margin.

Trap 3: Nozzle Area Requirements

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing afterburner operation requires a smaller exhaust nozzle exit area.

✅ UPSC Truth: Due to massive volumetric expansion from combustion, the exhaust nozzle must open wider (larger area) to maintain core pressure balance.

Trap 4: Oxygen Availability

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming afterburner chambers require independent oxidizer tanks like liquid rockets.

✅ UPSC Truth: Turbojet afterburners utilize unburnt atmospheric oxygen remaining in the turbine core exhaust gas stream.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A military turbojet engine operating at sea level ingests air at $50\,kg/s$ and produces a dry thrust of $60\,kN$. When the afterburner is engaged, additional fuel of $1.5\,kg/s$ is injected, raising total exhaust gas velocity from $600\,m/s$ to $850\,m/s$. Calculate the augmented net thrust assuming zero free-stream velocity.

Given Flight Parameters:
Core air mass flow rate $\dot{m}_a = 50\,kg/s$; Afterburner fuel mass flow rate $\dot{m}_{f,ab} = 1.5\,kg/s$; Dry exhaust velocity $v_{e,\text{dry}} = 600\,m/s$; Wet exhaust velocity $v_{e,\text{wet}} = 850\,m/s$; Free-stream velocity $v_0 = 0\,m/s$.
Governing Formula:
$$F_{ab} = \dot{m}_e v_e - \dot{m}_a v_0 + A_e(p_e - p_0)$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute total exhaust gas mass flow rate: $\dot{m}_e = 50 + 1.5 = 51.5\,kg/s$. Step 2: Calculate augmented gross thrust from momentum: $F_{\text{wet}} = 51.5\,kg/s \times 850\,m/s = 43,775\,N$. Step 3: Assuming negligible nozzle pressure differential term, total augmented net thrust is $43.775\,kN$.
Final Answer: $43.775 kN (Note: While gross momentum increases, net thrust increase depends on exact dry baseline comparison, yielding significant thrust augmentation).$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Sudden uncommanded thrust spikes or asymmetrical afterburner light-off require immediate flight crew recognition and adherence to engine limit procedures during critical takeoff or intercept phases.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary thermodynamic purpose of engaging an aircraft engine afterburner?
A To compress incoming ambient air
B To increase core turbine thermal efficiency
C To provide significant thrust augmentation through unburnt oxygen combustion
D To cool turbine blades via water evaporation
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Afterburners inject fuel into the high-temperature turbine exhaust stream, utilizing remaining atmospheric oxygen to release large amounts of thermal energy and increase jet velocity for thrust augmentation.
Question 2 • conceptual
How does water injection primarily restore or enhance engine performance on hot takeoff days?
A By chemically reacting with fuel to double energy output
B By evaporating and cooling the compressor intake, increasing air density and mass flow
C By acting as a lubricating fluid for turbine blades
D By decreasing exhaust nozzle exit area
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Water injection uses latent heat of vaporization to cool the compressor inlet air, increasing air density, mass flow rate, and pushing back compressor surge limits.
Question 3 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding afterburners: 1. They dramatically increase Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC). 2. They require the exhaust nozzle area to decrease during operation. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is correct because afterburners are extremely fuel-inefficient. Statement 2 is incorrect because the massive volumetric expansion requires the variable nozzle to OPEN (increase area), not decrease.
Question 4 • numerical
An engine has a dry exhaust gas mass flow of $40\,kg/s$ at $500\,m/s$. With afterburning, fuel flow adds $1\,kg/s$, and exhaust velocity rises to $750\,m/s$. What is the afterburner gross thrust?
A 20,000 N
B 30,750 N
C 31,500 N
D 40,000 N
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Total mass flow $\dot{m}_e = 40 + 1 = 41\,kg/s$. Gross thrust $F = 41 \times 750 = 30,750\,N$.
Question 5 • assertion
Assertion (A): Afterburners can operate indefinitely at maximum continuous rating. Reason (R): The high temperatures generated impose severe thermal stress on turbine and exhaust duct metallurgy.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Assertion (A) is false because afterburners can only be used for short durations (takeoff, combat) due to extreme thermal fatigue and fuel consumption. Reason (R) is true.
Question 6 • conceptual
Which component is strictly necessary for a gas turbine engine equipped with an afterburner?
A Centrifugal compressor
B Variable-geometry exhaust nozzle
C Water-methanol injection tank
D Free-turbine propeller shaft
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A variable-geometry exhaust nozzle is mandatory to accommodate the massive volume changes between dry and wet (afterburning) operation without causing engine surging.
Question 7 • statement
Consider water-methanol injection systems: 1. Methanol is added to prevent water from freezing at high altitudes. 2. Methanol also provides additional combustible fuel energy. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. Water-methanol mixtures (typically 50/50 or similar) prevent freezing in cold environments and add chemical fuel energy during combustion.
Question 8 • numerical
If the thrust augmentation ratio of an afterburning turbojet is $1.6$ and dry thrust is $50\,kN$, what is the wet (afterburning) thrust?
A 31.25 kN
B 50.0 kN
C 80.0 kN
D 96.0 kN
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Wet thrust = Dry thrust $\times$ Augmentation ratio = $50\,kN \times 1.6 = 80.0\,kN$.
Question 9 • conceptual
Why is the combustion efficiency in an afterburner generally lower than in the main combustor?
A Higher pressure and lower velocity in the main combustor
B Lower pressure and extremely high gas velocities in the afterburner duct
C Lack of any flame holders
D Excessive oxygen depletion
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The afterburner duct has high gas velocity and lower static pressure compared to the main combustor, reducing residence time and combustion efficiency.
Question 10 • assertion
Assertion (A): Water injection into a gas turbine compressor increases mass flow and pressure ratio. Reason (R): Evaporating water absorbs heat, reducing specific volume and cooling the air.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are fundamentally true; evaporative cooling shrinks specific volume and raises air density, which benefits compressor operation, though reason and assertion describe linked thermodynamic phenomena.
Question 11 • conceptual
What primary hazard does unburnt fuel accumulation pose during afterburner ignition attempts?
A Compressor stall
B Fuel tank cavitation
C Hard start or violent afterburner blow-out / explosion
D Bearing seizure
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Accumulated unburnt fuel pooling in the exhaust duct prior to ignition can cause a severe explosion, known as a hard start or hot streak.
Question 12 • statement
Which aircraft categories predominantly utilize afterburners? 1. Commercial wide-body turbofans. 2. Supersonic military interceptors and fighters. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Commercial airliners avoid afterburners due to prohibitive fuel consumption; they are exclusively used on military fighters, supersonic trainers, and select supersonic transports.

Rationale:

Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
During afterburner operation, the exhaust nozzle area must:
A Remain completely closed
B Open (increase) to pass the increased volumetric flow
C Close to maintain subsonic velocity
D Oscillate to prevent shock waves
Rationale:
Heat addition in the afterburner causes massive gas expansion, requiring the variable nozzle to open wider to prevent back-pressure and compressor stall.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
Water injection in gas turbines is primarily employed for:
A Continuous cruising thrust augmentation
B Temporary takeoff thrust restoration on hot/high density altitude days
C Lubricating turbine disc dovetails
D De-icing fuel lines
Rationale:
Water injection restores power on hot days where high ambient temperatures reduce air density and engine thrust.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the typical impact of afterburner operation on Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC)?
A Decreases by 10-20%
B Remains roughly constant
C Doubles or triples (increases by 100-200%)
D Drops to zero
Rationale:
Afterburners are notoriously inefficient, causing SFC to spike dramatically, limiting their use to brief intervals.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
Why is methanol often mixed with water in water-injection systems?
A To increase viscosity for better spray atomization
B To prevent freezing at high altitude and provide added fuel energy
C To act as a fire extinguishing agent
D To clean compressor blade deposits
Rationale:
Methanol lowers the freezing point of the mixture and serves as a secondary fuel source.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
If an afterburner fails to light during takeoff roll on a military fighter, what is the immediate pilot action per standard operating procedures?
A Eject immediately
B Abort takeoff if below $V_1$ or continue dry if safe
C Increase throttle to maximum emergency wet power
D Shut down both engines
Rationale:
Standard takeoff safety dictates aborting if below $V_1$, or continuing the takeoff on dry thrust if runway length permits.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
What thermodynamic cycle modification does an afterburner represent?
A Addition of heat at constant pressure in the exhaust duct
B Isentropic expansion through a converging nozzle
C Isothermal compression in the diffuser
D Adiabatic cooling across the turbine
Rationale:
An afterburner approximates constant pressure heat addition ($q = C_p \Delta T$) downstream of the turbine.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
Which regulatory body specifies airworthiness standards for military and civil afterburning engine type certificates in India?
A Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)
B Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA)
C Ministry of Road Transport
D Airports Authority of India (AAI)
Rationale:
DGCA sets airworthiness standards and compliance procedures for aircraft and engine type certification in India.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
What phenomenon occurs if the afterburner nozzle area is too small during ignition?
A Flameout
B Excessive cooling
C Back-pressure leading to engine surge or compressor stall
D Supersonic inlet shock unstart
Rationale:
An inadequate nozzle opening restricts gas flow expansion, causing high back-pressure that chokes the turbine and stalls the compressor.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
In water injection systems, where is the fluid typically introduced?
A Directly into the afterburner combustion zone
B Into the compressor inlet or diffuser casing
C Downstream of the exhaust nozzle
D Into the turbine cooling air passages
Rationale:
Water is injected into the compressor inlet or casing so evaporation cools the air stream and increases density prior to combustion.
