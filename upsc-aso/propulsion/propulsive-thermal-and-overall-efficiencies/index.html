Propulsive, thermal & overall efficiencies

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: FADEC engines monitor fuel flow transducers, EGT sensors, and Air Data Computers to compute real-time specific fuel consumption and thermal efficiency indicators.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Propulsion efficiency degradation directly restricts maximum payload-range charts, climb gradients, and engine-out net flight path clearances over terrain.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 mandate continuous engine trend monitoring (ETM) to detect thermal efficiency drop-offs signaling blade distress.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers analyze FDR efficiency trends during incident root-cause analysis to isolate compressor fouling, turbine distress, or fuel control unit faults.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Propulsive, thermal, and overall efficiencies quantify the thermodynamic conversion of fuel chemical energy into kinetic thrust power for gas turbine engines, governed by \( \eta_o = \eta_t \cdot \eta_p \).
  • Physical Meaning: Thermal efficiency measures core energy conversion, while propulsive efficiency measures how effectively net thrust power is transferred to the aircraft.
  • Governing Law: First and Second Laws of Thermodynamics combined with control volume momentum conservation across the propulsive stream.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid for macroscopic compressible fluid flows within gas turbine cycles where Knudsen number \( Kn \ll 0.01 \).

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\eta_t$ = Thermal efficiency of the core engine (Dimensionless)
  • $\eta_p$ = Propulsive efficiency of the propulsor (Dimensionless)
  • $\eta_o$ = Overall efficiency of the complete power plant (Dimensionless)
  • $V_0$ = Flight velocity or true airspeed ($m/s$)
  • $V_e$ = Jet exhaust velocity relative to the aircraft ($m/s$)
  • $\dot{m}$ = Engine mass flow rate ($kg/s$)
  • $f$ = Fuel-to-air ratio (Dimensionless)
  • $h_{PR}$ = Enthalpy of reaction or lower heating value of fuel ($J/kg$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Fuel hydrocarbon combustion releases thermal energy, increasing molecular random translational kinetic energy within the high-pressure turbine core.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Expansion across turbine blades converts thermal enthalpy into directed momentum via high pressure-to-velocity conversion in nozzle convergent-divergent ducts.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: The net momentum differential between exhaust jet velocity and flight speed generates net propulsive thrust force accelerating the aircraft.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Thermal Efficiency Formulation

Define thermal efficiency as net work or enthalpy rate divided by total fuel chemical energy input rate.

$$\eta_t = \frac{\text{Rate of Enthalpy/Work Addition to Core}}{\dot{m}_f h_{PR}}$$
Step 2: Propulsive Efficiency Formulation

Define propulsive efficiency as useful propulsive work output divided by kinetic energy change of working fluid.

$$\eta_p = \frac{F \cdot V_0}{\frac{1}{2}\dot{m}_e V_e^2 - \frac{1}{2}\dot{m}_0 V_0^2}$$
Step 3: Thrust and Momentum Integration

Substitute net thrust \( F = \dot{m}(V_e - V_0) \) into the propulsive efficiency numerator and simplify.

$$\eta_p = \frac{2 V_0 (V_e - V_0)}{V_e^2 - V_0^2} = \frac{2}{1 + \frac{V_e}{V_0}}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Combine thermal and propulsive efficiencies to formulate the overarching propulsion energy chain equation.

$$\eta_o = \eta_t \cdot \eta_p = \left( \frac{\text{Net Thrust Power}}{\text{Fuel Energy Rate}} \right)$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Efficiencies are dimensionless ratios, hence represented as \([M^0 L^0 T^0 \theta^0]\).
  • SI Unit: Expressed as a dimensionless fraction or percentage (\(\%\)).
  • CGS Unit: Dimensionless percentage, identical to SI systems.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Equivalent specific fuel consumption (SFC) measured in \( lb/(hr\cdot lbf) \) correlates inversely with overall efficiency.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'B', Part VII mandates strict adherence to approved engine performance limits and mandatory periodic borescope inspections.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Engine efficiency links FADEC fuel metering schedules, bleed air extraction limits, and thrust management computer calculations during critical takeoff rotations.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: British Airways Flight 38 (Boeing 777-200ER) experienced fuel icing leading to restricted fuel flow and power loss, highlighting the absolute necessity of maintaining fuel-thermal efficiency margins.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Propulsive Efficiency Limit

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming propulsive efficiency reaches 100% when flight velocity equals exhaust velocity.

✅ UPSC Truth: When \( V_e = V_0 \), net thrust becomes zero \( F = 0 \), forcing propulsive efficiency to drop to zero!

Trap 2: Turboprop vs Turbojet Efficiency

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming turbojets have higher overall efficiency than turboprops at low subsonic speeds.

✅ UPSC Truth: Turboprops achieve significantly higher propulsive efficiency at low speeds due to large propeller disk areas moving massive air masses at lower velocity increments.

Trap 3: Thermal vs Propulsive Trade-off

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing increasing overall pressure ratio (OPR) always improves overall aircraft range.

✅ UPSC Truth: Extremely high OPR increases thermal efficiency but can increase engine weight and reduce propulsive efficiency if bypass ratios are mismanaged.

Trap 4: Efficiency Unit Confusion

❌ Examiner Trap: Treating specific fuel consumption (SFC) as a direct efficiency percentage.

✅ UPSC Truth: SFC is fuel flow per unit thrust (e.g., \( lb/(hr\cdot lbf) \)), which is inversely proportional to overall efficiency.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A turbojet aircraft flies at a constant true airspeed of \( 250 \text{ m/s} \) with an effective exhaust jet velocity of \( 550 \text{ m/s} \). Given that the thermal efficiency of the engine cycle is \( 0.36 \), determine the propulsive efficiency and the overall efficiency.

Given Flight Parameters:
Flight velocity \( V_0 = 250 \text{ m/s} \); Exhaust velocity \( V_e = 550 \text{ m/s} \); Thermal efficiency \( \eta_t = 0.36 \)
Governing Formula:
$$\(\eta_p = \frac{2}{1 + \frac{V_e}{V_0}}\) and \(\eta_o = \eta_t \cdot \eta_p\)$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute velocity ratio \( \frac{V_e}{V_0} = \frac{550}{250} = 2.2 \). Step 2: Compute propulsive efficiency \( \eta_p = \frac{2}{1 + 2.2} = \frac{2}{3.2} = 0.625 \) or \( 62.5\% \). Step 3: Compute overall efficiency \( \eta_o = 0.36 \times 0.625 = 0.225 \) or \( 22.5\% \).
Final Answer: $Propulsive efficiency \( \eta_p = 62.5\% \); Overall efficiency \( \eta_o = 22.5\% \)$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate calculation of overall efficiency enables Air Safety Officers to verify fuel consumption anomalies during cruise audits and audit airline flight planning software compliance.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary physical reason why high-bypass turbofan engines achieve higher propulsive efficiencies than pure turbojets at subsonic speeds?
A They operate at much higher turbine entry temperatures.
B They impart a smaller velocity increment to a much larger mass of air.
C They completely eliminate thermal losses in the combustion chamber.
D They utilize supersonic fan blades to generate shock-free compression.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Propulsive efficiency increases when the exhaust velocity is closer to the flight velocity, achieved by moving a larger mass flow rate with a smaller velocity delta.
Question 2 • statement-based
Consider the following statements regarding gas turbine efficiencies: 1. Thermal efficiency increases with an increase in overall pressure ratio (OPR) and turbine entry temperature (TET). 2. Propulsive efficiency approaches 100% when flight speed equals exhaust gas velocity. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is correct (higher OPR and TET improve thermodynamic cycle efficiency). Statement 2 is incorrect because when flight speed equals exhaust velocity, net thrust and propulsive efficiency drop to zero.
Question 3 • numerical
An aircraft flies at \( 200 \text{ m/s} \) and the engine exhaust velocity is \( 600 \text{ m/s} \). What is the propulsive efficiency of the engine?
A 50.0%
B 62.5%
C 75.0%
D 80.0%
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using \( \eta_p = \frac{2}{1 + (V_e/V_0)} = \frac{2}{1 + (600/200)} = \frac{2}{1 + 3} = \frac{2}{4} = 0.50 \) or \( 50\% \).
Question 4 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Turboprop engines exhibit higher propulsive efficiencies than turbofans at low flight Mach numbers (below \( M = 0.6 \)). Reason (R): Propellers accelerate a massive volume of air to relatively low exhaust velocities compared to core engine nozzles.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
C (A) is true but (R) is false.
D (A) is false but (R) is true.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct and directly linked. Large propeller disk areas yield optimal momentum transfer at low subsonic speeds.
Question 5 • conceptual
How does increasing the compressor pressure ratio affect the specific fuel consumption (SFC) of an ideal gas turbine engine, assuming turbine inlet temperature remains constant?
A Increases SFC
B Decreases SFC
C Has no effect on SFC
D Increases SFC exponentially
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Increasing pressure ratio raises thermal efficiency, which means less fuel is required to produce the same net output, thereby decreasing specific fuel consumption.
Question 6 • statement-based
Which of the following engine types typically exhibits the highest propulsive efficiency at supersonic flight regimes above \( M = 2.0 \)?
A High-bypass turbofan
B Low-bypass turbofan
C Ramjet
D Turboprop
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Ramjets operate exceptionally well at supersonic speeds because high ram compression replaces mechanical compressors, yielding high propulsive efficiencies at Mach 2+.
Question 7 • numerical
If a gas turbine has a thermal efficiency of \( 40\% \) and a propulsive efficiency of \( 70\% \), what is the overall efficiency of the power plant?
A 28.0%
B 35.0%
C 55.0%
D 110.0%
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Overall efficiency is the product of thermal and propulsive efficiencies: \( \eta_o = 0.40 \times 0.70 = 0.28 \) or \( 28\% \).
Question 8 • conceptual
What limits the maximum achievable thermal efficiency in a practical Brayton gas turbine cycle?
A Maximum allowable turbine blade metallurgy temperature limits
B Atmospheric humidity at sea level
C Runway length during takeoff
D Fuel tank volumetric capacity
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Thermal efficiency depends on pressure ratio and peak cycle temperature. Metallurgical creep limits the maximum Turbine Inlet Temperature (TIT).
Question 9 • statement-based
Consider the following: 1. Net thrust is zero when \( V_e = V_0 \). 2. Overall efficiency is the product of thermal efficiency and propulsive efficiency. Which are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are foundational truths in gas turbine propulsion aerothermodynamics.
Question 10 • numerical
An engine consumes fuel with a lower heating value of \( 43,000 \text{ kJ/kg} \) at a rate of \( 1.5 \text{ kg/s} \). If the thermal efficiency is \( 35\% \), what is the total thermal power added to the core?
A 15,050 kW
B 22,575 kW
C 43,000 kW
D 64,500 kW
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Total fuel energy input rate = \( \dot{m}_f \cdot h_{PR} = 1.5 \times 43,000 = 64,500 \text{ kW} \). Thermal power added = \( 64,500 \times 0.35 = 22,575 \text{ kW} \).
Question 11 • conceptual
Why do ultra-high bypass (UHB) turbofan engines experience a decrease in propulsive efficiency if bypass ratio is increased indefinitely without design optimization?
A Fan diameter and nacelle skin friction drag penalties outweigh thrust gains
B Turbine inlet temperature drops to zero
C Fuel calorific value increases beyond combustion limits
D Core mass flow rate becomes supersonic
Solution & Technical Rationale:
While propulsive efficiency increases with bypass ratio, extremely large fan nacelles introduce severe weight and skin friction drag penalties.
Question 12 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Overall engine efficiency decreases at high altitudes during subsonic cruise compared to sea level static runs. Reason (R): Ambient temperature drops at altitude, altering the thermal cycle expansion ratio.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
C (A) is false but (R) is true.
D Both (A) and (R) are false.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Overall efficiency often improves or remains stable at high altitude due to lower ambient temperatures increasing effective thermal efficiency, making Assertion (A) false.

Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
For a turbojet engine, maximum propulsive efficiency occurs theoretically when:
A Exhaust velocity equals zero
B Flight velocity equals exhaust velocity
C Flight velocity is twice the exhaust velocity
D Exhaust velocity approaches infinity
Rationale:
Mathematically, \( \eta_p = \frac{2}{1 + (V_e/V_0)} \). As \( V_e \to V_0 \), \(\eta_p \to 1.0\) (though thrust goes to zero).
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
If \( \eta_t = 0.40 \) and \( \eta_p = 0.60 \), what is the overall efficiency \( \eta_o \)?
A 0.15
B 0.24
C 0.50
D 1.00
Rationale:
\( \eta_o = \eta_t \cdot \eta_p = 0.40 \times 0.60 = 0.24 \).
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
Which engine architecture provides the highest propulsive efficiency for regional transports flying at Mach 0.6?
A Pure turbojet
B Low-bypass turbofan
C Turboprop
D Ramjet
Rationale:
Turboprops dominate low Mach regimes because large propellers yield superior propulsive efficiency.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
In a gas turbine engine, thermal efficiency is primarily a function of:
A Flight Mach number and ambient pressure
B Overall pressure ratio and turbine inlet temperature
C Propeller diameter and gear ratio
D Exhaust nozzle exit area
Rationale:
Brayton cycle thermal efficiency depends directly on compressor pressure ratio and peak turbine entry temperature.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the effect of increasing flight velocity on propulsive efficiency for a fixed exhaust velocity \( V_e \)?
A Decreases propulsive efficiency
B Increases propulsive efficiency
C Has no effect on propulsive efficiency
D Reduces efficiency to zero instantly
Rationale:
As flight velocity \( V_0 \) approaches \( V_e \), the ratio \( V_e/V_0 \) decreases towards 1, increasing propulsive efficiency towards 100%.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC) is inversely proportional to:
A Overall efficiency
B Fuel heating value
C Exhaust velocity
D Compressor pressure ratio
Rationale:
Higher overall efficiency means less fuel is burned per unit thrust, resulting in lower specific fuel consumption.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
Why are ramjets inefficient at static ground conditions (\( V_0 = 0 \))?
A Turbines fail to spin
B Propulsive efficiency is zero because flight velocity is zero
C Fuel does not ignite without a spark plug
D Ambient pressure is too high
Rationale:
Propulsive efficiency formula \( \eta_p = \frac{2V_0 V_e}{V_e^2 + V_0^2} \) yields zero when \( V_0 = 0 \).
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
In an aircraft accident investigation involving high fuel burn rates, an Air Safety Officer evaluates overall efficiency using which primary sensor data?
A Pitot-static airspeed and fuel flow transmitters
B Cabin altitude and differential pressure sensors
C Landing gear squat switch signals
D Flap position potentiometer angles
Rationale:
Fuel flow transmitters combined with true airspeed data from air data computers provide the parameters required for efficiency and SFC calculations.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
Which DGCA regulatory requirement mandates continuous monitoring of engine health parameters and thermal efficiency trends?
A DGCA CAR Section 2, Airworthiness
B ICAO Annex 1, Personnel Licensing
C ICAO Annex 14, Aerodromes
D DGCA CAR Section 5, Air Safety
Rationale:
DGCA CAR Section 2 governs airworthiness requirements, including approved engine maintenance and performance monitoring.
