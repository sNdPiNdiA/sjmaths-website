Pressure ratio & thermal efficiency

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Engine Indicating and Crew Alerting System (EICAS) monitors Fan Speed ($N_1$), Core Speed ($N_2$), and Interstage Turbine Temperature (ITT) to calculate live pressure ratios.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: High compressor pressure ratios narrow compressor surge margins, requiring precise FADEC scheduling during takeoff and high-altitude upsets.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 Series 'C' and ICAO Annex 8 mandate strict adherence to engine operating limits and routine borescope inspections.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers analyze FDR pressure ratio trends to diagnose compressor stall, blade FOD, or thermal distress during incident probes.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Compressor pressure ratio ($\pi_c = \frac{P_{t3}}{P_{t2}}$) combined with turbine entry temperature dictates the ideal Brayton cycle thermal efficiency ($\eta_{th} = 1 - \frac{1}{\pi_c^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}}$).
  • Physical Meaning: It measures the overall fluid pressure increase across the gas turbine compression system.
  • Governing Law: Isentropic relations of state coupled with the First Law of Thermodynamics for open systems.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid under continuous fluid flow assumptions within rotating turbomachinery blades.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\pi_c$ = Compressor Total Pressure Ratio (Dimensionless)
  • $P_{t2}$ = Compressor Inlet Total Pressure ($Pa$ or $psi$)
  • $P_{t3}$ = Compressor Discharge Total Pressure ($Pa$ or $psi$)
  • $\eta_{th}$ = Thermal Efficiency of the Brayton Cycle (Dimensionless)
  • $\gamma$ = Ratio of Specific Heats for Air ($\approx 1.4$)
  • $T_t$ = Stagnation or Total Temperature ($K$ or $R$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Air entering the compressor encounters high-speed rotating rotor blades imparting kinetic energy to gas molecules.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Stator passages diffuse the flow, converting kinetic energy into static pressure and raising stagnation pressure.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Higher pressure ratios elevate working fluid density and enthalpy, boosting combustion intensity and net work output.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Thermal efficiency is defined by net work output divided by heat added.

$$\eta_{th} = \frac{W_{net}}{Q_{in}} = 1 - \frac{C_p(T_{t4} - T_{t5})}{C_p(T_{t3} - T_{t2})}$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Apply isentropic relations across compression and expansion stages.

$$\frac{T_{t3}}{T_{t2}} = \left(\frac{P_{t3}}{P_{t2}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} = \pi_c^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Substitute temperature ratios and turbine expansion ratios assuming equal pressure ratios.

$$\frac{T_{t4}}{T_{t5}} = \left(\frac{P_{t4}}{P_{t5}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} = \pi_c^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Simplify to express thermal efficiency solely as a function of pressure ratio and specific heat ratio.

$$\eta_{th} = 1 - \frac{1}{\pi_c^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Pressure ratio and thermal efficiency are strictly dimensionless quantities ($[M^0 L^0 T^0 \theta^0]$).
  • SI Unit: Dimensionless ratio, expressed as a numeric value or percentage (\%).
  • CGS Unit: Dimensionless ratio, identical to SI representation.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Inches of mercury ($inHg$) or pounds per square inch ($psi$) for absolute pressures before taking their ratio.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Thermal Efficiency vs Pressure Ratio Linearity

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming thermal efficiency increases linearly with compressor pressure ratio.

✅ UPSC Truth: Efficiency follows a diminishing returns logarithmic curve due to polytropic losses and high-temperature material limits.

Trap 2: Static vs Total Pressure Ratios

❌ Examiner Trap: Using static pressure measurements instead of stagnation pressures across compressor stations.

✅ UPSC Truth: Brayton cycle calculations strictly require total (stagnation) pressure ratios to account for dynamic head conversion.

Trap 3: Specific Heat Ratio Constancy

❌ Examiner Trap: Treating $\gamma$ as constant at $1.4$ throughout the entire gas turbine cycle.

✅ UPSC Truth: High turbine temperatures dissociate combustion gases, lowering effective $\gamma$ to approximately $1.33$ or lower.

Trap 4: Surge Margin Confusion

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing higher pressure ratios automatically improve engine surge margin.

✅ UPSC Truth: Higher pressure ratios compress the operating map, severely narrowing the surge margin and demanding active bleeding control.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An aircraft gas turbine engine operates with a compressor pressure ratio ($\pi_c$) of $16:1$. Assuming ambient air specific heat ratio $\gamma = 1.4$, compute the ideal thermal efficiency of the Brayton cycle.

Given Flight Parameters:
Compressor Pressure Ratio $\pi_c = 16$; Specific Heat Ratio $\gamma = 1.4$
Governing Formula:
$$\eta_{th} = 1 - \frac{1}{\pi_c^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
1. Compute the exponent term: $(\gamma - 1) / \gamma = (1.4 - 1) / 1.4 = 0.4 / 1.4 = 0.2857. 2. Evaluate pressure ratio term: $16^{0.2857} = \left(2^4\right)^{0.2857} = 2^{1.1428} = 2.2045. 3. Calculate thermal efficiency: $\eta_{th} = 1 - \frac{1}{2.2045} = 1 - 0.4536 = 0.5464$.
Final Answer: $54.64% (or 0.5464)$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

High thermal efficiency reduces specific fuel consumption (SFC), directly extending aircraft range and ensuring compliance with mandatory fuel reserves during flight planning audits.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary thermodynamic effect of increasing the compressor pressure ratio in an ideal Brayton cycle?
A Decreases thermal efficiency
B Increases ideal thermal efficiency
C Reduces turbine entry temperature
D Eliminates combustion losses
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Increasing the compressor pressure ratio expands the temperature range of the cycle, which directly increases the ideal thermal efficiency according to Brayton cycle relations.
Question 2 • statement-based
Consider the following statements regarding compressor pressure ratio (CPR): 1. CPR is the ratio of static pressure at compressor exit to compressor inlet. 2. Higher CPR increases core air density. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is false because CPR is defined using total (stagnation) pressures, not static pressures. Statement 2 is true as compression raises fluid density.
Question 3 • numerical
An ideal gas turbine engine has a compressor pressure ratio of 32 and $\gamma = 1.4$. What is the temperature ratio $T_{t3}/T_{t2}$ across the compressor?
A 2.05
B 2.46
C 3.12
D 4.01
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $T_{t3}/T_{t2} = \pi_c^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma} = 32^{0.4/1.4} = 32^{0.2857} \approx 2.46$.
Question 4 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Real gas turbine thermal efficiency does not increase indefinitely with extremely high pressure ratios. Reason (R): Polytropic efficiency losses and material temperature limits restrict the gains from ultra-high pressure ratios.
A Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
B Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
C A is true, but R is false.
D A is false, but R is true.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct; mechanical losses and compressor discharge air becoming too hot to effectively cool turbine blades offset efficiency gains at extreme pressure ratios.
Question 5 • conceptual
Why do modern high-bypass turbofans utilize multi-spool compressor designs?
A To reduce total engine weight to zero
B To optimize rotational speeds and maintain high surge margins across varying pressure ratios
C To eliminate the need for fuel controls
D To bypass turbine cooling requirements
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Multi-spool architectures allow low-pressure and high-pressure compressors to rotate at their optimal aerodynamic speeds, preventing stall and improving surge margins at high pressure ratios.
Question 6 • numerical
Calculate the thermal efficiency of a Brayton cycle with $\pi_c = 9$ and $\gamma = 1.4$.
A 33.3%
B 42.6%
C 50.0%
D 61.2%
Solution & Technical Rationale:
$\eta_{th} = 1 - (9)^{-0.4/1.4} = 1 - (9)^{-0.2857} = 1 - 0.574 = 0.426$ or 42.6%.
Question 7 • statement-based
Which parameters directly affect the actual thermal efficiency of a real gas turbine engine? 1. Compressor polytropic efficiency 2. Turbine polytropic efficiency 3. Combustor pressure losses Select the correct answer using the code below:
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2, and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Real thermal efficiency is degraded by aerodynamic irreversibilities in both compressor and turbine, as well as pressure drops across the combustion chamber.
Question 8 • conceptual
What is the consequence of exceeding the design compressor pressure ratio envelope during flight maneuvers?
A Flameout or compressor surge/stall
B Increased bypass ratio
C Infinite thermal efficiency
D Negative thrust generation
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Exceeding pressure ratio limits disrupts blade incidence angles, leading to flow separation, compressor stall, or destructive surge.
Question 9 • numerical
If compressor inlet total temperature $T_{t2} = 288$ K and pressure ratio is $16$ with $\gamma = 1.4$, what is the compressor exit stagnation temperature $T_{t3}$?
A 576 K
B 634 K
C 710 K
D 815 K
Solution & Technical Rationale:
$T_{t3} = T_{t2} \times \pi_c^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma} = 288 \times 16^{0.2857} = 288 \times 2.2045 = 634.9$ K.
Question 10 • conceptual
How does the specific heat ratio $\gamma$ change inside the turbine section compared to the compressor inlet?
A Increases due to higher pressure
B Decreases due to high temperature and combustion gas dissociation
C Remains constant at 1.4
D Drops to zero
Solution & Technical Rationale:
High combustion temperatures cause triatomic gas formation and molecular dissociation, reducing $\gamma$ from 1.4 to approximately 1.33.

Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct; thermal efficiency improvements directly yield lower specific fuel consumption, which is critical for aircraft range.
