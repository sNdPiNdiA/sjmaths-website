First & second laws

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: FADEC utilizes inter-turbine temperature (ITT) and exhaust gas temperature (EGT) sensor inputs to monitor thermodynamic cycle health.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Exceeding turbine entry temperature (TET) limits compromises material creep life and triggers engine over-temperature maintenance flags.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 Series 'B' Part VII mandates strict adherence to approved engine operating limits and thermodynamic cycle logs.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Investigators analyze FDR EGT traces against first and second law efficiencies to detect internal blade distress or compressor stalls.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

The first and second laws of thermodynamics establish energy conservation and directional irreversibility for open propulsion systems: $$dQ - dW = dE$$, and $$\oint \frac{dQ}{T} \le 0$$.
  • Physical Meaning: The First Law tracks total energy accounting, while the Second Law governs thermodynamic availability and entropy generation in gas turbine cycles.
  • Governing Law: Combined into the non-flow and steady-flow energy equations (SFEE) paired with the Clausius inequality.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid for macroscopic control volumes across compressor, combustor, turbine, and nozzle components.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $Q$ = Heat transfer into or out of the control volume ($J$)
  • $W$ = Work done by or on the system ($J$)
  • $E$ = Total stored energy of the system ($J$)
  • $h$ = Specific enthalpy ($J/kg$)
  • $s$ = Specific entropy ($J/(kg \cdot K)$)
  • $\dot{m}$ = Mass flow rate ($kg/s$)
  • $T$ = Absolute temperature ($K$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Thermal energy input elevates random molecular translational, rotational, and vibrational kinetic velocities within the air-fuel working fluid.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Expansion across turbine blades and exhaust nozzles converts thermal enthalpy into directed momentum via pressure and temperature gradients.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Irreversible losses through boundary layer friction and shock waves generate entropy, reducing overall engine thrust specific fuel consumption (TSFC).

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Steady-Flow Energy Equation

Apply conservation of energy to a control volume with single inlet and exit.

$$\dot{Q} - \dot{W}_c = \dot{m}\left(h_2 - h_1 + \frac{v_2^2 - v_1^2}{2} + g(z_2 - z_1)\right)$$
Step 2: Second Law Entropy Balance

Apply the entropy inequality for adiabatic irreversible gas turbine components.

$$s_2 - s_1 = \int \frac{dq}{T} + s_{gen} \ge 0$$
Step 3: Isentropic Efficiency Link

Relate actual enthalpy drop to ideal isentropic enthalpy drop across a turbine.

$$\eta_t = \frac{h_1 - h_2}{h_1 - h_{2s}}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Express temperature ratio in terms of pressure ratio for polytropic expansion.

$$\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}\eta_p}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Energy has dimensions $[M L^2 T^{-2}]$, specific enthalpy $[L^2 T^{-2}]$, and entropy $[L^2 T^{-2} \theta^{-1}]$.
  • SI Unit: Joules ($J$) for energy, Watts ($W$) for power, and $J/(kg \cdot K)$ for specific heat and entropy.
  • CGS Unit: Ergs for energy ($1 \text{ Erg} = 10^{-7} \text{ J}$) and calories for heat transfer ($1 \text{ cal} = 4.184 \text{ J}$).
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: BTU/lbm for specific enthalpy, horsepower (hp) for shaft work, and Rankine ($^\circ R$) for absolute temperature.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Adiabatic vs Isentropic Confusion

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming all adiabatic gas turbine components are frictionless and isentropic.

✅ UPSC Truth: Adiabatic means zero heat transfer ($Q=0$), but internal friction generates entropy ($s_{gen} > 0$), making actual processes polytropic.

Trap 2: Perpetual Motion Machine Fallacy

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing a 100% thermal efficiency cycle is achievable in a gas turbine.

✅ UPSC Truth: The Kelvin-Planck statement of the Second Law dictates that thermal efficiency must always be strictly less than Carnot efficiency ($1 - T_L/T_H$).

Trap 3: Work Sign Convention Errors

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing work done by the system with work done on the system in the steady-flow energy equation.

✅ UPSC Truth: Turbine work is extracted by the fluid (positive output), whereas compressor work requires external shaft input (negative work term).

Trap 4: Enthalpy vs Internal Energy in Open Systems

❌ Examiner Trap: Using internal energy change ($dU$) instead of enthalpy change ($dH$) for open-flow propulsion ducts.

✅ UPSC Truth: Open flow systems incorporate flow work ($Pdv$ or $v \, dp$), making enthalpy ($H = U + PV$) the correct property for energy balance.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An aircraft gas turbine compressor operates adiabatically with an inlet stagnation temperature of $288.15 \text{ K}$ and an inlet pressure of $101.325 \text{ kPa}$. If the pressure ratio is $12.0$ and the isentropic efficiency of compression is $0.85$, calculate the actual exit temperature.

Given Flight Parameters:
T1 = 288.15 K, P1 = 101.325 kPa, PR = 12.0, \eta_c = 0.85, \gamma = 1.4
Governing Formula:
$$T_{2s} = T_1 \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}} \quad \text{and} \quad \eta_c = \frac{T_{2s} - T_1}{T_2 - T_1}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute isentropic exit temperature: $T_{2s} = 288.15 \times (12.0)^{\frac{1.4 - 1}{1.4}} = 288.15 \times (12.0)^{0.2857} = 288.15 \times 2.1697 = 625.21 \text{ K}$. Step 2: Apply compressor isentropic efficiency definition: $0.85 = \frac{625.21 - 288.15}{T_2 - 288.15}$. Step 3: Solve for actual exit temperature: $T_2 - 288.15 = \frac{337.06}{0.85} = 396.54 \implies T_2 = 288.15 + 396.54 = 684.69 \text{ K}$.
Final Answer: $684.69 K$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate calculation of compressor discharge temperature ensures that high-pressure bleed air does not exceed structural thermal limits of downstream airframe pneumatic and environmental control systems.

What does the First Law of Thermodynamics state when applied to an adiabatic gas turbine control volume?
A Net heat transfer equals net work output
B Enthalpy drop plus kinetic energy change equals shaft work extracted
C Entropy remains constant across all components
D Total pressure must increase through the turbine
Solution & Technical Rationale:
For an adiabatic open system (Q=0), the steady-flow energy equation equates enthalpy and kinetic energy changes directly to shaft work output.
Question 2 • conceptual
Which thermodynamic law fundamentally limits the thermal efficiency of any gas turbine engine?
A Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics
B First Law of Thermodynamics
C Second Law of Thermodynamics
D Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The Second Law dictates that no cyclic heat engine can convert 100% of thermal energy into mechanical work, establishing maximum Carnot and cycle efficiency limits.
Question 3 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding gas turbine entropy changes: 1. Isentropic compression is completely reversible. 2. Actual compressor processes always experience an increase in entropy. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Isentropic processes are by definition reversible and adiabatic ($\Delta s = 0$). Actual fluid friction in compressors generates entropy ($\Delta s > 0$). Both statements are correct.

Question 5 • assertion_reasoning
Assertion (A): Polytropic efficiency of a multi-stage axial compressor is higher than its overall adiabatic efficiency. Reason (R): Polytropic efficiency accounts for stage-by-stage re-entry of lost work into internal energy, which benefits subsequent compression stages.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)

C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:

Question 6 • conceptual
What is the primary effect of combustion chamber pressure losses on the Second Law performance of a gas turbine?
A Increases thermal efficiency
B Reduces available turbine expansion work and generates entropy
C Increases mass flow rate through the core
D Decreases compressor surge margin
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Pressure drop in the combustor without work addition represents an irreversible loss that destroys available energy, increases entropy, and reduces turbine power output.
Question 7 • statement
Which of the following parameters are conserved in an adiabatic, frictionless nozzle flow? 1. Stagnation Enthalpy 2. Static Temperature 3. Stagnation Entropy Select the correct answer using the code below:
A 1 and 3 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1, 2 and 3
D 1 only
Solution & Technical Rationale:
In an adiabatic frictionless nozzle, stagnation enthalpy is conserved (no work, no heat) and entropy is conserved (reversible). Static temperature drops as velocity increases.
Question 8 • conceptual
How does intercooling between compressor stages affect the specific work input required for compression?
A Increases work input
B Has no effect on work input
C Reduces work input closer to isothermal compression
D Increases compressor surge margin only
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Intercooling removes heat between compression stages, lowering air temperature and reducing the specific work required to achieve high pressure ratios.
Question 9 • numerical
A turbine expands air from $1200 \text{ K}$ to $800 \text{ K}$ adiabatically. If the actual enthalpy drop is $400 \text{ kJ/kg}$ and the ideal isentropic enthalpy drop is $450 \text{ kJ/kg}$, what is the turbine isentropic efficiency?
A 85.5%
B 88.9%
C 92.1%
D 95.0%
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Turbine isentropic efficiency $\eta_t = \frac{\text{Actual Drop}}{\text{Ideal Drop}} = \frac{400}{450} = 0.8888$ or $88.9\%$. Verify calculation: $400/450 = 88.89\%$, matching option B.

Reason (R): High exhaust velocity represents unconverted kinetic energy leaving the control volume, creating a trade-off between propulsive and thermal efficiencies.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Overall aircraft propulsion efficiency is the product of thermal and propulsive efficiencies, highlighting the fundamental trade-off between exhaust kinetic energy and cycle temperature limits.
Question 11 • conceptual
What thermodynamic phenomenon does the Clausius inequality quantify in gas turbine cycle analysis?
A Mechanical work output
B Reversibility and entropy generation of cycles
C Compressor surge boundary
D Fuel-air ratio stoichiometric limits
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The Clausius inequality states that for any thermodynamic cycle, the cyclic integral of heat divided by absolute temperature is less than or equal to zero, quantifying irreversibility.
Question 12 • statement
Which factors contribute to entropy generation in a gas turbine engine? 1. Combustion process 2. Boundary layer friction on turbine blades 3. Shock waves in supersonic inlets Select the correct answer using the code below:
A 1 and 2 only

Combustion is highly irreversible, blade boundary layer friction generates viscous losses, and shock waves introduce wave drag and entropy generation. All three contribute.
