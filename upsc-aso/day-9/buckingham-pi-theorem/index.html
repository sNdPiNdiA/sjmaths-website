Buckingham Pi theorem

Day 9 of 129 Fluid Mechanics & Machinery Phase 1: Foundations 90-120 min Master Class

1. Understand (From Absolute Zero, 5–10 min)

In fluid dynamics and aeronautical engineering, we frequently encounter complex physical phenomena governed by multiple variables—such as the aerodynamic drag of a transonic wing profile, which depends on velocity, air density, dynamic viscosity, wing geometry, and air compressibility. Investigating these phenomena through purely exhaustive experimental sweeps of each variable is economically and computationally prohibitive. Buckingham's $\Pi$ Theorem provides the mathematical framework to systematically reduce the dimensional representation of any physical problem, simplifying a functional relationship of $n$ dimensional variables into a equivalent relationship of $k = n - r$ independent dimensionless parameters (known as $\Pi$-groups), where $r$ is the rank of the dimensional matrix (typically equal to the number of fundamental dimensions $m$).

The Intuitive Foundation: Dimensional Homogeneity

The theorem is built upon the fundamental postulate of Dimensional Homogeneity: any physically meaningful equation of the form:

$$ f(q_1, q_2, q_3, \dots, q_n) = 0 $$

must have terms with matching dimensions on both sides of the equality. Because physical laws do not depend on the arbitrary choice of measurement units (e.g., Metric vs. Imperial), the equation can always be rewritten in terms of dimensionless ratios:

$$ φ(\Pi_1, \Pi_2, \dots, \Pi_k) = 0 $$

UPSC Terminology and Definitions

  • Primary/Fundamental Dimensions: Independent physical dimensions that cannot be expressed in terms of one another. In aerospace engineering, these are typically Mass $[M]$, Length $[L]$, Time $[T]$, and Temperature $[θ]$.
  • Secondary/Derived Dimensions: Dimensions expressed as combinations of primary dimensions. For example, Dynamic Viscosity $[μ] = [M L-1 T-1]$, and Force $[F] = [M L T-2]$.
  • Repeating Variables: A subset of the original variables selected to form the dimensional "basis" for constructing the dimensionless groups. They must collectively contain all primary dimensions and must not form a dimensionless group among themselves.

2. Learn the Concept (Complete Theoretical Foundation & Derivations, 20–25 min)

Let us construct the complete mathematical formulation of dimensional analysis and its direct connection to fluid mechanics governing equations, potential flows, and boundary layer physics.

1. Step-by-Step Working Principle of Buckingham's $\Pi$ Theorem

Consider a physical system governed by $n$ dimensional variables $q_1, q_2, \dots, q_n$.

Step A: List the variables and their dimensions. Write the dimensions of each variable in terms of the primary dimensions $M, L, T$ (and $θ$ if thermal effects are present).

Step B: Set up the Dimensional Matrix. Construct a matrix where rows correspond to the primary dimensions and columns correspond to the variables. The entries are the exponents of the dimensions of each variable.

Step C: Determine the Rank ($r$) of the Matrix. The rank $r$ is the size of the largest non-zero determinant within the dimensional matrix. Usually, $r = m$ (the number of primary dimensions), but if some dimensions always appear in the same combination, $r < m$.

Step D: Select $r$ Repeating Variables. Choose $r$ variables that will appear in every $\Pi$-group. They must meet these conditions:

  1. They must include all $r$ primary dimensions collectively.
  2. No two repeating variables can have the same dimensional formula or differ only by a dimensionless exponent.
  3. They must not form a dimensionless parameter by themselves.
  4. Typically, we choose one geometric property (e.g., chord length $c$, diameter $d$), one kinematic property (e.g., velocity $V$, angular velocity $ω$), and one dynamic/fluid property (e.g., density $ρ$, dynamic viscosity $μ$).

Step E: Formulate the $\Pi$-groups. The remaining $n - r$ variables are non-repeating variables ($qr+1, \dots, q_n$). Each $\Pi_i$ group is constructed as:

$$ \Pi_i = (q_1)a_i (q_2)b_i \dots (q_r)s_i · qr+i $$

Solve for the exponents $a_i, b_i, \dots, s_i$ by enforcing the condition that $[\Pi_i] = M^0 L^0 T^0$.

2. Mathematical Connection to Navier-Stokes Equations

The physical origins of standard dimensionless groups (such as Reynolds, Euler, and Froude numbers) are rooted in the non-dimensionalization of the Navier-Stokes Momentum Equation for an incompressible, Newtonian fluid:

$$ ρ ( ∂ \vec{V}∂ t + (\vec{V} · \nabla)\vec{V} ) = -\nabla p + μ \nabla^2 \vec{V} + ρ \vec{g} $$

To non-dimensionalize this, we define characteristic reference scales: reference length $L_c$, reference velocity $V_c$, reference time $t_c = L_c/V_c$, and reference pressure dynamic scaling $p_c = ρ V_c^2$. Define the dimensionless variables as:

$$ \vec{V}^* = \vec{V}V_c, \quad \vec{x}^* = \vec{x}L_c, \quad t^* = t V_cL_c, \quad p^* = p - p_∞ρ V_c^2, \quad \nabla^* = L_c \nabla, \quad \vec{g}^* = \vec{g}g $$

Substituting these dimensionless definitions back into the Navier-Stokes equation yields:

$$ ρ V_c^2L_c ( ∂ \vec{V}^*∂ t^* + (\vec{V}^* · \nabla^*)\vec{V}^* ) = -ρ V_c^2L_c \nabla^* p^* + μ V_cL_c^2 \nabla*2 \vec{V}^* + ρ g \vec{g}^* $$

Dividing the entire equation by the convective inertial scaling factor $ρ V_c^2L_c$ gives:

$$ ∂ \vec{V}^*∂ t^* + (\vec{V}^* · \nabla^*)\vec{V}^* = -\nabla^* p^* + [ μρ V_c L_c ] \nabla*2 \vec{V}^* + [ g L_cV_c^2 ] \vec{g}^* $$

Here, the dimensionless coefficients represent the reciprocal of classic dimensionless $\Pi$-groups:

  • Reynolds Number ($Re$): Ratio of inertial forces to viscous forces: $$ Re = ρ V_c L_cμ $$
  • Froude Number ($Fr$): Ratio of inertial forces to gravitational forces: $$ Fr = V_c√g L_c $$

3. Potential Flow Superpositions and Vorticity

In irrotational, inviscid aerodynamics, potential flow solutions can be constructed by superposing basic solutions of Laplace's equation $\nabla^2 φ = 0$. These include:

  • Uniform Flow: Velocity potential $φ = V_∞ x = V_∞ r \cosθ$.
  • Source/Sink: Volumetric flow rate $Q$. Velocity potential $φ = Q2π \ln r$.
  • Doublet: Created by placing a source and sink infinitely close. Strength $\kappa = \limΔ s \to 0 (Q Δ s)$. Velocity potential $φ = \kappa \cosθ2π r$.
  • Vortex: Circulation $\Gamma$. Velocity potential $φ = \Gamma2π θ$, with velocity components $V_r = 0$, $V_θ = \Gamma2π r$ (representing a free, irrotational vortex).

Superposing a uniform flow, a doublet, and a vortex yields the potential flow past a rotating cylinder with circulation, generating lift described by the Kutta-Joukowski Theorem:

$$ L' = ρ_∞ V_∞ \Gamma $$

Vorticity $\vec{ω}$ is mathematically defined as the curl of the velocity vector:

$$ \vec{ω} = \nabla × \vec{V} $$

For an irrotational flow, $\vec{ω} = 0$. In real fluids, viscous effects generate vorticity at the solid boundary walls, which then diffuses and convects into the boundary layer.

4. Boundary Layer Separation Physics

Under a positive pressure gradient ($∂ p∂ x > 0$, known as an adverse pressure gradient), fluid molecules within the boundary layer experience deceleration. Because the momentum near the wall is already depleted due to viscous friction, the velocity gradient at the wall drops to zero. This marks the point of boundary layer separation:

$$ \left. ∂ u∂ y |y=0 = 0 \quad (Separation Point) $$

Downstream of this point, $\left. ∂ u∂ y |y=0 < 0$, causing flow reversal (recirculation), which increases pressure drag (form drag) and leads to wing stall.

3. Memorize Important Facts (10 min)

  • Core Formula (Buckingham $\Pi$ Theorem): $$ k = n - r $$ where $n$ is the total number of physical variables, $r$ is the rank of the dimensional matrix, and $k$ is the number of independent dimensionless $\Pi$-groups.
  • Dynamic Similarity Scaling Relations:
    • Reynolds Similarity: $( ρ V Lμ )_m = ( ρ V Lμ )_p$
    • Froude Similarity: $( V√g L )_m = ( V√g L )_p$
    • Mach Similarity: $( Va )_m = ( Va )_p$
  • Kutta-Joukowski Lift Equation: $$ L' = ρ_∞ V_∞ \Gamma \quad [ Units: N/m or M T-2 ] $$
  • Boundary Layer Separation Condition:
    • $\left. ∂ u∂ y |y=0 > 0$: Attached Flow (Favorable or mild adverse gradient)
    • $\left. ∂ u∂ y |y=0 = 0$: Critical Separation Point
    • $\left. ∂ u∂ y |y=0 < 0$: Separated, Reversed Flow

4. Aviation Application (5–10 min)

In aerospace design and flight testing, Buckingham's $\Pi$ theorem is a foundational tool for scaling wind-tunnel models and validating computational fluid dynamics (CFD) codes.

1. The Paradox of Wind Tunnel Scaling

When testing a scaled model of an aircraft in a wind tunnel, we must maintain physical similarity with the full-scale flight conditions. Complete dynamic similarity requires matching both the Reynolds number ($Re$, representing viscous scale effects) and the Mach number ($Ma$, representing compressibility effects):

$$ Re_m = Re_p \implies ρ_m V_m L_mμ_m = ρ_p V_p L_pμ_p $$ $$ Ma_m = Ma_p \implies V_ma_m = V_pa_p $$

If we use the same working fluid (air at the same temperature, meaning $a_m = a_p$ and $ν_m = ν_p$), Mach matching dictates $V_m = V_p$. However, Reynolds matching then requires:

$$ L_m = L_p $$

This means a scaled model ($L_m < L_p$) cannot achieve complete similarity in the same fluid under ambient conditions. To resolve this, aerospace engineers design specialized facilities, such as cryogenic wind tunnels (where lowering temperature drastically reduces $ν$) or pressurized wind tunnels (where increasing density $ρ$ compensates for the reduced scale $L_m$).

2. Vortex Lift and Wingtip Vortices

On delta wings at high angles of attack, flow separation is controlled by forming a stable, highly organized leading-edge vortex (LEV). The circulation $\Gamma$ of this vortex, characterized using dimensionless scaling $\GammaV_∞ c$, generates a low-pressure region over the upper wing surface, creating non-linear "vortex lift." At the wingtips of conventional aircraft, the pressure differential between the upper and lower surfaces causes flow to wrap around the tip, generating strong trailing vortices. Buckingham's theorem shows that the strength of these trailing vortices scales as:

$$ \Gamma \sim L'ρ_∞ V_∞ $$

5. UPSC ASO Focus (10 min)

  • The Matrix Rank Trap: UPSC exams often test cases where the number of primary dimensions is $3$ ($M, L, T$), but the actual rank $r$ of the dimensional matrix is $2$. This happens when two dimensions only appear in a fixed ratio, such as acceleration ($LT-2$) and gravity ($LT-2$) in problems without independent mass terms. Always calculate the determinant of the matrix rather than assuming $r = 3$.
  • Weber Number ($We$): Used in fuel droplet atomization studies inside gas turbine combustors: $$ We = ρ V^2 Lσ $$ where $σ$ is surface tension ($[M T-2]$).
  • Boundary Layer Separation Criteria: Candidates often confuse the pressure gradient and the velocity gradient. A positive pressure gradient ($∂ p∂ x > 0$) is *necessary* but not *sufficient* on its own to cause separation; separation only occurs when the velocity gradient at the wall $\left. ∂ u∂ y |y=0$ drops to exactly zero.
  • Doublet Strength Units: The strength of a 2D doublet $\kappa$ has dimensions of $[L^3 T-1]$, whereas a 2D source/sink strength $Q$ (per unit depth) has dimensions of $[L^2 T-1]$.

6. Practice Questions (30 Exhaustive UPSC ASO MCQs)

Q1. An aerodynamic drag force $D$ is known to depend on the aircraft forward velocity $V$, characteristic wing chord $c$, fluid density $ρ$, fluid dynamic viscosity $μ$, and bulk modulus of elasticity of the fluid $K$. Applying the Buckingham Pi theorem, what is the minimum number of independent dimensionless $\Pi$-groups required to characterize this relationship?
A) 2
B) 3
C) 4
D) 5
The total number of variables is $n = 6$ ($D, V, c, ρ, μ, K$). Let's list their dimensions:
  • $D = [M L T-2]$
  • $V = [L T-1]$
  • $c = [L]$
  • $rho = [M L-3]$
  • $μ = [M L-1 T-1]$
  • $K = [M L-1 T-2]$
The fundamental dimensions involved are $M, L, T$ ($m = 3$). Let's check the rank $r$ of the dimensional matrix using the repeating variables $(ρ, V, c)$: $$ \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -3 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = 1 · (0 - (-1)) = 1 \neq 0 $$ The rank is indeed $r = 3$. Therefore, the number of independent $\Pi$-groups is: $$ k = n - r = 6 - 3 = 3 $$
Q2. In a calibration experiment for a wind tunnel model, the drag force $D$ is expressed as $D = φ(ρ, V, L, μ)$. If we choose $(ρ, V, L)$ as the repeating variables, which of the following represents the correct functional form of the dimensionless drag coefficient ($\Pi_1$)?
A) $\Pi_1 = Dρ V L$
B) $\Pi_1 = Dρ V^2 L^2$
C) $\Pi_1 = Dρ^2 V^2 L^3$
D) $\Pi_1 = Dμ V L$
Let $\Pi_1 = ρ^a V^b L^c D^1$. Expressing this in fundamental dimensions: $$ [M^0 L^0 T^0] = [M L-3]^a [L T-1]^b [L]^c [M L T-2] $$ Equating exponents:
  • For $M$: $a + 1 = 0 \implies a = -1$
  • For $T$: $-b - 2 = 0 \implies b = -2$
  • For $L$: $-3a + b + c + 1 = 0 \implies -3(-1) + (-2) + c + 1 = 0 \implies 3 - 2 + c + 1 = 0 \implies c = -2$
Thus, $\Pi_1 = ρ-1 V-2 L-2 D = Dρ V^2 L^2$. This corresponds to option B.
Q3. A 1:10 scaled model of an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) is tested in a pressurized wind tunnel to match the Reynolds number. The prototype flies at a speed of $30 m/s$ in atmospheric air at $15^\circC$ and $1 atm$. The wind tunnel uses air at $15^\circC$ but compressed to $10 atm$ pressure. Assuming viscosity remains constant, what must be the velocity of the flow in the wind tunnel to achieve dynamic similarity?
A) $300 m/s$
B) $30 m/s$
C) $3 m/s$
D) $0.3 m/s$
For dynamic similarity, $Re_m = Re_p \implies ρ_m V_m L_mμ_m = ρ_p V_p L_pμ_p$. Since the temperature is the same, $μ_m = μ_p$. Using the ideal gas law $p = ρ R T$, density is directly proportional to pressure: $$ ρ_mρ_p = p_mp_p = 101 = 10 $$ The geometric scale ratio is $L_m/L_p = 1/10$. Substituting these values into the Reynolds relation: $$ 10 · V_m · 110 = 1 · V_p · 1 \implies V_m = V_p = 30 m/s $$
Q4. In some physical problems, the fundamental dimensions of mass $M$ never appear independently but always as part of force $F$. If a system consists of $n$ physical variables containing $m$ physical dimensions, and the rank of the dimensional matrix $r$ is less than $m$, what is the correct number of independent $\Pi$-groups according to the Buckingham theorem?
A) $n - m$
B) $n - r$
C) $n - m + r$
D) $n$
The Buckingham Pi theorem states that the number of dimensionless groups is $k = n - r$, where $r$ is the rank of the dimensional matrix. Although $r$ is often equal to the number of fundamental dimensions $m$, this is not always true. If the variables contain $m$ fundamental dimensions but their matrix has a rank $r < m$, we must use $r$ repeating variables, giving $n - r$ groups.
Q5. For potential flow past a circular cylinder of radius $R$ with a free stream velocity $V_∞$, a doublet of strength $\kappa$ is placed at the origin. What is the relation between the doublet strength $\kappa$, $V_∞$, and $R$?
A) $\kappa = 2 π V_∞ R$
B) $\kappa = 2 π V_∞ R^2$
C) $\kappa = π V_∞ R^2$
D) $\kappa = 4 π V_∞ R^2$
The stream function for the potential flow past a doublet of strength $\kappa$ superposed with a uniform flow $V_∞$ is: $$ ψ = V_∞ r \sinθ - \kappa \sinθ2π r = ( V_∞ r - \kappa2π r ) \sinθ $$ At the surface of the cylinder where $r = R$, the stream function must be constant ($ψ = 0$): $$ V_∞ R - \kappa2π R = 0 \implies \kappa = 2 π V_∞ R^2 $$

Q11. During a wind tunnel test, both Reynolds number similarity and Mach number similarity must be maintained between the scale model and the prototype. If the working fluid in the wind tunnel is air at the same temperature and pressure as the flight condition, which of the following statements is true?
A) Dynamic similarity can be achieved with any scale model size.
B) Dynamic similarity can only be achieved if the model scale ratio is $1:1$.
C) Only Mach number can be matched; Reynolds number mismatch is irrelevant.
D) Dynamic similarity can be achieved by doubling the model speed relative to the prototype.
To match both Mach and Reynolds numbers in the same fluid: $$ Ma_m = Ma_p \implies V_m = V_p \quad (since speed of sound a is equal) $$ $$ Re_m = Re_p \implies V_m L_mν = V_p L_pν \implies L_m = L_p \quad (since V_m = V_p) $$ Thus, matching both parameters in the same fluid is physically impossible unless the model is full-scale ($1:1$).
Q12. What is the physical significance of the Weber Number ($We = ρ V^2 Lσ$)?
A) Ratio of viscous force to surface tension force
B) Ratio of inertial force to elastic force
C) Ratio of inertial force to surface tension force
D) Ratio of pressure force to viscous force
The Weber number is defined as: $$ We = ρ V^2 Lσ $$ Where:
  • Inertial pressure scaling $≈ ρ V^2 \implies Inertial Force ≈ ρ V^2 L^2$
  • Surface Tension Force $≈ σ L$
The ratio of these two forces is: $$ ρ V^2 L^2σ L = ρ V^2 Lσ = We $$ Thus, it represents the ratio of inertial force to surface tension force.

Q19. Under what physical condition does the boundary layer velocity profile $u(y)$ exhibit an inflection point ($∂^2 u∂ y^2 = 0$ inside the boundary layer)?
A) In a favorable pressure gradient ($∂ p∂ x < 0$)
B) In an adverse pressure gradient ($∂ p∂ x > 0$)
C) In zero pressure gradient flow ($∂ p∂ x = 0$)
D) Only in turbulent boundary layers
From the boundary layer momentum equation at the wall ($y = 0$, $u = v = 0$): $$ μ \left. ∂^2 u∂ y^2 |y=0 = ∂ p∂ x $$ If there is an adverse pressure gradient ($∂ p∂ x > 0$), then $\left. ∂^2 u∂ y^2 |y=0 > 0$. Since the velocity gradient must eventually approach zero at the outer edge of the boundary layer ($∂^2 u∂ y^2 < 0$), the second derivative must pass through zero. This creates an inflection point inside the boundary layer, which is a key indicator of oncoming separation.

For a 2D source, the flow is purely radial ($V_θ = 0$). $$ \Gamma = \oint \vec{V} · d\vec{s} = ∫02π V_θ \, (r dθ) = 0 $$ Therefore, the circulation is zero.
Q23. In an experiment modeling fuel atomization in a jet engine combustor, the mean droplet size $d$ depends on the nozzle diameter $D$, liquid density $ρ$, liquid velocity $V$, dynamic viscosity $μ$, and surface tension $σ$. If $(ρ, V, D)$ are selected as repeating variables, which of the following is the $\Pi$ group corresponding to surface tension?
A) $μρ V D$
B) $σρ V^2 D$
C) $ρ V Dσ$
D) $σ Dρ V$
Let $\Pi = ρ^a V^b D^c σ^1$. Dimensions: $$ [M^0 L^0 T^0] = [M L-3]^a [L T-1]^b [L]^c [M T-2] $$
  • For $M$: $a + 1 = 0 \implies a = -1$
  • For $T$: $-b - 2 = 0 \implies b = -2$
  • For $L$: $-3a + b + c = 0 \implies -3(-1) - 2 + c = 0 \implies 3 - 2 + c = 0 \implies c = -1$
Thus, $\Pi = ρ-1 V-2 D-1 σ = σρ V^2 D$ (which is the reciprocal of the Weber number).
Q24. An aircraft wing of chord $c$ is tested in a wind tunnel under Froude scale similarity. If the velocity ratio $V_p/V_m = 4$, what is the geometric scale ratio of the model $c_p/c_m$?
A) 2
B) 4
C) 16
D) 8
Under Froude similarity: $$ Fr_p = Fr_m \implies V_p√g c_p = V_m√g c_m $$ $$ V_pV_m = √c_pc_m $$ Given $V_p/V_m = 4$: $$ 4 = √c_pc_m \implies c_pc_m = 16 $$
Q25. In potential flow, how is the strength of a 2D line source $Q$ defined?
A) Radial velocity at unit radius
B) Total volume flow rate per unit depth of the source
C) Circulation along the boundary
D) Velocity gradient at the origin
In 2D potential flow, a line source of strength $Q$ discharges a total volume of fluid per unit depth equal to $Q$. The velocity at a distance $r$ is $V_r = Q2π r$.
Q26. Which of the following conditions is necessary and sufficient for the boundary layer separation to occur on a smooth curved airfoil?
A) The pressure gradient must be adverse ($∂ p∂ x > 0$).
B) The pressure gradient must be favorable ($∂ p∂ x < 0$).
C) The velocity gradient at the wall must be zero ($\left. ∂ u∂ y |y=0 = 0$).
D) The flow must transition from laminar to turbulent.
An adverse pressure gradient is a necessary *pre-condition* for boundary layer separation, but it is not sufficient on its own (flow can remain attached under mild adverse gradients). The *necessary and sufficient* condition that defines the exact point of separation is when the wall velocity gradient drops to zero ($\left. ∂ u∂ y |y=0 = 0$).
Q27. The power $P$ developed by a wind turbine depends on the wind velocity $V$, air density $ρ$, rotor diameter $D$, and turbine efficiency $\eta$ (dimensionless). How many $\Pi$-groups can represent this system, and what is the standard dimensionless power coefficient ($C_P$)?
A) 2 groups; $C_P = Pρ V^2 D^2$
B) 2 groups; $C_P = Pρ V^3 D^2$
C) 3 groups; $C_P = Pρ^2 V^3 D^3$
D) 1 group; $C_P = Pμ V D$
Total variables $n = 5$ ($P, V, ρ, D, \eta$). Fundamental dimensions $m = 3$ ($M, L, T$). Rank $r = 3$. Number of $\Pi$-groups $k = n - r = 5 - 3 = 2$. One group is the dimensionless efficiency $\eta$ itself. The other group is found by non-dimensionalizing power $P$ ($[M L^2 T-3]$) using $(ρ, V, D)$: $$ \Pi_1 = Pρ^a V^b D^c $$ $$ [M L^2 T-3] = [M L-3]^a [L T-1]^b [L]^c = [M^a L-3a+b+c T-b] $$
  • $a = 1$
  • $-b = -3 \implies b = 3$
  • $-3a + b + c = 2 \implies -3(1) + 3 + c = 2 \implies c = 2$
$$ \Pi_1 = Pρ V^3 D^2 = C_P $$
Q28. In fluid mechanics, the Euler number $Eu$ represents the ratio of which of the following forces?
A) Viscous force to pressure force
B) Pressure force to inertial force
C) Inertial force to gravity force
D) Elastic force to surface tension force
The Euler number $Eu$ is defined as: $$ Eu = Δ pρ V^2 $$ Where:
  • $Δ p \implies Pressure Force ≈ Δ p L^2$
  • $ρ V^2 \implies Inertial Force ≈ ρ V^2 L^2$
Thus, $Eu$ represents the ratio of pressure forces to inertial forces.
Q29. A pipeline carrying oil ($ρ = 900 kg/m^3$, $ν = 10-4 m^2/s$) is modeled using water ($ρ = 1000 kg/m^3$, $ν = 10-6 m^2/s$) in a 1:5 scale model. If the prototype oil velocity is $2 m/s$, what water velocity is required in the model to achieve dynamic similarity?
A) $0.1 m/s$
B) $10 m/s$
C) $0.4 m/s$
D) $0.2 m/s$
Reynolds similarity requires: $$ V_m L_mν_m = V_p L_pν_p $$ $$ V_m = V_p ( L_pL_m ) ( ν_mν_p ) $$ Given $L_p/L_m = 5$, $ν_m = 10-6 m^2/s$, and $ν_p = 10-4 m^2/s$: $$ V_m = 2 × 5 × 10-610-4 = 10 × 10-2 = 0.1 m/s $$
Q30. In potential flow, the velocity potential for a 2D line vortex of circulation $\Gamma$ is $φ = \Gamma2π θ$. What is the corresponding radial velocity component $V_r$?
A) $V_r = \Gamma2π r$
B) $V_r = 0$
C) $V_r = -\Gamma2π r$
D) $V_r = \Gamma θ2π r$
In polar coordinates, the radial velocity component is given by: $$ V_r = ∂ φ∂ r $$ For a line vortex, $φ = \Gamma2π θ$. Since this expression does not contain $r$: $$ V_r = ∂∂ r ( \Gamma2π θ ) = 0 $$ The flow is purely azimuthal: $V_θ = 1r ∂ φ∂ θ = \Gamma2π r$.

7. Grand Mini Test (10 Exam-Level Questions)

Q1. Consider the following statements regarding the selection of repeating variables in the Buckingham Pi theorem:
1. The repeating variables must collectively contain all primary dimensions of the system.
2. They must not form a dimensionless group on their own.
3. We should always choose dependent variables as repeating variables to simplify calculations.
Which of these statements are correct?
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 2 and 3 only
C) 1 and 3 only
D) 1, 2, and 3
Statements 1 and 2 are basic requirements for repeating variables. Statement 3 is incorrect: we should avoid choosing the main dependent variable (e.g., drag force $D$) as a repeating variable, because we want that variable to appear in only one dimensionless group (the dependent group, like $C_D$).
Q2. Assertion (A): In a wind-tunnel test of a sub-scale model aircraft in ambient air, matching both the model Froude number and model Reynolds number to the prototype is physically impossible.
Reason (R): Reynolds number matching requires $V_m/V_p ∝ L_p/L_m$, whereas Froude number matching requires $V_m/V_p ∝ √L_m/L_p$.
A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
C) A is true, but R is false.
D) A is false, but R is true.
Both statements are true:
  • Reynolds match: $V_m L_mν = V_p L_pν \implies V_mV_p = L_pL_m$
  • Froude match: $V_m√g L_m = V_p√g L_p \implies V_mV_p = √L_mL_p$
For a sub-scale model ($L_m < L_p$), these two ratios require opposing velocity scalings. This makes simultaneous matching in the same fluid physically impossible, making R the correct explanation of A.
Q3. What is the rank of the dimensional matrix formed by the physical variables $\{ Δ p, ρ, V \}$ using the fundamental dimensions $M, L, T$?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 3
D) 0
Let's write the dimensional formulas for each variable:
  • $Δ p = [M L-1 T-2]$
  • $ρ = [M L-3 T^0]$
  • $V = [M^0 L T-1]$
The dimensional matrix with rows $[M, L, T]$ and columns $[Δ p, ρ, V]$ is: $$ \mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & -3 & 1 \\ -2 & 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} $$ Let's compute the determinant: $$ \det(\mathbf{A}) = 1(3 - 0) - 1(1 - (-2)) + 0 = 3 - 3 = 0 $$ Because the determinant is zero, the rank of this $3 × 3$ matrix is less than 3. Let's check a $2 × 2$ sub-matrix, for example the top left: $$ \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & -3 \end{pmatrix} = -3 - (-1) = -2 \neq 0 $$ This means the rank of the dimensional matrix is exactly 2.
Q4. A potential flow is constructed by superposing a uniform flow $V_∞$ along the $x$-axis, a doublet of strength $\kappa$ at the origin, and a line vortex of circulation $\Gamma$ at the origin. What is the value of the pressure coefficient $C_p = p - p_∞12ρ V_∞^2$ at the top of the cylinder ($r = R, θ = π/2$)? (Assume doublet strength matches cylinder radius $R$).
A) $1 - ( 2 + \Gamma2π R V_∞ )^2$
B) $1 - ( 1 + \Gamma2π R V_∞ )^2$
C) $1 - 4 \sin^2θ$
D) $-3$
The velocity components on the surface of a rotating cylinder ($r = R$) are: $$ V_r = 0, \quad V_θ = -2 V_∞ \sinθ - \Gamma2π R $$ At the top of the cylinder ($θ = π/2$): $$ V_θ = -2 V_∞ - \Gamma2π R = -V_∞ ( 2 + \Gamma2π R V_∞ ) $$ The pressure coefficient from Bernoulli's equation is: $$ C_p = 1 - ( VsurfaceV_∞ )^2 = 1 - ( V_θV_∞ )^2 = 1 - ( 2 + \Gamma2π R V_∞ )^2 $$
Q5. Statement 1: In potential flow past a circular cylinder, the drag force acting on the cylinder is zero.
Statement 2: The velocity distribution in potential flow is symmetric about both the $x$ and $y$ axes, which prevents any net pressure force from acting in the drag direction.
A) Both Statement 1 and Statement 2 are true, and Statement 2 is the correct explanation of Statement 1.
B) Both Statement 1 and Statement 2 are true, but Statement 2 is not the correct explanation of Statement 1.
C) Statement 1 is true, but Statement 2 is false.
D) Statement 1 is false, but Statement 2 is true.
Statement 1 is true and describes **d'Alembert's Paradox**: an inviscid, potential flow produces zero net drag on any closed body. Statement 2 is also true and explains this paradox: the potential flow field is symmetric upstream and downstream, meaning the high-pressure recovery on the rear of the cylinder perfectly balances the high pressure on the front, resulting in zero net drag.
Q6. If the boundary layer on a flat plate is laminar, the boundary layer thickness $\delta$ grows with distance $x$ from the leading edge as $\delta ∝ x0.5$. Under these conditions, how does the wall shear stress $τ_w$ scale with $x$?
A) $τ_w ∝ x0.5$
B) $τ_w ∝ x-0.5$
C) $τ_w ∝ x-1.0$
D) $τ_w$ is independent of $x$
From boundary layer scaling, the velocity gradient at the wall scales as: $$ \left. ∂ u∂ y |y=0 \sim V_∞\delta $$ Since $\delta ∝ x0.5$ in a laminar boundary layer: $$ τ_w = μ \left. ∂ u∂ y |y=0 ∝ 1x0.5 = x-0.5 $$ This matches the Blasius boundary layer solution.
Q7. An aircraft model of scale $1:16$ is tested in a wind tunnel at $V_m = 80 m/s$ to match the prototype flight Mach number. If the air temperature in the wind tunnel is identical to the flight altitude temperature, what is the flight speed of the prototype?
A) $5 m/s$
B) $80 m/s$
C) $1280 m/s$
D) $320 m/s$
Mach number similarity requires: $$ Ma_m = Ma_p \implies V_ma_m = V_pa_p $$ Since the temperature is identical, the speed of sound is the same ($a_m = a_p$). $$ V_p = V_m = 80 m/s $$
Q8. For a physical system where viscous forces, surface tension forces, and inertial forces are all significant, what dimensionless parameter represents the ratio of the Reynolds number to the Weber number?
A) Ohnesorge Number ($Oh = μ√ρ σ L$)
B) Capillary Number ($Ca = μ Vσ$)
C) Froude Number ($Fr = V√gL$)
D) Euler Number ($Eu = Δ pρ V^2$)
Let's evaluate the ratio of the Weber number to the Reynolds number: $$ WeRe = ρ V^2 L / σρ V L / μ = ρ V^2 Lσ × μρ V L = μ Vσ $$ This is the definition of the Capillary Number ($Ca = μ Vσ$), which represents the ratio of viscous forces to surface tension forces.
Q9. For a 2D potential flow, what does the velocity potential $φ = A (x^2 - y^2)$ (where $A$ is a constant) represent?
A) Flow past a flat plate
B) Flow in a $90^°$ corner (stagnation point flow)
C) Flow past a circular cylinder
D) Flow produced by a source-sink pair
The velocity potential $φ = A(x^2 - y^2)$ yields the velocity components: $$ u = ∂ φ∂ x = 2Ax, \quad v = ∂ φ∂ y = -2Ay $$ This is the classical potential solution for flow in a $90^°$ corner, also known as planar stagnation-point flow (or Hiemenz flow in viscous fluid mechanics).
Q10. Assertion (A): In the boundary layer equations, the pressure gradient $∂ p∂ x$ is assumed to be constant across the boundary layer thickness (i.e., $∂ p∂ y ≈ 0$).
Reason (R): The boundary layer thickness $\delta$ is extremely small relative to the characteristic body length $L$, making normal convective acceleration terms negligible.
A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
C) A is true, but R is false.
D) A is false, but R is true.
Both statements are true. From Prandtl's boundary layer scale analysis, the momentum equation in the normal ($y$) direction simplifies to $∂ p∂ y = \mathcal{O}(\delta)$, which is approximately 0 since $\delta/L \ll 1$. Thus, the pressure is determined entirely by the outer inviscid flow and is transmitted unchanged through the boundary layer to the wall. This makes R the correct physical explanation of A.

8. Error Analysis & Common Traps (5 min)

Common Student Mistakes & UPSC Pitfalls

  • The Rank of Matrix Trap: Students often assume the rank $r$ of the dimensional matrix is always equal to the number of fundamental dimensions (usually $3$ for $M, L, T$). In problems involving purely kinematic variables (e.g., $V, ω, t, L$) where mass $M$ never appears, or thermal problems where dimensions are redundant, the actual matrix rank is $r < 3$. Always verify the matrix determinant before choosing the number of repeating variables.
  • Invalid Repeating Variable Combinations: A common error is choosing repeating variables that can form a dimensionless group by themselves. For example, selecting velocity $V$ and speed of sound $a$ is invalid because their ratio is already dimensionless (the Mach number). Repeating variables must be dimensionally independent.
  • Pressure Units vs. Pressure Gradient Units: In boundary layer problems, students frequently mix up pressure $p$ ($[M L-1 T-2]$) and pressure gradient $∂ p∂ x$ ($[M L-2 T-2]$). This error leads to incorrect dimensional matrices and incorrect $\Pi$-groups.
  • Confusing Dynamic Similarity Requirements: In UPSC ASO exam questions, students often incorrectly apply Froude similarity scaling to aerodynamics problems (which are dominated by Reynolds/Mach scales) or Reynolds similarity to free-surface naval vessel problems (which are dominated by Froude/wave-making scales). Keep the physical driver of the system in mind when selecting similarity criteria.
