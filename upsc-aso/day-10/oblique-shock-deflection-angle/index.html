Oblique shock – deflection angle

Day 10 of 129 Compressible Flow & Compressors Phase 1: Foundations 90-120 min Master Class

1. Understand (From Absolute Zero, 5–10 min)

Welcome to Day 10 of your advanced aerospace engineering training. As a world-renowned professor and your UPSC Air Safety Officer (ASO) mentor, I will guide you through one of the most critical phenomena in compressible fluid dynamics and high-speed flight mechanics: the Oblique Shock Wave and its Deflection Angle.

To understand this from absolute scratch, imagine a supersonic aircraft flying through the atmosphere at a Mach number greater than $1$ ($M > 1$). Because the aircraft travels faster than the local speed of sound, information about its presence cannot travel upstream. The gas molecules ahead of the vehicle have no prior warning. When these molecules strike the nose or a sharp corner (like a wedge or a ramp turned into the flow), they experience an extremely abrupt, almost discontinuous compression and deceleration. Unlike a normal shock wave which sits perfectly perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to the incoming flow, an oblique shock wave is inclined at an angle $\beta$ relative to the upstream flow direction.

The deflection angle, denoted by the symbol $\theta$ (theta), represents the angle by which the streamline of the gas is bent or turned away from its original path as it crosses the oblique shock wave. In physical terms, when a supersonic flow encounters a solid wedge pointing into the stream with a half-angle $\theta$, the flow must turn parallel to the wedge surface. The oblique shock wave stands off or is attached at the apex, forcing the flow to deflect by exactly $\theta$.

Physical Meaning & SI Units: Angles $\beta$ and $\theta$ are measured in degrees ($^\circ$) or radians. The upstream Mach number $M_1$ is a dimensionless quantity. The normal component of the upstream Mach number, $M_{1n} = M_1 \sin\beta$, dictates the strength of the shock wave. If $M_{1n} > 1$, a valid shock wave forms, resulting in a sudden drop in Mach number ($M_2 < M_1$), an increase in static pressure ($p_2 > p_1$), density ($\rho_2 > \rho_1$), and temperature ($T_2 > T_1$), accompanied by an irreversible increase in specific entropy ($s_2 > s_1$).

In standard UPSC ASO terminology, mastering oblique shocks is vital for analyzing supersonic air intakes (diffusers), hypersonic vehicle geometries, wave drag calculations, and analyzing aircraft stability in high-Mach regimes.

2. Learn the Concept (Complete Theoretical Foundation & Derivations, 20–25 min)

Let us build the complete theoretical foundation step-by-step. We begin with the fundamental definitions of the speed of sound and isentropic stagnation relations, proceed through the conservation laws governing normal shocks, and extend them via vector resolution to oblique shock waves. We will also examine how Fanno flow (friction) and Rayleigh flow (heat addition) interact with high-speed internal and external aerodynamics.

1. Speed of Sound & Isentropic Stagnation Relations:
The local speed of sound $a$ in a perfect gas is given by:

$$ a = \sqrt{\gamma R T} $$

where $\gamma$ is the ratio of specific heats ($\approx 1.4$ for air), $R$ is the specific gas constant, and $T$ is the static temperature. The Mach number is defined as $M = V/a$. The isentropic stagnation (total) temperature $T_0$ and pressure $p_0$ relations with static properties are:

$$ \frac{T_0}{T} = 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2, \quad \frac{p_0}{p} = \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2 \right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}} $$

2. Rankine-Hugoniot Normal Shock Relations:
For a normal shock wave ($M_{1n} = M_1$), the governing equations of mass, momentum, and energy yield the normal shock jump conditions. The downstream Mach number $M_2$ and static pressure ratio across a normal shock are:

$$ M_2^2 = \frac{2 + (\gamma - 1)M_1^2}{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma - 1)}, \quad \frac{p_2}{p_1} = 1 + \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma + 1}(M_1^2 - 1) $$

3. The $\theta-\beta-M$ Relation (The Core Oblique Shock Equation):
When a supersonic stream with Mach number $M_1$ encounters a deflection angle $\theta$ across an oblique shock inclined at wave angle $\beta$, we resolve the velocity vector into normal and tangential components. Because the tangential velocity component $V_{t1} = V_{t2}$ remains continuous across the shock (no pressure forces act tangentially to the shock surface), the redirection of flow is entirely governed by the normal component reduction. Applying continuity and momentum yields the famous $\theta-\beta-M$ relation:

$$ \tan\theta = 2 \cot\beta \frac{M_1^2 \sin^2\beta - 1}{M_1^2 (\gamma + \cos 2\beta) + 2} $$

4. Analytical Sub-Cases and Mathematical Properties:

  • Detached vs. Attached Shocks: For a given upstream Mach number $M_1$ and wedge deflection angle $\theta$, the $\theta-\beta-M$ relation yields two possible values for the shock angle $\beta$: the weak shock solution (smaller $\beta$, higher downstream Mach number, lower pressure jump) and the strong shock solution (larger $\beta$, subsonic downstream normal Mach number, very high pressure jump).
  • Maximum Deflection Angle ($\theta_{\max}$): For any given $M_1$, there is a maximum deflection angle $\theta_{\max}$. If the wedge angle exceeds $\theta_{\max}$, an attached oblique shock cannot exist; instead, a detached bow shock forms upstream of the body nose.
  • Sonic Downstream Condition: The locus where the downstream normal Mach number $M_{2n} = 1$ separates the weak and strong shock domains.

5. Fanno and Rayleigh Line Principles:

  • Fanno Flow: Steady, one-dimensional compressible flow in a constant-area duct with friction. The governing equations lead to the Fanno line on the $h-s$ (enthalpy-entropy) diagram. Friction always drives the Mach number toward $M = 1$ (choking).
  • Rayleigh Flow: Steady, one-dimensional compressible flow in a constant-area duct with frictionless heat transfer. Heat addition drives subsonic flow toward $M = 1$ and supersonic flow toward $M = 1$, creating thermal choking.

$$ \Delta s = c_p \ln\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right) - R \ln\left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right) > 0 \quad (\text{Entropy increases across any shock}) $$

Analytical details reveal that oblique shocks are fundamentally entropy-increasing dissipative gas dynamic phenomena. Total temperature remains constant across the shock ($T_{01} = T_{02}$), but total pressure experiences an irreversible drop ($p_{02} < p_{01}$).

3. Memorize Important Facts (10 min)

  • Core Formula 1 & Mach Ratios: Normal Mach number relation $M_{1n} = M_1 \sin\beta$ and downstream normal Mach number $M_{2n} = \sqrt{\frac{2 + (\gamma - 1)M_{1n}^2}{2\gamma M_{1n}^2 - (\gamma - 1)}}$.
  • Standard Shock / Isentropic Jump Constant: $\gamma = 1.4$ for calorically perfect air; total temperature conservation $T_{01} = T_{02}$, while stagnation pressure drops $p_{02} < p_{01}$.
  • Key Thermal / Friction Choking Property: Maximum deflection angle $\theta_{\max}$ occurs when the flow behind the shock becomes sonic relative to the shock wave component ($M_{2n} = 1$ or close to it depending on exact algebraic maximization).

4. Aviation Application (5–10 min)

In supersonic aviation and jet engine design, the oblique shock wave and its deflection angle are paramount. Consider the following key aeronautical engineering applications:

  1. Supersonic Inlet Diffusers (Intake Design): Modern supersonic aircraft (like fighter jets operating at Mach 2+) require complex multi-ramp or axisymmetric centerbody inlets. These ramps generate a series of controlled weak oblique shocks that successively decelerate the supersonic airflow and compress it efficiently before it reaches the face of the turbojet/turbofan compressor. By utilizing multiple oblique shocks followed by a normal terminal shock (mixed-compression inlet), total pressure recovery is maximized, and shock-induced boundary layer separation is minimized.
  2. Supersonic Wing and Fuselage Aerodynamics: Wedge-shaped airfoils and sharp-leading-edge delta wings (such as those on the Concorde or modern supersonic transport concepts) rely on oblique shock waves generated at the lower surface and expansion fans on the upper surface to generate lift while managing wave drag.
  3. Sonic Boom Generation: The displacement of air by a supersonic aircraft creates a 3D N-wave signature composed of a primary bow oblique shock and a tail oblique shock, which propagate to the ground as a dual sonic boom.

5. UPSC ASO Focus (10 min)

  • Frequently Asked Exam Facts:
    • Specific heat ratio for air is standardly taken as $\gamma = 1.4$.
    • For $M_1 \to \infty$, the maximum oblique shock wave angle $\beta_{\max} \approx \arcsin(1/\sqrt{\gamma}) \approx 57.5^\circ$ for air, and maximum deflection angle approaches a limiting value ($\approx 45.6^\circ$ for $\gamma = 1.4$).
    • Total temperature $T_0$ is invariant across any adiabatic shock wave ($T_{01} = T_{02}$).
  • Conceptual Traps:
    • Trap 1: Assuming total pressure $p_0$ is conserved across a shock. (False! Shocks are highly irreversible; entropy increases, hence $p_{02} < p_{01}$).
    • Trap 2: Confusing the deflection angle $\theta$ with the shock angle $\beta$. Remember, $\beta$ is the angle of the shock wave relative to the upstream flow direction, whereas $\theta$ is the angle by which the streamline is physically turned.

Solution: The deflection angle $\theta$ represents the angle by which the gas streamline is turned relative to the upstream flow direction when passing through the oblique shock wave. Option B is correct.

B) Exactly one (strong shock only)
C) Two (weak shock and strong shock)
D) Infinite
Solution: For $\theta < \theta_{\max}$, the $\theta-\beta-M$ relation yields two mathematical solutions for the shock angle $\beta$: the weak shock solution and the strong shock solution. Option C is correct.
Q3. Which component of the upstream Mach number $M_1$ governs the thermodynamic jump conditions across an oblique shock wave?
A) Tangential component $M_{1t} = M_1 \cos\beta$
B) Normal component $M_{1n} = M_1 \sin\beta$
C) Total Mach number $M_1$ directly

Solution: Across an oblique shock wave, the tangential velocity component remains unchanged, while the normal component $M_{1n} = M_1 \sin\beta$ dictates the normal shock relations. Option B is correct.
Q4. What happens to the stagnation (total) temperature $T_0$ across an oblique shock wave?
A) It increases significantly
B) It decreases due to expansion
C) It remains constant ($T_{01} = T_{02}$)
D) It drops to zero
Solution: Shock waves are adiabatic processes with no external heat addition or work done. Therefore, stagnation temperature is conserved: $T_{01} = T_{02}$. Option C is correct.
Q5. What occurs when the wedge deflection angle $\theta$ exceeds the maximum possible deflection angle $\theta_{\max}$ for a given upstream Mach number $M_1$?
A) An attached weak oblique shock forms smoothly
C) A detached bow shock forms upstream of the body apex

Q6. Which of the following thermodynamic properties always increases across an oblique shock wave?

B) Stagnation pressure $p_0$ and Mach number $M$

Solution: Across any shock wave, static pressure, temperature, density, and specific entropy increase, while Mach number and stagnation pressure decrease. Option A is correct.
Q7. In the weak oblique shock solution, the flow behind the shock wave is typically:

Q8. What is the tangential velocity component relationship across an oblique shock wave? ($V_{t1}$ and $V_{t2}$)
A) $V_{t1} > V_{t2}$
B) $V_{t1} = V_{t2}$
C) $V_{t2} = 0$
D) $V_{t1} < V_{t2}$
Solution: Because there are no pressure forces or viscous shear forces acting tangentially along an infinitely thin shock wave, the tangential velocity component is conserved: $V_{t1} = V_{t2}$. Option B is correct.
Q9. For a Mach number $M_1 = 2.0$ and $\gamma = 1.4$, what is the maximum possible deflection angle $\theta_{\max}$ approximately?

Q10. In Fanno flow, what is the limiting Mach number toward which any duct flow evolves due to friction?
A) $M = 0$
B) $M = 1$
C) $M = 2$
D) Infinity
Solution: Fanno flow represents adiabatic flow with friction in a constant-area duct. Friction always drives the Mach number toward the sonic state ($M = 1$), causing choking. Option B is correct.
Q11. Rayleigh flow describes frictionless, one-dimensional constant-area flow with:
A) Heat addition or removal
B) Pure wall friction
C) Isentropic area change
D) Normal shock interactions only
Solution: Rayleigh flow governs constant-area duct flow with heat transfer (addition or removal). Option A is correct.
Q12. What is the behavior of stagnation pressure $p_0$ across an oblique shock wave?

B) $p_{02} = p_{01}$

Solution: Because oblique shocks are irreversible thermodynamic processes involving entropy generation ($\Delta s > 0$), stagnation pressure decreases across the shock ($p_{02} < p_{01}$). Option C is correct.
Q13. In the limiting case where the deflection angle $\theta = 0^\circ$ for a supersonic flow, what does the oblique shock wave degenerate into?

Q14. Which of the following parameters increases across a strong oblique shock wave compared to a weak oblique shock wave at the same $M_1$ and $\theta$?

C) Stagnation pressure recovery $p_{02}/p_{01}$

Solution: Strong oblique shocks have larger shock angles $\beta$, greater entropy increases, higher static pressure and temperature ratios, and much lower stagnation pressure recovery compared to weak shocks. Option B is correct.

B) Multiple shocks accelerate the flow to hypersonic speeds

Solution: Gradual compression through a series of weak oblique shocks followed by a weak normal shock is thermodynamically more reversible than a single strong normal shock, resulting in higher stagnation pressure recovery. Option A is correct.

Solution: The normal component behaves exactly like a normal shock wave; thus, for an oblique shock, $M_{1n} > 1$ and $M_{2n} < 1$. Option B is correct.
Q18. Which parameter governs the maximum turning angle $\theta_{\max}$ for a supersonic flow over a wedge?

Solution: From the $\theta-\beta-M$ relation, $\theta_{\max}$ is a function solely of the upstream Mach number $M_1$ and the specific heat ratio $\gamma$. Option A is correct.
Q19. In Rayleigh flow, what is the effect of heat addition ($q > 0$) on subsonic flow ($M < 1$)?

B) It decelerates the flow toward $M = 0$
C) It leaves the Mach number unchanged
D) It turns the flow into a supersonic state immediately
Solution: In Rayleigh flow, heat addition increases the Mach number of subsonic flow toward the sonic state ($M = 1$), leading to thermal choking. Option A is correct.
Q20. What is the value of specific entropy change across an ideal isentropic compression wave (expansion fan or Mach wave)?

B) $\Delta s < 0$

Solution: Isentropic waves (such as Prandtl-Meyer expansion waves and infinitesimal Mach waves) are reversible and adiabatic, meaning there is no change in specific entropy ($\Delta s = 0$). Option C is correct.

D) It becomes a normal shock wave
Solution: When an oblique shock strikes a solid boundary, the flow behind the first shock is deflected toward the wall. To satisfy the flow tangency condition at the flat wall, a second reflected oblique shock is generated to turn the flow back parallel to the wall. Option B is correct.
Q22. Which of the following is true regarding the total temperature $T_0$ across Rayleigh flow with heat addition?

B) Total temperature increases due to heat addition ($T_{02} > T_{01}$)
C) Total temperature decreases ($T_{02} < T_{01}$)

Solution: By definition, Rayleigh flow involves heat transfer, so adding heat increases the stagnation (total) temperature ($T_{02} > T_{01}$). Option B is correct.
Q23. In the $\theta-\beta-M$ chart, for a fixed supersonic Mach number $M_1$, as the shock wave angle $\beta$ increases from the Mach angle to $90^\circ$, what happens to the deflection angle $\theta$?

Q24. What is the primary cause of wave drag on a supersonic aircraft wing?

8. Error Analysis & Common Traps (5 min)

As your ASO examiner, I have observed several recurring conceptual and calculation errors made by candidates in high-speed aerodynamics:

  1. Confusing Shock Angle ($\beta$) with Deflection Angle ($\theta$): Students often plug $\theta$ directly into normal shock relations instead of calculating or looking up the correct shock angle $\beta$ from the $\theta-\beta-M$ relation. Remember, the thermodynamic jump conditions depend strictly on the normal Mach number $M_{1n} = M_1 \sin\beta$.
  2. Neglecting the Two Shock Solutions (Weak vs. Strong): For any $\theta < \theta_{\max}$, remember that both weak and strong shocks are mathematically valid. Unless specified otherwise by boundary conditions (such as a back-pressure or trailing edge condition), supersonic flow over a sharp wedge naturally selects the weak shock solution.
  3. Stagnation Temperature Misconceptions: Students frequently mistake static temperature increase across a shock for stagnation temperature change. Stagnation temperature $T_0$ remains strictly constant ($T_{01} = T_{02}$) across any adiabatic shock wave.
  4. Fanno/Rayleigh Choking Misapplications: Assuming friction or heat addition can indefinitely change Mach numbers without hitting $M = 1$. Both Fanno and Rayleigh flows choke at $M = 1$, beyond which supersonic flow cannot be sustained without area changes or removing friction/heat.

  1. Define the physical difference between wave angle $\beta$ and deflection angle $\theta$.
  2. Write down the formula relating upstream normal Mach number $M_{1n}$ to $M_1$ and $\beta$.
  3. State the conservation law that applies to the tangential velocity component across an oblique shock ($V_{t1}$ vs $V_{t2}$).
  4. What happens to stagnation temperature $T_0$ across an oblique shock wave?
  5. What happens to stagnation pressure $p_0$ across an oblique shock wave and why?
  6. Explain what occurs when a wedge deflection angle $\theta$ exceeds $\theta_{\max}$.
  7. What is the limiting Mach number for Fanno flow in a constant-area duct?
  8. What is the limiting Mach number for Rayleigh flow with heat addition?
  9. Write the specific entropy change equation for an ideal gas across a shock wave.
  10. How does the weak oblique shock solution differ from the strong shock solution regarding downstream Mach number $M_2$?
  11. What is a Mach wave, and what is its wave angle relation to freestream Mach number?
  12. Why are multi-ramp supersonic inlets used in jet aircraft instead of a single normal shock?
  13. What is the maximum density ratio across a shock wave in air ($\gamma = 1.4$) as $M_{1n} \to \infty$?
  14. What optical diagnostic technique is used in wind tunnels to visualize oblique shocks?
  15. What causes wave drag on a supersonic wing profile?
