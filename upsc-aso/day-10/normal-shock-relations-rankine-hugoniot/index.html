Normal shock relations Rankine-Hugoniot

Day 10 of 129 Compressible Flow & Compressors Phase 1: Foundations 90-120 min Master Class

1. Understand (From Absolute Zero, 5–10 min)

Welcome to Day 10 of your advanced preparation for the UPSC Air Safety Officer (ASO) examination. Today, we delve into one of the most fundamental pillars of gas dynamics and high-speed aerodynamics: Normal Shock Waves and the Rankine-Hugoniot Relations.

Imagine a military fighter jet flying at Mach 2.0. The air molecules ahead of the aircraft receive no acoustic warning because the vehicle is traveling faster than the local speed of sound ($a = \sqrt{\gamma R T}$). Consequently, the oncoming fluid must abruptly adjust its state within an infinitesimally thin layer—typically on the order of micrometers ($\mu m$). This microscopic discontinuity is called a normal shock wave. Perpendicular to the flow direction, the fluid experiences an almost instantaneous, violent deceleration from supersonic to subsonic speeds, accompanied by radical spikes in static pressure ($p$), static temperature ($T$), fluid density ($\rho$), and entropy ($s$).

The Rankine-Hugoniot relations provide the exact mathematical bridge linking the upstream thermodynamic state ($1$) to the downstream thermodynamic state ($2$) across a normal shock wave without requiring explicit knowledge of the irreversible viscous dissipation processes occurring inside the shock structure. In UPSC ASO examinations, mastery of these relations is critical for evaluating supersonic aircraft inlet design, supersonic nozzle performance, sonic boom hazard assessments, and engine-intake shock swallowing.

2. Learn the Concept (Complete Theoretical Foundation & Derivations, 20–25 min)

To derive and understand the Rankine-Hugoniot relations from first principles, we must build our framework step-by-step from zero background, progressing through speed of sound definitions, isentropic stagnation properties, fundamental conservation laws, and specialized internal flow curves (Fanno and Rayleigh).

1. Speed of Sound & Mach Number Foundations

In compressible fluid mechanics, disturbances propagate through a medium via acoustic waves at the local speed of sound $a$. For an ideal gas obeying $p = \rho R T$, assuming an isentropic (reversible and adiabatic) infinitesimal process, the speed of sound is defined as:

$$ a = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial \rho}\right)_s} = \sqrt{\gamma R T} $$

Where $\gamma$ is the ratio of specific heats ($C_p / C_v$, for air $\gamma = 1.4$), $R$ is the specific gas constant ($R = 287 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$ for air), and $T$ is the absolute static temperature in Kelvin. The dimensionless Mach number $M$ is defined as the ratio of flow velocity $V$ to the local speed of sound:

$$ M = \frac{V}{a} = \frac{V}{\sqrt{\gamma R T}} $$

2. Isentropic Stagnation Relations

When a flowing gas is brought to a complete rest isentropically (reversibly and adiabatically), its kinetic energy is converted into enthalpy, defining the stagnation (total) temperature $T_0$ and stagnation (total) pressure $p_0$:

$$ \frac{T_0}{T} = 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2 $$ $$ \frac{p_0}{p} = \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2 \right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}} $$

3. Conservation Equations Across a Normal Shock Wave

Consider a control volume enclosing a stationary normal shock wave. Let state $1$ be immediately upstream (supersonic, $M_1 > 1$) and state $2$ be immediately downstream (subsonic, $M_2 < 1$). Applying the fundamental conservation laws of steady 1D flow:

  • Conservation of Mass (Continuity): $\rho_1 V_1 = \rho_2 V_2 = G$ (Mass flux constancy)
  • Conservation of Momentum: $p_1 + \rho_1 V_1^2 = p_2 + \rho_2 V_2^2$
  • Conservation of Energy: $h_1 + \frac{1}{2} V_1^2 = h_2 + \frac{1}{2} V_2^2 = h_0$ (Since shocks are adiabatic, stagnation enthalpy and stagnation temperature remain constant: $T_{01} = T_{02}$)

4. Derivation of Rankine-Hugoniot Equations

Combining continuity, momentum, and energy with the ideal gas law ($p = \rho R T$ and $h = C_p T = \frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\frac{p}{\rho}$), we eliminate velocities and express the pressure ratio $\frac{p_2}{p_1}$ as a function of the density ratio $\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}$ (or specific volume ratio $\frac{v_1}{v_2}$). This yields the classic Rankine-Hugoniot relation:

$$ \frac{p_2}{p_1} = \frac{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1} \left(\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}\right) - 1}{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1} - \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}} = \frac{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1} \left(\frac{v_1}{v_2}\right) - 1}{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1} - \frac{v_1}{v_2}} $$

In terms of upstream Mach number $M_1$, the normal shock property jumps are given by:

$$ M_2^2 = \frac{2 + (\gamma - 1) M_1^2}{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma - 1)} $$ $$ \frac{p_2}{p_1} = 1 + \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma + 1} (M_1^2 - 1) = \frac{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma - 1)}{\gamma + 1} $$ $$ \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{\frac{v_1}{v_2} = \frac{(\gamma + 1) M_1^2}{2 + (\gamma - 1) M_1^2} $$ $$ \frac{T_2}{T_1} = \left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right) \left(\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2}\right) = \left[ \frac{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma - 1)}{\gamma + 1} \right] \left[ \frac{2 + (\gamma - 1) M_1^2}{(\gamma + 1) M_1^2} \right] $$

5. Total Pressure Drop & Entropy Change

Although stagnation temperature is conserved across a normal shock ($T_{01} = T_{02}$), the shock is fundamentally irreversible due to intense viscous dissipation and heat conduction within the shock thickness. Consequently, stagnation pressure drops ($p_{02} < p_{01}$), and entropy increases ($\Delta s > 0$). The entropy change across the normal shock is:

$$ \Delta s = s_2 - s_1 = C_p \ln\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right) - R \ln\left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right) = C_p \ln\left(\frac{T_{02}}{T_{01}}\right) - R \ln\left(\frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}}\right) = -R \ln\left(\frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}}\right) $$

6. Fanno Flow & Rayleigh Flow Choking Lines

To analyze internal flows with friction or heat transfer:

  • Fanno Flow: Isothermal or adiabatic pipe flow with friction. Governed by conservation of mass and energy (constant stagnation enthalpy). The maximum entropy point on the Fanno curve corresponds exactly to $M = 1$ (sonic choking).
  • Rayleigh Flow: Frictionless constant-area duct flow with heat addition or removal. Governed by conservation of mass and momentum. The maximum entropy and maximum enthalpy points occur at $M = 1$ (thermal choking). Heat addition drives subsonic flow *toward* $M = 1$ and supersonic flow *away* from $M = 1$.

3. Memorize Important Facts (10 min)

  • Core Formula 1 & Mach Ratios: Downstream Mach number downstream of a normal shock is always subsonic: $M_2 = \sqrt{\frac{2 + (\gamma-1)M_1^2}{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma-1)}}$, where $M_1 > 1 \implies M_2 < 1$.
  • Standard Shock / Isentropic Jump Constant: Stagnation temperature remains invariant across a shock wave: $T_{01} = T_{02}$, whereas stagnation pressure always decreases: $p_{02} < p_{01}$ due to positive entropy generation ($\Delta s > 0$).
  • Key Thermal / Friction Choking Property: Maximum density ratio limit across an infinitely strong normal shock ($M_1 \to \infty$) for a calorically perfect gas with $\gamma = 1.4$ is $\lim_{M_1\to\infty} \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1} = \frac{2.4}{0.4} = 6$.

4. Aviation Application (5–10 min)

In practical supersonic aviation and aerospace propulsion, normal shock relations are indispensable:

  • Supersonic Intake Diffusers: Airbreathing engines (ramjets, scramjets, and turbojets on aircraft like the MiG-25 or Concorde) operate at supersonic flight speeds. Air entering the engine must be decelerated to subsonic speeds before reaching the compressor face. Intake cowls use a system of oblique shocks followed by a terminal normal shock to decelerate the flow efficiently while minimizing total pressure losses ($p_0$ drop).
  • Supersonic Wind Tunnels & Nozzles: Laval nozzles expand gas to supersonic speeds. If back pressure is improperly regulated, a normal shock can form inside the diverging section of the nozzle, causing flow separation, structural vibration, and severe thrust loss.
  • Sonic Booms: When an aircraft exceeds Mach 1, pressure disturbances coalesce into a characteristic N-wave system bounded by shock waves. The pressure jump across these shocks generates the loud acoustic "sonic boom" heard on the ground.

5. UPSC ASO Focus (10 min)

  • Frequently Asked Exam Facts: For air ($\gamma = 1.4$), specific numerical limits are frequently tested: Maximum density ratio $\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = 6$, limiting downstream Mach number $M_2 \to \sqrt{\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.4}{2.8}} \approx 0.378$ as $M_1 \to \infty$.
  • Conceptual Traps: UPSC often asks whether $T_{02} > T_{01}$ across a shock wave. Candidates frequently fall into the trap of assuming stagnation temperature changes because static temperature increases drastically. Remember: Shocks are adiabatic ($q = 0, w = 0$), so $T_{01} = T_{02}$ strictly holds!
  • Entropy Trap: A common misconception is that entropy increases because heat is added. In reality, entropy increases entirely due to irreversibility (viscous dissipation and shock wave thickness gradients), even though the process is adiabatic.

6. Practice Questions (30 Exhaustive UPSC ASO MCQs)

Q1. What is the fundamental physical state of the fluid flow immediately downstream of a normal shock wave in an ideal gas?
A) Always supersonic ($M_2 > 1$)
B) Always subsonic ($M_2 < 1$)
C) Always sonic ($M_2 = 1$)
D) Indeterminate, depends entirely on duct friction
Across a normal shock wave, the upstream Mach number $M_1 > 1$ (supersonic) always drops to a subsonic downstream Mach number $M_2 < 1$. Thus, option B is correct.
Q2. Which of the following thermodynamic properties remains strictly constant across a normal shock wave?
A) Static pressure ($p_1 = p_2$)
B) Stagnation pressure ($p_{01} = p_{02}$)
C) Stagnation temperature ($T_{01} = T_{02}$)
D) Entropy ($s_1 = s_2$)
Normal shock waves are adiabatic with no shaft work ($q=0, w=0$). From energy conservation, stagnation enthalpy $h_0$ and thus stagnation temperature $T_0$ remain constant ($T_{01} = T_{02}$). Stagnation pressure decreases and entropy increases due to irreversibility.
Q3. For air ($\gamma = 1.4$), what is the theoretical maximum density ratio $\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}$ across an infinitely strong normal shock wave ($M_1 \to \infty$)?
A) $1.667$
B) $4.0$
C) $6.0$
D) $\infty$
The density ratio limit as $M_1 \to \infty$ is given by $\frac{\gamma + 1}{\gamma - 1}$. For $\gamma = 1.4$, this evaluates to $\frac{1.4 + 1}{1.4 - 1} = \frac{2.4}{0.4} = 6.0$.
Q4. What happens to the stagnation pressure ($p_0$) across a normal shock wave?
A) $p_{02} > p_{01}$ because static pressure increases
B) $p_{02} = p_{01}$ because the process is adiabatic
C) $p_{02} < p_{01}$ due to viscous dissipation and entropy generation
D) Stagnation pressure first increases then decreases
Shock waves are irreversible thermodynamic discontinuities. The second law of thermodynamics dictates that entropy must increase ($\Delta s > 0$), which directly corresponds to a drop in stagnation pressure ($p_{02} < p_{01}$).
Q5. An aircraft flies at $M_1 = 2.0$ in air ($\gamma = 1.4$). Calculate the downstream Mach number $M_2$ across a normal shock wave.
A) $0.500$
B) $0.577$
C) $0.750$
D) $1.000$
Using $M_2^2 = \frac{2 + (\gamma-1)M_1^2}{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma-1)}$ with $\gamma = 1.4$ and $M_1 = 2$:
$M_2^2 = \frac{2 + (0.4)(4)}{2(1.4)(4) - 0.4} = \frac{2 + 1.6}{11.2 - 0.4} = \frac{3.6}{10.8} = \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.333$. Thus, $M_2 = \sqrt{0.333} \approx 0.577$.
Q6. Which of the following relations correctly expresses the Rankine-Hugoniot equation linking pressure and density ratios across a normal shock?
A) $\frac{p_2}{p_1} = \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}$
B) $\frac{p_2}{p_1} = \left(\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}\right)^\gamma$
C) $\frac{p_2}{p_1} = \frac{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1} (\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}) - 1}{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1} - \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}}$
D) $\frac{p_2}{p_1} = \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1} M_1^2$
Option C is the exact Rankine-Hugoniot curve equation governing normal shocks. Note that option B represents an isentropic process, which does not apply across a shock wave due to entropy generation.
Q7. What is the lower limiting value of downstream Mach number $M_2$ as the upstream Mach number $M_1$ approaches infinity in air ($\gamma = 1.4$)?
A) $0.000$
B) $0.378$
C) $0.500$
D) $1.000$
Taking the limit of $M_2 = \sqrt{\frac{2 + (\gamma-1)M_1^2}{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma-1)}}$ as $M_1 \to \infty$ gives $M_{2,\text{min}} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.4}{2.8}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{7}} \approx 0.378$.
Q8. In Rayleigh flow (frictionless constant-area duct with heat transfer), what does heat addition ($dq > 0$) do to the Mach number of a subsonic flow?
A) Decreases Mach number away from $1$
B) Increases Mach number toward sonic ($M = 1$)
C) Has no effect on Mach number
D) Instantly converts the flow to supersonic
In Rayleigh flow, heat addition increases enthalpy and entropy, driving both subsonic and supersonic flows toward the thermal choking point ($M = 1$). Subsonic flow accelerates toward $M = 1$, while supersonic flow decelerates toward $M = 1$.
Q9. In Fanno flow (constant-area duct flow with friction and zero heat transfer), what is the direction of entropy change along the flow path?
A) Entropy always decreases
C) Entropy remains constant (isentropic)
C) Entropy always increases ($ds > 0$)
D) Entropy first increases then decreases
By the Second Law of Thermodynamics, wall friction is an irreversible dissipative process. Therefore, entropy must always increase along a Fanno flow duct ($ds > 0$) until choking ($M=1$) is reached.
Q10. Calculate the static pressure ratio $\frac{p_2}{p_1}$ across a normal shock wave when the upstream Mach number is $M_1 = 3.0$ for air ($\gamma = 1.4$).
A) $5.50$
B) $10.33$
C) $16.50$
D) $29.00$
Using the normal shock pressure ratio formula: $\frac{p_2}{p_1} = \frac{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}$.
For $M_1 = 3$ and $\gamma = 1.4$:
$\frac{p_2}{p_1} = \frac{2(1.4)(9) - 0.4}{1.4 + 1} = \frac{25.2 - 0.4}{2.4} = \frac{24.8}{2.4} = 10.333$.
Q11. Which statement best describes the physical thickness of a normal shock wave in a gas?
A) Infinitely thin mathematical discontinuity with zero thickness
B) On the order of a few mean free paths of gas molecules (micrometers)
C) Several centimeters to meters depending on duct diameter
D) Equal to the chord length of the aircraft wing
While modeled as a mathematical discontinuity in inviscid gas dynamics, a real physical shock wave has a finite thickness governed by molecular viscosity and heat conduction, typically spanning a few mean free paths of gas molecules (approx. $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-6}$ meters).
Q12. What is the temperature ratio $\frac{T_2}{T_1}$ across a normal shock wave if $M_1 = 1.0$?
A) $0.0$
B) $1.0$
C) $1.84$
D) $\infty$
When $M_1 = 1.0$, the shock wave degenerates into a standard Mach wave (sound wave). There is no property jump across a Mach wave, so $T_2/T_1 = 1.0$, $p_2/p_1 = 1.0$, and $M_2 = 1.0$.
Q13. In a supersonic wind tunnel, a normal shock forms inside the test section. What immediate effect does this have on downstream static pressure and Mach number?
A) Pressure drops and Mach number increases
B) Pressure increases and Mach number drops to subsonic
C) Both pressure and Mach number increase
D) Both pressure and Mach number decrease
A normal shock wave causes a sudden compression (pressure increase, $\frac{p_2}{p_1} > 1$) and decelerates the flow from supersonic to subsonic ($M_2 < 1$).
Q14. Which parameter is used to non-dimensionalize the momentum equation across a normal shock wave, combining static pressure and dynamic pressure?
A) Stagnation pressure $p_0$
B) Impulsive pressure function $p(1 + \gamma M^2)$
C) Dynamic head $\frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$
D) Critical pressure $p^*$
The momentum conservation equation across a normal shock is $p_1 + \rho_1 V_1^2 = p_2 + \rho_2 V_2^2$. Since $\rho V^2 = \gamma p M^2$, this can be written as $p_1(1 + \gamma M_1^2) = p_2(1 + \gamma M_2^2)$, showing that the impulse function $p(1 + \gamma M^2)$ is conserved across the shock.
Q15. How does the entropy change $\Delta s$ across a normal shock wave relate to the upstream Mach number $M_1$?
A) $\Delta s = 0$ for all $M_1 > 1$
B) $\Delta s$ increases monotonically as $M_1$ increases above $1.0$
C) $\Delta s$ decreases as $M_1$ increases
D) $\Delta s$ is independent of $M_1$
As the upstream Mach number $M_1$ increases, the shock strength increases, leading to greater viscous dissipation and larger entropy generation ($\Delta s = -R \ln(p_{02}/p_{01}) > 0$).
Q16. In supersonic aircraft engine inlets, why is a series of oblique shocks often preferred over a single strong normal shock?
A) Oblique shocks eliminate drag entirely
B) Oblique shocks result in less total pressure loss compared to a single strong normal shock
C) Oblique shocks increase the flight Mach number
D) Oblique shocks heat the air less than normal shocks
A weaker shock wave generates less entropy and suffers a smaller total pressure loss ($p_0$ drop) than a single powerful normal shock. Using multiple oblique shocks followed by a weak terminal normal shock maximizes total pressure recovery at the engine face.
Q17. Which of the following conditions is physically impossible for a normal shock wave in a continuous fluid?
A) $M_1 = 2.5, M_2 = 0.55$
B) $M_1 = 0.8, M_2 = 1.2$
C) $p_{02} < p_{01}$
D) $\rho_2 > \rho_1$
Option B describes an expansion shock ($M_1 < 1$ transitioning to $M_2 > 1$), which violates the Second Law of Thermodynamics ($\Delta s < 0$). Expansion shocks are physically impossible; shock waves must always be compressive ($\Delta s > 0, M_1 > 1 \to M_2 < 1$).
Q18. What happens to the stagnation temperature $T_0$ in Fanno flow with wall friction?
A) Stagnation temperature decreases along the duct
B) Stagnation temperature remains constant (adiabatic)
C) Stagnation temperature increases due to friction
D) Stagnation temperature drops to zero at choking
By definition, Fanno flow is adiabatic duct flow with friction ($q = 0$). Since no heat is added or removed, the stagnation temperature remains constant ($T_0 = \text{constant}$).
Q19. In Rayleigh flow, what occurs at the maximum entropy point on the Rayleigh curve?
A) The flow becomes supersonic ($M > 1$)
B) Thermal choking occurs ($M = 1$)
C) Static pressure reaches absolute zero
D) Stagnation pressure reaches its maximum value
The peak entropy point on both Fanno and Rayleigh curves corresponds to the sonic Mach number $M = 1$. In Rayleigh flow, this represents thermal choking caused by heat addition.
Q20. If the upstream stagnation pressure is $p_{01} = 1000 \text{ kPa}$ and the normal shock upstream Mach number is $M_1 = 2.0$ in air, what is the qualitative behavior of $p_{02}$?
A) $p_{02} = 1000 \text{ kPa}$
B) $p_{02} > 1000 \text{ kPa}$
C) $p_{02} < 1000 \text{ kPa}$
D) $p_{02} = 0 \text{ kPa}$
Stagnation pressure always decreases across a normal shock wave due to entropy generation ($p_{02} < p_{01}$). For $M_1 = 2.0$, $p_{02}$ drops to approximately $72\%$ of $p_{01}$ (approx. $720 \text{ kPa}$).
Q21. Which gas dynamic parameter is continuous (unchanged) across a normal shock wave?
A) Static pressure $p$
B) Stagnation enthalpy $h_0$
C) Mach number $M$
D) Stagnation pressure $p_0$
Stagnation enthalpy $h_0$ (and consequently stagnation temperature $T_0$) is conserved across an adiabatic normal shock wave ($h_{01} = h_{02}$).
Q22. What is the ratio of static temperatures $\frac{T_2}{T_1}$ across a normal shock wave when $M_1 \to \infty$ for air ($\gamma = 1.4$)?
A) Approaches $1.0$
B) Approaches $\infty$
C) Approaches $0.0$
D) Approaches $\frac{\gamma+1}{2}$
As $M_1 \to \infty$, the static pressure ratio $\frac{p_2}{p_1}$ scales as $M_1^2$ and the density ratio $\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}$ approaches the limit $\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}$. Using the ideal gas law $\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right)\left(\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2}\right)$, the static temperature ratio grows proportionally to $M_1^2$, approaching infinity ($\infty$).
Q23. In a constant-area duct with friction (Fanno flow), if the inlet flow is supersonic ($M_1 > 1$), what happens to the Mach number as the flow proceeds down the duct?
A) Mach number decreases toward $1.0$
B) Mach number increases
C) Mach number remains constant
D) Mach number drops instantly to zero
Fanno flow friction always drives the Mach number toward the sonic point ($M = 1$). Therefore, supersonic Fanno flow ($M > 1$) accelerates in terms of velocity relative to sound speed such that $M$ decreases toward $1.0$ as entropy increases.
Q24. Which thermodynamic relation is valid across an infinitesimal acoustic wave (Mach wave) but NOT across a finite normal shock wave?
A) Conservation of mass
B) Isentropic relation ($p v^\gamma = \text{constant}$)
C) Conservation of energy
D) Ideal gas law
Infinitesimal acoustic waves are reversible and adiabatic, making them isentropic ($ds = 0, p v^\gamma = \text{const}$). Finite normal shock waves involve irreversible viscous dissipation, resulting in a significant entropy increase ($\Delta s > 0$).
Q25. What is the physical significance of the critical Mach number $M^*$?
A) Ratio of local velocity to the sonic speed at throat conditions ($V / a^*$)
B) Maximum possible Mach number in a gas
C) Mach number at which shock waves first form
D) Upstream Mach number limit
The characteristic Mach number $M^*$ (often denoted $M$ prime or star) is defined as the ratio of local flow velocity to the critical sonic speed $a^*$ ($M^* = V / a^* = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma+1}{2}M^2 / (1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2)}$).
Q26. In a converging-diverging supersonic nozzle, if the back pressure is reduced below the design pressure, what type of shock wave forms outside the nozzle exit?
A) Normal shock wave inside the throat
B) Oblique shock waves
C) Expansion fans (Prandtl-Meyer expansion waves)
D) Detonation wave
When a nozzle is underexpanded (exit pressure greater than ambient back pressure), the gas expands further outside the exit through Prandtl-Meyer expansion fans. Conversely, overexpansion leads to external oblique shock waves.
Q27. How does the stagnation temperature $T_0$ behave in Rayleigh flow with heat addition ($dq > 0$)?
A) Stagnation temperature remains constant
B) Stagnation temperature increases
C) Stagnation temperature decreases
D) Stagnation temperature drops to zero
Rayleigh flow involves heat transfer. By definition, adding heat ($dq > 0$) to a flowing gas increases its stagnation enthalpy and stagnation temperature ($T_{02} > T_{01}$).
Q28. Which of the following conditions must be satisfied for a normal shock wave to be physically stable and realizable?
A) $M_1 < 1$ and $M_2 > 1$
B) $M_1 > 1$ and $M_2 < 1$
C) $M_1 = M_2 = 1$
D) $M_1$ can be any subsonic value
A physically stable shock wave requires an upstream supersonic flow ($M_1 > 1$) and a downstream subsonic flow ($M_2 < 1$), ensuring positive entropy generation ($\Delta s > 0$).
Q29. What is the primary cause of total pressure loss across a normal shock wave?
A) External heat rejection
B) Irreversible viscous dissipation and molecular heat conduction within the shock structure
C) Shaft work extraction
D) Duct wall friction
The total pressure drop across a normal shock wave is entirely caused by internal irreversible viscous dissipation and heat conduction within the microscopic shock thickness, leading to an increase in entropy.
Q30. If the upstream Mach number $M_1 = 1.5$, what is the density ratio $\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}$ across a normal shock wave in air ($\gamma = 1.4$)?
A) $1.50$
B) $1.86$
C) $2.50$
D) $4.50$
Using the normal shock density ratio formula: $\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{(\gamma+1)M_1^2}{2 + (\gamma-1)M_1^2}$.
For $M_1 = 1.5$ and $\gamma = 1.4$:
$\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{(2.4)(2.25)}{2 + (0.4)(2.25)} = \frac{5.4}{2 + 0.9} = \frac{5.4}{2.9} \approx 1.862$.

A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A)

C) Statement 1 is false, but Statement 2 is true
D) Both Statement 1 and Statement 2 are false
Statement 1 is true because normal shocks are adiabatic ($q=0, w=0$), conserving stagnation enthalpy and temperature. Statement 2 is false because normal shocks are irreversible, resulting in a drop in stagnation pressure ($p_{02} < p_{01}$).
Q6. (Grand Mini Test) Calculate the static pressure ratio $\frac{p_2}{p_1}$ across a normal shock wave for $M_1 = 2.0$ in air ($\gamma = 1.4$).
A) $2.67$
B) $4.50$
C) $5.40$
D) $10.33$
Using $\frac{p_2}{p_1} = \frac{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}$ for $M_1 = 2.0$:
$\frac{p_2}{p_1} = \frac{2(1.4)(4) - 0.4}{2.4} = \frac{11.2 - 0.4}{2.4} = \frac{10.8}{2.4} = 4.50$.
Q7. (Grand Mini Test) What is the change in entropy $\Delta s$ across a normal shock wave if the stagnation pressure ratio is $\frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}} = 0.5$ for air ($R = 287 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$)?
A) $0.00 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$
B) $+98.9 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$
C) $-98.9 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$
D) $+198.8 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$
Using $\Delta s = -R \ln\left(\frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}}\right)$:
$\Delta s = -287 \ln(0.5) = -287 (-0.6931) \approx +198.9 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$. Wait, let's recalculate: $-287 \times (-0.69315) = +198.9$. Note that $\Delta s = -R \ln(p_{02}/p_{01}) = R \ln(p_{01}/p_{02}) = 287 \ln(2) = 287 \times 0.69315 = 198.93 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$.
Q8. (Grand Mini Test) In Rayleigh flow, heat addition to a subsonic stream ($M < 1$) causes the flow velocity to:
A) Decrease
B) Increase toward the sonic speed ($M = 1$)
C) Remain constant
D) Drop to zero
Heat addition in Rayleigh flow drives the Mach number toward $1$. For a subsonic stream, this requires an increase in velocity toward the local speed of sound.
Q9. (Grand Mini Test) Which of the following describes an expansion shock ($M_1 < 1$ transitioning to $M_2 > 1$ across a discontinuity)?
A) Commonly observed in supersonic nozzles
B) Physically impossible because it violates the Second Law of Thermodynamics ($\Delta s < 0$)
C) Occurs only in high-temperature plasmas
D) Fully reversible and isentropic
Expansion shocks are strictly prohibited by the Second Law of Thermodynamics because they would result in a negative entropy change ($\Delta s < 0$). Continuous expansions occur via Prandtl-Meyer expansion fans.
Q10. (Grand Mini Test) In the context of aerospace safety and supersonic aircraft design, what is the primary operational hazard associated with a normal shock wave forming inside a supersonic engine intake?
A) Excessive cooling of turbine blades
B) Severe total pressure loss, flow distortion, and potential compressor stall or unstart
C) Infinite increase in engine thrust
D) Elimination of all aerodynamic drag
Uncontrolled normal shocks inside supersonic inlets result in massive stagnation pressure losses and severe flow distortion, which can trigger compressor stalls, engine unstart, and catastrophic loss of thrust in flight.

8. Error Analysis & Common Traps (5 min)

When solving problems involving normal shock relations and Rankine-Hugoniot equations, UPSC candidates frequently commit several critical errors:

  1. Confusing Static and Stagnation Temperature: Candidates often assume that because static temperature increases drastically across a normal shock ($T_2 > T_1$), stagnation temperature must also increase. Remember that shock waves are adiabatic ($q=0, w=0$), meaning stagnation temperature is strictly conserved ($T_{01} = T_{02}$).
  2. Assuming Isentropic Flow Across Shocks: Applying isentropic relations like $\frac{p_2}{p_1} = \left(\frac{p_02}{p_01}\right)$ or $\frac{p_2}{p_1} = \left(\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}\right)^\gamma$ across a normal shock wave is a fatal error. Shocks are highly irreversible and non-isentropic ($\Delta s > 0$).
  3. Misapplying Fanno and Rayleigh Choking Limits: Confusing the direction of Mach number adjustment under friction (Fanno) vs. heat addition (Rayleigh). In Fanno flow, friction always drives $M$ toward $1$ ($ds > 0$). In Rayleigh flow, heat addition drives $M$ toward $1$, while heat rejection drives subsonic flow away from $1$.
  4. Algebraic Sign Errors in Mach Number Conversions: When computing $M_2$ from $M_1$, failing to square $M_1$ correctly in the numerator and denominator formulae leads to severe numerical errors.
