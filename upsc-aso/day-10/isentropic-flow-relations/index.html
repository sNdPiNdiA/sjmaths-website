Isentropic flow relations

Day 10 of 129 Compressible Flow & Compressors Phase 1: Foundations 90-120 min Master Class

1. Understand (From Absolute Zero, 5–10 min)

Welcome, future Air Safety Officers and Aeronautical Engineers! Today we master Isentropic Flow Relations—the mathematical backbone of compressible aerodynamics. Before diving into differential equations, let us build absolute baseline intuition.

What is Isentropic Flow? An isentropic process is one that is both adiabatic (no heat is added to or removed from the fluid) and reversible (no viscous friction, thermal conduction, or turbulence losses). In thermodynamics, such a process has constant entropy ($ds = 0$). In high-speed aerodynamics, air flowing smoothly through well-contoured nozzles, diffusers, and past streamlined aerodynamic bodies at high Reynolds numbers can be approximated as isentropic.

Physical Meaning & SI Units: As a gas accelerates or decelerates, its pressure ($p$, in Pascals or $\text{N/m}^2$), temperature ($T$, in Kelvin), and density ($\rho$, in $\text{kg/m}^3$) trade kinetic energy for internal energy. In an isentropic expansion (acceleration), static pressure and temperature drop sharply while flow velocity increases. The Mach number ($M = \frac{V}{a}$, dimensionless) dictates the compressibility regime: subsonic ($M < 1$), sonic ($M = 1$), and supersonic ($M > 1$).

UPSC Terminology: In UPSC ASO and aerospace exams, you must distinguish between static properties (local values experienced by a particle moving with the fluid) and stagnation (total) properties (values attained if the fluid is brought to a complete halt isentropically). Governing parameters include the ratio of specific heats ($\gamma$, typically $1.4$ for air) and the universal gas constant ($R$).

2. Learn the Concept (Complete Theoretical Foundation & Derivations, 20–25 min)

Let us build the complete theoretical foundation from zero background. We begin with the fundamental definition of the speed of sound and scale up to complete isentropic relations, shocks, and choking phenomena.

1. Speed of Sound ($a$)

The speed of a small pressure disturbance (sound wave) through a compressible medium is derived from the continuity and momentum equations. For an ideal gas undergoing an isentropic (adiabatic reversible) compression:

$$ a = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial \rho}\right)_s} = \sqrt{\gamma R T} $$

Where $\gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v}$ is the specific heat ratio ($1.4$ for calorically perfect air), $R$ is the specific gas constant ($287.05 \, \text{J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$), and $T$ is absolute static temperature.

2. Isentropic Stagnation Relations

Applying the steady-flow energy equation (First Law of Thermodynamics) between any point in the flow and a stagnation point where velocity $V = 0$:

$$ h + \frac{V^2}{2} = h_0 \implies C_p T + \frac{V^2}{2} = C_p T_0 $$

Using the ideal gas relations $C_p = \frac{\gamma R}{\gamma - 1}$ and $a^2 = \gamma R T$, we express the stagnation-to-static temperature, pressure, and density ratios exclusively as functions of Mach number $M$:

$$ \frac{T_0}{T} = 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2 $$ $$ \frac{p_0}{p} = \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2 \right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}} $$ $$ \frac{\rho_0}{\rho} = \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2 \right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma - 1}} $$

3. Area-Mach Number Relation (Area-Velocity Duct Flow)

From the conservation of mass ($\rho A V = \text{constant}$), taking logarithmic differentials and combining with momentum and isentropic relations yields the famous area-velocity-Mach relation:

$$ \frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1) \frac{dV}{V} = -\left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2\right) \frac{d\rho}{\rho} $$

Duct Design Implications:

  • Subsonic ($M < 1$): $dA$ and $dV$ have the same sign. To accelerate flow, area must decrease ($dA < 0$, convergent duct). To decelerate flow, area must increase ($dA > 0$, divergent duct).
  • Supersonic ($M > 1$): $dA$ and $dV$ have opposite signs. To accelerate supersonic flow, area must increase ($dA > 0$, divergent duct). To decelerate supersonic flow, area must decrease ($dA < 0$, convergent duct).
  • Sonic Throat ($M = 1$): $dA = 0$, representing the minimum cross-sectional area (throat) of a Convergent-Divergent (CD / de Laval) nozzle.

The relation between local area $A$ and throat area $A^*$ (where $M=1$) is given by:

$$ \frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{M} \left[ \frac{2}{\gamma + 1} \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2 \right) \right]^{\frac{\gamma + 1}{2(\gamma - 1)}} $$

4. Rankine-Hugoniot Normal Shock Relations

When a supersonic flow experiences an abrupt adiabatic compression wave (Normal Shock), entropy increases ($ds > 0$). Across a normal shock (upstream state 1, downstream state 2):

$$ M_2^2 = \frac{2 + (\gamma - 1) M_1^2}{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma - 1)} $$ $$ \frac{p_2}{p_1} = 1 + \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma + 1}(M_1^2 - 1) = \frac{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma - 1)}{\gamma + 1} $$ $$ \frac{T_2}{T_1} = \left( 1 + \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma + 1}(M_1^2 - 1) \right) \left( \frac{2 + (\gamma - 1)M_1^2}{(\gamma + 1)M_1^2} \right) $$ $$ p_{02} < p_{01} \quad (\text{Stagnation pressure loss due to entropy generation}), \quad T_{02} = T_{01} $$

5. Fanno Flow (Constant Area, Friction) and Rayleigh Flow (Constant Area, Heat Transfer)

  • Fanno Flow: Adiabatic duct flow with internal viscous friction. Entropy increases in the direction of flow. Both subsonic and supersonic flows asymptotically approach $M = 1$ (choked flow) at the end of a sufficient duct length ($L_{\max}$).
  • Rayleigh Flow: Frictionless, constant-area duct flow with external heat addition or rejection. Maximum entropy occurs at $M = 1/\sqrt{\gamma}$ (approx $M = 0.845$ for air). Heat addition to subsonic flow accelerates it toward $M = 1$ (thermal choking); heat addition to supersonic flow decelerates it toward $M = 1$.

3. Memorize Important Facts (10 min)

  • Core Formula 1 & Mach Ratios: $\frac{T_0}{T} = 1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2} M^2$ and $\frac{p_0}{p} = \left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2} M^2\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}$.
  • Standard Shock / Isentropic Jump Constant: For air ($\gamma = 1.4$), critical area ratio is $\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{M} \left[ \frac{5 + M^2}{6} \right]^3$. Normal shock upstream-downstream Mach relation: $M_2^2 = \frac{2 + 0.4 M_1^2}{2.8 M_1^2 - 0.4}$.
  • Key Thermal / Friction Choking Property: Stagnation temperature $T_0$ remains constant in Fanno flow ($T_{02} = T_{01}$) and normal shocks ($T_{02} = T_{01}$), but changes in Rayleigh flow due to external heat addition ($q = C_p(T_{02} - T_{01})$).

4. Aviation Application (5–10 min)

Isentropic flow relations and shock dynamics govern every aspect of high-speed aerospace engineering:

  • Supersonic Inlets (Diffusers): Fighter jets (like the MiG-25, Su-35, and Rafale) and supersonic transports (like the Concorde) utilize variable geometry or multiple oblique shock cones in their engine inlets. These external and internal ramps decelerate incoming supersonic air to subsonic speeds ($M \approx 0.4 - 0.5$) before entering the compressor face, minimizing total pressure losses and recovering maximum stagnation pressure ($p_0$).
  • Rocket & Jet Nozzles: De Laval convergent-divergent nozzles expand combustion gases from high pressure/temperature chambers to supersonic exhaust velocities. The expansion ratio ($A_exit / A_throat$) is precisely designed using isentropic area-Mach relations to match ambient atmospheric pressure at cruising altitude (optimally expanded nozzle).
  • Sonic Booms & Shock Waves: When an aircraft exceeds Mach 1, pressure disturbances coalesce into Mach waves and conical shock structures (bow shock at the nose, tail shock). The resulting pressure jump perceived on the ground is quantified by Rankine-Hugoniot pressure ratios.

5. UPSC ASO Focus (10 min)

  • Frequently Asked Exam Facts:
    • Specific heat ratio for air $\gamma = 1.4$.
    • Sonic condition: $M = 1$, where static temperature $T^* = \frac{2}{\gamma + 1} T_0 = 0.833 T_0$ for air.
    • Maximum entropy in Rayleigh flow occurs at $M = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}} = 0.845$ (for air).
    • Normal shock waves are extremely thin ($\approx 10^{-5}$ meters), occurring within a few mean free paths of gas molecules.
  • Conceptual Traps:
    • Do not confuse Stagnation Temperature ($T_0$) with Static Temperature ($T$). While $T_0$ remains constant across adiabatic normal shocks ($T_{01} = T_{02}$), Stagnation Pressure ($p_0$) always drops across a shock wave due to entropy generation ($p_{02} < p_{01}$).
    • Heat addition in subsonic Rayleigh flow increases Mach number toward $1$, whereas heat addition in supersonic Rayleigh flow decreases Mach number toward $1$.

6. Practice Questions (30 Exhaustive UPSC ASO MCQs)

Q1. What is the fundamental definition of the Mach number $M$ in compressible fluid flow?
A) Ratio of flow velocity to kinematic viscosity
B) Ratio of local flow velocity to local speed of sound ($V/a$)
C) Ratio of stagnation pressure to static pressure
D) Ratio of inertial forces to surface tension forces
Mach number is defined as $M = \frac{V}{a}$, representing the ratio of flow velocity to the speed of sound in the fluid. Correct Option is B.
Q2. For an ideal gas with $\gamma = 1.4$, if the local static temperature is $300\text{ K}$, what is the local speed of sound? ($R = 287 \, \text{J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$)
A) $310.2 \, \text{m/s}$
B) $347.2 \, \text{m/s}$
C) $400.1 \, \text{m/s}$
D) $280.5 \, \text{m/s}$
Using $a = \sqrt{\gamma R T} = \sqrt{1.4 \times 287 \times 300} = \sqrt{120540} \approx 347.19 \, \text{m/s}$. Correct Option is B.
Q3. In an isentropic flow of air ($\gamma = 1.4$), if the stagnation temperature $T_0 = 400\text{ K}$ and Mach number $M = 2.0$, what is the static temperature $T$?
A) $400\text{ K}$
B) $250\text{ K}$
C) $222.2\text{ K}$
D) $150.5\text{ K}$
Using $\frac{T_0}{T} = 1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2} M^2 \implies \frac{400}{T} = 1 + \frac{1.4-1}{2}(2)^2 = 1 + 0.2(4) = 1.8$. Thus $T = \frac{400}{1.8} = 222.22\text{ K}$. Correct Option is C.
Q4. Which of the following thermodynamic relations holds true for an isentropic process of an ideal gas?
A) $p v = \text{constant}$
B) $p v^\gamma = \text{constant}$
C) $T v = \text{constant}$
D) $p / T = \text{constant}$
An isentropic process for an ideal gas is polytropic with polytropic exponent equal to the specific heat ratio $\gamma$, yielding $p v^\gamma = \text{constant}$. Correct Option is B.
Q5. For a supersonic nozzle operating isentropically, how does the cross-sectional area vary with downstream distance?
A) Convergent ($dA < 0$)
B) Constant ($dA = 0$)
C) Divergent ($dA > 0$)
D) First divergent then convergent
From the area-velocity relation $\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1)\frac{dV}{V}$, for supersonic flow ($M > 1$), $M^2 - 1 > 0$. To accelerate flow ($dV > 0$), the area must increase ($dA > 0$), requiring a divergent duct. Correct Option is C.
Q6. What happens to the stagnation pressure $p_0$ across a normal shock wave?
A) $p_{02} = p_{01}$
B) $p_{02} > p_{01}$
C) $p_{02} < p_{01}$
D) Stagnation pressure drops to zero
Normal shocks are irreversible thermodynamic discontinuities. Due to entropy generation ($\Delta s > 0$), stagnation pressure decreases ($p_{02} < p_{01}$). Correct Option is C.
Q7. What is the downstream Mach number $M_2$ across a normal shock if the upstream Mach number $M_1 = 1.0$ for air ($\gamma = 1.4$)?

When $M_1 = 1.0$, the shock wave degenerates into a simple Mach wave (infinitesimal wave), meaning no shock occurs and $M_2 = M_1 = 1.0$. Correct Option is C.

B) $M = 1.0$

Fanno flow accounts for friction in constant-area adiabatic ducts. Both subsonic and supersonic flows drive toward sonic choking ($M = 1.0$) at the maximum duct length. Correct Option is B.
Q9. For air ($\gamma = 1.4$), at what Mach number does maximum entropy occur in Rayleigh flow (frictionless constant-area duct with heat addition)?

In Rayleigh flow, entropy reaches its maximum value precisely at $M = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}$. For air ($\gamma = 1.4$), this is $M = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1.4}} \approx 0.845$. Correct Option is C.
Q10. What happens to the static pressure across a normal shock wave?

A normal shock wave is a compressive shock wave. Therefore, static pressure, temperature, and density all increase across the shock ($\frac{p_2}{p_1} > 1$). Correct Option is C.
Q11. In an isentropic flow, if the stagnation pressure $p_0 = 1000\text{ kPa}$ and Mach number $M = 1.0$ for air ($\gamma = 1.4$), what is the static pressure $p$?

Q15. What is the downstream Mach number $M_2$ for an upstream Mach number $M_1 = 2.0$ across a normal shock in air ($\gamma = 1.4$)?

8. Error Analysis & Common Traps (5 min)

Common Student Errors & Calculation Traps in Isentropic Flow:

  1. Stagnation vs Static Temperature Confusion: Students often mistake static temperature ($T$) for stagnation temperature ($T_0$) across normal shocks. Remember: $T_{01} = T_{02}$ because shocks are adiabatic, but static temperature jumps sharply ($T_2 > T_1$).
  2. Stagnation Pressure Drop Misunderstanding: Assuming $p_{02} = p_{01}$ across a normal shock. While energy is conserved ($T_0$ constant), entropy generation guarantees that stagnation pressure always drops ($p_{02} < p_{01}$).
  3. Fanno vs Rayleigh Choking Limits: Confusing the choking condition. Fanno flow chokes at $M = 1.0$ due to friction, whereas Rayleigh flow chokes at $M = 1.0$ due to heat addition, with maximum entropy occurring at $M = 1/\sqrt{\gamma} \approx 0.845$.
  4. Using the Wrong Area Ratio Branch: The area-Mach relation $\frac{A}{A^*}$ yields two Mach numbers for any area ratio $A/A^* > 1$: one subsonic ($M < 1$) and one supersonic ($M > 1$). Always verify whether your nozzle section is convergent (subsonic) or divergent (supersonic).
