Specific Weight, Density & Specific Gravity

Day 12 of 129 Fluid Properties & Statics Phase 1: Foundations 65-75 min Master Module

Level 1: Beginner's Glossary & Fluid Property Definitions Basic

Before applying hydrostatic equations, here is the complete beginner's dictionary of all density, gravity, and weight properties:

1. Mass Density ($ρ$)

  • Definition: Mass of fluid contained within a unit volume: $ρ = mV$.
  • Symbol: $ρ$ (rho). SI Unit: $kg/m^3$ (or $g/cm^3$).
  • Key Property: Intrinsic property that remains constant regardless of local gravitational field $g$ (same on Earth, Moon, or in zero gravity).
  • Standard Values: Water at $4^\circC = 1000 kg/m^3$; Standard Air at $15^\circC = 1.225 kg/m^3$.

2. Specific Weight / Weight Density ($γ$)

  • Definition: Gravitational force (weight) exerted on a unit volume of fluid: $γ = WV = mgV = ρ g$.
  • Symbol: $γ$ (gamma) or $w$. SI Unit: $N/m^3$ (or $kN/m^3$).
  • Gravity Dependence: Varies directly with local gravitational acceleration $g(z)$ (in zero-g, $γ = 0 N/m^3$).
  • Standard Values on Earth: Water = $9810 N/m^3$; Air = $12.013 N/m^3$; Mercury = $133,416 N/m^3$.

3. Specific Volume ($v$)

  • Definition: Volume occupied per unit mass of substance (reciprocal of mass density).
  • Formula: $v = Vm = 1ρ$ [$m^3/kg$].
  • Application: Extensively used in thermodynamics and gas turbine combustion modeling.

4. Specific Gravity ($SG$ / Relative Density)

  • Definition: Pure dimensionless ratio of the fluid's density to the density of a standard reference fluid:
    • Liquids (ref = water at $4^\circC$): $SG = ρliquid1000 kg/m^3 = γliquid9810 N/m^3$
    • Gases (ref = standard air): $SG = ρgasρair = Mgas28.97$
  • Dimension: Pure scalar with NO units. ($SGJet-A ≈ 0.804, SGHg = 13.6$).

5. Hydrostatic Pressure Gradient ($dpdh$)

  • Definition: Rate of pressure increase per unit vertical depth in a static fluid column is equal to specific weight: $$dpdh = γ = ρ g \implies Δ p = γ Δ h = ρ g Δ h$$

6. Archimedes Buoyancy Upthrust ($F_B$)

  • Definition: Vertical upward force exerted by a surrounding fluid on a submerged or floating body equals the weight of the displaced fluid: $$F_B = γfluid Vdisplaced = ρfluid g Vdisplaced \quad [N]$$

Level 2: Variations with Gravity, Temperature & Pressure Intermediate

1. Gravitational Dependency ($γ = ρ g$)

Mass density $ρ$ is an invariant intrinsic property (same on Earth, Moon, or in microgravity). Specific weight $γ$ is an extrinsic weight property that varies with local gravitational field $g(z)$:

$$ γ(z) = ρ g(z) = ρ g_0 (R_ER_E + z)^2 $$

(On the Moon where $gmoon ≈ 1.62 m/s^2$, water density remains $ρ = 1000 kg/m^3$, but specific weight drops to $γ = 1620 N/m^3$).

2. Ideal Gas Specific Weight Equation

Combining the ideal gas equation $p = ρ R T$ with $γ = ρ g$:

$$ γ = p gR T = p M gR_u T $$

Level 3: Hydrostatic Gradient & Archimedes Buoyancy Advanced

1. Fundamental Hydrostatic Equation

The rate of increase of pressure with depth $h$ in a static fluid is equal to the specific weight of the fluid:

$$ dpdh = γ = ρ g \implies p_2 - p_1 = γ Δ h = ρ g Δ h $$

2. Archimedes Buoyancy Principle

The buoyant upthrust force ($F_B$) on a submerged or floating body equals the weight of the fluid displaced by the body:

$$ F_B = γfluid Vsubmerged = ρfluid g Vsubmerged $$

Level 5: Aviation & ASO Practical Applications

  • Aircraft Fuel Mass & Center of Gravity (CG) Loading: Jet fuel is metered by volume at fueling trucks (liters / US gallons) but must be converted to **fuel mass (kg / lbs)** using temperature-corrected specific gravity ($SG$). If SG is miscalculated during cold weather, fuel weight is underestimated, risking runway overrun or CG departure.
  • Barometric Altimeter Calibration: Barometric altimeters calculate aircraft pressure altitude ($z$) based on the hydrostatic integration $dp = -γair dz = -ρ g dz$.
  • Airship & Hot Air Balloon Aerostatic Lift: Net lift payload is calculated by Archimedes principle: $Lnet = (γambient - γgas) Venvelope$.

Level 6: UPSC ASO Focus & Common Traps

  • Trap 1 (Units Distinction): Density is $kg/m^3$. Specific Weight is $N/m^3$ ($γ = ρ g$). Never confuse the two!
  • Trap 2 (Gravity Invariance): Density $ρ$ does NOT change with local gravitational acceleration $g$. Specific weight $γ$ changes directly in proportion to $g$.
  • Trap 3 (Specific Gravity is Dimensionless): $SG = ρρwater$ has NO units. It represents a pure scalar multiplier relative to $1000 kg/m^3$ (or $9810 N/m^3$).

UPSC ASO Practice Questions (30 MCQs)

Q1. Specific weight ($γ$) of a fluid is defined as:
A) Weight of the fluid per unit volume ($γ = ρ g$)
B) Mass of fluid per unit volume
C) Volume occupied by unit mass
D) Ratio of density to that of water
Specific weight (weight density) is weight per unit volume: $γ = WV = m gV = ρ g$ (in $N/m^3$).
Q2. What are the SI units of Specific Weight?
A) $N/m^3$
B) $kg/m^3$
C) $N·s/m^2$
D) Dimensionless
Weight is in Newtons ($N$) and Volume is in cubic meters ($m^3$), giving $N/m^3$.
Q3. If a liquid has a Specific Gravity of $SG = 0.85$, its mass density and specific weight on Earth are respectively: ($g = 9.81 m/s^2$)
A) $850 kg/m^3$ and $8338.5 N/m^3$
B) $85 kg/m^3$ and $850 N/m^3$
C) $1000 kg/m^3$ and $9810 N/m^3$
D) $8500 kg/m^3$ and $83385 N/m^3$
$ρ = SG × 1000 = 0.85 × 1000 = 850 kg/m^3$. $γ = ρ g = 850 × 9.81 = 8338.5 N/m^3$.
Q4. On the surface of the Moon where $gmoon = 1.62 m/s^2$, what is the specific weight of pure water ($ρ = 1000 kg/m^3$)?
A) $1620 N/m^3$
B) $9810 N/m^3$
C) $1000 N/m^3$
D) Zero
$γmoon = ρ · gmoon = 1000 × 1.62 = 1620 N/m^3$.
Q5. Specific Volume ($v$) is defined as:
A) Reciprocal of mass density ($v = 1/ρ$, in $m^3/kg$)
B) Product of density and volume
C) Ratio of weight to density
D) Volume of the entire container
Specific volume is volume occupied per unit mass: $v = Vm = 1ρ$.
Q6. What is the specific weight of mercury ($SG = 13.6$) at standard Earth gravity ($g = 9.81 m/s^2$)?
A) $133,416 N/m^3$ ($133.4 kN/m^3$)
B) $13,600 N/m^3$
C) $9810 N/m^3$
D) $1,360 N/m^3$
$γHg = SG × γwater = 13.6 × 9810 = 133,416 N/m^3 = 133.42 kN/m^3$.
Q7. An aircraft fuel tank is filled with $10,000 liters$ of Jet-A fuel ($SG = 0.80$). What is the total mass and weight of this fuel? ($g = 9.81$)
A) $8000 kg$ and $78,480 N$
B) $10,000 kg$ and $98,100 N$
C) $800 kg$ and $7848 N$
D) $12,500 kg$ and $122,625 N$
$V = 10,000 L = 10 m^3$. $ρ = 0.80 × 1000 = 800 kg/m^3$. $m = ρ V = 800 × 10 = 8000 kg$. $W = m g = 8000 × 9.81 = 78,480 N$.
Q8. For an ideal gas with characteristic gas constant $R$, absolute temperature $T$, and pressure $p$, the specific weight is:
A) $γ = p gR T$
B) $γ = p R Tg$
C) $γ = R Tp g$
D) $γ = gp R T$
From ideal gas equation $p = ρ R T \implies ρ = pR T$. Since $γ = ρ g$, we have $γ = p gR T$.
Q9. A U-tube manometer filled with mercury ($γHg = 133.4 kN/m^3$) indicates a level difference of $Δ h = 250 mm$. What is the pressure difference ($Δ p$)?
A) $33.35 kPa$
B) $2.45 kPa$
C) $133.4 kPa$
D) $66.7 kPa$
$Δ p = γ Δ h = 133,400 N/m^3 × 0.250 m = 33,350 Pa = 33.35 kPa$.
Q10. What is the buoyant force acting on an underwater probe of volume $V = 0.5 m^3$ fully submerged in seawater ($γsea = 10,050 N/m^3$)?
A) $5025 N$
B) $10,050 N$
C) $2512.5 N$
D) $9810 N$
$F_B = γfluid V = 10,050 × 0.5 = 5025 N$.
Q11. Why does aviation fuel specific gravity ($SG$) decrease as ambient temperature increases?
A) Thermal expansion increases fuel volume ($V \uparrow$) while mass remains constant, reducing density ($ρ = m/V \downarrow$)
B) Chemical composition changes into gas
C) Gravity decreases with temperature
D) Viscosity increases
Liquid hydrocarbons have a high thermal volumetric expansion coefficient ($β ≈ 0.001 K-1$); warm fuel is less dense, so a full tank holds fewer kilograms of energy.
Q12. What is the specific weight of standard sea-level air ($ρ = 1.225 kg/m^3, g = 9.80665 m/s^2$)?

B) $1.225 N/m^3$
C) $9810 N/m^3$
D) $101.3 N/m^3$
$γ = ρ g = 1.225 × 9.80665 ≈ 12.013 N/m^3$.
Q13. In the International Standard Atmosphere (ISA), the hydrostatic relation governing pressure variation with geopotential altitude ($h$) is:

B) $dpdh = +ρ g$

As altitude $h$ increases upward, static pressure decreases at a rate equal to the local specific weight: $dpdh = -ρ g = -γ$.
Q14. An object weighing $500 N$ in air is lowered into a water tank ($γ = 9810 N/m^3$). If its volume is $0.03 m^3$, what will be its apparent weight underwater?

Q27. In a compressible fluid, the bulk modulus of elasticity is $K = ρ dpdρ$. In terms of specific weight ($γ = ρ g$), this can be written as:

Top Candidate Error Traps

  1. Specific Weight vs Density: Specific weight is $γ = ρ g$ ($N/m^3$). Density is mass per volume $ρ$ ($kg/m^3$).
  2. Gravity Invariance: Density is invariant under gravity; specific weight scales directly with local $g$.
  3. Specific Gravity Dimensions: $SG$ is purely dimensionless (scalar ratio).
  4. Dimensions of $γ$: Specific weight has dimensions $[M L-2 T-2]$, unlike pressure $[M L-1 T-2]$ or density $[M L-3]$.
