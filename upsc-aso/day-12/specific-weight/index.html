Specific Weight, Density & Specific Gravity

Day 12 of 129 Fluid Properties & Statics Phase 1: Foundations 65-75 min Master Module

Level 1: Beginner's Glossary & Fluid Property Definitions Basic

Before applying hydrostatic equations, here is the complete beginner's dictionary of all density, gravity, and weight properties:

1. Mass Density ($\rho$)

  • Definition: Mass of fluid contained within a unit volume: $\rho = \frac{m}{V}$.
  • Symbol: $\rho$ (rho). SI Unit: $\text{kg/m}^3$ (or $\text{g/cm}^3$).
  • Key Property: Intrinsic property that remains constant regardless of local gravitational field $g$ (same on Earth, Moon, or in zero gravity).
  • Standard Values: Water at $4^\circ\text{C} = 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3$; Standard Air at $15^\circ\text{C} = 1.225 \text{ kg/m}^3$.

2. Specific Weight / Weight Density ($\gamma$)

  • Definition: Gravitational force (weight) exerted on a unit volume of fluid: $\gamma = \frac{W}{V} = \frac{mg}{V} = \rho g$.
  • Symbol: $\gamma$ (gamma) or $w$. SI Unit: $\text{N/m}^3$ (or $\text{kN/m}^3$).
  • Gravity Dependence: Varies directly with local gravitational acceleration $g(z)$ (in zero-g, $\gamma = 0 \text{ N/m}^3$).
  • Standard Values on Earth: Water = $9810 \text{ N/m}^3$; Air = $12.013 \text{ N/m}^3$; Mercury = $133,416 \text{ N/m}^3$.

3. Specific Volume ($v$)

  • Definition: Volume occupied per unit mass of substance (reciprocal of mass density).
  • Formula: $v = \frac{V}{m} = \frac{1}{\rho}$ [$\text{m}^3/\text{kg}$].
  • Application: Extensively used in thermodynamics and gas turbine combustion modeling.

4. Specific Gravity ($SG$ / Relative Density)

  • Definition: Pure dimensionless ratio of the fluid's density to the density of a standard reference fluid:
    • Liquids (ref = water at $4^\circ\text{C}$): $SG = \frac{\rho_{liquid}}{1000 \text{ kg/m}^3} = \frac{\gamma_{liquid}}{9810 \text{ N/m}^3}$
    • Gases (ref = standard air): $SG = \frac{\rho_{gas}}{\rho_{air}} = \frac{M_{gas}}{28.97}$
  • Dimension: Pure scalar with NO units. ($SG_{Jet-A} \approx 0.804, SG_{Hg} = 13.6$).

5. Hydrostatic Pressure Gradient ($\frac{dp}{dh}$)

  • Definition: Rate of pressure increase per unit vertical depth in a static fluid column is equal to specific weight: $$\frac{dp}{dh} = \gamma = \rho g \implies \Delta p = \gamma \Delta h = \rho g \Delta h$$

6. Archimedes Buoyancy Upthrust ($F_B$)

  • Definition: Vertical upward force exerted by a surrounding fluid on a submerged or floating body equals the weight of the displaced fluid: $$F_B = \gamma_{fluid} V_{displaced} = \rho_{fluid} g V_{displaced} \quad [\text{N}]$$

Level 2: Variations with Gravity, Temperature & Pressure Intermediate

1. Gravitational Dependency ($\gamma = \rho g$)

Mass density $\rho$ is an invariant intrinsic property (same on Earth, Moon, or in microgravity). Specific weight $\gamma$ is an extrinsic weight property that varies with local gravitational field $g(z)$:

$$ \gamma(z) = \rho g(z) = \rho g_0 \left(\frac{R_E}{R_E + z}\right)^2 $$

(On the Moon where $g_{moon} \approx 1.62 \text{ m/s}^2$, water density remains $\rho = 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3$, but specific weight drops to $\gamma = 1620 \text{ N/m}^3$).

2. Ideal Gas Specific Weight Equation

Combining the ideal gas equation $p = \rho R T$ with $\gamma = \rho g$:

$$ \gamma = \frac{p g}{R T} = \frac{p M g}{R_u T} $$

Level 3: Hydrostatic Gradient & Archimedes Buoyancy Advanced

1. Fundamental Hydrostatic Equation

The rate of increase of pressure with depth $h$ in a static fluid is equal to the specific weight of the fluid:

$$ \frac{dp}{dh} = \gamma = \rho g \implies p_2 - p_1 = \gamma \Delta h = \rho g \Delta h $$

2. Archimedes Buoyancy Principle

The buoyant upthrust force ($F_B$) on a submerged or floating body equals the weight of the fluid displaced by the body:

$$ F_B = \gamma_{fluid} V_{submerged} = \rho_{fluid} g V_{submerged} $$

Level 5: Aviation & ASO Practical Applications

  • Aircraft Fuel Mass & Center of Gravity (CG) Loading: Jet fuel is metered by volume at fueling trucks (liters / US gallons) but must be converted to **fuel mass (kg / lbs)** using temperature-corrected specific gravity ($SG$). If SG is miscalculated during cold weather, fuel weight is underestimated, risking runway overrun or CG departure.
  • Barometric Altimeter Calibration: Barometric altimeters calculate aircraft pressure altitude ($z$) based on the hydrostatic integration $dp = -\gamma_{air} dz = -\rho g dz$.
  • Airship & Hot Air Balloon Aerostatic Lift: Net lift payload is calculated by Archimedes principle: $L_{net} = (\gamma_{ambient} - \gamma_{gas}) V_{envelope}$.

Level 6: UPSC ASO Focus & Common Traps

  • Trap 1 (Units Distinction): Density is $\text{kg/m}^3$. Specific Weight is $\text{N/m}^3$ ($\gamma = \rho g$). Never confuse the two!
  • Trap 2 (Gravity Invariance): Density $\rho$ does NOT change with local gravitational acceleration $g$. Specific weight $\gamma$ changes directly in proportion to $g$.
  • Trap 3 (Specific Gravity is Dimensionless): $SG = \frac{\rho}{\rho_{water}}$ has NO units. It represents a pure scalar multiplier relative to $1000 \text{ kg/m}^3$ (or $9810 \text{ N/m}^3$).

UPSC ASO Practice Questions (30 MCQs)

Q1. Specific weight ($\gamma$) of a fluid is defined as:
A) Weight of the fluid per unit volume ($\gamma = \rho g$)
B) Mass of fluid per unit volume
C) Volume occupied by unit mass
D) Ratio of density to that of water
Specific weight (weight density) is weight per unit volume: $\gamma = \frac{W}{V} = \frac{m g}{V} = \rho g$ (in $\text{N/m}^3$).
Q2. What are the SI units of Specific Weight?
A) $\text{N/m}^3$
B) $\text{kg/m}^3$
C) $\text{N}\cdot\text{s/m}^2$
D) Dimensionless
Weight is in Newtons ($\text{N}$) and Volume is in cubic meters ($\text{m}^3$), giving $\text{N/m}^3$.
Q3. If a liquid has a Specific Gravity of $SG = 0.85$, its mass density and specific weight on Earth are respectively: ($g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$)
A) $850 \text{ kg/m}^3$ and $8338.5 \text{ N/m}^3$
B) $85 \text{ kg/m}^3$ and $850 \text{ N/m}^3$
C) $1000 \text{ kg/m}^3$ and $9810 \text{ N/m}^3$
D) $8500 \text{ kg/m}^3$ and $83385 \text{ N/m}^3$
$\rho = SG \times 1000 = 0.85 \times 1000 = 850 \text{ kg/m}^3$. $\gamma = \rho g = 850 \times 9.81 = 8338.5 \text{ N/m}^3$.
Q4. On the surface of the Moon where $g_{moon} = 1.62 \text{ m/s}^2$, what is the specific weight of pure water ($\rho = 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3$)?
A) $1620 \text{ N/m}^3$
B) $9810 \text{ N/m}^3$
C) $1000 \text{ N/m}^3$
D) Zero
$\gamma_{moon} = \rho \cdot g_{moon} = 1000 \times 1.62 = 1620 \text{ N/m}^3$.
Q5. Specific Volume ($v$) is defined as:
A) Reciprocal of mass density ($v = 1/\rho$, in $\text{m}^3/\text{kg}$)
B) Product of density and volume
C) Ratio of weight to density
D) Volume of the entire container
Specific volume is volume occupied per unit mass: $v = \frac{V}{m} = \frac{1}{\rho}$.
Q6. What is the specific weight of mercury ($SG = 13.6$) at standard Earth gravity ($g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$)?
A) $133,416 \text{ N/m}^3$ ($133.4 \text{ kN/m}^3$)
B) $13,600 \text{ N/m}^3$
C) $9810 \text{ N/m}^3$
D) $1,360 \text{ N/m}^3$
$\gamma_{Hg} = SG \times \gamma_{water} = 13.6 \times 9810 = 133,416 \text{ N/m}^3 = 133.42 \text{ kN/m}^3$.
Q7. An aircraft fuel tank is filled with $10,000 \text{ liters}$ of Jet-A fuel ($SG = 0.80$). What is the total mass and weight of this fuel? ($g = 9.81$)
A) $8000 \text{ kg}$ and $78,480 \text{ N}$
B) $10,000 \text{ kg}$ and $98,100 \text{ N}$
C) $800 \text{ kg}$ and $7848 \text{ N}$
D) $12,500 \text{ kg}$ and $122,625 \text{ N}$
$V = 10,000 \text{ L} = 10 \text{ m}^3$. $\rho = 0.80 \times 1000 = 800 \text{ kg/m}^3$. $m = \rho V = 800 \times 10 = 8000 \text{ kg}$. $W = m g = 8000 \times 9.81 = 78,480 \text{ N}$.
Q8. For an ideal gas with characteristic gas constant $R$, absolute temperature $T$, and pressure $p$, the specific weight is:
A) $\gamma = \frac{p g}{R T}$
B) $\gamma = \frac{p R T}{g}$
C) $\gamma = \frac{R T}{p g}$
D) $\gamma = \frac{g}{p R T}$
From ideal gas equation $p = \rho R T \implies \rho = \frac{p}{R T}$. Since $\gamma = \rho g$, we have $\gamma = \frac{p g}{R T}$.
Q9. A U-tube manometer filled with mercury ($\gamma_{Hg} = 133.4 \text{ kN/m}^3$) indicates a level difference of $\Delta h = 250 \text{ mm}$. What is the pressure difference ($\Delta p$)?
A) $33.35 \text{ kPa}$
B) $2.45 \text{ kPa}$
C) $133.4 \text{ kPa}$
D) $66.7 \text{ kPa}$
$\Delta p = \gamma \Delta h = 133,400 \text{ N/m}^3 \times 0.250 \text{ m} = 33,350 \text{ Pa} = 33.35 \text{ kPa}$.
Q10. What is the buoyant force acting on an underwater probe of volume $V = 0.5 \text{ m}^3$ fully submerged in seawater ($\gamma_{sea} = 10,050 \text{ N/m}^3$)?
A) $5025 \text{ N}$
B) $10,050 \text{ N}$
C) $2512.5 \text{ N}$
D) $9810 \text{ N}$
$F_B = \gamma_{fluid} V = 10,050 \times 0.5 = 5025 \text{ N}$.
Q11. Why does aviation fuel specific gravity ($SG$) decrease as ambient temperature increases?
A) Thermal expansion increases fuel volume ($V \uparrow$) while mass remains constant, reducing density ($\rho = m/V \downarrow$)
B) Chemical composition changes into gas
C) Gravity decreases with temperature
D) Viscosity increases
Liquid hydrocarbons have a high thermal volumetric expansion coefficient ($\beta \approx 0.001 \text{ K}^{-1}$); warm fuel is less dense, so a full tank holds fewer kilograms of energy.
Q12. What is the specific weight of standard sea-level air ($\rho = 1.225 \text{ kg/m}^3, g = 9.80665 \text{ m/s}^2$)?
A) $12.013 \text{ N/m}^3$
B) $1.225 \text{ N/m}^3$
C) $9810 \text{ N/m}^3$
D) $101.3 \text{ N/m}^3$
$\gamma = \rho g = 1.225 \times 9.80665 \approx 12.013 \text{ N/m}^3$.
Q13. In the International Standard Atmosphere (ISA), the hydrostatic relation governing pressure variation with geopotential altitude ($h$) is:
A) $\frac{dp}{dh} = -\rho g = -\gamma$
B) $\frac{dp}{dh} = +\rho g$
C) $\frac{dp}{dh} = -\frac{\rho}{g}$
D) $\frac{dp}{dh} = 0$
As altitude $h$ increases upward, static pressure decreases at a rate equal to the local specific weight: $\frac{dp}{dh} = -\rho g = -\gamma$.
Q14. An object weighing $500 \text{ N}$ in air is lowered into a water tank ($\gamma = 9810 \text{ N/m}^3$). If its volume is $0.03 \text{ m}^3$, what will be its apparent weight underwater?
A) $205.7 \text{ N}$
B) $500.0 \text{ N}$
C) $294.3 \text{ N}$
D) Zero
$F_B = \gamma V = 9810 \times 0.03 = 294.3 \text{ N}$. Apparent weight $W_{app} = W - F_B = 500 - 294.3 = 205.7 \text{ N}$.
Q15. Skydrol aviation hydraulic fluid has $SG = 1.005$. In a $3000 \text{ psi}$ ($20.68 \text{ MPa}$) aircraft hydraulic line, what is the static pressure head in meters of Skydrol? ($g = 9.81$)
A) $2098 \text{ m}$
B) $210.8 \text{ m}$
C) $20.68 \text{ m}$
D) $1000 \text{ m}$
$\gamma = 1.005 \times 9810 = 9859.05 \text{ N/m}^3$. $H = \frac{p}{\gamma} = \frac{20.68 \times 10^6}{9859.05} \approx 2097.57 \text{ m}$.
Q16. Liquid Hydrogen ($LH_2$) is a powerful rocket fuel with extremely low density ($\rho \approx 70.8 \text{ kg/m}^3$). What is its Specific Gravity relative to water?
A) $SG = 0.0708$
B) $SG = 0.708$
C) $SG = 7.08$
D) $SG = 1.0$
$SG = \frac{70.8}{1000} = 0.0708$. Because of this ultra-low density, rocket $LH_2$ tanks must be exceptionally voluminous.
Q17. In hydrometer measurements of aircraft battery electrolyte (Sulfuric acid), a fully charged lead-acid battery reads:
A) $SG \approx 1.265 - 1.280$
B) $SG = 0.800$
C) $SG = 1.000$ (water)
D) $SG = 13.6$
During charge, sulfuric acid concentration rises, increasing electrolyte density to $SG \approx 1.275$. Discharged batteries drop toward $SG \approx 1.150$.
Q18. What is the specific volume of dry air at $p = 100 \text{ kPa}$ and $T = 300 \text{ K}$? ($R = 287 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$)
A) $0.861 \text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$
B) $1.161 \text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$
C) $0.287 \text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$
D) $3.48 \text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$
$\rho = \frac{p}{R T} = \frac{100,000}{287 \times 300} = \frac{100,000}{86,100} \approx 1.1614 \text{ kg/m}^3$. $v = \frac{1}{\rho} = \frac{86,100}{100,000} = 0.861 \text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$.
Q19. At what temperature does liquid water achieve its maximum density and maximum specific weight?
A) $4^\circ\text{C}$ ($3.98^\circ\text{C}$)
B) $0^\circ\text{C}$ (Freezing point)
C) $100^\circ\text{C}$ (Boiling point)
D) $-4^\circ\text{C}$
Due to anomalous hydrogen-bond structure, water reaches its peak density $\rho = 999.97 \approx 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3$ at $4^\circ\text{C}$.
Q20. A hydraulic jack cylinder with piston area $A = 0.02 \text{ m}^2$ supports an aircraft axle load of $40 \text{ kN}$. If the fluid has $\gamma = 8500 \text{ N/m}^3$, what is the pressure generated?
A) $2.0 \text{ MPa}$ ($20 \text{ bar}$)
B) $0.8 \text{ MPa}$
C) $40.0 \text{ MPa}$
D) $170 \text{ kPa}$
$p = \frac{F}{A} = \frac{40,000 \text{ N}}{0.02 \text{ m}^2} = 2,000,000 \text{ Pa} = 2.0 \text{ MPa}$. (Specific weight determines reservoir line static head, while load creates hydrostatic working pressure).
Q21. What is the net aerostatic lift of a helium balloon of volume $V = 1000 \text{ m}^3$ in standard sea level air ($\gamma_{air} = 12.01 \text{ N/m}^3, \gamma_{He} = 1.66 \text{ N/m}^3$)?
A) $10,350 \text{ N}$ ($10.35 \text{ kN} \approx 1055 \text{ kg-force}$)
B) $12,010 \text{ N}$
C) $1660 \text{ N}$
D) $13,670 \text{ N}$
$F_{lift} = (\gamma_{air} - \gamma_{He}) V = (12.01 - 1.66) \times 1000 = 10.35 \times 1000 = 10,350 \text{ N}$.
Q22. An open cylindrical fuel tank of height $H = 4 \text{ m}$ is filled with AVGAS 100LL ($\gamma = 7014 \text{ N/m}^3$). What is the gage pressure at the tank bottom?
A) $28.056 \text{ kPa}$
B) $39.24 \text{ kPa}$
C) $7.014 \text{ kPa}$
D) $101.3 \text{ kPa}$
$p = \gamma H = 7014 \times 4 = 28,056 \text{ Pa} = 28.056 \text{ kPa}$.
Q23. In dimensional analysis, the dimensions of Specific Weight ($\gamma$) in terms of Mass ($M$), Length ($L$), and Time ($T$) are:
A) $[M L^{-2} T^{-2}]$
B) $[M L^{-3}]$
C) $[M L^{-1} T^{-2}]$
D) $[M L T^{-2}]$
$\gamma = \rho g = [M L^{-3}] \times [L T^{-2}] = [M L^{-2} T^{-2}]$. (Note: Pressure is $[M L^{-1} T^{-2}]$ and Density is $[M L^{-3}]$).
Q24. If two miscible fluids of equal volumes $V$ with specific weights $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ are thoroughly mixed, the specific weight of the resulting mixture is:
A) $\gamma_{mix} = \frac{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}{2}$
B) $\gamma_{mix} = \sqrt{\gamma_1 \gamma_2}$
C) $\gamma_{mix} = \frac{2 \gamma_1 \gamma_2}{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}$
D) $\gamma_{mix} = \gamma_1 + \gamma_2$
$\gamma_{mix} = \frac{W_1 + W_2}{V_1 + V_2} = \frac{\gamma_1 V + \gamma_2 V}{2 V} = \frac{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}{2}$.
Q25. If two fluids of equal weights $W$ with specific weights $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ are mixed, the specific weight of the mixture is:
A) $\gamma_{mix} = \frac{2 \gamma_1 \gamma_2}{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}$ (Harmonic mean)
B) $\gamma_{mix} = \frac{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}{2}$
C) $\gamma_{mix} = \sqrt{\gamma_1 \gamma_2}$
D) $\gamma_{mix} = \frac{\gamma_1 \gamma_2}{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}$
$\gamma_{mix} = \frac{2 W}{V_1 + V_2} = \frac{2 W}{\frac{W}{\gamma_1} + \frac{W}{\gamma_2}} = \frac{2}{\frac{1}{\gamma_1} + \frac{1}{\gamma_2}} = \frac{2 \gamma_1 \gamma_2}{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}$.
Q26. A differential manometer connects two points in a horizontal water pipe ($\gamma = 9810 \text{ N/m}^3$) with carbon tetrachloride manometric fluid ($SG = 1.60$). If manometer deflection is $y = 0.20 \text{ m}$, what is the pressure drop?
A) $1.177 \text{ kPa}$
B) $3.139 \text{ kPa}$
C) $19.62 \text{ kPa}$
D) $0.392 \text{ kPa}$
$\Delta h = y \left(\frac{SG_m}{SG_f} - 1\right) = 0.20 \left(\frac{1.60}{1.00} - 1\right) = 0.20 \times 0.60 = 0.12 \text{ m of water}$. $\Delta p = \gamma_{water} \Delta h = 9810 \times 0.12 = 1177.2 \text{ Pa} = 1.177 \text{ kPa}$.
Q27. In a compressible fluid, the bulk modulus of elasticity is $K = \rho \frac{dp}{d\rho}$. In terms of specific weight ($\gamma = \rho g$), this can be written as:
A) $K = \gamma \frac{dp}{d\gamma}$
B) $K = \frac{1}{\gamma} \frac{dp}{d\gamma}$
C) $K = g \frac{dp}{d\gamma}$
D) $K = \gamma^2 \frac{dp}{d\gamma}$
Since $d\gamma = g d\rho$, we have $\frac{\rho}{d\rho} = \frac{\rho g}{g d\rho} = \frac{\gamma}{d\gamma}$, therefore $K = \gamma \frac{dp}{d\gamma}$.
Q28. Why does an aircraft flight computer monitor fuel specific gravity continuously via capacitive fuel quantity probes?
A) Dielectric constant of fuel changes with density, enabling accurate real-time calculation of total fuel mass (burnable kilograms) from measured volume
B) To check fuel color
C) To prevent the tank from rusting
D) To measure aircraft speed
Fuel capacitance probes use the Clausius-Mossotti relation linking fuel dielectric permittivity directly to mass density $\rho$, providing true onboard fuel mass telemetry.
Q29. What is the ratio of the specific weight of mercury ($\gamma_{Hg}$) to the specific weight of water ($\gamma_{w}$)?
A) Exactly equal to the Specific Gravity of mercury ($13.6$)
B) $1.0$
C) $9.81$
D) $1360$
By definition, Specific Gravity $SG = \frac{\rho_{Hg}}{\rho_w} = \frac{\rho_{Hg} g}{\rho_w g} = \frac{\gamma_{Hg}}{\gamma_w} = 13.6$.
Q30. A sample of hydraulic oil occupies $V = 2.5 \text{ m}^3$ and has a total weight of $W = 21.25 \text{ kN}$. What are its specific weight, density, and specific gravity? ($g = 9.81$)
A) $\gamma = 8500 \text{ N/m}^3, \rho = 866.5 \text{ kg/m}^3, SG = 0.8665$
B) $\gamma = 850 \text{ N/m}^3, \rho = 86.65 \text{ kg/m}^3, SG = 0.0866$
C) $\gamma = 21250 \text{ N/m}^3, \rho = 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3, SG = 1.0$
D) $\gamma = 9810 \text{ N/m}^3, \rho = 850 \text{ kg/m}^3, SG = 0.85$
$\gamma = \frac{21,250 \text{ N}}{2.5 \text{ m}^3} = 8500 \text{ N/m}^3$. $\rho = \frac{\gamma}{g} = \frac{8500}{9.81} \approx 866.46 \text{ kg/m}^3$. $SG = \frac{866.46}{1000} \approx 0.8665$.

Top Candidate Error Traps

  1. Specific Weight vs Density: Specific weight is $\gamma = \rho g$ ($\text{N/m}^3$). Density is mass per volume $\rho$ ($\text{kg/m}^3$).
  2. Gravity Invariance: Density is invariant under gravity; specific weight scales directly with local $g$.
  3. Specific Gravity Dimensions: $SG$ is purely dimensionless (scalar ratio).
  4. Dimensions of $\gamma$: Specific weight has dimensions $[M L^{-2} T^{-2}]$, unlike pressure $[M L^{-1} T^{-2}]$ or density $[M L^{-3}]$.
