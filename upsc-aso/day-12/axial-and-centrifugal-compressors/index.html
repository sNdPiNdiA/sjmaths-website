Axial and Centrifugal Compressors

Day 12 of 129 Aero-Gas Dynamics & Compressors Phase 1: Foundations 80-90 min Master Module

Level 1: Beginner's Glossary & Compressor Mechanics Definitions Basic

Before comparing aerodynamic stage calculations, here is the complete beginner's dictionary of all compressor classifications, thermodynamic parameters, and instability modes:

1. Dynamic Gas Compressor

  • Definition: A continuous-flow turbomachine that adds kinetic energy to a gas via rotating blades and converts it into static pressure via diffusion.
  • Stage Pressure Ratio ($r_p$): Ratio of outlet total pressure to inlet total pressure: $r_p = \frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}}$.

2. Centrifugal Compressor

  • Definition: Air enters axially at the impeller eye, turns $90^\circ$, and is slung radially outward at high velocity into stationary diffuser passages.
  • Key Characteristics: High pressure rise per stage ($4:1$ to $8:1$), robust against foreign object damage (FOD), compact axial length. Used in helicopter turboshafts (PT6) and APUs.

3. Axial Flow Compressor

  • Definition: Air flows continuously parallel to the rotor axis across alternating rotating blade rows (Rotor) and fixed stationary blade rows (Stator).
  • Key Characteristics: Low pressure rise per stage ($1.15:1$ to $1.35:1$), but high multi-stage overall pressure ratio ($30:1$ to $45:1$) with massive mass flow rate and small frontal area. Used in commercial jet airliners.

4. Slip Factor ($\sigma$) & Work Factor ($\psi$)

  • Slip Factor ($\sigma = \frac{V_{w2}}{V_{w2\infty}}$): Accounts for the "relative eddy" circulating inside the blade passage that prevents the air from leaving at the geometric blade angle ($\sigma \approx 0.85 - 0.92$).
  • Work Input Factor ($\psi$): Accounts for parasitic aerodynamic windage and boundary layer casing friction ($\psi \approx 1.04 - 1.05$).

5. Degree of Reaction ($R$)

  • Definition: Ratio of static enthalpy/pressure rise in the moving rotor to total static pressure rise across the entire stage (Rotor + Stator): $$R = \frac{\Delta h_{rotor}}{\Delta h_{stage}} = \frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_3 - T_1}$$
  • 50% Reaction ($R = 0.5$): Symmetric velocity triangles ($\alpha_1 = \beta_2, \alpha_2 = \beta_1$) providing equal diffusion in rotor and stator.

6. Rotating Stall vs Deep Surge

  • Rotating Stall: Local boundary layer separation forming rotating "stall cells" that travel around the blade annulus at $30-50\%$ shaft speed.
  • Compressor Surge: Complete, violent macroscopic flow breakdown where high downstream combustor pressure pushes air backward through the entire compressor in explosive cycles!

Level 2: Core Engineering Formulations Intermediate

1. Centrifugal Compressor Calculations

  • Euler Work Done per unit mass ($W$): For radial blades at exit ($V_{w2} = \sigma u_2$) with zero inlet whirl ($V_{w1} = 0$):
$$ W = \psi \sigma u_2^2 \quad (\text{where } \sigma = \text{Slip Factor} \approx 0.85-0.92, \; \psi = \text{Power Input Factor} \approx 1.04) $$ $$ \text{Stagnation Pressure Ratio: } \quad r_p = \frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}} = \left(1 + \frac{\eta_c \psi \sigma u_2^2}{C_p T_{01}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}} $$

2. Axial Flow Compressor Degree of Reaction ($R$)

Fraction of total stage static enthalpy (or pressure) rise that occurs in the rotating rotor:

$$ R = \frac{\Delta h_{rotor}}{\Delta h_{stage}} = \frac{h_2 - h_1}{h_{03} - h_{01}} = \frac{V_a}{2 u}(\tan\beta_1 + \tan\beta_2) = 1 - \frac{V_a}{2 u}(\tan\alpha_1 + \tan\alpha_2) $$
  • $R = 0.50$ (50% Reaction / Symmetrical Blading): Rotor and Stator have identical blade profiles and mirror velocity triangles ($\alpha_1 = \beta_2, \alpha_2 = \beta_1$). Yields optimum pressure rise and minimum profile loss.
  • $R = 0$ (Impulse Stage): All pressure rise occurs in the stator; rotor only imparts kinetic energy without static pressure change.

Level 3: Compressor Instabilities (Stall vs Surge) Advanced

Rotating Stall

  • Nature: Localized aerodynamic boundary layer separation on individual blade suction surfaces.
  • Behavior: Stall "cells" rotate around the annulus at $30-50\%$ of rotor speed in the direction opposite to relative flow (same direction as shaft in absolute frame).
  • Effect: Blade fatigue vibration and reduced delivery pressure.

Deep Compressor Surge

  • Nature: Complete system instability with massive axial backflow oscillations across the entire compressor annulus.
  • Behavior: Low-frequency violent chugging ($2-20 \text{ Hz}$), loud banging, flame ejection from engine inlet and tailpipe, flameout.
  • Trigger: Operating past the peak of the $PR-\dot{m}$ characteristic curve.

Surge Margin ($SM$) & Mitigation

$$ SM = \left(\frac{PR_{surge} / \dot{m}_{corr,surge}}{PR_{op} / \dot{m}_{corr,op}} - 1\right) \times 100\% \quad (\text{typically designed } \ge 15 - 25\%) $$

Aviation Surge Control Systems: Variable Stator Vanes (VSVs), Variable Inlet Guide Vanes (VIGVs), interstage compressor bleed valves (open during spool acceleration/deceleration), and multi-spool architecture (N1/N2/N3 shafts).

Level 5: Aviation & ASO Practical Applications

  • Dual-Spool High-Bypass Turbofans (e.g. CFM LEAP, GE90): Feature a Low-Pressure Compressor (LPC / Booster) driven by the LP turbine and a multi-stage High-Pressure Axial Compressor (HPC) driven by the HP turbine, achieving overall pressure ratios $>40:1$.
  • Helicopter Turboshaft Engines (e.g. Safran Arriel, Pratt & Whitney PT6): Use a combination axial-centrifugal layout (several axial stages followed by a final centrifugal stage) for compact size and rugged FOD tolerance.
  • Auxiliary Power Units (APUs): Utilize single-stage or two-stage centrifugal compressors to supply high-pressure pneumatic air for main engine air-turbine starting and environmental control (ECS) packs.

Level 6: UPSC ASO Focus & Common Traps

  • Trap 1 (50% Reaction Symmetry): $R = 0.5$ does NOT mean velocities are equal; it means the velocity triangles for the rotor and stator are symmetrical mirror images ($\alpha_1 = \beta_2, \alpha_2 = \beta_1$).
  • Trap 2 (Surge vs Stall): Stall is a local rotational blade-cell aerodynamic stall. Surge is a global system-wide violent forward-backward flow oscillation. Surge always involves stall, but stall does not always escalate into full surge.
  • Trap 3 (Compressor vs Turbine Work Sign): In compressors, work is done ON the fluid ($W = \Delta h_0 > 0$). In turbines, work is extracted from fluid ($W = -\Delta h_0$).

UPSC ASO Practice Questions (30 MCQs)

Q1. Why are axial-flow compressors preferred over centrifugal compressors for large commercial transport aircraft jet engines?
A) Axial compressors have a significantly smaller frontal cross-sectional area per unit mass flow, dramatically reducing nacelle aerodynamic drag
B) Axial compressors are much cheaper to manufacture
C) Axial compressors have a higher pressure ratio per single stage
D) Axial compressors never suffer from stall or surge
Straight-through axial flow enables massive air throughput with minimal frontal area, crucial for minimizing supersonic/transonic nacelle drag in aircraft.
Q2. In a 50% Degree of Reaction ($R = 0.5$) axial compressor stage, which condition is satisfied?
A) Velocity triangles of the rotor and stator are symmetrical ($\alpha_1 = \beta_2$ and $\alpha_2 = \beta_1$)
B) Rotor outlet relative velocity is zero
C) Stator pressure rise is zero
D) Absolute inlet velocity is supersonic
A 50% reaction stage shares the static enthalpy rise equally between rotor and stator, creating perfectly symmetrical velocity triangles and equal blade profiles.
Q3. What is the slip factor ($\sigma$) in a centrifugal compressor with radial blades?
A) $\sigma = \frac{V_{w2}}{u_2}$ (ratio of actual whirl velocity to tip speed)
B) $\sigma = \frac{u_2}{V_{w2}}$
C) $\sigma = \frac{V_{f2}}{u_2}$
D) $\sigma = \frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}}$
Due to relative eddy inertia between finite blade passages, the fluid slips, reducing actual whirl velocity $V_{w2}$ below peripheral blade speed $u_2$; slip factor is $\sigma = V_{w2}/u_2 \approx 0.85-0.92$.
Q4. Compressor Surge is fundamentally characterized by:
A) Complete breakdown of continuous flow with violent, low-frequency cyclic backflow oscillations through the entire engine
B) Silent flow without vibration
C) Supersonic expansion in the combustor
D) Smooth acceleration of the rotor
Surge is a self-excited global acoustic system instability where high combustor pressure discharges backward through the stalled compressor in loud explosive pulses.
Q5. In gas turbine engines, how do Variable Stator Vanes (VSVs) prevent compressor stall during low-RPM engine acceleration?
A) By rotating to decrease flow incidence angle on front-stage rotor blades, preventing excessive angle of attack
B) By stopping air from entering the engine
C) By spraying fuel into the compressor
D) By heating the compressor casing
At off-design low RPM, reduced axial velocity $V_a$ causes high positive incidence on front stages. Actuating VSVs closes the passage angle to preserve optimal aerodynamic alignment.
Q6. What is the typical single-stage pressure ratio achieved in a modern centrifugal compressor?
A) $1.15 : 1$ to $1.30 : 1$
B) $4.0 : 1$ to $8.0 : 1$
C) $50 : 1$ to $100 : 1$
D) $1.01 : 1$
Centrifugal stages generate $4:1$ to $8:1$ pressure ratio per stage primarily through the powerful centrifugal body force field ($\frac{u_2^2 - u_1^2}{2}$).
Q7. What is the typical single-stage pressure ratio achieved in an axial compressor?
A) $1.15 : 1$ to $1.35 : 1$
B) $5.0 : 1$ to $10.0 : 1$
C) $20 : 1$ to $30 : 1$
D) $0.80 : 1$
Axial compressors rely purely on aerodynamic diffusion against an adverse pressure gradient, limiting stage pressure ratios to $1.15-1.35$ to avoid boundary layer separation.
Q8. In a centrifugal compressor, the Power Input Factor ($\psi$) accounts for:
A) Additional windage, disk friction, and casing parasite drag losses ($\psi \approx 1.035 - 1.05$)
B) Electrical motor resistance
C) Atmospheric humidity
D) Fuel chemical heating
Disk friction and parasitic churning require extra mechanical shaft work, so actual work input is $W = \psi \sigma u_2^2$, where $\psi > 1$.
Q9. Rotating stall cells in an axial compressor rotate in the:
A) Same direction as rotor rotation in absolute frame, at roughly 30% to 50% rotor speed
B) Opposite direction at supersonic speed
C) Pure axial direction toward the exhaust
D) Speed equal to $5 \times$ shaft speed
Blockage from a stalled blade diverts flow into adjacent blades, propagating the stall patch in the direction of rotor rotation at $0.3 - 0.5 \times N_{rotor}$.
Q10. What is the phenomenon called when an axial compressor reaches maximum possible mass flow rate at a given speed, where Mach number reaches 1.0 in blade throats?
A) Choking (Stone Wall condition)
B) Deep surge
C) Cavitation
D) Deadheading
Choking represents sonic saturation in passage throat sections ($M = 1$), causing the characteristic curve to drop vertically (the "stone wall").
Q11. The static enthalpy rise in the rotor of an axial compressor is given by:
A) $\Delta h_{rotor} = \frac{V_{r1}^2 - V_{r2}^2}{2}$
B) $\Delta h_{rotor} = \frac{V_1^2 - V_2^2}{2}$
C) $\Delta h_{rotor} = u_1 V_{w1}$
D) $\Delta h_{rotor} = C_p T_0$
Across the rotor, relative kinetic energy diffuses: $h_2 - h_1 = \frac{V_{r1}^2 - V_{r2}^2}{2} + \frac{u_2^2 - u_1^2}{2} = \frac{V_{r1}^2 - V_{r2}^2}{2}$ (for axial constant radius $u_1 = u_2$).
Q12. What is the function of the stationary Stator vanes in an axial compressor stage?
A) Decelerate the swirling absolute flow from the rotor ($V_2 \to V_3$), converting dynamic pressure into static pressure, and redirect flow into next rotor
B) Inject lubricating oil
C) Rotate faster than the rotor
D) Ignite fuel droplets
The stator serves dual roles: diffuses absolute kinetic energy ($\Delta h_{stator} = \frac{V_2^2 - V_3^2}{2}$) and straightens flow angle for the subsequent rotor stage.
Q13. In a centrifugal compressor impeller with tip diameter $D_2 = 0.5 \text{ m}$ running at $18,000 \text{ rpm}$, what is the tip speed ($u_2$)?
A) $235.6 \text{ m/s}$
B) $471.2 \text{ m/s}$
C) $942.5 \text{ m/s}$
D) $150.0 \text{ m/s}$
$u_2 = \frac{\pi D_2 N}{60} = \frac{\pi \times 0.5 \times 18000}{60} = 150 \pi \approx 471.24 \text{ m/s}$.
Q14. For the compressor in Q13, if $\sigma = 0.90, \psi = 1.04, \eta_c = 0.85, T_{01} = 288 \text{ K}, C_p = 1005 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$, and $\gamma = 1.4$, what is the stagnation pressure ratio ($r_p$)?
A) $2.15 : 1$
B) $4.85 : 1$
C) $12.5 : 1$
D) $1.35 : 1$
$W = 1.04 \times 0.90 \times (471.24)^2 = 0.936 \times 222067 \approx 207855 \text{ J/kg}$. $\frac{T_{02s} - T_{01}}{T_{01}} = \frac{0.85 \times 207855}{1005 \times 288} = \frac{176676}{289440} \approx 0.6104$. Thus $r_p = (1 + 0.6104)^{3.5} = (1.6104)^{3.5} \approx 4.85$.
Q15. Why are compressor blades in axial flow compressors twisted from root to tip?
A) Because blade peripheral speed increases linearly with radius ($u = \omega r$), requiring varying blade inlet angles to match the changing relative velocity vector
B) Purely for aesthetic aerodynamic styling
C) To reduce engine manufacturing cost
D) To allow blades to flex like feathers
Since $u = \omega r$ is small at the root and large at the tip while axial velocity $V_a$ is nearly constant, relative flow angle $\beta_1 = \tan^{-1}(u/V_a)$ increases from hub to tip, demanding a continuous 3D aerodynamic twist (Free Vortex design).
Q16. What is the function of inter-stage bleed valves in multi-stage axial gas turbine compressors?
A) Dump excess air during low-speed start and throttle transients, increasing front-stage flow velocity and preventing front-stage stall / rear-stage choke
B) Spray coolant water into the burner
C) Measure fuel density
D) Increase cabin noise
At low RPM, high density build-up in rear stages chokes exit flow, causing front stages to stall. Bleeding middle-stage air increases front stage throughput, restoring stability.
Q17. In centrifugal compressors, why are purely radial blades commonly used at the impeller tip rather than backward-curved blades?
A) Radial blades experience purely tensile centrifugal stresses with zero bending moments, withstanding extreme tip speeds ($500+ \text{ m/s}$)
B) Radial blades have higher efficiency than backward blades
C) Backward blades cannot pump gases
D) Radial blades weigh less
At $20,000-50,000 \text{ rpm}$, centrifugal acceleration exceeds $50,000 \text{ g}$. Radial vanes eliminate destructive aerodynamic/centrifugal bending moments.
Q18. The de Haller number criterion for avoiding stall in axial compressor blade cascades requires that:
A) $\frac{V_{r2}}{V_{r1}} \ge 0.72$ (or $0.65$)
B) $\frac{V_{r2}}{V_{r1}} \ge 2.0$
C) $\frac{V_{r2}}{V_{r1}} \le 0.10$
D) $\frac{V_{r2}}{V_{r1}} = 0$
De Haller's rule of thumb states that relative velocity diffusion ratio $\frac{V_{r2}}{V_{r1}}$ must not fall below $0.72$ (or $0.65$ modern limit) to prevent severe boundary layer separation.
Q19. What is Lieblein's Diffusion Factor ($D$) used for in axial compressor aerodynamic design?
A) To quantify blade suction surface velocity diffusion and ensure $D \le 0.45 - 0.55$ to avoid cascade stall
B) To compute bearing oil life
C) To measure noise suppression
D) To measure turbine inlet temperature
Lieblein's Diffusion Factor $D = \left(1 - \frac{V_{r2}}{V_{r1}}\right) + \frac{\Delta V_w}{2 \sigma V_{r1}}$ sets aerodynamic blade loading limits.
Q20. In an axial flow compressor stage, if the axial velocity $V_a$ is constant across the stage, the Work Done factor ($\Omega \approx 0.85 - 0.98$) accounts for:
A) Boundary layer growth on the hub and casing annulus walls causing peaky non-uniform axial velocity profiles in downstream stages
B) Leakage of fuel into air
C) Weight of the rotor blades
D) Atmospheric pressure change with altitude
Thickening annulus boundary layers compress the core flow, reducing effective work capacity; work done factor $\Omega$ decreases from $0.98$ (stage 1) to $0.85$ (stage 10+).
Q21. The vaned diffuser of a centrifugal compressor has the disadvantage compared to a vaneless diffuser of:
A) Narrower stable operating range and susceptibility to stall at off-design mass flow rates
B) Lower peak efficiency
C) Much larger outer casing diameter
D) Zero pressure recovery
Vaned diffusers provide high peak recovery at design point but suffer from incidence stall at off-design flows, narrowing the stable surge margin.
Q22. What happens to the annulus passage height ($h = r_{tip} - r_{hub}$) in a multi-stage axial compressor from stage 1 to the final stage?
A) It progressively decreases because air density increases dramatically under compression ($\rho \uparrow \implies A \downarrow$ for constant $\dot{m} = \rho A V_a$)
B) It increases to give more room
C) It remains exactly constant
D) It oscillates up and down
Continuity $\dot{m} = \rho A V_a = \text{const}$. As density $\rho$ increases by 30-40 times, passage cross-sectional area $A$ must taper down proportionally.
Q23. In aircraft gas turbine terminology, what is a Multi-Spool Compressor configuration (e.g. N1 and N2)?
A) Splitting the compressor into mechanically independent LP and HP spools rotating at different optimal speeds on concentric shafts
B) Using two propellers on the wing
C) Connecting two battery systems
D) Having spare blades in the cargo hold
Multi-spooling decouples low-speed front stages from high-speed rear stages, vastly improving surge margin and transient acceleration flexibility.
Q24. An axial compressor stage has Degree of Reaction $R = 0.60$. If total stage static temperature rise is $20 \text{ K}$, what is the temperature rise in the rotor?
A) $12 \text{ K}$
B) $8 \text{ K}$
C) $20 \text{ K}$
D) $10 \text{ K}$
$\Delta T_{rotor} = R \times \Delta T_{stage} = 0.60 \times 20 = 12 \text{ K}$. (The remaining $8 \text{ K}$ occurs in the stator).
Q25. In supersonic inlet fan blades of modern high-bypass turbofans (e.g. GE90 / CFM LEAP), the blade tip relative Mach number is typically:
A) Transonic to Supersonic ($M_{rel,tip} \approx 1.3 - 1.6$)
B) Low Subsonic ($M = 0.2$)
C) Hypersonic ($M = 6.0$)
D) Strictly zero
Large wide-chord fan tips reach $M_{rel} \approx 1.4-1.6$ at takeoff, generating leading-edge oblique shock waves handled by sculpted 3D swept wide-chord blades.
Q26. What happens if a bird strike severely deforms two rotor blades in stage 1 of an axial compressor?
A) Severe aerodynamic disturbance triggers rotating stall cells, high vibration, and immediate engine surge
B) Compressor efficiency increases
C) Pressure ratio doubles
D) Fuel burn drops to zero
Foreign Object Damage (FOD) disturbs cascade aerodynamics, causing localized flow detachment that cascades into rotating stall and compressor surge.
Q27. The compressor map plots which two primary parameters?
A) Stagnation Pressure Ratio ($p_{02}/p_{01}$) versus Corrected Mass Flow ($\frac{\dot{m}\sqrt{T_0}}{p_0}$) for constant Corrected Speed lines
B) Weight versus passenger count
C) Altitude versus fuel flow
D) Noise versus thrust
A compressor performance map shows $PR$ vs $\dot{m}_{corr}$ with speed lines, efficiency contours, and the surge line boundary.
Q28. In centrifugal compressor terminology, what is the Inducer?
A) The axial-to-radial curved inlet section of the impeller eye that receives oncoming axial flow and turns it smoothly into the radial channels
B) The electric starter motor
C) The turbine exhaust cone
D) The fuel pump regulator
The inducer is the aerodynamic curved helical inlet of the impeller eye designed for high shock-free mass ingestion.
Q29. What is the isentropic compressor efficiency ($\eta_c$)?
A) $\eta_c = \frac{\text{Isentropic Ideal Work}}{\text{Actual Work Input}} = \frac{T_{02s} - T_{01}}{T_{02} - T_{01}}$
B) $\eta_c = \frac{T_{02} - T_{01}}{T_{02s} - T_{01}}$
C) $\eta_c = \frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}}$
D) $\eta_c = \frac{u_2}{V_1}$
Compressor efficiency is ideal minimum work divided by actual work input: $\eta_c = \frac{h_{02s} - h_{01}}{h_{02} - h_{01}} = \frac{T_{02s} - T_{01}}{T_{02} - T_{01}}$.
Q30. In a gas turbine engine, an uncorrected compressor surge will quickly cause:
A) Rapid turbine overtemperature (EGT exceedance), severe mechanical vibrations, and possible combustor flameout
B) Engine cooling
C) Higher thrust
D) Smooth flight
When air mass flow stalls while fuel continues injecting, the mixture spikes rich, driving Turbine Gas Temperature (EGT) above metallurgical limits in seconds.

Top Candidate Error Traps

  1. Stall vs Surge Confusion: Rotating stall is localized blade-level flow detachment rotating at partial wheel speed. Surge is an engine-wide violent cyclic flow reversal.
  2. 50% Reaction Misconception: $R = 0.5$ does not mean 50% efficiency; it means static enthalpy rise is split 50/50 between rotor and stator ($\alpha_1 = \beta_2, \alpha_2 = \beta_1$).
  3. Slip Factor vs Work Factor: Slip Factor ($\sigma \approx 0.9$) occurs in centrifugal impellers due to fluid inertia. Work Done Factor ($\Omega \approx 0.85-0.98$) occurs in axial compressors due to annulus wall boundary layer growth.
