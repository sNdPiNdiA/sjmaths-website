Turbine Specific Speed & Affinity Laws

Day 12 of 129 Turbomachinery Scaling Phase 1: Foundations 70-80 min Master Module

Level 1: Beginner's Glossary & Unit Quantities Definitions Basic

Before deriving scaling equations, here is the complete beginner's dictionary of all turbine performance definitions, unit parameters, and scaling symbols:

1. Unit Speed ($N_u$)

  • Definition: The rotational speed at which a given turbine would operate if the net working head were reduced to exactly 1 meter ($H = 1 \text{ m}$).
  • Formula: $N_u = \frac{N}{\sqrt{H}}$ [$\text{rpm}$].
  • Use: Predicts turbine RPM when head fluctuates between rainy and dry seasons ($N_2 = N_1 \sqrt{H_2 / H_1}$).

2. Unit Discharge ($Q_u$)

  • Definition: The volumetric discharge rate passing through a turbine under a net working head of 1 meter ($H = 1 \text{ m}$).
  • Formula: $Q_u = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{H}}$ [$\text{m}^3/\text{s}$].
  • Use: Predicts flow consumption at different water reservoir levels ($Q_2 = Q_1 \sqrt{H_2 / H_1}$).

3. Unit Power ($P_u$)

  • Definition: The shaft mechanical power produced by a turbine under a net working head of 1 meter ($H = 1 \text{ m}$).
  • Formula: $P_u = \frac{P}{H^{3/2}}$ [$\text{kW}$].
  • Use: Calculates power scaling under varying dam heads ($P_2 = P_1 (H_2/H_1)^{3/2}$).

4. Turbine Specific Speed ($N_s$)

  • Definition: The rotational speed of a geometrically similar model turbine that produces $1 \text{ kW}$ shaft power under a net head of $1 \text{ meter}$.
  • Formula: $N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$ (where $P$ is in $\text{kW}$, $H$ in $\text{m}$, $N$ in $\text{rpm}$).
  • Shape Selection:
    • Pelton (Single Jet): $N_s = 10 - 35$
    • Francis Turbine: $N_s = 60 - 300$
    • Kaplan Turbine: $N_s = 300 - 1000$

5. Affinity Scaling Laws

  • Definition: Dimensionless similarity rules relating discharge, head, and power across geometrically similar machines of different diameters ($D$) and speeds ($N$):
    • Discharge: $Q \propto N D^3$
    • Head: $H \propto N^2 D^2$
    • Power: $P \propto N^3 D^5$

6. Moody Step-up Formula & Scale Effect

  • Scale Effect: A large prototype turbine is always more efficient than its small lab model ($\eta_{prototype} > \eta_{model}$) because relative surface boundary layer friction is smaller.
  • Formula: $\frac{1 - \eta_p}{1 - \eta_m} = \left(\frac{D_m}{D_p}\right)^{0.2} = \left(\frac{D_m}{D_p}\right)^{1/5}$.

Level 2: Derivation of Specific Speed & Affinity Laws Intermediate

1. Turbine Specific Speed ($N_s$) Derivation

Specific speed $N_s$ is the speed of a geometrically similar model turbine that produces unit power ($1 \text{ kW}$) under unit head ($1 \text{ m}$):

  1. Tangential speed: $u = \frac{\pi D N}{60} \propto \sqrt{H} \implies D \propto \frac{\sqrt{H}}{N}$
  2. Discharge: $Q \propto D^2 V_f \propto \left(\frac{\sqrt{H}}{N}\right)^2 \sqrt{H} \propto \frac{H^{3/2}}{N^2}$
  3. Power output: $P \propto \rho g Q H \propto \frac{H^{3/2}}{N^2} \cdot H = \frac{H^{5/2}}{N^2} \implies N^2 \propto \frac{H^{5/2}}{P} \implies N \propto \frac{H^{5/4}}{\sqrt{P}}$
  4. Setting $P = 1 \text{ kW}$ and $H = 1 \text{ m}$ gives $N = N_s$:
$$ N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}} \quad (P \text{ in kW}, H \text{ in m}, N \text{ in rpm}) $$

2. Turbine Affinity Laws (Scaling Laws)

For geometrically similar turbines of diameters $D_1, D_2$ running at speeds $N_1, N_2$ under heads $H_1, H_2$:

$$ \frac{H_1}{H_2} = \left(\frac{N_1}{N_2}\right)^2 \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^2 $$ $$ \frac{Q_1}{Q_2} = \left(\frac{N_1}{N_2}\right) \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^3 = \left(\frac{H_1}{H_2}\right)^{1/2} \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^2 $$ $$ \frac{P_1}{P_2} = \left(\frac{N_1}{N_2}\right)^3 \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^5 = \left(\frac{H_1}{H_2}\right)^{3/2} \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^2 $$

Level 3: Moody Scale-Up Formula & Boundary Corrections Advanced

1. Moody Efficiency Step-Up Formula

Because full-scale prototype turbines have larger hydraulic passages, relative surface roughness and boundary layer shear losses are smaller than in small scale laboratory models. Therefore, the prototype efficiency ($\eta_p$) is higher than model efficiency ($\eta_m$):

$$ \frac{1 - \eta_p}{1 - \eta_m} = \left(\frac{D_m}{D_p}\right)^n \left(\frac{H_m}{H_p}\right)^m \approx \left(\frac{D_m}{D_p}\right)^{0.2} $$

(Moody's standard empirical exponent is $n = 0.2 = 1/5$, and $m \approx 0.01-0.1$).

2. Cavitation Scaling (Thoma Parameter $\sigma$)

For cavitation similarity between model and prototype: $\sigma_p = \sigma_m = \frac{(p_{atm}/\rho g) - h_s - (p_v/\rho g)}{H}$.

Level 4: Master Summary & Turbine vs Pump Scaling

Parameter Hydraulic Turbine Formula Centrifugal Pump Formula Key Physical Contrast
Specific Speed ($N_s$) $$N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$$ $$N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{Q}}{H^{3/4}}$$ Turbine is power producer ($P$); Pump is flow deliverer ($Q$).
Unit Speed ($N_u$) $$N_u = \frac{N}{\sqrt{H}}$$ $$N_u = \frac{N}{\sqrt{H}}$$ Directly proportional to flow velocity $V \propto \sqrt{H}$.
Unit Flow / Power $$P_u = \frac{P}{H^{3/2}}$$ $$Q_u = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{H}}$$ $P_u = \rho g Q_u \eta$ connects both.
Speed Change at Const. $D$ $P \propto N^3$, $Q \propto N$, $H \propto N^2$ $P \propto N^3$, $Q \propto N$, $H \propto N^2$ Same kinematic scaling for all turbomachinery.

Level 5: Aviation & Aerospace Systems Relevance

  • Gas Turbine Engine Scale Rig Testing: Subscale aero-engine compressor and turbine rigs are aerodynamically scaled using non-dimensional speed $\frac{N}{\sqrt{T_0}}$ and corrected mass flow $\frac{\dot{m}\sqrt{T_0}}{p_0}$, derived directly from turbomachinery affinity laws.
  • Aircraft RAT (Ram Air Turbine) Scaling: Prototype flight performance across altitudes ($0 - 41,000 \text{ ft}$) is verified in wind tunnels by matching advance ratio and specific power output.
  • Turbopump Scaling: Cryogenic fuel and oxidizer turbopump drive turbines in space launch vehicles are designed via subscale cold-gas air turbine tests, applying Moody step-up relations for cryogenic flow.

Level 6: UPSC ASO Focus & Common Traps

  • Trap 1 (Exponent Confusion): In turbines, $N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$. Do not write $H^{3/4}$ (that is for pumps).
  • Trap 2 (Unit Power vs Unit Speed): Unit power has $H^{1.5} = H^{3/2}$ in denominator; Unit discharge has $H^{0.5} = \sqrt{H}$.
  • Trap 3 (Scale Efficiency Step-Up): Prototype efficiency is ALWAYS higher than model efficiency ($\eta_p > \eta_m$), because large prototypes have lower relative viscous wall friction ($1 - \eta_p < 1 - \eta_m$).

UPSC ASO Practice Questions (30 MCQs)

Q1. The specific speed of a hydraulic turbine is defined as the speed of a geometrically similar turbine that produces:
A) 1 unit of discharge under 1 unit of head
B) 1 unit of shaft power under 1 unit of head
C) 1 unit of torque at 1000 rpm
D) Maximum efficiency at runaway speed
By definition, turbine specific speed $N_s$ is the speed (in rpm) of a geometrically similar model turbine developing $1 \text{ kW}$ under a net head of $1 \text{ m}$.
Q2. What is the formula for the Unit Discharge ($Q_u$) of a turbine?
A) $Q_u = \frac{Q}{H}$
B) $Q_u = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{H}}$
C) $Q_u = \frac{Q}{H^{3/2}}$
D) $Q_u = Q \sqrt{H}$
Since flow velocity $V \propto \sqrt{H}$ and area is fixed, discharge under unit head is $Q_u = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{H}}$.
Q3. If the operating head on a turbine is increased by a factor of 4, the shaft power developed will increase by a factor of:
A) 2
B) 4
C) 8
D) 16
From unit power $P_u = \frac{P}{H^{3/2}}$, power scales as $P \propto H^{3/2}$. If $H \to 4H$, $P \to 4^{3/2} P = (\sqrt{4})^3 P = 2^3 P = 8 P$.
Q4. In Moody's step-up formula $\frac{1 - \eta_p}{1 - \eta_m} = \left(\frac{D_m}{D_p}\right)^n$, what is the typical empirical value of exponent $n$?
A) $0.20$ ($1/5$)
B) $0.50$ ($1/2$)
C) $1.00$
D) $2.00$
Moody's classic formula uses $n = 0.2 = \frac{1}{5}$, accounting for the scale effect where larger prototypes exhibit higher boundary layer Reynolds numbers and lower skin friction coefficients.
Q5. A 1:5 scale model of a Francis turbine is tested in a lab. If model efficiency is $\eta_m = 88\%$, what is the estimated prototype efficiency using Moody's formula ($n = 0.2$)?
A) $85.4\%$
B) $91.3\%$
C) $96.5\%$
D) $88.0\%$
$1 - \eta_p = (1 - 0.88)\left(\frac{1}{5}\right)^{0.2} = 0.12 \times 0.7248 = 0.087 \implies \eta_p = 1 - 0.087 = 0.913 = 91.3\%$.
Q6. When the rotational speed ($N$) of a hydraulic turbine is doubled while diameter remains constant, the shaft power developed increases by a factor of:
A) 2
B) 4
C) 8
D) 16
Affinity law for power at constant diameter is $P \propto N^3$. If speed doubles ($N \to 2N$), power scales by $2^3 = 8$.
Q7. What is the Unit Speed ($N_u$) of a turbine running at $300 \text{ rpm}$ under a net head of $25 \text{ m}$?
A) $12 \text{ rpm}$
B) $60 \text{ rpm}$
C) $1500 \text{ rpm}$
D) $7500 \text{ rpm}$
$N_u = \frac{N}{\sqrt{H}} = \frac{300}{\sqrt{25}} = \frac{300}{5} = 60 \text{ rpm}$.
Q8. For the turbine in Q7, if the head drops to $16 \text{ m}$, what will be the new operating speed to maintain homologous flow similarity?
A) $192 \text{ rpm}$
B) $240 \text{ rpm}$
C) $280 \text{ rpm}$
D) $300 \text{ rpm}$
Since $N_u = 60 = \frac{N_{new}}{\sqrt{16}} \implies N_{new} = 60 \times 4 = 240 \text{ rpm}$.
Q9. In aero-engine turbomachinery scaling, the corrected speed parameter is given by:
A) $\frac{N}{\sqrt{T_0}}$
B) $\frac{N}{T_0^2}$
C) $N \sqrt{p_0}$
D) $\frac{N}{\rho_0}$
Corrected rotational speed is non-dimensionalized by stagnation sound speed ($a_0 \propto \sqrt{\gamma R T_0}$), yielding $\frac{N}{\sqrt{T_0}}$.
Q10. Two geometrically similar turbines A and B have diameters in the ratio $D_A / D_B = 2$. If they operate under the same head, their rotational speeds $N_A / N_B$ will be in the ratio:
A) $1/2$
B) $2/1$
C) $4/1$
D) $1/4$
At constant head $H$, peripheral speed $u \propto N D = \text{const} \implies \frac{N_A}{N_B} = \frac{D_B}{D_A} = \frac{1}{2}$.
Q11. The dimensionless specific speed (shape number) in SI units is defined as:
A) $K_s = \frac{\omega \sqrt{P/\rho}}{(g H)^{5/4}}$
B) $K_s = \frac{N P}{H}$
C) $K_s = \frac{\omega Q}{g H}$
D) $K_s = \frac{P}{\rho N^3 D^5}$
The purely dimensionless form of turbine specific speed is $K_s = \frac{\omega \sqrt{P/\rho}}{(g H)^{5/4}}$ (where $\omega$ is in rad/s, $P$ is in W, $gH$ is specific energy in J/kg).
Q12. What is the relation between turbine power $P$, rotational speed $N$, and runner diameter $D$ according to turbomachinery affinity laws?
A) $P \propto N^3 D^5$
B) $P \propto N^2 D^3$
C) $P \propto N D^2$
D) $P \propto N^3 D^2$
Power coefficient $C_P = \frac{P}{\rho N^3 D^5} = \text{const} \implies P \propto N^3 D^5$.
Q13. If two homologous turbines have scale ratio $D_p / D_m = 4$ and operate under the same head, the power ratio $P_p / P_m$ is:
A) 4
B) 16
C) 64
D) 1024
At constant head $H$, power scales as $P \propto D^2 H^{3/2} \propto D^2$. Thus $\frac{P_p}{P_m} = \left(\frac{D_p}{D_m}\right)^2 = 4^2 = 16$.
Q14. Why is scale-up efficiency higher in full-scale prototype hydraulic turbines compared to lab models?
A) Larger hydraulic passages result in higher Reynolds numbers and smaller relative boundary layer thickness, reducing skin friction losses
B) Prototypes use magical anti-friction coatings
C) Models operate without gravity
D) Water becomes denser in large machines
Relative surface roughness $\epsilon/D$ is lower in large machines, and higher passage Reynolds numbers reduce friction coefficients ($C_f \propto Re^{-1/5}$).
Q15. A Pelton wheel develops $5000 \text{ kW}$ under head $H = 250 \text{ m}$ at $500 \text{ rpm}$. What is its Unit Power ($P_u$)?
A) $1.265 \text{ kW}$
B) $20.0 \text{ kW}$
C) $100.0 \text{ kW}$
D) $0.80 \text{ kW}$
$P_u = \frac{P}{H^{3/2}} = \frac{5000}{250^{1.5}} = \frac{5000}{250 \sqrt{250}} = \frac{20}{\sqrt{250}} = \frac{20}{15.811} \approx 1.265 \text{ kW}$.
Q16. The head coefficient ($C_H$) in non-dimensional turbomachinery analysis is given by:
A) $C_H = \frac{g H}{N^2 D^2}$
B) $C_H = \frac{N \sqrt{H}}{D}$
C) $C_H = \frac{H}{N D}$
D) $C_H = \frac{g H}{Q^2}$
Head coefficient is the ratio of specific fluid energy to blade kinetic energy: $C_H = \frac{g H}{N^2 D^2}$ (or $\frac{g H}{\omega^2 D^2}$).
Q17. The discharge coefficient ($C_Q$ or Flow Coefficient $\phi$) is defined as:
A) $\phi = \frac{Q}{N D^3}$
B) $\phi = \frac{Q}{N^2 D^2}$
C) $\phi = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{g H}}$
D) $\phi = \frac{Q N}{D^2}$
Flow coefficient is $\phi = \frac{Q}{N D^3}$ (or $\frac{Q}{\omega D^3}$).
Q18. If a turbine generates power $P$ at design speed $N$, what will be the power output at runaway speed where output torque drops to zero?
A) Zero ($P = 0$)
B) Maximum power
C) Double the rated power
D) $1.8 \times P$
At runaway speed, the resisting generator torque is zero ($T = 0$). Since Power $P = T \cdot \omega$, useful power output is strictly zero.
Q19. Specific speed is a criterion primarily used for:
A) Selecting the optimal type of turbine runner for a given head and discharge site
B) Measuring generator electrical resistance
C) Calculating concrete strength of the dam
D) Regulating penstock pipe temperature
Specific speed directly indicates runner geometry: low $N_s$ ($10-35$) for Pelton, medium $N_s$ ($60-300$) for Francis, high $N_s$ ($300-1000$) for Kaplan.
Q20. When testing a scale model turbine, geometric similarity requires:
A) Strict ratio equality of all linear dimensions and blade angles between model and prototype
B) Equal weight
C) Identical electrical output
D) Same speed in rpm regardless of size
Geometric similarity implies identical spatial proportions, blade profiles, curvature, and clearance ratios ($\frac{L_p}{L_m} = \lambda$).
Q21. In a hydroelectric plant, two identical turbines run in parallel under head $H$. If each develops power $P$, their combined specific speed is:
A) $\sqrt{2} N_s$
B) $2 N_s$
C) $N_s$ (for each unit)
D) $4 N_s$
Considering the two units as a single combined power station: $P_{total} = 2P \implies N_{s,total} = \frac{N\sqrt{2P}}{H^{5/4}} = \sqrt{2} N_s$. Individually, each machine maintains $N_s$.
Q22. A turbine model test under head $H_m = 9 \text{ m}$ produces $P_m = 30 \text{ kW}$ at $N_m = 450 \text{ rpm}$. The prototype turbine operates under $H_p = 36 \text{ m}$ with scale ratio $D_p / D_m = 3$. What is the prototype speed $N_p$?
A) $150 \text{ rpm}$
B) $300 \text{ rpm}$
C) $450 \text{ rpm}$
D) $900 \text{ rpm}$
$\frac{N_p D_p}{\sqrt{H_p}} = \frac{N_m D_m}{\sqrt{H_m}} \implies N_p = N_m \left(\frac{D_m}{D_p}\right) \sqrt{\frac{H_p}{H_m}} = 450 \times \left(\frac{1}{3}\right) \times \sqrt{\frac{36}{9}} = 150 \times 2 = 300 \text{ rpm}$.
Q23. For the prototype in Q22, what is the expected prototype shaft power ($P_p$) assuming equal efficiency?
A) $2160 \text{ kW}$
B) $720 \text{ kW}$
C) $5400 \text{ kW}$
D) $1080 \text{ kW}$
$\frac{P_p}{P_m} = \left(\frac{D_p}{D_m}\right)^2 \left(\frac{H_p}{H_m}\right)^{3/2} = 3^2 \times \left(\frac{36}{9}\right)^{1.5} = 9 \times 4^{1.5} = 9 \times 8 = 72$. Thus $P_p = 72 \times 30 = 2160 \text{ kW}$.
Q24. In compressible turbomachinery such as aircraft gas turbines, aerodynamic similarity requires matching:
A) Mach number, Reynolds number, and specific heat ratio ($\gamma$)
B) Only wheel weight
C) Ambient humidity only
D) Fuel tank volume
High-speed gas turbine stages operate in transonic regimes where compressibility (Mach number $M$), viscous shear (Reynolds number $Re$), and thermodynamics ($\gamma$) govern similarity.
Q25. If specific speed of a turbine is calculated as $N_s = 25$, the turbine is uniquely categorized as:
A) Pelton wheel (Single jet)
B) Francis turbine
C) Kaplan turbine
D) Axial propeller pump
Specific speed $N_s$ between $10$ and $35$ strictly corresponds to single-jet Pelton impulse turbines.
Q26. What happens to the Unit Discharge ($Q_u$) if the physical turbine runner geometry is unchanged?
A) It remains constant for all operating heads at homologous gate openings
B) It drops to zero when head increases
C) It doubles every minute
D) It is proportional to $H^2$
By definition, unit quantities ($N_u, Q_u, P_u$) are invariant characteristic parameters of a turbine geometry at a fixed gate opening.
Q27. The power developed by a turbine is $P = \rho g Q H \eta$. Substituting $Q = Q_u \sqrt{H}$, unit power can be written as:
A) $P_u = \rho g Q_u \eta$
B) $P_u = Q_u / \eta$
C) $P_u = \rho g \sqrt{Q_u}$
D) $P_u = H / Q_u$
$P = \rho g (Q_u \sqrt{H}) H \eta = (\rho g Q_u \eta) H^{3/2} \implies P_u = \frac{P}{H^{3/2}} = \rho g Q_u \eta$.
Q28. In wind tunnel testing of aircraft Ram Air Turbines (RAT), dynamic similarity is achieved by matching:
A) Tip speed ratio ($\lambda = \frac{\omega R}{V_\infty}$) and blade incidence angle
B) Fuselage paint color
C) Cockpit pressure only
D) Engine fuel flow rate
For axial wind/air turbines, the tip speed ratio $\lambda = \frac{\omega R}{V_\infty}$ establishes kinematic velocity triangle similarity.
Q29. A Francis turbine with $N_s = 150$ operates under $H = 100 \text{ m}$ producing $10,000 \text{ kW}$. If the speed is $N = 474 \text{ rpm}$, what happens if head drops to $81 \text{ m}$?
A) Speed must adjust to $426.6 \text{ rpm}$ to maintain maximum efficiency
B) Speed increases to $1000 \text{ rpm}$
C) Power increases by 50%
D) Runner stops immediately
$N_u = \frac{474}{\sqrt{100}} = 47.4 \implies N_{new} = 47.4 \times \sqrt{81} = 47.4 \times 9 = 426.6 \text{ rpm}$.
Q30. If the diameter of a turbine is reduced by 10% while speed and head are held constant, shaft power will decrease by approximately:
A) $10\%$
B) $19\%$ ($P \propto D^2$)
C) $40\%$
D) $0\%$
At constant head $H$, power $P \propto D^2$. If $D \to 0.9 D$, $P \to (0.9)^2 P = 0.81 P$ (a reduction of $1 - 0.81 = 19\%$).

MT10. What parameter links the unit power ($P_u$) and unit discharge ($Q_u$) of a turbine?
A) $P_u = \rho g Q_u \eta_o$

$P = \eta_o \rho g Q H \implies \frac{P}{H^{3/2}} = \rho g \eta_o \left(\frac{Q}{\sqrt{H}}\right) \implies P_u = \rho g \eta_o Q_u$.

Top Candidate Error Traps

  1. Mixing Turbine vs Pump Specific Speed: Turbines use power ($N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$), while pumps use discharge ($N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{Q}}{H^{3/4}}$).
  2. Unit Quantities Exponents: $N_u = \frac{N}{\sqrt{H}}$ (exponent $0.5$), $Q_u = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{H}}$ (exponent $0.5$), $P_u = \frac{P}{H^{3/2}}$ (exponent $1.5$).
  3. Scale Effect Assumption: Never assume model efficiency is equal to prototype efficiency ($\eta_m \neq \eta_p$). Prototypes are always more efficient due to lower relative boundary layer skin friction.
  4. Power vs Speed vs Diameter Scaling: $P \propto N^3 D^5$ is the fundamental affinity relation at varying speed and diameter.
