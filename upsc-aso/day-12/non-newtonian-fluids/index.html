Non-Newtonian Fluid Rheology

Day 12 of 129 Fluid Rheology & Viscosity Phase 1: Foundations 75-85 min Master Module

Level 1: Beginner's Glossary & Rheology Definitions Basic

Before comparing rheological curves, here is the complete beginner's dictionary of all fluid viscosity terms, power-law parameters, and fluid classification categories:

1. Newtonian Fluid ($\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy}$)

  • Definition: A fluid whose shear stress is directly and linearly proportional to the velocity gradient (shear rate).
  • Key Property: Dynamic viscosity $\mu$ is a constant material property independent of shear rate or agitation speed.
  • Examples: Pure water, air, gasoline, kerosene, light lubricating oil, mercury.

2. Non-Newtonian Fluid

  • Definition: A fluid that deviates from Newton's law of viscosity; its resistance to flow (apparent viscosity $\eta_{app}$) changes with the rate of shearing or duration of shearing.
  • Examples: Blood, paint, toothpaste, custard/cornstarch, quicksand, synthetic polymer lubricants.

3. Apparent Viscosity ($\eta_{app}$) & Shear Rate ($\dot{\gamma}$)

  • Shear Rate ($\dot{\gamma} = \frac{du}{dy}$): The rate of relative sliding deformation between adjacent fluid layers [$\text{s}^{-1}$].
  • Apparent Viscosity ($\eta_{app} = \frac{\tau}{\dot{\gamma}}$): The effective viscosity exhibited by the fluid at that specific shear rate [$\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$].

4. Power Law Index ($n$) & Consistency Index ($K$)

  • Power Law Model: $\tau = K \dot{\gamma}^n \implies \eta_{app} = K \dot{\gamma}^{n-1}$.
  • $K$ (Consistency Index): Measure of the average thickness/stickiness of the fluid [$\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}^n$].
  • $n$ (Flow Behavior Index):
    • $n = 1$: Newtonian (Constant viscosity)
    • $n < 1$: Pseudoplastic (Shear-thinning)
    • $n > 1$: Dilatant (Shear-thickening)

5. Yield Stress ($\tau_0$) & Bingham Plastics

  • Yield Stress ($\tau_0$): The threshold finite stress that must be exceeded before the material begins to flow like a fluid.
  • Bingham Plastic: Behaves as a rigid solid when $\tau < \tau_0$, and flows with constant plastic viscosity when $\tau \ge \tau_0$ ($\tau = \tau_0 + \mu_p \dot{\gamma}$, e.g. Toothpaste, mayonnaise, drilling mud).

6. Thixotropic vs Rheopectic (Time Effects)

  • Thixotropic (Time-Thinning): Viscosity decreases over time under constant continuous stirring (e.g. Printer ink, wall paint, ketchup).
  • Rheopectic (Time-Thickening): Viscosity increases over time under continuous agitation (e.g. Gypsum suspensions, printer toner pastes).

Level 2: Comprehensive Rheological Classifications Intermediate

1. Time-Independent Non-Newtonian Fluids

1. Pseudoplastic (Shear-Thinning)

  • Condition: $n < 1$, Yield stress $\tau_0 = 0$.
  • Behavior: Apparent viscosity decreases as shear rate increases ($\frac{d\eta_{app}}{d\dot{\gamma}} < 0$).
  • Examples: Polymer solutions, blood, aircraft anti-icing fluids, paints, paper pulp, multi-grade synthetic engine oils.

2. Dilatant (Shear-Thickening)

  • Condition: $n > 1$, Yield stress $\tau_0 = 0$.
  • Behavior: Apparent viscosity increases as shear rate increases ($\frac{d\eta_{app}}{d\dot{\gamma}} > 0$).
  • Examples: Cornstarch in water (Oobleck), quicksand, concentrated ceramic slurries, liquid armor shear-thickening fluids (STF).

3. Bingham Plastic (Ideal Plastic)

  • Condition: Requires a finite initial **yield stress ($\tau_0$)** before flow begins. Once $\tau > \tau_0$, flow is linear ($n = 1$): $$\tau = \tau_0 + \mu_p \frac{du}{dy}$$
  • Examples: Toothpaste, sewage sludge, drilling muds, mayonnaise, aircraft greases, chocolate.

4. Herschel-Bulkley Fluid

  • Condition: Yield stress plus non-linear power-law: $$\tau = \tau_0 + K \left(\frac{du}{dy}\right)^n$$
  • Examples: Polymeric aircraft sealants, rocket solid propellant binder slurries, heavy greases.

2. Time-Dependent Non-Newtonian Fluids

1. Thixotropic Fluids

  • Apparent viscosity decreases with time under constant sustained shear rate (structure breaks down reversible with time).
  • Examples: Aircraft Type IV anti-ice de-icing fluids, gelatin, printer inks, drilling muds, thixotropic paints.

2. Rheopectic Fluids

  • Apparent viscosity increases with time under constant sustained shear rate (structure builds up with agitation).
  • Examples: Gypsum pastes, bentonite clay lubricants, synovial fluid in biological joints.

3. Viscoelastic Fluids

Fluids that exhibit both viscous fluid behavior and elastic solid recovery (memory effect). Key macroscopic phenomena include Die Swell (extrudate swell) and the Weissenberg Effect (rod-climbing effect).

Level 3: Non-Newtonian Pipe Flow Velocity Profiles Advanced

1. Pipe Velocity Profile Shape vs Power Index ($n$)

For fully developed laminar flow of a power-law fluid in a circular pipe of radius $R$:

$$ u(r) = \left(\frac{\Delta p}{2 K L}\right)^{1/n} \left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right) R^{\frac{n+1}{n}} \left[1 - \left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}\right] $$
  • Newtonian ($n = 1$): Parabolic profile ($u \propto 1 - (r/R)^2$). Ratio $V_{max} / V_{avg} = 2.0$.
  • Pseudoplastic / Shear-Thinning ($n < 1$): **Blunter, plug-like profile** with steep velocity gradients near the wall. Ratio $V_{max}/V_{avg} = \frac{3n+1}{2n+1} < 2.0$ (e.g. For $n=0.2$, $V_{max}/V_{avg} = 1.14$).
  • Dilatant / Shear-Thickening ($n > 1$): **Sharper conical profile** with high centerline peak. Ratio $V_{max}/V_{avg} > 2.0$.

Level 5: Aviation & ASO Practical Applications

  • Aircraft Ground De-icing & Anti-icing Fluids (SAE AMS 1428 Type IV): Designed as highly pseudoplastic and thixotropic fluids. While the aircraft is stationary on the ground, high zero-shear viscosity holds the fluid as a thick protective blanket preventing snow/ice accretion (Holdover Time). During takeoff ground roll ($V > 80-100 \text{ knots}$), aerodynamic boundary layer shear rates spike to $\dot{\gamma} > 10,000 \text{ s}^{-1}$, causing viscosity to collapse instantly so the fluid shears cleanly off the wing before rotation, restoring wing aerodynamic lift profile!
  • Aircraft Synthetic Greases: Act as Bingham plastics in landing gear bushings and flight control hinge bearings, staying in place without dripping under gravity ($\tau < \tau_0$) but flowing freely when actuated by hydraulic torque ($\tau > \tau_0$).
  • Liquid Body Armor in Aviation Security: Kevlar vests impregnated with shear-thickening dilatant fluid (nanosilica in polyethylene glycol) remain flexible during normal pilot movement but instantly solidify into rigid composite plates upon high-strain projectile impact.

Level 6: UPSC ASO Focus & Common Traps

  • Trap 1 (Pseudoplastic vs Thixotropic): Pseudoplasticity is shear-rate dependent (viscosity drops with increasing speed). Thixotropy is time-dependent (viscosity drops with duration of stirring at constant speed).
  • Trap 2 (Bingham Plastic vs Herschel-Bulkley): Bingham plastic has a linear slope after yield stress ($n = 1$). Herschel-Bulkley has a non-linear power-law curve after yield stress ($n \neq 1$).
  • Trap 3 (Velocity Profile Bluntness): Pseudoplastic ($n < 1$) pipe flow is flatter/blunter than Newtonian parabolic flow ($V_{max}/V_{avg} < 2.0$). Dilatant ($n > 1$) is more peaked ($V_{max}/V_{avg} > 2.0$).

UPSC ASO Practice Questions (30 MCQs)

Q1. A fluid whose apparent viscosity decreases with an increase in velocity gradient (shear rate) is known as:
A) Pseudoplastic (Shear-Thinning) fluid
B) Dilatant (Shear-Thickening) fluid
C) Newtonian fluid
D) Ideal fluid
Pseudoplastic fluids have flow index $n < 1$, meaning apparent viscosity $\eta_{app} = K \dot{\gamma}^{n-1}$ decreases as shear rate $\dot{\gamma}$ increases.
Q2. In the Ostwald-de Waele power law model $\tau = K \left(\frac{du}{dy}\right)^n$, what value of $n$ corresponds to a Dilatant fluid?
A) $n > 1$
B) $n < 1$
C) $n = 1$
D) $n = 0$
For dilatant fluids, $n > 1$, causing apparent viscosity to increase with shear rate.
Q3. A Bingham Plastic fluid is characterized by which of the following rheological behaviors?
A) It behaves as a rigid solid until a critical yield stress ($\tau_0$) is exceeded, after which it flows with a constant plastic viscosity
B) Zero yield stress with viscosity dropping to zero
C) Viscosity increases with time of shear
D) Strictly infinite viscosity at all shear rates
Bingham plastics obey $\tau = \tau_0 + \mu_p \frac{du}{dy}$. Flow occurs only when applied stress $\tau > \tau_0$. (e.g. Toothpaste, heavy greases).
Q4. How do modern aircraft anti-icing fluids (SAE Type IV) utilize non-Newtonian pseudoplasticity during ground operations and takeoff?
A) They act as thick gels at low shear to protect wings against ice on the ground, but shear-thin to low viscosity at high takeoff airflow speeds, blowing cleanly off before rotation
B) They freeze into solid ice to strengthen the wing
C) They ignite fuel in the engine
D) They increase aerodynamic drag by 200%
High zero-shear viscosity provides long Holdover Time (HOT) on the ramp; high shear rate ($>10,000 \text{ s}^{-1}$) during the 100-knot takeoff roll causes the fluid to shear-thin and flow off, restoring clean wing aerodynamics.
Q5. A fluid whose apparent viscosity decreases with the duration of time under constant shear rate is classified as:
A) Thixotropic fluid
B) Rheopectic fluid
C) Dilatant fluid
D) Newtonian fluid
Thixotropy is time-dependent shear-thinning (viscosity decreases with time under continuous stirring).
Q6. Which of the following is a classic example of a Rheopectic fluid?
A) Gypsum paste and bentonite suspensions (viscosity builds up with time of agitation)
B) Pure water
C) Gasoline
D) Aviation kerosene
Rheopectic fluids thicken with duration of shearing. Gypsum paste and bentonite slurries solidify when gently sheared over time.
Q7. What is the shape of the fully developed laminar velocity profile for a Pseudoplastic fluid ($n < 1$) in a circular pipe?
A) Blunter and flatter (plug-like) than a Newtonian parabolic profile ($V_{max}/V_{avg} < 2.0$)
B) Sharper and more peaked than a parabola ($V_{max}/V_{avg} > 2.0$)
C) Exactly parabolic
D) Triangular
Because high shear near the walls thins the fluid while low shear near the center keeps it viscous, the center moves like a solid plug, creating a blunt velocity profile.
Q8. For a power-law fluid flowing in a pipe with $n = 0.5$, what is the ratio of maximum centerline velocity to average velocity ($V_{max}/V_{avg}$)?
A) $1.25$
B) $2.00$
C) $1.50$
D) $3.00$
For a power-law fluid in a pipe, $\frac{V_{max}}{V_{avg}} = \frac{3n + 1}{2n + 1} = \frac{3(0.5) + 1}{2(0.5) + 1} = \frac{1.5 + 1}{1.0 + 1} = \frac{2.5}{2.0} = 1.25$.
Q9. The rod-climbing phenomenon (Weissenberg Effect), where fluid climbs up a rotating vertical shaft, is a hallmark of:
A) Viscoelastic fluids (due to normal stress differences $\tau_{\theta\theta} - \tau_{rr} > 0$)
B) Ideal inviscid fluids
C) Pure water
D) Mercury
In viscoelastic fluids (e.g. Polymer melts), hoop stresses along circular streamlines squeeze fluid radially inward, overcoming centrifugal force and forcing fluid up the rod.
Q10. Which of the following fluids is strictly Newtonian under ordinary conditions?
A) Aviation Jet-A Kerosene
B) Human blood
C) Toothpaste
D) Cornstarch suspension
Simple low-molecular-weight hydrocarbons (kerosene, gasoline, pure water, air) exhibit constant dynamic viscosity and are strictly Newtonian.
Q11. The Herschel-Bulkley model is mathematically expressed as:
A) $\tau = \tau_0 + K \left(\frac{du}{dy}\right)^n$
B) $\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy}$
C) $\tau = K \left(\frac{du}{dy}\right)^n$
D) $\tau = \tau_0 + \mu_p \frac{du}{dy}$
The Herschel-Bulkley model combines a yield stress $\tau_0$ with non-linear power-law flow behavior ($\tau = \tau_0 + K \dot{\gamma}^n$).
Q12. What happens to blood viscosity when flowing through human capillaries at high shear rates versus low shear rates in veins?
A) Blood is pseudoplastic; at high shear rates, red blood cells deform and align, lowering apparent viscosity from $\sim 10 \text{ cP}$ to $\sim 3.5 \text{ cP}$
B) Blood viscosity increases by 1000%
C) Blood becomes an ideal solid
D) Viscosity remains strictly constant
Red blood cells form rouleaux aggregates at low shear, raising viscosity. At high shear, cells disentangle and streamline, exhibiting classic shear-thinning.
Q13. In a pseudoplastic fluid ($n = 0.6, K = 2.0 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}^{0.6}$), what is the apparent viscosity ($\eta_{app}$) at shear rate $\dot{\gamma} = 100 \text{ s}^{-1}$?
A) $0.317 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$
B) $2.000 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$
C) $12.62 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$
D) $0.020 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$
$\eta_{app} = K \dot{\gamma}^{n-1} = 2.0 \times (100)^{0.6 - 1} = 2.0 \times (100)^{-0.4} = 2.0 \times 0.15849 \approx 0.317 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$.
Q14. For the fluid in Q13, what is the shear stress ($\tau$) developed at $\dot{\gamma} = 100 \text{ s}^{-1}$?
A) $31.7 \text{ Pa}$
B) $200.0 \text{ Pa}$
C) $3.17 \text{ Pa}$
D) $317.0 \text{ Pa}$
$\tau = \eta_{app} \dot{\gamma} = 0.317 \times 100 = 31.7 \text{ Pa}$. (Or $\tau = K \dot{\gamma}^n = 2.0 \times 100^{0.6} = 2.0 \times 15.849 = 31.7 \text{ Pa}$).
Q15. In aircraft landing gear shock struts (oleo struts), multi-grade hydraulic fluids use Viscosity Index (VI) polymer improvers. These fluids behave as:
A) Mildly pseudoplastic fluids that minimize temperature-induced viscosity drops across $-54^\circ\text{C}$ to $+135^\circ\text{C}$
B) Bingham solids that never move
C) Dilatant slurries that seize during landing
D) Gases
Long-chain polymer VI improvers uncoil at high temperature to maintain damping viscosity while exhibiting shear-thinning during high-speed metering strokes.
Q16. What is the Die Swell (Barus effect) in polymer extrusion?
A) Viscoelastic polymer stream expands to a diameter significantly larger than the nozzle exit diameter upon emerging, due to elastic strain energy recovery
B) The nozzle shrinks due to cooling
C) Fluid evaporates instantly
D) Pressure drops to zero
Polymer chains stretched inside the die relax and snap back radially upon exiting the confined boundary, causing the jet to swell ($D_{jet} > D_{die}$).
Q17. On a rheogram ($\tau$ vs $\frac{du}{dy}$ plot), which curve passes through the origin $(0,0)$ with an upward concave curvature ($\frac{d^2\tau}{d\dot{\gamma}^2} > 0$)?
A) Dilatant fluid ($n > 1$)
B) Pseudoplastic fluid ($n < 1$)
C) Bingham plastic
D) Newtonian fluid
For $n > 1$ (dilatant), the slope increases with shear rate, giving upward concave curvature. (Pseudoplastic curves are downward convex).
Q18. On a rheogram, a Bingham plastic appears as:
A) A straight line with positive slope $\mu_p$ intercepting the vertical shear stress axis at $\tau = \tau_0$
B) A parabola passing through $(0,0)$
C) A horizontal line
D) A vertical line on the x-axis
$\tau = \tau_0 + \mu_p \dot{\gamma}$ is the equation of a straight line with y-intercept $\tau_0$ and slope $\mu_p$.
Q19. In solid rocket motor manufacturing, how does the composite propellant slurry (HTPB binder + ammonium perchlorate + aluminum powder) behave rheologically?
A) Highly filled Herschel-Bulkley / pseudoplastic paste with a yield stress, requiring vacuum casting vibration to fluidize and fill the rocket motor casing smoothly
B) Ideal inviscid gas
C) Pure water-like Newtonian fluid
D) Supercritical helium
With $86-88\%$ solids loading, propellant slurries possess high yield stress; vibrational casting shears the slurry above $\tau_0$, dropping apparent viscosity to fill mandrel cavities without voids.
Q20. What is the Deborah Number ($De$) in rheology?
A) Ratio of fluid relaxation time to observation/flow process time ($De = t_r / t_p$)
B) Ratio of density to viscosity
C) Ratio of speed to Mach number
D) Measure of fuel volatility
$De = \frac{t_{relaxation}}{t_{observation}}$. When $De \gg 1$, the material behaves as an elastic solid; when $De \ll 1$, it behaves as a purely viscous fluid.
Q21. Liquid body armor utilizes which non-Newtonian mechanism to dissipate bullet impact energy?
A) Shear-thickening (dilatancy) where extreme impact shear rates ($\dot{\gamma} > 10^4 \text{ s}^{-1}$) form instantaneous hydro-clusters that lock nanoparticles into a rigid structure
B) Evaporative cooling
C) Magnetic repulsion
D) Thixotropic liquefaction
At high impact rates, hydrodynamic forces overcome interparticle repulsion, jamming nanoparticles into rigid solid-like clusters that absorb kinetic energy across a wide area.
Q22. In pipe flow of a Bingham plastic, what is the flow structure in the core region where local shear stress is less than yield stress ($\tau < \tau_0$)?
A) A solid unsheared central cylindrical plug moving at uniform maximum velocity
B) Violent turbulence
C) Zero velocity (stationary fluid)
D) Counter-rotating vortices
Since shear stress varies linearly with radius ($\tau = \frac{r}{R}\tau_w$), near the axis $\tau < \tau_0$, so the central core cannot deform and travels as a solid plug.
Q23. For a dilatant fluid flowing in a pipe with $n = 2.0$, what is the ratio $V_{max}/V_{avg}$?
A) $1.40$
B) $2.00$
C) $1.75$
D) $0.80$
$\frac{V_{max}}{V_{avg}} = \frac{3n + 1}{2n + 1} = \frac{3(2) + 1}{2(2) + 1} = \frac{7}{5} = 1.40$.
Q24. A Bingham plastic has yield stress $\tau_0 = 50 \text{ Pa}$ and plastic viscosity $\mu_p = 0.4 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$. If the shear rate is $\dot{\gamma} = 200 \text{ s}^{-1}$, what is the shear stress?
A) $130 \text{ Pa}$
B) $80 \text{ Pa}$
C) $50 \text{ Pa}$
D) $10,000 \text{ Pa}$
$\tau = \tau_0 + \mu_p \dot{\gamma} = 50 + (0.4 \times 200) = 50 + 80 = 130 \text{ Pa}$.
Q25. Why do non-drip paints stay on the paintbrush without dripping but spread easily on the wall when brushed?
A) They are thixotropic/pseudoplastic: under low gravity shear on the brush they have high viscosity, but under high shear of the brush stroke their viscosity drops by orders of magnitude
B) Paint is magnetic
C) Wall creates a vacuum
D) Paint freezes on contact
Thixotropic gel structure prevents dripping at low shear ($\dot{\gamma} \approx 0$), while brushing applies high shear ($\dot{\gamma} > 1000 \text{ s}^{-1}$), thinning the paint for smooth leveling.
Q26. What is the unit of Consistency Index ($K$) in the Power Law model $\tau = K \dot{\gamma}^n$?
A) $\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}^n$ (or $\text{N}\cdot\text{s}^n/\text{m}^2$)
B) $\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$
C) $\text{m}^2/\text{s}$
D) Dimensionless
Since $\tau$ is in $\text{Pa}$ and $\dot{\gamma}$ is in $\text{s}^{-1}$, $K = \tau / \dot{\gamma}^n = \text{Pa}/(\text{s}^{-1})^n = \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}^n$.
Q27. In the Maxwell Model of viscoelasticity, the mechanical analog consists of:
A) A linear elastic spring and a viscous dashpot connected in series
B) A spring and dashpot connected in parallel (Kelvin-Voigt model)
C) Two springs in parallel
D) A single rigid rod
Maxwell model = Spring (Hookean elasticity) + Dashpot (Newtonian viscosity) in series ($\dot{\gamma} = \frac{\dot{\tau}}{G} + \frac{\tau}{\mu}$).
Q28. In an ideal plastic with infinite yield stress and zero subsequent viscosity, the rheological behavior corresponds to:
A) Perfectly rigid-plastic solid
B) Pure gas
C) Superfluid liquid
D) Newtonian fluid
Rigid-plastic materials withstand stress up to $\tau_0$ without deformation, then yield indefinitely.
Q29. What is the difference between an ideal fluid and a real fluid?
A) Ideal fluids are inviscid ($\mu = 0$) and incompressible ($\rho = \text{const}$); real fluids possess viscosity and compressibility
B) Ideal fluids only exist in winter
C) Real fluids have zero density
D) Ideal fluids have infinite weight
An ideal fluid has zero shear viscosity ($\mu = 0$), so shear stresses are zero everywhere ($\tau = 0$). Real fluids always exhibit finite viscosity.
Q30. If a fluid exhibits yield stress $\tau_0 = 30 \text{ Pa}$ and flows according to $\tau = 30 + 5 \dot{\gamma}^{0.5}$, it is classified as:
A) Herschel-Bulkley fluid (Yield-Pseudoplastic)
B) Bingham plastic
C) Newtonian fluid
D) Dilatant fluid
It possesses a non-zero yield stress ($\tau_0 = 30$) and a power-law index $n = 0.5 \neq 1$, which is the textbook definition of a Herschel-Bulkley fluid.

Top Candidate Error Traps

  1. Pseudoplastic vs Thixotropic: Pseudoplastic is shear-rate thinning ($n < 1$, instant recovery). Thixotropic is time-dependent thinning under constant shear.
  2. Bingham vs Herschel-Bulkley: Bingham plastic is linear after yield stress ($\tau = \tau_0 + \mu_p \dot{\gamma}$). Herschel-Bulkley is non-linear after yield stress ($\tau = \tau_0 + K \dot{\gamma}^n$).
  3. Pipe Velocity Profile: Pseudoplastic ($n < 1$) has $V_{max}/V_{avg} < 2$ (plug-like). Dilatant ($n > 1$) has $V_{max}/V_{avg} > 2$ (sharp peak).
