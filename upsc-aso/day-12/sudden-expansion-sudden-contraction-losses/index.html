Sudden Expansion & Contraction Head Losses

Day 12 of 129 Pipe Internal Flow & Minor Losses Phase 1: Foundations 70-80 min Master Module

Level 1: Beginner's Glossary & Minor Loss Definitions Basic

Before deriving loss equations, here is the complete beginner's dictionary of all pipe loss parameters, coefficients, geometric transitions, and energy lines:

1. Major Loss vs Minor Loss ($h_L$)

  • Major Loss ($h_f$): Continuous friction loss along straight pipe walls, governed by Darcy-Weisbach: $h_f = \frac{f L V^2}{2 g D}$.
  • Minor Loss ($h_m$): Localized head loss caused by flow separation and turbulent eddies at geometric discontinuities (expansions, contractions, valves, fittings, bends).
  • Formula: $h_L = K \frac{V^2}{2g}$ [$\text{meters of liquid}$].

2. Loss Coefficient ($K$) & Velocity Head

  • Velocity Head ($\frac{V^2}{2g}$): Kinetic energy per unit weight of moving fluid in units of height ($1 \text{ m/s} \approx 0.051 \text{ m}$).
  • Loss Coefficient ($K$): Dimensionless factor representing the fraction of velocity head dissipated irreversibly into heat: $K = \frac{h_L}{V^2 / 2g}$.

3. Sudden Expansion & Borda-Carnot Head ($h_e$)

  • Definition: Abrupt enlargement from small pipe $A_1$ to large pipe $A_2$. High-speed jet separates from sharp corners, creating turbulent dead-zone corner eddies.
  • Borda-Carnot Formula: $h_e = \frac{(V_1 - V_2)^2}{2g} = \left(1 - \frac{A_1}{A_2}\right)^2 \frac{V_1^2}{2g}$.
  • Pipe Exit Loss: When discharging into an infinite reservoir ($A_2 \to \infty, V_2 \to 0$), $K = 1.0 \implies h_{exit} = \frac{V_1^2}{2g}$.

4. Vena Contracta & Sudden Contraction ($h_c$)

  • Vena Contracta ($A_c$): The narrowest cross-sectional area of the converging fluid jet formed just downstream of a sharp pipe constriction ($A_c = C_c A_2$, where $C_c \approx 0.62 - 0.64$).
  • Contraction Loss: Arises solely from turbulent re-expansion from $A_c$ to $A_2$: $$h_c = \left(\frac{1}{C_c} - 1\right)^2 \frac{V_2^2}{2g} \approx 0.375 - 0.50 \frac{V_2^2}{2g}$$

5. Equivalent Pipe Length ($L_{eq}$)

  • Definition: The length of an imaginary straight pipe of diameter $D$ and friction factor $f$ that would generate the exact same head loss as the fitting: $$L_{eq} = \frac{K D}{f}$$

6. Hydraulic Grade Line (HGL) vs Total Energy Line (TEL)

  • Total Energy Line (TEL): Elevation of total energy head: $TEL = z + \frac{p}{\rho g} + \frac{V^2}{2g}$. TEL always drops in the direction of flow.
  • Hydraulic Grade Line (HGL): Elevation of piezometric head: $HGL = z + \frac{p}{\rho g} = TEL - \frac{V^2}{2g}$.
  • Expansion Effect: At sudden expansion, TEL drops by $h_e$, but HGL rises due to dynamic pressure conversion!

Level 2: Derivations of Borda-Carnot & Contraction Losses Intermediate

1. Sudden Expansion (Borda-Carnot Formulation)

Applying the 1D Momentum Equation and Energy Equation between sections 1 and 2, assuming dead-zone corner pressure $p' \approx p_1$:

$$ h_e = \frac{(V_1 - V_2)^2}{2g} = \frac{V_1^2}{2g}\left(1 - \frac{A_1}{A_2}\right)^2 = \frac{V_2^2}{2g}\left(\frac{A_2}{A_1} - 1\right)^2 $$
  • Special Case (Pipe Exit to Large Tank): As $A_2 \to \infty$ ($V_2 \to 0$), the entire kinetic head is dissipated: $$h_{exit} = \frac{V_1^2}{2g} \quad (K_{exit} = 1.0)$$

2. Sudden Contraction Formulation

Loss occurs during expansion from vena contracta section $c$ to downstream section $2$:

$$ h_c = \frac{(V_c - V_2)^2}{2g} = \left(\frac{1}{C_c} - 1\right)^2 \frac{V_2^2}{2g} = K_c \frac{V_2^2}{2g} $$
  • For standard water flow with $C_c \approx 0.62$: $K_c = \left(\frac{1}{0.62} - 1\right)^2 = (1.613 - 1)^2 \approx 0.375$.
  • Special Case (Sharp Pipe Entrance from Large Tank): For $A_1 \to \infty$ ($C_c \approx 0.60$), entrance loss coefficient is standard: $$h_{entrance} = 0.5 \frac{V_2^2}{2g} \quad (K_{entry} = 0.5)$$

Level 3: Gradual Transitions & Equivalent Pipe Length Advanced

1. Gradual Expansion (Conical Diffuser)

Head loss in a conical diffuser is parameterized by total expansion cone angle $2\theta$:

  • Optimal Angle ($2\theta \approx 7^\circ - 8^\circ$): Minimizes the sum of wall skin friction and eddy separation loss.
  • Wide Angles ($2\theta > 35^\circ$): Loss exceeds that of a sudden expansion due to severe stalled boundary layer flow.

2. Bellmouth & Rounded Entrances

Rounding the pipe entrance ($r/D \ge 0.15$) eliminates the vena contracta, reducing entrance loss coefficient from $K = 0.50$ down to $K \approx 0.04 - 0.05$.

3. Equivalent Length ($L_{eq}$) Concept

$$ h_L = K \frac{V^2}{2g} = f \frac{L_{eq}}{D}\frac{V^2}{2g} \implies L_{eq} = \frac{K D}{f} $$

Level 5: Aviation & ASO Practical Applications

  • Aircraft Fuel System Manifolds: Cross-feed fuel distribution lines incorporate bellmouth suction pickups in fuel tanks to minimize suction head loss and prevent fuel cavitation/vapor lock.
  • Aircraft Hydraulic Valve Metering: High-pressure 3000/5000 psi fly-by-wire servo-valves experience extreme localized sudden expansions, causing localized fluid heating and micro-cavitation pitting.
  • Turbine Blade Internal Cooling Ducts: Serpentine passages utilize turbulators, $180^\circ$ bends, and sudden area changes to enhance heat transfer while carefully managing compressor bleed pressure loss.

Level 6: UPSC ASO Focus & Common Traps

  • Trap 1 (Expansion vs Contraction Reference Velocity): Expansion loss $h_e = \frac{(V_1 - V_2)^2}{2g}$ uses $(V_1 - V_2)$. Contraction loss $h_c = K_c \frac{V_2^2}{2g}$ is referenced to the downstream (smaller pipe) velocity $V_2$, NOT $V_1$.
  • Trap 2 (Where Contraction Loss Occurs): Loss in sudden contraction does NOT occur during convergence; it occurs during the turbulent re-expansion from the vena contracta to the downstream wall.
  • Trap 3 (Diffuser Angle Trap): The optimum diffuser angle is around $7^\circ-8^\circ$. If angle $>35^\circ-40^\circ$, loss is actually worse than a sudden step expansion!

UPSC ASO Practice Questions (30 MCQs)

Q1. What is the theoretical head loss ($h_e$) due to a sudden enlargement in a pipe carrying liquid from velocity $V_1$ to $V_2$?
A) $h_e = \frac{V_1^2 - V_2^2}{2g}$
B) $h_e = \frac{(V_1 - V_2)^2}{2g}$
C) $h_e = \frac{V_1^2 + V_2^2}{2g}$
D) $h_e = \frac{(V_1 - V_2)}{2g}$
By the Borda-Carnot equation derived from momentum and energy conservation, $h_e = \frac{(V_1 - V_2)^2}{2g}$.
Q2. When water discharges from a pipe of velocity $V$ into a large reservoir, the exit loss is:
A) $0.5 \frac{V^2}{2g}$
B) $1.0 \frac{V^2}{2g}$
C) Zero
D) $2.0 \frac{V^2}{2g}$
In discharging into an infinitely large reservoir ($V_2 = 0$), all kinetic energy is dissipated into heat: $h_{exit} = \frac{(V - 0)^2}{2g} = \frac{V^2}{2g}$ ($K = 1.0$).
Q3. Head loss due to sudden contraction in a pipeline occurs primarily due to:
A) Flow convergence before entering the small pipe
B) Turbulent deceleration and eddy generation during re-expansion of the flow from the vena contracta to the downstream pipe walls
C) Wall roughness of the inlet edge
D) Gravity effects
Flow accelerates smoothly into the vena contracta with negligible loss; nearly all energy dissipation occurs during the turbulent deceleration from vena contracta to downstream pipe diameter.
Q4. A pipeline abruptly expands from diameter $D_1 = 100 \text{ mm}$ to $D_2 = 200 \text{ mm}$. If water velocity in the smaller pipe is $V_1 = 4 \text{ m/s}$, what is the head loss due to sudden expansion? ($g = 9.81$)
A) $0.459 \text{ m}$
B) $0.815 \text{ m}$
C) $0.229 \text{ m}$
D) $1.63 \text{ m}$
$V_2 = V_1 \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^2 = 4 \times \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = 1.0 \text{ m/s}$. $h_e = \frac{(V_1 - V_2)^2}{2g} = \frac{(4 - 1)^2}{2 \times 9.81} = \frac{9}{19.62} \approx 0.4587 \text{ m}$.
Q5. What is the standard loss coefficient for a sharp-edged flush pipe entrance from a large water tank?
A) $K = 0.50$

C) $K = 0.04$
D) $K = 0.00$
A sharp, square-edged flush pipe entry generates a vena contracta with $C_c \approx 0.60$, yielding $K = \left(\frac{1}{0.60} - 1\right)^2 \approx 0.44 - 0.50$.
Q6. What is the loss coefficient for a re-entrant (Borda) pipe entrance projecting inward into a reservoir?
A) $K \approx 0.80 - 1.00$
B) $K = 0.05$
C) $K = 0.20$
D) $K = 0.00$
Fluid must turn $180^\circ$ around the protruding pipe lip, contracting severely to $C_c \approx 0.50$, yielding $K = \left(\frac{1}{0.50} - 1\right)^2 = (2 - 1)^2 = 1.00$.
Q7. What is the optimum total included angle ($2\theta$) for a conical diffuser to minimize overall head loss?
A) $7^\circ - 8^\circ$
B) $45^\circ - 60^\circ$
C) $90^\circ$
D) $0.5^\circ$
At $7^\circ-8^\circ$, the trade-off between boundary layer skin friction (dominant at very low angles) and flow separation eddies (dominant at larger angles) reaches minimum total head loss.

Q9. If a well-rounded bellmouth entry ($r/D \ge 0.15$) is fitted to a pipe intake, the entrance loss coefficient drops from $0.50$ to:

Q24. A pipe of diameter $D = 0.1 \text{ m}$ has five $90^\circ$ standard elbows ($K = 0.8$ each) and one globe valve ($K = 10.0$). Given friction factor $f = 0.02$, what is the total equivalent length ($L_{eq}$) of these fittings?

Top Candidate Error Traps

  1. Expansion Loss Formula: $h_e = \frac{(V_1 - V_2)^2}{2g}$. Never confuse with $\frac{V_1^2 - V_2^2}{2g}$ (which is the difference in velocity heads, not head loss).
  2. Contraction Velocity Reference: Contraction loss is calculated with downstream velocity $V_2$ ($h_c = K_c \frac{V_2^2}{2g}$).
  3. Diffuser Angle Trap: Very wide diffuser angles ($>40^\circ$) cause higher head loss than an abrupt $90^\circ$ step expansion.
  4. TEL vs HGL at Sudden Expansion: TEL drops by $h_e$, but HGL rises due to dynamic-to-static pressure conversion ($V_2 < V_1$).
