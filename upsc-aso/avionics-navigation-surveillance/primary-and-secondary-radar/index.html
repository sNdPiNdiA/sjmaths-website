Primary radar

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Air traffic control primary radars interface directly with ground automation systems to display raw uncooperative target positions independently of aircraft transponders.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Ground-based PSR provides vital redundancy during air traffic control operations when aircraft transponders fail, experience electrical power loss, or suffer total avionics blackouts.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA India and ICAO Annex 11 mandate the provision of primary surveillance radar coverage at designated high-density aerodromes to ensure separation services for non-transponder equipped aircraft.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers analyze primary radar raw recordings during near-mid-air collisions or runway incursions to reconstruct aircraft trajectories independently of pilot position reports.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Primary Surveillance Radar (PSR) is an active radiolocation system that determines target range, azimuth, and occasionally elevation by transmitting high-power electromagnetic pulses and detecting the uncooperative radio frequency energy reflected from the aircraft body: $$P_r = \frac{P_t G^2 \lambda^2 \sigma}{(4\pi)^3 R^4}$$
  • Physical Meaning: PSR operates independently of onboard aircraft equipment by bouncing transmitted radio frequency waves directly off the conductive metal skin and structure of the airframe.
  • Governing Law: The radar range equation dictates received echo power as an inverse fourth-power function of target range, establishing strict limits on maximum detection distance.
  • Continuum Boundary: Propagation occurs through the neutral atmosphere where electromagnetic wave interaction follows Maxwell's equations and atmospheric refraction laws.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $P_r$ = Received echo power at the antenna terminals (Watts)
  • $P_t$ = Peak transmitted pulse power from the magnetron or klystron (Watts)
  • $G$ = Isotropic antenna gain focusing beam energy in a specific direction (Dimensionless)
  • $\lambda$ = Electromagnetic wavelength of the transmitted RF signal (Meters)
  • $\sigma$ = Radar cross section (RCS) representing effective reflective surface area (Square meters)
  • $R$ = Slant range distance between the radar antenna and the target aircraft (Meters)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Pulse Generation: High-voltage modulators discharge stored electrical energy into RF power amplifiers, producing short, high-power electromagnetic wave bursts at microwave frequencies.
2. Directive Radiation: Highly directional parabolic or phased-array antennas focus the transmitted RF energy into a narrow beam, sweeping azimuthally to illuminate airspace.
3. Electromagnetic Scattering: Incident wave fronts strike the aircraft metallic skin, inducing surface currents that scatter and re-radiate radio frequency energy in all directions.
4. Echo Reception: A fraction of the backscattered RF energy returns to the antenna, passes through duplexers, and undergoes amplification and signal processing to extract target tracks.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Power Density at Target

Power density $S_i$ decreases with the square of distance from an isotropic radiator.

$$S_i = \frac{P_t G}{4\pi R^2}$$
Step 2: Effective Target Retroradiation

The target intercepts power and re-radiates it based on its effective radar cross section $\sigma$.

$$P_{scattered} = S_i \cdot \sigma = \frac{P_t G \sigma}{4\pi R^2}$$
Step 3: Power Density Returning to Radar

The scattered power spreads over another spherical wavefront of radius $R$ back to the radar.

$$S_r = \frac{P_{scattered}}{4\pi R^2} = \frac{P_t G \sigma}{(4\pi)^2 R^4}$$
Step 4: Final Radar Range Equation

Multiplying returned power density by effective antenna capture area $A_e = \frac{G \lambda^2}{4\pi}$ yields received power.

$$P_r = \frac{P_t G^2 \lambda^2 \sigma}{(4\pi)^3 R^4}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Power $P_r$ is $[M L^2 T^{-3}]$, Range $R$ is $[L]$, Area $\sigma$ is $[L^2]$, and Wavelength $\lambda$ is $[L]$. Substituting these into the radar range equation confirms dimensional homogeneity.
  • SI Unit: Power is measured in Watts ($W$), equivalent to Joules per second or ($kg \cdot m^2 \cdot s^{-3}$).
  • CGS Unit: Power is expressed in Ergs per second ($erg \cdot s^{-1}$), where $1 \text{ Watt} = 10^7 \text{ erg} \cdot \text{s}^{-1}$.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Radar range is expressed in nautical miles ($NM$), frequency in Gigahertz ($GHz$), and antenna beamwidth in degrees ($^{\circ}$).

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Governed by ICAO Doc 4444 (Air Traffic Management), ICAO Annex 10 (Aeronautical Telecommunications), and DGCA CAR Section 3 Series C.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: PSR operates independently of internal aircraft avionics, serving as the ultimate fail-safe surveillance medium when airborne transponders or pitot-static systems fail.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: In historic mid-air collisions involving total electrical/transponder failure, air traffic controllers maintained situational awareness and reconstructed flight paths exclusively through primary radar return logs.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Transponder Dependence

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming primary radar requires an active aircraft transponder or squawk code to display a target return.

✅ UPSC Truth: Primary radar is completely passive regarding aircraft cooperation; it detects unmodified radio frequency energy reflected directly off the aircraft metallic airframe.

Trap 2: Range Power Law Exponent

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing that received radar echo power is inversely proportional to the square of target range ($R^2$).

✅ UPSC Truth: Due to two-way propagation losses (outward power spread and return power spread), received power scales inversely with the fourth power of range ($R^4$).

Trap 3: Altitude Determination in 2D Radar

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming standard 2D primary surveillance radar provides precise target flight level and altitude without secondary radar assistance.

✅ UPSC Truth: Standard 2D primary radar measures only slant range and azimuth angle; altitude requires specialized 3D radars or Mode C/S secondary radar integration.

Trap 4: Radar Cross Section (RCS) Linearity

❌ Examiner Trap: Treating Radar Cross Section ($\sigma$) as identical to the physical cross-sectional geometric area of the aircraft.

✅ UPSC Truth: RCS is an electromagnetic property depending on frequency, aspect angle, surface material conductivity, and structural shape, often differing vastly from physical dimensions.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An Airport Surveillance Radar operating at S-band transmits $50 \text{ kW}$ of peak power with an antenna gain of $30 \text{ dB}$ ($1000$ numerical). The wavelength is $0.1 \text{ m}$, and the target aircraft has a radar cross section of $10 \text{ m}^2$. Calculate the received echo power from an aircraft at a slant range of $50,000 \text{ m}$.

Given Flight Parameters:
Peak power $P_t = 50,000 \text{ W}$, Antenna gain $G = 1,000$, Wavelength $\lambda = 0.1 \text{ m}$, RCS $\sigma = 10 \text{ m}^2$, Slant range $R = 50,000 \text{ m}$.
Governing Formula:
$$P_r = \frac{P_t G^2 \lambda^2 \sigma}{(4\pi)^3 R^4}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute numerator terms: $P_t G^2 \lambda^2 \sigma = 50,000 \times (10^3)^2 \times (0.1)^2 \times 10 = 50,000 \times 1,000,000 \times 0.01 \times 10 = 5 \times 10^7$. Step 2: Compute denominator terms: $(4\pi)^3 R^4 = (64 \times 31.006) \times (50,000)^4 = 1984.387 \times 6.25 \times 10^{18} = 1.2402 \times 10^{22}$. Step 3: Divide numerator by denominator: $P_r = \frac{5 \times 10^7}{1.2402 \times 10^{22}} = 4.03 imes 10^{-15} \text{ Watts}$.
Final Answer: $4.03 \times 10^{-15} \text{ Watts}$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

The extremely small magnitude of received radar power ($10^{-15} \text{ W}$) highlights the necessity for highly sensitive low-noise microwave receiver amplifiers and advanced digital signal processing to eliminate clutter and false alarms.

Question 1 • conceptual
Which of the following fundamental parameters determines the maximum detection range of a primary surveillance radar according to the radar range equation?
A Aircraft transponder squawk code
B Radar cross section (RCS) of the aircraft
C Pilot voice modulation frequency
D Barometric altimeter setting
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Primary radar return power is directly proportional to the radar cross section ($\sigma$) of the target aircraft, as defined by the radar range equation.
Question 2 • conceptual
In primary surveillance radar, why does received echo power decay rapidly with distance?
A Inverse-square law due to two-way propagation
B Inverse fourth-power law due to two-way spherical spreading
C Linear decrease due to atmospheric attenuation
D Exponential decay caused by transponder saturation
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Because power spreads out spherically on the outward journey to the target and again on the return journey back to the radar, received power is proportional to $1/R^4$.
Question 3 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding Primary Surveillance Radar (PSR): 1. PSR requires an active response from an onboard aircraft transponder. 2. PSR operates independently of onboard aircraft electrical and avionics systems. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is false because PSR is uncooperative and does not require a transponder. Statement 2 is correct because PSR bounces RF waves off the physical airframe.
Question 4 • numerical
If the slant range of an aircraft from a primary radar is doubled while keeping all other parameters constant, how does the received echo power change?
A Decreases by a factor of 4
B Decreases by a factor of 8
C Decreases by a factor of 16
D Decreases by a factor of 32
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Since received power $P_r \propto 1/R^4$, doubling the range ($2^4$) results in the received power decreasing by a factor of 16.
Question 5 • conceptual
What is the primary operational limitation of a standard 2D Airport Surveillance Primary Radar?
A Inability to measure target azimuth angle
B Inability to measure aircraft altitude directly
C Inability to measure slant range
D Excessive dependence on GPS satellite constellations
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A standard 2D primary radar measures slant range and azimuth but cannot determine precise aircraft elevation or barometric altitude without secondary radar or 3D radar capabilities.
Question 6 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Stealth aircraft are designed with angled facet surfaces and radar-absorbent materials. Reason (R): This minimizes their radar cross section (RCS), drastically reducing primary radar detection range.
A Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true, but R is false
D A is false, but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Stealth design deflects incident radar waves away from the receiver and absorbs RF energy, reducing the effective RCS ($\sigma$) and shrinking the detection range in the radar range equation.
Question 7 • conceptual
Which radar frequency band is most commonly utilized for Airport Surveillance Radars (ASR) due to favorable balance between antenna size and weather attenuation?
A HF Band (3 - 30 MHz)
B VHF Band (30 - 300 MHz)
C S-Band (2 - 4 GHz)
D Ka-Band (26 - 40 GHz)
Solution & Technical Rationale:
S-band frequencies provide an optimal compromise between atmospheric weather clutter penetration and manageable physical antenna dimensions for terminal area surveillance.
Question 8 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding Radar Cross Section (RCS): 1. RCS is always equal to the physical projected geometric area of the aircraft. 2. RCS depends heavily on the frequency of the incident radar wave and aspect angle. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is false because RCS depends on electromagnetic scattering properties and can be much larger or smaller than physical area. Statement 2 is correct.
Question 9 • numerical
A primary radar pulse travels to an aircraft and returns in $1.0 \text{ millisecond}$. What is the slant range of the aircraft? (Assume speed of light $c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$)
A 75 km
B 150 km
C 300 km
D 600 km
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Slant range $R = \frac{c \cdot t}{2} = \frac{3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s} \times 1.0 \times 10^{-3} \text{ s}}{2} = 1.5 \times 10^5 \text{ m} = 150 \text{ km}$ total path, divided by 2 gives $75 \text{ km}$ slant range.
Question 10 • conceptual
What is the primary function of a duplexer in a primary radar system architecture?
A To convert digital target tracks into analog audio signals
B To protect the sensitive receiver from high-power transmitted pulses
C To rotate the antenna at a constant angular velocity
D To modulate transponder interrogation pulses
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A duplexer acts as a high-speed electronic switch that connects the antenna to the transmitter during pulse transmission and switches it to the receiver during the listening phase.
Question 11 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Ground clutter cancellation using Moving Target Indication (MTI) is essential in primary surveillance radars. Reason (R): Stationary objects like buildings and terrain produce high-amplitude radar echoes that can mask low-flying aircraft returns.
A Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true, but R is false
D A is false, but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Stationary ground clutter generates massive reflections. MTI filters out zero-Doppler frequency shifts to isolate moving aircraft targets.
Question 12 • conceptual
Which phenomenon limits the maximum unambiguous range of a pulsed primary radar?
A Pulse repetition frequency (PRF)
B Carrier wave amplitude
C Antenna rotation rate
D Receiver noise figure
Solution & Technical Rationale:
If the PRF is too high, echoes from distant targets arrive after the transmission of the next pulse, causing range ambiguity where the radar cannot determine which pulse generated the echo.

Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
According to the radar range equation, received power is proportional to which power of antenna gain ($G$)?
A $G^1$
B $G^2$
C $G^3$
D $G^4$
Rationale:
The radar range equation contains $G^2$ because antenna gain affects both the transmitted power density and the effective aperture area during reception.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the purpose of Circular Polarization in airport primary surveillance radars?
A To increase maximum radar range in clear air
B To suppress rain and precipitation clutter echoes
C To decode Mode S transponder altitude data
D To eliminate ground multipath interference
Rationale:
Circular polarization reverses wave handedness upon reflection from spherical raindrops, allowing the receiver to reject heavy precipitation clutter while detecting aircraft.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
If an aircraft radar cross section increases by a factor of 16, what is the resulting increase in maximum detection range, assuming all other radar parameters remain constant?
A Increased by a factor of 2
B Increased by a factor of 4
C Increased by a factor of 8
D Increased by a factor of 16
Rationale:
Since $R_{\max} \propto \sigma^{1/4}$, taking the fourth root of 16 yields a range increase factor of 2.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
Which radar subsystem is responsible for generating the high-frequency carrier wave oscillations in traditional primary radars?
A Duplexer
B Magnetron or Klystron
C Planar array feed
D Moving Target Indicator filter
Rationale:
Magnetrons and klystrons are high-power vacuum tubes traditionally used as microwave oscillators and amplifiers in primary radar transmitters.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the primary operational advantage of maintaining Primary Surveillance Radar alongside Secondary Surveillance Radar at major civil aerodromes?
A Reduced electrical power consumption
B Redundancy against aircraft transponder failure or total electrical blackout
C Elimination of all atmospheric ducting effects
D Precise satellite clock synchronization
Rationale:
PSR provides independent surveillance capability, ensuring air traffic control can track aircraft even if onboard transponders fail.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
What causes second-time-around echoes in pulsed primary radar systems?
A Echoes returning from targets located beyond the maximum unambiguous range
B Multi-path reflections off airport terminal buildings
C Side-lobe leakage during antenna rotation
D Transponder reply delay errors
Rationale:
Second-time-around echoes occur when an echo from a distant target arrives after the subsequent pulse has been transmitted, leading to incorrect range calculation.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
In the radar range equation, how does electromagnetic wavelength ($\lambda$) affect received power?
A Inversely proportional to $\lambda$
B Inversely proportional to $\lambda^2$
C Directly proportional to $\lambda^2$
D Directly proportional to $\lambda^4$
Rationale:
Received power is directly proportional to $\lambda^2$ because antenna capture area $A_e$ is proportional to $\lambda^2$ for a constant gain antenna.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
Which signal processing technique utilizes the Doppler frequency shift to distinguish moving aircraft from stationary ground clutter?
A Pulse Compression
B Moving Target Indication (MTI)
C Circular Polarization
D Amplitude Modulation
Rationale:
MTI filters out stationary clutter returns with zero Doppler shift while passing frequency-shifted returns from moving aircraft.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the typical angular rotation rate for an En-Route Primary Surveillance Radar antenna?
A 15 to 20 RPM
B 4 to 6 RPM
C 60 RPM
D 0.5 RPM
Rationale:
En-route radars rotate slowly at 4 to 6 RPM to allow longer dwell time on distant targets, building strong integrated echo returns over large operational radii.
