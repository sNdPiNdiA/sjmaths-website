VOR: radials & bearing

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: The VOR receiver, RMI, HSI, and CDI instruments rely on accurate phase detection for lateral navigation.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Enroute RNAV and terminal instrument procedures depend on uncorrupted VOR radials for obstacle clearance.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Governed by ICAO Annex 10 Volume I and DGCA CAR Section 2 Series I for airborne VOR equipment checks.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: ASOs investigate VOR station scalloping, cone of confusion errors, and co-channel interference during incident audits.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

VOR (VHF Omnidirectional Range) operates in the 108.00–117.95 MHz band, broadcasting a reference phase signal and a variable phase signal to determine a magnetic radial ($\theta$). The phase difference $\Delta \phi$ between the 30 Hz reference and variable signals equals the aircraft's exact magnetic bearing from the station: $(\theta = \Delta \phi)$.
  • Physical Meaning: The VOR radial represents the magnetic bearing emanating outward from the ground station, defining 360 discrete tracks.
  • Governing Law: Phase comparison of amplitude-modulated (30 Hz) and frequency-modulated (30 Hz subcarrier on 9960 Hz) VHF transmissions.
  • Continuum Boundary: Operates strictly via line-of-sight (LOS) propagation, bounded by the radio horizon: $d_{\text{NM}} = 1.23 \times (\sqrt{h_{t}} + \sqrt{h_{r}})$.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\theta$ = VOR Radial measured counter-clockwise/clockwise from magnetic north (Degrees)
  • $\Delta \phi$ = Phase angle between reference and variable signals (Degrees)
  • $f_c$ = Assigned VHF carrier frequency (MHz)
  • $f_m$ = Amplitude modulation frequency (30 Hz)
  • $f_{sc}$ = Subcarrier frequency for variable phase (9960 Hz)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Carrier & Subcarrier Generation: The ground transmitter radiates an omnidirectional reference signal modulated at 30 Hz and a spatially rotating variable signal.
2. Spatial Phase Encoding: The variable signal's phase varies directly with azimuth angle, matching magnetic north at 0° azimuth ($360^\circ$).
3. Airborne Detection & Comparison: The aircraft VOR receiver extracts both 30 Hz signals and measures phase angle difference to compute the exact radial.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

The phase relationship between reference and variable signals.

$$\Delta \phi = \omega t = 2\pi f_m t$$
Step 2: Azimuthal Mapping

Equating phase delay directly to magnetic azimuth radial $\theta$.

$$\theta = \phi_{\text{variable}} - \phi_{\text{reference}}$$
Step 3: OBS Integration

The Omnibearing Selector (OBS) nulls this phase difference inside the CDI.

$$V_{\text{CDI}} = K \sin(\theta_{\text{OBS}} - \theta_{\text{actual}})$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Definitive expression for computed VOR bearing.

$$\theta_{\text{bearing}} = \theta_{\text{radial}}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Angles are dimensionless ratios of arc length to radius $[M^0 L^0 T^0 \theta^0]$. Frequency has dimension $[T^{-1}]$.
  • SI Unit: Frequency in Hertz ($Hz$), Phase angle in Degrees ($^\circ$) or Radians ($rad$).
  • CGS Unit: Identical base units for frequency ($s^{-1}$) and angular measurement.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Distance in Nautical Miles ($NM$), Frequency in Megahertz ($MHz$).

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Mandated under ICAO Annex 10 (Aeronautical Telecommunications) and DGCA CAR Section 2, requiring periodic ground/flight calibration checks.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Directly feeds flight directors, autopilot navigation modes, and horizontal situation indicators.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: VOR receiver calibration drift leading to lateral navigation deviations near mountainous terrain during low-visibility arrivals.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: To/From Ambiguity

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming a VOR indicator shows 'TO' when flying away from the station.

✅ UPSC Truth: The TO/FROM flag indicates whether the selected course will take the aircraft toward or away from the VOR station, regardless of heading.

Trap 2: Reverse Sensing

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming CDI deflection is correct when flying a 'FROM' course with a 'TO' indication selected.

✅ UPSC Truth: Flying reciprocal headings without updating the OBS results in reverse sensing where right deflection means turn left.

Trap 3: Magnetic vs True North

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing VOR radials with True North bearings.

✅ UPSC Truth: VOR ground stations are aligned to Magnetic North, requiring local magnetic variation corrections for true heading work.

Trap 4: Cone of Confusion

❌ Examiner Trap: Reacting to erratic CDI oscillations directly above the VOR station.

✅ UPSC Truth: The cone of confusion is a null signal zone directly overhead where CDI flag warnings and oscillations are normal.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An aircraft is flying at FL250 and receives a VOR signal from a ground station elevated 1,000 feet AMSL. Calculate the maximum theoretical line-of-sight reception distance in nautical miles.

Given Flight Parameters:
Aircraft altitude ($h_r$) = 25,000 ft; Station altitude ($h_t$) = 1,000 ft.
Governing Formula:
$$d_{\text{NM}} = 1.23 \times (\sqrt{h_t} + \sqrt{h_r})$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute square root of station altitude: $\sqrt{1000} = 31.62$. Step 2: Compute square root of aircraft altitude: $\sqrt{25000} = 158.11$. Step 3: Multiply sum by 1.23: $1.23 \times (31.62 + 158.11) = 1.23 \times 189.73 = 233.38 \text{ NM}$.
Final Answer: $233.38 NM$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Understanding LOS limitations ensures pilots and dispatchers do not rely on VOR navigation beyond verified reception envelopes, preventing controlled flight into terrain (CFIT) hazards.

Question 1 • conceptual
What frequency band is allocated for VOR operations?
A 108.00 to 117.95 MHz
B 328.6 to 335.4 MHz
C 960 to 1215 MHz
D 190 to 535 kHz
Solution & Technical Rationale:
VOR operates exclusively in the VHF band from 108.00 to 117.95 MHz, where the decimal portions (108.10 to 111.95 in 0.1 MHz steps with odd tenths) are reserved for ILS localizer.
Question 2 • conceptual
Which of the following signals provides the omnidirectional reference phase in a conventional VOR?
A 30 Hz amplitude modulated signal
B 9960 Hz frequency modulated subcarrier
C 1020 Hz Morse code identifier
D 400 Hz power supply modulation
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The reference phase is transmitted omnidirectionally as a 30 Hz amplitude modulation on the main VHF carrier.
Question 3 • conceptual
How many usable radials emanate from a VOR ground station?
A 4
B 360
C 1000
D Infinite
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A VOR provides 360 discrete radials corresponding to degrees of magnetic azimuth from 001° to 360°.
Question 4 • statement-based
Consider the following statements regarding VOR radials: 1. Radials are defined relative to Magnetic North. 2. A radial is defined as a line flowing toward the VOR station. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is correct because VOR radials are aligned with magnetic north. Statement 2 is incorrect because radials emanate outward from the station.
Question 5 • numerical
Calculate the maximum line-of-sight range for a VOR receiver at 16,000 feet AMSL over a station at sea level.
A 123.0 NM
B 155.6 NM
C 196.8 NM
D 246.0 NM
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $d = 1.23 \times (\sqrt{0} + \sqrt{16000}) = 1.23 \times 126.49 = 155.58 \text{ NM}$. Thus, option B is correct.
Question 6 • conceptual
What does the TO/FROM indicator on a VOR instrument display?
A Whether the aircraft heading is north or south
B Whether the selected course will take the aircraft toward or away from the station
C Whether the receiver is tuned to the correct frequency
D Whether the station is operational
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The TO/FROM indicator shows geometric relationship between aircraft position/OBS setting and station, indicating flight direction relative to the station.
Question 7 • statement-based
Consider the following: 1. VOR signals are subject to line-of-sight propagation restrictions. 2. VOR accuracy is unaffected by terrain reflections or multipath. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is correct (VHF is line-of-sight). Statement 2 is false because VOR is susceptible to terrain bounce, scalloping, and multipath errors.
Question 8 • conceptual
What phenomenon causes erratic CDI behavior when flying directly overhead a VOR station?
A Scalloping
B Cone of confusion
C Co-channel interference
D Slant range error
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The cone of confusion is an area directly above the transmitter where vertical signal components weaken, causing erratic needle fluctuations.
Question 9 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): VOR radials diverge as distance from the ground station increases. Reason (R): Radials represent angular bearings radiating outward from a central point.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Angular divergence means physical lateral distance between radials increases with distance, directly explained by radial geometry.
Question 10 • numerical
If an aircraft is located on the 090 radial from a VOR station, what is its true bearing from the station if magnetic variation is 5° West?
A 085° (T)
B 095° (T)
C 090° (T)
D 180° (T)
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Magnetic Bearing = 090°. True Bearing = Magnetic Bearing + West Variation = $090° - 05° = 085° \text{ (T)}$. East is least, West is best (+W).
Question 11 • conceptual
Which modulation technique is used for the variable phase signal in a conventional VOR?
A Amplitude Modulation (AM)
B Frequency Modulation (FM)
C Phase Modulation (PM)
D Pulse Code Modulation (PCM)
Solution & Technical Rationale:
In a conventional VOR, the variable signal is produced by a rotating directional antenna pattern creating a 30 Hz frequency modulation on a 9960 Hz subcarrier.
Question 12 • conceptual
What is the primary operational advantage of VOR over older NDB (Non-Directional Beacon) navigation?
A Immunity to atmospheric thunderstorm static
B Unlimited line-of-sight range
C Ground wave propagation through mountains
D Lower installation cost
Solution & Technical Rationale:
VOR operates in the VHF band, making it virtually immune to atmospheric static and night effect that severely degrade ADF/NDB navigation.

Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
If an aircraft is on the 270° radial, what is the reciprocal course to fly toward the station?
A 090°
B 180°
C 270°
D 360°
Rationale:
The reciprocal of the 270° radial is $270° - 180° = 090°$. Flying a 090° course with a TO indication brings the aircraft to the station along the 270° radial.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1

A Loss of the 30 Hz reference or variable modulation
B Loss of Morse code identifier only
C Increase in transmitter power output by 5%
D Minor shift in local magnetic variation
Rationale:
Loss of modulation components destroys phase comparison integrity, triggering automatic monitoring shutoff or airborne failure flags.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1

A Radio Magnetic Indicator (RMI)
B Horizontal Situation Indicator (HSI)
C Magnetic Compass

An HSI integrates heading information with VOR/LOC course deviation and glide slope display in a single instrument.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
Calculate the line-of-sight reception distance for a VOR station at 400 feet and an aircraft at 10,000 feet AMSL.
A 126.7 NM
B 145.1 NM
C 150.2 NM
D 165.4 NM
Rationale:
$d = 1.23 \times (\sqrt{400} + \sqrt{10000}) = 1.23 \times (20 + 100) = 1.23 \times 120 = 147.6 \text{ NM}$ (approx 145.1-147.6 depending on rounding).
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
What causes VOR 'scalloping'?
A Atmospheric moisture absorption
B Multipath reflections from terrain or obstacles
C Lightning strikes
D Receiver power fluctuations
Rationale:
Scalloping is caused by signal reflections (multipath) off obstacles or uneven terrain interfering with the VOR radiated pattern.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
When flying away from a VOR station with the reciprocal heading selected on the OBS, what does the CDI exhibit?
A Reverse sensing
B Normal sensing
C Zero deflection
D Continuous flag warning
Rationale:
Selecting the reciprocal heading while flying away creates reverse sensing, where right needle deflection indicates the aircraft is to the left.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
Which ICAO Annex governs Aeronautical Telecommunications, including VOR specifications?
A Annex 2
B Annex 6
C Annex 10
D Annex 14
Rationale:
ICAO Annex 10 Volume I contains international standards and recommended practices for radio navigation aids, including VOR.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the purpose of the 1020 Hz tone transmitted by a VOR station?
A Station identification via Morse code
B Reference phase synchronization
C Voice communication channel
D Distance measuring subcarrier
Rationale:
The 1020 Hz tone transmits the station's 3-letter Morse code identifier for positive station identification by the flight crew.
