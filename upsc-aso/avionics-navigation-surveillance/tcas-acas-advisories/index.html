TCAS/ACAS: TA/RA

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: TCAS integrates with Mode S transponders, radio altimeters, directional antennas, and Primary Flight Displays (PFD/ND) for RA/TA visual and audio cues.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Dispatch requires fully operational dual TCAS II systems under MEL for RVSM airspace and commercial transport operations.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Governed by DGCA CAR Series 'S' Part III and ICAO Annex 10 Volume IV, mandating TCAS II Version 7.1 compliance.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: ASO evaluates FDR/CVR data and TCAS RA downlink logs to analyze separation losses, coordination failures, and pilot response compliance.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Traffic Alert and Collision Avoidance System (TCAS II) is an airborne collision avoidance system operating independently of ground-based air traffic control, mandated by ICAO Annex 10 and DGCA CAR to prevent mid-air collisions.
  • Physical Meaning: TCAS II interrogates Mode S and Mode C transponders of nearby intruder aircraft, computing relative range, altitude, and bearing to predict collision trajectories.
  • Governing Law: Time to Closest Approach (TCA) and Modified Tau logic define the alerting thresholds based on relative closing velocity ($v_c = -\dot{r}$).
  • Continuum Boundary: Operates within line-of-sight RF propagation boundaries at $1030\text{ MHz}$ (interrogation) and $1090\text{ MHz}$ (reply).

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $r$ = Relative slant range between ownship and intruder aircraft (NM or meters)
  • $\dot{r}$ = Range rate or radial closing velocity ($m/s$ or $knots$)
  • $\tau$ = Tau, defined as $-r / \dot{r}$ for converging traffic (seconds)
  • $DALT$ = Relative altitude difference derived from barometric altitude reporting (feet)
  • $TA$ = Traffic Advisory, providing positional awareness without vertical maneuvers
  • $RA$ = Resolution Advisory, issuing vertical climb or descent speed commands

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. RF Interrogation & Tracking: The ownship TCAS transmitter radiates $1030\text{ MHz}$ interrogations containing Mode S framing, prompting all surrounding transponders to reply on $1090\text{ MHz}$.
2. Kinematic State Estimation: The TCAS computer processes reply pulse trains to extract altitude, slant range, and bearing, tracking intruder trajectories via Kalman filtering algorithms.
3. Threat Evaluation & Tau Computation: The system calculates Tau ($\tau = -r / \dot{r}$) and vertical separation to classify traffic into Other Traffic, Proximate Traffic, Traffic Advisory, and Resolution Advisory.
4. Resolution Advisory Coordination: TCAS II establishes a synchronous air-to-air data link with the intruder TCAS to negotiate and execute complementary vertical resolution advisories (e.g., Climb vs. Descend).

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Slant range $r$ and closing speed $\dot{r}$ define the time-to-go parameter Tau ($\tau$).

$$\tau = -\frac{r}{\dot{r}}$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Alert thresholds vary dynamically based on ownship altitude and altitude layer boundaries.

$$\tau_{\text{threshold}} = \text{constant for altitude block } k$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Modified Tau logic incorporates vertical separation rate to prevent false alarms during level-offs.

$$\tau_{\text{mod}} = \max\left(\tau, \frac{|h - h_i| - \text{AL}}{|\dot{h} - \dot{h}_i|}\right)$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

An RA is triggered when Modified Tau falls below the critical limit for the current airspace.

$$\tau_{\text{mod}} \le \tau_{\text{RA limit}} \quad \text{and} \quad |DALT| < \text{AL}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Range has dimension $[L]$, closing velocity has $[L T^{-1}]$, and Tau has dimension $[T]$.
  • SI Unit: Range is measured in meters ($m$), closing speed in meters per second ($m/s$), and Tau in seconds ($s$).
  • CGS Unit: Centimeters and seconds are rarely used; aviation exclusively uses feet, nautical miles, and knots.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Slant range in Nautical Miles (NM), altitude in feet ($ft$), closing speed in knots ($kts$), frequency in Megahertz ($MHz$).

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'S', Part III and ICAO Annex 10 Volume IV mandate TCAS II installation for aircraft with MTOW > 5,700 kg or seating > 19 pax.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: TCAS interfaces with flight guidance computers, stall warning systems, autopilot disconnect logic, and audio warning generators.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: Überlingen mid-air collision (Bashkirian Airlines Flight 2937 vs. DHL Flight 611): Failure mode involved contradictory ATC instructions and TCAS RA; root safety cause established absolute priority of TCAS RA over ATC.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: ATC vs. TCAS Authority

❌ Examiner Trap: Pilots must obey Air Traffic Control instructions over a TCAS Resolution Advisory if they conflict.

✅ UPSC Truth: TCAS RA commands override all ATC instructions; pilots must immediately maneuver in accordance with the RA.

Trap 2: TA vs. RA Maneuvering

❌ Examiner Tag: Pilots should initiate aggressive evasive maneuvers upon receiving a Traffic Advisory (TA).

✅ UPSC Truth: A TA provides visual and auditory awareness only; visual search is required, and vertical maneuvers are strictly prohibited.

Trap 3: Version 6 vs Version 7.1 Logic

❌ Examiner Trap: TCAS Version 6 and Version 7 handle 'Adjust Vertical Speed' RAs identically.

✅ UPSC Truth: Version 7.1 introduced safety enhancements including safety-critical reversals for 'Adjust Vertical Speed' RAs when intruders fail to respond.

Trap 4: Mode C vs Mode S Altitude Reporting

❌ Examiner Trap: TCAS requires radio altimeter data to calculate the relative altitude of intruder aircraft.

✅ UPSC Truth: Intruder altitude is derived from barometric altitude encoders transmitted via Mode C or Mode S replies, while ownship radio altimeters inhibit RAs near the ground.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An airliner cruising at FL350 detects an intruder aircraft at a slant range of $12\text{ NM}$ on a direct collision course with a closing speed of $600\text{ knots}$. Calculate the time-to-go parameter Tau ($\tau$) and determine whether a Traffic Advisory (TA) is triggered based on high-altitude thresholds.

Given Flight Parameters:
Slant range $r = 12\text{ NM} = 72,913\text{ feet}$ ($22,224\text{ meters}$); Closing speed $\dot{r} = -600\text{ knots} = -308.67\text{ m/s}$; Altitude = FL350.
Governing Formula:
$$\tau = -\frac{r}{\dot{r}}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Convert slant range into meters and closing speed into meters per second. $$r = 12 \times 1852 = 22,224\text{ m}$$ $$\dot{r} = -600 \times 0.51444 = -308.66\text{ m/s}$$ Step 2: Substitute values into the Tau equation. $$\tau = -\frac{22224}{-308.66} = 71.99\text{ seconds}$$ Step 3: Compare computed Tau with high-altitude TA threshold (48 seconds). $$\tau (71.99\text{ s}) > 48\text{ s}$$
Final Answer: Tau equals $72.0\text{ seconds}$; no TA or RA is triggered yet as the time horizon exceeds the warning threshold.
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Understanding Tau calculation allows safety officers to audit TCAS sensitivity levels and verify that alerting logic triggers within certified temporal boundaries during flight data playback.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary RF interrogation frequency utilized by TCAS II to query surrounding aircraft transponders?
A 1030 MHz
B 1090 MHz
C 121.5 MHz
D 978 MHz
Solution & Technical Rationale:
TCAS interrogators transmit on 1030 MHz, while transponders reply on 1090 MHz. 121.5 MHz is the international emergency VHF frequency, and 978 MHz is used for UAT ADS-B.
Question 2 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding TCAS Resolution Advisories (RAs): 1. Pilots must immediately comply with RAs even if they contradict Air Traffic Control instructions. 2. RAs provide horizontal steering commands in addition to vertical speed limits. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is correct because TCAS RA authority supersedes ATC clearances. Statement 2 is incorrect because TCAS II provides vertical RAs only, not horizontal steering.
Question 3 • numerical
An aircraft detects an intruder at a slant range of 9,260 meters with a closing radial speed of 308.6 meters per second. What is the computed Tau ($\tau$)?
A 15 seconds
B 20 seconds
C 30 seconds
D 45 seconds
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $\tau = -r / \dot{r}$, Tau = $9260 / 308.6 = 30\text{ seconds}$.
Question 4 • assertion_reason
Assertion (A): TCAS II inhibits Resolution Advisories below 1,000 feet AGL. Reason (R): Low-altitude RA inhibitions prevent unnecessary terrain avoidance conflicts and premature descent commands close to touchdown.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
C A is true but R is false.
D A is false but R is true.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
TCAS II automatically inhibits RAs below 1,000 ft AGL and converts existing RAs to TAs below 400 ft AGL to prevent unsafe maneuvers near the runway.
Question 5 • conceptual
What is the primary safety enhancement introduced in TCAS II Version 7.1 compared to Version 7.0?
A Inclusion of horizontal resolution maneuvers
B Reversal of sense for 'Adjust Vertical Speed' RAs when the intruder fails to comply
C Integration of GPS satellite augmentation for tracking
D Reduction of interrogation frequency to 978 MHz
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Version 7.1 introduced automatic resolution advisory reversals when an intruder aircraft does not follow the coordinated RA, specifically addressing 'Adjust Vertical Speed' commands.
Question 6 • conceptual
How does TCAS II coordinate complementary resolution advisories between two conflicting aircraft?
A Via VHF radio voice broadcast
B Through ground-based radar relay stations
C Using air-to-air Mode S selective interrogations and data links
D By matching barometric altimeter settings automatically
Solution & Technical Rationale:
TCAS II units negotiate resolution advisories via air-to-air Mode S data links, ensuring one aircraft climbs while the other descends.
Question 7 • statement
Which of the following traffic categories require a mandatory pilot response resulting in vertical speed modifications?
A Other Traffic
B Proximate Traffic
C Traffic Advisory (TA)
D Resolution Advisory (RA)
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Only a Resolution Advisory (RA) requires immediate pilot response to alter vertical speed and achieve required separation.
Question 8 • numerical
An aircraft at FL300 has a TA threshold of 45 seconds. If an intruder is closing at 400 knots ($205.8\text{ m/s}$), at what slant range will the TA trigger?
A 4,500 meters
B 9,260 meters
C 15,000 meters
D 22,000 meters
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Slant range $r = \tau \times \dot{r} = 45\text{ s} \times 205.8\text{ m/s} = 9261\text{ meters}$ (approx 5 NM).
Question 9 • assertion_reason
Assertion (A): Pilots sighting traffic visually that has triggered a TCAS RA must still follow the RA maneuver. Reason (R): Visual meteorological conditions do not guarantee accurate judgment of closing rates and collision trajectories at high closing speeds.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
C A is true but R is false.
D A is false but R is true.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Visual acquisition is notoriously unreliable for determining true collision geometry, so TCAS RA compliance remains mandatory regardless of visual contact.
Question 10 • conceptual
What happens to Resolution Advisories when an aircraft descends below 400 feet AGL?
A They become more sensitive
B They are completely inhibited and downgraded to TA or cleared
C They switch to horizontal guidance mode
D They trigger emergency squawk 7700 automatically
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Below 400 feet AGL, RAs are inhibited, and existing RAs are cleared to prevent unsafe descent commands during landing flare.
Question 11 • conceptual
Which DGCA CAR section governs the mandatory carriage and operational requirements of airborne collision avoidance systems?
A CAR Section 2, Series 'S', Part III
B CAR Section 4, Series 'B', Part I
C CAR Section 8, Series 'O', Part II
D CAR Section 3, Series 'C', Part V
Solution & Technical Rationale:
DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'S', Part III prescribes the airworthiness and operational requirements for TCAS installation in Indian registered aircraft.
Question 12 • statement
Consider the following about TCAS surveillance range: 1. Standard TCAS II active interrogation range extends up to 14 nautical miles. 2. TCAS tracks up to 30 intruder aircraft simultaneously. Which statements are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct; standard active surveillance range is approximately 14 NM, and the processor tracks up to 30 surrounding targets simultaneously.

A Below 1,000 ft AGL
B FL050 to FL100
C FL100 to FL200
D Above FL200

Above FL200, closing speeds are higher, so RA Tau thresholds increase to 35 seconds to provide adequate maneuver reaction time.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the consequence of an intruder aircraft having an unserviceable or turned-off transponder when operating near a TCAS-equipped aircraft?
A TCAS will trigger a resolution advisory
B TCAS active surveillance will fail to detect the intruder
C TCAS will switch to passive ADS-B broadcast mode automatically
D ATC will automatically vector the aircraft
Rationale:
Active TCAS relies on transponder replies; un-transpondered targets cannot be actively interrogated or tracked by TCAS.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
Why does TCAS II use Modified Tau instead of pure Tau during level-off maneuvers?
A To account for ground reflection errors
B To prevent nuisance TAs and RAs when aircraft are leveling off with high vertical speed
C To compensate for wind shear effects
D To synchronize with GPS altitude data
Rationale:
Modified Tau logic evaluates altitude separation trends, preventing false alarms when an aircraft is rapidly leveling off near another's altitude.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1

A MTOW > 2,700 kg or seating > 6 pax
B MTOW > 5,700 kg or seating > 19 pax
C MTOW > 15,000 kg or seating > 50 pax
D MTOW > 45,000 kg or seating > 150 pax
Rationale:
ICAO and DGCA mandate TCAS II for turbine-powered aircraft with a maximum takeoff mass exceeding 5,700 kg or authorized seating capacity exceeding 19 passengers.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
If an air traffic controller issues a climb clearance that conflicts with an active TCAS Resolution Advisory 'DESCEND', what must the pilot do?
A Follow the ATC clearance because ATC has radar separation responsibility
B Follow the TCAS 'DESCEND' RA and notify ATC as soon as practicable
C Maintain level flight until the controller clarifies the instruction
D Execute a 360-degree turn
Rationale:
The pilot must follow the TCAS RA ('DESCEND') and report the deviation to ATC as soon as workload permits.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
What visual indication is displayed on the Navigation Display (ND) for a Traffic Advisory (TA)?
A Solid red square
B Solid amber/yellow circle
C Cyan hollow diamond
D White cross
Rationale:
TAs are displayed as solid amber circles, while RAs are displayed as solid red squares, and other traffic as open cyan diamonds.

High traffic density increases interrogation rates, leading to RF congestion, reply overlap, and synchronous garble.
