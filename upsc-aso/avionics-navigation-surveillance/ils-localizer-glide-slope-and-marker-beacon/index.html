ILS: localizer, glide slope & marker beacon

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: The ILS receiver interfaces directly with the Electronic Flight Instrument System (EFIS), autopilot, and flight directors to execute coupled approaches.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Aircraft dispatch under low-visibility conditions (CAT II/III) requires dual operable ILS receivers, flight directors, and autoland certification.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 8 and ICAO Annex 10 dictate ground facility classification, flight inspection periodicities, and airborne equipment tolerances.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: During runway excursion or undershoot investigations, Flight Data Recorders (FDR) capture ILS deviation parameters to isolate avionics faults from pilot error.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

The Instrument Landing System (ILS) is a precision ground-based radio navigation system providing aircraft with horizontal, vertical, and distance guidance during approach and landing: $DDM = |S_1 - S_2|$.
  • Physical Meaning: ILS uses radio frequency beams to create a spatial glide path and localizer course intersecting at the runway threshold.
  • Governing Law: Amplitude Modulation (AM) and phase comparison of 90 Hz and 150 Hz tone signals create spatial lobes of differing modulation depths.
  • Continuum Boundary: Signals operate in the VHF/UHF frequency bands, subject to line-of-sight propagation, terrain reflection, and aircraft scattering.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $DDM$ = Difference in Depth of Modulation (Dimensionless)
  • $SDM$ = Sum of Depth of Modulation (Dimensionless)
  • $f_{loc}$ = Localizer carrier frequency in VHF band (108.10 MHz - 111.95 MHz) (MHz)
  • $f_{gs}$ = Glide slope carrier frequency in UHF band (329.15 MHz - 335.00 MHz) (MHz)
  • $OM$ = Outer Marker beacon carrier frequency (75 MHz) (MHz)
  • $MM$ = Middle Marker beacon carrier frequency (75 MHz) (MHz)
  • $IM$ = Inner Marker beacon carrier frequency (75 MHz) (MHz)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Localizer Azimuth Guidance: Two distinct carrier frequencies modulated by 90 Hz and 150 Hz tones are transmitted off-set from the runway centerline, creating an equi-signal line where $DDM = 0$.
2. Glide Slope Elevation Guidance: Reflected and direct UHF signals form interference patterns in the vertical plane, generating a precise electronic descent path typically at $3^{\circ}$ above the horizontal.
3. Marker Beacon Position Fixes: Fan-shaped radiation patterns at 75 MHz provide vertical cross-checks along the approach path, broadcasting specific audio tones and blinking cockpit indicator lights.
4. Avionics Processing: Airborne receivers demodulate the signals, compute $DDM$, and drive course deviation indicators (CDI) or flight director cue bars to steer the aircraft.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Modulation Depth Relation

The composite RF signal containing 90 Hz and 150 Hz tones is expressed mathematically as:

$$e(t) = E_c [1 + m_1 \cos(\omega_1 t) + m_2 \cos(\omega_2 t)] \cos(\omega_c t)$$
Step 2: Difference in Depth of Modulation

The guidance error is proportional to the difference between the modulation depths of the two tones:

$$DDM = m_{90} - m_{150}$$
Step 3: Sum of Depth of Modulation

The total modulation index sum ensures transmitter power allocation remains constant:

$$SDM = m_{90} + m_{150} = 0.40$$
Step 4: Final CDI Deflection Law

The instrumentation deflection current is directly proportional to the normalized DDM ratio:

$$I = k \left( \frac{m_{90} - m_{150}}{m_{90} + m_{150}} \right)$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Modulation depth is dimensionless $[M^0 L^0 T^0]$, while carrier frequencies have the dimension of inverse time $[T^{-1}]$.
  • SI Unit: Frequency is measured in Hertz ($Hz$), and field strength is measured in Volts per meter ($V/m$).
  • CGS Unit: Electromagnetic fields use statvolts per centimeter ($statV/cm$) for localized theoretical formulations.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Frequencies are universally expressed in Megahertz ($MHz$), and distance to touchdown is measured in nautical miles ($NM$) or feet ($ft$).

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Governed by ICAO Annex 10 Volume I (Radio Navigation Aids) and DGCA CAR Section 3 Series C, mandating stringent ground and flight calibration standards.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Integrity relies on fault-tolerant multi-channel receivers, antenna placement free from structural shadowing, and uninterrupted DC/AC bus power.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: In historical low-visibility accidents, localizer false glideslope captures (backlobe reception) led to Controlled Flight Into Terrain (CFIT), prompting mandatory dual-receiver cross-checking mandates.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Localizer Frequency Confusion

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming localizer frequencies overlap randomly with commercial FM broadcast bands.

✅ UPSC Truth: Localizer operates strictly on odd tenths within the 108.10 MHz to 111.95 MHz band, whereas even tenths are reserved for VOR.

Trap 2: Modulation Tone Inversion

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing the 150 Hz tone dominates on the left of the localizer course and 90 Hz on the right.

✅ UPSC Truth: The 90 Hz tone is modulated on the left (port) side and the 150 Hz tone on the right (starboard) side (remember: 90 left, 150 right).

Trap 3: Glide Slope Frequency Pairing

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming UHF glide slope frequencies are tuned independently on the navigation radio control panel.

✅ UPSC Truth: UHF glide slope frequencies are automatically paired and tuned when the corresponding VHF localizer frequency is selected.

Trap 4: Marker Beacon Modulation

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing the Outer Marker audio modulation frequency with the Middle Marker.

✅ UPSC Truth: Outer Marker transmits 400 Hz keyed at 2 dashes per second, Middle Marker transmits 1300 Hz keyed alternately, and Inner Marker transmits 3000 Hz keyed at 6 dots per second.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An aircraft is executing an ILS approach where the localizer receiver measures a 90 Hz modulation depth of 22% and a 150 Hz modulation depth of 14%. Calculate the Difference in Depth of Modulation (DDM) and determine the angular displacement if the localizer course sensitivity factor is $k = 0.0015$ per microampere with a total course width equivalent to $DDM = 0.200$.

Given Flight Parameters:
m_90 = 0.22, m_150 = 0.14, SDM = m_90 + m_150
Governing Formula:
$$DDM = m_{90} - m_{150}; \quad \text{Percent DDM} = \frac{m_{90} - m_{150}}{m_{90} + m_{150}} \times 100$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute DDM by subtracting the modulation depths: DDM = 0.22 - 0.14 = 0.080. Step 2: Compute SDM to verify modulation limits: SDM = 0.22 + 0.14 = 0.360 (within standard 40% limits). Step 3: Calculate the relative displacement ratio: Ratio = 0.080 / 0.360 = 0.222 indicating aircraft displacement to the left of centerline.
Final Answer: $DDM = 0.080 (Aircraft is deflected to the left of the runway centerline).$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Monitoring DDM values allows air safety officers and flight inspectors to verify antenna alignment and receiver sensitivity during routine ILS flight checks.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary frequency band allocated for ILS Localizer transmissions?
A 108.10 MHz to 111.95 MHz
B 329.15 MHz to 335.00 MHz
C 74.60 MHz to 75.40 MHz
D 328 MHz to 336 kHz
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Localizer operates in the VHF band from 108.10 to 111.95 MHz on odd tenth decimal frequencies.
Question 2 • conceptual
Which audio tone and modulation pattern identifies the Outer Marker (OM) beacon?
A 1300 Hz tone keyed as alternating dots and dashes
B 400 Hz tone keyed as continuous dashes
C 3000 Hz tone keyed as rapid dots
D 1020 Hz tone keyed as Morse code IDENT
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The Outer Marker transmits a 400 Hz audio tone modulated at 95% and keyed with continuous dashes (2 dashes per second).
Question 3 • statement-based
Consider the following statements regarding ILS Localizer signals: 1. The 90 Hz tone is modulated on the left side of the localizer course. 2. The 150 Hz tone is modulated on the right side of the localizer course. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. The 90 Hz tone is transmitted predominantly to the left of the centerline and the 150 Hz tone to the right.
Question 4 • numerical
If an aircraft localizer receiver detects $m_{90} = 0.25$ and $m_{150} = 0.15$, what is the computed DDM?
A 0.10
B 0.40
C 0.0375
D 1.66
Solution & Technical Rationale:
DDM = m_90 - m_150 = 0.25 - 0.15 = 0.10.
Question 5 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): UHF glide slope frequencies are tuned independently on modern digital flight management systems. Reason (R): Glide slope receivers automatically pair with the selected VHF localizer frequency via channel pairing logic.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Assertion is false because pilots do not tune GS frequencies independently; Reason is true because localizer-glide slope channel pairing automates this.

B Middle Marker

Solution & Technical Rationale:
The Middle Marker (MM) is located approximately 3,500 feet (1,050 meters) from the threshold, signaling Category I decision height.

SDM = m_90 + m_150 = 0.18 + 0.12 = 0.30.

Inner Marker illuminates a white cockpit light and sounds a 3000 Hz tone keyed at 6 dots per second.
