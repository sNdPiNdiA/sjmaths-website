SSR & transponder Modes A/C/S

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Cockpit control units, encoding altimeters, and Mode S transponder transceivers interface directly with air data computers.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Fully functional SSR and transponders are Go/No-Go items under MEL for RVSM and controlled airspace operations.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Governed by DGCA CAR Series 'S' Part VII and ICAO Annex 10 Volume IV surveillance standards.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: ASO auditors examine transponder operational logs, radar tracks, and Mode C altitude reporting accuracy during near-miss investigations.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Secondary Surveillance Radar (SSR) operates on active interrogation ($1030\text{ MHz}$) and reply ($1090\text{ MHz}$) frequencies to interrogate aircraft transponders and process identity, altitude, and extended squitter data.
  • Physical Meaning: Ground interrogators transmit coded pulses that airborne transponders decode and reply to, eliminating reliance on skin paint reflections.
  • Governing Law: Interrogations use discrete or all-call protocols governed by ICAO Annex 10 standards for surveillance integrity.
  • Continuum Boundary: RF line-of-sight propagation limits SSR range based on Earth curvature and transponder output power ($P_t$).

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $f_{int}$ = Interrogation frequency, $1030\text{ MHz}$
  • $f_{rep}$ = Reply frequency, $1090\text{ MHz}$
  • $P_t$ = Transponder peak pulse output power (Watts)
  • $PW$ = Pulse width (microseconds)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Ground Interrogation Emission: The ground interrogator emits a synchronized trio of pulses ($P_1, P_2, P_3$) via a directional antenna to illuminate the target sector.
2. Sidelobe Suppression Check: The directional $P_1/P_3$ and omnidirectional $P_2$ pulses are compared by the transponder to reject sidelobe replies and prevent ghost targets.
3. Transponder Pulse Decoding & Reply: The transponder decodes the pulse spacing (Mode A: $8\,\mu\text{s}$, Mode C: $21\,\mu\text{s}$) and constructs a framed reply pulse train.
4. Ground Processing & Extraction: The ground station receiver decodes the pulse positions to extract 12-bit identity codes or pressure altitude data.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Radar Link Budget Equation

Defines the power received at the ground station from the transponder.

$$P_r = \frac{P_t G_t G_r \lambda^2 \sigma}{(4\pi)^3 R^4}$$
Step 2: Beacon Transponder Link Equation

Modified for one-way active transponder replies ($R^2$ loss instead of $R^4$).

$$P_{rx} = \frac{P_{tx} G_{tx} G_{rx} \lambda^2}{(4\pi)^2 R^2 L_s}$$
Step 3: Slant Range Calculation

Relates round-trip propagation time to physical distance.

$$R = \frac{c \cdot (t_{arrival} - t_{interrogation} - t_{reply})}{2}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Computes maximum operational line-of-sight range.

$$R_{max} = 4.12 \left(\sqrt{h_t} + \sqrt{h_r}\right)$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Frequency $[T^{-1}]$, Power $[M L^2 T^{-3}]$, Distance $[L]$.
  • SI Unit: Frequency in Hertz ($Hz$), Power in Watts ($W$), Distance in meters ($m$).
  • CGS Unit: CGS frequency units are $s^{-1}$, power in ergs per second ($erg/s$).
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Slant range in Nautical Miles ($NM$), altitude in feet ($ft$), frequency in Megahertz ($MHz$).

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2, Series I, Part V mandates standard airborne transponder equipment and altitude reporting.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Integrates with Air Data Computers (ADC), Traffic Collision Avoidance Systems (TCAS II), and Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS).
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: Mid-air collisions and level busts often involve transponder failure, incorrect altimeter subscale settings, or Mode C altitude truncation errors.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Mode A vs Mode C Pulse Spacing

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming Mode A and Mode C share identical pulse spacing intervals.

✅ UPSC Truth: Mode A uses an $8\,\mu\text{s}$ pulse spacing ($P_1$ to $P_3$), while Mode C uses a $21\,\mu\text{s}$ spacing for altitude telemetry.

Trap 2: Interrogation vs Reply Frequencies

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing SSR operates on a single transceiver frequency for both transmission and reply.

✅ UPSC Truth: SSR uses a paired frequency architecture: $1030\text{ MHz}$ uplink for ground interrogation and $1090\text{ MHz}$ downlink for airborne reply.

Trap 3: Mode S All-Call vs Roll-Call

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming Mode S transponders cannot respond to legacy Mode A/C interrogations.

✅ UPSC Truth: Mode S transponders feature hybrid surveillance capability, responding to legacy Mode A/C and selective Mode S interrogations.

Trap 4: Pressure Altitude vs Geometric Altitude

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing Mode C pressure altitude output with true height above ground level ($AGL$).

✅ UPSC Truth: Mode C transmits pressure altitude referenced strictly to the standard ISA datum ($1013.25\text{ hPa}$), regardless of local altimeter barometric settings.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An SSR ground station receives a transponder reply pulse train. Calculate the slant range of the aircraft if the total round-trip propagation time delay after subtracting internal transponder processing delay ($3\,\mu\text{s}$) is $423\,\mu\text{s}$.

Given Flight Parameters:
Round-trip propagation time $t_{prop} = 423\,\mu\text{s}$, Speed of light $c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$.
Governing Formula:
$R = \frac{c \cdot t_{prop}}{2}$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Substitute propagation time into range formula: $R = \frac{3 \times 10^8 \times 423 \times 10^{-6}}{2}$. Step 2: Multiply numerator terms: $126900 / 2$. Step 3: Compute final slant range: $63450\text{ meters}$ or $34.26\text{ NM}$.
Final Answer: $63.45\text{ km}$ ($34.26\text{ Nautical Miles}$)
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate round-trip timing measurement is vital for preventing radar position errors and ensuring precise separation standards in high-density airspace.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary frequency assigned for ground-to-air SSR interrogations?
A 1030 MHz
B 1090 MHz
C 121.5 MHz
D 978 MHz
Solution & Technical Rationale:
SSR interrogations are universally transmitted on 1030 MHz, while transponder replies are transmitted on 1090 MHz.
Question 2 • conceptual
Which transponder mode provides 4096 discrete identification codes?
A Mode A
B Mode C
C Mode S
D Mode B
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Mode A utilizes a 12-pulse framing system yielding $2^{12} = 4096$ distinct identity codes.
Question 3 • conceptual
What is the standard pulse separation for Mode C altitude interrogations?
A 8 microseconds
B 21 microseconds
C 25 microseconds
D 50 microseconds
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Mode C uses a 21-microsecond pulse spacing between P1 and P3 to trigger altitude telemetry transmission.
Question 4 • conceptual
Mode S transponders utilize which modulation technique for data transmission?
A Frequency Modulation (FM)
B Phase Position Modulation (PPM)
C Amplitude Shift Keying (ASK)
D Quadrature Phase Shift Keying (QPSK)
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Mode S employs Phase Position Modulation (PPM) at 1090 MHz for data exchange and selective addressing.
Question 5 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding SSR: 1. Mode C altitude reporting is referenced to local QNH. 2. Sidelobe suppression prevents ghost targets. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is false because Mode C is always referenced to standard 1013.25 hPa. Statement 2 is true; sidelobe suppression prevents false target bearings.
Question 6 • numerical
If a radar round-trip time delay is 200 microseconds, what is the approximate slant range in nautical miles?
A 16.2 NM
B 32.4 NM
C 54.0 NM
D 108.0 NM
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Range $R = (c \cdot t)/2 = (3 \times 10^8 \times 200 \times 10^{-6})/2 = 30,000$ meters = 16.2 NM.
Question 7 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Mode S allows selective interrogation of specific aircraft. Reason (R): Every Mode S transponder is assigned a unique 24-bit ICAO aircraft address.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Unique 24-bit ICAO addresses enable ground stations to selectively interrogate individual aircraft, eliminating general broadcast clutter.
Question 8 • conceptual
What is the function of the P2 pulse in SSR sidelobe suppression (ISLS)?
A To transmit altitude
B To identify aircraft ID
C To establish a reference amplitude threshold for sidelobe discrimination
D To switch transponder off
Solution & Technical Rationale:
P2 is transmitted omnidirectionally; if P2 amplitude exceeds P1, the transponder recognizes it is in a sidelobe and suppresses reply.
Question 9 • conceptual
Which ICAO Annex governs Aeronautical Telecommunications, including SSR and Secondary Surveillance systems?
A Annex 2
B Annex 6
C Annex 10
D Annex 14
Solution & Technical Rationale:
ICAO Annex 10 (Volume IV) sets international standards and recommended practices for radar and surveillance systems.
Question 10 • statement
Consider the following about Mode S Extended Squitter: 1. Operates on 1090 MHz. 2. Requires ground interrogation to transmit position. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is true. Statement 2 is false because Extended Squitter (1090ES) broadcasts autonomously without requiring ground interrogation.
Question 11 • numerical
Calculate the round-trip propagation time for an aircraft at a slant range of 150 km.
A 500 microseconds
B 1000 microseconds
C 1 millisecond
D Both B and C
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Time $t = 2R/c = (2 \times 150,000) / (3 \times 10^8) = 0.001\text{ s} = 1000\text{ }\mu\text{s}$.
Question 12 • conceptual
What emergency code must a pilot squawk on Mode A to indicate unlawful interference (hijacking)?
A 7500
B 7600
C 7700
D 7777
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Squawk 7500 indicates unlawful interference, 7600 indicates communication failure, and 7700 indicates general emergency.
Question 13 • conceptual
What is the primary limitation of traditional Mode A/C SSR systems in high-density airspace?
A Excessive power consumption
B Synchronous garble and fruit interference
C Low operating frequency
D Inability to interface with altimeters
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Simultaneous replies from multiple aircraft in high-density areas cause synchronous garble and fruit (asynchronous replies), degrading tracking.
Question 14 • conceptual
Which DGCA CAR series governs the airworthiness and operational requirements of airborne transponders in India?
A CAR Section 2, Series I
B CAR Section 4, Series A
C CAR Section 3, Part III
D CAR Section 5, Series C
Solution & Technical Rationale:
DGCA CAR Section 2, Series I, Part V governs radar transponder and altitude reporting equipment mandates for Indian registered aircraft.
