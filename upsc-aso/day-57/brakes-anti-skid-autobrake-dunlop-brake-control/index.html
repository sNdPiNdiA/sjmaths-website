Brakes anti-skid, autobrake (Dunlop brake control)

Day 57 of 129 Aircraft Systems and Avionics Phase 3: Systems and Applied 60-90 min Master Cycle

Step 1: Understand

The primary objective of an aircraft anti-skid system is to maximize braking efficiency by maintaining the tire-to-ground friction coefficient ($\mu$) at its peak value, while simultaneously preventing wheel lockup to ensure directional stability and prevent tire blowout.

Modern electronic anti-skid systems continuously monitor wheel speed via electromagnetic transducers. When the deceleration rate of a wheel exceeds a predetermined threshold (indicating an impending skid), the control unit commands a servo valve to reduce hydraulic pressure to that brake, allowing the wheel to spin back up to aircraft speed before reapplying pressure.

The Dunlop Maxaret system is a classic, purely hydro-mechanical anti-skid system. It utilizes a small, high-density flywheel driven by the main wheel via a rubber-tired drive wheel. During rapid wheel deceleration (skid), the flywheel's inertia causes it to overrun its drive shaft, operating a valve mechanism that vents brake hydraulic pressure directly to the return line.

Step 2: Learn

Key Formulas & Physical Relations

The efficiency of braking is governed by the Slip Ratio ($S$), which represents the relative difference between the aircraft's forward speed and the rotational speed of the tire:

$$S = \frac{V_a - V_w}{V_a} \times 100\%$$

Where:

  • $V_a$ = Aircraft ground speed (true forward velocity)
  • $V_w$ = Wheel tangential velocity ($\omega \cdot r$, where $\omega$ is angular velocity and $r$ is tire rolling radius)

An ideal anti-skid system modulates pressure to keep the slip ratio within the optimum range of 10% to 20%. A slip ratio of 100% indicates a completely locked wheel (skidding), which drastically reduces lateral force (loss of directional control) and lowers the braking friction coefficient.

System Comparison

Feature Dunlop Maxaret System (Hydro-Mechanical) Modern Electronic Anti-Skid System
Sensing Method Mechanical flywheel inertia (overrun clutch mechanism) Electromagnetic wheel speed transducers (AC/DC generators)
Control Valve Mechanical cam-operated valve venting to return line Electro-hydraulic servo valve (flapper-nozzle or torque motor)
Modulation On/Off cycling (rapid pressure dumping and restoration) Fully modulated (proportional-integral-derivative control)
Power Source Direct mechanical drive from the wheel rim Aircraft DC/AC electrical bus + Hydraulic system pressure
Special Protections None (basic skid prevention only) Locked wheel, Touchdown, Hydroplane, and Gear Retract braking

Anti-Skid Protection Modes

  • Touchdown Protection: Prevents hydraulic pressure from reaching the brakes prior to landing. The system holds the return valve open until the wheels spin up (typically above 35-50 knots) or a ground-shift signal (Squat Switch / WOW) is received.
  • Locked Wheel Protection: Compares the speed of paired wheels (usually inboard/outboard pairs). If one wheel's speed drops below a fraction (typically 30%) of its paired wheel, the system completely releases pressure to the slower wheel to break the skid.
  • Hydroplane Protection: Active when wheel speed drops to near zero on wet runways, preventing the wheel from remaining locked in an aquaplaning state.

UPSC Exam Tip: The Autobrake system in Landing Mode regulates pressure to maintain a constant deceleration rate. This means if reverse thrust or aerodynamic drag increases, the autobrake system will automatically decrease hydraulic brake pressure to maintain the selected target deceleration rate.
Key Distinction: In RTO (Rejected Takeoff) mode, the autobrake does NOT target a deceleration rate. Instead, it applies maximum available system pressure (typically 3,000 psi) immediately upon thrust lever retardation to idle at high ground speeds.

Step 4: Aviation Application

1. Airbus A320/A330 BSCU (Brake and Steering Control Unit)

The BSCU is a dual-channel digital computer that controls both normal braking and anti-skid. If a skid is detected by the wheel speed tachometers, the BSCU sends a modulated current to the anti-skid servo valves. During landing with Autobrake set to "MED", the BSCU targets a deceleration rate of $3.0 \text{ m/s}^2$. If the pilot deploys full reverse thrust, the BSCU reduces hydraulic pressure to the brake calipers to ensure the deceleration does not exceed the target rate, saving brake wear.

2. Classic Hawker Siddeley Trident (Dunlop Maxaret Installation)

In this classic installation, each main gear wheel has a Maxaret unit driven by a rubber tire pressing against the inner wheel rim. During a landing on a contaminated runway, as a wheel begins to lock, the internal flywheel overruns its drive shaft. This action rotates a control sleeve, which closes the inlet port from the brake control valve and opens the exhaust port to the reservoir. The brake releases instantly, allowing the wheel to spin up, which reverses the sleeve position and reapplies brake pressure.

Step 5: UPSC Focus

  • Common Trap: Candidates often confuse the function of Touchdown Protection. It does not apply the brakes automatically on touchdown; it actively prevents brake application to protect the tires from bursting if the pilot has their feet on the brake pedals during landing.
  • Exam Pattern: Questions frequently target the physical components of the Dunlop Maxaret system. Remember that it is a non-electric, purely hydro-mechanical unit using a flywheel, cam, and spring-loaded clutch.
  • Hydraulic Return Line: In all anti-skid systems, when a skid is corrected, the excess hydraulic fluid is routed to the low-pressure return line (reservoir), never blocked or pressurized back into the supply line.

Practice MCQs

Q1. What is the primary operational objective of an aircraft anti-skid system?
A) To minimize tire wear by preventing any tire slip relative to the runway surface.
B) To maintain the tire slip ratio at 0% throughout the braking phase.
C) To maintain the tire-to-ground friction coefficient at its maximum value while preserving directional control.
D) To assist the steering system by differentially braking the inboard wheels.
Correct Answer: C
The anti-skid system modulates hydraulic pressure to maintain the optimum slip ratio (usually 10-20%), which maximizes the friction coefficient ($\mu$) and ensures directional stability by preventing wheel lockup.
Q2. In a classic Dunlop Maxaret anti-skid unit, what physical phenomenon is utilized to detect an impending wheel skid?
A) Electromagnetic induction via a variable reluctance sensor.
B) Inertia of a high-density flywheel overrunning its drive shaft.
C) Differential pressure across a hydraulic orifice.
D) Centrifugal force acting on weighted governor flyballs.
Correct Answer: B
The Dunlop Maxaret is a mechanical system. When the main wheel decelerates rapidly during a skid, the internal flywheel's inertia causes it to overrun its drive shaft, which actuates the hydraulic dump valve.
Q3. An aircraft has an actual ground speed of 100 knots. If the wheel tangential speed drops to 85 knots during braking, what is the calculated Slip Ratio ($S$)?
A) 15%
B) 85%
C) 1.17%
D) 30%
Correct Answer: A
Using the formula: $S = \frac{V_a - V_w}{V_a} \times 100\% = \frac{100 - 85}{100} \times 100\% = 15\%$. This falls perfectly within the optimum braking range of 10% to 20%.
Q4. What is the purpose of the "Touchdown Protection" circuit in an electronic anti-skid system?
A) To automatically apply full braking pressure immediately upon main gear touchdown.
B) To prevent brake pressure application until the wheels have spun up to a reference speed or the ground switch is closed.
C) To isolate the nose wheel brakes from the main wheel brake system.
D) To disengage the autobrake if the landing is bounced.
Correct Answer: B
Touchdown protection prevents landing with locked wheels (which would blowout the tires instantly) by keeping the return valve open until the wheels spin up or the squat switch signals ground mode.
Q5. During a landing roll with the Autobrake set to "LO" (low deceleration), the pilot deploys thrust reversers. How does the autobrake system respond?
A) It increases hydraulic brake pressure to assist the thrust reversers.
B) It automatically disengages and reverts to manual braking.
C) It decreases hydraulic brake pressure to maintain the targeted constant deceleration rate.
D) It cycles the anti-skid valves to prevent hydroplaning.
Correct Answer: C
Because landing autobrake modes target a specific deceleration rate, any additional deceleration force (such as thrust reversers or speedbrakes) causes the system to reduce brake pressure to maintain that constant target rate.
Q6. In an electronic anti-skid system, "Locked Wheel Protection" is typically configured to compare the speeds of which wheel pairs?
A) Nose wheel and main wheels.
B) Left main gear wheels compared directly to right main gear wheels.
C) Associated inboard and outboard wheel pairs on the same or opposite gears.
D) Front wheels of a bogie compared to rear wheels of the same bogie.
Correct Answer: C
Locked wheel protection compares paired wheels (usually inboard-to-inboard or outboard-to-outboard). If one wheel speed drops below a set threshold (e.g., 30%) of its paired wheel, its brake pressure is completely dumped.
Q7. Which of the following statements is TRUE regarding the Rejected Takeoff (RTO) mode of an autobrake system?
A) It modulates pressure to maintain a medium deceleration rate of 4.0 m/s².
B) It applies full system hydraulic pressure to the brakes regardless of deceleration rate.
C) It is armed only when the aircraft is airborne.
D) It disengages automatically if the speed exceeds 80 knots.
Correct Answer: B
Unlike landing modes, RTO mode is designed to stop the aircraft as quickly as possible during an emergency abort. It bypasses deceleration limits and applies full available system hydraulic pressure (typically 3,000 psi).
Q8. When the anti-skid control valve dumps hydraulic pressure to correct a skid, where is the excess hydraulic fluid routed?
A) To an accumulator to be reused in the next cycle.
B) Directly into the atmosphere through a relief port.
C) Back to the hydraulic system low-pressure return line.
D) To the emergency brake system line.
Correct Answer: C
To rapidly reduce brake pressure, the anti-skid valve closes the supply port and opens a path from the brake cylinder to the low-pressure return line, sending the fluid back to the reservoir.
Q9. What is the function of "Gear Retract Braking" in a transport category aircraft?
A) To lock the wheels during flight to prevent aerodynamic vibration.
B) To apply hydraulic pressure to stop main wheel rotation immediately after takeoff during gear retraction.
C) To ensure the nose wheel does not rotate in the wheel well.
D) To test the anti-skid valves during landing gear retraction.
Correct Answer: B
Spinning main wheels carry massive gyroscopic energy. Gear retract braking applies a small amount of hydraulic pressure to stop the main wheels from spinning as they enter the wheel wells, protecting structural components.
Q10. In the Dunlop Maxaret system, what component directly forces the valve sleeve to return to its normal (non-skid) position when the wheel recovers speed?
A) An electric solenoid energized by the cockpit switch.
B) A mechanical return spring and the deceleration of the flywheel relative to the shaft.
C) Hydraulic pressure from the emergency accumulator.
D) Centrifugal weights moving outward.
Correct Answer: B
Once the wheel spins back up, the relative speed difference between the flywheel and its drive shell disappears. A mechanical return spring resets the cam and sleeve, closing the exhaust port and restoring brake pressure.
