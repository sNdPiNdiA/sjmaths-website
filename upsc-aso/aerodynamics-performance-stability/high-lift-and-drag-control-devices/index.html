01 Plain, Split, Slotted, and Fowler Flaps

Subtopic 1 of 5
1. Formal Technical Definition

High-Lift Flap Systems: Trailing-edge aerodynamic control devices deployed symmetrically to artificially camber the airfoil profile, shift zero-lift angle of attack $\alpha_{L0}$ more negative, increase maximum lift coefficient $C_{L,\max}$, and augment effective wing area (Fowler type), thereby permitting lower stall speeds $V_s$ and steepened approach glide paths without exceeding structural load limits.

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

At the continuum fluid level, deflecting a trailing-edge flap increases the local negative pressure peak on the upper surface and elevates positive stagnation pressure on the lower surface, effectively generating an additional circulation $\Delta \Gamma$. According to the Kutta-Joukowski theorem ($L' = \rho_\infty V_\infty \Gamma$), higher circulation yields higher lift.

  • Plain Flap: Hinged rear portion of the airfoil deflected downward. Increases camber but induces severe upper-surface flow separation early due to adverse pressure gradients.
  • Split Flap: Only the lower surface deflects downward. Creates immense form drag alongside modest lift augmentation; historically used on WWII aircraft (e.g., Douglas DC-3).
  • Slotted Flap: Incorporates a gap (slot) between the main airfoil and flap. High-pressure air from the lower surface bleeds through the slot into the upper boundary layer, energizing sluggish boundary layer fluid and delaying separation.
  • Fowler Flap: Translates backward and rotates downward simultaneously. Increases both camber and total wing surface area ($S$), providing maximum $C_{L,\max}$ enhancement among trailing-edge devices.
3. Mathematical Derivation & Equations

The lift generated by a finite wing with deployed flaps is expressed via the expanded lift equation:

$$L = \frac{1}{2} \rho_\infty V_\infty^2 S_{\text{eff}} \left( C_{L0} + \Delta C_{L,\text{flap}} + \frac{\partial C_L}{\partial \alpha}(\alpha - \alpha_{L0, \text{new}}) \right)$$

For a Fowler flap, the effective wing area expansion factor is defined as $\sigma_S = S_{\text{new}} / S_{\text{clean}}$. The incremental lift coefficient contribution due to flap deflection $\delta_f$ and area increase is modeled by thin airfoil theory adjustments:

$$\Delta C_L = C_{L,\alpha} \cdot \tau_f \cdot \delta_f \cdot \left(\frac{S_{\text{flap}}}{S_{\text{wing}}}\right)$$

Where: $\rho_\infty$ = free-stream air density ($\text{kg/m}^3$), $V_\infty$ = true airspeed ($\text{m/s}$), $C_{L,\alpha}$ = lift curve slope ($\text{rad}^{-1}$), $\tau_f$ = flap effectiveness factor (typically $0.6$ to $0.8$ depending on slot geometry), $\delta_f$ = flap deflection angle ($\text{rad}$), and $S_{\text{flap}}/S_{\text{wing}}$ = flap area ratio.

6. Aircraft Application & Air Safety Officer Connection

Modern airliners (e.g., Airbus A320, Boeing 737) utilize sophisticated multi-slotted Fowler flaps driven by torque tubes, hydraulic actuators, and ball-screw mechanisms monitored by slat/flap control computers (SFCC).

DGCA Airworthiness & Safety Mandates (CAR Section 2 / JAR/FAR 25):

Asymmetric flap deployment generates catastrophic roll moments that can exceed lateral control authority. Air Safety Officers investigating incidents must inspect asymmetric brake mechanisms, torque tube shear pins, and proximity sensor calibration. DGCA CAR mandates rigorous functional testing of flap asymmetry protection circuits during C-checks.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions
  • Trap 1: Assuming flaps only increase lift. Correction: Flaps drastically increase profile and induced drag ($C_D$), which is intentionally utilized during landing approaches to maintain high thrust and rapid engine spool-up capability.
  • Trap 2: Confusing slotted flaps with slats. Correction: Slats are leading-edge devices; slotted flaps are trailing-edge devices. Both use boundary-layer energization via Bernoulli slot flow, but their geometric locations and primary pitching moment effects differ.
8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem:

A transport aircraft of mass $m = 75,000 \, \text{kg}$ operates at sea-level standard conditions ($\rho = 1.225 \, \text{kg/m}^3$). Wing reference area $S = 125 \, \text{m}^2$. In landing configuration ($\delta_f = 35^\circ$), the maximum lift coefficient is $C_{L,\max} = 3.0$. Calculate the stall speed $V_s$ in knots.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Weight force ($W$): $W = m \cdot g = 75,000 \times 9.81 = 735,750 \, \text{N}$.

2. Stall speed formula from lift equilibrium ($L = W$):

$$V_s = \sqrt{\frac{2W}{\rho_\infty S C_{L,\max}}}$$

3. Substitution:

$$V_s = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 735,750}{1.225 \times 125 \times 3.0}} = \sqrt{\frac{1,471,500}{459.375}} = \sqrt{3,203.26} \approx 56.60 \, \text{m/s}$$

4. Convert to knots ($1 \, \text{knot} = 0.514444 \, \text{m/s}$):

$$V_{s, \text{knots}} = \frac{56.60}{0.514444} = 110.02 \, \text{kts}$$

Operational Takeaway:

Deployment of high-lift flaps drops the stall speed significantly (from ~155 kts clean to 110 kts), allowing safe low-speed operations on short runways.

02 Leading-Edge Slat & Slots

Subtopic 2 of 5
1. Formal Technical Definition

Leading-Edge Slats and Slots: Aerodynamic high-lift devices located at the airfoil leading edge. A slot is a fixed aerodynamic channel through the wing structure, whereas a slat is a movable auxiliary airfoil section that deploys forward and downward to create a converging duct, delaying flow separation to substantially higher angles of attack.

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

At high angles of attack ($\alpha$), the stagnation point moves beneath the leading edge, and air accelerates violently around the sharp leading-edge radius, creating an intense local suction peak. This produces an adverse pressure gradient that separates the boundary layer immediately.

Deploying a slat creates a carefully contoured slot between the slat and the main wing element. High-pressure air from the lower surface accelerates through this converging slot and injects a high-momentum jet tangentially over the upper surface of the main wing. This high-energy jet thickens and invigorates the boundary layer, allowing it to withstand much harsher adverse pressure gradients and delaying stall by $10^\circ$ to $15^\circ$ of additional angle of attack.

3. Mathematical Derivation & Equations

The increase in maximum angle of attack ($\Delta \alpha_{\text{stall}}$) is governed by the jet momentum coefficient $C_{\mu}$ supplied by the slot:

$$C_{\mu} = \frac{\dot{m}_{\text{jet}} V_{\text{jet}}}{\frac{1}{2} \rho_\infty V_\infty^2 S_{\text{wing}}}$$

The resulting upward shift in the lift curve allows the aircraft to achieve a higher maximum lift coefficient:

$$C_{L,\max, \text{slatted}} = C_{L,\max, \text{clean}} + \Delta C_{L, \text{slat}}$$

6. Aircraft Application & Air Safety Officer Connection

Leading-edge slats are critical on commercial jetliners (e.g., Boeing 777, Airbus A350) and STOL aircraft. They can be hydraulically or electrically actuated tracks (Fowler-type slats) or automatic leading-edge slats (e.g., Krueger slats on Boeing 727/737).

Safety Incident Investigation Focus:

Uncommanded slat retraction or asymmetric slat deployment at high angles of attack during takeoff rotation can cause an unrecoverable roll and subsequent stall-spin accident. ASOs must verify adherence to DGCA mandatory service bulletins (SBs) regarding slat track lubrication, gear-box wear limits, and proximity sensor integrity.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions
  • Trap 1: Believing slots and slats operate by increasing camber alone. Correction: While slats slightly increase camber and chord length, their primary aerodynamic mechanism is boundary layer energization via high-pressure bleed air flowing through the slot.
  • Trap 2: Confusing fixed slots (found on STOL aircraft like the Fieseler Storch) with movable slats found on modern jets.
8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem:

An aircraft wing with a clean $C_{L,\max} = 1.4$ has leading-edge slats deployed, increasing $C_{L,\max}$ by $55\%$. If the aircraft flies at $V_\infty = 70 \, \text{m/s}$ at an altitude where $\rho = 1.0 \, \text{kg/m}^3$ and wing area $S = 100 \, \text{m}^2$, compute the maximum lift force in both clean and slatted configurations.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Dynamic pressure $q_\infty = \frac{1}{2} \rho V_\infty^2 = 0.5 \times 1.0 \times (70)^2 = 2,450 \, \text{Pa}$.

2. Clean maximum lift ($L_{\max, \text{clean}}$):

$$L_{\max, \text{clean}} = q_\infty \cdot S \cdot C_{L,\max, \text{clean}} = 2,450 \times 100 \times 1.4 = 343,000 \, \text{N}$$

3. Slatted maximum lift ($C_{L,\max, \text{slatted}} = 1.4 \times 1.55 = 2.17$):

$$L_{\max, \text{slatted}} = 2,450 \times 100 \times 2.17 = 531,650 \, \text{N}$$

Operational Takeaway:

Slats provide an additional ~188 kN of lift capability at the same airspeed, drastically reducing takeoff and landing field lengths.

03 Spoilers

Subtopic 3 of 5
1. Formal Technical Definition

Spoilers: Spanwise plates or panels mounted on the upper surface of the aircraft wing that hinge upward into the airflow. By intentionally tripping the boundary layer and inducing massive flow separation, spoilers simultaneously reduce lift ($C_L$) and increase aerodynamic drag ($C_D$).

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

Normally, smooth upper-surface flow creates a large low-pressure suction zone that generates lift. When a spoiler plate deploys, it acts as a physical obstruction that halts the smooth pressure recovery. A massive turbulent wake forms downstream of the spoiler, destroying lift on that section of the wing and creating high form drag.

  • Roll Control (Differential Spoilers): Deployed on one wing only to decrease lift and increase drag, assisting or replacing ailerons for lateral roll control, especially on swept-wing aircraft where aileron-induced adverse yaw is a hazard.
  • Flight Spoilers (Speed Brakes): Deployed symmetrically in flight to increase descent rate without increasing airspeed.
3. Mathematical Derivation & Equations

The net lift coefficient with spoiler deflection angle $\delta_{\text{sp}}$ is modeled as:

$$C_L = C_{L,\alpha}(\alpha - \alpha_{L0}) - \left( \frac{\partial C_L}{\partial \delta_{\text{sp}}} \right) \delta_{\text{sp}}$$

Concurrently, the drag coefficient increases due to pressure form drag behind the spoiler plate:

$$C_D = C_{D0} + C_{Di} + \Delta C_{D, \text{sp}}(\delta_{\text{sp}})$$

6. Aircraft Application & Air Safety Officer Connection

Swept-wing aircraft (e.g., Boeing 737, Airbus A320) rely heavily on spoilers for roll control because traditional ailerons can twist the flexible wing tips at high speeds, causing a reversal of roll control.

DGCA Safety Concern & Accident Lessons:

Inadvertent inflight spoiler deployment (due to spoiler mixer valve failures or uncommanded actuator extension) has caused severe loss of altitude and severe buffet. ASOs must inspect spoiler auto-retract systems, hydraulic return line pressures, and cockpit warning logic.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions
  • Trap 1: Believing spoilers increase lift by changing camber. Correction: Spoilers kill lift by obstructing upper-surface flow; they never increase lift.
  • Trap 2: Confusing spoilers with slotted flaps. Spoilers are upper-surface flow-tripping drag/lift-reduction devices, whereas flaps are trailing-edge lift enhancers.
8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem:

An aircraft in steady level flight at $V_\infty = 150 \, \text{m/s}$ ($\rho = 0.90 \, \text{kg/m}^3$) has a wing area $S = 140 \, \text{m}^2$ and lift coefficient $C_L = 0.5$. Flight spoilers deploy symmetrically, reducing the lift coefficient by $0.20$. Calculate the immediate loss in lift force ($\Delta L$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Dynamic pressure $q_\infty = \frac{1}{2} \rho V_\infty^2 = 0.5 \times 0.90 \times (150)^2 = 10,125 \, \text{Pa}$.

2. Lift reduction formula:

$$\Delta L = q_\infty \cdot S \cdot \Delta C_L$$

3. Substitution:

$$\Delta L = 10,125 \times 140 \times 0.20 = 283,500 \, \text{N}$$

Operational Takeaway:

Deploying spoilers in cruise causes an immediate downward acceleration (~283.5 kN loss of lift), enabling rapid descent initiation.

04 Speed Brakes

Subtopic 4 of 5
1. Formal Technical Definition

Speed Brakes: Aerodynamic control surfaces (separate panels or extended fuselage flaps) deployed symmetrically into the airstream specifically to increase parasite and form drag ($C_D$) without substantially altering the aircraft's lift coefficient ($C_L$), thereby permitting rapid deceleration or steep descent profiles without overspeeding.

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

Unlike spoilers which are primarily designed to destroy lift, pure speed brakes are engineered to create a massive pressure drag wake with minimal disruption to overall lift distribution. They can be located on the fuselage tail (e.g., Blackburn Buccaneer, Fokker F28), under-fuselage panels, or upper/lower wing surfaces configured to cancel out pitching moment changes.

3. Mathematical Derivation & Equations

The aircraft equation of motion along the flight path during a descent with speed brakes deployed:

$$T - D - W \sin\gamma = m \frac{dV}{dt}$$

Where total drag $D$ includes the added speed brake drag increment $\Delta D_{\text{sb}}$:

$$D = \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 S \left( C_{D0} + \Delta C_{D, \text{sb}} + \frac{K L^2}{\frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 S^2} \right)$$

6. Aircraft Application & Air Safety Officer Connection

Air traffic control (ATC) frequently issues directives to "descend and expedite." Speed brakes allow pilots to steepen descent rates without exceeding maximum operating speed ($V_{\text{MO}}/M_{\text{MO}}$) or requiring idle thrust reduction that cools turbine engines too rapidly.

DGCA Safety & Airworthiness Alert:

Leaving speed brakes extended during low-altitude maneuvering or final approach increases stall speed and buffet margins. DGCA operations inspectors monitor Flight Data Recorder (FDR) readouts for incidents of "speed brake extended below 1,000 feet AGL" contrary to Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions
  • Trap 1: Equating speed brakes with ground spoilers. Correction: While many airliners use multifunction panels that serve as both speed brakes and ground spoilers, dedicated speed brakes are engineered specifically for drag augmentation with minimal pitching moment disruption.
  • Trap 2: Assuming speed brakes increase lift. Correction: Pure speed brakes increase drag while keeping lift relatively constant, whereas spoilers actively destroy lift.
8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem:

A jet aircraft weighing $600,000 \, \text{N}$ cruises at $V = 200 \, \text{m/s}$ ($\rho = 0.50 \, \text{kg/m}^3$). Wing area $S = 150 \, \text{m}^2$. Clean drag coefficient $C_{D0} = 0.020$, induced drag factor $K = 0.05$. Speed brakes are deployed, adding $\Delta C_{D} = 0.025$. Calculate the total aircraft drag before and after speed brake deployment.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Dynamic pressure $q = 0.5 \times 0.50 \times (200)^2 = 10,000 \, \text{Pa}$.

2. Lift coefficient in steady level flight ($L = W = 600,000 \, \text{N}$):

$$C_L = \frac{W}{q \cdot S} = \frac{600,000}{10,000 \times 150} = 0.40$$

3. Clean drag ($D_{\text{clean}}$):

$$C_{D, \text{clean}} = 0.020 + 0.05 \times (0.40)^2 = 0.020 + 0.008 = 0.028$$
$$D_{\text{clean}} = q \cdot S \cdot C_{D, \text{clean}} = 10,000 \times 150 \times 0.028 = 42,000 \, \text{N}$$

4. Drag with speed brakes ($\Delta C_D = 0.025$):

$$D_{\text{total}} = 10,000 \times 150 \times (0.028 + 0.025) = 1,500,000 \times 0.053 = 79,500 \, \text{N}$$

Operational Takeaway:

Speed brake deployment nearly doubles total aircraft drag (from 42 kN to 79.5 kN), providing rapid deceleration capability.

05 Lift Dumpers

Subtopic 5 of 5
1. Formal Technical Definition

Lift Dumpers: High-authority aerodynamic ground devices (maximum-deflection ground spoilers) deployed instantly upon touchdown to destroy residual wing lift, forcing the aircraft's weight onto its landing gear to maximize wheel braking friction and prevent aerodynamic bounce or runway excursions.

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

Upon landing flare, wings still generate substantial lift due to ground effect and residual angle of attack. If lift exceeds aircraft weight during wheel spin-up, the aircraft can skip or bounce down the runway, risking structural failure or runway overrun.

Lift dumpers deploy to maximum angle ($45^\circ$ to $60^\circ$) instantaneously upon receiving main landing gear squat switch signals (or thrust reverser activation). By destroying lift, the aircraft's entire gravitational weight presses down onto the tires, increasing normal force ($N$) and thus maximum static/sliding friction ($F_{\text{friction}} = \mu \cdot N$).

3. Mathematical Derivation & Equations

The normal force on the landing gear wheels immediately after touchdown with lift dumpers deployed:

$$N_{\text{wheel}} = W - L_{\text{residual}} = W - \left( \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 S (C_{L0} - \Delta C_{L, \text{dumper}}) \right)$$

The total deceleration force ($F_{\text{decel}}$) available for stopping the aircraft:

$$F_{\text{decel}} = \mu_{\text{tire}} \cdot N_{\text{wheel}} + D_{\text{clean}} + D_{\text{dumper}} + T_{\text{reverser}}$$

6. Aircraft Application & Air Safety Officer Connection

All modern commercial transport category aircraft feature automatic lift dumper deployment triggered by weight-on-wheels (WoW) sensors on main landing gear struts combined with wheel spin-up signals.

DGCA Safety & Accident Investigation Focus:

Failure of lift dumpers to deploy automatically upon landing has been a contributing factor in numerous runway overrun accidents (e.g., wet/contaminated runway excursions). DGCA Air Safety Officers investigate squib valve actuators, proximity switch microswitches, and auto-deploy logic computer faults.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions
  • Trap 1: Believing lift dumpers are deployed during takeoff to help the aircraft leave the ground. Correction: Lift dumpers are strictly ground-operation devices deployed after landing. Deploying them on takeoff would be catastrophic.
  • Trap 2: Confusing lift dumpers with thrust reversers. Lift dumpers are aerodynamic wing panels; thrust reversers redirect engine exhaust gases forward. Both work in synergy during landing roll.
8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem:

An aircraft lands at touchdown speed $V = 70 \, \text{m/s}$ ($\rho = 1.225 \, \text{kg/m}^3$). Aircraft mass $m = 80,000 \, \g{kg}$ ($W = 784,800 \, \text{N}$). Wing area $S = 130 \, \text{m}^2$. Residual lift coefficient before dumper deployment is $C_L = 1.8$. When lift dumpers deploy, $C_L$ drops to $0.0$. Tire-to-runway friction coefficient $\mu = 0.40$. Calculate the increase in maximum braking friction force ($F_{\text{friction}}$) resulting from lift dumper deployment.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Dynamic pressure at touchdown:

$$q = 0.5 \times 1.225 \times (70)^2 = 0.5 \times 1.225 \times 4900 = 3,001.25 \, \text{Pa}$$

2. Residual lift before dumpers ($L_1$):

$$L_1 = 3,001.25 \times 130 \times 1.8 = 702,292.5 \, \text{N}$$

3. Wheel normal force before dumpers ($N_1 = W - L_1$):

$$N_1 = 784,800 - 702,292.5 = 82,507.5 \, \text{N}$$

4. Wheel normal force after lift dumpers ($L_2 = 0 \implies N_2 = W = 784,800 \, \text{N}$):

$$N_2 = 784,800 \, \text{N}$$

5. Friction force before vs. after ($\mu = 0.40$):

$$F_{\text{friction, before}} = 0.40 \times 82,507.5 = 33,003 \, \text{N}$$
$$F_{\text{friction, after}} = 0.40 \times 784,800 = 313,920 \, \text{N}$$

6. Net increase in braking force:

$$\Delta F_{\text{friction}} = 313,920 - 33,003 = 280,917 \, \text{N} \approx 280.9 \, \text{kN}$$

Operational Takeaway:

Lift dumpers increase wheel braking force nearly tenfold (~281 kN additional normal force converted to friction), preventing high-speed runway overruns.

Aviation Case Studies & DGCA Airworthiness Standards

Real-world accident investigations and regulatory compliance frameworks for UPSC Air Safety Officers.

Case Study 1: Asymmetric Slat/Flap Deployment Incidents & FAA/DGCA Mandates

High-Yield ASO Topic

Background: Asymmetric deployment of high-lift devices (slats or Fowler flaps) creates a massive rolling moment due to lift differential between the left and right wings. In several historical transport aircraft incidents, mechanical failures in torque tubes, drive gearboxes, or asymmetry brake circuits caused one wing's flaps to retract or jam while the other side continued extending.

Aerodynamic Mechanism: When flap asymmetry occurs, the rolling moment $\Delta L_{\text{roll}}$ and induced yawing moment can easily exceed full aileron and rudder control authority, especially during low-speed takeoff rotation or final approach when dynamic pressure ($q$) is low.

DGCA CAR Section 2 Compliance:

DGCA Airworthiness Inspectors enforce CAR Section 2 (Airworthiness) and applicable Airworthiness Directives (ADs) requiring dual-channel slat/flap position sensors, independent asymmetry detection braking systems, and rigorous scheduled maintenance checks of ball-screw actuators and torque shaft shear pins.

Case Study 2: Ground Spoiler / Lift Dumper Non-Deployment & Runway Excursions

Runway Safety

Background: Several high-profile runway excursion accidents on wet or contaminated runways involved failure of ground spoilers (lift dumpers) to deploy automatically upon touchdown.

Aerodynamic Mechanism: Without lift dumpers, residual wing lift holds the aircraft's weight off the wheels. Tire normal force $N = W - L$ remains critically low, preventing antiskid braking systems from developing maximum friction force ($\mu \cdot N$). The aircraft hydroplanes or floats down the runway, leading to overruns.

ASO Investigation Protocol:

Air Safety Officers investigating runway excursions must examine Flight Data Recorder (FDR) parameters for weight-on-wheels (WoW) switch actuation timing, spoiler arming status, hydraulic actuator pressure, and proximity sensor calibration.

High-Yield Revision Summary (UPSC ASO Cheat Sheet)

Core aerodynamic formulas, device classifications, and critical exam takeaways at a glance.

High-Lift Devices Summary

  • Plain Flap: Hinged trailing edge; increases camber; early separation.
  • Split Flap: Lower surface deflects; high form drag + modest lift.
  • Slotted Flap: Gap permits high-pressure air bleed to energize boundary layer.
  • Fowler Flap: Translates back & rotates down; increases $S$ and camber for max $C_{L,\max}$.
  • Slats/Slots: Leading edge; high-pressure jet delays stall angle by $10^\circ - 15^\circ$.

Drag-Control Devices Summary

  • Spoilers: Upper surface plates; destroy lift ($C_L \downarrow$) and increase drag ($C_D \uparrow$). Used for roll control & speed braking.
  • Speed Brakes: Symmetric drag augmentation with minimal lift change; steepens descents.
  • Lift Dumpers: Maximum ground spoiler deployment upon touchdown; eliminates residual lift to maximize wheel normal force ($N$) and braking friction.

3. Slat/Flap Control Computer (SFCC) & Sensors: Check proximity sensors, resolver calibration, and software logic faults in the SFCC that inhibit deployment or fail to detect skewness."

Q: "Why do aircraft landing on wet or contaminated runways rely critically on lift dumpers rather than wheel brakes alone?"

Model Answer: "During touchdown flare, residual wing lift ($L$) can support up to 90-95% of the aircraft's weight. The normal force pressing the tires onto the runway ($N = W - L$) is extremely small. Without lift dumpers, wheel brakes cannot develop adequate friction ($F = \mu N$), risking hydroplaning and runway excursions. Lift dumpers instantly kill residual lift, transferring 100% of the aircraft's weight onto the wheels and maximizing braking friction."
