Starting vortex

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

The starting vortex is a transient vortex shed from the trailing edge of an airfoil when it transitions from rest to motion, maintaining total circulation at zero per Kelvin's Theorem. $$\Gamma_{\text{bound}} + \Gamma_{\text{starting}} = 0$$
  • Physical Meaning: It preserves angular momentum and total circulation in an inviscid fluid during flow acceleration.
  • Governing Law: Governed by Kelvin's Circulation Theorem, stating that substantial derivative of circulation in a barotropic inviscid fluid is zero.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid within continuum fluid mechanics where molecular mean free path is negligible relative to chord length.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\Gamma_{\text{bound}}$ = Bound circulation around the airfoil profile ($m^2/s$)
  • $\Gamma_{\text{starting}}$ = Circulation of the shed starting vortex ($m^2/s$)
  • $C_L$ = Section lift coefficient (Dimensionless)
  • $U_{\infty}$ = Free-stream air velocity ($m/s$)
  • $c$ = Airfoil chord length ($m$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Air molecules are initially at rest relative to the wing surface, establishing zero net angular momentum across the entire fluid domain.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Rapid acceleration creates a sharp adverse pressure gradient at the sharp trailing edge, forcing boundary layer separation and rolling up a concentrated vortex sheet.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: The shed starting vortex leaves behind the wake while bound circulation instantly builds up on the airfoil to generate steady aerodynamic lift.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Apply Kelvin's Theorem along a closed material loop enclosing both wing and future wake.

$$\frac{D}{Dt} \oint_{C} \mathbf{V} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Initially, the fluid is at rest everywhere, fixing initial total circulation to zero.

$$\Gamma_{\text{total}}(t=0) = 0$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

As the wing accelerates, bound circulation grows and equal and opposite vorticity is shed downstream.

$$\Gamma_{\text{bound}}(t) + \Gamma_{\text{starting}}(t) = 0$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Steady-state bound circulation is perfectly balanced by the shed starting vortex.

$$\Gamma_{\text{starting}} = -\frac{1}{2} C_L U_{\infty} c$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Circulation has dimensions of $[\text{Length}^2 \text{Time}^{-1}]$ or $[L^2 T^{-1}]$, derived from velocity multiplied by length.
  • SI Unit: Square meters per second ($m^2/s$), representing volumetric flow rate per unit span or circulation strength.
  • CGS Unit: Square centimeters per second ($cm^2/s$), with a conversion factor of $1 \text{ m}^2/s = 10,000 \text{ cm}^2/s$.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Expressed in square feet per second ($ft^2/s$), where $1 \text{ m}^2/s \approx 10.764 \text{ ft}^2/s$.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Governed by ICAO Doc 4444 (Procedures for Air Navigation Services) and DGCA CAR Section 2 Series X on wake turbulence separations.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Severe vortex encounters disrupt pitot-static sensing, flight control actuators, and induce structural load factor exceedances.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: Flight American Airlines 587 (A300-600) crashed due to severe wake turbulence encounters inducing structural tail failure. The root safety cause involved aggressive rudder reversals in strong wake vortices.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Starting Vortex Perpetuation

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming the starting vortex travels along with the aircraft indefinitely.

✅ UPSC Truth: The starting vortex is shed at the takeoff point and remains stationary in the fluid frame while the aircraft flies away.

Trap 2: Circulation Violation

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing total circulation around a closed circuit enclosing a newly accelerated wing is non-zero.

✅ UPSC Truth: By Kelvin's Theorem, total circulation remains identically zero everywhere and always in an inviscid fluid.

Trap 3: Viscous Decay Confusion

❌ Examiner Trap: Claiming starting vortices decay instantly due to compressibility effects.

✅ UPSC Truth: Starting vortices decay slowly due to viscous diffusion and turbulent mixing over time in real fluids.

Trap 4: Lift Generation Timing

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming lift is established instantaneously the exact microsecond brakes are released.

✅ UPSC Truth: Lift builds up gradually as the starting vortex is shed and bound circulation smoothly develops.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An aircraft with a chord of $3.5 \text{ m}$ accelerates from rest to a takeoff speed of $70 \text{ m/s}$ at sea level. Calculate the strength of the shed starting vortex if the section lift coefficient at rotation is $1.2$.

Given Flight Parameters:
Chord $c = 3.5 \text{ m}$, Free-stream velocity $U_{\infty} = 70 \text{ m/s}$, Lift coefficient $C_L = 1.2$, Air density $\rho = 1.225 \text{ kg/m}^3$.
Governing Formula:
$$\Gamma_{\text{starting}} = -\frac{1}{2} C_L U_{\infty} c$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Substitute given parameters into the circulation formula: $\Gamma_{\text{starting}} = -0.5 \times 1.2 \times 70 \times 3.5$. Step 2: Perform multiplication of velocity and chord: $70 \times 3.5 = 245 \text{ m}^2/s$. Step 3: Multiply by lift coefficient factor: $0.5 \times 1.2 \times 245 = 147 \text{ m}^2/s$.
Final Answer: $\Gamma_{\text{starting}} = -147 \text{ m}^2/s$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Understanding starting vortex magnitude helps safety engineers compute early wake vortex transport rates during low-speed departures.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary physical role of the starting vortex shed from an airfoil?
A To satisfy Kelvin's Theorem by ensuring total circulation remains zero
B To create additional profile drag during high-speed cruise
C To prevent boundary layer separation on the upper surface
D To increase pitching moment stability at high Mach numbers
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Kelvin's Theorem states that circulation in an inviscid fluid cannot change; hence, a bound vortex must be accompanied by an equal and opposite starting vortex.
Question 2 • conceptual
Where does the starting vortex reside after an aircraft takes off and climbs away?
A Attached permanently to the right wingtip
B Stationary in the air near the departure runway/takeoff point
C Trailing directly behind the aircraft fuselage throughout cruise
D Absorbed into the engine compressor intake
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The starting vortex is shed at the start of motion and remains left behind in the fluid near the departure point while the aircraft moves away.
Question 3 • statement-based
Consider the following statements regarding the starting vortex: 1. Total circulation around a contour enclosing both wing and starting vortex is zero. 2. The starting vortex strength is proportional to aircraft speed and lift coefficient. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. Total circulation is conserved at zero, and shed vortex strength scales directly with bound circulation.
Question 4 • numerical
Calculate the circulation of a starting vortex for an aircraft with chord $4.0 \text{ m}$, speed $50 \text{ m/s}$, and $C_L = 1.0$.
A $-100 \text{ m}^2/s$
B $-200 \text{ m}^2/s$
C $-140 \text{ m}^2/s$
D $-250 \text{ m}^2/s$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $\Gamma = -0.5 \times C_L \times U_{\infty} \times c = -0.5 \times 1.0 \times 50 \times 4.0 = -100 \text{ m}^2/s$.
Question 5 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Lift on an airfoil does not build up instantaneously from zero to its full steady-state value. Reason (R): The finite time required to shed the starting vortex delays the establishment of full bound circulation.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The gradual shedding of the starting vortex governs the Wagner effect, which describes the time-dependent growth of lift on an airfoil.
Question 6 • conceptual
Which aerodynamic law forms the theoretical bedrock for the existence of the starting vortex?
A Navier-Stokes momentum equations
B Kelvin's Circulation Theorem
C Bernoulli's incompressible energy equation
D Fick's law of diffusion
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Kelvin's Theorem proves that circulation around a closed material loop moving with the fluid remains constant in inviscid barotropic flows.
Question 7 • statement-based
Consider the following: 1. Air viscosity causes the starting vortex to eventually dissipate. 2. Compressibility prevents the formation of starting vortices. Which is correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Viscosity causes vortex decay over time. Compressibility does not prevent starting vortex formation, though it alters flow properties.
Question 8 • numerical
If an aircraft wing has a chord of $2.0 \text{ m}$, moves at $100 \text{ m/s}$, and generates a starting vortex of strength $-150 \text{ m}^2/s$, what is the corresponding section $C_L$?
A 1.0
B 1.5
C 2.0
D 0.75
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $\Gamma = 0.5 C_L U c \implies 150 = 0.5 \times C_L \times 100 \times 2.0 \implies C_L = 150 / 100 = 1.5$.
Question 9 • conceptual
What happens to the bound circulation of a wing when it accelerates and sheds a starting vortex?
A It decreases to zero
B It increases to a positive value equal in magnitude to the starting vortex
C It becomes negative
D It remains unaffected
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The bound circulation increases to a positive value while the starting vortex takes an equal and opposite negative value.
Question 10 • conceptual
In what flow regime is the classical starting vortex theorem strictly derived?
A Compressible turbulent boundary layer
B Inviscid, incompressible, irrotational flow outside boundary layers
C Hypersonic dissociated gas flow
D Creeping Stokes flow
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Classical potential flow theory assumes inviscid, incompressible, and irrotational fluid outside boundary layers to apply Kelvin's theorem.
Question 11 • statement-based
Consider: 1. The starting vortex is shed from the trailing edge. 2. Trailing vortices are shed from wingtips during steady flight. Which is correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. The starting vortex is shed during transient motion at the trailing edge, while tip vortices are shed continuously during lift generation.
Question 12 • numerical
Determine the circulation magnitude if $C_L = 0.8$, $U_{\infty} = 60 \text{ m/s}$, and $c = 2.5 \text{ m}$.
A $40 \text{ m}^2/s$
B $60 \text{ m}^2/s$
C $80 \text{ m}^2/s$
D $120 \text{ m}^2/s$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
$|\Gamma| = 0.5 \times 0.8 \times 60 \times 2.5 = 0.4 \times 150 = 60 \text{ m}^2/s$.

Why does a starting vortex eventually disappear in a real fluid?
A Fluid compressibility
