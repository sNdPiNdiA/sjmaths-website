Sweep contribution

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Flight management systems compute takeoff speeds and stall warning margins dynamically utilizing swept-wing lift-curve slope degradation curves.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: High sweep angles degrade low-speed lift, requiring rigorous verification of field length and takeoff climb gradients under DGCA dispatch rules.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Airworthiness directives mandate rigorous flight test validation of stall warning and pitch-up tendencies on swept-wing transport aircraft per CAR Section 2.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Investigators analyze sweep-induced tip stall and deep stall susceptibility when auditing loss-of-control (LOC-I) incident flight data recorder logs.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Sweep contribution refers to the modification of aerodynamic coefficients—specifically lift-curve slope, pitching moment, and damping derivatives—resulting from the geometric sweep angle ($\Lambda$) of a lifting surface. The fundamental scaling of sectional lift is governed by the independence principle: $C_{L_\alpha} = C_{L_\alpha,0} \cos\Lambda$.
  • Physical Meaning: Wing sweep delays compressibility drag by reducing the component of flow velocity normal to the leading edge.
  • Governing Law: Modified strip theory combined with Prandtl-Glauert compressibility transformations for swept wings.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid in subsonic, transonic, and supersonic regimes where boundary layer separation remains controlled.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\Lambda$ = Geometric sweep angle measured relative to the normal to the freestream (Degrees or Radians)
  • $C_{L_\alpha}$ = Three-dimensional lift-curve slope ($rad^{-1}$)
  • $C_{L_\alpha,0}$ = Unswept or baseline section lift-curve slope ($rad^{-1}$)
  • $M$ = Freestream Mach number (Dimensionless)
  • $V_\infty$ = Freestream true airspeed ($m/s$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Velocity Vector Resolution: The incoming freestream velocity $V_\infty$ is resolved into a normal component $V_n = V_\infty \cos\Lambda$ and a spanwise component $V_t = V_\infty \sin\Lambda$.
2. Pressure Distribution Scaling: Pressure forces and resultant lift are driven exclusively by the normal velocity component $V_n$, effectively reducing sectional dynamic pressure by $\cos^2\Lambda$.
3. Boundary Layer Cross-Flow: The spanwise pressure gradient drives boundary layer air toward the wingtip, creating spanwise outflow and altering stall characteristics.
4. Pitching Moment Contribution: The aerodynamic center shifts rearward on swept wings, creating powerful aeroelastic twist moments and distinct longitudinal stability shifts.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Express the effective normal Mach number acting perpendicular to the swept leading edge.

$$M_n = M_\infty \cos\Lambda$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Apply the modified Prandtl-Glauert compressibility correction rule for the normal section.

$$C_{L_\alpha, \text{sweep}} = \frac{C_{L_\alpha, 0} \cos\Lambda}{\sqrt{1 - M_\infty^2 \cos^2\Lambda}}$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Integrate sectional lift contributions across the finite span accounting for taper and twist.

$$L = \frac{1}{2} \rho_\infty V_\infty^2 S \int_{-b/2}^{b/2} C_{L_\alpha}(y) \alpha_{\text{eff}}(y) \cos^2\Lambda \, dy$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Extract the definitive analytical expression for swept wing lift-curve slope reduction.

$$C_{L_\alpha} = \frac{2\pi AR}{2 + \sqrt{4 + \frac{AR^2 \beta^2}{\cos^2\Lambda} \left(1 + \frac{\tan^2\Lambda}{\beta^2}\right)}}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Sweep contribution factors and derivatives are dimensionless $[M^0 L^0 T^0]$, while sweep angle has dimensions of angle (radians or degrees).
  • SI Unit: Sweep angle is measured in radians ($rad$) or degrees ($^{\circ}$). Lift derivatives are in $rad^{-1}$.
  • CGS Unit: Same fundamental geometric and angular units apply universally across CGS and SI systems.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Degrees of sweep arc ($^\circ$) utilized universally in flight manuals, planform drawings, and FAA/DGCA type certificate data sheets.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Compliance with DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'B', Part III and ICAO Annex 8 Airworthiness of Aircraft requires certified stall warning systems accounting for swept-wing pitch-up.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Wing sweep alters angle-of-attack transducer calibration, stick pusher activation thresholds, and auto-slat deployment schedules during high-g maneuvers.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: Flight data analyses of swept-wing transport deep stall accidents reveal unrecoverable pitch lock due to wake immersion of horizontal stabilizers.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Sweep Increases Lift-Curve Slope

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming that adding sweep angle increases total wing lift and lift-curve slope.

✅ UPSC Truth: Wing sweep decreases the lift-curve slope ($C_{L_\alpha}$) proportional to $\cos\Lambda$, reducing lift generation efficiency per degree of angle of attack.

Trap 2: Normal Component Independence

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing the spanwise flow component generates useful lifting pressure.

✅ UPSC Truth: Only the velocity component normal to the leading edge generates lift; spanwise flow promotes boundary layer thickening and tip stall.

Trap 3: Aerodynamic Center Position

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming the aerodynamic center remains fixed at quarter-chord ($25\% \text{ c}$) on swept wings.

✅ UPSC Truth: Sweepback shifts the aerodynamic center significantly rearward, altering static longitudinal stability margins.

Trap 4: Pitch-Up Phenomenon Mechanism

❌ Examiner Trap: Attributing swept-wing pitch-up entirely to elevator compressibility.

✅ UPSC Truth: Pitch-up is caused by premature tip stall on swept-back wings, which shifts center of pressure forward and induces an uncommanded nose-up pitching moment.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An executive jet features a wing sweep angle of $35^{\circ}$ and a baseline unswept lift-curve slope of $5.73 \text{ rad}^{-1}$. Calculate the effective lift-curve slope of the swept wing in incompressible flow.

Given Flight Parameters:
Sweep angle $\Lambda = 35^{\circ}$, Unswept lift-curve slope $C_{L_\alpha,0} = 5.73 \text{ rad}^{-1}$, Mach number $M = 0.0$ (Incompressible).
Governing Formula:
$$C_{L_\alpha} = C_{L_\alpha,0} \cos\Lambda$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute the cosine of the sweep angle: $\cos(35^{\circ}) = 0.81915$. Step 2: Multiply by the baseline lift-curve slope: $C_{L_\alpha} = 5.73 \times 0.81915$. Step 3: Evaluate the final product: $C_{L_\alpha} = 4.6937 \text{ rad}^{-1}$.
Final Answer: $4.69 \text{ rad}^{-1}$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Understanding lift-curve slope reduction ensures flight dispatchers and pilots account for decreased control sensitivity and longer takeoff rolls.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary aerodynamic benefit of introducing sweepback on a transonic transport wing?
A Increases maximum lift coefficient
B Delays critical Mach number by reducing normal velocity component
C Eliminates induced drag completely
D Moves aerodynamic center forward
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Wing sweep reduces the component of flow velocity normal to the leading edge ($V_n = V_\infty \cos\Lambda$), which delays the onset of sonic flow and shockwaves, thereby increasing critical Mach number.
Question 2 • conceptual
How does wing sweep affect the three-dimensional lift-curve slope ($C_{L_\alpha}$) compared to an unswept wing?
A It increases $C_{L_\alpha}$ by a factor of $1/\cos\Lambda$
B It has no effect on $C_{L_\alpha}$
C It decreases $C_{L_\alpha}$ proportional to $\cos\Lambda$
D It makes $C_{L_\alpha}$ entirely independent of aspect ratio
Solution & Technical Rationale:
According to simple sweep theory, the lift-curve slope of a swept wing is reduced by the cosine of the sweep angle ($C_{L_\alpha} = C_{L_\alpha,0} \cos\Lambda$).
Question 3 • conceptual
What undesirable aerodynamic phenomenon is commonly associated with highly sweptback wings near stall?
A Root stall with immediate nose-down pitching
B Spanwise boundary layer outflow leading to premature tip stall and pitch-up
C Symmetric dual-engine flameout
D Immediate rudder flow reversal
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Spanwise pressure gradients drive boundary layer air toward the tips of sweptback wings, causing tip stall first, which unloads the rear wing and produces a dangerous nose-up pitch-up.
Question 4 • conceptual
Where does the aerodynamic center of a lifting surface typically move when geometric sweepback is increased?
A Forward toward the leading edge
B Rearward toward the trailing edge
C Outward to the wingtip
D Remains fixed at 25% chord regardless of sweep
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Increasing sweepback shifts the aerodynamic center significantly rearward, which substantially increases the static longitudinal stability margin of the aircraft.
Question 5 • conceptual
Which velocity component is primarily responsible for generating sectional lift on a swept wing?
A The spanwise velocity component parallel to the leading edge
B The normal velocity component perpendicular to the leading edge
C The total freestream velocity magnitude regardless of angle
D The vertical induced downwash velocity
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The independence principle dictates that pressure distribution and lift are determined solely by the velocity component normal to the swept leading edge.
Question 6 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding wing sweep: 1. Sweepback decreases wave drag in the transonic regime. 2. Sweepback increases the lift-curve slope in incompressible flow. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is correct because sweep reduces normal Mach number and wave drag. Statement 2 is incorrect because sweep decreases (does not increase) the lift-curve slope.
Question 7 • statement
Consider the following statements on swept-wing aerodynamics: 1. Spanwise flow transports boundary layer air toward the wingtip. 2. Washout or leading-edge devices are used to mitigate swept-wing tip stall. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. Spanwise pressure gradients cause outward boundary layer flow, and aeronautical engineers employ twist (washout), slats, or fences to counteract tip stall.
Question 8 • numerical
A wing has a sweep angle of $60^{\circ}$ and a baseline unswept lift-curve slope of $6.0 \text{ rad}^{-1}$. What is the resulting swept lift-curve slope in incompressible flow?
A $6.0 \text{ rad}^{-1}$
B $5.2 \text{ rad}^{-1}$
C $3.0 \text{ rad}^{-1}$
D $1.5 \text{ rad}^{-1}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $C_{L_\alpha} = C_{L_\alpha,0} \cos\Lambda$, we get $6.0 \times \cos(60^{\circ}) = 6.0 \times 0.5 = 3.0 \text{ rad}^{-1}$.
Question 9 • numerical
An aircraft operating at Mach $0.8$ has a wing sweep angle of $36.87^{\circ}$ ($\cos\Lambda = 0.8$). What is the normal Mach number $M_n$?
A 0.8
B 0.64
C 1.0
D 0.5
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $M_n = M_\infty \cos\Lambda$, we calculate $M_n = 0.8 \times 0.8 = 0.64$.
Question 10 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Swept wings exhibit reduced lift-curve slope compared to straight wings. Reason (R): Only the velocity component normal to the leading edge contributes effectively to section lift generation.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both assertion and reason are true, and the reduction in lift-curve slope is a direct mathematical consequence of sectional lift depending solely on the normal velocity component ($V_\infty \cos\Lambda$).
Question 11 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Sweptback wings are prone to severe tip stall at high angles of attack. Reason (R): Spanwise boundary layer migration carries low-energy air toward the wing root.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Assertion (A) is true (sweptback wings are prone to tip stall). Reason (R) is false because spanwise flow actually carries boundary layer air toward the wingtip, not the root.
Question 12 • conceptual
Which aerodynamic device is frequently installed on swept wings to prevent spanwise boundary layer flow separation?
A Gurney flap
B Wing fence
C Vane-type pitot tube
D Vortex generator on the empennage
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Wing fences are vertical plates mounted chordwise on swept wings to block spanwise boundary layer flow and prevent tip stall propagation.

The primary aerodynamic penalty of increasing wing sweepback angle is:
A Increase in transonic wave drag
B Reduction in lift-curve slope
C Elimination of pitching moment
D Increase in root bending relief
Rationale:
The primary penalty is the reduction of lift-curve slope proportional to $\cos\Lambda$, requiring higher angles of attack for equivalent lift.
Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
If an aircraft wing has a sweep angle of $45^{\circ}$ and a freestream Mach number of $0.85$, what is the normal Mach number?
A 0.85
B 0.60
C 0.70
D 0.45
Rationale:
$M_n = 0.85 \times \cos(45^{\circ}) = 0.85 \times 0.7071 \approx 0.60$. This keeps the normal flow well below critical Mach.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the primary cause of 'pitch-up' in swept-wing aircraft during high-angle-of-attack maneuvers?
A Downwash hitting the vertical stabilizer
B Premature tip stall causing forward shift of center of pressure
C Elevator aerodynamic flutter
D Fuel transfer to forward tanks
Rationale:
Premature tip stall on swept wings unloads the rear portion of the wing, causing the center of lift to shift forward and produce an uncommanded nose-up pitching moment.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
How does wing sweep affect the effective aspect ratio in terms of induced drag?
A It increases effective aspect ratio and reduces induced drag factor
B It has no effect on induced drag
C It decreases effective aspect ratio
D It converts all induced drag to wave drag
Rationale:
Geometric sweep effectively increases the spanwise extent relative to chord, increasing effective aspect ratio and reducing induced drag at moderate lifts.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
In aircraft design, forward sweep (anhedral or forward-swept wings) results in:
A Increased tip stall tendencies
B Inboard boundary layer migration and delayed tip stall
C Infinite lift-curve slope
D Zero pitching moment
Rationale:
Forward-swept wings cause boundary layer air to flow inward toward the root, keeping tips unstalled at high angles of attack, though introducing severe aeroelastic divergence issues.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
According to simple sweep theory, if $\Lambda = 60^{\circ}$, what fraction of the unswept lift-curve slope remains?
A 100%
B 70.7%
C 50.0%
D 25.0%
Rationale:
$\cos(60^{\circ}) = 0.5$, meaning exactly 50% of the unswept lift-curve slope remains.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the purpose of incorporating wing twist (washout) on a sweptback wing?
A To increase root incidence angle relative to the tip, delaying tip stall
B To increase supersonic wave drag
C To make the wing structurally uniform
D To eliminate ground effect
Rationale:
Washout sets the wingtip at a lower angle of incidence than the root, ensuring the root stalls first and preventing dangerous pitch-up.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
The aerodynamic center shift due to sweepback primarily impacts which stability derivative?
A Roll damping ($C_{l_p}$)
B Static longitudinal stability ($C_{m_a}$)
C Directional stability ($C_{n_\beta}$)
D Yaw due to roll ($C_{n_r}$)
Rationale:
A rearward shift in the aerodynamic center directly increases static longitudinal stability ($C_{m_a}$), requiring adjustments to tail volume.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
Which regulatory body mandates airworthiness compliance regarding stall warning and pitch characteristics for swept-wing aircraft?
A International Maritime Organization
B DGCA and ICAO Airworthiness Annexes
C Federal Communications Commission
D World Meteorological Organization
Rationale:
DGCA (India) and ICAO Annex 8 govern airworthiness standards, requiring rigorous flight testing of stall warning systems on swept-wing aircraft.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
What happens to the drag polar curve when a wing is swept back at subsonic speeds?
A Profile drag decreases while induced drag increases significantly due to loss of lift
B Zero-lift drag coefficient increases slightly due to increased wetted area, but compressible wave drag is delayed
C Total drag becomes zero
D Drag is unaffected by sweep
Rationale:
Sweep increases surface area (wetted area), slightly raising skin friction drag, but substantially delays and reduces transonic wave drag.
