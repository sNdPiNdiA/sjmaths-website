Primary & secondary controls

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Flight data recorders monitor control surface positions, control column forces, and hydraulic actuator pressures.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Incorrect trim settings or jammed primary controls directly invalidate takeoff performance calculations and V-speeds.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Compliance with DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 mandates rigorous backlash and cable tension inspections.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air safety officers analyze control surface rigging, actuator fatigue, and reverse-connection hazards during maintenance audits.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Primary and secondary flight controls are aerodynamic surfaces that manipulate lift and moment vectors to govern aircraft attitude, trajectory, and energy state: $$M = \bar{q} S \bar{c} C_m$$
  • Physical Meaning: Primary controls manage three rotational axes, while secondary controls optimize lift and drag profiles.
  • Governing Law: Newton's Third Law of Motion combined with aerodynamic pressure integration across cambered profiles.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid exclusively within continuum aerodynamics where mean free path is negligible relative to chord.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\bar{q}$ = Dynamic pressure ($N/m^2$)
  • $S$ = Wing reference planform area ($m^2$)
  • $\bar{c}$ = Mean aerodynamic chord ($m$)
  • $C_m$ = Pitching moment coefficient (Dimensionless)
  • $\delta_e$ = Elevator deflection angle ($rad$ or $deg$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Control surface deflection alters local streamline curvature, changing local flow velocity and static pressure via molecular momentum exchange.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Pressure differentials across upper and lower surfaces generate net aerodynamic forces and resulting hinge moments.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: The resulting moments rotate the airframe about its center of gravity in pitch, roll, and yaw axes.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Express total aerodynamic hinge moment as an integral of pressure and shear stress distributions.

$$H = \int_0^c (p_l - p_u) x \, dx$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Apply thin airfoil theory assumptions where perturbation velocities are proportional to camber and control surface deflection.

$$\alpha_{eff} = \alpha + \tau \delta_f$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Integrate pressure distributions over the deflected flap chord to obtain control effectiveness derivatives.

$$C_{h\delta} = \frac{\partial C_h}{\partial \delta}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Establish final hinge moment coefficient equation utilized in actuator sizing and irreversible control design.

$$C_h = C_{h0} + C_{h\alpha} \alpha + C_{h\delta} \delta$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Deflection angles are dimensionless $[M^0 L^0 T^0]$, while hinge moments possess dimensions of torque $[M L^2 T^{-2}]$.
  • SI Unit: Hinge moments in Newton-meters ($N\cdot m$) and deflections in radians or degrees.
  • CGS Unit: Hinge moments in dyne-centimeters ($dyne\cdot cm$) with conversion factor $1 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m} = 10^7 \text{ dyne}\cdot\text{cm}$.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Foot-pounds ($ft\cdot lb$), inch-pounds ($in\cdot lb$), and pound-force feet ($lbf\cdot ft$).

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'B', Part III and ICAO Annex 8 mandate strict inspection of flight control rigging, cable tensions, and limit stops.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Hydraulic actuators, cable quadrants, push-pull rods, and autopilot servos depend on uninhibited control surface mechanics.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: Flight Alaska Airlines 261 suffered catastrophic loss of control due to unlubricated jackscrew assembly failure in the horizontal stabilizer trim system.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Flaps as Primary Controls

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming flaps are primary flight controls because they alter lift significantly.

✅ UPSC Truth: Flaps are secondary (high-lift) controls; primary controls govern rotation about the three principal axes.

Trap 2: Adverse Yaw Mechanics

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing adverse yaw is caused by rudder deflection rather than aileron deflection.

✅ UPSC Truth: Adverse yaw is caused by differential induced drag generated by downward-deflected ailerons rolling the aircraft.

Trap 3: Stabilizer vs Elevator

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing the stabilator with a standard movable elevator.

✅ UPSC Truth: A stabilator is an all-moving horizontal tail surface equipped with an anti-servo tab for artificial feel.

Trap 4: Trim Tab Operation

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming trim tabs move in the same direction as the desired control surface deflection.

✅ UPSC Truth: Trim tabs move in the opposite direction to aerodynamic forces, deflecting the primary control surface to the neutral position.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

Calculate the hinge moment of an elevator with chord $c_e = 0.8\text{ m}$, span $b_e = 4.0\text{ m}$, dynamic pressure $q = 5000\text{ N/m}^2$, and hinge moment coefficient derivative $C_{h\delta} = -0.4$ for a deflection $\delta_e = 15^\circ$ ($0.2618\text{ rad}$).

Given Flight Parameters:
c_e = 0.8 m, b_e = 4.0 m, S_e = 3.2 m^2, q = 5000 N/m^2, C_{h\delta} = -0.4 / rad, \delta_e = 0.2618 rad
Governing Formula:
$$H = q \cdot S_e \cdot c_e \cdot C_h \quad \text{where} \quad C_h = C_{h\delta} \cdot \delta_e$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute elevator area $S_e = b_e \cdot c_e = 4.0 \times 0.8 = 3.2 \text{ m}^2$. Step 2: Compute hinge moment coefficient $C_h = -0.4 \times 0.2618 = -0.10472$. Step 3: Compute hinge moment $H = 5000 \times 3.2 \times 0.8 \times (-0.10472) = -1340.42 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.
Final Answer: Hinge Moment $H = -1340.42 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ (nose-down restoring moment).
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate hinge moment calculations ensure hydraulic actuator sizing prevents control surface buzz and actuator stall during high-speed dives.

Question 1 • conceptual
Which of the following surfaces is classified as a primary flight control?
A Fowler flap
B Slat
C Elevator
D Trim tab
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The elevator is a primary flight control governing pitch. Flaps, slats, and trim tabs are secondary controls.
Question 2 • conceptual
What aerodynamic phenomenon causes adverse yaw when an aileron is deflected downward?
A Decreased lift and decreased induced drag on that wing
B Increased lift and increased induced drag on that wing
C Boundary layer separation over the leading edge
D Symmetric parasite drag increase
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Downward aileron deflection increases lift, which simultaneously increases induced drag on that wing, yawing the nose away from the turn.
Question 3 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding secondary controls: 1. They are used to adjust performance and reduce pilot operating loads. 2. Slats decrease stalling angle of attack. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is correct. Statement 2 is false because slats increase (extend) the stalling angle of attack by re-energizing the boundary layer.
Question 4 • numerical
An aircraft elevator has a hinge moment coefficient derivative $C_{h\delta} = -0.5 / \text{rad}$. If dynamic pressure is $4000 \text{ N/m}^2$, elevator area is $2.0 \text{ m}^2$, chord is $0.5 \text{ m}$, and deflection is $0.2 \text{ rad}$, what is the hinge moment?
A -200 N.m
B -400 N.m
C -800 N.m
D -1000 N.m
Solution & Technical Rationale:
H = q * S * c * (C_{h\delta} * \delta) = 4000 * 2.0 * 0.5 * (-0.5 * 0.2) = 4000 * (-0.1) = -400 N.m.
Question 5 • assertion-reason
Assertion (A): Anti-servo tabs move in the same direction as the control surface. Reason (R): They provide artificial feel and reduce control sensitivity on all-moving stabilators.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Anti-servo tabs move in the same direction as the trailing edge of the stabilator, increasing control resistance and stability.
Question 6 • conceptual
What is the primary function of a leading-edge slat during high-angle-of-attack flight?
A To increase parasite drag
B To create a slot that ducts high-pressure air to the upper surface, delaying separation
C To decrease effective wing span
D To act as a primary pitch control
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Slats create a high-velocity jet slot over the upper wing surface, energizing the boundary layer and delaying stall.
Question 7 • statement
Consider the following statements on rudder control: 1. Rudder controls yaw about the lateral axis. 2. Rudder is coordinated with ailerons during turns. Which statements are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is false because rudder controls yaw about the vertical axis, not the lateral axis. Statement 2 is correct.
Question 8 • numerical
If the aerodynamic chord of a control surface is $1.0 \text{ m}$, span is $3.0 \text{ m}$, dynamic pressure is $2000 \text{ N/m}^2$, and total hinge moment is $-600 \text{ N/m}$, what is the hinge moment coefficient $C_h$?
A -0.05
B -0.10
C -0.20
D -0.40
Solution & Technical Rationale:
C_h = H / (q * S * c) = -600 / (2000 * 3.0 * 1.0) = -600 / 6000 = -0.10.
Question 9 • conceptual
Frise ailerons are specifically designed to counteract which aerodynamic phenomenon?
A Dutch roll
B Adverse yaw
C Compressibility burble
D Wing flutter
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Frise ailerons project the upward-deflecting aileron's leading edge into the airflow to create form drag, counteracting adverse yaw.
Question 10 • assertion-reason
Assertion (A): Spoilers reduce lift and increase drag. Reason (R): Spoilers disrupt upper surface boundary layer flow when deployed.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Deploying spoilers creates controlled flow separation, dumping lift and increasing drag for descent and landing deceleration.
Question 11 • conceptual
Which aircraft axis is controlled exclusively by the elevator in conventional configurations?
A Longitudinal axis (Roll)
B Lateral axis (Pitch)
C Normal axis (Yaw)
D Thrust axis
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The elevator controls pitch about the lateral (transverse) axis.
Question 12 • conceptual
What is the primary design purpose of mass balancing on primary flight control surfaces?
A To reduce hydraulic pressure requirements
B To prevent catastrophic control surface flutter
C To increase stick force per g
D To eliminate adverse yaw entirely
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Mass balances shift the center of gravity forward of the hinge line, preventing high-frequency aeroelastic flutter.

A Spoiler
B Elevator

Adverse yaw is most pronounced under which flight condition?
A High-speed cruise
B Low-speed roll initiation with large aileron deflection
C Steady-state symmetric flight
D Level pitching maneuvers
Rationale:
Adverse yaw occurs predominantly when initiating rolls at lower speeds due to asymmetric induced drag.

What is the primary role of a balance tab?
A To augment pilot control forces by moving in the opposite direction of the primary control
B To increase control sensitivity
C To act as a primary lift device
D To dump wing lift upon landing
Rationale:
Balance tabs deflect opposite to the control surface, generating aerodynamic moments that assist pilot actuation.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
Which regulatory body issues airworthiness directives regarding flight control cable tension and rigging?
A IATA
B DGCA / FAA
C WMO
D WTO
Rationale:
National civil aviation authorities like DGCA and FAA mandate compliance via CARs and Airworthiness Directives.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the function of a Mach trimmer in high-speed transonic flight?
A Prevents Mach tuck by automatically applying elevator corrective inputs
B Controls fuel transfer
C Automatically adjusts cabin pressurization
D Deploys speed brakes
Rationale:
Mach trimmers compensate for center-of-pressure rearward shift in transonic flight by pitching the nose up.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
Which secondary control is deployed symmetrically on both wings to act as a speed brake and lift dumper?
A Aileron
B Spoiler
C Slat
D Elevator
Rationale:
Spoilers disrupt lift and increase drag when deployed symmetrically.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
If an aircraft experiences control surface flutter, what is the primary engineering remedy?
A Increasing flight speed
B Adding mass balance forward of the hinge line
C Removing all trim tabs
D Decreasing structural stiffness
Rationale:
Forward mass balancing shifts the center of gravity relative to the hinge line, damping aeroelastic flutter modes.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
What characterizes a differential aileron system?
A Upward aileron deflects through a larger angle than downward aileron
B Downward aileron deflects through a larger angle than upward aileron
C Both ailerons deflect downward
D Ailerons operate independently of flight speed
Rationale:
Differential ailerons deflect upward more than downward, reducing the induced drag difference and mitigating adverse yaw.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
In air safety investigations of control jams, what is a primary forensic check?
A Passenger seat pitch
B Cable tension, foreign object debris (FOD), and pulley alignment
C Galley oven electrical load
D Lavatory water pressure
Rationale:
Control rigging inspections focus heavily on cable tension, mechanical interference, and FOD in pulley wells.
