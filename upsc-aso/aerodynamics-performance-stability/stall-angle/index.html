Stall, surge & rotating stall

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Angle of Attack (AoA) vanes, stick shakers, stall warning computers, and engine EEC/FADEC vibration sensors rely on stall parameter tracking.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Determines aircraft takeoff decision speeds ($V_1, V_r, V_2$), stall warning margins, and compressor operating lines.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Governed by DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'F', Part VII and ICAO Annex 8 Airworthiness of Aircraft standards.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: DGCA ASOs inspect flight data recorder (FDR) exceedances, compressor blade distress, and stall warning system activations during incident probes.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Aerodynamic stall is the condition where an airfoil exceeds its critical angle of attack ($\alpha_{crit}$), causing boundary layer separation and a sudden loss of lift. Compressor surge is a complete breakdown of axial compressor operation, while rotating stall is a localized flow disruption propagating circumferentially.
  • Physical Meaning: Stall destroys lifting capability, whereas compressor surge induces violent engine flow reversal and thrust collapse.
  • Governing Law: Governed by the Navier-Stokes equations governing boundary layer separation and adverse pressure gradients.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid entirely within continuum fluid mechanics where Knudsen number $Kn \ll 0.01$.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\alpha$ = Angle of Attack (Degrees or Radians)
  • $\alpha_{crit}$ = Critical Angle of Attack (Degrees)
  • $C_L$ = Coefficient of Lift (Dimensionless)
  • $C_{D}$ = Coefficient of Drag (Dimensionless)
  • $N_{cor}$ = Corrected Compressor Rotational Speed (RPM)
  • $\Pi_c$ = Compressor Total Pressure Ratio (Dimensionless)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: High adverse pressure gradients decelerate fluid parcels in the boundary layer until kinetic energy drops to zero.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Viscous shear forces dominate over momentum transfer, forcing the boundary layer to detach from the solid contour.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Detached turbulent wakes create massive pressure drag and catastrophic loss of lift on wings or compressor blades.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Boundary layer momentum integral equation for turbulent separation.

$$\frac{d\theta}{dx} + \theta \frac{2+\{H\}}{U_e} \frac{dU_e}{dx} = \frac{C_f}{2}$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Shape factor $H = \delta^* / \theta$ exceeds critical threshold $H_{crit} \approx 2.4$ for laminar and $1.8$ for turbulent flows.

$$H \ge H_{crit}$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Setting wall shear stress to zero identifies the exact separation point.

$$\left. \mu \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} \right|_{y=0} = 0$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Onset of stall occurs when pressure gradient matches maximum static pressure recovery.

$$\left( \frac{dp}{dx} \right)_{sep} > \frac{1}{2} \rho U_e^2$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: $[M^0 L^0 T^0]$ for angles, coefficients, and pressure ratios.
  • SI Unit: Dimensionless ratios for $C_L$, $C_D$, and pressure ratio $\Pi_c$.
  • CGS Unit: Identical dimensionless forms as SI standards.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Pressure ratios in psi/atm; rotational speed in corrected RPM ($RPM/\sqrt{\theta}$).

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Compliance mandated by DGCA CAR Section 6 (Flight Data Recorders) and ICAO Annex 6 Part I (Commercial Air Transport).
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Directly impacts bleed air control valves, variable stator vanes (VSV), and electronic engine controllers (EEC).

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Stall Speed Stays Constant with Weight

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming stall speed is fixed regardless of aircraft gross weight or load factor.

✅ UPSC Truth: Stall speed increases proportionally with the square root of the load factor ($V_s = V_{s0} \sqrt{n}$).

Trap 2: Rotating Stall and Surge Are Identical

❌ Examiner Trap: Treating rotating stall and full compressor surge as interchangeable aerodynamic phenomena.

✅ UPSC Truth: Rotating stall is a localized circumferential flow breakdown, whereas surge is a total annulus-wide mass flow reversal.

Trap 3: Altitude Decreases True Stall Speed

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing that flying at higher altitudes reduces the indicated airspeed required for a stall.

✅ UPSC Truth: Indicated stall airspeed remains nearly constant with altitude, while true airspeed increases significantly.

Trap 4: Stall Only Occurs at Low Speeds

❌ Examiner Trap: Concluding that aerodynamic stalls can only happen when the aircraft is flying slowly.

✅ UPSC Truth: High-speed stalls occur when pulling excessive $g$-force at high velocities, exceeding $\alpha_{crit}$ dynamically.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An aircraft with a reference stall speed of $100$ knots CAS at a maximum weight of $70,000\text{ kg}$ executes a coordinated $60^{\circ}$ banked turn ($n = 2$). Calculate the accelerated stall speed.

Given Flight Parameters:
Reference stall speed $V_{s} = 100\text{ knots}$; Bank angle $\phi = 60^{\circ}$; Load factor $n = \frac{1}{\cos(60^{\circ})} = 2.0$.
Governing Formula:
$$V_{s,accel} = V_s \sqrt{n}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute the load factor $n = \frac{1}{\cos(60^{\circ})} = 2.0$. Step 2: Substitute values into the accelerated stall speed equation: $V_{s,accel} = 100 \times \sqrt{2.0}$. Step 3: Evaluate the numerical outcome: $V_{s,accel} = 100 \times 1.414 = 141.4\text{ knots}$.
Final Answer: $141.4 knots CAS$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Pilots and ASOs must recognize that high bank angle maneuvers dramatically elevate stall speeds, requiring strict adherence to operational maneuvering speed ($V_a$) limits.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary physical trigger for an aerodynamic stall on a symmetrical airfoil?
A Exceeding the critical angle of attack where adverse pressure gradients cause boundary layer separation
B Reaching supersonic local flow velocity over the upper camber
C Depleting the fuel supply to the boundary layer suction mechanism
D Excessive Reynolds number reducing skin friction drag to zero
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Stall is fundamentally caused by the boundary layer separating from the airfoil surface when the angle of attack exceeds $\alpha_{crit}$ due to severe adverse pressure gradients.
Question 2 • conceptual
Which phenomenon precedes a full compressor surge in an axial-flow gas turbine engine?
A Choked nozzle flow
B Rotating stall cells propagating around the compressor annulus
C Turbine blade thermal creep
D Inter-turbine duct overpressure
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Rotating stall typically develops first in compressor stages, creating local flow blockages that eventually trigger a complete compressor surge.
Question 3 • conceptual
How does an increase in aircraft weight affect its calibrated stall speed ($V_{s}$)?
A V_s decreases proportionally with weight reduction
B V_s remains completely unaffected by gross weight
C V_s increases proportionally with the square root of weight ratio
D V_s varies inversely with air density squared
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Stall speed scales with the square root of weight because lift must equal weight at stall ($L = W = \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 S C_{Lmax}$).
Question 4 • conceptual
What distinguishes compressor surge from rotating stall?
A Surge involves localized flow disruption while rotating stall is axisymmetric
B Surge is a complete breakdown of compressor operation with reverse mass flow, whereas rotating stall involves local blade passage flow separation
C Rotating stall only occurs in centrifugal compressors while surge occurs in axial compressors
D Surge produces steady thrust whereas rotating stall causes immediate engine flameout
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Compressor surge affects the entire compressor annulus with severe pressure fluctuations and mass flow reversal, unlike localized rotating stall.
Question 5 • conceptual
During a high-altitude stall investigation, an ASO notes that true airspeed (TAS) at stall was much higher than indicated airspeed (IAS). Why?
A Pitot tubes freeze at high altitudes
B Air density decreases, requiring higher true speed to generate identical dynamic pressure
C Compressibility effects increase the camber of the wing
D Engine bleed air draws energy away from the boundary layer
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Stall depends on dynamic pressure ($q = \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$). At high altitude, low $\rho$ demands higher TAS to achieve the stalling dynamic pressure.
Question 6 • conceptual
Which cockpit instrument directly warns pilots of an impending aerodynamic stall?
A Machmeter
B Vertical Speed Indicator
C Stick shaker and stall warning horn driven by AoA sensors
D Total Air Temperature gauge
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Modern aircraft utilize AoA vanes coupled with stall warning computers to activate stick shakers and audible horns prior to stall.
Question 7 • conceptual
What is the primary function of Variable Stator Vanes (VSV) in modern turbofan engines?
A To control bypass ratio at high Mach numbers
B To adjust blade incidence angles and prevent compressor stall across various RPM settings
C To regulate turbine entry temperature
D To increase fuel atomization efficiency
Solution & Technical Rationale:
VSVs optimize blade incidence angles across off-design operating speeds, widening the compressor surge margin.
Question 8 • conceptual
If an aircraft maintains a constant load factor of $n = 4$, its accelerated stall speed increases by what factor compared to $1g$ flight?
A 2.0
B 4.0
C 1.414
D 8.0
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Since $V_{s,accel} = V_s \sqrt{n}$, substituting $n = 4$ gives $\sqrt{4} = 2.0$, doubling the stall speed.
Question 9 • conceptual
What aerodynamic phenomenon occurs immediately after an airfoil stalls?
A Increase in lift coefficient and decrease in drag coefficient
B Sudden drop in lift coefficient and sharp increase in pressure drag
C Linear increase in lift-to-drag ratio
D Complete cessation of skin friction
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Separation destroys smooth pressure recovery, causing a sharp drop in $C_L$ and a massive surge in form drag ($C_D$).
Question 10 • conceptual
Which DGCA regulatory document governs airworthiness standards for transport category aircraft stall characteristics?
A DGCA CAR Section 2 (Airworthiness)
B DGCA CAR Section 5 (Air Safety)
C CAR Section 4 (Licensing)
D ICAO Annex 14 (Aerodromes)
Solution & Technical Rationale:
DGCA CAR Section 2 sets forth airworthiness standards, referencing FAA/EASA design codes like FAR 25 / CS 25 regarding stall behavior.
Question 11 • conceptual
How do vortex generators prevent or delay aerodynamic stall?
A By cooling the boundary layer with ambient air
B By injecting high-momentum external air into the sluggish boundary layer
C By increasing the wing surface area
D By eliminating skin friction completely
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Vortex generators form streamwise vortices that mix high-momentum free-stream air into the boundary layer, delaying separation.
Question 12 • conceptual
What is the physical cause of a compressor stall during rapid throttle acceleration?
A Fuel flow increases faster than compressor airflow response, causing rear stages to choke and front stages to stall
B Turbine blades melt due to over-temperature
C Intake pressure drops to zero instantly
D Bypass ratio exceeds 10:1
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Rapid fuel addition increases backpressure from the combustion chamber faster than the compressor spool accelerates, precipitating front-stage stall.

Rationale:

A Loud popping/banging noises, rapid EGT rise, and sudden loss of thrust
B Gradual decrease in oil pressure
C Cabin depressurization
D Autopilot disconnect without warning
Rationale:
Compressor surges generate characteristic loud bangs, flame bursts from the intake or exhaust, rapid EGT spikes, and immediate thrust loss.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
If an aircraft's gross weight decreases by $19\%$, by what percentage does its stall speed decrease approximately?
A 10%
B 19%
C 5%
D 38%
Rationale:
Since $V_s \propto \sqrt{W}$, a $19\%$ weight reduction means $\sqrt{0.81} = 0.9$, representing a $10\%$ reduction in stall speed.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
During rotating stall, at what speed do the stall cells typically propagate relative to the rotor blades?
A Equal to rotor speed
B 30% to 50% of rotor speed
C Exactly 100% supersonic speed
D Zero speed (stationary)
Rationale:
Stall cells in rotating stall propagate circumferentially around the compressor annulus at roughly half (30%-50%) of the rotor rotational speed.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
What design feature on swept-wing aircraft helps prevent tip stall at high angles of attack?
A Geometric washout and leading-edge slats
B Increased dihedral angle
C Thicker wing root profiles
D Elimination of ailerons
Rationale:
Swept wings tend to experience root-to-tip spanwise boundary layer flow. Washout and slats ensure root stall occurs first for lateral control retention.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
How do bleed valves protect a multi-stage axial compressor from surging during start-up?
A By bleeding air from intermediate stages to match front and rear mass flows
B By venting fuel overboard
C By cooling turbine blades
D By increasing exhaust nozzle area
Rationale:
Interstage bleed valves dump excess air from front stages during low-speed operation, preventing front-stage choking and rear-stage stall.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the effect of ice accretion on aircraft wings regarding stall speed?
A Increases stall speed and lowers critical angle of attack
B Decreases stall speed due to rough surface lift augmentation
C Has no effect on stall characteristics
D Eliminates boundary layer separation entirely
Rationale:
Ice roughness trips boundary layers prematurely and alters airfoil camber, raising stall speed and reducing $\alpha_{crit}$.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
In flight testing for aircraft certification under DGCA/ICAO rules, how is stall speed ($V_{sr}$) defined?
A Reference stall speed representing the 1g stall speed
B Maximum dive speed
C Maneuvering speed
D Minimum control speed on ground
Rationale:
$V_{sr}$ is the reference stall speed defined as the minimum calibrated airspeed obtained in the stall demonstration.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
What is 'deep stall' characteristic of T-tail aircraft configurations?
A A stable stalled condition where the wake blankets the horizontal stabilizer, making pitch recovery impossible
B Engine compressor surge at maximum altitude
C Supersonic shockwave separation at the wing root
D Hydraulic actuator failure during landing gear extension
Rationale:
In a deep stall, the pitched-up wing wake blankets the high-mounted T-tail elevator, rendering nose-down pitch control ineffective.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
Which parameter is most critical for a DGCA Air Safety Officer to analyze when reviewing an uncontained engine failure involving surge?
A FADEC fault logs and engine vibration historical data
B Cabin crew seating configurations
C Passenger manifest weights
D Runway lighting intensity
Rationale:
FADEC logs and vibration trends reveal pre-existing compressor instability, surge margins, and mechanical distress preceding failure.
