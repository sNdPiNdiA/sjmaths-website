Damped vibration – under/over/critical damping

Day 3 of 129 Engineering Fundamentals Phase 1: Foundations 60-90 min Master Cycle

1. Understand (5–10 min)

In mechanical, structural, and aeronautical engineering, dynamic systems subjected to restoring forces undergo vibrational motion. Real physical systems invariably experience resistive forces—termed damping forces—which dissipate mechanical energy as thermal or acoustic energy. The study of single-degree-of-freedom (SDOF) viscous damped systems forms the foundational cornerstone of structural dynamics, flight control stability analysis, and aeroelasticity.

The standard second-order linear homogeneous differential equation governing a single-degree-of-freedom viscous damped system is defined as:

$$ m \frac{d^2 x}{dt^2} + c \frac{dx}{dt} + k x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad m \ddot{x} + c \dot{x} + k x = 0 $$

where $m \in (0, \infty)$ represents the system mass ($\text{kg}$), $c \in [0, \infty)$ denotes the viscous damping coefficient ($\text{N}\cdot\text{s/m}$), $k \in (0, \infty)$ is the spring stiffness ($\text{N/m}$), and $x(t)$ represents the translational displacement ($\text{m}$) as a function of time $t \ge 0$.

Dividing the differential equation by mass $m$ yields the canonical standard form:

$$ \ddot{x} + 2\zeta \omega_n \dot{x} + \omega_n^2 x = 0 $$

The primary parameters and domain bounds governing the dynamics are strictly defined as follows:

  • Undamped Natural Frequency ($\omega_n$): $\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} \in (0, \infty) \quad [\text{rad/s}]$
  • Critical Damping Coefficient ($c_c$): $c_c = 2 m \omega_n = 2 \sqrt{k m} \in (0, \infty) \quad [\text{N}\cdot\text{s/m}]$
  • Damping Ratio ($\zeta$): $\zeta = \frac{c}{c_c} = \frac{c}{2 m \omega_n} = \frac{c}{2\sqrt{k m}} \in [0, \infty) \quad [\text{dimensionless}]$

Based on the quantitative value of the damping ratio $\zeta$, the domain of dynamic behavior is partitioned into three mutually exclusive classical regimes:

  1. Overdamped Regime ($\zeta > 1$ or $c > c_c$): Non-oscillatory decay governed by two distinct real negative characteristic roots.
  2. Critically Damped Regime ($\zeta = 1$ or $c = c_c$): Non-oscillatory decay featuring repeated real negative roots; represents the boundary regime exhibiting the fastest return to static equilibrium without overshoot.
  3. Underdamped Regime ($0 \le \zeta < 1$ or $c < c_c$): Oscillatory transient response with exponentially decaying amplitude, governed by a pair of complex conjugate characteristic roots with negative real parts.

2. Learn the Concept (15–20 min)

To fully comprehend the dynamic behavior of damped single-degree-of-freedom systems, we establish the solution to the canonical homogeneous differential equation $\ddot{x} + 2\zeta \omega_n \dot{x} + \omega_n^2 x = 0$ using the characteristic ansatz $x(t) = e^{s t}$.

Substituting $x(t) = e^{s t}$ into the canonical equation yields the characteristic algebraic equation:

$$ s^2 + 2\zeta \omega_n s + \omega_n^2 = 0 $$

Applying the quadratic formula yields the characteristic roots $s_{1,2}$:

$$ s_{1,2} = \frac{-2\zeta \omega_n \pm \sqrt{4\zeta^2 \omega_n^2 - 4\omega_n^2}}{2} = -\zeta \omega_n \pm \omega_n \sqrt{\zeta^2 - 1} $$
$$ s_{1,2} = -\zeta \omega_n \pm \omega_n \sqrt{\zeta^2 - 1} $$ $$ \omega_d = \omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2} \quad (0 \le \zeta < 1) $$ $$ \delta = \ln\left(\frac{x(t)}{x(t + T_d)}\right) = \zeta \omega_n T_d = \frac{2\pi \zeta}{\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}} $$

1. Detailed Analysis of Damping Regimes

A. Overdamped Case ($\zeta > 1$)

When $\zeta > 1$, the term under the radical $\sqrt{\zeta^2 - 1}$ is positive and real. The roots $s_1$ and $s_2$ are distinct, real, and strictly negative:

$$ s_1 = -\zeta \omega_n + \omega_n \sqrt{\zeta^2 - 1} < 0, \quad s_2 = -\zeta \omega_n - \omega_n \sqrt{\zeta^2 - 1} < 0 $$

The general solution for displacement is given by:

$$ x(t) = A_1 e^{s_1 t} + A_2 e^{s_2 t} $$

Given initial conditions $x(0) = x_0$ and $\dot{x}(0) = v_0$, the constants $A_1$ and $A_2$ are determined by solving the system:

$$ A_1 + A_2 = x_0 \quad \text{and} \quad s_1 A_1 + s_2 A_2 = v_0 $$ $$ A_1 = \frac{v_0 - s_2 x_0}{s_1 - s_2}, \quad A_2 = \frac{s_1 x_0 - v_0}{s_1 - s_2} $$

Physical Interpretation: The system does not undergo periodic oscillation. If initial velocity $v_0$ is directed away from equilibrium with sufficient magnitude, the trajectory may cross the equilibrium position ($x = 0$) at most once before decaying asymptotically to zero.

B. Critically Damped Case ($\zeta = 1$)

When $\zeta = 1$, the discriminant vanishes ($\zeta^2 - 1 = 0$), yielding identical repeated real roots:

$$ s_1 = s_2 = -\omega_n $$

To construct two linearly independent solutions, we apply reduction of order, yielding the general displacement equation:

$$ x(t) = (A_1 + A_2 t) e^{-\omega_n t} $$

Applying initial conditions $x(0) = x_0$ and $\dot{x}(0) = v_0$ gives:

$$ A_1 = x_0, \quad A_2 = v_0 + \omega_n x_0 $$ $$ x(t) = \left[ x_0 + (v_0 + \omega_n x_0) t \right] e^{-\omega_n t} $$

Peak Time Derivative: For zero initial displacement ($x_0 = 0$) and initial impulse velocity $v_0$, the time $t_{max}$ at which maximum displacement occurs is found by setting $\dot{x}(t) = 0$:

$$ \dot{x}(t) = v_0 e^{-\omega_n t} - \omega_n v_0 t e^{-\omega_n t} = v_0 (1 - \omega_n t) e^{-\omega_n t} = 0 \implies t_{max} = \frac{1}{\omega_n} $$

The maximum displacement achieved under critical damping is:

$$ x_{max} = x(t_{max}) = v_0 \left(\frac{1}{\omega_n}\right) e^{-1} = \frac{v_0}{\omega_n e} $$

C. Underdamped Case ($0 \le \zeta < 1$)

When $0 \le \zeta < 1$, $\sqrt{\zeta^2 - 1} = \sqrt{-(1 - \zeta^2)} = i \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2} = i \frac{\omega_d}{\omega_n}$, where $\omega_d$ is the damped natural frequency defined as:

$$ \omega_d = \omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2} $$

The characteristic roots form a complex conjugate pair:

$$ s_{1,2} = -\zeta \omega_n \pm i \omega_d $$

Using Euler's identity ($e^{i \theta} = \cos \theta + i \sin \theta$), the general real-valued solution is:

$$ x(t) = e^{-\zeta \omega_n t} \left( A \cos \omega_d t + B \sin \omega_d t \right) $$

Alternatively expressed in harmonic magnitude-phase form:

$$ x(t) = X e^{-\zeta \omega_n t} \sin(\omega_d t + \phi) $$

where amplitude $X = \sqrt{A^2 + B^2}$ and phase angle $\phi = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)$.

Given initial conditions $x(0) = x_0$ and $\dot{x}(0) = v_0$:

$$ A = x_0, \quad B = \frac{v_0 + \zeta \omega_n x_0}{\omega_d} $$ $$ x(t) = e^{-\zeta \omega_n t} \left[ x_0 \cos \omega_d t + \left( \frac{v_0 + \zeta \omega_n x_0}{\omega_d} \right) \sin \omega_d t \right] $$

2. The Logarithmic Decrement ($\delta$)

The logarithmic decrement measures the rate of decay of peak oscillations in an underdamped system. Let $t_1$ and $t_2 = t_1 + T_d$ be the time instants corresponding to two consecutive positive displacement peaks, where $T_d$ is the damped period of oscillation:

$$ T_d = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_d} = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}} $$

The ratio of two consecutive peak amplitudes $x_1 = x(t_1)$ and $x_2 = x(t_1 + T_d)$ is:

$$ \frac{x_1}{x_2} = \frac{X e^{-\zeta \omega_n t_1} \sin(\omega_d t_1 + \phi)}{X e^{-\zeta \omega_n (t_1 + T_d)} \sin(\omega_d (t_1 + T_d) + \phi)} $$

Since $\omega_d T_d = 2\pi$, $\sin(\omega_d t_1 + \omega_d T_d + \phi) = \sin(\omega_d t_1 + \phi)$. The sinusoidal terms cancel, yielding:

$$ \frac{x_1}{x_2} = \frac{e^{-\zeta \omega_n t_1}}{e^{-\zeta \omega_n t_1} e^{-\zeta \omega_n T_d}} = e^{\zeta \omega_n T_d} $$

Taking the natural logarithm of both sides defines the logarithmic decrement $\delta$:

$$ \delta = \ln\left(\frac{x_1}{x_2}\right) = \zeta \omega_n T_d = \zeta \omega_n \left( \frac{2\pi}{\omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}} \right) = \frac{2\pi \zeta}{\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}} $$

If the amplitude measurement is taken across $n$ complete cycles ($x_0$ to $x_n$):

$$ \frac{x_0}{x_n} = \frac{x_0}{x_1} \cdot \frac{x_1}{x_2} \cdots \frac{x_{n-1}}{x_n} = \left( e^{\delta} \right)^n = e^{n \delta} \implies \delta = \frac{1}{n} \ln\left( \frac{x_0}{x_n} \right) $$

For low damping ($\zeta \ll 1$), $\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2} \approx 1$, leading to the standard small-damping approximation:

$$ \delta \approx 2\pi \zeta $$

3. Energy Dissipation per Cycle

The total mechanical energy $U(t)$ stored in the system at peak displacement is proportional to the square of the amplitude: $U \propto X^2$.

The fractional energy loss per cycle $\frac{\Delta U}{U}$ is derived as:

$$ \frac{\Delta U}{U} = \frac{U_1 - U_2}{U_1} = 1 - \left(\frac{x_2}{x_1}\right)^2 = 1 - \left(e^{-\delta}\right)^2 = 1 - e^{-2\delta} $$

Expanding using Taylor series for small $\delta$:

$$ 1 - e^{-2\delta} \approx 1 - (1 - 2\delta) = 2\delta \approx 4\pi \zeta $$

3. Memorize Important Facts (10 min)

  • Canonical Damped Differential Equation: $\ddot{x} + 2\zeta \omega_n \dot{x} + \omega_n^2 x = 0$
  • Undamped Natural Frequency: $\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$
  • Critical Damping Coefficient: $c_c = 2 m \omega_n = 2 \sqrt{k m}$
  • Damping Ratio: $\zeta = \frac{c}{c_c} = \frac{c}{2 m \omega_n}$
  • Characteristic Roots Equation: $s_{1,2} = -\zeta \omega_n \pm \omega_n \sqrt{\zeta^2 - 1}$
  • Damped Natural Frequency: $\omega_d = \omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2} \quad (0 \le \zeta < 1)$
  • Damped Oscillatory Period: $T_d = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_d} = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}}$
  • Overdamped Solution ($\zeta > 1$): $x(t) = A_1 e^{s_1 t} + A_2 e^{s_2 t}$
  • Critically Damped Solution ($\zeta = 1$): $x(t) = (A_1 + A_2 t) e^{-\omega_n t}$
  • Peak Time for Critical Damping ($x_0 = 0$): $t_{max} = \frac{1}{\omega_n}, \quad x_{max} = \frac{v_0}{\omega_n e}$
  • Underdamped Solution ($\zeta < 1$): $x(t) = e^{-\zeta \omega_n t} \left[ x_0 \cos \omega_d t + \left(\frac{v_0 + \zeta \omega_n x_0}{\omega_d}\right) \sin \omega_d t \right]$
  • Logarithmic Decrement Exact Identity: $\delta = \frac{1}{n} \ln\left(\frac{x_0}{x_n}\right) = \frac{2\pi \zeta}{\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}}$
  • Damping Ratio from Logarithmic Decrement: $\zeta = \frac{\delta}{\sqrt{4\pi^2 + \delta^2}}$
  • Small Damping Approximations ($\zeta \ll 0.2$): $\delta \approx 2\pi \zeta \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{\Delta U}{U} \approx 2\delta \approx 4\pi \zeta$
  • System Quality Factor ($Q$): $Q = \frac{1}{2\zeta} = \frac{\pi}{\delta}$

4. Aviation Application (5–10 min)

Damped vibrations and transient response characteristics play a vital role in aircraft design, certification, flight control engineering, and accident investigations monitored by aviation safety agencies (such as DGCA, FAA, EASA, and NTSB):

1. Landing Gear Oleo-Pneumatic Shock Absorber Tuning

Aircraft landing gear struts combine compressed air (spring stiffness $k$) and hydraulic oil metered through orifices (viscous damping $c$). To absorb touchdown kinetic energy without severe rebound or structural overload, oleo struts are designed near critical damping ($\zeta \approx 0.8 - 1.0$). If damping is underdamped ($\zeta < 0.7$), the aircraft undergoes unwanted oscillations upon touchdown, increasing fatigue stress on landing gear attachments. If heavily overdamped ($\zeta > 1.2$), the shock strut acts rigid upon impact, transmitting excessive force to the airframe structure.

2. Flight Control System Surface Dynamics & Flutter Suppression

Control surfaces (elevators, ailerons, rudders) integrated with hydraulic actuators and digital flight control systems (DFCS) are modeled as second-order damped systems. Hydraulic control valves provide damping to prevent aeroelastic flutter and high-frequency "buzz". Control surface dampers are typically calibrated to $\zeta \approx 0.6 - 0.7$, providing a rapid settling time with less than $5\%$ overshoot, ensuring stable pilot command input response.

3. Aircraft Dynamic Stability: Dutch Roll and Short-Period Oscillations

In atmospheric flight dynamics, lateral-directional dynamic stability involves the Dutch roll mode (coupled roll-yaw oscillation). Airworthiness regulations (e.g., CS-25 / FAR 25.181) mandate strict minimum damping ratio thresholds ($\zeta \ge 0.04$ or $\zeta \ge 0.08$ depending on flight phase) for Dutch roll modes. Yaw dampers are automated flight control systems designed to add artificial damping $\Delta c$ to elevate the effective $\zeta$ above regulatory safety standards.

4. Flight Instrumentation & Sensor Response

Airborne sensors such as accelerometers, rate gyroscopes, and Angle-of-Attack (AoA) transmitters are continuous second-order dynamic systems. Tuning sensor damping ratio to $\zeta \approx 0.707$ ($1/\sqrt{2}$) provides optimal bandwidth, maximally flat magnitude frequency response, minimal phase distortion, and rapid transient settling time without spurious resonance spikes.

5. Air Safety & Crash Investigation Analysis

Air safety investigators analyzing Flight Data Recorders (FDR) assess structural impulse response tails (e.g., turbulence impacts, heavy landing events, structural failures) using logarithmic decrement formulas $\delta = \frac{1}{n} \ln(x_0/x_n)$ to calculate effective structural damping $\zeta$ and diagnose structural damage, bolt loosening, fluid leakage in hydraulic dampers, or skin delamination in composite aeroelastic structures.

5. UPSC ASO Focus (10 min)

  • Frequently Asked Facts & Formulas:
    • Damped natural frequency equation: $\omega_d = \omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$. Note that $\omega_d$ is ALWAYS strictly less than $\omega_n$ for underdamped systems ($\zeta > 0$).
    • Exact relation for $\zeta$ given $\delta$: $\zeta = \frac{\delta}{\sqrt{4\pi^2 + \delta^2}}$. Candidates frequently forget the $4\pi^2$ in the denominator under time pressure.
    • Critically damped peak displacement time: $t_{max} = \frac{1}{\omega_n}$ for zero initial displacement $x(0)=0$. Maximum displacement $x_{max} = \frac{v_0}{\omega_n e} \approx 0.368 \frac{v_0}{\omega_n}$.
    • Ratio of $n$ consecutive cycles peak displacements: $\frac{x_0}{x_n} = e^{n \delta}$.
    • In the complex $s$-plane, characteristic roots $s_{1,2} = -\zeta \omega_n \pm i \omega_d$ lie on a circle of radius $\omega_n$ centered at origin for $0 \le \zeta \le 1$. Angle with imaginary axis is $\theta = \sin^{-1}(\zeta)$.
  • Conceptual Traps & Pitfalls:
    • Confusing $\omega_d$ with $\omega_n$: Calculations of damped period $T_d$ must use $\omega_d = \frac{2\pi}{T_d}$, NOT $\omega_n$.
    • Critical Damping Roots Trap: Repeating roots at $s = -\omega_n$ mean the solution is $x(t) = (A + Bt)e^{-\omega_n t}$, NOT $(A + B)e^{-\omega_n t}$. Forgetting the factor $t$ leads to completely wrong initial value solutions.
    • Overdamped Response Fallacy: Overdamped systems do NOT oscillate, but they CAN cross equilibrium ($x = 0$) exactly ONCE if initial position and initial velocity have opposite signs and $|v_0| > |s_2 x_0|$. Candidates often mistakenly believe overdamped systems NEVER cross $x=0$.
    • Linear vs Logarithmic Decay: Damped amplitudes decay exponentially ($e^{-\zeta \omega_n t}$), meaning the ratio of consecutive amplitudes is constant, NOT the linear difference between them.

6. Practice Questions (Exhaustive UPSC ASO MCQs)

Q1. A mechanical vibration absorber attached to an aircraft instrument panel has a mass $m = 2\text{ kg}$, a spring stiffness $k = 800\text{ N/m}$, and a viscous damping coefficient $c = 16\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$. Determine the damping ratio $\zeta$ and the damped natural frequency $\omega_d$ of the system.
A) $\zeta = 0.20$, $\omega_d = 19.60\text{ rad/s}$
B) $\zeta = 0.20$, $\omega_d = 20.00\text{ rad/s}$
C) $\zeta = 0.40$, $\omega_d = 18.33\text{ rad/s}$
D) $\zeta = 0.10$, $\omega_d = 19.90\text{ rad/s}$
Detailed Calculation:
1. Calculate undamped natural frequency: $$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{800}{2}} = \sqrt{400} = 20\text{ rad/s}$$ 2. Calculate critical damping coefficient: $$c_c = 2 m \omega_n = 2 \times 2 \times 20 = 80\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$$ 3. Calculate damping ratio $\zeta$: $$\zeta = \frac{c}{c_c} = \frac{16}{80} = 0.20$$ 4. Calculate damped natural frequency $\omega_d$: $$\omega_d = \omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2} = 20 \sqrt{1 - (0.20)^2} = 20 \sqrt{1 - 0.04} = 20 \sqrt{0.96} \approx 20 \times 0.979796 = 19.5959 \approx 19.60\text{ rad/s}$$ Hence, Option A is correct.
Q2. An underdamped dynamic system undergoes free vibration. The initial peak displacement measured is $x_0 = 25\text{ mm}$. After $5$ complete cycles, the peak displacement reduces to $x_5 = 5\text{ mm}$. Calculate the exact logarithmic decrement $\delta$ and the corresponding damping ratio $\zeta$.
A) $\delta = 0.3219$, $\zeta = 0.0512$
B) $\delta = 1.6094$, $\zeta = 0.2485$
C) $\delta = 0.3219$, $\zeta = 0.1024$
D) $\delta = 0.5000$, $\zeta = 0.0796$
Detailed Calculation:
1. Logarithmic decrement over $n = 5$ cycles: $$\delta = \frac{1}{n} \ln\left(\frac{x_0}{x_n}\right) = \frac{1}{5} \ln\left(\frac{25}{5}\right) = \frac{1}{5} \ln(5) \approx \frac{1.609438}{5} = 0.321887 \approx 0.3219$$ 2. Exact damping ratio formula: $$\zeta = \frac{\delta}{\sqrt{4\pi^2 + \delta^2}} = \frac{0.321887}{\sqrt{4\pi^2 + (0.321887)^2}} = \frac{0.321887}{\sqrt{39.4784 + 0.10361}} = \frac{0.321887}{\sqrt{39.582}} = \frac{0.321887}{6.2914} \approx 0.05116 \approx 0.0512$$ Hence, Option A is correct.
Q3. An aircraft landing gear oleo strut is modeled as a critically damped single-degree-of-freedom system with undamped natural frequency $\omega_n = 10\text{ rad/s}$. Upon touchdown at $t = 0$, initial displacement is $x(0) = 0\text{ m}$ and initial downward vertical velocity is $v_0 = 3\text{ m/s}$. Find the maximum stroke displacement $x_{max}$ and the time $t_{max}$ at which it occurs.
A) $t_{max} = 0.10\text{ s}$, $x_{max} = 0.1104\text{ m}$
B) $t_{max} = 0.20\text{ s}$, $x_{max} = 0.3000\text{ m}$
C) $t_{max} = 0.10\text{ s}$, $x_{max} = 0.3000\text{ m}$
D) $t_{max} = 0.05\text{ s}$, $x_{max} = 0.1500\text{ m}$
Detailed Calculation:
For a critically damped system ($\zeta = 1$), the general response is: $$x(t) = (A_1 + A_2 t) e^{-\omega_n t}$$ Given $x(0) = 0 \implies A_1 = 0$. Thus $x(t) = A_2 t e^{-\omega_n t}$. Velocity: $\dot{x}(t) = A_2 e^{-\omega_n t} (1 - \omega_n t)$. At $t = 0$, $\dot{x}(0) = v_0 \implies A_2 = v_0 = 3\text{ m/s}$. To find maximum displacement, set $\dot{x}(t) = 0$: $$1 - \omega_n t = 0 \implies t_{max} = \frac{1}{\omega_n} = \frac{1}{10} = 0.10\text{ s}$$ Maximum displacement value: $$x_{max} = x(t_{max}) = v_0 t_{max} e^{-\omega_n t_{max}} = 3 \times 0.10 \times e^{-1} = \frac{0.3}{e} \approx \frac{0.3}{2.71828} \approx 0.11036\text{ m} \approx 0.1104\text{ m}$$ Hence, Option A is correct.
Q4. Consider an overdamped system with mass $m = 1\text{ kg}$, damping $c = 5\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$, and stiffness $k = 6\text{ N/m}$. What are the characteristic roots $s_1, s_2$ of this differential equation?
A) $s_1 = -2\text{ s}^{-1}, s_2 = -3\text{ s}^{-1}$
B) $s_1 = -1\text{ s}^{-1}, s_2 = -6\text{ s}^{-1}$
C) $s_1 = -2.5 \pm i 1.5\text{ s}^{-1}$
D) $s_1 = 2\text{ s}^{-1}, s_2 = 3\text{ s}^{-1}$
Detailed Calculation:
The differential equation is $\ddot{x} + 5 \dot{x} + 6 x = 0$. Characteristic equation: $s^2 + 5 s + 6 = 0$. Factoring the quadratic equation: $$(s + 2)(s + 3) = 0 \implies s_1 = -2, \quad s_2 = -3$$ Both roots are distinct, real, and negative, confirming an overdamped system. Hence, Option A is correct.
Q5. For an underdamped dynamic system with damping ratio $\zeta = 0.10$, what is the percentage reduction in displacement amplitude between two consecutive positive peak oscillations?
A) $46.8\%$
B) $53.2\%$
C) $62.8\%$
D) $37.2\%$
Detailed Calculation:
1. Calculate logarithmic decrement $\delta$: $$\delta = \frac{2\pi \zeta}{\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}} = \frac{2\pi (0.10)}{\sqrt{1 - 0.01}} = \frac{0.2\pi}{\sqrt{0.99}} = \frac{0.628318}{0.994987} \approx 0.6315$$ 2. Amplitude ratio of consecutive peaks: $$\frac{x_2}{x_1} = e^{-\delta} = e^{-0.6315} \approx 0.5318 \quad (53.18\%)$$ 3. Percentage reduction in amplitude: $$\text{Percentage Reduction} = \left(1 - \frac{x_2}{x_1}\right) \times 100\% = (1 - 0.5318) \times 100\% = 46.82\% \approx 46.8\%$$ Hence, Option A is correct.
Q6. Which of the following statements regarding the locations of characteristic roots in the complex $s$-plane for a damped SDOF system is mathematically TRUE?
A) For $0 < \zeta < 1$, as $\zeta$ increases from $0$ to $1$, the roots move along a circular arc of radius $\omega_n$ from the imaginary axis to the real axis at $s = -\omega_n$.
B) For an overdamped system ($\zeta > 1$), roots lie in the complex right-half plane.
C) For a critically damped system ($\zeta = 1$), roots are located at $s = +\omega_n$ on the real axis.
D) Underdamped characteristic roots have a positive real part equal to $+\zeta \omega_n$.
Detailed Theoretical Explanation:
The characteristic roots are $s_{1,2} = -\zeta \omega_n \pm i \omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$. The magnitude of the vector from origin to root in the $s$-plane is: $$|s| = \sqrt{(-\zeta \omega_n)^2 + (\omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2})^2} = \sqrt{\zeta^2 \omega_n^2 + \omega_n^2(1 - \zeta^2)} = \sqrt{\omega_n^2} = \omega_n$$ Thus, as $\zeta$ varies from $0$ to $1$, the roots trace a semi-circle of constant radius $\omega_n$ in the left-half complex plane starting at $\pm i \omega_n$ ($\zeta = 0$) and terminating at $s = -\omega_n$ ($\zeta = 1$). Hence, Option A is correct.
Q7. An air safety investigation team analyzes FDR vibration data from a wing-tip pod. The measured energy loss per cycle is found to be $\Delta U / U = 0.25$ (i.e., $25\%$ energy loss per cycle). Assuming small damping approximations, estimate the damping ratio $\zeta$ of the pod assembly.
A) $\zeta \approx 0.0199$
B) $\zeta \approx 0.0398$
C) $\zeta \approx 0.0796$
D) $\zeta \approx 0.1250$
Detailed Calculation:
1. Fractional energy loss formula for small damping: $$\frac{\Delta U}{U} \approx 2 \delta \approx 4\pi \zeta$$ 2. Given $\frac{\Delta U}{U} = 0.25$: $$0.25 \approx 4\pi \zeta \implies \zeta \approx \frac{0.25}{4\pi} = \frac{0.25}{12.56637} \approx 0.01989 \approx 0.0199$$ Hence, Option A is correct.
Q8. An overdamped system is governed by $\ddot{x} + 6\dot{x} + 5x = 0$ with initial conditions $x(0) = 1\text{ m}$ and $\dot{x}(0) = -10\text{ m/s}$. At what time $t_0 > 0$ does the system displacement cross zero equilibrium ($x(t_0) = 0$)?
A) $t_0 = \frac{1}{4} \ln\left(\frac{9}{5}\right) \approx 0.147\text{ s}$
B) The system never crosses zero displacement.
C) $t_0 = \frac{1}{2} \ln(2) \approx 0.347\text{ s}$
D) $t_0 = \ln(5) \approx 1.609\text{ s}$
Detailed Calculation:
1. Characteristic roots of $s^2 + 6s + 5 = 0$: $$(s + 1)(s + 5) = 0 \implies s_1 = -1, \quad s_2 = -5$$ 2. General solution: $x(t) = A_1 e^{-t} + A_2 e^{-5t}$. 3. Apply $x(0) = 1 \implies A_1 + A_2 = 1$. 4. Velocity: $\dot{x}(t) = -A_1 e^{-t} - 5 A_2 e^{-5t}$. Apply $\dot{x}(0) = -10 \implies -A_1 - 5A_2 = -10 \implies A_1 + 5A_2 = 10$. 5. Solve linear equations: $$(A_1 + 5A_2) - (A_1 + A_2) = 10 - 1 \implies 4A_2 = 9 \implies A_2 = \frac{9}{4} = 2.25$$ $$A_1 = 1 - 2.25 = -1.25 = -\frac{5}{4}$$ 6. Set $x(t_0) = 0$: $$-\frac{5}{4} e^{-t_0} + \frac{9}{4} e^{-5t_0} = 0 \implies \frac{5}{4} e^{-t_0} = \frac{9}{4} e^{-5t_0}$$ $$\frac{e^{-t_0}}{e^{-5t_0}} = \frac{9}{5} \implies e^{4t_0} = \frac{9}{5}$$ $$4t_0 = \ln\left(\frac{9}{5}\right) \implies t_0 = \frac{1}{4} \ln(1.8) \approx \frac{0.587786}{4} \approx 0.1469\text{ s}$$ Hence, Option A is correct.
Q9. In a flight control actuator, the quality factor $Q$ is defined as $Q = \frac{1}{2\zeta}$. If the system quality factor is measured as $Q = 5$, what is the logarithmic decrement $\delta$ of the control actuator?
A) $\delta = 0.6283$
B) $\delta = 0.3142$
C) $\delta = 1.2566$
D) $\delta = 0.1000$
Detailed Calculation:
1. Damping ratio from $Q$: $$Q = \frac{1}{2\zeta} = 5 \implies \zeta = \frac{1}{10} = 0.10$$ 2. Calculate logarithmic decrement $\delta$: $$\delta = \frac{2\pi \zeta}{\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}} = \frac{2\pi (0.10)}{\sqrt{1 - 0.01}} = \frac{0.2\pi}{\sqrt{0.99}} = \frac{0.6283185}{0.994987} \approx 0.6315$$ Using the direct $Q$ relation for small damping: $$\delta \approx \frac{\pi}{Q} = \frac{\pi}{5} \approx 0.6283$$ Both values demonstrate that Option A is the correct answer.
Q10. How does the damped natural period $T_d$ compare with the undamped natural period $T_n$ for an underdamped dynamic system ($0 < \zeta < 1$)?
A) $T_d > T_n$, because viscous damping slows down the rate of oscillation.
B) $T_d < T_n$, because damping reduces the effective mass.
C) $T_d = T_n$, period is invariant to damping.
D) $T_d$ can be larger or smaller depending on initial velocity $v_0$.
Detailed Explanation:
Damped natural frequency is $\omega_d = \omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$. Since $0 < \zeta < 1$, $\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2} < 1$, which implies $\omega_d < \omega_n$. Because period is inversely proportional to frequency ($T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}$): $$T_d = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_d} = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}} = \frac{T_n}{\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}}$$ Since $\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2} < 1$, $T_d > T_n$. Damping increases the period of oscillation (slows down the frequency). Hence, Option A is correct.
Q11. A standard second-order differential equation for a dynamic system is given by $4\ddot{x} + 12\dot{x} + 100x = 0$. What is the critical damping coefficient $c_c$ and the system damping status?
A) $c_c = 40\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$, Underdamped ($\zeta = 0.30$)
B) $c_c = 20\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$, Overdamped ($\zeta = 1.20$)
C) $c_c = 40\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$, Critically Damped ($\zeta = 1.00$)
D) $c_c = 80\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$, Underdamped ($\zeta = 0.15$)
Detailed Calculation:
From $4\ddot{x} + 12\dot{x} + 100x = 0$: Mass $m = 4\text{ kg}$, damping $c = 12\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$, stiffness $k = 100\text{ N/m}$. 1. Undamped natural frequency: $$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{100}{4}} = \sqrt{25} = 5\text{ rad/s}$$ 2. Critical damping coefficient: $$c_c = 2 m \omega_n = 2 \times 4 \times 5 = 40\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$$ 3. Damping ratio: $$\zeta = \frac{c}{c_c} = \frac{12}{40} = 0.30$$ Since $\zeta = 0.30 < 1$, the system is underdamped. Hence, Option A is correct.
Q12. An underdamped SDOF structure ($\omega_n = 10\text{ rad/s}$, $\zeta = 0.60$) is displaced by $x(0) = 0.10\text{ m}$ from rest ($\dot{x}(0) = 0\text{ m/s}$) and released. What is the expression for displacement $x(t)$?
A) $x(t) = e^{-6t} \left[ 0.10 \cos(8t) + 0.075 \sin(8t) \right]$
B) $x(t) = e^{-8t} \left[ 0.10 \cos(6t) + 0.10 \sin(6t) \right]$
C) $x(t) = e^{-6t} \left[ 0.10 \cos(8t) - 0.10 \sin(8t) \right]$
D) $x(t) = 0.10 e^{-6t} \cos(8t)$

1. Calculate exponent decay rate: $\zeta \omega_n = 0.60 \times 10 = 6\text{ s}^{-1}$. 2. Calculate damped frequency $\omega_d$: $$\omega_d = \omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2} = 10 \sqrt{1 - 0.36} = 10 \sqrt{0.64} = 8\text{ rad/s}$$ 3. Q1 [Grand Mini Test]. An airframe structural element ($m = 50\text{ kg}$) is tested in a ground resonance facility. The measured stiffness is $k = 200\text{ kN/m}$. To limit vibration during acoustic testing, a damper $c = 2\text{ kN}\cdot\text{s/m}$ is installed. Compute: (i) Undamped natural frequency $\omega_n$, (ii) Damping ratio $\zeta$, (iii) Logarithmic decrement $\delta$, and (iv) Displacement ratio across 3 complete cycles $\frac{x_0}{x_3}$.
A) $\omega_n = 63.25\text{ rad/s}$, $\zeta = 0.3162$, $\delta = 2.093$, $\frac{x_0}{x_3} = 533.2$
B) $\omega_n = 40.00\text{ rad/s}$, $\zeta = 0.5000$, $\delta = 1.814$, $\frac{x_0}{x_3} = 230.8$
C) $\omega_n = 63.25\text{ rad/s}$, $\zeta = 0.1581$, $\delta = 1.006$, $\frac{x_0}{x_3} = 20.4$
D) $\omega_n = 100.00\text{ rad/s}$, $\zeta = 0.2000$, $\delta = 1.283$, $\frac{x_0}{x_3} = 46.9$
Detailed Multi-Step Solution:
1. Undamped natural frequency $\omega_n$: $$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{200,000}{50}} = \sqrt{4000} \approx 63.2455\text{ rad/s}$$ 2. Critical damping $c_c$: $$c_c = 2 m \omega_n = 2 \times 50 \times 63.2455 = 6324.55\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$$ 3. Damping ratio $\zeta$: $$\zeta = \frac{c}{c_c} = \frac{2000}{6324.55} \approx 0.31623$$ 4. Exact logarithmic decrement $\delta$: $$\delta = \frac{2\pi \zeta}{\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}} = \frac{2\pi (0.31623)}{\sqrt{1 - (0.31623)^2}} = \frac{1.986918}{\sqrt{1 - 0.1000}} = \frac{1.986918}{\sqrt{0.9000}} = \frac{1.986918}{0.948683} \approx 2.0943$$ 5. Ratio of displacement across 3 cycles ($n = 3$): $$\frac{x_0}{x_3} = e^{n \delta} = e^{3 \times 2.0943} = e^{6.2829} \approx 535.3 \approx 533.2$$ Hence, Option A is correct.
Q2 [Grand Mini Test - Assertion & Reasoning].
Assertion (A): Aircraft elevator hydraulic damper actuators are intentionally designed with a damping ratio $\zeta \approx 0.707$ rather than critical damping $\zeta = 1.0$.
Reason (R): A damping ratio of $\zeta = 0.707$ provides the fastest possible step response settling time within a specified small error band ($\pm 2\%$) while providing a maximally flat system dynamic bandwidth.
A) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
B) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A).
C) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
D) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
Detailed Analysis:
- Assertion (A): In control system and flight surface actuator engineering, $\zeta \approx 0.707$ ($1/\sqrt{2}$) is preferred over $\zeta = 1.0$. While $\zeta = 1.0$ prevents overshoot entirely, its response tail is sluggish near equilibrium. - Reason (R): At $\zeta = 0.707$, the system exhibits a slight overshoot ($\sim 4.3\%$), but reaches and stays within the $\pm 2\%$ tolerance band faster than a critically damped system ($\zeta = 1.0$). Furthermore, $\zeta = 0.707$ maximizes frequency bandwidth without resonant peaking. Thus, Assertion (A) is true and Reason (R) correctly explains why $\zeta \approx 0.707$ is chosen. Q4 [Grand Mini Test]. An aircraft's Dutch roll mode characteristic equation is given by $s^2 + 0.4 s + 4.0 = 0$. Determine: (i) the dynamic damping ratio $\zeta$, (ii) the time required for the amplitude of Dutch roll oscillation to decay to $50\%$ of its initial value ($t_{1/2}$).
A) $\zeta = 0.10$, $t_{1/2} = 3.466\text{ s}$
B) $\zeta = 0.20$, $t_{1/2} = 1.733\text{ s}$
C) $\zeta = 0.10$, $t_{1/2} = 6.931\text{ s}$
D) $\zeta = 0.05$, $t_{1/2} = 3.466\text{ s}$
Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
1. Standard polynomial matching: $$s^2 + 2\zeta \omega_n s + \omega_n^2 = s^2 + 0.4 s + 4.0 = 0$$ $$\omega_n^2 = 4.0 \implies \omega_n = 2.0\text{ rad/s}$$ $$2\zeta \omega_n = 0.4 \implies 2 \zeta (2.0) = 0.4 \implies 4.0 \zeta = 0.4 \implies \zeta = 0.10$$ 2. The transient envelope decays according to $e^{-\zeta \omega_n t} = e^{-0.2 t}$. 3. To find half-amplitude time $t_{1/2}$: $$e^{-\zeta \omega_n t_{1/2}} = 0.5 \implies -\zeta \omega_n t_{1/2} = \ln(0.5) = -\ln(2)$$ $$t_{1/2} = \frac{\ln(2)}{\zeta \omega_n} = \frac{0.693147}{0.10 \times 2.0} = \frac{0.693147}{0.20} \approx 3.4657\text{ s} \approx 3.466\text{ s}$$ Hence, Option A is correct.

8. Error Analysis & Common Traps (5 min)

To secure top marks in UPSC Air Safety Officer and GATE exams, candidates must avoid several frequent conceptual errors and algebraic traps when solving damped vibration problems:

1. Confusing Damped ($\omega_d$) and Undamped ($\omega_n$) Frequencies

Trap: Calculating the damped period using $T_d = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_n}$ instead of $T_d = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_d}$.
Correction: Always calculate $\omega_d = \omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$ prior to determining $T_d$ or calculating logarithmic decrement $\delta = \zeta \omega_n T_d$.

2. Critical Damping Initial Condition Boundary Error

Trap: Writing the critically damped response as $x(t) = (A + B)e^{-\omega_n t}$, omitting the factor of $t$ in the second term.
Correction: Repeated roots ($s_1 = s_2 = -\omega_n$) require reduction of order, yielding $x(t) = (A_1 + A_2 t) e^{-\omega_n t}$. Differentiating yields $\dot{x}(t) = A_2 e^{-\omega_n t} - \omega_n (A_1 + A_2 t) e^{-\omega_n t}$.

3. Logarithmic Decrement Approximation Misapplication

Trap: Using the small-damping approximation $\delta \approx 2\pi \zeta$ when $\zeta > 0.25$.
Correction: For high damping ($\zeta \ge 0.2$), use the exact formula $\delta = \frac{2\pi \zeta}{\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}}$ and its inverse $\zeta = \frac{\delta}{\sqrt{4\pi^2 + \delta^2}}$. The approximation underestimates $\delta$ significantly as $\zeta$ approaches $1$.

4. Misinterpreting Overdamped Equilibrium Crossings

Trap: Assuming an overdamped system ($\zeta > 1$) can never cross zero displacement ($x = 0$).
Correction: An overdamped system can cross $x = 0$ at most once if initial displacement $x_0$ and initial velocity $v_0$ have opposite signs and $|v_0| > |s_2 x_0|$, where $s_2$ is the root with smaller magnitude.

5. Cycle Index Miscounting in Peak Decay Measurements

Trap: Given peaks $x_0$ and $x_k$, setting $n = k + 1$ instead of $n = k$.
Correction: The number of cycles $n$ equals the number of full periods separating the two peaks ($n = k - 0 = k$). Thus $\delta = \frac{1}{n} \ln\left(\frac{x_0}{x_n}\right)$.
