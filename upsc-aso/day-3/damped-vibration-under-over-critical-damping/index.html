Damped vibration – under/over/critical damping

Day 3 of 129 Engineering Fundamentals Phase 1: Foundations 60-90 min Master Cycle

1. Understand (5–10 min)

In mechanical, structural, and aeronautical engineering, dynamic systems subjected to restoring forces undergo vibrational motion. Real physical systems invariably experience resistive forces—termed damping forces—which dissipate mechanical energy as thermal or acoustic energy. The study of single-degree-of-freedom (SDOF) viscous damped systems forms the foundational cornerstone of structural dynamics, flight control stability analysis, and aeroelasticity.

The standard second-order linear homogeneous differential equation governing a single-degree-of-freedom viscous damped system is defined as:

$$ m d^2 xdt^2 + c dxdt + k x = 0 \quad or \quad m \ddot{x} + c \dot{x} + k x = 0 $$

where $m \in (0, ∞)$ represents the system mass ($kg$), $c \in [0, ∞)$ denotes the viscous damping coefficient ($N·s/m$), $k \in (0, ∞)$ is the spring stiffness ($N/m$), and $x(t)$ represents the translational displacement ($m$) as a function of time $t ≥ 0$.

Dividing the differential equation by mass $m$ yields the canonical standard form:

$$ \ddot{x} + 2\zeta ω_n \dot{x} + ω_n^2 x = 0 $$

The primary parameters and domain bounds governing the dynamics are strictly defined as follows:

  • Undamped Natural Frequency ($ω_n$): $ω_n = √km \in (0, ∞) \quad [rad/s]$
  • Critical Damping Coefficient ($c_c$): $c_c = 2 m ω_n = 2 √k m \in (0, ∞) \quad [N·s/m]$
  • Damping Ratio ($\zeta$): $\zeta = cc_c = c2 m ω_n = c2√k m \in [0, ∞) \quad [dimensionless]$

Based on the quantitative value of the damping ratio $\zeta$, the domain of dynamic behavior is partitioned into three mutually exclusive classical regimes:

  1. Overdamped Regime ($\zeta > 1$ or $c > c_c$): Non-oscillatory decay governed by two distinct real negative characteristic roots.
  2. Critically Damped Regime ($\zeta = 1$ or $c = c_c$): Non-oscillatory decay featuring repeated real negative roots; represents the boundary regime exhibiting the fastest return to static equilibrium without overshoot.
  3. Underdamped Regime ($0 ≤ \zeta < 1$ or $c < c_c$): Oscillatory transient response with exponentially decaying amplitude, governed by a pair of complex conjugate characteristic roots with negative real parts.

2. Learn the Concept (15–20 min)

To fully comprehend the dynamic behavior of damped single-degree-of-freedom systems, we establish the solution to the canonical homogeneous differential equation $\ddot{x} + 2\zeta ω_n \dot{x} + ω_n^2 x = 0$ using the characteristic ansatz $x(t) = es t$.

Substituting $x(t) = es t$ into the canonical equation yields the characteristic algebraic equation:

$$ s^2 + 2\zeta ω_n s + ω_n^2 = 0 $$

Applying the quadratic formula yields the characteristic roots $s1,2$:

$$ s1,2 = -2\zeta ω_n ± √4\zeta^2 ω_n^2 - 4ω_n^22 = -\zeta ω_n ± ω_n √\zeta^2 - 1 $$
$$ s1,2 = -\zeta ω_n ± ω_n √\zeta^2 - 1 $$ $$ ω_d = ω_n √1 - \zeta^2 \quad (0 ≤ \zeta < 1) $$ $$ \delta = \ln(x(t)x(t + T_d)) = \zeta ω_n T_d = 2π \zeta√1 - \zeta^2 $$

1. Detailed Analysis of Damping Regimes

A. Overdamped Case ($\zeta > 1$)

When $\zeta > 1$, the term under the radical $√\zeta^2 - 1$ is positive and real. The roots $s_1$ and $s_2$ are distinct, real, and strictly negative:

$$ s_1 = -\zeta ω_n + ω_n √\zeta^2 - 1 < 0, \quad s_2 = -\zeta ω_n - ω_n √\zeta^2 - 1 < 0 $$

The general solution for displacement is given by:

$$ x(t) = A_1 es_1 t + A_2 es_2 t $$

Given initial conditions $x(0) = x_0$ and $\dot{x}(0) = v_0$, the constants $A_1$ and $A_2$ are determined by solving the system:

$$ A_1 + A_2 = x_0 \quad and \quad s_1 A_1 + s_2 A_2 = v_0 $$ $$ A_1 = v_0 - s_2 x_0s_1 - s_2, \quad A_2 = s_1 x_0 - v_0s_1 - s_2 $$

Physical Interpretation: The system does not undergo periodic oscillation. If initial velocity $v_0$ is directed away from equilibrium with sufficient magnitude, the trajectory may cross the equilibrium position ($x = 0$) at most once before decaying asymptotically to zero.

B. Critically Damped Case ($\zeta = 1$)

When $\zeta = 1$, the discriminant vanishes ($\zeta^2 - 1 = 0$), yielding identical repeated real roots:

$$ s_1 = s_2 = -ω_n $$

To construct two linearly independent solutions, we apply reduction of order, yielding the general displacement equation:

$$ x(t) = (A_1 + A_2 t) e-ω_n t $$

Applying initial conditions $x(0) = x_0$ and $\dot{x}(0) = v_0$ gives:

$$ A_1 = x_0, \quad A_2 = v_0 + ω_n x_0 $$ $$ x(t) = [ x_0 + (v_0 + ω_n x_0) t ] e-ω_n t $$

Peak Time Derivative: For zero initial displacement ($x_0 = 0$) and initial impulse velocity $v_0$, the time $tmax$ at which maximum displacement occurs is found by setting $\dot{x}(t) = 0$:

$$ \dot{x}(t) = v_0 e-ω_n t - ω_n v_0 t e-ω_n t = v_0 (1 - ω_n t) e-ω_n t = 0 \implies tmax = 1ω_n $$

The maximum displacement achieved under critical damping is:

$$ xmax = x(tmax) = v_0 (1ω_n) e-1 = v_0ω_n e $$

C. Underdamped Case ($0 ≤ \zeta < 1$)

When $0 ≤ \zeta < 1$, $√\zeta^2 - 1 = √-(1 - \zeta^2) = i √1 - \zeta^2 = i ω_dω_n$, where $ω_d$ is the damped natural frequency defined as:

$$ ω_d = ω_n √1 - \zeta^2 $$

The characteristic roots form a complex conjugate pair:

$$ s1,2 = -\zeta ω_n ± i ω_d $$

Using Euler's identity ($ei θ = \cos θ + i \sin θ$), the general real-valued solution is:

$$ x(t) = e-\zeta ω_n t ( A \cos ω_d t + B \sin ω_d t ) $$

Alternatively expressed in harmonic magnitude-phase form:

$$ x(t) = X e-\zeta ω_n t \sin(ω_d t + φ) $$

where amplitude $X = √A^2 + B^2$ and phase angle $φ = \tan-1(AB)$.

Given initial conditions $x(0) = x_0$ and $\dot{x}(0) = v_0$:

$$ A = x_0, \quad B = v_0 + \zeta ω_n x_0ω_d $$ $$ x(t) = e-\zeta ω_n t [ x_0 \cos ω_d t + ( v_0 + \zeta ω_n x_0ω_d ) \sin ω_d t ] $$

2. The Logarithmic Decrement ($\delta$)

The logarithmic decrement measures the rate of decay of peak oscillations in an underdamped system. Let $t_1$ and $t_2 = t_1 + T_d$ be the time instants corresponding to two consecutive positive displacement peaks, where $T_d$ is the damped period of oscillation:

$$ T_d = 2πω_d = 2πω_n √1 - \zeta^2 $$

The ratio of two consecutive peak amplitudes $x_1 = x(t_1)$ and $x_2 = x(t_1 + T_d)$ is:

$$ x_1x_2 = X e-\zeta ω_n t_1 \sin(ω_d t_1 + φ)X e-\zeta ω_n (t_1 + T_d) \sin(ω_d (t_1 + T_d) + φ) $$

Since $ω_d T_d = 2π$, $\sin(ω_d t_1 + ω_d T_d + φ) = \sin(ω_d t_1 + φ)$. The sinusoidal terms cancel, yielding:

$$ x_1x_2 = e-\zeta ω_n t_1e-\zeta ω_n t_1 e-\zeta ω_n T_d = e\zeta ω_n T_d $$

Taking the natural logarithm of both sides defines the logarithmic decrement $\delta$:

$$ \delta = \ln(x_1x_2) = \zeta ω_n T_d = \zeta ω_n ( 2πω_n √1 - \zeta^2 ) = 2π \zeta√1 - \zeta^2 $$

If the amplitude measurement is taken across $n$ complete cycles ($x_0$ to $x_n$):

$$ x_0x_n = x_0x_1 · x_1x_2 ·s xn-1x_n = ( e\delta )^n = en \delta \implies \delta = 1n \ln( x_0x_n ) $$

For low damping ($\zeta \ll 1$), $√1 - \zeta^2 ≈ 1$, leading to the standard small-damping approximation:

$$ \delta ≈ 2π \zeta $$

3. Energy Dissipation per Cycle

The total mechanical energy $U(t)$ stored in the system at peak displacement is proportional to the square of the amplitude: $U ∝ X^2$.

The fractional energy loss per cycle $Δ UU$ is derived as:

$$ Δ UU = U_1 - U_2U_1 = 1 - (x_2x_1)^2 = 1 - (e-\delta)^2 = 1 - e-2\delta $$

Expanding using Taylor series for small $\delta$:

$$ 1 - e-2\delta ≈ 1 - (1 - 2\delta) = 2\delta ≈ 4π \zeta $$

  • Canonical Damped Differential Equation: $\ddot{x} + 2\zeta ω_n \dot{x} + ω_n^2 x = 0$
  • Undamped Natural Frequency: $ω_n = √km$
  • Critical Damping Coefficient: $c_c = 2 m ω_n = 2 √k m$
  • Damping Ratio: $\zeta = cc_c = c2 m ω_n$
  • Characteristic Roots Equation: $s1,2 = -\zeta ω_n ± ω_n √\zeta^2 - 1$
  • Damped Natural Frequency: $ω_d = ω_n √1 - \zeta^2 \quad (0 ≤ \zeta < 1)$
  • Damped Oscillatory Period: $T_d = 2πω_d = 2πω_n √1 - \zeta^2$
  • Overdamped Solution ($\zeta > 1$): $x(t) = A_1 es_1 t + A_2 es_2 t$
  • Critically Damped Solution ($\zeta = 1$): $x(t) = (A_1 + A_2 t) e-ω_n t$
  • Peak Time for Critical Damping ($x_0 = 0$): $tmax = 1ω_n, \quad xmax = v_0ω_n e$
  • Underdamped Solution ($\zeta < 1$): $x(t) = e-\zeta ω_n t [ x_0 \cos ω_d t + (v_0 + \zeta ω_n x_0ω_d) \sin ω_d t ]$
  • Logarithmic Decrement Exact Identity: $\delta = 1n \ln(x_0x_n) = 2π \zeta√1 - \zeta^2$
  • Damping Ratio from Logarithmic Decrement: $\zeta = \delta√4π^2 + \delta^2$
  • Small Damping Approximations ($\zeta \ll 0.2$): $\delta ≈ 2π \zeta \quad and \quad Δ UU ≈ 2\delta ≈ 4π \zeta$

4. Aviation Application (5–10 min)

Damped vibrations and transient response characteristics play a vital role in aircraft design, certification, flight control engineering, and accident investigations monitored by aviation safety agencies (such as DGCA, FAA, EASA, and NTSB):

1. Landing Gear Oleo-Pneumatic Shock Absorber Tuning

Aircraft landing gear struts combine compressed air (spring stiffness $k$) and hydraulic oil metered through orifices (viscous damping $c$). To absorb touchdown kinetic energy without severe rebound or structural overload, oleo struts are designed near critical damping ($\zeta ≈ 0.8 - 1.0$). If damping is underdamped ($\zeta < 0.7$), the aircraft undergoes unwanted oscillations upon touchdown, increasing fatigue stress on landing gear attachments. If heavily overdamped ($\zeta > 1.2$), the shock strut acts rigid upon impact, transmitting excessive force to the airframe structure.

2. Flight Control System Surface Dynamics & Flutter Suppression

Control surfaces (elevators, ailerons, rudders) integrated with hydraulic actuators and digital flight control systems (DFCS) are modeled as second-order damped systems. Hydraulic control valves provide damping to prevent aeroelastic flutter and high-frequency "buzz". Control surface dampers are typically calibrated to $\zeta ≈ 0.6 - 0.7$, providing a rapid settling time with less than $5%$ overshoot, ensuring stable pilot command input response.

3. Aircraft Dynamic Stability: Dutch Roll and Short-Period Oscillations

In atmospheric flight dynamics, lateral-directional dynamic stability involves the Dutch roll mode (coupled roll-yaw oscillation). Airworthiness regulations (e.g., CS-25 / FAR 25.181) mandate strict minimum damping ratio thresholds ($\zeta ≥ 0.04$ or $\zeta ≥ 0.08$ depending on flight phase) for Dutch roll modes. Yaw dampers are automated flight control systems designed to add artificial damping $Δ c$ to elevate the effective $\zeta$ above regulatory safety standards.

4. Flight Instrumentation & Sensor Response

Airborne sensors such as accelerometers, rate gyroscopes, and Angle-of-Attack (AoA) transmitters are continuous second-order dynamic systems. Tuning sensor damping ratio to $\zeta ≈ 0.707$ ($1/√2$) provides optimal bandwidth, maximally flat magnitude frequency response, minimal phase distortion, and rapid transient settling time without spurious resonance spikes.

5. Air Safety & Crash Investigation Analysis

Air safety investigators analyzing Flight Data Recorders (FDR) assess structural impulse response tails (e.g., turbulence impacts, heavy landing events, structural failures) using logarithmic decrement formulas $\delta = 1n \ln(x_0/x_n)$ to calculate effective structural damping $\zeta$ and diagnose structural damage, bolt loosening, fluid leakage in hydraulic dampers, or skin delamination in composite aeroelastic structures.

5. UPSC ASO Focus (10 min)

  • Frequently Asked Facts & Formulas:
    • Damped natural frequency equation: $ω_d = ω_n √1 - \zeta^2$. Note that $ω_d$ is ALWAYS strictly less than $ω_n$ for underdamped systems ($\zeta > 0$).
    • Exact relation for $\zeta$ given $\delta$: $\zeta = \delta√4π^2 + \delta^2$. Candidates frequently forget the $4π^2$ in the denominator under time pressure.
    • Critically damped peak displacement time: $tmax = 1ω_n$ for zero initial displacement $x(0)=0$. Maximum displacement $xmax = v_0ω_n e ≈ 0.368 v_0ω_n$.
    • Ratio of $n$ consecutive cycles peak displacements: $x_0x_n = en \delta$.
    • In the complex $s$-plane, characteristic roots $s1,2 = -\zeta ω_n ± i ω_d$ lie on a circle of radius $ω_n$ centered at origin for $0 ≤ \zeta ≤ 1$. Angle with imaginary axis is $θ = \sin-1(\zeta)$.
  • Conceptual Traps & Pitfalls:
    • Confusing $ω_d$ with $ω_n$: Calculations of damped period $T_d$ must use $ω_d = 2πT_d$, NOT $ω_n$.
    • Critical Damping Roots Trap: Repeating roots at $s = -ω_n$ mean the solution is $x(t) = (A + Bt)e-ω_n t$, NOT $(A + B)e-ω_n t$. Forgetting the factor $t$ leads to completely wrong initial value solutions.
    • Overdamped Response Fallacy: Overdamped systems do NOT oscillate, but they CAN cross equilibrium ($x = 0$) exactly ONCE if initial position and initial velocity have opposite signs and $|v_0| > |s_2 x_0|$. Candidates often mistakenly believe overdamped systems NEVER cross $x=0$.
    • Linear vs Logarithmic Decay: Damped amplitudes decay exponentially ($e-\zeta ω_n t$), meaning the ratio of consecutive amplitudes is constant, NOT the linear difference between them.

Q1. A mechanical vibration absorber attached to an aircraft instrument panel has a mass $m = 2 kg$, a spring stiffness $k = 800 N/m$, and a viscous damping coefficient $c = 16 N·s/m$. Determine the damping ratio $\zeta$ and the damped natural frequency $ω_d$ of the system.
A) $\zeta = 0.20$, $ω_d = 19.60 rad/s$
B) $\zeta = 0.20$, $ω_d = 20.00 rad/s$
C) $\zeta = 0.40$, $ω_d = 18.33 rad/s$
D) $\zeta = 0.10$, $ω_d = 19.90 rad/s$
Detailed Calculation:
1. Calculate undamped natural frequency: $$ω_n = √km = √8002 = √400 = 20 rad/s$$ 2. Calculate critical damping coefficient: $$c_c = 2 m ω_n = 2 × 2 × 20 = 80 N·s/m$$ 3. Calculate damping ratio $\zeta$: $$\zeta = cc_c = 1680 = 0.20$$ 4. Calculate damped natural frequency $ω_d$: $$ω_d = ω_n √1 - \zeta^2 = 20 √1 - (0.20)^2 = 20 √1 - 0.04 = 20 √0.96 ≈ 20 × 0.979796 = 19.5959 ≈ 19.60 rad/s$$ Hence, Option A is correct.
Q2. An underdamped dynamic system undergoes free vibration. The initial peak displacement measured is $x_0 = 25 mm$. After $5$ complete cycles, the peak displacement reduces to $x_5 = 5 mm$. Calculate the exact logarithmic decrement $\delta$ and the corresponding damping ratio $\zeta$.
A) $\delta = 0.3219$, $\zeta = 0.0512$
B) $\delta = 1.6094$, $\zeta = 0.2485$
C) $\delta = 0.3219$, $\zeta = 0.1024$
D) $\delta = 0.5000$, $\zeta = 0.0796$
Detailed Calculation:
1. Logarithmic decrement over $n = 5$ cycles: $$\delta = 1n \ln(x_0x_n) = 15 \ln(255) = 15 \ln(5) ≈ 1.6094385 = 0.321887 ≈ 0.3219$$ 2. Exact damping ratio formula: $$\zeta = \delta√4π^2 + \delta^2 = 0.321887√4π^2 + (0.321887)^2 = 0.321887√39.4784 + 0.10361 = 0.321887√39.582 = 0.3218876.2914 ≈ 0.05116 ≈ 0.0512$$ Hence, Option A is correct.
Q3. An aircraft landing gear oleo strut is modeled as a critically damped single-degree-of-freedom system with undamped natural frequency $ω_n = 10 rad/s$. Upon touchdown at $t = 0$, initial displacement is $x(0) = 0 m$ and initial downward vertical velocity is $v_0 = 3 m/s$. Find the maximum stroke displacement $xmax$ and the time $tmax$ at which it occurs.
A) $tmax = 0.10 s$, $xmax = 0.1104 m$
B) $tmax = 0.20 s$, $xmax = 0.3000 m$
C) $tmax = 0.10 s$, $xmax = 0.3000 m$
D) $tmax = 0.05 s$, $xmax = 0.1500 m$
Detailed Calculation:
For a critically damped system ($\zeta = 1$), the general response is: $$x(t) = (A_1 + A_2 t) e-ω_n t$$ Given $x(0) = 0 \implies A_1 = 0$. Thus $x(t) = A_2 t e-ω_n t$. Velocity: $\dot{x}(t) = A_2 e-ω_n t (1 - ω_n t)$. At $t = 0$, $\dot{x}(0) = v_0 \implies A_2 = v_0 = 3 m/s$. To find maximum displacement, set $\dot{x}(t) = 0$: $$1 - ω_n t = 0 \implies tmax = 1ω_n = 110 = 0.10 s$$ Maximum displacement value: $$xmax = x(tmax) = v_0 tmax e-ω_n tmax = 3 × 0.10 × e-1 = 0.3e ≈ 0.32.71828 ≈ 0.11036 m ≈ 0.1104 m$$ Hence, Option A is correct.
Q4. Consider an overdamped system with mass $m = 1 kg$, damping $c = 5 N·s/m$, and stiffness $k = 6 N/m$. What are the characteristic roots $s_1, s_2$ of this differential equation?
A) $s_1 = -2 s-1, s_2 = -3 s-1$
B) $s_1 = -1 s-1, s_2 = -6 s-1$
C) $s_1 = -2.5 ± i 1.5 s-1$
D) $s_1 = 2 s-1, s_2 = 3 s-1$
Detailed Calculation:
The differential equation is $\ddot{x} + 5 \dot{x} + 6 x = 0$. Characteristic equation: $s^2 + 5 s + 6 = 0$. Factoring the quadratic equation: $$(s + 2)(s + 3) = 0 \implies s_1 = -2, \quad s_2 = -3$$ Both roots are distinct, real, and negative, confirming an overdamped system. Hence, Option A is correct.
Q5. For an underdamped dynamic system with damping ratio $\zeta = 0.10$, what is the percentage reduction in displacement amplitude between two consecutive positive peak oscillations?
A) $46.8%$
B) $53.2%$
C) $62.8%$
D) $37.2%$
Detailed Calculation:

Amplitude ratio of consecutive peaks: $$x_2x_1 = e-\delta = e-0.6315 ≈ 0.5318 \quad (53.18%)$$ 3. Q6. Which of the following statements regarding the locations of characteristic roots in the complex $s$-plane for a damped SDOF system is mathematically TRUE?
A) For $0 < \zeta < 1$, as $\zeta$ increases from $0$ to $1$, the roots move along a circular arc of radius $ω_n$ from the imaginary axis to the real axis at $s = -ω_n$.
B) For an overdamped system ($\zeta > 1$), roots lie in the complex right-half plane.
C) For a critically damped system ($\zeta = 1$), roots are located at $s = +ω_n$ on the real axis.
D) Underdamped characteristic roots have a positive real part equal to $+\zeta ω_n$.
Detailed Theoretical Explanation:
The characteristic roots are $s1,2 = -\zeta ω_n ± i ω_n √1 - \zeta^2$. The magnitude of the vector from origin to root in the $s$-plane is: $$|s| = √(-\zeta ω_n)^2 + (ω_n √1 - \zeta^2)^2 = √\zeta^2 ω_n^2 + ω_n^2(1 - \zeta^2) = √ω_n^2 = ω_n$$ Thus, as $\zeta$ varies from $0$ to $1$, the roots trace a semi-circle of constant radius $ω_n$ in the left-half complex plane starting at $± i ω_n$ ($\zeta = 0$) and terminating at $s = -ω_n$ ($\zeta = 1$). Hence, Option A is correct.

B) $\zeta ≈ 0.0398$
C) $\zeta ≈ 0.0796$
D) $\zeta ≈ 0.1250$

Q8. An overdamped system is governed by $\ddot{x} + 6\dot{x} + 5x = 0$ with initial conditions $x(0) = 1 m$ and $\dot{x}(0) = -10 m/s$. At what time $t_0 > 0$ does the system displacement cross zero equilibrium ($x(t_0) = 0$)?
A) $t_0 = 14 \ln(95) ≈ 0.147 s$

C) $t_0 = 12 \ln(2) ≈ 0.347 s$
D) $t_0 = \ln(5) ≈ 1.609 s$

1. Characteristic roots of $s^2 + 6s + 5 = 0$: $$(s + 1)(s + 5) = 0 \implies s_1 = -1, \quad s_2 = -5$$ 2. General solution: $x(t) = A_1 e-t + A_2 e-5t$. 3. Apply $x(0) = 1 \implies A_1 + A_2 = 1$. 4. Velocity: $\dot{x}(t) = -A_1 e-t - 5 A_2 e-5t$. Apply $\dot{x}(0) = -10 \implies -A_1 - 5A_2 = -10 \implies A_1 + 5A_2 = 10$. 5. Q10. How does the damped natural period $T_d$ compare with the undamped natural period $T_n$ for an underdamped dynamic system ($0 < \zeta < 1$)?
A) $T_d > T_n$, because viscous damping slows down the rate of oscillation.
B) $T_d < T_n$, because damping reduces the effective mass.
C) $T_d = T_n$, period is invariant to damping.
D) $T_d$ can be larger or smaller depending on initial velocity $v_0$.
Detailed Explanation:
Damped natural frequency is $ω_d = ω_n √1 - \zeta^2$. Since $0 < \zeta < 1$, $√1 - \zeta^2 < 1$, which implies $ω_d < ω_n$. Because period is inversely proportional to frequency ($T = 2πω$): $$T_d = 2πω_d = 2πω_n √1 - \zeta^2 = T_n√1 - \zeta^2$$ Since $√1 - \zeta^2 < 1$, $T_d > T_n$. Damping increases the period of oscillation (slows down the frequency). Hence, Option A is correct.

A) $x(t) = e-6t [ 0.10 \cos(8t) + 0.075 \sin(8t) ]$
B) $x(t) = e-8t [ 0.10 \cos(6t) + 0.10 \sin(6t) ]$

Q4 [Grand Mini Test]. An aircraft's Dutch roll mode characteristic equation is given by $s^2 + 0.4 s + 4.0 = 0$. Determine: (i) the dynamic damping ratio $\zeta$, (ii) the time required for the amplitude of Dutch roll oscillation to decay to $50%$ of its initial value ($t1/2$).

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:

8. Error Analysis & Common Traps (5 min)

To secure top marks in UPSC Air Safety Officer and GATE exams, candidates must avoid several frequent conceptual errors and algebraic traps when solving damped vibration problems:

1. Confusing Damped ($ω_d$) and Undamped ($ω_n$) Frequencies

Trap: Calculating the damped period using $T_d = 2πω_n$ instead of $T_d = 2πω_d$.
Correction: Always calculate $ω_d = ω_n √1 - \zeta^2$ prior to determining $T_d$ or calculating logarithmic decrement $\delta = \zeta ω_n T_d$.

2. Critical Damping Initial Condition Boundary Error

Trap: Writing the critically damped response as $x(t) = (A + B)e-ω_n t$, omitting the factor of $t$ in the second term.
Correction: Repeated roots ($s_1 = s_2 = -ω_n$) require reduction of order, yielding $x(t) = (A_1 + A_2 t) e-ω_n t$. Differentiating yields $\dot{x}(t) = A_2 e-ω_n t - ω_n (A_1 + A_2 t) e-ω_n t$.

3. Logarithmic Decrement Approximation Misapplication

Trap: Using the small-damping approximation $\delta ≈ 2π \zeta$ when $\zeta > 0.25$.
Correction: For high damping ($\zeta ≥ 0.2$), use the exact formula $\delta = 2π \zeta√1 - \zeta^2$ and its inverse $\zeta = \delta√4π^2 + \delta^2$. The approximation underestimates $\delta$ significantly as $\zeta$ approaches $1$.

4. Misinterpreting Overdamped Equilibrium Crossings

Trap: Assuming an overdamped system ($\zeta > 1$) can never cross zero displacement ($x = 0$).
Correction: An overdamped system can cross $x = 0$ at most once if initial displacement $x_0$ and initial velocity $v_0$ have opposite signs and $|v_0| > |s_2 x_0|$, where $s_2$ is the root with smaller magnitude.

5. Cycle Index Miscounting in Peak Decay Measurements

Trap: Given peaks $x_0$ and $x_k$, setting $n = k + 1$ instead of $n = k$.
Correction: The number of cycles $n$ equals the number of full periods separating the two peaks ($n = k - 0 = k$). Thus $\delta = 1n \ln(x_0x_n)$.
