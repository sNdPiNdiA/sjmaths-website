Z-transform – definition, inverse Z-transform

Day 3 of 129 Engineering Fundamentals Phase 1: Foundations 60-90 min Master Cycle

1. Understand (5–10 min)

The Z-transform is the discrete-time equivalent of the continuous-time Laplace transform. It maps a discrete-time sequence $x[n]$ defined over $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ into a continuous complex domain represented by the complex variable $z \in \mathbb{C}$. In the domain of Digital Signal Processing (DSP), Avionics, and Flight Control Systems (FCS), the Z-transform acts as the primary mathematical engine for dynamic system analysis, digital filter design, stability assessment, and discretized differential equation modeling.

Mathematically, the Bilateral (Two-Sided) Z-transform of a discrete sequence $x[n]$ is defined as:

$$ X(z) = \mathcal{Z}\{x[n]\} = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n] z^{-n} $$

where $z = r e^{j\omega} = \sigma + j\omega \in \mathbb{C}$ is a complex variable, $r = |z|$ is the magnitude, and $\omega = \angle z$ is the normalized digital angular frequency (rad/sample).

The Unilateral (One-Sided) Z-transform is restricted to non-negative time indices $n \ge 0$, rendering it indispensable for solving discrete-time initial-value Cauchy problems in digital flight dynamics:

$$ X_u(z) = \mathcal{Z}_u\{x[n]\} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x[n] z^{-n} $$

The Region of Convergence (ROC) is defined as the set of all points in the complex $z$-plane for which the infinite power series converges absolutely:

$$ \text{ROC} = \left\{ z \in \mathbb{C} \;\middle|\; \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} |x[n]| |z|^{-n} < \infty \right\} $$

The mapping from $x[n]$ to $X(z)$ is not unique without the explicit specification of the ROC. The boundary conditions of the ROC dictate system properties such as causality, anti-causality, and Bounded-Input Bounded-Output (BIBO) stability.

The Inverse Z-transform restores the discrete-time sequence $x[n]$ from its $z$-domain algebraic representation $X(z)$ via the contour integral defined over a closed path $C$ encircling the origin within the ROC:

$$ x[n] = \mathcal{Z}^{-1}\{X(z)\} = \frac{1}{2\pi j} \oint_C X(z) z^{n-1} \, dz $$

Evaluating this integral is fundamental in digital flight control design to extract time-domain transient responses from discretized pulse transfer functions.

2. Learn the Concept (15–20 min)

Understanding the Z-transform requires mastering its analytical properties, the geometric behavior of the ROC, algebraic inversion methods, and boundary limit theorems.

1. Topological and Analytical Properties of the Region of Convergence (ROC)

  • Concentric Ring Geometry: The ROC of $X(z)$ consists of a ring or disk centered at the origin in the complex $z$-plane: $r_{\min} < |z| < r_{\max}$.
  • Absence of Poles: The ROC cannot contain any poles of $X(z)$, as poles represent points where $X(z) \to \infty$.
  • Finite-Duration Sequences: If $x[n]$ is a finite-duration sequence ($x[n] = 0$ for $n < N_1$ and $n > N_2$), the ROC is the entire $z$-plane except possibly $z = 0$ (if $N_2 > 0$) or $z = \infty$ (if $N_1 < 0$).
  • Right-Sided (Causal) Sequences: If $x[n]$ is a right-sided sequence ($x[n] = 0$ for $n < N_1$), the ROC extends strictly outward from the outermost pole: $|z| > r_{\max}$. If $x[n]$ is strictly causal ($n \ge 0$), the ROC includes $z = \infty$.
  • Left-Sided (Anti-Causal) Sequences: If $x[n]$ is a left-sided sequence ($x[n] = 0$ for $n > N_2$), the ROC extends strictly inward from the innermost pole: $|z| < r_{\min}$. If $x[n]$ is strictly anti-causal ($n \le 0$), the ROC includes $z = 0$.
  • Two-Sided Sequences: If $x[n]$ is infinite in both directions, the ROC is an open annulus $r_1 < |z| < r_2$. If $r_1 \ge r_2$, no ROC exists, and $x[n]$ has no Z-transform.
  • BIBO Stability Criterion: A discrete-time Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) system is BIBO stable if and only if the ROC of its system transfer function $H(z)$ contains the unit circle $|z| = 1$.
$$ X(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n] z^{-n}, \quad \text{ROC: } r_{\min} < |z| < r_{\max} $$ $$ x[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi j} \oint_C X(z) z^{n-1} dz = \sum \text{Res}\left\{ X(z) z^{n-1} \right\} $$ $$ H(z) = \frac{Y(z)}{X(z)}, \quad \text{Stable } \iff \{z : |z| = 1\} \subseteq \text{ROC} $$

2. Fundamental Theorems and Mathematical Operations

Let $x[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} X(z)$ with ROC $R_x$, and $y[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} Y(z)$ with ROC $R_y$.

  • Linearity: $a x[n] + b y[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} a X(z) + b Y(z)$, with $\text{ROC} \supseteq R_x \cap R_y$.
  • Time Shifting (Bilateral): $x[n - k] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} z^{-k} X(z)$, ROC is $R_x$ (except possible addition/deletion of $z=0$ or $z=\infty$).
  • Time Shifting (Unilateral): $$ \mathcal{Z}_u\{x[n-1]\} = z^{-1} X_u(z) + x[-1] $$ $$ \mathcal{Z}_u\{x[n-k]\} = z^{-k} X_u(z) + \sum_{m=1}^{k} x[-m] z^{-(k-m)} $$ $$ \mathcal{Z}_u\{x[n+k]\} = z^{k} X_u(z) - \sum_{m=0}^{k-1} x[m] z^{k-m} $$
  • Scaling in the Z-Domain: $a^n x[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} X(z/a)$, with ROC $|a| R_x$.
  • Time Reversal: $x[-n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} X(1/z)$, with ROC $1/R_x$.
  • Differentiation in the Z-Domain: $n x[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} -z \frac{d}{dz} X(z)$, with ROC $R_x$.
  • Complex Conjugation: $x^*[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} X^*(z^*)$, with ROC $R_x$.
  • Convolution Theorem: $x[n] * y[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} X(z) Y(z)$, with $\text{ROC} \supseteq R_x \cap R_y$.
  • Initial Value Theorem (IVT): For a causal sequence $x[n]$ ($x[n] = 0$ for $n < 0$), $$ x[0] = \lim_{z \to \infty} X(z) $$
  • Final Value Theorem (FVT): For a causal sequence $x[n]$, if all poles of $(z-1) X(z)$ lie strictly inside the unit circle $|z| < 1$, then: $$ \lim_{n \to \infty} x[n] = \lim_{z \to 1} (z-1) X(z) = \lim_{z \to 1} (1 - z^{-1}) X(z) $$

3. Methods of Computing Inverse Z-Transform

Three primary techniques are evaluated in engineering mathematics:

A. Partial Fraction Expansion (PFE)

For rational functions $X(z) = \frac{B(z)}{A(z)}$, write $\frac{X(z)}{z}$ as a sum of simple terms:

$$ \frac{X(z)}{z} = \sum_{k=1}^N \frac{A_k}{z - p_k} \implies X(z) = \sum_{k=1}^N A_k \frac{z}{z - p_k} $$

Applying the standard inverse transform for each term based on ROC boundaries yields:

$$ \mathcal{Z}^{-1}\left\{ \frac{z}{z - p_k} \right\} = \begin{cases} (p_k)^n u[n], & \text{if ROC is } |z| > |p_k| \\ -(p_k)^n u[-n-1], & \text{if ROC is } |z| < |p_k| \end{cases} $$

B. Power Series Expansion (Long Division)

Express $X(z)$ as a formal power series $\sum x[n] z^{-n}$. For a causal sequence (ROC $|z| > r$), divide numerator by denominator in descending powers of $z$ (or $z^{-1}$). For anti-causal sequences (ROC $|z| < r$), divide in ascending powers of $z$.

C. Cauchy's Residue Method (Contour Integration)

By Cauchy’s Residue Theorem, the inverse transformation integral evaluates to:

$$ x[n] = \sum_{k} \text{Res}\left[ X(z) z^{n-1}, p_k \right] $$

where $p_k$ are the poles of $X(z) z^{n-1}$ enclosed by the contour $C$ lying inside the ROC.

  • For a simple pole at $z = p_k$: $$ \text{Res}\left[ X(z) z^{n-1}, p_k \right] = \lim_{z \to p_k} (z - p_k) X(z) z^{n-1} $$
  • For a pole of multiplicity $m$ at $z = p_k$: $$ \text{Res}\left[ X(z) z^{n-1}, p_k \right] = \frac{1}{(m-1)!} \lim_{z \to p_k} \frac{d^{m-1}}{dz^{m-1}} \left[ (z - p_k)^m X(z) z^{n-1} \right] $$
  • Critical Oversight Warning: For $n < 0$, the factor $z^{n-1} = \frac{1}{z^{1-n}}$ introduces a pole at the origin $z = 0$. The residue at $z = 0$ MUST be calculated and included if $z = 0$ lies inside contour $C$.

  • Standard Transform Pairs:
    • $\delta[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} 1, \quad \text{ROC: All } z$
    • $u[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} \frac{z}{z-1} = \frac{1}{1-z^{-1}}, \quad \text{ROC: } |z| > 1$
    • $-u[-n-1] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} \frac{z}{z-1} = \frac{1}{1-z^{-1}}, \quad \text{ROC: } |z| < 1$
    • $a^n u[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} \frac{z}{z-a} = \frac{1}{1-a z^{-1}}, \quad \text{ROC: } |z| > |a|$
    • $-a^n u[-n-1] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} \frac{z}{z-a} = \frac{1}{1-a z^{-1}}, \quad \text{ROC: } |z| < |a|$
    • $n a^n u[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} \frac{a z}{(z-a)^2}, \quad \text{ROC: } |z| > |a|$
    • $\cos(\omega_0 n) u[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} \frac{z(z - \cos \omega_0)}{z^2 - 2z \cos \omega_0 + 1}, \quad \text{ROC: } |z| > 1$
    • $\sin(\omega_0 n) u[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} \frac{z \sin \omega_0}{z^2 - 2z \cos \omega_0 + 1}, \quad \text{ROC: } |z| > 1$
    • $a^n \cos(\omega_0 n) u[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} \frac{z(z - a\cos \omega_0)}{z^2 - 2az \cos \omega_0 + a^2}, \quad \text{ROC: } |z| > |a|$
  • Core Operators & Shift Properties:
    • $\mathcal{Z}\{n x[n]\} = -z \frac{d}{dz} X(z)$
    • $\mathcal{Z}_u\{x[n-1]\} = z^{-1} X_u(z) + x[-1]$
    • $\mathcal{Z}_u\{x[n-2]\} = z^{-2} X_u(z) + z^{-1} x[-1] + x[-2]$
  • Boundary Value Conditions:
    • Initial Value Theorem: $x[0] = \lim_{z \to \infty} X(z)$
    • Final Value Theorem: $\lim_{n \to \infty} x[n] = \lim_{z \to 1} (z-1) X(z) \quad (\text{Valid iff poles of } (z-1)X(z) \text{ lie inside } |z|<1)$

4. Aviation Application (5–10 min)

In modern aerospace systems engineering, digital fly-by-wire (FBW) flight control computers execute discrete algorithms at high sampling frequencies ($f_s = 1/T_s$). Continuous flight dynamics (Euler angles, pitch rate $q(t)$, angle of attack $\alpha(t)$) are discretized using Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) samplers, translating continuous-time Laplace domain dynamics $H(s)$ into discrete pulse transfer functions $H(z)$.

1. Discretization of Digital Autopilot Pitch Control Loop

Consider a continuous-time aircraft pitch damper transfer function $G(s) = \frac{K}{s + a}$. When interfaced with a Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) sampler operating at sampling period $T_s$, the discrete-time domain transfer function $G(z)$ is given by:

$$ G(z) = (1 - z^{-1}) \mathcal{Z}\left\{ \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{ \frac{G(s)}{s} \right\} \right\} = (1 - z^{-1}) \mathcal{Z}\left\{ \frac{K}{a} (1 - e^{-a t}) u(t) \right\} $$

Evaluating the Z-transform of the sampled continuous sequence at $t = n T_s$:

$$ G(z) = (1 - z^{-1}) \frac{K}{a} \left[ \frac{z}{z-1} - \frac{z}{z - e^{-a T_s}} \right] = \frac{K}{a} \frac{1 - e^{-a T_s}}{z - e^{-a T_s}} $$

The single continuous pole at $s = -a$ maps directly to the discrete $z$-plane pole $p_1 = e^{-a T_s}$. For aircraft stability, the discrete pole must reside strictly within the unit circle $|e^{-a T_s}| < 1 \implies a > 0$.

2. Digital Flight Data Recorder (DFDR) Signal Filtering

During an air safety accident investigation, turbulent pitch-rate sensor noise recorded by a Flight Data Recorder (FDR) is processed through a digital IIR filter defined by the recursive difference equation:

$$ y[n] - 1.1 y[n-1] + 0.30 y[n-2] = x[n] + 0.5 x[n-1] $$

Taking the Z-transform yields the system pulse transfer function $H(z)$:

$$ H(z) = \frac{Y(z)}{X(z)} = \frac{1 + 0.5 z^{-1}}{1 - 1.1 z^{-1} + 0.30 z^{-1}} = \frac{z(z + 0.5)}{z^2 - 1.1 z + 0.30} = \frac{z(z + 0.5)}{(z - 0.6)(z - 0.5)} $$

The system poles lie at $z_1 = 0.6$ and $z_2 = 0.5$. Since both poles satisfy $|z_i| < 1$, the digital filter is strictly stable and causal, ensuring that digital filtering of raw FDR telemetry introduces no spurious instabilities during flight trajectory reconstruction.

5. UPSC ASO Focus (10 min)

  • Frequently Asked Facts & Formulas:
    • BIBO Stability Condition: A system is stable if and only if the unit circle $|z|=1$ is contained entirely within the ROC. For causal systems, this requires all poles $p_i$ to lie strictly inside the unit circle ($|p_i| < 1$).
    • Unilateral Shift Formula: $\mathcal{Z}_u\{x[n-2]\} = z^{-2} X(z) + z^{-1} x[-1] + x[-2]$. The positive coefficients of non-zero initial conditions $x[-1], x[-2]$ are frequently tested in ASO control differential equations.
    • Initial Value Theorem Limit: $x[0] = \lim_{z \to \infty} X(z)$. If $X(z) = \frac{N(z)}{D(z)}$, $x[0]$ equals the ratio of leading coefficients if $\text{deg}(N) = \text{deg}(D)$, and $0$ if $\text{deg}(N) < \text{deg}(D)$.
    • Inverse Z-transform via Residues for Negative Indices: To compute $x[n]$ for $n < 0$, check whether $X(z) z^{n-1}$ creates additional poles at $z=0$. For example, for $x[-1]$, $X(z) z^{-2}$ has a double pole or higher at the origin.
  • Conceptual Traps & Pitfalls:
    • The Final Value Theorem Trap: Applying FVT without verifying pole locations. If $X(z) = \frac{z}{z - 2}$, applying FVT yields $\lim_{z \to 1} (z-1) \frac{z}{z-2} = 0$. However, $x[n] = 2^n u[n]$ diverges to $\infty$ because the pole $z = 2$ lies outside the unit circle! FVT fails and yields a false result.
    • Non-Uniqueness Trap: $X(z) = \frac{z}{z - 0.5}$ corresponds to $x[n] = (0.5)^n u[n]$ ONLY if ROC is $|z| > 0.5$. If ROC is $|z| < 0.5$, $x[n] = -(0.5)^n u[-n-1]$. Neglecting the ROC specification renders the answer invalid.
    • Shift Property Confusion: Confusing bilateral shift ($\mathcal{Z}\{x[n-k]\} = z^{-k} X(z)$) with unilateral shift (which includes initial conditions $x[-1], x[-2]$).

6. Practice Questions (Exhaustive UPSC ASO MCQs)

Q1. A discrete-time signal $x[n]$ has the Z-transform $X(z) = \frac{z}{(z-1)(z-2)}$ with an annular Region of Convergence given by $1 < |z| < 2$. The correct sequence $x[n]$ is:
A) $x[n] = u[n] + 2^n u[n]$
B) $x[n] = -u[-n-1] - 2^n u[n]$
C) $x[n] = u[n] - 2^n u[-n-1]$
D) $x[n] = -u[n] - 2^n u[-n-1]$
Detailed Solution:
Express $\frac{X(z)}{z}$ in partial fractions: $$ \frac{X(z)}{z} = \frac{1}{(z-1)(z-2)} = \frac{-1}{z-1} + \frac{1}{z-2} $$ $$ X(z) = \frac{-z}{z-1} + \frac{z}{z-2} = \frac{-1}{1 - z^{-1}} + \frac{1}{1 - 2 z^{-1}} $$ The ROC is $1 < |z| < 2$: - For the pole at $z=1$, the ROC $|z| > 1$ implies a right-sided sequence: $\mathcal{Z}^{-1}\left\{ \frac{-z}{z-1} \right\} = -u[n]$. - For the pole at $z=2$, the ROC $|z| < 2$ implies a left-sided sequence: $\mathcal{Z}^{-1}\left\{ \frac{z}{z-2} \right\} = -2^n u[-n-1]$. Combining both terms gives $x[n] = -u[n] - 2^n u[-n-1]$. Thus, option D is correct.
Q2. Using Cauchy's Residue Theorem, find $x[3]$ for a causal sequence $x[n]$ whose Z-transform is $X(z) = \frac{z}{z^2 - 3z + 2}$ with ROC $|z| > 2$.
A) 3
B) 7
C) 15
D) 8
Detailed Solution:
The inversion formula is $x[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi j} \oint_C X(z) z^{n-1} dz = \sum \text{Res}[X(z) z^{n-1}]$.
$$ X(z) z^{n-1} = \frac{z^n}{(z-1)(z-2)} $$ For $n=3$, $F(z) = X(z) z^2 = \frac{z^3}{(z-1)(z-2)}$. The poles inside contour $C$ ($|z| > 2$) are simple poles at $z=1$ and $z=2$. $$ \text{Res}(F, 1) = \lim_{z \to 1} (z-1) \frac{z^3}{(z-1)(z-2)} = \frac{1^3}{1 - 2} = -1 $$ $$ \text{Res}(F, 2) = \lim_{z \to 2} (z-2) \frac{z^3}{(z-1)(z-2)} = \frac{2^3}{2 - 1} = 8 $$ $$ x[3] = \text{Res}(F, 1) + \text{Res}(F, 2) = -1 + 8 = 7 $$ Thus, option B is correct.
Q3. The inverse Z-transform of $X(z) = \ln\left(1 + a z^{-1}\right)$ for $|z| > |a|$ is:
A) $x[n] = \frac{(-1)^{n-1} a^n}{n} u[n-1]$
B) $x[n] = \frac{(-1)^n a^n}{n} u[n]$
C) $x[n] = \frac{a^n}{n} u[n-1]$
D) $x[n] = \frac{(-1)^{n+1} a^n}{n!} u[n]$
Detailed Solution:
Using the logarithmic Maclaurin series $\ln(1 + v) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{k-1} v^k}{k}$ for $|v| < 1$:
Let $v = a z^{-1}$, valid for $|z| > |a|$: $$ X(z) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{k-1} (a z^{-1})^k}{k} = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{k-1} a^k}{k} z^{-k} $$ Comparing with $X(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n] z^{-n}$, we match coefficients for $n = k \ge 1$: $$ x[n] = \frac{(-1)^{n-1} a^n}{n} \quad \text{for } n \ge 1 $$ In unit step notation, $x[n] = \frac{(-1)^{n-1} a^n}{n} u[n-1]$. Option A is correct.
Q4. Determine the initial value $x[0]$ for a causal sequence whose Z-transform is $X(z) = \frac{2z^2 + 3z + 1}{z^2 - 1.5z + 0.5}$.
A) 0
B) 1
C) 2
D) 6
Detailed Solution:
By the Initial Value Theorem for causal signals: $$ x[0] = \lim_{z \to \infty} X(z) = \lim_{z \to \infty} \frac{2z^2 + 3z + 1}{z^2 - 1.5z + 0.5} $$ Divide numerator and denominator by $z^2$: $$ x[0] = \lim_{z \to \infty} \frac{2 + \frac{3}{z} + \frac{1}{z^2}}{1 - \frac{1.5}{z} + \frac{0.5}{z^2}} = \frac{2 + 0 + 0}{1 - 0 + 0} = 2 $$ Thus, option C is correct.
Q5. Find $\lim_{n \to \infty} x[n]$ for a system with $X(z) = \frac{z}{(z-1)(z-0.5)}$ given $x[n]$ is causal.
A) 0
B) 1
C) 2
D) Limit does not exist
Detailed Solution:
First verify if the Final Value Theorem (FVT) applies.
Poles of $X(z)$ are $z = 1$ and $z = 0.5$. The poles of $(z-1)X(z) = \frac{z}{z-0.5}$ is $z=0.5$, which lies strictly inside the unit circle $|z| < 1$. Thus, FVT is applicable.
Applying FVT: $$ \lim_{n \to \infty} x[n] = \lim_{z \to 1} (z-1) X(z) = \lim_{z \to 1} (z-1) \frac{z}{(z-1)(z-0.5)} = \lim_{z \to 1} \frac{z}{z - 0.5} = \frac{1}{1 - 0.5} = 2 $$ Option C is correct.
Q6. A discrete LTI flight system satisfies $y[n] - 0.5 y[n-1] = u[n]$ with non-zero initial condition $y[-1] = 2$. The unilateral Z-transform $Y(z)$ is:
A) $Y(z) = \frac{2z - 1}{(1 - 0.5z^{-1})(1 - z^{-1})}$
B) $Y(z) = \frac{2 - z^{-1}}{(1 - 0.5z^{-1})(1 - z^{-1})}$
C) $Y(z) = \frac{1}{(1 - 0.5z^{-1})(1 - z^{-1})}$
D) $Y(z) = \frac{z^{-1}}{(1 - 0.5z^{-1})(1 - z^{-1})}$
Detailed Solution:
Apply the unilateral Z-transform shift property $\mathcal{Z}_u\{y[n-1]\} = z^{-1} Y(z) + y[-1]$: $$ Y(z) - 0.5 \left[ z^{-1} Y(z) + y[-1] \right] = \mathcal{Z}\{u[n]\} = \frac{1}{1 - z^{-1}} $$ Substitute $y[-1] = 2$: $$ Y(z) (1 - 0.5 z^{-1}) - 0.5(2) = \frac{1}{1 - z^{-1}} $$ $$ Y(z) (1 - 0.5 z^{-1}) - 1 = \frac{1}{1 - z^{-1}} \implies Y(z) (1 - 0.5 z^{-1}) = 1 + \frac{1}{1 - z^{-1}} = \frac{2 - z^{-1}}{1 - z^{-1}} $$ $$ Y(z) = \frac{2 - z^{-1}}{(1 - 0.5 z^{-1})(1 - z^{-1})} $$ Option B is correct.
Q7. What is the Z-transform of the sequence $x[n] = n a^n u[n]$?
A) $\frac{a z}{(z-a)^2}, \quad |z| > |a|$
B) $\frac{z}{(z-a)^2}, \quad |z| > |a|$
C) $\frac{a z^{-1}}{(1-a z^{-1})^2}, \quad |z| < |a|$
D) $\frac{a}{(z-a)^2}, \quad |z| > |a|$
Detailed Solution:
We know $a^n u[n] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} \frac{z}{z-a}$.
Applying differentiation in the z-domain $\mathcal{Z}\{n x[n]\} = -z \frac{d}{dz} X(z)$: $$ \mathcal{Z}\{n a^n u[n]\} = -z \frac{d}{dz} \left( \frac{z}{z-a} \right) = -z \left[ \frac{(z-a)(1) - z(1)}{(z-a)^2} \right] = -z \left[ \frac{-a}{(z-a)^2} \right] = \frac{a z}{(z-a)^2} $$ The ROC remains $|z| > |a|$. Thus, option A is correct.
Q8. Which of the following statements regarding the Region of Convergence (ROC) of a Z-transform is INCORRECT?
A) The ROC consists of concentric rings in the complex z-plane.
B) The ROC must contain at least one pole of $X(z)$.
C) If $x[n]$ is a causal sequence, the ROC is of the form $|z| > r_{\max}$.
D) For an LTI system to be BIBO stable, its ROC must contain the unit circle $|z| = 1$.
Detailed Solution:
By definition, $X(z) \to \infty$ at any pole. Hence, the power series diverges at poles. Therefore, the ROC cannot contain any poles of $X(z)$. Statement B is incorrect, making B the correct answer choice.
Q9. Evaluate $x[-1]$ using contour integration for $X(z) = \frac{z}{z - 0.5}$ with ROC $|z| > 0.5$.
A) 0
B) 0.5
C) 1
D) -0.5
Detailed Solution:
Using the inversion integral: $x[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi j} \oint_C X(z) z^{n-1} dz$.
For $n = -1$: $$ F(z) = X(z) z^{-2} = \frac{z}{z - 0.5} \cdot z^{-2} = \frac{1}{z(z - 0.5)} $$ The integrand $F(z)$ has two simple poles: $z_1 = 0$ and $z_2 = 0.5$. For ROC $|z| > 0.5$, the contour $C$ encloses both poles.
$$ \text{Res}(F, 0) = \lim_{z \to 0} z \left( \frac{1}{z(z - 0.5)} \right) = \frac{1}{-0.5} = -2 $$ $$ \text{Res}(F, 0.5) = \lim_{z \to 0.5} (z - 0.5) \left( \frac{1}{z(z - 0.5)} \right) = \frac{1}{0.5} = 2 $$ $$ x[-1] = \text{Res}(F, 0) + \text{Res}(F, 0.5) = -2 + 2 = 0 $$ This matches causal sequence behavior since $x[n] = (0.5)^n u[n]$ has $x[-1] = 0$. Option A is correct.

A) $\frac{z^2}{(z - 0.5)(z - 0.8)}, \quad |z| > 0.8$
B) $\frac{z}{(z - 0.5)(z - 0.8)}, \quad |z| > 0.8$
C) $\frac{z^2}{(z - 0.5)(z - 0.8)}, \quad |z| < 0.5$
D) $\frac{1}{(z - 0.5)(z - 0.8)}, \quad 0.5 < |z| < 0.8$
Detailed Solution:
By the Convolution Theorem: $Y(z) = X_1(z) \cdot X_2(z)$.
$X_1(z) = \frac{z}{z - 0.5}$, ROC $R_1: |z| > 0.5$.
$X_2(z) = \frac{z}{z - 0.8}$, ROC $R_2: |z| > 0.8$.
$$ Y(z) = \frac{z}{z - 0.5} \cdot \frac{z}{z - 0.8} = \frac{z^2}{(z - 0.5)(z - 0.8)} $$ The combined ROC is $R_1 \cap R_2 = \{z : |z| > 0.5\} \cap \{z : |z| > 0.8\} = \{z : |z| > 0.8\}$. Option A is correct.
Q11. Using Power Series expansion (long division), find $x[2]$ for a causal system with $X(z) = \frac{1}{1 - 1.5 z^{-1} + 0.5 z^{-2}}$.
A) 1.0
B) 1.5
C) 1.75
D) 2.25
Detailed Solution:
Perform long division of $1$ by $(1 - 1.5 z^{-1} + 0.5 z^{-2})$:
1) First term: $1$. Multiply divisor: $1 - 1.5 z^{-1} + 0.5 z^{-2}$. Subtract $\implies 1.5 z^{-1} - 0.5 z^{-2}$.
2) Second term: $+ 1.5 z^{-1}$. Multiply divisor: $1.5 z^{-1} - 2.25 z^{-2} + 0.75 z^{-3}$. Subtract $\implies 1.75 z^{-2} - 0.75 z^{-3}$.
3) Third term: $+ 1.75 z^{-2}$.
The series expansion is $X(z) = 1 + 1.5 z^{-1} + 1.75 z^{-2} + \dots$
Comparing with $\sum x[n] z^{-n}$, we get $x[0] = 1$, $x[1] = 1.5$, and $x[2] = 1.75$. Option C is correct.
Q12. A discrete-time flight control system transfer function is $H(z) = \frac{z}{z^2 - 2.5z + 1}$. For the system to be causal, its BIBO stability status is:
A) Stable, because it has a zero at $z=0$.
B) Unstable, because one pole lies outside the unit circle at $z=2$.
C) Marginally stable, because poles are real.
D) Stable, because ROC is $0.5 < |z| < 2$.
Detailed Solution:
Find poles by setting $z^2 - 2.5z + 1 = 0 \implies (z - 0.5)(z - 2) = 0$.
Poles are at $z_1 = 0.5$ and $z_2 = 2$.
If the system is **causal**, the ROC must be the exterior of the outermost pole: $|z| > 2$.
Since the ROC $|z| > 2$ does NOT include the unit circle $|z| = 1$, the causal system is **UNSTABLE**. Option B is correct.
Q13. What is the Z-transform of $x[n] = -\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^n u[-n-1] - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n u[-n-1]$?
A) $\frac{z}{z - 1/3} + \frac{z}{z - 1/2}$ with ROC $|z| < 1/3$
B) $\frac{z}{z - 1/3} + \frac{z}{z - 1/2}$ with ROC $|z| > 1/2$
C) $\frac{z}{z - 1/3} - \frac{z}{z - 1/2}$ with ROC $1/3 < |z| < 1/2$
D) Transform does not exist
Detailed Solution:
Recall standard pair: $-a^n u[-n-1] \overset{\mathcal{Z}}{\longleftrightarrow} \frac{z}{z-a}$ with ROC $|z| < |a|$.
Term 1: $-\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^n u[-n-1] \longleftrightarrow \frac{z}{z - 1/3}$, ROC $R_1: |z| < 1/3$.
Term 2: $-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n u[-n-1] \longleftrightarrow \frac{z}{z - 1/2}$, ROC $R_2: |z| < 1/2$.
Overall ROC is $R_1 \cap R_2 = \{z : |z| < 1/3\} \cap \{z : |z| < 1/2\} = \{z : |z| < 1/3\}$.
Thus $X(z) = \frac{z}{z - 1/3} + \frac{z}{z - 1/2}$ with ROC $|z| < 1/3$. Option A is correct.

Configuration II: $0.4 < |z| < 1.25$

A) Config I is causal and stable; Config II is non-causal and unstable.
B) Config I is causal and unstable; Config II is non-causal (two-sided) and BIBO stable.
C) Config III is anti-causal and BIBO stable.
D) Config II is causal and BIBO stable.

Poles of $H(z)$ are $p_1 = 0.4$ and $p_2 = 1.25$. - **Config I ($|z| > 1.25$):** Exterior of outermost pole $\implies$ System is **causal**. Q702 [Grand Mini Test]. Compute $x[0]$ using Cauchy's Residue Method for $X(z) = \frac{z + 2}{z(z - 0.5)}$ where ROC is $|z| > 0.5$.
A) 0
B) 1
C) -4
D) 5
Grand Mini Test Solution:
Apply $x[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi j} \oint_C X(z) z^{n-1} dz$. For $n = 0$: $$ F(z) = X(z) z^{-1} = \frac{z + 2}{z(z - 0.5)} \cdot z^{-1} = \frac{z + 2}{z^2 (z - 0.5)} $$ $F(z)$ has a double pole at $z = 0$ and a simple pole at $z = 0.5$. The contour $C$ ($|z| > 0.5$) encloses both poles.
- Residue at simple pole $z = 0.5$: $$ \text{Res}(F, 0.5) = \lim_{z \to 0.5} (z - 0.5) \frac{z + 2}{z^2 (z - 0.5)} = \frac{0.5 + 2}{(0.5)^2} = \frac{2.5}{0.25} = 10 $$ - Residue at double pole $z = 0$: $$ \text{Res}(F, 0) = \frac{1}{(2-1)!} \lim_{z \to 0} \frac{d}{dz} \left[ z^2 \frac{z + 2}{z^2 (z - 0.5)} \right] = \lim_{z \to 0} \frac{d}{dz} \left[ \frac{z + 2}{z - 0.5} \right] $$ $$ = \lim_{z \to 0} \frac{(1)(z - 0.5) - (z + 2)(1)}{(z - 0.5)^2} = \frac{-0.5 - 2}{(-0.5)^2} = \frac{-2.5}{0.25} = -10 $$ Sum of residues: $x[0] = \text{Res}(F, 0.5) + \text{Res}(F, 0) = 10 + (-10) = 0$.
(Verification via Initial Value Theorem: $\lim_{z \to \infty} \frac{z+2}{z^2 - 0.5z} = 0$). Option A is correct.

Q704 [Grand Mini Test]. Consider the following statements:
Assertion (A): The Final Value Theorem can be applied to determine the steady-state response of a system with $X(z) = \frac{z}{z - 1.5}$.
Reason (R): The Final Value Theorem states that $\lim_{n \to \infty} x[n] = \lim_{z \to 1} (z-1) X(z)$, which yields 0 for $X(z) = \frac{z}{z - 1.5}$.
A) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
B) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A).
C) (A) is false, but (R) is true as an algebraic limit evaluation despite FVT being inapplicable.
D) Both (A) and (R) are false.
Grand Mini Test Solution:
- **Assertion (A) is FALSE:** The FVT requires all poles of $(z-1)X(z)$ to lie strictly inside the unit circle ($|p_k| < 1$). Here, $X(z)$ has a pole at $z = 1.5 > 1$, which corresponds to an unstable exploding sequence $x[n] = (1.5)^n u[n]$. FVT cannot be applied.

8. Error Analysis & Common Traps (5 min)

To score top marks in UPSC ASO and GATE examinations, students must actively eliminate the following high-frequency mathematical errors when solving Z-transform problems:

1. Omitting the Extra $z^{-1}$ Factor in Contour Residue Integration

A frequent mistake in applying Cauchy's Residue Theorem $x[n] = \sum \text{Res}[X(z) z^{n-1}]$ is replacing $z^{n-1}$ with $z^n$. For $n = 0$, $X(z) z^{-1}$ introduces a new pole at $z = 0$. Neglecting this pole at $z = 0$ leads to incorrect transient term calculations.

2. Blind Application of the Final Value Theorem (FVT)

Students frequently apply $\lim_{z \to 1} (z-1) X(z)$ without checking pole locations. If $X(z)$ has poles on or outside the unit circle ($|p_i| \ge 1$), the time sequence $x[n]$ is oscillatory or divergent, making FVT invalid.

### 3. Confusion Between Bilateral and Unilateral Time-Shift Properties

In bilateral Z-transforms, $\mathcal{Z}\{x[n-1]\} = z^{-1} X(z)$. However, in unilateral Z-transforms (used for non-zero initial condition differential equations), $\mathcal{Z}_u\{x[n-1]\} = z^{-1} X_u(z) + x[-1]$. Forgetting $+ x[-1]$ or using the wrong sign leads to errors in initial condition problems.

4. Inverse Z-Transform Without Specifying ROC

The algebraic function $X(z) = \frac{z}{z - a}$ corresponds to two entirely distinct signals:

$$ x[n] = \begin{cases} a^n u[n], & \text{if ROC is } |z| > |a| \text{ (Causal)} \\ -a^n u[-n-1], & \text{if ROC is } |z| < |a| \text{ (Anti-causal)} \end{cases} $$

Inverting $X(z)$ without confirming the ROC context results in choosing the wrong time-domain representation.
