Gradient, divergence, curl

Day 3 of 129 Engineering Fundamentals Phase 1: Foundations 60-90 min Master Cycle

1. Understand (5–10 min)

Vector calculus forms the mathematical bedrock of classical field theory, fluid dynamics, aerodynamics, and flight control systems evaluated in the UPSC Air Safety Officer (ASO) examination. The differential operations—Gradient, Divergence, and Curl—represent spatial derivative transformations applied to scalar and vector fields defined over three-dimensional Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^3$ or generalized curvilinear domains.

The foundational differential operator in vector calculus is the vector differential operator Del (or Nabla), defined in Cartesian coordinates as:

$$\nabla = \hat{i}∂∂ x + \hat{j}∂∂ y + \hat{k}∂∂ z$$

The operational definitions and domain parameters for these vector differential operators are established as follows:

  • Gradient ($\nabla f$): A linear differential operator mapping a scalar field $f(x,y,z) \in C^1(\mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R})$ to a vector field $\mathbf{V} \in C^0(\mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3)$. Geometrically, $\nabla f$ points in the direction of the maximum rate of increase of the scalar field $f$, and its magnitude equals this maximum rate of change.
  • Divergence ($\nabla · \mathbf{F}$): A linear differential operator mapping a vector field $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) \in C^1(\mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3)$ to a scalar field $s \in C^0(\mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R})$. Geometrically, it measures the net outward flux of the vector field per unit volume surrounding an infinitesimal point—characterizing sources ($\nabla · \mathbf{F} > 0$) or sinks ($\nabla · \mathbf{F} < 0$). Fields with $\nabla · \mathbf{F} = 0$ everywhere are termed solenoidal.
  • Curl ($\nabla × \mathbf{F}$): A linear differential operator mapping a vector field $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) \in C^1(\mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3)$ to another vector field $\mathbf{\Omega} \in C^0(\mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3)$. Geometrically, it quantifies the circulation density or micro-rotational tendency of the vector field about an axis at a point. Fields with $\nabla × \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$ everywhere are termed irrotational or conservative.

Domain Bounds and Continuity Rules: For these operators to be well-defined at a point $P(x_0, y_0, z_0)$, the component functions of $f$ or $\mathbf{F}$ must be continuously differentiable ($C^1$ smoothness class) in an open neighborhood containing $P$. Higher-order combinations (such as the Laplacian $\nabla^2 f = \nabla · \nabla f$) require $C^2$ smoothness.

2. Learn the Concept (15–20 min)

To master vector calculus for high-level competitive examinations like UPSC ASO and GATE Aeronautical/Aerospace, one must understand the rigorous definitions, coordinate transformations, geometric meanings, and classical vector identities governing Gradient, Divergence, and Curl.

1. Detailed Conceptual Breakdown

A. Gradient and Directional Derivatives

Let $f(x,y,z)$ be a continuous scalar field. The gradient of $f$ is given by:

$$\nabla f = ∂ f∂ x\hat{i} + ∂ f∂ y\hat{j} + ∂ f∂ z\hat{k}$$

Key Properties:

  1. Normal Vector to Level Surfaces: If $f(x,y,z) = c$ defines an implicit level surface $S$, then $\nabla f$ evaluated at point $P$ is perpendicular (normal) to the tangent plane of $S$ at $P$. The unit normal vector is: $$\hat{n} = \nabla f|\nabla f|$$
  2. Directional Derivative: The rate of change of $f$ at point $P$ in the direction of an arbitrary unit vector $\hat{u} = u_x \hat{i} + u_y \hat{j} + u_z \hat{k}$ is defined as: $$D_{\hat{u}}f = \nabla f · \hat{u} = |\nabla f| |\hat{u}| \cosθ = |\nabla f| \cosθ$$ The maximum value of $D_{\hat{u}}f$ occurs when $θ = 0^°$ (i.e., when $\hat{u}$ is parallel to $\nabla f$), and this maximum value is precisely $|\nabla f|$. The minimum value is $-|\nabla f|$, occurring when $θ = 180^°$. The rate of change is zero in any direction orthogonal to $\nabla f$ (i.e., along the level surface).

B. Divergence and Solenoidal Fields

Let $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = F_x \hat{i} + F_y \hat{j} + F_z \hat{k}$ be a differentiable vector field. The divergence of $\mathbf{F}$ is defined as the dot product of $\nabla$ with $\mathbf{F}$:

$$\nabla · \mathbf{F} = ∂ F_x∂ x + ∂ F_y∂ y + ∂ F_z∂ z$$

Physical Interpretation: By the Divergence Theorem, $\iiint_V (\nabla · \mathbf{F}) dV = \iint_S \mathbf{F} · \hat{n} dS$. Hence, divergence at a point represents the limit of outward flux per unit volume as the volume shrinks to zero:

$$\nabla · \mathbf{F}(P) = \limΔ V \to 0 1Δ V \iintΔ S \mathbf{F} · \hat{n} dS$$

If $\nabla · \mathbf{F} = 0$, the fluid or field is incompressible (no net creation or destruction of fluid mass within the region). Such vector fields are called solenoidal.

C. Curl, Vorticity, and Irrotational Fields

The curl of $\mathbf{F}$ is defined as the cross product of $\nabla$ with $\mathbf{F}$:

$$\nabla × \mathbf{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ ∂∂ x & ∂∂ y & ∂∂ z \\ F_x & F_y & F_z \end{vmatrix} = (∂ F_z∂ y - ∂ F_y∂ z)\hat{i} + (∂ F_x∂ z - ∂ F_z∂ x)\hat{j} + (∂ F_y∂ x - ∂ F_x∂ y)\hat{k}$$

Physical Interpretation & Vorticity: By Stokes' Theorem, $\iint_S (\nabla × \mathbf{F}) · \hat{n} dS = \oint_C \mathbf{F} · d\mathbf{r}$. In fluid mechanics, if $\mathbf{v}$ is the fluid velocity vector field, the vorticity vector $\mathbf{ω}$ is defined as twice the local angular velocity vector $\mathbf{\Omega}$ of a fluid particle:

$$\mathbf{ω} = \nabla × \mathbf{v} = 2\mathbf{\Omega}$$

If $\nabla × \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$, the field is irrotational (conservative). In a simply connected domain, an irrotational field can always be expressed as the gradient of a scalar potential function $φ$:

$$\mathbf{F} = \nabla φ \quad or \quad \mathbf{F} = -\nabla φ$$
$$ \begin{aligned} Gradient: \quad &\nabla f = ∂ f∂ x\hat{i} + ∂ f∂ y\hat{j} + ∂ f∂ z\hat{k} \\ Divergence: \quad &\nabla · \mathbf{F} = ∂ F_x∂ x + ∂ F_y∂ y + ∂ F_z∂ z \\ Curl: \quad &\nabla × \mathbf{F} = (∂ F_z∂ y - ∂ F_y∂ z)\hat{i} + (∂ F_x∂ z - ∂ F_z∂ x)\hat{j} + (∂ F_y∂ x - ∂ F_x∂ y)\hat{k} \\ Laplacian: \quad &\nabla^2 f = \nabla · (\nabla f) = ∂^2 f∂ x^2 + ∂^2 f∂ y^2 + ∂^2 f∂ z^2 \\ Vector Laplacian: \quad &\nabla^2 \mathbf{A} = \nabla(\nabla · \mathbf{A}) - \nabla × (\nabla × \mathbf{A}) \end{aligned} $$

2. Vector Operators in Orthogonal Curvilinear Coordinates

Let $(u_1, u_2, u_3)$ be orthogonal curvilinear coordinates with scale factors $h_1, h_2, h_3$, where $d\mathbf{r} = h_1 du_1 \hat{e}_1 + h_2 du_2 \hat{e}_2 + h_3 du_3 \hat{e}_3$. The general forms are:

$$\nabla f = 1h_1∂ f∂ u_1\hat{e}_1 + 1h_2∂ f∂ u_2\hat{e}_2 + 1h_3∂ f∂ u_3\hat{e}_3$$ $$\nabla · \mathbf{F} = 1h_1 h_2 h_3 [ ∂∂ u_1(h_2 h_3 F_1) + ∂∂ u_2(h_1 h_3 F_2) + ∂∂ u_3(h_1 h_2 F_3) ]$$ $$\nabla × \mathbf{F} = 1h_1 h_2 h_3 \begin{vmatrix} h_1 \hat{e}_1 & h_2 \hat{e}_2 & h_3 \hat{e}_3 \\ ∂∂ u_1 & ∂∂ u_2 & ∂∂ u_3 \\ h_1 F_1 & h_2 F_2 & h_3 F_3 \end{vmatrix}$$

Coordinate System Scale Factors:

  • Cartesian $(x, y, z)$: $h_x = 1, h_y = 1, h_z = 1$
  • Cylindrical Polar $(r, θ, z)$: $h_r = 1, h_θ = r, h_z = 1$
  • Spherical Polar $(r, θ, φ)$: $h_r = 1, h_θ = r, h_φ = r \sinθ$

3. Step-by-Step Rigorous Mathematical Derivations

Derivation 1: Curl of a Gradient is Identically Zero ($\nabla × (\nabla f) = \mathbf{0}$)

Let $f(x,y,z)$ be a scalar field of class $C^2$. Then $\nabla f = ∂ f∂ x\hat{i} + ∂ f∂ y\hat{j} + ∂ f∂ z\hat{k}$. Computing the curl:

$$\nabla × (\nabla f) = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ ∂∂ x & ∂∂ y & ∂∂ z \\ ∂ f∂ x & ∂ f∂ y & ∂ f∂ z \end{vmatrix}$$ $$\nabla × (\nabla f) = ( ∂^2 f∂ y ∂ z - ∂^2 f∂ z ∂ y )\hat{i} + ( ∂^2 f∂ z ∂ x - ∂^2 f∂ x ∂ z )\hat{j} + ( ∂^2 f∂ x ∂ y - ∂^2 f∂ y ∂ x )\hat{k}$$

By Schwarz's Theorem (Clairaut's Theorem) on equality of mixed partial derivatives for $C^2$ functions, $∂^2 f∂ y ∂ z = ∂^2 f∂ z ∂ y$, etc. Therefore, each component vanishes identically:

$$\nabla × (\nabla f) = 0\hat{i} + 0\hat{j} + 0\hat{k} = \mathbf{0}$$

Derivation 2: Divergence of a Curl is Identically Zero ($\nabla · (\nabla × \mathbf{A}) = 0$)

Let $\mathbf{A} = A_x\hat{i} + A_y\hat{j} + A_z\hat{k}$ be a $C^2$ vector field. The curl is $\mathbf{B} = \nabla × \mathbf{A} = (∂ A_z∂ y - ∂ A_y∂ z)\hat{i} + (∂ A_x∂ z - ∂ A_z∂ x)\hat{j} + (∂ A_y∂ x - ∂ A_x∂ y)\hat{k}$. Computing the divergence of $\mathbf{B}$:

$$\nabla · (\nabla × \mathbf{A}) = ∂∂ x(∂ A_z∂ y - ∂ A_y∂ z) + ∂∂ y(∂ A_x∂ z - ∂ A_z∂ x) + ∂∂ z(∂ A_y∂ x - ∂ A_x∂ y)$$ $$\nabla · (\nabla × \mathbf{A}) = (∂^2 A_z∂ x ∂ y - ∂^2 A_y∂ x ∂ z) + (∂^2 A_x∂ y ∂ z - ∂^2 A_z∂ y ∂ x) + (∂^2 A_y∂ z ∂ x - ∂^2 A_x∂ z ∂ y)$$

Grouping identical terms with opposite signs:

$$\nabla · (\nabla × \mathbf{A}) = (∂^2 A_z∂ x ∂ y - ∂^2 A_z∂ y ∂ x) + (∂^2 A_x∂ y ∂ z - ∂^2 A_x∂ z ∂ y) + (∂^2 A_y∂ z ∂ x - ∂^2 A_y∂ x ∂ z) = 0$$

Derivation 3: Radial Vector Identities ($\mathbf{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$, $r = |\mathbf{r}|$)

  1. Gradient of $r^n$: Note that $∂ r∂ x = xr$, $∂ r∂ y = yr$, $∂ r∂ z = zr$, so $\nabla r = \mathbf{r}r = \hat{r}$. $$\nabla(r^n) = n rn-1 \nabla r = n rn-1 \mathbf{r}r = n rn-2 \mathbf{r}$$
  2. Divergence of $r^n \mathbf{r}$: Using the product rule $\nabla · (f\mathbf{A}) = f(\nabla · \mathbf{A}) + \mathbf{A} · \nabla f$: $$\nabla · (r^n \mathbf{r}) = r^n (\nabla · \mathbf{r}) + \mathbf{r} · \nabla(r^n)$$ Since $\nabla · \mathbf{r} = ∂ x∂ x + ∂ y∂ y + ∂ z∂ z = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3$: $$\nabla · (r^n \mathbf{r}) = 3 r^n + \mathbf{r} · (n rn-2 \mathbf{r}) = 3 r^n + n rn-2 (\mathbf{r} · \mathbf{r}) = 3 r^n + n rn-2 (r^2) = (n + 3) r^n$$
  3. Laplacian of $r^n$: $$\nabla^2(r^n) = \nabla · \nabla(r^n) = \nabla · (n rn-2 \mathbf{r})$$ Applying the previous identity with power $(n-2)$: $$\nabla^2(r^n) = n [ (n - 2) + 3 ] rn-2 = n(n + 1) rn-2$$ Special Case: For $n = -1$, $\nabla^2(1/r) = (-1)(-1 + 1)r-3 = 0$ for $r \neq 0$.

3. Memorize Important Facts (10 min)

  • Core Gradient Definition: $\nabla f = ∂ f∂ x\hat{i} + ∂ f∂ y\hat{j} + ∂ f∂ z\hat{k}$
  • Directional Derivative: $D_{\hat{u}}f = \nabla f · \hat{u} = |\nabla f| \cos θ$
  • Unit Normal to Surface $f(x,y,z)=c$: $\hat{n} = \nabla f|\nabla f|$
  • Divergence Definition: $\nabla · \mathbf{F} = ∂ F_x∂ x + ∂ F_y∂ y + ∂ F_z∂ z$
  • Curl Definition: $\nabla × \mathbf{F} = (∂ F_z∂ y - ∂ F_y∂ z)\hat{i} + (∂ F_x∂ z - ∂ F_z∂ x)\hat{j} + (∂ F_y∂ x - ∂ F_x∂ y)\hat{k}$
  • Fundamental Vector Null Identities:
    • $\nabla × (\nabla f) = \mathbf{0}$ (Curl of any Gradient is zero)
    • $\nabla · (\nabla × \mathbf{A}) = 0$ (Divergence of any Curl is zero)
  • Vector Laplacian Identity: $\nabla × (\nabla × \mathbf{A}) = \nabla(\nabla · \mathbf{A}) - \nabla^2 \mathbf{A}$
  • Divergence of Cross Product Identity: $\nabla · (\mathbf{A} × \mathbf{B}) = \mathbf{B} · (\nabla × \mathbf{A}) - \mathbf{A} · (\nabla × \mathbf{B})$
  • Radial Vector ($\mathbf{r}$) Key Formulas:
    • $\nabla · \mathbf{r} = 3$
    • $\nabla × \mathbf{r} = \mathbf{0}$
    • $\nabla(r^n) = n rn-2\mathbf{r}$
    • $\nabla · (r^n \mathbf{r}) = (n + 3)r^n$
    • $\nabla × (r^n \mathbf{r}) = \mathbf{0}$
    • $\nabla^2(r^n) = n(n + 1)rn-2$
  • Cylindrical Divergence: $\nabla · \mathbf{A} = 1r∂∂ r(r A_r) + 1r∂ A_θ∂ θ + ∂ A_z∂ z$
  • Spherical Laplacian: $\nabla^2 f = 1r^2∂∂ r(r^2 ∂ f∂ r) + 1r^2 \sinθ∂∂ θ(\sinθ ∂ f∂ θ) + 1r^2 \sin^2θ∂^2 f∂ φ^2$

4. Aviation Application (5–10 min)

In Aeronautical Engineering, Flight Operations, and Air Safety Investigations, vector differential calculus is the fundamental language used to model aerodynamics, aircraft dynamics, and environmental hazards.

1. Aerodynamics & Vorticity Theory

The aerodynamic lift generated by an aircraft wing is directly linked to circulation $\Gamma = \oint_C \mathbf{v} · d\mathbf{r}$ via the Kutta-Joukowski theorem ($L' = ρ_∞ V_∞ \Gamma$). By Stokes' theorem, this circulation is the surface integral of the fluid vorticity:

$$\mathbf{ω} = \nabla × \mathbf{v}$$

In aircraft wake turbulence analysis, trailing wing-tip vortices are modeled as concentrated regions of curl ($\nabla × \mathbf{v} \neq \mathbf{0}$). Air Safety Officers analyze vortex core decay rates using Helmholtz's vorticity transport equation:

$$D\mathbf{ω}Dt = (\mathbf{ω} · \nabla)\mathbf{v} + ν \nabla^2 \mathbf{ω}$$

where $(\mathbf{ω} · \nabla)\mathbf{v}$ represents vortex stretching/tilting and $ν \nabla^2 \mathbf{ω}$ represents viscous dissipation of the hazard core.

2. Compressible Aerodynamics & Mass Conservation

In high-speed flight regimes (transonic/supersonic), fluid density $ρ$ varies spatially. The continuity equation governing flow over aerodynamic surfaces is expressed via divergence:

$$∂ ρ∂ t + \nabla · (ρ \mathbf{v}) = 0$$

For steady incompressible flow ($ρ = const$), this reduces to $\nabla · \mathbf{v} = 0$, meaning the velocity field is solenoidal. In aircraft accident investigation involving engine flameout or unstart, localized velocity divergence $\nabla · \mathbf{v} \neq 0$ highlights boundary layer separation and shock-wave boundary-layer interactions (SWBLI).

3. Aircraft Microburst / Low-Level Windshear Safety Detection

Microbursts present extreme hazards during final approach and takeoff. A microburst consists of a localized downburst of air impacting the ground and flowing outward. Airborne Doppler radar and flight data recorders (FDR) evaluate the spatial velocity field $\mathbf{v}wind(x,y,z)$ to compute the hazard index ($F$-factor):

$$F = d wdtg + \nabla · \mathbf{v}horizontalg$$

A strongly negative horizontal divergence ($\nabla · \mathbf{v}_h < 0$) combined with vertical velocity gradients signals imminent microburst hazard, allowing automatic flight guidance computers to trigger immediate go-around alerts.

5. UPSC ASO Focus (10 min)

  • Frequently Asked Facts & Formulas:
    • Condition for a field to be Solenoidal: $\nabla · \mathbf{F} = 0$.
    • Condition for a field to be Irrotational / Conservative: $\nabla × \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$.
    • $\nabla^2 (1/r) = 0$ everywhere except at the origin $r=0$, where it represents $-4π \delta(\mathbf{r})$ (Dirac delta function).
    • Maximum rate of change of scalar field $f$ at point $P$ is $|\nabla f|$; it acts along the normal vector to the level surface.
    • Work done by conservative force field $\mathbf{F} = \nabla φ$ along any path from $A$ to $B$ is path-independent: $W = ∫_A^B \mathbf{F} · d\mathbf{r} = φ(B) - φ(A)$.
    • Angle $θ$ between two surfaces $f(x,y,z)=c_1$ and $g(x,y,z)=c_2$ at point $P$: $\cos θ = \nabla f · \nabla g|\nabla f| |\nabla g|$.
  • Conceptual Traps & Pitfalls:
    • Trap 1: Confusing scalar Laplacian $\nabla^2 f$ with vector Laplacian $\nabla^2 \mathbf{A}$. Note that $\nabla^2 \mathbf{A} = \nabla(\nabla · \mathbf{A}) - \nabla × (\nabla × \mathbf{A})$.
    • Trap 2: Forgetting scale factors when working in polar or curvilinear coordinates. For example, in cylindrical coordinates, $\nabla f = ∂ f∂ r\hat{e}_r + 1r∂ f∂ θ\hat{e}_θ + ∂ f∂ z\hat{e}_z$. Missing the $1r$ factor is a very common numerical mistake in UPSC exams.
    • Trap 3: Assuming $\nabla(\mathbf{A} · \mathbf{B}) = (\mathbf{A} · \nabla)\mathbf{B} + (\mathbf{B} · \nabla)\mathbf{A}$. This is incomplete! The full identity requires cross product corrections: $\nabla(\mathbf{A} · \mathbf{B}) = (\mathbf{A} · \nabla)\mathbf{B} + (\mathbf{B} · \nabla)\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{A} × (\nabla × \mathbf{B}) + \mathbf{B} × (\nabla × \mathbf{A})$.
    • Trap 4: Forgetting unit vector normalization in directional derivative problems ($D_{\mathbf{v}} f = \nabla f · \mathbf{v}|\mathbf{v}|$). Dividing by the magnitude $|\mathbf{v}|$ is required unless $\mathbf{v}$ is already specified as a unit vector.

6. Practice Questions (Exhaustive UPSC ASO MCQs)

Q1. Find the directional derivative of the scalar function $f(x,y,z) = x^2 yz + 4xz^2$ at the point $P(1, -1, 2)$ in the direction of the vector $\mathbf{v} = 2\hat{i} - \hat{j} - 2\hat{k}$.
A) $83$
B) $-83$
C) $143$
D) $-143$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Compute the gradient of $f(x,y,z)$: $$\nabla f = ∂ f∂ x\hat{i} + ∂ f∂ y\hat{j} + ∂ f∂ z\hat{k} = (2xyz + 4z^2)\hat{i} + (x^2 z)\hat{j} + (x^2 y + 8xz)\hat{k}$$ 2. Evaluate $\nabla f$ at $P(1, -1, 2)$: $$∂ f∂ x = 2(1)(-1)(2) + 4(2)^2 = -4 + 16 = 12$$ $$∂ f∂ y = (1)^2(2) = 2$$ $$∂ f∂ z = (1)^2(-1) + 8(1)(2) = -1 + 16 = 15$$ Thus, $\nabla f(1,-1,2) = 12\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 15\hat{k}$.
3. Find the unit vector $\hat{u}$ in direction of $\mathbf{v} = 2\hat{i} - \hat{j} - 2\hat{k}$: $$|\mathbf{v}| = √2^2 + (-1)^2 + (-2)^2 = √4 + 1 + 4 = 3$$ $$\hat{u} = 23\hat{i} - 13\hat{j} - 23\hat{k}$$ 4. Compute directional derivative $D_{\hat{u}}f = \nabla f · \hat{u}$: $$D_{\hat{u}}f = (12)(23) + (2)(-13) + (15)(-23) = 8 - 23 - 10 = -2 - 23 = -83$$
Q2. A vector velocity field representing aircraft flow is given by $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = (x + 3y)\hat{i} + (y - 2z)\hat{j} + (x + az)\hat{k}$. If the fluid is incompressible (solenoidal), determine the constant value $a$.
A) $2$
B) $-2$
C) $0$
D) $-1$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. For a vector field to be solenoidal, its divergence must be zero everywhere: $$\nabla · \mathbf{F} = 0$$ 2. Calculate divergence: $$\nabla · \mathbf{F} = ∂∂ x(x + 3y) + ∂∂ y(y - 2z) + ∂∂ z(x + az)$$ $$\nabla · \mathbf{F} = 1 + 1 + a = 2 + a$$ 3. Equating to zero: $$2 + a = 0 \implies a = -2$$
Q3. Evaluate the scalar Laplacian $\nabla^2 (1r)$ for $r \neq 0$, where $r = √x^2 + y^2 + z^2$.
A) $-1r^3$
B) $2r^3$
C) $0$
D) $1r^2$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Use the standard formula for the Laplacian of a power of radial distance: $$\nabla^2(r^n) = n(n + 1)rn-2$$ 2. Here $1r = r-1$, so $n = -1$. 3. Substituting $n = -1$: $$\nabla^2(r-1) = (-1)(-1 + 1)r-1 - 2 = (-1)(0)r-3 = 0 \quad (for r \neq 0)$$
Q4. A conservative force field is given by $\mathbf{F} = (2xy + z^3)\hat{i} + x^2\hat{j} + 3xz^2\hat{k}$. Find the scalar potential function $φ(x,y,z)$ such that $\mathbf{F} = \nabla φ$, given that $φ(0,0,0) = 0$. Then evaluate $φ(1, 2, 1)$.
A) $1$
B) $2$
C) $3$
D) $5$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Set up partial differential equations for $\mathbf{F} = \nabla φ$: $$∂ φ∂ x = 2xy + z^3 \quad (1)$$ $$∂ φ∂ y = x^2 \quad (2)$$ $$∂ φ∂ z = 3xz^2 \quad (3)$$ 2. Integrate (1) with respect to $x$: $$φ(x,y,z) = x^2 y + x z^3 + g(y,z)$$ 3. Differentiate with respect to $y$ and compare with (2): $$∂ φ∂ y = x^2 + ∂ g∂ y = x^2 \implies ∂ g∂ y = 0 \implies g(y,z) = h(z)$$ 4. Differentiate with respect to $z$ and compare with (3): $$∂ φ∂ z = 3xz^2 + h'(z) = 3xz^2 \implies h'(z) = 0 \implies h(z) = C$$ 5. Hence $φ(x,y,z) = x^2 y + x z^3 + C$. Given $φ(0,0,0) = 0 \implies C = 0$. 6. Evaluate $φ(1, 2, 1)$: $$φ(1, 2, 1) = (1)^2(2) + (1)(1)^3 = 2 + 1 = 3$$
Q5. What is the value of $\nabla · (r^n \mathbf{r})$ where $\mathbf{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$ and $r = |\mathbf{r}|$?
A) $n r^n$
B) $(n + 3)r^n$
C) $(n + 1)r^n$
D) $3n rn-1$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Use the vector divergence identity for scalar-vector product: $$\nabla · (f\mathbf{A}) = f(\nabla · \mathbf{A}) + \mathbf{A} · \nabla f$$ 2. Set $f = r^n$ and $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{r}$: $$\nabla · (r^n \mathbf{r}) = r^n (\nabla · \mathbf{r}) + \mathbf{r} · \nabla(r^n)$$ 3. Note that $\nabla · \mathbf{r} = ∂ x∂ x + ∂ y∂ y + ∂ z∂ z = 3$. 4. Note that $\nabla(r^n) = n rn-2 \mathbf{r}$. 5. Substitute these into the expression: $$\nabla · (r^n \mathbf{r}) = r^n (3) + \mathbf{r} · (n rn-2 \mathbf{r}) = 3 r^n + n rn-2 (\mathbf{r} · \mathbf{r}) = 3 r^n + n rn-2 (r^2) = (n + 3) r^n$$
Q6. The velocity field of a fluid flow in an aircraft engine testing duct is given by $\mathbf{v} = (y^2 + z^2)\hat{i} + (z^2 + x^2)\hat{j} + (x^2 + y^2)\hat{k}$. Determine the magnitude of the vorticity vector $\mathbf{ω} = \nabla × \mathbf{v}$ at the point $(1, 2, 3)$.
A) $2√6$
B) $√24$
C) $√24$
D) $2√6$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Calculate curl $\mathbf{ω} = \nabla × \mathbf{v}$: $$\mathbf{ω} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ ∂∂ x & ∂∂ y & ∂∂ z \\ y^2+z^2 & z^2+x^2 & x^2+y^2 \end{vmatrix}$$ 2. Compute individual components: $$ω_x = ∂∂ y(x^2+y^2) - ∂∂ z(z^2+x^2) = 2y - 2z$$ $$ω_y = ∂∂ z(y^2+z^2) - ∂∂ x(x^2+y^2) = 2z - 2x$$ $$ω_z = ∂∂ x(z^2+x^2) - ∂∂ y(y^2+z^2) = 2x - 2y$$ 3. Q8. Determine the cosine of the angle $θ$ between the surfaces $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 9$ and $x^2 + y^2 - z = 3$ at the point $P(2, -1, 2)$.

1. Let Surface 1 be $f(x,y,z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 9 = 0$. Normal vector: $$\mathbf{n}_1 = \nabla f = 2x\hat{i} + 2y\hat{j} + 2z\hat{k}$$ At $(2, -1, 2)$: $\mathbf{n}_1 = 4\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 4\hat{k}$. Magnitude $|\mathbf{n}_1| = √16+4+16 = 6$.

Q9. For any arbitrary real power $n$, what is the expression for $\nabla × (r^n \mathbf{r})$?
A) $n rn-1\mathbf{r}$
B) $(n+3)r^n\mathbf{r}$
C) $\mathbf{0}$
D) $n rn-2(\mathbf{r} × \hat{i})$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Use vector identity for curl of a scalar times vector field: $$\nabla × (f \mathbf{A}) = f(\nabla × \mathbf{A}) + (\nabla f) × \mathbf{A}$$ 2. Set $f = r^n$ and $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{r}$: $$\nabla × (r^n \mathbf{r}) = r^n (\nabla × \mathbf{r}) + (\nabla r^n) × \mathbf{r}$$ 3. Note that $\nabla × \mathbf{r} = \mathbf{0}$ because $\mathbf{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$. 4. Note that $\nabla r^n = n rn-2\mathbf{r}$. 5. Substitute into expression: $$\nabla × (r^n \mathbf{r}) = r^n (\mathbf{0}) + (n rn-2\mathbf{r}) × \mathbf{r}$$ 6. Since the cross product of any vector with itself is zero ($\mathbf{r} × \mathbf{r} = \mathbf{0}$): $$\nabla × (r^n \mathbf{r}) = \mathbf{0}$$
Q10. Which of the following identity expansions represents $\nabla · (\mathbf{A} × \mathbf{B})$ correctly?
A) $\mathbf{A} · (\nabla × \mathbf{B}) + \mathbf{B} · (\nabla × \mathbf{A})$
B) $\mathbf{B} · (\nabla × \mathbf{A}) - \mathbf{A} · (\nabla × \mathbf{B})$
C) $\mathbf{A} × (\nabla · \mathbf{B}) - \mathbf{B} × (\nabla · \mathbf{A})$
D) $(\nabla · \mathbf{A})\mathbf{B} - (\nabla · \mathbf{B})\mathbf{A}$
Step-by-step Solution:
This is a standard fundamental identity in vector differential calculus derived using index notation ($εijk ∂_i (A_j B_k)$): $$\nabla · (\mathbf{A} × \mathbf{B}) = \mathbf{B} · (\nabla × \mathbf{A}) - \mathbf{A} · (\nabla × \mathbf{B})$$ Notice the negative sign on the second term due to the anti-symmetry of the Levi-Civita symbol.
Q11. In cylindrical polar coordinates $(r, θ, z)$, a velocity vector field is given by $\mathbf{v} = v_r \hat{e}_r + v_θ \hat{e}_θ + v_z \hat{e}_z$. What is the correct expression for the divergence $\nabla · \mathbf{v}$?
A) $∂ v_r∂ r + ∂ v_θ∂ θ + ∂ v_z∂ z$

Q13. Consider the force field $\mathbf{F} = (y^2 \cos x + z^3)\hat{i} + (2y \sin x - 4)\hat{j} + (3xz^2 + 2)\hat{k}$. Compute the line integral $∫_C \mathbf{F} · d\mathbf{r}$ along any smooth curve from $A(0,0,0)$ to $B(π/2, 1, 2)$.
A) $1 + 4π$
B) $4π - 1$
C) $4π$
D) $1 + 2π$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. First test if $\nabla × \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$: $$(\nabla × \mathbf{F})_x = ∂ (3xz^2+2)∂ y - ∂ (2y\sin x-4)∂ z = 0 - 0 = 0$$ $$(\nabla × \mathbf{F})_y = ∂ (y^2\cos x+z^3)∂ z - ∂ (3xz^2+2)∂ x = 3z^2 - 3z^2 = 0$$ $$(\nabla × \mathbf{F})_z = ∂ (2y\sin x-4)∂ x - ∂ (y^2\cos x+z^3)∂ y = 2y\cos x - 2y\cos x = 0$$ The field is conservative ($\nabla × \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$).
2. Construct the potential scalar $φ(x,y,z)$: $$φ(x,y,z) = y^2 \sin x + x z^3 - 4y + 2z + C$$ 3. Q1 [Grand Mini Test]. An air safety sensor measures velocity and thermal scalar distributions around a turbine blade. The thermal scalar is given by $φ(x,y,z) = x^3 - 3xy^2 + z^3$. Determine the point(s) where the magnitude of the gradient $|\nabla φ|$ attains a local minimum value of zero on the line $x = 1, y = 1$.

B) $(1, 1, 0)$
C) $(1, 1, -1)$
D) $(1, 1, 1)$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Compute $\nabla φ$: $$\nabla φ = (3x^2 - 3y^2)\hat{i} + (-6xy)\hat{j} + 3z^2 \hat{k}$$ 2. Evaluate $\nabla φ$ on line $x=1, y=1$: $$\nabla φ(1,1,z) = (3(1)^2 - 3(1)^2)\hat{i} - 6(1)(1)\hat{j} + 3z^2\hat{k} = 0\hat{i} - 6\hat{j} + 3z^2\hat{k}$$ 3. Compute magnitude $|\nabla φ(1,1,z)|$: $$|\nabla φ| = √0^2 + (-6)^2 + (3z^2)^2 = √36 + 9z^4$$ 4. For $|\nabla φ| = 0$, we require $36 + 9z^4 = 0 \implies z^4 = -4$, which has NO real solutions for $z$. 5. Hence, there is no real point on the line where $|\nabla φ| = 0$.
Q2 [Grand Mini Test]. Match List-I (Vector Calculus Identity / Expression) with List-II (Physical / Mathematical Meaning) and select the correct option:

List-I:
P. $\nabla · \mathbf{v} = 0$
Q. $\nabla × \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$
R. $\nabla × (\nabla × \mathbf{v}) = \mathbf{0}$ with $\nabla · \mathbf{v} = 0$
S. $\nabla · (\nabla × \mathbf{A}) = 0$

List-II:
1. Always holds for any $C^2$ vector field (Identity)
2. Vector field satisfies vector Laplace equation $\nabla^2 \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$
3. Irrotational flow / Conservative field
4. Incompressible flow / Solenoidal field
A) P-4, Q-3, R-2, S-1
B) P-3, Q-4, R-1, S-2
C) P-4, Q-3, R-1, S-2
D) P-1, Q-2, R-3, S-4
Step-by-step Solution:
- P: $\nabla · \mathbf{v} = 0 \implies$ Incompressible flow / Solenoidal field (4).
- Q: $\nabla × \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0} \implies$ Irrotational flow / Conservative field (3).
- R: $\nabla × (\nabla × \mathbf{v}) = \nabla(\nabla · \mathbf{v}) - \nabla^2 \mathbf{v}$. Given $\nabla · \mathbf{v} = 0$ and $\nabla × (\nabla × \mathbf{v}) = \mathbf{0}$, we get $\nabla^2 \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$ (Vector Laplace equation) (2).

Q4 [Grand Mini Test]. Assertion (A): The work done by a force field $\mathbf{F} = (y^2 z^3)\hat{i} + (2xyz^3)\hat{j} + (3xy^2 z^2)\hat{k}$ in moving a particle along any closed loop $C$ is strictly zero.
