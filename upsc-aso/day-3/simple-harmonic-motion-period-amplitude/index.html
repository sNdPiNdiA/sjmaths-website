Simple harmonic motion – period, amplitude

Day 3 of 129 Engineering Fundamentals Phase 1: Foundations 60-90 min Master Cycle

1. Understand (5–10 min)

Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) represents a fundamental class of bounded, oscillatory mechanical motion wherein the restoring force acting on a body is directly proportional to its displacement from a stable equilibrium position and acts strictly in the direction opposite to that displacement. In the context of Engineering Mathematics and Aeronautical Dynamics (specifically under the purview of UPSC Air Safety Officer parameters), SHM forms the baseline mathematical model for structural vibrations, sensor dynamics, dynamic stability derivatives, and aerodynamic flutter analysis.

Mathematically, a continuous physical system executes Linear Simple Harmonic Motion along a spatial axis $x(t)$ if its governing second-order linear homogeneous differential equation satisfies:

$$d^2 x(t)dt^2 + ω^2 x(t) = 0$$

where $x(t)$ is the instantaneous kinematic displacement from the equilibrium state ($x = 0$), and $ω$ represents the undamped natural angular frequency of the system, defined as $ω = √km > 0$. Here, $k$ denotes the effective linear restoring stiffness (N/m), and $m$ denotes the system's generalized inertial mass (kg).

Analogously, Angular Simple Harmonic Motion governs rotational oscillations about a fixed pivot axis via the differential equation:

$$d^2 θ(t)dt^2 + ω^2 θ(t) = 0 \quad where \quad ω = √CI$$

where $θ(t)$ is the angular displacement (rad), $C$ is the torsional rigidity or rotational restoring constant (N$·$m/rad), and $I$ is the mass moment of inertia about the axis of rotation (kg$·m^2$).

Fundamental Parameters and Domain Bounds

  • Amplitude ($A$ or $\Theta_0$): The absolute maximum kinematic displacement of the system from its mean equilibrium position. $A = \max |x(t)| \in (0, ∞)$. Domain bound: $x(t) \in [-A, A]$.
  • Time Period ($T$): The invariant time interval required for the system to complete one full cycle of oscillation, satisfying the periodicity condition $x(t + T) = x(t)$. Mathematically derived as $T = 2πω = 2π √mk \in (0, ∞)$.
  • Frequency ($f$ or $n$): The number of complete cycles per unit time, given by $f = 1T = ω2π$ measured in Hertz ($s-1$).
  • Phase Angle ($\Phi(t) = ω t + φ_0$): The instantaneous argument of the harmonic function describing the precise state of motion (position and direction) at time $t$. $φ_0 \in [-π, π]$ is defined as the initial phase angle or epoch at $t = 0$.
  • Kinematic Bounds:
    • Displacement: $x(t) \in [-A, A]$
    • Velocity: $v(t) = \dot{x}(t) \in [-Aω, Aω]$
    • Acceleration: $a(t) = \ddot{x}(t) \in [-Aω^2, Aω^2]$

2. Learn the Concept (15–20 min)

To fully master Simple Harmonic Motion for structural and flight stability analysis, we begin with Newton's Second Law applied to a linear restoring force. Consider a body of mass $m$ constrained to 1D motion under a conservative linear force field $F(x) = -kx$:

$$F = m d^2xdt^2 = -kx \implies d^2xdt^2 + km x = 0$$

Substituting $ω^2 = km$, we establish the characteristic algebraic equation for this homogeneous ODE by assuming a solution of the form $x(t) = er t$:

$$r^2 + ω^2 = 0 \implies r = ± i ω$$

Using Euler's formula ($e± i ω t = \cos ω t ± i \sin ω t$), the general real-valued solution is written as:

$$x(t) = C_1 \cos(ω t) + C_2 \sin(ω t)$$

By defining $C_1 = A \cos φ_0$ and $C_2 = -A \sin φ_0$, this can be compactly expressed in phase-amplitude form as:

$$x(t) = A \cos(ω t + φ_0)$$

Alternatively, using $C_1 = A \sin φ_1$ and $C_2 = A \cos φ_1$, $x(t) = A \sin(ω t + φ_1)$. Throughout aviation mechanics, both representations are equivalent, separated by a phase shift of $π/2$.

Kinematic Relations and Phase Relationships

Differentiating $x(t) = A \cos(ω t + φ_0)$ with respect to time yields velocity $v(t)$ and acceleration $a(t)$:

$$v(t) = dxdt = -A ω \sin(ω t + φ_0) = A ω \cos(ω t + φ_0 + π2)$$ $$a(t) = d^2xdt^2 = -A ω^2 \cos(ω t + φ_0) = A ω^2 \cos(ω t + φ_0 + π)$$

Key phase deductions:

  1. Velocity leads displacement by a phase shift of $π/2$ radians ($90^°$). When $x = ± A$ (extremes), $v = 0$. When $x = 0$ (mean position), $v = ± v\max = ± Aω$.
  2. Acceleration leads velocity by $π/2$ radians ($90^°$) and displacement by $π$ radians ($180^°$). Thus acceleration and displacement are strictly anti-phase: $a(t) = -ω^2 x(t)$.

Spatial Kinematics (Velocity and Acceleration as functions of $x$)

Eliminating time $t$ using the fundamental trigonometric identity $\cos^2(ω t + φ_0) + \sin^2(ω t + φ_0) = 1$:

$$(xA)^2 + (v-Aω)^2 = 1 \implies v^2 = ω^2 (A^2 - x^2) \implies v(x) = ± ω √A^2 - x^2$$

This demonstrates that the state space (phase space) trajectory in the $(x, v/ω)$ plane is a circle of radius $A$, and in the raw $(x, v)$ plane, it forms an ellipse centered at the origin with semi-axes $A$ and $Aω$.

$$ Governing ODE: d^2xdt^2 + ω^2 x = 0 $$ $$ Displacement: x(t) = A \cos(ω t + φ_0) $$ $$ Velocity: v(x) = ± ω √A^2 - x^2, \quad v\max = Aω $$ $$ Acceleration: a(x) = -ω^2 x, \quad a\max = Aω^2 $$ $$ Time Period: T = 2πω = 2π √mk $$ $$ Total Energy: E = K + U = 12 m ω^2 A^2 = 12 k A^2 $$

Energy Conservation and Partitioning in SHM

Simple Harmonic Motion is a conservative dynamical process. The potential energy $U(x)$ stored in the elastic restored system relative to $x=0$ is:

$$U(x) = ∫0x k x' dx' = 12 k x^2 = 12 m ω^2 x^2 = 12 m ω^2 A^2 \cos^2(ω t + φ_0)$$

The kinetic energy $K(v)$ possessed by the oscillating mass is:

$$K(x) = 12 m v^2 = 12 m ω^2 (A^2 - x^2) = 12 m ω^2 A^2 \sin^2(ω t + φ_0)$$

The total mechanical energy $E$ remains strictly invariant in time and independent of position $x$:

$$E = K(t) + U(t) = 12 m ω^2 A^2 [ \sin^2(ω t + φ_0) + \cos^2(ω t + φ_0) ] = 12 m ω^2 A^2 = 12 k A^2$$

Time-Average Energies over one complete period $T$:

$$\langle U \rangle_T = 1T∫0T 12 m ω^2 A^2 \cos^2(ω t + φ_0) dt = 14 m ω^2 A^2 = 12 E$$ $$\langle K \rangle_T = 1T∫0T 12 m ω^2 A^2 \sin^2(ω t + φ_0) dt = 14 m ω^2 A^2 = 12 E$$

Thus, over any integer number of cycles, energy is partitioned equally between potential and kinetic modes: $\langle U \rangle_T = \langle K \rangle_T = 12 E$.

Note on Oscillation Frequency of Energy: Since $\cos^2(ω t) = 1 + \cos(2ω t)2$, both potential and kinetic energy oscillate at twice the system frequency, i.e., $fenergy = 2 fmotion = ωπ$.

Superposition of Simple Harmonic Motions

When an aircraft component (e.g., control column, wing root, turbine blade) is subjected to two concurrent harmonic driving displacements along the same axis:

$$x_1(t) = A_1 \sin(ω t + φ_1), \quad x_2(t) = A_2 \sin(ω t + φ_2)$$

The resultant motion is $x(t) = x_1(t) + x_2(t) = A_R \sin(ω t + \Phi)$, where the resultant amplitude $A_R$ and phase angle $\Phi$ are given by the vector addition (phasor) rules:

$$A_R = √A_1^2 + A_2^2 + 2 A_1 A_2 \cos(φ_2 - φ_1)$$ $$\tan \Phi = A_1 \sin φ_1 + A_2 \sin φ_2A_1 \cos φ_1 + A_2 \cos φ_2$$

If the frequencies differ slightly ($ω_1 ≈ ω_2$), the phenomenon of beats occurs with a beat frequency $fbeat = |f_1 - f_2| = |ω_1 - ω_2|2π$.

3. Memorize Important Facts (10 min)

  • Core Governing Differential Equation: $\ddot{x} + ω^2 x = 0 \implies x(t) = A \cos(ω t + φ_0)$
  • Natural Angular Frequency & Period: $ω = √km = 2πT \implies T = 2π √mk$
  • Angular SHM Period: $T = 2π √IC$ (Torsional) or $T = 2π √Im g h$ (Compound Pendulum)
  • Velocity-Displacement Relation: $v(x) = ± ω √A^2 - x^2 \implies v\max = A ω$ at $x = 0$
  • Acceleration-Displacement Relation: $a(x) = -ω^2 x \implies a\max = A ω^2$ at $x = ± A$
  • Total Mechanical Energy: $E = K + U = 12 m ω^2 A^2 = 12 k A^2$
  • Energy Partition Intersections: $K = U \implies x = ± A√2 ≈ ± 0.707 A$
  • Ratio $K/U$ at Half-Amplitude: At $x = A2$, $U = 14 E \implies K = 34 E \implies KU = 3$
  • Time-Averaged Energies: $\langle K \rangle_T = \langle U \rangle_T = 12 E = 14 m ω^2 A^2$
  • Frequency of Energy Oscillation: $fenergy = 2 fmotion = ωπ$
  • Phase Lead Chain: Acceleration leads Velocity by $π/2$ rad ($90^°$), Velocity leads Displacement by $π/2$ rad ($90^°$), Acceleration leads Displacement by $π$ rad ($180^°$).
  • Resultant Amplitude (Collinear, Equal $ω$): $A_R = √A_1^2 + A_2^2 + 2 A_1 A_2 \cos Δ φ$

4. Aviation Application (5–10 min)

In Aeronautical Engineering and Air Safety Investigation (UPSC ASO syllabus alignment), Simple Harmonic Motion forms the foundational framework for analyzing structural dynamic modes, flight dynamics stability derivatives, and sensor response dynamics:

1. Aeroelastic Wing Flutter and Limit Cycle Oscillations (LCO)

Aircraft wings undergo complex coupled bending-torsion oscillations under aerodynamic loading. Prior to flutter onset, the linear uncoupled bending mode behaves as an SHM system governed by $\ddot{h} + ω_h^2 h = 0$ and torsional mode $\ddot{α} + ω_α^2 α = 0$. In flight safety investigations, flutter analysis examines how aerodynamic forces merge these two SHMs. When the bending frequency $ω_h$ approaches the torsional frequency $ω_α$, energy transfer causes neutral stability, leading to undamped SHM oscillations, which quickly transition to explosive flutter if speed increases further.

2. Avionics and Accelerometers (Inertial Measurement Units)

Modern aircraft IMUs utilize MEMS accelerometers modeled as micro-scale mass-spring-damper SHM systems. The seismic mass $m$ suspended on micro-springs of stiffness $k$ undergoes displacement $x$ proportional to the aircraft's local translational acceleration $aaircraft$:

$$xsteady = m aaircraftk = aaircraftω_0^2$$

Air Safety Officers evaluate the natural period $T = 2πω_0$ of these IMU sensors to verify that their operating bandwidth avoids resonance with engine rotational frequencies (N1/N2 rotor shaft speeds).

3. Aircraft Dynamic Stability Modes

In flight dynamics, the longitudinal motions are decomposed into two distinct oscillatory modes, both treated as simplified harmonic oscillations during safety audits:

  • Short-Period Pitching Oscillation (SPPO): A high-frequency, heavily damped harmonic motion ($ω_n ≈ 2 - 6 rad/s$, period $T ≈ 1 - 3 s$) where pitch angle $θ$ and angle of attack $α$ oscillate while speed remains nearly constant.
  • Phugoid Mode: A low-frequency harmonic interchange between kinetic energy (airspeed $V$) and potential energy (altitude $h$) with a long period $T ≈ √2 π V_0g$. For a transport jet flying at $V_0 = 240 m/s$, $T ≈ √2 · π · 240 / 9.81 ≈ 108.7 s$.

4. Air Safety Investigation and FDR Telemetry Analysis

Following an in-flight structural break-up or severe turbulence event, Air Safety Investigators analyze Flight Data Recorder (FDR) accelerometer traces. By calculating the fundamental period $T$ and peak amplitude $A$ of vertical acceleration ($g_z$), investigators apply fast Fourier transforms (FFT) to confirm whether control surface buffet was driven by atmospheric vortex shedding matching the surface's SHM natural frequency (vortex lock-in resonance).

5. UPSC ASO Focus (10 min)

  • Frequently Asked Facts & Formulas:
    • Maximum velocity occurs at $x = 0$ ($v\max = Aω$); Maximum acceleration occurs at $x = ± A$ ($a\max = Aω^2$).
    • Ratio of $a\max / v\max = ω = 2πT$. This ratio is completely independent of amplitude $A$.
    • At displacement $x = A√2$, kinetic energy equals potential energy ($K = U = 12 E$).
    • At displacement $x = A2$, $U = 14 E$ and $K = 34 E$, giving $K/U = 3$.
    • If mass $m$ attached to a spring is increased by $n$-times, the period increases by $√n$.
    • For a simple pendulum, $T = 2π √L/g$. Period is independent of mass $m$ and amplitude $A$ (for small angles $θ < 10^°$).
    • Equivalent length of a compound pendulum: $Leq = k_g^2h + h$, where $k_g$ is the radius of gyration about C.G. and $h$ is the distance from pivot to C.G.
  • Conceptual Traps & Pitfalls:
    • Trap 1: Frequency of Energy vs Frequency of Motion: Students often mistakenly assume kinetic/potential energy oscillates with frequency $f$. In reality, energy functions involve $\sin^2(ω t)$ or $\cos^2(ω t)$, so energy oscillates at double frequency ($2f$).
    • Trap 2: Non-Linear Forces are NOT SHM: A restoring force $F = -k x^3$ produces periodic motion, but it is NOT Simple Harmonic Motion because $F ∝ x^3$ (not $x^1$). Consequently, its period depends directly on amplitude!
    • Trap 3: Linear Addition of Amplitudes: Adding two perpendicular or out-of-phase SHMs does NOT mean $Anet = A_1 + A_2$. Phasor summation must be used ($Anet = √A_1^2 + A_2^2 + 2 A_1 A_2 \cos Δ φ$).
    • Trap 4: Simple Pendulum in Accelerating Frames: Inside an aircraft accelerating vertically upwards with acceleration $a_z$, effective gravity becomes $geff = g + a_z$, decreasing period $T = 2π √L/(g+a_z)$. In free-fall ($geff = 0$), $T \to ∞$ (motion ceases to oscillate).

6. Practice Questions (Exhaustive UPSC ASO MCQs)

Q1. A control stick displacement sensor in a flight simulator executes simple harmonic motion according to $x(t) = 0.08 \cos(10π t + π/6)$ meters, where $t$ is in seconds. What are the time period $T$, maximum velocity $v\max$, and maximum acceleration $a\max$ of the sensor?
A) $T = 0.2 s, v\max = 0.8π m/s, a\max = 8π^2 m/s^2$
B) $T = 0.1 s, v\max = 0.8π m/s, a\max = 80π^2 m/s^2$
C) $T = 0.2 s, v\max = 2.513 m/s, a\max = 78.96 m/s^2$
D) Both A and C are correct
Comparing $x(t) = 0.08 \cos(10π t + π/6)$ with $x(t) = A \cos(ω t + φ_0)$:
Amplitude $A = 0.08 m$, Angular frequency $ω = 10π rad/s$.
Time period $T = 2πω = 2π10π = 0.2 s$.
Maximum velocity $v\max = Aω = 0.08 × 10π = 0.8π m/s ≈ 2.5132 m/s$.
Maximum acceleration $a\max = Aω^2 = 0.08 × (10π)^2 = 8π^2 m/s^2 ≈ 78.9568 m/s^2$.
Since A presents exact symbolic values and C presents evaluation to 3 decimal places, both options A and C express identical physical quantities. Hence D is correct.
Q2. An aircraft wing flap dynamic testing rig uses a spring-mass assembly where mass $m = 4 kg$ and spring stiffness $k = 1600 N/m$. If the amplitude of SHM is $0.05 m$, at what displacement $x$ from the mean position does the kinetic energy equal three times the potential energy?
A) $x = 0.025 m$
B) $x = 0.0353 m$
C) $x = 0.0125 m$
D) $x = 0.0433 m$
Given $K = 3 U$.
Total mechanical energy $E = K + U = 3 U + U = 4 U$.
We know $E = 12 k A^2$ and $U = 12 k x^2$.
Therefore, $12 k A^2 = 4 ( 12 k x^2 ) \implies A^2 = 4 x^2 \implies x = A2$.
Given amplitude $A = 0.05 m$, we get $x = 0.052 = 0.025 m$.
Q3. For a simple harmonic oscillator representing a fuel valve pulse mode, the velocity is measured to be $8 m/s$ at a displacement of $0.03 m$ and $6 m/s$ at a displacement of $0.04 m$. The time period of oscillation $T$ is:
A) $π100 s$
B) $π1000 s$
C) $π10 s$
D) $π200 s$
Using $v^2 = ω^2 (A^2 - x^2)$:
Case 1: $8^2 = ω^2 (A^2 - 0.03^2) \implies 64 = ω^2 (A^2 - 0.0009)$ --- (Eq. 1)
Case 2: $6^2 = ω^2 (A^2 - 0.04^2) \implies 36 = ω^2 (A^2 - 0.0016)$ --- (Eq. 2)
Subtracting Eq. 2 from Eq. 1:
$64 - 36 = ω^2 (0.0016 - 0.0009) \implies 28 = ω^2 (0.0007) \implies ω^2 = 280.0007 = 40,000$.
Thus, $ω = 200 rad/s$.
Time period $T = 2πω = 2π200 = π100 s$.
Q4. Two simple harmonic motions acting along the same flight control axis are defined by $x_1(t) = 3 \sin(100 t)$ and $x_2(t) = 4 \sin(100 t + π/3)$. What is the amplitude of the resultant displacement $x(t) = x_1(t) + x_2(t)$?
A) $5.00 units$
B) $6.08 units$
C) $7.00 units$
D) $1.00 units$
The resultant amplitude $A_R$ of two collinear SHMs with identical angular frequency $ω = 100 rad/s$ is given by:
$A_R = √A_1^2 + A_2^2 + 2 A_1 A_2 \cos(Δ φ)$
Here $A_1 = 3$, $A_2 = 4$, $Δ φ = π/3 - 0 = 60^°$.
$A_R = √3^2 + 4^2 + 2(3)(4) \cos(60^°) = √9 + 16 + 24(0.5) = √25 + 12 = √37 ≈ 6.0828 units$.
Q5. An air safety investigation team analyzes a recorded vibration telemetry trace on an engine pylon given by differential equation $5 d^2 xdt^2 + 125 x = 0$. What is the frequency of oscillation in Hertz?
A) $5.00 Hz$
B) $2.50 Hz$
C) $52π Hz$
D) $10π Hz$
Divide the equation by $5$: $d^2 xdt^2 + 25 x = 0$.
Comparing with $d^2 xdt^2 + ω^2 x = 0$, we get $ω^2 = 25 \implies ω = 5 rad/s$.
Frequency $f = ω2π = 52π Hz ≈ 0.796 Hz$.
Q6. In an ideal undamped SHM system modeling a helicopter rotor blade damper, the total mechanical energy is $E$. What is the average potential energy $\langle U \rangle_T$ integrated over one full time period $T$?
A) $E$
B) $E2$
C) $E4$
D) $E√2$
Potential energy $U(t) = 12 k A^2 \cos^2(ω t + φ_0) = E \cos^2(ω t + φ_0)$.
Average over time period $T$:
$\langle U \rangle_T = 1T ∫_0^T E \cos^2(ω t + φ_0) dt = E · 12 = E2$.
Similarly, $\langle K \rangle_T = E2$.
Q7. A physical pendulum (compound pendulum) formed by an aircraft elevator surface pivoted about its hinge line has mass $M$, radius of gyration $k_g$ about its centre of gravity, and the C.G. is located at distance $h$ from the hinge line. Its time period for small oscillations is:
A) $T = 2π √k_g^2 + h^2g h$
B) $T = 2π √k_gg$
C) $T = 2π √hg$
D) $T = 2π √k_g^2 - h^2g h$
By the parallel axis theorem, moment of inertia about pivot $I = I_g + M h^2 = M k_g^2 + M h^2 = M(k_g^2 + h^2)$.
Restoring torque for small angle $θ$ is $τ = -M g h \sinθ ≈ -M g h θ$.
Equation of motion: $I \ddot{θ} + M g h θ = 0 \implies \ddot{θ} + M g hM(k_g^2 + h^2) θ = 0$.
Thus $ω = √g hk_g^2 + h^2$, giving period $T = 2πω = 2π √k_g^2 + h^2g h$.
Q8. If the frequency of oscillation of a simple harmonic oscillator is $f = 25 Hz$, what is the frequency at which its kinetic energy oscillates between zero and its maximum value?
A) $12.5 Hz$
B) $25 Hz$
C) $50 Hz$
D) $100 Hz$
Kinetic energy $K(t) = 12 m v^2 = 12 m A^2 ω^2 \sin^2(ω t + φ_0)$.
Using $\sin^2θ = 1 - \cos 2θ2$, $K(t) = 14 m A^2 ω^2 [1 - \cos(2ω t + 2φ_0)]$.
The angular frequency of energy oscillation is $2ω$, which implies $fenergy = 2 f = 2 × 25 = 50 Hz$.
Q9. A particle of mass $m = 0.5 kg$ executes simple harmonic motion under a linear force law $F = -50 x$ N. If the particle passes through the mean position with a speed of $4 m/s$, what is the amplitude $A$ of oscillation?
A) $0.2 m$
B) $0.4 m$
C) $0.8 m$
D) $0.1 m$
Restoring stiffness $k = 50 N/m$.
Angular frequency $ω = √km = √500.5 = √100 = 10 rad/s$.
Speed at mean position is maximum velocity $v\max = Aω = 4 m/s$.
Therefore, $A = v\maxω = 410 = 0.4 m$.
Q10. Consider two statement assertions regarding SHM phase portraits:
Statement I: The trajectory of simple harmonic motion in the velocity-displacement $(x, v)$ state space is always a circle regardless of frequency $ω$.
Statement II: In normalized state space $(x, v/ω)$, the phase space trajectory forms a circle of radius equal to amplitude $A$.
A) Both Statement I and Statement II are true
B) Statement I is false, but Statement II is true
C) Statement I is true, but Statement II is false
D) Both Statement I and Statement II are false
The kinematic relation is $v^2 = ω^2 (A^2 - x^2) \implies x^2A^2 + v^2(Aω)^2 = 1$.
In raw $(x, v)$ space, this represents an ellipse with semi-axes $A$ and $Aω$. It is a circle ONLY if $ω = 1 rad/s$. Thus Statement I is false.
In normalized $(x, v/ω)$ space, $x^2 + (v/ω)^2 = A^2$, which is strictly a circle of radius $A$. Thus Statement II is true.
Q11. A simple pendulum suspended inside an aircraft cabin has time period $T_0$ when the aircraft is cruising steadily in level flight. If the aircraft performs a steep bank turn at a bank angle $φ = 60^°$ maintaining constant altitude, what is the new time period $T$ of the pendulum?
A) $T = T_0 / √2$
B) $T = √2 T_0$
C) $T = T_0 / 2$
D) $T = 2 T_0$
In a coordinated level turn with bank angle $φ = 60^°$, load factor $n = 1\cosφ = 1\cos 60^° = 2$.
Effective gravitational acceleration experienced inside the cabin is $geff = n g = 2g$.
Original period $T_0 = 2π √L/g$.
New period $T = 2π √Lgeff = 2π √L2g = T_0√2$.
Q12. A mechanical sensor with spring constant $k$ and mass $m$ oscillates with amplitude $A$. If the mass is suddenly tripled ($m' = 3m$) while total mechanical energy $E$ and spring constant $k$ remain constant, how do amplitude $A'$ and maximum velocity $v\max'$ change?
A) $A' = A$, $v\max' = v\max / √3$
B) $A' = √3 A$, $v\max' = v\max$
C) $A' = A / √3$, $v\max' = v\max / 3$
D) $A' = A$, $v\max' = √3 v\max$
Total mechanical energy $E = 12 k A^2$. Since $E$ and $k$ are unchanged, amplitude $A' = A$.
Angular frequency changes: $ω' = √k3m = ω√3$.
Maximum velocity $v\max' = A' ω' = A (ω√3) = v\max√3$.
Q13. A particle executing SHM takes $0.5 seconds$ to move directly from its mean position ($x=0$) to half of its amplitude ($x = A/2$). What is the complete time period $T$ of the oscillation?
A) $3.0 s$
B) $4.0 s$
C) $6.0 s$
D) $12.0 s$
Let $x(t) = A \sin(ω t)$, with $x(0) = 0$ at the mean position.
At $t = 0.5 s$, $x(0.5) = A/2 \implies A \sin(0.5 ω) = A/2 \implies \sin(0.5 ω) = 12$.

Q2. [Grand Mini Test] An air safety laboratory tests a multi-spring suspension for a flight recorder. Two identical springs of stiffness $k$ are connected first in series, and then in parallel, supporting the same mass $m$. What is the ratio of the series period $T_s$ to the parallel period $T_p$?
A) $1 : 2$
B) $2 : 1$
C) $1 : 4$
D) $4 : 1$
In Series: Equivalent stiffness $k_s = k · kk + k = k2$.
Series Period: $T_s = 2π √mk_s = 2π √mk/2 = 2π √2mk$.

In Parallel: Equivalent stiffness $k_p = k + k = 2k$.
Parallel Period: $T_p = 2π √mk_p = 2π √m2k$.

Ratio $T_sT_p = 2π √2mk2π √m2k = √21/2 = √4 = 2$.
Thus $T_s : T_p = 2 : 1$.
Q3. [Grand Mini Test] Statement-Based Assertion Question:
Assertion (A): If the amplitude of a simple harmonic oscillator undergoing linear undamped motion is doubled, its time period remains completely unchanged.
Reason (R): The period of linear simple harmonic motion depends strictly on the system's mass $m$ and stiffness $k$, and is mathematically independent of amplitude $A$.
A) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A)
C) (A) is true, but (R) is false
D) (A) is false, but (R) is true
For linear SHM, $T = 2π √m/k$. The angular frequency $ω = √k/m$ is an intrinsic property determined solely by mass and stiffness. Thus, doubling amplitude $A$ increases maximum speed $v\max = Aω$ proportionally, keeping the time to complete a cycle ($T$) identical. Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) directly explains (A).
Q4. [Grand Mini Test] An avionics accelerometer contains a proof mass attached to a elastic structure. The system's displacement equation is $x(t) = A \cos(ω t) + A \sin(ω t)$. What is the maximum speed $v\max$ and total mechanical energy $E$ of this oscillator?
A) $v\max = A ω$, $E = 12 m ω^2 A^2$
B) $v\max = √2 A ω$, $E = m ω^2 A^2$
C) $v\max = 2 A ω$, $E = 2 m ω^2 A^2$
D) $v\max = √2 A ω$, $E = 12 m ω^2 A^2$
Rewrite $x(t) = A \cos(ω t) + A \sin(ω t)$ in phase-amplitude form:
$x(t) = √2 A [ 1√2 \cos(ω t) + 1√2 \sin(ω t) ] = √2 A \sin(ω t + π4)$.
The effective amplitude of motion is $Aeff = √2 A$.
Maximum speed $v\max = Aeff ω = √2 A ω$.
Total mechanical energy $E = 12 m ω^2 (Aeff)^2 = 12 m ω^2 (√2 A)^2 = 12 m ω^2 (2 A^2) = m ω^2 A^2$.

8. Error Analysis & Common Traps (5 min)

During the UPSC Air Safety Officer examination, candidate errors in Simple Harmonic Motion typically stem from the following subtle conceptual and algebraic traps:

1. Confusing Motion Frequency with Energy Frequency

Error: Stating that kinetic or potential energy oscillates with frequency $f = ω2π$.
Correction: Potential energy $U ∝ \cos^2(ω t) = 1 + \cos(2ω t)2$. Energy oscillates at twice the mechanical motion frequency ($2f = ωπ$).

2. Incorrect Phase Integration for Time Intervals

Error: Assuming the time taken to travel from $x = 0$ to $x = A/2$ equals the time taken from $x = A/2$ to $x = A$.
Correction: Because speed is non-uniform (highest at mean position, zero at extreme):

  • Time from $x=0$ to $x=A/2$: $t_1 = T12$ (since $\sin(ω t_1) = 1/2 \implies ω t_1 = π/6$).
  • Time from $x=A/2$ to $x=A$: $t_2 = T4 - T12 = T6$.
Thus, $t_2 = 2 t_1$. Travelling the outer half takes twice as long as the inner half!

3. Sign Missteps in Phase and Acceleration

Error: Forgetting the negative sign in $a(t) = -ω^2 x(t)$, leading to phase lead calculation errors.
Correction: Acceleration is $180^°$ ($π$ rad) out of phase with displacement. A negative displacement yields a positive acceleration force, and vice versa.

4. Phase-Space Geometry Traps

Error: Stating that the phase space portrait $(x, v)$ is a circle for any system.
Correction: $x^2A^2 + v^2(Aω)^2 = 1$ is an ellipse in $(x, v)$ coordinates with semi-axes $A$ and $Aω$. It becomes a circle ONLY when $ω = 1 rad/s$ or when plotted in normalized coordinates $(x, v/ω)$.

5. Misapplying Linear SHM Formulas to Non-Linear Restoring Forces

Error: Using $T = 2π/ω$ for forces like $F = -k x^3$ or simple pendulums at large initial swing angles ($θ_0 > 10^°$).
Correction: For $F = -k x^3$, the restoring force is non-linear; the motion is periodic but not simple harmonic, and $T$ depends on amplitude $A$. For a simple pendulum at large angles, $T ≈ 2π √Lg (1 + 14\sin^2θ_02)$.

9. Active Recall & Quick Revision (5 min)

Formula Flash-Facts

  • Standard SHM Differential Equation: $\ddot{x} + ω^2 x = 0$
  • General Solution (Displacement): $x(t) = A \cos(ω t + φ_0)$
  • Velocity in terms of Position: $v(x) = ± ω √A^2 - x^2$
  • Acceleration in terms of Position: $a(x) = -ω^2 x$
  • Maximum Kinematic Limits: $v\max = Aω$, $a\max = Aω^2$, Ratio $a\maxv\max = ω$
  • Time Period & Frequency: $T = 2πω = 2π √mk$, $f = 1T = 12π√km$
  • Total Energy Equation: $E = 12 m ω^2 A^2 = 12 k A^2$
  • Time-Averaged Energies over Period $T$: $\langle K \rangle_T = \langle U \rangle_T = 12 E = 14 k A^2$
  • Equal Energy Displacement: $K = U \implies x = ± A√2$
  • Quarter Potential Energy Displacement: $U = 14 E \implies x = ± A2$ (where $K = 34 E$)
  • Phasor Amplitude Addition: $A_R = √A_1^2 + A_2^2 + 2 A_1 A_2 \cos Δ φ$
