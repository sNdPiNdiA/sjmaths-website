Subtopic 1 of 5

Normal & Shear Stress/Strain

Continuum Mechanics Foundations

1. Formal Technical Definition

Stress (\(\sigma, \tau\)): The intensity of internal distributed forces acting across an infinitesimal area inside a continuous medium, defined as the limiting ratio of force vector increment to area increment as area approaches zero: \(\sigma_{ij} = \lim_{\Delta A \to 0} \frac{\Delta F_i}{\Delta A_j}\).

Strain (\(\varepsilon, \gamma\)): A kinematic measure of local deformation representing the relative displacement of particles in a continuous body, quantified as fractional change in length (normal strain) or angular distortion between intersecting material lines (shear strain).

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

At the molecular level, solids consist of atoms bound in an equilibrium lattice potential well. When an external mechanical load is applied, interatomic distances alter, creating restoring forces. Under the continuum hypothesis, atomic granularity is neglected, and matter is treated as infinitely divisible. A fluid or solid parcel is in static equilibrium when surface tractions and body forces balance. Normal stress acts perpendicular to a cutting plane (pulling apart or pushing together atomic bonds), while shear stress acts parallel to the plane (sliding atomic planes past one another). Normal strain measures stretching/compression along coordinate axes, whereas engineering shear strain (\(\gamma\)) measures the total decrease in a right angle between two initially orthogonal material line elements.

3. Mathematical Derivation & Equations

Cauchy Stress Tensor Formulation:

Consider an arbitrary infinitesimal tetrahedron with faces orthogonal to coordinate axes \(x, y, z\) and an inclined face with outward unit normal vector \(\mathbf{n} = (n_x, n_y, n_z)\) and area \(dA\).

Let the resultant stress vector on the inclined plane be $\mathbf{T}^{(\mathbf{n})}$. By applying Newton's Second Law (linear momentum balance) in the limit as volume \(dV \to 0\):

\(\mathbf{T}^{(\mathbf{n})} = \mathbf{n} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}\)

Expressed in component index notation:

\(\sigma_{ij} = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_{xx} & \tau_{xy} & \tau_{xz} \\ \tau_{yx} & \sigma_{yy} & \yz_z \\ \tau_{zx} & \tau_{zy} & \sigma_{zz} \end{bmatrix}\)

Kinematic Strain Definitions:

Normal strain: \(\varepsilon_{xx} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\), where \(u\) is displacement in \(x\)-direction.

Engineering shear strain: \(\gamma_{xy} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} = 2\varepsilon_{xy}\).

4. Dimensions & Units

Stress (\(\sigma, \tau\)):
  • • Fundamental Dimension: \([M L^{-1} T^{-2}]\)
  • • SI Unit: Pascal (\(\text{Pa}\)) or \(\text{N/m}^2\) (Megapascal, \(\text{MPa}\), commonly used in aerospace)
  • • CGS Unit: Barye (\(\text{dyn/cm}^2\))
  • • Imperial/FPS: psi ($\text{lb/in}^2$) or ksi ($\text{kips/in}^2$)
Strain (\(\varepsilon, \gamma\)):
  • • Fundamental Dimension: Dimensionless \([M^0 L^0 T^0]\)
  • • SI Unit: Metre per metre (\(\text{m/m}\)) or dimensionless ratio (often expressed in microstrain, \(\mu\varepsilon = 10^{-6}\))
  • • Conversion: \(1 \text{ psi} = 6.89476 \text{ kPa}\); \(1 \text{ MPa} = 145.038 \text{ psi}\)

6. Aircraft Application & DGCA Air Safety Connection

Wing Spar Bending & Shear Webs: Aircraft wing spars experience combined normal stress (from bending moments bending the wing upward during positive load factor \(n\) maneuvers) and shear stress (from transverse shear forces). The web of an I-beam spar resists principal shear stresses, while caps resist axial normal stresses.

DGCA CAR Section 2 Compliance: Under CAR Section 2, Airworthiness Series 'L', structural integrity must be verified against limit loads (maximum loads expected in service) multiplied by an ultimate safety factor of 1.5. Air Safety Officers investigating structural fatigue failures inspect rivet lines where high shear stress concentration occurs.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions

Trap 1: Confusing engineering strain (\(\Delta L / L_0\)) with true (logarithmic) strain (\(\int dL/L = \ln(L/L_0)\)). For small aircraft elastic deformations, they are nearly identical, but UPSC numericals involving large plastic deformation require true strain.

Trap 2: Assuming shear stress acts only in isolation. By Cauchy's reciprocity theorem (\(\tau_{xy} = \tau_{yx}\)), shear stress on orthogonal planes is always coupled.

8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem:

A solid circular steel control pushrod of diameter \(d = 20 \text{ mm}\) on a transport aircraft experiences an axial tensile load \(P = 31.4 \text{ kN}\). Determine the normal stress in the pushrod and the total elongation over a gauge length \(L_0 = 500 \text{ mm}\). Take Young's modulus \(E = 200 \text{ GPa}\).

Solution Steps:

1. Cross-sectional Area: \(A = \frac{\pi d^2}{4} = \frac{\pi (0.020)^2}{4} = 3.1416 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m}^2\)

2. Normal Stress: \(\sigma = \frac{P}{A} = \frac{31.4 \times 10^3 \text{ N}}{3.1416 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m}^2} = 100 \times 10^6 \text{ Pa} = 100 \text{ MPa}\)

3. Normal Strain: \(\varepsilon = \frac{\sigma}{E} = \frac{100 \times 10^6}{200 \times 10^9} = 0.0005\) (\(500 \, \mu\varepsilon\))

4. Elongation: \(\Delta L = \varepsilon \times L_0 = 0.0005 \times 500 \text{ mm} = 0.25 \text{ mm}\)

Operational Takeaway:

The pushrod operates safely below the yield limit of structural steel (~250 MPa), with minimal elastic elongation ensuring precise control cable/pushrod authority in flight control linkages.

Subtopic 2 of 5

Hooke's Law & Generalized Elasticity

Constitutive Relations

1. Formal Technical Definition

Hooke's Law: Within the elastic limit of a material, stress is directly proportional to strain. For multi-axial isotropic linear elastic materials, the generalized Hooke's law relates the six independent stress components linearly to the six independent strain components via elasticity tensors governed by engineering constants ($E, G, K, \nu$).

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

Microscopically, linear elasticity corresponds to the quadratic approximation of the interatomic potential energy well near the equilibrium spacing. Stretching bonds requires energy proportional to the square of displacement, leading to linear force-displacement behaviour. Young's modulus (\(E\)) measures resistance to uniaxial stretching; shear modulus (\(G\)) measures resistance to shape distortion (sliding); bulk modulus (\(K\)) measures resistance to hydrostatic volume change. Isotropic materials exhibit identical mechanical properties in all directions, simplifying constitutive equations.

3. Mathematical Derivation & Equations

Uniaxial Form:

\(\sigma = E \cdot \varepsilon \implies \varepsilon = \frac{\sigma}{E}\)

Generalized 3D Hooke's Law (Isotropic):

Considering lateral strains induced by Poisson's effect:

\(\varepsilon_x = \frac{1}{E} \left[ \sigma_x - \nu(\sigma_y + \sigma_z) \right]\)

\(\varepsilon_y = \frac{1}{E} \left[ \sigma_y - \nu(\sigma_x + \sigma_z) \right]\)

\(\varepsilon_z = \frac{1}{E} \left[ \sigma_z - \nu(\sigma_x + \sigma_y) \right]\)

Shear relations: \(\gamma_{xy} = \frac{\tau_{xy}}{G}\), \(\gamma_{yz} = \frac{\tau_{yz}}{G}\), \(\gamma_{zx} = \frac{\tau_{zx}}{G}\)

4. Dimensions & Units

Elastic Moduli (\(E, G, K\)):
  • • Fundamental Dimension: \([M L^{-1} T^{-2}]\) (identical to stress)
  • • SI Unit: Gigapascals (\(\text{GPa}\))
  • • Typical Aluminum \(E \approx 70 \text{ GPa}\); Steel \(E \approx 200 \text{ GPa}\)

5. Engineering Reference Data Table

Aerospace Material Young's Modulus (\(E\)) Shear Modulus (\(G\)) Bulk Modulus (\(K\))
Aluminium Alloys 70 - 73 GPa 26 - 28 GPa ~75 GPa
Structural Steels 200 - 210 GPa 77 - 80 GPa ~160 GPa
Titanium Alloys 105 - 120 GPa 40 - 45 GPa ~100 GPa

6. Aircraft Application & DGCA Air Safety Connection

Aeroelastic Tailoring & Flutter: Wing stiffness is directly governed by \(E\) and \(G\). In swept-wing transport aircraft, if torsional stiffness ($G J$) is too low, aerodynamic lift forces induce twist, leading to control reversal or destructive aeroelastic flutter. DGCA certification (CAR 25.629) requires rigorous flutter clearance flight tests.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions

Trap: Assuming Hooke's law applies past the proportional limit. In cyclic loading (e.g., landing gear struts), hysteresis occurs due to plastic micro-yielding even below macroscopic yield.

8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem:

An aluminium plate ($\sigma_x = 150 \text{ MPa}$ in tension, $\sigma_y = 50 \text{ MPa}$ in compression, $\sigma_z = 0$) has $E = 70 \text{ GPa}$ and $\nu = 0.33$. Calculate strains $\varepsilon_x$ and $\varepsilon_y$.

Solution:

\(\varepsilon_x = \frac{1}{70 \times 10^3} [150 - 0.33(-50 - 0)] = \frac{150 + 16.5}{70 \times 10^3} = \frac{166.5}{70000} = +0.002379\)

\(\varepsilon_y = \frac{1}{70 \times 10^3} [-50 - 0.33(150 - 0)] = \frac{-50 - 49.5}{70 \times 10^3} = \frac{-99.5}{70000} = -0.001421\)

Operational Takeaway:

Bi-axial stress states significantly alter transverse strain dimensions, crucial for rivet hole clearance checks during heavy maintenance overhauls.

Subtopic 3 of 5

Poisson's Ratio & Inter-Constant Relations

Material Symmetry

1. Formal Technical Definition

Poisson's Ratio (\(\nu\)): The negative ratio of transverse strain to axial strain in a material undergoing uniaxial stress: \(\nu = -\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{trans}}}{\varepsilon_{\text{axial}}}\). For isotropic elastic materials, independent elastic constants are reduced to any two (e.g., \(E\) and \(\nu\)), with others derived via exact inter-constant relations.

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

When a structural member is stretched axially, lateral atomic spacing contracts to maintain volume consistency or due to directional lattice bond coupling. For isotropic solids thermodynamically, Poisson's ratio is bounded between \(-1.0\) and \(+0.5\). Most structural metals have \(\nu \approx 0.30\) to \(0.35\), indicating slight volume increase under tension. Cork and specialized auxetic foams exhibit negative Poisson's ratios (expanding laterally when stretched).

3. Mathematical Derivation & Equations

Inter-Constant Relations (Isotropic Elasticity):

1. Shear Modulus: \(G = \frac{E}{2(1 + \nu)}\)

2. Bulk Modulus: \(K = \frac{E}{3(1 - 2\nu)}\)

3. Lamé Constants (\(\lambda, \mu\)): \(\lambda = \frac{E\nu}{(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)}\), \(\mu = G = \frac{E}{2(1+\nu)}\)

4. Dimensions & Units

Poisson's Ratio:
  • • Fundamental Dimension: Dimensionless (\([M^0 L^0 T^0]\))
  • • Typical Metals: \(0.25\) (Cast Iron) to \(0.35\) (Aluminium) to \(0.45\) (Lead). Incompressible rubber \(\nu = 0.5\).

5. Engineering Reference Data Table

Material Poisson's Ratio (\(\nu\)) Derived Shear Modulus ($G$) Derived Bulk Modulus ($K$)
Aluminium Alloy (E=70 GPa) 0.33 26.3 GPa 68.6 GPa
Steel (E=200 GPa) 0.30 76.9 GPa 166.7 GPa
Titanium (E=110 GPa) 0.34 41.0 GPa 114.6 GPa

6. Aircraft Application & DGCA Air Safety Connection

Pressurised Fuselage Shells: Fuselages of transport aircraft (e.g., Boeing 737, Airbus A320) experience circumferential hoop stress ($\sigma_h = pr/t$) and longitudinal axial stress ($\sigma_l = pr/2t$). Due to Poisson's ratio, circumferential expansion induces longitudinal contraction, which must be accounted for in skin lap-joint fatigue life calculations mandated by DGCA mandatory modification directives.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions

Trap: Forgetting the negative sign in Poisson's ratio definition, leading to sign errors in lateral strain calculations.

8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem:

For an isotropic structural alloy with $E = 120 \text{ GPa}$ and $G = 45 \text{ GPa}$, calculate Poisson's ratio $\nu$ and Bulk Modulus $K$.

Solution:

1. From $G = \frac{E}{2(1+\nu)} \implies 1 + \nu = \frac{E}{2G} = \frac{120}{2(45)} = \frac{120}{90} = 1.3333$

2. $\nu = 1.3333 - 1 = 0.3333$

3. Bulk Modulus: $K = \frac{E}{3(1 - 2\nu)} = \frac{120}{3(1 - 2(0.3333))} = \frac{120}{3(0.3334)} = \frac{120}{1.0002} \approx 120 \text{ GPa}$

Operational Takeaway:

Cross-verifying elastic constants ensures finite element models (FEM) used in aircraft certification accurately reflect material stiffness tensors.

Subtopic 4 of 5

Principal Stresses & Maximum Shear

Tensor Transformations

1. Formal Technical Definition

Principal Stresses (\(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3\)): The normal stresses acting on mutually orthogonal planes where shear stress vanishes completely. The planes on which they act are termed principal planes. Maximum shear stress occurs on planes inclined at $45^\circ$ to principal planes.

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

When an aircraft structural component (like a skin panel or engine turbine blade) is subjected to complex multi-axial loads, the stress state at any point depends on the orientation of the coordinate system. By rotating coordinate axes, we can always find an orientation where shear components disappear. These extreme normal stresses ($\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \sigma_3$) dictate material failure initiation under yield criteria such as Von Mises or Tresca.

3. Mathematical Derivation & Equations

2D Principal Stress Equations:

\(\sigma_{1,2} = \frac{\sigma_x + \sigma_y}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2}\)

Orientation of principal planes (\(\theta_p\)):

\(\tan(2\theta_p) = \frac{2\tau_{xy}}{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}\)

Maximum Shear Stress (\(\tau_{\max}\)):

\(\tau_{\max} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_3}{2} = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2}\)

4. Dimensions & Units

Principal Stresses & Shear:
  • • Units: Pascals (\(\text{Pa}\)), Megapascals (\(\text{MPa}\)), or \(\text{psi}\).

5. Engineering Reference Data Table

Failure Criterion Mathematical Condition Aerospace Application
Maximum Shear Stress (Tresca) \(\tau_{\max} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_3}{2} \ge \frac{\sigma_y}{2}\) Conservative ductile metal design
Von Mises Distortion Energy \(\sigma_v = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2]} \ge \sigma_y\) Standard for aircraft turbine disks & spars

6. Aircraft Application & DGCA Air Safety Connection

Turbine Disk Burst Prevention: Jet engine turbine disks spin at >10,000 RPM, inducing immense centrifugal tensile stresses combined with thermal gradients. Calculating principal stresses ensures disks do not exceed Von Mises yield limits, preventing catastrophic uncontained engine rotor failures (UERF) investigated by DGCA and AAIB.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions

Trap: Confusing principal planes angle $\theta_p$ with maximum shear planes angle $\theta_s$, which are separated by $45^\circ$ geometrically.

8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem:

At a critical skin point on an aircraft fuselage, $\sigma_x = 120 \text{ MPa}$, $\sigma_y = 40 \text{ MPa}$, and $\tau_{xy} = 30 \text{ MPa}$. Find the principal stresses and maximum shear stress.

Solution:

1. Average normal stress: \(\sigma_{\text{avg}} = \frac{120 + 40}{2} = 80 \text{ MPa}\)

2. Radius term: \(R = \sqrt{\left(\frac{120 - 40}{2}\right)^2 + 30^2} = \sqrt{40^2 + 30^2} = \sqrt{1600 + 900} = \sqrt{2500} = 50 \text{ MPa}\)

3. Principal stresses: \(\sigma_{1,2} = 80 \pm 50 \implies \sigma_1 = 130 \text{ MPa}, \sigma_2 = 30 \text{ MPa}\)

4. Max Shear Stress: \(\tau_{\max} = R = 50 \text{ MPa}\)

Operational Takeaway:

Maximum principal stress of 130 MPa is well below the 345 MPa yield strength of 2024-T3 aluminium skin, confirming structural airworthiness.

Subtopic 5 of 5

Mohr's Circle Graphical Method

Graphical Tensor Analysis

1. Formal Technical Definition

Mohr's Circle: A graphical representation of the transformation equations for 2D stress tensors, where normal stress ($\sigma$) is plotted on the horizontal axis and shear stress ($\tau$) on the vertical axis, forming a circle whose center and radius yield principal stresses and maximum shear stresses directly.

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

Christian Otto Mohr developed this geometric construction to avoid cumbersome trigonometric transformations. By plotting points corresponding to orthogonal face stress states $(\sigma_x, -\tau_{xy})$ and $(\sigma_y, \tau_{xy})$, a circle is established. Every point on Mohr's circle represents the state of stress on a specific inclined plane, with rotation angles on the circle doubled ($2\theta$) compared to physical space angles.

3. Mathematical Derivation & Equations

Circle Geometric Parameters:

Center coordinate (C): \(\left(\frac{\sigma_x + \sigma_y}{2}, 0\right)\)

Radius (R): \(R = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2}\)

Equation of circle in $\sigma-\tau$ plane:

\(\left(\sigma - \frac{\sigma_x + \sigma_y}{2}\right)^2 + \tau^2 = R^2\)

4. Dimensions & Units

Axes Coordinates:
  • • Both horizontal (\(\sigma\)) and vertical (\(\tau\)) axes have units of \(\text{MPa}\) or \(\text{psi}\).

5. Engineering Reference Data Table

State of Stress Mohr's Circle Appearance Principal Stresses
Pure Shear Center at origin $(0,0)$, Radius $R = \tau_{xy}$ \(\sigma_1 = \tau_{xy}, \sigma_2 = -\tau_{xy}\)
Equibiaxial Tension Collapses to a single point on $\sigma$ axis \(\sigma_1 = \sigma_2 = \sigma_x\)

6. Aircraft Application & DGCA Air Safety Connection

Fuselage Skin Diagonal Tension (Wagner Beams): Thin-skinned aircraft semi-monocoque structures buckle under shear loads, forming tension field bands at approximately $45^\circ$. Mohr's circle analysis is used by stress engineers during Type Certification (DGCA CAR 25) to verify skin diagonal tension stresses do not exceed ultimate tearing limits.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions

Trap: Sign convention for shear stress on Mohr's circle. Typically, clockwise shear stress is plotted upwards (or counter-clockwise downwards depending on convention text). Ensure consistency when locating reference points.

8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem:

Using Mohr's circle geometry, find principal stresses for $\sigma_x = 90 \text{ MPa}$, $\sigma_y = -10 \text{ MPa}$, and $\tau_{xy} = 40 \text{ MPa}$.

Solution:

1. Center C = \(\frac{90 + (-10)}{2} = \frac{80}{2} = 40 \text{ MPa}\)

2. Radius R = \(\sqrt{\left(\frac{90 - (-10)}{2}\right)^2 + 40^2} = \sqrt{50^2 + 40^2} = \sqrt{2500 + 1600} = \sqrt{4100} = 64.03 \text{ MPa}\)

3. Principal Stresses: \(\sigma_1 = C + R = 40 + 64.03 = 104.03 \text{ MPa}\)

\(\sigma_2 = C - R = 40 - 64.03 = -24.03 \text{ MPa}\)

Operational Takeaway:

The presence of a compressive principal stress ($\sigma_2 = -24.03 \text{ MPa}$) highlights potential local buckling susceptibility in thin aircraft stringer panels under combined torsional and bending loads.

Aviation Safety Case Studies & DGCA Regulatory Framework

Real-world aviation disaster investigations linked directly to stress concentrations, elasticity failures, and airworthiness directives.

Case Study 01

Aloha Airlines Flight 243 (1988)

Incident: A Boeing 737 suffered an explosive decompression and structural failure of the upper forward fuselage skin due to multiple site fatigue cracking along rivet holes.

Stress & Elasticity Connection: High cyclic hoop stress ($\sigma_h = pr/t$) combined with stress concentrations ($\text{SCF}$) around unbonded lap-joint rivet holes initiated micro-cracks. As stress intensity factors exceeded fracture toughness ($K_{Ic}$), sudden unstable crack propagation occurred.

DGCA / FAA Regulatory Impact: Led to the mandatory implementation of the Aging Aircraft Safety Act, rigorous eddy-current NDT inspections, and widespread structural modification directives (Supplemental Inspection Documents - SIDs).

Case Study 02

De Havilland Comet Disasters (1954)

Incident: Two commercial jet airliners disintegrated in flight due to catastrophic fatigue failure originating from square window cutouts.

Stress & Elasticity Connection: Square window corners acted as severe stress concentration points where elastic stress concentration factor ($\text{SCF}$) exceeded 5.0, pushing local stresses far beyond the yield strength of the aluminium alloy under repeated cabin pressurization cycles.

DGCA / ICAO Regulatory Impact: Revolutionized fatigue testing of full-scale fuselage water tanks and enforced mandatory round-corner window designs with gradual stress redistribution paths.

High-Yield Revision Cheatsheet

Essential equations and principles for quick recall during UPSC ASO exam preparation.

Core Equations

  • • Uniaxial Hooke's Law: $\sigma = E\varepsilon$
  • • Shear Hooke's Law: $\tau = G\gamma$
  • • Poisson's Ratio: $\nu = -\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{trans}}}{\varepsilon_{\text{axial}}}$
  • • Moduli Relations: $G = \frac{E}{2(1+\nu)}$, $K = \frac{E}{3(1-2\nu)}$
  • • Principal Stresses: $\sigma_{1,2} = \frac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2}$
  • • Max Shear: $\tau_{\max} = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2}$

Key Aerospace Constants

  • • Aluminium $E \approx 70-73 \text{ GPa}, \nu \approx 0.33$
  • • Steel $E \approx 200-210 \text{ GPa}, \nu \approx 0.30$
  • • Ultimate Safety Factor (DGCA CAR 25): $1.50$ on Limit Load
  • • Thermodynamic limits for Poisson's ratio: $-1.0 \le \nu \le 0.5$

UPSC Air Safety Officer Interview Corner

Expert model answers for board viva voce questions.

Q: As an Air Safety Officer investigating a suspected wing spar fatigue failure, what initial calculations would you perform on site? ▼

Model Answer: As an ASO, I would immediately inspect the fracture surface for beach marks and chevron patterns indicating fatigue island progression. I would verify the flight logs for gust load history and maneuver load factors ($n$). Subsequently, I would calculate nominal bending stress ($\sigma = My/I$) and shear stress across the spar web, checking against material fatigue limits and identifying potential stress concentration sites such as fastener holes or machining radii.

Q: Why is Poisson's ratio limited to a maximum of 0.5 for isotropic elastic materials? ▼

Model Answer: Bulk modulus is given by $K = \frac{E}{3(1-2\nu)}$. If $\nu$ reached 0.5, the denominator would become zero, making the bulk modulus infinite. An infinite bulk modulus implies the material is completely incompressible (volume change $\Delta V / V = 0$ under hydrostatic pressure), requiring infinite energy to compress. Thermodynamics dictates that strain energy density must remain positive definite, restricting isotropic Poisson's ratio to $\nu \le 0.5$.
