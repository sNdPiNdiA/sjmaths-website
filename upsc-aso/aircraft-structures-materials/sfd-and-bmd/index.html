SFD & BMD

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Strain gauges and fiber-optic Bragg grating sensors mounted on wing spars monitor real-time bending moments during maneuver g-loading.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Exceeding maximum bending moment limits during turbulent penetration or high-g pull-ups compromises structural integrity and flight envelope limits.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 mandate strict adherence to static strength and fatigue life limits derived from SFD/BMD envelopes.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: ASO investigators analyze wing root spar fracture surfaces and corresponding SFD/BMD models to reconstruct inflight structural breakup sequences.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Shear Force Diagram (SFD) and Bending Moment Diagram (BMD) are graphical representations of internal shear forces ($V$) and bending moments ($M$) acting at any cross-section along the longitudinal axis of an aircraft structural member: $V(x) = \frac{dM(x)}{dx}$ and $q(x) = \frac{dV(x)}{dx}$.
  • Physical Meaning: SFD illustrates internal transverse forces resisting sliding, while BMD depicts internal couple moments resisting structural rotation and curvature under aerodynamic loads.
  • Governing Law: Equilibrium of differential beam elements establishes differential relations between distributed load intensity $q(x)$, shear force $V(x)$, and bending moment $M(x)$.
  • Continuum Boundary: Assumes prismatic or variable-section Euler-Bernoulli or Timoshenko beam elements under static or quasi-steady flight load conditions.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $V(x)$ = Internal shear force at longitudinal coordinate $x$ ($\text{N}$)
  • $M(x)$ = Internal bending moment at longitudinal coordinate $x$ ($\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$)
  • $q(x)$ = Distributed transverse load intensity per unit span ($\text{N}/\text{m}$)
  • $x$ = Longitudinal neutral axis coordinate along the structural member ($\text{m}$)
  • $\frac{dM}{dx}$ = Rate of change of bending moment equal to shear force ($\text{N}$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. External Aerodynamic Loading: Spanwise lift distributions and inertial mass distributions exert distributed transverse loads $q(x)$ and concentrated point loads on wing spars and fuselage frames.
2. Internal Load Transference: Differential elements experience internal shearing stresses and normal bending stresses to maintain static equilibrium against external loads.
3. Shear Force Generation: Integrating distributed load $q(x)$ along the span yields the internal shear force $V(x)$ at any given station.
4. Bending Moment Accumulation: Integrating the shear force function $V(x)$ along the span generates the internal bending moment $M(x)$ causing structural curvature.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Differential Equilibrium

Summing vertical forces on a differential beam element of length $dx$ yields the shear-load relationship.

$$\sum F_y = 0 \implies V - q(x)dx - (V + dV) = 0 \implies \frac{dV}{dx} = -q(x)$$
Step 2: Moment Equilibrium

Summing moments about the right edge of the differential element yields the moment-shear relationship.

$$\sum M_{right} = 0 \implies M + Vdx - q(x)dx\left(\frac{dx}{2}\right) - (M + dM) = 0 \implies \frac{dM}{dx} = V(x)$$
Step 3: Direct Integration

Integrating the differential relations between two arbitrary stations $x_1$ and $x_2$ along the span.

$$V(x_2) - V(x_1) = -\int_{x_1}^{x_2} q(x)dx, \quad M(x_2) - M(x_1) = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} V(x)dx$$
Step 4: Boundary Conditions

Applying free, pinned, or clamped boundary conditions to evaluate constants of integration for specific aircraft spar configurations.

$$V(0) = R_1, \quad M(0) = 0, \quad V(L) = -R_2, \quad M(L) = 0$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Shear Force $[M^1 L^1 T^{-2}]$; Bending Moment $[M^1 L^2 T^{-2}]$; Distributed Load $[M^1 L^0 T^{-2}]$.
  • SI Unit: Shear Force in Newtons ($\text{N}$); Bending Moment in Newton-meters ($\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$); Distributed Load in Newtons per meter ($\text{N}/\text{m}$).
  • CGS Unit: Shear Force in dynes ($\text{dyn}$); Bending Moment in dyne-centimeters ($\text{dyn}\cdot\text{cm}$).
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Pounds-force ($\text{lbf}$), Pound-inches ($\text{lbf}\cdot\text{in}$), and Pound-feet ($\text{lbf}\cdot\text{ft}$); $1 \text{ lbf} \approx 4.448 \text{ N}$, $1 \text{ lbf}\cdot\text{ft} \approx 1.3558 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'I', Part VII and ICAO Annex 8 Airworthiness of Aircraft require static test verification of limit load bending moments.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Wing root bending directly impacts fuel tank sealing, control surface hinge alignment, and wing clearance tolerances during gust loads.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: Boeing 747 cargo door and fuselage separation incidents highlighted the necessity of accounting for multi-axle bending moments and internal pressure coupling.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Sign Convention Confusion

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming sagging bending moment is negative and hogging is positive.

✅ UPSC Truth: Standard engineering convention defines sagging (tension on bottom) as positive bending moment ($M > 0$) and hogging as negative.

Trap 2: Point Load Discontinuity in BMD

❌ Examiner Trap: Drawing a sharp point or jump in the Bending Moment Diagram under a concentrated point load.

✅ UPSC Truth: Concentrated point loads cause a sharp jump (discontinuity) in the Shear Force Diagram, whereas bending moment diagrams exhibit a continuous corner (slope discontinuity).

Trap 3: Uniformly Distributed Load Slope

❌ Examiner Trap: Treating the shear force diagram as a constant rectangular block under a UDL.

✅ UPSC Truth: Under a UDL, the shear force varies linearly with a slope equal to $-q$, while the bending moment diagram is a parabolic curve.

Trap 4: Inflection Point Misidentification

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing the point of zero shear with the point of zero bending moment.

✅ UPSC Truth: The bending moment is maximum or minimum where the shear force is zero ($V = \frac{dM}{dx} = 0$), whereas inflection points occur where bending moment is zero ($M = 0$).

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A cantilever aircraft wing spar of length $L = 10 \text{ m}$ is subjected to a uniformly distributed upward lift load $q_0 = 5000 \text{ N/m}$ along its entire span. Determine the maximum shear force and maximum bending moment at the wing root ($x = 0$).

Given Flight Parameters:
Span length $L = 10 \text{ m}$; Uniform lift load intensity $q(x) = 5000 \text{ N/m}$; Cantilever fixed support at $x = 0$.
Governing Formula:
$$V(x) = \int_{x}^{L} q(x)dx, \quad M(x) = -\int_{0}^{x} V(x)dx$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Integrate the distributed load to find the shear force equation: $V(x) = \int_{x}^{10} 5000 dx = 5000(10 - x) \text{ N}$. Step 2: Evaluate maximum shear force at the wing root ($x = 0$): $V(0) = 5000(10 - 0) = 50,000 \text{ N} = 50 \text{ kN}$. Step 3: Integrate the shear force to find the bending moment equation and evaluate at $x = 0$: $M(x) = -\int_{0}^{x} 5000(10 - x)dx = -5000\left(10x - \frac{x^2}{2}\right)$. At $x = 10 \text{ m}$ (root), $M(0) = -\int_{0}^{10} 5000(10 - x)dx = -\frac{5000(10)^2}{2} = -250,000 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m} = -250 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$.
Final Answer: Maximum Shear Force $V_{\max} = 50 \text{ kN}$ at wing root; Maximum Bending Moment $M_{\max} = 250 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$ at wing root.
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate root bending moment calculations dictate wing spar skin thickness and fastener sizing, directly preventing fatigue cracking and catastrophic inflight wing separation.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the physical relationship between the distributed load $q(x)$ and the shear force $V(x)$ in a beam?
A $\frac{dV}{dx} = -q(x)$
B $\frac{dV}{dx} = q(x)$
C $\frac{d^2V}{dx^2} = q(x)$
D $V(x) = \frac{dq}{dx}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
From vertical equilibrium of a differential beam element, $\sum F_y = 0 \implies V - q(x)dx - (V + dV) = 0$, leading directly to $\frac{dV}{dx} = -q(x)$.
Question 2 • conceptual
At a point where the shear force diagram crosses zero and transitions from positive to negative, what occurs on the bending moment diagram?
A Local minimum bending moment
B Local maximum bending moment
C Point of inflection (Zero bending moment)
D Discontinuity step in bending moment
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Since $\frac{dM}{dx} = V(x)$, when shear force $V(x)$ is zero, the slope of the bending moment is zero, indicating a local maximum or minimum bending moment.
Question 3 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding SFD and BMD: 1. A concentrated point load causes a linear jump in the SFD. 2. A concentrated couple moment causes a discontinuity jump in the BMD. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Concentrated forces create step changes (jumps) in SFD, and concentrated couple moments create step changes in BMD. Both statements are correct.
Question 4 • numerical
A simply supported beam of length $6 \text{ m}$ carries a central concentrated load of $12 \text{ kN}$. What is the maximum bending moment in the beam?
A $12 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$
B $18 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$
C $36 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$
D $72 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Reactions at each support are $6 \text{ kN}$. Maximum bending moment occurs at mid-span ($x = 3 \text{ m}$): $M_{\max} = 6 \text{ kN} \times 3 \text{ m} = 18 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$.
Question 5 • assertion_reason
Assertion (A): The bending moment is zero at the free end of a cantilever beam with no applied terminal couple. Reason (R): The bending moment is obtained by integrating the shear force from the free end inward, and no shear force exists outboard of the free end.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A free end has no external forces or moments applied, so internal shear and bending moments must vanish at the boundary, making both Assertion and Reason true with correct linkage.
Question 6 • conceptual
If a cantilever beam is subjected to a clockwise concentrated couple at its free end, what is the shape of its bending moment diagram along the span?
A Parabolic curve
B Triangular linear variation
C Constant rectangular block
D Cubic curve
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A concentrated couple at the free end creates a constant internal bending moment equal to the magnitude of the applied couple all along the span, as shear force is zero.
Question 7 • numerical
A cantilever beam of length $4 \text{ m}$ carries a UDL of $2 \text{ kN/m}$ over its entire length. What is the bending moment at the fixed root?
A $-8 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$
B $-16 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$
C $-32 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$
D $-4 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Total load $W = 2 \times 4 = 8 \text{ kN}$ acting at centroid $2 \text{ m}$ from root. Bending moment at root $M = -W \times \bar{x} = -8 \times 2 = -16 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$.
Question 8 • statement
Which of the following relations hold true for any beam segment under static equilibrium? 1. $\frac{d^2M}{dx^2} = -q(x)$ 2. $\frac{dV}{dx} = M(x)$
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Since $\frac{dM}{dx} = V$ and $\frac{dV}{dx} = -q$, differentiating $M$ twice gives $\frac{d^2M}{dx^2} = \frac{dV}{dx} = -q(x)$. Statement 1 is correct, statement 2 is false.
Question 9 • conceptual
What does an inflection point (point of contraflexure) on a bending moment diagram signify for an aircraft structure?
A Maximum shear stress
B Change in curvature sign from sagging to hogging
C Zero shear force
D Maximum tensile stress
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A point of contraflexure is where the bending moment changes sign (e.g., from positive sagging to negative hogging), meaning the beam curvature reverses.
Question 10 • numerical
A simply supported beam has a span of $10 \text{ m}$ and carries a uniformly distributed load of $3 \text{ kN/m}$ across the entire span. What is the maximum bending moment?
A $30 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$
B $37.5 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$
C $75 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$
D $150 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
For a simply supported beam with UDL, $M_{\max} = \frac{qL^2}{8} = \frac{3 \times (10)^2}{8} = \frac{300}{8} = 37.5 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$.
Question 11 • assertion_reason
Assertion (A): The shear force diagram is always a straight horizontal line between point loads. Reason (R): The derivative of shear force equals the distributed load intensity, so zero distributed load results in zero slope for the shear force diagram.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Since $\frac{dV}{dx} = -q(x)$, where $q(x) = 0$ (no distributed load), the slope $\frac{dV}{dx} = 0$, making SFD a horizontal straight line. Both statements are true and linked.
Question 12 • conceptual
In aircraft wing structural analysis, why is the shear flow distribution critical alongside the shear force diagram?
A To determine cabin pressure altitude
B To calculate torsional shear stress distribution in thin-walled open and closed sections
C To evaluate fuel flow rates in engines
D To calibrate pitot-static airspeed indicators
Solution & Technical Rationale:
While SFD gives total transverse shear force $V$, aircraft wings are thin-walled box beams requiring shear flow ($q = \frac{V Q}{I}$) analysis to find localized shear stresses.

Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
A cantilever beam of length $L$ carries a point load $P$ at its free end. The bending moment diagram is:
A Parabolic with peak at root
B Triangular (linear) with maximum value $PL$ at the root
C Rectangular constant value $PL$
D Cubic curve
Rationale:
For a cantilever with tip load $P$, $M(x) = -P(L - x)$. At root ($x=0$), $M = -PL$, varying linearly to zero at the tip.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
If the shear force diagram between two points is a sloping straight line, what type of loading acts on that segment?
A No load ($q = 0$)
B Uniformly distributed load ($q = \text{constant}$)
C Concentrated point load
D Linearly varying distributed load (Triangular load)
Rationale:
Since $\frac{dV}{dx} = -q$, a constant negative slope of SFD indicates a constant distributed load intensity $q$.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the bending moment at a simple support (pin or roller) located at the end of a beam?
A Maximum positive
B Maximum negative
C Zero
D Equal to support reaction
Rationale:
Simple supports (pins and rollers) cannot sustain rotational moments, so the bending moment at a simple end support is always zero.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
For a simply supported beam carrying a UDL $q$ over span $L$, where does maximum bending moment occur?
A At the left support
B At the right support
C At the exact mid-span ($x = L/2$)
D At $x = L/4$
Rationale:
Symmetry dictates that zero shear force occurs at mid-span, which corresponds to the location of the maximum bending moment $M = \frac{qL^2}{8}$.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
In an aircraft wing spar analysis, a concentrated engine pylon vertical load creates which feature on the SFD?
A A smooth parabolic curve
B A vertical step discontinuity equal to the engine weight
C An inflection point with zero shear
D A cubic transition
Rationale:
Concentrated forces such as engine weight or landing gear reactions manifest as vertical jump discontinuities in the shear force diagram.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
If the bending moment diagram is a parabolic curve, what is the nature of the corresponding shear force diagram?
A Zero
B Horizontal straight line
C Sloping straight line (Linear)
D Cubic curve
Rationale:
Since $V = \integrand{q} = \frac{dM}{dx}$, differentiating a second-degree polynomial (parabola) yields a first-degree polynomial (sloping straight line).
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the primary sign convention for internal bending moment that produces tension in the bottom fibers of a horizontal beam?
A Negative bending moment (Hogging)
B Positive bending moment (Sagging)
C Zero bending moment
D Neutral bending moment
Rationale:
Sagging creates concavity upwards, placing bottom fibers in tension and top fibers in compression, defined as positive bending moment.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
A beam of length $L$ has a fixed support at one end and is free at the other. If a downward point load is applied at the free tip, what is the sign of the bending moment along the span?
A Entirely positive
B Entirely negative
C Zero everywhere
D Positive at tip and negative at root
Rationale:
A tip load on a cantilever causes hogging curvature (tension on top, compression on bottom), making the bending moment negative throughout the span.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
Why is the calculation of maximum shear force and bending moment critical for DGCA airworthiness certification of transport aircraft?
A To determine cabin humidity levels
B To ensure structural margins of safety against yield and ultimate failure under limit and ultimate flight loads
C To calculate fuel burn efficiency during cruise
D To set transponder squawk codes
Rationale:
Airworthiness compliance requires proving that structural members withstand limit loads without permanent deformation and ultimate loads without fracture.
