High/low-cycle fatigue

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Engine vibration monitoring systems and digital flight data recorders (DFDR) track rotor speeds and high-cycle vibration signatures.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Aircraft structural inspection thresholds and safe-life limits directly dictate operational dispatch envelopes and maintenance deferrals.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 mandate strict adherence to manufacturer Supplemental Structural Inspection Documents (SSID).
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: ASO investigators use fractography (beach marks and striations) during wreckage analysis to distinguish HCF from LCF failures.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

High-cycle and low-cycle fatigue govern structural degradation under cyclic loading, quantified via stress-life ($\sigma$-$N$) and strain-life (Coffin-Manson) formulations. $$N_f = \left(\frac{\Delta \sigma}{2K'}\right)^{\frac{1}{n'}}$$
  • Physical Meaning: Fatigue is progressive structural damage under fluctuating stress amplitudes below ultimate tensile strength.
  • Governing Law: Basquin's equation rules elastic high-cycle fatigue, while Coffin-Manson rules plastic low-cycle fatigue.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid for macro-cracks spanning multiple grains, transitioning to micromechanics at dislocation scales.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\sigma_{max}$ = Maximum cyclic stress (MPa)
  • $\sigma_{min}$ = Minimum cyclic stress (MPa)
  • $R$ = Stress ratio ($\sigma_{min} / \sigma_{max}$) (Dimensionless)
  • $N_f$ = Cycles to failure (Cycles)
  • $\Delta \epsilon_p$ = Plastic strain range (Dimensionless)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Cyclic Slip & Dislocation Motion: Alternating shear stresses cause persistent slip bands (PSBs) and dislocation pile-ups on specific crystallographic planes.
2. Microcrack Initiation: Persistent slip bands develop surface extrusions and intrusions, acting as primary stress concentrators for microcrack nucleation.
3. Crack Propagation: Tensile loading cycles advance the crack front, leaving characteristic microscopic striations on the fracture surface.
4. Catastrophic Failure: The remaining net section experiences sudden ductile or brittle overstress fracture when reduced below critical load-bearing capacity.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Basquin High-Cycle Relation

Relates stress amplitude to fatigue life in the elastic regime.

$$\sigma_a = \sigma_f' (2N_f)^b$$
Step 2: Coffin-Manson Low-Cycle Relation

Relates plastic strain range to fatigue life in the plastic regime.

$$\frac{\Delta \varepsilon_p}{2} = \varepsilon_f' (2N_f)^c$$
Step 3: Total Strain-Life Superposition

Combines elastic and plastic components using Morrow's formulation.

$$\frac{\Delta \varepsilon}{2} = \frac{\sigma_f'}{E}(2N_f)^b + \varepsilon_f'(2N_f)^c$$
Step 4: Goodman Mean Stress Correction

Accounts for tensile mean stress reduction on fatigue limit.

$$\frac{\sigma_a}{\sigma_e} + \frac{\sigma_m}{\sigma_{uts}} = 1$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Stress dimensions are $[M L^{-1} T^{-2}]$, while fatigue life $N_f$ is strictly dimensionless (integer count).
  • SI Unit: Stress is measured in Pascals ($Pa$ or $N/m^2$), and strain amplitude in meter per meter ($m/m$).
  • CGS Unit: Stress is expressed in Barye ($dyn/cm^2$) or $g/(cm \cdot s^2)$.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Stress uses pounds per square inch ($psi$) or ksi, where $1 \text{ ksi} = 6.895 \text{ MPa}$.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'B', Part VII and ICAO Annex 8 mandate rigorous fatigue evaluation and crack detection thresholds.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Engine compressor discs, turbine blades, wing spar lower caps, and landing gear attachment lugs are highly vulnerable to fatigue.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: Aloha Airlines Flight 243 experienced fuselage lap joint fatigue failure, leading to explosive decompression and strict multi-site damage mandates.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: HCF vs LCF Stress Levels

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming LCF occurs exclusively at high temperatures.

✅ UPSC Truth: LCF is defined by plastic deformation and low cycles ($< 10^4$), which can happen at room temperature due to high mechanical loads.

Trap 2: Basquin Equation Application

❌ Examiner Trap: Applying Basquin's stress-life equation directly to high plastic strain regions.

✅ UPSC Truth: Basquin's equation is valid strictly for the elastic high-cycle regime; Coffin-Manson must be used for plastic LCF.

Trap 3: Mean Stress Effect on Fatigue

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming compressive mean stress reduces fatigue life just like tensile mean stress.

✅ UPSC Truth: Compressive mean stress ($R < -1$) actually retards crack closure and improves fatigue life.

Trap 4: Striations vs Beach Marks

❌ Examiner Trap: Treating macroscopic beach marks and microscopic striations as identical phenomena.

✅ UPSC Truth: Beach marks represent macroscopic arrest lines from variable loading, whereas striations represent single-cycle advance.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A titanium compressor blade experiences high-cycle vibratory fatigue with a maximum stress of $600 \text{ MPa}$ and minimum stress of $-200 \text{ MPa}$. Determine the stress range, stress amplitude, and stress ratio.

Given Flight Parameters:
$\sigma_{max} = 600 \text{ MPa}$, $\sigma_{min} = -200 \text{ MPa}$
Governing Formula:
$$\Delta \sigma = \sigma_{max} - \sigma_{min}, \quad \sigma_a = \frac{\Delta \sigma}{2}, \quad R = \frac{\sigma_{min}}{\sigma_{max}}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Calculate stress range $\Delta \sigma = 600 - (-200) = 800 \text{ MPa}$. Step 2: Calculate stress amplitude $\sigma_a = 800 / 2 = 400 \text{ MPa}$. Step 3: Calculate stress ratio $R = -200 / 600 = -0.333$.
Final Answer: $Stress Range = 800 MPa, Stress Amplitude = 400 MPa, Stress Ratio R = -0.333$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate calculation of stress ratios is vital for applying appropriate S-N curves and ensuring safe turbine blade inspection intervals under DGCA airworthiness directives.

Question 1 • conceptual
Which of the following best differentiates High-Cycle Fatigue (HCF) from Low-Cycle Fatigue (LCF)?
A HCF involves macroscopic plastic deformation and fewer than $10^4$ cycles.
B LCF involves cyclic loading predominantly within the elastic region with long lives.
C HCF involves elastic deformation with high cycle counts ($> 10^4$), whereas LCF involves significant plastic strain.
D LCF is caused exclusively by aerodynamic flutter at supersonic speeds.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
HCF is characterized by high cycle counts and predominantly elastic stresses, whereas LCF involves high plastic strain and low cycle counts.
Question 2 • conceptual
In aircraft structural analysis, what does a stress ratio $R = -1$ signify?
A Fully reversed cyclic loading (tension-compression)
B Pulsating tension from zero to maximum load
C Static tensile loading with zero variation
D Fully compressive fatigue loading
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A stress ratio $R = \sigma_{min} / \sigma_{max} = -1$ indicates fully reversed loading where peak tensile and compressive stresses are equal.
Question 3 • conceptual
Which empirical relation is primarily used to model the elastic regime of high-cycle fatigue?
A Coffin-Manson relation
B Basquin's equation
C Paris-Erdogan law
D Ramberg-Osgood relation
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Basquin's equation relates stress amplitude to fatigue life in the elastic region of high-cycle fatigue.
Question 4 • conceptual
What is the primary physical feature observed on a fracture surface under scanning electron microscopy (SEM) confirming fatigue failure?
A Dimple rupture formations
B Cleavage facets
C Microscopic fatigue striations
D Intergranular thermal cracking
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Fatigue striations represent the incremental advance of the crack front per loading cycle and are definitive proof of fatigue failure.
Question 5 • conceptual
How does tensile mean stress affect the fatigue life of an aircraft structural component?
A It increases fatigue life by promoting work hardening.
B It has no impact on fatigue life.
C It decreases fatigue life by accelerating crack opening and propagation.
D It converts low-cycle fatigue into infinite life.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Tensile mean stress keeps cracks open longer during the cycle, accelerating crack growth and reducing fatigue life.
Question 6 • conceptual
Which Paris-Erdogan law parameter governs the steady-state stage II crack propagation rate?
A Ultimate tensile strength
B Stress intensity factor range ($\Delta K$)
C Brinell hardness number
D Shear modulus
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The Paris law governs Stage II fatigue crack growth using the stress intensity factor range $\Delta K$.
Question 7 • conceptual
Which aircraft component is most susceptible to Low-Cycle Fatigue (LCF) due to thermal and centrifugal start-stop cycles?
A Pitot-static probe housing
B Turbine rotor disc
C Aileron control cable
D Static wick discharger
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Turbine rotor discs undergo severe thermal gradients and centrifugal loading during engine start-stop cycles, driving LCF.
Question 8 • conceptual
What is the primary purpose of shot peening in aircraft manufacturing?
A To increase surface roughness for paint adhesion
B To induce beneficial compressive residual stresses that retard fatigue crack initiation
C To reduce component weight
D To relieve residual thermal stresses from welding
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Shot peening introduces surface compressive residual stresses, which significantly improve high-cycle fatigue resistance.
Question 9 • conceptual
What does the endurance limit signify on an S-N (Wöhler) curve for ferrous alloys?
A The stress level below which fatigue failure theoretically never occurs regardless of cycles.
B The ultimate tensile strength of the material.
C The yield point under dynamic loading.
D The threshold stress intensity factor.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The endurance limit is the stress threshold below which ferrous metals can endure infinite loading cycles without failing.
Question 10 • conceptual
Which failure theory or correction accounts for non-zero mean stresses in fatigue design?
A Von Mises yield criterion
B Goodman, Soderberg, and Gerber relations
C Tresca maximum shear stress theory
D Hooke's law of elasticity
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Goodman, Soderberg, and Gerber criteria are standard mean stress correction methods used in fatigue design.
Question 11 • conceptual
Why are fastener holes in aircraft wing skins prime locations for fatigue crack initiation?
A They act as geometric stress concentrations and are prone to fretting.
B They are made of softer materials.
C They experience zero stress during flight.
D They are subjected exclusively to thermal creep.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Fastener holes cause stress concentration and fastener-to-hole contact can cause fretting fatigue.
Question 12 • conceptual
In the context of DGCA airworthiness audits, what is 'Safe-Life' design philosophy?
A Components are replaced after a specified operational hour limit regardless of inspection results.
B Components are used until visible cracks appear.
C Components are designed to tolerate undetected cracks indefinitely.
D Components require no maintenance throughout aircraft lifecycle.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Safe-life philosophy mandates mandatory retirement of critical parts upon reaching certified operating hour limits.

A steel aircraft component undergoes cyclic loading with $\sigma_{max} = 300 \text{ MPa}$ and $\sigma_{min} = 100 \text{ MPa}$. What is the stress amplitude?
A 100 MPa
B 200 MPa
C 150 MPa
D 400 MPa
Rationale:
Stress amplitude $\sigma_a = (300 - 100) / 2 = 100 \text{ MPa}$.

Which of the following materials typically exhibits a true fatigue endurance limit?
A Aluminum alloys
B Titanium alloys
C Low-carbon steels
D Austenitic stainless steels
Rationale:
Steels and ferrous alloys exhibit a distinct endurance limit, whereas aluminum alloys show continuous fatigue strength reduction.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the physical significance of Paris Law exponent '$m$' in crack propagation?
A It represents the slope of the linear region in Stage II crack growth on a log-log plot.
B It defines the ultimate tensile strength.
C It gives the exact cycle count at crack initiation.
D It measures material density.
Rationale:
In $da/dN = C(\Delta K)^m$, '$m$' is the slope of the log-log crack growth rate versus stress intensity range curve.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1

A Overload tensile fracture from bird strike
B Variable amplitude loading arrest fronts over multiple operational cycles
C High-temperature creep deformation
D Hydrogen embrittlement
Rationale:
Beach marks are macroscopic macro-fractographic features indicating intermittent arrest of fatigue crack propagation under varying loads.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
According to Miner's Rule for cumulative fatigue damage, failure occurs when the cumulative damage index $\sum (n_i / N_i)$ reaches:
A 0.5
B 1.0
C 2.0
D 10.0
Rationale:
Miner's linear damage hypothesis predicts failure when cumulative damage fraction $\sum (n_i / N_i) = 1.0$.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
Which surface treatment is most effective in preventing fatigue crack initiation by inducing compressive stresses?
A Chemical etching
B Shot peening
C Standard annealing
D Electroplating with cadmium
Rationale:
Shot peening bombards the surface with media, inducing a high-magnitude residual compressive stress layer that resists fatigue.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
What distinguishes Low-Cycle Fatigue (LCF) mechanics from High-Cycle Fatigue (HCF)?
A LCF is governed by total strain range including plastic strain; HCF is purely elastic.
B LCF occurs at ultrasonic frequencies.
C LCF does not involve dislocations.
D HCF requires plastic deformation in every cycle.
Rationale:
LCF incorporates plastic strain formulations (Coffin-Manson), whereas HCF assumes cyclic stresses remain within the elastic limit (Basquin).
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
In structural airworthiness, what does the term 'Damage Tolerance' signify?
A The structure is designed to sustain operational loads with pre-existing cracks until detected by scheduled inspections.
B The structure will never develop cracks.
C All cracked parts must be grounded immediately.
D Inspection intervals are set to zero.
Rationale:
Damage tolerance assumes flaws exist and requires structures to safely sustain loads until inspections catch them.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the stress ratio $R$ for a zero-to-tension cyclic loading condition?
A $R = -1$
B $R = 0$
C $R = +1$
D $R = \infty$
Rationale:
Zero-to-tension loading has $\sigma_{min} = 0$, resulting in a stress ratio $R = 0 / \sigma_{max} = 0$.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
Why is fretting fatigue particularly dangerous in riveted aircraft lap joints?
A Small relative slip between mating surfaces under vibration nucleates microcracks rapidly.
B It increases the yield strength beyond design limits.
C It prevents corrosion entirely.
D It eliminates stress concentrations.
Rationale:
Fretting involves microscopic oscillatory slip between contacting surfaces, severely reducing fatigue life through premature crack nucleation.
