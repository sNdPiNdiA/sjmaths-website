Circular shafts/tubes

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Turboprop engine torque meters and dual-concentric flight control actuation torque tubes rely on accurate structural torsional calibration.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Exceeding torsional fatigue limits in main rotor shafts or tail rotor drive shafts can lead to catastrophic in-flight structural separation.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'B', Part VII mandates rigorous nondestructive testing (NDT) and crack inspection for torque-transmitting shafts.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: ASR analysts examine beach marks and ratchet marks on fractured tubular drive shafts during metallurgical failure analysis of helicopter gearboxes.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Circular shafts and hollow tubes subjected to pure torsion obey the Navier-Bernoulli elastic torsion theory, expressed as $\frac{T}{J} = \frac{\tau}{r} = \frac{G\theta}{L}$.
  • Physical Meaning: Torsion induces internal shear stresses on transverse cross-sections and complementary longitudinal shear stresses.
  • Governing Law: Hooke's Law in shear states that shear stress $\tau$ is directly proportional to shear strain $\gamma$ via $\tau = G\gamma$.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid strictly within the elastic limit for homogeneous, isotropic, linearly elastic materials under small deformations.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $T$ = Applied internal torque or twisting moment ($\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$)
  • $J$ = Polar moment of inertia of the cross-section ($\text{m}^4$)
  • $\tau$ = Induced torsional shear stress at radius $r$ ($\text{Pa}$ or $\text{N}/\text{m}^2$)
  • $r$ = Radial distance from the centroidal axis to the fiber of interest ($\text{m}$)
  • $G$ = Modulus of rigidity or shear modulus of the material ($\text{Pa}$)
  • $\theta$ = Angle of twist of the shaft over length $L$ (radians)
  • $L$ = Effective length of the structural shaft ($\text{m}$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Torsional moments displace atomic crystal planes relative to one another, shearing interatomic bonds without altering bond length.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Internal shear stresses vary linearly from zero at the neutral axis to a maximum extreme at the outer periphery ($r = c$).
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Structural components like aileron torque tubes and helicopter rotor drive shafts transmit high cyclic torque under dynamic aeroelastic loads.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Kinematic assumption of rigid cross-sectional rotation by angle $\phi$ per unit length.

$$\gamma = r\frac{d\phi}{dx}$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Constitutive relation substituting shear modulus $G$ into the kinematic strain field.

$$\tau(r) = G \cdot r \cdot \theta$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Equating internal resisting torque to applied external torque by integrating over area.

$$T = \int_A \tau(r) r \, dA = \frac{G\theta}{L} \int_A r^2 \, dA$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Defining polar moment of inertia $J = \int r^2 dA$ yielding the standard torsion equation.

$$\frac{T}{J} = \frac{\tau}{r} = \frac{G\theta}{L}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Torque $[M L^2 T^{-2}]$, Polar Moment $[L^4]$, Shear Stress $[M L^{-1} T^{-2}]$, Shear Modulus $[M L^{-1} T^{-2}]$.
  • SI Unit: Torque in Newton-meters ($\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$), Stress in Pascals ($\text{Pa}$ or $\text{N}/\text{m}^2$), Polar Moment in meters to the fourth ($\text{m}^4$).
  • CGS Unit: Torque in Dyne-centimeters ($\text{dyn}\cdot\text{cm}$), Stress in Baryes ($\text{dyn}/\text{cm}^2$), Polar Moment in $\text{cm}^4$.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Torque in Pound-inches ($\text{lb}\cdot\text{in}$) or Pound-feet ($\text{lb}\cdot\text{ft}$), Stress in pounds per square inch ($\text{psi}$).

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Compliance with ICAO Annex 8 and DGCA CAR Section 2 requires mandatory life-limited parts (LLP) tracking for primary drive shafts.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Helicopter main transmission shafts, constant speed drive (CSD) shafts, and flap torque tubes are classified as flight-critical primary structures.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: Inter-shaft torsional fatigue failures in auxiliary power units (APUs) have caused localized uncontained thermal events and smoke ingestion.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Solid vs. Hollow Polar Moment Formula

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming $J = \frac{\pi d^4}{32}$ is valid for thin-walled circular tubes without inner diameter correction.

✅ UPSC Truth: For hollow tubes, $J = \frac{\pi}{32}(d_o^4 - d_i^4)$ where $d_o$ and $d_i$ are outer and inner diameters.

Trap 2: Maximum Shear Stress Location

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing torsional shear stress is uniform across the entire cross-sectional area of a solid shaft.

✅ UPSC Truth: Shear stress varies linearly, reaching zero at the center and maximum at the extreme outer radius $r = c$.

Trap 3: Units of Angle of Twist

❌ Examiner Trap: Directly substituting degrees into the torsion angle formula $\theta = \frac{TL}{GJ}$.

✅ UPSC Truth: The angle of twist $\theta$ derived from integration is strictly in radians; conversion to degrees requires multiplication by $180/\pi$.

Trap 4: Superposition of Bending and Torsion

❌ Examiner Trap: Adding torsional shear stress directly to bending normal stress using standard algebraic addition.

✅ UPSC Truth: Normal stresses and shear stresses must be combined using Mohr's Circle or principal stress theories (Tresca/Von Mises).

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A hollow steel drive shaft of outer diameter 80 mm and inner diameter 60 mm transmits a torque of $4.5 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$. Calculate the maximum shear stress induced in the shaft and the angle of twist over a length of $1.5 \text{ m}$ if $G = 80 \text{ GPa}$.

Given Flight Parameters:
Outer diameter $d_o = 0.08 \text{ m}$, Inner diameter $d_i = 0.06 \text{ m}$, Torque $T = 4500 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$, Length $L = 1.5 \text{ m}$, Shear modulus $G = 80 \times 10^9 \text{ Pa}$.
Governing Formula:
$$J = \frac{\pi}{32}(d_o^4 - d_i^4), \quad \tau_{\max} = \frac{T \cdot c}{J}, \quad \theta = \frac{T \cdot L}{G \cdot J}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute polar moment of inertia $J = \frac{\pi}{32}(0.08^4 - 0.06^4) = \frac{\pi}{32}(4.096 \times 10^{-6} - 1.296 \times 10^{-6}) = 2.7489 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^4$. Step 2: Compute maximum shear stress $\tau_{\max} = \frac{4500 \times 0.04}{2.7489 \times 10^{-6}} = 65.48 \times 10^6 \text{ Pa} = 65.48 \text{ MPa}$. Step 3: Compute angle of twist $\theta = \frac{4500 \times 1.5}{80 \times 10^9 \times 2.7489 \times 10^{-6}} = 0.03068 \text{ radians} (1.758^\circ)$.
Final Answer: Maximum shear stress $\tau_{\max} = 65.48 \text{ MPa}$, Angle of twist $\theta = 0.0307 \text{ rad}$.
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Verifying that computed operating shear stress remains well below the material yield limit ensures structural airworthiness and prevents permanent torsional deformation.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary assumption regarding cross-sections of circular shafts undergoing pure torsion?
A Cross-sections warp out of plane significantly
B Cross-sections remain plane and unwarped
C Cross-sections expand radially
D Cross-sections experience bending moments
Solution & Technical Rationale:
In circular shafts under pure torsion, symmetry ensures that cross-sections remain plane and rotate rigidly about the longitudinal axis without warping.
Question 2 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding torsion of circular tubes: 1. Shear stress is maximum at the outer surface. 2. Polar moment of inertia for a hollow tube depends on the difference of fourth powers of diameters. 3. Shear stress is maximum at the neutral axis. Which of the statements given above are correct?
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2 and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Shear stress varies linearly from zero at the center to maximum at the outer surface, making statement 1 correct and statement 3 incorrect. Statement 2 is correct based on $J = \frac{\pi}{32}(d_o^4 - d_i^4)$.
Question 3 • numerical
If the polar moment of inertia $J$ of a solid circular shaft is doubled, what happens to its torsional stiffness for a fixed length?
A Halved
B Unchanged
C Doubled
D Quadrupled
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Torsional stiffness is defined as $k_t = \frac{T}{\theta} = \frac{GJ}{L}$. Since $GJ/L$ is directly proportional to $J$, doubling $J$ doubles the torsional stiffness.
Question 4 • conceptual
Why are hollow circular shafts preferred over solid circular shafts of equal cross-sectional area in aerospace applications?
A They are cheaper to manufacture
B Material is distributed further from the axis, increasing torsional resistance
C They have higher torsional stiffness per unit weight
D Both B and C
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Hollow shafts place more material at a greater radial distance from the centroid, yielding a higher polar moment of inertia per unit weight, which is vital for aerospace weight-saving.
Question 5 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Maximum shear stress in a solid circular shaft occurs at the outer boundary. Reason (R): The shear strain varies linearly with radial distance from the longitudinal axis.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Since $\gamma = r\theta/L$ and $\tau = G\gamma$, shear stress is directly proportional to radius $r$, making it maximum at the outer boundary.
Question 6 • conceptual
What happens to the angle of twist of a circular shaft if its length is doubled while keeping torque, shear modulus, and diameter constant?
A Halved
B Unchanged
C Doubled
D Quadrupled
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The formula $\theta = \frac{TL}{GJ}$ shows that the angle of twist $\theta$ is directly proportional to length $L$.
Question 7 • numerical
Calculate the polar moment of inertia $J$ for a solid circular shaft of diameter $0.1 \text{ m}$.
A $9.82 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^4$
B $7.85 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^4$
C $3.14 \times 10^{-5} \text{ m}^4$
D $1.96 \times 10^{-5} \text{ m}^4$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $J = \frac{\pi d^4}{32} = \frac{\pi (0.1)^4}{32} = \frac{3.14159 \times 10^{-4}}{32} = 9.8175 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^4$.
Question 8 • statement
Which of the following conditions must be satisfied for the validity of the standard elastic torsion formula? 1. Material is homogeneous and isotropic. 2. Cross-section is circular. 3. Deformations exceed the elastic limit. Which statements are correct?
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2 and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The torsion formula requires linear elasticity (small deformations within the elastic limit), making statement 3 false. Statements 1 and 2 are standard assumptions.
Question 9 • conceptual
In a thin-walled circular tube subjected to torsion, how is the shear flow $q$ distributed around the cross-section?
A Varies linearly from center to edge
B Varies sinusoidally
C Remains approximately constant around the circumference
D Zero at the outer wall
Solution & Technical Rationale:
In thin-walled circular tubes, shear stress and shear flow $q = \tau t$ are nearly constant across the thin wall thickness and uniform around the circumference.
Question 10 • numerical
A steel shaft transmits $10 \text{ kW}$ at $3000 \text{ RPM}$. What is the applied torque $T$?
A $31.83 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$
B $63.66 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$
C $318.31 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$
D $1000 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Power $P = T \omega$. Here $\omega = \frac{2 \pi \times 3000}{60} = 100\pi \text{ rad/s}$. Thus $T = \frac{10000}{100\pi} = \frac{100}{\pi} \approx 31.83 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.
Question 11 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Torsional failure of brittle aircraft engine shafts typically occurs along a helical 45-degree fracture plane. Reason (R): Brittle materials are weakest in tension, and pure torsion creates principal tensile stresses at 45 degrees to the shaft axis.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Pure torsion produces equal principal tensile and compressive stresses at 45-degree planes. Brittle materials fail in tension along these helical 45-degree planes.
Question 12 • conceptual
What is the effect of increasing the inner diameter of a hollow tube while keeping the outer diameter constant on its polar moment of inertia $J$?
A $J$ increases
B $J$ decreases
C $J$ remains unaffected
D $J$ becomes zero
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Since $J = \frac{\pi}{32}(d_o^4 - d_i^4)$, increasing $d_i$ decreases the value of $J$, thereby reducing torsional rigidity.

The polar moment of inertia of a solid circular shaft of diameter $d$ is given by:
A $\frac{\pi d^4}{64}$
B $\frac{\pi d^4}{32}$
C $\frac{\pi d^4}{16}$
D $\frac{\pi d^4}{128}$
Rationale:
The polar moment of inertia $J$ for a solid circular cross-section is $\frac{\pi d^4}{32}$.
Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
In the elastic torsion equation $\frac{T}{J} = \frac{\tau}{r} = \frac{G\theta}{L}$, what does $G$ represent?
A Young's Modulus
B Bulk Modulus
C Modulus of Rigidity (Shear Modulus)
D Poisson's Ratio
Rationale:
$G$ denotes the modulus of rigidity or shear modulus of the material.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
If a solid shaft is replaced by a hollow shaft of the same material, length, and outer diameter, its torsional strength will:
A Increase
B Decrease
C Remain the same
D Drop to zero
Rationale:
Removing core material decreases the polar moment of inertia $J$ for the same outer diameter, reducing total torque capacity.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the unit of torsional shear stress $\tau$ in SI units?
A $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$
B $\text{Pa}$ or $\text{N}/\text{m}^2$
C $\text{rad/s}$
D $\text{m}^4$
Rationale:
Stress has units of force per unit area, which is Pascals ($\text{Pa}$) or $\text{N}/\text{m}^2$.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
The angle of twist $\theta$ in a circular shaft is inversely proportional to:
A Applied Torque $T$
B Shaft Length $L$
C Polar Moment of Inertia $J$
D Modulus of Rigidity $G$ and Length $L$
Rationale:
From $\theta = \frac{TL}{GJ}$, $\theta$ is inversely proportional to $G$ and $J$, but among the choices, $J$ is listed as a single proportional term.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
For a hollow circular shaft with outer diameter $d_o$ and inner diameter $d_i$, the polar moment of inertia $J$ is:
A $\frac{\pi}{32}(d_o^4 + d_i^4)$
B $\frac{\pi}{32}(d_o^4 - d_i^4)$
C $\frac{\pi}{64}(d_o^4 - d_i^4)$
D $\frac{\pi}{16}(d_o^4 - d_i^4)$
Rationale:
The correct expression for a hollow circular tube is $J = \frac{\pi}{32}(d_o^4 - d_i^4)$.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
What type of failure is typically observed in a ductile circular shaft subjected to excessive torsion?
A Clean transverse flat fracture
B Helical fracture at 45 degrees
C Longitudinal splitting
D Crushing failure
Rationale:
Ductile materials fail in shear along transverse planes perpendicular to the longitudinal axis, resulting in a clean flat fracture.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
Torsional rigidity of a structural shaft is defined as:
A $\frac{GJ}{L}$
B $GJ$
C $\frac{G}{J}$
D $\frac{JL}{G}$
Rationale:
Torsional rigidity is the product of shear modulus and polar moment of inertia ($GJ$). Stiffness includes length ($GJ/L$).
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
If the diameter of a solid circular shaft is doubled, its polar moment of inertia increases by a factor of:
A 2
B 4
C 8
D 16
Rationale:
Since $J$ is proportional to $d^4$, doubling the diameter increases $J$ by $2^4 = 16$ times.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
Which DGCA regulatory document governs airworthiness standards and structural integrity testing of aircraft control systems and drive shafts?
A DGCA CAR Section 2, Airworthiness
B CAR Section 4, Aerodromes
C CAR Section 6, Personnel Licensing
D CAR Section 3, Air Transport
Rationale:
DGCA CAR Section 2 establishes airworthiness standards, structural limitations, and inspection requirements for aircraft components.
