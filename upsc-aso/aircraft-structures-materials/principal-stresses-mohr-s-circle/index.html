Mohr-circle concept

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Structural health monitoring systems on large transport aircraft utilize strain gauge rosettes whose raw voltage outputs are converted via Mohr-circle algorithms into principal stresses.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Wing root bending moment limits and fuselage pressurization hoop stress thresholds rely on exact principal stress calculations to prevent catastrophic skin wrinkling.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Airworthiness inspectors verify structural repair schemes against maximum allowable principal shear limits specified in FAA AC 43.13-1B and DGCA advisory circulars.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: During accident investigations involving structural in-flight breakup, fracture surface analysis and Mohr-circle stress reconstructions determine the primary fatigue initiation planes.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

The Mohr-circle concept is a graphical representation of the transformation of 2D and 3D plane stress tensors, mapping normal and shear stress components across arbitrary oblique planes. $\sigma_{\theta} = \frac{\sigma_x + \sigma_y}{2} + \frac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2}\cos(2\theta) + \tau_{xy}\sin(2\theta)$
  • Physical Meaning: It translates complex tensorial stress transformations into clean geometric circle coordinates $(\sigma, \tau)$.
  • Governing Law: Equilibrium of static forces on an arbitrarily oriented infinitesimal triangular wedge element under plane stress.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid only for continuous, homogeneous, isotropic linear elastic materials under small deformation assumptions.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\sigma_x$ = Normal stress along the longitudinal aircraft reference axis ($N/m^2$ or $Pa$)
  • $\sigma_y$ = Normal stress along the transverse spar or rib axis ($N/m^2$ or $Pa$)
  • $\tau_{xy}$ = Shear stress acting on the orthogonal coordinate planes ($N/m^2$ or $Pa$)
  • $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$ = Principal maximum and minimum normal stresses ($N/m^2$ or $Pa$)
  • $\tau_{\max}$ = Maximum in-plane shear stress ($N/m^2$ or $Pa$)
  • $\theta_p$ = Principal stress orientation angle ($radians$ or $degrees$)
  • $R$ = Radius of the Mohr circle representing maximum shear stress magnitude ($N/m^2$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Infinitotimal Element Isolation: A differential structural skin element of aircraft fuselage is isolated, identifying acting direct and shear stresses on orthogonal faces.
2. Oblique Plane Equilibrium: Equilibrium equations are resolved along inclined cutting planes, yielding parametric equations of an ellipse that simplify to a circle.
3. Cartesian Mapping: Normal stress $\sigma$ is mapped along the horizontal abscissa and shear stress $\tau$ along the vertical ordinate.
4. Geometric Locus Generation: As the plane orientation angle $\theta$ varies from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$, the stress state sweeps out a complete circular locus of radius $R$.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Resolve forces on an inclined structural wedge of area $dA$ and orientation angle $\theta$.

$$\sigma_{\theta} = \sigma_x \cos^2\theta + \sigma_y \sin^2\theta + 2\tau_{xy}\sin\theta\cos\theta$$
Step 2: Trigonometric Identity Substitution

Apply double-angle trigonometric identities to separate direct and shear components.

$$\sigma_{\theta} = \frac{\sigma_x + \sigma_y}{2} + \frac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2}\cos(2\theta) + \tau_{xy}\sin(2\theta)$$
Step 3: Shear Stress Transformation

Derive the corresponding transformation equation for shear stress on the inclined plane.

$$\tau_{\theta} = -\frac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2}\sin(2\theta) + \tau_{xy}\cos(2\theta)$$
Step 4: Circle Equation Formulation

Square and add transformed stress equations to eliminate angle $\theta$ and obtain the circle equation.

$$\left( \sigma_{\theta} - \frac{\sigma_x + \sigma_y}{2} \right)^2 + \tau_{\theta}^2 = \left( \frac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2} \right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: $[M^1 L^{-1} T^{-2}]$ derived from force divided by area ($Force/Area$).
  • SI Unit: Pascal ($Pa$) or Newton per square meter ($N/m^2$), with MegaPascals ($MPa$) commonly used in aerospace engineering.
  • CGS Unit: Barye or dyne per square centimeter ($dyn/cm^2$), converted via $10\; dyn/cm^2 = 1\; Pa$.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Pounds per square inch ($psi$), kips per square inch ($ksi$), where $1\; ksi = 6.895\; MPa$.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'L', Part III and ICAO Annex 8 mandate strict structural fatigue and static strength margins where principal stress trajectories dictate inspection intervals.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Mohr Circle Angle vs Physical Angle

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming the angle $\theta$ on the physical element equals the angle on the Mohr circle.

✅ UPSC Truth: Angles on the Mohr circle are doubled ($2\theta$). A physical rotation of $30^\circ$ corresponds to a $60^\circ$ rotation on the Mohr circle.

Trap 2: Sign Convention for Shear Stress

❌ Examiner Trap: Treating clockwise and counter-clockwise shear stresses with identical directional signs on the circle.

✅ UPSC Truth: Positive shear stress ($\tau_{xy}$) that tends to rotate the element clockwise is plotted downwards, while counter-clockwise shear is plotted upwards.

Trap 3: Maximum Shear Stress Plane Confusion

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing maximum shear stress occurs on planes experiencing zero normal stress.

✅ UPSC Truth: Maximum shear stress occurs on planes inclined at $45^\circ$ to principal planes, and these planes generally carry non-zero average normal stress.

Trap 4: Three-Dimensional Mohr Circle Envelope

❌ Examiner Trap: Applying 2D Mohr circle logic directly when out-of-plane principal stress is the absolute maximum.

✅ UPSC Truth: True maximum shear stress in 3D is given by $\tau_{\max} = \frac{\sigma_{max} - \sigma_{min}}{2}$, requiring three interlocking circles when all three principal stresses are non-zero.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An aircraft fuselage skin panel is subjected to a tensile longitudinal stress of $120\; MPa$, a compressive transverse hoop stress of $40\; MPa$, and a clockwise shear stress of $50\; MPa$. Calculate the principal stresses and the maximum in-plane shear stress.

Given Flight Parameters:
$\sigma_x = 120\; MPa$, $\sigma_y = -40\; MPa$, $\tau_{xy} = 50\; MPa$ (clockwise)
Governing Formula:
$$\sigma_{1,2} = \frac{\sigma_x + \sigma_y}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left( \frac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2} \right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2}, \quad \tau_{\max} = \sqrt{\left( \frac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2} \right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute center of the Mohr circle $C = \frac{120 + (-40)}{2} = 40\; MPa$. Step 2: Compute radius $R = \sqrt{\left(\frac{120 - (-40)}{2}\right)^2 + 50^2} = \sqrt{80^2 + 50^2} = \sqrt{6400 + 2500} = \sqrt{8900} = 94.34\; MPa$. Step 3: Determine principal stresses $\sigma_1 = C + R = 40 + 94.34 = 134.34\; MPa$ and $\sigma_2 = C - R = 40 - 94.34 = -54.34\; MPa$, with max in-plane shear $\tau_{\max} = 94.34\; MPa$.
Final Answer: $\sigma_1 = 134.34\; MPa$, $\sigma_2 = -54.34\; MPa$, and $\tau_{\max} = 94.34\; MPa$.
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate principal stress calculation prevents unexpected shear yielding in thin-walled monocoque aircraft skins during high-g load factor maneuvers.

Question 1 • conceptual
What does the radius of the Mohr circle physically represent in plane stress analysis?
A Maximum normal stress
B Maximum in-plane shear stress
C Average normal stress
D Minimum principal stress
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The radius of the Mohr circle represents the maximum in-plane shear stress magnitude experienced by the structural element.
Question 2 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding Mohr's circle: 1. The center of the circle is located at $(\sigma_x + \sigma_y)/2$. 2. Principal planes are free from shear stress. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. The center lies at the average normal stress coordinate, and by definition, principal planes have zero shear stress.
Question 3 • numerical
If $\sigma_x = 100\; MPa$ and $\sigma_y = 40\; MPa$ with zero shear stress, what is the maximum in-plane shear stress?
A $70\; MPa$
B $30\; MPa$
C $50\; MPa$
D $20\; MPa$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Radius $R = (100 - 40)/2 = 30\; MPa$. Since shear stress is zero, radius equals the maximum in-plane shear stress.
Question 4 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Mohr's circle can be constructed for strain tensors identically to stress tensors. Reason (R): Strain components $\epsilon_x$, $\epsilon_y$, and $\gamma_{xy}/2$ obey transformation equations identical in form to plane stress equations.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both assertion and reason are correct because plane strain transformation equations are mathematically identical to plane stress transformation equations.
Question 5 • conceptual
What is the angle between principal planes and maximum shear stress planes on the Mohr circle?
A $45^\circ$
B $90^\circ$
C $180^\circ$
D $360^\circ$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
On the Mohr circle, principal planes (on the $\sigma$-axis) and maximum shear stress planes (at top/bottom peaks) are separated by $90^\circ$, corresponding to $45^\circ$ on the physical aircraft element.
Question 6 • numerical
Given principal stresses $\sigma_1 = 200\; MPa$ and $\sigma_2 = -50\; MPa$, what is the center coordinate of the Mohr circle?
A $125\; MPa$
B $75\; MPa$
C $100\; MPa$
D $150\; MPa$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Center $C = (\sigma_1 + \sigma_2)/2 = (200 + (-50))/2 = 150/2 = 75\; MPa$.
Question 7 • statement
Which of the following conditions yields a Mohr circle that collapses to a single point? 1. Hydrostatic pressure state. 2. Pure uniaxial tension with zero transverse stress. Which is correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Hydrostatic pressure ($\sigma_x = \sigma_y = \sigma_z$ and $\tau = 0$) results in a zero radius circle (a single point). Uniaxial tension has a non-zero radius.
Question 8 • conceptual
Why are shear stresses on orthogonal planes perpendicular to each other equal in magnitude?
A Due to plane strain assumption
B To satisfy static rotational equilibrium (moment equilibrium $\tau_{xy} = \tau_{yx}$)
C Because of Hooke's Law
D Due to principal stress invariance
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Complementary shear stresses $\tau_{xy}$ and $\tau_{yx}$ must be equal in magnitude to maintain moment equilibrium about the center of an infinitesimal element.
Question 9 • numerical
If the radius of a Mohr circle is $60\; MPa$ and the center is at $40\; MPa$, what is the value of the maximum principal stress?
A $20\; MPa$
B $60\; MPa$
C $100\; MPa$
D $120\; MPa$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Maximum principal stress $\sigma_1 = C + R = 40 + 60 = 100\; MPa$.
Question 10 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): The algebraic sum of normal stresses acting on any two orthogonal planes in plane stress is invariant. Reason (R): The sum $\sigma_x + \sigma_y$ equals $2C$, which corresponds to the trace of the 2D stress tensor.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The sum of normal stresses on orthogonal planes is invariant and equal to the first invariant of the stress tensor, explaining why $2C = \sigma_x + \sigma_y$.
Question 11 • conceptual
In aircraft structural design, why is maximum shear stress theory (Tresca criterion) often used alongside von Mises yield criteria?
A Tresca is easier to compute graphically using Mohr's circle
B Tresca accounts for fatigue better
C Von Mises only applies to fluids
D Mohr circles cannot represent von Mises stress
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Tresca's yield criterion directly relates maximum shear stress (the radius of the largest Mohr circle) to material shear yield strength, making graphical assessment intuitive.
Question 12 • numerical
A structural member experiences $\sigma_x = 80\; MPa$, $\sigma_y = 20\; MPa$, and $\tau_{xy} = 40\; MPa$. What is the radius of the Mohr circle?
A $30\; MPa$
B $40\; MPa$
C $50\; MPa$
D $60\; MPa$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Radius $R = \sqrt{((80 - 20)/2)^2 + 40^2} = \sqrt{30^2 + 40^2} = \sqrt{900 + 1600} = \sqrt{2500} = 50\; MPa$.

Rationale:
Since shear stress is zero, the reference planes are already principal planes, so $\theta_p = 0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$.
Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the maximum absolute shear stress for a 3D stress state where principal stresses are $\sigma_1 = 300\; MPa$, $\sigma_2 = 100\; MPa$, and $\sigma_3 = -50\; MPa$?
A $125\; MPa$
B $175\; MPa$
C $350\; MPa$
D $200\; MPa$
Rationale:
Absolute maximum shear stress in 3D is $(\sigma_1 - \sigma_3)/2 = (300 - (-50))/2 = 350/2 = 175\; MPa$.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
In a Mohr's circle construction, if point X representing the x-face is located at $(\sigma_x, \tau_{xy})$ and point Y on the y-face is at $(\sigma_y, -{xy})$, what is the angle subtended between X and Y on the circle?
A $90^\circ$
B $180^\circ$
C $270^\circ$
D $360^\circ$
Rationale:
Orthogonal physical planes ($90^\circ$ apart) are separated by $180^\circ$ ($2\theta$) on the Mohr circle.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
An aircraft skin panel has $\sigma_x = -80\; MPa$ (compression) and $\sigma_y = -20\; MPa$ (compression) with $\tau_{xy} = 40\; MPa$. What is the center of the Mohr circle?
A $-50\; MPa$
B $50\; MPa$
C $-30\; MPa$
D $30\; MPa$
Rationale:
Center $C = ((-80) + (-20))/2 = -100/2 = -50\; MPa$.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
Which failure criterion assumes yielding begins when the maximum shear stress reaches a critical threshold?
A Von Mises Criterion
B Tresca Criterion
C Rankine Criterion
D Coulomb-Mohr Criterion
Rationale:
The Tresca (Maximum Shear Stress) criterion posits that material yield initiates when $\tau_{\max} = \sigma_{ys}/2$.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the maximum shear stress value if the Mohr circle degenerates into a single point?
A Maximum principal stress
B Zero
C Half of the normal stress
D Infinite
Rationale:
A circle degenerating into a point has zero radius, meaning zero shear stress on all planes.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
If the maximum principal stress is $200\; MPa$ and minimum principal stress is $-100\; MPa$, what is the radius of the Mohr circle?
A $100\; MPa$
B $150\; MPa$
C $200\; MPa$
D $300\; MPa$
Rationale:
Radius $R = (\sigma_1 - \sigma_2)/2 = (200 - (-100))/2 = 300/2 = 150\; MPa$.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
In aircraft wing box structure design, principal stress analysis helps airworthiness engineers identify:
A Fuel burn rates
B Critical buckling and shear tear lines
C Cabin pressurization leaks
D Engine compressor stall margins
Rationale:
Principal stress and Mohr circle analysis identify critical shear and tensile trajectories prone to buckling or fatigue tearing.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the slope of the line connecting the center of the Mohr circle to a point representing an inclined plane at angle $\theta$?
A $\theta$
B $2\theta$
C $90 - \theta$
D $45^\circ$
Rationale:
The geometric angle on the Mohr circle corresponding to an inclined plane at physical angle $\theta$ is $2\theta$.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
For pure shear stress state where $\sigma_x = 0$, $\sigma_y = 0$, and $\tau_{xy} = \tau_0$, where is the center of the Mohr circle located?
A At the origin $(\sigma = 0, \tau = 0)$
B At $\sigma = \tau_0$
C At $\sigma = 2\tau_0$
D At $\tau = \tau_0$
Rationale:
Center $C = (0 + 0)/2 = 0$, placing the circle center directly at the origin of the coordinate system.
