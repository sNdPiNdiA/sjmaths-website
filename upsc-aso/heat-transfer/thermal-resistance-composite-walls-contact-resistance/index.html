Contact resistance

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Avionics bay cooling racks and FADEC housing heat sinks rely on minimized contact resistance to prevent thermal runaway.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: High thermal contact resistance in turbine blade thermal barrier coatings or turbine casing joints causes localized overheating and premature structural failure.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 mandate strict structural and thermal integrity audits for primary load-bearing and engine-mounted thermal joints.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: ASR investigators inspect joint interfacial corrosion, fretting, and thermal degradation during root-cause analysis of uncontained engine failures or electrical fires.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Thermal contact resistance ($R_{tc}$) is the temperature drop across the interface of two contacting solid bodies divided by the heat transfer rate: $$R_{tc} = \frac{T_1 - T_2}{Q} = \frac{1}{h_{tc}}$$
  • Physical Meaning: Surface roughness creates microscopic voids filled with air or gas, drastically increasing thermal impedance across bolted or joined aero-structures.
  • Governing Law: Fourier's Law of heat conduction modified across an interface layer: $$Q = \frac{T_1 - T_2}{R_{tc} + \sum (L/kA)}$$
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid when mean free path of interstitial gas is much smaller than the interface gap height, ensuring Fourier's conduction holds locally.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $R_{tc}$ = Thermal contact resistance ($K/W$)
  • $h_{tc}$ = Thermal contact conductance ($W/(m^2\cdot K)$)
  • $T_1, T_2$ = Interface surface temperatures ($K$)
  • $Q$ = Rate of heat transfer ($W$)
  • $k_{air}$ = Thermal conductivity of interstitial gas ($W/(m\cdot K)$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Microscopic Asperities: Machined engineering surfaces appear smooth but feature microscopic peaks and valleys called asperities that touch at discrete contact spots.
2. Interstitial Trapped Gas: The voids between contacting asperities contain stagnant air or trapped gases acting as thermal insulators with very low thermal conductivity.
3. Constriction and Spreading Resistance: Heat flow lines must constrict to pass through microscopic solid-to-solid contact points, multiplying local thermal resistance.
4. Interfacial Radiative and Convective Transfer: Radiation across micro-cavities and micro-convection contribute marginally, while thermal greases or foils eliminate air gaps to lower resistance.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Define total interface heat flux via apparent contact area.

$$q'' = \frac{Q}{A_{app}} = h_{tc}(T_{s1} - T_{s2})$$
Step 2: Component Resistance Formulation

Deconstruct $h_{tc}$ into solid conduction ($h_{sp}$) and gas gap conduction ($h_{gas}$ plus radiation $h_{rad}$).

$$h_{tc} = h_{sp} + h_{gas} + h_{rad}$$
Step 3: Mikic Correlation Integration

Relate solid conductance to micro-hardness ($H_c$) and effective slope of asperities ($m$).

$$h_{sp} = 1.25 \cdot k_{mean} \cdot \left(\frac{P}{H_c}\right)^{0.95} \left(\frac{m}{\sigma}\right)$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Express total joint thermal resistance per unit area.

$$R''_{tc} = \frac{1}{1.25 k_{mean} (P/H_c)^{0.95} (m/\sigma) + (k_{gas}/t_v)}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Thermal contact resistance ($R_{tc}$) has dimensions $[M^{-1} L^2 T^3 \theta]$. Specific contact resistance ($R''_{tc}$) has dimensions $[M^{-1} L^4 T^3 \theta]$.
  • SI Unit: $K/W$ for total resistance, and $m^2\cdot K/W$ for area-normalized contact resistance ($R''_{tc}$).
  • CGS Unit: $cm^2\cdot \degree C/W$ ($1 \, m^2\cdot K/W = 10^4 \, cm^2\cdot \degree C/W$).
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Fahrenheit-hour-square foot per British thermal unit ($ft^2\cdot \hr\cdot \degree F / BTU$). Conversion: $1 \, m^2\cdot K/W = 5.678 \, ft^2\cdot \hr\cdot \degree F / BTU$.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2 (Airworthiness) and ICAO Annex 14 dictate maintenance schedules for engine cowlings and thermal barrier interfaces.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Severe contact resistance in bleed-air duct flanges leads to unmonitored thermal expansion, duct rupture, and critical wing anti-ice system failure.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: British Airways Flight 38 engine roll-back incident highlighted fuel-oil heat exchanger thermal equilibrium failures linked to fouled boundary interfaces.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Contact Resistance Independence

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming thermal contact resistance is constant for a given pair of metal plates regardless of tightening torque.

✅ UPSC Truth: Contact resistance decreases non-linearly as contact pressure (bolt torque) increases because higher pressure deforms asperities and enlarges actual contact area.

Trap 2: Vacuum Interface Behaviour

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming interstitial air has negligible effect on joint thermal resistance in high-altitude flight.

✅ UPSC Truth: At high altitude or space vacuum, $k_{gas}$ drops to zero, dramatically increasing total contact resistance unless conductive paste or gap pads are used.

Trap 3: Surface Roughness Fallacy

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing that polishing contacting aircraft skins to mirror-smooth finishes always lowers thermal contact resistance.

✅ UPSC Truth: Extremely smooth surfaces lack macro-cavities for thermal grease entrapment and can exhibit increased flatness deviation, sometimes raising net thermal resistance.

Trap 4: Dimension and Unit Confusion

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing thermal contact conductance ($h_{tc}$) units with overall heat transfer coefficient units.

✅ UPSC Truth: Both use $W/(m^2\cdot K)$, but $h_{tc}$ specifically characterizes dry or greased solid-solid interfaces, excluding external fluid boundary layer resistance.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

Two 20 mm thick Aluminium alloy plates ($k = 180 \, W/m\cdot K$) with a cross-sectional area of $0.01 \, m^2$ are pressed together with a contact pressure yielding a thermal contact conductance $h_{tc} = 5000 \, W/m^2\cdot K$. If a steady heat flux of $25,000 \, W/m^2$ passes through the joint, calculate the total temperature drop across the interface.

Given Flight Parameters:
Plate thickness $L = 0.02 \, m$; Conductivity $k = 180 \, W/m\cdot K$; Area $A = 0.01 \, m^2$; Contact conductance $h_{tc} = 5000 \, W/m^2\cdot K$; Heat flux $q'' = 25,000 \, W/m^2$.
Governing Formula:
$$\Delta T_{interface} = \frac{q''}{h_{tc}}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Identify the heat flux passing through the interface: $q'' = 25,000 \, W/m^2$. Step 2: Use the thermal contact conductance relation: $\Delta T_{interface} = \frac{q''}{h_{tc}}$. Step 3: Substitute the numerical values: $\Delta T_{interface} = \frac{25000}{5000} = 5.0 \, K$.
Final Answer: $5.0 K (or \degree C)$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Even a minor interface imperfection can introduce discrete temperature drops that trigger thermal stresses in critical high-load aircraft structural joints.

Question 1 • conceptual
Thermal contact resistance across two joined metallic plates is primarily caused by:
A Molecular diffusion across the crystal lattice
B Microscopic surface asperities and trapped interstitial gas pockets
C Radiation losses from external edges
D High bulk thermal conductivity of the constituent metals
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Surface roughness creates micro-scale gaps filled with low-conductivity gas, restricting heat flow to discrete contact spots.
Question 2 • conceptual
How does increasing the bolt tightening torque across an aircraft structural joint affect thermal contact resistance?
A Increases contact resistance linearly
B Has zero effect on contact resistance
C Decreases contact resistance by increasing solid-to-solid contact area
D Causes infinite thermal impedance
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Higher pressure deforms asperities, increasing actual contact area and reducing overall interface thermal resistance.
Question 3 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding thermal contact resistance: 1. It decreases at higher contact pressures. 2. Thermal grease eliminates interstitial air gaps and increases contact conductance. 3. It is completely independent of the interstitial fluid medium. Which of the statements given above are correct?
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2 and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 3 is incorrect because contact resistance is heavily dependent on the thermal conductivity of the interstitial fluid.
Question 4 • numerical
If the thermal contact conductance between two titanium plates is $4000 \, W/m^2\cdot K$ and the interface heat flux is $20,000 \, W/m^2$, what is the interface temperature drop?
A 2.5 K
B 5.0 K
C 8.0 K
D 10.0 K
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $\Delta T = q'' / h_{tc} = 20000 / 4000 = 5.0 \, K$.
Question 5 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Thermal contact resistance becomes critical in high-altitude aerospace applications. Reason (R): Decreasing ambient pressure at altitude evacuates interstitial air gaps, lowering effective gap thermal conductivity.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Vacuum conditions eliminate gas conduction across micro-gaps, worsening thermal contact resistance unless thermal interface materials are used.
Question 6 • conceptual
What is the SI unit of thermal contact conductance ($h_{tc}$)?
A $W/m\cdot K$
B $W/m^2\cdot K$
C $m^2\cdot K/W$
D $J/kg\cdot K$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Thermal contact conductance has units of $W/(m^2\cdot K)$, representing heat flux per unit temperature difference.
Question 7 • numerical
Calculate the area-normalized thermal contact resistance ($R''_{tc}$) if the contact conductance is $2500 \, W/m^2\cdot K$.
A $0.0004 \, m^2\cdot K/W$
B $0.0025 \, m^2\cdot K/W$
C $2.5 \, m^2\cdot K/W$
D $400 \, m^2\cdot K/W$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
$R''_{tc} = 1 / h_{tc} = 1 / 2500 = 0.0004 \, m^2\cdot K/W$.
Question 8 • conceptual
Which material property primarily dictates the solid spot conductance ($h_{sp}$) in metallic interfaces under load?
A Dynamic viscosity
B Micro-hardness and thermal conductivity of the bulk metals
C Specific heat capacity at constant pressure
D Kinematic viscosity
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Mikic correlation shows solid conductance depends on harmonic mean thermal conductivity and micro-hardness under pressure.
Question 9 • statement
Consider the following about aerospace thermal joint design: 1. Thermal interface materials (TIMs) are applied to reduce contact resistance. 2. Bolted joints in turbine casings require high contact resistance to protect exterior structures. Which statement is correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 2 is false; engineers always try to minimize contact resistance in joints to prevent localized overheating and thermal stress.

Question 11 • numerical
A composite wall consists of two metal plates with an interface resistance of $0.002 \, m^2\cdot K/W$. If the heat flux is $10,000 \, W/m^2$, what is the temperature drop across this interface?

A Thermal contact conductance

By definition, $R_{tc} = 1 / h_{tc}$, making contact resistance inversely proportional to thermal contact conductance.

If contact pressure between two aluminium plates is doubled, the thermal contact resistance will:
A Double
B Remain unchanged
C Decrease
D Increase exponentially
Rationale:
Increased contact pressure flattens micro-asperities, increasing real contact area and lowering contact resistance.

A Biot number
B Asperity slope to roughness height ratio ($m/\sigma$)
C Nusselt number
D Prandtl number

Mikic correlation incorporates the effective slope-to-roughness ratio ($m/\sigma$) to evaluate solid constriction resistance.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
In an aircraft turbine engine casing joint, high thermal contact resistance leads to:
A Uniform thermal expansion
B Localized hot spots and thermal distortion
C Enhanced cooling efficiency
D Decreased thermal stress
Rationale:
Impeded heat flow across joints causes localized high temperature gradients and thermal buckling.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
The unit of area-normalized thermal contact resistance ($R''_{tc}$) is:
A $W/m^2\cdot K$
B $m^2\cdot K/W$
C $K/W$
D $m/W$
Rationale:
Area-normalized resistance is $R''_{tc} = R_{tc} \cdot A$, yielding $m^2\cdot K/W$.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
Which of the following methods is least effective at reducing thermal contact resistance in a dry joint?
A Increasing bolt torque
B Lapping the surfaces for optimal flatness
C Applying high-conductivity thermal paste
D Applying a coat of high-emissivity paint on exterior edges
Rationale:
Exterior paint has no effect on microscopic solid contact or gap conduction within the joint interface.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
In a cryogenic aircraft fuel tank joint, helium gas is sometimes introduced in interface gaps because:
A Helium has an extremely high thermal conductivity compared to air
B Helium freezes and acts as a solid bridge
C Helium reduces contact pressure
D Helium is heavier than air
Rationale:
Helium has high thermal conductivity, significantly increasing gap conductance ($h_{gas}$) in cryogenic insulation.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
What happens to the gas gap contribution ($h_{gas}$) to thermal contact conductance as atmospheric pressure approaches zero?
A Increases linearly
B Approaches zero
C Remains constant
D Becomes infinite
Rationale:
In a vacuum, no gas molecules are present to conduct heat, reducing $h_{gas}$ to zero.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
A DGCA airworthiness inspector checks an aircraft heat exchanger flange and notes heavy corrosion. How does this impact $R_{tc}$?
A Decreases $R_{tc}$ by sealing gaps
B Has no effect on heat transfer
C Increases $R_{tc}$ due to low-conductivity oxide layers
D Converts the interface into a superconductor
Rationale:
Oxide and corrosion layers have poor thermal conductivity, sharply increasing contact resistance and causing overheating.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
The temperature profile across a solid-solid interface with thermal contact resistance exhibits:
A Continuous smooth slope
B A distinct temperature jump (discontinuity)
C Zero temperature gradient
D Negative temperature values
Rationale:
Thermal contact resistance creates a sharp temperature drop $\Delta T$ right across the interface plane.
