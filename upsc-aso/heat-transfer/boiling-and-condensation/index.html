Boiling & condensation basics

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Fuel boost pump cavitation and oil temperature gauges rely on accurate boiling point margin calculations to prevent engine fuel starvation.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Vapor lock vulnerability during hot-weather high-altitude dispatches directly restricts allowable fuel grades and operational flight levels.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 Series 'F' and ICAO Annex 8 mandate strict fuel system thermal qualification to prevent boiling and vapor lock.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers investigate engine flameouts and fuel system malfunctions by auditing fuel volatility, heat exchanger clogging, and boiling thresholds.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Boiling and condensation represent fundamental phase-change convective heat transfer mechanisms governed by latent heat exchange, expressed via Newton's law of cooling: $$q'' = h(T_s - T_f)$$
  • Physical Meaning: Boiling involves liquid-to-vapor phase transition at a heated solid boundary, whereas condensation represents vapor-to-liquid transformation over a cooled surface.
  • Governing Law: Heat transfer rates are controlled by the temperature potential, interfacial surface tension, and latent heat of vaporization ($h_{fg}$).
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid down to the micro-scale where molecular interfacial resistance and Knudsen number effects become non-negligible.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $q''$ = Heat flux per unit area ($W/m^2$)
  • $h$ = Convective heat transfer coefficient ($W/(m^2\cdot K)$
  • $T_s$ = Surface temperature ($K$)
  • $T_{sat}$ = Saturation temperature ($K$)
  • $h_{fg}$ = Latent heat of vaporization ($J/kg$)
  • $\sigma$ = Surface tension coefficient ($N/m$)
  • $\rho_l, \rho_v$ = Liquid and vapor densities ($kg/m^3$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Molecules near the phase interface acquire sufficient thermal kinetic energy to overcome intermolecular cohesive forces, resulting in vapor bubble nucleation or liquid film formation.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Buoyancy forces, surface tension gradients (Marangoni effect), and momentum boundary layers dictate vapor bubble detachment or liquid condensate drainage rates.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: In high-speed aerospace thermal systems, nucleate boiling prevents catastrophic surface burnout, while dropwise condensation maximizes heat exchanger efficiency in environmental control systems (ECS).

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Energy balance across a laminar condensate film.

$$\frac{d}{dx}\left( k_l \frac{dT}{dy} \right) = 0$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Velocity and temperature conditions at the solid wall and liquid-vapor interface.

$$T(0) = T_s, \quad T(\delta) = T_{sat}, \quad \left.\frac{du}{dy}\right|_{y=\delta} = 0$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Integrating momentum and energy equations to yield film thickness profile.

$$\delta(x) = \left[ \frac{4 k_l \mu_l (T_{sat} - T_s) x}{g ( \rho_l - \rho_v ) \rho_l h_{fg}} \right]^{1/4}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Nusselt's analytical expression for mean local film condensation heat transfer coefficient.

$$h_x = 0.943 \left[ \frac{g \rho_l ( \rho_l - \rho_v ) k_l^3 h_{fg}}{\mu_l (T_{sat} - T_s) x} \right]^{1/4}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Heat transfer coefficient $h$ has dimensions $[M L^0 T^{-3} \theta^{-1}]$. Latent heat $h_{fg}$ has dimensions $[L^2 T^{-2}]$.
  • SI Unit: Heat transfer coefficient is expressed in $W/(m^2\cdot K)$ or kg/(s^3\cdot K). Latent heat is expressed in $J/kg$ ($m^2/s^2$).
  • CGS Unit: $erg/(cm^2\cdot s\cdot^\circ C)$ for heat transfer coefficients; conversion factor requires multiplication by $10^{-3}$ for SI kW equivalents.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: BTU/(hr\cdot ft^2\cdot^\circ F) for heat flux; conversion factor $1 \text{ BTU}/(\text{hr}\cdot\text{ft}^2\cdot^\circ\text{F}) = 5.678 \text{ W}/(\text{m}^2\cdot\text{K})$.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Compliance with DGCA CAR Section 2, Airworthiness Requirements, and ICAO Annex 8 mandates that aircraft fuel and environmental control systems prevent phase-change anomalies across the entire flight envelope.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Uncontrolled boiling in fuel lines causes vapor lock and cavitates fuel boost pumps, leading to immediate engine flameout.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: Commercial transport aircraft experienced dual-engine rollback during high-altitude climb due to fuel vaporization caused by booster pump heating and low fuel vapor-to-liquid ratios.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Dropwise vs Film Condensation

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming film condensation yields higher heat transfer coefficients than dropwise condensation.

✅ UPSC Truth: Dropwise condensation yields heat transfer coefficients up to 10 times higher because the bare surface is intermittently exposed without an insulating liquid film barrier.

Trap 2: Boiling Curve Regimes

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming heat flux increases monotonically with excess temperature across the entire boiling curve.

✅ UPSC Truth: Beyond critical heat flux (CHF), transition boiling occurs where a vapor blanket forms, sharply decreasing heat transfer and causing surface burnout.

Trap 3: Latent Heat Independence

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing latent heat of vaporization remains constant at varying flight altitudes and pressures.

✅ UPSC Truth: Latent heat varies significantly with saturation pressure; it increases at lower pressures encountered during high-altitude flight.

Trap 4: Condensate Film Shear Stress

❌ Examiner Trap: Neglecting vapor shear stress on thick condensate films in high-velocity aerospace heat exchangers.

✅ UPSC Truth: Vapor drag accelerates condensate drainage, significantly modifying local Nusselt numbers in high-speed turbine cooling ducts.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

Calculate the local film condensation heat transfer coefficient for saturated water vapor condensing at 100 C on a vertical flat plate of height 0.5 m, maintained at a uniform surface temperature of 90 C.

Given Flight Parameters:
Plate height $L = 0.5\text{ m}$, $T_{sat} = 373.15\text{ K}$, $T_s = 363.15\text{ K}$, $k_l = 0.68\text{ W}/(\text{m}\cdot\text{K})$, $\mu_l = 3.14\times 10^{-4}\text{ kg}/(\text{m}\cdot\text{s})$, $\rho_l = 958.4\text{ kg}/\text{m}^3$, $\rho_v = 0.6\text{ kg}/\text{m}^3$, $h_{fg} = 2257\times 10^3\text{ J}/\text{kg}$, $g = 9.81\text{ m}/\text{s}^2$.
Governing Formula:
$$h_L = 0.943 \left[ \frac{g \rho_l ( \rho_l - \rho_v ) k_l^3 h_{fg}}{\mu_l (T_{sat} - T_s) L} \right]^{1/4}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute property term numerator $g \rho_l ( \rho_l - \rho_v ) k_l^3 h_{fg} = 9.81 \times 958.4 \times (958.4 - 0.6) \times (0.68)^3 \times 2257000 = 3.864 \times 10^{10}$. Step 2: Compute denominator terms $\mu_l \Delta T L = (3.14 \times 10^{-4}) \times 10 \times 0.5 = 1.57 \times 10^{-3}$. Step 3: Divide and take the 4th root, then multiply by 0.943 to yield $h_L = 9524.3 \text{ W}/(\text{m}^2\cdot\text{K})$.
Final Answer: $9524.3 W/(m^2 K)$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

High condensation heat transfer coefficients are essential for sizing aircraft environmental control condensers, ensuring reliable cabin temperature and moisture regulation.

Question 1 • conceptual
Which mode of condensation provides a significantly higher heat transfer coefficient on a clean metallic surface?
A Film condensation
B Dropwise condensation
C Homogeneous condensation
D Flash condensation
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Dropwise condensation yields much higher heat transfer coefficients because droplets form and shed rapidly, leaving bare metal exposed for direct vapor contact without an insulating liquid film barrier.
Question 2 • conceptual
What is the primary physical phenomenon responsible for limiting maximum heat flux in nucleate pool boiling?
A Marangoni convection
B Critical Heat Flux (CHF) or burnout point
C Laminar boundary layer detachment
D Vapor condensation collapse
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Critical Heat Flux (CHF) marks the transition where bubbles coalesce into an insulating vapor blanket, drastically reducing heat transfer and causing surface temperature spikes.
Question 3 • conceptual
In aircraft fuel systems, what operational hazard is directly caused by localized fluid boiling at elevated flight altitudes?
A Hydraulic lock
B Vapor lock and boost pump cavitation
C Thermal shock
D Condensate icing
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Boiling in aviation fuel lines at low ambient pressure generates vapor pockets that cause vapor lock, starving the engine of fuel and cavitating boost pumps.
Question 4 • conceptual
How does the latent heat of vaporization ($h_{fg}$) behave as saturation pressure decreases with altitude?
A It decreases linearly
B It increases
C It remains constant
D It drops to zero immediately
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Thermodynamically, latent heat of vaporization increases as saturation pressure and temperature decrease towards the triple point.
Question 5 • conceptual
Which dimensionless parameter group characterizes film condensation heat transfer on vertical surfaces according to Nusselt's theory?
A Prandtl and Reynolds numbers
B Nusselt number as a function of Galileo and liquid property ratios
C Biot and Fourier numbers
D Grashof and Mach numbers
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Nusselt's film condensation analysis expresses mean heat transfer coefficient in terms of fluid properties, gravity, latent heat, and film temperature differential.
Question 6 • conceptual
What is the primary function of promoter additives in industrial condenser heat exchangers?
A To convert film condensation into dropwise condensation
B To suppress nucleate boiling
C To increase fluid dynamic viscosity
D To eliminate vapor shear stress
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Promoters make surfaces non-wetting, ensuring dropwise condensation which greatly enhances heat transfer rates.
Question 7 • conceptual
Which boiling regime is characterized by periodic bubble formation and detachment from discrete active nucleation sites?
A Film boiling
B Nucleate boiling
C Transition boiling
D Convective subcooled boiling
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Nucleate boiling features localized bubble growth and detachment, providing extremely high heat transfer coefficients at moderate excess temperatures.
Question 8 • conceptual
In the context of aircraft air conditioning packs, moisture condensation is modeled primarily as:
A Transient film condensation over cold turbine duct walls
B Adiabatic flash boiling
C Nucleate pool boiling
D Radiation-dominated phase change
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Air conditioning packs remove moisture via film condensation over expansion turbine heat exchanger surfaces.
Question 9 • conceptual
What happens to the convective heat transfer coefficient during film condensation as the vertical plate height increases?
A It increases linearly with height
B It decreases as $x^{-1/4}$ due to thickening condensate film
C It remains entirely independent of height
D It drops instantaneously to zero
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Nusselt's equation shows $h_x \propto x^{-1/4}$ because the cumulative film thickness grows down the plate, increasing thermal resistance.
Question 10 • conceptual
Which factor most heavily influences the boiling point of aviation turbine fuel (Jet A-1) inside aircraft fuel tanks?
A Ambient barometric pressure and altitude
B Aircraft Mach number only
C Wing dihedral angle
D Rudder deflection frequency
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Boiling point is governed by vapor-liquid equilibrium thermodynamics, where ambient static pressure determines saturation temperature.
Question 11 • conceptual
Assertion (A): Dropwise condensation is difficult to maintain continuously in industrial service without chemical promoters. Reason (R): Condensate films naturally wet untreated metal surfaces over time.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Surfaces tend to flood and form films over time as promoters wash away, making dropwise condensation hard to sustain without continuous chemical injection.
Question 12 • conceptual
Which region of the boiling curve exhibits negative slope where surface temperature increases while heat flux decreases?
A Nucleate boiling region
B Transition boiling region
C Natural convection region
D Stable film boiling region
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Transition boiling is an unstable regime characterized by alternating liquid-solid contact and vapor blanket shielding, yielding a negative $dq''/dT$ slope.

Question 1 of 10 +3 / -1
For laminar film condensation on a vertical plate, if the temperature difference $(T_{sat} - T_s)$ is doubled, the local heat transfer coefficient $h_x$ changes by a factor of:
A $2^{1/4}$
B $2^{-1/4}$
C $0.5$

Rationale:
Nusselt's equation dictates $h_x \propto \Delta T^{-1/4}$, so doubling $\Delta T$ scales $h_x$ by $2^{-1/4}$.

In aircraft fuel management, vapor lock is prevented primarily by:
A Maintaining fuel tank pressure above vapor pressure using boost pumps
B Lowering fuel flash point
C Increasing cabin altitude
D Adding anti-icing additives (DiEGME)
Rationale:
Boost pumps maintain positive pressurization in fuel feed lines to keep fuel in liquid phase above its boiling threshold.

A Jakob number
B Nusselt number
C Reynolds number of film
D All of the above
Rationale:
Film condensation analysis utilizes Jakob number (sensible-to-latent energy ratio), Nusselt number, and film Reynolds number.

A Leidenfrost Point

Critical Heat Flux represents the operational ceiling for nucleate boiling before thermal runaway and burnout.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
The Leidenfrost phenomenon is observed when:
A Liquid drops onto a surface heated far above the saturation temperature, forming an insulating vapor cushion
B Vapor condenses instantly in supersonic intake ducts
C Fuel freezes at high altitude
D Oil viscosity drops to zero
Rationale:
The Leidenfrost effect creates a vapor barrier that cushions liquid droplets, drastically reducing evaporation rates.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
In aviation gas turbine oil coolers, phase change is normally avoided because:
A Lubricating oil breakdown and coking occurs at boiling thresholds
B Oil density becomes infinite
C Viscosity increases exponentially
D Pressure spikes destroy pumps
Rationale:
Boiling or thermal degradation of synthetic engine oil leads to coking, bearing coking, and catastrophic mechanical failure.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
According to Nusselt's theory, the average heat transfer coefficient $\bar{h}$ for a vertical plate of height $L$ is how many times the local coefficient at $x = L$?
A Equal
B $1.333$ ($4/3$ times)
C $0.75$
D $2.0$
Rationale:
Integrating local $h_x \propto x^{-1/4}$ over height $L$ yields $\bar{h} = \frac{4}{3} h(L)$.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1

A To mandate inerting systems and minimum pressure margins against vaporization
B To regulate ticket pricing
C To specify pilot oxygen masks
D To set runway lighting standards
Rationale:
DGCA and ICAO regulations require rigorous fuel system safety margins to prevent vapor generation and explosion hazards.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
When non-condensable gases are present in a vapor stream undergoing condensation, the heat transfer coefficient:
A Increases drastically
B Decreases due to mass transfer resistance of the gas blanket
C Remains completely unaffected
D Becomes infinite
Rationale:
Non-condensable gases accumulate near the liquid-vapor interface, acting as a diffusion barrier that degrades heat transfer.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the primary reason dropwise condensation is difficult to sustain in commercial aircraft environmental systems?
A Surface promoters wash away or degrade over operational cycles
B High cabin pressure forces film formation

Promoters that maintain dropwise condensation erode due to moisture and contaminant exposure over time.
