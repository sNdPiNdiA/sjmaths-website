Inverse Laplace transform – partial fractions

Day 2 of 129 Engineering Fundamentals Phase 1: Foundations 60-90 min Master Cycle

1. Understand (5–10 min)

In Aeronautical Engineering and Flight Control System Design, dynamic responses of aircraft, dynamic sensors, actuators, and structural modes are predominantly characterized in the complex frequency domain (s-domain) through rational transfer functions $F(s) = P(s)Q(s)$. The Inverse Laplace Transform via Partial Fraction Decomposition serves as the fundamental analytical bridge connecting linear differential system descriptions to time-domain transient and steady-state responses $f(t) = \mathcal{L}-1\{F(s)\}$.

Mathematically, the inverse Laplace transform is formally defined by Mellin's Inverse Formula (Bromwich Contour Integral):

$$ f(t) = \mathcal{L}-1\{F(s)\} = 12π j ∫γ - j∞γ + j∞ F(s) est \, ds, \quad for t > 0 $$

where the path of integration lies strictly within the Region of Convergence ($Re(s) > γ$). For algebraic transfer functions encountered in aeronautical stability analysis, Partial Fraction Decomposition transforms a high-order, complex algebraic fraction $F(s)$ into a sum of elementary linear and quadratic basis functions whose inverse transforms are known in closed form.

Vector Space and Functional Framework: The set of strictly proper rational functions $\mathbb{R}(s)$ forms a vector space over $\mathbb{R}$. Partial fraction decomposition is an isomorphism mapping $\mathbb{R}(s)$ onto a direct sum of orthogonal algebraic subspaces spanned by simple-pole basis elements $\left\{1s-p_k\right\}$, repeated-pole elements $\left\{1(s-p_k)^m\right\}$, and irreducible second-order quadratic modes $\left\{Bs+C(s-α)^2 + β^2\right\}$.

UPSC Standard Terminology and Classifications:

  • Proper Rational Function: $\deg(P(s)) < \deg(Q(s))$. The time response contains purely bounded exponential and harmonic continuous modes.
  • Improper Rational Function: $\deg(P(s)) ≥ \deg(Q(s))$. Requires polynomial division before decomposition. Yields singular terms such as the Dirac delta function $\delta(t)$ and its higher-order derivatives $\delta(n)(t)$.
  • Simple Real Poles: Distinct roots of $Q(s) = 0$. Represents decoupled non-oscillatory exponential dynamic modes (e.g., aircraft spiral mode, roll subsidence).
  • Repeated Real Poles: Multiplicity $m > 1$. Produces secular terms $t^k ept$, representing critically damped dynamic system responses.
  • Complex Conjugate Poles: Irreducible quadratic factors $(s-α)^2 + β^2$. Represents underdamped oscillatory modes (e.g., Phugoid, Short-Period pitching oscillation, Dutch Roll, wing flutter).

2. Learn the Concept (15–20 min)

To perform exhaustive, mathematically rigorous partial fraction expansion and compute $f(t) = \mathcal{L}-1\{F(s)\}$, we evaluate four canonical algebraic cases, alongside the treatment of improper functions.

1. Case I: Distinct Real Poles

If $Q(s) = (s-p_1)(s-p_2)·s(s-p_n)$ with all $p_i$ real and distinct:

$$ F(s) = P(s)Q(s) = ∑k=1n A_ks - p_k $$

The residue coefficients $A_k$ are determined using Heaviside's Cover-Up Method or Cauchy's Residue Formula:

$$ A_k = \lims \to p_k (s - p_k) F(s) = \left. P(s)Q'(s) |s = p_k $$

Taking the inverse Laplace transform yields:

$$ f(t) = ∑k=1n A_k ep_k t u(t) $$

2. Case II: Repeated Real Poles

If $Q(s)$ contains a factor $(s - p)^m$ of multiplicity $m$:

$$ F(s) = P(s)(s-p)^m R(s) = ∑k=1m A_k(s - p)^k + terms for other poles $$

The coefficients $A_k$ (for $k = 1, 2, \dots, m$) are determined via successive differentiation:

$$ A_k = 1(m-k)! \lims \to p dm-kdsm-k [ (s - p)^m F(s) ] $$

Using the standard pair $\mathcal{L}-1\left\{1(s-p)^k\right\} = tk-1(k-1)! ept$, the inverse transform is:

$$ f(t) = ( ∑k=1m A_k tk-1(k-1)! ) ept + \dots $$
$$ A_k = 1(m-k)! \left. dm-kdsm-k [ (s - p)^m F(s) ] |s = p, \quad \mathcal{L}-1\left\{ 1(s-p)^k \right\} = tk-1(k-1)! ept $$

3. Case III: Irreducible Quadratic Factors (Complex Conjugate Poles)

When $Q(s)$ contains non-repeated quadratic factors $s^2 + bs + c = (s-α)^2 + β^2$ where $α = -b/2$ and $β = √4c - b^22$:

$$ F(s) = Bs + C(s-α)^2 + β^2 + \dots $$

Completing the square in the numerator gives:

$$ Bs + C(s-α)^2 + β^2 = B s - α(s-α)^2 + β^2 + (C + Bαβ) β(s-α)^2 + β^2 $$

Applying First Shifting Theorem ($\mathcal{L}-1\{G(s-a)\} = eatg(t)$):

$$ f(t) = B eα t \cos(β t) + (C + Bαβ) eα t \sin(β t) $$

4. Case IV: Improper Rational Functions ($\deg(P) ≥ \deg(Q)$)

If $\deg(P) = \deg(Q) + m$ ($m ≥ 0$), polynomial long division yields:

$$ F(s) = C_m s^m + Cm-1 sm-1 + \dots + C_1 s + C_0 + R(s)Q(s) $$

where $\deg(R) < \deg(Q)$. Using $\mathcal{L}-1\{s^n\} = \delta(n)(t)$ (the $n$-th derivative of Dirac delta function):

$$ f(t) = C_m \delta(m)(t) + Cm-1 \delta(m-1)(t) + \dots + C_0 \delta(t) + \mathcal{L}-1\left\{ R(s)Q(s) \right\} $$

3. Memorize Important Facts (10 min)

  • Heaviside Cover-Up Formula (Simple Pole): $A_k = \left. (s - p_k) F(s) |s = p_k = P(p_k)Q'(p_k)$
  • Repeated Pole Formula: $A_k = 1(m-k)! \left. dm-kdsm-k [ (s - p)^m F(s) ] |s = p$
  • Simple Pole Transform: $\mathcal{L}-1\left\{1s - a\right\} = eat u(t)$
  • Repeated Pole Transform: $\mathcal{L}-1\left\{1(s - a)^n\right\} = tn-1(n-1)! eat u(t)$
  • Damped Cosine Transform: $\mathcal{L}-1\left\{s - α(s - α)^2 + β^2\right\} = eα t \cos(β t) u(t)$
  • Damped Sine Transform: $\mathcal{L}-1\left\{β(s - α)^2 + β^2\right\} = eα t \sin(β t) u(t)$
  • Repeated Quadratic Pair: $\mathcal{L}-1\left\{1(s^2 + ω^2)^2\right\} = 12ω^3 (\sin(ω t) - ω t \cos(ω t))$
  • Polynomial Term (Impulse Basis): $\mathcal{L}-1\{s^n\} = \delta(n)(t)$ where $\delta(n)$ is the $n$-th derivative of the Dirac delta distribution.

4. Aviation Application (5–10 min)

In Aeronautical Engineering, partial fraction evaluation of inverse Laplace transforms is indispensable for analyzing aircraft dynamic stability, response to elevator/rudder inputs, and aeroelastic interactions.

1. Longitudinal Dynamics: Phugoid and Short-Period Modes

The longitudinal characteristic equation of a rigid aircraft derived from linearized Navier-Stokes/Euler flight dynamic equations is a 4th-order polynomial:

$$ Δ(s) = s^4 + A s^3 + B s^2 + C s + D = (s^2 + 2\zetaspωsps + ωsp^2)(s^2 + 2\zetaphωphs + ωph^2) = 0 $$

When calculating the pitch rate response $q(t) = \mathcal{L}-1\left\{ Nq\delta_e(s)Δ(s) Δ \delta_e(s) \right\}$ due to a step elevator deflection $Δ \delta_e(s) = \delta_0s$, partial fraction expansion decomposes $q(s)$ into:

$$ q(s) = R_0s + B_1 s + C_1s^2 + 2\zetaspωsps + ωsp^2 + B_2 s + C_2s^2 + 2\zetaphωphs + ωph^2 $$

The corresponding time response explicitly isolates the rapid short-period oscillation (frequency $ωsp\sqrt{1-\zetasp^2}$, heavy damping $\zetasp$) from the slow phugoid trajectory interchange (frequency $ωph\sqrt{1-\zetaph^2}$, light damping $\zetaph$):

$$ q(t) = R_0 + e-\zetaspωspt[ X_1 \cos(ωd,spt) + Y_1 \sin(ωd,spt) ] + e-\zetaphωpht[ X_2 \cos(ωd,pht) + Y_2 \sin(ωd,pht) ] $$

2. Aeroelasticity & Wing Flutter Analysis

In wing flutter analysis, coupling between bending $h(t)$ and torsional $α(t)$ modes yields complex poles $s = σ ± jω$. Partial fraction decomposition of the flutter determinant response directly indicates system stability:

  • If $σ < 0$: Standard stable damped oscillation $eσ t \sin(ω t)$.
  • If $σ = 0$: Undamped harmonic oscillations (Flutter Boundary / Divergence Speed). The partial fraction term $Bs+Cs^2 + ω^2$ produces sustained sinusoidal vibrations.
  • If $σ > 0$: Exponentially growing divergent oscillations (Catastrophic Flutter).

5. UPSC ASO Focus (10 min)

  • Frequently Asked Facts:
    • For a simple pole at $s = p_k$, the coefficient $A_k = P(p_k)Q'(p_k)$ avoids evaluating long products in high-degree polynomials.
    • Improper rational functions ($\deg(P) ≥ \deg(Q)$) MUST generate Dirac delta terms $\delta(t), \delta'(t)$, which represent ideal impulse inputs or immediate derivative jumps.
    • If $F(s) = F^*(s^*)$, complex conjugate poles $s = α ± jβ$ yield complex conjugate residues $A$ and $A^*$. The combined time domain response is $2 |A| eα t \cos(β t + \angle A)$.
  • Conceptual Traps:
    • Trap 1: Forgetting the factorial factor $1(m-k)!$ in the derivative formula for repeated poles.
    • Trap 2: Direct application of Heaviside cover-up method on repeated or complex poles without proper differentiation or grouping, leading to wrong constant values.
    • Trap 3: Incorrectly completing the square for $s^2 + bs + c$. Remember: $(s + b/2)^2 + (c - b^2/4)$. If $c - b^2/4 < 0$, the roots are distinct real, NOT complex conjugate!
    • Trap 4: Ignoring polynomial long division when $\deg(P) = \deg(Q)$, resulting in incorrect constant residues.

Express $F(s) = As+1 + Bs+2 + Cs+4$.
Using Heaviside Cover-Up Method:
$$ A = \left. s+3(s+2)(s+4) |s=-1 = -1+3(-1+2)(-1+4) = 2(1)(3) = 23 $$ $$ B = \left. s+3(s+1)(s+4) |s=-2 = -2+3(-2+1)(-2+4) = 1(-1)(2) = -12 $$ $$ C = \left. s+3(s+1)(s+2) |s=-4 = -4+3(-4+1)(-4+2) = -1(-3)(-2) = -16 $$ Taking inverse Laplace transform: $$ f(t) = 23e-t - 12e-2t - 16e-4t $$

Q9. Assertion (A): The inverse Laplace transform of $F(s) = 2s + 3s+1$ contains an impulse term $2\delta(t)$.
Reason (R): $F(s)$ is an improper rational function where the degree of the numerator equals the degree of the denominator.
A) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
B) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A).
C) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
D) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
Step-by-Step Solution:
Divide numerator by denominator: $F(s) = 2(s+1) + 1s+1 = 2 + 1s+1$.
Inverting: $f(t) = 2\delta(t) + e-t$.
The impulse term $2\delta(t)$ arises directly because $F(s)$ is improper with equal degrees ($\deg(P) = \deg(Q) = 1$), producing a constant quotient term of 2. Thus (R) is the true and correct explanation for (A).

3. Damped sinusoidal terms $e-t(B_1 \cos t + B_2 \sin t)$
4. An ideal Dirac derivative term $\delta'(t)$
A) 1, 2, and 3 only
B) 1 and 3 only
C) 2 and 4 only
D) 1, 2, 3, and 4
Exhaustive Dynamic Analysis:
Examine the degree of numerator $\deg(P) = 2$ and denominator $\deg(Q) = 1 + 2 + 2 = 5$. Since $\deg(P) < \deg(Q)$, $G(s)$ is strictly proper. No delta functions $\delta(t)$ or higher derivatives exist ($Item 4 is FALSE$).
Poles breakdown:
- Simple pole at $s = 0 \implies A_0s \to A_0 u(t)$ (Item 1 is TRUE).
- Repeated pole at $s = -1$ of multiplicity 2 $\implies A_1(s+1)^2 + A_2s+1 \to (A_1 t + A_2)e-t$ (Item 2 is TRUE).

Partial Fractions for Inverse Laplace
 ├── Strictly Proper (deg P < deg Q)
 │    ├── Distinct Real Poles: A/(s-p) ──> A*exp(pt)
 │    ├── Repeated Real Poles: A_k/(s-p)^k ──> A_k * t^(k-1)/(k-1)! * exp(pt)
 │    ├── Complex Conjugate Poles: (Bs+C)/((s-a)^2 + b^2) ──> exp(at)[B*cos(bt) + ((C+B*a)/b)*sin(bt)]
 │    └── Repeated Complex Poles: 1/(s^2+w^2)^2 ──> (1/(2*w^3))*(sin(wt) - wt*cos(wt))
 └── Improper (deg P >= deg Q)
      └── Long Division: F(s) = Poly(s) + R(s)/Q(s)
           ├── Constant Term C_0 ──> C_0 * delta(t)
           ├── Term C_1 * s ──> C_1 * delta'(t)
           └── Proper Remainder R(s)/Q(s) ──> Standard Partial Fractions
