Strouhal-number concept

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Pitot-static probes, angle-of-attack vanes, and vortex-shedding flowmeters rely on Strouhal number constancy to measure airspeed and fuel flow rates accurately.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Prevents resonant aeroelastic coupling (flutter) on control surfaces, elevator tabs, and engine fan blades during high-speed cruise dispatch.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Governed by DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 Airworthiness standards concerning vibration monitoring and structural fatigue testing.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers evaluate vortex shedding and Strouhal resonance during accident root-cause analysis involving unexpected structural failure or sensor telemetry anomalies.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

The Strouhal number ($St$) is a dimensionless parameter describing oscillating flow mechanisms, defined as $St = \frac{f L}{U_{\infty}}$, where $f$ is shedding frequency, $L$ is characteristic length, and $U_{\infty}$ is flow velocity.
  • Physical Meaning: It characterizes unsteady flow phenomena, specifically vortex shedding frequencies behind bluff and streamlined bodies in external aerodynamics.
  • Governing Law: It scales unsteadiness against convection, dictating dynamic similarity in fluid-structure interactions and aeroelastic flutter.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid exclusively within the continuum mechanics domain where Knudsen number ($Kn < 0.01$) ensures fluid property validity.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $St$ = Strouhal number (Dimensionless)
  • $f$ = Vortex shedding frequency ($Hz$ or $s^{-1}$)
  • $L$ = Characteristic length, e.g., cylinder diameter or airfoil chord ($m$)
  • $U_{\infty}$ = Free-stream flow velocity ($m/s$)
  • $f_{v}$ = Structural natural frequency ($Hz$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Fluid molecules collide continuously while transferring momentum, establishing a macroscopic velocity field around aircraft control surfaces and structural pylons.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Adverse pressure gradients and viscous shear layers cause boundary layer separation, rolling up the separated shear layers into discrete vortices.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Periodic vortex shedding creates fluctuating lift and drag forces, inducing structural fatigue, acoustic resonance, and potentially catastrophic flutter in flight.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Dimensional analysis of unsteady Navier-Stokes equations isolates frequency scaling.

$$St = \frac{\text{Characteristic Unsteady Time Scale}}{\text{Convective Time Scale}} = \frac{1 / f}{L / U_{\infty}}$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Incompressible flow assumption applies for Mach numbers below $M = 0.3$, keeping density constant.

$$\nabla \cdot \vec{u} = 0, \quad \rho = \text{constant}$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Substitute frequency $f$, characteristic dimension $L$, and free-stream velocity $U_{\infty}$ into the dimensionless grouping.

$$St = \frac{f \cdot L}{U_{\infty}}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

The resulting Strouhal number remains remarkably constant over specific Reynolds number ranges for bluff bodies.

$$St \approx 0.2 \quad (40 < Re < 10^5 ext{ for circular cylinders})$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: $[M^0 L^0 T^0]$. Frequency is $[T^{-1}]$, length is $[L]$, and velocity is $[LT^{-1}]$, yielding $[T^{-1} L (LT^{-1})^{-1}] = 1$.
  • SI Unit: Dimensionless scalar quantity with no primary physical units.
  • CGS Unit: Dimensionless scalar quantity identical across all coordinate systems.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Dimensionless ratio invariant across English engineering, imperial, and metric flight planning units.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Compliance with DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'I', Part V and ICAO Annex 8 mandates rigorous evaluation of aeroelastic stability and flutter margins.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Vortex shedding directly impacts external pitot probes, engine bleed air ducting, antenna fairings, and trailing-edge control surface actuators.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: In vibrational fatigue incidents, unexpected structural resonance occurs when vortex shedding frequency matches the natural structural frequency ($St \cdot U_{\infty} / L = f_v$).

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Strouhal Number Dependence on Reynolds Number

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming the Strouhal number varies wildly with velocity across all flow regimes.

✅ UPSC Truth: For bluff bodies in the subcritical Reynolds number regime ($10^3 < Re < 10^5$), $St$ remains nearly constant around $0.21$.

Trap 2: Strouhal vs. Reynolds vs. Mach Distinction

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing Strouhal number with Reynolds number or Mach number in dimensional analysis.

✅ UPSC Truth: Strouhal number strictly characterizes unsteady, periodic flow unsteadiness, whereas Reynolds and Mach quantify viscous and compressibility effects respectively.

Trap 3: Dimensional Homogeneity Error

❌ Examiner Trap: Treating $St$ as having units of Hertz or meters per second during calculations.

✅ UPSC Truth: $St$ is strictly dimensionless ($[M^0 L^0 T^0]$), serving as a scaling ratio rather than a physical dimensional property.

Trap 4: Flow Regime Applicability

❌ Examiner Trap: Applying standard $St = 0.2$ bluff-body values directly to highly streamlined laminar airfoils.

✅ UPSC Truth: Streamlined bodies do not exhibit regular von Kármán vortex streets, making classical Strouhal shedding approximations invalid without boundary layer separation.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An aircraft pitot-static probe with a cylindrical stem diameter of $0.04 \text{ m}$ is exposed to a true airspeed of $120 \text{ m/s}$ at ISA sea level. Calculate the vortex shedding frequency if the Strouhal number is $0.21$.

Given Flight Parameters:
Diameter ($L$) = $0.04 \text{ m}$; Velocity ($U_{\infty}$) = $120 \text{ m/s}$; Strouhal number ($St$) = $0.21$
Governing Formula:
$$St = \frac{f \cdot L}{U_{\infty}} \implies f = \frac{St \cdot U_{\infty}}{L}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
1. Identify governing formula: $f = \frac{St \cdot U_{\infty}}{L}$. \n 2. Substitute numerical values: $f = \frac{0.21 \cdot 120 \text{ m/s}}{0.04 \text{ m}}$. \n 3. Compute final frequency: $f = \frac{25.2}{0.04} = 630 \text{ Hz}$.
Final Answer: $630 Hz$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

A vortex shedding frequency of $630 \text{ Hz}$ near structural components requires verification that internal structural natural frequencies do not coincide, preventing fatigue failure.

Question 1 • conceptual
What does the Strouhal number primarily characterize in fluid mechanics?
A Compressibility effects in supersonic flow
B Unsteady, oscillating flow and vortex shedding frequency
C Viscous shear forces relative to inertial forces
D Heat transfer coefficients in boundary layers
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The Strouhal number ($St = fL/U$) is a dimensionless parameter specifically used to characterize oscillating flow phenomena and vortex shedding.
Question 2 • conceptual
What are the fundamental dimensions of the Strouhal number?
A $[ML^{-1}T^{-1}]$
B $[LT^{-1}]$
C $[M^0 L^0 T^0]$ (Dimensionless)
D $[T^{-1}]$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Because frequency ($T^{-1}$) multiplied by length ($L$) and divided by velocity ($LT^{-1}$) cancels out all dimensions, $St$ is strictly dimensionless.
Question 3 • statement-based
Consider the following statements regarding the Strouhal number: 1. It is dimensionless. 2. For bluff bodies in the subcritical Reynolds number regime, $St$ is approximately $0.2$. 3. It depends directly on fluid dynamic viscosity. Which of the statements given above are correct?
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2 and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is false because $St$ is primarily a kinematic scaling parameter for frequency rather than a direct function of viscosity.
Question 4 • numerical
A cylindrical antenna of diameter $0.02 \text{ m}$ experiences a flow velocity of $80 \text{ m/s}$. If the Strouhal number is $0.20$, what is the vortex shedding frequency?
A $400 \text{ Hz}$
B $800 \text{ Hz}$
C $1600 \text{ Hz}$
D $320 \text{ Hz}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $f = \frac{St \cdot U}{L} = \frac{0.20 \cdot 80}{0.02} = \frac{16}{0.02} = 800 \text{ Hz}$.
Question 5 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): The Strouhal number remains relatively constant across a wide range of Reynolds numbers for bluff bodies. Reason (R): Vortex shedding frequency is directly proportional to free-stream velocity in the subcritical regime.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Because $f$ is proportional to $U$, the ratio $St = fL/U$ remains nearly constant ($~0.2$) across the subcritical Reynolds number range.
Question 6 • conceptual
Why is the Strouhal number critical in aircraft design and airworthiness?
A It determines maximum fuel tank capacity
B It helps prevent aeroelastic resonance and structural fatigue from vortex shedding
C It calculates cabin pressurization outflow valve rates
D It dictates optimal engine oil viscosity
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Matching vortex shedding frequency to structural natural frequencies causes resonance and fatigue; $St$ analysis prevents this.
Question 7 • numerical
If a vortex shedding frequency of $500 \text{ Hz}$ is measured behind a strut of characteristic width $0.05 \text{ m}$ in a flow of $125 \text{ m/s}$, what is the Strouhal number?
A $0.15$
B $0.20$
C $0.25$
D $0.30$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
$St = \frac{f \cdot L}{U} = \frac{500 \cdot 0.05}{125} = \frac{25}{125} = 0.20$.
Question 8 • statement-based
Which of the following flow parameters are required to calculate the Strouhal number?
A Frequency, characteristic length, and velocity
B Density, dynamic viscosity, and temperature
C Pressure, volume, and mass flow rate
D Mach number, angle of attack, and chord length
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The formula $St = fL/U_{\infty}$ explicitly requires shedding frequency ($f$), characteristic length ($L$), and velocity ($U_{\infty}$).
Question 9 • conceptual
What phenomenon is directly associated with von Kármán vortex shedding governed by the Strouhal number?
A Wave drag at transonic speeds
B Alternating low-pressure vortex cores resulting in transverse forces
C Cavitation in fuel boost pumps
D Thermal boundary layer separation
Solution & Technical Rationale:
von Kármán vortex streets shed alternating vortices that create periodic transverse pressure fluctuations on structures.
Question 10 • numerical
A flow velocity of $200 \text{ m/s}$ produces a vortex shedding frequency of $400 \text{ Hz}$ across a cylinder. If the Strouhal number is $0.20$, what is the cylinder diameter ($L$)?
A $0.05 \text{ m}$
B $0.10 \text{ m}$
C $0.20 \text{ m}$
D $0.40 \text{ m}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
$L = \frac{St \cdot U}{f} = \frac{0.20 \cdot 200}{400} = \frac{40}{400} = 0.10 \text{ m}$.
Question 11 • conceptual
Under what flow condition does the classical Strouhal number concept break down or become less applicable?
A Incompressible low-speed laminar flow
B Highly streamlined bodies with fully attached boundary layers
C Subcritical cylinder flow
D Steady wind tunnel testing
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Streamlined bodies do not experience regular, periodic vortex shedding, rendering classical bluff-body Strouhal scaling inapplicable.
