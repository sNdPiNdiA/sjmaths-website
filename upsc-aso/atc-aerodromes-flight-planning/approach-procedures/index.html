Approach procedures

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

An approach procedure is a series of predetermined manoeuvres by reference to flight instruments with specified protection from obstacles from the initial approach fix, or where applicable, from the beginning of a defined arrival route to a point from which a landing can be completed and thereafter, if a landing is not completed, to a holding or en-route place. ($ \text{Obstacle Clearance Altitude} \ge \text{Minimum Sector Altitude} $)
  • Physical Meaning: It creates a structured, obstacle-cleared vertical and horizontal flight path transitioning high-altitude en-route flight to terminal landing.
  • Governing Law: Governed by ICAO Doc 8168 (PANS-OPS Volume II) and DGCA CAR Section 8 defining terminal instrument flight rules.
  • Continuum Boundary: Bridges high-speed cruise airframe configurations down to low-speed, high-lift landing configurations within terminal airspace limits.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $IAF$ = Initial Approach Fix, where the instrument approach procedure begins (Dimensionless)
  • $FAF$ = Final Approach Fix, where the final approach segment commences (Dimensionless)
  • $MAPt$ = Missed Approach Point, point where missed approach procedure begins ($m$)
  • $OCA/H$ = Obstacle Clearance Altitude / Height ($m$ or $ft$)
  • $DA/H$ = Decision Altitude / Height for precision approaches ($ft$)
  • $MDA/H$ = Minimum Descent Altitude / Height for non-precision approaches ($ft$)

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Descent angle relationship with groundspeed and vertical speed.

$$\text{ROD} = \text{GS} \times \tan(\theta) \times 101.27$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Nominal glide slope angle standard.

$$\theta = 3.0^\circ$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Applying standard conversion factor for knots to feet per minute.

$$\text{ROD}_{\text{fpm}} \approx 5 \times \text{GS}_{\text{kts}}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Final required rate of descent calculation.

$$\text{ROD} = \text{Groundspeed} \times 5$$

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: MDA vs DA Confusion

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming Decision Altitude (DA) and Minimum Descent Altitude (MDA) can be used interchangeably for any approach type.

✅ UPSC Truth: DA is strictly for precision and APV approaches with vertical guidance, whereas MDA is used for non-precision approaches where level flight at MDA is permitted until the MAPt.

Trap 2: Missed Approach Point Timing

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing a missed approach must be initiated immediately upon reaching the MDA regardless of distance or time.

✅ UPSC Truth: On non-precision approaches without DME, the missed approach is initiated at the Missed Approach Point (MAPt) determined by timing from the FAF.

Trap 3: Obstacle Clearance Margins

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming the 1,000 ft clearance margin applies equally throughout the final approach segment.

✅ UPSC Truth: The final approach segment reduces required obstacle clearance down to 246 ft (75 m) or lower for precision approaches.

Trap 4: Transition to Visual Flight

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming crossing the DA/MDA automatically permits landing even without visual reference.

✅ UPSC Truth: Landing is prohibited unless distinct visual reference to the runway environment is established and maintained.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

Calculate the required rate of descent (ROD) for an aircraft executing a standard 3.0-degree ILS glide slope approach at a constant groundspeed of 140 knots.

Given Flight Parameters:
Groundspeed (GS) = 140 kts, Glide slope angle (\theta) = 3.0^\circ
Governing Formula:
$$\text{ROD} = \text{GS} \times \tan(3.0^\circ) \times 101.27$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Convert groundspeed knots to feet per minute conversion constant ($1 \text{ knot} = 101.27 \text{ ft/min}$). Step 2: Calculate tangent of 3 degrees: $\tan(3.0^\circ) \approx 0.0524$. Step 3: Multiply groundspeed by angle tangent and conversion factor: $140 \times 0.0524 \times 101.27 = 743.3 \text{ ft/min}$.
Final Answer: $743 ft/min$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Monitoring correct rate of descent against groundspeed ensures the aircraft stays within the obstacle clearance protection envelope during low-visibility operations.

Question 1 • conceptual
Which of the following segments is NOT a standard component of an Instrument Approach Procedure?
A Initial approach segment
B Intermediate approach segment
C Supersonic cruise segment
D Final approach segment
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Standard instrument approach procedures consist of arrival, initial, intermediate, final, and missed approach segments. Supersonic cruise is never part of an approach procedure.
Question 2 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding Decision Altitude (DA): 1. DA is referenced to mean sea level. 2. DA is used exclusively in non-precision approach procedures. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is correct as DA is referenced to MSL. Statement 2 is incorrect because DA is used for precision approaches (PA), while MDA is used for non-precision approaches (NPA).
Question 3 • numerical
Calculate the approximate rate of descent required for a 3-degree glide slope at a groundspeed of 120 knots using the quick estimation rule ($5 \times \text{GS}$).
A 500 ft/min
B 600 ft/min
C 700 ft/min
D 800 ft/min
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using the 5-times rule: $5 \times 120 = 600 \text{ ft/min}$.
Question 4 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): A missed approach procedure must be initiated if visual references are lost below the DA/DH. Reason (R): Continuing below DA/DH without visual contact violates safety margins and risks obstacle collision.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both assertion and reason are true, and the reason correctly explains why the missed approach is mandatory when visual reference is lost.
Question 5 • conceptual
What is the primary purpose of the Intermediate Approach Segment?
A To decelerate and configure the aircraft for the final approach
B To hold the aircraft clear of thunderstorms
C To establish supersonic flight
D To execute a 360-degree holding pattern
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The intermediate segment aligns the aircraft with the final approach course and allows deceleration and configuration changes.
Question 6 • statement
Which of the following defines the Missed Approach Point (MAPt) in a non-precision approach? 1. Arrival at a specified DME distance. 2. Completion of timing from the Final Approach Fix. 3. Interception of the localizer back beam. Select the correct answer:
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2 and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
MAPt in an NPA is defined by timing from FAF, DME distance, or a navigational fix. Localizer back beam interception is not a standard MAPt definition.
Question 7 • numerical
An aircraft crosses the Final Approach Fix at 3,000 feet MSL and must cross the runway threshold at 50 feet MSL over a distance of 10 nautical miles. What is the average vertical descent gradient?
A 200 ft/NM
B 250 ft/NM
C 295 ft/NM
D 318 ft/NM
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Descent = $3000 - 50 = 2950 \text{ ft}$. Distance = $10 \text{ NM}$. Gradient = $2950 / 10 = 295 \text{ ft/NM}$.
Question 8 • conceptual
Under ICAO PANS-OPS, what is the standard obstacle clearance provided in the final approach segment for a precision approach?
A 1,000 feet
B 500 feet
C Obstacle clearance height (OCH) based on precise surfaces
D Zero feet
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Precision approaches utilize collision risk models and OCH surfaces rather than a flat vertical buffer like 1000 ft.
Question 9 • statement
Consider the following about RNAV (GPS) approach procedures: 1. They provide LNAV and LNAV/VNAV minima. 2. They require ground-based VOR stations for primary lateral guidance. Which statement is correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
RNAV approaches rely on satellite navigation and onboard databases, not ground-based VOR stations for primary guidance.
Question 10 • conceptual
What does 'Stabilized Approach' criteria dictate regarding aircraft configuration at 1,000 feet AFE in IMC?
A Aircraft must be fully configured for landing with stable speed and sink rate
B Aircraft may remain in clean configuration until 500 feet
C Engines may be kept at idle thrust
D Autopilot must be disconnected
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Stabilized approach criteria require the aircraft to be on profile, stable speed, landing configuration, and stabilized sink rate by 1,000 ft IMC.
Question 11 • numerical
If an aircraft maintains a groundspeed of 150 knots on a 3-degree glide slope, what is the exact rate of descent?
A 750 ft/min
B 797 ft/min
C 850 ft/min
D 900 ft/min
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Calculation: $150 \times \tan(3.0^\circ) \times 101.27 = 150 \times 0.052407 \times 101.27 \approx 796.5 \text{ ft/min}$.
Question 12 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Circling approaches have higher minimums than straight-in approaches. Reason (R): Circling maneuvers expose the aircraft to lower obstacle clearance and increased manoeuvring risk close to terrain.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both assertion and reason are true, explaining why circling minimums are higher.
