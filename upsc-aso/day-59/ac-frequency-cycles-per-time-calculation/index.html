AC frequency - cycles per time calculation

Day 59 of 129 Aircraft Systems and Avionics Phase 3: Systems and Applied 60-90 min Master Cycle
Step 1: Understand AC Frequency Basics
  • Alternating Current (AC) Frequency: Represents the number of complete cycles of alternating voltage or current completed per unit time (typically one second), measured in Hertz (Hz). One Hertz equals one cycle per second.
  • Generation Principle: In an aircraft alternator (synchronous generator), AC frequency is directly proportional to the rotational speed of the rotor (RPM) and the number of magnetic poles built into the rotor assembly.
  • The Aviation Standard (400 Hz): Unlike terrestrial power grids operating at 50 Hz or 60 Hz, aerospace applications standardly utilize 400 Hz AC. This high frequency drastically minimizes the size and weight of magnetic cores in transformers, motors, and generators, which is critical for aircraft efficiency.
Step 2: Learn Formulas and Key Parameters

To calculate the frequency, speed, or pole count of an alternator, use the fundamental synchronous frequency equation:

$f = \frac{N \times P}{120}$

Where:

  • $f$ = Electrical Frequency in Hertz (Hz) or cycles per second.
  • $N$ = Rotational Speed of the rotor in Revolutions Per Minute (RPM).
  • $P$ = Total number of magnetic poles (always an even integer).
  • $120$ = Constant factor converting minutes to seconds ($60$) and poles to pole pairs ($2$).

If rotational speed is given in Revolutions Per Second (RPS, denoted as $n$):

$f = \frac{n \times P}{2} = n \times p$

Where $p$ is the number of pole pairs ($p = P/2$).

Step 3: Memorize Key Constants & Shortcuts
  • Standard Aircraft Generator Speeds:
    • For a 4-pole generator to output 400 Hz: Speed must be exactly 12,000 RPM.
    • For a 6-pole generator to output 400 Hz: Speed must be exactly 8,000 RPM.
    • For an 8-pole generator to output 400 Hz: Speed must be exactly 6,000 RPM.
  • Time Period ($T$): The period of a 400 Hz wave is $T = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{400} = 2.5\text{ milliseconds}$.

Step 4: Aviation Applications

1. Constant Speed Drive (CSD) & IDG

Since aircraft jet engines operate at variable RPMs (from idle to takeoff thrust), direct-drive generators would produce variable frequencies. To maintain a strict 400 Hz output, a Constant Speed Drive (CSD)—a hydromechanical transmission—is placed between the engine accessory gearbox and the generator. Modern aircraft combine these into a single unit called the Integrated Drive Generator (IDG), which mechanically regulates the input speed to a constant 12,000 RPM (for 4-pole alternators).

2. "Wild" Frequency AC Systems

In newer-generation aircraft like the Boeing 787 and Airbus A350, heavy CSDs are eliminated to save weight. Instead, they use Variable Frequency Starter Generators (VFSG). The output frequency varies directly with engine speed (typically ranging from 360 Hz to 800 Hz). This "Wild AC" is fed directly to non-frequency-sensitive loads (like heating elements) or rectified to DC and then inverted back to stable 400 Hz AC for sensitive avionics.

Step 5: UPSC ASO Exam Focus & Patterns
  • Numerical Dominance: Expect direct calculations of frequency, poles, or RPM. Always convert time periods to seconds before calculating frequency ($f = 1/T$).
  • Reactance Calculations: UPSC often combines frequency calculations with AC circuit theory. Remember that at 400 Hz, inductive reactance ($X_L = 2\pi f L$) is roughly 8 times higher than at 50 Hz, meaning inductors block AC more effectively.
  • Conceptual Questions: Questions frequently target the trade-off of 400 Hz systems (e.g., "Why not use 400 Hz on municipal grids?" $\rightarrow$ High line impedance and inductive losses over transmission lines).
Practice MCQs (Topic-focused)
Q1. An aircraft auxiliary power unit (APU) drives an 8-pole alternator. If the alternator rotates at a constant speed of 6,000 RPM, what is the frequency of the generated AC voltage?
A) 200 Hz
B) 400 Hz
C) 800 Hz
D) 50 Hz
Correct Answer: B (400 Hz)
Using the frequency formula:
$f = \frac{N \times P}{120}$
$f = \frac{6000 \times 8}{120} = \frac{48000}{120} = 400\text{ Hz}$.
Q2. To produce the standard aircraft electrical frequency of 400 Hz using a 6-pole generator, what must be the regulated speed of the drive shaft?
A) 4,000 RPM
B) 6,000 RPM
C) 8,000 RPM
D) 12,000 RPM
Correct Answer: C (8,000 RPM)
Rearranging the formula for speed ($N$):
$N = \frac{120 \times f}{P}$
$N = \frac{120 \times 400}{6} = \frac{48000}{6} = 8,000\text{ RPM}$.
Q3. A variable-speed alternator on an aircraft engine has 4 pole pairs. If the engine accessory gearbox drives the alternator at 4,500 RPM, what is the output AC frequency?
A) 150 Hz
B) 300 Hz
C) 450 Hz
D) 600 Hz
Correct Answer: B (300 Hz)
Note the term pole pairs ($p = 4$). The total number of poles ($P$) is $2 \times p = 8$.
$f = \frac{N \times P}{120} = \frac{4500 \times 8}{120} = 300\text{ Hz}$.
Alternatively, using pole pairs directly: $f = \frac{N \times p}{60} = \frac{4500 \times 4}{60} = 300\text{ Hz}$.
Q4. What is the primary engineering reason for selecting 400 Hz AC power for aircraft systems instead of the standard terrestrial 50 Hz or 60 Hz?
A) 400 Hz reduces the skin effect in aluminum wiring.
B) 400 Hz allows the use of significantly lighter transformers and motors.
C) 400 Hz minimizes inductive reactance losses over long wire runs.
D) 400 Hz eliminates the need for three-phase power.
Correct Answer: B (400 Hz allows the use of significantly lighter transformers and motors.)
According to transformer design principles, the required magnetic core cross-sectional area is inversely proportional to the frequency. Increasing frequency from 50/60 Hz to 400 Hz allows for much smaller magnetic cores, saving up to 70% of the weight of electrical machines.
Q5. An oscilloscope displays an aircraft AC voltage signal that completes exactly 10 cycles in 25 milliseconds. What is the frequency of this signal?
A) 250 Hz
B) 400 Hz
C) 500 Hz
D) 1000 Hz
Correct Answer: B (400 Hz)
Frequency is cycles per second:
$f = \frac{\text{Cycles}}{\text{Time in seconds}} = \frac{10}{25 \times 10^{-3}\text{ s}} = \frac{10}{0.025} = 400\text{ Hz}$.
Q6. What is the time period ($T$) of one complete cycle of a standard 400 Hz aircraft AC power supply?
A) 0.25 milliseconds
B) 2.50 milliseconds
C) 25.0 milliseconds
D) 4.00 milliseconds
Correct Answer: B (2.50 milliseconds)
$T = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{400} = 0.0025\text{ seconds} = 2.5\text{ ms}$.
