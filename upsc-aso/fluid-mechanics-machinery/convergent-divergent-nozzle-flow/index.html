Convergent/divergent nozzles

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Engine pressure ratio (EPR) transducers and FADEC exhaust gas sensors directly monitor convergent-divergent nozzle operational states.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Incorrect nozzle geometry or unstarted supersonic inlets cause catastrophic thrust loss and compressor stalls during high-altitude climb.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Compliance with DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 mandates strict structural integrity and thermal endurance checks for exhaust systems.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers investigate exhaust duct thermal fatigue, actuator jamming, and acoustic resonance during accident root-cause audits.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

A convergent-divergent nozzle is a pneumatic duct profile that accelerates compressible fluids to supersonic speeds by dropping static pressure and temperature through a throat. Governed by 1D isentropic flow relations: $\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1)\frac{du}{u}$.
  • Physical Meaning: It converts high thermal and pressure energy into directed kinetic exhaust velocity via continuous cross-sectional area variation.
  • Governing Law: Conservation of mass (continuity), momentum, and energy combined with the ideal gas law and isentropic relations.
  • Continuum Boundary: Fluid behavior assumes Knudsen number $\text{Kn} < 0.01$, validating Navier-Stokes continuum equations.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $A$ = Cross-sectional area of the duct ($\text{m}^2$)
  • $M$ = Mach number, ratio of local flow velocity to sonic speed (Dimensionless)
  • $u$ = Flow velocity vector magnitude ($\text{m/s}$)
  • $\rho$ = Fluid density ($\text{kg/m}^3$)
  • $p$ = Static pressure of the gas ($\text{Pa}$)
  • $T$ = Static absolute temperature ($\text{K}$)
  • $\gamma$ = Ratio of specific heats, $C_p/C_v$ (Dimensionless, $1.4$ for air)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: High-pressure stagnation gas molecules possess rapid thermal random motion, which transforms into ordered downstream translation inside the converging section.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Steep pressure gradients drive the fluid toward the throat where the flow area is a strict local minimum.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: When back pressure drops below critical pressure ratio, the throat chokes ($M=1$), forcing supersonic acceleration within the diverging section.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Combine differential continuity and momentum equations for steady 1D flow.

$$\frac{d\rho}{\rho} + \frac{du}{u} + \frac{dA}{A} = 0$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Apply isentropic relation linking density and velocity gradients via speed of sound $a$.

$$\frac{d\rho}{\rho} = -M^2 \frac{du}{u}$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Substitute the density differential into the continuity equation to isolate area-velocity changes.

$$\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1)\frac{du}{u}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Deduce that subsonic flow accelerates in converging ducts, whereas supersonic flow accelerates in diverging ducts.

$$\frac{dA}{A} = \frac{M^2 - 1}{1 - M^2} \dots \text{ (Area-Mach relation)}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Mach number is dimensionless $[M^0 L^0 T^0]$, while area ratio is $[M^0 L^0 T^0]$. Mass flow rate carries dimensions of $[M L^{-1} T^{-1}]$.
  • SI Unit: Mass flow rate is measured in kilograms per second ($\text{kg/s}$), pressure in Pascals ($\text{Pa}$), and temperature in Kelvin ($\text{K}$).
  • CGS Unit: Pressure is expressed in baryes or $\text{dyn/cm}^2$, and mass flow in $\text{g/s}$.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Pressure in $\text{psi}$, thrust in pounds-force ($\text{lbf}$), and mass flow in slugs per second ($slug/s$).

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Supersonic Acceleration in Converging Ducts

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming a converging duct can accelerate subsonic flow to supersonic speeds.

✅ UPSC Truth: Converging ducts only accelerate flow up to Mach 1 ($M=1$) at the exit; supersonic flow requires a diverging section.

Trap 2: Choked Flow Mass Independence

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing that lowering downstream pressure past the critical value continues to increase mass flow through a choked nozzle.

✅ UPSC Truth: Once choked, mass flow rate depends solely on upstream stagnation pressure and temperature, remaining invariant to further downstream pressure drops.

Trap 3: Isentropic Assumptions in Shocks

❌ Examiner Trap: Applying isentropic expansion relations across normal shock waves inside an overexpanded nozzle.

✅ UPSC Truth: Normal shock waves are distinctly non-isentropic, causing an entropy rise, stagnation pressure loss, and total temperature conservation.

Trap 4: Throat Area Definition

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing the geometric minimum area with the effective sonic throat area when boundary layer displacement thickness is large.

✅ UPSC Truth: The sonic throat occurs where Mach equals one, which may shift slightly downstream of the geometric minimum area due to boundary layer growth.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A convergent-divergent rocket nozzle operates with stagnation pressure $p_0 = 1.0 \times 10^6 \text{ Pa}$ and stagnation temperature $T_0 = 800 \text{ K}$. Assuming air with $\gamma = 1.4$ and $R = 287 \text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$, compute the throat area required to pass a mass flow rate of $\dot{m} = 5.0 \text{ kg/s}$.

Given Flight Parameters:
$\dot{m} = 5.0 \text{ kg/s}$, $p_0 = 1.0 \times 10^6 \text{ Pa}$, $T_0 = 800 \text{ K}$, $\gamma = 1.4$, $R = 287 \text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$.
Governing Formula:
$$A^* = \frac{\dot{m} \sqrt{RT_0}}{p_0 \gamma \left(\frac{2}{\gamma + 1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma + 1}{2(\gamma - 1)}}}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute Critical Temperature

Calculate static temperature at the throat ($T^*$).

$$T^* = T_0 \left(\frac{2}{\gamma + 1}\right) = 800 \times \left(\frac{2}{2.4} ight) = 666.67 \text{ K}$$
Step 2: Compute Critical Pressure and Density

Calculate throat density using ideal gas law.

$$\rho^* = \frac{p^*}{R T^*} = \frac{1.0 \times 10^6 \times (2/2.4)^{3.5}}{287 \times 666.67} = 3.824 \text{ kg/m}^3$$
Step 3: Calculate Throat Area

Determine sonic velocity and evaluate area from mass flow rate.

$$a^* = \sqrt{\gamma R T^*}= \sqrt{1.4 \times 287 \times 666.67} = 517.9 \text{ m/s}; \quad A^* = \frac{\dot{m}}{\rho^* a^*} = \frac{5.0}{3.824 \times 517.9} = 0.00252 \text{ m}^2$$
Final Answer: Throat Area $A^* = 2.52 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m}^2$ ($25.2 \text{ cm}^2$)
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Precise sizing of nozzle throats ensures FADEC engine management systems avoid thermal choking failures and unstarted inlet surges during critical takeoff phases.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary condition required for a convergent-divergent nozzle to achieve supersonic exhaust velocities?
A The back pressure must exceed the stagnation pressure.
B The flow at the throat must reach sonic speed ($M=1$) and the exit pressure must match ambient pressure.
C The diverging section must have a smaller cross-sectional area than the throat.
D The working fluid must be treated as an incompressible liquid.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
To achieve supersonic flow, the throat must choke ($M=1$) and the downstream pressure must be sufficiently low to allow expansion in the diverging section.
Question 2 • conceptual
In a converging-diverging nozzle operating under choked conditions, what happens to the mass flow rate if the exit back pressure is decreased further?
A Increases linearly with pressure drop
B Decreases due to aerodynamic choking
C Remains constant regardless of downstream pressure
D Drops to zero instantly
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Once choked ($M=1$ at throat), pressure perturbations cannot travel upstream, making the mass flow rate dependent solely on upstream stagnation conditions.
Question 3 • conceptual
How does the static temperature behave as a compressible gas accelerates from subsonic to supersonic speeds through a C-D nozzle?
A Increases continuously
B Remains constant (isothermal)
C Decreases continuously as enthalpy is converted to kinetic energy
D First decreases then rises above stagnation temperature
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Isentropic expansion converts internal thermal energy into kinetic energy, causing both static pressure and static temperature to drop.
Question 4 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding C-D nozzles: 1. Subsonic flow accelerates in a converging duct. 2. Supersonic flow accelerates in a diverging duct. 3. A normal shock can occur in an overexpanded nozzle. Which of the statements given above are correct?
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2 and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
All three statements are fundamental tenets of 1D compressible gas dynamics and C-D nozzle aerodynamics.
Question 5 • statement
Which of the following statements about choked flow in nozzles is/are correct? 1. Mach number at the throat is exactly 1. 2. Mass flow rate is at its maximum for given upstream conditions. 3. Velocity at the exit is always supersonic.
A 1 and 2 only
B 1 only
C 2 and 3 only
D 1, 2 and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statements 1 and 2 are true. Statement 3 is false because if the nozzle is unchoked or sub-optimally expanded, exit velocity can remain subsonic.
Question 6 • numerical
Calculate the critical pressure ratio $p^*/p_0$ for air with $\gamma = 1.4$ flowing through a choked nozzle throat.
A 0.528
B 0.634
C 0.843
D 0.456
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using the formula $(2/(\gamma + 1))^{\gamma/(\gamma - 1)} = (2/2.4)^{3.5} \approx 0.528$.
Question 7 • numerical
An air nozzle has stagnation temperature $T_0 = 400 \text{ K}$ and $\gamma = 1.4$. What is the static temperature at the throat ($T^*$) where $M=1$?
A 400 K
B 333.3 K
C 285.7 K
D 200.5 K
Solution & Technical Rationale:
$T^* = T_0 (2/(\gamma + 1)) = 400 \times (2/2.4) = 333.33 \text{ K}$.
Question 8 • assertion-reason
Assertion (A): A converging nozzle alone cannot produce supersonic exhaust velocities. Reason (R): The area-Mach number relation dictates that $\frac{dA}{dx} = 0$ when $M=1$, meaning the sonic condition can only occur at a geometric minimum (throat).
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
C (A) is true but (R) is false.
D (A) is false but (R) is true.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct, and the area-Mach relation rigorously explains why converging ducts choke at $M=1$ without supersonic acceleration.
Question 9 • assertion-reason
Assertion (A): Overexpanded C-D nozzles experience internal normal shock waves. Reason (R): When exit pressure is less than ambient pressure, the ambient air pushes into the divergent section, creating compression shocks.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
C (A) is true but (R) is false.
D (A) is false but (R) is true.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
An overexpanded nozzle has exit pressure below ambient, causing adverse pressure gradients that manifest as internal oblique or normal shocks.
Question 10 • conceptual
What is the effect of boundary layer growth on the effective throat area of a supersonic nozzle?
A Increases effective throat area
B Decreases effective mass flow capacity by reducing effective flow area
C Has zero impact on mass flow
D Eliminates the need for a diverging section
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Displacement thickness of the boundary layer reduces the geometric open area, requiring slight over-sizing during aerodynamic design.
Question 11 • numerical
If stagnation pressure is $2.0 \times 10^5 \text{ Pa}$ and critical pressure ratio for air is $0.528$, what is the static pressure at the choked throat?
A $1.056 \times 10^5 \text{ Pa}$
B $2.000 \times 10^5 \text{ Pa}$
C $0.528 \times 10^5 \text{ Pa}$
D $1.500 \times 10^5 \text{ Pa}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
$p^* = 0.528 \times 2.0 \times 10^5 = 1.056 \times 10^5 \text{ Pa}$.
Question 12 • conceptual
Which thermodynamic property remains constant across a normal shock wave inside a C-D nozzle?
A Static pressure
B Stagnation pressure
C Stagnation temperature
D Entropy
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Normal shocks are adiabatic processes in the absence of external heat transfer, meaning stagnation temperature remains conserved while stagnation pressure drops.

Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1

A To maintain constant fuel temperature
B To adjust throat and exit areas for optimal expansion across dry and wet thrust regimes

Rationale:
Actuating the nozzle flaps alters throat and exit areas to match changing mass flow and temperature demands between dry and afterburner operation.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the maximum theoretical velocity attainable by a gas expanding in a C-D nozzle from stagnation temperature $T_0$?
A Speed of sound at throat
B Infinite velocity
C Maximum escape velocity corresponding to complete enthalpy conversion ($T=0$) $V_{max} = \sqrt{2 C_p T_0}$
D Mach 1.0
Rationale:
Complete conversion of thermal enthalpy to kinetic energy yields the maximum theoretical velocity $V_{max} = \sqrt{2 C_p T_0}$.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
In an underexpanded nozzle operating in ambient air, what aerodynamic structures are visible in the exhaust plume?
A Expansion fans and oblique shock diamonds
B Isothermal streamlines
C Subsonic boundary layers
D Cavitation bubbles
Rationale:
Underexpanded jets have exit pressure greater than ambient, expanding via expansion fans and reflecting as shock diamonds.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the impact of friction on compressible flow inside a long constant-area or C-D duct?
A Increases stagnation pressure (Fanno flow phenomenon)
B Causes stagnation pressure losses and tends to drive Mach number toward 1
C Eliminates boundary layer separation
D Has zero effect on pressure
Rationale:
Friction (Fanno flow) causes total pressure loss and drives subsonic flow upward toward $M=1$ and supersonic flow downward toward $M=1$.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
How does an increase in the ratio of specific heats ($\gamma$) affect the critical pressure ratio $p^*/p_0$ for air?
A Increases critical pressure ratio
B Decreases critical pressure ratio
C Has no effect
D Causes the nozzle to un-choke
Rationale:
Formula $(2/(\gamma+1))^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}$ shows that increasing $\gamma$ lowers the critical pressure ratio.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1

A Localized shock-induced thermal-acoustic fatigue and flow separation
B Excessive subsonic diffuser efficiency

Shock wave oscillations and flow separation in misbehaved C-D nozzles generate severe localized thermal-acoustic loads and buckling.
