Metacentre & metacentric height

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Seaplane hull stability, amphibious landing gear sponsons, and fuel tank baffling systems rely directly on metacentric principles to prevent catastrophic capsize during water operations.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Incorrect fuel distribution across multi-tank configurations alters center of gravity ($G$), reducing $GM$ and causing dangerous uncommanded roll tendencies during takeoff and landing.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Governed by DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 Airworthiness standards concerning structural mass distribution, fuel management, and amphibious flotation integrity.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers investigate seaplane water loops and fuel sloshing destabilization by auditing mass properties, moment of inertia calculations, and center of gravity limits.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

The metacentre ($M$) is the theoretical point about which a floating body oscillates when given a small transverse angular displacement, defined mathematically as the intersection of the vertical line through the buoyant force with the initial vertical centerline. Metacentric height ($GM$) is the vertical distance between the center of gravity ($G$) and the metacentre ($M$), governing rotational stability.
  • Physical Meaning: It dictates whether a floating object returns to equilibrium, capsides, or remains in neutral equilibrium upon perturbation.
  • Governing Law: Archimedes' Principle combined with rotational moments of inertia determines the restoring couple.
  • Continuum Boundary: Assumes incompressible fluid continua and small displacement angles where $\sin\theta \approx \theta$.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $M$ = Metacentre position ($m$)
  • $G$ = Center of gravity of the vessel ($m$)
  • $B$ = Center of buoyancy ($m$)
  • $GM$ = Metacentric height ($m$)
  • $I$ = Least moment of inertia of the waterplane area about the longitudinal axis ($m^4$)
  • $\nabla$ = Submerged volume of displacement ($m^3$)
  • $\theta$ = Angle of heel or tilt (radians)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Hydrostatic pressure forces act normal to the submerged wetted surface, creating an integrated upward buoyant force vector acting through the center of buoyancy ($B$).
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: When a floating body heels by angle $\theta$, the submerged geometry shifts laterally, moving the line of action of the buoyant force outwards from the centerline.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: The offset between the weight acting downward through $G$ and buoyancy acting upward through $M$ generates a restoring or overturning moment couple.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Equating the lateral shift of the center of buoyancy to the moment of inertia of the waterplane area.

$$BB' = \frac{I}{\nabla}$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Relating the shift $BB'$ to the metacentric radius $BM$ and small angle of heel $\theta$.

$$BB' = BM \cdot \theta$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Equating both expressions for $BB'$ and resolving for the distance between buoyancy and metacentre.

$$BM \cdot \theta = \frac{I}{\nabla} \cdot \theta \implies BM = \frac{I}{\nabla}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Expressing the metacentric height $GM$ as the algebraic sum of $BM$ and $BG$.

$$GM = BM \pm BG = \frac{I}{\nabla} \pm BG$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Length dimension $[L^1]$ derived from macroscopic spatial separation metrics.
  • SI Unit: Meter ($m$) representing fundamental spatial intervals in metric standards.
  • CGS Unit: Centimeter ($cm$) representing $10^{-2}$ meters.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Feet ($ft$) where $1 \text{ m} \approx 3.28084 \text{ ft}$, commonly used in aviation hydrodynamics and seaplane hull design.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Compliance with ICAO Annex 8 and DGCA CAR Section 2 mandates strict verification of mass and balance limits for all aircraft operating on water or utilizing complex fuel transfer systems.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Free surface liquid motion in fuel tanks decreases effective metacentric height, directly impacting lateral stability and roll control authority during critical phases of flight.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: Amphibious floatplane ground/water loop incidents caused by improper center of gravity placement and unbaffled floatation compartment flooding leading to sudden loss of metacentric stability.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Metacentre Fixed Location

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming the metacentre $M$ is a fixed geometric point inside a floating body regardless of heel angle.

✅ UPSC Truth: The metacentre is only fixed for small angles of heel (up to $10^\circ$ to $15^\circ$); for large angles, the line of action shifts and the metacentric radius changes.

Trap 2: Sign of Metacentric Height ($GM$)

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming $GM = \frac{I}{\nabla} + BG$ is always valid regardless of the relative positions of $G$ and $B$.

✅ UPSC Truth: When $G$ is above $B$, $GM = \frac{I}{\nabla} - BG$; when $G$ is below $B$ (ballasted hulls), $GM = \frac{I}{\nabla} + BG$.

Trap 3: Free Surface Effect Sign

❌ Examiner Trap: Treating liquid sloshing in partially filled tanks as increasing stability.

✅ UPSC Truth: Free surface effect always effectively raises the center of gravity and *reduces* the effective metacentric height by $\frac{i}{\nabla}$.

Trap 4: Dimension Confusion

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing the moment of inertia of the waterplane area ($I$) with the mass moment of inertia of the hull body.

✅ UPSC Truth: $I$ is strictly the geometrical area moment of inertia of the waterplane surface about the longitudinal centerline axis.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An amphibious seaplane float displacing $2.5 \text{ m}^3$ of seawater has a waterplane area moment of inertia $I = 0.45 \text{ m}^4$. The center of gravity $G$ is located $0.12 \text{ m}$ above the center of buoyancy $B$. Calculate the metacentric height ($GM$) and determine its static stability status.

Given Flight Parameters:
Displacement volume $\nabla = 2.5 \text{ m}^3$, Waterplane moment of inertia $I = 0.45 \text{ m}^4$, Distance $BG = 0.12 \text{ m}$, Seawater density $\rho = 1025 \text{ kg/m}^3$.
Governing Formula:
$$GM = \frac{I}{\nabla} - BG$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Calculate the metacentric radius $BM = \frac{I}{\nabla} = \frac{0.45}{2.5} = 0.18 \text{ m}$. Step 2: Compute the metacentric height $GM = BM - BG = 0.18 - 0.12 = 0.06 \text{ m}$. Step 3: Since $GM > 0$, the metacentre lies above the center of gravity, confirming positive stable equilibrium.
Final Answer: $GM = +0.06 m (Stable Equilibrium)$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

A positive metacentric height of $0.06 \text{ m}$ ensures the seaplane will generate a restoring righting moment upon water surface disturbance, preventing capsize during taxiing.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the physical definition of the metacentre for a floating body?
A The point about which the body oscillates for small angular displacements
B The center of mass of the displaced fluid volume
C The intersection of the waterline with the longitudinal axis
D The point where total aerodynamic drag acts
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The metacentre is defined as the point of intersection of the line of action of the buoyant force with the initial vertical centerline when the body is tilted by a small angle.
Question 2 • conceptual
For a floating body to be in stable equilibrium, the metacentric height ($GM$) must be:
A Negative ($GM < 0$)
B Zero ($GM = 0$)
C Positive ($GM > 0$)
D Equal to the draft
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A positive metacentric height ($GM > 0$) means the metacentre $M$ is above the center of gravity $G$, producing a restoring righting couple upon heeling.
Question 3 • conceptual
How does free liquid surface inside a partially filled fuel tank affect the effective metacentric height?
A It increases $GM$ and enhances stability
B It has no effect on $GM$
C It decreases $GM$ and reduces stability
D It shifts the center of buoyancy downwards
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Free surface effect causes liquid to shift toward the heel side, raising the effective center of gravity and reducing the effective metacentric height by $\frac{i}{\nabla}$.
Question 4 • conceptual
The metacentric radius ($BM$) is given by which of the following expressions?
A $BM = \frac{\nabla}{I}$
B $BM = \frac{I}{\nabla}$
C $BM = \frac{BG}{I}$
D $BM = I \cdot \nabla$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
From buoyant geometry derivation, $BM = \frac{I}{\nabla}$, where $I$ is the moment of inertia of the waterplane area and $\nabla$ is the submerged volume.
Question 5 • statement-based
Consider the following statements regarding floating body stability: 1. When $GM = 0$, the body is in neutral equilibrium. 2. The metacentric height is independent of the center of gravity position. 3. A larger waterplane moment of inertia increases metacentric height. Which of the statements given above are correct?
A 1 and 2 only
B 1 and 3 only
C 2 and 3 only
D 1, 2 and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is correct ($GM=0$ is neutral equilibrium). Statement 2 is false because $GM = BM \pm BG$ directly depends on $G$'s position. Statement 3 is correct because $BM = I/\nabla$, so higher $I$ increases $BM$ and thus $GM$.
Question 6 • numerical
A pontoon has a submerged volume of $4.0 \text{ m}^3$ and a waterplane moment of inertia $I = 0.8 \text{ m}^4$. If the center of gravity is $0.10 \text{ m}$ above the center of buoyancy, what is the metacentric height?
A $0.05 \text{ m}$
B $0.10 \text{ m}$
C $0.20 \text{ m}$
D $0.30 \text{ m}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
$BM = \frac{I}{\nabla} = \frac{0.8}{4.0} = 0.20 \text{ m}$. $GM = BM - BG = 0.20 - 0.10 = 0.10 \text{ m}$.
Question 7 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): As an aircraft's fuel is consumed from wing tanks, the longitudinal and transverse stability characteristics can alter. Reason (R): Fuel burn changes the mass distribution and center of gravity position, directly impacting metacentric and inertial stability parameters.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both assertion and reason are true; fuel burn shifts $G$ and introduces free surface effects in partially filled tanks, altering stability.
Question 8 • conceptual
What happens to a floating body if its metacentric height ($GM$) is negative?
A It returns immediately to its upright position
B It capsizes or heels over to a new angle of heel
C It sinks vertically without tilting
D It enters neutral equilibrium oscillation
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A negative $GM$ creates an overturning couple (rather than a restoring couple), causing the body to capsize or heel over until a stable angle is reached.
Question 9 • numerical
Calculate the metacentric radius ($BM$) for a hull displacing $10 \text{ m}^3$ of water with a waterplane moment of inertia $I = 2.5 \text{ m}^4$.
A $0.10 \text{ m}$
B $0.25 \text{ m}$
C $0.40 \text{ m}$
D $2.50 \text{ m}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
$BM = \frac{I}{\nabla} = \frac{2.5 \text{ m}^4}{10 \text{ m}^3} = 0.25 \text{ m}$.
Question 10 • statement-based
Which of the following conditions define neutral equilibrium for a floating body? 1. Center of gravity coincides with the metacentre ($GM = 0$). 2. The body remains in whatever tilted position it is placed. 3. A righting couple is generated upon displacement. Select the correct answer:
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2 and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Neutral equilibrium occurs when $GM = 0$ (Statement 1) and the body stays in its displaced position without returning or capsizing (Statement 2). Statement 3 describes stable equilibrium.
Question 11 • conceptual
In naval architecture and seaplane design, why is a very large metacentric height undesirable?
A It causes the vessel to be too stiff, resulting in violent and uncomfortable rolling oscillations
B It makes the vessel completely unstable and prone to capsizing
C It prevents the vessel from floating at its design waterline
D It eliminates all buoyancy forces
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A excessively large $GM$ creates a very strong restoring moment, leading to rapid, violent rolling motions that stress structural frames and endanger personnel.
Question 12 • numerical
A seaplane float has $BM = 0.35 \text{ m}$ and $BG = 0.20 \text{ m}$, with $G$ above $B$. If a free surface effect reduces $GM$ by $0.05 \text{ m}$, what is the final net metacentric height?
A $0.10 \text{ m}$
B $0.15 \text{ m}$
C $0.20 \text{ m}$
D $0.50 \text{ m}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Initial $GM = BM - BG = 0.35 - 0.20 = 0.15 \text{ m}$. Subtracting free surface loss: $0.15 - 0.05 = 0.10 \text{ m}$.

Question 1 of 10 +3 / -1
The metacentric height ($GM$) of a floating body is defined as the distance between:
A Center of buoyancy and waterline
B Center of gravity and metacentre
C Keel and center of buoyancy
D Waterline and metacentre
Rationale:
Metacentric height is strictly the vertical distance between the center of gravity ($G$) and the metacentre ($M$).
Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
If a floating body has its center of gravity located above the metacentre ($GM < 0$), the equilibrium is:
A Stable
B Neutral
C Unstable
D Isentropic
Rationale:
When $GM < 0$, any small tilt produces an overturning couple, classifying the equilibrium as unstable.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
The metacentric radius $BM$ is directly proportional to:
A Submerged volume
B Waterplane moment of inertia
C Center of gravity height
D Fluid dynamic viscosity
Rationale:
Since $BM = I / \nabla$, the metacentric radius is directly proportional to the moment of inertia $I$ of the waterplane area.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the primary consequence of free surface effect in partially filled fluid tanks?
A Increases effective metacentric height
B Decreases effective metacentric height
C Eliminates center of buoyancy
D Increases displacement volume
Rationale:
Free surface effect causes liquid mass to shift during heel, raising the effective center of gravity and reducing $GM$.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
A pontoon displaces $5 \text{ m}^3$ of water and has $I = 1.0 \text{ m}^4$. If $BG = 0.10 \text{ m}$, what is $GM$?
A $0.05 \text{ m}$
B $0.10 \text{ m}$
C $0.15 \text{ m}$
D $0.20 \text{ m}$
Rationale:
$BM = 1.0 / 5 = 0.20 \text{ m}$. $GM = BM - BG = 0.20 - 0.10 = 0.10 \text{ m}$.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
In neutral equilibrium ($GM = 0$), if a floating body is tilted by an angle $\theta$:
A It returns to upright position
B It capsizes completely
C It remains in the tilted position
D It oscillates indefinitely with increasing amplitude
Rationale:
In neutral equilibrium, no restoring or overturning couple is generated, so the body stays in the displaced position.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
Which parameter governs the 'stiffness' or roll period of a floating vessel?
A Metacentric height ($GM$)
B Dynamic viscosity ($\mu$)
C Surface tension
D Mach number
Rationale:
A larger $GM$ shortens the natural roll period, making the vessel 'stiff' with rapid oscillations.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
For a submerged (completely underwater) body, stability is maintained when:
A Metacentre is above center of gravity
B Center of gravity is directly below the center of buoyancy
C Waterplane moment of inertia is maximum
D Metacentric height is zero
Rationale:
Completely submerged bodies have no waterplane area; stability requires $G$ to lie directly below $B$.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the SI unit of metacentric height ($GM$)?
A $\text{m}^2$
B $\text{m}$
C $\text{m}^4$
D $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$
Rationale:
Metacentric height is a linear distance measured in meters ($m$).
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
In aircraft and seaplane engineering, why must fuel transfer sequences be strictly controlled?
A To prevent asymmetric weight distribution and loss of metacentric stability
B To maintain supersonic speeds
C To prevent hydraulic fluid cavitation
D To maximize engine bleed pressure
Rationale:
Uncontrolled fuel transfer can shift $G$ and reduce $GM$ or create dangerous rolling moments on amphibious aircraft.
