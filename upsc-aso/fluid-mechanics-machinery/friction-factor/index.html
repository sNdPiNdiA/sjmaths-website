Friction factor

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Fuel transfer lines, hydraulic actuators, and environmental control system (ECS) ducting rely heavily on accurate friction factor models to prevent starvation or pressure collapse.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Incorrect friction factor estimations in fuel feed networks can lead to premature engine flameout or degraded transfer rates during high-altitude maneuvers.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Airworthiness compliance under DGCA CAR Section 2 mandates strict verification of fluid distribution systems against design pressure drop limits.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air safety officers scrutinize fluid friction degradation during accident investigations involving clogged fuel filters or unexpected hydraulic pressure drops.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

The Darcy-Weisbach friction factor ($f$) is a dimensionless parameter quantifying head loss due to viscous shear in internal pipe flows, governed by $h_f = f \frac{L}{D} \frac{V^2}{2g}$.
  • Physical Meaning: It measures the resistance to fluid flow imposed by internal boundary layer friction and wall roughness.
  • Governing Law: It bridges the Navier-Stokes momentum equations with empirical boundary shear stress measurements in fully developed internal flows.
  • Continuum Boundary: It remains valid under continuum mechanics assumptions where the Knudsen number $Kn < 0.001$.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $f$ = Darcy friction factor (Dimensionless)
  • $f_F$ = Fanning friction factor, equal to $f/4$ (Dimensionless)
  • $h_f$ = Head loss due to friction ($\text{m}$)
  • $L$ = Length of the duct or pipe ($\text{m}$)
  • $D$ = Hydraulic diameter of the pipe ($\text{m}$)
  • $V$ = Mean flow velocity ($\text{m/s}$)
  • $g$ = Acceleration due to gravity ($\text{m/s}^2$)
  • $\epsilon$ = Absolute wall roughness height ($\text{m}$)
  • $Re$ = Reynolds number (Dimensionless)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Fluid molecules near solid boundaries experience viscous drag via intermolecular momentum exchange within the laminar sublayer.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Turbulent fluctuations transfer transverse momentum across the boundary layer, converting static pressure energy into thermal dissipation via wall shear stress ($\tau_w$).
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: In aircraft fuel lines and environmental control system (ECS) ducts, this friction causes critical pressure drops that dictate pump sizing and bleed air mass flow rates.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Apply steady-state force balance on a cylindrical fluid control volume of length $L$ and diameter $D$.

$$\Delta P \cdot \frac{\pi D^2}{4} = \tau_w \cdot \pi D L$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Express wall shear stress $\tau_w$ in terms of dynamic pressure and the Darcy friction factor $f$.

$$\tau_w = \frac{f}{8} \rho V^2$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Substitute $\tau_w$ into the momentum balance equation and simplify the pressure drop expression.

$$\Delta P = \frac{4 L}{D} \left( \frac{f}{8} \rho V^2 \right) = f \frac{L}{D} \frac{\rho V^2}{2}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Convert pressure drop into head loss $h_f = \frac{\Delta P}{\rho g}$ to obtain the Darcy-Weisbach relation.

$$h_f = f \frac{L}{D} \frac{V^2}{2g}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: The Darcy friction factor is strictly dimensionless, represented dimensionally as $[M^0 L^0 T^0]$.
  • SI Unit: None, as it is a dimensionless ratio of shear stress to dynamic pressure.
  • CGS Unit: None, maintaining unit independence across all metric systems.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Unaffected by imperial conversions; directly applicable alongside feet, pounds per square inch (psi), and knots in aerospace engineering.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Governed by DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'M', Part III and ICAO Annex 8 Airworthiness of Aircraft regarding fuel and hydraulic system integrity.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Direct impact on engine fuel boost pumps, bleed air pneumatic systems, and high-pressure hydraulic flight control actuators.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: British Airways Flight 38 (Boeing 777-236ER, Rolls-Royce Trent 895 engines) suffered fuel flow restriction due to ice crystal accumulation in fuel-oil heat exchangers, altering effective line friction and pressure drop mechanics. Root safety cause led to mandatory redesign of fuel-oil heat exchanger thermal dynamics and system pressure drop tolerances.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Darcy vs. Fanning Friction Factor

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming the Darcy friction factor ($f$) and Fanning friction factor ($f_F$) are identical in numerical value.

✅ UPSC Truth: The Darcy factor is exactly four times the Fanning factor ($f = 4f_F$). Always verify which factor is requested in pipe friction formulas.

Trap 2: Laminar Flow Roughness Independence

❌ Examiner Trap: Applying the Moody diagram relative roughness curves ($ \epsilon/D $) to laminar pipe flow ($Re < 2300$).

✅ UPSC Truth: In laminar flow, $f = 64/Re$, which is completely independent of pipe wall roughness.

Trap 3: Fully Rough Turbulent Flow Reynolds Number Independence

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming the friction factor continues to decrease indefinitely as Reynolds number increases in turbulent pipe flow.

✅ UPSC Truth: In the fully rough turbulent zone (Moody chart plateau), $f$ depends solely on relative roughness ($\epsilon/D$) and is independent of $Re$.

Trap 4: Hydraulic Diameter Miscalculation

❌ Examiner Trap: Using the geometric diameter instead of hydraulic diameter when calculating $Re$ and $f$ for non-circular aircraft ducts.

✅ UPSC Truth: For non-circular ducts, use $D_h = \frac{4A}{P}$, where $A$ is cross-sectional area and $P$ is wetted perimeter.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A commercial transport aircraft fuel transfer line with an internal diameter of $0.05 \text{ m}$ and length of $20 \text{ m}$ carries Jet A-1 kerosene at a mean velocity of $2.5 \text{ m/s}$. Given density $\rho = 804 \text{ kg/m}^3$ and dynamic viscosity $\mu = 0.00132 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, determine the Reynolds number, the Darcy friction factor for smooth pipe flow, and the total head loss.

Given Flight Parameters:
Diameter $D = 0.05 \text{ m}$, Length $L = 20 \text{ m}$, Velocity $V = 2.5 \text{ m/s}$, Density $\rho = 804 \text{ kg/m}^3$, Viscosity $\mu = 0.00132 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$.
Governing Formula:
$$Re = \frac{\rho V D}{\mu}, \quad f = \frac{0.316}{Re^{0.25}}, \quad h_f = f \frac{L}{D} \frac{V^2}{2g}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Calculate Reynolds number: Re = \frac{804 \times 2.5 \times 0.05}{0.00132} = 76136.36. \nStep 2: Determine Darcy friction factor using Blasius equation for smooth turbulent flow: f = \frac{0.316}{(76136.36)^{0.25}} = \frac{0.316}{16.75} = 0.01886. \nStep 3: Calculate head loss: h_f = 0.01886 \times \frac{20}{0.05} \times \frac{2.5^2}{2 \times 9.81} = 0.01886 \times 400 \times 0.3185 = 2.403 \text{ m}.
Final Answer: $Re = 76,136; f = 0.0189; Head Loss h_f = 2.403 \text{ m of fluid}.$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate head loss calculation ensures fuel boost pumps are correctly sized to prevent cavitation and ensure positive net positive suction head (NPSH) during high-altitude climb.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary physical significance of the Darcy friction factor ($f$) in internal aircraft fluid systems?
A It measures absolute fluid temperature drop along a duct
B It quantifies head loss and flow resistance due to viscous shear
C It defines the compressibility of high-speed aerodynamic boundary layers
D It calculates the molecular mean free path of fuel vapors
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The Darcy friction factor directly relates shear stress and viscous dissipation to pressure drop and head loss in internal flows.
Question 2 • conceptual
For fully developed laminar flow in a circular pipe, the Darcy friction factor depends on which of the following parameters?
A Pipe relative roughness ($\epsilon/D$) only
B Reynolds number ($Re$) only
C Both Reynolds number and relative roughness
D Mach number and Prandtl number
Solution & Technical Rationale:
In laminar flow ($f = 64/Re$), the friction factor is entirely a function of the Reynolds number and is independent of pipe wall roughness.
Question 3 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding the Moody diagram: 1. In the completely rough turbulent zone, $f$ is independent of $Re$. 2. The transition zone depends on both $Re$ and relative roughness. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. In fully rough turbulent flow, roughness elements protrude through the laminar sublayer making $f$ independent of $Re$, while the transition zone is governed by both factors.
Question 4 • numerical
If the Reynolds number for a smooth pipe flow is $2,000$, what is the Darcy friction factor ($f$)?
A 0.0125
B 0.0320
C 0.0460
D 0.0640
Solution & Technical Rationale:
For laminar flow, $f = 64/Re = 64/2000 = 0.0320$.
Question 5 • assertion
Assertion (A): The Fanning friction factor ($f_F$) is exactly four times larger than the Darcy friction factor ($f$). Reason (R): The Darcy-Weisbach equation incorporates hydraulic radius variations differently than the shear stress force balance.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false and (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Assertion (A) is false because the Darcy friction factor is four times the Fanning friction factor ($f = 4f_F$), not vice versa.
Question 6 • conceptual
What is the relationship between the Darcy friction factor ($f$) and the Fanning friction factor ($f_F$)?
A $f = f_F$
B $f = 2f_F$
C $f = 4f_F$
D $f = 16f_F$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
By definition, the Darcy friction factor utilizes the full hydraulic diameter and is four times larger than the Fanning friction factor ($f = 4f_F$).
Question 7 • statement
Which of the following dimensionless groups are used to determine the friction factor in rough pipes? 1. Reynolds Number ($Re$) 2. Relative Roughness ($\epsilon/D$) 3. Mach Number ($Ma$) Select the correct answer using the code below:
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2 and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The Colebrook-White equation and Moody chart relate $f$ exclusively to $Re$ and relative roughness ($\epsilon/D$) for incompressible internal flows.
Question 8 • numerical
A hydraulic line has a Darcy friction factor of $0.04$, length $10 \text{ m}$, diameter $0.02 \text{ m}$, and mean velocity $2 \text{ m/s}$. What is the head loss ($h_f$)? (Take $g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$)
A 0.816 m
B 1.631 m
C 3.262 m
D 6.524 m
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $h_f = f \frac{L}{D} \frac{V^2}{2g}$, $h_f = 0.04 \times \frac{10}{0.02} \times \frac{2^2}{2 \times 9.81} = 20 imes \frac{4}{19.62} = 1.631 \text{ m}$.
Question 9 • conceptual
Why is the Darcy friction factor critical in aircraft fuel system airworthiness audits?
A It determines the aerodynamic lift coefficient of the wing
B It governs fuel feed line pressure drops and pump cavitation limits
C It calculates cabin pressurization outflow valve discharge
D It measures engine turbine blade creep rates
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Fuel line friction factors dictate pressure losses that must be overcome by boost pumps to prevent vapor lock and cavitation.
Question 10 • assertion
Assertion (A): Pipe roughness has a massive impact on turbulent pipe flow friction factor. Reason (R): Roughness elements protrude through the laminar sublayer into the turbulent core, increasing form drag and shear stress.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false and (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Roughness elements disrupt the laminar sublayer, significantly increasing form drag and turbulent mixing, which elevates the friction factor.
Question 11 • conceptual
Which empirical equation is universally used to solve implicitly for the Darcy friction factor in turbulent pipe flow across all roughness regimes?
A Bernoulli Equation
B Colebrook-White Equation
C Navier-Stokes Exact Equation
D Hagen-Poiseuille Equation
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The Colebrook-White equation combines smooth and rough pipe turbulent regimes into a single implicit formula for $f$.
Question 12 • numerical
If a non-circular aircraft ECS duct has a cross-sectional area of $0.02 \text{ m}^2$ and a wetted perimeter of $0.5 \text{ m}$, what is its hydraulic diameter ($D_h$)?
A 0.04 m
B 0.08 m
C 0.16 m
D 0.40 m
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $D_h = \frac{4A}{P} = \frac{4 \times 0.02}{0.5} = \frac{0.08}{0.5} = 0.16 \text{ m}$.

Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
In fully rough turbulent pipe flow, the friction factor $f$ is:
A Directly proportional to $Re$
B Inversely proportional to $Re$
C Independent of $Re$ and dependent only on $\epsilon/D$
D Equal to zero
Rationale:
In the fully rough zone, viscous forces are negligible compared to roughness form drag, making $f$ depend solely on relative roughness.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
Which of the following parameters does NOT affect the Darcy friction factor in turbulent flow?
A Reynolds number
B Pipe absolute roughness
C Pipe diameter
D Fluid bulk temperature (independent of viscosity change)
Rationale:
Temperature affects viscosity and density, which alter $Re$. If temperature changes do not alter fluid properties or $Re$ and $\epsilon/D$, temperature itself is not an independent variable.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
The Moody diagram plots the Darcy friction factor against which two variables?
A Mach number and Prandtl number
B Reynolds number and relative roughness
C Head loss and velocity
D Shear stress and dynamic pressure
Rationale:
The Moody chart is a logarithmic plot of $f$ versus $Re$ parameterized by relative roughness $\epsilon/D$.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the ratio of the Darcy friction factor ($f$) to the Fanning friction factor ($f_F$)?
A 0.25
B 1.0
C 2.0
D 4.0
Rationale:
The Darcy friction factor is 4 times the Fanning friction factor ($f/f_F = 4$).듯
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
For an aircraft hydraulic line, if the flow velocity is doubled while maintaining laminar flow, the friction factor $f$ will:
A Double
B Halve
C Remain unchanged
D Quadruple
Rationale:
In laminar flow, $f = 64/Re$. Doubling velocity doubles $Re$, which halves the friction factor ($f \propto 1/V$).
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
Which DGCA regulatory document governs aircraft fuel system airworthiness and pressure drop limits?
A CAR Section 2, Airworthiness
B CAR Section 4, Aerodromes
C CAR Section 7, Flight Crew

Rationale:
DGCA CAR Section 2 governs airworthiness requirements for aircraft structures, systems, and powerplants, including fuel distribution.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the hydraulic diameter of a rectangular aircraft ventilation duct measuring $0.4 \text{ m}$ by $0.2 \text{ m}$?
A 0.133 m
B 0.267 m
C 0.400 m
D 0.600 m
Rationale:
Using $D_h = \frac{4(0.4 \times 0.2)}{2(0.4 + 0.2)} = \frac{0.32}{1.2} = 0.267 \text{ m}$.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
In turbulent pipe flow, if the Reynolds number increases while keeping relative roughness constant, the friction factor will:
A Increase
B Decrease
C Remain constant
D Drop to zero
Rationale:
In smooth and transition turbulent zones, increasing $Re$ decreases the friction factor until the fully rough plateau is reached.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
Which aerodynamic phenomenon is analogous to pipe friction factor in external boundary layer flows?
A Induced drag coefficient
B Skin friction drag coefficient ($C_f$)
C Wave drag coefficient
D Interference drag
Rationale:
Skin friction drag coefficient ($C_f$) in external boundary layers is the direct external aerodynamic counterpart to internal pipe friction factors.
