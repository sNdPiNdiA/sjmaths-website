Fanno-flow & Rayleigh-flow concepts

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Gas turbine combustion chambers and bleed air ducts rely on Rayleigh and Fanno flow equations to predict pressure losses and thermal choking.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Accurate internal duct flow modeling prevents unstarted inlets and compressor stalls during high-incidence maneuvers.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Compliance with DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 ensures bleed air and pneumatic ducts withstand maximum operating pressure differentials.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers investigate turbine duct failures and thermal distress by evaluating friction factor anomalies and heat addition limits.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Fanno flow describes steady, one-dimensional compressible flow through a constant-area duct with friction and adiabatic walls ($dQ = 0$), while Rayleigh flow represents frictionless, one-dimensional flow with heat transfer ($dQ \neq 0$) through a constant-area duct.
  • Physical Meaning: Fanno flow isolates wall shear stress effects on gas dynamics, whereas Rayleigh flow isolates thermal energy addition or rejection.
  • Governing Law: Both are governed by simultaneous conservation of mass, momentum, and energy coupled with the ideal gas law and second law of thermodynamics.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid only within the continuum hypothesis regime where Knudsen number $Kn < 0.01$, ensuring thermodynamic equilibrium.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $M$ = Mach number (Dimensionless)
  • $f$ = Darcy friction factor (Dimensionless)
  • $D_h$ = Hydraulic diameter of the duct ($m$)
  • $q$ = Heat transfer per unit mass ($J/kg$)
  • $T_0$ = Stagnation temperature ($K$)
  • $p_0$ = Stagnation pressure ($Pa$)
  • $s$ = Specific entropy ($J/(kg\cdot K)$)
  • $\gamma$ = Ratio of specific heats (Dimensionless)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Viscous wall friction in Fanno flow removes momentum through molecular shear, whereas external heating in Rayleigh flow alters molecular kinetic energy.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Momentum loss in Fanno flow causes pressure drop for subsonic flow but pressure rise for supersonic flow as entropy increases.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Both flows drive the Mach number toward choking ($M = 1$) at the duct exit, maximizing entropy production.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Conservation Relations

Differential equations combining mass, momentum, and energy for constant area ducts.

$$\frac{dM^2}{M^2} = \frac{\gamma M^2 (1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2)}{1 - M^2} \left( \frac{4f}{D_h}dx \right)$$
Step 2: Fanno Flow Entropy Change

Expressing specific entropy change as a function of Mach number.

$$\frac{ds}{c_p} = \frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma} \frac{dp_0}{p_0} = -\frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2 \frac{dM^2}{M^2}$$
Step 3: Rayleigh Flow Energy Coupling

Relating stagnation temperature change to heat addition and Mach number.

$$\frac{dT_0}{T_0} = \frac{1 + \gamma M^2}{1 - M^2} \frac{1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2}{1 + \gamma M^2} \frac{dQ}{C_p T}$$
Step 4: Choking Limit Condition

Setting denominator to zero yields the maximum entropy and choking condition $M=1$.

$$M^2 = 1$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Mach number is dimensionless $[M^0 L^0 T^0]$, friction factor is dimensionless, heat transfer $q$ is $[L^2 T^{-2}]$.
  • SI Unit: Heat transfer per unit mass in $J/kg$ ($m^2/s^2$), friction factor dimensionless, hydraulic diameter in meters ($m$).
  • CGS Unit: Heat transfer in $erg/g$ ($cm^2/s^2$), length in centimeters ($cm$).
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Pressure in $psi$ ($lb/in^2$), heat in $BTU/lbm$, velocity in knots ($kts$).

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2 Series 'L' and ICAO Annex 8 mandate strict integrity checks for pneumatic ducting and combustor liners.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Directly affects bleed air distribution manifolds, environmental control systems (ECS), and FADEC turbine temperature limiters.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: Uncontained engine failure caused by Rayleigh flow thermal choking in damaged combustor liners, leading to rigorous FADEC software updates across commercial fleets.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Is Fanno flow isothermal?

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming Fanno flow is isothermal because the duct walls are insulated.

✅ UPSC Truth: Fanno flow is adiabatic ($dQ=0$), but temperature changes due to friction converting kinetic energy into enthalpy.

Trap 2: Rayleigh flow stagnation temperature constancy

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing stagnation temperature $T_0$ remains constant in Rayleigh flow.

✅ UPSC Truth: External heat addition ($dQ \neq 0$) directly changes stagnation temperature in Rayleigh flow ($dT_0 \neq 0$).

Trap 3: Maximum entropy location

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming entropy decreases at supersonic Mach numbers in Fanno flow.

✅ UPSC Truth: By the second law of thermodynamics, entropy always increases along the flow direction, reaching a maximum exactly at $M=1$.

Trap 4: Choking criteria confusion

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing area change choking (Nozzle throat) with Fanno/Rayleigh choking.

✅ UPSC Truth: Fanno and Rayleigh flow achieve choking ($M=1$) due to cumulative friction or heat transfer in constant area ducts.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

Air enters a constant-area insulated duct at Mach $M_1 = 0.3$, $T_1 = 300 K$, and $p_1 = 100 \, kPa$. Given a friction factor $f = 0.02$ and hydraulic diameter $D_h = 0.05 \, m$, calculate the maximum duct length $L_{max}$ required to choke the flow.

Given Flight Parameters:
Initial Mach number $M_1 = 0.3$, Inlet temperature $T_1 = 300 K$, Inlet pressure $p_1 = 100 \, kPa$, Friction factor $f = 0.02$, Hydraulic diameter $D_h = 0.05 \, m$, Ratio of specific heats $\gamma = 1.4$.
Governing Formula:
$$\frac{f L_{max}}{D_h} = \frac{1 - M_1^2}{\gamma M_1^2} + \frac{\gamma + 1}{2\gamma} \ln \left( \frac{(\gamma + 1)M_1^2}{2 + (\gamma - 1)M_1^2} \right)$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute the friction parameter term for $M_1 = 0.3$: $\frac{1 - (0.3)^2}{1.4 (0.3)^2} = \frac{0.91}{0.126} = 7.222$. Step 2: Compute the logarithmic term: $\frac{2.4}{2.8} \ln \left( \frac{2.4 \times 0.09}{2 + 0.4 \times 0.09} \right) = 0.8571 \ln(0.1047) = -1.945$. Step 3: Combine terms to find $\frac{f L_{max}}{D_h} = 7.222 - 1.945 = 5.277$, leading to $L_{max} = \frac{5.277 \times 0.05}{0.02} = 13.19 \, m$.
Final Answer: $13.19 meters$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Knowing maximum duct length prevents unexpected thermal and pressure choking in aircraft environmental control system (ECS) pneumatic lines during high-flow operations.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary thermodynamic assumption governing Fanno flow?
A Isothermal flow with friction
B Adiabatic flow with friction
C Frictionless flow with heat transfer
D Isentropic flow through a variable area
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Fanno flow is defined as steady, frictional, one-dimensional compressible flow through a constant-area duct with adiabatic walls ($dQ=0$). Options A, C, and D violate these fundamental constraints.
Question 2 • conceptual
In Rayleigh flow, what happens to the stagnation temperature ($T_0$) when heat is added to a subsonic stream ($M < 1$)?
A Stagnation temperature decreases
B Stagnation temperature remains constant
C Stagnation temperature increases
D Stagnation temperature drops to zero
Solution & Technical Rationale:
By definition of Rayleigh flow energy equations, adding heat ($dQ > 0$) to any subsonic or supersonic stream increases the stagnation temperature $T_0$. Therefore, option C is correct.
Question 3 • statement-based
Consider the following statements regarding Fanno flow: 1. Entropy always increases along the direction of Fanno flow. 2. The Mach number at the exit of a sufficiently long duct always approaches $M = 1$. 3. Pressure always decreases monotonically for both subsonic and supersonic Fanno flows. Which of the statements given above are correct?
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2, and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statement 1 is correct due to the second law of thermodynamics. Statement 2 is correct as friction drives $M \to 1$. Statement 3 is false because pressure increases in supersonic Fanno flow due to deceleration.
Question 4 • numerical
For a Rayleigh flow with $\gamma = 1.4$, if heat is added until thermal choking occurs, what is the Mach number at the exit?
A $M = 0.5$
B $M = 1.0$
C $M = 1.5$
D $M = 2.0$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Choking in both Fanno and Rayleigh flows in constant area ducts invariably occurs at the sonic condition where the Mach number equals 1.0.
Question 5 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Static pressure drops continuously in subsonic Fanno flow. Reason (R): Wall friction accelerates subsonic flow, causing a drop in static pressure. Select the correct option:
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Static pressure does drop in subsonic Fanno flow, but friction decelerates stagnation pressure while accelerating the flow velocity, making Reason (R) fundamentally incorrect.
Question 6 • conceptual
How does entropy behave in Rayleigh flow when heat is rejected ($dQ < 0$) from a supersonic stream?
A Entropy increases
B Entropy remains constant
C Entropy decreases
D Entropy becomes negative
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Heat rejection ($dQ < 0$) combined with supersonic flow physics causes a reduction in specific entropy, satisfying the differential entropy relation for Rayleigh flow.
Question 7 • statement-based
Which of the following parameters remain constant along a Rayleigh flow duct? 1. Stagnation Pressure ($p_0$) 2. Area ($A$) 3. Momentum function ($F$) Select the correct option:
A 1 only
B 2 only
C 2 and 3 only
D 1, 2, and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Rayleigh flow assumes constant duct area ($A = \text{const}$) and frictionless walls, which implies the momentum function $F$ is constant. Stagnation pressure changes due to heat transfer.
Question 8 • numerical
In a Fanno flow duct, if the inlet Mach number is $M_1 = 2.0$ and friction is present, what happens to the static temperature along the duct?
A Static temperature decreases
B Static temperature increases
C Static temperature remains constant
D Static temperature drops to absolute zero
Solution & Technical Rationale:
For supersonic Fanno flow ($M > 1$), friction causes deceleration, which converts kinetic energy into enthalpy, thereby increasing static temperature.
Question 9 • conceptual
What is the maximum possible entropy point on the Fanno line?
A At $M = 0$
B At $M = 1$
C At $M = \infty$
D At the entrance of the duct
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The Fanno line reaches its maximum entropy point precisely at the sonic Mach number $M = 1$, representing the choking limit.
Question 10 • statement-based
Consider Rayleigh flow heating in a subsonic duct: 1. Mach number increases toward 1. 2. Static temperature increases initially then decreases. 3. Density decreases as flow accelerates. Which are correct?
A 1 and 3 only
B 2 only
C 1, 2, and 3
D None
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Subsonic heating increases Mach number (Statement 1) and decreases density due to acceleration (Statement 3). Static temperature behavior depends on the specific subsonic regime, making Statement 2 misleading.
Question 11 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): A supersonic Fanno flow cannot be choked if the duct is shorter than $L_{max}$. Reason (R): Choking in Fanno flow requires the cumulative friction effect to drive the exit Mach number to exactly 1.0. Select the correct option:
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both assertion and reason are correct. Duct length must equal or exceed $L_{max}$ for the friction to achieve the sonic exit condition.
Question 12 • conceptual
In aircraft propulsion, combustion in a ramjet burner is best modeled as:
A Fanno flow
B Rayleigh flow
C Isentropic flow
D Isothermal flow
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Combustion involves significant heat addition in a duct with negligible friction compared to heat addition effects, making Rayleigh flow the ideal analytical model.

A Increases

A Increases
B Decreases toward 1
C Remains constant

Which of the following parameters is constant in Rayleigh flow?
A Stagnation Pressure
B Static Pressure
C Momentum Function $F$
D Stagnation Temperature

Frictionless constant-area flow ensures the momentum function $F = pA + \rho V^2 A$ remains constant.

What is the primary cause of choking in Fanno flow?
A Area reduction
B Cumulative wall friction
C External heat rejection
D Shock wave formation

Cumulative wall friction drives the flow state toward the sonic point, resulting in friction choking.

In subsonic Fanno flow, as friction acts on the fluid, the velocity:
A Decreases
B Increases
C Remains constant
D Reverses direction

Friction accelerates subsonic Fanno flow, increasing its velocity toward $M = 1$.

What governs the maximum duct length $L_{max}$ in Fanno flow?

The maximum duct length depends directly on inlet Mach number $M_1$, friction factor $f$, and hydraulic diameter $D_h$.

Ramjet combustor analysis utilizes which internal compressible flow model?
A Fanno Flow

Ramjet combustion is modeled as Rayleigh flow due to dominant heat addition in a constant-area duct.
