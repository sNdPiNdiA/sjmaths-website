Prandtl-Meyer expansion

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Supersonic inlets and convergent-divergent nozzles rely on Prandtl-Meyer expansions to manage core engine airflow.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Incorrect expansion wave calculations cause unstart phenomena in supersonic intakes, leading to severe thrust loss.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 mandate strict aerodynamic design reviews for high-Mach aircraft structures.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: ASOs examine supersonic inlet flow distortion and shock-boundary layer interactions during high-speed mishap audits.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

The Prandtl-Meyer expansion describes the isentropic, continuous turning of a supersonic flow around a convex corner through a fan of Mach waves. The governing relation for the expansion angle $\nu(M)$ is given by $\nu(M) = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}} \tan^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}(M^2-1)}\right) - \tan^{-1}(\sqrt{M^2-1})$.
  • Physical Meaning: Flow accelerates continuously, pressure and density drop rapidly, and Mach number increases across the expansion fan.
  • Governing Law: Derived from the conservation of mass, momentum, and energy combined with isentropic gas dynamics relations.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid for inviscid, adiabatic, and continuous supersonic flows where boundary layer separation is absent.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $M$ = Mach number (Dimensionless)
  • $\nu$ = Prandtl-Meyer function (Radians or Degrees)
  • $\gamma$ = Ratio of specific heats (Dimensionless)
  • $\theta$ = Wall turning angle (Radians or Degrees)
  • $p$ = Static pressure ($Pa$)
  • $T$ = Static temperature ($K$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Supersonic molecules encounter a geometry away from the flow, reducing local static pressure and expanding the mean free path.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Expansion waves fan out from the convex corner, creating smooth property gradients rather than discontinuous shock jumps.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Total enthalpy remains constant while static temperature and pressure decrease, yielding high exhaust velocities in nozzles.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Apply infinitesimal turning angle relation across an elementary Mach wave.

$$\tan(\mu) = \frac{d\theta}{d\ln V}$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Substitute Mach angle $\mu = \sin^{-1}(1/M)$ and velocity relations.

$$d\theta = \sqrt{M^2-1} \frac{dv}{v}$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Integrate the differential turning angle using Crocco's relation and isentropic state variables.

$$\nu(M) = \int \frac{\sqrt{M^2-1}}{1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2} \frac{dM}{M}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Evaluate the integral to yield the exact Prandtl-Meyer function.

$$\nu(M_2) = \nu(M_1) + \theta$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Angles and Mach numbers are fundamentally dimensionless $[M^0 L^0 T^0 \theta^0]$.
  • SI Unit: Radians ($rad$) or Degrees ($^{\circ}$) for turning and expansion angles.
  • CGS Unit: Radians ($rad$) or Degrees ($^{\circ}$) since angles are scaling-independent.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Pressure in psi, temperature in Rankine ($^{\circ}R$), and velocities in feet per second ($ft/s$).

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Governed by DGCA CAR Section 2 (Airworthiness) and ICAO Annex 8 for supersonic flight envelope clearance.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Isentropy Assumption

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming Prandtl-Meyer expansion is non-isentropic because pressure drops sharply.

✅ UPSC Truth: The flow across an expansion fan is continuous and isentropic, conserving total enthalpy and entropy.

Trap 2: Subsonic Expansion

❌ Examiner Trap: Applying Prandtl-Meyer equations directly to subsonic convex corner turning.

✅ UPSC Truth: Prandtl-Meyer expansion applies exclusively to supersonic flows ($M > 1$). Subsonic turning uses elliptic potential flow.

Trap 3: Temperature Increase

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing that high-speed acceleration increases static temperature.

✅ UPSC Truth: Expansion converts internal thermal energy into kinetic energy, causing static temperature and pressure to drop.

Trap 4: Maximum Turning Angle

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming flow can turn by an infinite angle around a corner.

✅ UPSC Truth: The maximum turning angle is limited by the Prandtl-Meyer function evaluated as $M \to \infty$.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A supersonic air stream at $M_1 = 2.0$ turns around a convex corner of angle $\theta = 15^{\circ}$. Calculate the final Mach number $M_2$ given $\gamma = 1.4$.

Given Flight Parameters:
Initial Mach number $M_1 = 2.0$, Corner angle $\theta = 15^{\circ} = 0.2618 \ rad$, Ratio of specific heats $\gamma = 1.4$.
Governing Formula:
$$\nu(M_2) = \nu(M_1) + \theta$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute $\nu(M_1)$ for $M_1 = 2.0$ using $\gamma = 1.4$. Formula yields $\nu(2.0) = 26.38^{\circ}$. Step 2: Add turning angle: $\nu(M_2) = 26.38^{\circ} + 15.0^{\circ} = 41.38^{\circ}$. Step 3: Invert Prandtl-Meyer function for $\nu = 41.38^{\circ}$ to find final Mach number $M_2 = 2.58$.
Final Answer: Final Mach number $M_2 = 2.58$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate calculation of downstream Mach number ensures supersonic intake structures remain matched and prevents destructive shock oscillations during flight.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary thermodynamic characteristic of a Prandtl-Meyer expansion fan?
A Isentropic and continuous
B Adiabatic with entropy increase
C Isothermal and shock-dominated
D Polytropic with heat addition
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Prandtl-Meyer expansion is a centered, isentropic, and continuous wave pattern where total pressure and entropy remain constant across the fan.
Question 2 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding Prandtl-Meyer expansion: 1. Static pressure decreases across the expansion fan. 2. Mach number increases across the expansion fan. 3. Total enthalpy decreases across the expansion fan. Which of the statements given above are correct?
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2, and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Statements 1 and 2 are correct because velocity increases and pressure/temperature drop. Statement 3 is false because total enthalpy is constant in an adiabatic isentropic flow.
Question 3 • numerical
If the initial Mach number is $1.5$ and the turning angle is $10^{\circ}$ with $\gamma = 1.4$, what happens to static temperature?
A Increases
B Decreases
C Remains constant
D Drops to absolute zero
Solution & Technical Rationale:
As flow expands supersonically, thermal energy is converted to kinetic energy, resulting in a decrease in static temperature.
Question 4 • conceptual
Which flow regime is mandatory for a Prandtl-Meyer expansion to occur?
A Subsonic ($M < 1$)
B Sonic ($M = 1$)
C Supersonic ($M > 1$)
D Hypersonic stagnation
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Prandtl-Meyer expansion waves only form and propagate in supersonic flows where pressure signals travel at characteristic Mach angles.
Question 5 • assertion-reason
Assertion (A): Prandtl-Meyer expansion waves are centered expansion fans. Reason (R): All expansion waves emanate from a sharp convex corner intersecting a supersonic stream.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. A sharp convex corner focuses the expansion origin into a centered fan of Mach waves.
Question 6 • conceptual
What happens to the stagnation pressure ($p_0$) across a Prandtl-Meyer expansion fan?
A Increases
B Decreases
C Remains constant
D Drops to zero
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Because the flow is isentropic and frictionless, stagnation pressure ($p_0$) remains constant across the expansion fan.
Question 7 • numerical
Calculate the maximum turning angle for air ($\gamma = 1.4$) expanding from an initial Mach number of $1.0$ to infinity.
A $90.0^{\circ}$
B $130.5^{\circ}$
C $180.0^{\circ}$
D Infinitely large
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Evaluating the Prandtl-Meyer function as $M \to \infty$ for $\gamma=1.4$ yields a maximum turning angle of approximately $130.5^{\circ}$.
Question 8 • statement
Which of the following parameters increase across a Prandtl-Meyer expansion? 1. Mach Number 2. Flow Velocity 3. Entropy Which are correct?
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2, and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Mach number and flow velocity increase. Entropy remains constant because the flow is isentropic (frictionless and adiabatic).
Question 9 • conceptual
How do the leading and trailing edges of a Prandtl-Meyer expansion fan behave relative to each other?
A They converge into a shock wave
B They diverge, spreading out the expansion fan
C They remain parallel
D They cancel each other instantly
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The expansion fan spreads out because the leading edge Mach wave is more inclined than the trailing edge Mach wave.
Question 10 • assertion-reason
Assertion (A): Shock waves increase entropy while Prandtl-Meyer waves are isentropic. Reason (R): Shock waves are discontinuous property jumps, whereas expansion fans are continuous wave systems.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Discontinuous shock waves generate entropy, while continuous expansions preserve isentropic conditions.
Question 11 • conceptual
In supersonic nozzle design, what role does the divergent section play?
A Generates compression shocks
B Facilitates Prandtl-Meyer expansion to accelerate flow
C Slows flow to subsonic speeds
D Maintains constant Mach number
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The divergent contour acts as a convex corner series, causing continuous Prandtl-Meyer expansion that accelerates flow to high supersonic Mach numbers.
Question 12 • numerical
If $\nu(M_1) = 20^{\circ}$ and the wall turns by $\theta = 25^{\circ}$, what is the final Prandtl-Meyer function $\nu(M_2)$?
A $5^{\circ}$
B $20^{\circ}$
C $45^{\circ}$
D $500^{\circ}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using the relation $\nu(M_2) = \nu(M_1) + \theta$, we get $\nu(M_2) = 20^{\circ} + 25^{\circ} = 45^{\circ}$.

Which thermodynamic property remains strictly constant across a Prandtl-Meyer expansion fan?
A Static Pressure
B Static Temperature
C Total Enthalpy
D Density
Rationale:
Total enthalpy remains constant across adiabatic, isentropic expansion flows.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
If a supersonic flow turns into a concave corner instead of a convex corner, what wave is formed?
A Expansion fan
B Oblique shock wave
C Expansion wave cancellation
D Prandtl-Meyer vortex
Rationale:
Concave corners compress supersonic flow, producing oblique shock waves rather than Prandtl-Meyer expansion fans.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
How does density vary across a Prandtl-Meyer expansion fan?
A Increases
B Decreases
C Remains constant
D Drops to zero instantaneously
Rationale:
Expansion accelerates flow and reduces static pressure, causing density to drop accordingly.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
What governs the angular spread of a Prandtl-Meyer expansion fan?
A Mach angle difference between leading and trailing edges
B Skin friction coefficient
C Reynolds number
D Prandtl number
Rationale:
The fan is bounded by Mach waves corresponding to the initial and final Mach numbers.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
In supersonic aircraft intakes, why is flow expansion carefully controlled?
A To prevent boundary layer separation and unstart
B To increase fuel density
C To eliminate engine cowl drag completely
D To cool turbine blades directly
Rationale:
Uncontrolled expansion and shock interaction can cause inlet unstart and catastrophic thrust loss.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
Which ratio dictates the maximum turning angle of a Prandtl-Meyer expansion?
A Ratio of specific heats ($\gamma$)
B Prandtl number ($Pr$)
C Mach number ($M$)
D Reynolds number ($Re$)
Rationale:
The maximum turning angle depends solely on $\gamma$ as $M \to \infty$.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the nature of the pressure gradient across a Prandtl-Meyer expansion fan?
A Discontinuous step change
B Smooth and continuous
C Zero pressure gradient
D Oscillatory sinusoidal
Rationale:
Unlike shock waves, expansion fans are smooth and continuous, distributing pressure changes across a fan of Mach waves.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
If $\gamma = 1.4$, what is the Prandtl-Meyer function formula in terms of $M$?
A $\sqrt{6} \tan^{-1}(\sqrt{\frac{M^2-1}{6}}) - \tan^{-1}(\sqrt{M^2-1})$
B $\tan^{-1}(M)$
C $\sqrt{2} \sin(M)$
D $\ln(M)$
Rationale:
Substituting $\gamma = 1.4$ into the general Prandtl-Meyer formula yields $\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1} = 6$, giving the exact expression.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
Under what condition is the Prandtl-Meyer relation invalid?
A Flow is supersonic
B Flow is viscous and turbulent with strong boundary layer separation
C Flow is isentropic
D Flow turns around a convex corner
Rationale:
The standard Prandtl-Meyer relation assumes inviscid flow; significant viscosity and separation invalidate simple inviscid wave calculations.
