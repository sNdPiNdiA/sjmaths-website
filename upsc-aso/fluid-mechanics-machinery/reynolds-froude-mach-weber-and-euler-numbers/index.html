Reynolds, Froude, Mach, Weber & Euler numbers

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Pitot-static systems use differential pressure ($Eu$) and Mach meters ($Ma$) to compute indicated and true airspeed for flight crew display.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Reynolds number governs boundary layer transition on airfoils, directly impacting stall warnings, maximum lift coefficients, and take-off field lengths.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 mandate strict adherence to flight envelope limits defined by Mach and Reynolds similarity in type certification.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air safety officers evaluate Mach tuck, compressibility buffet, and fuel slosh dynamics ($We$) during high-speed loss-of-control investigations.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Dimensionless parameters (Reynolds, Froude, Mach, Weber, and Euler numbers) govern dynamic similarity in fluid mechanics, defined respectively as $Re = \frac{\rho v L}{\mu}$, $Fr = \frac{v}{\sqrt{g L}}$, $Ma = \frac{v}{a}$, $We = \frac{\rho v^2 L}{\sigma}$, and $Eu = \frac{\Delta p}{\rho v^2}$.
  • Physical Meaning: They represent ratios of active fluid forces such as inertia, viscous, gravitational, compressibility, surface tension, and pressure forces.
  • Governing Law: Buckingham $\pi$ theorem dictates that physical phenomena depending on $n$ variables are reducible to $n-m$ dimensionless similarity groups.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid only when Knudsen number $Kn = \lambda / L < 0.01$, ensuring molecular mean free path is negligible compared to characteristic length.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\rho$ = Fluid density ($\text{kg/m}^3$)
  • $v$ = Flow velocity or free-stream speed ($\text{m/s}$)
  • $L$ = Characteristic length or chord ($\text{m}$)
  • $\mu$ = Dynamic viscosity ($\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$)
  • $g$ = Acceleration due to gravity ($\text{m/s}^2$)
  • $a$ = Speed of sound in medium ($\text{m/s}$)
  • $\sigma$ = Surface tension coefficient ($\text{N/m}$)
  • $\Delta p$ = Pressure differential ($\text{Pa}$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Molecular momentum exchange and thermal agitation dictate macroscopic fluid viscosity, acoustic propagation speed, and interfacial tension.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Local pressure gradients, shear stress distributions, gravitational body forces, and surface curvature maintain dynamic equilibrium across fluid control volumes.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: The relative magnitude of these forces dictates boundary layer separation, shockwave formation, wave drag, spray generation, and cavitation inception.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Equating inertia force to viscous, gravity, compressibility, surface tension, and pressure forces respectively.

$$F_i = \rho L^2 v^2, \quad F_v = \mu L v, \quad F_g = \rho L^3 g, \quad F_c = K L^2, \quad F_s = \sigma L, \quad F_p = \Delta p L^2$$
Step 2: Force Ratio Formulation

Forming dimensionless ratios by dividing characteristic inertia force by individual force fields.

$$Re = \frac{F_i}{F_v} = \frac{\rho L^2 v^2}{\mu L v}, \quad Fr = \sqrt{\frac{F_i}{F_g}} = \frac{v}{\sqrt{g L}}, \quad Ma = \sqrt{\frac{F_i}{F_c}} = \frac{v}{\sqrt{K/\rho}}$$
Step 3: Substitution of Thermodynamic Speed of Sound

Replacing bulk modulus $K$ with acoustic propagation speed $a = \sqrt{\gamma R T}$ for compressible fluids.

$$Ma = \frac{v}{\sqrt{\gamma R T}} = \frac{v}{a}, \quad We = \frac{F_i}{F_s} = \frac{\rho v^2 L}{\sigma}, \quad Eu = \frac{F_p}{F_i} = \frac{\Delta p}{\rho v^2}$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Obtaining final dimensionless similarity criteria governing scale-model testing in wind tunnels and tow tanks.

$$Re = \frac{\rho v L}{\mu}, \quad Fr = \frac{v}{\sqrt{g L}}, \quad Ma = \frac{v}{a}, \quad We = \frac{\rho v^2 L}{\sigma}, \quad Eu = \frac{\Delta p}{\rho v^2}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: All five dimensionless numbers are purely dimensionless with dimensional representation $[M^0 L^0 T^0]$.
  • SI Unit: Dimensionless numbers carry no physical units; individual constituent terms use SI base units like $\text{kg}$, $\text{m}$, and $\text{s}$.
  • CGS Unit: Dimensionless ratios remain invariant under CGS conversion systems (grams, centimeters, seconds).
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Velocities in knots, lengths in feet, pressures in psi, and viscosities in slug/(ft·s) yield identical dimensionless values when converted consistently.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2 Airworthiness and ICAO Annex 6 mandate strict adherence to maximum operating Mach number ($M_{MO}$) and airspeed limits ($V_{MO}$).
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Compressibility effects alter pitot-static calibration and flight control hinge moments, requiring precise Mach trim compensator operation.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: High-altitude Mach tuck incidents (e.g., severe transonic upsets) stem from supersonic shockwave formation moving aft, causing nose-down pitching moments that exceed elevator authority.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Reynolds Number Scale Effect

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming wind tunnel models tested at the exact same velocity as flight achieve full Reynolds number similarity without scaling.

✅ UPSC Truth: Full dynamic similarity requires $Re_{model} = Re_{flight}$, necessitating pressurized wind tunnels or cryogenic nitrogen to match viscosity and density ratios.

Trap 2: Mach Number Incompressibility Limit

❌ Examiner Trap: Treating air as compressible at all Mach numbers because it is a gas.

✅ UPSC Truth: Fluid compressibility is negligible below $Ma \le 0.3$, allowing density changes to be ignored and Bernoulli's incompressible equations to apply safely.

Trap 3: Froude vs. Reynolds Concurrency

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing aircraft in flight must maintain Froude number similitude alongside Mach number similitude.

✅ UPSC Truth: Froude number ($Fr$) governs free-surface gravity waves (seaplanes, water takeoffs), whereas Mach number ($Ma$) governs high-speed flight; both cannot be simultaneously satisfied in wind tunnels.

Trap 4: Weber Number Relevance in Flight

❌ Examiner Trap: Neglecting Weber number entirely in standard transport category aircraft design.

✅ UPSC Truth: Weber number is vital for aircraft fuel atomizer design, combustion chamber droplet breakup, and heavy rain ingestion performance during takeoff.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A transport aircraft cruises at FL350 ($T = 218.8 \text{ K}$, $a = 296.5 \text{ m/s}$, $\rho = 0.380 \text{ kg/m}^3$, $\mu = 1.44 \times 10^{-5} \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$) with a true airspeed of $250 \text{ m/s}$ and a mean aerodynamic chord of $4.2 \text{ m}$. Calculate its flight Mach number and Reynolds number.

Given Flight Parameters:
Velocity $v = 250 \text{ m/s}$, Chord $L = 4.2 \text{ m}$, Density $\rho = 0.380 \text{ kg/m}^3$, Viscosity $\mu = 1.44 \times 10^{-5} \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, Speed of sound $a = 296.5 \text{ m/s}$.
Governing Formula:
$$Ma = \frac{v}{a}, \quad Re = \frac{\rho v L}{\mu}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute Mach number: $Ma = \frac{250}{296.5} = 0.843$. Step 2: Compute Reynolds number numerator: $\rho v L = 0.380 \times 250 \times 4.2 = 399.0 \text{ kg/s}$. Step 3: Compute final Reynolds number: $Re = \frac{399.0}{1.44 \times 10^{-5}} = 2.77 \times 10^7$.
Final Answer: Mach Number $Ma = 0.843$ (Transonic regime), Reynolds Number $Re = 2.77 \times 10^7$.
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Operating at $Ma = 0.843$ places the aircraft near its critical Mach number where localized supersonic flow and shockwave boundary layer interactions demand rigorous monitoring by air safety officers.

Question 1 • conceptual
Which dimensionless parameter represents the ratio of inertial forces to viscous forces in a fluid flow?
A Mach number
B Reynolds number
C Froude number
D Weber number
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Reynolds number ($Re = \rho v L / \mu$) is defined specifically as the ratio of inertial forces to viscous forces.
Question 2 • conceptual
At what threshold of Mach number is a gas flow typically treated as incompressible in aerodynamic analysis?
A Ma \le 0.1
B Ma \le 0.3
C Ma \le 0.5
D Ma \le 1.0
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Density changes remain below 5% for Mach numbers up to $0.3$, allowing the incompressible flow assumption to hold.
Question 3 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding dimensionless numbers: 1. Froude number is paramount in analyzing seaplane hull hydrodynamics. 2. Weber number evaluates surface tension effects in fuel atomization. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. Froude number governs free-surface gravity waves, and Weber number governs surface tension and droplet breakup.
Question 4 • numerical
An aircraft model has a characteristic length of $2 \text{ m}$ tested in a wind tunnel where velocity is $50 \text{ m/s}$. If kinematic viscosity $\nu = 1.5 \times 10^{-5} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$, what is the Reynolds number?
A $3.33 \times 10^4$
B $6.67 \times 10^6$
C $1.50 \times 10^5$
D $1.00 \times 10^7$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $Re = v L / \nu = (50 \times 2) / (1.5 \times 10^{-5}) = 100 / (1.5 \times 10^{-5}) = 6.67 \times 10^6$.
Question 5 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): It is impossible to achieve simultaneous Mach and Reynolds number similitude in a standard atmospheric wind tunnel for a scaled aircraft model. Reason (R): Dynamic viscosity and kinematic viscosity cannot be arbitrarily scaled independently of density and temperature in real air.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both assertion and reason are correct, explaining why cryogenic wind tunnels are required to match both Mach and Reynolds numbers.
Question 6 • conceptual
The Euler number is formed by the ratio of which two fluid forces?
A Inertia force to viscous force
B Pressure force to inertia force
C Gravity force to surface tension
D Elastic force to inertia force
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Euler number ($Eu = \Delta p / \rho v^2$) represents the ratio of pressure forces to inertial forces.
Question 7 • conceptual
Which dimensionless number governs the study of shockwave formation and transonic compressible aerodynamics?
A Froude number
B Reynolds number
C Mach number
D Weber number
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Mach number ($Ma = v/a$) is the primary governing parameter for compressibility and shockwave phenomena.
Question 8 • numerical
Calculate the acoustic speed in standard air at sea level where $\gamma = 1.4$, gas constant $R = 287 \text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$, and temperature $T = 288.15 \text{ K}$.
A $310.2 \text{ m/s}$
B $340.3 \text{ m/s}$
C $360.5 \text{ m/s}$
D $295.1 \text{ m/s}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
$a = \sqrt{\gamma R T} = \sqrt{1.4 \times 287 \times 288.15} = \sqrt{115848.7} = 340.3 \text{ m/s}$.
Question 9 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding Mach number regimes: 1. Subsonic flow occurs when $Ma < 1.0$ everywhere. 2. Transonic flow features mixed regions of subsonic and supersonic local velocities. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements accurately describe subsonic and transonic flight regimes encountered by modern civil transport aircraft.
Question 10 • conceptual
What is the physical significance of the Froude number in aeronautics and fluid engineering?
A Ratio of inertia force to gravitational body force
B Ratio of viscous shear to surface tension
C Ratio of pressure differential to dynamic pressure
D Ratio of acoustic velocity to flow velocity
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Froude number ($Fr = v / \sqrt{g L}$) represents the square root of the ratio of inertial forces to gravitational forces.
Question 11 • conceptual
In gas turbine combustion chambers, droplet breakup and fuel spray atomization are primarily scaled using which dimensionless number?
A Reynolds number
B Weber number
C Mach number
D Euler number
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Weber number ($We = \rho v^2 L / \sigma$) measures inertial disruptive forces against surface tension holding liquid droplets together.
Question 12 • conceptual
According to the Buckingham \(\pi\) theorem, if a physical problem involves $n$ dimensional variables and $m$ fundamental dimensions, how many dimensionless $\pi$ groups are formed?
A $m$
B $n$
C $n - m$
D $n + m$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Buckingham $\pi$ theorem states that the number of independent dimensionless groups is exactly $n - m$.

Which of the following dimensionless numbers is defined as the square root of the ratio of inertia force to elastic force?
A Reynolds number
B Mach number
C Euler number

Rationale:
Mach number is defined as $v/a$, where $a = \sqrt{K/\rho}$ represents the speed of sound derived from fluid elasticity (bulk modulus $K$).
Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
For a fully developed laminar pipe flow, the friction factor $f$ is inversely proportional to which dimensionless parameter?
A Mach number
B Froude number
C Reynolds number
D Weber number
Rationale:
In laminar pipe flow, Darcy friction factor is given by $f = 64 / Re$, making it inversely proportional to Reynolds number.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the primary reason why large transport aircraft cannot maintain strict Reynolds number similarity when tested in small wind tunnels at atmospheric pressure?
A Mach number would exceed unity
B Required wind tunnel velocity would need to be impractically high to compensate for model length reduction
C Gravitational acceleration varies inside wind tunnels
D Surface tension becomes zero
Rationale:
Since $Re = v L / \nu$, reducing characteristic length $L$ requires an inversely proportional increase in velocity $v$, leading to compressibility choking.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
In an open-channel water tow tank or hydrofoil testing facility, dynamic similitude strictly requires the matching of which dimensionless number?
A Mach number
B Reynolds number
C Froude number
D Weber number
Rationale:
Free-surface gravity flows (such as flying boat hulls during takeoff) are governed by Froude number similitude.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
What does an Euler number of $Eu = 1.0$ signify in a fluid flow field?
A Pressure forces equal viscous shear forces
B Pressure drop equals dynamic pressure
C Inertia forces equal gravitational forces
D Acoustic speed equals flow speed
Rationale:
Since $Eu = \Delta p / (\rho v^2)$, an Euler number of $1.0$ means the static pressure drop equals the dynamic pressure head.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
During high-altitude high-Mach number flight, why does boundary layer transition shift upstream compared to low-speed conditions?
A Increase in local Reynolds number due to temperature drop
B Compressibility and aerodynamic heating destabilize the laminar boundary layer
C Surface tension increases at altitude
D Gravity decreases at high altitudes
Rationale:
Compressibility effects, adverse pressure gradients, and aerodynamic heating at high Mach numbers promote earlier transition to turbulence.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
Which of the following describes the correct mathematical expression for the Weber number?
A $\frac{\rho v L}{\mu}$
B $\frac{v}{\sqrt{g L}}$
C $\frac{\rho v^2 L}{\sigma}$
D $\frac{\Delta p}{\rho v^2}$
Rationale:
Weber number is defined as $We = \rho v^2 L / \sigma$, representing inertia force divided by surface tension force.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
If an aircraft flies at $M = 2.0$ at an altitude where air temperature is $216.7 \text{ K}$ ($\gamma = 1.4$, $R = 287 \text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$), what is its true airspeed?
A $295.1 \text{ m/s}$
B $590.2 \text{ m/s}$
C $680.4 \text{ m/s}$
D $440.0 \text{ m/s}$
Rationale:
Speed of sound $a = \sqrt{1.4 \times 287 \times 216.7} = 295.1 \text{ m/s}$. True airspeed $v = Ma \times a = 2.0 \times 295.1 = 590.2 \text{ m/s}$.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
What physical phenomenon occurs when the local Mach number on an aircraft wing upper surface reaches $1.0$?
A Engine flameout
B Formation of sonic shockwaves and local flow separation
C Complete loss of hydraulic pressure
D Zero lift generation
Rationale:
Reaching local sonic velocity ($Ma = 1.0$) marks the critical Mach number where shockwaves and wave drag initiate.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
In dimensional analysis, how are secondary dimensionless parameters like Reynolds and Mach numbers derived from primary physical quantities?
A By empirical guesswork during flight testing
B By systematically applying the Buckingham \(\pi\) theorem to governing fluid dynamic equations
C By integrating Navier-Stokes equations analytically without assumptions
D By measuring pitot-static pressures in wind tunnels
Rationale:
Dimensionless parameters are rigorously derived using the Buckingham $\pi$ theorem or by nondimensionalizing the Navier-Stokes equations.
