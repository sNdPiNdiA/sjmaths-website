Newtonian & non-Newtonian fluids

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Fluid viscosity directly impacts hydraulic actuator response times and FADEC fuel metering accuracy across wide temperature ranges.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Incorrect fuel or lubricant viscosity under cold-soak Arctic conditions can cause pump cavitation, fuel starvation, or bearing seizure.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 mandates strict compliance with fluid specifications outlined in aircraft maintenance manuals and approved flight manuals.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers investigate contamination, viscosity breakdown, and thermal degradation during engine failure and hydraulic lockout root-cause analyses.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Fluids are continuous media that deform perpetually under an applied shear stress, governed by Newton's law of viscosity: $$\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy}$$
  • Physical Meaning: Fluids cannot sustain static shear forces, resulting in continuous angular deformation when subjected to any tangential force.
  • Governing Law: Newtonian fluids exhibit a constant dynamic viscosity $\mu$ independent of the shear rate magnitude.
  • Continuum Boundary: Fluid mechanics assumes macroscopic properties vary continuously, breaking down only when the Knudsen number exceeds 0.01.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\tau$ = Shear stress within the fluid layer ($N/m^2$)
  • $\mu$ = Dynamic viscosity coefficient ($kg/(m\cdot s)$)
  • $\frac{du}{dy}$ = Velocity gradient or shear rate ($s^{-1}$)
  • $\nu$ = Kinematic viscosity ($m^2/s$)
  • $\rho$ = Fluid mass density ($kg/m^3$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Molecules in liquids are bound by cohesive intermolecular forces, whereas gases rely on rapid random molecular translation and momentum exchange.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: When a velocity gradient exists, momentum transfers across adjacent fluid layers, generating internal frictional resistance quantified as shear stress.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Viscous shear stress directly dictates boundary layer formation, skin friction drag on aircraft lifting surfaces, and internal pipe flow losses.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Proportionality between shear stress and velocity gradient.

$$\tau \propto \frac{du}{dy}$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Introduction of the constant coefficient of dynamic viscosity $\mu$.

$$\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy}$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Applying no-slip condition at solid boundaries where fluid velocity equals wall velocity.

$$u(0) = 0, \quad u(h) = U$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Linear velocity profile for Newtonian fluids between parallel plates.

$$u(y) = U \frac{y}{h}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Dynamic viscosity $\mu$ has dimensions $[M^1 L^{-1} T^{-1}]$, derived from force divided by area over velocity gradient.
  • SI Unit: Pascal-second ($Pa\cdot s$) or $kg/(m\cdot s)$ for dynamic viscosity.
  • CGS Unit: Poise ($P$), where $1 \text{ Poise} = 0.1 \text{ } Pa\cdot s$, and centipoise ($cP$) is widely used in aviation.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Kinematic viscosity is measured in Stokes ($St$) or centistokes ($cSt$), where $1 \text{ cSt} = 10^{-6} \text{ m}^2/s$.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Series 'O' Part III and ICAO Annex 8 govern airworthiness standards for aircraft fluid systems and quality control.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Viscosity Temperature Dependence

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming liquids and gases behave identically regarding temperature changes.

✅ UPSC Truth: Liquid viscosity decreases with increasing temperature, whereas gas viscosity increases with temperature due to increased molecular collision frequency.

Trap 2: Pseudoplastic vs Dilatant Definition

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing shear-thinning (pseudoplastic) with shear-thickening (dilatant) fluids.

✅ UPSC Truth: Pseudoplastic fluids show decreasing apparent viscosity with shear rate, while dilatant fluids exhibit increasing viscosity under high shear.

Trap 3: Kinematic vs Dynamic Viscosity Units

❌ Examiner Trap: Using dynamic viscosity units in kinematic viscosity calculations or vice versa.

✅ UPSC Truth: Kinematic viscosity is dynamic viscosity divided by density ($\nu = \mu / \rho$), with units of $m^2/s$.

Trap 4: Ideal Fluid Assumption

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming inviscid flow applies universally across all aircraft boundary layers.

✅ UPSC Truth: Inviscid flow is an approximation ($μ = 0$) valid only outside boundary layers; viscosity is essential for generating skin friction drag and lift separation.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A hydraulic actuator cylinder of diameter $50 \text{ mm}$ contains Skydrol LD-4 hydraulic fluid at $40^{\circ}\text{C}$. A close-fitting inner piston of length $100 \text{ mm}$ moves at a velocity of $2.5 \text{ m/s}$ inside a cylinder with a uniform radial clearance gap of $0.1 \text{ mm}$. Calculate the viscous drag force acting on the piston.

Given Flight Parameters:
Diameter $D = 0.05 \text{ m}$, Length $L = 0.1 \text{ m}$, Velocity $U = 2.5 \text{ m/s}$, Clearance $h = 0.0001 \text{ m}$, Dynamic Viscosity $\mu = 0.0105 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$.
Governing Formula:
$$F_v = \tau \cdot A = \left( \mu \frac{U}{h} \right) \cdot (\pi D L)$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute shear rate in the annular clearance gap: $\frac{U}{h} = \frac{2.5}{0.0001} = 25,000 \text{ s}^{-1}$. Step 2: Calculate shear stress using Newton's law: $\tau = 0.0105 \times 25,000 = 262.5 \text{ N/m}^2$. Step 3: Compute wetted surface area: $A = \pi D L = \pi \times 0.05 \times 0.1 = 0.015708 \text{ m}^2$. Step 4: Multiply shear stress by area for total viscous drag force: $F_v = 262.5 \times 0.015708 = 4.124 \text{ N}$.
Final Answer: $4.124 N$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate calculation of viscous friction forces in hydraulic actuators ensures proper sizing of flight control servos and prevents sluggish control responses during critical flight maneuvers.

Question 1 • conceptual
Which of the following fluids exhibits a linear relationship between shear stress and velocity gradient?
A Bingham plastic
B Newtonian fluid
C Pseudoplastic fluid
D Dilatant fluid
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Newtonian fluids obey Newton's law of viscosity where shear stress is directly proportional to the velocity gradient with a constant dynamic viscosity.
Question 2 • conceptual
How does the dynamic viscosity of air change with an increase in absolute temperature?
A Increases
B Decreases
C Remains constant
D Drops to zero
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Gases exhibit an increase in dynamic viscosity with temperature due to increased molecular thermal velocity and momentum transfer across layers.
Question 3 • conceptual
What is the physical significance of kinematic viscosity?
A Ratio of dynamic viscosity to density
B Product of density and dynamic viscosity
C Ratio of shear force to normal pressure
D Measure of bulk modulus
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Kinematic viscosity $\nu = \mu / \rho$ represents the diffusion of momentum through a fluid relative to its inertia.
Question 4 • conceptual
Toothpaste and drilling mud are classic examples of which type of non-Newtonian fluid?
A Dilatant fluid
B Newtonian fluid
C Bingham plastic
D Pseudoplastic fluid
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Bingham plastics require a finite yield stress before they begin to flow and exhibit linear viscosity thereafter.
Question 5 • conceptual
What is the dimensional formula for dynamic viscosity $\mu$?
A $[ML^{-1}T^{-1}]$
B $[MLT^{-2}]$
C $[ML^{-2}T^{-1}]$
D $[M^0L^2T^{-1}]$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Dynamic viscosity is force per unit area divided by velocity gradient, yielding dimensions $[ML^{-1}T^{-1}]$.
Question 6 • conceptual
Which non-Newtonian fluid category exhibits shear-thinning behavior without any initial yield stress?
A Pseudoplastic
B Dilatant
C Bingham plastic
D Thixotropic
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Pseudoplastic fluids show decreasing viscosity with increasing shear rate without needing a yield threshold.
Question 7 • conceptual
What effect does fluid temperature increase have on the dynamic viscosity of aviation lubricating oils?
A Viscosity increases significantly
B Viscosity decreases significantly
C Viscosity remains completely unchanged
D Viscosity becomes negative
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Liquid lubricants experience weakened intermolecular cohesive forces at higher temperatures, causing a sharp drop in dynamic viscosity.
Question 8 • conceptual
In the context of boundary layer theory, what condition applies at a solid aerodynamic surface?
A Slip condition ($u = U_{\infty}$)
B No-slip condition ($u = 0$)
C Maximum velocity condition
D Zero shear stress condition
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Viscous fluids adhere to solid boundaries, enforcing the no-slip condition where relative velocity between fluid and wall is zero.
Question 9 • conceptual
Which parameter characterizes the relative importance of viscous forces to inertial forces in fluid flow?
A Mach number
B Reynolds number
C Prandtl number
D Froude number
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The Reynolds number represents the ratio of inertial forces to viscous forces within a fluid flow field.
Question 10 • conceptual
What is the SI unit of kinematic viscosity?
A $kg/(m\cdot s)$
B $Pa\cdot s$
C $m^2/s$
D $N/m^2$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Kinematic viscosity has dimensions of $L^2/T$, yielding the SI unit $m^2/s$.
Question 11 • conceptual
Consider the following statements regarding non-Newtonian fluids: 1. Apparent viscosity depends on the shear rate. 2. They do not obey Newton's law of viscosity. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Non-Newtonian fluids deviate from Newton's law, and their apparent viscosity varies as a function of the applied shear rate.
Question 12 • conceptual
Assertion (A): Lubricating oil viscosity must be carefully selected for jet turbine engines operating at high altitudes. Reason (R): Extremely low temperatures at cruise altitudes can cause oil viscosity to rise excessively, risking pump cavitation or bearing failure.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both assertion and reason are correct; cold soaking at high altitudes increases oil viscosity, presenting severe operational risks for turbine lubrication.

Question 1 of 10 +3 / -1
A fluid with shear stress $\tau$ and velocity gradient $du/dy$ has a constitutive equation given by $\tau = A(du/dy)^n + \tau_0$. For a Bingham plastic, what are the values of $n$ and $\tau_0$?
A $n = 1, \tau_0 > 0$
B $n > 1, \tau_0 = 0$
C $n < 1, \tau_0 = 0$
D $n = 1, \tau_0 = 0$
Rationale:
A Bingham plastic exhibits linear behavior ($n=1$) after surpassing a distinct positive yield stress threshold ($\tau_0 > 0$).
Question 2 of 10 +3 / -1
Calculate the kinematic viscosity of an aviation fluid having a dynamic viscosity of $0.002 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ and a mass density of $800 \text{ kg/m}^3$.
A $1.6 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$
B $2.5 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$
C $4.0 \times 10^{-5} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$
D $1.0 \times 10^{-5} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$
Rationale:
Kinematic viscosity $\nu = \mu / \rho = 0.002 / 800 = 2.5 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$.
Question 3 of 10 +3 / -1
Which of the following dimensionless numbers represents the ratio of momentum diffusivity to thermal diffusivity?
A Reynolds number
B Nusselt number
C Prandtl number
D Grashof number
Rationale:
The Prandtl number ($Pr = \nu / \alpha$) relates viscous boundary layer thickness to thermal boundary layer thickness.
Question 4 of 10 +3 / -1
A flat plate is pulled at $2 \text{ m/s}$ over a stationary flat plate separated by a $1 \text{ mm}$ oil film ($\mu = 0.2 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$). What is the resulting shear stress?
A $100 \text{ N/m}^2$
B $200 \text{ N/m}^2$
C $400 \text{ N/m}^2$
D $50 \text{ N/m}^2$
Rationale:
Shear stress $\tau = \mu (U/h) = 0.2 \times (2 / 0.001) = 400 \text{ N/m}^2$.
Question 5 of 10 +3 / -1
Thixotropic fluids exhibit which of the following time-dependent rheological characteristics?
A Viscosity increases with time under constant shear
B Viscosity decreases with time under constant shear
C Viscosity remains constant indefinitely
D Viscosity fluctuates sinusoidally
Rationale:
Thixotropic fluids show time-dependent shear-thinning behavior where apparent viscosity decreases under continuous applied shear.
Question 6 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the primary physical mechanism responsible for viscosity in liquids?
A Molecular momentum exchange via free translation
B Intermolecular cohesive forces and momentum transfer
C Magnetic dipole interactions
D Electrostatic repulsion
Rationale:
Liquid viscosity is primarily governed by intermolecular cohesive forces between adjacent molecular layers.
Question 7 of 10 +3 / -1
In aviation engineering, why are multigrade oils preferred over single-grade oils for turbine engines?
A They have zero viscosity at high temperatures
B They maintain stable viscosity across a wider operating temperature range
C They eliminate the need for oil filters
D They completely prevent oxidation
Rationale:
Multigrade oils use viscosity index improvers to resist excessive thinning at high operating temperatures while remaining fluid at cold startup.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
If the velocity distribution in a laminar boundary layer over a flat plate is approximated as $u(y) = U(2(y/\delta) - (y/\delta)^2)$, what is the shear stress at the wall ($y = 0$)?
A $\frac{2\mu U}{\delta}$
B $\frac{\mu U}{\delta}$
C $\frac{4\mu U}{\delta}$
D Zero
Rationale:
Differentiating $u(y)$ gives $\frac{du}{dy} = U(\frac{2}{\delta} - \frac{2y}{\delta^2})$. At $y=0$, $\frac{du}{dy} = \frac{2U}{\delta}$, hence $\tau_w = \frac{2\mu U}{\delta}$.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
Which statement accurately contrasts Newtonian and ideal fluids?
A Newtonian fluids have zero viscosity; ideal fluids have constant viscosity
B Newtonian fluids have constant nonzero viscosity; ideal fluids are frictionless ($\mu = 0$)
C Both have identical viscosity coefficients
D Ideal fluids are compressible; Newtonian fluids are incompressible
Rationale:
An ideal fluid is strictly inviscid ($\mu = 0$), whereas a Newtonian fluid has a definite constant dynamic viscosity coefficient.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
During an airworthiness audit, an inspector notes abnormal thickening of hydraulic fluid in aircraft control systems. Which rheological anomaly is most likely indicated?
A Reopectic or dilatant contamination
B Ideal Newtonian behavior
C Perfect thermal equilibrium
D Zero-viscosity state
Rationale:
Abnormal thickening under shear or stress points toward rheopectic or dilatant contamination compromising fluid operational integrity.
