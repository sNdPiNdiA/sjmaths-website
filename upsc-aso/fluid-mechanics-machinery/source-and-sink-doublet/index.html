Doublet

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Pitot-static probes and airspeed transducers rely on stagnation and doublet-like pressure field assumptions around cylindrical tubes.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Accurate flow separation and pressure modeling prevent unverified aerodynamic stalls during high-alpha aircraft maneuver envelopes.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 mandate rigorous validation of aerodynamic design data and structural load limits.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers review pressure sensor calibration anomalies and local flow distortions during incident root-cause investigations.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

A two-dimensional doublet is formed by the limiting combination of an equal source and sink of strength $\\Lambda$, separated by an infinitesimal distance $ds$, such that the product $\\mu = \\Lambda ds$ remains constant. The complex potential for a 2D doublet of strength $\\mu$ oriented along the x-axis is given by $w(z) = \\frac{\\mu}{2\\pi z}$.
  • Physical Meaning: Represents a localized source of fluid momentum without net mass injection, modeling the fundamental singularity of a circular cylinder in uniform flow.
  • Governing Law: Governed by potential flow theory, satisfying the Laplace equation $\\nabla^2 \\phi = 0$ for incompressible, irrotational flow fields.
  • Continuum Boundary: Valid strictly in inviscid, irrotational fluid regions outside viscous boundary layers and wake zones.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $\\mu$ = Doublet strength or moment ($m^2/s$)
  • $\\Lambda$ = Source or sink strength ($m^2/s$)
  • $ds$ = Infinitesimal separation distance between source and sink ($m$)
  • $r, \\theta$ = Polar coordinates for radial and angular positions ($m$, rad)
  • $\\phi$ = Velocity potential ($m^2/s$)
  • $\\psi$ = Stream function ($m^2/s$)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Molecular Kinetic State: Fluid parcels maintain continuous momentum exchange while macroscopically conforming to potential flow constraints without viscous dissipation.
2. Continuum Forces & Gradients: Pressure gradients and velocity fields are symmetric and governed entirely by spatial derivatives of the velocity potential.
3. Aerodynamic Consequence: Superimposing a uniform stream onto a doublet generates the exact pressure distribution and streamlines around a circular cylinder.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Governing Relation

Combine complex potentials of a source at $-ds/2$ and sink at $+ds/2$.

$$w(z) = \\frac{\\Lambda}{2\\pi} \\ln\u0028z - \\frac{ds}{2}\u0029 - \\frac{\\Lambda}{2\\pi} \\ln\u0028z + \\frac{ds}{2}\u0029$$
Step 2: Boundary / Thermodynamic Conditions

Apply Taylor series expansion for infinitesimal separation $ds \\to 0$.

$$w(z) = \\frac{\\Lambda}{2\\pi} \\left[ \\ln z - \\frac{ds}{2z} - \\left(\\ln z + \\frac{ds}{2z}\u0029 \\right] = - \\frac{\\Lambda ds}{2\\pi z}$$
Step 3: Integration & Substitution

Substitute doublet strength $\\mu = \\Lambda ds$ into the expanded expression.

$$w(z) = - \\frac{\\mu}{2\\pi z} = - \\frac{\\mu}{2\\pi r e^{i\\theta}} = - \\frac{\\mu}{2\\pi r} \\left(\\cos\\theta - i\\sin\\theta\\right)$$
Step 4: Final Working Law

Extract real and imaginary parts to obtain velocity potential and stream function.

$$\\phi = - \\frac{\\mu \\cos\\theta}{2\\pi r}, \\quad \\psi = - \\frac{\\mu \\sin\\theta}{2\\pi r}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: $[M^0 L^2 T^{-1} \\theta^0]$ derived from velocity multiplied by area ($[L/T] \cdot [L^2]$ per unit span).
  • SI Unit: Square meters per second ($m^2/s$).
  • CGS Unit: Square centimeters per second ($cm^2/s$), with a conversion factor of $1 \\text{ m}^2/\text{s} = 10^4 \\text{ cm}^2/\text{s}$.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Expressed in square feet per second ($ft^2/s$) for US standard atmosphere calculations ($1 \\text{ m}^2/\text{s} = 10.764 \\text{ ft}^2/\text{s}$).

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): Compliance with DGCA CAR Section 2 Airworthiness and ICAO Annex 6 governs operational testing of aerodynamic pressure instruments.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Directly impacts pitot-static systems, angle of attack (AoA) vanes, and engine cowl pressure distribution monitoring.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: In Aeroflot Flight 593 and Air France Flight 447, pitot probe icing corrupted local pressure fields, leading to erroneous airspeed singularities and catastrophic autopilot disconnects.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: Doublet Net Mass Flow

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming a doublet injects net mass into the control volume because it contains a source.

✅ UPSC Truth: A doublet has a net source strength of zero since the source strength equals the sink strength.

Trap 2: Velocity Potential Sign Convention

❌ Examiner Trap: Neglecting the negative sign in the complex potential expression for a standard doublet.

✅ UPSC Truth: The complex potential for a doublet oriented along the positive x-axis is $w(z) = -\\mu / \\left(2\\pi z\\right)$.

Trap 3: Superposition with Uniform Flow

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing the combination of uniform flow and doublet with a Rankine oval.

✅ UPSC Truth: A uniform stream combined with a doublet produces a circular cylinder, whereas a source-sink pair combined with uniform flow produces a Rankine oval.

Trap 4: Radial Velocity at Cylinder Surface

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming radial velocity is non-zero at the surface of a cylinder modeled by a doublet.

✅ UPSC Truth: The radial velocity $v_r$ is identically zero at the cylinder radius $r = R$, satisfying the solid wall boundary condition.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

A two-dimensional doublet of strength $\\mu = 50 \\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$ is placed in a uniform free stream of velocity $V_5 = 20 \\text{ m/s}$ flowing in the positive x-direction. Calculate the radius of the resulting cylinder and the maximum tangential velocity on its surface.

Given Flight Parameters:
Doublet strength $\\mu = 50 \\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$, Free stream velocity $V_5 = 20 \\text{ m/s}$
Governing Formula:
$$R = \\sqrt{\\frac{\\mu}{2\\pi V_5}}, \quad V_{\\theta,\\text{max}} = 2 V_5$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute the radius of the cylinder using $R = \\sqrt{\\frac{\\mu}{2\\pi V_5}} = \\sqrt{\\frac{50}{2 \\pi \\cdot 20}} = \\sqrt{\\frac{50}{40\\pi}} = \\sqrt{0.3979} = 0.6307 \\text{ m}$. Step 2: Determine the maximum tangential velocity on the cylinder surface using $V_{\\theta,\\text{max}} = 2 V_5 = 2 \\cdot 20 = 40 \\text{ m/s}$. Step 3: Verify boundary conditions; radial velocity vanishes at $r = R$.
Final Answer: Cylinder radius $R = 0.631 \\text{ m}$, Maximum tangential velocity $V_{\\theta,\\text{max}} = 40.0 \\text{ m/s}$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate calculation of surface velocity peaks enables air safety officers and aerodynamicists to predict localized supersonic flow regions and critical Mach number limits during high-speed subsonic flight.

Question 1 • conceptual
Which of the following fundamental singularity elements forms a 2D doublet when combined in the limit of zero separation?
A A source and a vortex of equal strength
B A source and a sink of equal strength
C Two vortices of equal magnitude and opposite sign
D A uniform stream and a free vortex
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A doublet is created by the limiting case of an equal source and sink brought infinitesimally close together while maintaining a constant product of strength and distance.
Question 2 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding a two-dimensional doublet: 1. The stream function $\\psi$ varies inversely with radial distance $r$. 2. The net mass flux emitted by a doublet is zero. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. The stream function $\\psi = -\\frac{\\mu \\sin\\theta}{2\\pi r}$ varies inversely with $r$, and net mass flux is zero because source and sink strengths are equal.
Question 3 • numerical
A doublet of strength $20\\pi \\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$ is placed in a uniform stream of $10 \\text{ m/s}$. What is the radius of the resulting circular cylinder?
A $0.5 \\text{ m}$
B $1.0 \\text{ m}$
C $2.0 \\text{ m}$
D $4.0 \\text{ m}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $R = \\sqrt{\\frac{\\mu}{2\\pi V_5}} = \\sqrt{\\frac{20\\pi}{2\\pi \\cdot 10}} = \\sqrt{1} = 1.0 \\text{ m}$.
Question 4 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): The radial velocity component vanishes at the surface of a circular cylinder formed by a doublet in uniform flow. Reason (R): The cylinder surface coincides with the zero-streamline of the combined flow field.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The cylinder surface is a streamline ($\\psi = 0$), meaning fluid flows tangentially along it, resulting in zero radial velocity component ($v_r = 0$).
Question 5 • conceptual
What is the physical dimension of a two-dimensional doublet strength $\\mu$?
A $m/s$
B $m^2/s$
C $m^3/s$
D $m^2/s^2$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Doublet strength $\\mu$ is the product of source strength ($m^2/s$) and distance ($m$), divided by length in derivation, yielding $m^2/s$ for 2D flow.
Question 6 • statement
Which statement best describes the pressure distribution on a circular cylinder generated by a doublet in uniform flow? 1. Maximum pressure occurs at the stagnation points. 2. Minimum pressure occurs at the crown of the cylinder ($\\theta = \\pm 90^\\circ$).
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
By Bernoulli's equation, maximum pressure occurs at stagnation points where velocity is zero, and minimum pressure occurs where tangential velocity reaches its maximum.
Question 7 • numerical
If a doublet of strength $\\mu = 100 \\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$ is placed in a uniform stream, and the resulting cylinder radius is $2 \\text{ m}$, what is the free stream velocity $V_5$?
A $1.99 \\text{ m/s}$
B $3.98 \\text{ m/s}$
C $7.96 \\text{ m/s}$
D $15.92 \\text{ m/s}$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Rearranging $R = \\sqrt{\\frac{\\mu}{2\\pi V_5}}$ yields $V_5 = \\frac{\\mu}{2\\pi R^2} = \\frac{100}{2 \\pi \\cdot 4} = \\frac{100}{25.1327} \\approx 3.98 \\text{ m/s}$.
Question 8 • conceptual
In potential flow theory, what happens to the velocity potential $\\phi$ of a doublet as distance $r$ approaches infinity?
A Approaches infinity
B Approaches zero
C Remains constant at $\\mu$
D Oscillates periodically
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The velocity potential $\\phi = -\\frac{\\mu \\cos\\theta}{2\\pi r}$ varies inversely with $r$, approaching zero as $r \\to \\infty$.
Question 9 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Potential flow theory predicts zero aerodynamic drag on a circular cylinder represented by a doublet in a uniform stream. Reason (R): The pressure distribution around the cylinder is perfectly symmetric between upstream and downstream halves.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
D'Alembert's paradox states that inviscid flow around a symmetric body yields fore-and-aft pressure symmetry, resulting in zero net drag.
Question 10 • conceptual
Which of the following flow combinations produces a lifting circular cylinder (Magnus effect)?
A Uniform stream + Doublet
B Uniform stream + Source + Sink
C Uniform stream + Doublet + Vortex
D Source + Sink + Vortex
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Adding a vortex element to the uniform stream and doublet combination introduces circulation, generating lift via the Kutta-Joukowski theorem.
Question 11 • statement
Which of the following properties hold true for the streamlines of a 2D doublet? 1. They are circles passing through the origin. 2. Their centers lie on the y-axis for a doublet oriented along the x-axis.
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The stream function equation $\\psi = -\\frac{\\mu \\sin\\theta}{2\\pi r}$ describes a family of circles tangent to the x-axis at the origin with centers on the y-axis.

Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
