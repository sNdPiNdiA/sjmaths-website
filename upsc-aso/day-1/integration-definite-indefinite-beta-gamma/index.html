Integration – definite/indefinite, Beta-Gamma

Day 1 of 129 Engineering Fundamentals Phase 1: Foundations 60-90 min Master Cycle

1. Understand (5–10 min)

In Aeronautical Engineering and Air Safety Analysis, integral calculus serves as the foundation for modeling continuous physical phenomena, ranging from unsteady aerodynamic circulation and turbulent boundary layer momentum deficits to aircraft trajectory optimization and flight recorder data reconstruction. Under the UPSC Air Safety Officer (ASO) syllabus, the topic "Integration – Definite/Indefinite, Beta-Gamma" demands both rigorous analytical competency and precise functional manipulation.

1. Indefinite Integration (Antiderivatives):
An indefinite integral represents the general family of antiderivatives of a real-valued function $f(x)$. Formally, if $F(x)$ is differentiable on an open interval $I$ such that $ddx[F(x)] = f(x)$, then: $$∫ f(x) \, dx = F(x) + C$$ where $C \in \mathbb{R}$ is the arbitrary constant of integration. Indefinite integration maps a continuous function space to a family of shifted functions, serving as the inverse operator to differentiation up to an additive constant.

2. Definite Integration & Riemann Integration:
The definite integral evaluates the net accumulated measure (area, volume, impulse, circulation) of a function over a compact domain $[a, b]$. Defined as the limit of Riemann sums: $$∫ab f(x) \, dx = \lim\|P\| \to 0 ∑i=1n f(\xi_i) Δ x_i$$ provided the limit exists independently of the partition $P$ choice. By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (FTC), if $f(x)$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and $F'(x) = f(x)$, then $∫ab f(x) \, dx = F(b) - F(a)$. Definite integrals under improper bounds (where either limits extend to $± ∞$ or the integrand exhibits an essential singularity) require limit-based convergence definitions.

3. Euler's Gamma Function $\Gamma(n)$:
The Gamma function extends the factorial operator from non-negative integers to the complex plane (excluding non-positive integers). Defined as an improper integral of the second kind: $$\Gamma(n) = ∫0∞ xn-1 e-x \, dx, \quad for Re(n) > 0$$ It satisfies the foundational recurrence relation $\Gamma(n+1) = n \Gamma(n)$, yielding $\Gamma(n+1) = n!$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$. Its domain extends analytically to $\mathbb{C} \setminus \{0, -1, -2, \dots\}$ via functional equations, featuring simple poles at non-positive integers with residue $Res(\Gamma, -k) = (-1)^kk!$.

4. Euler's Beta Function $B(m, n)$:
The Beta function (Eulerian Integral of the First Kind) is a symmetric bivariate special function defined over $Re(m) > 0, Re(n) > 0$ as: $$B(m, n) = ∫01 xm-1 (1-x)n-1 \, dx$$ It acts as a normalized continuous weight distribution function, fundamentally connected to the Gamma function through the identity $B(m, n) = \Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)\Gamma(m+n)$. It frequently arises in compressible flow velocity distributions, structural reliability integrals, and probability distributions governing safety tolerances.

2. Learn the Concept (15–20 min)

To master advanced integral calculus for UPSC engineering examinations, candidates must understand the formal mathematical framework, transformation theorems, and analytical derivations connecting Eulerian special functions to trigonometric and algebraic integrals.

1. Structural Relations & Proofs of Special Functions

Derivation A: Fundamental Identity $B(m, n) = \Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)\Gamma(m+n)$

Consider the product of two independent Gamma integrals in dummy variables $x$ and $y$:

$$\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n) = ( ∫0∞ xm-1 e-x \, dx ) ( ∫0∞ yn-1 e-y \, dy )$$

Combining into a double integral over the first quadrant of the $xy$-plane:

$$\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n) = ∫0∞ ∫0∞ xm-1 yn-1 e-(x+y) \, dx \, dy$$

Transform coordinates from $(x, y)$ to $(u, v)$ via the mapping $x = u v$ and $y = u(1-v)$, where $u \in [0, ∞)$ and $v \in [0, 1]$. The Jacobian determinant $J$ of this transformation is:

$$J = \begin{vmatrix} ∂ x∂ u & ∂ x∂ v \\ ∂ y∂ u & ∂ y∂ v \end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} v & u \\ 1-v & -u \end{vmatrix} = -u v - u(1-v) = -u \implies |J| = u$$

Substituting into the integral yields:

$$\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n) = ∫01 ∫0∞ (uv)m-1 [u(1-v)]n-1 e-u · u \, du \, dv$$ $$\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n) = ( ∫0∞ um+n-1 e-u \, du ) ( ∫01 vm-1 (1-v)n-1 \, dv )$$ $$\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n) = \Gamma(m+n) · B(m, n) \implies B(m, n) = \Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)\Gamma(m+n)$$
$$ B(m, n) = \Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)\Gamma(m+n) = B(n, m) $$

Derivation B: Legendre's Duplication Formula

Legendre's duplication formula scales the argument of the Gamma function by a factor of 2:

$$\Gamma(z) \Gamma(z + 12) = √π22z-1 \Gamma(2z)$$

Proof Outline: By definition, $B(z, z) = ∫01 xz-1 (1-x)z-1 \, dx$. Substitute $x = 1+t2 \implies dx = 12 dt$ with $t \in [-1, 1]$:

$$B(z, z) = ∫-11 (1+t2)z-1 (1-t2)z-1 dt2 = 122z-1 ∫-11 (1-t^2)z-1 \, dt = 222z-1 ∫01 (1-t^2)z-1 \, dt$$

Substituting $t = √u \implies dt = 12√u du$:

$$B(z, z) = 122z-1 ∫01 u-1/2 (1-u)z-1 \, du = 122z-1 B(12, z)$$

Expressing in terms of Gamma functions:

$$[\Gamma(z)]^2\Gamma(2z) = 122z-1 \Gamma(12) \Gamma(z)\Gamma(z + 12)$$

Noting $\Gamma(1/2) = √π$ and rearranging yields Legendre's identity.

2. Trigonometric Integral Evaluation via Beta Functions (Wallis' Generalization)

Consider the general definite integral $I = ∫0π/2 \sin^p θ \cos^q θ \, dθ$. Substituting $x = \sin^2 θ \implies dx = 2 \sinθ \cosθ \, dθ$:

$$I = ∫01 xp/2 (1-x)q/2 dx2 x1/2 (1-x)1/2 = 12 ∫01 xp+12 - 1 (1-x)q+12 - 1 \, dx$$
$$ ∫0π/2 \sin^p θ \cos^q θ \, dθ = 12 B(p+12, q+12) = \Gamma(p+12)\Gamma(q+12)2 \, \Gamma(p+q+22) $$

3. Differentiation Under the Integral Sign (Leibniz Integral Rule)

For parameter-dependent dynamic integrals, if $f(x, t)$ and $∂ f∂ x$ are continuous over the region of integration, and the boundaries $a(x), b(x)$ are differentiable functions of $x$:

$$ ddx [ ∫a(x)b(x) f(x, t) \, dt ] = f(x, b(x)) · b'(x) - f(x, a(x)) · a'(x) + ∫a(x)b(x) ∂ f(x, t)∂ x \, dt $$

4. Aviation Application (5–10 min)

Integral calculus and Eulerian special functions underpin key analytical derivations across aeronautical disciplines:

1. Boundary Layer Theory (Aerodynamic Drag Analysis)

In viscous flow analysis over an aircraft wing, velocity distribution inside the boundary layer is approximated by empirical power laws: $uU_∞ = (y\delta)1/n$. The displacement thickness ($\delta^*$) and momentum thickness ($θ$) are defined via definite integrals over the boundary layer thickness $\delta$:

$$\delta^* = ∫0\delta (1 - uU_∞) \, dy = \delta ∫01 (1 - \eta1/n) \, d\eta = \delta [1 - nn+1] = \deltan+1$$ $$θ = ∫0\delta uU_∞ (1 - uU_∞) \, dy = \delta ∫01 \eta1/n (1 - \eta1/n) \, d\eta = \delta [ B(1 + 1n, 1) - B(1 + 2n, 1) ]$$

For a turbulent boundary layer with $n=7$, these definite integrals yield exact values $\delta^* = 18\delta$ and $θ = 772\delta$, directly establishing skin-friction drag coefficients.

2. Trajectory Integration & Breguet Range Equation

In flight performance, the total range $R$ of a jet aircraft is evaluated by integrating fuel consumption rate over total mass depletion from initial weight $W_0$ to final weight $W_1$:

$$R = ∫t_0t_1 V \, dt = ∫W_0W_1 V-c · D \, dW = ∫W_1W_0 Vc (D/L) dWW = Vc (LD) \ln(W_0W_1)$$

When air density or lift-to-drag ratio varies along optimal climb paths, the integral transforms into Beta/Gamma family improper integrals requiring exact convergence limits.

3. Air Safety Investigation: Flight Data Recorder (FDR) Reconstruction

During aircraft accident analysis, investigators reconstruct impact trajectories from discrete tri-axial accelerometer telemetric data $a_z(t)$ recorded by the Crash Survivable Memory Unit (CSMU). Continuous position profiles $z(t)$ are evaluated by double integration with initial velocity boundary conditions:

$$v_z(t) = v_z(0) + ∫0t a_z(τ) \, dτ, \quad z(t) = z(0) + ∫0t v_z(τ) \, dτ$$

When high-frequency vibrational noise corrupts raw sensor data, integral smoothing via convolution integrals utilizing Gaussian kernels $1σ√2π e-t^2 / 2σ^2$ (evaluated via Gamma integrals) is mandatory to isolate true structural dynamic response prior to structural failure.

5. UPSC ASO Focus (10 min)

  • Frequently Asked Facts:
    • Exact numerical value of $\Gamma(1/2) = √π$ and its application to Gaussian distribution integrals.
    • Domain of convergence for $B(m, n)$: strictly $Re(m) > 0$ and $Re(n) > 0$.
    • Improper integral evaluation of $∫0∞ x^a1+x^b \, dx = πb \sin((a+1)πb)$ via transformation to Beta function $B(a+1b, 1 - a+1b)$.
    • Leibniz rule differentiation applied to moving limits on fluid dynamics transport theorems (Reynolds Transport Theorem).
  • Conceptual Traps:
    • The Power Mismatch in Beta Trigonometric Integration: Misinterpreting $∫0π/2 \sin^p x \cos^q x \, dx$. The arguments for $B(m, n)$ are $m = p+12$ and $n = q+12$, NOT $p$ and $q$.
    • Gamma Poles Misconception: Assuming $\Gamma(0)$ or $\Gamma(-n)$ (for integer $n$) equals zero. In reality, Gamma functions possess infinite essential pole singularities at non-positive integers!
    • Chain-Rule Neglect in Leibniz Rule: Forgetting to multiply by $b'(x)$ and $a'(x)$ when differentiating integrals with non-linear functional bounds $∫a(x)b(x) f(t) dt$.
    • Improper Limit Evaluation: Evaluating $∫01 xm-1 (1-x)n-1 dx$ without ensuring $m>0, n>0$, leading to non-convergent logarithmic singularities at limits $0$ or $1$.

6. Practice Questions (10-12 UPSC ASO Standard MCQs)

Q1. Evaluate the definite integral $I = ∫01 x^4 (1-x)^3 \, dx$ using Eulerian special functions.
A) $1140$
B) $1280$
C) $1560$
D) $1105$
Detailed Solution:
The integral matches the standard Beta function definition: $$B(m, n) = ∫01 xm-1 (1-x)n-1 \, dx$$ Comparing exponents: $m-1 = 4 \implies m = 5$ and $n-1 = 3 \implies n = 4$.
Thus, $I = B(5, 4) = \Gamma(5) \Gamma(4)\Gamma(5+4) = 4! × 3!8!$.
Calculating factorials: $$I = 24 × 640320 = 14440320 = 1280$$ Hence, Option B is correct.
Q2. Evaluate the improper integral $I = ∫0∞ x^6 e-3x \, dx$.
A) $80243$
B) $160729$
C) $7202187$
D) $240243$
Detailed Solution:
Substitute $u = 3x \implies x = u3$ and $dx = du3$. Limits remain $0$ to $∞$: $$I = ∫0∞ (u3)^6 e-u du3 = 13^7 ∫0∞ u^6 e-u \, du$$ By definition of Gamma function, $∫0∞ u7-1 e-u \, du = \Gamma(7) = 6! = 720$.
$$I = 7203^7 = 7202187 = 80243$$ Hence, Option A is correct.
Q3. What is the value of the trigonometric integral $I = ∫0π/2 \sin^5 θ \cos^4 θ \, dθ$?
A) $8315$
B) $16315$
C) $4105$
D) $263$
Detailed Solution:
Using the identity $∫0π/2 \sin^p θ \cos^q θ \, dθ = 12 B(p+12, q+12)$:
Here $p = 5, q = 4 \implies p+12 = 3, q+12 = 52$.
$$I = 12 B(3, 52) = 12 \Gamma(3) \Gamma(5/2)\Gamma(3 + 5/2) = 12 \Gamma(3) \Gamma(5/2)\Gamma(11/2)$$ Recall $\Gamma(3) = 2! = 2$, and $\Gamma(112) = 92 · 72 · 52 \Gamma(52) = 3158 \Gamma(52)$.
Substituting these values: $$I = 12 2 · \Gamma(5/2)3158 \Gamma(5/2) = 8315$$ Hence, Option A is correct.
Q4. If $F(x) = ∫\sin x\cos x et^2 \, dt$, then the value of derivative $F'(0)$ is:
A) $e$
B) $-1$
C) $1$
D) $-e$
Detailed Solution:
Applying Leibniz Rule for differentiation under the integral sign: $$F'(x) = e(\cos x)^2 · ddx(\cos x) - e(\sin x)^2 · ddx(\sin x)$$ $$F'(x) = e\cos^2 x (-\sin x) - e\sin^2 x (\cos x)$$ Evaluating at $x = 0$: $$F'(0) = e\cos^2 0 (-\sin 0) - e\sin^2 0 (\cos 0) = e^1 (0) - e^0 (1) = 0 - 1 = -1$$ Hence, Option B is correct.
Q5. Evaluate the infinite domain integral $I = ∫0∞ x^3(1+x)^7 \, dx$.
A) $130$
B) $160$
C) $1120$
D) $120$
Detailed Solution:
Using the improper domain representation of Beta function: $$B(m, n) = ∫0∞ ym-1(1+y)m+n \, dy$$ Here $m-1 = 3 \implies m = 4$.
The denominator power $m+n = 7 \implies 4+n = 7 \implies n = 3$.
Therefore, $I = B(4, 3) = \Gamma(4)\Gamma(3)\Gamma(7) = 3! × 2!6! = 6 × 2720 = 12720 = 160$.
Hence, Option B is correct.
Q6. Using the analytical continuation and recurrence properties of the Gamma function, determine the exact value of $\Gamma(-12)$.
A) $2√π$
B) $-2√π$
C) $-√π$
D) $√π2$
Detailed Solution:
Use the fundamental recurrence relation $\Gamma(z+1) = z \Gamma(z)$, rewritten as: $$\Gamma(z) = \Gamma(z+1)z$$ Substitute $z = -12$: $$\Gamma(-12) = \Gamma(-1/2 + 1)-1/2 = \Gamma(1/2)-1/2 = √π-1/2 = -2√π$$ Hence, Option B is correct.
Q7. Compute the integral $I = ∫0π/2 √\tan θ \, dθ$ via Euler's Beta function.
A) $π√2$
B) $π√2$
C) $π2$
D) $√2π4$
Detailed Solution:
Express $I = ∫0π/2 \sin1/2θ \cos-1/2θ \, dθ$.
Using $I = 12 B(p+12, q+12)$ with $p = 1/2, q = -1/2$: $$m = 1/2 + 12 = 34, \quad n = -1/2 + 12 = 14$$ $$I = 12 B(34, 14) = 12 \Gamma(3/4) \Gamma(1/4)\Gamma(1)$$ By Euler's Reflection Formula $\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z) = π\sin(π z)$, for $z = 1/4$: $$\Gamma(1/4)\Gamma(3/4) = π\sin(π/4) = π1/√2 = π √2$$ Since $\Gamma(1) = 1$, we get: $$I = 12 (π√2) = π√2$$ Hence, Option A is correct.
Q8. Evaluate $I = ∫0π x \sin x1 + \cos^2 x \, dx$.
A) $π^22$
B) $π^24$
C) $π^28$
D) $π^2$
Detailed Solution:
Apply King's property $∫0a f(x) \, dx = ∫0a f(a-x) \, dx$: $$I = ∫0π (π-x) \sin(π-x)1 + \cos^2(π-x) \, dx = ∫0π (π-x) \sin x1 + \cos^2 x \, dx$$ Adding the two forms of $I$: $$2I = π ∫0π \sin x1 + \cos^2 x \, dx$$ Substitute $u = \cos x \implies du = -\sin x \, dx$. Limits change from $x=0 \to u=1$ and $x=π \to u=-1$: $$2I = π ∫1-1 -du1+u^2 = π ∫-11 du1+u^2 = π [\arctan(u)]-11$$ $$2I = π [π4 - (-π4)] = π (π2) = π^22 \implies I = π^24$$ Hence, Option B is correct.
Q9. In turbulent boundary layer aerodynamics over an aircraft fuselage, the normalized boundary layer velocity profile is given by $uU_∞ = \eta1/7$ where $\eta = y/\delta$. Calculate the displacement thickness ratio $\delta^*\delta$.
A) $17$
B) $18$
C) $78$
D) $19$
Detailed Solution:
The displacement thickness $\delta^*$ is defined as: $$\delta^* = ∫0\delta (1 - uU_∞) \, dy$$ Substituting $\eta = y/\delta \implies dy = \delta \, d\eta$: $$\delta^* = \delta ∫01 (1 - \eta1/7) \, d\eta = \delta [ \eta - 78\eta8/7 ]01 = \delta (1 - 78) = 18\delta$$ Therefore, $\delta^*\delta = 18$.
Hence, Option B is correct.
Q10. Determine the value of the improper integral $I = ∫0∞ e-x^4 \, dx$ in terms of the Gamma function.
A) $\Gamma(14)$
B) $14 \Gamma(14)$
C) $12 \Gamma(14)$
D) $4 \Gamma(54)$
Detailed Solution:
Substitute $t = x^4 \implies x = t1/4$ and $dx = 14 t-3/4 \, dt$: $$I = ∫0∞ e-t (14 t-3/4) \, dt = 14 ∫0∞ t14 - 1 e-t \, dt$$ By definition of Gamma function $∫0∞ tn-1 e-t \, dt = \Gamma(n)$ with $n = 1/4$: $$I = 14 \Gamma(14)$$ Note: Since $14\Gamma(1/4) = \Gamma(5/4)$, Option B is the exact direct expansion.
Q11. Evaluate the definite integral $I = ∫01 x^m (\ln x)^n \, dx$ where $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and $m > -1$.
A) $(-1)^n n!(m+1)n+1$
B) $n!(m+1)n+1$
C) $(-1)^n \Gamma(n)(m+1)^n$
D) $(-1)n+1 n!(m+1)n$
Detailed Solution:
Substitute $x = e-t \implies \ln x = -t$ and $dx = -e-t \, dt$. Limits map $x=0 \to t=∞$ and $x=1 \to t=0$: $$I = ∫∞0 (e-t)^m (-t)^n (-e-t \, dt) = (-1)^n ∫0∞ t^n e-(m+1)t \, dt$$ Substitute $u = (m+1)t \implies t = um+1$ and $dt = dum+1$: $$I = (-1)^n ∫0∞ (um+1)^n e-u dum+1 = (-1)^n(m+1)n+1 ∫0∞ u^n e-u \, du$$ Since $∫0∞ u^n e-u \, du = \Gamma(n+1) = n!$: $$I = (-1)^n n!(m+1)n+1$$ Hence, Option A is correct.
Q12. The improper integral $I = ∫0∞ xp-11+x \, dx$ converges strictly when $p$ satisfies which range condition?
A) $p > 1$
B) $0 < p < 1$
C) $-1 < p < 0$
D) $p > 0$
Detailed Solution:
The integral corresponds to Beta function $B(m, n) = ∫0∞ ym-1(1+y)m+n \, dy$.
Comparing powers: $m-1 = p-1 \implies m = p$.
The denominator power $m+n = 1 \implies p+n = 1 \implies n = 1-p$.
For $B(m, n)$ to converge, both parameters must be strictly positive:
1) $m > 0 \implies p > 0$
2) $n > 0 \implies 1-p > 0 \implies p < 1$
Combining both conditions yields $0 < p < 1$. Furthermore, its closed form is $B(p, 1-p) = π\sin(π p)$.
Hence, Option B is correct.

Q15 (Grand Test). Assertion (A): The integral $I = ∫0π/2 \ln(\sin x) \, dx$ evaluates exactly to $-π2 \ln 2$.
Reason (R): By applying King's property and trigonometric sum identities, $2I = ∫0π/2 \ln(\sin 2x2) \, dx$, which splits into $I - π2 \ln 2$.
A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A).
C) (A) is true but (R) is false.
D) (A) is false but (R) is true.
Detailed Solution:
Let $I = ∫0π/2 \ln(\sin x) \, dx$.
Using $x \to π/2 - x$: $I = ∫0π/2 \ln(\cos x) \, dx$.
Adding both: $$2I = ∫0π/2 [\ln(\sin x) + \ln(\cos x)] \, dx = ∫0π/2 \ln(\sin x \cos x) \, dx = ∫0π/2 \ln(\sin 2x2) \, dx$$ $$2I = ∫0π/2 \ln(\sin 2x) \, dx - ∫0π/2 \ln 2 \, dx$$ Substituting $2x = u$ in the first term yields $12 ∫0π \ln(\sin u) \, du = ∫0π/2 \ln(\sin u) \, du = I$.
Thus $2I = I - π2 \ln 2 \implies I = -π2 \ln 2$.
Both Assertion and Reason are true, and Reason correctly proves Assertion. Option A is correct.

8. Error Analysis & Common Traps (5 min)

To avoid penalty marks in UPSC ASO technical papers, eliminate these common candidate errors:

  1. Confusing Gamma Power Substitutions:
    When integrating $I = ∫0∞ x^n e-a x^k \, dx$, candidates often forget to substitute $u = a x^k$. The correct transformation requires $x = (ua)1/k$ and $dx = 1k a1/k u1k-1 du$, yielding: $$I = 1k \, an+1k \Gamma(n+1k)$$
  2. Neglecting Boundary Limits in Leibniz Differentiation:
    In dynamic pressure or potential flow integrations where boundary limits are functions of $x$, e.g., $∫0x^2 √t \, dt$, candidates incorrectly apply standard differentiation under integral sign without applying the chain rule term $b'(x) = 2x$.
  3. Beta Function Argument Indexing Off-by-One Errors:
    The integral $∫01 x^p (1-x)^q \, dx$ is $B(p+1, q+1)$, NOT $B(p, q)$. Always increment exponents by 1!
  4. Sign Errors in Negative Fraction Gamma Evaluation:
    Using $\Gamma(z) = z \Gamma(z-1)$ instead of the inverse relation $\Gamma(z-1) = \Gamma(z)z-1$ leads to sign inversions when evaluating terms like $\Gamma(-1/2)$ or $\Gamma(-3/2)$.
