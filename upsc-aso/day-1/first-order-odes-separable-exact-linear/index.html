First-order ODEs – separable, exact, linear

Day 1 of 129 Engineering Fundamentals Phase 1: Foundations 60-90 min Master Cycle

1. Understand (5–10 min)

In Aeronautical Engineering and Air Safety Analysis, physical phenomena such as aircraft speed decay during rollout, cabin pressure depletion during uncoordinated altitude changes, engine thermal transients, and atmospheric boundary layer velocity distributions are fundamentally modeled as differential equations. A First-Order Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) is an equation containing an unknown function $y(x)$ and its first derivative $\frac{dy}{dx}$, expressing a relation of the general implicit form:

$$ F\left(x, y, \frac{dy}{dx}\right) = 0 \quad \text{or in explicit form} \quad \frac{dy}{dx} = f(x, y) $$

Alternatively, first-order ODEs are frequently expressed in differential form as $M(x,y)dx + N(x,y)dy = 0$. The mathematical scope of this microtopic encompasses three foundational sub-classes of first-order ODEs essential for competitive examinations (such as UPSC ASO, GATE, and ESE):

1. Separable ODEs: Equations where functions of independent variable $x$ and dependent variable $y$ can be factored and segregated on opposite sides of the equality sign: $g(y)dy = f(x)dx$. 2. Exact ODEs: Equations derived directly from the total differential $d\psi(x,y) = 0$ of a continuous scalar field $\psi(x,y)$, satisfying the Cauchy-Riemann derivative compatibility criterion $\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial N}{\partial x}$. 3. Linear ODEs (Leibniz Form): Equations linear in $y$ and $\frac{dy}{dx}$ having the canonical structure $\frac{dy}{dx} + P(x)y = Q(x)$, along with non-linear equations reducible to linear form (e.g., Bernoulli's equation).

UPSC Standard Terminology:

  • Order: The order of the highest derivative present ($1$ for this module).
  • Degree: The algebraic power of the highest-order derivative after removing radical/fractional exponents.
  • General Solution: A solution set containing as many arbitrary constants ($C$) as the order of the ODE ($1$ constant for first-order ODEs).
  • Particular Solution: A specific solution derived by evaluating $C$ using an Initial Value Problem (IVP) boundary condition $y(x_0) = y_0$.
  • Singular Solution: A solution that satisfies the ODE but cannot be obtained from the general solution for any real value of $C$ (often envelope curves).
  • Picard–Lindelöf Existence & Uniqueness Theorem: For $\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x,y)$ with $y(x_0) = y_0$, if $f(x,y)$ and $\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}$ are continuous in a closed rectangle $R$ containing $(x_0, y_0)$, then there exists a unique continuous solution $y(x)$ in an interval $|x - x_0| \le h$.

2. Learn the Concept (15–20 min)

The mathematical principles and derivations for each analytical technique form the theoretical backbone of first-order differential calculus.

1. Separable First-Order Differential Equations

A first-order ODE is separable if it can be written as:

$$ \frac{dy}{dx} = h(x)g(y) \implies \frac{1}{g(y)} dy = h(x) dx \quad (g(y) \neq 0) $$

Integrating both sides yields the general implicit solution:

$$ \int \frac{dy}{g(y)} = \int h(x) dx + C $$

Equations Reducible to Separable Form:

Functions of the form $\frac{dy}{dx} = f(ax + by + c)$ are reduced to separable form using the substitution $u = ax + by + c$. Differentiating with respect to $x$ gives $\frac{du}{dx} = a + b\frac{dy}{dx}$, yielding the separable ODE:

$$ \frac{1}{a + bf(u)} du = dx $$

2. Exact Differential Equations and Integrating Factors

A differential equation $M(x,y)dx + N(x,y)dy = 0$ is exact if there exists a twice continuously differentiable potential function $\psi(x,y)$ such that:

$$ d\psi(x,y) = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x} dx + \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y} dy = M(x,y)dx + N(x,y)dy $$

Comparing coefficients gives $\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x} = M$ and $\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y} = N$. By Schwarz's theorem on mixed partial derivatives (assuming $M, N$ and their partials are continuous):

$$ \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial y \partial x} = \frac{\partial M}{\partial y} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial x \partial y} = \frac{\partial N}{\partial x} \implies \frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial N}{\partial x} $$

Derivation of Solution to Exact ODEs:

1. Integrate $M(x,y)$ with respect to $x$, keeping $y$ constant: $\psi(x,y) = \int M(x,y) dx + h(y)$.

2. Differentiate $\psi(x,y)$ with respect to $y$ and set equal to $N(x,y)$:

$$ \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left( \int M(x,y) dx \right) + h'(y) = N(x,y) \implies h'(y) = N(x,y) - \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left( \int M(x,y) dx \right) $$

3. Integrate $h'(y)$ to find $h(y)$, and express the general solution as $\psi(x,y) = C$.

Integrating Factors ($\mu$) for Non-Exact Equations:

If $M dx + N dy = 0$ is non-exact, we multiply by an integrating factor $\mu(x,y)$ such that $\frac{\partial (\mu M)}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial (\mu N)}{\partial x}$. Key cases tested in UPSC:

  • Case I (Function of $x$ only): If $\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial N}{\partial x}\right) = f(x)$, then $\mu(x) = e^{\int f(x) dx}$.
  • Case II (Function of $y$ only): If $\frac{1}{M}\left(\frac{\partial N}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial M}{\partial y}\right) = g(y)$, then $\mu(y) = e^{\int g(y) dy}$.
  • Case III (Homogeneous Equations): If $M(x,y)$ and $N(x,y)$ are homogeneous functions of the same degree and $xM + yN \neq 0$, then $\mu(x,y) = \frac{1}{xM + yN}$.
  • Case IV (Form $y f(xy)dx + x g(xy)dy = 0$): If $xM - yN \neq 0$, then $\mu(x,y) = \frac{1}{xM - yN}$.

3. First-Order Linear ODEs & Bernoulli's Equation

The standard Leibniz linear ODE is written as:

$$ \frac{dy}{dx} + P(x)y = Q(x) $$

Multiplying by an unknown integrating factor $I(x)$ gives $I(x)\frac{dy}{dx} + I(x)P(x)y = I(x)Q(x)$. Enforcing the product rule $\frac{d}{dx}[y \cdot I(x)] = I(x)\frac{dy}{dx} + I'(x)y$ requires:

$$ I'(x) = I(x)P(x) \implies \frac{dI}{I} = P(x)dx \implies I(x) = e^{\int P(x) dx} $$

Thus, the complete general solution is:

$$ y \cdot e^{\int P(x) dx} = \int \left( Q(x) e^{\int P(x) dx} \right) dx + C $$

Bernoulli's Non-Linear ODE:

Equations of the form $\frac{dy}{dx} + P(x)y = Q(x)y^n$ ($n \neq 0, 1$) are linearized by dividing both sides by $y^n$:

$$ y^{-n} \frac{dy}{dx} + P(x) y^{1-n} = Q(x) $$

Substituting $v = y^{1-n} \implies \frac{dv}{dx} = (1-n) y^{-n} \frac{dy}{dx}$, the equation reduces to a linear ODE in $v$:

$$ \frac{dv}{dx} + (1-n)P(x)v = (1-n)Q(x) $$
$$ \text{Linear ODE Solution: } y \cdot I(x) = \int Q(x) I(x) dx + C \quad \text{where} \quad I(x) = \exp\left(\int P(x) dx\right) $$ $$ \text{Exactness Criterion: } \frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial N}{\partial x} \implies \psi(x,y) = \int_{y=\text{const}} M dx + \int (\text{terms in } N \text{ free from } x) dy = C $$ $$ \text{Bernoulli Reduction: } y' + P(x)y = Q(x)y^n \xrightarrow{v = y^{1-n}} \frac{dv}{dx} + (1-n)P(x)v = (1-n)Q(x) $$

3. Memorize Important Facts (10 min)

  • Separable Form Integration: $\int \frac{dy}{g(y)} = \int f(x) dx + C$
  • Exact Condition (Schwarz Criterion): $\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial N}{\partial x}$
  • Exact ODE Solution Formula: $\int_{y=\text{const}} M(x,y) dx + \int (\text{terms in } N \text{ containing NO } x) dy = C$
  • Integrating Factor Rule 1 (Pure $x$): $\mu(x) = \exp\left(\int \left[\frac{M_y - N_x}{N}\right] dx\right)$
  • Integrating Factor Rule 2 (Pure $y$): $\mu(y) = \exp\left(\int \left[\frac{N_x - M_y}{M}\right] dy\right)$
  • Homogeneous IF Rule: $\mu(x,y) = \frac{1}{xM + yN} \quad (\text{for } xM + yN \neq 0)$
  • Special Product IF Rule: $\mu(x,y) = \frac{1}{xM - yN} \quad (\text{for } y f(xy)dx + x g(xy)dy = 0)$
  • Linear ODE Integrating Factor: $I(x) = e^{\int P(x) dx}$
  • Linear ODE General Solution: $y \cdot I(x) = \int Q(x) I(x) dx + C$
  • Bernoulli Substitution power: For $y' + P(x)y = Q(x)y^n$, substitute $v = y^{1-n}$ to yield $v' + (1-n)P(x)v = (1-n)Q(x)$.

4. Aviation Application (5–10 min)

First-order ODEs serve as foundational physics models across aeronautical engineering and air safety accident reconstruction:

1. Aircraft Landing Ground Roll Deceleration (Separable ODE):
During touchdown and rollout, an aircraft decelerates under aerodynamic drag $D = \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 S C_D$ and mechanical braking force $\mu_r (W - L) = \mu_r (W - \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 S C_L)$. Newton's second law yields: $$ m \frac{dV}{dt} = -\left[ \mu_r W + \frac{1}{2}\rho S (C_D - \mu_r C_L) V^2 \right] $$ This is a separable ODE of the form $\frac{dV}{A + B V^2} = -dt$. Integrating gives exact ground roll time and stopping distance predictions crucial for investigating runway overrun incidents.

2. Rapid Cabin Depressurization Dynamics (First-Order Linear ODE):
If a cabin pressure vessel suffers a hull breach or faulty outflow valve actuation at altitude, air mass balance dictates: $$ V_{\text{cabin}} \frac{d\rho_c}{dt} = \dot{m}_{\text{in}} - \dot{m}_{\text{out}}(t) $$ Assuming ideal gas properties ($p = \rho R T$), cabin pressure decay follows a first-order linear ODE: $\frac{dp_c}{dt} + K(t) p_c = S(t)$. The integrating factor method calculates time-to-useful-consciousness (TUC) for flight crew safety assessments.

3. Engine Flameout Airspeed Decay (Bernoulli/Separable ODE):
Post-flameout glide performance under pitch-constrained equilibrium is governed by $\frac{dV}{dt} = -g \sin \gamma - \frac{\rho S C_{D0}}{2m} V^2 - \frac{2K m g^2 \cos^2 \gamma}{\rho S} \frac{1}{V^2}$. In simplified operational regimes, airspeed decay simplifies to a Bernoulli ODE, enabling flight data recorder (FDR) analysts to pinpoint exact flameout timing during crash investigations.

5. UPSC ASO Focus (10 min)

  • Frequently Asked Facts:
    • The general solution of a first-order differential equation must contain exactly ONE arbitrary independent constant.
    • If $M dx + N dy = 0$ is exact, $\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} \equiv \frac{\partial N}{\partial x}$ holds identically over the domain.
    • In Bernoulli's equation $y' + Py = Q y^n$, when $n=0$ or $n=1$, the equation is ALREADY linear and requires no substitution transformation.
    • An integrating factor $\mu(x,y)$ turns an inexact differential equation into an exact one, converting $d\psi = 0$.
  • Conceptual Traps:
    • Trap 1 (Singular Solution Loss): When separating variables $\frac{dy}{g(y)} = f(x)dx$, roots of $g(y) = 0$ yield singular constant solutions (e.g., $y=0$) that may be lost if divided without validation.
    • Trap 2 (Sign Error in Exact Integrals): Integrating $N(x,y)$ with respect to $y$ requires discarding terms that contain $x$. Students frequently forget to drop $x$-dependent terms already accounted for in $\int M dx$.
    • Trap 3 (Integrating Factor Exponents): $I(x) = e^{\int P(x)dx}$. If $\int P(x)dx = \ln(x^{-2})$, then $I(x) = x^{-2}$, NOT $-2 \ln x$ or $x^2$. Do not forget the exponential identity $e^{\ln g(x)} = g(x)$.
    • Trap 4 (Bernoulli Scaling Factor): When transforming $y' + P(x)y = Q(x)y^n$ via $v = y^{1-n}$, the RHS becomes $(1-n)Q(x)$, NOT just $Q(x)$. The multiplier $(1-n)$ must scale BOTH $P(x)$ and $Q(x)$.

6. Practice Questions (10-12 UPSC ASO Standard MCQs)

Q1. Solve the initial value problem $(x^2 + 1)\frac{dy}{dx} + 2xy = 4x^3$ with $y(0) = 1$. What is the value of $y(1)$?
A) $0.5$
B) $1.0$
C) $1.5$
D) $2.0$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Write in standard Leibniz linear form $\frac{dy}{dx} + \frac{2x}{x^2+1}y = \frac{4x^3}{x^2+1}$.
2. Here, $P(x) = \frac{2x}{x^2+1}$ and $Q(x) = \frac{4x^3}{x^2+1}$.
3. Calculate Integrating Factor: $I(x) = e^{\int \frac{2x}{x^2+1}dx} = e^{\ln(x^2+1)} = x^2 + 1$.
4. General solution: $y(x^2+1) = \int \frac{4x^3}{x^2+1}(x^2+1) dx = \int 4x^3 dx = x^4 + C$.
5. Apply initial condition $y(0) = 1$: $1(0+1) = 0 + C \implies C = 1$.
6. Solution is $y(x) = \frac{x^4 + 1}{x^2 + 1}$.
7. At $x = 1$: $y(1) = \frac{1^4 + 1}{1^2 + 1} = \frac{2}{2} = 1.0$.
Q2. For what value of constant $k$ is the differential equation $(2x y^3 + k x^2 y) dx + (3 x^2 y^2 + x^3) dy = 0$ exact?
A) $1$
B) $2$
C) $3$
D) $6$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Identify $M(x,y) = 2x y^3 + k x^2 y$ and $N(x,y) = 3 x^2 y^2 + x^3$.
2. Compute partial derivatives:
$\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = 6x y^2 + k x^2$
$\frac{\partial N}{\partial x} = 6x y^2 + 3 x^2$
3. For exactness, $\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial N}{\partial x} \implies 6x y^2 + k x^2 = 6x y^2 + 3 x^2$.
4. Equating $x^2$ coefficients yields $k = 3$.
Q3. Find the integrating factor $\mu(x)$ for the differential equation $(2 y^2 + 3x) dx + 2xy dy = 0$.
A) $x$
B) $x^2$
C) $\frac{1}{x}$
D) $e^x$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. $M = 2y^2 + 3x \implies \frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = 4y$.
2. $N = 2xy \implies \frac{\partial N}{\partial x} = 2y$.
3. Check non-exactness: $\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial N}{\partial x} = 4y - 2y = 2y \neq 0$.
4. Evaluate $\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial N}{\partial x}\right) = \frac{2y}{2xy} = \frac{1}{x}$ (a function of $x$ alone).
5. Integrating Factor $\mu(x) = e^{\int \frac{1}{x} dx} = e^{\ln x} = x$.
Q4. What is the substitute variable substitution transformation that reduces the Bernoulli equation $\frac{dy}{dx} + \frac{y}{x} = y^2 \ln x$ to a linear ODE?
A) $v = y^{-1}$
B) $v = y^2$
C) $v = \ln y$
D) $v = y^{-2}$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. The equation is in standard Bernoulli form: $y' + P(x)y = Q(x)y^n$ with $n = 2$.
2. The standard substitution for Bernoulli's equation is $v = y^{1-n}$.
3. Here $n = 2 \implies v = y^{1-2} = y^{-1} = \frac{1}{y}$.
4. Differentiating gives $\frac{dv}{dx} = -y^{-2}\frac{dy}{dx}$. Multiplying original equation by $-y^{-2}$ yields $\frac{dv}{dx} - \frac{1}{x}v = -\ln x$, which is linear in $v$.
Q5. An unpowered test aircraft post-touchdown undergoes pure aerodynamic drag deceleration according to $m \frac{dV}{dt} = -k V^2$. If $V(0) = V_0$, which expression represents the airspeed $V(t)$ at time $t$?
A) $V(t) = V_0 e^{-\frac{k}{m}t}$
B) $V(t) = \frac{V_0}{1 + \frac{k V_0}{m}t}$
C) $V(t) = V_0 - \frac{k}{m}t^2$
D) $V(t) = \frac{V_0}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{2k}{m}t}}$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Separate variables: $\frac{dV}{V^2} = -\frac{k}{m} dt$.
2. Integrate both sides: $\int V^{-2} dV = \int -\frac{k}{m} dt \implies -\frac{1}{V} = -\frac{k}{m}t + C$.
3. Apply boundary condition $V(0) = V_0$: $-\frac{1}{V_0} = 0 + C \implies C = -\frac{1}{V_0}$.
4. Thus: $\frac{1}{V} = \frac{1}{V_0} + \frac{k}{m}t = \frac{m + k V_0 t}{m V_0}$.
5. Inverting gives $V(t) = \frac{V_0}{1 + \frac{k V_0}{m}t}$.
Q6. Using the substitution $u = x + y + 1$, solve the differential equation $\frac{dy}{dx} = (x + y + 1)^2$. The general solution is:
A) $y = \tan(x + C) - x - 1$
B) $y = \tan(x + C) + x + 1$
C) $x + y + 1 = \ln|\sec(x + C)|$
D) $y = e^{x+C} - x - 1$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Let $u = x + y + 1 \implies \frac{du}{dx} = 1 + \frac{dy}{dx} \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{du}{dx} - 1$.
2. Substitute into ODE: $\frac{du}{dx} - 1 = u^2 \implies \frac{du}{dx} = 1 + u^2$.
3. Separate variables: $\frac{du}{1 + u^2} = dx$.
4. Integrate: $\arctan(u) = x + C \implies u = \tan(x + C)$.
5. Substitute back $u = x + y + 1$: $x + y + 1 = \tan(x + C) \implies y = \tan(x + C) - x - 1$.
Q7. The homogeneous differential equation $(x^2 - y^2) dx + 2xy dy = 0$ yields a family of curves given by:
A) $x^2 + y^2 = C x$
B) $x^2 - y^2 = C x$
C) $x^2 + y^2 = C y$
D) $x y = C(x + y)$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Substitute $y = vx \implies dy = v dx + x dv$.
2. Equation becomes: $(x^2 - v^2 x^2) dx + 2x(vx)(v dx + x dv) = 0$.
3. Factor out $x^2$: $(1 - v^2 + 2v^2) dx + 2x v dv = 0 \implies (1 + v^2) dx + 2x v dv = 0$.
4. Separate variables: $\frac{dx}{x} + \frac{2v}{1 + v^2} dv = 0$.
5. Integrate: $\ln|x| + \ln(1 + v^2) = \ln C \implies x(1 + v^2) = C$.
6. Replace $v = y/x$: $x\left(1 + \frac{y^2}{x^2}\right) = C \implies x + \frac{y^2}{x} = C \implies x^2 + y^2 = C x$.
Q8. Find the continuous potential function $\psi(x,y) = C$ satisfying the exact differential equation $(3x^2 + 2xy) dx + (x^2 + 4y^3) dy = 0$.
A) $x^3 + x^2 y + y^4 = C$
B) $x^3 + 2x^2 y + 4y^4 = C$
C) $3x^3 + x^2 y + y^4 = C$
D) $x^3 + x y^2 + y^4 = C$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Check exactness: $M = 3x^2 + 2xy \implies M_y = 2x$. $N = x^2 + 4y^3 \implies N_x = 2x$. Exact!
2. Compute $\psi(x,y) = \int_{y=\text{const}} (3x^2 + 2xy) dx + h(y) = x^3 + x^2 y + h(y)$.
3. Differentiate with respect to $y$: $\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y} = x^2 + h'(y)$.
4. Set equal to $N$: $x^2 + h'(y) = x^2 + 4y^3 \implies h'(y) = 4y^3 \implies h(y) = y^4$.
5. Hence, the general solution potential is $\psi(x,y) = x^3 + x^2 y + y^4 = C$.
Q9. What is the integrating factor $\mu(x,y)$ for the homogeneous non-exact equation $(x^2 y - 2x y^2) dx - (x^3 - 3x^2 y) dy = 0$?
A) $\frac{1}{x y}$
B) $\frac{1}{x^2 y^2}$
C) $\frac{1}{x^3 y}$
D) $\frac{1}{x^2 + y^2}$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Verify homogeneity: Both $M = x^2 y - 2xy^2$ and $N = -(x^3 - 3x^2 y)$ are homogeneous of degree $3$.
2. Use Homogeneous Integrating Factor rule $\mu = \frac{1}{xM + yN}$ provided $xM + yN \neq 0$.
3. Calculate $xM + yN = x(x^2 y - 2xy^2) + y(-x^3 + 3x^2 y) = x^3 y - 2x^2 y^2 - x^3 y + 3x^2 y^2 = x^2 y^2$.
4. Since $x^2 y^2 \neq 0$, the integrating factor is $\mu(x,y) = \frac{1}{x^2 y^2}$.
Q10. Solve the initial value problem $\frac{dy}{dx} + y \tan x = \cos x$ subject to $y(0) = 0$. What is $y\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)$?
A) $\frac{\pi}{3}$
B) $\frac{\pi}{6}$
C) $\frac{\pi \sqrt{3}}{6}$
D) $\frac{\pi}{12}$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. $P(x) = \tan x, Q(x) = \cos x$.
2. Integrating Factor $I(x) = e^{\int \tan x dx} = e^{\ln|\sec x|} = \sec x$.
3. General Solution: $y \cdot \sec x = \int \cos x \cdot \sec x dx = \int 1 dx = x + C$.
4. Thus $y(x) = (x + C) \cos x$.
5. Apply boundary condition $y(0) = 0 \implies (0 + C) \cos(0) = 0 \implies C = 0$.
6. Solution is $y(x) = x \cos x$.
7. Evaluate at $x = \frac{\pi}{3}$: $y\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = \frac{\pi}{3} \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = \frac{\pi}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{\pi}{6}$.

Q501. Consider the following mathematical statements regarding first-order ODEs:
I. The differential equation $\frac{dy}{dx} = y^{2/3}$ with initial condition $y(0) = 0$ has a unique solution in the interval containing $x = 0$.
II. If $\mu(x,y)$ is an integrating factor for $M dx + N dy = 0$, then $c \cdot \mu(x,y)$ (where $c \neq 0$) is also an integrating factor.
III. A Bernoulli differential equation $y' + P(x)y = Q(x)y^n$ is linear when $n = 0$ or $n = 1$.
Which of the above statements are CORRECT?
A) I and II only
B) II and III only
C) I and III only
D) I, II and III
Detailed Analysis:
- Statement I is INCORRECT: For $f(x,y) = y^{2/3}$, the partial derivative $\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = \frac{2}{3} y^{-1/3}$ is discontinuous at $y = 0$. By Picard-Lindelöf theorem, uniqueness is violated! In fact, $y(x) = 0$ and $y(x) = \frac{1}{27}x^3$ are BOTH solutions satisfying $y(0) = 0$.
- Statement II is CORRECT: Multiplying an exact equation $d\psi = 0$ by any non-zero constant $c$ keeps $c d\psi = 0$ exact.
- Statement III is CORRECT: For $n=0$, $y' + Py = Q$ (standard linear). For $n=1$, $y' + (P-Q)y = 0$ (homogeneous linear).
Therefore, statements II and III are correct.

Q503. Aircraft Safety Investigation Problem: An atmospheric research probe descending vertically through low altitude density layers experiences aerodynamic drag. Its vertical velocity $v(t)$ satisfies the first-order ODE: $$ \frac{dv}{dt} + k v = g $$ where $k > 0$ is the aerodynamic friction parameter per unit mass and $g$ is gravity. If the probe is dropped from rest ($v(0) = 0$), what is its asymptotic terminal velocity $v_{\infty} = \lim_{t \to \infty} v(t)$, and what percentage of $v_{\infty}$ is reached at time $t = \frac{1}{k}$?
A) $v_{\infty} = \frac{g}{k}$, reaching $63.2\%$ at $t = 1/k$
B) $v_{\infty} = \frac{k}{g}$, reaching $50.0\%$ at $t = 1/k$
C) $v_{\infty} = \frac{g}{k}$, reaching $86.5\%$ at $t = 1/k$
D) $v_{\infty} = g k$, reaching $36.8\%$ at $t = 1/k$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Standard First-order Linear ODE: $P(t) = k, Q(t) = g$.
2. Integrating factor: $I(t) = e^{\int k dt} = e^{kt}$.
3. Solution: $v(t) e^{kt} = \int g e^{kt} dt = \frac{g}{k} e^{kt} + C \implies v(t) = \frac{g}{k} + C e^{-kt}$.
4. Apply initial condition $v(0) = 0 \implies 0 = \frac{g}{k} + C \implies C = -\frac{g}{k}$.
5. Hence $v(t) = \frac{g}{k}\left(1 - e^{-kt}\right)$.
6. As $t \to \infty$, $e^{-kt} \to 0 \implies v_{\infty} = \frac{g}{k}$.
7. At time $t = \frac{1}{k}$: $v(1/k) = v_{\infty}\left(1 - e^{-1}\right) \approx v_{\infty}(1 - 0.36788) = 0.63212 v_{\infty} = 63.2\%$.

8. Error Analysis & Common Traps (5 min)

Top structural errors and cognitive traps committed by candidates in UPSC Air Safety Officer exams:

  1. Omission of Constant of Integration Before Function Inversion:
    Incorrect: $\int \frac{dy}{y} = \int x dx \implies \ln y = \frac{x^2}{2} \implies y = e^{x^2/2} + C$.
    Correct: $\ln|y| = \frac{x^2}{2} + C_1 \implies y = e^{\frac{x^2}{2} + C_1} = A e^{x^2/2}$ (where $A = e^{C_1}$).
  2. Integrating Factor Power Algebraic Errors:
    When calculating $I(x) = e^{\int -\frac{2}{x} dx} = e^{-2 \ln x}$, writing $I(x) = -2x$ instead of $I(x) = x^{-2} = \frac{1}{x^2}$. Remember $a \ln b = \ln(b^a)$.
  3. Confusing the Integrands for IF Rules:
    Swapping $\frac{M_y - N_x}{N}$ (pure $x$) with $\frac{N_x - M_y}{M}$ (pure $y$). Notice the denominator! Dividing by $N$ gives a function of $x$, while dividing by $M$ gives a function of $y$.
  4. Improper Evaluation of Partial Integral Terms:
    In exact ODEs $\psi(x,y) = \int M dx + \int (N \text{ without } x) dy = C$, students often integrate full $N(x,y)$ rather than deleting terms containing $x$.

9. Quick Revision & Mind Map (5 min)

Rapid Recall Mind-Map & Formula Flash-Facts:

  • Separable ODE: $g(y)dy = f(x)dx \implies \int g(y)dy = \int f(x)dx + C$.
    • Special Form $y' = f(ax+by+c) \implies$ Let $u = ax+by+c$.
  • Exact ODE Criterion: $M dx + N dy = 0$ is exact $\iff \frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial N}{\partial x}$.
    • Solution: $\int_{y=\text{const}} M dx + \int (\text{terms of } N \text{ free of } x) dy = C$.
  • Integrating Factors ($\mu$):
    • $\frac{M_y - N_x}{N} = f(x) \implies \mu = e^{\int f(x) dx}$
    • $\frac{N_x - M_y}{M} = g(y) \implies \mu = e^{\int g(y) dy}$
    • Homogeneous: $\mu = \frac{1}{xM + yN}$
  • First-Order Linear Leibniz ODE: $\frac{dy}{dx} + P(x)y = Q(x)$
    • $I(x) = e^{\int P(x) dx}$
    • General Solution: $y \cdot I(x) = \int Q(x) I(x) dx + C$
  • Bernoulli Equation: $\frac{dy}{dx} + P(x)y = Q(x) y^n$
    • Substitute $v = y^{1-n} \implies \frac{dv}{dx} + (1-n)P(x)v = (1-n)Q(x)$.
