Second-order ODEs – homogeneous, particular integral

Day 1 of 129 Engineering Fundamentals Phase 1: Foundations 60-90 min Master Cycle

1. Understand (5–10 min)

In classical mathematical physics and flight dynamics analysis, a linear second-order differential equation with constant or variable coefficients serves as the governing model for dynamic systems with two degrees of freedom, structural vibrations, aeroelastic flutter, and aircraft stability modes. The general standard form of a non-homogeneous second-order Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) is defined as:

$$ P_0(x) d^2ydx^2 + P_1(x) dydx + P_2(x) y = R(x) $$

where $P_0(x) \neq 0$, $P_1(x)$, $P_2(x)$, and $R(x)$ are continuous real-valued functions defined on a specified interval $I \subseteq \mathbb{R}$. When $P_0, P_1, P_2$ are real constants $a, b, c$ with $a \neq 0$, the equation simplifies to the linear second-order ODE with constant coefficients:

$$ a d^2ydx^2 + b dydx + c y = R(x) \quad or \quad f(D) y = R(x) $$

where $D \equiv ddx$ represents the differential operator, and $f(D) = a D^2 + b D + c$ is the characteristic linear differential operator. By the Principle of Superposition for linear differential equations, the general solution $y(x)$ consists of two distinct components:

$$ y(x) = y_c(x) + y_p(x) $$

Here, $y_c(x)$ is the Complementary Function (CF) (the general solution of the homogeneous equation $f(D)y = 0$, containing two arbitrary linearly independent integration constants $C_1$ and $C_2$), while $y_p(x)$ is the Particular Integral (PI) (any specific solution that satisfies the non-homogeneous equation $f(D)y = R(x)$ and contains no arbitrary constants).

In UPSC Air Safety Officer (ASO) and Aeronautical Engineering frameworks, mastering the algebraic and operational properties of $f(D)$—including inverse operator mechanics, Wronskian determinant criteria for linear independence, method of variation of parameters, and Cauchy-Euler transformations—is critical for analyzing dynamic response, gust excitation, landing gear shock absorption, and aircraft longitudinal pitching dynamics.

2. Learn the Concept (15–20 min)

To rigorously solve $a d^2ydx^2 + b dydx + c y = R(x)$, we break down the operational theory into two distinct mathematical frameworks: Homogeneous Analysis (Complementary Function) and Non-Homogeneous Analysis (Particular Integral).

1. Complementary Function ($y_c$) and Characteristic Equations

The homogeneous equation is $f(D) y = (a D^2 + b D + c) y = 0$. Assuming a trial solution of the exponential form $y = emx$, substitution into the operator yields the algebraic auxiliary equation (characteristic polynomial):

$$ a m^2 + b m + c = 0 $$

The roots $m_1, m_2 = -b ± √b^2 - 4ac2a$ dictate the physical system response (overdamped, critically damped, or underdamped):

  • Case I: Real and Distinct Roots ($b^2 - 4ac > 0$)
    $$ y_c(x) = C_1 em_1 x + C_2 em_2 x $$
  • Case II: Real and Equal Roots ($b^2 - 4ac = 0$, $m_1 = m_2 = m$)
    $$ y_c(x) = (C_1 + C_2 x) emx $$
  • Case III: Complex Conjugate Roots ($b^2 - 4ac < 0$, $m1,2 = α ± i β$)
    $$ y_c(x) = eα x ( C_1 \cos(β x) + C_2 \sin(β x) ) $$

2. The Inverse Operator Method for Particular Integrals ($y_p$)

The Particular Integral is formally expressed as $y_p = 1f(D) R(x)$. The operator $1f(D)$ is defined as the linear inverse mapping such that $f(D) [ 1f(D) R(x) ] = R(x)$.

$$ General Inverse Operator Fundamental Rules: $$ $$ 1.\ 1f(D) eax = 1f(a) eax, \quad provided f(a) \neq 0 $$ $$ 2.\ 1f(D^2) \sin(ax + b) = 1f(-a^2) \sin(ax + b), \quad provided f(-a^2) \neq 0 $$ $$ 3.\ 1f(D^2) \cos(ax + b) = 1f(-a^2) \cos(ax + b), \quad provided f(-a^2) \neq 0 $$ $$ 4.\ 1f(D) [eax V(x)] = eax 1f(D + a) V(x) \quad (Exponential Shift Theorem) $$ $$ 5.\ 1f(D) [x V(x)] = x 1f(D) V(x) - f'(D)[f(D)]^2 V(x) $$

3. Resonant Cases (Failure of Inverse Operators)

When $f(a) = 0$ or $f(-a^2) = 0$, the standard expressions yield division by zero. We apply Leibnitz's theorem on operator differentiation:

If $f(D) = (D - a)^k φ(D)$ where $φ(a) \neq 0$:

$$ y_p = 1(D - a)^k φ(D) eax = x^kk! \, φ(a) eax $$

For trigonometric forcing functions where $f(-a^2) = 0$:

$$ 1f(D^2) \sin(ax) = x · 1f'(D) \sin(ax) $$

4. Method of Variation of Parameters

For arbitrary forcing functions $R(x)$ (e.g., $\sec x, \tan x, 1/x$), inverse operators may fail. Given the homogeneous linearly independent fundamental solutions $y_1(x)$ and $y_2(x)$, the Particular Integral is assumed as:

$$ y_p(x) = u(x) y_1(x) + v(x) y_2(x) $$

Where the dynamic coefficients $u(x)$ and $v(x)$ are solved via the Wronskian determinant $W(y_1, y_2) = y_1 y_2' - y_2 y_1'$:

$$ u(x) = - ∫ y_2(x) R(x)W(y_1, y_2) dx, \quad v(x) = ∫ y_1(x) R(x)W(y_1, y_2) dx $$

5. Cauchy-Euler Reducible Equations

An equation of the form $a x^2 d^2ydx^2 + b x dydx + c y = R(x)$ is transformed into a constant coefficient ODE by substituting $x = e^z \implies z = \ln x$. Setting $θ \equiv ddz$:

$$ x dydx = θ y, \quad x^2 d^2ydx^2 = θ(θ - 1) y $$

Substituting these transforms converts the Cauchy-Euler equation into $[a θ(θ - 1) + b θ + c] y = R(e^z)$, which can be solved using standard linear differential operator rules.

3. Memorize Important Facts (10 min)

  • Core Formula (General Solution): $y(x) = y_c(x) + y_p(x) = C_1 y_1(x) + C_2 y_2(x) + y_p(x)$
  • Resonance Failure Rule ($eax$): $1(D - a)^n eax = x^nn! eax$
  • Trigonometric Resonance Shortcut: $1D^2 + a^2 \sin(ax) = -x2a \cos(ax)$ and $1D^2 + a^2 \cos(ax) = x2a \sin(ax)$
  • Polynomial Rule ($x^m$): $1f(D) x^m = [f(D)]-1 x^m$, expanded using Binomial Expansion $(1 ± z)-1 = 1 \mp z + z^2 \mp z^3 + \dots$
  • Variation of Parameters Wronskian: $W(y_1, y_2) = \begin{vmatrix} y_1 & y_2 \\ y_1' & y_2' \end{vmatrix} = y_1 y_2' - y_2 y_1' \neq 0$
  • Variation of Parameters Formulas: $u(x) = -∫ y_2 RW dx$, $v(x) = ∫ y_1 RW dx$
  • Cauchy-Euler Substitution: $x = e^z \implies x D = θ, \, x^2 D^2 = θ(θ - 1)$

4. Aviation Application (5–10 min)

Second-order linear differential equations are the mathematical cornerstone of flight dynamics, aircraft stability, aeroelasticity, and air safety engineering. The application scenarios directly tested in aeronautical evaluations include:

1. Longitudinal Aircraft Stability (Short-Period Pitch and Phugoid Modes):
The baseline linearized longitudinal pitching equation of motion for an aircraft trimmed at speed $U_0$ is governed by: $$ \ddot{α} - (M_q + M_{\dot{α}}) \dot{α} - M_α α = M\delta_e \delta_e(t) $$ Where $α$ is the angle of attack disturbance, $M_q$ is pitch damping, $M_α$ is static longitudinal stability, and $\delta_e$ is elevator deflection. The homogeneous solution $y_c(t)$ determines whether the dynamic response is statically and dynamically stable (damped sinusoidal oscillation), while the particular integral $y_p(t)$ provides the steady-state trim pitch response under control surface input $\delta_e(t)$.

2. Landing Gear Shock Absorber Dynamics:
During touchdown landing impact, the vertical displacement $z(t)$ of the aircraft main landing gear strut is modeled as a forced damped harmonic oscillator: $$ m \ddot{z} + c \dot{z} + k z = Frunway(t) $$ The damping ratio $\zeta = c2√km$ determines structural energy dissipation. Air crash safety investigation analyzes $y_p(t)$ under transient runway unevenness forcing functions $F(t)$ to assess bottoming-out structural load limits.

3. Aeroelastic Wing Flutter and Gust Load Response:
Under atmospheric turbulence, structural bending deflection $h(t)$ responds to continuous sharp gust profiles modeled as $R(t) = F_0 e-λ t \cos(ω t)$. The inverse operator exponential shift theorem is directly deployed to extract transient deflection signatures and compute maximum wing root bending moments.

5. UPSC ASO Focus (10 min)

  • Frequently Asked Facts:
    • The Wronskian of linearly independent solutions to $y'' + P(x)y' + Q(x)y = 0$ obeys Abel's Identity: $W(x) = W(x_0) e-∫x_0^x P(t) dt$.
    • In inverse operators, replacing $D^2 \to -a^2$ applies strictly to $D^2$ terms, never to individual $D$ terms directly without rationalization.
    • For Cauchy-Euler equations, $x=0$ is a regular singular point; the method of substitution $x=e^z$ is valid strictly for $x > 0$.
  • Conceptual Traps:
    • Trap 1 (Boundary Condition Premature Evaluation): Applying initial conditions $y(0)=y_0, y'(0)=v_0$ directly to the Complementary Function $y_c$ before adding the Particular Integral $y_p$. Initial conditions MUST be applied to the complete total solution $y(x) = y_c(x) + y_p(x)$.
    • Trap 2 (Trigonometric Operator Substitution Error): Writing $1D^2 + 4 \cos(2x) = 1-4 + 4 \cos(2x) = 10$ and stopping, instead of recognizing resonance and applying $x 12D \cos(2x) = x2 ∫ \cos(2x) dx = x \sin(2x)4$.
    • Trap 3 (Sign Error in $D^2 \to -a^2$): Confusing $-a^2$ with $(-a)^2$. For $\sin(3x)$, $D^2$ is replaced by $-3^2 = -9$, NOT $+9$.

6. Practice Questions (10-12 UPSC ASO Standard MCQs)

Q1. What is the Particular Integral ($y_p$) of the differential equation $d^2ydx^2 + 4y = \sin(2x)$?
A) $-x4 \cos(2x)$
B) $x4 \cos(2x)$
C) $-14 \cos(2x)$
D) $x2 \sin(2x)$
Step-by-step solution:
The ODE is $(D^2 + 4) y = \sin(2x)$.
Applying inverse operator: $y_p = 1D^2 + 4 \sin(2x)$.
Substituting $D^2 \to -2^2 = -4$ gives $f(-a^2) = -4 + 4 = 0$ (Resonance Case).
Using derivative rule: $y_p = x · 1ddD(D^2 + 4) \sin(2x) = x · 12D \sin(2x)$.
Since $1D$ represents integration w.r.t. $x$:
$y_p = x2 ∫ \sin(2x) dx = x2 (-\cos(2x)2) = -x4 \cos(2x)$.
Q2. Find the Wronskian $W(y_1, y_2)$ of the two fundamental solutions $y_1(x) = e2x$ and $y_2(x) = e-3x$ for a homogeneous second-order system.
A) $5 e-x$
B) $-5 e-x$
C) $-e-x$
D) $e-x$
Step-by-step solution:
$y_1 = e2x \implies y_1' = 2 e2x$.
$y_2 = e-3x \implies y_2' = -3 e-3x$.
$W(y_1, y_2) = y_1 y_2' - y_2 y_1' = (e2x)(-3 e-3x) - (e-3x)(2 e2x) = -3 e-x - 2 e-x = -5 e-x$.
Q3. What is the complementary function of the Cauchy-Euler equation $x^2 d^2ydx^2 - 3x dydx + 4y = 0$ for $x > 0$?
A) $y_c = C_1 x^2 + C_2 x-2$
B) $y_c = (C_1 + C_2 \ln x) x^2$
C) $y_c = C_1 e2x + C_2 x e2x$
D) $y_c = (C_1 + C_2 x) x^2$
Step-by-step solution:
Let $x = e^z \implies \ln x = z$. Define $θ = ddz$.
$x y' = θ y$ and $x^2 y'' = θ(θ - 1)y$.
Substitute into equation: $[θ(θ - 1) - 3θ + 4] y = 0 \implies (θ^2 - 4θ + 4) y = 0$.
Auxiliary equation: $m^2 - 4m + 4 = 0 \implies (m - 2)^2 = 0 \implies m_1 = 2, m_2 = 2$.
In $z$-domain: $y(z) = (C_1 + C_2 z) e2z$.
Transforming back to $x$-domain ($e^z = x, z = \ln x$): $y_c(x) = (C_1 + C_2 \ln x) x^2$.
Q4. Determine the Particular Integral for $(D^2 - 3D + 2)y = e3x$.
A) $12 e3x$
B) $13 e3x$
C) $x e3x$
D) $16 e3x$
Step-by-step solution:
$y_p = 1f(D) e3x = 1D^2 - 3D + 2 e3x$.
Substitute $D = 3$:
$f(3) = (3)^2 - 3(3) + 2 = 9 - 9 + 2 = 2 \neq 0$.
Therefore, $y_p = 12 e3x$.
Q5. An aircraft pitch dynamic model yields characteristic response $(D^2 + 2D + 1)y = e-x$. The Particular Integral $y_p$ is:
A) $x^22 e-x$
B) $x^2 e-x$
C) $x2 e-x$
D) $12 e-x$
Step-by-step solution:
$y_p = 1(D + 1)^2 e-x$.
Here $a = -1$, and $f(-1) = (-1+1)^2 = 0$. The root $-1$ has multiplicity $k = 2$.
Using formula $1(D - a)^k eax = x^kk! eax$ for $a = -1, k = 2$:
$y_p = x^22! e-x = x^22 e-x$.
Q6. Evaluate the Particular Integral of $(D^2 - 4)y = x^2$.
A) $-x^24 - 18$
B) $x^24 + 18$
C) $-x^24 + 14$
D) $-x^22 - 14$
Step-by-step solution:
$y_p = 1D^2 - 4 x^2 = 1-4(1 - D^24) x^2 = -14 (1 - D^24)-1 x^2$.
Binomial expansion $(1 - z)-1 = 1 + z + z^2 + \dots$:
$y_p = -14 (1 + D^24 + \mathcal{O}(D^4)) x^2$.
Compute derivatives: $D(x^2) = 2x$, $D^2(x^2) = 2$.
$y_p = -14 ( x^2 + 24 ) = -14 ( x^2 + 12 ) = -x^24 - 18$.
Q7. Using Exponential Shift Theorem, the Particular Integral of $(D^2 - 2D + 1)y = x e^x$ is:
A) $x^36 e^x$
B) $x^22 e^x$
C) $x^3 e^x$
D) $x^33 e^x$
Step-by-step solution:
$y_p = 1(D - 1)^2 [e^x · x]$.
By Exponential Shift: $1f(D) [eax V(x)] = eax 1f(D + a) V(x)$.
Here $a = 1$: $y_p = e^x 1((D + 1) - 1)^2 x = e^x 1D^2 x$.
$1D^2 x$ implies integrating $x$ twice w.r.t $x$:
$1D x = x^22 \implies 1D^2 x = ∫ x^22 dx = x^36$.
Thus, $y_p = x^36 e^x$.
Q8. For an un-damped flight control actuator system governed by $d^2ydt^2 + 9y = 0$ with initial conditions $y(0) = 2$ and $y'(0) = 6$, the displacement $y(t)$ is:
A) $2 \cos(3t) + 2 \sin(3t)$
B) $2 \cos(3t) + 6 \sin(3t)$
C) $3 \cos(2t) + 2 \sin(2t)$
D) $\cos(3t) + 2 \sin(3t)$
Step-by-step solution:
Auxiliary equation: $m^2 + 9 = 0 \implies m = ± 3i$.
General Solution: $y(t) = C_1 \cos(3t) + C_2 \sin(3t)$.
Apply $y(0) = 2$: $C_1 \cos(0) + C_2 \sin(0) = 2 \implies C_1 = 2$.
Derivative: $y'(t) = -3 C_1 \sin(3t) + 3 C_2 \cos(3t)$.
Apply $y'(0) = 6$: $-3(2)(0) + 3 C_2 (1) = 6 \implies 3 C_2 = 6 \implies C_2 = 2$.
Solution: $y(t) = 2 \cos(3t) + 2 \sin(3t)$.
Q9. In the Method of Variation of Parameters for $y'' + y = \sec x$, what is the calculated value of $u(x)$ if $y_p(x) = u(x)\cos x + v(x)\sin x$?
A) $\ln|\cos x|$
B) $-\ln|\cos x|$
C) $x$
D) $\tan x$
Step-by-step solution:
$y_1 = \cos x, y_2 = \sin x, R(x) = \sec x$.
Wronskian $W = y_1 y_2' - y_2 y_1' = \cos x(\cos x) - \sin x(-\sin x) = \cos^2 x + \sin^2 x = 1$.
$u(x) = -∫ y_2 RW dx = -∫ \sin x · \sec x1 dx = -∫ \tan x \, dx = \ln|\cos x|$.
Q10. The short-period pitching oscillation of an aircraft has natural frequency $ω_n = 5 rad/s$ and damping ratio $\zeta = 0.6$. The roots of its homogeneous second-order characteristic equation are:
A) $-3 ± 4i$
B) $-4 ± 3i$
C) $-0.6 ± 5i$
D) $-3 ± 5i$
Step-by-step solution:
Standard 2nd-order characteristic equation: $m^2 + 2\zetaω_n m + ω_n^2 = 0$.
Here $2\zetaω_n = 2(0.6)(5) = 6$ and $ω_n^2 = 25$.
Equation: $m^2 + 6m + 25 = 0$.
Roots: $m = -6 ± √36 - 1002 = -6 ± √-642 = -3 ± 4i$.
Q11. What is the value of $1D - 2 (x)$?
A) $-x2 - 14$
B) $x2 + 14$
C) $-x2 + 14$
D) $-x - 12$
Step-by-step solution:
$1D - 2 x = 1-2(1 - D/2) x = -12 (1 - D2)-1 x$.
Expand binomially: $-12 (1 + D2 + \dots) x = -12 (x + 12) = -x2 - 14$.
Q12. What is the total general solution of $x^2 y'' - x y' + y = 0$?
A) $y = (C_1 + C_2 \ln x) x$
B) $y = C_1 x + C_2 x^2$
C) $y = C_1 e^x + C_2 x e^x$
D) $y = C_1 \cos(\ln x) + C_2 \sin(\ln x)$
Step-by-step solution:
Cauchy-Euler form with $x = e^z, θ = ddz$.
$[θ(θ - 1) - θ + 1] y = 0 \implies (θ^2 - 2θ + 1) y = 0$.
Auxiliary equation: $m^2 - 2m + 1 = 0 \implies (m - 1)^2 = 0 \implies m = 1, 1$.
In $z$-domain: $y = (C_1 + C_2 z) e^z$.
In $x$-domain: $y = (C_1 + C_2 \ln x) x$.

Q13. (Grand Test - Statement Analysis)
Consider two continuous functions $y_1(x)$ and $y_2(x)$ defined on an interval $I$, which are solutions to $y'' + P(x)y' + Q(x)y = 0$. Consider the following statements:
I. If $W(y_1, y_2) = 0$ at even a single point $x_0 \in I$, then $W(y_1, y_2) = 0$ everywhere on $I$.
II. If $y_1(x)$ and $y_2(x)$ are linearly independent, their Wronskian can vanish at isolated boundary points if $P(x)$ is discontinuous.
III. The Particular Integral $y_p$ obtained via inverse operators depends on the choice of arbitrary integration constants.
Which of the above statements are CORRECT?
A) I and II only
B) II and III only
C) I and III only
D) I, II and III
Detailed Analysis:
- Statement I is CORRECT: By Abel's Identity $W(x) = W(x_0) e-∫x_0^x P(t)dt$, since the exponential factor is never zero, if $W(x_0) = 0$, then $W(x) = 0$ for all $x \in I$ (assuming continuous $P(x)$).
- Statement II is CORRECT: If $P(x)$ is discontinuous at a point, Abel's identity breaks down at that location, allowing the Wronskian to vanish locally.
- Statement III is INCORRECT: The Particular Integral $y_p$ is a specific solution with NO arbitrary integration constants.
Q14. (Grand Test - Matching)
Match the non-homogeneous term $R(x)$ in $(D^2 + 4)y = R(x)$ with its corresponding Particular Integral $y_p(x)$:

List I ($R(x)$):
P. $e2x$
Q. $\sin(2x)$
R. $x^2$
S. $e0x = 4$

List II ($y_p(x)$):
1. $-x4\cos(2x)$
2. $18e2x$
3. $1$
4. $x^24 - 18$
A) P-2, Q-1, R-4, S-3
B) P-1, Q-2, R-3, S-4
C) P-2, Q-4, R-1, S-3
D) P-3, Q-1, R-4, S-2
Step-by-step evaluation:

Q15. (Grand Test - Flight Dynamics Aeroelastic Gust Problem)
An aircraft wing dynamic bending response subject to dynamic engine unbalance forcing is modeled as:
$$ d^2ydt^2 + 2 dydt + 2y = e-t \cos t $$ What is the steady-state Particular Integral $y_p(t)$?
A) $t2 e-t \sin t$
B) $t e-t \cos t$
C) $t^22 e-t \cos t$
D) $12 e-t \sin t$
Step-by-step solution:
$y_p = 1D^2 + 2D + 2 [e-t \cos t]$.
Apply Exponential Shift ($a = -1$):
$y_p = e-t 1(D - 1)^2 + 2(D - 1) + 2 \cos t = e-t 1(D^2 - 2D + 1) + (2D - 2) + 2 \cos t = e-t 1D^2 + 1 \cos t$.
Now apply trigonometric resonance rule for $1D^2 + 1 \cos t$ ($a = 1, f(-1^2) = 0$):
$1D^2 + 1 \cos t = t2(1) \sin t = t \sin t2$.
Multiplying by $e-t$: $y_p(t) = t2 e-t \sin t$.

8. Error Analysis & Common Traps (5 min)

In competitive examinations (UPSC ASO / GATE Aeronautical), candidates frequently make preventable mistakes in second-order ODEs. Master these core error patterns to avoid traps:

  1. Premature Application of Initial Conditions:
    Incorrect: Solving $y_c(x) = C_1 y_1 + C_2 y_2$, setting $y_c(0) = y_0$ to find $C_1, C_2$, and then adding $y_p(x)$.
    Correct: Construct the FULL general solution $y(x) = y_c(x) + y_p(x)$ FIRST, then differentiate to find $y'(x)$, and ONLY THEN apply initial conditions $y(0) = y_0, y'(0) = v_0$ to evaluate $C_1$ and $C_2$.
  2. Misapplication of $D^2 \to -a^2$ in Inverse Operators:
    Incorrect: Replacing $D$ with $-a$ in $1D^2 + D + 1 \sin(2x)$, or writing $D^2 \to (-2)^2 = +4$.
    Correct: $D^2$ MUST be replaced strictly by $-a^2 = - (2)^2 = -4$. If a linear term $D$ remains in the denominator (e.g., $1D - 3$), rationalize by multiplying numerator and denominator by $(D + 3)$ to convert the denominator back into $D^2 - 9$.
  3. Cauchy-Euler Independent Variable Confusion:
    Incorrect: Solving $x^2 y'' + x y' + y = x$ and writing the complementary function as $y_c = C_1 \cos x + C_2 \sin x$.
    Correct: The transformed auxiliary roots correspond to the variable $z = \ln x$. Thus $y_c = C_1 \cos(\ln x) + C_2 \sin(\ln x)$.
