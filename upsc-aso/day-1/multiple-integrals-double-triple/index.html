Multiple integrals – double, triple

Day 1 of 129 Engineering Fundamentals Phase 1: Foundations 60-90 min Master Cycle

1. Understand (5–10 min)

In Aeronautical Engineering and Mathematical Physics, continuous field variables such as mass density distributions, pressure distributions over aerodynamic surfaces, velocity potentials, and thermal flux profiles are analyzed using Multiple Integrals. Double integrals ($\iint$) extend the concept of a single definite integral to functions of two real variables $f(x,y)$ over a two-dimensional domain $R \subset \mathbb{R}^2$, whereas triple integrals ($\iiint$) extend integration to functions of three variables $f(x,y,z)$ over a three-dimensional spatial region $V \subset \mathbb{R}^3$.

Under the mathematical framework prescribed for the UPSC Air Safety Officer (ASO) examination, multiple integrals are treated not merely as geometric volume computations, but as foundational tools for evaluating tensor quantities—such as aircraft moments of inertia ($Ixx, Iyy, Izz, Ixy$), centers of gravity ($\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z}$), aerodynamic lift and drag forces via pressure field integration, and boundary layer mass displacement thickness. Mastery of change of variables via the Jacobian determinant ($J$), region partitioning, order-of-integration reversals, and curvilinear coordinate systems (polar, cylindrical, spherical) is essential for solving UPSC-level problem sets.

2. Learn the Concept (15–20 min)

The mathematical theory of multiple integration relies on Riemann sums over multi-dimensional partitions. Let $R$ be a closed, bounded region in $\mathbb{R}^2$. Partition $R$ into $n$ sub-rectangles $Δ A_k = Δ x_k Δ y_k$. The double integral of $f(x,y)$ over $R$ is defined as the limit of the Riemann sum as the diameter of the partition approaches zero:

$$\iint_R f(x,y) \, dA = \lim\max(Δ x_k, Δ y_k) \to 0 ∑k=1^n f(x_k^*, y_k^*) \, Δ A_k$$

1. Fubini's Theorem & Iterated Integrals

If $f(x,y)$ is continuous on a region $R$, Fubini's Theorem permits the evaluation of a double integral via iterated single integrals:

  • Type I Region (x-simple): Bounded by $a ≤ x ≤ b$ and $g_1(x) ≤ y ≤ g_2(x)$: $$\iint_R f(x,y) \, dA = ∫_a^b [ ∫g_1(x)g_2(x) f(x,y) \, dy ] dx$$
  • Type II Region (y-simple): Bounded by $c ≤ y ≤ d$ and $h_1(y) ≤ x ≤ h_2(y)$: $$\iint_R f(x,y) \, dA = ∫_c^d [ ∫h_1(y)h_2(y) f(x,y) \, dx ] dy$$

2. Transformation of Variables and the Jacobian Determinant

To simplify complex integration domains, a spatial transformation $x = g(u,v), y = h(u,v)$ maps a region $Rxy$ in the $xy$-plane to a region $Ruv$ in the $uv$-plane. The differential area element transforms as $dx \, dy = |J| \, du \, dv$, where $J$ is the Jacobian determinant:

$$ J = ∂(x,y)∂(u,v) = \begin{vmatrix} ∂ x∂ u & ∂ x∂ v \\[0.5em] ∂ y∂ u & ∂ y∂ v \end{vmatrix} = ∂ x∂ u∂ y∂ v - ∂ x∂ v∂ y∂ u $$

For 3D transformation $x = g(u,v,w), y = h(u,v,w), z = k(u,v,w)$, the differential volume element transforms as $dx \, dy \, dz = |J| \, du \, dv \, dw$, where:

$$ J = ∂(x,y,z)∂(u,v,w) = \begin{vmatrix} ∂ x∂ u & ∂ x∂ v & ∂ x∂ w \\[0.5em] ∂ y∂ u & ∂ y∂ v & ∂ y∂ w \\[0.5em] ∂ z∂ u & ∂ z∂ v & ∂ z∂ w \end{vmatrix} $$

3. Curvilinear Coordinate Systems

A. Polar Coordinates $(r, θ)$: $x = r \cosθ$, $y = r \sinθ$. The Jacobian is $J = r$. Thus, $dA = r \, dr \, dθ$.

B. Cylindrical Coordinates $(r, θ, z)$: $x = r \cosθ$, $y = r \sinθ$, $z = z$. The Jacobian is $J = r$. Thus, $dV = r \, dr \, dθ \, dz$.

C. Spherical Coordinates $(ρ, φ, θ)$: $x = ρ \sinφ \cosθ$, $y = ρ \sinφ \sinθ$, $z = ρ \cosφ$ (where $φ$ is the polar angle from the zenith, $0 ≤ φ ≤ π$). The Jacobian is $J = ρ^2 \sinφ$. Thus, $dV = ρ^2 \sinφ \, dρ \, dφ \, dθ$.

4. Surface Area Integral Derivation

For a continuous surface defined explicitly as $z = f(x,y)$ over a projected planar region $Rxy$, the differential surface element $dS$ is given by:

$$ dS = √1 + (∂ z∂ x)^2 + (∂ z∂ y)^2 \, dx \, dy $$

The total surface area $S$ is calculated as $S = \iint_{Rxy} √1 + z_x^2 + z_y^2 \, dx \, dy$.

5. Dirichlet's and Liouville's Theorems for Multiple Integrals

Dirichlet's Integral Theorem: For a 3D domain $V$ bounded by $x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0, z ≥ 0$ and $x + y + z ≤ 1$:

$$ \iiint_V xl-1 ym-1 zn-1 \, dx \, dy \, dz = \Gamma(l)\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)\Gamma(l + m + n + 1) $$

Liouville's Extension: If the domain is $x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0, z ≥ 0$ and $h_1 ≤ x + y + z ≤ h_2$:

$$ \iiint_V f(x+y+z) \, xl-1 ym-1 zn-1 \, dx \, dy \, dz = \Gamma(l)\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)\Gamma(l + m + n) ∫h_1h_2 f(u) \, ul+m+n-1 \, du $$

3. Memorize Important Facts (10 min)

  • Polar Coordinate Area Element: $dA = r \, dr \, dθ$ where $J = r$
  • Cylindrical Coordinate Volume Element: $dV = r \, dr \, dθ \, dz$ where $J = r$
  • Spherical Coordinate Volume Element: $dV = ρ^2 \sinφ \, dρ \, dφ \, dθ$ where $J = ρ^2 \sinφ$
  • Surface Area Element: $dS = √1 + (∂ z∂ x)^2 + (∂ z∂ y)^2 \, dx \, dy$
  • Dirichlet's Integral Formula (3D): $\iiint_V xl-1 ym-1 zn-1 \, dx \, dy \, dz = \Gamma(l)\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)\Gamma(l+m+n+1)$ for $x,y,z ≥ 0, x+y+z ≤ 1$
  • Centroid Coordinates ($\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z}$): $\bar{x} = 1M \iiint_V x ρ(x,y,z) \, dV$ where total mass $M = \iiint_V ρ(x,y,z) \, dV$
  • Moment of Inertia about z-axis ($Izz$): $Izz = \iiint_V (x^2 + y^2) ρ(x,y,z) \, dV$
  • Product of Inertia ($Ixy$): $Ixy = \iiint_V x y ρ(x,y,z) \, dV$

4. Aviation Application (5–10 min)

Multiple integration forms the computational backbone of aeronautical analysis, air safety investigations, and flight safety certification. Key engineering applications include:

1. Determination of Aircraft Center of Gravity (CG) and Inertia Tensors

Aircraft dynamic stability depends heavily on the precise location of the Center of Gravity ($\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z}$) and the rigid-body mass moment of inertia matrix $[I]$. For an asymmetric structural wing panel with variable density distribution $ρ(x,y,z)$:

$$ [I] = \begin{bmatrix} Ixx & -Ixy & -Ixz \\ -Iyx & Iyy & -Iyz \\ -Izx & -Izy & Izz \end{bmatrix}, \quad Ixx = \iiint_V (y^2 + z^2) ρ(x,y,z) \, dx \, dy \, dz $$

In crash investigations, post-impact structural deformities alter the moment of inertia tensor, which safety officers calculate using triple integrals across recovered structural components to reconstruct aircraft spin and tumbling dynamics prior to impact.

2. Aerodynamic Pressure Integration and Wing Load Distribution

The total aerodynamic lift vector $\mathbf{L}$ and pitching moment $MCG$ acting on an aircraft wing are derived by double-integrating the local pressure differential $P(x,y) = Plower(x,y) - Pupper(x,y)$ and skin-friction shear stress $τ_w(x,y)$ over the wing surface planform $S$:

$$ \mathbf{L} = \iint_S ( P(x,y) \cosα + τ_w(x,y) \sinα ) \, dx \, dy $$

3. Aircraft Fuel Slosh and Volumetric Capacity

Integral fuel tanks located inside swept wings are bounded by complex wing skins, spar webs, and rib surfaces. Calculating total fuel volume $Vfuel$ and dynamic slosh forces during high-$g$ maneuvers requires calculating triple integrals in transformed boundary coordinates:

$$ Vfuel = \iiint_V \, dx \, dy \, dz = ∫_{xroot}xtip ∫_{yLE(x)}yTE(x) ∫_{zbottom(x,y)}ztop(x,y) \, dz \, dy \, dx $$

5. UPSC ASO Focus (10 min)

  • Frequently Asked Facts:
    • Order of integration changes alter the limits, but the numerical evaluation remains invariant (Fubini's Theorem).
    • In Gaussian double integrals $∫-∞∞ ∫-∞∞ e-(x^2+y^2) \, dx \, dy$, converting to polar coordinates gives $π$, yielding $∫-∞∞ e-x^2 dx = √π$.
    • The Jacobian of transformation satisfies the reciprocal property: $J(x,y) \to (u,v) · J(u,v) \to (x,y) = 1$.
    • Dirichlet's integral is extended using Gamma function identities: $\Gamma(n) = (n-1)!$ for positive integers, and $\Gamma(1/2) = √π$.
  • Conceptual Traps:
    • Jacobian Omission Trap: Forgetting the scaling factor $r$ in polar/cylindrical coordinates ($r \, dr \, dθ$) or $ρ^2 \sinφ$ in spherical coordinates.
    • Limit Reversal Fallacy: Incorrectly swapping limit boundaries when reversing order of integration without drawing the 2D bounding region.
    • Spherical Angle Confusion: Reversing the limits of $φ$ ($0 ≤ φ ≤ π$, zenith angle) with $θ$ ($0 ≤ θ ≤ 2π$, azimuthal angle).
    • Symmetry Abuse: Assuming $\iint_R f(x,y) \, dA = 0$ for odd functions without ensuring the region $R$ is strictly symmetric about the corresponding axis.

6. Practice Questions (10-12 UPSC ASO Standard MCQs)

Q1. Evaluate the double integral $I = ∫_0^1 ∫_y^1 ex^2 \, dx \, dy$ by reversing the order of integration.
A) $12(e - 1)$
B) $e - 1$
C) $12 e$
D) $2(e - 1)$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Region of integration in original integral: $0 ≤ y ≤ 1$ and $y ≤ x ≤ 1$.
2. Sketching the region $R$: bounded by $x = y$, $x = 1$, $y = 0$, and $y = 1$. This is a triangle in the $xy$-plane with vertices at $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, and $(1,1)$.
3. Reversing order of integration (x-simple to y-simple): $x$ ranges from $0$ to $1$, and for a fixed $x$, $y$ ranges from $0$ to $x$.
4. Expressing the reversed integral: $$ I = ∫_0^1 [ ∫_0^x ex^2 \, dy ] dx = ∫_0^1 ex^2 [y]_0^x \, dx = ∫_0^1 x ex^2 \, dx $$ 5. Substitute $u = x^2 \Rightarrow du = 2x \, dx$: $$ I = 12 ∫_0^1 e^u \, du = 12 [e^u]_0^1 = 12(e - 1) $$ Thus, Option A is correct.
Q2. Calculate the value of Dirichlet's triple integral $I = \iiint_V x^0 y^0 z^0 \, dx \, dy \, dz = \iiint_V dx \, dy \, dz$ over the region $x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0, z ≥ 0$ such that $x + y + z ≤ 1$.
A) $1/2$
B) $1/3$
C) $1/6$
D) $1/24$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Using Dirichlet's formula $\iiint_V xl-1 ym-1 zn-1 \, dx \, dy \, dz = \Gamma(l)\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)\Gamma(l+m+n+1)$.
2. Here, $l-1 = 0 \implies l = 1$, $m-1 = 0 \implies m = 1$, $n-1 = 0 \implies n = 1$.
3. Substituting into the formula: $$ I = \Gamma(1)\Gamma(1)\Gamma(1)\Gamma(1+1+1+1) = 1 · 1 · 1\Gamma(4) = 13! = 16 $$ Thus, Option C is correct.
Q3. What is the Jacobian $J = ∂(x,y,z)∂(ρ,φ,θ)$ for transformation from Cartesian to Spherical polar coordinates ($x = ρ \sinφ \cosθ, y = ρ \sinφ \sinθ, z = ρ \cosφ$)?
A) $ρ \sinφ$
B) $ρ^2 \sinφ$
C) $ρ^2 \cosφ$
D) $ρ \cos^2φ$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Construct the $3 × 3$ Jacobian matrix: $$ J = \begin{vmatrix} \sinφ\cosθ & ρ\cosφ\cosθ & -ρ\sinφ\sinθ \\ \sinφ\sinθ & ρ\cosφ\sinθ & ρ\sinφ\cosθ \\ \cosφ & -ρ\sinφ & 0 \end{vmatrix} $$ 2. Expanding along the third row: $$ J = \cosφ ( ρ^2 \sinφ \cosφ \cos^2θ + ρ^2 \sinφ \cosφ \sin^2θ ) - (-ρ \sinφ) ( ρ \sin^2φ \cos^2θ + ρ \sin^2φ \sin^2θ ) $$ 3. Simplifying: $$ J = \cosφ (ρ^2 \sinφ \cosφ) + ρ \sinφ (ρ \sin^2φ) = ρ^2 \sinφ \cos^2φ + ρ^2 \sin^3φ $$ $$ J = ρ^2 \sinφ (\cos^2φ + \sin^2φ) = ρ^2 \sinφ $$ Thus, Option B is correct.
Q4. Compute the area enclosed by the cardioid $r = a(1 + \cosθ)$ using double integration.
A) $π a^2$
B) $32π a^2$
C) $2π a^2$
D) $52π a^2$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Area $A = \iint_R dA = ∫_02π ∫_0a(1+\cosθ) r \, dr \, dθ$.
2. Inner integration with respect to $r$: $$ ∫_0a(1+\cosθ) r \, dr = [ r^22 ]_0a(1+\cosθ) = a^22 (1 + \cosθ)^2 $$ 3. Outer integration with respect to $θ$: $$ A = a^22 ∫_02π (1 + 2\cosθ + \cos^2θ) \, dθ $$ 4. Evaluate components: $∫_02π 1 \, dθ = 2π$, $∫_02π \cosθ \, dθ = 0$, and $∫_02π \cos^2θ \, dθ = π$.
5. Summing terms: $$ A = a^22 (2π + 0 + π) = 32π a^2 $$ Thus, Option B is correct.
Q5. Find the centroid $\bar{y}$ of a uniform semi-circular lamina defined by $x^2 + y^2 ≤ a^2$ and $y ≥ 0$.
A) $2aπ$
B) $3a4π$
C) $4a3π$
D) $a2$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Total Area $A = π a^22$.
2. Moment about x-axis $M_x = \iint_R y \, dA$. Converting to polar coordinates ($y = r\sinθ, dA = r\,dr\,dθ$): $$ M_x = ∫_0^π ∫_0^a (r \sinθ) r \, dr \, dθ = ∫_0^π \sinθ \, dθ ∫_0^a r^2 \, dr $$ 3. Calculate integrals: $∫_0^π \sinθ \, dθ = [-\cosθ]_0^π = 2$, and $∫_0^a r^2 \, dr = a^33$.
4. $M_x = 2 · a^33 = 2a^33$.
5. Centroid $\bar{y} = M_xA = 2a^33π a^22 = 4a3π$.
Thus, Option C is correct.
Q6. Evaluate the total mass $M$ of a solid cylinder $x^2 + y^2 ≤ R^2, 0 ≤ z ≤ h$ if the mass density is given by $ρ(r, θ, z) = z \, r^2$.
A) $π R^4 h^24$
B) $π R^3 h^23$
C) $π R^4 h2$
D) $π R^2 h^36$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Set up mass integral in cylindrical coordinates: $dV = r \, dr \, dθ \, dz$. $$ M = ∫_02π ∫_0^R ∫_0^h (z \, r^2) r \, dz \, dr \, dθ $$ 2. Separate variables: $$ M = ( ∫_02π dθ ) ( ∫_0^R r^3 \, dr ) ( ∫_0^h z \, dz ) $$ 3. Evaluate each integral: $$ ∫_02π dθ = 2π $$ $$ ∫_0^R r^3 \, dr = R^44 $$ $$ ∫_0^h z \, dz = h^22 $$ 4. Multiply results: $$ M = 2π · R^44 · h^22 = π R^4 h^24 $$ Thus, Option A is correct.
Q7. The value of the integral $I = ∫_0^∞ ∫_0^∞ e-(x^2 + y^2) \, dx \, dy$ is:
A) $√π$
B) $π2$
C) $π4$
D) $π$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Transform to polar coordinates: $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$, $dx \, dy = r \, dr \, dθ$.
2. Limits in first quadrant ($x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0$): $r \in [0, ∞)$, $θ \in [0, π/2]$.
3. Integral becomes: $$ I = ∫_0π/2 ∫_0^∞ e-r^2 r \, dr \, dθ $$ 4. Compute inner integral with $u = r^2 \implies du = 2r\,dr$: $$ ∫_0^∞ e-r^2 r \, dr = 12 ∫_0^∞ e-u du = 12 [ -e-u ]_0^∞ = 12 $$ 5. Compute outer integral: $$ I = ∫_0π/2 12 \, dθ = 12 · π2 = π4 $$ Thus, Option C is correct.
Q8. What is the surface area of the paraboloid $z = x^2 + y^2$ cut off by the plane $z = 1$?
A) $π6(5√5 - 1)$
B) $π3(5√5 - 1)$
C) $π6(5√5 + 1)$
D) $π4(3√3 - 1)$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Surface area formula: $S = \iint_R √1 + (∂ z∂ x)^2 + (∂ z∂ y)^2 \, dx \, dy$.
2. Partial derivatives: $∂ z∂ x = 2x$, $∂ z∂ y = 2y$.
3. Integrand term: $√1 + 4x^2 + 4y^2$.
4. Projection $R$ on $xy$-plane for $z ≤ 1$ is $x^2 + y^2 ≤ 1$. Transform to polar coordinates ($r \in [0,1], θ \in [0, 2π]$): $$ S = ∫_02π ∫_0^1 √1 + 4r^2 \, r \, dr \, dθ $$ 5. Substitute $u = 1 + 4r^2 \implies du = 8r \, dr$: $$ ∫_0^1 √1 + 4r^2 \, r \, dr = 18 ∫_1^5 u1/2 du = 18 [ 23 u3/2 ]_1^5 = 112 (53/2 - 1) = 112 (5√5 - 1) $$ 6. Multiply by outer integral $∫_02π dθ = 2π$: $$ S = 2π · 112 (5√5 - 1) = π6(5√5 - 1) $$ Thus, Option A is correct.
Q9. Transform the integral $I = ∫_0^a ∫_0√a^2 - x^2 (x^2 + y^2) \, dy \, dx$ into polar coordinates. Which of the following represents the correct transformed integral?
A) $∫_0π ∫_0^a r^3 \, dr \, dθ$
B) $∫_0π/2 ∫_0^a r^3 \, dr \, dθ$
C) $∫_0π/2 ∫_0^a r^2 \, dr \, dθ$
D) $∫_02π ∫_0^a r^3 \, dr \, dθ$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Region limits: $x$ goes from $0$ to $a$; $y$ goes from $0$ to $√a^2 - x^2$.
2. This describes the upper-right quarter of a circular disk of radius $a$ in the first quadrant ($x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0$).
3. In polar coordinates: $r \in [0, a]$, $θ \in [0, π/2]$.

Q12. What is the volume of the region bounded by the paraboloid $z = x^2 + y^2$ and the plane $z = 4$?
A) $4π$
B) $8π$
C) $16π$
D) $32π$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Volume $V = \iiint_V dV = \iint_R [ ∫x^2+y^2^4 dz ] dA = \iint_R (4 - (x^2 + y^2)) \, dA$.
2. Bounding region $R$ on $xy$-plane is intersection where $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ (circle of radius $R=2$).
3. Convert to polar coordinates ($r \in [0, 2], θ \in [0, 2π]$): $$ V = ∫_02π ∫_0^2 (4 - r^2) r \, dr \, dθ $$ 4. Compute inner integral: $$ ∫_0^2 (4r - r^3) \, dr = [ 2r^2 - r^44 ]_0^2 = 2(4) - 164 = 8 - 4 = 4 $$ 5. Compute outer integral: $$ V = ∫_02π 4 \, dθ = 8π $$ Thus, Option B is correct.

7. Grand Mini Test (Self-Evaluation)

Q13 [UPSC ASO Mini-Test]. Consider the following statements regarding multiple integrals and transformation of variables:
1. If $f(x,y)$ is continuous on a rectangular region $R$, then Fubini's theorem guarantees that the order of integration can be reversed without altering the result.
2. The transformation from Cartesian $(x,y,z)$ to Cylindrical coordinates $(r,θ,z)$ yields a Jacobian determinant equal to $r^2 \sinθ$.
3. The value of the double integral $\iint_R x \, dy \, dx$ over a region symmetric about the $y$-axis is identically zero.
Which of the statements given above are CORRECT?
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 1 and 3 only
C) 2 and 3 only
D) 1, 2 and 3
Detailed Analysis:
• Statement 1 is CORRECT: Fubini's Theorem guarantees that for a continuous function on a compact domain, iterated integrals in any order yield the exact same scalar value.
• Statement 2 is INCORRECT: The Jacobian determinant for Cylindrical coordinates is $J = r$, NOT $r^2 \sinθ$ (which is for Spherical coordinates with $φ$).
• Statement 3 is CORRECT: The integrand $f(x,y) = x$ is an odd function with respect to $x$. Integrating an odd function of $x$ over a domain symmetric about the $y$-axis (where for every $(x,y) \in R$, $(-x,y) \in R$) yields zero.
Hence, statements 1 and 3 are correct $\implies$ Option B.

Q15 [UPSC ASO Mini-Test]. An aircraft wing panel is approximated by a triangular region bounded by $x=0$, $y=0$, and $x+y=L$ in the $xy$-plane with constant surface density $ρ_0$. Evaluate the product of inertia $Ixy = \iint_R x y ρ_0 \, dx \, dy$.
A) $ρ_0 L^412$
B) $ρ_0 L^424$
C) $ρ_0 L^448$
D) $ρ_0 L^36$
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Set up product of inertia integral: $Ixy = ρ_0 ∫_0^L ∫_0L-x x y \, dy \, dx$.
2. Perform inner integral with respect to $y$: $$ ∫_0L-x x y \, dy = x [ y^22 ]_0L-x = x (L - x)^22 = 12 x (L^2 - 2Lx + x^2) = 12 (L^2 x - 2L x^2 + x^3) $$ 3. Perform outer integral with respect to $x$: $$ Ixy = ρ_02 ∫_0^L (L^2 x - 2L x^2 + x^3) \, dx = ρ_02 [ L^2 x^22 - 2L x^33 + x^44 ]_0^L $$ 4. Substitute limits $x = L$: $$ Ixy = ρ_02 ( L^42 - 2L^43 + L^44 ) = ρ_0 L^42 ( 6 - 8 + 312 ) = ρ_0 L^42 ( 112 ) = ρ_0 L^424 $$ Thus, Option B is correct.

8. Error Analysis & Common Traps (5 min)

During competitive UPSC examinations, students frequently commit procedural and analytical errors when evaluating multiple integrals. The most prevalent mistakes include:

  1. Omission of Differential Scale Factors (Jacobians):
    Mistake: Writing $dx \, dy \, dz = dρ \, dφ \, dθ$ in spherical coordinates.
    Correction: Always include the Jacobian scaling factor $J = ρ^2 \sinφ$, giving $dV = ρ^2 \sinφ \, dρ \, dφ \, dθ$.
  2. Incorrect Boundary Identification in Order Reversal:
    Mistake: Simply swapping integral limits ($∫_a^b ∫_c^d \to ∫_c^d ∫_a^b$) when variable limits are present.
    Correction: Non-constant limits mean the region is non-rectangular. Sketch the 2D plane bounding region $R$, determine new intersection points, and re-express variable functions ($x$ as $g(y)$ or $y$ as $f(x)$).
  3. Misinterpreting Spherical Azimuthal and Zenith Angles:
    Mistake: Integrating $φ$ from $0$ to $2π$ and $θ$ from $0$ to $π$.
    Correction: Polar/Zenith angle $φ$ ranges strictly from $0$ (north pole) to $π$ (south pole). Azimuthal angle $θ$ ranges from $0$ to $2π$.
  4. Misapplication of Dirichlet's Theorem:
    Mistake: Applying Dirichlet's integral when variables are not restricted to the positive octant ($x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0, z ≥ 0$) or when $x+y+z ≤ k$ where $k \neq 1$ without scaling.
    Correction: For $x+y+z ≤ k$, transform variables $x = k X, y = k Y, z = k Z$ first before applying the standard unit-tetrahedron formula.

9. Quick Revision & Mind Map (5 min)

Use this rapid-recall cheat sheet for immediate revision prior to the examination:

  • Double Integral Area: $A = \iint_R dx \, dy = \iint_R r \, dr \, dθ$
  • Triple Integral Volume: $V = \iiint_V dx \, dy \, dz = \iiint_V r \, dr \, dθ \, dz = \iiint_V ρ^2 \sinφ \, dρ \, dφ \, dθ$
  • Jacobian Formula (2D): $J = ∂ x∂ u∂ y∂ v - ∂ x∂ v∂ y∂ u$
  • Surface Area: $S = \iint_R √1 + (∂ z∂ x)^2 + (∂ z∂ y)^2 \, dx \, dy$
  • Dirichlet's 3D Integral: $\iiintx,y,z ≥ 0, x+y+z ≤ 1 xl-1 ym-1 zn-1 \, dx \, dy \, dz = \Gamma(l)\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)\Gamma(l+m+n+1)$
  • Gamma Function Values: $\Gamma(1) = 1$, $\Gamma(n) = (n-1)!$, $\Gamma(1/2) = √π$
  • Moment of Inertia Formulas:
    • $Ixx = \iiint (y^2 + z^2) ρ \, dV$
    • $Iyy = \iiint (x^2 + z^2) ρ \, dV$
    • $Izz = \iiint (x^2 + y^2) ρ \, dV$
    • $Ixy = \iiint x y ρ \, dV$
