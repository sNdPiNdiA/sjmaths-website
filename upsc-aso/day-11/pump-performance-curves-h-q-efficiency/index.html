Pump Performance Curves: H-Q, Power & Efficiency

Day 11 of 129 Fluid Machinery & Performance Phase 1: Foundations 70-85 min In-Depth Module

Level 1: Beginner's Glossary & Performance Curve Definitions Basic

Before studying characteristic curves, here is the complete beginner's dictionary of all pump performance terms, parameters, symbols, and operational curves:

1. Discharge / Flow Rate ($Q$)

  • Definition: The volumetric quantity of liquid delivered by the pump per unit time.
  • Symbol: $Q$. SI Unit: $\text{m}^3/\text{s}$ (or $\text{L/s}, \text{m}^3/\text{hr}$).
  • Formula: $Q = A \cdot V_f = \pi D_2 b_2 V_{f2}$.

2. Total Head ($H$) & Shut-Off Head ($H_0$)

  • Head ($H$): The vertical height of a liquid column that the pump can support or lift against gravity. Unit: meters of liquid ($\text{m}$).
  • Shut-Off Head ($H_0$): The maximum theoretical pressure head developed by the pump when the discharge valve is completely closed ($Q = 0$).

3. Best Efficiency Point (BEP)

  • Definition: The specific combination of flow rate ($Q_{BEP}$) and head ($H_{BEP}$) at which the pump achieves its maximum overall efficiency ($\eta_{max}$).
  • Significance: Operating far to the left or right of BEP causes high vibration, recirculation cavitation, and bearing failure.

4. Brake Power ($P_b$) & Hydraulic Power ($P_h$)

  • Hydraulic Power ($P_h$): Useful power transferred to the liquid: $P_h = \rho g Q H$ [$\text{Watts}$].
  • Brake Power ($P_b$): Total mechanical shaft power supplied to the pump by the motor/engine: $P_b = \frac{P_h}{\eta_{overall}} = \frac{\rho g Q H}{\eta}$ [$\text{kW}$].

5. System Resistance Curve ($H_{sys}$)

  • Definition: Total head required to force liquid through external piping, consisting of static elevation lift ($H_{stat}$) plus pipe friction losses ($h_f = K Q^2$): $$H_{sys} = H_{stat} + K Q^2$$
  • Shape: Parabolic curve starting at vertical intercept $H_{stat}$ and rising quadratically with $Q^2$.

6. Operating / Duty Point

  • Definition: The exact point where the Pump $H-Q$ Characteristic Curve intersects the Piping System Curve ($H_{pump}(Q) = H_{sys}(Q)$).
  • Control: Throttling the discharge valve steepens the system curve, moving the operating point to a lower flow rate.

Level 2: Theoretical vs Actual H-Q Derivation & Losses Intermediate

1. Theoretical Euler Head Line

For an idealized pump with infinite blades and radial entry ($V_{w1} = 0$):

$$ H_e = \frac{u_2(u_2 - V_{f2}\cot\beta_2)}{g} = \frac{u_2^2}{g} - \left(\frac{u_2}{\pi D_2 b_2 g}\cot\beta_2\right) Q = A - B Q \cot\beta_2 $$

2. Real Internal Head Losses

The actual manometric head ($H_m$) delivered by the pump is obtained by subtracting three major loss components from the theoretical line:

  • 1. Slip Loss ($h_{slip}$): Due to finite blade passages, $\sigma < 1 \implies \Delta H_{slip} = (1 - \sigma)\frac{V_{w2\infty}u_2}{g}$.
  • 2. Fluid Friction & Viscous Shear Loss ($h_f$): Flow through impeller channels, volute casing, and delivery diffuser: $$h_f = K_1 Q^2$$
  • 3. Shock / Incidence Losses ($h_{shock}$): Occurs at impeller inlet when operating away from the design discharge ($Q \ne Q_{des}$): $$h_{shock} = K_2(Q - Q_{des})^2$$
$$ H_m(Q) = \sigma H_e - K_1 Q^2 - K_2(Q - Q_{des})^2 $$

This parabolic deduction transforms the straight theoretical line into the characteristic drooping actual $H-Q$ curve.

Level 3: System Head Curve & Operating Point (Duty Point) Advanced

1. The System Resistance Curve ($H_{sys}$)

The total head required by the piping system to sustain a discharge $Q$ is composed of static elevation lift plus dynamic frictional resistance:

$$ H_{sys} = H_{stat} + \sum h_f = (h_s + h_d) + \left(\frac{4 f L}{2 g D A^2} + \sum \frac{K_{minor}}{2 g A^2}\right) Q^2 = H_{stat} + K_{sys} Q^2 $$

2. Operating Point (Duty Point)

  • The stable working condition occurs exactly where the Pump Performance Curve intersects the System Resistance Curve ($H_m(Q) = H_{sys}(Q)$).
  • Throttling Regulation: Closing a delivery discharge valve increases $K_{sys}$, making the system curve steeper and shifting the operating point to a lower flow ($Q$) at a higher head ($H$), but wastes throttling energy ($\Delta H_{valve}$).
  • Speed Regulation (VSD): Varying impeller speed $N$ shifts the entire pump curve via affinity laws ($Q \propto N, H \propto N^2, P \propto N^3$), saving up to 50% electrical power compared to throttling.

Level 4: Performance Curves Summary & Key Formulas

Curve Parameter Behavior at $Q = 0$ (Shut-Off) Behavior at BEP ($Q_{des}$) Behavior at High $Q$ ($Q > Q_{des}$)
Head ($H$) Maximum (Shut-off head $H_0 \approx \frac{u_2^2}{2g} \text{ to } \frac{u_2^2}{g}$) Rated design head ($H_{des}$) Drops continuously (Drooping curve)
Power ($P$) Minimum (Shut-off power $\approx 30-40\%$ of rated) Optimal efficient power Reaches maximum and flattens (Non-overloading)
Efficiency ($\eta$) $0\%$ (No fluid discharged) Maximum ($\eta_{max} \approx 80-92\%$) Drops sharply due to friction and shock losses

Level 5: Aviation & ASO Practical Applications

  • Aircraft Fuel Cross-Feed Management: Multi-tank fuel feed systems balance pipeline resistance curves ($H_{sys}$) across wing and center tanks to guarantee identical delivery pressures to engine fuel control units (FCU).
  • Engine Variable-Speed Fuel Pumps: Engine-driven pumps use variable-frequency AC generator drives to track the fuel burn curve precisely, preventing hydraulic recirculation heating.
  • De-icing Fluid Spray Systems: Ground de-icing trucks utilize calibrated $H-Q$ positive-displacement/centrifugal hybrid pump curves to maintain stable spray droplet momentum across wide ambient temperatures.

Level 6: UPSC ASO Focus & Common Traps

  • Trap 1 (Zero Discharge Efficiency): At shut-off ($Q = 0$), pump head is at its maximum, but efficiency is strictly zero ($\eta = 0$) because useful hydraulic work done is zero ($P_{water} = \rho g Q H = 0$).
  • Trap 2 (System Curve Shift): Changing pump speed $N$ shifts the pump curve, NOT the system curve. Throttling a valve shifts the system curve, NOT the pump curve.
  • Trap 3 (Best Efficiency Point): Operating a pump significantly left or right of the BEP leads to internal flow recirculation, high vibration, bearing fatigue, and premature seal failure.

UPSC ASO Practice Questions (30 MCQs)

Q1. What is the Best Efficiency Point (BEP) of a centrifugal pump?
A) The point where pump discharge is maximum at zero head
B) The operating point on the $H-Q$ curve where overall efficiency ($\eta$) reaches its maximum value
C) The point where the delivery valve is completely shut
D) The minimum speed required to start the pump
BEP is the design flow condition where internal hydraulic shock losses and friction losses are at their minimum, maximizing overall efficiency $\eta_o$.
Q2. The system resistance curve for a pipeline is given by $H_{sys} = H_{stat} + K Q^2$. If the static lift $H_{stat} = 0$ (pure friction loop), the system curve:
A) Is a horizontal straight line
B) Is a parabola passing through the origin $(0,0)$
C) Is a vertical straight line
D) Drops with increasing flow rate
When $H_{stat} = 0$, $H_{sys} = K Q^2$, which represents a standard upward parabola originating at $Q = 0, H = 0$.
Q3. What happens to the operating point of a pumping system when the delivery discharge valve is partially closed (throttled)?
A) Discharge increases and head decreases
B) Discharge decreases and developed head increases
C) Both discharge and head remain identical
D) Pump speed automatically doubles
Throttling increases system resistance $K_{sys}$, rotating the system curve upward and shifting the intersection point to lower $Q$ and higher $H$ along the pump curve.
Q4. What is the value of overall efficiency ($\eta$) of a centrifugal pump operating at shut-off condition ($Q = 0$)?
A) $100\%$
B) $50\%$
C) $0\%$
D) Indeterminate
Useful water power is $P_w = \rho g Q H$. When $Q = 0$, $P_w = 0$, so efficiency $\eta = \frac{P_w}{P_{shaft}} = 0\%$.
Q5. In a centrifugal pump with backward-curved vanes ($\beta_2 < 90^\circ$), what is the nature of the power-discharge ($P-Q$) curve?
A) Continuously rising without bound
B) Non-overloading (reaches a peak and flattens)
C) Decreases linearly with $Q$
D) Strictly constant at all flow rates
Backward-curved vanes exhibit a non-overloading power characteristic, meaning the drive motor will not burn out even if the delivery pipe ruptures (zero head).
Q6. Fluid friction losses inside the impeller and casing channels vary with discharge ($Q$) as:
A) $h_f \propto Q$
B) $h_f \propto Q^2$
C) $h_f \propto \sqrt{Q}$
D) $h_f \propto \frac{1}{Q}$
Hydraulic friction head loss is governed by Darcy-Weisbach: $h_f = \frac{4 f L V^2}{2 g D} \propto V^2 \propto Q^2$.
Q7. Shock (incidence) losses at the inlet tips of the impeller blades are zero at:
A) Zero flow rate ($Q = 0$)
B) Rated design discharge ($Q = Q_{des}$)
C) Maximum possible discharge ($Q_{max}$)
D) Critical speed only
Shock loss varies as $h_{shock} = K_2(Q - Q_{des})^2$. At design flow ($Q = Q_{des}$), fluid enters tangent to the blade angle without incidence shock.
Q8. Muschel curves (constant efficiency curves) for a pump are plotted as:
A) Straight lines on a $P-N$ plot
B) Closed loops / contours of equal efficiency on $H-Q$ coordinates
C) Logarithmic decay curves
D) Inverted parabolas on $T-s$ chart
Muschel curves are iso-efficiency contour maps linking points of equal efficiency across varying speeds and discharges on the $H-Q$ plane.
Q9. If a pump operates at speed $N = 1000 \text{ rpm}$ delivering $Q = 20 \text{ L/s}$ against head $H = 15 \text{ m}$, what is its discharge at $1500 \text{ rpm}$?
A) $25 \text{ L/s}$
B) $30 \text{ L/s}$
C) $45 \text{ L/s}$
D) $60 \text{ L/s}$
From Affinity laws: $\frac{Q_2}{Q_1} = \frac{N_2}{N_1} \implies Q_2 = 20 \times \frac{1500}{1000} = 30 \text{ L/s}$.
Q10. For the pump in Q9, what is the head developed at $1500 \text{ rpm}$?
A) $22.5 \text{ m}$
B) $33.75 \text{ m}$
C) $45.0 \text{ m}$
D) $60.0 \text{ m}$
From Affinity laws: $\frac{H_2}{H_1} = \left(\frac{N_2}{N_1}\right)^2 \implies H_2 = 15 \times \left(\frac{1500}{1000}\right)^2 = 15 \times 2.25 = 33.75 \text{ m}$.
Q11. Why is speed control using Variable Frequency Drives (VFD) more energy-efficient than discharge valve throttling?
A) VFD eliminates motor friction completely
B) Power varies as the cube of speed ($P \propto N^3$), avoiding artificial valve head dissipation ($\Delta H_{valve}$)
C) VFD increases liquid density
D) Throttling reduces pump head too drastically
Throttling forces the pump to operate at high head while dissipating excess head across the valve. VFD lowers the entire pump curve to match system demand with $P \propto N^3$ power savings.
Q12. What is the shut-off head ($H_0$) developed by a centrifugal pump?
A) The head when discharge $Q = 0$ with the delivery valve closed
B) The head when the pump stalls at maximum flow
C) The atmospheric pressure head
D) The head at minimum speed
Shut-off head is the maximum pressure head generated by the rotating liquid vortex when discharge is zero ($Q = 0$).
Q13. In a flat system curve (dominated by static lift $H_{stat}$ with little pipe friction), reducing pump speed:
A) Causes rapid flow drop and risks zero delivery if $H_0 < H_{stat}$
B) Always increases flow rate
C) Maintains constant flow rate
D) Eliminates pump vibration
If static head $H_{stat}$ is large, reducing speed drops developed head ($H \propto N^2$). If $H_0$ falls below $H_{stat}$, flow ceases completely.
Q14. The intersection of the pump $H-Q$ curve with the $Q$-axis ($H = 0$) represents:
A) Shut-off condition
B) Maximum possible discharge ($Q_{max}$ / Free delivery)
C) BEP condition
D) Choking Mach number
At $H = 0$, the pump operates with zero external resistance, delivering its maximum theoretical flow (free delivery).
Q15. Operating a pump continuously at flow rates below $20\%$ of its BEP discharge results in:
A) Increased pump efficiency
B) Severe suction/discharge flow recirculation, pressure pulsation, and liquid overheating
C) Complete elimination of cavitation
D) Negative motor current
Low flow operation leads to internal eddy recirculation at impeller eye/discharge tips, causing intense vibration, mechanical seal damage, and fluid temperature rise.
Q16. If a pump impeller diameter is reduced by machining its outer diameter from $D_1$ to $D_2$ at constant speed $N$, the new head scales as:
A) $H_2 = H_1 \left(\frac{D_2}{D_1}\right)$
B) $H_2 = H_1 \left(\frac{D_2}{D_1}\right)^2$
C) $H_2 = H_1 \left(\frac{D_2}{D_1}\right)^3$
D) $H_2 = H_1 \left(\frac{D_2}{D_1}\right)^5$
For impeller diameter trimming at constant speed: $Q \propto D, H \propto D^2, P \propto D^3$.
Q17. In pump terminology, what is meant by a 'drooping' head-capacity curve?
A) A curve where developed head continuously decreases as flow rate increases
B) A curve where head rises with flow rate
C) A curve that oscillates periodically
D) A vertical line
A drooping (or falling) characteristic has maximum head at shut-off and decreases monotonically with discharge, ensuring stable operation without hunting.
Q18. What is an 'unstable' pump characteristic curve?
A) A curve where head first rises with discharge before falling, creating two possible flow rates at the same head
B) A curve where efficiency is 100%
C) A curve where speed is constant
D) A straight horizontal line
A rising-falling curve creates multiple operating points for a given system head, leading to flow hunting and pressure surging.
Q19. If two identical pumps with head curve $H = 40 - 1000 Q^2$ operate on a system with $H_{sys} = 10 + 2000 Q^2$, what is the single pump operating flow rate?
A) $0.05 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
B) $0.10 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
C) $0.15 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
D) $0.20 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
Equating $H_{pump} = H_{sys} \implies 40 - 1000 Q^2 = 10 + 2000 Q^2 \implies 30 = 3000 Q^2 \implies Q^2 = 0.01 \implies Q = 0.10 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$.
Q20. For the operating condition in Q19, what is the operating head ($H_{op}$)?
A) $20 \text{ m}$
B) $30 \text{ m}$
C) $40 \text{ m}$
D) $50 \text{ m}$
$H_{op} = 40 - 1000(0.1^2) = 40 - 10 = 30 \text{ m}$ (or $10 + 2000(0.01) = 30 \text{ m}$).
Q21. The term 'Minimum Continuous Stable Flow' (MCSF) of a pump refers to:
A) The lowest flow at which the pump can operate continuously without exceeding vibration and temperature limits
B) Zero flow rate
C) Flow rate at free discharge
D) Sonic choking velocity
MCSF is specified by manufacturers to protect pump bearings, mechanical seals, and hydraulic passages from recirculation-induced damage.
Q22. In aircraft auxiliary power units (APU) and fuel delivery systems, a bypass recirculation valve is fitted across the pump to:
A) Keep the pump operating above its MCSF during low-demand engine idle conditions
B) Increase fuel freezing point
C) Stop the impeller rotation
D) Increase aerodynamic drag
When engine fuel demand is minimal, the bypass line returns fuel to the tank, ensuring the pump stays above MCSF and avoiding fuel overheating.
Q23. If the pipeline friction factor increases due to pipe aging and scale deposit, the system curve:
A) Flattens toward the horizontal axis
B) Steistens upward, shifting the operating point to lower discharge
C) Remains unchanged
D) Shifts horizontally to the right
Higher friction increases $K_{sys}$, rotating the system resistance curve upward and reducing delivered discharge.
Q24. What is the relation between pump brake horsepower ($BHP$) and liquid specific gravity ($SG$)?
A) $BHP \propto SG$
B) $BHP \propto \frac{1}{SG}$
C) $BHP$ is independent of $SG$
D) $BHP \propto SG^2$
$P = \frac{\rho g Q H}{\eta_o} = \frac{(SG \times \rho_{water}) g Q H}{\eta_o}$. Pumping denser liquids directly increases the required brake horsepower.
Q25. In the $H-Q$ characteristic of a forward-curved impeller, why is operation generally unstable?
A) A decrease in system head causes a huge increase in flow and massive power surge (overloading)
B) Developed head drops to zero immediately
C) Efficiency is always zero
D) Fluid boils inside the volute
Rising head and power curves cause run-away overloading whenever system resistance drops, tripping circuit breakers or burning motors.
Q26. If the speed of a pump is reduced by $10\%$ ($N_2 = 0.9 N_1$), the power required decreases by approximately:
A) $10\%$
B) $19\%$
C) $27\%$
D) $35\%$
$P_2 = P_1 (0.9)^3 = 0.729 P_1$. The power drops by $1 - 0.729 = 0.271 = 27.1\%$.
Q27. When trimming an impeller diameter by $10\%$ ($D_2 = 0.9 D_1$), the head developed at the same speed drops to approximately:
A) $90\%$ of original
B) $81\%$ of original
C) $72.9\%$ of original
D) $50\%$ of original
For diameter trimming: $H \propto D^2 \implies H_2 = H_1 (0.9)^2 = 0.81 H_1 = 81\%$.
Q28. In an aircraft fuel system, if an air bubble enters the centrifugal fuel pump suction line, the immediate effect on the performance curve is:
A) Head and discharge drop drastically (vapor locking)
B) Head doubles instantly
C) Motor draws 10 times rated current
D) Efficiency rises to 99%
Vapor/air pockets in the impeller eye ruin the pressure gradient, collapsing the $H-Q$ curve and leading to vapor lock and engine fuel starvation.
Q29. The point of inflection on a centrifugal compressor or pump characteristic curve where flow separation and stall initiate is called:
A) Surge line / Surge limit
B) Choke line
C) Critical Mach number
D) Reynolds transition
The surge limit marks the boundary of unstable operation where low flow causes boundary layer separation and violent flow reversal.
Q30. What parameter remains invariant at homologous (dynamically similar) operating points on pump characteristic curves?
A) Head coefficient ($C_H = \frac{g H}{N^2 D^2}$) and Flow coefficient ($C_Q = \frac{Q}{N D^3}$)
B) Motor horsepower only
C) Liquid temperature
D) Casing wall thickness
Dimensionless head, flow, and power coefficients are constant across all geometrically similar pumps operating at corresponding speeds.

MT7. In variable speed pumping, what is the power saving ratio when reducing flow rate to $50\%$ using speed control compared to constant speed?
A) Power drops to $\approx 12.5\%$ of full power ($P \propto N^3$)
B) Power drops to $50\%$
C) Power drops to $75\%$
D) No power savings occur
When $Q \propto N$, halving flow requires halving speed ($N_2 = 0.5 N_1$). Power consumed is $P_2 = (0.5)^3 P_1 = 0.125 P_1$ (87.5% power savings).
MT8. What type of loss in a centrifugal pump is primarily responsible for the peak in the efficiency curve?
A) Trade-off between leakage/mechanical losses at low flow and friction/shock losses at high flow
B) Speed variation
C) Static lift changes
D) Casing corrosion
At low flow, mechanical friction and leakage dominate percentage losses; at high flow, $Q^2$ fluid friction dominates. The minimum total loss percentage defines the BEP peak.
MT9. The shut-off head of a centrifugal pump is approximately related to outer peripheral velocity $u_2$ as:
A) $H_0 \approx \frac{u_2^2}{2g} \text{ to } \frac{u_2^2}{g}$

D) $H_0 = \infty$
Under closed valve conditions, the forced vortex pressure head rise is $H_0 = \frac{u_2^2 - u_1^2}{2g} \approx \frac{u_2^2}{2g}$ (and up to $u_2^2/g$ with casing recovery).
MT10. What is the effect of pumping high-viscosity oil on the pump characteristic curves compared to water?
A) Head and efficiency decrease, while required brake horsepower increases
B) Head and efficiency increase, while power decreases
C) No change in any curve
D) Discharge increases to infinity
Viscous drag increases skin friction and disk friction drastically, reducing $H$ and $\eta$ and demanding substantially more motor power.

Top Candidate Error Traps

  1. Assuming Shut-Off Efficiency is Maximum: At shut-off ($Q=0$), head is maximum, but efficiency is zero because no liquid is delivered ($P_w = 0$).
  2. Confusing Pump Curve Shift with System Curve Shift: Changing pump speed moves the pump curve. Throttling or changing pipe diameter moves the system curve.
  3. Ignoring MCSF (Minimum Continuous Stable Flow): Running a pump below 20% BEP flow causes intense internal recirculation, cavitation-like pitting, and bearing failure.
  4. Trimming Impeller Laws: Remember for diameter trimming at constant speed: $Q \propto D, H \propto D^2, P \propto D^3$.
