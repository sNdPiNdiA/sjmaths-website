Cavitation, NPSH & Specific Speed

Day 11 of 129 Pump Dynamics & Suction Limits Phase 1: Foundations 80-95 min Master Module

Level 1: Beginner's Glossary & Cavitation Definitions Basic

Before deriving NPSH equations, here is the complete beginner's dictionary of all cavitation terminology, pressure thresholds, and dimensionless shape factors:

1. Cavitation & Saturated Vapor Pressure ($p_v$)

  • Saturated Vapor Pressure ($p_v$): The thermodynamic pressure at which a liquid boils at its current temperature (Water at $20^\circ\text{C}$ has $p_v = 2.34 \text{ kPa} = 0.24 \text{ m}$ head).
  • Cavitation: Physical phenomenon where local static pressure drops to $p \le p_v$, boiling the liquid into vapor bubbles which later implode violently upon reaching higher pressure zones, eroding metal blades.

2. Net Positive Suction Head ($NPSH$)

  • Definition: Total absolute suction energy head above the liquid's vapor pressure head, measured at the pump's suction flange.
  • Unit: Meters of liquid column ($\text{m}$).
  • Formula: $NPSH = \frac{p_s}{\rho g} + \frac{V_s^2}{2g} - \frac{p_v}{\rho g}$.

3. $NPSH_A$ (Available) vs $NPSH_R$ (Required)

  • $NPSH_A$ (Available): Net head supplied by the suction piping system installation: $NPSH_A = \frac{p_{atm}}{\rho g} \pm z_s - h_{fs} - \frac{p_v}{\rho g}$.
  • $NPSH_R$ (Required): Minimum net head specified by the manufacturer to prevent bubble inception inside the impeller eye.
  • Golden Safety Rule: For safe, cavitation-free operation: $NPSH_A \ge NPSH_R + \text{Margin } (0.5 - 1.0 \text{ m})$.

4. Pump Specific Speed ($N_s$)

  • Definition: A dimensionless shape index representing the speed of a geometrically similar pump delivering $1 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ at $1 \text{ m}$ head: $$N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{Q}}{H^{3/4}}$$
  • Classification:
    • Radial Flow: $N_s = 10 - 30$ (High head, low flow)
    • Mixed Flow: $N_s = 30 - 80$ (Medium head & flow)
    • Axial Flow: $N_s = 80 - 200$ (Low head, high flow)

5. Thoma's Cavitation Factor ($\sigma$)

  • Definition: Ratio of net positive suction head to total pump head: $\sigma = \frac{NPSH}{H}$.
  • Critical Factor ($\sigma_c$): Threshold below which cavitation begins: $\sigma_c \approx 1.032 \times 10^{-3} N_s^{4/3}$.

Level 2: Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH) Formulations Intermediate

1. Net Positive Suction Head Available ($NPSH_A$)

Total absolute suction head available at the pump suction nozzle above the liquid vapor pressure head:

$$ NPSH_A = \frac{p_{atm}}{\rho g} - h_s - h_{fs} - \frac{p_v}{\rho g} = \frac{p_s}{\rho g} + \frac{V_s^2}{2g} - \frac{p_v}{\rho g} $$
  • $p_{atm}$: Atmospheric pressure on suction sump surface.
  • $h_s$: Static suction lift (height of pump center above sump surface; positive if pump is above sump, negative if flooded suction).
  • $h_{fs}$: Total frictional head loss in suction piping, foot valve, and strainer ($h_{fs} \propto Q^2$).
  • $p_v$: Vapor pressure of the pumped liquid at operating temperature.

2. Net Positive Suction Head Required ($NPSH_R$) & Cavitation Criterion

  • $NPSH_R$ is the minimum suction head required by the pump manufacturer to limit cavitation (empirically determined at $3\%$ head drop, $NPSH_{3\%}$).
  • Safe Cavitation-Free Condition:
$$ NPSH_A > NPSH_R \quad (\text{Recommended Margin: } NPSH_A \ge NPSH_R + 0.5 \text{ to } 1.0 \text{ m}) $$

3. Thoma's Cavitation Factor ($\sigma$) & Max Permissible Suction Lift ($h_{s,max}$)

$$ \sigma = \frac{NPSH}{H} = \frac{(p_{atm}/\rho g) - h_s - h_{fs} - (p_v/\rho g)}{H} $$ $$ h_{s,max} = \frac{p_{atm} - p_v}{\rho g} - h_{fs} - \sigma_c H $$

Cavitation strictly occurs whenever the operating cavitation factor is less than the critical threshold ($\sigma < \sigma_c$).

Level 3: Specific Speed ($N_s$) & Suction Specific Speed ($S$) Advanced

1. Pump Specific Speed ($N_s$)

The speed of a geometrically similar model pump which produces unit discharge ($1 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$) under unit head ($1 \text{ m}$):

$$ N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{Q}}{H^{3/4}} \quad (\text{Metric SI: } N \text{ in rpm, } Q \text{ in m}^3/\text{s}, H \text{ in m}) $$ $$ \text{Dimensionless Specific Speed (Shape Number): } \quad \Omega_s = \frac{\omega \sqrt{Q}}{(g H)^{3/4}} $$

2. Classification of Pumps by Specific Speed ($N_s$)

Pump Type Metric $N_s$ Range Flow / Head Regime Impeller Geometry
Radial Flow (Pure Centrifugal) $10 - 30$ High Head, Low Discharge Large outer diameter ($D_2 \gg D_1$), narrow blade width ($b_2$).
Mixed Flow (Francis type) $30 - 80$ Medium Head, Medium Discharge Medium diameter, inclined flow discharge.
Axial Flow (Propeller pump) $80 - 220+$ Low Head, Very High Discharge Propeller blades with open hub ($D_2 \approx D_1$), axial flow.

3. Suction Specific Speed ($S$ or $N_{ss}$)

$$ S = N_{ss} = \frac{N \sqrt{Q}}{(NPSH_R)^{3/4}} $$

Characterizes the anti-cavitation capability of an impeller design. Standard commercial pumps have $N_{ss} \approx 7,000 - 9,000$ (US units). Rocket turbopumps with specialized helical inducers achieve $N_{ss} > 25,000 - 40,000$.

Level 4: Remedial Actions & Formula Summary

Method to Prevent Cavitation Effect on $NPSH_A$ / $NPSH_R$ Practical Engineering Implementation
Lower the Pump Installation Level Increases $NPSH_A$ (decreases suction lift $h_s$) Place pump below sump level (Flooded suction).
Increase Suction Pipe Diameter Increases $NPSH_A$ (reduces friction $h_{fs} \propto 1/D^5$) Use one pipe size larger on suction than delivery.
Reduce Liquid Temperature Increases $NPSH_A$ (lowers vapor pressure $p_v$) Subcooling cryogenic fluids or avoiding hot sumps.
Use Double-Suction Impeller Decreases $NPSH_R$ by factor $(1/2)^{1/2} \approx 0.707$ Splits flow $Q/2$ into two eyes, lowering eye velocity.
Install an Axial Inducer Greatly decreases $NPSH_R$ Helical screw ahead of main centrifugal impeller.

Level 5: Aviation & ASO Practical Applications

  • High-Altitude Fuel Boiling & Vapor Lock: At $35,000 - 45,000 \text{ ft}$, ambient pressure drops to $\approx 3.5 \text{ psi}$. Aviation Jet A-1 or Avgas can boil in unpressurized fuel lines. Tank boost pumps maintain high suction pressure to prevent engine flameout.
  • Cryogenic Rocket Turbopumps (e.g., ISRO Vikas, CE-20, NASA SSME): Liquid Hydrogen ($LH_2$) and Liquid Oxygen ($LOX$) operate very close to their boiling points. High-solidity helical inducers are mandatory ahead of the centrifugal impeller to prevent catastrophic cavitation burn-through.
  • Helicopter Fuel Boost Feed: Negative-g flight maneuvers can cause fuel sump uncovering and air ingestion, which triggers instant cavitation unless accumulator surge pots are incorporated.

Level 6: UPSC ASO Focus & Common Traps

  • Trap 1 (Pump vs Turbine Specific Speed): For pumps, $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{Q}}{H^{3/4}}$ (uses $Q$). For hydraulic turbines, $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$ (uses Power $P$ and $H^{5/4}$). Never confuse the exponents!
  • Trap 2 (Atmospheric Pressure with Altitude): As altitude increases, $p_{atm}$ decreases, which severely lowers $NPSH_A$ and reduces the maximum permissible suction lift $h_{s,max}$.
  • Trap 3 (Flooded Suction Sign): When the pump is located below the sump liquid level (flooded suction), $h_s$ is negative in the formula $\frac{p_{atm}}{\rho g} - h_s$, which effectively adds static head, increasing $NPSH_A$.

UPSC ASO Practice Questions (30 MCQs)

Q1. Cavitation in a centrifugal pump is initiated when the local static pressure drops below:
A) Atmospheric pressure ($p_{atm}$)
B) Saturated vapor pressure of the liquid ($p_v$) at the operating temperature
C) Zero absolute pressure
D) Critical shock pressure
Cavitation begins when local pressure drops to the saturated vapor pressure ($p \le p_v$), causing localized boiling and vapor bubble formation.
Q2. Where is cavitation most likely to occur in a centrifugal pump?
A) Delivery pipe exit
B) Volute casing tongue
C) Suction eye and inlet tips of the impeller blades
D) Motor coupling shaft
The lowest static pressure in a centrifugal pump occurs at the suction eye of the impeller where fluid accelerates into the vane leading edges.
Q3. What is the formula for the specific speed ($N_s$) of a centrifugal pump?
A) $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$
B) $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{Q}}{H^{3/4}}$
C) $N_s = \frac{N Q^2}{H}$
D) $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{H}}{Q^{3/4}}$
For pumps, specific speed is $N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{Q}}{H^{3/4}}$ (where $Q$ is discharge in $\text{m}^3/\text{s}$ and $H$ is head in $\text{m}$).
Q4. To prevent cavitation during pump operation, which condition must strictly be satisfied?
A) $NPSH_A < NPSH_R$
B) $NPSH_A > NPSH_R$
C) $NPSH_A = 0$
D) $p_{atm} = p_v$
Available Net Positive Suction Head ($NPSH_A$) must exceed the manufacturer required suction head ($NPSH_R$) by a safe margin (typically $\ge 0.5 \text{ m}$).
Q5. A pump delivers $Q = 0.04 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ against head $H = 25 \text{ m}$ at speed $N = 1450 \text{ rpm}$. What is its specific speed ($N_s$)?
A) $12.5$
B) $25.9$
C) $52.3$
D) $116.0$
$N_s = \frac{1450 \times \sqrt{0.04}}{25^{3/4}} = \frac{1450 \times 0.2}{(5^2)^{3/4}} = \frac{290}{5^{1.5}} = \frac{290}{11.1803} \approx 25.938$. (Classifies as a radial-flow centrifugal pump).
Q6. Which category of rotodynamic pump has the highest specific speed ($N_s > 80$)?
A) Pure radial-flow centrifugal pump
B) Axial-flow (propeller) pump
C) Mixed-flow Francis pump
D) Multi-stage boiler feed pump
Axial-flow propeller pumps handle massive volumetric discharges at very low heads, giving the highest specific speeds ($N_s = 80 - 220+$).
Q7. What is Thoma's cavitation factor ($\sigma$) defined as?
A) $\sigma = \frac{NPSH}{H}$
B) $\sigma = \frac{H}{NPSH}$
C) $\sigma = \frac{NPSH_A}{NPSH_R}$
D) $\sigma = \frac{p_v}{p_{atm}}$
Thoma's cavitation factor is the dimensionless ratio of Net Positive Suction Head to total manometric pump head: $\sigma = \frac{NPSH}{H}$.
Q8. If atmospheric pressure is $10.3 \text{ m}$ of water, vapor pressure is $0.3 \text{ m}$, suction lift $h_s = 3.0 \text{ m}$, and suction pipe friction loss $h_{fs} = 1.0 \text{ m}$, what is $NPSH_A$?
A) $4.0 \text{ m}$
B) $6.0 \text{ m}$
C) $8.0 \text{ m}$
D) $10.0 \text{ m}$
$NPSH_A = \frac{p_{atm}}{\rho g} - h_s - h_{fs} - \frac{p_v}{\rho g} = 10.3 - 3.0 - 1.0 - 0.3 = 6.0 \text{ m}$.
Q9. What happens to $NPSH_A$ if the temperature of the pumped liquid increases?
A) $NPSH_A$ increases
B) $NPSH_A$ decreases because vapor pressure ($p_v$) rises exponentially with temperature
C) $NPSH_A$ remains unchanged
D) Head doubles
Higher temperature increases vapor pressure $p_v$, reducing the difference $\frac{p_{atm} - p_v}{\rho g}$ and making the pump much more susceptible to cavitation.
Q10. In liquid rocket engine turbopumps, an axial-flow helical inducer is added ahead of the centrifugal impeller in order to:
A) Greatly increase suction specific speed ($N_{ss}$) and allow operation at very low $NPSH_A$
B) Eliminate the need for rocket propellant tanks
C) Stop the turbine rotation

Helical inducers provide a slight pressure rise at extremely low inlet NPSH without stalling, feeding the main centrifugal impeller with cavitation-free fluid.
Q11. The characteristic high-frequency acoustic noise produced by severe cavitation inside a pump sounds like:
A) Whistling supersonic jet
B) Pumping gravel, rocks, or marbles through the casing
C) Low-frequency 50 Hz hum

Violent micro-jet implosions against metal walls produce high-frequency rattling/crackling noises similar to gravel passing through the pipe.
Q12. What is the standard industry criterion used to define $NPSH_R$ during manufacturer test bed trials?

B) $3\%$ drop in total developed head ($NPSH_{3\%}$)

According to ANSI/HI and ISO standards, $NPSH_R$ is formally defined at the suction pressure where the first-stage head drops by 3% ($NPSH_{3\%}$).
Q13. How does increasing the suction pipe diameter affect $NPSH_A$?

B) Increases $NPSH_A$ by reducing suction pipe friction head loss ($h_{fs}$)

Friction loss $h_{fs} \propto \frac{V^2}{D} \propto \frac{1}{D^5}$. A larger suction pipe reduces $h_{fs}$, directly increasing $NPSH_A$.
Q14. In aircraft fuel systems, Avgas (aviation gasoline) is more susceptible to vapor lock than Jet A-1 kerosene because:

D) Jet A-1 has zero vapor pressure
Avgas has volatile lighter hydrocarbon fractions ($RVP \approx 38 - 48 \text{ kPa}$), meaning it vaporizes readily at high altitudes under low ambient pressures.
Q15. If a pump operates with flooded suction where the sump liquid level is $2.0 \text{ m}$ ABOVE the pump centerline ($h_s = -2.0 \text{ m}$), how is $NPSH_A$ affected?
A) $NPSH_A$ increases by $2.0 \text{ m}$ (minus small pipe friction)
B) $NPSH_A$ decreases by $2.0 \text{ m}$

Static head of liquid above the pump acts as positive pressure: $NPSH_A = \frac{p_{atm}}{\rho g} + h_{static} - h_{fs} - \frac{p_v}{\rho g}$. Flooded suction is the most effective anti-cavitation installation.
Q16. What is the dimensionless specific speed (Shape Number $\Omega_s$) in terms of angular velocity $\omega$ (rad/s)?
A) $\Omega_s = \frac{\omega \sqrt{Q}}{(g H)^{3/4}}$

Q17. In a double-suction impeller, the flow rate entering each individual eye is $Q/2$. How does its required $NPSH_R$ compare to a single-suction impeller of the same overall capacity?
A) $NPSH_R$ is reduced by approximately $30-40\%$

C) $NPSH_R$ remains identical
D) $NPSH_R$ becomes zero
Since each eye receives only $Q/2$, eye velocity is reduced by half ($V_{eye} \propto Q$), dropping the eye dynamic depression ($V_{eye}^2/2g$) and reducing $NPSH_R$ significantly.
Q18. What is the maximum theoretical suction lift of cold water at sea level under ideal zero-friction conditions? ($p_{atm} = 101.3 \text{ kPa}, \rho = 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3, g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2, p_v \approx 2.3 \text{ kPa}$)
A) $10.09 \text{ m}$
B) $15.50 \text{ m}$
C) $7.50 \text{ m}$
D) $32.2 \text{ m}$
$h_{s,max} = \frac{p_{atm} - p_v}{\rho g} = \frac{101300 - 2300}{1000 \times 9.81} = \frac{99000}{9810} \approx 10.09 \text{ m}$. In practice, due to friction and $NPSH_R$, actual suction lift is limited to $6 - 7.5 \text{ m}$.
Q19. Pumping boiling water at $100^\circ\text{C}$ from an open hotwell tank requires:
A) A positive flooded suction head because $p_v = p_{atm}$

At $100^\circ\text{C}$, $p_v = p_{atm}$, so $\frac{p_{atm} - p_v}{\rho g} = 0$. Therefore $NPSH_A = h_{static} - h_{fs}$. The pump must be installed well below the tank level ($h_{static} > NPSH_R + h_{fs}$).
Q20. What is Suction Specific Speed ($S$ or $N_{ss}$)?

Suction specific speed characterizes the suction geometry. Higher $S$ values indicate superior suction capability and resistance to cavitation at lower available heads.

Top Candidate Error Traps

  1. Pump vs Turbine Specific Speed Exponent: Pump $N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{Q}}{H^{3/4}}$. Turbine $N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$. Do not mix up the power and head exponents!
  2. Sign of Suction Lift in $NPSH_A$: In $NPSH_A = \frac{p_{atm} - p_v}{\rho g} - h_s - h_{fs}$, suction lift $h_s$ (pump above sump) is subtracted. For flooded suction (pump below sump), it is added.
  3. Speed Scaling of $NPSH_R$: $NPSH_R$ scales quadratically with speed ($NPSH_R \propto N^2$), meaning high-speed pumps require significantly higher suction heads.
  4. Temperature Effect on Vapor Pressure: Boiling or hot liquids have high $p_v$, destroying available NPSH and mandating flooded suction installations.
