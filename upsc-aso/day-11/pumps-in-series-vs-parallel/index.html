Pumps in Series vs Parallel Combinations

Day 11 of 129 Fluid Machinery & Piping Systems Phase 1: Foundations 65-80 min Master Module

Level 1: Beginner's Glossary & Combination Rules Basic

Before constructing combined $H-Q$ curves, here is the complete beginner's dictionary of all pump staging terms, electrical circuit analogies, and hydraulic rules:

1. Pumps in Series (Head Boosting)

  • Definition: Configuration where the delivery outlet of Pump 1 connects directly into the suction inlet of Pump 2.
  • Circuit Analogy: Like batteries in series adding voltages ($V_{total} = V_1 + V_2$).
  • Fundamental Rules:
    • Flow Rate is constant: $Q_{total} = Q_1 = Q_2$
    • Heads add vertically: $H_{total}(Q) = H_1(Q) + H_2(Q)$
  • Primary Purpose: Pumping against extreme heads or long vertical elevations (Boiler feed pumps, multi-stage borehole pumps).

2. Pumps in Parallel (Discharge Boosting)

  • Definition: Configuration where two or more pumps draw liquid from a common intake reservoir and discharge into a shared delivery header.
  • Circuit Analogy: Like electrical branches in parallel adding currents ($I_{total} = I_1 + I_2$).
  • Fundamental Rules:
    • Head across branches is identical: $H_{total} = H_1 = H_2$
    • Discharges add horizontally: $Q_{total}(H) = Q_1(H) + Q_2(H)$
  • Primary Purpose: Moving large volumetric flow rates or providing operational redundancy (Aircraft fuel cross-feed, municipal water supply).

3. Multi-Stage Centrifugal Pump

  • Definition: A single casing containing $n$ identical impellers mounted in series on a single common drive shaft.
  • Formula: Total head produced is $H_{total} = n \cdot H_{single}$.
  • Efficiency: Provides high delivery pressure without requiring impellers of enormous diameters.

4. Dissimilar Pumps in Parallel (Check-Valve Trap)

  • The Hazard: If Pump A has a higher shut-off head than Pump B ($H_{0A} > H_{0B}$), operating at high system head will force Pump B into dead-head shutoff or backward flow!
  • Protection: Non-return check valves must always be installed on the discharge branch of every parallel pump.

Level 2: Graphical Construction & Mathematical Addition Intermediate

1. Series Combination Mathematics

For two pumps with individual head curves $H_1(Q)$ and $H_2(Q)$:

$$ H_{series}(Q) = H_1(Q) + H_2(Q) \quad \text{at constant flow } Q $$ $$ \text{For } n \text{ identical pumps: } \quad H_{series}(Q) = n \times H_{single}(Q) $$

Graphical Construction: Draw individual curves on $H-Q$ axes. At any selected flow $Q$, sum the vertical ordinates ($H_1 + H_2$) to plot the combined series curve.

2. Parallel Combination Mathematics

For two pumps with individual flow characteristics $Q_1(H)$ and $Q_2(H)$:

$$ Q_{parallel}(H) = Q_1(H) + Q_2(H) \quad \text{at constant head } H $$ $$ \text{For } n \text{ identical pumps: } \quad Q_{parallel}(H) = n \times Q_{single}(H) $$

Graphical Construction: At any selected head $H$, sum the horizontal abscissas ($Q_1 + Q_2$) to plot the combined parallel curve.

Level 3: System Pipeline Interaction & Operational Traps Advanced

1. Influence of Pipeline Resistance Curve Shape

  • Steep System Curve (Friction-Dominated, high $K Q^2$): Series connection yields a major increase in discharge. Parallel connection yields very little flow increase because the high friction destroys additional head.
  • Flat System Curve (Static-Lift-Dominated, high $H_{stat}$, low $K Q^2$): Parallel connection yields a major increase in discharge (close to $2\times$). Series connection delivers excess unused head.

2. Dissimilar Pumps in Parallel (Deadheading Risk)

  • If Pump A has a shut-off head $H_{0A} = 60 \text{ m}$ and Pump B has $H_{0B} = 40 \text{ m}$:
  • When the system operating head exceeds $40 \text{ m}$, Pump B delivers ZERO flow and is deadheaded.
  • Water inside Pump B overheats within minutes. Non-return check valves are mandatory to prevent reverse flow through Pump B.

3. Dissimilar Pumps in Series (Casing Over-pressurization)

  • The pump with the higher pressure rating must always be placed downstream (second stage) because its suction pressure will equal the discharge pressure of the first pump.

Level 4: Series vs Parallel Comparison Summary

Operational Feature Pumps in Series Pumps in Parallel
Primary Objective High Head / High Pressure High Discharge / Flow Rate
Discharge Relation $Q_{total} = Q_1 = Q_2$ $Q_{total} = Q_1 + Q_2$
Head Relation $H_{total} = H_1 + H_2$ $H_{total} = H_1 = H_2$
Best Suited System Steep / Long frictional pipelines Flat / Short low-friction pipelines
Multi-Stage Machine Equivalence Multi-stage centrifugal pump (shaft series) Double-suction centrifugal impeller
Critical Safety Risk Second stage casing burst if over-pressurized Deadheading / Backflow through lower-head pump

Level 5: Aviation & ASO Practical Applications

  • Aircraft Redundant Fuel Boost Pumps: Commercial jet aircraft (e.g., Boeing 737/777, Airbus A320/A350) install dual electric fuel boost pumps in parallel inside each fuel tank with one-way flapper check valves, ensuring uninterrupted engine fuel feed if one pump fails.
  • Engine High-Pressure Fuel System Staging: An engine uses a low-pressure boost pump in series with an engine-driven high-pressure main pump to provide sufficient NPSH and raise pressure to $1200 \text{ psi}$ for atomizers.
  • Flight Control Hydraulic Power: Triplex independent parallel hydraulic systems (Green, Blue, Yellow in Airbus) provide hydraulic flow to fly-by-wire flight control servos.

Level 6: UPSC ASO Focus & Common Traps

  • Trap 1 (Flow Doubling Myth): Operating two identical pumps in parallel does NOT double the actual delivered system flow ($Q_{sys} < 2 Q_1$) because pipeline friction increases as $Q^2$, raising the system head.
  • Trap 2 (Series Operating Flow): Two identical pumps in series will deliver higher flow than a single pump in a frictional system because the doubled head overcomes the $K Q^2$ pipeline resistance at a higher flow rate.
  • Trap 3 (Deadhead Operating Point): In parallel operation of dissimilar pumps, if the system head exceeds the shut-off head of the smaller pump, the combined curve is identical to the single large pump curve alone!

UPSC ASO Practice Questions (30 MCQs)

Q1. When two identical centrifugal pumps are operated in series, the combined arrangement delivers:
A) Double the flow rate at the same head
B) Double the head at any given discharge rate
C) Half the power requirement
D) Zero manometric efficiency
In series, fluid passes sequentially through each impeller, so heads are added: $H_{comb}(Q) = H_1(Q) + H_2(Q) = 2 H(Q)$ at the same flow $Q$.
Q2. When two identical centrifugal pumps are connected in parallel, the combined performance curve is obtained by:
A) Adding the heads at constant flow rate
B) Adding the flow rates at constant head
C) Multiplying the individual pump heads
D) Subtracting the pipe friction head
In parallel, both pumps operate against the same manifold head, so discharges add: $Q_{comb}(H) = Q_1(H) + Q_2(H) = 2 Q(H)$.
Q3. In a pipeline system with very steep frictional resistance ($H_{sys} = K Q^2$ with large $K$), which pump combination provides the most effective increase in discharge?
A) Pumps in parallel
B) Pumps in series
C) Both provide identical flow increase
D) Neither can increase flow
In steep friction-dominated systems, additional head (series) is required to overcome the steep $K Q^2$ resistance, resulting in a substantial flow increase. Parallel pumps increase head very little.
Q4. Why does running two identical pumps in parallel in a real piping system NOT double the delivered flow rate?
A) Motor speeds decrease by half
B) Pipeline frictional head loss increases quadratically with flow ($h_f \propto Q^2$), requiring higher head and reducing per-pump discharge
C) One pump always runs backward
D) Liquid boils in the manifold
Higher combined flow increases system head $H_{sys} = H_{stat} + K Q^2$. This higher operating head forces each individual pump to operate at a lower flow rate along its drooping curve.
Q5. Two pumps with characteristics $H_1 = 30 - 100 Q_1^2$ and $H_2 = 30 - 100 Q_2^2$ operate in parallel. What is the equation of the combined curve?
A) $H = 60 - 200 Q^2$
B) $H = 30 - 25 Q^2$
C) $H = 30 - 50 Q^2$
D) $H = 60 - 100 Q^2$
In parallel, $Q = Q_1 + Q_2 = 2 Q_1 \implies Q_1 = Q/2$. Substitute into $H = 30 - 100(Q/2)^2 = 30 - 100(Q^2/4) = 30 - 25 Q^2$.
Q6. For the pumps in Q5, what is the combined characteristic equation if they are connected in series?
A) $H = 60 - 200 Q^2$
B) $H = 60 - 100 Q^2$
C) $H = 30 - 200 Q^2$
D) $H = 60 - 50 Q^2$
In series, $H = H_1 + H_2 = (30 - 100 Q^2) + (30 - 100 Q^2) = 60 - 200 Q^2$.
Q7. What is the major operational risk of running two dissimilar pumps in parallel if Pump A has shut-off head $H_{0A} = 50 \text{ m}$ and Pump B has $H_{0B} = 35 \text{ m}$?
A) If system head exceeds $35 \text{ m}$, Pump B delivers no flow (deadheaded) and may suffer reverse flow/overheating
B) Pump A will automatically stop
C) Flow rate drops to zero immediately
D) Both motors consume zero power
When operating head $H > 35 \text{ m}$, Pump B cannot overcome manifold pressure. Check valves must prevent backflow, but churning in Pump B causes rapid overheating.
Q8. What mechanical design achieves the hydraulic equivalent of two identical pumps in parallel within a single casing?
A) Multi-stage pump with series diffusers
B) Double-suction impeller (back-to-back suction eyes)
C) Forward-curved blade impeller
D) Open swirl inducer
A double-suction impeller splits flow equally across two symmetrical eyes in parallel, doubling flow capacity and balancing axial hydraulic thrust.
Q9. A multi-stage centrifugal pump has 4 identical stages. If each stage develops $25 \text{ m}$ head at $1500 \text{ rpm}$, the total pump head is:
A) $25 \text{ m}$
B) $50 \text{ m}$
C) $100 \text{ m}$
D) $400 \text{ m}$
In a multi-stage pump, impellers are in series: $H_{total} = n \times H_{stage} = 4 \times 25 = 100 \text{ m}$.
Q10. In an aircraft wing tank, two electric fuel boost pumps are arranged in parallel. What essential valve is installed at the discharge of each pump?
A) Gate valve
B) Non-return (check) valve
C) Pressure relief valve set to zero
D) Globe valve
Check valves prevent fuel pumped by the active pump from recirculating backward through the idle/failed standby pump back into the tank.
Q11. In a flat pipeline system dominated by static elevation ($H_{sys} = 40 \text{ m}$ with negligible friction), which pump arrangement is most effective?
A) Parallel connection (if single pump head exceeds $40 \text{ m}$)
B) Series connection only
C) Gravity feed without pump
D) Reciprocating vacuum pump
With flat system curves, parallel pumps provide near double flow rate because the head required remains almost constant as flow increases.
Q12. Two identical pumps in series operate on a system $H_{sys} = 10 + 500 Q^2$. If single pump curve is $H = 25 - 200 Q^2$, what is the combined operating discharge?
A) $0.150 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
B) $0.211 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
C) $0.300 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
D) $0.500 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
Combined series curve: $H = 2(25 - 200 Q^2) = 50 - 400 Q^2$. Equating to system head: $50 - 400 Q^2 = 10 + 500 Q^2 \implies 40 = 900 Q^2 \implies Q^2 = \frac{40}{900} \implies Q = \frac{\sqrt{40}}{30} \approx 0.2108 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$.
Q13. For the single pump operating alone on the same system in Q12, the single pump discharge is:
A) $0.146 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
B) $0.211 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
C) $0.292 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
D) $0.050 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
$25 - 200 Q^2 = 10 + 500 Q^2 \implies 15 = 700 Q^2 \implies Q^2 = \frac{15}{700} \approx 0.02143 \implies Q \approx 0.1464 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$. (Notice series increased flow from $0.146$ to $0.211 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$).
Q14. What happens if the two pumps in Q12 are operated in parallel on the same system ($H_{sys} = 10 + 500 Q^2$)?
A) $Q \approx 0.165 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
B) $Q \approx 0.292 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
C) $Q \approx 0.450 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
D) $Q = 0$
Parallel curve: $H = 25 - 200(Q/2)^2 = 25 - 50 Q^2$. Equating to system: $25 - 50 Q^2 = 10 + 500 Q^2 \implies 15 = 550 Q^2 \implies Q = \sqrt{15/550} \approx 0.165 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$. (Parallel gave only $0.165$ vs Series $0.211 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ due to steep friction).
Q15. In a parallel pump configuration, what is the combined electrical power consumed by $n$ identical pumps compared to a single pump operating at its individual duty point?
A) Exactly $n$ times the individual power at that operating point
B) Power is independent of the number of pumps
C) Zero power
D) $n^3$ times
Each pump operates at $Q_i = Q_{total}/n$ against head $H$, drawing power $P_i$. Total power is the direct sum $P_{total} = \sum P_i = n P_i$.
Q16. When two pumps are connected in series, the suction pressure of the second pump is equal to:
A) Sump atmospheric pressure
B) Discharge pressure of the first pump minus inter-stage pipe losses
C) Zero absolute
D) Twice the final delivery pressure
The second pump receives the pressurized liquid leaving the first pump stage, boosting it further.
Q17. Why is cavitation rarely an issue in the second stage of a series pump combination?
A) The second stage runs at lower RPM
B) Suction pressure entering the second stage is already very high (high $NPSH_A$)
C) Second stage has no impeller
D) Fluid temperature drops to $0 \text{ K}$
The first pump stage pressurizes the fluid well above its vapor pressure, providing enormous available NPSH for the second stage.
Q18. If a pump station has 3 identical parallel pumps, operating 1 pump delivers $100 \text{ m}^3/\text{hr}$. Operating 2 pumps delivers $160 \text{ m}^3/\text{hr}$. Operating all 3 will likely deliver:
A) $300 \text{ m}^3/\text{hr}$
B) $200 \text{ m}^3/\text{hr}$
C) $100 \text{ m}^3/\text{hr}$
D) $50 \text{ m}^3/\text{hr}$
Due to diminishing returns from increasing pipe friction ($h_f \propto Q^2$), each added parallel pump contributes progressively less additional flow ($\approx 200 \text{ m}^3/\text{hr}$).
Q19. In parallel pumping, 'hunting' or load-swinging between two pumps occurs when:
A) The pumps have rising (unstable) head-capacity curves in the operating region
B) The pumps have steep drooping curves
C) Both pumps run at exactly the same speed
D) Piping diameter is extremely large
If $H-Q$ curves have a hump (rising branch), a single head corresponds to multiple flow rates, causing cyclic load oscillations and vibration between parallel pumps.
Q20. In an aircraft hydraulic system, what is the primary reason for utilizing three independent parallel hydraulic circuits?
A) Triple triple flight control redundancy (fail-operational capability)
B) To triple the aircraft flight speed
C) To reduce fuel consumption to zero
D) To warm up the passenger cabin
Aviation airworthiness regulations (FAR/CS 25) require fail-safe multi-channel redundancy so that if one or two hydraulic systems fail, primary flight control surfaces remain operable.
Q21. How does the specific speed ($N_s$) of each individual impeller stage compare to the specific speed of a multi-stage pump as a whole ($N_{s,total}$)?
A) $N_{s,stage} = N_{s,total} \times n^{3/4}$
B) $N_{s,stage} = N_{s,total} / n$
C) $N_{s,stage} = N_{s,total}$
D) $N_{s,stage} = N_{s,total} \times n^2$
$N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{Q}}{H^{3/4}}$. For each stage, head is $H/n$, so $N_{s,stage} = \frac{N\sqrt{Q}}{(H/n)^{3/4}} = N_{s,total} \times n^{3/4}$.
Q22. In a series pumping installation, if the first pump trips/fails while the second continues running, the second pump:
A) Experiences a severe suction pressure drop and is at immediate risk of violent cavitation
B) Doubles its output head
C) Runs completely unharmed
D) Discharges into the atmosphere
The stationary first pump becomes a major flow restriction, dropping inlet suction pressure to the second pump below vapor pressure ($NPSH_A < NPSH_R$).
Q23. When operating two identical pumps in parallel, the combined Best Efficiency Point (BEP) discharge occurs at:
A) Twice the single-pump BEP discharge ($2 Q_{BEP}$) at the single-pump BEP head ($H_{BEP}$)
B) Four times the single-pump BEP discharge
C) Half the single-pump BEP discharge
D) Zero head
Since both pumps operate at identical efficiency points, maximum efficiency of the parallel pair occurs at $Q = 2 Q_{BEP}$ and $H = H_{BEP}$.
Q24. When operating two identical pumps in series, the combined Best Efficiency Point (BEP) occurs at:
A) The single-pump BEP discharge ($Q_{BEP}$) at twice the single-pump BEP head ($2 H_{BEP}$)
B) Twice the discharge and twice the head
C) Half the head
D) Zero discharge
In series, each pump passes the same flow $Q_{BEP}$ while each contributes $H_{BEP}$, giving combined BEP at $(Q_{BEP}, 2H_{BEP})$.
Q25. What is the effect on total flow rate if one of two parallel operating pumps is shut down in a friction-dominated pipeline?
A) Flow drops by significantly less than $50\%$ (typically only $20-30\%$ reduction)
B) Flow drops to exactly zero
C) Flow drops by exactly $50\%$
D) Flow increases
Because system head drops when total flow reduces ($H_{sys} \propto Q^2$), the remaining single pump operates at a lower head and delivers higher individual discharge.
Q26. Why are deep-well submersible borehole pumps designed as multi-stage series units rather than a single large-diameter impeller?
A) Borehole casing diameter strictly restricts maximum impeller diameter ($D_2$), requiring staging to achieve high lift head
B) Multi-stage pumps run on DC batteries only
C) Single impellers cannot rotate underwater
D) To eliminate electrical wiring
Boreholes are narrow (4 to 8 inches). A single small-diameter impeller cannot produce hundreds of meters of head, so 10 to 30 small impellers are stacked in series.
Q27. In a rocket engine with staged combustion (e.g. Space Shuttle Main Engine SSME), low-pressure boost turbopumps (LPFTP/LPOTP) feed high-pressure turbopumps (HPFTP/HPOTP) in what configuration?
A) Series staging
B) Parallel staging
C) Gravity feed
D) Venturi ejector
Low-pressure pumps prevent propellant tank cavitation, feeding in series to high-pressure main pumps which boost liquid hydrogen to over $400 \text{ bar}$.
Q28. If two dissimilar pumps in parallel have head curves $H_1 = 40 - 50 Q_1^2$ and $H_2 = 25 - 20 Q_2^2$, what is the combined curve for heads between $25 \text{ m}$ and $40 \text{ m}$?
A) $H = 40 - 50 Q^2$ (Pump 1 operating alone)
B) $H = 65 - 70 Q^2$
C) $H = 25 - 20 Q^2$
D) $H = 0$
Above $25 \text{ m}$, Pump 2 cannot deliver flow (shut-off head is $25 \text{ m}$). Thus Pump 1 operates alone, and the combined curve is simply $H = 40 - 50 Q^2$.
Q29. What is the total flow delivered in Q28 when operating head is $H = 20 \text{ m}$?
A) $Q \approx 1.132 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
B) $Q \approx 0.632 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
C) $Q \approx 0.500 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
D) $Q = 0$
At $H = 20$: $Q_1 = \sqrt{(40 - 20)/50} = \sqrt{20/50} = \sqrt{0.4} \approx 0.6325 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$. $Q_2 = \sqrt{(25 - 20)/20} = \sqrt{5/20} = \sqrt{0.25} = 0.500 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$. Total $Q = 0.6325 + 0.500 = 1.1325 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$.
Q30. In multi-pump station design, what is a 'lead-lag' control strategy?
A) A primary lead pump runs continuously, while secondary lag pumps automatically start in parallel as system flow demand increases
B) Pumps alternate rotating clockwise and counter-clockwise
C) All pumps run at zero speed
D) Lead pump pumps water, lag pump pumps air
Lead-lag staging optimizes energy efficiency by running only the minimum number of parallel pumps needed to satisfy real-time flow demand.

Overcoming high frictional resistance requires high head capability, which is directly provided by series staging.

A) $30 \text{ m}$
B) $45 \text{ m}$
C) $90 \text{ m}$
D) $270 \text{ m}$
$H_{stage} = \frac{H_{total}}{n} = \frac{90}{3} = 30 \text{ m}$.

A) The smaller pump is deadheaded and delivers zero flow, with severe risk of overheating
B) Both pumps double their speed
C) The larger pump shuts down
D) Power consumption drops to zero
The smaller pump cannot generate enough head to push past the manifold check valve, churning trapped liquid and overheating.
MT5. What is the purpose of check (non-return) valves on parallel pump discharge lines?
A) To prevent reverse flow through an inactive or lower-head pump
B) To increase total fluid velocity
C) To eliminate pump priming
D) To measure flow rate
Check valves prevent reverse rotation, manifold pressure loss, and back-leakage when one pump is stopped.
MT6. In an aircraft fuel system, why are main and standby boost pumps connected in parallel rather than in series?
A) To provide hot-standby redundancy where either pump alone can supply full engine demand
B) To produce 10,000 psi fuel pressure

Parallel redundancy ensures that if the primary pump fails, the check valve seats and the parallel standby pump maintains full engine flow.
MT7. If a single pump delivers $Q_1 = 40 \text{ L/s}$ in a friction-free static lift pipeline ($H_{sys} = \text{const}$), two identical pumps in parallel will deliver:

B) $40 \text{ L/s}$
C) $160 \text{ L/s}$
D) $20 \text{ L/s}$
In the absence of pipeline friction ($h_f = 0$), system head does not increase with flow, so parallel flow doubles exactly ($2 \times 40 = 80 \text{ L/s}$).
MT8. In a two-stage series pump, if Stage 1 develops $40 \text{ m}$ head and Stage 2 develops $50 \text{ m}$ head at flow $Q$, the total head is:
A) $90 \text{ m}$

MT9. What mechanical component in a multi-stage centrifugal pump guides liquid from the exit of one impeller to the suction eye of the next impeller?
A) Return guide passage (return channel with vanes)
B) Butterfly throttle valve
C) Needle nozzle

Stationary return channels decelerate the outward flow from stage $n$ and guide it radially inward to the eye of stage $n+1$.
MT10. What is the primary hydraulic benefit of a double-suction impeller over two separate parallel single-suction pumps?
A) Compact single shaft with perfect hydrodynamic balance of opposing axial suction thrust forces
B) Triple the output head
C) 100% mechanical efficiency

Symmetrical dual suction entries cancel axial thrust loads completely, reducing bearing wear while doubling suction capacity in a single compact machine.

Top Candidate Error Traps

  1. Assuming Parallel Operation Doubles System Discharge: In any real pipeline with friction ($h_f \propto Q^2$), operating two identical pumps in parallel yields significantly less than $2\times$ flow because operating head increases.
  2. Deadheading in Dissimilar Parallel Pumps: If system head exceeds the shut-off head of the weaker pump, that pump delivers zero flow and rapidly overheats.
  3. Ignoring Casing Pressure Limits in Series Pumps: The second stage pump casing must be structurally rated for the combined total discharge pressure ($p_{stage1} + p_{stage2}$).
  4. Reversing Stage Failure in Series: If Stage 1 trips, it becomes a major obstruction, severely starving the Stage 2 impeller and inducing violent cavitation.
