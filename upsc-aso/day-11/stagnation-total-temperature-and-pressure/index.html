Stagnation (Total) Temperature and Pressure

Day 11 of 129 Fluid Mechanics & Compressible Flow Phase 1: Foundations 70-90 min Comprehensive Module

Level 1: Beginner's Glossary & Fundamental Definitions of Terms Basic

Before exploring derivations, here is the complete beginner's dictionary of all physical terms, parameters, symbols, and units used in compressible fluid thermodynamics:

1. Static Temperature ($T$)

  • Definition: The true thermodynamic temperature of a fluid particle in motion, measured by a thermometer traveling at the exact same velocity ($V$) as the fluid stream.
  • Symbol: $T$ (or $T_{static}, T_\infty$). SI Unit: Kelvin ($\text{K}$) or Celsius ($^\circ\text{C}$).
  • Intuition: If you are floating inside a cloud at $800 \text{ km/h}$, the air temperature your skin feels is the static temperature $T$.

2. Static Pressure ($p$)

  • Definition: The actual thermodynamic compressive force per unit area exerted by fluid molecules colliding randomly against adjacent surfaces parallel to the flow.
  • Symbol: $p$ (or $p_{static}, p_\infty$). SI Unit: Pascal ($\text{Pa} = \text{N/m}^2$) or $\text{kPa}, \text{bar}$.
  • Measurement: Measured via flush wall static pressure taps perpendicular to streamlines.

3. Dynamic Pressure ($q$)

  • Definition: The kinetic energy of directed bulk fluid motion per unit volume.
  • Symbol: $q$. SI Unit: Pascal ($\text{Pa}$).
  • Formulas: Incompressible: $q = \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$. Compressible: $q = \frac{1}{2}\gamma p M^2$.
  • Intuition: The force pushing your hand backward when held out of a fast-moving aircraft or car window.

4. Stagnation / Total Temperature ($T_0$ or $T_t$)

  • Definition: The elevated temperature reached when a moving fluid stream of velocity $V$ is brought to complete rest ($V = 0$) adiabatically (without heat transfer $q = 0$).
  • Symbol: $T_0$ (or $T_t$). SI Unit: Kelvin ($\text{K}$).
  • Physical Cause: Entire kinetic energy of flow ($\frac{V^2}{2}$) is converted into thermal enthalpy ($C_p \Delta T$).
  • Formula: $T_0 = T + \frac{V^2}{2 C_p} = T \left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2\right)$.

5. Stagnation / Total Pressure ($p_0$ or $p_t$)

  • Definition: The maximum pressure attained when a moving fluid is brought to rest isentropically (both adiabatically and reversibly with zero friction).
  • Symbol: $p_0$ (or $p_t$). SI Unit: Pascal ($\text{Pa}$) or $\text{kPa}$.
  • Incompressible: $p_0 = p + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$.
  • Compressible: $p_0 = p \left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}$.

6. Mach Number ($M$) & Speed of Sound ($a$)

  • Speed of Sound ($a$): Speed at which acoustic pressure waves propagate: $a = \sqrt{\gamma R T}$ ($\approx 340 \text{ m/s}$ at sea level).
  • Mach Number ($M$): Dimensionless ratio of flow velocity to local speed of sound: $M = \frac{V}{a} = \frac{V}{\sqrt{\gamma R T}}$.
  • Regimes: $M < 0.3$ (Incompressible), $0.3 \le M < 0.8$ (Subsonic), $0.8 \le M \le 1.2$ (Transonic), $M > 1.2$ (Supersonic), $M > 5$ (Hypersonic).

Level 2: Core Formulations & Mathematical Derivations Intermediate

1. Stagnation Enthalpy & Temperature Derivation

Applying the Steady Flow Energy Equation (SFEE) for an adiabatic fluid stream ($q = 0, w = 0$):

$$ h_0 = h + \frac{V^2}{2} $$ $$ C_p T_0 = C_p T + \frac{V^2}{2} \implies T_0 = T + \frac{V^2}{2 C_p} $$

Using $C_p = \frac{\gamma R}{\gamma - 1}$ and the speed of sound relation $a^2 = \gamma R T$ with $M = \frac{V}{a}$:

$$ \frac{T_0}{T} = 1 + \frac{\frac{V^2}{2}}{\frac{\gamma R T}{\gamma - 1}} = 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2}\left(\frac{V^2}{\gamma R T}\right) = 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2}M^2 $$

2. Stagnation Pressure & Density Ratios

Assuming the deceleration to rest is reversible and adiabatic (isentropic, $p \propto T^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}}$):

$$ \frac{p_0}{p} = \left(\frac{T_0}{T}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}} = \left(1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2}M^2\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}} $$ $$ \frac{\rho_0}{\rho} = \left(\frac{T_0}{T}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma - 1}} = \left(1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2}M^2\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma - 1}} $$

3. Critical (Sonic, $M = 1$) Stagnation State (for Air, $\gamma = 1.4$)

Property Ratio General Expression Value for Air ($\gamma = 1.4$)
Critical Temperature Ratio ($T^*/T_0$) $\frac{2}{\gamma + 1}$ $0.8333$
Critical Pressure Ratio ($p^*/p_0$) $\left(\frac{2}{\gamma + 1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}}$ $0.5283$ (Choking threshold)
Critical Density Ratio ($\rho^*/\rho_0$) $\left(\frac{2}{\gamma + 1}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma - 1}}$ $0.6339$

Level 3: Advanced Analysis & Boundary Conditions Advanced

1. Total Pressure Loss Across Normal Shock Waves

  • Across a stationary normal shock, the flow is adiabatic $\implies$ $T_{01} = T_{02}$ (Stagnation temperature is strictly conserved).
  • However, normal shocks are highly irreversible ($\Delta s > 0$) $\implies$ $p_{02} < p_{01}$ (Severe total pressure loss).
  • The entropy generation link:
$$ s_2 - s_1 = C_p \ln\left(\frac{T_{02}}{T_{01}}\right) - R \ln\left(\frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}}\right) = -R \ln\left(\frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}}\right) > 0 $$

2. Pitot Tube Measurement in Supersonic Flow (Rayleigh Pitot Formula)

When a Pitot tube is placed in a supersonic stream ($M_1 > 1$), a bow shock forms ahead of the probe nose. The probe reads the stagnation pressure downstream of the normal shock ($p_{02}$):

$$ \frac{p_{02}}{p_1} = \left[\frac{(\gamma + 1)^2 M_1^2}{4\gamma M_1^2 - 2(\gamma - 1)}\right]^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}} \left[\frac{1 - \gamma + 2\gamma M_1^2}{\gamma + 1}\right] $$

3. Stagnation Quantities in Rotating Turbomachinery (Rothalpy)

In rotating blades (pumps, compressors, turbines), total enthalpy in the stationary frame is not constant. Instead, Rothalpy ($I$) is invariant along a streamline:

$$ I = h_0 - u \cdot V_w = h + \frac{V_r^2 - u^2}{2} = \text{constant} $$

Level 4: High-Yield Memorization Summary

Process / Component Total Temperature ($T_0$) Total Pressure ($p_0$) Reason / Governing Principle
Isentropic Nozzle / Diffuser Constant Constant Adiabatic & Reversible ($dq = 0, ds = 0$)
Frictional Duct Flow (Fanno) Constant Decreases Adiabatic but irreversible friction ($ds > 0$)
Heat Addition (Rayleigh) Increases Decreases Energy addition ($dq > 0$) causes total pressure drop
Stationary Shock Wave Constant Decreases Adiabatic flow with large entropy generation
Compressor / Pump Impeller Increases Increases External shaft work input ($w_{in} > 0$)
Gas Turbine / Expander Decreases Decreases Shaft work output delivered ($w_{out} > 0$)

Level 5: Aviation & ASO Practical Applications

  • Pitot-Static System & Airspeed Indicator (ASI): Differential pressure diaphragm measures $p_{impact} - p_{static} = p_0 - p$. Calibrated airspeed (CAS) is derived directly from this stagnation pressure difference.
  • Total Air Temperature (TAT) Probe: Measures recovery stagnation temperature $T_0$ on the aircraft skin. Static Air Temperature (SAT / OAT) is computed by avionics via $SAT = \frac{TAT}{1 + 0.2 M^2}$ for airspeed and icing management.
  • Engine Intake Total Pressure Recovery ($\eta_R$): Defines inlet aerodynamic efficiency $\eta_R = \frac{p_{02}}{p_{0\infty}}$. High total pressure recovery directly maximizes jet engine thrust and prevents compressor surge.

Level 6: UPSC ASO Focus & Common Traps

  • Trap 1 (Shocks): Total temperature across a normal shock wave remains unchanged ($T_{01} = T_{02}$), while static temperature $T$ jumps. Total pressure $p_0$ always drops.
  • Trap 2 (Compressibility Threshold): Incompressible Bernoulli $p_0 = p + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$ is only valid for $M < 0.3$. For $M \ge 0.3$, the isentropic binomial expansion introduces compressibility error exceeding 5%.
  • Trap 3 (Units): In all isentropic ratio formulas, temperatures must strictly be in Kelvin (K), not Celsius.

UPSC ASO Practice Questions (30 MCQs)

Q1. What is the fundamental condition required for stagnation pressure ($p_0$) to remain constant along a streamline?
A) The flow must only be adiabatic
B) The flow must be steady, adiabatic, and reversible (isentropic)
C) The flow must be purely isothermal
D) The flow must be supersonic everywhere
Stagnation temperature requires only adiabatic flow, but stagnation pressure requires both adiabatic and reversible (isentropic) flow without friction or shocks.
Q2. For an ideal gas with $\gamma = 1.4$, what is the ratio of stagnation temperature to static temperature ($T_0 / T$) at Mach 1.0?
A) $1.00$
B) $1.20$
C) $1.40$
D) $1.89$
$\frac{T_0}{T} = 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2}M^2 = 1 + \frac{0.4}{2}(1.0)^2 = 1 + 0.2 = 1.20$.
Q3. What is the critical pressure ratio ($p^*/p_0$) for air ($\gamma = 1.4$) at sonic conditions ($M = 1$)?
A) $0.528$
B) $0.833$
C) $0.634$
D) $0.714$
$\frac{p^*}{p_0} = \left(\frac{2}{\gamma + 1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}} = \left(\frac{2}{2.4}\right)^{3.5} = (0.8333)^{3.5} \approx 0.5283$.
Q4. Across a stationary normal shock wave in an ideal gas, which of the following statements is true?
A) Both $T_0$ and $p_0$ remain constant
B) $T_0$ increases and $p_0$ decreases
C) $T_0$ remains constant while $p_0$ decreases
D) Both $T_0$ and $p_0$ decrease
Since the shock is adiabatic, $T_0$ is constant ($T_{01} = T_{02}$). However, because entropy is generated ($\Delta s > 0$), total pressure drops ($p_{02} < p_{01}$).
Q5. An aircraft flies at Mach 2.0 where the ambient static temperature is $220 \text{ K}$. What is the stagnation temperature ($T_0$)? ($\gamma = 1.4$)
A) $264 \text{ K}$
B) $396 \text{ K}$
C) $440 \text{ K}$
D) $528 \text{ K}$
$T_0 = T\left(1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2}M^2\right) = 220\left(1 + 0.2(2^2)\right) = 220(1 + 0.8) = 220(1.8) = 396 \text{ K}$.
Q6. In incompressible fluid flow, what instrument measures the stagnation pressure directly?
A) Static tap perpendicular to the wall
B) Open-ended tube facing directly into the flow (Pitot tube)
C) Venturi throat tap
D) Orifice meter downstream tap
A forward-facing open tube decelerates the oncoming fluid to zero velocity at its tip, measuring total (stagnation) pressure.
Q7. What is the relation between total pressure loss ($\Delta p_0$) and entropy change ($\Delta s$) in an adiabatic gas flow?
A) $\Delta s = R \ln(p_{01}/p_{02})$
B) $\Delta s = C_p \ln(p_{01}/p_{02})$
C) $\Delta s = \frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma} \ln(p_{01}/p_{02})$
D) $\Delta s = 0$ always
$\Delta s = s_2 - s_1 = -R \ln\left(\frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}}\right) = R \ln\left(\frac{p_{01}}{p_{02}}\right)$ when $T_{01} = T_{02}$.
Q8. For an airflow with static pressure $100 \text{ kPa}$ and Mach number $M = 0.5$, what is the stagnation pressure $p_0$? ($\gamma = 1.4$)
A) $105.2 \text{ kPa}$
B) $118.6 \text{ kPa}$
C) $128.3 \text{ kPa}$
D) $152.8 \text{ kPa}$
$\frac{p_0}{p} = (1 + 0.2(0.5^2))^{3.5} = (1 + 0.05)^{3.5} = (1.05)^{3.5} \approx 1.1862$. Thus $p_0 = 100 \times 1.1862 = 118.62 \text{ kPa}$.
Q9. In a subsonic compressible flow, the dynamic pressure is related to static pressure and Mach number by:
A) $q = \frac{1}{2}\gamma p M^2$
B) $q = \gamma p M$
C) $q = \frac{p}{\gamma M^2}$
D) $q = \frac{1}{2}p M^2$
$q = \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 = \frac{1}{2}\rho (M a)^2 = \frac{1}{2}\rho M^2 (\gamma R T) = \frac{1}{2}\gamma (\rho R T) M^2 = \frac{1}{2}\gamma p M^2$.
Q10. What happens to total pressure ($p_0$) during heat addition in frictionless duct flow (Rayleigh flow)?
A) $p_0$ increases
B) $p_0$ decreases
C) $p_0$ remains constant
D) $p_0$ drops to zero immediately
Heat addition in Rayleigh flow always reduces stagnation pressure ($p_0$) due to thermal throttling/losses, whether the flow is subsonic or supersonic.
Q11. In aircraft flight testing, Total Air Temperature (TAT) is higher than Static Air Temperature (SAT) due to:
A) Solar radiation on the sensor
B) Compressibility ram heating effect
C) Atmospheric humidity condensation
D) Engine exhaust recirculation
As air is brought to rest on the temperature probe, its kinetic energy converts to thermal energy (ram rise: $\Delta T = T_0 - T = \frac{V^2}{2 C_p}$).
Q12. What is the value of the recovery factor ($r$) for a standard laminar boundary layer on a temperature probe?
A) $r = \sqrt{Pr}$
B) $r = Pr^{1/3}$
C) $r = 1.0$ exactly
D) $r = Pr^2$
For laminar boundary layers, the recovery factor is $r = \sqrt{Pr} \approx \sqrt{0.72} \approx 0.85$. For turbulent boundary layers, $r = Pr^{1/3} \approx 0.89$.
Q13. In a supersonic wind tunnel, if the test section Mach number is $M = 3.0$, the stagnation-to-static pressure ratio ($p_0/p$) for $\gamma = 1.4$ is approximately:
A) $12.5$
B) $24.8$
C) $36.7$
D) $48.2$
$\frac{p_0}{p} = (1 + 0.2(3^2))^{3.5} = (1 + 1.8)^{3.5} = (2.8)^{3.5} \approx 36.73$.
Q14. The binomial expansion of the isentropic stagnation pressure equation yields: $\frac{p_0 - p}{\frac{1}{2}\rho V^2} = 1 + \frac{1}{4}M^2 + \frac{2-\gamma}{24}M^4 + \dots$ At what Mach number is the compressibility error approximately 1%?
A) $M \approx 0.10$
B) $M \approx 0.20$
C) $M \approx 0.50$
D) $M \approx 1.00$
At $M = 0.2$, the first correction term is $\frac{1}{4}M^2 = \frac{1}{4}(0.04) = 0.01$ (1% error). Beyond $M = 0.3$ (2.25% error), flow must be treated as compressible.
Q15. In a turbomachine impeller, the parameter that remains constant across the rotor in the relative frame of reference is called:
A) Stagnation enthalpy ($h_0$)
B) Rothalpy ($I = h_0 - u V_w$)
C) Total pressure ($p_0$)
D) Static pressure ($p$)
Rothalpy ($I = h + \frac{V_r^2 - u^2}{2} = h_0 - u V_w$) is invariant along a streamline in rotating turbomachinery under steady adiabatic conditions.
Q16. If air flows at $V = 300 \text{ m/s}$ with $T = 300 \text{ K}$ and $C_p = 1005 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$, calculate its stagnation temperature ($T_0$).
A) $315 \text{ K}$
B) $344.8 \text{ K}$
C) $389.5 \text{ K}$
D) $412.0 \text{ K}$
$T_0 = T + \frac{V^2}{2 C_p} = 300 + \frac{300^2}{2 \times 1005} = 300 + \frac{90000}{2010} = 300 + 44.78 = 344.78 \text{ K}$.
Q17. Which of the following is true for an adiabatic expansion through a convergent nozzle?
A) Static temperature increases, stagnation temperature decreases
B) Static temperature decreases, stagnation temperature remains constant
C) Both static and stagnation temperature decrease
D) Both static and stagnation temperature remain constant
In an adiabatic expansion, thermal energy converts to kinetic energy ($V$ increases $\implies T$ drops), while total enthalpy/temperature $T_0$ remains constant.
Q18. The total pressure ratio across a normal shock wave ($p_{02}/p_{01}$) as upstream Mach number $M_1 \to 1$ approaches:
A) $0.0$
B) $0.528$
C) $1.0$
D) $\infty$
As $M_1 \to 1$, the shock wave degenerates into an isentropic acoustic wave where entropy generation $\Delta s = 0$, so $p_{02}/p_{01} = 1.0$.
Q19. For a gas with $\gamma = 1.33$ (such as high-temperature combustion gas), what is the critical temperature ratio ($T^*/T_0$)?
A) $0.858$
B) $0.833$
C) $0.528$
D) $0.750$
$\frac{T^*}{T_0} = \frac{2}{\gamma + 1} = \frac{2}{1.33 + 1} = \frac{2}{2.33} \approx 0.8584$.
Q20. When a Pitot-static tube in a wind tunnel experiences flow with $M = 0.8$, the measured impact pressure corresponds to:
A) Isentropic stagnation pressure $p_0$
B) Post-shock stagnation pressure $p_{02}$
C) Incompressible stagnation pressure $p + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$
D) Static pressure only
Because the flow is subsonic ($M < 1$), no shock wave forms ahead of the probe. Deceleration to rest is isentropic, so it measures the free-stream $p_0$.
Q21. The inlet total pressure recovery factor $\eta_R$ of a supersonic jet intake is defined as:
A) $\eta_R = \frac{p_2}{p_1}$
B) $\eta_R = \frac{p_{02}}{p_{0\infty}}$
C) $\eta_R = \frac{T_{02}}{T_{0\infty}}$
D) $\eta_R = \frac{M_2}{M_1}$
Inlet pressure recovery is the ratio of total pressure at the engine compressor face ($p_{02}$) to ambient free-stream total pressure ($p_{0\infty}$).
Q22. In Fanno flow (flow with friction in a constant-area adiabatic pipe), the stagnation temperature $T_0$:
A) Remains constant along the entire duct
B) Continuously increases
C) Continuously decreases
D) First increases, then decreases
Because Fanno flow is adiabatic ($q = 0$) with no shaft work ($w = 0$), $h_0$ and therefore $T_0$ remain strictly constant.
Q23. In an isentropic flow of air ($\gamma = 1.4$), the ratio of static density to stagnation density ($\rho / \rho_0$) at $M = 1$ is:
A) $0.528$
B) $0.634$
C) $0.833$
D) $1.000$
$\frac{\rho^*}{\rho_0} = \left(\frac{2}{\gamma + 1}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma - 1}} = \left(\frac{2}{2.4}\right)^{2.5} = (0.8333)^{2.5} \approx 0.6339$.
Q24. An airflow has static temperature $250 \text{ K}$ and speed $200 \text{ m/s}$. Given $R = 287 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$ and $\gamma = 1.4$, what is its Mach number?
A) $0.55$
B) $0.63$
C) $0.72$
D) $0.80$
$a = \sqrt{\gamma R T} = \sqrt{1.4 \times 287 \times 250} = \sqrt{100450} \approx 316.94 \text{ m/s}$. Then $M = \frac{200}{316.94} \approx 0.631$.
Q25. For an isentropic gas flow, as the Mach number approaches infinity ($M \to \infty$), the static temperature $T$:
A) Approaches $T_0$
B) Approaches zero ($0 \text{ K}$)
C) Approaches infinity
D) Remains constant
As $M \to \infty$, all thermal enthalpy converts into directed kinetic energy, so $T \to 0$ and $V \to V_{max} = \sqrt{2 C_p T_0}$.
Q26. What is the maximum theoretical velocity ($V_{max}$) achievable by expanding an ideal gas from stagnation temperature $T_0$?
A) $V_{max} = \sqrt{2 C_p T_0}$
B) $V_{max} = \sqrt{\gamma R T_0}$
C) $V_{max} = \sqrt{2 R T_0}$
D) $V_{max} = \sqrt{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma + 1} R T_0}$
From energy conservation: $C_p T_0 = C_p T + \frac{V^2}{2}$. When $T = 0 \text{ K}$, $V_{max} = \sqrt{2 C_p T_0} = \sqrt{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma - 1} R T_0}$.
Q27. When an aircraft flies through the stratosphere, if its true airspeed doubles while ambient temperature is constant, the ram temperature rise ($\Delta T = T_0 - T$):
A) Doubles
B) Quadruples (increases 4 times)
C) Remains unchanged
D) Increases by $\sqrt{2}$ times
Ram temperature rise is $\Delta T = \frac{V^2}{2 C_p} \propto V^2$. If $V$ doubles, $\Delta T$ increases by $2^2 = 4$ times.
Q28. In supersonic flow past a wedge, what is the nature of the shock wave formed, and what happens to stagnation pressure across it?
A) Oblique shock; $p_0$ remains constant
B) Oblique shock; $p_0$ decreases (but loss is less than a normal shock at the same Mach number)
C) Normal shock; $p_0$ increases
D) Expansion fan; $p_0$ decreases
An attached oblique shock forms. Because the normal component $M_{1n} < M_1$, total pressure loss is less severe than across a normal shock.
Q29. A convergent-divergent supersonic nozzle operates under design condition. What happens to stagnation pressure throughout the nozzle if the flow is isentropic?
A) Increases in convergent, decreases in divergent
B) Decreases continuously
C) Remains completely uniform and constant ($p_0 = \text{const}$)
D) Drops only at the throat
In an isentropic nozzle without shock waves or friction, stagnation pressure $p_0$ is constant from inlet to exit.
Q30. If a pressure sensor on an aircraft wing surface is flush with the skin, it measures:
A) Local static pressure $p$
B) Free-stream total pressure $p_0$
C) Dynamic pressure $q$

MT1. What is the fundamental difference between stagnation temperature and stagnation pressure requirements?
A) $T_0$ requires isothermal flow; $p_0$ requires isobaric flow

$T_0$ is governed purely by the first law (SFEE) with $q = 0$, while $p_0$ additionally requires second law reversibility ($\Delta s = 0$).

D) $1708.6 \text{ kPa}$
$\frac{p_0}{p} = (1 + 0.2(2.5^2))^{3.5} = (1 + 1.25)^{3.5} = (2.25)^{3.5} = 17.0859$. Thus $p_0 = 50 \times 17.0859 = 854.3 \text{ kPa}$.

A) Heat is added to the shock
B) The shock is adiabatic ($q = 0$), but irreversible viscous dissipation creates entropy ($\Delta s > 0$)
C) Mass flow is not conserved

Because no external heat transfer occurs, $h_0$ is conserved. But massive internal friction inside the thin shock layer generates entropy, destroying available total pressure.

Top Candidate Error Traps

  1. Confusing Static and Total Temperatures in Shock Problems: Static temperature ($T$) rises sharply across a normal shock, but total temperature ($T_0$) is strictly constant because no heat is added or removed ($q = 0$).
  2. Using Celsius instead of Kelvin: Forgetting to add $273.15$ to temperatures before applying $\frac{T_0}{T} = 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2}M^2$.
  3. Applying Incompressible Bernoulli at High Speeds: Applying $p_0 = p + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$ when $M > 0.3$. Always use the isentropic relation $\frac{p_0}{p} = (1 + 0.2 M^2)^{3.5}$ for air.
  4. Assuming $p_0$ is Constant in Frictional Flows: Fanno flow is adiabatic ($T_0 = \text{const}$), but friction generates entropy and continuously destroys total pressure ($p_0$ decreases).
