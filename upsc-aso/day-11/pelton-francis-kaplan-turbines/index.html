Pelton, Francis & Kaplan Hydraulic Turbines

Day 11 of 129 Hydraulic Turbomachinery Phase 1: Foundations 85-100 min Master Module

Level 1: Beginner's Glossary & Hydraulic Turbine Definitions Basic

Before comparing turbine mechanics, here is the complete beginner's dictionary of all hydraulic turbine classifications, parameters, symbols, and components:

1. Hydraulic Turbine & Prime Mover

  • Definition: A rotodynamic machine that converts the potential and kinetic energy of flowing water into mechanical rotational shaft power to drive an electric generator.
  • Power Output ($P$): $P = \eta_o \rho g Q H$ [$\text{kW}$].

2. Impulse Turbine (Pelton Wheel)

  • Definition: A turbine where all the available hydraulic pressure head is converted entirely into kinetic energy in stationary nozzles at atmospheric pressure ($p = p_{atm}$) before striking the runner buckets.
  • Degree of Reaction: $R = 0$ (Zero pressure drop across runner).
  • Application: High Head ($H > 250 \text{ m}$), Low Flow Rate.

3. Reaction Turbine (Francis & Kaplan)

  • Definition: A turbine where water enters the runner under both pressure and velocity; pressure drops continuously as water expands across the moving runner blades ($0 < R < 1$).
  • Francis: Mixed radial-inflow for Medium Head ($60 - 250 \text{ m}$).
  • Kaplan: Pure axial-flow with adjustable blades for Low Head ($H < 60 \text{ m}$), High Flow.

4. Degree of Reaction ($R$)

  • Definition: The fraction of total stage static pressure/enthalpy drop that occurs within the rotating runner: $$R = \frac{\Delta p_{runner}}{\Delta p_{total}} = \frac{H_{static}}{H_{total}}$$
  • • Impulse (Pelton): $R = 0$
    • Francis Turbine: $0.3 \le R \le 0.7$
    • Kaplan Turbine: $R \ge 0.7$

5. Draft Tube

  • Definition: A gradually expanding airtight pipe connecting the reaction turbine runner outlet to the tailrace channel.
  • Two Functions:
    1. Allows placing the turbine above tailrace water level without losing net head.
    2. Converts discarded runner exit kinetic energy ($\frac{V_2^2}{2g}$) into useful suction pressure head.

6. Jet Ratio ($m$) & Speed Ratio ($\phi$)

  • Jet Ratio ($m$): Ratio of Pelton wheel pitch diameter ($D$) to water jet diameter ($d$): $m = \frac{D}{d}$ (standard design: $m \approx 11 - 15$).
  • Speed Ratio ($\phi$): Ratio of blade speed to jet velocity: $\phi = \frac{u}{\sqrt{2gH}}$ ($\phi \approx 0.45 - 0.48$ for Pelton).

Level 2: Core Engineering Formulations & Velocity Triangles Intermediate

1. Pelton Turbine Mechanics

  • Nozzle Jet Velocity: $V_1 = C_v \sqrt{2gH}$ (where $C_v \approx 0.98 - 0.99$).
  • Speed Ratio ($k_u$): $k_u = \frac{u}{\sqrt{2gH}} \approx 0.45 - 0.46$ (for maximum efficiency, $u = \frac{V_1}{2}$).
  • Jet Ratio ($m$): $m = \frac{D}{d} \approx 11 - 14$ (Pitch diameter $D$ to nozzle jet diameter $d$).
  • Bucket Geometry: Double-hemispherical cup with central splitter dividing jet into two equal streams, deflected by angle $\delta = 165^\circ - 170^\circ$ ($\beta = 180^\circ - \delta = 10^\circ - 15^\circ$).
  • Maximum Theoretical Hydraulic Efficiency ($\eta_{h,max}$):
$$ \eta_h = \frac{2 u(V_1 - u)(1 + k\cos\beta)}{V_1^2} \implies \eta_{h,max} = \frac{1 + k\cos\beta}{2} \approx 95 - 98\% \quad \text{at } u = \frac{V_1}{2} $$

2. Francis Turbine Mechanics

  • Degree of Reaction ($R$): Fraction of total head drop occurring inside the runner: $$R = \frac{\text{Static Pressure Drop in Runner}}{\text{Total Head Across Turbine}} \approx 0.5 \quad (50\% \text{ in guide vanes, } 50\% \text{ in runner})$$
  • Radial Discharge at Outlet ($\alpha_2 = 90^\circ$): To maximize energy extraction, water leaves without whirl ($V_{w2} = 0$).
  • Euler Turbine Equation:
$$ H_e = \frac{V_{w1} u_1 - V_{w2} u_2}{g} = \frac{V_{w1} u_1}{g} \quad (\text{when } V_{w2} = 0) $$ $$ \text{Hydraulic Efficiency: } \quad \eta_h = \frac{V_{w1} u_1}{g H} $$

3. Kaplan Turbine Mechanics

  • Flow Rate Equation: $Q = \frac{\pi}{4}(D_o^2 - D_h^2) V_f$ (where $D_o$ is outer runner tip diameter and $D_h$ is hub/boss diameter, $D_h/D_o \approx 0.35 - 0.6$).
  • Double Regulation: Both guide vanes (wicket gates) and runner blades rotate automatically via governor servos, maintaining exceptional efficiency ($\approx 90\%$) from $20\%$ to $100\%$ full load.

Level 3: Draft Tube Theory, Specific Speed & Cavitation Advanced

1. Draft Tube Function & Efficiency

The draft tube is an airtight expanding pipe connecting the reaction turbine runner exit to the tailrace. It performs two vital functions:

  • 1. Head Recovery: Allows turbine installation at height $h_s$ above tailrace level without losing static suction head.
  • 2. Kinetic Energy Conversion: Diffuser decelerates exit water from $V_2$ down to $V_3$, converting dynamic head into negative suction pressure head at the runner exit.
$$ \frac{p_2}{\rho g} = \frac{p_{atm}}{\rho g} - h_s - \left(\frac{V_2^2 - V_3^2}{2g} - h_{f,dt}\right) $$ $$ \text{Draft Tube Efficiency: } \quad \eta_{dt} = \frac{\frac{V_2^2 - V_3^2}{2g} - h_f}{\frac{V_2^2}{2g}} $$

2. Turbine Specific Speed ($N_s$)

Speed of a geometrically similar model turbine producing unit power ($1 \text{ kW}$) under unit head ($1 \text{ m}$):

$$ N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}} \quad (P \text{ in kW, } H \text{ in m, } N \text{ in rpm}) $$

3. Cavitation & Setting Height in Reaction Turbines

To avoid cavitation at runner exit, Thoma's factor $\sigma = \frac{(p_{atm}/\rho g) - h_s - (p_v/\rho g)}{H} \ge \sigma_c$. The maximum allowable installation height above tailrace is:

$$ h_{s,max} = \frac{p_{atm} - p_v}{\rho g} - \sigma_c H $$

Level 5: Aviation & ASO Practical Applications

  • Ram Air Turbine (RAT): Emergency deployable wind turbine on modern commercial aircraft (Airbus A320/A350, Boeing 777/787). Operates on pure axial impulse-reaction principles to extract kinetic energy from the oncoming slipstream, driving an emergency hydraulic pump and 400 Hz electrical generator during dual-engine failure.
  • Rocket Turbopump Drive Turbines: Supersonic gas turbines driven by preburner or gas generator exhaust (Curtis velocity-compounded or Rateau pressure-compounded impulse stages) generate $20,000 - 100,000 \text{ HP}$ to drive fuel and oxidizer pumps.
  • APU Radial-Inflow Expansion Turbines: Aircraft auxiliary power units use compact radial-inflow gas expansion turbines (similar to inward Francis reaction runners) for cabin air cycle machine (ACM) refrigeration.

Level 6: UPSC ASO Focus & Common Traps

  • Trap 1 (Draft Tube on Pelton): A Pelton wheel never uses a draft tube because its casing is vented to the atmosphere. Francis and Kaplan turbines must have an airtight draft tube.
  • Trap 2 (Turbine Specific Speed Exponent): Turbine $N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$ (exponent is $5/4 = 1.25$). Pump $N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{Q}}{H^{3/4}}$ (exponent is $3/4 = 0.75$).
  • Trap 3 (Optimal Pelton Blade Speed): For max theoretical efficiency, bucket peripheral speed is exactly half the jet speed ($u = 0.5 V_1$). In actual practice with friction, $u \approx 0.46 V_1$.

UPSC ASO Practice Questions (30 MCQs)

Q1. A hydraulic power plant has a net available head of $400 \text{ m}$. Which hydraulic turbine is most appropriate?
A) Kaplan turbine
B) Propeller turbine
C) Pelton wheel
D) Archimedes screw turbine
High heads ($H > 250 \text{ m}$) are the standard operational domain of impulse Pelton wheels.
Q2. What is the specific speed formula for a hydraulic turbine in terms of shaft power $P$ (kW), net head $H$ (m), and rotational speed $N$ (rpm)?
A) $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{P}}{H^{3/4}}$
B) $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$
C) $N_s = \frac{N P^2}{H^{5/4}}$
D) $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{H}}{P^{3/4}}$
For hydraulic turbines, specific speed is $N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$ (where $P$ is in kW, $H$ is in m).
Q3. For maximum theoretical hydraulic efficiency of a Pelton wheel, the peripheral bucket speed ($u$) should be:
A) Equal to jet velocity ($u = V_1$)
B) Half of the jet velocity ($u = \frac{V_1}{2}$)
C) Twice the jet velocity ($u = 2 V_1$)
D) Zero
Differentiating hydraulic efficiency $\eta_h = \frac{2u(V_1 - u)(1 + k\cos\beta)}{V_1^2}$ with respect to $u$ gives $\frac{d\eta_h}{du} = 0 \implies u = \frac{V_1}{2}$.
Q4. What is the primary function of a draft tube fitted at the exit of a reaction turbine runner?
A) To increase the exit velocity of water into the tailrace
B) To recover the kinetic energy leaving the runner by converting it into suction pressure head, preventing head loss
C) To eliminate the need for stay vanes
D) To reverse the direction of rotation
The expanding draft tube decelerates runner exit flow ($V_2 \to V_3$), converting dynamic head into negative suction pressure at the runner exit, increasing effective turbine head.
Q5. In a Kaplan turbine, what feature distinguishes it from a simple Propeller turbine?
A) Kaplan turbine has adjustable runner blades during operation
B) Kaplan turbine has no runner blades
C) Kaplan turbine is a pure impulse wheel
D) Kaplan turbine operates only at supersonic speeds
In Kaplan turbines, runner blades are automatically pitched by governor servomotors along with wicket gates (double regulation), yielding high part-load efficiency.
Q6. What is the jet ratio ($m$) of a Pelton wheel?
A) Ratio of jet velocity to blade speed ($V_1 / u$)
B) Ratio of pitch diameter of the wheel to diameter of the water jet ($D / d$)
C) Ratio of nozzle area to bucket volume
D) Ratio of gross head to net head
Jet ratio is $m = D/d$, typically designed between $11$ and $14$ (minimum $\approx 6$).
Q7. The central ridge or splitter in a Pelton bucket divides the incoming water jet into:
A) Four equal parts
B) Two equal halves, balancing axial thrust on the wheel shaft
C) One steam pocket
D) Radial vortices
The sharp splitter divides the circular jet into two symmetrical hemispherical halves that deflect sideways by $165^\circ$, perfectly neutralizing axial thrust forces.
Q8. A Francis turbine is designed to operate with radial discharge at outlet. This condition implies:
A) Outlet whirl velocity $V_{w2} = 0$ and guide angle $\alpha_2 = 90^\circ$
B) Absolute velocity $V_2 = 0$
C) Relative velocity $V_{r2} = 0$
D) Blade velocity $u_2 = 0$
Radial discharge means absolute outlet velocity has no tangential whirl component ($V_{w2} = 0$), minimizing residual kinetic energy loss in the tailrace.
Q9. A turbine develops $10,000 \text{ kW}$ under net head $H = 100 \text{ m}$ at speed $N = 300 \text{ rpm}$. What is its specific speed ($N_s$)?
A) $30$
B) $94.8$
C) $189.7$
D) $450$
$N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}} = \frac{300 \times \sqrt{10000}}{100^{1.25}} = \frac{300 \times 100}{(10^2)^{1.25}} = \frac{30000}{10^{2.5}} = \frac{30000}{316.227} \approx 94.87$. (Classifies as a Francis turbine).
Q10. Why is a draft tube NEVER used on a Pelton wheel installation?
A) The runner operates entirely at atmospheric pressure in an unflooded open casing
B) Draft tubes only work with hot oil
C) Pelton wheels rotate too fast for draft tubes
D) The jet cannot pass through metal
In impulse turbines, all pressure drop occurs in the nozzle. The runner rotates in air at atmospheric pressure, making an airtight draft tube meaningless.
Q11. What is the typical range of specific speeds ($N_s$) for a Kaplan turbine in metric SI units?
A) $10 - 35$
B) $60 - 300$
C) $300 - 1000$
D) $1500 - 3000$
Kaplan turbines handle huge flow at low head, giving high specific speeds ($N_s = 300 - 1000$).
Q12. In an aircraft emergency Ram Air Turbine (RAT), how is turbine rotational speed regulated across varying aircraft airspeeds?
A) Mechanical flyweight governor altering blade pitch angle (variable pitch mechanism)
B) Jet fuel throttle injection
C) Deploying a parachute behind the aircraft
D) Switching off the aircraft battery
The RAT uses counterweight spring-loaded governors to feather and adjust blade pitch automatically, maintaining constant generator/pump RPM regardless of flight speed.
Q13. What is the Degree of Reaction ($R$) for a pure impulse Pelton turbine?
A) $R = 0$
B) $R = 0.5$
C) $R = 1.0$
D) $R = \infty$
In an impulse turbine, there is zero static pressure change across the runner ($p_1 = p_2 = p_{atm}$), so Degree of Reaction is $R = 0$.
Q14. In a Pelton wheel governing system, what is the function of the jet deflector plate?
A) Instantly divert the jet away from the buckets during sudden generator load rejection, preventing runaway overspeed without causing water hammer in the penstock
B) Increase water pressure in the nozzle
C) Cool the generator bearings
D) Stop water flow completely in 1 millisecond
Rapid needle valve closure creates destructive water hammer in high-head penstocks. The deflector instantly steers the jet away while the spear needle closes slowly.
Q15. The number of buckets ($Z$) on a Pelton wheel is estimated using Tygun's formula:
A) $Z = 15 + 0.5 m$
B) $Z = 15 + m$
C) $Z = 20 - 0.5 m$
D) $Z = \pi m$
Tygun's standard empirical rule is $Z = 15 + \frac{m}{2} = 15 + 0.5\frac{D}{d}$.
Q16. In a Francis turbine, the scroll casing is designed with a continuously decreasing cross-sectional area around the circumference in order to:
A) Maintain a uniform water velocity and pressure around the entire runner perimeter
B) Accelerate flow to supersonic speed
C) Reduce turbine manufacturing weight
D) Trap sediment at the top
As water enters the guide vanes progressively around the circle, flow in the casing reduces. Decreasing the area proportionately keeps velocity constant: $V = Q/A = \text{const}$.
Q17. What type of draft tube provides the highest hydraulic efficiency in reaction turbine powerhouses?
A) Straight divergent conical tube ($\approx 90\%$)
B) Simple cylindrical pipe of constant diameter
C) Convergent nozzle
D) Perforated mesh tube
Straight conical diffusers with total cone angle $< 8^\circ$ yield the highest kinetic energy recovery efficiency ($\approx 85-90\%$). Elbow diffusers are used for layout compactness ($\approx 60-75\%$).
Q18. The overall efficiency ($\eta_o$) of a hydraulic turbine developing shaft power $P$ from discharge $Q$ under net head $H$ is:
A) $\eta_o = \frac{P}{\rho g Q H}$
B) $\eta_o = \frac{\rho g Q H}{P}$
C) $\eta_o = \frac{P}{\frac{1}{2}\rho V_1^2}$
D) $\eta_o = \frac{V_{w1}u_1}{g H}$
Overall efficiency is shaft mechanical power output divided by total incoming hydraulic water power: $\eta_o = \frac{P}{\rho g Q H}$.
Q19. In a Kaplan turbine, water flows through the runner blades in which direction?
A) Tangentially
B) Parallel to the axis of runner rotation (Axially)
C) Purely radially inward
D) Backwards through the draft tube
Kaplan and propeller turbines are strictly axial-flow machines where fluid enters and exits parallel to the shaft axis.
Q20. If a Pelton wheel bucket deflects the jet by $165^\circ$, the bucket angle ($\beta$) is:
A) $15^\circ$
B) $165^\circ$
C) $90^\circ$
D) $180^\circ$
Deflection angle $\delta = 180^\circ - \beta \implies \beta = 180^\circ - 165^\circ = 15^\circ$. (A small angle of $10^\circ - 15^\circ$ ensures the exit jet clears following buckets).
Q21. Why is the efficiency-load curve of a Kaplan turbine flatter across part-load compared to a Francis turbine?
A) Dual adjustment of guide vanes and runner blade pitch eliminates shock losses across the entire load spectrum
B) Kaplan turbines do not use water
C) Francis turbines have no guide vanes
D) Kaplan turbines operate at constant head only
By adjusting blade pitch to match wicket gate angle, relative velocity aligns tangent to the blade leading edge at all flows, avoiding incidence shock.
Q22. What happens if the runner of a reaction turbine is set too high above the tailrace level ($h_s > h_{s,max}$)?
A) Output power doubles
B) Severe cavitation occurs at runner blade exit tips, causing pitting and vibration
C) Draft tube efficiency reaches 100%
D) Penstock bursts
Excessive suction height creates high vacuum at runner exit, dropping local static pressure below water vapor pressure ($p_2 \le p_v$) and inducing violent cavitation.
Q23. In an inward radial-flow Francis runner, water flows:
A) From smaller diameter at inlet to larger diameter at outlet
B) From larger outer circumference at inlet toward the inner central axis at outlet ($D_1 > D_2$)
C) Purely axially from top to bottom
D) Backwards toward the reservoir
Inward flow enters the outer perimeter ($D_1$) and flows inward toward the center hub ($D_2 < D_1$), where centrifugal forces act in opposition to flow, stabilizing regulation.
Q24. A multi-jet Pelton wheel with 4 nozzles operating under the same head and wheel speed develops:
A) 4 times the power and 2 times the specific speed of a single-jet wheel ($N_{s,multi} = \sqrt{4} N_{s,single} = 2 N_{s,single}$)
B) 16 times the power
C) Same power as a single jet
D) Half the power
With $n$ identical jets, total power is $P = n P_1$. Specific speed is $N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{n P_1}}{H^{5/4}} = \sqrt{n} N_{s,single} = \sqrt{4} N_{s,single} = 2 N_{s,single}$.
Q25. What is the runaway speed of a hydraulic turbine?
A) Speed under normal rated load
B) Maximum speed reached when external generator load is completely disconnected while the governor/gates are fully open
C) Speed during emergency braking
D) Zero speed
Runaway speed ($\approx 1.8 \times \text{rated}$ for Pelton, $\approx 2.0-2.2 \times \text{rated}$ for Francis, $\approx 2.5-3.0 \times \text{rated}$ for Kaplan) dictates structural centrifugal stress design of the generator rotor.
Q26. In turbine unit testing, the Unit Power ($P_u$) is defined as:
A) $P_u = \frac{P}{H^{3/2}}$
B) $P_u = \frac{P}{H^{1/2}}$
C) $P_u = \frac{P}{H^2}$
D) $P_u = \frac{P}{H}$
Unit quantities represent performance under 1 meter head: Unit Speed $N_u = \frac{N}{\sqrt{H}}$, Unit Discharge $Q_u = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{H}}$, Unit Power $P_u = \frac{P}{H^{3/2}}$.
Q27. In a reaction turbine, what is the purpose of stay vanes placed upstream of the movable wicket gates?
A) Structural support of the scroll casing top and bottom covers against internal water pressure, while guiding flow smoothly into wicket gates
B) Rotating to adjust turbine speed
C) Acting as electrical insulators
D) Heating incoming water
Stay vanes are heavy fixed structural aerodynamic columns that brace the casing structure and deliver pre-aligned swirl into the wicket gates.
Q28. A Pelton turbine operates under net head $H = 200 \text{ m}$. Given $C_v = 0.98$ and $g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$, what is the velocity of the water jet ($V_1$)?
A) $31.3 \text{ m/s}$
B) $61.4 \text{ m/s}$
C) $122.8 \text{ m/s}$
D) $196.2 \text{ m/s}$
$V_1 = C_v \sqrt{2gH} = 0.98 \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 200} = 0.98 \sqrt{3924} = 0.98 \times 62.64 \approx 61.39 \text{ m/s}$.
Q29. For the Pelton turbine in Q28, what is the optimum bucket peripheral speed ($u$) if speed ratio $k_u = 0.46$?
A) $14.4 \text{ m/s}$
B) $28.8 \text{ m/s}$
C) $57.6 \text{ m/s}$
D) $92.0 \text{ m/s}$
$u = k_u \sqrt{2gH} = 0.46 \times 62.64 \approx 28.81 \text{ m/s}$.
Q30. In high-power rocket engines (e.g. SpaceX Raptor or NASA RS-25), what type of turbine is selected to drive the oxidizer and fuel turbopumps?
A) Axial-flow supersonic impulse/reaction gas turbines driven by high-pressure combustion gas
B) Hydraulic Pelton wheel using lake water
C) Archimedes screw
D) Open wind windmill
High-energy gas turbines driven by fuel-rich or oxygen-rich preburners operate at $30,000+ \text{ rpm}$ to deliver tens of megawatts of shaft power to the turbopumps.

Top Candidate Error Traps

  1. Assuming Pelton Wheels Use Draft Tubes: Pelton wheels operate in open air at atmospheric pressure ($p_1 = p_2 = p_{atm}$). A draft tube is strictly for reaction turbines (Francis/Kaplan).
  2. Mixing Specific Speed Exponents: Turbine $N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$ (uses $H^{1.25}$ and kW power). Pump $N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{Q}}{H^{3/4}}$ (uses $H^{0.75}$ and $\text{m}^3/\text{s}$).
  3. Optimum Pelton Bucket Speed: Ideal theoretical speed is $u = 0.5 V_1$. In actual practice with bucket friction, $k_u = \frac{u}{\sqrt{2gH}} \approx 0.45 - 0.46$.
  4. Kaplan vs Propeller Regulation: Propeller turbines have fixed runner blades (steep efficiency droop at off-design loads). Kaplan turbines have adjustable runner blades (flat efficiency curve across wide load range).
