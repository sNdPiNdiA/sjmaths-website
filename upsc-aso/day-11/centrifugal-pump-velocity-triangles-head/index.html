Centrifugal Pump: Velocity Triangles & Head

Day 11 of 129 Fluid Machinery & Turbomachinery Phase 1: Foundations 75-90 min Master Module

Level 1: Beginner's Glossary & Velocity Triangle Definitions Basic

Before constructing velocity triangles, here is the complete beginner's glossary of all terms, symbols, velocity vectors, and heads in centrifugal turbomachinery:

1. Impeller & Tangential Speed ($u$)

  • Impeller: The rotating wheel fitted with backward curved blades that imparts kinetic and pressure energy to the liquid.
  • Tangential Blade Velocity ($u$): Linear peripheral speed of the impeller tip at radius $R$: $$u_1 = \frac{\pi D_1 N}{60} \text{ (Inlet)}, \quad u_2 = \frac{\pi D_2 N}{60} \text{ (Outlet)} \quad [\text{m/s}]$$

2. Absolute Fluid Velocity ($V$ or $C$)

  • Definition: The actual velocity of a fluid particle relative to the stationary ground/casing.
  • Vector Relation: $\vec{V} = \vec{u} + \vec{V}_r$.
  • Angle ($\alpha$): Guide angle / absolute fluid angle made by $V$ with the tangential direction of motion ($u$).

3. Relative Fluid Velocity ($V_r$ or $W$)

  • Definition: Velocity of fluid moving across the curved blade surface as seen by an observer riding on the rotating impeller.
  • Blade Angle ($\beta$): Angle made by relative velocity $V_r$ with the tangential direction of motion ($u$). $\beta_1$ = inlet vane angle, $\beta_2$ = outlet vane angle.

4. Whirl Velocity ($V_w$ or $C_u$)

  • Definition: Tangential component of absolute velocity in the direction of blade motion ($V_w = V \cos\alpha$).
  • Physical Role: This is the only component responsible for transferring angular momentum and mechanical work to the liquid!
  • Radial Entry Rule: For shockless radial entry at inlet, $\alpha_1 = 90^\circ \implies V_{w1} = 0$.

5. Flow / Radial Velocity ($V_f$ or $C_m$)

  • Definition: Radial component of absolute velocity perpendicular to blade motion ($V_f = V \sin\alpha = V_r \sin\beta$).
  • Discharge Calculation: Controls volume flow rate through the impeller: $Q = \pi D_1 b_1 V_{f1} = \pi D_2 b_2 V_{f2}$ (where $b$ is impeller width).

6. Euler Head ($H_e$) vs Manometric Head ($H_m$)

  • Euler Head ($H_e$): Ideal theoretical head transferred by impeller: $H_e = \frac{u_2 V_{w2} - u_1 V_{w1}}{g}$ [$\text{m}$].
  • Manometric Head ($H_m$): Actual net useful head produced by pump after subtracting internal hydraulic friction and eddy losses: $H_m = H_e - h_{losses} = H_{stat} + h_f + \frac{V_d^2}{2g}$.

Level 2: Velocity Triangles & Euler's Head Derivation Intermediate

1. Velocity Vector Notation

  • Tangential Blade Speed ($u$): $u_1 = \frac{\pi D_1 N}{60}$ (inlet), $u_2 = \frac{\pi D_2 N}{60}$ (outlet).
  • Absolute Fluid Velocity ($V$): Vector sum $\vec{V} = \vec{u} + \vec{V}_r$.
  • Whirl Velocity ($V_w$): Tangential component of absolute velocity in the direction of motion ($V_w = V \cos\alpha$).
  • Flow Velocity ($V_f$): Radial component of absolute velocity responsible for discharge ($V_f = V \sin\alpha$).
  • Relative Velocity ($V_r$): Fluid velocity relative to rotating blade, tangent to blade surface at angle $\theta$ (inlet) or $\beta$ (outlet).

2. Standard Radial Inlet Condition

In standard centrifugal pump design, water enters the impeller eye radially without whirl: $$\alpha_1 = 90^\circ \implies V_{w1} = 0, \quad V_1 = V_{f1}$$

3. Euler's Head Equation ($H_e$)

Applying the angular momentum theorem (Euler's Turbomachine Equation), the work done per unit weight of fluid is:

$$ H_e = \frac{V_{w2} u_2 - V_{w1} u_1}{g} $$ $$ \text{For radial entry } (V_{w1} = 0): \quad H_e = \frac{V_{w2} u_2}{g} $$

From the outlet velocity triangle: $V_{w2} = u_2 - V_{f2}\cot\beta_2$, giving:

$$ H_e = \frac{u_2(u_2 - V_{f2}\cot\beta_2)}{g} = \frac{u_2^2}{g} - \frac{u_2 Q \cot\beta_2}{\pi D_2 b_2 g} $$

Level 3: Manometric Head, Efficiencies & Slip Factor Advanced

1. Classification of Heads

  • Static Head ($H_s$): $H_s = h_s + h_d$ (vertical lift between sump level and delivery level).
  • Frictional Head Loss ($h_f$): $h_f = h_{fs} + h_{fd} = \frac{4 f L_s V_s^2}{2g d_s} + \frac{4 f L_d V_d^2}{2g d_d}$.
  • Manometric Head ($H_m$): Net actual head developed by the pump against external system resistance:
$$ H_m = h_s + h_d + h_{fs} + h_{fd} + \frac{V_d^2}{2g} = \frac{p_d - p_s}{\rho g} + \frac{V_d^2 - V_s^2}{2g} + (z_d - z_s) $$ $$ H_m = H_e - \text{Internal Impeller & Casing Hydraulic Losses} $$

2. Pump Efficiencies

Efficiency Type Definition Formula Physical Significance
Manometric ($\eta_{mano}$) $\eta_{mano} = \frac{g H_m}{V_{w2} u_2} = \frac{H_m}{H_e}$ Measures fluid friction and eddy conversion efficiency inside impeller & casing ($70-90\%$).
Volumetric ($\eta_v$) $\eta_v = \frac{Q}{Q + \Delta Q}$ Accounts for leakage loss ($\Delta Q$) across wear rings from high-pressure delivery back to suction ($95-98\%$).
Mechanical ($\eta_m$) $\eta_m = \frac{\rho(Q + \Delta Q) V_{w2} u_2}{P_{shaft}}$ Accounts for bearing friction, gland seal packing, and disk friction ($90-95\%$).
Overall ($\eta_o$) $\eta_o = \eta_{mano} \times \eta_v \times \eta_m = \frac{\rho g Q H_m}{P_{shaft}}$ Ratio of actual useful hydraulic water horsepower to motor electrical shaft input power.

3. Slip Factor & Circulatory Flow

In real pumps with a finite number of blades ($Z$), the fluid does not perfectly follow the blade angle due to relative eddy circulation. The actual whirl velocity $V_{w2} < V_{w2\infty}$.

$$ \text{Slip Factor } (\sigma) = \frac{V_{w2}}{V_{w2\infty}} = 1 - \frac{\pi}{Z}\sin\beta_2 \quad (\text{Stodola Formula}) $$

Level 4: Vane Curvature Comparison Summary

Blade Type Outlet Angle ($\beta_2$) $H-Q$ Characteristic Power ($P-Q$) Behavior Application / Suitability
Backward Curved $\beta_2 < 90^\circ$ (typically $20^\circ - 30^\circ$) Drooping ($H$ decreases with $Q$) Self-limiting / Non-overloading Standard for all liquid pumps (Stable, high efficiency $\approx 85-90\%$).
Radial Blades $\beta_2 = 90^\circ$ ($\cot\beta_2 = 0$) Flat ($H = u_2^2/g = \text{const}$) Linear rise with $Q$ Compressor impellers, dust/slurry handling.
Forward Curved $\beta_2 > 90^\circ$ ($\cot\beta_2 < 0$) Rising ($H$ increases with $Q$) Overloading (Motor burnout risk) Low-pressure ventilation blowers (High head at low RPM).

Level 5: Aviation & Aerospace Systems Applications

  • Aircraft Fuel Boost & Transfer Pumps: Submerged centrifugal pumps located inside wing tanks deliver pressurized aviation kerosene (Jet A-1) at high volume to high-pressure engine-driven pumps.
  • Engine High-Pressure Fuel Pumps: Multi-stage centrifugal or gear/centrifugal hybrids develop over $1000 \text{ psi}$ for fuel atomization nozzles and hydraulic stator vane actuators.
  • Liquid Rocket Turbopumps (e.g., SSME, Merlin, Vikas Engine): High-speed centrifugal pumps driven by gas turbines operate at $20,000 - 35,000 \text{ rpm}$ to pump cryogenic liquid oxygen ($LOX$) and kerosene ($RP-1$) into combustion chambers at $100+ \text{ bar}$.
  • Aircraft Environmental Control Systems (ECS): High-speed centrifugal scavenge and coolant circulation pumps maintain electronic avionics temperature control.

Level 6: UPSC ASO Focus & Common Traps

  • Trap 1 (Priming): A centrifugal pump cannot self-prime if filled with air because air density is $\approx 1/800$ of water, generating negligible pressure head ($H \propto \rho$), preventing suction lift. Priming is mandatory!
  • Trap 2 (Euler Head vs Manometric Head): Euler head ($H_e = \frac{V_{w2}u_2}{g}$) is the ideal energy transferred by the impeller. Manometric head ($H_m$) is the actual head delivered to the external piping. Always $H_m = \eta_{mano} \times H_e$.
  • Trap 3 (Vane Curvature Stability): Backward curved vanes ($\beta_2 < 90^\circ$) are universally preferred because their power curve flattens at high discharge, preventing motor overload.

UPSC ASO Practice Questions (30 MCQs)

Q1. What is the fundamental expression for Euler's head ($H_e$) imparted to fluid by a centrifugal pump impeller with radial entry ($V_{w1} = 0$)?
A) $H_e = \frac{u_2^2}{2g}$
B) $H_e = \frac{V_{w2} u_2}{g}$
C) $H_e = \frac{V_{f2} u_2}{g}$
D) $H_e = \frac{V_{w2}^2 - V_{w1}^2}{2g}$
From Euler's angular momentum equation, work done per unit weight is $H_e = \frac{V_{w2}u_2 - V_{w1}u_1}{g}$. With radial entry, $V_{w1} = 0 \implies H_e = \frac{V_{w2}u_2}{g}$.
Q2. Why are backward-curved vanes ($\beta_2 < 90^\circ$) universally preferred in centrifugal pumps?
A) They produce the highest pressure head at low rotational speeds
B) They have a self-limiting power characteristic and superior hydraulic efficiency
C) They eliminate the need for priming entirely
D) They prevent cavitation completely under all suction conditions
Backward-curved vanes provide a stable drooping head-capacity curve and non-overloading power characteristics, protecting the drive motor.
Q3. Manometric efficiency ($\eta_{mano}$) of a centrifugal pump is defined as the ratio of:
A) Static head to Manometric head ($H_s / H_m$)
B) Manometric head to Euler's impeller head ($H_m / H_e = \frac{g H_m}{V_{w2} u_2}$)
C) Water power output to Shaft power input
D) Delivery pipe kinetic head to suction head
$\eta_{mano} = \frac{\text{Actual Manometric Head Developed}}{\text{Ideal Head Imparted by Impeller}} = \frac{H_m}{H_e} = \frac{g H_m}{V_{w2} u_2}$.
Q4. A centrifugal pump impeller has an outer diameter $D_2 = 0.4 \text{ m}$ and rotates at $N = 1500 \text{ rpm}$. What is its peripheral tangential velocity ($u_2$)?
A) $15.7 \text{ m/s}$
B) $31.4 \text{ m/s}$
C) $47.1 \text{ m/s}$
D) $62.8 \text{ m/s}$
$u_2 = \frac{\pi D_2 N}{60} = \frac{\pi \times 0.4 \times 1500}{60} = \frac{600 \pi}{60} = 10 \pi \approx 31.416 \text{ m/s}$.
Q5. Why must a centrifugal pump be primed before starting?
A) To cool down the electrical motor windings
B) Because air generates negligible pressure head due to its very low density ($\rho_{air} \ll \rho_{water}$)
C) To balance the mechanical dynamic unbalance of the impeller
D) To reverse the direction of blade rotation
Head developed is $H = \frac{V_{w2}u_2}{g}$, which is independent of fluid density in meters of fluid. But pressure rise is $\Delta p = \rho g H$. Because air density is ~800 times lower than water, the pressure generated is insufficient to lift water from the sump.
Q6. In a centrifugal pump with radial inlet, if $u_2 = 30 \text{ m/s}$, $V_{w2} = 20 \text{ m/s}$, and $g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$, the Euler head ($H_e$) is:
A) $30.6 \text{ m}$
B) $61.16 \text{ m}$
C) $91.7 \text{ m}$
D) $122.3 \text{ m}$
$H_e = \frac{V_{w2} u_2}{g} = \frac{20 \times 30}{9.81} = \frac{600}{9.81} \approx 61.16 \text{ m}$.
Q7. What is the primary function of a volute casing in a centrifugal pump?
A) To increase the absolute velocity of the liquid leaving the impeller
B) To gradually reduce liquid kinetic energy and convert it into static pressure head
C) To eliminate axial thrust completely
D) To increase the temperature of the pumped fluid
The spiral volute casing has a gradually expanding flow area, decelerating the high-speed discharge from the impeller and converting its kinetic energy into static pressure.
Q8. If the manometric efficiency is $80\%$ and Euler head is $50 \text{ m}$, what is the manometric head ($H_m$)?
A) $40 \text{ m}$
B) $50 \text{ m}$
C) $62.5 \text{ m}$
D) $80 \text{ m}$
$H_m = \eta_{mano} \times H_e = 0.80 \times 50 = 40 \text{ m}$.
Q9. The phenomenon of 'Slip' in centrifugal pump impellers causes:
A) Increase in whirl velocity and increase in developed head
B) Reduction in outlet whirl velocity and decrease in developed head
C) Direct cavitation at the impeller eye
D) Reversal of pump rotation
Due to relative eddy circulating in a direction opposite to impeller rotation, actual whirl velocity $V_{w2} < V_{w2\infty}$, resulting in a lower energy transfer than the theoretical infinite-blade value.
Q10. What is Stodola's formula for the slip factor ($\sigma$) in an impeller with $Z$ blades and outlet blade angle $\beta_2$?
A) $\sigma = 1 - \frac{\pi}{Z}\sin\beta_2$
B) $\sigma = 1 + \frac{\pi}{Z}\cos\beta_2$
C) $\sigma = \frac{Z}{Z + \pi}$
D) $\sigma = 1 - \frac{Z}{\pi}\tan\beta_2$
Stodola's classic slip factor correlation is $\sigma = 1 - \frac{\pi}{Z}\sin\beta_2$.
Q11. The volumetric efficiency ($\eta_v$) of a centrifugal pump accounts for:
A) Frictional losses in the bearings
B) Internal leakage of fluid from the impeller outlet back to the suction eye
C) Eddy losses in the volute casing
D) Vapour pocket formation
$\eta_v = \frac{Q}{Q + \Delta Q}$ accounts for high-pressure fluid leaking back across clearances between the impeller eye and casing wear rings.
Q12. What is the overall efficiency ($\eta_o$) of a pump delivering $Q = 0.05 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ against $H_m = 30 \text{ m}$ if shaft power input is $20 \text{ kW}$? ($\rho = 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3, g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$)
A) $65.2\%$
B) $73.6\%$
C) $82.4\%$
D) $90.5\%$
Water power $P_w = \rho g Q H_m = 1000 \times 9.81 \times 0.05 \times 30 = 14715 \text{ W} = 14.715 \text{ kW}$. $\eta_o = \frac{P_w}{P_{shaft}} = \frac{14.715}{20} = 0.73575 \approx 73.58\%$.
Q13. In a centrifugal pump with forward curved blades ($\beta_2 > 90^\circ$), the theoretical head-discharge ($H-Q$) characteristic:
A) Drops steeply with increasing discharge
B) Increases with increasing discharge
C) Remains horizontal and independent of discharge
D) First drops, then rises asymptotically
Because $\cot\beta_2 < 0$ when $\beta_2 > 90^\circ$, $H_e = A - B Q \cot\beta_2 = A + B Q |\cot\beta_2|$, so theoretical head rises with discharge.
Q14. The mechanical efficiency ($\eta_m$) of a centrifugal pump is primarily determined by:
A) Losses in suction pipe only
B) Disk friction, bearing friction, and mechanical gland seal losses
C) Thermodynamic expansion in the volute
D) Shock loss at impeller entry
$\eta_m = \frac{\text{Impeller Fluid Power}}{\text{Shaft Mechanical Power}}$ accounts for mechanical friction in bearings, packings, and viscous disk friction on the impeller outer shroud surfaces.
Q15. In an aircraft fuel booster pump, what component is often installed ahead of the main centrifugal impeller to enhance suction capability?
A) Axial-flow helical inducer
B) Convergent throttle nozzle
C) Pelton bucket wheel
D) Butterfly valve
An axial helical inducer provides a small pressure boost at very low NPSH requirements, preventing cavitation at the main centrifugal impeller eye.
Q16. The static head ($H_s$) of a pumping installation is equal to:
A) Suction lift minus delivery lift ($h_s - h_d$)
B) Vertical distance between sump liquid level and delivery tank liquid level ($h_s + h_d$)
C) Manometric head minus pipe friction
D) Velocity head at delivery pipe exit
Static head is purely the vertical elevation difference between suction sump free surface and delivery discharge free surface: $H_s = h_s + h_d$.
Q17. In the outlet velocity triangle of a centrifugal pump, the angle between relative velocity ($V_{r2}$) and the reverse direction of blade motion is:
A) Guide blade angle ($\alpha_2$)
B) Vane exit angle ($\beta_2$)
C) Inlet vane angle ($\theta$)
D) Mach angle ($\mu$)
$\beta_2$ is the impeller blade angle at the exit measured with respect to the tangent to the impeller periphery.
Q18. If a pump operates with discharge $Q = 0.1 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$, delivery pipe diameter $0.2 \text{ m}$, what is the exit velocity head ($\frac{V_d^2}{2g}$)? ($g = 9.81$)
A) $0.256 \text{ m}$
B) $0.516 \text{ m}$
C) $1.032 \text{ m}$
D) $2.050 \text{ m}$
Area $A_d = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.2^2) = 0.031416 \text{ m}^2$. Velocity $V_d = \frac{0.1}{0.031416} = 3.183 \text{ m/s}$. Velocity head = $\frac{3.183^2}{2 \times 9.81} = \frac{10.13}{19.62} \approx 0.516 \text{ m}$.
Q19. In a multi-stage centrifugal pump, impellers are mounted in series on the same shaft to achieve:
A) High discharge at low head
B) High total manometric head at constant discharge
C) Zero shaft power consumption
D) Inward axial flow
Staging impellers in series sums their individual head capacities ($H_{total} = n \times H_{stage}$) while maintaining the same mass flow rate $Q$.
Q20. A double-suction impeller in a centrifugal pump is primarily utilized to:
A) Double the delivery head
B) Handle large flow discharge while eliminating net axial hydraulic thrust
C) Operate without an external motor drive
D) Replace the delivery non-return valve
Liquid enters the impeller symmetrically from both sides, doubling suction flow capacity and neutralizing opposing axial suction forces.
Q21. The pressure rise inside the impeller alone is given by which component of the Bernoulli-Euler head equation?
A) $\frac{V_2^2 - V_1^2}{2g}$
B) $\frac{u_2^2 - u_1^2}{2g} + \frac{V_{r1}^2 - V_{r2}^2}{2g}$
C) $\frac{u_2^2}{g}$ only
D) $\frac{V_{w2}^2}{2g}$
Static pressure head rise across the impeller consists of centrifugal head rise ($\frac{u_2^2 - u_1^2}{2g}$) and relative diffusion head rise ($\frac{V_{r1}^2 - V_{r2}^2}{2g}$).
Q22. If the outlet flow velocity $V_{f2}$ is kept constant across all radii, the impeller passage width $b$ must vary with radius $r$ as:
A) $b \propto r$
B) $b \propto \frac{1}{r}$
C) $b \propto r^2$
D) $b = \text{constant}$
Flow rate $Q = \pi D b V_f = 2\pi r b V_f$. For constant $Q$ and $V_f$, $r \cdot b = \text{constant} \implies b \propto \frac{1}{r}$ (impeller narrows toward the periphery).
Q23. If the rotational speed of a centrifugal pump is doubled ($N_2 = 2 N_1$), the head developed $H$ increases by a factor of:
A) $2$
B) $4$
C) $8$
D) $16$
From pump affinity laws, $H \propto N^2$. Doubling the speed increases head by $2^2 = 4$ times.
Q24. If the rotational speed of a centrifugal pump is doubled ($N_2 = 2 N_1$), the power required $P$ increases by a factor of:
A) $2$
B) $4$
C) $8$
D) $16$
From affinity laws, $P = \rho g Q H \propto (N)(N^2) = N^3$. Doubling the speed increases power requirement by $2^3 = 8$ times.
Q25. What type of valve is fitted at the bottom of the suction pipe of a centrifugal pump to retain liquid during shut-down?
A) Gate valve
B) Foot valve with strainer (one-way non-return valve)
C) Pressure relief valve
D) Needle throttle valve
A foot valve is a spring/gravity loaded non-return valve that allows upward flow into the pump during operation and holds the prime when the pump stops.
Q26. If the delivery valve of a centrifugal pump is fully closed, the power consumed by the drive motor:
A) Is maximum (overloading)
B) Is minimum (shut-off power)
C) Drops to exactly zero
D) Causes motor to reverse
For backward-curved impellers, power increases with discharge. At $Q = 0$ (shut-off), power is minimum (only disk friction and churn losses). Hence centrifugal pumps are always started with the delivery valve closed!
Q27. Why are centrifugal pumps started with the delivery valve closed, whereas reciprocating pumps are started with the delivery valve open?
A) Starting centrifugal pumps closed minimizes starting current; starting reciprocating pumps closed causes dangerous over-pressurization
B) Reciprocating pumps cannot produce suction lift
C) Centrifugal pumps generate vacuum when closed
D) Both pumps operate identically
Centrifugal pump starting power is minimum at $Q = 0$. Positive displacement reciprocating pumps have a fixed displacement per stroke; closing the delivery valve causes infinite pressure buildup and pipe rupture.
Q28. What is the effect of increasing the number of impeller blades ($Z$) in a centrifugal pump?
A) Decreases slip and increases hydraulic friction losses
B) Increases slip and decreases friction
C) Has no effect on slip factor
D) Eliminates blade wear
More blades guide the fluid better ($\sigma \to 1$), reducing slip, but increase wetted surface area and skin friction losses. Standard impellers use $6-12$ blades as an optimal trade-off.
Q29. A pump with manometric efficiency $75\%$ operates against a manometric head of $45 \text{ m}$. If the peripheral speed $u_2 = 30 \text{ m/s}$, the outlet whirl velocity $V_{w2}$ is: ($g = 10 \text{ m/s}^2$)
A) $15 \text{ m/s}$
B) $20 \text{ m/s}$
C) $25 \text{ m/s}$
D) $30 \text{ m/s}$
$\eta_{mano} = \frac{g H_m}{V_{w2} u_2} \implies 0.75 = \frac{10 \times 45}{V_{w2} \times 30} = \frac{450}{30 V_{w2}} = \frac{15}{V_{w2}} \implies V_{w2} = \frac{15}{0.75} = 20 \text{ m/s}$.
Q30. In liquid propellant rocket engines, what type of pump is universally selected for fuel and oxidizer feed systems?
A) Reciprocating piston pump
B) High-speed centrifugal turbopump with inducer
C) Peristaltic hose pump
D) Rotary vane pump
High-speed centrifugal pumps driven by gas turbines offer the highest power-to-weight ratio and continuous non-pulsating flow required for rocket combustion chambers.

Top Candidate Error Traps

  1. Confusing Euler Head ($H_e$) with Manometric Head ($H_m$): $H_e = \frac{V_{w2}u_2}{g}$ is the theoretical work done by the impeller. $H_m$ is the actual net external head produced ($H_m = \eta_{mano} H_e$).
  2. Forgetting Radial Inlet Implication: "Radial inlet" strictly implies $\alpha_1 = 90^\circ$ and $V_{w1} = 0$, which simplifies Euler's equation to $H_e = \frac{V_{w2}u_2}{g}$.
  3. Misinterpreting Starting Valve Procedures: Centrifugal pumps are started with the delivery valve closed to minimize motor starting current. Reciprocating pumps must be started open.
  4. Slip Factor Effect on Whirl Velocity: Slip reduces the effective whirl velocity ($V_{w2} < V_{w2\infty}$), reducing the head developed compared to theoretical one-dimensional blade theory.
