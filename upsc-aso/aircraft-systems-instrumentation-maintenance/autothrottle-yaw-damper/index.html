Automatic landing (concept)

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: Dual/Triple Flight Control Computers (FCC), radio altimeters, and multi-channel ILS receivers interact to execute autoland.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Aircraft dispatch under CAT III conditions strictly requires serviceable redundant autopilot, autothrottle, and dual radio altimeter systems.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Governed strictly by DGCA CAR Section 8 Series S and ICAO Annex 10 (Aeronautical Telecommunications) Volume I.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: ASOs analyze FDR parameters like pitch rate, radio altitude decay, and control surface deflections during hard landing incidents.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

Automatic landing is a fully automated flight control mode where the aircraft guides itself from approach down to touchdown and rollout without manual pilot intervention. Governed by control law dynamics: $\mathbf{u}(t) = -K \mathbf{x}(t)$.
  • Physical Meaning: Closes the guidance loop by coupling avionics directly to localizer, glideslope, and radar altimeter sensors.
  • Governing Law: Multi-input multi-output (MIMO) state-space feedback control governing pitch, roll, yaw, and throttle commands.
  • Continuum Boundary: Operates from approach gate (typically 1500 ft AGL) through flare maneuver, touchdown, and runway deceleration.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $h$ = Radio altitude measured by radar altimeter ($m$)
  • $\theta$ = Pitch angle of the aircraft airframe ($rad$ or $deg$)
  • $\delta_e$ = Elevator control surface deflection angle ($rad$)
  • $V_{as}$ = True airspeed relative to ambient air mass ($m/s$)
  • $K_{c}$ = Closed-loop autopilot feedback gain matrix (dimensionless)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. Sensor Data Fusion: Inertial reference systems, radio altimeters, and ILS/GPS receivers continuously stream position and velocity data to flight computers.
2. Guidance and Trajectory Generation: The flight guidance computer computes error vectors comparing aircraft position against the optimal 3-degree glidepath.
3. Actuator Command Execution: Servo actuators drive control surfaces and autothrottles to eliminate tracking errors while compensating for wind shear gusts.
4. Flare and Touchdown Execution: At 50 feet AGL, the flare law initiates a pitch-up maneuver to reduce sink rate below $2\,ft/s$ at main gear contact.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Longitudinal State-Space Model

Express aircraft longitudinal perturbation dynamics in matrix form.

$$\dot{\mathbf{x}} = A\mathbf{x} + B\mathbf{u}$$
Step 2: ILS Beam Error Formulation

Define vertical displacement error $z_e$ relative to the electronic glideslope beam.

$$z_e = h - x\tan(\theta_{gs})$$
Step 3: Optimal Control Law Synthesis

Minimize quadratic cost function J balancing state error and control effort.

$$J = \int_{0}^{\infty} (\mathbf{x}^T Q \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{u}^T R \mathbf{u}) dt$$
Step 4: Final Autopilot Law

Derive the deterministic full-state feedback gain matrix for autoland execution.

$$\mathbf{u}^* = -R^{-1} B^T P \mathbf{x}$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Dimensional homogeneity requires tracking length $[L]$, mass $[M]$, time $[T]$, and angle $[\theta]$ across all control loops.
  • SI Unit: Vertical speed in $m/s$, radio altitude in $m$, control deflection in $rad$, and feedback gains in appropriate reciprocal units.
  • CGS Unit: Centimeters and seconds utilized in localized high-precision laboratory servo-valve test rigs.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Altitude is measured in feet ($ft$), sink rate in feet per minute ($fpm$), and airspeed in knots ($kts$).

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 8 Series S mandates strict approval procedures for low-visibility operations (LVO) and autoland capabilities.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: Relies on uninterruptible bus power, triplex hydraulic actuators, and fault-tolerant inertial reference systems (IRUs).
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: British Airways Flight 038 (B777, 2008) experienced dual engine power loss on final approach due to fuel icing, highlighting autoland vulnerability to thrust loss.
  • Root Safety Cause: Inadequate fuel-oil heat exchanger design caused ice crystal buildup, crippling manual and automatic energy management near touchdown.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: CAT IIIc Zero RVR Autoland Operational Status

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming CAT IIIc operations with zero decision height and zero RVR are widely implemented across Indian civil airports today.

✅ UPSC Truth: While technically defined by ICAO, CAT IIIc is rarely authorized in actual airline operations due to ground movement and taxiway guidance limitations.

Trap 2: Flare Initiation Mechanism

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing the flare maneuver during autoland is initiated using barometric altitude sensing.

✅ UPSC Truth: Flare initiation relies exclusively on high-precision radio altimeter data because barometric altimeters lack the required resolution near the ground.

Trap 3: Fail-Passive vs Fail-Operational Autoland

❌ Examiner Trap: Confusing fail-passive systems with fail-operational systems during low visibility approaches.

✅ UPSC Truth: A fail-passive system simply disconnects upon a single failure requiring manual takeover, whereas fail-operational systems complete the landing safely.

Trap 4: Autoland Wind Limits

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming aircraft can execute autolands in unlimited crosswind and tailwind conditions.

✅ UPSC Truth: Strict tailwind (typically 10 kts) and crosswind limits apply during autoland to prevent structural side-load exceedances at touchdown.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An airliner conducting a CAT III autoland approaches threshold at a true airspeed of $V_{as} = 75\,m/s$ on a standard $3^{\circ}$ glideslope. Calculate the nominal descent rate and the required pitch rate adjustment when the radio altimeter reads $h = 15\,m$, assuming a flare transition time constant of $3\,seconds$.

Given Flight Parameters:
True airspeed $V_{as} = 75\,m/s$; Glideslope angle $\gamma = 3^{\circ}$; Radio altitude $h = 15\,m$; Flare time constant $\tau = 3\,s$.
Governing Formula:
Rate of descent $ROD = V_{as} \sin(\gamma)$, and flare vertical acceleration $a_z = \frac{ROD}{\tau}$.
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Compute nominal descent rate: $ROD = 75 \times \sin(3^{\circ}) = 75 \times 0.05233 = 3.925\,m/s$. Step 2: Compute required vertical deceleration for flare: $a_z = \frac{3.925}{3} = 1.308\,m/s^2$. Step 3: Determine pitch rate command: $\dot{\theta} = \frac{a_z}{V_{as}} = \frac{1.308}{75} = 0.01744\,rad/s$.
Final Answer: Pitch rate command $\dot{\theta} = 0.01744\,rad/s$ (approx $1.0\,deg/s$).
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Accurate calculation of flare pitch rates ensures smooth touchdown loads, preventing structural gear fatigue and dynamic bounce during low-visibility landings.

Question 1 • conceptual
What primary sensor provides the absolute height reference needed for initiating the flare maneuver in an automatic landing system?
A Barometric altimeter
B Radio altimeter
C Inertial reference unit
D GPS altitude sensor
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Radio altimeters measure exact clearance above the terrain directly below the aircraft using FMCW radar, offering the high precision required for flare initiation.
Question 2 • statement
Consider the following statements regarding CAT III automatic landing systems: 1. Fail-operational autoland systems require redundant flight control computers. 2. CAT IIIc operations currently require zero decision height and zero RVR. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. Fail-operational architecture requires multi-channel redundancy, and CATIIIc is defined by zero DH and zero RVR.
Question 3 • numerical
An aircraft is descending on a $3^{\circ}$ glideslope at a true airspeed of $80\,m/s$. What is the nominal vertical descent rate?
A $2.50\,m/s$
B $4.18\,m/s$
C $5.20\,m/s$
D $6.10\,m/s$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Using $ROD = V_{as} \sin(\gamma)$, $80 \times \sin(3^{\circ}) = 80 \times 0.05233 = 4.186\,m/s$.
Question 4 • assertion-reason
Assertion (A): Autoland systems utilize Localizer and Glideslope signals for precise lateral and vertical guidance. Reason (R): ILS localizer signals are immune to terrain reflection and multipath interference.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Assertion is true as ILS provides lateral/vertical guidance. Reason is false because ILS localizer and glideslope signals are notoriously susceptible to multipath interference from terrain and taxiing aircraft.
Question 5 • conceptual
Which autopilot mode engages immediately following the flare maneuver during a full autoland sequence?
A Go-Around mode
B Rollout guidance mode
C Altitude hold mode
D Heading select mode
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Following touchdown and flare, the rollout submode engages, using nosewheel steering and rudder commands to track the runway centerline.
Question 6 • conceptual
What is the primary operational distinction between a fail-passive and a fail-operational autoland system?
A Fail-passive requires manual intervention upon single failure; fail-operational completes the landing automatically.
B Fail-passive cannot perform instrument approaches.
C Fail-operational does not require radio altimeters.
D Fail-passive operates only in CAT I conditions.
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A fail-passive system alerts the crew and disconnects upon single failure, whereas a fail-operational system continues safely to touchdown.
Question 7 • statement
Consider the requirements for low visibility operations: 1. Ground equipment must include Category III ILS transmitters. 2. Flight crew must be specially trained and certified. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both ground infrastructure (CAT III ILS/MLS/GBAS) and specialized flight crew training/currency are mandatory for low visibility operations.
Question 8 • numerical
If an aircraft approaches touchdown with a vertical descent rate of $3.6\,m/s$ and the flare computer commands a flare time constant of $2.5\,seconds$, what is the resulting initial vertical deceleration?
A $0.96\,m/s^2$
B $1.44\,m/s^2$
C $2.10\,m/s^2$
D $3.20\,m/s^2$
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Vertical deceleration $a_z = \frac{3.6}{2.5} = 1.44\,m/s^2$.
Question 9 • conceptual
During an automatic landing, what system controls engine thrust to maintain the programmed approach speed?
A Flight Management System
B Autothrottle (A/T) system
C Mach trimmer
D Air Data Computer
Solution & Technical Rationale:
The autothrottle system interfaces directly with the flight guidance computers to manage engine thrust and maintain approach speed profiles.
Question 10 • assertion-reason
Assertion (A): Radio altimeters are critical for automatic landing flare execution. Reason (R): Barometric altimeters exhibit lag and pressure error fluctuations that make them unsuitable for precision flare height control.
A Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
B Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
C A is true but R is false
D A is false but R is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct, and the unsuitability of barometric altimeters due to pressure lag directly justifies the necessity of radio altimeters.
Question 11 • conceptual
What is the typical maximum tailwind component permitted for standard commercial aircraft autoland operations?
A 5 knots
B 10 knots
C 15 knots
D 20 knots
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Standard autoland certification limits generally restrict tailwind components to a maximum of 10 knots to maintain safe flare and touchdown margins.
Question 12 • statement
Consider the following aircraft systems involved in autoland redundancy: 1. Hydraulic actuators. 2. Electrical bus power sources. 3. Pitot-static probes. Which of these must be configured with redundancy for fail-operational autoland?
A 1 and 2 only
B 2 and 3 only
C 1 and 3 only
D 1, 2 and 3
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Fail-operational autoland requires redundancy across all supporting infrastructure including hydraulics, electrical buses, and air data/pitot-static systems.
