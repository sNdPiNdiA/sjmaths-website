TRU & inverters

Textbook-grade UPSC Air Safety Officer deep-dive: Comprehensive theoretical foundations, analytical derivations, engineering reference data, DGCA/ICAO airworthiness integration, 14 practice MCQs, and timed mini-test.

How This Topic Is Useful for Air Safety Officers & Aircraft Operations
DGCA CAR & ICAO Mandate

In aviation operations and UPSC ASO assessments, this parameter is critical across 4 core operational pillars:

  • 1. Aircraft Systems & Flight Deck Monitoring: TRUs and inverters power flight management computers, primary flight displays, and engine instruments via essential DC and AC buses.
  • 2. Flight Safety & Dispatch Envelopes: Minimum Equipment List (MEL) dispatch requires dual functional TRUs for ETOPS operations to prevent total electrical bus collapse.
  • 3. DGCA CAR & ICAO Airworthiness Compliance: Compliance with DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'I', Part III and ICAO Annex 8 mandates redundant independent electrical power sources.
  • 4. Air Safety Investigation & Incident Prevention: Air Safety Officers audit bus tie contactor logs and TRU ripple voltage during electrical fire or avionics blackout investigations.

Pillar 1: Formal Technical Definition & Nomenclature

A Transformer Rectifier Unit (TRU) converts 115V AC 400Hz power to 28V DC power, while static inverters invert 28V DC power back into 115V AC 400Hz power ($V_{AC} \leftrightarrow V_{DC}$).
  • Physical Meaning: TRUs use step-down transformers and multi-pulse diode/thyristor bridge rectifiers, whereas inverters employ high-frequency solid-state switching bridges and sine-wave output filters.
  • Governing Law: Faraday's Law of Induction governs transformer step-down operation ($V_s / V_p = N_s / N_p$), and Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) theory dictates inverter synthesis.
  • Continuum Boundary: Electrical energy conversion efficiency is constrained by semiconductor thermal limits and core saturation thresholds.

Nomenclature & Symbols

  • $V_{AC}$ = Alternating Current Voltage (Volts)
  • $V_{DC}$ = Direct Current Voltage (Volts)
  • $f$ = Frequency, typically 400 Hz in aviation systems (Hertz)
  • $I_{TRU}$ = Direct Current output rating of the TRU (Amperes)
  • $THD$ = Total Harmonic Distortion ratio (Dimensionless percentage)

Pillar 2: Microscopic Physics & Continuum Mechanics

1. AC Generation & Distribution: Main engine-driven generators supply 3-phase 115V AC at 400 Hz to the primary aircraft AC buses.
2. Step-Down & Rectification: TRUs step down the 115V AC via isolation transformers and rectify it into a smooth 28V DC bus supply.
3. Inversion & Synthesis: Inverters chop 28V DC into high-frequency pulses and filter them to recreate stable 115V AC 400Hz power.
4. Bus Isolation & Redundancy: Solid-state contactors and auto-reconfiguration logic ensure uninterrupted bus power during single or dual generator failures.

Pillar 3: Mathematical Derivations & Governing Laws

Step 1: Transformer Turns Ratio

The secondary voltage depends directly on the primary voltage and the turns ratio.

$$\frac{V_{s}}{V_{p}} = \frac{N_{s}}{N_{p}}$$
Step 2: Full-Wave Rectification Voltage Output

For a three-phase bridge rectifier, the ideal mean DC output voltage is derived from peak secondary voltage.

$$V_{DC} = \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{\pi} V_{LL(rms)} - V_{drop}$$
Step 3: Inverter Output Synthesis via PWM

The synthesized fundamental AC output voltage is modulated by the DC input and modulation index ($m_a$).

$$V_{AC(rms)} = \frac{m_a}{\sqrt{2}} V_{DC}$$
Step 4: Total Harmonic Distortion (THD)

Power quality is evaluated by summing the harmonic RMS components relative to the fundamental component.

$$THD = \frac{\sqrt{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} V_{n}^{rms}}}{V_{1}^{rms}} \times 100\%$$

Pillar 4: Dimensional Analysis, SI/CGS Units & Conversion Factors

  • Fundamental Dimensions: Electrical power and voltage utilize dimensions $[M L^2 T^{-3} I^{-1}]$ for voltage and $[I T]$ for charge.
  • SI Unit: Volts (V) for electric potential, Amperes (A) for current, and Hertz (Hz) for frequency ($s^{-1}$).
  • CGS Unit: Statvolts and statamperes within the electrostatic and electromagnetic CGS frameworks.
  • Aeronautical & Imperial Equivalents: Kilovolt-Amperes (kVA) for power rating, pounds per square inch (psi) for cooling airflow pressure drop, and degrees Celsius ($^{\circ}C$) for thermal limits.

Pillar 6: Aircraft Airworthiness, Systems & DGCA CAR / ICAO Compliance

  • Regulatory Standards (DGCA CAR & ICAO): DGCA CAR Section 2 and ICAO Annex 8 require multi-engine aircraft to maintain safe electrical power following any single generator or TRU failure.
  • Critical Aircraft Systems: TRU and inverter failures threaten flight-critical EFIS screens, autopilot servos, stall warning computers, and fuel quantity indicators.
  • Aviation Incident Case Study: In Swissair Flight 111 (MD-11), in-flight arcing originating from poorly regulated non-essential bus power propagation led to total electrical failure, highlighting strict TRU circuit protection needs.

Pillar 7: High-Frequency UPSC Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: TRU Frequency Conversion

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming TRUs convert 50Hz/60Hz ground power into 400Hz AC power.

✅ UPSC Truth: TRUs convert AC to DC. Static inverters or frequency converters handle DC-to-AC or frequency transformation.

Trap 2: Inverter Input Source

❌ Examiner Trap: Claiming static inverters draw their primary power directly from the 3-phase variable frequency generator bus.

✅ UPSC Truth: Static inverters primarily draw power from the 28V DC essential or battery bus to supply emergency AC instruments.

Trap 3: TRU Ripple Voltage Limit

❌ Examiner Trap: Believing a TRU produces completely flat DC voltage without any AC ripple component.

✅ UPSC Truth: Rectification produces a small AC ripple voltage superimposed on the DC, limited strictly by design specifications to prevent avionics noise.

Trap 4: ETOPS Electrical Redundancy

❌ Examiner Trap: Assuming dispatch is permitted for ETOPS with only one operational TRU if the battery is fully charged.

✅ UPSC Truth: ETOPS dispatch mandates complete multi-TRU redundancy; losing one TRU reduces operational flight time limits significantly.

Pillar 8: Step-by-Step Worked Aircraft Numerical Problem

Realistic Aircraft Scenario

An aircraft static inverter converts a 28 VDC input into a 115 VAC 400 Hz output powering a 500 VA flight instrument load at a lagging power factor of 0.8. Calculate the DC input current drawn by the inverter assuming an overall efficiency of 85%.

Given Flight Parameters:
Output Apparent Power ($S$) = 500 VA; Input Voltage ($V_{DC}$) = 28 V; Power Factor ($PF$) = 0.8; Efficiency ($\eta$) = 85% or 0.85.
Governing Formula:
$$P_{out} = S \times PF, \quad P_{in} = \frac{P_{out}}{\eta}, \quad I_{DC} = \frac{P_{in}}{V_{DC}}$$
Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
Step 1: Calculate real output power: $P_{out} = 500 \times 0.8 = 400\text{ W}$. \nStep 2: Calculate required DC input power: $P_{in} = \frac{400}{0.85} = 470.59\text{ W}$. \nStep 3: Calculate DC input current: $I_{DC} = \frac{470.59}{28} = 16.81\text{ A}$.
Final Answer: $16.81 Amperes$
Air Safety Officer Operational Takeaway:

Knowing the exact DC input current draw ensures that battery bus circuit breakers and thermal overload relays do not trip prematurely during emergency inverter operation.

Question 1 • conceptual
What is the primary function of a Transformer Rectifier Unit (TRU) in a modern transport aircraft electrical system?
A Convert 28V DC to 115V AC 400Hz
B Convert 115V AC 400Hz to 28V DC
C Step up 28V DC to 270V DC
D Invert variable frequency AC to constant frequency AC
Solution & Technical Rationale:
A TRU uses a transformer and a rectifier bridge to convert 115V AC (typically 400 Hz from main buses) into regulated 28V DC power.
Question 2 • conceptual
Which aircraft component is specifically responsible for converting 28V DC battery power into 115V AC 400Hz for essential instruments?
A TRU
B IDG
C Static Inverter
D GCU
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Static inverters use solid-state switching circuits to convert DC power into stable AC power for emergency and essential instruments.
Question 3 • conceptual
Why is 400 Hz chosen as the standard AC electrical frequency in commercial aviation?
A It minimizes transmission line losses over long distances
B It allows significantly smaller and lighter transformers and motors
C It prevents radio frequency interference with VHF communications
D It matches standard municipal electrical grids globally
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Higher frequency (400 Hz) allows magnetic cores in transformers and motors to be much smaller and lighter, which is critical for aircraft weight savings.
Question 4 • statement-based
Consider the following statements regarding TRUs: 1. TRUs contain moving parts such as commutators. 2. TRUs utilize multi-pulse diode or thyristor bridges. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
TRUs are static solid-state devices with no moving parts. Statement 2 is correct as they use multi-pulse semiconductor diode or thyristor rectifying bridges.
Question 5 • numerical
An aircraft TRU supplies a 28V DC bus drawing 150 Amperes. If the conversion efficiency is 90% and the input voltage is 115V AC, what is the approximate input AC power?
A 4200 W
B 4667 W
C 5200 W
D 3780 W
Solution & Technical Rationale:
DC output power $P_{out} = 28 \times 150 = 4200\text{ W}$. Input power $P_{in} = P_{out} / \eta = 4200 / 0.90 = 4666.67\text{ W} \approx 4667\text{ W}$.
Question 6 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): Static inverters are protected against short-circuit conditions on the AC output bus. Reason (R): Short circuits on the inverter output cause excessive current draw, leading to thermal breakdown of switching transistors.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Static inverters feature electronic current limiting and short-circuit protection because excessive output currents would rapidly destroy semiconductor switching transistors.
Question 7 • conceptual
What happens to a typical multi-TRU aircraft electrical system if one TRU fails during flight?
A Total electrical power collapse
B Automatic bus tie closure maintains power to affected DC buses from remaining operational TRUs
C Immediate engine flameout due to FADEC power loss
D Manual deployment of the Ram Air Turbine (RAT)
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Modern aircraft electrical distribution systems use automatic bus tie contactors to redistribute DC loads across remaining healthy TRUs.
Question 8 • conceptual
What is Total Harmonic Distortion (THD) in the context of static inverter output power quality?
A The ratio of DC ripple voltage to peak AC voltage
B The measure of deviation of the AC waveform from a pure sinusoidal wave caused by harmonics
C The percentage of power lost due to core hysteresis in the inverter transformer
D The phase angle lag between voltage and current
Solution & Technical Rationale:
THD measures the presence of harmonic frequencies that distort the inverter's AC output waveform away from an ideal pure sine wave.
Question 9 • statement-based
Consider the following: 1. ETOPS dispatch rules frequently mandate multiple independent TRUs. 2. Loss of all TRUs forces reliance on battery power with strict time limitations. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. ETOPS rules require high electrical redundancy, and losing all TRUs exhausts the battery rapidly within certified emergency time limits.
Question 10 • numerical
A static inverter delivers 1 kVA of apparent power at a power factor of 0.75. What is the true real power delivered to the AC bus?
A 1000 W
B 750 W
C 1333 W
D 500 W
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Real power $P = S \times PF = 1000\text{ VA} \times 0.75 = 750\text{ W}$.
Question 11 • conceptual
What cooling method is most commonly utilized for high-capacity TRUs installed in transport category aircraft avionics bays?
A Natural convection cooling only
B Forced air cooling via dedicated avionics cooling fans or ram air
C Direct immersion in engine oil
D Cryogenic liquid nitrogen cooling
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Due to high heat dissipation from high-current rectification, TRUs require forced air cooling via avionics ventilation systems.
Question 12 • assertion-reasoning
Assertion (A): TRU failure warnings are displayed on the Electronic Flight Instrument System (EFIS) or EICAS. Reason (R): TRUs are unmonitored passive components that do not interface with aircraft data concentrator units.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Assertion (A) is true as TRU status is continuously monitored and displayed on EICAS/ECAM. Reason (R) is false because TRUs are actively monitored via voltage and current sensors.
Question 13 • conceptual
Which semiconductor devices are predominantly used in modern static inverter switching bridges to achieve high switching frequencies and efficiency?
A Carbon-zinc galvanic cells
B Vacuum tube tetrodes
C Insulated Gate Bipolar Transistors (IGBTs) and MOSFETs
D Mercury-arc rectifiers
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Modern inverters use high-speed solid-state switching devices such as IGBTs and power MOSFETs to execute Pulse Width Modulation efficiently.
Question 14 • statement-based
Consider the following regarding static inverter output filters: 1. They remove high-frequency switching harmonics. 2. They ensure the output voltage meets stringent aviation sinusoidal quality standards. Which statements are correct?
A 1 only
B 2 only
C Both 1 and 2
D Neither 1 nor 2
Solution & Technical Rationale:
Both statements are correct. Inverter output LC filters smooth out PWM chopped square waves into clean, low-distortion sinusoidal AC voltage.

What causes the ripple voltage observed at the output of a TRU?
A Mechanical vibration of the transformer core
B Imperfect smoothing of rectified AC pulses
C Battery discharging across the bus
D Excessive cooling fan rpm
Rationale:
Rectification of AC sinusoidal waves produces pulsating DC, which filter circuits smooth out, leaving a small residual ripple voltage.
Question 8 of 10 +3 / -1
Why are IGBTs preferred over older vacuum tubes or basic transistors in static inverters?
A They operate at high temperatures without cooling
B They offer high-speed switching capability with low conduction losses
C They generate mechanical torque directly
D They require zero input control voltage
Rationale:
IGBTs combine high input impedance of MOSFETs with low saturation voltage of bipolar transistors, yielding high efficiency in PWM circuits.
Question 9 of 10 +3 / -1
What is the consequence of exceeding a TRU's maximum continuous DC current rating?
A Immediate over-frequency warning on EICAS
B Overheating of semiconductor diode junctions and thermal shutdown
C Reversal of the AC phase sequence
D Automatic engine shutdown
Rationale:
Excessive current causes thermal overload in semiconductor junctions, risking thermal runaway and diode failure.
Question 10 of 10 +3 / -1
During an Air Safety Officer's electrical incident investigation, what indicates an internal TRU diode short-circuit failure?
A Increased AC ripple voltage and possible input circuit breaker tripping
B Complete absence of cabin illumination
C Zero fuel flow indication on all engines
D Uncontrolled rudder hardover
Rationale:
A shorted diode in a TRU bridge alters rectification symmetry, sharply increasing ripple voltage and often tripping feeder breakers.
