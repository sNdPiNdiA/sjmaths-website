Subtopic 1 of 6

Engineering Drawings & Geometric Dimensioning

ISO 128 / ASME Y14.5

1. Formal Technical Definition

An engineering drawing is a standardized graphic language governed by ISO and ASME standards that unequivocally communicates physical geometry, dimensions, tolerances, material specifications, and surface finish requirements for the manufacture, inspection, and airworthiness certification of aircraft components.

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

Engineering drawings rely on Orthographic Projection (First Angle in ISO/European practice, Third Angle in ANSI/US practice) to project 3D spatial geometry onto 2D orthogonal planes (Front, Top, Side views). At the continuum level, drawings translate the macroscopic structural stress requirements into geometric boundaries. Sectional views reveal internal cavities (such as hydraulic fluid galleries or turbine blade cooling channels) without violating solid boundary visualization. Dimensioning systems define feature locations relative to datums—the theoretical exact planes, axes, or points from which dimensional variations are controlled.

3. Mathematical Scaling & Projection Equations

The relationship between drawing dimension ($D_d$) and actual part dimension ($D_a$) is governed by the drawing scale factor ($S$):

$$S = \frac{D_d}{D_a}$$

Where $S = 1:1$ represents full scale, $S > 1$ represents enlargement (e.g., $5:1$ for miniature instrument gears), and $S < 1$ represents reduction (e.g., $1:50$ for fuselage general arrangement). Geometric Tolerancing (GD&T) positional error vector ($\delta P$) for a cylindrical feature is calculated as:

$$\delta P = \sqrt{(\Delta X)^2 + (\Delta Y)^2} \le \text{Tolerance Zone Diameter } (T)$$

Variables: $\Delta X, \Delta Y$ = Cartesian coordinate deviations from true position datum; $T$ = Diameter of cylindrical tolerance zone.

Fundamental Dimensions

[L] (Length / Linear Dimension)

Units & Conversions

SI: Millimetres ($\text{mm}$)
Imperial: Inches ($\text{in}$)
Conversion: $1 \text{ in} = 25.4 \text{ mm}$ exactly.

5. Engineering Reference Data Table

Drawing Line Type Standard Thickness Aeronautical Application
Continuous Thick (Type A) 0.5mm – 0.7mm Visible outlines of aircraft structural ribs and spars
Continuous Thin (Type B) 0.18mm – 0.25mm Dimension lines, leader lines, hatching
Dashed Thin (Type E/F) 0.25mm – 0.35mm Hidden edges (internal hydraulic routing)
Chain Thin (Type G) 0.25mm – 0.35mm Centre lines, axes of symmetry (engine crankshafts)

6. Aircraft Application & DGCA Air Safety Connection

Air Safety Officers inspect engineering drawings during major aircraft structural repairs (Form 337 equivalents under DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'M'). Misinterpretation of third-angle vs. first-angle projection symbols can lead to incorrect fabrication of replacement brackets, resulting in flight control jamming. Case Study Link: Structural failure investigations often trace back to drawing revisions where modified rivet edge-distances were omitted from the shop floor blueprint.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions

Trap: Assuming First Angle and Third Angle projections arrange views identically. In First Angle (ISO), the object is between the observer and the projection plane (Top view is placed below front view). In Third Angle (US), the projection plane is between observer and object (Top view is placed above front view). UPSC frequently tests view transposition errors.

8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem: An aircraft main landing gear trunnion pin is drawn at a scale of $1:2$. The measured length of the pin on the engineering drawing is $147.5\text{ mm}$. Calculate the actual manufactured length of the pin in SI units.

Given: Scale $S = 1:2 = 0.5$; Drawing dimension $D_d = 147.5\text{ mm}$.

Formula: $S = \frac{D_d}{D_a} \implies D_a = \frac{D_d}{S}$

Calculation: $D_a = \frac{147.5}{0.5} = 295.0\text{ mm} = 0.295\text{ m}$.

Operational Takeaway: Quality control inspectors must verify actual micrometer measurements against this baseline before granting a Certificate of Release to Service (CRS).

Subtopic 2 of 6

Fits, Tolerances & Dimensional Metrology

ISO 286

1. Formal Technical Definition

Tolerance is the permissible variation in a physical dimension. Fit refers to the mechanical relationship resulting from the intentional clearance or interference between two mating cylindrical or prismatic parts (hole and shaft) assembled in aircraft mechanical systems.

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

At the atomic and microscopic level, mating metal surfaces feature asperities (surface roughness peaks). Fits control the relative movement and load transfer between parts.

  • Clearance Fit: Hole is always larger than the shaft; permits relative motion (e.g., control cable pulleys).
  • Interference Fit: Shaft is larger than the hole; relies on elastic strain and hoop stress to lock parts together (e.g., turbine rotor blade roots in disks, bearing outer races in gearboxes).
  • Transition Fit: Overlapping tolerance zones; can result in either clearance or interference upon assembly.

3. Mathematical Derivation & Equations

Let $D_{max}, D_{min}$ be hole limits and $d_{max}, d_{min}$ be shaft limits.

Hole Tolerance ($T_h$) = $D_{max} - D_{min}$

Shaft Tolerance ($T_s$) = $d_{max} - d_{min}$

Maximum Clearance = $D_{max} - d_{min}$

Minimum Clearance = $D_{min} - d_{max}$ (Negative for interference)

Fundamental Dimensions

[L] (Micrometres, $\mu\text{m}$)

Standard Grades

ISO IT grades IT01 to IT18 (IT6 to IT11 typical for aircraft machining).

5. Engineering Reference Data Table

Fit Classification Hole / Shaft System Aero Application
H7/g6 (Running/Clearance) Hole basic / Close clearance Actuator piston rods and valve guides
H7/h6 (Locational Clearance) Zero minimum clearance Removable gear pins and structural clevis joints
H7/p6 (Interference) Press / Shrink fit Accessory gearbox drive gear bushings

6. Aircraft Application & DGCA Air Safety Connection

Incorrect fit selection leads to catastrophic fatigue failures. If a landing gear bushing has excessive clearance (loose fit), impact loads during touchdown cause severe fretting corrosion and ovalization of housing bores. Air Safety Officers verify that overhaul agencies strictly adhere to Component Maintenance Manual (CMM) fit allowances.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions

Trap: Confusing "Hole-basis" and "Shaft-basis" systems. In a hole-basis system, the lower deviation of the hole is zero (H), and the fundamental deviation of the shaft is varied to achieve the required fit. UPSC numericals often test maximum and minimum clearance calculations given upper and lower limits.

8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem: A mating hole and shaft are specified as $\varnothing 50\,H7/g6$. Given hole limits $\varnothing 50.000\text{ mm}$ to $\varnothing 50.025\text{ mm}$ and shaft limits $\varnothing 49.980\text{ mm}$ to $\varnothing 49.991\text{ mm}$, calculate the maximum and minimum clearances.

Given: $D_{max} = 50.025$, $D_{min} = 50.000$, $d_{max} = 49.991$, $d_{min} = 49.980\text{ mm}$.

Formula: $\text{Max Clearance} = D_{max} - d_{min}$; $\text{Min Clearance} = D_{min} - d_{max}$.

Calculation:

$\text{Max Clearance} = 50.025 - 49.980 = +0.045\text{ mm} = 45\,\mu\text{m}$.

$\text{Min Clearance} = 50.000 - 49.991 = +0.009\text{ mm} = 9\,\mu\text{m}$.

Operational Takeaway: Both values are positive, confirming a guaranteed clearance fit suitable for lubricated journal bearings.

Subtopic 3 of 6

Limits, Surface Finish & Tribology

ISO 4287 / ASME B46.1

1. Formal Technical Definition

Surface finish (or surface texture) comprises the micro-geometric irregularities of a solid surface, quantified by parameters such as Arithmetic Mean Roughness ($R_a$) and Maximum Peak-to-Valley Height ($R_z$), which critically govern friction, wear, sealing integrity, and fatigue crack initiation in aircraft components.

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

No machined surface is perfectly smooth. At the microscopic level, peaks (asperities) and valleys exist. Under cyclic aerodynamic and mechanical loading (e.g., turbine compressor disks or landing gear actuator struts), sharp surface valleys act as stress concentrators where micro-cracks nucleate via fatigue. Conversely, ultra-smooth surfaces can prevent proper lubricant retention. Tribological optimization requires controlled surface finishing (honing, polishing, shot peening) to balance low friction with adequate lubricant film retention.

3. Mathematical Equations for Surface Roughness

The Arithmetic Mean Roughness ($R_a$) is defined as the integral of the absolute profile deviation $y(x)$ over a evaluation length $L$:

$$R_a = \frac{1}{L} \int_{0}^{L} |y(x)| \, dx$$

Discrete approximation used in digital profilometers:

$$R_a \approx \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} |y_i|$$
Fundamental Dimensions

[L] (Length)

SI Units

Micrometres ($\mu\text{m}$) or Microinches ($\mu\text{in}$). $1\,\mu\text{in} = 0.0254\,\mu\text{m}$.

5. Engineering Reference Data Table

Machining Process Typical $R_a$ Range ($\mu\text{m}$) Aeronautical Application
Precision Turning / Boring 1.6 – 3.2 General structural brackets and housings
Grinding 0.4 – 0.8 Engine shaft journals and gear teeth flanks
Honing / Lapping 0.05 – 0.2 Hydraulic actuator cylinder bores and spool valves

6. Aircraft Application & DGCA Air Safety Connection

Hydraulic actuator piston rods require hard chrome plating finished to an extremely fine $R_a$ ($\approx 0.2\,\mu\text{m}$). If surface roughness is too high, it destroys high-pressure elastomeric seals, causing hydraulic fluid leakage and potential flight control failure. Air Safety Officers review non-destructive testing (NDT) and surface metrology logs during incident investigations involving hydraulic power loss.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions

Trap: Believing that $R_a$ provides information on the spacing of peaks or the presence of deep flaws. $R_a$ is an average; two surfaces with identical $R_a$ values can have completely different bearing area ratios if one has sharp isolated grooves and the other has rounded sinusoidal waves.

8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem: A profilometer measuring a turbine shaft journal records 5 equally spaced surface deviations from the mean line: $y_1 = +0.4\,\mu\text{m}$, $y_2 = -0.3\,\mu\text{m}$, $y_3 = +0.5\,\mu\text{m}$, $y_4 = -0.6\,\mu\text{m}$, $y_5 = +0.2\,\mu\text{m}$. Calculate the Arithmetic Mean Roughness ($R_a$).

Given: $n = 5$; $y_1 = 0.4, y_2 = -0.3, y_3 = 0.5, y_4 = -0.6, y_5 = 0.2\ (\mu\text{m})$.

Formula: $R_a = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} |y_i|$

Calculation:

$\sum |y_i| = |0.4| + |-0.3| + |0.5| + |-0.6| + |0.2| = 0.4 + 0.3 + 0.5 + 0.6 + 0.2 = 2.0\,\mu\text{m}$

$R_a = \frac{2.0}{5} = 0.4\,\mu\text{m}$.

Operational Takeaway: $R_a = 0.4\,\mu\text{m}$ complies with precision ground aviation shaft standards.

Subtopic 4 of 6

Aerospace Fasteners: Bolts, Nuts & Rivets

NAS / MS / AN Standards

1. Formal Technical Definition

Aerospace fasteners are precision-engineered mechanical joining devices (threaded bolts, self-locking nuts, and solid/blind rivets) designed to transfer shear and tensile loads across structural joints while maintaining high fatigue strength and resistance to vibrational loosening under extreme flight loads.

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

Fasteners operate under two primary modes of load transfer:

  • Shear Joints: Fasteners resist forces perpendicular to their axis (e.g., lap joints in fuselage skin).
  • Tension Joints: Fasteners resist forces parallel to their axis (e.g., engine mount bolts). Preloading (torque tightening) induces elastic tensile stress in the bolt shank, clamping the plates together so external loads do not cause joint separation or fatigue.
Solid rivets (e.g., Monel or 2017/2056 aluminium alloy) fill the hole completely via plastic deformation during bucking.

3. Mathematical Stress Equations

Shear stress ($\tau$) on a bolt shank of diameter $d$ subjected to shear force $F_s$:

$$\tau = \frac{F_s}{A_s} = \frac{4 F_s}{\pi d^2}$$

Bolt preload ($T_p$) relationship with tightening torque ($T$), nominal diameter ($d$), and torque coefficient ($K$):

$$T = K \cdot F_p \cdot d$$

Variables: $T$ = Torque ($\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$); $K$ = Nut factor (dimensionless, typically 0.15 for lubricated steel); $F_p$ = Preload force ($\text{N}$); $d$ = Nominal diameter ($\text{m}$).

Fundamental Dimensions

[M][L]⁻¹[T]⁻² (Stress: Pascals, $\text{Pa}$ or $\text{N/m}^2$)

Engineering Units

MegaPascals ($\text{MPa}$); Torque in $\text{lbf}\cdot\text{in}$ or $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$.

5. Engineering Reference Data Table

Fastener Standard Material Principal Aero Application
AN3 – AN20 Bolts Cadmium-plated Alloy Steel (4037) General primary structural airframe joints
NAS6200 Series Close Tolerance Inconel / Titanium / Alloy Steel High-fatigue areas (wing attachment fittings)
MS20470 Universal Head Rivet Aluminium Alloy 2117-T4 (Ad) Skin and stringer riveting (field universal standard)

6. Aircraft Application & DGCA Air Safety Connection

Under DGCA CAR Section 2, replacement of critical fasteners requires exact traceability to OEM part numbers. Using commercial grade bolts (e.g., Grade 5/8 automotive bolts instead of AN/NAS aerospace bolts) in flight control linkages is a major airworthiness violation. Low shear strength in non-aerospace bolts causes premature structural shear under gust loads.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions

Trap: Assuming aircraft bolts are threaded along their entire length. AN bolts feature an unthreaded grip length specifically sized to match the combined thickness of the joined plates, ensuring that shear forces act on the smooth shank rather than the stress-concentrating thread roots.

8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem: An AN6 bolt (nominal diameter $d = 3/8\text{ in} = 9.525\text{ mm}$) is subjected to a direct shear force $F_s = 12.5\text{ kN}$. Calculate the induced shear stress in the shank.

Given: $d = 9.525\times 10^{-3}\text{ m}$; $F_s = 12.5\times 10^{3}\text{ N}$.

Formula: $\tau = \frac{4 F_s}{\pi d^2}$

Calculation:

$A_s = \frac{\pi (9.525\times 10^{-3})^2}{4} = \frac{\pi (9.0725\times 10^{-5})}{4} = 7.125\times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^2$

$\tau = \frac{12.5\times 10^{3}}{7.125\times 10^{-5}} = 1.754\times 10^{8}\text{ Pa} = 175.4\text{ MPa}$.

Operational Takeaway: $175.4\text{ MPa}$ is well within the shear yield limit of alloy steel ($>400\text{ MPa}$), ensuring structural safety.

Subtopic 5 of 6

Positive Locking Devices & Safetying

FAA AC 43.13-1B

1. Formal Technical Definition

Positive locking devices are mechanical safetying mechanisms (such as split cotter pins, safety wire, self-locking prevailing torque nuts, and tab washers) designed to prevent threaded fasteners from backing off or loosening under severe airframe vibrations and cyclic thermal shocks.

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

During flight, high-frequency engine and aerodynamic vibrations generate transverse slip between mating thread turns. Without locking provisions, this relative motion overcomes thread friction, causing self-loosening.

  • Safety Wire (MS20995): Mechanically ties bolts in a dual-opposition tension loop so that if one bolt attempts to loosen, the wire is placed in tension by the adjacent bolt, arresting rotation.
  • Self-Locking Nuts (Nylon insert / Elastic stop nuts): A non-metallic or distorted metal collar exerts continuous radial clamping force on the bolt threads, creating constant frictional drag torque.

3. Mathematical Verification Equations

Total tightening torque ($T_{total}$) required for a prevailing torque locknut is the sum of elastic clamping torque ($T_c$) and prevailing torque ($T_p$) required to overcome internal collar friction:

$$T_{total} = T_c + T_p$$

Safety wire gauge diameter ($d_{wire}$) selection criteria based on bolt head drill hole diameter ($d_h$):

$$d_{wire} \le 0.8 \times d_h \quad (\text{Typically } 0.032\text{ in} \text{ or } 0.040\text{ in stainless steel})$$
Fundamental Dimensions

[L] (Wire Diameter in inches or mm)

Standard Wire Sizes

0.020", 0.032", 0.040" Annealed Corrosion-Resisting Steel (CRES).

5. Engineering Reference Data Table

Locking Device Type Max Operating Temp Primary Aviation Application
Nylon Insert Locknut (MS20365) 121°C (250°F) Cabin interior and low-temp airframe systems
All-Metal Locknut (Elastic offset) 450°C (850°F) Engine cowlings and exhaust accessory mounts
Stainless Steel Safety Wire >800°C Turbine engine fuel controls and brake assemblies

6. Aircraft Application & DGCA Air Safety Connection

Unsecured flight control linkage bolts have caused numerous fatal accidents. Under DGCA CAR Section 2, all safety wiring must be inspected by a licensed AME (Aircraft Maintenance Engineer) using the "pigtail" method (3 to 6 twists per inch) pulling in the tightening direction. Safety audit focus: Air Safety Officers verify that nylon locknuts are never reused beyond manufacturer specified torque run-downs.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions

Trap: Assuming safety wire exerts clamping force on the joint. Safety wire does not clamp the parts; its sole purpose is to mechanically prevent fastener rotation if preload is momentarily bypassed by vibration.

8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem: A self-locking castellated nut requires a standard clamping torque $T_c = 35\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$. The prevailing run-down torque measured before final seating is $T_p = 4.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$. Calculate the total torque wrench reading required for proper installation.

Given: $T_c = 35\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$; $T_p = 4.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.

Formula: $T_{total} = T_c + T_p$

Calculation: $T_{total} = 35 + 4.5 = 39.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.

Operational Takeaway: The torque wrench must be set to $39.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ so that the actual net clamping torque reaches the design requirement of $35\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.

Subtopic 6 of 6

Bearings & Gear Kinematics in Aircraft Systems

AGMA / ISO Standards

1. Formal Technical Definition

Bearings are mechanical assemblies that constrain relative motion and reduce friction between rotating shafts and static housings (using rolling elements or fluid films). Gears are Toothed machine elements that transmit power and torque synchronously between rotating axes with exact velocity ratios via continuous meshing profile curves.

2. Basic Concept & Physical Mechanism

  • Rolling Element Bearings: Ball bearings (handle radial and light axial loads) and roller bearings (cylindrical/tapered, handle heavy radial/axial loads). Contact stresses are extremely high (Hertzian contact stress), requiring vacuum-melted bearing steels (e.g., M50 or 52100).
  • Gears: Spur, helical, and bevel gears transmit power in accessory gearboxes (AGB) and helicopter main rotor transmissions. Involute tooth profiles ensure constant angular velocity ratio even if center distances shift slightly due to thermal expansion.

3. Mathematical Gear Kinematics Equations

Velocity ratio ($VR$) and gear tooth / pitch circle diameter ($d$) relationships:

$$VR = \frac{N_1}{N_2} = \frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{d_2}{d_1}$$

Where $N$ = rotational speed ($\text{RPM}$), $T$ = number of teeth, $d$ = pitch circle diameter. Bearing fatigue life (L10 life in hours):

$$L_{10h} = \frac{10^6}{60 \cdot N} \left( \frac{C}{P} \right)^p$$

Variables: $C$ = Dynamic load rating ($\text{kN}$); $P$ = Equivalent dynamic load ($\text{kN}$); $p = 3$ for ball bearings, $10/3$ for roller bearings.

Fundamental Dimensions

[T]⁻¹ (Speed: RPM); [L] (Diameter: mm)

Engineering Units

Load rating in KiloNewtons ($\text{kN}$); Module ($\text{m}$) in mm for gears.

5. Engineering Reference Data Table

Bearing / Gear Type Max Speed Parameter Aviation Application
Angular Contact Ball Bearing High ($>20,000\text{ RPM}$) Gas turbine main spool thrust positions
Spherical Roller Bearing Moderate (Heavy radial) Helicopter rotor mast supports
Spiral Bevel Gear High torque transmission Turboprop reduction gearboxes and tail rotor drives

6. Aircraft Application & DGCA Air Safety Connection

Bearing spalling or gear tooth pitting generates metal debris caught by magnetic chip detectors (MCD) in engine oil scavenge lines. Under DGCA CAR mandated maintenance schedules, MCD actuation triggers mandatory spectrometric oil analysis program (SOAP) checks. Failure to ground an aircraft upon magnetic chip accumulation has historically led to uncontained gearbox lockups and inflight engine fires.

7. UPSC Traps & Common Misconceptions

Trap: Confusing the exponents in bearing life equations ($p=3$ for balls, $p=10/3$ for rollers). UPSC numericals test whether candidates apply the correct exponent when calculating fatigue life under doubled equivalent loads.

8. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical

Problem: A turbine accessory gearbox spur gear has $T_1 = 20$ teeth and rotates at $N_1 = 6000\text{ RPM}$. It drives a larger gear with $T_2 = 50$ teeth. Calculate the output rotational speed ($N_2$) and velocity ratio.

Given: $T_1 = 20$, $T_2 = 50$, $N_1 = 6000\text{ RPM}$.

Formula: $\frac{N_1}{N_2} = \frac{T_2}{T_1} \implies N_2 = N_1 \left(\frac{T_1}{T_2}\right)$

Calculation:

$N_2 = 6000 \times \left(\frac{20}{50}\right) = 6000 \times 0.4 = 2400\text{ RPM}$.

Velocity Ratio $VR = \frac{50}{20} = 2.5:1$.

Operational Takeaway: The output speed steps down to $2400\text{ RPM}$, increasing torque proportionally by a factor of $2.5$ to drive heavy fuel pump accessories.

Case Study 01

Improper Fastener Substitution & Flight Control Jamming

Incident Summary: A twin-turboprop commuter aircraft experienced severe control wheel stiffness during approach. Post-incident inspection revealed that during maintenance on the elevator bellcrank assembly, an unapproved commercial hex bolt (Grade 5) was installed in place of an NAS high-shear bolt. The unthreaded shank length was too short, allowing threads to bear directly against the aluminum clevis hole. Under cyclic flight loads, thread galling and severe wear caused the hole to elongate and jam the control cable pulley.

DGCA Air Safety Officer Examination Linkage

Under DGCA CAR Section 2, Series 'I' (Airworthiness Requirements relating to Modifications and Repairs), maintenance personnel must maintain strict traceability of standard parts. Air Safety Officers investigating incidents must verify part conformity against the Illustrated Parts Catalog (IPC) and ensure that grip lengths are calculated correctly to prevent thread shear.

Case Study 02

Bearing Spalling and Accessory Gearbox Failure

Incident Summary: A turbofan-powered transport aircraft suffered an inflight engine shutdown due to accessory gearbox seizure. Investigation revealed that the high-pressure fuel pump drive bearing experienced subsurface fatigue flaking (spalling) caused by excessive operating hours beyond the overhaul limit. Although magnetic chip detectors (MCD) had illuminated twice prior to the flight, line maintenance personnel cleared the fault without spectrometric oil analysis, assuming it was "normal carbon fuzz."

DGCA CAR Compliance & Safety Takeaway

CAR Section 2 mandates that any metallic debris collected on an MCD must be subjected to metallurgical inspection. Air Safety Officers must audit maintenance logbooks to ensure operators never dismiss MCD activation without thorough SOAP laboratory analysis.

Day 77 High-Yield Revision Summary

Review these core takeaways before entering the UPSC ASO examination hall:

1. Engineering Drawing Standards

  • First Angle: ISO standard; object between observer and projection plane (Top view below front).
  • Third Angle: ANSI standard; projection plane between observer and object (Top view above front).
  • Line Types: Continuous thick (visible outlines), Dashed thin (hidden edges), Chain thin (centre lines).

2. Fits & Tolerances

  • Clearance Fit: Hole lower limit $\ge$ Shaft upper limit (guaranteed gap).
  • Interference Fit: Hole upper limit $\le$ Shaft lower limit (press/shrink fit).
  • Hole Basis (H): Lower deviation of hole is zero; shaft tolerances varied for fit.

3. Surface Finish & Fasteners

  • $R_a$: Arithmetic mean roughness in micrometres ($\mu\text{m}$). Vital for seal longevity.
  • AN Bolts: Unthreaded grip length must match joined plate thickness to keep shear stress off threads.
  • Torque Formula: $T = K \cdot F_p \cdot d$.

4. Locking Devices, Bearings & Gears

  • Safety Wire: Prevents backup rotation; does not provide joint clamping force.
  • Bearing Life: $L_{10h}$ formula uses exponent $p=3$ for balls, $p=10/3$ for rollers.
  • Gear Ratio: $\frac{N_1}{N_2} = \frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{d_2}{d_1}$.

UPSC Air Safety Officer Interview Corner

Prepare for the personality test viva voce with expert model answers:

Q: As an Air Safety Officer inspecting an accident wreckage, how would you determine if an unapproved fastener contributed to structural failure? ▼

Model Answer: "I would recover fastener fragments from the fractured joint and inspect head markings (AN, NAS, MS codes vs unmarked commercial hardware). Next, I would verify metallurgical composition via positive material identification (PMI) and examine the shear lips and thread engagement zones under a stereomicroscope. If commercial bolts with inappropriate grip lengths were used, the resulting thread bearing and lack of shear shank resistance would appear as accelerated localized shear deformation and ovalization of structural bolt holes, constituting a clear violation of DGCA CAR Section 2 airworthiness standards."

Q: Explain the tribological importance of surface roughness ($R_a$) in aircraft hydraulic actuator cylinder bores. ▼

Model Answer: "Hydraulic actuators operate under pressures exceeding 3000 psi. The cylinder bore and piston rod surface finish ($R_a \approx 0.05 - 0.2\,\mu\text{m}$) must be precisely controlled. If the surface is too rough, the peaks act as microscopic cutting tools that abrade elastomeric O-rings and backup rings, causing hydraulic fluid leakage and actuator depressurization. Conversely, if the surface is mirror-smooth, it fails to retain a microscopic lubricating oil film, leading to adhesive wear and scoring. Precision honing ensures an optimized plateau texture for both low friction and fluid retention."
