Depressurisation - engine start sequence

Day 58 of 129 Aircraft Systems and Avionics Phase 3: Systems and Applied 60-90 min Master Cycle

Step 1: Understand

1. Pneumatic Starting Mechanism: Modern turbofan engines use an Air Turbine Starter (ATS) mounted on the accessory gearbox. The ATS requires high-velocity, high-mass-flow bleed air to spin the high-pressure compressor (N2/N3 spool) up to its self-sustaining speed before fuel and ignition are introduced.

2. The Need for Pack Isolation: The air conditioning packs are the largest consumers of bleed air. To guarantee that the pneumatic manifold maintains sufficient pressure ($\ge 30 \text{ psi}$) for the ATS, the Pack Flow Control Valves (FCVs) must be closed during the start sequence. This temporary shutdown of cabin air supply is known as pack shedding or pack isolation.

3. Cabin Pressure Transient Management: When packs are isolated, the inflow of fresh air ceases, which can cause a sudden change in cabin pressure (a "pressure bump"). To prevent passenger discomfort, the Cabin Pressure Control System (CPCS) coordinates with the engine start sequence to modulate the Outflow Valve (OFV), maintaining a stable cabin altitude during the temporary depressurisation phase.

Step 2: Learn

Starter Torque Equation: $$\tau_{starter} = I_{spool} \cdot \alpha + \tau_{aerodynamic\_drag}$$ Where: $I_{spool}$ is the mass moment of inertia of the N2 spool, $\alpha$ is the angular acceleration, and $\tau_{aerodynamic\_drag}$ is the drag opposing rotation (which increases quadratically with N2 speed).

Minimum Duct Pressure Requirement: $$P_{duct} \ge P_{min} \approx 30 \text{ psig} \quad (\text{at sea level})$$

Key Components in the Start/Depressurisation Loop

  • Air Turbine Starter (ATS): Converts pneumatic energy from bleed air into mechanical energy to drive the N2 accessory gearbox.
  • Starter Valve: Electrically controlled, pneumatically operated valve that opens to admit bleed air to the ATS. Has a manual override wrench flat.
  • Pack Flow Control Valves (FCVs): Spring-loaded closed, pneumatically operated valves that shut off bleed air to the A/C packs during start.
  • Outflow Valve (OFV): Modulates position during pack isolation to prevent rapid cabin rate of climb or descent.

Step 3: Memorize

Mnemonic: S.P.I.T. (Engine Start Sequence)

  • S - Starter Valve Opens (Bleed air enters ATS, N2 rotates)
  • P - Packs Shed/Close (Isolates pneumatic duct to maintain pressure >30 psi)
  • I - Ignition & Fuel ON (Typically at 15% - 25% N2)
  • T - Termination / Cutout (Starter valve closes automatically at 45% - 55% N2)

High-Yield Memory Anchors

  • Starter Cutout Speed: Typically 50% N2 (range: 45% - 55%). Above this speed, the engine is self-sustaining and starter centrifugal clutches disengage.
  • Pack Re-engagement Delay: After starter cutout, packs re-open after a 10-second delay to prevent thermal shock and cabin pressure surges.
  • Crossbleed Target: Minimum duct pressure before initiating a crossbleed start is 30 psi (with donor engine thrust advanced).
ASO Exam Tip: If the starter valve fails to close after starter cutout speed (50% N2), the "ENG STARTER VALVE" warning activates. The crew must immediately shut down the engine or isolate the pneumatic source to prevent starter overspeed and catastrophic disintegration.
Pneumatic Priority Rule: APU Bleed air always takes precedence over Engine Bleed air. Check valves prevent engine bleed air from back-pressuring the APU.

Step 4: Aviation Application

1. Airbus A320 Automatic Pack Shedding

On the A320, when the ENG START selector is turned to IGN/START, the FADEC signals the Pack Flow Control Valves to close. This isolates the cabin completely. The Outflow Valve is driven slightly closed to "trap" cabin pressure, minimizing the pressure drop. Once the starter cutout speed (50% N2) is reached, the starter valve closes, and the packs automatically ramp back open after a 10-second delay.

2. Boeing 737 Manual Pack Control (Non-Shedding Variants)

On legacy B737 aircraft, the auto-pack-trip system does not shed packs during a normal ground start. The pilot checklist explicitly mandates: "PACKS - OFF" prior to turning the Engine Start Switch to GRD. If the pilot fails to turn the packs OFF, the duct pressure will drop below 20 psi, leading to a Hung Start (N2 stagnates below self-sustaining speed) or a Hot Start due to insufficient cooling airflow through the engine core.

3. Crossbleed Start Operational Hazards

During a crossbleed start, the donor engine's thrust is increased to achieve 30 psi. If the isolation valve fails to open, the receiving engine will not receive air, while the donor engine's bleed air pressure will spike. This requires immediate thrust reduction to prevent duct overpressure and structural damage to the pneumatic manifold.

Step 5: UPSC Focus

  • Typical Question Trap: The exam may state that packs are closed during start to "prevent fumes from entering the cabin." While this is a secondary benefit, the primary aerodynamic/system reason is to prevent pneumatic duct pressure drops and ensure sufficient starter torque.
  • System Interlocks: Understand the role of the Dual-Purpose Valve / Isolation Valve. During engine start, the isolation valve automatically opens if the APU is the bleed source, but must be manually opened for crossbleed starts.
  • Failure Modes:
    • Starter Valve fails to open: No N2 rotation.
    • Starter Valve fails to close: Risk of ATS overspeed and structural breakup.
    • Pack Valve fails to close: Low duct pressure, resulting in a Hung Start.

Practice MCQs

Q1. What is the primary reason for isolating the air conditioning packs during the jet engine pneumatic start sequence?
A) To prevent toxic engine oil fumes from entering the cabin during start
B) To conserve electrical power for the high-energy ignition exciters
C) To ensure maximum bleed air pressure and mass flow are directed to the Air Turbine Starter
D) To allow the cabin outflow valves to fully close and pre-pressurize the cabin
Correct Answer: C
The primary reason is pneumatic. Air conditioning packs consume a massive volume of bleed air. Isolating them ensures that 100% of the available bleed air pressure (minimum 30 psi) is directed to the Air Turbine Starter (ATS) to spin the N2 spool.
Q2. During a crossbleed engine start, what action must the flight crew perform regarding the operating (donor) engine?
A) Retard the throttle to idle to prevent pneumatic manifold overpressure
B) Advance the throttle to increase N2/thrust to achieve a minimum duct pressure of 30 psi
C) Switch off the generator of the operating engine to reduce load
D) Select the engine bleed switch of the operating engine to OFF
Correct Answer: B
To perform a crossbleed start, the donor engine's thrust must be advanced (typically to 60-80% N2) to generate sufficient bleed air pressure ($\ge 30 \text{ psi}$) in the pneumatic manifold for the receiving engine's starter.
Q3. At what approximate N2 speed does the Starter Valve automatically close (starter cutout) during a normal engine start?
A) 15% to 20% N2
B) 45% to 55% N2
C) 65% to 70% N2
D) Immediately upon fuel flow initiation
Correct Answer: B
Starter cutout typically occurs between 45% and 55% N2. At this point, the engine has reached self-sustaining speed, and the starter valve closes to prevent starter overspeed and mechanical damage.
Q4. What is the consequence of attempting an engine start with the air conditioning packs left ON (non-shedding system)?
A) Rapid cabin over-pressurization
B) Tailpipe fire due to fuel enrichment
C) A hung start or hot start due to insufficient pneumatic pressure to accelerate the N2 spool
D) Immediate trip of the APU generator
Correct Answer: C
With packs ON, the pneumatic duct pressure drops significantly. This reduces starter torque, causing the N2 spool to accelerate too slowly (hung start) or stall, leading to rapid turbine temperature rise (hot start) due to lack of cooling airflow.
Q5. On modern FADEC-controlled aircraft, when does the automatic pack shedding sequence initiate?
A) When N2 reaches 15%
B) When the Engine Start Selector is moved to the IGN/START position
C) Only after the starter valve fully opens
D) When the master switch is turned ON, provided duct pressure is below 20 psi
Correct Answer: B
Moving the Engine Start Selector to IGN/START signals the controller to close the Pack Flow Control Valves (FCVs) in anticipation of the pneumatic demand of the starter.
Q6. How does the Cabin Pressure Control System (CPCS) minimize the cabin "pressure bump" when packs are isolated and re-engaged?
A) By fully opening the outflow valve immediately
B) By modulating the outflow valve to control the cabin pressure rate of change and introducing a delay in pack valve opening
C) By dumping cabin pressure through the safety valves
D) By activating the emergency ram air inlet
Correct Answer: B
The CPCS modulates the Outflow Valve to manage the rate of pressure change. Additionally, a 10-second delay is introduced before the pack flow control valves re-open post-start to prevent a sudden surge of pressurized air.
Q7. Which valve regulates the direct flow of bleed air into the Air Turbine Starter (ATS)?
A) Isolation Valve
B) Pack Flow Control Valve
C) Starter Valve
D) APU Bleed Valve
Correct Answer: C
The Starter Valve (or Start Valve) is the dedicated valve that opens to allow pneumatic bleed air to enter the Air Turbine Starter.
Q8. What is the typical minimum pneumatic duct pressure required *prior* to initiating an engine start?
A) 15 psi
B) 30 psi
C) 75 psi
D) 120 psi
Correct Answer: B
A minimum of 30 psi (psig) is generally required in the pneumatic manifold to ensure the Air Turbine Starter can generate sufficient torque to overcome spool inertia and aerodynamic drag.
Q9. What safety device prevents the reverse flow of high-pressure engine bleed air into the APU compressor during engine start or crossbleed operations?
A) The APU Bleed Control Valve
B) The APU Non-Return (Check) Valve
C) The Isolation Valve
D) The Pack Flow Control Valve
Correct Answer: B
A mechanical check valve (non-return valve) is installed in the APU bleed line to prevent high-pressure engine bleed air from back-flowing into and damaging the APU compressor.
