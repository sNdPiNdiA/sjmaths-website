Transformer Rectifier Unit (TRU) - 3-phase to DC

Day 58 of 129 Aircraft Systems and Avionics Phase 3: Systems and Applied 60-90 min Master Cycle

Step 1: Understand the Core Concepts

A Transformer Rectifier Unit (TRU) is an essential solid-state electrical component in aircraft power systems that converts 115 Volts AC, 3-phase, 400 Hz electrical power generated by the engine-driven generators (IDGs) into unregulated 28 Volts DC power to feed the aircraft's DC buses and charge the main batteries.

Unlike rotating converters (such as rotary converters or motor-generator sets), a TRU has no moving parts (except for an optional integral cooling fan), making it highly reliable, efficient, and lightweight. The conversion process relies on electromagnetic induction for voltage step-down followed by solid-state diode bridge rectification.

Modern aircraft utilize multi-pulse rectification (typically 6-pulse or 12-pulse configurations) to minimize output voltage ripple and reduce total harmonic distortion (THD) reflected back into the primary AC distribution system, complying with strict aviation standards like RTCA DO-160.

Step 2: Key Formulas & Technical Specifications

The average DC output voltage ($V_{dc}$) for an uncontrolled 3-phase 6-pulse full-wave rectifier is calculated using the secondary line-to-line RMS voltage ($V_{L-L, rms}$):

$$V_{dc} = \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{\pi} \times V_{L-L, rms} \approx 1.35 \times V_{L-L, rms}$$

If using secondary phase-to-neutral voltage ($V_{L-N, rms}$):

$$V_{dc} = \frac{3\sqrt{6}}{\pi} \times V_{L-N, rms} \approx 2.34 \times V_{L-N, rms}$$

The ripple frequency ($f_{ripple}$) depends on the input AC frequency ($f_{in}$) and the pulse number ($p$):

$$f_{ripple} = p \times f_{in}$$

For a standard 400 Hz aircraft system:

  • 6-Pulse Rectifier: $f_{ripple} = 6 \times 400\text{ Hz} = 2400\text{ Hz}$
  • 12-Pulse Rectifier: $f_{ripple} = 12 \times 400\text{ Hz} = 4800\text{ Hz}$

TRU Internal Architecture Components

  • Step-Down Transformer: Reduces high-voltage AC (115V) to low-voltage AC (approx. 20-21V line-to-line secondary).
  • Rectifier Bridge: Solid-state silicon diodes arranged in a bridge configuration to allow current to flow in one direction only.
  • Filtering Stage: Inductors ($L$) and capacitors ($C$) forming a low-pass filter to smooth out remaining voltage ripples.
  • Thermal Protection: Thermal switches that open the input circuit breaker or trip the control relay if internal temperatures exceed safe limits (typically $>120^\circ\text{C}$).

Step 3: Memorization & High-Yield Facts

  • Standard Output: Aircraft TRUs are designed to output a nominal 28V DC (acceptable range: 24V to 30V DC under varying loads).
  • Current Rating: Typical commercial airliner TRUs (e.g., Boeing 737, Airbus A320) are rated between 100A and 250A.
  • Diode PIV: Peak Inverse Voltage rating of the diodes must be at least twice the peak AC secondary voltage to prevent reverse breakdown.
  • Cooling: TRUs are cooled either by dedicated internal electric fans or by ducting from the aircraft's avionics ventilation system.

Mnemonic for 12-Pulse Harmonic Elimination

"F-S Gone, E-T On"
Fifth & Seventh harmonics are Gone (cancelled out by the $30^\circ$ phase shift).
Eleventh & Thirteenth harmonics are On (remain as the lowest order harmonics in a 12-pulse system).

UPSC ASO Exam Tip: Questions frequently test the relationship between secondary line voltage and DC output. Always verify if the voltage given is Line-to-Line ($V_{L-L}$) or Line-to-Neutral ($V_{L-N}$). For $V_{L-L}$, multiply by $1.35$. For $V_{L-N}$, multiply by $2.34$.

Step 4: Aviation Applications & Systems Integration

Airbus A320 DC Distribution

The Airbus A320 utilizes two main TRUs (TRU 1 and TRU 2) and one Essential TRU (ESS TRU). Under normal operations, TRU 1 powers DC Bus 1, and TRU 2 powers DC Bus 2. If TRU 1 fails, the DC Bat Bus automatically connects to TRU 2 via the DC Bus Tie Contactor. If AC Bus 1 fails, the ESS TRU is powered by the Emergency Generator or AC ESS Bus to maintain power to vital flight instruments.

Boeing 737 NG/MAX TRU Redundancy

The Boeing 737 features three identical TRUs. TRU 1 and TRU 2 act as primary sources, while TRU 3 serves as a backup. If TRU 1 or TRU 2 fails, automatic bus transfer relays switch the load of the failed unit to TRU 3, ensuring no loss of critical DC systems during flight.

Thermal Overload and Off-line Logic

When a TRU overheats due to a diode short-circuit or cooling fan failure, an internal thermal switch closes, illuminating an "ELECD" or "TRU" amber caution light in the cockpit. The system automatically opens the AC input contactor to prevent thermal runaway and fire in the electronics bay.

Step 5: UPSC ASO Exam Focus & Common Traps

  • The Ripple Frequency Trap: UPSC often asks for the ripple frequency of a 3-phase full-wave rectifier. Candidates mistakenly select 1200 Hz (thinking of 3-pulse or single-phase full-wave). Remember: 3-phase full-wave is a 6-pulse rectifier, so $6 \times 400\text{ Hz} = 2400\text{ Hz}$.
  • Diode Failure Modes: If one diode in a 3-phase bridge fails open, the output voltage drops, the ripple factor increases significantly, and the ripple frequency spectrum gains a strong $2\times f_{in}$ (800 Hz) component. If a diode fails short, it causes an immediate AC phase-to-phase short-circuit, tripping the input circuit breaker.
  • Efficiency vs. Power Factor: TRUs have high efficiency ($>90\%$), but their displacement power factor can be low and harmonic distortion high unless passive/active power factor correction (PFC) circuits are integrated.

Practice Questions (10 MCQs)

Q1. A transformer rectifier unit (TRU) on a modern transport aircraft is primarily used to convert which of the following power configurations?
A) 28V DC to 115V AC, 1-phase, 400 Hz
B) 115V AC, 3-phase, 400 Hz to 28V DC
C) 115V AC, 1-phase, 400 Hz to 28V AC, 3-phase
D) 28V DC to 115V AC, 3-phase, 400 Hz
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: A TRU is a step-down transformer combined with a rectifier bridge that converts 115V AC, 3-phase, 400 Hz power (from the main generators) to 28V DC power to feed the DC buses.
Q2. What is the fundamental ripple frequency of an unfiltered 6-pulse three-phase rectifier operating on an aircraft 400 Hz AC system?
A) 400 Hz
B) 1200 Hz
C) 2400 Hz
D) 4800 Hz
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The ripple frequency ($f_{ripple}$) for a $p$-pulse rectifier is $p \times f_{in}$. For a 6-pulse rectifier on a 400 Hz system: $6 \times 400\text{ Hz} = 2400\text{ Hz}$.
Q3. Why is a 12-pulse TRU configuration preferred over a 6-pulse TRU configuration in high-power avionics applications?
A) It uses fewer diodes, making it lighter and highly reliable.
B) It eliminates the 5th and 7th harmonics, lowering total harmonic distortion (THD).
C) It can operate directly from a single-phase AC source.
D) It does not require a step-down transformer.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: A 12-pulse TRU uses a delta-delta-wye transformer configuration to introduce a $30^\circ$ phase shift. This phase shift cancels out the lowest-order harmonics (5th and 7th), significantly reducing THD on the AC bus.
Q4. If a 3-phase TRU has a secondary line-to-line RMS voltage of 20V, what is the theoretical average DC output voltage ($V_{dc}$) neglecting diode voltage drops?
A) 18.0 V
B) 27.0 V
C) 34.2 V
D) 46.8 V
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: For a 3-phase 6-pulse full-wave rectifier, $V_{dc} = 1.35 \times V_{L-L, rms} = 1.35 \times 20\text{V} = 27\text{V}$.
Q5. What is the effect on a TRU's output if one of the diodes in its 3-phase bridge rectifying circuit fails in an open-circuit state?
A) The DC output voltage drops to exactly zero.
B) The output voltage ripple increases and average DC voltage decreases.
C) The output ripple frequency increases to 4800 Hz.
D) The input AC circuit breaker immediately trips due to overcurrent.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: An open diode prevents current flow during its conduction phase. This causes the average DC output voltage to drop and significantly increases the ripple voltage (introducing a prominent lower-frequency ripple component).
Q6. In an aircraft electrical system, what prevents a short-circuited diode inside a TRU from causing catastrophic damage to the AC distribution bus?
A) A reverse-current cutout relay on the DC output side.
B) The TRU's internal thermal switch shutting down the cooling fan.
C) Fast-acting input fuses or circuit breakers on the AC input lines.
D) The automatic voltage regulator (AVR) of the main generator.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: A short-circuited diode creates a direct phase-to-phase short circuit across the transformer secondary during parts of the AC cycle. Fast-acting input fuses or circuit breakers on the AC input side isolate the faulty TRU to protect the primary AC bus.
Q7. What is the nominal output voltage range of a standard aircraft TRU under normal operating conditions?
A) 12V to 14V DC
B) 24V to 30V DC
C) 110V to 115V DC
D) 200V to 215V DC
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Although nominal aircraft DC systems are referred to as 28V DC systems, the actual operating voltage range of a TRU under varying loads and line conditions is typically 24V to 30V DC.
Q8. Which transformer winding connection is used in a 12-pulse TRU to achieve the $30^\circ$ phase shift necessary for harmonic cancellation?
A) Single primary winding and a single wye secondary winding.
B) Delta primary winding with dual secondary windings: one Delta and one Wye.
C) Wye primary winding with a single delta secondary winding.
D) Auto-transformer configuration with no galvanic isolation.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: A 12-pulse TRU uses a delta primary with two secondary windings: one connected in Delta ($\Delta$) and the other in Wye ($Y$). The line-to-line voltages of the Wye secondary lag the Delta secondary by $30^\circ$, which is key to harmonic elimination.
