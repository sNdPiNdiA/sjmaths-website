Commutator - function in DC generator

Day 58 of 129 Aircraft Systems and Avionics Phase 3: Systems and Applied 60-90 min Master Cycle

Step 1: Understand the Core Concepts

  • Mechanical Rectification: The primary function of a commutator in a DC generator is to convert the alternating current (AC) induced in the rotating armature windings into a unidirectional direct current (DC) at the stationary external terminals.
  • Switching Action: It acts as a mechanical rotary switch. As the armature rotates, the commutator segments automatically reverse the connections of the coils to the external circuit at the exact moment the induced voltage in those coils passes through zero (magnetic neutral axis).
  • Commutation Physics: The process of current reversal in an armature coil when it is short-circuited by a brush is called commutation. Due to the coil's self-inductance, this reversal is opposed by a self-induced EMF (reactance voltage), which can cause severe sparking at the brushes if not properly managed.

Step 2: Key Formulas, Comparisons & Methods

Reactance Voltage ($e_r$):

$$e_r = L \cdot \frac{2 I_c}{t_c}$$

Where:

  • $L$ = Self-inductance of the coil undergoing commutation (Henrys)
  • $I_c$ = Current in the armature coil (Amperes)
  • $t_c$ = Commutation period (seconds)

Commutator Segment Relationship:

$$\text{Number of Commutator Segments } (N_s) = \text{Number of Armature Coils } (C)$$

Ideal vs. Practical Commutation

Parameter Ideal (Linear) Commutation Practical (Delayed) Commutation
Current Reversal Uniform and complete within time $t_c$ Delayed due to self-induced reactance voltage
Sparking Zero sparking at the trailing brush edge Heavy sparking at the trailing brush edge
Current at end of $t_c$ Reaches $-I_c$ exactly Does not reach $-I_c$, causing an arc

Methods to Improve Commutation

  • Resistance Commutation: Replacing low-resistance copper brushes with high-resistance carbon brushes. This increases the contact resistance, forcing current to reverse faster.
  • Voltage Commutation (Interpoles): Using small auxiliary poles (interpoles) placed between the main poles. They induce an "interpolar EMF" in the short-circuited coil that directly opposes and neutralizes the reactance voltage.

Step 3: Memorize Key Facts & Tips

  • Construction: Commutator segments are made of high-conductivity, hard-drawn copper to resist wear, separated by thin sheets of high-grade mica insulation (typically 0.6 mm to 0.8 mm thick).
  • Mica Undercutting: Mica is harder than copper. Over time, copper wears down faster, leaving protruding mica ("high mica") which causes brush bounce and sparking. To prevent this, mica is undercut by about 1 mm.
  • Brush Pressure: Standard aircraft generator brush pressure is maintained between 1.5 to 2.5 psi (10 to 18 kPa) to balance wear and electrical contact.

Mnemonic: C.O.P.S.

Remember how to manage commutation issues:

  • C - Carbon brushes (High resistance improves commutation)
  • O - Opposing EMF (Induced by Interpoles)
  • P - Polarity of Interpole (Same as main pole ahead in direction of rotation for a generator)
  • S - Spark-free operation

UPSC ASO Exam Tip

Always double-check the question context! In a Generator, the interpole must have the same polarity as the main pole ahead of it in the direction of rotation. In a Motor, it must have the opposite polarity (same as the main pole behind it).

Step 4: Aviation & Aircraft Applications

1. Starter-Generator Units (SGUs)

In turboprop aircraft (like the ATR 72 or King Air), a single DC machine acts as a starter motor during engine start and transitions into a DC generator once the engine reaches self-sustaining speed. The commutator must handle extremely high starting currents (up to 1000A) without flashover, and then switch to stable continuous generator output.

2. High-Altitude Brush Wear & "Adjuvants"

At high flight altitudes (above 30,000 feet), the low humidity and lack of oxygen prevent the formation of the protective copper oxide-graphite film on the commutator surface. This causes catastrophic brush wear (known as "rapid high-altitude dusting"). Aircraft brushes are specially treated with chemical adjuvants (like barium fluoride or lithium carbonate) to maintain this film in vacuum-like conditions.

3. Radio Frequency Interference (RFI) Mitigation

Commutator sparking acts as a miniature spark-gap transmitter, generating high-frequency electromagnetic interference (EMI/RFI) that disrupts VHF navigation and communication systems. Aircraft DC generators use heavy shielding, bypass capacitors, and precisely tuned interpoles to suppress this RFI at the source.

Step 5: UPSC ASO Exam Focus & Traps

  • The "No-Change" Trap: UPSC often asks: "What is the frequency of the current in the armature winding of a DC generator?" The answer is always AC, not DC. The commutator only rectifies it after it leaves the armature.
  • Interpole Connection Trap: Interpole windings are always connected in series with the armature circuit, never in parallel (shunt), because the reactance voltage is proportional to the armature current, requiring the neutralizing interpolar field to scale dynamically with load current.
  • Brush Shift Direction: To assist commutation without interpoles, brushes are shifted in the direction of rotation for a generator, and against the direction of rotation for a motor.

Practice Questions (10 MCQs)

Q1. The primary function of a commutator in an aircraft DC generator is to:
A) Collect current from the armature with minimum friction
B) Convert induced AC in the armature to DC for the external circuit
C) Reduce the reactance voltage of the winding coils
D) Increase the overall efficiency by reducing copper losses
Correct Answer: B
The commutator acts as a mechanical rectifier that reverses the connections of the armature coils to the external circuit at the moment of current reversal, converting internal AC to external DC.
Q2. The segments of an aircraft DC generator commutator are insulated from one another using:
A) Bakelite sheets
B) Thin sheets of high-grade mica
C) Asbestos lining
D) Ceramic coating
Correct Answer: B
High-grade mica is used because of its excellent dielectric strength and ability to withstand high temperatures generated by brush friction.
Q3. Reactance voltage in a DC machine coil undergoing commutation is caused by:
A) Armature reaction neutralizing the main field
B) Self-inductance of the coil opposing rapid current reversal
C) Centrifugal forces acting on the commutator segments
D) Interpolar flux leakage
Correct Answer: B
Reactance voltage is given by $e_r = L \frac{di}{dt}$. It is a self-induced EMF caused by the self-inductance ($L$) of the short-circuited coil which opposes the change of current during commutation.
Q4. In a DC generator, the winding of the interpoles is connected in:
A) Series with the field winding
B) Parallel with the armature winding
C) Series with the armature winding
D) Parallel with the shunt field winding
Correct Answer: C
Interpoles must carry the full armature current so that the neutralizing magnetic field they produce scales proportionally with the load current. Thus, they are connected in series with the armature.
Q5. For sparkless commutation in a DC generator, the polarity of an interpole must be:
A) Same as the main pole ahead of it in the direction of rotation
B) Same as the main pole behind it in the direction of rotation
C) Opposite to the main pole ahead of it in the direction of rotation
D) Independent of the direction of rotation
Correct Answer: A
In a generator, the interpole polarity must match the polarity of the next main pole ahead in the direction of rotation to induce an EMF that opposes the reactance voltage.
Q6. What is the total number of commutator segments in a simplex lap-wound DC generator?
A) Half the number of armature coils
B) Equal to the number of armature coils
C) Twice the number of armature coils
D) Equal to the number of poles
Correct Answer: B
Regardless of the type of winding (lap or wave), the number of commutator segments is always equal to the number of armature coils.
Q7. Why do aircraft DC generators require "altitude-treated" carbon brushes?
A) To prevent high-altitude freezing of the spring mechanism
B) To maintain a protective lubrication film on the commutator in low humidity
C) To reduce the electrical resistance of the brush at low temperatures
D) To prevent cosmic radiation damage to the carbon structure
Correct Answer: B
At high altitudes, the lack of moisture and oxygen causes standard carbon brushes to wear rapidly (dusting). Altitude-treated brushes contain chemical adjuvants to maintain the protective oxide film.
Q8. "Undercutting" of a commutator refers to:
A) Reducing the diameter of the copper segments at the ends
B) Cutting the mica insulation below the level of the copper segments
C) Grooving the brushes to fit the commutator curvature
D) Reducing the thickness of the copper segments near the shaft
Correct Answer: B
Mica is harder than copper. To prevent the mica from protruding as the copper wears down (which causes brush bounce and sparking), the mica is undercut (usually by about 1 mm).
Q9. How does using high-resistance carbon brushes improve the commutation process?
A) It reduces the self-inductance ($L$) of the armature coils
B) It increases the contact resistance, forcing current to reverse more rapidly
C) It eliminates the need for mica insulation between segments
D) It decreases the physical temperature of the commutator
Correct Answer: B
High contact resistance of carbon brushes limits the short-circuit current in the coil undergoing commutation and encourages the current to divert to the next segment quickly, facilitating smooth reversal.
Q10. In a DC generator, the current flowing inside the armature windings is:
A) Pure, ripple-free direct current
B) Pulsating direct current
C) Alternating current
D) Zero, as current only flows in the external load
Correct Answer: C
Inside the armature windings of any standard DC generator, the induced EMF and resulting current are always alternating (AC). The commutator only rectifies it to DC at the external brush terminals.

Grand Test (UPSC ASO Level)

Q1. An aircraft DC generator rotating clockwise (viewed from the drive end) experiences severe sparking at the trailing edge of the brushes under load. To correct this using interpoles, how should the interpoles be configured?
A) Placed in parallel with the main poles, having the same polarity as the main pole behind them.
B) Placed in series with the armature, having the same polarity as the main pole ahead in the clockwise direction.
C) Placed in series with the shunt field, having the opposite polarity to the main pole ahead.
D) Placed in parallel with the armature, having the same polarity as the main pole ahead.
Correct Answer: B
For a generator, interpoles must be connected in series with the armature (to track load current variations) and must have the same polarity as the main pole ahead of them in the direction of rotation (clockwise in this case).
