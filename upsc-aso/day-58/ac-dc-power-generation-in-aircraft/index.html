AC/DC power generation in aircraft

Day 58 of 129 Aircraft Systems and Avionics Phase 3: Systems and Applied 60-90 min Master Cycle

Step 1: Understand the Core Concepts

  • Primary AC Generation: Modern civil transport aircraft generate alternating current (AC) as their primary power source using engine-driven brushless alternators. The standard output is 115V / 200V AC at 3-phase 400 Hz. The high frequency of 400 Hz is selected specifically to reduce the physical size and weight of magnetic cores in transformers, motors, and alternators compared to 50/60 Hz terrestrial grids.
  • Frequency Regulation (CSD vs. VSCF): Because aircraft engines operate at continuously variable RPMs, maintaining a constant 400 Hz frequency requires specialized systems. This is traditionally achieved via a hydro-mechanical Constant Speed Drive (CSD) coupled to the generator, combined as an Integrated Drive Generator (IDG). Modern aircraft also use Variable Speed Constant Frequency (VSCF) systems, which generate variable frequency AC and convert it to constant 400 Hz AC electronically using solid-state cycloconverters.
  • DC Power and Emergency Systems: Direct Current (DC) power at 28V DC is primarily derived from the AC buses through Transformer Rectifier Units (TRUs). Emergency power is supplied by onboard Nickel-Cadmium (Ni-Cd) or Lithium-Ion batteries (acting as a temporary DC source), a static inverter (converting DC to AC for critical instruments), or a deployable air-driven Ram Air Turbine (RAT).

Step 2: Formulas, Comparisons & Classifications

Key Electrical Formulas for ASO Exam:

1. Generator Output Frequency ($f$):

$$f = \frac{N \times P}{120}$$

Where $N$ is the rotor speed in RPM, and $P$ is the number of poles on the rotor.

2. Three-Phase Apparent Power ($S$):

$$S = \sqrt{3} \times V_L \times I_L \quad \text{(VA)}$$

Where $V_L$ is Line-to-Line voltage (200V) and $I_L$ is Line current.

3. Phase-to-Neutral ($V_N$) vs. Line-to-Line ($V_L$) Voltage:

$$V_L = \sqrt{3} \times V_N \approx 1.732 \times 115\text{V} \approx 200\text{V}$$

Aircraft Bus Classification

  • Hot Battery Bus: Continuously connected directly to the battery. Powers critical safety items (e.g., fire extinguishers, clock, dome lights) regardless of cockpit switch positions.
  • Essential/Standby Bus: Powers flight-critical instruments and navigation systems required for safe flight and landing. Can be powered by the battery via a static inverter in emergencies.
  • Main/Utility Bus: Powers non-essential loads (e.g., galley power, cabin lighting, in-flight entertainment). Shed automatically during load shedding events.

Step 3: Memorization Aids & Exam Tips

  • Standard Voltages: Primary AC is 115V / 400 Hz. Secondary DC is 28V. Battery emergency output is typically 24V DC nominal.
  • Generator Paralleling Rule: For two AC generators to operate in parallel on a split-bus system, they must match in Voltage, Frequency, Phase, and Phase Sequence. Most modern aircraft use non-parallel systems (split-bus) to avoid complex synchronization.
  • Thermal Runaway: Ni-Cd batteries are highly susceptible to thermal runaway. As temperature rises, internal resistance decreases, causing an exponential increase in charging current, leading to boiling electrolyte and potential fire.

Mnemonic: F-I-S-T (Power Conversion & Control)

  • Frequency: 400 Hz (Lightweight magnetic cores)
  • IDG: Integrated Drive Generator (CSD + Alternator in one housing)
  • Static Inverter: Converts DC to AC (Emergency backup)
  • TRU: Converts AC to DC (Transformer Rectifier Unit)

UPSC ASO Exam Tip: The CSD Disconnect Trap

If an IDG experiences high oil temperature or low oil pressure, the pilot can disconnect the CSD from the engine gearbox using a cockpit switch. Crucial Fact: Once disconnected in flight, a CSD/IDG cannot be reconnected in flight. It can only be mechanically reconnected on the ground by maintenance personnel.

Step 4: Real-World Aviation Application

1. Boeing 787 "More Electric Aircraft" (MEA) Architecture

The B787 eliminates traditional pneumatic bleed air systems and relies heavily on electrical power. It utilizes four 250 kVA Variable Frequency Starter Generators (VFSGs) directly driven by the engines. The system outputs a variable frequency ranging from 360 Hz to 800 Hz at 230V AC. This eliminates the heavy CSD completely, reducing weight and improving fuel efficiency by 3%.

2. Airbus A320 Emergency Power Generation (RAT Deployment)

In the event of a total loss of normal AC power (both main generators and APU generator offline), a Ram Air Turbine (RAT) automatically deploys from the belly of the fuselage. The RAT uses airflow to drive a hydraulic pump, which in turn drives an Emergency Generator (CSM/G - Constant Speed Motor/Generator) to deliver 5 kVA of 115V/400Hz AC power to the essential buses.

3. Transformer Rectifier Unit (TRU) Cooling Failures

TRUs contain heavy-duty solid-state silicon diodes to rectify AC into DC. Because they dissipate significant heat, they are equipped with internal cooling fans and thermal switches. In ASO accident investigations, a blocked cooling duct or TRU fan failure has been linked to thermal degradation, leading to automatic bus isolation and subsequent loss of DC essential buses.

Step 5: UPSC ASO Exam Focus & Common Traps

  • The 115V vs. 200V Trap: UPSC questions often confuse phase-to-neutral and phase-to-phase voltages. Remember: 115V AC is Phase-to-Neutral, while 200V AC is Phase-to-Phase (Line-to-Line).
  • Static Inverter vs. TRU:
    • TRU = AC to DC conversion (Transformer drops voltage, Rectifier converts to DC).
    • Static Inverter = DC to AC conversion (Uses solid-state switching to create 400Hz AC from 24V/28V DC).
  • Battery Charging Methods: Aircraft batteries are charged using Constant Voltage or Constant Current / Pulsed methods. Ni-Cd batteries require precise temperature monitoring to prevent thermal runaway; hence, battery chargers monitor temperature sensors embedded inside the battery case.

Practice MCQs (Topic-Specific)

Q1. An aircraft generator has 6 poles and rotates at a constant speed of 8,000 RPM. What is the frequency of the generated AC voltage?
A) 240 Hz
B) 400 Hz
C) 480 Hz
D) 800 Hz
Using the formula: $f = \frac{N \times P}{120}$
$f = \frac{8000 \times 6}{120} = \frac{48000}{120} = 400\text{ Hz}$. This is the standard aviation frequency.
Q2. Why is 400 Hz AC power preferred over 50/60 Hz power in commercial transport aircraft?
A) It reduces skin effect losses in the aluminum airframe wiring.
B) It allows the use of smaller, lighter magnetic cores in transformers and alternators.
C) It eliminates the need for shielding sensitive avionics.
D) It prevents corona discharge at high altitudes.
Inductive reactance increases with frequency, which allows transformers and motors to achieve the same magnetic flux with much smaller and lighter iron cores, saving massive amounts of weight.

A) 115V AC
B) 230V AC
C) 200V AC
D) 400V AC
Line-to-Line voltage $V_L = \sqrt{3} \times V_N$.
$V_L = 1.732 \times 115\text{V} \approx 199.18\text{V}$, which is standardized as 200V AC.
Q4. Which component is responsible for converting 115V AC power to 28V DC power in an aircraft?
A) Static Inverter
B) Constant Speed Drive
C) Transformer Rectifier Unit (TRU)
D) Voltage Regulator
A Transformer Rectifier Unit (TRU) steps down the 115V AC to a lower AC voltage using a transformer, and then rectifies it to 28V DC using solid-state diodes.
Q5. If an Integrated Drive Generator (IDG) experiences low oil pressure in flight, what action must be taken, and what is its limitation?
A) Disconnect the IDG; it can be reconnected by cycling the cockpit switch.
B) Disconnect the IDG; it can only be reconnected on the ground.
C) Reduce engine power to idle; the IDG will automatically reconnect when pressure rises.
D) Switch to battery power; the IDG will self-disconnect when oil temperature exceeds 150°C.
To prevent catastrophic mechanical failure, the pilot must manually disconnect the IDG using a solenoid-actuated mechanical clutch. This disconnect is irreversible in flight and requires ground maintenance to reset.
Q6. What is the primary function of a Static Inverter in an aircraft electrical system?
A) To convert 115V AC to 28V DC during normal operations.
B) To convert 28V DC from the battery to 115V AC for essential instruments in an emergency.
C) To match the phase angle of two parallel AC generators.
D) To step up DC voltage from 12V to 28V.
A Static Inverter uses solid-state electronics to convert DC battery power (typically 24V or 28V) to 115V, 400 Hz AC power to run critical flight instruments during a total AC generation failure.
Q7. What physical phenomenon causes thermal runaway in Nickel-Cadmium (Ni-Cd) aircraft batteries?
A) Increased internal resistance with temperature rise under constant current charging.
B) Decreased internal resistance with temperature rise under constant voltage charging.
C) Electrolyte depletion leading to high voltage spikes.
D) Short-circuiting of the positive plate to the steel battery casing.
In Ni-Cd batteries, as temperature increases, the internal resistance decreases. Under a constant-voltage charging system, this decrease in resistance causes a massive increase in charging current, generating more heat and leading to a destructive thermal loop.
Q8. In a split-bus electrical system, what is the role of the Bus Tie Breakers (BTBs)?
A) To parallel the main AC generators during takeoff.
B) To automatically isolate a failed generator and allow the operating generator to power both main buses without paralleling.
C) To connect the battery directly to the hot battery bus during emergency situations.
D) To convert variable frequency AC to constant frequency AC.
Modern commercial aircraft use a split-bus system. Generators are never operated in parallel. If one generator fails, the Bus Tie Breakers (BTBs) close to allow the remaining generator to power the entire system, keeping the two sides isolated phase-wise.
Q9. Why is it dangerous to actuate the IDG disconnect switch when the engine is not running or is below idle speed?
A) It will cause immediate electrical fire in the stator.
B) The disconnect solenoid can burn out or damage the mechanical disconnect jaw due to incomplete separation.
C) It will discharge the main aircraft batteries completely.
D) It causes hydraulic fluid leakage from the CSD into the engine gearbox.
The IDG disconnect mechanism relies on the rotation of the engine drive shaft to assist the spring-loaded solenoid in sliding the disconnect gear. Actuating it without rotation can damage the mechanism or burn out the solenoid.
