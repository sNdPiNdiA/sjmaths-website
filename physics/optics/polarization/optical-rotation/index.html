Optical Rotation and Specific Rotation

Optical rotation is the phenomenon in which a chiral medium rotates the plane of polarization of linearly polarized light passing through it. This property, also known as circular birefringence, arises from the structural asymmetry of optically active substances.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of optical activity, chiral media, plane of polarization, Biot's laws of optical rotation, and basic dependence on path length and concentration.

PGT extension

Fresnel's theory of optical rotation based on circular components, differential phase velocity for left and right circularly polarized light, specific rotation, and wavelength dependence (Rotatory Dispersion).

Prerequisites

  • Nature of unpolarized and polarized light
  • Malus Law and Double Refraction
  • Circularly and elliptically polarized light

Origin of Optical Activity

  • Optically active substances possess molecular or crystalline asymmetry, lacking a center and plane of symmetry.
  • When linearly polarized light enters an optically active medium, it resolves into two counter-rotating circular polarization components (left-circular and right-circular).
  • The medium exhibits different refractive indices for left-circular (n_L) and right-circular (n_R) polarized light, a phenomenon termed circular birefringence.

Biot's Laws of Optical Rotation

  • The angle of rotation (θ) is directly proportional to the thickness (l) of the active medium traversed: θ ∝ l.
  • For solutions, the rotation is directly proportional to the concentration (c) of the active solute and the path length: θ ∝ c · l.
  • The rotation is inversely proportional to the square of the wavelength (λ) of the incident light, expressed approximately as $\theta = k / \lambda^{2}$.

Specific Rotation

  • Specific rotation ([θ]λ^T) is defined as the rotation produced by a solution of length 1 decimetre (10 cm) and concentration 1 gram per millilitre (g/cm³) at a specified temperature T and wavelength λ.
  • It is a characteristic constant for a given optically active substance under fixed physical conditions.
  • Units of specific rotation are typically degree · dm² / g or degree · cm² / g depending on convention.

Fresnel's Explanation of Optical Rotation

  • Linearly polarized light is mathematically equivalent to the superposition of two oppositely rotating circular vibrations of equal amplitude and frequency.
  • Due to circular birefringence, the left and right circular components travel at different phase velocities (v_L ≠ v_R) inside the medium.
  • Upon emergence, the phase difference between the two circular components introduces a spatial rotation of the resultant linear vibration vector.

Derivations

Angle of Rotation via Fresnel's Theory

Conditions: Light traveling a distance l through an optically active medium with refractive indices n_L and n_R for left and right circularly polarized light.

  • Let the incident light have angular frequency ω and vacuum wavelength λ0.
  • The phase velocity of left-circularly polarized light is $v_L = c / n_L$ and that of right-circularly polarized light is $v_R = c / n_R$.
  • After traveling path length l, the phase accumulated by the left-circular component is φ_$L = (\omega / v_L) l = (2\pi n_L / \lambda_0) l$.
  • Similarly, the phase accumulated by the right-circular component is φ_$R = (2\pi n_R / \lambda_0) l$.
  • The angle of rotation θ of the resultant plane of polarization is half the phase difference between the two components: $\theta = 1/2 (\phi_L - \phi_R)$.

Result: the t$a = \frac{\pi l}{\lambda_0} (n_L - n_R)$

Relation for Specific Rotation

Conditions: Solution containing mass m of active solute in volume V, path length l.

  • The measured angle of rotation θ is proportional to the path length l and concentration c.
  • Concentration c is defined as mass m per unit volume V ($c = m / V)$.
  • The rotation formula is written as $\theta = [\theta] \cdot c \cdot l$, where [θ] is the specific rotation.
  • If l is measured in decimetres (dm) and c in g/cm³, the expression yields the standard specific rotation definition.

Result: [\theta] = \frac{\theta}{c cdot l}

Draw: Schematic diagram showing sodium light source, condensing lens, Nicol prism polarizer, half-shade device, polarimeter tube, analyzer Nicol prism, and eyepiece.

Interpret: The half-shade device divides the field of view into halves of differing brightness; rotation of the analyzer until equal intensity (matching field) is achieved gives accurate angular measurement.

Exam use: Questions on experimental setup components, purpose of half-shade device, and zero-setting adjustment.

Rotatory Dispersion Curve

Draw: A graph of angle of rotation (θ) on the y-axis versus wavelength (λ) on the x-axis showing a steep continuous decrease towards longer wavelengths.

Interpret: Demonstrates that shorter wavelengths undergo significantly larger rotation than longer wavelengths, following approximately an inverse square law.

Exam use: Interpreting normal versus anomalous optical rotatory dispersion (ORD).

Linear vs. Circular Birefringence Comparison

Draw: Side-by-side vector diagrams of ordinary/extraordinary orthogonal linear polarizations versus left/right circular polarizations.

Interpret: Contrasts crystal birefringence (phase velocity difference between orthogonal linear states) with optical activity (phase velocity difference between circular states).

Exam use: Conceptual distinction between standard crystal optics and circular birefringence.

Solved numericals

TGT core

A tube of length 20 cm containing a sugar solution of concentration 50 g/L produces an optical rotation of 13 degrees. Calculate the specific rotation of the sugar solution.

  • Identify given values: path length l = 20 cm = 2 dm, concentration $c = 50 g/L = 0.05 g/cm^{3} (or use g/mL directly if standard)$, $\theta = 13 degrees$.
  • Formula for specific rotation: [θ] = θ / (c · l).
  • Substitute values: [θ] = 13 / (0.05 × 2) where c is in g/cm³ and l is in dm. Alternatively, using $c = 0.05 g/cm^{3}$ and $l = 2 dm gives [\theta] = 13 / (0.05 \times 2) = 13 / 0.1 = 130 degree \cdot dm^{2} / g$.

Answer: 130 degree · dm² / g

PGT extension

Light of vacuum wavelength 589 nm passes through an optically active quartz crystal of thickness 2 mm cut perpendicular to the optic axis. If the refractive indices for left and right circularly polarized light are 1.5542 and 1.5534 respectively, find the angle of rotation.

  • Identify given data: λ_$0 = 589 nm = 589 \times 10^-9 m$, $l = 2 mm = 2 \times 10^-3 m$, $n_L = 1.5542$, $n_R = 1.5534$.
  • Use Fresnel's rotation formula: $\theta = [\pi l / \lambda_0] \cdot (n_L - n_R)$.
  • Calculate (n_L - $n_R) = 1.5542 - 1.5534 = 0.0008$.
  • Substitute into equation: $\theta = [\pi \times 2 \times 10^-3 / (589 \times 10^-9)] \times 0.0008$.
  • $\theta = (3.1416 \times 2000 / 589) \times 0.0008 = (6283.2 / 589) \times 0.0008 = 10.6675 \times 0.0008 = 0.008534 radians$.
  • Convert to degrees: θ in degree$s = 0.008534 \times (180 / \pi) \approx 0.489 degrees$.

Answer: 0.489 degrees (or 0.00853 radians)

PGT extension

Two sugar solutions A and B of concentrations 40 g/L and 60 g/L are placed in polarimeter tubes of lengths 30 cm and 20 cm respectively. If their specific rotations are in the ratio 2:3, find the ratio of their optical rotations (θ_A / θ_B).

  • Write Biot's law for both solutions: θ_$A = [\theta]_A \cdot c_A \cdot l_A$ and θ_$B = [\theta]_B \cdot c_B \cdot l_B$.
  • Given: $c_A = 40 g/L$, $l_A = 30 cm = 3 dm; c_B = 60 g/L$, $l_B = 20 cm = 2 dm; [\theta]_A / [\theta]_B = 2 / 3$.
  • Form the ratio: θ_A / θ_$B = ([\theta]_A / [\theta]_B) \cdot (c_A / c_B) \cdot (l_A / l_B)$.
  • Substitute the values: θ_A / θ_$B = (2 / 3) \cdot (40 / 60) \cdot (3 / 2)$.
  • Simplify: 40/$60 = 2/3$. Thus, θ_A / θ_$B = (2/3) \cdot (2/3) \cdot (3/2) = (2/3) \cdot 1 = 2/3$.

Answer: 2:3

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes homogeneous distribution of optically active molecules without intermolecular interaction concentration anomalies.
  • Valid only for transparent media away from regions of anomalous optical rotatory dispersion and strong absorption bands.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Optical rotation is caused by reflection and refraction at the container walls.

Correct: Optical rotation is an intrinsic bulk property of the chiral medium due to circular birefringence.

The phenomenon depends strictly on the molecular chirality and path length traversed inside the substance, independent of container boundaries.

Common error: Specific rotation depends on the length of the polarimeter tube used.

Correct: Specific rotation is an intensive property and is independent of path length and concentration.

Path length and concentration are accounted for in the definition; specific rotation is a characteristic constant for a given substance at a fixed temperature and wavelength.

Common error: All organic compounds exhibit optical rotation.

Correct: Only chiral compounds lacking a center of inversion and internal mirror plane exhibit optical rotation.

Achiral molecules possess symmetry elements that cancel out differential phase accumulation for circular polarization components.

Quick revision

  • Optical rotation is the rotation of the plane of polarization by chiral media possessing circular birefringence (n_L ≠ n_R).
  • Biot's Law for Solutions: $\theta = [\theta]_{\lambda}^{T} \cdot c \cdot l$
  • Fresnel's Rotation Angle: $\theta = \frac{\pi l}{\lambda_0} (n_L - n_R)$

Must remember

  • Optically active substances lack a center and plane of symmetry, possessing molecular or crystalline asymmetry.
  • Circular birefringence refers to the phenomenon where a medium exhibits different refractive indices for left-circular ($n_L$) and right-circular ($n_R$) polarized light.
  • Biot's law states that the angle of rotation is directly proportional to path length ($l$) and concentration ($c$), and inversely proportional to the square of the wavelength ($\lambda^2$).
  • Specific rotation is defined at a path length of $1$ decimetre ($10\text{ cm}$) and concentration of $1\text{ g/cm}^3$ at a specified temperature and wavelength.
  • Linearly polarized light is equivalent to two oppositely rotating circular vibrations of equal amplitude and frequency.
  • Specific rotation is an intensive property and is independent of the length of the polarimeter tube or concentration used.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following symmetry elements is absent in optically active substances?

Explanation: Optically active substances possess molecular or crystalline asymmetry, lacking both a center of inversion and a plane of symmetry.

2. According to Biot's laws, the angle of rotation $\theta$ of linearly polarized light through an active solution is directly proportional to:

Explanation: Biot's law for solutions states that the rotation is directly proportional to the concentration $c$ of the active solute and the path length $l$, given as $\theta \propto c \cdot l$.

3. How does the angle of optical rotation $\theta$ depend approximately on the wavelength $\lambda$ of incident light?

Explanation: The rotation is inversely proportional to the square of the wavelength of the incident light, expressed approximately as $\theta = k / \lambda^2$.

4. Circular birefringence in an optically active medium means that the medium exhibits:

Explanation: Circular birefringence is characterized by different refractive indices for left-circular ($n_L$) and right-circular ($n_R$) polarized light ($n_L \neq n_R$).

5. What are the standard units of specific rotation typically expressed as?

Explanation: Specific rotation units are typically $\text{degree} \cdot \text{dm}^2 / \text{g}$ or $\text{degree} \cdot \text{cm}^2 / \text{g}$ depending on the path length convention used.

6. In Fresnel's explanation, linearly polarized light is equivalent to the superposition of:

Explanation: Fresnel's theory models linearly polarized light as the superposition of two oppositely rotating circular vibrations of equal amplitude and frequency.

7. Specific rotation is defined for a solution of length:

Explanation: Specific rotation is defined as the rotation produced by a solution of length $1$ decimetre ($10\text{ cm}$) and concentration $1\text{ gram per millilitre}$ ($1\text{ g/cm}^3$).

8. If the path length is given in centimetres ($l_{\text{cm}}$), how is it converted to decimetres ($l_{\text{dm}}$)?

Explanation: Since $1\text{ dm} = 10\text{ cm}$, the path length in decimetres is $l_{\text{dm}} = l_{\text{cm}} / 10$.

9. Which of the following statements regarding specific rotation is correct?

Explanation: Specific rotation is an intensive property and a characteristic constant for a given optically active substance under fixed physical conditions (temperature and wavelength).

10. The phase difference $\Delta \phi$ between left and right circular components after traversing a path length $l$ is given by:

Explanation: The phase difference accumulated due to unequal phase velocities is $\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi l}{\lambda_0}(n_L - n_R)$.

11. Fresnel's rotation angle formula for optical rotation is expressed as:

Explanation: The rotation angle from Fresnel's theory is half of the phase difference, yielding $\theta = \frac{\pi l}{\lambda_0} (n_L - n_R)$.

12. What causes optical rotation at the molecular level in chiral media?

Explanation: Optical rotation arises because left and right circular components travel at different phase velocities ($v_L \neq v_R$) inside the chiral medium.

13. For a mixture of non-interacting optically active solutes, the total rotation $\theta$ is given by:

Explanation: For non-interacting solutes, the total rotation is the sum of rotations contributed by each component: $\theta = l \sum_{i} [\theta]_i c_i$.

14. The rotatory dispersion empirical relation near absorption bands is expressed as:

Explanation: The empirical relation for rotatory dispersion is $\theta = \frac{A}{\lambda^2 - \lambda_0^2}$ away from anomalous dispersion regions.

15. Which of the following is a common misconception about optical rotation?

Explanation: Optical rotation is an intrinsic bulk property of the chiral medium due to circular birefringence, not container walls.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. If the angle of rotation produced by a solution of concentration $0.05\text{ g/cm}^3$ and path length $20\text{ cm}$ is $2.0^\circ$, what is the specific rotation of the substance?

Explanation: Path length $l = 20\text{ cm} = 2\text{ dm}$. Using Biot's law $\theta = [\theta] \cdot c \cdot l$, we have $2.0 = [\theta] \cdot 0.05 \cdot 2 = [\theta] \cdot 0.1$, so $[\theta] = 2.0 / 0.1 = 20\text{ degree} \cdot \text{dm}^2 / \text{g}$.

2. In a polarimeter experiment, if the wavelength of light is halved, the angle of rotation according to Biot's approximate relation approximately:

Explanation: Since $\theta \propto 1 / \lambda^2$, if $\lambda$ becomes $\lambda / 2$, then $\theta$ becomes proportional to $1 / ((\lambda / 2)^2) = 4 / \lambda^2$, increasing by a factor of $4$.

3. A solution of cane sugar in a polarimeter tube of length $2\text{ dm}$ causes a rotation of $13.2^\circ$. If the specific rotation of sugar is $66\text{ degree} \cdot \text{dm}^2 / \text{g}$, the concentration of the sugar solution is:

Explanation: Using $\theta = [\theta] \cdot c \cdot l$, $13.2 = 66 \cdot c \cdot 2 \implies 13.2 = 132 \cdot c \implies c = 13.2 / 132 = 0.10\text{ g/cm}^3$.

4. According to Fresnel's theory, the rotation of the plane of polarization per unit length is proportional to:

Explanation: From Fresnel's formula $\theta = \frac{\pi l}{\lambda_0} (n_L - n_R)$, the rotation per unit length ($\theta / l$) is directly proportional to the difference in refractive indices $(n_L - n_R)$.

5. Two solutions of optically active substances $A$ and $B$ have concentrations $c_A$ and $c_B$ and specific rotations $[\theta]_A$ and $[\theta]_B$ respectively. When placed in tubes of equal length, they produce equal angles of rotation. The ratio of their concentrations $c_A / c_B$ is:

Explanation: Since $\theta_A = \theta_B$, we have $[\theta]_A c_A l = [\theta]_B c_B l$, which gives $c_A / c_B = [\theta]_B / [\theta]_A$.

6. Why is sodium light (yellow doublet, $\lambda \approx 589.3\text{ nm}$) commonly used as a standard source in polarimetry?

Explanation: Sodium light provides a stable, nearly monochromatic emission source necessary because optical rotation is strongly wavelength-dependent.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which physical property is primarily responsible for optical rotation in quartz crystals?

Explanation: Circular birefringence—differing phase velocities for left and right circularly polarized light—is responsible for optical rotation.

2. If the path length of a polarimeter is doubled and the concentration is halved, how does the angle of rotation change?

Explanation: Since $\theta \propto c \cdot l$, if $l$ becomes $2l$ and $c$ becomes $c/2$, the product $c \cdot l$ remains unchanged, so $\theta$ remains unchanged.

3. What is the phase difference between left and right circular components when linearly polarized light emerges after being rotated by an angle $\theta$?

Explanation: The rotation angle $\theta$ is half of the phase difference $\Delta \phi$ between the two circular components, so $\Delta \phi = 2\theta$.

4. Specific rotation is independent of which of the following parameters?

Explanation: Specific rotation is an intensive property defined independently of the path length or concentration used; it does, however, depend on temperature and wavelength.

5. In the empirical relation for rotatory dispersion $\theta = A / (\lambda^2 - \lambda_0^2)$, what does $\lambda_0$ represent?

Explanation: In rotatory dispersion formulas, $\lambda_0$ represents the characteristic absorption band wavelength of the substance.

6. A solution contains two non-interacting active solutes with specific rotations $[\theta]_1 = 40^\circ$ and $[\theta]_2 = 20^\circ$ in concentrations $c_1 = 0.02$ and $c_2 = 0.03\text{ g/cm}^3$. For a path length of $10\text{ cm}$ ($1\text{ dm}$), the total rotation is:

Explanation: Total rotation $\theta = l \cdot ([\theta]_1 c_1 + [\theta]_2 c_2) = 1 \cdot (40 \times 0.02 + 20 \times 0.03) = 1 \cdot (0.8 + 0.6) = 1.4^\circ$.

7. Which of the following conditions is necessary for Fresnel's explanation of optical rotation?

Explanation: Fresnel's explanation assumes normal incidence along the optic axis of a uniaxial active medium.

8. If the specific rotation of a substance is given in $\text{degree} \cdot \text{cm}^2 / \text{g}$ and path length is in centimetres, the formula for specific rotation is:

Explanation: When using standard laboratory conditions with $\theta$ in degrees, $c$ in $\text{g/cm}^3$, and $l$ in $\text{cm}$, specific rotation is defined as $[\theta] = \frac{\theta}{c \cdot l}$.

9. Chirality in molecules refers to:

Explanation: Chirality is the geometric property of an object or molecule lacking reflection symmetry, making it non-superimposable on its mirror image.

10. What happens to the optical rotation of a sugar solution when inversion (hydrolysis) takes place over time?

Explanation: During sugar inversion (e.g., sucrose breaking into glucose and fructose), the optical rotation changes progressively, a phenomenon used in polarimetry (saccharimetry).
