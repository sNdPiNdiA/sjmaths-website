Optics

Circular Polarization

Circular polarization is a state of optical polarization where the electric field vector of a light wave maintains a constant magnitude while continuously rotating in a plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation at an optical frequency. It is produced by the superposition of two orthogonal, phase-quadrature linear polarizations of equal amplitude.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of circularly polarized light, conditions for production using quarter-wave plates, and qualitative distinction between linear, circular, and elliptical polarization.

PGT extension

Mathematical description of Jones vectors for circular polarization, phase difference condition (pi/2 or odd multiples), optical rotation, handedness (left-circular and right-circular), and birefringence in uniaxial crystals.

Prerequisites

  • Transverse nature of electromagnetic waves
  • Superposition principle of orthogonal harmonic oscillations
  • Double refraction and uniaxial crystals (ordinary and extraordinary rays)

Definition and Wave Characteristics

  • The electric field vector traces out a circle as a function of time at a given point in space, or as a function of position at a given instant.
  • The tip of the electric field vector rotates with an angular frequency equal to the angular frequency of the light wave, omega.
  • The magnitude of the electric field remains constant: Ex2 + Ey2 = E02.

Conditions for Production

  • Two mutually orthogonal linear polarization components (Ex and Ey) must have equal amplitudes.
  • There must be a constant phase difference of delt$a = (2n + 1)\pi/2 between the two orthogonal components$, where n is an integer.
  • Typically achieved by passing plane-polarized light through a quarter-wave plate with its optic axis oriented at 45 degrees to the incident polarization azimuth.

Handedness (Right- and Left-Circular Polarization)

  • Right-Circularly Polarized (RCP) light corresponds to a clockwise rotation of the electric vector when viewed facing the oncoming wave.
  • Left-Circularly Polarized (LCP) light corresponds to a counter-clockwise rotation of the electric vector when viewed facing the oncoming wave.
  • In IEEE convention, RCP is designated by a phase delay of Ey relative to Ex by +π/2.

Detection and Analysis

  • Circularly polarized light cannot be distinguished from unpolarized light using a single rotating polarizer alone, as the transmitted intensity remains constant.
  • A quarter-wave plate is used to convert circularly polarized light back into linearly polarized light, which can then be completely extinguished by an analyzer.
  • Combination of a quarter-wave plate and a linear analyzer uniquely identifies circular polarization.

Derivations

Mathematical Derivation of Circular Polarization

Conditions: Two orthogonal linear waves of equal amplitude E0 and a phase difference of π/2

  • Write the x-component of the electric field: E_x(z, t) = E_0 \cos(\omega t - kz)
  • Write the y-component with a phase lead of π/2: E_y(z, t) = E_0 \cos(\omega t - kz + \frac{\pi}{2}) = -E_0 \sin(\omega t - kz)
  • Square both components and add them: E_x^2 + $E_y^2 = E_0^2 \cos^2(\omega t - kz) + E_0^2 \sin^2(\omega t - kz) = E_0^2$

Result: E_x^2 + $E_y^2 = E_0^2$, which represents the equation of a circle in the xy-plane.

Action of a Quarter-Wave Plate

Conditions: Incident linearly polarized light at 45 degrees to the optic axis of a negative uniaxial crystal

  • Resolve incident amplitude E0 into components parallel (E||) and perpendicular (E⊥) to the optic axis: $E_x = E_0 \cos(45^\circ)$ and $E_y = E_0 \sin(45^\circ)$
  • Introduce a phase difference Δ$\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(n_e - n_o)d = \frac{\pi}{2} due$ to the thickness d of the quarter-wave plate
  • The emergent orthogonal components have equal amplitude and a π/2 phase difference, satisfying the circular polarization condition.

Result: Emergent beam is circularly polarized.

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the minimum thickness of a quarter-wave plate for light of wavelength 589 nm, given that the ordinary and extraordinary refractive indices are $n_o = 1.544$ and $n_e = 1.553$.

  • Identify given values: $\lambda = 589 nm = 589 \times 10^{-9} m$, $n_o = 1.544$, $n_e = 1.553$
  • Use the quarter-wave plate thickness formula: $d = \frac{\lambda}{4(n_e - n_o)}$
  • Substitute the values: $d = \frac{589 \times 10^{-9}}{4(1.553 - 1.544)} = \frac{589 \times 10^{-9}}{4(0.009)}$
  • Calculate d: $d = \frac{589 \times 10^{-9}}{0.036} = 1.636 \times 10^{-5} m$

Answer: 1.636 \times 10^{-5} m (or 16.36 \mu m)

PGT extension

The electric field of a monochromatic light wave is given by \vec{E} = E_0 [\hat{i} \cos(\omega t - kz) - \hat{j} \sin(\omega t - kz)]. Determine the state of polarization and its handedness.

  • Identify components: $E_x = E_0 \cos(\omega t - kz)$ and $E_y = -E_0 \sin(\omega t - kz)$
  • Check amplitudes: Both components have equal amplitude E_0.
  • Check phase relationship: E_y can be written as E_0 \cos(\omega t - kz + \pi/2), meaning E_y leads E_x by π/2.
  • Determine rotation direction: At z = 0, at t = 0, $E = E_0 \hat{i}$. At \omega $t = \pi/2$, $E = -E_0 \hat{j}$. The vector rotates clockwise when facing the source.

Answer: Right-Circularly Polarized (RCP) light

PGT extension

A beam of circularly polarized light of intensity I_0 is incident normally on a rotating linear polarizer. What is the intensity of the transmitted light as a function of time, and what is its average value?

  • Circularly polarized light has an electric field vector of constant magnitude E_0 rotating at angular frequency ω.
  • When passing through a linear polarizer, only the component parallel to the transmission axis is transmitted: E(t) = E_0 \cos(\omega t).
  • Transmitted intensity is proportional to the square of the electric field: I(t) = I_0 \cos^2(\omega t), where I_0 is the maximum intensity.
  • Take the time average over one complete cycle: \langle \cos^2(\omega t) $\rangle = 1/2$.

Answer: I(t) = I_0 \cos^2(\omega t) with a time-averaged intensity of I_0 / 2

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes monochromatic light waves of stable frequency and well-defined phase.
  • Birefringent crystals are treated as homogeneous and uniaxial with fixed principal refractive indices.
  • Multiple internal reflections within the wave plate are neglected.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Circularly polarized light can be identified by passing it through a single rotating polarizer and observing extinction.

Correct: A single rotating polarizer yields constant intensity for both unpolarized and circularly polarized light.

A single polarizer cannot introduce the phase retardation needed to analyze circular polarization; a quarter-wave plate must precede the polarizer.

Common error: The magnitude of the electric field vector changes as it rotates in circular polarization.

Correct: The magnitude of the electric field vector remains strictly constant in circular polarization.

A constant magnitude with a continuously changing direction defines the circle in the transverse plane; varying magnitude results in elliptical polarization.

Quick revision

  • Circular polarization requires two orthogonal linear components of equal amplitude and a π/2 phase difference.
  • A quarter-wave plate introduces a π/2 phase shift to convert linear polarization to circular polarization.
  • The intensity of circularly polarized light does not change upon rotating a single linear analyzer.

Must remember

  • In circular polarization, the electric field vector maintains a constant magnitude while its direction rotates continuously in the transverse plane.
  • The essential conditions for producing circular polarization are two mutually orthogonal linear components with equal amplitudes and a phase difference of $\Delta = (2n + 1)\pi/2$.
  • A quarter-wave plate introduces a phase retardation of $\pi/2$ (or odd multiples) between ordinary and extraordinary rays, converting linear polarization to circular polarization and vice versa.
  • Circularly polarized light cannot be distinguished from unpolarized light by a single rotating polarizer because the transmitted intensity remains constant in both cases.
  • Right-Circularly Polarized (RCP) light corresponds to clockwise rotation, whereas Left-Circularly Polarized (LCP) light corresponds to counter-clockwise rotation when viewed facing the oncoming wave.

Glossary

Circular PolarizationA polarization state where the electric field vector traces out a circle as a function of time or position, with a constant magnitude.
Quarter-Wave Plate (QWP)An optical device made of a birefringent crystal cut parallel to its optic axis, designed to introduce a $\pi/2$ phase shift between orthogonal polarization components.
Jones VectorA mathematical matrix representation used in optics to describe the polarization state of plane electromagnetic waves.
BirefringenceThe optical property of a material having a refractive index that depends on the polarization and propagation direction of light (difference between $n_e$ and $n_o$).
HandednessThe directional sense of rotation of the electric field vector, categorized into Right-Circularly Polarized (RCP) and Left-Circularly Polarized (LCP).

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the locus of the electric field vector tip in a circularly polarized light wave?

Explanation: In circular polarization, the electric field vector traces out a circle as a function of time or position due to equal amplitudes and a $\pi/2$ phase difference.

2. What phase difference between two mutually orthogonal linear components of equal amplitude is required to produce circular polarization?

Explanation: Circular polarization requires a phase difference of odd multiples of $\pi/2$, i.e., $(2n + 1)\pi/2$, along with equal amplitudes for $E_x$ and $E_y$.

3. Which optical component is typically used to convert circularly polarized light into linearly polarized light?

Explanation: A quarter-wave plate introduces a $\pi/2$ phase shift that undoes the phase retardation of circular polarization, converting it back into linear polarization.

4. Why can circularly polarized light not be distinguished from unpolarized light using a single rotating polarizer?

Explanation: A single rotating polarizer yields a constant transmitted intensity for both unpolarized and circularly polarized light because time-averaged projections remain uniform.

5. What is the mathematical condition for the magnitude of the electric field vector in circular polarization?

Explanation: The magnitude of the electric field vector remains constant, satisfying the equation of a circle: $E_x^2 + E_y^2 = E_0^2$.

6. For Right-Circularly Polarized (RCP) light, how does the electric vector rotate when viewed facing the oncoming wave?

Explanation: Right-Circularly Polarized (RCP) light corresponds to a clockwise rotation of the electric vector when viewed facing the oncoming wave.

7. What is the Jones vector representation for Right Circular Polarization in the standard Cartesian basis?

Explanation: The standard normalized Jones vector for Right Circular Polarization (RCP) is $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -i \end{pmatrix}$.

8. What is the correct expression for the thickness $d$ of a quarter-wave plate?

Explanation: The thickness of a quarter-wave plate providing a $\pi/2$ phase retardation is given by $d = \frac{\lambda}{4|n_e - n_o|}$.

9. Which statement regarding the magnitude of the electric field in circular polarization is correct?

Explanation: The magnitude of the electric field vector remains constant in circular polarization, while only its direction changes continuously.

10. What orientation of the optic axis of a quarter-wave plate relative to the incident plane-polarized light azimuth is required to produce circular polarization?

Explanation: Passing plane-polarized light through a quarter-wave plate with its optic axis oriented at $45^{\circ}$ to the incident polarization azimuth produces circular polarization.

11. What is the Jones vector for Left-Circularly Polarized (LCP) light?

Explanation: The normalized Jones vector for Left Circular Polarization (LCP) is $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ i \end{pmatrix}$.

12. What kind of medium is required to construct a quarter-wave plate?

Explanation: A uniaxial birefringent crystal (having different refractive indices $n_e$ and $n_o$ for ordinary and extraordinary rays) is used to create wave plates.

13. What is the expression for the time-averaged intensity of circularly polarized light in terms of $E_0$?

Explanation: The average intensity over one optical cycle for a circularly polarized wave is given by $I = \frac{1}{2} \varepsilon_0 c E_0^2$.

14. In Left-Circularly Polarized (LCP) light, how does the electric field vector rotate when viewed facing the source?

Explanation: LCP light corresponds to a counter-clockwise rotation of the electric vector when viewed facing the oncoming wave.

15. What combination of optical elements is required to uniquely identify and analyze circular polarization?

Explanation: A quarter-wave plate converts circular polarization back to linear polarization, which can then be completely extinguished by a subsequent linear analyzer.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. If two mutually perpendicular electric field components $E_x = E_0 \cos(\omega t - kz)$ and $E_y = E_0 \sin(\omega t - kz)$ combine, what is the resulting polarization state?

Explanation: Equal amplitudes and a phase difference of $\pi/2$ between $E_x$ and $E_y$ produce circularly polarized light.

2. What phase retardation is introduced by a quarter-wave plate for light passing normally through it?

Explanation: By definition, a quarter-wave plate introduces a phase retardation of $\pi/2$ between the ordinary and extraordinary ray components.

3. A beam of light passes through a polaroid and the intensity remains unchanged when the polaroid is rotated. The light could be:

Explanation: Both unpolarized and circularly polarized light yield a constant intensity when analyzed with a single rotating polarizer.

4. The thickness of a quartz quarter-wave plate for wavelength $\lambda = 589\text{ nm}$ with principal refractive indices $n_e = 1.553$ and $n_o = 1.544$ is proportional to:

Explanation: The standard formula for the thickness of a quarter-wave plate is $d = \frac{\lambda}{4|n_e - n_o|}$.

5. Which of the following Jones vectors represents circular polarization?

Explanation: The vector $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ i \end{pmatrix}$ has orthogonal components of equal magnitude with a $\pi/2$ phase difference, representing circular polarization.

6. In a uniaxial birefringent crystal, the phase difference $\Delta \phi$ introduced over a thickness $d$ for wavelength $\lambda$ is given by:

Explanation: Phase retardation is given by $\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi d}{\lambda}|n_e - n_o|$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What is the condition on the amplitudes of $E_x$ and $E_y$ for circular polarization?

Explanation: Circular polarization strictly requires the two mutually orthogonal linear components to have equal amplitudes ($E_x = E_y = E_0$).

2. Which optical device can be used to distinguish between circularly polarized light and unpolarized light?

Explanation: A quarter-wave plate converts circular light to linear light, which can then be extinguished by an analyzer; unpolarized light becomes partially polarized but does not extinguish completely in the same manner.

3. What happens to the electric field vector's tip in a left-circularly polarized wave over time at a fixed position in space?

Explanation: Left-circularly polarized light is defined by counter-clockwise rotation of the electric vector.

4. If the phase difference between two equal orthogonal components is $\Delta = \pi/2$, and $E_y$ lags $E_x$ by $\pi/2$, what is the polarization state?

Explanation: In IEEE convention, a phase delay of $E_y$ relative to $E_x$ by $+\pi/2$ corresponds to Right-Circularly Polarized (RCP) light.

5. Which parameter among the following remains invariant for a circularly polarized wave propagating in free space?

Explanation: While the direction of the electric field vector rotates continuously, its magnitude $E_0$ remains strictly constant.

6. What is the primary material property required to create a quarter-wave plate?

Explanation: Birefringence (having different refractive indices for ordinary and extraordinary rays) is essential for creating wave plates.

7. For a wave described by $E_x = E_0 \cos(\omega t - kz)$ and $E_y = E_0 \sin(\omega t - kz)$, what is $E_x^2 + E_y^2$?

Explanation: $E_x^2 + E_y^2 = E_0^2(\cos^2(\omega t - kz) + \sin^2(\omega t - kz)) = E_0^2$.

8. In terms of Jones calculus, which operation represents the transformation by an ideal quarter-wave plate with fast axis horizontal?

Explanation: A quarter-wave plate with its fast axis vertical/horizontal introduces a $\pi/2$ phase shift represented by multiplying one component by $i$ (or $-i$). Specifically, with fast axis horizontal, $E_y$ is delayed by $\pi/2$, giving $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -i \end{pmatrix}$ or similar convention depending on axis definition.

9. How does circular polarization differ from elliptical polarization in terms of component amplitudes?

Explanation: Circular polarization is a special case of elliptical polarization where the orthogonal component amplitudes are strictly equal ($E_x = E_y$).

10. What is the unit of the wave propagation constant $k$ in the expression $E_x = E_0 \cos(\omega t - kz)$?

Explanation: The propagation constant $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$ has units of radians per meter ($\text{rad/m}$).
