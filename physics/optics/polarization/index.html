Polarization of Light

Polarization is the restriction of the vibration of electric field vectors of a transverse wave to a single plane. It provides direct, unambiguous evidence of the transverse nature of light waves.

UP TGT ScienceUP PGT PhysicsStudy Notes + Practice

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Basic concepts of transverse nature, unpolarized versus linearly polarized light, Malus's Law, Brewster's Law, and polarization by reflection and simple refraction.

PGT extension

Double refraction in uniaxial crystals, ordinary and extraordinary rays, Nicol prism, production and analysis of circularly and elliptically polarized light, optical activity, and specific rotation.

Prerequisites

  • Transverse and longitudinal wave kinematics
  • Maxwell's electromagnetic wave equations
  • Snell's law and refractive index

Nature of Unpolarized and Polarized Light

  • Unpolarized light contains rapid, random changes in the orientation of the transverse electric field vector E with equal probability in all directions perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
  • Linearly polarized light possesses an electric field vector that oscillates along one fixed, specific straight line transverse to the propagation direction.
  • Natural light sources like the Sun or incandescent lamps emit unpolarized light due to independent atomic emissions with random phases.

Polarization by Reflection and Brewster's Law

  • When unpolarized light is incident on a transparent dielectric surface at a specific angle called the polarizing angle or Brewster's angle (p), the reflected light is completely plane-polarized.
  • At Brewster's angle, the reflected ray and the refracted (transmitted) ray are mutually perpendicular to each other.
  • Brewster's law states that the tangent of the polarizing angle equals the refractive index of the reflecting medium relative to the surrounding medium.

Double Refraction (Birefringence) and Uniaxial Crystals

  • Anisotropic crystals like calcite and quartz split an unpolarized incident ray into two refracted rays: the Ordinary ray (O-ray) and the Extraordinary ray (E-ray).
  • The O-ray obeys ordinary Snell's law of refraction and travels with the same speed in all directions within the crystal.
  • The E-ray does not obey Snell's law, its velocity depends on the direction of propagation inside the crystal, and it has a variable refractive index.

Production and Detection of Polarized Light

  • Polarizers act as optical filters that transmit only those electric field components parallel to their transmission axis.
  • Malus's Law governs the intensity of transmitted light when a plane-polarized beam passes through an analyzer.
  • Quarter-wave plates and half-wave plates introduce phase differences of pi/2 and pi respectively between O-rays and E-rays to manipulate polarization states.

Derivations

Brewster's Law Derivation

Conditions: Light incident from a rarer medium of refractive index n_1 onto a denser dielectric medium of refractive index n_2 at polarizing angle i_p.

  • Write Snell's law at the interface: n_1 \sin(i_p) = n_2 \sin(r), where r is the angle of refraction.
  • Apply the condition that at Brewster's angle, the reflected ray and refracted ray are perpendicular: i_p + r = 90 degrees, which gives r = 90 degrees - i_p.
  • Substitute r into Snell's law: n_1 \sin(i_p) = n_2 \sin(90^circ - i_p) = n_2 \cos(i_p).
  • Rearrange to find the ratio of sine and cosine: \frac{\sin(i_p)}{\cos(i_p)} = \frac{n_2}{n_1}.

Result: \tan(i_p) = n_{21}

Malus's Law Derivation

Conditions: Plane-polarized light of initial amplitude E_0 incident on an analyzer whose transmission axis makes an angle theta with the vibration direction of the incident light.

  • Resolve the incident electric field amplitude E_0 into two rectangular components: E_0 \cos(\theta) parallel to the transmission axis and E_0 \sin(\theta) perpendicular to it.
  • The analyzer absorbs or blocks the perpendicular component E_0 \sin(\theta), transmitting only the parallel component E = E_0 \cos(\theta).
  • Recall that the intensity of a light wave is directly proportional to the square of its electric field amplitude: I \propto E^2 and I_0 \propto E_0^2.
  • Substitute the transmitted amplitude: I \propto (E_0 \cos(\theta))^2, leading directly to I = I_0 \cos^2(\theta).

Result: I = I_0 \cos^2(\theta)

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the polarizing angle for glass of refractive index 1.5.

  • Recall Brewster's law: \tan(i_p) = n.
  • Substitute the given value: \tan(i_p) = 1.5.
  • Find the inverse tangent: i_p = \arctan(1.5).

Answer: 56.3 degrees

TGT core

Unpolarized light of intensity 32 W/m^2 passes through a polarizer. What is the intensity of the transmitted light?

  • A polarizer reduces unpolarized light intensity to half its initial value.
  • Use formula: I = \frac{1}{2} I_0.
  • Substitute I_0 = 32 W/m^2: I = 32 / 2 = 16 W/m^2.

Answer: 16 W/m^2

PGT extension

Plane-polarized light passes through an analyzer whose axis is rotated by 60 degrees relative to the polarizer. What fraction of the initial polarized light intensity is transmitted?

  • Apply Malus's law: I = I_0 \cos^2(\theta).
  • Substitute \theta = 60 degrees: \cos(60^\circ) = 1/2.
  • Calculate intensity ratio: I / I_0 = (1/2)^2 = 1/4.

Answer: 0.25 (or 25% of initial intensity)

Assumptions and limitations

  • Polarizers are assumed to be ideal, absorbing 100% of the orthogonal component and transmitting 100% of the parallel component without internal absorption losses.
  • Dielectric media are assumed isotropic and non-magnetic for Brewster's law unless specified otherwise.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Sound waves can be polarized just like light waves.

Correct: Sound waves are longitudinal and cannot be polarized.

Polarization requires transverse oscillations occurring perpendicular to the direction of energy propagation.

Common error: Unpolarized light has no electric field oscillations.

Correct: Unpolarized light has rapid, randomly fluctuating electric field oscillations in all transverse directions.

Zero polarization means absence of preferred orientation, not absence of electric fields.

Common error: At Brewster's angle, the reflected and refracted rays have the same intensity.

Correct: At Brewster's angle, the reflected ray is completely polarized, but its intensity is generally much smaller than that of the refracted ray.

Reflectance and transmittance depend on Fresnel's equations, not on equal energy splitting.

Quick revision

  • Light is a transverse electromagnetic wave, proven conclusively by polarization.
  • Brewster's law: \tan(i_p) = n_2 / n_1; reflected and refracted rays are mutually perpendicular.
  • Malus's law: Intensity varies as I = I_0 \cos^2(\theta).
  • Unpolarized light passing through a single ideal polarizer loses exactly half its intensity.

Must remember

  • Polarization is an exclusive property of transverse waves, confirming that light waves are transverse in nature.
  • Brewster's Law states that $\tan(i_p) = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$, and at this polarizing angle, the reflected and refracted rays are mutually perpendicular ($i_p + r = 90^\circ$).
  • When unpolarized light passes through an ideal linear polarizer, its intensity is reduced exactly by half ($I = \frac{1}{2} I_0$).
  • Malus's Law dictates that transmitted intensity through an analyzer varies as $I = I_0 \cos^2(\theta)$.
  • Double refraction in uniaxial crystals splits unpolarized light into an Ordinary ray (O-ray, obeying Snell's law) and an Extraordinary ray (E-ray, variable velocity).
  • A quarter-wave plate introduces a phase difference of $\frac{\pi}{2}$ between the O-ray and E-ray to produce circularly or elliptically polarized light.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following waves cannot be polarized?

Explanation: Sound waves in air are longitudinal waves, meaning their oscillations occur along the direction of propagation. Polarization is exclusively a property of transverse waves.

2. Unpolarized light of intensity $$I_{0}$$ is incident on a linear polarizer. What is the intensity of the transmitted light?

Explanation: Unpolarized light has electric field components distributed equally in all transverse directions. Averaging $\cos^2(\$\theta$)$ over all random angles yields $\frac{1}{2}$, so the transmitted intensity is $I = \frac{1}{2} $I_{0}$$.

3. According to Brewster's law, what is the relation between the polarizing angle $i_p$ and the refractive index $n$ of a dielectric medium (assuming surrounding medium is air with $n_1 = 1$)?

Explanation: Brewster's law states that the tangent of the polarizing angle equals the refractive index of the reflecting medium, i.e., $\tan(i_p) = n$.

4. At Brewster's angle, what is the angle between the reflected ray and the refracted (transmitted) ray?

Explanation: At Brewster's angle, the reflected ray and the refracted ray are mutually perpendicular, making the angle between them exactly $90^\circ$.

5. Malus's Law is expressed mathematically as:

Explanation: Malus's Law states that when completely plane-polarized light is incident on an analyzer, the transmitted intensity varies directly as the square of the cosine of the angle between their transmission axes: $I = $I_{0}$ \cos^2(\$\theta$)$.

6. In a birefringent crystal, the Ordinary ray (O-ray) differs from the Extraordinary ray (E-ray) because:

Explanation: The O-ray obeys ordinary Snell's law of refraction and travels with the same speed in all directions inside the crystal, possessing a constant refractive index $n_o$.

7. What phase difference is introduced between the O-ray and E-ray by a quarter-wave plate?

Explanation: A quarter-wave plate is specifically designed to introduce a phase difference of $\frac{\$\pi$}{2}$ (or an optical path difference of $\frac{\lambda}{4}$) between the ordinary and extraordinary rays.

8. If the angle between the polarizer and analyzer transmission axes is $60^\circ$, what fraction of the incident polarized light intensity is transmitted?

Explanation: Using Malus's Law $I = $I_{0}$ \cos^2(60^\circ) = $I_{0}$ (\frac{1}{2})^2 = \frac{1}{4} $I_{0}$$. Thus, a quarter of the intensity is transmitted.

9. Optical rotation specific rotation $[\$\alpha$]_t^\lambda$ depends upon which of the following parameters?

Explanation: Specific rotation is calculated via $[\$\alpha$]_t^\lambda = \frac{\$\theta$}{l \cdot c}$, where $\$\theta$$ is rotation angle, $l$ is path length in decimeters, and $c$ is concentration.

10. Which crystal is commonly cited as a classic example of an uniaxial birefringent crystal exhibiting double refraction?

Explanation: Calcite ($CaCO_3$) and quartz are classic anisotropic uniaxial crystals widely used to demonstrate double refraction.

11. What is the degree of polarization for unpolarized light?

Explanation: Unpolarized light has equal intensity in all transverse directions, making $I_{max} = I_{min}$, leading to a degree of polarization $P = \frac{I_{max} - I_{min}}{I_{max} + I_{min}} = 0$.

12. The refractive index of a glass medium is $1.732$ (which is $\sqrt{3}$). What is its Brewster's polarizing angle?

Explanation: Using $\tan(i_p) = n = \sqrt{3}$, we find $i_p = \arctan(\sqrt{3}) = 60^\circ$.

13. What thickness $t$ is required for a quarter-wave plate for wavelength $\lambda$ where ordinary and extraordinary refractive indices are $n_o$ and $n_e$?

Explanation: Equating the phase shift formula $\delta = \frac{2\$\pi$}{\lambda}(n_o - n_e)t = \frac{\$\pi$}{2}$ gives the thickness $t = \frac{\lambda}{4(n_o - n_e)}$.

14. Which of the following statements regarding unpolarized light is correct?

Explanation: Unpolarized light features rapid, random changes in the orientation of the transverse electric field vector with equal probability in all directions perpendicular to propagation.

15. If a polarizer and analyzer are crossed (their transmission axes are at $90^\circ$ to each other), what is the transmitted intensity of incident plane-polarized light?

Explanation: When polarizer and analyzer are crossed, $\$\theta$ = 90^\circ$, so $I = $I_{0}$ \cos^2(90^\circ) = 0$, resulting in complete extinction (zero intensity).

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. Light is incident on a transparent medium at Brewster's angle $i_p$. If the refractive index of the medium is $n = 1.5$, the angle of refraction $r$ is:

Explanation: At Brewster's angle, $i_p + r = 90^\circ$. Also, $\tan(i_p) = 1.5 \implies i_p \approx 56.3^\circ$. Therefore, $r = 90^\circ - 56.3^\circ = 33.7^\circ$.

2. Two polaroids are placed with their transmission axes parallel. By what angle must one polaroid be rotated so that the transmitted intensity becomes one-fourth of the maximum intensity?

Explanation: According to Malus's Law, $I = $I_{0}$ \cos^2(\$\theta$)$. We are given $I = \frac{1}{4} $I_{0}$$, so $\cos^2(\$\theta$) = \frac{1}{4} \implies \cos(\$\theta$) = \frac{1}{2} \implies \$\theta$ = 60^\circ$.

3. Unpolarized light of intensity $16 \text{ W/m}^2$ is passed through a sequence of two ideal polarizers whose transmission axes make an angle of $30^\circ$ with each other. The final transmitted intensity is:

Explanation: After the first polarizer, unpolarized light becomes polarized with intensity $$I_{1}$ = \frac{16}{2} = 8 \text{ W/m}^2$. Passing through the second polarizer at $\$\theta$ = 30^\circ$: $$I_{2}$ = 8 \cos^2(30^\circ) = 8 \times (\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})^2 = 8 \times \frac{3}{4} = 6 \text{ W/m}^2$.

4. The refractive indices for ordinary and extraordinary rays in a calcite crystal are $n_o = 1.658$ and $n_e = 1.486$ for sodium light of wavelength $\lambda = 589 \text{ nm}$. What is the nature of this crystal?

Explanation: Since $n_e (1.486) < n_o (1.658)$, the velocity of the extraordinary ray is greater than that of the ordinary ray in directions perpendicular to the optic axis, classifying calcite as a negative uniaxial crystal.

5. A beam of unpolarized light falls on a glass plate at Brewster's angle. If the refractive index of glass is $1.5$, what is the angle of incidence?

Explanation: Brewster's angle is defined by $\tan(i_p) = n$, which means the angle of incidence is $i_p = \arctan(1.5)$.

6. If the degree of polarization of a partially polarized beam of light is $0.6$, and the maximum intensity transmitted by a rotating analyzer is $80 \text{ W/m}^2$, what is the minimum intensity transmitted?

Explanation: Using $P = \frac{I_{max} - I_{min}}{I_{max} + I_{min}}$, substitute $P = 0.6$ and $I_{max} = 80$: $0.6 = \frac{80 - I_{min}}{80 + I_{min}} \implies 48 + 0.6 I_{min} = 80 - I_{min} \implies 1.6 I_{min} = 32 \implies I_{min} = 20 \text{ W/m}^2$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which phenomenon provides direct and conclusive evidence of the transverse nature of light waves?

Explanation: Polarization is unique to transverse waves because it requires asymmetry perpendicular to the direction of wave travel, conclusively proving light is transverse.

2. At the polarizing angle, sunlight reflected from a calm lake is:

Explanation: When unpolarized light reflects off a dielectric surface at Brewster's angle, the reflected ray is completely plane-polarized with its electric field vector parallel to the reflecting surface.

3. A half-wave plate introduces what phase difference between the ordinary and extraordinary rays?

Explanation: A half-wave plate introduces a phase difference of $\$\pi$$ (or an optical path difference of $\frac{\lambda}{2}$) between the O-ray and E-ray.

4. In a uniaxial crystal, the ray that travels with a constant velocity in all directions and obeys Snell's law is the:

Explanation: The Ordinary ray (O-ray) obeys ordinary Snell's law of refraction and maintains a constant speed and refractive index in all directions within the crystal.

5. If the angle between the polarizer and analyzer transmission axes is $45^\circ$, what percentage of the incoming plane-polarized intensity is transmitted?

Explanation: Using Malus's Law, $I = $I_{0}$ \cos^2(45^\circ) = $I_{0}$ (\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})^2 = 0.5 $I_{0}$$, which corresponds to $50\%$ transmission.

6. Which of the following conditions is necessary for Brewster's Law to hold precisely?

Explanation: Brewster's Law $\tan(i_p) = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$ is standardly valid for non-magnetic dielectric media where relative permeability $\mu_r \approx 1$.

7. Specific rotation of an optically active substance is measured in units of:

Explanation: From $[\$\alpha$]_t^\lambda = \frac{\$\theta$}{l \cdot c}$, rotation angle $\$\theta$$ is in degrees, path length $l$ in decimeters, and concentration $c$ in $\text{g/cm}^3$, giving $\text{degrees} \cdot \text{dm}^{-1} \cdot (\text{g/cm}^3)^{-1}$.

8. What happens to the intensity of light passing through a single ideal polarizer when unpolarized light is incident upon it, regardless of the polarizer's orientation?

Explanation: A single polarizer reduces the intensity of unpolarized light by half ($I = \frac{1}{2}$I_{0}$$) due to the averaging of transverse electric field components ($\cos^2(\$\theta$) = 1/2$), regardless of orientation.

9. Double refraction in uniaxial crystals splits an unpolarized incident ray into:

Explanation: Anisotropic uniaxial crystals split an unpolarized ray into two orthogonally polarized refracted rays: the Ordinary ray and the Extraordinary ray.

10. If the phase difference $\delta$ between two mutually perpendicular equal-amplitude electric field components is $\frac{\$\pi$}{2}$ and their amplitudes are equal, the resulting polarization state is:

Explanation: Superposition of two perpendicular electric field oscillations of equal amplitude with a phase difference of $\frac{\$\pi$}{2}$ produces circularly polarized light.
