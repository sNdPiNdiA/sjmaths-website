Double Refraction and Polarization in Anisotropic Crystals

Double refraction is the optical phenomenon in which an unpolarized light beam incident on an anisotropic crystal, such as calcite, splits into two refracted rays known as the ordinary and extraordinary rays. These two rays travel with different velocities and are polarized in mutually perpendicular planes.

Phenomenology of Double Refraction

  • When a ray of light is incident on an anisotropic crystal like calcite (CaCO3), it divides into two refracted rays: the Ordinary (O) ray and the Extraordinary (E) ray.
  • The O-ray obeys ordinary laws of refraction, its wavefront is spherical, and its refractive index (μo) is constant in all directions.
  • The E-ray does not obey Snell's law in general, its wavefront is ellipsoidal, and its refractive index (μe) varies with the angle of incidence relative to the optic axis.

Optic Axis and Principal Section

  • The optic axis is a specific direction inside the anisotropic crystal along which the velocities of the O-ray and E-ray are identical, resulting in no double refraction.
  • Uniaxial crystals possess a single optic axis, whereas biaxial crystals possess two optic axes.
  • The principal section of a crystal is a plane passing through the optic axis and perpendicular to the opposite faces of the crystal.

Positive and Negative Uniaxial Crystals

  • In negative uniaxial crystals (e.g., calcite), the velocity of the E-ray is greater than that of the O-ray for directions other than the optic axis, meaning μe < μo.
  • In positive uniaxial crystals (e.g., quartz), the velocity of the E-ray is less than that of the O-ray, meaning μe > μo.
  • At the optic axis itself, the velocities of both rays are equal: vo = ve.

Huygens Wavefront Construction

  • According to Huygens' principle, each point on a wavefront inside a birefringent crystal acts as a source of secondary disturbances giving rise to two wavelets.
  • The spherical wavelet corresponds to the O-ray, spreading out uniformly with velocity vo.
  • The ellipsoidal wavelet corresponds to the E-ray, where the sphere and ellipsoid touch at points lying along the optic axis.

Derivations

Refractive Index Relationship for Extraordinary Ray in Uniaxial Crystals

Conditions: Light propagating at an angle &theta; to the optic axis in a uniaxial crystal

  • Consider an incident plane wavefront striking the crystal surface containing the optic axis.
  • Write the equation of the spherical wavefront for the O-ray: x2 + y2 + z2 = vo2t2.
  • Write the equation of the ellipsoidal wavefront for the E-ray with the optic axis along the z-axis: \frac{x2+y2}{v_{o}2} + \frac{z2}{v_{e}2} = t2.
  • Express the effective velocity vθ of the E-ray at an angle θ to the optic axis using coordinate transformations.
  • Substitute spherical polar coordinates to relate the angular variation of the extraordinary refractive index.

Result: \frac{1}{\mu_{\theta}^{2}} = \frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\mu_{e}^{2}} + \frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{\mu_{o}^{2}}

Thickness of a Quarter-Wave Plate

Conditions: Normal incidence of monochromatic light of wavelength &lambda;<sub>0</sub>

  • The phase difference Δφ introduced between the O-ray and E-ray after traversing a thickness d is given by \Delta\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{0}} (| \mu_{o} - \mu_{e} |) d.
  • For a quarter-wave plate, the required phase retardation is Δφ = π / 2.
  • Equate the phase expression: \frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{0}} (| \mu_{o} - \mu_{e} |) d = \frac{\pi}{2}.
  • Solve for the thickness d.

Result: d = \frac{\lambda_{0}}{4 |\mu_{o} - \mu_{e}|}

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the thickness of a calcite quarter-wave plate for light of wavelength 589 nm. Given &mu;<sub>o</sub> = 1.658 and &mu;<sub>e</sub> = 1.486.

  • Identify given values: \lambda_{0} = 589 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}, \mu_{o} = 1.658, \mu_{e} = 1.486.
  • Use the formula for quarter-wave plate thickness: d = \frac{\lambda_{0}}{4 |\mu_{o} - \mu_{e}|}.
  • Substitute the values: d = \frac{589 \times 10^{-9}}{4 |1.658 - 1.486|}.
  • Calculate the difference: |1.658 - 1.486| = 0.172.
  • Perform division: d = \frac{589 \times 10^{-9}}{4 \times 0.172} = \frac{589 \times 10^{-9}}{0.688} = 856.23 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}.

Answer: 856.2 nm

PGT extension

Light of wavelength 600 nm is incident normally on a quartz crystal acting as a half-wave plate. For quartz, &mu;<sub>o</sub> = 1.544 and &mu;<sub>e</sub> = 1.553. Find the minimum thickness of the plate.

  • Identify given parameters: \lambda_{0} = 600 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}, \mu_{o} = 1.544, \mu_{e} = 1.553.
  • Use the half-wave plate formula: d = \frac{\lambda_{0}}{2 |\mu_{o} - \mu_{e}|}.
  • Find the absolute difference: |1.544 - 1.553| = 0.009.
  • Substitute into the equation: d = \frac{600 \times 10^{-9}}{2 \times 0.009} = \frac{600 \times 10^{-9}}{0.018}.
  • Calculate final value: d = 33333.3 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m} = 33.33 \mu\text{m}.

Answer: 33.33 μm

PGT extension

Find the refractive index for the extraordinary ray propagating at an angle of 30 degrees to the optic axis in calcite, given &mu;<sub>o</sub> = 1.658 and &mu;<sub>e</sub> = 1.486.

  • Write the governing formula: \frac{1}{\mu_{\theta}^{2}} = \frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\mu_{e}^{2}} + \frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{\mu_{o}^{2}}.
  • Substitute \theta = 30^{\circ}, \mu_{o} = 1.658, \mu_{e} = 1.486. Note that \sin(30^{\circ}) = 0.5 and \cos(30^{\circ}) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.
  • Calculate \sin^{2}(30^{\circ}) = 0.25 and \cos^{2}(30^{\circ}) = 0.75.
  • Compute individual terms: \frac{0.25}{(1.486)^{2}} = \frac{0.25}{2.2082} \approx 0.1132 and \frac{0.75}{(1.658)^{2}} = \frac{0.75}{2.7489} \approx 0.2728.
  • Sum the terms: \frac{1}{\mu_{\theta}^{2}} = 0.1132 + 0.2728 = 0.3860.
  • Invert and take the square root: \mu_{\theta}^{2} = \frac{1}{0.3860} = 2.5906, \mu_{\theta} = \sqrt{2.5906} = 1.6095.

Answer: 1.610

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes homogeneous and uniaxial anisotropic media unless stated otherwise.
  • Neglects internal absorption and dichroism within the crystal body.
  • Valid only for monochromatic light approximations when calculating exact retardation plate thicknesses.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The ordinary ray travels faster than the extraordinary ray in all crystals.

Correct: The relative speeds depend on whether the crystal is positive or negative; in positive crystals like quartz, the O-ray travels faster than the E-ray.

In positive uniaxial crystals, &mu;<sub>e</sub> > &mu;<sub>o</sub>, implying v<sub>e</sub> < v<sub>o</sub>.

Common error: Double refraction violates the law of conservation of energy.

Correct: The incident beam energy is conserved and simply redistributed between the orthogonally polarized O-ray and E-ray components.

Intensity is split based on the angle of incidence and polarization state relative to the principal plane.

Common error: The optic axis is a single unique physical line drawn inside the crystal.

Correct: The optic axis represents a specific optical direction, not a single physical line; any line parallel to this direction has identical optical properties.

Crystals exhibit translational symmetry regarding optical anisotropy.

Quick revision

  • Calcite is a negative uniaxial crystal with μo = 1.658 and μe = 1.486.
  • Canada balsam layer in a Nicol prism matches the O-ray's refractive index for total internal reflection.
  • Quarter-wave plates introduce a π/2 phase difference; half-wave plates introduce a π phase difference.

Must remember

  • When a ray of light is incident on an anisotropic crystal like calcite ($ ext{CaCO}_3$), it splits into an Ordinary (O) ray and an Extraordinary (E) ray.
  • The O-ray obeys ordinary laws of refraction, has a spherical wavefront, and constant refractive index $mu_o$.
  • The E-ray does not obey Snell's law in general, has an ellipsoidal wavefront, and its refractive index $mu_e$ varies with direction.
  • In negative uniaxial crystals (e.g., calcite), $v_e > v_o$ and $mu_e < mu_o$.
  • In positive uniaxial crystals (e.g., quartz), $v_e < v_o$ and $mu_e > mu_o$.
  • Along the optic axis, the velocities of both O-ray and E-ray are identical ($v_o = v_e$), resulting in no double refraction.
  • A Quarter-Wave Plate produces a phase shift of $pi/2$ and has a thickness $d_{QWP} = rac{lambda_0}{4 |mu_o - mu_e|}$.
  • A Half-Wave Plate produces a phase shift of $pi$ and has a thickness $d_{HWP} = rac{lambda_0}{2 |mu_o - mu_e|}$.

Glossary

Ordinary Ray (O-ray)A refracted ray in a birefringent crystal that obeys ordinary laws of refraction and travels with a constant speed in all directions.
Extraordinary Ray (E-ray)A refracted ray in a birefringent crystal that does not obey Snell's law and travels with a velocity dependent on the direction relative to the optic axis.
Optic AxisA specific direction inside an anisotropic crystal along which the velocities of the O-ray and E-ray are identical, causing no double refraction.
Negative Uniaxial CrystalA birefringent crystal in which the velocity of the E-ray is greater than that of the O-ray ($mu_e < mu_o$), such as calcite.
Positive Uniaxial CrystalA birefringent crystal in which the velocity of the E-ray is less than that of the O-ray ($mu_e > mu_o$), such as quartz.
Phase RetardationThe phase difference $Delta phi$ introduced between the O-ray and E-ray upon traversing a thickness $d$ of a birefringent crystal plate.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. When unpolarized light is incident on a calcite crystal, it splits into two rays known as:

Explanation: Anisotropic crystals exhibit double refraction, splitting the incident light into the Ordinary ($O$) ray and the Extraordinary ($E$) ray.

2. Which of the following rays obeys the ordinary laws of refraction (Snell's Law) in a calcite crystal?

Explanation: The Ordinary ($O$) ray strictly obeys ordinary laws of refraction and has a constant refractive index $mu_o$ in all directions.

3. What is the shape of the wavefront corresponding to the Ordinary ray inside a birefringent crystal according to Huygens' theory?

Explanation: The O-ray wavelet spreads uniformly in all directions with speed $v_o$, forming a spherical wavefront.

4. In a negative uniaxial crystal like calcite, the relationship between the refractive indices of the O-ray ($mu_o$) and E-ray ($mu_e$) is:

Explanation: In negative uniaxial crystals, the E-ray travels faster than the O-ray ($v_e > v_o$), which means its refractive index is smaller ($mu_e < mu_o$).

5. What is the primary characteristic of the optic axis in an anisotropic crystal?

Explanation: The optic axis is a specific direction along which $v_o = v_e$, resulting in zero double refraction.

6. In positive uniaxial crystals such as quartz, how do the velocities of the O-ray ($v_o$) and E-ray ($v_e$) compare?

Explanation: In positive uniaxial crystals, the E-ray travels slower than the O-ray, meaning $v_e < v_o$ and $mu_e > mu_o$.

7. The thickness of a quarter-wave plate for a vacuum wavelength $lambda_0$ is given by:

Explanation: A quarter-wave plate introduces a phase difference of $$\pi$/2$, corresponding to a thickness $d_{QWP} = rac{lambda_0}{4 |mu_o - mu_e|}.

8. What phase retardation is introduced by a half-wave plate?

Explanation: A half-wave plate introduces a phase shift of $$\pi$$ radians between the O-ray and E-ray components.

9. How many optic axes are present in a uniaxial crystal?

Explanation: By definition, uniaxial crystals possess a single optic axis, whereas biaxial crystals possess two.

10. The angular dependence of the extraordinary refractive index $mu_ heta$ at angle $ heta$ with the optic axis is given by:

Explanation: The ellipse equation for index variation yields $ rac{1}{mu_ heta^2} = rac{sin^2 heta}{mu_e^2} + rac{cos^2 heta}{mu_o^2}$ for uniaxial crystals.

11. What is the phase retardation $Delta $\phi$$ for normal incidence through a crystal plate of thickness $d$?

Explanation: Phase retardation is given by $Deltaphi = rac{2pi d}{lambda_0} |mu_o - mu_e|$.

12. Which crystal is a classic example of a negative uniaxial crystal?

Explanation: Calcite ($ ext{CaCO}_3$) is the quintessential negative uniaxial crystal where $mu_e < mu_o$.

13. Which crystal is a classic example of a positive uniaxial crystal?

Explanation: Quartz ($ ext{SiO}_2$) is a well-known positive uniaxial crystal where $mu_e > mu_o$.

14. According to Huygens' secondary wavelet construction, what is the geometric relationship between the O-sphere and E-ellipsoid in a negative uniaxial crystal?

Explanation: In negative uniaxial crystals ($v_e > v_o$), the spherical O-wavelet is enclosed by the ellipsoidal E-wavelet, touching only along the optic axis.

15. What is the principal section of a birefringent crystal?

Explanation: The principal section is defined as a plane containing the optic axis and oriented perpendicular to the crystal faces.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. If the ordinary and extraordinary refractive indices for calcite are approximately $mu_o = 1.658$ and $mu_e = 1.486$, what type of uniaxial crystal is calcite?

Explanation: Since $mu_e < mu_o$ ($1.486 < 1.658$), the velocity of the E-ray is greater than that of the O-ray, characterizing calcite as a negative uniaxial crystal.

2. Calculate the thickness of a quarter-wave plate for light of wavelength $lambda_0 = 5890 ext{ AA}$ given $mu_o = 1.658$ and $mu_e = 1.486$.

Explanation: Using $d_{QWP} = rac{lambda_0}{4 |mu_o - mu_e|} = rac{5890 imes 10^{-10}}{4 imes |1.658 - 1.486|} = rac{5890 imes 10^{-10}}{4 imes 0.172} approx 0.856 ext{ }mu ext{m}$.

3. Along the optic axis of a uniaxial crystal, the velocity of the O-ray ($v_o$) and the velocity of the E-ray ($v_e$) satisfy which condition?

Explanation: By definition, the optic axis is the direction along which the velocities of the O-ray and E-ray are identical ($v_o = v_e$).

4. What is the phase difference introduced by a half-wave plate between the ordinary and extraordinary rays?

Explanation: A half-wave plate is designed to introduce a phase retardation of $$\pi$$ radians (or a path difference of $lambda_0 / 2$).

5. Which of the following wave equations correctly represents the Huygens secondary E-wavelet in a uniaxial crystal relative to principal axes?

Explanation: The spheroidal wavefront equation for the E-ray is given by $ rac{X^2}{v_o^2 t^2} + rac{Z^2}{v_e^2 t^2} = 1$ where $Z$ is along the optic axis.

6. In a positive uniaxial crystal like quartz, the extraordinary refractive index $mu_e$ and ordinary refractive index $mu_o$ obey:

Explanation: In positive uniaxial crystals, $v_e < v_o$, which implies $mu_e = c/v_e > c/v_o = mu_o$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Double refraction in anisotropic crystals produces two rays that are:

Explanation: The O-ray and E-ray are polarized in mutually orthogonal planes (perpendicular to each other).

2. If a crystal plate has a thickness such that $Delta $\phi$ = $\pi$/2$, it functions as a:

Explanation: A phase shift of $$\pi$/2$ corresponds to a quarter-wave plate.

3. Which of the following statements about the ordinary ray is incorrect?

Explanation: The ordinary ray has a constant refractive index $mu_o$ independent of direction; angle dependence is a property of the E-ray.

4. The refractive index of the E-ray in a uniaxial crystal for propagation perpendicular to the optic axis ($ heta = $\pi$/2$) is:

Explanation: When light travels perpendicular to the optic axis ($ heta = $\pi$/2$), the extraordinary refractive index attains its principal extreme value $mu_e$.

5. What happens to unpolarized light traveling exactly along the optic axis of a calcite crystal?

Explanation: Along the optic axis, $v_o = v_e$, meaning there is no velocity difference and hence no double refraction.

6. In Huygens' construction for a positive uniaxial crystal, the O-sphere and E-ellipsoid touch at points along:

Explanation: The sphere and ellipsoid always touch at the points lying along the optic axis, where velocities are equal.

7. Calculate the phase retardation produced by a calcite plate of thickness $0.01 ext{ mm}$ for $lambda_0 = 589 ext{ nm}$, given $mu_o = 1.658$ and $mu_e = 1.486$.

Explanation: Using $Deltaphi = rac{2pi d}{lambda_0} |mu_o - mu_e| = rac{2pi imes 10^{-5} ext{ m}}{5.89 imes 10^{-7} ext{ m}} |1.658 - 1.486| approx rac{2pi imes 10^{-5}}{5.89 imes 10^{-7}} imes 0.172 approx 5.82 ext{ rad}$.

8. Biaxial crystals possess how many optic axes?

Explanation: By definition, biaxial crystals possess two optic axes.

9. If a half-wave plate is placed between crossed polarizers with its optic axis at $45^circ$ to the polarizer transmission axis, what is the effect on incident plane-polarized light?

Explanation: A half-wave plate rotates the plane of polarization of linearly polarized light by twice the angle between the incident polarization direction and the plate's optic axis ($2 imes 45^circ = 90^circ$).

10. Why does the extraordinary ray not obey Snell's law in general?

Explanation: Since the refractive index $mu_e$ depends on angle $ heta$ with the optic axis, the angle of refraction does not bear a constant sine ratio as dictated by standard Snell's law.
