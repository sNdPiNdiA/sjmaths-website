Huygens' Law of Double Refraction

Double refraction is the splitting of an unpolarized light ray into two refracted rays—the ordinary (O) ray and the extraordinary (E) ray—when passing through an anisotropic birefringent crystal like calcite. Huygens explained this phenomenon by proposing that each point on a wavefront inside a uniaxial crystal acts as a source of two wavelets: a spherical wavelet for the O-ray and an ellipsoidal wavelet for the E-ray.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Qualitative understanding of double refraction, ordinary and extraordinary rays, wavefront shapes in uniaxial crystals (spherical and ellipsoidal), and the distinction between positive and negative crystals.

PGT extension

Huygens' construction for uniaxial crystals cut parallel and perpendicular to the optic axis, quantitative determination of wavefront propagation angles, velocity relations, and verification of Snell's law for both O-ray and E-ray.

Prerequisites

  • Wave theory of light and Huygens' principle
  • Anisotropy and optical properties of crystals
  • Polarization of light waves

Anisotropy and Birefringence

  • Crystals like calcite (CaCO3) and quartz possess different physical and optical properties in different directions due to their asymmetric internal atomic lattice.
  • When a ray of unpolarized light strikes a calcite crystal, it splits into two distinct refracted rays, exhibiting two different refractive indices for a single angle of incidence.
  • The ray that obeys ordinary Snell's law of refraction and has a constant refractive index (μo) in all directions is called the Ordinary (O) ray.

Ordinary and Extraordinary Rays

  • The second ray does not obey Snell's law, its velocity depends on the direction of propagation relative to the optic axis, and its refractive index (μe) varies with the angle of incidence; this is the Extraordinary (E) ray.
  • Both the O-ray and E-ray are plane polarized with their vibration directions mutually perpendicular to each other.
  • The optic axis is a specific direction inside a uniaxial crystal along which the velocities of the O-ray and E-ray are equal, meaning double refraction does not occur for light traveling along this axis.

Huygens' Wavelet Theory

  • According to Huygens, every point on a primary wavefront inside a birefringent crystal sends out two secondary wavelets simultaneously.
  • The secondary wavelet for the O-ray spreads out uniformly in all directions with a constant velocity vo, producing a spherical wavefront.
  • The secondary wavelet for the E-ray travels with a velocity ve that varies with direction, producing an ellipsoidal wavefront whose axis of revolution coincides with the optic axis of the crystal.

Positive and Negative Uniaxial Crystals

  • In negative uniaxial crystals (e.g., calcite), the velocity of the E-ray is greater than the velocity of the O-ray in directions perpendicular to the optic axis (ve > vo, μe < μo), making the sphere lie entirely inside the ellipsoid.
  • In positive uniaxial crystals (e.g., quartz), the velocity of the E-ray is less than that of the O-ray perpendicular to the optic axis (ve < vo, μe > μo), making the ellipsoid lie entirely inside the sphere.
  • At the point of tangency along the optic axis, the spherical and ellipsoidal wavelets touch each other.

Derivations

Huygens' Construction for Refraction at a Calcite Crystal Surface (Optic Axis Parallel to Surface)

Conditions: Uniaxial negative crystal, incident plane wave normal to the crystal surface, optic axis lying in the plane of incidence parallel to the surface.

  • Let a plane wavefront AB be incident normally on the surface of a calcite crystal MN.
  • When point A reaches the crystal surface at time t = 0, it starts acting as a source of secondary wavelets inside the crystal.
  • After time t, the secondary wavelet for the O-ray from A travels a distance Ro = vo t and forms a hemisphere of radius vo t.
  • Simultaneously, the secondary wavelet for the E-ray travels a distance Re = ve t and forms an ellipsoid of revolution.
  • Draw a tangent plane from the point B' (where the wavefront reaches later) to the spherical wavelet to get the refracted wavefront for the O-ray, and to the ellipsoidal wavelet to get the refracted wavefront for the E-ray.
  • Since ve > vo for calcite, the E-ray wavefront penetrates deeper than the O-ray wavefront, showing that the E-ray travels faster than the O-ray in directions perpendicular to the optic axis.

Result: The O-ray wavefront is spherical of radius vot and the E-ray wavefront is ellipsoidal with semi-axes vot and vet.

Relation between Refractive Indices and Velocities

Conditions: Isotropic medium vs. Anisotropic uniaxial crystal principal directions.

  • Define the refractive index for the ordinary ray as μo = c / vo, where c is the speed of light in vacuum and vo is the velocity of the O-ray in the crystal.
  • Define the principal refractive index for the extraordinary ray as μe = c / ve(max) or c / ve(min) depending on propagation direction perpendicular to the optic axis.
  • Express the spatial dimensions of Huygens' secondary wavelets after time t in terms of these refractive indices: ro = (c / μo)t and re = (c / μe)t.

Result: μo = c / vo and μe = c / ve

Solved numericals

TGT core

The refractive index of calcite for the ordinary ray is 1.658. Calculate the velocity of the ordinary ray inside the calcite crystal. Given the speed of light in vacuum is 3 × 108 m/s.

  • Identify the given values: μo = 1.658, $c = 3 × 10^{8} m/s$.
  • Use the formula for ordinary velocity: vo = c / μo.
  • Substitute the values: vo = (3 × 108) / 1.658.
  • Calculate the result: vo ≈ 1.81 × 108 m/s.

Answer: 1.81 × 108 m/s

PGT extension

In a negative uniaxial crystal, the ordinary and extraordinary refractive indices are μo = 1.66 and μe = 1.48 respectively. If a light pulse of duration t = 1 ns is emitted inside the crystal, find the ratio of the maximum radii of the O-ray and E-ray secondary wavelets perpendicular to the optic axis.

  • Write the expressions for the maximum radii perpendicular to the optic axis: ro(max) = vo $t = (c / μ_{o})t$ and re(max) = ve $t = (c / μ_{e})t$.
  • Take the ratio of the radii: ro(max) / re(max) = (c / μo) / (c / μe) = μe / μo.
  • Substitute the given refractive index values: Rati$o = 1.48 / 1.66$.
  • Simplify the fraction: 1.48 / 1.66 ≈ 0.892.

Answer: 0.892

PGT extension

A ray of light is incident normally on a calcite crystal face cut parallel to the optic axis. If the crystal thickness is 2 mm, μo = 1.658, and μe = 1.486, calculate the time taken by the O-ray and E-ray to cross the crystal.

  • Given thickness $d = 2 mm = 2 × 10^{-3} m$, $c = 3 × 10^{8} m/s$.
  • Time for O-ray: to = d / vo = (d · μo) / c.
  • Substitute values for O-ray: to = (2 × 10-3 × 1.658) / (3 × 108) = 1.105 × 10-11 s.
  • Time for E-ray: te = d / ve = (d · μe) / c.
  • Substitute values for E-ray: te = (2 × 10-3 × 1.486) / (3 × 108) = 9.907 × 10-12 s.

Answer: to = 1.11 × 10-11 s, te = 9.91 × 10-12 s

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumption: The crystal is optically uniaxial and homogeneous throughout the path of light.
  • Limitation: Huygens' construction gives geometric wavefront propagation but does not account for the intensity distribution or phase changes associated with polarization states without electromagnetic boundary conditions.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Both ordinary and extraordinary rays travel with the same velocity in all directions inside a birefringent crystal.

Correct: Only the ordinary ray travels with a constant velocity in all directions; the extraordinary ray's velocity varies with the direction of propagation relative to the optic axis.

Anisotropy of the crystal lattice causes directional dependence for the E-ray but isotropic response for the O-ray.

Common error: Double refraction does not occur if light is incident normally on the crystal surface.

Correct: Double refraction can occur even at normal incidence if the incident wavefront is not parallel to the optic axis or if the optic axis is inclined to the surface.

Normal incidence only guarantees no deviation at the boundary interface; the separation into O and E rays depends on internal wavelet velocities and crystal orientation.

Common error: The optic axis is a single unique physical line drawn through the center of the crystal.

Correct: The optic axis is a direction, not a specific line; any line parallel to this direction inside the crystal serves as an optic axis.

Optical properties are uniform along parallel planes throughout the crystal volume.

Quick revision

  • Uniaxial crystals split unpolarized light into an O-ray (spherical wavefront, obeys Snell's law) and an E-ray (ellipsoidal wavefront, breaks Snell's law).
  • In negative crystals like calcite, ve > vo; in positive crystals like quartz, ve < vo.
  • The optic axis is the direction of unique symmetry where O-ray and E-ray velocities are identical.

Must remember

  • Unpolarized light splitting into two refracted rays inside a birefringent crystal like calcite is called double refraction.
  • The Ordinary (O) ray obeys standard Snell's law and has a constant refractive index $\mu_o$ in all directions.
  • The Extraordinary (E) ray does not obey Snell's law and its velocity $v_e$ depends on the direction of propagation relative to the optic axis.
  • According to Huygens' wavelet theory, the O-ray produces a spherical wavefront ($v_o$), while the E-ray produces an ellipsoidal wavefront ($v_e$).
  • In negative uniaxial crystals like calcite, $v_e > v_o$ and $\mu_e < \mu_o$, making the sphere lie entirely inside the ellipsoid.
  • In positive uniaxial crystals like quartz, $v_e < v_o$ and $\mu_e > \mu_o$, making the ellipsoid lie entirely inside the sphere.
  • Both O-ray and E-ray are plane polarized with mutually perpendicular vibration directions.
  • Along the optic axis, the velocities of the O-ray and E-ray are equal, meaning double refraction does not occur in this specific direction.

Glossary

BirefringenceThe optical property of splitting a light ray into two distinct polarized rays (ordinary and extraordinary) when passing through an anisotropic medium.
Ordinary Ray (O-ray)The refracted ray that obeys standard Snell's law and travels with a constant velocity $v_o$ in all directions inside a birefringent crystal.
Extraordinary Ray (E-ray)The refracted ray that does not obey Snell's law and whose velocity $v_e$ varies with the direction of propagation relative to the optic axis.
Optic AxisA specific direction inside a uniaxial crystal along which the velocities of the O-ray and E-ray are equal and double refraction vanishes.
Negative Uniaxial CrystalA birefringent crystal in which the velocity of the extraordinary ray is greater than that of the ordinary ray ($v_e > v_o$ or $\mu_e < \mu_o$), such as calcite.
Positive Uniaxial CrystalA birefringent crystal in which the velocity of the extraordinary ray is less than that of the ordinary ray ($v_e < v_o$ or $\mu_e > \mu_o$), such as quartz.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following crystals exhibits double refraction and is commonly used as a prime example of an anisotropic medium?

Explanation: Calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) possesses an asymmetric internal atomic lattice making it optically anisotropic, which readily exhibits birefringence (double refraction).

2. Which of the two refracted rays in a doubly refracting crystal always obeys ordinary Snell's law of refraction?

Explanation: The Ordinary (O) ray obeys standard Snell's law and has a constant refractive index $\mu_o$ in all directions.

3. According to Huygens' theory, the secondary wavelet for the ordinary ray spreads out uniformly producing which type of wavefront?

Explanation: The secondary wavelet for the O-ray spreads out uniformly in all directions with a constant velocity $v_o$, producing a spherical wavefront.

4. What shape does the secondary wavefront of the extraordinary (E) ray take inside a uniaxial crystal?

Explanation: The secondary wavelet for the E-ray travels with a velocity $v_e$ that varies with direction, producing an ellipsoidal wavefront whose axis of revolution coincides with the optic axis.

5. In a negative uniaxial crystal like calcite, what is the relation between the velocities of the extraordinary ray ($v_e$) and ordinary ray ($v_o$) perpendicular to the optic axis?

Explanation: In negative uniaxial crystals (e.g., calcite), the velocity of the E-ray is greater than the velocity of the O-ray perpendicular to the optic axis ($v_e > v_o$).

6. How are the vibration directions of the Ordinary ray and Extraordinary ray related to each other?

Explanation: Both the O-ray and E-ray are plane polarized with their vibration directions mutually perpendicular to each other.

7. What is the condition for double refraction along the optic axis of a uniaxial crystal?

Explanation: The optic axis is a specific direction along which the velocities of the O-ray and E-ray are equal, meaning double refraction does not occur for light traveling along this axis.

8. Which of the following is true for a positive uniaxial crystal such as quartz?

Explanation: In positive uniaxial crystals like quartz, $v_e < v_o$, which implies that the extraordinary refractive index is greater than the ordinary refractive index ($\mu_e > \mu_o$).

9. The formula for the ordinary refractive index $\mu_o$ is given by:

Explanation: The ordinary refractive index is defined as $\mu_o = \frac{c}{v_o}$, where $c$ is the speed of light in vacuum and $v_o$ is the velocity of the O-ray in the crystal.

10. What geometric relationship exists between the spherical and ellipsoidal wavelets in a negative uniaxial crystal?

Explanation: In negative uniaxial crystals (calcite), since $v_e > v_o$, the sphere lies entirely inside the ellipsoid except at the points touching along the optic axis.

11. What does the radius of the ordinary wavelet ($r_o$) equal after time $t$?

Explanation: The secondary wavelet for the O-ray spreads out uniformly with velocity $v_o$, giving a radius $r_o = v_o t = \frac{c}{\mu_o} t$.

12. The cross-section of the extraordinary wavefront in Cartesian coordinates containing the optic axis is represented by the equation:

Explanation: The ellipsoidal cross-section of the extraordinary wavefront is given by $\frac{x^2}{v_o^2 t^2} + \frac{y^2}{v_e^2 t^2} = 1$.

13. Which of the following statements about the optic axis is correct?

Explanation: The optic axis is a direction, not a specific line; any line parallel to this direction inside the crystal volume serves as an optic axis.

14. For a positive uniaxial crystal, how are the spherical and ellipsoidal wavelets arranged relative to each other?

Explanation: In positive uniaxial crystals (e.g., quartz), $v_e < v_o$, making the ellipsoidal wavefront lie entirely inside the spherical wavefront.

15. Why does the extraordinary ray fail to obey standard Snell's law of refraction?

Explanation: Anisotropy of the crystal lattice causes the velocity of the E-ray to vary with direction relative to the optic axis, causing non-compliance with standard Snell's law.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. When an unpolarized light beam is incident on a calcite crystal, it splits into two rays (O-ray and E-ray). Which of the following properties distinguishes the O-ray from the E-ray?

Explanation: The O-ray obeys ordinary Snell's law and has a constant velocity $v_o$, whereas the E-ray's velocity varies with direction relative to the optic axis.

2. In a negative uniaxial crystal like calcite, the relationship between ordinary and extraordinary velocities and refractive indices perpendicular to the optic axis is:

Explanation: In negative uniaxial crystals (calcite), $v_e > v_o$. Since refractive index is inversely proportional to velocity ($\mu = c/v$), this implies $\mu_e < \mu_o$.

3. According to Huygens' construction for double refraction in calcite, the wavefront of the extraordinary ray is:

Explanation: Huygens' wavelet theory dictates that the O-ray produces a spherical wavefront, while the E-ray produces an ellipsoidal wavefront.

4. What is the physical significance of the optic axis in a uniaxial birefringent crystal?

Explanation: The optic axis is a specific direction inside a uniaxial crystal along which the velocities of the O-ray and E-ray are equal, meaning double refraction does not occur.

5. If $\mu_o$ and $\mu_e$ are the ordinary and extraordinary refractive indices of a quartz crystal (positive uniaxial), which of the following inequalities holds true?

Explanation: For positive uniaxial crystals like quartz, $v_e < v_o$, which implies $\mu_e > \mu_o$.

6. Both the Ordinary ray and Extraordinary ray emerging from a calcite crystal are:

Explanation: Both the O-ray and E-ray are plane polarized, and their vibration directions are mutually perpendicular to each other.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What is the primary cause of birefringence or double refraction in crystals like calcite and quartz?

Explanation: Crystals like calcite possess an asymmetric internal atomic lattice that results in different optical and physical properties in different directions (anisotropy).

2. Which of the following equations correctly represents Snell's law for the Ordinary ray?

Explanation: The O-ray always obeys standard Snell's law, given by $\frac{\sin i}{\sin r_o} = \mu_o$.

3. In Huygens' wavelet construction, if time is $t$, what is the expression for the semi-axes $a_e$ and $b_e$ of the extraordinary wavefront cross-section?

Explanation: The principal semi-axes of the ellipsoidal E-wavefront cross-section are $a_e = v_o t$ and $b_e = v_e t$.

4. Which statement best describes the wavefront geometry in a negative uniaxial crystal like calcite?

Explanation: In negative uniaxial crystals, $v_e > v_o$, making the sphere (O-ray) lie entirely inside the ellipsoid (E-ray).

5. What is the refractive index condition for a positive uniaxial crystal?

Explanation: In positive uniaxial crystals (e.g., quartz), $v_e < v_o$, which leads to $\mu_e > \mu_o$.

6. If light travels along the optic axis of a uniaxial birefringent crystal, what happens to the incident unpolarized light beam?

Explanation: Along the optic axis, the velocities of the O-ray and E-ray are equal, meaning double refraction does not occur.

7. Which of the following statements is a common misconception regarding double refraction?

Explanation: It is a misconception that double refraction cannot occur at normal incidence; it can occur if the optic axis is inclined or if the wavefront orientation permits it.

8. The velocity of the ordinary ray in a crystal is $2 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$. Taking the speed of light in vacuum as $3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$, what is the ordinary refractive index $\mu_o$?

Explanation: Using $\mu_o = \frac{c}{v_o} = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{2 \times 10^8} = 1.5$.

9. Which type of crystal has the ellipsoidal E-wavefront lying entirely inside the spherical O-wavefront?

Explanation: In positive uniaxial crystals like quartz, $v_e < v_o$, making the ellipsoid lie entirely inside the sphere.

10. What does the variable $t$ represent in Huygens' wavefront equations such as $r_o = v_o t$?

Explanation: In Huygens' wavelet theory, $t$ represents the time duration for which the secondary wavelets propagate from the primary wavefront points.
