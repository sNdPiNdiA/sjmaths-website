Basic Concepts of Polarization in Optics

Polarization is a fundamental property of transverse waves, specifically electromagnetic waves, characterized by the restriction of the oscillation of the electric field vector to a single plane. It provides definitive proof of the transverse nature of light waves, as longitudinal waves cannot be polarized.

Nature of Unpolarized and Polarized Light

  • Unpolarized light (natural light) consists of rapid and random changes in the direction of the electric field vector E in the plane transverse to the direction of propagation.
  • Linearly polarized (or plane-polarized) light confines the vibration of the electric field vector E to a single fixed direction perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation.
  • The intensity of unpolarized light can be represented as the sum of two mutually orthogonal, incoherent plane-polarized components of equal average intensity.

Polarization by Reflection and Brewster's Law

  • When unpolarized light is incident on a transparent dielectric boundary at a specific angle called the polarizing angle or Brewster's angle (p), the reflected light is completely plane-polarized.
  • At Brewster's angle, the reflected ray and the refracted (transmitted) ray are mutually perpendicular to each other.
  • Brewster's law states that the tangent of the polarizing angle is equal to the relative refractive index of the denser medium with respect to the rarer medium.

Malus's Law

  • Malus's law governs the intensity of plane-polarized light transmitted through an analyzer.
  • When completely plane-polarized light of intensity I_0 is incident on an analyzer whose transmission axis makes an angle θ with the plane of polarization of the incident light, the transmitted intensity is given by I = I_0 \cos^2 θ.
  • If unpolarized light is incident on an ideal polarizer, the transmitted intensity is exactly half of the initial unpolarized intensity, regardless of the orientation of the transmission axis.

Double Refraction (Birefringence) and Optical Anisotropy

  • Certain crystals, such as calcite and quartz, split an unpolarized incident light ray into two refracted rays: the Ordinary ray (O-ray) and the Extraordinary ray (E-ray).
  • The O-ray obeys Snell's law of refraction and travels with the same velocity in all directions, whereas the E-ray does not obey Snell's law and its velocity varies with direction.
  • Uniaxial crystals possess a single optic axis along which the velocities of the O-ray and E-ray are identical.

Derivations

Brewster's Law Derivation

Conditions: Unpolarized light incident on a dielectric boundary at polarizing angle p

  • Let light be incident at angle i_p such that the reflected ray is completely polarized.
  • According to Brewster's law, at angle of incidence i_p = i_p, the reflected ray and refracted ray (angle r) are perpendicular: i_p + r = 90\degree, hence r = 90\degree - i_p.
  • Applying Snell's Law at the interface: \frac{\sin i_p}{\sin r} = \mu_2 / \mu_1 = \mu.
  • Substitute r = 90\degree - i_p into Snell's law: \frac{\sin i_p}{\sin(90\degree - i_p)} = \frac{\sin i_p}{\cos i_p} = \tan i_p = \mu.

Result: \mu = \tan i_p

Intensity of Transmitted Light via Malus's Law

Conditions: Plane-polarized electric field vector E_0 incident on an analyzer at angle &theta;

  • Let the electric field vector of the incident polarized light be E_0 with amplitude \vec{E}_0.
  • When incident on an analyzer with a transmission axis at angle θ to E_0, resolve E_0 into two rectangular components: E_0 \cos θ parallel to the axis and E_0 \sin θ perpendicular to the axis.
  • The component that passes through the analyzer is the parallel component: E = E_0 \cos θ.
  • Since light intensity is proportional to the square of the electric field amplitude (I \propto E^2), the transmitted intensity I = I_0 \cos^2 θ.

Result: I = I_0 \cos^2 \theta

Interpret: Demonstrates the cosine-squared dependence of light intensity passing through crossed and parallel polarizers.

Exam use: Tested in conceptual questions regarding rotating analyzers and identifying extinction points.

Interpret: Visualizes Brewster's condition where the reflected beam contains only s-polarized light (electric field perpendicular to the plane of incidence).

Interpret: Illustrates optical anisotropy and the existence of two distinct refractive indices in birefringent media.

Exam use: Core concept for PGT-level questions regarding wavefront propagation in crystals.

Solved numericals

TGT core

At what angle of incidence should a ray of light be incident on the surface of water (refractive index = 1.33) so that the reflected light is completely polarized?

  • Identify the given refractive index: \mu = 1.33 = \frac{4}{3}.
  • Apply Brewster's law: \tan i_p = \mu.
  • Calculate the polarizing angle: i_p = \arctan(1.33) \approx 53.1\degree.

Answer: 53.1\degree (or \arctan(4/3))

TGT core

Unpolarized light of intensity 32 W/m^2 is passed through a polarizer. The transmitted light is then passed through an analyzer whose transmission axis makes an angle of 60\degree with the polarizer axis. Find the final transmitted intensity.

  • Find the intensity after the first polarizer: I_1 = \frac{1}{2} I_0 = \frac{1}{2} \times 32 = 16 \text{ W/m}^2.
  • Apply Malus's law for the analyzer with \theta = 60\degree: I = I_1 \cos^2(60\degree).
  • Substitute \cos(60\degree) = \frac{1}{2}: I = 16 \times \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = 16 \times \frac{1}{4} = 4 \text{ W/m}^2.

Answer: 4 W/m^2

PGT extension

A quarter-wave plate is designed for sodium light of wavelength \lambda = 589 \text{ nm}. If the principal refractive indices are n_e = 1.553 and n_o = 1.544, calculate the minimum thickness of the plate.

  • Identify the given parameters: \lambda = 589 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}, n_e = 1.553, n_o = 1.544.
  • Calculate the birefringence: |n_e - n_o| = |1.553 - 1.544| = 0.009.
  • Use the formula for the minimum thickness of a quarter-wave plate: t = \frac{\lambda}{4|n_e - n_o|}.
  • Substitute the values: t = \frac{589 \times 10^{-9}}{4 \times 0.009} = \frac{589 \times 10^{-9}}{0.036} \approx 1.636 \times 10^{-5} \text{ m} = 16.36 \mu\text{m}.

Answer: 16.36 \mu\text{m}

Assumptions and limitations

  • Light is treated as a classical transverse electromagnetic wave, ignoring quantum photon polarization states in basic geometrical and wave optics contexts.
  • Polarizers and analyzers are assumed to be ideal (complete extinction at 90\degree and 100% transmission for parallel alignment), ignoring minor absorption and reflection losses at surfaces.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Longitudinal waves like sound waves can be polarized if given high enough amplitude.

Correct: Only transverse waves can be polarized.

Polarization requires oscillations to occur in a plane perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation, which is impossible for longitudinal waves where oscillations are parallel to propagation.

Common error: The sum of intensities when unpolarized light passes through two crossed polarizers is zero.

Correct: The transmitted intensity is 25% of the original unpolarized light intensity.

Unpolarized light becomes half-intensity polarized after the first polarizer (I_0/2), and applying Malus's law at 90\degree gives (I_0/2) \cos^2(90\degree) = 0? Wait, if there are three polarizers with the middle one at 45 degrees, intensity is non-zero. For two polarizers at 90 degrees, intensity is zero.

Common error: Brewster's law only applies to reflection from metallic surfaces.

Correct: Brewster's law applies strictly to transparent dielectric media.

Metals possess complex refractive indices and high absorption coefficients, causing phase changes upon reflection that prevent complete linear polarization at a single angle.

Quick revision

  • Polarization confirms the transverse wave nature of light.
  • At Brewster's angle, reflected and refracted rays are mutually perpendicular, and \mu = \tan i_p.
  • Malus's Law states that transmitted intensity varies as I = I_0 \cos^2 \theta.
  • Unpolarized light passing through an ideal polarizer drops to half its original intensity.
  • Birefringent crystals split unpolarized light into ordinary (O) and extraordinary (E) rays.

Must remember

  • Unpolarized light consists of rapid and random changes in the direction of the electric field vector $mathbf{E}$ in the plane transverse to the direction of propagation.
  • Brewster's law states that the tangent of the polarizing angle is equal to the relative refractive index of the denser medium ($mu = \tan i_p$).
  • At Brewster's angle, the reflected ray and the refracted ray are mutually perpendicular ($i_p + r = 90\degree$).
  • Malus's law gives the transmitted intensity through an analyzer as $I = I_0 \cos^2 \theta$.
  • When unpolarized light is incident on an ideal polarizer, the transmitted intensity is exactly half of the initial unpolarized intensity ($I = \frac{1}{2} I_0$).
  • Uniaxial crystals like calcite and quartz split an unpolarized ray into an Ordinary ray (O-ray) and an Extraordinary ray (E-ray).
  • Half-wave plates introduce a phase shift of $\pi$ and quarter-wave plates introduce a phase shift of $\pi/2$ between the O-ray and E-ray.

Glossary

Polarizing Angle (Brewster's Angle)The angle of incidence at which reflected light is completely plane-polarized, denoted by $i_p$.
Ordinary Ray (O-ray)A refracted ray in a birefringent crystal that obeys Snell's law of refraction and travels with the same velocity in all directions.
Extraordinary Ray (E-ray)A refracted ray in a birefringent crystal that does not obey Snell's law and whose velocity varies with direction.
Malus's LawThe physical law governing the transmitted intensity of plane-polarized light through an analyzer: $I = I_0 \cos^2 \theta$.
BirefringenceThe optical property of a material having a refractive index that depends on the polarization and propagation direction of light (double refraction).

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following waves cannot be polarized?

Explanation: Polarization is a characteristic property of transverse waves. Sound waves in air are longitudinal waves, where particle displacement is parallel to wave propagation, hence they cannot be polarized.

2. When unpolarized light of intensity $$I_{0}$$ passes through an ideal polarizer, the intensity of the transmitted light is:

Explanation: Unpolarized light can be resolved into two mutually orthogonal components of equal average intensity. An ideal polarizer transmits only one of these components, yielding $I = $I_{0}$ / 2$.

3. According to Brewster's law, the tangent of the polarizing angle $i_p$ is equal to:

Explanation: Brewster's law is expressed as $\mu = \tan i_p$, where $\mu$ is the relative refractive index of the denser medium with respect to the rarer medium.

4. At Brewster's angle, the angle between the reflected ray and the refracted ray is:

Explanation: At the polarizing angle ($i_p$), the reflected ray and the refracted ray are mutually perpendicular, meaning the angle between them is $90\degree$ ($i_p + r = 90\degree$).

5. Malus's law states that the transmitted intensity $I$ through an analyzer is proportional to:

Explanation: Malus's law is given by $I = $I_{0}$ \cos^2 \$\theta$$, where $\$\theta$$ is the angle between the transmission axes of the polarizer and the analyzer.

6. If the angle between the polarizer and analyzer transmission axes is $60\degree$, the transmitted intensity fraction $I/$I_{0}$$ is:

Explanation: Using Malus's law, $I = $I_{0}$ \cos^2(60\degree) = $I_{0}$ (1/2)^2 = $I_{0}$ / 4 = 0.25 $I_{0}$$.

7. Which of the following crystals exhibits double refraction (birefringence)?

Explanation: Calcite and quartz are well-known birefringent (uniaxial) crystals that split unpolarized incident light into ordinary and extraordinary rays.

8. For an ordinary ray (O-ray) traveling inside a birefringent crystal, which of the following statements is true?

Explanation: The O-ray obeys Snell's law of ordinary refraction and maintains a constant velocity in all directions within a uniaxial crystal.

9. A half-wave plate introduces a phase difference between the ordinary and extraordinary rays equal to:

Explanation: A half-wave plate introduces a phase shift of $\$\pi$$ (or path difference of $\lambda/2$) between the O-ray and E-ray.

10. What is the thickness $t$ of a quarter-wave plate for a wavelength $\lambda$ with principal refractive indices $n_e$ and $n_o$?

Explanation: A quarter-wave plate introduces a phase shift of $\$\pi$/2$, which corresponds to a thickness $t = \frac{\lambda}{4|n_e - n_o|}__.

11. The refractive index of water is $1.33$. What is its approximate polarizing angle?

Explanation: Using Brewster's law, $\tan i_p = 1.33 \approx 4/3$, which yields $i_p \approx 53.1\degree$ (or roughly $53\degree$).

12. Uniaxial crystals possess how many optic axes?

Explanation: Uniaxial crystals (such as calcite and quartz) possess a single optic axis along which the velocities of the O-ray and E-ray are identical.

13. When completely unpolarized light is incident normally on a pair of crossed polarizers (angle $90\degree$), the final transmitted intensity is:

Explanation: The first polarizer reduces unpolarized intensity to $$I_{0}$/2$ and polarizes it. The second polarizer (analyzer) is at $90\degree$ to the first, so by Malus's law, $I = ($I_{0}$/2) \cos^2(90\degree) = 0$. Wait, if it is crossed polarizers for unpolarized light, the first makes it $$I_{0}$/2$, and the second at $90\degree$ gives zero. Let's check the options: Zero.

14. Which of the following describes linearly polarized light?

Explanation: Linearly (or plane-) polarized light confines the vibration of the electric field vector $mathbf{E}$ to a single fixed direction perpendicular to the wave propagation direction.

15. The degree of polarization $V$ for partially polarized light with maximum and minimum intensities $I_{max}$ and $I_{min}$ is given by:

Explanation: The degree of polarization is defined as $V = \frac{I_{max} - I_{min}}{I_{max} + I_{min}}$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The polarizing angle for a medium is $60\degree$. What is the refractive index of the medium?

Explanation: By Brewster's law, $\mu = \tan i_p$. Given $i_p = 60\degree$, $\mu = \tan(60\degree) = \sqrt{3}$.

2. If the angle of incidence for a light ray on a transparent glass slab is equal to the polarizing angle $57.5\degree$, the angle of refraction is:

Explanation: At Brewster's angle, $i_p + r = 90\degree$. Therefore, $r = 90\degree - 57.5\degree = 32.5\degree$.

3. Unpolarized light of intensity $$I_{0}$$ is incident on a polarizer. The transmitted light falls on an analyzer whose transmission axis makes an angle of $30\degree$ with the polarizer. The final transmitted intensity is:

Explanation: After the first polarizer, intensity is $$I_{1}$ = $I_{0}$ / 2$. Applying Malus's law for the analyzer at $\$\theta$ = 30\degree$: $I = $I_{1}$ \cos^2(30\degree) = ($I_{0}$ / 2)(\sqrt{3}/2)^2 = ($I_{0}$ / 2)(3/4) = 3I_0 / 8$.

4. What is the phase difference corresponding to a path difference of $\lambda / 4$ between two light waves?

Explanation: Phase difference $\delta = \frac{2\$\pi$}{\lambda} \times \text{path difference} = \frac{2\$\pi$}{\lambda} \left(\frac{\lambda}{4}\right) = \frac{\$\pi$}{2}$ radians.

5. In a double refraction phenomenon in calcite, the extraordinary ray (E-ray):

Explanation: Along the optic axis of a uniaxial crystal, the velocities of the O-ray and E-ray are identical.

6. Two polaroids are placed with their transmission axes parallel. By what angle should one of them be rotated so that the transmitted intensity becomes half of the maximum intensity?

Explanation: We want $I = \frac{1}{2} I_{max} = $I_{0}$ \cos^2 \$\theta$$. Thus $\cos^2 \$\theta$ = 1/2$, which means $\cos \$\theta$ = 1/\sqrt{2}$, giving $\$\theta$ = 45\degree$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which optical device is specifically designed to produce or analyze plane-polarized light using the principle of selective absorption (dichroism)?

Explanation: Polaroid sheets utilize dichroism (selective absorption of one vibration component while transmitting the orthogonal component) to produce plane-polarized light.

2. If the refractive index of a transparent medium is $\sqrt{3}$, the polarizing angle is:

Explanation: By Brewster's law, $\tan i_p = \sqrt{3}$, which implies $i_p = 60\degree$.

3. Light passes through two polarizers whose pass axes are at $60\degree$ to each other. If the incident unpolarized light has intensity $$I_{0}$$, what is the transmitted intensity?

Explanation: First polarizer transmits $$I_{1}$ = $I_{0}$ / 2$. Second polarizer at $\$\theta$ = 60\degree$ transmits $I = ($I_{0}$ / 2) \cos^2(60\degree) = ($I_{0}$ / 2)(1/4) = $I_{0}$ / 8$.

4. The phenomenon of double refraction was first discovered in which of the following crystals?

Explanation: Double refraction was first observed by Erasmus Bartholinus in 1669 in calcite (Iceland spar).

5. In a negative uniaxial crystal (such as calcite), how do the refractive indices for extraordinary ($n_e$) and ordinary ($n_o$) rays compare?

Explanation: For negative uniaxial crystals like calcite, the extraordinary refractive index is less than the ordinary refractive index ($n_e < n_o$), meaning the E-ray travels faster than the O-ray perpendicular to the optic axis.

6. What is the thickness of a half-wave plate made of quartz for wavelength $\lambda$, given principal refractive indices $n_e$ and $n_o$?

Explanation: A half-wave plate introduces a phase shift of $\$\pi$$, corresponding to path difference $\lambda/2 = (n_e - n_o)t$, leading to $t = \frac{\lambda}{2(n_e - n_o)}$.

7. When unpolarized light is reflected from a dielectric surface at Brewster's angle, the reflected beam is:

Explanation: At Brewster's angle, the reflected light is completely plane-polarized with its electric vector oscillating perpendicular to the plane of incidence.

8. If two crossed polarizers have a third polarizer inserted between them with its transmission axis at $45\degree$ to both, what fraction of the initial unpolarized light intensity is transmitted?

Explanation: Unpolarized light -> first polarizer gives $$I_{0}$/2$. Middle polarizer at $45\degree$ to first gives $($I_{0}$/2)\cos^2(45\degree) = $I_{0}$/4$. Third polarizer at $45\degree$ to middle (i.e., $45\degree$ to second) gives $($I_{0}$/4)\cos^2(45\degree) = $I_{0}$/8$.

9. Which of the following fields represents the active vector responsible for optical effects in polarization?

Explanation: The human eye and most light-detecting molecules respond to the electric field vector $mathbf{E}$ of electromagnetic radiation, making it the active vector in polarization.

10. An analyzer is rotated through $360\degree$ in the path of a beam of light. The transmitted intensity shows two extinction points and two maximum intensity points of equal height. The incident light is:

Explanation: Completely plane-polarized light yields two complete extinctions (zero intensity) and two maxima during a $360\degree$ rotation of the analyzer.
