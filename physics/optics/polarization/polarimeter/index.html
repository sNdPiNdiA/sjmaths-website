Polarimeter and Optical Activity

A polarimeter is an analytical instrument used to determine the angle of rotation caused by passing polarized light through an optically active substance. It is fundamentally applied to measure specific rotation, concentration, and sugar content in solutions.

Optical Activity and Chiral Media

  • Certain substances rotate the plane of vibration of plane-polarized light passing through them, a property known as optical activity.
  • Examples include crystalline quartz, sugar solutions, turpentine, and tartaric acid.
  • Optical activity originates from the asymmetric molecular structure or crystalline lattice asymmetry that interacts differently with left and right circularly polarized light.

Biot's Laws of Optical Rotation

  • The angle of rotation θ is directly proportional to the thickness (length l) of the optically active medium traversed: θ ∝ l.
  • The angle of rotation is directly proportional to the concentration c of the solution: θ ∝ c.
  • The angle of rotation is inversely proportional to the square of the wavelength λ of the incident light: θ ∝ 1/λ², and depends on temperature.

Laurent's Half-Shade Polarimeter Construction

  • Consists of a monochromatic light source, a polarizer (Nicol prism), a half-shade device, a sample tube, and an analyzer (Nicol prism).
  • The half-shade device divides the field of view into two halves (usually half quartz and half glass) to create equal semi-darkness, allowing precise determination of the extinction point.
  • The analyzer is mounted on a circular scale calibrated in degrees to measure the angular rotation.

Specific Rotation

  • Specific rotation [S] is defined as the rotation produced by a solution of length 1 decimeter (10 cm) and unit concentration (1 g/cm³) at a specified temperature and wavelength.
  • It is a characteristic physical constant for a given optically active substance under standard conditions.
  • Units of specific rotation are typically degrees per decimeter per (g/cm³) or degrees per decimeter per (g/mL).

Derivations

Relation for Specific Rotation

Conditions: Light of wavelength λ passing through a solution of length l containing mass m in volume V.

  • State Biot's law: θ ∝ l × c, where c is the concentration.
  • Express concentration as mass m per unit volume V, so $c = m/V$.
  • Introduce the proportionality constant [S], known as specific rotation: $\theta = [S] \times l \times (m/V)$.
  • Rearrange to express specific rotation: [S] = (θ × V) / (l × m).

Result: [S] = \frac{\theta \cdot V}{l \cdot m}

Fresnel's Explanation of Optical Rotation

Conditions: Plane-polarized light incident along the optic axis of an optically active crystal.

  • Resolve incident plane-polarized light into two equal, oppositely rotating circular polarizations (left-handed and right-handed).
  • In an optically active medium, the refractive indices for left circular (μ_L) and right circular (μ_R) polarized light are different (μ_L ≠ μ_R).
  • Due to different phase velocities, one circular component travels faster than the other, causing a relative phase difference δ after traversing thickness l.
  • The angle of rotation of the resultant plane-polarized light is given by $\theta = \pi l (\mu_L - \mu_R) / \lambda$.

Result: $\theta = \frac{\pi l (\mu_L - \mu_R)}{\lambda}$

Solved numericals

TGT core

A tube of length 20 cm containing a sugar solution of unknown concentration rotates the plane of polarization by 11 degrees. If the specific rotation of sugar is 66 degree dm^-1 (g/cm^3)^-1, find the concentration of the solution.

  • Identify given values: $\theta = 11°$, l = 20 cm = 2 dm, [S] = 66 ° dm^-1 (g/cm^3)^-1.
  • Apply the formula: $c = \theta / ([S] \times l)$.
  • Substitute values: $c = 11 / (66 \times 2) = 11 / 132 = 1/12 g/cm^3$.
  • Calculate final value: c ≈ 0.0833 g/cm^3.

Answer: 0.0833 g/cm^3

PGT extension

A 30 cm long polarimeter tube filled with a solution containing 15 g of cane sugar in 100 mL of water produces an optical rotation of 19.8 degrees. Calculate the specific rotation of cane sugar.

  • Identify given values: l = 30 cm = 3 dm, mass m = 15 g, Volume V = 100 mL, concentration $c = m/V = 15/100 = 0.15 g/mL$, $\theta = 19.8°$.
  • Use the specific rotation formula: [S] = θ / (l × c).
  • Substitute values: [S] = 19.8 / (3 × 0.15) = 19.8 / 0.45.
  • Calculate result: [S] = 44 ° dm^-1 (g/mL)^-1.

Answer: 44 degree dm^-1 (g/mL)^-1

PGT extension

Two optically active solutions A and B have specific rotations in the ratio 2:3. Solution A has a concentration of 0.04 g/mL in a 2 dm tube, and Solution B has a concentration of 0.02 g/mL in a 3 dm tube. Find the ratio of their observed rotations (θ_A : θ_B).

  • Write formula for rotation: $\theta = [S] \times l \times c$.
  • Set up the ratio for solutions A and B: θ_A / θ_$B = ([S]_A \times l_A \times c_A) / ([S]_B \times l_B \times c_B)$.
  • Substitute given ratios and values: [S]_A/[S]_$B = 2/3$, l_A = 2, l_B = 3, c_A = 0.04, c_B = 0.02.
  • Calculate ratio: θ_A / θ_$B = (2/3) \times (2/3) \times (0.04/0.02) = (4/9) \times 2 = 8/9$.

Answer: 8:9

Assumptions and limitations

  • Biot's laws assume dilute solutions where solute-solute interactions are negligible.
  • The polarimeter must use monochromatic light to prevent angular dispersion and broadening of the extinction band.
  • Temperature fluctuations can alter specific rotation; measurements must be conducted at a standard reference temperature (usually 20°C).

Common misconceptions

Common error: Polarimeters can measure the concentration of any chemical solution regardless of its molecular structure.

Correct: Polarimeters only measure optically active substances possessing chiral centers or asymmetric molecular configurations.

Acheiral molecules do not rotate the plane of polarization of light.

Common error: Specific rotation depends on the length of the sample tube used in the experiment.

Correct: Specific rotation is an intrinsic property and is independent of tube length or concentration in ideal conditions.

Path length and concentration effects cancel out in the definition of specific rotation.

Quick revision

  • Specific rotation is rotation per unit decimeter path length and unit concentration.
  • Biot's Law (Length dependence): \theta \propto l
  • Biot's Law (Concentration dependence): \theta \propto c

Must remember

  • Optical activity is the ability of certain substances to rotate the plane of vibration of plane-polarized light.
  • According to Biot's laws, the angle of rotation $\theta$ is directly proportional to path length $l$ and concentration $c$, and inversely proportional to the square of the wavelength $\lambda^2$.
  • Specific rotation $[S]$ is defined as the rotation produced by a solution of length $1$ decimeter and unit concentration ($1\text{ g/cm}^3$) at a specified temperature and wavelength.
  • Laurent's half-shade polarimeter uses a half-shade device dividing the field of view into two halves to precisely determine the extinction point.
  • Fresnel's theory explains optical rotation through circular birefringence, involving differing refractive indices $\mu_L$ and $\mu_R$ for left and right circularly polarized light.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the phenomenon called when a substance rotates the plane of polarization of incident plane-polarized light?

Explanation: Optical activity is the property where certain substances rotate the plane of vibration of plane-polarized light passing through them.

2. Which of the following substances is known to exhibit optical activity?

Explanation: Crystalline quartz, sugar solutions, turpentine, and tartaric acid are classic examples of optically active substances.

3. According to Biot's laws, how does the angle of rotation $\theta$ vary with the length $l$ of the optically active medium traversed?

Explanation: Biot's law states that the angle of rotation $\theta$ is directly proportional to the thickness or length $l$ of the medium traversed: $\theta \propto l$.

4. How does the angle of rotation $\theta$ depend on the wavelength $\lambda$ of the incident light according to Biot's laws?

Explanation: The angle of rotation is inversely proportional to the square of the wavelength of the incident light: $\theta \propto 1/\lambda^2$.

5. What is the standard unit of path length $l$ used in the formula for specific rotation?

Explanation: In specific rotation calculations, path length $l$ is standardly expressed in decimeters (dm), where $1\text{ dm} = 10\text{ cm}$.

6. What is the primary function of the half-shade device in Laurent's half-shade polarimeter?

Explanation: The half-shade device divides the field of view into two halves (usually half quartz and half glass) to create equal semi-darkness, allowing precise determination of the extinction point.

7. What is the formula for specific rotation $[S]_\lambda^T$ in terms of observed rotation $\theta$, path length $l$, and concentration $c$?

Explanation: Specific rotation is defined as $[S]_\lambda^T = \frac{\theta}{l \cdot c}$, where $\theta$ is observed rotation, $l$ is length in dm, and $c$ is concentration in g/mL.

8. What is the microscopic origin of optical activity in substances like quartz or sugar solutions?

Explanation: Optical activity originates from asymmetric molecular structure or crystalline lattice asymmetry that interacts differently with left and right circularly polarized light.

9. If the concentration of an optically active solution is doubled while keeping the tube length constant, what happens to the angle of rotation $\theta$?

Explanation: Since $\theta \propto c$ according to Biot's law, doubling the concentration doubles the angle of rotation.

10. According to Fresnel's theory, optical rotation is caused by:

Explanation: Fresnel explained optical rotation by considering that plane-polarized light resolves into left and right circular polarizations which travel at different speeds ($\mu_L \neq \mu_R$).

11. What are the typical units of specific rotation $[S]$?

Explanation: Specific rotation units are degrees per decimeter per $(\text{g/cm}^3)$ or degrees per decimeter per $(\text{g/mL})$.y

12. Which component in Laurent's half-shade polarimeter is mounted on a circular scale calibrated in degrees?

Explanation: The analyzer is mounted on a circular scale calibrated in degrees to measure the angular rotation accurately.

13. What is the total rotation $\theta$ for a non-reacting mixture of two optically active substances with specific rotations $[S]_1, [S]_2$ and concentrations $c_1, c_2$ in path length $l$?

Explanation: The total rotation for a non-reacting mixture is the algebraic sum of rotations produced by each component: $\theta = [S]_1 l c_1 + [S]_2 l c_2$.

14. Which of the following statements regarding specific rotation is correct?

Explanation: Specific rotation is an intrinsic characteristic physical constant for a given optically active substance under standard temperature and wavelength conditions.

15. Why do achiral molecules fail to exhibit optical activity?

Explanation: Acheiral molecules lack chiral centers or asymmetric structures, meaning left and right circularly polarized light experience identical refractive indices and speeds.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following molecules typically exhibits optical activity?

Explanation: Tartaric acid possesses chiral centers and asymmetric molecular structures, making it optically active, whereas the others are achiral.

2. If the path length of a polarimeter tube is increased from $1\text{ dm}$ to $4\text{ dm}$, how does the observed rotation angle change for a given solution?

Explanation: By Biot's law, $\theta \propto l$. Increasing the length $l$ 4 times increases the angle of rotation $\theta$ 4 times.

3. What is the primary reason for using monochromatic light (such as the sodium D-line) in polarimetry?

Explanation: Because optical rotation depends inversely on the square of wavelength ($\theta \propto 1/\lambda^2$), using polychromatic light would cause angular dispersion, preventing a sharp extinction point.

4. In Laurent's half-shade device, what constitutes the two halves of the field of view?

Explanation: A Laurent half-shade device typically consists of a semi-circular plate of quartz and a semi-circular plate of glass (or half-wave quartz plate).

5. If a solution's specific rotation is $[S]$ and its concentration is halved while doubling the tube length, what is the net effect on the observed rotation $\theta$?

Explanation: Since $\theta = [S] \cdot l \cdot c$, if $l$ is doubled ($2l$) and $c$ is halved ($c/2$), the product $l \cdot c$ remains unchanged, hence $\theta$ remains the same.

6. Which of the following equations represents the approximate Drude equation for optical rotatory dispersion in the normal dispersion region?

Explanation: The approximate Drude equation is $\theta = \frac{k}{\lambda^2 - \lambda_0^2}$, relating rotation to wavelength $\lambda$ and characteristic absorption wavelength $\lambda_0$.

7. What is the condition of the field of view in a Laurent's half-shade polarimeter when the analyzer is set to the correct matching position?

Explanation: At the correct extinction/matching point, the half-shade device ensures both halves of the field of view exhibit equal semi-darkness.

8. If two optically active solutions with specific rotations $[S]_1$ and $[S]_2$ are mixed in equal volumes with concentrations $c_1$ and $c_2$, what is the combined rotation in a tube of length $l$?

Explanation: When mixed in equal volumes, each component's concentration is halved in the mixture, yielding $\theta = [S]_1 l \frac{c_1}{2} + [S]_2 l \frac{c_2}{2} = \frac{1}{2} l ([S]_1 c_1 + [S]_2 c_2)$. Note: if concentrations refer to the original stock solutions combined 1:1, concentration in the mixture becomes $c_1/2$ and $c_2/2$.

9. Which physical parameter does NOT affect the specific rotation of a given sugar solution under standard testing?

Explanation: Specific rotation is independent of path length $l$ and concentration $c$ (under ideal conditions), but depends heavily on temperature, wavelength, and solvent.

10. In Fresnel's theory of optical rotation, plane-polarized light is resolved into two circularly polarized components rotating in which directions?

Explanation: Plane-polarized light can be decomposed into equal amplitudes of left-circularly and right-circularly polarized light components.
