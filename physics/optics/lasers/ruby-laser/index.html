Ruby Laser

The ruby laser is a solid-state three-level laser that uses a synthetic ruby rod as the active medium and produces coherent red light at a wavelength of 694.3 nm. Optical pumping via a high-intensity flashlamp is employed to achieve population inversion between the excited and ground energy states of chromium ions.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Prerequisites

  • Energy levels of atoms and ions
  • Spontaneous and stimulated emission
  • Population inversion and optical pumping

Active Medium and Crystal Structure

  • The active medium of a ruby laser consists of a synthetic sapphire crystal (aluminum oxide, Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>) doped with approximately 0.05% by weight of trivalent chromium ions (Cr<sup>3+</sup>).
  • The host Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> lattice is optically transparent and mechanically robust, while the Cr<sup>3+</sup> ions provide the active optical transition responsible for fluorescence and laser action.
  • The chromium ions substitute for aluminum ions in the crystal lattice, experiencing a strong crystalline electric field that splits their d-orbitals.

Three-Level Energy Scheme

  • The ruby laser operates on a three-level laser system comprising the ground state (E<sub>1</sub>), a broad pump band (E<sub>3</sub>), and a metastable upper laser level (E<sub>2</sub>).
  • Optical pumping excites Cr<sup>3+</sup> ions from E<sub>1</sub> to the broad absorption bands E<sub>3</sub> using intense green and blue light from a xenon flashlamp.
  • From E<sub>3</sub>, the ions undergo rapid, non-radiative radiationless transitions (phonon relaxation) to the metastable level E<sub>2</sub> with a lifetime of approximately 3 ms.

Optical Resonator and Feedback

  • The ruby rod has its ends flat, parallel, highly polished, and coated with reflective films to form a Fabry-Perot optical cavity.
  • One end mirror is total reflecting (>99% reflectivity), while the output coupler mirror is partially reflecting (typically 70-80% reflectivity) to allow laser beam extraction.
  • Photons bouncing back and forth along the axis stimulate emission from the E<sub>2</sub> to E<sub>1</sub> transition, building up an intense, highly directional coherent beam.

Pulsed Operation Characteristics

  • Because the lower laser level is the ground state (E<sub>1</sub>), more than 50% of the total chromium ions must be excited to the metastable state E<sub>2</sub> to achieve population inversion.
  • This high pumping threshold requires very intense optical pumping, making continuous-wave (CW) operation extremely difficult; thus, ruby lasers typically operate in pulsed mode.
  • The output appears as a train of intense microsecond spikes corresponding to dynamic gain switching within the resonator cavity.

Derivations

Threshold Population Inversion Density

Conditions: Steady-state operation of a three-level laser medium with cavity length L and mirror reflectivities R&lt;sub&gt;1&lt;/sub&gt; and R&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt;

  • Write the condition for net optical gain per unit length: gamma(\nu) = \sigma(\nu)(N_2 - N_1) \ge \alpha_{int} + \frac{1}{2L}\ln\left(\frac{1}{R_1 R_2}\right), where \sigma is the stimulated emission cross-section and \alpha_{int} is internal cavity loss.
  • For a three-level system where the lower level is the ground state, let total ion density be N_t = N_1 + N_2.
  • At threshold, set the net gain equal to the total cavity loss coefficient \alpha_{th} = \alpha_{int} + \frac{1}{2L}\ln\left(\frac{1}{R_1 R_2}\right).
  • Substitute N_1 = N_t - N_2 into the gain equation to solve for the threshold population inversion density (N_2 - N_1)_{th}.

Result: (N_2 - N_1)_{th} = \frac{\alpha_{int} + \frac{1}{2L}\ln\left(\frac{1}{R_1 R_2}\right)}{\sigma}

Threshold Pump Power Rate

Conditions: Three-level system with pump band lifetime \tau_{31} \ll \tau_{21} and pumping rate W_{13}

  • Consider the rate equation for the upper laser level population density N_2: \frac{dN_2}{dt} = W_{13}N_1 - \frac{N_2}{\tau_2}
  • In steady state ( rac{dN_2}{dt} = 0), equate the pumping rate from ground state to the spontaneous and stimulated decay rates: W_{13}(N_t - N_2) = \frac{N_2}{\tau_2}
  • Express the threshold pumping rate W_{th} required to maintain the threshold population N_{2th}: W_{th} = \frac{N_{2th}}{\tau_2 (N_t - N_{2th})}
  • Relate the critical pumping rate to the absorbed pump threshold power per unit volume P_{th} = W_{th} h \nu_p N_t.

Result: P_{th} = \frac{h \nu_p N_{2th}}{\tau_2 \left(1 - \frac{N_{2th}}{N_t}\right)}

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the energy of a single photon emitted by a ruby laser operating at a wavelength of 694.3 nm. (Given h = 6.626 x 10&lt;sup&gt;-34&lt;/sup&gt; J s, c = 3 x 10&lt;sup&gt;8&lt;/sup&gt; m/s)

  • Identify given values: \lambda = 694.3 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}, h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}, c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}.
  • Use the photon energy formula: E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}.
  • Substitute values: E = \frac{(6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}) (3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s})}{694.3 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}}.
  • Calculate result: E = \frac{1.9878 \times 10^{-25}}{694.3 \times 10^{-9}} = 2.863 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}.

Answer: 2.863 x 10<sup>-19</sup> J (or 1.79 eV)

PGT extension

A ruby laser rod has a length L = 10 cm and refractive index n = 1.76. Calculate the frequency separation between adjacent longitudinal modes of the optical cavity.

  • Identify given parameters: L = 0.1 \text{ m}, n = 1.76, c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}.
  • Use the longitudinal mode frequency spacing formula: \Delta \nu = \frac{c}{2nL}.
  • Substitute the values: \Delta \nu = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{2 \times 1.76 \times 0.1}.
  • Perform the calculation: \Delta \nu = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{0.352} = 8.52 \times 10^8 \text{ Hz}.

Answer: 852 MHz (or 8.52 x 10<sup>8</sup> Hz)

PGT extension

Determine the minimum fraction of chromium ions that must be excited to the metastable state to achieve population inversion in a ruby laser, and state why continuous-wave (CW) operation is difficult.

  • Recall that ruby operates as a three-level laser system where the lower laser level is the ground state (E<sub>1</sub>).
  • For population inversion, the population of the upper level N<sub>2</sub> must exceed the population of the lower level N<sub>1</sub> (i.e., N<sub>2</sub> > N<sub>1</sub>).
  • Given the total ion density N<sub>t</sub> = N<sub>1</sub> + N<sub>2</sub>, set N<sub>2</sub> = N<sub>t</sub> / 2 at the threshold condition.
  • Conclude that more than 50% of the total ions must be pumped into the excited state, requiring immense pump power which causes severe thermal loading and precludes easy CW operation.

Answer: Greater than 50% (> 0.5); CW operation is difficult due to massive thermal dissipation required for continuous threshold pumping of the ground state.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes a uniform distribution of Cr<sup>3+</sup> dopant ions throughout the host Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> crystal lattice.
  • Neglects thermal lensing and refractive index gradients induced by high-power flashlamp pumping.
  • Limitation: Three-level operation inherently demands high pump power thresholds, leading to high thermal stress and restricting output to pulsed rather than continuous-wave operation under normal conditions.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Ruby laser is a four-level laser system.

Correct: Ruby laser is a classic three-level laser system.

The lower laser level is the ground state itself, requiring more than 50% of the total ions to be pumped before inversion is achieved.

Common error: The active substance that emits laser light in ruby is the aluminum oxide crystal.

Correct: The active ions are trivalent chromium (Cr&lt;sup&gt;3+&lt;/sup&gt;) impurities.

The sapphire (Al&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt;O&lt;sub&gt;3&lt;/sub&gt;) host crystal only provides a transparent mechanical matrix; optical transitions occur entirely within the chromium ion energy levels.

Common error: Ruby lasers easily produce continuous high-power laser beams.

Correct: Ruby lasers operate primarily in pulsed mode.

The high threshold requirement generates excessive heat, making continuous-wave operation extremely difficult without cryogenic cooling.

Quick revision

  • Ruby laser active medium: Cr<sup>3+</sup> ions doped in Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> crystal.
  • Operating wavelength: 694.3 nm (deep red R<sub>1</sub> line).
  • Energy level scheme: Three-level laser system.
  • Pumping mechanism: Optical pumping using a xenon flashlamp.
  • Metastable state lifetime: ~3 ms.
  • Population inversion threshold: Greater than 50% of total ions must be excited.
  • Operation mode: Pulsed laser output with relaxation spikes.

Must remember

  • The ruby laser is a classic three-level laser system.
  • The active medium is a synthetic sapphire crystal ($Al_2O_3$) doped with approximately $0.05%$ by weight of trivalent chromium ions ($Cr^{3+}$).
  • The laser emission wavelength of the ruby laser (R1 line) is $694.3 \text{ nm}$ in the red region.
  • Optical pumping is typically achieved using an intense xenon flashlamp, exciting ions from the ground state ($E_1$) to the broad absorption bands ($E_3$).
  • Because the lower laser level is the ground state ($E_1$), more than $50\%$ of the total $Cr^{3+}$ ions must be pumped to the metastable state ($E_2$) to achieve population inversion.
  • Ruby lasers primarily operate in a pulsed mode rather than continuous-wave (CW) due to the high pumping threshold.
  • The radiative lifetime of the metastable upper laser level ($E_2$) is approximately $\tau_2 \approx 3 \times 10^{-3} \text{ s}$.
  • The host lattice ($Al_2O_3$) is optically transparent and mechanically robust, while $Cr^{3+}$ ions provide the active optical transitions.

Glossary

Active MediumThe material in a laser—specifically $Cr^{3+}$-doped $Al_2O_3$ for a ruby laser—that amplifies light via stimulated emission.
Metastable StateAn excited energy level ($E_2$) with a relatively long lifetime ($\approx 3 \text{ ms}$) where population inversion can be sustained.
Optical PumpingThe process of exciting atoms or ions to higher energy levels using light energy from a flashlamp or secondary laser.
Three-Level LaserA laser system where population inversion occurs between a metastable upper level and the ground state.
Population InversionA condition where a higher energy state has a greater population of ions than a lower energy state ($N_2 > N_1$).
Threshold ConditionThe critical condition of gain required to overcome cavity losses and sustain laser oscillation.

6. What is the approximate radiative lifetime of the metastable upper laser level ($E_2$) in a ruby laser?

Explanation: From $E_3$, ions undergo rapid non-radiative transitions to the metastable level $E_2$, which has a lifetime of approximately $\tau_2 \approx 3 \times 10^{-3} \text{ s}$.

Explanation: The host $Al_2O_3$ lattice provides an optically transparent matrix, while the $Cr^{3+}$ ions substitute for aluminum ions and provide the active optical transition.

Explanation: One end mirror is total reflecting ($>99\%$), while the output coupler mirror is partially reflecting, typically $70-80\%$ reflectivity.

Explanation: The output appears as a train of intense microsecond spikes corresponding to dynamic gain switching within the resonator cavity.

10. What is the formula for the longitudinal cavity mode frequency spacing $\Delta \nu$ in a ruby laser resonator of length $L$ and refractive index $n$?

Explanation: The longitudinal cavity mode frequency spacing is given by $\Delta \nu = \frac{c}{2nL}$.

11. In the steady-state threshold gain condition for a ruby laser, what represents the internal cavity losses?

Explanation: The threshold condition is $\sigma(N_2 - N_1) \ge \alpha_{int} + \frac{1}{2L}\ln\left(\frac{1}{$R_{1}$ $R_{2}$}\right)$, where $\alpha_{int}$ represents the internal scattering and absorption losses.

12. In a ruby laser, what happens during the non-radiative transition from $E_3$ to $E_2$?

Explanation: From $E_3$, the ions undergo rapid, non-radiative radiationless transitions (phonon relaxation) to the metastable level $E_2$.

13. What is the approximate total chromium ion density fraction relation in a standard steady-state condition where pump band population is negligible?

Explanation: Since the population in the pump band $N_3 \approx 0$ in steady state, the total ion density is approximated by $N_t \approx N_1 + N_2$.

14. Which of the following best describes the crystal field effect on $Cr^{3+}$ ions in sapphire?

Explanation: The chromium ions substitute for aluminum ions in the crystal lattice, experiencing a strong crystalline electric field that splits their d-orbitals.

15. What is the threshold population inversion fraction requirement for a three-level system like ruby?

Explanation: Because the lower level is the ground state, more than $50\%$ of the total ions must be in $E_2$, meaning $\frac{N_2}{N_t} > 0.5$.
