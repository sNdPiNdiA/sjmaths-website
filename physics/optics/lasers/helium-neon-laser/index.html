Helium-Neon (He-Ne) Laser

The Helium-Neon laser is a continuous-wave gas laser that utilizes a mixture of helium and neon gases as the active medium. It typically operates at a primary emission wavelength of 632.8 nm in the visible red region, pumped by an electrical DC discharge.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Working principle of lasers, population inversion, optical pumping via electric discharge, pumping mechanism in gas lasers, and basic identification of the active medium and output wavelength.

PGT extension

Detailed energy level diagram of the He-Ne system, resonant energy transfer between He and Ne atoms, Doppler broadening of spectral lines, longitudinal and transverse modes (TEM_{00}), and cavity resonance conditions.

Prerequisites

  • Energy states and Bohr's atomic model
  • Spontaneous and stimulated emission, Einstein A and B coefficients
  • Population inversion and metastable states

Active Medium and Gas Mixture Ratio

  • The active medium consists of a mixture of helium and neon gases enclosed in a quartz discharge tube.
  • The typical partial pressure ratio of helium to neon is maintained at approximately 10:1.
  • Helium acts as the primary excitation medium while neon provides the laser active energy levels.

Pumping Mechanism and Resonant Energy Transfer

  • Free electrons accelerated by an electric DC discharge collide with helium atoms, exciting them to high-lying metastable states (2^1S and 2^3S).
  • Excited helium atoms collide non-radiatively with unexcited neon atoms, transferring energy due to nearly matching excitation energies.
  • This resonant collisional transfer selectively populates the 3s and 2s levels of neon, achieving population inversion relative to lower lying p-levels.

Laser Transitions and Emission Wavelengths

  • The primary visible laser transition occurs from the Ne(3s_2) to Ne(2p_4) state, yielding coherent radiation at 632.8 nm.
  • Other infrared transitions also occur in the He-Ne system, notably at 1.15 um and 3.39 um.
  • Selective mirrors coated for 632.8 nm are used in the optical cavity to suppress infrared lasing and favor the visible red output.

De-excitation and Ground State Repopulation

  • From the lower laser level (2p states), neon atoms rapidly decay radiatively to the 1s metastable states.
  • De-excitation from the 1s state to the absolute ground state (1s^0) occurs primarily through non-radiative collisions with the inner walls of the narrow discharge tube.
  • A narrow tube diameter is crucial to ensure fast de-excitation of lower states and prevent bottlenecking.

Derivations

Optical Cavity Resonance Frequency Condition

Conditions: Fabry-Perot resonator of length L filled with a medium of refractive index mu

  • For a standing wave to form inside the optical cavity, an integer multiple of half-wavelengths must equal the cavity optical path length.
  • Condition equation: 2 \mu L = q \lambda, where q is a large integer representing the longitudinal mode number.
  • Expressing wavelength in terms of frequency: \lambda = \frac{c}{\nu}, where c is the speed of light in vacuum.
  • Substitute into the resonance condition: 2 \mu L = q \frac{c}{\nu}, yielding \nu = \frac{q c}{2 \mu L}.
  • The frequency separation between adjacent longitudinal modes (Delta \nu) is obtained by setting Delta q = 1.

Result: \Delta \nu = \frac{c}{2 \mu L}

Doppler Broadening of the Laser Transition

Conditions: Thermal motion of emitting neon atoms at absolute temperature T

  • Due to thermal Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution, emitting atoms possess a velocity component v along the line of sight.
  • The emitted frequency is Doppler shifted according to $\nu = \nu_0 \left(1 \pm \frac{v}{c}\right)$.
  • The root-mean-square thermal velocity is given by $v_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{k_B T}{M}}$, where M is the mass of the neon atom.
  • The full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the Gaussian gain profile due to Doppler broadening is expressed as \Delta \nu_D.
  • Evaluating the Doppler width gives \Delta \n$u_D = \nu_0 \sqrt{\frac{8 k_B T \ln 2}{M c^2}}$.

Result: \Delta \n$u_D = \nu_0 \sqrt{\frac{8 k_B T \ln 2}{M c^2}}$

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the frequency separation between adjacent longitudinal modes of a He-Ne laser having an optical cavity length of 30 cm. Take speed of light $c = 3 x 10^8 m/s$.

  • Identify given parameters: Cavity length L = 30 cm = 0.3 m, refractive index \mu = 1.
  • Recall the longitudinal mode frequency spacing formula: \Delta \nu = \frac{c}{2 L}.
  • Substitute the values: \Delta $\nu = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{2 \times 0.3}$.
  • Calculate the final value: \Delta $\nu = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{0.6} = 5 \times 10^8 \text{ Hz} = 500 \text{ MHz}$.

Answer: 500 MHz

PGT extension

A He-Ne laser operates at 632.8 nm. If the Doppler broadened gain linewidth is 1.5 GHz, estimate the coherence length of the laser output assuming single-mode operation governed by this linewidth.

  • Identify given parameters: Wavelength \lambda = 632.8 nm, linewidth \Delta $\nu = 1.5 \text{ GHz} = 1.5 \times 10^9 \text{ Hz}$, $c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$.
  • Use the coherence length approximation formula: L_c \approx \frac{c}{\Delta \nu}.
  • Substitute the values: $L_c = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{1.5 \times 10^9}$.
  • Perform division: $L_c = 0.2 \text{ meters} = 20 \text{ cm}$.

Answer: 0.2 m (or 20 cm)

PGT extension

Determine the approximate mode number q for a He-Ne laser operating at \lambda = 632.8 nm with a cavity length L = 50 cm. Assume refractive index \mu = 1.

  • Recall the resonance condition: 2 L = q \lambda.
  • Rearrange to solve for mode number q: q = \frac{2 L}{\lambda}.
  • Substitute the given values: L = 0.5 \text{ m}, $\lambda = 632.8 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}$.
  • Calculate q: $q = \frac{2 \times 0.5}{632.8 \times 10^{-9}} = \frac{1}{632.8 \times 10^{-9}} \approx 1.58 \times 10^6$.

Answer: 1.58 x 10^6

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes a uniform refractive index (mu = 1) inside the active discharge region.
  • Neglects intra-cavity diffraction losses and mirror transmission losses in elementary gain threshold calculations.
  • Limitation: He-Ne laser output power is relatively low (typically 1 mW to 50 mW) compared to solid-state or CO_2 lasers due to low discharge efficiency.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Helium atoms directly emit the laser beam in a He-Ne laser.

Correct: Neon atoms emit the laser photons.

Helium atoms serve only to absorb discharge energy and transfer it collisionally to neon atoms, which provide the actual laser active transitions.

Common error: The 632.8 nm line is the only transition possible in a He-Ne laser.

Correct: Infrared transitions at 1.15 um and 3.39 um also exist.

The laser cavity mirrors are specifically coated to introduce high losses for infrared wavelengths, forcing oscillation only at the 632.8 nm visible red wavelength.

Common error: Population inversion in He-Ne requires optical pumping with a flashlamp.

Correct: Population inversion is achieved via electrical discharge pumping and resonant collisional transfer.

Gas lasers like He-Ne utilize DC electrical discharges rather than optical lamps for excitation.

Quick revision

  • Active medium in He-Ne laser: Mixture of Helium and Neon in 10:1 ratio.
  • Principal emission wavelength: 632.8 nm (visible red).
  • Pumping mechanism: Electric DC discharge combined with resonant collisional energy transfer.
  • Lower laser state depopulation: Requires narrow discharge tube diameter for fast wall collisions.
  • Longitudinal mode frequency separation: Delta $\nu = c / (2 L)$.

Must remember

  • The active medium in a He-Ne laser is a mixture of helium and neon gases with a typical partial pressure ratio of $10:1$.
  • Helium acts as the primary excitation medium via electron impact, while neon provides the active laser energy levels.
  • Population inversion is achieved through resonant collisional energy transfer from excited metastable helium states ($2^1S$ and $2^3S$) to neon atoms ($3s$ and $2s$ levels).
  • The primary visible laser transition occurs from the $\text{Ne}(3s_2)$ to $\text{Ne}(2p_4)$ state, yielding coherent red light at $\lambda = 632.8 \text{ nm}$.
  • Infrared transitions also occur at $1.15 \text{ } \mu\text{m}$ and $3.39 \text{ } \mu\text{m}$, which are suppressed using selective dielectric mirror coatings.
  • A narrow discharge tube diameter is essential to ensure rapid non-radiative de-excitation of lower laser level atoms ($2p$ to $1s$ and to the absolute ground state via wall collisions) to prevent bottlenecking.
  • Longitudinal mode frequency spacing is given by $\Delta \nu = \frac{c}{2 L}$ where $L$ is the cavity length.
  • Doppler broadening ($\Delta \nu_D$) dictates the gain linewidth due to thermal motion of gas atoms.

Glossary

Active MediumThe collection of atoms or molecules (He-Ne mixture) in which population inversion and stimulated emission take place.
Metastable StateAn excited atomic state with a relatively long lifetime from which spontaneous radiative decay is forbidden or highly improbable.
Resonant Energy TransferCollision-mediated transfer of excitation energy between atoms whose energy levels nearly match ($E(He^*) \approx E(Ne)$).
Population InversionA non-equilibrium condition where a higher energy state holds a greater population of atoms than a lower energy state.
Longitudinal ModesStanding wave frequency patterns along the optical cavity axis satisfying phase resonance conditions, separated by $\Delta \nu = \frac{c}{2 L}$.
Doppler BroadeningThe frequency spread of spectral emission lines caused by the thermal random motion of emitting atoms relative to the observer.
Threshold GainThe minimum optical gain required per unit length ($\gamma_{th}$) to overcome cavity losses and sustain laser oscillation.
BottleneckingAn undesirable accumulation of atoms in the lower laser level that quenches population inversion, prevented in He-Ne by narrow tube dimensions.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the typical partial pressure ratio of helium to neon in a standard He-Ne laser tube?

Explanation: In a standard He-Ne laser, the active medium consists of helium and neon maintained at a partial pressure ratio of approximately $10:1$ to ensure efficient energy transfer.

2. Which component of the gas mixture is directly excited by free electrons in the electric DC discharge?

Explanation: Free electrons accelerated by the electric DC discharge collide primarily with helium atoms, exciting them to high-lying metastable states ($2^1S$ and $2^3S$).

3. How is energy transferred from excited helium atoms to unexcited neon atoms?

Explanation: Excited helium atoms collide non-radiatively with unexcited neon atoms, transferring energy efficiently because their excitation energies nearly match ($E(He^*) \approx E(Ne)$).

4. What is the wavelength of the primary visible red laser transition in a He-Ne laser?

Explanation: The primary visible red laser transition occurs from the $\text{Ne}(3s_2)$ to $\text{Ne}(2p_4)$ state, yielding coherent radiation at $\lambda = 632.8 \text{ nm}$.

5. Which neon energy states are selectively populated via resonant collisions with helium atoms?

Explanation: Resonant collisional transfer selectively populates the $3s$ and $2s$ levels of neon, achieving population inversion relative to lower-lying $p$-levels.

6. Why is the discharge tube in a He-Ne laser kept narrow in diameter?

Explanation: A narrow tube diameter is crucial so that neon atoms in lower laser states collide rapidly with the inner tube walls, returning to the ground state and preventing population bottlenecking.

7. What is the formula for the longitudinal mode frequency spacing $\Delta \nu$ of an optical cavity of length $L$?

Explanation: For a refractive index $\mu = 1$ inside the discharge tube, the longitudinal mode frequency separation is given by $\Delta \nu = \frac{c}{2 L}$.

8. How are undesired infrared transitions at $1.15 \text{ } \mu\text{m}$ and $3.39 \text{ } \mu\text{m}$ suppressed in a visible He-Ne laser?

Explanation: Optical cavity mirrors are selectively coated for $632.8 \text{ nm}$ to introduce high reflection losses for infrared wavelengths, suppressing lasing at $1.15 \text{ } \mu\text{m}$ and $3.39 \text{ } \mu\text{m}$.

9. What is the ultimate ground state of neon atoms to which atoms return before being re-excited?

Explanation: From the $1s$ metastable state, neon atoms de-excite to the absolute ground state ($1s^0$) primarily through non-radiative collisions with the inner walls of the narrow discharge tube.

10. What causes Doppler broadening ($\Delta \nu_D$) of spectral lines in a gas laser like He-Ne?

Explanation: Doppler broadening arises due to the thermal equilibrium and random velocity distribution of the gas atoms emitting the radiation.

11. Which of the following statements about the He-Ne laser pumping mechanism is correct?

Explanation: Gas lasers such as He-Ne utilize DC electrical discharges for initial excitation, followed by resonant collisional energy transfer from helium to neon.

12. What parameter is evaluated by the expression $\gamma_{th} = \alpha + \frac{1}{2 L} \ln\left(\frac{1}{R_1 R_2}\right)$?

Explanation: This formula defines the threshold gain $\gamma_{th}$ required for steady-state laser oscillation, accounting for internal losses $\alpha$ and mirror reflectivities $R_1, R_2$.

13. Which atom actually emits the coherent laser photons in a He-Ne laser?

Explanation: Neon atoms provide the laser active energy levels and emit the laser photons, while helium atoms act solely as the primary excitation medium.

14. What is the physical significance of the Einstein $B_{12}$ coefficient in the stimulated emission rate formula $R_{stim} = B_{12} \rho(\nu) N$?

Explanation: The Einstein $B$ coefficient relates the rate of stimulated emission (or absorption) to the electromagnetic energy density $\rho(\nu)$ of the radiation field.

15. In the context of optical resonators, what does $d_{opt} = \mu L$ represent?

Explanation: The optical path length $d_{opt}$ is the product of the refractive index $\mu$ of the intra-cavity medium and the geometric length $L$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. In a Helium-Neon laser, population inversion between energy levels is achieved primarily through which process?

Explanation: Excited metastable helium atoms transfer their energy via non-radiative resonant collisions to unexcited neon atoms, selectively populating the $3s$ and $2s$ levels to achieve population inversion.

2. What is the primary role of helium gas in a He-Ne laser system?

Explanation: Helium atoms are easily excited by electron impact in the DC discharge and serve to transfer energy collisionally to neon atoms, which are the active lasing species.

3. The longitudinal mode frequency spacing of a laser cavity of length $L$ with intra-cavity refractive index $\mu = 1$ is given by:

Explanation: The frequency separation between adjacent longitudinal modes of a Fabry-Perot cavity is given by $\Delta \nu = \frac{c}{2 L}$ when $\mu = 1$.

4. Why are narrow discharge tubes utilized in Helium-Neon lasers?

Explanation: A narrow tube diameter allows neon atoms in the lower $2p$ and $1s$ states to collide rapidly with the tube walls, ensuring fast de-excitation and preventing population bottlenecking.

5. Which transition in a He-Ne laser corresponds to the emission of coherent red light at $632.8 \text{ nm}$?

Explanation: The well-known visible red laser transition in a He-Ne laser takes place from the $3s_2$ state to the $2p_4$ state in neon, producing $632.8 \text{ nm}$ light.

6. What is the main purpose of coating optical cavity mirrors specifically for $632.8 \text{ nm}$ in a He-Ne laser?

Explanation: Selective mirror coatings introduce high reflection losses for competing infrared transitions ($1.15 \text{ } \mu\text{m}$ and $3.39 \text{ } \mu\text{m}$), forcing the laser to oscillate predominantly at $632.8 \text{ nm}$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What specific metastable states of helium are primarily responsible for energy transfer to neon atoms?

Explanation: Free electrons excite helium atoms to high-lying metastable states denoted as $2^1S$ and $2^3S$, which possess lifetimes and energies matching neon excitation levels.

2. Which of the following expressions correctly represents the Doppler gain linewidth $\Delta \nu_D$ for gas atoms of atomic mass $M$ at temperature $T$?

Explanation: The Doppler broadening formula for thermal equilibrium of gas atoms is given by $\Delta \nu_D = \frac{\nu_0}{c} \sqrt{\frac{8 k_B T \ln 2}{M}}$.

3. What would happen to population inversion in a He-Ne laser if the discharge tube diameter were made excessively large?

Explanation: If the tube is too wide, atoms in the lower $2p$ laser levels cannot collide efficiently with the tube walls to de-excite, leading to bottlenecking and loss of population inversion.

4. In the threshold gain condition formula $\gamma_{th} = \alpha + \frac{1}{2 L} \ln\left(\frac{1}{R_1 R_2}\right)$, what does $\alpha$ represent?

Explanation: In laser threshold equations, $\alpha$ represents the internal losses distributed per unit length within the optical resonator.

5. Which statement correctly contrasts TGT core and PGT extension expectations for the He-Ne laser?

Explanation: UP TGT focuses on conceptual working principles and basic identification, whereas UP PGT expects deeper analytical knowledge such as energy level diagrams, line broadening, and mode spacing.

6. What is the coherence length $L_c$ approximately equal to for a spectral linewidth $\Delta \nu$?

Explanation: The coherence length is approximately given by $L_c \approx \frac{c}{\Delta \nu}$, relating spatial coherence to spectral linewidth.

7. Why is neon rather than helium chosen as the laser active gas in a He-Ne system?

Explanation: Neon has energy level structures that facilitate effective four-level laser transitions, whereas helium's role is strictly auxiliary for energy absorption and transfer.

8. What optical phenomenon defines the frequency distribution of radiation emitted within the gain bandwidth of a He-Ne laser?

Explanation: The output spectrum is determined by the Doppler gain linewidth enveloping discrete longitudinal modes permitted by the cavity length $L$.

9. If the length $L$ of a He-Ne laser optical cavity is increased, how does the longitudinal mode frequency spacing $\Delta \nu$ change?

Explanation: Since $\Delta \nu = \frac{c}{2 L}$, increasing the cavity length $L$ decreases the frequency spacing between adjacent longitudinal modes.

10. Which of the following correctly pairs a He-Ne laser transition with its respective energy states in Neon?

Explanation: The visible red $632.8 \text{ nm}$ laser transition proceeds specifically from the $\text{Ne}(3s_2)$ state to the $\text{Ne}(2p_4)$ state.
