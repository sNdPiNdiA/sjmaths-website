General Concept of Laser Emission

Laser emission is the amplification of light by stimulated emission of radiation, producing a monochromatic, coherent, and highly directional beam. The foundational mechanism involves pumping atoms into an excited metastable state to achieve population inversion.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Basic principles of laser action, spontaneous versus stimulated emission, Einstein's A and B coefficients, pumping mechanisms, and essential properties of laser light.

PGT extension

Quantitative derivations of Einstein coefficients, threshold conditions for lasing, three-level and four-level laser pumping schemes, and line broadening mechanisms.

Prerequisites

  • Bohr's atomic model and energy levels
  • Planck's radiation law and thermal equilibrium
  • Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution of energy states

Stimulated and Spontaneous Emission

  • Spontaneous emission occurs when an electron in an excited state drops to a lower energy state randomly, emitting a photon with random phase and direction.
  • Stimulated emission occurs when an incident photon of energy equal to the energy level difference (E&sub2; - E&sub1;) triggers an excited electron to drop, releasing a second identical photon.
  • The photon produced via stimulated emission is strictly in phase, has the same frequency, polarization, and travels in the same direction as the incident photon, ensuring coherence.

Population Inversion and Metastable States

  • Under thermal equilibrium, the population of lower energy states always exceeds higher energy states according to the Boltzmann factor.
  • Population inversion is a non-equilibrium condition where the population of a higher energy level exceeds that of a lower energy level (N&sub2; > N&sub1;).
  • A metastable state with a relatively long lifetime (typically 10&sup-;³ s compared to 10&sup-;&sup8; s for ordinary excited states) is essential for accumulating atoms and sustaining population inversion.

Pumping Mechanisms and Optical Resonators

  • Pumping is the external process of supplying energy to the active medium to raise atoms from the ground state to higher excited states.
  • Common pumping methods include optical pumping (using flashlamps or lasers), electrical discharge, and chemical reactions.
  • An optical resonator, consisting of a pair of parallel mirrors enclosing the active medium, provides optical feedback to amplify the stimulated emission along the axis.

Three-Level and Four-Level Laser Systems

  • In a three-level laser, lasing occurs between the upper laser state and the ground state, requiring more than half the ground-state atoms to be pumped.
  • In a four-level laser, lasing occurs between an upper laser state and a lower laser state that rapidly empties into the ground state, significantly lowering the pump threshold.
  • Four-level systems are much more efficient and can operate in continuous-wave (CW) mode at room temperature.

Derivations

Einstein's Relations for Spontaneous and Stimulated Emission

Conditions: Thermal equilibrium between radiation field and atomic energy levels E1 and E2.

  • Write the rate of absorption from level 1 to level 2: $R_{12} = N_1 \rho(\nu) B_{12}$, where N_1 is population, \rho(\nu) is energy density, and B_{12} is Einstein coefficient for absorption.
  • Write the total rate of emission from level 2 to level 1, combining spontaneous and stimulated terms: $R_{21} = N_2 A_{21} + N_2 \rho(\nu) B_{21}$.
  • Equate absorption rate to total emission rate under thermal equilibrium: N_1 \rho(\nu) $B_{12} = N_2 A_{21} + N_2 \rho(\nu) B_{21}$.
  • Use Boltzmann distribution for populations: N_2 / $N_1 = (g_2 / g_1) exp(-h\nu / k_B T)$, and Planck's law for energy density \rho(\nu) to solve for A_{21} / B_{21} ratio.

Result: A_{21} / $B_{21} = \frac{8pi h \nu^3}{c^3}$ and g_1 $B_{12} = g_2 B_{21}$

Threshold Gain Condition for Laser Oscillation

Conditions: Steady-state operation in a Fabry-Perot optical resonator of length L with mirror reflection coefficients R1 and R2.

    Result: gamm$a_{th} = \alpha + \frac{1}{2L} \ln\left(\frac{1}{R_1 R_2}\right)$

    Solved numericals

    TGT core

    Calculate the ratio of the rate of spontaneous emission to stimulated emission for a radiation field at temperature 300 K operating at a wavelength of 500 nm.

    • Identify given values: $\lambda = 500 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}$, T = 300 \text{ K}, $h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}$, $c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$, $k_B = 1.38 \times 10^{-23} \text{ J/K}$.
    • Calculate photon energy: h$\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = \frac{(6.63 \times 10^{-34})(3 \times 10^8)}{500 \times 10^{-9}} = 3.978 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}$.
    • Calculate thermal energy term: k_B $T = (1.38 \times 10^{-23})(300) = 4.14 \times 10^{-21} \text{ J}$.
    • Compute exponent: \frac{h\nu}{k_B T} = \frac{3.978 \times 10^{-19}}{4.14 \times 10^{-21}} \approx 96.08.
    • Calculate ratio: \frac{R_{spont}}{R_{stim}} = \frac{1}{\exp(96.08) - 1} \approx \exp(-96.08) \approx 3.3 \times 10^{-42}.

    Answer: 3.3 x 10^-42 (Spontaneous emission is entirely negligible compared to stimulated emission in the visible range at room temperature).

    PGT extension

    A ruby laser has a rod of length 10 cm with refractive index 1.78. The reflectivities of the two end mirrors are R1 = 1.0 and R2 = 0.8. Calculate the threshold gain coefficient if internal scattering losses are negligible.

    • Identify given parameters: L = 0.1 \text{ m}, $R_1 = 1.0$, $R_2 = 0.8$, \alph$a_s = 0$.
    • Use the threshold gain formula: \gamm$a_{th} = \alpha_s + \frac{1}{2L} \ln\left(\frac{1}{R_1 R_2}\right)$.
    • Substitute values: \gamm$a_{th} = 0 + \frac{1}{2(0.1)} \ln\left(\frac{1}{(1.0)(0.8)}\right)$.
    • Simplify terms: \gamm$a_{th} = \frac{1}{0.2} \ln(1.25) = 5 \times 0.2231$.
    • Calculate final numerical value: \gamm$a_{th} = 1.115 \text{ m}^{-1}$.

    Answer: 1.115 m^-1

    PGT extension

    Find the frequency separation between adjacent longitudinal modes of a He-Ne laser having a cavity length of 30 cm and refractive index of the medium equal to 1.

    • Identify given data: L = 0.3 \text{ m}, n = 1, $c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$.
    • Recall longitudinal mode spacing formula: \Delta \nu = \frac{c}{2nL}.
    • Substitute values: \Delta $\nu = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{2 \times 1 \times 0.3}$.
    • Compute result: \Delta $\nu = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{0.6} = 5 \times 10^8 \text{ Hz} = 500 \text{ MHz}$.

    Answer: 500 MHz

    Assumptions and limitations

    • Einstein's coefficient derivation assumes a blackbody radiation isotropic field interacting with two-level atomic systems.
    • Fabry-Perot cavity analysis assumes uniform plane wave propagation and neglects transverse mode variations (TEM_00 mode assumption).
    • Steady-state gain conditions assume homogeneous line broadening unless saturation intensity parameters are explicitly integrated.

    Common misconceptions

    Common error: Spontaneous emission contributes to the coherent beam in a laser.

    Correct: Spontaneous emission provides the initial seed photons, but the amplified laser beam itself consists entirely of coherent photons created via stimulated emission.

    Spontaneous emission is random in phase and direction, whereas stimulated emission clones the phase and direction of the stimulating photon.

    Common error: Population inversion means that the total number of atoms in the excited state is greater than the ground state absolute total.

    Correct: Population inversion specifically refers to the population of a higher laser level exceeding that of the specific lower laser level involved in the transition, not necessarily the absolute ground state in multilevel systems.

    In four-level lasers, the lower laser level is nearly empty, so inversion can be achieved with relatively few atoms excited.

    Quick revision

    • Laser stands for Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation.
    • Population inversion (N_2 > N_1) is a mandatory requirement for net optical amplification.
    • Einstein A coefficient governs spontaneous emission; B coefficients govern absorption and stimulated emission.
    • Four-level lasers have lower thresholds than three-level lasers because the lower laser level empties rapidly.

    Must remember

    • Stimulated emission produces a photon strictly in phase, of the same frequency, polarization, and direction as the incident photon.
    • Population inversion ($N_2 > N_1$) is a non-equilibrium state essential for laser action and requires external pumping.
    • A metastable state possesses a relatively long lifetime (typically $10^{-3} \text{ s}$) compared to ordinary excited states ($10^{-8} \text{ s}$).
    • Four-level laser systems are more efficient than three-level systems because the lower laser level rapidly empties into the ground state, lowering the threshold.
    • The Einstein A and B ratio is given by $\frac{A_{21}}{B_{21}} = \frac{8\pi h \nu^3}{c^3}$, connecting spontaneous and stimulated emission probabilities.

    Glossary

    Population InversionA non-thermal equilibrium condition where the population of atoms in a higher energy level exceeds that of a lower energy level ($N_2 > N_1$).
    Metastable StateAn intermediate excited energy state with an unusually long lifetime, crucial for accumulating atoms to achieve population inversion.
    Optical PumpingThe process of using high-intensity light sources such as flashlamps or secondary lasers to excite atoms to higher energy levels.
    Optical ResonatorAn arrangement of mirrors that reflects light back and forth through the active laser medium, providing feedback and amplification.
    Einstein CoefficientsCoefficients ($A_{21}, B_{12}, B_{21}$) quantifying the probabilities of spontaneous emission, stimulated absorption, and stimulated emission.

    Practice Quiz

    Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

    1. Which of the following processes is responsible for the amplification of light in a laser?

    Explanation: Stimulated emission triggers an excited electron to drop and release a second identical photon with the same phase, frequency, and direction, providing optical amplification.

    2. What is the typical lifetime of a metastable state compared to ordinary excited states?

    Explanation: Metastable states have a relatively long lifetime of typically $10^{-3} \text{ s}$, whereas ordinary excited states decay within about $10^{-8} \text{ s}$.

    3. Under thermal equilibrium, the population of energy states follows which distribution?

    Explanation: According to the Boltzmann factor, the population of lower energy states always exceeds higher energy states under thermal equilibrium.

    4. Population inversion is defined as a condition where:

    Explanation: Population inversion is a non-equilibrium state where the population of the higher energy level exceeds that of the lower energy level ($N_2 > N_1$).

    5. What is the primary function of an optical resonator in a laser system?

    Explanation: An optical resonator consisting of parallel mirrors encloses the active medium and provides optical feedback to amplify stimulated emission along the axis.

    6. In a three-level laser system, the lower laser level is typically:

    Explanation: In a three-level laser, lasing occurs between the upper laser state and the ground state, requiring more than half the ground-state atoms to be pumped.

    7. Why are four-level lasers generally more efficient than three-level lasers?

    Explanation: In four-level lasers, the lower laser level rapidly empties, maintaining a near-zero population in that level and significantly lowering the pump threshold.

    8. The Einstein A to B ratio $\frac{A_{21}}{B_{21}}$ is proportional to:

    Explanation: From Planck's radiation law and Einstein coefficients, $\frac{A_{21}}{B_{21}} = \frac{8\pi h \nu^3}{c^3}$, which shows proportionality to $\nu^3$.

    9. The Bohr frequency condition for the energy of an emitted photon is given by:

    Explanation: The energy difference between two levels equals the energy of the emitted photon: $E_2 - E_1 = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$.

    10. Which pumping method is commonly used in solid-state lasers like Ruby?

    Explanation: Ruby lasers use optical pumping via high-intensity flashlamps or secondary lasers to excite chromium ions.

    11. What does spontaneous emission contribute directly to in a laser?

    Explanation: Spontaneous emission provides the initial random seed photons that trigger subsequent stimulated emissions inside the cavity.

    12. The threshold gain coefficient $\gamma_{th}$ depends on internal loss $\alpha_s$, cavity length $L$, and mirror reflectivities $R_1, R_2$ as:

    Explanation: The steady-state threshold gain condition is given by $\gamma_{th} = \alpha_s + \frac{1}{2L} \ln\left(\frac{1}{R_1 R_2}\right)$.

    13. Longitudinal mode frequencies $\nu_q$ of an optical resonator of length $L$ and refractive index $n$ are given by:

    Explanation: Fabry-Perot longitudinal mode frequencies are given by $\nu_q = \frac{qc}{2nL}$ where $q$ is an integer mode number.

    14. Which of the following statements about stimulated emission is correct?

    Explanation: Stimulated emission produces a photon identical in phase, frequency, polarization, and direction to the incident photon, ensuring spatial and temporal coherence.

    15. What is the Boltzmann population ratio for two energy levels $E_1$ and $E_2$ with degeneracies $g_1$ and $g_2$ at temperature $T$?

    Explanation: The Boltzmann population ratio under thermal equilibrium is $\frac{N_2}{N_1} = \frac{g_2}{g_1} \exp\left(-\frac{h\nu}{k_B T}\right)$.

    PYQ Patterns

    These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

    1. If the ratio of Einstein coefficients $A_{21}$ to $B_{21}$ for a transition at frequency $\nu$ is proportional to $\nu^n$, the value of $n$ is:

    Explanation: From the Einstein relation $\frac{A_{21}}{B_{21}} = \frac{8\pi h \nu^3}{c^3}$, the ratio is proportional to $\nu^3$, so $n = 3$.

    2. In a laser cavity of length $L = 30 \text{ cm}$ filled with a medium of refractive index $n = 1.5$, what is the frequency separation $\Delta \nu$ between adjacent longitudinal modes ($q$ and $q+1$)?

    Explanation: The longitudinal mode frequency is $\nu_q = \frac{qc}{2nL}$. The separation between adjacent modes is $\Delta \nu = \frac{c}{2nL} = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{2 \times 1.5 \times 0.3} = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{0.9} \approx 3.33 \times 10^8 \text{ Hz}$ (Wait, recalculating: $2 \times 1.5 \times 0.3 = 0.9$, $3 \times 10^8 / 0.9 = 3.33 \times 10^8$. Let's adjust options: if $L=50 \text{ cm}$, $2 \times 1.5 \times 0.5 = 1.5$, $3 \times 10^8 / 1.5 = 2 \times 10^8$. Let's use $L = 50 \text{ cm}$, refractive index $n = 1.5$. Then $\Delta \nu = \frac{c}{2nL} = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{2 \times 1.5 \times 0.5} = 2 \times 10^8 \text{ Hz}$). Let's fix the option values. Option B: $2 \times 10^8 \text{ Hz}$.

    3. Which condition is fundamentally necessary to achieve laser action in any active medium?

    Explanation: Population inversion ($N_2 > N_1$) is the non-equilibrium condition required to ensure that stimulated emission exceeds stimulated absorption, enabling net optical gain.

    4. The quality factor $Q$ of an optical laser cavity is given in terms of resonance frequency $\nu_0$ and power loss per cycle as:

    Explanation: The quality factor $Q$ of an oscillator is defined as $2\pi \times \frac{\text{stored energy}}{\text{energy loss per cycle}} = \frac{2\pi \nu_0 \times \text{stored energy}}{\text{power loss per cycle}}.$

    5. What is the primary reason why three-level lasers require high pumping power compared to four-level lasers?

    Explanation: In a three-level system, the lower laser level is the ground state, which initially holds nearly all the atoms. To achieve inversion ($N_2 > N_1$), over $50\%$ of the atoms must be pumped to the upper state.

    6. The ratio of spontaneous emission rate to stimulated emission rate is given by:

    Explanation: Under thermal equilibrium blackbody radiation field, $\frac{R_{spont}}{R_{stim}} = \frac{1}{\exp(h\nu / k_B T) - 1}$.

    Timed Topic Test

    Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

    1. What characteristic property of laser light distinguishes it fundamentally from ordinary thermal light sources?

    Explanation: Laser light is characterized by high spatial and temporal coherence because all photons are produced via stimulated emission, sharing identical phase, frequency, and direction.

    2. If the mirror reflectivities of a laser cavity are $R_1 = 0.9$ and $R_2 = 0.9$, and internal losses are negligible ($\alpha_s = 0$), what term accounts for mirror transmission losses in the threshold gain condition for a cavity of length $L$?

    Explanation: Since $\ln(R_1 R_2) = \ln(0.9 \times 0.9) = \ln(0.81)$, the mirror loss term in $\gamma_{th}$ is $\frac{1}{2L} \ln\left(\frac{1}{R_1 R_2}\right) = -\frac{1}{2L} \ln(0.81)$.

    3. In the Einstein relation for stimulated emission and absorption coefficients, assuming non-degenerate levels ($g_1 = g_2 = 1$), the relationship between $B_{12}$ and $B_{21}$ is:

    Explanation: For non-degenerate energy levels, the probability coefficients for stimulated absorption and stimulated emission are equal: $B_{12} = B_{21}$.

    4. Which of the following optical pumping mechanisms is typically used in gas lasers such as He-Ne lasers?

    Explanation: He-Ne lasers utilize electrical discharge (electron collision) to excite helium atoms, which subsequently transfer energy to neon atoms via resonant collision.

    5. What is the physical significance of a metastable state in laser physics?

    Explanation: Because of its unusually long lifetime ($10^{-3} \text{ s}$), a metastable state traps electrons long enough to build up a large population, making population inversion achievable.

    6. The spatial coherence area $A_c$ of a laser beam in the far-field approximation is inversely proportional to:

    Explanation: The spatial coherence area is given by $A_c \approx \frac{\lambda^2}{\Omega}$, meaning it is inversely proportional to the solid angle of beam divergence $\Omega$.

    7. Which laser system is a classic example of a four-level solid-state laser?

    Explanation: The Neodymium-doped YAG (Nd:YAG) laser is a prominent example of a four-level solid-state laser operating efficiently in continuous-wave mode at room temperature.

    8. In a blackbody radiation field at very high temperatures ($k_B T \gg h\nu$), the ratio of stimulated emission to spontaneous emission rates becomes:

    Explanation: Since $\frac{R_{stim}}{R_{spont}} = \exp(h\nu / k_B T) - 1 \approx \frac{h\nu}{k_B T}$ when $k_B T \gg h\nu$, stimulated emission dominates over spontaneous emission at high temperatures or low frequencies.

    9. What happens to the threshold pump power if the internal scattering losses $\alpha_s$ of a laser cavity increase?

    Explanation: Higher internal scattering losses increase the threshold gain coefficient $\gamma_{th}$, which in turn requires a higher pump power to achieve lasing.

    10. Which of the following correctly describes the phase relationship between photons generated in stimulated emission?

    Explanation: Stimulated emission produces photons that are strictly in phase with the incident photon, which is the foundational requirement for optical coherence.
