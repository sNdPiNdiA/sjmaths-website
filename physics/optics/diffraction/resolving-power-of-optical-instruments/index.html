Resolving Power of Optical Instruments

The resolving power of an optical instrument is its ability to distinguish between two closely spaced point objects as separate entities. It is fundamentally limited by the diffraction of light waves at the aperture of the instrument.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Rayleigh's criterion for resolution, resolving power of microscopes and telescopes, dependence on wavelength and aperture size.

PGT extension

Quantitative derivation of resolving power for rectangular and circular apertures, diffraction grating resolution, Rayleigh limit for spectral lines.

Prerequisites

  • Wave theory of light
  • Fraunhofer diffraction at a single slit and circular aperture
  • Constructive and destructive interference conditions

Rayleigh Criterion for Resolution

  • Two point sources are resolved if the principal maximum of the diffraction pattern of one falls exactly over the first minimum of the diffraction pattern of the other.
  • When the central maximum of one pattern coincides with the central maximum of the other such that their separation is less than the Rayleigh limit, the sources are unresolved.
  • At the limit of resolution, the dip between the two central maxima in the combined intensity distribution is approximately 20% lower than the maximum intensity.

Resolving Power of a Microscope

  • Resolving power of a microscope is defined as the reciprocal of the minimum linear distance between two point objects that can be separately distinguished.
  • It is directly proportional to the numerical aperture of the objective lens and inversely proportional to the wavelength of light used for illumination.
  • Oil immersion objectives are used to increase the refractive index of the medium between the object and the objective, thereby enhancing resolving power.

Resolving Power of a Telescope

  • Resolving power of a telescope is defined as the reciprocal of the smallest angular separation (dθ) between two distant point objects whose images can be resolved.
  • It is directly proportional to the diameter of the objective lens or mirror and inversely proportional to the wavelength of light.
  • Larger aperture telescopes are required to resolve closely spaced binary stars or fine details on planetary surfaces.

Resolving Power of a Diffraction Grating

  • The resolving power of a diffraction grating measures its ability to form separate diffraction maxima for two nearby wavelengths (λ and λ + dλ).
  • It depends directly on the total number of rulings on the grating and the order of the diffraction spectrum.
  • It is expressed mathematically as R = (lambda / dlambda) = Nn, where N is total lines and n is spectral order.

Derivations

Resolving Power of a Telescope

Conditions: Fraunhofer diffraction by a circular aperture of diameter D

  • The first minimum of the diffraction pattern produced by a circular aperture of diameter D occurs at an angular position given by sin(θ) = 1.22 (λ / D).
  • For small angles, sin(θ) ≈ θ.
  • Therefore, the minimum angular separation for two point objects to be resolved according to Rayleigh's criterion is dθ = 1.22 (λ / D).

Result: RP = 1 / dθ = D / (1.22 λ)

Resolving Power of a Microscope

Conditions: Light of wavelength &lambda; entering an objective of semi-vertical angle u in a medium of refractive index &mu;

  • The limit of resolution (minimum distance d) for a circular aperture is given by Abbe's theory combined with Rayleigh's criterion as d = (λ / (2 μ sin(u))).
  • The term μ sin(u) is known as the Numerical Aperture (NA) of the microscope.
  • Resolving Power (RP) is the reciprocal of the limit of resolution d.

Result: RP = 1 / d = (2 μ sin(u)) / λ = (2 NA) / λ

Solved numericals

TGT core

A telescope has an objective lens of diameter 1.0 m. Calculate its resolving power for light of wavelength 550 nm.

  • Identify given values: D = 1.0 m, \lambda = 550 \text{ nm} = 5.5 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}.
  • Use the formula for resolving power of a telescope: RP = D / (1.22 \lambda).
  • Substitute the values: RP = 1.0 / (1.22 \times 5.5 \times 10^{-7}) = 1.0 / (6.71 \times 10^{-7}).
  • Calculate final value: RP \approx 1.49 \times 10^6 \text{ rad}^{-1}.

Answer: 1.49 \times 10^6 \text{ rad}^{-1}

TGT core

Light of wavelength 6000 Å is incident on a microscope objective with a numerical aperture of 0.12. Find the limit of resolution.

  • Identify given values: \lambda = 6000 \text{ Å} = 6 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}, \text{NA} = 0.12.
  • Use the formula for limit of resolution of a microscope: d = \lambda / (2 \text{ NA}).
  • Substitute the values: d = (6 \times 10^{-7}) / (2 \times 0.12) = (6 \times 10^{-7}) / 0.24.
  • Calculate final value: d = 2.5 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m} = 2.5 \mu\text{m}.

Answer: 2.5 \mu\text{m}

PGT extension

A diffraction grating has 500 lines per cm and is 5 cm wide. Find its resolving power in the second order for a wavelength of 5000 Å.

  • Find the total number of lines N = (lines per cm) \times (width in cm) = 500 \text{ cm}^{-1} \times 5 \text{ cm} = 2500.
  • Identify order n = 2 and wavelength \lambda = 5000 \text{ Å}.
  • Use the formula for resolving power of a grating: R = N n.
  • Substitute values: R = 2500 \times 2 = 5000.

Answer: 5000

Assumptions and limitations

  • Rayleigh's criterion assumes incoherent point sources of equal intensity.
  • The analysis assumes Fraunhofer diffraction conditions (source and screen at infinity or via lenses).
  • Aberrations in optical lenses are neglected when calculating theoretical resolving power limits.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Magnification and resolving power mean the same thing for an optical instrument.

Correct: Magnification enlarges the image size, whereas resolving power allows two close details to be distinguished separately.

An image can be made arbitrarily large (high magnification) without adding any new structural details if the resolving power is insufficient.

Common error: Decreasing the wavelength of light decreases the resolving power of a microscope.

Correct: Shorter wavelengths increase the resolving power.

Resolving power is inversely proportional to wavelength (&lambda;); hence, ultraviolet or electron beams provide higher resolution than visible light.

Quick revision

  • Telescope resolving power increases with larger objective diameter D.
  • Microscope resolving power increases with higher numerical aperture (NA) and shorter wavelength (λ).
  • Grating resolving power depends solely on total lines N and spectral order n.

Must remember

  • According to Rayleigh's criterion, two point sources are just resolved when the central maximum of one diffraction pattern falls on the first minimum of the other.
  • The resolving power of a telescope is given by $RP = \frac{D}{1.22 \lambda}$, showing direct proportionality with objective diameter $D$ and inverse with wavelength $\lambda$.
  • The resolving power of a microscope is expressed as $RP = \frac{2 \mu \sin(u)}{\lambda} = \frac{2 \text{NA}}{\lambda}$, depending directly on numerical aperture and inversely on wavelength.
  • The resolving power of a diffraction grating is $R = \frac{\lambda}{d\lambda} = N n$, depending on the total number of lines $N$ and the spectral order $n$.
  • Magnification increases the visual size of an image, whereas resolving power reveals fine structural details that would otherwise blur together.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. According to Rayleigh's criterion, two point sources are resolved when:

Explanation: Rayleigh's criterion states that two point sources are just resolved if the principal maximum of the diffraction pattern of one falls exactly over the first minimum of the diffraction pattern of the other.

2. The resolving power of a telescope can be increased by:

Explanation: The resolving power of a telescope is given by $RP = \frac{D}{1.22 \lambda}$. It is directly proportional to the diameter $D$ of the objective aperture.

3. What is the limit of resolution for a microscope objective with numerical aperture $\text{NA}$ and illumination wavelength $\lambda$?

Explanation: The limit of resolution for a microscope is $d = \frac{\lambda}{2 \mu \sin(u)} = \frac{\lambda}{2 \text{NA}}$. It represents the minimum distance between two distinguishable point objects.

4. The resolving power of a diffraction grating of total rulings $N$ operating in spectral order $n$ is given by:

Explanation: The resolving power of a diffraction grating is expressed as $R = \frac{\lambda}{d\lambda} = N n$, where $N$ is the total number of lines and $n$ is the spectral order.

5. Why are oil immersion objectives used in high-power microscopes?

Explanation: Oil immersion objectives use a liquid of high refractive index $\mu$ between the specimen and lens, which increases the numerical aperture ($\text{NA} = \mu \sin u$) and thus improves the resolving power.

6. If the wavelength of light used in a telescope is halved, its resolving power will:

Explanation: Since resolving power is inversely proportional to wavelength ($RP \propto \frac{1}{\lambda}$), halving the wavelength doubles the resolving power.

7. At the limit of resolution according to Rayleigh's criterion, the dip between the two central maxima in the combined intensity distribution is:

Explanation: At the exact limit of resolution, the dip between the two central maxima in the combined diffraction intensity curve drops by about 20% relative to the peak intensity.

8. Which of the following statements distinguishes magnification from resolving power?

Explanation: Magnification enlarges the visual size, but without sufficient resolving power (which reveals fine details), an enlarged image remains blurry and resolves no new structural details.

9. The angular separation $d\$\theta$$ of two distant point objects for a telescope with circular aperture of diameter $D$ is:

Explanation: Rayleigh's angular resolution for a circular aperture telescope is given by $d\$\theta$ = \frac{1.22 \lambda}{D}$ in radians.

10. What is the unit of the numerical aperture (NA) of a microscope objective?

Explanation: Numerical aperture is defined as $\text{NA} = \mu \sin(u)$. Since refractive index $\mu$ and sine of an angle are dimensionless, NA is dimensionless.

11. To resolve two spectral lines of wavelengths $589.0 \text{ nm}$ and $589.6 \text{ nm}$ using a diffraction grating in the first order, what minimum resolving power is required?

Explanation: Resolving power required is $R = \frac{\lambda}{d\lambda} = \frac{589.0 \text{ nm}}{0.6 \text{ nm}} = 981.6$.

12. If the diameter of a telescope objective is doubled, the angular resolution limit $d\$\theta$$ becomes:

Explanation: Since $d\$\theta$ = \frac{1.22 \lambda}{D}$, the angular limit of resolution is inversely proportional to objective diameter $D$. Doubling $D$ halves $d\$\theta$$ (improving resolution).

13. What condition must be satisfied for a diffraction grating to resolve two wavelengths $\lambda$ and $\lambda + d\lambda$ in order $n$?

Explanation: The criterion for resolution by a grating equates its resolving power $R = Nn$ to the ratio $\frac{\lambda}{d\lambda}$.

14. Why do ultraviolet microscopes provide higher resolution than visible light microscopes?

Explanation: Resolving power is inversely proportional to wavelength ($\lambda$). Shorter wavelengths, such as ultraviolet rays or electron beams, yield superior resolution.

15. The limit of resolution for a rectangular slit aperture of width $a$ is given by:

Explanation: For a rectangular slit aperture (single slit diffraction), the first minimum occurs at angle $d\$\theta$ = \frac{\lambda}{a}$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. According to Rayleigh's criterion, the minimum angular separation for a telescope is independent of which of the following?

Explanation: Rayleigh's angular separation is $d\$\theta$ = \frac{1.22 \lambda}{D}$, which depends on wavelength $\lambda$ and aperture diameter $D$, but is independent of focal length.

2. In a microscope, if the semi-vertical angle $u$ is $30^\circ$ and the refractive index of the immersion medium is $1.5$, what is the numerical aperture (NA)?

Explanation: $\text{NA} = \mu \sin(u) = 1.5 \times \sin(30^\circ) = 1.5 \times 0.5 = 0.75$.

3. Calculate the resolving power of a telescope whose objective has a diameter of $1.22 \text{ m}$ for light of wavelength $500 \text{ nm}$.

Explanation: $RP = \frac{D}{1.22 \lambda} = \frac{1.22}{1.22 \times 500 \times 10^{-9}} = \frac{1}{500 \times 10^{-9}} = 2.0 \times 10^6 \text{ rad}^{-1}$.

4. A diffraction grating has 4000 lines per cm and is 5 cm wide. What is its maximum resolving power in the 2nd order?

Explanation: Total lines $N = 4000 \text{ lines/cm} \times 5 \text{ cm} = 20000$. Resolving power $R = Nn = 20000 \times 2 = 4.0 \times 10^4$.

5. Which optical instrument utilizes the principle of diffraction limits governed by numerical aperture to resolve sub-micron cellular structures?

Explanation: A compound microscope uses numerical aperture and short-wavelength illumination to resolve microscopic, sub-micron biological and material structures.

6. If the refractive index of the medium between object and microscope objective is increased, the limit of resolution $d$:

Explanation: Since $d = \frac{\lambda}{2 \mu \sin(u)}$, an increase in refractive index $\mu$ decreases the limit of resolution $d$, meaning smaller distances can be resolved.

7. Two star systems are barely resolved by a telescope. If we switch to an infrared filter of longer wavelength, what happens to their resolvability?

Explanation: Longer wavelengths increase $d\$\theta$$ ($d\$\theta$ = \frac{1.22 \lambda}{D}$), making the angular resolution limit larger, so the stars will become unresolved.

8. What is the minimum resolvable wavelength difference ($d\lambda$) for a grating with resolving power $50,000$ at a mean wavelength of $5000 \text{ \AA}$?

Explanation: $R = \frac{\lambda}{d\lambda} \implies d\lambda = \frac{\lambda}{R} = \frac{5000 \text{ \AA}}{50000} = 0.1 \text{ \AA}$.

9. In terms of Rayleigh criterion intensity distribution, what is true at the exact limit of resolution?

Explanation: At Rayleigh's limit of resolution, the combined intensity dip at the center falls to approximately 80% of the maximum peak intensity (i.e., 20% lower).

10. Why are electron microscopes capable of achieving much higher resolving power than optical light microscopes?

Explanation: De Broglie wavelength of electrons ($\lambda = \frac{h}{p}$) is thousands of times smaller than visible light wavelengths, leading to exceptionally high resolving power.
