Optics

Resolving Power of Optical Instruments

Resolving power is the capacity of an optical instrument to distinguish between two closely spaced point objects as separate entities. It is fundamentally limited by the wave nature of light causing diffraction.

UP TGT ScienceUP PGT PhysicsStudy Notes + Practice

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Rayleigh's criterion of resolution, resolving power of microscopes and astronomical telescopes, dependence on wavelength and aperture size.

PGT extension

Derivation of resolving power limits for circular apertures (telescope and microscope), Rayleigh criterion for spectral lines (gratings and prisms), numerical aperture effects.

Prerequisites

  • Fraunhofer diffraction through a single slit and circular aperture
  • Formation of Airy disc pattern
  • Interference and diffraction grating principles

Rayleigh's Criterion for Resolution

  • Two equal point sources are just resolved when the central maximum of the diffraction pattern of one falls on the first minimum of the diffraction pattern of the other.
  • The angular separation theta between two point objects must be at least equal to the angular radius of the first dark ring of the Airy disc.
  • If the separation is less than Rayleigh's limit, the combined intensity profile appears as a single broad maximum, rendering the objects unresolved.

Resolving Power of an Astronomical Telescope

  • Defined as the reciprocal of the smallest angular separation (dθ) between two distant point objects that can be clearly distinguished.
  • Given by the expression RP = rac{1}{d heta} = rac{D}{1.22lambda}, where D is the diameter of the objective lens and λ is the wavelength of light.
  • Increasing the aperture diameter D improves the resolving power and collects more light for faint astronomical objects.

Resolving Power of a Compound Microscope

  • Defined as the reciprocal of the minimum linear distance (Δx) between two point objects that can be resolved.
  • Given by Abbe's criterion: RP = rac{1}{Delta x} = rac{2mu sin heta}{lambda}, where μ is the refractive index of the medium between object and objective, and θ is the semi-vertical angle of the cone of light.
  • The term μ sin(θ) is known as the numerical aperture (NA) of the microscope objective.

Resolving Power of Diffraction Grating

  • Defined as the ability to separate two neighboring spectral lines of wavelengths λ and λ + dλ.
  • Expressed as RP = rac{lambda}{dlambda} = n N, where n is the order of the spectrum and N is the total number of rulings on the grating surface.
  • Higher orders and a greater total number of rulings yield higher spectral resolution.

Derivations

Resolving Power of a Telescope

Conditions: Fraunhofer diffraction of light from a circular aperture of diameter D

  • The angular position of the first minimum of a circular aperture diffraction pattern is given by sin heta approx heta = rac{1.22lambda}{D}.
  • According to Rayleigh's criterion, two point sources are just resolved if their angular separation equals this minimum angle θ.
  • Taking the reciprocal, the resolving power of the telescope is defined as RP = rac{1}{ heta}.

Result: RP = rac{D}{1.22lambda}

Resolving Power of a Microscope

Conditions: Object placed close to the objective lens in a medium of refractive index &mu;

  • The minimum distance for resolution in a medium of refractive index μ considering marginal rays is given by Abbe's theory as Delta x = rac{lambda}{2mu sin heta}.
  • The resolving power is the inverse of this minimum resolvable distance: RP = rac{1}{Delta x}.
  • Substituting the expression for Δx yields the final formula.

Result: RP = rac{2mu sin heta}{lambda}

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the resolving power of a telescope objective of diameter 200 cm for light of wavelength 550 nm.

  • Identify given parameters: D = 200 cm = 2.0 m, λ = 550 nm = 5.5 imes 10^-7 m.
  • Use the formula for telescope resolving power: RP = rac{D}{1.22lambda}.
  • Substitute values: RP = rac{2.0}{1.22 imes 5.5 imes 10^-7} = rac{2.0}{6.71 imes 10^-7}.
  • Calculate result: RP approx 2.98 imes 10^6.

Answer: 2.98 x 10^6

PGT extension

A microscope has an objective with a numerical aperture of 0.12. Using light of wavelength 6000 A, find the minimum separation between two points that can be resolved.

  • Identify parameters: NA = mu sin heta = 0.12, λ = 6000 A = 6 imes 10^-7 m.
  • Use Abbe's criterion for minimum separation: Delta x = rac{lambda}{2mu sin heta} (or 0.61λ / NA depending on convention, using standard textbook form Delta x = rac{lambda}{2 cdot NA}).
  • Substitute values: Delta x = rac{6 imes 10^-7}{2 imes 0.12} = rac{6 imes 10^-7}{0.24}.
  • Calculate final distance: Delta x = 2.5 imes 10^-6 m = 2.5 mu m.

Answer: 2.5 micrometers

PGT extension

Find the minimum number of lines a diffraction grating must have to resolve the sodium doublet lines (&lambda;_1 = 589.0 nm and &lambda;_2 = 589.6 nm) in the second order spectrum.

  • Calculate mean wavelength λ = rac{589.0 + 589.6}{2} = 589.3 nm.
  • Calculate wavelength difference dλ = 589.6 - 589.0 = 0.6 nm.
  • Use resolving power of grating formula: rac{lambda}{dlambda} = nN.
  • Substitute values for order n = 2: rac{589.3}{0.6} = 2N.
  • Solve for N: N = rac{589.3}{1.2} approx 491.

Answer: 491 lines

Assumptions and limitations

  • Rayleigh's criterion is a subjective limit; two point sources can sometimes be resolved even if they do not strictly meet the intensity dip condition by using image processing or high contrast.
  • Derivations assume monochromatic, incoherent point sources situated in the object plane.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Magnification and resolving power are the same thing.

Correct: Magnification only enlarges the image size without adding detail, whereas resolving power determines the ability to reveal fine structural details.

A blurry image can be magnified infinitely, but it will still lack resolution if diffraction limits are breached.

Common error: Decreasing the wavelength of light decreases the resolving power of a microscope.

Correct: Shorter wavelengths increase the resolving power.

Since resolving power is inversely proportional to wavelength (&lambda;), reducing &lambda; (e.g., using ultraviolet or electron beams) increases RP.

Quick revision

  • Telescope resolving power increases with objective diameter D.
  • Microscope resolving power increases with numerical aperture (μ sin θ) and decreases with wavelength λ.
  • Grating resolving power depends directly on the product of order and total number of lines (nN).

Must remember

  • Two equal point sources are just resolved when the central maximum of one falls on the first minimum of the other according to Rayleigh's criterion.
  • The resolving power of an astronomical telescope is given by $RP = \frac{D}{1.22\lambda}$, directly proportional to the aperture diameter $D$.
  • The resolving power of a compound microscope is given by $RP = \frac{2\mu \sin\theta}{\lambda}$, where $\mu \sin\theta$ is the numerical aperture ($NA$).
  • The resolving power of a diffraction grating is expressed as $RP = \frac{\lambda}{d\lambda} = nN$, depending on the order $n$ and total rulings $N$.
  • Magnification and resolving power are distinct concepts; magnification enlarges the image without adding fine details, while resolving power reveals structural details limited by diffraction.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. According to Rayleigh's criterion, two point sources are just resolved when the central maximum of one diffraction pattern falls on the:

Explanation: Rayleigh's criterion states that two equal point sources are just resolved when the central maximum of the diffraction pattern of one falls on the first minimum of the diffraction pattern of the other.

2. The resolving power of an astronomical telescope with objective diameter $D$ for wavelength $\lambda$ is given by:

Explanation: The resolving power of a telescope is defined as the reciprocal of the smallest angular separation, given by $RP = \frac{D}{1.22\lambda}$.

3. What is the numerical aperture ($NA$) of a microscope objective in terms of refractive index $\mu$ and semi-vertical angle $\$\theta$$?

Explanation: The numerical aperture is defined as $NA = \mu \sin\$\theta$$, where $\mu$ is the refractive index of the medium between the object and the objective.

4. The resolving power of a compound microscope is inversely proportional to:

Explanation: Abbe's criterion for the resolving power of a microscope is $RP = \frac{2\mu \sin\$\theta$}{\lambda}$, which shows inverse proportionality to the wavelength $\lambda$.

5. If the diameter of the objective lens of a telescope is doubled, its resolving power will:

Explanation: Since resolving power is directly proportional to the aperture diameter $D$ ($RP = \frac{D}{1.22\lambda}$), doubling $D$ doubles the resolving power.

6. The resolving power of a diffraction grating of total rulings $N$ in the $n$-th order spectrum is expressed as:

Explanation: The resolving power of a diffraction grating is given by $\frac{\lambda}{d\lambda} = nN$.

7. Which of the following changes will increase the resolving power of a compound microscope?

Explanation: Resolving power is given by $\frac{2\mu \sin\$\theta$}{\lambda}$. Increasing the refractive index $\mu$ (e.g., using immersion oil) increases the resolving power.

8. The minimum resolvable linear distance $\Delta x$ in a microscope is given by:

Explanation: The minimum resolvable distance is $\Delta x = \frac{0.61\lambda}{\mu \sin\$\theta$}$ based on circular aperture diffraction limits.

9. Magnification and resolving power of optical instruments are:

Explanation: Magnification only enlarges the image size, whereas resolving power determines the ability to reveal fine structural details limited by diffraction.

10. What happens to the combined intensity profile of two point objects if their angular separation is less than Rayleigh's limit?

Explanation: If the separation is less than Rayleigh's limit, the combined intensity profile appears as a single broad maximum, rendering the objects unresolved.

11. The angular half-width of the Airy disc for a circular aperture of diameter $d$ is given by:

Explanation: The angular radius to the first dark ring of the Airy disc (angular half-width) is $\$\theta_{1}$ = \frac{1.22\lambda}{d}$.

12. To resolve spectral lines of very close wavelengths using a diffraction grating, one should:

Explanation: The resolving power of a grating is $nN$, so increasing the total number of rulings $N$ or working in a higher order $n$ increases spectral resolution.

13. Why are ultraviolet or electron microscopes used instead of visible light microscopes when high resolution is required?

Explanation: Resolving power is inversely proportional to wavelength $\lambda$. Using shorter wavelengths (UV or electron beams) significantly increases resolving power.

14. The units of the resolving power of an astronomical telescope can be expressed as:

Explanation: Since resolving power is the reciprocal of angular separation ($d\$\theta$$), its unit is $\text{rad}^{-1}$ or dimensionless in angular terms.

15. The resolving power of a prism is expressed by the formula:

Explanation: The resolving power of a prism is given by $RP = t \frac{d\mu}{d\lambda}$, where $t$ is the base length of the prism.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. Two point sources of light separated by a small distance are viewed through a telescope. The optical limit of resolution depends primarily on:

Explanation: The resolution limit of a telescope depends on the diameter $D$ of the objective and the wavelength $\lambda$ of light, as given by $\$\theta$ = \frac{1.22\lambda}{D}$.

2. In a compound microscope, if the wavelength of light used is halved, the resolving power of the microscope will:

Explanation: Since resolving power is inversely proportional to wavelength ($RP = \frac{2\mu \sin\$\theta$}{\lambda}$), halving the wavelength doubles the resolving power.

3. The resolving power of a diffraction grating depends on:

Explanation: The resolving power of a grating is given by $nN$, which depends directly on both the order of the spectrum $n$ and the total number of rulings $N$.

4. Rayleigh's criterion for the resolution of two point objects requires that the central maximum of the diffraction pattern of one object coincides with:

Explanation: Rayleigh's criterion states that the central maximum of one must fall on the first minimum of the other for two point sources to be just resolved.

5. Immersion oil is used in high-power microscopes to:

Explanation: Immersion oil has a high refractive index $\mu$, which increases the numerical aperture ($NA = \mu \sin\$\theta$$) and consequently improves the resolving power.

6. If the aperture of a telescope is 1 m and the wavelength of light is $500\text{ nm}$, the angular limit of resolution is approximately:

Explanation: Using $\$\theta$ = \frac{1.22\lambda}{D} = \frac{1.22 \times 500 \times 10^{-9}}{1} = 6.1 \times 10^{-7}\text{ radians}$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What is the primary condition for two spectral lines of wavelengths $\lambda$ and $\lambda + d\lambda$ to be resolved by a diffraction grating?

Explanation: Rayleigh's criterion applies to spectral lines as well: two spectral lines are just resolved when the principal maximum of one falls on the first minimum of the other.

2. Which optical instrument's resolving power depends on the base length $t$ and refractive index gradient $\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda}$?

Explanation: The resolving power of a prism is given by $RP = t \frac{d\mu}{d\lambda}$.

3. In a telescope, if the wavelength of light is increased from $400\text{ nm}$ to $800\text{ nm}$, the resolving power will:

Explanation: Resolving power of a telescope is inversely proportional to wavelength $\lambda$. Doubling the wavelength halves the resolving power.

4. The numerical aperture of a microscope objective lens with a semi-vertical angle of $30^\circ$ and medium refractive index of $1.5$ is:

Explanation: $NA = \mu \sin\$\theta$ = 1.5 \times \sin(30^\circ) = 1.5 \times 0.5 = 0.75$.

5. Why does a larger aperture telescope perform better in astronomical observations compared to a smaller one?

Explanation: A larger aperture $D$ increases resolving power ($RP \propto D$) and collects more light, making faint stars visible.

6. The minimum distance between two point objects that can be resolved by a microscope depends on all of the following EXCEPT:

Explanation: The minimum resolvable distance is $\Delta x = \frac{0.61\lambda}{\mu \sin\$\theta$}$, which depends on objective parameters, wavelength, and refractive index, but not on the eyepiece focal length.

7. If a diffraction grating has $5000\text{ lines/cm}$ and is used in the 2nd order, its resolving power over a wavelength of $500\text{ nm}$ depends on the total lines $N$ illuminated. If $N = 10000$, the resolving power is:

Explanation: Resolving power = $nN = 2 \times 10000 = 20000$.

8. Which misconception is correctly addressed by wave optics regarding optical instruments?

Explanation: Wave optics proves that diffraction sets a fundamental limit on the resolution of optical instruments, regardless of magnification.

9. In Fraunhofer diffraction through a circular aperture, the angular radius of the first dark ring is given by $\$\theta$ = \frac{1.22\lambda}{D}$. This formula is valid under:

Explanation: The formula assumes the small angle approximation where $\sin\$\theta$ \approx \$\theta$$ for circular aperture diffraction.

10. Comparing a telescope and a microscope, which statement is true regarding their resolving power formulas?

Explanation: Telescope resolving power depends on objective diameter $D$ and wavelength, whereas microscope resolving power depends on numerical aperture ($NA = \mu \sin\$\theta$$) and wavelength.
