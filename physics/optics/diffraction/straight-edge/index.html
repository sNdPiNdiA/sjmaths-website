Fresnel Diffraction by a Straight Edge

Diffraction by a straight edge is a classical example of Fresnel diffraction where a semi-infinite opaque screen obstructs half of a cylindrical or spherical wavefront. The resulting intensity distribution on a parallel viewing screen exhibits alternating fringes of maximum and minimum intensity that extend well into the geometrical shadow region.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Qualitative understanding of the intensity distribution, definition of half-period zones, and formation of fringes above and below the geometrical shadow boundary.

PGT extension

Quantitative treatment using Cornu's spiral, calculation of intensity at any point inside and outside the geometrical shadow, and position of successive maxima and minima.

Prerequisites

  • Wave theory of light and Huygens-Fresnel principle
  • Fresnel half-period zones concept
  • Distinction between Fresnel and Fraunhofer diffraction

Geometry of Straight Edge Diffraction

  • A monochromatic light source illuminates a sharp, straight, opaque edge parallel to the source.
  • The wavefront incident on the edge is cylindrical (for a line source) or spherical (for a point source).
  • The viewing screen is divided into two regions by the geometrical shadow boundary: the illuminated region and the geometrical shadow region.

Wavefront Division and Half-Period Zones

  • The portion of the wavefront above the straight edge is completely unobstructed and sends secondary wavelets to the screen.
  • The portion of the wavefront below the edge is blocked by the opaque screen.
  • The resultant amplitude at any point on the screen is determined by integrating the contributions from the visible half-period zones of the wavefront.

Intensity Distribution on the Screen

  • In the illuminated region (above the geometrical shadow), the intensity oscillates between maxima and minima with a decreasing amplitude fluctuation as distance from the edge increases.
  • At the geometrical shadow boundary, the intensity drops to exactly one-fourth (1/4) of the unobstructed intensity.
  • In the geometrical shadow region (below the boundary), the intensity falls off monotonically and smoothly to zero without any oscillations.

Role of Cornu's Spiral (Clothoid)

  • Cornu's spiral is a graphical representation of the Fresnel integrals used to evaluate diffraction amplitudes.
  • The coordinates on Cornu's spiral are Fresnel integrals C(v) and S(v), parameterized by the variable v.
  • It provides a direct visual method for determining the phase and amplitude of light diffracted by a straight edge.

Derivations

Position of Maxima and Minima in Straight Edge Diffraction

Conditions: Point source, cylindrical/spherical wavefront, distance b from edge to screen, distance a from source to edge.

  • Express the position coordinate y on the screen relative to the geometrical shadow boundary.
  • Define the dimensionless Fresnel parameter v as v = y sqrt{ rac{2(a+b)}{lambda ab}}.
  • Locate the extreme values of intensity by differentiating the Fresnel integral amplitudes, leading to condition equations involving tangents of phase parameters.
  • Set the derivative of the intensity with respect to v to zero to obtain the transcendental equation for maxima and minima: tan( rac{pi v^2}{2}) = rac{pi v^2}{2}.

Result: The nth maximum or minimum occurs when v satisfies the condition for the turning points on Cornu's spiral, with positions y_n = v_n sqrt{ rac{lambda ab}{2(a+b)}}.

Intensity at the Geometrical Shadow Boundary

Conditions: Light passing a semi-infinite screen evaluated right at the edge shadow line (v = 0).

  • Write the general expression for the complex amplitude in terms of Fresnel integrals C(v) and S(v) as U = rac{A_0}{sqrt{2}} [(0.5 + C(v)) + i(0.5 + S(v))].
  • At the geometrical shadow boundary, the upper edge of the visible wavefront corresponds to v = 0.
  • Substitute v = 0 into the Fresnel integrals, knowing that C(0) = 0 and S(0) = 0.
  • Calculate the intensity I as proportional to the squared modulus of the complex amplitude: I propto |U|^2 = rac{A_0^2}{2} [(0.5)^2 + (0.5)^2] = rac{A_0^2}{4}.

Result: I = rac{I_0}{4}, where I_0 is the uniform intensity that would be observed in the absence of the obstacle.

Solved numericals

TGT core

A monochromatic light of wavelength 589 nm is incident on a straight edge. The distance from the source to the edge is 1 m, and the distance from the edge to the screen is 1 m. Find the value of the Fresnel parameter v for a point on the screen located at a distance of 1 mm from the geometrical shadow boundary.

  • Identify given parameters: lambda = 589 \times 10^{-9} m, a = 1 m, b = 1 m, y = 1 \times 10^{-3} m.
  • Write the formula for the Fresnel parameter: v = y \sqrt{\frac{2(a+b)}{\lambda ab}}.
  • Substitute the values: v = (1 \times 10^{-3}) \sqrt{\frac{2(1 + 1)}{(589 \times 10^{-9}) \times (1) \times (1)}}.
  • Simplify the expression under the square root: \frac{4}{589 \times 10^{-9}} = 6.791 \times 10^6.
  • Calculate the square root: \sqrt{6.791 \times 10^6} = 2606.
  • Multiply by y: v = 1 \times 10^{-3} \times 2606 = 2.606.

Answer: 2.606

PGT extension

In a straight edge diffraction experiment, light of wavelength 5000 A is used with a point source at a distance of 50 cm from the edge and a screen at a distance of 50 cm from the edge. Calculate the intensity of light at the geometrical shadow edge relative to the unobstructed intensity.

  • Recall the theoretical derivation for the intensity at the geometrical shadow edge (where the boundary parameter v = 0).
  • At v = 0, the Fresnel integrals are C(0) = 0 and S(0) = 0.
  • The complex amplitude is U = \frac{A_0}{\sqrt{2}} [(0.5 + 0) + i(0.5 + 0)] = \frac{A_0(1 + i)}{2\sqrt{2}}.
  • The intensity I is proportional to |U|^2, so I = |U|^2 = \frac{A_0^2}{8} \times 2 = \frac{A_0^2}{4}.
  • Since the unobstructed intensity I_0 corresponds to amplitude A_0^2, the boundary intensity is I_0 / 4.

Answer: 0.25 I_0 (or one-fourth of the unobstructed intensity)

PGT extension

Using the approximate formula for the first maximum in straight edge diffraction, find the distance y_1 of the first bright fringe from the edge shadow line, given a = 1 m, b = 1 m, and lambda = 6000 A.

  • Convert wavelength to SI units: lambda = 6000 \times 10^{-10} m = 6 \times 10^{-7} m.
  • Identify distance parameters: a = 1 m, b = 1 m.
  • Use the position formula for the first maximum: y_1 \approx \sqrt{\frac{\lambda ab}{a+b} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{16} \right)}.
  • Substitute numerical values: \frac{\lambda ab}{a+b} = \frac{6 \times 10^{-7} \times 1 \times 1}{1 + 1} = 3 \times 10^{-7} m.
  • Evaluate the bracket term: 1 - \frac{1}{16} = \frac{15}{16} = 0.9375.
  • Multiply and take the square root: y_1 = \sqrt{3 \times 10^{-7} \times 0.9375} = \sqrt{2.8125 \times 10^{-7}} = \sqrt{28.125 \times 10^{-8}} \approx 5.30 imes 10^{-4} m.

Answer: 0.53 mm (or 5.30 \times 10^{-4} m)

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes the diffracting edge is infinitely sharp and opaque, which is an idealization; real edges scatter light at microscopic asperities.
  • Scalar diffraction theory is used, neglecting polarization effects which become significant when the edge is extremely sharp or illuminated at grazing angles.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Light cannot enter the geometrical shadow region behind an opaque obstacle.

Correct: Light penetrates into the geometrical shadow region with an exponentially decaying intensity.

Wave nature and Huygens' secondary wavelets ensure bending around obstacles.

Common error: The intensity at the edge of the geometrical shadow is zero.

Correct: The intensity at the shadow boundary is exactly one-fourth of the unobstructed beam intensity.

Half of the wavefront is unobstructed, contributing a resultant amplitude vector that squares to 1/4 of the total intensity.

Common error: Fringes in straight edge diffraction are equally spaced like double-slit interference fringes.

Correct: Fringes are unequally spaced, getting closer together as distance from the edge into the illuminated region increases.

The phase parameter relation involves nonlinear Fresnel integrals rather than linear path differences.

Quick revision

  • Straight edge diffraction produces fringes in the illuminated region and smooth decay in the shadow region with boundary intensity equal to I_0 / 4.
  • Fresnel Parameter (v): v = y \sqrt{\frac{2(a+b)}{\lambda ab}}
  • Intensity at Shadow Boundary: I_{boundary} = \frac{I_0}{4}

Must remember

  • In Fresnel diffraction by a straight edge, the intensity at the geometrical shadow boundary is exactly $1/4$ of the unobstructed beam intensity.
  • The intensity in the geometrical shadow region falls off monotonically and smoothly to zero without any oscillations.
  • In the illuminated region, fringes are unequally spaced, getting closer together as the distance from the edge increases.
  • Cornu's spiral is a graphical clothoid representing Fresnel integrals $C(v)$ and $S(v)$ used to evaluate diffraction amplitudes.
  • The Fresnel parameter is defined as $v = y \sqrt{\frac{2(a+b)}{\lambda ab}}$ where $y$ is the distance from the shadow edge.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the intensity at the geometrical shadow boundary for a straight edge in Fresnel diffraction compared to the unobstructed intensity $$I_{0}$$?

Explanation: At the geometrical shadow boundary, exactly half of the wavefront is unobstructed, contributing a resultant amplitude vector that yields an intensity of $$I_{0}$ / 4$.

2. How does the intensity vary in the geometrical shadow region behind a straight edge?

Explanation: In the geometrical shadow region, the intensity falls off monotonically and smoothly to zero without any oscillations due to light bending into the shadow.

3. Which of the following describes the spacing of fringes in the illuminated region of a straight edge diffraction pattern?

Explanation: Fringes are unequally spaced and get closer together as the distance from the edge into the illuminated region increases because the Fresnel integrals depend nonlinearly on position.

4. What does Cornu's spiral graphically represent in the study of Fresnel diffraction?

Explanation: Cornu's spiral is a graphical representation of the Fresnel integrals $C(v) = \int_{0}^{v} \cos(\frac{\$\pi$ t^2}{2}) dt$ and $S(v) = \int_{0}^{v} \sin(\frac{\$\pi$ t^2}{2}) dt$.

5. What is the correct expression for the Fresnel parameter $v$ used in straight edge diffraction?

Explanation: The Fresnel parameter is defined as $v = y \sqrt{\frac{2(a+b)}{\lambda ab}}$, where $y$ is the distance from the shadow edge, $a$ and $b$ are source-to-edge and edge-to-screen distances, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength.

6. Why does light penetrate into the geometrical shadow region in straight edge diffraction?

Explanation: The wave nature of light allows secondary wavelets from the unobstructed parts of the wavefront to propagate and bend into the geometrical shadow region.

7. What type of wavefront is typically incident on a straight edge when using a point source?

Explanation: A point source produces a spherical wavefront, whereas a line source produces a cylindrical wavefront incident on the straight edge.

8. What does the chord length on Cornu's spiral physically represent?

Explanation: The distance between two points on Cornu's spiral represents the resultant amplitude vector $R = \sqrt{C(v)^2 + S(v)^2}$ for diffracted light.

9. In the illuminated region above the straight edge shadow, the intensity oscillations are characterized by:

Explanation: In the illuminated region, intensity oscillates between maxima and minima with a decreasing amplitude fluctuation as the distance from the edge increases, eventually merging into uniform illumination.

10. What is the condition for validity of the Fresnel parameter $v$ formula in straight edge diffraction?

Explanation: The standard Fresnel parameter and fringe formulas rely on the Fresnel approximation applicable to small-angle diffraction.

11. What is the approximate position of the first bright maximum ($y_1$) outside the shadow for a straight edge?

Explanation: The distance to the first bright maximum outside the shadow is given by the approximation $y_1 \approx 1.22 \sqrt{\frac{\lambda ab}{2(a+b)}}$.

12. If the wavelength $\lambda$ of the incident light is doubled in a straight edge diffraction setup, how does the fringe pattern scale?

Explanation: Since fringe positions $y_n$ are proportional to $\sqrt{\lambda}$, doubling $\lambda$ causes the fringe pattern to expand by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$.

13. Which mathematical integrals form the basis of Cornu's spiral construction?

Explanation: Cornu's spiral plots $C(v) = \int_{0}^{v} \cos(\frac{\$\pi$ t^2}{2}) dt$ against $S(v) = \int_{0}^{v} \sin(\frac{\$\pi$ t^2}{2}) dt$.

14. What happens to the intensity pattern when the edge-to-screen distance $b$ is significantly increased?

Explanation: Since fringe positions depend on $\sqrt{b}$, increasing $b$ increases the spatial spread of the diffraction pattern on the screen.

15. Which of the following statements is a common misconception about straight edge diffraction?

Explanation: A common misconception is that light cannot enter the geometrical shadow region, whereas wave optics proves that light penetrates into it with exponentially decaying intensity.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. In Fresnel diffraction by a straight edge, the intensity at the geometrical shadow boundary is equal to:

Explanation: At the shadow boundary, exactly half of the wavefront contributes, yielding an amplitude of half the unobstructed value and an intensity of $$I_{0}$ / 4$.

2. The Cornu spiral is plotted using which coordinates?

Explanation: Cornu's spiral is a parametric curve defined by the Fresnel integrals $C(v) = \int_{0}^{v} \cos(\frac{\$\pi$ t^2}{2}) dt$ and $S(v) = \int_{0}^{v} \sin(\frac{\$\pi$ t^2}{2}) dt$.

3. What is the nature of fringes observed in the illuminated region of a straight edge diffraction pattern?

Explanation: In straight edge diffraction, the fringes in the illuminated region are unequally spaced, getting progressively closer together as the distance from the geometrical shadow edge increases.

4. The Fresnel parameter $v$ for a straight edge is proportional to which of the following?

Explanation: The Fresnel parameter is $v = y \sqrt{\frac{2(a+b)}{\lambda ab}}$, which shows direct proportionality to distance $y$ from the shadow edge.

5. In the geometrical shadow region behind a straight edge, the intensity distribution:

Explanation: Unlike the illuminated region, the geometrical shadow region features an intensity that decreases monotonically and smoothly to zero without any subsidiary maxima or minima.

6. For a straight edge diffraction setup, if the source-to-edge distance $a$ and edge-to-screen distance $b$ are equal, how does the fringe width behavior change?

Explanation: Substituting $a=b$ into the position formula simplifies the scaling factor while maintaining the nonlinear fringe spacing characteristic of Fresnel diffraction.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What is the physical significance of the asymptotic points ($v = \pm \infty$) on Cornu's spiral?

Explanation: The asymptotic points at $v = \pm \infty$ on Cornu's spiral correspond to the total contributions from the entire unobstructed wavefront.

2. If the distance $b$ from the edge to the screen is doubled, what happens to the linear position $y_n$ of the $n$-th maximum?

Explanation: Since fringe position $y_n \propto \sqrt{b}$, doubling $b$ increases $y_n$ by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$.

3. Which region of the straight edge diffraction pattern exhibits intensity oscillations with decreasing amplitude fluctuation?

Explanation: The illuminated region (above the shadow boundary) exhibits intensity oscillations that damp out as distance from the edge increases.

4. What is the value of $C(v)$ and $S(v)$ at $v = 0$ on Cornu's spiral?

Explanation: Since the lower limit of both Fresnel integrals is $0$, at $v = 0$ both $C(0) = 0$ and $S(0) = 0$, placing the origin at $(0,0)$.

5. In straight edge diffraction, how does an increase in wavelength $\lambda$ affect the intensity at the geometrical shadow boundary?

Explanation: The intensity at the shadow boundary is always $$I_{0}$ / 4$ regardless of wavelength, as half of the wavefront is unobstructed.

6. What is the approximate formula for the position of the $n$-th dark minimum ($y_n'$) in the illuminated region?

Explanation: The approximate position of the $n$-th minimum in the illuminated region is given by $y_n' = \sqrt{\frac{\lambda ab}{a+b} \left( n + \frac{3}{16} \right)}$.

7. Why are diffraction effects more pronounced for a straight edge than in ordinary rectilinear propagation experiments?

Explanation: Diffraction is prominent when obstacles have sharp boundaries interacting with secondary wavelets of the wavefront.

8. What does the square of the chord length between any two points on Cornu's spiral measure?

Explanation: The square of the chord length $R^2 = C(v)^2 + S(v)^2$ is proportional to the light intensity at the corresponding point on the screen.

9. Which statement correctly distinguishes Fresnel diffraction from Fraunhofer diffraction in the context of a straight edge?

Explanation: Fresnel diffraction involves finite distances where wavefronts are spherical or cylindrical, requiring zone analysis.

10. If an opaque straight edge is moved closer to the viewing screen (decreasing $b$), what happens to the fringe width in the illuminated region?

Explanation: Since fringe positions depend on $\sqrt{b}$, decreasing $b$ compresses the diffraction pattern, reducing the fringe width.
