Optics

Zone Plate

A zone plate is an optical device based on the principle of diffraction and constructive interference, designed to focus light without refraction. It consists of a series of concentric annular regions, known as Fresnel zones, which alternately block and transmit light.

Construction and Principle of Zone Plate

  • A zone plate is constructed by drawing concentric circles on a glass plate whose radii are proportional to the square roots of natural numbers.
  • The areas of all Fresnel zones are approximately equal, regardless of their order number n.
  • Alternate zones are rendered opaque (blackened) so that light waves reaching the observation point from consecutive transparent zones differ in phase by pi radians.

Difference Between Zone Plate and Convex Lens

  • A convex lens focuses light using refraction based on Snell's law, whereas a zone plate focuses light using diffraction and interference.
  • A convex lens has a single principal focus, while a zone plate has multiple foci corresponding to different orders of diffraction (m = 1, 3, 5...).
  • Chromatic aberration in a zone plate is extremely large because focal length is inversely proportional to the wavelength of light.

Phase Reversal Zone Plate

  • Instead of blocking alternate zones, a phase reversal zone plate retards the light passing through alternate zones by a phase of pi (or half a wavelength).
  • This modification ensures that all transmitted waves reach the focal point in phase, doubling the amplitude and quadrupling the intensity compared to a standard zone plate.
  • It acts as a phase hologram for a point source.

Multiple Foci and Focal Length

  • The primary focal length of a zone plate is given by $f = r_{n}^{2} / (n * \lambda)$.
  • Secondary foci occur at shorter focal lengths given by fm = f / m, where m is an odd integer (1, 3, 5...).
  • The intensity at the primary focus is much higher than at the secondary foci.

Derivations

Radius of the n-th Fresnel Zone

Conditions: Plane wavefront incident normally on an aperture at a distance b from the screen.

  • Let S be a source at a large distance, producing a spherical wavefront. Consider a point P at a distance b from the pole O of the wavefront.
  • The distance from the n-th boundary of the zone on the wavefront to the point P is given by SO + rn2 / (2*b) approximately.
  • The condition for the boundary of the n-th half-period zone is that the path difference between waves from the pole O and the n-th boundary to P is equal to n * lambda / 2.
  • Equating path difference: rn2 / (2*b) = n * lambda / 2.

Result: $r_n = \sqrt{n \lambda b}$

Focal Length of a Zone Plate

Conditions: Monochromatic light of wavelength lambda incident on a zone plate of radius rn.

  • The general expression for the radius of the n-th zone is rn2 = n * lambda * f, where f is the focal length corresponding to distance b.
  • Rearranging the formula to solve for the focal length f.
  • Substituting the values of radius and wavelength for a specific zone n.

Result: $f = \frac{r_n^2}{n \lambda}$

Solved numericals

TGT core

The radius of the first half-period zone of a zone plate is 0.5 mm for light of wavelength 5000 Å. Calculate the focal length of the zone plate.

  • Identify given values: $r_1 = 0.5 mm = 5 x 10^-4 m$, lambd$a = 5000 Å = 5 x 10^-7 m$, n = 1.
  • Use the focal length formula: $f = r_1^2 / (1 * \lambda)$.
  • Substitute values: $f = (5 x 10^-4)^2 / (5 x 10^-7) = 25 x 10^-8 / (5 x 10^-7) = 0.5 m$.

Answer: 0.5 m

PGT extension

A zone plate has 10 zones. The radius of the 10th zone is 2 mm. Find the principal focal length for light of wavelength 6000 Å, and determine the position of the first secondary focus.

  • Identify given values: $r_10 = 2 mm = 2 x 10^-3 m$, n = 10, lambd$a = 6000 Å = 6 x 10^-7 m$.
  • Calculate principal focal length: $f = r_10^2 / (n * \lambda) = (2 x 10^-3)^2 / (10 * 6 x 10^-7)$.
  • $f = 4 x 10^-6 / (6 x 10^-6) = 2/3 m = 0.667 m$.
  • Calculate first secondary focus: $f_3 = f / 3 = (2/3) / 3 = 2/9 m = 0.222 m$.

Answer: Principal focal length = 0.667 m, First secondary focus = 0.222 m

PGT extension

Find the radius of the 4th zone of a zone plate designed to have a focal length of 1 meter for a wavelength of 5890 Å.

  • Identify given values: f = 1 m, n = 4, lambd$a = 5890 Å = 5.89 x 10^-7 m$.
  • Use the radius formula derived from focal length: $r_n = \sqrt{n * \lambda * f}$.
  • Substitute values: $r_4 = \sqrt{4 * 5.89 x 10^-7 * 1} = \sqrt{2.356 x 10^-6}$.
  • $r_4 = 1.535 x 10^-3 m = 1.535 mm$.

Answer: 1.535 mm

Assumptions and limitations

  • It is assumed that the distance from the source and screen to the zone plate is much larger than the radius of the zones (b >> r_n).
  • Zone plates have extremely high chromatic aberration, making them impractical for use with white light in imaging applications.

Common misconceptions

Common error: A zone plate forms images by refraction similar to a glass lens.

Correct: A zone plate forms images entirely through diffraction and constructive interference.

It contains no refracting surfaces; alternate zones simply block light to phase-align the rest.

Common error: A zone plate has only one focal point like a standard lens.

Correct: A zone plate has multiple focal points corresponding to different odd integers (m = 1, 3, 5...).

Higher-order diffraction maxima lead to secondary focal planes closer to the plate.

Quick revision

  • Zone plate focal length is given by $f = r_n^2 / (n * \lambda)$ and is inversely proportional to wavelength.
  • Radius of n-th Zone: $r_n = \sqrt{n \lambda b}$
  • Area of n-th Zone: $A_n = \pi r_n^2 - \pi r_{n-1}^2 \approx \pi b \lambda$

Must remember

  • The radii of the zones in a zone plate are proportional to the square roots of natural numbers ($r_n \propto \sqrt{n}$).
  • The areas of all Fresnel zones are approximately equal to $\pi b \lambda$.
  • A zone plate focuses light using diffraction and interference, whereas a convex lens uses refraction.
  • A zone plate has multiple principal focal lengths given by $f_m = \frac{f}{m}$ where $m = 1, 3, 5, \dots$.
  • Chromatic aberration in a zone plate is extremely high because focal length is inversely proportional to the wavelength ($f \propto \frac{1}{\lambda}$).
  • A phase reversal zone plate retards alternate zones by $\pi$ radians instead of blocking them, quadrupling the focal intensity.

Glossary

Zone PlateAn optical device based on the principle of diffraction and interference used to focus light by blocking or phase-shifting alternate zones.
Primary Focal LengthThe longest focal length of a zone plate, given by $f = \frac{r_n^2}{n \lambda}$.
Phase Reversal Zone PlateA modified zone plate where alternate zones introduce a phase shift of $\pi$ instead of being opaque, enhancing focal intensity.
Fresnel ZonesConcentric annular regions on a zone plate whose radii are proportional to the square roots of natural numbers.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. The radii of the concentric circles forming a zone plate are proportional to:

Explanation: A zone plate is constructed by drawing concentric circles whose radii are proportional to the square roots of natural numbers, i.e., $r_n \propto \sqrt{n}$.

2. What is the primary physical phenomenon responsible for light focusing in a zone plate?

Explanation: Unlike a convex lens that relies on refraction, a zone plate focuses light entirely through diffraction and constructive interference.

3. The area of the $n$-th Fresnel zone in a zone plate is approximately equal to:

Explanation: The area of any individual Fresnel zone is approximately constant and given by $A_n \approx \pi b \lambda$, independent of the zone number $n$ for early zones.

4. How does the focal length $f$ of a zone plate vary with the wavelength $\lambda$ of incident light?

Explanation: Since $f = \frac{r_n^2}{n \lambda}$, the focal length of a zone plate is inversely proportional to the wavelength of light, $f \propto \frac{1}{\lambda}$.

5. A zone plate behaves similarly to which optical component in terms of multiple focal points?

Explanation: Due to multiple orders of diffraction ($m = 1, 3, 5, \dots$), a zone plate possesses multiple focal points, much like diffraction grating orders.

6. Secondary focal lengths $f_m$ of a zone plate are related to the primary focal length $f$ by:

Explanation: Secondary foci occur at shorter focal lengths given by $f_m = \frac{f}{m}$, where $m$ is an odd integer ($1, 3, 5, \dots$).

7. In a standard zone plate, alternate zones are blackened to:

Explanation: Blackening alternate zones ensures that light waves reaching the observation point from consecutive transparent zones arrive in phase by eliminating destructive $\pi$-phase contributions.

8. What modification is made in a phase reversal zone plate instead of blackening alternate zones?

Explanation: A phase reversal zone plate retards light passing through alternate zones by a phase of $\pi$ (or half a wavelength) rather than blocking it.

9. How does the intensity at the primary focus of a phase reversal zone plate compare to that of a standard zone plate?

Explanation: By shifting phase instead of blocking, all waves arrive in phase, doubling the amplitude and quadrupling the intensity ($I \propto 4I_0$) compared to a standard zone plate.

10. Why is chromatic aberration extremely large in a zone plate?

Explanation: Since $f \propto \frac{1}{\lambda}$, different wavelengths focus at vastly different distances, causing massive chromatic aberration.

11. If the radius of the first zone of a zone plate is $1\text{ mm}$ for light of wavelength $5000~\mathring{\text{A}}$, what is the primary focal length?

Explanation: Using $f = \frac{r_1^2}{1 \cdot \lambda} = \frac{(10^{-3})^2}{5 \times 10^{-7}} = \frac{10^{-6}}{5 \times 10^{-7}} = 2\text{ m}$.

12. Which of the following statements is true regarding the images formed by a zone plate?

Explanation: Because of its multiple foci and diffraction properties, a zone plate simultaneously acts as a converging lens (real image) and diverging lens (virtual image).

13. The area of the $n$-th Fresnel zone is independent of which of the following parameters for small $n$?

Explanation: The area $A_n \approx \pi b \lambda$ is approximately constant for all zones, meaning it is independent of the zone number $n$.

14. A zone plate can be considered as a type of:

Explanation: A phase reversal zone plate acts structurally and functionally as a phase hologram for a point source.

15. What is the condition on distance $b$ for the formula $r_n = \sqrt{n \lambda b}$ to hold accurately?

Explanation: The approximations used in deriving zone plate radii require that the distance to the screen $b$ is much greater than the radius of the $n$-th zone ($b \gg r_n$).

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The focal length of a zone plate for a wavelength $\lambda = 6000~\mathring{\text{A}}$ is $2\text{ m}$. What will be its focal length for $\lambda = 4000~\mathring{\text{A}}$?

Explanation: Since $f \propto \frac{1}{\lambda}$, we have $f_1 \lambda_1 = f_2 \lambda_2$. Thus $2 \times 6000 = f_2 \times 4000 \implies f_2 = \frac{12000}{4000} = 3\text{ m}$.

2. A zone plate has a primary focal length of $1.5\text{ m}$ for a given wavelength. The position of the first secondary focus is at:

Explanation: Secondary focal lengths are given by $f_m = \frac{f}{m}$, where $m = 1, 3, 5, \dots$. For the first secondary focus, $m = 3$, so $f_3 = \frac{1.5}{3} = 0.5\text{ m}$ (note: $m=1$ gives the primary focus $f$).

3. Which of the following optical devices exhibits multiple foci and extremely high chromatic aberration?

Explanation: A zone plate is characterized by multiple foci ($f_m = f/m$) and extremely high chromatic aberration ($f \propto 1/\lambda$).

4. In a zone plate, if the radius of the $10$-th zone is $2\text{ mm}$, the radius of the $40$-th zone will be:

Explanation: Since $r_n \propto \sqrt{n}$, we have $\frac{r_{40}}{r_{10}} = \sqrt{\frac{40}{10}} = \sqrt{4} = 2$. Therefore, $r_{40} = 2 \times 2\text{ mm} = 4\text{ mm}$.

5. The intensity at the focal point of a phase reversal zone plate is how many times the intensity obtained with a single unblocked Fresnel zone?

Explanation: If $R_1$ is the amplitude from one zone, a phase reversal zone plate allows all zones to add constructively, making the amplitude $R_1 + R_3 + R_5 + \dots$. With $n$ zones contributing constructively instead of alternating cancellation, the intensity becomes $16$ times that of a single zone (or 4 times a standard zone plate depending on baseline definition).

6. A convex lens forms images via refraction, whereas a zone plate forms images via:

Explanation: A convex lens operates on refraction governed by Snell's law, while a zone plate operates entirely through diffraction and interference of light waves.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. The ratio of the area of the $5$-th zone to the area of the $10$-th zone in a zone plate is approximately:

Explanation: The area of each Fresnel zone is approximately constant ($A_n \approx \pi b \lambda$) for early zones, making the ratio approximately $1:1$.

2. What happens to the focal length of a zone plate if the wavelength of light is doubled?

Explanation: Since $f \propto \frac{1}{\lambda}$, doubling the wavelength cuts the focal length in half.

3. Which of the following diffraction phenomena is the zone plate based upon?

Explanation: A zone plate is an application of Fresnel diffraction where wavefronts are divided into half-period Fresnel zones.

4. If $f$ is the principal focal length of a zone plate, the secondary foci occur at distances:

Explanation: Secondary foci are given by $f_m = \frac{f}{m}$ where $m = 1, 3, 5, \dots$, yielding $f, \frac{f}{3}, \frac{f}{5}, \dots$.

5. In the construction of a zone plate, the radius of the $n$-th circle is given by $r_n = \sqrt{n \lambda b}$. Here $b$ represents:

Explanation: In the formula $r_n = \sqrt{n \lambda b}$, $b$ is the distance from the zone plate to the point source or the screen.

6. Compared to a glass convex lens, a zone plate has:

Explanation: A zone plate has extremely high chromatic aberration because its focal length is inversely proportional to wavelength.

7. Alternate zones of a zone plate are blackened to achieve:

Explanation: Blackening alternate zones blocks out light waves that would otherwise arrive out of phase and cause destructive interference, leaving only constructive contributions.

8. A phase reversal zone plate achieves phase shift in alternate zones by:

Explanation: A phase reversal zone plate uses a transparent dielectric film that retards alternate zones by half a wavelength ($\pi$ phase shift).

9. The intensity at the primary focus of a zone plate is proportional to:

Explanation: When alternate zones are blocked, the resultant amplitude at the focus is the sum of amplitudes from the remaining open zones: $R_1 + R_3 + R_5 + \dots$.

10. Why does a zone plate form multiple images?

Explanation: Multiple focal points arise from higher-order diffraction maxima where phase conditions are satisfied at distances $f/m$, producing multiple focal planes.
