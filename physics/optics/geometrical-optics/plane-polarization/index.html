Plane Polarization of Light

Plane polarization is the restriction of the vibration of the electric field vector of a light wave to a single plane containing the direction of propagation. It provides unequivocal proof of the transverse nature of light waves.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Qualitative understanding of transverse waves, polarization by reflection (Brewster's law), polarization by refraction, and polaroids.

PGT extension

Quantitative treatment of Malus's law, double refraction in uniaxial crystals (ordinary and extraordinary rays), Nicole prism, quarter-wave and half-wave plates, and optical activity.

Prerequisites

  • Transverse and longitudinal wave kinematics
  • Maxwell's electromagnetic wave equations (electric and magnetic field vectors)
  • Snell's law of refraction and total internal reflection

Nature of Unpolarized and Polarized Light

  • Unpolarized light consists of electromagnetic waves with rapidly and randomly changing planes of electric field vibration perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
  • Plane-polarized (or linearly polarized) light confines its electric field vector E to a fixed plane throughout its propagation.
  • The intensity of unpolarized light is equally distributed in all azimuths perpendicular to the wave normal.

Polarization by Reflection and Brewster's Law

  • When unpolarized light is incident at a specific angle called the polarizing angle (or Brewster's angle, i<sub>p</sub>) on a transparent medium, the reflected light is completely plane-polarized.
  • At Brewster's angle, the reflected ray and the refracted ray are mutually perpendicular to each other.
  • Brewster's law states that the tangent of the polarizing angle is numerically equal to the refractive index of the medium (μ = tan i<sub>p</sub>).

Double Refraction and Birefringence

  • Certain crystals like calcite and quartz split an unpolarized incident ray into two refracted rays: the Ordinary ray (O-ray) and the Extraordinary ray (E-ray).
  • The O-ray obeys ordinary laws of refraction and travels with the same speed in all directions within the crystal.
  • The E-ray does not obey Snell's law, travels with varying speeds depending on the direction, and its refractive index varies with the angle of incidence.

Optical Activity and Specific Rotation

  • Optically active substances (e.g., quartz, sugar solution, turpentine) rotate the plane of polarization of linearly polarized light passing through them.
  • The angle of rotation θ is directly proportional to the length of the path (l) and the concentration (c) of the solution.
  • Specific rotation is defined as the rotation produced by a solution of length 1 decimetre and unit concentration (1 g/cm<sup>3</sup>) at a specified temperature for a given wavelength.

Derivations

Derivation of Brewster's Law

Conditions: Light incident on a dielectric boundary at polarizing angle i&lt;sub&gt;p&lt;/sub&gt;

  • Let unpolarized light be incident at Brewster's angle i<sub>p</sub> such that the reflected ray is completely polarized with electric vibrations perpendicular to the plane of incidence.
  • At this angle, the angle between the reflected ray and the refracted ray (angle of refraction r) is exactly 90 degrees.
  • From the geometry of the interface, the angle of incidence i<sub>p</sub> + 90\deg + r = 180\deg, which gives r = 90\deg - i<sub>p</sub>.
  • Applying Snell's law at the boundary: \mu = \frac{\sin i_p}{\sin r}.
  • Substitute r = 90\deg - i<sub>p</sub> into Snell's law: \mu = \frac{\sin i_p}{\sin(90\deg - i_p)} = \frac{\sin i_p}{\cos i_p} = \tan i_p.

Result: \mu = \tan i_p

Derivation of Malus's Law

Conditions: Plane-polarized light of initial amplitude E&lt;sub&gt;0&lt;/sub&gt; incident on an analyzer with transmission axis at angle \theta

  • Let the electric field vector of the incoming plane-polarized light be E<sub>0</sub>.
  • When this light passes through an analyzer whose transmission axis makes an angle \theta with the vibration plane of the incident light, the electric vector is resolved into two orthogonal components.
  • The component parallel to the transmission axis is E<sub>0</sub> \cos \theta, and the component perpendicular is E<sub>0</sub> \sin \theta.
  • Only the parallel component is transmitted by the analyzer.
  • Since intensity is proportional to the square of the amplitude (I \propto E<sup>2</sup>), the transmitted intensity I is given by I = I_0 \cos^2 \theta, where I<sub>0</sub> is the initial intensity.

Result: I = I_0 \cos^2 \theta

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the polarizing angle for glass of refractive index 1.5.

  • Recall Brewster's law: \mu = \tan i_p
  • Substitute the given value: \tan i_p = 1.5
  • Determine the angle: i_p = \arctan(1.5)

Answer: i_p \approx 56.3\deg

PGT extension

Unpolarized light of intensity 32 W/m&lt;sup&gt;2&lt;/sup&gt; is incident on a polarizer. The transmitted light falls on a second polarizer (analyzer) whose transmission axis is oriented at 60 degrees to the first polarizer. Find the final transmitted intensity.

  • When unpolarized light passes through the first polarizer, its intensity is reduced to half: I_1 = \frac{1}{2} I_0 = \frac{1}{2} \times 32 = 16 \text{ W/m}^2.
  • Apply Malus's law for the second polarizer: I_2 = I_1 \cos^2 \theta
  • Substitute \theta = 60\deg: I_2 = 16 \times \cos^2(60\deg) = 16 \times \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = 16 \times \frac{1}{4} = 4 \text{ W/m}^2.

Answer: 4 W/m<sup>2</sup>

PGT extension

A quarter-wave plate is designed for sodium light of wavelength 589 nm. If the principal refractive indices are n_e = 1.553 and n_o = 1.544, calculate the minimum thickness of the plate.

  • Use the formula for the thickness of a quarter-wave plate: t = \frac{\lambda}{4(n_e - n_o)}
  • Substitute the given values: \lambda = 589 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}, n_e = 1.553, n_o = 1.544
  • Calculate the birefringence: n_e - n_o = 1.553 - 1.544 = 0.009
  • Compute t: t = \frac{589 \times 10^{-9}}{4 \times 0.009} = \frac{589 \times 10^{-9}}{0.036} \approx 1.636 \times 10^{-5} \text{ m} = 16.36 \mu\text{m}

Answer: 1.64 \times 10^{-5} m (or 16.36 \mu m)

Assumptions and limitations

  • Ideal polarizers are assumed to have 100% transmission for parallel electric vector components and 0% for perpendicular components (infinite extinction ratio).
  • Linear optical media are assumed where refractive indices are independent of light intensity.
  • Birefringent plates assume uniform thickness and homogeneous crystal structure throughout the active aperture.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Longitudinal waves like sound waves can be polarized.

Correct: Only transverse waves can be polarized.

Polarization requires oscillations to occur perpendicular to the direction of propagation, which is impossible in longitudinal compression waves.

Common error: Unpolarized light has no electric field components.

Correct: Unpolarized light has rapid, randomly fluctuating electric field components in all directions perpendicular to the ray.

Zero time-average value across all azimuths does not mean the instantaneous field vector is zero.

Common error: The angle between the reflected and refracted rays at any incidence angle is 90 degrees.

Correct: The reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular only when incidence is at Brewster's polarizing angle.

Snell's law dictates that r varies with i, and orthogonality is a special geometric consequence unique to i = i_p.

Quick revision

  • Polarization proves the transverse nature of light waves.
  • Brewster's Law: \mu = \tan i_p with reflected and refracted rays mutually perpendicular.
  • Malus's Law: I = I_0 \cos^2 \theta.
  • Unpolarized light passing through an ideal polarizer drops to exactly half its initial intensity.
  • Calcite crystal exhibits double refraction producing O-ray and E-ray.
  • Quarter-wave plate introduces a phase difference of \pi/2; half-wave plate introduces \pi.

Must remember

  • Only transverse waves exhibit polarization; longitudinal waves cannot be polarized.
  • According to Brewster's law, the tangent of the polarizing angle is equal to the refractive index of the medium ($\mu = \tan i_p$).
  • At Brewster's angle, the reflected and refracted rays are mutually perpendicular.
  • Unpolarized light passing through an ideal linear polarizer has its intensity reduced by exactly half ($I = \frac{1}{2} I_0$).
  • Birefringent crystals like calcite split unpolarized light into an Ordinary ray (O-ray) and an Extraordinary ray (E-ray).
  • A quarter-wave plate introduces a phase difference of $\pi/2$ or a path difference of $\lambda/4$.
  • A half-wave plate introduces a phase difference of $\pi$ or a path difference of $\lambda/2$.
  • Optical rotation angle is directly proportional to the path length and concentration of the active solution ($\theta = S \cdot l \cdot c$).

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following types of waves cannot exhibit the phenomenon of polarization?

Explanation: Polarization is exclusively a property of transverse waves where oscillations occur perpendicular to the direction of propagation. Sound waves in air are longitudinal compression waves and cannot be polarized.

2. According to Brewster's law, when unpolarized light is incident at the polarizing angle $i_p$ on a transparent medium of refractive index $\mu$, the angle between the reflected and refracted rays is:

Explanation: At Brewster's polarizing angle, the reflected ray and the refracted ray are mutually perpendicular, making the angle between them exactly $90^\circ$.

3. If the refractive index of a glass medium is $\sqrt{3}$, what is its Brewster's polarizing angle?

Explanation: According to Brewster's law, $\mu = \tan i_p$. Given $\mu = \sqrt{3}$, we have $\tan i_p = \sqrt{3}$, which yields $i_p = 60^\circ$.

4. Unpolarized light of intensity $$I_{0}$$ is incident on a linear polaroid. What is the intensity of the transmitted light?

Explanation: Unpolarized light has equal intensity components in all directions. Averaging $\cos^2 \$\theta$$ over all azimuth angles yields $1/2$, so the transmitted intensity is $I = \frac{1}{2} $I_{0}$$.

5. Malus's law is represented by the mathematical relation:

Explanation: Malus's law states that when completely plane-polarized light is incident on an analyzer, the transmitted intensity $I$ varies directly as the square of the cosine of the angle $\$\theta$$ between their transmission axes: $I = $I_{0}$ \cos^2 \$\theta$$.

6. Which ray in a doubly refracting uniaxial crystal obeys the ordinary laws of refraction (Snell's law)?

Explanation: The Ordinary ray (O-ray) obeys Snell's law of refraction and travels with the exact same speed in all directions within a birefringent crystal.

7. The thickness of a quarter-wave plate for a wavelength $\lambda$ and principal refractive indices $n_e$ and $n_o$ is given by:

Explanation: A quarter-wave plate introduces a path difference of $\lambda/4$, which corresponds to a thickness $t = \frac{\lambda}{4(n_e - n_o)}$.CORRECT.

8. Optical activity in a solution depends on which of the following factors?

Explanation: The angle of optical rotation is directly proportional to both the path length ($l$) and the concentration ($c$) of the active solution: $\$\theta$ = S \cdot l \cdot c$.

9. If two crossed polarizers have their transmission axes perpendicular to each other ($\$\theta$ = 90^\circ$), the intensity of light transmitted through the second polarizer (analyzer) when illuminated by unpolarized light is:

Explanation: Unpolarized light becomes polarized at $$I_{0}$ / 2$ after the first polarizer. When incident on a crossed analyzer at $90^\circ$, Malus's law gives $I = ($I_{0}$ / 2) \cos^2(90^\circ) = 0$.

10. Which of the following substances is optically active?

Explanation: Sugar solution is a classic example of an optically active substance that rotates the plane of polarization of linearly polarized light.

11. What is the phase difference introduced by a half-wave plate between the ordinary and extraordinary rays?

Explanation: A half-wave plate introduces a path difference of $\lambda/2$, which corresponds to a phase difference of $\$\pi$$ radians.

12. The specific rotation $S$ of a solution is measured for a path length of:

Explanation: Specific rotation is defined as the rotation produced by a solution of length 1 decimetre ($1 \text{ dm}$) and unit concentration at a specified temperature.

13. Which phenomenon confirms the transverse nature of light waves?

Explanation: Polarization uniquely demonstrates that light vibrations occur transverse to the direction of propagation, as longitudinal waves cannot be polarized.

14. In a uniaxial birefringent crystal, the extraordinary ray (E-ray):

Explanation: Unlike the O-ray, the E-ray does not obey Snell's law and its effective refractive index varies depending on the direction of propagation inside the crystal.

15. If the angle between the transmission axes of a polarizer and an analyzer is $60^\circ$, what fraction of the incident polarized light intensity is transmitted?

Explanation: According to Malus's law, $I = $I_{0}$ \cos^2(60^\circ) = $I_{0}$ (1/2)^2 = $I_{0}$ / 4$. Thus, a fraction of $1/4$ is transmitted.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The polarizing angle for a medium is $60^\circ$. What is the refractive index of the medium?

Explanation: By Brewster's law, $\mu = \tan i_p = \tan 60^\circ = \sqrt{3}$.

2. Unpolarized light passes through two polaroids placed with their pass axes at an angle of $45^\circ$ to each other. If the initial intensity is $$I_{0}$$, the final transmitted intensity is:

Explanation: After the first polaroid, intensity becomes $$I_{1}$ = $I_{0}$ / 2$. Applying Malus's law at the second polaroid: $$I_{2}$ = $I_{1}$ \cos^2(45^\circ) = ($I_{0}$ / 2) \times (1/2) = $I_{0}$ / 4$.

3. The angle between the reflected and refracted rays when sunlight is reflected from a lake's surface as completely plane-polarized light is:

Explanation: When light is reflected as completely plane-polarized, reflection occurs at Brewster's angle where the reflected and refracted rays are at right angles ($90^\circ$) to each other.

4. A half-wave plate is designed for a wavelength of $600 \text{ nm}$. What is the path difference introduced by this plate for this wavelength?

Explanation: A half-wave plate introduces a path difference of $\lambda/2$. For $\lambda = 600 \text{ nm}$, path difference = $600 / 2 = 300 \text{ nm}$.

5. If the specific rotation of cane sugar is $66^\circ \text{ dm}^{-1} (\text{g/cm}^3)^{-1}$, the rotation produced by $20 \text{ cm}$ of sugar solution of concentration $0.05 \text{ g/cm}^3$ is:

Explanation: Path length $l = 20 \text{ cm} = 2 \text{ dm}$. Concentration $c = 0.05 \text{ g/cm}^3$. Rotation $\$\theta$ = S \cdot l \cdot c = 66 \times 2 \times 0.05 = 6.6^\circ$.

6. Which of the following statements regarding the ordinary and extraordinary rays in a calcite crystal is correct?

Explanation: In uniaxial birefringent crystals, the O-ray travels with the same speed in all directions, whereas the E-ray travels with varying speeds depending on the direction of propagation.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. The refractive index of a medium is $1.5$. The polarizing angle for this medium is given by:

Explanation: From Brewster's law, $\mu = \tan i_p$, which implies $i_p = \tan^{-1}(\mu) = \tan^{-1}(1.5)$.

2. What is the electric field vibration characteristic of plane-polarized light?

Explanation: Plane-polarized light restricts its electric field vector $E$ to a single fixed plane throughout propagation.

3. Unpolarized light of intensity $16 \text{ W/m}^2$ is incident on a polarizer. The transmitted intensity is:

Explanation: When unpolarized light passes through a single polarizer, its intensity is reduced by half: $I = \frac{1}{2} $I_{0}$ = \frac{1}{2} \times 16 = 8 \text{ W/m}^2$.

4. The phase difference corresponding to a path difference of $\lambda / 4$ is:

Explanation: Phase difference $\Delta \$\phi$ = \frac{2\$\pi$}{\lambda} \times \text{path difference} = \frac{2\$\pi$}{\lambda} \times \frac{\lambda}{4} = \frac{\$\pi$}{2}$ radians.

5. Which crystal is commonly used to produce plane-polarized light using double refraction and total internal reflection (Nicol prism)?

Explanation: A Nicol prism is constructed from a calcite crystal cut in a specific manner to isolate the extraordinary ray by total internal reflection of the ordinary ray.

6. If the angle between the pass axes of polaroid P1 and P2 is $30^\circ$, the ratio of transmitted intensity to incident polarized intensity is:

Explanation: By Malus's law, $I/$I_{0}$ = \cos^2(30^\circ) = (\sqrt{3}/2)^2 = 3/4$.

7. Which of the following waves can be polarized?

Explanation: Light waves are electromagnetic transverse waves and can be polarized. Sound and pressure waves are longitudinal.

8. The specific rotation of an optically active compound depends upon:

Explanation: Specific rotation is a characteristic property defined for a given wavelength of light and a specified temperature.

9. A quarter-wave plate is placed between crossed polarizers with its optic axis at $45^\circ$ to the polarizer transmission axis. The resulting light is:

Explanation: When linearly polarized light passes through a quarter-wave plate oriented at $45^\circ$ to its optic axis, the orthogonal components have equal amplitudes with a $\$\pi$/2$ phase difference, yielding circularly polarized light.

10. If the refractive indices for ordinary and extraordinary rays in a quartz crystal are $n_o$ and $n_e$ respectively, the condition for a positive uniaxial crystal is:

Explanation: In a positive uniaxial crystal like quartz, the extraordinary refractive index is greater than the ordinary refractive index ($n_e > n_o$).CORRECT.
