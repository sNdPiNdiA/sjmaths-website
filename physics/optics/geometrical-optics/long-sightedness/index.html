Long-Sightedness (Hypermetropia)

Long-sightedness, or hypermetropia, is a defect of vision where distant objects are seen clearly but near objects appear blurred because light rays focus behind the retina. It is corrected by using a converging (convex) lens of appropriate focal length.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of hypermetropia, identification of the defect, near point shift from normal (25 cm) to a greater distance (D'), and calculation of power and focal length of the correcting convex lens.

PGT extension

Detailed analysis of retinal image formation, crystalline lens accommodation limits, shift in the least distance of distinct vision, and calculations involving combined optical systems (eye plus corrective lens) situated at a given corneal distance.

Prerequisites

  • Geometrical optics and refraction through spherical surfaces
  • Lens maker's formula and thin lens formula
  • Anatomy of the human eye and accommodation

Anatomical and Physiological Causes of Hypermetropia

  • The eyeball is unusually short along its optical axis, causing the retina to lie closer to the cornea-lens system than the focal point of the relaxed eye.
  • The curvature of the cornea or the crystalline lens is insufficient, leading to a weaker converging power than required for normal vision.
  • The near point of the eye shifts away from the standard reference distance of 25 cm to a greater distance D', so that rays from an object at 25 cm form a virtual image beyond the retina.

Optical Correction Mechanism

  • A converging lens (convex lens) is placed in front of the eye to supplement the optical power of the defective eye.
  • The corrective lens forms a virtual image of an object placed at the normal near point (u = -25 cm) at the patient's actual shifted near point (v = -D').
  • The net focal length of the combined spectacle lens and eye system allows parallel or diverging rays from near objects to converge precisely on the retina.

Power Sign Convention and Dioptric Strength

  • The focal length (f) and power (P) of the corrective convex lens are always positive.
  • Power is measured in diopters (D) when the focal length is expressed in meters.
  • If a spectacle lens is worn at a specific distance (d) from the cornea, this gap must be accounted for in precise clinical and optical calculations.

Comparison with Presbyopia and Myopia

  • Unlike myopia (near-sightedness) where the image falls in front of the retina and is corrected by concave lenses, hypermetropia focuses behind the retina and requires convex lenses.
  • Presbyopia involves a similar need for convex lenses due to age-related loss of accommodation, but hypermetropia is fundamentally a structural refractive error independent of age.
  • Astigmatism involves non-spherical curvatures, whereas hypermetropia is spherically symmetrical.

Derivations

Derivation of Corrective Lens Formula

Conditions: An eye with hypermetropia has a near point at D' instead of the normal near point D (25 cm). The spectacle lens is placed in contact with the eye (negligible corneal distance).

  • State the thin lens formula: \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u}
  • Substitute the object distance u equal to the normal near point with sign convention: u = -D = -25 \text{ cm}.
  • Substitute the image distance v equal to the patient's actual near point with sign convention: v = -D'.
  • Evaluate the focal length f: \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{-D'} - \frac{1}{-D} = \frac{1}{D} - \frac{1}{D'}

Result: f = \frac{D \cdot D'}{D' - D}

Derivation with Corneal Distance Consideration

Conditions: The corrective spectacle lens is placed at a distance d from the cornea of the hypermetropic eye.

  • The object is placed at the normal near point distance D from the lens, so u = -D.
  • The lens must form a virtual image at a position such that the eye can view it clearly. The eye's actual near point is at distance D' from the cornea.
  • Therefore, the image distance from the corrective lens must be v = -(D' - d).
  • Apply the thin lens formula: \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{-(D' - d)} - \frac{1}{-D}

Result: P = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{D} - \frac{1}{D' - d}

Solved numericals

TGT core

A hypermetropic person has a near point of 50 cm. Calculate the focal length and power of the convex lens required to enable him to read a book held at 25 cm.

  • Identify given values: Normal near point u = -25 cm, Defective near point v = -50 cm.
  • Apply the lens formula: \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u}
  • Substitute values: \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{-50} - \frac{1}{-25} = -\frac{1}{50} + \frac{1}{25} = \frac{-1 + 2}{50} = \frac{1}{50} \text{ cm}^{-1}.
  • Calculate focal length: f = +50 \text{ cm} = +0.5 \text{ m}.
  • Calculate power: P = \frac{1}{f(\text{in meters})} = \frac{1}{0.5} = +2.0 \text{ D}.

Answer: Focal length = +50 cm (+0.5 m), Power = +2.0 D

PGT extension

The near point of a hypermetropic eye is 1.0 m. What is the power of the corrective lens required if the spectacles are worn 2.0 cm in front of the cornea? Assume standard near point to be 25 cm.

  • Identify standard near point D = 25 cm = 0.25 m, so u = -0.25 m.
  • Identify patient's actual near point from cornea D' = 1.0 m = 100 cm.
  • Spectacle-cornea distance d = 2 cm = 0.02 m.
  • Image distance from the lens v = -(D' - d) = -(100 - 2) \text{ cm} = -98 \text{ cm} = -0.98 \text{ m}.
  • Apply lens formula: \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{-0.98} - \frac{1}{-0.25} = -1.0204 + 4.0 = +2.9796 \text{ m}^{-1}.

Answer: Power = +2.98 D

TGT core

A person cannot see objects distinctly placed closer than 75 cm. What should be the power of the lens in spectacles if he wishes to read a newspaper at 25 cm?

  • Object distance u = -25 cm, Image distance v = -75 cm.
  • Use lens formula: \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{-75} - \frac{1}{-25}
  • Simplify fraction: \frac{1}{f} = -\frac{1}{75} + \frac{3}{75} = \frac{2}{75} \text{ cm}^{-1}.
  • Convert focal length to meters: f = \frac{75}{2} \text{ cm} = 37.5 \text{ cm} = 0.375 \text{ m}.
  • Calculate power P = \frac{1}{0.375} = \frac{8}{3} \text{ D} \approx +2.67 \text{ D}.

Answer: Power = +2.67 D (or +8/3 D)

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumption: The eye is treated as a centered optical system with a single equivalent refracting surface and thin lens approximation holds for the corrective eyewear.
  • Limitation: Physiological variations in accommodation, pupil size diffraction effects, and off-axis aberrations (astigmatism of oblique incidence) in spectacle lenses are neglected in elementary calculations.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Long-sighted people cannot see distant objects clearly.

Correct: Long-sighted people can see distant objects clearly; they have difficulty seeing near objects.

The term 'long-sighted' refers to the eye being able to focus on distant objects well, while the near point has receded.

Common error: Concave lenses are used to correct hypermetropia.

Correct: Convex lenses are used to correct hypermetropia.

Hypermetropia requires converging power to shift the focal point forward onto the retina, which is achieved using convex lenses.

Common error: The near point of a hypermetropic eye is closer than 25 cm.

Correct: The near point of a hypermetropic eye is further than 25 cm (e.g., 50 cm or 1 m).

A closer near point is characteristic of myopia, whereas hypermetropia causes the near point to recede.

Quick revision

  • Hypermetropia is corrected using a positive (convex) lens of appropriate focal length.
  • The near point in hypermetropia shifts farther away than the standard 25 cm.
  • Image formation in an uncorrected hypermetropic eye occurs behind the retina.

Must remember

  • Hypermetropia (long-sightedness) is caused by an unusually short eyeball along its optical axis or insufficient converging power of the cornea-lens system.
  • The near point of a hypermetropic eye recedes from the standard reference distance of $25 ext{ cm}$ to a greater distance $D'$.
  • A convex (converging) lens is used to correct hypermetropia by forming a virtual image of a near object at the patient's shifted near point.
  • The focal length ($f$) and optical power ($P$) of the corrective convex lens are always positive.
  • When a spectacle lens is worn at a distance $d$ from the cornea, the effective object and image distances must account for this gap: $u = -25 ext{ cm}$ and $v = -(D' - d)$.

Glossary

HypermetropiaAn eye defect where distant objects are seen clearly, but near objects are blurred because images focus behind the retina.
Near Point ($D'$)The closest point to the eye at which an object can be brought into sharp focus by accommodation; shifted outward in hypermetropia.
Optical Power ($P$)The degree of convergence or divergence of a lens, measured in diopters (D), equal to the reciprocal of focal length in meters ($P = 1/f$).
Convex LensA converging lens thicker at the center than at the edges, used to add positive dioptric power for hypermetropia correction.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which type of lens is used to correct hypermetropia?

Explanation: Hypermetropia requires additional converging power to shift the focal point forward onto the retina, which is achieved using a convex lens.

2. What is the primary anatomical cause of hypermetropia?

Explanation: An abnormally short eyeball along its optical axis causes the retina to lie closer to the cornea-lens system than the focal point, resulting in hypermetropia.

3. The near point of a hypermetropic person is at $50 ext{ cm}$. To read a book placed at the normal near point ($25 ext{ cm}$), what should be the focal length of the corrective lens (assuming $d = 0$)?

Explanation: Using the lens formula with $u = -25 ext{ cm}$ and $v = -50 ext{ cm}$: $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{-50} - \frac{1}{-25} = -\frac{1}{50} + \frac{1}{25} = \frac{1}{50}$, giving $f = +50 ext{ cm}$.

4. What is the sign of the power of a lens prescribed for hypermetropia?

Explanation: A corrective convex lens has a positive focal length, and therefore its optical power ($P = 1/f$) is always positive.

5. Where does the image form in an uncorrected hypermetropic eye for an object placed at the normal near point ($25 ext{ cm}$)?

Explanation: In hypermetropia, light rays from a near object converge to a point behind the retina, producing a blurred image.

6. How does the near point of a hypermetropic eye compare to that of a normal eye?

Explanation: The near point of a hypermetropic eye recedes to a greater distance $D'$ than the standard reference distance of $25 ext{ cm}$.

7. If a spectacle lens for hypermetropia is worn at a distance $d$ from the cornea, what is the effective object distance $u$ when reading at $25 ext{ cm}$?

Explanation: The standard reading distance is measured from the eye's natural near point, so taking $u = -25 ext{ cm}$ relative to the spectacle plane is standard for standard near-point reading tasks.

8. Which condition is fundamentally a structural refractive error independent of age, unlike presbyopia?

Explanation: Hypermetropia is typically a structural defect involving an eyeball of shorter optical length or insufficient corneal curvature, whereas presbyopia is age-related loss of accommodation.

9. What is the optical power of a convex lens with a focal length of $+0.5 ext{ m}$?

Explanation: Optical power $P = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{+0.5 ext{ m}} = +2.0 ext{ Diopters}$.

10. In a hypermetropic eye, distant objects are typically:

Explanation: Long-sighted (hypermetropic) people can see distant objects clearly because parallel rays from infinity require minimal or no accommodation to focus on the retina.

11. What happens to the shift in near point magnitude ($\Delta x$) if a patient's near point moves from $25 ext{ cm}$ to $75 ext{ cm}$?

Explanation: The shift in near point is $\Delta x = D' - D = 75 ext{ cm} - 25 ext{ cm} = 50 ext{ cm}$.

12. Which formula correctly represents the lens power $P$ in terms of focal length $f$?

Explanation: By definition, optical power in diopters is the reciprocal of focal length expressed in meters ($P = 1/f$).i

13. Why does a convex lens correct hypermetropia?

Explanation: A convex lens converges the light rays before they enter the eye, supplementing the insufficient optical power of the defective crystalline lens/cornea.

14. If a person's near point is $1 ext{ m}$ ($100 ext{ cm}$), what is the power of the lens required to read at $25 ext{ cm}$ (assuming $d=0$)?

Explanation: Using $u = -0.25 ext{ m}$ and $v = -1.0 ext{ m}$: $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{-1.0} - \frac{1}{-0.25} = -1 + 4 = +3 ext{ m}^{-1} = +3.0 ext{ D}$.

15. Which of the following statements is a common misconception about hypermetropia?

Explanation: A widespread misconception is that long-sighted people cannot see distant objects; in reality, their distance vision is usually fine, but near vision is impaired.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. A person cannot see objects distinctly closer than $50 ext{ cm}$. Calculate the power of the lens he should use to read at $25 ext{ cm}$:

Explanation: Given $u = -25 ext{ cm} = -0.25 ext{ m}$ and $v = -50 ext{ cm} = -0.5 ext{ m}$. Using $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{-0.5} - \frac{1}{-0.25} = -2 + 4 = +2 ext{ D}$.

2. The near point of a hypermetropic eye is $1.0 ext{ m}$. Find the focal length of the convex lens needed to enable the person to read clearly at $25 ext{ cm}$:

Explanation: Here $u = -0.25 ext{ m}$ and $v = -1.0 ext{ m}$. $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{-1.0} - \frac{1}{-0.25} = -1 + 4 = +3 ext{ m}^{-1}$. Thus $f = \frac{1}{3} ext{ m} = +0.33 ext{ m}$.

3. A hypermetropic person has a near point of $75 ext{ cm}$. The spectacle lens is placed $2 ext{ cm}$ in front of the cornea. What is the effective image distance $v$ for reading an object at $25 ext{ cm}$?

Explanation: The actual near point from the cornea is $75 ext{ cm}$. Since the lens is $2 ext{ cm}$ in front of the cornea, the virtual image must be formed at a distance of $75 - 2 = 73 ext{ cm}$ from the lens. Thus $v = -73 ext{ cm}$.

4. Which of the following optical adjustments occurs when a convex lens is prescribed for hypermetropia?

Explanation: The convex lens provides converging power so that light rays from a near point converge adequately to form an image on the retina rather than behind it.

5. If the optical power of a corrective lens for a hypermetropic eye is $+4.0 ext{ D}$, what is its focal length in centimeters?

Explanation: Focal length $f = \frac{1}{P} = \frac{1}{+4} ext{ m} = 0.25 ext{ m} = +25 ext{ cm}$.

6. In hypermetropia, the refractive power of the eye lens is:

Explanation: Hypermetropia arises either because the eyeball is unusually short or because the cornea/crystalline lens has insufficient curvature (weaker converging power than required).

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. A student calculates the power of a lens for a hypermetropic patient whose near point is $40 ext{ cm}$. Taking $u = -25 ext{ cm}$, what is the correct power?

Explanation: $u = -0.25 ext{ m}$ and $v = -0.40 ext{ m}$. $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{-0.4} - \frac{1}{-0.25} = -2.5 + 4.0 = +1.5 ext{ D}$.

2. Which equation correctly gives the corrective focal length when the lens touches the cornea ($d = 0$)?

Explanation: Solving $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{-D'} - \frac{1}{-D} = \frac{1}{D} - \frac{1}{D'} = \frac{D' - D}{D \cdot D'}$ gives $f = \frac{D \cdot D'}{D' - D}$.

3. What is the primary difference in optical correction between myopia and hypermetropia?

Explanation: Myopia requires divergence (concave lens), whereas hypermetropia requires convergence (convex lens).

4. A hypermetropic person cannot see objects closer than $40 ext{ cm}$. Find the power of the spectacle lens if worn at a distance of $1 ext{ cm}$ from the eye when reading at $25 ext{ cm}$ (take distances relative to lens).

Explanation: Object distance $u = -25 ext{ cm}$. 5. Why does accommodation fail to correct hypermetropia for near objects in severe cases?

Explanation: Even with maximum accommodation (ciliary muscles fully contracting), the eye lens cannot achieve enough converging power to focus near objects onto the retina.

6. If a lens has a power of $+2.5 ext{ D}$, what is its focal length?

Explanation: $f = \frac{1}{P} = \frac{1}{+2.5 ext{ D}} = 0.40 ext{ m}$ ($40 ext{ cm}$).

7. Which of the following describes the near point recession in hypermetropia?

Explanation: In hypermetropia, the near point recedes outward to a distance greater than the normal $25 ext{ cm}$ mark.

8. What is the net optical power of a combined system where an eye with power $P_{eye}$ is aided by a corrective lens of power $P_{lens}$ in close contact?

Explanation: For thin lenses in close contact, optical powers are additive: $P_{net} = P_{eye} + P_{lens}$.

9. A patient reads a chart at $25 ext{ cm}$ using a $+3.0 ext{ D}$ lens. What is the approximate defective near point $D'$ (assuming $d=0$)?

Explanation: Using $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} \implies 3 = \frac{1}{-D'} - \frac{1}{-0.25} = -\frac{1}{D'} + 4 \implies \frac{1}{D'} = 4 - 3 = 1 ext{ m}^{-1}$, so $D' = 1 ext{ m} = 100 ext{ cm}$.

10. Which optical instrument or lens property is shared by both hypermetropia correction and simple magnifying glasses?

Explanation: Both hypermetropia correction and simple magnifiers utilize convex lenses to produce virtual, upright images that appear larger or within the eye's comfortable viewing range.
