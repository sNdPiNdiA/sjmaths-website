Optics

Half-Wave Plate

A half-wave plate is an optical device made of a birefringent crystal that introduces a phase difference of pi radians (or a path difference of lambda/2) between the ordinary and extraordinary rays. It is used to rotate the plane of polarization of linearly polarized light.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of crystal birefringence, ordinary and extraordinary refractive indices, optical axis orientation, and qualitative understanding of polarization state conversion.

PGT extension

Quantitative derivation of thickness for a half-wave plate, quantitative analysis of rotation of the plane of polarization for incident linearly polarized light, and applications involving elliptical and circular polarizations.

Prerequisites

  • Nature of unpolarized and polarized light
  • Double refraction in uniaxial crystals (Calcite and Quartz)
  • Huygens' explanation of ordinary and extraordinary wave fronts

Principle of Birefringence and Wave Plates

  • Uniaxial crystals like calcite and quartz possess two refractive indices: no for the ordinary ray and ne for the extraordinary ray.
  • When a light wave enters the crystal parallel to its surface while the optical axis is cut parallel to the faces, it splits into two mutually perpendicular plane-polarized components traveling at different speeds.
  • A wave plate is cut with its optical axis parallel to the refracting faces so that the O-ray and E-ray travel in the same direction but with different phase velocities.

Action of a Half-Wave Plate (HWP)

  • A half-wave plate introduces a phase difference of &pi; (180&deg;) or a path difference of &lambda;/2 between the O-ray and E-ray.
  • For a negative crystal (like calcite where ne < no), the extraordinary ray travels faster than the ordinary ray.
  • For a positive crystal (like quartz where ne > no), the ordinary ray travels faster than the extraordinary ray.

Effect on Polarized Light

  • When linearly polarized light is incident on a half-wave plate such that its electric vector makes an angle &theta; with the optical axis, the emergent light remains linearly polarized.
  • The plane of vibration of the emergent linearly polarized light is rotated by an angle 2&theta; with respect to the incident plane of vibration, in the same direction if &theta; < 90&deg;.
  • If circularly polarized light is incident on a half-wave plate, the emergent light is also circularly polarized but with the opposite handedness.

Material Requirements and Thickness

  • The thickness of a half-wave plate is precisely calibrated to satisfy the phase retardation condition for a specific wavelength &lambda;.
  • Because thickness depends on wavelength (&lambda;), a half-wave plate designed for sodium light (589 nm) will not act as an exact half-wave plate for other spectral regions.
  • Quartz is commonly used for constructing wave plates due to its mechanical durability and optical clarity.

Derivations

Derivation of Thickness of a Half-Wave Plate

Conditions: Normal incidence, light of wavelength lambda, uniaxial crystal with ordinary and extraordinary refractive indices n_o and n_e.

  • Let the thickness of the crystal plate be t.
  • The time taken by the ordinary ray to cross the thickness t is $t_o = t / v_o = n_o t / c$, and by the extraordinary ray is $t_e = n_e t / c$.
  • The optical path difference between the two rays is given by Delt$a = |n_o - n_e| t$.
  • For a half-wave plate, the path difference must equal lambda / 2, or the phase difference must equal pi.
  • Equating path difference: |n_o - n_e| $t = \lambda / 2$.
  • Solving for thickness t gives $t = \lambda / (2 |n_o - n_e|)$.

Result: $t = \frac{\lambda}{2|n_o - n_e|}$

Derivation of Rotation of Plane of Polarization

Conditions: Linearly polarized incident light making an angle theta with the optical axis of a half-wave plate.

  • Let the incident electric field vector be E_0, making an angle theta with the optical axis (X-axis).
  • Resolve the incident vibration into two orthogonal components along the optical axis and perpendicular to it: $E_x = E_0 \cos(\theta) \cos(\omega t)$ and $E_y = E_0 \sin(\theta) \cos(\omega t)$.
  • Upon traversing the half-wave plate, a phase change of pi is introduced between the two components. The emergent components become E'_$x = E_0 \cos(\theta) \cos(\omega t)$ and E'_$y = E_0 \sin(\theta) \cos(\omega t + \pi) = -E_0 \sin(\theta) \cos(\omega t)$.
  • Let alpha be the angle that the emergent vibration makes with the optical axis. Then tan(alpha) = E'_y / E'_$x = -\tan(\theta)$.
  • This implies alph$a = -\theta$, meaning the plane of vibration is rotated to an angle -theta on the other side of the optical axis.
  • The total angle between the incident vibration direction and the emergent vibration direction is theta - (-theta) = 2theta.

Result: $\alpha = -\theta \text{ (total rotation } 2\theta\text{)}$

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the thickness of a half-wave plate for light of wavelength 589 nm. Given ordinary refractive index $n_o = 1.544$ and extraordinary refractive index $n_e = 1.553$ for quartz.

  • Identify given values: lambd$a = 589 nm = 5.89 x 10^-7 m$, $n_o = 1.544$, $n_e = 1.553$.
  • Write the formula for the thickness of a half-wave plate: $t = \lambda / (2 |n_o - n_e|)$.
  • Substitute the values: $t = (5.89 x 10^-7) / (2 x |1.544 - 1.553|)$.
  • Calculate the denominator: 2 x 0.009 = 0.018.
  • Compute t: $t = (5.89 x 10^-7) / 0.018 = 3.272 x 10^-5 m = 32.72 micrometers$.

Answer: 3.27 x 10^-5 m (or 32.72 micrometers)

PGT extension

A beam of linearly polarized light is incident on a half-wave plate. The plane of vibration of the incident light makes an angle of 30 degrees with the optical axis of the plate. Find the angle by which the plane of polarization is rotated.

  • Identify the angle theta between the incident polarization direction and the optical axis: theta = 30 degrees.
  • Recall the relation for the rotation of the plane of polarization by a half-wave plate: Rotation angle phi = 2theta.
  • Substitute the value of theta: phi = 2 x 30 degrees = 60 degrees.

Answer: 60 degrees

PGT extension

A calcite half-wave plate is designed for a wavelength of 500 nm. If $n_o = 1.658$ and $n_e = 1.486$ for calcite, what is the minimum thickness of the plate?

  • Identify given values: lambd$a = 500 nm = 5 x 10^-7 m$, $n_o = 1.658$, $n_e = 1.486$.
  • Use the half-wave plate thickness formula: $t = \lambda / (2 |n_o - n_e|)$.
  • Calculate |n_o - $n_e| = |1.658 - 1.486| = 0.172$.
  • Substitute into the formula: $t = (5 x 10^-7) / (2 x 0.172) = (5 x 10^-7) / 0.344$.
  • Compute t: $t = 1.453 x 10^-6 m = 1.45 micrometers$.

Answer: 1.45 x 10^-6 m (or 1.45 micrometers)

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes normal incidence of light on the parallel-cut crystal plate.
  • Assumes the refractive indices n_o and n_e remain constant across the entire wavefront aperture.
  • Limitation: A half-wave plate is strictly monochromatic; it acts as a half-wave plate for only one specific wavelength and fails to do so for a broad spectrum of white light.

Common misconceptions

Common error: A half-wave plate converts unpolarized light into circularly polarized light.

Correct: A half-wave plate rotates the plane of polarization of linearly polarized light or alters the handedness of circularly polarized light; it cannot polarize unpolarized light.

Unpolarized light lacks a coherent phase relationship between orthogonal components, which is required for deterministic retardation.

Common error: The thickness of a half-wave plate is independent of the wavelength of light used.

Correct: The thickness is directly proportional to the wavelength lambda.

From the formula $t = \lambda / (2 |n_o - n_e|)$, changing lambda requires a proportional change in thickness t to maintain the pi phase shift.

Quick revision

  • A half-wave plate introduces a phase difference of pi and a path difference of lambda/2 between O-ray and E-ray.
  • Phase Retardation: $\delta = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} |n_o - n_e| t$
  • Half-Wave Plate Condition (Path Difference): $\Delta = |n_o - n_e| t = \frac{\lambda}{2}$

Must remember

  • A half-wave plate introduces a phase difference of $\pi$ or path difference of $\lambda/2$ between the ordinary (O-ray) and extraordinary (E-ray) components.
  • The thickness of a half-wave plate is given by $t = \frac{\lambda}{2|n_o - n_e|}$.
  • When linearly polarized light is incident on a half-wave plate at an angle $\theta$ to the optical axis, the emergent light remains linearly polarized but its plane of vibration is rotated by an angle $2\theta$.
  • In a negative uniaxial crystal like calcite, $n_e < n_o$, meaning the extraordinary ray travels faster than the ordinary ray.
  • In a positive uniaxial crystal like quartz, $n_e > n_o$, meaning the ordinary ray travels faster than the extraordinary ray.
  • A half-wave plate alters the handedness of circularly polarized light (e.g., right-handed becomes left-handed).

Glossary

Ordinary Ray (O-ray)A polarized component of light inside a birefringent crystal that obeys ordinary laws of refraction and travels with velocity $v_o = c/n_o$.
Extraordinary Ray (E-ray)A polarized component of light inside a birefringent crystal that does not obey ordinary laws of refraction and travels with velocity $v_e = c/n_e$.
BirefringenceThe optical property of a material having a refractive index that depends on the polarization and propagation direction of light, characterized by $n_o$ and $n_e$.
Half-Wave Plate (HWP)An optical device made of a birefringent crystal cut parallel to the optical axis designed to introduce a $\pi$ phase shift between orthogonal polarization components.
Optical AxisA specific direction in a birefringent crystal along which the ordinary and extraordinary rays travel with the same velocity and experience no birefringence.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What phase difference is introduced between the ordinary and extraordinary rays by a half-wave plate?

Explanation: A half-wave plate introduces a phase shift of $\pi$ (or $180^\circ$) between the O-ray and E-ray.

2. What is the path difference corresponding to a half-wave plate for wavelength $\lambda$?

Explanation: A half-wave plate introduces a path difference of $\Delta = \lambda / 2$ between the two mutually perpendicular polarized components.

3. Which of the following expressions gives the correct thickness $t$ of a half-wave plate?

Explanation: Using the path difference condition $|n_o - n_e|t = \lambda / 2$, the thickness is $t = \frac{\lambda}{2|n_o - n_e|}__.

4. When linearly polarized light is incident on a half-wave plate with its electric vector making an angle $\theta$ with the optical axis, what is the angle of rotation of the plane of polarization of the emergent light?

Explanation: The plane of vibration of emergent linearly polarized light is rotated by an angle of $2\theta$ with respect to the incident plane.

5. What happens when circularly polarized light passes through a half-wave plate?

Explanation: A half-wave plate alters circularly polarized light into circularly polarized light of the opposite handedness (e.g., right to left).

6. In a negative uniaxial crystal like calcite, how do the refractive indices compare?

Explanation: In negative crystals like calcite, the extraordinary refractive index is less than the ordinary refractive index ($n_e < n_o$).

7. Which material is commonly used for constructing wave plates due to optical clarity and mechanical durability?

Explanation: Quartz is widely used for constructing wave plates because of its excellent optical clarity and mechanical durability.

8. What is the ordinary wave velocity inside a uniaxial crystal in terms of $c$ and $n_o$?

Explanation: The ordinary wave travels with a speed $v_o = c / n_o$, where $c$ is the speed of light in vacuum.

9. Can a half-wave plate convert unpolarized light into circularly polarized light?

Explanation: Unpolarized light has rapidly fluctuating, random phase relations between orthogonal components; a wave plate cannot polarize it.

10. If a half-wave plate is designed for sodium light ($\lambda = 589\text{ nm}$), will it function as an exact half-wave plate for blue light ($\lambda = 450\text{ nm}$) without adjustment?

Explanation: Since thickness $t$ depends directly on $\lambda$, a plate calibrated for sodium light will not provide a $\pi$ phase shift for blue light.

11. How are wave plates typically cut with respect to their optical axis?

Explanation: A wave plate is cut with its optical axis parallel to the refracting faces so that O-ray and E-ray travel in the same direction.

12. In a positive uniaxial crystal like quartz, which ray travels faster?

Explanation: In positive crystals ($n_e > n_o$), the ordinary ray travels faster than the extraordinary ray ($v_o > v_e$).

13. What is the general formula for phase retardation $\delta$ in a wave plate of thickness $t$?

Explanation: The general phase retardation is given by $\delta = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} |n_o - n_e| t$.

14. If the angle between the incident plane of vibration of linearly polarized light and the optical axis of a half-wave plate is $30^\circ$, what is the angle of rotation of the emergent plane of polarization?

Explanation: The rotation angle is $\phi = 2\theta = 2(30^\circ) = 60^\circ$.

15. What is the unit of phase retardation $\delta$?

Explanation: Phase retardation $\delta$ represents a phase angle and is measured in radians.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. A half-wave plate is designed for light of wavelength $5890\text{ Å}$. If $n_o = 1.544$ and $n_e = 1.553$ for quartz, what is the approximate thickness of the plate?

Explanation: Using $t = \frac{\lambda}{2|n_o - n_e|} = \frac{5.89 \times 10^{-7}}{2 \times 0.009} = \frac{5.89 \times 10^{-7}}{0.018} \approx 3.27 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}$.

2. Linearly polarized light is incident normally on a half-wave plate. If the angle between the electric vector of incident light and the optical axis is $45^\circ$, the emergent light will be:

Explanation: For incident linearly polarized light, a half-wave plate yields linearly polarized light with rotation $2\theta = 2(45^\circ) = 90^\circ$.

3. Which of the following correctly defines the extraordinary refractive index $n_e(\theta)$ when the wavefront normal makes an angle $\theta$ with the optical axis in a uniaxial crystal?

Explanation: The index ellipsoid equation for the extraordinary refractive index at an angle $\theta$ to the optical axis is $\frac{1}{n_e^2(\theta)} = \frac{\cos^2\theta}{n_o^2} + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{n_e^2}$.

4. If a quartz half-wave plate is placed between crossed polarizers with its optical axis at $45^\circ$ to the polarizer transmission axis, what is the state and intensity transmission of the emergent light?

Explanation: A half-wave plate rotates the polarization plane by $2(45^\circ) = 90^\circ$, aligning it parallel to the analyzer axis, resulting in maximum transmission.

5. In a birefringent crystal, the phase difference $\delta$ and path difference $\Delta$ are related by:

Explanation: Phase difference is related to path difference by $\delta = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \Delta$, where $\Delta = |n_o - n_e|t$.

6. Why does a half-wave plate exhibit chromatic aberration (wavelength dependence)?

Explanation: The required thickness $t$ depends directly on $\lambda$ and the dispersive difference $(n_o - n_e)$, making exact half-wave action wavelength-specific.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Calculate the path difference introduced by a half-wave plate for light of wavelength $600\text{ nm}$.

Explanation: A half-wave plate introduces a path difference of $\Delta = \lambda / 2 = 600 / 2 = 300\text{ nm}$.

2. Which of the following wave plates would convert circularly polarized light into linearly polarized light?

Explanation: A quarter-wave plate introduces a $\pi/2$ phase shift, converting circularly polarized light into linearly polarized light (and vice versa).

3. If the refractive indices for ordinary and extraordinary rays in a calcite crystal are $1.658$ and $1.486$ respectively, for $\lambda = 589\text{ nm}$, what is the thickness of a half-wave plate?

Explanation: $t = \frac{5.89 \times 10^{-7}}{2 |1.658 - 1.486|} = \frac{5.89 \times 10^{-7}}{2(0.172)} = \frac{5.89 \times 10^{-7}}{0.344} \approx 1.71 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}$.

4. A beam of linearly polarized light is incident on a half-wave plate. If the incident vibration makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the optical axis, what is the angle between the incident and emergent planes of polarization?

Explanation: The rotation angle is $2\theta = 2(60^\circ) = 120^\circ$.

5. What is the extraordinary wave velocity for propagation perpendicular to the optical axis in terms of $c$ and $n_e$?

Explanation: The principal velocity of the extraordinary ray is $v_e = c / n_e$.

6. In a zero-order half-wave plate, what is the integer order of retardation $m$?

Explanation: For zero-order wave plates, the integer multiple $m$ in the general retardation equation $R = 2\pi m + \pi$ is equal to $0$.

7. Which type of crystal has $n_e > n_o$?

Explanation: Positive uniaxial crystals such as quartz satisfy the condition $n_e > n_o$.

8. If right-handed circularly polarized light is passed through a half-wave plate, what is the handedness of the emergent light?

Explanation: A half-wave plate reverses the handedness of circularly polarized light, turning right-handed into left-handed circular polarization.

9. What happens to the state of polarization of linearly polarized light if its electric vector is parallel ($\theta = 0$) to the optical axis of a half-wave plate?

Explanation: If $\theta = 0$, the rotation angle $2\theta = 0$, so the plane of polarization is unchanged.

10. Which equation represents the general retardation for a half-wave plate including multiple orders?

Explanation: The general retardation equation for a half-wave plate accounting for multi-order plates is $R = 2\pi m + \pi$.
