Cardinal Points of a Coaxial Optical System

A coaxial optical system comprising multiple refracting surfaces can be completely characterized by six cardinal points situated on its principal axis. These points include two focal points, two principal points, and two nodal points.

Introduction to Cardinal Points

  • A coaxial system of centring lenses and spherical refracting surfaces has six designated cardinal points on its principal axis.
  • These points consist of three pairs: two principal foci (F1, F2), two principal points (H1, H2), and two nodal points (N1, N2).
  • Knowing these points allows any complex optical system to be treated as a 'black box', simplifying ray tracing and image formation calculations.

Principal Foci (F<sub>1</sub>, F<sub>2</sub>)

  • The first focal point (F1) or object focus is an axial point for which rays starting from it emerge parallel to the principal axis after refraction.
  • The second focal point (F2) or image focus is an axial point where rays parallel to the principal axis incident on the system converge or appear to diverge from after refraction.
  • The distance from the first principal point to F1 is the first focal length (f1), and from the second principal point to F2 is the second focal length (f2).

Principal Planes and Points (H<sub>1</sub>, H<sub>2</sub>)

  • Principal planes are a pair of conjugate planes on the optical axis for which the transverse magnification is +1.
  • This implies that an object of height y placed in the first principal plane forms an erect image of the same height y in the second principal plane.
  • The points of intersection of these planes with the principal axis are called the principal points (H1 and H2).

Nodal Points and Optical Center (N<sub>1</sub>, N<sub>2</sub>)

  • Nodal points are a pair of conjugate points on the axis having an angular magnification of +1.
  • A ray directed toward the first nodal point (N1) emerges from the second nodal point (N2) parallel to its initial direction.
  • If the medium on both sides of the optical system is the same, the nodal points coincide with the principal points.

Derivations

Relation between Focal Lengths and Refractive Indices

Conditions: Coaxial system bounded by refractive indices mu_1 on the object side and mu_2 on the image side.

  • Consider paraxial rays traveling from a medium of refractive index mu_1 to mu_2 through the optical system.
  • Using Lagrange's invariant or the refraction matrix elements (A, B, C, D) for the system, relate object and image coordinates.
  • Apply the condition for principal foci where parallel incident rays yield a focus, and vice versa.
  • Express the ratio of focal lengths in terms of the terminal refractive indices.

Result: \frac{f_1}{$f_2 = -\frac{\mu_1}{\mu_2}$

Newton's Optical Formula for Cardinal Points

Conditions: Distances measured from the respective principal foci.

  • Let x_1 be the distance of the object from the first principal focus F_1, and x_2 be the distance of the image from the second principal focus F_2.
  • Using the linear magnification equations referenced to the principal foci, write x_1 and x_2.
  • Multiply the distances x_1 and x_2 to find the constant product relation.

Result: x_1 $x_2 = f_1 f_2$

Solved numericals

TGT core

The focal lengths of a coaxial optical system in air are given as $f_1 = -15 cm$ and $f_2 = +15 cm$. Find the optical power of the system.

  • Identify the medium: since it is in air, \m$u_1 = \mu_2 = 1$.
  • Use the power formula $P = \frac{1}{f_2} (in meters)$.
  • Substitute $f_2 = +0.15 m into the formula$.
  • Calculate $P = \frac{1}{0.15} = +6.67 \text{ m}^{-1} (Diopters)$.

Answer: +6.67 D

PGT extension

For a coaxial optical system separating two media with \m$u_1 = 1.0$ and \m$u_2 = 1.5$, the first focal length f_1 is -10 cm. Determine the second focal length f_2.

  • Recall the relation between focal lengths and refractive indices: \frac{f_1}{$f_2 = -\frac{\mu_1}{\mu_2}$.
  • Rearrange to solve for f_2: $f_2 = -f_1 \left(\frac{\mu_2}{\mu_1}\right)$.
  • Substitute the given values: $f_2 = -(-10 \text{ cm}) \left(\frac{1.5}{1.0}\right)$.
  • Calculate the final value: $f_2 = 10 \times 1.5 = +15 \text{ cm}$.

Answer: +15 cm

PGT extension

In a coaxial system, an object placed 5 cm from the first principal focus F_1 forms an image at a distance of 20 cm from the second principal focus F_2. Find the product of the focal lengths f_1 f_2.

  • Use Newton's optical formula: x_1 $x_2 = f_1 f_2$.
  • Substitute the given distances from the foci: $x_1 = 5 \text{ cm}$ and $x_2 = 20 \text{ cm}$.
  • Calculate the product: f_1 $f_2 = 5 \times 20 = 100 \text{ cm}^2$.

Answer: 100 cm^2

Assumptions and limitations

  • Paraxial approximation (sin \theta \approx \theta, \tan \theta \approx \theta) must hold valid for all rays.
  • The system is assumed to be strictly coaxial with spherical refracting surfaces.
  • Apertures are assumed small to avoid spherical and chromatic aberrations affecting cardinal point locations.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Principal points are always located inside the physical boundaries of the lenses.

Correct: Principal points can lie outside the lens system, depending on the curvatures and separation of the component lenses.

The principal planes are mathematical constructs defined by unit magnification, not physical surfaces.

Common error: Nodal points and principal points are always at the exact same positions.

Correct: They coincide only when the refractive index of the medium on the object side equals that of the image side.

When \mu_1 \neq \mu_2, the separation between principal points and nodal points depends on the ratio of the refractive indices and focal lengths.

Quick revision

  • A coaxial system has exactly 6 cardinal points: 2 foci, 2 principal points, and 2 nodal points.
  • Principal planes have a transverse magnification of +1.
  • Newton's formula is given by x_1 $x_2 = f_1 f_2$.
  • The ratio of focal lengths satisfies f_1 / $f_2 = - \mu_1 / \mu_2$.

Must remember

  • A coaxial optical system has six cardinal points: two principal foci ($F_1, F_2$), two principal points ($H_1, H_2$), and two nodal points ($N_1, N_2$).
  • Principal planes are conjugate planes with a transverse magnification of $m = +1$.
  • Newton's formula for a coaxial optical system is given by $x_1 x_2 = f_1 f_2$, where distances are measured from the principal foci.
  • The ratio of focal lengths is related to the initial and final refractive indices by $\frac{f_1}{f_2} = -\frac{\mu_1}{\mu_2}$.
  • Nodal points coincide with principal points if and only if the refractive index of the medium on both sides of the system is the same.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. How many cardinal points are defined for a coaxial optical system on its principal axis?

Explanation: A coaxial system of centring lenses and spherical refracting surfaces has six designated cardinal points: two focal points, two principal points, and two nodal points.

2. What is the transverse magnification for the principal planes in a coaxial optical system?

Explanation: Principal planes are a pair of conjugate planes on the optical axis for which the transverse magnification is +1.

3. Newton's formula for a coaxial optical system is expressed as:

Explanation: Newton's formula is given by $x_1 x_2 = f_1 f_2$, where $x_1$ and $x_2$ are object and image distances measured from the principal foci.

4. The ratio of the first and second focal lengths of a general coaxial optical system in different media is given by:

Explanation: The ratio of focal lengths for a general coaxial system is $\frac{f_1}{f_2} = -\frac{\mu_1}{\mu_2}$, accounting for different initial and final refractive indices.

5. When do the nodal points coincide with the principal points in an optical system?

Explanation: If the medium on both sides of the optical system is the same ($\mu_1 = \mu_2$), the nodal points coincide with the principal points.

6. What is the angular magnification associated with a pair of nodal points?

Explanation: Nodal points are a pair of conjugate points on the axis having an angular magnification of +1, meaning a ray directed toward $N_1$ emerges from $N_2$ parallel to its initial direction.

7. What is the expression for the optical power $P$ of a coaxial system in terms of the second focal length $f_2$ and initial refractive index $\mu_1$?

Explanation: The power of the optical system is defined by $P = -\frac{\mu_2}{f_1} = \frac{\mu_1}{f_2}$.

8. Which of the following best describes the first principal focus ($F_1$)?

Explanation: The first focal point ($F_1$) or object focus is an axial point for which rays starting from it emerge parallel to the principal axis after refraction.

9. Can principal points lie outside the physical boundaries of the lenses in a complex optical system?

Explanation: Principal points can lie outside the lens system entirely, as principal planes are mathematical constructs defined by unit magnification.

10. If an object of height $y$ is placed in the first principal plane, what is the height and nature of the image formed in the second principal plane?

Explanation: Because transverse magnification is $+1$ at the principal planes, an object of height $y$ forms an erect image of the exact same height $y$ in the second principal plane.

11. What is the unit of optical power $P$ for a coaxial system?

Explanation: Optical power is measured in diopters, which correspond to inverse meters ($m^{-1}$).

12. In matrix methods, what does the element $C$ in the system ABCD matrix predominantly help determine?

Explanation: Using system matrix elements ($A, B, C, D$), the positions of principal points from the surfaces are given by formulas involving $C$, such as $d_1 = \frac{1-D}{C}$ and $d_2 = \frac{A-1}{C}$.

13. Which cardinal points serve to simplify ray tracing by allowing a complex system to be treated as a 'black box'?

Explanation: Knowing all six cardinal points allows any complex optical system to be treated as a 'black box', simplifying ray tracing and image formation calculations.

14. What happens to the nodal point separation from the principal points ($H_1 N_1 = H_2 N_2$) in a general coaxial system?

Explanation: The separation between principal points and their corresponding nodal points is given by $H_1 N_1 = H_2 N_2 = f_1 + f_2$ in a general coaxial system.

15. Which statement is correct regarding misconceptions about nodal and principal points?

Explanation: Nodal points and principal points coincide only when the refractive index of the medium on the object side equals that of the image side.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. In a coaxial optical system separating two different media where $\mu_1 \neq \mu_2$, what is the relation between the first focal length $f_1$ and the second focal length $f_2$?

Explanation: For a general coaxial system in different media, the ratio of focal lengths is governed by $\frac{f_1}{f_2} = -\frac{\mu_1}{\mu_2}$.

2. A ray of light directed toward the first nodal point $N_1$ of a coaxial optical system emerges from the second nodal point $N_2$:

Explanation: By definition of nodal points (angular magnification $+1$), a ray directed toward $N_1$ emerges from $N_2$ parallel to its initial direction.

3. Using the ABCD system matrix, if the element $C$ and $D$ are known for the refraction up to the system boundary, the position of the first principal point from the first surface is given by:

Explanation: The position of the first principal point from the first surface is formulated as $d_1 = \frac{1 - D}{C}$ using the matrix method.

4. What is the transverse magnification of the principal planes $H_1$ and $H_2$ in any standard coaxial optical system?

Explanation: Principal planes are defined specifically as a pair of conjugate planes on the optical axis for which the transverse magnification is $+1$.

5. If Newton's formula $x_1 x_2 = f_1 f_2$ is applied to a lens system, what are $x_1$ and $x_2$ measured from?

Explanation: In Newton's formula, distances $x_1$ and $x_2$ are strictly measured from the principal foci $F_1$ and $F_2$ respectively.

6. Which of the following pairs of cardinal points coincide when the initial and final refractive indices of the optical system are equal?

Explanation: When the medium on both sides of the optical system is the same, the nodal points coincide with the principal points ($H_1$ with $N_1$ and $H_2$ with $N_2$).
