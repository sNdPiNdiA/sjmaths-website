Optics

Quarter-Wave Plate

A quarter-wave plate is an optical device made of a birefringent crystal cut parallel to its optic axis, introducing a phase difference of pi/2 radians (or a path difference of lambda/4) between the ordinary and extraordinary rays. It is fundamentally used for converting linearly polarized light into circularly or elliptically polarized light and vice versa.

Scope and prerequisites

Prerequisites

  • Wave nature of light and electromagnetic wave polarization
  • Double refraction in uniaxial crystals (Ordinary and Extraordinary rays)
  • Phase difference and path difference relationships

Principle of Birefringence

  • Uniaxial crystals like calcite (negative, mue < muo) and quartz (positive, mue > muo) possess two distinct principal refractive indices.
  • When unpolarized or linearly polarized light enters the crystal, it splits into two orthogonally polarized components: the O-ray and the E-ray.
  • Due to different refractive indices (muo and mue), the O-ray and E-ray travel at different phase velocities inside the crystal.

Optical Path and Phase Retardation

  • As the rays traverse a crystal of thickness d, a relative optical path difference Delta = |muo - mue|d is introduced.
  • The corresponding phase difference delta between the emergent O-ray and E-ray is given by delta = (2*pi / lambda) * |muo - mue|d.
  • For a quarter-wave plate, the thickness d is precisely calibrated so that delta = pi / 2 radians (or 90 degrees).

Production of Circularly Polarized Light

  • To produce circularly polarized light, the incident linearly polarized light must have its electric vector making an angle of 45 degrees with the optic axis of the quarter-wave plate.
  • This splits the incident amplitude into two equal components along the fast and slow axes of the crystal.
  • With a quarter-wave phase shift introduced between these equal orthogonal components, the resultant tip of the electric field vector traces out a circle.

Wavelength Dependence and Achromatism

  • The retardation delta depends explicitly on the wavelength lambda, meaning a quarter-wave plate is strictly monochromatic.
  • Both the birefringence (|muo - mue|) and the wavelength lambda vary with dispersion.
  • Achromatic quarter-wave plates are constructed using two different birefringent materials (such as quartz and magnesium fluoride) to compensate for dispersion over a finite wavelength band.

Derivations

Thickness of a Quarter-Wave Plate

Conditions: Uniaxial crystal cut with optic axis parallel to the refracting faces and normal to the incident light.

  • Let the thickness of the crystal plate be d and the wavelength of incident light in vacuum be lambda.
  • The refractive index for the ordinary ray is muo and for the extraordinary ray is mue.
  • The path difference introduced between the O-ray and E-ray in traversing thickness d is Delta = |muo - mue|d.
  • Equating the path difference to lambda / 4 for a quarter-wave plate: |muo - mue|d = lambda / 4.

Result: d = \frac{\lambda}{4 |\mu_o - \mu_e|}

Emergent State of Polarization from a Quarter-Wave Plate

Conditions: Linearly polarized light incident at an angle theta = 45 degrees to the optic axis.

  • Let the incident electric field be E = E_0 \cos(\omega t). Resolve it into components along the fast axis (x-axis) and slow axis (y-axis): E_x = E_0 \cos(45^\circ) \cos(\omega t) and E_y = E_0 \sin(45^\circ) \cos(\omega t).
  • Since E_x = E_y = \frac{E_0}{\sqrt{2}} \cos(\omega t), both components have equal amplitudes a = \frac{E_0}{\sqrt{2}}.
  • The quarter-wave plate introduces a phase difference of pi/2 to one of the components, say E_y = a \cos(\omega t + \frac{\pi}{2}) = -a \sin(\omega t).
  • Eliminating time t from the equations yields \frac{E_x^2}{a^2} + \frac{E_y^2}{a^2} = 1, which represents the equation of a circle.

Result: E_x^2 + E_y^2 = a^2 \quad \text{(Circularly Polarized Light)}

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the thickness of a quarter-wave plate for light of wavelength 589 nm. Given the ordinary and extraordinary refractive indices for quartz are mu_o = 1.544 and mu_e = 1.553.

  • Identify the given values: lambda = 589 nm = 589 * 10^-9 m, mu_o = 1.544, mu_e = 1.553.
  • Calculate the magnitude of birefringence: |mu_o - mu_e| = |1.544 - 1.553| = 0.009.
  • Use the quarter-wave plate thickness formula: d = lambda / (4 * |mu_o - mu_e|).
  • Substitute the values: d = (589 * 10^-9) / (4 * 0.009) = (589 * 10^-9) / 0.036 = 1.636 * 10^-5 m = 16.36 micrometers.

Answer: 1.636 * 10^-5 m (or 16.36 micrometers)

PGT extension

A quarter-wave plate is designed for a wavelength of 500 nm. What will be the actual phase retardation produced by this plate when light of wavelength 600 nm is passed through it, assuming the birefringence remains approximately constant?

  • Design wavelength lambda_1 = 500 nm gives a phase retardation of delta_1 = pi / 2 radians.
  • The phase retardation formula is delta = (2 * pi / lambda) * |mu_o - mu_e| * d.
  • Since |mu_o - mu_e| * d is constant for the plate, delta is inversely proportional to lambda: delta_2 / delta_1 = lambda_1 / lambda_2.
  • Substitute the known values: delta_2 = (pi / 2) * (500 nm / 600 nm) = (pi / 2) * (5 / 6) = 5pi / 12 radians.

Answer: 5pi / 12 radians (or 75 degrees)

PGT extension

Linearly polarized light is incident on a quarter-wave plate with its electric vector at an angle of 30 degrees to the optic axis. Determine the ratio of the maximum to minimum intensities of the emergent elliptically polarized light when examined through a rotating analyzer.

  • The amplitudes along the fast and slow axes are proportional to a = E_0 cos(30^\circ) = E_0 * (sqrt(3)/2) and b = E_0 sin(30^\circ) = E_0 * (1/2).
  • A quarter-wave plate introduces a pi/2 phase difference, converting the orthogonal linear components into orthogonal circular/elliptic components whose semi-axes of the polarization ellipse correspond to a and b.
  • When examined with a rotating analyzer (like a polarizer), the maximum transmitted intensity I_max is proportional to the square of the semi-major axis (a^2), and I_min is proportional to the square of the semi-minor axis (b^2).
  • Calculate the ratio: I_max / I_min = (a^2) / (b^2) = (cos(30^\circ) / sin(30^\circ))^2 = (cot(30^\circ))^2 = (sqrt(3))^2 = 3.

Answer: 3 : 1

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes normal incidence of light rays onto the parallel faces of the crystal plate.
  • Neglects internal reflections and multiple reflections within the thin crystal plate.
  • Valid strictly for the specific design wavelength unless an achromatic composite wave plate is utilized.

Common misconceptions

Common error: A quarter-wave plate converts unpolarized light into circularly polarized light.

Correct: A quarter-wave plate converts linearly polarized light into circularly polarized light.

Unpolarized light lacks a fixed phase relationship between orthogonal components, so passing it through a QWP simply yields another form of unpolarized light.

Common error: The thickness of a quarter-wave plate is independent of the wavelength of light.

Correct: The required thickness depends directly on the wavelength of light being used.

The formula d = lambda / (4 * |mu_o - mu_e|) shows that d is proportional to lambda.

Common error: Any orientation of incident linear polarization produces circular polarization through a QWP.

Correct: Circular polarization is only produced when the incident polarization is at 45 degrees to the optic axis.

At other angles (0 or 90 degrees), the light remains linearly polarized, and at general angles, it becomes elliptically polarized.

Quick revision

  • A quarter-wave plate introduces a phase difference of pi/2 (path difference of lambda/4) between O-ray and E-ray, with thickness d = lambda / (4 * |mu_o - mu_e|).
  • Phase Retardation: \delta = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} |\mu_o - \mu_e| d
  • Quarter-Wave Plate Thickness: d = \frac{\lambda}{4 |\mu_o - \mu_e|}

Must remember

  • A quarter-wave plate introduces a phase difference of $\delta = \frac{\pi}{2}$ radians ($90^\circ$) between the ordinary (O-ray) and extraordinary (E-ray) components.
  • The thickness of a first-order quarter-wave plate is given by $d = \frac{\lambda}{4 |\mu_o - \mu_e|}$.
  • To produce circularly polarized light from linearly polarized light, the electric vector must make an angle of $45^\circ$ with the optic axis of the plate.
  • Calcite is a negative uniaxial crystal ($\mu_e < \mu_o$) while quartz is a positive uniaxial crystal ($\mu_e > \mu_o$).
  • Quarter-wave plates are strictly monochromatic unless achromatic combinations of multiple birefringent materials are used.

Glossary

Ordinary Ray (O-ray)The polarized component of light that obeys Snell's law of refraction and travels with a constant velocity $\frac{c}{\mu_o}$ in all directions within a uniaxial crystal.
Extraordinary Ray (E-ray)The polarized component of light whose velocity depends on its direction of propagation within the crystal, governed by the principal refractive index $\mu_e$.
BirefringenceThe optical property of a material having a refractive index that depends on the polarization and propagation direction of light, quantified as $|\mu_o - \mu_e|$.
Phase RetardationThe phase difference $\delta$ introduced between the orthogonal O-ray and E-ray components as they emerge from a birefringent wave plate of thickness $d$.
Achromatic Quarter-Wave PlateA specialized wave plate constructed from two different birefringent crystals to provide uniform quarter-wave retardation over a finite wavelength range.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the phase difference introduced between the O-ray and E-ray by a quarter-wave plate?

Explanation: A quarter-wave plate is precisely calibrated to introduce a phase retardation of $delta = rac{$\pi$}{2}$ radians ($90^circ$) between the emerging O-ray and E-ray components.

2. Which of the following crystals is classified as a negative uniaxial crystal?

Explanation: Calcite is a negative uniaxial crystal where the extraordinary refractive index is less than the ordinary refractive index ($mu_e < mu_o$).

3. What is the required thickness of a first-order quarter-wave plate for wavelength $lambda$?

Explanation: Equating the phase retardation formula $delta = rac{2pi}{lambda} |mu_o - mu_e| d$ to $ rac{$\pi$}{2}$ yields the first-order thickness $d = rac{lambda}{4 |mu_o - mu_e|}boks$.

4. To produce circularly polarized light using a quarter-wave plate, what must be the angle between the electric vector of incident linear polarization and the optic axis?

Explanation: The incident electric field must make an angle of $45^circ$ with the optic axis to resolve into two equal orthogonal amplitude components along the fast and slow axes.

5. What happens when unpolarized light is passed through a quarter-wave plate?

Explanation: Unpolarized light lacks a fixed phase relationship between orthogonal components; passing it through a QWP simply yields another form of unpolarized light.

6. In a positive uniaxial crystal such as quartz, how do the refractive indices relate?

Explanation: In positive uniaxial crystals like quartz, the extraordinary refractive index is greater than the ordinary refractive index ($mu_e > mu_o$).

7. What is the optical path difference $Delta$ introduced by a wave plate of thickness $d$?

Explanation: The optical path difference is given by $Delta = |mu_o - mu_e| d$, representing the difference in optical path lengths traversed by the O-ray and E-ray.

8. Why is a standard quarter-wave plate strictly monochromatic?

Explanation: The retardation formula $delta = rac{2pi}{lambda} |mu_o - mu_e| d$ shows that $delta$ depends directly on $lambda$, making the plate function correctly for only one specific wavelength.

9. What is the general thickness formula for a higher-order quarter-wave plate where $m = 0, 1, 2, ...$?

Explanation: Higher-order wave plates have a total phase retardation of $delta = (2m + 1) rac{$\pi$}{2}$, leading to the general thickness expression $d = rac{(2m + 1)lambda}{4 |mu_o - mu_e|}$.

10. If linearly polarized light is incident parallel to the optic axis ($0^circ$ or $90^circ$) of a quarter-wave plate, what is the emergent state?

Explanation: When incident parallel to the optic axis, light travels entirely along one principal axis without splitting into components, so its polarization state remains unchanged (linear).

11. How are achromatic quarter-wave plates constructed?

Explanation: Achromatic quarter-wave plates use two distinct birefringent materials (such as quartz and magnesium fluoride) with complementary dispersion properties to function across a finite wavelength band.

12. Which of the following represents the condition for producing circular polarization from linear polarization?

Explanation: Circular polarization is produced when the angle $ heta$ between the incident polarization and the optic axis satisfies $ heta = 45^circ + n(90^circ)$ for any integer $n$.

13. What happens to the O-ray and E-ray velocities inside a uniaxial negative crystal?

Explanation: In a negative uniaxial crystal ($mu_e < mu_o$), the extraordinary ray travels faster than the ordinary ray ($v_e > v_o$, meaning $v_o < v_e$).

14. What is the unit of phase retardation $delta$?

Explanation: Phase retardation $delta$ represents a phase angle and is measured in radians (or degrees).

15. If the angle $ heta$ between incident linear polarization and the optic axis is neither $0^circ$, $45^circ$, nor $90^circ$, what polarization state emerges from a quarter-wave plate?

Explanation: At arbitrary angles other than multiples of $45^circ$, the orthogonal components have unequal amplitudes, resulting in elliptical polarization upon passing through the quarter-wave plate.

2. Which crystal and refractive index condition correctly defines a positive uniaxial crystal?

Explanation: Quartz is a classic positive uniaxial crystal characterized by $\mu_e > \mu_o$, meaning the extraordinary refractive index exceeds the ordinary refractive index.

3. What phase change is introduced by a half-wave plate compared to a quarter-wave plate?

Explanation: A half-wave plate introduces a phase retardation of $\$\pi$$ radians ($180^\circ$), whereas a quarter-wave plate introduces $\frac{\$\pi$}{2}$ radians ($90^\circ$).

4. In the general elliptic polarization equation produced by a wave plate, what value of phase retardation $\delta$ corresponds to a quarter-wave plate?

Explanation: For a quarter-wave plate, the phase difference $\delta = \frac{\$\pi$}{2}$, which simplifies the general ellipse equation to $\frac{E_x^2}{a^2} + \frac{E_y^2}{b^2} = 1$ when axes align.

5. If a beam of linearly polarized light is incident normally on a quarter-wave plate with its polarization azimuth at $45^\circ$ to the optic axis, the emergent light is:

Explanation: An azimuth of $45^\circ$ splits the incoming linear vibration into two equal orthogonal components with a $\frac{\$\pi$}{2}$ phase shift, resulting in circularly polarized light.

6. Why does the required thickness of a quarter-wave plate change when switching from sodium light ($\lambda_1$) to mercury green light ($\lambda_2$)?

Explanation: The formula $d = \frac{\lambda}{4 |\mu_o - \mu_e|}$ shows that thickness $d$ is directly proportional to wavelength $\lambda$ (ignoring minor dispersion changes in birefringence).

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Calculate the phase difference introduced by a quarter-wave plate for light of wavelength $500 \text{ nm}$ if its thickness is perfectly calibrated.

Explanation: By definition, a quarter-wave plate is calibrated to produce a phase retardation of exactly $\frac{\$\pi$}{2}$ radians ($90^\circ$).

2. Which of the following statements is correct regarding the O-ray and E-ray in a uniaxial crystal?

Explanation: The ordinary ray (O-ray) obeys ordinary refraction laws and Snell's law with constant refractive index $\mu_o$, whereas the extraordinary ray (E-ray) has a direction-dependent velocity.

3. If the order $m = 2$ in a higher-order quartz wave plate, what multiple of $\frac{\lambda}{4 |\mu_o - \mu_e|}$ gives the thickness?

Explanation: Using the general thickness formula $d = \frac{(2m + 1)\lambda}{4 |\mu_o - \mu_e|}$, substituting $m = 2$ gives $(2(2) + 1) = 5$ times $\frac{\lambda}{4 |\mu_o - \mu_e|}boks$.

4. What is the primary limitation of using a single quartz quarter-wave plate across a broad visible spectrum?

Explanation: Since phase retardation $\delta = \frac{2\$\pi$}{\lambda} |\mu_o - \mu_e| d$, a single plate only provides exactly $\frac{\$\pi$}{2}$ retardation for one specific wavelength.

5. In a negative uniaxial crystal, which refractive index is larger?

Explanation: In a negative uniaxial crystal, $\mu_e < \mu_o$, meaning the ordinary refractive index $\mu_o$ is larger than the extraordinary refractive index $\mu_e$.

6. If linearly polarized light is incident on a quarter-wave plate at an angle of $30^\circ$ to the optic axis, what is the emergent polarization?

Explanation: An angle of $30^\circ$ (not $45^\circ$) creates unequal orthogonal amplitude components, resulting in elliptical polarization after the quarter-wave phase shift.

7. What is the optical path difference corresponding to a quarter-wave plate phase retardation of $\frac{\$\pi$}{2}$?

Explanation: Phase difference $\delta = \frac{2\$\pi$}{\lambda} \Delta$. Setting $\delta = \frac{\$\pi$}{2}$ gives optical path difference $\Delta = \frac{\lambda}{4}$.

8. Which material combination is typically used to fabricate an achromatic quarter-wave plate?

Explanation: Achromatic wave plates commonly combine quartz and magnesium fluoride to offset dispersion across a wider wavelength band.

9. If a quarter-wave plate is rotated by $90^\circ$ about the propagation direction, what happens to the output circular polarization state (assuming $45^\circ$ incidence)?

Explanation: Rotating the crystal axes by $90^\circ$ interchanges the fast and slow axes, which reverses the sign of the phase shift and thus inverts the handedness (right-to-left or left-to-right) of the circular polarization.

10. Which of the following conditions is essential for observing birefringence in a crystal?

Explanation: Birefringence and splitting into O-ray and E-ray occur when light propagates at an angle to the optic axis; propagation strictly along the optic axis exhibits no double refraction.
