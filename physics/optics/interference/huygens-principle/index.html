Huygens' Principle and Wavefront Propagation

Huygens' principle is a geometric construction method used to determine the shape of a wavefront at any subsequent time from its known shape at an earlier time. It postulates that every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Geometrical construction of spherical and plane wavefronts, laws of reflection and refraction derived using wave theory, and elementary understanding of secondary wavelets.

PGT extension

Fresnel's modification of Huygens' principle incorporating obliquity factor, Kirchhoff's integral theorem, zone plate analysis, and phase relationships of secondary wavelets.

Prerequisites

  • Wave motion and wave equation
  • Concept of wavefronts and rays
  • Phase and amplitude of harmonic waves

Definition of Wavefront

  • A wavefront is the locus of all adjacent points in a medium vibrating in the same phase.
  • The direction of propagation of light (ray) is always perpendicular to the wavefront at any point.
  • Depending on the source geometry, wavefronts are classified as spherical, cylindrical, or plane.

Huygens' Postulates

  • Every point on a given wavefront acts as a secondary source of light, emitting secondary wavelets in all directions with the speed of light in that medium.
  • The envelope (tangent surface) of these secondary wavelets, considered in the forward direction after a time interval t, defines the new position of the wavefront.
  • The back-propagation of secondary wavelets is non-existent or has zero amplitude, as accounted for by the obliquity factor.

Laws of Reflection via Wave Theory

  • A plane wavefront incident obliquely on a reflecting surface establishes secondary wavelets successively along the boundary.
  • The time taken by the wave to travel from the incident wavefront to the reflecting surface equals the time taken by the secondary wavelet to travel back.
  • This equality of distances proves that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection (i = r).

Laws of Refraction (Snell's Law) via Wave Theory

  • When a plane wavefront crosses the boundary between two optical media of refractive indices n&subscript;1&subscript; and n&subscript;2&subscript;, its speed changes from v&subscript;1&subscript; to v&subscript;2&subscript;.
  • The secondary wavelets in the second medium travel a distance v&subscript;2&subscript;t in time t, while those in the first medium travel v&subscript;1&subscript;t.
  • The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction yields the constant refractive index ratio v&subscript;1&subscript;/v&subscript;2&subscript;.

Derivations

Derivation of the Law of Reflection

Conditions: Plane wavefront incident on a plane reflecting surface at an angle i.

  • Let a plane wavefront AB be incident on a reflecting surface XY at an angle i.
  • As point A strikes the surface first, it begins to emit secondary wavelets. By the time point B travels a distance BC = v&subscript;t to reach the surface at C, the secondary wavelet from A has spread out to a radius AD = v&subscript;t.
  • Drawing a tangent from C to the sphere of radius vt gives the reflected wavefront CD.
  • In triangles ABC and ADC, angle ABC = angle ADC = 90 degrees, side AC is common, and BC = AD = vt.
  • Therefore, triangles ABC and ADC are congruent, yielding angle BAC = angle DCA, which proves angle of incidence i equals angle of reflection r.

Result: i = r

Derivation of Snell's Law of Refraction

Conditions: Plane wavefront incident on an interface separating two media with speeds v&subscript;1&subscript; and v&subscript;2&subscript;.

  • Consider a plane wavefront AB incident on the interface XY separating medium 1 (speed v&subscript;1&subscript;) and medium 2 (speed v&subscript;2&subscript;) at angle of incidence i.
  • When point B reaches the interface at C in time t, distance BC = v&subscript;1&subscript;t.
  • During this time t, the secondary wavelet originating from A travels a distance AD = v&subscript;2&subscript;t in medium 2.
  • The refracted wavefront is represented by the tangent CD, making an angle of refraction r with the surface XY, such that angle DCA = r.
  • From triangle ABC, sin(i) = BC / AC = v&subscript;1&subscript;t / AC. From triangle ADC, sin(r) = AD / AC = v&subscript;2&subscript;t / AC.
  • Dividing sin(i) by sin(r) gives v&subscript;1&subscript; / v&subscript;2&subscript;, which equals the constant refractive index n&subscript;21&subscript;.

Result: \frac{sin(i)}{sin(r)} = \frac{v_1}{v_2} = n_{21}

Solved numericals

TGT core

Light of wavelength 6000 Å in vacuum enters a medium of refractive index 1.5. Calculate the wavelength of light in the medium.

  • Identify given values: \lambda_0 = 6000 Å = 6 \times 10^{-7} m, n = 1.5.
  • Use the relation \lambda_m = \lambda_0 / n.
  • Substitute the values: \lambda_m = (6 \times 10^{-7}) / 1.5 = 4 \times 10^{-7} m.

Answer: 4000 Å (or 4 \times 10^{-7} m)

TGT core

A plane wavefront is incident at an angle of 30 degrees on a reflecting surface. What is the angle between the incident wavefront and the reflecting surface?

  • The angle of incidence i is defined between the incident ray and the normal to the reflecting surface, given as 30 degrees.
  • The incident wavefront is perpendicular to the incident ray.
  • The angle between the incident wavefront and the reflecting surface is 90 degrees minus the angle of incidence i.
  • Calculation: 90 degrees - 30 degrees = 60 degrees.

Answer: 60 degrees

PGT extension

Monochromatic light of frequency 5 \times 10^{14} Hz passes from air into water of refractive index 4/3. Determine the speed, wavelength, and frequency of the light in water.

  • Frequency f remains unchanged upon refraction, so f_water = f_air = 5 \times 10^{14} Hz.
  • Calculate speed in water v = c / n = (3 \times 10^8 m/s) / (4/3) = 2.25 \times 10^8 m/s.
  • Calculate wavelength in water \lambda_water = v / f = (2.25 \times 10^8 m/s) / (5 \times 10^{14} Hz) = 4.5 \times 10^{-7} m.

Answer: Frequency = 5 \times 10^{14} Hz, Speed = 2.25 \times 10^8 m/s, Wavelength = 4500 Å

Assumptions and limitations

  • Huygens' original construction does not explain the absence of backward wavelets; this requires Fresnel's obliquity factor or Kirchhoff's rigorous scalar diffraction theory.
  • It assumes secondary wavelets have uniform amplitude in all forward directions, which is modified in rigorous diffraction treatments.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Frequency of light changes when it enters a denser medium.

Correct: Frequency remains constant; only speed and wavelength change.

Frequency is determined by the source and does not change across material boundaries.

Common error: Huygens' wavelets propagate backward with equal intensity.

Correct: Backward propagation has zero intensity due to phase cancellation and obliquity factors.

The interference of infinite secondary wavelets causes destructive interference in the reverse direction.

Quick revision

  • Wavefronts are surfaces of constant phase, always perpendicular to light rays.
  • Every point on a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets (Huygens' principle).
  • Snell's law is derived as sin(i)/sin(r) = v_1/v_2 = n_2/n_1.
  • Frequency remains invariant across media, while wavelength and speed scale inversely with refractive index.

Must remember

  • A wavefront is the locus of all adjacent points in a medium vibrating in the same phase.
  • The direction of propagation of light (ray) is always perpendicular to the wavefront at any point.
  • Every point on a given wavefront acts as a secondary source of light, emitting secondary wavelets in all directions.
  • The frequency of light remains completely invariant when it crosses an optical boundary between two different media.
  • The back-propagation of secondary wavelets is non-existent or has zero amplitude due to the obliquity factor.

Glossary

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the angle between a wavefront and a light ray in an isotropic medium?

Explanation: The direction of propagation of light (ray) is always perpendicular to the wavefront at any point, making the angle $90^\circ$.

2. Which of the following parameters of a light wave remains unchanged when it travels from a rarer medium to a denser medium?

Explanation: Frequency is determined entirely by the source and remains completely invariant when light crosses material boundaries.

3. What type of wavefront is produced by a point source of light at a finite distance?

Explanation: A point source emits light uniformly in all directions, forming a spherical wavefront.

4. What type of wavefront is formed by a linear source of light, such as a slit?

Explanation: A linear source of light produces cylindrical wavefronts where the locus of points in the same phase forms a cylinder.

5. Why do secondary wavelets not propagate backward in Huygens' construction?

Explanation: The obliquity factor introduces a directional dependence (proportional to $1 + \cos\$\theta$$) which makes the backward amplitude zero.

6. If the refractive index of a medium is $1.5$, what is the velocity of light in that medium? (Take $c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$)

Explanation: Using $v = c/n$, we get $v = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{1.5} = 2.0 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$.

7. How does the wavelength of monochromatic light of vacuum wavelength $\lambda_0$ change inside a medium of refractive index $n$?

Explanation: Since $v = f\lambda$ and frequency $f$ is constant while speed becomes $v = c/n$, the wavelength in the medium becomes $\lambda_m = \frac{\lambda_0}{n}$.

8. According to Huygens' wave theory, the law of reflection ($i = r$) is derived by proving that:

Explanation: Equality of distances traveled by the wave along the reflecting boundary establishes that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.

9. Snell's law of refraction derived via wave theory yields the ratio $\frac{\sin i}{\sin r}$ equal to:

Explanation: Wave theory proves that $\frac{\sin i}{\sin r} = \frac{n_2}{n_1} = \frac{v_1}{v_2}$.

10. What is the phase difference corresponding to a path difference of $\lambda$?

Explanation: Using the phase difference relation $\Delta \$\phi$ = \frac{2\$\pi$}{\lambda} \Delta x$, substituting $\Delta x = \lambda$ gives $\Delta \$\phi$ = 2\$\pi$$ radians.

11. Which PGT extension concept modifies elementary Huygens' principle by incorporating rigorous amplitude and phase factors?

Explanation: Kirchhoff's integral theorem provides a rigorous mathematical foundation to Huygens' construction by determining exact amplitudes and phases of secondary wavelets.

12. In zone plate analysis (PGT extension), the radii of successive half-period zones are proportional to:

Explanation: The radii of half-period zones in a zone plate are proportional to the square root of natural numbers, i.e., $r_n \propto \sqrt{n}$.

13. What is the time period $T$ of a continuous periodic wave of frequency $f$?

Explanation: The time period of a wave is inversely proportional to its frequency, expressed as $T = \frac{1}{f}$.

14. What physical quantity does the wave function $\psi$ represent in the 3D wave equation $\nabla^2 \psi = \frac{1}{v^2} \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial t^2}$?

Explanation: In classical wave mechanics, $\psi$ represents the wave disturbance or displacement field propagating through the medium.

15. When a plane wavefront passes from an optically rarer medium into an optically denser medium, what happens to its width or geometrical spread?

Explanation: Since speed decreases ($v_2 < v_1$), the secondary wavelets travel a shorter distance in a given time interval, compressing the wavefront spacing.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. According to Huygens' principle, the forward envelope of secondary wavelets represents:

Explanation: The envelope (tangent surface) of secondary wavelets in the forward direction after a time interval $t$ defines the new position of the wavefront.

2. The refractive index of water with respect to air is $4/3$. If the speed of light in vacuum is $3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$, what is the speed of light in water?

Explanation: Using $v = c/n$, $v = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{4/3} = \frac{9}{4} \times 10^8 = 2.25 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$.

3. Which of the following wavefronts is obtained when light emanates from a distant star?

Explanation: Light from a source at infinity (such as a distant star) has negligible curvature over finite laboratory scales, approximating a plane wavefront.

4. In wave refraction, if the angle of incidence is $i$ and angle of refraction is $r$, the relative refractive index $n_{21}$ is given by:

Explanation: Snell's law states that $\frac{\sin i}{\sin r} = n_{21}$, where $n_{21} = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$.

5. What is the path difference corresponding to a phase difference of $\$\pi$$ radians?

Explanation: Using $\Delta \$\phi$ = \frac{2\$\pi$}{\lambda} \Delta x$, substituting $\Delta \$\phi$ = \$\pi$$ gives $\$\pi$ = \frac{2\$\pi$}{\lambda} \Delta x \implies \Delta x = \frac{\lambda}{2}$.

6. Fresnel's modification of Huygens' principle primarily resolved which limitation of the original theory?

Explanation: Fresnel introduced the obliquity factor to successfully account for why secondary wavelets do not propagate backward and to calculate precise intensities.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What is the locus of all points in a medium vibrating in the same phase called?

Explanation: By definition, a wavefront is the locus of all adjacent points in a medium vibrating in the same phase.

2. If a light wave travels a distance $s$ in time $t$ through a homogeneous medium with wave speed $v$, the equation relating them is:

Explanation: The distance traveled by a secondary wavelet is given by the standard kinematic formula $s = vt$.

3. What is the frequency of a light wave of wavelength $500 \text{ nm}$ in water ($n = 4/3$), given $c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$?

Explanation: Vacuum frequency $f_0 = \frac{c}{\lambda_0} = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{500 \times 10^{-9}} = 6.0 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz}$. Since frequency remains invariant, the frequency in water is also $6.0 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz}$.

4. Which of the following statements about secondary wavelets is incorrect?

Explanation: Secondary wavelets do not propagate backward with maximum intensity; rather, backward propagation has zero intensity due to the obliquity factor.

5. If the refractive indices of two media are $n_1 = 1.0$ and $n_2 = 1.5$, what is the ratio of the speed of light in the first medium to that in the second medium ($v_1 / v_2$)?

Explanation: Since $v_1 = c/n_1$ and $v_2 = c/n_2$, the ratio $\frac{v_1}{v_2} = \frac{n_2}{n_1} = \frac{1.5}{1.0} = 1.5$.

6. The phase difference $\Delta \$\phi$$ for a path difference $\Delta x$ in a wave of wavelength $\lambda$ is expressed as:

Explanation: The standard relation connecting phase difference and path difference is $\Delta \$\phi$ = \frac{2\$\pi$}{\lambda} \Delta x$.

7. Which differential equation correctly describes 3D wave propagation in a linear homogeneous medium?

Explanation: The standard 3D wave equation is $\nabla^2 \psi = \frac{1}{v^2} \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial t^2}$.

8. In wave theory, when a plane wavefront undergoes refraction, the frequency of the wave:

Explanation: Frequency is a characteristic property of the source and remains completely invariant across any optical boundary.

9. What is the wavelength of light in a medium of refractive index $n = 4/3$ if its vacuum wavelength is $600 \text{ nm}$?

Explanation: Using $\lambda_m = \frac{\lambda_0}{n}$, we get $\lambda_m = \frac{600 \text{ nm}}{4/3} = 450 \text{ nm}$.

10. Which PGT-level mathematical framework provides the rigorous foundation for evaluating diffraction by incorporating secondary wavelets via surface integrals?

Explanation: Kirchhoff's integral theorem rigorously connects wave disturbances on a closed surface to the field at an internal point, giving a mathematical justification to Huygens-Fresnel secondary wavelets.
