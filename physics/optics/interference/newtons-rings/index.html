Newton's Rings by Interference of Light

Newton's rings are an interference pattern produced by the reflection of light between a spherical surface and an adjacent flat glass plate. The resulting concentric bright and dark circular rings provide a direct method for determining the wavelength of light or testing the sphericity of optical surfaces.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Conditions for maxima and minima, radii of dark and bright rings, determination of wavelength of monochromatic light, and effect of introduction of a liquid between the lens and glass plate.

PGT extension

Formation of rings with white light, determination of refractive index of a liquid, measurement of curvature of a plano-convex lens, and phase change of pi on reflection from a denser medium.

Prerequisites

  • Wave nature of light and superposition principle
  • Conditions for constructive and destructive interference
  • Optical path difference and phase change on reflection (Stokes' relation)

Formation of Newton's Rings

  • A plano-convex lens of large radius of curvature R is placed with its convex surface on a plane glass plate, enclosing a air film of varying thickness t.
  • When illuminated normally by an extended monochromatic source, interference occurs between rays reflected from the upper and lower surfaces of the air film.
  • The interference pattern consists of a central dark spot surrounded by alternate bright and dark concentric circular rings.

Nature of the Central Spot

  • At the point of contact, the thickness of the air film t = 0.
  • Ray reflected from the upper surface of the glass plate suffers a phase change of pi (or path difference of lambda/2) due to reflection at a denser medium boundary, while the ray reflected from the lower surface of the lens does not.
  • At t = 0, the total path difference is lambda/2, producing destructive interference, which makes the central spot dark.

Shape of the Fringes

  • The fringes are circular because the locus of points having the same air film thickness t forms a circle concentric with the point of contact.
  • Therefore, Newton's rings are loci of constant thickness, categorized as fringes of equal thickness (Fizeau fringes).

Effect of Medium and White Light

  • When a liquid of refractive index mu is introduced between the lens and the glass plate, the radii of the rings decrease by a factor of 1/sqrt(mu).
  • If white light is used, only a few colored rings are observed near the center because different wavelengths form fringes of different radii, and higher-order rings overlap to produce general white illumination.

Derivations

Radius of Dark and Bright Rings in Air

Conditions: Normal incidence of monochromatic light of wavelength lambda on a plano-convex lens of radius R.

  • Let R be the radius of curvature of the plano-convex lens and t be the thickness of the air film at a distance r from the point of contact.
  • From the geometric property of a circle, the intersecting chord theorem gives: t(2R - t) = r^2.
  • Since R is very large compared to t, 2R - t approx 2R, leading to 2Rt = r^2 or t = r^2 / (2R).
  • The optical path difference between the interfering reflected rays is delta = 2mu t cos(r) + lambda/2. For normal incidence (cos(r) = 1) and air (mu = 1), delta = 2t + lambda/2.
  • For destructive interference (dark rings): 2t = n lambda, which gives 2(r_n^2 / 2R) = n lambda, yielding r_n^2 = n R lambda.

Result: Radius of n-th dark ring r_n = sqrt(n R lambda) and n-th bright ring r_n = sqrt((n - 1/2) R lambda)

Determination of Wavelength using Newton's Rings

Conditions: Using diameters of m-th and n-th dark rings.

  • Let the diameter of the n-th dark ring be D_n and that of the (n+m)-th dark ring be D_(n+m).
  • We have D_n^2 = 4 n R lambda and D_(n+m)^2 = 4 (n + m) R lambda.
  • Subtracting the two equations: D_(n+m)^2 - D_n^2 = 4 m R lambda.
  • Solving for wavelength lambda gives the working formula.

Result: lambda = (D_(n+m)^2 - D_n^2) / (4 m R)

Solved numericals

TGT core

In a Newton's rings experiment, the diameter of the 10th dark ring is found to be 0.4 cm. If the radius of curvature of the plano-convex lens is 2 m, calculate the wavelength of light used.

  • Given: n = 10, D_10 = 0.4 cm = 4 x 10^-3 m, R = 2 m.
  • Formula: D_n^2 = 4 n R lambda => lambda = D_n^2 / (4 n R).
  • Substitute values: lambda = (4 x 10^-3)^2 / (4 x 10 x 2) = (16 x 10^-6) / 80.
  • lambda = 0.2 x 10^-6 m = 2000 Å or 200 nm.

Answer: 2000 Å (or 200 nm)

PGT extension

The diameters of the 10th and 20th dark rings of Newton's rings are 0.70 cm and 1.00 cm respectively. If the radius of curvature of the lens is 2 m, calculate the wavelength of light.

  • Given: n = 10, D_10 = 0.70 cm = 0.007 m; n+m = 20 (so m = 10), D_20 = 1.00 cm = 0.01 m; R = 2 m.
  • Formula: lambda = (D_20^2 - D_10^2) / (4 m R).
  • Calculate squares: D_20^2 = 0.0001 m^2, D_10^2 = 0.000049 m^2.
  • Numerator = 0.0001 - 0.000049 = 0.000051 m^2.
  • Denominator = 4 x 10 x 2 = 80.
  • lambda = 0.000051 / 80 = 6.375 x 10^-7 m = 6375 Å.

Answer: 6375 Å

PGT extension

When a liquid is introduced between the lens and the glass plate in a Newton's rings apparatus, the diameter of the 10th dark ring changes from 1.2 cm to 1.0 cm. Find the refractive index of the liquid.

  • Given: D_10(air) = 1.2 cm, D_10(liquid) = 1.0 cm.
  • Formula relating refractive index: mu = (D_n(air) / D_n(liquid))^2.
  • Substitute values: mu = (1.2 / 1.0)^2 = (1.2)^2 = 1.44.

Answer: 1.44

Assumptions and limitations

  • The air film thickness t is assumed to be very small compared to the radius of curvature R of the lens (t << R).
  • The angle of incidence is assumed to be normal (cos(r) = 1), which limits applicability if large extended sources cause oblique rays.
  • The lower surface of the lens and upper surface of the glass plate must be optically flat and free from dust particles to maintain regular circular symmetry.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The central spot of Newton's rings is bright because zero thickness means zero path difference.

Correct: The central spot is dark.

Reflection at the lower surface of the lens involves a rarer-to-denser boundary causing a phase change of pi (lambda/2 path difference), whereas reflection at the glass plate does not, leading to destructive interference at t = 0.

Common error: Newton's rings are formed by interference of light of equal path lengths.

Correct: They are fringes of equal thickness.

All points forming a specific ring lie at the same thickness t of the air film, hence they are termed fringes of equal thickness.

Common error: The spacing between successive Newton's rings is constant.

Correct: Ring spacing decreases as the order n increases.

Since radius is proportional to sqrt(n), the difference r_(n+1) - r_n varies inversely with sqrt(n), causing rings to crowd together outward.

Quick revision

  • Central spot in Newton's rings is dark due to a pi phase change on reflection.
  • Radius of n-th dark ring is proportional to sqrt(n).
  • Fringes are circular and localized in the air film (fringes of equal thickness).
  • Introducing a liquid of refractive index mu decreases ring diameters by 1/sqrt(mu).
  • White light illumination produces only a few colored rings around a dark center.

Must remember

  • The central spot of Newton's rings formed by reflected light is dark due to a phase change of $\pi$ (path difference of $\lambda/2$) introduced upon reflection at the denser glass plate boundary.
  • Newton's rings are concentric circular fringes of constant thickness, also known as Fizeau fringes.
  • The radius of the $n$-th dark ring is given by $r_n = \sqrt{n R \lambda}$, and the diameter is $D_n = 2\sqrt{n R \lambda}$.
  • The spacing between consecutive Newton's rings decreases as the order $n$ increases, causing the rings to crowd closer together outward.
  • When a liquid of refractive index $\mu$ is introduced into the air film, the diameters of the rings decrease by a factor of $1/\sqrt{\mu}$.
  • When white light is used, only a few colored rings are observed near the center before higher-order rings overlap to produce uniform white illumination.

Glossary

Fizeau FringesFringes of equal thickness formed by the interference of light reflected from the upper and lower surfaces of a film of variable thickness.
Plano-Convex LensA lens with one flat surface and one spherical convex surface, used to create a variable air film with a glass plate.
Stokes' Phase ChangeA phase change of $\pi$ experienced by a light wave when it is reflected from the surface of a denser medium.
Refractive Index ($\mu$)The ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to that in a medium, determining the contraction of ring diameters when introduced between the lens and plate.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the nature of the central spot in Newton's rings formed by reflected monochromatic light?

Explanation: At the point of contact ($t = 0$), the ray reflected from the glass plate suffers a phase change of $\$\pi$$ ($\lambda/2$ path difference) due to reflection from a denser medium, while the ray from the lens does not, producing destructive interference.

2. Why are Newton's rings circular in shape?

Explanation: The locus of points having the same air film thickness $t$ forms a circle concentric with the point of contact, resulting in circular fringes.

3. What type of fringes are represented by Newton's rings?

Explanation: Newton's rings are formed by interference in a film of varying thickness and are classic examples of fringes of equal thickness, also known as Fizeau fringes.

4. What is the formula for the radius of the $n$-th dark ring in Newton's rings?

Explanation: For dark rings in reflected light, destructive interference occurs at $2t = n\lambda$, substituting $t = \frac{r^2}{2R}$ gives $r_n = \sqrt{n R \lambda}$.

5. How does the spacing between consecutive Newton's rings change as the order $n$ increases?

Explanation: Since radius $r_n \propto \sqrt{n}$, the difference $r_{n+1} - r_n$ varies inversely with $\sqrt{n}$, causing the rings to crowd closer together as $n$ increases.

6. What happens to the diameters of Newton's rings when a liquid of refractive index $\mu$ is introduced between the lens and the glass plate?

Explanation: In a liquid medium, the wavelength becomes $\lambda' = \lambda/\mu$, which shrinks the radii and diameters of the rings by a factor of $1/\sqrt{\mu}$.

7. What is observed when white light is used to illuminate the Newton's rings apparatus?

Explanation: Different wavelengths form fringes of different radii, causing them to overlap except near the center, resulting in a few colored rings and then general white illumination.

8. Which formula is used to determine the wavelength of monochromatic light using Newton's rings diameters?

Explanation: Using $D_n^2 = 4n R \lambda$ and $D_{n+m}^2 = 4(n+m)R\lambda$, subtracting them yields $\lambda = \frac{D_{n+m}^2 - D_n^2}{4 m R}$.

9. What is the thickness of the air film $t$ at a distance $r$ from the point of contact, given $R$ is the radius of curvature?

Explanation: From geometrical properties of a spherical surface on a flat plate, the air film thickness is approximated as $t = \frac{r^2}{2R}$ for $r \ll R$.

10. What is the phase change experienced by a light ray reflected from the lower surface of the plano-convex lens (air-glass boundary from rarer to denser medium)?

Explanation: Reflection at a denser medium boundary (air to glass) introduces a phase change of $\$\pi$$ (equivalent to a path difference of $\lambda/2$).

11. If the radius of curvature $R$ of the lens is increased, what happens to the diameter of a given dark ring?

Explanation: Since $D_n = 2\sqrt{n R \lambda}$, the diameter $D_n$ is directly proportional to $\sqrt{R}$, so increasing $R$ increases the ring diameter.

12. What is the condition for the $n$-th bright ring in reflected monochromatic light?

Explanation: Accounting for the $\lambda/2$ phase change, constructive interference for bright rings requires total path difference $2t + \lambda/2 = (2n-1)\frac{\lambda}{2}$, which simplifies to $2t = (n - \frac{1}{2})\lambda$.

13. How can the refractive index $\mu$ of a liquid be calculated using Newton's rings diameters in air and liquid?

Explanation: Since diameters scale by $1/\sqrt{\mu}$, $D_{n(liquid)}^2 = D_{n(air)}^2 / \mu$, rearranging gives $\mu = \frac{D_{n(air)}^2}{D_{n(liquid)}^2}$.

14. Which of the following is assumed when deriving the formula for Newton's rings radius $r_n = \sqrt{n R \lambda}$?

Explanation: The approximation $t = \frac{r^2}{2R}$ and normal incidence assumptions require that $r \ll R$ (the radius of the ring is much smaller than the radius of curvature).

15. Why does the central spot appear dark in reflected light instead of bright, given that thickness $t = 0$ implies zero path difference?

Explanation: The ray reflecting from the lower glass plate undergoes a $\$\pi$$ phase change while the ray from the lens does not, introducing an effective $\lambda/2$ path difference that causes destructive interference at $t = 0$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. In a Newton's rings experiment, the diameter of the $4^{\text{th}}$ dark ring is $4\text{ mm}$ and that of the $12^{\text{th}}$ dark ring is $ef\text{ mm}$ (assume proportionality). If the $12^{\text{th}}$ ring diameter is $6.93\text{ mm}$ ($~\sqrt{12} / \sqrt{4} = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$), what is the order of interference pattern?

Explanation: Newton's rings are classical Fizeau fringes formed due to interference in a film of constant thickness (loci of constant $t$).

2. If the diameter of the $n$-th dark ring in Newton's rings changes from $3\text{ mm}$ in air to $2.4\text{ mm}$ when a liquid is introduced, what is the refractive index $\mu$ of the liquid?

Explanation: Using $\mu = \frac{D_{n(air)}^2}{D_{n(liquid)}^2} = \frac{3^2}{2.4^2} = \frac{9}{5.76} = 1.5625 \approx 1.56$.

3. In the determination of wavelength using Newton's rings, why are the diameters of higher-order rings ($D_{n+m}^2 - D_n^2$) used instead of a single ring measurement?

Explanation: Using squared diameter differences $D_{n+m}^2 - D_n^2 = 4 m R \lambda$ eliminates the error in locating the true center (point of contact $t=0$).

4. A plano-convex lens of radius of curvature $R$ is placed on a glass plate. If light of wavelength $\lambda$ falls normally, what is the air film thickness $t$ corresponding to the $10^{\text{th}}$ dark ring?

Explanation: For the $n$-th dark ring, the condition in reflected light is $2t = n\lambda$, so $t = \frac{n\lambda}{2}$. For $n = 10$, $t = \frac{10\lambda}{2} = 5\lambda$. Wait, $2t = n\lambda \implies t = n\lambda/2 = 5\lambda$. Let's check options: $5\lambda$ is option 0! Correct condition: $2t = n\lambda \implies t = n\lambda / 2 = 10\lambda / 2 = 5\lambda$.

5. What happens to the fringe width (spacing between successive rings) as we move from the center outward in Newton's rings?

Explanation: Since $r_n = \sqrt{n R \lambda}$, the spacing $r_{n+1} - r_n$ is proportional to $(\sqrt{n+1} - \sqrt{n})$, which decreases as $n$ increases, making rings crowd together outward.

6. If white light is used in Newton's rings experiment, the central spot is:

Explanation: At $t = 0$, destructive interference occurs for all wavelengths due to the $\$\pi$$ phase change, keeping the central spot dark even under white light illumination.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. The radius of the $5^{\text{th}}$ dark ring in Newton's rings is $2\text{ mm}$. What is the radius of the $20^{\text{th}}$ dark ring?

Explanation: Since $r_n \propto \sqrt{n}$, we have $\frac{r_{20}}{r_5} = \sqrt{\frac{20}{5}} = \sqrt{4} = 2$. Thus, $r_{20} = 2 \times r_5 = 2 \times 2\text{ mm} = 4\text{ mm}$.

2. In a Newton's rings arrangement, if the radius of curvature $R$ of the plano-convex lens is $1\text{ m}$ and wavelength $\lambda = 5890\text{ \AA}$, what is the radius of the $10^{\text{th}}$ dark ring?

Explanation: $r_{10} = \sqrt{n R \lambda} = \sqrt{10 \times 1\text{ m} \times 5.89 \times 10^{-7}\text{ m}} = \sqrt{5.89 \times 10^{-6}} = 2.427 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m} \approx 2.43\text{ mm}$.

Let's select $1.0\text{ m}$ assuming standard textbook values for that ring diameter.

4. What is the primary reason why Newton's rings are observed as concentric circles rather than straight lines?

Explanation: Points of equal thickness $t$ form concentric circles around the central point of contact between the spherical lens and flat glass plate.

5. If a few drops of water ($mu = 4/3$) are introduced in the air film of a Newton's rings setup, the diameter of a particular ring becomes $3\text{ mm}$. What was its diameter in air?

Explanation: $D_{liquid} = \frac{D_{air}}{\sqrt{\mu}} \implies D_{air} = D_{liquid} \times \sqrt{\mu} = 3 \times \sqrt{4/3} = 3 \times \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} = 2\sqrt{3} \approx 3.46\text{ mm}$.

6. In the formation of Newton's rings, the path difference between interfering reflected rays from the air film is given by:

Explanation: The optical path difference in the film is $2\mu t \cos r$, and including Stokes' phase change of $\$\pi$$ (equivalent to $\lambda/2$), the total effective path difference is $2t \pm \frac{\lambda}{2}$ for normal incidence.

7. Why are Newton's rings not observed with an extended source of white light beyond a few rings?

Explanation: Different constituent wavelengths form rings of varying diameters; beyond the first few orders, these overlap completely to produce general white illumination.

8. What happens to the radius of the $n$-th dark ring if the wavelength of incident light is increased from $\lambda_1$ to $\lambda_2$?

Explanation: Since $r_n = \sqrt{n R \lambda}$, the radius is directly proportional to $\sqrt{\lambda}$, so increasing the wavelength increases the ring radius.

9. Which order of interference corresponds to the center of the Newton's rings pattern in reflected light?

Explanation: At the center, $t = 0$ and $n = 0$, leading to complete destructive interference due to the $\$\pi$$ phase change.

10. If the plano-convex lens is lifted vertically upwards at a constant speed, how do the Newton's rings appear to move?

Explanation: As the air film thickness changes and air is squeezed or adjusted, or as the lens moves, the path differences shift, causing new rings to form at the center and expand outwards.
