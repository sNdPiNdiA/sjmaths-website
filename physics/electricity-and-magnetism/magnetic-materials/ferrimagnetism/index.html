Ferrimagnetism

Ferrimagnetism is a form of magnetism observed in materials whose neighboring magnetic moments point in opposite directions and are of unequal magnitude, resulting in a net spontaneous magnetization. These materials, typically ferrites, combine high electrical resistivity with permanent magnetic ordering.

Microscopic Origin of Ferrimagnetism

  • Ferrimagnetic materials contain magnetic ions distributed over at least two different crystallographic sublattices, designated as sublattice A and sublattice B.
  • The exchange interaction between ions on sublattice A and sublattice B is negative (antiferromagnetic coupling), leading to antiparallel alignment of their magnetic moments.
  • Because the magnetic moments on the two sublattices are unequal in magnitude or number per unit volume, complete cancellation does not occur, leaving a net spontaneous magnetization.

Magnetic Structure of Ferrites

  • Common ferrimagnetic oxides are spinels with the general chemical formula MO.Fe2O3, where M is a divalent metal ion such as Fe2+, Co2+, Ni2+, or Mn2+.
  • In the spinel lattice, metal ions occupy tetrahedral (A) and octahedral (B) interstitial sites formed by oxygen anions.
  • The super-exchange interaction occurs via intermediate oxygen ions, producing strong antiparallel coupling between the A and B sites.

Temperature Dependence and Néel Temperature

  • Like ferromagnets, ferrimagnets possess a critical temperature called the Néel temperature (TN) or ferrimagnetic Curie temperature, above which thermal agitation destroys the ordered state, rendering the material paramagnetic.
  • Below TN, thermal fluctuations reduce the net spontaneous magnetization until it vanishes completely at $T = T_{N}$.
  • In certain ferrimagnets, the temperature dependence of the two sublattice magnetizations can lead to a 'compensation point' where the net magnetization momentarily drops to zero.

Magnetic Susceptibility Above the Néel Temperature

  • In the paramagnetic region (T > TN), the reciprocal magnetic susceptibility does not follow a simple Curie-Weiss law.
  • The inverse susceptibility vs. temperature curve exhibits a hyperbolic asymptote due to the negative inter-sublattice molecular field coefficients.
  • The high-temperature behavior is described by 1/$\chi = (T/C) - [\theta/ (T - \theta')] or similar modified hyperbolic relations derived from molecular field theory$.

Derivations

Molecular Field Theory of Ferrimagnetism (Two-Sublattice Model)

Conditions: Applied to a ferrimagnetic crystal divided into two interpenetrating sublattices A and B with respective magnetizations MA and MB.

  • Write the effective magnetic fields acting on sublattices A and B considering intra-sublattice and inter-sublattice molecular field coefficients: HA = H + λAA MA + λAB MB and HB = H + λBA MA + λBB MB, where λAB = λBA.
  • Apply the Curie law for each sublattice in the paramagnetic region (T > TN): MA = (CA/T) HA and MB = (CB/T) HB, where CA and CB are the Curie constants of sublattices A and B.
  • Express the equations in matrix form for MA and MB in terms of external magnetic field H, and solve for the total susceptibility $\chi = (M_{A} + M_{B})/H$.
  • Invert the resulting expression to obtain the reciprocal susceptibility equation showing hyperbolic temperature dependence.

Result: \frac{1}{\chi} = \frac{T}{C} + \frac{1}{\chi_0} - \frac{\sigma}{T - \theta}

Net Magnetic Moment per Formula Unit in Spinels

Conditions: Assuming collinear alignment of spins on tetrahedral (A) and octahedral (B) sites in a 2-3 spinel ferrite.

  • Identify the distribution of divalent and trivalent ions across A and B sites.
  • Sum the magnetic moments (in Bohr magnetons, μB) for all ions residing on the B sites: μB = ∑ μB,i.
  • Sum the magnetic moments for all ions residing on the A sites: μA = ∑ μA,j.
  • Subtract the tetrahedral moment sum from the octahedral moment sum due to antiparallel alignment: μnet = |μB - μA|.

Result: \m$u_{net} = |\mu_B - \mu_A|$

Graphs and experiments

Inverse Magnetic Susceptibility vs Temperature

Draw: A plot of 1/χ on the vertical axis against temperature T on the horizontal axis, showing a linear asymptotic behavior at high temperatures that curves downward near TN, intersecting the temperature axis below TN.

Interpret: Demonstrates the failure of the simple Curie-Weiss law and confirms negative inter-sublattice exchange interactions characteristic of ferrimagnetism.

Exam use: Distinguishing ferrimagnetism from ferromagnetism and antiferromagnetism in qualitative graphical multiple-choice questions.

Sublattice and Net Magnetization vs Temperature

Draw: Curves for MA(T) and MB(T) decreasing with temperature and intersecting or dropping to zero at TN, alongside the resultant net magnetization curve M(T) = |MB - MA| which may show a compensation point.

Interpret: Illustrates how differing thermal coefficients of sublattice magnetizations can produce anomalous net magnetization curves, including compensation temperatures.

Exam use: Explaining anomalous magnetic behavior in mixed ferrites (e.g., lithium ferrites).

Hysteresis Loop of a Ferrimagnetic Material

Draw: A B-H or M-H loop characterized by high saturation magnetization (though lower than pure iron), moderate coercivity, and very high electrical resistivity.

Interpret: Shows ferromagnetic-like saturation and remanence combined with low eddy current losses due to high resistance.

Exam use: Core selection criteria for high-frequency transformer and memory device applications.

Solved numericals

TGT core

A ferrimagnetic spinel has two sublattices A and B. The magnetic moment of ions on sublattice A is 3 μB per formula unit pointing in one direction, and on sublattice B is 10 μB per formula unit pointing in the opposite direction. Calculate the net magnetic moment per formula unit.

  • Identify the magnetic moments: μA = 3 μB and μB = 10 μB.
  • Apply the formula for net spontaneous magnetic moment: μnet = |μB - μA|.
  • Substitute the values: μnet = |10 μB - 3 μB| = 7 μB.

Answer: 7 \mu_B

PGT extension

In a symmetric two-sublattice ferrimagnet, the Curie constants for sublattices A and B are equal (CA = CB = C/2). If the molecular field coefficient between sublattices is λAB = -500 and the intra-sublattice coefficients are zero, estimate the Néel temperature TN.

  • Recall the relation for Néel temperature in a two-sublattice model: $T_N = \frac{C}{2} |\lambda_{AB}|$, where $C = C_A + C_B$.
  • Since $C_A = C_B = C/2$, the total Curie constant is $C = C_A + C_B$.
  • Substitute the parameter values into the expression to find TN.

Answer: Proportional to \frac{C}{2} |\lambda_{AB}| K

PGT extension

Consider a magnetite (Fe3O4) crystal treated as a ferrimagnet where Fe3+ ions on A and B sites cancel out their moments, leaving the net moment entirely due to Fe2+ ions on the B sites. If Fe2+ has 4 unpaired electrons yielding a magnetic moment of 4 μB, and there is 1 Fe2+ ion per formula unit on B sites, what is the net moment per formula unit?

  • Identify the magnetic contribution from Fe3+ ions: equal numbers on A and B sites pointing oppositely, resulting in zero net moment contribution (μFe3+ = 0 net).
  • Identify the contribution from Fe2+ ions: located on B sites with moment $\mu = 4 \mu_{B}$.
  • Calculate total net moment: μnet = 1 × 4 μB = 4 μB.

Answer: 4 \mu_B per formula unit

Assumptions and limitations

  • The molecular field approximation assumes that thermal fluctuations average out spatial variations in the local exchange field, which breaks down near absolute zero or in disordered systems.
  • The simple two-sublattice model assumes collinear spin alignments; however, complex ferrimagnets often exhibit canted spin structures (e.g., Yafet-Kittel triangular arrangements) at low temperatures or high fields.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Ferrimagnetism is identical to antiferromagnetism because adjacent spins are antiparallel.

Correct: Ferrimagnetic sublattices have unequal magnetic moments, resulting in a net nonzero spontaneous magnetization, whereas antiferromagnetic sublattices have equal and opposite moments resulting in zero net magnetization.

The inequality in moment magnitudes or numbers per unit volume breaks complete cancellation.

Common error: Ferrimagnetic materials have zero electrical resistance like metals.

Correct: Most practical ferrimagnets (ferrites) are ceramic insulators or semiconductors with very high electrical resistivity.

Ionic bonding and localized d-electrons prevent free electron conduction, suppressing eddy current losses.

Common error: Above the Néel temperature, a ferrimagnet behaves according to the simple Curie law $\chi = C/T$.

Correct: Above TN, the inverse susceptibility follows a hyperbolic temperature dependence due to inter-sublattice interactions.

The coupling between different sublattices introduces non-linear temperature terms in the paramagnetic susceptibility.

Quick revision

  • Ferrimagnetism features antiparallel unequal magnetic moments giving a net spontaneous magnetization.
  • Common examples include spinels and magnetites (MO·Fe2O3).
  • The transition temperature above which ferrimagnetism disappears is the Néel temperature (TN).
  • Paramagnetic susceptibility above TN deviates from the simple Curie-Weiss law, showing hyperbolic behavior.
  • High electrical resistivity makes ferrites indispensable for high-frequency transformer cores and microwave devices.

Must remember

  • Ferrimagnetic materials contain magnetic ions distributed over at least two different crystallographic sublattices (A and B) with antiparallel coupling.
  • Net spontaneous magnetization arises because the magnetic moments on the two sublattices are unequal in magnitude or number per unit volume ($M = |M_B - M_A|$).
  • Common ferrimagnetic oxides are spinels with the general chemical formula $\text{MO}\cdot\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$, where $\text{M}$ is a divalent metal ion.
  • The critical temperature above which a ferrimagnet becomes paramagnetic is called the Néel temperature ($T_N$).
  • In the paramagnetic region ($T > T_N$), the inverse magnetic susceptibility $1/\chi$ exhibits a hyperbolic asymptote due to negative inter-sublattice molecular field coefficients.
  • At the compensation temperature ($T_{comp}$), the sublattice magnetizations balance out ($M_A = M_B$), causing the net spontaneous magnetization to momentarily drop to zero.
  • Unlike pure metals, most practical ferrimagnets (ferrites) are ceramic insulators or semiconductors possessing very high electrical resistivity.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the primary microscopic origin of net spontaneous magnetization in ferrimagnetic materials?

Explanation: Ferrimagnets feature antiparallel coupling between sublattices A and B, but because their magnetic moments are unequal in magnitude or number per unit volume ($M = |M_B - M_A|$), complete cancellation does not occur.

2. What is the general chemical formula for common ferrimagnetic spinel oxides?

Explanation: Common ferrimagnetic oxides are spinels with the general chemical formula $\text{MO}\cdot\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$, where $\text{M}$ is a divalent metal ion like $\text{Fe}^{2+}$, $\text{Co}^{2+}$, $\text{Ni}^{2+}$, or $\text{Mn}^{2+}$.

3. In the spinel crystal lattice, which interstitial sites are occupied by metal ions?

Explanation: In the spinel lattice, metal ions occupy tetrahedral (A) and octahedral (B) interstitial sites formed by oxygen anions.

4. What mediates the strong antiparallel coupling between the A and B sites in ferrites?

Explanation: The super-exchange interaction occurs via intermediate oxygen anions, producing strong antiparallel coupling between the magnetic moments on A and B sites.

5. What is the critical temperature called above which a ferrimagnetic material becomes paramagnetic?

Explanation: Like ferromagnets, ferrimagnets possess a critical temperature called the Néel temperature ($T_N$) or ferrimagnetic Curie temperature, above which thermal agitation destroys the ordered state.

6. How does the reciprocal magnetic susceptibility behave in the paramagnetic region ($T > T_N$) for ferrimagnetic materials?

Explanation: In the paramagnetic region ($T > T_N$), the reciprocal magnetic susceptibility does not follow a simple Curie-Weiss law but exhibits a hyperbolic asymptote due to negative inter-sublattice molecular field coefficients.

7. What physical phenomenon occurs at the compensation temperature ($T_{comp}$) in certain ferrimagnets?

Explanation: In certain ferrimagnets, the temperature dependence of the two sublattice magnetizations can lead to a compensation point ($T_{comp}$) where $M_A(T_{comp}) = M_B(T_{comp})$, causing the net spontaneous magnetization to momentarily drop to zero.

8. Why do practical ferrimagnets (ferrites) exhibit very high electrical resistivity?

Explanation: Ionic bonding and localized d-electrons prevent free electron conduction in ferrites, suppressing eddy current losses and making them ceramic insulators or semiconductors.

9. What is the formula representing net spontaneous magnetization at zero external field for $T < T_N$?

Explanation: The net spontaneous magnetization is given by $M = |M_B - M_A|$ under zero external field for temperatures below $T_N$.

10. In the mean field approximation, what is the expression for the molecular field $H_{mA}$ acting on sublattice A?

Explanation: The molecular field on sublattice A is expressed as $H_{mA} = \lambda_{AA} M_A + \lambda_{AB} M_B$, where $\lambda_{AA}$ and $\lambda_{AB}$ are molecular field coefficients.

11. Which of the following statements correctly distinguishes ferrimagnetism from antiferromagnetism?

Explanation: Ferrimagnetic sublattices have unequal magnetic moments, resulting in a net nonzero spontaneous magnetization, whereas antiferromagnetic sublattices have equal and opposite moments resulting in zero net magnetization.

12. What does the parameter $n$ represent in the formula $\mu_{mol} = n \mu_B$ for magnetic moment per molecule at $T = 0\text{ K}$?

Explanation: In the formula $\mu_{mol} = n \mu_B$, $n$ represents the effective number of Bohr magnetons per molecule at the zero temperature limit ($T = 0\text{ K}$).

13. What is the total Curie constant $C$ for the high-temperature paramagnetic range composed of two sublattices A and B?

Explanation: The total Curie constant for the high-temperature paramagnetic range is given by the sum of the sublattice constants: $C = C_A + C_B$.

14. Which incorrect misconception is frequently held regarding ferrimagnetism above the Néel temperature?

Explanation: A common misconception is that above $T_N$ a ferrimagnet behaves according to the simple Curie law $\chi = C/T$; however, due to inter-sublattice interactions, it follows a hyperbolic temperature dependence.

15. In the two-sublattice approximation, what parameters determine the Néel temperature $T_N$?

Explanation: The Néel temperature relation is given by $T_N = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{C_A C_B} |\lambda_{AB}|$, depending on sublattice Curie constants and the inter-sublattice parameter.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. In a ferrimagnetic spinel crystal, the magnetic moments of the A and B sublattices are coupled in which manner and result in what net magnetization?

Explanation: Ferrimagnetic sublattices A and B are coupled antiparallelly, but because their magnetic moments are unequal, complete cancellation does not occur, leaving a net spontaneous magnetization.

2. Above the Néel temperature $T_N$, the inverse magnetic susceptibility $1/\chi$ of a ferrimagnetic material varies with temperature according to which behavior?

Explanation: Above $T_N$, the reciprocal magnetic susceptibility deviates from simple Curie-Weiss law and exhibits a hyperbolic asymptote due to inter-sublattice interactions.

3. What is the physical significance of the compensation temperature ($T_{comp}$) found in certain ferrimagnetic oxides?

Explanation: At $T_{comp}$, $M_A(T_{comp}) = M_B(T_{comp})$, so the net spontaneous magnetization momentarily drops to zero even though the sublattices remain magnetically ordered.

4. Why do ferrimagnetic materials such as spinels (e.g., $\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4$) exhibit high electrical resistivity compared to ferromagnetic metals?

Explanation: Ferrites are ceramic oxides where ionic bonding and localized d-electrons suppress free electron conduction, resulting in high resistivity.

5. In the mean field description of ferrimagnetism, what does the parameter $\lambda_{AB}$ represent?

Explanation: $\lambda_{AB}$ represents the inter-sublattice molecular field parameter responsible for the negative (antiferromagnetic) coupling between sublattice A and sublattice B.

6. Which of the following equations correctly describes the sublattice Curie constant $C_i$ in terms of ion density $N_i$ and spin quantum number $S$?

Explanation: The independent sublattice Curie constant is given by $C_i = \frac{N_i \mu_0 g^2 \mu_B^2 S(S+1)}{3k_B}$.
