Electricity and Magnetism

Diamagnetism

Diamagnetism is a fundamental magnetic property exhibited by all materials in which induced magnetic moments oppose the applied magnetic field. It originates from the modification of orbital motion of atomic electrons under an external magnetic field according to Lenz's law.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of diamagnetic substances, negative and small magnetic susceptibility, relative permeability slightly less than one, independence of temperature (Curie-Weiss law non-applicability), and qualitative behavior in non-uniform magnetic fields (repulsion from stronger to weaker regions).

PGT extension

Langevin's classical theory of diamagnetism, derivation of diamagnetic susceptibility using Larmor precession frequency, temperature independence proof, and quantitative calculations involving atomic number and mean square radius of electron orbits.

Prerequisites

  • Atomic structure and electron orbital motion
  • Electromagnetic induction and Lenz's law
  • Magnetic field intensity, magnetization, and magnetic susceptibility definitions

Origin of Diamagnetism

  • Every material possesses diamagnetism because all atoms contain orbiting electrons that constitute microscopic current loops.
  • When an external magnetic field is applied, it induces an electromotive force via Faraday's law of induction.
  • According to Lenz's law, the induced current creates a magnetic moment that opposes the applied external magnetic field.

Magnetic Susceptibility and Permeability

  • Diamagnetic materials have a small and negative magnetic susceptibility (χ), typically on the order of 10-5 to 10-9.
  • The relative magnetic permeability (μr = 1 + χ) is slightly less than unity.
  • The magnetic permeability (μ) of a diamagnetic substance is less than the permeability of free space (μ0).

Temperature Independence

  • Unlike paramagnetism and ferromagnetism, diamagnetic susceptibility is independent of absolute temperature (T).
  • Thermal agitation does not disrupt the induced atomic currents because the induced magnetic moment is a direct dynamical response to the external field rather than an alignment of permanent dipoles.
  • Exceptions occur only in certain semimetals or superconductors where structural phase changes alter carrier density with temperature.

Behavior in Magnetic Fields

  • When placed in a non-uniform magnetic field, diamagnetic materials move from regions of stronger magnetic field to regions of weaker magnetic field.
  • A freely suspended diamagnetic rod aligns itself perpendicular to the direction of a uniform magnetic field.
  • Superconductors exhibit perfect diamagnetism (Meissner effect), expelling all magnetic flux completely with $\chi = -1$.

Derivations

Langevin Theory of Diamagnetism

Conditions: Spherical charge distribution of Z electrons per atom, weak external magnetic field B

  • An electron of charge -e and mass m moving in a circular orbit of radius r experiences a change in angular frequency Δω equal to the Larmor frequency given by Δ$\omega = \frac{eB}{2m}$.
  • This change in frequency induces an additional circular current $i = -\frac{e Deltaomega}{2pi} = -\frac{e^2 B}{4pi m}$.
  • The induced magnetic moment μind for a single electron orbit of area $A = \pi\langle r^{2} \rangle$ is given by μind = i $A = -\frac{e^2 B}{4pi m} \pi \langle r^{2} \rangle = -\frac{e^2 B}{4m} \langle r^{2} \rangle$, where <r2> is the mean square projection of the orbit perpendicular to the field.
  • Summing over Z electrons per atom and multiplying by the number density of atoms N gives the magnetization $M = N \sum \mu_{ind} = -\frac{Ne^2 Z \langle r^{2} \rangle B}{6m}$.
  • Magnetic susceptibility $\chi = \frac{M}{H} = \mu_0 \frac{M}{B} (since B \approx \mu_0 H$ for weak fields) yields the final diamagnetic susceptibility expression.

Result: $\chi = -\frac{\mu_0 Ne^2 Z \langle r^{2} \rangle}{6m}$

Behavior of Diamagnetic Rod in a Non-Uniform Field

Conditions: Rod of volume V and susceptibility χ suspended in magnetic field gradient

  • The potential energy U of a magnetic dipole of moment M in a magnetic field B is $U = -M \cdot B$.
  • The induced magnetic moment per unit volume is $I = \chi H \approx \frac{\chi B}{\mu_0}$.
  • The total induced magnetic moment is $M = V I = \frac{V \chi B}{\mu_0}$.
  • Substituting M into the potential energy equation yields $U = -\frac{V \chi B^2}{\mu_0}$.
  • The force F acting on the rod along the field gradient is $F = -\frac{dU}{ds} = \frac{V \chi}{\mu_0} \frac{d}{ds}(B^2)$. Since χ is negative, the force acts in the direction of decreasing magnetic field strength.

Result: $F = \frac{V \chi}{\mu_0} B \frac{dB}{ds} (pointing towards weaker field regions)$

Solved numericals

TGT core

A diamagnetic substance has a magnetic susceptibility of -1.5 × 10-5. Calculate its relative permeability.

  • Recall the relation between relative permeability and magnetic susceptibility: μr = 1 + χ.
  • Substitute the given value of susceptibility: μr = 1 + (-1.5 × 10-5).
  • Calculate the final numerical value: μr = 1 - 0.000015 = 0.999985.

Answer: 0.999985

PGT extension

Estimate the atomic diamagnetic susceptibility of a substance given that the atomic number density $N = 5 × 10^{28} m^{-3}$, Z = 10, and mean square radius <r2$ \rangle = 1 × 10^{-20} m^{2}$. Take μ0 = 4π × 10-7 T m A-1, $e = 1.6 × 10^{-19} C$, $m = 9.1 × 10^{-31} kg$.

  • Use Langevin formula: $\chi = -\frac{\mu_0 Ne^2 Z \langle r^{2} \rangle}{6m}$.
  • Substitute the numerical values: $\chi = -\frac{(4pi \times 10^{-7}) \times (5 \times 10^{28}) \times (1.6 \times 10^{-19})^2 \times 10 \times (1 \times 10^{-20})}{6 \times (9.1 \times 10^{-31})}$.
  • Calculate numerator: 4π × 10-7 × 5 × 1028 × 2.56 × 10-38 × 10 × 10-20 = 4π × 1.28 × 10-8 ≈ 1.609 × 10-7.
  • Calculate denominator: 6 × 9.1 × 10-31 = 5.46 × 10-30.
  • Divide and evaluate: $\chi = -\frac{1.609 \times 10^{-7}}{5.46 \times 10^{-30}} \times 10^{-20} \text{ (re-evaluating powers carefully)} -> Let us compute directly: \chi = - \frac{4pi \times 10^{-7} \times 5 \times 10^{28} \times 2.56 \times 10^{-38} \times 10 \times 10^{-20}}{5.46 \times 10^{-30}} = -1.96 \times 10^{-6}$.

Answer: -1.96 × 10-6

PGT extension

A small bismuth sphere of volume 10-6 m-3 and magnetic susceptibility -1.6 × 10-5 is placed in a non-uniform magnetic field where the product B(dB/dx) is 102 T2 m-1. Calculate the magnetic force acting on the sphere. (μ0 = 4π × 10-7 T m A-1)

  • Use the force formula for a magnetic sample in a field gradient: $F = \frac{V \chi}{\mu_0} B \frac{dB}{dx}$.
  • Substitute $V = 10^{-6} m^{-3}$, $\chi = -1.6 × 10^{-5}$, B(dB/dx) = 102 T2 m-1, and μ0 = 4π × 10-7.
  • $F = \frac{10^{-6} \times (-1.6 \times 10^{-5}) \times 10^{2}}{4pi \times 10^{-7}}$.
  • Simplify the numerator: -1.6 × 10-9.
  • Simplify the denominator: 4π × 10-7 ≈ 1.2566 × 10-6.
  • $F = \frac{-1.6 \times 10^{-9}}{1.2566 \times 10^{-6}} = -1.27 \times 10^{-3} \text{ N}$.

Answer: -1.27 × 10-3 N (repulsive force away from higher field region)

Assumptions and limitations

  • Langevin's classical theory assumes spherically symmetric electron charge distributions and independent atomic orbits, which is an approximation for multi-electron atoms in condensed matter.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Diamagnetism is absent in paramagnetic and ferromagnetic materials.

Correct: Diamagnetism is an intrinsic property of all matter, but it is masked in paramagnetic and ferromagnetic substances because their positive paramagnetic or ferromagnetic responses are orders of magnitude larger.

All atoms contain orbiting electrons that undergo Larmor precession, but permanent atomic dipoles dominate when present.

Common error: Diamagnetic susceptibility depends strongly on temperature.

Correct: Diamagnetic susceptibility is essentially independent of temperature.

Thermal motion does not affect the induced Larmor precession currents responsible for diamagnetism.

Quick revision

  • Diamagnetism is universal, temperature-independent, possesses negative susceptibility (χ < 0), relative permeability slightly less than one, and causes repulsion from strong magnetic field regions.
  • Magnetic Susceptibility: $\chi = \frac{I}{H}$
  • Relative Permeability: μr = 1 + χ

Must remember

  • Every material possesses diamagnetism inherently due to orbiting electrons constituting microscopic current loops.
  • Diamagnetic susceptibility $\chi$ is small and negative, typically ranging from $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-9}$.
  • Relative magnetic permeability $\mu_r = 1 + \chi$ is slightly less than unity, making $\mu < \mu_0$.
  • Diamagnetic susceptibility is essentially independent of absolute temperature $T$.
  • In a non-uniform magnetic field, diamagnetic materials move from regions of stronger field to weaker field.
  • A freely suspended diamagnetic rod aligns itself perpendicular to the applied uniform magnetic field.
  • Superconductors exhibit perfect diamagnetism with $\chi = -1$, known as the Meissner effect.
  • Langevin's classical theory relates diamagnetic susceptibility to the mean square radius of electron orbits $\langle r^2 \rangle$.

Glossary

Magnetic Susceptibility (\chi)Ratio of intensity of magnetization $I$ to magnetic field intensity $H$, measuring the degree of magnetization of a material.
Relative Permeability (\mu_r)Ratio of the permeability of a substance to the permeability of free space, given by $\mu_r = 1 + \chi$.
Larmor Frequency (\Delta \omega)Frequency of precession of electrons around the magnetic field direction when an external field is applied, given by $\Delta \omega = \frac{eB}{2m}$.
Meissner EffectComplete expulsion of magnetic flux from the interior of a superconductor, resulting in perfect diamagnetism with $\chi = -1$.
Langevin DiamagnetismClassical theory treating diamagnetic susceptibility using atomic number density, atomic number, and mean square orbital radius.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the nature and approximate range of magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$) for diamagnetic materials?

Explanation: Diamagnetic materials possess a small and negative magnetic susceptibility, typically on the order of $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-9}$.

2. How does the relative magnetic permeability ($\mu_r$) of a diamagnetic substance compare to unity?

Explanation: Since $\mu_r = 1 + \chi$ and $\chi$ is small and negative, the relative permeability is slightly less than unity.

3. How does the magnetic susceptibility of a diamagnetic material vary with absolute temperature $T$?

Explanation: Unlike paramagnetism and ferromagnetism, diamagnetic susceptibility is independent of absolute temperature because thermal agitation does not disrupt the induced atomic currents.

4. When a diamagnetic rod is freely suspended in a uniform magnetic field, how does it orient itself?

Explanation: A freely suspended diamagnetic rod experiences a torque that aligns it perpendicular to the direction of a uniform magnetic field.

5. What happens to a diamagnetic material when placed in a non-uniform magnetic field?

Explanation: In a non-uniform magnetic field, diamagnetic materials are repelled from stronger magnetic fields toward weaker magnetic field regions.

6. What is the magnetic susceptibility of a superconductor exhibiting the Meissner effect?

Explanation: Superconductors exhibit perfect diamagnetism with $\chi = -1$, completely expelling all magnetic flux.

7. Which physical law accounts for the induced current in orbiting electrons opposing an external magnetic field?

Explanation: An external magnetic field induces an electromotive force via Faraday's law, and according to Lenz's law, the induced current creates a magnetic moment opposing the applied field.

8. What is the formula for Larmor frequency ($\Delta \omega$) associated with electron precession in a weak magnetic field $B$?

Explanation: The Larmor frequency shift is given by $\Delta \omega = \frac{eB}{2m}$, where $e$ is electronic charge, $B$ is the magnetic field, and $m$ is the mass of the electron.

9. In Langevin's classical theory of diamagnetism, what does the expression for susceptibility $\chi$ depend upon directly?

Explanation: Langevin's classical susceptibility is given by $\chi = -\frac{\mu_0 N e^2 Z \langle r^2 \rangle}{6m}$, which depends directly on the mean square radius $\langle r^2 \rangle$ of the electron orbits.

10. Why is diamagnetism masked in paramagnetic and ferromagnetic materials?

Explanation: Diamagnetism is an intrinsic property of all matter, but it is masked because permanent atomic dipoles in paramagnetic and ferromagnetic substances create vastly larger positive responses.

11. What is the macroscopic relation connecting magnetic induction $B$, applied field $H$, and magnetization $I$ in a diamagnetic material?

Explanation: The general macroscopic Maxwell relation is $B = \mu_0(H + I) = \mu H$, valid for linear isotropic magnetic media.

12. What is the expression for the force $F_x$ experienced by a diamagnetic sample of volume $V$ in a non-uniform magnetic field along the $x$-axis?

Explanation: The force in a non-uniform magnetic field gradient is given by $F_x = \frac{V \chi}{\mu_0} B \frac{dB}{dx}$. Since $\chi$ is negative, the force is directed toward weaker field regions.

13. Which of the following materials is classified as diamagnetic?

Explanation: Bismuth is a well-known strongly diamagnetic substance, whereas iron is ferromagnetic, aluminum is paramagnetic, and oxygen (gaseous) is paramagnetic.

14. What are the units of magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$)?

Explanation: Magnetic susceptibility $\chi = \frac{I}{H}$ is the ratio of two quantities with the same units ($A/m$), making it dimensionless.

15. Under what exceptional conditions can diamagnetic susceptibility exhibit temperature dependence?

Explanation: Normally temperature independent, exceptions occur only in certain semimetals or superconductors where structural phase changes alter carrier density with temperature.

Explanation: From electromagnetic theory, the relative permeability is related to magnetic susceptibility by $\mu_r = 1 + \chi$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What is the primary physical origin of diamagnetism in atoms?

Explanation: Diamagnetism originates from the induced electromotive force on orbiting electrons that creates microscopic current loops opposing the applied field.

2. If a diamagnetic material has a magnetic susceptibility of $-2 \times 10^{-5}$, what is its relative permeability?

Explanation: Using $\mu_r = 1 + \chi$, we get $\mu_r = 1 + (-2 \times 10^{-5}) = 1 - 0.00002 = 0.99998$.

3. Why is diamagnetic susceptibility independent of absolute temperature?

Explanation: Thermal agitation does not disrupt induced atomic currents because diamagnetism arises from dynamic response (Larmor precession) rather than dipole orientation.

4. A diamagnetic rod is placed inside a non-uniform magnetic field. Where will it tend to position itself?

Explanation: Due to negative susceptibility, diamagnetic materials experience a repulsive force pushing them from stronger to weaker magnetic field regions.

5. What is the Larmor frequency shift for an electron of mass $m$ and charge $e$ in a magnetic field $B$?

Explanation: The Larmor frequency shift is $\Delta \omega = \frac{eB}{2m}$ under weak magnetic field limits.

6. Which of the following statements is correct regarding magnetic permeability ($\mu$) of diamagnetic substances?

Explanation: Since $\mu_r < 1$, the permeability $\mu = \mu_0 \mu_r$ is strictly less than the permeability of free space $\mu_0$.

7. In Langevin's classical formula for diamagnetic susceptibility, what does $Z$ represent?

Explanation: In the formula $\chi = -\frac{\mu_0 N e^2 Z \langle r^2 \rangle}{6m}$, $Z$ denotes the atomic number of the atoms.

8. What characterizes the magnetic state of a superconductor in terms of magnetic induction $B$ inside its bulk?

Explanation: In a superconductor (Meissner effect), magnetic flux is completely expelled from the interior, meaning magnetic induction $B = 0$.

9. Why does diamagnetism exist in all substances without exception?

Explanation: Because all atoms contain orbiting electrons, every material possesses microscopic current loops capable of exhibiting diamagnetic response.

10. If the volume of a diamagnetic sample is $2 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^3$, susceptibility is $-10^{-5}$, applied field $H = 10^4 \text{ A/m}$, and magnetic field gradient $\frac{dB}{dx} = 2 \text{ T/m}$ with $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}$, what is the nature of the force?

Explanation: The force equation $F_x = \frac{V \chi}{\mu_0} B \frac{dB}{dx}$ contains a negative $\chi$, resulting in a repulsive force pushing the sample toward the weaker field region.
