Magnetic Field on the Axis of a Circular Coil

The magnetic field at any axial point of a current-carrying circular coil is directed along its axis and is derived using the Biot-Savart law. It forms the foundation for calculating magnetic fields of complex winding geometries in electrical machines.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Calculation of magnetic field at the center and axial points of a circular coil, variation of field with distance, and application of the Right-Hand Rule.

PGT extension

Derivation of the axial field formula using Biot-Savart law, spatial second derivative of the field, Helmholtz coil configuration for uniform magnetic field, and integration over multiple turns.

Prerequisites

  • Biot-Savart Law and its vector form
  • Integration techniques for trigonometric functions
  • Right-hand thumb and palm rules for magnetic fields

Biot-Savart Law Application to a Circular Loop

  • Consider a circular coil of radius R carrying current I with N turns, lying in the y-z plane with its center at the origin.
  • An elemental current length Idl is perpendicular to the position vector r connecting the element to an axial point P at distance x.
  • By the Biot-Savart law, the magnitude of the magnetic field dB due to Idl is independent of the azimuthal angle around the loop by symmetry.

Symmetry and Vector Components

  • The magnetic field vector dB makes an angle with the axis of the coil due to the cross product in the Biot-Savart law.
  • Perpendicular components (dB_y) cancel out diametrically opposite elements, leaving only the axial components (dB_x) to contribute to the net magnetic field.
  • The net magnetic field is directed entirely along the axis of the coil (the x-axis).

Special Case: Center of the Coil

  • At the center of the circular coil, the axial distance x becomes zero.
  • The general axial formula reduces to the maximum field value at the center: B = \mu_0 N I / (2R).
  • The field lines are straight and parallel near the center, representing a uniform magnetic field over a small region.

Inflection Points and Helmholtz Coils

  • The derivative of the axial magnetic field vanishes at x = \pm R/2, which correspond to points of inflection where the field gradient is maximum.
  • When two identical coaxial coils are separated by a distance equal to their radius R, their combined field has a region of high uniformity between them, known as a Helmholtz coil setup.
  • In a Helmholtz coil, the first and second spatial derivatives of the magnetic field vanish at the midpoint, ensuring uniform magnetic field distribution.

Derivations

Derivation of Magnetic Field on the Axis of a Circular Coil

Conditions: Coil of radius R, N turns, current I, point P at axial distance x.

  • Write the Biot-Savart law for a current element Idl: dB = (\mu_0 / 4\pi) * (I |dl \times r|) / r^3.
  • Recognize that angle between dl and r is 90 degrees, so |dl \times r| = dl * r.
  • Substitute r = \sqrt{R^2 + x^2}, giving dB = (\mu_0 I dl) / (4\pi (R^2 + x^2)).
  • Resolve dB into axial (dB cos\theta) and radial (dB sin\theta) components, where cos\theta = R / \sqrt{R^2 + x^2}.
  • Integrate dB cos\theta around the loop: B = \int dB (R / \sqrt{R^2 + x^2}) = (\mu_0 I R / 4\pi (R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}) \int dl.
  • Since \int dl = 2\pi R for a single turn and multiplying by N turns, obtain the final result.

Result: B = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}

Derivation of Position of Inflection Points (d2B/dx2 = 0)

Conditions: Single circular coil of radius R, finding points where curvature of B(x) changes sign.

  • Start with B(x) = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}.
  • Find the first derivative dB/dx = -\frac{3 \mu_0 I R^2 x}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{5/2}}.
  • Apply the quotient rule to find the second derivative d^2B/dx^2 = -\frac{3 \mu_0 I R^2}{2} * \frac{(R^2 + x^2)^{5/2} - x * (5/2)(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}(2x)}{(R^2 + x^2)^5}.
  • Simplify the numerator by factoring out (R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}: numerator proportional to (R^2 + x^2) - 5x^2 = R^2 - 4x^2.
  • Set d^2B/dx^2 = 0 to solve for x: R^2 - 4x^2 = 0.

Result: x = \pm \frac{R}{2}

Interpret: Demonstrates how combining two coils at a separation equal to their radius produces a region of nearly constant magnetic field strength.

Solved numericals

TGT core

A circular coil of 100 turns has a radius of 0.1 m and carries a current of 2 A. Calculate the magnetic field at its center.

  • Identify given values: N = 100, R = 0.1 m, I = 2 A, \mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7} T m A^{-1}.
  • Use formula for field at the center: B = \mu_0 N I / (2R).
  • Substitute values: B = (4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 100 \times 2) / (2 \times 0.1).
  • Calculate result: B = (8\pi \times 10^{-5}) / 0.2 = 40\pi \times 10^{-5} T = 1.256 \times 10^{-3} T.

Answer: 1.26 \times 10^{-3} \text{ T}

TGT core

At what distance along the axis of a circular coil of radius R is the magnetic field equal to 1/8th of its value at the center?

  • Use ratio formula: B(x) / B(0) = (R^2 / (R^2 + x^2))^{3/2}.
  • Set ratio equal to 1/8: 1/8 = (R^2 / (R^2 + x^2))^{3/2}.
  • Take the 2/3 power on both sides: (1/8)^{2/3} = R^2 / (R^2 + x^2), which gives 1/4 = R^2 / (R^2 + x^2).
  • Cross multiply: R^2 + x^2 = 4R^2, so x^2 = 3R^2.
  • Solve for x: x = \sqrt{3} R.

Answer: \sqrt{3}R

PGT extension

Two identical coaxial circular coils each of radius 0.5 m and 50 turns are placed 0.5 m apart. If a current of 1 A flows through both in the same direction, find the magnetic field at the midpoint between them.

  • Identify that the separation d equals the radius R (0.5 m), which satisfies the Helmholtz coil condition.
  • Use the Helmholtz coil formula: B_{total} = (4/5)^{3/2} * (\mu_0 N I / R) * 2 (since there are two identical coils).
  • Substitute values: B_{total} = 2 * (4/5)^{3/2} * (4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 50 \times 1) / 0.5.
  • Calculate term by term: (4/5)^{3/2} = 0.8^{3/2} \approx 0.7155.
  • Calculate single coil center field: (4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 50) / 0.5 = 4\pi \times 10^{-5} T.
  • Multiply together: B_{total} = 2 \times 0.7155 \times 4\pi \times 10^{-5} = 5.724 \times 10^{-5} \pi \approx 1.798 \times 10^{-4} T.

Answer: 1.80 \times 10^{-4} \text{ T}

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes the coil wire is thin and the current is uniformly distributed across its cross-section.
  • Edge effects and finite thickness of wire insulation are neglected in standard academic derivations.
  • Formula assumes free space or non-magnetic medium (\mu_r \approx 1) unless explicitly modified by core permeability.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The magnetic field is maximum at points other than the center along the axis.

Correct: The magnetic field is always maximum at the center (x = 0) of a circular current-carrying coil.

The denominator (R^2 + x^2)^{3/2} is minimum when x = 0, making the field magnitude maximum at the center.

Common error: Radial components of the magnetic field do not exist anywhere on the axis.

Correct: Radial components cancel out strictly along the axis due to symmetry, but exist off-axis.

Diametrically opposite current elements produce radial components that cancel out only on the symmetrical central axis.

Quick revision

  • Axial magnetic field formula: B = \mu_0 N I R^2 / 2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}.
  • Axial Magnetic Field of Circular Coil: B = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}
  • Magnetic Field at the Center: B_{center} = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2R}

Must remember

  • The magnitude of the magnetic field at the axial distance $x$ from the center of a circular coil of radius $R$, $N$ turns, and current $I$ is given by $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$.
  • The magnetic field is maximum at the center ($x = 0$) of the circular coil, having the value $B_{center} = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2R}$.
  • Perpendicular components of $dB$ cancel out due to diametrically opposite symmetry, leaving only the axial components along the $x$-axis.
  • Points of inflection (where the field gradient is maximum and the second spatial derivative vanishes) occur at axial distances $x = \pm R/2$.
  • In a Helmholtz coil setup, two identical coaxial coils are separated by a distance equal to their radius $R$, producing a highly uniform magnetic field region between them.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the formula for the magnetic field at the center of a circular coil of $N$ turns, radius $R$, carrying current $I$?

Explanation: At the center of the circular coil, $x = 0$. Substituting $x = 0$ into the general axial formula $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$ yields $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2R}$.

2. Along the axis of a circular current-carrying coil, which components of the elemental magnetic field $dB$ cancel out due to symmetry?

Explanation: Diametrically opposite current elements produce perpendicular (radial) components of $dB$ that point in opposite directions and cancel out, leaving only the axial components.

3. At what axial distance $x$ from the center do the points of inflection of the magnetic field occur?

Explanation: The derivative of the axial magnetic field vanishes at $x = \pm R/2$, which correspond to points of inflection where the field gradient is maximum.

4. What is the separation distance between two identical coaxial coils in a Helmholtz coil configuration?

Explanation: In a Helmholtz coil setup, two identical coaxial coils are separated by a distance equal to their radius $R$ ($d = R$) to create a region of high magnetic field uniformity.

5. How does the magnetic field $B$ vary with axial distance $x$ when $x$ is much greater than the radius $R$ ($x \gg R$)?

Explanation: When $x \gg R$, the term $R^2$ in the denominator $(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}$ can be neglected, reducing the expression to $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2x^3}$, meaning $B \propto \frac{1}{x^3}$.

6. What is the magnetic dipole moment $M$ of a planar circular coil with $N$ turns, radius $R$, and current $I$?

Explanation: The magnetic dipole moment of a planar circular coil is given by $M = N I A$, where the area $A = \$\pi$ R^2$, thus $M = N I \$\pi$ R^2$.

7. What is the unit of the permeability of free space $\mu_0$?

Explanation: The SI unit of $\mu_0$ is Tesla meter per Ampere ($\text{T m A}^{-1}$) or Henry per meter ($\text{H/m}$).

8. Near the center of a circular current-carrying coil, the magnetic field lines are:

Explanation: Near the center of the circular coil, the field lines are straight and parallel, indicating a uniform magnetic field over a small region.

9. If the radius of a circular coil is doubled while keeping the current and number of turns constant, what happens to the magnetic field at its center?

Explanation: The center magnetic field is $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2R}$. Since $B$ is inversely proportional to $R$, doubling $R$ will halve the magnetic field.

10. What is the ratio of the magnetic field at an axial distance $x = R$ to the magnetic field at the center $x = 0$ for a circular coil?

Explanation: Using the ratio formula $\frac{B(x)}{B(0)} = \left(1 + \frac{x^2}{R^2}\right)^{-3/2}$, substituting $x = R$ gives $(1 + 1)^{-3/2} = (2)^{-3/2} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}$.

11. Which of the following describes the first derivative of the axial magnetic field $\frac{dB}{dx}$?

Explanation: Differentiating $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$ with respect to $x$ using the chain rule gives $\frac{dB}{dx} = -\frac{3 \mu_0 N I R^2 x}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{5/2}}$.

12. What is the value of the combined magnetic field $B_{total}$ at the midpoint of a Helmholtz coil setup?

Explanation: The combined magnetic field at the midpoint of two identical coaxial coils separated by $d = R$ is given by $B_{total} = \left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{3/2} \frac{\mu_0 N I}{R}$.

13. Why is the magnetic field maximum at the center ($x = 0$) of a circular coil?

Explanation: In the formula $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$, the numerator is constant for a given coil, and the denominator is minimized when $x = 0$, resulting in the maximum field magnitude.

14. What physical property is ensured when the first and second spatial derivatives of the magnetic field vanish at the midpoint of a Helmholtz coil?

Explanation: Vanishing first and second spatial derivatives at the midpoint ensure that the magnetic field is extremely flat and uniform across that region.

15. In which plane is the circular coil typically considered to lie when deriving its axial magnetic field using the Biot-Savart law in standard textbook problems?

Explanation: By convention in standard derivations, the circular coil is considered to lie in the y-z plane with its center at the origin, so its axis coincides with the x-axis.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. A circular coil of radius $R$ and $N$ turns carries a current $I$. The magnetic field at a point on its axis at a distance $R$ from its center is proportional to:

Explanation: Substitute $x = R$ into the axial formula: $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2(R^2 + R^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2(2R^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2(2^{3/2} R^3)} = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{4\sqrt{2} R}$. Thus, $B$ is proportional to $R^{-1}$.

2. The ratio of the magnetic field at the center of a circular coil to that at a distance equal to its radius along the axis is:

Explanation: The ratio of center field to axial field at $x = R$ is $\frac{B(0)}{B(R)} = \left(1 + \frac{R^2}{R^2}\right)^{3/2} = (2)^{3/2} = 2\sqrt{2}$.

3. Two identical circular coils of radius $R$ carry equal currents in the same direction and are placed coaxially at a separation $R$. The setup forms a:

Explanation: Two identical coaxial coils separated by a distance equal to their radius $R$ form a Helmholtz coil setup, which provides a uniform magnetic field.

4. At what distance along the axis of a circular coil of radius $R$ is the magnetic field intensity equal to $\frac{1}{8}$th of its value at the center?

Explanation: We need $\frac{B(x)}{B(0)} = \frac{1}{8} \implies \left(1 + \frac{x^2}{R^2}\right)^{-3/2} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3 \implies 1 + \frac{x^2}{R^2} = 4 \implies \frac{x^2}{R^2} = 3 \implies x = \sqrt{3}R$.

5. A circular loop of radius $r$ carrying a current $I$ has a magnetic moment $M$. If the loop is reshaped into a square of side $a$ carrying the same current $I$, what is the new magnetic moment?

Explanation: Perimeter remains equal: $2\$\pi$ r = 4a \implies a = \frac{\$\pi$ r}{2}$. Original moment $M = I(\$\pi$ r^2)$. New moment $M' = I(a^2) = I\left(\frac{\$\pi$ r}{2}\right)^2 = I \frac{\$\pi$^2 r^2}{4} = \frac{\$\pi$}{4} (I \$\pi$ r^2) = \frac{\$\pi$}{4} M$.

6. The magnetic field at the center of a circular coil of radius $R$ is $B_1$. The magnetic field at a point on its axis at a distance $x = \sqrt{3}R$ is $B_2$. The ratio $\frac{B_1}{B_2}$ is:

Explanation: From the previous relation, when $x = \sqrt{3}R$, the ratio $\frac{B(x)}{B(0)} = \frac{1}{8}$. Thus, $\frac{B(0)}{B(x)} = \frac{B_1}{B_2} = 8$.
