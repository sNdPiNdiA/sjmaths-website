Electricity and Magnetism

Applications of Biot-Savart Law

The Biot-Savart law quantitatively evaluates the magnetic field produced by a steady electric current element at any given point in space. It forms the foundational vector principle for calculating magnetic induction across symmetric current configurations such as circular loops and straight conductors.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Calculation of magnetic field at the center of a circular current-carrying coil, along the axis of a circular loop, and infinitely long straight conductors using standard geometry.

PGT extension

Off-axis field components, magnetic vector potential formulations, finite and semi-infinite solenoids, Helmholtz coils uniformity criteria, and relativistic transformation of fields.

Prerequisites

  • Vector cross product and spatial coordinate systems
  • Fundamental definition of steady electric current and current element (I dl)
  • Superposition principle for magnetic fields

Biot-Savart Law Vector Formulation

  • The differential magnetic field dB produced by a current element I dvec{l} at position vector r relative to the element is given by the inverse-square vector relation.
  • The direction of dB is determined by the right-hand rule for the cross product dvec{l} x rhat, being perpendicular to both the current element and the displacement vector.
  • The magnitude scales directly with current I and length element dl, and inversely with the square of the distance r from the element.

Magnetic Field of a Straight Conductor

  • For a straight wire of finite length, the magnetic field lines form concentric circles centered around the wire conductor in planes perpendicular to it.
  • The magnitude depends on the perpendicular distance from the wire and the angles subtended by the ends of the conductor at the field point.
  • In the limiting case of an infinitely long straight wire, the angular terms simplify, yielding a field inversely proportional to the perpendicular distance.

Magnetic Field of a Circular Current Loop

  • At the exact center of a circular loop, the magnetic field is directed perpendicularly to the plane of the loop, pointing outwards or inwards depending on the sense of the current.
  • At an axial point located at distance x from the center, the radial components of dB cancel out due to cylindrical symmetry, leaving only the axial components.
  • The magnetic dipole moment of a circular loop of N turns, area A, and current I is defined as M = N I A.

Helmholtz Coils Arrangement

  • A Helmholtz setup consists of two identical coaxial circular coils separated by a distance equal to their common radius R.
  • This configuration produces a region of nearly uniform magnetic field in the space midway between the two coils.
  • Mathematically, this corresponds to the vanishing of both the first and second spatial derivatives of the magnetic field along the axis at the midpoint.

Derivations

Magnetic Field on the Axis of a Circular Current Loop

Conditions: Circular coil of radius R carrying current I with N turns; finding field at axial distance x from the center.

  • Consider a current element I dl at the bottom of the loop. The vector displacement to axial point P at distance x is r = sqrt(R2 + x2).
  • The angle between current element dvec{l} and position vector r is 90 degrees, so the magnitude of differential field is dB = (μ0 / 4π) * (I dl sin(90)) / (R2 + x2).
  • By symmetry, radial components of dB cancel when integrated around the loop, and only the axial components dB cos(θ) survive.
  • Substitute cos(θ) = R / sqrt(R2 + x2) and integrate dl around the loop circumference (∫ dl = 2πR).

Result: B = (μ0 N I R2) / (2 (R2 + x2)3/2)

Magnetic Field due to an Infinitely Long Straight Conductor

Conditions: Straight wire carrying steady current I; evaluating field at perpendicular distance a.

  • Let a wire lie along the y-axis. Consider an element dy at coordinate y. The distance to field point P at distance a on the x-axis is r = sqrt(a2 + y2).
  • Using the Biot-Savart law, dB = (μ0 I / 4π) * (dy sin(θ)) / (r2), where θ is the angle between dvec{l} and r.
  • Express y in terms of the angle φ subtended at point P: y = a tan(φ), so dy = a sec2(φ) dφ and r = a sec(φ).
  • Substitute into the integral for limits from φ = -π/2 to π/2 for an infinite wire: B = (μ0 I / 4πa) ∫-π/2π/2 cos(φ) dφ.

Result: B = (μ0 I) / (2 π a)

Solved numericals

TGT core

A circular coil of radius 0.1 m has 50 turns and carries a current of 2 A. Calculate the magnetic field at the center of the coil.

  • Identify given values: R = 0.1 m, N = 50, I = 2 A, and \mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10-7 T m/A.
  • Apply the formula for the magnetic field at the center of a circular coil: B = (\mu_0 N I) / (2 R).
  • Substitute values: B = (4\pi \times 10-7 \times 50 \times 2) / (2 \times 0.1).
  • Calculate the result: B = (4\pi \times 10-7 \times 100) / 0.2 = 200\pi \times 10-7 / 0.2 = 1000\pi \times 10-7 = π \times 10-4 T.

Answer: 3.14 x 10-4 T

PGT extension

A circular loop of radius R = 0.05 m carries a current of 5 A. At what axial distance x from the center of the loop is the magnetic field equal to 1/8th of its value at the center?

  • Write the formula for the center field: Bcenter = (μ0 I) / (2 R).
  • Write the formula for the axial field: Baxis = (\mu_0 I R2) / (2 (R2 + x2)3/2).
  • Set Baxis = (1/8) Bcenter: (R2) / (R2 + x2)3/2 = (1/8) (1 / R), leading to (R / sqrt(R2 + x2))3 = 1/8.
  • Take cube roots on both sides: R / sqrt(R2 + x2) = 1/2, which implies sqrt(R2 + x2) = 2R.
  • Square both sides: R2 + x2 = 4R2 => x2 = 3R2 => x = R sqrt(3).
  • Substitute R = 0.05 m: x = 0.05 \times \sqrt{3} m.

Answer: 0.0866 m (or \sqrt{3} R)

TGT core

A long straight wire carries a current of 10 A. Find the magnetic field at a perpendicular distance of 5 cm from the wire.

  • Identify given data: I = 10 A, a = 5 cm = 0.05 m, \mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10-7 T m/A.
  • Use the infinite straight wire formula: B = (\mu_0 I) / (2 \pi a).
  • Substitute parameters: B = (4\pi \times 10-7 \times 10) / (2 \pi \times 0.05).
  • Simplify terms: B = (4\pi \times 10-6) / (0.1 \pi) = 40 \times 10-6 T = 4 \times 10-5 T.

Answer: 4 x 10-5 T

Assumptions and limitations

  • The Biot-Savart law assumes steady currents (DC) where charge accumulation does not vary with time; time-dependent displacement currents are excluded.
  • Calculations in standard TGT/PGT derivations assume free space or vacuum magnetic permeability (\mu_0) unless modified by relative permeability (\mu_r) for material media.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The magnetic field at the axis of a circular loop is directed radially outward from the axis.

Correct: The magnetic field vector along the axis of a circular loop is directed entirely parallel to the axis of symmetry.

Symmetry cancellation eliminates all perpendicular/radial components of differential magnetic fields contributed by opposing current elements.

Common error: Biot-Savart law is only applicable to closed loops of current.

Correct: The differential law applies to any infinitesimal current element dvec{l}, though closed circuits are required to sustain steady currents.

Isolated current elements cannot exist independently in nature due to charge conservation, but the law mathematically computes the field of any differential segment.

Quick revision

  • Biot-Savart law relates current element to magnetic induction via an inverse-square distance dependence and vector cross product.
  • Magnetic field at the center of a circular coil of N turns is proportional to NI/R.
  • Magnetic field at distance a from an infinite straight wire is inversely proportional to a.
  • Helmholtz coils provide a uniform magnetic field region when separation distance equals the coil radius.

Must remember

  • The Biot-Savart law in vector form is given by $d\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I d\vec{l} \times \hat{r}}{r^2}$, which follows an inverse-square distance dependence.
  • The magnetic field at the center of a circular current-carrying coil of $N$ turns and radius $R$ is $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2 R}$.
  • For an infinitely long straight conductor carrying current $I$, the magnetic field at a perpendicular distance $a$ is $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2 \pi a}$.
  • The magnetic field along the axis of a circular loop at distance $x$ is $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2 (R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}.$
  • In a Helmholtz coil arrangement, two identical coaxial coils are separated by a distance equal to their radius $R$, producing a nearly uniform magnetic field at the midpoint where first and second spatial derivatives vanish.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the direction of the magnetic field vector $d\vec{B}$ produced by a current element $I d\vec{l}$ at a position vector $\vec{r}$?

Explanation: The cross product $d\vec{l} \times \hat{r}$ dictates that $d\vec{B}$ is perpendicular to the plane containing both the current element and the position vector.

2. The magnitude of the magnetic field at the center of a circular loop of radius $R$ carrying current $I$ is proportional to:

Explanation: The formula for the center of a circular loop is $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2 R}$, showing an inverse relationship with radius $R$.

3. What is the magnetic field at a perpendicular distance $a$ from an infinitely long straight wire carrying current $I$?

Explanation: Integrating the Biot-Savart law for an infinite straight wire yields $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2 \$\pi$ a}$.

4. At the midpoint between two identical coaxial coils forming a Helmholtz setup of radius $R$, the separation between the coils is:

Explanation: By definition, a Helmholtz coil arrangement requires the separation distance between the two coaxial coils to be exactly equal to their common radius $R$.

5. Why do radial components of differential magnetic fields $d\vec{B}$ cancel out at axial points of a circular current loop?

Explanation: Cylindrical symmetry ensures that transverse components of $d\vec{B}$ from opposing differential elements cancel out pairwise, leaving only axial components.

6. What is the magnetic dipole moment of a circular current loop having $N$ turns, area $A$, and current $I$?

Explanation: The magnetic dipole moment $M$ for a multi-turn planar coil is defined as $M = N I A$.

7. What is the magnetic field inside an infinitely long solenoid with $n$ turns per unit length carrying current $I$?

Explanation: Applying Ampere's law or summing Biot-Savart contributions for a long solenoid gives $B = \mu_0 n I$ well inside the solenoid.

8. What is the unit of the permeability of free space $\mu_0$?

Explanation: From the Biot-Savart law or force between wires, $\mu_0$ has units of Tesla-meter per Ampere ($\text{T}\cdot\text{m}/\text{A}$).

9. If the current through a circular loop is doubled and its radius is halved, the magnetic field at its center becomes how many times the initial value?

Explanation: $B \propto I / R$. If $I \rightarrow 2I$ and $R \rightarrow R/2$, then $B' \propto 2I / (R/2) = 4(I/R)$, so it increases by a factor of 4.

10. What does the vanishing of the first and second spatial derivatives of the magnetic field at the midpoint of Helmholtz coils signify?

Explanation: The vanishing of the first and second derivatives along the axis ensures that the field profile is extremely flat near the midpoint, creating a uniform magnetic field region.

11. What is the formula for the magnetic field at the center of a circular arc of radius $R$ subtending an angle $\$\theta$$ in radians?

Explanation: Integrating over an arc length $R\$\theta$$, the magnetic field is $B = \frac{\mu_0 I \$\theta$}{4 \$\pi$ R}$.

12. For a finite straight wire subtending angles $\$\theta_{1}$$ and $\$\theta_{2}$$ at a point distance $a$ away, the magnetic field expression is:

Explanation: The standard derivation for a finite straight conductor yields $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\$\pi$ a}(\sin\$\theta_{1}$ + \sin\$\theta_{2}$)$.

13. Which physical law or principle is the Biot-Savart law in magnetostatics analogous to in electrostatics?

Explanation: The Biot-Savart law computes magnetic fields from current elements with an inverse-square distance dependence, mirroring Coulomb's law for electric fields from point charges.

14. What is the nature of magnetic field lines around a long straight current-carrying conductor?

Explanation: By symmetry and the right-hand rule, magnetic field lines around a straight wire form concentric circles centered on the conductor in planes perpendicular to it.

15. At an axial distance $x = R$ from the center of a circular loop of radius $R$, how does the magnetic field compare to the field at the center ($x=0$)?

Explanation: Substitute $x = R$ into $B = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2+x^2)^{3/2}} \rightarrow \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(2R^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2 R (2\sqrt{2})} = \frac{B_{center}}{2\sqrt{2}}$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. A circular coil of radius $R$ and $N$ turns carries a current $I$. What is the magnetic field at its axial point at a distance $x$ from its center?

Explanation: The standard axial field formula derived via Biot-Savart law is $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$.

2. An infinite straight wire carries a current of $5\text{ A}$. What is the magnetic field at a perpendicular distance of $10\text{ cm}$ from the wire? ($\mu_0 = 4\$\pi$ \times 10^{-7} \text{ T}\cdot\text{m/A}$)

Explanation: $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\$\pi$ a} = \frac{(4\$\pi$ \times 10^{-7})(5)}{2\$\pi$ (0.1)} = \frac{2 \times 10^{-6} \times 5}{0.1} = 1 \times 10^{-5} \text{ T}$.

3. What is the magnetic field at the center of a circular loop of radius $0.1\text{ m}$ carrying $2\text{ A}$ current?

Explanation: $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R} = \frac{(4\$\pi$ \times 10^{-7})(2)}{2(0.1)} = \frac{4\$\pi$ \times 10^{-7}}{0.1} = 4\$\pi$ \times 10^{-6} \text{ T}$.

4. In a Helmholtz coil configuration, two coils of radius $R$ are separated by a distance $d$. For optimal magnetic field uniformity between them, what must $d$ be?

Explanation: The defining condition for a Helmholtz coil setup is that the separation distance between the two coaxial coils equals their radius $R$.

5. A current $I$ flows along an infinitely long straight wire. If the distance from the wire is doubled, how does the magnitude of the magnetic field change?

Explanation: Since $B \propto 1/a$, doubling the distance $a$ reduces the magnetic field magnitude by half.

6. What is the magnetic field at the center of a semi-circular arc of radius $R$ carrying current $I$?

Explanation: For a semi-circular arc, $\$\theta$ = \$\pi$$ radians. Substituting into $B = \frac{\mu_0 I \$\theta$}{4 \$\pi$ R}$ gives $B = \frac{\mu_0 I \$\pi$}{4 \$\pi$ R} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4 R}$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. If the radius and number of turns of a circular coil are both doubled while keeping the current constant, the magnetic field at its center will:

Explanation: $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2 R}$. If $N \rightarrow 2N$ and $R \rightarrow 2R$, then $B' = \frac{\mu_0 (2N) I}{2 (2R)} = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2 R}$, so it remains unchanged.

2. A long straight wire carries a current $I$. At what perpendicular distance from the wire is the magnitude of the magnetic field equal to $\frac{\mu_0 I}{4\$\pi$}$?

Explanation: $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\$\pi$ a} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\$\pi$} \implies \frac{1}{2a} = \frac{1}{4} \implies 2a = 4 \implies a = 0.5\text{ m}$.

3. What is the ratio of the magnetic field at the center of a circular coil of radius $R$ to the magnetic field at an axial point at distance $x = \sqrt{3}R$?

Explanation: Center field $B_0 = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R}$. Axial field $B_{ax} = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2 + 3R^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(4R^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{16 R} = \frac{B_0}{8}$. Thus $B_0 / B_{ax} = 8:1$.

4. Which of the following statements about the Biot-Savart law is correct?

Explanation: The Biot-Savart law calculates the differential magnetic field $d\vec{B}$ produced by an infinitesimal current element $I d\vec{l}$.

5. Two concentric coplanar circular loops of radii $$R_{1}$$ and $$R_{2}$$ ($$R_{2}$ = 2 $R_{1}$$) carry currents $$I_{1}$$ and $$I_{2}$$ in opposite directions. If their magnetic fields at the center cancel each other out, what is $$I_{2}$ / $I_{1}$$?

Explanation: For fields to cancel at the center, $\frac{\mu_0 $I_{1}$}{2 $R_{1}$} = \frac{\mu_0 $I_{2}$}{2 $R_{2}$} \implies \frac{$I_{1}$}{$R_{1}$} = \frac{$I_{2}$}{2 $R_{1}$} \implies $I_{2}$ / $I_{1}$ = 2$.

6. What happens to the magnetic field at the center of a circular loop if the direction of the current is reversed?

Explanation: Reversing the current reverses the sense of the cross product $d\vec{l} \times \hat{r}$, thereby reversing the direction of the magnetic field vector according to the right-hand rule.

7. An arc of a circle of radius $R$ subtends an angle of $90^\circ$ at the center. If current $I$ flows through it, the magnetic field at the center is:

Explanation: Substitute $\$\theta$ = \$\pi$/2$ into $B = \frac{\mu_0 I \$\theta$}{4 \$\pi$ R} \implies \frac{\mu_0 I (\$\pi$/2)}{4 \$\pi$ R} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{8 R}$.

8. In a long solenoid, the magnetic field at the ends compared to the field well inside at the center is approximately:

Explanation: At the ends of a sufficiently long solenoid, the magnetic field drops to approximately half of its uniform interior value ($B = \mu_0 n I$ inside, $B = \frac{1}{2} \mu_0 n I$ at the ends).

9. The magnetic field vector $d\vec{B}$ at a point due to a current element depends on the angle $\$\theta$$ between $d\vec{l}$ and $\vec{r}$. At what angle is the magnetic field maximum?

Explanation: The cross product magnitude is $|d\vec{l} \times \hat{r}| = dl \sin\$\theta$$, which reaches its maximum when $\$\theta$ = 90^\circ$.

10. What is the dimension of the magnetic field $B$ in terms of Mass ($M$), Length ($L$), Time ($T$), and Current ($A$)?

Explanation: Using Lorentz force $F = q v B$ or Biot-Savart law, $B = \frac{F}{I L} \implies \frac{[M L T^{-2}]}{[A][L]} = [M T^{-2} A^{-1}]$.
