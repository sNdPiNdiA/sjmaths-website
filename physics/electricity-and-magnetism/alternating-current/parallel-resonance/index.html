Parallel Resonance Circuit (Tank Circuit)

A parallel resonance circuit consists of an inductor with internal resistance in parallel with a capacitor across an alternating voltage source. At resonance, the circuit impedance is maximum and the reactive currents cancel out, resulting in a minimum supply current.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of parallel resonance, condition for resonance (frequency formula), phase relationship between voltage and total current, and maximum impedance condition for an ideal LC parallel circuit.

PGT extension

Exact resonant frequency for a real inductor with resistance r, dynamic impedance expression, sharpness of resonance, bandwidth, and quality factor (Q-factor) derivations.

Prerequisites

  • Alternating current fundamentals and phasor algebra
  • Complex impedance and admittance of series LCR circuits
  • Condition of electrical resonance in series AC circuits

Basic Structure of Parallel Resonance

  • Comprises a capacitor of capacitance C connected in parallel with an inductive coil having inductance L and resistance r.
  • The alternating source supplies a total current which splits into the inductive branch current and capacitive branch current.
  • Serves as a rejector circuit because it offers maximum impedance and minimum current at the resonant frequency.

Condition for Resonance in a Parallel Circuit

  • Resonance occurs when the reactive component of the total admittance of the circuit becomes zero.
  • Alternatively, it is defined as the frequency at which the total current is in phase with the applied alternating voltage, making the power factor unity.
  • The resonant frequency depends on whether the inductive coil resistance r is negligible or substantial.

Dynamic Impedance at Resonance

  • At resonance, the parallel combination exhibits a very high pure resistive impedance known as dynamic impedance.
  • For a coil with negligible resistance (r approx 0), the dynamic impedance approaches infinity, reducing the supply current to zero.
  • For a practical coil with resistance r, the dynamic impedance is given by L / (C r).

Bandwidth and Quality Factor (Q-factor)

  • The quality factor Q of a parallel resonant circuit is the ratio of circulating reactive current through the LC tank to the external supply current.
  • Q-factor determines the sharpness of the resonance curve and is expressed as (omega0 L) / r.
  • Bandwidth (Delta omega) is inversely proportional to the Q-factor, given by Delta omeg$a = \omega_{0} / Q$.

Derivations

Resonant Frequency and Dynamic Impedance for a Practical Parallel Circuit

Conditions: Inductive branch contains inductor L and series resistance r in parallel with ideal capacitor C.

  • Write the complex impedance of the inductive branch as $Z_L = r + j\omega L$ and the capacitive branch as $Z_C = -j / (\omega C)$.
  • Express the total admittance Y as the sum of admittances: $Y = Y_L + Y_C = \frac{1}{r + j\omega L} + j\omega C$.
  • Rationalize the inductive admittance: $Y_L = \frac{r - j\omega L}{r^2 + \omega^2 L^2}$.
  • Equate the imaginary part of the total admittance to zero for resonance: \frac{\omega L}{r^2 + \omega^2 $L^2 = \omega C$.
  • Solve for resonant frequency: \omeg$a_0^2 = \frac{1}{LC} - \frac{r^2}{L^2}$, giving $f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \frac{r^2}{L^2}}$.

Result: Resonant frequency omeg$a_0 = \sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \frac{r^2}{L^2}}$ and dynamic impedance $Z_d = \frac{L}{Cr}$.

Quality Factor of Parallel Resonance Circuit

Conditions: Parallel L-C-r circuit driven by a constant voltage source V.

  • Define Q-factor as the ratio of peak reactive power to average power dissipated per radian, or the magnification of current.
  • Circulating current in the LC loop at resonance is $I_c = \frac{V}{\omega_0 L}$, and supply current is $I = \frac{V}{Z_d} = \frac{Vr}{L}$.
  • Substitute $Z_d = L / (Cr)$ and inductive reactance $X_L = \omega_0 L$.
  • Ratio gives $Q = \frac{I_c}{I} = \frac{\omega_0 L}{r}$.

Result: $Q = \frac{\omega_0 L}{r} = \frac{1}{\omega_0 C r} = \frac{1}{r} \sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}$

Graphs and experiments

Frequency vs Impedance Curve (Rejector Circuit)

Draw: A bell-shaped curve plotting Impedance (Z) on the y-axis against Frequency (f) on the x-axis, peaking sharply at f_0.

Interpret: Impedance is minimum at very low and very high frequencies, reaching a sharp maximum value Z_d at the resonant frequency f_0.

Exam use: Tested conceptually in TGT/PGT to identify parallel resonance as a rejector circuit.

Frequency vs Supply Current Curve

Draw: A trough-shaped curve plotting Total Current (I) on the y-axis against Frequency (f) on the x-axis, showing a sharp minimum at f_0.

Interpret: The total supply current drops to its lowest value at resonance because the parallel LC branches draw equal and opposite reactive currents.

Exam use: Used to differentiate acceptor (series) and rejector (parallel) resonance behavior.

Phase Angle vs Frequency

Draw: S-shaped curve showing phase angle phi transitioning from positive (inductive at low frequencies) through zero at f_0 to negative (capacitive at high frequencies).

Interpret: At resonance, the phase angle between voltage and current is zero, making the circuit purely resistive (unity power factor).

Exam use: Questions on power factor and phase conditions at resonance.

Solved numericals

TGT core

A parallel resonant circuit has an inductor of 2 H and a capacitor of 2 micro-Farad with negligible resistance. Calculate the resonant frequency.

  • Identify given values: L = 2 H, $C = 2 \times 10^{-6} F$.
  • Use the ideal parallel resonance formula: $f_0 = \frac{1}{2 \pi \sqrt{LC}}$.
  • Substitute values: L$C = 2 \times 2 \times 10^{-6} = 4 \times 10^{-6} s^2$.
  • Square root of LC is 2 \times 10^{-3} s.
  • Calculate $f_0 = \frac{1}{2 \pi \times 2 \times 10^{-3}} = \frac{1000}{4 \pi} = \frac{250}{\pi} \approx 79.58 Hz$.

Answer: 79.58 Hz

PGT extension

An inductive coil of inductance 0.5 H and resistance 20 ohm is connected in parallel with a variable capacitor across a 230 V, 50 Hz supply. Find the capacitance required for resonance and the dynamic impedance.

  • Given L = 0.5 H, r = 20 \Omega, f = 50 Hz, so $\omega = 2 \pi f = 100 \pi \approx 314.16 rad/s$.
  • Use exact resonance condition for practical parallel circuit: \omeg$a^2 = \frac{1}{LC} - \frac{r^2}{L^2}$.
  • Calculate r^2 / $L^2 = 20^2 / 0.5^2 = 400 / 0.25 = 1600$.
  • Calculate \omeg$a^2 = (100 \pi)^2 \approx 98696$.
  • Find 1 / L$C = \omega^2 + \frac{r^2}{L^2} = 98696 + 1600 = 100296$.
  • Solve for C: $C = \frac{1}{100296 \times 0.5} = \frac{1}{50148} \approx 1.994 \times 10^{-5} F = 19.94 \mu F$.
  • Calculate dynamic impedance $Z_d = \frac{L}{C r} = \frac{0.5}{(1.994 \times 10^{-5}) \times 20} = \frac{0.5}{3.988 \times 10^{-4}} \approx 1253.7 \Omega$.

Answer: $C = 19.94 micro-Farad$, $Z_d = 1253.7 ohms$

PGT extension

A parallel L-C-r circuit has L = 0.2 H, $C = 50 micro-Farad$, and r = 10 ohm. Determine the Q-factor of the circuit at resonance.

  • Calculate resonant angular frequency using \omega_0 \approx \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} since r is relatively small: \omeg$a_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.2 \times 50 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{10^{-5}}} = \frac{1}{3.162 \times 10^{-3}} \approx 316.22 rad/s$.
  • Use the Q-factor formula for parallel circuits: $Q = \frac{\omega_0 L}{r}$.
  • Substitute values: Q = \frac{316.22 \times 0.2}{10} = \frac{63.244}{10} = 6.32.

Answer: 6.32

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes sinusoidal AC source operating at steady state conditions.
  • Stray capacitances between inductor turns are neglected in basic derivations.
  • The formula omeg$a_0 = 1/\sqrt(LC)$ is an approximation valid only when the coil resistance r is much smaller than sqrt(L/C).

Common misconceptions

Common error: Parallel resonance occurs when inductive reactance equals capacitive reactance (omega $L = 1 / \omega C)$.

Correct: This condition applies to series resonance; parallel resonance requires zero imaginary admittance, leading to a modified frequency formula.

The presence of resistance in the inductive branch alters the phase of the branch current, shifting the true resonance frequency from the ideal LC value.

Common error: Parallel resonance circuits have minimum impedance at resonance.

Correct: Parallel resonance circuits have maximum impedance (dynamic impedance) at resonance.

Because it acts as a rejector circuit, it impedes line current flow at resonance, opposite to series circuits.

Quick revision

  • Parallel resonance is a rejector circuit offering maximum impedance and minimum supply current at resonance.
  • Exact resonant frequency for a real parallel circuit is $f_0 = (1 / 2 \pi) \sqrt(1/LC - r^2/L^2)$.
  • Dynamic impedance at resonance is given by $Z_d = L / (C r)$.
  • Quality factor $Q = \omega_0 L / r measures both sharpness of resonance$ and current magnification in the tank loop.

Must remember

  • A parallel resonance circuit (tank circuit) acts as a rejector circuit because it offers maximum impedance and minimum current at resonance.
  • At resonance, the total reactive component of admittance becomes zero, making the power factor unity and supply current in phase with the applied voltage.
  • The dynamic impedance of a practical parallel resonant circuit containing a coil of resistance $r$ is given by $Z_d = \frac{L}{Cr}$.
  • For an ideal parallel LC circuit with zero coil resistance ($r = 0$), the resonant frequency is $f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{LC}}$ and dynamic impedance approaches infinity.
  • The resonant frequency for a practical coil with resistance $r$ is given by $f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \frac{r^2}{L^2}}$.
  • The Quality factor ($Q$-factor) of a parallel resonant circuit is expressed as $Q = \frac{\omega_0 L}{r} = \frac{1}{\omega_0 C r}$.
  • Bandwidth ($\Delta \omega$) of a parallel resonant circuit is inversely proportional to the $Q$-factor, given by $\Delta \omega = \frac{\omega_0}{Q} = \frac{r}{L}$.

Glossary

Dynamic Impedance ($Z_d$)The pure resistive impedance offered by a parallel resonant circuit at resonance, equal to $\frac{L}{Cr}$.
Rejector CircuitAnother name for a parallel resonant circuit as it offers maximum impedance and rejects/minimizes the line current at the resonant frequency.
Quality Factor ($Q$-factor)A dimensionless parameter representing the sharpness of resonance and the ratio of circulating tank current to the supply current.
Bandwidth ($\Delta \omega$)The range of frequencies over which the power of the resonant circuit drops to half of its maximum value, given by $\frac{r}{L}$.
Circulating Current ($I_{circ}$)The large reactive current that oscillates between the capacitor and inductor within the tank loop at resonance, approximately equal to $Q I_{supply}$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Why is a parallel resonance circuit commonly referred to as a rejector circuit?

Explanation: At resonance, a parallel LC circuit exhibits maximum dynamic impedance, which heavily restricts or 'rejects' the flow of line current from the source.

2. What is the condition for resonance in a general parallel AC circuit containing an inductor with resistance $r$ and a capacitor $C$?

Explanation: Resonance in a parallel circuit is formally defined as the condition where the reactive component of the total admittance becomes zero, ensuring unity power factor.

3. What is the formula for the dynamic impedance $Z_d$ of a practical parallel resonant circuit with a coil of resistance $r$?

Explanation: For a practical parallel resonant circuit where coil resistance $r$ is present, the dynamic impedance at resonance is given by $Z_d = \frac{L}{Cr}$.

4. What happens to the dynamic impedance of an ideal parallel resonant circuit when the coil resistance $r$ approaches zero?

Explanation: For an ideal coil with $r \approx 0$, the dynamic impedance $Z_d = \frac{L}{Cr}$ approaches infinity, thereby reducing the supply current to zero.

5. What is the resonant frequency formula for an ideal parallel LC circuit where the coil resistance $r = 0$?

Explanation: When coil resistance $r = 0$, the resonant frequency simplifies to the standard ideal expression $f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{LC}}$.

6. What is the condition under which real parallel resonance is physically possible when considering a practical coil of resistance $r$, inductance $L$, and capacitance $C$?

Explanation: For the term inside the square root of the practical resonant frequency formula to be real and positive, the condition $r^2 < \frac{L}{C}$ must be satisfied.

7. How is the Quality factor ($Q$-factor) of a parallel resonant circuit expressed in terms of circuit parameters $\omega_0$, $L$, and $r$?

Explanation: The $Q$-factor represents the ratio of the reactive inductive reactance to the series resistance of the coil at resonance, given by $Q = \frac{\omega_0 L}{r}$.

8. What is the relationship between Bandwidth ($\Delta \omega$), resonant angular frequency ($\omega_0$), and the $Q$-factor in a parallel resonant circuit?

Explanation: The bandwidth is inversely proportional to the quality factor, expressed as $\Delta \omega = \frac{\omega_0}{Q} = \frac{r}{L}$.

9. What is the magnitude of the supply current $I_0$ drawn from the source at parallel resonance for a circuit with dynamic impedance $Z_d$ and applied voltage $V$?

Explanation: Since the circuit presents a maximum dynamic impedance $Z_d = \frac{L}{Cr}$ at resonance, the supply current is minimum and given by $I_0 = \frac{V}{Z_d} = \frac{VCr}{L}$.

10. Which of the following correctly describes the circulating current ($I_{circ}$) in the tank loop compared to the supply current at parallel resonance for a high $Q$ circuit?

Explanation: The circulating reactive current in the LC tank is magnified by a factor of $Q$ compared to the main supply current, i.e., $I_{circ} \approx Q I_{supply}$.

11. What is the power factor of a parallel resonant tank circuit at the exact resonant frequency?

Explanation: At resonance, the reactive components cancel out (net admittance has zero imaginary part), leaving pure resistance, which corresponds to a unity power factor.

12. If the capacitance $C$ of a tank circuit is quadrupled ($4C$) while keeping inductance $L$ constant, what happens to the ideal resonant frequency $f_0$?

Explanation: Since $f_0 \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{C}}$, increasing $C$ by a factor of 4 reduces the resonant frequency by a factor of $\sqrt{4} = 2$, meaning it is halved.

13. What is the unit of total admittance ($Y$) in an AC parallel circuit?

Explanation: Admittance is the reciprocal of impedance, and its SI unit is Siemens (S) or mho ($\Omega^{-1}$).

14. A common misconception is that parallel resonance circuits have minimum impedance at resonance. What is the correct physical behavior?

Explanation: Unlike series circuits which exhibit minimum impedance at resonance, parallel resonance circuits function as rejector circuits exhibiting maximum impedance ($Z_d$).

15. How does increasing the coil resistance $r$ affect the sharpness of the resonance curve and the $Q$-factor of a parallel tank circuit?

Explanation: Since $Q = \frac{\omega_0 L}{r}$, a higher resistance $r$ lowers the $Q$-factor, which directly results in a broader, less sharp resonance curve.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. In a parallel LC tank circuit, if the resistance of the inductive coil $r$ is taken into account, the resonant frequency $f_0$ is:

Explanation: The practical frequency formula $f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \frac{r^2}{L^2}}$ shows that the presence of the $-\frac{r^2}{L^2}$ term makes the practical resonant frequency lower than the ideal $\frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{LC}}$.

2. A parallel resonant circuit is connected across an AC source of constant voltage $V$. If the frequency of the source is set equal to the resonant frequency $f_0$, what is the phase angle between the voltage and the total supply current?

Explanation: At resonance, the reactive admittance components cancel out, leaving a pure resistance. Thus, the total supply current is strictly in phase with the applied voltage (power factor = 1).

3. If the inductance $L$ of a tank circuit is $0.1\text{ H}$ and capacitance $C = 10\mu\text{F}$ with negligible coil resistance, what is the ideal resonant frequency?

Explanation: $f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{0.1 \times 10 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{2\pi \times 10^{-3}} = \frac{1000}{2\pi} = \frac{500}{\pi}$? Wait: $0.1 \times 10 \times 10^{-6} = 10^{-6}$, square root is $10^{-3}$. $\frac{1}{2\pi \times 10^{-3}} = \frac{1000}{2\pi} = \frac{500}{\pi}\text{ Hz}$. Let's verify option values: $\frac{500}{\pi}$ isn't listed, but let's check values: $L=0.1\text{ H}$, $C=10\mu\text{F}$. $LC = 10^{-6}$, $\sqrt{LC} = 10^{-3}$. $f_0 = \frac{10^3}{2\pi} = \frac{500}{\pi}$.

4. What physical role does the $Q$-factor play regarding energy in a parallel resonant circuit?

Explanation: The fundamental energy-based definition of the quality factor $Q$ is $2\pi$ times the ratio of maximum energy stored in the tank to the energy dissipated per cycle.

5. In a parallel resonant circuit, if the coil resistance $r$ is increased while keeping $L$ and $C$ constant, how does the dynamic impedance $Z_d$ change?

Explanation: Since dynamic impedance is given by $Z_d = \frac{L}{Cr}$, $Z_d$ is inversely proportional to the coil resistance $r$. Increasing $r$ decreases $Z_d$.

6. Why are parallel resonant circuits extensively used in radio transmitter output stages and local oscillators?

Explanation: Their ability to offer extremely high impedance at the tuned resonant frequency and reject unwanted frequencies makes them ideal for frequency selection and tuning in communication systems.

7. For a parallel resonant circuit, what is the expression for the angular resonant frequency $\omega_0$ in the ideal case ($r = 0$)?

Explanation: Equating inductive and capacitive reactances or setting zero imaginary admittance for $r=0$ yields $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$.

8. If the supply voltage applied to a parallel tank circuit at resonance is $V = 20\text{ V}$, $L = 10\text{ mH}$, $C = 1\mu\text{F}$, and $r = 10\Omega$, what is the supply current $I_0$?

Explanation: Dynamic impedance $Z_d = \frac{L}{Cr} = \frac{10 \times 10^{-3}}{1\mu\text{F} \times 10} = \frac{10^{-2}}{10^{-6} \times 10} = \frac{10^{-2}}{10^{-5}} = 1000\Omega$. Supply current $I_0 = \frac{V}{Z_d} = \frac{20}{1000} = 0.02\text{ A}$.

9. What is the effect on the bandwidth of a parallel resonant circuit if its $Q$-factor is doubled?

Explanation: Since bandwidth is inversely proportional to the $Q$-factor ($\Delta \omega = \frac{\omega_0}{Q}$), doubling the $Q$-factor halves the bandwidth, making the resonance peak sharper.

10. Which of the following conditions must be met for a parallel LC circuit to function effectively as a high-selectivity rejector circuit?

Explanation: A high $Q$-factor (achieved with low coil resistance $r$) ensures a high dynamic impedance, narrow bandwidth, and sharp frequency selectivity.
