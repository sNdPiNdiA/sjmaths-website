Parallel LCR Circuit

A parallel LCR circuit is an alternating current network where an inductor, a capacitor, and a resistor are connected in parallel across a sinusoidal voltage source. It functions as a rejector circuit because its impedance is maximum and the current is minimum at resonance.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Conditions for parallel resonance, resonance frequency formula, phase relationship between voltage and branch currents, and basic numericals on impedance and current at resonance.

PGT extension

Admittance and susceptance analysis, dynamic resistance at resonance, quality factor derivation, sharpness of resonance, and bandwidth in parallel resonant circuits.

Prerequisites

  • Alternating Current and L-C-R Circuits
  • Complex numbers and phasor algebra
  • Concept of admittance, conductance, and susceptance in AC circuits

Circuit Configuration and Phasor Relations

  • In a parallel LCR circuit, a pure resistor of resistance R, a pure inductor of inductance L, and an ideal capacitor of capacitance C are connected in parallel across an AC source of voltage V = V0sin(ωt).
  • The alternating voltage across each parallel branch is the same, whereas the branch currents (IR, IL, IC) differ in phase relative to the common voltage.
  • The current through the resistor (IR) is in phase with the voltage, the current through the inductor (IL) lags the voltage by 90°, and the current through the capacitor (IC) leads the voltage by 90°.

Parallel Resonance Phenomenon

  • Parallel resonance occurs when the reactive component of the total current is zero, meaning the inductive susceptance equals the capacitive susceptance.
  • At resonance, the reactive branch currents (IL and IC) are equal in magnitude and opposite in phase, completely canceling each other out in the external circuit.
  • Consequently, the total line current drawn from the source is solely determined by the resistive branch and is at its minimum value, making the circuit impedance maximum.

Dynamic Resistance at Resonance

  • At resonance, the parallel LCR circuit acts as a pure resistance known as the dynamic resistance or rejector resistance.
  • The expression for dynamic resistance is given by Rd = L / (CR), which can also be written as Zmax = L / (CR).
  • Since L, C, and R are typical laboratory values, the dynamic resistance can be very large, resulting in a very small total current at resonance.

Quality Factor (Q-factor) and Bandwidth

  • The quality factor Q of a parallel LCR circuit is defined as the ratio of the circulating reactive current in the LC tank to the total supply current at resonance.
  • It is also expressed as Q = ωrCR = R / (ωrL), which is the inverse of the series LCR circuit Q-factor expression.
  • The bandwidth (BW) of the resonance curve is given by BW = fr / Q = 1 / (2πRC), determining the selectivity of the circuit.

Derivations

Resonance Frequency and Impedance of Parallel LCR Circuit

Conditions: Ideal parallel LCR circuit connected to an AC source of angular frequency &omega;

  • Write the admittance Y of the parallel combination: Y = G + j(BC - BL), where conductance G = 1/R, capacitive susceptance BC = ωC, and inductive susceptance BL = 1/(ωL).
  • Substitute the terms into the admittance equation: Y = (1/R) + j(ωC - 1/(ωL)).
  • The total impedance Z is the reciprocal of admittance: Z = 1 / sqrt((1/R²) + (ωC - 1/(ωL))²).
  • At resonance, the imaginary part of admittance becomes zero: ωrC - 1/(ωrL) = 0.
  • Solve for the resonant angular frequency: ωr = 1 / sqrt(LC).

Result: Resonant frequency f_r = (1 / 2π) * sqrt(1 / LC) and maximum impedance Z_max = L / (CR)

Derivation of Quality Factor (Q-factor)

Conditions: Parallel LCR circuit at resonance

  • Define Q-factor as the ratio of magnitude of inductive (or capacitive) current to the total supply current at resonance: Q = IC / IR.
  • Express the currents in terms of voltage V: IC = V(ωrC) and IR = V / R.
  • Substitute the currents into the Q-factor ratio: Q = V(ωrC) / (V / R) = ωrCR.
  • Substitute ωr = 1 / sqrt(LC) into the equation: Q = (1 / sqrt(LC)) * CR = R * sqrt(C / L).

Result: Q = ω_rCR = R * sqrt(C / L)

Draw: A bell-shaped curve plotting Impedance (Z) on the y-axis against Frequency (f) on the x-axis, showing a sharp peak at f = f_r.

Interpret: Impedance reaches its maximum value (Z_max = L/CR) at the resonant frequency f_r, causing the circuit to reject currents of this frequency.

Exam use: Tested via conceptual MCQs on why parallel LCR circuits are called rejector circuits.

Frequency Response: Current vs Frequency

Draw: A dip-shaped curve plotting Line Current (I) on the y-axis against Frequency (f) on the x-axis, showing a sharp minimum at f = f_r.

Interpret: Line current drops to a minimum value (I = V/Z_max) at resonance because inductive and capacitive susceptances cancel each other out.

Exam use: Used to evaluate understanding of minimum current conditions in rejector circuits.

Phase Angle vs Frequency Curve

Draw: A sigmoid-like curve plotting phase angle (&phi;) on the y-axis against frequency (f) on the x-axis, crossing zero at f_r.

Interpret: Below resonance (&omega; < &omega;<sub>r</sub>), the circuit is inductive (&phi; < 0); at resonance, &phi; = 0; above resonance (&omega; > &omega;<sub>r</sub>), the circuit is capacitive (&phi; > 0).

Exam use: Asked in PGT questions regarding the inductive and capacitive behavior of the circuit off-resonance.

Solved numericals

TGT core

A parallel LCR circuit has an inductor of 0.2 H, a capacitor of 5 &mu;F, and a resistor of 100 &Omega; connected across a 220 V AC source. Calculate the resonant frequency of the circuit.

  • Identify given parameters: L = 0.2 H, C = 5 × 10-6 F, R = 100 Ω.
  • Recall the resonance frequency formula: f_r = (1 / 2π) * sqrt(1 / LC).
  • Calculate LC product: LC = 0.2 × 5 × 10-6 = 1 × 10-6 s2.
  • Calculate square root of LC: sqrt(LC) = sqrt(1 × 10-6) = 1 × 10-3 s.
  • Substitute into the frequency formula: f_r = 1 / (2 × 3.1416 × 1 × 10-3).
  • Compute final value: f_r = 1000 / 6.2832 = 159.15 Hz.

Answer: 159.15 Hz

PGT extension

Find the dynamic resistance and the minimum current at resonance for a parallel LCR circuit with L = 2 H, C = 0.5 &mu;F, and R = 50 &Omega;, when driven by a 100 V AC source.

  • Identify given parameters: L = 2 H, C = 0.5 × 10-6 F, R = 50 Ω, V = 100 V.
  • Recall the formula for dynamic resistance: Zmax = L / (CR).
  • Substitute the values: Zmax = 2 / (0.5 × 10-6 × 50).
  • Simplify the denominator: 0.5 × 50 = 25, so denominator = 25 × 10-6.
  • Calculate Zmax: Zmax = 2 / (25 × 10-6) = 0.08 × 106 = 80,000 Ω = 80 kΩ.
  • Calculate minimum line current at resonance: Imin = V / Zmax = 100 / 80000 = 1.25 × 10-3 A.

Answer: Dynamic resistance = 80 kΩ, Minimum current = 1.25 mA

PGT extension

Calculate the quality factor and bandwidth of a parallel LCR circuit having L = 1 H, C = 1 &mu;F, and R = 1000 &Omega; at resonance.

  • Identify given parameters: L = 1 H, C = 1 × 10-6 F, R = 1000 Ω.
  • Calculate resonant frequency fr: fr = 1 / (2π sqrt(LC)) = 1 / (2 × 3.1416 × sqrt(1 × 1 × 10-6)) = 159.15 Hz.
  • Calculate Q-factor using Q = R * sqrt(C / L): Q = 1000 * sqrt(1 × 10-6 / 1) = 1000 × 10-3 = 1.
  • Alternatively, calculate Q via ωrCR: Q = (2 × 3.1416 × 159.15) × 1000 × 1 × 10-6 = 1.
  • Calculate bandwidth: Δf = fr / Q = 159.15 / 1 = 159.15 Hz.

Answer: Q = 1, Bandwidth = 159.15 Hz

Assumptions and limitations

  • The inductor is assumed to have negligible resistance, or its resistance is lumped into the total branch resistance R.
  • The source is assumed to be an ideal sinusoidal voltage source with zero internal resistance.
  • The formulas for parallel resonance assume high Q-factor approximations (ωr ≈ 1 / sqrt(LC)) where exact resonance and anti-resonance frequencies converge.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Parallel LCR circuits have maximum current at resonance.

Correct: Parallel LCR circuits have minimum current at resonance.

At resonance, the impedance is maximum (dynamic resistance), which minimizes the total line current drawn from the source.

Common error: The resonance frequency formula for parallel LCR is different from series LCR.

Correct: The ideal resonance frequency formula f_r = 1 / (2&pi;sqrt(LC)) is identical for both ideal series and parallel LCR circuits.

Both circuits achieve resonance when the net reactive susceptance or reactance becomes zero, yielding the same unconditioned angular frequency expression.

Common error: Branch currents are zero at parallel resonance.

Correct: Only the net external line current is minimum; individual branch currents (I<sub>L</sub> and I<sub>C</sub>) can be very large.

A large circulating current flows between the inductor and capacitor inside the parallel tank circuit due to energy oscillation.

Quick revision

  • A parallel LCR circuit acts as a rejector circuit because its impedance is maximum at resonance.
  • At parallel resonance, total line current is minimum and in phase with the applied voltage (φ = 0).
  • Dynamic resistance of a parallel LCR circuit is given by Zmax = L / (CR).
  • The quality factor Q for a parallel LCR circuit is expressed as Q = ωrCR = R * sqrt(C / L).

Must remember

  • In a parallel LCR circuit, the alternating voltage across each parallel branch is the same, whereas branch currents differ in phase.
  • Parallel resonance occurs when the reactive component of the total current is zero, meaning inductive susceptance equals capacitive susceptance.
  • At resonance, total line current drawn from the source is at its minimum value, and the circuit impedance is at its maximum.
  • The dynamic resistance (or rejector resistance) at resonance is given by $R_d = \frac{L}{CR}$.
  • The Quality factor ($Q$) of a parallel LCR circuit is given by $Q = \omega_r CR = R \sqrt{\frac{C}{L}}$, which is the inverse of the series LCR Q-factor expression.
  • The bandwidth of a parallel resonant circuit is given by $\Delta f = \frac{f_r}{Q} = \frac{1}{2\pi RC}$.

Glossary

Parallel ResonanceCondition in a parallel LCR circuit where the reactive branch currents ($I_L$ and $I_C$) cancel out, resulting in minimum line current and maximum impedance.
Dynamic ResistanceThe equivalent pure resistance offered by a parallel LCR circuit at resonance, expressed as $Z_{max} = \frac{L}{CR}$.
Admittance ($Y$)The reciprocal of impedance, representing how easily a circuit permits current flow, expressed as $Y = \sqrt{\frac{1}{R^2} + \left(\omega C - \frac{1}{\omega L}\right)^2}$.
Rejector CircuitAnother name for a parallel LCR circuit because it offers maximum impedance at resonance and rejects (minimizes) the current at the resonant frequency.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the phase relationship between the alternating voltage and the current through the pure resistor in a parallel LCR circuit connected to $V = $V_{0}$ \sin(\$\omega$ t)$?

Explanation: In a pure resistive branch, the current is always in phase with the applied alternating voltage.

2. In a parallel LCR circuit, the current through the inductor ($$I_{L}$$) relative to the source voltage:

Explanation: In a pure inductive branch, the alternating current lags the voltage by $90^\circ$.

3. What happens to the total line current drawn from the source in a parallel LCR circuit at resonance?

Explanation: At parallel resonance, reactive branch currents cancel out, resulting in maximum impedance and minimum total line current.

4. What is the expression for the resonant frequency ($f_r$) of an ideal parallel LCR circuit?

Explanation: The resonant frequency formula is $f_r = \frac{1}{2\$\pi$ \sqrt{LC}}$, which is identical to that of a series LCR circuit.

5. What is the dynamic resistance ($Z_{max}$) of a parallel LCR circuit at resonance?

Explanation: At parallel resonance, the dynamic resistance (or rejector resistance) is given by $Z_{max} = \frac{L}{CR}$.

6. Why is a parallel LCR circuit referred to as a 'rejector circuit'?

Explanation: Because it offers maximum impedance at resonance, it impedes (rejects) the flow of total line current at the resonant frequency.

7. Which of the following represents the correct expression for the total admittance ($Y$) of a parallel LCR circuit?

Explanation: Admittance is the reciprocal of impedance. For parallel branches with conductance $G = \frac{1}{R}$ and net susceptance $B = \$\omega$ C - \frac{1}{\$\omega$ L}$, $Y = \sqrt{G^2 + B^2}$.

8. What is the Quality factor ($Q$) expression for a parallel LCR circuit?

Explanation: The Q-factor of a parallel LCR circuit is given by $Q = \$\omega_{r}$ CR = R\sqrt{\frac{C}{L}}$, which is the inverse of the series LCR Q-factor expression.

9. How is the bandwidth ($\Delta f$) of a parallel LCR resonance curve related to its resonant frequency and Q-factor?

Explanation: The bandwidth of resonance is given by $\Delta f = \frac{f_r}{Q} = \frac{1}{2\$\pi$ RC}$.

10. What is the phase angle ($\$\phi$$) between the voltage and total current in a parallel LCR circuit at resonance?

Explanation: At resonance, the reactive components cancel out entirely, making the circuit purely resistive, so the phase angle $\$\phi$ = 0^\circ$.

11. What is the minimum line current ($I_{min}$) drawn from the source at parallel resonance for a voltage $V$?

Explanation: The minimum line current is given by $I_{min} = \frac{V}{Z_{max}} = \frac{VCR}{L}$.

12. Which of the following branch currents is in quadrature (phase difference of $90^\circ$) leading the voltage in a parallel LCR circuit?

Explanation: The current through an ideal capacitor ($$I_{C}$$) leads the alternating voltage by $90^\circ$.

13. If the inductance $L$ and capacitance $C$ of a parallel LCR circuit are both doubled, the resonant frequency $f_r$ will:

Explanation: Since $f_r = \frac{1}{2\$\pi$ \sqrt{LC}}$, if both $L$ and $C$ are multiplied by 2, the product $LC$ increases by a factor of 4, meaning the frequency becomes half ($\frac{1}{2}$).

14. What physical phenomenon occurs inside the parallel tank circuit (LC combination) at resonance, despite the external line current being minimal?

Explanation: Large reactive branch currents ($$I_{L}$$ and $$I_{C}$$) circulate within the LC tank due to energy oscillation, even though they cancel each other out in the external line feed.

15. What are the SI units of electrical admittance ($Y$)?

Explanation: Admittance is the inverse of impedance, measured in Siemens (S) or ohm$^{-1}$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. In a parallel LCR circuit, the condition for resonance is that the reactive component of the total current is zero. This implies:

Explanation: Parallel resonance occurs when inductive susceptance equals capacitive susceptance, i.e., $\$\omega$ C = \frac{1}{\$\omega$ L}$ (or equivalently, inductive reactance equals capacitive reactance $\$\omega$ L = \frac{1}{\$\omega$ C}$).

2. A parallel LCR circuit has $L = 2\text{ H}$, $C = 32\mu\text{F}$, and $R = 10\,\$\omega$$. What is the dynamic resistance of the circuit at resonance?

Explanation: Dynamic resistance $Z_{max} = \frac{L}{CR} = \frac{2}{(32 \times 10^{-6}) \times 10} = \frac{2}{3.2 \times 10^{-4}} = 6.25 \times 10^3\,\$\omega$ = 6.25\, k\$\omega$$.

3. Which of the following statements is INCORRECT regarding a parallel LCR resonant circuit?

Explanation: At resonance, the circuit is purely resistive ($\$\phi$ = 0^\circ$), so the power factor is $\cos(0^\circ) = 1$ (unity), not zero.

4. The Q-factor of a parallel LCR circuit is given by $Q = 50$. If the resonant frequency is $10\text{ kHz}$, what is the bandwidth of the circuit?

Explanation: Bandwidth $\Delta f = \frac{f_r}{Q} = \frac{10000}{50} = 200\text{ Hz}$.

5. How does the resonant frequency of an ideal parallel LCR circuit change if the resistance $R$ of the resistor branch is increased?

Explanation: The ideal resonant frequency formula is $f_r = \frac{1}{2\$\pi$ \sqrt{LC}}$, which is independent of resistance $R$ in the idealized circuit model.

6. In a parallel LCR circuit at resonance, the branch currents $$I_{L}$$ and $$I_{C}$$ are:

Explanation: At parallel resonance, inductive and capacitive susceptances are equal, meaning $$I_{L}$$ and $$I_{C}$$ are equal in magnitude and $180^\circ$ out of phase, canceling each other out.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What is the primary operational characteristic that distinguishes a parallel LCR circuit from a series LCR circuit?

Explanation: A parallel LCR circuit acts as a rejector circuit with maximum impedance at resonance, whereas a series LCR circuit acts as an acceptor circuit with minimum impedance.

2. If a parallel LCR circuit has $L = 0.5\text{ H}$ and $C = 2\,\mu\text{F}$, what is its resonant angular frequency ($\$\omega_{r}$$)?

Explanation: $\$\omega_{r}$ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.5 \times 2 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{10^{-6}}} = 10^3 = 1000\text{ rad/s}$.

3. The sharpness of resonance in a parallel LCR circuit is directly determined by:

Explanation: The Q-factor determines the sharpness of the resonance peak and selectivity of the circuit.

4. In terms of susceptance $B_L$ and $B_C$, the parallel resonance condition can be written as:

Explanation: Parallel resonance requires that the inductive susceptance ($B_L = \frac{1}{\$\omega$ L}$) equals the capacitive susceptance ($B_C = \$\omega$ C$), so $B_L = B_C$.

5. If the capacitance of a parallel resonant circuit is quadrupled (multiplied by 4), how does the dynamic resistance $Z_{max}$ change, assuming $L$ and $R$ are constant?

Explanation: Since $Z_{max} = \frac{L}{CR}$, if $C$ is multiplied by 4, the dynamic resistance becomes $\frac{1}{4}$ of its initial value.

6. What is the power factor of a parallel LCR circuit when operating at frequencies higher than the resonant frequency $f_r$?

Explanation: For $f > f_r$, capacitive susceptance exceeds inductive susceptance ($\$\omega$ C > \frac{1}{\$\omega$ L}$), making the circuit capacitive, so the total current leads the voltage (leading power factor).

8. Which component in a parallel LCR circuit introduces electrical conductance ($G = \frac{1}{R}$)?

Explanation: The pure resistor branch provides conductance $G = \frac{1}{R}$ in parallel admittance calculations.

9. At a frequency below parallel resonance, the parallel LCR circuit behaves predominantly as:

Explanation: At frequencies below resonance, inductive susceptance dominates over capacitive susceptance ($\frac{1}{\$\omega$ L} > \$\omega$ C$), making the circuit inductive (lagging current).

10. If an AC source of constant RMS voltage $V$ supplies a parallel LCR circuit at resonance, the minimum line current depends directly on:

Explanation: Since $I_{min} = \frac{VCR}{L}$, the minimum current depends on the voltage and the circuit parameter ratio $\frac{CR}{L}$.
