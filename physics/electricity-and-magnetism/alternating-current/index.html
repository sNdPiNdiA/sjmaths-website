Alternating Current and L-C-R Circuits

Alternating current refers to an electrical current that reverses direction periodically, typically sinusoidally with time. It is fundamentally characterized by parameters such as root-mean-square value, frequency, and phase relationships across passive circuit elements.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Mean and RMS values of AC, pure resistor, inductor, and capacitor in AC circuits, series LCR resonance, quality factor, and power in AC circuits.

Prerequisites

  • Electromagnetic induction and Faraday's laws
  • Self and mutual inductance, capacitance
  • Complex numbers and trigonometric identities

Mean and Root Mean Square (RMS) Values of Alternating Current

  • The mean or average value of a sinusoidal alternating current over a complete cycle is zero due to equal positive and negative half-cycles.
  • The mean value over a half-cycle is given by Imean = (2I0)/π ≈ 0.637I0, where I0 is the peak current.
  • The root-mean-square (RMS) or virtual value is defined as the square root of the mean of squared instantaneous values, given by Irms = I0/&sqrt;2 ≈ 0.707I0.

AC through Pure R, L, and C Circuits

  • In a pure resistor, alternating voltage and current are in phase, with impedance Z = R.
  • In a pure inductor of inductance L, the alternating voltage leads the current by a phase angle of π/2 radians, and the inductive reactance is XL = ωL.
  • In a pure capacitor of capacitance C, the alternating current leads the voltage by π/2 radians, and the capacitive reactance is XC = 1/(ωC).

Series LCR Circuit and Resonance

  • In a series combination of R, L, and C connected to an AC source, the total impedance is Z = √[R2 + (XL - XC)2].
  • Resonance occurs when the inductive reactance equals the capacitive reactance (XL = XC), minimizing impedance to Z = R.
  • The resonant angular frequency is given by ω0 = 1/√(LC), at which the current is maximum and in phase with the applied voltage.

Quality Factor (Q-factor) and Power in AC Circuits

  • The Quality factor (Q-factor) measures the sharpness of resonance, defined as the ratio of voltage across L or C to the applied voltage at resonance: Q = (ω0L)/R.
  • The true power dissipated in an AC circuit is given by Pavg = Vrms Irms cos φ, where cos φ is the power factor.
  • Power factor can be improved by connecting a suitable capacitor in parallel with inductive loads.

Derivations

Impedance and Phase Angle of a Series LCR Circuit

Conditions: AC voltage source v = V<sub>0</sub> sin(&omega;t) connected in series with R, L, and C.

  • Write the voltage equations across each component: VR = I R (in phase with I), VL = I XL (leads I by 90°), VC = I XC (lags I by 90°).
  • Use phasor addition for the total voltage: V02 = VR2 + (VL - VC)2 = (I0R)2 + (I0XL - I0XC)2.
  • Factor out I02 to express total impedance Z = V0 / I0 = √[R2 + (XL - XC)2].
  • Determine the phase angle φ between voltage and current using tan φ = (XL - XC) / R.

Result: Z = √[R2 + (ωL - 1/(ωC))2] and φ = tan-1[(ωL - 1/(ωC))/R]

Resonant Frequency and Bandwidth of Series LCR Circuit

Conditions: Series LCR circuit driven at varying frequencies with constant voltage amplitude.

  • Condition for resonance is when reactive terms cancel out: XL = XC.
  • Substitute expressions: ω0L = 1 / (ω0C).
  • Solve for resonant angular frequency ω0: ω02 = 1 / (LC).
  • Define half-power frequencies where current drops to 1/√2 of its maximum value, yielding bandwidth Δω = R / L.

Result: f0 = 1 / (2π√(LC)) and Q = ω0 / Δω = (ω0L) / R

Solved numericals

TGT core

A 100 &Omega; resistor is connected to a 220 V, 50 Hz AC source. Calculate the RMS value of current and the peak current.

  • Identify given values: Vrms = 220 V, R = 100 &Omega.
  • Calculate RMS current: Irms = Vrms / R = 220 / 100 = 2.2 A.
  • Calculate peak current: I0 = Irms × √2 = 2.2 × 1.414 = 3.11 A.

Answer: Irms = 2.2 A, I0 = 3.11 A

TGT core

An inductor of inductance 0.5 H and a resistor of 200 &Omega; are connected in series to a 240 V, 50 Hz AC supply. Find the maximum current in the circuit.

  • Calculate inductive reactance XL = 2 π f L = 2 × (22/7) × 50 × 0.5 = 157.1 Ω.
  • Calculate total impedance Z = √(R2 + XL2) = √(2002 + 157.12) = √(40000 + 24680) = √64680 = 254.3 Ω.
  • Calculate peak voltage V0 = Vrms × √2 = 240 × 1.414 = 339.4 V.
  • Calculate peak current I0 = V0 / Z = 339.4 / 254.3 = 1.33 A.

Answer: I0 = 1.33 A

PGT extension

A series LCR circuit with L = 2.0 H, C = 32 &mu;F, and R = 10 &Omega; is connected to a 200 V variable frequency AC source. Find (a) the resonant frequency, (b) the Q-factor of the circuit, and (c) the bandwidth.

  • (a) Resonant angular frequency ω0 = 1 / √(LC) = 1 / √(2.0 × 32 × 10-6) = 1 / √(64 × 10-6) = 1 / (8 × 10-3) = 125 rad/s. Resonant frequency f0 = ω0 / (2π) = 125 / (2 × 3.1416) = 19.89 Hz.
  • (b) Q-factor Q = (ω0 L) / R = (125 × 2.0) / 10 = 25.
  • (c) Bandwidth Δω = R / L = 10 / 2.0 = 5 rad/s.

Answer: (a) 19.89 Hz, (b) Q = 25, (c) Bandwidth = 5 rad/s

Assumptions and limitations

  • Components (R, L, C) are assumed to be ideal, linear, and constant with respect to temperature and frequency unless specified otherwise.
  • The source EMF is assumed to be purely sinusoidal.
  • Stray capacitances and lead resistances are neglected in theoretical derivations.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The average power consumed by a pure inductor or pure capacitor in an AC circuit is zero.

Correct: The average power consumed over a complete cycle by pure L and pure C is indeed zero, but reactive power oscillates between the source and the field.

The phase angle between voltage and current is 90&deg;, making cos(&pi;/2) = 0.

Common error: At resonance in a series LCR circuit, the voltage across the inductor or capacitor is zero.

Correct: At resonance, the net reactive voltage (V<sub>L</sub> - V<sub>C</sub>) is zero, but individual voltages V<sub>L</sub> and V<sub>C</sub> can be very large depending on the Q-factor.

V<sub>L</sub> and V<sub>C</sub> are 180&deg; out of phase with each other and cancel out externally, but individually maintain potential drops equal to Q times the supply voltage.

Quick revision

  • In pure R, voltage and current are in phase; in L, voltage leads current by 90°; in C, current leads voltage by 90°.
  • Series resonance condition: XL = XC, Z = R, and current is maximum.
  • Power factor cos φ = R / Z; true power P = Vrms Irms cos φ.

Must remember

  • The mean or average value of a sinusoidal alternating current over a complete cycle is zero, while over a half-cycle it is I_mean = 2I_0 / pi approx 0.637 I_0.
  • The RMS (virtual) value of sinusoidal AC is given by I_rms = I_0 / sqrt(2) approx 0.707 I_0.
  • In a pure resistive AC circuit, voltage and current are in phase; in a pure inductive circuit, voltage leads current by pi/2 radians; in a pure capacitive circuit, current leads voltage by pi/2 radians.
  • Impedance of a series LCR circuit is Z = sqrt(R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2) where X_L = omega L and X_C = 1 / (omega C).
  • Resonance in a series LCR circuit occurs when X_L = X_C, yielding minimum impedance Z = R and maximum current.
  • The resonant angular frequency of a series LCR circuit is omega_0 = 1 / sqrt(LC), and the resonant frequency is f_0 = 1 / (2 pi sqrt(LC)).
  • The Quality factor (Q-factor) of a series LCR circuit represents the sharpness of resonance and is defined as Q = (omega_0 L) / R = 1 / (omega_0 C R).
  • The average power dissipated in an AC circuit is P_avg = V_rms * I_rms * cos(phi), where cos(phi) is the power factor equal to R / Z.

Glossary

Alternating Current (AC)An electric current that reverses direction periodically, typically sinusoidally in standard applications.
RMS ValueThe root-mean-square or virtual value of an alternating quantity, representing its equivalent DC heating effect.
Inductive Reactance (X_L)The opposition offered by a pure inductor to the flow of alternating current, given by X_L = omega L.
Capacitive Reactance (X_C)The opposition offered by a pure capacitor to the flow of alternating current, given by X_C = 1 / (omega C).
Impedance (Z)The total effective opposition offered by an LCR circuit to the flow of alternating current, measured in ohms.
ResonanceA condition in an AC circuit where inductive reactance equals capacitive reactance, resulting in minimum impedance and maximum current.
Power Factor (cos phi)The ratio of true power to apparent power in an AC circuit, mathematically expressed as R / Z.
Quality Factor (Q-factor)A dimensionless parameter that characterizes the resonance sharpness and voltage magnification of a resonant circuit.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the mean value of a sinusoidal alternating current over a complete cycle?

Explanation: Over a complete cycle, the positive and negative half-cycles of a sinusoidal AC cancel each other out, making the net mean value zero.

2. The root-mean-square (RMS) value of an alternating current of peak value $I_{0}$ is:

Explanation: By definition, the RMS value for a sinusoidal waveform is $I_{rms}$ = $I_{0}$ / sqrt(2) approx 0.707 $I_{0}$.

3. In a pure inductive circuit, what is the phase relationship between the alternating voltage and current?

Explanation: In a pure inductor, the back-emf causes the alternating voltage to lead the current by a phase angle of $\pi$/2 radians.

4. What is the formula for inductive reactance?

Explanation: Inductive reactance is given by $X_{L}$ = $\omega$ L = 2 $\pi$ f L.

5. How does capacitive reactance vary with the frequency of the AC source?

Explanation: Capacitive reactance is $X_{C}$ = 1 / ($\omega$ C) = 1 / (2 $\pi$ f C), which shows it is inversely proportional to the frequency f.

6. What is the total impedance of a series LCR circuit?

Explanation: In a series LCR circuit, the net reactance is ($X_{L}$ - $X_{C}$), and combining it orthogonally with resistance R gives Z = sqrt(R^2 + ($X_{L}$ - $X_{C}$)^2).

7. At resonance in a series LCR circuit, the impedance of the circuit is:

Explanation: At resonance, $X_{L}$ = $X_{C}$, so the impedance expression reduces to Z = sqrt(R^2 + 0) = R, which is the minimum possible impedance.

8. What is the resonant angular frequency $\omega_{0}$ of a series LCR circuit?

Explanation: Resonance occurs when $\omega$ L = 1 / ($\omega$ C), which solves for angular frequency as $\omega_{0}$ = 1 / sqrt(LC).

9. The power factor of an AC circuit is defined as:

Explanation: The power factor is cos($\phi$) = R / Z in a series LCR circuit.

10. What is the average power dissipated in a pure inductor connected to an AC source over a complete cycle?

Explanation: For a pure inductor, the phase angle between voltage and current is $\pi$/2, so cos($\pi$/2) = 0, leading to zero average power dissipation.

11. Which of the following expressions represents the Quality factor (Q-factor) of a series LCR circuit?

Explanation: The Q-factor is defined as the ratio of the voltage across L or C to the applied voltage at resonance, which simplifies to Q = ($\omega_{0}$ L) / R.

12. How can the power factor of an inductive AC circuit be improved?

Explanation: Connecting a suitable capacitor in parallel provides leading reactive current that neutralizes the lagging reactive current of the inductive load, thereby improving the power factor.

13. What happens to the current in a series LCR circuit when it is tuned to resonance?

Explanation: At resonance, impedance is minimum (Z = R), causing the current to reach its maximum possible value, and since $X_{L}$ = $X_{C}$, the current is completely in phase with the applied voltage.

14. If the frequency of an AC source connected to a capacitor is doubled, what happens to the capacitive reactance?

Explanation: Since $X_{C}$ is inversely proportional to frequency f ($X_{C}$ = 1 / (2 $\pi$ f C)), doubling the frequency halves the capacitive reactance.

15. In a parallel LCR circuit, resonance is typically characterized by:

Explanation: Unlike series resonance where impedance is minimum, parallel resonance (rejector circuit) is characterized by maximum impedance and minimum line current.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. An alternating current is given by I = $I_{1}$ cos($\omega$ t) + $I_{2}$ sin($\omega$ t). The RMS value of this current is:

Explanation: For two orthogonal sinusoidal components, the peak resultant current is $I_{0}$ = sqrt($I_{1}^{2}$ + $I_{2}^{2}$). The RMS value is therefore $I_{0}$ / sqrt(2) = sqrt($I_{1}^{2}$ + $I_{2}^{2}$) / sqrt(2).

2. In a series LCR circuit connected to an AC source, the voltages across the resistor, inductor, and capacitor are 40 V, 60 V, and 30 V respectively. What is the applied RMS voltage?

Explanation: Applied voltage $V_{rms}$ = sqrt($V_{R}^{2}$ + ($V_{L}$ - $V_{C}$)^2) = sqrt(40^2 + (60 - 30)^2) = sqrt(40^2 + 30^2) = 50 V.

3. The sharpness of resonance in a series LCR circuit is measured by its Q-factor. Which of the following increases the Q-factor?

Explanation: Since Q = ($\omega_{0}$ L) / R, increasing L and decreasing R will increase the Q-factor, resulting in a sharper resonance curve.

4. A bulb and a capacitor are connected in series to an AC source. What happens to the brightness of the bulb if the dielectric slab is inserted into the capacitor?

Explanation: Inserting a dielectric slab increases capacitance C (C' = k C), which decreases capacitive reactance $X_{C}$ = 1 / ($\omega$ C'). Lower reactance increases the circuit current, increasing the brightness of the bulb.

6. In a series LCR circuit, the phase difference between the voltage across the inductor and the voltage across the capacitor is:

Explanation: The voltage across an inductor leads the current by $\pi$/2, while the voltage across a capacitor lags the current by $\pi$/2. Therefore, the phase difference between $V_{L}$ and $V_{C}$ is $\pi$ radians (180 degrees).

7. A choking coil is preferred over a resistor for controlling current in an AC circuit because:

Explanation: A choke coil has high inductance and very low resistance, allowing it to control AC current via reactance without significant power dissipation compared to a thermal resistor.

8. The power factor of a purely resistive AC circuit is:

Explanation: In a pure resistor, voltage and current are in phase ($\phi$ = 0), so the power factor cos(0) = 1.

9. If the angular frequency of an AC source in a series LCR circuit is very large ($\omega$ -> infinity), the circuit behaves primarily as:

Explanation: As $\omega$ approaches infinity, inductive reactance $X_{L}$ = $\omega$ L becomes extremely large, while capacitive reactance $X_{C}$ = 1 / ($\omega$ C) approaches zero. Thus, the circuit is dominated by inductance.

10. Which of the following parameters remains unaffected when an iron core is inserted into an inductor coil connected to an AC source?

Explanation: The frequency of the AC source is determined solely by the external generator/oscillator and remains completely unaffected by internal circuit modifications such as inserting an iron core.
