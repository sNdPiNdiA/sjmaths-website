Internal Resistance of Cells and Batteries

Internal resistance is the opposition offered by the electrolyte and electrodes of a source of electromotive force to the flow of electric current within itself. It causes a potential drop across the source when current is drawn, reducing the terminal potential difference below the EMF.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of internal resistance, relation between EMF, terminal voltage, current, and internal resistance, simple series and parallel combinations of cells, maximum power transfer theorem basics.

Prerequisites

  • Ohm's Law
  • Electric Current and Drift Velocity
  • Kirchhoff's Laws and Terminal Potential Difference

Origin of Internal Resistance

  • Internal resistance arises due to the finite resistivity of the electrolytic solution and the electrodes inside a galvanic cell.
  • It depends inversely on the area of the electrodes immersed in the electrolyte and directly on the distance between the electrodes.
  • It increases with the age and usage of the cell as chemical polarization and depletion of active material occur.

EMF and Terminal Potential Difference

  • Electromotive Force (EMF) epsilon is the potential difference between the terminals of a cell when no current is drawn from it (open circuit).
  • Terminal Potential Difference (V) is the potential difference across the terminals when current is flowing through an external circuit.
  • When discharging, V = epsilon - Ir; when charging, V = epsilon + Ir.

Combination of Cells

  • In series combination, total EMF is the sum of individual EMFs and total internal resistance is the sum of individual internal resistances.
  • In parallel combination, equivalent EMF depends on individual EMFs and resistances, useful for minimizing internal resistance.
  • Mixed grouping optimizes current delivery when external resistance equals total internal resistance.

Maximum Power Transfer Theorem

  • Maximum power is delivered to an external load resistor when the load resistance equals the internal resistance of the source.
  • Under this condition, the efficiency of power transfer is only 50 percent, as half of the total power generated is dissipated inside the source.
  • Expressed mathematically as R = r, yielding P_{max} = rac{epsilon^{2}}{4r}.

Derivations

Relation between E, V, I, and r

Conditions: A cell of EMF epsilon and internal resistance r connected to an external resistance R.

  • Let a current I flow through the circuit containing external resistance R and internal resistance r in series.
  • By Ohm's Law, the total resistance of the circuit is R + r.
  • The current is given by I = rac{epsilon}{R + r}.
  • Rearranging gives epsilon = I(R + r) = IR + Ir.
  • Since terminal voltage V = IR, substitute V into the equation to get epsilon = V + Ir, or V = epsilon - Ir.

Result: V = epsilon - Ir

Internal Resistance using a Potentiometer

Conditions: Potentiometer wire connected to a driver cell, with the test cell connected across a resistance box R.

  • With switch open, balance length l_1 is obtained for open-circuit EMF epsilon, so epsilon = k l_1, where k is potential gradient.
  • With switch closed, a resistance R is introduced, and balance length l_2 is obtained for terminal voltage V, so V = k l_2.
  • Taking the ratio, rac{epsilon}{V} = rac{l_1}{l_2}.
  • We know rac{epsilon}{V} = rac{I(R+r)}{IR} = rac{R+r}{R} = 1 + rac{r}{R}.
  • Equating the two ratios: 1 + rac{r}{R} = rac{l_1}{l_2}, which yields r = R( rac{l_1}{l_2} - 1).

Result: r = R( rac{l_1}{l_2} - 1)

Graphs and experiments

V versus I Graph for a Cell

Draw: A straight line graph plotted with terminal voltage V on the y-axis and current I on the x-axis.

Interpret: The y-intercept represents the EMF epsilon of the cell, and the magnitude of the negative slope (- dV/dI) gives the internal resistance r.

Exam use: Frequently tested in MCQs to extract EMF and internal resistance from experimental data points.

Power versus Load Resistance Graph

Draw: A curve showing external power P on the y-axis and load resistance R on the x-axis, rising to a peak and then decreasing.

Interpret: The peak of the curve occurs precisely when R equals r, representing maximum power transfer.

Exam use: Tested numerically and conceptually in both TGT and PGT exams.

Potentiometer Experiment for Internal Resistance

Draw: Circuit diagram showing driver cell, rheostat, potentiometer wire, test cell with resistance box across it, and a galvanometer with a jockey.

Interpret: Direct comparison of null deflection lengths l_1 and l_2 allows accurate calculation of internal resistance without drawing current from the cell during balance.

Exam use: Core practical concept asked in viva voce and theoretical problem formulation.

Solved numericals

TGT core

A cell of EMF 2.0 V and internal resistance 0.5 ohm is connected across an external resistor of 3.5 ohm. Calculate the terminal potential difference and the current flowing through the circuit.

  • Identify given values: epsilon = 2.0 V, r = 0.5 ohm, R = 3.5 ohm.
  • Calculate total circuit resistance: R_{total} = R + r = 3.5 + 0.5 = 4.0 ohm.
  • Calculate current: I = rac{epsilon}{R_{total}} = rac{2.0}{4.0} = 0.5 A.
  • Calculate terminal voltage: V = I * R = 0.5 * 3.5 = 1.75 V.

Answer: Current = 0.5 A, Terminal Voltage = 1.75 V

PGT extension

In a potentiometer experiment, the balancing length for an open cell is 560 cm. When a resistance of 10 ohm is connected across the cell terminals, the balancing length drops to 500 cm. Find the internal resistance of the cell.

  • Identify given data: l_1 = 560 cm, l_2 = 500 cm, R = 10 ohm.
  • Use the formula for internal resistance using a potentiometer: r = R ( rac{l_1}{l_2} - 1).
  • Substitute values: r = 10 * ( rac{560}{500} - 1) = 10 * (1.12 - 1) = 10 * 0.12.
  • Calculate final value: r = 1.2 ohm.

Answer: 1.2 ohm

PGT extension

A battery of EMF 12 V and internal resistance 2 ohm is connected to a variable resistor R. What should be the value of R so that the thermal power dissipated in R is maximum? Also find this maximum power.

  • For maximum power dissipation in R, the load resistance must equal the internal resistance: R = r = 2 ohm.
  • Calculate maximum power using the formula P_{max} = rac{epsilon^{2}}{4r}.
  • Substitute values: P_{max} = rac{12^{2}}{4 * 2} = rac{144}{8}.
  • Calculate final power: P_{max} = 18 W.

Answer: R = 2 ohm, P_{max} = 18 W

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes internal resistance remains constant during current flow, which is an approximation as temperature and electrolyte concentration change with continuous current.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Internal resistance is only present in secondary (rechargeable) cells.

Correct: Internal resistance is present in all sources of EMF, including primary cells, accumulators, and electronic DC power supplies.

All practical sources have material components through which charge must flow, inherently creating resistance.

Common error: Terminal potential difference is always less than the EMF of a cell.

Correct: Terminal voltage can be greater than EMF when the cell is being charged.

During charging, the current enters the positive terminal, resulting in V = epsilon + Ir.

Quick revision

  • Internal resistance r reduces terminal voltage: V = epsilon - Ir when discharging and V = epsilon + Ir when charging.
  • Terminal Potential Difference (Discharging): V = epsilon - Ir
  • Terminal Potential Difference (Charging): V = epsilon + Ir

Must remember

  • Internal resistance $r$ arises due to the finite resistivity of the electrolytic solution and electrodes inside a galvanic cell.
  • Terminal potential difference during discharging is given by $V = \epsilon - Ir$.
  • Terminal potential difference during charging is given by $V = \epsilon + Ir$.
  • Maximum power is delivered to an external load resistor when the load resistance equals the internal resistance of the source ($R = r$).
  • Under the maximum power transfer condition, the efficiency of power transfer is exactly $50\%$.
  • Internal resistance can be determined experimentally using a potentiometer with balancing lengths $l_1$ and $l_2$ via $r = R(\frac{l_1}{l_2} - 1)$.

Glossary

Electromotive Force ($\epsilon$)The potential difference between the terminals of a cell when no current is drawn from it (open circuit).
Terminal Potential Difference ($V$)The potential difference across the terminals of a cell when current is flowing through an external circuit.
Internal Resistance ($r$)The opposition offered by the electrolyte and electrodes of a cell to the flow of electric current.
Maximum Power TheoremThe principle stating that maximum power is transferred from a source to a load when the load resistance equals the source's internal resistance.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the origin of internal resistance in a galvanic cell?

Explanation: Internal resistance arises due to the finite resistivity of the electrolytic solution and the electrodes inside a galvanic cell.

2. How does internal resistance vary with the area of the electrodes immersed in the electrolyte?

Explanation: Internal resistance depends inversely on the area of the electrodes immersed in the electrolyte.

3. What happens to the internal resistance of a cell as it ages and is used extensively?

Explanation: Internal resistance increases with the age and usage of the cell as chemical polarization and depletion of active material occur.

4. Which of the following correctly expresses the terminal potential difference $V$ when a cell is discharging?

Explanation: When a cell is discharging, current flows and there is a potential drop across internal resistance $r$, giving $V = \epsilon - Ir$.

5. Under what condition is the terminal potential difference greater than the EMF of a cell?

Explanation: When a cell is being charged, current enters its positive terminal, leading to the relation $V = \epsilon + Ir$, making $V > \epsilon$.

6. A cell of EMF $\epsilon$ and internal resistance $r$ is connected across an external resistor $R$. The formula for internal resistance derived from circuit parameters is:

Explanation: From $V = IR = \frac{\epsilon}{R+r}R$, we get $\frac{\epsilon}{V} = \frac{R+r}{R} = 1 + \frac{r}{R}$, which rearranges to $r = R(\frac{\epsilon}{V} - 1)$ or $\frac{R(\epsilon - V)}{V}$.

7. In a potentiometer experiment for measuring internal resistance, balancing lengths for open circuit and closed circuit across resistance $R$ are $l_1$ and $l_2$ respectively. The internal resistance $r$ is given by:

Explanation: The ratio of balancing lengths equals the ratio of EMF to terminal voltage: $\frac{\epsilon}{V} = \frac{l_1}{l_2}$. Substituting into $r = R(\frac{\epsilon}{V} - 1)$ gives $r = R(\frac{l_1}{l_2} - 1)$.

8. For $n$ identical cells each of EMF $\epsilon$ and internal resistance $r$ connected in series, the equivalent EMF and internal resistance are:

Explanation: In series combination, total EMF is the sum of individual EMFs ($n\epsilon$) and total internal resistance is the sum of individual internal resistances ($nr$).

9. What is the primary utility of connecting cells in a parallel combination?

Explanation: Parallel combination of cells is useful for minimizing equivalent internal resistance and increasing current delivery capacity for low external resistances.

10. According to the Maximum Power Transfer Theorem, maximum power is delivered to an external load resistor when:

Explanation: Maximum power is delivered to an external load resistor when the load resistance equals the internal resistance of the source ($R = r$).

11. What is the efficiency of power transfer under the condition of maximum power transfer ($R = r$)?

Explanation: Under the maximum power transfer condition, efficiency is only $50\%$ because half of the generated power is dissipated inside the source as heat.

12. What is the maximum power $P_{max}$ delivered by a cell of EMF $\epsilon$ and internal resistance $r$?

Explanation: With $R = r$, current $I = \frac{\epsilon}{2r}$, so power $P = I^2 R = (\frac{\epsilon}{2r})^2 r = \frac{\epsilon^2}{4r}$.

13. Which of the following statements is a common misconception about internal resistance?

Explanation: It is a misconception that internal resistance is only in secondary cells; it is present in all sources of EMF including primary cells and DC power supplies.

14. When $m$ rows in parallel, each containing $n$ identical cells in series, are connected to an external resistance $R$, the condition for maximum current is:

Explanation: For mixed grouping, the total resistance of cells is $\frac{nr}{m}$. For maximum current, the external resistance must equal this internal resistance: $R = \frac{nr}{m}$.

15. How does the distance between electrodes affect the internal resistance of a galvanic cell?

Explanation: Internal resistance depends directly on the distance between the electrodes, as greater distance increases the path length for ion flow through the electrolyte.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. A cell of EMF $2\text{ V}$ and internal resistance $0.5\ \$\omega$$ is connected across an external resistor of $4.5\ \$\omega$$. What is the terminal potential difference across the cell?

Explanation: Total resistance = $R + r = 4.5 + 0.5 = 5.0\ \$\omega$$. Current $I = \frac{\epsilon}{R+r} = \frac{2}{5} = 0.4\text{ A}$. Terminal voltage $V = \epsilon - Ir = 2 - (0.4 \times 0.5) = 2 - 0.2 = 1.8\text{ V}$ gegen.

2. When a resistance of $2\ \$\omega$$ is connected across a cell, the current is $0.5\text{ A}$. When a resistance of $5\ \$\omega$$ is connected, the current is $0.25\text{ A}$. What is the internal resistance of the cell?

Explanation: From $\epsilon = $I_{1}$($R_{1}$ + r)$ and $\epsilon = $I_{2}$($R_{2}$ + r)$: $0.5(2 + r) = 0.25(5 + r) \Rightarrow 1 + 0.5r = 1.25 + 0.25r \Rightarrow 0.25r = 0.25 \Rightarrow r = 1.0\ \$\omega$$.

3. In a potentiometer arrangement, a cell gives a balancing length of $120\text{ cm}$. When a shunt resistance of $5\ \$\omega$$ is connected across the cell, the balancing length reduces to $60\text{ cm}$. The internal resistance of the cell is:

Explanation: Using $r = R(\frac{l_1}{l_2} - 1) = 5(\frac{120}{60} - 1) = 5(2 - 1) = 5.0\ \$\omega$$.

4. Twelve cells, each of EMF $1.5\text{ V}$ and internal resistance $0.5\ \$\omega$$, are arranged in a mixed grouping (m rows, n cells in series) to pass maximum current through an external resistor of $1.5\ \$\omega$$. What is the number of rows $m$ and cells per row $n$?

Explanation: Total cells $mn = 12$. For maximum current, $R = \frac{nr}{m} \Rightarrow 1.5 = \frac{n(0.5)}{m} \Rightarrow \frac{n}{m} = 3 \Rightarrow n = 3m$. Substituting into $mn = 12$ gives $3m^2 = 12 \Rightarrow m^2 = 4 \Rightarrow m = 2$, and $n = 6$.

5. A battery of EMF $\epsilon$ and internal resistance $r$ gives the same current $0.5\text{ A}$ when connected to an external resistor of $11\ \$\omega$$ and $23\ \$\omega$$ respectively. What is the internal resistance $r$?

Explanation: For maximum power transfer, external resistance $R$ must equal internal resistance $r$, hence $R = 2\ \$\omega$$.

6. A storage battery of EMF $8\text{ V}$ and internal resistance $0.5\ \$\omega$$ is being charged by a $120\text{ V}$ DC supply using a series resistor of $15.5\ \$\omega$$. What is the terminal voltage of the battery during charging?

Explanation: During charging, net voltage opposing circuit is $V_{net} = 120 - 8 = 112\text{ V}$. Total resistance = $15.5 + 0.5 = 16\ \$\omega$$. Charging current $I = \frac{112}{16} = 7\text{ A}$. Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which factor does NOT affect the internal resistance of a cell?

Explanation: Internal resistance is an intrinsic property of the cell itself (electrodes, electrolyte, distance, area) and is independent of external connecting wires.

2. A cell supplies a current of $0.9\text{ A}$ through a $2\ \$\omega$$ resistor and $0.3\text{ A}$ through a $7\ \$\omega$$ resistor. The internal resistance of the cell is:

Explanation: $\epsilon = 0.9(2 + r) = 0.3(7 + r) \Rightarrow 1.8 + 0.9r = 2.1 + 0.3r \Rightarrow 0.6r = 0.3 \Rightarrow r = 0.5\ \$\omega$$.

3. Two identical cells each of EMF $\epsilon$ and internal resistance $r$ are connected in parallel. The equivalent EMF and equivalent internal resistance are:

Explanation: For identical cells in parallel, the equivalent EMF is equal to the EMF of a single cell ($\epsilon$) and equivalent internal resistance is $\frac{r}{2}$.

4. The terminal potential difference of a cell becomes zero when:

Explanation: When the cell is short-circuited ($R = 0$), terminal voltage $V = IR = 0$, even though current is maximum.

5. What is the efficiency of power transfer when the load resistance $R$ is four times the internal resistance $r$ ($R = 4r$)?

Explanation: Efficiency $\eta = \frac{P_{load}}{P_{total}} = \frac{I^2 R}{I^2 (R+r)} = \frac{4r}{4r + r} = \frac{4}{5} = 80\%$.

6. In a potentiometer measurement, the balancing length for a cell alone is $350\text{ cm}$. When the cell is shunted with a $10\ \$\omega$$ resistor, the balancing length is $300\text{ cm}$. What is the internal resistance?

Explanation: $r = R(\frac{l_1}{l_2} - 1) = 10(\frac{350}{300} - 1) = 10(\frac{35}{30} - 1) = 10(\frac{5}{30}) = \frac{10}{6} = 1.67\ \$\omega$$.

7. Why does a secondary cell have a very low internal resistance compared to a primary cell?

Explanation: Secondary cells are designed to deliver high surge currents (like starting an automobile engine), achieved by using large surface area electrodes placed close together.

8. If $n$ identical cells are connected in series with wrong polarity for one cell, what is the effective EMF?

Explanation: If one cell is connected with reverse polarity, its EMF opposes the others, reducing total EMF by $2\epsilon$ (one to cancel the positive contribution and one for the negative contribution), giving $(n-2)\epsilon$.

9. A battery has an open-circuit voltage of $12\text{ V}$ and a terminal voltage of $10\text{ V}$ when drawing a current of $2\text{ A}$. What is its internal resistance?

Explanation: $\epsilon = 12\text{ V}$, $V = 10\text{ V}$, $I = 2\text{ A}$. From $V = \epsilon - Ir$, $10 = 12 - 2r \Rightarrow 2r = 2 \Rightarrow r = 1.0\ \$\omega$$.

10. What is the primary condition for maximum current delivery from a mixed grouping of $mn$ cells?

Explanation: For mixed grouping, maximum current is delivered when the external load resistance equals the internal resistance of the entire cell combination ($R = \frac{nr}{m}$).
