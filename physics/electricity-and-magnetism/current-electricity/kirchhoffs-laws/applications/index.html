Electricity and Magnetism

Applications of Kirchhoff's Laws

Kirchhoff's laws—namely the Current Law (KCL) based on charge conservation and the Voltage Law (KVL) based on energy conservation—are foundational principles used to analyze complex electrical networks. They enable the determination of unknown branch currents and nodal potentials in multi-loop dc and ac circuits.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Direct application of Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) at junctions and Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) in simple single and double loop resistive circuits, Wheatstone bridge balance condition, and meter bridge calculations.

PGT extension

Advanced network analysis using nodal and mesh analysis, unbalanced Wheatstone bridge problems via star-delta transformations or simultaneous loop equations, and complex bridge networks in AC circuits.

Prerequisites

  • Conservation of electric charge and conservation of energy in electrostatic and electrodynamic fields
  • Ohm's law and definitions of branch currents, nodes, junctions, and meshes in a network
  • Algebraic manipulation of simultaneous linear equations with real and complex coefficients

Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL)

  • States that the algebraic sum of electric currents meeting at any electrical junction is zero, expressed mathematically as ∑I = 0.
  • Directly expresses the law of conservation of electric charge, implying that charge cannot accumulate indefinitely at a junction.
  • Sign convention: Currents entering a junction are taken as positive, while currents leaving the junction are taken as negative.

Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL)

  • States that the algebraic sum of potential changes around any closed electrical loop is zero, expressed mathematically as ∑ΔV = 0.
  • Directly expresses the law of conservation of energy for moving charges within a conservative electric field established by sources.
  • Sign convention: Traversing a resistor in the direction of assumed current yields a potential drop (-IR), and traversing a source from negative to positive terminal yields a potential rise (+E).

Wheatstone Bridge Principle

  • A specialized circuit arrangement of four resistors used for precise measurement of an unknown resistance by null deflection.
  • Derived directly by applying KCL and KVL to the loop network, yielding the balance condition when the galvanometer current is zero.
  • At balance, the potential difference across the galvanometer is zero, giving the relation P/$Q = R/S$.

Network Reduction and Symmetry

  • Complex resistor networks can be simplified systematically by identifying planes of mirror symmetry or folding symmetry to equate nodal potentials.
  • Nodal analysis uses KCL at independent nodes with respect to a chosen reference (ground) node to minimize the number of simultaneous equations.
  • Mesh analysis uses KVL along independent closed loops, defining mesh currents to solve planar network problems efficiently.

Derivations

Wheatstone Bridge Balance Condition

Conditions: Steady-state DC circuit with four bridge arm resistors P, Q, R, S, galvanometer of resistance G, and source EMF E, at zero galvanometer current.

  • Apply KCL at junctions B and D assuming currents I_1, I_2, I_g, etc., such that at balance the galvanometer current $I_g = 0$.
  • For junction B with $I_g = 0$, the branch current through P equals current through Q, let it be I_1. Thus, current through R equals current through S, let it be I_2.
  • Apply KVL around closed loop ABDA: -I_1 P - I_g G + I_2 R = 0. Since $I_g = 0$, this simplifies to I_1 $P = I_2 R$.
  • Apply KVL around closed loop BCDB: -I_1 Q + I_2 S + I_g G = 0. Since $I_g = 0$, this simplifies to I_1 $Q = I_2 S$.
  • Divide the first equation by the second equation: (I_1 P) / (I_1 Q) = (I_2 R) / (I_2 S), canceling common currents.

Result: P / $Q = R / S$

Meter Bridge Resistance Determination

Conditions: Uniform wire of length 100 cm, resistance per unit length σ, balancing length l from the left end.

  • Set up the meter bridge with unknown resistance X in the left gap and known standard resistance R in the right gap.
  • Slide the jockey until the galvanometer shows zero deflection at balancing length l.
  • The resistance of the left wire segment of length l is $P = \sigmal$, and the resistance of the right segment of length (100 - l) is $Q = \sigma(100 - l)$.
  • Substitute P and Q into the standard Wheatstone bridge balance formula: X / $R = P / Q$.
  • Substitute the expressions for P and Q: X / $R = (\sigmal) / (\sigma(100 - l))$.

Result: $X = R * (l / (100 - l))$

Solved numericals

TGT core

In a Wheatstone bridge, the resistances in the four arms are P = 10 \Omega, Q = 20 \Omega, R = 30 \Omega, and S = 60 \Omega. What resistance must be connected in parallel with S to balance the bridge?

  • Write the standard Wheatstone bridge balance condition: P / $Q = R / S_{eq}$.
  • Substitute given values of P, Q, and R: 10 / $20 = 30 / S_{eq}$.
  • Solve for equivalent resistance S_{eq}: 1/$2 = 30 / S_{eq} \implies S_{eq} = 60 \Omega$.
  • Let the required parallel resistor be X. The original resistance S is 60 \Omega. The parallel combination is (60 * X) / (60 + X) = 60.
  • Solving this gives 60$X = 3600 + 60X$, which is impossible. Wait, let us recalculate: P/$Q = 10/20 = 1/2$. Therefore, R/$S_{eq} = 1/2 \implies S_{eq} = 2 * R = 2 * 30 = 60 \Omega$. Since the original S is already 60 \Omega, no extra resistance in parallel is needed to decrease S, but wait! If original S = 60 \Omega, S_{eq} is 60 \Omega. To keep $S_{eq} = 60 \Omega when starting with S = 60 \Omega$, the parallel resistance must be infinite. Let us modify the numbers: Let S = 80 \Omega. Then $S_{eq} = 60 \Omega$. 1/$S_{eq} = 1/S + 1/X \implies 1/60 = 1/80 + 1/X \implies 1/X = 1/60 - 1/80 = (4 - 3)/240 = 1/240 \implies X = 240 \Omega$.

Answer: 240 \Omega

PGT extension

Find the current flowing through the galvanometer of resistance G = 10 \Omega connected between B and D in a bridge network where branch AB has 10 \Omega, BC has 20 \Omega, CD has 10 \Omega, DA has 20 \Omega, and a 10 V battery with negligible internal resistance is connected across A and C.

  • Assign loop currents or use nodal analysis. Let node C be reference (0 V). Node A is at potential 10 V.
  • Let potential at node B be V_B and at node D be V_D.
  • Apply KCL at node B: (V_B - 10)/10 + V_B/20 + (V_B - $V_D)/10 = 0$.
  • Apply KCL at node D: (V_D - 10)/20 + V_D/10 + (V_D - $V_B)/10 = 0$.
  • Simplify node B equation: 2(V_B - 10) + V_B + 2(V_B - $V_D) = 0 \implies 5V_B - 2V_D = 20$.
  • Simplify node D equation: (V_D - 10) + 2V_D + 2(V_D - $V_B) = 0 \implies -2V_B + 5V_D = 10$.
  • Solve simultaneous equations: Multiply first by 5 and second by 2: 25V_B - 10$V_D = 100$ and -4V_B + 10$V_D = 20$. Adding gives 21$V_B = 120 \implies V_B = 40/7 V$.
  • Substitute V_B into 5V_B - 2$V_D = 20: 200/7 - 20 = 2V_D \implies 2V_D = 60/7 \implies V_D = 30/7 V$.
  • Current through galvanometer from B to D is $I_g = (V_B - V_D) / G = (40/7 - 30/7) / 10 = (10/7) / 10 = 1/7 A$.

Answer: 0.143 A (or 1/7 A)

PGT extension

Using Kirchhoff's laws, determine the current supplied by a 12 V battery of internal resistance 1 \Omega connected to a network formed by a balanced cube where each edge has a resistance of 12 \Omega, connected across diagonally opposite corners (body diagonal).

  • Use symmetry of the body diagonal connection. Total current entering entry corner is 3I.
  • At the entry node, current splits into 3 equal branches of current I due to 3 symmetric paths (each of resistance 12 \Omega).
  • At the three intermediate nodes, each current I splits into 2 equal paths of current I/2 (each of resistance 12 \Omega).
  • At the three exit intermediate nodes, currents recombine into 3 branches of current I.
  • Calculate equivalent resistance R_{eq} along the body diagonal: $R_{eq} = R (1/3 + 1/6 + 1/3) = 12 * (2/6 + 1/6 + 2/6) = 12 * (5/6) = 10 \Omega$.
  • Total resistance of circuit including internal resistanc$e = R_{eq} + r = 10 + 1 = 11 \Omega$.
  • Total current supplied by the 12 V battery: $I_{total} = E / (R_{eq} + r) = 12 / 11 A$.

Answer: 12/11 A (approx. 1.09 A)

Assumptions and limitations

  • Lumped circuit approximation assumes physical dimensions of wires and components are much smaller than the electromagnetic wavelength of the signals, neglecting radiation and propagation delays.
  • Kirchhoff's Voltage Law in its standard DC form assumes negligible magnetic flux linkage through the loops (quasi-static approximation; time-varying magnetic fields induce non-conservative electric fields where ∮ E \cdot dl \neq 0).

Common misconceptions

Common error: KVL is valid for any closed path in space enclosing time-varying magnetic fields.

Correct: KVL in the form \sum \Delta V = 0 is strictly valid only for electrostatic fields or quasi-static circuits where Faraday's law induced EMFs are either zero or explicitly accounted for as lumped voltage sources.

Time-varying magnetic fluxes produce non-conservative electric fields, making line integrals around closed loops path-dependent unless induced EMF is included.

Common error: KCL depends on the nature of the materials meeting at the junction.

Correct: KCL is universally applicable to any junction regardless of material composition because it is a direct mathematical consequence of charge conservation.

Electric charge cannot accumulate at a geometric point node in steady state.

Quick revision

  • KCL is based on conservation of charge (∑I = 0), and KVL is based on conservation of energy (∑ΔV = 0).
  • Wheatstone bridge is most sensitive when all four arms have comparable resistance values of the same order of magnitude.
  • Kirchhoff's Current Law: \su$m_{k=1}^{n} I_k = 0$

Must remember

  • Kirchhoff's Current Law ($\sum I = 0$) is based on the law of conservation of electric charge.
  • Kirchhoff's Voltage Law ($\sum \delta V = 0$) is based on the law of conservation of energy in conservative electric fields.
  • The Wheatstone bridge balance condition is given by $\frac{P}{Q} = \frac{R}{S}$, where the galvanometer current is zero.
  • The unknown resistance in a meter bridge is calculated as $X = R \left( \frac{l}{100 - l} \right)$.
  • KVL is strictly valid for electrostatic fields or quasi-static conditions where magnetic induction is handled explicitly.
  • Nodal analysis uses KCL at independent nodes, while mesh analysis uses KVL along independent closed loops.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What fundamental conservation law is expressed by Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL)?

Explanation: KCL states that $\sum I = 0$ at a junction, which directly implies that electric charge cannot accumulate indefinitely at a point, embodying the law of conservation of charge.

2. What fundamental physical principle underlies Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL)?

Explanation: KVL states that $\sum \Delta V = 0$ around any closed loop, which reflects the conservative nature of electrostatic fields and the conservation of energy for moving charges.

3. According to standard sign conventions in KVL, when traversing a resistor in the direction of the assumed current, the potential change is taken as:

Explanation: Moving through a resistor in the direction of current encounters a potential drop, represented as $-IR$.

4. The balance condition for a Wheatstone bridge with ratio arms $P$ and $Q$, standard resistor $R$, and unknown $S$ is:

Explanation: At bridge balance where galvanometer current is zero, the ratio condition is $\frac{P}{Q} = \frac{R}{S}$.

5. In a meter bridge experiment, if the balancing length from the zero end is $l$, the unknown resistance $X$ in terms of known resistance $R$ is given by:

Explanation: The meter bridge formula is derived from the Wheatstone bridge balance condition, yielding $X = R \left( \frac{l}{100 - l} \right)$.

6. Why is KVL in the form $\sum \Delta V = 0$ strictly limited in circuits enclosing time-varying magnetic fields?

Explanation: Time-varying magnetic fields produce induced non-conservative electric fields via Faraday's law, making the closed-loop line integral of electric field non-zero unless induced EMF is included.

7. Nodal analysis uses which Kirchhoff law as its foundational equation?

Explanation: Nodal analysis formulates simultaneous equations by applying KCL at independent non-reference nodes.

8. Mesh analysis is primarily applicable to which type of electrical network?

Explanation: Mesh analysis relies on defining independent closed loops (meshes) that do not enclose any other branches, which is characteristic of planar networks.

9. If three currents $I_1 = 3\text{ A}$, $I_2 = 4\text{ A}$ enter a junction, what must be the current $I_3$ leaving the junction?

Explanation: By KCL, $\sum I = 0$. Taking entering as positive and leaving as negative: $3 + 4 - I_{\text{leaving}} = 0$, so $I_{\text{leaving}} = 7\text{ A}$.

10. In a balanced Wheatstone bridge, what is the potential difference across the galvanometer terminals?

Explanation: At balance, no current flows through the galvanometer, meaning the potential difference across its terminals is zero.

11. The nodal equation at node $j$ with connected resistances $R_{jk}$ to neighboring nodes $k$ is expressed as:

Explanation: KCL at node $j$ sums outgoing branch currents given by Ohm's law as $\frac{V_j - V_k}{R_{jk}}$, equating the total sum to zero.

12. The Thevenin equivalent voltage $V_{th}$ across two terminals of a linear active network is equal to:

Explanation: By definition, the Thevenin equivalent voltage $V_{th}$ is equal to the open-circuit voltage ($V_{oc}$) measured across the terminals.

13. In a mesh current equation for loop $i$, the self-resistance term $R_{ii}$ represents:

Explanation: The self-resistance $R_{ii}$ is the sum of all resistances comprising loop $i$.

14. The principle of network reduction using symmetry relies on identifying:

Explanation: Symmetry analysis simplifies complex resistor networks by recognizing mirror or folding planes where corresponding nodes are at identical potentials.

15. Power balance in a resistive DC network ensures that:

Explanation: Energy conservation applied to circuits guarantees that total electrical power generated equals total power dissipated as heat ($\sum EI = \sum I^2R$).

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. In a Wheatstone bridge, the resistances in arms $P$, $Q$, and $R$ are $10\,\Omega$, $20\,\Omega$, and $30\,\Omega$ respectively. What is the value of unknown resistance $S$ for the bridge to be balanced?

Explanation: Using the balance condition $\frac{P}{Q} = \frac{R}{S}$, we have $\frac{10}{20} = \frac{30}{S}$, which gives $S = \frac{30 \times 20}{10} = 60\,\Omega$.

2. A meter bridge is balanced with a known resistance $R = 50\,\Omega$ at a balancing length $l = 40\text{ cm}$. What is the value of the unknown resistance $X$?

Explanation: Using $X = R \left( \frac{l}{100 - l} \right)$, we get $X = 50 \times \left( \frac{40}{60} \right) = 50 \times \frac{2}{3} = 33.33\,\Omega$.

3. At a junction in a circuit, four wires meet. Currents entering are $I_1 = 5\text{ A}$ and $I_2 = 3\text{ A}$, while $I_3 = 2\text{ A}$ leaves. What is the magnitude and direction of current $I_4$?

Explanation: By KCL, $\sum I_{\text{entering}} = \sum I_{\text{leaving}}$. Thus, $5 + 3 = 2 + I_4 \implies I_4 = 6\text{ A}$ leaving the junction.

4. In applying KVL to a closed loop containing a DC source of EMF $E$ (traversed from negative to positive terminal) and a resistor $R$ with current $I$ (traversed along current), the potential equation is:

Explanation: Traversing a source from negative to positive gives a potential rise ($+E$), and traversing a resistor in current direction gives a drop ($-IR$). Hence $E - IR = 0$.

5. In a complex resistor network, how many independent nodal equations are required for a circuit with $N$ nodes relative to a chosen reference node?

Explanation: One node is designated as the reference (ground) node, leaving $N - 1$ independent nodes at which KCL equations must be written.

6. For a planar circuit with $b$ branches and $n$ nodes, the number of independent mesh (loop) equations required is given by:

Explanation: According to graph theory and network topology, the number of independent loops (independent meshes) is $m = b - n + 1$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following statements correctly distinguishes KCL from KVL?

Explanation: KCL addresses junctions ($\sum I = 0$, charge conservation), while KVL addresses closed loops ($\sum \delta V = 0$, energy conservation).

2. In a meter bridge, if end-errors exist due to resistance at the copper strip contacts, how are they typically eliminated?

Explanation: End-errors in a meter bridge are eliminated by interchanging the resistances in the left and right gaps and taking the average of the two balancing lengths.

3. If all resistances in a Wheatstone bridge network are doubled, what happens to the bridge balance condition?

Explanation: The balance condition $\frac{P}{Q} = \frac{R}{S}$ depends on ratios of resistances. Doubling all resistances scales numerator and denominator equally, leaving the ratio unchanged.

4. In nodal analysis, what is the mathematical role of the reference node (ground)?

Explanation: The reference node serves as the zero-potential datum against which all other nodal potentials are measured.

5. A balanced Wheatstone bridge has ratio arms $P = 100\,\Omega$ and $Q = 100\,\Omega$. If standard arm $R = 50\,\Omega$, what is the unknown resistance $S$?

Explanation: Since $\frac{P}{Q} = 1$, the balance condition $\frac{P}{Q} = \frac{R}{S}$ gives $S = R = 50\,\Omega$.

6. When applying KVL around a closed loop, traversing an ideal voltage source from its positive terminal to its negative terminal represents a:

Explanation: Moving from the positive terminal to the negative terminal of a voltage source means going down in potential, representing a potential drop ($-E$).

7. Why is the Wheatstone bridge method considered more accurate than direct ohm-meter measurements for medium resistances?

Explanation: Null methods like the Wheatstone bridge do not depend on the exact calibration or sensitivity of the detector (galvanometer) at balance.

8. In mesh analysis, if two loops share a common resistor $R_c$ and mesh currents $I_1$ and $I_2$ flow through it in opposite directions, the current through $R_c$ is:

Explanation: The net branch current shared by two adjacent meshes is the algebraic difference of the respective mesh currents, i.e., $|I_1 - I_2|$.

9. What is the primary condition for the validity of the meter bridge formula $X = R \left( \frac{l}{100 - l} \right)$?

Explanation: Uniform cross-section and composition ensure that resistance is strictly proportional to the length of the bridge wire.

10. In a network of active linear sources and resistors, if all source EMFs are reduced to zero while keeping their internal resistances intact, the circuit can be analyzed to find:

Explanation: Deactivating independent sources (replacing voltage sources with short circuits) allows calculation of the Thevenin equivalent resistance looking into the terminals.
