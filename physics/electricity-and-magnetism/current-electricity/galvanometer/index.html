Moving Coil Galvanometer

A moving coil galvanometer is an electromechanical measuring instrument used for detecting and indicating small electric currents. It operates on the principle that a current-carrying coil placed in a uniform magnetic field experiences a deflecting torque.

Prerequisites

  • Magnetic effect of electric current and Lorentz force
  • Torque on a current loop in a magnetic field
  • Hooke's law of elasticity and torsional constant of a suspension wire

Principle of Moving Coil Galvanometer

  • A current-carrying coil suspended in a uniform magnetic field experiences a magnetic torque.
  • The deflecting torque is directly proportional to the current flowing through the coil.
  • Restoring torque is provided by the elastic twist of the suspension wire or a hairspring.

Radial Magnetic Field

  • A radial magnetic field is achieved by using a cylindrical soft iron core and concave pole pieces.
  • In a radial field, the magnetic field lines are always parallel to the plane of the coil for any orientation.
  • This ensures that the deflecting torque remains independent of the angle of deflection, making the scale linear.

Conversion into Ammeter

  • A galvanometer is converted into an ammeter by connecting a very low resistance called a shunt in parallel with it.
  • The shunt bypasses the major portion of the main current, protecting the delicate galvanometer coil.
  • The effective resistance of the resulting ammeter is extremely small compared to the galvanometer resistance.

Conversion into Voltmeter

  • A galvanometer is converted into a voltmeter by connecting a very high resistance in series with it.
  • The high series resistance limits the current passing through the galvanometer when large potential differences are applied.
  • The effective resistance of the resulting voltmeter is very large, drawing negligible current from the circuit being measured.

Derivations

Torque and Deflection in Galvanometer

Conditions: Radial magnetic field, N turns of coil with area A

  • Let a coil of N turns and area A carry current I in a radial magnetic field B.
  • The magnetic force on each vertical side of length l is F = NIbB, where b is the breadth and $A = l * b$.
  • The deflecting torque is given by ta$u_d = N I A B$.
  • Equating deflecting torque to restoring torque ta$u_r = k * \theta$, where k is the torsional constant and theta is deflection: N I A $B = k * \theta$.

Result: thet$a = (NAB / k) * I$

Shunt Resistance for Ammeter Conversion

Conditions: Galvanometer resistance G, full-scale deflection current I_g, maximum range I

  • Let the maximum current to be measured be I, and the current passing through the galvanometer at full-scale deflection be I_g.
  • The remaining current (I - I_g) must pass through the parallel shunt resistance S.
  • Since the galvanometer and shunt are in parallel, the potential difference across them is equal: I_g * $G = (I - I_g) * S$.
  • Solving for S yields the required shunt resistance.

Result: $S = \frac{I_g G}{I - I_g}$

Solved numericals

TGT core

A galvanometer has a resistance of 50 ohm and shows full scale deflection for a current of 2 mA. Calculate the value of the shunt resistance required to convert it into an ammeter of range 0 to 5 A.

  • Identify given values: G = 50 ohm, $I_g = 2 mA = 2 * 10^-3 A$, I = 5 A.
  • Apply the shunt formula: $S = (I_g * G) / (I - I_g)$.
  • Substitute values: $S = (2 * 10^-3 * 50) / (5 - 0.002) = 0.1 / 4.998 \approx 0.020008 ohm$.

Answer: 0.02 ohm

TGT core

A galvanometer has a coil resistance of 30 ohm and gives full-scale deflection for 15 mA. Find the series resistance needed to convert it into a voltmeter reading up to 150 V.

  • Identify given values: G = 30 ohm, $I_g = 15 mA = 15 * 10^-3 A$, V = 150 V.
  • Apply the multiplier formula: $R = (V / I_g) - G$.
  • Calculate V / I_g = 150 / (15 * 10^-3) = 10,000 ohm.
  • Subtract G: $R = 10000 - 30 = 9970 ohm$.

Answer: 9970 ohm

PGT extension

A moving coil galvanometer has 100 turns, each of area 2 cm^2. The magnetic field is 0.2 T, and the torsional constant of the suspension strip is 10^-6 N*m/rad. Find its current sensitivity.

  • Identify given parameters: N = 100, $A = 2 cm^2 = 2 * 10^-4 m^2$, B = 0.2 T, $k = 10^-6 N*m/rad$.
  • Formula for current sensitivity: $S_i = (N * A * B) / k$.
  • Substitute values: $S_i = (100 * 2 * 10^-4 * 0.2) / 10^-6$.
  • Calculate numerator: 100 * 2 * 10^-4 * $0.2 = 4 * 10^-3$.
  • Divide by k: (4 * 10^-3) / 10^-$6 = 4000 rad/A$.

Answer: 4000 rad/A

Assumptions and limitations

  • The magnetic field in the gap is assumed to be strictly radial to maintain a linear scale, which holds true only for carefully shaped pole pieces and small deflections.
  • The restoring torque is assumed to follow Hooke's torsional law linearly, which fails if the elastic limit of the suspension wire is exceeded.

Common misconceptions

Common error: An ideal galvanometer has zero resistance.

Correct: A practical galvanometer has a definite finite coil resistance (G).

The coil is made of fine insulated copper wire which inherently possesses electrical resistance.

Common error: Connecting a shunt increases the current measuring capacity by blocking all current from the galvanometer.

Correct: The galvanometer still carries its full-scale current I_g while the shunt carries the excess current (I - I_g).

Current divides inversely proportional to resistances in a parallel network.

Quick revision

  • Radial magnetic field ensures linear deflection scale: theta is proportional to I.
  • Ammeter uses a small parallel shunt; Voltmeter uses a large series multiplier.
  • Deflection Torque: tau = NIAB

Must remember

  • The deflecting torque in a moving coil galvanometer is given by $\tau = NIAB$, where $N$ is number of turns, $I$ is current, $A$ is area, and $B$ is the magnetic field.
  • A radial magnetic field ensures that the deflecting torque remains independent of the angle of deflection, making the scale linear.
  • A galvanometer is converted into an ammeter by connecting a very low resistance (shunt $S$) in parallel.
  • A galvanometer is converted into a voltmeter by connecting a very high resistance ($R$) in series.
  • Current sensitivity is given by $S_i = \frac{\theta}{I} = \frac{NAB}{k}$, and voltage sensitivity by $S_v = \frac{S_i}{G} = \frac{NAB}{kG}.
  • For a ballistic galvanometer, the charge sensitivity relates to the first maximum deflection $\theta_0$ via $q = \frac{T k}{\pi NAB} \theta_0$.

Glossary

Radial Magnetic FieldA magnetic field configuration where field lines are always parallel to the plane of the coil, achieved using a soft iron core and concave pole pieces.
Shunt Resistance ($S$)A very low resistance connected in parallel with a galvanometer to convert it into an ammeter.
Multiplier ($R$)A very high resistance connected in series with a galvanometer to convert it into an voltmeter.
Figure of Merit ($k'$)The current required to produce a deflection of one scale division in the galvanometer, $k' = \frac{I}{\theta} = \frac{k}{NAB}$.
Torsional Constant ($k$)Restoring torque per unit twist of the suspension wire or hairspring.
Ballistic GalvanometerA specialized galvanometer designed to measure total charge passing through it in a transient discharge.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the primary function of a radial magnetic field in a moving coil galvanometer?

Explanation: In a radial magnetic field, the magnetic field lines are always parallel to the plane of the coil for any orientation, making $\tau = NIAB$ constant with respect to angle, thus linearizing the scale.

2. How is a galvanometer converted into an ammeter?

Explanation: Connecting a very low resistance (shunt $S$) in parallel bypasses most of the current, protecting the galvanometer and allowing large currents to be measured.

3. The deflecting torque of a moving coil galvanometer is given by which expression?

Explanation: The magnetic torque experienced by a current-carrying coil with $N$ turns, area $A$, and current $I$ in a magnetic field $B$ is $\tau = NIAB$.

4. What is the formula for the shunt resistance $S$ required to extend the range of a galvanometer of resistance $G$ to a total current $I$, given full-scale deflection current $I_g$?

Explanation: Using potential divider/parallel circuit relations, the potential across the galvanometer $I_g G = (I - I_g)S$, giving $S = \frac{I_g G}{I - I_g}$.

5. Which of the following describes the voltage sensitivity $S_v$ of a galvanometer?

Explanation: Voltage sensitivity is deflection per unit voltage: $S_v = \frac{\theta}{V} = \frac{\theta}{I \cdot G} = \frac{S_i}{G}$.

6. How is a galvanometer converted into a voltmeter?

Explanation: A high series resistance limits the current through the galvanometer when large potential differences are applied, preventing damage.

7. What provides the restoring torque in a moving coil galvanometer?

Explanation: Restoring torque is provided by the elastic twist of the suspension wire or a hairspring, proportional to the angle of twist $\tau_{restoring} = k\theta$.

8. If the number of turns $N$ in a galvanometer coil is doubled, what happens to its current sensitivity $S_i$ (assuming other parameters remain constant)?

Explanation: Current sensitivity $S_i = \frac{NAB}{k}$ is directly proportional to $N$. Doubling $N$ doubles the current sensitivity.

9. What is the effective resistance $R_{eff}$ of an ammeter formed by a galvanometer of resistance $G$ and a shunt $S$?

Explanation: Since the galvanometer and shunt are connected in parallel, their effective resistance is given by the parallel combination formula $\frac{GS}{G + S}$.

10. What is the formula for the series resistance $R$ needed to convert a galvanometer into a voltmeter of range $V$?

Explanation: The total resistance of the voltmeter is $R + G = \frac{V}{I_g}$, therefore $R = \frac{V}{I_g} - G$.

11. For a ballistic galvanometer, what is the charge sensitivity relation with respect to the first maximum deflection $\theta_0$?

Explanation: The charge passing through a ballistic galvanometer is proportional to the first throw: $q = \frac{T k}{\pi NAB} \theta_0$.

12. What is the figure of merit ($k'$) of a galvanometer defined as?

Explanation: Figure of merit is $k' = \frac{I}{\theta}$, which is the current required to produce a deflection of a single scale division.

13. Why is a soft iron core used inside the coil of a moving coil galvanometer?

Explanation: A cylindrical soft iron core along with concave pole pieces creates a strong radial magnetic field, concentrating magnetic field lines.

14. What is a common misconception regarding an ideal galvanometer's resistance?

Explanation: Students often mistakenly think an ideal galvanometer has zero resistance, whereas practical ones have a finite coil resistance $G$.

15. When a shunt is connected across a galvanometer, what happens to the current flowing through the galvanometer coil itself during full-scale deflection?

Explanation: During full-scale deflection, the galvanometer carries its maximum rated current $I_g$, while the shunt carries the excess current $I - I_g$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. A galvanometer of resistance $G$ is converted into an ammeter by connecting a shunt resistance $S$ in parallel. If the total current is $I$ and full-scale current is $I_g$, the ratio of the shunt resistance to galvanometer resistance $\frac{S}{G}$ is:

Explanation: From $S(I - I_g) = I_g G$, we get $\frac{S}{G} = \frac{I_g}{I - I_g}$.

2. A moving coil galvanometer has a coil of $N$ turns, area $A$, and is placed in a magnetic field $B$. The torsional constant of the spring is $k$. The current sensitivity of the galvanometer is doubled if:

Explanation: Current sensitivity $S_i = \frac{NAB}{k}$. Doubling $N$ and halving $k$ increases sensitivity by a factor of $2 / (1/2) = 4$, wait. If $N$ is doubled ($2N$) and $k$ is halved ($k/2$), then $S_i' = \frac{(2N)AB}{k/2} = 4 \frac{NAB}{k}$, which quadruples it. Let's check option 1: if $N, A, B$ are doubled, $S_i$ becomes $8$ times. If $N$ is doubled and $k$ is doubled, sensitivity remains unchanged. Let's re-verify option formatting. Actually, if $N$ is doubled ($2N$) and $k$ is doubled ($2k$), sensitivity remains same. If only $k$ is halved, sensitivity is doubled: $S_i' = \frac{NAB}{k/2} = 2 S_i$. Thus, halving $k$ alone doubles sensitivity.

3. A galvanometer has a resistance of $50\ \Omega$ and full-scale deflection current of $2\text{ mA}$. What is the value of the resistance needed to convert it into a voltmeter of range $0 - 10\text{ V}$?

Explanation: Using $R = \frac{V}{I_g} - G$, $R = \frac{10}{2 \times 10^{-3}} - 50 = 5000 - 50 = 4950\ \Omega$.

4. If the shunt resistance $S$ in an ammeter conversion is very small compared to the galvanometer resistance $G$, the effective resistance of the ammeter $R_{eff}$ is approximately equal to:

Explanation: When $S \ll G$, $R_{eff} = \frac{GS}{G + S} \approx \frac{GS}{G} = S$.

5. In a ballistic galvanometer, the charge sensitivity depends on the time period $T$ of oscillation as:

Explanation: From the formula $q = \frac{T k}{\pi NAB} \theta_0$, charge $q$ is directly proportional to $T$.

6. A galvanometer of resistance $G$ gives full-scale deflection for current $I_g$. A shunt $S_1$ is used to convert it into an ammeter of range $I$, and another shunt $S_2$ is used to convert it into an ammeter of range $2I$. What is the relation between $S_1$ and $S_2$?

Explanation: Using $S = \frac{I_g G}{I_{range} - I_g}$, for range $I$, $S_1 = \frac{I_g G}{I - I_g}$. For range $2I$, $S_2 = \frac{I_g G}{2I - I_g}$. If $I \gg I_g$, $S_1 \approx \frac{I_g G}{I}$ and $S_2 \approx \frac{I_g G}{2I}$, hence $S_1 \approx 2S_2$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What happens to the voltage sensitivity of a galvanometer if its coil resistance $G$ is increased while keeping other factors constant?

Explanation: Voltage sensitivity $S_v = \frac{S_i}{G}$. Since $G$ is in the denominator, increasing $G$ decreases the voltage sensitivity.

2. A galvanometer has a current sensitivity of $10\text{ div/mA}$ and voltage sensitivity of $2\text{ div/V}$. What is the resistance of the galvanometer?

Explanation: Resistance $G = \frac{S_i}{S_v} = \frac{10\text{ div/mA}}{2\text{ div/V}} = \frac{10 / 10^{-3}}{2} = \frac{10^4}{2} = 5000\ \Omega$.

3. Which of the following components helps in creating a radial magnetic field in a moving coil galvanometer?

Explanation: Concave pole pieces and a soft iron cylindrical core shape the magnetic field into a radial pattern.

4. If a galvanometer is wrongly connected in series as an ammeter in a high-current circuit, what is most likely to happen?

Explanation: An ammeter has very low resistance and is connected in parallel (via shunt). Connecting a galvanometer directly in series without a proper shunt exposes it to high current, burning the fine coil.

5. The units of current sensitivity are:

Explanation: Current sensitivity $S_i = \frac{\theta}{I}$, measured in rad/A or scale divisions per ampere.

6. In a ballistic galvanometer, damping is accounted for by measuring:

Explanation: Logarithmic decrement is used in ballistic galvanometer corrections for damping oscillations.

7. Why does a voltmeter need a very high resistance?

Explanation: A high resistance ensures that the voltmeter draws negligible current from the circuit, preserving the circuit conditions.

8. Calculate the shunt resistance required to allow $10\%$ of the main current to pass through a galvanometer of resistance $99\ \Omega$.

Explanation: Here $I_g = 10\% \text{ of } I = 0.1 I$. Shunt $S = \frac{I_g G}{I - I_g} = \frac{0.1 I \times 99}{I - 0.1 I} = \frac{9.9 I}{0.9 I} = 11\ \Omega$. Wait, $9.9 / 0.9 = 11$. Let's check: $I_g G = (I - I_g)S \implies (0.1 I)(99) = (0.9 I) S \implies S = 9.9 / 0.9 = 11\ \Omega$. Correct answer is $11\ \Omega$ (Option B).

9. Which material is commonly used for the suspension wire of a moving coil galvanometer due to its low restoring torque per unit twist and high tensile strength?

Explanation: Phosphor-bronze is preferred for suspension wires because of its small torsional constant and high elasticity/tensile strength.

10. What is the primary difference between a standard moving coil galvanometer and a ballistic galvanometer?

Explanation: A ballistic galvanometer is designed with a large time period so that the entire charge passes through before the coil begins to move significantly.
