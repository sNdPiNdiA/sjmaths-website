Electricity and Magnetism

Maxwell's Equations

Maxwell's equations are a set of four fundamental partial differential equations that formulate the laws of classical electromagnetism. They unify electricity and magnetism and predict the existence of electromagnetic waves traveling at the speed of light.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Integral and differential forms of Maxwell's equations in free space and simple material media, physical significance of Gauss's law for electricity and magnetism, Faraday's law of induction, and Ampere-Maxwell law.

PGT extension

Displacement current derivation, wave equation for electromagnetic waves in free space and conducting media, Poynting vector, energy conservation, boundary conditions for electric and magnetic fields, and electromagnetic potentials.

Prerequisites

  • Vector calculus (Gradient, Divergence, Curl, Stokes' theorem, Divergence theorem)
  • Electrostatics and Magnetostatics fundamental laws
  • Time-varying magnetic and electric fields

Gauss's Law for Electricity (Maxwell's First Equation)

  • Relates the electric displacement field or electric field to the enclosed free charge density.
  • Differential form states that the divergence of the electric field is proportional to the local charge density, indicating that electric charges are sources and sinks of electric field lines.
  • Integral form states that the total electric flux through any closed surface is equal to the total charge enclosed divided by the permittivity of free space.

Gauss's Law for Magnetism (Maxwell's Second Equation)

  • States that the divergence of the magnetic field is identically zero everywhere.
  • Implies the non-existence of magnetic monopoles; magnetic field lines are continuous closed loops.
  • Integral form shows that the net magnetic flux through any closed Gaussian surface is always zero.

Faraday's Law of Induction (Maxwell's Third Equation)

  • Relates a time-varying magnetic field to a circulating electric field.
  • Differential form shows that the curl of the electric field equals the negative time rate of change of the magnetic field vector.
  • Explains the principle behind electric generators, transformers, and electromagnetic induction.

Ampere-Maxwell Law (Maxwell's Fourth Equation)

  • Relates magnetic fields to electric currents and the time rate of change of the electric displacement field.
  • Maxwell introduced the displacement current term to resolve the inconsistency of Ampere's law for non-steady currents in circuits with capacitors.
  • Differential form indicates that the curl of the magnetic field is the sum of conduction current density and displacement current density.

Derivations

Derivation of Displacement Current and Inconsistency of Ampere's Law

Conditions: Time-varying electric fields, charging capacitor circuit

  • Consider Ampere's circuital law in differential form for steady currents: \nabla \times B = \mu_0 J.
  • Take the divergence of both sides: \nabla \cdot (\nabla \times B) = \mu_0 (\nabla \cdot J). Since the divergence of a curl is always zero, \nabla \cdot J = 0.
  • However, the equation of continuity for charge conservation is \nabla \cdot J + \frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} = 0, which implies \nabla \cdot J \neq 0 for time-varying fields.
  • Substitute Gauss's law \rho = \nabla \cdot D (or \rho = \epsilon_0 \nabla \cdot E) into the continuity equation: \nabla \cdot J + \frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\nabla \cdot (\epsilon_0 E)) = 0.
  • Rearrange to write \nabla \cdot \left( J + \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial E}{\partial t} \right) = 0.
  • Define the displacement current density as J_d = \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial E}{\partial t}, leading to the modified Ampere-Maxwell law: \nabla \times B = \mu_0 J + \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial E}{\partial t}.

Result: \nabla \times B = \mu_0 J + \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial E}{\partial t}

Derivation of the Electromagnetic Wave Equation in Free Space

Conditions: Free space (\rho = 0, J = 0)

  • Start with Maxwell's curl equations for free space: \nabla \times E = -\frac{\partial B}{\partial t} and \nabla \times B = \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial E}{\partial t}.
  • Take the curl of Faraday's equation: \nabla \times (\nabla \times E) = \nabla \times \left(-\frac{\partial B}{\partial t}\right) = -\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\nabla \times B).
  • Use the vector identity \nabla \times (\nabla \times E) = \nabla(\nabla \cdot E) - \nabla^2 E. In free space, \nabla \cdot E = 0, so \nabla \times (\nabla \times E) = -\nabla^2 E.
  • Substitute the Ampere-Maxwell law into the right side: -\nabla^2 E = -\frac{\partial}{\partial t} (\mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial E}{\partial t}) = -\mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial^2 E}{\partial t^2}.
  • Cancel negative signs to obtain the wave equation for electric field: \nabla^2 E = \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial^2 E}{\partial t^2}.
  • Identify the propagation speed as c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}}.

Result: \nabla^2 E = \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial^2 E}{\partial t^2} \text{ and } \nabla^2 B = \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial^2 B}{\partial t^2}

Plane Electromagnetic Wave Propagation

Draw: Orthogonal sinusoidal waves representing E vector along y-axis and B vector along z-axis, propagating along the x-axis, with arrows indicating cross-product direction E \times B.

Interpret: Demonstrates transverse nature of electromagnetic waves where electric and magnetic fields oscillate perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation.

Exam use: Frequently asked in conceptual questions regarding field orientations and wave vector direction.

Charging Capacitor and Displacement Current Loop

Draw: A parallel plate capacitor connected to a battery, showing conduction current in wires and a dashed contour loop between capacitor plates passing through the electric field.

Interpret: Proves that Ampere's law fails without the displacement current term because the contour can be bounded by different surfaces with the same edge.

Exam use: Tested via theoretical reasoning questions on consistency of Maxwell's equations.

Hertz's Spark Gap Experiment

Draw: An induction coil connected to a spark gap (transmitter) and a distant resonant wire loop with a micro spark gap (receiver).

Interpret: First experimental confirmation of the production and detection of high-frequency electromagnetic waves predicted by Maxwell.

Exam use: Historical and factual multiple-choice questions in PGT exams.

Solved numericals

TGT core

If the electric field in a region is given by E = E_0 \sin(kx - \omega t)\hat{j}, find the corresponding magnetic field B assuming propagation in free space.

  • Use Faraday's law in differential form: \nabla \times E = -\frac{\partial B}{\partial t}.
  • Calculate the curl of E: \nabla \times E = \left| \begin{matrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & E_0 \sin(kx - \omega t) & 0 \end{matrix} \right| = \hat{k} \frac{\partial}{\partial x} [E_0 \sin(kx - \omega t)] = k E_0 \cos(kx - \omega t) \hat{k}.
  • Integrate with respect to time t: B = - \int (k E_0 \cos(kx - \omega t) \hat{k}) dt = \frac{k E_0}{\omega} \sin(kx - \omega t) \hat{k}.
  • Since \frac{\omega}{k} = c and \frac{1}{c} = \sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}, write B in terms of amplitude B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c}.

Answer: B = \frac{E_0}{c} \sin(kx - \omega t)\hat{k}

PGT extension

A parallel plate capacitor with circular plates of radius R = 0.1 m is being charged with a constant charging current I = 2 A. Calculate the displacement current density through the plates.

  • The area of the circular plates is A = \pi R^2 = \pi (0.1)^2 = 0.01\pi \text{ m}^2.
  • The total displacement current in the capacitor equals the conduction current in the connecting wires: I_d = I = 2 \text{ A}.
  • Displacement current density J_d is given by J_d = \frac{I_d}{A}.
  • Substitute the values: J_d = \frac{2}{0.01\pi} = \frac{200}{\pi} \approx 63.66 \text{ A/m}^2.

Answer: 63.66 A/m^2

PGT extension

In free space, the magnetic field of an electromagnetic wave is given by B = 10^{-7} \sin(10^7 x - \omega t) \hat{k} \text{ Tesla}. Determine the angular frequency \omega and the electric field amplitude E_0.

  • From the argument of the sine function, the wave number is k = 10^7 \text{ m}^{-1}.
  • The relation between angular frequency and wave number for light in free space is \omega = c k.
  • Substitute c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s} and k = 10^7 \text{ m}^{-1}: \omega = (3 \times 10^8) \times (10^7) = 3 \times 10^{15} \text{ rad/s}.
  • The relation between electric and magnetic field amplitudes is E_0 = c B_0.
  • Substitute B_0 = 10^{-7} \text{ T}: E_0 = (3 \times 10^8) \times (10^{-7}) = 30 \text{ V/m}.

Answer: \omega = 3 \times 10^{15} \text{ rad/s} and E_0 = 30 \text{ V/m}

Assumptions and limitations

  • Maxwell's equations are formulated within the framework of classical macroscopic electrodynamics and do not account for quantum electrodynamic effects at atomic scales.
  • Material media are assumed to be linear, isotropic, and homogeneous unless tensor permittivities and permeabilities are explicitly introduced.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Displacement current is an actual flow of physical electric charges across the capacitor gap.

Correct: Displacement current is not a movement of charge, but a term proportional to the time rate of change of the electric field.

It produces a magnetic field just like conduction current does, but no real charge crosses the dielectric or vacuum gap.

Common error: Gauss's law for magnetism implies magnetic charges or monopoles exist in pairs.

Correct: Gauss's law for magnetism states that \nabla \cdot B = 0, proving magnetic monopoles do not exist in classical physics.

Every magnetic field line is a closed continuous loop with no starting or ending point.

Quick revision

  • \nabla \cdot E = \rho / \epsilon_0 (Sources of E are charges)
  • \nabla \cdot B = 0 (No magnetic monopoles)
  • \nabla \times E = -\partial B / \partial t (Changing B induces E)
  • \nabla \times B = \mu_0 J + \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \partial E / \partial t (Currents and changing E induce B)
  • Electromagnetic waves are transverse, with c = 1 / \sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}

Must remember

  • Maxwell's first equation is Gauss's Law for Electricity: $\nabla \cdot E = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}$.
  • Maxwell's second equation is Gauss's Law for Magnetism: $\nabla \cdot B = 0$, indicating the non-existence of magnetic monopoles.
  • Maxwell's third equation is Faraday's Law of Induction: $\nabla \times E = -\frac{\partial B}{\partial t}$.
  • Maxwell's fourth equation is the Ampere-Maxwell Law: $\nabla \times B = \mu_0 J + \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial E}{\partial t}$.
  • Displacement current was introduced by Maxwell to resolve the inconsistency of Ampere's Law for non-steady currents and accounts for time-varying electric fields.
  • The speed of electromagnetic waves in a vacuum is given by $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}}$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of Maxwell's equations mathematically states the non-existence of magnetic monopoles?

Explanation: Gauss's Law for Magnetism, given by $\nabla \cdot B = 0$, states that the net magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero, implying magnetic monopoles do not exist.

2. What is the differential form of Gauss's Law for Electricity in free space?

Explanation: Maxwell's first equation (Gauss's Law for Electricity) in differential form is $\nabla \cdot E = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}$.

3. Faraday's Law of Induction relates a time-varying magnetic field to which of the following?

Explanation: Faraday's Law states that $\nabla \times E = -\frac{\partial B}{\partial t}$, which means a time-varying magnetic field creates a circulating electric field.

4. Who introduced the displacement current term into Ampere's Law?

Explanation: James Clerk Maxwell introduced displacement current to fix the mathematical inconsistency in Ampere's Law for non-steady currents.

5. What is the unit of the electric displacement field $E$ in Maxwell's equations?

Explanation: The electric field $E$ has units of Volts per meter ($\text{V}/\text{m}$).

6. In the Ampere-Maxwell law, what does the term $\epsilon_0 \frac{\partial E}{\partial t}$ represent?

Explanation: The term $\epsilon_0 \frac{\partial E}{\partial t}$ represents the displacement current density contributing to the magnetic field.

7. What does the integral form of Gauss's Law for Magnetism, $\oint_S B \cdot dA = 0$, signify?

Explanation: Since the net flux through any closed surface is zero, whatever magnetic field lines enter the surface must also leave it, meaning they form closed loops.

8. What is the speed of electromagnetic waves in free space expressed in terms of fundamental constants?

Explanation: From Maxwell's wave equation derivation, the speed of light in vacuum is $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}}$.

9. Which of Maxwell's equations is associated with the principle behind electric generators and transformers?

Explanation: Faraday's Law explains electromagnetic induction, which is the operational principle behind generators and transformers.

10. What is the differential form of the Ampere-Maxwell Law in free space where $J = 0$?

Explanation: When conduction current density $J$ is zero, the differential Ampere-Maxwell Law reduces to $\nabla \times B = \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial E}{\partial t}$.

11. Which misconception about displacement current is correct according to classical electrodynamics?

Explanation: Displacement current is not actual charge motion, but an effective current due to a time-varying electric field that produces a magnetic field.

12. What is the integral form of Faraday's Law for a stationary loop $C$ bounding surface $S$?

Explanation: The integral form of Faraday's Law is $\oint_C E \cdot dl = -\frac{d}{dt} \int_S B \cdot dA$.

13. What are the units of volume charge density $\rho$ in Gauss's Law for Electricity?

Explanation: Volume charge density $\rho$ is charge per unit volume, measured in Coulombs per cubic meter ($\text{C}/\text{m}^3$).

14. In a material medium, permeability $\mu_0$ and permittivity $\epsilon_0$ in Maxwell's equations are replaced by:

Explanation: In material media, free space values $\mu_0$ and $\epsilon_0$ are replaced by the material's permeability $\mu$ and permittivity $\epsilon$.

15. What does the curl of the electric field vector represent in Maxwell's third equation?

Explanation: Differential Faraday's Law equates $\nabla \times E$ to $-\frac{\partial B}{\partial t}$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. If the electric field in a region is given by $E = E_0 \sin(\$\omega$ t - kz) \hat{j}$, what is the corresponding magnetic field direction according to Maxwell's equations?

Explanation: The direction of propagation is $\hat{k}$ (along $z$). Since $E$ is along $\hat{j}$, the magnetic field $B$ must be along $\hat{i}$ so that the cross product $E \times B$ points in the direction of wave propagation ($\hat{j} \times \hat{i} = -\hat{k}$ is incorrect; $\hat{i} \times \hat{j} = \hat{k}$, so $B$ is along $\hat{i}$ for $E \times B$ to be along $\hat{k}$).

2. Which of Maxwell's equations directly shows that electric field lines originate from electric charges?

Explanation: Gauss's Law for Electricity states that $\nabla \cdot E = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}$, indicating that charges act as sources and sinks for electric field lines.

3. In an electromagnetic wave, the ratio of the magnitudes of the electric field to the magnetic field ($E/B$) is equal to:

Explanation: In an electromagnetic wave, the ratio $E/B = c$, the speed of light in vacuum.

4. Why did Maxwell add the displacement current term to Ampere's circuital law?

Explanation: Original Ampere's law failed for circuits with capacitors because the curl of B was inconsistent with the divergence of J; adding displacement current resolved this via the continuity equation.

5. What is the value of the divergence of the magnetic field vector $B$ everywhere in space?

Explanation: By Gauss's Law for Magnetism, $\nabla \cdot B = 0$ everywhere.

6. The dimensions of $\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}}$ are the same as:

Explanation: The quantity $\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}}$ represents the speed of light $c$, which has dimensions of velocity ($LT^{-1}$).

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of Maxwell's equations represents the absence of isolated magnetic charges?

Explanation: $\nabla \cdot B = 0$ (Gauss's Law for Magnetism) proves that magnetic monopoles do not exist.

2. If a parallel plate capacitor is being charged, the displacement current between the plates is:

Explanation: To maintain continuity of current, the displacement current across the capacitor gap equals the conduction current in the wires.

3. What is the differential form of Faraday's Law of Induction?

Explanation: Faraday's Law in differential form is $\nabla \times E = -\frac{\partial B}{\partial t}$.

4. What physical quantity does the closed surface integral $\oint_S E \cdot dA$ calculate?

Explanation: The surface integral of the electric field over a closed surface $S$ gives the total electric flux.

5. In free space, if the electric field is static (time-independent), what does Faraday's Law yield?

Explanation: If the electric field is static, or if the magnetic field is time-independent ($\frac{\partial B}{\partial t} = 0$), then $\nabla \times E = 0$, indicating an electrostatic field is conservative.

6. Which of the following describes the correct pairing of Maxwell's equations with their physical laws?

Explanation: The second equation is Gauss's Law for Magnetism. (1st is Gauss for Elec, 3rd is Faraday, 4th is Ampere-Maxwell).

7. What is the SI unit of displacement current density?

Explanation: Current density, whether conduction or displacement, is measured in amperes per square meter ($\text{A}/\text{m}^2$).

8. Maxwell's equations form the foundation of which branch of physics?

Explanation: Maxwell's equations are the fundamental set of partial differential equations underlying classical electrodynamics.

9. What does the term $\mu_0 J$ in the Ampere-Maxwell equation represent?

Explanation: The term $\mu_0 J$ represents the contribution of real conduction current density $J$ to the magnetic field.

10. How do Maxwell's equations behave under parity (spatial inversion) transformation?

Explanation: Electric field is a polar vector (reverses sign under parity), whereas the magnetic field is an axial vector / pseudovector (does not reverse sign under parity).
