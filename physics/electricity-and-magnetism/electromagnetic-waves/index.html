Electricity and Magnetism

Electromagnetic Waves

Electromagnetic waves are coupled oscillating electric and magnetic fields propagating through space, transferring energy and momentum. They are transverse waves generated by accelerated charges and travel through free space with a constant speed equal to the speed of light.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Basic characteristics of electromagnetic waves, transverse nature, electromagnetic spectrum order, wavelength/frequency ranges, and the relation between electric and magnetic field amplitudes (E = cB).

PGT extension

Maxwell's equations, displacement current, derivation of the wave equation for electric and magnetic fields from Maxwell's equations, Poynting vector, energy density, momentum, and radiation pressure.

Prerequisites

  • Maxwell's equations in differential and integral forms
  • Vector calculus (curl, divergence, and vector identities)
  • Alternating current fundamentals and displacement current concept

Nature and Generation of Electromagnetic Waves

  • Electromagnetic waves are produced by accelerated or oscillating charges, which create time-varying electric fields that in turn induce time-varying magnetic fields.
  • The electric field vector (E), magnetic field vector (B), and the direction of propagation (k) are mutually perpendicular, forming a right-handed triad (E × B points along k).
  • They are transverse in nature and do not require any material medium for propagation, travelling through a vacuum with speed c = 1 / sqrt(μ0ε0).

Maxwell's Equations and Displacement Current

  • Maxwell modified Ampere's circuital law by introducing the displacement current density Jd = ε0 (∂E / ∂t) to maintain consistency with the equation of continuity.
  • Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction ensures that a changing magnetic field produces an electric field (curl E = -∂B / ∂t).
  • The generalized Ampere-Maxwell law states that magnetic fields can be generated by both conduction currents and changing electric fields.

Energy, Momentum, and Poynting Vector

  • The energy density associated with an electromagnetic wave is shared equally between the electric field component (&frac{1}{2}ε0E2) and the magnetic field component (&frac{1}{2μ0}B2).
  • The rate of energy transport per unit area is given by the Poynting vector S = &frac{1}{mu0} (E × B).
  • Electromagnetic waves exert radiation pressure on surfaces they strike, transferring linear momentum p = U / c when completely absorbed.

Electromagnetic Spectrum

  • The electromagnetic spectrum spans a continuous range of frequencies and wavelengths, categorized from radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, to gamma rays.
  • All electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed c in free space, but differ significantly in their methods of generation and interaction with matter.
  • High-frequency waves (X-rays, gamma rays) exhibit more particle-like interactions, whereas low-frequency waves (radio, microwaves) exhibit pronounced wave characteristics.

Derivations

Derivation of Wave Equation for Electric Field in Vacuum

Conditions: Free space (charge density &rho; = 0, current density J = 0)

  • Start with Faraday's law in differential form: \nabla \times E = -\frac{\partial B}{\partial t}
  • Take the curl of both sides: \nabla \times (\nabla \times E) = \nabla \times \left(-\frac{\partial B}{\partial t}\right) = -\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\nabla \times B)
  • Use the vector identity \nabla \times (\nabla \times E) = \nabla(\nabla \cdot E) - \nabla^{2}E and noting that ∇ · E = 0 in free space, obtaining -\nabla^{2}E = -\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\nabla \times B)
  • Substitute Ampere-Maxwell law in free space \nabla \times B = \mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0}\frac{\partial E}{\partial t} into the equation
  • Resulting in \nabla^{2}E = \mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\partial^{2}E}{\partial t^{2}}

Result: \nabla^{2}E = \mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\partial^{2}E}{\partial t^{2}} with wave speed v = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}}

Relation between Electric and Magnetic Field Amplitudes

Conditions: Monochromatic plane electromagnetic wave propagating in vacuum along z-axis

  • Let the electric field be E = E_{0} \sin(kz - \omega t)\hat{i} and magnetic field be B = B_{0} \sin(kz - \omega t)\hat{j}
  • Apply Faraday's law \nabla \times E = -\frac{\partial B}{\partial t} in Cartesian coordinates
  • Evaluate the curl of E: \frac{\partial E_{x}}{\partial z} \hat{j} = k E_{0} \cos(kz - \omega t)\hat{j}
  • Evaluate the time derivative of B: -\frac{\partial B}{\partial t} = \omega B_{0} \cos(kz - \omega t)\hat{j}
  • Equate the components: k E_{0} = \omega B_{0}, which simplifies using \frac{\omega}{k} = c

Result: E_{0} = c B_{0} \text{ or } \frac{E}{B} = c

Solved numericals

TGT core

The amplitude of the magnetic field part of a harmonic electromagnetic wave in vacuum is B_{0} = 510 nT. What is the amplitude of the electric field part of the wave?

  • Identify given values: B_{0} = 510 \times 10^{-9} T, c = 3 \times 10^{8} m/s.
  • Use the relation between electric and magnetic field amplitudes: E_{0} = c B_{0}.
  • Substitute values: E_{0} = (3 \times 10^{8} m/s) \times (510 \times 10^{-9} T).
  • Calculate result: E_{0} = 153 V/m.

Answer: 153 V/m

PGT extension

Light with an energy flux of 18 W/cm^{2} falls on a non-reflecting surface at normal incidence. If the surface has an area of 20 cm^{2}, find the average total momentum delivered to the surface during a 30-minute time span.

  • Convert intensity to SI units: I = 18 W/cm^{2} = 18 \times 10^{4} W/m^{2} = 1.8 \times 10^{5} W/m^{2}.
  • Calculate total energy incident: U = I \times A \times t where A = 20 \times 10^{-4} m^{2} and t = 30 \times 60 = 1800 s.
  • U = (1.8 \times 10^{5}) \times (20 \times 10^{-4}) \times 1800 = 6.48 \times 10^{5} J.
  • For complete absorption, momentum p = U / c = \frac{6.48 \times 10^{5}}{3 \times 10^{8}}.
  • Calculate final momentum: p = 2.16 \times 10^{-3} kg \cdot m/s.

Answer: 2.16 × 10^{-3} kg·m/s

PGT extension

In a plane electromagnetic wave, the electric field oscillates sinusoidally at a frequency of 2.0 × 10^{10} Hz and an amplitude of 48 V/m. Calculate the average energy density of the electromagnetic wave.

  • Identify given values: E_{0} = 48 V/m, \varepsilon_{0} = 8.854 \times 10^{-12} C^{2}/N \cdot m^{2}.
  • Recall the formula for average energy density: u_{avg} = \frac{1}{2} \varepsilon_{0} E_{0}^{2}.
  • Substitute values: u_{avg} = \frac{1}{2} \times (8.854 \times 10^{-12}) \times (48)^{2}.
  • Calculate: u_{avg} = 0.5 \times 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \times 2304 = 1.02 \times 10^{-8} J/m^{3}.

Answer: 1.02 × 10^{-8} J/m^{3}

Assumptions and limitations

  • Plane wave approximation assumes wavefronts are infinite flat planes, valid only locally far from small localized sources.
  • Linear medium assumptions apply; nonlinear optical effects at extremely high laser intensities are neglected.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Electromagnetic waves require a material medium like ether or air to propagate.

Correct: Electromagnetic waves are self-propagating oscillations of electric and magnetic fields and can travel through a perfect vacuum.

Unlike mechanical waves (sound, water waves), EM waves do not rely on mechanical vibrations of matter.

Common error: The magnetic field in an electromagnetic wave carries much less energy than the electric field.

Correct: The electric and magnetic fields carry equal amounts of average energy density in free space.

Since E<sub>0</sub> = cB<sub>0</sub> and u<sub>E</sub> = &frac{1}{2}&epsilon;<sub>0</sub>E<sub>0</sub><sup>2</sup> while u<sub>B</sub> = B<sub>0</sub><sup>2</sup> / (2&mu;<sub>0</sub>), substituting c<sup>2</sup> = 1/(&mu;<sub>0</sub>&epsilon;<sub>0</sub>) shows u<sub>E</sub> = u<sub>B</sub>.

Quick revision

  • EM waves are transverse waves with E and B fields mutually perpendicular and normal to the direction of wave propagation.
  • Speed of Electromagnetic Waves: c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}}
  • Field Amplitude Ratio: E_{0} = c B_{0}

Must remember

  • Electromagnetic waves are produced by accelerated or oscillating charges, generating mutually perpendicular electric field E, magnetic field B, and propagation vector k forming a right-handed triad.
  • The speed of electromagnetic waves in a vacuum is given by $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}}$.
  • Maxwell modified Ampere's circuital law by introducing the displacement current density $J_d = \varepsilon_0 \frac{\partial E}{\partial t}$.
  • The ratio of peak electric field to peak magnetic field in free space is equal to the speed of light: $E_0 = c B_0$.
  • The energy density in an electromagnetic wave is shared equally between the electric field component ($\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2$) and the magnetic field component ($\frac{1}{2\mu_0} B^2$).
  • The rate of energy transport per unit area is given by the Poynting vector $S = \frac{1}{\mu_0} (E \times B)$.
  • Radiation pressure for a completely absorbing surface is $P_{rad} = \frac{I}{c}$, and for a completely reflecting surface, it is $P_{rad} = \frac{2I}{c}$.
  • All electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed $c$ in free space but differ in their frequency and wavelength ranges spanning from radio waves to gamma rays.

Glossary

Displacement CurrentA quantity appearing in Maxwell's equations defined as $I_d = \varepsilon_0 \frac{\partial \Phi_E}{\partial t}$, which acts like a conduction current to produce a magnetic field.
Poynting VectorVector representing the directional energy flux density of an electromagnetic field, given by $S = \frac{1}{\mu_0}(E \times B)$.
Radiation PressureThe mechanical pressure exerted upon any surface exposed to electromagnetic radiation.
Permittivity of Free SpaceConstant $\varepsilon_0$ measuring the resistance that is encountered when forming an electric field in a vacuum.
Permeability of Free SpaceConstant $\mu_0$ measuring the ability of a vacuum to permit magnetic field lines.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following generates electromagnetic waves?

Explanation: Accelerated or oscillating charges produce time-varying electric and magnetic fields, which propagate as electromagnetic waves.

2. The speed of electromagnetic waves in free space is given by:

Explanation: From Maxwell's equations, the speed of light in vacuum is derived as $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0}}$.

3. In an electromagnetic wave, the angle between the electric field vector $E$, magnetic field vector $B$, and propagation direction $k$ is:

Explanation: The vectors $E$, $B$, and $k$ are mutually perpendicular to each other, forming a right-handed triad, meaning the angle between any two of them is $90^\circ$.

4. What is the relation between the peak amplitudes of the electric field ($E_0$) and magnetic field ($B_0$) in a vacuum?

Explanation: The amplitudes are related by $E_0 = c B_0$, where $c$ is the speed of light.

5. Which scientist modified Ampere's circuital law by introducing displacement current?

Explanation: James Clerk Maxwell introduced the displacement current term to Ampere's circuital law to make it logically consistent with the conservation of charge.

6. The displacement current is produced by:

Explanation: Displacement current is defined as $$I_{d}$ = \varepsilon_0 \frac{\partial \$\phi_{E}$}{\partial t}$, which arises due to a time-varying electric field.

7. The Poynting vector $S$ represents:

Explanation: The Poynting vector $S = \frac{1}{\mu_0}(E \times B)$ gives the directional energy flux (energy transported per unit time per unit area) of an electromagnetic field.

8. In an electromagnetic wave propagating in a vacuum, the average energy density stored in the electric field is:

Explanation: Energy in an EM wave is shared equally between the electric and magnetic field components, so $u_E = u_B$.

9. Radiation pressure exerted by an electromagnetic wave of intensity $I$ on a completely absorbing surface normal to the beam is:

Explanation: For complete absorption, momentum transferred per second per unit area gives radiation pressure $P_{rad} = \frac{I}{c}$.

10. Which of the following waves has the highest frequency in the electromagnetic spectrum?

Explanation: $\gamma$ rays occupy the highest frequency and shortest wavelength end of the electromagnetic spectrum.

11. The dimensions of the Poynting vector $S$ are:

Explanation: Poynting vector is energy per unit area per unit time, so its dimensions are $[Energy / (Area \times Time)] = [ML^2 T^{-2} / (L^2 T)] = [MT^{-3}]$.

12. Which component of Maxwell's equations explicitly states that magnetic monopoles do not exist?

Explanation: Gauss's law for magnetism, $\nabla \cdot B = 0$, implies that magnetic field lines are continuous loops and isolated magnetic monopoles do not exist.

13. If the peak value of electric field in a plane electromagnetic wave is $$V_{0}$$, the average energy density of the wave is:

Explanation: Total average energy density is the sum of electric and magnetic energy densities, each equal to $\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon_0 E_0^2$, yielding a total average energy density of $\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E_0^2$ (or replacing field symbol with $$V_{0}$$).

14. Electromagnetic waves are transverse in nature. This is established by the fact that:

Explanation: Polarization is a characteristic strictly exhibited by transverse waves, confirming the transverse nature of electromagnetic waves.

15. The propagation constant $k$ (wave number) and angular frequency $\$\omega$$ are related to the speed of light $c$ by:

Explanation: The relationship is given by $\$\omega$ = vk$, and since the wave travels at speed $c$ in a vacuum, $\$\omega$ = ck$ or $k = \frac{\$\omega$}{c}$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The magnetic field in a plane electromagnetic wave is given by $B_y = 2 \times 10^{-7} \sin(\text{1000}t + 0.005 x)$ tesla. The expression for the electric field is:

Explanation: Peak electric field $E_0 = c B_0 = (3 \times 10^8) \times (2 \times 10^{-7}) = 60$ V/m. Since $B$ is along $y$ and propagation is along $-x$ (indicated by $+1000t + 0.005x$), $E$ must be along $z$ to form a right-handed triad with $k$.

2. If $\varepsilon_0$ and $\mu_0$ are the electric permittivity and magnetic permeability of free space, respectively, the dimensions of $(\mu_0 \varepsilon_0)^{-1/2}$ are:

Explanation: The expression $(\mu_0 \varepsilon_0)^{-1/2}$ represents the speed of light $c$, whose dimensional formula is $[LT^{-1}]$.

3. Light with an energy flux of $18 \text{ W/m}^2$ falls on a non-reflecting surface at normal incidence. If the surface has an area of $20 \text{ cm}^2$, the total momentum delivered to the surface in 30 minutes is:

Explanation: Total energy $U = I \times A \times t = 18 \times (20 \times 10^{-4}) \times (30 \times 60) = 64.8 \text{ J}$. Momentum $p = \frac{U}{c} = \frac{64.8}{3 \times 10^8} = 2.16 \times 10^{-4} \text{ kg m/s}$.

4. The rms value of the electric field of an electromagnetic wave is $E_{\text{rms}} = 72 \text{ V/m}$. What is the peak value of the magnetic field?

Explanation: Peak electric field $E_0 = \sqrt{2} E_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{2} \times 72 \approx 101.82 \text{ V/m}$. Peak magnetic field $B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c} = \frac{101.82}{3 \times 10^8} \approx 3.39 \times 10^{-7} \text{ T}$.

5. A parallel plate capacitor with circular plates of radius $R$ is being charged with a current $I$. The displacement current acrossing the center between the plates is:

Explanation: To maintain continuity of current in the circuit, the displacement current across the plates of a charging capacitor is exactly equal to the conduction current $I$ supplying the plates.

6. The electric field of an electromagnetic wave is given by $E = 100 \sin(6 \times 10^8 t + 4x) \text{ V/m}$. The wavelength of the wave is:

Explanation: Comparing with $E = E_0 \sin(\$\omega$ t + kx)$, wave number $k = 4 \text{ rad/m}$. Wavelength $\lambda = \frac{2\$\pi$}{k} = \frac{2\$\pi$}{4} = \frac{\$\pi$}{2} \text{ m}$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following electromagnetic radiations has the longest wavelength?

Explanation: Radio waves reside at the longest wavelength end of the electromagnetic spectrum.

2. In a plane electromagnetic wave, the vector $E \times B$ points in:

Explanation: The Poynting vector and wave propagation direction $k$ align with the direction of the cross product $E \times B$.

3. The energy density of a magnetic field in an electromagnetic wave with peak magnetic field $B_0$ is given by:

Explanation: The instantaneous magnetic energy density is $\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}$, and its peak/maximum value is $\frac{B_0^2}{2\mu_0}$.

4. Which equations are collectively known as Maxwell's equations in free space?

Explanation: Maxwell's equations comprise Gauss's law for electricity, Gauss's law for magnetism, Faraday's law of induction, and the Ampere-Maxwell law.

5. If a surface is completely reflecting and an electromagnetic wave falls normally on it with intensity $I$, the radiation pressure is:

Explanation: For a completely reflecting surface, momentum is reversed upon reflection, doubling the momentum transfer and resulting in a radiation pressure of $P_{rad} = \frac{2I}{c}$.

6. The ratio of the magnitudes of electric and magnetic fields in an electromagnetic wave has the dimensions of:

Explanation: Since $E / B = c$, the ratio has the dimensions of speed or velocity ($LT^{-1}$).

7. Which of the following electromagnetic waves is used in radar systems for navigation and tracking?

Explanation: Microwaves are short-wavelength radio waves commonly used in radar systems due to their directive properties.

8. The existence of electromagnetic waves was first experimentally confirmed by:

Explanation: Heinrich Hertz experimentally demonstrated the generation and detection of electromagnetic waves in 1888.

9. The electric field part of an electromagnetic wave is $E = 3.1 \times 10^4 \cos[\$\pi$ \times 10^6 (t - x/c)] \text{ V/m}$. What is the intensity of the wave?

Explanation: Intensity $I = \frac{1}{2} c \varepsilon_0 E_0^2$. Substituting $E_0 = 3.1 \times 10^4 \text{ V/m}$, $c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$, and $\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \times 10^{-12} \text{ F/m}$ yields approximately $1.28 \times 10^3 \text{ W/m}^2$.

10. What is the source of the magnetic field in a region where there are no conduction currents?

Explanation: According to the generalized Ampere-Maxwell law, a time-varying electric field (displacement current) generates a magnetic field even in the absence of any conduction current.
