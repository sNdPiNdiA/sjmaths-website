Hooke's Law and Elasticity

Hooke's law states that within the elastic limit, the stress developed in a body is directly proportional to the strain produced. For a simple spring, it establishes a linear relation between restoring force and displacement from equilibrium.

Fundamentals of Hooke's Law

  • States that extension (or compression) x is directly proportional to the applied load F within the proportional limit.
  • Mathematically expressed as F = -kx, where k is the stiffness constant or spring constant.
  • The negative sign indicates that the restoring force acts in the direction opposite to the displacement.

Stress-Strain Relationship

  • Normal stress (sigma) is defined as restoring force per unit area, measured in N/m² or Pascal.
  • Longitudinal strain (epsilon) is the fractional change in length (Δl / l).
  • Within elastic limits, Hooke's law is written as Stress = E × Strain, where E is the relevant modulus of elasticity.

Elastic Moduli

  • Young's modulus (Y) relates longitudinal stress to longitudinal strain for solid wires and rods.
  • Bulk modulus (B) relates hydrostatic pressure to volumetric strain, applicable to fluids and solids alike.
  • Modulus of rigidity (η) relates shearing stress to shearing strain, governing shape deformation.

Energy Stored in an Elastic Medium

  • Work done in stretching an elastic spring is stored as potential energy within the material.
  • Elastic potential energy density is proportional to the square of the strain multiplied by the elastic modulus.
  • Total potential energy U = 1/2 kx² for a linearly elastic spring.

Derivations

Potential Energy of a Stretched Spring

Conditions: Spring obeys Hooke's law up to extension x

  • Consider an external force F_ext = kx required to stretch a spring slowly by an infinitesimal displacement dx.
  • The elemental work done dW is given by dW = F_{ext} dx = kx dx.
  • Integrate the work done from zero extension to a final extension x: W = \int_{0}^{x} kx dx = \frac{1}{2}kx^2.
  • By conservation of energy, this work appears as elastic potential energy U.

Result: U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2

Work Done in Stretching a Wire

Conditions: Wire of initial length L and cross-sectional area A stretched by length &Delta;L

  • From Hooke's law, tension T in the wire for an elongation l is given by T = \frac{YA}{L} l.
  • The small work done to further elongate the wire by dl is dW = T dl = \frac{YA}{L} l dl.
  • Integrating from l = 0 to l = \Delta L gives W = \int_{0}^{\Delta L} \frac{YA}{L} l dl = \frac{YA}{2L} (\Delta L)^2.
  • Expressing in terms of stress and strain: W = \frac{1}{2} \times (Stress) \times (Strain) \times (Volume).

Result: W = \frac{1}{2} \frac{YA (\Delta L)^2}{L}

Solved numericals

TGT core

A spring of force constant 200 N/m is compressed by 5 cm. Calculate the elastic potential energy stored in the spring.

  • Identify given values: k = 200 N/m, x = 5 cm = 0.05 m.
  • Apply the formula for potential energy: U = 1/2 kx².
  • Substitute values: U = 0.5 × 200 × (0.05)².
  • Calculate result: U = 100 × 0.0025 = 0.25 J.

Answer: 0.25 J

PGT extension

A steel wire of length 2 m and cross-sectional area 1 mm&sup2; is suspended vertically. When a mass of 10 kg is attached to its lower end, find the elongation produced. (Given Y_steel = 2 &times; 10^{11} N/m&sup2;, g = 9.8 m/s&sup2;)

  • Identify parameters: L = 2 m, A = 1 mm² = 10^{-6} m², M = 10 kg, g = 9.8 m/s², Y = 2 × 10^{11} N/m².
  • Force F = Mg = 10 × 9.8 = 98 N.
  • Rearrange Young's modulus formula for elongation: ΔL = FL / (YA).
  • Substitute values: ΔL = (98 × 2) / (2 × 10^{11} × 10^{-6}).
  • Calculate: ΔL = 196 / (2 × 10^5) = 9.8 × 10^{-4} m = 0.98 mm.

Answer: 9.8 × 10^{-4} m (0.98 mm)

PGT extension

Two identical springs of spring constant k are connected in parallel and then in series. Find the ratio of their effective spring constants (k_{parallel} / k_{series}).

  • For springs in parallel: k_{parallel} = k + k = 2k.
  • For springs in series: 1 / k_{series} = 1/k + 1/k = 2/k, so k_{series} = k / 2.
  • Take the ratio: k_{parallel} / k_{series} = 2k / (k / 2) = 4.

Answer: 4

Assumptions and limitations

  • Hooke's law is an empirical approximation valid only within the proportional (elastic) limit of a material.
  • Assumes homogeneous, isotropic materials when defining macroscopic elastic constants like Young's and Bulk moduli.
  • Fails under large deformations where plastic flow, permanent set, or material rupture occurs.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Hooke's law is a universal law applicable to all materials under any load.

Correct: Hooke's law holds true only up to the proportional limit for elastic deformations.

Beyond the elastic limit, stress is no longer proportional to strain, and permanent deformation ensues.

Common error: Cutting a spring in half halves its spring constant.

Correct: Cutting a spring into half doubles its spring constant.

Spring constant is inversely proportional to its length (k &propto; 1/L); shorter springs are stiffer.

Common error: Modulus of elasticity has dimensions of force.

Correct: Modulus of elasticity has dimensions of pressure or stress (ML^{-1}T^{-2}).

It is the ratio of stress to dimensionless strain.

Quick revision

  • Hooke's law: F = -kx or Stress = E × Strain.
  • Young's modulus governs longitudinal strain, Bulk modulus governs volumetric strain, and Rigidity modulus governs shear.
  • Elastic potential energy stored in a spring is U = 1/2 kx².
  • Spring constant in series combination: 1/k_{eq} = sum(1/k_i); in parallel: k_{eq} = sum(k_i).

Must remember

  • Hooke's law states that extension or compression is directly proportional to the applied load within the proportional limit, expressed mathematically as $F = -kx$.
  • The negative sign in $F = -kx$ indicates that the restoring force acts in the direction opposite to the displacement.
  • Young's modulus ($Y$) relates longitudinal stress to longitudinal strain for solid wires and rods: $Y = \frac{FL}{A \Delta L}$.
  • Bulk modulus ($B$) relates hydrostatic pressure to volumetric strain: $B = -V \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta V}$, and compressibility is $K = \frac{1}{B}$.
  • Modulus of rigidity ($\eta$) governs shape deformation without volume change: $\eta = \frac{F/A}{\theta}$.
  • Elastic potential energy stored in a linearly elastic spring is given by $U = \frac{1}{2} kx^2$, and potential energy density is $u = \frac{1}{2} (Stress) \times (Strain)$.
  • Cutting a spring of length $L$ and spring constant $k$ into two equal halves doubles its spring constant to $2k$, since $k \propto \frac{1}{L}$.
  • The dimensions of any modulus of elasticity are $[ML^{-1}T^{-2}]$, which is identical to the dimensions of stress and pressure.

Glossary

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What does the negative sign in Hooke's law expression $F = -kx$ signify?

Explanation: The negative sign indicates that the restoring force is directed opposite to the displacement vector, returning the system to equilibrium.

2. If a spring of spring constant $k$ is cut into two equal halves, what is the spring constant of each new piece?

Explanation: Spring constant is inversely proportional to length ($k \propto 1/L$). Halving the length doubles the spring constant to $2k$.

3. What are the dimensional formula and SI units of the modulus of elasticity?

Explanation: Modulus of elasticity is stress divided by strain (dimensionless). Thus its dimensions are $[ML^{-1}T^{-2}]$ and its unit is Pascal or $N/m^2$.

4. Which elastic modulus is applicable to fluids (liquids and gases)?

Explanation: Fluids do not sustain shearing stress or tensile stress permanently, but they resist uniform compression, making the bulk modulus applicable.

5. The elastic potential energy stored in a stretched spring of constant $k$ for an extension $x$ is given by:

Explanation: Integrating the restorative force $F = kx$ with respect to displacement gives the work done, $U = \frac{1}{2} kx^2$.

6. What is compressibility defined as in relation to elastic moduli?

Explanation: Compressibility $K$ is defined as the inverse of the bulk modulus ($K = 1/B$). It measures how easily a substance can be compressed.

7. Within what limit does Hooke's law hold true for typical structural materials?

Explanation: Hooke's law is valid strictly within the proportional limit, where stress is linearly proportional to strain.

8. How is elastic potential energy density $u$ expressed in terms of stress and strain within the linear elastic regime?

Explanation: Potential energy density (energy per unit volume) is given by the area under the stress-strain curve, which evaluates to $\frac{1}{2} \times \text{Stress} \times \text{Strain}$.

9. The speed of a transverse wave in a stretched string depends on tension $T$ and mass per unit length $\mu$ as:

Explanation: Dimensional analysis and wave equation derivation for stretched strings yield $v = \sqrt{T/\mu}$.

10. Modulus of rigidity ($\eta$) is defined as the ratio of:

Explanation: Modulus of rigidity relates shearing stress to shearing strain ($\eta = \frac{F/A}{\$\theta$}$).

11. If two identical springs of constant $k$ are connected in series, the effective spring constant $k_{eff}$ is:

Explanation: For springs in series, $\frac{1}{k_{eff}} = \frac{1}{k} + \frac{1}{k}$, which yields $k_{eff} = k/2$.

12. If two identical springs of constant $k$ are connected in parallel, the effective spring constant $k_{eff}$ is:

Explanation: For springs in parallel, their stiffnesses add up directly, giving $k_{eff} = k + k = 2k$.

13. Which of the following statements regarding permanent deformation is correct?

Explanation: Exceeding the elastic limit causes permanent (plastic) deformation, meaning the material does not return to its original dimensions when unloaded.

14. What happens to the longitudinal strain of a wire if its length is doubled under the same tensile force, assuming elastic behavior?

Explanation: Longitudinal strain is $\Delta L / L$. If original length $L$ and extension $\Delta L$ both scale proportionally under identical stress, fractional strain remains constant for a given material and stress.

15. What is the primary difference between longitudinal stress and shearing stress?

Explanation: Normal/longitudinal stress acts perpendicularly to the surface area, whereas shearing stress acts tangentially or parallel to the surface.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. A wire of length $L$ and cross-sectional area $A$ is stretched by a force $F$. If Young's modulus of the material is $Y$, the work done in stretching the wire by an amount $\Delta L$ is:

Explanation: Work done $W = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Force} \times \text{Extension} = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{Y A \Delta L}{L}\right) \Delta L = \frac{YA (\Delta L)^2}{2L}$.

2. The bulk modulus of an ideal gas at constant temperature (isothermal process) is numerically equal to:

Explanation: For an isothermal process, $PV = \text{constant}$, differentiating gives $P \Delta V + V \Delta P = 0 \implies -V \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta V} = P$, so $B = P$.

3. Two wires A and B are made of the same material and have equal lengths. The radius of wire A is twice that of wire B. If they are stretched by the same load, the ratio of their elongations ($\Delta $L_{A}$ / \Delta $L_{B}$$) is:

Explanation: Since $\Delta L = \frac{FL}{A Y}$ and $A = \$\pi$ r^2$, elongation is inversely proportional to $r^2$. Thus $\frac{\Delta $L_{A}$}{\Delta $L_{B}$} = \frac{r_B^2}{r_A^2} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{4}$.

4. A spring with spring constant $k$ is stretched by distance $x$. The potential energy stored is $U$. If it is further stretched by an additional distance $x$, the total potential energy stored becomes:

Explanation: Initial extension is $x$ ($U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$). Total extension becomes $2x$. Total potential energy $U_{total} = \frac{1}{2}k(2x)^2 = 4 \left(\frac{1}{2}kx^2\right) = 4U$.

5. Which of the following physical quantities has dimensions different from the other three?

Explanation: Stress, Young's modulus, and Bulk modulus all have dimensions of pressure $[ML^{-1}T^{-2}]$. Strain is a dimensionless quantity.

6. The fractional change in volume of a glass slab under a uniform hydrostatic pressure $\Delta P$ is given by (where $B$ is bulk modulus):

Explanation: Bulk modulus is $B = -V \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta V}$, which rearranges to fractional volume change $\frac{\Delta V}{V} = -\frac{\Delta P}{B}$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What is the main physical significance of the elastic limit in material science?

Explanation: The elastic limit is the boundary up to which a material recovers its original dimensions completely upon load removal; beyond it, permanent deformation sets in.

2. Calculate the force required to stretch a spring of spring constant $200\text{ N/m}$ by $5\text{ cm}$ within its elastic limit.

Explanation: Using $F = kx$, where $k = 200\text{ N/m}$ and $x = 0.05\text{ m}$, we get $F = 200 \times 0.05 = 10\text{ N}$.

3. For an adiabatic process in an ideal gas, what is its bulk modulus $B_{ad}$ equal to?

Explanation: For an adiabatic process, $PV^\$\gamma$ = \text{constant}$, differentiating yields $B_{ad} = \$\gamma$ P$, where $\$\gamma$$ is the adiabatic index.

4. If a solid metal cube experiences hydraulic compression on all faces, which elastic modulus is directly involved?

Explanation: Hydraulic compression involves uniform volume change under hydrostatic pressure, which is governed by the bulk modulus.

5. What is the elastic potential energy stored in a spring of constant $100\text{ N/m}$ when extended by $0.2\text{ m}$?

Explanation: Using $U = \frac{1}{2} kx^2 = \frac{1}{2} (100) (0.2)^2 = 50 \times 0.04 = 2\text{ J}$.

6. Why does a spring become stiffer when it is cut into shorter pieces?

Explanation: Each segment experiences a larger fraction of stress for a given applied load because fewer bonds share the stretch, resulting in a higher spring constant ($k \propto 1/L$).

7. Which of the following correctly pairs an elastic modulus with its primary deformation type?

Explanation: Modulus of rigidity governs shearing deformation (change in shape without change in volume).

8. A uniform wire of length $L$ and radius $r$ is subjected to tensile force $F$. The extension produced is $\Delta L$. If the radius of the wire is halved ($r/2$) while keeping everything else same, the extension becomes:

Explanation: Since $\Delta L = \frac{FL}{\$\pi$ r^2 Y}$, extension is inversely proportional to $r^2$. Halving the radius increases the extension by a factor of $2^2 = 4$.

9. What is the dimensional formula for compressibility ($K$)?

Explanation: Compressibility is $K = 1/B$. Since bulk modulus $B$ has dimensions $[ML^{-1}T^{-2}]$, compressibility has dimensions $[M^{-1}LT^2]$ (inverse of pressure).

10. In the context of the stress-strain curve, what represents the yield point?

Explanation: The yield point marks the transition from elastic behavior to plastic behavior, where permanent deformation occurs upon load removal.
