Mechanics

Cantilever

A cantilever is a rigid structural member supported horizontally at one end and loaded at the other free end. It undergoes elastic bending under transverse loads, serving as a classic application of the theory of elasticity and bending moments.

Prerequisites

  • Hooke's Law and Elastic Constants

  • Bending Moment and Geometrical Moment of Inertia

Definition and Mechanics of a Cantilever

  • A cantilever is rigidly clamped at one end and free to move under loads applied at the other end.
  • Under a downward load at the free end, the upper fibers of the beam experience tension while the lower fibers experience compression.
  • The neutral surface or neutral axis is an imaginary layer within the beam that undergoes neither elongation nor compression during bending.

Bending Moment in a Cantilever

  • The external bending moment at a distance x from the fixed end due to a load W at the free end is given by W(l - x), where l is the length of the cantilever.
  • This external moment is balanced by the internal elastic restoring couple, expressed as Y · Ig / R, where R is the radius of curvature.
  • Equating external and internal moments yields the fundamental differential equation of the elastic curve: Y Ig (d2y/dx2) = W(l - x).

Geometrical Moment of Inertia (I<sub>g</sub>)

  • For a beam of rectangular cross-section with breadth b and thickness (depth) d, the geometrical moment of inertia about the neutral axis is Ig = bd3 / 12.
  • For a beam of circular cross-section with radius r, the geometrical moment of inertia is Ig = πr4 / 4.
  • The stiffness of a cantilever depends heavily on the depth d for rectangular beams due to the cubic dependence (d3).

Depression of a Cantilever

  • The vertical displacement (depression) δ at the free end of a rectangular cantilever loaded with mass M is proportional to l3 and inversely proportional to bd3.
  • Effect of weight of the cantilever: When the weight of the cantilever itself is considered, it is statically equivalent to a uniform distributed load, contributing an effective depression equal to one-third of that produced by an equivalent load concentrated at the free end.
  • End-loaded cantilevers provide a standard laboratory method for measuring the Young's modulus of a material.

Derivations

Depression at the Free End of a Cantilever of Rectangular Cross-Section

Conditions: Light cantilever of length l, breadth b, thickness d, loaded with load W at the free end, operating within elastic limits.

  • Consider a cantilever fixed at x = 0 and loaded with W at x = l. The bending moment at any section at distance x from the fixed end is M = W(l - x).
  • From the theory of bending, the internal resisting couple is Y Ig / R = Y Ig (d2y / dx2), where y is the depression at distance x.
  • Equating the bending moment to the internal couple: Y Ig (d2y / dx2) = W(l - x).
  • Integrating once with respect to x using boundary conditions (dy/dx = 0 at x = 0): dy/dx = (W / Y Ig) (lx - x2/2).
  • Integrating a second time using boundary conditions (y = 0 at x = 0): y = (W / Y Ig) (lx2/2 - x3/6).
  • At the free end x = l, substitute y = δ and Ig = bd3/12 to obtain the final depression.

Result: \delta = \frac{4Wl^3}{Ybd^3}

Depression of a Cantilever Loaded at the Free End Including Its Own Weight

Conditions: Cantilever of length l, mass m uniformly distributed along its length, loaded with additional mass M at the free end.

  • Consider an element of length dx at a distance x from the fixed end. The mass of this element is (m/l)dx.
  • The weight of this element acts through its center at distance x from the fixed end, contributing to the bending moment at any section.
  • The total bending moment at distance x is given by M = W(l - x) + \int_{x}^{l} \frac{m}{l} g (x' - x) dx'.
  • Integrating this gives the modified differential equation: Y Ig \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = W(l - x) + \frac{mg}{2l}(l - x)^2.
  • Integrating twice with appropriate boundary conditions and evaluating at x = l yields the total depression.

Result: \delta = \frac{Wl^3}{3Y I_g} + \frac{ml^3}{8Y I_g}

Solved numericals

TGT core

A rectangular cantilever of length 1 m, breadth 0.04 m, and thickness 0.01 m is depressed by 0.05 m when a load of 1 kg is attached to its free end. Calculate the Young's modulus of the material of the cantilever. (Take g = 9.8 m/s<sup>2</sup>)

  • Identify given data: l = 1 m, b = 0.04 m, d = 0.01 m, M = 1 kg, δ = 0.05 m, g = 9.8 m/s2.
  • Calculate load W = Mg = 1 × 9.8 = 9.8 N.
  • Use the formula for depression of a rectangular cantilever: δ = 4Wl3 / (Ybd3).
  • Rearrange for Y: Y = 4Wl3 / (δbd3).
  • Substitute values: Y = (4 × 9.8 × 13) / (0.05 × 0.04 × (0.01)3).
  • Calculate denominator: 0.05 × 0.04 × 10-6 = 2 × 10^{-9}.
  • Calculate Y: Y = 39.2 / (2 × 10^{-9}) = 1.96 × 10^{10} N/m2.

Answer: 1.96 × 10^{10} N/m2

PGT extension

A uniform cantilever of length 1.5 m and mass 2 kg is loaded with a mass of 3 kg at its free end. If the cross-section is rectangular with breadth 0.03 m and thickness 0.015 m, find the total depression at the free end. Take Y = 1.0 &times; 10<sup>11} N/m<sup>2</sup> and g = 9.8 m/s<sup>2</sup>.

  • Identify given values: l = 1.5 m, m = 2 kg, M = 3 kg, b = 0.03 m, d = 0.015 m, Y = 1.0 × 1011 N/m2, g = 9.8 m/s2.
  • Calculate geometrical moment of inertia Ig = bd3 / 12 = (0.03 × (0.015)3) / 12 = (0.03 × 3.375 × 10^{-6}) / 12 = 8.4375 × 10^{-9} m4.
  • Calculate load W = Mg = 3 × 9.8 = 29.4 N.
  • Use the formula including self-weight: δ = (Wl3 / 3Y Ig) + (mlg / 8Y Ig) [Note: mg is total weight mg = 2 × 9.8 = 19.4 N].
  • First term (due to load M): δ1 = (29.4 × (1.5)3) / (3 × 1.0 × 10^{11} × 8.4375 × 10^{-9}) = (29.4 × 3.375) / (2531.25) = 99.225 / 2531.25 = 0.0392 m.
  • Second term (due to self-weight): δ2 = ( (2 × 9.8) × (1.5)3 ) / (8 × 1.0 × 10^{11} × 8.4375 × 10^{-9}) = (19.6 × 3.375) / (6750) = 66.15 / 6750 = 0.0098 m.
  • Total depression δ = 0.0392 + 0.0098 = 0.049 m.

Answer: 0.049 m (or 4.9 cm)

TGT core

Two cantilevers of the same material and same length have square cross-sections of sides 1 cm and 2 cm respectively. Find the ratio of their depressions for the same load applied at the free end.

  • For a square cross-section, breadth b = d = a, hence Ig = a4 / 12.
  • The depression formula is δ = 4Wl3 / (Y a4).
  • Since W, l, and Y are the same for both cantilevers, δ is inversely proportional to a4 (δ ∝ 1 / a4).
  • Given side lengths: a1 = 1 cm, a2 = 2 cm.
  • Ratio of depressions: δ1 / δ2 = (a2 / a1)4 = (2 / 1)4 = 16 / 1.

Answer: 16 : 1

Assumptions and limitations

  • The deformations are well within the elastic limit so that Hooke's law remains strictly valid.
  • The cross-section of the beam remains plane and normal to the neutral surface after bending (Bernoulli-Euler hypothesis).
  • The slope of the deflected beam is small (dy/dx << 1), allowing the approximation (d2y/dx2) for curvature.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The neutral axis of a bent cantilever undergoes stretching.

Correct: The neutral axis experiences zero longitudinal strain.

Fibers above the neutral axis lengthen and those below shorten, but the neutral layer itself remains unaltered in length.

Common error: Doubling the thickness of a rectangular cantilever doubles its depression resistance.

Correct: Doubling the thickness reduces the depression by a factor of 8.

Depression is inversely proportional to thickness cubed (d<sup>3</sup>), so 2<sup>3</sup> = 8.

Common error: The weight of the cantilever has no effect if it is much lighter than the load.

Correct: Even a light cantilever contributes an effective load equal to one-third of its total weight.

Distributed self-weight contributes continuously to the bending moment along the entire span.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What happens to the upper fibers of a cantilever when a downward load is applied at the free end?

Explanation: Under a downward load, the upper fibers are stretched, experiencing tension, while the lower fibers are squeezed, experiencing compression.

2. Which imaginary layer within a bent beam undergoes neither elongation nor compression?

Explanation: The neutral surface or neutral axis is the layer within the beam that undergoes neither elongation nor compression during bending.

3. What is the expression for the external bending moment at a distance $x$ from the fixed end of a cantilever of length $l$ due to a load $W$ at the free end?

Explanation: The external bending moment at a distance $x$ from the fixed end is given by $W(l - x)$.

4. What is the geometrical moment of inertia $$I_{g}$$ for a rectangular beam of breadth $b$ and thickness $d$ about its neutral axis?

Explanation: For a beam of rectangular cross-section with breadth $b$ and thickness $d$, $$I_{g}$ = \frac{bd^3}{12}$.

5. What is the geometrical moment of inertia $$I_{g}$$ for a circular cross-section beam of radius $r$?

Explanation: For a beam of circular cross-section with radius $r$, the geometrical moment of inertia is $$I_{g}$ = \frac{\$\pi$ r^4}{4}$.

6. How does the depression $\delta$ at the free end of a rectangular cantilever depend on its thickness $d$?

Explanation: The vertical displacement $\delta$ is inversely proportional to $bd^3$, meaning $\delta \propto \frac{1}{d^3}$.

7. What is the fundamental differential equation of the elastic curve for a cantilever?

Explanation: Equating external and internal moments yields the differential equation $Y $I_{g}$ \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = W(l - x)$.

8. What is the depression formula for a rectangular cantilever of length $l$, breadth $b$, and thickness $d$ loaded with load $W$ at its free end?

Explanation: Substituting $$I_{g}$ = \frac{bd^3}{12}$ into $\delta = \frac{Wl^3}{3YI_g}$ yields $\delta = \frac{4Wl^3}{Ybd^3}$.

9. If the thickness of a rectangular cantilever is doubled while keeping all other dimensions constant, its depression for the same load will become:

Explanation: Since $\delta$ is inversely proportional to $d^3$, doubling $d$ reduces the depression by a factor of $2^3 = 8$.

10. What is the effect of the self-weight of a uniform cantilever on its free-end depression?

Explanation: When the weight of the cantilever itself is considered, it acts as a uniformly distributed load contributing an effective depression equal to one-third of that produced by an equivalent load concentrated at the free end.

11. What is the internal resisting couple balancing the external bending moment in a cantilever?

Explanation: The internal elastic restoring couple is expressed as $\frac{Y $I_{g}$}{R}$, where $R$ is the radius of curvature.

12. Which laboratory property of a material is commonly measured using an end-loaded cantilever setup?

Explanation: End-loaded cantilevers provide a standard laboratory method for measuring the Young's modulus ($Y$) of a material.

13. What is the depression formula for a circular cantilever of radius $r$ and length $l$ loaded with load $W$ at its free end?

Explanation: Substituting $$I_{g}$ = \frac{\$\pi$ r^4}{4}$ into $\delta = \frac{Wl^3}{3YI_g}$ yields $\delta = \frac{4Wl^3}{3\$\pi$ r^4 Y}$.

14. Under what condition is the differential equation $Y $I_{g}$ \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = W(l - x)$ valid for a bending beam?

Explanation: The differential equation assumes small slopes where $\frac{dy}{dx} \ll 1$, allowing the approximation $\frac{1}{R} \approx \frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$.

15. Which of the following dimensions has the maximum impact on the stiffness and resistance to depression of a rectangular cantilever?

Explanation: Stiffness depends heavily on the depth $d$ for rectangular beams due to its cubic dependence ($d^3$). playroom effect.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. A rectangular cantilever of length $l$, breadth $b$, and thickness $d$ is depressed by $\delta$ under a load $W$ at the free end. If the length is doubled while keeping the load and cross-section constant, the new depression will be:

Explanation: Depression $\delta \propto l^3$. If length becomes $2l$, the new depression is $(2)^3\delta = 8\delta$.

2. Two rectangular beams of the same material and same length have dimensions $b \times d$ and $2b \times \frac{d}{2}$ respectively. The ratio of their depressions for the same load at the free end is:

Explanation: Depression $\delta \propto \frac{1}{bd^3}$. For first beam: $\delta_1 \propto \frac{1}{b \cdot d^3}$. For second beam: $\delta_2 \propto \frac{1}{(2b) \cdot (d/2)^3} = \frac{1}{2b \cdot d^3 / 8} = \frac{4}{bd^3}$. Thus, $\delta_2 / \delta_1 = 4$.

3. A uniform cantilever of mass $m$ and length $l$ carries a load $M$ at its free end. The effective depression at the free end is given by:

Explanation: The total depression is the sum of depression due to end load $M$ (which is $\frac{Mgl^3}{3YI_g}$) and self-weight $m$ (which is $\frac{mgl^3}{8YI_g}$).

4. What is the dimensional formula for the geometrical moment of inertia $$I_{g}$$ of a beam cross-section?

Explanation: Geometrical moment of inertia involves dimensions of area multiplied by distance squared ($Ar^2$), resulting in $[L^2][L^2] = [L^4]$.

5. If a circular cantilever of radius $r$ is replaced by another circular cantilever of radius $2r$ of the same material and length, the depression for a given load will become:

Explanation: Depression $\delta$ for a circular cantilever is inversely proportional to $r^4$ ($\delta \propto \frac{1}{r^4}$). Doubling $r$ multiplies the denominator by $2^4 = 16$, reducing depression to $\frac{1}{16}$ of its original value.

6. Which of the following statements about the neutral axis of a bent beam is correct?

Explanation: The neutral axis is an imaginary layer within the beam that undergoes neither elongation nor compression, meaning it experiences zero longitudinal strain.

5. What is the unit of the internal resisting couple in a cantilever?

Explanation: A couple has the same units as torque or moment of force, which is Newton-meter (N$\cdot$m).

6. In the measurement of Young's modulus using a cantilever, why is it important to measure the thickness $d$ with high precision (such as with a screw gauge)?

Explanation: Because depression is inversely proportional to $d^3$, any small percentage error in measuring $d$ gets tripled in the calculated value of Young's modulus.

7. What is the radius of curvature $R$ of the neutral surface related to in pure bending?

Explanation: From the internal resisting couple equation $\tau = \frac{Y $I_{g}$}{R}$, rearranging gives $R = \frac{Y $I_{g}$}{M}$.

8. When a cantilever of uniform cross-section and mass $m$ is considered, what fraction of its self-weight contributes to the depression at the free end compared to an identical load placed at the free end?

Explanation: The self-weight of a uniform cantilever contributes an effective depression equal to one-third ($1/3$) of that produced by an equivalent load concentrated at the free end.

9. If the length of a cantilever is halved, keeping its cross-section and load unchanged, how does its depression change?

Explanation: Since $\delta \propto l^3$, halving the length ($l/2$) reduces the depression by a factor of $2^3 = 8$.

10. Which stress components are primarily responsible for the bending behavior of a cantilever beam?

Explanation: Bending causes longitudinal fibers above the neutral axis to undergo tension and fibers below to undergo compression.
