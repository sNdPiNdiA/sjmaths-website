Relation Between Stress and Strain

Stress is defined as the internal restoring force per unit area set up inside a deformed body, while strain is the fractional change in dimension resulting from that stress. Hooke's law establishes a linear proportionality between stress and strain within the elastic limit of a material.

Concept of Stress

  • Stress is a tensor quantity measured as force per unit cross-sectional area, F/A.
  • Normal stress acts perpendicular to the surface (causing elongation or compression), whereas tangential or shearing stress acts parallel to the surface.
  • SI unit of stress is N/m² or Pascal (Pa) with dimensional formula [ML-1T-2].

Concept of Strain

  • Strain is a dimensionless and unitless quantity representing the relative deformation of a body.
  • Longitudinal strain is ΔL/L, volumetric strain is ΔV/V, and shearing strain is θ or tan θ ≈ Δx/l.
  • It expresses how much a given body is deformed relative to its original configuration.

Hooke's Law and Elastic Moduli

  • Within the elastic limit, stress is directly proportional to strain, meaning Stres$s = E \times Strain$, where E is the modulus of elasticity.
  • Young's modulus (Y) governs longitudinal deformation, Bulk modulus (K) governs volumetric compression, and Rigidity modulus (η) governs shape change under shear.
  • The reciprocal of bulk modulus is known as compressibility.

Poisson's Ratio and Elastic Constants Interrelation

  • Poisson's ratio (σ) is the ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain under uniaxial stress.
  • Theoretical isotropic limits for Poisson's ratio are -1 to 0.5, while practical materials exhibit values between 0 and 0.5.
  • Inter-relations include $Y = 3K(1 - 2\sigma)$ and $Y = 2\eta(1 + \sigma)$.

Derivations

Derivation of Elastic Potential Energy Density

Conditions: Isotropic, linearly elastic material stressed within the proportional limit

  • Consider a wire of original length L and cross-sectional area A subjected to a variable stretching force F.
  • The incremental work done in stretching the wire by an infinitesimal length dl is dW = F dl.
  • Substitute $F = (YA/L)l into the work integral from l = 0$ to $l = \DeltaL$.
  • Integrate to get $W = (1/2) \times (YA/L) (\DeltaL)^{2}$.
  • Divide by volume (V = AL) to obtain energy density $U = (1/2) \times Stress \times Strain$.

Result: $U = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Stress} \times \text{Strain} = \frac{1}{2} Y \epsilon^2$

Relation between Young's Modulus, Bulk Modulus, and Modulus of Rigidity

Conditions: Homogeneous, isotropic elastic solid

  • Write the independent constitutive relations linking Young's modulus Y, Bulk modulus K, and Rigidity modulus η.
  • Use Poisson's ratio σ to eliminate lateral strain components from volumetric and shear strain equations.
  • Apply the standard identities: $\sigma = \frac{3K - 2\eta}{6K + 2\eta}$.
  • Substitute σ back into either $Y = 3K(1 - 2\sigma) or Y = 2\eta(1 + \sigma)$.
  • Obtain the master relation connecting all three moduli: \frac{9}{Y} = \frac{1}{K} + \frac{3}{\eta}.

Result: \frac{9}{Y} = \frac{1}{K} + \frac{3}{\eta}

Graphs and experiments

Stress-Strain Curve

Draw: A plot of Stress (y-axis) versus Strain (x-axis) showing proportional limit, elastic limit, yield point, ultimate tensile strength, and fracture point.

Interpret: The linear region represents Hooke's law validity. Beyond the yield point, permanent plastic deformation occurs leading up to necking and fracture.

Exam use: Direct identification questions on ductile vs. brittle materials and the exact definitions of yield and breaking stress.

Searle's Apparatus Experiment

Draw: Experimental setup featuring two long parallel wires (experimental and reference), a spirit level, a micrometer screw gauge, and added dead weights.

Interpret: Used to determine Young's modulus of a metallic wire accurately by measuring small elongations for known loads.

Exam use: Questions on least count, error propagation, and formula application for Y.

Compressibility Measurement via Bulk Modulus

Draw: Graph of fractional volume change (ΔV/V) versus applied uniform pressure (ΔP).

Interpret: The slope of the linear graph yields the inverse of the bulk modulus, which is the compressibility factor.

Exam use: Conceptual questions on fluids and solids under high hydrostatic pressure.

Solved numericals

TGT core

A steel wire of length 2 m and cross-sectional area 1 mm² is pulled by a force of 200 N. If Young's modulus of steel is 2 × 10¹¹ N/m², calculate the elongation produced in the wire.

  • Identify given values: L = 2 m, $A = 1 mm^{2} = 1 \times 10^{-6} m^{2}$, F = 200 N, $Y = 2 \times 10^{11} N/m^{2}$.
  • Rearrange Young's modulus formula for elongation: ΔL = \frac{FL}{YA}.
  • Substitute values: Δ$L = \frac{200 \times 2}{2 \times 10^{11} \times 1 \times 10^{-6}}$.
  • Simplify the arithmetic: Δ$L = \frac{400}{2 \times 10^5} = 2 \times 10^{-3} m$.

Answer: 2 mm (or 2 × 10&sup-;³ m)

PGT extension

For a given isotropic material, the Young's modulus is equal to its bulk modulus. Find the Poisson's ratio of the material.

  • State the relation connecting Young's modulus, bulk modulus, and Poisson's ratio: $Y = 3K(1 - 2\sigma)$.
  • Substitute the given condition $Y = K into the equation: K = 3K(1 - 2\sigma)$.
  • Divide both sides by K (since K ≠ 0): $1 = 3(1 - 2\sigma)$.
  • Solve for σ: 1/$3 = 1 - 2\sigma => 2\sigma = 1 - 1/3 = 2/3 => \sigma = 1/3$.

Answer: 1/3 (or 0.33)

PGT extension

Calculate the elastic potential energy stored per unit volume in a copper wire subject to a longitudinal stress of 4 × 10&sup7; N/m². Take Young's modulus of copper as 1.2 × 10¹¹ N/m².

  • Identify given values: Stress $\sigma = 4 \times 10^{7} N/m^{2}$, $Y = 1.2 \times 10^{11} N/m^{2}$.
  • Use the energy density formula in terms of stress and Young's modulus: $u = \frac{\sigma^2}{2Y}$.
  • Substitute the numerical values: $u = \frac{(4 \times 10^7)^2}{2 \times 1.2 \times 10^{11}}$.
  • Calculate numerator: 16 × 10¹&sup4;.
  • Calculate denominator: 2.4 × 10¹¹.
  • Perform division: $u = \frac{16 \times 10^{14}}{2.4 \times 10^{11}} = 6.67 \times 10^{2} J/m^{3}$.

Answer: 6.67 × 10² J/m³

Assumptions and limitations

  • Hooke's law is strictly valid only within the proportional limit of the stress-strain curve.
  • Materials are assumed to be perfectly homogeneous and isotropic for standard elastic constant inter-relations.
  • Deformations are assumed to be small so that higher-order nonlinear strain terms can be neglected.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Strain has SI units of percentage or ratio dimensions like meters.

Correct: Strain is strictly a dimensionless quantity.

It is the ratio of two like quantities (change in dimension to original dimension).

Common error: Poisson's ratio can take any positive value up to infinity.

Correct: Poisson's ratio for stable isotropic materials lies strictly between -1 and 0.5.

Thermodynamic stability requirements dictate that bulk and shear moduli must remain positive.

Common error: A stiffer material has a lower Young's modulus.

Correct: A higher Young's modulus indicates greater stiffness.

Modulus is the slope of the stress-strain curve; higher slope means less deformation for the same applied stress.

Quick revision

  • Stress is restoring force per unit area (N/m²), while strain is fractional deformation (dimensionless).
  • Hooke's law states Stres$s = E \times Strain within the elastic limit$.
  • Young's modulus (Y), Bulk modulus (K), and Rigidity modulus (η) characterize tensile, volumetric, and shear elasticity respectively.
  • The inter-relation master formula is 9/$Y = 1/K + 3/\eta$.
  • Poisson's ratio σ relates lateral strain to longitudinal strain with theoretical bounds of -1 to 0.5.

Must remember

  • Stress is a tensor quantity measured as force per unit cross-sectional area ($F/A$), with SI unit $N/m^2$ or Pascal (Pa) and dimensional formula $[ML^{-1}T^{-2}]$.
  • Strain is a dimensionless and unitless quantity representing relative deformation: longitudinal ($\Delta L/L$), volumetric ($\Delta V/V$), and shearing ($\theta$).
  • Hooke's law states that within the elastic limit, stress is directly proportional to strain: $\sigma = E \epsilon$.
  • Young's modulus ($Y$), Bulk modulus ($K$), and Rigidity modulus ($\eta$) govern longitudinal, volumetric, and shape changes respectively.
  • The reciprocal of bulk modulus is known as compressibility.
  • Poisson's ratio ($\sigma$) is the ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain, with theoretical isotropic limits of $-1$ to $0.5$.
  • Inter-relations between elastic constants are given by $Y = 3K(1 - 2\sigma)$ and $Y = 2\eta(1 + \sigma)$.
  • Elastic potential energy density is expressed as $u = \frac{1}{2} \text{Stress} \times \text{Strain} = \frac{\sigma^2}{2Y}$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the dimensional formula of stress?

Explanation: Stress is force per unit area: $[MLT^{-2}] / [L^2] = [ML^{-1}T^{-2}]$.

2. Which of the following quantities is dimensionless and unitless?

Explanation: Strain is the ratio of two like quantities (change in dimension to original dimension), making it dimensionless and unitless. (Note: Poisson's ratio is also dimensionless, but strain is the baseline dimensionless deformation quantity).

3. Hooke's law is valid strictly:

Explanation: Hooke's law states that stress is proportional to strain only within the elastic limit.

4. What is the reciprocal of the bulk modulus called?

Explanation: Compressibility is defined as the inverse of the bulk modulus ($1/K$).

5. The theoretical isotropic limits for Poisson's ratio are:

Explanation: Thermodynamic stability requires isotropic materials to have Poisson's ratio between $-1$ and $0.5$.

6. Which modulus of elasticity is involved in changing the volume of a body without changing its shape?

Explanation: Bulk modulus ($K$) governs volumetric compression or expansion under uniform hydrostatic pressure.

7. What is the SI unit of stress?

Explanation: Stress is force per unit area, measured in $N/m^2$ or Pascals (Pa).

8. If a wire is stretched to double its length, the longitudinal strain is:

Explanation: Longitudinal strain is $\Delta L / L$. If final length is $2L$, $\Delta L = L$, so strain = $L / L = 1.0$.

9. The elastic potential energy density stored in a strained wire is given by:

Explanation: Energy density $u = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Stress} \times \text{Strain}$.

10. Which inter-relation correctly links Young's modulus ($Y$), Bulk modulus ($K$), and Poisson's ratio ($\sigma$)?

Explanation: The standard inter-relation is $Y = 3K(1 - 2\sigma)$.

11. Tangential or shearing stress causes:

Explanation: Shearing stress acts parallel to the surface, causing angular deformation or change in shape.

12. A higher Young's modulus for a material indicates:

Explanation: A higher Young's modulus means less deformation for a given stress, denoting greater stiffness.

13. What is the relation between Young's modulus ($Y$), Rigidity modulus ($\eta$), and Poisson's ratio ($\sigma$)?

Explanation: The correct inter-relation is $Y = 2\eta(1 + \sigma)$.

14. What is the nature of stress when it acts perpendicular to the surface, causing compression?

Explanation: Normal stress acts perpendicular to the surface; when it causes compression, it is normal compressive stress.

15. Why can Poisson's ratio for practical stable materials not exceed $0.5$?

Explanation: If $\sigma = 0.5$, the bulk modulus becomes infinite and volume change becomes zero ($\Delta V/V = 0$). Exceeding $0.5$ would imply a physically impossible negative volume change under tension.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. If the length of a wire is doubled and radius is also doubled, what happens to its Young's modulus?

Explanation: Young's modulus is a characteristic material property independent of the dimensions of the wire.

2. For a given isotropic material, if the Poisson's ratio is $0.25$, the ratio of Young's modulus to rigidity modulus ($Y/\eta$) is:

Explanation: Using $Y = 2\eta(1 + \sigma)$, we get $Y/\eta = 2(1 + 0.25) = 2(1.25) = 2.5$.

3. A wire of length $L$ and cross-sectional area $A$ is stretched by force $F$. The work done per unit volume is:

Explanation: Stored elastic potential energy density (work done per unit volume) is $\frac{1}{2} \text{Stress} \times \text{Strain}$.

4. The bulk modulus of an incompressible liquid is:

Explanation: For an incompressible liquid, volume change $\Delta V = 0$ for any pressure change, making $K = -V(\Delta P / \Delta V) = \infty$.

5. If Poisson's ratio of a material is $0.5$, then the material is:

Explanation: When $\sigma = 0.5$, $Y = 3K(0) = 0$ or $K$ is infinite, indicating that volumetric strain is zero (incompressible).

6. A force $F$ is applied to a wire of radius $r$ and length $L$, producing an elongation $\Delta L$. If the same force is applied to a wire of the same material with radius $2r$ and length $2L$, the elongation will be:

Explanation: Elongation $\Delta L = \frac{FL}{A Y} = \frac{FL}{\pi r^2 Y}$. For new wire, $\Delta L' = \frac{F(2L)}{\pi (2r)^2 Y} = \frac{2FL}{4\pi r^2 Y} = \frac{\Delta L}{2}$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following is the correct dimensional formula for Young's modulus?

Explanation: Young's modulus has the same dimensions as stress (force per unit area), which is $[ML^{-1}T^{-2}]$..

2. What is the value of Poisson's ratio if $Y = 2\eta$?

Explanation: From $Y = 2\eta(1 + \sigma)$, if $Y = 2\eta$, then $2\eta = 2\eta(1 + \sigma) \implies 1 + \sigma = 1 \implies \sigma = 0$.

3. A cube of side $a$ is subjected to a uniform hydrostatic pressure $P$. If the bulk modulus is $K$, the fractional change in volume ($\Delta V / V$) is:

Explanation: Bulk modulus $K = -V(\Delta P / \Delta V)$. Thus, fractional change $\Delta V / V = -\Delta P / K = -P / K$.

4. The potential energy per unit volume of a stretched wire is proportional to:

Explanation: Energy density $u = \frac{\sigma^2}{2Y}$, which is proportional to the square of the stress.

5. When a solid cylindrical rod is twisted, the deformation involved is governed by:

Explanation: Twisting a cylinder produces shear strain and shape change, which is governed by the modulus of rigidity ($\eta$).

6. If the ratio of radii of two wires of the same material is $2:1$ and lengths are equal, the ratio of their Young's moduli is:

Explanation: Young's modulus is an intrinsic property of the material and does not depend on dimensions; hence the ratio is $1:1$.

7. What does the slope of the linear region in a stress-strain curve represent?

Explanation: Within the elastic limit, stress = $E \times$ strain, so the slope of the stress-strain curve is the elastic modulus $E$.

8. For an isotropic elastic material, the relationship between $Y$, $K$, and $\eta$ is:

Explanation: Eliminating $\sigma$ from $Y = 3K(1 - 2\sigma)$ and $Y = 2\eta(1 + \sigma)$ yields $Y = \frac{9K\eta}{3K + \eta}$.

9. Longitudinal strain is defined as:

Explanation: Longitudinal strain is $\Delta L / L$, representing the relative change in length.

10. Which of the following materials generally exhibits the highest Young's modulus?

Explanation: Steel is much stiffer than rubber, wood, or glass, resulting in a significantly higher Young's modulus.
