Critical Velocity in Fluid Dynamics

Critical velocity in fluid dynamics refers to the threshold velocity below which the flow of a fluid through a pipe is streamline (laminar) and above which it becomes turbulent. In the context of orbital mechanics, it also represents the minimum horizontal velocity required to launch a satellite into a stable circular orbit close to the Earth's surface.

Streamline versus Turbulent Flow

  • In streamline (laminar) flow, every fluid particle passing a given point maintains the same velocity and path as preceding particles, and flow lines do not cross.
  • When fluid velocity exceeds a certain limit called the critical velocity (vc), the flow pattern becomes irregular and chaotic, characterized by eddies and cross-currents known as turbulent flow.
  • The transition from laminar to turbulent flow depends on fluid density, viscosity, tube diameter, and mean velocity.

Reynolds Number and Flow Regimes

  • The dimensionless Reynolds number (Re) governs the regime of fluid flow: Re = (\rho v d)/(\eta), where \rho is density, v is velocity, d is pipe diameter, and \eta is dynamic viscosity.
  • For flow in a circular pipe, Re < 2000 corresponds strictly to steady laminar flow.
  • Flow transitions between Re values of 2000 and 3000, while Re > 3000 guarantees fully developed turbulent flow.

Dimensional Analysis of Critical Velocity in Pipes

  • Applying dimensional analysis, critical velocity vc depends on coefficient of viscosity \eta, density \rho, and tube radius r.
  • Dimensional formula of critical velocity is [M0 L1 T-1], viscosity is [M1 L-1 T-1], density is [M1 L-3 T0], and radius is [M0 L1 T0].
  • Equating exponents yields the proportional relation vc \propto \eta / (\rho r).

Terminal Velocity and Viscous Drag

  • When a spherical body falls through an infinite viscous medium, viscous drag increases with velocity until it balances the net downward gravitational force minus buoyant force.
  • The constant maximum velocity attained by the falling body is termed the terminal velocity, given by vt = 2r2(\rho - \sigma)g / (9\eta).
  • Terminal velocity represents a steady-state condition distinct from pipe critical velocity, though both involve fluid viscosity parameters.

Derivations

Derivation of Critical Velocity via Dimensional Analysis

Conditions: Incompressible fluid flowing through a smooth cylindrical pipe of radius r.

  • Assume critical velocity vc depends on coefficient of viscosity \eta, fluid density \rho, and pipe radius r such that vc = k \etax \rhoy rz, where k is a dimensionless constant.
  • Substitute the dimensional formulas: [M0 L1 T-1] = [M1 L-1 T-1]x [M1 L-3 T0]y [M0 L1 T0]z.
  • Equate the exponents of M, L, and T on both sides: For M: x + y = 0; For L: -x - 3y + z = 1; For T: -$x = -1$.
  • Solve the simultaneous equations: x = 1, $y = -1$, $z = -1$.
  • Substitute back to get the formula: vc = k \eta / (\rho r).

Result: $v_c = \frac{k \eta}{\rho r}$

Derivation of Terminal Velocity for a Spherical Body

Conditions: Sphere of radius r and density \rho falling through a fluid of density \sigma and viscosity \eta under acceleration due to gravity g.

  • Downward forces acting on the sphere include true weight $W = (4/3)\pi r^{3} \rho g$.
  • Upward buoyant force equals the weight of displaced fluid Fb = (4/3)\pi r3 \sigma g.
  • Viscous drag force opposing motion is given by Stokes' law: Fv = 6\pi \eta r vt.
  • At terminal velocity vt, net downward force equals viscous drag: (4/3)\pi r3 (\rho - \sigma)$g = 6\pi \eta r v_{t}$.
  • Rearrange the equation to isolate terminal velocity vt.

Result: $v_t = \frac{2r^2(\rho - \sigma)g}{9\eta}$

Solved numericals

TGT core

Water at 20°C flows through a tube of radius 1 cm. If the coefficient of viscosity of water is 1.0 × 10-3 Pa·s and density is 1000 kg/m3, find the maximum average velocity for steady laminar flow, assuming the critical Reynolds number is 2000.

  • Identify given values: r = 0.01 m, D = 2r = 0.02 m, $\eta = 1.0 × 10^{-3} Pa·s$, $\rho = 1000 kg/m^{3}$, Re = 2000.
  • Use the Reynolds number relation for critical velocity: $v_c = (R_e \times \eta) / (\rho \times D)$.
  • Substitute the values: $v_c = (2000 \times 1.0 \times 10^{-3}) / (1000 \times 0.02)$.
  • Calculate the result: $v_c = 2 / 20 = 0.1 m/s$.

Answer: 0.1 m/s

PGT extension

A small steel ball of radius 2 mm and density 7800 kg/m3 falls through glycerin of density 1200 kg/m3 and viscosity 0.85 Pa·s. Calculate its terminal velocity. (Take $g = 9.8 m/s^{2})$

  • Identify given parameters: $r = 2 × 10^{-3} m$, $\rho = 7800 kg/m^{3}$, $\sigma = 1200 kg/m^{3}$, \eta = 0.85 Pa·s, $g = 9.8 m/s^{2}$.
  • Apply the terminal velocity formula: $v_t = 2 r^{2} (\rho - \sigma) g / (9 \eta)$.
  • Substitute numbers: $v_t = [2 × (2 × 10^{-3})^{2} × (7800 - 1200) × 9.8] / (9 × 0.85)$.
  • Evaluate numerator: 2 × (4 × 10-6) × 6600 × 9.8 = 0.51744.
  • Evaluate denominator: 9 × 0.85 = 7.65.
  • Compute division: $v_t = 0.51744 / 7.65 ≈ 0.0676 m/s$.

Answer: 0.0676 m/s

PGT extension

Determine the critical velocity of a satellite orbiting the Earth in a circular path just above its surface. Given Earth's radius R = 6400 km and $g = 9.8 m/s^{2}$.

  • Identify parameters: $R = 6400 km = 6.4 × 10^{6} m$, $g = 9.8 m/s^{2}$.
  • Use the orbital critical velocity formula: $v_c = \sqrt{gR}$.
  • Substitute values: $v_c = \sqrt{9.8 \times 6.4 \times 10^6}$.
  • Calculate product: 9.8 × 6.4 × 106 = 6.272 × 107 = 62.72 × 106.
  • Take square root: $v_c = \sqrt{62.72} × 10^{3} ≈ 7.92 × 10^{3} m/s$.

Answer: 7.92 km/s

Assumptions and limitations

  • Fluids are assumed to be incompressible and Newtonian, meaning viscosity remains constant regardless of shear rate.
  • Pipe walls are assumed to be perfectly smooth; surface roughness significantly alters the critical Reynolds number threshold.
  • Stokes' law and derived terminal velocity formulas assume an infinite fluid medium, ignoring boundary wall effects from container sides.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Critical velocity is a fixed, universal constant for all fluids.

Correct: Critical velocity depends on tube dimensions, fluid density, and viscosity, varying from system to system.

It is a threshold parameter determined by the dimensionless Reynolds number of the specific flow geometry rather than a material constant.

Common error: Turbulent flow instantly occurs everywhere in a pipe once critical velocity is exceeded.

Correct: Transition begins near the walls or downstream disturbances and gradually develops into fully turbulent flow across the cross-section.

Boundary layer separation and localized eddy formation precede complete flow breakdown.

Quick revision

  • Critical velocity marks the upper limit of streamline flow and the onset of turbulence in fluid conduits.
  • Reynolds number ($R_e = \rho v D / \eta) determines flow regimes: R_e < 2000$ is laminar, R_e > 3000 is turbulent.
  • Critical Velocity in Pipe: $v_c = \frac{R_e \eta}{\rho D}$

Must remember

  • In streamline flow, fluid particles maintain the same velocity and path, and flow lines do not cross.
  • The dimensionless Reynolds number ($R_e = \frac{\rho v D}{\eta}$) governs fluid flow regimes.
  • For flow in a circular pipe, $R_e < 2000$ corresponds strictly to steady laminar flow, while $R_e > 3000$ guarantees fully developed turbulent flow.
  • By dimensional analysis, critical velocity is proportional to $\frac{\eta}{\rho r}$.
  • Terminal velocity of a spherical body is given by $v_t = \frac{2r^2(\rho - \sigma)g}{9\eta}$.
  • Stokes' drag force on a sphere moving through a viscous fluid is given by $F = 6\pi \eta r v$.
  • Poiseuille's equation expresses the volume flow rate as $V = \frac{\pi P r^4}{8\eta l}$.
  • Kinematic viscosity is defined as $\nu = \frac{\eta}{\rho}$.

Glossary

Critical Velocity ($v_c$)The threshold velocity up to which fluid flow is streamline and above which it becomes turbulent.
Reynolds Number ($R_e$)A dimensionless number representing the ratio of inertial forces to viscous forces in fluid flow.
Dynamic Viscosity ($\eta$)A fluid's internal resistance to shear flow, measured in $\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ or $\text{kg}/(\text{m}\cdot\text{s})$.
Terminal Velocity ($v_t$)The constant maximum velocity attained by a body falling freely through a viscous medium when viscous drag balances the net gravitational force.
Kinematic Viscosity ($\nu$)The ratio of dynamic viscosity to fluid density, $\nu = \frac{\eta}{\rho}$, with units $\text{m}^2/\text{s}$.
Stokes' Drag ForceThe frictional resistive force experienced by a small sphere moving slowly through a viscous fluid, $F = 6\pi \eta r v$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the dimensionless parameter that determines whether fluid flow in a pipe is laminar or turbulent?

Explanation: The dimensionless Reynolds number ($R_e = \frac{\rho v D}{\eta}$) governs the regime of fluid flow, separating laminar and turbulent regions.

2. For fluid flow in a circular pipe, a Reynolds number strictly less than which value corresponds to steady laminar flow?

Explanation: For flow in a circular pipe, $R_e < 2000$ corresponds strictly to steady laminar flow.

3. What is the threshold Reynolds number above which fluid flow in a circular pipe is guaranteed to be fully developed turbulent flow?

Explanation: Flow transitions between $R_e = 2000$ and $3000$, while $R_e > 3000$ guarantees fully developed turbulent flow.

4. What is the dimensional formula for critical velocity $v_c$?

Explanation: Velocity has dimensions of distance divided by time, which is $[M^0 L^1 T^{-1}]$.

5. Using dimensional analysis, how does the critical velocity $v_c$ in a pipe depend on the coefficient of viscosity $\eta$, fluid density $\rho$, and tube radius $r$?

Explanation: Equating exponents in dimensional analysis yields the proportional relation $v_c \propto \frac{\eta}{\rho r}$.

6. What is the expression for Stokes' drag force on a spherical body of radius $r$ moving with velocity $v$ through a fluid of viscosity $\eta$?

Explanation: Stokes' law gives the viscous drag force as $F = 6\pi \eta r v$ under creeping flow conditions ($R_e \ll 1$).

7. What is the formula for the terminal velocity $v_t$ of a spherical body falling through an infinite viscous medium?

Explanation: The constant maximum terminal velocity is given by $v_t = \frac{2r^2(\rho - \sigma)g}{9\eta}$, where $\rho$ and $\sigma$ are the densities of the sphere and fluid respectively.

8. Which equation gives the volume flow rate of a viscous fluid through a cylindrical pipe under laminar flow conditions (Poiseuille's equation)?

Explanation: Poiseuille's equation states that volume flow rate $V = \frac{\pi P r^4}{8\eta l}$, where $P$ is the pressure difference and $l$ is pipe length.

9. How is kinematic viscosity $\nu$ defined in terms of dynamic viscosity $\eta$ and fluid density $\rho$?

Explanation: Kinematic viscosity is defined as the ratio of dynamic viscosity to density: $\nu = \frac{\eta}{\rho}$.

10. What are the SI units of kinematic viscosity $\nu$?

Explanation: Kinematic viscosity $\nu = \frac{\eta}{\rho}$ has units of $\frac{\text{kg}/(\text{m}\cdot\text{s})}{\text{kg}/\text{m}^3} = \text{m}^2/\text{s}$.

11. In streamline flow, what happens to the velocity and path of fluid particles passing a given point?

Explanation: In streamline flow, every fluid particle passing a given point maintains the same velocity and path as preceding particles, and flow lines do not cross.

12. What is the hydraulic radius $R_h$ for a full flowing circular pipe of diameter $D$?

Explanation: For a full flowing circular pipe, the hydraulic radius is defined as cross-sectional area divided by wetted perimeter, which equals $\frac{D}{4}$.

13. What physical phenomenon occurs when fluid velocity exceeds the critical velocity in a conduit?

Explanation: When velocity exceeds $v_c$, the flow becomes turbulent, characterized by eddies, cross-currents, and irregular particle trajectories.

14. Which of the following correctly describes the misconception regarding critical velocity?

Explanation: It is a common misconception that critical velocity is a fixed, universal constant; in reality, it depends on tube dimensions, density, and viscosity.

15. What condition is required for Stokes' Law and the standard terminal velocity formula to apply?

Explanation: Stokes' law assumes creeping flow ($R_e \ll 1$) for a small sphere falling through an infinite viscous medium.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The terminal velocity of a small spherical drop of radius $r$ falling through a viscous medium is proportional to:

Explanation: From the formula $v_t = \frac{2r^2(\rho - \sigma)g}{9\eta}$, terminal velocity is directly proportional to the square of the radius ($r^2$).

2. If the radius of a pipe through which a fluid is flowing with streamline motion is halved, keeping other parameters constant, how does the critical velocity change?

Explanation: Dimensional analysis shows $v_c \propto \frac{1}{r}$. Therefore, if the radius $r$ is halved, the critical velocity is doubled.

3. The dimensions of dynamic viscosity $\eta$ are:

Explanation: Using Newton's law of viscosity $F = \eta A \frac{dv}{dx}$, the dimensions of $\eta$ are $\frac{[MLT^{-2}]}{[L^2][LT^{-1}/L]} = [M^1 L^{-1} T^{-1}]$.

4. In Poiseuille's formula $V = \frac{\pi P r^4}{8\eta l}$, the volume flow rate $V$ depends on the radius $r$ of the tube as:

Explanation: Poiseuille's equation clearly shows that the volume flow rate $V$ is directly proportional to the fourth power of the radius ($r^4$).

5. What is the Reynolds number range that typically indicates the transition region between laminar and turbulent flow in pipes?

Explanation: Flow transitions between $R_e$ values of $2000$ and $3000$ before becoming fully turbulent above $3000$.

6. If the density of a falling spherical body is equal to the density of the fluid through which it is falling ($\rho = \sigma$), what is its terminal velocity?

Explanation: From $v_t = \frac{2r^2(\rho - \sigma)g}{9\eta}$, when $\rho = \sigma$, the numerator becomes zero, making the terminal velocity zero.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following expressions represents the critical velocity in a pipe in terms of Reynolds number $R_e$, dynamic viscosity $\eta$, fluid density $\rho$, and pipe diameter $D$?

Explanation: Rearranging the Reynolds number formula $R_e = \frac{\rho v D}{\eta}$ gives $v_c = \frac{R_e \eta}{\rho D}$.

2. When a small metal sphere falls through a viscous oil column, what forces act on it when it attains terminal velocity?

Explanation: At terminal velocity, the net downward force (gravity minus buoyancy) is exactly balanced by the upward viscous drag force.

3. What is the unit of dynamic viscosity $\eta$ in SI base units?

Explanation: Since dimensions of $\eta$ are $[M^1 L^{-1} T^{-1}]$, its SI base units are $\text{kg}/(\text{m}\cdot\text{s})$ or $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$.

4. If two identical spherical balls of different densities are dropped in the same viscous liquid, which one will have the greater terminal velocity?

Explanation: Since $v_t \propto (\rho - \sigma)$, a higher sphere density $\rho$ increases the numerator, resulting in a greater terminal velocity.

5. In fluid dynamics, what does kinematic viscosity measure?

Explanation: Kinematic viscosity ($\nu = \frac{\eta}{\rho}$) characterizes the diffusive transport of momentum relative to inertial effects, effectively measuring resistive flow under gravity where density acts as inertia.

6. What happens to turbulent flow in a pipe if the mean flow velocity is reduced such that $R_e$ falls well below $2000$?

Explanation: Flow regimes are governed by instantaneous Reynolds numbers; dropping $R_e$ below $2000$ causes turbulent disturbances to dampen out, restoring laminar flow.

7. If the radius of a falling spherical droplet is doubled, its terminal velocity will increase by a factor of:

Explanation: Terminal velocity is proportional to $r^2$ ($v_t \propto r^2$). Doubling the radius increases the terminal velocity by a factor of $2^2 = 4$.

8. Which of the following statements regarding the transition from laminar to turbulent flow is correct?

Explanation: Transition begins near boundaries or disturbances through boundary layer separation and localized eddy formation before fully developing into turbulence.

9. What is the orbital critical velocity for a satellite orbiting very close to a planetary surface of radius $R$ and surface gravity $g$?

Explanation: For a satellite orbiting near a planetary surface, the orbital critical velocity is given by $v_c = \sqrt{gR}$.

10. According to Poiseuille's equation, if the pressure difference across a pipe of fixed dimensions is doubled, the volume flow rate will:

Explanation: Poiseuille's equation $V = \frac{\pi P r^4}{8\eta l}$ shows that volume flow rate $V$ is directly proportional to the pressure difference $P$.
