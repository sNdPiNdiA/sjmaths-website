Turbulent Flow in Fluids

Turbulent flow is a fluid motion regime characterized by chaotic changes in pressure and flow velocity, unlike laminar flow where fluids flow in parallel layers. It occurs at high Reynolds numbers where inertial forces dominate over viscous forces.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of turbulent flow, distinction from streamline and laminar flow, critical velocity, and Reynolds number formula with its dimensionless nature.

PGT extension

Navier-Stokes equation applications in high Reynolds number limits, eddy viscosity, Prandtl mixing length theory, and Kolmogorov's theory of turbulence.

Prerequisites

  • Viscosity and coefficient of viscosity
  • Equation of continuity and Bernoulli's theorem
  • Reynolds number concept

Characteristics of Turbulent Flow

  • Fluid particles move in a zig-zag, irregular, and haphazard path leading to rapid mixing.
  • Velocity at any point in the fluid fluctuates randomly in both magnitude and direction with time.
  • Energy dissipation is significantly higher than in laminar flow due to internal fluid friction caused by eddies.

Reynolds Number (Re)

  • A dimensionless quantity used to predict flow transition in different fluid flow situations.
  • Defined mathematically as Re = (ρvd)/(η) or Re = (vd)/(ν), where ρ is density, v is velocity, d is characteristic length, η is dynamic viscosity, and ν is kinematic viscosity.
  • For flow through a cylindrical pipe, Re < 2000 indicates laminar flow, 2000 < Re < 3000 represents an unstable transition state, and Re > 3000 ensures fully developed turbulent flow.

Critical Velocity

  • Defined as the maximum velocity of fluid flow up to which the flow remains streamline or laminar.
  • If the velocity exceeds this critical value, the smooth flow breaks down and becomes turbulent.
  • Depends directly on the viscosity and inversely on the density and radius of the pipe.

Role of Inertial and Viscous Forces

  • Inertial forces tend to perpetuate the velocity fluctuations and spatial disorder of fluid elements.
  • Viscous forces act to damp out velocity gradients and suppress small-scale fluctuations, maintaining order.
  • Turbulence dominates when inertial forces vastly overwhelm the stabilizing viscous forces.

Derivations

Dimensional Analysis of Reynolds Number

Conditions: Incompressible fluid flow in a straight cylindrical pipe

  • Assume critical velocity (v) depends on fluid density (ρ), dynamic viscosity (η), and pipe diameter (d).
  • Write the dimensional equation: [v] = [ρ]a [η]b [d]c.
  • Substitute dimensions: [LT-1] = [ML-3]a [ML-1T-1]b [L]c.
  • Equate powers of M, L, and T: For M: a + b = 0; For L: -3a - b + c = 1; For T: -b = -1.
  • Solve for constants: b = 1, a = -1, c = -1.
  • Substitute back to get the proportionality: v ∝ η / (ρd), which rearranges to (ρvd)/(η) = constant.

Result: Re = \frac{\rho v d}{\eta} = \text{constant}

Head Loss in Turbulent Pipe Flow (Darcy-Weisbach Relation)

Conditions: Fully developed turbulent flow in a constant diameter horizontal pipe

  • Consider shear stress at the pipe wall (τw) balancing the pressure drop (ΔP).
  • Express pressure force as (ΔP)(πd2)/4 and frictional force as τw(πd)L.
  • Equate the forces: ΔP = 4(τwL)/d.
  • Introduce the Darcy friction factor f where τw = (f/8)ρv2.
  • Substitute τw into the pressure drop equation to obtain head loss hf.

Result: h_f = f \frac{L}{d} \frac{v^2}{2g}

Solved numericals

TGT core

Water at 20°C flows through a pipe of diameter 0.05 m. If the density of water is 1000 kg m⁻³ and dynamic viscosity is 1.0 × 10⁻³ Pa s, what is the maximum velocity for laminar flow? (Assume critical Reynolds number is 2000)

  • Identify given parameters: d = 0.05 m, ρ = 1000 kg m⁻³, η = 1.0 × 10⁻³ Pa s, Re = 2000.
  • Use the critical velocity formula: v_c = (Re * η) / (ρ * d).
  • Substitute the values: v_c = (2000 * 1.0 × 10⁻³) / (1000 * 0.05).
  • Calculate the result: v_c = 2 / 50 = 0.04 m s⁻¹.

Answer: 0.04 m s⁻¹

PGT extension

Air with density 1.2 kg m⁻³ and kinematic viscosity 1.5 × 10⁻⁵ m² s⁻¹ flows through a large ventilation duct of diameter 2.0 m. Calculate the Reynolds number when the average flow velocity is 5 m s⁻¹ and determine the flow regime.

  • Identify parameters: v = 5 m s⁻¹, d = 2.0 m, ν = 1.5 × 10⁻⁵ m² s⁻¹.
  • Use the Reynolds number formula involving kinematic viscosity: Re = (v * d) / ν.
  • Substitute values: Re = (5 * 2.0) / (1.5 × 10⁻⁵) = 10 / (1.5 × 10⁻⁵).
  • Compute value: Re ≈ 6.67 × 10⁵.
  • Compare with threshold: Since Re >> 3000, the flow is highly turbulent.

Answer: Re = 6.67 × 10⁵ (Fully turbulent flow)

PGT extension

Oil of density 900 kg m⁻³ and dynamic viscosity 0.1 Pa s flows through a pipe of diameter 0.1 m at a velocity of 2 m s⁻¹. Find the head loss due to friction over a length of 50 m using the friction factor relationship f = 64/Re for laminar, or appropriate turbulent correlation if applicable.

  • Calculate Reynolds number: Re = (ρ * v * d) / η = (900 * 2 * 0.1) / 0.1 = 1800.
  • Since Re = 1800 (< 2000), the flow is actually laminar. Use laminar friction factor f = 64 / Re.
  • Calculate f: f = 64 / 1800 = 0.03556.
  • Apply Darcy-Weisbach equation for head loss: h_f = (f * L * v²) / (2 * g * d).
  • Substitute values (g = 9.8 m s⁻²): h_f = (0.03556 * 50 * 2²) / (2 * 9.8 * 0.1) = 7.112 / 1.96 = 3.63 m.

Answer: 3.63 m (Note: Flow verified as laminar despite turbulent context)

Assumptions and limitations

  • Navier-Stokes equations apply universally, but exact analytical solutions for turbulent flow are intractable due to non-linearity and three-dimensional vorticity fluctuations, necessitating statistical or empirical models.
  • The Reynolds number threshold (Re = 2000 to 3000) is empirical and depends heavily on pipe entrance conditions, wall roughness, and external vibrations.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Turbulent flow has a constant velocity at any given point if averaged over time.

Correct: Velocity fluctuates randomly and persistently around a mean value in turbulent flow.

Turbulence is inherently unsteady at small time scales, even if macroscopic boundary conditions are steady.

Common error: Viscosity plays no role in turbulent flow since inertial forces dominate.

Correct: Viscosity remains crucial in the microscopic boundary layer (viscous sublayer) and dictates energy dissipation at the Kolmogorov microscale.

Viscous dissipation is the ultimate mechanism converting kinetic energy of turbulence into internal thermal energy.

Quick revision

  • Turbulent flow occurs when the Reynolds number exceeds approximately 3000 in pipe flow.
  • Critical velocity is inversely proportional to fluid density and pipe radius, and directly proportional to viscosity.
  • Darcy-Weisbach equation relates head loss directly to pipe length and square of velocity, and inversely to pipe diameter.

Must remember

  • Reynolds number is a dimensionless quantity given by $Re = \frac{\rho v d}{\eta}$.
  • For flow through a cylindrical pipe, $Re < 2000$ indicates laminar flow, $2000 < Re < 3000$ indicates an unstable transition state, and $Re > 3000$ indicates fully developed turbulent flow.
  • Critical velocity is the maximum velocity up to which fluid flow remains streamline or laminar.
  • Turbulence is characterized by irregular, zig-zag fluid particle motion and rapid mixing with random velocity fluctuations.
  • Inertial forces promote turbulence and fluctuations, whereas viscous forces damp out velocity gradients and maintain stability.

Glossary

Reynolds Number ($Re$)A dimensionless parameter representing the ratio of inertial forces to viscous forces in a fluid flow.
Critical Velocity ($v_c$)The maximum velocity of fluid flow up to which the flow remains streamline or laminar.
Kinematic Viscosity ($\nu$)The ratio of dynamic viscosity to fluid density, expressed as $\nu = \frac{\eta}{\rho}$.
Eddy Viscosity ($\eta_t$)An apparent viscosity used in turbulent flow modeling to account for momentum transfer by turbulent eddies.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the dimensionless quantity used to predict the transition from laminar to turbulent flow?

Explanation: The Reynolds number ($Re$) is a dimensionless quantity used specifically to predict flow transition in different fluid flow situations.

2. For fluid flow through a cylindrical pipe, a Reynolds number value of $2500$ indicates which type of flow?

Explanation: For pipe flow, $2000 < Re < 3000$ represents an unstable transition state between laminar and turbulent flow.

3. How is the Reynolds number ($Re$) mathematically defined in terms of density $\rho$, velocity $v$, characteristic length $d$, and dynamic viscosity $\eta$?

Explanation: The Reynolds number is defined mathematically as $Re = \frac{\rho v d}{\eta}$.

4. What happens to energy dissipation in turbulent flow compared to laminar flow?

Explanation: Energy dissipation is significantly higher in turbulent flow than in laminar flow due to internal fluid friction caused by eddies.

5. Which forces tend to perpetuate velocity fluctuations and spatial disorder of fluid elements in a flow?

Explanation: Inertial forces tend to perpetuate the velocity fluctuations and spatial disorder of fluid elements.

6. What is the role of viscous forces in fluid flow dynamics?

Explanation: Viscous forces act to damp out velocity gradients and suppress small-scale fluctuations, maintaining order.

7. What is defined as the maximum velocity of fluid flow up to which the flow remains streamline or laminar?

Explanation: Critical velocity is defined as the maximum velocity of fluid flow up to which the flow remains streamline or laminar.

8. Kinematic viscosity ($\nu$) is related to dynamic viscosity ($\eta$) and density ($\rho$) by which expression?

Explanation: Kinematic viscosity is defined as $\nu = \frac{\eta}{\rho}$.

9. What is the unit of kinematic viscosity ($\nu$)?

Explanation: The unit of kinematic viscosity is square meters per second ($\text{m}^2 \text{s}^{-1}$).

10. In the Darcy-Weisbach equation $h_f = \frac{f L v^2}{2 g d}$, what does $h_f$ represent?

Explanation: In the Darcy-Weisbach equation, $h_f$ represents head loss due to friction.

11. According to the power law velocity profile in turbulent pipe flow, $\frac{v(y)}{v_{max}} = \left(\frac{y}{R}\right)^{1/n}$, what is the typical value of $n$?

Explanation: For fully turbulent pipe flow, the empirical power law exponent $n$ is approximately $7$.

12. What does shear stress in turbulent flow incorporate alongside molecular viscosity $\eta$ according to the Prandtl mixing length model?

Explanation: The shear stress formula in turbulent flow is $\tau = (\eta + \eta_t) \frac{dv}{dy}$, where $\eta_t$ is eddy viscosity.

13. Which misconception about turbulent flow is incorrect regarding time-averaged velocity?

Explanation: The misconception states that turbulent flow has a constant velocity at any point if averaged over time; in reality, turbulence is unsteady and fluctuating.

14. What is the primary mechanism that converts kinetic energy of turbulence into internal thermal energy?

Explanation: Viscous dissipation is the ultimate mechanism converting kinetic energy of turbulence into internal thermal energy.

15. When does turbulence completely dominate over stabilizing forces in a fluid flow?

Explanation: Turbulence dominates when inertial forces vastly overwhelm the stabilizing viscous forces.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. If the Reynolds number for a fluid flowing through a pipe exceeds $3000$, the flow is typically:

Explanation: For pipe flow, $Re > 3000$ ensures fully developed turbulent flow.

2. Critical velocity of a fluid flowing in a pipe depends:

Explanation: Critical velocity depends directly on viscosity and inversely on the density and radius (or diameter) of the pipe.

3. What is the dimensional nature of the Reynolds number?

Explanation: The Reynolds number is a dimensionless quantity used to predict flow transition.

4. In the Blasius formula for friction factor in smooth pipes ($f = 0.316 \cdot Re^{-0.25}$), what is the valid range of the Reynolds number?

Explanation: The Blasius formula applies for smooth pipes in the turbulent range $3000 < Re < 10^5$.

5. Which of the following statements regarding turbulent flow is correct?

Explanation: In turbulent flow, velocity at any point fluctuates randomly in both magnitude and direction with time.

6. What is the expression for critical velocity $v_c$ derived from the Reynolds number formula?

Explanation: Rearranging the Reynolds number equation $Re = \frac{\rho v d}{\eta}$ gives critical velocity $v_c = \frac{Re \eta}{\rho d}$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following flow regimes corresponds to $Re < 2000$ in a cylindrical pipe?

Explanation: For flow through a cylindrical pipe, $Re < 2000$ indicates laminar flow.

2. What is the physical significance of eddy viscosity in turbulent flow?

Explanation: Eddy viscosity ($\eta_t$) is used in models like Prandtl's mixing length to account for momentum transfer by turbulent eddies.

3. In turbulent flow, fluid particles exhibit:

Explanation: Fluid particles in turbulent flow move in a zig-zag, irregular, and haphazard path leading to rapid mixing.

4. If a fluid's density is $1000 \text{ kg m}^{-3}$, dynamic viscosity is $10^{-3} \text{ Pa s}$, velocity is $2 \text{ m s}^{-1}$, and pipe diameter is $0.05 \text{ m}$, what is the Reynolds number?

Explanation: Using $Re = \frac{\rho v d}{\eta} = \frac{1000 \times 2 \times 0.05}{10^{-3}} = \frac{100}{10^{-3}} = 100000$.

5. What does Kolmogorov's theory of turbulence primarily deal with?

Explanation: Kolmogorov's theory deals with the energy cascade in turbulence, transferring energy from large eddies down to small scales where viscous dissipation occurs.

6. Why is energy dissipation higher in turbulent flow compared to laminar flow?

Explanation: Internal fluid friction caused by eddies drastically increases energy dissipation in turbulent flow.

7. What happens to the volumetric flow rate $Q$ given by $Q = A v$ when flow transitions from laminar to turbulent at the same average velocity?

Explanation: The volumetric flow rate is governed by the continuity equation $Q = A v$; the average velocity and area dictate $Q$ regardless of the internal velocity profile shape.

8. Which parameter acts to damp out velocity gradients and maintain order in fluid flow?

Explanation: Viscous forces act to damp out velocity gradients and suppress small-scale fluctuations, maintaining order.

9. In the power law velocity profile $\frac{v(y)}{v_{max}} = \left(\frac{y}{R}\right)^{1/n}$, what does $y$ represent?

Explanation: In the power law profile, $y$ represents the distance from the pipe wall.

10. Which statement best describes the role of viscosity in fully developed turbulence according to misconceptions?

Explanation: Although inertial forces dominate the bulk flow, viscosity is crucial in the viscous sublayer and for ultimate kinetic energy dissipation into heat.
