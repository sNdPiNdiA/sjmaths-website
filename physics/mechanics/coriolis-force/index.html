Coriolis Force in Rotating Frames of Reference

The Coriolis force is a fictitious inertial force experienced by objects in motion relative to a non-inertial reference frame rotating at a constant angular velocity. It acts perpendicularly to both the velocity vector of the particle and the axis of rotation of the frame.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Qualitative understanding of non-inertial frames, direction of Coriolis force, effects on falling bodies, and basic conceptual differences between centrifugal and Coriolis forces.

PGT extension

Complete mathematical derivation from rotating coordinate transformations, quantitative calculation of deflection of freely falling bodies, winds, and Foucault pendulum principles.

Prerequisites

  • Vector kinematics and cross products
  • Newton's laws of motion in non-inertial reference frames
  • Angular velocity vector representation

Origin of the Coriolis Force

  • Arises due to the time derivative of rotating unit vectors in a non-inertial reference frame.
  • Does not perform work on the particle because the force vector is always perpendicular to the velocity vector in the rotating frame.
  • Depends linearly on the mass, velocity of the particle in the rotating frame, and the angular velocity of the frame.

Direction and Magnitude

  • Given mathematically by the vector expression F = -2m(omega x v), where v is the relative velocity.
  • In the Northern Hemisphere, the Coriolis force deflects moving objects to the right of their direction of motion.
  • In the Southern Hemisphere, the deflection is to the left of the direction of motion.

Terrestrial Effects

  • Responsible for the counter-clockwise rotation of cyclones in the Northern Hemisphere and clockwise in the Southern Hemisphere.
  • Causes a freely falling body dropped from a height to be deflected eastward due to the Earth's rotation.
  • Zero at the equator where the rotation axis is parallel to the local vertical, and maximum at the poles.

Distinction from Centrifugal Force

  • Centrifugal force depends on the position vector from the axis of rotation, whereas Coriolis force depends on the velocity vector.
  • Centrifugal force is radially outward, while Coriolis force is transverse to the motion.
  • Both are apparent forces introduced to apply Newton's laws in rotating coordinate systems.

Derivations

Transformation of Velocity and Acceleration to a Rotating Frame

Conditions: Frame rotating with constant angular velocity omega

  • Express a position vector r in terms of rotating unit vectors (i', j', k'): r = x'i' + y'j' + z'k'.
  • Take the time derivative in the inertial frame using operator identity: (dr/dt)_inertial = (dr/dt)_rotating + omega x r.
  • Apply the operator to the velocity vector v_rotating to obtain the acceleration in the inertial frame: a_inertial = a_rotating + 2(omega x v_rotating) + omega x (omega x r).

Result: a_rotating = a_inertial - 2(omega x v) - omega x (omega x r)

Deflection of a Freely Falling Body

Conditions: Body dropped from height h at latitude lambda in Northern Hemisphere

  • Write the equation of motion in the rotating earth frame: a = g_eff - 2(omega x v).
  • For zeroth-order motion, falling vertically under gravity: v_z = -gt, so z = h - (1/2)gt^2.
  • Substitute v into the Coriolis term: F_coriolis / m = -2(omega x v). Evaluating components shows an eastward acceleration equal to 2 omega v_z cos(lambda).
  • Integrate the eastward acceleration twice with respect to time to find the total eastward displacement.

Result: Eastward deflection delta = (1/3) omega g t^3 cos(lambda) = (1/3) omega cos(lambda) sqrt(8h^3 / g)

Solved numericals

TGT core

A body of mass 2 kg is moving with a velocity of 10 m/s from west to east at the equator. If the angular velocity of the Earth is 7.27 x 10^-5 rad/s, calculate the magnitude of the Coriolis force.

  • Identify given values: m = 2 kg, v = 10 m/s, omega = 7.27 x 10^-5 rad/s.
  • At the equator, the velocity vector is perpendicular to the Earth's rotation axis, so theta = 90 degrees.
  • Use formula F_cor = 2 m v omega sin(90) = 2 * 2 * 10 * (7.27 x 10^-5) * 1.
  • Calculate result: 4 * 10 * 7.27 x 10^-5 = 2.908 x 10^-3 N.

Answer: 2.91 x 10^-3 N

PGT extension

An object is dropped from a height of 100 meters at a place with latitude 30 degrees north. Given g = 9.8 m/s^2 and omega = 7.27 x 10^-5 rad/s, find its eastward deflection.

  • Recall formula for eastward deflection: delta = (1/3) * omega * cos(lambda) * sqrt(8 h^3 / g).
  • Substitute values: h = 100 m, lambda = 30 degrees (cos(30) = sqrt(3)/2), g = 9.8 m/s^2, omega = 7.27 x 10^-5 rad/s.
  • Calculate time of fall t = sqrt(2h / g) = sqrt(200 / 9.8) = 4.517 s.
  • Calculate h^3 = 10^6, sqrt(8 * 10^6 / 9.8) = 903.51.
  • Evaluate delta = (1/3) * (7.27 x 10^-5) * (0.866) * 903.51 = 0.0179 meters.

Answer: 1.79 cm (Eastward)

PGT extension

Find the magnitude of the Coriolis acceleration for a vehicle moving due North at 72 km/h at a latitude of 45 degrees.

  • Convert velocity to SI units: v = 72 km/h = 72 * (5/18) = 20 m/s.
  • Identify Earth angular velocity omega = 7.27 x 10^-5 rad/s and latitude lambda = 45 degrees.
  • The effective vertical component of angular velocity contributing to horizontal Coriolis force is omega_z = omega * sin(lambda).
  • Coriolis acceleration a_cor = 2 * omega_z * v = 2 * omega * sin(45) * v.
  • Substitute: 2 * (7.27 x 10^-5) * (0.7071) * 20 = 2.05 x 10^-3 m/s^2.

Answer: 2.05 x 10^-3 m/s^2

Assumptions and limitations

  • The rotating frame has a constant angular velocity vector omega (d(omega)/dt = 0).
  • Applicable only within non-relativistic mechanics where v is much less than the speed of light.
  • Assumes the Earth can be treated as a uniformly rotating reference frame, ignoring local crustal anomalies or non-spherical perturbations for basic derivations.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The Coriolis force does physical work on moving objects because it accelerates them.

Correct: The Coriolis force does zero work on a particle.

Since the Coriolis force is always perpendicular to the instantaneous velocity vector in the rotating frame, the instantaneous power (F . v) is identically zero.

Common error: Water drains clockwise or counter-clockwise in washbasins strictly due to the Coriolis force.

Correct: The Coriolis force is too weak to affect small-scale phenomena like draining sinks.

Initial asymmetries, container geometry, and residual angular momentum dominate washbasin vortex directions; Coriolis force only dictates large-scale meteorological systems like typhoons.

Common error: Coriolis force is a real fundamental force of nature.

Correct: Coriolis force is a fictitious or inertial force.

It arises purely as a mathematical consequence of formulating Newtonian mechanics in an accelerated (rotating) coordinate system.

Quick revision

  • Coriolis force formula is F_cor = -2m(omega x v).
  • It causes rightward deflection in the Northern Hemisphere and leftward in the Southern Hemisphere.
  • It does no work as it remains perpendicular to the relative velocity vector.
  • Maximum at the poles and zero at the equator.
  • Responsible for the rotation direction of large-scale weather cyclones and Foucault pendulum precession.

Must remember

  • The Coriolis force is given by the vector expression $F = -2m(\omega \times v)$ in a uniformly rotating frame of reference.
  • The Coriolis force does zero work on a particle because it is always perpendicular to the velocity vector in the rotating frame.
  • In the Northern Hemisphere, the Coriolis force deflects moving objects to the right, while in the Southern Hemisphere, the deflection is to the left.
  • The Coriolis force is zero at the equator and achieves its maximum value at the poles.
  • A freely falling body dropped from a height experiences an eastward deflection due to the Earth's rotation.
  • Unlike the centrifugal force which depends on the position vector $r$, the Coriolis force depends on the velocity vector $v$.

Glossary

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the vector expression for the Coriolis force in a rotating frame with angular velocity $\$\omega$$ and relative velocity $v$ for a mass $m$?

Explanation: The Coriolis force is mathematically defined as $F_{cor} = -2m(\$\omega$ \times v)$, where $v$ is the velocity measured in the rotating coordinate system.

2. How much work does the Coriolis force perform on a moving particle in a rotating frame?

Explanation: The Coriolis force is always perpendicular to the instantaneous velocity vector of the particle in the rotating frame, hence the instantaneous power $(F \cdot v)$ is identically zero, meaning it does no work.

3. In which direction is a horizontally moving object deflected by the Coriolis force in the Northern Hemisphere?

Explanation: Due to the cross product in $F = -2m(\$\omega$ \times v)$, the Coriolis force causes a deflection to the right of the direction of motion in the Northern Hemisphere.

4. At which location on Earth is the magnitude of the Coriolis force maximum for a given velocity?

Explanation: The magnitude of the Coriolis force depends on $\sin(\lambda)$ where $\lambda$ is the latitude (or angle with respect to the local vertical). At the poles, $\sin(90^\circ) = 1$, making it maximum, while at the equator it is zero.

5. A freely falling body dropped from a height $h$ is deflected in which direction due to the Earth's rotation?

Explanation: As a body falls, its downward velocity combined with the Earth's eastward angular rotation produces a westward Coriolis force component, leading to a net eastward deflection when calculated relative to the plumb line.

6. What is the primary reason why the Coriolis force cannot dictate the direction in which water drains in a household washbasin?

Explanation: The Coriolis force is extremely weak and requires large spatial and temporal scales (like cyclones or ocean currents) to produce observable rotation. Small-scale flow is dominated by initial asymmetry and container shape.

7. Which of the following forces is classified as a fictitious or inertial force introduced in rotating coordinate systems?

Explanation: The Coriolis force is an apparent (fictitious or inertial) force that arises purely as a mathematical consequence of transforming Newton's laws into a rotating, non-inertial reference frame.

8. What is the correct expression for the magnitude of the Coriolis force acting on a particle of mass $m$ moving with speed $v$ at an angle $\$\theta$$ to the rotation axis?

Explanation: The scalar magnitude of the cross product $\$\omega$ \times v$ is $v\$\omega$ \sin(\$\theta$)$, which when multiplied by $2m$ yields $F_{cor} = 2mv\$\omega$ \sin(\$\theta$)$.

9. What causes the counter-clockwise rotation of cyclones in the Northern Hemisphere?

Explanation: As air rushes toward a low-pressure center, the Coriolis force deflects the winds to the right in the Northern Hemisphere, causing a counter-clockwise spiral pattern.

10. How does the Coriolis acceleration component for horizontal motion on Earth scale with latitude $\lambda$?

Explanation: The effective vertical component of the Earth's rotation vector relevant to horizontal motion introduces a $\sin(\lambda)$ factor, giving $a_{cor} = 2\$\omega$ v \sin(\lambda)$.

11. What is the precession rate $\$\omega_{p}$$ of a Foucault pendulum at latitude $\lambda$?

Explanation: The plane of oscillation of a Foucault pendulum rotates at a rate proportional to the vertical component of the Earth's angular velocity, which is given by $\$\omega_{p}$ = \$\omega$ \sin(\lambda)$.

12. In the total inertial acceleration expression for a rotating frame, $a_{in} = a_{rot} + 2(\$\omega$ \times v) + \$\omega$ \times (\$\omega$ \times r) + \dot{\$\omega$} \times r$, what does the term $2(\$\omega$ \times v)$ represent?

Explanation: The term $2(\$\omega$ \times v)$ is the Coriolis acceleration arising from the coupling of the frame's angular velocity and the particle's relative velocity.

13. In the Southern Hemisphere, how is a moving projectile deflected by the Coriolis force?

Explanation: The direction of the Coriolis force reverses in the Southern Hemisphere because the sign of the projection of the rotation vector relative to the local upward vertical inverts, causing a leftward deflection.

14. Under what condition is the Coriolis force equal to zero for a moving particle in a rotating frame?

Explanation: Since the Coriolis force involves the cross product $\$\omega$ \times v$, if the particle's velocity vector $v$ is parallel (or anti-parallel) to the angular velocity vector $\$\omega$$, the cross product vanishes.

15. What distinguishes the centrifugal force from the Coriolis force regarding their physical dependencies?

Explanation: The centrifugal force is given by $-m[\$\omega$ \times (\$\omega$ \times r)]$ which depends on position $r$, while the Coriolis force is given by $-2m(\$\omega$ \times v)$ which depends on velocity $v$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. A particle of mass $m$ moves with velocity $v$ in a frame rotating with uniform angular velocity $\$\omega$$. What is the magnitude and direction of the Coriolis force experienced by the particle?

Explanation: The Coriolis force vector is $-2m(\$\omega$ \times v)$, which has a magnitude of $2mv\$\omega$ \sin(\$\theta$)$ and acts in a direction perpendicular to the plane formed by $\$\omega$$ and $v$.

2. Which of the following statements regarding the work done by the Coriolis force is correct?

Explanation: Because the Coriolis force vector is perpendicular to the velocity vector $v$ at all times in the rotating frame, the instantaneous power transferred is zero, meaning no work is done.

3. A body is dropped freely from a height $h$ above the Earth's surface at a latitude $\lambda$. Due to the Coriolis effect, the eastward deflection $x$ is proportional to:

Explanation: The formula for eastward deflection of a falling body is $x = \frac{1}{3}\$\omega$ \cos(\lambda) \sqrt{\frac{8h^3}{g}}$, which shows that the deflection is directly proportional to $h^{3/2}$.

4. At which of the following places is the Coriolis force on a moving train strictly zero?

Explanation: The Coriolis force vanishes where the local rotation vector component perpendicular to velocity is zero, or more fundamentally at the equator where the rotation axis is parallel to the local horizontal plane for specific velocity vectors, or when velocity is zero.

5. In the analysis of non-inertial frames, the total inertial acceleration includes the Euler acceleration term $\dot{\$\omega$} \times r$. When does this term vanish?

Explanation: The Euler acceleration $\dot{\$\omega$} \times r$ accounts for non-uniform rotation. In a uniformly rotating frame, $\$\omega$$ is constant, so its time derivative $\dot{\$\omega$}$ is zero, making this term vanish.

6. What is the precession frequency of a Foucault pendulum located at the North Pole ($\lambda = 90^\circ$)?

Explanation: The precession rate is given by $\$\omega_{p}$ = \$\omega$ \sin(\lambda)$. At the North Pole, $\sin(90^\circ) = 1$, so the precession rate equals the Earth's angular velocity $\$\omega$$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. If a particle moves eastward along the equator, what is the direction of the Coriolis force acting on it?

Explanation: At the equator, the Earth's angular velocity vector $\$\omega$$ points north. For an eastward velocity $v$, $\$\omega$ \times v$ points vertically upward, and with the negative sign in $F = -2m(\$\omega$ \times v)$, the Coriolis force is directed vertically downward.

2. Which of the following kinematic terms in a rotating frame represents the apparent force depending linearly on the particle's velocity?

Explanation: The Coriolis force is the only apparent force in rotating frames that depends linearly on the relative velocity $v$ of the particle.

3. What happens to the Coriolis force on a moving object if the angular velocity of the reference frame is doubled while keeping the object's relative velocity constant?

Explanation: Since the Coriolis force equation $F_{cor} = -2m(\$\omega$ \times v)$ depends linearly on $\$\omega$$, doubling the angular velocity doubles the Coriolis force.

4. Why does a Foucault pendulum change its plane of oscillation over time?

Explanation: The precession of the Foucault pendulum is a direct macroscopic manifestation of the Coriolis force acting on the laterally moving bob of the pendulum in the Earth's rotating reference frame.

5. In meteorological systems, geostrophic wind is achieved when there is a balance between which two forces?

Explanation: Geostrophic balance occurs in large-scale atmospheric motion where the horizontal pressure gradient force is exactly balanced by the Coriolis force.

6. What is the effective acceleration due to gravity $g_{eff}$ modified by?

Explanation: The effective acceleration due to gravity incorporates the true gravitational acceleration corrected by the centrifugal force term, $g_{eff} = g - \$\omega$ \times (\$\omega$ \times r)$.

7. If an object is projected vertically upward from the Earth's surface, in which direction is it deflected due to the Coriolis force?

Explanation: An upward moving body ($v$ is upward) in the Northern Hemisphere ($\$\omega$$ has an upward-north component) experiences a cross product $\$\omega$ \times v$ directed westward, which with the negative sign yields an eastward deflection.

8. Which statement correctly contrasts real forces and inertial forces like the Coriolis force?

Explanation: Real forces stem from fundamental physical interactions (like gravity or electromagnetism) and obey Newton's third law, while inertial (fictitious) forces arise solely because the chosen reference frame is accelerated.

9. What is the angle $\$\theta$$ between the velocity vector and the Earth's rotation axis for a horizontally moving object at the equator?

Explanation: At the equator, the Earth's rotation axis lies in the plane of the horizon. A horizontal motion vector is perpendicular to the local vertical, and for motion parallel to the meridian/east-west, the angle with the rotation axis is $90^\circ$.

10. If the Earth were to stop rotating ($\$\omega$ = 0$), what would happen to the Coriolis force acting on moving air masses?

Explanation: Since the Coriolis force is directly proportional to the angular velocity $\$\omega$$, if $\$\omega$ = 0$, the Coriolis force vanishes entirely.
