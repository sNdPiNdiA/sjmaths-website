Rotating Frame of Reference and Fictitious Forces

A rotating frame of reference is a non-inertial coordinate system that undergoes rotation with respect to an inertial frame. In such a frame, Newton's laws of motion do not hold unless fictitious forces such as centrifugal and Coriolis forces are introduced.

Prerequisites

  • Vector kinematics and cross products

Inertial vs. Rotating Non-Inertial Frames

  • An inertial frame is one in which Newton's first law of motion is valid, meaning bodies remain at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a net external force.
  • A rotating frame rotates with a constant or time-varying angular velocity ω relative to an inertial frame, making it accelerated and hence non-inertial.
  • Observers in a rotating frame must introduce fictitious or pseudo forces to apply Newton's laws successfully.

Centrifugal Force

  • The centrifugal force is an apparent outward radial force experienced by objects in a rotating frame of reference.
  • Its magnitude is given by F_c = m(ω x (r x ω)) or mω<sup>2</sup>r for perpendicular distance r.
  • It acts radially outward from the axis of rotation and is responsible for the reduction of effective gravitational acceleration at the Earth's equator.

Coriolis Force

  • The Coriolis force is a deflection force experienced by moving objects within a rotating frame of reference.
  • Its magnitude depends on the velocity of the particle relative to the rotating frame and is given by F_cor = -2m(ω x v'), where v' is the relative velocity.
  • It acts perpendicular to both the velocity vector of the object and the angular velocity vector of the rotating frame, causing rightward deflection in the Northern Hemisphere.

Euler Force

  • The Euler force arises in a rotating frame when the angular velocity of the frame is not constant (i.e., α = dω/dt is non-zero).
  • Its magnitude is given by F_Euler = -m(α x r), where r is the position vector from the axis of rotation.
  • It acts in the transverse direction, producing linear acceleration during non-uniform rotation.

Derivations

Transformation of Time Derivatives of a Vector

Conditions: Relating a vector measured in a stationary inertial frame S to a rotating frame S' sharing the same origin.

  • Express any arbitrary vector Q in terms of its components along the unit vectors of the rotating frame: Q = Q_x i' + Q_y j' + Q_z k'.
  • Take the time derivative in the inertial frame S: dQ/dt = (dQ_x/dt)i' + (dQ_y/dt)j' + (dQ_z/dt)k' + Q_x(di'/dt) + Q_y(dj'/dt) + Q_z(dk'/dt).
  • Recognize that the time derivative of any unit vector rotating with angular velocity ω is given by du/dt = ω x u.
  • Substitute these derivatives to yield the operator relation: (d/dt)_inertial = (d/dt)_rotating + ω x.

Result: (dQ/dt)_i = (dQ/dt)_r + \omega \times Q

Transformation of Acceleration to a Rotating Frame

Conditions: Differentiating the position vector r in both frames to find total acceleration.

  • The position vector r in both frames is identical if origins coincide: r_i = r_r = r.
  • Apply the operator relation to velocity v_i = v_r + ω x r.
  • Apply the operator relation a second time to velocity to obtain acceleration: a_i = a_r + ω x v_r + ω x (v_r + ω x r).
  • Collect terms to isolate the rotating frame acceleration a_r: a_r = a_i - 2(ω x v_r) - ω x (ω x r).

Result: a_r = a_i - 2(\omega \times v_r) - \omega \times (\omega \times r) - \alpha \times r

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the centrifugal acceleration of a point on the Earth's equator. Take Earth's radius R = 6400 km and angular velocity &omega; = 7.3 x 10^-5 rad/s.

  • Identify the formula for centrifugal acceleration: a_c = ω<sup>2</sup>R.
  • Substitute the given values: ω = 7.3 x 10^-5 rad/s and R = 6.4 x 10<sup>6</sup> m.
  • Calculate: a_c = (7.3 x 10^-5)<sup>2</sup> x 6.4 x 10<sup>6</sup>.
  • a_c = 5.329 x 10^-9 x 6.4 x 10<sup>6</sup> = 0.0341 m/s<sup>2</sup>.

Answer: 0.0341 m/s^2

PGT extension

A body is dropped from a height of 100 m at a latitude of 45° in the Northern Hemisphere. Calculate its eastward deflection due to the Coriolis force. Take g = 9.8 m/s^2 and &omega; = 7.27 x 10^-5 rad/s.

  • Time of fall t is given by t = \sqrt(2h/g) = \sqrt(2 x 100 / 9.8) = \sqrt(20.408) = 4.517 s.
  • Eastward deflection formula: s = \frac{1}{3} ω g t^3 \cos(λ).
  • Substitute values: ω = 7.27 x 10^-5, g = 9.8, t = 4.517, λ = 45°, cos(45°) = 0.7071.
  • s = \frac{1}{3} x (7.27 x 10^-5) x 9.8 x (4.517)<sup>3</sup> x 0.7071.
  • s = \frac{1}{3} x (7.27 x 10^-5) x 9.8 x 92.16 x 0.7071 = 0.0152 meters = 1.52 cm.

Answer: 1.52 cm (Eastward)

PGT extension

A particle of mass 2 kg moves with a velocity of 5 m/s along the x'-axis inside a coordinate frame rotating with a constant angular velocity &omega; = 2 rad/s along the z-axis. Find the magnitude and direction of the Coriolis force.

  • Formula for Coriolis force: F_cor = -2 m (ω x v_r).
  • Given m = 2 kg, v_r = 5 i' m/s, ω = 2 k' rad/s.
  • Evaluate cross product: ω x v_r = (2 k') x (5 i') = 10 (k' x i') = 10 j' N/m.
  • Substitute into force equation: F_cor = -2 (2) (10 j') = -40 j' N.
  • Magnitude is 40 N acting along the negative y'-axis.

Answer: 40 N along negative y'-axis

Assumptions and limitations

  • The angular velocity ω of the rotating frame is often assumed uniform unless Euler force terms are explicitly included.
  • Derivations assume standard rigid body rotation where internal distances remain invariant.
  • Relativistic effects are ignored; classical Newtonian mechanics applies.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Centrifugal force is a reaction force to centripetal force according to Newton's third law.

Correct: Centrifugal force is a fictitious force acting on masses in non-inertial rotating frames, not a true interactive force pair.

Newton's third law requires forces to arise from interaction between two bodies; centrifugal force depends on the choice of coordinate system.

Common error: Coriolis force performs work on a moving body and changes its kinetic energy.

Correct: Coriolis force is always perpendicular to the velocity of the particle in the rotating frame, so it does zero work.

Since W = F . v and F_cor is perpendicular to v, the work done is zero and kinetic energy in the rotating frame remains unchanged by it.

Quick revision

  • Rotating frames require fictitious forces: centrifugal, Coriolis, and Euler.
  • Centrifugal force points radially outward: F_c = mω<sup>2</sup>r.
  • Coriolis force depends on velocity: F_cor = -2m(ω x v_r).
  • Effective gravity is minimum at the equator and maximum at the poles.
  • Coriolis force deflects moving objects to the right in the Northern Hemisphere.

Must remember

  • An inertial frame is one where Newton's laws hold valid, whereas a rotating frame is accelerated and non-inertial.
  • Centrifugal force is a fictitious force directed radially outward with magnitude $F_c = m \omega^2 r$.
  • Coriolis force depends on the relative velocity of the particle and acts perpendicular to both velocity and angular velocity: $F_{cor} = -2m(\omega \times v')$.
  • Euler force arises during non-uniform rotation when angular acceleration $\alpha$ is non-zero: $F_{Euler} = -m(\alpha \times r)$.
  • The Coriolis force does no work on a moving particle because it is always perpendicular to its velocity vector in the rotating frame.
  • Effective acceleration due to gravity on Earth is modified by the centrifugal term as $g' = g - \omega^2 R \cos^2(\lambda)$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following frames of reference is classified as an inertial frame?

Explanation: An inertial frame is one in which bodies remain at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a net external force. A rocket moving with constant velocity represents an inertial frame, whereas accelerating or rotating frames are non-inertial.

2. What is the magnitude of the centrifugal force experienced by a mass $m$ at a perpendicular distance $r$ from the axis of a rotating frame with angular speed $\$\omega$$?

Explanation: The centrifugal force is given by $F_c = m \$\omega$^2 r$ for a perpendicular distance $r$ from the axis of rotation, acting radially outward.

3. The Coriolis force experienced by a particle of mass $m$ moving with velocity $v'$ in a rotating frame with angular velocity $\$\omega$$ is given by:

Explanation: The Coriolis force is mathematically expressed as $F_{cor} = -2m(\$\omega$ \times v')$, where $v'$ is the relative velocity in the rotating frame.

4. Under what condition does the Euler force arise in a rotating reference frame?

Explanation: The Euler force arises in a non-uniformly rotating frame where the angular velocity changes with time, making the angular acceleration $\$\alpha$$ non-zero ($F_{Euler} = -m(\$\alpha$ \times r)$).

5. Why does the Coriolis force do zero work on a moving particle in a rotating frame?

Explanation: Since work done $W = \int F \cdot dr$, and the Coriolis force is always perpendicular to the velocity ($v'$), the dot product is zero, resulting in zero work done.

6. What is the primary cause of the reduction of effective gravitational acceleration at the Earth's equator compared to the poles?

Explanation: The centrifugal force acts radially outward from the axis of rotation, opposing gravity most effectively at the equator where the distance from the axis is maximum, reducing the effective gravity ($g' = g - \$\omega$^2 R$).vida

7. In the Northern Hemisphere, moving objects experience a deflection due to the Coriolis force. In which direction is this deflection relative to the direction of motion?

Explanation: Due to the cross-product direction in $-2m(\$\omega$ \times v')$, moving objects in the Northern Hemisphere are deflected to the right.

8. What is the expression for apparent acceleration $a_r$ in a general rotating reference frame relative to an inertial frame acceleration $a_i$?

Explanation: The full apparent acceleration includes contributions from inertial acceleration, Coriolis acceleration, centrifugal acceleration, and Euler acceleration.

9. Which of the following statements is a common misconception about the centrifugal force?

Explanation: Centrifugal force is often mistakenly called the reaction to centripetal force. However, Newton's third law requires forces to act on two interacting bodies, whereas centrifugal force arises solely due to the non-inertial nature of the coordinate system.

10. For an object dropped from rest from a height $h$, the eastward deflection due to the Coriolis force at latitude $\lambda$ is proportional to which power of the time of fall $t$?

Explanation: The eastward deflection formula is given by $s = \frac{1}{3} \$\omega$ g t^3 \cos(\lambda)$, which shows it is proportional to $t^3$.

11. If a frame of reference rotates with a constant angular velocity $\$\omega$$, which fictitious force(s) must be considered for a stationary object in that frame?

Explanation: For a stationary object in the rotating frame, relative velocity $v' = 0$, so the Coriolis force is zero. Only the centrifugal force acts.

12. The operator transformation relation between an inertial frame ($i$) and a rotating frame ($r$) for an arbitrary vector $Q$ is given by:

Explanation: The time derivative transformation is $(\frac{dQ}{dt})_i = (\frac{dQ}{dt})_r + \$\omega$ \times Q$, accounting for the rotation of the coordinate axes.

13. What is the direction of the Euler force vector relative to the position vector $r$ and angular acceleration $\$\alpha$$?

Explanation: Since $F_{Euler} = -m(\$\alpha$ \times r)$, the force is directed perpendicular to both $\$\alpha$$ and $r$ according to the cross product definition.

14. At what location on Earth is the Coriolis force due to Earth's rotation equal to zero for a horizontally moving wind current?

Explanation: The magnitude of the Coriolis force depends on $\sin(\$\theta$)$ or latitude components. At the equator, the horizontal component of angular velocity perpendicular to the velocity yields zero vertical/horizontal deflection effect of that form, or mathematically $\$\theta$ = 0$ alignment makes the cross product vanish for horizontal motion.

15. Which branch of mechanics primarily requires the introduction of pseudo forces to solve dynamic problems correctly?

Explanation: Non-inertial frames are accelerated, necessitating the introduction of fictitious or pseudo forces (like centrifugal and Coriolis forces) so that Newton's laws can be applied.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. An object of mass $m$ is placed on a rotating turntable at a distance $r$ from the axis. If the turntable rotates with angular velocity $\$\omega$$, the magnitude of the centrifugal force experienced by the object is:

Explanation: The centrifugal force in a rotating frame is a fictitious outward force given by $F_c = m \$\omega$^2 r$.

2. Which of the following forces is responsible for the rightward deflection of winds in the Northern Hemisphere?

Explanation: The Coriolis force, given by $F_{cor} = -2m(\$\omega$ \times v')$, deflects moving air masses to the right in the Northern Hemisphere.

3. If a reference frame rotates with a non-zero and time-dependent angular velocity $\$\omega$$, the fictitious force that accounts for transverse acceleration is:

Explanation: The Euler force $F_{Euler} = -m(\$\alpha$ \times r)$ arises explicitly when the angular velocity is non-uniform ($\$\alpha$ = d\$\omega$/dt \neq 0$).

4. What is the work done by the Coriolis force on a particle moving in a rotating frame of reference?

Explanation: Since the Coriolis force is always perpendicular to the velocity vector of the particle, the dot product $F_{cor} \cdot v' = 0$, meaning zero work is done.

5. The effective acceleration due to gravity $g'$ on the surface of the Earth at latitude $\lambda$ is expressed as:

Explanation: The centrifugal acceleration due to Earth's rotation modifies true gravity, resulting in $g' = g - \$\omega$^2 R \cos^2(\lambda)$.

6. Why are fictitious forces introduced when applying Newton's laws of motion in a rotating reference frame?

Explanation: Fictitious forces are introduced in non-inertial frames to compensate for the fact that the reference frame is accelerating, allowing Newton's laws to be formally applied.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following describes a non-inertial frame of reference?

Explanation: Any frame that undergoes acceleration (linear or rotational) is non-inertial.

2. What is the formula for Centrifugal Acceleration in steady rotation?

Explanation: Centrifugal acceleration is given by $a_c = -\$\omega$ \times (\$\omega$ \times r)$.

3. If an object is dropped from a tower, in which direction does it deflect due to the Earth's rotation in the Northern Hemisphere?

Explanation: A falling body has a velocity component downwards, and the Coriolis deflection leads to an eastward displacement.

4. In the relation $a_r = a_i - 2(\$\omega$ \times v_r) - \$\omega$ \times (\$\omega$ \times r) - \$\alpha$ \times r$, the term $-2(\$\omega$ \times v_r)$ represents:

Explanation: The term $-2(\$\omega$ \times v_r)$ corresponds to the Coriolis acceleration.

5. Can a centrifugal force exist in an inertial frame of reference?

Explanation: Centrifugal force is a fictitious force that only appears when observations are made from a rotating (non-inertial) frame.

6. What is the magnitude of the Coriolis force when the velocity of the particle is parallel to the angular velocity vector $\$\omega$$?

Explanation: Since $F_{cor} = -2m(\$\omega$ \times v')$, if $\$\omega$$ and $v'$ are parallel, the cross product is zero, making the Coriolis force zero.

7. Which of the following statements about Foucault's pendulum is correct?

Explanation: Foucault's pendulum is a classic demonstration of the Earth's rotation, where the plane of oscillation rotates due to the Coriolis force.

8. If the angular velocity of a rotating platform doubles while keeping the distance constant, how does the centrifugal force change?

Explanation: Since $F_c = m \$\omega$^2 r$, doubling $\$\omega$$ increases the force by a factor of $2^2 = 4$.

9. Which force is responsible for generating cyclones and anticyclones on a planetary scale?

Explanation: The Coriolis force plays a dominant role in large-scale meteorological phenomena like the rotation of winds in cyclones and anticyclones.

10. What are the units of the Coriolis force?

Explanation: Being a force, its SI unit is the Newton (N).
