Background of Vector Calculus in Mechanics

Vector calculus provides the foundational mathematical framework for describing spatial rates of change and integration of physical quantities in mechanics. It enables rigorous formulation of position, velocity, acceleration, and the interaction of forces within scalar and vector fields.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Basic vector algebra, dot and cross products, scalar and vector point functions, and time derivatives of vectors in cartesian coordinates.

PGT extension

Gradient, divergence, curl, line, surface, and volume integrals, Green's, Gauss's divergence, and Stokes' theorems applied to central forces and gravitational fields.

Prerequisites

  • Fundamental geometry and trigonometry
  • Cartesian coordinate systems
  • Differential and integral calculus of single and multiple variables

Scalars and Vectors in Mechanics

  • A scalar quantity is completely specified by a magnitude with appropriate units (e.g., mass, energy, time).
  • A vector quantity possesses both magnitude and direction and obeys the triangle law of addition (e.g., displacement, velocity, force).
  • In a three-dimensional Cartesian coordinate system, a vector A is expressed as A = Ax î + Ay ĵ + Az k̂.

Time Derivatives of Vectors

  • The time derivative of a vector whose magnitude and direction change with time requires accounting for the rotation of the unit vectors.
  • For a rotating reference frame, the time derivative of vector A is given by d/dt (A) = (dA/dt)rel + ω × A.
  • This formulation is essential for analyzing kinematics in non-inertial systems, including the derivation of Coriolis and centrifugal forces.

Gradient, Divergence, and Curl

  • The gradient of a scalar field φ yields a vector pointing in the direction of the maximum spatial rate of change.
  • The divergence of a vector field F measures the net outward flux per unit volume from an infinitesimal element, representing sources or sinks.
  • The curl of a vector field F measures the local rotation or circulation of the field about a point.

Integral Theorems in Mechanics

  • Gauss's Divergence Theorem relates the volume integral of the divergence of a vector field to the surface enclosing it.
  • Stokes' Theorem connects the surface integral of the curl of a vector field over an open surface to the line integral around its boundary curve.
  • These theorems are directly applied to derive differential forms of conservation laws, such as Gauss's law for gravity.

Derivations

Time Derivative of a Rotating Unit Vector

Conditions: A unit vector rotating with an angular velocity vector &omega; about a fixed axis.

  • Consider a unit vector û rotating in a plane with angular speed dθ/dt = ω.
  • Express the components of û in Cartesian coordinates as û = cos(θ) î + sin(θ) ĵ.
  • Differentiate with respect to time t: dû/dt = -sin(θ)(dθ/dt) î + cos(θ)(dθ/dt) ĵ.
  • Substitute ω and recognize that the resulting vector is perpendicular to û, matching the cross product ω × û.

Result: \frac{d\hat{u}}{dt} = \vec{\omega} \times \hat{u}

Conservative Force and Potential Gradient

Conditions: A force field F(r) satisfying &nabla; &times; F = 0.

  • Define the work done W in moving a particle in a conservative force field as W = ∫ F · dr = - ΔU, where U is potential energy.
  • Express the infinitesimal change in potential energy as dU = -F · dr = - (Fx dx + Fy dy + Fz dz).
  • By definition of the total differential of U(x, y, z), dU = (∂U/∂x)dx + (∂U/∂y)dy + (∂U/∂z)dz.
  • Compare coefficients of dx, dy, and dz to obtain Fx = -∂U/∂x, etc., leading to vector form.

Result: \vec{F} = -\vec{\nabla}U

Scalar Field Contour Lines and Gradient

Draw: Concentric closed loops representing equipotential contours with orthogonal arrows pointing uphill representing the gradient vector field.

Interpret: The gradient vector is everywhere perpendicular to the equipotential contour lines and points in the direction of the steepest ascent.

Exam use: Tested in conceptual questions linking potential energy contours to conservative force directions.

Physical Interpretation of Divergence

Draw: A small control volume with vector field lines expanding outward (positive divergence) versus contracting inward (negative divergence).

Interpret: Positive divergence denotes a source where net flux originates; negative divergence denotes a sink where flux terminates.

Exam use: Used in questions related to fluid flow continuity and inverse-square central gravitational fields.

Physical Interpretation of Curl

Draw: A vector field showing shear or circular flow around an axis, with a small paddle wheel placed in the flow to indicate rotation.

Interpret: Non-zero curl indicates rotational flow or the presence of a localized vortex; zero curl denotes an irrotational field.

Exam use: Frequently tested to distinguish between conservative (irrotational) and non-conservative force fields.

Solved numericals

TGT core

Find the angle between the two vectors A = 2î + 2ĵ - k̂ and B = 6î - 3ĵ + 2k̂.

  • Calculate the dot product: A · B = (2)(6) + (2)(-3) + (-1)(2) = 12 - 6 - 2 = 4.
  • Calculate the magnitude of A: |A| = √(22 + 22 + (-1)2) = √(4 + 4 + 1) = √9 = 3.
  • Calculate the magnitude of B: |B| = √(62 + (-3)2 + 22) = √(36 + 9 + 4) = √49 = 7.
  • Use the dot product formula cos(θ) = (A · B) / (|A||B|) = 4 / (3 × 7) = 4/21.

Answer: \theta = \cos^{-1}\left(\frac{4}{21}\right)

PGT extension

Determine the divergence and curl of the vector field F = xz<sup>3</sup> î - 2x<sup>2</sup>yz ĵ + 2pz<sup>2</sup>k̂ at point (1, -1, 1).

  • Identify components: Fx = xz3, Fy = -2x2yz, Fz = 2yz2 (taking standard scalar parameters).
  • Compute divergence: ∇ · F = ∂/∂x(xz3) + ∂/∂y(-2x2yz) + ∂/∂z(2yz2) = z3 - 2x2z + 4yz.
  • Substitute point (1, -1, 1): ∇ · F = (1)3 - 2(1)2(1) + 4(-1)(1) = 1 - 2 - 4 = -5.
  • Compute curl components: (∇ × F)x = ∂Fz/∂y - ∂Fy/∂z = 2z2 - (-2x2y) = 2(1)2 + 2(1)2(-1) = 2 - 2 = 0.

Answer: Divergence = -5, Curl x-component = 0

PGT extension

Show that the force field F = yz î + xz ĵ + xy k̂ is conservative and find its corresponding scalar potential U.

  • Compute curl of F: ∇ × F = î(∂/∂y(xy) - ∂/∂z(xz)) - ĵ(∂/∂x(xy) - ∂/∂z(yz)) + k̂(∂/∂x(xz) - ∂/∂y(yz)) = î(x - x) - ĵ(y - y) + k̂(z - z) = 0.
  • Since ∇ × F = 0, the force field is conservative.
  • Integrate Fx = -∂U/∂x = yz ⇒ U = -xyz + f(y,z).
  • Differentiate U with respect to y and equate to -Fy: -∂U/∂y = xz + ∂f/∂y = xz ⇒ ∂f/∂y = 0.
  • Integrate with respect to z to confirm consistency, yielding U = -xyz + C.

Answer: U = -xyz + C (Conservative as ∇ × F = 0)

Assumptions and limitations

  • Vector calculus operations assume space is continuous and differentiable.
  • Cartesian coordinate formulations presume an inertial reference frame unless fictitious forces are explicitly introduced via rotating frame transformations.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The cross product of two vectors is another vector with commutative properties (A &times; B = B &times; A).

Correct: The cross product is anti-commutative: A &times; B = -(B &times; A).

Reversing the vector order reverses the direction of the resultant vector according to the right-hand rule.

Common error: Any arbitrary force field can be expressed as the negative gradient of a scalar potential.

Correct: Only conservative (irrotational) force fields can be expressed as F = -&nabla;U.

Non-conservative forces like friction have non-zero curl (&nabla; &times; F &ne; 0) and cannot be derived from a single scalar potential.

Quick revision

  • Gradient converts a scalar to a vector; divergence and curl operate on vector fields via dot and cross products with the del operator.
  • Gradient of a Scalar Field: \vec{\nabla}\phi = \hat{i}\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x} + \hat{j}\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y} + \hat{k}\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z}
  • Divergence of a Vector Field: \vec{\nabla} \cdot \vec{F} = \frac{\partial F_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_z}{\partial z}

Must remember

  • A scalar quantity is completely specified by magnitude, whereas a vector quantity possesses both magnitude and direction and obeys the triangle law of addition.
  • In a three-cartesian coordinate system, vector $\vec{A}$ is expressed as $\vec{A} = A_x \hat{i} + A_y \hat{j} + A_z \hat{k}$.
  • The time derivative of a vector in a rotating reference frame accounts for angular velocity: $\frac{d\vec{A}}{dt} = \left(\frac{d\vec{A}}{dt}\right)_{rel} + \vec{\omega} \times \vec{A}$.
  • The gradient of a scalar field $\phi$ yields a vector field pointing in the direction of the maximum spatial rate of change: $\vec{\nabla}\phi$.
  • The divergence of a vector field $\vec{F}$ measures the net outward flux per unit volume: $\vec{\nabla} \cdot \vec{F}$.
  • The curl of a vector field $\vec{F}$ measures the local rotation or circulation: $\vec{\nabla} \times \vec{F}$.
  • Gauss's Divergence Theorem relates a volume integral of divergence to a closed surface integral: $\iiint_V (\vec{\nabla} \cdot \vec{F}) dV = \iint_S \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{S}$.
  • Stokes' Theorem connects an open surface integral of curl to a line integral around its boundary: $\iint_S (\vec{\nabla} \times \vec{F}) \cdot d\vec{S} = \oint_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$.
  • Only conservative force fields possess zero curl and can be represented as the negative gradient of a scalar potential: $\vec{\nabla} \times \vec{F} = 0$.
  • The cross product of two vectors is anti-commutative: $\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = -(\vec{B} \times \vec{A})$.

Glossary

Scalar QuantityA physical quantity completely specified by a magnitude with appropriate units, such as mass and energy.
Vector QuantityA physical quantity possessing both magnitude and direction that obeys the triangle law of addition, such as displacement and velocity.
GradientAn operator applied to a scalar field yielding a vector pointing in the direction of the maximum rate of increase.
DivergenceA vector operator that measures the magnitude of a vector field's source or sink at a given point.
CurlA vector operator that describes the infinitesimal rotation of a vector field in three-dimensional space.
Gauss's Divergence TheoremA theorem equating the volume integral of a divergence over a region to the flux of the vector field through the boundary surface.
Stokes' TheoremA theorem relating the surface integral of the curl of a vector field over a surface to the line integral of the vector field over its boundary curve.
Coriolis AccelerationAn acceleration experienced by bodies in a rotating reference frame, given by $\vec{a}_{cor} = 2(\vec{\omega} \times \vec{v}_{rel})$.
Central ForceA force directed toward or away from a fixed center whose magnitude depends only on the distance from the center.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following quantities is a vector in mechanics?

Explanation: Displacement possesses both magnitude and direction and obeys the triangle law of addition, classifying it as a vector.

2. The cross product of two vectors $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ satisfies which of the following relations?

Explanation: The cross product is anti-commutative, meaning reversing the order reverses the direction of the resultant vector: $\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = -(\vec{B} \times \vec{A})$.

3. In a three-dimensional Cartesian coordinate system, a vector $\vec{A}$ is expressed as:

Explanation: A vector in Cartesian coordinates is resolved into its orthogonal components along the unit vectors $\hat{i}$, $\hat{j}$, and $\hat{k}$ as $\vec{A} = A_x \hat{i} + A_y \hat{j} + A_z \hat{k}$.

4. The gradient of a scalar potential function $\$\phi$$ is represented mathematically as:

Explanation: The gradient of a scalar field $\$\phi$$ is denoted by $\vec{\nabla}\$\phi$$ and yields a vector field pointing in the direction of the maximum spatial rate of change.

5. The divergence of a vector field $\vec{F}$ is given by:

Explanation: Divergence involves the dot product of the del operator with a vector field, written as $\vec{\nabla} \cdot \vec{F}$.

6. What physical property does the curl of a vector field $\vec{F}$ measure?

Explanation: The curl of a vector field ($\vec{\nabla} \times \vec{F}$) measures the local rotation or circulation of the vector field about a point.

7. Gauss's Divergence Theorem relates a volume integral to which of the following?

Explanation: Gauss's Divergence Theorem states that $\iiint_V (\vec{\nabla} \cdot \vec{F}) dV = \iint_S \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{S}$, linking a volume integral to a closed surface integral.

8. Stokes' Theorem connects the surface integral of the curl of a vector field to:

Explanation: Stokes' Theorem is given by $\iint_S (\vec{\nabla} \times \vec{F}) \cdot d\vec{S} = \oint_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$, linking an open surface integral to a closed line integral around its boundary.

9. For a rotating reference frame, the time derivative of vector $\vec{A}$ includes which additional term compared to an inertial frame?

Explanation: The time derivative of a vector in a rotating frame is $\frac{d\vec{A}}{dt} = \left(\frac{d\vec{A}}{dt}\right)_{rel} + \vec{\$\omega$} \times \vec{A}$, accounting for the rotation of unit vectors via $\vec{\$\omega$} \times \vec{A}$.

10. Coriolis acceleration in a rotating non-inertial reference frame is expressed as:

Explanation: Coriolis acceleration is given by $\vec{a}_{cor} = 2(\vec{\$\omega$} \times \vec{v}_{rel})$, which is essential for motion analysis in rotating frames.

11. A force field $\vec{F}$ is conservative if and only if:

Explanation: An irrotational vector field has zero curl ($\vec{\nabla} \times \vec{F} = 0$), which is the necessary and sufficient condition for a force field to be conservative.

12. What is the acceleration component along the radial direction in a plane polar coordinate system?

Explanation: In polar coordinates, the acceleration vector is $\vec{a} = (\ddot{r} - r\dot{\$\theta$}^2)\hat{r} + (r\ddot{\$\theta$} + 2\dot{r}\dot{\$\theta$})\hat{_{\$\theta$}}$, making the radial component $\ddot{r} - r\dot{\$\theta$}^2$.

13. Which differential operator applied to a vector field results in a scalar field?

Explanation: The divergence operator computes the dot product of the del operator with a vector field, yielding a scalar quantity.

14. If a force field is central, which condition must it satisfy?

Explanation: A central force is directed radially along $\hat{r}$ with a magnitude depending only on $r$, and it is also irrotational ($\vec{\nabla} \times \vec{F} = 0$).

15. What is the divergence of the position vector $\vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$ in three dimensions?

Explanation: The divergence of $\vec{r}$ is $\frac{\partial x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial z}{\partial z} = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3$.

2. Calculate the divergence of the vector field $\vec{F} = x^2 y \hat{i} + y^2 z \hat{j} + z^2 x \hat{k}$ at point $(1, 2, 3)$.

Explanation: Divergence is $\vec{\nabla} \cdot \vec{F} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^2 y) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(y^2 z) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(z^2 x) = 2xy + 2yz + 2zx$. At $(1,2,3)$: $2(1)(2) + 2(2)(3) + 2(3)(1) = 4 + 12 + 6 = 22$. Wait, let's check: $4+12+6 = 22$. Option C is 22.

3. What is the curl of the vector field $\vec{F} = yz\hat{i} + zx\hat{j} + xy\hat{k}$?

Explanation: Computing curl: $\vec{\nabla} \times \vec{F} = \left(\frac{\partial (xy)}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial (zx)}{\partial z}\right)\hat{i} + \dots = (x - x)\hat{i} + (y - y)\hat{j} + (z - z)\hat{k} = 0$. The field is conservative/irrotational.

4. If $\vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$ and $r = |\vec{r}|$, what is the gradient of $r^n$?

Explanation: Using chain rule, $\vec{\nabla}(r^n) = n r^{n-1} \vec{\nabla}r = n r^{n-1} \left(\frac{\vec{r}}{r}\right) = n r^{n-2} \vec{r}$.

5. Evaluate the line integral $\oint_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$ for a conservative force field $\vec{F}$ around any closed loop $C$.

Explanation: For any conservative force field, the work done around a closed path is zero because $\vec{\nabla} \times \vec{F} = 0$ and the field can be derived from a scalar potential.

6. The transverse component of acceleration in plane polar coordinates is given by:

Explanation: In polar coordinates, the acceleration vector is $\vec{a} = (\ddot{r} - r\dot{\$\theta$}^2)\hat{r} + (r\ddot{\$\theta$} + 2\dot{r}\dot{\$\theta$})\hat{\$\theta$}$, where the transverse component is $r\ddot{\$\theta$} + 2\dot{r}\dot{\$\theta$}$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which mathematical theorem links a volume integral of divergence to a surface integral?

Explanation: Gauss's Divergence Theorem establishes the relation between the volume integral of a vector field's divergence and its flux across the bounding surface.

2. What is the Laplacian of a scalar field $\$\phi$$, denoted as $\nabla^2 \$\phi$$?

Explanation: The Laplacian is defined as the divergence of the gradient of a scalar field: $\nabla^2 \$\phi$ = \vec{\nabla} \cdot (\vec{\nabla} \$\phi$)$.

3. If $\vec{F} = 2x\hat{i} + 3y\hat{j} + 4z\hat{k}$, what is its divergence?

Explanation: Divergence is $\frac{\partial (2x)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (3y)}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial (4z)}{\partial z} = 2 + 3 + 4 = 9$.

4. The curl of the gradient of any continuously differentiable scalar field $\$\phi$$ is always:

Explanation: An identity in vector calculus states that the curl of a gradient is identically zero: $\vec{\nabla} \times (\vec{\nabla} \$\phi$) = 0$.

5. The divergence of the curl of any continuously differentiable vector field $\vec{F}$ is always:

Explanation: Another fundamental vector identity states that the divergence of a curl is always zero: $\vec{\nabla} \cdot (\vec{\nabla} \times \vec{F}) = 0$.

6. In a non-inertial reference frame rotating with angular velocity $\vec{\$\omega$}$, the centrifugal acceleration experienced by a particle at position $\vec{r}$ is:

Explanation: Centrifugal acceleration is given by $-\vec{\$\omega$} \times (\vec{\$\omega$} \times \vec{r})$ (or directed radially outward from the axis of rotation as $\$\omega$^2 \rho$). Formally expressed via vector cross products as $-\vec{\$\omega$} \times (\vec{\$\omega$} \times \vec{r})$.

7. Which of the following conditions ensures a vector field $\vec{A}$ is solenoidal?

Explanation: A solenoidal vector field is one whose divergence is identically zero everywhere ($\vec{\nabla} \cdot \vec{A} = 0$).

8. What does Stokes' Theorem relate?

Explanation: Stokes' Theorem relates the surface integral of the curl of a vector field over an open surface $S$ to the line integral around its boundary curve $C$.

9. If $\vec{r}$ is the position vector, what is $\vec{\nabla} \cdot (r^2 \vec{r})$?

Explanation: Using product rule for divergence: $\vec{\nabla} \cdot (r^2 \vec{r}) = r^2 (\vec{\nabla} \cdot \vec{r}) + \vec{r} \cdot (\vec{\nabla} r^2) = r^2(3) + \vec{r} \cdot (2r \frac{\vec{r}}{r}) = 3r^2 + 2r^2 = 5r^2$.

10. Which of the following force fields is non-conservative?

Explanation: Frictional force depends on the path taken and dissipates mechanical energy, possessing non-zero curl, making it non-conservative.
