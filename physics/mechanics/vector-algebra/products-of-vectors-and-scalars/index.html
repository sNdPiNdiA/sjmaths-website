Products of Vectors and Scalars in Mechanics

Vector algebra involves operations such as scalar (dot) and vector (cross) products that combine vector quantities in mechanics. These operations form the mathematical foundation for analyzing force, torque, angular momentum, and work.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Geometrical interpretation of dot and cross products, orthogonal unit vectors, work done by constant and variable forces, torque, and condition for perpendicularity and parallelism.

PGT extension

Scalar triple products (box product), vector triple products, applications to central forces, Coriolis acceleration, angular velocity vectors, and rotational mechanics.

Prerequisites

  • Definition of scalars and vectors
  • Resolution of a vector into rectangular components in 3D Cartesian coordinates
  • Basic trigonometric identities and properties of determinants

Scalar Product (Dot Product)

  • Defined as the product of the magnitudes of two vectors and the cosine of the smaller angle between them: A · B = AB cos(θ).
  • Results in a scalar quantity; commutative (A · B = B · A) and distributive over vector addition.
  • Orthogonal unit vectors satisfy i · i = j · j = k · k = 1 and i · j = j · k = k · i = 0.

Vector Product (Cross Product)

  • Defined as a vector whose magnitude is AB sin(θ) and whose direction is given by the right-hand rule perpendicular to the plane containing A and B.
  • Non-commutative and anticommutative: A × B = -(B × A).
  • Orthogonal unit vectors satisfy i × i = 0, i × j = k, j × k = i, and k × i = j.

Scalar Triple Product

  • Expressed as A · (B × C), representing the volume of the parallelepiped formed by vectors A, B, and C.
  • Cyclic permutation leaves the scalar triple product invariant: A · (B × C) = B · (C × A) = C · (A × B).
  • If the scalar triple product is zero, the three vectors are coplanar.

Vector Triple Product

  • Expressed as A × (B × C), resulting in a vector lying in the plane of B and C.
  • Evaluated using the BAC-CAB rule: A × (B × C) = B(A · C) - C(A · B).
  • Essential in deriving the kinematics of rotational motion and central force trajectories.

Derivations

Cartesian Component Expansion of Dot Product

Conditions: Vectors given in terms of their orthogonal components in 3D space.

  • Let A = A_x i + A_y j + A_z k and B = B_x i + B_y j + B_z k.
  • Expand the dot product: A · B = (A_x i + A_y j + A_z k) · (B_x i + B_y j + B_z k).
  • Apply distributive property and orthogonality relations (i · i = 1, i · j = 0).
  • Collect non-zero terms.

Result: A · B = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z

Cartesian Component Expansion of Cross Product

Conditions: Vectors given in terms of their orthogonal components in 3D space.

  • Let A = A_x i + A_y j + A_z k and B = B_x i + B_y j + B_z k.
  • Write the cross product as a determinant: A × B = det([[i, j, k], [A_x, A_y, A_z], [B_x, B_y, B_z]]).
  • Expand the determinant along the first row.
  • Substitute fundamental cross products of unit vectors.

Result: A × B = (A_y B_z - A_z B_y)i - (A_x B_z - A_z B_x)j + (A_x B_y - A_y B_x)k

Draw: A parallelogram formed by adjacent vectors A and B, highlighting the height h = B sin(&theta;).

Interpret: The magnitude of the cross product |A &times; B| equals the area of the parallelogram spanned by A and B.

Exam use: Frequently used in calculating angular momentum and torque magnitudes.

Right-Hand Screw Rule Verification

Draw: A 3D coordinate system showing the rotation from vector A to vector B curling the fingers of the right hand, with the thumb pointing along A &times; B.

Interpret: Determines the unambiguous direction of axial vectors like torque and angular velocity.

Exam use: Crucial for sign conventions in rotational dynamics and electromagnetism.

Solved numericals

TGT core

Find the angle between the two vectors A = 3i + 4j + 5k and B = 3i + 4j - 5k.

  • Calculate the dot product: A · B = (3)(3) + (4)(4) + (5)(-5) = 9 + 16 - 25 = 0.
  • Use the dot product formula: A · B = AB cos(θ).
  • Since A · B = 0 and magnitudes are non-zero, cos(θ) = 0.
  • Therefore, θ = 90°.

Answer: 90 degrees

TGT core

Determine the torque of a force F = (2i - 3j + 4k) N acting at a position r = (3i + 2j + 3k) m about the origin.

  • Set up the cross product matrix for τ = r × F = det([[i, j, k], [3, 2, 3], [2, -3, 4]]).
  • Expand i component: i[(2)(4) - (3)(-3)] = i(8 + 9) = 17i.
  • Expand j component: -j[(3)(4) - (3)(2)] = -j(12 - 6) = -6j.
  • Expand k component: k[(3)(-3) - (2)(2)] = k(-9 - 4) = -13k.

Answer: (17i - 6j - 13k) N m

PGT extension

Calculate the volume of the parallelepiped formed by the vectors A = i - j + k, B = 2i + j - k, and C = i + 2j + 2k.

  • The volume is given by the scalar triple product V = |A · (B × C)|.
  • Construct the determinant with rows as components of A, B, and C: det([[1, -1, 1], [2, 1, -1], [1, 2, 2]]).
  • Evaluate determinant: 1[(1)(2) - (-1)(2)] - (-1)[(2)(2) - (-1)(1)] + 1[(2)(2) - (1)(1)].
  • Simplify terms: 1(2 + 2) + 1(4 + 1) + 1(4 - 1) = 4 + 5 + 3 = 12.

Answer: 12 cubic units

Assumptions and limitations

  • Vector products assume a three-dimensional Euclidean space with a right-handed Cartesian coordinate system.
  • Scalar and vector products are defined for spatial vectors; pseudo-vectors (axial vectors like torque) obey specific transformation rules under spatial inversion (parity).

Common misconceptions

Common error: The cross product of two vectors is another vector that depends on the choice of origin.

Correct: The cross product of two free vectors is independent of the coordinate origin, though position-dependent vector products (like torque) do depend on the chosen pivot.

Cross products of displacement or force vectors independent of reference points are geometric entities.

Common error: A &times; B is equal to B &times; A.

Correct: A &times; B = -(B &times; A) due to the anticommutative property of the vector product.

Reversing the order changes the direction of the right-hand rule thumb by 180 degrees.

Quick revision

  • Dot product yields a scalar (A B cos θ); cross product yields a vector (A B sin θ n-hat).
  • Perpendicular vectors have zero dot product; parallel vectors have zero cross product.
  • BAC-CAB rule: A × (B × C) = B(A · C) - C(A · B).
  • Scalar triple product vanishing implies coplanarity of three vectors.

Must remember

  • Scalar product $A \cdot B = AB \cos(\theta)$ results in a scalar quantity and is commutative.
  • Vector product $|A \times B| = AB \sin(\theta)$ results in a vector and is anticommutative: $A \times B = -(B \times A)$.
  • Condition for two non-zero vectors to be perpendicular is $A \cdot B = 0$.
  • Condition for two non-zero vectors to be parallel or antiparallel is $A \times B = 0$.
  • Scalar triple product $A \cdot (B \times C)$ represents the volume of the parallelepiped formed by the three vectors.
  • Vector triple product is evaluated using the BAC-CAB rule: $A \times (B \times C) = B(A \cdot C) - C(A \cdot B)$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the result of the dot product of two orthogonal unit vectors $i \cdot j$?

Explanation: Orthogonal unit vectors are perpendicular to each other, so $\cos(90^{\circ}) = 0$, making $i \cdot j = 0$.

2. Which of the following represents the vector product cross operation for unit vectors $i \times j$?

Explanation: By definition of the right-hand rule for cross products of orthogonal unit vectors, $i \times j = k$.

3. If two non-zero vectors $A$ and $B$ satisfy $A \cdot B = 0$, what is the angle between them?

Explanation: The dot product is zero when $\cos(\$\theta$) = 0$, which occurs at an angle of $90^{\circ}$.

4. Work done by a constant force $F$ over a displacement $d$ is expressed as:

Explanation: Work is a scalar quantity defined as the dot product of the force vector and the displacement vector: $W = F \cdot d$.

5. Torque ($\tau$) about a pivot is defined as the cross product of position vector $r$ and force $F$:

Explanation: Torque is defined as $\tau = r \times F$, representing the rotational analog of force.

6. What is the geometric interpretation of the scalar triple product $A \cdot (B \times C)$?

Explanation: The scalar triple product geometrically represents the volume of the parallelepiped formed by vectors $A$, $B$, and $C$.

7. Which of the following rules evaluates the vector triple product $A \times (B \times C)$?

Explanation: The vector triple product is expanded using the BAC-CAB rule: $A \times (B \times C) = B(A \cdot C) - C(A \cdot B)$.

8. If the scalar triple product of three vectors is zero, what can be deduced about the vectors?

Explanation: A zero scalar triple product implies that the volume of the parallelepiped is zero, meaning the three vectors lie in the same plane (coplanar).

9. Which property correctly describes the vector product?

Explanation: The vector product is anticommutative, meaning $A \times B = -(B \times C)$ is incorrect, but $A \times B = -(B \times A)$.

10. What is the cross product of a vector with itself, i.e., $A \times A$?

Explanation: The angle between a vector and itself is $0^{\circ}$, and $\sin(0^{\circ}) = 0$, so $A \times A = 0$.

11. Which of the following is true for cyclic permutation in a scalar triple product?

Explanation: Cyclic permutation leaves the scalar triple product invariant: $A \cdot (B \times C) = B \cdot (C \times A) = C \cdot (A \cdot B)$.

12. Under what condition are two non-zero vectors parallel?

Explanation: Parallel vectors have an angle of $0^{\circ}$ or $180^{\circ}$, making $\sin(\$\theta$) = 0$, hence $A \times B = 0$.

13. The scalar product of two vectors $A = 2i + 3j$ and $B = i - j$ is:

Explanation: Computing component-wise dot product: $(2)(1) + (3)(-1) = 2 - 3 = -1$.

14. What is the direction of the vector resulting from the cross product $A \times B$?

Explanation: By definition, the cross product yields a vector perpendicular to the plane containing both $A$ and $B$ as given by the right-hand rule.

15. Which quantity in mechanics is expressed as a scalar triple product application or volume element?

Explanation: The scalar triple product directly evaluates the geometric volume of a parallelepiped formed by three vectors.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. If $A = 2i + 2j - k$ and $B = 6i - 3j + 2k$, find the angle or check perpendicularity. What is $A \cdot B$?

Explanation: $A \cdot B = (2)(6) + (2)(-3) + (-1)(2) = 12 - 6 - 2 = 4$. Wait, let's re-verify: $(2)(6)=12$, $(2)(-3)=-6$, $(-1)(2)=-2$. $12 - 6 - 2 = 4$. Let's check options: option 0 is 4.

2. Find the magnitude of the cross product of vectors $A = i + j$ and $B = i - j$.

Explanation: $A \times B = (i + j) \times (i - j) = i \times i - i \times j + j \times i - j \times j = 0 - k - k - 0 = -2k$. The magnitude is $|-2k| = 2$.

3. For what value of $m$ are the vectors $A = i + 2j + 3k$ and $B = 2i + 4j + mk$ parallel?

Explanation: For parallel vectors, the components must be proportional: $1/2 = 2/4 = 3/m$, which gives $m = 6$. Alternatively, $A \times B = 0$.

4. Evaluate the scalar triple product $i \cdot (j \times k)$:

Explanation: Since $j \times k = i$, the expression becomes $i \cdot i = 1$.

5. If $A$, $B$, and $C$ are vectors, what does the vector triple product $A \times (B \times C)$ lie in?

Explanation: According to the BAC-CAB rule, $A \times (B \times C) = B(A \cdot C) - C(A \cdot B)$, which is a linear combination of $B$ and $C$, meaning it lies in the plane of $B$ and $C$.

6. Calculate the work done by a force $F = 3i + 4j$ N acting on a particle that undergoes a displacement $d = 2i - j$ m.

Explanation: $W = F \cdot d = (3)(2) + (4)(-1) = 6 - 4 = 2$ Joules.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What is the projection of vector $A$ on vector $B$?

Explanation: The scalar projection of vector $A$ onto vector $B$ is given by $A \cdot \hat{B} = (A \cdot B) / |B|$.

2. If the magnitudes of two vectors are $3$ and $4$ and their scalar product is $6$, what is the angle between them?

Explanation: $A \cdot B = AB \cos(\$\theta$) \implies 6 = (3)(4) \cos(\$\theta$) \implies \cos(\$\theta$) = 6/12 = 1/2$, so $\$\theta$ = 60^{\circ}$.

3. Which vector identity is correct for the vector triple product?

Explanation: The BAC-CAB rule states $A \times (B \times C) = B(A \cdot C) - C(A \cdot B)$, which matches option $(A \cdot C)B - (A \cdot B)C$.

4. What is the torque produced by a force $F = 2i$ N acting at a position vector $r = 3j$ m?

Explanation: $\tau = r \times F = (3j) \times (2i) = 6 (j \times i) = 6(-k) = -6k$ N m. Wait, $j \times i = -k$, so $(3j) \times (2i) = -6k$. Let's check correct option: option 1 is $-6k$ N m.

5. If $A \cdot B = |A \times B|$, what is the angle between $A$ and $B$?

Explanation: $AB \cos(\$\theta$) = AB \sin(\$\theta$) \implies \tan(\$\theta$) = 1 \implies \$\theta$ = 45^{\circ}$.

6. The scalar triple product $A \cdot (B \times C)$ is also denoted in determinant form using components. Which row order is standard?

Explanation: The rows of the determinant correspond to the components of vector $A$, then $B$, then $C$ respectively.

7. If $A = i + j + k$ and $B = 2i + 3j - k$, their dot product is:

Explanation: $(1)(2) + (1)(3) + (1)(-1) = 2 + 3 - 1 = 4$.

8. Which of the following operations is not mathematically meaningful?

Explanation: $B \cdot C$ is a scalar, so $A \cdot (B \cdot C)$ means dot product of a vector and a scalar, which is undefined.

9. If three vectors $A$, $B$, and $C$ are coplanar, then:

Explanation: For coplanar vectors, the volume of the parallelepiped is zero, meaning the scalar triple product $A \cdot (B \times C) = 0$.

10. What is the angle between the vectors $A = i + j$ and $B = i - j$?

Explanation: Their dot product is $(1)(1) + (1)(-1) = 0$, which proves that the angle between them is $90^{\circ}$.
