Scalar Product of Vectors

The scalar product, or dot product, of two vectors is defined as the product of their magnitudes multiplied by the cosine of the angle between them, resulting in a scalar quantity. It serves as a fundamental mathematical operation in mechanics for determining work, potential energy, and projections.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Geometric interpretation, orthogonal conditions, calculation of work done by constant forces, and projection of one vector onto another.

PGT extension

Application to central forces, work-energy theorem derivation, differentiation of scalar products with respect to time, and utilization in conservative force fields.

Prerequisites

  • Definition of vector and scalar quantities
  • Vector addition and resolution into Cartesian components
  • Basic trigonometric identities

Definition and Algebraic Representation

  • The scalar product of two vectors A and B is written as A · B = AB cos θ.
  • In Cartesian coordinate systems, A · B = AxBx + AyBy + AzBz.
  • The commutative property holds: A · B = B · A.

Orthogonality and Parallelism

  • For orthogonal vectors (θ = 90°), the scalar product is zero: î · ĵ = ĵ · k̂ = k̂ · î = 0.
  • For parallel vectors (θ = 0°), the scalar product equals the product of their magnitudes: î · î = ĵ · ĵ = k̂ · k̂ = 1.
  • If A · B = 0 for non-zero vectors, the vectors are strictly perpendicular.

Geometric Interpretation and Projection

  • Geometrically, A · B represents the product of the magnitude of A and the scalar component of B along A (B cos θ).
  • The scalar projection of vector B onto vector A is given by (B · A) / A.
  • It determines whether the angle between two vectors is acute (θ < 90°, dot product > 0), obtuse (θ > 90°, dot product < 0), or right angle (θ = 90°, dot product = 0).

Distributive Law over Vector Addition

  • The scalar product obeys the distributive law: A · (B + C) = (A · B) + (A · C).
  • This property allows the expansion of dot products using Cartesian unit vector components.
  • It is essential in computing work done when forces are expressed as sums of orthogonal components.

Derivations

Cartesian Expansion of Scalar Product

Conditions: Vectors given in terms of orthogonal unit vectors î, ĵ, k̂

  • Write vectors in component form: A = Axî + Ayĵ + Azk̂ and B = Bxî + Byĵ + Bzk̂.
  • Apply the distributive law: A · B = (Axî + Ayĵ + Azk̂) · (Bxî + Byĵ + Bzk̂).
  • Expand the cross terms and apply orthonormal conditions: î · î = 1, î · ĵ = 0, etc.

Result: A · B = AxBx + AyBy + AzBz

Work Done by a Constant Force using Scalar Product

Conditions: Particle displaced by displacement vector <b>s</b> under a constant force <b>F</b>

  • Express work W as the product of the component of force along the displacement and the magnitude of displacement.
  • Let θ be the angle between force vector F and displacement vector s.
  • Write the definition of scalar product: W = (F cos θ)s.

Result: W = F · s

Solved numericals

TGT core

Find the angle between the two vectors <b>A</b> = 3î + 4ĵ + 5k̂ and <b>B</b> = 3î + 4ĵ - 5k̂.

  • Calculate the dot product: A · B = (3)(3) + (4)(4) + (5)(-5) = 9 + 16 - 25 = 0.
  • Use the angle formula: cos θ = (A · B) / (AB).
  • Since A · B = 0, cos θ = 0, therefore θ = 90°.

Answer: 90° (or π/2 radians)

TGT core

Determine the work done by a force <b>F</b> = 2î - 3ĵ + k̂ N in displacing a particle from position <b>r</b><sub>1</sub> = î + 2ĵ - k̂ m to <b>r</b><sub>2</sub> = 3î + ĵ + 2k̂ m.

  • Find the displacement vector s = r2 - r1 = (3-1)î + (1-2)ĵ + (2-(-1))k̂ = 2î - ĵ + 3k̂ m.
  • Compute the scalar product W = F · s.
  • W = (2)(2) + (-3)(-1) + (1)(3) = 4 + 3 + 3 = 10 J.

Answer: 10 J

PGT extension

Find the value of constant 'c' such that the vectors <b>A</b> = 2î - cĵ + k̂ and <b>B</b> = î + 2ĵ - 3k̂ are mutually perpendicular.

  • For mutually perpendicular vectors, the scalar product must be zero: A · B = 0.
  • Compute the dot product in component form: (2)(1) + (-c)(2) + (1)(-3) = 0.
  • Simplify the equation: 2 - 2c - 3 = 0 => -1 - 2c = 0 => 2c = -1 => c = -0.5.

Answer: c = -0.5

Assumptions and limitations

  • The scalar product is defined strictly for vectors operating in Euclidean spaces of dimension 3 (or n-dimensional vector spaces with a positive-definite metric).

Common misconceptions

Common error: The scalar product of two vectors can result in a vector pointing along a new direction.

Correct: The scalar product always yields a scalar real number, never a vector.

By definition, multiplying magnitudes and the cosine of the angle collapses directional vectors into a single scalar magnitude.

Common error: If <b>A</b> &middot; <b>B</b> = <b>A</b> &middot; <b>C</b>, then vector <b>B</b> must equal vector <b>C</b>.

Correct: Vector <b>B</b> does not necessarily equal vector <b>C</b>.

The dot product only equates the components along vector <b>A</b>; components perpendicular to <b>A</b> can differ arbitrarily.

Quick revision

  • The scalar product A · B = AB cos θ is commutative, distributive, and yields zero for orthogonal vectors.
  • Definition of Scalar Product: A \cdot B = AB \cos \theta
  • Cartesian Component Form: A \cdot B = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z

Must remember

  • The scalar product of two vectors yields a scalar quantity given by $A \cdot B = AB \cos \theta$.
  • For orthogonal vectors, the scalar product is zero: $A \cdot B = 0$.
  • In Cartesian coordinates, $A \cdot B = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z$.
  • The scalar projection of vector $B$ onto vector $A$ is expressed as $\frac{A \cdot B}{|A|}$.
  • The time derivative of a scalar product follows the product rule: $\frac{d}{dt}(A \cdot B) = \frac{dA}{dt} \cdot B + A \cdot \frac{dB}{dt}$.

Glossary

Scalar ProductAn algebraic operation that takes two equal-length sequences of numbers (usually coordinate vectors) and returns a single number.
Orthogonal VectorsVectors whose scalar product is zero, indicating an angle of $90^\circ$ between them.
Scalar ProjectionThe length of the orthogonal projection of one vector onto a line containing the other vector.
Work DoneThe scalar product of the constant force vector and the displacement vector, $W = F \cdot s$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the scalar product of two mutually orthogonal unit vectors $\hat{i}$ and $\hat{j}$?

Explanation: Orthogonal vectors have an angle of $\$\theta$ = 90^\circ$ between them, so $\hat{i} \cdot \hat{j} = (1)(1)\cos(90^\circ) = 0$.

2. If $A \cdot B = AB$, what is the angle between vectors $A$ and $B$?

Explanation: The condition $A \cdot B = AB$ implies $\cos \$\theta$ = 1$, which occurs when $\$\theta$ = 0^\circ$ (parallel vectors).

3. What is the Cartesian component form of the scalar product for vectors $A = A_x\hat{i} + A_y\hat{j} + A_z\hat{k}$ and $B = B_x\hat{i} + B_y\hat{j} + B_z\hat{k}$?

Explanation: Due to the orthogonality of distinct unit vectors and the unity of identical unit vectors, $A \cdot B = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z$.

4. The scalar projection of vector $B$ onto vector $A$ is given by which expression?

Explanation: The scalar projection of vector $B$ along the direction of vector $A$ is defined as $(B \cos \$\theta$) = \frac{A \cdot B}{|A|}.

5. If the scalar product of two non-zero vectors $A$ and $B$ is negative ($A \cdot B < 0$), the angle $\$\theta$$ between them is:

Explanation: Since $\cos \$\theta$$ is negative for angles strictly between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, a negative dot product indicates an obtuse angle.

6. Which of the following properties holds true for the scalar product of vectors?

Explanation: The scalar product obeys the distributive law: $A \cdot (B + C) = (A \cdot B) + (A \cdot C)$. Associativity does not apply because the dot product of two vectors is a scalar, making $(A \cdot B) \cdot C$ a dot product of a scalar and a vector which is undefined.

7. Calculate the work done when a constant force $F = 2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} - \hat{k}$ N causes a displacement $s = \hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$ m.

Explanation: Work $W = F \cdot s = (2)(1) + (3)(-2) + (-1)(3) = 2 - 6 - 3 = -7$ J.

9. If two vectors $A = 3\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + \hat{k}$ and $B = \lambda\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} - 2\hat{k}$ are orthogonal, what is the value of $\lambda$?

Explanation: For orthogonality, $A \cdot B = 0 \implies 3\lambda + (-2)(4) + (1)(-2) = 0 \implies 3\lambda - 10 = 0 \implies \lambda = \frac{10}{3}$.

10. What is the time derivative of the scalar product of two time-dependent vectors $A(t)$ and $B(t)$?

Explanation: Following the product rule for scalar products, $\frac{d}{dt}(A \cdot B) = \frac{dA}{dt} \cdot B + A \cdot \frac{dB}{dt}$.

11. In conservative force fields, work done along a closed path is zero. How is this mathematically represented using the scalar product of force and infinitesimal displacement?

Explanation: By definition, for a conservative force field, the line integral of the force vector over any closed loop vanishes: $\oint F \cdot ds = 0$.

12. Which of the following correctly describes the misconception that $A \cdot B = A \cdot C$ implies $B = C$?

Explanation: The scalar product only provides information about the projection along $A$. Components orthogonal to $A$ can differ while yielding the same dot product.

13. What is the magnitude of the scalar product of two vectors of magnitudes $4$ and $5$ units respectively, acting at an angle of $60^\circ$?

Explanation: $A \cdot B = AB \cos \$\theta$ = (4)(5) \cos(60^\circ) = 20 \times \frac{1}{2} = 10$.

14. For any vector $A$, what is $A \cdot A$ equal to?

Explanation: $A \cdot A = |A||A| \cos(0^\circ) = A^2$, where $A$ is the magnitude of the vector.

15. What is the angle between the vectors $A = \hat{i} + \hat{j}$ and $B = \hat{i} - \hat{j}$?

Explanation: $A \cdot B = (1)(1) + (1)(-1) = 1 - 1 = 0$. Since the dot product is zero, the vectors are orthogonal, meaning the angle is $90^\circ$.

16. Which PGT-level concept utilizes the differentiation of scalar products with respect to time?

Explanation: Time derivatives of scalar products (such as $r \cdot v$) are extensively used in analyzing central forces, orbital mechanics, and verifying conservation theorems.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What is the scalar product of two vectors that are parallel and in opposite directions (antiparallel)?

Explanation: For antiparallel vectors, the angle $\$\theta$ = 180^\circ$, so $A \cdot B = AB \cos(180^\circ) = -AB$.

2. If the magnitudes of two vectors are $3$ and $4$ and their scalar product is $12$, the vectors are:

Explanation: Since $A \cdot B = 12 = (3)(4) = AB$, $\cos \$\theta$ = 1 \implies \$\theta$ = 0^\circ$, meaning the vectors are parallel.

3. Which of the following expressions represents the square of the magnitude of vector sum $A + B$?

Explanation: $|A + B|^2 = (A + B) \cdot (A + B) = A \cdot A + B \cdot B + 2(A \cdot B) = A^2 + B^2 + 2(A \cdot B)$.

4. If $A \cdot B = 0$ and $A \times B = 0$ for two non-zero vectors $A$ and $B$, what can be deduced?

Explanation: A dot product of zero implies orthogonality ($\$\theta$ = 90^\circ$), while a cross product of zero implies collinearity ($\$\theta$ = 0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$). Both cannot hold simultaneously for non-zero vectors, indicating a contradiction unless they are zero vectors.

5. What is the scalar product of $\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}$ and $2\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + \hat{k}$?

Explanation: Scalar product = $(1)(2) + (1)(-3) + (1)(1) = 2 - 3 + 1 = 0$. Wait, $2 - 3 + 1 = 0$! Let's check options: `["0", "1", "-1", "2"]`. Correct index is 0. Let's fix correct index to 0.

6. What is the scalar product of $\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}$ and $2\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + \hat{k}$?

Explanation: Scalar product = $(1)(2) + (1)(-3) + (1)(1) = 2 - 3 + 1 = 0$.

7. In potential energy formulations, a conservative force $F$ is related to potential energy $U$ via $F = -\nabla U$. The work done by this force in moving a particle through displacement $ds$ can be written as:

Explanation: $W = \int F \cdot ds = \int -(\nabla U) \cdot ds = -\int dU = -\Delta U$. Thus $dW = -dU$.

8. What is the angle $\$\theta$$ between two vectors $A$ and $B$ if their scalar product is equal to the magnitude of their vector product ($A \cdot B = |A \times B|$)?

Explanation: $AB \cos \$\theta$ = AB \sin \$\theta$ \implies \tan \$\theta$ = 1 \implies \$\theta$ = 45^\circ$.

9. If vector $A = 3\hat{i} + 4\hat{j}$, what is the scalar component (projection) of $A$ along the $x$-axis?

Explanation: The scalar projection of $A$ along the $x$-axis (unit vector $\hat{i}$) is $A \cdot \hat{i} = 3$.

10. For any two vectors $A$ and $B$, which inequality always holds true according to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for scalar products?

Explanation: Since $|A \cdot B| = AB |\cos \$\theta$|$ and $|\cos \$\theta$| \le 1$, it always follows that $|A \cdot B| \le AB$.

11. If the position vector of a particle is given by $r(t)$, what does the time derivative $\frac{d}{dt}(r \cdot r)$ represent physically?

Explanation: Using the rule for time derivative of scalar products, $\frac{d}{dt}(r \cdot r) = \frac{dr}{dt} \cdot r + r \cdot \frac{dr}{dt} = 2(r \cdot v)$, where $v$ is the velocity vector.
