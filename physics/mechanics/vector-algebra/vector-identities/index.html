Vector Identities in Mechanics

Vector identities form the mathematical foundation for analyzing vector fields, forces, and kinematics in analytical mechanics. They simplify complex differential operations involving scalar and vector products in three-dimensional Euclidean space.

UP TGT ScienceUP PGT PhysicsStudy Notes + Practice

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Basic vector algebra, scalar and vector products, directional derivatives, and application to work and torque.

PGT extension

Advanced triple products, gradient, divergence, curl operations, vector differential identities, and applications to central forces and Coriolis acceleration.

Prerequisites

  • Cartesian coordinate systems and unit vectors (i, j, k)
  • Dot and cross products of vectors
  • Partial differentiation of multivariable functions

Scalar Triple Product

  • Defined as a.(b x c), representing the signed volume of the parallelepiped formed by vectors a, b, and c.
  • Cyclic permutation of vectors leaves the scalar triple product invariant: a.(b x c) = b.(c x a) = c.(a x b).
  • If any two vectors are collinear or coplanar, the scalar triple product vanishes identically.

Vector Triple Product

  • Defined as a x (b x c), resolving into a linear combination of the vectors in the parenthesis.
  • Expands via the 'BAC minus CAB' rule: a x (b x c) = b(a.c) - c(a.b).
  • Non-associative property: a x (b x c) does not equal (a x b) x c in general.

Differential Vector Operators

  • The del operator (nabla) acts as a vector differential operator: ∇ = i(∂/∂x) + j(∂/∂y) + k(∂/∂z).
  • Gradient (∇φ) yields a vector pointing in the direction of the maximum spatial rate of change of a scalar field φ.
  • Divergence (∇.V) and curl (∇ x V) measure the source strength and rotational circulation of a vector field V respectively.

Applications to Mechanics

  • Central forces satisfy × F = 0, indicating they are conservative and can be derived from a scalar potential.
  • Angular momentum L = r x p utilizes vector products to describe rotational state.
  • Coriolis acceleration $a_c = 2(Ω x v) requires vector triple products$ and cross products for rotating reference frames.

Derivations

Expansion of Vector Triple Product a x (b x c)

Conditions: Three arbitrary vectors a, b, and c in Cartesian coordinates.

  • Let $D = b x c = i(b_yc_z - b_zc_y) + j(b_zc_x - b_xc_z) + k(b_xc_y - b_yc_x)$.
  • Compute a x D using the determinant expansion for cross products with unit vectors i, j, k.
  • Group terms corresponding to components of vectors b and c to establish the identity a x (b x c) = b(a.c) - c(a.b).

Result: a \times (b \times c) = b(a \cdot c) - c(a \cdot b)

Divergence of a Cross Product \nabla \cdot (A \times B)

Conditions: Differentiable vector fields A and B in a continuous domain.

  • Write the component form of \nabla \cdot (A \times B) using index notation or expansion rules.
  • Apply the product rule of differentiation to each term: \partial_i (A \times B)_i.
  • Rearrange terms to express the result in terms of curls of A and B.

Result: \nabla \cdot (A \times B) = B \cdot (\nabla \times A) - A \cdot (\nabla \times B)

Solved numericals

TGT core

Find the scalar triple product a.(b x c) where $a = i + j + k$, $b = 2i - j + k$, and $c = i + 2j - k$.

  • Set up the determinant with rows formed by the components of a, b, and c: |1 1 1; 2 -1 1; 1 2 -1|.
  • Expand along the first row: 1((-1)(-1) - (1)(2)) - 1((2)(-1) - (1)(1)) + 1((2)(2) - (-1)(1)).
  • Calculate each term: 1(1 - 2) - 1(-2 - 1) + 1(4 + 1) = 1(-1) - 1(-3) + 1(5).
  • Sum the values: -1 + 3 + 5 = 7.

Answer: 7

PGT extension

Evaluate the vector triple product a x (b x c) for $a = i - 2j$, $b = 2i + k$, and $c = j - k$.

  • First calculate the cross product b x $c = (2i + k) x (j - k)$.
  • Compute determinant: i(0*(-1) - 1*1) - j(2*(-1) - 0*1) + k(2*1 - 0*0) = -i + 2j + 2k.
  • Now compute a x (b x c) = (i - 2j) x (-i + 2j + 2k).
  • Expand determinant: i((-2)*2 - (-2)*2) - j(1*2 - 0) + k(1*2 - (-2)*(-1)) = 0i - 2j + (2 - 2)$k = -2j$.

Answer: -2j

PGT extension

Prove that if r is the position vector $r = \xi + yj + zk$ and r = |r|, then ∃ ($r^n) = n r^(n-2) r$.

  • Write $r = (x^2 + y^2 + z^2)^(n/2)$.
  • Take the partial derivative with respect to x: ∂/∂x ($r^n) = n r^(n-1) (∂r/∂x)$.
  • Since $r^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$, differentiating implicitly gives 2r (∂r/∂x) = 2x, so ∂r/∂$x = x/r$.
  • Substitute back: ∂/∂x ($r^n) = n r^(n-1) (x/r) = n r^(n-2) x$.
  • Combine all components for gradient: ∃($r^n) = i (n r^(n-2) x) + j (n r^(n-2) y) + k (n r^(n-2) z) = n r^(n-2) r$.

Answer: n r^(n-2) r

Assumptions and limitations

  • Vector identities assume a right-handed three-dimensional Cartesian coordinate system unless generalized to curvilinear coordinates.
  • Differential identities require the scalar and vector fields to be continuously differentiable to the requisite order.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Assuming vector cross product is associative, so that a x (b x c) equals (a x b) x c.

Correct: Vector cross product is non-associative; a x (b x c) != (a x b) x c in general.

Cross products depend strictly on spatial orientation and parentheses grouping according to the BAC-CAB rule.

Common error: Believing that the curl of any vector field is zero.

Correct: Only curl of a gradient field (or conservative field) is identically zero.

General vector fields possess non-zero curl representing local rotation or vorticity.

Quick revision

  • Scalar triple product a.(b x c) represents volume and cyclic permutation preserves its value.
  • Vector triple product expands as a x (b x c) = b(a.c) - c(a.b).
  • Curl of gradient and divergence of curl are always identically zero: ∃ x (∃φ) = 0 and ∃.(∃ x A) = 0.

Must remember

  • The scalar triple product $a \cdot (b \times c)$ represents the signed volume of the parallelepiped formed by vectors $a$, $b$, and $c$, and is invariant under cyclic permutation.
  • The vector triple product expands according to the BAC-CAB rule: $a \times (b \times c) = b(a \cdot c) - c(a \cdot b)$.
  • Vector cross product is non-associative; $a \times (b \times c) \neq (a \times b) \times c$ in general.
  • The curl of a gradient field is identically zero: $\nabla \times (\nabla \phi) = 0$, which implies conservative forces satisfy $\nabla \times F = 0$.
  • The divergence of a curl is identically zero: $\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times A) = 0$ for any continuously twice-differentiable vector field $A$.

Glossary

Scalar Triple ProductThe dot product of one vector with the cross product of two other vectors, $a \cdot (b \times c)$.
Vector Triple ProductThe cross product of a vector with the cross product of two other vectors, $a \times (b \times c)$.
Nabla OperatorThe vector differential operator $\nabla = i(\partial/\partial x) + j(\partial/\partial y) + k(\partial/\partial z)$.
CurlA differential operator measuring the rotational circulation of a vector field, given by $\nabla \times V$.
DivergenceA differential operator measuring the source or sink strength of a vector field, given by $\nabla \cdot V$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What does the scalar triple product $a \cdot (b \times c)$ geometrically represent?

Explanation: The scalar triple product $a \cdot (b \times c)$ geometrically represents the signed volume of the parallelepiped constructed on the three vectors.

2. Which of the following identities correctly expands the vector triple product $a \times (b \times c)$?

Explanation: The vector triple product expands via the BAC-CAB rule: $a \times (b \times c) = b(a \cdot c) - c(a \cdot b)$.

3. Which of the following is true regarding the cyclic permutation of vectors in a scalar triple product?

Explanation: Cyclic permutation of vectors leaves the scalar triple product invariant: $a \cdot (b \times c) = b \cdot (c \times a) = c \cdot (a \times b)$.

4. What is the result of the curl of a gradient of a scalar field $\phi$, i.e., $\nabla \times (\nabla \phi)$?

Explanation: The curl of any gradient field is identically zero: $\nabla \times (\nabla \phi) = 0$, demonstrating that gradient fields are irrotational.

5. What is the value of the divergence of a curl of a vector field $A$, i.e., $\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times A)$?

Explanation: For any continuously twice-differentiable vector field $A$, the divergence of its curl is identically zero: $\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times A) = 0$.

6. Which condition must be met for the scalar triple product $a \cdot (b \times c)$ to vanish identically?

Explanation: If any two vectors in a scalar triple product are collinear or coplanar, the parallelepiped has zero volume, making the product vanish.

7. Which of the following vector cross product properties is generally false?

Explanation: The vector cross product is non-associative; $a \times (b \times c) \neq (a \times b) \times c$ in general.

8. For a conservative central force field $F$, what is the value of its curl $\nabla \times F$?

Explanation: Central forces satisfy $\nabla \times F = 0$, indicating they are conservative and can be derived from a scalar potential.

9. How is the Coriolis acceleration $a_c$ in a rotating reference frame expressed using vector operations?

Explanation: The Coriolis acceleration is given by $a_c = 2(\Omega \times v)$, involving the cross product of the angular velocity vector and linear velocity.

10. What does the divergence operator $\nabla \cdot V$ physically measure for a vector field $V$?

Explanation: The divergence $\nabla \cdot V$ measures the source strength or flux per unit volume of the vector field $V$.

11. Which identity expands the curl of a scalar-vector product $\nabla \times (\phi A)$?

Explanation: The curl of the product of a scalar $\phi$ and a vector $A$ expands as $\nabla \times (\phi A) = \nabla \phi \times A + \phi(\nabla \times A)$.

12. What is the expansion for the curl of a curl of a vector field, $\nabla \times (\nabla \times A)$?

Explanation: The curl of a curl identity is given by $\nabla \times (\nabla \times A) = \nabla(\nabla \cdot A) - \nabla^2 A$.

13. What does the gradient operator $\nabla \phi$ yield when applied to a scalar field $\phi$?

Explanation: The gradient $\nabla \phi$ yields a vector pointing in the direction of the maximum spatial rate of change of the scalar field $\phi$.

14. What is the divergence of a scalar-vector product $\nabla \cdot (\phi A)$?

Explanation: The divergence of a scalar-vector product expands as $\nabla \cdot (\phi A) = \phi(\nabla \cdot A) + A \cdot (\nabla \phi)$.

15. In classical mechanics, angular momentum $L$ of a particle with position vector $r$ and momentum $p$ is defined as:

Explanation: Angular momentum is defined via the vector cross product as $L = r \times p$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. For any three vectors $a$, $b$, and $c$, the vector triple product $a \times (b \times c)$ is equal to:

Explanation: According to the BAC-CAB rule, $a \times (b \times c) = b(a \cdot c) - c(a \cdot b)$.

2. If $A$ is a differentiable vector field, then the value of $\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times A)$ is always:

Explanation: The divergence of the curl of any twice-differentiable vector field is identically zero.

3. The scalar triple product $a \cdot (b \times c)$ is NOT equal to which of the following?

Explanation: Cyclic permutations preserve value: $a \cdot (b \times c) = b \cdot (c \times a) = c \cdot (a \times b)$. Swapping two vectors changes the sign, so $a \cdot (c \times b) = -a \cdot (b \times c)$.

4. A force field $F$ is conservative if and only if:

Explanation: A vector field is conservative (derivable from a scalar potential) if and only if its curl is identically zero ($\nabla \times F = 0$).

5. What is the expansion of $\nabla \times (\nabla \times A)$ in standard vector differential operator form?

Explanation: The standard vector identity for the curl of a curl is $\nabla \times (\nabla \times A) = \nabla(\nabla \cdot A) - \nabla^2 A$.

6. The Coriolis acceleration acting on a particle moving with velocity $v$ in a rotating frame with angular velocity $\Omega$ is:

Explanation: The Coriolis acceleration formula is $a_c = 2(\Omega \times v)$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. If vectors $a$, $b$, and $c$ are coplanar, what is the value of $a \cdot (b \times c)$?

Explanation: If vectors are coplanar, the parallelepiped they form has zero height, hence zero volume, making the scalar triple product zero.

2. Which differential identity represents the irrotational nature of any gradient field $\phi$?

Explanation: The identity $\nabla \times (\nabla \phi) = 0$ confirms that the curl of a gradient is always zero.

3. Evaluate the vector triple product $i \times (i \times j)$, where $i, j, k$ are standard unit vectors.

Explanation: Using BAC-CAB: $i \times (i \times j) = i \times k = -j$. Alternatively, $i \times j = k$, and $i \times k = -j$.

4. What does the Lagrange's identity for cross products express for $(a \times b) \cdot (c \times d)$?

Explanation: Lagrange's identity states that $(a \times b) \cdot (c \times d) = (a \cdot c)(b \cdot d) - (a \cdot d)(b \cdot c)$.

5. If a vector field $A$ satisfies $\nabla \cdot A = 0$, the field is termed:

Explanation: A vector field with zero divergence ($\nabla \cdot A = 0$) is called solenoidal (incompressible).

6. Which of the following operations is meaningless in vector calculus?

Explanation: The cross product operator requires a vector operand; taking the curl of a scalar ($\nabla \times \phi$) is mathematically undefined.

7. What is the Laplacian of a scalar field $\phi$, denoted as $\nabla^2 \phi$, equivalent to?

Explanation: The Laplacian of a scalar field is defined as the divergence of its gradient: $\nabla^2 \phi = \nabla \cdot (\nabla \phi)$.

8. In the expansion of $\nabla \times (\phi A)$, what are $\phi$ and $A$ respectively?

Explanation: In $\phi A$, $\phi$ represents a scalar field and $A$ represents a vector field.

9. If $a$, $b$, and $c$ are mutually orthogonal unit vectors forming a right-handed system, what is $a \cdot (b \times c)$?

Explanation: Since $b \times c = a$, $a \cdot (b \times c) = a \cdot a = 1$ for orthonormal unit vectors.

10. Which physical law or force field in mechanics is best characterized by a non-zero curl in rotating frames?

Explanation: Coriolis acceleration and forces in rotating reference frames explicitly incorporate cross products and rotation vector kinematics.
