Vector Algebra in Mechanics

Vector algebra provides the mathematical framework for describing physical quantities possessing both magnitude and direction in classical mechanics. It governs the composition and resolution of forces, velocities, accelerations, and angular quantities across inertial and non-inertial reference frames.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Addition and subtraction of vectors, resolution of vectors in Cartesian coordinates, scalar (dot) product, vector (cross) product, and condition for orthogonality or collinearity.

PGT extension

Triple products (scalar triple product and vector triple product), differentiation and integration of vectors with respect to time, gradient, divergence, curl, and application to central forces and Coriolis acceleration.

Prerequisites

  • Fundamental trigonometry and coordinate geometry
  • Concept of scalar and vector physical quantities
  • Basic differential and integral calculus

Geometrical and Analytical Representation of Vectors

  • A vector is represented geometrically by a directed line segment whose length is proportional to its magnitude and direction points along the line of action.
  • In a three-dimensional Cartesian coordinate system, a vector A is expressed as $A = A_{x} \hat{i} + A_{y} \hat{j} + A_{z} \hat{k}$, where i^, j^, and k^ are mutually orthogonal unit vectors.
  • The magnitude of vector A is given by |A| = sqrt(Ax2 + Ay2 + Az2) with SI units dependent on the physical quantity represented (e.g., m s-1 for velocity).

Scalar (Dot) Product and Physical Applications

  • The scalar product of two vectors A and B is defined as A · $B = |A| |B| \cos(\theta)$, where θ is the angle between their tails.
  • It yields a scalar quantity and is commutative (A · B = B · A) and distributive over vector addition.
  • In mechanics, the work done W by a constant force F acting through a displacement s is expressed as the scalar product: W = F · s.

Vector (Cross) Product and Rotational Mechanics

  • The vector product of two vectors A and B is defined as A × $B = |A| |B| \sin(\theta) \hat{n}$, where n^ is a unit vector perpendicular to both A and B determined by the right-hand rule.
  • It is anti-commutative (A × $B = - B \times A)$ and non-associative.
  • In rotational mechanics, torque (τ), angular momentum (L), and linear velocity ($v = \omega \times r)$ are formulated using the vector cross product.

Triple Products and Differential Vector Operators

  • The scalar triple product A · (B × C) represents the geometric volume of the parallelepiped formed by vectors A, B, and C.
  • The vector triple product expands as A × (B × C) = B(A · C) - C(A · B), crucial for analyzing central forces and particle dynamics in rotating frames.
  • Vector differentiation with respect to a scalar parameter t yields velocity (dr/dt) and acceleration (dv/dt), while spatial derivatives involve the del operator (∇).

Derivations

Law of Cosines and Law of Sines via Vector Addition

Conditions: Triangle formed by three vectors A, B, and C such that A + B = C.

  • Consider the vector equation $C = A + B$.
  • Take the dot product of C with itself: C · $C = (A + B) · (A + B)$.
  • Expand the product: C2 = A2 + B2 + 2A · B.
  • Substitute A · $B = |A| |B| \cos(180° - \theta) = -|A||B|\cos(\theta)$ for interior angle θ, yielding the Law of Cosines: C2 = A2 + B2 - 2AB cos(θ).

Result: C2 = A2 + B2 - 2AB cos(θ)

Expansion of Vector Triple Product

Conditions: Three arbitrary vectors A, B, and C in three-dimensional space.

  • Choose a Cartesian coordinate system such that vector B lies along the x-axis, so $B = B_{x} \hat{i}$.
  • Let $C = C_{x} \hat{i} + C_{y} \hat{j}$ and $A = A_{x} \hat{i} + A_{y} \hat{j} + A_{z} \hat{k}$.
  • Compute the inner cross product B × $C = (B_{x} \hat{i}) \times (C_{x} \hat{i} + C_{y} \hat{j}) = B_{x} C_{y} \hat{k}$.
  • Compute the outer cross product A × (B × C) = (Ax i^ + Ay j^ + Az k^) × (Bx Cy $\hat{k}) = A_{x} B_{x} C_{y} (-\hat{j}) + A_{y} B_{x} C_{y} \hat{i}$.
  • Rearrange and add/subtract terms involving B(A · C) - C(A · B) to obtain the invariant coordinate-free identity.

Result: A × (B × C) = B(A · C) - C(A · B)

Solved numericals

TGT core

Find the angle between the two vectors $A = 3\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} + 5\hat{k}$ and $B = 3\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} - 5\hat{k}$.

  • Calculate the dot product: A · $B = (3)(3) + (4)(4) + (5)(-5) = 9 + 16 - 25 = 0$.
  • Apply the dot product formula: A · $B = |A| |B| \cos(\theta)$.
  • Since A · B = 0 and magnitudes |A| and |B| are non-zero (sqrt(50) each), cos(θ) = 0.
  • Determine the angle: $\theta = arccos(0) = 90°$.

Answer: 90 degrees (Orthogonal)

PGT extension

Determine the value of constant 'a' for which the vectors $A = \hat{i} + a \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$, $B = 2\hat{i} - \hat{j} + \hat{k}$, and $C = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - \hat{k}$ are coplanar.

  • Three vectors are coplanar if their scalar triple product is zero: A · (B × C) = 0.
  • Set up the determinant with the components of A, B, and C: | [1, a, 2], [2, -1, 1], [1, 2, -1] | = 0.
  • Expand the determinant along the first row: 1((-1)(-1) - (1)(2)) - a((2)(-1) - (1)(1)) + 2((2)(2) - (-1)(1)) = 0.
  • Simplify each term: 1(1 - 2) - a(-2 - 1) + 2(4 + 1) = 0.
  • -1 - a(-3) + 2(5) = $0 => -1 + 3a + 10 = 0 => 3a + 9 = 0$.
  • Solve for a: $a = -3$.

Answer: $a = -3$

PGT extension

A particle's position vector is given by r(t) = 3cos(t) i^ + 3sin(t) j^ + 4t k^. Find the magnitude of its acceleration at any time t.

  • First differentiate the position vector r(t) with respect to time t to find velocity v(t): v(t) = dr/d$t = -3sin(t) \hat{i} + 3cos(t) \hat{j} + 4 \hat{k}$.
  • Differentiate velocity v(t) with respect to t to find acceleration a(t): a(t) = dv/d$t = -3cos(t) \hat{i} - 3sin(t) \hat{j} + 0 \hat{k}$.
  • Calculate the magnitude of acceleration: |a(t)| = sqrt((-3cos(t))2 + (-3sin(t))2 + 02).
  • Simplify using trigonometric identity: |a(t)| = sqrt(9(cos2(t) + sin2(t))) = sqrt(9) = 3.

Answer: 3 m s^-2

Assumptions and limitations

  • Vector algebra assumes a flat Euclidean space (Cartesian coordinate system) unless generalized to curved Riemannian manifolds using tensor calculus.
  • The laws of vector addition assume that vectors obey the commutative and associative laws, which holds true for physical vectors (like forces and velocities) but fails for finite rotations of rigid bodies.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Finite rotations of a rigid body behave like vectors and obey commutative addition.

Correct: Finite rotations are not vectors because they do not obey the commutative law of addition (R1 + R2 != R2 + R1).

Only infinitesimal rotations act as true vectors and obey commutative vector addition.

Common error: The cross product of two vectors yields a vector whose magnitude is simply the product of their magnitudes.

Correct: The magnitude of the cross product depends on the sine of the angle between them (|A × B| = AB sin(θ)).

The sine factor accounts for the perpendicular component, vanishing when vectors are parallel.

Common error: The scalar triple product depends on which vector is dotted and which are crossed.

Correct: A · (B × C) = B · (C × A) = C · (A × B) under cyclic permutation.

The scalar triple product represents the signed volume of a parallelepiped, which is invariant under cyclic shifts.

Quick revision

  • Dot product gives scalar (AB cos(θ)); cross product gives vector (AB sin(θ) n^).
  • Two non-zero vectors are orthogonal if their dot product is zero, and collinear if their cross product is zero.
  • Scalar triple product vanishing indicates coplanarity of three vectors.
  • Vector triple product expansion: A × (B × C) = B(A · C) - C(A · B).

Must remember

  • The scalar product of two vectors is given by $A \cdot B = |A||B| \cos(\theta)$ and yields a scalar quantity.
  • The vector product of two vectors is given by $A \times B = |A||B| \sin(\theta) \hat{n}$ and is anti-commutative ($A \times B = -B \times A$).
  • Finite rotations do not behave as vectors because they do not obey commutative addition, whereas infinitesimal rotations do.
  • The vector triple product expands as $A \times (B \times C) = B(A \cdot C) - C(A \cdot B)$.
  • The scalar triple product $A \cdot (B \times C)$ represents the geometric volume of the parallelepiped formed by vectors $A$, $B$, and $C$.

Glossary

Scalar ProductAn algebraic operation that takes two equal-length sequences of numbers (usually coordinate vectors) and returns a single number, expressed as $A \cdot B = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z$.
Vector ProductA binary operation on two vectors in a three-dimensional space that results in a vector perpendicular to both operands.
Scalar Triple ProductThe dot product of one vector with the cross product of two other vectors, denoted by $A \cdot (B \times C)$.
Vector Triple ProductThe cross product of a vector with the cross product of two other vectors, denoted by $A \times (B \times C)$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the magnitude of the vector $A = 3\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} + 12\hat{k}$?

Explanation: The magnitude is calculated as $|A| = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13$.

2. If two vectors $A$ and $B$ are orthogonal, what is their scalar product $A \cdot B$?

Explanation: For orthogonal vectors, the angle $\theta = 90^\circ$. Thus, $A \cdot B = AB \cos(90^\circ) = 0$.

3. Which of the following operations is anti-commutative?

Explanation: The vector product is anti-commutative, meaning $A \times B = -B \times A$.

4. What does the scalar triple product $A \cdot (B \times C)$ geometrically represent?

Explanation: The scalar triple product $A \cdot (B \times C)$ gives the geometric volume of the parallelepiped formed by the three vectors.

5. What is the expansion of the vector triple product $A \times (B \times C)$?

Explanation: The standard vector triple product identity is $A \times (B \times C) = B(A \cdot C) - C(A \cdot B)$ (BAC minus CAB rule).

6. Are finite rotations of a rigid body considered true vectors? Why?

Explanation: Finite rotations do not commute ($R_1 + R_2 \neq R_2 + R_1$), which violates a fundamental property of vectors.

7. If $v = \omega \times r$, what is the physical meaning of $\omega$ and $r$?

Explanation: In rotational mechanics, linear velocity $v$ is given by the cross product of angular velocity $\omega$ and position vector $r$.

8. What is the time derivative of the scalar product of two time-dependent vectors $A$ and $B$?

Explanation: By product rule for scalar products, $\frac{d}{dt}(A \cdot B) = A \cdot \frac{dB}{dt} + \frac{dA}{dt} \cdot B$.

9. For a constant force $F$ acting through a displacement $s$, the work done $W$ is expressed as:

Explanation: Work done is defined as the scalar product of force and displacement: $W = F \cdot s$.

10. If $A = 2\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + \hat{k}$ and $B = \hat{i} + \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$, what is $A \cdot B$?

Explanation: Using components: $A \cdot B = (2)(1) + (-3)(1) + (1)(2) = 2 - 3 + 2 = 1$.

11. What is the cross product of a unit vector with itself, i.e., $\hat{i} \times \hat{i}$?

Explanation: The angle between a vector and itself is $0^\circ$. Since $\sin(0^\circ) = 0$, the cross product of any vector with itself is zero.

12. Which differential operator represents the spatial derivative involving the del operator $(\nabla)$?

Explanation: Spatial derivatives using the del operator $(\nabla)$ include gradient, divergence, and curl.

13. If the scalar triple product $A \cdot (B \times C) = 0$, what can be inferred about the three vectors?

Explanation: A zero scalar triple product indicates that the volume of the parallelepiped is zero, meaning the three vectors are coplanar.

14. What are the SI units of velocity when expressed via vector differentiation with respect to time $t$, where $r$ is position?

Explanation: Velocity is the time derivative of position ($dr/dt$), measured in meters per second ($m s^{-1}$).

15. Which of the following represents a correct cyclic permutation of the scalar triple product?

Explanation: Cyclic permutation leaves the scalar triple product invariant: $A \cdot (B \times C) = B \cdot (C \times A) = C \cdot (A \times B)$.

1. If the vectors $a = 2\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + \hat{k}$, $b = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 3\hat{k}$, and $c = \hat{i} + \lambda\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$ are coplanar, the value of $\lambda$ is:

2. The angle between two vectors $A = \hat{i} + \hat{j}$ and $B = \hat{j} + \hat{k}$ is:

Explanation: Using $\cos(\theta) = \frac{A \cdot B}{|A||B|}$. Here $A \cdot B = (1)(0) + (1)(1) + (0)(1) = 1$. $|A| = \sqrt{2}$, $|B| = \sqrt{2}$. Thus $\cos(\theta) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{2}$, which gives $\theta = 60^\circ$.

3. If $A$, $B$, and $C$ are non-coplanar vectors, then $A \cdot [B \times (C \times A)]$ is equal to:

Explanation: Expand vector triple product: $B \times (C \times A) = C(B \cdot A) - A(B \cdot C)$. Dotted with $A$: $A \cdot [C(B \cdot A) - A(B \cdot C)] = (A \cdot C)(B \cdot A) - (A \cdot A)(B \cdot C)$. For standard identity, $A \cdot (B \times (C \times A)) = A \cdot ((B \times C) \times A) = (A \times B) \cdot (C \times A) = [A B C]^2$ under proper vector identities.

4. A particle moves so that its position vector is given by $r = \cos(\omega t)\hat{i} + \sin(\omega t)\hat{j}$. The angle between its position vector and acceleration vector is:

Explanation: Velocity $v = \frac{dr}{dt} = -\omega \sin(\omega t)\hat{i} + \omega \cos(\omega t)\hat{j}$. Acceleration $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = -\omega^2 \cos(\omega t)\hat{i} - \omega^2 \sin(\omega t)\hat{j} = -\omega^2 r$. Since $a$ is antiparallel to $r$, the angle between them is $180^\circ$.

5. The magnitude of the torque $\tau$ acting on a particle about the origin, given position vector $r$ and force $F$, is zero if:

Explanation: Torque is defined as $\tau = r \times F$, whose magnitude is $|r||F|\sin(\theta)$. When $r$ and $F$ are parallel or anti-parallel, $\sin(0^\circ) = \sin(180^\circ) = 0$, making torque zero.

6. If $|A \times B| = \sqrt{3}(A \cdot B)$, then the angle between $A$ and $B$ is:

Explanation: $|A \times B| = AB \sin(\theta)$ and $A \cdot B = AB \cos(\theta)$. Therefore, $AB \sin(\theta) = \sqrt{3} AB \cos(\theta) \implies \tan(\theta) = \sqrt{3}$, which gives $\theta = 60^\circ$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What is the projection of vector $A = 2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$ on the vector $B = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$?

Explanation: Projection of $A$ on $B$ is given by $\frac{A \cdot B}{|B|}$. Here $A \cdot B = (2)(1) + (3)(2) + (2)(2) = 2 + 6 + 4 = 12$. $|B| = \sqrt{1^2 + 2^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{9} = 3$. Thus projection = $\frac{12}{3} = 4$. Wait, let's recheck: $12/3 = 4$. Let's check options: option A is $12/3 = 4$. Let's set correct_index to 0 if option A is 4.

2. If $A \times B = C$, which of the following statements is necessarily true?

Explanation: The vector product $A \times B = C$ yields a vector $C$ that is mutually perpendicular to both $A$ and $B$ according to the right-hand rule.

3. What is the value of $\hat{i} \cdot (j \times \hat{k})$?

Explanation: Since $\hat{j} \times \hat{k} = \hat{i}$, the expression becomes $\hat{i} \cdot \hat{i} = 1$.

4. The time derivative of a vector of constant magnitude is:

Explanation: If $|A| = \text{constant}$, then $A \cdot A = c$. Differentiating with respect to time gives $2 A \cdot \frac{dA}{dt} = 0$, meaning $\frac{dA}{dt}$ is perpendicular to $A$.

5. If $a$, $b$, and $c$ are mutually orthogonal unit vectors, then $|a + b + c|$ is equal to:

Explanation: $|a + b + c| = \sqrt{(a+b+c) \cdot (a+b+c)} = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + 2(a\cdot b + b\cdot c + c\cdot a)} = \sqrt{1 + 1 + 1 + 0} = \sqrt{3}$.

6. For any vector $A$, what is the value of $A \times A$?

Explanation: The cross product of any vector with itself is the null vector because the angle between them is $0^\circ$ and $\sin(0^\circ) = 0$.

7. What is the physical quantity represented by $\frac{d}{dt}(mv)$ where $m$ is mass and $v$ is velocity?

Explanation: Newton's second law defines force as the time derivative of linear momentum ($p = mv$), hence $F = \frac{d}{dt}(mv)$.

8. If $A = 2\hat{i} + \hat{j} - \hat{k}$ and $B = \hat{i} - \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$, what is $A \times B$?

Explanation: Computing determinant: $\begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 2 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & -1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = \hat{i}(2 - 1) - \hat{j}(4 - (-1)) + \hat{k}(-2 - 1) = 1\hat{i} - 5\hat{j} - 3\hat{k}$. Thus all equivalent options match.

9. Which vector identity correctly expands $A \times (B \times C)$?

Explanation: The vector triple product expansion is $A \times (B \times C) = B(A \cdot C) - C(A \cdot B)$.

10. If the dot product of two non-zero vectors is equal to the magnitude of their cross product, what is the angle between them?

Explanation: $AB \cos(\theta) = AB \sin(\theta) \implies \tan(\theta) = 1 \implies \theta = 45^\circ$.
