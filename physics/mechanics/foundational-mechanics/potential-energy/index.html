Potential Energy in Mechanics

Potential energy is the mechanical energy stored within a system due to the spatial configuration or relative positions of its interacting bodies under conservative forces. It is defined mathematically through the negative line integral of a conservative force field.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of potential energy, work-energy relation for conservative forces, gravitational potential energy near the Earth's surface (U = mgh), elastic potential energy of a spring (U = &frac{1}{2}kx&sup2;), and conservation of mechanical energy.

PGT extension

General conservative force fields, derivation of force from potential (F = -&nabla;U), central force potentials, gravitational potential due to mass distributions, stability analysis of equilibrium using potential energy curves (d&sup2;U/dx&sup2; &gt; 0), and potential energy in non-inertial frames.

Prerequisites

  • Work, power, and kinetic energy theorem
  • Line integrals and vector calculus basics
  • Newton's laws of motion and conservative forces

Definition and Conservative Force Field

  • Potential energy U is defined only for conservative forces where work done is independent of the path taken.
  • The change in potential energy ΔU between two points is equal to the negative of the work done by the conservative force: ΔU = -W = -∫AB F · dr.
  • The dimension of potential energy is [ML²T&sup-;²] and its SI unit is the joule (J).

Relation Between Force and Potential Energy

  • In one dimension, the conservative force is related to potential energy by F(x) = -&frac{dU}{dx}.
  • In three dimensions, force is the negative gradient of the potential energy scalar field: F = -∇U = -(&frac{partial U}{partial x}i + rac{partial U}{partial y}j + rac{partial U}{partial z}k).
  • The negative sign indicates that the force always acts in the direction of decreasing potential energy.

Mechanical Energy Conservation

  • The total mechanical energy E = K + U of a system remains constant if only conservative internal forces do work.
  • Any increase in kinetic energy corresponds to an equal decrease in potential energy and vice versa.
  • Non-conservative forces like friction dissipate mechanical energy into thermal energy, violating simple mechanical energy conservation.

Equilibrium and Potential Energy Curves

  • Stable equilibrium occurs at local minima of potential energy, where dU/dx = 0 and d²U/dx² > 0.
  • Unstable equilibrium occurs at local maxima of potential energy, where dU/dx = 0 and d²U/dx² < 0.
  • Neutral equilibrium occurs where dU/dx = 0 and d²U/dx² = 0 over a finite displacement region.

Derivations

Gravitational Potential Energy Near Earth's Surface

Conditions: Uniform gravitational field (g is constant), vertical displacement y.

  • Write the gravitational force acting on a mass m pointing downward: F = -mg j.
  • Express the change in potential energy using the line integral: ΔU = -∫y1y2 Fy dy.
  • Substitute Fy = -mg: ΔU = -∫y1y2 (-mg) dy = mg[y2 - y1].
  • Setting U = 0 at reference level y1 = 0, the potential energy at height h is U(h) = mgh.

Result: U = mgh

Elastic Potential Energy of a Linear Spring

Conditions: Spring obeys Hooke's Law, displacement x from natural length.

  • Write the restoring force exerted by the spring: Fs = -kx, where k is the spring constant.
  • Use the work-potential energy relation: dU = -Fs dx = -(-kx) dx = kx dx.
  • Integrate from equilibrium position (x = 0) to extension x: U(x) = ∫0x kx' dx'.
  • Perform the integration to obtain the stored elastic potential energy.

Result: U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2

Graphs and experiments

Potential Energy Well and Phase Interpretation

Draw: A curve of U(x) versus x showing a smooth bowl-like minimum, with horizontal total energy line E intersecting the curve.

Interpret: The region where E &gt; U represents allowed motion where kinetic energy is positive. The turning points occur where E = U. The minimum represents stable equilibrium.

Exam use: To determine turning points of motion, particle velocity at various positions, and stability analysis.

Force Determination from U-x Graph

Draw: An arbitrary non-linear potential energy curve U(x) with tangent lines drawn at various points.

Interpret: The force at any point is the negative slope (-dU/dx) of the tangent at that point. Steeper slopes indicate larger magnitudes of force.

Exam use: Direct qualitative and quantitative questions asking for the direction and relative magnitude of force from a given U-x graph.

Verification of Hooke's Law and Elastic PE

Draw: A graph of restoring force vs displacement (straight line passing through origin) and work done (area under the curve representing U).

Interpret: The area enclosed under the F-x graph up to extension x equals the potential energy stored in the spring (&frac{1}{2}kx&sup2;).

Exam use: TGT/PGT conceptual questions linking graphical integration to stored energy.

Solved numericals

TGT core

A body of mass 2 kg is lifted vertically upwards through a height of 5 m. Calculate the change in its gravitational potential energy. (Take g = 9.8 m/s&sup2;)

  • Identify given values: m = 2 kg, h = 5 m, g = 9.8 m/s².
  • Apply the formula for gravitational potential energy: ΔU = mgh.
  • Substitute the values: ΔU = 2 × 9.8 × 5 = 98 J.

Answer: 98 J

PGT extension

The potential energy of a particle of mass 1 kg moving in one dimension is given by U(x) = 8x&sup2; - 4x&sup3; Joules. Determine the position of stable equilibrium and the angular frequency of small oscillations about this equilibrium point.

  • Find the first derivative of U(x) with respect to x: dU/dx = 16x - 12x².
  • Set dU/dx = 0 for equilibrium: 4x(4 - 3x) = 0, giving x = 0 and x = 4/3 m.
  • Find the second derivative: d²U/dx² = 16 - 24x.
  • Test stability at x = 0: d²U/dx² = 16 > 0 (Stable equilibrium).
  • Test stability at x = 4/3: d²U/dx² = 16 - 24(4/3) = 16 - 32 = -16 < 0 (Unstable equilibrium).
  • Calculate angular frequency at stable equilibrium (x = 0): ω = √(k/m) where k = d²U/dx² = 16 N/m. Thus ω = √(16 / 1) = 4 rad/s.

Answer: Stable equilibrium at x = 0; Angular frequency ω = 4 rad/s

PGT extension

A conservative force field is given by F = (2xy + z&sup3;) i + x&sup2; j + 3xz&sup2; k. Find the potential energy function U(x, y, z), assuming U = 0 at the origin (0, 0, 0).

  • Use the relation F = -∇U, which gives &partial;U/&partial;x = -(2xy + z³), &partial;U/&partial;y = -x², and &partial;U/&partial;z = -3xz².
  • Integrate &partial;U/&partial;x with respect to x: U(x,y,z) = -∫ (2xy + z³) dx = -x²y - xz³ + f(y, z).
  • Differentiate this intermediate U with respect to y and equate to -&partial;U/&partial;y (-x²): -x² + &partial;f/&partial;y = -x² &implies; &partial;f/&partial;y = 0, so f depends only on z: f(y,z) = g(z).
  • Differentiate U = -x²y - xz³ + g(z) with respect to z and equate to -3xz²: -3xz² + g'(z) = -3xz² &implies; g'(z) = 0 &implies; g(z) = C (constant).
  • Using the boundary condition U(0,0,0) = 0, find that C = 0.

Answer: U(x, y, z) = -x²y - xz³ J

Assumptions and limitations

  • Potential energy is strictly defined only for conservative forces; dissipative forces like friction cannot be represented by a potential energy function.
  • The absolute value of potential energy is arbitrary and depends on the choice of the reference zero level; only potential energy differences have physical significance.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Potential energy is an intrinsic property of a single isolated object.

Correct: Potential energy is a property of a system of two or more interacting bodies interacting via a conservative force.

Gravitational or elastic potential energy depends on the relative separation between the object and the Earth or spring respectively.

Common error: A negative potential energy means the particle is bound and cannot move.

Correct: Negative potential energy simply reflects the choice of setting U = 0 at infinity; particles with negative total mechanical energy are bound, but they can still move between turning points.

Zero potential energy is a matter of convention; kinetic energy can still be positive.

  • Potential energy is defined via change in energy as ΔU = -Wconservative.
  • Force is the negative spatial derivative of potential energy: F = -dU/dx.
  • Stable equilibrium corresponds to a local minimum of potential energy (dU/dx = 0, d²U/dx² > 0).

Must remember

  • Potential energy $U$ is defined exclusively for conservative forces, where the change in potential energy is $\Delta U = -W = -\int_{A}^{B} F \cdot dr$.
  • In one dimension, conservative force is related to potential energy by $F(x) = -\frac{dU}{dx}$.
  • In three dimensions, force is the negative gradient of the potential energy scalar field: $F = -\nabla U$.
  • Stable equilibrium occurs at local minima of potential energy where $\frac{dU}{dx} = 0$ and $\frac{d^2U}{dx^2} > 0$.
  • Unstable equilibrium occurs at local maxima of potential energy where $\frac{dU}{dx} = 0$ and $\frac{d^2U}{dx^2} < 0$.
  • Neutral equilibrium occurs where $\frac{dU}{dx} = 0$ and $\frac{d^2U}{dx^2} = 0$ over a finite region.
  • The total mechanical energy $E = K + U$ remains constant if only conservative internal forces perform work.
  • Gravitational potential energy near the Earth's surface is given by $U = mgh$, while the universal form is $U(r) = -\frac{GMm}{r}$.
  • Elastic potential energy stored in a stretched or compressed spring is $U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$ within the elastic limit.
  • Potential energy is a property of an interacting system of bodies, not of a single isolated object.

Glossary

Conservative ForceA force for which work done in moving between two points is independent of the path taken and depends only on the endpoints.
Potential Energy ($U$)The energy possessed by a system due to the relative positions of its constituent parts within a conservative force field.
Gradient Operator ($\nabla$)A vector differential operator used in three dimensions to find the spatial rate of change and direction of maximum increase of a scalar field.
Stable EquilibriumA state of a system where a slight displacement creates a restoring force pointing back toward the initial equilibrium position.
Unstable EquilibriumA state of a system where a slight displacement creates a force that pushes the system further away from the equilibrium position.
Neutral EquilibriumA state of a system where a slight displacement results in no net force, and the system remains in equilibrium at its new position.
Mechanical Energy ($E$)The sum of the kinetic energy and potential energy of a mechanical system: $E = K + U$.
Spring Constant ($k$)A measure of the stiffness of a spring, representing the restoring force per unit displacement according to Hooke's law.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following conditions must be satisfied for a force field to allow the definition of a potential energy function?

Explanation: Potential energy $U$ is defined exclusively for conservative forces where the work done is independent of the path taken between two points.

2. What is the relationship between a one-dimensional conservative force $F(x)$ and its potential energy function $U(x)$?

Explanation: In one dimension, the conservative force is related to potential energy by the negative spatial derivative: $F(x) = -\frac{dU}{dx}$.

3. In three-dimensional space, how is a conservative force vector $F$ expressed in terms of the potential energy scalar field $U$?

Explanation: In three dimensions, force is the negative gradient of the potential energy scalar field: $F = -\nabla U = -(\frac{\partial U}{\partial x}i + \frac{\partial U}{\partial y}j + \frac{\partial U}{\partial z}k)$.

4. What are the dimensional formula and SI unit of potential energy?

Explanation: Potential energy has the same dimension as work and energy, which is $[ML^2T^{-2}]$, and its SI unit is the joule (J).

5. A particle is in stable equilibrium at a position $x_0$ if the potential energy $U(x)$ satisfies which of the following?

Explanation: Stable equilibrium occurs at local minima of the potential energy curve, where the first derivative is zero and the second derivative is strictly positive ($\frac{d^2U}{dx^2} > 0$).

6. Under what condition does the total mechanical energy $E = K + U$ of a system remain conserved?

Explanation: The total mechanical energy remains constant if and only if only conservative internal forces do work within the system.

7. What is the elastic potential energy stored in a spring of stiffness constant $k$ compressed by a distance $x$ within its elastic limit?

Explanation: The elastic potential energy stored in an ideal spring obeying Hooke's law is given by $U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$.

8. What does a negative sign in the relation $F = -\nabla U$ physically indicate?

Explanation: The negative sign signifies that the conservative force always acts in the direction pointing toward decreasing potential energy.

9. What is the universal gravitational potential energy between two masses $M$ and $m$ separated by distance $r$, taking $U=0$ at $r = \infty$?

Explanation: Universal gravitational potential energy is given by $U(r) = -\frac{GMm}{r}$, adopting the standard convention that $U=0$ at infinity.

10. Which of the following statements correctly distinguishes potential energy from kinetic energy?

Explanation: Potential energy is a property of a system of interacting bodies via conservative forces, whereas kinetic energy depends on an object's speed relative to a reference frame.

11. At a point of unstable equilibrium in a potential energy curve, what are the properties of the first and second derivatives?

Explanation: Unstable equilibrium occurs at local maxima of the potential energy function where $\frac{dU}{dx} = 0$ and $\frac{d^2U}{dx^2} < 0$.

12. If a potential energy function is given by $U(x) = ax^3 - bx^2$, what is the corresponding force $F(x)$?

Explanation: Using $F(x) = -\frac{dU}{dx}$, we get $F(x) = -\frac{d}{dx}(ax^3 - bx^2) = -(3ax^2 - 2bx) = -3ax^2 + 2bx$.

13. What happens to the total mechanical energy of a system when non-conservative forces like friction perform work?

Explanation: Non-conservative forces such as friction dissipate mechanical energy into thermal energy, meaning mechanical energy is not conserved.

14. What condition characterizes neutral equilibrium in a mechanical system?

Explanation: Neutral equilibrium occurs where both the first derivative and second derivative of potential energy are zero ($\frac{dU}{dx} = 0$ and $\frac{d^2U}{dx^2} = 0$) over a finite region of displacement.

15. How is the change in potential energy $\Delta U$ between points A and B mathematically related to the conservative work $W$?

Explanation: The change in potential energy is defined as the negative of the work done by the conservative force: $\Delta U = -W = -\int_{A}^{B} F \cdot dr$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The potential energy of a particle moving along the x-axis is given by $U(x) = \frac{a}{x^2} - \frac{b}{x}$, where $a$ and $خاذ$ are positive constants. What is the position of stable equilibrium?

Explanation: For equilibrium, $\frac{dU}{dx} = -\frac{2a}{x^3} + \frac{b}{x^2} = 0 \implies \frac{2a}{x^3} = \frac{b}{x^2} \implies x = \frac{2a}{b}$. Testing the second derivative yields $\frac{d^2U}{dx^2} > 0$ at this point, confirming stable equilibrium.

2. A force $F = (2xy + z^3)i + x^2 j + (3xz^2)k$ acts on a particle. What is the potential energy function $U(x, y, z)$ associated with this conservative force, assuming $U(0,0,0) = 0$?

Explanation: Using $F = -\nabla U$, we have $-\frac{\partial U}{\partial x} = 2xy + z^3 \implies U = -x^2 y - xz^3 + C$. Integrating with respect to $y$ and $z$ confirms consistency, giving $U = -x^2 y - xz^3$.

3. A particle of mass $m$ is released from rest at height $h$ in a uniform gravitational field. What is its speed just before hitting the ground using mechanical energy conservation?

Explanation: By conservation of mechanical energy, initial potential energy $mgh$ equals final kinetic energy $\frac{1}{2}mv^2$. Thus, $mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \implies v = \sqrt{2gh}$.

4. If the potential energy between two atoms in a diatomic molecule is given by $U(x) = \frac{A}{x^{12}} - \frac{B}{x^6}$, what is the equilibrium separation between the atoms?

Explanation: Setting $\frac{dU}{dx} = -\frac{12A}{x^{13}} + \frac{6B}{x^7} = 0 \implies \frac{12A}{x^{13}} = \frac{6B}{x^7} \implies x^6 = \frac{2A}{B} \implies x_0 = \left(\frac{2A}{B}\right)^{1/6}$.

5. A block of mass $m$ is attached to a vertical spring of constant $k$. When gently lowered until it hangs in equilibrium, the extension of the spring is $y_0$. What is the elastic potential energy stored in the spring at this equilibrium position?

Explanation: At equilibrium, $ky_0 = mg \implies y_0 = \frac{mg}{k}$. The elastic potential energy is $U = \frac{1}{2}ky_0^2 = \frac{1}{2}k\left(\frac{mg}{k}\right)^2 = \frac{m^2 g^2}{2k}$.

6. A conservative force acts on a particle in one dimension such that $F = -kx + bx^3$, where $k$ and $b$ are positive constants. What is the potential energy function $U(x)$ if $U(0) = 0$?

Explanation: Since $F = -\frac{dU}{dx}$, we have $U(x) = -\int (-kx + bx^3)dx = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 - \frac{1}{4}bx^4$ (taking integration constant as zero for $U(0)=0$).

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following force fields is non-conservative, prohibiting the definition of a scalar potential energy function?

Explanation: Friction is a non-conservative dissipative force whose work depends on the path taken; thus, no potential energy function can be defined for it.

2. The potential energy of a body of mass $m$ is given by $U = ax + by$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants. What is the magnitude of the force acting on the body?

Explanation: The force components are $F_x = -\frac{\partial U}{\partial x} = -a$ and $F_y = -\frac{\partial U}{\partial y} = -b$. The magnitude is $|F| = \sqrt{(-a)^2 + (-b)^2} = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$.

3. If a particle oscillates about a stable equilibrium point with small amplitude, what determines the angular frequency $\$\omega$$ of oscillation?

Explanation: For small oscillations around a minimum of potential energy, the effective spring constant is $k = \left.\frac{d^2U}{dx^2}\right|_{x=x_0}$, giving angular frequency $\$\omega$ = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{d^2U/dx^2}{m}}$.

4. What is the work done by a conservative force in moving a particle around any closed loop?

Explanation: By definition, the work done by a conservative force around any closed path is identically zero: $\oint F \cdot dr = 0$.

5. A particle moves in a potential energy field $U(x) = E_0 \left(\frac{x}{a} - \frac{a}{x}\right)$. At what position is the potential energy zero?

Explanation: Setting $U(x) = E_0 \left(\frac{x}{a} - \frac{a}{x}\right) = 0$ gives $\frac{x}{a} = \frac{a}{x} \implies x^2 = a^2 \implies x = a$ (for positive physical position).

6. Which misconception about potential energy is corrected by proper physical formulation?

Explanation: Potential energy is fundamentally a property of an interacting system of two or more bodies (such as mass-Earth or charges), not of a single isolated object.

8. What is the physical significance of negative potential energy values in gravitational or electrostatic systems?

Explanation: Negative potential energy simply arises from setting $U=0$ at infinite separation; particles with negative total mechanical energy are bound within turning points but can still possess positive kinetic energy.

9. A spring with spring constant $k$ is stretched by $x_1$ and then further stretched to $x_2$. What is the work done by the external agent against the spring force during this process?

Explanation: The work done by an external agent equals the change in elastic potential energy: $W_{\text{ext}} = U_2 - U_1 = \frac{1}{2}kx_2^2 - \frac{1}{2}kx_1^2 = \frac{1}{2}k(x_2^2 - x_1^2)$.

10. In a conservative force field, if a particle is released from rest at a point where potential energy is at a maximum (unstable equilibrium), what happens initially?

Explanation: At a local maximum (unstable equilibrium), any slight displacement results in a force directed away from the maximum (since $F = -\frac{dU}{dx}$ points toward decreasing $U$), causing the particle to accelerate away.
