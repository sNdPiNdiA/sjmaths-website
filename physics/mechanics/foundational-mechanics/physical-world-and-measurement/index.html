Physical World and Measurement in Mechanics

Physical world and measurement form the empirical foundation of mechanics, establishing standardized units, dimensions, and error analysis techniques for physical quantities. Measurement involves comparing an unknown physical quantity with a standard reference unit, quantifying experimental uncertainties essential for rigorous physical validation.

Prerequisites

  • Basic trigonometry and logarithms
  • Exponential and differential notation

Fundamental and Derived Quantities

  • Physical quantities are categorized into fundamental (independent) and derived (combinations of fundamental) quantities.
  • The SI system defines seven base units: meter (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), ampere (A), kelvin (K), mole (mol), and candela (cd), along with two supplementary units (radian and steradian).
  • Derived quantities such as force, energy, and momentum are expressed as products or ratios of powers of fundamental quantities.

Dimensions and Dimensional Analysis

  • The dimension of a physical quantity denotes the powers to which fundamental quantities must be raised to represent that quantity.
  • The Principle of Homogeneity states that only quantities with identical dimensions can be added, subtracted, or equated.
  • Dimensional analysis is used to check the correctness of physical equations and to derive relations between physical quantities up to a dimensionless constant.

Errors in Measurement

  • Measurements are inherently subject to uncertainties, categorized into systematic errors (instrumental, environmental) and random errors (unpredictable fluctuations).
  • Absolute error is the magnitude of the difference between the measured value and the true value; relative error is the ratio of absolute error to the true value.
  • Percentage error is relative error expressed as a percentage, critical for assessing experimental precision.

Significant Figures and Rounding

  • Significant figures represent all the reliably known digits in a measurement plus the first uncertain estimated digit.
  • In multiplication and division, the final result retains as many significant figures as the measurement with the fewest significant figures.
  • In addition and subtraction, the final result retains as many decimal places as the quantity with the fewest decimal places.

Derivations

Propagation of Errors in Multiplication and Division

Conditions: Let $Z = X \times Y or Z = X / Y$, where ΔX and ΔY are absolute errors in X and Y respectively.

  • Take the natural logarithm of both sides: ln $Z = \ln X + \ln Y (for multiplication) or \ln Z = \ln X - \ln Y (for division)$.
  • Differentiate implicitly to find fractional changes: dZ / $Z = dX / X + dY / Y$.
  • Convert differentials to maximum fractional errors: (ΔZ) / $Z = (\DeltaX) / X + (\DeltaY) / Y$.

Result: The maximum fractional error in a product or quotient is the sum of the fractional errors of its individual factors.

Derivation of Time Period of a Simple Pendulum using Dimensional Analysis

Conditions: Assume the time period T depends on mass of the bob (m), length of the string (l), and acceleration due to gravity (g).

  • Write the functional dependence as $T = k \cdot m^{a} \cdot l^{b} \cdot g^{c}$, where k is a dimensionless constant.
  • Substitute dimensional formulas: [T1] = [M]a · [L]b · ([L][T]-2)c = [M]a · [L]b+c · [T]-2c.
  • Equate powers of M, L, and T on both sides: a = 0, b + c = 0, -2c = 1, yielding $c = -1/2$, $b = 1/2$, a = 0.

Result: $T = k \cdot \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$

Solved numericals

TGT core

If the error in measuring the radius of a sphere is 2%, what is the maximum percentage error in calculating its volume?

  • Formula for volume of a sphere: $V = (4/3) \pi r^3$.
  • Express fractional error using power rule: (Delta V)/$V = 3(\delta r)/r$.
  • Multiply by 100 to get percentage error: % Error in V = 3 \times (% Error in r) = 3 \times 2% = 6%.

Answer: 6%

PGT extension

The velocity v of a wave depends on the tension T in the string and mass per unit length m. Use dimensional analysis to establish the formula for wave velocity.

  • Assume $v = k \cdot T^x \cdot m^y$, where [v] = LT^{-1}, [T] = MLT^{-2}, and [m] = ML^{-1}.
  • Substitute dimensions: [LT^{-1}] = (MLT^{-2})^x (ML^{-1})^y = M^{x+y} L^{x-y} T^{-2x}$.
  • Equate indices: x + y = 0, x - y = 1, -2$x = -1$. Solving gives $x = 1/2$, $y = -1/2$.

Answer: $v = k \sqrt{T/m}$

PGT extension

A physical quantity P is related to four observables a, b, c, and d as $P = a^3 b^2 / (\sqrt{c} d)$. The percentage errors of measurement in a, b, c, and d are 1%, 2%, 4%, and 2% respectively. What is the percentage error in P?

  • Write the maximum fractional error expression: (Delta P)/$P = 3(\delta a)/a + 2(\delta b)/b + (1/2)(\delta c)/c + (\delta d)/d$.
  • Substitute the given percentage errors: % Error in $P = 3(1%) + 2(2%) + (0.5)(4%) + 1(2%)$.
  • Calculate the sum: 3% + 4% + 2% + 2% = 11%.

Answer: 11%

Assumptions and limitations

  • Dimensional analysis cannot determine dimensionless constants (such as 2pi or 1/2) appearing in physical equations.
  • Dimensional analysis fails if a physical quantity depends on the sum of two or more terms with the same dimensions.
  • Equations containing exponential, logarithmic, or trigonometric functions cannot be fully derived using pure dimensional methods.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Quantities with the same dimensions can always be added or equated regardless of their physical nature.

Correct: Only dimensionally identical quantities representing the same physical property can be added or subtracted.

Dimensional homogeneity is necessary, but physical identity is also required for meaningful algebraic addition.

Common error: Adding measurements always reduces the overall relative error.

Correct: Absolute errors always add up in worst-case maximum error estimation for addition and subtraction.

Uncertainties accumulate and propagate forward, never cancelling out in worst-case error bounds.

Common error: A dimensionally correct equation is always physically true.

Correct: A dimensionally correct equation may still be incorrect if it misses dimensionless factors or terms.

Dimensional analysis is a necessary test for consistency, not a sufficient proof of complete physical validity.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the dimensional formula of the universal gravitational constant $G$?

Explanation: From Newton's law of gravitation $F = \frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}$, we get $G = \frac{Fr^2}{m_1m_2}$. Substituting dimensions, $[G] = \frac{[MLT^{-2}][L^2]}{[M^2]} = M^{-1}L^3T^{-2}$.

2. Which of the following sets of quantities has the same dimensional formula?

Explanation: Work is Force $\times$ Displacement ($[ML^2T^{-2}]$) and Torque is Force $\times$ Distance ($[ML^2T^{-2}]$). Both share identical dimensions.

3. The number of significant figures in $0.00450$ is:

Explanation: Leading zeros are not significant. Trailing zeros after a decimal point are significant. Thus, $4, 5,$ and the final $0$ give 3 significant figures.

4. According to the principle of homogeneity, which operation is valid?

Explanation: Kinetic energy and potential energy both have the dimension $[ML^2T^{-2}]$, so they can be added. Other options involve dimensionally distinct quantities.

5. If the error in measuring the radius of a sphere is $2\%$, what is the percentage error in calculating its volume?

Explanation: Volume of a sphere $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$. The relative error in volume is $\frac{\Delta V}{V} = 3 \frac{\Delta r}{r}$. With $\frac{\Delta r}{r} = 2\%$, the volume error is $3 \times 2\% = 6\%ویر$

6. Which of the following is a supplementary unit in the SI system?

Explanation: Radian and steradian are the two supplementary units defined in the SI system for plane and solid angles respectively.

7. The dimensional formula of the coefficient of viscosity $\eta$ is:

Explanation: From Stokes' law or viscous force formula $F = 6\pi \eta r v$, we get $[\eta] = \frac{[F]}{[r][v]} = \frac{MLT^{-2}}{(L)(LT^{-1})} = ML^{-1}T^{-1}$.

8. When rounding the number $3.745$ to 3 significant figures, the result is:

Explanation: When the digit following the last retained place is 5 followed by zeros, if the preceding digit is even (like 4 in $3.74$), it is left unchanged.

9. Systematic errors can be minimized by:

Explanation: Systematic errors stem from predictable flaws like zero error or calibration issues, which are resolved by instrument calibration, whereas random errors are reduced by averaging.

10. What is the result of $12.4 + 2.15$ with due regard to significant figures/decimal places?

Explanation: In addition, the result must be reported to the same number of decimal places as the measurement with the fewest decimal places (12.4 has 1 decimal place). $12.4 + 2.15 = 14.55$, rounded to 1 decimal place gives $14.6$.

11. A dimensionally correct equation:

Explanation: Dimensional analysis only tests consistency. An equation like $s = ut + \frac{1}{3}at^2$ is dimensionally correct but physically wrong due to the incorrect numerical factor.

12. Which physical quantity has the same dimensions as Planck's constant $h$?

Explanation: Since $E = h\nu$, the dimensions of $h$ are $[E]/[\nu] = (ML^2T^{-2})/T^{-1} = ML^2T^{-1}$, which matches the dimensions of angular momentum ($mvr$).y

13. If $Z = \frac{A^2 B^3}{C^\frac{1}{2}}$, the maximum fractional error in $Z$ is given by:

Explanation: Errors in powers combine additively in worst-case analysis: $\frac{\Delta Z}{Z} = |n_1|\frac{\Delta A}{A} + |n_2|\frac{\Delta B}{B} + |n_3|\frac{\Delta C}{C}$.

14. The dimensional formula $[ML^{-1}T^{-2}]$ represents which of the following quantities?

Explanation: Pressure = Force / Area = $\frac{MLT^{-2}}{L^2} = ML^{-1}T^{-2}$. Stress shares the same definition and dimension.

15. What is the number of base quantities in the SI system?

Explanation: There are seven fundamental (base) quantities in the SI system: length, mass, time, electric current, thermodynamic temperature, amount of substance, and luminous intensity.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. If force ($F$), velocity ($v$), and time ($T$) are chosen as fundamental physical quantities, find the dimensional formula for mass.

Explanation: Let Mass $M \propto F^x v^y T^z$. Substituting dimensions: $M = (MLT^{-2})^x (LT^{-1})^y (T)^z$. Equating powers of $M$: $x=1$. For $L$: $x+y=0 \implies y=-1$. For $T$: $-2x - y + z = 0 \implies -2(1) - (-1) + z = 0 \implies z=1$. Thus, $[M] = F v^{-1} T$.

2. The velocity of a particle is given by $v = At^2 + Bt + C$, where $t$ is time. What are the dimensions of constant $B$?

Explanation: By the principle of homogeneity, each term on the right must have the same dimension as velocity $v$ ($[LT^{-1}]$). Therefore, $[Bt] = [LT^{-1}] \implies [B] = \frac{[LT^{-1}]}{[T]} = LT^{-2}$.

3. In an experiment, the percentage errors incurred in measuring mass and speed are $2\%$ and $3\%$ respectively. What is the maximum percentage error in kinetic energy?

Explanation: Kinetic energy $K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$. The maximum fractional error is $\frac{\Delta K}{K} = \frac{\Delta m}{m} + 2\frac{\Delta v}{v} = 2\% + 2(3\%) = 8\%$.

4. Which of the following pairs has different dimensions?

Explanation: Dipole moment ($q \times l$) has dimensions $[LTA]$, while electric/magnetic flux has different dimensions ($[ML^2T^{-2}A^{-1}]$). Pressure and Young's modulus are both $[ML^{-1}T^{-2}]$; Emf and Potential difference are both $[ML^2T^{-3}A^{-1}]$; Heat and Work are energy ($[ML^2T^{-2}]$).

5. State the number of significant figures in $6.023 \times 10^{23}$:

Explanation: The exponential term ($10^{23}$) does not contribute to significant figures. The numerical coefficient $6.023$ contains 4 significant figures.

6. If energy ($E$), velocity ($V$), and time ($T$) are chosen as fundamental quantities, the dimensional formula for surface tension is:

Explanation: Surface tension $S = \text{Force}/\text{Length} = \text{Energy}/\text{Area} = E L^{-2}$. Since $L = VT$, substitute to get $[S] = E (VT)^{-2} = E V^{-2} T^{-2}$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. The unit of surface tension in SI base units is:

Explanation: Surface tension $S = \frac{F}{l}$. Units are $\frac{\text{N}}{\text{m}} = \frac{\text{kg } \text{m } \text{s}^{-2}}{\text{m}} = \text{kg } \text{s}^{-2}$.

2. Dimensional analysis cannot be used to:

Explanation: Dimensional analysis is blind to dimensionless constants (like $\frac{1}{2}$ or $2\pi$), hence it cannot determine them.

3. If the length of a simple pendulum increases by $2\%$, the percentage change in its time period is approximately:

Explanation: Time period $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$. Thus $\frac{\Delta T}{T} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{\Delta L}{L} = \frac{1}{2}(2\%) = 1\%ویر$.

4. Which physical quantity has dimensions $[ML^2T^{-2}Q^{-1}]$ where $Q$ represents charge?

Explanation: Electric potential $V = \frac{\text{Work}}{\text{Charge}} = \frac{ML^2T^{-2}}{Q} = ML^2T^{-2}Q^{-1}$.

5. Subtracting $2.5 \times 10^3$ from $4.2 \times 10^4$ gives the correct result in proper significant notation as:

Explanation: Aligning powers of 10: $42.0 \times 10^3 - 2.5 \times 10^3 = 39.5 \times 10^3 = 3.95 \times 10^4$. Rounding to the correct decimal position (tenths place corresponding to hundreds in unscaled numbers) gives $3.9 \times 10^4$.

6. The dimensional formula for gravitational potential is:

Explanation: Gravitational potential is work done per unit mass: $\frac{W}{m} = \frac{ML^2T^{-2}}{M} = M^0L^2T^{-2}$.

7. Random errors can be evaluated and minimized by:

Explanation: Random errors fluctuate randomly; taking an arithmetic mean over numerous observations averages out positive and negative deviations.

8. The dimensional representation of universal gas constant $R$ is:

Explanation: From ideal gas law $PV = nRT$, $R = \frac{PV}{nT}$. Pressure $\times$ Volume = Energy ($ML^2T^{-2}$), so $[R] = \frac{ML^2T^{-2}}{(\text{mol})(\text{K})} = ML^2T^{-2}K^{-1}mol^{-1}$.

9. In an experiment, four quantities $a, b, c$ and $d$ are measured with percentage errors $1\%, 2\%, 3\%$ and $4\%$ respectively. Quantity $P$ is calculated as $P = \frac{a^3 b^2}{cd}$. The percentage error in $P$ is:

Explanation: $\frac{\Delta P}{P} = 3\frac{\Delta a}{a} + 2\frac{\Delta b}{b} + \frac{\Delta c}{c} + \frac{\Delta d}{d} = 3(1\%) + 2(2\%) + 3\% + 4\% = 3 + 4 + 3 + 4 = 14\%$.

10. Which of the following units is NOT a base unit in SI?

Explanation: Coulomb is a derived unit of electric charge (Ampere $\times$ second). Ampere, Kelvin, and Mole are fundamental SI base units.
