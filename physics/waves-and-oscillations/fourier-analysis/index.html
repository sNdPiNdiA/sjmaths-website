Fourier Analysis of Periodic Waves

Fourier analysis is a mathematical framework that decomposes any arbitrary periodic waveform into a convergent infinite sum of harmonically related sines and cosines. It forms the foundation for understanding complex vibrations in continuous media, acoustics, and signal processing.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Calculation of average values, basic trigonometric Fourier coefficients for simple waveforms like square and triangular waves, orthogonality of trigonometric functions, and fundamental harmonic identification.

PGT extension

Complex exponential form of Fourier series, Dirichlet's conditions for convergence, Parseval's identity for power spectrum analysis, and Fourier transforms for non-periodic continuous waves.

Prerequisites

  • Orthogonality relations of sine and cosine functions over a complete period
  • Definite integration techniques involving trigonometric identities
  • Differential equations governing harmonic oscillators

Dirichlet's Conditions for Convergence

  • A periodic function f(t) with period T can be represented by a Fourier series if it satisfies specific mathematical constraints.
  • The function must be single-valued, continuous, or have a finite number of finite discontinuities within any one period.
  • The function must have a finite number of maxima and minima within the period T and be absolutely integrable.

Trigonometric Fourier Series Expansion

  • Any periodic function f(t) of period $T = 2\pi/\omega can be expressed$ as an infinite series containing a constant term, fundamental frequencies, and integer multiples (harmonics).
  • The presence of even or odd symmetries in f(t) simplifies the series by systematically eliminating sine or cosine terms.
  • The constant term a0 represents the direct current (DC) or average value of the waveform over one complete period.

Harmonic Content and Waveform Symmetry

  • Even symmetry, defined by f(-t) = f(t), yields only cosine terms and a0, meaning all bn coefficients are zero.
  • Odd symmetry, defined by f(-t) = -f(t), yields only sine terms, meaning all an and a0 coefficients are zero.
  • Half-wave symmetry, defined by f(t ± T/2) = -f(t), ensures that the Fourier expansion contains only odd harmonics.

Parseval's Theorem for Power Spectra

  • Parseval's theorem relates the total average power of a periodic signal to the sum of the powers contained in its individual harmonic components.
  • It states that the mean square value of a periodic function over a period equals the sum of the squares of the amplitudes of its Fourier components.
  • This theorem is essential for analyzing energy distribution across different frequency channels in oscillating systems.

Derivations

Derivation of Fourier Coefficients

Conditions: Function f(t) is periodic with period $T = 2\pi/\omega$ and satisfies Dirichlet's conditions.

  • Write the general trigonometric Fourier series: f(t) = \frac{a_0}{2} + su$m_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( a_n \cos(nomega t) + b_n \sin(nomega t) \right)$.
  • Integrate both sides of the equation from -T/2 to T/2 over one complete period to isolate a0, utilizing the orthogonality of sines and cosines.
  • To find an, multiply both sides by cos(momega t) and integrate over one period, using orthogonality relations int_{-T/2}^{T/2} cos(nomega t)cos(momega t) d$t = \frac{T}{2}\delta_{nm}$.
  • To find bn, multiply both sides by sin(momega t) and integrate over one period, yielding the integral expression for bn.

Result: $a_0 = \frac{2}{T} \int_{-T/2}^{T/2} f(t) dt$, quad $a_n = \frac{2}{T} \int_{-T/2}^{T/2} f(t) \cos(nomega t) dt$, quad $b_n = \frac{2}{T} \int_{-T/2}^{T/2} f(t) \sin(nomega t) dt$

Fourier Series of a Symmetric Square Wave

Conditions: Square wave defined as f(t) = +V for 0 < t < T/2 and f(t) = -V for -T/2 < t < 0.

  • Observe that the function is an odd function, meaning f(-t) = -f(t), which implies $a_0 = 0$ and all $a_n = 0$.
  • Set up the integral for the sine coefficients: $b_n = \frac{2}{T} \left[ \int_{-T/2}^{0} (-V)\sin(nomega t)dt + \int_{0}^{T/2} Vsin(nomega t)dt \right]$.
  • Evaluate the integrals using int sin(nomega t) d$t = -\frac{\cos(nomega t)}{nomega}$.
  • Substitute the integration limits and simplify using ω$T = 2\pi$ to get the final expression for bn in terms of n.

Result: f(t) = \frac{4V}{pi} sum_{n=1,3,5...}^{infty} \frac{1}{n} sin(nomega t)

Solved numericals

TGT core

Find the constant term a_0 of a periodic function defined as f(t) = t for 0 < t < 2π, with period $T = 2\pi$.

  • Recall the formula for the DC coefficient: $a_0 = \frac{2}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) dt$.
  • Substitute $T = 2\pi$ and f(t) = t into the integral: $a_0 = \frac{2}{2pi} \int_{0}^{2pi} t dt$.
  • Evaluate the integral: int_{0}^{2pi} t d$t = \left[ \frac{t^2}{2} \right]_{0}^{2pi} = \frac{4pi^2}{2} = 2pi$.
  • Multiply by the outer factor: $a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi} (2pi) = 2$.

Answer: 2

TGT core

Determine whether the function f(t) = t^3 cos(t) contains sine or cosine terms in its Fourier series expansion over a symmetric interval about the origin.

  • Test for symmetry by replacing t with -t: f(-t) = (-t)^3 cos(-t).
  • Use trigonometric and algebraic properties: (-t)^3 = -t^3$ and cos(-t) = cos(t).
  • Combine the results: f(-t) = (-$t^3)\cos(t) = -f(t)$.
  • Since f(-t) = -f(t), the function is an odd function, which means its Fourier expansion contains exclusively sine terms ($a_0 = 0$, $a_n = 0)$.

Answer: Only sine terms (b_n \neq 0, $a_0 = 0$, $a_n = 0)$

PGT extension

Using Parseval's identity, calculate the sum of the series sum_{n=1,3,5...}^{infty} \frac{1}{n^2} given that the root-mean-square value of a symmetric square wave of amplitude V is V.

  • Write Parseval's identity for the square wave: \frac{1}{T} int_{0}^{T} [f(t)]^2 dt = \frac{a_0^2}{4} + \frac{1}{2} sum_{n=1,3...}^{infty} (a_n^2 + b_n^2).
  • For a symmetric square wave, $a_0 = 0$, $a_n = 0$, and $b_n = \frac{4V}{npi}$.
  • Substitute the RMS value squared on the left side: \frac{1}{T} int_{0}^{T} [f(t)]^2 d$t = V^2$.
  • Substitute b_n into the right side: V^2 = \frac{1}{2} sum_{n=1,3...}^{infty} left(\frac{4V}{npi}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \frac{16V^2}{pi^2} sum_{n=1,3...}^{infty} \frac{1}{n^2}.
  • Simplify and solve for the target sum: sum_{n=1,3...}^{infty} \frac{1}{$n^2 = \frac{V^2 \pi^2}{8 V^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{8}$.

Answer: \frac{\pi^2}{8}

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumption: The function under analysis is strictly periodic with a well-defined, constant time period T.
  • Limitation: Fourier series cannot represent transient or non-periodic signals directly; such signals require the Fourier Integral Transform instead.
  • Limitation: Sharp discontinuities in waveforms lead to the Gibbs phenomenon, where finite partial sums overshoot the true value by approximately 9% near the jump.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Fourier series can only be applied to continuous sinusoidal waves.

Correct: Fourier series can decompose any piecewise continuous, periodic waveform, including square, sawtooth, and triangular waves.

The basis functions are sines and cosines, but their linear superposition can model abrupt changes and non-sinusoidal profiles.

Common error: An even function contains both sine and cosine terms.

Correct: An even function contains only cosine terms and the DC constant term a_0.

Sine functions are odd functions; integrating an even function multiplied by an odd function over a symmetric interval yields zero.

Common error: The number of harmonics required to reconstruct a wave is always infinite in practical physical systems.

Correct: Band-limited physical systems act as low-pass filters, effectively eliminating high-frequency harmonics above a cutoff threshold.

Real physical media and instruments have inertia and damping that prevent the propagation of infinitely high frequencies.

Quick revision

  • Fourier analysis breaks down periodic waves into fundamental and harmonic sine/cosine components.
  • Dirichlet's conditions ensure the existence and convergence of the Fourier series.
  • Even functions yield only cosine terms (a_n); odd functions yield only sine terms (b_n).
  • Square waves contain only odd harmonics with amplitudes proportional to 1/n.
  • Triangular waves contain only odd harmonics with amplitudes proportional to 1/n^2.
  • Parseval's theorem equates total signal power to the sum of powers in its harmonic components.
  • The Gibbs phenomenon causes a 9% overshoot near sharp discontinuities when using truncated Fourier series.

Must remember

  • A periodic function $f(t)$ with period $T$ can be expanded in a Fourier series if it satisfies Dirichlet's conditions: single-valued, finite number of finite discontinuities, and finite number of maxima and minima within one period.
  • The constant term $a_0$ in a trigonometric Fourier series represents the DC or average value of the waveform over one complete period: $a_0 = \frac{2}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) dt$.
  • If a function possesses even symmetry ($f(-t) = f(t)$), all sine coefficients ($b_n$) are zero, and the series contains only cosine terms and $a_0$.
  • If a function possesses odd symmetry ($f(-t) = -f(t)$), all cosine coefficients ($a_n$) and the DC term ($a_0$) are zero, leaving only sine terms.
  • Half-wave symmetry defined by $f(t \pm T/2) = -f(t)$ ensures that the Fourier expansion contains only odd harmonics.
  • Parseval's theorem equates the mean square value of a periodic signal to the sum of the squares of the amplitudes of its Fourier components: $P_{avg} = \frac{a_0^2}{4} + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (a_n^2 + b_n^2)$.
  • The complex exponential Fourier series combines sines and cosines into a compact form using $C_n = \frac{1}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) e^{-in\omega t} dt$.
  • Real physical systems acting as low-pass filters limit high-frequency harmonic propagation due to inertia and damping.

Glossary

DC Component ($a_0 / 2$)The average or constant value of a periodic signal over one complete period.
HarmonicA frequency component of a periodic wave that is an integer multiple of the fundamental frequency.

Odd SymmetrySymmetry condition where $f(-t) = -f(t)$, eliminating all cosine coefficients and the DC term.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What does the constant term $a_0$ in a trigonometric Fourier series represent for a periodic function $f(t)$?

Explanation: The coefficient $a_0$ is calculated as $a_0 = \frac{2}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) dt$, which gives twice the average value of $f(t)$ over one complete period $T$. Thus, $a_0/2$ represents the DC or average value.

2. Which of the following is NOT one of Dirichlet's conditions for the convergence of a Fourier series?

Explanation: Dirichlet's conditions allow for a finite number of finite discontinuities within any one period; strict continuity everywhere is not required.

3. If a periodic function $f(t)$ possesses even symmetry such that $f(-t) = f(t)$, which Fourier coefficients are guaranteed to be zero?

Explanation: An even function multiplied by an odd function (sine) integrated over a symmetric interval yields zero. Hence, all sine coefficients $b_n = 0$ for even functions.

4. For a purely odd periodic function where $f(-t) = -f(t)$, which statement is correct regarding its Fourier expansion?

Explanation: An odd function contains only sine terms. Its integration over a symmetric interval gives zero for the DC term $a_0$ and all cosine coefficients $a_n$.

5. What is the expression for the cosine coefficient $a_n$ in a trigonometric Fourier series for a function of period $T$?

Explanation: The cosine coefficient $a_n$ is defined by the integral formula $a_n = \frac{2}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) \cos(n\omega t) dt$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$

6. Half-wave symmetry is mathematically defined as $f(t \pm T/2) = -f(t)$. What does this condition imply for the Fourier series expansion?

Explanation: Half-wave symmetry ensures that shifting the waveform by half a period inverts its sign, which systematically eliminates all even harmonics, leaving only odd harmonics in the Fourier series.

7. What does Parseval's theorem relate in the context of Fourier analysis of periodic waves?

Explanation: Parseval's theorem states that the mean square value (average power) of a periodic function over a period equals the sum of the squares of the amplitudes of its Fourier components.

8. In the complex exponential Fourier series $f(t) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} C_n e^{in\omega t}$, how is the complex coefficient $C_n$ calculated?

Explanation: The complex Fourier coefficient is given by $C_n = \frac{1}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) e^{-in\omega t} dt$ for all integer values of $n$ from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$.

9. Which of the following common misconceptions about Fourier series is correct?

Explanation: Fourier series are not restricted to continuous sinusoidal waves; they can represent any piecewise continuous periodic waveform through the superposition of sines and cosines.

10. What is the angular frequency $\omega$ in terms of the time period $T$ of a periodic wave?

Explanation: The fundamental angular frequency $\omega$ is related to the time period $T$ by the standard relation $\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T}$.

11. Why do real physical systems and instruments fail to propagate infinitely high harmonics present in theoretical Fourier expansions?

Explanation: Real physical media and instruments possess inertia and damping, which act as low-pass filters and attenuate or eliminate high-frequency harmonics above a certain cutoff threshold.

12. What is the mathematical expression for Parseval's identity for power in terms of trigonometric Fourier coefficients?

Explanation: Parseval's identity states that the mean square power is given by $P_{avg} = \frac{1}{T} \int_{0}^{T} [f(t)]^2 dt = \frac{a_0^2}{4} + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (a_n^2 + b_n^2)$.

13. If a symmetrical triangular wave passing through the origin is expanded in a Fourier series, which harmonic terms are present?

Explanation: A symmetrical triangular wave passing through the origin is an odd function with half-wave symmetry, resulting in a Fourier series containing only odd sine terms: $f(t) = \frac{8V}{\pi^2} \sum_{n=1,3,5...}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{(n-1)/2}}{n^2} \sin(n\omega t)$.

14. What is the primary role of orthogonality of trigonometric functions in Fourier analysis?

Explanation: The orthogonality of sine and cosine functions over a period allows multiplying the series by $\cos(n\omega t)$ or $\sin(n\omega t)$ and integrating, which isolates the specific coefficient $a_n$ or $b_n$ by making all other terms vanish.

15. Which of the following wave types typically contains only cosine terms in its standard trigonometric Fourier expansion shifted to an even reference?

Explanation: A square wave centered such that it is symmetric about the vertical axis ($f(-t) = f(t)$) exhibits even symmetry, meaning its Fourier series contains only cosine terms and $a_0$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. If a periodic function $f(t)$ is defined as an even function over the interval $[-T/2, T/2]$, which of the following coefficients will vanish in its trigonometric Fourier series?

Explanation: An even function satisfies $f(-t) = f(t)$. When multiplied by $\sin(n\omega t)$ (which is odd), the integrand is an odd function over a symmetric interval, resulting in zero for all sine coefficients ($b_n = 0$).

2. What is the value of the DC coefficient $a_0$ for a full period $T$ of a periodic waveform $f(t)$?

Explanation: By definition, the DC coefficient $a_0$ is given by $a_0 = \frac{2}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) dt$, which represents twice the average value of the waveform over one complete period.

3. Half-wave symmetry in a periodic waveform $f(t)$ with period $T$ is expressed by the relation:

Explanation: Half-wave symmetry is defined by $f(t \pm T/2) = -f(t)$, meaning shifting the wave by half a period inverts its amplitude. This property eliminates all even harmonics from the Fourier series.

4. According to Parseval's theorem, the total average power $P_{avg}$ of a periodic signal is related to its Fourier coefficients by:

Explanation: Parseval's identity equates the mean square power over one period to $\frac{a_0^2}{4} + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (a_n^2 + b_n^2)$.

5. In the complex exponential Fourier series expansion $f(t) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} C_n e^{in\omega t}$, the complex coefficient $C_n$ for a function of period $T$ is given by:

Explanation: The complex coefficient $C_n$ is defined by $C_n = \frac{1}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) e^{-in\omega t} dt$ for all integer values of $n$ from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$.

6. Which of the following conditions is NOT required by Dirichlet's theorem for a function to be expandable into a convergent Fourier series?

Explanation: Dirichlet's conditions permit a finite number of finite discontinuities and do not require continuous differentiability at every point; piecewise continuity and bounded variation are sufficient.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. If a periodic function $f(t)$ is odd ($f(-t) = -f(t)$), which coefficients are identically zero in its trigonometric Fourier series?

Explanation: For an odd function, integration of the function multiplied by $\cos(n\omega t)$ or a constant over a symmetric interval gives zero. Hence, the DC term $a_0$ and all cosine coefficients $a_n$ are zero.

2. What is the formula for the sine coefficient $b_n$ of a periodic function $f(t)$ with period $T$?

Explanation: The sine coefficient $b_n$ is defined by $b_n = \frac{2}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) \sin(n\omega t) dt$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$

3. A periodic signal has half-wave symmetry. Which harmonics are eliminated from its Fourier expansion?

Explanation: Half-wave symmetry ($f(t \pm T/2) = -f(t)$) ensures that all even harmonic coefficients ($a_n$ and $b_n$ for even $n$) evaluate to zero, leaving only odd harmonics.

4. What does the term $\frac{a_0}{2}$ represent in the general trigonometric Fourier series expansion?

Explanation: Since $a_0 = \frac{2}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) dt$, dividing $a_0$ by 2 yields $\frac{1}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) dt$, which is the exact average or DC value of $f(t)$ over one period.

5. Which theorem links the time-domain mean square value of a periodic signal to its frequency-domain harmonic power spectrum?

Explanation: Parseval's Theorem relates the total average power (mean square value) of a periodic signal to the sum of the powers of its individual Fourier components.

6. In the Fourier series expansion of a symmetrical triangular wave passing through the origin, how do the amplitudes of the harmonic components scale with harmonic number $n$?

Explanation: The triangular wave Fourier series formula is $f(t) = \frac{8V}{\pi^2} \sum_{n=1,3,5...}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{(n-1)/2}}{n^2} \sin(n\omega t)$, showing that amplitudes fall off as $1/n^2$.

7. What is the relationship between the complex exponential coefficients $C_n$ and the trigonometric coefficients $a_n, b_n$ for $n > 0$?

Explanation: Using Euler's formula, the complex coefficients for positive integers are related to trigonometric coefficients by $C_n = \frac{1}{2}(a_n - i b_n)$, and $C_{-n} = \frac{1}{2}(a_n + i b_n)$.

8. Which of the following functions satisfies Dirichlet's conditions for Fourier expansion?

Explanation: A finite square wave is piecewise continuous with a finite number of finite discontinuities and is absolutely integrable, fully satisfying Dirichlet's conditions.

9. If a waveform is shifted in time by a time $\tau$, what effect does it have on the Fourier series of the periodic wave?

Explanation: A time shift $t \to t - \tau$ in the time domain corresponds to multiplying each complex Fourier coefficient $C_n$ by $e^{-in\omega \tau}$, which introduces a phase shift to each harmonic without altering its amplitude.

10. Why is Fourier analysis particularly powerful in analyzing linear differential equations with periodic forcing functions?

Explanation: By breaking a complex periodic forcing function into a superposition of sinusoidal harmonics, linear differential equations can be solved independently for each frequency component using linear superposition.
