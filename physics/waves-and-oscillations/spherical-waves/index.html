Spherical Waves

Spherical waves are three-dimensional mechanical or electromagnetic waves where wavefronts form concentric spheres originating from a point source. Their amplitude decreases inversely with distance from the source to conserve total radiated energy.

UP TGT ScienceUP PGT PhysicsStudy Notes + Practice

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of spherical wavefronts, inverse-square law for intensity, and relation between amplitude and distance from a point source.

PGT extension

Rigorous derivation of the three-dimensional scalar wave equation in spherical polar coordinates and its divergent wave solutions of the form Psi(r,t) = (A/r)cos(kr - omega t).

Prerequisites

  • Simple harmonic motion and wave kinematics
  • Wave equation in one dimension
  • Vector calculus and spherical polar coordinate systems

Wavefronts and Rays in Three Dimensions

  • A wavefront is the locus of all adjacent points vibrating in the same phase, forming concentric spheres for a point source.
  • Wave normals (rays) point radially outward, indicating the direction of energy propagation.
  • As the radius r of the wavefront increases, the local surface area of the sphere grows as 4πr2.

Amplitude Variation with Distance

  • Total wave power P crossing any spherical surface of radius r remains constant in a lossless medium.
  • Since intensity I is power per unit area, $I = P / (4\pir^{2})$, meaning intensity varies inversely as r2.
  • Because wave intensity is proportional to the square of the amplitude (I ∝ A2), the displacement amplitude A must vary inversely with distance r: A(r) = A0 / r.

The 3D Wave Equation in Spherical Coordinates

  • In regions devoid of sources and assuming spherical symmetry, the scalar wave equation depends only on the radial coordinate r and time t.
  • The Laplacian operator in spherical coordinates reduces to (1/r2) ∂/∂r(r2 ∂/∂r).
  • The differential equation takes the form ∂2(rΨ)/∂t2 = v2 ∂2(rΨ)/∂r2.

Energy Transport and Intensity

  • The average intensity of a spherical wave is given by Iavg = 2π2 ρ v f2 A02 / r2.
  • Spherical waves attenuate purely due to geometric spreading, independent of medium absorption (which causes exponential damping).
  • At distances very large compared to the source dimensions, spherical wavefronts can be approximated locally as planar wavefronts.

Derivations

Derivation of Spherical Wave Equation Solution

Conditions: Homogeneous, isotropic, lossless medium with spherical symmetry

  • Write the three-dimensional scalar wave equation: ∇2$\Psi = (1/v^{2})(\partial ^{2}\Psi/\partial t^{2})$.
  • Expand the Laplacian for spherically symmetric fields where $\Psi = \Psi(r$,t): (1/r2)∂/∂r(r2 ∂Ψ/∂r) = (1/v2)(∂2Ψ/∂t2).
  • Substitute u(r,t) = rΨ(r,t). Then ∂Ψ/∂$r = (1/r)(\partial u/\partial r - u/r)$, leading to ∂2u/∂r2 = (1/v2)(∂2u/∂t2).
  • Solve the resulting 1D wave equation for u: u(r,t) = f(r - vt) + g(r + vt).
  • Divide by r to obtain the spherical wave solution: Ψ(r,t) = (1/r)f(r - vt).

Result: \Psi(r,t) = \frac{A}{r} \cos(kr - \omega t)

Derivation of Inverse Square Law for Intensity

Conditions: Isotropic point source emitting constant average power P in a non-absorbing medium

  • Consider a point source emitting power P uniformly in all directions.
  • Enclose the source with an imaginary sphere of radius r. The total surface area of this sphere is $S = 4\pi r^2$.
  • Define intensity I as the power per unit area: $I = \frac{P}{S} = \frac{P}{4\pi r^2}$.
  • Express wave intensity in terms of displacement amplitude A: $I = \frac{1}{2} \rho v \omega^2 A^2$.
  • Equate the two expressions to show A^2 \propto \frac{1}{r^2}, yielding A \propto \frac{1}{r}.

Result: I(r) = \frac{P}{4\pi r^2}

Solved numericals

TGT core

If the amplitude of a spherical wave is 5.0 cm at a distance of 2.0 m from a point source, what is the amplitude at a distance of 10.0 m?

  • Identify the inverse proportionality between amplitude and distance for a spherical wave: A_1 $r_1 = A_2 r_2$.
  • Substitute the given values: $A_1 = 5.0 cm$, $r_1 = 2.0 m$, $r_2 = 10.0 m$.
  • Solve for A_2: $A_2 = (A_1 r_1) / r_2 = (5.0 cm * 2.0 m) / 10.0 m$.
  • Calculate the final value: $A_2 = 10 / 10 = 1.0 cm$.

Answer: 1.0 cm

PGT extension

A small acoustic point source radiates sound power uniformly at 31.4 W in an open field. Calculate the sound intensity and the displacement amplitude of air particles at a distance of 5.0 m. Take density of air $\rho = 1.2 kg/m^3$, speed of sound $v = 340 m/s$, and frequency f = 500 Hz.

  • Calculate intensity using $I = P / (4\pir^2): I = 31.4 / (4 * 3.1416 * 5^2) = 31.4 / 314.16 = 0.1 W/m^2$.
  • Relate intensity to amplitude using $I = (1/2) \rho v \omega^2 A^2$, where $\omega = 2\pif = 2 * 3.1416 * 500 = 3141.6 rad/s$.
  • Rearrange for amplitude A: $A = \sqrt(2I / (\rho v \omega^2))$.
  • Substitute values: $A = \sqrt((2 * 0.1) / (1.2 * 340 * (3141.6)^2)) = \sqrt(0.2 / (408 * 9869604)) = \sqrt(0.2 / 4.026 * 10^9) \approx 7.05 * 10^-6 m$.

Answer: Intensit$y = 0.1 W/m^2$, Amplitude ≈ 7.05 μm

PGT extension

At what distance from an isotropic point source does the intensity of a spherical wave drop to 1/9th of its value at 3.0 m?

  • Recall the inverse square law: I ∝ 1/r^2, which implies I_1 / $I_2 = (r_2 / r_1)^2$.
  • Given $I_2 = (1/9) I_1$, substitute this ratio into the equation: $9 = (r_2 / 3.0)^2$.
  • Take the square root on both sides: r_2 / 3.0 = 3.
  • Solve for r_2: $r_2 = 3 * 3.0 = 9.0 m$.

Answer: 9.0 m

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes the medium is homogeneous, isotropic, and infinite, neglecting boundary reflections and refraction.
  • Assumes a point source of zero physical dimensions; real sources have finite sizes where near-field deviations occur.
  • Does not account for attenuation due to medium absorption or scattering, which introduces an exponential damping factor e^{-\alpha r}.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Spherical wave intensity is inversely proportional to distance r.

Correct: Intensity is inversely proportional to the square of the distance (r^2).

Energy spreads over the surface area of a sphere, which grows as 4πr^2, not its radius.

Common error: Frequency of a spherical wave changes as it propagates outward.

Correct: Frequency remains constant and depends solely on the source.

Wave propagation through a stationary, linear medium alters amplitude and phase, but not frequency.

Common error: Spherical waves can only exist in three dimensions for sound.

Correct: Spherical waves occur in any three-dimensional isotropic medium, including light, sound, and seismic waves.

The geometric requirement of 1/r amplitude dependence arises purely from 3D divergence of wavefronts.

Quick revision

  • Spherical wavefronts emerge from point sources and expand with surface area 4πr^2.
  • Displacement amplitude decays as A(r) = A_0 / r.
  • Wave intensity obeys the inverse square law $I = P / (4\pir^2)$.
  • The 3D scalar wave equation reduces to a 1D wave equation via the substitution $u = r\Psi$.

Must remember

  • The displacement amplitude of a spherical wave varies inversely with distance $r$ from the source, given by $A(r) = \frac{A_0}{r}$.
  • The intensity of a spherical wave follows an inverse-square law with distance: $I = \frac{P}{4\pi r^2}$.
  • In spherical coordinates under purely radial symmetry, the Laplacian operator reduces to $\nabla^2 \Psi = \frac{1}{r^2} \frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^2 \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial r}\right)$.
  • By substituting $u = r\Psi$, the spherical scalar wave equation transforms into a standard 1D wave equation: $\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = v^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial r^2}$.
  • Spherical waves attenuate purely due to geometric spreading in a lossless medium, independent of absorption damping.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the geometric shape of wavefronts produced by a point source of light in a uniform isotropic medium?

Explanation: A point source radiates disturbances equally in all directions, creating concentric spheres of constant phase known as spherical wavefronts.

2. How does the displacement amplitude $A$ of a spherical wave change with distance $r$ from the source in a lossless medium?

Explanation: Since total power remains constant and intensity $I \propto A^2 \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$, the displacement amplitude must vary as $A(r) = \frac{A_0}{r}$.

3. If the distance from a point source emitting spherical waves is doubled, the intensity of the wave becomes:

Explanation: Intensity varies inversely as the square of the distance ($I \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$). When $r$ is doubled, intensity becomes $\frac{1}{(2r)^2} = \frac{1}{4}I$.

4. What is the form of the scalar Laplacian operator $\nabla^2 \Psi$ under purely radial symmetry in spherical coordinates?

Explanation: In regions of spherical symmetry where $\Psi$ depends only on $r$, the Laplacian reduces to $\nabla^2 \Psi = \frac{1}{r^2} \frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^2 \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial r}\right)$.

5. By substituting $u = r\Psi$, the spherical wave equation transforms into which standard differential equation?

Explanation: Making the substitution $u = r\Psi$ transforms the radial part of the 3D wave equation into the standard 1D linear wave equation for $u(r,t)$.

6. What is the primary cause of wave attenuation for spherical waves propagating through a lossless medium?

Explanation: In a lossless medium, spherical waves attenuate solely due to geometric spreading, where constant total power is distributed over an ever-increasing surface area $4\pi r^2$.

7. The propagation constant $k$ of a wave is related to angular frequency $\omega$ and wave speed $v$ by:

Explanation: By definition, the wave number or propagation constant is $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} = \frac{2\pi f}{v} = \frac{\omega}{v}$.

8. At distances very large compared to the dimensions of the source, spherical wavefronts can be approximated locally as:

Explanation: Far from the source, the radius of curvature of a spherical wavefront is extremely large, making a small local patch effectively indistinguishable from a plane wave.

9. What is the SI unit of the wave propagation constant $k$?

Explanation: The propagation constant $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$ has units of radians per meter (rad/m).

10. If the amplitude of a spherical wave at distance $r_1$ is $A_1$, what is its amplitude at distance $r_2$ in a lossless medium?

Explanation: Since amplitude varies inversely with distance ($A(r) = \frac{A_1 r_1}{r}$), the amplitude at $r_2$ is $A_1 \frac{r_1}{r_2}$.

11. Which of the following parameters remains constant as a spherical wave propagates outward through a stationary, uniform, lossless medium?

Explanation: Frequency depends exclusively on the source and remains constant during propagation, whereas amplitude, intensity, and energy density decrease with distance $r$.

12. What does the wave normal (ray) indicate in a spherical wave system?

Explanation: Wave normals point radially outward, representing the direction along which wave energy and wavefronts advance.

13. The average energy density $\langle E \rangle$ of a harmonic spherical wave is proportional to:

Explanation: Average energy density is given by $\langle E \rangle = \frac{1}{2} \rho \omega^2 A(r)^2$, showing it is directly proportional to the square of the displacement amplitude.

14. Which differential relation correctly defines the transformed variable $u = r\Psi$ substitution for spherical waves?

Explanation: Substitution of $u = r\Psi$ simplifies the 3D scalar wave equation into the standard 1D wave equation: $\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = v^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial r^2}$.

15. Which of the following media can support spherical waves?

Explanation: Spherical waves occur in any 3D isotropic medium whenever disturbances originate from a localized point source (e.g., light, sound, and seismic waves).

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The intensity $I$ of a spherical wave at a radial distance $r$ from a point source of power $P$ in a lossless medium is given by:

Explanation: Intensity is power per unit area. For a spherical wavefront of radius $r$, the surface area is $4\pi r^2$, giving $I = \frac{P}{4\pi r^2}$.

2. If the displacement amplitude of a spherical wave at distance $r$ is $A(r) = \frac{A_0}{r}$, how does the wave intensity $I(r)$ vary with distance?

Explanation: Since wave intensity is proportional to the square of the displacement amplitude ($I \propto A^2$), substituting $A(r) = \frac{A_0}{r}$ yields $I(r) \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$.

3. In the analysis of three-dimensional scalar wave equation with spherical symmetry, the function $u(r,t) = r\Psi(r,t)$ satisfies which equation of motion?

Explanation: Applying the transformation $u = r\Psi$ to the spherically symmetric wave equation directly yields the standard one-dimensional wave equation for $u$: $\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = v^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial r^2}$.

4. A point source radiates sound power uniformly in all directions in a non-absorbing medium. If the amplitude of the sound wave at a distance of $2\text{ m}$ is $A$, what is the amplitude at a distance of $6\text{ m}$?

Explanation: Amplitude varies inversely with distance ($A \propto \frac{1}{r}$). When distance increases from $2\text{ m}$ to $6\text{ m}$ (a factor of 3), the amplitude decreases by a factor of 3, becoming $A / 3$.

5. Which of the following represents a valid divergent spherical wave solution for the displacement function $\Psi(r,t)$?

Explanation: A divergent spherical wave has an amplitude inversely proportional to $r$, represented by $\Psi(r,t) = \frac{A_0}{r} \sin(kr - \omega t)$.

6. What happens to the frequency of a spherical wave when it passes from one lossless isotropic medium into another with a different wave speed?

Explanation: Frequency is determined entirely by the source and remains invariant when a wave crosses boundaries into different media, whereas wavelength and speed change accordingly.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. The local surface area of a spherical wavefront of radius $r$ expands as:

Explanation: The surface area of a sphere of radius $r$ is given by $4\pi r^2$.

2. If the intensity of a spherical wave at distance $r_1$ is $I_1$, what is the intensity at distance $r_2 = 3r_1$?

Explanation: Intensity follows the inverse-square law: $I \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$. Thus, at $r_2 = 3r_1$, intensity is $I_1 / (3)^2 = I_1 / 9$.

3. In the spherical wave displacement equation $\Psi(r,t) = \frac{A_0}{r} \sin(kr - \omega t + \phi)$, what represents the spatial oscillation frequency?

Explanation: The propagation constant $k$ (wave number) determines the spatial oscillation frequency and phase progression along $r$.

4. The average energy density $\langle E \rangle$ of a spherical harmonic wave at distance $r$ varies with distance as:

Explanation: Energy density is proportional to $A(r)^2$. Since $A(r) \propto \frac{1}{r}$, the energy density varies as $\frac{1}{r^2}$.

5. Which coordinate system is naturally suited for formulating wave equations with central or point symmetry?

Explanation: Spherical polar coordinates exploit radial symmetry, simplifying the Laplacian operator when wave properties depend only on distance $r$.

6. What is the phase velocity $v_p$ of a spherical wave in terms of angular frequency $\omega$ and propagation constant $k$?

Explanation: Phase velocity is defined as $v_p = \frac{\omega}{k} = f\lambda$.

7. Why does a spherical wave decrease in amplitude as it propagates outward?

Explanation: Total wave power remains constant while spreading across a growing surface area ($4\pi r^2$), requiring amplitude to drop as $1/r$.

8. If a source of spherical waves has its power output doubled, what happens to the displacement amplitude $A(r)$ at any given distance $r$?

Explanation: Power $P$ is proportional to intensity $I$, and intensity is proportional to amplitude squared ($I \propto A^2$). If power doubles, $A^2$ doubles, so amplitude increases by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$.

9. Under what condition can a spherical wavefront be treated as a plane wavefront?

Explanation: When observed at a large distance from the source, the curvature of the spherical wavefront is negligible across a local region, allowing it to be approximated as a plane wave.

10. Which of the following is a false statement regarding spherical waves?

Explanation: Frequency does not vary with distance; it remains constant and is determined entirely by the source. Thus, option 3 is false.
