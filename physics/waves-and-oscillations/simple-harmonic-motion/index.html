Simple Harmonic Motion

Simple harmonic motion (SHM) is a special type of periodic motion where the restoring force is directly proportional to the displacement from a fixed equilibrium position and is directed towards that position. It forms the foundation for understanding oscillatory phenomena, wave propagation, and acoustics in classical mechanics.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Kinematics of SHM, displacement, velocity, acceleration equations, time period and frequency of mass-spring systems, simple pendulum, and energy conservation.

PGT extension

Phase and phase difference, composition of two SHM lines (orthogonal and parallel), angular harmonic oscillators, physical pendulum, torsional pendulum, and differential formulation.

Prerequisites

  • Newton's laws of motion
  • Restoring force and Hooke's law
  • Differential equations of second order with constant coefficients

Kinematics and Differential Equation of SHM

  • Defined by the differential equation d²x/dt² + ω²x = 0, where ω is the angular frequency.
  • Displacement as a function of time is given by x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ), where A is amplitude and φ is initial phase.
  • Acceleration is always directed towards the mean position and has a magnitude proportional to displacement.

Energy Conservation in SHM

  • Potential energy (U) varies parabolically with displacement, given by U = (1/2)mω²x².
  • Kinetic energy (K) is maximum at the mean position and zero at the extreme positions, given by K = (1/2)mω²(A² - x²).
  • Total mechanical energy E = U + K = (1/2)mω²A² remains constant throughout the motion in the absence of damping.

Simple and Physical Pendulums

  • A simple pendulum consists of a point mass suspended by a massless, inextensible string of length l, with time period T = 2π&sqrt;(l/g).
  • A physical (compound) pendulum is any rigid body oscillating about a horizontal axis, with T = 2π&sqrt;(I / mgd), where I is moment of inertia and d is distance from pivot to center of mass.
  • Kater's pendulum is a reversible pendulum used for the precise experimental determination of acceleration due to gravity (g).

Superposition of Simple Harmonic Motions

  • Collinear SHMs of the same frequency combine to yield a single SHM whose amplitude depends on the phase difference between them.
  • Perpendicular SHMs of equal frequency combine to form circular or elliptical trajectories depending on the phase difference.
  • Lissajous figures are formed when two mutually perpendicular SHMs of different frequency ratios are superimposed.

Derivations

Differential Equation and Time Period of Mass-Spring System

Conditions: Horizontal frictionless surface, ideal spring obeying Hooke's law

  • Apply Hooke's law for restoring force: F = -kx
  • Apply Newton's second law: m(d²x/dt²) = -kx
  • Rearrange to standard form: d²x/dt² + (k/m)x = 0
  • Compare with standard SHM equation d²x/dt² + ω²x = 0 to get angular frequency ω = sqrt(k/m)
  • Obtain time period T = 2π/ω = 2π sqrt(m/k)

Result: T = 2π \sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}

Time Period of a Simple Pendulum

Conditions: Small amplitude approximation (&theta; << 1 radian), inextensible string

  • Write the equation of torque about the point of suspension: τ = -mg l sin(θ)
  • Use rotational analogue of Newton's second law: I (d²θ/dt²) = -mgl sin(θ)
  • Substitute moment of inertia I = ml² and apply small angle approximation sin(θ) ≈ θ
  • Simplify to obtain ml² (d²θ/dt²) + mglθ = 0 or d²θ/dt² + (g/l)θ = 0
  • Determine angular frequency ω = sqrt(g/l) and time period T = 2π sqrt(l/g)

Result: T = 2π \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}

Solved numericals

TGT core

A particle executes SHM with an amplitude of 5 cm and a frequency of 2 Hz. Calculate its maximum velocity and maximum acceleration.

  • Identify given values: A = 0.05 m, f = 2 Hz.
  • Calculate angular frequency: ω = 2πf = 2 × π × 2 = 4π rad s^-1.
  • Calculate maximum velocity: v_max = Aω = 0.05 × 4π = 0.2π m s^-1 ≈ 0.628 m s^-1.
  • Calculate maximum acceleration: a_max = Aω² = 0.05 × (4π)² = 0.05 × 16π² = 0.8π² m s^-2 ≈ 7.89 m s^-2.

Answer: v_max = 0.628 m s^-1, a_max = 7.89 m s^-2

TGT core

At what displacement from the mean position is the kinetic energy of a simple harmonic oscillator equal to its potential energy? Given amplitude is A.

  • Write expression for kinetic energy: K = (1/2)mω²(A² - x²).
  • Write expression for potential energy: U = (1/2)mω²x².
  • Equate K = U: (1/2)mω²(A² - x²) = (1/2)mω²x².
  • Simplify equation: A² - x² = x^2 Rightarrow 2x² = A².
  • Solve for x: x = ± A / \sqrt{2}.

Answer: x = \pm \frac{A}{\sqrt{2}}

PGT extension

A uniform slender rod of mass M and length L is pivoted at one end and allowed to oscillate as a physical pendulum in a vertical plane. Find its time period for small oscillations.

  • Identify moment of inertia of a uniform rod about one end: I = (1/3)ML².
  • Determine distance from pivot to center of mass: d = L/2.
  • Substitute I and d into the physical pendulum formula: T = 2π \sqrt{\frac{I}{mgd}}.
  • T = 2π \sqrt{\frac{(1/3)ML^2}{Mg(L/2)}}.
  • Simplify the radical: T = 2π \sqrt{\frac{2L}{3g}}.

Answer: T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{2L}{3g}}

Assumptions and limitations

  • Restoring force is strictly linear (F = -kx) which holds true only for small displacements within the elastic limit.
  • Damping forces (like air resistance or internal friction) are assumed to be zero unless explicitly stated otherwise in damped harmonic motion models.
  • For pendulums, angle θ must be small enough so that sin(θ) ≈ θ holds with negligible error.

Common misconceptions

Common error: SHM is the only type of periodic motion.

Correct: All SHMs are periodic, but not all periodic motions are simple harmonic (e.g., planetary motion, motion of a simple clock pendulum with large amplitude).

Common error: Acceleration is zero at the extreme positions in SHM.

Correct: Acceleration is maximum at the extreme positions and zero at the mean (equilibrium) position.

Common error: The time period of a simple pendulum depends on its mass.

Correct: The time period formula T = 2&pi; \sqrt{l/g} is independent of the mass of the bob.

Quick revision

  • Displacement equation: x = A cos(ωt + φ)
  • Restoring force: F = -kx
  • Angular frequency: ω = \sqrt{k/m}
  • Simple pendulum period: T = 2\pi \sqrt{l/g}
  • Total energy: E = (1/2)kA^2
  • Velocity amplitude: v_max = Aω
  • Acceleration amplitude: a_max = Aω²
  • Physical pendulum period: T = 2\pi \sqrt{I/(mgd)}

Must remember

  • The differential equation defining SHM is given by $\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + \omega^2 x = 0$.
  • Total mechanical energy of an SHM system is $E = \frac{1}{2} m \omega^2 A^2 = \frac{1}{2} k A^2$, which remains conserved in the absence of damping.
  • The time period of a simple pendulum is $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$ and is independent of the mass of the bob.
  • For a physical pendulum, the time period is $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{I}{mgd}}$, where $I$ is the moment of inertia about the pivot and $d$ is the distance from pivot to the center of mass.
  • Acceleration in SHM is maximum at the extreme positions and zero at the mean position.

Glossary

Amplitude ($A$)The maximum displacement of a particle executing simple harmonic motion from its mean position.
Angular Frequency ($\omega$)A scalar measure of rotation rate or oscillation frequency, expressed in radians per second, related to frequency $f$ by $\omega = 2\pi f$.
Phase Constant ($\phi$)An angle that determines the initial state of motion of a particle executing SHM at $t = 0$.
Physical PendulumAny rigid body free to oscillate about a fixed horizontal axis under the action of gravity.
Lissajous FiguresCurves generated by the superposition of two mutually perpendicular simple harmonic motions of different frequencies or phases.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following differential equations represents Simple Harmonic Motion?

Explanation: The standard differential equation governing Simple Harmonic Motion is $\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + \$\omega$^2 x = 0$, where $\$\omega$$ is the angular frequency.

2. At what position is the acceleration of a particle executing SHM maximum?

Explanation: Acceleration in SHM is given by $a = -\$\omega$^2 x$. Its magnitude is directly proportional to displacement $x$, making it maximum at the extreme positions ($x = \pm A$) and zero at the mean position.

3. What is the total mechanical energy of a particle of mass $m$ executing SHM with amplitude $A$ and angular frequency $\$\omega$$?

Explanation: The total mechanical energy $E$ in SHM is the sum of maximum potential energy or maximum kinetic energy, which evaluates to $E = \frac{1}{2}m\$\omega$^2 A^2$.

4. The time period of a simple pendulum depends on which of the following parameters?

Explanation: The time period of an ideal simple pendulum is given by $T = 2\$\pi$ \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$, which depends solely on the effective length $l$ and acceleration due to gravity $g$ (for small amplitudes).

5. For a physical pendulum consisting of a rigid body, the time period is given by $T = 2\$\pi$ \sqrt{\frac{I}{mgd}}$. Here, $I$ represents:

Explanation: In the time period formula of a physical pendulum, $I$ denotes the moment of inertia of the rigid body about the fixed horizontal axis of rotation (pivot point).

6. What is the velocity of a particle executing SHM at its extreme position ($x = \pm A$)?

Explanation: The velocity in SHM is given by $v = \pm \$\omega$ \sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$. At the extreme positions ($x = \pm A$), the velocity becomes zero.

7. If the displacement equation of an oscillator is given by $x(t) = A \cos(\$\omega$ t + \$\phi$)$, what is the initial phase if at $t = 0$, $x = 0$ and velocity is negative?

Explanation: If $x(0) = 0$, then $A \cos(\$\phi$) = 0 \implies \$\phi$ = \pm \frac{\$\pi$}{2}$. Since velocity is negative, $v(0) = -A\$\omega$ \sin(\$\phi$) < 0$, which requires $\sin(\$\phi$) > 0$, hence $\$\phi$ = \frac{\$\pi$}{2}$.

8. How does the potential energy $U$ vary with displacement $x$ in SHM?

Explanation: Potential energy in SHM is given by $U = \frac{1}{2}m\$\omega$^2 x^2$, which indicates a parabolic variation with displacement $x$.

9. What is the frequency of total energy variation in simple harmonic motion compared to the frequency of the displacement oscillation?

Explanation: Kinetic and potential energies fluctuate at twice the frequency of displacement and velocity because terms like $x^2$ and $\sin^2(\$\omega$ t)$ complete two cycles per oscillation cycle.

10. Which instrument is specifically used for the precise experimental determination of acceleration due to gravity ($g$) utilizing the principle of interchangeability of center of oscillation and suspension?

Explanation: Kater's pendulum is a reversible physical pendulum designed to measure $g$ with high precision by ensuring the time period remains unchanged when the pivot and center of oscillation are interchanged.

11. The superposition of two mutually perpendicular SHMs of equal frequency with a phase difference of $\frac{\$\pi$}{2}$ and equal amplitudes results in:

Explanation: Perpendicular SHMs of equal frequency and amplitude with a phase difference of $\frac{\$\pi$}{2}$ combine to form a circular trajectory.

12. What is the time period of a torsional pendulum given by $T = 2\$\pi$ \sqrt{\frac{I}{C}}$, where $C$ is:

Explanation: In a torsional pendulum, $C$ represents the restoring torsional couple per unit angle of twist.

13. If mass $m$ is attached to a vertical spring of spring constant $k$, its time period of oscillation is:

Explanation: The time period of an ideal mass-spring system is given by $T = 2\$\pi$ \sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}$.

14. Which of the following statements is correct regarding periodic motion and simple harmonic motion?

Explanation: Simple harmonic motion is a special type of periodic motion governed by a linear restoring force, but periodic motions can be non-harmonic (e.g., planetary motion).

15. What are Lissajous figures?

Explanation: Lissajous figures are trajectory patterns produced by superimposing two mutually perpendicular simple harmonic motions.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. A particle executes simple harmonic motion with an amplitude $A$. At what displacement from the mean position is its kinetic energy equal to its potential energy?

Explanation: Kinetic energy $K = \frac{1}{2}m\$\omega$^2(A^2 - x^2)$ and potential energy $U = \frac{1}{2}m\$\omega$^2 x^2$. Equating $K = U$ gives $A^2 - x^2 = x^2 \implies 2x^2 = A^2 \implies x = \pm \frac{A}{\sqrt{2}}.$

2. The maximum velocity of a particle executing SHM is $v_{max}$ and maximum acceleration is $a_{max}$. What is the time period of oscillation?

Explanation: We know $v_{max} = A\$\omega$$ and $a_{max} = A\$\omega$^2$. Dividing $v_{max}$ by $a_{max}$ gives $\frac{v_{max}}{a_{max}} = \frac{1}{\$\omega$}$. Since $\$\omega$ = \frac{2\$\pi$}{T}$, we get $T = \frac{2\$\pi$ v_{max}}{a_{max}}.$

3. Two springs of spring constants $k_1$ and $k_2$ are connected in series. The effective spring constant of the combination is:

Explanation: For springs in series, the reciprocal of the effective spring constant is the sum of reciprocals: $\frac{1}{k_{eff}} = \frac{1}{k_1} + \frac{1}{k_2}$, which simplifies to $k_{eff} = \frac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_2}$.

4. A simple pendulum of length $l$ has a time period $T$. If its length is increased by $300%$, what will be the percentage change in its time period?

Explanation: If length increases by $300%$, the new length $l' = l + 3l = 4l$. The new time period $T' = 2\$\pi$ \sqrt{\frac{4l}{g}} = 2T$. The percentage increase is $\frac{T' - T}{T} \times 100\% = \frac{2T - T}{T} \times 100\% = 100\%$.

5. The displacement of a particle in SHM is given by $x = 5 \sin(2\$\pi$ t + \frac{\$\pi$}{4})$. What is the maximum acceleration of the particle?

Explanation: Comparing with $x = A \sin(\$\omega$ t + \$\phi$)$, amplitude $A = 5$ and angular frequency $\$\omega$ = 2\$\pi$$. Maximum acceleration $a_{max} = A\$\omega$^2 = 5 \times (2\$\pi$)^2 = 5 \times 4\$\pi$^2 = 20\$\pi$^2$.

6. A uniform rod of mass $m$ and length $L$ is pivoted about one of its ends and oscillates as a physical pendulum. Its time period for small oscillations is:

Explanation: For a uniform rod pivoted at one end, moment of inertia $I = \frac{1}{3}mL^2$ and distance to center of mass $d = \frac{L}{2}$. Substituting into $T = 2\$\pi$ \sqrt{\frac{I}{mgd}}$ gives $T = 2\$\pi$ \sqrt{\frac{\frac{1}{3}mL^2}{mg(L/2)}} = 2\$\pi$ \sqrt{\frac{2L}{3g}}$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. If the total mechanical energy of an oscillator in SHM is doubled, what happens to its amplitude?

Explanation: Total energy $E = \frac{1}{2}m\$\omega$^2 A^2 \implies E \propto A^2$. If energy is doubled ($2E$), amplitude becomes $\sqrt{2}A$.

2. A particle executes SHM along a straight line with a frequency $f$. With what frequency does its kinetic energy oscillate?

Explanation: Kinetic energy contains terms dependent on $\sin^2(\$\omega$ t)$, which oscillates at twice the frequency of the displacement oscillation, i.e., $2f$.

3. What is the phase difference between velocity and displacement of a particle executing simple harmonic motion?

Explanation: Displacement is proportional to $\cos(\$\omega$ t + \$\phi$)$ and velocity is proportional to $-\sin(\$\omega$ t + \$\phi$) = \cos(\$\omega$ t + \$\phi$ + \frac{\$\pi$}{2})$, indicating a phase difference of $\frac{\$\pi$}{2}$.

4. Two identical springs are connected in parallel and a mass $m$ is suspended from them. If the individual spring constant is $k$, the time period of oscillation is:

Explanation: For springs in parallel, the effective spring constant is $k_{eff} = k_1 + k_2 = 2k$. The time period is $T = 2\$\pi$ \sqrt{\frac{m}{2k}}.$

5. The differential equation of a damped harmonic oscillator is given by $\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + 2b \frac{dx}{dt} + \$\omega_{0}$^2 x = 0$. Here, $2b$ represents:

Explanation: In the damped harmonic oscillator equation, $2b$ (or $\$\gamma$$) represents the damping coefficient per unit mass.

6. If a simple pendulum is taken inside a satellite orbiting the earth, its time period will be:

Explanation: Inside an orbiting satellite, effective acceleration due to gravity $g_{eff} = 0$. Since $T = 2\$\pi$ \sqrt{\frac{l}{g_{eff}}}$, the time period becomes infinite (the pendulum does not oscillate).

7. A particle is subjected to two simple harmonic motions in the same direction given by $x_1 = A_1 \cos(\$\omega$ t)$ and $x_2 = A_2 \sin(\$\omega$ t)$. The amplitude of the resultant motion is:

Explanation: Rewriting $x_2 = A_2 \cos(\$\omega$ t - \frac{\$\pi$}{2})$, the phase difference between the two collinear SHMs is $\frac{\$\pi$}{2}$. The resultant amplitude is $A = \sqrt{A_1^2 + A_2^2 + 2A_1 A_2 \cos(\$\pi$/2)} = \sqrt{A_1^2 + A_2^2}$.

8. At what displacement is the kinetic energy of an SHM oscillator equal to three times its potential energy ($K = 3U$)?

Explanation: Given $K = 3U \implies \frac{1}{2}m\$\omega$^2(A^2 - x^2) = 3 \left(\frac{1}{2}m\$\omega$^2 x^2\right) \implies A^2 - x^2 = 3x^2 \implies 4x^2 = A^2 \implies x = \pm \frac{A}{2}$.

9. What is the condition for a physical pendulum to have the minimum possible time period for a given mass and center of mass distance $d$?

Explanation: Since $T = 2\$\pi$ \sqrt{\frac{I}{mgd}}$, to minimize the time period $T$, the moment of inertia $I$ about the pivot must be minimized.

10. Which of the following represents the correct dimension of the angular frequency $\$\omega$$?

Explanation: Angular frequency is expressed in radians per second, making its dimensional formula $[T^{-1}]$. latter.
