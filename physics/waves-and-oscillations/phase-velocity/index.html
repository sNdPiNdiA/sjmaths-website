Phase Velocity in Waves

Phase velocity is the rate at which the phase of a wave propagates in space. It represents the velocity at which a point of constant phase (such as a crest or trough) travels along the wave.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of phase velocity, relation between wave velocity, frequency, and wavelength, and simple numerical problems involving sinusoidal wave equations.

PGT extension

Derivation of phase velocity from the wave equation, relation between phase velocity and group velocity, dispersion relations, and dispersive media.

Prerequisites

  • Simple harmonic motion and wave equation
  • Angular frequency, wave number, and wavelength
  • Partial differentiation

Definition and Physical Meaning

  • Phase velocity (vp) is defined as vp = ω/k, where ω is the angular frequency and k is the angular wave number.
  • It characterizes the propagation speed of a single frequency component (monochromatic wave) through a medium.
  • In a non-dispersive medium, all frequency components travel at the same phase velocity.

Relation with Frequency and Wavelength

  • Expressing $\omega = 2\pi\nu$ and $k = 2\pi/\lambda$, the phase velocity is given by vp = νλ.
  • Here, ν is the frequency of the wave and λ is its spatial wavelength.
  • Any change in medium properties that alters λ while keeping ν constant will change the phase velocity.

Dispersive vs Non-Dispersive Media

  • In a non-dispersive medium, phase velocity is independent of frequency or wavelength.
  • In a dispersive medium, the phase velocity depends on wavelength (dvp/dλ ≠ 0), leading to wave packet spreading.
  • Examples of dispersive media include deep water gravity waves and light passing through glass prisms.

Relation to Group Velocity

  • Group velocity vg represents the velocity of the overall wave packet or envelope energy transport.
  • The fundamental relation linking them is vg = vp - λ(dvp/dλ).
  • In a normal dispersion medium, phase velocity is greater than group velocity (vp > vg).

Derivations

Derivation of Phase Velocity from Wave Equation

Conditions: Homogeneous, one-dimensional linear wave equation

  • Consider a one-dimensional traveling harmonic wave represented by y(x, t) = A cos(kx - ωt + φ).
  • For a point of constant phase along the wave profile, the phase term must remain constant: kx - ωt = constant.
  • Differentiate both sides with respect to time t: k(dx/dt) - $\omega = 0$.
  • Recognize dx/dt as the phase velocity vp, giving vp = ω/k.

Result: $v_p = \frac{\omega}{k}$

Relation between Phase Velocity and Group Velocity

Conditions: Dispersive medium where ω is a non-linear function of k

  • Define group velocity as vg = dω/dk.
  • Express angular frequency in terms of phase velocity: $\omega = k v_{p}$.
  • Differentiate ω with respect to k using the product rule: vg = \frac{d}{dk}(k vp) = vp + k \frac{dv_p}{dk}.
  • Substitute $k = 2\pi/\lambda$ and use chain rule \frac{dv_p}{dk} = \frac{dv_p}{d\lambda} \frac{d\lambda}{dk} = -\frac{\lambda}{2\pi} \lambda^2 \frac{dv_p}{d\lambda} to obtain vg = vp - λ\frac{dv_p}{d\lambda}.

Result: $v_g = v_p - \lambda \frac{dv_p}{d\lambda}$

Solved numericals

TGT core

A progressive wave is represented by the equation y(x, t) = 0.05 sin(100πt - 2πx), where x is in meters and t is in seconds. Calculate the phase velocity of the wave.

  • Compare the given equation with the standard wave equation y(x, t) = A sin(ωt - kx).
  • Identify angular frequency $\omega = 100\pi rad s^{-1}$ and wave number $k = 2\pi rad m^{-1}$.
  • Calculate phase velocity using vp = ω/$k = (100\pi) / (2\pi)$.

Answer: 50 m s^-1

TGT core

Find the frequency of a wave whose phase velocity is 330 m s^-1 and wavelength is 1.5 m.

  • Use the relation between phase velocity, frequency, and wavelength: vp = νλ.
  • Rearrange to solve for frequency: $\nu = v_{p} / \lambda$.
  • Substitute values: $\nu = 330 / 1.5 = 220 Hz$.

Answer: 220 Hz

PGT extension

In a dispersive medium, the phase velocity is given by vp = a + bλ, where a and b are constants. Derive an expression for the group velocity vg in terms of a, b, and λ.

  • Start with the general relation between group velocity and phase velocity: vg = vp - λ(dvp/dλ).
  • Differentiate the given phase velocity expression with respect to λ: dvp/d$\lambda = d/d\lambda(a + b\lambda) = b$.
  • Substitute vp and dvp/dλ into the group velocity formula: vg = (a + bλ) - λ(b).
  • Simplify the expression: vg = a + bλ - b$\lambda = a$.

Answer: a

Assumptions and limitations

  • Phase velocity applies strictly to monochromatic, single-frequency continuous waves and cannot directly represent the propagation speed of information or signal energy in dispersive media.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Phase velocity can always exceed the speed of light in vacuum without physical restriction.

Correct: While phase velocity (vp = ω/k) can exceed c in certain anomalous dispersion regions or wave guides, it transmits no energy or information, thus not violating relativity.

Information and energy travel at the group velocity or signal velocity, which never exceeds c.

Common error: Phase velocity and group velocity are always identical.

Correct: They are identical only in non-dispersive media where phase velocity is independent of frequency.

In dispersive media, different frequency components travel at different speeds, making vp different from vg.

Quick revision

  • Phase velocity is given by $v_p = \omega/k = \nu\lambda$ and represents the speed of constant phase points.
  • Phase Velocity Definition: $v_p = \frac{\omega}{k}$
  • Frequency-Wavelength Form: $v_p = \nu \lambda$

Must remember

  • Phase velocity is defined as $v_p = \frac{\omega}{k}$, representing the propagation speed of a single frequency component (monochromatic wave).
  • The relation between phase velocity, frequency, and wavelength is given by $v_p = \nu \lambda$.
  • In a non-dispersive medium, phase velocity is independent of frequency or wavelength.
  • The relation connecting group velocity and phase velocity is $v_g = v_p - \lambda \frac{dv_p}{d\lambda}$.
  • In a normal dispersion medium, the phase velocity is greater than the group velocity ($v_p > v_g$).
  • Phase velocity for a stretched string is $v_p = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}$, where $T$ is tension and $\mu$ is mass per unit length.
  • Phase velocity for longitudinal acoustic waves in a gas is $v_p = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma P}{\rho}}.
  • Phase velocity for electromagnetic waves in a medium is $v_p = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu \epsilon}}$.

Glossary

Phase Velocity ($v_p$)The speed at which a point of constant phase of a wave travels, given by $\frac{\omega}{k}$.
Angular Frequency ($\omega$)The rate of change of phase of a sinusoidal waveform, measured in rad/s, $\omega = 2\pi \nu$.
Angular Wave Number ($k$)The spatial frequency of a wave, representing the number of radians per unit distance, $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$.
Group Velocity ($v_g$)The velocity with which the overall envelope of a wave packet and its energy are propagated.
Dispersive MediumA medium in which the phase velocity of a wave depends on its frequency or wavelength ($\frac{dv_p}{d\lambda} \neq 0$).
Non-dispersive MediumA medium in which all frequency components travel at the same phase velocity, independent of wavelength.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the fundamental definition of phase velocity ($v_p$) in terms of angular frequency ($\omega$) and angular wave number ($k$)?

Explanation: Phase velocity is defined as the ratio of angular frequency to angular wave number, $v_p = \frac{\omega}{k}$.

2. How is phase velocity expressed in terms of wave frequency ($\nu$) and wavelength ($\lambda$)?

Explanation: Using $\omega = 2\pi \nu$ and $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$, the phase velocity becomes $v_p = \frac{2\pi \nu}{2\pi / \lambda} = \nu \lambda$.

3. In a non-dispersive medium, how does phase velocity vary with frequency?

Explanation: In a non-dispersive medium, all frequency components travel at the exact same speed, meaning phase velocity is independent of frequency or wavelength.

4. What is the correct relation between group velocity ($v_g$) and phase velocity ($v_p$) in a dispersive medium?

Explanation: The fundamental relation linking group velocity to phase velocity is $v_g = v_p - \lambda \frac{dv_p}{d\lambda}$.

5. For a transverse wave on a stretched string of tension $T$ and mass per unit length $\mu$, the phase velocity is given by:

Explanation: The phase velocity of transverse waves on a stretched string is derived from wave mechanics as $v_p = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}$.

6. What condition characterizes a dispersive medium with respect to phase velocity?

Explanation: In a dispersive medium, the phase velocity depends on wavelength, meaning its derivative with respect to wavelength is non-zero ($\frac{dv_p}{d\lambda} \neq 0$).

7. In a normal dispersion medium, what is the relationship between phase velocity ($v_p$) and group velocity ($v_g$)?

Explanation: In normal dispersion, $\frac{dv_p}{d\lambda} < 0$, which makes $v_g = v_p - \lambda \frac{dv_p}{d\lambda}$ result in $v_p > v_g$.

8. What is the formula for the phase velocity of longitudinal acoustic waves in an ideal gas of density $\rho$, pressure $P$, and adiabatic index $\gamma$?

Explanation: Using Newton-Laplace correction, the phase velocity of sound waves in a gas is $v_p = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma P}{\rho}}$.

9. How is the angular wave number $k$ defined in terms of spatial wavelength $\lambda$?

Explanation: The angular wave number is defined as $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$, representing radians per unit length.

10. What is the phase velocity of electromagnetic waves in a dielectric medium having magnetic permeability $\mu$ and electrical permittivity $\epsilon$?

Explanation: From Maxwell's equations, the phase velocity of an electromagnetic wave in a dielectric medium is given by $v_p = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu \epsilon}}$.

11. A wave is represented by the equation $y = A \sin(50t - 2x)$. What is the phase velocity of this wave?

Explanation: Comparing with $y = A \sin(\omega t - kx)$, we get $\omega = 50$ rad/s and $k = 2$ rad/m. Thus, $v_p = \frac{\omega}{k} = \frac{50}{2} = 25$ m/s.

12. Why does the phase velocity of a wave sometimes exceed the speed of light in a vacuum without violating special relativity?

Explanation: Phase velocity corresponds to the movement of a mathematical phase point and does not carry physical energy or information; hence, no relativistic limit is violated.

13. If the angular frequency of a wave is doubled while its wave number remains constant, how does its phase velocity change?

Explanation: Since $v_p = \frac{\omega}{k}$, if $\omega$ is doubled and $k$ is constant, $v_p$ is directly doubled.

14. Which of the following is a classic example of a dispersive medium?

Explanation: Deep water gravity waves are dispersive because their phase velocity depends on wavelength ($v_p = \sqrt{\frac{g\lambda}{2\pi}}$).

15. If the wavelength of a sinusoidal wave in a non-dispersive medium is halved, what happens to its phase velocity?

Explanation: In a non-dispersive medium, phase velocity is independent of wavelength and frequency; therefore, changing wavelength while frequency adjusts accordingly leaves $v_p$ unchanged.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The phase velocity of a wave is given by $v_p = \frac{\omega}{k}$. For a wave described by $\omega = c k$ (where $c$ is a constant), what is the relationship between phase velocity and group velocity?

Explanation: If $\omega = c k$, then $v_p = \frac{\omega}{k} = c$. The group velocity is $v_g = \frac{d\omega}{dk} = c$. Thus, $v_p = v_g = c$ in this non-dispersive linear relation.

2. A transverse wave on a string has a tension of $100 \text{ N}$ and a linear mass density of $0.25 \text{ kg/m}$. What is the phase velocity of the wave?

Explanation: Using $v_p = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}} = \sqrt{\frac{100}{0.25}} = \sqrt{400} = 20 \text{ m/s}$.

3. If the dispersion relation for a certain medium is given by $\omega^2 = c^2 k^2 + \omega_0^2$, what is the group velocity $v_g$?

Explanation: Differentiating $\omega^2 = c^2 k^2 + \omega_0^2$ with respect to $k$ gives $2\omega \frac{d\omega}{dk} = 2c^2 k$, so $v_g = \frac{d\omega}{dk} = \frac{c^2 k}{\omega}$.

4. In a medium, the phase velocity is related to wavelength by $v_p = A \sqrt{\lambda}$ (where $A$ is a positive constant). What is the ratio of group velocity to phase velocity $\frac{v_g}{v_p}$?

Explanation: Here $\frac{dv_p}{d\lambda} = \frac{A}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}$. Then $v_g = v_p - \lambda \frac{dv_p}{d\lambda} = v_p - \lambda \left(\frac{A}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}\right) = v_p - \frac{1}{2}v_p = 0.5 v_p$. Thus $\frac{v_g}{v_p} = 0.5$.

5. A wave equation is given as $y(x, t) = 0.05 \sin(100\pi t - 2\pi x)$. Calculate the phase velocity of the wave.

Explanation: Here $\omega = 100\pi$ and $k = 2\pi$. Phase velocity $v_p = \frac{\omega}{k} = \frac{100\pi}{2\pi} = 50 \text{ m/s}$.

6. For deep water gravity waves, phase velocity is given by $v_p = \sqrt{\frac{g\lambda}{2\pi}}$. What is the group velocity $v_g$ in terms of phase velocity $v_p$?

Explanation: Since $v_p \propto \lambda^{1/2}$, $\frac{dv_p}{d\lambda} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{v_p}{\lambda}$. Then $v_g = v_p - \lambda \left(\frac{1}{2} \frac{v_p}{\lambda}\right) = v_p - 0.5 v_p = 0.5 v_p$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following expressions correctly defines the angular wave number $k$?

Explanation: Both definitions are valid since $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$ and from $v_p = \frac{\omega}{k}$, we get $k = \frac{\omega}{v_p}$.

2. If a medium exhibits anomalous dispersion, how does phase velocity behave with respect to wavelength?

Explanation: In anomalous dispersion, phase velocity increases with increasing wavelength, making $\frac{dv_p}{d\lambda} > 0$.

3. What is the phase velocity of a wave with frequency $50 \text{ Hz}$ and wavelength $4 \text{ m}$?

Explanation: Using $v_p = \nu \lambda = 50 \times 4 = 200 \text{ m/s}$.

4. In a dispersive medium, if group velocity $v_g$ is greater than phase velocity $v_p$, what type of dispersion is present?

Explanation: When $v_g > v_p$, $\frac{dv_p}{d\lambda} > 0$, which defines anomalous dispersion.

5. Which physical parameter remains constant when a monochromatic wave passes from one medium into another of different optical density?

Explanation: Frequency is a characteristic of the source and remains invariant when a wave crosses boundaries between media, while wavelength and phase velocity change.

6. Calculate the angular frequency of a wave whose phase velocity is $300 \text{ m/s}$ and angular wave number is $1.5 \text{ rad/m}$.

Explanation: Using $\omega = v_p k = 300 \times 1.5 = 450 \text{ rad/s}$.

7. The phase velocity of sound in an ideal gas depends on:

Explanation: Since $v_p = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma P}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma RT}{M}}$, phase velocity depends on the adiabatic index $\gamma$, absolute temperature $T$, and molecular weight $M$.

8. If the phase velocity of a wave in a string is $50 \text{ m/s}$ and the tension is quadrupled while keeping mass per unit length constant, what is the new phase velocity?

Explanation: Since $v_p = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}$, scaling tension $T$ by 4 increases $v_p$ by a factor of $\sqrt{4} = 2$. Thus, $50 \times 2 = 100 \text{ m/s}$.

9. What does the term $\lambda \frac{dv_p}{d\lambda}$ represent in the relation between group and phase velocity?

Explanation: The term $\lambda \frac{dv_p}{d\lambda}$ accounts for the correction due to medium dispersion when relating group velocity to phase velocity.

10. If a wave packet is constructed from sinusoidal components whose phase velocity is independent of wavelength, then:

Explanation: When phase velocity is independent of wavelength (non-dispersive medium), $\frac{dv_p}{d\lambda} = 0$, leading to $v_g = v_p$.
