Doppler Effect in Sound and Light

The Doppler effect is the apparent change in the frequency of a wave received by an observer due to relative motion between the source and the observer. It applies to both longitudinal waves (such as sound in a medium) and transverse waves (such as light in a vacuum).

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Apparent frequency formulas for a moving source and/or moving observer in a stationary medium; reflections from moving walls; basic numerical problem-solving.

PGT extension

General relative motion at an arbitrary angle, relativistic Doppler effect for light, longitudinal and transverse Doppler effect, and astrophysical applications.

Prerequisites

  • Wave motion and wave equation kinematics
  • Relationship between frequency, wavelength, and wave velocity
  • Galilean and Lorentz coordinate transformations

Fundamental Mechanism of Doppler Shift

  • When a source moves toward an observer, successive wave crests are emitted closer together, effectively reducing the observed wavelength.
  • When an observer moves toward a source, the rate of intercepting wave fronts increases, leading to a higher observed frequency.
  • The medium provides a fixed frame of reference for sound waves, meaning the speed of sound depends on the motion of the observer and source relative to the medium.

Asymmetry in Sound Doppler Effect

  • The apparent frequency depends on whether the source is moving or the observer is moving, even if the relative speed between them is identical.
  • For a stationary observer and moving source, the wavelength changes in the medium.
  • For a moving observer and stationary source, the wavelength in the medium remains unchanged, but the relative speed of wave interception changes.

Reflections and Echoes

  • When a sound wave reflects from a moving wall or obstacle, the wall acts first as a moving observer and then as a moving source.
  • The beat frequency heard when combining the direct and reflected sound is frequently tested in examinations.
  • Doppler radar utilizes this principle to measure the speed of moving vehicles or meteorological formations.

Relativistic Doppler Effect for Light

  • Light waves require no material medium; thus, only the relative velocity between source and observer matters.
  • Longitudinal relativistic Doppler shift accounts for both time dilation and the classical frequency shift.
  • Transverse Doppler effect is purely a relativistic phenomenon arising entirely from time dilation.

Derivations

General Formula for Sound Doppler Effect

Conditions: Source and observer moving along the line joining them in a stationary medium

  • Let v be the speed of sound, v_s be the speed of the source, and v_o be tobacco/observer speed. Let f be the source frequency.
  • If the source moves toward a stationary observer at speed v_s, the effective wavelength becomes lambda' = (v - v_s) / f.
  • The observed frequency is f' = v / lambda' = v / [(v - v_s) / f] = f (v / (v - v_s)).
  • If the observer also moves toward the source at speed v_o, the relative velocity of waves with respect to the observer is v + v_o.
  • Combining both motions yields the general expression: f' = f * rac{v pm v_o}{v mp v_s}.

Result: f' = f * rac{v pm v_o}{v mp v_s}

Relativistic Longitudinal Doppler Effect for Light

Conditions: Source and observer moving directly toward or away from each other in free space

  • Consider a source emitting light of frequency f in its proper frame. In the observer's frame, time dilation stretches the period by a factor of gamma.
  • Account for the propagation delay as the source moves closer or further away during emission of successive cycles.
  • Combine the Doppler factor with the Lorentz transformation factor sqrt( rac{1 - eta}{1 + eta}), where eta = v / c.
  • For a source approaching the observer: f' = f sqrt{ rac{1 + eta}{1 - eta}}.

Result: f' = f sqrt{ rac{1 + eta}{1 - eta}}

Interpret: As the train approaches, f' is constant and higher than f. At the instant of crossing, there is an abrupt step-down to a constant lower frequency as it recedes.

Solved numericals

TGT core

A source of sound emitting a frequency of 500 Hz moves toward a stationary observer with a velocity of 30 m/s. If the velocity of sound in air is 330 m/s, calculate the apparent frequency heard by the observer.

  • Identify given parameters: f = 500 Hz, v_s = 30 m/s, v_o = 0, v = 330 m/s.
  • Select the appropriate formula for a moving source and stationary observer: f' = f * [v / (v - v_s)].
  • Substitute the values: f' = 500 * [330 / (330 - 30)].
  • Simplify the denominator: 330 - 30 = 300.
  • Calculate the result: f' = 500 * (330 / 300) = 500 * 1.1 = 550 Hz.

Answer: 550 Hz

TGT core

An observer is moving with a velocity of 20 m/s toward a stationary source emitting sound of frequency 400 Hz. What is the apparent frequency heard? (Speed of sound = 340 m/s).

  • Identify parameters: f = 400 Hz, v_o = 20 m/s (moving toward), v_s = 0, v = 340 m/s.
  • Use formula for moving observer: f' = f * [(v + v_o) / v].
  • Substitute values: f' = 400 * [(340 + 20) / 340].
  • Simplify: f' = 400 * (360 / 340) = 400 * (36 / 34) = 423.53 Hz.

Answer: 423.53 Hz

PGT extension

A star is moving away from the Earth with a speed of 3.0 * 10^6 m/s. What is the fractional change in the wavelength of a spectral line observed from the star? (Speed of light c = 3.0 * 10^8 m/s).

  • Identify parameters: v = 3.0 * 10^6 m/s, c = 3.0 * 10^8 m/s.
  • Use the Doppler formula for light at low velocities: Delta_lambda / lambda = v / c.
  • Substitute the values: Delta_lambda / lambda = (3.0 * 10^6) / (3.0 * 10^8).
  • Calculate the ratio: Delta_lambda / lambda = 10^-2 = 0.01.

Answer: 0.01 or 1%

Assumptions and limitations

  • The medium is assumed to be uniform, homogeneous, and stationary unless wind velocity is explicitly factored in.
  • The speeds of the source and observer must be less than the wave propagation speed in the medium (v_s < v) to avoid shock wave formation (Mach cone).
  • Classical acoustic Doppler formulas do not account for relativistic effects, which become necessary only at extreme astrophysical velocities.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The apparent frequency depends only on the relative speed between the source and observer.

Correct: For sound waves, the apparent frequency depends separately on the individual velocities of the source and observer relative to the medium.

The medium establishes a preferred reference frame for acoustic waves, violating simple Galilean velocity addition symmetry.

Common error: At the exact instant the source passes the observer, the observed frequency equals the true frequency.

Correct: The observed frequency experiences an abrupt drop at the crossing point because the component of velocity along the line of sight changes sign instantaneously.

Finite propagation delay and transverse velocity components dictate that the transition is discontinuous in idealized models.

Common error: Light experiences the same Doppler formula as sound because both are wave phenomena.

Correct: Light requires no medium and is governed by relativistic kinematics, resulting in a different mathematical formula.

Electromagnetic waves obey the theory of special relativity, incorporating time dilation and Lorentz contraction.

Quick revision

  • Apparent frequency increases when source/observer approach each other and decreases when they recede.
  • A moving source alters the wavelength in the medium, whereas a moving observer alters the rate of wave interception.
  • The relativistic transverse Doppler effect is a purely kinematic consequence of time dilation with no classical acoustic analog.

Must remember

  • For sound waves, the apparent frequency depends separately on the individual velocities of the source and observer relative to the medium, illustrating acoustic asymmetry.
  • The general frequency formula for sound is $f' = f \left( \frac{v \pm v_o}{v \mp v_s} \right)$, where upper signs correspond to approaching and lower to receding motions.
  • When a sound wave reflects from a moving wall, the wall acts first as a moving observer and then as a moving source, producing a beat frequency of $f_b \approx \frac{2 v_s f}{v}$.
  • Light waves require no material medium; hence, the relativistic longitudinal Doppler effect incorporates time dilation via $f' = f \sqrt{\frac{1 \pm \beta}{1 \mp \beta}}$.
  • The transverse Doppler effect is a purely relativistic phenomenon arising entirely from time dilation, given by $f' = f \sqrt{1 - \beta^2}$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the general expression for the apparent frequency $f'$ heard by an observer moving with speed $v_o$ towards a stationary source emitting frequency $f$?

Explanation: When an observer moves towards a stationary source, the rate of intercepting wave fronts increases, giving $f' = f \left( \frac{v + v_o}{v} \right)$.

2. Which of the following best explains why the sound Doppler effect exhibits asymmetry between source and observer motion?

Explanation: The medium establishes a preferred reference frame for acoustic waves, meaning swapping source and observer speeds yields different apparent frequencies.

3. An acoustic source emits a frequency $f$ and moves towards a stationary observer at speed $v_s$. What is the apparent wavelength $\lambda'$ in the medium?

Explanation: When a source moves towards a point of observation, successive wave crests are emitted closer together, reducing the wavelength to $\lambda' = \lambda \left( 1 - \frac{v_s}{v} \right)$.

4. When a sound wave reflects normally from a wall moving towards the source with speed $v_s$, what is the approximate beat frequency heard between direct and reflected sound?

Explanation: The wall acts first as a moving observer and then as a moving source, resulting in a beat frequency of $f_b \approx \frac{2 v_s f}{v}$ for $v_s \ll v$.

5. What is the formula for the longitudinal relativistic Doppler effect for light when the source and observer are receding from each other?

Explanation: For receding motion in the longitudinal relativistic Doppler effect, the frequency formula is $f' = f \sqrt{\frac{1 - \$\beta$}{1 + \$\beta$}}$.

6. The transverse Doppler effect for light is a consequence of:

Explanation: The transverse Doppler effect is purely a relativistic phenomenon arising entirely from time dilation when motion is perpendicular to the line of sight.

7. For light waves moving at velocities much less than the speed of light ($v \ll c$), the fractional shift in wavelength $\frac{\Delta \lambda}{\lambda}$ is approximately equal to:

Explanation: For low velocities ($v \ll c$), the first-order approximation yields $\frac{\Delta \lambda}{\lambda} \approx \frac{v}{c}$.

8. What happens to the observed frequency of sound when both the source and observer move towards each other?

Explanation: Approaching motion in both source and observer increases the observed frequency because wave crests are intercepted at a faster rate.

9. In the general Doppler formula for sound $f' = f \left( \frac{v \pm v_o}{v \mp v_s} \right)$, what condition must be satisfied by the speeds $v_o$ and $v_s$?

Explanation: The standard acoustic Doppler equations assume subsonic speeds where $v_o < v$ and $v_s < v$.

10. Why does the Doppler effect for light depend only on the relative velocity between source and observer?

Explanation: Since electromagnetic waves require no material medium, there is no preferred reference frame, so only relative velocity matters.

11. If a source of sound moves away from a stationary observer, how does the observed frequency compare to the emitted frequency?

Explanation: When a source moves away, successive wave crests are emitted farther apart, leading to an apparent frequency lower than the true frequency.

12. What is the formula for the transverse Doppler effect in light?

Explanation: The transverse Doppler effect frequency formula is $f' = f \sqrt{1 - \$\beta$^2}$, reflecting pure relativistic time dilation.

13. Which UP TGT/PGT concept is highlighted by Doppler radar used in meteorological formations?

Explanation: Doppler radar measures the speed of moving formations by analyzing the frequency shift of reflected waves (echoes).

14. If an observer moves away from a stationary source, the apparent frequency is given by:

Explanation: An observer moving away intercepts fewer wave fronts per unit time, resulting in $f' = f \left( \frac{v - v_o}{v} \right)$.

15. What is the primary difference in formula structure between longitudinal sound Doppler and longitudinal relativistic light Doppler?

Explanation: Sound relies on linear velocity ratios in a medium, while relativistic light Doppler utilizes square root terms of $(1 \pm \$\beta$) / (1 \mp \$\beta$)$.

1. A whistle emitting a sound of frequency $450\text{ Hz}$ approaches a stationary observer with a speed of $30\text{ m/s}$. If the speed of sound is $330\text{ m/s}$, what is the apparent frequency heard by the observer?

Explanation: Using $f' = f \left( \frac{v}{v - v_s} \right) = 450 \left( \frac{330}{330 - 30} \right) = 450 \left( \frac{330}{300} \right) = 450 \times 1.1 = 495\text{ Hz}$$.

2. An observer is moving with a speed of $20\text{ m/s}$ towards a stationary sound source of frequency $600\text{ Hz}$. Taking the speed of sound to be $340\text{ m/s}$, the apparent frequency heard by the observer is:

4. A star is moving away from the Earth with a speed of $3000\text{ km/s}$. What is the fractional shift in wavelength $\frac{\Delta \lambda}{\lambda}$ of the emitted light?

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. In the Doppler effect for sound, if both source and observer move in the same direction with the same speed relative to the medium, what is the apparent frequency?

Explanation: When source and observer move with identical velocities relative to the medium, relative motion between them is zero and the apparent frequency equals the true frequency.

2. A sound source emits a frequency of $1000\text{ Hz}$ and moves towards a wall at $20\text{ m/s}$. What is the frequency of the sound reflected off the wall as heard by a stationary observer behind the source? (Speed of sound $= 320\text{ m/s}$)

Explanation: The wall receives frequency $f' = 1000 \left(\frac{320}{320 - 20}\right) = 1066.6\text{ Hz}$, which is then reflected back towards the stationary observer.

3. What happens to the observed wavelength of sound when the observer moves towards a stationary source?

Explanation: When only the observer moves, the source is stationary in the medium, so the wavelength of the sound waves in the medium remains completely unchanged.

4. A rocket moves with speed $0.8c$ ($v = 0.8c$) perpendicular to the line of sight of an observer. What is the transverse Doppler frequency shift factor $f'/f$?

Explanation: For the transverse Doppler effect, $f'/f = \sqrt{1 - \$\beta$^2} = \sqrt{1 - (0.8)^2} = \sqrt{1 - 0.64} = \sqrt{0.36} = 0.6$.

5. Which acoustic phenomenon is directly responsible for the 'warble' or pulsing sound heard when a car with its horn sounding passes an observer?

Explanation: The abrupt frequency transition and potential reflection interference create the characteristic drop and modulation in observed pitch.

6. If the speed of sound in air is $v$, and a source moves at speed $v$ (Mach 1) towards an observer, what does the apparent frequency formula predict?

Explanation: When $v_s = v$, the denominator $v - v_s$ becomes zero, causing the apparent frequency to approach infinity (sonic boom formation).

7. A radar system operates at frequency $f = 3 \times 10^9\text{ Hz}$. If a vehicle moving towards the radar reflects the signal, what determines the frequency of the echo?

Explanation: Radar reflections involve a double Doppler shift because the wave travels to a moving target and then the target reflects it back as a moving source.

8. In the relativistic longitudinal Doppler effect, if $\$\beta$ \ll 1$, the expression $f' = f \sqrt{\frac{1 + \$\beta$}{1 - \$\beta$}}$ reduces approximately to:

Explanation: Using binomial expansion for small $\$\beta$$, $\sqrt{\frac{1+\$\beta$}{1-\$\beta$}} \approx (1+\$\beta$)$, yielding $f' \approx f(1 + \$\beta$)$.

9. Which of the following statements is correct regarding the Doppler effect for light versus sound?

Explanation: The fundamental distinction is that light requires no medium and is governed purely by relative velocity and special relativity.

10. An ambulance emits a siren at $500\text{ Hz}$ and travels at $20\text{ m/s}$ towards a cliff. What is the frequency of the echo heard by the ambulance driver if the speed of sound is $340\text{ m/s}$?

Explanation: The cliff receives frequency $f' = 500 \left(\frac{340}{340 - 20}\right) = 531.25\text{ Hz}$, which reflects back to the moving ambulance acting as a moving observer: $f'' = 531.25 \left(\frac{340 + 20}{340}\right) \approx 562.5\text{ Hz}$.
