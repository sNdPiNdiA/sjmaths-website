Behaviour of Ideal Gas

An ideal gas is a theoretical gas composed of a set of randomly moving point particles that interact only through elastic collisions. It strictly obeys the universal gas equation under all conditions of temperature and pressure.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Boyle's Law, Charles's Law, Gay-Lussac's Law, ideal gas equation PV = nRT, universal gas constant, and basic absolute temperature scale.

PGT extension

Postulates of Kinetic Theory of Gases

  • All gases consist of a very large number of identical, rigid, spherical molecules moving in random directions with all possible speeds.
  • The actual volume occupied by the gas molecules is negligible compared to the total volume of the container.
  • Collisions between molecules and with the container walls are perfectly elastic, conserving both total momentum and kinetic energy.

Equation of State for an Ideal Gas

  • The macroscopic behavior of an ideal gas is summarized by the equation PV = nRT, where n is the number of moles.
  • Alternatively, in terms of Boltzmann's constant, PV = NkT, where N is the total number of molecules and k = R/N_A.
  • The constant R has a value of approximately 8.314 J mol<sup>-1</sup> K<sup>-1</sup>.

Kinetic Interpretation of Pressure

  • Pressure arises due to the continuous bombardment of gas molecules against the walls of the container.
  • The pressure exerted by an ideal gas is given by P = (1/3)mnv<sup>2</sup>_{rms}, where m is molecular mass, n is number density, and v_{rms} is root mean square velocity.
  • Pressure depends directly on the translational kinetic energy per unit volume of the gas.

Absolute Temperature and Kinetic Energy

  • Temperature is a direct measure of the average translational kinetic energy of gas molecules.
  • For a monoatomic gas, the average kinetic energy per molecule is given by E = (3/2)kT.
  • At absolute zero (T = 0 K), the average kinetic energy of gas molecules theoretically drops to zero.

Derivations

Pressure Exerted by an Ideal Gas

Conditions: N identical molecules in a cubical container of side L

  • Consider a molecule of mass m moving with velocity component v_x along the x-axis inside a cube of volume L<sup>3</sup>.
  • The change in momentum when the molecule undergoes an elastic collision with the wall perpendicular to the x-axis is Delta p_x = 2mv_x.
  • The time interval between successive collisions with the same wall is Delta t = 2L / v_x.
  • The force exerted by a single molecule on the wall is F_x = Delta p_x / Delta t = mv_x<sup>2</sup> / L.
  • Summing over all N molecules and taking the isotropic average v_x<sup>2</sup> = (1/3)v<sup>2</sup>_{rms}, the total pressure is P = F / L<sup>2</sup> = (1/3) (Nm/V) v<sup>2</sup>_{rms}.

Result: P = \frac{1}{3} \rho v_{rms}^2

Relation between Pressure and Kinetic Energy

Conditions: Ideal gas containing N molecules in volume V

  • Start from the kinetic theory expression for pressure: P = (1/3) (Nm/V) v<sup>2</sup>_{rms}.
  • Multiply and divide the right-hand side by 2 to introduce kinetic energy: P = (2/3) \times (1/2) (Nm v<sup>2</sup>_{rms}) / V.
  • Recognize that the total translational kinetic energy of the gas is E = (1/2) Nm v<sup>2</sup>_{rms}.
  • Substitute E into the pressure equation to obtain P = (2/3) (E / V).

Result: PV = \frac{2}{3} E

Isobars on P-T Diagram

Draw: Straight lines passing through the origin (or extrapolating to -273.15 °C on Celsius scale) with different slopes for different pressures.

Interpret: Illustrates Charles's law where volume is directly proportional to absolute temperature.

Exam use: Finding slopes representing V/R ratios and determining absolute zero temperature.

Maxwell-Boltzmann Speed Distribution Curve

Draw: Asymmetric bell-shaped curve plotting fraction of molecules against molecular speed, showing distinct peaks for most probable, average, and rms speeds.

Interpret: Demonstrates statistical distribution of molecular speeds at a given temperature.

Exam use: Relating peak position to temperature and distinguishing v_{mp}, v_{avg}, and v_{rms}.

Solved numericals

TGT core

Find the root mean square velocity of oxygen molecules at 27 °C. Given R = 8.314 J mol&lt;sup&gt;-1&lt;/sup&gt; K&lt;sup&gt;-1&lt;/sup&gt; and molar mass of oxygen M = 32 × 10&lt;sup&gt;-3&lt;/sup&gt; kg mol&lt;sup&gt;-1&lt;/sup&gt;.

  • Convert temperature to Kelvin: T = 27 + 273 = 300 K.
  • Use the formula v_{rms} = \sqrt{3RT / M}.
  • Substitute values: v_{rms} = \sqrt{(3 \times 8.314 \times 300) / (32 \times 10<sup>-3</sup>)}.
  • Calculate the value: v_{rms} = \sqrt{74826 / 0.032} = \sqrt{2338312.5} \approx 483.5 \text{ m s<sup>-1</sup>}.

Answer: 483.5 m s^-1

PGT extension

Calculate the total translational kinetic energy of 2 moles of an ideal gas at 300 K. Take R = 8.314 J mol&lt;sup&gt;-1&lt;/sup&gt; K&lt;sup&gt;-1&lt;/sup&gt;.

  • Recall the formula for total internal kinetic energy of n moles of an ideal gas: E = (3/2) nRT.
  • Substitute the given values: n = 2, R = 8.314, T = 300.
  • E = (3/2) \times 2 \times 8.314 \times 300.
  • E = 3 \times 8.314 \times 300 = 7482.6 \text{ J}.

Answer: 7482.6 J

TGT core

A given mass of an ideal gas occupies 400 mL at 27 °C and 700 mm Hg pressure. What will be its volume at standard temperature and pressure (0 °C and 760 mm Hg)?

  • Identify initial states: P_1 = 700 mm Hg, V_1 = 400 mL, T_1 = 27 + 273 = 300 K.
  • Identify final states: P_2 = 760 mm Hg, T_2 = 0 + 273 = 273 K, V_2 = ?.
  • Use combined gas law: (P_1 V_1) / T_1 = (P_2 V_2) / T_2.
  • Rearrange for V_2: V_2 = (P_1 V_1 T_2) / (P_2 T_1) = (700 \times 400 \times 273) / (760 \times 300).
  • Calculate result: V_2 = 76440000 / 228000 \approx 335.26 \text{ mL}.

Answer: 335.26 mL

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumption: Intermolecular forces are completely zero except during instantaneous elastic collisions. Limitation: Fails at high pressures where molecular volume and attractive forces become significant.
  • Assumption: Size of molecules is infinitesimally small compared to interparticle distance. Limitation: Fails at low temperatures near liquefaction point where real gases deviate heavily from ideal behavior.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Temperature is a measure of the total kinetic energy of all molecules in a body.

Correct: Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy per molecule, not the total sum.

A large pool of water at low temperature has more total kinetic energy than a boiling drop of water, yet its temperature is lower.

Common error: Ideal gas molecules lose energy during wall collisions.

Correct: Collisions with container walls are perfectly elastic.

By definition, kinetic energy is conserved in ideal gas collisions; otherwise, gas pressure would continuously decay over time.

Quick revision

  • PV = nRT governs macroscopic ideal gas behavior, where R is the universal gas constant.
  • Ideal Gas Equation: PV = nRT
  • Molecular Ideal Gas Equation: PV = NkT

Must remember

  • The macroscopic behavior of an ideal gas is governed by the equation $PV = nRT$.
  • Collisions between ideal gas molecules and container walls are perfectly elastic, conserving both momentum and kinetic energy.
  • Absolute temperature ($T$) is a direct measure of the average translational kinetic energy per molecule ($E = \frac{3}{2}kT$).
  • Root mean square velocity of gas molecules is given by $v_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}} = \sqrt{\frac{3kT}{m}}$.
  • At absolute zero ($T = 0\text{ K}$), the average kinetic energy of gas molecules theoretically drops to zero.

Glossary

Ideal GasA hypothetical gas that perfectly obeys the gas laws ($PV = nRT$) under all conditions of temperature and pressure, with negligible molecular volume and no intermolecular forces.
Root Mean Square Velocity ($v_{rms}$)The square root of the average of the squares of the speeds of the individual molecules in a gas sample.
Boltzmann's Constant ($k$)The gas constant per molecule, defined as $k = \frac{R}{N_A}$, equal to $1.38 \times 10^{-23}\text{ J K}^{-1}$.
Absolute TemperatureTemperature measured on the Kelvin scale, starting from absolute zero where molecular motion theoretically ceases.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following equations represents the ideal gas law for $N$ molecules?

Explanation: In terms of the total number of molecules $N$ and Boltzmann's constant $k$, the ideal gas equation is written as $PV = NkT$.

2. Collisions between gas molecules and the container walls are assumed to be:

Explanation: Postulates of kinetic theory state that collisions between molecules and container walls are perfectly elastic, conserving both total momentum and kinetic energy.

3. What is the average kinetic energy of a monoatomic ideal gas molecule at absolute temperature $T$?

Explanation: For a monoatomic gas, the average kinetic energy per molecule is given by $E = \frac{3}{2}kT$.

4. Root mean square velocity of gas molecules is directly proportional to:

Explanation: Since $v_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$, the root mean square velocity is directly proportional to the square root of absolute temperature ($\sqrt{T}$).

5. Which law states that at constant temperature, the volume of a given mass of gas is inversely proportional to its pressure?

Explanation: Boyle's Law states that $$P_{1}$ $V_{1}$ = $P_{2}$ $V_{2}$$, implying that pressure and volume are inversely proportional at constant temperature.

6. What is the value and unit of the universal gas constant $R$?

Explanation: The universal gas constant $R$ has an approximate value of $8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$.

7. According to the kinetic interpretation of pressure, pressure is directly proportional to:

Explanation: Pressure is given by $P = \frac{1}{3}mnv_{rms}^2 = \frac{2}{3}E_{v}$, showing it depends directly on translational kinetic energy per unit volume.

8. At absolute zero ($T = 0\text{ K}$), what happens to the average kinetic energy of gas molecules?

Explanation: At $T = 0\text{ K}$, the average translational kinetic energy of gas molecules theoretically drops to zero, corresponding to complete cessation of thermal motion.

9. Which of the following conditions favors the validity of the ideal gas equation $PV = nRT$?

Explanation: Gases behave most like ideal gases at low pressures and high temperatures, where intermolecular forces and molecular volumes are negligible.

10. Charles's Law maintains which of the following quantities constant?

Explanation: Charles's Law states that $\frac{$V_{1}$}{T_1} = \frac{$V_{2}$}{T_2}$ under conditions of constant pressure and mass.

11. Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures states that the total pressure of a mixture of non-reacting gases is equal to:

Explanation: Dalton's Law states that $P = $P_{1}$ + $P_{2}$ + $P_{3}$ + \dots$ for non-reacting gas mixtures.

12. What does temperature measure microscopically in an ideal gas?

Explanation: Temperature is a direct measure of the average translational kinetic energy per molecule, not the total energy.

13. Gay-Lussac's Law relates which two variables at constant volume and mass?

Explanation: Gay-Lussac's Law establishes that $\frac{$P_{1}$}{T_1} = \frac{$P_{2}$}{T_2}$ when volume and mass are held constant.

14. If the absolute temperature of an ideal gas is quadrupled, its root mean square velocity will:

Explanation: Since $v_{rms} \propto \sqrt{T}$, quadrupling $T$ causes $v_{rms}$ to increase by a factor of $\sqrt{4} = 2$.

15. Which of the following is a primary assumption of the kinetic theory regarding gas molecules?

Explanation: A core postulate of kinetic theory is that the actual volume occupied by gas molecules is negligible compared to the total volume of the container.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The root mean square velocity of hydrogen molecules at $300\text{ K}$ is $v$. At what temperature will the root mean square velocity be double ($2v$)?

Explanation: Since $v_{rms} \propto \sqrt{T}$, doubling the velocity ($v_{rms} \to 2v$) requires quadrupling the absolute temperature ($T \to 4T$). Therefore, $T_2 = 4 \times 300\text{ K} = 1200\text{ K}$? Wait, $2^2 = 4$, so $4 \times 300 = 1200\text{ K}$. Let's verify options: $1200\text{ K}$ is index 2.

2. If the pressure of an ideal gas in a closed container is increased by $0.5\%$ at constant volume, the percentage increase in temperature is:

Explanation: By Gay-Lussac's law at constant volume, $P \propto T$. Therefore, fractional change $\frac{\Delta P}{P} = \frac{\Delta T}{T} = 0.5\%$.

3. The kinetic energy of $1\text{ mole}$ of an ideal monoatomic gas at temperature $T$ is given by:

Explanation: Energy per molecule is $\frac{3}{2}kT$. For one mole containing $N_A$ molecules, total kinetic energy is $N_A \times \frac{3}{2}kT = \frac{3}{2}R T$ since $R = N_A k$.

4. Two identical vessels contain equal masses of helium (molecular weight $4$) and hydrogen (molecular weight $2$) at the same temperature. The ratio of pressure exerted by helium to hydrogen is:

Explanation: Using $PV = \frac{m}{M}RT$, pressure $P \propto \frac{1}{M}$ for equal mass $m$, volume $V$, and temperature $T$. Thus $\frac{P_{He}}{P_{H_2}} = \frac{M_{H_2}}{M_{He}} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$.

5. At what temperature is the root mean square velocity of an oxygen molecule equal to $1000\text{ m s}^{-1}$? (Given molar mass of oxygen is $32\times 10^{-3}\text{ kg mol}^{-1}$, $R = 8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$)

Explanation: From $v_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$, $T = \frac{M v_{rms}^2}{3R} = \frac{32 \times 10^{-3} \times (1000)^2}{3 \times 8.314} \approx 1282\text{ K}$.

6. A gas mixture consists of $2$ moles of oxygen and $4$ moles of argon at temperature $T$. Considering only translational degrees of freedom, the total internal energy of the system is:

Explanation: Both oxygen and argon are treated as ideal gases with translational energy $\frac{3}{2}RT$ per mole. Total moles $n = 2 + 4 = 6$ moles. Total energy $U = n \left(\frac{3}{2}RT\right) = 6 \times \frac{3}{2}RT = 9RT$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. The pressure $P$ of an ideal gas is related to its density $\rho$ and root mean square velocity $v_{rms}$ by:

Explanation: Since $P = \frac{1}{3}mnv_{rms}^2$ and mass density $\rho = mn$, substituting gives $P = \frac{1}{3}\rho v_{rms}^2$.

2. Which of the following statements is incorrect regarding ideal gas molecules?

Explanation: An ideal gas has no intermolecular forces, hence its potential energy is zero and internal energy depends entirely on temperature (kinetic energy).

3. If the root mean square speed of gas molecules is $v$ at pressure $P$ and density $\rho$, what happens to $v_{rms}$ if pressure is doubled at constant density?

Explanation: From $P = \frac{1}{3}\rho v_{rms}^2$, $v_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3P}{\rho}}$. If $P$ is doubled while $\rho$ is constant, $v_{rms}$ increases by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$.

4. At absolute zero temperature, the root mean square velocity of gas molecules becomes:

Explanation: Since $v_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$, when $T = 0\text{ K}$, $v_{rms}$ becomes zero.

5. The average kinetic energy of $2\text{ moles}$ of an ideal gas at $27^{\circ}\text{C}$ is: (Given $R = 8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$)

Explanation: Temperature $T = 27 + 273 = 300\text{ K}$. Total kinetic energy for $n$ moles is $E = \frac{3}{2}nRT = \frac{3}{2} \times 2 \times 8.314 \times 300 = 3 \times 8.314 \times 300 = 7482\text{ J}$? Wait, $3 \times 8.314 = 24.942$, $24.942 \times 300 = 7482\text{ J}$. Let's check index 1 for $7482\text{ J}$.

6. A cylinder contains $5\text{ L}$ of ideal gas at $2\text{ atm}$ pressure. If the volume is compressed to $2\text{ L}$ at constant temperature, the new pressure is:

Explanation: By Boyle's Law ($$P_{1}$ $V_{1}$ = $P_{2}$ $V_{2}$$), $2 \times 5 = $P_{2}$ \times 2 \implies $P_{2}$ = 5\text{ atm}$.

7. The kinetic theory of gases explains pressure as resulting from:

Explanation: Pressure arises directly from the continuous bombardment and momentum exchange of gas molecules against container walls.

8. Which parameter remains constant during an isothermal process described by Boyle's Law?

Explanation: An isothermal process occurs at constant temperature ($T = \text{constant}$).

9. What is the ratio of root mean square velocities of hydrogen ($H_2$, molar mass $2$) and oxygen ($O_2$, molar mass $32$) at the same temperature?

Explanation: Since $v_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$, $\frac{v_{H_2}}{v_{O_2}} = \sqrt{\frac{M_{O_2}}{M_{H_2}}} = \sqrt{\frac{32}{2}} = \sqrt{16} = 4:1$.

10. The number of degrees of freedom for a monoatomic ideal gas molecule is:

Explanation: A monoatomic ideal gas molecule has $3$ translational degrees of freedom.
