Mean Free Path in Gases

The mean free path is the average distance traveled by a gas molecule between successive collisions with other molecules. It depends inversely on the molecular number density and the effective collision cross-sectional area.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition, formula, dependence on pressure, temperature, and molecular diameter for Maxwellian distribution of molecules.

PGT extension

Rigorous derivation including the Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution correction factor (sqrt(2) denominator), collision frequency, and transport phenomena relationships.

Prerequisites

  • Kinetic theory of gases and ideal gas laws
  • Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution
  • Concept of molecular collision cross-section

Definition and Physical Origin

  • Molecules in a gas undergo continuous chaotic motion, colliding elastically with one another.
  • Between collisions, a molecule travels in a straight line with uniform velocity.
  • The mean free path (lambda) represents the statistical average of these free path lengths over time or over an ensemble of molecules.

Dependence on Thermodynamic Parameters

  • Mean free path is directly proportional to temperature (T) at constant pressure.
  • Mean free path is inversely proportional to pressure (P) at constant temperature.
  • At a given temperature and pressure, lambda is inversely proportional to the square of the molecular diameter (d^2).

Collision Frequency and Time

  • Collision frequency (f or nu) is defined as the average number of collisions suffered by a single molecule per unit time.
  • It is given by the ratio of the mean molecular speed to the mean free path.
  • The mean free time between collisions is the reciprocal of the collision frequency.

Role in Transport Phenomena

  • Mean free path is a fundamental parameter in determining transport coefficients such as viscosity, thermal conductivity, and diffusion.
  • The coefficient of viscosity of a gas is directly proportional to the product of density, mean molecular speed, and mean free path.
  • Transport coefficients are independent of pressure over moderate ranges because density and mean free path compensate each other.

Derivations

Clausius Derivation (Stationary Target Molecules)

Conditions: Assuming all molecules except one are at rest

  • Consider a molecule of diameter d moving with average speed vbar through other stationary identical molecules of number density n.
  • The moving molecule sweeps out a cylindrical volume of cross-sectional area pi d^2 per unit time, clearing out a path of length vbar.
  • The number of collisions in time t is equal to the number of stationary molecules present in this swept volume: $N_c = n \pi d^2 vbar t$.
  • The mean free path lambda is the total distance traveled divided by the total number of collisions: lambd$a = (vbar t) / (n \pi d^2 vbar t) = 1 / (n \pi d^2)$.

Result: lambd$a = 1 / (n \pi d^2)$

Maxwell Correction (Maxwellian Velocity Distribution)

Conditions: Accounting for the random thermal motion of all colliding molecules

  • When all molecules are in motion, the relative velocity v_rel between two colliding molecules must be considered instead of the average speed vbar.
  • For an isotropic Maxwellian distribution, the mean relative speed is related to the root-mean-square or average speed by $v_rel = \sqrt{2} vbar$.
  • Substituting the relative velocity into the collision rate equation modifies the effective collision volume.
  • The corrected expression incorporates an additional factor of 1 / sqrt{2} in the denominator.

Result: lambd$a = 1 / (\sqrt{2} \pi n d^2)$

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the mean free path of gas molecules at a temperature of 27 deg C and pressure of 1.01 x 10^5 N/m^2, given that the molecular diameter is 2 x 10^-10 m and Boltzmann constant is 1.38 x 10^-23 J/K.

  • Identify given parameters: $T = 27 + 273 = 300 K$, $P = 1.01 x 10^5 Pa$, $d = 2 x 10^-10 m$, $k_B = 1.38 x 10^-23 J/K$.
  • Substitute values into the formula lambd$a = (k_B T) / (\sqrt(2) \pi d^2 P)$.
  • Calculate denominator: sqrt(2) * 3.1416 * (2 x 10^-10)^2 * (1.01 x 10^5) = 1.414 * 3.1416 * 4 x 10^-20 * 1.01 x 10^5 = 5.67 x 10^-15$.
  • Calculate numerator: 1.38 x 10^-23 * $300 = 4.14 x 10^-21$.
  • Evaluate lambda: (4.14 x 10^-21) / (5.67 x 10^-15) = 7.3 x 10^-7 m.

Answer: 7.3 x 10^-7 m

PGT extension

If the pressure of an ideal gas is doubled while its absolute temperature is increased by four times, by what factor does the mean free path of the molecules change?

  • Recall the formula relating mean free path to pressure and temperature: lambda is proportional to T / P.
  • Let initial state be T_1 and P_1, so lambda_1 is proportional to T_1 / P_1.
  • In the final state, $T_2 = 4 T_1$ and $P_2 = 2 P_1$.
  • Write final mean free path: lambda_2 is proportional to T_2 / $P_2 = (4 T_1) / (2 P_1) = 2 (T_1 / P_1)$.
  • Determine the ratio: lambda_2 / lambd$a_1 = 2$.

Answer: Increases by a factor of 2

PGT extension

Estimate the collision frequency of a nitrogen molecule in air at standard temperature and pressure (STP). Given: molecular diameter $d = 3.1 x 10^-10 m$, number density $n = 2.7 x 10^25 m^-3$, and root-mean-square speed $v_rms = 500 m/s$.

  • Identify given parameters: $n = 2.7 x 10^25 m^-3$, $d = 3.1 x 10^-10 m$, $v_rms = 500 m/s (approximating average speed vbar roughly equal$ to v_rms for order estimation).
  • Write formula for collision frequency: $f = \sqrt(2) \pi n d^2 v_rms$.
  • Compute d^2: (3.1 x 10^-10)^2 = 9.61 x 10^-20 m^2$.
  • Compute product: 1.414 * 3.1416 * (2.7 x 10^25) * (9.61 x 10^-20) * 500.
  • Evaluate f: approximately 5.8 x 10^9 s^-1.

Answer: 5.8 x 10^9 s^-1

Assumptions and limitations

  • Molecules are treated as hard, smooth, perfectly elastic spheres, which neglects intermolecular potential wells and quantum mechanical effects.
  • The derivation assumes Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution, valid only in thermal equilibrium at moderate densities and temperatures.
  • At extremely high pressures or near liquefaction, the volume occupied by the molecules becomes comparable to container volume, invalidating ideal gas simplifications.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Mean free path depends directly on the density of the gas in such a way that it increases with increasing density.

Correct: Mean free path is inversely proportional to the number density of the gas.

More molecules per unit volume lead to more frequent collisions, thereby shortening the average distance traveled between collisions.

Common error: Viscosity of a gas depends strongly on pressure because the mean free path changes with pressure.

Correct: The viscosity of a gas is independent of pressure over wide ranges.

Although lambda is inversely proportional to pressure, density is directly proportional to pressure, and their product remains constant in the viscosity expression.

Quick revision

  • Lambd$a = 1 / (\sqrt(2) \pi n d^2) = k_B T / (\sqrt(2) \pi d^2 P)$.
  • Mean Free Path (Maxwell Corrected): -$\lambda = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} \pi n d^2}$
  • Mean Free Path in terms of Pressure and Temperature: -$\lambda = \frac{k_B T}{\sqrt{2} \pi d^2 P}$

Must remember

  • The mean free path $\lambda$ is given by the Maxwell corrected formula $\lambda = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} \pi n d^2}$.
  • At constant pressure, mean free path is directly proportional to absolute temperature $T$.
  • At constant temperature, mean free path is inversely proportional to pressure $P$ and number density $n$.
  • Collision frequency $f$ is defined as the average number of collisions per unit time and equals $\frac{\bar{v}}{\lambda}$.
  • The coefficient of viscosity $\eta$ and thermal conductivity $K$ of a gas are independent of pressure over moderate ranges.

Glossary

Mean Free Path ($\lambda$)The statistical average distance a gas molecule travels between successive collisions.
Collision Frequency ($f$)The average number of collisions experienced by a single gas molecule per unit time.
Number Density ($n$)The number of molecules present per unit volume of the gas.
Molecular Diameter ($d$)The effective diameter of a gas molecule treated as a hard sphere for collision cross-section.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the dependence of the mean free path $\lambda$ of a gas molecule on its absolute temperature $T$ at constant pressure?

Explanation: From the equation $\lambda = \frac{k_B T}{\sqrt{2} \pi d^2 P}$, at constant pressure $P$, $\lambda$ is directly proportional to absolute temperature $T$.

2. How does the mean free path $\lambda$ vary with pressure $P$ at constant temperature?

Explanation: At constant temperature, number density $n$ is directly proportional to pressure $P$. Since $\lambda = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} \pi n d^2}$, $\lambda$ is inversely proportional to $P$.

3. If the diameter of a gas molecule is doubled while keeping other parameters constant, the mean free path becomes:

Explanation: The mean free path is inversely proportional to the square of the molecular diameter ($d^2$). Doubling $d$ reduces $\lambda$ by a factor of $2^2 = 4$.

4. What is the collision frequency $f$ of a gas molecule in terms of average speed $\bar{v}$ and mean free path $\lambda$?

Explanation: Collision frequency is defined as the average number of collisions per unit time, given by the ratio of average molecular speed to the mean free path ($f = \frac{\bar{v}}{\lambda}$).

5. Which of the following transport coefficients of a gas is independent of pressure over moderate ranges?

Explanation: Both viscosity ($\eta = \frac{1}{3} \rho \bar{v} \lambda$) and thermal conductivity ($K = \frac{1}{3} \rho \bar{v} \lambda C_v$) are independent of pressure because density $\rho$ is proportional to $P$ and $\lambda$ is inversely proportional to $P$, keeping their product constant.

6. The correction factor $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ in the Maxwellian expression for mean free path accounts for:

Explanation: Maxwell accounted for the fact that gas molecules are not stationary but possess a distribution of relative velocities, introducing the factor of $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ compared to the elementary kinetic theory.

7. What are the SI units of mean free path $\lambda$?

Explanation: Mean free path is a distance representing the average length between collisions, so its SI unit is meters ($\text{m}$).

8. The mean free time $\tau$ between collisions is given by:

Explanation: Mean free time $\tau$ is the reciprocal of the collision frequency $f$, hence $\tau = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{\lambda}{\bar{v}}$.

9. How does the coefficient of viscosity of a gas depend on absolute temperature $T$?

Explanation: Since $\eta = \frac{1}{3} \rho \bar{v} \lambda$, and $\bar{v} \propto \sqrt{T}$ while $\lambda$ at constant density is independent of $T$ (or if looking at constant pressure, $\lambda \propto T$ and $\bar{v} \propto \sqrt{T}$ making $\eta \propto T^{3/2}$), standard kinetic theory shows $\eta \propto \sqrt{T}$ for constant density and $\eta \propto T^{1/2}$.

10. If number density $n$ of a gas is doubled, the mean free path $\lambda$ becomes:

Explanation: Since $\lambda = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} \pi n d^2}$, $\lambda$ is inversely proportional to $n$. Doubling $n$ halves the mean free path.

11. What is the physical meaning of $Z_{11} = \frac{1}{2} n f$?

Explanation: $Z_{11}$ represents the total number of binary collisions per unit volume per unit time, accounting for the double-counting of collisions by multiplying by $\frac{1}{2}$.

12. In transport phenomena, the mean free path acts as the microscopic characteristic length analogous to:

Explanation: In kinetic theory, transport coefficients depend on $\lambda$ as the characteristic distance over which momentum, energy, or mass is transported before a collision resets the property.

13. Why is the mean free path much larger in gases than in liquids at room temperature?

Explanation: Gases are much less dense than liquids, resulting in a drastically lower number density $n$, which inversely increases the mean free path.

14. Which parameter among the following does NOT directly appear in the Maxwell-corrected expression for mean free path?

Explanation: The formula $\lambda = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} \pi n d^2}$ contains $n$ and $d$. Temperature $T$ appears only when pressure $P$ is used instead of number density $n$ via the ideal gas law.

15. What is the relation between mass density $\rho$, number density $n$, and mass of a single molecule $m$?

Explanation: Mass density $\rho$ is the total mass per unit volume, which is the product of number density $n$ (molecules per unit volume) and mass of a single molecule $m$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The mean free path of gas molecules is inversely proportional to:

Explanation: The mean free path is given by $\lambda = \frac{k_B T}{\sqrt{2} \pi d^2 P}$, showing it is inversely proportional to both pressure $P$ and the square of molecular diameter $d^2$.

2. If the pressure of a given mass of gas is doubled at constant temperature, the mean free path of its molecules will:

Explanation: At constant temperature, mean free path is inversely proportional to pressure. Doubling the pressure halves the mean free path.

3. The collision frequency of a gas molecule is proportional to:

Explanation: Collision frequency is given by $f = \frac{\bar{v}}{\lambda} = \sqrt{2} \pi n d^2 \bar{v}$.

4. The coefficient of dynamic viscosity $\eta$ of an ideal gas depends on its absolute temperature $T$ as:

Explanation: Since $\eta = \frac{1}{3} \rho \bar{v} \lambda$ and average speed $\bar{v} \propto \sqrt{T}$ (while $\lambda$ at constant density is independent of $T$), the viscosity of a gas is proportional to $\sqrt{T}$.

5. At constant pressure, the mean free path $\lambda$ of gas molecules varies with absolute temperature $T$ as:

Explanation: Using $\lambda = \frac{k_B T}{\sqrt{2} \pi d^2 P}$, at constant pressure $P$, $\lambda$ is directly proportional to $T$.

6. The fact that the viscosity of a gas is independent of its pressure over moderate ranges is explained by:

Explanation: In $\eta = \frac{1}{3} \rho \bar{v} \lambda$, density $\rho$ increases linearly with pressure while $\lambda$ decreases inversely with pressure, causing the pressure terms to cancel out.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What happens to the mean free path of gas molecules if the absolute temperature is doubled and pressure is halved simultaneously?

Explanation: Since $\lambda \propto \frac{T}{P}$, doubling $T$ ($2T$) and halving $P$ ($P/2$) results in $\lambda' \propto \frac{2T}{P/2} = 4 \left(\frac{T}{P}\right)$, so it increases by 4 times.

2. The mean free path $\lambda$ in terms of mass density $\rho$ and molecular mass $m$ is expressed as:

Explanation: Since $n = \frac{\rho}{m}$, substituting $n$ into $\lambda = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} \pi n d^2}$ gives $\lambda = \frac{m}{\sqrt{2} \pi d^2 \rho}$.

3. If the number density of a gas is $2.5 \times 10^{25} \text{ m}^{-3}$ and molecular diameter is $0.37 \text{ nm}$, what is the approximate order of magnitude of the mean free path under standard conditions?

Explanation: Calculation yields $\lambda \approx 10^{-7} \text{ m}$ (or roughly $100 \text{ nm}$), which is typical for gases at standard temperature and pressure.

4. Which of the following statements about collision frequency $f$ is correct?

Explanation: Since $f = \sqrt{2} \pi n d^2 \bar{v}$, collision frequency increases with larger molecular diameter $d$, higher number density $n$, and higher average speed $\bar{v}$.

5. In the kinetic theory expression for thermal conductivity $K = \frac{1}{3} \rho \bar{v} \lambda C_v$, what does $C_v$ represent?

Explanation: $C_v$ represents the specific heat capacity of the gas at constant volume per unit mass.

6. Why does the mean free path become extremely large at high altitudes in the Earth's atmosphere?

Explanation: At high altitudes, atmospheric pressure and number density drop sharply, leading to a large increase in the mean free path.

7. If two different gases at the same temperature and pressure have molecular diameters in the ratio $1:2$, what is the ratio of their mean free paths?

Explanation: Since $\lambda \propto \frac{1}{d^2}$, the ratio of mean free paths is $\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2} = \frac{d_2^2}{d_1^2} = \left(\frac{2}{1}\right)^2 = 4:1$.

8. What is the relation between thermal conductivity $K$, dynamic viscosity $\eta$, and specific heat $C_v$ for a monatomic ideal gas according to simple kinetic theory?

Explanation: Since $K = \frac{1}{3} \rho \bar{v} \lambda C_v$ and $\eta = \frac{1}{3} \rho \bar{v} \lambda$, it directly follows that $K = \eta C_v$ in the simplest kinetic theory approximation.

9. The concept of mean free path is fundamental in establishing the transition from continuous fluid dynamics to:

Explanation: When characteristic system dimensions become comparable to or smaller than the mean free path, fluid dynamics breaks down and free molecular or Knudsen flow regime applies.

10. Which of the following molecules would have the shortest mean free path under identical temperature and pressure conditions, assuming spherical geometry?

Explanation: Since $\lambda$ is inversely proportional to $d^2$, the molecule with the largest diameter $d$ will have the shortest mean free path due to higher collision probability.
