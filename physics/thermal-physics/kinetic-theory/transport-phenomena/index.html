Transport Phenomena in Gases

Transport phenomena in gases refer to the net transfer of physical properties such as momentum, heat, and mass due to molecular collisions and thermal motion. These irreversible processes occur when a gas is subjected to gradients of velocity, temperature, or concentration.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Qualitative understanding of viscosity, thermal conduction, and diffusion in gases, along with their dependence on temperature, pressure, and density based on the kinetic theory of gases.

PGT extension

Rigorous derivation of coefficients of viscosity, thermal conductivity, and diffusion using mean free path formalism, and analysis of pressure independence of viscosity and thermal conductivity at moderate pressures.

Prerequisites

  • Kinetic theory of gases and Maxwell's distribution of velocities
  • Concept of mean free path and collision frequency
  • Degrees of freedom and Law of equipartition of energy

Introduction to Transport Phenomena

  • Transport phenomena arise from the molecular transport of momentum, energy, and mass across planes within a non-equilibrium gas.
  • Molecules crossing a reference plane from regions of different average properties carry their local momentum, energy, or mass into a new region.
  • These processes lead to the restoration of equilibrium via macroscopic dissipation: viscosity (momentum transport), thermal conduction (energy transport), and diffusion (mass transport).

Coefficient of Viscosity (Momentum Transport)

  • Viscosity in gases is caused by the transfer of directed momentum between adjacent layers moving with different velocities.
  • According to kinetic theory, the coefficient of viscosity η is directly proportional to absolute temperature (√T) and is independent of pressure (or density) over a wide range of moderate pressures.
  • At very low pressures (when mean free path is comparable to vessel dimensions) and very high pressures, this independence breaks down.

Coefficient of Thermal Conductivity (Energy Transport)

  • Thermal conduction in gases is the net transport of kinetic energy of random thermal motion down a temperature gradient.
  • The coefficient of thermal conductivity K is related to viscosity η and specific heat capacity at constant volume C_v via the relation K = f η C_v, where f is a numerical factor dependent on degrees of freedom.
  • Like viscosity, thermal conductivity is independent of pressure at moderate densities and increases with absolute temperature.

Coefficient of Diffusion (Mass Transport)

  • Diffusion is the transport of mass of a particular chemical species down a concentration gradient.
  • The coefficient of self-diffusion D is inversely proportional to the density (or pressure) of the gas and directly proportional to T^(3/2).
  • It describes how gas molecules intermingle due to random thermal agitation and molecular collisions.

Derivations

Derivation of Coefficient of Viscosity of a Gas

Conditions: Steady state, steady velocity gradient along the z-axis, low density gas where mean free path &lambda; is much smaller than macroscopic dimensions.

  • Consider a gas with density ρ, mean molecular velocity v̄, and mean free path λ. Let the flow velocity u_x vary along the z-axis such that the velocity gradient is du_x/dz.
  • Molecules crossing a horizontal plane at z = 0 from a distance equal to the mean free path above (&zbar; = +λ) and below (&zbar; = -λ) transport momentum parallel to the x-axis.
  • The net viscous drag (force per unit area) F/A across the plane is equated to η (du_x/dz), yielding η = (1/3) ρ λ v̄.

Result: \eta = \frac{1}{3} \rho \lambda \bar{v}

Derivation of Coefficient of Thermal Conductivity

Conditions: Stationary gas with a temperature gradient dT/dz along the z-axis.

  • Consider layers at distance λ above and below the reference plane z = 0 with temperatures T + λ(dT/dz) and T - λ(dT/dz) respectively.
  • The net rate of heat energy transport per unit area across the plane is given by Fourier's law of heat conduction: Q/A = -K (dT/dz).
  • Using the molecular flux and average energy per molecule (Cv T), the thermal conductivity K is derived as K = (1/3) ρ λ v̄ C_v, or in terms of viscosity, K = η C_v (neglecting internal degrees of freedom correction factors).

Result: K = \frac{1}{3} \rho \lambda \bar{v} C_v

Graphs and experiments

Viscosity vs Pressure of a Gas

Draw: A plot of viscosity (&eta;) on the y-axis versus pressure (P) on the x-axis, showing a constant horizontal line over a wide range of moderate pressures, dropping off only at very low vacuums.

Interpret: Demonstrates the surprising kinetic theory result that gas viscosity is independent of pressure because increased molecular density is exactly compensated by a decreased mean free path.

Exam use: Frequently tested in conceptual MCQs asking about the effect of compressing a gas on its viscosity.

Viscosity vs Temperature

Draw: A parabolic-like curve showing viscosity increasing with absolute temperature, typically plotted as &eta; versus T^(1/2).

Interpret: Reflects that higher temperatures increase molecular thermal speeds v&#772;, thereby increasing the rate of momentum transfer across layers.

Exam use: Distinguishing liquid viscosity (which decreases with temperature) from gas viscosity (which increases with temperature).

Maxwell's Determination of Viscosity (Experimental concept)

Draw: Schematic of oscillating disks or concentric cylinders where an outer rotating cylinder drags an inner suspended cylinder.

Interpret: Used to measure gas viscosity by observing the damping of rotational oscillations due to momentum transfer to the surrounding gas.

Exam use: Understanding experimental verification of kinetic theory transport coefficients.

Solved numericals

TGT core

If the temperature of an ideal gas is increased from 27 &deg;C to 327 &deg;C, by what factor does its coefficient of viscosity change?

  • Recall the temperature dependence of viscosity: η ∝ \sqrt{T}.
  • Convert temperatures to Kelvin: T_1 = 27 + 273 = 300 K, T_2 = 327 + 273 = 600 K.
  • Calculate the ratio: \frac{\eta_2}{\eta_1} = \sqrt{\frac{T_2}{T_1}} = \sqrt{\frac{600}{300}} = \sqrt{2}.

Answer: \sqrt{2} (or approximately 1.414)

PGT extension

Calculate the coefficient of thermal conductivity of helium gas at NTP, given that its coefficient of viscosity is 2.0 &times; 10^-5 kg m^-1 s^-1 and its specific heat at constant volume C_v is 3120 J kg^-1 K^-1.

  • Use the kinetic theory relation between thermal conductivity and viscosity: K = η C_v.
  • Substitute the given values: K = (2.0 × 10^-5 kg m^-1 s^-1) × (3120 J kg^-1 K^-1).
  • Perform the multiplication: K = 6.24 × 10^-2 W m^-1 K^-1.

Answer: 6.24 \times 10^{-2} \text{ W m}^{-1} \text{ K}^{-1}

PGT extension

Explain how the coefficient of self-diffusion D in a gas depends on pressure P and absolute temperature T. Find the combined proportionality.

  • The diffusion coefficient is given by D = (1/3) λ v̄.
  • Mean free path λ is inversely proportional to pressure P (or number density n), and average speed v̄ is proportional to \sqrt{T}.
  • Density ρ is proportional to P/T. Since λ ∝ T/P and v̄ ∝ T^(1/2), combining gives D ∝ \frac{T^{3/2}}{P}.

Answer: D \propto \frac{T^{3/2}}{P}

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes molecules are hard, elastic spheres with no intermolecular forces of attraction or repulsion (ideal gas approximation).
  • Assumes the velocity distribution locally follows the Maxwell-Boltzmann equilibrium distribution even in the presence of gradients.
  • Fails at extremely high pressures where molecular volumes are comparable to total volume, and at extremely low vacuums where mean free path exceeds container dimensions.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Gas viscosity increases with an increase in pressure because more molecules are packed together.

Correct: Gas viscosity is independent of pressure over moderate ranges.

While higher density provides more carriers of momentum, the mean free path decreases proportionally, neutralizing the net effect on transport.

Common error: Like liquids, gas viscosity decreases as temperature increases.

Correct: Gas viscosity increases with temperature.

Liquid viscosity is dominated by intermolecular cohesive forces which weaken with heat, whereas gas viscosity is driven by molecular thermal velocity which increases with temperature.

Common error: Thermal conductivity of a gas depends strongly on the container size.

Correct: It is independent of container size as long as the mean free path is much smaller than the container dimensions.

Transport is determined by local collisions within the bulk gas rather than boundary collisions.

Quick revision

  • Viscosity, thermal conduction, and diffusion are molecular transport phenomena driven by gradients in velocity, temperature, and concentration respectively.
  • Both viscosity (η) and thermal conductivity (K) are independent of pressure at moderate densities.
  • Viscosity and thermal conductivity are proportional to \sqrt{T}, whereas self-diffusion varies as T^(3/2) / P.

Must remember

  • Viscosity, thermal conductivity, and diffusion in gases are macroscopic dissipation processes arising from the molecular transport of momentum, energy, and mass, respectively.
  • The coefficient of viscosity $\eta$ and thermal conductivity $K$ of a gas are independent of pressure (or density) over moderate ranges of pressure.
  • The coefficient of viscosity is directly proportional to the square root of absolute temperature ($\eta \propto \sqrt{T}$), unlike liquids where viscosity decreases with temperature.
  • The coefficient of self-diffusion $D$ is inversely proportional to pressure (or density) and directly proportional to $T^{3/2}$.
  • Mean free path $\lambda$ is given by $\lambda = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} \pi n d^2}$, representing the average distance a molecule travels between successive collisions.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following transport phenomena in gases corresponds to the transport of momentum?

Explanation: Viscosity in gases is caused by the transfer of directed momentum between adjacent layers moving with different velocities.

2. How does the coefficient of viscosity $\eta$ of a dilute gas vary with absolute temperature $T$ according to the simple kinetic theory?

Explanation: According to kinetic theory, $\eta = \frac{1}{3} \rho \lambda \bar{v}$. Since $\rho \lambda$ is independent of $T$ and $\bar{v} \propto \sqrt{T}$, viscosity is directly proportional to $\sqrt{T}$.

3. How does the coefficient of viscosity of a gas depend on its pressure at moderate pressures?

Explanation: Over a wide range of moderate pressures, gas viscosity is independent of pressure because an increase in density increases the number of carriers, but the mean free path decreases proportionally.

4. The coefficient of self-diffusion $D$ in a gas is inversely proportional to:

Explanation: The self-diffusion coefficient is given by $D = \frac{1}{3} \lambda \bar{v}$. Since $\lambda \propto 1/P$, $D$ is inversely proportional to the gas pressure or density.

5. What is the relationship between thermal conductivity $K$, viscosity $\eta$, and specific heat $$C_{v}$$ for a monatomic gas?

Explanation: Kinetic theory relates thermal conductivity and viscosity via $K = f \eta $C_{v}$$. For ideal monatomic gases, the numerical factor $f approx 1$, leading to $K = \eta $C_{v}$$.

6. Why does the viscosity of liquids decrease with an increase in temperature, whereas gas viscosity increases?

Explanation: Liquid transport is hindered by strong intermolecular attractive forces which loosen upon heating. Gas transport depends on molecular thermal speed, which increases with temperature.

7. If the absolute temperature of a gas is increased by a factor of $4$, its coefficient of viscosity will increase by a factor of:

Explanation: Since $\eta \propto \sqrt{T}$, increasing temperature by a factor of $4$ increases viscosity by $\sqrt{4} = 2$.

8. The mean free path $\lambda$ of gas molecules is expressed as:

Explanation: By definition from kinetic theory, the mean free path for rigid spherical molecules with number density $n$ and diameter $d$ is $\lambda = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} \$\pi$ n d^2} $.

9. Which of the following transport coefficients depends on the concentration gradient?

Explanation: Diffusion is the transport of mass down a concentration gradient.

10. Under what condition does the pressure independence of gas viscosity break down?

Explanation: At extremely low pressures (Knudsen gas regime) or extremely high pressures (where molecular volume is non-negligible), the standard pressure independence fails.

11. The temperature dependence of the coefficient of self-diffusion $D$ is proportional to:

Explanation: Since $D = \frac{1}{3} \lambda \bar{v}$, and $\lambda$ is independent of $T$ while $\bar{v} \propto T^{1/2}$ and number density considerations give a $T$ factor, $D \propto T^{3/2}$ (specifically, $\lambda \propto T$ at constant pressure and $\bar{v} \propto T^{1/2}$, yielding $T^{3/2}$).

12. In transport phenomena, thermal conduction represents the molecular transport of:

Explanation: Thermal conduction is the net transport of kinetic energy of random thermal motion down a temperature gradient.

13. What is the SI unit for the coefficient of viscosity $\eta$?

Explanation: Viscosity has dimensions of $[M L^{-1} T^{-1}]$, with standard units $\text{kg m}^{-1} \text{s}^{-1}$ or Pascal-seconds.

14. What is the physical reason why gas viscosity is independent of density (at moderate pressures)?

Explanation: Higher density provides more carriers of momentum, but each carrier travels a shorter distance (smaller mean free path), neutralizing the net transport rate.

15. The transport phenomenon associated with mass transport is:

Explanation: Diffusion is defined as the transport of mass of a particular chemical species down a concentration gradient.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. According to the kinetic theory of gases, the coefficient of viscosity $\eta$ is:

Explanation: Over moderate pressure ranges, gas viscosity is independent of pressure as predicted by simple kinetic theory.

2. The coefficient of thermal conductivity $K$ of a gas is related to its viscosity $\eta$ and specific heat $$C_{v}$$ by:

Explanation: Simple kinetic theory yields $K = \eta $C_{v}$$ for monatomic gases.

3. If the temperature of a gas is raised from $27^{\circ}\text{C}$ to $327^{\circ}\text{C}$, the coefficient of viscosity will approximately:

Explanation: Absolute temperatures are $T_1 = 300\text{ K}$ and $T_2 = 600\text{ K}$. Since $\eta \propto \sqrt{T}$, the ratio is $\sqrt{600/300} = \sqrt{2}$.

4. The dimensions of the coefficient of thermal conductivity $K$ are:

Explanation: Thermal conductivity units are $\text{W m}^{-1} \text{K}^{-1} = \text{kg m s}^{-3} \text{K}^{-1}$, giving dimensions $[M L T^{-3} K^{-1}]$.

5. Which of the following transport coefficients depends inversely on the density of the gas?

Explanation: Self-diffusion coefficient $D \propto 1/\rho$, whereas viscosity and thermal conductivity are independent of density at moderate pressures.

6. In transport phenomena, momentum transport is responsible for:

Explanation: Viscosity is the macroscopic manifestation of molecular momentum transport across planes in a non-equilibrium gas.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which property of gases confirms that viscosity is independent of density over moderate pressure ranges?

Explanation: Experiments involving the damping of an oscillating disc or cylinder in a gas verify that viscosity is independent of gas pressure/density over a wide range.

2. If the mean free path of gas molecules is $\lambda$ and average speed is $\bar{v}$, the expression for the diffusion coefficient $D$ is:

Explanation: Kinetic theory derives the self-diffusion coefficient as $D = \frac{1}{3} \lambda \bar{v}$.

3. How does the thermal conductivity $K$ of a gas change with absolute temperature $T$?

Explanation: Since $K = \eta $C_{v}$$ and $\eta \propto \sqrt{T}$ (with $$C_{v}$$ constant for ideal gases), thermal conductivity is proportional to $\sqrt{T}$.

4. Why does thermal conductivity of a gas remain independent of container dimensions under normal conditions?

Explanation: As long as $\lambda \ll \text{dimensions}$, energy transport is governed by local molecular collisions within the bulk gas rather than boundary interactions.

5. The ratio $\frac{K}{\eta $C_{v}$}$ from elementary kinetic theory is approximately equal to:

Explanation: Elementary kinetic theory predicts $K / (\eta $C_{v}$) = 1$, though more rigorous treatments give factors depending on degrees of freedom (e.g., Eucken's correction).

6. What happens to the mean free path of gas molecules if the pressure is doubled at constant temperature?

Explanation: Since mean free path $\lambda \propto \frac{1}{n}$ and number density $n$ doubles when pressure doubles at constant temperature, $\lambda$ is halved.

7. Which of the following transport phenomena involves the transfer of scalar quantity rather than vector quantity?

Explanation: Energy (temperature) and mass (concentration) are scalar quantities, whereas momentum is a vector (or tensor component).

8. If the molecular diameter $d$ of a gas is decreased, its mean free path will:

Explanation: Since $\lambda = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} \$\pi$ n d^2}$, the mean free path is inversely proportional to $d^2$. A smaller diameter increases the mean free path.

9. In a gas mixture, self-diffusion refers to:

Explanation: Self-diffusion describes how identical gas molecules intermingle due to thermal agitation and molecular collisions.

10. Which transport coefficient has the same dimensional formula as kinematic viscosity ($v = \eta/\rho$)?

Explanation: Kinematic viscosity and the coefficient of self-diffusion both have dimensions of $[L^2 T^{-1}]$ (units of $\text{m}^2 \text{s}^{-1}$).
