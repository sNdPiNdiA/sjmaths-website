Most Probable Velocity of Gas Molecules

The most probable velocity is the speed possessed by the maximum fraction of molecules in a gas at a given absolute temperature. It corresponds to the peak of the Maxwell-Boltzmann molecular speed distribution curve.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition, formula, dependence on temperature and molar mass, comparison with root-mean-square and average velocities.

PGT extension

Derivation from Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution function, differentiation to find the maxima, and kinetic interpretation of temperature.

Prerequisites

  • Concept of Temperature
  • Kinetic theory of gases
  • Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution law

Definition and Physical Origin

  • It is the velocity corresponding to the peak (maximum) of the Maxwell-Boltzmann speed distribution curve.
  • Molecules of a gas possess a wide range of speeds due to continuous elastic collisions among themselves.
  • The number of molecules having the most probable velocity is greater than the number having any other specific velocity interval.

Dependence on Temperature and Mass

  • Directly proportional to the square root of the absolute temperature of the gas (vmp ∝ √T).
  • Inversely proportional to the square root of the molecular mass or molar mass of the gas (vmp ∝ 1 / √M).
  • Independent of pressure at constant temperature for an ideal gas.

Comparison of Molecular Speeds

  • The three characteristic speeds follow the fixed ratio: vmp : vavg : vrms = √(2RT/M) : √(8RT/πM) : √(3RT/M) = √2 : √(8/π) : √3 ≈ 1 : 1.128 : 1.224.
  • Root-mean-square velocity is always greater than average velocity, which in turn is greater than most probable velocity (vrms > vavg > vmp).
  • This ordering reflects the asymmetry of the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution curve.

Role in Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution

  • Obtained mathematically by setting the first derivative of the Maxwell-Boltzmann speed distribution function with respect to speed equal to zero.
  • Represents the most frequent outcome of random thermal motion in macroscopic thermodynamic equilibrium.
  • Shifts to higher values as thermal energy increases.

Derivations

Derivation of Most Probable Velocity from Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution

Conditions: Ideal gas in thermal equilibrium

  • Start with the Maxwell-Boltzmann speed distribution function: dNv / N = 4π (m / 2πkT)3/2 v2 exp(-mv2 / 2kT) dv.
  • To find the peak of the distribution, differentiate the function with respect to v and set the derivative equal to zero: d/dv [v2 exp(-mv2 / 2kT)] = 0.
  • Apply the product rule of differentiation: 2v exp(-mv2 / 2kT) + v2 (-mv / kT) exp(-mv2 / 2kT) = 0.
  • Factor out common terms: v exp(-mv2 / 2kT) [2 - (mv2 / kT)] = 0.
  • Since v ≠ 0 at the peak and exponential term is non-zero, equate the bracketed term to zero: 2 - (mv2 / kT) = 0.
  • Solve for v to get the most probable velocity: vmp = √(2kT / m) = √(2RT / M).

Result: v_mp = \sqrt{\frac{2RT}{M}}

Graphs and experiments

Maxwell-Boltzmann Speed Distribution Curve

Draw: A plot of fraction of molecules (dN/dv) on the y-axis against molecular speed (v) on the x-axis, showing an asymmetric bell-shaped curve skewed to the right.

Interpret: The peak of the curve corresponds to v_mp. The area under the curve represents the total number of molecules. v_avg and v_rms lie to the right of v_mp due to the long tail.

Exam use: Frequently asked in identifying positions of v_mp, v_avg, and v_rms on the curve, and effect of temperature change.

Effect of Temperature on Distribution Curve

Interpret: As temperature increases, the most probable velocity increases, and the spread of molecular velocities becomes wider.

Exam use: Tested in conceptual questions regarding shifting of peaks and molecular speed changes at varying temperatures.

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the most probable velocity of oxygen molecules (M = 32 g/mol) at 27 &deg;C. Given R = 8.314 J/mol K.

  • Identify given parameters: T = 27 °C = 300 K, M = 32 g/mol = 32 × 10-3 kg/mol, R = 8.314 J/mol K.
  • Write formula: v_mp = √(2RT / M).
  • Substitute values: v_mp = √(2 × 8.314 × 300 / (32 × 10-3)).
  • Calculate numerator: 2 × 8.314 × 300 = 4988.4.
  • Divide by M: 4988.4 / 0.032 = 155887.5.
  • Take square root: √155887.5 ≈ 394.8 m/s.

Answer: 394.8 m/s

PGT extension

At what temperature will the most probable velocity of hydrogen molecules (M = 2 g/mol) be equal to the root-mean-square velocity of oxygen molecules (M = 32 g/mol) at 300 K?

  • Find v_rms of oxygen at 300 K: v_rms(O2) = √(3 R T_O2 / M_O2) = √(3 R × 300 / 32).
  • Equate to v_mp of hydrogen at unknown temperature T: v_mp(H2) = √(2 R T / M_H2) = √(2 R T / 2) = √(RT).
  • Set them equal: √(RT) = √(900 R / 32).
  • Square both sides: RT = 900 R / 32.
  • Cancel R and solve for T: T = 900 / 32 = 28.125 K.

Answer: 28.125 K

PGT extension

If the absolute temperature of an ideal gas is increased four times, by what factor does the most probable velocity of its molecules increase?

  • Write the proportionality relation: v_mp ∝ √T.
  • Express initial and final states: v_mp1 = K √T_1 and v_mp2 = K √T_2.
  • Given T_2 = 4 T_1.
  • Take the ratio: v_mp2 / v_mp1 = √(T_2 / T_1) = √(4 T_1 / T_1) = √4 = 2.

Answer: Factor of 2

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes the gas obeys the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution law, which strictly holds only for ideal gases in thermal equilibrium at low densities.
  • Fails at extremely high pressures and low temperatures where intermolecular quantum effects and real gas van der Waals interactions become significant.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Most probable velocity is equal to the average velocity of gas molecules.

Correct: Most probable velocity is less than the average velocity (v_mp < v_avg).

The distribution curve is asymmetric (skewed to the right), which shifts the arithmetic mean and root-mean-square values to higher speeds than the peak.

Common error: Most probable velocity depends on the pressure of the gas.

Correct: Most probable velocity depends only on temperature and molar mass (v_mp = &radic;(2RT/M)).

Pressure changes alter density proportionally at constant temperature, keeping the ratio P/&rho; constant for an ideal gas.

Quick revision

  • Formula: v_mp = \sqrt{2RT/M} = \sqrt{2kT/m}
  • Most Probable Velocity (in terms of Molar Mass): v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2RT}{M}}
  • Most Probable Velocity (in terms of Boltzmann Constant): v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2kT}{m}}

Must remember

  • Most probable velocity ($v_{mp}$) corresponds to the peak of the Maxwell-Boltzmann speed distribution curve.
  • The formula for most probable velocity is $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2RT}{M}} = \sqrt{\frac{2kT}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2P}{\rho}}$.
  • The characteristic speeds follow the strict order: $v_{rms} > v_{avg} > v_{mp}$ with a ratio of $\sqrt{3} : \sqrt{8/\pi} : \sqrt{2} \approx 1.224 : 1.128 : 1$.
  • $v_{mp}$ is directly proportional to the square root of absolute temperature ($T$) and inversely proportional to the square root of molar mass ($M$).
  • At constant temperature, most probable velocity is completely independent of the pressure of an ideal gas.
  • Mathematically, $v_{mp}$ is obtained by setting the first derivative of the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution function with respect to speed equal to zero.

Glossary

Most Probable Velocity ($v_{mp}$)The speed possessed by the maximum fraction of molecules in a gas sample at a given temperature.
Root-Mean-Square Velocity ($v_{rms}$)The square root of the average of the squares of the speeds of the gas molecules.
Average Velocity ($v_{avg}$)The arithmetic mean of the speeds of all gas molecules in a given sample.
Maxwell-Boltzmann DistributionA probability distribution describing particle speeds in idealized gases where particles move randomly.
Universal Gas Constant ($R$)A physical constant used in the equation of state for a gas, equal to the product of Boltzmann constant and Avogadro constant.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What does the most probable velocity of gas molecules represent on the Maxwell-Boltzmann speed distribution curve?

Explanation: The most probable velocity $v_{mp}$ is defined as the velocity possessed by the maximum number of molecules, which corresponds to the peak of the Maxwell-Boltzmann speed distribution curve.

2. How does the most probable velocity $v_{mp}$ vary with the absolute temperature $T$ of an ideal gas?

Explanation: From the formula $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2RT}{M}}$, the most probable velocity is directly proportional to the square root of the absolute temperature ($v_{mp} \propto \sqrt{T}$).

3. What is the correct relationship between most probable velocity ($v_{mp}$), average velocity ($v_{avg}$), and root-mean-square velocity ($v_{rms}$)?

Explanation: Due to the asymmetry of the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution, $v_{rms} > v_{avg} > v_{mp}$ with exact ratios of $\sqrt{3} : \sqrt{8/\$\pi$} : \sqrt{2}$.

4. At constant temperature, if the pressure of an ideal gas is doubled, what happens to its most probable velocity?

Explanation: For an ideal gas at constant temperature, $v_{mp}$ depends only on temperature and molar mass ($v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2P}{\rho}}$ where pressure changes are proportionally offset by density changes), so it remains unchanged.

5. What is the ratio of most probable velocity to average velocity to root-mean-square velocity ($v_{mp} : v_{avg} : v_{rms}$)?

Explanation: The ratio is $\sqrt{2} : \sqrt{\frac{8}{\$\pi$}} : \sqrt{3} \approx 1 : 1.128 : 1.224$.

6. How is the most probable velocity derived from the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution function $f(v)$?

Explanation: The peak of the distribution function is found mathematically by setting its first derivative with respect to speed equal to zero ($\frac{df(v)}{dv} = 0$).

7. If the absolute temperature of a gas is quadrupled, its most probable velocity will:

Explanation: Since $v_{mp} \propto \sqrt{T}$, if the temperature $T$ is multiplied by 4, $v_{mp}$ is multiplied by $\sqrt{4} = 2$ (doubled).

8. What is the expression for most probable velocity in terms of pressure $P$ and density $\rho$?

Explanation: Using the ideal gas equation $P = \frac{\rho RT}{M}$, we substitute $\frac{RT}{M} = \frac{P}{\rho}$ into $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2RT}{M}}$ to get $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2P}{\rho}}$.

9. Which of the following statements is correct regarding the molecules possessing the most probable velocity?

Explanation: By definition, the peak of the Maxwell-Boltzmann curve represents the velocity interval possessed by the maximum number of molecules compared to any other specific interval.

10. If the molar mass of a gas is increased by a factor of 4, the most probable velocity of its molecules will:

Explanation: Since $v_{mp} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}$, increasing $M$ by a factor of 4 results in $v_{mp}$ changing by a factor of $\frac{1}{\sqrt{4}} = \frac{1}{2}$ (halved).

11. What is the unit of most probable velocity?

Explanation: Being a velocity, the SI unit of most probable velocity is meters per second ($\text{m/s}$).

12. Why is the average velocity greater than the most probable velocity?

Explanation: The asymmetry (right-skewness) of the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution stretches the tail towards higher speeds, pulling the arithmetic mean (average) above the peak (most probable velocity).

13. Which molecular speed is most directly associated with the kinetic interpretation of temperature?

Explanation: Root-mean-square velocity ($v_{rms}$) is directly linked via kinetic energy per mole ($E = \frac{3}{2}RT$) to absolute temperature, though all characteristic speeds scale with $\sqrt{T}$.

14. What happens to the peak of the Maxwell-Boltzmann speed distribution curve when the temperature of the gas is increased?

Explanation: As temperature increases, $v_{mp}$ increases (shifting the peak to the right), and because the total area under the curve (total probability) remains constant, the peak height decreases (broadening the distribution).

15. Which of the following expressions correctly gives $v_{mp}$ in terms of the Boltzmann constant $k$ and molecular mass $m$?

Explanation: Substituting $R = N_A k$ and $M = N_A m$ into $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2RT}{M}}$ yields $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2kT}{m}}$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The most probable speed of the molecules of a gas at temperature $T$ is given by:

Explanation: By definition and derivation from the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution, the most probable speed is $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2RT}{M}}$ (where $v_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$ and $v_{avg} = \sqrt{\frac{8RT}{\$\pi$ M}}$).

2. At what temperature will the most probable velocity of oxygen molecules be equal to that of hydrogen molecules at $300\text{ K}$?

Explanation: Since $v_{mp} \propto \sqrt{\frac{T}{M}}$, for equal most probable velocities: $\frac{T_{O_2}}{M_{O_2}} = \frac{T_{H_2}}{M_{H_2}}$. Thus, $T_{O_2} = T_{H_2} \times \left(\frac{M_{O_2}}{M_{H_2}}\right) = 300 \times \left(\frac{32}{2} ight) = 300 \times 16 = 4800\text{ K}$.

3. The ratio of most probable velocity, average velocity, and root-mean-square velocity of gas molecules is:

Explanation: The ratio is $v_{mp} : v_{avg} : v_{rms} = \sqrt{2} : \sqrt{\frac{8}{\$\pi$}} : \sqrt{3} \approx 1 : 1.128 : 1.224$.

4. If the absolute temperature of an ideal gas is increased 4 times, the most probable velocity of its molecules becomes:

Explanation: Most probable velocity $v_{mp} \propto \sqrt{T}$. When temperature $T$ increases by a factor of 4, $v_{mp}$ increases by a factor of $\sqrt{4} = 2$.

5. Which of the following relations is true for the speeds of gas molecules?

Explanation: Due to the asymmetrical nature of the Maxwell-Boltzmann speed distribution curve, the root-mean-square speed is the largest, followed by the average speed, and the most probable speed is the smallest ($v_{mp} < v_{avg} < v_{rms}$).

6. The most probable velocity of a gas under isothermal conditions depends upon:

Explanation: From $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2RT}{M}}$, most probable velocity depends directly on absolute temperature $T$ and inversely on molar mass $M$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. For a Maxwellian gas, what is the ratio of the most probable speed to the root-mean-square speed ($v_{mp} / v_{rms}$)?

Explanation: $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2RT}{M}}$ and $v_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$. Taking their ratio gives $\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}$.

2. If the kinetic energy of translation of a gas molecule is $E$, then its most probable velocity $v_{mp}$ (where $m$ is mass of molecule) is given by:

Explanation: Translational kinetic energy per molecule is $E = \frac{1}{2}m v_{rms}^2$. However, $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2kT}{m}}$ and mean translational kinetic energy is $\frac{3}{2}kT$. For most probable speed, $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2kT}{m}}$. Since $\frac{1}{2}m v_{mp}^2$ does not equal total kinetic energy unless specified by definition of thermal energy scale, substituting $kT = \frac{2}{3}E_{avg}$ gives $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{4E_{avg}}{3m}}$? Wait, let's check directly: $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2RT}{M}} = \sqrt{\frac{2kT}{m}}$. Energy $E = \frac{1}{2}mv_{mp}^2$ is not exact unless defined. Let's use $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2E}{m}}$ if $E = kT$. Let's re-verify: $kT$ is thermal energy per degree of freedom. Thus $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2kT}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2E}{m}}$ where $E = kT$.

3. In a gas sample, let $N(v)$ be the number of molecules with speed $v$. The most probable speed corresponds to the condition:

Explanation: The peak of the distribution represents a local maximum, requiring the first derivative to be zero and the second derivative to be negative (concave down).

4. Two different gases $A$ and $B$ have molar masses $M_A$ and $M_B$ such that $M_A = 4M_B$. At what temperatures $T_A$ and $T_B$ will their most probable velocities be equal?

Explanation: For $v_{mpA} = v_{mpB}$, we require $\frac{T_A}{M_A} = \frac{T_B}{M_B}$. Since $M_A = 4M_B$, it follows that $T_A = 4T_B$.

5. What is the percentage difference between root-mean-square velocity and most probable velocity approximately?

Explanation: The ratio $v_{rms} / v_{mp} = \sqrt{3} / \sqrt{2} = \sqrt{1.5} \approx 1.224$, which represents a $22.4\%$ excess of $v_{rms}$ over $v_{mp}$.

6. Which of the following speed parameters is most affected by molecules in the high-speed tail of the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution?

Explanation: Because $v_{rms}$ involves squaring the velocities ($v^2$), molecules in the high-speed tail contribute disproportionately heavily to its value compared to $v_{mp}$.

7. If the pressure of a given mass of an ideal gas is doubled while keeping its volume constant, the most probable velocity of the gas molecules will:

Explanation: From the ideal gas law $PV = nRT$, doubling pressure at constant volume means temperature $T$ is doubled. Since $v_{mp} \propto \sqrt{T}$, doubling $T$ increases $v_{mp}$ by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$. (Wait, let's check option A vs B. If pressure is doubled at constant volume, $T$ doubles, so $v_{mp}$ increases by $\sqrt{2}$ times. Option A is correct).

8. The Maxwell speed distribution function $f(v)$ has its maximum at $v = v_{mp}$. The value of $f(v_{mp})$ scales with temperature $T$ as:

Explanation: The distribution pre-factor contains $(m/kT)^{3/2}$, meaning the peak height $f(v_{mp})$ scales as $T^{-3/2}$ as temperature increases and the curve flattens.

9. For a mixture of two non-reacting ideal gases at temperature $T$, helium ($M = 4\text{ g/mol}$) and argon ($M = 40\text{ g/mol}$), what is the ratio of their most probable velocities ($v_{mp,He} / v_{mp,Ar}$)?

Explanation: $v_{mp} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}$. Thus, $\frac{v_{mp,He}}{v_{mp,Ar}} = \sqrt{\frac{M_{Ar}}{M_{He}}} = \sqrt{\frac{40}{4}} = \sqrt{10}$.

10. In terms of the most probable speed $v_{mp}$, what is the value of the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution function $f(v_{mp})$?

Explanation: Substituting $v_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2kT}{m}}$ into the exponential term $-\frac{mv^2}{2kT}$ gives $-\frac{m(2kT/m)}{2kT} = -1/2$. Thus the exponential factor is $e^{-1/2}$.
