Thermal Physics

Radiation of Heat

Radiation of heat is the mode of heat transfer via electromagnetic waves, primarily in the infrared spectrum, requiring no material medium for propagation. All bodies above absolute zero emit thermal radiation due to thermal agitation of charged particles.

Prevost's Theory of Heat Exchange

  • Every body at a temperature above absolute zero (0 K) emits thermal radiation to its surroundings and simultaneously absorbs radiation from them.
  • A body cools when its rate of emission exceeds its rate of absorption, and warms when absorption exceeds emission.
  • In thermal equilibrium, the rate of emission equals the rate of absorption.

Thermal Radiations and Their Properties

  • Thermal radiations are electromagnetic waves belonging to the infrared and visible regions of the spectrum, travelling with the speed of light ($c = 3 x 10^{8} m/s in vacuum)$.
  • They obey the inverse square law of intensity and exhibit phenomena of reflection, refraction, interference, diffraction, and polarization.
  • Unlike conduction and convection, radiation requires no intervening medium and passes readily through a vacuum.

Emissive Power and Absorptive Power

  • Radiant emissive power (E) of a surface is defined as the total amount of radiant energy emitted per unit time per unit surface area for a specific wavelength interval.
  • Absorptive power (a) or absorptivity is the ratio of the radiant energy absorbed by a body to the total radiant energy incident upon it.
  • A perfectly black body has an absorptivity of exactly 1 for all incident wavelengths and angles.

Kirchhoff's Law of Radiation

  • At any given temperature, the ratio of the emissive power (E) to the absorptive power (a) for a particular wavelength is a constant for all bodies and is equal to the emissive power of a perfectly black body at that same temperature.
  • Expressed mathematically as E/$a = E_{b}$, implying that good absorbers are good emitters.
  • Explains Fraunhofer lines in the solar spectrum, where cooler outer vapors of the sun absorb specific wavelengths emitted by the hot inner core.

Derivations

Stefan-Boltzmann Law and Net Radiation Heat Transfer

Conditions: Black body in an environment at constant absolute temperature

  • The total radiant energy emitted per second per unit area by a black body is directly proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature T: $E = \sigma T^{4}$
  • If a black body of surface area A at temperature T is placed in an enclosure at temperature T0, it emits energy at a rate Qemit = \sigma A T^{4}
  • Simultaneously, the body absorbs radiant energy from the enclosure at a rate Qabs = \sigma A T_{0}^{4}
  • The net rate of heat loss by radiation is given by subtracting absorption from emission: $Q_{net} = \sigma A (T^{4} - T_{0}^{4})$

Result: $Q_{net} = \sigma A (T^{4} - T_{0}^{4})$

Newton's Law of Cooling from Stefan-Boltzmann Law

Conditions: Small temperature difference between the body and surroundings ((T - T_{0}) \ll T_{0})

  • Start with the net rate of heat loss from Stefan-Boltzmann law: - \frac{dQ}{dt} = \sigma A e (T^{4} - T_{0}^{4}), where e is emissivity.
  • Factor the temperature term: T^{4} - T_{0}^{4} = (T^{2} - T_{0}^{2})(T + T_{0}) = (T - T_{0})(T + T_{0})(T^{2} + T_{0}^{2}).
  • Substitute T \approx T_{0} for the terms other than (T - T_{0}): T^{4} - T_{0}^{4} \approx (T - T_{0})(2T_{0})(2T_{0}^{2}) = 4T_{0}^{3}(T - T_{0}).
  • Substitute this approximation back into the rate equation and write dQ = mc dT to obtain \frac{dT}{dt} = - \frac{4\sigma A e T_{0}^{3}}{mc}(T - T_{0}) = - K(T - T_{0}).

Result: \frac{dT}{dt} = - K(T - T_{0})

Blackbody Spectral Radiance (Planck's Distribution Curves)

Draw: Curves of spectral emissive power E_{\lambda} versus wavelength \lambda for various absolute temperatures T, showing asymmetric peaks that shift to shorter wavelengths as temperature increases.

Interpret: The area under each curve represents total emissive power \sigma T^{4}. The peak shifts toward shorter wavelengths with rising temperature, illustrating Wien's displacement law.

Exam use: Directly tested in questions regarding shifts in peak wavelength and verification of Stefan-Boltzmann law integration.

Newton's Law of Cooling Experimental Verification Curve

Draw: A semi-log plot of ln(T - T_{0}) versus time t, yielding a straight line with a negative slope equal to -K.

Interpret: Linearity of the semi-log plot confirms the exponential decay nature of Newton's law of cooling.

Exam use: Interpreting experimental gradient to find cooling constant K for a calorimeter.

Ferry's Black Body Apparatus

Draw: A schematic double-walled hollow sphere with a small aperture and an internal conical projection opposite the aperture.

Interpret: Any radiation entering through the aperture undergoes multiple internal reflections, with a negligible fraction escaping, achieving an effective absorptivity close to 1.

Exam use: Conceptual questions on the practical realization of a perfectly black body.

Solved numericals

TGT core

A metal sphere of radius 0.1 m is heated to 500 K and placed in an environment at 300 K. Assuming it behaves as a black body, calculate the net rate of heat loss by radiation. (Stefan-Boltzmann constant $\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8} W m^{-2} K^{-4})$

  • Determine the surface area of the sphere: $A = 4 \pi r^{2} = 4 \times \pi \times (0.1)^{2} = 0.04 \pi \approx 0.12566 m^{2}$.
  • Apply the net radiation formula: $Q_{net} = \sigma A (T^{4} - T_{0}^{4})$.
  • Calculate T^{4} - T_{0}^{4} = 500^{4} - 300^{4} = 6.25 \times 10^{10} - 0.81 \times 10^{10} = 5.44 \times 10^{10} K^{4}.
  • Substitute values: $Q_{net} = (5.67 \times 10^{-8}) \times (0.12566) \times (5.44 \times 10^{10}) = 387.6 W$.

Answer: 387.6 W

PGT extension

The spectral emissive power of a black body peaks at a wavelength of \lambd$a_{m} = 500 nm at a certain temperature T_{1}$. If the temperature is increased such that the peak wavelength shifts to 400 nm, find the percentage change in the total emissive power of the black body.

  • Use Wien's displacement law to find the new temperature T_{2}: \lambda_{1} $T_{1} = \lambda_{2} T_{2} \implies 500 T_{1} = 400 T_{2} \implies T_{2} = \frac{5}{4} T_{1} = 1.25 T_{1}$.
  • The total emissive power is proportional to T^{4}: $E_{1} = \sigma T_{1}^{4}$ and $E_{2} = \sigma T_{2}^{4} = \sigma (1.25 T_{1})^{4} = 2.4414 \sigma T_{1}^{4}$.
  • Calculate the percentage change: \frac{E_{2} - E_{1}}{E_{1}} \times $100 = (2.4414 - 1) \times 100 = 144.14%$.

Answer: 144.14% increase

PGT extension

A body cools from 80°C to 64°C in 5 minutes in a room maintained at 24°C. Find the time taken by the body to cool from 64°C to 52°C, assuming Newton's law of cooling holds.

  • Find the average temperature for the first interval: $T_{avg1} = \frac{80 + 64}{2} = 72°C$.
  • Apply Newton's law of cooling differential form: \frac{T_{1} - T_{2}}{t} = K(T_{avg} - T_{0}) \implies \frac{80 - 64}{5} = K(72 - 24) \implies \frac{16}{5} = K(48) \implies $K = \frac{16}{5 \times 48} = \frac{1}{15} min^{-1}$.
  • Find the average temperature for the second interval: $T_{avg2} = \frac{64 + 52}{2} = 58°C$.
  • Apply the equation for the second interval: \frac{64 - 52}{t'} = K(58 - 24) \implies \frac{12}{t'} = \frac{1}{15} (34) \implies t' = \frac{12 \times 15}{34} = \frac{180}{34} \approx 5.29 min.

Answer: 5.29 minutes

Assumptions and limitations

  • Stefan-Boltzmann law strictly applies only to ideal black bodies; real grey bodies require an emissivity multiplier e.
  • Newton's law of cooling is an approximation valid only for small temperature differences ((T - T_{0}) \ll T_{0}) where heat loss by conduction and convection is linear with temperature.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Radiation of heat requires air or a material medium to travel from one place to another.

Correct: Thermal radiation consists of electromagnetic waves and propagates perfectly through a vacuum.

Confusion with conduction and convection mechanisms which necessitate particle collisions or bulk fluid motion.

Common error: A body at room temperature does not emit any thermal radiation because it feels cold to the touch.

Correct: All bodies above 0 K emit thermal radiation continuously.

Net emission determines cooling or warming; a body at room temperature emits radiation while simultaneously absorbing radiation from its surroundings.

Common error: Newton's law of cooling applies for any arbitrary temperature difference between a body and its surroundings.

Correct: Newton's law is valid only for small temperature differences (typically < 35°C to 40°C in air).

For large temperature differences, radiation heat loss follows the fourth-power Stefan-Boltzmann relation rather than a linear temperature difference.

Quick revision

  • Thermal radiation travels at the speed of light in a vacuum as electromagnetic waves.
  • A perfect black body has absorptivity a = 1 and emits maximum radiation at any temperature.
  • Kirchhoff's law states that good absorbers are good emitters at thermal equilibrium (E/$a = E_{b})$.
  • Stefan-Boltzmann law gives total emissive power proportional to T^{4}.
  • Wien's displacement law establishes that \lambda_{m}T = constant, meaning peak wavelength shifts shorter as temperature rises.
  • Newton's law of cooling simplifies the Stefan-Boltzmann law for small temperature differences into a linear exponential decay.

Must remember

  • Every body above absolute zero ($0\text{ K}$) emits and absorbs thermal radiation simultaneously.
  • Thermal radiations are electromagnetic waves traveling at the speed of light in vacuum and do not require any medium.
  • Kirchhoff's Law states that the ratio of emissive power to absorptive power for any given wavelength is constant for all bodies at the same temperature and equals the emissive power of a black body ($E/a = E_b$).
  • Stefan-Boltzmann Law relates emissive power of a black body to absolute temperature as $E = \sigma T^4$.
  • Newton's Law of Cooling is an approximation valid only for small temperature differences between a body and its surroundings, stating rate of cooling is proportional to temperature difference.

Glossary

Radiant Emissive Power ($E$)Total amount of radiant energy emitted per unit time per unit surface area for a specific wavelength interval.
Absorptive Power ($a$)The ratio of radiant energy absorbed by a body to the total radiant energy incident upon it.
Perfectly Black BodyAn idealized body that absorbs all incident radiation of all wavelengths falling on it, having absorptivity $a = 1$.
Emissivity ($e$)The ratio of the emissive power of a real body to the emissive power of a black body at the same temperature.
Wien's Displacement Law Constant ($b$)The product of the absolute temperature $T$ and the wavelength of maximum emission $\lambda_m$, which is a constant.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. According to Prevost's theory of heat exchange, when does a body cool?

Explanation: A body cools when energy leaving the body via radiation emission is greater than energy entering via absorption, i.e., rate of emission exceeds rate of absorption.

2. Thermal radiations belong primarily to which region of the electromagnetic spectrum?

Explanation: Thermal radiations are electromagnetic waves belonging chiefly to the infrared and visible regions of the spectrum.

3. What is the absorptivity of a perfectly black body for all incident wavelengths?

Explanation: By definition, a perfectly black body absorbs all incident radiation falling on it, giving an absorptivity of exactly $1$.

4. Kirchhoff's law of radiation is expressed mathematically as:

Explanation: At a given temperature, the ratio of emissive power $E$ to absorptive power $a$ for any body is constant and equal to the emissive power of a black body $E_b$, so $\frac{E}{a} = E_b$.

5. The Stefan-Boltzmann law states that the total radiant energy emitted per second per unit area of a perfect black body is directly proportional to:

Explanation: The emissive power of a black body is given by $E = \sigma T^4$, meaning it varies directly as the fourth power of absolute temperature $T$.

6. What are the dimensions or units of radiant emissive power ($E$)?

Explanation: Emissive power is energy emitted per unit time per unit surface area, which translates to power per unit area, i.e., $\text{W m}^{-2}$.

7. Which of the following phenomena is NOT exhibited by thermal radiations?

Explanation: Thermal radiations are electromagnetic waves and thus exhibit reflection, refraction, interference, diffraction, and polarization, but not acoustic streaming.

8. According to Wien's displacement law, the product $\lambda_m T$ is:

Explanation: Wien's displacement law states that $\lambda_m T = b$, where $b$ is Wien's constant, independent of the material of the black body.

9. Under what condition is Newton's law of cooling valid?

Explanation: Newton's law of cooling is an approximation valid strictly for small temperature differences (typically $< 35^{\circ}\text{C}$ to $40^{\circ}\text{C}$) between a body and its surroundings.

10. Why are good absorbers also good emitters, according to Kirchhoff's law?

Explanation: Since $\frac{E}{a} = E_b$ and $E_b$ is constant for a given temperature, a body with a large absorptivity $a$ must have a correspondingly large emissive power $E$.

11. What is the net rate of heat loss by radiation for a grey body of emissivity $e$ and surface area $A$ at temperature $T$ in a room at temperature $T_0$?

Explanation: The net heat radiation exchange considering emission and simultaneous absorption from surroundings at $T_0$ is given by $E_{net} = \sigma e A (T^4 - T_0^4)$.

12. Fraunhofer lines observed in the solar spectrum are explained by:

Explanation: Cooler outer vapors of the sun absorb specific wavelengths emitted by the hot inner core, which is a direct application of Kirchhoff's law.

13. If the absolute temperature of a black body is doubled, its emissive power increases by a factor of:

Explanation: By Stefan-Boltzmann law $E \propto T^4$. If $T$ becomes $2T$, $E$ becomes $(2)^4 = 16$ times.

14. What is the correct differential form of Newton's law of cooling?

Explanation: The differential form is $\frac{dT}{dt} = -K(T - T_0)$, representing a linear decrease in temperature difference over time for small intervals.

15. Which mode of heat transfer does NOT require any material medium?

Explanation: Radiation travels via electromagnetic waves and can propagate through a complete vacuum without any intervening medium.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. If the temperature of a black body is increased from $27^{\circ}\text{C}$ to $327^{\circ}\text{C}$, the ratio of total radiant energy emitted per second per unit area will be:

Explanation: Absolute temperatures are $T_1 = 27 + 273 = 300\text{ K}$ and $T_2 = 327 + 273 = 600\text{ K}$. Ratio of emissive powers $E_1 / E_2 = (T_1 / T_2)^4 = (300 / 600)^4 = (1 / 2)^4 = 1 / 16$. Thus, energy emitted increases by 16 times (or ratio is 1:16 from $T_1$ to $T_2$).

2. A body cools down from $60^{\circ}\text{C}$ to $50^{\circ}\text{C}$ in $5\text{ minutes}$. The surrounding temperature is $20^{\circ}\text{C}$. What is the time taken for it to cool from $50^{\circ}\text{C}$ to $40^{\circ}\text{C}$?

Explanation: According to Newton's law of cooling, rate of cooling is proportional to the average temperature excess over surroundings. As the body cools further, the temperature difference decreases, hence the rate of cooling decreases, making the time taken for the next $10^{\circ}\text{C}$ drop greater than $5\text{ minutes}$.

3. The wavelength corresponding to maximum energy emitted by a source is $\lambda_m$. As the temperature of the source is increased, the wavelength $\lambda_m$ will:

Explanation: By Wien's displacement law ($\lambda_m T = b$), $\lambda_m$ is inversely proportional to absolute temperature $T$. As temperature increases, $\lambda_m$ decreases, shifting the peak towards shorter wavelengths.

4. Two spheres of the same material and same radius have temperatures $1000\text{ K}$ and $500\text{ K}$ respectively. The ratio of their radiant power emitted is:

Explanation: Radiant power $P = E \cdot A = \sigma A T^4$. Since area $A$ and material emissivity are identical, $P_1 / P_2 = (T_1 / T_2)^4 = (1000 / 500)^4 = 2^4 = 16$.

5. Which of the following statements is correct regarding thermal radiation?

Explanation: Thermal radiations are electromagnetic waves (infrared/visible) and share optical properties such as traveling in straight lines at speed $c$, unaffected by electric and magnetic fields.

6. If the absorptive power of a body is $0.7$ and its reflection coefficient is $0.2$, what is its transmission coefficient?

Explanation: For any incident radiation, $a + r + t = 1$, where $a$ is absorptivity, $r$ is reflectivity, and $t$ is transmissivity. Given $a = 0.7$ and $r = 0.2$, $t = 1 - (0.7 + 0.2) = 0.1$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. The emissive power of a black body at temperature $T$ is $E$. If its temperature is reduced to $T/2$, its emissive power becomes:

Explanation: By Stefan-Boltzmann law, $E \propto T^4$. When $T$ is reduced to $T/2$, emissive power becomes $(1/2)^4 E = E / 16$.

2. According to Prevost's theory of heat exchange, thermal equilibrium is achieved when:

Explanation: In thermal equilibrium, emission and absorption continue, but the rate of emission exactly equals the rate of absorption.

3. A polished metallic surface is a:

Explanation: A polished metallic surface has low absorptivity (bad absorber), and by Kirchhoff's law, it must also have low emissivity (bad emitter).

4. The unit of Wien's constant ($b$) is:

Explanation: Since $\lambda_m T = b$, the unit is product of wavelength ($\text{m}$) and temperature ($\text{K}$), i.e., $\text{m K}$.

5. The rate of cooling of a body according to Newton's law of cooling is proportional to the temperature difference provided that difference is:

Explanation: Newton's law of cooling holds as an approximation strictly for small temperature differences with the surroundings.

6. If the solar constant is $S$, radius of the sun is $R_s$, and distance from sun to earth is $d$, the relation for solar constant is given by:

Explanation: Based on the formula given in study notes, $S = \frac{R_s^2}{d^2} \sigma T_s^4$.

7. Which misconception about thermal radiation is correct according to physical principles?

Explanation: All bodies above absolute zero ($0\text{ K}$) emit thermal radiation continuously.

8. What is the physical significance of emissivity $e$ of a body?

Explanation: Emissivity $e = E_{body}/E_{blackbody}$ is the ratio of real body emission to black body emission at the same temperature, ranging from $0$ to $1$.

9. In the integrated form of Newton's law of cooling, $T(t) = T_0 + (T_i - T_0)e^{-Kt}$, what happens to the temperature $T(t)$ as $t \to \infty$?

Explanation: As time $t$ becomes very large, the exponential term $e^{-Kt} \to 0$, so $T(t) \to T_0$, meaning the body eventually attains thermal equilibrium with the surroundings.

10. Why do cloudy nights tend to be warmer than clear nights?

Explanation: Clouds act as a blanket by absorbing outgoing terrestrial infrared radiation and re-emitting downward radiation towards the Earth, preventing rapid cooling.
