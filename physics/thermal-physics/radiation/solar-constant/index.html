Solar Constant and Surface Temperature of the Sun

The solar constant is the amount of radiant energy received per unit time on a unit area surface perpendicular to the Sun's rays at the mean Earth-Sun distance. It represents the extraterrestrial solar irradiance outside the Earth's atmosphere.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition, numerical value, units, basic Stefan-Boltzmann law application to find solar temperature, and inverse square law dependence.

PGT extension

Derivation of solar constant from Stefan-Boltzmann law, angular diameter of the Sun, atmospheric absorption corrections, and spectral distribution considerations.

Prerequisites

  • Stefan-Boltzmann law of blackbody radiation
  • Inverse square law of radiation intensity
  • Concept of Temperature and blackbody approximation

Definition and Standard Value

  • The solar constant (denoted by S) is defined as the total radiant energy received per second per unit normal area at the outer boundary of the Earth's atmosphere.
  • The globally accepted mean value of the solar constant is approximately 1.367 kW/m² or 1367 W/m².
  • It fluctuates by about ±3.3% due to the eccentricity of Earth's elliptical orbit around the Sun (perihelion and aphelion).

Inverse Square Law Dependence

  • Total energy emitted per second by the Sun is distributed uniformly over a sphere of radius equal to the mean Earth-Sun distance (R).
  • The intensity of radiation decreases inversely as the square of the distance from the source.
  • $S = (L) / (4 \pi R^{2})$, where L is the total luminosity of the Sun.

Determination of Solar Temperature

  • Treating the Sun as a blackbody of radius r at absolute temperature T, its total emissive power is given by Stefan-Boltzmann law.
  • By equating the total energy emitted by the Sun's surface to the energy intercepted at radius R, the effective surface temperature T can be calculated.
  • The calculated effective temperature of the photosphere is approximately 5778 K (~5800 K).

Atmospheric Effects and Terrestrial Insolation

  • Part of the incoming solar radiation is absorbed, scattered, and reflected by atmospheric gases, water vapor, and dust particles.
  • Only about 51% of the total solar radiation reaches the Earth's surface directly or diffusely.
  • Instruments used to measure the solar constant on Earth's surface include pyrheliometers and pyranometers, combined with atmospheric correction factors.

Derivations

Relation between Solar Constant and Solar Temperature

Conditions: Assuming the Sun behaves as a perfect blackbody of radius r at temperature T, and Earth is at a mean distance R from the Sun.

  • Step 1: Write the total energy emitted per second by the Sun using Stefan-Boltzmann law: $E = 4 \pi r^2 \sigma T^4$
  • Step 2: Assume this energy spreads uniformly over a spherical shell of radius R centered at the Sun. The intensity at distance R is the solar constant S: $S = \frac{4 \pi r^2 \sigma T^4}{4 \pi R^2} = \sigma T^4 \left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^2$
  • Step 3: Rearrange the expression to solve for the surface temperature of the Sun: $T^4 = \frac{S R^2}{\sigma r^2}$
  • Step 4: Take the fourth root on both sides to obtain the final formula for T: $T = \left(\frac{S R^2}{\sigma r^2}\right)^{1/4}$

Result: $T = \left(\frac{S R^2}{\sigma r^2}\right)^{1/4}$

Expression in terms of Angular Radius of the Sun

Conditions: Using the angular subtended radius θ of the Sun from Earth.

  • Step 1: The ratio of the Sun's radius r to the Earth-Sun distance R is equal to the sine of the angular radius (θ) of the Sun: sin\theta approx \frac{r}{R}
  • Step 2: Substitute this geometric relation into the solar constant equation $S = \sigma T^4 (\frac{r}{R})^2: S = \sigma T^4 (\sin\theta)^2$
  • Step 3: For very small angles, substitute sin\theta approx \theta in radians: $S = \sigma T^4 \theta^2$
  • Step 4: Express temperature in terms of S and θ: $T = \left(\frac{S}{\sigma \theta^2}\right)^{1/4}$

Result: $T = \left(\frac{S}{\sigma \theta^2}\right)^{1/4}$

Solved numericals

TGT core

If the solar constant is 1.4 kW/m² and the mean radius of the Earth is 6400 km, calculate the total solar power intercepted by the Earth.

  • Step 1: Identify given values: $S = 1.4 kW/m^2 = 1400 W/m^2$, $R_e = 6400 km = 6.4 \times 10^6 m$.
  • Step 2: Use formula for intercepted power: $P = S \pi R_e^2$.
  • Step 3: Substitute values: $P = 1400 \times \pi \times (6.4 \times 10^6)^2$.
  • Step 4: Calculate: $P = 1400 \times 3.1416 \times 4.096 \times 10^{13} = 1.8 \times 10^{17} W$.

Answer: 1.8 \times 10^{17} W

PGT extension

Assuming the Sun is a blackbody of radius 7 \times 10^8 m and the solar constant is 1367 W/m^2 at a distance of 1.5 \times 10^{11} m, find the effective surface temperature of the Sun. (Stefan-Boltzmann constant $\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8} W/m^2 K^4)$

  • Step 1: Use the temperature formula: $T = \left(\frac{S R^2}{\sigma r^2}\right)^{1/4}$
  • Step 2: Substitute the given values: S = 1367, $R = 1.5 \times 10^{11}$, $r = 7 \times 10^8$, sigm$a = 5.67 \times 10^{-8}$.
  • Step 3: Compute term inside parentheses: \frac{1367 \times (1.5 \times 10^{11})^2}{(5.67 \times 10^{-8}) \times (7 \times 10^8)^2} = \frac{1367 \times 2.25 \times 10^{22}}{5.67 \times 10^{-8} \times 4.9 \times 10^{17}} = \frac{3.07575 \times 10^{25}}{2.7783 \times 10^{10}} = 1.107 \times 10^{15}.
  • Step 4: Take the fourth root: $T = (1.107 \times 10^{15})^{1/4} \approx 5778 K$.

Answer: 5778 K

PGT extension

The angular diameter of the Sun subtended at the Earth is approximately 32 minutes of arc (0.0093 radians, or angular radius $\theta = 4.65 \times 10^{-3} rad)$. If the solar constant is 1367 W/m² and $\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8} SI units$, estimate the surface temperature of the Sun.

  • Step 1: Use angular radius formula: $T = \left(\frac{S}{\sigma \theta^2}\right)^{1/4}$
  • Step 2: Substitute values: S = 1367, sigm$a = 5.67 \times 10^{-8}$, $\theta = 4.65 \times 10^{-3}$.
  • Step 3: Calculate denominator: sigma \thet$a^2 = 5.67 \times 10^{-8} \times (4.65 \times 10^{-3})^2 = 5.67 \times 10^{-8} \times 2.162 \times 10^{-5} = 1.2259 \times 10^{-12}$.
  • Step 4: Divide S by denominator: \frac{1367}{1.2259 \times 10^{-12}} = 1.115 \times 10^{15}.
  • Step 5: Compute fourth root: $T = (1.115 \times 10^{15})^{1/4} = 5782 K$.

Answer: 5782 K

Assumptions and limitations

  • The Sun is assumed to radiate as a uniform, ideal blackbody radiator, which neglects local variations like sunspots and solar flares.
  • Atmospheric transmission losses are assumed constant or corrected via standard coefficients, though local weather and humidity cause real-time variations.
  • The Earth's orbit is treated as circular in basic calculations, ignoring the actual orbital eccentricity when precise instantaneous values are required.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The solar constant is strictly constant and never changes throughout the year.

Correct: The solar constant varies by about ±3.3% annually because Earth's orbit is elliptical.

Earth is closer to the Sun in January (perihelion) and farther in July (aphelion), changing the received intensity.

Common error: The solar constant is measured directly on the ground.

Correct: The solar constant is defined outside the Earth's atmosphere.

Atmospheric absorption and scattering filter out a significant portion of solar radiation before it reaches sea level.

Common error: The entire surface of the Earth receives the solar constant flux simultaneously.

Correct: Only a cross-sectional disc area (π R_e^2) receives direct beam flux, and when averaged over the entire spherical surface, the flux is S/4.

Spherical geometry distributes the incoming solar energy over a surface area four times larger than its cross-sectional projection.

Quick revision

  • Solar constant S equals approximately 1367 W/m² outside the Earth's atmosphere.
  • The inverse square law governs how solar radiation intensity drops with distance from the Sun: S proportional to 1 / R².
  • Sun's effective surface temperature is calculated using Stefan-Boltzmann law as $T = (S / (\sigma \theta^{2}))^{1/4} \approx 5800 K$.
  • Average solar radiation received over the entire Earth surface is S/4 due to spherical geometry.

Must remember

  • The globally accepted mean value of the solar constant ($S$) is approximately $1367\text{ W/m}^2$.
  • The solar constant fluctuates by about $\pm 3.3\%$ throughout the year due to the Earth's elliptical orbit.
  • The effective surface temperature of the Sun is approximately $5778\text{ K}$ or $\sim 5800\text{ K}$.
  • Only about $51\%$ of incoming solar radiation reaches the Earth's surface directly or diffusely due to atmospheric absorption, scattering, and reflection.
  • The average solar flux over the Earth's entire spherical surface is $S/4$, accounting for the spherical geometry.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the standard globally accepted mean value of the solar constant ($S$)?

Explanation: The standard mean value of the solar constant measured at the outer boundary of Earth's atmosphere is $1367\text{ W/m}^2$.

2. Why does the solar constant fluctuate by about $\pm 3.3\%$ annually?

Explanation: The Earth-Sun distance varies between perihelion and aphelion because of the elliptical orbit, changing the received intensity inversely as the square of the distance.

3. How is the solar constant $S$ related to the Sun's luminosity $L$ and mean Earth-Sun distance $R$?

Explanation: By the inverse square law, luminosity distributed over a sphere of radius $R$ gives $S = \frac{L}{4 \pi R^2}$.

4. What is the approximate effective surface temperature of the Sun ($T$) derived using Stefan-Boltzmann law?

Explanation: Equating the Sun's blackbody emission to the intercepted energy yields an effective photospheric temperature of approximately $5778\text{ K}$.

5. What percentage of incoming solar radiation approximately reaches the Earth's surface directly or diffusely?

Explanation: Atmospheric absorption, scattering, and reflection filter out a portion, leaving only about $51\%$ of total solar radiation to reach the Earth's surface.

6. Which instruments are primarily used to measure solar radiation and the solar constant?

Explanation: Pyrheliometers measure direct beam radiation, while pyranometers measure global solar radiation on a surface.

7. What is the average solar radiation flux per unit surface area over the entire Earth's surface?

Explanation: Since the cross-sectional area intercepting radiation is $\pi R_e^2$ and the total spherical surface area is $4 \pi R_e^2$, the averaged flux is $S/4$.

8. If the Sun is treated as a blackbody of radius $r$ at absolute temperature $T$, which formula correctly expresses the solar constant at distance $R$?

Explanation: Total power emitted is $4 \pi r^2 \sigma T^4$, and dividing by $4 \pi R^2$ gives $S = \sigma T^4 (r/R)^2$.

9. What is the misconception regarding where the solar constant is measured?

Explanation: A common misconception is that $S$ is measured on the ground; however, it is defined and measured outside the absorbing and scattering layers of Earth's atmosphere.

10. How does Wien's Displacement Law apply to solar radiation?

Explanation: Wien's displacement law states that $\lambda_m T = b$, where $b$ is Wien's constant, determining the peak wavelength of solar emission.

11. In terms of the angular radius $\theta$ of the Sun as seen from Earth, the surface temperature $T$ is given by:

Explanation: Since the ratio $r/R$ equals the angular radius $\theta$ (in radians) in the small angle approximation, $S = \sigma T^2 \theta^2$ yields $T = (S / \sigma \theta^2)^{1/4}$.

12. What is the total power intercepted by the Earth, treating the Earth as a flat circular disc of radius $R_e$?

Explanation: The Earth intercepts solar radiation over its projected cross-sectional area, which is $\pi R_e^2$, giving intercepted power $P = S \pi R_e^2$.

13. What physical law governs the blackbody emissive power of the Sun's surface?

Explanation: The total emissive power per unit area of a blackbody is given by the Stefan-Boltzmann law ($E = \sigma T^4$).

14. Which statement correctly describes the variation of the solar constant $S(\theta)$ with distance $R$ in an elliptical orbit?

Explanation: By inverse square law dependence, the solar constant at distance $R$ is scaled by $(R_0/R)^2$ relative to the mean solar constant $S_0$ at mean distance $R_0$.

15. What is the primary reason why the solar constant cannot be measured accurately without correction at sea level?

Explanation: The Earth's atmosphere significantly absorbs and scatters incoming radiation, requiring empirical corrections or space-based instruments to determine the true solar constant.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. If the solar constant is $S$, the radius of the Sun is $r$, the mean Earth-Sun distance is $R$, and $\sigma$ is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant, the surface temperature of the Sun is given by:

Explanation: From $S = \sigma T^4 (r/R)^2$, rearranging for $T$ gives $T = (S R^2 / \sigma r^2)^{1/4}$.

2. The intensity of solar radiation at the Earth's outer atmosphere is $1367\text{ W/m}^2$. If Earth's distance from the Sun were halved, the solar constant would become approximately:

Explanation: Since intensity is inversely proportional to the square of the distance ($S \propto 1/R^2$), halving the distance increases the solar constant by a factor of $2^2 = 4$. Thus, $1367 \times 4 = 5468\text{ W/m}^2$.

3. What is the average radiant flux density received over the entire surface of the Earth in terms of the solar constant $S$?

Explanation: The total power received is $S \pi R_e^2$, which is distributed over the entire surface area of the Earth $4 \pi R_e^2$. Dividing gives $S/4$.

4. The percentage variation in the solar constant between perihelion and aphelion is closest to:

Explanation: Due to the eccentricity of Earth's orbit, the solar constant fluctuates by about $\pm 3.3\%$ throughout the year.

5. Which of the following units is correct for expressing the solar constant?

Explanation: The solar constant represents radiant power received per unit normal area, making its SI unit Watts per square meter ($\text{W/m}^2$).

6. If the Sun is treated as a blackbody radiating at temperature $T$ with radius $r$, its total luminosity $L$ is expressed as:

Explanation: Luminosity is total power emitted by the Sun's surface area ($4 \pi r^2$) multiplied by emissive power ($\sigma T^4$), yielding $L = 4 \pi r^2 \sigma T^4$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. The solar constant depends on which of the following properties of the Sun?

Explanation: Through Stefan-Boltzmann law and luminosity, the solar constant depends on the Sun's radius and surface temperature.

2. Why is the effective temperature calculated for the Sun referred to as 'photospheric' temperature?

Explanation: The photosphere is the outer layer of the Sun that radiates blackbody-like thermal energy into space, from which $T \approx 5778\text{ K}$ is derived.

3. If the Earth-Sun distance were doubled, what would happen to the solar constant?

Explanation: According to the inverse square law ($S \propto 1/R^2$), doubling the distance reduces the solar constant by $2^2 = 4$ times.

4. What is the primary role of atmospheric correction factors when measuring the solar constant using ground instruments?

Explanation: Ground instruments must compensate for the attenuation caused by atmospheric gases, moisture, and dust.

5. If the angular radius of the Sun is $\theta$ and the solar constant is $S$, which expression correctly links $S$, $\sigma$, $\theta$, and $T$?

Explanation: Since $S = \sigma T^4 (r/R)^2$ and the angular radius $\theta = r/R$ in radians, it follows that $S = \sigma T^4 \theta^2$.

6. Which of the following statements about terrestrial insolation is correct?

Explanation: Roughly 49% of incoming radiation is intercepted by the atmosphere (reflected, scattered, or absorbed), leaving about 51% to reach the surface.

7. In the Stefan-Boltzmann relation $S = \sigma T^4 (r/R)^2$, what does the ratio $(r/R)$ represent geometrically?

Explanation: Geometrically, $r/R$ is the sine of the angular semi-diameter of the Sun, which for small angles approximates the angular radius $\theta$.

8. How does perihelion affect the solar constant received by Earth compared to the mean value?

Explanation: At perihelion (January), Earth is closest to the Sun ($R$ is minimum), making the solar constant reach its maximum value.

9. What is the main assumption made when treating the Sun as a blackbody in temperature calculations?

Explanation: Treating the Sun as a blackbody assumes it absorbs all incident radiation and emits thermal radiation strictly following Stefan-Boltzmann and Planck's laws.

10. If luminosity $L$ of the Sun increases by $10\%$ while its distance $R$ remains constant, how does the solar constant change?

Explanation: Since $S = \frac{L}{4 \pi R^2}$, $S$ is directly proportional to $L$. A $10\%$ increase in $L$ results in a $10\%$ increase in $S$.
