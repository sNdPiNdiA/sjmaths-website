Carnot Engine and Reversible Thermodynamic Cycles

A Carnot engine is an idealized thermal engine operating on a reversible thermodynamic cycle consisting of two isothermal and two adiabatic processes. It establishes the theoretical upper limit for the conversion of heat into mechanical work between two thermal reservoirs.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of efficiency, dependence on source and sink temperatures, calculation of work done, and direct application of Carnot efficiency formula.

PGT extension

Coefficient of performance of refrigerators, Carnot theorem proofs, entropy changes in reversible and irreversible cycles, and T-S diagram analysis.

Prerequisites

  • Concept of Temperature
  • First and Second Laws of Thermodynamics
  • Adiabatic and Isothermal Processes

Cycle Structure and Processes

  • The Carnot cycle comprises four successive reversible processes: isothermal expansion, adiabatic expansion, isothermal compression, and adiabatic compression.
  • During isothermal stages, the working substance is in thermal contact with a heat reservoir maintaining constant temperature T<sub>1</sub> or T<sub>2</sub>.
  • During adiabatic stages, the system is thermally isolated, and temperature changes from T<sub>1<sup>&#9circ;</sup></sub> to T<sub>2</sub> without heat exchange.

Efficiency of a Carnot Engine

  • Efficiency (&eta;) is defined as the ratio of net mechanical work done by the engine to the total heat absorbed from the high-temperature source.
  • It depends exclusively on the absolute temperatures of the source (T<sub>1</sub>) and the sink (T<sub>2</sub>), expressed as &eta; = 1 - (T<sub>2</sub> / T<sub>1</sub>).
  • Efficiency is always less than unity unless the sink temperature T<sub>2</sub> is absolute zero, which is unattainable by the Third Law of Thermodynamics.

Carnot's Theorem

  • No heat engine operating between two given thermal reservoirs can be more efficient than a reversible Carnot engine operating between the same two reservoirs.
  • All reversible engines operating between the same two temperatures have the same efficiency, regardless of the working substance used.
  • The theorem provides the fundamental basis for defining the thermodynamic absolute temperature scale.

Carnot Refrigerator and Heat Pump

  • Running a Carnot cycle in reverse converts it into a refrigerator or heat pump, transferring heat from a low-temperature sink to a high-temperature source by consuming external work.
  • The coefficient of performance (COP) for a refrigerator is given by COP<sub>ref</sub> = T<sub>2</sub> / (T<sub>1</sub> - T<sub>2</sub>).
  • For a heat pump, COP<sub>hp</sub> = T<sub>1</sub> / (T<sub>1</sub> - T<sub>2</sub>) = COP<sub>ref</sub> + 1.

Derivations

Derivation of Carnot Engine Efficiency

Conditions: Ideal gas working substance operating in a complete cyclic process.

  • Step 1: Write heat absorbed during isothermal expansion at temperature T<sub>1</sub> from volume V<sub>1</sub> to V<sub>2</sub>: Q<sub>1</sub> = nRT<sub>1</sub> ln(V<sub>2</sub>/V<sub>1</sub>).
  • Step 2: Write heat rejected during isothermal compression at temperature T<sub>2</sub> from volume V<sub>4</sub> to V<sub>3</sub>: Q<sub>2</sub> = nRT<sub>2</sub> ln(V<sub>3</sub>/V<sub>4</sub>).
  • Step 3: Relate volumes during intermediate adiabatic processes: T<sub>1</sub>V<sub>2</sub><sup>&gamma;-1</sup> = T<sub>2</sub>V<sub>3</sub><sup>&gamma;-1</sup> and T<sub>1</sub>V<sub>1</sub><sup>&gamma;-1</sup> = T<sub>2</sub>V<sub>4</sub><sup>&gamma;-1</sup>, yielding V<sub>2</sub>/V<sub>1</sub> = V<sub>3</sub>/V<sub>4</sub>.
  • Step 4: Substitute heat values into efficiency definition &eta; = 1 - (Q<sub>2</sub>/Q<sub>1</sub>).

Result: \eta = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1}

Coefficient of Performance of a Carnot Refrigerator

Conditions: Reversed Carnot cycle operating between temperatures T&lt;sub&gt;1&lt;/sub&gt; and T&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt;.

  • Step 1: Define COP as desired output over required input: COP = Q<sub>2</sub> / W.
  • Step 2: Apply conservation of energy to the cycle: W = Q<sub>1</sub> - Q<sub>2</sub>.
  • Step 3: Substitute Q<sub>1</sub>/Q<sub>2</sub> = T<sub>1</sub>/T<sub>2</sub> into the COP expression: COP = Q<sub>2</sub> / (Q<sub>1</sub> - Q<sub>2</sub>) = (Q<sub>2</sub>/Q<sub>1</sub>) / (1 - Q<sub>2</sub>/Q<sub>1</sub>).
  • Step 4: Replace heat ratio with absolute temperature ratio.

Result: \text{COP} = \frac{T_2}{T_1 - T_2}

Graphs and experiments

P-V Diagram of Carnot Cycle

Draw: A closed loop on a pressure-volume plane consisting of two hyperbolic isothermal curves (T&lt;sub&gt;1&lt;/sub&gt; and T&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt;) connected by two steeper adiabatic curves.

Interpret: The area enclosed by the cycle represents the net mechanical work done by the engine during one complete cycle.

Exam use: Frequently tested in identifying cycle directions, calculating work via area integration, and distinguishing isothermal from adiabatic slopes.

T-S (Temperature-Entropy) Diagram of Carnot Cycle

Draw: A rectangle on the temperature-entropy plane bounded by temperatures T&lt;sub&gt;1&lt;/sub&gt; and T&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt; and entropy values S&lt;sub&gt;A&lt;/sub&gt; and S&lt;sub&gt;B&lt;/sub&gt;.

Interpret: The area of the rectangle represents the total heat absorbed (Q&lt;sub&gt;1&lt;/sub&gt;), and the lower inner area represents heat rejected (Q&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt;). The difference represents net work.

Exam use: Used in advanced PGT questions to evaluate entropy transformations and simplify graphical efficiency calculations.

Efficiency vs Sink Temperature Graph

Draw: A downward sloping curve (or straight line depending on variable representation) showing &amp;eta; decreasing as T&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt; increases for a fixed T&lt;sub&gt;1&lt;/sub&gt;.

Interpret: Demonstrates that efficiency increases as the temperature difference (T&lt;sub&gt;1&lt;/sub&gt; - T&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt;) increases.

Exam use: Tested in conceptual multiple-choice questions regarding thermal plant optimization and limits of efficiency.

Solved numericals

TGT core

A Carnot engine operates between a source at 500 K and a sink at 300 K. If it absorbs 1200 J of heat from the source, calculate the work done by the engine.

  • Step 1: Identify given parameters: T<sub>1</sub> = 500 K, T<sub>2</sub> = 300 K, Q<sub>1</sub> = 1200 J.
  • Step 2: Calculate efficiency using &eta; = 1 - (T<sub>2</sub> / T<sub>1</sub>) = 1 - (300 / 500) = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4.
  • Step 3: Calculate work done using W = &eta; * Q<sub>1</sub> = 0.4 * 1200 J = 480 J.

Answer: 480 J

PGT extension

A Carnot refrigerator absorbs heat from a freezer maintained at -23°C and rejects heat to the surrounding room at 27°C. If 500 J of work is consumed per cycle, find the heat rejected to the room.

  • Step 1: Convert temperatures to Kelvin: T<sub>2</sub> = -23 + 273 = 250 K, T<sub>1</sub> = 27 + 273 = 300 K.
  • Step 2: Calculate COP of the refrigerator using COP = T<sub>2</sub> / (T<sub>1</sub> - T<sub>2</sub>) = 250 / (300 - 250) = 250 / 50 = 5.
  • Step 3: Relate COP to heat removed: COP = Q<sub>2</sub> / W &rArr; Q<sub>2</sub> = COP * W = 5 * 500 J = 2500 J.
  • Step 4: Calculate heat rejected Q<sub>1</sub> = Q<sub>2</sub> + W = 2500 J + 500 J = 3000 J.

Answer: 3000 J

PGT extension

By what percentage does the efficiency of a Carnot engine increase if the source temperature is raised from 500 K to 600 K, while the sink temperature remains constant at 300 K?

  • Step 1: Calculate initial efficiency &eta;<sub>i</sub> = 1 - (300 / 500) = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4 (40%).
  • Step 2: Calculate final efficiency &eta;<sub>f</sub> = 1 - (300 / 600) = 1 - 0.5 = 0.5 (50%).
  • Step 3: Calculate percentage increase in efficiency: ((&eta;<sub>f</sub> - &eta;<sub>i</sub>) / &eta;<sub>i</sub>) * 100 = ((0.5 - 0.4) / 0.4) * 100 = (0.1 / 0.4) * 100 = 25%.

Answer: 25%

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumption: Working parts are frictionless and thermal insulation is perfect, which is practically impossible to achieve in real engineering systems.
  • Limitation: The Carnot cycle is quasistatic and infinitely slow, rendering it useless for practical high-power output engines operating at finite speeds.

Common misconceptions

Common error: A Carnot engine can achieve 100% efficiency if the sink is kept at room temperature.

Correct: 100% efficiency is impossible unless the sink is at absolute zero (0 K), which violates the Third Law of Thermodynamics.

Efficiency depends on the ratio T&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt;/T&lt;sub&gt;1&lt;/sub&gt;; as long as T&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt; is above zero, some heat must be rejected.

Common error: The efficiency of a Carnot engine depends on the nature of the working fluid (e.g., whether it is a monoatomic or diatomic gas).

Correct: The efficiency depends solely on the temperatures of the source and sink.

Carnot's theorem proves that efficiency is independent of the working substance.

Quick revision

  • Carnot efficiency depends only on source and sink absolute temperatures: &eta; = 1 - (T<sub>2</sub> / T<sub>1</sub>).
  • Carnot Engine Efficiency: \eta = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{Q_1 - Q_2}{Q_1}
  • Work Done in Carnot Cycle: W = Q_1 - Q_2 = nR(T_1 - T_2)\ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right)

Must remember

  • The Carnot cycle consists of two isothermal and two adiabatic processes.
  • The efficiency of a Carnot engine depends exclusively on the absolute temperatures of the source ($T_1$) and sink ($T_2$): $\eta = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1}$.
  • Efficiency is always less than 1 (100%) unless $T_2 = 0\text{ K}$, which is unattainable due to the Third Law of Thermodynamics.
  • No engine can be more efficient than a reversible Carnot engine operating between the same two temperatures.
  • Coefficient of performance for a refrigerator is given by $\text{COP}_{\text{ref}} = \frac{T_2}{T_1 - T_2}$.
  • Coefficient of performance for a heat pump is $\text{COP}_{\text{hp}} = \frac{T_1}{T_1 - T_2} = \text{COP}_{\text{ref}} + 1$.
  • Net entropy change for a complete reversible Carnot cycle is zero ($\Delta S = 0$).
  • Efficiency of a Carnot engine is independent of the nature of the working substance.

Glossary

Carnot EngineAn idealized reversible heat engine operating between two thermal reservoirs that achieves the maximum possible efficiency.
Source ($T_1$)A high-temperature thermal reservoir that supplies heat $Q_1$ to the engine at a constant temperature.
Sink ($T_2$)A low-temperature thermal reservoir that absorbs rejected heat $Q_2$ from the engine at a constant temperature.
Coefficient of Performance (COP)The ratio of desired output (heat removed or delivered) to the work input for a refrigerator or heat pump.
Adiabatic ProcessA thermodynamic process in which no heat is exchanged with the surroundings ($Q = 0$).
Isothermal ProcessA thermodynamic process that occurs at a constant temperature ($\Delta T = 0$).

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following processes constitute a complete Carnot cycle?

Explanation: The Carnot cycle comprises four successive reversible processes: isothermal expansion, adiabatic expansion, isothermal compression, and adiabatic compression.

2. The efficiency ($\eta$) of a Carnot engine operating between temperatures $T_1$ and $T_2$ depends on:

Explanation: Efficiency depends exclusively on the absolute temperatures of the source ($T_1$) and the sink ($T_2$), expressed as $\eta = 1 - (T_2 / T_1)$.

3. What is the efficiency of a Carnot engine if the temperature of the sink is $0\text{ K}$?

Explanation: If $T_2 = 0\text{ K}$, then $\eta = 1 - (0 / T_1) = 1$, which corresponds to $100\%$ efficiency, though unattainable in practice due to the Third Law.

4. According to Carnot's Theorem, among all engines operating between the same two temperatures:

Explanation: Carnot's theorem states that no heat engine operating between two given thermal reservoirs can be more efficient than a reversible Carnot engine operating between the same two reservoirs.

5. The coefficient of performance ($\text{COP}_{\text{ref}}$) of a Carnot refrigerator working between temperatures $T_1$ and $T_2$ is given by:

Explanation: For a Carnot refrigerator, $\text{COP}_{\text{ref}} = \frac{T_2}{T_1 - T_2}$.

6. How is the coefficient of performance of a heat pump ($\text{COP}_{\text{hp}}$) related to that of a refrigerator ($\text{COP}_{\text{ref}}$)?

Explanation: For a heat pump, $\text{COP}_{\text{hp}} = \frac{T_1}{T_1 - T_2} = \text{COP}_{\text{ref}} + 1$.

7. During the adiabatic stages of a Carnot cycle, what happens to the temperature of the working substance?

Explanation: During adiabatic stages, the system is thermally isolated, and temperature changes from $T_1$ to $T_2$ without heat exchange.

8. What is the net entropy change of the universe for a complete reversible Carnot cycle?

Explanation: For a complete reversible cycle, $\Delta S = \frac{$Q_{1}$}{T_1} - \frac{$Q_{2}$}{T_2} = 0$, meaning no net entropy change for the universe.

9. If a Carnot engine absorbs $2000\text{ J}$ of heat from a source at $500\text{ K}$ and rejects heat to a sink at $300\text{ K}$, what is the work done by the engine?

Explanation: Efficiency $\eta = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1} = 1 - \frac{300}{500} = 0.4$. Work done $W = \eta $Q_{1}$ = 0.4 \times 2000\text{ J} = 800\text{ J}$.

10. Which law of thermodynamics states that absolute zero temperature cannot be attained in a finite number of steps, thus limiting Carnot efficiency to less than 100%?

Explanation: The Third Law of Thermodynamics establishes that absolute zero is unattainable, making $100\%$ efficiency impossible.

11. For an ideal gas working substance undergoing an adiabatic stroke in a Carnot cycle, which relation holds true between initial and final states of the stroke?

Explanation: The adiabatic relation for the Carnot stroke is $T_1 $V_{2}$^{\$\gamma$-1} = T_2 $V_{3}$^{\$\gamma$-1}$.

12. Can two different reversible Carnot engines operating between the same source and sink temperatures have different efficiencies?

Explanation: Carnot's theorem proves that all reversible engines operating between the same two temperatures have the same efficiency, regardless of the working substance used.

13. In a Carnot cycle, heat is absorbed during:

Explanation: Heat is absorbed from the high-temperature reservoir during the isothermal expansion process.

14. What is the unit of the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of a refrigerator?

Explanation: COP is a ratio of energies (heat removed to work input), making it a dimensionless quantity.

15. The efficiency of a Carnot engine is $50\%$ and the sink temperature is $300\text{ K}$. What is the source temperature?

Explanation: Using $\eta = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1}$, $0.5 = 1 - \frac{300}{T_1} \implies \frac{300}{T_1} = 0.5 \implies T_1 = 600\text{ K}$..

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. A Carnot engine has an efficiency of $25\%$ when operating between a source and a sink at temperature $T_2$. If the temperature of the source is increased by $50\text{ K}$, the efficiency becomes $50\%$. The original temperatures of the source ($T_1$) and sink ($T_2$) are:

Explanation: From $\eta_1 = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1} = 0.25 \implies \frac{T_2}{T_1} = 0.75 \implies T_2 = 0.75 T_1$. With source increased by $50\text{ K}$, $\eta_2 = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1 + 50} = 0.50 \implies \frac{T_2}{T_1 + 50} = 0.50$. Substituting $T_2 = 0.75 T_1$: $\frac{0.75 T_1}{T_1 + 50} = 0.50 \implies 0.75 T_1 = 0.50 T_1 + 25 \implies 0.25 T_1 = 25 \implies T_1 = 400\text{ K}$. Thus, $T_2 = 0.75 \times 400 = 300\text{ K}$.

2. If the ratio of heat absorbed to heat rejected by a Carnot engine is $\frac{5}{3}$, and the sink temperature is $27^{\circ}\text{C}$ ($300\text{ K}$), what is the temperature of the source?

Explanation: For a Carnot engine, $\frac{$Q_{1}$}{$Q_{2}$} = \frac{T_1}{T_2}$. Given $\frac{$Q_{1}$}{$Q_{2}$} = \frac{5}{3}$ and $T_2 = 300\text{ K}$, $\frac{T_1}{300} = \frac{5}{3} \implies T_1 = \frac{5}{3} \times 300 = 500\text{ K}$.

3. A Carnot refrigerator extracts heat from a cold storage at $200\text{ K}$ and rejects it to the atmosphere at $300\text{ K}$. If $600\text{ J}$ of work is done on the refrigerator per cycle, the heat delivered to the atmosphere is:

Explanation: $\text{COP}_{\text{ref}} = \frac{T_2}{T_1 - T_2} = \frac{200}{300 - 200} = \frac{200}{100} = 2$. Since $\text{COP}_{\text{ref}} = \frac{$Q_{2}$}{W} = 2$, heat extracted $$Q_{2}$ = 2 \times W = 2 \times 600 = 1200\text{ J}$. Heat rejected to atmosphere $$Q_{1}$ = $Q_{2}$ + W = 1200 + 600 = 1800\text{ J}$.

4. Two Carnot engines $A$ and $B$ are operated in series. Engine $A$ receives heat at $T_1$ and rejects heat to an intermediate reservoir at temperature $T$. Engine $B$ receives heat at $T$ and rejects heat to a sink at $T_2$. For the efficiencies of both engines to be equal, the intermediate temperature $T$ must be equal to:

Explanation: For equal efficiencies, $\eta_A = \eta_B \implies 1 - \frac{T}{T_1} = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T} \implies \frac{T}{T_1} = \frac{T_2}{T} \implies T^2 = T_1 T_2 \implies T = \sqrt{T_1 T_2}$ (geometric mean).

5. In a Carnot cycle, the working gas undergoes isothermal expansion at temperature $T_1$ from volume $$V_{1}$$ to $$V_{2}$$. The work done during this process is:

Explanation: Work done in an isothermal expansion for $n$ moles of an ideal gas at temperature $T_1$ from $$V_{1}$$ to $$V_{2}$$ is $W = n R T_1 \ln\left(\frac{$V_{2}$}{$V_{1}$}\right)$.

6. What is the power output of a Carnot engine that absorbs $5000\text{ J}$ of heat per cycle from a source at $500\text{ K}$, rejects heat to a sink at $300\text{ K}$, and completes one cycle in $2\text{ seconds}$?

Explanation: Efficiency $\eta = 1 - \frac{300}{500} = 0.4$. Work per cycle $W = 0.4 \times 5000\text{ J} = 2000\text{ J}$. Power $P = \frac{W}{t} = \frac{2000\text{ J}}{2\text{ s}} = 1000\text{ W}$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. The efficiency of a Carnot engine is $40\%$ when operating between source and sink temperatures $T_1$ and $T_2$. If both temperatures are increased by $100\text{ K}$ each, the new efficiency will be:

Explanation: Let $\eta = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1} = 0.4 \implies \frac{T_2}{T_1} = 0.6 \implies T_2 = 0.6 T_1$. With both increased by $100\text{ K}$, $\eta' = 1 - \frac{T_2 + 100}{T_1 + 100}$. Since $\frac{T_2 + 100}{T_1 + 100} > \frac{T_2}{T_1}$ (when $T_1 > T_2$), $\eta'$ will be less than $40\%$.

2. A reversible engine takes in heat from a reservoir at $527^{\circ}\text{C}$ and gives out heat to a sink at $127^{\circ}\text{C}$. The work done by the engine for $1000\text{ J}$ of absorbed heat is:

Explanation: $T_1 = 527 + 273 = 800\text{ K}$, $T_2 = 127 + 273 = 400\text{ K}$. Efficiency $\eta = 1 - \frac{400}{800} = 0.5$. Work done $W = 0.5 \times 1000\text{ J} = 500\text{ J}$.

3. Which of the following curves represents isothermal processes on a $P-V$ diagram for a Carnot cycle?

Explanation: Isothermal processes follow Boyle's Law ($PV = \text{constant}$), which forms rectangular hyperbolas on a $P-V$ diagram.

4. If a Carnot refrigerator has a COP of $4$, what is the ratio of the temperature of the sink ($T_2$) to the temperature of the source ($T_1$)?

Explanation: $\text{COP} = \frac{T_2}{T_1 - T_2} = 4 \implies T_2 = 4(T_1 - T_2) \implies T_2 = 4 T_1 - 4 T_2 \implies 5 T_2 = 4 T_1 \implies \frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{4}{5} = 0.8$.

5. In a Carnot cycle, during which processes is there no change in entropy of the system?

Explanation: Adiabatic processes are isentropic ($\Delta S = 0$) because they are reversible and involve no heat transfer ($dQ = 0$).

6. A Carnot engine operates between $200\text{ K}$ and $400\text{ K}$. If it absorbs $100\text{ kJ}$ of heat from the source, how much heat is rejected to the sink?

Explanation: Using $\frac{$Q_{2}$}{$Q_{1}$} = \frac{T_2}{T_1} \implies $Q_{2}$ = $Q_{1}$ \times \frac{T_2}{T_1} = 100\text{ kJ} \times \frac{200}{400} = 50\text{ kJ}$.

7. Why is a practical realization of a Carnot engine impossible?

Explanation: Frictionless movement and perfect thermal insulation are idealizations that cannot be achieved in the real world, making Carnot engines theoretical constructs.

8. If the efficiency of a Carnot engine is $\eta$, the ratio of heat rejected to heat absorbed ($$Q_{2}$ / $Q_{1}$$) is equal to:

Explanation: Since $\eta = 1 - \frac{$Q_{2}$}{$Q_{1}$}$, rearranging gives $\frac{$Q_{2}$}{$Q_{1}$} = 1 - \eta$.

9. An ideal heat pump is used to heat a building. If the outside temperature is $-3^{\circ}\text{C}$ ($270\text{ K}$) and the indoor temperature is $27^{\circ}\text{C}$ ($300\text{ K}$), what is the coefficient of performance of the heat pump?

Explanation: $\text{COP}_{\text{hp}} = \frac{T_1}{T_1 - T_2} = \frac{300}{300 - 270} = \frac{300}{30} = 10$.

10. On a Temperature-Entropy ($T-S$) diagram, what geometric shape does a reversible Carnot cycle form?

Explanation: On a $T-S$ diagram, isothermal processes are horizontal lines (constant $T$) and adiabatic processes are vertical lines (constant $S$), forming a rectangle.
