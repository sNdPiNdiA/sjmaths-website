Thermal Physics

Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics and Concept of Temperature

The Zeroth law of thermodynamics establishes thermal equilibrium as an equivalence relation between thermodynamic systems. It provides the logical foundation for the existence of temperature as a fundamental state property.

UP TGT ScienceUP PGT PhysicsStudy Notes + Practice

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of thermal equilibrium, statement and physical significance of the Zeroth law, and the working principle of mercury-in-glass and constant-volume gas thermometers.

PGT extension

Transitivity property of thermal equilibrium, mathematical formulation using empirical temperature functions, thermodynamic temperature scale, and microscopic justification via the kinetic theory of gases.

Prerequisites

  • Concept of macroscopic thermodynamic systems and boundaries
  • Thermal contact, diathermic and adiabatic walls
  • Equation of state for ideal gases

Thermal Equilibrium and Diathermic Walls

  • Two systems are in thermal contact if they can exchange energy in the form of heat through a conducting boundary known as a diathermic wall.
  • An adiabatic wall is an insulating boundary that prevents any heat transfer between systems.
  • Thermal equilibrium is reached when macroscopic properties (such as pressure, volume, and electrical resistance) of systems in thermal contact cease to change with time.

Statement of the Zeroth Law

  • If system A is in thermal equilibrium with system C, and system B is also in thermal equilibrium with system C, then system A and system B are in thermal equilibrium with each other.
  • Mathematically, if T_A = T_C and T_B = T_C, then T_A = T_B.
  • This law forms the basis of temperature measurement, validating the use of a third system (thermometer).

Concept of Temperature

  • Temperature is a scalar property that determines whether a system is in thermal equilibrium with other systems.
  • It is a macroscopic manifestation of the average translational kinetic energy of constituent molecules derived from the kinetic theory of gases.
  • Temperature equality is a necessary and sufficient condition for the absence of net heat flow between two bodies in thermal contact.

Empirical Temperature Scales

  • An empirical temperature scale is constructed using a thermometric property X of a substance that varies monotonically with temperature.
  • Linear interpolation between two fixed reference points (ice point and steam point) defines temperature as t(X) = 100 imes rac{X_t - X_0}{X_{100} - X_0}.
  • Different thermometers may yield slightly different empirical temperatures intermediate to the fixed points due to non-linear properties of thermometric substances.

Derivations

Existence of Empirical Temperature Function

Conditions: Three thermodynamic systems A, B, and C with equations of state involving pressures and volumes.

  • Let system A have equation of state f_A(P_A, V_A) = 0, system B have f_B(P_B, V_B) = 0, and system C have f_C(P_C, V_C) = 0.
  • When A and C are in thermal equilibrium, their coordinates are related by a constraint equation: F_1(P_A, V_A, P_C, V_C) = 0, which can be solved to express P_C = g_1(P_A, V_A, V_C).
  • Similarly, when B and C are in thermal equilibrium, P_C = g_2(P_B, V_B, V_C).
  • Equating the two expressions for P_C eliminates V_C, yielding a relation between the coordinates of A and B: h(P_A, V_A) = k(P_B, V_B).
  • The Zeroth law implies that this same relation holds when A and B are in direct thermal equilibrium, meaning both functions can be equated to a universal property called empirical temperature heta(P, V).

Result: \theta_A(P_A, V_A) = \theta_B(P_B, V_B) = \theta

Microscopic Temperature Derivation from Kinetic Theory

Conditions: Ideal gas containing N molecules enclosed in a volume V.

  • From kinetic theory, the pressure exerted by an ideal gas is given by P = \frac{1}{3}\frac{N}{V} m \bar{v^2}.
  • Using the ideal gas law equation of state, PV = N k_B T, where k_B is the Boltzmann constant.
  • Equating the two expressions for PV gives N k_B T = \frac{1}{3} N m \bar{v^2}.
  • Solving for temperature yields k_B T = \frac{2}{3} \left(\frac{1}{2} m \bar{v^2}\right), where \frac{1}{2} m \bar{v^2} is the average translational kinetic energy per molecule.

Result: T = \frac{2}{3k_B} E_{k, \text{avg}}

Solved numericals

TGT core

On a new scale of temperature (called the N scale), the freezing point of water is 0^\circ N and the boiling point is 150^\circ N. What is the temperature on the Celsius scale corresponding to 60^\circ N?

  • Use the linear temperature scale conversion formula: \frac{X - X_0}{X_{100} - X_0} = \frac{C - 0}{100 - 0}.
  • Substitute the given values for the N scale: X = 60, X_0 = 0, X_{100} = 150.
  • \frac{60 - 0}{150 - 0} = \frac{C}{100}.
  • \frac{60}{150} = \frac{C}{100} \implies C = \frac{2}{5} \times 100 = 40^\circ C.

Answer: 40^\circ C

PGT extension

The resistance of a platinum resistance thermometer at the ice point (0^\circ C) is 5.00 \Omega and at the steam point (100^\circ C) is 5.23 \Omega. When the thermometer is immersed in a hot liquid, its resistance becomes 5.79 \Omega. Calculate the temperature of the liquid on this platinum scale.

  • Assume a linear relation between resistance and temperature: t = \frac{R_t - R_0}{R_{100} - R_0} \times 100.
  • Substitute the given values: R_0 = 5.00 \Omega, R_{100} = 5.23 \Omega, and R_t = 5.79 \Omega.
  • R_t - R_0 = 5.79 - 5.00 = 0.79 \Omega.
  • R_{100} - R_0 = 5.23 - 5.00 = 0.23 \Omega.
  • t = \frac{0.79}{0.23} \times 100 = \frac{790}{23} \approx 34.35^\circ C.

Answer: 34.35^\circ C

PGT extension

A constant-volume gas thermometer registers a pressure of 1.50 \times 10^4 \text{ Pa} at the triple point of water (273.16 K). What pressure will it register when immersed in a chamber maintained at 350 K?

  • For a constant volume gas thermometer, pressure is directly proportional to absolute temperature: \frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2}.
  • Identify knowns: P_1 = 1.50 \times 10^4 \text{ Pa}, T_1 = 273.16 \text{ K}, T_2 = 350 \text{ K}.
  • Rearrange to solve for P_2: P_2 = P_1 \times \left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right).
  • P_2 = 1.50 \times 10^4 \times \left(\frac{350}{273.16}\right) = 1.50 \times 10^4 \times 1.2813.
  • P_2 = 1.922 \times 10^4 \text{ Pa}.

Answer: 1.92 imes 10^4 Pa

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumption: Systems in thermal equilibrium are isolated from external environmental fluctuations, which is idealized in practical setups.
  • Limitation: The Zeroth law establishes the existence of temperature but does not define internal energy or heat transfer magnitude, which requires the First and Second laws.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The Zeroth law states that heat flows equally between three bodies in contact.

Correct: The Zeroth law deals with thermal equilibrium where net heat flow is zero, not the rate or equality of heat content.

Heat is energy in transit due to a temperature difference; bodies in thermal equilibrium have no temperature difference and hence zero net heat flow.

Common error: Temperature is a measure of the total thermal energy contained in a body.

Correct: Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy per degree of freedom of the molecules, independent of system size.

Thermal energy (internal energy) depends on the total mass and number of particles, whereas temperature is an intensive property.

Quick revision

  • Zeroth law of thermodynamics introduces the concept of temperature and transitivity of thermal equilibrium: if A~C and B~C, then A~B.
  • Zeroth Law Equivalence: A \sim C \text{ and } B \sim C \implies A \sim B
  • Linear Thermometric Temperature Scale: T(X) = \frac{X - X_{triple}}{X_{steam} - X_{triple}} \times 273.16

Must remember

  • The Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics states that if system A is in thermal equilibrium with system C, and system B is also in thermal equilibrium with system C, then A and B are in thermal equilibrium with each other.
  • A diathermic wall allows heat exchange between systems, whereas an adiabatic wall is an insulating boundary preventing heat transfer.
  • Temperature is an intensive scalar property representing the macroscopic manifestation of average translational kinetic energy per molecule ($E_k = \frac{3}{2} k_B T$).
  • Thermal equilibrium is achieved when macroscopic properties such as pressure, volume, and electrical resistance cease to change with time.
  • The absolute temperature scale defines the triple point of water precisely at $273.16\text{ K}$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following physical laws forms the fundamental basis for the measurement of temperature?

Explanation: The Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics validates the use of a third system (thermometer) to compare temperatures, establishing the concept of temperature and its measurement.

2. Two systems are separated by an adiabatic wall. What can be inferred about their states?

Explanation: An adiabatic wall is an insulating boundary that explicitly prevents any heat transfer between systems in contact.

3. Thermal equilibrium between two systems in thermal contact is characterized by:

Explanation: Thermal equilibrium is reached when macroscopic properties such as pressure, volume, and electrical resistance cease to change with time.

4. If system $A$ is in thermal equilibrium with system $C$, and system $B$ is also in thermal equilibrium with system $C$, then $A$ and $B$ are in thermal equilibrium with each other. This principle is known as:

Explanation: This statement defines the Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics, highlighting the transitivity property of thermal equilibrium.

5. Temperature is considered a scalar property that determines whether a system is in thermal equilibrium with other systems. Microscopically, it is a manifestation of:

Explanation: From the kinetic theory of gases, temperature is proportional to the average translational kinetic energy of constituent molecules ($E_k = \frac{3}{2} k_B T$).

6. What is the standard temperature assigned to the triple point of water on the Kelvin absolute temperature scale?

Explanation: By international convention, the triple point of water is assigned the exact value of $273.16\text{ K}$ on the Kelvin temperature scale.

7. The relation between Celsius temperature ($t_{^{\circ}C}$) and Fahrenheit temperature ($t_{^{\circ}F}$) is given by:

Explanation: The standard conversion formula between Celsius and Fahrenheit scales is $\frac{t_{^{\circ}C}}{5} = \frac{t_{^{\circ}F} - 32}{9}$.

8. A constant volume gas thermometer measures temperature based on the variation of which property?

Explanation: A constant volume gas thermometer relies on the proportional change in pressure ($P$) of a fixed mass of gas with temperature at a constant volume ($V$).

9. Why do different empirical thermometers (e.g., mercury-in-glass and constant-volume gas) give slightly different readings at temperatures intermediate to the fixed points?

Explanation: Different thermometric substances exhibit slightly non-linear behaviors between fixed points, causing slight variations in empirical temperature readings.

10. What is the condition for net heat flow between two bodies placed in thermal contact through a diathermic wall?

Explanation: Net heat flow occurs only when there is a temperature difference between two bodies in thermal contact; zero temperature difference implies zero net heat flow.

11. Using a platinum resistance thermometer, if the resistance at the triple point of water is $R_{tr}$ and at temperature $T$ is $R$, the temperature is calculated as:

Explanation: For a constant volume gas or resistance thermometer utilizing a single fixed point, $T = 273.16 \left(\frac{R}{R_{tr}}\right)$.

12. Which of the following statements correctly distinguishes temperature from internal thermal energy?

Explanation: Temperature is an intensive property independent of system size, whereas internal energy is an extensive property proportional to the total mass and particle count.

13. What is the value of the Celsius temperature corresponding to $0\text{ K}$ on the absolute thermodynamic scale?

Explanation: Using the conversion $T(K) = t(^{\circ}C) + 273.15$, absolute zero ($0\text{ K}$) corresponds to $-273.15\text{ }^{\circ}\text{C}$.

14. In the ideal gas temperature scale, the temperature is defined in the limit of:

Explanation: The ideal gas temperature scale uses the low pressure limit ($P \to 0$) where real gases behave ideally.

15. What is the average translational kinetic energy of a single molecule of a monatomic ideal gas at temperature $T$?

Explanation: The average translational kinetic energy per molecule for a monatomic ideal gas is given by $E_k = \frac{3}{2} k_B T$, where $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant.

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which boundary completely isolates a system thermally from its surroundings?

Explanation: An adiabatic wall is an insulating boundary that prevents any heat transfer between systems, ensuring thermal isolation.

2. What is the physical significance of thermal equilibrium between two systems?

Explanation: Thermal equilibrium implies that temperature equality has been reached and there is no net heat flow between the systems.

3. According to kinetic theory, the absolute temperature of an ideal gas is directly proportional to:

Explanation: Absolute temperature is directly proportional to the average translational kinetic energy per molecule ($E_k = \frac{3}{2} k_B T$).

4. If a constant volume gas thermometer registers pressure $$P_{0}$$ at $0\text{ }^{\circ}\text{C}$ and $P_{100}$ at $100\text{ }^{\circ}\text{C}$, the linear interpolation formula for temperature $t$ is:

Explanation: Linear interpolation between two fixed reference points defines temperature as $t(X) = 100 \times \frac{$X_{t}$ - $X_{0}$}{X_{100} - $X_{0}$}$.

5. Which of the following is an intensive property of a thermodynamic system?

Explanation: Temperature is an intensive property because its value does not depend on the size or mass of the system.

Explanation: The Zeroth Law formalizes the transitivity of thermal equilibrium: if $A \sim C$ and $B \sim C$, then $A \sim B$.

8. Why does a thermometer inserted into a system register the temperature of the system accurately?

Explanation: A thermometer reaches thermal equilibrium with the system via a diathermic wall, after which its thermometric property reflects the common temperature.

9. The constant volume gas thermometer is considered a standard thermometer because:

Explanation: Constant volume gas thermometers are standard because dilute real gases behave very closely to ideal gases, providing consistent temperature scales.
