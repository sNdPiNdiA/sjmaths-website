Absolute Temperature and the Kelvin Scale

Absolute temperature is a thermodynamic measure of temperature scaled from absolute zero, the theoretical state where particles possess minimum thermal motion and zero entropy. It is fundamentally defined via the second law of thermodynamics using Carnot engines, making it independent of the working substance.

Definition and Thermodynamic Basis

  • Absolute temperature is measured on the Kelvin scale, where 0 K represents absolute zero.
  • It is independent of the thermometric properties of any particular substance, unlike mercury-in-glass or constant-volume gas thermometers.
  • The thermodynamic temperature scale is established using the efficiencies of reversible Carnot engines operating between two heat reservoirs.

Ideal Gas Scale vs Thermodynamic Scale

  • In the limit of zero pressure, an ideal gas thermometer defines an absolute temperature scale that is identical to the thermodynamic Kelvin scale.
  • The pressure of a given mass of gas at constant volume extrapolates to zero at absolute zero temperature (-273.15 deg C).
  • Real gases deviate from this ideal behavior at high pressures and low temperatures due to intermolecular forces.

Triple Point of Water Reference

  • Since 1954 and reaffirmed in the 2019 SI redefinition, the Kelvin is defined by fixing the Boltzmann constant k to exactly 1.380649 x 10-23 J K-1.
  • Historically and practically, it is anchored to the triple point of water, where ice, liquid water, and water vapor coexist in equilibrium.
  • The temperature of the triple point of water is defined as exactly 273.16 K.

Kinetic Interpretation

  • According to the kinetic theory of gases, the absolute temperature of an ideal gas is directly proportional to the average translational kinetic energy per molecule.
  • Equipartition of energy states that each active degree of freedom contributes (1/2)kBT to the internal energy per molecule.
  • At absolute zero, molecular motion ceases entirely, though quantum zero-point energy remains in harmonic oscillators.

Derivations

Thermodynamic Temperature Scale from Carnot Efficiency

Conditions: Reversible Carnot engine operating between temperatures T_1 and T_2

  • The efficiency eta of any reversible engine operating between two heat reservoirs at empirical temperatures theta_1 and theta_2 depends only on these temperatures: et$a = 1 - Q_2/Q_1 = f(\theta_1$, theta_2).
  • Consider three engines operating with heat exchanges Q_1, Q_2, and Q_3 at temperatures theta_1, theta_2, and theta_3 such that $Q_2/Q_1 = f(\theta_1$, theta_2), $Q_3/Q_2 = f(\theta_2$, theta_3), and $Q_3/Q_1 = f(\theta_1$, theta_3).
  • Since $Q_3/Q_1 = (Q_3/Q_2) x (Q_2/Q_1)$, it follows that f(theta_1, thet$a_3) = f(\theta_1$, theta_2) x f(theta_2, theta_3).
  • This functional equation implies that the ratio of heats can be written as the ratio of a universal function of temperature alone: $Q_2/Q_1 = T_2/T_1$.
  • Defining T as this absolute temperature scale gives et$a = 1 - T_2/T_1$.

Result: frac{T_2}{$T_1 = \frac{Q_2}{Q_1}$

Relation between Absolute Temperature and Mean Kinetic Energy

Conditions: Ideal gas containing N molecules

  • The pressure of an ideal gas from kinetic theory is given by $P = (1/3)(Nm/V)v_{rms}^2$.
  • Using the ideal gas law P$V = N k_B T$, equate the two expressions: (1/3)Nm v_{rms}^2 = N k_B T$.
  • Rearrange to solve for the total translational kinetic energy $E_k = (3/2)N k_B T$.
  • Divide by N to find the average translational kinetic energy per molecule: <$E_k> = (3/2)k_B T$.

Result: $T = \frac{2}{3k_B} \langle E_k \rangle$

Solved numericals

TGT core

At what temperature is the Fahrenheit scale reading equal to twice the Celsius scale reading?

  • Let the Celsius temperature be t. Then the Fahrenheit temperature is F = 2t.
  • Use the conversion formula: (F - 32)/$9 = t/5$.
  • Substitute $F = 2t into the equation: (2t - 32)/9 = t/5$.
  • Cross-multiply and solve: 5(2t - 32) = 9$t => 10t - 160 = 9t => t = 160 °C$.
  • Verify Fahrenheit reading: F = 2(160) = 320°F. Check: (320 - 32)/$9 = 288/9 = 32$, and 160/5 = 32. Both match.

Answer: 160 °C (or 433.15 K)

PGT extension

Calculate the root mean square velocity of oxygen molecules ($M = 32 g/mol) at an absolute temperature of 300 K$. Given $R = 8.314 J mol^-1 K^-1$.

  • Identify given parameters: T = 300 K, $M = 32 g/mol = 32 x 10^-3 kg/mol$, $R = 8.314 J mol^-1 K^-1$.
  • Write the formula for root mean square velocity: $v_{rms} = \sqrt(3RT / M)$.
  • Substitute values: $v_{rms} = \sqrt((3 x 8.314 x 300) / (32 x 10^-3))$.
  • Calculate numerator: 3 x 8.314 x 300 = 7482.6.
  • Divide by molar mass: 7482.6 / 0.032 = 233831.25.
  • Take square root: sqrt(233831.25) approx 483.56 m/s.

Answer: 483.56 m s^-1

PGT extension

A Carnot engine absorbs 1200 kJ of heat from a reservoir at 500 K and rejects heat to a sink at 300 K. Calculate the work done by the engine and the entropy change of the universe during one cycle.

  • Identify given values: $Q_H = 1200 kJ$, $T_H = 500 K$, $T_C = 300 K$.
  • Find efficiency of the Carnot engine: et$a = 1 - (T_C / T_H) = 1 - (300/500) = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4$.
  • Calculate work done: $W = \eta x Q_H = 0.4 x 1200 kJ = 480 kJ$.
  • Calculate heat rejected to sink: $Q_C = Q_H - W = 1200 - 480 = 720 kJ$.
  • Calculate entropy change of source: Delta $S_H = -Q_H / T_H = -1200 kJ / 500 K = -2.4 kJ/K$.
  • Calculate entropy change of sink: Delta $S_C = Q_C / T_C = 720 kJ / 300 K = +2.4 kJ/K$.
  • Total entropy change of the universe for a reversible cycle: Delta $S_univ = \delta S_H + \delta S_C = -2.4 + 2.4 = 0$.

Answer: Work done = 480 kJ, Universe entropy change = 0

Assumptions and limitations

  • Absolute zero (0 K) is unattainable in finite operational steps, as stated by the Third Law of Thermodynamics.
  • Real gases liquefy or solidify before reaching absolute zero, making the ideal gas scale extrapolation hypothetical at extreme lows.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Absolute zero means molecules stop moving completely and have zero total energy.

Correct: Molecules possess minimum energy corresponding to quantum zero-point energy, but translational kinetic energy vanishes macroscopically.

Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle prevents particles from having simultaneously zero position uncertainty and zero momentum.

Common error: The Kelvin scale depends on the properties of the thermometric substance used.

Correct: The thermodynamic Kelvin scale is completely independent of any working substance.

It is defined via Carnot engine efficiencies operating between heat reservoirs.

Quick revision

  • Absolute zero is 0 K or -273.15 °C, where ideal gas pressure and molecular translational kinetic energy extrapolate to zero.
  • Celsius to Kelvin Conversion: $T = t + 273.15$
  • Fahrenheit to Absolute Temperature: $T = \frac{5}{9}(F - 32) + 273.15$

Must remember

  • Absolute zero is defined as $0\text{ K}$, which corresponds to $-273.15^{\circ}\text{C}$.
  • The thermodynamic Kelvin scale is independent of the thermometric properties of any particular substance.
  • The triple point of water is defined as exactly $273.16\text{ K}$ where ice, liquid water, and water vapor coexist.
  • According to kinetic theory, the average translational kinetic energy per molecule is given by $\langle E \rangle = \frac{3}{2}k_B T$.
  • In the 2019 SI redefinition, the Kelvin is fixed by assigning an exact numerical value to the Boltzmann constant $k_B$.

Glossary

Absolute TemperatureTemperature measured on the Kelvin scale starting from absolute zero ($0\text{ K}$).
Triple Point of WaterThe unique temperature ($273.16\text{ K}$) and pressure at which ice, liquid water, and water vapor are in equilibrium.
Carnot EfficiencyThe maximum theoretical efficiency of a heat engine operating between two absolute temperatures $T_C$ and $T_H$, given by $\eta = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$.
Zero-point EnergyThe lowest possible energy that a quantum mechanical physical system may possess, remaining present even at absolute zero ($0\text{ K}$).

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the temperature of the triple point of water on the Kelvin scale?

Explanation: By definition, the triple point of water is set at exactly $273.16\text{ K}$.

2. Which of the following statements about absolute zero ($0\text{ K}$) is scientifically accurate?

Explanation: At absolute zero, classical translational kinetic energy vanishes, but harmonic oscillators retain quantum zero-point energy due to Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle.

3. The thermodynamic temperature scale is fundamentally established using:

Explanation: The thermodynamic temperature scale is defined independently of working substances via reversible Carnot engine efficiencies.

4. What is the conversion formula between Celsius temperature ($t$) and Kelvin absolute temperature ($T$)?

Explanation: The standard conversion relation is $T = t + 273.15$.

5. According to the kinetic theory of gases, the average translational kinetic energy per molecule of a monatomic ideal gas is:

Explanation: The average translational kinetic energy per molecule is $\frac{3}{2}k_B T$ (while $\frac{3}{2}RT$ is per mole).

6. Real gases deviate from ideal gas absolute temperature behavior at high pressures and low temperatures due to:

Explanation: Real gases deviate from ideality at high pressures and low temperatures because intermolecular attractive/repulsive forces and finite molecular volumes become significant.

7. What is the root mean square velocity ($v_{rms}$) formula for an ideal gas of molar mass $M$ at absolute temperature $T$?

Explanation: The standard expression derived from kinetic theory is $v_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$.

8. In a constant volume gas thermometer, the absolute temperature $T$ in the limit of vanishing pressure is given by:

Explanation: The relation is $T = 273.16 \left(\frac{P}{P_{tp}}\right)$ in the limit as $P \to 0$.

9. How is the Kelvin defined in the 2019 SI system redefinition?

Explanation: Since the 2019 SI redefinition, the Kelvin is defined by fixing the numerical value of the Boltzmann constant $k_B$ to $1.380649 \times 10^{-23}\text{ J K}^{-1}$.

10. According to the law of equipartition of energy, how much energy is associated with each quadratic degree of freedom per molecule in thermal equilibrium?

Explanation: Each active quadratic degree of freedom contributes an average energy of $\frac{1}{2}k_B T$ per molecule.

11. Convert a Fahrenheit temperature of $98.6^{\circ}\text{F}$ to absolute temperature in Kelvin.

Explanation: Using $T = \frac{5}{9}(98.6 - 32) + 273.15 = \frac{5}{9}(66.6) + 273.15 = 37 + 273.15 = 310.15\text{ K}$.

12. What is the maximum theoretical efficiency of a Carnot engine operating between a source at $500\text{ K}$ and a sink at $300\text{ K}$?

Explanation: $\eta = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H} = 1 - \frac{300}{500} = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4$ or $40\%$.

13. What is the internal energy of an ideal gas with $f$ degrees of freedom containing $n$ moles at absolute temperature $T$?

Explanation: The total internal energy is given by $U = \frac{f}{2}nRT$.

14. Which temperature scale property distinguishes the thermodynamic scale from mercury-in-glass thermometer scales?

Explanation: The thermodynamic temperature scale is universal and independent of any particular thermometric substance.

15. If the absolute temperature of an ideal gas is doubled, what happens to the root mean square velocity $v_{rms}$ of its molecules?

Explanation: Since $v_{rms} \propto \sqrt{T}$, doubling $T$ increases $v_{rms}$ by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The pressure of a gas at constant volume is $200\text{ Pa}$ at the triple point of water. When immersed in a thermostat, the pressure becomes $250\text{ Pa}$. What is the temperature of the thermostat?

Explanation: Using $T = 273.16 \left(\frac{P}{P_{tp}}\right) = 273.16 \times \left(\frac{250}{200}\right) = 273.16 \times 1.25 = 341.45\text{ K}$.

2. At what temperature do the Celsius and Fahrenheit temperature scales coincide?

Explanation: Setting $t_C = t_F = x$, $\frac{x}{5} = \frac{x - 32}{9} \implies 9x = 5x - 160 \implies 4x = -160 \implies x = -40^{\circ}$.

3. The efficiency of a Carnot engine is $50%$, and the temperature of the sink is $300\text{ K}$. What is the temperature of the source?

Explanation: $\eta = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H} \implies 0.5 = 1 - \frac{300}{T_H} \implies \frac{300}{T_H} = 0.5 \implies T_H = 600\text{ K}$.

4. If the root mean square velocity of hydrogen molecules at a given temperature is $v$, what is the rms velocity of oxygen molecules at the same absolute temperature?

Explanation: Since $v_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$, $v_{rms} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}$. The molar mass of $O_2$ is $32\text{ g/mol}$ and $H_2$ is $2\text{ g/mol}$. Thus, $\frac{v_{O_2}}{v_{H_2}} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{32}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{16}} = \frac{1}{4}$.

5. What is the average translational kinetic energy of $2\text{ moles}$ of an ideal gas at temperature $300\text{ K}$? (Take $R = 8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$)

Explanation: Total translational kinetic energy for $n$ moles is $E = \frac{3}{2}nRT = \frac{3}{2} \times 2 \times 8.314 \times 300 = 3 \times 8.314 \times 300 = 7482.6\text{ J}$? Wait: $3 \times 8.314 = 24.942$, $24.942 \times 300 = 7482.6$. Let's check options: Option 1 is $3741.3$ (which is half) or Option 2 is $7482.6$. Correct index is 1 ($7482.6\text{ J}$).

6. Which misconception about absolute zero is corrected by quantum mechanics?

Explanation: Classical physics assumed total cessation of motion and zero energy at absolute zero, but quantum mechanics proves zero-point energy persists.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. A centigrade thermometer and a Kelvin thermometer are used to measure the temperature of a system. If the Kelvin thermometer reads $300\text{ K}$, what does the centigrade thermometer read?

Explanation: $t = T - 273.15 = 300 - 273.15 = 26.85^{\circ}\text{C}$.

2. The triple point of water is designated as $273.16\text{ K}$. In Celsius, this is exactly:

Explanation: $t = 273.16 - 273.15 = 0.01^{\circ}\text{C}$.

3. What is the internal energy of $1\text{ mole}$ of a rigid diatomic gas at absolute temperature $T$?

Explanation: A rigid diatomic gas has $5$ degrees of freedom ($3$ translational and $2$ rotational), so $U = \frac{5}{2}nRT = \frac{5}{2}RT$.

4. Two ideal gas thermometers $A$ and $B$ use different gases. As the reference pressure at the triple point of water goes to zero, what can be said about their temperature readings at any given thermal state?

Explanation: In the limit of vanishing pressure ($P \to 0$), all ideal gas thermometers yield identical absolute temperature scales matching the thermodynamic Kelvin scale.

5. If a Carnot engine operates between $400\text{ K}$ and $200\text{ K}$, what is its efficiency?

Explanation: \eta = 1 - \frac{200}{400} = 1 - 0.5 = 0.5$ or $50\%$.

6. What is the ratio of root mean square speeds of oxygen ($O_2$) and helium ($He$) gases at the same absolute temperature? (Atomic mass of $He = 4$, Molar mass of $O_2 = 32$)

Explanation: \frac{v_{O_2}}{v_{He}} = \sqrt{\frac{M_{He}}{M_{O_2}}} = \sqrt{\frac{4}{32}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{8}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{8}}$.

7. Which of the following physical quantities is directly proportional to the absolute temperature of an ideal gas?

Explanation: The average translational kinetic energy per mole is $\frac{3}{2}RT$, which is directly proportional to $T$.

8. The absolute zero temperature on the Fahrenheit scale is approximately:

Explanation: Using $T = \frac{5}{9}(F - 32) + 273.15 = 0 \implies \frac{5}{9}(F - 32) = -273.15 \implies F - 32 = -491.67 \implies F = -459.67^{\circ}\text{F}$.

9. Why can absolute zero ($0\text{ K}$) never be attained experimentally in a finite number of steps?

Explanation: The Third Law of Thermodynamics states that it is impossible by any procedure, no matter how idealized, to reduce any system to absolute zero in a finite number of operations.

10. In the relation $T = 273.16 \left(\frac{P}{P_{tp}}\right)$, the requirement $P \to 0$ is necessary because:

Explanation: Extrapolating to zero pressure eliminates the effects of intermolecular interactions, ensuring the gas behaves ideally and matches the thermodynamic temperature scale.
