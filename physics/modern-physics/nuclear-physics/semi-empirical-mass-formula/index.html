Semi-Empirical Mass Formula (Weizsäcker's Formula)

The semi-empirical mass formula is a mathematical expression used to predict the binding energy and mass of an atomic nucleus based on the liquid drop model. It accounts for five distinct energetic contributions: volume, surface, Coulomb, asymmetry, and pairing energies.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Understanding the individual terms (volume, surface, Coulomb, asymmetry, pairing) of the mass formula, dependence of binding energy on mass number A and atomic number Z, and identifying stable isobars.

PGT extension

Detailed derivation of the volume and surface energy terms, finding the most stable charge Z for a given mass number A by minimization of mass, and calculating beta decay endpoints.

Prerequisites

  • Liquid drop model of the nucleus
  • Nuclear binding energy and mass defect
  • Coulomb electrostatic potential energy

Volume Energy Term

  • Arises from the short-range attractive nature of strong nuclear forces exhibiting saturation.
  • Proportional to the total number of nucleons, hence directly proportional to mass number A.
  • Contributes positively to the total binding energy of the nucleus: E_v = a_v A.

Surface Energy Term

  • Correction to the volume term because nucleons on the surface have fewer neighboring nucleons to interact with.
  • Proportional to the surface area of the nucleus, which scales as R^2 or A^{2/3}.
  • Acts to decrease the total binding energy: E_s = -a_s A^{2/3}.

Coulomb Energy Term

  • Represents the mutual electrostatic repulsion among the Z protons distributed uniformly throughout the nuclear volume.
  • Proportional to Z(Z-1) and inversely proportional to the nuclear radius R (proportional to A^{1/3}).
  • Reduces the binding energy: E_c = -a_c \frac{Z(Z-1)}{A^{1/3}}.

Asymmetry and Pairing Energy Terms

  • Asymmetry energy accounts for the stability preference of having an equal number of neutrons and protons (N = Z), reducing binding for Z != N.
  • Pairing energy reflects the quantum mechanical tendency of nucleons to form pairs, making even-even nuclei most stable and odd-odd nuclei least stable.
  • Asymmetry term: E_a = -a_a \frac{(N-Z)^2}{A}; Pairing term: E_p = \pm \delta(A) or 0 depending on neutron-proton parity.

Derivations

Derivation of Volume and Surface Energy Terms

Conditions: Assuming the nucleus behaves like an incompressible liquid drop of radius R = r_0 A^{1/3}.

  • Assume total nuclear volume V is proportional to mass number A, so V = \frac{4}{3} \pi R^3 = \frac{4}{3} \pi r_0^3 A.
  • Since each interior nucleon interacts with a constant number of neighbors, the total binding energy due to these interactions is proportional to A, giving the volume term a_v A.
  • Nucleons on the surface lack neighbors on the outer side, causing a reduction in binding energy proportional to the surface area 4\pi R^2 \propto A^{2/3}.

Result: Binding energy contribution = a_v A - a_s A^{2/3}

Derivation of the Most Stable Charge Z_0 for a Given Mass Number A

Conditions: For odd-A isobars where pairing energy delta is zero, differentiate the mass or binding energy equation with respect to Z.

  • Write the total binding energy as B(A, Z) = a_v A - a_s A^{2/3} - a_c \frac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}} - a_a \frac{(A-2Z)^2}{A}.
  • Set the derivative of binding energy with respect to Z equal to zero to find the maximum binding energy (minimum mass): \frac{\partial B}{\partial Z} = 0.
  • Differentiating gives: -\frac{2 a_c Z}{A^{1/3}} + \frac{4 a_a (A-2Z)}{A} = 0.

Result: Z_0 = \frac{A}{2 + \frac{a_c}{2 a_a} A^{2/3}}

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the volume energy contribution to the total binding energy of a nucleus with mass number A = 64, given the volume energy coefficient a_v = 15.8 MeV.

  • Identify the formula for volume energy: E_v = a_v A.
  • Substitute the given values: a_v = 15.8 MeV and A = 64.
  • Calculate: E_v = 15.8 \times 64 = 1011.2 \text{ MeV}.

Answer: 1011.2 MeV

PGT extension

Using the semi-empirical mass formula, find the surface energy contribution for a nucleus with A = 125, given the surface energy coefficient a_s = 18 MeV.

  • Identify the formula for surface energy: E_s = a_s A^{2/3}.
  • Find A^{2/3}: (125)^{2/3} = (5^3)^{2/3} = 5^2 = 25.
  • Multiply by a_s: E_s = 18 \times 25 = 450 \text{ MeV}.

Answer: 450 MeV

PGT extension

For an isobar with mass number A = 27, calculate the asymmetry energy term if the atomic number Z = 13 and asymmetry coefficient a_a = 23 MeV.

  • Determine the number of neutrons N = A - Z = 27 - 13 = 14.
  • Identify the asymmetry energy formula: E_a = a_a \frac{(N-Z)^2}{A}.
  • Substitute values: E_a = 23 \times \frac{(14-13)^2}{27} = 23 \times \frac{1}{27} = \frac{23}{27} \approx 0.852 \text{ MeV}.

Answer: 0.852 MeV

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumption: Nucleus is spherical and incompressible with uniform density, limiting applicability to deformed or halo nuclei.
  • Limitation: Fails for very light nuclei (A < 15) where shell effects and quantum granularity dominate over macroscopic liquid drop behavior.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The Coulomb term adds to the nuclear binding energy because protons attract each other.

Correct: The Coulomb term subtracts from the binding energy.

Protons experience electrostatic repulsion, which destabilizes the nucleus and reduces total binding energy.

Common error: Odd-odd nuclei are the most stable because they have equal numbers of unpaired nucleons.

Correct: Even-even nuclei are the most stable.

Nucleon pairing leads to an energetic bonus (pairing energy), making even-even configurations tightly bound and odd-odd the least bound.

Quick revision

  • Volume term scales as A, surface term as A^{2/3}, Coulomb term as Z^2 A^{-1/3}, asymmetry term as (N-Z)^2 A^{-1}, and pairing term is plus/minus delta for even-even/odd-odd nuclei.
  • Semi-Empirical Mass Formula (Total Binding Energy): B(A, Z) = a_v A - a_s A^{2/3} - a_c \frac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}} - a_a \frac{(A-2Z)^2}{A} \pm \delta(A)
  • Volume Energy Term: E_v = a_v A

Must remember

  • The volume energy term in Weizsäcker's formula is proportional to mass number $A$ and reflects the saturation property of nuclear forces ($E_v = a_v A$).
  • The surface energy term acts as a correction reducing binding energy and scales with surface area as $A^{2/3}$.
  • The Coulomb energy term accounts for proton-proton electrostatic repulsion and is proportional to $Z(Z-1)/A^{1/3}$.
  • The asymmetry energy term penalizes unequal numbers of neutrons and protons, scaling as $(N-Z)^2/A$.
  • Even-even nuclei have positive pairing energy ($+\delta$), making them the most stable, whereas odd-odd nuclei are the least stable.
  • The condition for beta stability for a given mass number $A$ is derived by minimizing the nuclear mass with respect to $Z$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which term in the Semi-Empirical Mass Formula is directly proportional to the mass number $A$?

Explanation: The volume energy term is given by $E_v = a_v A$, which is directly proportional to the total number of nucleons $A$ due to the saturation property of nuclear forces.

2. The surface energy term in Weizsäcker's formula varies with mass number $A$ as:

Explanation: The surface energy term corrects for nucleons on the surface having fewer neighbors. It is proportional to the nuclear surface area, which scales as $R^2 \propto A^{2/3}$.

3. How does the Coulomb energy term affect the total binding energy of a nucleus?

Explanation: The Coulomb term ($E_c = -a_c \frac{Z(Z-1)}{A^{1/3}}$) represents the electrostatic repulsion among protons, which destabilizes the nucleus and reduces total binding energy.

4. Which type of nuclei generally exhibits the highest stability according to the pairing energy term?

Explanation: Even-even nuclei have all nucleons paired, contributing a positive pairing energy ($+\delta$), making them the most tightly bound and stable.

5. The asymmetry energy term in the Semi-Empirical Mass Formula accounts for the preference of nuclei to have:

Explanation: The asymmetry term penalizes deviations from $N = Z$ due to consequences of the Pauli exclusion principle, favoring equal numbers of neutrons and protons.

6. What is the approximate empirical value of the volume energy constant $a_v$?

Explanation: The volume energy constant $a_v$ is empirically determined to be approximately $15.8 \text{ MeV}$ per nucleon.

7. For an odd-$A$ nucleus, what is the value of the pairing energy term $E_p$?

Explanation: For odd-$A$ nuclei, there is an unpaired nucleon, resulting in a pairing energy contribution of $E_p = 0$.

8. The radius of a nucleus is proportional to $A^{1/3}$. How does this dependence affect the Coulomb energy denominator?

Explanation: Since nuclear radius $R \propto A^{1/3}$, the electrostatic potential energy term contains $R$ in the denominator, resulting in a dependence on $A^{1/3}$.

9. Which term in the Semi-Empirical Mass Formula becomes negative for odd-odd nuclei?

Explanation: The pairing term is $-\delta(A)$ for odd-odd nuclei, representing a decrease in binding energy due to lack of pairing.

10. Why do nucleons on the surface of a nucleus contribute less to the binding energy compared to interior nucleons?

Explanation: Surface nucleons have fewer neighboring nucleons surrounding them, reducing the attractive strong force interactions, which leads to the surface energy correction.

11. The asymmetry energy term is proportional to which of the following expressions?

Explanation: The asymmetry energy is given by $E_a = -a_a \frac{(N-Z)^2}{A}$, reflecting energy penalty for neutron-proton imbalance.

12. Semi-Empirical Mass Formula is generally considered valid for nuclei with mass number:

Explanation: The liquid drop model basis of the semi-empirical mass formula breaks down for very light nuclei; it is typically valid for nuclei with $A > 15$.

13. What is the main physical origin of the volume energy term?

Explanation: The volume term arises from the short-range, saturating nature of strong nuclear forces where each interior nucleon interacts with a nearly constant number of neighbors.

14. In the context of the nuclear mass formula, how is total nuclear mass $M(Z, A)$ related to total binding energy $B(A, Z)$?

Explanation: Total nuclear mass is the sum of constituent nucleon masses minus the mass equivalent of the total binding energy: $M(Z, A) = Z m_p + (A - Z) m_n - \frac{1}{c^2} B(A, Z)$.

15. Which constant in the Semi-Empirical Mass Formula has the largest numerical value in MeV?

Explanation: Among the typical empirical constants, $a_a \approx 23.2 \text{ MeV}$ is the largest, followed by $a_s \approx 18.3 \text{ MeV}$ and $a_v \approx 15.8 \text{ MeV}$.

Explanation: The asymmetry energy term $E_a = -a_a \frac{(N-Z)^2}{A}$ explicitly depends on the neutron-proton asymmetry $(N-Z)$.

1. What is the physical reason behind the negative sign in the surface energy term $-a_s A^{2/3}$?

Explanation: Nucleons at the surface have fewer adjacent neighbors than those in the core, resulting in fewer strong force bonds and a net reduction in total binding energy.

2. In the Coulomb energy term $E_c = -a_c \frac{Z(Z-1)}{A^{1/3}}$, why is the numerator $Z(Z-1)$ instead of $Z^2$?

Explanation: A proton cannot repel itself, so the electrostatic potential energy summing over pairs considers $Z(Z-1)$ pairs, which correctly excludes self-interaction.

4. For a nucleus with $N = Z$, what is the contribution of the asymmetry energy term?

Explanation: Since $N = Z$, the term $(N-Z)^2 / A$ becomes zero, making the asymmetry energy contribution $E_a = 0$.

5. Which of the following statements about pairing energy $\delta(A)$ is correct?

Explanation: The pairing parameter is given by $\delta = \frac{a_p}{A^{3/4}}$, meaning it decreases as mass number $A$ increases.

6. In the liquid drop model analogy used for the Semi-Empirical Mass Formula, the nucleus is treated as:

Explanation: Weizsäcker's formula is based on the liquid drop model, where the nucleus is modeled as a drop of quantum-fluid nuclear matter exhibiting volume, surface tension, and electrostatic properties.

7. Why does the binding energy per group nucleon peak around $A \approx 60$ (Iron group)?

Explanation: The binding energy per nucleon reaches a maximum near $A = 60$ because the positive volume energy dominates for lower $A$ while the negative surface, Coulomb, and asymmetry penalties dominate for higher $A$.

8. If a nucleus undergoes spontaneous fission, which term in the Semi-Empirical Mass Formula primarily drives the instability for very heavy nuclei ($A > 230$)?

Explanation: For very large $A$, the Coulomb repulsion term ($E_c \propto Z^2/A^{1/3}$) grows much faster ($Z^2 \approx A^2$) than the surface term, eventually making heavy nuclei unstable against fission.

9. What is the primary physical phenomenon explained by the Pauli exclusion principle in the context of Weizsäcker's formula?

Explanation: The asymmetry energy arises from the Pauli exclusion principle, which forces nucleons into higher energy quantum states when $N \neq Z$, costing extra energy.

10. In calculating nuclear mass using the semi-empirical mass formula, why are electron masses typically neglected or handled separately?

Explanation: The formula estimates the total nuclear binding energy and nuclear mass $M(Z, A)$, whereas atomic masses include the rest mass of $Z$ electrons and their binding energies.
