Elementary Theory of Alpha Decay

Alpha decay is a radioactive process in which an unstable atomic nucleus ejects an alpha particle, transforming into a daughter nucleus with a mass number smaller by 4 and an atomic number smaller by 2. The elementary quantum-mechanical theory explains this process through quantum tunneling of the pre-formed alpha particle through a Coulomb potential barrier.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of alpha decay, conservation laws of mass number and atomic number, Geiger-Nuttall empirical law relating decay constant and range of alpha particles, and basic energetics (Q-value).

PGT extension

Quantum mechanical tunneling model (Gamow theory), barrier penetration probability, derivation of decay constant, and dependence on disintegration energy.

Prerequisites

  • Conservation of energy and linear momentum in nuclear reactions
  • De Broglie wavelength and elementary wave mechanics
  • Coulomb's law and potential energy of point charges

Energetics of Alpha Decay

  • The Q-value of alpha decay is defined as $Q = (m_P - m_D - m_alpha) c^{2}$, representing the total energy released during the transformation.
  • The disintegration energy Q is shared between the recoiling daughter nucleus and the alpha particle inversely proportional to their masses to conserve linear momentum.
  • For an alpha decay to occur spontaneously, the Q-value must be strictly positive (Q > 0).

The Coulomb Potential Barrier

  • Outside the nuclear radius R, the potential energy experienced by the alpha particle is purely electrostatic: V(r) = (1 / 4πε_0) (2 Z e²) / r.
  • At the nuclear surface, the attractive nuclear forces form a potential well, while the repulsive electrostatic force forms a barrier of height V_max exceeding the kinetic energy of the alpha particle.
  • Classically, an alpha particle with kinetic energy less than the barrier height cannot escape the nucleus.

Quantum Mechanical Tunneling (Gamow Theory)

  • According to quantum mechanics, particles possess a non-zero probability wave that penetrates classically forbidden regions.
  • George Gamow treated the alpha particle as an independent entity bouncing back and forth inside the nuclear potential well, striking the barrier f times per second.
  • The decay constant λ is the product of the assault frequency f and the quantum transmission coefficient T ($\lambda = f T)$.

Geiger-Nuttall Law

  • Geiger and Nuttall established an empirical linear relation between the logarithm of the decay constant (λ) and the logarithm of the range (R) of the emitted alpha particle in air: log $\lambda = A \log R + B$.
  • Higher energy alpha particles have shorter lifetimes (larger decay constants) because they encounter a thinner and lower effective potential barrier.
  • The law is naturally explained by Gamow's tunneling theory.

Derivations

Transmission Coefficient for a Coulomb Barrier

Conditions: Assuming a rectangular barrier approximation or WKB approximation for the Coulomb potential V(r) = (2Ze²/(4πε_0 r))

  • Apply the WKB approximation to find the decay constant or barrier penetration factor $P = exp(-2 \int _{R}^{r_0} k(r) dr)$, where k(r) = &sqrt;(2μ(V(r) - E)) / ℏ.
  • Evaluate the integral between the nuclear radius R and the classical turning point r_0 where V($r_0) = E$.
  • Perform the integration analytically by substituting $r = r_0 \cos^{2}\theta$ to obtain the Gamow factor.
  • Express the transmission coefficient P in terms of the charge numbers, fine structure constant, and velocity of the alpha particle.

Result: $P = exp(-2 G)$, where $G = ( \pi Z_1 Z_2 e^{2} / 4\pi\epsilon_0 \hbar v ) - 4 ( Z_1 Z_2 R / 4\pi\epsilon_0 \hbar^2 \mu )^(1/2)$

Kinetic Energy Distribution of Decay Products

Conditions: Rest frame of the parent nucleus, conservation of total energy and linear momentum.

  • Set initial momentum of parent nucleus to zero: $p_P = 0$.
  • Apply momentum conservation: p_alpha + $p_D = 0$, hence $p_alpha = -p_D$.
  • Express kinetic energy in terms of momentum: $K_alpha = p_alpha^2 / (2m_alpha)$ and $K_D = p_D^2 / (2m_D)$.
  • Substitute $Q = K_alpha + K_D$ and eliminate momentum to relate K_alpha directly to Q and masses.

Result: $K_alpha = Q \left( \frac{m_D}{m_alpha + m_D} \right)$

Graphs and experiments

Nuclear Potential Well and Coulomb Barrier

Draw: Potential energy V(r) on y-axis versus radial distance r on x-axis, showing a deep attractive well inside nuclear radius R, a Coulomb barrier peak of height V_max, and a horizontal line representing alpha particle energy E.

Interpret: Illustrates that the alpha particle energy E is less than the barrier peak V_max, making classical escape impossible, yet quantum tunneling allows escape.

Exam use: Frequently asked in conceptual multiple-choice questions to explain why alpha particles emerge with energy less than the barrier height.

Geiger-Nuttall Plot

Draw: Straight lines plotting log(λ) against log(R) for different radioactive series (Uranium, Thorium, Actinium).

Interpret: Shows parallel straight lines confirming that isotopes with higher energy (longer range R) have exponentially shorter half-lives.

Exam use: Tested via interpretation of slopes and intercepts or matching decay constants with particle ranges.

Cloud Chamber Track of Alpha Particles

Draw: Straight, dense, parallel ionization tracks of uniform length originating from a point source.

Interpret: Demonstrates that monoenergetic alpha particles travel a well-defined range in matter before losing all their energy.

Exam use: Used in experimental physics questions related to the detection and characteristic properties of alpha radiation.

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the Q-value of the alpha decay of 226Ra to 222Rn, given the masses: m(^226Ra) = 226.0254 u, m(^222Rn) = 222.0175 u, and m(α) = 4.0015 u. (1 $u = 931.5 MeV/c^2)$

  • Write the mass defect Δ$m = m(Parent) - m(Daughter) - m(\alpha)$.
  • Substitute the given atomic masses: Δ$m = 226.0254 u - 222.0175 u - 4.0015 u$.
  • Calculate Δ$m = 226.0254 - 226.0190 = 0.0064 u$.
  • Multiply by 931.5 MeV/u to find Q: $Q = 0.0064 \times 931.5 MeV$.

Answer: 5.96 MeV

PGT extension

An alpha particle is emitted with a kinetic energy of 4.8 MeV from a nucleus with atomic number Z = 90. Assuming the daughter nucleus has mass number A = 228, find the kinetic energy of the recoiling daughter nucleus.

  • Identify the given values: $K_alpha = 4.8 MeV$, m_alpha ≈ 4 u, m_D ≈ A - $4 = 228 - 4 = 224 u$.
  • Use momentum conservation relation between kinetic energies: K_D / $K_alpha = m_alpha / m_D$.
  • Substitute the mass values: $K_D = 4.8 MeV \times (4 / 224)$.
  • Evaluate the division: 4 / $224 = 1 / 56$, so $K_D = 4.8 / 56 MeV$.

Answer: 0.0857 MeV (or 85.7 keV)

PGT extension

If the disintegration energy Q for an alpha decay is 5.4 MeV, determine the exact kinetic energy of the emitted alpha particle, taking the exact mass of the alpha particle as 4.0015 u and the daughter nucleus mass as 218 u.

  • Recall the formula relating alpha kinetic energy to Q: $K_alpha = Q [ m_D / (m_alpha + m_D) ]$.
  • Substitute the given mass values: $m_D = 218 u$, $m_alpha = 4.0015 u$, total mass = 222.0015 u.
  • Calculate the mass ratio: 218 / 222.0015 = 0.98197.
  • Multiply by $Q = 5.4 MeV: K_alpha = 5.4 \times 0.98197$.

Answer: 5.302 MeV

Assumptions and limitations

  • The alpha particle is assumed to pre-form inside the nucleus prior to emission, though nuclear structure models view it as a cluster formation probability.
  • The WKB approximation used in calculating the barrier penetration assumes the potential varies slowly compared to the de Broglie wavelength of the alpha particle.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Alpha particles possess kinetic energy greater than the height of the Coulomb barrier inside the nucleus.

Correct: The kinetic energy of the alpha particle is less than the barrier peak, and it escapes via quantum mechanical tunneling.

Classically, an energy deficit would prevent escape; quantum mechanics permits barrier penetration.

Common error: All alpha particles emitted from a specific radioactive isotope have continuous kinetic energy spectra like beta decay.

Correct: Alpha particles from a given transition are monoenergetic.

Two-body decay kinematics dictates a fixed energy sharing between the alpha particle and the daughter nucleus.

Quick revision

  • Alpha decay reduces mass number by 4 and atomic number by 2.
  • Spontaneous alpha decay requires a positive Q-value.
  • Gamow theory successfully explains alpha decay via quantum tunneling through the Coulomb barrier.
  • Geiger-Nuttall law states a linear relation between log(λ) and log(R).

Must remember

  • The Q-value of alpha decay must be strictly positive ($Q > 0$) for the decay to occur spontaneously.
  • Alpha particles are monoenergetic because alpha decay is a two-body decay process governed by momentum and energy conservation.
  • Classically, an alpha particle cannot escape the nucleus because its kinetic energy is less than the Coulomb barrier height.
  • Gamow's theory explains alpha decay using quantum mechanical tunneling through the Coulomb barrier.
  • The Geiger-Nuttall law establishes a linear relationship between $\log \lambda$ and $\log R$, where $R$ is the range of the alpha particle in air.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the primary condition for an $\alpha$ decay to occur spontaneously?

Explanation: For an $\alpha$ decay to occur spontaneously, the total disintegration energy must be strictly positive ($Q > 0$), meaning the parent mass exceeds the sum of the daughter and $\alpha$ particle masses.

2. Are $\alpha$ particles emitted from a given radioactive isotope monoenergetic or continuous in energy?

Explanation: $\alpha$ particles from a specific transition are monoenergetic because $\alpha$ decay is a two-body decay process, resulting in a fixed energy sharing between the $\alpha$ particle and the daughter nucleus.

3. How is the kinetic energy of the emitted $\alpha$ particle ($K_\alpha$) expressed in terms of the Q-value and masses?

Explanation: From momentum conservation, the kinetic energy of the $\alpha$ particle is shared inversely proportional to masses, yielding $K_\alpha = \frac{M_D}{M_P} Q$ where $M_P = M_D + m_\alpha$.

4. What physical mechanism allows an $\alpha$ particle to escape the nucleus even when its kinetic energy is less than the Coulomb barrier height?

Explanation: According to quantum mechanics, particles have a non-zero probability wave that penetrates classically forbidden regions, a phenomenon known as quantum mechanical tunneling (Gamow theory).

5. What does the Geiger-Nuttall law relate?

Explanation: The Geiger-Nuttall law establishes an empirical linear relation between the logarithm of the decay constant ($\lambda$) and the logarithm of the range ($R$) of the $\alpha$ particle in air: $\log \lambda = A \log R + B$.

6. Why do higher energy $\alpha$ particles generally exhibit shorter lifetimes (larger decay constants)?

Explanation: Higher energy $\alpha$ particles penetrate closer to the classical turning point, effectively encountering a thinner and lower potential barrier, which significantly increases their transmission coefficient and decay constant.

7. According to Gamow's theory, the decay constant $\lambda$ is expressed as the product of the assault frequency $f$ and which parameter?

Explanation: Gamow treated the $\alpha$ particle as bouncing back and forth inside the nuclear potential well, striking the barrier $f$ times per second, so the decay constant is given by $\lambda = f T$.

8. What is the nature of the potential energy function $V(r)$ experienced by an $\alpha$ particle outside the nuclear radius $R$?

Explanation: Outside the nuclear radius $R$, the attractive nuclear forces become negligible, and the potential energy experienced by the $\alpha$ particle is purely electrostatic: $V(r) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{2(Z-2)e^2}{r}$.

9. If the parent nucleus has mass $M_P$, daughter nucleus $M_D$, and $\alpha$ particle mass $m_\alpha$, what is the expression for the Q-value?

Explanation: The Q-value represents the mass energy converted into kinetic energy: $Q = [m(Parent) - m(Daughter) - m(\alpha)] c^2$.

10. Which of the following describes the kinetic energy of the daughter nucleus $K_D$ in $\alpha$ decay?

Explanation: By energy and momentum conservation, the daughter nucleus receives a kinetic energy inversely proportional to its mass share: $K_D = \frac{m_\alpha}{M_P} Q$.

11. In the context of the Gamow factor $G$, what does $r_0$ represent?

Explanation: In the Gamow factor integral $G = \frac{\sqrt{2m}}{\hbar} \int_{R}^{r_0} \sqrt{V(r) - E} \, dr$, $R$ is the nuclear radius and $r_0$ is the classical turning point where $V(r_0) = E$.

12. What misconception is corrected by Gamow's theory of $\alpha$ decay?

Explanation: Classically, it was believed $\alpha$ particles needed kinetic energy greater than the barrier height to escape; Gamow corrected this by showing they escape via quantum mechanical tunneling despite having lower energy.

13. Which parameter in the Geiger-Nuttall law ($\log \lambda = A \log R + B$) represents the slope?

Explanation: The equation is in the linear form $y = m x + c$, where $A$ is the slope and $B$ is the intercept.

14. What conservation law ensures that the $\alpha$ particle and daughter nucleus recoil in opposite directions with specific energy ratios?

Explanation: Linear momentum conservation requires that the momenta of the $\alpha$ particle and the recoiling daughter nucleus are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction ($p_\alpha = p_D$).शैक्षणिक

15. What is the units of the transmission coefficient $T$ in Gamow's decay theory?

Explanation: The transmission coefficient $T$ is a probability ratio representing the fraction of wave function transmitted through the barrier, making it dimensionless.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. In an $\alpha$ decay process, if the Q-value is $5.5\text{ MeV}$, what is the approximate kinetic energy of the emitted $\alpha$ particle from a parent nucleus with mass number $A = 212$?

Explanation: Using $K_\alpha \approx \frac{A - 4}{A} Q = \frac{208}{212} \times 5.5\text{ MeV} \approx 5.39\text{ MeV} \approx 5.4\text{ MeV}$ using mass number proportionality.

2. According to the Geiger-Nuttall law, if the range of an $\alpha$ particle in air is increased, how does its decay constant $\lambda$ behave?

Explanation: Since $\log \lambda = A \log R + B$ with positive slope $A$, a larger range $R$ corresponds to higher energy and a larger decay constant $\lambda$ (shorter half-life).

3. What is the physical significance of the classical turning point $r_0$ in the Gamow barrier penetration integral?

Explanation: The classical turning point $r_0$ is where the kinetic energy $E$ equals the potential energy $V(r_0)$, marking the boundary of the classically forbidden region.

4. If the nuclear radius $R$ of a parent nucleus decreases while the Q-value remains constant, how is the transmission coefficient $T$ affected?

Explanation: A smaller nuclear radius increases the width of the Coulomb barrier that the $\alpha$ particle must tunnel through, increasing the Gamow factor $G$ and decreasing the transmission coefficient $T$.

5. In the disintegration energy equation $Q = [m(Parent) - m(Daughter) - m(\alpha)] c^2$, what does a negative Q-value signify?

Explanation: A negative Q-value means mass-energy must be supplied from the outside for the reaction to take place; hence, spontaneous $\alpha$ decay ($Q > 0$) cannot occur.

6. Why are $\alpha$ particles monoenergetic whereas $\beta$ particles exhibit a continuous energy spectrum?

Explanation: $\alpha$ decay is a two-body decay, leading to a fixed energy sharing dictated by momentum conservation. $\beta$ decay involves three bodies (nucleus, electron/positron, and neutrino/antineutrino), distributing energy continuously.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Calculate the kinetic energy of an $\alpha$ particle emitted in a decay with a Q-value of $4.0\text{ MeV}$ from a parent nucleus of mass number $A = 236$.

Explanation: $K_\alpha = \frac{A_D}{A_P} Q = \frac{232}{236} \times 4.0\text{ MeV} \approx 3.932\text{ MeV}$.

2. Which of the following correctly represents the dependence of the Gamow factor $G$ on particle velocity $\nu$ in low-energy barrier penetration?

Explanation: The approximate transmission probability formula $T \approx \exp(-2 \frac{e^2}{\hbar \nu} Z)$ shows that the exponent and attenuation factor depend inversely on velocity $\nu$.

3. What is the primary reason why heavy nuclei undergo $\alpha$ decay rather than proton emission?

Explanation: The $\alpha$ particle possesses a very high binding energy ($28.3\text{ MeV}$), making its formation inside heavy nuclei energetically favorable ($Q > 0$), whereas single proton emission usually has a negative Q-value.

4. If the decay constant of a radioactive substance emitting $\alpha$ particles is $\lambda = 1.5 \times 10^{-5} \text{ s}^{-1}$ and the assault frequency is $f = 10^{21} \text{ s}^{-1}$, what is the approximate transmission coefficient $T$?

Explanation: Since $\lambda = f T$, we have $T = \frac{\lambda}{f} = \frac{1.5 \times 10^{-5}}{10^{21}} = 1.5 \times 10^{-26}$.

5. In the Coulomb potential barrier formula $V(r) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{2(Z-2)e^2}{r}$, what does the term $2(Z-2)$ represent?

Explanation: The numerator represents the electrostatic interaction between the $\alpha$ particle ($2e$) and the daughter nucleus of atomic number $Z' = Z-2$ (charge $(Z-2)e$).

6. How does an increase in the atomic number $Z$ of the parent nucleus affect the height of the Coulomb potential barrier?

Explanation: The Coulomb potential $V(r)$ is directly proportional to the atomic number $Z$ of the parent nucleus, so a higher $Z$ raises the electrostatic barrier peak.

7. Which of the following statements about Gamow's theory is INCORRECT?

Explanation: Gamow's theory specifically accounts for $\alpha$ particles having kinetic energy *less* than the barrier peak, overcoming it via tunneling.

8. If the kinetic energy of the daughter nucleus is $K_D$ and the $\alpha$ particle is $K_\alpha$, what is the ratio $\frac{K_D}{K_\alpha}$?

Explanation: Since $K_\alpha = \frac{M_D}{M_P} Q$ and $K_D = \frac{m_\alpha}{M_P} Q$, the ratio is $\frac{K_D}{K_\alpha} = \frac{m_\alpha}{M_D}$.

9. What physical quantity does the area under the curve $\sqrt{V(r) - E}$ between $R$ and $r_0$ determine in $\alpha$ decay?

Explanation: The integral $\int_{R}^{r_0} \sqrt{V(r) - E} \, dr$ forms the core of the exponent in the Gamow factor $G$, which governs barrier transparency.

10. Why is $\alpha$ decay rarely observed in light nuclei ($A < 150$)?

Explanation: In light nuclei, the mass of the parent is generally less than or equal to the sum of the daughter and $\alpha$ masses ($Q \le 0$), making spontaneous $\alpha$ decay energetically forbidden.
