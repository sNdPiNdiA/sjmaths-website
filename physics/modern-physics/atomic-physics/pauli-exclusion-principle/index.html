Pauli's Exclusion Principle

Pauli's Exclusion Principle states that no two identical fermions can occupy the same quantum state simultaneously within a quantum system. It governs the electronic structure of atoms, determines the periodicity of the periodic table, and accounts for the stability of bulk matter.

Statement of Pauli's Exclusion Principle

  • No two electrons in an atom can have the exact same set of all four quantum numbers (n, l, ml, ms).
  • Formulated by Wolfgang Pauli in 1925 to explain the anomalous Zeeman effect and the shell structure of atoms.
  • Applies strictly to fermions (particles with half-integer spin, such as electrons, protons, and neutrons), whereas bosons (integer spin) do not obey it.

Quantum Numbers and Maximum Capacity

  • Principal quantum number (n) determines the main energy shell, with maximum orbital capacity given by 2n2.
  • Azimuthal (l), magnetic (ml), and spin (ms) quantum numbers define individual orbital states.
  • Each orbital, defined by a specific (n, l, ml), can accommodate a maximum of two electrons due to the two allowed spin orientations (ms = +1/2 and -1/2).

Antisymmetric Wavefunctions

  • In quantum mechanics, the total wavefunction for a system of identical fermions must be antisymmetric under the interchange of the spatial and spin coordinates of any two electrons.
  • If two electrons occupied the exact same quantum state, exchanging them would leave the wavefunction unchanged (symmetric), violating the antisymmetry requirement.
  • Mathematical expression requires the use of Slater determinants where the interchange of any two rows changes the sign of the determinant, yielding zero if two rows are identical.

Physical Consequences and Stability

  • Prevents all electrons in a multi-electron atom from collapsing into the lowest energy 1s state, thereby explaining atomic volume and chemical periodicity.
  • Responsible for degeneracy pressure in white dwarf stars and neutron stars, preventing gravitational collapse.
  • Underpins the Fermi-Dirac statistics governing electrons in metals.

Derivations

Maximum Electron Capacity of a Principal Shell

Conditions: For a given principal quantum number n

  • For a given principal quantum number n, the azimuthal quantum number l ranges from 0 to n - 1.
  • For each value of l, the magnetic quantum number ml ranges from -l to +l, giving a total of (2l + 1) distinct spatial orbitals.
  • Since each spatial orbital can hold 2 electrons corresponding to ms = +1/2 and ms = -1/2, the total capacity N for a shell n is the sum N = 2 * sum_{l=0}^{n-1} (2l + 1).
  • Evaluating the arithmetic series: sum_{l=0}^{n-1} (2l + 1) = n2.

Result: N = 2n2

Slater Determinant Formulation for Two Electrons

Conditions: System of two identical fermions with spatial and spin coordinates (1) and (2)

  • Let phi_a(1) and phi_b(2) represent individual single-particle quantum states.
  • A simple product wavefunction phi_a(1)phi_b(2) does not satisfy exchange symmetry.
  • Construct an antisymmetric linear combination: Psi(1,2) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} [phi_a(1)phi_b(2) - phi_a(2)phi_b(1)].
  • If state a equals state b, then Psi(1,2) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} [phi_a(1)phi_a(2) - phi_a(2)phi_a(1)] = 0.

Result: Psi(1,2) = 0, proving that two fermions cannot occupy the identical quantum state.

Stern-Gerlach Experiment

Draw: A beam of silver atoms passing through an inhomogeneous magnetic field splitting into two distinct traces on a detector plate.

Interpret: Proves space quantization of angular momentum and confirms the existence of two possible electron spin states (m<sub>s</sub> = +1/2 and -1/2).

Exam use: Directly tested to establish the physical origin of the spin quantum number required by Pauli's exclusion principle.

Solved numericals

TGT core

What is the maximum number of electrons that can be accommodated in the principal shell with n = 3?

  • Identify the formula for maximum capacity of a principal shell: N = 2n2.
  • Substitute n = 3 into the formula.
  • N = 2 * (3)2 = 2 * 9 = 18.

Answer: 18 electrons

TGT core

Determine if an electron state with quantum numbers n = 2, l = 1, m<sub>l</sub> = 0, m<sub>s</sub> = +1/2 is permissible, and state how many electrons can share these exact n, l, and m<sub>l</sub> values.

  • Check constraints: For n = 2, l can be 0 or 1 (valid). For l = 1, ml can range from -1 to +1, so ml = 0 is valid. ms = +1/2 is valid.
  • The state is completely permissible.
  • For a given set of (n, l, ml), only two electrons can exist, corresponding to ms = +1/2 and ms = -1/2.

Answer: Permissible state; exactly 2 electrons can share the same n, l, and ml.

PGT extension

Find the number of possible sets of quantum numbers (n, l, m<sub>l</sub>, m<sub>s</sub>) for an electron in the n = 4 shell.

  • For n = 4, the total number of orbitals is given by n2 = 42 = 16.
  • Each orbital accommodates 2 electrons with opposite spins.
  • Total number of electron states = 2 * n2 = 2 * 42 = 2 * 16 = 32.

Answer: 32 possible sets

Assumptions and limitations

  • Applies exclusively to identical fermions possessing half-integer spin; does not apply to bosons such as photons or alpha particles which obey Bose-Einstein statistics.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Pauli's exclusion principle applies to all particles in the universe indiscriminately.

Correct: It applies only to identical fermions (like electrons, protons, neutrons). Bosons can occupy the same quantum state.

Bosons have symmetric wavefunctions and tend to bunch together (e.g., lasers, Bose-Einstein condensates), whereas fermions have antisymmetric wavefunctions.

Common error: Two electrons in the same orbital have identical quantum numbers.

Correct: They differ by their spin quantum number m<sub>s</sub>.

If n, l, and m<sub>l</sub> are identical, m<sub>s</sub> must be +1/2 for one and -1/2 for the other to satisfy the exclusion principle.

Quick revision

  • No two electrons in an atom can have the exact same set of all four quantum numbers (n, l, ml, ms).
  • Maximum capacity of principal shell: N = 2n^{2}
  • Subshell orbital degeneracy: g_{l} = 2(2l + 1)

Must remember

  • No two identical fermions (such as electrons) in an atom can have the exact same set of all four quantum numbers $(n, l, m_l, m_s)$.
  • The maximum capacity of any principal shell $n$ is given by $N = 2n^2$, and subshell orbital degeneracy is $g_l = 2(2l + 1)$.
  • Total wavefunction for a system of identical fermions must be antisymmetric under the interchange of spatial and spin coordinates, expressed mathematically via Slater determinants.
  • Pauli's exclusion principle applies strictly to fermions (half-integer spin) and does not apply to bosons (integer spin).
  • It prevents the collapse of multi-electron atoms into the lowest energy state, determines chemical periodicity, and explains degeneracy pressure in white dwarfs.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following sets of quantum numbers is not permissible for an electron in an atom?

Explanation: For a given principal quantum number $n$, the azimuthal quantum number $l$ can take integer values from $0$ to $n-1$. When $n = 3$, $l$ can only be $0, 1,$ or $2$. Therefore, $l = 3$ is invalid.

2. What is the maximum number of electrons that can be accommodated in a principal shell with $n = 4$?

Explanation: The maximum capacity of a principal shell is given by the formula $N = 2n^2$. For $n = 4$, $N = 2(4)^2 = 2(16) = 32$.

3. The maximum number of electrons in a subshell with azimuthal quantum number $l = 2$ is:

Explanation: The subshell degeneracy is given by $g_l = 2(2l + 1)$. For $l = 2$ ($d$-subshell), $g_2 = 2(2(2) + 1) = 2(5) = 10$.

4. Pauli's exclusion principle is strictly applicable to which type of particles?

Explanation: Pauli's exclusion principle applies strictly to fermions (particles with half-integer spin, such as electrons, protons, and neutrons). Bosons do not obey it.

5. Who formulated the exclusion principle and in which year?

Explanation: Wolfgang Pauli formulated the exclusion principle in 1925 to explain the anomalous Zeeman effect and atomic shell structure.

6. If two electrons occupy the exact same spatial orbital, how must their quantum states differ?

Explanation: An orbital defined by $(n, l, m_l)$ can hold a maximum of two electrons, which must have opposite spin orientations ($m_s = +1/2$ and $m_s = -1/2$) to satisfy Pauli's principle.

7. In quantum mechanics, the total wavefunction for a system of identical fermions must be:

Explanation: The total wavefunction of a system of identical fermions must be antisymmetric under the interchange of the spatial and spin coordinates of any two particles.

8. What mathematical construct is used to guarantee the antisymmetry of a multi-electron fermion wavefunction?

Explanation: Slater determinants are used to construct antisymmetric wavefunctions for multi-electron systems, where swapping rows changes the sign of the determinant.

9. What physical phenomenon in dense stellar bodies is directly supported by Pauli's exclusion principle?

Explanation: Electron degeneracy pressure, arising from Pauli's exclusion principle, supports white dwarf stars against gravitational collapse.

10. The range of the magnetic quantum number $m_l$ for a given value of $l$ is:

Explanation: The magnetic quantum number $m_l$ can take integer values ranging from $-l$ to $+l$, including zero.

11. Which statistical distribution describes the behavior of electrons governed by Pauli's exclusion principle at thermal equilibrium?

Explanation: Fermi-Dirac statistics governs particles that obey Pauli's exclusion principle, such as electrons in metals.

12. Why would two electrons occupying the exact same quantum state violate quantum mechanical principles?

Explanation: If two identical fermions occupied the same state, exchanging them would leave the wavefunction symmetric, which violates the fundamental antisymmetry requirement for fermions.

13. What is the spin quantum number value $m_s$ for an electron?

Explanation: An electron is a spin-$1/2$ particle, and its spin projection along a quantization axis can be either $+1/2$ or $-1/2$.

14. Which quantum number determines the main energy shell of an electron?

Explanation: The principal quantum number $n$ defines the primary energy level or shell of the electron.

15. What is a major macroscopic consequence of Pauli's exclusion principle in chemistry?

Explanation: By preventing all electrons from occupying the lowest energy level ($1s$), Pauli's principle forces electrons into higher shells, dictating atomic shell structure and chemical periodicity.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The number of possible values of the magnetic quantum number $m_l$ for an azimuthal quantum number $l$ is given by:

Explanation: Since $m_l$ ranges from $-l$ to $+l$ in integer steps, the total number of allowed $m_l$ values for a given $l$ is $2l + 1$.

2. Which of the following wavefunctions for a two-electron system is antisymmetric under particle exchange?

Explanation: An antisymmetric wavefunction changes sign when the coordinates of particles 1 and 2 are swapped. The difference $\psi_1(1)\psi_2(2) - \psi_2(1)\psi_1(2)$ satisfies this property.

3. What is the maximum capacity of electrons in the $f$-subshell ($l = 3$)?

Explanation: Using the subshell capacity formula $g_l = 2(2l + 1)$, for $l = 3$, $g_3 = 2(2(3) + 1) = 2(7) = 14$ electrons.

4. According to Pauli's Exclusion Principle, what is the maximum number of electrons that can share the same principal ($n=2$) and azimuthal ($l=1$) quantum numbers?

Explanation: For $n = 2, l = 1$ ($2p$ subshell), the magnetic quantum number $m_l$ can take $3$ values ($-1, 0, +1$). Each orbital can hold $2$ electrons with opposite spins, giving a total of $3 \times 2 = 6$ electrons.

5. The Fermi-Dirac distribution function $f(E)$ at absolute zero ($T = 0$) for energy states below the Fermi energy ($E < E_F$) evaluates to:

Explanation: At $T = 0$, all energy levels below the Fermi energy $E_F$ are completely filled, making the occupation probability $f(E) = 1$.

6. Why do bosons not obey Pauli's Exclusion Principle?

Explanation: Bosons possess symmetric wavefunctions under particle exchange, allowing multiple identical bosons to occupy the same quantum state simultaneously.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. If the principal quantum number is $n = 5$, what is the maximum possible value of the azimuthal quantum number $l$?

Explanation: The azimuthal quantum number ranges from $0$ to $n-1$. For $n = 5$, the maximum value of $l$ is $5 - 1 = 4$.

2. Which of the following sets of quantum numbers describes a valid state for an electron in a $3d$ orbital?

Explanation: For $3d$, $n = 3$ and $l = 2$. The magnetic quantum number $m_l$ must be between $-2$ and $+2$. Thus, $m_l = -2$ with $m_s = -1/2$ is valid.

3. In the Slater determinant expression for an $N$-electron system, what does the prefactor $\frac{1}{\sqrt{N!}}$ represent?

Explanation: The prefactor $\frac{1}{\sqrt{N!}}$ normalizes the antisymmetric multi-electron wavefunction.

4. What happens to the determinant in a Slater formulation if two identical electrons are assigned the exact same set of quantum numbers?

Explanation: Properties of determinants dictate that if any two rows (representing identical quantum states for two fermions) are identical, the determinant evaluates to zero.

5. The total number of orbitals in a principal shell with quantum number $n$ is:

Explanation: Each principal shell $n$ contains subshells with $l = 0, 1, \dots, n-1$. The sum of orbitals across these subshells is $\sum_{l=0}^{n-1} (2l+1) = n^2$.

6. Particles with integer spin values ($s = 0, 1, 2, \dots$) are classified as:

Explanation: Particles with integer spin are bosons and follow Bose-Einstein statistics, not Pauli's exclusion principle.

7. In the Fermi-Dirac distribution function, when the particle energy $E$ equals the Fermi energy $E_F$, what is the value of $f(E)$ at $T > 0$?

Explanation: When $E = E_F$, the exponent in the denominator is $e^0 = 1$, making $f(E_F) = \frac{1}{1 + 1} = \frac{1}{2}$ regardless of temperature.

8. Which physical property of matter is fundamentally explained by the prohibition of electrons occupying the same state?

Explanation: Pauli's exclusion principle provides the repulsive quantum pressure that prevents matter from collapsing, establishing atomic volume and macroscopic incompressibility.

9. How many electrons can have the quantum numbers $n = 2, l = 0$ in an atom?

Explanation: For $n = 2, l = 0$ ($2s$ subshell), there is only $1$ orbital ($m_l = 0$), which can accommodate a maximum of $2$ electrons with opposite spins.

10. Which of the following scientists received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1945 for the discovery of the Exclusion Principle?

Explanation: Wolfgang Pauli was awarded the 1945 Nobel Prize in Physics for his discovery of the exclusion principle, also known as the Pauli principle.
