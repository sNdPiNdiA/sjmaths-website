L-S Coupling (Russell-Saunders Coupling)

L-S coupling is a quantum mechanical scheme in multi-electron atoms where individual orbital angular momenta (l<sub>i</sub>) couple together to form a resultant total orbital angular momentum (L), and individual spin angular momenta (s<sub>i</sub>) couple to form a resultant total spin angular momentum (S). These resultant vectors then interact via spin-orbit coupling to yield the total angular momentum (J).

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Basic understanding of quantum numbers n, l, m<sub>l</sub>, m<sub>s</sub>, vector atom model representation, and total angular momentum quantum number J in multi-electron systems.

PGT extension

Detailed derivation of vector addition for L-S coupling, determination of term symbols (<sup>2S+1</sup>L<sub>J</sub>), Hund's rules for ground state determination, and application to multi-electron atomic spectra.

Prerequisites

  • Bohr's theory of hydrogen atom and spatial quantization
  • Electron spin and quantum numbers
  • Pauli's exclusion principle

Physical Origin of L-S Coupling

  • Valid primarily for light and medium-weight atoms where electrostatic Coulomb repulsion between electrons is much stronger than the magnetic spin-orbit interaction.
  • Individual orbital angular momenta (li) of all electrons vector-add to form a total orbital angular momentum vector L.
  • Individual spin angular momenta (si) of all electrons vector-add to form a total spin angular momentum vector S.

Formation of Total Angular Momentum J

  • The resultant orbital vector L and resultant spin vector S interact via spin-orbit interaction to form the total angular momentum vector J.
  • Mathematically expressed as $J = L + S$.
  • The magnitude of total angular momentum is given by |J| = \sqrt{J(J+1)}\hbar, where J can take values ranging from |L - S| to L + S in integer steps.

Russell-Saunders Term Symbols

  • State of an atom in L-S coupling is represented by the spectroscopic term symbol 2S+1LJ.
  • 2S+1 represents the spin multiplicity of the state.
  • L is denoted by letters S, P, D, F, G... corresponding to total orbital quantum numbers L = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4...

Hund's Rules for Ground State Determination

  • Rule 1: For a given electron configuration, the term with the maximum multiplicity (2S+1) lies lowest in energy.
  • Rule 2: For a given multiplicity, the term with the largest value of total orbital angular momentum L lies lowest in energy.
  • Rule 3: For shells less than half-filled, the level with the minimum J value ($J = |L - S|) has the lowest energy;$ for shells more than half-filled, the level with the maximum J value ($J = L + S) has the lowest energy$.

Derivations

Magnitude of Total Angular Momentum in L-S Coupling

Conditions: Assuming vectors L and S precess rapidly about their resultant J due to spin-orbit interaction.

  • Write the vector sum relation for total angular momentum: $J = L + S$.
  • Take the dot product of J with itself: $J^2 = (L + S)\cdot(L + S) = L^2 + S^2 + 2(L\cdot S)$.
  • Substitute quantum mechanical operator eigenvalues: J(J+1)\hba$r^2 = L(L+1)\hbar^2 + S(S+1)\hbar^2 + 2(L\cdot S)$.

Result: J(J+1) = L(L+1) + S(S+1) + 2(L\cdot S)

Spin-Orbit Interaction Energy Shift in L-S Coupling

Conditions: First-order perturbation theory applied to spin-orbit interaction energy Eso.

  • The spin-orbit Hamiltonian is given by Hso = \zeta_{LS}(L\cdot S), where \zeta_{LS} is the spin-orbit coupling constant.
  • Evaluate the dot product L\cdot S using L^2, S^2, and J^2 operator relations: L\cdot $S = \frac{1}{2}[J(J+1) - L(L+1) - S(S+1)]\hbar^2$.
  • Substitute into the energy shift equation: \Delta $E_{so} = \frac{1}{2}\zeta_{LS}[J(J+1) - L(L+1) - S(S+1)]\hbar^2$.

Result: \Delta $E_{so} = \frac{1}{2}\zeta_{LS}[J(J+1) - L(L+1) - S(S+1)]$

Graphs and experiments

Vector Model of L-S Coupling

Draw: Vectors L and S precessing rapidly around their resultant vector J, while J itself precesses slowly around an external axis (or in space).

Interpret: Illustrates conservation of angular momentum components and the hierarchical coupling strength where L and S couple first before interacting with J.

Exam use: Frequently asked in conceptual multiple-choice questions regarding vector orientations and precession cones.

Stern-Gerlach Experiment (Spatial Quantization)

Draw: A beam of silver atoms passing through an inhomogeneous magnetic field splitting into two distinct traces on a detector plate.

Interpret: Proves space quantization of angular momentum and existence of electron spin ($s = 1/2$, giving 2s+1 = 2 components).

Exam use: Directly tested as foundational background for understanding vector atom models and angular momentum coupling.

Multiplet Splitting Energy Level Diagram

Draw: Energy levels of a term symbol splitting into multiple J-levels under spin-orbit interaction (e.g., 2P splitting into 2P1/2 and 2P3/2).

Interpret: Demonstrates the Landé interval rule where the separation between adjacent J levels is proportional to the larger J value.

Exam use: Used in numerical and theoretical questions regarding fine structure splitting in atomic spectra.

Solved numericals

TGT core

Find the possible values of total angular momentum quantum number J for an atomic state with L = 2 and S = 1.

  • Identify given values: L = 2, S = 1.
  • Use the rule for possible J values: J ranges from |L - S| to L + S in integer steps.
  • Calculate lower limit: |2 - 1| = 1.
  • Calculate upper limit: 2 + 1 = 3.
  • List all intermediate integer values.

Answer: J = 1, 2, 3

PGT extension

Determine the ground state term symbol for an atom with configuration np2 using Hund's rules.

  • For np2, two p-electrons have n = np, l = 1, s1 = 1/2, s2 = 1/2.
  • Possible ml values are 1, 0, -1. Applying Pauli's exclusion principle, maximum total spin $S = \sum s_i = 1$, giving multiplicity 2S+1 = 3 (Triplet).
  • Maximum total orbital angular momentum L compatible with Pauli exclusion is L = 1 (P state).
  • For a shell less than half-filled (np2 has 2 electrons out of 6), the lowest J value is $J = |L - S| = |1 - 1| = 0$.
  • Combine into the term symbol 2S+1LJ.

Answer: 3P0

PGT extension

Calculate the Landé g-factor for the state 2P3/2.

  • Identify quantum numbers from 2P3/2: Multiplicity 2S+$1 = 2 \implies S = 1/2$. Letter P \implies L = 1. Subscript $J = 3/2$.
  • Write the Landé g-factor formula: $g = 1 + \frac{J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)}$.
  • Substitute $J = 3/2$, $S = 1/2$, $L = 1 into the expression: J(J+1) = (3/2)(5/2) = 15/4 = 3.75; S(S+1) = (1/2)(3/2) = 3/4 = 0.75; L(L+1) = (1)(2) = 2$.
  • Compute numerator: 3.75 + 0.75 - 2 = 2.5.
  • Compute denominator: 2 \times 3.75 = 7.5.
  • Calculate $g = 1 + (2.5 / 7.5) = 1 + (1/3) = 4/3$.

Answer: $g = 4/3 (or 1.33)$

Assumptions and limitations

  • L-S coupling assumes electrostatic repulsion between electrons is much stronger than magnetic spin-orbit interaction, which fails for very heavy elements where j-j coupling dominates.

Common misconceptions

Common error: L-S coupling means individual electron spins couple directly to individual orbital angular momenta of other electrons.

Correct: Individual orbital momenta couple to form L, and individual spins couple to form S; L and S then couple together.

Russell-Saunders coupling assumes intra-atomic electrostatic forces group all li together and all si together before spin-orbit interaction takes place.

Common error: The term symbol 2S+1LJ has J as the principal quantum number.

Correct: J represents the total angular momentum quantum number of the atom.

J is the vector sum of L and S, completely distinct from the principal quantum number n.

Quick revision

  • In L-S coupling, $L = \sum l_i$, $S = \sum s_i$, and $J = L + S with magnitude J ranging from |L - S|$ to L + S.
  • Total Orbital Angular Momentum Magnitude: |L| = \sqrt{L(L+1)}\hbar
  • Total Spin Angular Momentum Magnitude: |S| = \sqrt{S(S+1)}\hbar

Must remember

  • In L-S (Russell-Saunders) coupling, individual orbital angular momenta vector-add to form $L = \sum l_i$ and individual spins vector-add to form $S = \sum s_i$.
  • The total angular momentum $J$ is given by vector addition: $J = L + S$, with magnitude $|J| = \sqrt{J(J+1)}\hbar$.
  • The allowed range for total angular momentum quantum number $J$ is $|L - S|, |L - S| + 1, \dots, L + S$.
  • The spectroscopic term symbol is represented as $^{2S+1}L_J$, where $2S+1$ is the spin multiplicity.
  • Hund's Rule 1: The term with maximum multiplicity ($2S+1$) lies lowest in energy.
  • Hund's Rule 2: For a given multiplicity, the term with the largest value of $L$ lies lowest in energy.
  • Hund's Rule 3: For shells less than half-filled, the level with $J = |L - S|$ has the lowest energy; for shells more than half-filled, $J = L + S$ has the lowest energy.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the physical assumption underlying L-S coupling in multi-electron atoms?

Explanation: L-S coupling is valid primarily for light and medium-weight atoms where electrostatic Coulomb repulsion between electrons is much stronger than the magnetic spin-orbit interaction.

2. How are individual orbital angular momenta combined to form the total orbital angular momentum $L$ in L-S coupling?

Explanation: Individual orbital angular momenta ($l_i$) vector-add to form a total orbital angular momentum vector $L$.

3. What is the magnitude of the total angular momentum vector $J$?

Explanation: The magnitude of total angular momentum is given by $|J| = \sqrt{J(J+1)}\hbar$, where $J$ is the total angular momentum quantum number.

4. For given values of $L = 2$ and $S = 1$, what are the possible values of the total angular momentum quantum number $J$?

Explanation: The range of $J$ is from $|L - S|$ to $L + S$ in integer steps. Here, $|2 - 1| = 1$ up to $2 + 1 = 3$, giving $J = 1, 2, 3$.

5. In the spectroscopic term symbol $^{2S+1}L_J$, what does the superscript $2S+1$ designate?

Explanation: The term $^{2S+1}L_J$ uses $2S+1$ to represent the spin multiplicity of the atomic state.

6. Which letter corresponds to total orbital quantum number $L = 2$ in term symbols?

Explanation: Letters $S, P, D, F, G\dots$ correspond to total orbital quantum numbers $L = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4\dots$ respectively. Thus, $L = 2$ corresponds to $D$.

7. According to Hund's first rule, which term lies lowest in energy for a given electron configuration?

Explanation: Hund's Rule 1 states that for a given electron configuration, the term with the maximum multiplicity $(2S+1)$ lies lowest in energy due to exchange energy stabilization.

8. According to Hund's third rule, for an atomic subshell that is more than half-filled, which $J$ level has the lowest energy?

Explanation: For shells less than half-filled, the level with the minimum $J$ value ($J = |L - S|$) has the lowest energy; for shells more than half-filled, the level with maximum $J$ value ($J = L + S$) has the lowest energy.

9. What is the formula for the Landé $g$-factor in L-S coupling?

Explanation: The Landé splitting factor is given by $g = 1 + \frac{J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)}$.

10. What is the spin multiplicity for a total spin quantum number $S = \frac{3}{2}$?

Explanation: Spin multiplicity is given by $2S + 1$. For $S = \frac{3}{2}$, multiplicity $= 2(\frac{3}{2}) + 1 = 4$ (quartet state).

11. Which of the following is a common misconception regarding L-S coupling?

Explanation: It is incorrect to think individual spins couple directly to individual orbital momenta of other electrons; rather, all $l_i$ couple to form $L$ and all $s_i$ couple to form $S$ first.

12. For a single valence electron with $l = 1$ and $s = \frac{1}{2}$, what are the possible term symbols?

Explanation: For one electron, $L = l = 1$ (P state) and $S = s = 1/2$, giving multiplicity $2S+1 = 2$. $J$ can be $|1 - 1/2| = 1/2$ and $1 + 1/2 = 3/2$. Thus, $^2P_{1/2}$ and $^2P_{3/2}$.

13. What physical interaction causes the splitting of a term into multiple $J$ levels (fine structure)?

Explanation: The resultant orbital vector $L$ and resultant spin vector $S$ interact via spin-orbit interaction to form total angular momentum $J$, causing fine structure splitting.

14. In Hund's second rule, between two terms of the same multiplicity, which one has lower energy?

Explanation: Hund's Rule 2 states that for a given multiplicity, the term with the largest value of total orbital angular momentum $L$ lies lowest in energy.

15. What is the range of values for the spin quantum number $S$ for two interacting electrons with individual spins $s_1 = 1/2$ and $s_2 = 1/2$?

Explanation: Two electron spins vector-add to give total spin $S = |s_1 - s_2|$ to $s_1 + s_2$, which is $0$ (singlet) and $1$ (triplet).

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. What is the ground state term symbol for an atom with a $p^2$ electronic configuration following Hund's rules?

Explanation: For $p^2$, possible terms are $^3P$, $^1D$, $^1S$. By Hund's Rule 1, max multiplicity is $3$ ($^3P$). For $p^2$ (less than half-filled), Rule 3 gives the lowest $J$ value $J = |L - S| = |1 - 1| = 0$. Thus, $^3P_0$.

2. For a state with $L = 2$ and $S = 3/2$, what is the maximum possible value of the total angular momentum quantum number $J$?

Explanation: The maximum value of $J$ is given by $L + S = 2 + \frac{3}{2} = \frac{7}{2}$.

3. Which of the following electronic configurations yields a spectroscopic term symbol of $^2S_{1/2}$?

Explanation: For a single $s$ electron ($l = 0$), $L = 0$ (which corresponds to symbol $S$), $S = 1/2$ (multiplicity $2S+1 = 2$), and $J = 0 + 1/2 = 1/2$. Thus, $^2S_{1/2}$.

4. Calculate the Landé $g$-factor for a state with $L = 0$, $S = 1/2$, and $J = 1/2$.

Explanation: Using $g = 1 + \frac{J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)}$, substituting $L=0, S=1/2, J=1/2$: $g = 1 + \frac{(3/4) + (3/4) - 0}{2(3/4)} = 1 + \frac{3/2}{3/2} = 2$.

5. According to Hund's rules, for a shell that is more than half-filled (such as $p^4$), which fine-structure level of the ground term lies lowest in energy?

Explanation: For shells more than half-filled, the spin-orbit interaction causes the level with the maximum $J$ value ($J = L + S$) to lie lowest in energy.

6. What is the spin multiplicity of an atomic state with three unpaired electrons?

Explanation: Three unpaired electrons give total spin $S = 3 \times (1/2) = 3/2$. The spin multiplicity is $2S + 1 = 2(3/2) + 1 = 4$ (quartet).

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. In which regime of atomic weight is L-S (Russell-Saunders) coupling most applicable?

Explanation: L-S coupling is valid primarily for light and medium-weight atoms where electrostatic Coulomb repulsion dominates over spin-orbit interactions.

2. What is the total orbital angular momentum quantum number $L$ for a configuration of two equivalent $p$ electrons ($p^2$) that yields maximum $L$?

Explanation: For two $p$ electrons ($l_1 = 1, l_2 = 1$), the possible values of $L$ are $2, 1, 0$. The maximum value of $L$ is $2$ (corresponding to a $D$ term).

3. What is the spin-orbit interaction energy shift $E_{so}$ proportional to?

Explanation: The spin-orbit interaction energy shift is given by $E_{so} = A_{LS} \frac{J(J+1) - L(L+1) - S(S+1)}{2}$.

4. For a term symbol $^4D_{7/2}$, what are the values of $S$, $L$, and $J$?

Explanation: Multiplicity $2S+1 = 4 \Rightarrow S = 3/2$. Letter $D \Rightarrow L = 2$. Subscript gives $J = 7/2$. (Note: $L+S = 2 + 3/2 = 7/2$, which matches).

5. Which of the following coupling schemes is preferred for very heavy atoms where spin-orbit interaction exceeds electrostatic repulsion?

Explanation: For heavy atoms, individual spin-orbit interactions ($l_i$ with $s_i$ for each electron) become stronger than inter-electronic electrostatic repulsion, leading to $j-j$ coupling.

6. What is the number of microstates for a $p^2$ electronic configuration?

Explanation: For $p^2$, number of ways to put 2 electrons in 6 states is $\binom{6}{2} = \frac{6 \times 5}{2} = 15$ microstates.

7. What is the ground state term for a single $d$ electron ($d^1$)?

Explanation: For $d^1$, $l = 2 (D), s = 1/2 (multiplicity = 2)$. Shell is less than half-filled ($1 < 5$), so $J = |L - S| = |2 - 1/2| = 3/2$. Thus, $^2D_{3/2}$.

8. Which quantum number remains a good quantum number in both L-S and j-j coupling schemes?

Explanation: The total angular momentum quantum number $J$ remains a good quantum number in both coupling schemes because total angular momentum is always conserved in a rotationally invariant isolated atom.

9. If $L = 1$ and $S = 1$, what are the possible term symbols for this configuration?

Explanation: Multiplicity is $2(1)+1 = 3$ (triplet). $L = 1$ corresponds to $P$. $J$ ranges from $|1-1|=0$ to $1+1=2$, giving $J = 0, 1, 2$. Thus, $^3P_0, ^3P_1, ^3P_2$.

10. In the vector model of Russell-Saunders coupling, what precesses around what?

Explanation: In L-S coupling, the vectors $L$ and $S$ vector-add to form $J$ and precess rapidly about the direction of the total angular momentum vector $J$ due to the internal spin-orbit torque.
