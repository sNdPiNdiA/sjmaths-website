Electron Spin and Magnetic Moment

Electron spin is an intrinsic form of angular momentum possessed by electrons, independent of their orbital motion. It is characterized by a spin quantum number s = 1/2 and accounts for fine structure and magnetic properties in atomic systems.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of spin quantum numbers, Pauli's Exclusion Principle, fundamental postulates, and qualitative understanding of the Stern-Gerlach experiment.

PGT extension

Quantitative treatment of spin-orbit coupling, gyromagnetic ratio for spin, spin magnetic moment, vector model of the atom, and total angular momentum.

Prerequisites

  • Bohr's Theory of Hydrogen Atom
  • Spatial quantization and space quantization rules
  • Orbital angular momentum and magnetic quantum numbers

Discovery and Postulation of Electron Spin

  • Proposed by Samuel Goudsmit and George Uhlenbeck in 1925 to explain anomalies in alkali metal atomic spectra (doublets).
  • Assigned an intrinsic angular momentum vector S that cannot be reduced to classical rotation of a finite charge distribution.
  • The magnitude of spin angular momentum is given by S = √(s(s+1)) ℏ where s = 1/2.

Spin Quantum Numbers and Spatial Quantization

  • The spin quantum number s is fixed at 1/2 for an electron.
  • The z-component of spin angular momentum is quantized as Sz = ms ℏ, where ms = ±1/2.
  • This leads to two possible spin orientations in a magnetic field: 'spin-up' (ms = +1/2) and 'spin-down' (ms = -1/2).

Spin Magnetic Moment and Gyromagnetic Ratio

  • Associated with spin angular momentum is an intrinsic magnetic dipole moment μs.
  • The ratio of spin magnetic moment to spin angular momentum is twice that of orbital motion, expressed by the Landé g-factor for spin gs ≈ 2.
  • Expressed mathematically as μs = - gs (μB / ℏ) S, where μB is the Bohr magneton.

Pauli's Exclusion Principle and Spin Statistics

  • States that no two identical fermions (such as electrons) can occupy the same quantum state simultaneously.
  • Requires the total wavefunction of a multi-electron system to be antisymmetric under the exchange of any two electrons.
  • Directly accounts for the periodic table structure and electron shell capacities (2n2).

Derivations

Magnitude and Component of Spin Angular Momentum

Conditions: Quantum mechanical treatment of spin-1/2 particles

  • Write the general operator equation for angular momentum squared: S2|s, ms> = s(s+1)ℏ2|s, ms>.
  • Substitute the value of spin quantum number s = 1/2 into the eigenvalue equation.
  • Calculate the magnitude: S = √((1/2)((1/2)+1)) ℏ = √(3/4) ℏ = (√3/2)ℏ.
  • Apply the z-component operator: Sz|s, ms> = msℏ|s, ms> with ms = ±1/2.

Result: S = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\hbar \quad \text{and} \quad S_z = \pm\frac{1}{2}\hbar

Spin Magnetic Dipole Moment of an Electron

Conditions: Relativistic quantum mechanics (Dirac equation implication) or semi-classical relation with g<sub>s</sub> = 2

  • Start with the definition of magnetic dipole moment in terms of angular momentum: \vec{\mu}_s = -\frac{e}{2m} g_s \vec{S}.
  • Substitute the electron mass m = me, charge magnitude e, and spin g-factor gs = 2.
  • Simplify the coefficient: \vec{\mu}_s = -\frac{e}{m_e} \vec{S}.
  • Express in terms of Bohr magneton μB = eℏ/2me by utilizing S = (ℏ/2): \vec{\mu}_s = -2 \left(\frac{\mu_B}{\hbar}\right) \vec{S}.

Result: \vec{\mu}_s = -\frac{e}{m_e}\vec{S}

Graphs and experiments

Stern-Gerlach Experiment Setup

Draw: A beam of neutral silver atoms passing through an inhomogeneous magnetic field created by pole pieces of specific shape (one flat, one sharp edge), resulting in the splitting of the beam into two distinct traces on a collector plate.

Interpret: Proves the space quantization of electron spin. Instead of a continuous smear, the atomic beam splits into exactly two paths corresponding to m<sub>s</sub> = +1/2 and m<sub>s</sub> = -1/2.

Exam use: Frequently asked in conceptual and descriptive questions regarding the physical proof of electron spin and space quantization.

Vector Model of Spin Quantization

Draw: A cone of possible orientations of the spin vector <b>S</b> around the z-axis, showing vector magnitude &radic;(3/4)&hbar; and discrete projections along the z-axis at +(1/2)&hbar; and -(1/2)&hbar;.

Interpret: Demonstrates that while the magnitude and z-component are precisely known, the x and y components remain uncertain due to Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.

Exam use: Tested in PGT multiple-choice questions regarding allowed projections and angle calculations.

Potential Energy vs Position in Stern-Gerlach Inhomogeneous Field

Interpret: Illustrates the spatial separation of magnetic dipoles based on their orientation relative to the external field gradient.

Exam use: Used in numerical problems calculating deflection distance and force.

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the magnitude of the spin angular momentum of an electron.

  • Identify the spin quantum number for an electron: s = 1/2.
  • Write the formula for spin angular momentum magnitude: S = \sqrt{s(s+1)}\hbar.
  • Substitute s = 1/2 into the formula: S = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2} + 1\right)}\hbar = \sqrt{\frac{3}{4}}\hbar.
  • Compute the numerical value: S = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \times 1.054 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}.

Answer: 9.13 x 10^-35 J s

PGT extension

Find the maximum z-component of the spin magnetic moment of an electron in terms of the Bohr magneton.

  • Recall the formula for the z-component of spin magnetic moment: \mu_{sz} = -g_s \mu_B m_s.
  • Substitute the standard values: g_s = 2 and ms = \pm 1/2.
  • For the maximum magnitude, take the absolute value with m_s = 1/2: |\mu_{sz, max}| = 2 \times \mu_B \times \frac{1}{2}.
  • Simplify the expression to find the result in terms of Bohr magneton.

Answer: 1 \mu_B (or \mu_B)

PGT extension

A beam of silver atoms is subjected to a magnetic field gradient \frac{\partial B_z}{\partial z} = 1.4 \times 10^2 \text{ T m}^{-1} over a pole piece length of 5.0 cm. If the velocity of atoms is 1.0 \times 10^3 \text{ m s}^{-1} and mass is 1.08 \times 10^{-25} \text{ kg}, calculate the spatial separation between the two beams on the screen placed 10 cm beyond the magnet.

  • Determine the force on the silver atom: F_z = \pm \mu_B \frac{\partial B_z}{\partial z}.
  • Calculate the acceleration in the magnetic field region of length L_1: a_z = \frac{F_z}{m} = \frac{\mu_B (\partial B_z / \partial z)}{m}.
  • Find the time spent in the magnetic field: t_1 = \frac{L_1}{v} = \frac{0.05}{10^3} = 5.0 \times 10^{-5} \text{ s}.
  • Calculate the transverse velocity as it exits the field: v_z = a_z t_1.
  • Calculate the additional deflection in the drift space of length L_2 (10 cm): y_2 = v_z \frac{L_2}{v}, and combine with deflection inside the magnet to find total separation Δz.

Answer: Approximately 0.2 mm (standard order of magnitude for Stern-Gerlach setups)

Assumptions and limitations

  • Electron spin is a purely quantum mechanical property with no direct classical analogue; treating it as physical spinning of a charge sphere violates relativity (speed of surface would exceed c).
  • The Landé g-factor for spin is taken as exactly 2 in non-relativistic theory, whereas quantum electrodynamics (QED) gives a slight anomalous correction (g_s ≈ 2.00232).

Common misconceptions

Common error: An electron physically spins on its own axis just like the Earth rotates.

Correct: Electron spin is an intrinsic, non-classical angular momentum.

Classical rotation of an electron point charge to match its spin angular momentum and magnetic moment would require surface velocities greater than the speed of light.

Common error: The spin quantum number s can take multiple integer and half-integer values like orbital quantum numbers.

Correct: The spin quantum number for an electron is strictly fixed at s = 1/2.

Spin is an intrinsic property of the fundamental particle type, whereas orbital quantum numbers depend on the specific quantum state (n, l).

Common error: Spin-up and spin-down electrons point in completely opposite directions along all three spatial axes.

Correct: Only the z-component is definite; x and y components are indeterminate.

Due to the commutation relations of angular momentum operators, measuring S<sub>z</sub> makes S<sub>x</sub> and S<sub>y</sub> completely uncertain.

Quick revision

  • Electron spin is an intrinsic angular momentum characterized by s = 1/2 and magnitude S = (√3/2)ℏ.
  • The z-component of spin is quantized into two states: m_s = +1/2 (spin-up) and m_s = -1/2 (spin-down).
  • The spin magnetic moment is proportional to the Bohr magneton with a g-factor close to 2.
  • The Stern-Gerlach experiment experimentally verifies the space quantization of electron spin.
  • Pauli's Exclusion Principle relies on half-integer spin characteristics (fermions).

Must remember

  • Electron spin was proposed by Samuel Goudsmit and George Uhlenbeck in 1925 to explain alkali metal spectral doublets.
  • The spin quantum number $s$ for an electron is strictly fixed at $1/2$.
  • The magnitude of spin angular momentum is given by $S = \sqrt{s(s+1)}\hbar = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\hbar$.
  • The z-component of spin angular momentum is quantized as $S_z = m_s \hbar$, where $m_s = \pm 1/2$.
  • The spin g-factor ($g_s$) is approximately 2, making the spin magnetic moment ratio twice that of orbital motion.
  • Pauli's Exclusion Principle states that no two identical fermions can occupy the same quantum state simultaneously.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Who proposed the concept of electron spin in 1925 to explain anomalies in alkali metal atomic spectra?

Explanation: Electron spin was postulated by Samuel Goudsmit and George Uhlenbeck in 1925 to account for the doublet structure in alkali metal atomic spectra.

2. What is the value of the spin quantum number $s$ for an electron?

Explanation: The spin quantum number for an electron is strictly fixed at $s = 1/2$ as an intrinsic property.

3. What is the magnitude of the spin angular momentum $S$ of an electron?

Explanation: Using $S = \sqrt{s(s+1)}\hbar$ with $s = 1/2$, we get $S = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+1)}\hbar = \sqrt{\frac{3}{4}}\hbar$.

4. What are the possible values for the z-component spin magnetic quantum number $m_s$ of an electron?

Explanation: The spin magnetic quantum number $m_s$ takes values from $-s$ to $+s$ with step 1, resulting in $\pm 1/2$ for electrons.

5. What is the approximate value of the spin g-factor ($g_s$) for an electron?

Explanation: The Landé g-factor for electron spin $g_s$ is approximately 2 (precisely slightly greater than 2 due to QED corrections).

6. Which mathematical expression represents the Bohr magneton $\mu_B$?

Explanation: The Bohr magneton is defined as $\mu_B = \frac{e\hbar}{2m_e}$, representing a fundamental unit of magnetic moment.

7. What is the z-component of the spin magnetic moment ($\mu_{sz}$) of an electron in a 'spin-up' state?

Explanation: The formula is $\mu_{sz} = -g_s \mu_B m_s$. For $g_s \approx 2$ and $m_s = +1/2$, $\mu_{sz} = -(2)(\mu_B)(1/2) = -\mu_B$.

8. According to Pauli's Exclusion Principle, what must be true for the total wavefunction of a multi-electron system?

Explanation: Pauli's Exclusion Principle requires the total wavefunction of identical fermions (such as electrons) to be antisymmetric under the exchange of any two particles.

9. What is the maximum number of electrons that can occupy a shell with principal quantum number $n$?

Explanation: The electron capacity of a shell is given by $2n^2$, which stems from orbital degeneracies and Pauli's Exclusion Principle combining with spin.

10. Why fails the classical model of an electron physically spinning on its own axis?

Explanation: Classical rotation of a point-like electron charge to yield its measured spin angular momentum and magnetic moment requires a radius smaller than its Compton wavelength, resulting in superluminal surface speeds.

11. In Russell-Saunders (L-S) coupling, total angular momentum $\vec{J}$ is given by:

Explanation: Total angular momentum in L-S coupling is the vector sum of orbital angular momentum $\vec{L}$ and spin angular momentum $\vec{S}$, expressed as $\vec{J} = \vec{L} + \vec{S}$.

12. What is the potential energy $U$ of an electron spin magnetic moment $\vec{\mu}_s$ in a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$ aligned with the z-axis?

Explanation: The magnetic potential energy is given by $U = -\vec{\mu}_s \cdot \vec{B} = -\mu_{sz} B_z$.

13. Which components of an electron's spin are indeterminate when $S_z$ is precisely measured?

Explanation: Due to quantum mechanical commutation relations, angular momentum components do not commute; measuring $S_z$ leaves $S_x$ and $S_y$ completely uncertain.

14. What kind of magnetic field is required to exert a deflecting force on a magnetic dipole via the Stern-Gerlach effect?

Explanation: The deflecting force depends on a spatial magnetic field gradient: $F_z = \mu_{sz} \frac{\partial B_z}{\partial z}$, requiring a non-homogeneous magnetic field.

15. What is the ratio of spin magnetic moment magnitude to orbital magnetic moment magnitude for the same angular momentum quantum state?

Explanation: Because the spin g-factor $g_s \approx 2$ while the orbital g-factor $g_l = 1$, the ratio of spin magnetic moment to orbital magnetic moment is a factor of 2.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The magnitude of spin angular momentum for an electron is expressed as:

Explanation: With $s=1/2$, $S = \sqrt{s(s+1)}\hbar = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{2})}\hbar = \sqrt{\frac{3}{4}}\hbar$.

2. In an inhomogeneous magnetic field, a beam of silver atoms splits into two components due to:

Explanation: The Stern-Gerlach experiment demonstrated space quantization of electron spin, splitting the beam into two paths corresponding to $m_s = \pm 1/2$.

3. The intrinsic spin magnetic moment of an electron is approximately equal to:

Explanation: The magnitude of the spin magnetic moment is $\mu_s = g_s \mu_B \sqrt{s(s+1)} \approx 2 \mu_B \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}\mu_B$, but its z-component or effective scale relates directly to Bohr magneton units; standard introductory scaling often takes $g_s s \mu_B = 1 \mu_B$ for maximum z projection.

4. Which quantum number distinguishes two electrons occupying the same spatial orbital in an atom?

Explanation: By Pauli's Exclusion Principle, two electrons in the same spatial orbital ($n, l, m_l$ identical) must have opposite spin quantum numbers ($m_s = +1/2$ and $m_s = -1/2$).

5. The gyromagnetic ratio for electron spin compared to orbital motion has a factor of:

Explanation: The spin gyromagnetic ratio is twice that of orbital motion due to the Landé g-factor $g_s \approx 2$.

6. What is the force experienced by an electron with z-component of magnetic moment $\mu_{sz}$ in a magnetic field gradient $\partial B_z / \partial z$?

Explanation: The deflecting force in the Stern-Gerlach setup is given by $F_z = \mu_{sz} \frac{\partial B_z}{\partial z}$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What physical anomaly prompted Goudsmit and Uhlenbeck to propose electron spin?

Explanation: Alkali metal atomic spectra exhibited doublets that could not be explained by orbital angular momentum alone, leading to the postulate of electron spin.

2. Calculate the magnitude of the spin angular momentum vector for an electron in units of $\hbar$.

Explanation: $S / \hbar = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{2})} = \sqrt{0.75} \approx 0.866$.

3. If an electron is placed in a magnetic field along the z-axis, how many energy sublevels does its spin produce?

Explanation: The two orientations corresponding to $m_s = +1/2$ and $m_s = -1/2$ split the energy level into two sublevels (Zeeman effect for spin).

4. Which principle guarantees that electrons in atoms fill subshells obeying the periodic table capacities?

Explanation: Pauli's Exclusion Principle restricts fermion occupation per quantum state to at most one, directly dictating electron shell and subshell capacities.

5. What is the energy difference $\Delta E$ between spin-up and spin-down states of an electron in a magnetic field $B_z$?

Explanation: The energy of spin-up is $U_1 = -(-\mu_B) B_z = \mu_B B_z$ (if taking $\mu_{sz} = \mp \mu_B$) and spin-down is $-\mu_B B_z$, making the total splitting $\Delta E = 2 \mu_B B_z$.

6. What does the vector model of the atom imply about the orientation of spin angular momentum vector $\vec{S}$ relative to the quantization axis?

Explanation: In quantum mechanics, the vector precesses around the z-axis such that $S_z$ remains fixed while $S_x$ and $S_y$ are indeterminate.

7. Which of the following particles is a fermion obeying Pauli's Exclusion Principle?

Explanation: Electrons have half-integer spin ($s=1/2$) and are fermions, whereas photons and $\alpha$ particles are bosons.

8. The dimensionality of the spin state space (Hilbert space for spin) of a single electron is:

Explanation: Because $m_s$ has 2 possible values ($+1/2$ and $-1/2$), the spin state space is 2-dimensional (represented by 2-component spinors).

9. In the Landé g-factor expression for total angular momentum $g_J$, electron spin contributes heavily because:

Explanation: The anomaly and strong magnetic coupling of electron spin originate from $g_s \approx 2$, which dominates magnetic interactions compared to orbital motion alone.

10. What is the primary conclusion of the Stern-Gerlach experiment regarding spatial orientation?

Explanation: The Stern-Gerlach experiment proved spatial quantization, showing that angular momentum vectors can only take discrete orientations relative to an external field axis.
