Vector Atom Model

The vector atom model extends Bohr's semiclassical theory by incorporating spatial quantization and electron spin through angular momentum vectors. It provides a comprehensive framework to describe multi-electron atoms and accounts for fine structure and magnetic interactions.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Qualitative understanding of spatial quantization, orbital and spin magnetic moments, and basic quantum numbers for single-electron systems.

PGT extension

Detailed treatment of space quantization, coupling schemes (L-S and j-j coupling), Lande g-factor, and vector addition of angular momenta in multi-electron systems.

Prerequisites

  • Bohr's theory of hydrogen atom
  • Electron spin and spin quantum number
  • Pauli's exclusion principle

Spatial Quantization

  • In the vector atom model, orbital angular momentum vector L is quantized in magnitude as sqrt(l(l+1)) ℏ.
  • The component of L along an external magnetic field (z-axis) is also quantized: Lz = ml ℏ, where ml takes values from -l to +l.
  • This restriction of orientations relative to a reference axis is known as spatial quantization.

Electron Spin and Spin-Orbit Interaction

  • The electron possesses an intrinsic spin angular momentum S with magnitude sqrt(s(s+1)) ℏ, where $s = 1/2$.
  • The spin component along the z-axis is Sz = ms ℏ, with ms = ±1/2.
  • Spin-orbit interaction arises from the interaction between the magnetic moment due to electron spin and the magnetic field experienced by the electron due to its orbital motion around the nucleus.

L-S Coupling (Russell-Saunders Coupling)

  • Valid for lighter atoms where electrostatic and spin-orbit interactions are dominant.
  • Individual orbital angular momenta li couple to form a resultant total orbital angular momentum $L = \sum l_{i}$.
  • Individual spin angular momenta si couple to form a resultant total spin angular momentum $S = \sum s_{i}$. L and S then couple to form total angular momentum $J = L + S$.

j-j Coupling

  • Valid for heavier atoms where individual spin-orbit coupling for each electron is stronger than the electrostatic interaction between electrons.
  • For each electron, the orbital angular momentum li and spin angular momentum si couple to form individual total angular momentum ji = li + si.
  • The individual ji vectors then couple to form the total angular momentum of the atom $J = \sum j_{i}$.

Derivations

Magnitude of Total Angular Momentum Vector

Conditions: Single electron system or total resultant vector

  • Express the angular momentum operator or vector magnitude equation: $J = \sqrt(j(j+1)) \hbar$.
  • Consider the components along a chosen z-axis: Jz = mj ℏ.
  • Apply the condition that the maximum projection of J on the z-axis cannot exceed its total magnitude, which intrinsically yields the quantum mechanical bounds for j and mj.

Result: $J = \sqrt{j(j+1)} \hbar$

Magnetic Interaction Energy in Spin-Orbit Coupling

Conditions: Perturbation approach for single electron

  • Write the interaction energy as ΔE = A (L ⋅ S), where A is the spin-orbit coupling parameter.
  • Express the dot product using the resultant vector $J = L + S$, so that J2 = L2 + S2 + 2(L ⋅ S).
  • Substitute the quantum mechanical eigenvalues for L2, S2, and J2 to solve for the dot product and evaluate ΔE.

Result: \Delta $E = \frac{A}{2} [j(j+1) - l(l+1) - s(s+1)] \hbar^2$

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the magnitude of orbital angular momentum of an electron in a d-state (l = 2).

  • Identify the orbital quantum number: l = 2.
  • Use the formula for orbital angular momentum: $L = \sqrt(l(l+1)) \hbar$.
  • Substitute $l = 2: L = \sqrt(2(2+1)) \hbar = \sqrt(6) \hbar$.

Answer: \sqrt{6} \hbar

PGT extension

Find the possible values of the total angular momentum quantum number j for an electron in a p-state (l = 1) where spin $s = 1/2$.

  • Identify the given quantum numbers: l = 1, $s = 1/2$.
  • Determine the range of j using the relation $j = |l - s|$ to (l + s) in integer steps.
  • Calculate lower bound: |1 - 1/2| = 1/2.
  • Calculate upper bound: 1 + 1/$2 = 3/2$.
  • List all permitted values: $j = 1/2$, 3/2.

Answer: $j = 1/2$, 3/2

PGT extension

Calculate the Lande g-factor for an atomic state with quantum numbers l = 1, $s = 1/2$, and $j = 3/2$.

  • Recall the formula for Lande g-factor: $g = 1 + [j(j+1) + s(s+1) - l(l+1)] / [2j(j+1)]$.
  • Substitute the values: $j = 3/2$, j(j+1) = (3/2)(5/2) = 15/4 = 3.75.
  • Substitute $s = 1/2$, s(s+1) = (1/2)(3/2) = 3/4 = 0.75.
  • Substitute l = 1, l(l+1) = (1)(2) = 2.
  • Numerato$r = 3.75 + 0.75 - 2 = 2.5$.
  • Denominato$r = 2 * 3.75 = 7.5$.
  • $g = 1 + 2.5 / 7.5 = 1 + 1/3 = 4/3$.

Answer: 4/3 (or 1.33)

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumption: Electrons can be treated as having well-defined classical-like angular momentum vectors that obey quantum mechanical commutation rules.
  • Limitation: Fails to fully account for relativistic quantum electrodynamic effects in very heavy, highly ionized atoms without Dirac equation corrections.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The angular momentum vector L can point in any arbitrary direction in space.

Correct: L is spatially quantized and can only take orientations such that its z-component is an integer multiple of ℏ.

Quantum mechanics restricts the simultaneous measurement of the magnitude and components of angular momentum.

Common error: In j-j coupling, individual orbital and spin momenta are independent of each other.

Correct: In j-j coupling, the spin and orbital momenta of the same electron strongly couple first to form ji.

Spin-orbit interaction for individual electrons dominates over electrostatic repulsion between different electrons in heavy atoms.

Quick revision

  • Orbital angular momentum magnitude is given by sqrt(l(l+1)) ℏ with z-projection ml ℏ.
  • Orbital Angular Momentum: $L = \sqrt{l(l+1)} \hbar$
  • Z-component of Orbital Angular Momentum: $L_z = m_l \hbar$

Must remember

  • In the vector atom model, the magnitude of orbital angular momentum is given by $L = \sqrt{l(l+1)} \hbar$.
  • The spatial quantization restricts the z-component of orbital angular momentum to $L_z = m_l \hbar$, where $m_l$ ranges from $-l$ to $+l$.
  • The intrinsic spin quantum number for an electron is $s = 1/2$, with spin angular momentum magnitude $S = \sqrt{s(s+1)} \hbar$.
  • L-S coupling (Russell-Saunders coupling) is valid typically for lighter atoms where electrostatic interactions dominate over spin-orbit interactions.
  • j-j coupling is valid for heavier atoms where the spin-orbit interaction of individual electrons is stronger than the electrostatic interaction between electrons.
  • The Lande g-factor is given by $g = 1 + \frac{j(j+1) + s(s+1) - l(l+1)}{2j(j+1)}$.
  • Bohr magneton is defined as $\mu_B = \frac{e \hbar}{2m_e}$.

Glossary

Spatial QuantizationThe restriction of the orientation of angular momentum vectors relative to a reference axis (such as an external magnetic field).
L-S CouplingRussell-Saunders coupling scheme where individual orbital and spin momenta couple separately into total $L$ and $S$, which then combine to form $J = L + S$.
j-j CouplingCoupling scheme where individual orbital $l_i$ and spin $s_i$ momenta couple first to form individual $j_i$, which then combine to form total $J = \sum j_i$.
Bohr MagnetonFundamental unit of magnetic moment for an electron, expressed as $\mu_B = \frac{e \hbar}{2m_e}$.
Lande g-factorDimensionless factor representing the ratio of the magnetic dipole moment to the angular momentum multiplied by a magneton ratio.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the magnitude of the orbital angular momentum $L$ for an electron in an $l = 2$ state in the vector atom model?

Explanation: The magnitude of orbital angular momentum is given by $L = \sqrt{l(l+1)} \hbar$. For $l = 2$, $L = \sqrt{2(2+1)} \hbar = \sqrt{6} \hbar$.

2. In the presence of an external magnetic field, the z-component of orbital angular momentum $L_z$ is quantized as:

Explanation: The component of orbital angular momentum along the z-axis is spatially quantized and given by $L_z = m_l \hbar$, where $m_l$ takes values from $-l$ to $+l$.

3. What is the spin quantum number $s$ for a single electron?

Explanation: An electron possesses an intrinsic spin quantum number $s = 1/2$.

4. Russell-Saunders (L-S) coupling is primarily applicable to which type of atoms?

Explanation: L-S coupling is valid for lighter atoms where electrostatic interactions and collective spin-orbit interactions are dominant.

5. In j-j coupling, what is the first step of vector combination for an individual electron?

Explanation: In j-j coupling, the orbital angular momentum $l_i$ and spin angular momentum $s_i$ of each individual electron couple first to form $j_i = l_i + s_i$.

6. What does the Lande g-factor describe?

Explanation: The Lande g-factor accounts for the anomalous magnetic moment contribution and relates the magnetic moment to the angular momentum vector in a weak magnetic field.

7. What is the formula for the Bohr magneton $\mu_B$?

Explanation: The fundamental constant for magnetic moment, the Bohr magneton, is given by $\mu_B = \frac{e \hbar}{2m_e}$.

8. Which of the following describes spatial quantization in the vector atom model?

Explanation: Spatial quantization restricts the orientation of angular momentum vectors such that their components along a reference axis are quantized values (e.g., $m_l \hbar$).

9. For an electron with $s = 1/2$, what are the possible values of the z-component spin quantum number $m_s$?

Explanation: The spin component along the z-axis is $S_z = m_s \hbar$, where $m_s = \pm 1/2$.

10. In L-S coupling, how is the total angular momentum $J$ formed?

Explanation: In Russell-Saunders coupling, individual orbital momenta combine into $L$ and individual spin momenta into $S$, which then couple to form the total angular momentum $J = L + S$.

11. Why is j-j coupling preferred for heavier atoms?

Explanation: In heavy atoms, the inner electrons experience strong spin-orbit interactions, making individual $j_i$ coupling more appropriate than collective L-S coupling.

12. What is the magnitude of the spin angular momentum $S$ for a single electron?

Explanation: Using $S = \sqrt{s(s+1)} \hbar$ with $s = 1/2$: $S = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{2})} \hbar = \sqrt{\frac{3}{4}} \hbar$.

13. What physical phenomenon causes spin-orbit interaction in an electron?

Explanation: Spin-orbit interaction arises from the magnetic field experienced by the electron in its rest frame due to the nuclear electric field appearing as a current loop (orbital motion).

14. In L-S coupling, if $L = 2$ and $S = 1$, what are the possible values of the total angular momentum quantum number $j$?

Explanation: The quantum number $j$ ranges from $|L-S|$ to $L+S$ in integer steps. For $L=2$ and $S=1$, $j$ can take values $|2-1| = 1$ up to $2+1 = 3$, i.e., $1, 2, 3$.

15. Which expression represents the orbital magnetic moment $\mu_L$ vector in terms of $L$?

Explanation: The classical-quantum correspondence for orbital magnetic moment gives $\mu_L = - \frac{e}{2m_e} L$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. For an electron state with $l = 3$, the maximum possible value of the z-component of orbital angular momentum $L_z$ is:

Explanation: The z-component is given by $L_z = m_l \hbar$. For $l=3$, the maximum value of $m_l$ is $+3$, hence max $L_z = 3\hbar$.

2. The Lande g-factor for a pure orbital magnetic moment (where $s=0, j=l$) is:

Explanation: Substituting $s=0$ and $j=l$ into the Lande g-factor formula $g = 1 + \frac{j(j+1) + s(s+1) - l(l+1)}{2j(j+1)}$, since $j=l$ and $s=0$, the numerator terms cancel out to 0, leaving $g = 1 + 0 = 1$.

3. In Russell-Saunders (L-S) coupling scheme, which interaction is neglected or considered secondary compared to electrostatic interactions?

Explanation: In L-S coupling, electrostatic repulsion and collective spin-orbit interactions are dominant, while individual spin-orbit interactions are treated as weak perturbations initially.

4. What is the total number of allowed orientations (spatial quantization states) for the orbital angular momentum vector $L$ of an electron with $l = 1$ in a magnetic field?

Explanation: The number of allowed orientations is given by $2l + 1$. For $l = 1$, $2(1) + 1 = 3$ states ($m_l = -1, 0, 1$).

5. If two electrons have individual orbital angular momenta $l_1 = 1$ and $l_2 = 1$, the possible values of the total orbital angular momentum quantum number $L$ in L-S coupling are:

Explanation: Vector addition of $l_1 = 1$ and $l_2 = 1$ yields total $L$ ranging from $|l_1 - l_2|$ to $l_1 + l_2$, i.e., $0, 1, 2$.

6. The intrinsic magnetic moment associated with electron spin is approximately how many times larger than its orbital counterpart for the same angular momentum unit due to the anomaly factor?

Explanation: Due to quantum electrodynamics corrections (Dirac equation + QED), the spin gyromagnetic ratio is approximately twice the orbital ratio ($g_s \approx 2$), making the spin magnetic moment twice as effective per unit angular momentum.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Calculate the magnitude of total angular momentum vector $J$ when quantum number $j = 3/2$.

Explanation: Magnitude of $J = \sqrt{j(j+1)} \hbar = \sqrt{\frac{3}{2}(\frac{5}{2})} \hbar = \sqrt{\frac{15}{4}} \hbar = \frac{\sqrt{15}}{2} \hbar$.

2. Which quantum numbers completely specify a state of a single electron in a central potential under the vector atom model formulation?

Explanation: Depending on whether we include spin and magnetic fields, states are described by principal ($n$), orbital ($l$), magnetic ($m_l$), spin ($s$), and spin magnetic ($m_s$) quantum numbers (or $j, m_j$). Thus, comprehensive frameworks utilize these sets.

3. In the vector model, if $L = 1$ and $S = 1/2$, what are the possible values for total angular momentum $j$?

Explanation: For $L=1$ and $S=1/2$, $j$ ranges from $|L-S|$ to $L+S$ in integer steps: $|1 - 1/2| = 1/2$ to $1 + 1/2 = 3/2$. Thus $j = 1/2, 3/2$.

4. What is the physical significance of spatial quantization?

Explanation: Spatial quantization dictates that the angle $\theta$ between the angular momentum vector and the z-axis is quantized because $\cos\theta = \frac{m_l}{\sqrt{l(l+1)}}$.

5. For heavy multi-electron atoms, why does L-S coupling break down?

Explanation: In heavy atoms, high nuclear charge creates strong inner electric fields leading to powerful spin-orbit coupling for each electron individually ($l_i$ with $s_i$), invalidating Russell-Saunders collective coupling.

6. What is the value of the magnetic quantum number $m_l$ for an electron in a p-orbital ($l = 1$)?

Explanation: For $l = 1$, the magnetic orbital quantum number $m_l$ takes integer values from $-l$ to $+l$, which are $-1, 0, +1$.

7. If an atom has $L = 2$, $S = 0$, and $J = 2$, what is the Lande g-factor?

Explanation: For a singlet state ($S=0$), $J=L$, substituting $s=0, j=l$ into the g-factor formula gives $g = 1$

8. The orbital magnetic dipole moment $\mu_L$ and the orbital angular momentum $L$ are related by a negative sign because:

Explanation: The negative sign in $\mu_L = -\frac{e}{2m_e}L$ reflects the negative charge of the electron; the circulation of negative charge creates a magnetic moment vector pointing opposite to the orbital angular momentum vector.

9. In j-j coupling, if two electrons have $j_1 = 1/2$ and $j_2 = 3/2$, what are the possible values of the total atomic angular momentum $J$?

Explanation: The total $J$ is obtained by vector addition of $j_1$ and $j_2$: $|1/2 - 3/2| = 1$ up to $1/2 + 3/2 = 2$. Thus $J$ can be 1 or 2.

10. Which of the following statements about electron spin in the vector atom model is correct?

Explanation: Electron spin is an intrinsic, immutable quantum property of leptons with spin quantum number $s = 1/2$.
