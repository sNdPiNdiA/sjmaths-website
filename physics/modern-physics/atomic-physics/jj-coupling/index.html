j-j Coupling Scheme in Atomic Physics

j-j coupling is an angular momentum coupling scheme where the spin angular momentum (<b>s</b>&lt;sub&gt;i&lt;/sub&gt;) and orbital angular momentum (<b>l</b>&lt;sub&gt;i&lt;/sub&gt;) of each individual electron couple strongly to form individual total angular momentum vectors (<b>j</b>&lt;sub&gt;i&lt;/sub&gt;). It is predominantly observed in heavy multi-electron atoms where spin-orbit interaction exceeds electrostatic repulsion.

Prerequisites

  • Vector model of the atom and spatial quantization
  • Orbital and spin angular momentum quantum numbers (l and s)
  • Pauli's exclusion principle and central field approximation

Physical Origin of j-j Coupling

  • In heavy multi-electron atoms (high atomic number Z), the nuclear charge is large, resulting in extremely strong internal magnetic fields due to orbital motion.
  • The spin-orbit interaction energy (proportional to li &sdot; si) for each individual electron becomes significantly larger than the electrostatic repulsion (residual Coulomb interaction) between electrons.
  • Consequently, the individual spin and orbital momenta of each electron are tightly bound to one another before interacting with the momenta of other electrons.

Vector Coupling Mechanism

  • For each electron 'i', the orbital angular momentum vector li and spin angular momentum vector si couple to form the individual total angular momentum vector ji</sub> = li + si.
  • The magnitude of ji is given by √(j_i(j_i + 1))ℏ, where j_i ranges from |l_i - s_i| to (l_i + s_i).
  • The individual total angular momentum vectors j1, j2, etc., then weakly couple together to form the resultant total angular momentum J of the entire atom.

Formation of Total Angular Momentum J

  • The resultant total angular momentum of the atom is given by the vector sum J = &sum; ji.
  • For a two-electron system, J = j1 + j2.
  • The magnitude of J is √(J(J + 1))ℏ, where J takes values from |j_1 - j_2| to (j_1 + j_2) in integer steps.

Comparison with L-S (Russell-Saunders) Coupling

  • L-S coupling applies when electrostatic repulsion is much stronger than spin-orbit interaction (light atoms, low Z), yielding good quantum numbers L and S.
  • j-j coupling applies when spin-orbit interaction dominates electrostatic repulsion (heavy atoms, high Z), yielding good individual quantum numbers j_i.
  • In j-j coupling, neither L nor S are good quantum numbers because the strong spin-orbit force rapidly precesses l and s about their resultant j.

Derivations

Total Angular Momentum for a Two-Electron System in j-j Coupling

Conditions: Two non-equivalent or equivalent electrons under strong spin-orbit interaction.

  • Step 1: Write the expression for the individual total angular momentum of electron 1: j1 = l1 + s1, with magnitude determined by quantum number j_1.
  • Step 2: Write the expression for the individual total angular momentum of electron 2: j2 = l2 + s2, with magnitude determined by quantum number j_2.
  • Step 3: Combine j1 and j2 vectorially to obtain the total atomic angular momentum: J = j1 + j2.
  • Step 4: Apply the triangle rule of vector addition to find the allowed values of the total angular momentum quantum number J.

Result: $J = |j_1 - j_2|$, |j_1 - j_2| + 1, ..., (j_1 + j_2)

Spin-Orbit Interaction Energy Shift in j-j Coupling

Conditions: Perturbation treatment for an electron in a central nuclear field.

  • Step 1: Express the spin-orbit Hamiltonian for an electron as $H_{so} = zeta(r)(l ⋅ s)$.
  • Step 2: Use the identity j = l + s to square both sides: $j^2 = l^2 + s^2 + 2(l ⋅ s)$.
  • Step 3: Solve for the dot product: (l &sdot; s) = (1/2)[j(j + 1) - l(l + 1) - s(s + 1)]ℏ^2.
  • Step 4: Substitute into the energy shift expectation value to find the level splitting.

Result: Δ$E_{so} = \frac{1}{2} \langle zeta(r) \rangle [j(j+1) - l(l+1) - s(s+1)] hbar^2$

Solved numericals

TGT core

For an electron with orbital quantum number l = 1 and spin quantum number $s = 1/2$, calculate the possible values of the individual total angular momentum quantum number j.

  • Step 1: Identify the formula for j: j = |l ± s|.
  • Step 2: Substitute l = 1 and $s = 1/2$.
  • Step 3: Calculate lower limit: |1 - 1/2| = 1/2.
  • Step 4: Calculate upper limit: 1 + 1/$2 = 3/2$.

Answer: $j = 1/2$ and 3/2

PGT extension

In a j-j coupling scheme, two electrons have individual total angular momentum quantum numbers $j_1 = 3/2$ and $j_2 = 1/2$. Determine all possible values of the total angular momentum quantum number J for the atom.

  • Step 1: Write the formula for the resultant total angular momentum J in j-j coupling: $J = |j_1 - j_2|$, |j_1 - j_2| + 1, ..., (j_1 + j_2).
  • Step 2: Substitute $j_1 = 3/2$ and $j_2 = 1/2 into the formula$.
  • Step 3: Calculate minimum value: |3/2 - 1/2| = 1.
  • Step 4: Calculate maximum value: 3/2 + 1/2 = 2.
  • Step 5: List all integer steps between minimum and maximum: J = 1 and 2.

Answer: J = 1, 2

PGT extension

Calculate the magnitude of the individual total angular momentum vector j for an electron in a state where l = 2 and $s = 1/2$, taking the higher energy spin-orbit component.

  • Step 1: Find the higher spin-orbit component where $j = l + s = 2 + 1/2 = 5/2$.
  • Step 2: Use the magnitude formula |j| = √(j(j + 1))ℏ.
  • Step 3: Substitute $j = 5/2: √((5/2)(5/2 + 1))ℏ = √((5/2)(7/2))ℏ$.
  • Step 4: Simplify the radical: √(35/4)ℏ = (√35 / 2)ℏ.

Answer: (\sqrt{35}/2) \hbar

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumption: Central field approximation holds where each electron moves independently in a spherically symmetric potential created by the nucleus and averaged charge cloud of other electrons.
  • Limitation: Breaks down for intermediate coupling regimes where electrostatic repulsion and spin-orbit interactions are of comparable magnitude (neither pure L-S nor pure j-j coupling applies).

Common misconceptions

Common error: L and S are good quantum numbers in j-j coupling.

Correct: L and S are not good quantum numbers in j-j coupling.

Strong spin-orbit interaction destroys the independent conservation of total orbital (L) and total spin (S) angular momenta.

Common error: j-j coupling is typically found in light atoms like Helium.

Correct: j-j coupling is found in heavy atoms with large atomic numbers (Z).

Heavy atoms have strong internal magnetic fields that make spin-orbit coupling much larger than electrostatic repulsion.

Quick revision

  • In j-j coupling, individual l and s couple first to form j_i, and then individual j_i couple to form total J.
  • Individual Total Angular Momentum: $j_i = l_i + s_i$
  • Magnitude of Individual j_i: |$j_i| = \sqrt{j_i(j_i + 1)} \hbar$

Must remember

  • In the j-j coupling scheme, the spin-orbit interaction energy for each electron is significantly larger than the electrostatic repulsion between electrons.
  • j-j coupling predominantly occurs in heavy multi-electron atoms with high atomic numbers ($Z$).
  • For each electron, the orbital angular momentum $l_i$ and spin angular momentum $s_i$ couple to form individual total angular momentum $j_i = l_i + s_i$.
  • In j-j coupling, neither total orbital angular momentum $L$ nor total spin angular momentum $S$ are good quantum numbers.
  • The resultant total angular momentum $J$ of the atom is obtained by the vector sum of individual $j_i$ vectors: $J = \sum j_i$.

Glossary

j-j CouplingAn angular momentum coupling scheme where individual spin-orbit interactions are stronger than residual electrostatic interactions.
Individual Total Angular Momentum ($j_i$)The vector sum of the orbital angular momentum $l_i$ and spin angular momentum $s_i$ of a single electron.
Total Angular Momentum ($J$)The vector sum of all individual electron total angular momenta $j_i$ in an atom.
Spin-Orbit InteractionThe interaction between an electron's spin magnetic moment and the magnetic field created by its orbital motion around the nucleus.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. In the $j$-$j$ coupling scheme, which interaction is much stronger than the electrostatic repulsion between electrons?

Explanation: In $j$-$j$ coupling, the internal magnetic field is very strong due to high nuclear charge in heavy atoms, making the spin-orbit interaction ($l_i \cdot s_i$) significantly larger than the residual electrostatic repulsion.

2. For which type of atoms is the $j$-$j$ coupling scheme most appropriate?

Explanation: Heavy atoms possess large nuclear charges that generate extremely strong internal magnetic fields, favoring the $j$-$j$ coupling scheme over L-S coupling.

3. Which of the following are considered 'good' quantum numbers in the $j$-$j$ coupling scheme?

Explanation: In $j$-$j$ coupling, individual $j_i$ and the total resultant angular momentum $J$ remain good quantum numbers, whereas $L$ and $S$ lose their significance.

4. The individual total angular momentum vector for an electron in $j$-$j$ coupling is given by:

Explanation: For each individual electron, the orbital angular momentum $l_i$ and spin angular momentum $s_i$ couple vectorially to form the individual total angular momentum $j_i = l_i + s_i$.

5. What is the magnitude of the individual total angular momentum vector $|j_i|$?

Explanation: The quantum mechanical magnitude of any angular momentum vector associated with quantum number $j_i$ is given by $\sqrt{j_i(j_i+1)} \hbar$.

6. In a two-electron system under $j$-$j$ coupling, how is the total angular momentum $J$ obtained?

Explanation: The resultant total angular momentum $J$ in $j$-$j$ coupling is found by the vector sum of the individual total angular momenta: $J = j_1 + j_2$.

7. Why are $L$ and $S$ not good quantum numbers in $j$-$j$ coupling?

Explanation: The strong spin-orbit interaction tightly binds $l_i$ and $s_i$ to form $j_i$, causing rapid precession that destroys the independent conservation of total $L$ and total $S$.

8. For a two-electron system, if $j_1 = 3/2$ and $j_2 = 1/2$, what are the possible values of the total angular momentum quantum number $J$?

Explanation: According to vector addition rules, $J$ ranges from $|j_1 - j_2|$ to $(j_1 + j_2)$ in integer steps. Thus, $|3/2 - 1/2| = 1$ to $3/2 + 1/2 = 2$, giving possible integer step values $1$ and $2$. Wait, $|3/2-1/2|=1$ and $3/2+1/2=2$, so $J = 1, 2$. Let's check options: $1, 2$ is option B.

9. What is the range of values for the individual total angular momentum quantum number $j_i$ of an electron with orbital quantum number $l_i$ and spin $s_i = 1/2$?

Explanation: The quantum number $j_i$ ranges from $|l_i - s_i|$ to $(l_i + s_i)$ in integer steps. Since $s_i = 1/2$, $j_i$ takes values $l_i + 1/2$ and $l_i - 1/2$ (for $l_i > 0$).

10. What is the scalar product expansion for $l_i \cdot s_i$ in terms of quantum numbers?

Explanation: Squaring $j_i = l_i + s_i$ yields $j_i^2 = l_i^2 + s_i^2 + 2 l_i \cdot s_i$, leading to $l_i \cdot s_i = \frac{1}{2} [j_i(j_i + 1) - l_i(l_i + 1) - s_i(s_i + 1)] \hbar^2$.

11. In the transition from light atoms to heavy atoms, which coupling scheme tends to break down in favor of $j$-$j$ coupling?

Explanation: As atomic number increases, spin-orbit interaction overtakes electrostatic repulsion, causing L-S coupling to break down and transition into $j$-$j$ coupling.

12. For non-equivalent electrons, the total number of microstates (degeneracy) for a given set of $j_i$ values is given by:

Explanation: The total number of states or degeneracy for non-equivalent electrons in $j$-$j$ coupling is the product of the individual state degeneracies: $\prod (2j_i + 1)$.

13. Which of the following statements is correct regarding the vector coupling mechanism in $j$-$j$ coupling?

Explanation: In $j$-$j$ coupling, individual electron orbital ($l_i$) and spin ($s_i$) vectors couple first to form $j_i$, and then these $j_i$ vectors couple to form total $J$.

14. If $j_1 = 1/2$ and $j_2 = 1/2$, what are the possible values of the total angular momentum quantum number $J$?

Explanation: Using vector addition rules for $j_1 = 1/2$ and $j_2 = 1/2$, $J$ ranges from $|1/2 - 1/2| = 0$ to $1/2 + 1/2 = 1$. Thus, $J$ can be $0$ or $1$.

15. What is the physical cause of the strong spin-orbit interaction in heavy multi-electron atoms?

Explanation: A high nuclear charge ($Z$) in heavy atoms results in strong internal electric fields felt by electrons, leading to strong internal magnetic fields via relative motion, which amplifies spin-orbit interaction.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. In heavy atoms where the spin-orbit interaction is much stronger than electrostatic repulsion, the appropriate coupling scheme is:

Explanation: When spin-orbit interaction dominates over electrostatic repulsion (typical of heavy atoms), the $j$-$j$ coupling scheme is applicable.

2. Which of the following quantum numbers are NOT conserved or 'good' in the $j$-$j$ coupling scheme?

Explanation: Because the strong spin-orbit interaction couples $l_i$ and $s_i$ tightly into $j_i$, total orbital angular momentum $L$ and total spin $S$ are not good quantum numbers in $j$-$j$ coupling.

3. For an electron with $l = 2$ and $s = 1/2$, the possible values of the individual total angular momentum quantum number $j$ are:

Explanation: The quantum number $j$ ranges from $|l - s|$ to $(l + s)$. For $l = 2$ and $s = 1/2$, $j = |2 - 1/2| = 3/2$ and $j = 2 + 1/2 = 5/2$.

4. In a two-electron system governed by $j$-$j$ coupling, if $j_1 = 5/2$ and $j_2 = 3/2$, the maximum possible value of the total angular momentum quantum number $J$ is:

Explanation: The maximum value of $J$ is given by $j_1 + j_2 = 5/2 + 3/2 = 8/2 = 4$.

5. The first-order energy correction due to spin-orbit interaction in $j$-$j$ coupling involves which dot product summation?

Explanation: The spin-orbit energy correction is proportional to the sum of dot products of individual electron orbital and spin angular momenta: $\sum l_i \cdot s_i$.

6. What is the primary physical reason for the breakdown of L-S coupling in heavy atoms?

Explanation: L-S coupling relies on electrostatic repulsion dominating spin-orbit interaction. In heavy atoms, spin-orbit interaction increases dramatically with $Z$ ($Z^4$ dependence), causing L-S coupling to break down.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. In the $j$-$j$ coupling model, what is the coupling sequence for forming the total angular momentum $J$?

Explanation: In $j$-$j$ coupling, each electron first couples its own $l_i$ and $s_i$ to form $j_i$, and then these individual $j_i$ vectors combine to form the total angular momentum $J$.

2. If an electron has $l = 1$ and $s = 1/2$, the possible values of $j$ are:

Explanation: For $l = 1$ and $s = 1/2$, $j$ ranges from $|1 - 1/2| = 1/2$ to $1 + 1/2 = 3/2$. Thus, $j$ can be $1/2$ or $3/2$.

3. Which coupling scheme is generally valid when electrostatic repulsion between electrons is much stronger than spin-orbit interaction?

Explanation: When electrostatic repulsion dominates over spin-orbit interaction (typically in light atoms), Russell-Saunders (L-S) coupling applies.

4. For a two-electron configuration where $j_1 = 3/2$ and $j_2 = 3/2$, what are the possible integer values of total angular momentum $J$?

Explanation: Using vector addition for $j_1 = 3/2$ and $j_2 = 3/2$, $J$ ranges from $|3/2 - 3/2| = 0$ to $3/2 + 3/2 = 3$ in integer steps: $0, 1, 2, 3$.

5. In $j$-$j$ coupling, what happens to the vectors $l_i$ and $s_i$ due to the strong spin-orbit interaction?

Explanation: The strong internal spin-orbit force causes $l_i$ and $s_i$ to precess rapidly about their resultant individual $j_i$, tightly binding them together.

6. What is the dimension or expression for the degeneracy (number of states) of an electron state with total angular momentum quantum number $j_i$?

Explanation: A quantum state characterized by total angular momentum $j_i$ has a spatial orientation degeneracy of $2j_i + 1$.

7. In the context of multi-electron atoms, why does L-S coupling fail for very heavy elements?

Explanation: The spin-orbit interaction energy increases strongly with atomic number $Z$ (approximately as $Z^4$), making it much larger than electrostatic repulsion in heavy atoms.

8. If two non-equivalent electrons have individual total angular momenta $j_1 = 1/2$ and $j_2 = 3/2$, what is the total number of microstates for this configuration?

Explanation: The total number of microstates for non-equivalent electrons is given by $\prod (2j_i + 1) = (2(1/2) + 1)(2(3/2) + 1) = (2)(4) = 8$.

9. Which of the following parameters is conserved in the absence of external fields for both L-S and $j$-$j$ coupling schemes?

Explanation: Regardless of whether L-S or $j$-$j$ coupling holds, the total angular momentum $J$ and its projection $M_J$ remain conserved quantities for an isolated atom.

10. How does the Landé g-factor formula in $j$-$j$ coupling differ conceptually from L-S coupling?

Explanation: In $j$-$j$ coupling, the Landé g-factor accounts for the magnetic moment contributions summed over the individual electron states ($j_i, l_i, s_i$) since $L$ and $S$ are not defined.
