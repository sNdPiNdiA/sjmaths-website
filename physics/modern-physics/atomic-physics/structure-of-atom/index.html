Structure of Atom

The structure of the atom encompasses the nuclear model surrounded by orbiting electrons governed by quantum mechanics. It forms the foundation for understanding atomic spectra, electron configurations, and multi-electron coupling phenomena.

TGT core

Prerequisites

  • Basic vector algebra and quantum numbers

Bohr's Postulates and Quantization of Angular Momentum

  • Electrons revolve around the nucleus in certain stable, non-radiating circular orbits called stationary states.
  • The angular momentum of an orbiting electron is quantized as an integral multiple of h/2π.
  • Radiation is emitted or absorbed only when an electron transitions between two allowed energy states according to h$\nu = E_2 - E_1$.

Spatial Quantization and Electron Spin

  • Spatial quantization refers to the restriction of the orientation of angular momentum vectors in space relative to an external magnetic field.
  • Electron spin is an intrinsic angular momentum characterized by spin quantum number $s = 1/2$ and magnetic spin quantum number $m_s = \pm 1/2$.
  • The total angular momentum vector and its z-component are quantized, giving rise to space quantization.

Pauli's Exclusion Principle and Electronic Configuration

  • No two electrons in an atom can have the exact same set of all four quantum numbers (n, l, m_l, m_s).
  • Each orbital can accommodate a maximum of two electrons with opposite spins.
  • This principle explains the periodic table structure and shell capacities given by 2n^2.

Coupling Schemes (L-S and j-j Coupling)

  • In L-S (Russell-Saunders) coupling, individual orbital angular momenta (l_i) couple to form total L, and spin angular momenta (s_i) couple to form total S; L and S then couple to form total J.
  • L-S coupling is valid for light and medium-weight atoms where spin-orbit interaction is weaker than electrostatic repulsion.
  • In j-j coupling, individual orbital and spin angular momenta couple first for each electron ($j_i = l_i + s_i)$, and then these j_i vectors couple to form the total angular momentum J.

Derivations

Radius and Velocity of n-th Bohr Orbit

Conditions: Circular orbit of an electron around a stationary nucleus of atomic number Z

  • Equate electrostatic force to centripetal force: \frac{1}{4\pi \epsilon_0} \frac{Ze^2}{$r^2 = \frac{mv^2}{r}$
  • Apply Bohr's quantization condition for angular momentum: mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi}
  • Eliminate velocity v from the two equations to solve for radius r_n
  • Substitute r_n back into the velocity equation to get v_n

Result: $r_n = \frac{\epsilon_0 n^2 h^2}{\pi m Z e^2}$ and $v_n = \frac{Ze^2}{2 \epsilon_0 n h}$

Total Energy of Electron in n-th Bohr Orbit

Conditions: Hydrogen-like atom (single electron)

  • Calculate kinetic energy $K = \frac{1}{2} mv^2 = \frac{1}{4\pi \epsilon_0} \frac{Ze^2}{2r}$
  • Calculate potential energy $U = -\frac{1}{4\pi \epsilon_0} \frac{Ze^2}{r}$
  • Sum kinetic and potential energy: $E = K + U = -\frac{1}{4\pi \epsilon_0} \frac{Ze^2}{2r}$
  • Substitute the expression for r_n into the total energy equation

Result: $E_n = -\frac{m Z^2 e^4}{8 \epsilon_0^2 h^2 n^2} = -13.6 \frac{Z^2}{n^2} \text{ eV}$

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the wavelength of the first line of the Balmer series in the hydrogen spectrum. (Given $R = 1.097 x 10^7 m^-1)$

  • Identify the transitions for the first line of Balmer series: $n_1 = 2$ and $n_2 = 3$.
  • Use the Rydberg formula: 1/$\lambda = R (1/n_1^2 - 1/n_2^2)$.
  • Substitute values: 1/$\lambda = 1.097 \times 10^7 (1/2^2 - 1/3^2) = 1.097 \times 10^7 (1/4 - 1/9) = 1.097 \times 10^7 (5/36)$.
  • Calculate $\lambda = 36 / (5 \times 1.097 \times 10^7) = 6.563 \times 10^-7 \text{ m}$.

Answer: 656.3 nm (or 6563 Å)

PGT extension

Find the possible values of total angular momentum quantum number J for two electrons with orbital quantum numbers $l_1 = 1$ and $l_2 = 1 under L-S coupling$.

  • For $l_1 = 1$ and $l_2 = 1$, total orbital angular momentum L can take values from |l_1 - l_2| to l_1 + l_2, i.e., L = 0, 1, 2.
  • For two electrons, individual spins are $s_1 = 1/2$ and $s_2 = 1/2$. Total spin S can take values 0 and 1.
  • Total angular momentum J for each (L, S) combination ranges from |L - S| to L + S.
  • For L = 0, $S = 0 -> J = 0$. For L = 0, $S = 1 -> J = 1$. For L = 1, $S = 0 -> J = 1$. For L = 1, $S = 1 -> J = 0$, 1, 2. For L = 2, $S = 0 -> J = 2$. For L = 2, $S = 1 -> J = 1$, 2, 3.

Answer: J values are 0, 1, 2, and 3 depending on the L and S state combinations.

TGT core

What is the radius of the third orbit of a hydrogen atom? (Bohr radius $r_1 = 0.53 Å)$

  • Use the radius relation for hydrogen-like atoms: $r_n = r_1 \times (n^2 / Z)$.
  • For hydrogen, Z = 1, and for the third orbit, n = 3.
  • Substitute the values: $r_3 = 0.53 \times (3^2 / 1) = 0.53 \times 9$.
  • Multiply: $r_3 = 4.77 Å$.

Answer: 4.77 Å

Assumptions and limitations

  • Bohr's model assumes circular orbits, whereas Sommerfeld later introduced elliptical orbits with relativistic corrections.
  • Bohr's theory is strictly applicable only to single-electron systems (hydrogen, He^+, Li^2+) and fails for multi-electron atoms due to inter-electronic repulsions.
  • Bohr's model treats electrons as classical particles with precise trajectories, violating Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Bohr's orbits are three-dimensional spherical shells.

Correct: Bohr's stationary states are planar circular orbits, whereas quantum mechanical orbitals are three-dimensional probability distribution regions.

Bohr's model is semi-classical and predates wave mechanics and the Schrödinger equation.

Common error: Total energy of an orbiting electron is positive.

Correct: The total energy of a bound electron in an atom is negative.

Negative energy signifies a bound state where external work is required to remove the electron to infinity.

Common error: L-S coupling applies strictly to all heavy atoms.

Correct: L-S coupling is valid mainly for light atoms, whereas j-j coupling is preferred for heavy atoms where spin-orbit interaction is dominant.

Electrostatic forces dominate in light atoms, while relativistic spin-orbit interactions dominate in heavy nuclear fields.

Quick revision

  • Radius of n-th orbit is proportional to n^2/Z, and velocity is proportional to Z/n.
  • Total energy of hydrogen in state n is $E_n = -13.6/n^2 eV$.
  • Lyman series lies in ultraviolet, Balmer in visible, and Paschen/Brackett/Pfund in infrared regions.
  • Pauli's exclusion principle states no two electrons can share all four quantum numbers (n, l, m_l, m_s).
  • In L-S coupling, L and S couple to form J; in j-j coupling, individual j_i vector components couple to form total J.

Must remember

  • The angular momentum of an orbiting electron in Bohr's model is quantized as $L = \frac{nh}{2\pi}$.
  • The total energy of an electron in the $n$-th Bohr orbit of a hydrogen-like atom is $E_n = -13.6 \frac{Z^2}{n^2} \text{ eV}$.
  • Pauli's exclusion principle states that no two electrons can have identical sets of all four quantum numbers ($n, l, m_l, m_s$).
  • In L-S (Russell-Saunders) coupling, individual orbital and spin momenta couple separately into total $L$ and $S$, then combine to form $J$.
  • In j-j coupling, individual orbital and spin momenta couple first for each electron ($j_i = l_i + s_i$), which then combine to form total $J$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the angular momentum of an electron in the $n$-th Bohr orbit?

Explanation: According to Bohr's postulate, the orbital angular momentum is quantized as an integral multiple of $\frac{h}{2\pi}$, i.e., $L = \frac{nh}{2\pi}$.

2. What is the expression for the total energy of an electron in the $n$-th orbit of a hydrogen-like atom with atomic number $Z$?

Explanation: The total energy in the $n$-th stationary state is given by $E_n = -13.6 \frac{Z^2}{n^2} \text{ eV}$, reflecting the bound nature of the electron.

3. How does the velocity of an electron vary with principal quantum number $n$ in the Bohr model?

Explanation: The velocity in the $n$-th orbit is given by $v_n = \frac{c}{137} \frac{Z}{n}$, showing it is inversely proportional to $n$.

4. What is the maximum capacity of a shell with principal quantum number $n$ according to Pauli's exclusion principle?

Explanation: Each principal shell can hold a maximum of $2n^2$ electrons, derived from the fact that each orbital accommodates two electrons with opposite spins.

5. Which quantum number characterizes the intrinsic spin angular momentum of an electron?

Explanation: Electron spin is an intrinsic property characterized by spin quantum number $s = 1/2$ and magnetic spin quantum number $m_s = \pm 1/2$.

6. In L-S coupling, individual orbital angular momenta $l_i$ first combine to form:

Explanation: In L-S (Russell-Saunders) coupling, individual $l_i$ vectors couple to form total $L$, and individual $s_i$ vectors couple to form total $S$.

7. Under what condition is L-S coupling generally valid?

Explanation: L-S coupling is valid for light and medium-weight atoms where spin-orbit interaction is weaker than electrostatic repulsion.

8. In j-j coupling, the coupling scheme for an individual electron is:

Explanation: In j-j coupling, individual orbital and spin angular momenta couple first for each electron to form $j_i = l_i + s_i$.

9. What is the unit of the Bohr Magneton $\mu_B$?

Explanation: The Bohr magneton is a fundamental unit for magnetic dipole moment, expressed in $\text{J/T}$ (Joules per Tesla) or $\text{A m}^2$.

10. Why is the total energy of a bound electron negative?

Explanation: A negative total energy signifies a bound state where energy must be supplied from outside to free the electron.

11. What does spatial quantization refer to?

Explanation: Spatial quantization restricts the possible orientations of angular momentum vectors relative to a reference axis defined by an external magnetic field.

12. Which of the following describes the Rydberg formula for wave number in atomic spectra?

Explanation: The Rydberg formula gives the wave number of emitted or absorbed radiation as $\bar{\nu} = R Z^2 \left(\frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2}\right)$.

13. What is the orbital magnetic moment $\mu_l$ associated with an electron having orbital angular momentum $L$?

Explanation: The orbital magnetic moment is given by $\mu_l = -\frac{e}{2m} L$, where the negative sign accounts for the negative charge of the electron.

14. What is the expression for the Bohr radius $r_1$ in the ground state of hydrogen?

Explanation: Substituting $n=1$ and $Z=1$ into the radius formula yields $r_1 = \frac{\epsilon_0 h^2}{\pi m e^2}$.

15. According to Pauli's Exclusion Principle, what is the maximum number of electrons that can occupy a single orbital?

Explanation: An orbital can accommodate a maximum of two electrons, and they must have opposite spins ($m_s = \pm 1/2$).

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The radius of the second Bohr orbit of a hydrogen atom is given in terms of the Bohr radius $r_1$ as:

Explanation: Since the radius of the $n$-th orbit is proportional to $n^2$ ($r_n = r_1 n^2$), for $n = 2$, the radius is $2^2 r_1 = 4 r_1$.

2. What is the ratio of frequencies of the second line of the Balmer series to the series limit of the Lyman series?

Explanation: The second line of Balmer corresponds to $n_1=2$ to $n_2=4$. The Lyman limit corresponds to $n_1=1$ to $n_2=\infty$. Calculating the wave numbers/frequencies gives the ratio $3/32$.

3. In the vector atom model, what is the Landé g-factor for a pure orbital magnetic moment where $S=0$ and $J=L$?

Explanation: Substituting $S=0$ and $J=L$ into the Landé g-factor formula $g = 1 + \frac{J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)}$ yields $g = 1$.

4. The total number of possible values of magnetic quantum number $m_l$ for a given value of azimuthal quantum number $l$ is:

Explanation: For a given $l$, $m_l$ can take integer values from $-l$ to $+l$, making a total of $2l + 1$ values.

5. For a multi-electron atom, when is j-j coupling preferred over L-S coupling?

Explanation: Heavy atoms experience strong nuclear charges leading to relativistic spin-orbit interactions exceeding electrostatic forces, making j-j coupling appropriate.

6. What is the energy required to ionize a hydrogen atom from its ground state?

Explanation: The ground state energy is $-13.6 \text{ eV}$. To remove the electron to infinity ($E=0$), an energy of $+13.6 \text{ eV}$ must be supplied.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following transitions in a hydrogen atom emits a photon of maximum frequency?

Explanation: Maximum frequency corresponds to maximum energy difference. The transition from $n=6$ to $n=1$ involves the largest energy drop among the finite options listed (note: $n=\infty$ to $n=1$ is absorption/ionization limit, but for emission, $6 \to 1$ yields the highest energy change among discrete states).

2. What is the orbital angular momentum of an electron in a $d$-orbital ($l=2$)?

Explanation: Orbital angular momentum is given by $\sqrt{l(l+1)} \hbar$. For $l=2$, $\sqrt{2(2+1)} \hbar = \sqrt{6} \hbar = \sqrt{6} \frac{h}{2\pi}$.

3. In L-S coupling, if $L=1$ and $S=1/2$, what are the possible values of the total angular momentum quantum number $J$?

Explanation: Total $J$ ranges from $|L-S|$ to $L+S$ in integer steps. $|1 - 1/2| = 1/2$ to $1 + 1/2 = 3/2$. Thus, $J = 1/2, 3/2$.

4. Which postulate correctly distinguishes Bohr's model from classical electrodynamics?

Explanation: Bohr introduced stationary states where electrons do not radiate energy, resolving the classical electrodynamic dilemma of atomic collapse.

5. What is the ratio of velocities of an electron in the first Bohr orbit of hydrogen ($Z=1$) to that in the second Bohr orbit of He+ ($Z=2$)?

Explanation: Velocity is $v_n \propto \frac{Z}{n}$. For hydrogen ($Z=1, n=1$), $v_1 \propto 1$. For He+ ($Z=2, n=2$), $v_2 \propto \frac{2}{2} = 1$. Thus, the ratio is $1:1$.

6. The existence of electron spin was initially introduced to explain:

Explanation: Electron spin and its associated magnetic moment account for the fine structure splitting in atomic spectra and the anomalous Zeeman effect.

7. How many quantum numbers are required to completely specify the state of an electron in an atom?

Explanation: Four quantum numbers ($n, l, m_l, m_s$) are required to fully specify the energy, orbital shape, orientation, and spin of an electron.

8. In j-j coupling, if two electrons have $j_1 = 1/2$ and $j_2 = 3/2$, what are the possible values of the total angular momentum $J$?

Explanation: The total $J$ for $j-j$ coupling combines $j_1$ and $j_2$ vectorially: $|1/2 - 3/2| = 1$ to $1/2 + 3/2 = 2$. Thus, $J = 1, 2$.

9. What does the principal quantum number $n$ primarily determine in a hydrogen atom?

Explanation: The principal quantum number $n$ determines the main energy level and the effective size or average distance of the electron from the nucleus.

10. Which series of the hydrogen spectrum lies in the ultraviolet region?

Explanation: Transitions ending at $n_1 = 1$ constitute the Lyman series, which falls in the ultraviolet region of the electromagnetic spectrum.
