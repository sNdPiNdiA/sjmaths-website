Crystal Lattices and Solid State Physics

A crystal lattice is a three-dimensional periodic array of points representing the positions of atoms, ions, or molecules in a crystalline solid. It provides the foundational framework for analyzing the structural, thermal, and electronic properties of solids through periodicity and translational symmetry.

Crystal Lattice and Basis

  • A space lattice is an infinite array of discrete points in three dimensions where each point has an identical environment.
  • A crystal structure is formed by attaching a motif or basis of one or more atoms to every lattice point: Crystal Structure = Lattice + Basis.
  • Translational symmetry of the lattice is defined by the translation vector R = n1a + n2b + n3c, where a, b, c are primitive translation vectors and ni are integers.

Unit Cell and Bravais Lattices

  • A unit cell is the fundamental geometric block that, when repeated in three dimensions, generates the entire crystal lattice.
  • Primitive unit cell contains only one lattice point and possesses the minimum volume.
  • There are 7 crystal systems (cubic, tetragonal, orthorhombic, monoclinic, triclinic, hexagonal, rhombohedral) which expand into 14 Bravais lattices in three dimensions.

Miller Indices and Interplanar Spacing

  • Miller indices (hkl) are a set of integers used to designate crystallographic planes, obtained by taking the reciprocals of fractional intercepts the plane makes with the crystal axes, cleared of fractions.
  • The interplanar spacing dhkl depends on the Miller indices and the lattice parameters.
  • For a cubic system with lattice parameter a, the interplanar spacing is given by dhkl = a / sqrt(h2 + k2 + l2).

Reciprocal Lattice

  • The reciprocal lattice is a lattice in Fourier space corresponding to the direct crystal lattice, crucial for analyzing diffraction and band structures.
  • The primitive reciprocal vectors are defined as a* = 2\pi (b \times c) / (a \cdot (b \times c)), with cyclic permutations.
  • The volume of a reciprocal unit cell is inversely proportional to the volume of the direct real-space unit cell.

Derivations

Bragg's Law of X-ray Diffraction

Conditions: Monochromatic X-rays incident on parallel crystal planes with spacing d at grazing angle \theta.

  • Consider two parallel atomic planes separated by an interplanar distance d.
  • X-ray waves strike the planes at an angle \theta. The path difference between rays reflected from successive planes is derived from geometry as \Delta = d \sin\theta + d \sin\theta = 2d \sin\theta.
  • For constructive interference to occur, the path difference must be an integral multiple of the wavelength \lambda.

Result: 2d \sin\theta = n\lambda

Interplanar Spacing for Cubic Crystal System

Conditions: Cubic crystal system with lattice parameter a and Miller indices (hkl).

  • Consider a plane (hkl) making intercepts a/h, a/k, and a/l along the three orthogonal axes x, y, and z.
  • The normal unit vector n to the plane has direction cosines proportional to (h/a, k/a, l/a).
  • The interplanar spacing d is the perpendicular distance from the origin to the plane, expressed as d = (a/h) \cos\alpha = a / \sqrt{h^2 + k^2 + l^2}.

Result: d_{hkl} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2 + l^2}}

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the interplanar spacing for (110) planes in a simple cubic lattice having a lattice parameter of 0.3 nm.

  • Identify given parameters: a = 0.3 nm, h = 1, k = 1, l = 0.
  • Use the interplanar spacing formula for a cubic system: d = a / \sqrt{h^2 + k^2 + l^2}.
  • Substitute values: d = 0.3 / \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2 + 0^2} = 0.3 / \sqrt{2} nm.
  • Calculate final numerical value: d = 0.3 / 1.414 = 0.212 nm.

Answer: 0.212 nm

PGT extension

X-rays of wavelength 0.154 nm are diffracted from (111) planes of a face-centered cubic crystal with lattice parameter 0.4 nm at first-order diffraction. Find the Bragg angle.

  • Find interplanar spacing d for (111) in cubic system: d = a / \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2 + 1^2} = 0.4 / \sqrt{3} nm = 0.2309 nm.
  • Apply Bragg's law: 2d \sin\theta = n\lambda with n = 1.
  • Substitute values: 2(0.2309) \sin\theta = (1)(0.154).
  • Calculate sin\theta = 0.154 / 0.4618 = 0.3335.
  • Find \theta = \arcsin(0.3335) \approx 19.48°.

Answer: 19.48°

PGT extension

Determine the atomic packing factor for a Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) crystal structure assuming hard sphere contact along the body diagonal.

  • Relation between lattice parameter a and atomic radius r for BCC: 4r = \sqrt{3}a, hence a = 4r / \sqrt{3}.
  • Number of atoms per unit cell for BCC, Z = 2.
  • Volume of unit cell V_c = a^3 = (4r / \sqrt{3})^3 = 64r^3 / (3\sqrt{3}).
  • Total volume of spheres in unit cell V_s = 2 \times (4/3)\pi r^3 = (8/3)\pi r^3.
  • APF = V_s / V_c = [(8/3)\pi r^3] / [64r^3 / (3\sqrt{3})] = \sqrt{3}\pi / 8 \approx 0.68.

Answer: 0.68 (or 68%)

Assumptions and limitations

  • Crystals are assumed to be infinitely periodic, ignoring finite size effects and surface boundaries.
  • Thermal vibrations of atoms are initially neglected in static geometrical lattice models (harmonic approximation used later).
  • Real crystals contain point defects, dislocations, and impurities which break exact periodicity.

Common misconceptions

Common error: A crystal lattice and crystal structure are identical physical entities.

Correct: A crystal lattice is purely a mathematical array of points, whereas crystal structure is lattice plus basis.

The lattice provides only the periodicity; the basis determines the actual atoms placed at each lattice point.

Common error: Miller indices (hkl) represent a single unique lattice point.

Correct: Miller indices represent a family of parallel crystallographic planes throughout the crystal.

Planes with the same ratio of intercepts define parallel planes having identical interplanar spacing.

Quick revision

  • Crystal Structure = Lattice + Basis
  • There are 7 crystal systems and 14 Bravais lattices.
  • Bragg's Law: 2d \sin\theta = n\lambda
  • Cubic interplanar spacing: d_{hkl} = a / \sqrt{h^2 + k^2 + l^2}
  • APF values: SC = 0.52, BCC = 0.68, FCC = 0.74

Must remember

  • A crystal structure is formed by the combination of a space lattice and a basis: $\text{Crystal Structure} = \text{Lattice} + \text{Basis}$.
  • There are 7 crystal systems and 14 Bravais lattices in three-dimensional space.
  • The reciprocal lattice vector $\mathbf{a}^*$ is defined orthogonally to direct lattice vectors such that $\mathbf{a}_i \cdot \mathbf{a}_j^* = 2\pi \delta_{ij}$.
  • Bragg's Law of X-ray diffraction is given by $2d \sin\theta = n\lambda$.
  • For a cubic crystal system, the interplanar spacing is $d_{hkl} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2 + l^2}}$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the correct mathematical relation defining a crystal structure?

Explanation: A crystal structure is physically formed by attaching a motif or basis of atoms to every point of a space lattice: $\text{Crystal Structure} = \text{Lattice} + \text{Basis}$.

2. How many crystal systems and Bravais lattices exist in three-dimensional space respectively?

Explanation: There are 7 distinct crystal systems (cubic, tetragonal, orthorhombic, monoclinic, triclinic, hexagonal, rhombohedral) which expand into 14 Bravais lattices in three dimensions.

3. What does a primitive unit cell contain?

Explanation: A primitive unit cell contains only one lattice point and possesses the minimum possible volume for that crystal system.

4. What is the formula for the interplanar spacing $d_{hkl}$ in a cubic crystal system with lattice parameter $a$?

Explanation: For a cubic system, the interplanar spacing formula is $d_{hkl} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2 + l^2}}$ derived from orthogonal axis geometry.

5. Which of the following represents Bragg's Law of X-ray diffraction?

Explanation: Bragg's Law is given by $2d \sin\$\theta$ = n\lambda$, representing constructive interference from parallel atomic planes separated by distance $d$.

6. What is the volume $V^*$ of a reciprocal unit cell in terms of the direct real-space unit cell volume $V$?

Explanation: The volume of a reciprocal unit cell is given by $V^* = \frac{(2\$\pi$)^3}{V}$, demonstrating an inverse relationship with direct space volume.

7. What are Miller indices $(hkl)$ used to designate?

Explanation: Miller indices $(hkl)$ designate a family or set of parallel crystallographic planes throughout the crystal lattice obtained from axis intercepts.

8. Which translation vector defines the periodicity of a general 3D space lattice?

Explanation: Translational symmetry of a 3D lattice is defined by $\mathbf{R} = n_1 \mathbf{a} + n_2 \mathbf{b} + n_3 \mathbf{c}$, where $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}$ are primitive vectors and $n_i$ are integers.

9. What is the SI unit of a reciprocal lattice vector $\mathbf{a}^*$?

Explanation: Since reciprocal space corresponds to wave vector space ($k$-space), the reciprocal lattice vectors have units of inverse length, i.e., $\text{m}^{-1}$.

10. Which of the following crystal systems has all three axes unequal and all three interaxial angles different from $90^{\circ}$?

Explanation: The triclinic system has the lowest symmetry where $a \neq b \neq c$ and $\$\alpha$ \neq \$\beta$ \neq \$\gamma$ \neq 90^{\circ}$.

11. What does the density $\rho$ of a crystal depend upon in terms of unit cell parameters ($Z$ is effective atoms, $M$ is molar mass, $N_A$ is Avogadro number, $$V_{c}$$ is volume)?

Explanation: The macroscopic density of a crystalline substance is given by $\rho = \frac{ZM}{N_A $V_{c}$}$.

12. What is the condition for Brillouin zone boundaries in terms of wave vector $\mathbf{k}$ and reciprocal lattice vector $\mathbf{G}$?

Explanation: The nearly free electron model zone boundary condition is $\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{G} = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{G}^2$, which represents the Bragg reflection planes in reciprocal space.

13. Why is a reciprocal lattice useful in solid state physics?

Explanation: The reciprocal lattice maps complex periodic spatial functions into Fourier components, making diffraction conditions and electronic band structures much easier to solve.

15. What is the fundamental difference between a crystal lattice and a crystal structure?

Explanation: A crystal lattice is purely a mathematical periodicity of points, whereas a crystal structure is formed by adding a basis of atoms to each point.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. If the lattice parameter of a cubic crystal is $a$, the interplanar spacing for the $(111)$ planes is:

Explanation: Using the cubic interplanar spacing formula $d_{hkl} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2 + l^2}}$, for $(111)$, $h=1, k=1, l=1$, so $d_{111} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{1^2 + 1^2 + 1^2}} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{3}}$.

2. The reciprocal lattice vector $\mathbf{a}^*$ corresponding to direct primitive vectors $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}$ is given by:

Explanation: By definition, primitive reciprocal lattice vectors are expressed as $\mathbf{a}^* = \frac{2\$\pi$ (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c})}{\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c})}$, ensuring orthogonality to direct lattice planes.

3. In X-ray diffraction, if Bragg angle $\$\theta$ = 30^{\circ}$ and wavelength $\lambda = 1.54 \text{ Å}$ for first-order diffraction ($n=1$), the interplanar spacing $d$ is:

Explanation: From Bragg's Law $2d \sin\$\theta$ = n\lambda$, we get $2d \sin(30^{\circ}) = 1 \times 1.54 \implies 2d (0.5) = 1.54 \implies d = 1.54 \text{ Å}$.

4. What is the number of Bravais lattices in the cubic crystal system?

Explanation: The cubic crystal system has 3 Bravais lattices: Simple Cubic (SC), Body-Centered Cubic (BCC), and Face-Centered Cubic (FCC).

5. A crystallographic plane makes intercepts of $2a, 3a, 6a$ along the three crystal axes. Its Miller indices are:

Explanation: Fractional intercepts: $(2, 3, 6)$. Reciprocals: $(\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{6})$. Multiplying by 6 to clear fractions gives $(3, 2, 1)$.

6. The volume of the reciprocal unit cell for a direct lattice of volume $V$ is proportional to:

Explanation: The reciprocal unit cell volume is $V^* = \frac{(2\$\pi$)^3}{V}$, which is inversely proportional to the direct cell volume $V$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following crystal systems exhibits the maximum number of Bravais lattices?

Explanation: The orthorhombic system has 4 Bravais lattices (Simple, Base-centered, Body-centered, Face-centered), which is the maximum among all crystal systems.

2. If a crystal lattice vector is $\mathbf{R}$ and reciprocal lattice vector is $\mathbf{G}$, what is the value of $\exp(i \mathbf{G} \cdot \mathbf{R})$?

Explanation: By definition of the reciprocal lattice, $\mathbf{G} \cdot \mathbf{R} = 2\$\pi$ m$ where $m$ is an integer, making $\exp(i 2\$\pi$ m) = 1$.

3. For a cubic lattice, what is the ratio of interplanar spacings $d_{100} : d_{110} : d_{111}$?

Explanation: The interplanar spacing is $d_{hkl} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{h^2+k^2+l^2}}$. Thus $d_{100} : d_{110} : d_{111} = \frac{a}{1} : \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}} : \frac{a}{\sqrt{3}} = 1 : \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} : \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$.

4. Which crystal system is characterized by $a = b = c$ and $\$\alpha$ = \$\beta$ = \$\gamma$ \neq 90^{\circ}$?

Explanation: The rhombohedral system has equal axial lengths ($a=b=c$) and equal interaxial angles that are not equal to $90^{\circ}$.

5. In terms of Miller indices, what does a plane parallel to an axis have as its intercept?

Explanation: A plane parallel to an axis never intersects it, meaning its intercept is at infinity ($\infty$), resulting in a Miller index of 0 ($1/\infty = 0$).

6. What is the coordination number of a simple cubic (SC) lattice?

Explanation: In a simple cubic lattice, each atom has 6 nearest neighbors, so its coordination number is 6.

7. The reciprocal lattice of a Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) lattice in real space is:

Explanation: The reciprocal lattice of a BCC lattice in real space is an FCC lattice, and vice versa.

8. What is the atomic packing factor (APF) for a Simple Cubic (SC) unit cell?

Explanation: The APF for a simple cubic structure is $\frac{\$\pi$}{6} \approx 0.52$ (or $52\%$) assuming hard spheres.

9. Which of the following laws states that the angle between corresponding crystal faces of all crystals of the same substance is constant?

Explanation: Steno's Law of Constancy of Interfacial Angles states that the angles between corresponding faces of crystals of the same chemical substance are constant.

10. If the translation vector components are integers $n_1, n_2, n_3$ and primitive vectors are $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}$, the displacement vector representing lattice periodicity is:

Explanation: The translation vector $\mathbf{R} = n_1 \mathbf{a} + n_2 \mathbf{b} + n_3 \mathbf{c}$ connects any two lattice points that possess identical local environments in the space lattice.
