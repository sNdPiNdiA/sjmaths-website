Energy Gap in Solids

The energy gap is a forbidden energy range in crystalline solids where no electron quantum states can exist. Its magnitude determines whether a material acts as an insulator, semiconductor, or conductor.

UP TGT ScienceUP PGT PhysicsStudy Notes + Practice

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of energy gap, distinction between conductors, semiconductors, and insulators based on energy bands, effect of temperature on electrical conductivity, and basic valence and conduction band concepts.

PGT extension

Band theory of solids using Bloch theorem and Kronig-Penney model, direct and indirect band gaps, temperature dependence of Fermi level, and effective mass of electrons.

Prerequisites

  • Bohr's theory of hydrogen atom
  • Pauli's exclusion principle
  • Wave-particle duality and de Broglie wavelength

Origin of Energy Bands and Gap

  • In isolated atoms, electrons occupy discrete energy levels governed by quantum numbers.
  • When atoms are brought together to form a crystal lattice, interatomic interactions split these discrete levels into closely spaced continuous bands.
  • The forbidden region between the valence band and the conduction band is known as the energy gap (Eg).

Classification of Materials

  • Insulators possess a very large energy gap (Eg > 3 eV), preventing thermal excitation of electrons from the valence to the conduction band.
  • Semiconductors have a moderate energy gap (Eg &approx; 0.1 eV to 3 eV), allowing limited carrier excitation at room temperature.
  • Conductors (metals) have either an overlapping valence and conduction band or a partially filled band with zero energy gap.

Direct and Indirect Band Gaps

  • In a direct band gap semiconductor, the minimum of the conduction band and the maximum of the valence band occur at the same crystal momentum (k = 0).
  • In an indirect band gap semiconductor, the conduction band minimum and valence band maximum occur at different crystal momenta.
  • Direct band gap materials (e.g., GaAs) efficiently emit photons, making them suitable for optoelectronic devices.

Temperature Dependence of Energy Gap

  • The energy gap of semiconductors decreases with an increase in temperature due to thermal expansion of the lattice and increased atomic vibrations.
  • Empirically expressed as Eg(T) = Eg(0) - \frac{\alpha T^2}{T + \beta}, where \alpha and \beta are material constants.
  • This reduction allows higher intrinsic carrier concentration at elevated temperatures.

Derivations

Intrinsic Carrier Concentration and Energy Gap

Conditions: Non-degenerate intrinsic semiconductor with parabolic bands

  • Express the electron concentration in the conduction band as $n = N_{c} exp(-(E_{c} - E_{f})/kT)$.
  • Express the hole concentration in the valence band as $p = N_{v} exp(-(E_{f} - E_{v})/kT)$.
  • Use the mass-action law for intrinsic semiconductors where $n = p = n_{i}$, leading to ni2 = NcNv exp(-Eg/kT).
  • Take the natural logarithm to relate the band gap to the temperature variation of the intrinsic carrier concentration.

Result: $n_i = \sqrt{N_c N_v} \exp\left(-\frac{E_g}{2kT}\right)$

Band Gap Determination from Conductivity Measurements

Conditions: Intrinsic semiconductor operating in the thermal excitation region

  • Write electrical conductivity as \sigma \approx e(\mu_n n + \mu_p p) \approx e \mu n_i.
  • Substitute the temperature dependence of intrinsic carrier concentration: $\sigma = \sigma_0 \exp\left(-\frac{E_g}{2kT}\right)$.
  • Take natural logarithms on both sides: \ln $\sigma = \ln \sigma_0 - \frac{E_g}{2kT}$.
  • Plot \ln \sigma versus 1/T to obtain a straight line whose slope yields the energy gap.

Result: $E_g = -2k \times \text{slope}$

Solved numericals

TGT core

The energy gap of germanium is approximately 0.72 eV. Calculate the maximum wavelength of a photon that can excite an electron from the valence band to the conduction band in germanium.

  • Given $E_g = 0.72 eV = 0.72 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J} = 1.152 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}$.
  • Use the relation $\lambda = \frac{hc}{E_g}$.
  • Substitute constants: $h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}$, $c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$.
  • $\lambda = \frac{(6.63 \times 10^{-34})(3 \times 10^8)}{1.152 \times 10^{-19}}$.
  • $\lambda = \frac{1.989 \times 10^{-25}}{1.152 \times 10^{-19}} = 1.726 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m} = 1726 \text{ nm}$.

Answer: 1726 nm (or 1.726 \mu m)

PGT extension

The electrical conductivity of an intrinsic semiconductor doubles when its temperature is raised from 300 K to 330 K. Assuming the band gap remains constant over this temperature range, determine the energy gap E_g.

  • Use the conductivity relation: $\sigma = C \exp\left(-\frac{E_g}{2kT}\right)$.
  • At $T_1 = 300 K$, \sigm$a_1 = C \exp\left(-\frac{E_g}{2kT_1}\right); at T_2 = 330 K$, \sigm$a_2 = C \exp\left(-\frac{E_g}{2kT_2}\right)$.
  • Given \sigma_2 / \sigm$a_1 = 2$, take the natural log: \ln(2) = \frac{E_g}{2k} \left(\frac{1}{T_1} - \frac{1}{T_2}\right).
  • Substitute values: $k = 8.617 \times 10^{-5} \text{ eV/K}$, $T_1 = 300$, $T_2 = 330$.
  • \ln(2) = $0.6931 = \frac{E_g}{2 \times 8.617 \times 10^{-5}} \left(\frac{330 - 300}{300 \times 330}\right) = \frac{E_g}{1.7234 \times 10^{-4}} \left(\frac{30}{99000}\right)$.
  • $E_g = \frac{0.6931 \times 1.7234 \times 10^{-4} \times 99000}{30} = 0.394 \text{ eV}$.

Answer: 0.394 eV

PGT extension

Find the threshold frequency of light for photoelectric-like interband absorption in a semiconductor with a band gap of 1.2 eV.

  • Given $E_g = 1.2 eV = 1.2 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J} = 1.92 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}$.
  • Use formula \n$u_g = \frac{E_g}{h}$.
  • Substitute Planck's constant $h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}$.
  • \n$u_g = \frac{1.92 \times 10^{-19}}{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}$.
  • \n$u_g = 2.896 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz}$.

Answer: 2.896 \times 10^{14} Hz

Assumptions and limitations

  • The Kronig-Penney model assumes an idealized 1D periodic rectangular potential, which is a simplification of actual 3D atomic potentials.
  • Effective mass approximation assumes electrons remain close to the band extrema where the E-k dispersion relation can be approximated as parabolic.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Electrons flow freely inside the forbidden energy gap.

Correct: No electronic energy states exist within the energy gap, so electrons cannot occupy or travel through energy levels in this range.

Quantum mechanical solutions to the periodic potential yield zero probability density for states inside the forbidden band.

Common error: The energy gap increases with an increase in temperature.

Correct: The energy gap decreases as temperature increases.

Thermal expansion and increased lattice vibrations broaden the interatomic spacing fluctuations, reducing the crystal potential amplitude and narrowing the band gap.

Quick revision

  • Energy gap is the forbidden energy zone separating valence and conduction bands.
  • Insulators have Eg > 3 eV, semiconductors have 0.1 eV < Eg < 3 eV, and conductors have Eg = 0.
  • Intrinsic carrier concentration depends exponentially on -Eg/(2kT).
  • Semiconductor band gap decreases monotonically with increasing temperature.

Must remember

  • The forbidden region between the valence band and the conduction band is known as the energy gap ($E_g$).
  • Insulators have $E_g > 3\text{ eV}$, semiconductors have $E_g \approx 0.1\text{ eV to } 3\text{ eV}$, and conductors have zero energy gap.
  • In a direct band gap semiconductor, the conduction band minimum and valence band maximum occur at the same crystal momentum ($k = 0$).
  • The energy gap of semiconductors decreases with an increase in temperature due to lattice expansion and increased atomic vibrations.
  • The effective mass of an electron in a crystal lattice is given by $m^* = \hbar^2 \left( \frac{d^2E}{dk^2} \right)^{-1}$.

Glossary

Energy Gap ($E_g$)The forbidden energy region separating the valence band and the conduction band in solids.
Direct Band GapA band structure where the conduction band minimum and valence band maximum align at the same crystal momentum $k=0$.
Indirect Band GapA band structure where the conduction band minimum and valence band maximum occur at different crystal momenta.
Effective Mass ($m^*$)The apparent mass of an electron when it moves through a periodic potential of a crystal lattice.
Intrinsic Carrier Concentration ($n_i$)The number of electrons in the conduction band (or holes in the valence band) in an intrinsic semiconductor.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the primary definition of the energy gap ($E_g$) in solids?

Explanation: The forbidden region between the valence band and the conduction band is known as the energy gap ($E_g$), where no electronic energy states exist.

2. Which of the following conditions characterizes an insulator based on band theory?

Explanation: Insulators possess a very large energy gap ($E_g > 3\text{ eV}$), preventing thermal excitation of electrons from the valence to the conduction band.

3. In a direct band gap semiconductor:

Explanation: In a direct band gap semiconductor, the minimum of the conduction band and the maximum of phase-space valence band occur at the same crystal momentum ($k = 0$).

4. How does the energy gap of a semiconductor change with an increase in temperature?

Explanation: The energy gap of semiconductors decreases with an increase in temperature due to the thermal expansion of the lattice and increased atomic vibrations.

5. What is the relationship between the threshold wavelength ($\lambda_g$) and the energy gap ($E_g$)?

Explanation: The energy gap relation with wavelength is given by $E_g = \frac{hc}{\lambda_g}$, where $h$ is Planck's constant and $c$ is the speed of light.

6. The effective mass of an electron in a crystal lattice is expressed as:

Explanation: The effective mass of an electron in a lattice is defined through the dispersion relation as $m^* = \hbar^2 \left( \frac{d^2E}{dk^2} \right)^{-1}$.

7. Which model explains the origin of energy bands and forbidden gaps using a 1D periodic square well potential?

Explanation: The Kronig-Penney Model uses a 1D periodic square well potential to derive the allowed and forbidden energy bands in crystal lattices.

8. Direct band gap materials like GaAs are commonly used for:

Explanation: Direct band gap materials efficiently emit photons because radiative recombination does not require a phonon momentum change, making them ideal for optoelectronic devices.

9. What is the approximate thermal energy value $kT$ at room temperature ($300\text{ K}$)?

Explanation: At room temperature ($300\text{ K}$), the Boltzmann constant scaled energy expression is $kT \approx 0.026\text{ eV}$.

10. In an intrinsic semiconductor, the electrical conductivity dependence on temperature follows:

Explanation: The intrinsic conduction region follows the temperature dependence given by $\sigma = \sigma_0 \exp\left(-\frac{E_g}{2kT}\right)$.

11. Why can electrons not occupy energy levels inside the forbidden energy gap?

Explanation: Quantum mechanical solutions to the periodic potential yield zero probability density for states inside the forbidden band, hence no electronic states exist there.

12. What is the threshold frequency ($\nu_g$) required for interband optical absorption in a direct band gap semiconductor?

Explanation: The minimum frequency for interband transition is given by the threshold frequency formula $\nu_g = \frac{E_g}{h}$.

13. The intrinsic carrier concentration squared ($n_i^2$) depends on temperature and energy gap through which expression?

Explanation: For a non-degenerate semiconductor, the intrinsic carrier concentration is expressed as $n_i^2 = N_c N_v \exp\left(-\frac{E_g}{kT}\right)$.

14. What happens to the Fermi level of an intrinsic semiconductor if the effective masses of electrons and holes are equal?

Explanation: The Fermi level for an intrinsic semiconductor is given by $E_f = \frac{E_c + E_v}{2} + \frac{kT}{2} \ln\left(\frac{N_v}{N_c}\right)$. When effective masses are equal, $N_v = N_c$, placing $E_f$ right in the middle.

15. What is the typical range of the energy gap for semiconductors?

Explanation: Semiconductors have a moderate energy gap ranging approximately from $0.1\text{ eV}$ to $3\text{ eV}$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The energy gap of silicon is approximately $1.1\text{ eV}$. What is the maximum wavelength of a photon that can create an electron-hole pair in silicon? ($hc \approx 1240\text{ eV}\cdot\text{nm}$)

Explanation: Using $\lambda_g = \frac{hc}{E_g} = \frac{1240\text{ eV}\cdot\text{nm}}{1.1\text{ eV}} \approx 1127\text{ nm}$.

2. In the Kronig-Penney model, the permitted energy bands are obtained when the parameter evaluation satisfies which condition?

Explanation: Since the right-hand side of the Kronig-Penney equation is $\cos(ka)$ (which ranges between $-1$ and $1$), real solutions for $k$ exist only when the left-hand side lies between $-1$ and $1$.

3. If the temperature of an intrinsic semiconductor is increased, how does its electrical conductivity behave and why?

Explanation: Higher temperatures provide thermal energy to excite electrons from the valence to the conduction band, increasing carrier concentration and thus conductivity according to $\sigma = \sigma_0 \exp\left(-\frac{E_g}{2kT}\right)$.

4. Why are direct band gap semiconductors preferred over indirect band gap semiconductors in optoelectronic emitter applications?

Explanation: In direct band gap semiconductors, electron-hole recombination directly emits a photon because initial and final states share the same momentum $k=0$, avoiding phonon-assisted losses.

5. Which of the following statements regarding the effective mass ($m^*$) of an electron in a solid is correct?

Explanation: Near the top of the valence band, the curvature $\frac{d^2E}{dk^2}$ is negative, which results in a negative effective mass for holes/electrons.

6. A semiconductor has an energy gap $E_g = 1.2\text{ eV}$. Assuming room temperature $kT = 0.026\text{ eV}$, the exponential factor governing intrinsic carrier concentration $\exp\left(-\frac{E_g}{2kT}\right)$ is approximately proportional to:

Explanation: Evaluating $\frac{E_g}{2kT} = \frac{1.2}{2 \times 0.026} = \frac{1.2}{0.052} \approx 23.08$, so the factor is approximately $e^{-23}$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. When isolated atoms form a crystal lattice, what happens to the discrete energy levels?

Explanation: Interatomic interactions in a crystal lattice split discrete atomic energy levels into closely spaced continuous bands.

2. What distinguishes conductors from semiconductors in terms of band structure?

Explanation: Conductors have either an overlapping valence and conduction band or a partially filled band with zero energy gap.

3. In an indirect band gap semiconductor, what auxiliary particle is typically involved in optical transitions to conserve momentum?

Explanation: Because the conduction band minimum and valence band maximum have different crystal momenta, a phonon must participate to conserve momentum during absorption or emission.

4. The empirical relation for temperature dependence of the energy gap is $E_g(T) = E_g(0) - \frac{\alpha T^2}{T + \beta}$. What does this formula indicate about $E_g$ at higher temperatures?

Explanation: The negative subtracted term $\frac{\alpha T^2}{T + \beta}$ shows that the energy gap decreases as temperature $T$ increases.

5. If the effective mass $m^*$ of an electron in a specific band region has a very large positive value, what does it imply about the $E-k$ curve curvature?

Explanation: Since $m^* = \hbar^2 \left( \frac{d^2E}{dk^2} \right)^{-1}$, a very large $m^*$ implies a very small second derivative $\frac{d^2E}{dk^2}$ (small positive curvature).

6. What is the unit of the effective mass $m^*$ of an electron in a solid?

Explanation: Effective mass has the dimensions of mass, so its SI unit is kilogram (kg).

7. Which material among the following typically exhibits an indirect band gap?

Explanation: Silicon is a prominent example of an indirect band gap semiconductor, whereas GaAs is a direct band gap semiconductor.

8. What is the consequence of having a large energy gap ($E_g > 3\text{ eV}$) in insulators?

Explanation: A large $E_g$ prevents thermal excitation of electrons from the valence to the conduction band, meaning very few charge carriers are available for conduction.

9. In the Kronig-Penney model, what does the parameter $P'$ represent?

Explanation: In the Kronig-Penney model equation, $P'$ represents the scattering strength parameter of the periodic potential barriers.

10. Which misconception about the energy gap is explicitly debunked by quantum mechanics?

Explanation: The misconception that electrons flow freely inside the forbidden energy gap is false because no electronic energy states exist in that region.
