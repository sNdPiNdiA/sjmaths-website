Variation of Fermi Level with Temperature in Semiconductors and Metals

The Fermi level represents the energy state at which the probability of occupation by an electron is exactly one-half under thermal equilibrium. Its position shifts with temperature depending on whether the material is an intrinsic or extrinsic semiconductor or a metal.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Definition of Fermi energy, temperature dependence in intrinsic semiconductors (centering near the middle of the forbidden energy gap), and qualitative behavior in metals.

PGT extension

Mathematical derivation of the temperature coefficient of the Fermi level in extrinsic semiconductors, impurity freeze-out effects, and exact carrier concentration equations.

Prerequisites

  • Fermi-Dirac Distribution Function
  • Density of States in Solids
  • Band Theory of Solids

Definition and Physical Significance of Fermi Level

  • The Fermi energy EF is the electrochemical potential of electrons at thermodynamic equilibrium.
  • At absolute zero (T = 0 K), EF defines the boundary between filled and empty electronic states.
  • The probability of occupation f(E) at E = EF is strictly equal to 1/2 for all temperatures T > 0 K.

Fermi Level in Intrinsic Semiconductors

  • In an intrinsic semiconductor, the effective masses of electrons (mn*) and holes (mp*) are generally unequal.
  • The intrinsic Fermi level Ei lies very close to the middle of the forbidden energy gap.
  • If mn* equals mp*, the Fermi level remains exactly at the mid-gap and is independent of temperature.

Fermi Level in Extrinsic (n-type and p-type) Semiconductors

  • In an n-type semiconductor at low temperatures, EF lies between the donor level and the conduction band minimum due to ionization of donors.
  • As temperature increases into the intrinsic range, intrinsic carrier concentration exceeds impurity concentration, causing EF to move toward the mid-gap.
  • In p-type semiconductors, EF shifts upward toward the mid-gap from the acceptor level with increasing temperature.

Fermi Level in Metals

  • In metals, the Fermi energy EF(0) at T = 0 K is high, typically on the order of several electron-volts.
  • The temperature variation of the Fermi level in metals is proportional to (kT/EF)2, which is extremely small at ordinary temperatures.
  • For all practical purposes, the Fermi level in a metal is treated as constant with respect to temperature.

Derivations

Temperature Dependence of Fermi Level in Intrinsic Semiconductors

Conditions: Pure semiconductor with unequal effective masses for electrons and holes

  • Equate the electron concentration n and hole concentration p: n = Nc exp(-(Ec - EF)/kT) and p = Nv exp(-(EF - Ev)/kT).
  • Substitute effective mass expressions for density of states: Nc = 2(2πmn*kT/h2)3/2 and Nv = 2(2πmp*kT/h2)3/2.
  • Take the natural logarithm of both sides of the equation n = p to solve for EF relative to the mid-gap energy Ei = (Ec + Ev)/2.
  • Simplify the resulting equation to obtain the temperature-dependent shift of the Fermi level.

Result: E_F = \frac{E_c + E_v}{2} + \frac{3}{4} kT \ln\left(\frac{m_p^*}{m_n^*}\right)

Fermi-Dirac Distribution Function at E = E_F

Conditions: Thermodynamic equilibrium at temperature T > 0 K

  • Write the standard Fermi-Dirac distribution expression: f(E) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp((E - E_F)/kT)}.
  • Substitute E = EF into the exponent: f(E_F) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp((E_F - E_F)/kT)}.
  • Evaluate the exponential term: exp(0) = 1.
  • Compute the final fraction: f(E_F) = 1 / (1 + 1) = 1/2.

Result: f(E_F) = \frac{1}{2}

Solved numericals

TGT core

At what energy level is the probability of finding an electron equal to 0.5 in a semiconductor at 300 K?

  • Recall the definition of the Fermi-Dirac distribution function: f(E) = 1 / (exp((E - E_F)/kT) + 1).
  • Set f(E) = 0.5.
  • Solve 0.5 = 1 / (exp((E - E_F)/kT) + 1), which leads to exp((E - E_F)/kT) = 1.
  • Take the natural logarithm: (E - E_F)/kT = ln(1) = 0, giving E = E_F.

Answer: Fermi level (E_F)

PGT extension

Calculate the position of the intrinsic Fermi level relative to the center of the band gap in silicon at 300 K, given that the effective mass of electrons is 1.08 m_0 and the effective mass of holes is 0.56 m_0.

  • Use the formula: E_F - (E_c + E_v)/2 = (3/4)kT ln(m_p^* / m_n^*).
  • Substitute the given values: m_p^* = 0.56 m_0 and m_n^* = 1.08 m_0.
  • Calculate the mass ratio: 0.56 / 1.08 = 0.5185.
  • Compute ln(0.5185) = -0.6567.
  • Calculate kT at 300 K: (1.38 x 10^-23 J/K) x 300 K = 4.14 x 10^-21 J = 0.02585 eV.
  • Multiply terms: (3/4) x (0.02585 eV) x (-0.6567) = -0.0127 eV.

Answer: -0.0127 eV (below the mid-gap)

PGT extension

If the Fermi energy of a metal at 0 K is 5.0 eV, estimate the shift in the Fermi level at a temperature of 1000 K using the Sommerfeld expansion.

  • Use the formula: E_F(T) = E_F(0) [1 - (\pi^2/12)(kT / E_F(0))^2].
  • Calculate kT in eV at 1000 K: k = 8.617 x 10^-5 eV/K, so kT = 8.617 x 10^-5 x 1000 = 0.08617 eV.
  • Compute the ratio kT / E_F(0) = 0.08617 / 5.0 = 0.017234.
  • Square the ratio: (0.017234)^2 = 0.000297.
  • Multiply by (π^2 / 12) ≈ 0.8224: 0.8224 x 0.000297 = 0.000244.
  • Calculate the fractional change: 5.0 x 0.000244 = 0.00122 eV.

Answer: Shift is approximately -0.00122 eV

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes non-degenerate semiconductor statistics where Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics can approximate Fermi-Dirac statistics when applicable.
  • Effective masses are assumed to be isotropic and independent of temperature over moderate temperature ranges.
  • Band gap energy E_g is treated as nearly constant or having a linear temperature dependence (Varshni relation approximation).

Common misconceptions

Common error: The Fermi level is the energy level that contains the maximum number of electrons.

Correct: The Fermi level is the energy state where the probability of electron occupation is exactly 50% (1/2).

The density of states multiplied by the Fermi distribution determines actual electron occupancy, not the Fermi level itself.

Common error: In an intrinsic semiconductor, the Fermi level changes drastically with temperature.

Correct: In an intrinsic semiconductor, the Fermi level remains very close to the center of the forbidden gap, shifting only slightly if effective masses differ.

The logarithmic dependence on effective mass ratio keeps the intrinsic Fermi level anchored near mid-gap.

Common error: At absolute zero temperature, all energy states in a metal are vacant.

Correct: At T = 0 K, all states below the Fermi level are completely filled, and all states above it are empty.

Fermions obey Pauli's exclusion principle and fill lowest available quantum states up to E_F at zero temperature.

Quick revision

  • The Fermi-Dirac occupation probability at the Fermi energy is always 1/2 for T > 0 K.
  • Fermi-Dirac Distribution Function: f(E) = \frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{E - E_F}{kT}\right) + 1}
  • Intrinsic Fermi Level Position: E_i = \frac{E_c + E_v}{2} + \frac{3}{4}kT \ln\left(\frac{m_p^*}{m_n^*}\right)

Must remember

  • The Fermi energy $E_F$ is the electrochemical potential of electrons at thermodynamic equilibrium.
  • The probability of occupation $f(E)$ at $E = E_F$ is strictly equal to $1/2$ for all temperatures $T > 0\text{ K}$.
  • In an intrinsic semiconductor, if $m_n^* = m_p^*$, the Fermi level remains exactly at the mid-gap and is independent of temperature.
  • In metals, the temperature variation of the Fermi level is proportional to $(kT / E_F)^2$, which is extremely small at ordinary temperatures.
  • The intrinsic carrier concentration $n_i$ depends exponentially on $-\frac{E_g}{2kT}$.

Glossary

Fermi Energy ($E_F$)The energy level up to which available electron states are filled at absolute zero temperature.
Fermi-Dirac Distribution ($f(E)$)The probability that an energy state $E$ is occupied by an electron at thermal equilibrium.
Intrinsic SemiconductorA pure semiconductor material without any significant dopant impurity atoms.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the probability of occupation of an energy state equal to the Fermi energy ($E = E_F$) at $T > 0\text{ K}$?

Explanation: According to the Fermi-Dirac distribution function, $f(E) = \frac{1}{\exp(\frac{E - E_F}{kT}) + 1}$. When $E = E_F$, the exponent is $0$, giving $f(E_F) = \frac{1}{1 + 1} = 1/2 = 0.5$.

2. Where does the intrinsic Fermi level $E_i$ lie in an intrinsic semiconductor when the effective masses of electrons and holes are equal ($m_n^* = m_p^*$)?

Explanation: When $m_n^* = m_p^*$, the logarithmic term in the intrinsic Fermi level equation vanishes, leaving $E_i = \frac{E_c + E_v}{2}$, which is precisely the middle of the forbidden gap and independent of temperature.

3. How does the Fermi level behave in an n-type semiconductor as the temperature increases from low temperatures into the intrinsic range?

Explanation: At low temperatures, $E_F$ lies between the donor level and the conduction band minimum. As temperature increases and intrinsic carrier concentration exceeds impurity concentration, $E_F$ moves toward the mid-gap.

4. What is the order of magnitude of the Fermi energy $E_F(0)$ in typical metals at $T = 0\text{ K}$?

Explanation: In metals, the Fermi energy $E_F(0)$ is high, typically on the order of several electron-volts (e.g., $\approx 5\text{ eV}$ to $10\text{ eV}$).

5. Which of the following expressions represents the Fermi-Dirac distribution function $f(E)$?

Explanation: The Fermi-Dirac distribution function for fermions is given by $f(E) = \frac{1}{\exp(\frac{E - E_F}{kT}) + 1}$.

6. What is the physical significance of the Fermi energy $E_F$ at absolute zero ($T = 0\text{ K}$)?

Explanation: At absolute zero ($T = 0\text{ K}$), $E_F$ defines the boundary between filled and empty electronic states, with all states below $E_F$ filled and all states above $E_F$ empty.

7. According to the Sommerfeld expansion, the temperature variation of the Fermi level in metals is proportional to which of the following terms?

Explanation: The temperature variation of the Fermi level in metals is proportional to $(\frac{kT}{E_F(0)})^2$, making the shift extremely small at ordinary temperatures.

8. In a p-type semiconductor, what happens to the Fermi level as the temperature increases from low values?

Explanation: In p-type semiconductors, the Fermi level starts near the acceptor level at low temperatures and shifts upward toward the mid-gap with increasing temperature as intrinsic carriers begin to dominate.

9. Which equation relates electron concentration $n$, hole concentration $p$, and intrinsic carrier concentration $n_i$?

Explanation: The mass action law for semiconductors states that $n \cdot p = n_i^2$ under thermal equilibrium.

10. What is the intrinsic carrier concentration $n_i$ proportional to in terms of the band gap energy $E_g$?

Explanation: The intrinsic carrier concentration formula contains the exponential term $\exp(-\frac{E_g}{2kT})$, showing its strong temperature dependence governed by half the band gap.

11. What is the relationship between the Boltzmann constant $k$, the universal gas constant $R$, and the Avogadro number $N_A$?

Explanation: The Boltzmann constant is defined as $k = \frac{R}{N_A}$, representing the gas constant per molecule.

12. Why is the temperature variation of the Fermi level in metals considered practically negligible for most applications?

Explanation: Since $E_F(0)$ is of the order of several electron-volts and $kT$ at room temperature is about $0.026\text{ eV}$, the ratio $\frac{kT}{E_F(0)}$ is very small, making its square negligible.

13. What does the effective density of states in the conduction band ($N_c$) depend upon?

Explanation: The effective density of states $N_c$ depends on temperature and the effective mass of electrons, specifically proportional to $T^{3/2} (m_n^*)^{3/2}$.

14. In non-degenerate semiconductors, the electron concentration in the conduction band is given by $n = N_c \exp(-\frac{E_c - E_F}{kT})$. What does this equation assume?

Explanation: Non-degenerate statistics apply when the Fermi level is located in the forbidden gap and separated from the band edges by several $kT$, allowing the Boltzmann approximation to be valid.

15. Which of the following statements about the Fermi level is correct?

Explanation: In semiconductors, the Fermi level typically lies inside the forbidden energy gap, where no electron quantum states are allowed to exist.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. For an intrinsic semiconductor with effective masses $m_n^*$ and $m_p^*$, the intrinsic Fermi level $E_i$ is expressed relative to the mid-gap by which term?

Explanation: The exact position of the intrinsic Fermi level is given by $E_i = \frac{E_c + E_v}{2} + \frac{3}{4}kT \ln\left(\frac{m_p^*}{m_n^*}\right)$.

2. The Fermi-Dirac distribution function gives the probability $f(E)$ of occupation of an energy state $E$. At temperatures much higher than absolute zero, what is the value of $f(E)$ when $E \ll E_F$?

Explanation: When $E \ll E_F$, the term $\frac{E - E_F}{kT}$ is a large negative number, so $\exp(\frac{E - E_F}{kT}) \approx 0$, making $f(E) \approx \frac{1}{0 + 1} = 1$.

3. In an n-type semiconductor at very low temperatures, the Fermi level lies:

Explanation: At low temperatures in an n-type semiconductor, donor atoms are ionized, and the Fermi level is positioned between the donor level and the conduction band minimum.

4. How does the Fermi level in a metal vary with temperature according to the Sommerfeld expansion at low temperatures?

Explanation: The Sommerfeld expansion gives $E_F(T) \approx E_F(0) [1 - \frac{\$\pi$^2}{12}(\frac{kT}{E_F(0)})^2]$, showing a quadratic decrease with temperature.

5. In a non-degenerate semiconductor, if the temperature increases, what happens to the intrinsic carrier concentration $n_i$?

Explanation: The intrinsic carrier concentration $n_i$ depends on temperature through $T^{3/2}$ and $\exp(-\frac{E_g}{2kT})$, resulting in a rapid exponential increase with temperature.

6. Which of the following best explains why the Fermi level in an intrinsic semiconductor remains close to the middle of the forbidden gap?

Explanation: The intrinsic Fermi level is anchored near the mid-gap because the effective density of states in the conduction and valence bands are comparable, leading to symmetric carrier distribution requirements.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What is the electrochemical potential of electrons at thermodynamic equilibrium called?

Explanation: The Fermi energy $E_F$ is defined as the electrochemical potential of electrons at thermodynamic equilibrium.

2. At $T = 0\text{ K}$, what is the probability $f(E)$ for an energy state where $E > E_F$?

Explanation: At absolute zero, all states above $E_F$ are completely empty, meaning the occupation probability $f(E) = 0$.

3. In an intrinsic semiconductor, if $m_n^* > m_p^*$, how does the intrinsic Fermi level $E_i$ shift with respect to the mid-gap as temperature increases?

Explanation: From $E_i = \frac{E_c + E_v}{2} + \frac{3}{4}kT \ln(\frac{m_p^*}{m_n*})$, if $m_n^* > m_p^*$, the logarithmic term is negative, causing $E_i$ to shift downward toward the valence band.

4. Which formula correctly gives the hole concentration in the valence band for a non-degenerate semiconductor?

Explanation: The hole concentration in the valence band is given by $p = N_v \exp(-\frac{E_F - E_v}{kT})$.

5. What is the physical meaning of the term $N_c$ in semiconductor physics?

Explanation: $N_c$ represents the effective density of states in the conduction band.

6. If the Fermi level in a semiconductor is located exactly at the intrinsic Fermi level $E_i$, the semiconductor is:

Explanation: When $E_F = E_i$, the material is intrinsically doped (or intrinsic), having equal concentrations of electrons and holes.

7. What is the temperature dependence of the Fermi level in metals at ordinary temperatures?

Explanation: The Fermi level shift in metals is proportional to $(\frac{kT}{E_F})^2$ as derived from the Sommerfeld expansion.

8. In an extrinsic p-type semiconductor at very low temperatures, where is the Fermi level located?

Explanation: At low temperatures in a p-type semiconductor, acceptors are ionized, placing the Fermi level between the acceptor level and the valence band maximum.

9. What happens to the product $n \cdot p$ when a semiconductor is heated from $300\text{ K}$ to $400\text{ K}$?

Explanation: The mass action law states $n \cdot p = n_i^2$. Since $n_i$ increases exponentially with temperature, $n \cdot p$ increases significantly.

10. Which of the following assumptions is fundamental to deriving the carrier concentration equations in non-degenerate semiconductors?

Explanation: When energy states are well above $E_F$ (specifically $E - E_F \gg kT$), the Fermi-Dirac distribution simplifies to the classical Boltzmann distribution.
