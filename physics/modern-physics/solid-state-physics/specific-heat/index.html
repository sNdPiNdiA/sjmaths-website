Specific Heat of Solids: Classical and Quantum Theories

The specific heat of solids refers to the amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of a unit mass of a solid by one degree Kelvin. Classical thermodynamics fails at low temperatures, necessitating quantum models developed by Einstein and Debye.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Dulong-Petit law, classical theory of specific heat based on equipartition theorem, vibrational degrees of freedom, and basic temperature dependence.

PGT extension

Einstein's quantum theory of specific heat, Debye's continuum theory, low-temperature behavior (Debye T&sup3; law), and electronic contribution in metals.

Prerequisites

  • Equipartition theorem of energy
  • Harmonic oscillator in quantum mechanics
  • Density of states and Bose-Einstein statistics

Classical Theory and Dulong-Petit Law

  • Each atom in a monoatomic solid is treated as a 3D harmonic oscillator with 3 degrees of freedom for kinetic energy and 3 for potential energy.
  • According to the equipartition theorem, the average energy per degree of freedom is (1/2)kBT.
  • The molar specific heat at constant volume is given by Cv = 3R ≈ 25 J/mol·K, which is independent of temperature.

Failure of Classical Theory

  • At low temperatures, experimental specific heat drops sharply and approaches zero as T → 0 K, violating the constant 3R prediction of the Dulong-Petit law.
  • Classical physics assumes continuous energy states, whereas atomic vibrations are actually quantized.
  • The degrees of freedom 'freeze out' at temperatures lower than the characteristic temperature of the solid.

Einstein's Quantum Theory

  • Albert Einstein assumed all N atoms in a solid vibrate independently with the same single characteristic angular frequency ωE.
  • Using Planck's quantum hypothesis, the average energy of a 1D quantum oscillator is given by ⟨E⟩ = (ℏω)/(exp(ℏω/kBT) - 1).
  • Einstein's model successfully explains the decrease of Cv at low temperatures, though it drops exponentially instead of as T³.

Debye's Theory of Specific Heat

  • Peter Debye treated the solid as an elastic continuum capable of supporting standing acoustic waves rather than independent oscillators.
  • Introduced a maximum cutoff frequency called the Debye frequency (ωD) corresponding to 3N degrees of freedom.
  • At low temperatures, Debye's theory yields the famous Cv ∝ T³ law, which matches experimental observations exceptionally well.

Derivations

Einstein Specific Heat Formula Derivation

Conditions: N atoms vibrating independently in 3 dimensions with identical frequency &omega;<sub>E</sub>

  • Write the average energy of a 1D quantum harmonic oscillator: ⟨E⟩ = rac{hbar omega_E}{e^{ rac{hbar omega_E}{k_B T}} - 1}
  • Multiply by 3N to account for three dimensions and N atoms: U = 3N rac{hbar omega_E}{e^{ rac{hbar omega_E}{k_B T}} - 1}
  • Differentiate internal energy U with respect to temperature T to find C_v: C_v = left( rac{partial U}{partial T} ight)_V
  • Substitute characteristic Einstein temperature heta_E = rac{hbar omega_E}{k_B} to obtain the final expression.

Result: C_v = 3R left( rac{ heta_E}{T} ight)^2 rac{e^{ rac{ heta_E}{T}}}{(e^{ rac{ heta_E}{T}} - 1)^2}

Debye Low-Temperature T-Cubed Law Derivation

Conditions: Temperature T much smaller than Debye temperature heta_D (T ll heta_D)

  • Express internal energy using Debye density of states g(omega) = rac{9N omega^2}{omega_D^3}: U = int_0^{omega_D} g(omega) rac{hbar omega}{e^{ rac{hbar omega}{k_B T}} - 1} domega
  • Substitute x = rac{hbar omega}{k_B T} and extend the upper limit to infinity for low temperatures: U = 9Nk_B T left( rac{T}{ heta_D} ight)^3 int_0^infty rac{x^3}{e^x - 1} dx
  • Evaluate the standard integral int_0^infty rac{x^3}{e^x - 1} dx = rac{pi^4}{15}
  • Differentiate U with respect to T to find the specific heat C_v.

Result: C_v = rac{12 pi^4}{5} N k_B left( rac{T}{ heta_D} ight)^3

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the molar specific heat of a solid monoatomic substance according to the classical Dulong-Petit law. Take R = 8.314 J/mol&middot;K.

  • Recall Dulong-Petit formula: C_v = 3R
  • Substitute R = 8.314 J/mol·K
  • Calculate C_v = 3 × 8.314 = 24.942 J/mol·K

Answer: 24.94 J/mol·K

PGT extension

For a certain solid, the Debye temperature &theta;<sub>D</sub> is 300 K. Calculate the molar specific heat at T = 10 K. (Take R = 8.314 J/mol&middot;K, &pi;&sup4; &approx; 97.4)

  • Check condition: T = 10 K is much less than θD = 300 K, so use Debye T-cube law.
  • Formula: C_v = rac{12 pi^4}{5} R left( rac{T}{ heta_D} ight)^3
  • Substitute values: C_v = rac{12 imes 97.4}{5} imes 8.314 imes left( rac{10}{300} ight)^3
  • Compute ratio: (10/300)³ = 1/27000
  • C_v = rac{1168.8}{5} imes 8.314 imes 0.00003704 = 0.072 J/mol·K

Answer: 0.072 J/mol·K

PGT extension

A metal at low temperature has an electronic specific heat coefficient &gamma; = 9.5 mJ/mol&middot;K&sup2; and Debye coefficient A = 0.75 mJ/mol&middot;K<sup>4</sup>. Find the molar specific heat at T = 2 K.

  • Use the combined low-temperature specific heat relation for metals: C_v = gamma T + A T^3
  • Substitute γ = 9.5 mJ/mol·K², A = 0.75 mJ/mol·K4, and T = 2 K
  • Electronic term: γ T = 9.5 imes 2 = 19 mJ/mol·K
  • Lattice term: A T^3 = 0.75 imes (2)^3 = 0.75 imes 8 = 6 mJ/mol·K
  • Total C_v = 19 + 6 = 25 mJ/mol·K

Answer: 25 mJ/mol·K

Assumptions and limitations

  • Einstein model assumes all atoms vibrate with a single discrete frequency, which ignores phonon dispersion.
  • Debye model treats the solid as an elastic continuum, which fails at high frequencies near the Brillouin zone boundary.
  • Both models neglect anharmonic interactions between atoms, which become significant at high temperatures and cause thermal expansion.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The Dulong-Petit law is valid for all temperatures as long as the solid is crystalline.

Correct: Dulong-Petit law is only valid at high temperatures (T &gt; &theta;<sub>D</sub>).

Quantum effects freeze out vibrational degrees of freedom at low temperatures, causing C<sub>v</sub> to drop.

Common error: Einstein's theory predicts a T&sup3; dependence at very low temperatures.

Correct: Einstein's theory predicts an exponential decrease of specific heat at low temperatures.

Assuming a single oscillator frequency creates an energy gap, whereas Debye's continuum of frequencies yields the T&sup3; law.

Quick revision

  • Classical Dulong-Petit specific heat is constant at 3R.
  • Einstein model uses a single frequency ωE and gives exponential drop at low T.
  • Debye model uses acoustic continuum approximation and gives Cv ∝ T³ at low T.
  • Metals include an electronic term (γT) alongside the lattice term (AT³).

Must remember

  • According to the classical Dulong-Petit law, the molar heat capacity of a monoatomic solid is $C_v = 3R \approx 25\text{ J/mol}\cdot\text{K}$, which is independent of temperature.
  • The classical equipartition theorem assigns an average energy of $\frac{1}{2}k_B T$ to each degree of freedom, with 3 degrees of freedom for kinetic energy and 3 for potential energy per atom.
  • At low temperatures, experimental specific heat drops sharply and approaches zero as $T \to 0\text{ K}$, violating the constant $3R$ prediction.
  • Einstein's quantum theory assumes all $N$ atoms vibrate independently at a single characteristic frequency $\omega_E$, leading to an exponential drop at low temperatures.
  • Debye's theory treats the solid as an elastic continuum with a maximum cutoff frequency (Debye frequency $\omega_D$), yielding the famous low-temperature $C_v \propto T^3$ law.
  • The total specific heat of metals at low temperatures includes both an electronic contribution and a lattice contribution: $C_v = \gamma T + A T^3$.

Glossary

Dulong-Petit LawA classical law stating that the molar heat capacity of solid elements is constant at high temperatures, equal to $3R$.
Einstein TemperatureA characteristic temperature $\theta_E = \frac{\hbar \omega_E}{k_B}$ representing the quantum vibration frequency of atoms in Einstein's model.
Debye TemperatureA characteristic temperature $\theta_D = \frac{\hbar \omega_D}{k_B}$ corresponding to the maximum cutoff frequency of acoustic waves in a solid.
Equipartition TheoremA classical statistical mechanics theorem stating that each degree of freedom has an average thermal energy of $\frac{1}{2}k_B T$.
Degrees of FreedomIndependent ways in which a dynamical system can store energy; a 3D harmonic oscillator has 6 degrees of freedom (3 kinetic, 3 potential).

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the molar specific heat at constant volume predicted by the classical Dulong-Petit law for a monoatomic solid?

Explanation: According to the Dulong-Petit law, each atom has 6 degrees of freedom. By the equipartition theorem, the molar specific heat is $$C_{v}$ = 3R \approx 25\text{ J/mol}\cdot\text{K}$.

2. Why does the classical Dulong-Petit law fail at very low temperatures?

Explanation: At low temperatures, thermal energy $k_B T$ becomes smaller than the quantum energy spacing, causing the vibrational modes to 'freeze out' and specific heat to drop to zero.

3. In Einstein's quantum theory of specific heat, how are the atoms in a solid treated?

Explanation: Einstein assumed that all $N$ atoms in a solid vibrate independently around their equilibrium positions with an identical angular frequency $\$\omega_{E}$$.

4. What is the low-temperature behavior of specific heat predicted by Debye's theory?

Explanation: Debye's continuum theory correctly predicts that at very low temperatures ($T \ll \$\theta_{D}$$), the specific heat varies as $$C_{v}$ \propto T^3$.

5. What does the Einstein characteristic temperature $\$\theta_{E}$$ equal?

Explanation: The Einstein characteristic temperature is defined as $\$\theta_{E}$ = \frac{\hbar \$\omega_{E}$}{k_B}$.

6. Which of the following describes the electronic contribution to the specific heat of metals at very low temperatures?

Explanation: In metallic solids at low temperatures, the free electrons contribute a term linear in temperature, giving $$C_{v}$ = \$\gamma$ T + AT^3$.

7. What is the Planck average energy $\langle E \rangle$ of a 1D quantum harmonic oscillator?

Explanation: Using Planck's quantum hypothesis and Bose-Einstein statistics for oscillator quanta, the average energy is $\langle E \rangle = \frac{\hbar \$\omega$}{e^{\hbar \$\omega$ / k_B T} - 1}$.

8. How does Einstein's theory of specific heat behave at extremely low temperatures compared to experiment?

Explanation: Because Einstein's model assumes a single oscillator frequency, it creates an energy gap leading to an exponential drop in $$C_{v}$$ as $T \to 0\text{ K}$, unlike the experimental $T^3$ behavior.

9. What physical concept did Peter Debye introduce to improve upon Einstein's theory?

Explanation: Debye treated the solid as a continuous elastic medium capable of supporting acoustic vibrational modes up to a maximum cutoff frequency $\$\omega_{D}$$.

10. Under what condition is the Dulong-Petit law considered valid?

Explanation: The Dulong-Petit law is a high-temperature limit valid when the temperature is greater than the Debye or Einstein temperature ($T > \$\theta_{D}$$).

11. What are the units of the universal gas constant $R$ and molar specific heat $$C_{v}$$?

Explanation: Molar heat capacity $$C_{v}$$ and the universal gas constant $R$ are expressed in units of Joules per mole per Kelvin (J/mol$\cdot$K).

12. What does the parameter $\$\omega_{D}$$ represent in Debye's theory?

Explanation: $\$\omega_{D}$$ is the Debye maximum cutoff frequency, chosen so that the total number of allowed acoustic wave modes equals $3N$ for $N$ atoms.

13. According to the classical equipartition theorem, what is the average kinetic energy associated with one mole of a monoatomic solid?

Explanation: Each atom has 3 degrees of freedom for kinetic energy, giving $\frac{3}{2}k_B T$ per atom, which multiplies by Avogadro's number to give $\frac{3}{2}RT$ per mole.

14. Which theory successfully accounts for the $T^3$ dependence of specific heat at low temperatures?

Explanation: Debye's continuum theory accounts for a spectrum of acoustic wave frequencies, successfully yielding the $$C_{v}$ \propto T^3$ law at low temperatures.

15. What is the expression for the Debye cutoff frequency $\$\omega_{D}$$ in terms of sound velocity $v_s$ and volume $V$?

Explanation: The Debye cutoff frequency is given by $\$\omega_{D}$ = v_s \left( \frac{6\$\pi$^2 N}{V} \right)^{1/3}$ derived from acoustic wave density of states.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. At very low temperatures, the specific heat of solid insulators varies proportionally with:

Explanation: According to Debye's theory, the specific heat of non-metals (insulators) at low temperatures is proportional to $T^3$.

2. The Dulong-Petit law fails at low temperatures because:

Explanation: Quantum quantization dictates that when $k_B T \ll \hbar \$\omega$$, oscillators cannot be thermally excited, causing degrees of freedom to freeze out.

3. In Einstein's model of specific heat, the specific heat at high temperature ($T \gg \$\theta_{E}$$) approaches:

Explanation: At high temperatures, Einstein's specific heat formula reduces to $3R$, matching the classical Dulong-Petit law.

4. In metals at low temperatures, the total specific heat is expressed as $$C_{v}$ = \$\gamma$ T + A T^3$. What does the term $\$\gamma$ T$ represent?

Explanation: The linear term $\$\gamma$ T$ represents the contribution of conduction electrons, while $A T^3$ represents the lattice contribution from Debye theory.

5. What is the dimensional formula for the Debye temperature $\$\theta_{D}$$?

Explanation: Since $\$\theta_{D}$$ is a characteristic temperature defined by $\$\theta_{D}$ = \frac{\hbar \$\omega_{D}$}{k_B}$, its dimension is simply temperature ($[K]$).

6. Which of the following assumptions distinguishes Debye's theory from Einstein's theory?

Explanation: Einstein assumed a single frequency for all atoms, whereas Debye assumed a continuum of acoustic modes spanning from zero up to $\$\omega_{D}$$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. According to classical statistical mechanics, what is the total energy of one mole of a monoatomic solid at absolute temperature $T$?

Explanation: Each atom has 3 degrees of freedom for kinetic energy and 3 for potential energy, totaling 6 degrees of freedom. Energy per mole is $6 \times (1/2)RT = 3RT$.

2. What is the high-temperature limit of Einstein's specific heat formula when $T \to \infty$?

Explanation: Taking the limit of Einstein's specific heat equation as $T \to \infty$ (or $\$\theta_{E}$/T \to 0$), $$C_{v}$$ approaches $3R$.

3. In the Debye model, the number of allowed modes in the frequency range $\$\omega$$ to $\$\omega$ + d\$\omega$$ for a 3D isotropic solid is proportional to:

Explanation: The density of states for acoustic phonons in a 3D elastic continuum is proportional to $\$\omega$^2$, giving $g(\$\omega$)d\$\omega$ \propto \$\omega$^2 d\$\omega$$.

4. If the Debye temperature of a solid is $300\text{ K}$, at approximately what temperature range does the $T^3$ law hold remarkably well?

Explanation: The Debye $T^3$ law is valid in the low-temperature regime where $T \ll \$\theta_{D}$$ (typically $T < \$\theta_{D}$ / 10$).

5. What is the main reason Einstein's specific heat curve drops off too rapidly (exponentially) at very low temperatures?

Explanation: Assuming a single frequency $\$\omega_{E}$$ creates an energy gap $\hbar \$\omega_{E}$$, making it exponentially difficult to excite vibrations at low $T$.

6. For a solid with $N$ atoms, what is the total number of vibrational degrees of freedom considered in Debye's theory?

Explanation: For $N$ atoms in 3 dimensions, there are $3N$ independent vibrational degrees of freedom.

7. Which parameter determines the scale of temperature below which quantum effects become prominent in Einstein's theory?

Explanation: The Einstein temperature $\$\theta_{E}$ = \frac{\hbar \$\omega_{E}$}{k_B}$ serves as the characteristic temperature threshold.

8. In the low-temperature specific heat of metals, the electronic heat capacity varies with temperature as:

Explanation: The electronic specific heat in metals is directly proportional to temperature ($C_{el} = \$\gamma$ T$) due to Fermi-Dirac statistics.

9. What happens to the specific heat capacity $$C_{v}$$ of a solid according to classical physics as temperature approaches absolute zero?

Explanation: Classical physics predicts $$C_{v}$ = 3R$ regardless of how low the temperature is, which is a major failure of classical theory.

10. Which of the following theories treats thermal vibrations of a solid as phonons in an elastic medium?

Explanation: Debye's theory treats the solid as an elastic continuum, effectively introducing acoustic phonons to model specific heat.
