Kronig-Penney Model of Band Theory in Solids

The Kronig-Penney model is a one-dimensional idealized quantum mechanical model that describes the motion of an electron in a periodic potential. It provides fundamental insight into the formation of energy bands and forbidden gaps in crystalline solids.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Qualitative understanding of periodic potentials, energy bands, forbidden gaps, and the distinction between conductors, insulators, and semiconductors.

PGT extension

Detailed mathematical treatment leading to the transcendental dispersion relation, evaluation of the P parameter, effective mass concept, and Brillioun zone boundary conditions.

Prerequisites

  • Time-independent Schrödinger equation
  • Bloch's theorem in periodic lattices
  • Particle in a one-dimensional box and tunneling effect

Periodic Potential Energy

  • The potential energy V(x) experienced by an electron in a crystal lattice is periodic with lattice constant a+b, such that V(x) = V(x + a + b).
  • It is modeled as a 1D array of rectangular potential wells of width a and potential barriers of height V₀ and width b.
  • The periodic nature reflects the regular repetition of positive ionic cores in a crystalline lattice.

Bloch's Theorem and Wave Function

  • According to Bloch's theorem, the wave function psi(x) of an electron in a periodic potential is modulated by a plane wave.
  • It takes the form psi(x) = exp(i k x) u_k(x), where u_k(x) has the periodicity of the lattice u_k(x + a + b) = u_k(x).
  • The parameter k is the crystal momentum wave vector, which labels the electronic states within the first Brillouin zone.

Origin of Energy Bands and Forbidden Gaps

  • Matching the wave functions and their derivatives at the boundaries of the potential wells and barriers yields a transcendental equation.
  • Solutions to this equation exist only for specific ranges of energy, forming allowed energy bands.
  • Energy ranges where no real solutions exist form forbidden energy gaps (band gaps).

Effect of Barrier Strength Parameter (P)

  • The scattering strength of the potential barrier is represented by the dimensionless parameter $P = (m b V₀ a) / (hbar^2)$, where V₀ approaches infinity and b approaches zero.
  • When P approaches infinity, the allowed energy bands shrink to infinitely narrow discrete energy levels corresponding to isolated atoms.
  • When P approaches zero, the potential vanishes entirely, reducing the system to the free electron gas model with a continuous energy spectrum.

Derivations

Derivation of the Kronig-Penney Dispersion Relation

Conditions: 1D rectangular periodic potential with well width a and barrier width b, for region 0 < x < a (V=0) and -b < x < 0 (V=V₀).

  • Write the time-independent Schrödinger equations for the two regions: d^2psi/dx^2 + alpha^2 psi = 0 for 0 < x < a (where alph$a^2 = 2 m E / hbar^2)$, and d^2psi/dx^2 - beta^2 psi = 0 for -b < x < 0 (where bet$a^2 = 2 m (V₀ - E) / hbar^2)$.
  • Apply Bloch's theorem to relate the wave functions at boundaries x = a and $x = -b$, ensuring continuity of both psi(x) and dpsi/dx at x = 0 and x = a.
  • Set up the 4x4 determinant for the boundary condition coefficients and equate the determinant to zero for non-trivial solutions.
  • Take the limiting case where b -> 0, V₀ -> f while keeping the product V₀ b a / (2 hba$r^2) = P finite$.

Result: P \frac{\sin(\alpha a)}{\alpha a} + \cos(\alpha a) = \cos(k a)

Effective Mass of an Electron in a Periodic Lattice

Conditions: Near the inflection point of the E-k dispersion curve inside an energy band.

  • Start with the dispersion relation connecting energy E and crystal momentum k.
  • Differentiate the energy E with respect to k twice to find the curvature of the E-k band.
  • Define the effective mass m* using the relation m* = hbar^2 / (d^2E / dk^2).
  • Interpret the physical meaning: m* can be positive, negative, or infinite depending on the curvature at specific points in the Brillouin zone.

Result: $m^* = \frac{\hbar^2}{\frac{d^2E}{dk^2}}$

Solved numericals

TGT core

In the Kronig-Penney model, if the parameter P approaches infinity, what happens to the allowed energy bands?

  • Examine the Kronig-Penney dispersion relation: P(sin(alpha a)/alpha a) + cos(alpha a) = cos(ka).
  • When P -> infinity, the left side can only remain finite and bounded between -1 and 1 if sin(alpha a) -> 0.
  • This implies alpha a = n pi, which means the allowed energy values collapse to discrete, sharp energy levels corresponding to bound states in an isolated potential well.

Answer: Allowed energy bands shrink to discrete, sharp energy levels.

PGT extension

For a Kronig-Penney model with very weak potential (P -> 0), calculate the energy of an electron when k = 0.

  • Substitute P = 0 into the dispersion relation: cos(alpha a) = cos(ka).
  • For k = 0, cos(ka) = 1, which implies cos(alpha a) = 1.
  • Therefore, alpha a = 2 n pi (where n is an integer).
  • Using alph$a = \sqrt(2 m E / hbar^2)$, substitute alph$a = 2 n \pi / a$ to solve for E.
  • $E = (hbar^2 \alpha^2) / (2 m) = (hbar^2 (2 n \pi / a)^2) / (2 m)$.

Answer: $E = \frac{n^2 \pi^2 \hbar^2}{2 m a^2} (for n = 1$, 2, ...)

PGT extension

An electron moves in a 1D lattice with lattice constant a = 0.5 nm. Find the wave number k at the first Brillouin zone boundary.

  • Identify the condition for the first Brillouin zone boundary in a 1D lattice of spacing a: k = \pm \frac{\pi}{a}.
  • Substitute the given lattice constant $a = 0.5 nm = 0.5 * 10^-9 m$.
  • $k = \frac{3.1416}{0.5 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}}$.
  • $k = 6.283 \times 10^9 \text{ m}^{-1}$.

Answer: 6.28 x 10^9 m^-1

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes a one-dimensional crystal lattice, ignoring three-dimensional directional bonding and complex lattice structures.
  • Approximates the actual smooth electrostatic potential of ionic cores as rectangular wells and barriers, which is an oversimplification.
  • Ignores electron-electron interactions and electron-phonon interactions (rigid lattice approximation).

Common misconceptions

Common error: The Kronig-Penney model explains superconductivity.

Correct: The Kronig-Penney model explains normal electronic band structures and band gaps in semiconductors and insulators.

Superconductivity involves Cooper pairs and phonon-mediated interactions, which are beyond single-electron periodic potentials.

Common error: Forbidden energy gaps mean electrons cannot exist anywhere in the crystal with those energies.

Correct: Electrons cannot propagate as Bloch waves with energies in the forbidden gap, but evanescent waves can exist near boundaries.

Solutions to the Schrödinger equation yield imaginary wave numbers in the gap, leading to exponentially decaying wave functions.

Quick revision

  • The Kronig-Penney model simplifies the crystal potential to a 1D rectangular periodic array.
  • The dispersion relation is P'((sin(alpha a))/(alpha a)) + cos(alpha a) = cos(ka).
  • Energy bands form where |F(alpha a)| <= 1, and band gaps form where |F(alpha a)| > 1.
  • As P -> infinity, energy bands reduce to discrete atomic energy levels.
  • As P -> 0, the model reduces to the free electron gas with continuous parabola E-k dispersion.

Must remember

  • The Kronig-Penney model uses a 1D periodic array of rectangular potential wells of width $a$ and barriers of height $V_0$ and width $b$.
  • Bloch's theorem states that the electron wave function in a periodic potential is given by $\psi(x) = e^{i k x} u_k(x)$ where $u_k(x)$ has the periodicity of the lattice.
  • The scattering strength parameter is defined as $P = \frac{m V_0 b a}{\hbar^2}$ in the limit $V_0 \to \infty$ and $b \to \0$.
  • When $P \to \infty$, the allowed energy bands shrink to discrete energy levels corresponding to isolated atoms.
  • When $P \to \0$, the potential vanishes entirely, reducing the system to a free electron gas with a continuous energy spectrum.
  • Energy ranges where no real solutions exist for the transcendental dispersion relation form forbidden energy gaps.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the mathematical form of Bloch's theorem for an electron wave function in a 1D periodic potential?

Explanation: According to Bloch's theorem, the wave function is a plane wave modulated by a periodic function $u_k(x)$ having the periodicity of the lattice, yielding $\psi(x) = e^{i k x} u_k(x)$.

2. In the Kronig-Penney model, the potential energy $V(x)$ of an electron in a crystal lattice is modeled as a 1D array of:

Explanation: The periodic potential is modeled as a 1D array of rectangular potential wells of width $a$ and barriers of height $V_0$ and width $b$.

3. How is the scattering strength parameter $P$ defined in the Kronig-Penney model under the limit $V_0 \to \infty$ and $b \to 0$?

Explanation: The dimensionless scattering strength parameter is defined as $P = \frac{m V_0 b a}{\hbar^2}$ where $V_0 \to \infty$ and $b \to 0$ with the product $V_0 b$ remaining finite.

4. What happens to the allowed energy bands of a crystal when the parameter $P$ approaches infinity?

Explanation: When $P \to \infty$, the potential barriers become impenetrable, isolating the potential wells and causing allowed energy bands to degenerate into discrete atomic energy levels.

5. What happens to the electron energy spectrum in the Kronig-Penney model when the parameter $P$ approaches zero?

Explanation: When $P \to \0$, the potential vanishes entirely, and the model reduces to the free electron gas model with a continuous energy spectrum.

6. What is the physical origin of forbidden energy gaps in solids according to the Kronig-Penney model?

Explanation: Forbidden gaps arise because electron waves undergo Bragg reflection at the Brillouin zone boundaries, preventing propagation for certain energy ranges.

7. What does the wave number $\alpha$ represent inside the potential well ($0 < x < a$) for an electron of energy $E < V_0$?

Explanation: Inside the well where potential is zero, the wave number is given by $\alpha = \sqrt{\frac{2mE}{\hbar^2}}$.

8. What is the expression for the attenuation constant $\beta$ of an electron wave inside the potential barrier ($-b < x < 0$) for $E < V_0$?

Explanation: Within the barrier where $V = V_0 > E$, the wave number becomes imaginary, leading to an attenuation constant $\beta = \sqrt{\frac{2m(V_0 - E)}{\hbar^2}}$.

9. Which of the following conditions marks the boundaries of the Brillouin zones in a 1D lattice of spacing $a + b$?

Explanation: The Brillouin zone boundaries are located at $k = \pm \frac{n \pi}{a + b}$ where $n$ is an integer and $a+b$ is the lattice constant.

10. How is the effective mass $m^*$ of an electron defined in terms of the energy dispersion relation $E(k)$?

Explanation: The effective mass is given by the inverse curvature of the energy band: $m^* = \frac{\hbar^2}{\frac{d^2E}{dk^2}}$.

11. What does the function $u_k(x)$ in Bloch's theorem satisfy regarding the crystal lattice period $a+b$?

Explanation: The function $u_k(x)$ has the exact periodicity of the lattice, meaning $u_k(x + a + b) = u_k(x)$.

12. Which equation represents the Kronig-Penney dispersion relation in terms of scattering parameter $P'$?

Explanation: The transcendental dispersion relation is given by $P' \frac{\sin(\alpha a)}{\alpha a} + \cos(\alpha a) = \cos(k a)$.

13. What is the common misconception regarding the Kronig-Penney model and superconductivity?

Explanation: The Kronig-Penney model explains normal electronic band structures and band gaps in semiconductors and insulators, whereas superconductivity involves pairing mechanisms beyond single-electron periodic potentials.

14. What is true about forbidden energy gaps regarding the existence of electrons?

Explanation: Solutions to the Schrödinger equation yield imaginary wave numbers in the gap, leading to evanescent or exponentially decaying wave functions rather than propagating Bloch waves.

15. What is the unit of crystal momentum $p_{crystal} = \hbar k$?

Explanation: Crystal momentum $p_{crystal} = \hbar k$ has the dimensions of momentum, which is $\text{kg m s}^{-1}$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. In the Kronig-Penney model of a 1D periodic potential, the condition for allowed energy bands is that the magnitude of the function $f(\alpha a) = P' \frac{\sin(\alpha a)}{\alpha a} + \cos(\alpha a)$ must lie within what range?

Explanation: Since $\cos(k a)$ can only take values between $-1$ and $+1$, real solutions for $k$ exist only when the left side of the dispersion relation lies in the range $[-1, 1]$ (or strictly $(-1, 1)$ for non-zero angles).

2. If the potential barrier width $b$ approaches zero and the height $V_0$ approaches infinity such that the product $V_0 b$ remains constant, the parameter $P$ is given by:

Explanation: The scattering strength parameter in the Kronig-Penney delta-function potential limit is defined as $P = \frac{m V_0 b a}{\hbar^2}$.

3. According to Bloch's theorem, if an electron moves through a potential with periodicity $a+b$, the probability density $|\psi(x)|^2$ is:

Explanation: Since $\psi(x) = e^{ikx}u_k(x)$ where $u_k(x)$ is periodic with period $a+b$, the probability density $|\psi(x)|^2 = |u_k(x)|^2$ also shares the periodicity of the lattice.

4. At the boundaries of the first Brillouin zone ($k = \pm \pi / (a+b)$), the electron wave undergoes:

Explanation: At the Brillouin zone boundary, Bragg reflection occurs, leading to standing waves and the opening of a band gap.

5. What is the behavior of the effective mass $m^*$ of an electron near the bottom of an allowed energy band?

Explanation: Near the bottom of an energy band, the curvature $d^2E/dk^2 > 0$, making the effective mass $m^* = \hbar^2 / (d^2E/dk^2)$ positive and finite.

6. In the limit where the scattering parameter $P \to 0$, what does the Kronig-Penney dispersion relation reduce to?

Explanation: As $P \to 0$, the first term in the Kronig-Penney equation vanishes, leaving $\cos(\alpha a) = \cos(k a)$, which implies $\alpha = \pm k$ (free electron dispersion $E = \hbar^2 k^2 / 2m$).

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following best describes the crystal momentum $k$ in Bloch's theorem?

Explanation: Crystal momentum $k$ is a quasi-momentum that is conserved up to a reciprocal lattice vector in periodic potentials.

2. If the width of the potential barrier $b \to 0$ and height $V_0 \to \infty$, the rectangular wells turn into:

Explanation: Taking the limit where barrier width goes to zero and height goes to infinity transforms the rectangular barriers into a periodic array of Dirac delta functions.

3. Why do forbidden gaps appear in the energy spectrum of electrons in a crystal?

Explanation: Forbidden gaps arise from the constructive/destructive interference of electron wave functions scattered by the periodic lattice, causing Bragg reflection.

4. At an inflection point of an energy band ($d^2E/dk^2 = 0$), the effective mass of the electron is:

Explanation: Since $m^* = \hbar^2 / (d^2E/dk^2)$, when the second derivative approaches zero, the effective mass approaches infinity.

5. What is the periodicity of the potential energy $V(x)$ in a 1D crystal lattice with well width $a$ and barrier width $b$?

Explanation: The lattice constant or spatial period of the potential is $a + b$, such that $V(x) = V(x + a + b)$.

6. In the Kronig-Penney model, what happens to the width of allowed energy bands as the energy increases?

Explanation: As energy increases, electrons behave more like free electrons, causing the allowed energy bands to widen and forbidden gaps to narrow.

7. What is the physical meaning of the function $u_k(x)$ in Bloch's wave function?

Explanation: The function $u_k(x)$ modulates the plane wave $e^{ikx}$ to reflect the periodic environment of the lattice ions.

8. If an electron wave has an imaginary wave number inside the barrier region, what is the nature of the wave function there?

Explanation: An imaginary wave number ($\beta$) results in exponential terms $e^{\pm \beta x}$, representing evanescent or decaying waves inside the barrier.

9. Which zone corresponds to the range $-\frac{\pi}{a+b} \le k \le \frac{\pi}{a+b}$?

Explanation: The first Brillouin zone spans from $-\pi/(a+b)$ to $\pi/(a+b)$ in 1D.

10. What is the primary conclusion of the Kronig-Penney model regarding electronic states in solids?

Explanation: The model demonstrates that periodic potentials split allowed electron energies into bands separated by forbidden band gaps, explaining conductors, semiconductors, and insulators.
