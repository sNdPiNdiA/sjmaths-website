One-Dimensional Kronig-Penney Model

The one-dimensional Kronig-Penney model is a simplified quantum mechanical treatment of an electron moving in a periodic rectangular potential well. It explains the emergence of energy bands and forbidden gaps in crystalline solids.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Basic understanding of periodic potentials, qualitative energy band formation, and Bloch theorem statement.

PGT extension

Detailed mathematical derivation of the dispersion relation, evaluation of the P-function, effective mass, and classification of conductors, insulators, and semiconductors.

Prerequisites

  • Time-independent Schrödinger equation
  • Quantum tunneling and wave packet behavior
  • Bloch's theorem in periodic lattices

Periodic Potential Model

  • The potential energy V(x) satisfies the periodicity condition V(x + a) = V(x), where a is the lattice constant.
  • In the Kronig-Penney model, this potential is approximated as an infinite array of rectangular potential barriers of height V0 and width b, separated by wells of width a - b.
  • As V0 approaches infinity and b approaches zero such that their product V0ba remains finite, the potential reduces to a series of Dirac delta functions.

Bloch's Theorem

  • Bloch's theorem states that the wavefunction psi(x) of an electron in a periodic potential is modulated by a plane wave.
  • Mathematically, psi(x) = exp(ikx) uk(x), where uk(x) has the periodicity of the lattice: uk(x + a) = uk(x).
  • The parameter k is the crystal momentum wavevector, which determines the translational symmetry state of the electron.

Origin of Energy Bands and Forbidden Gaps

  • Matching the wavefunctions and their derivatives at the boundaries leads to a transcendental equation involving the energy E.
  • Since the left-hand side of the Kronig-Penney condition must lie between -1 and +1 for real solutions of k to exist, allowed energy bands are formed.
  • Values of energy for which the condition is violated correspond to forbidden energy gaps (band gaps).

Effective Mass of an Electron

  • An electron moving through a periodic lattice experiences internal forces from the ion cores, altering its response to external electric and magnetic fields.
  • The effective mass m* is defined via the curvature of the E-k dispersion relation: m* = h_bar2 / (d2E / dk2).
  • Near the Brillouin zone boundary, d2E / dk2 can become negative, resulting in a negative effective mass.

Derivations

Kronig-Penney Dispersion Relation Derivation

Conditions: One-dimensional rectangular potential barrier of width b and height V<sub>0</sub>, with well width a - b.

  • Write the time-independent Schrödinger equation for Region I (0 < x < a-b, V=0) and Region II (-b < x < 0, V=V0).
  • Assume solutions using Bloch theorem: psiI(x) = A exp(i alpha x) + B exp(-i alpha x) and psiII(x) = C exp(beta x) + D exp(-beta x), where alpha2 = 2mE/h_bar2 and beta2 = 2m(V0 - E)/h_bar2.
  • Apply continuity conditions for the wavefunctions and their first derivatives at x = 0 and x = a - b.
  • Set the determinant of the resulting homogeneous system of linear equations for coefficients A, B, C, D to zero.
  • Take the limit V0 -> infinity and b -> 0 such that (m V0 b a) / h_bar2 = P (scattering strength).

Result: (P \sin(\alpha a) / \alpha a) + \cos(\alpha a) = \cos(ka)

Number of Allowed States per Band

Conditions: A crystal lattice containing N unit cells with periodic boundary conditions.

  • Apply Born-Karman periodic boundary conditions over a crystal of length L = Na: psi(x + L) = psi(x).
  • This implies exp(ikL) = 1, leading to k = (2\pi / L) n, where n is an integer.
  • The interval of k in the first Brillouin zone is from -\pi/a to \pi/a, spanning a total range of 2\pi/a.
  • Divide the total k-space range by the spacing between allowed k states: \Delta k = 2\pi / L = 2\pi / (Na).
  • Total number of k-states = (2\pi/a) / (2\pi/Na) = N. Considering two electrons per state due to spin, each band accommodates 2N electrons.

Result: Each energy band contains exactly 2N allowed states, where N is the number of unit cells.

Solved numericals

TGT core

What is the periodicity condition of the potential energy V(x) in a one-dimensional crystal lattice with lattice parameter a?

  • Recall the definition of a periodic crystal lattice.
  • The potential experienced by an electron must reflect the periodic arrangement of positive ion cores.
  • Therefore, V(x) must be invariant under a translation by any lattice vector R = na.

Answer: V(x + a) = V(x)

PGT extension

In the limiting case where the scattering power P -> 0, what does the Kronig-Penney dispersion relation reduce to?

  • Start with the Kronig-Penney equation: (P sin(alpha a) / alpha a) + cos(alpha a) = cos(ka).
  • Set P = 0, which eliminates the first term on the left-hand side.
  • This simplifies the expression to cos(alpha a) = cos(ka).
  • Therefore, alpha = k, substituting alpha yields E = h_bar^2 k^2 / 2m.

Answer: Free electron energy relation: E = \frac{\hbar^2 k^2}{2m}

PGT extension

Calculate the width of the first allowed energy band for alpha a -> 0 when P is very large, given lattice constant a = 0.3 nm.

  • For small alpha a, the Kronig-Penney relation simplifies near the band edge.
  • Using P sin(alpha a) / alpha a + cos(alpha a) = 1 for the lower band edge when alpha a is small.
  • Approximating sin(alpha a) / alpha a \approx 1 and cos(alpha a) \approx 1 - (alpha a)^2 / 2.
  • Solving for alpha gives the lowest allowed energy value corresponding to the bottom of the band.

Answer: Energy corresponds to discrete quantized levels of a particle in a box as P -> \infty

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumption: The potential is strictly one-dimensional, ignoring transverse components of electron motion. Limitation: Real crystals are three-dimensional, requiring reciprocal lattice vectors in three dimensions.
  • Assumption: The ion core potential is represented by rectangular or delta-function barriers. Limitation: Real potentials are smooth, Coulombic, and dynamic due to lattice vibrations (phonons).

Common misconceptions

Common error: Forbidden energy gaps mean electrons cannot exist anywhere inside the crystal at those energies.

Correct: Forbidden gaps mean no traveling wave solutions with real wavevector k exist within the crystal for those specific energy values.

Wavefunctions at forbidden energies correspond to complex wavevectors, leading to exponentially decaying evanescent waves.

Common error: Bloch electrons have the same mass as free electrons.

Correct: Bloch electrons move through a periodic lattice and possess an effective mass m* that can be different from m and even negative.

The periodic interaction with the lattice modifies the kinematic response of the electron to external forces.

Quick revision

  • The Kronig-Penney model solves the 1D Schrödinger equation for a periodic rectangular well potential to explain energy bands.
  • Bloch's theorem states that wavefunctions in a periodic potential are plane waves modulated by a lattice-periodic function.
  • The consistency equation (P sin(alpha a)/alpha a) + cos(alpha a) = cos(ka) dictates allowed and forbidden energy bands.
  • Effective mass m* accounts for internal lattice forces and is determined by the curvature of the E-k dispersion curve.

Must remember

  • The potential energy in a 1D crystal satisfies the periodicity condition $V(x + a) = V(x)$, where $a$ is the lattice constant.
  • Bloch's theorem states that the wavefunction of an electron in a periodic potential is given by $\psi(x) = e^{ikx} u_k(x)$, where $u_k(x + a) = u_k(x)$.
  • The Kronig-Penney consistency equation is $\frac{P \sin(\alpha a)}{\alpha a} + \cos(\alpha a) = \cos(ka)$.
  • The scattering strength parameter is defined as $P = \frac{m a V_0 b}{\hbar^2}$ in the limit of delta-function barriers.
  • Allowed energy bands occur when the left-hand side of the Kronig-Penney condition lies strictly between $-1$ and $+1$.
  • The effective mass of an electron is defined via the curvature of the dispersion relation: $m^* = \hbar^2 \left( \frac{d^2E}{dk^2} \right)^{-1}$.
  • Near the Brillouin zone boundary given by $k = \pm \frac{n\pi}{a}$, Bragg reflection sets up standing waves and creates forbidden energy gaps.

Glossary

Lattice Constant ($a$)The periodic repeat distance of the crystal lattice along one dimension.
Crystal Momentum ($k$)A wavevector that characterizes the translational symmetry state of an electron moving in a periodic potential.
Scattering Strength Parameter ($P$)A dimensionless parameter representing the strength of the delta-function potential barrier in the Kronig-Penney model, given by $P = \frac{m a V_0 b}{\hbar^2}$.
Effective Mass ($m^*$)The apparent mass of an electron inside a crystal lattice, accounting for internal forces exerted by ion cores, defined by $m^* = \hbar^2 / (d^2E/dk^2)$.
Bloch Function ($\psi_k(x)$)A wavefunction consisting of a plane wave modulated by a periodic function having the period of the lattice.
Brillouin Zone BoundaryPoints in reciprocal space where Bragg reflection occurs, satisfying $k = \pm \frac{n\pi}{a}$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following mathematical conditions represents the periodicity of the potential energy in a one-dimensional crystal lattice with lattice parameter $a$?

Explanation: The fundamental property of a periodic lattice potential is spatial translational periodicity, represented by $V(x + a) = V(x)$.

2. According to Bloch's theorem, the wavefunction $\psi(x)$ of an electron in a periodic potential is expressed as:

Explanation: Bloch's theorem states that $\psi(x) = e^{ikx} u_k(x)$, where $u_k(x)$ has the lattice periodicity $u_k(x + a) = u_k(x)$.

3. In the Kronig-Penney model, the rectangular potential barriers of height $$V_{0}$$ and width $b$ are reduced to Dirac delta functions by taking the limit:

Explanation: The delta-function potential limit requires the barrier height $$V_{0}$ \to \infty$ and width $b \to 0$ such that their product (and hence the scattering strength $P$) remains finite.

4. What is the expression for the scattering strength parameter $P$ in the Kronig-Penney model?

Explanation: The dimensionless scattering strength parameter is defined as $P = \frac{m a $V_{0}$ b}{\hbar^2}$ in the delta-function potential limit.

5. What is the physical significance of the left-hand side of the Kronig-Penney consistency equation having values outside the range $[-1, 1]$?

Explanation: Since $\cos(ka)$ must lie between $-1$ and $+1$ for real values of $k$, any energy values for which the left-hand side of the consistency equation exceeds this range yield no real wavevector solutions, creating forbidden energy gaps.

6. The effective mass $m^*$ of an electron in a crystal lattice is defined in terms of the dispersion relation $E(k)$ as:

Explanation: The effective mass is inversely proportional to the curvature of the $E-k$ dispersion curve: $m^* = \hbar^2 / (d^2E/dk^2)$.

7. Under what condition can the effective mass $m^*$ of an electron become negative?

Explanation: Near inflection points and Brillouin zone boundaries, the dispersion relation $E(k)$ can become concave down ($d^2E/dk^2 < 0$), resulting in a negative effective mass.

8. In the region where the potential $V = 0$ between potential barriers, the wave vector $\$\alpha$$ of the electron is given by:

Explanation: From the Schrödinger equation in the potential well region ($V=0$), $\frac{\hbar^2 \$\alpha$^2}{2m} = E$, which gives $\$\alpha$ = \sqrt{\frac{2mE}{\hbar^2}}$.

9. The boundaries of the Brillouin zones in a one-dimensional crystal lattice of lattice constant $a$ occur at crystal momentum values of:

Explanation: Bragg reflection condition in the reciprocal lattice for zone boundaries is given by $k = \pm \frac{n\$\pi$}{a}$.

10. What happens to the allowed energy bands as the scattering strength parameter $P$ approaches infinity in the Kronig-Penney model?

Explanation: As $P \to \infty$, the potential barriers become impenetrable, decoupling the lattice cells and causing the allowed energy bands to degenerate into sharp, discrete atomic energy levels.

11. In Bloch's theorem, what is the property of the modulating function $u_k(x)$?

Explanation: The function $u_k(x)$ possesses the exact periodicity of the lattice, meaning $u_k(x + a) = u_k(x)$.

12. What is the physical meaning of the crystal momentum wavevector $k$?

Explanation: Crystal momentum $k$ is a quantum number associated with translation symmetry under lattice shifts, serving as a wavevector for the envelope Bloch function.

13. In the limit where the scattering parameter $P \to 0$, what does the Kronig-Penney model reduce to?

Explanation: When $P \to 0$, the potential barriers vanish, and the model reduces to the free electron gas model where $E = \frac{\hbar^2 k^2}{2m}$.

14. What causes the formation of energy bands and forbidden gaps in a crystalline solid?

Explanation: The interference of electron waves scattered by the periodic ionic potential creates standing waves at Brillouin zone boundaries, opening up forbidden energy gaps and splitting the spectrum into bands.

15. Which of the following expressions correctly gives the allowed values of crystal momentum $k$ for a finite crystal of $N$ cells with lattice constant $a$ under periodic boundary conditions?

Explanation: Applying periodic boundary conditions over a crystal of length $Na$ yields allowed crystal momentum values of $k = \frac{2\$\pi$ n}{Na}$ where $n$ is an integer.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. The Kronig-Penney model assumes a potential consisting of an infinite array of rectangular potential barriers. The transcendental equation determining the allowed energy values is:

Explanation: Matching boundary conditions in the Kronig-Penney rectangular barrier model yields the consistency relation $\frac{P \sin(\$\alpha$ a)}{\$\alpha$ a} + \cos(\$\alpha$ a) = \cos(ka)$.

2. In the context of Bloch's theorem, if $\psi_k(x)$ is the wavefunction of an electron in a periodic potential $V(x) = V(x+a)$, then $\psi_k(x+a)$ equals:

Explanation: Since $\psi_k(x) = e^{ikx} u_k(x)$, translating by $a$ gives $\psi_k(x+a) = e^{ik(x+a)} u_k(x+a) = e^{ika} e^{ikx} u_k(x) = e^{ika} \psi_k(x)$.

3. The effective mass $m^*$ of an electron is infinite at certain points in the energy band. What does an infinite effective mass signify regarding the electron's motion?

Explanation: Since $m^* = \hbar^2 (d^2E/dk^2)^{-1}$, an inflection point where $d^2E/dk^2 = 0$ results in an infinite effective mass, meaning zero acceleration under external forces.

4. What is the physical significance when the scattering parameter $P \to 0$ in the Kronig-Penney dispersion relation?

Explanation: When $P \to 0$, the potential barriers disappear, reducing the Kronig-Penney equation to $\cos(\$\alpha$ a) = \cos(ka)$, which implies $\$\alpha$ = k$ and yields the continuous free electron parabolic energy spectrum.

5. Near the Brillouin zone boundary in a one-dimensional lattice, the energy $E(k)$ as a function of wavevector $k$ exhibits:

Explanation: At the Brillouin zone boundary ($k = \pm n\$\pi$/a$), Bragg reflection creates standing waves resulting in a vanishing group velocity, meaning the slope $dE/dk = 0$.

6. Which of the following statements is correct regarding the Kronig-Penney model?

Explanation: The Kronig-Penney model is a simplified 1D quantum mechanical model that provides invaluable qualitative and semi-quantitative insight into band theory and Bloch states.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. In the Kronig-Penney model, if the parameter $P$ is very large ($P \gg 1$), the allowed values of $\$\alpha$ a$ for the lowest energy band are approximately given by roots of:

Explanation: For large $P$, the Kronig-Penney equation $\frac{P \sin(\$\alpha$ a)}{\$\alpha$ a} + \cos(\$\alpha$ a) = \cos(ka)$ requires $\frac{\sin(\$\alpha$ a)}{\$\alpha$ a}$ to be very small, leading to $\tan(\$\alpha$ a) \approx \$\alpha$ a / P$ or roots close to $\sin(\$\alpha$ a) = 0$ for isolated atomic levels.

2. What is the group velocity $v_g$ of an electron wavepacket in a crystal lattice in terms of the dispersion relation $E(k)?$

Explanation: By quantum mechanical wavepacket dynamics, the group velocity is given by $v_g = \frac{1}{\hbar} \frac{dE}{dk}$.

3. If the dispersion relation for an electron in a narrow band is given by $E(k) = E_0 - 2 \cos(ka)$, what is its effective mass at $k = 0$?

Explanation: Differentiating twice: $\frac{dE}{dk} = 2a \sin(ka)$, $\frac{d^2E}{dk^2} = 2a^2 \cos(ka)$. At $k=0$, $\frac{d^2E}{dk^2} = 2a^2$. Thus $m^* = \hbar^2 / (2a^2)$.

4. Which of the following correctly describes the periodicity of the probability density $|\psi_k(x)|^2$ of a Bloch electron?

Explanation: Since $\psi_k(x+a) = e^{ika} \psi_k(x)$, taking the square modulus eliminates the phase factor: $|\psi_k(x+a)|^2 = |e^{ika}|^2 |\psi_k(x)|^2 = |\psi_k(x)|^2$. The probability density is strictly periodic with period $a$.

5. In a one-dimensional crystal lattice, if there are $N$ primitive unit cells of length $a$, how many allowed $k$-values exist within the first Brillouin zone?

Explanation: The first Brillouin zone spans from $-\$\pi$/a$ to $\$\pi$/a$, containing exactly $N$ allowed uniform crystal momentum values spaced by $2\$\pi$ / (Na)$.

6. Why do forbidden energy gaps appear at the boundaries of the Brillouin zone ($k = \pm n\$\pi$/a$)?

Explanation: At the Brillouin zone boundaries, forward and backward travelling electron waves undergo Bragg reflection, forming standing waves (cosine and sine combinations) with different potential energies, which opens up an energy gap.

7. If the effective mass $m^*$ of an electron is negative, how does the electron respond to an applied electric field $E$?

Explanation: Since force $F = qE = m^* a$, a negative effective mass results in an acceleration vector opposite in direction to the force, manifesting as positive charge carriers (holes).

8. In the Kronig-Penney model, what happens to the width of the allowed energy bands as the energy $E$ increases?

Explanation: At higher energies, the kinetic energy of the electron vastly exceeds the potential barrier height, making the periodic potential a minor perturbation, so the allowed bands widen and look increasingly free-electron-like.

9. Which theorem forms the foundational basis for understanding electronic band structure in crystalline solids?

Explanation: Bloch's theorem is the fundamental theorem that describes the form of electron wavefunctions in any periodic potential, enabling band theory.

10. What is the value of the scattering parameter $P$ when the potential barrier height $$V_{0}$ \to 0$?

Explanation: Since $P = \frac{m a $V_{0}$ b}{\hbar^2}$, if $$V_{0}$ \to 0$, the scattering parameter $P$ becomes zero.
