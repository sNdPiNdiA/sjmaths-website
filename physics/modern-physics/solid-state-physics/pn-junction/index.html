P-N Junction and Semiconductor Diodes

A P-N junction is formed by joining p-type and n-type semiconductor materials, creating a localized depletion region devoid of mobile charge carriers. It exhibits asymmetric conductivity, allowing high current flow under forward bias and negligible current under reverse bias.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Qualitative understanding of p-type and n-type semiconductors, barrier potential, depletion region formation, forward and reverse biasing characteristics, and half-wave/full-wave diode rectification.

PGT extension

Quantitative analysis of depletion layer width, barrier potential derivation from doping concentrations, diode equation (Shockley equation), diffusion capacitance, transition capacitance, and Zener breakdown mechanisms.

Prerequisites

  • Energy band theory in solids (valence, conduction, and forbidden energy gaps)
  • Intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductors (Donor and acceptor impurity doping)
  • Fermi-Dirac distribution function and Fermi level position

Formation of P-N Junction and Depletion Region

  • When p-type and n-type semiconductors are brought into contact, a concentration gradient causes electrons to diffuse from the n-side to the p-side and holes from the p-side to the n-side.
  • Diffusion of charge carriers leaves behind uncompensated immobile ionized donors (positive) on the n-side and ionized acceptors (negative) on the p-side near the interface.
  • The resulting space-charge region is called the depletion region, which establishes an internal electric field opposing further diffusion of majority carriers.

Barrier Potential and Equilibrium

  • The accumulation of space charges sets up a potential barrier (V_0) across the junction that stops net diffusion current in thermal equilibrium.
  • Under equilibrium, the drift current due to thermally generated minority carriers exactly balances the diffusion current due to majority carriers.
  • The magnitude of the barrier potential depends on temperature and intrinsic carrier concentration as well as doping levels.

Biasing Modes and V-I Characteristics

  • Under forward bias (p-side connected to positive terminal), the external field opposes the barrier potential, narrowing the depletion region and permitting large forward current.
  • Under reverse bias (n-side connected to positive terminal), the external field aids the barrier potential, widening the depletion region and allowing only a minute reverse saturation current (I_s).
  • At a critical high reverse voltage, avalanche breakdown or Zener breakdown occurs, causing a sharp increase in reverse current.

Junction Capacitance Effects

  • Transition capacitance (C_T) or depletion layer capacitance arises from the change in stored space charge with applied reverse voltage, acting like a parallel plate capacitor.
  • Diffusion capacitance (C_D) arises from stored minority carrier charges in the neutral regions under forward bias conditions.
  • Both capacitances dictate the high-frequency response limitations of semiconductor diodes.

Derivations

Barrier Potential Equation

Conditions: Uniform doping, abrupt junction, thermal equilibrium at temperature T

  • Write the expression for hole concentration on p-side and n-side using Boltzmann approximation: $p_p = n_i exp((E_Fi - E_F)/kT)$ and $p_n = n_i exp((E_Fn - E_F)/kT)$.
  • Relate the concentration ratio across the junction to the built-in potential V_0 using $p_n = p_p exp(-qV_0 / kT)$.
  • Substitute donor concentration N_D and acceptor concentration N_A for majority carrier levels: p_p approx N_A and n_n approx N_D, keeping $n_i^2 = N_A N_D$.
  • Take the natural logarithm of both sides to isolate the potential V_0.

Result: $V_0 = \frac{kT}{q} \ln\left(\frac{N_A N_D}{n_i^2}\right)$

Depletion Layer Width Derivation

Conditions: Abrupt asymmetrical p-n junction under thermal equilibrium

  • Apply Poisson's equation in one dimension across the depletion width: d^2V / d$x^2 = - \rho / \epsilon_s$.
  • Express charge density rho as qN_D for x > 0 and -qN_A for x < 0, where x_n and x_p are the penetration depths into n and p sides.
  • Use charge neutrality condition: N_A $x_p = N_D x_n$.
  • Integrate the electric field and impose boundary conditions to find total depletion width $W = x_p + x_n$.

Result: $W = \sqrt{\frac{2 \epsilon_s V_0}{q} \left(\frac{1}{N_A} + \frac{1}{N_D}\right)}$

Solved numericals

TGT core

A silicon diode operating at room temperature has a reverse saturation current of 20 nA. Calculate the forward diode current for a forward bias voltage of 0.6 V, assuming ideality factor eta = 1 and thermal voltage VT = 26 mV.

  • Identify given values: $I_s = 20 \times 10^{-9} \text{ A}$, V = 0.6 \text{ V}, $V_T = \frac{kT}{q} = 0.026 \text{ V}$, \eta = 1.
  • Substitute values into the Shockley diode equation: $I = I_s \left( \exp\left(\frac{V}{\eta V_T}\right) - 1 \right)$.
  • Calculate exponent term: \frac{0.6}{0.026} = 23.076.
  • Compute exponential: \exp(23.076) \approx 1.043 \times 10^{10}.
  • Multiply by saturation current: $I = 20 \times 10^{-9} \times 1.043 \times 10^{10} \approx 208.6 \text{ A (or dominant term approximation)}$.

Answer: 208.6 A

PGT extension

For a germanium p-n junction at 300 K, the acceptor doping is $N_A = 10^{17} \text{ cm}^{-3}$ and donor doping is $N_D = 10^{14} \text{ cm}^{-3}$. Given the intrinsic concentration $n_i = 2.5 \times 10^{13} \text{ cm}^{-3}$ and thermal voltage $V_T = 0.026 \text{ V}$, calculate the built-in potential V_0.

  • Write formula: $V_0 = V_T \ln\left(\frac{N_A N_D}{n_i^2}\right)$.
  • Substitute given numbers: $N_A = 10^{17}$, $N_D = 10^{14}$, $n_i = 2.5 \times 10^{13}$.
  • Calculate numerator: N_A $N_D = 10^{31}$.
  • Calculate denominator: $n_i^2 = (2.5 \times 10^{13})^2 = 6.25 \times 10^{26}$.
  • Find ratio: \frac{10^{31}}{6.25 \times 10^{26}} = 1.6 \times 10^4.
  • Take natural logarithm: \ln(1.6 \times 10^4) = \ln(16000) = 9.68.
  • Multiply by thermal voltage: $V_0 = 0.026 \times 9.68 = 0.252 \text{ V}$.

Answer: 0.252 V

PGT extension

A silicon step junction has relative permittivity \epsilo$n_r = 11.8$, and total depletion width at zero bias is 0.5 \mu\text{m} with cross-sectional area 1 \text{ mm}^2. Find the transition capacitance C_T. (\epsilo$n_0 = 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \text{ F/m})$

  • Permittivity of silicon: \epsilo$n_s = \epsilon_r \epsilon_0 = 11.8 \times 8.854 \times 10^{-12} = 1.045 \times 10^{-10} \text{ F/m}$.
  • Area $A = 1 \text{ mm}^2 = 10^{-6} \text{ m}^2$.
  • Depletion width $W = 0.5 \mu\text{m} = 0.5 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}$.
  • Use formula: $C_T = \frac{\epsilon_s A}{W}$.
  • Substitute values: $C_T = \frac{1.045 \times 10^{-10} \times 10^{-6}}{0.5 \times 10^{-6}} = 2.09 \times 10^{-10} \text{ F}$.

Answer: 209 pF

Assumptions and limitations

  • The abrupt (step) junction approximation assumes an instantaneous transition from p-type to n-type doping, which is idealized compared to real diffused or ion-implanted junctions.
  • Boltzmann statistics are used instead of full Fermi-Dirac statistics, which holds true only for non-degenerate semiconductors where the Fermi level lies within the forbidden gap away from band edges.

Common misconceptions

Common error: No current flows through a p-n junction under thermal equilibrium.

Correct: Net current is zero, but individual drift and diffusion currents flow and precisely cancel each other out.

Dynamic equilibrium implies opposing microscopic processes balance out on average.

Common error: The depletion region contains no charge carriers whatsoever.

Correct: The depletion region is depleted of mobile majority carriers, but immobile ionized donor and acceptor ions are present.

Ions fixed to the crystal lattice create the space-charge region.

Common error: A reverse-biased diode has absolute zero current.

Correct: A very small reverse saturation current (due to minority carrier thermal generation) constantly flows.

Minority carriers are swept across the junction by the electric field.

Quick revision

  • Depletion layer width decreases under forward bias and increases under reverse bias.
  • Barrier Potential: $V_0 = \frac{kT}{q} \ln\left(\frac{N_A N_D}{n_i^2}\right)$
  • Shockley Diode Equation: $I = I_s \left( \exp\left(\frac{qV}{\eta kT}\right) - 1 \right)$

Must remember

  • Diffusion of majority charge carriers across a P-N junction creates a depletion region devoid of mobile carriers but containing immobile ionized donors and acceptors.
  • In thermal equilibrium, the drift current due to minority carriers exactly balances the diffusion current due to majority carriers, resulting in zero net current.
  • Under forward bias, the external voltage opposes the barrier potential $V_0$, narrowing the depletion layer and permitting large forward current.
  • Under reverse bias, the external voltage aids the barrier potential, widening the depletion layer and allowing only a minute reverse saturation current $I_s$.
  • Transition capacitance $C_T$ arises from stored space-charge in the depletion region under reverse bias, while diffusion capacitance $C_D$ arises from stored minority carriers under forward bias.
  • The Shockley diode equation is given by $I = I_s \left( \exp\left(\frac{qV}{\eta kT}\right) - 1 \right)$.
  • Total depletion layer width depends on the applied voltage as $W = \sqrt{\frac{2 \epsilon_s (V_0 - V)}{q} \left(\frac{N_A + N_D}{N_A N_D}\right)}$.

Glossary

Barrier Potential ($V_0$)The potential difference built across a P-N junction due to space-charge accumulation that stops net diffusion of majority carriers.
Depletion RegionA space-charge region near the P-N junction interface containing immobile ionized donors and acceptors, depleted of mobile majority carriers.
Transition Capacitance ($C_T$)Capacitance associated with the voltage-dependent change in space charge within the depletion region of a reverse-biased diode.
Diffusion Capacitance ($C_D$)Capacitance arising from stored minority carrier charges in neutral regions under forward-bias conditions.
Shockley Diode EquationMathematical relation describing the exponential V-I characteristic of an ideal P-N junction diode.
Dynamic Resistance ($r_d$)Small-signal AC resistance of a diode defined as the reciprocal of the slope of its V-I characteristic curve.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the primary cause of the initial movement of charge carriers across a newly formed P-N junction?

Explanation: When p-type and n-type semiconductors are brought into contact, a large concentration gradient causes electrons to diffuse from the n-side to the p-side and holes from the p-side to the n-side.

2. The depletion region of a P-N junction consists of:

Explanation: Diffusion of charge carriers leaves behind uncompensated immobile ionized donors (positive) on the n-side and ionized acceptors (negative) on the p-side near the interface, forming the depletion region.

3. In thermal equilibrium, the net current across a P-N junction is:

Explanation: Under thermal equilibrium, the drift current due to thermally generated minority carriers exactly balances the diffusion current due to majority carriers, making the net current zero.

4. How does forward biasing affect the depletion region and barrier potential?

Explanation: Under forward bias, the external field opposes the barrier potential, thereby narrowing the depletion region and lowering the potential barrier to permit large forward current.

5. What is the expression for the barrier potential $V_0$ in terms of doping concentrations $N_A$ and $N_D$?

Explanation: The barrier potential formula is $V_0 = \frac{kT}{q} \ln\left(\frac{N_A N_D}{n_i^2}\right)$, derived from carrier statistics in thermal equilibrium.

6. Transition capacitance ($C_T$) of a P-N junction is prominent under which condition?

Explanation: Transition capacitance (or depletion layer capacitance) arises from the change in stored space charge with applied reverse voltage across the reverse-biased or zero-biased junction.

7. Diffusion capacitance ($C_D$) is directly proportional to:

Explanation: Diffusion capacitance is given by $C_D = \frac{q \tau_p I}{kT}$, which shows it is directly proportional to the forward DC current $I$.

8. In the Shockley diode equation $I = I_s \left( \exp\left(\frac{qV}{\eta kT}\right) - 1 \right)$, what is the ideality factor $\eta$ for Silicon at room temperature under normal conditions?

Explanation: The ideality factor $\eta$ is typically 1 for Germanium and 2 for Silicon at low-level injection currents.

9. The total depletion layer width $W$ for an abrupt step junction varies with reverse bias voltage $V$ as:

Explanation: From the formula $W = \sqrt{\frac{2 \epsilon_s (V_0 - V)}{q} \left(\frac{N_A + N_D}{N_A N_D}\right)}$, the depletion width is proportional to the square root of the net reverse voltage $(V_0 - V)$.

10. What is the small-signal AC dynamic resistance ($r_d$) of a diode operating at forward current $I$?

Explanation: Dynamic resistance $r_d$ is the reciprocal of the derivative of the diode equation, yielding $r_d = \frac{\eta kT}{q I}$.

11. Which of the following describes the correct behavior of a reverse-biased P-N junction diode?

Explanation: Under reverse bias, minority carriers are swept across the junction by the electric field, producing a very small reverse saturation current ($I_s$).

12. Transition capacitance $C_T$ of a P-N junction with cross-sectional area $A$ and depletion width $W$ is expressed as:

Explanation: The depletion layer acts like a parallel plate capacitor, giving $C_T = \frac{\epsilon_s A}{W}$.

13. What happens to the barrier potential when temperature increases?

Explanation: As temperature increases, intrinsic carrier concentration $n_i$ increases exponentially, which decreases the barrier potential $V_0 = \frac{kT}{q} \ln\left(\frac{N_A N_D}{n_i^2}\right)$.

14. The peak electric field $E_{max}$ at the metallurgical junction of an abrupt P-N junction is given by:

Explanation: For an abrupt junction with a triangular charge distribution, the peak electric field at the junction is $E_{max} = \frac{2 V_0}{W}$.

15. What is the primary mechanism of current breakdown in a heavily doped P-N junction under a small reverse voltage?

Explanation: Zener breakdown occurs in heavily doped P-N junctions where a narrow depletion region allows quantum mechanical tunneling of electrons under high electric fields at relatively low reverse voltages.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. In a P-N junction diode, the depletion layer is caused by:

Explanation: The diffusion of electrons from n-side to p-side and holes from p-side to n-side results in recombination near the interface, leaving behind uncompensated ions that form the depletion layer.

2. The reverse saturation current in a P-N junction diode is mainly due to:

Explanation: Minority carriers thermally generated in the p-type and n-type regions are swept across the junction by the reverse bias electric field, constituting the reverse saturation current.

3. If the reverse voltage applied to a P-N junction is increased, the transition capacitance $C_T$:

Explanation: Increasing reverse voltage widens the depletion width $W$. Since $C_T = \frac{\epsilon_s A}{W}$, a larger $W$ results in a smaller capacitance $C_T$.

4. The cut-in voltage ($V_\gamma$) for a silicon diode at room temperature is approximately:

Explanation: The threshold or cut-in voltage is approximately equal to $E_g / q$, which is about $0.7\text{ V}$ for Silicon and $0.3\text{ V}$ for Germanium at room temperature.

5. Which capacitance dominates when a P-N junction diode is strongly forward-biased?

Explanation: Under forward bias, minority carrier injection dominates, making diffusion capacitance $C_D$ much larger than transition capacitance $C_T$.

6. According to the Shockley diode equation, if the forward voltage $V$ across a diode is doubled at constant temperature, the forward current $I$:

Explanation: The Shockley equation $I = I_s \left( \exp\left(\frac{qV}{\eta kT}\right) - 1 \right)$ shows an exponential dependence of current on applied voltage.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. What creates the internal electric field in a P-N junction at thermal equilibrium?

Explanation: The space-charge region formed by immobile ionized donors on the n-side and acceptors on the p-side sets up an internal electric field opposing further diffusion.

2. In a symmetrically doped P-N junction, if $N_A = N_D = N_0$, how does the total depletion width $W$ scale with the built-in potential $V_0$ (at zero applied bias)?

Explanation: Substituting $N_A = N_D = N_0$ into the depletion width formula gives $W = \sqrt{\frac{4 \epsilon_s V_0}{q N_0}}$, showing $W$ is proportional to the square root of $V_0$.

3. The dynamic resistance of a silicon diode operating at a forward current of $1\text{ mA}$ at room temperature ($\eta = 2$, $\frac{kT}{q} \approx 26\text{ mV}$) is approximately:

Explanation: Using $r_d = \frac{\eta kT}{q I} = \frac{2 \times 26\text{ mV}}{1\text{ mA}} = 52\text{ }\Omega$.

4. Which of the following statements about avalanche breakdown and Zener breakdown is correct?

Explanation: Avalanche breakdown occurs when thermally or field-accelerated carriers gain enough kinetic energy to create new electron-hole pairs via impact ionization, typically in wider depletion regions of lightly/moderately doped diodes.

5. Under forward bias, the minority carrier injection into the neutral regions leads to:

Explanation: Diffusion capacitance $C_D$ is a measure of the stored minority carrier charges in the neutral regions adjacent to the depletion layer under forward bias.

6. If the absolute temperature $T$ of a P-N junction diode increases, what happens to the reverse saturation current $I_s$?

Explanation: Reverse saturation current $I_s$ is strongly temperature-dependent because thermal generation of minority carriers increases rapidly with temperature.

7. The unit of transition capacitance $C_T$ is:

Explanation: Capacitance, whether transition or diffusion, is measured in Farads (F).

8. In an abrupt junction, if the acceptor doping $N_A$ is much greater than the donor doping $N_D$ ($N_A \gg N_D$), the depletion layer width extends primarily into:

Explanation: Charge neutrality requires $q N_A x_p = q N_D x_n$. If $N_A \gg N_D$, then $x_p \ll x_n$, meaning the depletion region extends much further into the lightly doped n-side.

9. What is the physical significance of setting $V = 0$ in the Shockley diode equation?

Explanation: When $V = 0$, $\exp(0) = 1$, so $I = I_s(1 - 1) = 0$, representing thermal equilibrium where net current is zero.

10. Which of the following factors does NOT directly affect the barrier potential $V_0$ of a P-N junction?

Explanation: Barrier potential depends on material properties ($n_i$), temperature ($T$), and doping levels ($N_A, N_D$), but is independent of the cross-sectional area $A$.
