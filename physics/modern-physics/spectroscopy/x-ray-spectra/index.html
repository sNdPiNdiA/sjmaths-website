X-ray Spectra

X-ray spectra consist of a continuous bremsstrahlung background produced by the deceleration of high-speed electrons and characteristic line spectra arising from electronic transitions between inner atomic shells. These high-energy electromagnetic radiations follow Moseley's law, connecting frequency to atomic number.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Continuous and characteristic X-rays, Coolidge tube working, Duane-Hunt law, minimum wavelength, and qualitative aspects of X-ray production.

PGT extension

Moseley's law derivation and application, fine structure of characteristic X-rays, screening constants, absorption edges, and Auger effect.

Prerequisites

  • Bohr's theory of hydrogen atom and energy levels
  • Pauli's exclusion principle and inner shell electron configurations
  • Bremsstrahlung radiation mechanics

Origin of Continuous X-ray Spectra

  • Produced when high-energy electrons strike a heavy metal target and undergo sudden deceleration (bremsstrahlung) in the Coulomb field of target nuclei.
  • The energy of the emitted photon ranges from zero up to the maximum kinetic energy of the incident electron, E = eV.
  • The short-wavelength limit (cut-off wavelength) is independent of the target material and depends solely on the accelerating potential.

Origin of Characteristic X-ray Spectra

  • Arises when an incident electron knocks out an inner-shell electron (K or L shell), creating a vacancy.
  • An electron from an outer shell jumps down to fill the vacancy, emitting a photon with energy equal to the difference between the two orbital energy levels.
  • The spectrum consists of sharp lines grouped into series named K, L, M, N based on the principal quantum number of the shell where the vacancy originated.

Moseley's Law and Atomic Number

  • H.G.J. Moseley established that the square root of the frequency of a given characteristic X-ray line is directly proportional to the atomic number Z of the target element.
  • Expressed mathematically as sqrt(nu) = a(Z - b), where a is a proportionality constant and b is the screening constant.
  • Proved that atomic number, rather than atomic weight, is the fundamental property governing the chemical and physical identity of elements.

Fine Structure and X-ray Notation

  • Characteristic lines are designated using Siegbahn notation (Ka1, Ka2, Kb1, etc.) or IUPAC notation.
  • Transitions into the K shell from L_II and L_III subshells give rise to the doublet Ka2 and Ka1, explained by spin-orbit coupling.
  • Selection rules for X-ray transitions follow quantum mechanics: delta(l) = +-1 and delta(j) = 0, +-1.

Derivations

Duane-Hunt Law for Minimum Wavelength

Conditions: Electron accelerated through potential V striking a target

  • Equate the maximum kinetic energy of the incident electron to the maximum energy of the emitted X-ray photon.
  • $K_max = e V = h \nu_{max}$
  • Substitute frequency relation \n$u_{max} = \frac{c}{\lambda_{min}} into the energy equation$.
  • e $V = \frac{h c}{\lambda_{min}}$

Result: \lambd$a_{min} = \frac{h c}{e V}$

Moseley's Law Frequency Derivation

Conditions: Transition of an electron between principal quantum numbers n_2 and n_1 in a multielectron atom

  • Use Bohr's energy expression modified for an effective nuclear charge $Z_{eff} = Z - b$.
  • $E_n = - \frac{m e^4 (Z - b)^2}{8 \epsilon_0^2 h^2 n^2}$
  • Write the energy difference for the transition from n_2 to n_1: \Delta $E = h \nu = E_{n2} - E_{n1}$.
  • $\nu = \frac{m e^4}{8 \epsilon_0^2 h^3} (Z - b)^2 \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right)$
  • Take the square root of both sides to obtain a linear relation with (Z - b).

Result: \sqrt{\nu} = \sqrt{ \frac{m e^4}{8 \epsilon_0^2 h^3} \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right) } (Z - b)

Exam use: Questions test the dependence of lambda_min on accelerating voltage and the independence of characteristic peak positions from voltage.

Moseley's Plot (sqrt(nu) vs Z)

Draw: Straight line plot of sqrt(nu) on the y-axis against atomic number Z on the x-axis for K_alpha lines, with a small positive intercept on the Z axis.

Interpret: Confirms the linear relationship predicted by Moseley's law, validating the atomic number concept over atomic mass.

Exam use: Frequently tested for identifying slopes, screening constants, or finding unknown atomic numbers from frequency data.

Coolidge Tube Experimental Setup

Draw: Evacuated glass bulb with a heated cathode filament, focusing cup, high-voltage DC supply across terminals, and a cooled heavy metal anode (tungsten) inclined at 45 degrees.

Interpret: Demonstrates the conversion of electrical energy into thermal energy and X-ray radiation, highlighting water cooling to prevent target melting.

Exam use: Conceptual questions on the role of filament current (controls intensity) and accelerating voltage (controls hardness/wavelength).

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the minimum wavelength of X-rays produced by an X-ray tube operating at an accelerating potential of 40 kV.

  • Identify given values: $V = 40 kV = 40 \times 10^3 V$, $h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34} J s$, $c = 3 \times 10^8 m/s$, $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} C$.
  • Use Duane-Hunt law: \lambd$a_{min} = \frac{h c}{e V}$.
  • Substitute values: \lambd$a_{min} = \frac{(6.63 \times 10^{-34}) (3 \times 10^8)}{(1.6 \times 10^{-19}) (40 \times 10^3)}$.
  • Calculate result: \lambd$a_{min} = \frac{1.989 \times 10^{-25}}{6.4 \times 10^{-15}} = 3.11 \times 10^{-11} m = 0.311 nm$.

Answer: 0.311 nm (or 0.311 Å)

PGT extension

Using Moseley's law sqrt(\nu) = a(Z - b), find the frequency of the K_alpha line for an element with atomic number Z = 41, given constant $a = 4.95 \times 10^7 Hz^{1/2}$ and screening constant b = 1 for the K shell.

  • Identify given parameters: Z = 41, b = 1, $a = 4.95 \times 10^7 Hz^{1/2}$.
  • Calculate effective atomic number: Z - $b = 41 - 1 = 40$.
  • Calculate square root of frequency: \sqrt{\nu} = (4.95 \times 10^7) \times $40 = 1.98 \times 10^9 Hz^{1/2}$.
  • Square both sides to find frequency: $\nu = (1.98 \times 10^9)^2 = 3.92 \times 10^{18} Hz$.

Answer: 3.92 \times 10^{18} Hz

PGT extension

In a Coolidge tube, if the accelerating potential is increased from 20 kV to 40 kV, what is the ratio of the minimum wavelength of continuous X-rays produced?

  • Recall Duane-Hunt law: \lambda_{min} is inversely proportional to accelerating voltage V, i.e., \lambd$a_{min} = \frac{hc}{eV}$.
  • Set up the ratio: \frac{\lambda_{min1}}{\lambda_{min2}} = \frac{V_2}{V_1}.
  • Substitute given voltages: $V_1 = 20 kV$ and $V_2 = 40 kV$.
  • Ratio becomes: \frac{\lambda_{min1}}{\lambda_{min2}} = \frac{40 kV}{20 kV} = \frac{2}{1}.

Answer: 2 : 1

Assumptions and limitations

  • Moseley's simple screening formula assumes single-electron transitions from the L to K shell, ignoring complex multi-electron screening interactions in higher shells.
  • The Duane-Hunt limit assumes all kinetic energy of a single incident electron is converted into a single photon in a single collision.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The characteristic X-ray wavelength depends on the accelerating voltage of the tube.

Correct: Characteristic X-ray wavelengths depend only on the target material's atomic structure.

Accelerating voltage only determines whether the inner shell electron can be knocked out; once emitted, the line wavelength is governed by atomic energy levels.

Common error: Continuous X-ray spectra are produced by electronic transitions between discrete atomic energy levels.

Correct: Continuous X-rays are produced by bremsstrahlung radiation due to deceleration of electrons.

Energy loss is continuous during deceleration in the nuclear Coulomb field, unlike discrete transitions.

Quick revision

  • Minimum wavelength lambd$a_min = hc / (eV) depends solely on the accelerating potential V$.
  • Minimum X-ray Wavelength (Duane-Hunt Law): \lambd$a_{min} = \frac{h c}{e V}$
  • Moseley's Law: \sqrt{\nu} = a(Z - b)

Must remember

  • Continuous X-rays are produced by bremsstrahlung radiation due to sudden deceleration of high-energy electrons in the Coulomb field of target nuclei.
  • The short-wavelength cut-off (Duane-Hunt law) is given by $\lambda_{min} = \frac{hc}{eV}$, which depends only on accelerating potential and is independent of target material.
  • Characteristic X-rays arise from electronic transitions filling inner-shell vacancies (K, L, M series) and have wavelengths dependent on target atomic number.
  • Moseley's Law states that $\sqrt{\nu} = a(Z - b)$, proving that atomic number $Z$ is the fundamental property governing X-ray spectra.
  • Fine structure of characteristic X-rays (like $K_{\alpha 1}$ and $K_{\alpha 2}$ doublets) is explained by spin-orbit coupling and quantum mechanical selection rules ($\Delta l = \pm 1$, $\Delta j = 0, \pm 1$).

Glossary

BremsstrahlungBraking radiation emitted when high-energy charged particles are decelerated by the electric field of atomic nuclei.
Duane-Hunt LawRelation giving the minimum wavelength of continuous X-rays corresponding to the maximum photon energy equal to the incident electron kinetic energy.
Moseley's LawEmpirical law relating the square root of characteristic X-ray frequency to the atomic number of the target element.
Screening ConstantConstant $b$ in Moseley's law accounting for the shielding of nuclear charge by inner electrons.
Siegbahn NotationTraditional naming convention for characteristic X-ray spectral lines using Greek letters like $K_{\alpha}$, $K_{\beta}$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the physical origin of continuous X-rays (Bremsstrahlung)?

Explanation: Continuous X-rays are produced when high-energy electrons undergo sudden deceleration (bremsstrahlung) in the electric field of target nuclei.

2. The short-wavelength cut-off $\lambda_{min}$ of continuous X-rays depends on:

Explanation: According to the Duane-Hunt law, $\lambda_{min} = \frac{hc}{eV}$, which depends solely on the accelerating potential $V$ and fundamental constants.

3. Characteristic X-ray spectra consist of:

Explanation: Characteristic X-ray spectra consist of sharp lines grouped into series ($K, L, M$) corresponding to discrete energy level transitions.

4. Moseley's law relates the frequency $\nu$ of characteristic X-rays to which property?

Explanation: Moseley's law is given by $\sqrt{\nu} = a(Z - b)$, establishing a direct linear relation with atomic number $Z$.

5. What is the condition for the minimum wavelength of continuous X-rays for an accelerating voltage of $V$?

Explanation: The maximum energy of an emitted photon equals the maximum kinetic energy of the incident electron, $h\nu_{max} = \frac{hc}{\lambda_{min}} = eV$.

6. Which transition gives rise to the $K_{\alpha}$ characteristic X-ray line?

Explanation: The $K_{\alpha}$ line is emitted when an electron drops from the $L$ shell to fill a vacancy in the $K$ shell.

7. The fine structure splitting of characteristic X-ray lines (such as $K_{\alpha 1}$ and $K_{\alpha 2}$) is primarily due to:

Explanation: Transitions from $L_{II}$ and $L_{III}$ subshells into the $K$ shell produce doublets due to spin-orbit coupling.

8. According to quantum mechanical selection rules, X-ray electronic transitions must satisfy:

Explanation: The selection rules for X-ray dipole transitions are $\Delta l = \pm 1$ and $\Delta j = 0, \pm 1$.

9. If the accelerating potential in an X-ray tube is doubled, the minimum wavelength $\lambda_{min}$ will:

Explanation: Since $\lambda_{min} = \frac{hc}{eV}$, wavelength is inversely proportional to potential $V$. Doubling $V$ halves $\lambda_{min}$.

10. Which of the following parameters does NOT affect the wavelength of characteristic X-rays?

Explanation: Accelerating voltage only determines if inner-shell electrons can be knocked out; once emitted, characteristic wavelength depends strictly on atomic energy levels.

11. The efficiency of X-ray production in a Coolidge tube is approximately proportional to:

Explanation: The efficiency formula is given by $\eta = k Z V$, showing direct proportionality with target atomic number $Z$ and voltage $V$.

12. The linear absorption coefficient $\mu$ describes how an X-ray beam intensity decreases according to:

Explanation: The absorption coefficient law is expressed exponentially as $I = I_0 e^{-\mu x}$ for a monochromatic beam through a homogeneous absorber.

13. What happens to the continuous X-ray spectrum when the tube current is increased while keeping the voltage constant?

Explanation: Increasing the tube current increases the number of electrons striking the target per second, increasing the intensity without changing $\lambda_{min}$.

14. Bragg's law for X-ray diffraction in crystals is given by:

Explanation: Bragg's law is $2d \sin\theta = n\lambda$, relating interplanar spacing $d$, Bragg angle $\theta$, and wavelength $\lambda$.

15. Moseley's discovery proved that the fundamental property governing an element's chemical and physical identity is its:

Explanation: Moseley's law proved that atomic number $Z$, rather than atomic weight, is the fundamental property of elements.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. If the accelerating voltage of an X-ray tube is $40\text{ kV}$, the minimum wavelength of the emitted continuous X-rays is approximately:

Explanation: Using $\lambda_{min} = \frac{hc}{eV} = \frac{12400\text{ eV}\cdot\text{\AA}}{40000\text{ V}} = 0.31\text{ \AA} = 0.031\text{ nm}$.

2. The frequency of the $K_{\alpha}$ line for an element of atomic number $Z = 41$ is such that $\sqrt{\nu}$ is proportional to $(41 - b)$. If the screening constant for the $K$ shell is $b = 1$, find the relative ratio of frequencies for $Z = 41$ and $Z = 21$ (with same $b$).

Explanation: By Moseley's law, $\sqrt{\nu} \propto (Z - b)$. Thus $\frac{\sqrt{\nu_1}}{\sqrt{\nu_2}} = \frac{41 - 1}{21 - 1} = \frac{40}{20} = 2$. Squaring both sides gives $\frac{\nu_1}{\nu_2} = 4$.

3. In an X-ray tube, electrons are accelerated through $50\text{ kV}$ and strike a target of $Z = 80$. What is the approximate power input if the tube current is $10\text{ mA}$?

Explanation: Power input $P = V \times I = 50 \times 10^3\text{ V} \times 10 \times 10^{-3}\text{ A} = 500\text{ W}$.

4. The wavelength of the $K_{\alpha}$ X-ray line of an element is $0.71\text{ \AA}$. Assuming Rydberg constant $R = 1.1 \times 10^7\text{ m}^{-1}$ and screening constant $b = 1$ for the $K$ shell ($n_1 = 1, n_2 = 2$), the atomic number $Z$ is approximately:

Explanation: Using $\frac{1}{\lambda} = R(Z-1)^2(\frac{1}{1^2} - \frac{1}{2^2}) = \frac{3}{4} R (Z-1)^2$. Substituting $\lambda = 0.71 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m}$ yields $Z - 1 \approx 41 \implies Z = 42$.

5. If a monochromatic X-ray beam of intensity $I_0$ passes through a sheet of metal of thickness $2\text{ mm}$ and its intensity drops to $I_0 / e$, what is the linear absorption coefficient $\mu$ of the metal?

Explanation: From $I = I_0 e^{-\mu x}$, given $I = I_0/e$, we have $e^{-1} = e^{-\mu x} \implies \mu x = 1$. With $x = 2\text{ mm} = 2 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}$, $\mu = \frac{1}{2 \times 10^{-3}} = 500\text{ m}^{-1}$.

6. In a Bragg diffraction experiment using X-rays, the first-order diffraction maximum is observed at an angle of $30^\circ$ for a crystal plane spacing of $0.28\text{ nm}$. The wavelength of the X-rays is:

Explanation: Using Bragg's law $2d \sin\theta = n\lambda$, for $n=1$, $\lambda = 2(0.28\text{ nm}) \sin(30^\circ) = 2(0.28)(0.5) = 0.28\text{ nm}$? Wait, $2 \times 0.28 \times 0.5 = 0.28$. Let's check: $2 \times 0.14 \times 0.5 = 0.14$. If $d = 0.28$, then $2(0.28)(0.5) = 0.28$. Let's correct option A to $0.28\text{ nm}$ or adjust values. Let's make $d = 0.14\text{ nm}$, then $\lambda = 2(0.14)(0.5) = 0.14\text{ nm}$. Option A: $0.14\text{ nm}$. Correct index 0.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following statements about continuous X-ray spectra is correct?

Explanation: Continuous X-ray photon energies range from zero up to the maximum kinetic energy $E = eV$ of the incident electron.

2. In Moseley's equation $\sqrt{\nu} = a(Z - b)$, the constant $a$ depends on:

Explanation: The proportionality constant $a$ depends on the principal quantum numbers involved in the transition (e.g., $K_{\alpha}$ vs $L_{\alpha}$).

3. What is the primary reason why heavy metals like Tungsten are used as targets in X-ray tubes?

Explanation: Tungsten has a high atomic number (enhancing X-ray production efficiency) and a high melting point to withstand intense heat generated by electron bombardment.

4. If the accelerating potential of an X-ray tube is increased, what happens to the characteristic X-ray wavelengths?

Explanation: Characteristic X-ray wavelengths depend solely on target atomic energy levels and are independent of accelerating voltage (provided voltage exceeds the threshold).

5. The Duane-Hunt law sets a lower limit on which of the following quantities?

Explanation: The Duane-Hunt law gives the short-wavelength cut-off limit $\lambda_{min}$ for continuous X-rays.

6. A vacancy in the $L$ shell of an atom can be filled by an electron transition from which shell?

Explanation: Vacancies in an inner shell ($L$) are filled by electrons dropping from higher outer shells ($M, N$, etc.).

7. The efficiency of X-ray generation for a $100\text{ kV}$ tube operating on a Tungsten target ($Z = 74$) is roughly:

Explanation: Over $99\%$ of electrical energy is converted into heat; X-ray production efficiency is very low, typically less than $1\%$ for medium voltages.

8. Which quantum number change governs the allowed X-ray transitions under dipole selection rules?

Explanation: The orbital angular momentum quantum number must change by $\Delta l = \pm 1$ for electric dipole X-ray transitions.

9. In the absorption of X-rays, an 'absorption edge' occurs when:

Explanation: An absorption edge appears when photon energy equals the inner-shell binding energy, abruptly enabling photoelectric ejection and increasing absorption.

10. Moseley's law failed to give linear plots when atomic weight was used instead of atomic number because:

Explanation: Atomic weight does not strictly increase monotonically (e.g., Argon and Potassium), and isotopic mixtures affect atomic weight, whereas nuclear charge ($Z$) is fundamental.
