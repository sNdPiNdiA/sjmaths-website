Fine Structure of Spectral Lines

The fine structure of spectral lines refers to the splitting of hydrogen and hydrogen-like atomic spectral lines due to relativistic corrections and electron spin-orbit interaction. It provides experimental evidence for electron spin and relativistic mass variation with velocity.

UP TGT ScienceUP PGT PhysicsStudy Notes + Practice

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Qualitative understanding of Bohr-Sommerfeld elliptical orbits, introduction of electron spin, and basic conceptual splitting of spectral lines under magnetic fields.

PGT extension

Quantitative treatment of spin-orbit coupling, relativistic correction to kinetic energy, Darwin term, total angular momentum quantum number j, and calculation of fine structure energy shifts in hydrogen.

Prerequisites

  • Bohr's theory of hydrogen atom
  • Electron spin and magnetic moment
  • Spatial quantization and angular momentum coupling (L-S and j-j coupling)

Relativistic Correction to Kinetic Energy

  • In the Bohr model, the electron's velocity v is treated classically, but for inner shells and principal quantum numbers n, relativistic velocities become significant.
  • The relativistic kinetic energy expansion modifies the purely Newtonian term p2/(2m), introducing a correction proportional to v4/c2.
  • This relativistic correction removes the classical l-degeneracy of energy levels for a given principal quantum number n.

Spin-Orbit Interaction

  • In the rest frame of the electron, the positively charged nucleus orbits the electron, creating an effective magnetic field B at the location of the electron.
  • The intrinsic magnetic moment mus of the electron interacts with this magnetic field, yielding an interaction energy of the form Hso = f(r) L dot S.
  • This interaction couples the orbital angular momentum L and spin angular momentum S to form the total angular momentum J = L + S.

Darwin Term

  • The Darwin term arises from quantum mechanical Zitterbewegung (rapid jittery motion) of the electron, effectively smearing out the electrostatic potential over a Compton wavelength.
  • It affects only states with orbital angular momentum l = 0 (s-states) because only s-wave wavefunctions have a non-zero probability density at the nucleus (r = 0).
  • It contributes an upward energy shift to s-states, lifting the degeneracy between s and p states of the same n.

Total Fine Structure Energy Shift

  • The net fine structure shift is the sum of relativistic corrections, spin-orbit interaction, and the Darwin term.
  • The energy levels depend exclusively on the principal quantum number n and the total angular momentum quantum number j, remaining degenerate for a given pair (n, j) regardless of l.
  • For hydrogen, the magnitude of splitting is scaled by the fine-structure constant alpha squared relative to the principal Rydberg energy.

Derivations

Spin-Orbit Interaction Energy Derivation

Conditions: Single-electron atom, non-relativistic limit with Thomas precession correction

  • Write the magnetic field experienced by the electron in its rest frame due to the moving nucleus: B = (1/2) (1/(4*pi*epsilon_0)) (Ze/c2r3) (L), where the factor of 1/2 is the Thomas precession correction.
  • Express the potential energy of the electron's magnetic dipole moment mu_s in this magnetic field: E_so = -mu_s dot B.
  • Substitute mu_s = -(e/m)S and L = r x p to obtain E_so = (e2 / (8*pi*epsilon_0*m2c2r3)) (L dot S).
  • Evaluate the expectation value of 1/r3 using hydrogenic wavefunctions: <1/r3> = 1 / (a_03 n3 l(l+1/2)(l+1)).

Result: Delta E_{so} = \frac{m_e c^2 \alpha^4 Z^4}{2 n^3} \frac{j(j+1) - l(l+1) - s(s+1)}{l(l+1/2)(l+1)}

Dirac Fine Structure Energy Shift

Conditions: Relativistic Dirac equation perturbation theory for hydrogen atom

  • Start with the exact Dirac Hamiltonian for a single electron in a Coulomb potential.
  • Treat the relativistic kinetic energy correction and spin-orbit coupling as perturbations to the non-relativistic Hamiltonian.
  • Combine the expectation values of the relativistic kinetic energy operator and the spin-orbit Hamiltonian.
  • Express the combined energy shift in terms of n and j using the Dirac exact energy formula expansion in powers of alpha.

Result: E_{n,j} = - \frac{13.6 Z^2}{n^2} \left[ 1 + \frac{\alpha^2 Z^2}{n} \left( \frac{1}{j + 1/2} - \frac{3}{4n} \right) \right] \text{ eV}

Solved numericals

TGT core

For an electron in the state with orbital quantum number l = 1, find the possible values of the total angular momentum quantum number j.

  • Identify the orbital quantum number l = 1 and the electron spin quantum number s = 1/2.
  • Use the rule for vector addition of angular momenta: j = |l - s| to l + s.
  • Calculate the lower limit: j = |1 - 1/2| = 1/2.
  • Calculate the upper limit: j = 1 + 1/2 = 3/2.

Answer: j = 1/2 and 3/2

PGT extension

Calculate the fine-structure separation (in eV) between the 2p_{3/2} and 2p_{1/2} states in hydrogen (Z = 1). Given alpha = 1/137 and ground state energy = 13.6 eV.

  • Identify principal quantum number n = 2 and l = 1 for the 2p states.
  • Identify the total angular momentum values: j = 3/2 for 2p_{3/2} and j = 1/2 for 2p_{1/2}.
  • Use the fine structure energy formula: Delta E = E_n * ((alpha^2 Z^2)/n) * [ (1 / (j_1 + 1/2)) - (1 / (j_2 + 1/2)) ] with E_n = -13.6/n^2 = -3.4 eV.
  • Substitute values: Delta E = (-3.4 eV) * ((1/137)^2 * 1) / 2 * [ (1 / (1/2 + 1/2)) - (1 / (3/2 + 1/2)) ] = 3.4 * (1 / 18769) * (1 / 2) * [ 1 - 1/2 ] = 3.4 * (1 / 37538) * 0.5 = 4.53 * 10^{-5} eV.

Answer: 4.53 x 10^{-5} eV

PGT extension

Determine the degeneracy (including spin) of the hydrogen atom energy level corresponding to principal quantum number n = 3, considering fine structure effects.

  • For a given principal quantum number n, the orbital quantum number l ranges from 0 to n-1 (i.e., l = 0, 1, 2 for n = 3).
  • For each l, the total angular momentum quantum number j can take values j = l + 1/2 and j = l - 1/2 (except l = 0 where j = 1/2).
  • The degeneracy of each fine-structure level (n, j) is given by 2j + 1.
  • For n = 3: l = 0 (2s_{1/2}, j=1/2, deg = 2); l = 1 (2p_{1/2}, j=1/2, deg = 2; 2p_{3/2}, j=3/2, deg = 4); l = 2 (3d_{3/2}, j=3/2, deg = 4; 3d_{5/2}, j=5/2, deg = 6).
  • Sum the degeneracies: 2 + 2 + 4 + 4 + 6 = 18.

Answer: 18

Assumptions and limitations

  • The treatment assumes a single-electron (hydrogenic) system; multi-electron atoms require complex coupling schemes (L-S or j-j coupling) and screening constants.
  • Calculations ignore nuclear spin hyperfine interactions, which produce even finer splittings (hyperfine structure).

Common misconceptions

Common error: Fine structure splitting is caused solely by the external magnetic field of the laboratory.

Correct: Fine structure is an internal relativistic and magnetic effect due to electron motion and spin within the atom itself.

External magnetic fields cause the Zeeman effect, whereas fine structure exists even in zero external field.

Common error: States with the same n and l always have the exact same energy in the Dirac theory.

Correct: States with the same n and j are degenerate in Dirac theory, regardless of l.

Spin-orbit coupling ties energy to total angular momentum j rather than orbital angular momentum l alone.

Quick revision

  • Fine structure of hydrogen originates from relativistic kinetic energy correction, spin-orbit coupling, and the Darwin term.
  • Fine-structure constant: \alpha = \frac{e^2}{4 \pi \epsilon_0 \hbar c} \approx \frac{1}{137}
  • Spin-Orbit Operator L dot S: L \cdot S = \frac{\hbar^2}{2} [j(j+1) - l(l+1) - s(s+1)]

Must remember

  • Fine structure splitting in hydrogenic atoms removes the orbital $l$-degeneracy, leaving levels degenerate only in principal quantum number $n$ and total angular momentum $j$.
  • The fine-structure constant $\alpha \approx \frac{1}{137}$ is a dimensionless quantity that characterizes the strength of the electromagnetic interaction.
  • The Darwin term contributes a non-zero energy shift exclusively to states with orbital angular momentum $l = 0$ ($s$-states).
  • Spin-orbit interaction couples the electron's orbital angular momentum $L$ and spin angular momentum $S$ to form the total angular momentum $J = L + S$.
  • The relativistic kinetic energy correction arises from the expansion of the relativistic Hamiltonian and introduces a correction proportional to $v^4/c^2$.

Glossary

Fine-structure constant ($\alpha$)A dimensionless fundamental physical constant quantifying the strength of the electromagnetic interaction, approximately equal to $1/137$.
Darwin TermA quantum mechanical correction term due to Zitterbewegung (rapid jittery motion) of the electron, affecting only $s$-states ($l=0$).
Spin-Orbit CouplingAn interaction between the electron's intrinsic magnetic moment and the effective magnetic field created by its orbital motion around the nucleus.
Total Angular Momentum Quantum Number ($j$)The quantum number resulting from the vector addition of orbital angular momentum $l$ and spin angular momentum $s$, given by $j = |l \pm s|$.
Bohr Radius ($a_0$)The most probable distance between the nucleus and the electron in the ground state of a hydrogen atom, approximately $0.529 \times 10^{-10} \text{ m}$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the approximate numerical value of the fine-structure constant $\$\alpha$$?

Explanation: The fine-structure constant $\$\alpha$ = \frac{e^2}{4 \$\pi$ \epsilon_0 \hbar c}$ is approximately $\frac{1}{137}$.

2. Which quantum mechanical correction term is responsible for lifting the degeneracy between $s$ and $p$ states of the same principal quantum number $n$?

Explanation: The Darwin term affects only $s$-states ($l=0$) because only they have a non-zero probability density at the nucleus, thereby lifting the $s-p$ degeneracy.

3. What is the total angular momentum quantum number $j$ for a $p_{3/2}$ electron state?

Explanation: For a $p$ state, $l=1$. With electron spin $s=1/2$, the possible values of $j$ are $|1 - 1/2| = 1/2$ and $1 + 1/2 = 3/2$. Thus $p_{3/2}$ corresponds to $j = 3/2$.

4. In hydrogenic atoms, fine structure energy shifts depend exclusively on which pair of quantum numbers?

Explanation: Due to the exact cancellation of certain relativistic and spin-orbit terms, the fine-structure energy levels depend only on $n$ and $j$, remaining degenerate for a given pair regardless of $l$.

5. Which of the following physical phenomena causes the spin-orbit interaction in a hydrogen-like atom?

Explanation: In the rest frame of the electron, the positively charged nucleus orbits the electron, creating an effective magnetic field that interacts with the electron's intrinsic spin magnetic moment.

6. What is the expression for the first-order relativistic kinetic energy correction $E_K^{(1)}$?

Explanation: The relativistic kinetic energy expansion modifies the Newtonian term, yielding a first-order correction of $E_K^{(1)} = - \frac{p^4}{8 m^3 c^2}$.

7. For which orbital angular momentum states is the expectation value $\langle \frac{1}{r^3} \rangle$ undefined or zero?

Explanation: The formula $\langle \frac{1}{r^3} \rangle = \frac{Z^3}{a_0^3 n^3 l(l + 1/2)(l + 1)}$ diverges or is not applicable for $l = 0$ because $l$ appears in the denominator.

8. What is the physical origin of the Darwin term?

Explanation: The Darwin term arises from the Zitterbewegung (rapid jittery motion) of the electron predicted by Dirac theory, effectively smearing out the electrostatic potential over a Compton wavelength.

9. How many allowed values of $j$ exist for a given orbital quantum number $l$ (where $l > 0$)?

Explanation: For $l > 0$, the vector addition $j = |l \pm s|$ yields two values: $j = l + 1/2$ and $j = l - 1/2$.

10. What is the unit of the fine-structure constant $\$\alpha$$?

Explanation: The fine-structure constant $\$\alpha$ = \frac{e^2}{4 \$\pi$ \epsilon_0 \hbar c}$ is a dimensionless combination of fundamental constants.

11. In the vector model, the spin-orbit operator $L \cdot S$ depends on which quantum numbers?

Explanation: The operator is given by $L \cdot S = \frac{\hbar^2}{2} [j(j+1) - l(l+1) - s(s+1)]$, depending on $j$, $l$, and $s$.

12. What is the magnitude of scaling of fine-structure energy splitting relative to the principal Rydberg energy in hydrogen?

Explanation: The magnitude of fine-structure splitting is scaled by $\$\alpha$^2$ relative to the principal Rydberg (Bohr) energy.

13. Which of the following statements is true regarding the Bohr model versus relativistic quantum mechanics for hydrogen?

Explanation: Relativistic corrections and spin-orbit coupling remove the classical $l$-degeneracy present in the standard Bohr model.

14. What is the value of the electron spin quantum number $s$?

Explanation: An electron is a fermion with an intrinsic spin quantum number $s = 1/2$.

15. What effect does the total fine-structure energy shift $\Delta E_{fs}$ have on the ground state ($n=1$) of hydrogen?

Explanation: For $n=1$, $l=0$, and $j=1/2$, there is only one state ($1s_{1/2}$), so it undergoes an overall energy shift (from relativistic and Darwin terms) but does not experience splitting since there are no other states with the same $n$ and different $j$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. For a hydrogen atom in the $2p$ state ($n=2, l=1$), what are the possible values of the total angular momentum quantum number $j$?

Explanation: For $l=1$ and $s=1/2$, the possible values of $j$ are $|1 - 1/2| = 1/2$ and $1 + 1/2 = 3/2$.

2. The Darwin term in the fine structure of hydrogen contributes an energy shift that is non-zero exclusively for states with:

Explanation: Only $s$-states ($l=0$) have a non-zero wavefunction probability density at the origin ($r=0$), making the Darwin term contribution zero for all $l > 0$ states.

3. What is the expression for the operator $L \cdot S$ in terms of $j$, $l$, and $s$?

Explanation: Squaring $J = L + S$ gives $J^2 = L^2 + S^2 + 2 L \cdot S$, leading directly to $L \cdot S = \frac{\hbar^2}{2} [j(j+1) - l(l+1) - s(s+1)]$.

4. In the fine structure formula for hydrogenic atoms, the energy shift $\Delta E_{fs}$ is proportional to which power of the atomic number $Z$?

Explanation: The fine-structure energy shift formula is given by $\Delta E_{fs} = E_n \frac{\$\alpha$^2 Z^2}{n} \left( \frac{3}{4n} - \frac{1}{j + 1/2} \right)$, which is proportional to $Z^2$ explicitly (since $E_n$ is proportional to $Z^2$).

5. Which of the following corrections is NOT a component of the fine structure of spectral lines in hydrogen?

Explanation: Nuclear hyperfine splitting arises from the interaction between the nuclear magnetic dipole moment and the electron's magnetic field, which is a finer effect than standard fine structure.

3. Which term in the fine structure Hamiltonian contributes directly to the lifting of the $2s_{1/2}$ and $2p_{1/2}$ degeneracy found in the Dirac theory?

Explanation: In the Dirac theory, $2s_{1/2}$ and $2p_{1/2}$ remain degenerate. The physical lifting of this specific degeneracy is actually due to QED radiative corrections known as the Lamb shift, though Darwin and spin-orbit split $s$ and $p$ otherwise.

4. What is the physical significance of the Bohr radius $a_0$ in the formula for $\langle \frac{1}{r^3} \rangle$?

Explanation: The Bohr radius $a_0$ sets the fundamental length scale for atomic orbitals in hydrogenic systems.

5. For a hydrogen atom, how does the fine structure splitting scale with the principal quantum number $n$?

Explanation: Since $E_n \propto 1/n^2$ and the bracket term introduces an additional factor of $1/n$ (from $3/4n$), the overall fine structure splitting scales as $\$\alpha$^2 Z^2 / n^3$ (or $1/n^3$). Let's verify: $\Delta E_{fs} \propto E_n / n \propto 1/n^3$.

6. Which electron state in a hydrogen atom experiences the largest Darwin term energy shift?

Explanation: The Darwin term is proportional to $|\psi(0)|^2$. For hydrogenic wavefunctions, $|\psi(0)|^2$ is largest for $n=1$ ($1s$ state) because it scales as $Z^3 / (\$\pi$ a_0^3 n^3)$.

7. In the context of spin-orbit interaction, what represents the source of the effective magnetic field experienced by the electron?

Explanation: Transforming to the electron's rest frame, the nucleus orbits the electron, creating a current loop and an associated magnetic field at the electron's position.

8. What is the primary reason why fine structure corrections are significantly larger in heavy atoms (large $Z$) compared to hydrogen?

Explanation: The fine-structure splitting scales with $Z^2$, making the relativistic and spin-orbit splittings much more prominent in heavy multi-electron elements (inner shells).

9. Which of the following statements correctly contrasts the Zeeman effect and fine structure?

Explanation: Fine structure is an internal atomic property present even in zero external field, whereas the Zeeman effect describes spectral splitting due to an applied external magnetic field.

10. What is the value of the orbital quantum number $l$ for a $d$-state?

Explanation: Standard spectroscopic notation assigns $l = 0, 1, 2, 3$ to $s, p, d, f$ states respectively. Thus, a $d$-state corresponds to $l = 2$.
