Paschen-Back Effect in Atomic Spectra

The Paschen-Back effect is the splitting of spectral lines in a strong external magnetic field where the magnetic interaction energy significantly exceeds the internal spin-orbit coupling energy. In this regime, the orbital and spin angular momenta decouple from each other and precess independently about the direction of the external magnetic field.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Basic definition of the effect, comparison with the normal and anomalous Zeeman effects, distinction between weak and strong field regimes based on internal vs external magnetic interactions, and the lifting of spatial degeneracy.

PGT extension

Detailed quantum mechanical derivation of energy shifts using first-order perturbation theory, evaluation of matrix elements for weak vs strong fields, selection rules for transitions (delta m_l = 0, +-1 and delta m_s = 0), and calculation of shifted frequency intervals.

Prerequisites

  • Vector model of the atom and space quantization
  • Spin-orbit coupling and fine structure of hydrogen-like atoms
  • Normal and anomalous Zeeman effects

Condition for Paschen-Back Effect

  • The external magnetic field B must be extremely strong, such that the magnetic interaction energy (mu_B B) is much greater than the internal spin-orbit interaction energy (Delta E_so).
  • Under this condition, the internal coupling between orbital angular momentum L and spin angular momentum S is broken.
  • Both L and S become uncoupled and independently precess around the direction of the external magnetic field.

Decoupling of L and S

  • In the weak-field Zeeman effect, L and S couple to form total angular momentum J, which then precesses around B.
  • In the Paschen-Back effect, L and S precess independently, meaning their z-components m_l and m_s are separately quantized along the magnetic field direction.
  • The total magnetic quantum number becomes m = m_l + m_s.

Splitting Pattern and Simplification

  • The complex anomalous Zeeman pattern simplifies in the Paschen-Back regime to a normal triplet pattern.
  • The energy shift depends on the sum of the orbital magnetic quantum number and twice the spin magnetic quantum number.
  • Because m_s can take values like +1/2 and -1/2, each Zeeman component splits into doublets or multiplets depending on spin states, though many lines coalesce.

Selection Rules

  • The selection rules for transitions in the Paschen-Back regime are delta m_l = 0, +-1 and delta m_s = 0.
  • Since delta m_s = 0, spin does not flip during the optical transition.
  • The resulting spectrum typically consists of three main groups of lines corresponding to delta m_l = 0, +-1, mimicking the normal Zeeman effect.

Derivations

Energy Shift Calculation in the Paschen-Back Effect

Conditions: Strong external magnetic field B directed along the z-axis, where magnetic interaction >> spin-orbit interaction.

  • Write the Hamiltonian of the atom in a magnetic field as H = H_0 + H_so + H_B, where H_0 is the unperturbed Hamiltonian, H_so = A L \cdot S is the spin-orbit term, and H_B = \frac{\mu_B}{\hbar} (L + 2S) \cdot B is the magnetic interaction term.
  • Treat H_B as the dominant perturbation compared to H_so, or evaluate matrix elements in the uncoupled basis |n, l, m_l, m_s>. Energy shift due to magnetic field is Delta E_B = \langle m_l, m_s | H_B | m_l, m_s \rangle.
  • Substitute H_B into the expectation value using B along the z-axis: Delta E_B = \mu_B B (m_l + 2m_s), noting \langle L_z \rangle = m_l \hbar and \langle S_z \rangle = m_s \hbar.
  • Include the first-order perturbation correction due to spin-orbit coupling as a diagonal matrix element of A L \cdot S \approx A m_l m_s.
  • Combine terms to get the total energy expression including both magnetic and first-order spin-orbit contributions.

Result: Delta E = \mu_B B (m_l + 2m_s) + A m_l m_s

Frequency Shift of Spectral Lines

Conditions: Transition between an upper state (1) and lower state (2) in the Paschen-Back regime.

  • Express the frequency of the emitted or absorbed radiation as \nu = \nu_0 + \frac{\Delta E_1 - \Delta E_2}{h}.
  • Substitute the energy shifts for both states: \Delta E_1 = \mu_B B (m_{l1} + 2m_{s1}) and \Delta E_2 = \mu_B B (m_{l2} + 2m_{s2}).
  • Apply the selection rules: delta m_s = 0 (implying m_{s1} = m_{s2}) and delta m_l = m_{l1} - m_{l2} = 0, +-1.
  • Factor out the common terms to find the frequency displacement from the unperturbed central frequency \nu_0.

Result: \nu = \nu_0 + \frac{\mu_B B}{h} (\Delta m_l)

Solved numericals

TGT core

Calculate the magnitude of the Bohr magneton given electronic charge e = 1.6 x 10^-19 C, Planck constant h = 6.63 x 10^-34 J s, and electron mass m_e = 9.1 x 10^-31 kg.

  • Recall the formula for Bohr magneton: \mu_B = \frac{e \hbar}{2 m_e} = \frac{e h}{4 \pi m_e}.
  • Substitute the given values: \mu_B = \frac{1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{4 \times 3.1416 \times 9.1 \times 10^{-31}}.
  • Calculate numerator: 1.6 \times 6.63 \times 10^{-53} = 1.0608 \times 10^{-52}.
  • Calculate denominator: 4 \times 3.1416 \times 9.1 \times 10^{-31} = 1.1435 \times 10^{-29}.
  • Divide: \mu_B = \frac{1.0608 \times 10^{-52}}{1.1435 \times 10^{-29}} = 9.27 \times 10^{-24} \text{ J/T}.

Answer: 9.27 x 10^-24 J/T

PGT extension

In the Paschen-Back effect for a particular atomic transition, an electron transitions from a state with m_l = 1, m_s = +1/2 to m_l = 0, m_s = +1/2 in a magnetic field of 2.0 T. Find the energy shift of the upper state in Joules.

  • Identify the formula for energy shift in the Paschen-Back effect: \Delta E = \mu_B B (m_l + 2m_s).
  • Substitute the upper state quantum numbers: m_l = 1, m_s = +1/2, and magnetic field B = 2.0 T.
  • Use \mu_B = 9.27 \times 10^{-24} \text{ J/T}.
  • Compute the term inside parentheses: m_l + 2m_s = 1 + 2(0.5) = 1 + 1 = 2.
  • Calculate \Delta E = 9.27 \times 10^{-24} \times 2.0 \times 2 = 3.708 \times 10^{-23} \text{ J}.

Answer: 3.71 x 10^-23 J

PGT extension

Determine the frequency shift (in Hz) for the transition where delta m_l = +1, given a magnetic field B = 1.5 T and Planck's constant h = 6.63 x 10^-34 J s.

  • Use the frequency shift formula in the Paschen-Back effect: \Delta \nu = \frac{\mu_B B}{h} \Delta m_l.
  • Substitute the values: \mu_B = 9.27 \times 10^{-24} \text{ J/T}, B = 1.5 \text{ T}, \Delta m_l = 1, and h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}.
  • Calculate numerator: 9.27 \times 10^{-24} \times 1.5 \times 1 = 1.3905 \times 10^{-23}.
  • Divide by Planck's constant: \Delta \nu = \frac{1.3905 \times 10^{-23}}{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}.
  • Evaluate division: \Delta \nu = 2.097 \times 10^{10} \text{ Hz}.

Answer: 2.10 x 10^10 Hz

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes the external magnetic field is sufficiently uniform across the radiating atoms.
  • Neglects nuclear spin interactions (hyperfine structure) which would introduce further small splittings.
  • Valid only in the strong-field limit where the magnetic interaction energy greatly exceeds the internal spin-orbit coupling energy.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The Paschen-Back effect produces the exact same splitting pattern as the normal Zeeman effect in all aspects.

Correct: While it resembles a triplet like the normal Zeeman effect, each component actually consists of multiple unresolved lines because individual m_s states still contribute to the total energy shift.

Although selection rule delta m_s = 0 yields a triplet-like main structure, individual levels retain dependence on m_s.

Common error: In the Paschen-Back effect, total angular momentum J remains a good quantum number.

Correct: Total angular momentum J is not a good quantum number in the Paschen-Back regime because L and S decouple.

The strong external field breaks the internal L-S coupling, making m_l and m_s separately conserved instead of j.

Common error: The Paschen-Back effect occurs at very low magnetic field strengths.

Correct: Low magnetic field strengths produce the anomalous Zeeman effect, whereas the Paschen-Back effect requires extremely high magnetic fields.

The external magnetic energy must overcome the internal spin-orbit interaction energy.

Quick revision

  • The Paschen-Back effect occurs in strong magnetic fields where orbital (L) and spin (S) angular momenta decouple.
  • The energy shift is given by Delta E = \mu_B B (m_l + 2m_s).
  • Selection rules are delta m_l = 0, +-1 and delta m_s = 0.
  • The complex anomalous Zeeman pattern transitions into a simpler triplet-like pattern under extreme magnetic fields.

Must remember

  • The Paschen-Back effect occurs in extremely strong magnetic fields where the external magnetic interaction energy ($mu_B B$) is much greater than the internal spin-orbit interaction energy ($Delta E_{so}$).
  • In the Paschen-Back regime, the internal coupling between orbital angular momentum $L$ and spin angular momentum $S$ is broken, causing them to precess independently around the external magnetic field $B$.
  • The z-components $m_l$ and $m_s$ are separately quantized, and the total magnetic quantum number is given by $m = m_l + m_s$.
  • The selection rules for the Paschen-Back effect are $\Delta m_l = 0, \pm 1$ and $\Delta m_s = 0$.
  • Total angular momentum $J$ is not a good quantum number in the Paschen-Back regime because $L$ and $S$ decouple.
  • The energy shift in the Paschen-Back effect is given by $\Delta E = \mu_B B (m_l + 2m_s)$.
  • The complex anomalous Zeeman pattern simplifies in the Paschen-Back regime to a triplet-like pattern corresponding to $\Delta m_l = 0, \pm 1$.
  • The magnetic interaction Hamiltonian for the Paschen-Back effect is $H_B = \frac{\mu_B}{\hbar} (L_z + 2S_z) B$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What is the primary condition for observing the Paschen-Back effect in atomic spectra?

Explanation: The Paschen-Back effect requires an extremely strong external magnetic field such that the magnetic interaction energy ($mu_B B$) greatly exceeds the internal spin-orbit interaction energy ($Delta E_{so}$).

2. What happens to the internal coupling between orbital angular momentum $L$ and spin angular momentum $S$ in the Paschen-Back regime?

Explanation: Under the influence of an extremely strong external magnetic field, the internal coupling between $L$ and $S$ is broken, and they precess independently.

3. Which quantum numbers are separately quantized along the magnetic field direction in the Paschen-Back effect?

Explanation: Because $L$ and $S$ precess independently in the Paschen-Back regime, their z-components $m_l$ and $m_s$ are separately quantized along the direction of the magnetic field.

4. What is the expression for the total magnetic quantum number $m$ in the Paschen-Back regime?

Explanation: The total magnetic quantum number in the strong-field uncoupled representation is given by the algebraic sum $m = m_l + m_s$.

5. What is the selection rule for the orbital magnetic quantum number $Delta m_l$ in the Paschen-Back regime?

Explanation: The selection rules for optical transitions in the Paschen-Back regime are $Delta m_l = 0, \pm 1$ and $Delta m_s = 0$.

6. What is the selection rule for the spin magnetic quantum number $Delta m_s$ during optical transitions in the Paschen-Back effect?

Explanation: The selection rule $Delta m_s = 0$ indicates that the spin does not flip during the optical transition because the electric dipole operator is spin-independent.

7. What is the magnetic interaction Hamiltonian $H_B$ for the Paschen-Back effect?

Explanation: The magnetic interaction Hamiltonian accounts for both orbital and spin contributions with their respective g-factors: $H_B = \frac{\mu_B}{\hbar} ($L_{z}$ + 2S_z) B$.

8. What is the energy shift $\Delta E$ formula in the Paschen-Back effect?

Explanation: The energy shift in the Paschen-Back regime, ignoring higher-order corrections, is given by $\Delta E = \mu_B B (m_l + 2m_s)$.

9. Why is total angular momentum $J$ not a good quantum number in the Paschen-Back regime?

Explanation: The strong external magnetic field overcomes the internal spin-orbit coupling, breaking the coupling between $L$ and $S$, so $J$ is no longer a conserved or good quantum number.

10. What does the complex anomalous Zeeman pattern simplify to in the Paschen-Back regime?

Explanation: The complex anomalous Zeeman pattern simplifies in the strong-field Paschen-Back regime to a normal triplet pattern governed by $Delta m_l = 0, \pm 1$.

11. What is the formula for the Bohr magneton $\mu_B$?

Explanation: The Bohr magneton is defined as $\mu_B = \frac{e \hbar}{2 m_e}$, representing the natural unit of atomic magnetic moment.

12. What is the frequency shift expression $\Delta \nu$ for spectral lines in the Paschen-Back effect?

Explanation: The frequency shift is given by $\Delta \nu = \frac{\mu_B B}{h} \Delta m_l$, since $Delta m_s = 0$ for allowed transitions.

13. Which phenomenon occurs at low magnetic field strengths where spin-orbit coupling dominates over the magnetic interaction?

Explanation: Low magnetic field strengths produce the anomalous Zeeman effect, where spin-orbit coupling remains intact and $J$ is a good quantum number.

14. What is the approximate form of the spin-orbit perturbation energy $E_{so}$ in the strong field limit?

Explanation: In the strong field uncoupled representation, the first-order perturbation energy due to spin-orbit interaction is approximated as $E_{so} \approx A \langle L \cdot S \rangle \approx A m_l m_s$.

15. In the Paschen-Back effect, how many main groups of spectral lines are typically observed corresponding to orbital transitions?

Explanation: The resulting spectrum typically consists of three main groups of lines corresponding to $Delta m_l = 0, \pm 1$, mimicking the normal Zeeman effect.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. Under what condition does the Paschen-Back effect replace the anomalous Zeeman effect in atomic spectra?

Explanation: The transition from the anomalous Zeeman effect to the Paschen-Back effect occurs when the external magnetic field becomes sufficiently strong that $mu_B B \gg \Delta E_{so}$.

2. What are the allowed selection rules for $m_l$ and $m_s$ during optical transitions in the Paschen-Back regime?

Explanation: In the Paschen-Back regime, electric dipole transitions obey the selection rules $Delta m_l = 0, \pm 1$ and $Delta m_s = 0$.

3. Calculate the energy shift $\Delta E$ for an electron state with $m_l = 1$ and $m_s = -1/2$ in the Paschen-Back regime.

Explanation: Using $\Delta E = \mu_B B (m_l + 2m_s)$, substitute $m_l = 1$ and $m_s = -1/2$: $\Delta E = \mu_B B (1 + 2(-1/2)) = \mu_B B (1 - 1) = 0$.

4. Which of the following quantum numbers ceases to be a 'good quantum number' when transitioning from the weak-field Zeeman effect to the strong-field Paschen-Back effect?

Explanation: Total angular momentum $J$ ceases to be a good quantum number in the Paschen-Back regime because the external magnetic field decouples $L$ and $S$.

5. What is the physical reason why $\Delta m_s = 0$ is a strict selection rule in the Paschen-Back effect?

Explanation: The electric dipole operator representing optical transitions is spin-independent and cannot flip the electron spin, leading to $Delta m_s = 0$.

6. In a strong magnetic field $B$, what is the splitting energy interval between adjacent $\Delta m_l$ components in the Paschen-Back spectrum?

Explanation: The frequency shift depends on $\Delta m_l$, and for adjacent levels where $\Delta m_l = \pm 1$, the separation in energy is $\mu_B B$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. In the Paschen-Back regime, if $m_l = -1$ and $m_s = +1/2$, what is the energy shift $\Delta E$?

Explanation: Using $\Delta E = \mu_B B (m_l + 2m_s)$, substitute $m_l = -1$ and $m_s = +1/2$: $\Delta E = \mu_B B (-1 + 2(1/2)) = \mu_B B (-1 + 1) = 0$.

2. Which interaction energy is neglected or treated as a perturbation when calculating energies in the extreme Paschen-Back limit first-order approximation?

Explanation: In the extreme Paschen-Back limit, the external magnetic interaction is dominant, and the spin-orbit interaction is treated as a small perturbation.

3. What is the total magnetic quantum number $m$ for an electron state with $m_l = 2$ and $m_s = -1/2$?

Explanation: The total magnetic quantum number is $m = m_l + m_s = 2 + (-1/2) = 1.5$.

4. Why does the Paschen-Back effect resemble the normal Zeeman effect in its overall pattern?

Explanation: With $Delta m_s = 0$, the transitions are governed solely by $Delta m_l = 0, \pm 1$, yielding three primary groups of lines just like the normal Zeeman triplet.

5. What is the unit of the Bohr magneton $\mu_B$ in SI units?

Explanation: The Bohr magneton is expressed in Joule per Tesla ($J/T$) or Ampere-meter squared ($A m^2$).

6. If an external magnetic field is increased continuously from weak to extremely strong, what is the sequence of spectral regimes observed for a multi-electron atom?

Explanation: At low fields, anomalous Zeeman effect is observed. As the field increases and exceeds the internal spin-orbit splitting, it transitions into the Paschen-Back effect.

7. What is the effect of the strong magnetic field on the spatial orientation of $L$ and $S$ in the Paschen-Back regime?

Explanation: In the Paschen-Back effect, $L$ and $S$ break their mutual coupling and independently precess around the external magnetic field direction.

8. Which of the following expressions correctly gives the frequency shift $\Delta \nu$ for a transition with $\Delta m_l = +1$?

Explanation: Using $\Delta \nu = \frac{\mu_B B}{h} \Delta m_l$, substituting $Delta m_l = 1$ yields $\Delta \nu = \frac{\mu_B B}{h}$.

9. What distinguishes the Paschen-Back triplet from the normal Zeeman triplet upon closer spectroscopic inspection?

Explanation: While it appears as a triplet, individual levels retain $m_s$ dependence, meaning each component consists of multiple unresolved lines.

10. In the context of the Paschen-Back effect, what does the quantum number $m_l$ physically represent?

Explanation: The quantum number $m_l$ represents the z-component or projection of the orbital angular momentum vector along the external magnetic field direction.
