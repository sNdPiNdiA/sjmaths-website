Spectral Terms in Atomic Physics

Spectral terms denote the energy states of multi-electron atoms characterized by total orbital, spin, and total angular momentum quantum numbers. They provide a concise nomenclature for classifying atomic energy levels and optical transitions.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Basic understanding of term symbols for single-valence electron systems (like alkali atoms), quantum numbers n, l, s, j, and simple transitions using selection rules.

PGT extension

Advanced coupling schemes (L-S and j-j coupling), Hund's rules for ground state determination, Landé interval rule, and Zeeman effect considerations.

Prerequisites

  • Bohr's theory of hydrogen atom and quantum numbers
  • Electron spin and spin-orbit interaction
  • Pauli's exclusion principle and orbital degeneracy

Definition and Anatomy of a Spectral Term

  • A spectral term is represented in the spectroscopic notation as 2S+1LJ, where S is the total spin quantum number, L is the total orbital angular momentum quantum number represented by letters (S, P, D, F, G...), and J is the total electronic angular momentum quantum number.
  • The multiplicity of the term is given by (2S+1), which indicates the number of sublevels associated with spin-orbit coupling for a given L and S (when L ≥ S).
  • The quantum number J takes values ranging from |L-S| to L+S in integer steps, representing vector addition of total orbital and spin angular momenta.

L-S Coupling (Russell-Saunders Coupling)

  • Applicable predominantly to light and medium-mass atoms where electrostatic Coulomb interactions and exchange energies are much stronger than spin-orbit interactions.
  • Individual orbital angular momenta (li) couple to form a resultant total orbital angular momentum L = ∑ li, and individual spin angular momenta (si) couple to form a resultant total spin S = ∑ si.
  • L and S then couple weakly via spin-orbit interaction to form the total angular momentum J = L + S.

j-j Coupling

  • Applicable to heavy multi-electron atoms where the spin-orbit interaction of individual electrons exceeds the electrostatic interaction between different electrons.
  • Each electron's orbital angular momentum li couples strongly with its own spin si to form an individual total angular momentum ji = li + si.
  • The individual ji vectors then couple weakly to yield the total electronic angular momentum J = ∑ ji.

Hund's Rules for Ground State Determination

  • Rule 1: For a given electron configuration, the term with the maximum multiplicity (2S+1) lies lowest in energy due to minimization of electrostatic repulsion (Pauli exclusion principle keeps parallel-spin electrons apart).
  • Rule 2: For terms with the same multiplicity, the term with the largest value of total orbital angular momentum L lies lowest in energy.
  • Rule 3: For outer shells less than half-filled, the level with the lowest J value (J = |L-S|) has the lowest energy (normal multiplet). For shells more than half-filled, the level with the highest J value (J = L+S) has the lowest energy (inverted multiplet).

Derivations

Derivation of Total Angular Momentum J Range in L-S Coupling

Conditions: Vector addition model of angular momenta in quantum mechanics

  • Write the operators for total orbital angular momentum L and total spin S.
  • Apply quantum mechanical vector addition rules for angular momenta: |L - S| ≤ J ≤ L + S.
  • Determine the allowed integer steps for J based on the triangle inequality and spatial quantization of angular momentum.

Result: J = |L-S|, |L-S|+1, ..., (L+S)

Calculation of Multiplicity and Number of Components

Conditions: L &ge; S condition for Russell-Saunders coupling

  • Identify the number of unpaired electrons n in the subshell.
  • Calculate total spin S = n × (1/2).
  • Determine the multiplicity as (2S+1) and count the distinct J values from |L-S| to L+S.

Result: Number of J components equals 2S+1 when L ≥ S, and 2L+1 when L < S.

Solved numericals

TGT core

Find the spectroscopic term symbol for an electron configuration with L = 1 and S = 1/2.

  • Calculate multiplicity: 2S + 1 = 2(1/2) + 1 = 2.
  • Identify the letter for L = 1, which is P.
  • Determine possible J values: J = L + S to |L - S| = 1 + 1/2 = 3/2 and 1 - 1/2 = 1/2.
  • Construct the term symbols: 2P3/2 and 2P1/2.

Answer: 2P3/2 and 2P1/2

PGT extension

Determine the ground state spectral term for carbon atom (atomic number Z = 6) using Hund's rules.

  • Write the electronic configuration of carbon: 1s2 2s2 2p2.
  • Consider the valence electrons in the 2p subshell (n=2, l=1). The magnetic quantum numbers ml are +1, 0, -1.
  • To maximize multiplicity (Hund's Rule 1), place two electrons in separate orbitals with parallel spins: ml1 = +1 (s=+1/2) and ml2 = 0 (s=+1/2).
  • Total spin S = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1. Multiplicity = 2S + 1 = 3.
  • Total orbital angular momentum L = ∑ ml = +1 + 0 = +1, which corresponds to P.
  • Since the 2p shell is less than half-filled (2 electrons out of 6), apply Hund's Rule 3: J = |L - S| = |1 - 1| = 0.

Answer: 3P0

PGT extension

Calculate the Landé g-factor for the spectral term <sup>2</sup>D<sub>3/2</sub>.

  • Identify the quantum numbers from 2D3/2: Multiplicity 2S+1 = 2 ⇒ S = 1/2. Letter D ⇒ L = 2. J = 3/2.
  • Substitute values into the Landé g-factor formula: g = 1 + [J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)] / [2J(J+1)].
  • Calculate numerator terms: J(J+1) = (3/2)(5/2) = 15/4 = 3.75; S(S+1) = (1/2)(3/2) = 3/4 = 0.75; L(L+1) = 2(3) = 6.
  • Calculate expression inside brackets: 3.75 + 0.75 - 6 = -1.5.
  • Calculate denominator: 2 × 3.75 = 7.5.
  • Compute g: g = 1 + (-1.5 / 7.5) = 1 - 0.2 = 0.8.

Answer: g = 0.8

Assumptions and limitations

  • Russell-Saunders coupling assumes electrostatic forces dominate over magnetic spin-orbit interactions, which breaks down for heavy atoms where j-j coupling must be used.
  • Central field approximation assumes each electron moves in an average spherically symmetric potential created by the nucleus and other electrons.

Common misconceptions

Common error: The multiplicity (2S+1) always represents the number of sub-levels in a fine structure multiplet.

Correct: The number of fine structure levels is (2S+1) only when L &ge; S. If L < S, the number of levels is (2L+1).

J values are constrained by vector triangle inequalities |L-S| to L+S.

Common error: Hund's third rule always gives lowest energy for J = L - S.

Correct: J = L - S is lowest only for subshells less than half-filled; for more than half-filled shells, J = L + S is lowest.

Spin-orbit coupling sign inverts due to negative effective nuclear charge gradient or orbital-spin orientation changes in fuller subshells.

Quick revision

  • Spectroscopic term format: 2S+1LJ
  • L values 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 correspond to S, P, D, F, G letters.
  • Hund's 1st rule: Maximize multiplicity (2S+1).
  • Hund's 2nd rule: Maximize L for same multiplicity.
  • Hund's 3rd rule: J = |L-S| for < half-filled, J = L+S for > half-filled.

Must remember

  • A spectral term is represented in spectroscopic notation as $^{2S+1}L_J$, where $2S+1$ is the multiplicity, $L$ is the total orbital letter, and $J$ is the total electronic angular momentum quantum number.
  • L-S (Russell-Saunders) coupling applies predominantly to light and medium-mass atoms where electrostatic interactions dominate spin-orbit interactions.
  • j-j coupling applies to heavy multi-electron atoms where individual spin-orbit interactions exceed electrostatic repulsion between electrons.
  • Hund's Rule 1: For a given configuration, the term with the maximum multiplicity $(2S+1)$ lies lowest in energy.
  • Hund's Rule 3: For shells less than half-filled, the level with $J = |L-S|$ has the lowest energy (normal multiplet); for more than half-filled, $J = L+S$ has the lowest energy (inverted multiplet).

Glossary

MultiplicityGiven by $2S+1$, indicating the number of sublevels associated with spin-orbit coupling when $L \ge S$.
L-S CouplingCoupling scheme where individual $l_i$ couple to $L$ and individual $s_i$ couple to $S$, and then $L$ and $S$ couple to form $J$.
j-j CouplingCoupling scheme where individual $l_i$ and $s_i$ first couple to form $j_i$, which then combine to give total $J$.
Landé g-factorA dimensionless factor representing the ratio of magnetic dipole moment to Bohr magneton in weak magnetic fields.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. What does the term symbol notation $^{2S+1}$L_{J}$$ designate in atomic physics?

Explanation: The spectroscopic notation $^{2S+1}$L_{J}$$ represents a spectral term where $S$ is total spin, $L$ is total orbital angular momentum, and $J$ is total electronic angular momentum.

2. In the spectroscopic term symbol $^{2P}_{1/2}$, what is the value of the total spin quantum number $S$?

Explanation: The multiplicity is given by $2S+1 = 2$. Therefore, $2S = 1$, which gives $S = 1/2$.

3. For a term with $L = 2$ and $S = 1$, what are the possible values of the total angular momentum quantum number $J$?

Explanation: $J$ ranges from $|L-S|$ to $L+S$ in integer steps. For $L=2$ and $S=1$, $|2-1| = 1$ to $2+1 = 3$, giving $J = 1, 2, 3$.

4. Russell-Saunders (L-S) coupling is predominantly applicable to which type of atoms?

Explanation: L-S coupling is valid when electrostatic Coulomb and exchange interactions are much stronger than spin-orbit interactions, characteristic of light and medium-mass atoms.

5. In j-j coupling, what quantity is formed first for each individual electron before combining into total $J$?

Explanation: In j-j coupling, each electron's orbital angular momentum $l_i$ couples strongly with its own spin $s_i$ to form $j_i$.

6. According to Hund's first rule, for a given electron configuration, which term lies lowest in energy?

Explanation: Hund's first rule states that the term with maximum multiplicity lies lowest in energy due to minimization of electrostatic repulsion.

7. For an outer subshell less than half-filled, which $J$ value corresponds to the lowest energy state according to Hund's third rule?

Explanation: For subshells less than half-filled, the level with the lowest $J$ value ($J = |L-S|$) has the lowest energy (normal multiplet).

8. How many fine structure sublevels are associated with a spectral term where $L = 1$ and $S = 3/2$?

Explanation: When $L < S$, the number of fine structure levels is given by $2L+1$. Here $L = 1$, so $2(1) + 1 = 3$ levels ($J = 1/2, 3/2, 5/2$). Note that the multiplicity $2S+1 = 4$, but because $L < S$, the levels are restricted to $2L+1$.

9. What is the degeneracy (number of magnetic states) of a level with total angular momentum $J$ in the absence of an external magnetic field?

Explanation: The spatial degeneracy of a level with total angular momentum $J$ is given by $g_J = 2J + 1$, corresponding to the $2J+1$ possible orientations of $J$ along a quantization axis.

10. What is the spectroscopic term symbol for an electron configuration with $L = 0$ and $S = 1/2$?

Explanation: For $S = 1/2$, multiplicity $2S+1 = 2$. For $L = 0$, letter is $S$. For $S = 1/2$ and $L = 0$, $J = |0 - 1/2| = 1/2$. Thus, $^{2}S_{1/2}$.

11. Which of the following conditions correctly describes when Hund's third rule results in an inverted multiplet?

Explanation: For shells more than half-filled, the level with the highest $J$ value ($J = L+S$) has the lowest energy, forming an inverted multiplet.

12. What is the physical reason behind Hund's first rule maximizing multiplicity?

Explanation: Parallel-spin electrons are kept apart by the Pauli exclusion principle, reducing Coulomb electrostatic repulsion and lowering total energy.

13. In the L-S coupling scheme, what is the resultant total spin $S$ for two electrons with individual spins $s_1 = 1/2$ and $s_2 = 1/2$?

Explanation: Two electron spins combine via vector addition to give $S = s_1 + s_2, \dots, |s_1 - s_2|$, which evaluates to $1/2 + 1/2 = 1$ or $1/2 - 1/2 = 0$.

14. What does the Landé g-factor describe?

Explanation: The Landé g-factor accounts for the coupling of spin and orbital angular momenta when determining the magnetic moment of an atomic state in a weak magnetic field.

15. For a p-electron ($l = 1$), what are the possible values of the individual total angular momentum $j$ in j-j coupling?

Explanation: For an electron with orbital quantum number $l_i$ and spin $s_i = 1/2$, $j_i = l_i \pm 1/2$. For $l = 1$, $j = 1 \pm 1/2$, yielding $1/2$ and $3/2$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. What is the ground state spectral term symbol for a single valence electron in an alkali atom with configuration $ns^1$ ($n ge 1$)?

Explanation: For $ns^1$, $l = 0 \implies L = S$ (Wait, $L = 0, S = 1/2$), multiplicity $2S+1 = 2$, $J = |0 - 1/2| = 1/2$. Thus, the term is $^2S_{1/2}$.

2. Find the ground state term for the carbon atom (outer electronic configuration $2p^2$) using Hund's rules.

Explanation: For $2p^2$, max $S = 1$ (multiplicity $3$). Max $L = 1$ ($P$ term). Since the $p$ shell is less than half-filled ($2$ out of $6$), Hund's third rule gives the lowest $J = |L-S| = |1-1| = 0$. Thus, $^3P_0$.

3. In a Russell-Saunders coupling scheme, if two non-equivalent electrons have orbital quantum numbers $l_1 = 1$ and $l_2 = 2$, what are the possible values of the total orbital angular momentum $L$?

Explanation: The total orbital angular momentum $L$ ranges from $|l_1 - l_2|$ to $l_1 + l_2$ in integer steps. Here, $|1-2| = 1$ to $1+2 = 3$, giving $L = 1, 2, 3$ ($P, D, F$).

4. What is the ground state spectral term for a completely filled subshell (such as a closed $p^6$ shell)?

Explanation: For a completely filled subshell, all electronic spins and orbital momenta cancel out completely, yielding $S = 0$, $L = 0$, and $J = 0$, which corresponds to the $^1S_0$ term.

5. According to Hund's rules, what is the ground state spectral term for nitrogen atom ($2p^3$ configuration)?

Explanation: For $2p^3$, maximum $S = 3/2$ (multiplicity $4$). The maximum $L = 0$ (since magnetic quantum numbers sum to $1+0+(-1)=0$). Thus $L=0$ ($S$ term). With $S=3/2$ and $L=0$, $J = |0 - 3/2| = 3/2$. Result is $^4S_{3/2}$.

6. What is the energy shift due to spin-orbit interaction $\Delta E_{SO}$ given by when expressed in terms of $J$, $L$, and $S$?

Explanation: The spin-orbit energy shift is given by perturbation theory as $\Delta E_{SO} = \frac{1}{2} A \left[ J(J+1) - L(L+1) - S(S+1) \right]$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. For a spectral term $^4D_{7/2}$, what are the values of $S$, $L$, and $J$ respectively?

Explanation: Multiplicity $2S+1 = 4 \implies 2S = 3 \implies S = 3/2$. Letter $D$ corresponds to $L = 2$. Subscript gives $J = 7/2$. Note that $J = 7/2$ is valid since $|L-S| = |2 - 3/2| = 1/2 \le 7/2 \le 2 + 3/2 = 7/2$.

2. Which of the following atoms or ions is most likely to exhibit pure j-j coupling rather than L-S coupling?

Explanation: Heavy multi-electron atoms like Bismuth ($Z = 83$) have very large spin-orbit interactions due to strong nuclear charge, making j-j coupling more appropriate.

3. What is the number of fine structure levels in a $^3D$ term?

Explanation: For $^3D$, $S = 1$ and $L = 2$. Since $L \ge S$, the number of fine structure levels is equal to the multiplicity $2S+1 = 3$ ($J = 1, 2, 3$).

4. In the L-S coupling scheme, if $L = 3$ and $S = 1/2$, what are the possible term symbols for the configuration?

Explanation: $L = 3$ corresponds to $F$. $S = 1/2$ gives multiplicity $2(1/2)+1 = 2$. $J$ values range from $|3 - 1/2| = 5/2$ to $3 + 1/2 = 7/2$. Thus, $^2F_{5/2}$ and $^2F_{7/2}$.

5. According to Hund's rules, what is the ground state term for oxygen atom ($2p^4$ configuration)?

Explanation: For $2p^4$, $S = 1$ ($3$ multiplicity), $L = 1$ ($P$ term). Since the shell is more than half-filled ($4$ out of $6$), the lowest energy level has $J = L + S = 1 + 1 = 2$. Thus, $^3P_2$.

6. What is the Landé g-factor for a pure spin state where $L = 0$ and $J = S$?

Explanation: Substituting $L = 0$ and $J = S$ into Landé formula: $g = 1 + \frac{S(S+1) + S(S+1) - 0}{2S(S+1)} = 1 + \frac{2S(S+1)}{2S(S+1)} = 1 + 1 = 2$.

7. Which quantum numbers are conserved in the j-j coupling scheme?

Explanation: In j-j coupling, individual $l_i$ and $s_i$ strongly couple to form individual $j_i$, and individual $j_i$ combine to form total $J$. $L$ and $S$ are not good quantum numbers.

8. What is the maximum number of electrons that can occupy a spectral subshell characterized by orbital quantum number $l$?

Explanation: The maximum capacity of a subshell with orbital quantum number $l$ is $2(2l+1)$, which equals $4l+2$.

9. If a spectral term has $L = 0$ and $S = 0$, what is the multiplicity and term symbol?

Explanation: For $S = 0$, multiplicity is $2(0)+1 = 1$. For $L = 0$, the letter is $S$, and $J = 0$. Thus, $^1S_0$.

10. Why does Hund's second rule state that among terms with the same multiplicity, the one with the largest $L$ lies lowest in energy?

Explanation: Higher $L$ values correspond to orbits where electron charge distributions keep electrons farther apart on average, further minimizing electrostatic repulsion.
