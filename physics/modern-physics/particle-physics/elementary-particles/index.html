Elementary Particles and Conservation Laws

Elementary particles are subatomic constituents of matter and radiation that are not known to have any substructure. They are classified fundamentally into fermions and bosons, interacting via four fundamental forces governed by strict conservation laws.

Scope and prerequisites

TGT core

Classification of elementary particles into leptons, mesons, and baryons; basic conservation laws of charge, mass-energy, and linear/angular momentum.

PGT extension

Detailed classification using standard model terminology, strangeness, isospin, baryon number, lepton number, and conservation laws in strong, electromagnetic, and weak interactions.

Prerequisites

  • Conservation laws in mechanics and electrodynamics
  • Relativistic energy-momentum relation
  • Quantum numbers and Pauli's exclusion principle

Classification of Elementary Particles

  • Particles are broadly divided based on their spin and interactions into Hadrons (participating in strong interactions) and Leptons (participating in weak and electromagnetic interactions).
  • Hadrons are further subdivided into Baryons (fermions with half-integral spin, such as protons and neutrons) and Mesons (bosons with integral spin, such as pions).
  • Leptons include the electron, muon, tau, and their respective neutrinos, all possessing spin-1/2 and obeying Fermi-Dirac statistics.

Conservation Laws in Particle Interactions

  • The total electric charge (Q), baryon number (B), and individual lepton numbers (Le, Lμ, Lτ) are conserved in all known interactions (strong, electromagnetic, and weak).
  • Strangeness (S) and Isospin (I3) are conserved in strong and electromagnetic interactions but can be violated by ΔS = ±1 in weak interactions.
  • Parity (P), charge conjugation (C), and time reversal (T) are conserved in strong and electromagnetic interactions, while parity and C-parity are maximally violated in weak interactions.

The Quark Model of Hadrons

  • Hadrons are not truly elementary; baryons are composed of three quarks (qqq), and mesons are composed of a quark-antiquark pair (qq̄).
  • The six flavors of quarks are up (u), down (d), strange (s), charm (c), bottom (b), and top (t), each carrying fractional electric charge.
  • Color charge (red, green, blue) is introduced as the quantum number for the strong interaction, governed by Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD).

Fundamental Interactions and Gauge Bosons

  • Gravitational interaction is mediated by the hypothetical graviton; it has negligible effect on subatomic particle scales.
  • Electromagnetic interaction is mediated by photons, strong interaction by gluons, and weak interaction by W± and Z0 bosons.
  • Each interaction has a characteristic coupling constant and relative strength, dictating the decay rates and cross-sections of particle reactions.

Derivations

Threshold Energy for Particle Production

Conditions: A projectile particle of mass m<sub>1</sub> and total energy E incident on a stationary target particle of mass m<sub>2</sub> to produce new particles with total rest mass ∑M.

  • Write the relativistic energy-momentum four-vector invariant for the initial state in the lab frame: (E + m2c2)2 - p2c2 = Ecm2.
  • Express the square of the total center-of-mass energy using E2 - p2c2 = m12c4: Ecm2 = m12c4 + m22c4 + 2Em2c2.
  • At threshold, the final products are produced at rest relative to each other in the center-of-mass frame, so the minimum center-of-mass energy is Ecm,min = (∑M)c2.
  • Equating the invariant mass-energy squares yields the threshold energy condition for the incident particle.

Result: E_{th} = rac{(sum M)^2 c^2 - m_1^2 c^2 - m_2^2 c^2}{2 m_2}

Q-Value of a Nuclear or Particle Reaction

Conditions: Reaction of the form A + B \rightarrow C + D at non-relativistic or relativistic limits.

  • Write the conservation of total relativistic energy: E_A + E_B = E_C + E_D.
  • Expand each total energy into its rest mass energy and kinetic energy: (m_A c^2 + K_A) + (m_B c^2 + K_B) = (m_C c^2 + K_C) + (m_D c^2 + K_D).
  • Define the Q-value as the difference between the total kinetic energy after and before the reaction: Q = (K_C + K_D) - (K_A + K_B).
  • Substitute back to express Q purely in terms of rest mass energies.

Result: Q = [m_A + m_B - (m_C + m_D)] c^2

Solved numericals

TGT core

Determine whether the following reaction is allowed by conservation laws: p + p \rightarrow p + n + \pi^+.

  • Check Baryon Number (B): Initial B = 1 + 1 = 2. Final B = 1 (proton) + 1 (neutron) + 0 (pion) = 2. Baryon number is conserved.
  • Check Electric Charge (Q): Initial Q = 1 + 1 = 2. Final Q = 1 (proton) + 0 (neutron) + 1 (pion) = 2. Charge is conserved.
  • Check Lepton Number (L): Initial L = 0, Final L = 0. Lepton number is conserved.

Answer: Allowed by conservation of charge, baryon number, and lepton number.

PGT extension

Check the validity of the reaction K^- + p \rightarrow \Lambda^0 + \pi^0 using conservation of strangeness, baryon number, and charge.

  • Write quantum numbers for K^-: B = 0, Q = -1, S = -1. For proton (p): B = 1, Q = 1, S = 0. Initial totals: B = 1, Q = 0, S = -1.
  • Write quantum numbers for \Lambda^0: B = 1, Q = 0, S = -1. For \pi^0: B = 0, Q = 0, S = 0. Final totals: B = 1, Q = 0, S = -1.
  • Compare initial and final totals: B_initial = B_final (1 = 1), Q_initial = Q_final (0 = 0), S_initial = S_final (-1 = -1).

Answer: Allowed in strong interactions as strangeness, baryon number, and charge are all conserved.

PGT extension

Calculate the threshold kinetic energy of a proton incident on a stationary proton to produce an antiproton via the reaction p + p \rightarrow p + p + p + \bar{p}.

  • Identify masses: m_1 = m_2 = m_3 = m_4 = m_p = m. Total rest mass of final products ∑M = 4m.
  • Use the threshold energy formula: E_{th} = \frac{(\sum M)^2 c^2 - m_1^2 c^2 - m_2^2 c^2}{2 m_2}.
  • Substitute values: E_{th} = \frac{(4m)^2 c^2 - m^2 c^2 - m^2 c^2}{2 m c^2} = \frac{16m^2 c^2 - 2m^2 c^2}{2mc^2} = 7mc^2.
  • Since total incident energy E = K + mc^2, the threshold kinetic energy is K_{th} = E_{th} - mc^2 = 7mc^2 - mc^2 = 6mc^2.

Answer: 6mc^2 (or 6 times the rest mass energy of a proton, approximately 5.63 GeV)

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes relativistic mechanics apply identically to point-like elementary particles without extended classical boundary corrections.
  • Quantum field theory framework assumes perturbative convergence, which fails at extremely high energies where strong coupling limits are reached.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Neutrons and protons are elementary particles because they cannot be broken down chemically.

Correct: Neutrons and protons are composite particles made of three valence quarks.

High-energy deep inelastic scattering experiments reveal point-like constituents (quarks) inside nucleons.

Common error: Strangeness is always conserved in all particle decays.

Correct: Strangeness is violated in weak interactions (by \Delta S = \pm 1).

While strong and electromagnetic forces conserve strangeness, the weak force permits quark flavor changing.

Quick revision

  • Hadrons feel the strong force; leptons do not.
  • Baryons consist of 3 quarks; mesons consist of a quark-antiquark pair.
  • Electric charge, baryon number, and lepton numbers are universally conserved.
  • Strangeness is conserved in strong and electromagnetic interactions but violated in weak interactions.

Must remember

  • Hadrons participate in strong interactions and are divided into Baryons ($qqq$, fermions, half-integral spin) and Mesons ($q\bar{q}$, bosons, integral spin).
  • Leptons (electron, muon, tau and their neutrinos) are fundamental fermions with spin $1/2$ that do not participate in strong interactions.
  • Total electric charge ($Q$), baryon number ($B$), and individual lepton numbers ($L_e, L_\mu, L_\tau$) are conserved in all interactions.
  • Strangeness ($S$) and isospin ($I_3$) are conserved in strong and electromagnetic interactions but can be violated by $\Delta S = \pm 1$ in weak interactions.
  • Baryons are composed of three quarks ($qqq$) and mesons are composed of a quark-antiquark pair ($q\bar{q}$).
  • The Gell-Mann-Nishijima formula relates electric charge, isospin, baryon number, and strangeness as $Q = I_3 + \frac{B + S}{2}$.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Which of the following subatomic particles is classified as a lepton?

Explanation: Muons are fundamental leptons with spin $1/2$ that do not participate in strong interactions, whereas protons, neutrons, and pions are hadrons.

2. Hadrons composed of a quark-antiquark pair ($q\bar{q}$) are known as:

Explanation: Mesons are composite hadrons consisting of one quark and one antiquark, possessing integral spin.

3. Which conservation law is violated in weak interactions?

Explanation: Strangeness ($S$) is conserved in strong and electromagnetic interactions but can be violated by $\Delta S = \pm 1$ in weak interactions.

4. What is the quark composition of a free proton?

Explanation: A proton is a baryon composed of two up quarks and one down quark ($u u d$), yielding a total charge of $+1e$.

5. Which of the following mediators is responsible for the electromagnetic interaction?

Explanation: Electromagnetic interactions are mediated by photons. Gluons mediate strong forces, and $W^{\pm}/Z^0$ mediate weak forces.

6. According to the Gell-Mann-Nishijima formula $Q = $I_{3}$ + \frac{B + S}{2}$, what is the strangeness $S$ of a proton ($Q=1, B=1, $I_{3}$=1/2$)?

Explanation: Substituting $Q=1$, $B=1$, and $$I_{3}$=1/2$ into $1 = 1/2 + (1+S)/2$ yields $S = 0$.

7. Which particle has a baryon number $B = 1$?

Explanation: Neutrons are baryons (made of 3 quarks) and carry a baryon number $B = 1$. Leptons and gauge bosons have $B = 0$.

8. Which of the following sets consists entirely of leptons?

Explanation: Neutrinos, muons, and taus are all leptons. Protons, neutrons, pions, kaons, and quarks are hadrons.

9. What is the quark composition of a neutron?

Explanation: A neutron consists of one up quark and two down quarks ($u d d$), giving a net charge of zero.

10. Which interaction is responsible for radioactive $\beta$ decay?

Explanation: $\beta$ decay involves quark flavor changing and is mediated by the weak interaction ($W^{\pm}$ bosons).

11. What is the spin characteristic of all leptons?

Explanation: All known leptons are fermions possessing a spin of $1/2$ and obeying Fermi-Dirac statistics.

12. Which quantum number is strictly conserved across all known interactions (strong, electromagnetic, and weak)?

Explanation: Baryon number ($B$), electric charge ($Q$), and lepton numbers are strictly conserved in all known interactions. Parity and strangeness are violated in weak interactions.

13. Color charge (red, green, blue) is associated with which fundamental interaction?

Explanation: Color charge is the fundamental charge of quarks and gluons in Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), governing the strong interaction.

14. What is the lepton number $$L_{e}$$ for an electron?

Explanation: The electron carries an electronic lepton number $$L_{e}$ = +1$. Its antiparticle (positron) carries $$L_{e}$ = -1$.

15. The rest mass energy equation for a relativistic particle is given by $E^2 = p^2 c^2 + m_0^2 c^4$. For a massless particle like a photon:

Explanation: Setting rest mass $m_0 = 0$ in the relativistic energy-momentum invariant gives $E^2 = p^2 c^2$, or $E = p c$.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. Which of the following particle reactions is forbidden by the conservation of lepton number?

Explanation: In $\$\pi$^+ \to e^+ + \nu_\mu$, initial lepton number is $0$ (pion is a meson). Final state has positron ($$L_{e}$ = -1$) and muon neutrino ($L_\mu = +1$), but individual lepton numbers must be conserved separately, making this reaction invalid without matching lepton flavors.

2. In the quark model, what is the combination of quarks for a $\$\pi$^+$ meson?

Explanation: A $\$\pi$^+$ meson has charge $+1e$. An up quark ($u$) has charge $+2/3e$ and an anti-down quark ($\bar{d}$) has charge $+1/3e$. Their combination $u \bar{d}$ gives charge $+1e$.

3. Which of the following interactions conserves strangeness ($S$)?

Explanation: Reaction $\$\pi$^- + p \to K^0 + \Lambda^0$ is a strong interaction where initial strangeness is $0 + 0 = 0$, and final strangeness is $(+1) + (-1) = 0$, thus strangeness is conserved.

4. What are the baryon number ($B$) and strangeness ($S$) of a $\Lambda^0$ hyperon?

Explanation: The $\Lambda^0$ baryon is composed of quarks $u d s$. Since it contains three quarks, $B = 1$. Since it contains one strange quark, $S = -1$.

5. Which particle exchange mediates the weak neutral current interactions?

Explanation: Weak charged currents are mediated by $W^{\pm}$ bosons, whereas weak neutral currents are mediated by the $Z^0$ boson.

6. A particle has quark content $u u s$. What are its electric charge $Q$ and baryon number $B$?

Explanation: For quark content $u u s$: $Q = \frac{2}{3} + \frac{2}{3} - \frac{1}{3} = +1e$. Since it has three quarks, baryon number $B = 1$.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following decays is strictly forbidden by baryon number conservation?

Explanation: Proton decay $p \to e^+ + \$\gamma$$ violates baryon number conservation ($B = 1$ initially, $B = 0$ finally). Proton is experimentally stable against such decay.

2. Calculate the Q-value of a reaction where initial rest mass energy is $2000\text{ MeV}$ and final rest mass energy is $1990\text{ MeV}$.

Explanation: Using $Q = (\sum m_{\text{initial}} - \sum m_{\text{final}}) c^2$, $Q = 2000 - 1990 = +10\text{ MeV}$.

3. Which quark flavor has a fractional electric charge of $-\frac{1}{3}e$ and strangeness $S = -1$?

Explanation: The strange quark ($s$) carries an electric charge of $-\frac{1}{3}e$ and a strangeness quantum number $S = -1$.

4. What is the third component of isospin ($$I_{3}$$) for a neutron ($u d d$)?

Explanation: An up quark has $$I_{3}$ = +1/2$ and a down quark has $$I_{3}$ = -1/2$. For neutron ($u d d$), $$I_{3}$ = +1/2 - 1/2 - 1/2 = -1/2$.

5. Which of the fundamental interactions has the strongest coupling constant at short distances?

Explanation: The strong interaction has the largest relative strength (coupling constant $\approx 1$), making it the strongest fundamental force.

6. An unstable particle has a proper mean lifetime $\tau = 2 \times 10^{-8}\text{ s}$. If there are $N_0$ particles initially, how many remain after time $t = 2 \times 10^{-8}\text{ s}$?

Explanation: Using $N(t) = N_0 e^{-t / \tau}$, when $t = \tau$, $N(\tau) = N_0 e^{-1} = N_0 / e$.

7. Which of the following particles is classified as a boson?

Explanation: Pions are mesons composed of a quark-antiquark pair, giving them an integral spin ($0$), hence they are bosons. Electrons, protons, and fermions have half-integral spin.

8. What is the baryon number of an anti-proton ($\bar{p}$)?

Explanation: Antiparticles of baryons have opposite baryon numbers; thus, an anti-proton has $B = -1$.

9. Which force is completely negligible in particle physics reactions at subatomic scales?

Explanation: Gravitational interaction between elementary particles is extremely weak and negligible compared to other fundamental forces at subatomic scales.

10. Which conservation law prevents a free electron from decaying into a photon and a neutrino?

Explanation: An electron has $$L_{e}$ = 1$. A photon has $$L_{e}$ = 0$. If a neutrino is produced, lepton number is conserved, but kinematic and charge constraints require strict adherence to individual lepton family numbers ($$L_{e}$ = 1$ initially must equal $$L_{e}$$ of final lepton).
