Linear Accelerator

A linear accelerator (Linac) is a particle accelerator that propels charged particles to high speeds along a straight linear path by applying a series of oscillating electric potentials. It avoids the energy losses due to synchrotron radiation that plague circular accelerators.

Prerequisites

  • Motion of charged particles in uniform and alternating electric fields
  • Lorentz force equation
  • Radio frequency (RF) oscillators and electromagnetic wave resonance

Working Principle of Drift Tube Linac (Alvarez Structure)

  • Consists of a sequence of hollow cylindrical metal tubes (drift tubes) placed inside a resonant cavity along a common axis.
  • Alternating radio-frequency (RF) voltage is applied between successive drift tubes, creating an accelerating electric field in the gaps.
  • Inside the drift tubes, the electric field is zero (shielding effect), so particles coast at a constant velocity without deceleration during the retarding half-cycle.

Phase Stability and Bunching

  • Particles must arrive at the accelerating gaps when the RF electric field has the correct magnitude and direction.
  • Synchronous particles arrive at an equilibrium phase angle where they gain the exact design energy per gap.
  • Particles arriving slightly early or late experience restoring electric forces that bunch them around the synchronous phase, ensuring beam stability.

Velocity and Length Progression of Drift Tubes

  • As the charged particle gains kinetic energy across each gap, its velocity increases progressively.
  • To maintain synchronism with the constant RF frequency, each successive drift tube must be made longer than the preceding one.
  • The length of the n-th drift tube is proportional to the particle velocity at that stage.

Advantages and Limitations

  • Eliminates synchrotron radiation losses, allowing very high energy electrons to be accelerated cleanly.
  • Can achieve extremely high beam currents and is widely used for medical therapy and as injectors for synchrotrons.
  • Physical length can become excessively large for very high energies, requiring immense real estate.

Derivations

Derivation of Drift Tube Length

Conditions: Non-relativistic charged particle of mass m and charge q accelerated by an RF oscillator of frequency f.

  • Let v_n be the velocity of the particle after passing through n gaps. The time taken to traverse the n-th drift tube of length L_n is t_n = L_n / v_n.
  • For synchronism, this transit time must be equal to half the period of the RF oscillation: t_n = T / 2 = 1 / (2f).
  • Equating the two expressions for time: L_n / v_n = 1 / (2f), which gives the length of the n-th drift tube as L_n = v_n / (2f).

Result: L_n = \frac{v_n}{2f}

Total Kinetic Energy after N Gaps

Conditions: Particle starting from rest with charge q and passing through N identical gaps with peak potential V_0.

  • The energy gained in a single gap is given by \Delta E = q V_0 \cos\phi_s, where \phi_s is the synchronous phase angle.
  • Assuming the phase angle is adjusted such that \cos\phi_s \approx 1 for maximum acceleration, the energy gained per gap is q V_0.
  • For N acceleration gaps, the total kinetic energy accumulated by the particle is the product of the number of gaps and the energy gain per gap.

Result: E_k = N q V_0

Solved numericals

TGT core

In a linear accelerator, protons are accelerated through a series of gaps. If the peak voltage across each gap is 100 kV and there are 50 gaps, calculate the total kinetic energy gained by a proton in MeV.

  • Identify given values: Number of gaps N = 50, Peak voltage V_0 = 100 kV = 10^5 V, Charge of proton q = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} C.
  • Apply total energy formula: E = N q V_0.
  • Substitute values: E = 50 \times (1.6 \times 10^{-19} C) \times (10^5 V) = 8 \times 10^{-13} J.
  • Convert joules to MeV: E = (8 \times 10^{-13}) / (1.6 \times 10^{-13}) = 5 MeV.

Answer: 5 MeV

PGT extension

Find the length of the 4th drift tube in a linear accelerator operating at an RF frequency of 200 MHz, given that a proton has attained a velocity of 3 \times 10^7 m/s at that stage.

  • Identify given parameters: Frequency f = 200 MHz = 2 \times 10^8 Hz, Velocity v_4 = 3 \times 10^7 m/s.
  • Use the drift tube length formula for the n-th tube: L_n = v_n / (2f).
  • Substitute the given values: L_4 = (3 \times 10^7) / (2 \times 2^8 \times 10^6 ... wait, 2 \times 200 \times 10^6) = (3 \times 10^7) / (4 \times 10^8).
  • Calculate result: L_4 = 3 / 40 = 0.075 m = 7.5 cm.

Answer: 0.075 m (7.5 cm)

PGT extension

Calculate the mass of an electron required to reach a relativistic velocity where its total energy is 10 times its rest mass energy inside a linear accelerator, given rest mass m_0 = 9.1 \times 10^{-31} kg.

  • Relativistic total energy is given by E = \gamma m_0 c^2.
  • Given condition: E = 10 E_0 = 10 m_0 c^2, which implies the Lorentz factor \gamma = 10.
  • Relativistic mass m is related to rest mass by m = \gamma m_0.
  • Substitute values: m = 10 \times 9.1 \times 10^{-31} kg = 9.1 \times 10^{-30} kg.

Answer: 9.1 \times 10^{-30} kg

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes uniform electric field across the accelerating gaps, neglecting fringe field effects at high frequencies.
  • Non-relativistic formulas fail at very high energies where particle velocities approach the speed of light, requiring velocity-dependent relativistic corrections for drift tube sizing.

Common misconceptions

Common error: Particles are accelerated while passing through the interior of the drift tubes.

Correct: Particles are shielded inside the drift tubes and experience zero electric field; acceleration occurs only across the gaps between tubes.

Electrostatic shielding inside hollow conductors ensures no net electric force acts on the particle inside the drift tube.

Common error: All drift tubes in a linear accelerator have the exact same length.

Correct: Drift tubes must progressively increase in length as the particle speeds up to maintain synchronism with the fixed RF frequency.

Since velocity increases after each gap, a longer tube is required to maintain the correct half-period traversal time.

Quick revision

  • Linacs accelerate charged particles along a straight line using RF electric fields across alternating drift tube gaps, avoiding synchrotron radiation losses.
  • Drift Tube Length: L_n = \frac{v_n}{2f}
  • Total Kinetic Energy: E = N q V_0

Must remember

  • Particles in a drift tube linear accelerator are accelerated only across the gaps between drift tubes by the alternating RF electric field.
  • Inside the hollow drift tubes, the electric field is zero due to electrostatic shielding, allowing particles to coast at constant velocity during retarding half-cycles.
  • The length of the n-th drift tube is given by $L_n = \frac{v_n}{2f}$, meaning tubes must progressively increase in length as particle velocity increases.
  • Linear accelerators eliminate synchrotron radiation losses, making them ideal for high-energy electron acceleration.
  • Phase stability ensures that particles arriving slightly early or late experience restoring electric forces to bunch around the synchronous phase.

Glossary

Drift TubeA hollow cylindrical metal tube inside a resonant cavity that shields charged particles from retarding electric fields.
Synchronous PhaseThe equilibrium phase angle of the RF electric field at which particles gain the exact design energy per gap.
Transit Time FactorAn efficiency factor $T_t = \frac{\sin(\theta/2)}{\theta/2}$ accounting for the variation of the gap field during particle transit.
Lorentz Factor ($\gamma$)A relativistic factor defined as $\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}$ used for high-velocity particle energy calculations.

Practice Quiz

Choose an option to reveal the explanation.

1. Where does the actual acceleration of charged particles take place in a linear accelerator (Alvarez structure)?

Explanation: Particles are shielded inside the drift tubes where the electric field is zero. Acceleration occurs only across the gaps between tubes due to the alternating RF electric field.

2. Why do successive drift tubes in a linear accelerator increase in length?

Explanation: As the charged particle gains kinetic energy, its velocity increases. To ensure it takes the same half-period ($1/2f$) to traverse each tube, each successive tube must be longer ($$L_{n}$ = v_n / 2f$).

3. What is the electric field inside a hollow metallic drift tube?

Explanation: Due to electrostatic shielding inside hollow conductors, the electric field is zero, ensuring particles coast without deceleration during retarding half-cycles.

4. The total non-relativistic kinetic energy $E$ acquired after $N$ gaps with peak gap voltage $$V_{0}$$ and charge $q$ is given by:

Explanation: In an Alvarez linac with optimum phase alignment, the total kinetic energy is the sum of energy gains across $N$ gaps, giving $E = N q $V_{0}$$.

5. What is the primary advantage of using a linear accelerator for electrons compared to circular accelerators like synchrotrons?

Explanation: Circular accelerators cause charged particles to radiate energy (synchrotron radiation) when deflected. Linear accelerators eliminate these losses, allowing clean high-energy electron beams.

6. The transit time factor $T_t$ for a gap is expressed as:

Explanation: The transit time factor is defined as $T_t = \frac{\sin(\$\theta$/2)}{\$\theta$/2}$, where $\$\theta$$ is the transit angle across the gap.

7. What is the main limitation of high-energy linear accelerators?

Explanation: For very high energies, the physical length of a linac becomes excessively large because particles travel in a straight line, requiring immense real estate.

8. If the RF frequency of a linac is $f$ and the velocity of a particle inside the $n$-th tube is $v_n$, what is the length of the $n$-th tube ($$L_{n}$$)?

Explanation: The time spent inside each drift tube must equal half the RF period ($T/2 = 1/2f$). Therefore, $$L_{n}$ = v_n \times (1/2f) = \frac{v_n}{2f}$.

9. What happens to particles arriving slightly early or late relative to the synchronous particle in an RF linac?

Explanation: Phase stability provides restoring forces for particles arriving slightly off-phase, bunching them around the synchronous phase for beam stability.

10. The relativistic energy expression for high-velocity particles in an electron linac is given by:

Explanation: The kinetic energy of relativistic particles is given by $E = (\$\gamma$ - 1) m_0 c^2$, where $\$\gamma$$ is the Lorentz factor and $m_0$ is the rest mass.

11. What is the non-relativistic velocity $v_N$ of a particle after passing through $N$ accelerating gaps with peak voltage $$V_{0}$$?

Explanation: Equating kinetic energy $\frac{1}{2} m v_N^2 = N q $V_{0}$$ gives $v_N = \sqrt{\frac{2 N q $V_{0}$}{m}}$.

12. Which of the following components is NOT part of a standard Alvarez drift tube linac structure?

Explanation: A linear accelerator accelerates particles in a straight line, so circular bending magnets are not used (unlike cyclotrons or synchrotrons).

13. What determines the synchronous phase angle $\$\phi_{s}$$ in a linear accelerator?

Explanation: The synchronous phase condition is given by $W_{n+1} - W_n = q $V_{0}$ \cos\$\phi_{s}$$, which dictates the design energy gain per gap.

14. Why is the magnetic field component generally neglected when calculating the force on a particle in a linac gap?

Explanation: In standard linac gaps, the time-varying electric field $E(t)$ provides the primary accelerating force $F = q E(t)$.

15. Which misconception about linear accelerators is corrected by drift tube shielding principles?

Explanation: A common misconception is that particles accelerate inside tubes; electrostatic shielding ensures acceleration occurs only across gaps.

PYQ Patterns

These are pattern-based practice questions. Authentic year labels are only used when verified from a source.

1. In an Alvarez type linear accelerator operating at a constant RF frequency $f$, if a non-relativistic proton's velocity in the $n$-th drift tube is $v_n$, the length of the drift tube $$L_{n}$$ must satisfy:

Explanation: To maintain synchronism, the time taken to traverse the $n$-th drift tube must equal half the RF period ($1/2f$). Thus, $$L_{n}$ = v_n \times \frac{1}{2f} = \frac{v_n}{2f}$.

2. A charged particle of mass $m$ and charge $q$ passes through $N$ accelerating gaps in a linear accelerator, each having a peak voltage $$V_{0}$$. Assuming non-relativistic motion, the final velocity $v_N$ is proportional to:

Explanation: From the formula $v_N = \sqrt{\frac{2 N q $V_{0}$}{m}}$, the final velocity $v_N$ is directly proportional to the square root of the number of gaps $\sqrt{N}$.

3. What is the physical reason for making successive drift tubes progressively longer in a linear accelerator?

Explanation: As particles gain energy across gaps, their velocity increases. Since the RF frequency is fixed, longer tubes are required so the particle takes exactly half a cycle to pass through each tube.

4. The transit time factor $T_t$ in a linear accelerator gap accounts for:

Explanation: The transit time factor $T_t = \frac{\sin(\$\theta$/2)}{\$\theta$/2}$ corrects for the fact that the electric field changes during the time the particle is crossing the gap.

5. In high-energy electron linear accelerators, why is the relativistic energy formula $E = (\$\gamma$ - 1) m_0 c^2$ used instead of the classical kinetic energy formula $\frac{1}{2}mv^2$?

Explanation: High-energy electrons attain speeds comparable to $c$, making relativistic effects and the Lorentz factor $\$\gamma$$ essential for accurate energy calculations.

6. Phase stability in a drift tube linac ensures that:

Explanation: Phase stability provides restoring forces that keep off-phase particles oscillating stably around the synchronous phase.

Timed Topic Test

Attempt these questions under a ten-minute limit using a separate timer.

1. Which of the following statements regarding the electric field in an Alvarez linac is correct?

Explanation: The RF electric field alternates in time in the gaps and is shielded to zero inside the hollow drift tubes.

2. If a proton is accelerated through 10 gaps with a peak gap voltage of $50\text{ kV}$, what is its total non-relativistic kinetic energy?

Explanation: Using $E = N q $V_{0}$$, $E = 10 \times (1\text{ elementary charge}) \times 50\text{ kV} = 500\text{ keV}$.

3. What phenomenon is successfully avoided in linear accelerators that limits circular electron accelerators?

Explanation: Linear accelerators do not bend particle trajectories into circles, entirely eliminating synchrotron radiation losses.

4. If the velocity of a particle in the 4th drift tube is $2 \times 10^7\text{ m/s}$ and the RF frequency is $200\text{ MHz}$ ($2 \times 10^8\text{ Hz}$), what is the length of the 4th drift tube?

Explanation: Using $$L_{n}$ = \frac{v_n}{2f}$, $$L_{4}$ = \frac{2 \times 10^7}{2 \times 200 \times 10^6} = \frac{2 \times 10^7}{4 \times 10^8} = 0.05\text{ m}$.

5. The synchronous phase condition is expressed as $W_{n+1} - W_n = q $V_{0}$ \cos\$\phi_{s}$$. What does $\$\phi_{s}$$ represent?

Explanation: $\$\phi_{s}$$ is the synchronous phase angle at which the particle arrives relative to the peak of the RF accelerating voltage.

6. In a drift tube linac, what keeps the beam tightly bunched longitudinally?

Explanation: Phase stability creates restoring forces that bunch particles around the synchronous phase.

7. What is the dimensional nature of the transit time factor $T_t$?

Explanation: $T_t = \frac{\sin(\$\theta$/2)}{\$\theta$/2}$ is a ratio of angles/sines, making it a dimensionless efficiency factor.

8. Why are linear accelerators often used as injectors for synchrotrons?

Explanation: Linacs provide clean, high-current beams up to moderate/high injection energies required by large synchrotrons.

9. If a particle's kinetic energy is much smaller than its rest mass energy, which energy expression is appropriate?

Explanation: For low velocities, non-relativistic kinetic energy formulas like $E = N q $V_{0}$$ or $\frac{1}{2}mv^2$ are accurate.

10. Which misconception is explicitly addressed by the working principle of drift tubes?

Explanation: Drift tubes shield particles, ensuring acceleration happens only across gaps, correcting the misconception of continuous internal acceleration.
