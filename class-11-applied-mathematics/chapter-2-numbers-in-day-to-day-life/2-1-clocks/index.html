Introductory Discussion: Time & Distance

Lalita Babar is a three-time winner of the Mumbai Marathon. She won a Bronze medal in the 3000 m steeplechase at the 2014 Asian Games and broke the National record with a time of 9 hours 35 minutes and 37 seconds.

Time To Work:
1. Write the length of steeplechase race in decimeters:
Since $1\text{ meter} = 10\text{ decimeters}$,
$\text{Length} = 3000\text{ m} \times 10 = \mathbf{30,000\text{ decimeters}}$.

2. Convert the time taken into seconds:
$\text{Time} = 9\text{ hours } 35\text{ minutes } 37\text{ seconds}$
$= (9 \times 3600) + (35 \times 60) + 37 = 32400 + 2100 + 37 = \mathbf{34,537\text{ seconds}}$.

For thousands of years, humans measured time by observing the passage of day and night, the position of stars, and the cyclical change of seasons. To achieve higher precision, tools such as Sundials, hourglasses, and water clocks were invented over centuries. The most accurate clock in the world today is the Cesium Fountain Atomic Clock developed at NIST Laboratories in Colorado, USA.

Example 1 (CBSE Support Material): What would be the time four and a half hours before 2:15 pm?
Show Step-by-Step Solution
$\text{Target Time} = 2:15\text{ pm} - 4\frac{1}{2}\text{ hours}$
$= 2:15\text{ pm} - (4\text{ hours} + 30\text{ minutes})$
$= 2:15\text{ pm} - 4\text{ hours} - 30\text{ minutes}$
$= 10:15\text{ am} - 30\text{ minutes} = \mathbf{9:45\text{ am}}$.

Structure of a Clock & Angular Kinematics

HOROLOGY: Study and measurement of time & clock-making

A typical analog clock face represents a circular dial of $360^\circ$, divided into 12 major hour intervals (each $30^\circ$) and 60 minute intervals (each $6^\circ$). It is equipped with three concentric hands: the Hour hand, Minute hand, and Seconds hand.

Angular Velocity Derivation:
  • • A complete circular revolution $= 360^\circ$.
  • • Hour Hand: Completes $360^\circ$ in 12 hours $\implies \frac{360^\circ}{12} = \mathbf{30^\circ\text{ per hour}} = \frac{30^\circ}{60} = \mathbf{0.5^\circ\text{ per minute}}$.
  • • Minute Hand: Completes $360^\circ$ in 60 minutes $\implies \frac{360^\circ}{60} = \mathbf{6^\circ\text{ per minute}}$.
  • • Seconds Hand: Completes $360^\circ$ in 60 seconds $\implies \frac{360^\circ}{60} = \mathbf{6^\circ\text{ per second}} = 360^\circ\text{ per minute}$.
Investigation 1:

Calculate the speed of the seconds hand?
Speed $= \frac{360^\circ}{60\text{ s}} = \mathbf{6^\circ/\text{second}}$ (or $360^\circ/\text{minute}$).

Investigation 2:

Find the difference in speed between the minute hand and hour hand?
$\text{Relative Speed} = 6^\circ/\text{min} - 0.5^\circ/\text{min} = \mathbf{5.5^\circ/\text{min}} = \mathbf{\left(\frac{11}{2}\right)^\circ/\text{min}}$.

Collisions (Coincidence) of Hands of a Clock

Two hands collide when the angle between them is $0^\circ$. How many times in a day do the minute and hour hands coincide?

Why Hands Coincide 22 Times in 24 Hours (Not 24 Times):
In a 24-hour day, the hour hand completes two full revolutions. One might intuitively expect $24$ collisions, but between 11:00 and 1:00, the hands collide only once at exactly 12:00 (they do not collide between 11:00 and 12:00). This occurs once in the daytime and once at night.
$$\text{Total Collisions in a Day} = 24 - 2 = \mathbf{22\text{ times}}.$$

Time Between Collisions:
Since 22 collisions occur uniformly across 24 hours:
$$\text{Time of one collision} = \frac{24}{22}\text{ hours} = \frac{12}{11}\text{ hours} = \frac{12 \times 60}{11}\text{ minutes} = \frac{720}{11}\text{ minutes} = \mathbf{65\frac{5}{11}\text{ minutes}} \approx 65.45\text{ minutes}.$$

Time and Angle Formulas

Let $H$ be the running hour and $M$ be the minutes. The angular displacement of the hour hand is $(30H + 0.5M)^\circ$, and that of the minute hand is $6M^\circ$. The angle $\theta$ (or $A$) between them is:

$$\mathbf{A = \left|30H - \frac{11}{2}M\right|}$$ $$\mathbf{T = \frac{2}{11}(30H \pm A)}$$

Note: The angle $A$ is taken as positive for hand movements in the first half (12 to 6) and negative in the second half (6 to 12). For absolute geometric angle, the smaller interior angle is $\le 180^\circ$.

Example 2 (CBSE Support Material): At what time between 4 o'clock and 5 o'clock will the hands of a clock make an angle of $20^\circ$?
Show Step-by-Step Solution
Given: Running hour $H = 4$, target angle $A = 20^\circ$.
Using $T = \frac{2}{11}(H \times 30^\circ \pm A)$:
In the first half (12 to 6), $A$ is taken positive:
$T = \frac{2}{11}[4 \times 30^\circ + 20^\circ] = \frac{2}{11}[120^\circ + 20^\circ] = \frac{2}{11}[140^\circ] = \frac{280^\circ}{11} = 25\frac{5}{11}\text{ minutes}$.
Convert $\frac{5}{11}\text{ minutes}$ to seconds: $\frac{5}{11} \times 60 = \frac{300}{11} \approx 27\text{ seconds}$.
Answer: The hands will make an angle of $20^\circ$ at 4:25:27 (or $25\frac{5}{11}\text{ min past } 4$).
Example 3 (CBSE Support Material): At what time between 3 o'clock and 4 o'clock will the hands of a clock collide?
Show Step-by-Step Solution
At 3 o'clock, the hour hand is at 3 and the minute hand is at 12, so the minute hand is 15 minute spaces behind the hour hand.
Since the minute hand gains 55 minutes in 60 minutes (or $\frac{12}{11}$ min per minute space):
$T = 15 \times \frac{12}{11} = \frac{180}{11} = \mathbf{16\frac{4}{11}\text{ minutes}}$.
Alternatively, using formula with $A = 0^\circ$:
$T = \frac{2}{11}(3 \times 30^\circ) = \frac{180}{11} = 16\frac{4}{11}\text{ minutes}$.
$\frac{4}{11} \times 60 = \frac{240}{11} \approx 22\text{ seconds}$.
Answer: The hands collide at $16\frac{4}{11}$ minutes past 3 (exact time 3:16:22).
Example 4 (CBSE Support Material): Find the angle between the hands of a clock when it struck 15 minutes past 7.
Show Step-by-Step Solution
Given: $H = 7$, $M = 15$.
$A = \left|30H - \frac{11}{2}M\right|$
$= \left|30(7) - \frac{11}{2}(15)\right|$
$= |210^\circ - 82.5^\circ| = \mathbf{127.5^\circ}$.
Answer: The angle formed is $127.5^\circ$ (or $127^\circ 30'$).

Problem 1 Comprehensive Train Problem • 4 Sub-questions

A local train from Mumbai leaves every 40 minutes from the station. When inquired by a passenger, the help desk executive informed that the train had already left 10 minutes ago. If this information was given at 10:15 a.m.:

  1. At what time did the train leave the station?
  2. At what time will the next train leave the station?
  3. For how long will that man have to wait for the next train?
  4. Find the angle formed between the minute hand and the hour hand when the passenger will board the next train?
Show Complete Model Solution
a) Time previous train left:
Information given at 10:15 a.m., 10 minutes after departure:
$\text{Departure time} = 10:15\text{ a.m.} - 10\text{ min} = \mathbf{10:05\text{ a.m.}}$

b) Time next train leaves:
Trains leave every 40 minutes:
$\text{Next departure} = 10:05\text{ a.m.} + 40\text{ min} = \mathbf{10:45\text{ a.m.}}$

c) Waiting duration for passenger:
Passenger is at station at 10:15 a.m., next train is at 10:45 a.m.:
$\text{Wait time} = 10:45 - 10:15 = \mathbf{30\text{ minutes}}$.

d) Angle between hands at 10:45 a.m.:
$H = 10, M = 45$.
Angle $A = \left|30H - \frac{11}{2}M\right| = \left|30(10) - \frac{11}{2}(45)\right| = |300 - 247.5| = \mathbf{52.5^\circ}$ (or $52^\circ 30'$).
Problem 2 CBSE Support Material • 1 Mark

In a day, how many times is a straight angle ($180^\circ$) formed between the minute and hour hands?

Show Step-by-Step Solution
In every 12-hour interval, the hands form a straight angle ($180^\circ$) 11 times (between 5 o'clock and 7 o'clock, they point in opposite directions only once, at exactly 6 o'clock).
Therefore, in a full 24-hour day:
$\text{Occurrences} = 11 \times 2 = \mathbf{22\text{ times}}$.
Problem 3 CBSE Support Material • 1 Mark

In a day, how many times is a right angle ($90^\circ$) formed between the minute and hour hands?

Show Step-by-Step Solution
In 1 hour, hands are normally at right angles twice. However, between 2:00 and 4:00, they make right angles 3 times (instead of 4), and between 8:00 and 10:00, they also make right angles 3 times.
Thus, in 12 hours: $22\text{ times}$.
In a full 24-hour day:
$\text{Occurrences} = 22 \times 2 = \mathbf{44\text{ times}}$.
Problem 4 CBSE Support Material • 1 Mark

Find the angle between the minute hand and the hour hand at 7:40 pm?

Show Step-by-Step Solution
$H = 7, M = 40$.
$A = \left|30H - \frac{11}{2}M\right| = \left|30(7) - \frac{11}{2}(40)\right| = |210 - 220| = |-10^\circ| = \mathbf{10^\circ}$.
Answer: The angle formed is $10^\circ$.
Problem 5 CBSE Support Material • 2 Marks

By 20 minutes past 5, how many degrees has the hour hand turned through starting from 12:00?

Show Step-by-Step Solution
Total time elapsed from 12:00 to 5:20 $= 5\text{ hours } 20\text{ minutes}$
$= (5 \times 60) + 20 = 320\text{ minutes}$.
Speed of hour hand $= 0.5^\circ\text{ per minute}$.
$\text{Angle turned} = 320 \times 0.5^\circ = \mathbf{160^\circ}$.
Alternative Method: $(5 \times 30^\circ) + (20 \times 0.5^\circ) = 150^\circ + 10^\circ = \mathbf{160^\circ}$.
Problem 6 CBSE Support Material • 3 Marks

At what time between 3 o'clock and 4 o'clock are the hands of a clock three degrees ($3^\circ$) apart?

Show Step-by-Step Solution
$H = 3$, $A = 3^\circ$. Using $T = \frac{2}{11}(30H \pm A)$:

Case 1 (Minute hand is $3^\circ$ behind hour hand):
$T = \frac{2}{11}(30 \times 3 - 3) = \frac{2}{11}(90 - 3) = \frac{2}{11}(87) = \frac{174}{11} = \mathbf{15\frac{9}{11}\text{ minutes past } 3}$ (approx. 3:15:49).

Case 2 (Minute hand is $3^\circ$ ahead of hour hand):
$T = \frac{2}{11}(30 \times 3 + 3) = \frac{2}{11}(90 + 3) = \frac{2}{11}(93) = \frac{186}{11} = \mathbf{16\frac{10}{11}\text{ minutes past } 3}$ (approx. 3:16:55).

Answer: The hands are $3^\circ$ apart at $15\frac{9}{11}$ min past 3 and at $16\frac{10}{11}$ min past 3.
Problem 7 CBSE Support Material • 2 Marks

At what time do the hands of the clock meet between 6:00 to 7:00?

Show Step-by-Step Solution
At 6 o'clock, the hour hand is at 6 and the minute hand is at 12 (30 minute spaces apart).
To coincide, the minute hand must gain 30 minute spaces over the hour hand.
$T = 30 \times \frac{12}{11} = \frac{360}{11} = \mathbf{32\frac{8}{11}\text{ minutes past } 6}$.
Convert $\frac{8}{11}\text{ min}$ to seconds: $\frac{8 \times 60}{11} = \frac{480}{11} \approx 43\text{ seconds}$.
Answer: The hands meet at $32\frac{8}{11}$ minutes past 6 (or 6:32:43).
Problem 8 CBSE Support Material • 2 Marks

India is 9 hours and 30 minutes ahead of Ottawa ON, Canada. What is the time in Canada when it is 1:25 am in India?

Show Step-by-Step Solution
Since India is 9 hours and 30 minutes ahead, Canada is 9 hours and 30 minutes behind India.
$\text{Time in Canada} = 1:25\text{ a.m.} - 9\text{ hrs } 30\text{ mins}$
Step 1: Subtract 1 hr 25 mins to reach 12:00 midnight (00:00 of the day).
Step 2: Remaining hours to subtract $= 9\text{ hrs } 30\text{ min} - 1\text{ hr } 25\text{ min} = 8\text{ hrs } 05\text{ min}$ from the previous evening.
Step 3: $12:00\text{ midnight} - 8\text{ hrs } 05\text{ min} = \mathbf{3:55\text{ p.m.}}$ of the previous day.
Answer: It is 3:55 p.m. (previous day) in Ottawa, Canada.

At what time between 8 and 9 o'clock will the hands of a clock be in the same straight line but not together?

Show Solution
Hands are opposite ($180^\circ$). At 8 o'clock, the minute hand is 40 min spaces behind, or the opposite point is at 2 (10 min spaces).

Section C: Advanced Faulty Clock Drills

WS-C1 4 Marks

A watch which gains uniformly was observed to be 5 minutes slow at 8:00 a.m. on Monday, and it was 5 minutes 48 seconds fast at 8:00 p.m. on the following Monday. When was it correct?

Show Solution
Total time from 8 a.m. Monday to 8 p.m. next Monday $= 7\text{ days } 12\text{ hours} = 180\text{ hours}$.
Total gain $= 5\text{ min} + 5\text{ min } 48\text{ sec} = 5 + 5.8 = 10.8\text{ minutes} = \frac{54}{5}\text{ minutes}$.
Time taken to gain 5 minutes (to become correct):
$\text{Hours} = 5 \times \frac{180}{54/5} = \frac{5 \times 180 \times 5}{54} = \frac{4500}{54} = 83\frac{1}{3}\text{ hours} = 3\text{ days } 11\text{ hours } 20\text{ minutes}$.
3 days from Monday 8 a.m. $\to$ Thursday 8 a.m. $+ 11\text{ hrs } 20\text{ min} = \mathbf{7:20\text{ p.m. on Thursday}}$.

Chapter 2.1 Clocks: Quick Revision Sheet

Angular Velocities
  • Minute Hand: $6^\circ/\text{min}$
  • Hour Hand: $30^\circ/\text{hr} = 0.5^\circ/\text{min}$
  • Second Hand: $6^\circ/\text{s} = 360^\circ/\text{min}$
  • Relative Speed: $5.5^\circ/\text{min} = \left(\frac{11}{2}\right)^\circ/\text{min}$
24-Hour Day Statistics
  • Coincidence ($0^\circ$): 22 times
  • Opposite Direction ($180^\circ$): 22 times
  • Right Angles ($90^\circ$): 44 times
  • Time between collisions: $65\frac{5}{11}\text{ minutes}$
Core Formulas to Remember:
1. Angle between hands: $\theta = \left|30H - \frac{11}{2}M\right|$
2. Time for a specific angle $A$: $T = \frac{2}{11}(30H \pm A)$
3. Minute spaces gained: Minute hand gains 55 minute spaces in 60 minutes (Factor: $\frac{60}{55} = \frac{12}{11}$).
Common Examination Pitfalls:
  • Remember that the hour hand does not stay fixed at $H$; it advances by $0.5^\circ$ every single minute!
  • When the question asks for the reflex angle, compute $360^\circ - \theta$.
  • Be cautious with $180^\circ$ and $0^\circ$ between 11:00 and 1:00 (only one collision at 12:00:00).

Q4 Assertion-Reason • 1 Mark

Reason (R): The relative speed of the minute hand with respect to the hour hand is $5.5^\circ$ per minute, meaning they take $65\frac{5}{11}$ minutes between successive coincidences.

B) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A.
