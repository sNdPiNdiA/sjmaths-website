Structure of the Calendar: Ordinary Year vs. Leap Year

A clock apprises us about smaller spans of time (seconds, minutes, hours), whereas a calendar depicts the days, weeks, and months of a specific year.

Parameter Ordinary Year Leap Year
Number of Days 365 Days 366 Days
Number of Weeks 52 Weeks and 1 Day 52 Weeks and 2 Days
Number of Odd Days 1 Odd Day ($365 = 52 \times 7 + 1$) 2 Odd Days ($366 = 52 \times 7 + 2$)
Definition of Odd Day:
The extra day(s) left after counting an exact number of complete 7-day weeks is considered an Odd Day. That is, for any given duration $N$ days:
$$\text{Odd Days} = N \pmod 7 = \text{Remainder when } N \text{ is divided by } 7.$$

Decoding the Day of the Week

Coding and decoding is foundational to modern computing and cryptography (e.g. phone passwords, bank debit cards, hashing). In calendar mathematics, decoding a date into a day of the week is performed via Modulo 7 arithmetic.

0 = Sunday 1 = Monday 2 = Tuesday 3 = Wednesday 4 = Thursday 5 = Friday 6 = Saturday
Example 5 (CBSE Support Material): If $30^{\text{th}}$ March 2020 was Monday. What would be the day after 61 days?
Show Step-by-Step Solution
Reference Date: 30 March 2020
Reference Day: Monday
Duration: 61 days

Step 1: Divide duration by 7:
$\frac{61}{7} = 8\text{ weeks and } 5\text{ days}$ ($61 = 8 \times 7 + 5$).

Step 2: The day after 8 complete weeks ($56^{\text{th}}$ day) is Monday.
$57^{\text{th}}$ day = Tuesday
$58^{\text{th}}$ day = Wednesday
$59^{\text{th}}$ day = Thursday
$60^{\text{th}}$ day = Friday
$61^{\text{st}}$ day = Saturday.

Answer: The $61^{\text{st}}$ day will be a Saturday.

Leap Year Rules & Odd Days in a Century

A normal year is a leap year if it is divisible by 4. Fun Fact: A number is divisible by 4 if its last two digits are divisible by 4. However, for century years (100, 200, 300, 400, 1900, 2000), it must be divisible by 400 to be a leap year.

Illustration: What day of the week was on 31st December 100 A.D.?
  • Step 1: Total number of years $= 100$. Number of leap years $= 24$ (because $100 / 4 = 25$, but century year 100 is not divisible by 400, so $25 - 1 = 24$).
  • Step 2: Number of ordinary years $= 100 - 24 = 76\text{ ordinary years}$.
  • Step 3: Odd days in 100 years $= (76 \times 1) + (24 \times 2) = 76 + 48 = \mathbf{124\text{ days}}$.
  • Step 4: Divide by 7: $124 = (17 \times 7) + \mathbf{5\text{ odd days}}$.
  • Conclusion: 5 odd days corresponds to Friday. Hence, 31st December 100 A.D. was a Friday!

Theorem (Last Day of a Century):
Since centuries have odd days of 5, 3, 1, or 0, the last day of a century can only be Friday, Wednesday, Monday, or Sunday. It can never be Tuesday, Thursday, or Saturday.

Problem 1 CBSE Support Material • 1 Mark

If today is a Tuesday, what will be the day on $7706^{\text{th}}$ day?

Show Step-by-Step Solution
Given: Today = Tuesday.
We need to find the remainder when $7706$ is divided by 7:
$7706 = (7 \times 1100) + 6 = 7700 + 6$.
Remainder $= 6\text{ odd days}$.
Adding 6 days to Tuesday:
$\text{Tuesday} + 6\text{ days} = \mathbf{Monday}$ (or $\text{Tuesday} - 1\text{ day} = \mathbf{Monday}$).
Answer: The $7706^{\text{th}}$ day will be a Monday.
Problem 2 CBSE Support Material • 2 Marks

Find the total number of days from $26^{\text{th}}$ January 2008 to $15^{\text{th}}$ May 2008?

Show Step-by-Step Solution
Note that the year 2008 is a leap year ($08$ is divisible by 4), so February has 29 days.

Counting days (inclusive of both endpoints):
• January ($26^{\text{th}}$ to $31^{\text{st}}$): $31 - 26 + 1 = \mathbf{6\text{ days}}$
• February (leap year): $\mathbf{29\text{ days}}$
• March: $\mathbf{31\text{ days}}$
• April: $\mathbf{30\text{ days}}$
• May ($1^{\text{st}}$ to $15^{\text{th}}$): $\mathbf{15\text{ days}}$

$\text{Total Number of Days} = 6 + 29 + 31 + 30 + 15 = \mathbf{111\text{ days}}$.
(Note: If excluding the start date: $5 + 29 + 31 + 30 + 15 = 110\text{ days}$.)
Answer: 111 days (inclusive).
Problem 3 CBSE Support Material • 2 Marks

If the second day of April month is a Friday, then find the last day of the next month?

Show Step-by-Step Solution
• Second day of April $= \text{April } 2$ is a Friday.
• April has 30 days. Remaining days in April after April 2 $= 30 - 2 = 28\text{ days}$.
• The next month is May, which has 31 days. The last day of May is May 31.
• Total days from April 2 to May 31 $= 28 + 31 = 59\text{ days}$.
• Find odd days: $59 = (8 \times 7) + 3\text{ odd days}$.
• Add 3 days to Friday: $\text{Friday} + 1 = \text{Saturday}, \text{Friday} + 2 = \text{Sunday}, \text{Friday} + 3 = \mathbf{Monday}$.
Answer: The last day of May is a Monday.
Problem 4 CBSE Support Material • 4 Sub-questions • 4 Marks

Workout the day of the week on the given dates:

(a) 15th August 1947
(b) 22nd November 2025
(c) 21st September 2080
(d) 18th October 2100
Show Step-by-Step Solutions for All 4 Dates
(a) 15th August 1947:
• Completed years $= 1946 = 1600 + 300 + 46$.
• 1600 years $= 0$ odd days.
• 300 years $= 1$ odd day.
• 46 years: $46/4 = 11\text{ leap} + 35\text{ ordinary} \implies (11 \times 2) + 35 = 57 \equiv 1\text{ odd day}$.
• Year 1947 up to 15 Aug: Jan(3) + Feb(0) + Mar(3) + Apr(2) + May(3) + Jun(2) + Jul(3) + Aug 15 ($15 \equiv 1$) $= 17 \equiv 3\text{ odd days}$.
• Total odd days $= 0 + 1 + 1 + 3 = 5\text{ odd days}$. Code 5 corresponds to Friday.

(b) 22nd November 2025:
• Completed years $= 2024 = 2000 + 24$.
• 2000 years $= 0$ odd days.
• 24 years: $24/4 = 6\text{ leap} + 18\text{ ordinary} \implies (6 \times 2) + 18 = 30 \equiv 2\text{ odd days}$.
• Year 2025 up to 22 Nov: Jan(3) + Feb(0) + Mar(3) + Apr(2) + May(3) + Jun(2) + Jul(3) + Aug(3) + Sep(2) + Oct(3) + Nov 22 ($22 \equiv 1$) $= 25 \equiv 4\text{ odd days}$.
• Total odd days $= 0 + 2 + 4 = 6\text{ odd days}$. Code 6 corresponds to Saturday.

(c) 21st September 2080:
• Completed years $= 2079 = 2000 + 79$.
• 2000 years $= 0$. 79 years: $79/4 = 19\text{ leap} + 60\text{ ordinary} \implies (19 \times 2) + 60 = 98 \equiv 0\text{ odd days}$.
• Year 2080 is a leap year: Jan(3) + Feb(1) + Mar(3) + Apr(2) + May(3) + Jun(2) + Jul(3) + Aug(3) + Sep 21 ($21 \equiv 0$) $= 20 \equiv 6\text{ odd days}$.
• Total odd days $= 0 + 0 + 6 = 6\text{ odd days}$. Code 6 corresponds to Saturday.

(d) 18th October 2100:
• Completed years $= 2099 = 2000 + 99$.
• 2000 years $= 0$. 99 years: $99/4 = 24\text{ leap} + 75\text{ ordinary} \implies (24 \times 2) + 75 = 123 \equiv 4\text{ odd days}$.
• Year 2100 is NOT a leap year ($2100$ is not divisible by 400).
• Year 2100 up to 18 Oct: Jan(3) + Feb(0) + Mar(3) + Apr(2) + May(3) + Jun(2) + Jul(3) + Aug(3) + Sep(2) + Oct 18 ($18 \equiv 4$) $= 25 \equiv 4\text{ odd days}$.
• Total odd days $= 0 + 4 + 4 = 8 \equiv 1\text{ odd day}$. Code 1 corresponds to Monday.
Problem 5 CBSE Support Material • Logical Reasoning • 2 Marks

Pranil works in an electronics goods shop and the shop has offered 30% discount on MRP on all goods in the month of October. On top of it, the shop gives successive discount of 10% if the person uses e-payment mode. Pranil remembers that the maximum sale occurred after 17th October but before 21st October, while his colleague remembers that the maximum sale happened after 19th October but before 24th October. The owner listens to both of them and concludes a date which is precisely common. What is the date as per your point of view?

Show Step-by-Step Solution
• According to Pranil, maximum sale date $\in \{18^{\text{th}}, 19^{\text{th}}, 20^{\text{th}}\}$.
• According to his colleague, maximum sale date $\in \{20^{\text{th}}, 21^{\text{st}}, 22^{\text{nd}}, 23^{\text{rd}}\}$.
• The owner takes the date common to both observations:
$\text{Common Date} = \{18, 19, 20\} \cap \{20, 21, 22, 23\} = \mathbf{20^{\text{th}}\text{ October}}$.
Answer: 20th October.
Problem 6 CBSE Support Material • Real-World Case • 3 Marks

The Indian government announced a complete lockdown for the entire country from the Midnight of 25th March 2020 till the midnight of 14th April 2020 due to the pandemic disease Covid-19 outbreak. But keeping in view the grim situation, the lockdown was further extended till 3rd May 2020.
(a) Calculate the total number of days for which the lockdown lasted.
(b) If 25th March 2020 was a Wednesday, then find which day of the week will fall on 3rd May 2020.

Show Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Total number of days for which the lockdown lasted:
• From Midnight of 25th March means effective lockdown began on 26th March.
• Days in March (26, 27, 28, 29, 30, 31): $31 - 25 = \mathbf{6\text{ days}}$.
• Full month of April: $\mathbf{30\text{ days}}$.
• Days in May (up to 3rd May): $\mathbf{3\text{ days}}$.
$\text{Total Days} = 6 + 30 + 3 = \mathbf{39\text{ days}}$.
(Note: If 25th March itself is included as day 1, total $= 7 + 30 + 3 = 40\text{ days}$.)

(b) Day of the week on 3rd May 2020:
• Reference Day: 25th March 2020 is a Wednesday.
• Days elapsed from 25th March to 3rd May $= 39\text{ days}$.
• Odd days $= 39 \pmod 7 = (5 \times 7) + 4\text{ odd days}$.
• Add 4 days to Wednesday: $\text{Wednesday} + 4 = \mathbf{Sunday}$.
Answer: (a) 39 days (or 40 days inclusive), (b) Sunday.

How many odd days are there in 500 consecutive years?

Show Solution

WS-A2 1 Mark

Which of the following days can never be the last day of a century: (a) Friday, (b) Wednesday, (c) Tuesday, (d) Sunday?

Show Solution
Century odd days are 5 (Friday), 3 (Wednesday), 1 (Monday), 0 (Sunday). Hence, the last day can never be Tuesday (Option c).

Section B: Calendar Repetition & Intervals

WS-B1 2 Marks

The calendar for the year 2021 will be identical to which future year?

Show Solution
2021 is an ordinary year following a leap year ($2020 + 1$). Ordinary years immediately following a leap year repeat after 6 years.
$2021 + 6 = \mathbf{2027}$.
Verification of odd days: 2021(1) + 2022(1) + 2023(1) + 2024(2) + 2025(1) + 2026(1) $= 7 \equiv 0$ odd days.
WS-B2 3 Marks

Prove that in any normal year, the months of January and October always start on the exact same day of the week.

Show Solution
Count odd days from January 1 to October 1:
Jan (3) + Feb (0, non-leap) + Mar (3) + Apr (2) + May (3) + Jun (2) + Jul (3) + Aug (3) + Sep (2) $= 21\text{ days}$.
$21 \pmod 7 = 0\text{ odd days}$.
Since the net odd days between Jan 1 and Oct 1 is 0, both months begin on the exact same day of the week!

Chapter 2.2 Calendar: Quick Revision Sheet

Year Classification
  • Ordinary Year: 365 days = 52 wks + 1 odd day
  • Leap Year: 366 days = 52 wks + 2 odd days
  • Divisibility by 4 rule: Last two digits divisible by 4
  • Century rule: Must be divisible by 400
Century Cycles & Last Days
  • 100 years: 5 odd days (Friday)
  • 200 years: 3 odd days (Wednesday)
  • 300 years: 1 odd day (Monday)
  • 400 years: 0 odd days (Sunday)
Month Odd Days Reference:
• 3 Odd Days: Jan, Mar, May, Jul, Aug, Oct, Dec (31-day months)
• 2 Odd Days: Apr, Jun, Sep, Nov (30-day months)
• 0 or 1 Odd Day: Feb (28 days non-leap = 0; 29 days leap = 1)
Calendar Repetition Rules:
  • Leap Year: Repeats after +28 years (e.g., 2024 $\to$ 2052).
  • Leap Year + 1: Repeats after +6 years (e.g., 2025 $\to$ 2031).
  • Leap Year + 2 or + 3: Repeats after +11 years (e.g., 2026 $\to$ 2037).

Case Study: Pandemic Lockdown Timeline & Retail Schedule Analysis
The Indian government announced a nationwide lockdown from the midnight of 25th March 2020 till 14th April 2020, later extended up to 3rd May 2020. During this period, an online retailer held an inventory clearance sale. The store manager recalls that maximum sales happened after 17th October but before 21st October, while the logistics head recalls it was after 19th October but before 24th October.
