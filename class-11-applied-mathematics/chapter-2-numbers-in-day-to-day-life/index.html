Clocks & Calendars

  • Minute Hand Speed: $6^\circ/\text{min}$ ($360^\circ$ in 60 mins)

  • Relative Speed of Hands: $5.5^\circ/\text{min} = \left(\frac{11}{2}\right)^\circ/\text{min}$
  • Angle Between Hands: $$\theta = \left|30H - \frac{11}{2}M\right|$$
  • Alignment Frequencies (24 hrs): Coinciding: $22\text{ times} \quad | \quad$ Opposite: $22\text{ times} \quad | \quad$ Straight line: $44\text{ times} \quad | \quad$ Right angle: $44\text{ times}$
  • Years & Odd Days: Ordinary Year: $365\text{ days} = 52\text{ wks} + 1\text{ odd day}$
    Leap Year: $366\text{ days} = 52\text{ wks} + 2\text{ odd days}$
  • Century Odd Days: $100\to 5, \quad 200\to 3, \quad 300\to 1, \quad 400\to 0$

Time, Work & Cisterns

  • Fundamental Work Law: $$\text{Work} = \text{Rate} \times \text{Time}$$
  • Two Workers Combined: $$\frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{A} + \frac{1}{B} \implies T = \frac{AB}{A + B}$$
  • Three Workers Combined: $$T = \frac{ABC}{AB + BC + CA}$$
  • Pipes & Cisterns Net Rate: $$\text{Net Rate} = \frac{1}{\text{Inlet}} - \frac{1}{\text{Leak/Outlet}}$$
  • Compound Proportions: $$\frac{M_1 D_1 H_1}{W_1} = \frac{M_2 D_2 H_2}{W_2}$$
  • Wage Distribution Rule: Wages are divided strictly in direct proportion to the quantum of work completed by each individual.

Speed, Motion & Seating

  • Speed Conversion: $$1\text{ km/h} = \frac{5}{18}\text{ m/s}, \quad 1\text{ m/s} = \frac{18}{5}\text{ km/h}$$

  • Relative Speeds: Opposite directions: $u + v$

Q1 Time & Work 1 Mark
80 labourers, working 7 hours a day can finish a piece of work in 18 days. If the labourers work 6 hours a day, then the number of labourers to finish the same piece of work in 30 days, will be:
(a) 15
(b) 56
(c) 22
(d) 25
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (b)

Method — Compound Proportionality / Total Person-Hours:

Total work is constant and given by the product of Men ($M$), Days ($D$), and Daily Hours ($H$):

$$W = M_1 \times D_1 \times H_1 = M_2 \times D_2 \times H_2$$

Given data:

  • $M_1 = 80$, $D_1 = 18\text{ days}$, $H_1 = 7\text{ hours/day}$
  • $M_2 = ?$, $D_2 = 30\text{ days}$, $H_2 = 6\text{ hours/day}$

Equating the work expressions:

$$80 \times 18 \times 7 = M_2 \times 30 \times 6$$

$$10080 = 180 M_2 \implies M_2 = \frac{10080}{180} = \mathbf{56}$$

Answer: (b) 56 labourers.

Q2 Pipes & Cisterns 1 Mark
If $\frac{3}{5}$ of a cistern is filled in 1 minute, how much more time will be required to fill the rest of it?
(a) 30 sec
(b) 40 sec
(c) 36 sec
(d) 24 sec
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (b)

Method — Unitary Rate of Filling:

  • Portion filled $= \frac{3}{5}$ in $1\text{ minute} = 60\text{ seconds}$.
  • Remaining portion to fill $= 1 - \frac{3}{5} = \frac{2}{5}$.

Time taken to fill $\frac{3}{5}$ portion $= 60\text{ seconds}$.

$$\text{Time to fill } 1 \text{ complete cistern} = \frac{60}{3/5} = 60 \times \frac{5}{3} = 100\text{ seconds}$$

$$\text{Time to fill remaining } \frac{2}{5} = \frac{2}{5} \times 100 = \mathbf{40\text{ seconds}}$$

Answer: (b) 40 sec.

Q3 Provisions & Work 1 Mark
In a camp, 95 men had provisions for 200 days. After 5 days, 30 men left the camp. For how many days will the remaining food last now?
(a) 180
(b) 285
(c) $139\frac{16}{19}$
(d) None of these
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (b)

Method — Remaining Man-Days Balance:

  • Initial provisions: $95 \text{ men} \times 200 \text{ days}$.
  • After 5 days, 95 men have consumed food for 5 days.
  • Remaining provisions could feed $95\text{ men}$ for $200 - 5 = 195\text{ days}$.
  • Total remaining man-days $= 95 \times 195$.
  • Number of men remaining after 30 leave $= 95 - 30 = 65\text{ men}$.

Let the remaining food last for $D$ days:

$$65 \times D = 95 \times 195$$

$$D = \frac{95 \times 195}{65} = \frac{95 \times 3}{1} = \mathbf{285\text{ days}}$$

Answer: (b) 285 days.

Q4 Work & Absenteeism 1 Mark
A contractor undertook to do a certain piece of work in 9 days. He employed certain number of men, but 6 of them being absent from the very first day, the rest could finish the work in 15 days. The number of men originally employed were:
(a) 12
(b) 15
(c) 18
(d) 24
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (b)

Method — Inverse Variation & Algebraic Equation:

Let the original number of men employed be $x$.

  • Planned work $= 9 \times x = 9x\text{ man-days}$.
  • Actual men present $= x - 6$, who took 15 days.
  • Actual work $= 15 \times (x - 6)\text{ man-days}$.

Equating both expressions for total work:

$$9x = 15(x - 6)$$

$$9x = 15x - 90 \implies 6x = 90 \implies x = \mathbf{15}$$

Answer: (b) 15 men.

Q5 Partial Work 1 Mark
A can finish a work in 18 days and B can do the same work in 15 days. B worked for 10 days and left the job. In how many days, A alone can finish the remaining work?
(a) 5
(b) $5\frac{1}{2}$
(c) 6
(d) 8
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (c)

Method — Individual Work Fractions:

  • B's 1-day work $= \frac{1}{15}$.
  • Work done by B in 10 days $= 10 \times \frac{1}{15} = \frac{2}{3}$.
  • Remaining work $= 1 - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{3}$.
  • A's 1-day work $= \frac{1}{18}$.

Number of days taken by A to complete $\frac{1}{3}$ of the work:

$$\text{Time} = \frac{\text{Remaining Work}}{\text{A's Rate}} = \frac{1/3}{1/18} = \frac{18}{3} = \mathbf{6\text{ days}}$$

Answer: (c) 6 days.

Q6 Multi-Worker Scheduling 1 Mark
A can finish a work in 24 days, B in 9 days and C in 12 days. B and C start the work but are forced to leave after 3 days. The remaining work was done by A in:
(a) 5 days
(b) 6 days
(c) 10 days
(d) 10 days
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (c)

Method — Group Work Fraction & Remainder:

  • Work done by B and C in 1 day $= \frac{1}{9} + \frac{1}{12} = \frac{4 + 3}{36} = \frac{7}{36}$.
  • Work done by B and C in 3 days $= 3 \times \frac{7}{36} = \frac{7}{12}$.
  • Remaining work $= 1 - \frac{7}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$.
  • A's 1-day work $= \frac{1}{24}$.

Time taken by A alone to finish the remaining $\frac{5}{12}$ work:

$$\text{Time} = \frac{5/12}{1/24} = \frac{5}{12} \times 24 = \mathbf{10\text{ days}}$$

Answer: (c) 10 days.

Q7 Speed & Distance 1 Mark
A man can reach a certain place in 30 hours. If he reduces his speed by $\frac{1}{15}\text{th}$, he goes 10 km less in that time. Find his actual speed.
(a) 4 km/hr
(b) 5 km/hr
(c) $5\frac{1}{2}$ km/hr
(d) 6 km/hr
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (b)

Method — Speed-Distance Proportionality:

Let actual speed be $v\text{ km/hr}$.

  • Distance covered at actual speed in 30 hours $= 30v\text{ km}$.
  • Reduced speed $= v - \frac{1}{15}v = \frac{14}{15}v\text{ km/hr}$.
  • Distance covered at reduced speed in 30 hours $= 30 \times \left(\frac{14}{15}v ight) = 28v\text{ km}$.

Given that the difference in distance is $10\text{ km}$:

$$30v - 28v = 10 \implies 2v = 10 \implies v = \mathbf{5\text{ km/hr}}$$

Answer: (b) 5 km/hr.

Q8 Uniform Motion 1 Mark
With a uniform speed a car covers the distance in 8 hours. Had the speed been increased by 4 km/hr, the same distance could have been covered in $7\frac{1}{2}$ hours. What is the distance covered?
(a) 420 km
(b) 480 km
(c) 640 km
(d) None of these
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (b)

Method — Distance Equating Equation:

Let the uniform speed be $s\text{ km/hr}$. The distance $D$ is constant:

$$D = 8s$$

At increased speed $(s + 4)\text{ km/hr}$ and time $7.5\text{ hours}$:

$$D = (s + 4) \times 7.5 = 7.5s + 30$$

Equating the two expressions:

$$8s = 7.5s + 30 \implies 0.5s = 30 \implies s = 60\text{ km/hr}$$

$$\text{Total Distance } D = 8 \times 60 = \mathbf{480\text{ km}}$$

Answer: (b) 480 km.

Q9 Relative Speed (Chase) 1 Mark
A thief is noticed by a policeman from a distance of 200 m. The thief starts running and the policeman chase him. The thief and the policeman run at the rate of 10 kmph and 11 kmph respectively. What is the distance between them after 6 minutes?
(a) 100 m
(b) 150 m
(c) 190 m
(d) 200 m
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (a)

Method — Relative Speed in Same Direction:

  • Speed of policeman $= 11\text{ kmph}$.
  • Speed of thief $= 10\text{ kmph}$.
  • Relative speed of policeman with respect to thief $= 11 - 10 = 1\text{ kmph}$.

Distance gained by the policeman in $6\text{ minutes} = \frac{6}{60}\text{ hours} = 0.1\text{ hours}$:

$$\text{Distance Gained} = 1\text{ km/hr} \times 0.1\text{ hr} = 0.1\text{ km} = 100\text{ metres}$$

Initial gap between them was $200\text{ metres}$.

$$\text{Remaining distance after 6 minutes} = 200\text{ m} - 100\text{ m} = \mathbf{100\text{ metres}}$$

Answer: (a) 100 m.

Q10 Trains (Pedestrians) 1 Mark
A train overtakes two persons walking along a railway track. The first one walks at 4.5 km/hr and the other one walks at 5.4 km/hr. The train needs 8.4 and 8.5 seconds respectively to overtake them. What is the speed of the train if both the persons are walking in the same direction as the train?
(a) 66 km/hr
(b) 72 km/hr
(c) 78 km/hr
(d) 81 km/hr
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (d)

Method — Relative Speed & Train Length Invariant:

Convert persons' speeds to $\text{m/s}$:

  • Person 1: $4.5 \times \frac{5}{18} = 1.25\text{ m/s}$.
  • Person 2: $5.4 \times \frac{5}{18} = 1.5\text{ m/s}$.

Let train speed be $u\text{ m/s}$ and train length be $L\text{ metres}$.

$$L = (u - 1.25) \times 8.4 = 8.4u - 10.5$$

$$L = (u - 1.5) \times 8.5 = 8.5u - 12.75$$

Equating the two lengths:

$$8.4u - 10.5 = 8.5u - 12.75 \implies 0.1u = 2.25 \implies u = 22.5\text{ m/s}$$

Convert back to $\text{km/hr}$:

$$\text{Speed} = 22.5 \times \frac{18}{5} = 4.5 \times 18 = \mathbf{81\text{ km/hr}}$$

Answer: (d) 81 km/hr.

Q11 Trains (Meeting) 1 Mark
Two stations A and B are 110 km apart on a straight line. One train starts from A at 7 a.m. and travels towards B at 20 kmph. Another train starts from B at 8 a.m. and travels towards A at a speed of 25 kmph. At what time will they meet?
(a) 9 a.m.
(b) 10 a.m.
(c) 10.30 a.m.
(d) 11 a.m.
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (b)

Method — Distance Synchronization:

  • From 7:00 a.m. to 8:00 a.m. (1 hour), train A travels alone at $20\text{ km/hr}$.
  • Distance covered by train A $= 20 \times 1 = 20\text{ km}$.
  • Remaining distance between them at 8:00 a.m. $= 110 - 20 = 90\text{ km}$.

From 8:00 a.m. onwards, both trains move towards each other:

$$\text{Relative Speed} = 20 + 25 = 45\text{ km/hr}$$

$$\text{Time taken to meet} = \frac{90\text{ km}}{45\text{ km/hr}} = 2\text{ hours}$$

$$\text{Meeting Time} = 8\text{:00 a.m.} + 2\text{ hours} = \mathbf{10\text{:00 a.m.}}$$

Answer: (b) 10 a.m.

Q12 Trains (Post-Meeting) 1 Mark
Two trains, one from Howrah to Patna and the other from Patna to Howrah, start simultaneously. After they meet, the trains reach their destinations after 9 hours and 16 hours respectively. The ratio of their speeds is:
(a) 2 : 3
(b) 4 : 3
(c) 6 : 7
(d) 9 : 16
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (b)

Method — Inverse Square Root Formula:

If two bodies start simultaneously from points A and B towards each other and after crossing each other take $T_1$ and $T_2$ hours to complete their journeys, the ratio of their speeds is:

$$\frac{S_1}{S_2} = \sqrt{\frac{T_2}{T_1}}$$

Here $T_1 = 9\text{ hours}$ and $T_2 = 16\text{ hours}$:

$$\frac{S_1}{S_2} = \sqrt{\frac{16}{9}} = \frac{4}{3} = \mathbf{4 : 3}$$

Answer: (b) 4 : 3.

Q13 Clocks (Coincidence) 1 Mark
At what time between 5 and 6 O'clock are the hands of a clock together?
(a) $27\frac{3}{11}\text{ min past } 5$
(b) $11\frac{3}{11}$
(c) $\frac{220}{11}$
(d) None of these
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (a)

Method — Relative Minute-Space Gain:

  • At 5:00, the hour hand is at 25 minute-spaces ahead of the 12 position.
  • For hands to coincide, the minute hand must gain 25 minute-spaces.
  • Since minute hand gains 55 min-spaces in 60 minutes, it gains 1 min-space in $\frac{60}{55} = \frac{12}{11}\text{ minutes}$.

$$\text{Time} = 25 \times \frac{12}{11} = \frac{300}{11} = \mathbf{27\frac{3}{11}\text{ minutes past 5}}$$

Answer: (a) $27\frac{3}{11}$ min past 5.

Q14 Clocks (Right Angle) 1 Mark
At what time between 5 and 6 O'clock will the hands of a clock be at right angle?
(a) $43\frac{7}{11}$
(b) $43\frac{7}{12}\text{ min. past } 5$
(c) $43\frac{7}{11}\text{ min. past } 5$
(d) None of these
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (c)

Method — 15 Minute-Spaces Separation:

At 5 o'clock, the hour hand is at 25 minute-spaces. A right angle occurs when the hands are 15 minute-spaces apart:

  • Case 1 (Before meeting): Minute hand is 15 spaces behind $\implies (25 - 15) \times \frac{12}{11} = 10 \times \frac{12}{11} = 10\frac{10}{11}\text{ min past 5}$.
  • Case 2 (After meeting): Minute hand is 15 spaces ahead $\implies (25 + 15) \times \frac{12}{11} = 40 \times \frac{12}{11} = \frac{480}{11} = \mathbf{43\frac{7}{11}\text{ min. past 5}}$.

Looking at the options, Case 2 is represented by option (c).

Answer: (c) $43\frac{7}{11}\text{ min. past } 5$.

Q15 Clocks (Opposite) 1 Mark
Find at what time between 2 and 3 O'clock will the hands of a clock be in the same straight line but not together.
(a) $43\frac{7}{11}\text{ min past } 2$
(b) $45\frac{7}{11}$
(c) $\frac{485}{11}$
(d) None of these
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (a)

Method — 30 Minute-Spaces Separation ($180^\circ$):

  • At 2:00, the hour hand is at 10 minute-spaces.
  • To be directly opposite ($180^\circ$), the minute hand must be 30 spaces ahead of the hour hand.
  • Required minute-space position $= 10 + 30 = 40$ minute-spaces.

$$\text{Time} = 40 \times \frac{12}{11} = \frac{480}{11} = \mathbf{43\frac{7}{11}\text{ minutes past 2}}$$

Answer: (a) $43\frac{7}{11}$ (min past 2).

Q16 Clocks (Separation) 1 Mark
Find the time between 4 & 5 O'clock when the two hands of a clock are 4 minutes apart.
(a) $17\frac{5}{11}\text{ min. past 4 O' clock}$
(b) $17\frac{6}{11}\text{ min. past 4 O' clock}$
(c) $17\frac{1}{11}\text{ min. past 40' clock}$
(d) None of these
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (a)

Method — Relative Separation:

At 4 o'clock, the hour hand is at 20 minute-spaces.

  • Case 1 (Minute hand 4 minutes behind): $$\text{Spaces to gain} = 20 - 4 = 16$$ $$\text{Time} = 16 \times \frac{12}{11} = \frac{192}{11} = \mathbf{17\frac{5}{11}\text{ min. past 4 O'clock}}$$
  • Case 2 (Minute hand 4 minutes ahead): $$\text{Spaces to gain} = 20 + 4 = 24 \implies 24 \times \frac{12}{11} = \frac{288}{11} = 26\frac{2}{11}\text{ min. past 4 O'clock}$$

Case 1 matches option (a).

Answer: (a) $17\frac{5}{11}\text{ min. past 4 O' clock}$.

Q17 Clocks (Angle) 1 Mark
Find the angle between the two hands of a clock at 15 minutes past 4 O'clock.
(a) $37.5^\circ$
(b) $38^\circ$
(c) $38.5^\circ$
(d) $42^\circ$
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (a)

Method — Standard Angle Formula:

$$\theta = \left|30H - \frac{11}{2}M ight|$$

Substituting $H = 4$ and $M = 15$:

$$\theta = \left|30(4) - \frac{11}{2}(15) ight| = |120 - 82.5| = \mathbf{37.5^\circ}$$

Q18 Calendars (Forward) 1 Mark
11 January 1997 was a Sunday, What day of the week was on 7 January 2000?
(a) Sunday
(b) Saturday
(c) Friday
(d) Thursday
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (b)

Method — Odd Days Analysis:

  • From 11 Jan 1997 to 11 Jan 1998: 1997 is an ordinary year $\implies 1\text{ odd day}$.
  • From 11 Jan 1998 to 11 Jan 1999: 1998 is an ordinary year $\implies 1\text{ odd day}$.
  • From 11 Jan 1999 to 11 Jan 2000: 1999 is an ordinary year $\implies 1\text{ odd day}$.
  • Note: The leap day of year 2000 occurs in February, which is after 11 January.
  • Total odd days from 11 Jan 1997 to 11 Jan 2000 $= 1 + 1 + 1 = 3\text{ odd days}$.

Therefore, 11 January 2000 was $\text{Sunday} + 3 = \mathbf{\text{Wednesday}}$.

Now, 7 January 2000 is 4 days before 11 January 2000:

$$\text{Day on 7 Jan 2000} = \text{Wednesday} - 4\text{ days} = \mathbf{\text{Saturday}}$$

Answer: (b) Saturday.

Q19 Calendars (Day Decoding) 1 Mark
What day of the week was on 5 June 1999?
(a) Saturday
(b) Sunday
(c) Monday
(d) Tuesday
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (a)

Method — Century & Year Odd Days Summation:

  • 1600 years: $0\text{ odd days}$.
  • 300 years (1601–1900): $1\text{ odd day}$.
  • 98 completed years (1901–1998): $$98 \div 4 = 24\text{ leap years}, \quad 98 - 24 = 74\text{ ordinary years}$$ $$\text{Odd days} = 24 \times 2 + 74 \times 1 = 48 + 74 = 122 \equiv 122 \pmod 7 = 3\text{ odd days}$$
  • Total odd days up to 31 Dec 1998 $= 0 + 1 + 3 = 4\text{ odd days}$.
  • Days in 1999 up to 5 June: $$\text{Jan (31)} \to 3, \; \text{Feb (28)} \to 0, \; \text{Mar (31)} \to 3, \; \text{Apr (30)} \to 2, \; \text{May (31)} \to 3, \; \text{June} = 5$$ $$\text{Sum} = 3 + 0 + 3 + 2 + 3 + 5 = 16 \equiv 2\text{ odd days}$$
  • Total Odd Days $= 4 + 2 = 6\text{ odd days}$.

Code 6 corresponds to Saturday (0=Sun, 1=Mon, ..., 6=Sat).

Answer: (a) Saturday.

Q20 Clocks (Coincidence) 1 Mark

Q21 Clocks (3 Min Apart) 1 Mark
At what time between 5 and 6 O'clock are the hands of a clock 3 minutes apart?
(a) 24 minutes past 5
(b) 22 minutes past 3
(c) 26 minutes past 4
(d) None of these
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (a)

Method — Minute-Space Separation:

At 5 o'clock, the hour hand is at 25 minute-spaces.

  • Case 1 (Minute hand 3 minutes behind): $$\text{Spaces to gain} = 25 - 3 = 22$$ $$\text{Time} = 22 \times \frac{12}{11} = 2 \times 12 = \mathbf{24\text{ minutes past 5}}$$
  • Case 2 (Minute hand 3 minutes ahead): $$\text{Spaces to gain} = 25 + 3 = 28 \implies 28 \times \frac{12}{11} = \frac{336}{11} = 30\frac{6}{11}\text{ min past 5}$$

Case 1 exactly matches option (a).

Answer: (a) 24 minutes past 5.

Q22 Clocks (Angle at 4:30) 1 Mark
Find the angle between the two hands of a clock at 30 minutes past 4 O'clock.
(a) $40^\circ$
(b) $30^\circ$
(c) $45^\circ$
(d) None of these
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (c)

Method — Angle Formula:

$$\theta = \left|30H - \frac{11}{2}M ight|$$

Substituting $H = 4$ and $M = 30$:

$$\theta = \left|30(4) - \frac{11}{2}(30) ight| = |120 - 165| = |-45^\circ| = \mathbf{45^\circ}$$

Answer: (c) $45^\circ$.

Q23 Clocks (Collinear Straight) 1 Mark
Number of times the hands of a clock are in a straight line everyday is:
(a) 44
(b) 24
(c) 42
(d) 22
View Step-by-Step Model Solution Correct: (a)

Method — Collinear Alignment Analysis:

Hands of a clock are in a straight line when they are either:

  • Coinciding ($0^\circ$): Occurs 11 times in 12 hours $\implies 22\text{ times in 24 hours}$.
  • Directly opposite ($180^\circ$): Occurs 11 times in 12 hours $\implies 22\text{ times in 24 hours}$.

$$\text{Total Collinear Occurrences in a day} = 22 + 22 = \mathbf{44\text{ times}}$$

Answer: (a) 44.

Q24 System of Work Rates Numerical Problem
A and B together can complete a work in 6 days, and A and C together can do it in 10 days. If A, B, and C can finish the work together in 4 days, in how many days can B and C finish it?
View Complete Analytical Solution Answer: 4 2/7 Days

Step-by-Step Algebraic Derivation:

Let $A, B, C$ denote the daily work rate of each person:

$$A + B = \frac{1}{6} \quad \text{--- (1)}$$

$$A + C = \frac{1}{10} \quad \text{--- (2)}$$

$$A + B + C = \frac{1}{4} \quad \text{--- (3)}$$

Subtracting equation (1) from (3) gives C's individual rate:

$$C = (A + B + C) - (A + B) = \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{3 - 2}{12} = \frac{1}{12}$$

Subtracting equation (2) from (3) gives B's individual rate:

$$B = (A + B + C) - (A + C) = \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{10} = \frac{5 - 2}{20} = \frac{3}{20}$$

Combined daily work rate of B and C together:

$$B + C = \frac{3}{20} + \frac{1}{12} = \frac{9 + 5}{60} = \frac{14}{60} = \frac{7}{30}$$

Number of days required by B and C together:

$$\text{Time} = \frac{1}{B + C} = \frac{30}{7} = \mathbf{4\frac{2}{7}\text{ days}}$$

Final Answer: B and C can complete the work together in $\mathbf{4\frac{2}{7}}$ days (approx $4.29$ days).

Q25 Trains (Same Direction) Numerical Problem
Two trains 150 m and 200 m long run in the same direction at 72 km/hr and 54 km/hr. How long will the faster train take to pass the slower one?
View Complete Analytical Solution Answer: 70 Seconds

Step-by-Step Working:

  • Total distance to be cleared $= L_1 + L_2 = 150\text{ m} + 200\text{ m} = 350\text{ metres}$.
  • Since both trains run in the same direction, relative speed is the difference of their speeds: $$\text{Relative Speed} = 72 - 54 = 18\text{ km/hr}$$
  • Convert relative speed to $\text{m/s}$: $$18 \times \frac{5}{18} = 5\text{ m/s}$$

Time taken for the faster train to pass the slower train:

$$\text{Time} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Relative Speed}} = \frac{350\text{ m}}{5\text{ m/s}} = \mathbf{70\text{ seconds}}$$

Final Answer: $\mathbf{70\text{ seconds}}$ ($1\text{ minute } 10\text{ seconds}$).

Q26 Converging Motion Numerical Problem
A car and a bike start from opposite ends of a 210 km road at the same time. Car at 70 km/hr and bike at 35 km/hr. How long until they meet and at what distance from the car's starting point?
View Complete Analytical Solution Answer: 2 Hours, 140 km

Step-by-Step Working:

  • Total road length $= 210\text{ km}$.
  • Moving towards each other (opposite directions): $$\text{Relative Speed} = 70 + 35 = 105\text{ km/hr}$$

Time taken until they meet:

$$\text{Time} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Relative Speed}} = \frac{210\text{ km}}{105\text{ km/hr}} = \mathbf{2\text{ hours}}$$

Distance covered by the car from its starting point in 2 hours:

$$\text{Distance}_{\text{car}} = \text{Speed}_{\text{car}} \times \text{Time} = 70 \times 2 = \mathbf{140\text{ km}}$$

Final Answer: They meet after $\mathbf{2\text{ hours}}$ at a distance of $\mathbf{140\text{ km}}$ from the car's starting point.

Q27 Round-Trip Distance Numerical Problem
A boy walks from A to B at 4 km/hr and returns at 6 km/hr. Total time taken is 5 hours. Find distance AB.
View Complete Analytical Solution Answer: 12 km

Step-by-Step Working:

Let the one-way distance between A and B be $d\text{ km}$.

  • Time taken to walk from A to B $= \frac{d}{4}\text{ hours}$.
  • Time taken to return from B to A $= \frac{d}{6}\text{ hours}$.
  • Total round-trip time $= 5\text{ hours}$.

Setting up the time equation:

$$\frac{d}{4} + \frac{d}{6} = 5$$

$$\frac{3d + 2d}{12} = 5 \implies \frac{5d}{12} = 5 \implies d = \mathbf{12\text{ km}}$$

Final Answer: Distance AB is $\mathbf{12\text{ km}}$.

Q28 Wages & Work Distribution Numerical Problem
A and B undertake to do a piece of work for Rs. 450. A can do it in 20 days and B in 40 days. With the help of C, they finish it in 8 days. How much should C be paid?
View Complete Analytical Solution Answer: Rs. 180

Step-by-Step Working:

Wages are distributed in direct proportion to the quantum of work completed by each individual.

  • Work done by A in 8 days $= 8 \times \frac{1}{20} = \frac{8}{20} = \frac{2}{5}$.
  • Work done by B in 8 days $= 8 \times \frac{1}{40} = \frac{8}{40} = \frac{1}{5}$.
  • Combined work done by A and B $= \frac{2}{5} + \frac{1}{5} = \frac{3}{5}$.
  • Work done by C $= 1 - \frac{3}{5} = \frac{2}{5}$.

Payment to C is proportional to his share of work:

$$\text{C's Payment} = \frac{2}{5} \times \text{Rs. } 450 = 2 \times 90 = \mathbf{\text{Rs. } 180}$$

Final Answer: C should be paid $\mathbf{\text{Rs. } 180}$.

Q29 Corporate Fund Depletion Numerical Problem
A Corporate firm is running in loss, they had categorized their employees into two teams, Team A and Team B. The firm has funds to pay Team A for 21 days only. If they work with Team B then the firm can pay for 28 days only. Management has decided that they will allow both the teams to work for as long as they are having enough funds to pay them. Find out for how many days both the teams could work together?
View Complete Analytical Solution Answer: 12 Days

Step-by-Step Working:

Let the total available fund reserve be $F$.

  • Daily payroll expenditure for Team A $= \frac{F}{21}$ per day.
  • Daily payroll expenditure for Team B $= \frac{F}{28}$ per day.

When both teams work together simultaneously, the total daily expenditure is:

$$\text{Combined Daily Burn Rate} = \frac{F}{21} + \frac{F}{28} = F \left(\frac{4 + 3}{84} ight) = \frac{7F}{84} = \frac{F}{12}$$

Number of days the available funds $F$ will sustain both teams:

$$\text{Number of Days} = \frac{F}{F/12} = \mathbf{12\text{ days}}$$

Final Answer: Both teams can work together for $\mathbf{12\text{ days}}$.

Case Study I: The Big Ben Clock Tower (London)

The Big Ben clock tower in London is one of the most famous clocks in the world. It has four faces, and each has two hands: an hour hand and a minute hand.

  • The hour hand is 6 feet long ($r_h = 6\text{ ft}$).
  • The minute hand is 9 feet long ($r_m = 9\text{ ft}$).
  • The hands move continuously in a circular path.
  • The movement of the hands can be described using angular speed.
  • The clock was reset at 12:00 midnight (00:00).

a. What is the angle between the hour and minute hands of the Big Ben clock at 5:30 p.m.?

View Solution Angle: 15°

Using the angular formula $\theta = |30H - 5.5M|$ with $H = 5, M = 30$:

$$\theta = |30(5) - 5.5(30)| = |150 - 165| = |-15^\circ| = \mathbf{15^\circ}$$

Answer: $\mathbf{15^\circ}$

b. After the clock is set at 12:00 a.m., at what time between 1:00 a.m. and 2:00 a.m. will the hands of the clock coincide?

View Solution Time: 1:05 5/11 a.m.

At 1:00 a.m., the hour hand is 5 minute-spaces ahead. The minute hand must gain 5 minute-spaces at the rate of $\frac{12}{11}\text{ min/space}$:

$$\text{Minutes} = 5 \times \frac{12}{11} = \frac{60}{11} = \mathbf{5\frac{5}{11}\text{ minutes}}$$

Answer: Coincide at $\mathbf{5\frac{5}{11}\text{ minutes past 1:00 a.m.}}$ (or $1:05:27\text{ a.m.}$)

c. How many times in a day (24 hours) will the hour and minute hands of the clock coincide?

View Solution Coincidences: 22 Times

The hands coincide 11 times every 12 hours (due to the single coincidence between 11:00 and 1:00 at exactly 12:00).

$$\text{Total in 24 hours} = 11 \times 2 = \mathbf{22\text{ times}}$$

Answer: $\mathbf{22\text{ times}}$

d. Through what angle does the minute hand rotate between 2:00 p.m. and 2:40 p.m. on the same day?

View Solution Rotation: 240°

Elapsed time between 2:00 p.m. and 2:40 p.m. $= 40\text{ minutes}$.

Angular speed of minute hand $= 6^\circ/\text{minute}$.

$$\text{Angle Rotated} = 40 \times 6^\circ = \mathbf{240^\circ}$$

Answer: $\mathbf{240^\circ}$

e. A tourist wants to know how much area the hour hand sweeps between 11:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m. on a particular day. Calculate this area for Big Ben's hour hand.

View Solution Area: 396/7 ≈ 56.57 sq ft

Duration from 11:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. $= 6\text{ hours}$.

  • A complete 12-hour cycle sweeps $360^\circ$ (full circle).
  • In 6 hours, the hour hand sweeps exactly half the circular clock face ($180^\circ$).
  • Hour hand radius $r = 6\text{ feet}$.

$$\text{Area Swept} = \frac{1}{2} \pi r^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{22}{7} \times (6)^2 = \frac{11 \times 36}{7} = \frac{396}{7} \approx \mathbf{56.57\text{ sq ft}}$$

Answer: $\mathbf{\frac{396}{7}\text{ sq ft} \approx 56.57\text{ sq ft}}$ (or $18\pi\text{ sq ft}$)

Case Study II: Executive Boardroom Seating Arrangement

At the board meeting of a certain company, six officials sit around a circular table facing the centre of the table and the chairs are equally placed around it. There are two Directors (I and II), a Chairman, a Vice Chairman, a CEO and a CFO.

Given Information & Constraints:

  1. Director I is sitting to the right of Director II.
  2. The Chairman is not sitting next to the Vice Chairman.
  3. Director I is equally far from the CEO and the CFO. All seats are equally spaced.
  4. The Vice Chairman is sitting to the immediate right of CEO.
Circular Arrangement Deduction:

Number 6 positions clockwise around the table facing the centre ($1$ to $6$). When facing the centre, to one's right means counter-clockwise:

Seat 1: CEO
Seat 2: Chairman
Seat 3: Director I
Seat 4: Director II
Seat 5: CFO
Seat 6: Vice Chairman

a. Which employee is definitely sitting at the same distance from Director II as Director I is sitting from the CEO or CFO?

View Solution Answer: Chairman / Vice Chairman
  • Director I (Seat 3) is at a distance of 2 seats from both CEO (Seat 1) and CFO (Seat 5).
  • Director II is seated at Seat 4.
  • The officials situated at distance 2 from Director II (Seat 4) are Seat 2 (Chairman) and Seat 6 (Vice Chairman).
Answer: The Chairman (or Vice Chairman) is definitely sitting at distance 2 from Director II.

b. Which employee is sitting exactly opposite to the Vice Chairman?

View Solution Answer: Director I

In a 6-chair circular table, the opposite position to Seat $n$ is Seat $(n + 3) \pmod 6$.

Vice Chairman sits at Seat 6. The position directly opposite is Seat 3.

Seat 3 is occupied by Director I.

Answer: Director I

c. One of the board members leaves, leaving a gap of one seat between the Chairman and the Vice Chairman. Based on the information given, which member/s could have left?

View Solution Answer: CEO

Chairman is at Seat 2 and Vice Chairman is at Seat 6.

  • Between Seat 2 and Seat 6 along the short arc lies Seat 1.
  • Seat 1 is occupied by the CEO.
  • If the CEO leaves, Seat 1 becomes vacant, leaving a gap of exactly one empty chair between the Chairman (Seat 2) and the Vice Chairman (Seat 6).
Answer: The CEO could have left.
