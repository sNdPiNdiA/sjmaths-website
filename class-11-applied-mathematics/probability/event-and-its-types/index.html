Event and Its Types

Basic Definitions and Sample Space

An experiment is a process that results in a well-defined outcome. The set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment is called the sample space, denoted by S. An event E is a subset of the sample space S, representing a specific outcome or a collection of outcomes.

n(E) ≤ n(S)
Example: Solved Example: Rolling a die
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify sample space S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}.
Step 2: Define event E as getting an even number, E = {2, 4, 6}.
Step 3: Calculate n(E) = 3.
Answer: The event E has 3 outcomes.

Types of Events: Simple and Compound

A simple event (or elementary event) contains exactly one sample point from the sample space. A compound event contains more than one sample point. For example, in tossing two coins, {HH} is a simple event, while {at least one head} is a compound event.

n(E) = 1 (Simple Event)
Example: Solved Example: Tossing two coins
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: S = {HH, HT, TH, TT}.
Step 2: Simple event E₁ = {HH}.
Step 3: Compound event E₂ = {HH, HT, TH}.
Answer: E₁ is simple, E₂ is compound.

Mutually Exclusive and Exhaustive Events

Two events A and B are mutually exclusive if A ∩ B = ∅, meaning they cannot occur simultaneously. Events are collectively exhaustive if their union equals the sample space S, meaning at least one of them must occur.

A ∩ B = ∅ (Mutually Exclusive); A ∪ B = S (Exhaustive)
Example: Solved Example: Verify mutual exclusivity
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Let S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}.
Step 2: Let A = {1, 2} and B = {4, 5}.
Step 3: A ∩ B = ∅.
Answer: A and B are mutually exclusive.

Assertion (A): If A = {1, 2} and B = {2, 3}, then A and B are mutually exclusive.
Reason (R): Two events are mutually exclusive if their intersection is the empty set.

Choose the correct option:
(A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
(B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(C) A is true but R is false.
(D) A is false but R is true.

Show Answer

✔ Correct: D

The assertion is false because A ∩ B = {2} ≠ ∅. The reason is a correct definition.

2

Assertion (A): For a fair die, the event E = {1, 3, 5} and F = {2, 4, 6} are exhaustive.
Reason (R): Events are exhaustive if their union is the sample space S.

Choose the correct option:
(A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
(B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(C) A is true but R is false.

✔ Correct: A

E ∪ F = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6} = S. Both assertion and reason are true and correctly linked.

Section D: Long Answer Questions

1

hardIn a survey of 20 students, 12 like Mathematics, 10 like Science, and 5 like both. Let M be the event a student likes Math and S be the event a student likes Science. (i) Find n(M ∪ S) (ii) Are M and S mutually exclusive? (iii) Find the number of students who like only Math.

Show Answer & Solution

Step 1: Given n(M) = 12, n(S) = 10, n(M ∩ S) = 5.
Step 2: Use formula n(M ∪ S) = n(M) + n(S) - n(M ∩ S).
Step 3: n(M ∪ S) = 12 + 10 - 5 = 17.
Step 4: Since n(M ∩ S) = 5 ≠ 0, they are not mutually exclusive.
Step 5: Only Math = n(M) - n(M ∩ S) = 12 - 5 = 7.

2

very hardA bag contains 5 red, 3 blue, and 2 green balls. Two balls are drawn. (i) Define the sample space size (ii) Find event E₁: both are red (iii) Find event E₂: one red and one blue.

Show Answer & Solution

Step 1: Total balls = 5 + 3 + 2 = 10. Total ways to draw 2 = ¹⁰C₂ = 45.
Step 2: Ways to draw 2 red = ⁵C₂ = 10.
Step 3: Ways to draw 1 red and 1 blue = ⁵C₁ × ³C₁ = 5 × 3 = 15.
Step 4: E₁ = 10/45 = 2/9.
Step 5: E₂ = 15/45 = 1/3.

Section E: Case-Based Question

★

Case Study: A school club has 50 members. 30 members participate in Debate, 20 participate in Quiz, and 10 participate in both. Let D be the event of choosing a Debate participant and Q be the event of choosing a Quiz participant.

Sub-question 1: (i) How many members participate in either Debate or Quiz? (ii) How many participate in neither?

Show Solution

(i) n(D ∪ Q) = n(D) + n(Q) - n(D ∩ Q) = 30 + 20 - 10 = 40.
(ii) Neither = Total - n(D ∪ Q) = 50 - 40 = 10.

Sub-question 2: If a member is chosen at random, what is the probability they participate in only Debate?

Show Solution

Only Debate = n(D) - n(D ∩ Q) = 30 - 10 = 20. Probability = 20/50 = 0.4.
