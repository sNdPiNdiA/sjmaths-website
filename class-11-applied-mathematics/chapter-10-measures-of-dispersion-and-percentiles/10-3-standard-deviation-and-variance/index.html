Comprehensive Concepts & Principles: Standard Deviation & Variance

Introduced by Karl Pearson in 1893, the Standard Deviation ($sigma$) is the premier, mathematically rigorous measure of statistical dispersion. Defined as the positive square root of the arithmetic mean of squared deviations from the arithmetic mean, standard deviation overcomes the algebraic limitations of mean deviation by squaring deviations $(x_i - \bar{X})^2$ rather than discarding signs with absolute values.

Key Formulas for Variance & Standard Deviation:
  • Variance ($\sigma^2$):
    • Individual Series: $$\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{X})^2}{N} = \frac{\sum x_i^2}{N} - (\bar{X})^2$$
    • Grouped Frequency Data: $$\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum f_i (x_i - \bar{X})^2}{N} = \frac{\sum f_i x_i^2}{N} - \left(\frac{\sum f_i x_i}{N}\right)^2, \quad N = \sum f_i$$
  • Standard Deviation ($\sigma$):
    $$\sigma = +\sqrt{\sigma^2}$$
  • Short-cut Method (Assumed Mean $A$, $d_i = x_i - A$):
    $$\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum f_i d_i^2}{N} - \left(\frac{\sum f_i d_i}{N}\right)^2}$$
  • Step-Deviation Method (Class Width $h$, $u_i = \frac{x_i - A}{h}$):
    $$\sigma = h \times \sqrt{\frac{\sum f_i u_i^2}{N} - \left(\frac{\sum f_i u_i}{N}\right)^2}$$
  • Coefficient of Variation ($CV$): The universal relative measure of variability:
    $$CV = \frac{\sigma}{\bar{X}} \times 100\%$$ A series with a LOWER $CV$ is more consistent, more homogeneous, and more stable.

Invariance Properties: Origin vs. Scale

Standard deviation exhibits distinct mathematical responses to linear transformations $y_i = a + b x_i$:

• Independent of Change of Origin: Adding or subtracting a constant $a$ from every observation leaves standard deviation completely unchanged: $\sigma_{x \pm a} = \sigma_x$.
• Dependent on Change of Scale: Multiplying every observation by a constant $b$ scales standard deviation by $|b|$: $\sigma_{bx} = |b| \cdot \sigma_x$, and scales variance by $b^2$: $\sigma_{bx}^2 = b^2 \cdot \sigma_x^2$.

Worked Benchmark Examples

Example 1 (Variance & SD of Individual Series):

Find the Variance and Standard Deviation of the first 5 positive multiples of 3:
$$3, \quad 6, \quad 9, \quad 12, \quad 15$$

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $N = 5$. Sum $\sum x_i = 3 + 6 + 9 + 12 + 15 = 45$.
• Mean $\bar{X} = 45/5 = \mathbf{9}$.
• Deviations $(x_i - \bar{X})$ and squared deviations $(x_i - \bar{X})^2$:
$(3-9)^2 = (-6)^2 = 36$
$(6-9)^2 = (-3)^2 = 9$
$(9-9)^2 = 0^2 = 0$
$(12-9)^2 = 3^2 = 9$
$(15-9)^2 = 6^2 = 36$
• $\sum (x_i - \bar{X})^2 = 36 + 9 + 0 + 9 + 36 = \mathbf{90}$.
• Variance: $\sigma^2 = \frac{90}{5} = \mathbf{18}$.
• Standard Deviation: $\sigma = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2} \approx \mathbf{4.242}$.

Example 2 (Comparing Consistency using CV):

The batting records of two cricketers A and B across a season are:
• Batsman A: Mean $= 50$ runs, $\sigma = 12$ runs
• Batsman B: Mean $= 40$ runs, $\sigma = 8$ runs
(i) Who is the higher run-scorer on average?
(ii) Who is the more consistent batsman?

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• (i) Since $\bar{X}_A = 50 > \bar{X}_B = 40$, Batsman A is the higher run-scorer on average.
• (ii) To evaluate consistency, compute the Coefficient of Variation ($CV$): $$CV_A = \frac{\sigma_A}{\bar{X}_A} \times 100 = \frac{12}{50} \times 100 = \mathbf{24\%}$$ $$CV_B = \frac{\sigma_B}{\bar{X}_B} \times 100 = \frac{8}{40} \times 100 = \mathbf{20\%}$$
• Conclusion: Batsman B is more consistent because $CV_B (20\%) < CV_A (24\%)$.

Problem 1 Raw Data SD

Calculate the Variance and Standard Deviation of the numbers:
$$6, \quad 8, \quad 10, \quad 12, \quad 14$$

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $N = 5$. Mean $\bar{X} = \frac{6+8+10+12+14}{5} = \frac{50}{5} = 10$.
• Squared deviations from 10: $(6-10)^2 = 16, \quad (8-10)^2 = 4, \quad (10-10)^2 = 0, \quad (12-10)^2 = 4, \quad (14-10)^2 = 16$.
• $\sum (x_i - \bar{X})^2 = 16 + 4 + 0 + 4 + 16 = 40$.
• $\sigma^2 = \frac{40}{5} = \mathbf{8}$.
• $\sigma = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2} \approx \mathbf{2.83}$.

Problem 2 First n Natural Numbers

Prove that the variance of the first $n$ natural numbers $1, 2, 3, \dots, n$ is given by $\sigma^2 = \frac{n^2 - 1}{12}$, and compute the standard deviation for the first 10 natural numbers.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $\sum x = \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \implies \bar{X} = \frac{n+1}{2}$.
• $\sum x^2 = \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} \implies \frac{\sum x^2}{n} = \frac{(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$.
• $\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum x^2}{n} - (\bar{X})^2 = \frac{(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} - \frac{(n+1)^2}{4} = \frac{n+1}{12}\left[2(2n+1) - 3(n+1)\right]$
• $\sigma^2 = \frac{n+1}{12}(4n + 2 - 3n - 3) = \frac{(n+1)(n-1)}{12} = \mathbf{\frac{n^2 - 1}{12}}$.
• For $n = 10$: $\sigma^2 = \frac{100 - 1}{12} = \frac{99}{12} = 8.25 \implies \sigma = \sqrt{8.25} \approx \mathbf{2.872}$.

Problem 3 Scale Transformation

A dataset has a standard deviation of 6.
(a) If 5 is added to every observation, what is the new standard deviation?
(b) If every observation is multiplied by 3, what are the new standard deviation and variance?

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• (a) Adding a constant shifts the origin without affecting spread: New $\sigma = \mathbf{6}$.
• (b) Multiplying by 3 scales the spread by 3:
- New $\sigma = 3 \times 6 = \mathbf{18}$.
- New Variance $\sigma^2 = 18^2 = \mathbf{324}$ (which is $3^2 \times 36$).

Problem 4 Step-Deviation Calculation

Using the step-deviation method with $h = 10$ and $A = 35$, the following summary values were obtained for 50 items:
$\sum f_i u_i = 10, \quad \sum f_i u_i^2 = 98$.
Calculate the Mean and Standard Deviation of the distribution.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $N = 50, A = 35, h = 10$.
• $\bar{X} = A + h\left(\frac{\sum f_i u_i}{N}\right) = 35 + 10\left(\frac{10}{50}\right) = 35 + 2 = \mathbf{37}$.
• $\sigma = h \times \sqrt{\frac{\sum f_i u_i^2}{N} - \left(\frac{\sum f_i u_i}{N}\right)^2} = 10 \times \sqrt{\frac{98}{50} - (0.2)^2}$
• $\sigma = 10 \times \sqrt{1.96 - 0.04} = 10 \times \sqrt{1.92} = 10 \times 1.3856 = \mathbf{13.856}$.

Problem 5 Coefficient of Variation Analysis

Two industrial factories A and B report weekly wages:
• Factory A: Mean weekly wage = ₹5,000, $\sigma = ₹400$
• Factory B: Mean weekly wage = ₹6,000, $\sigma = ₹450$
Which factory has greater wage inequality (variability in wages)?

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $CV_A = \frac{400}{5000} \times 100 = \mathbf{8\%}$.
• $CV_B = \frac{450}{6000} \times 100 = \mathbf{7.5\%}$.
• Conclusion: Factory A has greater wage variability ($8\% > 7.5\%$), meaning wages are relatively more unequally distributed in Factory A.

SECTION B: 2-Mark Short Answer Problems

Q3: The sum and sum of squares of 10 observations are $\sum x = 60$ and $\sum x^2 = 400$. Calculate the variance and standard deviation.

Show Answer & Scheme

• $\bar{X} = \frac{60}{10} = 6$ [0.5 Mark].
• $\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum x^2}{N} - (\bar{X})^2 = \frac{400}{10} - (6)^2 = 40 - 36 = \mathbf{4}$ [1 Mark].
• $\sigma = \sqrt{4} = \mathbf{2}$ [0.5 Mark].

Q4: Why is Standard Deviation considered superior to Mean Deviation in higher mathematical statistics?

Show Answer & Scheme

Explanation [2 Marks]:
(1) Standard deviation squares deviations algebraically instead of artificially discarding signs with absolute value bars, making it continuously differentiable.
(2) It possesses well-defined algebraic properties that allow the calculation of combined standard deviation of multiple subgroups, which is impossible with mean deviation.

SECTION C: 4-Mark Comprehensive Numerical Drill

Q5: An agricultural researcher tests the crop yield (in quintals per hectare) from two organic fertilizers Alpha and Beta across 5 experimental plots:
• Fertilizer Alpha: $18, \quad 20, \quad 22, \quad 24, \quad 26$
• Fertilizer Beta: $14, \quad 17, \quad 23, \quad 25, \quad 31$
(i) Find the Mean and Standard Deviation of both fertilizers.
(ii) Compute the Coefficient of Variation for both and determine which fertilizer provides more consistent yields.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Fertilizer Alpha [1.5 Marks]:
• $\bar{X}_A = \frac{18+20+22+24+26}{5} = \frac{110}{5} = \mathbf{22}$.
• Deviations from 22: $-4, -2, 0, 2, 4 \implies$ Squares: $16, 4, 0, 4, 16 \implies \sum d^2 = 40$.
• $\sigma_A^2 = 40/5 = 8 \implies \sigma_A = \sqrt{8} \approx \mathbf{2.83}$.

Fertilizer Beta [1.5 Marks]:
• $\bar{X}_B = \frac{14+17+23+25+31}{5} = \frac{110}{5} = \mathbf{22}$.
• Deviations from 22: $-8, -5, 1, 3, 9 \implies$ Squares: $64, 25, 1, 9, 81 \implies \sum d^2 = 180$.
• $\sigma_B^2 = 180/5 = 36 \implies \sigma_B = \sqrt{36} = \mathbf{6.00}$.

Coefficient of Variation & Comparison [1 Mark]:
• $CV_A = \frac{2.83}{22} \times 100 = \mathbf{12.86\%}$.
• $CV_B = \frac{6.00}{22} \times 100 = \mathbf{27.27\%}$.
• Conclusion: Fertilizer Alpha provides far superior consistency ($CV_A = 12.86\% < 27.27\%$), exhibiting less than half the yield volatility of Beta.

Master Formula Sheet

Method Formula for Standard Deviation ($\sigma$)
Actual Mean Method $\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum f_i (x_i - \bar{X})^2}{N}}$
Assumed Mean ($d_i = x_i - A$) $\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum f_i d_i^2}{N} - \left(\frac{\sum f_i d_i}{N}\right)^2}$
Step-Deviation ($u_i = \frac{x_i - A}{h}$) $\sigma = h \times \sqrt{\frac{\sum f_i u_i^2}{N} - \left(\frac{\sum f_i u_i}{N}\right)^2}$
Coefficient of Variation $CV = \frac{\sigma}{\bar{X}} \times 100\%$

Linear Transformation Rules

If $y = a + bx$:
• $\bar{Y} = a + b\bar{X}$ (Mean responds to BOTH origin and scale)
• $\sigma_y = |b| \cdot \sigma_x$ (Standard deviation responds ONLY to scale $|b|$)
• $\sigma_y^2 = b^2 \cdot \sigma_x^2$ (Variance responds ONLY to squared scale $b^2$)

PART 1: Objective Section (4 Marks)

Q1: If every value in a dataset is increased by 10, the variance of the new dataset will:

Solution: (C) Adding a constant changes only the origin, leaving variance and standard deviation completely unchanged.

Q2: If the standard deviation of $x$ is 4, then the standard deviation of $y = 3 - 2x$ is:

Solution: (B) $\sigma_y = |-2| \cdot \sigma_x = 2 \times 4 = 8$. Standard deviation is always non-negative.

Q3: A factory produces items with Mean $= 200$ and $CV = 5\%$. The standard deviation of the items is:

Solution: (A) $CV = (\sigma / \bar{X}) \times 100 \implies 5 = (\sigma / 200) \times 100 \implies \sigma = 10$.

Q4 (Assertion-Reason):
Assertion (A): When comparing two distributions with different units of measurement, the distribution with the smaller Coefficient of Variation is considered more consistent.
Reason (R): The Coefficient of Variation is a dimensionless percentage that normalizes standard deviation by its arithmetic mean.

Solution: (A) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason provides the precise normalization logic of $CV$.

PART 2: Subjective Section (Q5 - Q10)

Q5 2 Marks

Find the variance of the numbers $2, 4, 6, 8, 10$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Notice these are $2 \times (1, 2, 3, 4, 5)$.
• Variance of first 5 natural numbers: $\frac{5^2 - 1}{12} = \frac{24}{12} = 2$ [1 Mark].
• By scale property: $\sigma^2(2x) = 2^2 \times \sigma^2(x) = 4 \times 2 = \mathbf{8}$ [1 Mark].

Q6 2 Marks

The mean of 5 observations is 4.4 and their variance is 8.24. If three of the observations are 1, 2, and 6, find the remaining two observations.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Let remaining observations be $x$ and $y$.
• Sum: $1 + 2 + 6 + x + y = 5 \times 4.4 = 22 \implies x + y = 13$ [1 Mark].
• $\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum x_i^2}{5} - (4.4)^2 = 8.24 \implies \frac{\sum x_i^2}{5} - 19.36 = 8.24 \implies \frac{\sum x_i^2}{5} = 27.60 \implies \sum x_i^2 = 138$.
• $1^2 + 2^2 + 6^2 + x^2 + y^2 = 1 + 4 + 36 + x^2 + y^2 = 41 + x^2 + y^2 = 138 \implies x^2 + y^2 = 97$.
• $(x+y)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + 2xy \implies 169 = 97 + 2xy \implies 2xy = 72 \implies xy = 36$.
• Solving $x+y=13$ and $xy=36$ gives $\mathbf{x = 4, y = 9}$ (or vice versa) [1 Mark].

Q7 3 Marks

Prove that for any dataset, the Standard Deviation is always greater than or equal to the Mean Deviation about the Mean ($\sigma \ge MD(\bar{X})$).

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Let deviations be $z_i = |x_i - \bar{X}|$.
• The mean of $z_i$ is $\bar{z} = \frac{\sum |x_i - \bar{X}|}{N} = MD(\bar{X})$ [1 Mark].
• The mean of $z_i^2$ is $\frac{\sum |x_i - \bar{X}|^2}{N} = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{X})^2}{N} = \sigma^2$ [1 Mark].
• Since the variance of any variable is always non-negative: $$\text{Var}(z) = \frac{\sum z_i^2}{N} - (\bar{z})^2 \ge 0 \implies \sigma^2 - [MD(\bar{X})]^2 \ge 0$$ $$\sigma^2 \ge [MD(\bar{X})]^2 \implies \mathbf{\sigma \ge MD(\bar{X})} \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$ • Equality holds if and only if all deviations are identical in absolute magnitude.

Q8 3 Marks

For a group of 50 items, $\sum x = 500$ and $\sum x^2 = 6000$. It was subsequently detected that two values originally recorded as 8 and 12 were actually misread, and their correct values are 10 and 10. Compute the corrected Mean and corrected Standard Deviation.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Correction in Sum [1 Mark]:
Corrected $\sum x = 500 - (8 + 12) + (10 + 10) = 500 - 20 + 20 = 500$.
Corrected $\bar{X} = 500/50 = \mathbf{10}$.
• Correction in Sum of Squares [1 Mark]:
Corrected $\sum x^2 = 6000 - (8^2 + 12^2) + (10^2 + 10^2) = 6000 - (64 + 144) + (100 + 100) = 6000 - 208 + 200 = \mathbf{5992}$.
• Corrected Variance & SD [1 Mark]:
$\sigma_{\text{corrected}}^2 = \frac{5992}{50} - (10)^2 = 119.84 - 100 = 19.84$.
$\sigma_{\text{corrected}} = \sqrt{19.84} \approx \mathbf{4.454}$.

Q9 & Q10 Case Study (4 Marks Total)

Case Context: Financial Investment Risk Analysis

A portfolio manager analyzes two mutual fund assets over a 5-year period. Fund Blue delivered an average annual return of 15% with a standard deviation of 3%. Fund Gold delivered an average annual return of 25% with a standard deviation of 6%.

Q9 (2 Marks): Calculate the Coefficient of Variation for both Fund Blue and Fund Gold.
Q10 (2 Marks): Which mutual fund provides higher return per unit of volatility risk, and which fund should a risk-averse conservative investor prefer?

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Solution to Q9 [2 Marks]:
• $CV_{\text{Blue}} = \frac{\sigma}{\bar{X}} \times 100 = \frac{3}{15} \times 100 = \mathbf{20\%}$.
• $CV_{\text{Gold}} = \frac{\sigma}{\bar{X}} \times 100 = \frac{6}{25} \times 100 = \mathbf{24\%}$.

Solution to Q10 [2 Marks]:
• Fund Blue has a lower Coefficient of Variation ($20\% < 24\%$), meaning it has lower relative volatility per unit of return.
• Recommendation: A risk-averse conservative investor should select Fund Blue because its returns are significantly more stable and consistent around the mean.
