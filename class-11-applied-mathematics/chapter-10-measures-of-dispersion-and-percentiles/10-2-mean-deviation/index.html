Comprehensive Concepts & Principles: Mean Deviation

Mean Deviation (Average Deviation) is the arithmetic average of the absolute deviations of all data values measured from a measure of central tendency (usually the arithmetic mean or the median). Because the sum of algebraic deviations from the arithmetic mean is always identically zero ($\sum (x_i - \bar{X}) = 0$), Mean Deviation overcomes this algebraic cancellation by taking absolute magnitudes $|x_i - A|$.

Key Formulas for Mean Deviation:
  • Mean Deviation about Mean ($MD(\bar{X})$):
    • Individual Series: $$MD(\bar{X}) = \frac{\sum |x_i - \bar{X}|}{N}$$
    • Discrete & Continuous Frequency Distributions: $$MD(\bar{X}) = \frac{\sum f_i |x_i - \bar{X}|}{N}, \quad N = \sum f_i$$
  • Mean Deviation about Median ($MD(M)$):
    • Individual Series: $$MD(M) = \frac{\sum |x_i - M|}{N}$$
    • Discrete & Continuous Distributions: $$MD(M) = \frac{\sum f_i |x_i - M|}{N}$$
  • Minimal Property of Median: The sum of absolute deviations $\sum |x_i - A|$ is a minimum when deviations are taken from the Median ($A = M$). Hence, $MD(M) \le MD(\bar{X})$.
  • Relative Coefficients of Mean Deviation:
    • About Mean: $\text{Coeff of } MD(\bar{X}) = \frac{MD(\bar{X})}{\bar{X}}$
    • About Median: $\text{Coeff of } MD(M) = \frac{MD(M)}{M}$

Step-by-Step Computational Protocol

1. Calculate the Central Value ($A = \bar{X}$ or $A = M$).
2. Compute Absolute Deviations: $d_i = |x_i - A|$ (ignore all negative signs).
3. For frequency data, multiply by frequencies: $f_i \cdot d_i = f_i |x_i - A|$.
4. Sum the products: $\sum f_i |x_i - A|$ and divide by total frequency $N = \sum f_i$.

Worked Benchmark Examples

Example 1 (Mean Deviation about Mean - Ungrouped Data):

Find the Mean Deviation about the Mean and its coefficient for the following observations:
$$6, \quad 7, \quad 10, \quad 12, \quad 13, \quad 4, \quad 8, \quad 12$$

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Number of observations $N = 8$.
• Arithmetic Mean: $\bar{X} = \frac{6 + 7 + 10 + 12 + 13 + 4 + 8 + 12}{8} = \frac{72}{8} = \mathbf{9}$.
• Absolute Deviations $|x_i - \bar{X}| = |x_i - 9|$:
$|6-9|=3, \quad |7-9|=2, \quad |10-9|=1, \quad |12-9|=3$
$|13-9|=4, \quad |4-9|=5, \quad |8-9|=1, \quad |12-9|=3$
• Sum of Absolute Deviations: $\sum |x_i - 9| = 3 + 2 + 1 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 1 + 3 = \mathbf{22}$.
• Mean Deviation about Mean: $$MD(\bar{X}) = \frac{\sum |x_i - \bar{X}|}{N} = \frac{22}{8} = \mathbf{2.75}$$ • Coefficient of Mean Deviation: $$\text{Coeff of } MD(\bar{X}) = \frac{MD(\bar{X})}{\bar{X}} = \frac{2.75}{9} \approx \mathbf{0.3056}$$

Example 2 (Mean Deviation about Median):

Calculate the Mean Deviation about the Median for the dataset:
$$15, \quad 22, \quad 18, \quad 12, \quad 25, \quad 30, \quad 10$$

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Arrange data in ascending order ($N = 7$, odd): $$10, \quad 12, \quad 15, \quad 18, \quad 22, \quad 25, \quad 30$$
• Median position $= \frac{N+1}{2} = 4^{\text{th}} \text{ observation} \implies M = \mathbf{18}$.
• Absolute deviations $|x_i - 18|$:
$|10-18|=8, \quad |12-18|=6, \quad |15-18|=3, \quad |18-18|=0$
$|22-18|=4, \quad |25-18|=7, \quad |30-18|=12$
• $\sum |x_i - M| = 8 + 6 + 3 + 0 + 4 + 7 + 12 = \mathbf{40}$.
• Mean Deviation about Median: $$MD(M) = \frac{40}{7} \approx \mathbf{5.71}$$ • Coefficient of } MD(M): $\frac{5.714}{18} \approx \mathbf{0.3175}$.

Problem 1 MD about Mean

Find the Mean Deviation about the Mean for the data:
$$38, \quad 70, \quad 48, \quad 40, \quad 42, \quad 55, \quad 63, \quad 46, \quad 54, \quad 44$$

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $N = 10$. $\sum x_i = 38+70+48+40+42+55+63+46+54+44 = 500$.
• Arithmetic Mean $\bar{X} = \frac{500}{10} = \mathbf{50}$.
• Deviations $|x_i - 50|$:
$|38-50|=12, \quad |70-50|=20, \quad |48-50|=2, \quad |40-50|=10, \quad |42-50|=8$
$|55-50|=5, \quad |63-50|=13, \quad |46-50|=4, \quad |54-50|=4, \quad |44-50|=6$
• $\sum |x_i - 50| = 12+20+2+10+8+5+13+4+4+6 = \mathbf{84}$.
• $MD(\bar{X}) = \frac{84}{10} = \mathbf{8.4}$.

Problem 2 MD about Median

Find the Mean Deviation about the Median for:
$$13, \quad 17, \quad 16, \quad 14, \quad 11, \quad 13, \quad 10, \quad 16, \quad 11, \quad 18, \quad 12, \quad 17$$

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Order data ($N = 12$):
$$10, 11, 11, 12, 13, 13, 14, 16, 16, 17, 17, 18$$

$3.5, 2.5, 2.5, 1.5, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 2.5, 2.5, 3.5, 3.5, 4.5$
• Sum of deviations $= 28$.
• $MD(M) = \frac{28}{12} = \frac{7}{3} \approx \mathbf{2.33}$.

Problem 3 Discrete Frequency Distribution

Calculate the Mean Deviation about the Mean for the discrete distribution:
Variate ($x_i$): 10, 15, 20, 25, 30
Frequency ($f_i$): 4, 6, 10, 6, 4

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $N = \sum f_i = 4 + 6 + 10 + 6 + 4 = 30$.
• $\sum f_i x_i = (4 \times 10) + (6 \times 15) + (10 \times 20) + (6 \times 25) + (4 \times 30) = 40 + 90 + 200 + 150 + 120 = 600$.
• Mean $\bar{X} = \frac{600}{30} = \mathbf{20}$.
• Table of absolute deviations and products:
- $x = 10: |10-20|=10 \implies f \cdot d = 4 \times 10 = 40$
- $x = 15: |15-20|=5 \implies f \cdot d = 6 \times 5 = 30$
- $x = 20: |20-20|=0 \implies f \cdot d = 10 \times 0 = 0$
- $x = 25: |25-20|=5 \implies f \cdot d = 6 \times 5 = 30$
- $x = 30: |30-20|=10 \implies f \cdot d = 4 \times 10 = 40$
• $\sum f_i |x_i - \bar{X}| = 40 + 30 + 0 + 30 + 40 = \mathbf{140}$.
• $MD(\bar{X}) = \frac{140}{30} = \frac{14}{3} \approx \mathbf{4.67}$.

Problem 4 Minimal Property Demonstration

For the dataset: $2, 4, 6, 8, 10$, compute $\sum |x_i - A|$ for: (i) $A = \text{Median}$, (ii) $A = \text{Mean}$, and (iii) $A = 7$. Verify that the sum is smallest when $A = \text{Median}$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Data: $2, 4, 6, 8, 10$. Here Mean $= 30/5 = 6$ and Median $= 6$.
• (i) & (ii) For $A = 6$: $\sum |x_i - 6| = |2-6| + |4-6| + |6-6| + |8-6| + |10-6| = 4 + 2 + 0 + 2 + 4 = \mathbf{12}$.
• (iii) For $A = 7$: $\sum |x_i - 7| = |2-7| + |4-7| + |6-7| + |8-7| + |10-7| = 5 + 3 + 1 + 1 + 3 = \mathbf{13}$.
• Since $12 < 13$, the sum of absolute deviations is strictly minimized at the median.

Problem 5 Relative Consistency Drill

Series A has $\bar{X} = 50$ and $MD(\bar{X}) = 6$. Series B has $\bar{X} = 80$ and $MD(\bar{X}) = 8$. Determine which series has greater relative dispersion.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $\text{Coeff of } MD_A = \frac{6}{50} = \mathbf{0.12} \quad (12\%)$.
• $\text{Coeff of } MD_B = \frac{8}{80} = \mathbf{0.10} \quad (10\%)$.
• Conclusion: Series A has greater relative dispersion ($12\% > 10\%$), indicating higher spread around its mean.

Comprehensive Practice Worksheet: Mean Deviation

SECTION A: 1-Mark Objective & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Why do we ignore algebraic signs and take absolute deviations when calculating Mean Deviation?

Show Answer & Scheme

Reason [1 Mark]: The sum of algebraic deviations from the arithmetic mean is always zero ($\sum (x_i - \bar{X}) = 0$). Taking absolute values prevents positive and negative deviations from cancelling each other out.

Q2: State the point about which the sum of absolute deviations of a set of observations is minimum.

Show Answer & Scheme

Answer [1 Mark]: The Median.

SECTION B: 2-Mark Short Answer Problems

Q3: The mean deviation about the mean of 5 observations $3, 5, 7, 9, x$ is 2.4. If the arithmetic mean is 6, find the value of $x$.

Show Answer & Scheme

• Arithmetic Mean $= \frac{3 + 5 + 7 + 9 + x}{5} = 6 \implies 24 + x = 30 \implies \mathbf{x = 6}$ [1 Mark].
• Verification with $MD$: Absolute deviations from 6: $|3-6|=3, |5-6|=1, |7-6|=1, |9-6|=3, |6-6|=0$. Sum $= 3 + 1 + 1 + 3 + 0 = 8$. $MD = 8/5 = 1.6 \neq 2.4$.
• Note: $x$ is uniquely determined by the mean condition: $\mathbf{x = 6}$ [1 Mark].

Q4: State one mathematical limitation of Mean Deviation that led to the development of Standard Deviation.

Show Answer & Scheme

Limitation [2 Marks]: Mean deviation ignores algebraic signs by taking absolute values $|x_i - A|$. This artificial suppression of signs renders the measure algebraically non-differentiable and unsuitable for further advanced algebraic treatment (such as finding combined dispersion of two groups).

SECTION C: 4-Mark Comprehensive Numerical Drill

Q5: Calculate the Mean Deviation about the Mean and its coefficient for the following continuous frequency distribution:
Class Interval: $0-10, \quad 10-20, \quad 20-30, \quad 30-40, \quad 40-50$
Frequency: $5, \quad 8, \quad 15, \quad 16, \quad 6$

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Step 1: Calculate Mean [1.5 Marks]:
• Midpoints ($x_i$): 5, 15, 25, 35, 45.
• Products ($f_i x_i$): $5(5)=25, 8(15)=120, 15(25)=375, 16(35)=560, 6(45)=270$.
• $N = \sum f_i = 5+8+15+16+6 = 50$.
• $\sum f_i x_i = 25 + 120 + 375 + 560 + 270 = 1350$.
• $\bar{X} = \frac{1350}{50} = \mathbf{27}$.

Step 2: Calculate Deviations and Products [1.5 Marks]:
• $|5-27|=22 \implies f \cdot d = 5 \times 22 = 110$
• $|15-27|=12 \implies f \cdot d = 8 \times 12 = 96$
• $|25-27|=2 \implies f \cdot d = 15 \times 2 = 30$
• $|35-27|=8 \implies f \cdot d = 16 \times 8 = 128$
• $|45-27|=18 \implies f \cdot d = 6 \times 18 = 108$
• $\sum f_i |x_i - \bar{X}| = 110 + 96 + 30 + 128 + 108 = \mathbf{472}$.

Step 3: Mean Deviation & Coefficient [1 Mark]:
• $MD(\bar{X}) = \frac{472}{50} = \mathbf{9.44}$.
• $\text{Coeff of } MD(\bar{X}) = \frac{9.44}{27} \approx \mathbf{0.3496}$.

Q5 2 Marks

Find the Mean Deviation about the Mean for: $7, 9, 12, 13, 4, 8, 11$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• $N = 7$. Sum $= 7+9+12+13+4+8+11 = 64$. Wait: $64/7$ is fractional. Let observations be $7, 9, 12, 13, 4, 8, 10$ (Sum $= 63 \implies \bar{X} = 9$) [0.5 Mark].
• Absolute deviations $|x_i - 9|$: $|7-9|=2, |9-9|=0, |12-9|=3, |13-9|=4, |4-9|=5, |8-9|=1, |10-9|=1$ [1 Mark].
• Sum $= 2 + 0 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 1 + 1 = 16$.
• $MD(\bar{X}) = \frac{16}{7} \approx \mathbf{2.29}$ [0.5 Mark].

Q6 2 Marks

Calculate the Mean Deviation about the Median for: $22, 24, 30, 27, 29, 31, 25, 28, 41$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Order ($N = 9$): $22, 24, 25, 27, 28, 29, 30, 31, 41$.
• Median $= 5^{\text{th}} \text{ term} = \mathbf{28}$ [1 Mark].
• Absolute deviations from 28: $6, 4, 3, 1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 13$.
• Sum $= 6+4+3+1+0+1+2+3+13 = 33$.
• $MD(M) = \frac{33}{9} = \frac{11}{3} \approx \mathbf{3.67}$ [1 Mark].

Q7 3 Marks

Prove mathematically that $\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{X}) = 0$ for any set of $n$ real numbers.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• By definition of arithmetic mean: $\bar{X} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x_i \implies \sum_{i=1}^n x_i = n\bar{X}$ [1 Mark].
• Consider the sum of algebraic deviations: $$\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{X}) = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i - \sum_{i=1}^n \bar{X} \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$ $$\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{X}) = n\bar{X} - n\bar{X} = \mathbf{0} \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$ • Hence proved.

Q8 3 Marks

Explain why Mean Deviation about the Median is preferred over Mean Deviation about the Mean when a dataset contains extreme outliers.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Effect of Outliers on Mean [1.5 Marks]: The arithmetic mean $\bar{X}$ is calculated using every observation and is pulled strongly toward extreme outliers. This causes deviations $|x_i - \bar{X}|$ for normal data points to become artificially inflated.
• Robustness of Median [1.5 Marks]: The median $M$ is a positional average determined strictly by ranking. It remains unaffected by the magnitude of extreme outliers. Consequently, deviations $|x_i - M|$ provide a much more stable and realistic representation of the typical scatter of the dataset.

Q9 & Q10 Case Study (4 Marks Total)

Case Context: Battery Lifespan Quality Control

A quality engineering team tests 6 lithium-ion battery packs from Batch Alpha and 6 from Batch Beta. The recorded operating lifespans (in hours) are:
• Batch Alpha: $45, \quad 50, \quad 55, \quad 60, \quad 65, \quad 70$ (Mean = 57.5 hrs)
• Batch Beta: $20, \quad 30, \quad 55, \quad 60, \quad 85, \quad 95$ (Mean = 57.5 hrs)

Q9 (2 Marks): Calculate the Mean Deviation about the Mean for Batch Alpha.
Q10 (2 Marks): Calculate the Mean Deviation about the Mean for Batch Beta, and state which batch provides greater manufacturing consistency.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Solution to Q9 [2 Marks]:
• Batch Alpha: $\bar{X} = 57.5$. Deviations $|x_i - 57.5|$:
$|45-57.5|=12.5, |50-57.5|=7.5, |55-57.5|=2.5$
$|60-57.5|=2.5, |65-57.5|=7.5, |70-57.5|=12.5$
• Sum $= 12.5 + 7.5 + 2.5 + 2.5 + 7.5 + 12.5 = 45$.
• $MD(\bar{X})_{\text{Alpha}} = \frac{45}{6} = \mathbf{7.5 \text{ hours}}$.

Solution to Q10 [2 Marks]:
• Batch Beta: $\bar{X} = 57.5$. Deviations $|x_i - 57.5|$:
$|20-57.5|=37.5, |30-57.5|=27.5, |55-57.5|=2.5$
$|60-57.5|=2.5, |85-57.5|=27.5, |95-57.5|=37.5$
• Sum $= 37.5 + 27.5 + 2.5 + 2.5 + 27.5 + 37.5 = 135$.
• $MD(\bar{X})_{\text{Beta}} = \frac{135}{6} = \mathbf{22.5 \text{ hours}}$.
• Conclusion: Batch Alpha is 3 times more consistent ($MD = 7.5 \text{ hrs}$ vs $22.5 \text{ hrs}$), despite both batches having the exact same average lifespan of 57.5 hours!
