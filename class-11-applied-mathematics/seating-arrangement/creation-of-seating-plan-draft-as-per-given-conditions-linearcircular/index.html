Linear Seating Arrangements

Linear arrangements involve placing $n$ distinct objects in a single row. The number of ways to arrange $n$ objects is given by the factorial of $n$. When specific conditions are introduced—such as individuals sitting together or restricted positions—we treat the 'together' group as a single entity and then multiply by the internal permutations of that group.

Number of permutations of n distinct objects = n! = n × (n-1) × (n-2) × ... × 1. If 'r' objects must be together, treat them as 1 unit, calculate (n-r+1)! arrangements, and multiply by r! for the internal arrangement.
Example 1: In how many ways can 5 students sit in a row if two specific students must always sit together?
Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Treat the two specific students as one single unit (a block).
• This leaves us with 4 entities (the block + 3 other students).
• The 4 entities can be arranged in 4! = 24 ways.
• The two students inside the block can switch places in 2! = 2 ways.
• Total arrangements = 24 * 2 = 48.

Answer: There are 48 ways to arrange the students under the given condition.

Circular Seating Arrangements

Circular arrangements differ from linear ones because there is no fixed 'start' or 'end' position. Fixing one position to break the symmetry, the number of ways to arrange $n$ distinct objects in a circle is $(n-1)!$. In cases where clockwise and anti-clockwise arrangements are considered identical (like a necklace or a garland), the formula is divided by 2.

Circular Permutation of n distinct items = (n-1)!. If clockwise and anti-clockwise are same: (n-1)! / 2.
Example 1: Find the number of ways 6 people can sit around a round table.
Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Identify n = 6.
• Apply the circular permutation formula (n-1)!.
• Calculate (6-1)! = 5!.
• 5! = 5 * 4 * 3 * 2 * 1 = 120.

Answer: There are 120 distinct ways to seat 6 people around a round table.

Section C: Short Answer Questions

1

Find the number of ways 4 people can be seated in a row if one person refuses to sit at either end.

Show Answer & Solution

Total arrangements = 4! = 24. If the person sits at an end, there are 2 positions (left or right) * 3! arrangements = 2 * 6 = 12. Total valid ways = 24 - 12 = 12.

2

How many ways can 4 beads of different colors be strung into a necklace?

Show Answer & Solution

For a necklace, circular permutation is (n-1)!/2. So, (4-1)!/2 = 3!/2 = 6/2 = 3 ways.

Section D: Long Answer Questions

1

A committee of 6 people sits at a round table. Two members insist on sitting together, and one member refuses to sit next to them. How many arrangements are possible?

Show Answer & Solution

1. Treat the 2 people as one block (X). We have 5 entities to arrange in a circle: (5-1)! = 24. 2. Since X can be arranged in 2 ways, 24 * 2 = 48 total ways where 2 sit together. 3. Subtract cases where the 3rd person (Y) sits next to the block. If Y is on one side, there are 2!*2! arrangements. Calculating valid arrangements excluding the restricted adjacent positions yields 24.

2

Six guests are to be seated in a row. A and B refuse to sit together. Calculate the total number of arrangements.

Show Answer & Solution

1. Total arrangements of 6 people = 6! = 720. 2. Treat A and B as one unit. We now have 5 units to arrange in 5! ways = 120. 3. Within the unit, A and B can arrange themselves in 2! ways = 2. 4. Total arrangements where A and B sit together = 120 * 2 = 240. 5. Total arrangements where A and B do not sit together = 720 - 240 = 480.

Section E: Case-Based Questions

1

Case Study: A school organizes a debate competition. 5 participants are to be seated in a row on a stage. However, the school has a rule that the captain and vice-captain must always sit at the two ends of the row.

Sub-question 1: In how many ways can the captain and vice-captain be placed at the ends?

Show Solution

There are 2 ends and 2 people, so they can be placed in 2! = 2 ways.

Sub-question 2: What is the total number of seating arrangements for all 5 participants?

Show Solution

With captain and vice-captain fixed at ends, the 3 remaining participants can be arranged in the 3 middle seats in 3! = 6 ways. Total arrangements = 2 * 6 = 12 ways.
