Locating the position of a person in a seating arrangement

Comprehensive guide on locating positions in linear and circular seating arrangements, covering coordinate-based logic, indexing, and relative positioning for CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics.

Linear Seating Arrangement

In a linear arrangement, individuals are placed in a row. The position is determined by their index from the left or right extreme. If a row has 'n' people, the position of a person from the right can be derived from their position from the left.

Position from Right = (Total Number of People - Position from Left) + 1
Example 1: In a row of 20 students, Rahul is standing at the 7th position from the left. What is his position from the right?
Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Identify given values: Total (n) = 20, Position from left = 7.
• Apply the formula: Position from right = (20 - 7) + 1.
• Calculate: 13 + 1 = 14.

Answer: Rahul is at the 14th position from the right.

Circular Seating Arrangement

In circular arrangements, there is no fixed beginning or end. Positions are defined relative to others (to the immediate left or right). For a circle of 'n' people, moving 'k' positions clockwise or counter-clockwise requires modular arithmetic thinking.

New Position = (Current Position ± Shift) mod n
Example 1: Five people (A, B, C, D, E) are sitting around a circular table. If A is at position 1, who is sitting 2 places to the right of A (moving clockwise)?
Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Assign positions 1 to 5 in a clockwise direction.
• Start at position 1 (A).
• Move 2 places clockwise: 1 + 2 = 3.
• Identify the person at position 3.

Answer: The person at position 3 is sitting 2 places to the right of A.

Section A: Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs)

1

In a classroom seating arrangement, if student A is at (3, 4) and student B is at (7, 7) on a coordinate grid, what is the horizontal distance between them?

Show Answer

✔ Correct: B

The horizontal distance is the difference in x-coordinates: |7 - 3| = 4 units.

2

If a seat is located at the midpoint of two students sitting at (2, 2) and (6, 8), what are the coordinates of the seat?

Show Answer

✔ Correct: A

Midpoint formula: ((x1+x2)/2, (y1+y2)/2) = ((2+6)/2, (2+8)/2) = (4, 5).

3

If a person moves 3 units right and 2 units up from position (1, 1), what is their new position?

Show Answer

✔ Correct: A

New x = 1 + 3 = 4; New y = 1 + 2 = 3. Position is (4, 3).

Section B: Assertion-Reason Questions

1

Assertion (A): The distance between two students at (0, 0) and (3, 4) is 5 units.
Reason (R): The distance formula is square root of ((x2-x1)^2 + (y2-y1)^2).

Directions: Choose the correct option:
(A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
(B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(C) A is true but R is false.
(D) A is false but R is true.

Show Answer

✔ Correct: A

Both Assertion and Reason are true, and the Reason is the correct explanation. Distance = sqrt(3^2 + 4^2) = sqrt(9+16) = 5.

2

Assertion (A): If three students are sitting at (1, 1), (2, 2), and (3, 3), they lie on a straight line.
Reason (R): Points are collinear if the slope between the first two is equal to the slope between the last two.

Directions: Choose the correct option:
(A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
(B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(C) A is true but R is false.
(D) A is false but R is true.

Show Answer

✔ Correct: A

Both Assertion and Reason are true. Slope of (1,1)-(2,2) is 1; Slope of (2,2)-(3,3) is 1. Since slopes are equal, they are collinear.

Section C: Short Answer Questions

1

Find the distance between student P at (1, 2) and student Q at (4, 6).

Show Answer & Solution

Using distance formula d = sqrt((4-1)^2 + (6-2)^2) = sqrt(3^2 + 4^2) = sqrt(9 + 16) = sqrt(25) = 5 units.

2

Determine the new position if a student at (5, 5) rotates 180 degrees around the origin.

Show Answer & Solution

Rotation by 180 degrees around the origin transforms (x, y) to (-x, -y). Thus, (5, 5) becomes (-5, -5).

Section D: Long Answer Questions

1

A teacher wants to place a desk such that it is equidistant from three students sitting at A(0, 0), B(4, 0), and C(0, 3). Find the coordinates of the desk.

Show Answer & Solution

Let the desk be at (x, y). Since it is equidistant, distance to A, B, and C must be equal. From dist(desk, A) = dist(desk, B): x^2 + y^2 = (x-4)^2 + y^2. This simplifies to x^2 = x^2 - 8x + 16, so 8x = 16, x = 2. From dist(desk, A) = dist(desk, C): x^2 + y^2 = x^2 + (y-3)^2. This simplifies to y^2 = y^2 - 6y + 9, so 6y = 9, y = 1.5. Desk is at (2, 1.5).

2

Four students form a square in a room at coordinates (1, 1), (4, 1), (4, 4), and (1, 4). Calculate the length of the diagonal of this seating arrangement.

Show Answer & Solution

Step 1: Identify vertices A(1, 1), B(4, 1), C(4, 4), D(1, 4). Step 2: The diagonal connects non-adjacent vertices, e.g., A(1, 1) and C(4, 4). Step 3: Use distance formula d = sqrt((x2-x1)^2 + (y2-y1)^2). Step 4: d = sqrt((4-1)^2 + (4-1)^2) = sqrt(3^2 + 3^2) = sqrt(9 + 9) = sqrt(18) = 3*sqrt(2) approx 4.24 units.

Section E: Case-Based Questions

1

Case Study: In a computer lab, five students are seated at specific coordinates: Amit(1, 2), Bhanu(1, 5), Chitra(5, 5), Divya(5, 2), and Esha(3, 3.5).

Sub-question 1: Which four students form a rectangular seating arrangement?

Show Solution

Amit(1, 2), Bhanu(1, 5), Chitra(5, 5), and Divya(5, 2) form a rectangle as their sides are parallel to axes and opposite sides are equal.

Sub-question 2: Is Esha exactly in the center of the rectangle formed by the other four students?

Show Solution

The center of the rectangle is the midpoint of diagonal AC: ((1+5)/2, (2+5)/2) = (3, 3.5). Since Esha is at (3, 3.5), she is exactly at the center.
