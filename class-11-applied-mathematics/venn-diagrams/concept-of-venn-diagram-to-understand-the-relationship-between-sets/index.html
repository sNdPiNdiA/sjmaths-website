Concept of Venn Diagram to Understand the Relationship Between Sets

Master Venn Diagrams for CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics. Learn set relationships, union, intersection, and complement with step-by-step examples.

Introduction to Venn Diagrams

A Venn diagram is a visual representation of sets using geometric shapes, typically circles enclosed within a rectangle representing the universal set (U). Elements are placed inside these shapes to illustrate the relationship between sets, such as inclusion, intersection, or disjoint status. It serves as a powerful tool to solve complex problems involving multiple sets by providing a clear spatial perspective.

n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B)
Example: Solved Example: Visualizing Set Union
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Draw a rectangle for U = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}.
Step 2: Draw two circles A = {1, 2, 3} and B = {3, 4, 5}.
Step 3: Place 3 in the overlapping region, 1 and 2 in A only, and 4 and 5 in B only.
Answer: The union A ∪ B contains elements {1, 2, 3, 4, 5}.

Operations on Sets via Venn Diagrams

Operations like intersection (A ∩ B) and complement (A') are easily identified through shaded regions in a Venn diagram. The intersection is represented by the common area shared by two circles, while the complement of set A consists of all elements in the universal set U that are not in A. These diagrams simplify the verification of set identities like De Morgan's Laws.

(A ∪ B)′ = A′ ∩ B′
Example: Solved Example: Finding the Complement
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Let U = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8} and A = {2, 4, 6, 8}.
Step 2: Shade the entire region inside the rectangle except for the circle representing A.
Step 3: Identify the remaining elements in the universal set.
Answer: A′ = {1, 3, 5, 7}.

Three-Set Venn Diagrams

When dealing with three sets A, B, and C, the Venn diagram consists of three overlapping circles. This allows for the analysis of regions representing elements belonging to exactly one, two, or all three sets simultaneously. The principle of inclusion-exclusion is essential for calculating the total number of elements in the union of three sets.

n(A ∪ B ∪ C) = n(A) + n(B) + n(C) − n(A ∩ B) − n(B ∩ C) − n(A ∩ C) + n(A ∩ B ∩ C)
Example: Solved Example: Calculating Union of Three Sets
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Given n(A)=10, n(B)=12, n(C)=8, n(A∩B)=4, n(B∩C)=3, n(A∩C)=2, n(A∩B∩C)=1.
Step 2: Apply the formula: 10 + 12 + 8 − 4 − 3 − 2 + 1.
Step 3: Calculate the sum: 30 − 9 + 1 = 22.
Answer: n(A ∪ B ∪ C) = 22.

1

Assertion (A): If A = {1, 2} and B = {2, 3}, then n(A ∪ B) = 3.
Reason (R): The formula n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B) accounts for the overlap element {2}.

Choose the correct option:
(A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
(B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(C) A is true but R is false.
(D) A is false but R is true.

Show Answer

✔ Correct: A

Both are true. A ∪ B = {1, 2, 3}, so n(A ∪ B) = 3. Formula gives 2 + 2 − 1 = 3.

2

Assertion (A): The complement of the universal set U is the empty set φ.
Reason (R): U′ = U − U = φ, which is consistent with Venn diagram shading.

Choose the correct option:
(A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
(B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(C) A is true but R is false.
(D) A is false but R is true.

Show Answer

✔ Correct: A

The complement of a set contains all elements in U not in the set. U contains all elements, so U′ has none.

Show Answer & Solution

Section D: Long Answer Questions

1

hardA survey of 100 students shows 40 study Physics, 30 study Chemistry, and 25 study Mathematics. 15 study P and C, 10 study C and M, 8 study P and M, and 5 study all three. Find the number of students who study: (i) Exactly one subject (ii) At least two subjects.

Show Answer & Solution

Step 1: Use Venn diagram regions. Let x = P∩C∩M = 5.
Step 2: P∩C only = 15 − 5 = 10; C∩M only = 10 − 5 = 5; P∩M only = 8 − 5 = 3.
Step 3: P only = 40 − (10 + 5 + 3) = 22; C only = 30 − (10 + 5 + 5) = 10; M only = 25 − (3 + 5 + 5) = 12.
Step 4: Exactly one = 22 + 10 + 12 = 44.
Step 5: At least two = (10 + 5 + 3) + 5 = 23.

2

very hardIn a class of 60 students, 30 play Football, 25 play Cricket, and 20 play Hockey. 10 play F and C, 8 play C and H, 7 play F and H, and 5 play all three. Calculate: (i) Number of students who play none of the games (ii) Number of students who play exactly two games.

Show Answer & Solution

Step 1: n(F∪C∪H) = 30 + 25 + 20 − (10 + 8 + 7) + 5 = 75 − 25 + 5 = 55.
Step 2: None = 60 − 55 = 5.
Step 3: Exactly two (F&C) = 10 − 5 = 5.
Step 4: Exactly two (C&H) = 8 − 5 = 3; Exactly two (F&H) = 7 − 5 = 2.
Step 5: Total exactly two = 5 + 3 + 2 = 10.

Section E: Case-Based Question

★

Case Study: A local club has 80 members. 45 members play Badminton, 35 play Table Tennis, and 20 play both. Every member plays at least one of the two games.

Sub-question 1: (i) Find the number of members who play only Badminton. (ii) Find the number of members who play only Table Tennis.

Show Solution

(i) Only Badminton = n(B) − n(B ∩ T) = 45 − 20 = 25. (ii) Only Table Tennis = n(T) − n(B ∩ T) = 35 − 20 = 15.

Sub-question 2: Verify if the total number of members matches the sum of those playing only B, only T, and both.

Show Solution

Sum = 25 (only B) + 15 (only T) + 20 (both) = 60. Since the total members are 80, there are 20 members who play neither, contradicting the premise that every member plays at least one.

CBSE Board & Sample Paper Past Year Questions

If A and B are two sets such that n(A)=20, n(B)=25, and n(A ∪ B)=40, find n(A ∩ B).

Show Answer & Solution

n(A ∩ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∪ B) = 20 + 25 − 40 = 5.

2
📋 CBSE 2022 📝 2 Marks

In a set of 40 elements, if n(A)=25 and n(B)=20, find the minimum possible value of n(A ∩ B).

Show Answer & Solution

n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B). Since n(A ∪ B) ≤ 40, 25 + 20 − n(A ∩ B) ≤ 40, so 45 − n(A ∩ B) ≤ 40, which means n(A ∩ B) ≥ 5.

3
📋 CBSE Sample Paper 2024 📝 2 Marks

If A = {x : x is a prime number < 10} and B = {x : x is an even number < 10}, find n(A ∪ B).

Show Answer & Solution

A = {2, 3, 5, 7}, B = {2, 4, 6, 8}. A ∪ B = {2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8}. n(A ∪ B) = 7.

4
📋 CBSE 2020 📝 4 Marks

Given n(U)=100, n(A)=50, n(B)=40, n(A ∩ B)=20. Find n(A′ ∩ B′).

Show Answer & Solution

n(A ∪ B) = 50 + 40 − 20 = 70. n(A′ ∩ B′) = n(U) − n(A ∪ B) = 100 − 70 = 30.
