Comprehensive Concepts & Principles: Utility Bills

Utility Billing mathematically models the consumption and cost accounting of essential public services, primarily electricity, municipal water, and piped natural gas (PNG). In CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics, utility bill computations apply piecewise step functions, progressive tiered pricing, standing fixed demand charges, regulatory fuel surcharges, and municipal duties.

Key Utility Billing Components:
  • Kilowatt-Hour (kWh) or "Unit": The standard commercial unit of electrical energy. $1 \text{ Unit} = 1 \text{ kWh} = 1000 \text{ watts} \times 3600 \text{ seconds} = 3.6 \times 10^6 \text{ Joules}$.
    $$\text{Units Consumed} = \text{Current Meter Reading} - \text{Previous Meter Reading}$$
  • Telescopic (Tiered) Tariff Slabs: A progressive pricing model designed to promote conservation, where increasing blocks of consumption are billed at progressively higher per-unit rates.
  • Fixed / Sanctioned Load Charges: A mandatory recurring monthly fee based on the customer's contracted maximum connected electrical load in kilowatts (kW) or kilovolt-amperes (kVA).
  • Fuel Surcharge Adjustment (FSA): A variable tariff component adjusted periodically by power utilities to reflect fluctuations in the wholesale price of coal and natural gas used for generation.
  • Electricity Duty: A statutory state government consumption tax levied as a percentage (e.g. 5% to 9%) on energy charges and fixed charges.
  • Water Consumption Unit: Billed in kilolitres (kL) where $1 \text{ kL} = 1,000 \text{ litres} = 1 \text{ m}^3$. Often includes a sewerage maintenance surcharge (typically 60% of basic water charges).

Mathematical Framework & Tariff Equations

Total monthly utility cost follows an affine piecewise linear function:

$$\text{Total Electricity Bill} = \text{Energy Charges (EC)} + \text{Fixed Charges (FC)} + \text{FSA} + \text{Electricity Duty (ED)} - \text{Rebates}$$
• $\text{Energy Charges } (EC) = \sum_{i=1}^k u_i \cdot r_i$ (where $u_i$ is units in slab $i$, $r_i$ is rate per unit)
• $\text{Fixed Charges } (FC) = \text{Connected Load (kW)} \times \text{Rate per kW}$
• $\text{FSA} = \text{Total Units Consumed} \times \text{FSA Rate per Unit}$
• $\text{Electricity Duty } (ED) = (EC + FC) \times \text{Duty Rate}\%$

Worked Benchmark Examples

Example 1 (Telescopic Electricity Bill Calculation):

A household with a sanctioned load of $3 \text{ kW}$ consumes $340 \text{ units}$ of electricity in a month. The tariff structure is:
• First 100 units @ ₹3.50/unit
• Next 100 units (101-200) @ ₹4.80/unit
• Next 140 units (above 200) @ ₹6.50/unit
Fixed charge is ₹75 per kW per month. Fuel surcharge is ₹0.30 per unit. Electricity duty is 5% on (Energy Charges + Fixed Charges). Calculate the total bill amount.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Compute Energy Charges (Telescopic Slabs):
• Slab 1 (First 100 units): $100 \times 3.50 = ₹350$
• Slab 2 (Next 100 units): $100 \times 4.80 = ₹480$
• Slab 3 (Remaining 140 units): $140 \times 6.50 = ₹910$
Total Energy Charges ($EC$) = $350 + 480 + 910 = \mathbf{₹1,740}$.

Step 2: Compute Fixed Charges:
Fixed Charges ($FC$) = $3 \text{ kW} \times 75 = \mathbf{₹225}$.

Step 3: Compute Fuel Surcharge (FSA):
$\text{FSA} = 340 \times 0.30 = \mathbf{₹102}$.

Step 4: Compute Electricity Duty (5%):
$\text{Base for Duty} = EC + FC = 1,740 + 225 = ₹1,965$.
$\text{Duty} = 5\% \text{ of } 1,965 = 0.05 \times 1,965 = \mathbf{₹98.25}$.

Step 5: Total Monthly Bill:
$\text{Total Bill} = 1,740 + 225 + 102 + 98.25 = \mathbf{₹2,165.25}$ (Rounded off to ₹2,165).

Example 2 (Municipal Water & Sewerage Bill):

A residential society consumes $45 \text{ kL}$ of water in a billing cycle. Slabs: First $20 \text{ kL}$ @ ₹5/kL; remaining consumption @ ₹12/kL. Sewerage charge is 60% of water charges, plus a flat meter service fee of ₹50. Calculate the total water bill.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Water Charges:
• First 20 kL: $20 \times 5 = ₹100$
• Next 25 kL (45 - 20): $25 \times 12 = ₹300$
Total Water Consumption Charges = $100 + 300 = \mathbf{₹400}$.

Sewerage Charges:
$\text{Sewerage} = 60\% \text{ of } 400 = 0.60 \times 400 = \mathbf{₹240}$.

Meter Fee: ₹50.
Total Water Bill: $400 + 240 + 50 = \mathbf{₹690}$.

Problem 1 Meter Reading & Slab Breakdown

An electricity meter displayed a reading of $6,842 \text{ kWh}$ on 1st October and $7,112 \text{ kWh}$ on 1st November. Calculate the energy charges if the tariff is: 0-100 units @ ₹3.00/unit, 101-200 units @ ₹4.50/unit, and above 200 units @ ₹6.00/unit.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Total Units Consumed:
$\text{Units} = 7,112 - 6,842 = \mathbf{270 \text{ units}}$.

Step 2: Telescopic Slab Allocation:
• First 100 units: $100 \times 3.00 = ₹300$
• Next 100 units (101-200): $100 \times 4.50 = ₹450$
• Next 70 units (201-270): $70 \times 6.00 = ₹420$

Total Energy Charges: $300 + 450 + 420 = \mathbf{₹1,170}$.

Problem 2 Full Commercial Electricity Bill

A retail shop with a connected load of $5 \text{ kW}$ consumes $520 \text{ units}$ in a month. Commercial tariff:
• Energy charges: Flat ₹7.50 per unit for all consumption.
• Fixed demand charge: ₹120 per kW per month.
• Electricity duty: 6% on total bill before duty.
• A rebate of 1% is offered for online payment before the due date.
Calculate the net amount payable if the shopkeeper pays online on time.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

1. Energy Charges: $520 \times 7.50 = \mathbf{₹3,900}$.
2. Fixed Demand Charges: $5 \times 120 = \mathbf{₹600}$.
Subtotal = $3,900 + 600 = ₹4,500$.
3. Electricity Duty (6%): $0.06 \times 4,500 = \mathbf{₹270}$.
Gross Bill Amount = $4,500 + 270 = \mathbf{₹4,770}$.
4. Online Payment Rebate (1%): $0.01 \times 4,770 = \mathbf{₹47.70}$.
Net Payable Amount: $4,770 - 47.70 = \mathbf{₹4,722.30}$ (Rounded off to ₹4,722).

Problem 3 Municipal Water Tariff with Sewerage

A family consumes $32 \text{ kL}$ of water in a month. Tariff: 0-15 kL @ ₹4/kL, 16-30 kL @ ₹8/kL, above 30 kL @ ₹15/kL. If sewerage surcharge is 60% of water charges and service charge is ₹40, compute the total water bill.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

1. Water Slab Charges:
• 0-15 kL (15 kL): $15 \times 4 = ₹60$
• 16-30 kL (15 kL): $15 \times 8 = ₹120$
• Above 30 kL (2 kL): $2 \times 15 = ₹30$
Total Water Charge = $60 + 120 + 30 = \mathbf{₹210}$.

2. Sewerage Surcharge (60%): $0.60 \times 210 = \mathbf{₹126}$.
3. Service Charge: ₹40.
Total Water Bill: $210 + 126 + 40 = \mathbf{₹376}$.

Problem 4 Piped Natural Gas (PNG) Tariff

A household PNG meter records consumption of $42 \text{ SCM}$ (Standard Cubic Meters) in a two-month cycle. Slabs: First 30 SCM @ ₹48/SCM; beyond 30 SCM @ ₹54/SCM. Fixed network access fee is ₹100. If GST is applicable at 5% on the entire bill, find the total amount payable.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

1. Gas Consumption Charges:
• First 30 SCM: $30 \times 48 = ₹1,440$
• Remaining 12 SCM (42 - 30): $12 \times 54 = ₹648$
Total Gas Charge = $1,440 + 648 = \mathbf{₹2,088}$.

2. Subtotal with Fixed Fee: $2,088 + 100 = \mathbf{₹2,188}$.
3. GST (5%): $0.05 \times 2,188 = \mathbf{₹109.40}$.
Total PNG Bill: $2,188 + 109.40 = \mathbf{₹2,297.40}$.

Q1. Physical Definition of Electrical Unit 1 Mark

Define one commercial unit of electricity in terms of power and time, and express it in Joules.

Show Model Solution

One commercial unit is 1 Kilowatt-hour (kWh), which is the electrical energy consumed by a device of 1,000 watts operating continuously for 1 hour.
$1 \text{ Unit} = 1000 \text{ W} \times 3600 \text{ s} = \mathbf{3.6 \times 10^6 \text{ Joules}}$.

Q2. Telescopic vs Non-Telescopic Tariff 1 Mark

What is the fundamental mathematical difference between a telescopic tariff and a non-telescopic tariff?

Show Model Solution

In a telescopic tariff, only units falling strictly within each respective slab are charged at that slab's rate. In a non-telescopic tariff, once total consumption crosses a threshold, the ENTIRE consumption from unit 1 is billed at the highest slab rate.

Q3. Connected Load Surcharge 1 Mark

If a customer with a 4 kW connected load is billed a fixed charge of ₹90 per kW per month, calculate the annual fixed demand charge.

Show Model Solution

Monthly Fixed Charge = $4 \times 90 = ₹360$.
Annual Fixed Charge = $360 \times 12 = \mathbf{₹4,320}$.

Q4. Two-Tier Apartment Complex Billing 2 Marks

An apartment complex consumes $1,800 \text{ units}$ through a bulk commercial meter. Tariff: Flat ₹6.80 per unit, fixed charge of ₹1,500, and electricity duty of 7%. Compute the total bill.

Show Model Solution

Fixed Charges = ₹1,500.
Subtotal = $12,240 + 1,500 = \mathbf{₹13,740}$.

Q5. Fuel Surcharge Sensitivity 2 Marks

A manufacturing unit consumes $14,000 \text{ units}$ in June and $16,500 \text{ units}$ in July. In July, the electricity board hikes the Fuel Surcharge Adjustment from ₹0.25 to ₹0.40 per unit. Calculate the net increase in the company's fuel surcharge expense in July compared to June.

Show Model Solution

Step 1: June Surcharge [1 Mark]:
$\text{FSA}_{\text{June}} = 14,000 \times 0.25 = \mathbf{₹3,500}$.

Step 2: July Surcharge [1 Mark]:
$\text{FSA}_{\text{July}} = 16,500 \times 0.40 = \mathbf{₹6,600}$.
Net Increase: $6,600 - 3,500 = \mathbf{₹3,100}$.

Q6. Comprehensive Municipal Utility Cost Model 4 Marks

A small hospital facility has monthly utility records as follows:
1. Electricity: Connected load $15 \text{ kW}$, consumption $2,400 \text{ units}$. Slabs: First 500 units @ ₹4.50, next 1,000 units @ ₹6.00, remaining @ ₹7.50. Fixed charge ₹100/kW/month. FSA ₹0.35/unit. Electricity duty 6% on (Energy + Fixed charges).
2. Water: Consumption $80 \text{ kL}$. First 30 kL @ ₹6/kL, next 50 kL @ ₹14/kL. Sewerage surcharge 60%, meter rent ₹150.
Calculate: (i) Total electricity bill, (ii) Total water bill, and (iii) Aggregate monthly utility expenditure.

Show Model Solution

(i) Electricity Bill Calculation [2 Marks]:
• Energy Charges: (500 × 4.50 = ₹2,250) + (1,000 × 6.00 = ₹6,000) + (900 × 7.50 = ₹6,750) = ₹15,000.
• Fixed Demand Charges: $15 \text{ kW} \times 100 = ₹1,500$.
• FSA: $2,400 \times 0.35 = ₹840$.
• Electricity Duty (6% of $15,000 + 1,500$): $0.06 \times 16,500 = ₹990$.
$\text{Total Electricity Bill} = 15,000 + 1,500 + 840 + 990 = \mathbf{₹18,330}$.

(ii) Water Bill Calculation [1.5 Marks]:
• Water Charges: (30 × 6 = ₹180) + (50 × 14 = ₹700) = ₹880.
• Sewerage (60%): $0.60 \times 880 = ₹528$.
• Meter Rent: ₹150.
$\text{Total Water Bill} = 880 + 528 + 150 = \mathbf{₹1,558}$.

(iii) Aggregate Utility Expenditure [0.5 Mark]:
$\text{Total Utility Cost} = 18,330 + 1,558 = \mathbf{₹19,888}$.

Printable high-yield summary, tariff formulas, and billing calculation protocols for Utility Bills in CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics.

Systematic Electricity Billing Formulae

1. $\text{Units Consumed} = \text{Current Reading} - \text{Previous Reading}$
2. $\text{Energy Charges (EC)} = \sum_{i} (\text{Units in Slab } i) \times (\text{Rate } i)$
3. $\text{Fixed Charges (FC)} = \text{Sanctioned Load (kW)} \times \text{Rate per kW}$
4. $\text{FSA} = \text{Total Units} \times \text{Fuel Surcharge per Unit}$
5. $\text{Electricity Duty (ED)} = (EC + FC) \times \text{Duty}\%$
6. $\text{Gross Bill} = EC + FC + FSA + ED$
7. $\text{Net Bill} = \text{Gross Bill} - \text{Rebates} + \text{Late Surcharges}$

Water & Gas Tariff Rules

Municipal Water Billing

Measured in kilolitres (kL).
$\text{Bill} = \text{Slab Charges} + \text{Sewerage (60%)} + \text{Meter Fee}$.

Piped Natural Gas (PNG)

Measured in SCM (Standard Cubic Meters).
$\text{Bill} = (\text{Gas Consumed} \times \text{Slab Rate}) + \text{Fixed Access Fee} + \text{GST (5%)}$.

Common Exam Traps

  • Slab Allocation Overflow: When allocating 340 units across slabs of 100, 100, and above, do not multiply 340 by the highest rate! Break it strictly into 100, 100, and 140.
  • Duty Base: Electricity duty is usually computed on $(EC + FC)$, NOT including the Fuel Surcharge Adjustment (FSA) unless specifically stated.

Question 9 & 10 Case Study Case Study • 4 Marks

Case Background: Energy Efficiency Audit in a School Campus
A secondary school campus with a sanctioned load of 20 kW consumed 3,200 units in April under standard halogen lighting. In May, the school retrofitted all classrooms with LED lighting, reducing energy consumption by 25%.
Tariff: Flat ₹7.00/unit; fixed demand charge ₹90/kW/month; FSA ₹0.25/unit; duty 6% on (EC + FC).

Q9 (2 Marks): Calculate the school's total electricity bill for April.
Q10 (2 Marks): Calculate the May electricity bill post-LED retrofit and state the net monthly financial savings achieved.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Solution to Q9 (April Bill) [2 Marks]:
• EC ($3,200 \times 7$): ₹22,400.
• FC ($20 \times 90$): ₹1,800.
• FSA ($3,200 \times 0.25$): ₹800.
• Duty (6% of $22,400 + 1,800 = 24,200$): $0.06 \times 24,200 = ₹1,452$.
Total April Bill = $22,400 + 1,800 + 800 + 1,452 = \mathbf{₹26,452}$.

Solution to Q10 (May Bill & Savings) [2 Marks]:
• May Consumption (25% reduction): $3,200 \times 0.75 = 2,400 \text{ units}$.
• EC ($2,400 \times 7$): ₹16,800.
• FC ($20 \times 90$): ₹1,800.
• FSA ($2,400 \times 0.25$): ₹600.
• Duty (6% of $16,800 + 1,800 = 18,600$): $0.06 \times 18,600 = ₹1,116$.
Total May Bill = $16,800 + 1,800 + 600 + 1,116 = \mathbf{₹20,316}$.
Monthly Savings: $26,452 - 20,316 = \mathbf{₹6,136}$.
