Comprehensive Concepts & Principles: Goods and Services Tax (GST)

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based indirect consumption tax levied on the manufacture, sale, and consumption of goods and services throughout India. In CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics, GST applications provide practical commercial training in percentage markups, multi-tiered supply chains, value addition, and Input Tax Credit (ITC) balance accounting.

Key GST Architectural Components:
  • Intra-State Supply: When the location of the supplier and the place of supply are within the same state or union territory. Tax is split equally:
    $$\text{CGST Rate} = \text{SGST Rate} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Total GST Rate}$$
  • Inter-State Supply: When the location of the supplier and the place of supply are in different states/UTs, or in cases of import/export. Integrated GST (IGST) is levied at the full rate: $\text{IGST} = \text{CGST} + \text{SGST}$.
  • Input Tax Credit (ITC): The tax paid by a registered dealer on purchases of business inputs. Dealers can deduct Input GST from Output GST collected on sales, remitting only the net tax to the government:
    $$\text{Net GST Payable} = \text{Output GST (on Sales)} - \text{Input GST (ITC on Purchases)}$$
  • Taxable Value (Selling Price): GST is ALWAYS computed on the net discounted price:
    $$\text{Taxable Value} = \text{List Price (MRP)} - \text{Trade Discount}$$
  • Invoice Total: $\text{Total Invoice Amount} = \text{Taxable Value} + \text{CGST} + \text{SGST}$ (or $+\text{IGST}$).

Mathematical Framework & Invoicing Formulations

GST computations follow exact linear commercial percentage relationships across transaction stages:

Intra-State Invoicing Equations

  • $\text{Taxable Value } V = \text{List Price} \times (1 - d)$, where $d$ is discount rate.
  • $\text{CGST} = V \times \frac{r}{200}$
  • $\text{SGST} = V \times \frac{r}{200}$
  • $\text{Total Amount} = V \times \left(1 + \frac{r}{100}\right)$

Inter-State Invoicing Equations

  • $\text{Taxable Value } V = \text{List Price} \times (1 - d)$
  • $\text{IGST} = V \times \frac{r}{100}$
  • $\text{Total Amount} = V + \text{IGST} = V \times \left(1 + \frac{r}{100}\right)$
  • $\text{Net IGST Payable} = \text{Output IGST} - \text{Input ITC}$

Worked Benchmark Examples

Example 1 (Intra-State Retail Sale with Discount):

A laptop has a marked price of ₹60,000. A dealer in Mumbai sells it to a consumer in Pune at a discount of 10%. If GST is 18%, calculate: (i) Taxable Value, (ii) CGST and SGST, (iii) Total bill amount paid by the consumer.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Compute Taxable Value:
Discount = $10\% \text{ of } 60,000 = ₹6,000$.
$\text{Taxable Value} = 60,000 - 6,000 = \mathbf{₹54,000}$.

Step 2: Compute CGST & SGST (Intra-State Supply within Maharashtra):
Since both Mumbai and Pune are in Maharashtra, supply is Intra-State.
GST rate = $18\% \implies \text{CGST} = 9\%$, $\text{SGST} = 9\%$.
$\text{CGST} = 9\% \text{ of } 54,000 = 0.09 \times 54,000 = \mathbf{₹4,860}$.
$\text{SGST} = 9\% \text{ of } 54,000 = 0.09 \times 54,000 = \mathbf{₹4,860}$.

Step 3: Total Bill Amount:
$\text{Total Amount} = 54,000 + 4,860 + 4,860 = \mathbf{₹63,720}$.

Example 2 (Input Tax Credit Across Supply Chain):

A manufacturer sells goods to a wholesaler for ₹50,000. The wholesaler sells them to a retailer at a profit of ₹10,000. The retailer sells to the end consumer at a profit of ₹8,000. If GST rate is 12% and all transactions are intra-state, calculate the net tax paid by the wholesaler and the total tax collected by the government.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

1. Wholesaler Stage:
• Purchase price = ₹50,000. Input GST paid = $12\% \text{ of } 50,000 = ₹6,000$ (Input Credit).
• Selling price to retailer = $50,000 + 10,000 = ₹60,000$.
• Output GST collected = $12\% \text{ of } 60,000 = ₹7,200$.
• Net GST paid by Wholesaler = $\text{Output GST} - \text{Input GST} = 7,200 - 6,000 = \mathbf{₹1,200}$ (CGST ₹600 + SGST ₹600).

2. Retailer Stage:
• Selling price to consumer = $60,000 + 8,000 = ₹68,000$.
• Output GST collected = $12\% \text{ of } 68,000 = ₹8,160$.
• Net GST paid by Retailer = $8,160 - 7,200 = \mathbf{₹960}$ (CGST ₹480 + SGST ₹480).

3. Total Government Revenue:
Total GST = Manufacturer (₹6,000) + Wholesaler (₹1,200) + Retailer (₹960) = $\mathbf{₹8,160}$.
Note: Total tax collected matches exactly $12\%$ of final retail price ($12\% \text{ of } ₹68,000 = ₹8,160$).

Problem 1 Intra-State Invoicing Drill

A dealer in Jaipur (Rajasthan) sells kitchen appliances with a marked price of ₹35,000 to a customer in Udaipur (Rajasthan) at a trade discount of 15%. If the GST rate is 18%, calculate: (i) the Taxable Value, (ii) the CGST amount, (iii) the SGST amount, and (iv) the total invoice value.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Compute Taxable Value:
Trade Discount = $15\% \text{ of } 35,000 = 0.15 \times 35,000 = ₹5,250$.
$\text{Taxable Value} = 35,000 - 5,250 = \mathbf{₹29,750}$.

Step 2: Compute CGST & SGST (Intra-State Supply in Rajasthan):
CGST rate = $9\%$, SGST rate = $9\%$.
$\text{CGST} = 9\% \text{ of } 29,750 = 0.09 \times 29,750 = \mathbf{₹2,677.50}$.
$\text{SGST} = 9\% \text{ of } 29,750 = 0.09 \times 29,750 = \mathbf{₹2,677.50}$.

Step 3: Total Invoice Value:
$\text{Total Amount} = 29,750 + 2,677.50 + 2,677.50 = \mathbf{₹35,105}$.

Problem 2 Inter-State Supply (IGST)

A manufacturer in Surat (Gujarat) sells 500 meters of silk fabric to a boutique in Bengaluru (Karnataka). The invoice rate is ₹400 per meter with a 5% bulk discount. If the applicable GST rate is 5%, determine the type of GST applicable and calculate the total tax amount and invoice total.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Taxable Value:
Gross Value = $500 \times 400 = ₹2,00,000$.
Discount ($5\%$) = $0.05 \times 2,00,000 = ₹10,000$.
$\text{Taxable Value} = 2,00,000 - 10,00, = \mathbf{₹1,90,000}$.

Step 2: Applicable Tax Type & Calculation:
Since supplier is in Gujarat and buyer in Karnataka, the transaction is Inter-State Supply.
Therefore, Integrated GST (IGST) is applicable at $5\%$.
$\text{IGST Amount} = 5\% \text{ of } 1,90,000 = 0.05 \times 1,90,000 = \mathbf{₹9,500}$.

Step 3: Total Invoice Total:
$\text{Total Invoice Amount} = 1,90,000 + 9,500 = \mathbf{₹1,99,500}$.

Problem 3 Input Tax Credit (ITC) Settlement

A registered trader in Delhi buys raw materials for ₹1,20,000 and pays 18% GST. He processes the materials incurring manufacturing expenses of ₹30,000 and sells the finished goods within Delhi for ₹2,10,000 (GST 18%). Compute: (i) Input Tax Credit, (ii) Output GST, and (iii) Net GST payable to the government.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Input Tax Credit (ITC):
$\text{Input GST paid on purchases} = 18\% \text{ of } 1,20,000 = \mathbf{₹21,600}$ (CGST ₹10,800 + SGST ₹10,800).

Step 2: Output GST on Sales:
$\text{Output GST collected on sales} = 18\% \text{ of } 2,10,000 = \mathbf{₹37,800}$ (CGST ₹18,900 + SGST ₹18,900).

Step 3: Net GST Payable to Government:
$\text{Net CGST Payable} = 18,900 - 10,800 = \mathbf{₹8,100}$.
$\text{Net SGST Payable} = 18,900 - 10,800 = \mathbf{₹8,100}$.
$\text{Total Net GST Payable} = 8,100 + 8,100 = \mathbf{₹16,200}$.
Check via Value Addition: Value Added = $2,10,000 - 1,20,000 = ₹90,000$. Tax on Value Added = $18\% \text{ of } 90,000 = ₹16,200$.

Problem 4 Comprehensive Multi-Item Invoice

A retail hardware store prepares an intra-state invoice with the following items:
• Item A: ₹12,000 (GST rate 12%)
• Item B: ₹25,000 (GST rate 18%)
• Item C: ₹8,000 (GST rate 28%)
Compute the total CGST, total SGST, and the final grand invoice total.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Compute Taxes per Item:

  • Item A: CGST ($6\%$) = ₹720, SGST ($6\%$) = ₹720
  • Item B: CGST ($9\%$) = ₹2,250, SGST ($9\%$) = ₹2,250
  • Item C: CGST ($14\%$) = ₹1,120, SGST ($14\%$) = ₹1,120

Total CGST = $720 + 2,250 + 1,120 = \mathbf{₹4,090}$.
Total SGST = $720 + 2,250 + 1,120 = \mathbf{₹4,090}$.
Total Taxable Value = $12,000 + 25,000 + 8,000 = ₹45,000$.
Grand Invoice Total: $45,000 + 4,090 + 4,090 = \mathbf{₹53,180}$.

Section A: Conceptual & Objective Drill 3 Questions • 1 Mark Each

Q1. CGST & SGST Proportion 1 Mark

If the total GST on an intra-state sale of electronic goods is ₹7,200, state the exact amounts allocated to CGST and SGST.

Show Model Solution

In an intra-state transaction, the total GST is shared equally between the Central Government (CGST) and the State Government (SGST):
$\text{CGST} = \text{SGST} = \frac{₹7,200}{2} = \mathbf{₹3,600}$.

Q2. Inter-State Supply Tax Identification 1 Mark

A dealer in Punjab sells machine parts to a factory in Haryana. Name the specific tax component collected on this transaction and state which government collects it.

Show Model Solution

Since the transaction occurs between two different states (Punjab and Haryana), it is an inter-state supply. The applicable tax is Integrated GST (IGST), collected by the Central Government.

Q3. Destination-Based Principle Definition 1 Mark

Explain why GST is classified as a destination-based consumption tax.

Show Model Solution

GST is destination-based because tax revenue accrues to the state where the goods or services are finally consumed, rather than the originating state where they were produced or manufactured.

Q4. Discount & Multi-Tax Calculation 2 Marks

A shopkeeper in Bhopal offers a 20% discount on a sofa set with a marked price of ₹40,000. If the customer resides in Bhopal and GST is 12%, calculate the CGST, SGST, and the total invoice amount.

Show Model Solution

Step 1: Taxable Value [1 Mark]:
Discount = $0.20 \times 40,000 = ₹8,000$.
Taxable Value = $40,000 - 8,000 = \mathbf{₹32,000}$.

Step 2: Tax Calculation [1 Mark]:
Intra-State: CGST = $6\%$, SGST = $6\%$.
$\text{CGST} = 0.06 \times 32,000 = \mathbf{₹1,920}$.
$\text{SGST} = 0.06 \times 32,000 = \mathbf{₹1,920}$.
$\text{Total Invoice Amount} = 32,000 + 1,920 + 1,920 = \mathbf{₹35,840}$.

Q5. Input Tax Credit Offsetting 2 Marks

A trader has an Input Tax Credit of ₹18,000 (CGST ₹9,000, SGST ₹9,000). During the tax period, his output tax collected on sales is ₹26,000 (CGST ₹13,000, SGST ₹13,000). Calculate his net tax payment to the Central and State Governments.

Show Model Solution

Step 1: Set off CGST [1 Mark]:
$\text{Net CGST Payable} = \text{Output CGST} - \text{Input CGST} = 13,000 - 9,000 = \mathbf{₹4,000}$.

Step 2: Set off SGST [1 Mark]:
$\text{Net SGST Payable} = \text{Output SGST} - \text{Input SGST} = 13,000 - 9,000 = \mathbf{₹4,000}$.
Total Net Tax Paid = ₹8,000.

Section C: Comprehensive Board-Level Problem 1 Question • 4 Marks

Q6. Three-Tier Supply Chain Tax Ledger 4 Marks

A manufacturer in Kanpur (UP) sells machinery to a wholesaler in Kanpur for ₹1,00,000 (GST 18%). The wholesaler sells it to a retailer in Lucknow (UP) for ₹1,30,000. Finally, the retailer sells it to an industrial user in Lucknow for ₹1,60,000. All transactions are intra-state.
Calculate:
(i) Total tax collected by the Central Government (CGST).
(ii) Total tax collected by the State Government of UP (SGST).
(iii) Net tax paid by the wholesaler and the retailer respectively.
(iv) The total price paid by the final industrial user.

Show Model Solution

(i) & (ii) Total Government Revenues [1.5 Marks]:
Since GST is a consumption tax on value addition, the total tax equals the GST on the final consumer sale ($₹1,60,000 @ 18\%$):
Total GST = $0.18 \times 1,60,000 = ₹28,800$.
• Total CGST collected: $\frac{₹28,800}{2} = \mathbf{₹14,400}$.
• Total SGST collected: $\frac{₹28,800}{2} = \mathbf{₹14,400}$.

(iii) Stage-by-Stage Net Tax Payments [1.5 Marks]:
• Manufacturer: Pays $18\% \text{ of } 1,00,000 = \mathbf{₹18,000}$ (CGST ₹9,000, SGST ₹9,000).
• Wholesaler: Output Tax ($18\% \text{ of } 1,30,000 = ₹23,400$) - Input Tax ($₹18,000$) = $\mathbf{₹5,400}$ (CGST ₹2,700, SGST ₹2,700).
• Retailer: Output Tax ($18\% \text{ of } 1,60,000 = ₹28,800$) - Input Tax ($₹23,400$) = $\mathbf{₹5,400}$ (CGST ₹2,700, SGST ₹2,700).
Sum of taxes: $18,000 + 5,400 + 5,400 = ₹28,800$. Verified!

(iv) Total Price Paid by End User [1 Mark]:
$\text{Total Cost} = 1,60,000 + 28,800 = \mathbf{₹1,88,800}$.

Exercise 14.2 Comprehensive Revision Sheet

Printable high-yield summary, supply chain flow charts, and quick reconciliation checklists for Goods and Services Tax in CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics.

GST Structural Decision Matrix

Intra-State Supply (Within State)

Supplier & Buyer in same State/UT.
Tax: CGST ($r/2$) + SGST ($r/2$).
Split equally between Centre and State.

Inter-State Supply (Between States)

Supplier & Buyer in different States/UTs.
Tax: IGST (Full rate $r$).
Collected by Centre and apportioned to destination State.

Input Tax Credit (ITC) Rule

$\text{Net Tax Payable} = \text{Output GST} - \text{Input Credit}$.
Prevents cascading tax-on-tax effect.

Essential Formulae & Invoicing Identities

• $\text{Taxable Value } (V) = \text{Marked Price} - \text{Trade Discount}$
• $\text{CGST} = \text{SGST} = V \times \frac{\text{GST Rate}}{200}$
• $\text{IGST} = V \times \frac{\text{GST Rate}}{100}$
• $\text{Total Invoice Amount} = V + \text{CGST} + \text{SGST} = V \times \left(1 + \frac{\text{GST Rate}}{100}\right)$
• $\text{Tax on Value Addition} = (\text{Selling Price} - \text{Purchase Price}) \times \text{GST Rate}$

Common Exam Pitfalls

  • Discount Order Trap: Never compute GST on the Marked Price before applying trade discounts. The statute mandates GST only on actual transaction value after discount.
  • IGST Split Error: Never split IGST into two halves! IGST is levied as a single integrated tax at the full statutory rate.
  • Cash Discount vs Trade Discount: Trade discount is deducted from list price to arrive at taxable value. Cash discount given for prompt payment does not alter the taxable supply value for GST.

For an intra-state transaction where the applicable GST rate is 18%, what are the respective rates of CGST and SGST?

Explanation: Intra-state GST is split equally between CGST (9%) and SGST (9%).
Question 2 MCQ • 1 Mark

A trader pays ₹14,000 as GST on purchasing merchandise and collects ₹22,000 as GST on selling it. How much net tax does he deposit with the government?

Correct Answer: (C) ₹8,000
Explanation: $\text{Net GST} = \text{Output GST} - \text{Input ITC} = 22,000 - 14,000 = ₹8,000$.
Question 3 MCQ • 1 Mark

When goods are transported from a supplier in Kerala to a buyer in Tamil Nadu, which tax is levied?

Correct Answer: (C) Integrated GST (IGST)
Explanation: Supply between two different states is an inter-state supply subject to IGST.
Question 4 Assertion-Reason • 1 Mark

Assertion (A): Under the GST regime, cascading of taxes (tax on tax) is eliminated across the supply chain.
Reason (R): Input Tax Credit (ITC) allows every registered intermediary to deduct the tax already paid on purchases from the output tax liability.

Correct Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) explains (A).
Explanation: Continuous seamless credit of input taxes eliminates cascading.

Question 5 2 Marks

A home appliance has a marked price of ₹45,000 and is sold at a 10% discount in an intra-state sale. If the GST rate is 18%, calculate the CGST and SGST.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Step 1: Compute Taxable Value [1 Mark]:
Discount = $0.10 \times 45,000 = ₹4,500$.
Taxable Value = $45,000 - 4,500 = \mathbf{₹40,500}$.

Step 2: Compute CGST & SGST [1 Mark]:
$\text{CGST} = 9\% \text{ of } 40,500 = 0.09 \times 40,500 = \mathbf{₹3,645}$.
$\text{SGST} = 9\% \text{ of } 40,500 = 0.09 \times 40,500 = \mathbf{₹3,645}$.

Question 6 2 Marks

Explain the difference between an Exempt Supply and a Zero-Rated Supply under the GST regime.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Exempt Supply [1 Mark]: Supplies of goods/services that attract 0% GST or are specifically exempt (e.g. fresh milk, unbranded food grains). Crucially, no Input Tax Credit can be claimed on inputs used to produce exempt goods.

Zero-Rated Supply [1 Mark]: Supplies exported outside India or to Special Economic Zones (SEZs). The output tax is 0%, but the exporter is entitled to claim full ITC refund on inputs.

Section C: Short Answer Type II (Q7 – Q8) 2 Questions • 3 Marks Each

Question 7 3 Marks

A dealer in Delhi buys an air conditioner for ₹30,000 from a manufacturer in Haryana (Inter-state supply, GST 28%). He spends ₹2,000 on transportation and sells it to a customer in Delhi for ₹38,000 (Intra-state supply). Calculate:
(i) Input IGST paid,
(ii) Output CGST and SGST collected, and
(iii) Net tax payable by the dealer to the government.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

(i) Input IGST [1 Mark]:
$\text{Input IGST} = 28\% \text{ of } 30,000 = 0.28 \times 30,000 = \mathbf{₹8,400}$.

(ii) Output Tax on Intra-State Sale [1 Mark]:
Output CGST ($14\%$) = $0.14 \times 38,000 = \mathbf{₹5,320}$.
Output SGST ($14\%$) = $0.14 \times 38,000 = \mathbf{₹5,320}$.
Total Output Tax = $5,320 + 5,320 = ₹10,640$.

(iii) Net Tax Payable after ITC Utilization [1 Mark]:
Under GST cross-utilization rules, Input IGST of ₹8,400 can be used first against Output CGST (₹5,320), leaving an IGST credit balance of $8,400 - 5,320 = ₹3,080$.
This remaining balance of ₹3,080 is then applied against Output SGST (₹5,320):
Net SGST payable in cash = $5,320 - 3,080 = \mathbf{₹2,240}$.
Net CGST payable in cash = $\mathbf{₹0}$.
Total Net Cash Tax Deposited: ₹2,240.

Question 8 3 Marks

A retail store issues an intra-state tax invoice for two items:
• Article 1: List price ₹8,000, trade discount 10%, GST rate 12%
• Article 2: List price ₹15,000, trade discount 20%, GST rate 18%
Calculate the net invoice total payable by the customer.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Article 1 [1.5 Marks]:
Taxable Value = $8,000 - (0.10 \times 8,000) = ₹7,200$.
GST ($12\%$) = $0.12 \times 7,200 = ₹864$ (CGST ₹432 + SGST ₹432).
Amount 1 = $7,200 + 864 = ₹8,064$.

Article 2 [1.5 Marks]:
Taxable Value = $15,000 - (0.20 \times 15,000) = ₹12,000$.
GST ($18\%$) = $0.18 \times 12,000 = ₹2,160$ (CGST ₹1,080 + SGST ₹1,080).
Amount 2 = $12,000 + 2,160 = ₹14,160$.
Total Net Invoice Total: $8,064 + 14,160 = \mathbf{₹22,224}$.

Question 9 & 10 Case Study Case Study • 4 Marks

Case Background: Automotive Dealership Supply Chain Audit
An authorized commercial vehicle dealer in Kolkata (West Bengal) purchases 5 electric delivery vans from a manufacturing plant in Pune (Maharashtra) for ₹8,00,000 each (GST rate 5%). The dealer incurs ₹50,000 total freight charges (GST 5%). He then sells all 5 vans to a logistics firm in Kolkata (intra-state) at a profit markup of ₹1,00,000 per vehicle.

Q9 (2 Marks): Calculate the total Input Tax Credit (ITC) accumulated by the Kolkata dealer on the vehicle purchase and transportation.
Q10 (2 Marks): Compute the dealer's output tax liability and the net GST payable in cash to the government.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Solution to Q9 [2 Marks]:
• Vehicle Purchase (Inter-State from Maharashtra to West Bengal):
5 vehicles @ ₹8,00,000 = ₹40,00,000.
Input IGST on vehicles = $5\% \text{ of } 40,00,000 = \mathbf{₹2,00,000}$.
• Freight charges = ₹50,000. Input IGST on freight = $5\% \text{ of } 50,000 = \mathbf{₹2,500}$.
Total Input Tax Credit (ITC) = ₹2,02,500 (IGST).

Solution to Q10 [2 Marks]:
• Selling Price to Logistics firm in Kolkata (Intra-State):
Selling Price per vehicle = $8,00,000 + 1,00,000 = ₹9,00,000$.
Total Selling Price for 5 vehicles = $5 \times 9,00,000 = ₹45,00,000$.
Output GST ($5\%$) = $0.05 \times 45,00,000 = ₹2,25,000$ (CGST ₹1,12,500 + SGST ₹1,12,500).
• ITC Utilization:
Total Output GST = ₹2,25,000.
Available Input IGST = ₹2,02,500.
$\text{Net Tax Payable in Cash} = 2,25,000 - 2,02,500 = \mathbf{₹22,500}$ (Split as CGST ₹11,250 + SGST ₹11,250).
Note: Notice that ₹22,500 is exactly $5\%$ of the dealer's net value addition ($₹45,00,000 - ₹40,50,000 = ₹4,50,000 \times 0.05 = ₹22,500$).
