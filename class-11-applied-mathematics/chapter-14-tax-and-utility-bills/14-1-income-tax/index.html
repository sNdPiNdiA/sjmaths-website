Comprehensive Concepts & Principles: Income Tax

Income Tax is an annual direct tax levied by the Central Government of India on the net taxable income earned by individuals, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), firms, and companies during a financial year. In CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics, income tax principles connect linear algebra, piecewise step functions, percentage calculations, and financial decision-making.

Key Tax Terms & Definitions:
  • Financial Year (FY) vs Assessment Year (AY): The Financial Year is the 12-month period (1 April to 31 March) in which income is earned. The Assessment Year is the immediately following year in which the income of the FY is evaluated and taxed. Example: Income earned in FY 2024-25 is assessed in AY 2025-26.
  • Gross Total Income (GTI): Aggregate income from all five taxable heads: (i) Income from Salaries, (ii) Income from House Property, (iii) Profits and Gains of Business/Profession, (iv) Capital Gains, and (v) Income from Other Sources.
  • Standard Deduction: A flat statutory deduction of ₹50,000 available to salaried individuals and pensioners without requiring proof of actual expense.
  • Chapter VI-A Deductions: Exemptions permissible under the Old Tax Regime, notably Section 80C (investments in PPF, EPF, ELSS, Life Insurance, NSC, up to ₹1,50,000) and Section 80D (medical insurance premiums up to ₹25,000 for self/family and ₹50,000 for senior citizen parents).
  • Taxable Income: $\text{Taxable Income} = \text{Gross Total Income} - \text{Deductions}$. Rounded off to the nearest multiple of ₹10.

Mathematical Framework & Tax Slab Formulations

Income tax follows a piecewise progressive step function where marginal increments are taxed at higher percentages.

Old Tax Regime (Individuals < 60 Years)

  • Up to ₹2,50,000: Nil
  • ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000: 5% of (Income - ₹2,50,000)
  • ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000: ₹12,500 + 20% of (Income - ₹5,00,000)
  • Above ₹10,00,000: ₹1,12,500 + 30% of (Income - ₹10,00,000)
  • Section 87A Rebate: Full rebate up to ₹12,500 if Taxable Income $\le$ ₹5,00,000 (Tax = Nil).

New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC)

  • Up to ₹3,00,000: Nil
  • ₹3,00,001 to ₹6,00,000: 5%
  • ₹6,00,001 to ₹9,00,000: 10%
  • ₹9,00,001 to ₹12,00,000: 15%
  • ₹12,00,001 to ₹15,00,000: 20%
  • Above ₹15,00,000: 30%
  • Section 87A Rebate: Full rebate up to ₹25,000 if Taxable Income $\le$ ₹7,00,000 (Tax = Nil).
Health & Education Cess: An additional 4% cess is computed on the total tax payable after adjusting for the Section 87A rebate: $\text{Total Tax Liability} = (\text{Tax} - \text{Rebate 87A}) \times 1.04$.

Worked Benchmark Examples

Example 1 (Salaried Individual under Old Regime):

Mr. Rajesh (age 42) has a gross salary of ₹9,20,000 in FY 2024-25. He deposits ₹1,40,000 in PPF, ₹20,000 in ELSS, and pays ₹24,000 for medical insurance (Section 80D). Calculate his total tax liability under the Old Regime.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Compute Total Deductions:
• Standard Deduction = ₹50,000
• Section 80C: PPF (₹1,40,000) + ELSS (₹20,000) = ₹1,60,000. Capped at maximum statutory limit = ₹1,50,000.
• Section 80D: Health insurance premium = ₹24,000 (within ₹25,000 limit).
Total Deductions = $50,000 + 1,50,000 + 24,000 = \mathbf{₹2,24,000}$.

Step 2: Compute Taxable Income:
$\text{Taxable Income} = 9,20,000 - 2,24,000 = \mathbf{₹6,96,000}$.

Step 3: Calculate Income Tax (Old Regime Slabs):
• On first ₹2,50,000: Nil
• On ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000 (₹2,50,000 @ 5%): $0.05 \times 2,50,000 = ₹12,500$
• On remaining ₹1,96,000 (₹6,96,000 - ₹5,00,000) @ 20%: $0.20 \times 1,96,000 = ₹39,200$
Total Tax before Cess = $12,500 + 39,200 = \mathbf{₹51,700}$.

Step 4: Add 4% Health & Education Cess:
$\text{Cess} = 0.04 \times 51,700 = ₹2,068$.
$\text{Net Tax Payable} = 51,700 + 2,068 = \mathbf{₹53,768}$.

Example 2 (Section 87A Full Rebate Scenario):

Ms. Ananya has a taxable income of ₹4,80,000 under the Old Regime. Determine her tax payable after applying Section 87A rebate.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Tax before rebate:
Up to ₹2,50,000: Nil
₹2,50,001 to ₹4,80,000: $5\% \text{ of } (4,80,000 - 2,50,000) = 0.05 \times 2,30,000 = ₹11,500$.

Section 87A Rebate:
Since Taxable Income $\le ₹5,00,000$, she is eligible for full rebate up to ₹12,500. Here, rebate = ₹11,500.
$\text{Net Tax Payable} = 11,500 - 11,500 = \mathbf{₹0}$ (Nil).

An employee earns a monthly basic salary of ₹65,000 and allowances of ₹15,000 per month. $\text{Taxable Income} = 9,60,000 - 2,22,000 = \mathbf{₹7,38,000}$.

New Regime Slabs Breakdown:

Problem 4 Rebate Threshold Evaluation

An assessee has a taxable income of ₹6,95,000 under the New Tax Regime. Determine whether any tax is payable, explaining the Section 87A rebate rules applicable to the New Regime.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Slab Tax on ₹6,95,000 under New Regime:
• 0 to 3L: Nil
• 3L to 6L (₹3,00,000 @ 5%): ₹15,000
• 6L to 6.95L (₹95,000 @ 10%): ₹9,500
Total Tax before rebate = $15,000 + 9,500 = ₹24,500$.

Section 87A under New Regime: Under Section 115BAC, a resident individual with taxable income not exceeding ₹7,00,000 is entitled to a 100% tax rebate up to a maximum of ₹25,000.
Since ₹6,95,000 $\le$ ₹7,00,000, full rebate of ₹24,500 is granted.
Net Tax Payable = ₹0 (Nil).

Q5. Old vs New Regime Breakeven Comparison 2 Marks

An executive with Gross Salary of ₹12,00,000 has total eligible deductions (Standard Deduction + 80C + 80D) of ₹2,50,000 under the Old Regime. Under the New Regime, only Standard Deduction of ₹50,000 is allowed. Determine which tax regime is more beneficial.

Show Model Solution

Option 1: Old Regime:
Taxable Income = $12,00,000 - 2,50,000 = ₹9,50,000$.
Tax: $12,500 + 20\% \text{ of } (9,50,000 - 5,00,000) = 12,500 + 90,000 = ₹1,02,500$.
With 4% Cess: $1,02,500 \times 1.04 = \mathbf{₹1,06,600}$.

Option 2: New Regime:
Taxable Income = $12,00,000 - 50,000 = ₹11,50,000$.
Tax: 3L-6L (₹15,000) + 6L-9L (₹30,000) + 9L-11.5L (15% of ₹2,50,000 = ₹37,500) = ₹82,500.
With 4% Cess: $82,500 \times 1.04 = \mathbf{₹85,800}$.

Conclusion: The New Tax Regime is more beneficial, saving $\mathbf{₹20,800}$ ($1,06,600 - 85,800$).

Q6. Comprehensive Senior Citizen Tax Assessment 4 Marks

Mr. Sharma (aged 66 years, Senior Citizen) provides the following income details for FY 2024-25:
• Pension: ₹6,20,000
• Interest on Bank Fixed Deposits: ₹85,000
• Health Insurance Premium paid for self: ₹38,000
• Contribution to Senior Citizen Savings Scheme (SCSS): ₹1,40,000
Calculate: (i) Gross Total Income, (ii) Deductions allowable under Chapter VI-A (including Section 80TTB up to ₹50,000 for senior citizens), (iii) Taxable Income, and (iv) Net Tax Liability under the Old Tax Regime (Senior Citizen exemption limit is ₹3,00,000).

Show Model Solution

(i) Gross Total Income (GTI) [1 Mark]:
Pension (₹6,20,000) + Bank FD Interest (₹85,000) = $\mathbf{₹7,05,000}$.

(ii) Deductions Permissible [1 Mark]:
• Standard Deduction on Pension: ₹50,000
• Section 80C: SCSS contribution = ₹1,40,000 (within ₹1.5L limit)
• Section 80D: Medical insurance for senior citizen = ₹38,000 (limit is ₹50,000)
• Section 80TTB: Interest income deduction for senior citizen = ₹50,000 (out of ₹85,000)
Total Deductions = $50,000 + 1,40,000 + 38,000 + 50,000 = \mathbf{₹2,78,000}$.

(iii) Taxable Income [1 Mark]:
$\text{Taxable Income} = 7,05,000 - 2,78,000 = \mathbf{₹4,27,000}$.

(iv) Tax Liability (Senior Citizen Slabs) [1 Mark]:
• Up to ₹3,00,000: Nil
• ₹3,00,001 to ₹4,27,000 (₹1,27,000 @ 5%): $0.05 \times 1,27,000 = ₹6,350$.
Section 87A Rebate: Since Taxable Income (₹4,27,000) is below ₹5,00,000, Mr. Sharma is entitled to 100% rebate up to ₹12,500.
Rebate under 87A = ₹6,350.
$\text{Tax after rebate} = 6,350 - 6,350 = \mathbf{₹0}$.
Net Tax Liability = Nil (₹0).

Systematic 4-Step Tax Computation Protocol

Step 1: Compute GTI

Aggregate income from salary, house property, business, capital gains, and interest.

Step 2: Subtract Deductions

Apply standard deduction (₹50k), 80C (max ₹1.5L), 80D (₹25k/₹50k), and 80TTA/TTB.

Step 3: Apply Slabs & Rebate

Calculate piecewise progressive tax. Deduct Section 87A rebate if income $\le$ ₹5L (Old) or $\le$ ₹7L (New).

Step 4: Add 4% Cess

Multiply net tax by $1.04$ ($4\%$ Health & Education Cess). Round to nearest ₹10.

Tax Slab Comparison Cheat-Sheet

Income Range Old Regime (Age < 60) New Regime (Sec 115BAC)
Up to ₹2,50,000 Nil Nil (Up to ₹3,00,000)
₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000 5% 5% (₹3,00,001 to ₹6,00,000)
₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 ₹12,500 + 20% 10% (6L-9L); 15% (9L-12L)
Above ₹10,00,000 ₹1,12,500 + 30% 20% (12L-15L); 30% (> 15L)

Common Exam Traps

  • The 80C Cap Error: Always check if total 80C investments exceed ₹1,50,000. Even if a taxpayer invests ₹2,20,000 across PPF and ELSS, deduction cannot exceed ₹1,50,000.
  • Rebate Ordering: Section 87A rebate must ALWAYS be subtracted BEFORE calculating the 4% Health & Education Cess, not after!
  • Senior Citizen Age Threshold: 60 years or more at any time during the financial year qualifies for Senior Citizen status (₹3,00,000 basic exemption under Old Regime). Super senior citizen (80+ years) has ₹5,00,000 basic exemption.
