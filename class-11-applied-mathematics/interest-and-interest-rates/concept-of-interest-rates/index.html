Concept of Interest Rates

Master Interest Rates for CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics. Learn simple vs compound interest, effective rates, and calculations with solved examples.

Simple Interest Fundamentals

Simple interest is the interest calculated only on the principal amount borrowed or invested over a specific period. It remains constant for each time period because the principal does not change. The formula relates the principal, rate of interest, and time.

I = (P × R × T) / 100
Example: Solved Example: Calculating Simple Interest
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify P = ₹5000, R = 10% p.a., T = 3 years.
Step 2: Apply formula I = (5000 × 10 × 3) / 100.
Step 3: Calculate I = 1500.
Answer: The simple interest is ₹1500.

Compound Interest Mechanics

Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus the accumulated interest from previous periods. This leads to exponential growth of the investment. The amount after n periods is determined by the compound interest formula.

A = P(1 + r/100)ⁿ
Example: Solved Example: Calculating Compound Amount
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify P = ₹10000, r = 5%, n = 2 years.
Step 2: Apply A = 10000(1 + 5/100)².
Step 3: A = 10000(1.05)² = 10000 × 1.1025 = 11025.
Answer: The total amount is ₹11025.

Effective Rate of Interest

The effective annual rate (EAR) represents the actual interest earned due to compounding within a year. If interest is compounded k times per year, the effective rate is higher than the nominal rate. This allows for comparing different investment schemes.

EAR = (1 + r/k)ᵏ - 1
Example: Solved Example: Finding EAR
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Nominal rate r = 12% (0.12), compounded quarterly (k=4).
Step 2: EAR = (1 + 0.12/4)⁴ - 1.
Step 3: EAR = (1.03)⁴ - 1 = 1.1255 - 1 = 0.1255.
Answer: The effective rate is 12.55%.

mediumA sum of ₹4000 amounts to ₹4410 in 2 years at compound interest. Find the rate per annum.

Show Answer

✔ Correct: B

4410 = 4000(1 + r/100)². 1.1025 = (1 + r/100)². √1.1025 = 1.05. r = 5%.

5

hardFind the difference between simple and compound interest on ₹10000 for 2 years at 10% per annum.

Show Answer

✔ Correct: B

SI = 2000. CI = 10000(1.1)² - 10000 = 2100. Difference = 100.

6

hardIf the effective rate of interest is 10.25% for a nominal rate compounded semi-annually, find the nominal rate.

Show Answer

✔ Correct: C

(1 + r/2)² - 1 = 0.1025. (1 + r/2)² = 1.1025. 1 + r/2 = 1.05. r = 0.10 or 10%.

7

very hardA sum of money doubles itself in 8 years at simple interest. In how many years will it triple itself?

Show Answer

✔ Correct: B

P becomes 2P in 8 years, so interest = P. P = (P × R × 8)/100 → R = 12.5%. To triple, interest = 2P. 2P = (P × 12.5 × T)/100 → T = 200/12.5 = 16 years.

mediumGiven P = ₹8000, R = 5%, T = 3 years. Find: (i) Simple Interest (ii) Amount at Simple Interest (iii) Difference if compounded annually.

mediumGiven nominal rate 8% compounded quarterly. Find: (i) Periodic rate (ii) Number of periods in 2 years (iii) Effective annual rate.

(i) 8%/4 = 2% or 0.02.
(ii) 2 years × 4 = 8 periods.
(iii) EAR = (1 + 0.02)⁴ - 1 = 1.0824 - 1 = 8.24%.
