6.3 Relationship Between AM and GM: Inequality Proofs and Optimization

In Applied Mathematics, cost minimization, and financial engineering, the relationship between the Arithmetic Mean (AM) and the Geometric Mean (GM) provides one of the most powerful algebraic bounds in existence. It enables analysts to find minimum operating costs and maximum asset yields without requiring differential calculus.

Fundamental Theorems & Properties

  • The AM-GM Inequality: For any two positive real numbers \(a > 0\) and \(b > 0\): \[ \text{AM} \ge \text{GM} \iff \frac{a + b}{2} \ge \sqrt{ab} \] Equality holds (\(\text{AM} = \text{GM}\)) if and only if \(a = b\).
  • Rigorous Algebraic Proof: Consider the difference: \[ \text{AM} - \text{GM} = \frac{a + b}{2} - \sqrt{ab} = \frac{a + b - 2\sqrt{ab}}{2} = \frac{(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b})^2}{2} \] Since the square of any real number is non-negative, \(\frac{(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b})^2}{2} \ge 0\). Hence, \(\text{AM} \ge \text{GM}\).
  • Quadratic Equation Construction: If \(A\) and \(G\) are the AM and GM of two positive numbers \(a\) and \(b\): \[ a + b = 2A, \quad ab = G^2 \] The quadratic equation whose roots are \(a\) and \(b\) is: \[ x^2 - 2Ax + G^2 = 0 \] By the quadratic formula, the numbers are given by: \[ a, b = A \pm \sqrt{A^2 - G^2} \]
  • The Ratio Theorem: If the AM of two positive numbers is to their GM in the ratio \(m : n\) (where \(m > n > 0\)), then the ratio of the two numbers is: \[ \frac{a}{b} = \frac{m + \sqrt{m^2 - n^2}}{m - \sqrt{m^2 - n^2}} \]
  • Generalization to \(n\) Positive Variables: \[ \frac{x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n}{n} \ge \sqrt[n]{x_1 x_2 \dots x_n} \]

Applied Business Optimization: Minimizing Operating Cost

Example: Inventory Carrying and Ordering Costs

Scenario: A supply chain manager models the annual operating cost \(C(x)\) of maintaining a warehouse stocking quantity \(x > 0\) as: \[ C(x) = 4x + \frac{2500}{x} \] where \(4x\) represents carrying storage costs and \(\frac{2500}{x}\) represents ordering overheads. Using the AM-GM inequality, determine the minimum annual operating cost and the optimal stocking quantity \(x\).

Solution:

Let \(a = 4x\) and \(b = \frac{2500}{x}\). Since \(x > 0\), both \(a > 0\) and \(b > 0\).

By the AM-GM Inequality:

\[ \frac{a + b}{2} \ge \sqrt{ab} \implies a + b \ge 2\sqrt{ab} \] \[ C(x) = 4x + \frac{2500}{x} \ge 2 \sqrt{4x \cdot \frac{2500}{x}} = 2 \sqrt{10{,}000} = 2 \times 100 = 200 \]

The minimum annual operating cost is ₹200.

This minimum occurs when equality holds in the AM-GM inequality, which requires \(a = b\):

\[ 4x = \frac{2500}{x} \implies 4x^2 = 2500 \implies x^2 = 625 \implies x = 25 \text{ units.} \]

Thus, the optimal stocking order is 25 units.

Problem 1 (Finding Numbers from AM and GM)

If the Arithmetic Mean (AM) of two positive numbers is 10 and their Geometric Mean (GM) is 8, find the two numbers.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

Given \(A = 10\) and \(G = 8\).

The numbers are given by the roots of \(x^2 - 2Ax + G^2 = 0\):

\[ x^2 - 2(10)x + 8^2 = 0 \implies x^2 - 20x + 64 = 0 \] Factoring: \[ (x - 16)(x - 4) = 0 \implies x = 16 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 4 \] Alternatively, using the formula: \[ a, b = A \pm \sqrt{A^2 - G^2} = 10 \pm \sqrt{100 - 64} = 10 \pm \sqrt{36} = 10 \pm 6 \] This gives \(10 + 6 = 16\) and \(10 - 6 = 4\). Answer: The numbers are 16 and 4.

Problem 2 (Ratio of Numbers from AM:GM Ratio)

If the AM and GM of two positive numbers are in the ratio \(5 : 4\), show that the ratio of the numbers is \(4 : 1\).

View Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

Given \(\frac{\text{AM}}{\text{GM}} = \frac{a + b}{2\sqrt{ab}} = \frac{5}{4}\).

Using the Ratio Theorem with \(m = 5\) and \(n = 4\):

\[ \frac{a}{b} = \frac{m + \sqrt{m^2 - n^2}}{m - \sqrt{m^2 - n^2}} = \frac{5 + \sqrt{25 - 16}}{5 - \sqrt{25 - 16}} = \frac{5 + \sqrt{9}}{5 - \sqrt{9}} = \frac{5 + 3}{5 - 3} = \frac{8}{2} = \frac{4}{1} \] Hence, the ratio of the numbers is \(4 : 1\).

Problem 3 (Inequality Bound for Sum of Reciprocals)

If \(a, b, c\) are positive real numbers, prove that: \[ (a + b + c) \left( \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{c} \right) \ge 9 \]

View Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

Apply the AM-GM Inequality to \(a, b, c\):

\[ \frac{a + b + c}{3} \ge \sqrt[3]{abc} \implies a + b + c \ge 3 \sqrt[3]{abc} \quad \text{--- (1)} \] Apply the AM-GM Inequality to \(\frac{1}{a}, \frac{1}{b}, \frac{1}{c}\):

\[ \frac{\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{c}}{3} \ge \sqrt[3]{\frac{1}{abc}} \implies \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{c} \ge 3 \sqrt[3]{\frac{1}{abc}} \quad \text{--- (2)} \] Multiplying inequalities (1) and (2): \[ (a + b + c) \left( \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{c} \right) \ge \left( 3 \sqrt[3]{abc} \right) \left( 3 \sqrt[3]{\frac{1}{abc}} \right) = 9 \] This completes the proof.

Quick Revision & Formula Cheat Sheet

AM-GM Inequality

  • \(\frac{a + b}{2} \ge \sqrt{ab}\) for all \(a, b > 0\)
  • Equality holds \(\iff a = b\)
  • Minimum value of \(x + \frac{k}{x}\) is \(2\sqrt{k}\) (for \(x > 0\))
  • \(\text{AM} - \text{GM} = \frac{(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b})^2}{2}\)

Forming Quadratic Equations

  • \(x^2 - 2Ax + G^2 = 0\)
  • Roots: \(a, b = A \pm \sqrt{A^2 - G^2}\)
  • Discriminant \(D = 4(A^2 - G^2) \ge 0\) ensures real roots

Ratio Formula

  • If \(\text{AM} : \text{GM} = m : n\):
  • \(\frac{a}{b} = \frac{m + \sqrt{m^2 - n^2}}{m - \sqrt{m^2 - n^2}}\)
