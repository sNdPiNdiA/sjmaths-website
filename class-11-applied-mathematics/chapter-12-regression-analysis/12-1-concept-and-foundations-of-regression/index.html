Comprehensive Concepts & Principles: Foundations of Regression Analysis

First introduced into biological statistics by Sir Francis Galton in 1877 during his seminal studies on hereditary stature ("stepping back" or "regressing" toward the average height of the population), Regression Analysis has evolved into the foremost mathematical tool for economic forecasting and causal modeling. While correlation assesses whether two variables move together, regression quantifies the precise mathematical functional relationship to predict the unknown value of a dependent variable from known values of an independent variable.

Correlation vs. Regression: Core Analytical Distinctions:
  • 1. Objective: Correlation measures the strength and direction of mutual co-variation. Regression enables estimation and prediction of one variable given another.
  • 2. Symmetry: Correlation is completely symmetric ($r_{xy} = r_{yx}$). Regression is strictly asymmetric: the regression line of $Y$ on $X$ is distinct from the regression line of $X$ on $Y$.
  • 3. Dimensionality & Units: Correlation coefficient $r$ is a pure dimensionless number without units ($-1 \le r \le 1$). Regression coefficients ($b_{yx}, b_{xy}$) are dimensional rates of change carrying physical units (e.g., ₹ of sales per ₹ of advertising).
  • 4. Causality & Dependency: In correlation, neither variable is treated as dependent or independent. In regression, one variable is designated as the independent predictor ($X$) and the other as the dependent response ($Y$).

The Principle of Least Squares & The Two Regression Lines

A fundamental question in bivariate statistics is: Why are there two distinct lines of regression?

• Line of Regression of $Y$ on $X$:
  - Purpose: Predicts $Y$ for a known value of $X$.
  - Mathematical Criterion: Minimizes the sum of squares of vertical deviations: $\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2 = \text{minimum}$.

• Line of Regression of $X$ on $Y$:
  - Purpose: Predicts $X$ for a known value of $Y$.
  - Mathematical Criterion: Minimizes the sum of squares of horizontal deviations: $\sum (x_i - \hat{x}_i)^2 = \text{minimum}$.

• Point of Intersection: The two regression lines ALWAYS intersect at the point of means $(\bar{X}, \bar{Y})$.
• Angular Separation:
  - If $r = \pm 1$: The two regression lines coincide into a single line (zero angle).
  - If $r = 0$: The two regression lines are perpendicular to each other ($90^\circ$ angle).

Worked Benchmark Examples

Example 1 (Intersection Point & Means Determination):

The equations of two lines of regression obtained from a market survey are:
$$2X + 3Y - 18 = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad 4X + Y - 16 = 0$$ Find the arithmetic mean of $X$ ($\bar{X}$) and the arithmetic mean of $Y$ ($\bar{Y}$).

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Property: The two regression lines always pass through the mean point $(\bar{X}, \bar{Y})$. Therefore, $(\bar{X}, \bar{Y})$ must satisfy both equations simultaneously:
(1) $2\bar{X} + 3\bar{Y} = 18$
(2) $4\bar{X} + \bar{Y} = 16$
• Multiply equation (2) by 3: $12\bar{X} + 3\bar{Y} = 48$.
• Subtract equation (1): $$(12\bar{X} + 3\bar{Y}) - (2\bar{X} + 3\bar{Y}) = 48 - 18 \implies 10\bar{X} = 30 \implies \mathbf{\bar{X} = 3}$$
• Substitute $\bar{X} = 3$ into equation (2): $$4(3) + \bar{Y} = 16 \implies 12 + \bar{Y} = 16 \implies \mathbf{\bar{Y} = 4}$$
Answer: $\bar{X} = 3$ and $\bar{Y} = 4$.

Example 2 (Appropriate Line Selection for Prediction):

A corporate analyst establishes the regression line of sales ($Y$) on advertising expenditure ($X$) as $Y = 4X + 25$, and the regression line of $X$ on $Y$ as $X = 0.16Y - 2$.
(a) If advertising budget is set to ₹10 Lakhs, which line should be used to forecast sales, and what is the predicted sales figure?
(b) If the board mandates a target sales revenue of ₹125 Lakhs, which line should be used to estimate required advertising, and what is the estimate?

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• (a) Predicting $Y$ given $X = 10$:
Use the regression line of $Y$ on $X$: $$\hat{Y} = 4(10) + 25 = 40 + 25 = \mathbf{₹65 \text{ Lakhs}}$$ • (b) Predicting $X$ given $Y = 125$:
Use the regression line of $X$ on $Y$ (never invert the $Y$ on $X$ line!): $$\hat{X} = 0.16(125) - 2 = 20 - 2 = \mathbf{₹18 \text{ Lakhs}}$$

Problem 1 Mean Determination Drill

The two regression lines between variables $X$ and $Y$ are given by:
$$3X + 2Y - 26 = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad 6X + Y - 31 = 0$$ Find the mean of $X$ and the mean of $Y$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Since regression lines intersect at $(\bar{X}, \bar{Y})$:
(1) $3\bar{X} + 2\bar{Y} = 26$
(2) $6\bar{X} + \bar{Y} = 31$
• From (2), $\bar{Y} = 31 - 6\bar{X}$. Substitute into (1): $$3\bar{X} + 2(31 - 6\bar{X}) = 26 \implies 3\bar{X} + 62 - 12\bar{X} = 26$$ $$-9\bar{X} = 26 - 62 = -36 \implies \mathbf{\bar{X} = 4}$$
• $\bar{Y} = 31 - 6(4) = 31 - 24 = \mathbf{7}$.
Answer: Mean of $X = 4$, Mean of $Y = 7$.

Problem 2 Line Selection Rule

Explain why we cannot simply solve the regression line of $Y$ on $X$ for $X$ to estimate $X$ from $Y$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• The regression line of $Y$ on $X$ is derived by minimizing the sum of squares of vertical errors $\sum (y - \hat{y})^2$ under the assumption that $X$ is error-free.
• When estimating $X$ from $Y$, the objective is to minimize horizontal errors $\sum (x - \hat{x})^2$ treating $Y$ as given.
• Mathematically, the line minimizing vertical errors is distinct from the line minimizing horizontal errors (unless $r = \pm 1$). Inverting the $Y$ on $X$ equation produces a mathematically suboptimal, biased estimate of $X$.

Problem 3 Angle Between Regression Lines

What is the geometric relationship between the two regression lines when:
(a) $r = +1$
(b) $r = -1$
(c) $r = 0$?

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• (a) When $r = +1$, the two lines coincide into a single upward-sloping line.
• (b) When $r = -1$, the two lines coincide into a single downward-sloping line.
• (c) When $r = 0$, the two lines are perpendicular ($90^\circ$): the regression line of $Y$ on $X$ is horizontal ($Y = \bar{Y}$), and the regression line of $X$ on $Y$ is vertical ($X = \bar{X}$).

Problem 4 Origin of the Word 'Regression'

Briefly explain the historical context of the word 'regression' as coined by Sir Francis Galton in his biological studies.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Sir Francis Galton observed that although tall parents tended to have tall children, the average height of their sons was closer to the general population mean than the parents' height (and vice versa for short parents).
• He called this tendency to revert back to the population average a "regression toward mediocrity". Over time, the mathematical statistical technique developed for this prediction retained the name 'regression'.

Problem 5 Least Squares Formulation

State the mathematical least-squares minimization condition used to derive the regression line $\hat{Y} = a + bX$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• The criterion requires minimizing the sum of squared residuals ($S$): $$S = \sum_{i=1}^n e_i^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n [y_i - (a + bx_i)]^2 = \text{Minimum}$$ • Setting partial derivatives $\frac{\partial S}{\partial a} = 0$ and \frac{\partial S}{\partial b} = 0$ yields the two normal equations of linear regression.

SECTION B: 2-Mark Short Answer Problems

Q3: State two fundamental differences between Correlation and Regression.

Show Answer & Scheme

Differences [2 Marks]:
(1) Purpose: Correlation measures the strength and direction of linear association; Regression models the functional relationship to forecast or predict values.
(2) Units: Correlation coefficient $r$ is dimensionless; Regression coefficients carry physical units ($ ext{units of } Y / ext{units of } X$).

Q4: If two regression lines are given by $X + 2Y - 5 = 0$ and $2X + 3Y - 8 = 0$, determine $\bar{X}$ and $\bar{Y}$.

Show Answer & Scheme

• Equations: (1) $\bar{X} + 2\bar{Y} = 5 \implies \bar{X} = 5 - 2\bar{Y}$
• Substitute into (2): $2(5 - 2\bar{Y}) + 3\bar{Y} = 8 \implies 10 - 4\bar{Y} + 3\bar{Y} = 8 \implies -\bar{Y} = -2 \implies \mathbf{\bar{Y} = 2}$ [1 Mark].
• $\bar{X} = 5 - 2(2) = \mathbf{1}$ [1 Mark].
• Mean point is $(1, 2)$.

SECTION C: 4-Mark Comprehensive Scenario Drill

Q5: A retail commercial chain determines the following regression equations connecting weekly advertising expenditure ($X$ in ₹ Thousands) and weekly sales revenue ($Y$ in ₹ Lakhs):
• Regression of $Y$ on $X$: $Y = 0.5X + 12$
• Regression of $X$ on $Y$: $X = 0.8Y - 4$
(i) Find the average weekly advertising expenditure $\bar{X}$ and the average weekly sales revenue $\bar{Y}$.
(ii) If the store plans to spend ₹20,000 on advertising next week ($X = 20$), predict the expected sales revenue.
(iii) If the store targets a sales revenue of ₹25 Lakhs ($Y = 25$), estimate the required advertising expenditure.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Part (i) Means Calculation [1.5 Marks]:
• Regression lines meet at $(\bar{X}, \bar{Y})$: $$\bar{Y} = 0.5\bar{X} + 12 \quad \text{and} \quad \bar{X} = 0.8\bar{Y} - 4$$ • Substitute $\bar{X}$: $\bar{Y} = 0.5(0.8\bar{Y} - 4) + 12 = 0.4\bar{Y} - 2 + 12 = 0.4\bar{Y} + 10$
• $0.6\bar{Y} = 10 \implies \mathbf{\bar{Y} = \frac{10}{0.6} = 16.67 \text{ Lakhs}}$.
• $\bar{X} = 0.8(16.667) - 4 = 13.333 - 4 = \mathbf{9.33 \text{ Thousands}}$ (₹9,333).

Part (ii) Predicting $Y$ from $X=20$ [1.25 Marks]:
• Use line of $Y$ on $X$: $$\hat{Y} = 0.5(20) + 12 = 10 + 12 = \mathbf{₹22 \text{ Lakhs}}$$

Part (iii) Predicting $X$ from $Y=25$ [1.25 Marks]:
• Use line of $X$ on $Y$: $$\hat{X} = 0.8(25) - 4 = 20 - 4 = \mathbf{₹16 \text{ Thousands}} \quad (₹16,000)$$

Correlation vs. Regression Master Table

Criteria Correlation ($r$) Regression ($b_{yx}, b_{xy}$)
Purpose Measures degree of co-association Estimates unknown values of dependent variable
Symmetry Symmetric: $r_{xy} = r_{yx}$ Asymmetric: Line of $Y$ on $X \neq$ Line of $X$ on $Y$
Units Pure number, dimensionless Expressed in physical units ($ ext{units of } Y / ext{units of } X$)
Mathematical Bound $-1 \le r \le +1$ Coefficients can exceed 1 (provided $b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} \le 1$)

Essential Exam Rules

  • Intersection Point: If you are given two regression equations and asked to find the arithmetic means $\bar{X}$ and $\bar{Y}$, simply solve the two equations simultaneously.
  • Line Selection Protocol: To predict $Y$, use the line of $Y$ on $X$. To predict $X$, use the line of $X$ on $Y$. Never algebraicly re-arrange one line to predict the other!

PART 2: Subjective Section (Q5 - Q10)

Q5 2 Marks

If the two regression lines are $8X - 10Y + 66 = 0$ and $40X - 18Y = 214$, find the mean of $X$ and the mean of $Y$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Lines intersect at $(\bar{X}, \bar{Y})$:
(1) $8\bar{X} - 10\bar{Y} = -66 \implies 4\bar{X} - 5\bar{Y} = -33$
(2) $40\bar{X} - 18\bar{Y} = 214 \implies 20\bar{X} - 9\bar{Y} = 107$
• Multiply (1) by 5: $20\bar{X} - 25\bar{Y} = -165$ [1 Mark].
• Subtract from (2): $(20\bar{X} - 9\bar{Y}) - (20\bar{X} - 25\bar{Y}) = 107 - (-165) \implies 16\bar{Y} = 272 \implies \mathbf{\bar{Y} = 17}$.
• $4\bar{X} = 5(17) - 33 = 85 - 33 = 52 \implies \mathbf{\bar{X} = 13}$ [1 Mark].

Q6 2 Marks

State the condition under which the two regression lines coincide into a single line.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Condition [2 Marks]:
The two regression lines coincide into a single straight line if and only if there is perfect linear correlation between the two variables ($r = +1$ or $r = -1$). In this case, all data points lie exactly on the line, leaving zero residual error in both directions.

Q7 3 Marks

Explain the Principle of Least Squares and illustrate the geometric difference between vertical residuals and horizontal residuals.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Principle of Least Squares [1.5 Marks]: The principle of least squares chooses the parameters of a curve such that the sum of the squares of the differences between observed values and estimated values is minimized: $\sum e_i^2 = \text{minimum}$.
• Geometric Difference [1.5 Marks]:
- Vertical Residuals: Distances measured parallel to the $Y$-axis ($y_i - \hat{y}_i$). Minimized when predicting $Y$ given $X$.
- Horizontal Residuals: Distances measured parallel to the $X$-axis ($x_i - \hat{x}_i$). Minimized when predicting $X$ given $Y$.

Q8 3 Marks

Show that if $r = 0$, the regression line of $Y$ on $X$ is parallel to the $X$-axis, and the regression line of $X$ on $Y$ is parallel to the $Y$-axis.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Regression equation of $Y$ on $X$ is $Y - \bar{Y} = r\frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}(X - \bar{X})$ [1 Mark].
• When $r = 0$: $Y - \bar{Y} = 0 \implies \mathbf{Y = \bar{Y}}$. This is a horizontal line parallel to the $X$-axis at height $\bar{Y}$ [1 Mark].
• Regression equation of $X$ on $Y$ is $X - \bar{X} = r\frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}(Y - \bar{Y})$.
• When $r = 0$: $X - \bar{X} = 0 \implies \mathbf{X = \bar{X}}$. This is a vertical line parallel to the $Y$-axis at position $\bar{X}$ [1 Mark].
• The two lines meet perpendicularly at $(\bar{X}, \bar{Y})$.

Q9 & Q10 Case Study (4 Marks Total)

Case Context: Agricultural Irrigation & Crop Yield

An agronomist studies water irrigation volume ($X$ in kilolitres) and paddy yield ($Y$ in tonnes) across commercial farms. The regression lines are determined as:
• Paddy Yield on Irrigation: $Y = 0.25X + 1.5$
• Irrigation on Paddy Yield: $X = 1.44Y - 0.16$

Q9 (2 Marks): Find the mean irrigation volume $\bar{X}$ and the mean paddy yield $\bar{Y}$.
Q10 (2 Marks): If an arid farm is allocated 12 kilolitres of water ($X = 12$), forecast its expected paddy yield.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Solution to Q9 [2 Marks]:
• Substitute $\bar{X} = 1.44\bar{Y} - 0.16$ into the first equation: $$\bar{Y} = 0.25(1.44\bar{Y} - 0.16) + 1.5 = 0.36\bar{Y} - 0.04 + 1.5 = 0.36\bar{Y} + 1.46$$ $$0.64\bar{Y} = 1.46 \implies \mathbf{\bar{Y} = \frac{1.46}{0.64} = 2.28125 \text{ tonnes}}$$ • $\bar{X} = 1.44(2.28125) - 0.16 = 3.285 - 0.16 = \mathbf{3.125 \text{ kilolitres}}$.

Solution to Q10 [2 Marks]:
• To predict $Y$ from $X=12$, use line of $Y$ on $X$: $$\hat{Y} = 0.25(12) + 1.5 = 3.0 + 1.5 = \mathbf{4.5 \text{ tonnes}}$$
