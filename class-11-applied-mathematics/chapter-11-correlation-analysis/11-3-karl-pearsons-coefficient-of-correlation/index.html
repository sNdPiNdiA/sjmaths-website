Comprehensive Concepts & Principles: Karl Pearson's Coefficient of Correlation

Karl Pearson's Coefficient of Correlation ($r$), also known as the Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient, is the mathematical standard for quantifying the strength and direction of linear association between two quantitative continuous variables. It standardizes the covariance of $X$ and $Y$ by the product of their individual standard deviations, yielding a pure, dimensionless index bounded strictly between $-1$ and $+1$.

Key Formulations of Karl Pearson's $r$:
  • 1. Product-Moment Definition (Actual Mean Method):
    $$r = \frac{\text{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_x \cdot \sigma_y} = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{X})(y_i - \bar{Y})}{\sqrt{\sum (x_i - \bar{X})^2 \cdot \sum (y_i - \bar{Y})^2}}$$
  • 2. Direct Raw Data Formula:
    $$r = \frac{N\sum XY - (\sum X)(\sum Y)}{\sqrt{\left[N\sum X^2 - (\sum X)^2\right]\left[N\sum Y^2 - (\sum Y)^2\right]}}$$
  • 3. Short-cut Method (Assumed Means $A$ and $B$, $u_i = X_i - A, v_i = Y_i - B$):
    $$r = \frac{N\sum uv - (\sum u)(\sum v)}{\sqrt{\left[N\sum u^2 - (\sum u)^2\right]\left[N\sum v^2 - (\sum v)^2\right]}}$$

Fundamental Properties of Pearson's $r$

• Property 1 (Range): $-1 \le r \le +1$. (Proven via Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality).
• Property 2 (Dimensionless): $r$ is completely independent of the physical units of measurement.
• Property 3 (Symmetry): $r_{xy} = r_{yx}$.
• Property 4 (Invariance of Origin & Scale): If $u = \frac{X - a}{h}$ and $v = \frac{Y - b}{k}$:
  - If $h$ and $k$ share the same sign ($h \cdot k > 0$), then $r_{uv} = r_{xy}$.
  - If $h$ and $k$ have opposite signs ($h \cdot k < 0$), then $r_{uv} = -r_{xy}$.

Worked Benchmark Examples

Example 1 (Actual Mean Method):

Compute Karl Pearson's correlation coefficient $r$ between variables $X$ and $Y$:
$X$: $2, \quad 4, \quad 5, \quad 6, \quad 8, \quad 11$
$Y$: $18, \quad 12, \quad 10, \quad 8, \quad 7, \quad 5$

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $N = 6$.
$\sum X = 2+4+5+6+8+11 = 36 \implies \bar{X} = 36/6 = \mathbf{6}$.
$\sum Y = 18+12+10+8+7+5 = 60 \implies \bar{Y} = 60/6 = \mathbf{10}$.
• Compute deviations $x = X - 6$ and $y = Y - 10$:
- Point 1: $x = -4, y = +8 \implies x^2 = 16, y^2 = 64, xy = -32$
- Point 2: $x = -2, y = +2 \implies x^2 = 4, y^2 = 4, xy = -4$
- Point 3: $x = -1, y = 0 \implies x^2 = 1, y^2 = 0, xy = 0$
- Point 4: $x = 0, y = -2 \implies x^2 = 0, y^2 = 4, xy = 0$
- Point 5: $x = +2, y = -3 \implies x^2 = 4, y^2 = 9, xy = -6$
- Point 6: $x = +5, y = -5 \implies x^2 = 25, y^2 = 25, xy = -25$
• Sums: $\sum x^2 = 16+4+1+0+4+25 = \mathbf{50}$.
$\sum y^2 = 64+4+0+4+9+25 = \mathbf{106}$.
$\sum xy = -32 - 4 + 0 + 0 - 6 - 25 = \mathbf{-67}$.
$$r = \frac{\sum xy}{\sqrt{\sum x^2 \cdot \sum y^2}} = \frac{-67}{\sqrt{50 \times 106}} = \frac{-67}{\sqrt{5300}} = \frac{-67}{72.80} = \mathbf{-0.9203}$$
Answer: Karl Pearson's correlation coefficient is -0.92, indicating a very high negative linear correlation.

Example 2 (Change of Origin and Scale Property):

If the correlation coefficient between $X$ and $Y$ is $r_{xy} = 0.65$, find the correlation coefficient between:
(a) $u = X + 10$ and $v = Y - 25$
(b) $u = \frac{X - 50}{5}$ and $v = \frac{Y - 30}{10}$
(c) $u = \frac{X - 10}{-2}$ and $v = \frac{Y - 20}{4}$

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• (a) Origin shifts (+10, -25) have zero effect on correlation: $r_{uv} = r_{xy} = \mathbf{0.65}$.
• (b) Here $h = 5 > 0$ and $k = 10 > 0$. Since $h \cdot k = 50 > 0$, both scale factors are positive: $r_{uv} = r_{xy} = \mathbf{0.65}$.
• (c) Here $h = -2 < 0$ and $k = 4 > 0$. The product $h \cdot k = -8 < 0$ (opposite signs!). Therefore: $$r_{uv} = -r_{xy} = \mathbf{-0.65}$$

Problem 1 Direct Evaluation Drill

Calculate Karl Pearson's $r$ from the following data:
$N = 10, \quad \sum X = 140, \quad \sum Y = 150, \quad \sum (X - 14)^2 = 180, \quad \sum (Y - 15)^2 = 215, \quad \sum (X - 14)(Y - 15) = 60$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Note: $\bar{X} = 140/10 = 14$ and $\bar{Y} = 150/10 = 15$.
• Therefore, deviations are taken directly from actual means: $\sum x^2 = 180, \quad \sum y^2 = 215, \quad \sum xy = 60$.
$$r = \frac{\sum xy}{\sqrt{\sum x^2 \cdot \sum y^2}} = \frac{60}{\sqrt{180 \times 215}} = \frac{60}{\sqrt{38700}} = \frac{60}{196.72} \approx \mathbf{0.305}$$
Answer: $r = 0.305$ (moderate positive correlation).

Problem 2 Raw Data Product Moment

Given 5 pairs of observations $(X, Y)$:
$(1, 2), \quad (2, 5), \quad (3, 3), \quad (4, 8), \quad (5, 7)$
Calculate Karl Pearson's correlation coefficient using the raw data formula.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $N = 5$.
$\sum X = 1+2+3+4+5 = 15$
$\sum Y = 2+5+3+8+7 = 25$
$\sum X^2 = 1+4+9+16+25 = 55$
$\sum Y^2 = 4+25+9+64+49 = 151$
$\sum XY = (1)(2) + (2)(5) + (3)(3) + (4)(8) + (5)(7) = 2 + 10 + 9 + 32 + 35 = 88$.
$$r = \frac{5(88) - (15)(25)}{\sqrt{\left[5(55) - 15^2\right]\left[5(151) - 25^2\right]}} = \frac{440 - 375}{\sqrt{(275 - 225)(755 - 625)}}$$ $$r = \frac{65}{\sqrt{50 \times 130}} = \frac{65}{\sqrt{6500}} = \frac{65}{80.62} \approx \mathbf{0.806}$$
Answer: $r = 0.806$ (high positive linear correlation).

Problem 3 Covariance Formulation

The covariance between two variables $X$ and $Y$ is $-16.5$. The variance of $X$ is 25 and the variance of $Y$ is 16. Find the correlation coefficient between $X$ and $Y$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $\text{Cov}(X, Y) = -16.5$.
• $\sigma_x = \sqrt{25} = 5$, $\sigma_y = \sqrt{16} = 4$.
$$r = \frac{\text{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_x \cdot \sigma_y} = \frac{-16.5}{5 \times 4} = \frac{-16.5}{20} = \mathbf{-0.825}$$
Answer: $r = -0.825$.

Problem 4 Interpretation Scale

Interpret the practical commercial meaning of each of the following correlation coefficients:
(a) $r = +0.94$ between R&D budget and patent filings.
(b) $r = -0.89$ between car fuel efficiency and carbon emissions per km.
(c) $r = +0.03$ between employee height and sales targets achieved.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• (a) $r = +0.94$: Extremely strong positive linear correlation. Increasing R&D expenditure consistently leads to higher patent output.
• (b) $r = -0.89$: Strong negative linear correlation. Cars with higher fuel efficiency emit substantially lower carbon per kilometre.
• (c) $r = +0.03$: Negligible / near-zero correlation. Employee height has no linear bearing whatsoever on sales achievements.

Problem 5 Opposite Signs Scale Property

If $r(X, Y) = 0.72$, determine the correlation coefficient between $U = -3X + 5$ and $V = 2Y - 8$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $U = -3X + 5 \implies$ scale factor $b_1 = -3 < 0$.
• $V = 2Y - 8 \implies$ scale factor $b_2 = 2 > 0$.
• Product of scale factors: $b_1 \cdot b_2 = -6 < 0$ (opposite signs!).
• Therefore: $r(U, V) = \frac{b_1 \cdot b_2}{|b_1 \cdot b_2|} r(X, Y) = -1 \times 0.72 = \mathbf{-0.72}$.

Q1: State the mathematical boundaries between which Karl Pearson's correlation coefficient must always lie.

Show Answer & Scheme

Q2: Is Karl Pearson's correlation coefficient affected by change of origin? State yes or no with reason.

Show Answer & Scheme

Answer [1 Mark]: No. Adding or subtracting a constant to $X$ or $Y$ shifts the origin but leaves the deviations $(x_i - \bar{X})$ and standard deviations completely unchanged.

SECTION B: 2-Mark Short Answer Problems

Q3: For 10 pairs of observations on $X$ and $Y$, the sum of products of deviations from their respective means is 120. If $\sigma_x = 4$ and $\sigma_y = 5$, find $r$.

Show Answer & Scheme

• Given: $N = 10, \sum (x - \bar{X})(y - \bar{Y}) = 120, \sigma_x = 4, \sigma_y = 5$.
• $\text{Cov}(X, Y) = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{X})(y - \bar{Y})}{N} = \frac{120}{10} = 12$ [1 Mark].
• $r = \frac{\text{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_x \sigma_y} = \frac{12}{4 \times 5} = \frac{12}{20} = \mathbf{0.60}$ [1 Mark].

Q4: State two critical assumptions underlying Karl Pearson's correlation coefficient.

Show Answer & Scheme

Assumptions [2 Marks]:
(1) Linearity: The relationship between the two variables must be linear.
(2) Normality: The variables should follow an approximately bivariate normal distribution, with neither variable being heavily distorted by extreme outliers.

SECTION C: 4-Mark Comprehensive Numerical Drill

Q5: An economist investigates the relationship between the price of crude oil ($X$ in $ per barrel) and the price of aviation jet fuel ($Y$ in cents per gallon) over 6 quarters:
$X$: $60, \quad 65, \quad 70, \quad 75, \quad 80, \quad 85$
$Y$: $150, \quad 165, \quad 170, \quad 185, \quad 200, \quad 210$
(i) Using Assumed Means $A = 70$ and $B = 180$, compute $u = X - 70$ and $v = Y - 180$.
(ii) Construct the short-cut table showing $u, v, u^2, v^2, uv$.
(iii) Calculate Karl Pearson's correlation coefficient and interpret the result.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Deviations & Table [2 Marks]:
• Point 1: $(60, 150) \implies u = -10, v = -30 \implies u^2 = 100, v^2 = 900, uv = 300$
• Point 2: $(65, 165) \implies u = -5, v = -15 \implies u^2 = 25, v^2 = 225, uv = 75$
• Point 3: $(70, 170) \implies u = 0, v = -10 \implies u^2 = 0, v^2 = 100, uv = 0$
• Point 4: $(75, 185) \implies u = +5, v = +5 \implies u^2 = 25, v^2 = 25, uv = 25$
• Point 5: $(80, 200) \implies u = +10, v = +20 \implies u^2 = 100, v^2 = 400, uv = 200$
• Point 6: $(85, 210) \implies u = +15, v = +30 \implies u^2 = 225, v^2 = 900, uv = 450$
• Sums: $\sum u = 15, \quad \sum v = 0, \quad \sum u^2 = 475, \quad \sum v^2 = 2550, \quad \sum uv = 1050$. $N = 6$.

Calculation [1.5 Marks]:
$$r = \frac{N\sum uv - (\sum u)(\sum v)}{\sqrt{\left[N\sum u^2 - (\sum u)^2\right]\left[N\sum v^2 - (\sum v)^2\right]}}$$ $$r = \frac{6(1050) - (15)(0)}{\sqrt{\left[6(475) - 15^2\right]\left[6(2550) - 0^2\right]}} = \frac{6300}{\sqrt{(2850 - 225)(15300)}} = \frac{6300}{\sqrt{2625 \times 15300}}$$ $$r = \frac{6300}{\sqrt{40,162,500}} = \frac{6300}{6337.39} \approx \mathbf{0.994}$$

Interpretation [0.5 Mark]:
• $r = +0.994$ establishes an almost perfect positive linear correlation between crude oil costs and jet fuel prices.

High-Yield Quick Revision: Karl Pearson's Correlation

Summary of formulas, properties, and transformation rules for Chapter 11.3.

Master Formula Reference

Method Formula
Actual Mean $r = \frac{\sum (X - \bar{X})(Y - \bar{Y})}{\sqrt{\sum (X - \bar{X})^2 \sum (Y - \bar{Y})^2}}$
Covariance Form $r = \frac{\text{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_x \sigma_y}$
Raw Data $r = \frac{N\sum XY - \sum X \sum Y}{\sqrt{[N\sum X^2 - (\sum X)^2][N\sum Y^2 - (\sum Y)^2]}}$
Assumed Mean ($u, v$) $r = \frac{N\sum uv - \sum u \sum v}{\sqrt{[N\sum u^2 - (\sum u)^2][N\sum v^2 - (\sum v)^2]}}$

Critical Scale Sign Inversion Rule

If $u = ax + b$ and $v = cy + d$:
• If $a \cdot c > 0$ (same signs): $r_{uv} = r_{xy}$.
• If $a \cdot c < 0$ (opposite signs): $r_{uv} = -r_{xy}$.
Exam Trap: Be vigilant when one variable is multiplied by a negative number; it flips the sign of $r$!

PART 1: Objective Section (4 Marks)

Q1: Karl Pearson's coefficient of correlation $r$ always satisfies:

Solution: (B) Pearson's correlation coefficient is strictly bounded: $-1 \le r \le +1$.

Q2: If $\text{Cov}(X, Y) = 24$, $\sigma_x = 6$, and $\sigma_y = 5$, then $r(X, Y)$ is:

Solution: (A) $r = \frac{\text{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_x \sigma_y} = \frac{24}{6 \times 5} = \frac{24}{30} = 0.80$.

Q3: If $r(X, Y) = 0.5$, then the correlation coefficient between $U = 2X + 3$ and $V = -4Y + 1$ is:

Solution: (B) The scale factors are $+2$ and $-4$. Since $(+2)(-4) = -8 < 0$, the sign flips: $r(U, V) = -0.5$.

Q4 (Assertion-Reason):
Assertion (A): Karl Pearson's correlation coefficient is a pure, dimensionless number without any physical units.
Reason (R): The units of the numerator (covariance) are the product of units of $X$ and $Y$, which cancel exactly with the product of the units of $\sigma_x$ and $\sigma_y$ in the denominator.

Solution: (A) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason provides the precise dimensional analysis.

PART 2: Subjective Section (Q5 - Q10)

Q5 2 Marks

If $\sum (x_i - \bar{X})^2 = 49$, $\sum (y_i - \bar{Y})^2 = 81$, and $\sum (x_i - \bar{X})(y_i - \bar{Y}) = -56.7$, find $r$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

$$r = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{X})(y - \bar{Y})}{\sqrt{\sum (x - \bar{X})^2 \cdot \sum (y - \bar{Y})^2}} = \frac{-56.7}{\sqrt{49 \times 81}} \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$ $$r = \frac{-56.7}{7 \times 9} = \frac{-56.7}{63} = \mathbf{-0.90} \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$

Q6 2 Marks

State whether the correlation coefficient changes when temperature is converted from Celsius to Fahrenheit ($F = \frac{9}{5}C + 32$). Justify mathematically.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Answer [1 Mark]: The correlation coefficient remains completely unchanged.
• Justification [1 Mark]: The conversion $F = \frac{9}{5}C + 32$ is a positive linear transformation with scale factor $a = \frac{9}{5} > 0$. Pearson's $r$ is invariant under change of origin and positive scale changes ($r_{F, Y} = r_{C, Y}$).

Q7 3 Marks

Prove using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that Karl Pearson's correlation coefficient cannot exceed 1 in magnitude (i.e. $|r| \le 1$).

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Let $a_i = x_i - \bar{X}$ and $b_i = y_i - \bar{Y}$.
• By Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality for real vectors: $$\left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i b_i\right)^2 \le \left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i^2\right) \left(\sum_{i=1}^n b_i^2\right) \quad [1.5 \text{ Marks}]$$ • Dividing both sides by the right-hand term: $$\frac{\left(\sum a_i b_i\right)^2}{\left(\sum a_i^2\right)\left(\sum b_i^2\right)} \le 1 \implies r^2 \le 1 \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$ • Taking the square root: $|r| \le 1 \implies \mathbf{-1 \le r \le +1}$ [0.5 Mark].

Q8 3 Marks

If $r(X, Y) = 0.8$, $\sigma_x = 2$, $\sigma_y = 3$, and $\text{Cov}(X, Y)$ is calculated, what happens to the covariance and correlation if all $X$ values are doubled and all $Y$ values are multiplied by $-3$?

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Original $\text{Cov}(X, Y) = r \cdot \sigma_x \cdot \sigma_y = 0.8 \times 2 \times 3 = \mathbf{4.8}$ [1 Mark].
• Let $X' = 2X$ and $Y' = -3Y$.
• $\text{Cov}(X', Y') = (2)(-3) \text{Cov}(X, Y) = -6 \times 4.8 = \mathbf{-28.8}$ [1 Mark].
• New correlation: Scale multipliers are $a = 2 > 0$ and $b = -3 < 0$. Since $ab = -6 < 0$, the sign reverses: $r' = -r = \mathbf{-0.8}$ [1 Mark].

Q9 & Q10 Case Study (4 Marks Total)

Case Context: Portfolio Asset Diversification

An investment manager analyzes the monthly returns of a Tech Stock ETF ($X$) and a Treasury Bond ETF ($Y$) over 5 months:
$X$: $4\%, \quad 6\%, \quad 8\%, \quad 10\%, \quad 12\%$ (Mean = 8%)
$Y$: $9\%, \quad 8\%, \quad 6\%, \quad 5\%, \quad 2\%$ (Mean = 6%)

Q9 (2 Marks): Calculate Karl Pearson's correlation coefficient $r$ between the Tech Stock ETF and Treasury Bond ETF.
Q10 (2 Marks): From a portfolio risk reduction perspective, explain why combining assets with this correlation value is highly advantageous for an investor.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Solution to Q9 [2 Marks]:
• Deviations from means $\bar{X}=8, \bar{Y}=6$:
- Point 1: $x = -4, y = +3 \implies x^2 = 16, y^2 = 9, xy = -12$
- Point 2: $x = -2, y = +2 \implies x^2 = 4, y^2 = 4, xy = -4$
- Point 3: $x = 0, y = 0 \implies x^2 = 0, y^2 = 0, xy = 0$
- Point 4: $x = +2, y = -1 \implies x^2 = 4, y^2 = 1, xy = -2$
- Point 5: $x = +4, y = -4 \implies x^2 = 16, y^2 = 16, xy = -16$
• Sums: $\sum x^2 = 40, \quad \sum y^2 = 30, \quad \sum xy = -34$.
$$r = \frac{-34}{\sqrt{40 \times 30}} = \frac{-34}{\sqrt{1200}} = \frac{-34}{34.64} = \mathbf{-0.9815}$$

Solution to Q10 [2 Marks]:
• Portfolio Diversification: A near-perfect negative correlation ($r \approx -0.98$) means when the stock market plunges, the bond asset predictably surges in value, offsetting losses.
• This powerful hedge dramatically dampens overall portfolio volatility without sacrificing long-term capital preservation.
