Comprehensive Concepts & Principles: Scatter Diagram Method

The Scatter Diagram (Scatter Plot) is the simplest visual and graphical technique for inspecting bivariate data. By plotting paired numerical observations $(x_1, y_1), (x_2, y_2), \dots, (x_n, y_n)$ as discrete points on a two-dimensional Cartesian plane, the scatter plot visually reveals the presence, direction, linearity, and approximate strength of correlation before undertaking any intensive algebraic calculations.

Key Scatter Diagram Patterns & Correlation Archetypes:
  • Perfect Positive Correlation ($r = +1$): All data points lie exactly on a single straight line rising upward from the lower-left to the upper-right quadrant.
  • High Degree Positive Correlation ($0.75 \le r < 1$): Points cluster tightly along an upward-sloping straight band from bottom-left to top-right.
  • Low / Moderate Positive Correlation ($0 < r < 0.75$): Points show a discernible upward trend but are widely dispersed across the plane.
  • Perfect Negative Correlation ($r = -1$): All data points lie exactly on a single straight line sloping downward from upper-left to lower-right.
  • High Degree Negative Correlation ($-1 < r \le -0.75$): Points cluster tightly along a downward-sloping linear band from top-left to bottom-right.
  • Zero Correlation ($r = 0$): Points are scattered haphazardly over the entire plot without any directional drift (forming a circular or diffuse cloud), or form a strictly horizontal or vertical line.
  • Curvilinear (Non-Linear) Correlation ($r \approx 0$ linearly): Points follow a well-defined curved path (e.g., U-shape, inverted U, or parabola). While linear correlation $r$ may compute to zero, a strong non-linear functional dependency exists!

Merits & Limitations of Scatter Diagrams

Merits

  • Extremely simple and intuitive visual inspection requiring zero mathematical calculations.
  • Instantly identifies whether the underlying relationship is linear or non-linear.
  • Easily highlights extreme data outliers and anomalous entry points.
  • Unaffected by mathematical scale distortions.

Limitations

  • Purely qualitative: cannot provide an exact, quantified numerical value for correlation (e.g., cannot distinguish between $r = 0.78$ and $r = 0.84$).
  • Subjective interpretation: different observers may judge the dispersion of the point band differently.
  • Unsuitable when multiple observations share identical $(x_i, y_i)$ coordinates (overplotting).

Worked Benchmark Examples

Example 1 (Interpreting Direction and Degree):

A scatter plot of employee years of experience ($X$) versus annual salary ($Y$) reveals that all points form a narrow, tightly clustered band rising upward from the bottom-left to the top-right. What can you infer about:
(a) The direction of correlation?
(b) The degree of correlation?
(c) The approximate numerical range of Karl Pearson's coefficient $r$?

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• (a) Direction: Because the band rises from bottom-left to top-right, the correlation is Positive (Direct).
• (b) Degree: Because the points form a narrow, tightly clustered band rather than a wide diffuse cloud, the degree of correlation is High.
• (c) Numerical Range: For a high degree positive linear correlation, Karl Pearson's coefficient lies approximately in the range $\mathbf{+0.75 \le r < +1.0}$.

Example 2 (The Curvilinear Trap):

Consider the bivariate dataset:
$$(-3, 9), \quad (-2, 4), \quad (-1, 1), \quad (0, 0), \quad (1, 1), \quad (2, 4), \quad (3, 9)$$
(i) Plotting these points on a scatter diagram reveals what geometric curve?
(ii) If Karl Pearson's linear correlation coefficient computes to $r = 0$, does this mean there is no relationship between $X$ and $Y$?

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• (i) Geometric Shape: The points follow a symmetric U-shaped parabolic curve defined by the exact functional rule $Y = X^2$.
• (ii) Interpretation: No! A linear correlation coefficient of $r = 0$ only means there is no linear (straight-line) relationship. Here, there is a perfect non-linear deterministic relationship ($Y = X^2$). A scatter diagram is vital because it reveals non-linear patterns that linear correlation formulas completely miss!

Problem 1 Visual Archetype Identification

State the type and degree of correlation indicated by each of the following scatter diagram patterns:
(a) All plotted points lie exactly on a straight line falling from upper-left to lower-right.
(b) Points are widely scattered in a circular cloud with no discernible trend.
(c) Points cluster closely along a straight line rising from lower-left to upper-right.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• (a) Perfect Negative Correlation ($r = -1$).
• (b) Zero / No Correlation ($r = 0$).
• (c) High Degree Positive Correlation ($0.75 \le r < 1$).

Problem 2 Plot Construction & Inspection

Given the paired observations of temperature ($X$ in °C) and sales of hot beverages ($Y$ in cups):
$X$: $10, \quad 15, \quad 20, \quad 25, \quad 30, \quad 35$
$Y$: $90, \quad 80, \quad 65, \quad 50, \quad 35, \quad 20$
Describe the appearance of its scatter plot and specify the type of correlation.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• As $X$ increases by uniform steps of $+5$ ($10 \to 35$), $Y$ decreases consistently:
$(10, 90), (15, 80), (20, 65), (25, 50), (30, 35), (35, 20)$
• The plotted points form a downward-sloping linear band falling from upper-left to lower-right.
• Conclusion: The scatter plot indicates a High Degree Negative Correlation.

Problem 3 Qualitative vs Quantitative

Why is the scatter diagram method termed a 'qualitative' rather than a 'quantitative' method of correlation analysis?

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• A scatter diagram reveals the visual nature and general direction (positive/negative/zero) of the relationship, but does not yield a precise mathematical number.
• It cannot quantify whether correlation is $0.82$ or $0.88$, and hence cannot be used in formal statistical hypothesis testing without algebraic coefficients.

Problem 4 Outlier Detection on Scatter Plots

In a scatter plot of study hours vs marks, 24 students lie in a tight positive linear band, while 1 student who studied 15 hours scored 10 marks. How does this point appear on the scatter plot and what is its statistical term?

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• The point $(15, 10)$ appears isolated far away in the bottom-right corner, completely detached from the general upward-trending cluster.
• This point is termed an Outlier (or Influential Point). Scatter diagrams are especially valuable because they make such anomalies instantly visible.

Problem 5 Slope & Degree Distinction

Does a steeper upward slope on a scatter plot imply a higher degree of correlation $r$? Explain clearly.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• NO. The slope of the line reflects the rate of change ($b = Delta Y / Delta X$) and depends on the scale/units of measurement.
• The degree of correlation ($r$) depends strictly on how closely the points cluster around the line, not how steep the line is. Points clustered tightly around a gentle slope have $r approx 0.95$, while points widely dispersed around a steep slope may have $r approx 0.40$.

Show Answer & Scheme

The statement is accurate. A scatter plot provides a qualitative visual snapshot of the direction and linearity of association. SECTION C: 4-Mark Comprehensive Scenario Drill

Q5: An automobile manufacturer tests the braking performance of 6 test vehicles under varying speeds:
• Speed ($X$ in km/h): $20, \quad 40, \quad 60, \quad 80, \quad 100, \quad 120$
• Stopping Distance ($Y$ in metres): $6, \quad 16, \quad 36, \quad 64, \quad 100, \quad 144$
(i) Plotting $(X, Y)$ reveals what shape of scatter diagram?
(ii) Is the relationship linear or non-linear? Explain by examining ratios $\Delta Y / \Delta X$.
(iii) What is the physical/mathematical law connecting $X$ and $Y$?

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Part (i) Shape of Scatter Diagram [1 Mark]:
• The points curve upward steeply, forming a convex parabolic curve rising from bottom-left to top-right.

Part (ii) Linearity Inspection [1.5 Marks]:
• $\Delta X = +20$ km/h consistently.
• $\Delta Y$: $16-6=10, 36-16=20, 64-36=28, 100-64=36, 144-100=44$.
• $\Delta Y / \Delta X$ increases steadily ($0.5 \to 1.0 \to 1.4 \to 1.8 \to 2.2$).
• Because the slope accelerates, the relationship is strictly Non-Linear.

Part (iii) Mathematical Law [1.5 Marks]:
• In physics, kinetic energy $E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$. Work done by braking force $F \cdot d = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \implies d \propto v^2$.
• Here $Y = 0.01 X^2$ (e.g. $0.01 \times 100^2 = 100$, $0.01 \times 120^2 = 144$).
• The scatter plot perfectly exposes this quadratic physical law.

Visual Archetype Summary Table

Pattern Description Visual Trend Correlation Category Approx. $r$ Value
Straight line rising right Bottom-left to top-right Perfect Positive $r = +1$
Narrow band rising right Bottom-left to top-right High Positive $+0.75 \le r < +1$

Crucial Exam Distinction

Do not confuse slope with correlation tightness. A steep line with widely scattered dots has a lower correlation than a gentle slope with dots resting almost directly on the line. Correlation measures the tightness of fit around the line, not the inclination of the line.

Q5 2 Marks

Define a 'Scatter Diagram' and state two practical situations where constructing a scatter plot is recommended prior to formal modeling.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Definition [1 Mark]: A scatter diagram is a Cartesian coordinate plot where paired bivariate observations $(x_i, y_i)$ are plotted as individual points to visualize their relationship.
• Situations [1 Mark]: (1) Checking whether the data satisfies the linearity assumption required for linear regression; (2) Detecting extreme outliers and data entry errors.

Q6 2 Marks

(a) $r = +1$
(b) $r = -1$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• (a) When $r = +1$, every single point lies precisely on an upward-sloping straight line ($45^circ$ or positive slope) [1 Mark].
• (b) When $r = -1$, every single point lies precisely on a downward-sloping straight line (negative slope) [1 Mark].

Q7 3 Marks

State three distinct merits and two limitations of the Scatter Diagram Method in statistical quality control.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Merits [1.5 Marks]: (1) Visual and instantly understandable by non-mathematicians; (2) Unaffected by extreme values in terms of distorting formula calculations; (3) Reveals non-linear curvature.
• Limitations [1.5 Marks]: (1) Fails to yield an exact numerical index of correlation; (2) Subject to human visual interpretation bias.

Q8 3 Marks

Explain why a scatter diagram where points lie on a horizontal line ($Y = c$) indicates zero correlation, even though all points lie perfectly on a straight line.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Mathematical Analysis [1.5 Marks]: If $Y = c$ (constant), then for every observation $y_i = c$ and mean $ar{Y} = c$. The deviations $(y_i - ar{Y}) = 0$ for all points.
• Correlation Implication [1.5 Marks]: The covariance $sum (x_i - ar{X})(y_i - ar{Y}) = 0$, and the variance $sigma_y = 0$. Since $Y$ does not vary at all with changes in $X$, knowing $X$ provides zero discriminatory information about changes in $Y$. Hence, $r = 0$.

Q9 & Q10 Case Study (4 Marks Total)

Case Context: Chemical Synthesis Yield vs Temperature

A pharmaceutical researcher tests the yield of an active drug ingredient ($Y$ in grams) at different reaction temperatures ($X$ in °C):
$(20^circ, 30g), \quad (40^circ, 70g), \quad (60^circ, 100g), \quad (80^circ, 75g), \quad (100^circ, 35g)$.

Q9 (2 Marks): Describe the geometric shape formed by these points on a scatter diagram.
Q10 (2 Marks): If linear correlation $r$ is computed for this data, explain why $r$ is approximately zero, and explain the chemical phenomenon behind the curve.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Solution to Q9 [2 Marks]:
• The points rise to an optimal peak at $(60^circ, 100g)$ and then symmetrically decline at higher temperatures.
• The scatter plot forms an inverted U-shape (parabolic arc).

Solution to Q10 [2 Marks]:
• Linear $r approx 0$: Because the strong positive correlation in the first half ($20^circ o 60^circ$) is cancelled out by the equally strong negative correlation in the second half ($60^circ o 100^circ$), Karl Pearson's linear $r$ averages out to near zero.
• Chemical Phenomenon: Reaction rates accelerate with temperature up to an optimum ($60^circ$), beyond which thermal denaturation/decomposition degrades the active compound.
