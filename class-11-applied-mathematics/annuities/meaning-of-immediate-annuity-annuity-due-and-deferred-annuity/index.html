Meaning of Immediate Annuity, Annuity Due and Deferred Annuity

Master Immediate Annuity, Annuity Due, and Deferred Annuity for CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics with clear definitions, formulas, and solved examples.

Immediate Annuity (Ordinary Annuity)

An immediate annuity is a series of equal payments made at the end of each period for a specified number of periods. It is the most common form of annuity, often used in loan repayments or standard savings plans. If payments occur at the end of each interval, the first payment is made after the first period has elapsed.

FV = P × [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1] / r
Example: Solved Example: Future Value of Immediate Annuity
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify P = ₹1000, r = 0.05, n = 3 years.
Step 2: Apply formula: FV = 1000 × [(1 + 0.05)³ − 1] / 0.05.
Step 3: Calculate: 1000 × [1.157625 − 1] / 0.05 = 1000 × 3.1525.
Answer: ₹3152.50

Annuity Due

An annuity due is a series of equal payments made at the beginning of each period. Because payments are made earlier than in an immediate annuity, they earn interest for one additional period. This results in a higher accumulated value compared to an ordinary annuity.

FV_due = P × [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1] / r × (1 + r)
Example: Solved Example: Future Value of Annuity Due
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify P = ₹1000, r = 0.05, n = 3 years.
Step 2: Apply formula: FV_due = 3152.50 × (1 + 0.05).
Step 3: Calculate: 3152.50 × 1.05.
Answer: ₹3310.13

Deferred Annuity

A deferred annuity is an annuity where the first payment is delayed for a certain number of periods (the deferment period). The payments start only after the deferment period has passed. It is commonly used in retirement planning where contributions are made now to receive payments later.

PV = P × [(1 − (1 + r)⁻ⁿ) / r] × (1 + r)⁻ᵏ
Example: Solved Example: Present Value of Deferred Annuity
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: P = ₹500, r = 0.10, n = 2 years, k = 1 year deferment.
Step 2: PV = 500 × [(1 − (1.1)⁻²) / 0.10] × (1.1)⁻¹.
Step 3: PV = 500 × 1.7355 × 0.9091.
Answer: ₹788.75

mediumCalculate the difference in future value between an immediate annuity and an annuity due of ₹1000 for 2 years at 10% interest.

1

Assertion (A): Annuity due always has a higher future value than an immediate annuity for the same payment and interest rate.
Reason (R): Annuity due payments are made at the beginning of the period, allowing them to earn interest for one extra period.

Choose the correct option:
(A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
(B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(C) A is true but R is false.
(D) A is false but R is true.

Show Answer

✔ Correct: A

Both the assertion and reason are correct, and the reason explains the assertion.

2

Assertion (A): A deferred annuity has a lower present value than an immediate annuity of the same duration.
Reason (R): The payments in a deferred annuity are discounted over a longer period due to the deferment phase.

Choose the correct option:
(A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
(B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(C) A is true but R is false.
(D) A is false but R is true.

Show Answer

✔ Correct: A

Delaying payments reduces their present value because they are discounted by an additional factor of (1+r)⁻ᵏ.

1

hardA person invests ₹5000 annually for 4 years at 8% interest. Compare the accumulated amount if the investment is an immediate annuity versus an annuity due.

Show Answer & Solution

Step 1: Identify P=5000, r=0.08, n=4.
Step 2: Formula for immediate: FV = 5000 × ((1.08)⁴ − 1) / 0.08.
Step 3: Calculate: 5000 × (1.360489 − 1) / 0.08 = 5000 × 4.5061 = 22530.56.
Step 4: Formula for due: FV_due = 22530.56 × 1.08.
Step 5: Result: 24333.00.

very hardCalculate the present value of a deferred annuity of ₹1000 per year for 3 years, where the first payment is made at the end of year 4. Assume interest rate is 10%.

Show Answer & Solution

Step 1: Identify P=1000, r=0.10, n=3, k=3 (deferred 3 years).
Step 2: PV of immediate annuity for 3 years = 1000 × (1 − 1.1⁻³) / 0.1 = 2486.85.
Step 3: Discount this value back by 3 years: PV = 2486.85 / (1.1)³.
Step 4: Calculate (1.1)³ = 1.331.
Step 5: Final PV = 2486.85 / 1.331 = 1868.41.
