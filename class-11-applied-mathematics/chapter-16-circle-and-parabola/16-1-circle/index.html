Exercise 16.1: Circle

Comprehensive guide and practice questions for CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics curriculum (2026-27).

1. Fundamental Definition and Standard Form of a Circle

In analytical geometry, a circle is defined as the locus of a point that moves in a two-dimensional plane such that its distance from a fixed point (called the center) remains constant (called the radius).

Standard (Center-Radius) Equation of a Circle

For a circle with center $C(h, k)$ and constant radius $r > 0$, any arbitrary point $P(x, y)$ on its circumference satisfies the Euclidean distance condition:

$$\sqrt{(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2} = r$$

Squaring both sides yields the standard equation:

$$(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$$

If the center is positioned at the origin $O(0, 0)$, the standard equation simplifies to:

$$x^2 + y^2 = r^2$$

2. The General Equation of a Circle

Expanding the standard equation gives:

$$(x^2 - 2hx + h^2) + (y^2 - 2ky + k^2) = r^2 \implies x^2 + y^2 - 2hx - 2ky + (h^2 + k^2 - r^2) = 0$$

Substituting $g = -h$, $f = -k$, and $c = h^2 + k^2 - r^2$, we obtain the general second-degree equation of a circle:

General Equation and Properties
$$x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$$
  • Center: $(-g, -f) = \left(-\frac{1}{2}\text{coeff of } x, -\frac{1}{2}\text{coeff of } y\right)$
  • Radius: $r = \sqrt{g^2 + f^2 - c}$

Geometric Nature of the Locus:

  1. If $g^2 + f^2 - c > 0$: The equation represents a real circle with positive radius.
  2. If $g^2 + f^2 - c = 0$: The equation represents a point circle (radius is zero, locus is strictly the single point $(-g, -f)$).
  3. If $g^2 + f^2 - c < 0$: The radius is imaginary (no real coordinates satisfy the equation).
Key Algebraic Rule: Before identifying $g, f, c$, the coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ must be equal and normalized to $1$. There must be no cross product term ($xy$). If the equation is given as $Ax^2 + Ay^2 + Bx + Cy + D = 0$, divide every term by $A$ first.

3. Circle Described on a Given Diameter

Let $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$ be the endpoints of a diameter of a circle. For any point $P(x, y)$ on the circumference (other than $A$ or $B$), the angle $\angle APB$ inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle ($90^\circ$).

Therefore, the product of the slopes of line segments $AP$ and $BP$ equals $-1$:

$$\left(\frac{y - y_1}{x - x_1}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{y - y_2}{x - x_2}\right) = -1 \implies (x - x_1)(x - x_2) + (y - y_1)(y - y_2) = 0$$
Diameter Form
$$(x - x_1)(x - x_2) + (y - y_1)(y - y_2) = 0$$

4. Intercepts Made by a Circle on Coordinate Axes

To determine the length of the chords intercepted by the circle $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$ on the coordinate axes:

  • X-axis Intercept: Setting $y = 0$ gives the quadratic $x^2 + 2gx + c = 0$. The roots $x_1, x_2$ represent the x-coordinates of the intersection points. The intercept length is $|x_1 - x_2| = \sqrt{(x_1 + x_2)^2 - 4x_1 x_2} = 2\sqrt{g^2 - c}$.
  • Y-axis Intercept: Setting $x = 0$ gives $y^2 + 2fy + c = 0$. The intercept length is $2\sqrt{f^2 - c}$.
Tangency Condition to Axes:
  • The circle touches the x-axis if $g^2 - c = 0 \implies c = g^2$.
  • The circle touches the y-axis if $f^2 - c = 0 \implies c = f^2$.
  • The circle touches both coordinate axes in the first quadrant if $g = -r, f = -r$ and $c = r^2$.

5. Step-by-Step Worked Examples

Example 1: Finding Center and Radius from General Form

Problem: Find the center and radius of the circle given by $2x^2 + 2y^2 - 12x + 16y - 6 = 0$.

Solution:

  1. Normalize the equation by dividing all terms by the leading coefficient $2$: $$x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 8y - 3 = 0$$
  2. Compare with standard general form $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$: $$2g = -6 \implies g = -3$$ $$2f = 8 \implies f = 4$$ $$c = -3$$
  3. Compute the coordinates of the center: $$\text{Center} = (-g, -f) = (3, -4)$$
  4. Calculate the radius: $$r = \sqrt{g^2 + f^2 - c} = \sqrt{(-3)^2 + 4^2 - (-3)} = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 3} = \sqrt{28} = 2\sqrt{7} \approx 5.29\text{ units}$$
Example 2: Determining Circle Equation from Three Non-Collinear Points

Problem: Find the equation of the circle passing through the origin $(0, 0)$ and having intercepts $4$ and $6$ on the positive x-axis and positive y-axis, respectively.

Solution:

  1. The circle passes through $O(0, 0)$, $A(4, 0)$, and $B(0, 6)$.
  2. Because the coordinate axes are perpendicular ($\angle AOB = 90^\circ$), the line segment $AB$ connecting $(4, 0)$ and $(0, 6)$ is a diameter of the circle (Thales's Theorem).
  3. Applying the diameter form $(x - x_1)(x - x_2) + (y - y_1)(y - y_2) = 0$: $$(x - 4)(x - 0) + (y - 0)(y - 6) = 0$$ $$x(x - 4) + y(y - 6) = 0 \implies x^2 - 4x + y^2 - 6y = 0$$
  4. Rearranging into standard general form: $$x^2 + y^2 - 4x - 6y = 0$$ Here, center is $(2, 3)$ and radius is $\sqrt{2^2 + 3^2 - 0} = \sqrt{13}$.
Example 3: Real-World Case Study — Telecom Signal Coverage Zone

Problem: A 5G cellular transceiver tower is installed at geographical coordinates $(2, -1)$ (units in kilometers). Field engineering tests indicate an omnidirectional coverage radius of $5\text{ km}$.

(a) Formulate the equation representing the outer boundary of this cellular coverage cell.
(b) An automated industrial IoT warehouse is situated at $(5, 3)$. Verify whether this facility lies inside, outside, or on the signal coverage boundary.

Solution:

  1. Coverage Boundary Equation:
    The tower is at center $(h, k) = (2, -1)$ with radius $r = 5$. $$(x - 2)^2 + (y - (-1))^2 = 5^2 \implies (x - 2)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 25$$ Expanding: $$x^2 - 4x + 4 + y^2 + 2y + 1 = 25 \implies x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 2y - 20 = 0$$
  2. Facility Location Evaluation:
    Compute the Euclidean distance $d$ from the tower $(2, -1)$ to the warehouse $(5, 3)$: $$d = \sqrt{(5 - 2)^2 + (3 - (-1))^2} = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5\text{ km}$$ Alternatively, substitute $(5, 3)$ into $S = x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 2y - 20$: $$S(5, 3) = 5^2 + 3^2 - 4(5) + 2(3) - 20 = 25 + 9 - 20 + 6 - 20 = 0$$ Since $d = r = 5\text{ km}$ ($S = 0$), the warehouse is located precisely on the outer perimeter of the cell coverage zone. It maintains optimal line-of-sight signal access.

Tier 1: Foundations & Parameter Identification

Problem 1: Find the center and radius of the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 10x + 4y + 20 = 0$.

View Complete Solution

Comparing with $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$:

$2g = -10 \implies g = -5$, $2f = 4 \implies f = 2$, $c = 20$.

Center $= (-g, -f) = (5, -2)$.

Radius $r = \sqrt{(-5)^2 + 2^2 - 20} = \sqrt{25 + 4 - 20} = \sqrt{9} = 3\text{ units}$.

Problem 2: Find the equation of the circle whose center is at $(-3, 4)$ and which passes through the point $(2, -1)$.

View Complete Solution

The radius $r$ is the distance between $(-3, 4)$ and $(2, -1)$:

$r^2 = (2 - (-3))^2 + (-1 - 4)^2 = 5^2 + (-5)^2 = 25 + 25 = 50$.

Standard form: $(x + 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 50$.

General form: $x^2 + 6x + 9 + y^2 - 8y + 16 - 50 = 0 \implies x^2 + y^2 + 6x - 8y - 25 = 0$.

Tier 2: Geometric & Tangency Conditions

Problem 3: A circle with center $(3, -2)$ touches the straight line $5x + 12y - 17 = 0$. Determine its radius and Cartesian equation.

View Complete Solution

The radius $r$ is the perpendicular distance from the center $(3, -2)$ to the tangent line:

$$r = \frac{|5(3) + 12(-2) - 17|}{\sqrt{5^2 + 12^2}} = \frac{|15 - 24 - 17|}{\sqrt{169}} = \frac{|-26|}{13} = 2\text{ units}$$

Equation of circle: $(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 2^2 = 4$.

Expanding: $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y + 9 = 0$.

Problem 4: Find the equation of the circle concentric with $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0$ and passing through $(-1, 1)$.

View Complete Solution

Concentric circles share the identical center $(-g, -f) = (3, -2)$, so their equations differ solely in the constant term:

Let equation be $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y + k = 0$.

Since it passes through $(-1, 1)$:

$(-1)^2 + (1)^2 - 6(-1) + 4(1) + k = 0 \implies 1 + 1 + 6 + 4 + k = 0 \implies 12 + k = 0 \implies k = -12$.

Equation: $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0$ (the given point already lies on this circle!).

Tier 3: Applied Real-World Problems

Problem 5: An earthquake reporting station at the origin $O(0, 0)$ detects tremors originating $10\text{ km}$ away. A second station at $(12, 0)$ records the epicenter at $10\text{ km}$ away. Assuming the epicenter lies in the northern quadrant ($y > 0$), triangulate its coordinates.

View Complete Solution

First station locus: $x^2 + y^2 = 10^2 = 100$.

Second station locus: $(x - 12)^2 + y^2 = 10^2 = 100$.

Expanding second equation: $x^2 - 24x + 144 + y^2 = 100$.

Subtracting the first equation from the second: $-24x + 144 = 0 \implies 24x = 144 \implies x = 6$.

Substituting $x = 6$ into $x^2 + y^2 = 100$:

$36 + y^2 = 100 \implies y^2 = 64 \implies y = 8$ (since $y > 0$).

The seismic epicenter is precisely at $(6, 8)\text{ km}$.

Solve these rigorous curriculum questions covering all forms and geometric constraints of circles.

1. Find the center and radius of the circle $3x^2 + 3y^2 - 6x + 9y - 15 = 0$.

Show Solution

Divide by 3: $x^2 + y^2 - 2x + 3y - 5 = 0$.

$g = -1, f = \frac{3}{2}, c = -5$.

Center $= (1, -1.5)$.

Radius $r = \sqrt{(-1)^2 + (1.5)^2 - (-5)} = \sqrt{1 + 2.25 + 5} = \sqrt{8.25} = \frac{\sqrt{33}}{2} \approx 2.87\text{ units}$.

2. Find the equation of the circle whose diameter has endpoints $(-2, 3)$ and $(4, 5)$.

Show Solution

Using diameter form: $(x - x_1)(x - x_2) + (y - y_1)(y - y_2) = 0$

$(x + 2)(x - 4) + (y - 3)(y - 5) = 0$

$(x^2 - 2x - 8) + (y^2 - 8y + 15) = 0 \implies x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 8y + 7 = 0$.

3. Determine the length of the intercepts cut off on the x-axis and y-axis by the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 8y + 9 = 0$.

Show Solution

$g = -3, f = -4, c = 9$.

X-intercept $= 2\sqrt{g^2 - c} = 2\sqrt{9 - 9} = 0$ (touches x-axis at $x = 3$).

Y-intercept $= 2\sqrt{f^2 - c} = 2\sqrt{16 - 9} = 2\sqrt{7}\text{ units}$.

4. Find the value of $k$ if the point $(2, k)$ lies on the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 4x - 6y - 12 = 0$.

Show Solution

Substitute $(2, k)$:

$2^2 + k^2 - 4(2) - 6k - 12 = 0 \implies 4 + k^2 - 8 - 6k - 12 = 0 \implies k^2 - 6k - 16 = 0$.

$(k - 8)(k + 2) = 0 \implies k = 8$ or $k = -2$.

5. A circle passes through the points $(0, 0)$, $(0, 5)$, and $(3, 3)$. Find its equation.

Show Solution

Let equation be $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$.

Through $(0, 0) \implies c = 0$.

Through $(0, 5) \implies 0 + 25 + 0 + 10f = 0 \implies f = -2.5$.

Through $(3, 3) \implies 9 + 9 + 6g + 6(-2.5) = 0 \implies 18 + 6g - 15 = 0 \implies 6g = -3 \implies g = -0.5$.

Equation: $x^2 + y^2 - x - 5y = 0$.

Quick Reference & High-Yield Summary

Form of Circle Equation Center Radius
Central (Origin) $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ $(0, 0)$ $r$
Standard (Center-Radius) $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$ $(h, k)$ $r$
General Second-Degree $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$ $(-g, -f)$ $\sqrt{g^2 + f^2 - c}$
Diameter Form $(x - x_1)(x - x_2) + (y - y_1)(y - y_2) = 0$ $\left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$ $\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$
Key Intercepts & Contact Properties:
  • Length of x-intercept: $2\sqrt{g^2 - c}$
  • Length of y-intercept: $2\sqrt{f^2 - c}$
  • Touches x-axis: $c = g^2$
  • Touches y-axis: $c = f^2$
  • Position of point $P(x_1, y_1)$ relative to circle:
    • $S_1 = x_1^2 + y_1^2 + 2gx_1 + 2fy_1 + c > 0 \implies$ Point lies outside
    • $S_1 = 0 \implies$ Point lies on circumference
    • $S_1 < 0 \implies$ Point lies inside
