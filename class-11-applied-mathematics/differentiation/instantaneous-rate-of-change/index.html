Instantaneous Rate of Change

Master Instantaneous Rate of Change for CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics. Learn to calculate derivatives as slopes and rates with solved examples.

Concept of Instantaneous Rate of Change

The instantaneous rate of change of a function f(x) at a specific point x = a represents the slope of the tangent line to the curve at that point. It is defined as the limit of the average rate of change as the interval approaches zero. This concept is fundamental in physics and economics to describe velocity or marginal cost.

f'(a) = lim(h→0) [f(a+h) - f(a)] / h
Example: Solved Example: Find the rate of change of f(x) = x² at x = 3
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify f(x) = x² and a = 3.
Step 2: Apply the limit definition: f'(3) = lim(h→0) [(3+h)² - 3²] / h.
Step 3: Expand: lim(h→0) [9 + 6h + h² - 9] / h = lim(h→0) [6h + h²] / h.
Step 4: Simplify: lim(h→0) (6 + h) = 6.
Answer: 6

Derivative as a Function

Instead of evaluating at a single point, we can define the derivative f'(x) as a new function that gives the instantaneous rate of change for any x in the domain. This allows us to determine the slope of the tangent at any point on the curve. For power functions, we use the power rule to simplify calculations.

d/dx(xⁿ) = n·xⁿ⁻¹
Example: Solved Example: Find the derivative of f(x) = 4x³
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify n = 3 and the constant 4.
Step 2: Apply the power rule: f'(x) = 4 · (3x³⁻¹).
Step 3: Multiply constants: 4 · 3 = 12.
Answer: 12x²

Applications in Economics

In economics, the instantaneous rate of change of the total cost function C(x) with respect to the quantity x is known as the marginal cost. It represents the approximate cost of producing one additional unit. Similarly, marginal revenue is the derivative of the total revenue function.

MC = dC/dx
Example: Solved Example: Find marginal cost if C(x) = 5x² + 10x + 500
Show Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Given C(x) = 5x² + 10x + 500.
Step 2: Differentiate with respect to x: d/dx(5x²) + d/dx(10x) + d/dx(500).
Step 3: Apply rules: 10x + 10 + 0.
Answer: 10x + 10

Section D: Long Answer Questions

1

hardA company's revenue function is R(x) = 50x - 0.5x². Find the marginal revenue at x = 10, x = 20, and x = 50. Also, determine the value of x where marginal revenue is zero.

Show Answer & Solution

Step 1: Identify R(x) = 50x - 0.5x².
Step 2: Find MR = dR/dx = 50 - x.
Step 3: Calculate MR(10) = 50 - 10 = 40.
Step 4: Calculate MR(20) = 50 - 20 = 30.
Step 5: Calculate MR(50) = 50 - 50 = 0.
Step 6: Set MR = 0: 50 - x = 0, so x = 50.

2

very hardThe cost of producing x units is C(x) = 0.02x³ - 0.5x² + 10x + 500. Find the marginal cost function and calculate the marginal cost at x = 5 and x = 10. Compare the rates of change.

Show Answer & Solution

Step 1: C(x) = 0.02x³ - 0.5x² + 10x + 500.
Step 2: MC = C'(x) = 0.06x² - 1.0x + 10.
Step 3: MC(5) = 0.06(25) - 1.0(5) + 10 = 1.5 - 5 + 10 = 6.5.
Step 4: MC(10) = 0.06(100) - 1.0(10) + 10 = 6 - 10 + 10 = 6.
Step 5: Compare: The marginal cost decreases from 6.5 to 6 as production increases from 5 to 10 units.

Section E: Case-Based Question

★

Case Study: A bakery tracks its daily production cost C(x) in rupees for x loaves of bread, given by C(x) = 0.01x² + 2x + 100.

Sub-question 1: (i) Find the marginal cost function. (ii) Calculate the marginal cost when 50 loaves are produced.

Show Solution

(i) MC = d/dx(0.01x² + 2x + 100) = 0.02x + 2.
(ii) MC(50) = 0.02(50) + 2 = 1 + 2 = 3.

Sub-question 2: If the bakery increases production from 50 to 100 loaves, calculate the marginal cost at 100 loaves and compare it with the result at 50 loaves.

Show Solution

MC(100) = 0.02(100) + 2 = 2 + 2 = 4. The marginal cost increases from 3 to 4 as production increases.
