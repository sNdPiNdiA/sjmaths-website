Odd days in a month/ year/ century

Comprehensive study of odd days in calendars, covering calculations for months, years, and centuries, essential for CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics students to master chronological reasoning.

Understanding Odd Days

An 'odd day' is the remainder obtained when the total number of days in a given period is divided by 7. Since a week consists of 7 days, the remaining days represent the offset beyond complete weeks. This is a fundamental concept for determining days of the week for specific dates.

Odd Days = (Total Number of Days) mod 7
Example 1: Calculate the number of odd days in a leap year.
Show Step-by-Step Solution

• A leap year has 366 days.
• Divide 366 by 7: 366 = (52 * 7) + 2.
• The remainder is 2.

Answer: There are 2 odd days in a leap year.

Odd Days in Months

The number of odd days in a month depends on the total number of days in that specific month. For months with 31 days, 31 mod 7 = 3 odd days. For months with 30 days, 30 mod 7 = 2 odd days.

Odd Days in month = (Days in month) mod 7
Example 1: Find the number of odd days in the month of February in a non-leap year.
Show Step-by-Step Solution

• A non-leap year February has 28 days.
• Divide 28 by 7: 28 = (4 * 7) + 0.
• The remainder is 0.

Answer: There are 0 odd days in February of a non-leap year.

Odd Days in a Century

A century consists of 100 years. It contains 76 ordinary years and 24 leap years (since every 4th year is a leap year, 100/4 = 25, but the 100th year is not a leap year unless divisible by 400). Total days = (76 * 365) + (24 * 366) = 36524 days. Dividing 36524 by 7 leaves a remainder of 5.

100 years = 5 odd days; 200 years = 3 odd days; 300 years = 1 odd day; 400 years = 0 odd days.
Example 1: Calculate the total number of odd days in 200 years.
Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Number of odd days in 100 years = 5.
• Number of odd days in 200 years = (5 * 2) = 10.
• Divide 10 by 7: 10 mod 7 = 3.

Answer: There are 3 odd days in 200 years.

How many odd days are present in a non-leap year?

Section C: Short Answer Questions

1

Calculate the number of odd days in 45 days.

Show Answer & Solution

Divide 45 by 7. 45 = 6 * 7 + 3. The remainder is 3. Therefore, there are 3 odd days.

2

If 1st January 2023 was a Sunday, what day was 1st January 2024?

Show Answer & Solution

2023 is a non-leap year (365 days). It has 1 odd day. Thus, the day shifts by 1. Sunday + 1 = Monday.

Section D: Long Answer Questions

1

Find the number of odd days in 300 years and hence determine the day of the week on 31st December 300 AD if 1st January 1 AD was a Monday.

Show Answer & Solution

Step 1: Odd days in 100 years = 5. Step 2: Odd days in 300 years = 5 * 3 = 15. Step 3: 15 mod 7 = 1. Step 4: Starting from Monday, 1 odd day implies the last day is Monday.

2

Calculate the total number of odd days in 550 years.

Show Answer & Solution

Step 1: 550 years = 400 years + 150 years. Step 2: Odd days in 400 years = 0. Step 3: Odd days in 100 years = 5. Step 4: Odd days in 50 years = (12 leap years + 38 ordinary years) = (12*2 + 38*1) = 62 days. 62 mod 7 = 6. Step 5: Total = 0 + 5 + 6 = 11. 11 mod 7 = 4. Final answer: 4 odd days.

Section E: Case-Based Questions

1

Case Study: A calendar system is based on the solar year. Leap years are essential to keep the calendar synchronized with the Earth's orbit. A leap year occurs every 4 years, except for century years that are not divisible by 400.

Sub-question 1: How many leap years are there in a span of 200 years starting from year 1?

Show Solution

In the first 200 years, the years divisible by 4 are 50. Since 100 and 200 are not divisible by 400, they are not leap years. So, 50 - 2 = 48 leap years.

Sub-question 2: What is the total number of odd days in 200 years?

Show Solution

Odd days = (48 * 2) + (152 * 1) = 96 + 152 = 248. 248 / 7 leaves a remainder of 3. Total odd days = 3.
