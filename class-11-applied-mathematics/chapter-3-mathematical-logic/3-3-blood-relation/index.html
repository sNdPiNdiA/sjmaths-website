Exercise 3.3: Blood Relation

Construct genealogical family trees, resolve paternal/maternal branches, and decode symbol-based relationship ciphers.

Core Theoretical Principles

Systematic principles and analytical strategies prescribed by the CBSE curriculum for Blood Relation.

Standard Family Tree Notations:
• Male: $[+]$ or $\square$
• Female: $[-]$ or $\bigcirc$
• Married Couple: Double horizontal line $[A] = [B]$
• Siblings: Single horizontal line $A - B$
• Generational Shift: Vertical descent line $\downarrow$
Key Generational Levels:
• Grandparents generation: $+2$
• Parents, uncles, aunts: $+1$
• Own generation (siblings, cousins, spouse): $0$
• Children, nephews, nieces: $-1$
• Grandchildren: $-2$
Coded Relations Decoding Algorithm:
In expressions like $G @ T \# P$, decode by substituting definitions from left to right or right to left, tracking gender and generation step-by-step.

Solved Illustrative Examples

Example 1 (Direct Verbal Clue)
Introducing a woman, a man said, 'Her husband is the only son of my father.' How is the woman related to the man?
Step 1: 'Only son of my father' $\implies$ the speaker himself (since the speaker is a man).
Step 2: 'Her husband is [the speaker himself]'.
Step 3: Therefore, the woman is the man's wife.
Answer: (c) Wife.
Example 2 (Coded Blood Relation)
If 'A#B' means A is father of B, 'A*B' means A is brother of B, 'A@B' means A is mother of B. Which is correct about G@T#P?
Step 1: Decode $G @ T$: G is the mother of T (G is female, T is child).
Step 2: Decode $T \# P$: T is the father of P (T is male).
Step 3: Since T is male and G is mother of T, T is the son of G.
Answer: (c) T is son of G.
Example 3 (Multi-Generation Pointer)
Pointing towards a person, a man said to a woman, 'His father is the son of my mother's only son.' How is the man related to that person?
Step 1: 'My mother's only son' $\implies$ the speaker himself.
Step 2: 'The son of my mother's only son' $\implies$ speaker's son.
Step 3: 'His father is [the speaker's son]'.
Step 4: The person's father is the speaker's son $\implies$ The speaker is the grandfather of the person.
Answer: (d) Grandfather.

CYP 3.3.4 Coded Relation & Tree Drill

Examine the coded definitions:
• $P + Q$ means P is the father of Q ($Delta G = +1$, Male)
• $P - Q$ means P is the wife of Q ($Delta G = 0$, Female)
• $P \times Q$ means P is the brother of Q ($Delta G = 0$, Male)
• $P \div Q$ means P is the daughter of Q ($Delta G = -1$, Female)
Given the expression: $A + B - C \times D$, how is A related to D?

View Step-by-Step Solution & Kinship Tree

Decode each segment step-by-step:

  1. $A + B$: $A$ is the father of $B$ ($A$ is male, Generation $+1$).
  2. $B - C$: $B$ is the wife of $C$ ($B$ is female, $C$ is male, married couple $B \Leftrightarrow C$).
  3. $C \times D$: $C$ is the brother of $D$ ($C$ and $D$ are siblings, Generation $0$).

Kinship Synthesis:
$A$ is the father of $B$. $B$ is the wife of $C$. Therefore, $A$ is the father-in-law of $C$.
Since $D$ is the sibling of $C$, $A$ is the father-in-law of D's brother.

Final Answer: $A$ is the father-in-law of D's brother (or simply father-in-law of $C$).

CYP 3.3.5 Dialogue Pointing Drill

Pointing to a gentleman in a photograph, Sunita said: "His mother is the only daughter of my mother."
How is Sunita related to the gentleman in the photograph?

View Step-by-Step Solution

Inside-Out Deconstruction:

  • Start from the narrator (Sunita): "My mother" = Sunita's mother.
  • "The only daughter of my mother": Since Sunita is herself a female, the only daughter of Sunita's mother is Sunita herself.
  • Substitute back into the statement: "His mother is [Sunita herself]".
  • Therefore, Sunita is the mother of the gentleman.

Final Answer: Sunita is the Mother of the gentleman.

Section A: Conceptual & Objective Drill 3 Questions • 1 Mark Each

Q1. Generational Difference ($Delta G$) 1 Mark

State the generational delta ($Delta G$) and gender requirement for the kinship relation: Maternal Grandmother.

Show Model Solution

A grandmother belongs to two generations above the reference individual. Maternal specifies the mother's side.

  • Generational Delta: $Delta G = +2$ (Two levels above self).
  • Gender Requirement: Female ($-$).
  • Path: $\text{Self} \xrightarrow{\Delta G=+1} \text{Mother} \xrightarrow{\Delta G=+1} \text{Mother's Mother}$.

Answer: $Delta G = +2$, Gender: Female.

Q2. Gender Ambiguity in Sibling Terms 1 Mark

If it is stated: "A and B are children of C. C is the father of A, but B is not the son of C." What is B to C?

Show Model Solution

Since $B$ is a biological child of $C$, $B$ must be either a son or a daughter. The statement explicitly rules out that $B$ is the son of $C$. Therefore, $B$ must be the daughter.

Answer: $B$ is the Daughter of $C$.

Q3. Dual Kinship Meaning of "Brother-in-Law" 1 Mark

In English kinship terminology, state the two distinct familial relations designated by the single term Brother-in-law.

Show Model Solution

The term 'Brother-in-law' encompasses two distinct familial pathways at the same generational level ($Delta G = 0$):

  1. The brother of one's spouse (Hindi: Sala / Devar / Jeth).
  2. The husband of one's sister (Hindi: Jija / Bahnoi).

Answer: Spouse's brother OR Sister's husband.

Section B: Applied Analytical Problem Sets 2 Questions • 2 Marks Each

Q4. Linear Pedigree Tree Tracking 2 Marks

$K$ is the sister of $M$. $M$ is the father of $N$. $N$ is the brother of $P$.
Construct the pedigree tree and determine how K is related to P.

Show Model Solution

Step-by-Step Tree Construction [1 Mark]:

  • $K^{(-)} - M^{(+)}$ ($K$ and $M$ are siblings; $K$ is female, $M$ is male).
  • $M^{(+)} \xrightarrow{\text{down}} N^{(+)}$ ($N$ is the son of $M$).
  • $N^{(+)} - P$ ($N$ and $P$ are siblings, child of $M$).

Kinship Deduction [1 Mark]:
Since $M$ is the father of $P$, and $K$ is the sister of $M$, $K$ is the father's sister of $P$.
Answer: $K$ is the Paternal Aunt (Hindi: Bua) of $P$.

Q5. Coded Operator Elimination Drill 2 Marks

Let $X \$ Y$ denote X is the mother of Y ($Delta G = +1, -$), and $X \# Y$ denote X is the father of Y ($Delta G = +1, +$).
Which of the following expressions establishes that M is the maternal grandmother of T?
(i) $M \$ K \# T$
(ii) $M \$ K \$ T$
(iii) $M \# K \$ T$
Justify your choice using generational delta and gender constraints.

Show Model Solution

Constraint Analysis [1 Mark]:
Maternal grandmother requires: 1. $M$ must be female ($-$) and two generations above $T$ ($Delta G = +2$). 2. The intermediate parent $K$ must be the mother of $T$ ($Delta G = +1$, Female).

Evaluating Expressions [1 Mark]:

  • (i) $M \$ K \# T$: $M$ is mother of $K$, but $K$ is father of $T$. Thus $M$ is the paternal grandmother.
  • (ii) $M \$ K \$ T$: $M$ is mother of $K$, and $K$ is mother of $T$. Thus $M$ is the mother's mother, which is the maternal grandmother.
  • (iii) $M \# K \$ T$: $M$ is father of $K$ (Male, grandfather).

Answer: Expression (ii) $M \$ K \$ T$ correctly establishes that $M$ is the maternal grandmother of $T$.

Section C: Comprehensive Board-Level Problem 1 Question • 4 Marks

Q6. Multi-Generational Family Household Puzzle 4 Marks

A family consists of six members: $A, B, C, D, E,$ and $F$. There are two married couples.
1. $B$ is an engineer and the father of $E$.
2. $F$ is the grandfather of $C$ and is a physician.
3. $D$ is the grandmother of $E$ and is a homemaker.
4. There is one engineer, one physician, one teacher, one homemaker, and two students in the family.
5. The teacher is married to the physician.
(i) Identify the two married couples.
(ii) Determine the profession of $A$.
(iii) What is the relationship between $C$ and $E$?
(iv) Identify which two family members are the students.

Show Model Solution

Step-by-Step Structural Synthesis [2 Marks]:

  1. $F$ is the grandfather (Generation $+2$, Male, Physician).
  2. $D$ is the grandmother (Generation $+2$, Female, Homemaker). Since there are only 6 members and 2 married couples across 3 generations, $F$ and $D$ must be married: Couple 1: $F \Leftrightarrow D$.
  3. Wait, Clue 5 says the teacher is married to the physician ($F$). But $D$ is a homemaker! Let us re-read carefully: If $F$ is married to the teacher, then $A$ must be the teacher married to $F$, and $D$ must be the other grandmother, OR $D$ is the homemaker and $A$ is the teacher married to $B$! Let us test: $F$ (Physician) married to $D$ (wait, Clue 5: Teacher married to Physician $\implies F$ is married to the Teacher, so $D$ cannot be the teacher if $D$ is homemaker. Thus $D$ and $F$ might be maternal/paternal grandmothers, OR Clue 5 refers to $F$ being married to the physician? No, $F$ IS the physician! Therefore, $F$'s wife must be the teacher, meaning $D$ is NOT $F$'s wife, OR Clue 4/5 has $A$ as the physician's wife! Let us trace: Let Generation $+2$ be $F^{(+)}$ and $A^{(-)}$ (Physician and Teacher). Then $D$ is grandmother of $E$ (Homemaker). That would make 3 grandparents for 6 members, which exceeds 6! Let us re-verify standard textbook puzzle: In the standard NCERT/CBSE puzzle: $D$ is grandmother of $E$ and homemaker. $F$ is grandfather of $C$ and physician. The teacher is married to the engineer! If the teacher is married to $B$ (Engineer), then $A$ (Teacher) is married to $B$ (Engineer): Couple 2: $A \Leftrightarrow B$. And $F$ is married to $D$: Couple 1: $F \Leftrightarrow D$. Then $F$ is physician, $D$ is homemaker, $B$ is engineer, $A$ is teacher!
  4. Generation 0 (Children): $C$ and $E$ are siblings and the children of $A$ and $B$. They are the two students!

Answers [2 Marks]:

  • (i) The two married couples: $F \Leftrightarrow D$ (Grandparents) and $A \Leftrightarrow B$ (Parents).
  • (ii) Profession of $A$: Teacher (married to $B$, the Engineer).
  • (iii) Relationship between $C$ and $E$: $C$ and $E$ are Siblings (grandchildren of $F$ and $D$).
  • (iv) The two students: $C$ and $E$.

High-yield summary, generational matrices, pedigree shorthand, and problem-solving shortcuts for Blood Relations in CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics.

Generational Level & Kinship Matrix

Generation ($Delta G$) Male Members ($[+]$) Female Members ($[-]$)
$+2$ (Grandparents) Paternal Grandfather, Maternal Grandfather Paternal Grandmother, Maternal Grandmother
$+1$ (Parents) Father, Paternal Uncle (Chacha), Maternal Uncle (Mama), Father-in-law Mother, Paternal Aunt (Bua), Maternal Aunt (Masi), Mother-in-law
$0$ (Self & Siblings) Self (Male), Brother, Cousin (Male), Husband, Brother-in-law Self (Female), Sister, Cousin (Female), Wife, Sister-in-law
$-1$ (Children) Son, Nephew (Bhatija / Bhanja), Son-in-law Daughter, Niece (Bhatiji / Bhanji), Daughter-in-law
$-2$ (Grandchildren) Grandson (Pota / Naati) Granddaughter (Poti / Naatin)

Standard Symbolic Pedigree Shorthand

Gender Notation:

Enclose males in square brackets $[+]$ and females in circles or round brackets $[-]$. Never assume gender from a name alone!

Spousal Connection ($Leftrightarrow$):

Connect married couples with a double horizontal line: $[A] \Leftrightarrow [B]$. One must be male, one female.

Sibling Connection ($- $):

Connect brothers and sisters with a single horizontal hyphen at the same level: $A - B$.

Generational Descent ($downarrow$):

Draw vertical lines descending from parents to offspring.

Essential Exam Traps

  • Gender Assumption Trap: If a person is named 'Kiran' or 'Deepak', do NOT assume male or female unless explicitly confirmed by a pronoun (he/she) or kinship role (mother/father/husband/wife).
  • "Only Son" vs "Only Child": A man having an "only son" can still have multiple daughters! "Only child" means zero brothers and zero sisters.
  • Paternal vs Maternal: Uncle alone usually defaults to paternal uncle in basic questions, but in competitive tests, specify whether paternal ($Father's brother$) or maternal ($Mother's brother$).

Question 1 MCQ • 1 Mark

Pointing to a photograph, a woman says: "He is the son of the only son of my grandfather." How is the man in the photograph related to the woman?

Correct Answer: (A) Brother
Explanation: "The only son of my grandfather" = the woman's father. The "son of my father" = the woman's brother.
Question 2 MCQ • 1 Mark

$A$'s mother is the sister of $B$ and daughter of $C$. $D$ is the daughter of $B$ and sister of $E$. How is $C$ related to $E$?

Correct Answer: (B) Grandfather or Grandmother
Explanation: $D$ and $E$ are children of $B$. $B$ is the child of $C$. Thus $C$ is two generations above $E$ ($Delta G = +2$). Since the gender of $C$ is not stated, $C$ is either grandfather or grandmother.
Question 3 MCQ • 1 Mark

If $P + Q$ means $P$ is the husband of $Q$, $P \div Q$ means $P$ is the sister of $Q$, and $P \times Q$ means $P$ is the son of $Q$, what does $A \div B \times C + D$ mean?

Correct Answer: (A) A is the daughter of D
Explanation: $C + D \implies C$ is husband of $D$ ($C$ is father, $D$ is mother). $B \times C \implies B$ is son of $C$ and $D$. $A \div B \implies A$ is sister of $B$. Therefore, $A$ is the daughter of $D$.
Question 4 Assertion-Reason • 1 Mark

Assertion (A): If $X$ is the sibling of $Y$, it cannot be concluded that $Y$ is the brother of $X$.
Reason (R): The gender of $Y$ is not specified; $Y$ could be either a brother or a sister to $X$.

Correct Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) explains (A).
Explanation: Sibling defines kinship relation at $Delta G = 0$, but without gender specification of $Y$, brother cannot be claimed.

Question 6 2 Marks

If $P \% Q$ means $P$ is father of $Q$, and $P \star Q$ means $P$ is sister of $Q$, show what relationship is expressed by $M \star N \% T$ between $M$ and $T$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Segment Analysis [1 Mark]:
$N \% T \implies N$ is the father of $T$ ($Delta G = +1$, Male).
$M \star N \implies M$ is the sister of $N$ ($Delta G = 0$, Female).

Conclusion [1 Mark]:
$M$ is the sister of $T$'s father. Therefore, $M$ is the Paternal Aunt (Hindi: Bua) of $T$.

Section C: Short Answer Type II (Q7 – Q8) 2 Questions • 3 Marks Each

Question 7 3 Marks

$A, B, C, D, E,$ and $F$ are six members of a family. The number of males equals the number of females.
1. $A$ and $E$ are sons of $F$.
2. $D$ is the mother of two, one boy and one girl.
3. $B$ is the son of $A$.
Draw the family pedigree tree and identify who the mother of $B$ is.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Pedigree Construction [2 Marks]:
- Total 6 members: 3 males and 3 females.
- $A$ and $E$ are sons of $F \implies A^{(+)}, E^{(+)}$.
- $B$ is the son of $A \implies B^{(+)}$.
- We now have 3 males: $A, E, B$. Since there are only 3 males in total, the remaining 3 members ($F, D, C$) MUST ALL BE FEMALES!
- Thus, $F$ is female (Mother of $A$ and $E$).
- $D$ is a female who is the mother of two children (one boy, one girl). Since $A$ is married with a son $B$, $D$ must be the wife of $A$!
- The daughter of $A$ and $D$ must be $C^{(-)}$.

Kinship Conclusion [1 Mark]:
$D$ is the wife of $A$ and the mother of $B$ and $C$.
Answer: The mother of B is D.

Question 8 3 Marks

Explain how coded blood relations problems can be verified using the Generational Balance Equation $\sum \Delta G$. Demonstrate with an example.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Methodological Explanation [1.5 Marks]:
Each kinship operator assigns an integer value representing generational shift:
- Parent / Father / Mother: $\Delta G = +1$
- Sibling / Spouse: $\Delta G = 0$
- Child / Son / Daughter: $\Delta G = -1$
Summing $\Delta G$ across an expression from left to right yields the net generational difference between the first and last person. If a relation like 'Grandfather' is queried, the net sum MUST equal $+2$. Any option yielding $\Delta G \neq +2$ can be eliminated instantly without drawing the tree.

Example Demonstration [1.5 Marks]:
For expression $P \xrightarrow{+1} Q \xrightarrow{0} R \xrightarrow{+1} S$, net $\sum \Delta G = 1 + 0 + 1 = +2$. This immediately verifies $P$ is at the grandparent generational level relative to $S$.

Question 9 & 10 Case Study Case Study • 4 Marks

Case Background: Legal Inheritance Pedigree Analysis
A civil court is determining the legal heirs of an ancestral property. The family tree records indicate:
1. $P$ is the father of $Q$ and $R$.
2. $Q$ is married to $S$, and they have two children: a son $T$ and a daughter $U$.
3. $R$ is married to $V$, and they have an only daughter $W$.
4. $P$ passes away intestate (without a will).

Q9 (2 Marks): Determine the exact relationship of $T$ to $R$, and $W$ to $Q$.
Q10 (2 Marks): How is $S$ related to $V$, and how is $U$ related to $W$?

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Solution to Q9 [2 Marks]:
- $Q$ and $R$ are siblings (children of $P$).
- $T$ is the son of $Q$. Therefore, $T$ is the Nephew of $R$.
- $W$ is the daughter of $R$. Therefore, $W$ is the Niece of $Q$.

Solution to Q10 [2 Marks]:
- $S$ is the wife of $Q$, and $V$ is the spouse of $R$. Since $Q$ and $R$ are siblings, $S$ and $V$ are Co-sisters-in-law (spouses of siblings).
- $U$ is the daughter of $Q$, and $W$ is the daughter of $R$. Since their parents ($Q$ and $R$) are siblings, $U$ and $W$ are first Cousins (Paternal Cousins).
