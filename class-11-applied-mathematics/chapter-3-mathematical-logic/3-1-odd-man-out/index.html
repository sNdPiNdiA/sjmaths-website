Exercise 3.1: Odd Man Out

Identify and isolate anomalies, classification rules, divisibility traits, and letter patterns in numerical and semantic sets.

Core Theoretical Principles

Systematic principles and analytical strategies prescribed by the CBSE curriculum for Odd Man Out.

Numerical Classification & Divisibility:
In numerical sets, examine prime numbers, parity (even/odd), perfect squares/cubes, digit sums, and divisibility rules (especially divisibility by 11: alternating sum difference must be $0$ or a multiple of $11$).
Alphabetical Letter Groups:
Examine forward positional values (EJOTY: $E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25$), reverse opposite pairs (summing to $27$, such as $A-Z, B-Y, C-X$), and vowel/consonant distribution.
Semantic & Real-World Classification:
Analyze taxonomic hierarchies (living organisms vs. inanimate objects), administrative geography (state capitals vs. secondary cities), or tool applications.

Solved Illustrative Examples

Example 1 (Numerical Divisibility)
Find the odd man out from the set: $5720, 6710, 2640, 4270$.
Step 1: Apply divisibility rule for 11:
• $5720: (5+2)-(7+0)=0$ (divisible by 11)
• $6710: (6+1)-(7+0)=0$ (divisible by 11)
• $2640: (2+4)-(6+0)=0$ (divisible by 11)
• $4270: (4+7)-(2+0)=9 \neq 0$ (not divisible by 11)
Answer: 4270 is the odd one out.
Example 2 (Alphabetical Pairs)
Find the odd letter cluster among: $ABYZ, CDWX, EFUV, GHTV$.
Step 1: Examine letter symmetry and opposite sum 27:
• $A(1) + Z(26) = 27, B(2) + Y(25) = 27$
• $C(3) + X(24) = 27, D(4) + W(23) = 27$
• $E(5) + V(22) = 27, F(6) + U(21) = 27$
• In $GHTV$: $G(7)+T(20)=27$, but $H(8)+V(22)=30 \neq 27$ (should be $S$).
Answer: GHTV is the odd cluster.
Example 3 (Semantic & Category Classification)
Find the odd man out: (a) Bird (b) Kite (c) Crow (d) Sparrow.
Step 1: In standard textbook classification, 'Bird' denotes the universal biological class (superordinate genus), whereas Kite, Crow, and Sparrow denote individual specific members.
Answer: (a) Bird is the general category classification.

CYP 3.1.1 CBSE Textbook Question
Find the odd one out: 16-18, 56-63, 96-108, 86-99.
Reduce each ratio to simplest fractional form:
• $16:18 = 8:9$
• $56:63 = 8:9$
• $96:108 = 8:9$
• $86:99 \neq 8:9$
Answer: 86-99 is the odd pair.
CYP 3.1.2 CBSE Textbook Question
Find the odd man out: Bangalore, Nagpur, Bhopal, Ranchi.
Classify by administrative capital status:
• Bangalore: Capital of Karnataka
• Bhopal: Capital of Madhya Pradesh
• Ranchi: Capital of Jharkhand
• Nagpur: Not a state capital
Answer: Nagpur is the odd city.
CYP 3.1.3 CBSE Textbook Question
Find the odd one out: PQXZ, CQBN, ABDF, PRMN.
Analyze vowel and consonant composition:
• PQXZ, CQBN, and PRMN contain only consonants.
• ABDF contains the vowel 'A'.
Answer: ABDF is the odd letter group.

Question 1 1 Mark

Find the odd number: 121, 169, 225, 289.

(a) 121
(b) 169
(c) 225
(d) 289
Correct Answer: (C)
121 ($11^2$), 169 ($13^2$), and 289 ($17^2$) are squares of prime numbers. 225 ($15^2$) is the square of a composite number.
Question 2 1 Mark

Find the odd letter pair: DW, GT, KP, HS, MN, AZ, BY, CX, EV, FU, IR, JQ, LO, PK.

(a) AZ
(b) MN
(c) PK
(d) EV
Correct Answer: (C)
P and K form opposite pair, but standard alphabetical order is KP. All other pairs follow standard alphabet convention.
Question 3 1 Mark

Find the odd word: Copper, Iron, Silver, Brass.

(a) Copper
(b) Iron
(c) Silver
(d) Brass
Correct Answer: (D)
Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc, whereas copper, iron, and silver are pure elemental metals.
