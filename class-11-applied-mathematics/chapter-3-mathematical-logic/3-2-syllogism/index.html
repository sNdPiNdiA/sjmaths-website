Exercise 3.2: Syllogism

Master categorical propositions, Venn diagram models, mediate deductions, immediate conversions, and valid deductive conclusions.

Core Theoretical Principles

Systematic principles and analytical strategies prescribed by the CBSE curriculum for Syllogism.

Standard Proposition Classifications:
Four fundamental forms:
• A (Universal Affirmative): 'All S are P'
• E (Universal Negative): 'No S is P'
• I (Particular Affirmative): 'Some S are P'
• O (Particular Negative): 'Some S are not P'
Immediate Conversion Laws:
• A-type converts by limitation to: 'Some P are S'
• E-type converts exactly to: 'No P is S'
• I-type converts exactly to: 'Some P are S'
• O-type has no valid standard conversion.
Deductive Rules & Possibility Analysis:
• Two negative or two particular premises yield no valid deduction.
• If one premise is negative, the conclusion must be negative.
• Definite conclusion vs. Possibility ('can be'): If an intersection is not forbidden by any premise, the possibility holds.

Solved Illustrative Examples

Example 1 (Universal & Particular Deduction)
Statements: All pencils are sticks. Some sticks are boxes.
Conclusions: I. Some boxes are pencils. II. All pencils are boxes.
Analysis:
• The set of pencils is entirely inside sticks.
• The set of boxes overlaps with sticks, but is not required to touch pencils.
• Conclusion I ('Some boxes are pencils') is possible but not definitely certain.
• Conclusion II ('All pencils are boxes') is definitely false.
Answer: Neither (I) nor (II) follows.
Example 2 (Possibility 'Can Be')
Statements: All doctors are humans. Some humans are teachers.
Conclusions: I. Some doctors can be teachers. II. Some teachers are doctors.
Analysis:
• Doctors are a subset of humans. Humans overlap with teachers.
• Since there is no contradiction or negative constraint between doctors and teachers, the possibility holds: Some doctors can be teachers.
• However, as a definite certainty, conclusion II does not necessarily follow.
Answer: Only conclusion (I) follows.
Example 3 (Negative Proposition)
Statements: No bird is a mammal. All mammals are animals.
Conclusions: I. No bird is an animal. II. No mammal is a bird.
Analysis:
• Statement: 'No bird is a mammal' is an E-type proposition.
• E-type converts directly to: 'No mammal is a bird'. Hence Conclusion II follows.
• Conclusion I does not follow because birds could overlap with animals outside mammals.
Answer: Only conclusion (II) follows.

CYP 3.2.1 CBSE Textbook Question
Statements: Some fruits are sweets. All sweets are tasty.
Conclusions: I. Some fruits are tasty. II. All fruits are tasty.
Fruits overlap sweets (I). Sweets are contained in tasty (A). $\implies$ Intersection of fruits and sweets is inside tasty. Hence, 'Some fruits are tasty' follows definitely. 'All fruits are tasty' does not follow.
Answer: Only conclusion (I) follows.
CYP 3.2.2 CBSE Textbook Question
Statements: All books are papers. Some papers are files.
Conclusions: I. Some files are books. II. Some papers are books.
A-type proposition 'All books are papers' converts by limitation to 'Some papers are books'. Hence II follows. The overlap between papers and files does not necessarily reach books. Hence I does not follow.
Answer: Only conclusion (II) follows.
CYP 3.2.3 CBSE Textbook Question
Statements: No pen is a marker. Some markers are erasers.
Conclusions: I. Some erasers are pens. II. No pen is an eraser.
Erasers overlap markers, but their relation to pens is unconstrained. Either can be true, but neither follows with certainty. Since 'Some erasers are pens' and 'No pen is an eraser' form a complementary pair, either (I) or (II) or neither follows depending on context.
Answer: Neither follows definitely.

Question 1 1 Mark

Statements: All cats are dogs. All dogs are birds. Conclusions: I. All cats are birds. II. Some birds are cats.

(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Either I or II follows
(d) Both I and II follow
Correct Answer: (D)
By transitivity, All cats are birds (I follows). By conversion by limitation, Some birds are cats (II follows).
Question 2 1 Mark

Statements: Some kings are queens. All queens are beautiful. Conclusion: Some kings are beautiful.

(a) True / Follows
(b) False / Does not follow
(c) Ambiguous
(d) Cannot be determined
Correct Answer: (A)
Intersection of kings and queens must be within beautiful, so some kings are beautiful follows.
Question 3 1 Mark

What is the valid conversion of 'No tiger is a herbivore'?

(a) All herbivores are tigers
(b) No herbivore is a tiger
(c) Some herbivores are tigers
(d) Some tigers are herbivores
Correct Answer: (B)
Universal negative (E-type) converts directly to another universal negative with swapped terms.
