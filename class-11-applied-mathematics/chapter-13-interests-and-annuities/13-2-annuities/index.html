Comprehensive Concepts & Principles: Annuities

An annuity is a sequence of equal payments made at equal intervals of time over a designated period. In financial mathematics and CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics, annuities form the foundational framework for analyzing recurring deposits, pensions, leasing contracts, insurance premiums, sinking funds, and loan amortizations (EMIs).

Key Terminologies & Variables:
  • Periodic Payment ($R$ or $C$): The constant amount paid or received at the end or beginning of each period.
  • Payment Interval (Period): The span of time between two successive payments (e.g., monthly, quarterly, semi-annually, annually).
  • Term of an Annuity ($t$): The total duration from the start of the first payment interval to the completion of the final payment interval.
  • Compounding Frequency ($m$): Number of compounding/payment periods per year ($m=1$ for annual, $m=2$ for half-yearly, $m=4$ for quarterly, $m=12$ for monthly).
  • Periodic Interest Rate ($i$): $i = \frac{r}{m} = \frac{R\%}{100 \times m}$, where $r$ is the nominal annual rate.
  • Total Number of Payments ($n$): $n = m \times t$.

Comprehensive Classification of Annuities

Annuities are systematically categorized along three distinct dimensions:

1. By Payment Timing

  • Ordinary Annuity (Annuity Immediate): Payments occur at the END of each payment interval (e.g., home loan EMIs, bond coupon payments, recurring deposits matured at period end).
  • Annuity Due: Payments occur at the BEGINNING of each payment interval (e.g., apartment advance rent, life insurance policy premiums, equipment lease charges).

2. By Term / Duration

  • Annuity Certain: Payments begin and terminate on fixed, specified calendar dates (e.g., 5-year car loan, 10-year recurring deposit).
  • Contingent Annuity: Payments depend on the occurrence of an uncertain future event (e.g., life pension terminating upon death, fire insurance indemnity).
  • Perpetuity: An annuity whose periodic payments continue forever ($n \to \infty$) (e.g., endowment scholarship fund, British consol bonds).

3. By Commencement

  • Immediate Annuity: Periodic payments begin right away at the first interval.
  • Deferred Annuity: Payments begin only after a specified waiting period (deferment period $k$). The duration before payments commence earns compound interest.

Worked Benchmark Examples

Example 1 (Classification of Cash Flows):

Classify each of the following cash flows into its appropriate annuity category:
(a) A tenant pays ₹25,000 rent on the 1st day of every calendar month.
(b) A car buyer pays ₹18,000 at the end of each month for 3 years.
(c) A donor deposits ₹20,00,000 to fund an annual scholarship of ₹1,40,000 forever.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

(a) Annuity Due Certain: Payments occur on the 1st day (beginning of interval) and have fixed duration.
(b) Ordinary Annuity Certain: Payments occur at the end of each interval for a definite duration of 3 years ($n = 36$).
(c) Perpetuity (Perpetual Annuity): Payments continue indefinitely forever funded by the capital interest yield.

Example 2 (Parameter Calculation):

An individual deposits ₹5,000 at the end of every quarter into an account paying 8% per annum compounded quarterly for 4 years. Identify: (i) periodic payment $R$, (ii) compounding frequency $m$, (iii) periodic interest rate $i$, (iv) total number of payments $n$, and (v) type of annuity.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• (i) Periodic payment $R = ₹5,000$
• (ii) Compounding frequency $m = 4$ (quarterly)
• (iii) Periodic interest rate $i = \frac{r}{m} = \frac{0.08}{4} = 0.02$
• (iv) Total number of conversion periods $n = m \times t = 4 \times 4 = 16$
• (v) Since payments are made at the end of each quarter for a predetermined term, this is an Ordinary Annuity Certain.

Problem 1 Conceptual Distinction

State whether each of the following statements is True or False, giving reasons:
(a) In an annuity due, each payment earns interest for one conversion period more than in an ordinary annuity of identical term and rate.
(b) A perpetuity has a finite future value.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

(a) TRUE: In an annuity due, payments are deposited at the start of each period, so every individual payment compounds for exactly one additional period compared to an ordinary annuity where payments occur at the end.
(b) FALSE: Because payments in a perpetuity continue forever ($n \to \infty$), the accumulated future value approaches infinity ($∞$). Only its present value is finite ($PV = R/i$).

Problem 2 Parameter Conversion

An investor agrees to pay ₹12,000 semi-annually for $6\frac{1}{2}$ years at $9\%$ per annum compounded semi-annually. Compute:
(i) The periodic rate of interest $i$.
(ii) The total number of payments $n$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Nominal annual rate $r = 9\% = 0.09$
• Frequency $m = 2$ (semi-annually)
• Term $t = 6.5$ years
(i) Periodic interest rate: $i = \frac{r}{m} = \frac{0.09}{2} = \mathbf{0.045}$ (or $4.5\%$ per half-year).
(ii) Total number of payments: $n = m \times t = 2 \times 6.5 = \mathbf{13}$ payments.

Problem 3 Deferred Annuity Identification

A person purchases an annuity plan where annual payments of ₹40,000 will be paid for 10 years, but the first payment begins 5 years from today.
(a) What type of annuity is this?
(b) Determine the deferment period ($k$).

Show Step-by-Step Solution

(a) Type: Deferred Annuity Certain.
(b) Deferment period: In an ordinary annuity, payment 1 is made at $t=1$. Since the first payment occurs at $t=5$, there are 4 dormant years where no payments occur. Thus, deferment period $k = 4$ years.

Problem 4 Real-World Application

Distinguish clearly between an ordinary annuity and an annuity due using a lease agreement example where rent is ₹30,000 per month payable for 1 year at 12% p.a. compounded monthly. How many total months of interest does the 1st installment earn under both?

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• In an Ordinary Annuity, payment 1 is paid at the end of month 1. It remains in the account for $12 - 1 = \mathbf{11}$ months of compounding interest.
• In an Annuity Due, payment 1 is paid at the start of month 1 ($t=0$). It remains in the account for the entire term of $\mathbf{12}$ months of compounding interest.
• Therefore, every cash flow in an annuity due compounds for 1 full extra period.

Problem 5 Classification Matrix

Match the financial product with its annuity classification:
1. Old Age Monthly Pension payable till demise of beneficiary.
2. 15-Year Bank Recurring Deposit paid at the start of each month.
3. Government Infrastructure Perpetual Bond yielding 7% coupon annually.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

1. Contingent Annuity (Life Annuity): Term depends on the survival of the individual.
2. Annuity Due Certain: Term is strictly fixed (15 years) and payment occurs at the beginning of each month.
3. Perpetuity: No maturity date; principal is never redeemed, interest is paid indefinitely.

SECTION A: 1-Mark Objective & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Define an Annuity Certain with a suitable financial illustration.

Show Answer & Scheme

Definition [1 Mark]: An annuity certain is an annuity whose payments begin and end on specified, fixed calendar dates irrespective of any external contingent event. Example: A 5-year vehicle loan requiring 60 equal monthly payments.

Q2: If ₹8,000 is deposited at the start of each quarter for 5 years at 6% p.a. compounded quarterly, state the periodic rate $i$ and conversion periods $n$.

Show Answer & Scheme

• Periodic rate $i = \frac{0.06}{4} = \mathbf{0.015}$ [0.5 Mark]
• Number of conversion periods $n = 5 \times 4 = \mathbf{20}$ [0.5 Mark].

SECTION B: 2-Mark Short Answer Problems

Q3: Differentiate clearly between an Ordinary Annuity and an Annuity Due on the basis of: (i) Payment timing, and (ii) Compounding duration of the final installment.

Show Answer & Scheme

Comparison [2 Marks]:
(i) Payment Timing: Ordinary annuity payments are paid at the end of each period; Annuity due payments are paid at the beginning of each period.
(ii) Final Installment: In an ordinary annuity, the final payment is deposited at the end of the term, so it earns 0 periods of interest. In an annuity due, the final payment is deposited at the start of the last period, earning 1 period of interest.

Q4: What is a Deferred Annuity? If an ordinary annuity of 12 semi-annual payments begins 3 years from today, determine the deferment period $k$ in terms of half-years.

Show Answer & Scheme

Definition [1 Mark]: A deferred annuity is an annuity in which payments do not start immediately, but after the lapse of a designated interval called the deferment period.
Calculation [1 Mark]: The first payment occurs at $t = 3$ years $= 6$ half-years. Since in an immediate semi-annual annuity the first payment occurs at half-year 1, the number of dormant half-years is $k = 6 - 1 = \mathbf{5}$ periods.

SECTION C: 4-Mark Comprehensive & Real-World Case Applications

Q5: An engineering firm has to choose between two leasing arrangements for a computational server costing ₹10,00,000:
• Plan A: Lease payments of ₹35,000 paid at the end of every month for 36 months.
• Plan B: Lease payments of ₹35,000 paid at the start of every month for 36 months.
(i) Categorize Plan A and Plan B using formal annuity definitions.
(ii) From the perspective of the server lessor (the party receiving rent), explain mathematically which plan yields higher total compound value at the end of 36 months, and why.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Part (i) Classification [2 Marks]:
• Plan A is an Ordinary Annuity Certain because payments occur at the end of each month for a definite term of 36 months.
• Plan B is an Annuity Due Certain because payments occur at the beginning of each month for a definite term of 36 months.

Part (ii) Mathematical Comparison [2 Marks]:
• Under Plan B (Annuity Due), each payment is deposited exactly 1 month earlier than under Plan A.
• Therefore, every cash flow compounds for one additional month at monthly periodic rate $i$.
• Mathematically: $FV_{\text{due}} = FV_{\text{ordinary}} \times (1 + i)$.
• Since $(1+i) > 1$ for any positive interest rate, Plan B yields higher total accumulated value for the lessor.

Top 3 Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Pitfall 1 (Ordinary vs Due Confusion): In an ordinary annuity of $n$ periods, the last payment earns zero interest because it is paid at maturity. In an annuity due, the last payment earns interest for one period.
  • Pitfall 2 (Periodic Rate $i$): Never substitute the annual rate $r$ directly when compounding is non-annual. Always compute $i = r/m$ (e.g., $12\%$ compounded monthly is $i = 0.12/12 = 0.01$).
  • Pitfall 3 (Future Value of Perpetuity): Never attempt to calculate the future value of a perpetuity. A perpetuity continues infinitely, so its future value is mathematically undefined (infinite). Only its present value ($PV = R/i$) exists.

PART 1: Objective Section (4 Marks)

Q1: An annuity in which payments are made at the beginning of each period is termed as:

Solution: (C) By definition, an annuity due has cash flows at the beginning of each payment interval.

Q2: If ₹10,000 is deposited at the end of every half-year for 4 years at 8% per annum compounded half-yearly, what is the value of periodic rate $i$ and number of payments $n$?

Solution: (B) $m = 2 \implies i = 0.08/2 = 0.04$; $n = 4 \times 2 = 8$.

Q3: Which of the following is an example of a Perpetuity?

Solution: (B) An endowment fund paying scholarships forever continues indefinitely, which defines a perpetuity.

Q4 (Assertion-Reason):
Assertion (A): The future value of an annuity due is always greater than the future value of an ordinary annuity with identical payments, rate, and term.
Reason (R): In an annuity due, every payment is deposited one period earlier and thus earns interest for one additional compounding period.

Solution: (A) Since $FV_{\text{due}} = FV_{\text{ordinary}} \times (1 + i)$, where $(1 + i) > 1$, Assertion is true and Reason correctly explains it.

PART 2: Subjective Section (Q5 - Q10)

Q5 2 Marks

Define 'Contingent Annuity' and give two real-life examples where payments are contingent rather than certain.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Definition [1 Mark]: A contingent annuity is an annuity whose starting date, terminal date, or total number of payments depends upon the occurrence or non-occurrence of an uncertain future event.
• Examples [1 Mark]: (1) Life pension paid monthly until the death of the pensioner; (2) Disability insurance monthly stipend paid only for the duration that the worker remains medically incapacitated.

Q6 2 Marks

A company establishes a fund by depositing ₹20,000 at the end of each month. If the nominal annual interest rate is 6% compounded monthly:
(a) Find the periodic rate of interest $i$.
(b) State the number of payments $n$ over a 5-year duration.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• (a) Periodic rate $i = \frac{0.06}{12} = \mathbf{0.005}$ (or $0.5\%$ per month) [1 Mark].
• (b) Total payments $n = 5 \times 12 = \mathbf{60}$ payments [1 Mark].

Q7 3 Marks

Explain why the final payment of an ordinary annuity does not accumulate any compound interest, whereas the final payment of an annuity due does accumulate compound interest.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Ordinary Annuity Analysis [1.5 Marks]: In an ordinary annuity, payments occur at the end of each conversion period. The $n^{\text{th}}$ (final) payment is deposited exactly at the terminal date of the annuity (maturity). Since no time elapses between this deposit and valuation, compounding duration is 0 periods, accumulating ₹0 interest.
• Annuity Due Analysis [1.5 Marks]: In an annuity due, payments occur at the beginning of each period. The $n^{\text{th}}$ payment is deposited at the start of the final $n^{\text{th}}$ period. It remains invested for that full period until maturity, thus earning interest for 1 compounding period.

Q8 3 Marks

Define a Deferred Annuity. If payments of ₹15,000 per quarter are to be received for 4 years, with the first payment deferred until the end of the 2nd year (i.e. at $t=2$ years):
(a) What is the total number of payments $n$?
(b) How many payment intervals constitute the deferment period $k$?

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• (a) Payments occur quarterly for 4 years: $n = 4 \times 4 = \mathbf{16}$ payments [1.5 Marks].
• (b) $t=2$ years corresponds to quarter 8 ($2 \times 4$). In an immediate ordinary annuity, payment 1 is due at quarter 1. Hence, there are $8 - 1 = \mathbf{7}$ dormant quarters. Therefore, deferment period $k = 7$ quarters [1.5 Marks].

Q9 & Q10 Case Study (4 Marks Total)

Case Context: Commercial Property Lease Agreement

A retail chain signs a 3-year commercial property lease requiring ₹50,000 per quarter. The prevailing cost of capital is 8% per annum compounded quarterly ($i = 0.02$). The landlord requests lease payments to be made in advance (Annuity Due), whereas the tenant proposes paying at the end of each quarter (Ordinary Annuity).

Q9 (2 Marks): Calculate the compounding multiplier difference factor between the Future Value of the landlord's Annuity Due vs the tenant's Ordinary Annuity.
Q10 (2 Marks): If the landlord agrees to the Ordinary Annuity proposal, what percentage compensation surcharge on quarterly rent must be applied so that the landlord achieves identical accumulated future value?

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Solution to Q9 [2 Marks]:
• The relationship between Annuity Due and Ordinary Annuity is: $$FV_{\text{due}} = FV_{\text{ordinary}} \times (1 + i)$$ • Here $i = \frac{0.08}{4} = 0.02$.
• Thus, the multiplier difference factor is $(1 + 0.02) = \mathbf{1.02}$ (a $2\%$ increase).

Solution to Q10 [2 Marks]:
• Let adjusted quarterly rent be $R'$. We need: $$FV_{\text{ordinary}}(R') = FV_{\text{due}}(R) = R \cdot s_{\overline{n}|i} \cdot (1 + i)$$ $$R' \cdot s_{\overline{n}|i} = R \cdot s_{\overline{n}|i} \cdot (1.02) \implies R' = 1.02 R$$ • The required rent is $50,000 \times 1.02 = \mathbf{₹51,000}$.
• Surcharge percentage is $\mathbf{2\%}$ (or ₹1,000 extra per quarter).
