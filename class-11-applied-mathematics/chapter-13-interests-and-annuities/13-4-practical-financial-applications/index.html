Comprehensive Concepts & Principles: Sinking Funds & Loan Amortization (EMIs)

In modern corporate finance and retail banking, annuities are directly operationalized through two dominant financial mechanisms: Sinking Funds (accumulating capital to satisfy a future liability) and Amortization Schedules / EMIs (liquidating an existing debt through periodic installments). These practical applications form a cornerstone of CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics.

Key Formulas & Frameworks:
  • Sinking Fund Periodic Deposit ($R$): To accumulate a target future sum $A$ over $n$ conversion periods at periodic interest rate $i$:
    $$R = \frac{A \cdot i}{(1 + i)^n - 1} = \frac{A}{s_{\overline{n}|i}}$$ Total interest accumulated $= A - n \cdot R$.
  • Loan Amortization (Reducing Balance EMI Method): The loan amount $P$ is the Present Value of an ordinary annuity of $n$ payments:
    $$\text{EMI} = P \left[ \frac{i}{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}} \right] = P \left[ \frac{i(1 + i)^n}{(1 + i)^n - 1} \right] = \frac{P}{a_{\overline{n}|i}}$$
  • Flat Rate EMI Method (Non-reducing):
    $$I = \frac{P \cdot r \cdot t}{100}, \quad \text{EMI}_{\text{flat}} = \frac{P + I}{n}$$ Note: The flat rate method ignores the declining principal balance, resulting in a significantly higher effective APR.
  • Structure of an Amortization Schedule (Period $k$):
    • Interest Component: $I_k = B_{k-1} \cdot i$
    • Principal Repayment Component: $P_k = \text{EMI} - I_k$
    • Outstanding Principal Balance: $B_k = B_{k-1} - P_k$

Anatomy of a Loan Amortization Schedule

While the monthly EMI remains constant, the composition of each payment shifts dynamically over the term of the loan:

• Early Payments: High Outstanding Balance $\implies$ High Interest Component ($I_k$), Low Principal Repayment ($P_k$).
• Later Payments: Diminishing Outstanding Balance $\implies$ Low Interest Component ($I_k$), High Principal Repayment ($P_k$).
• Final Period: $B_n = 0$ (Debt is completely extinguished).

Worked Benchmark Examples

Example 1 (Sinking Fund for Machine Replacement):

A manufacturing company purchases a CNC machine costing ₹10,00,000. Its estimated useful life is 5 years, after which its scrap value is estimated to be ₹1,00,000. To replace the machine at the end of 5 years, the company creates a sinking fund earning 8% per annum compounded annually. Find the annual deposit required. [Given: $(1.08)^5 = 1.4693$]

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Net capital required to replace the machine: $$A = \text{Cost} - \text{Scrap Value} = 10,00,000 - 1,00,000 = ₹9,00,000$$ • $i = 0.08, n = 5$. $$R = \frac{A \cdot i}{(1 + i)^n - 1} = \frac{9,00,000 \times 0.08}{(1.08)^5 - 1}$$ $$R = \frac{72,000}{1.4693 - 1} = \frac{72,000}{0.4693} = \mathbf{₹1,53,420}$$
Answer: The company must deposit ₹1,53,420 at the end of each year.

Example 2 (Reducing Balance EMI Calculation):

A loan of ₹5,00,000 is to be repaid in 36 equal monthly installments. If interest is charged at 12% per annum compounded monthly, calculate the Equated Monthly Installment (EMI). [Given: $(1.01)^{-36} = 0.6989$]

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Principal $P = ₹5,00,000$
• Monthly rate $i = 0.12/12 = 0.01$
• Number of months $n = 36$
$$\text{EMI} = \frac{P \cdot i}{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}} = \frac{5,00,000 \times 0.01}{1 - (1.01)^{-36}}$$ $$\text{EMI} = \frac{5,000}{1 - 0.6989} = \frac{5,000}{0.3011} = \mathbf{₹16,606}$$
Answer: The monthly EMI is ₹16,606.

Example 3 (Flat Rate vs Reducing Balance):

Compare the monthly EMI of a personal loan of ₹1,20,000 for 1 year at 12% p.a. calculated under: (i) Flat Rate Method, and (ii) Reducing Balance Method ($a_{\overline{12}|0.01} = 11.255$).

Show Step-by-Step Solution

(i) Flat Rate Method:
Total Interest $I = \frac{1,20,000 \times 12 \times 1}{100} = ₹14,400$.
Total Repayable = $1,20,000 + 14,400 = ₹1,34,400$.
$$\text{EMI}_{\text{flat}} = \frac{1,34,400}{12} = \mathbf{₹11,200}$$

(ii) Reducing Balance Method:
$$\text{EMI}_{\text{reducing}} = \frac{P}{a_{\overline{12}|0.01}} = \frac{1,20,000}{11.255} = \mathbf{₹10,662}$$
Observation: The reducing balance EMI is ₹538 lower per month because interest is only charged on the declining outstanding balance!

Problem 1 Sinking Fund Sizing

A local municipality issues ₹20,00,000 worth of debentures repayable at par after 10 years. A sinking fund is created to redeem these debentures, yielding 9% per annum compounded annually. Find the equal annual contribution required. [Use: $(1.09)^{10} = 2.3674$]

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $A = ₹20,00,000$, $i = 0.09$, $n = 10$. $$R = \frac{A \cdot i}{(1 + i)^n - 1} = \frac{20,00,000 \times 0.09}{(1.09)^{10} - 1}$$ $$R = \frac{1,80,000}{2.3674 - 1} = \frac{1,80,000}{1.3674} = \mathbf{₹1,31,637}$$
Answer: The municipality must contribute ₹1,31,637 annually.

Problem 2 Car Loan EMI Drill

A consumer avails a car loan of ₹4,00,000 repayable in 24 monthly installments at 12% per annum compounded monthly. Calculate the EMI. [Use: $(1.01)^{-24} = 0.7876$]

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $P = ₹4,00,000$, $i = 0.12/12 = 0.01$, $n = 24$. $$\text{EMI} = \frac{P \cdot i}{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}} = \frac{4,00,000 \times 0.01}{1 - 0.7876}$$ $$\text{EMI} = \frac{4,000}{0.2124} = \mathbf{₹18,832.39}$$
Answer: The monthly EMI is ₹18,832.39.

Problem 3 First Period Interest Breakdown

For the car loan in Problem 2 (Loan = ₹4,00,000, Monthly rate = 1%, EMI = ₹18,832), calculate:
(a) The interest component in the 1st installment.
(b) The principal repayment component in the 1st installment.
(c) The outstanding principal balance immediately after the 1st installment.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• (a) Interest Component ($I_1$): $B_0 \times i = 4,00,000 \times 0.01 = \mathbf{₹4,000}$.
• (b) Principal Component ($P_1$): $\text{EMI} - I_1 = 18,832 - 4,000 = \mathbf{₹14,832}$.
• (c) Outstanding Balance ($B_1$): $B_0 - P_1 = 4,00,000 - 14,832 = \mathbf{₹3,85,168}$.

Problem 4 Sinking Fund Interest Yield

An educational trust deposits ₹50,000 at the end of each year for 6 years into a sinking fund yielding 10% p.a. compounded annually. Find the accumulated fund value and the total interest generated. [Given: $(1.10)^6 = 1.7716$]

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $R = ₹50,000$, $i = 0.10$, $n = 6$.
$$A = R \left[ \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i} \right] = 50,000 \left[ \frac{1.7716 - 1}{0.10} \right] = 50,000 \times 7.716 = \mathbf{₹3,85,800}$$ • Total Principal Deposited = $50,000 \times 6 = ₹3,00,000$.
• Total Interest Earned = $3,85,800 - 3,00,000 = \mathbf{₹85,800}$.

Problem 5 Second Period Schedule Calculation

Continuing from Problem 3, calculate the interest component and principal component of the 2nd monthly installment ($B_1 = ₹3,85,168$, EMI = ₹18,832, $i = 0.01$).

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Interest Component ($I_2$): $B_1 \times i = 3,85,168 \times 0.01 = \mathbf{₹3,851.68}$.
• Principal Component ($P_2$): $\text{EMI} - I_2 = 18,832 - 3,851.68 = \mathbf{₹14,980.32}$.
• Notice: Interest dropped by ₹148.32, while principal repayment increased by ₹148.32!

SECTION B: 2-Mark Short Answer Problems

Q3: A borrower takes a loan of ₹2,00,000 for 2 years at 10% p.a. flat rate. Calculate: (i) total interest payable, and (ii) the flat monthly EMI.

Show Answer & Scheme

• (i) Total Flat Interest: $$I = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100} = \frac{2,00,000 \times 10 \times 2}{100} = \mathbf{₹40,000} \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$ • (ii) Total Amount $= 2,00,000 + 40,000 = ₹2,40,000$. $$\text{EMI}_{\text{flat}} = \frac{2,40,000}{24} = \mathbf{₹10,000} \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$

Q4: In an amortization schedule, how do the interest component and principal component of consecutive EMIs change over time? Explain mathematically.

Show Answer & Scheme

Explanation [2 Marks]:
• Interest in period $k$ is $I_k = B_{k-1} \times i$. Since outstanding balance $B_{k-1}$ decreases with each installment, the interest component strictly decreases.
• Since $\text{EMI} = I_k + P_k$ is constant, as $I_k$ decreases, the principal component $P_k$ strictly increases over successive installments.

Q5: A solar power enterprise borrows ₹15,00,000 to install rooftop photovoltaic panels. The loan is to be repaid in 6 equal semi-annual installments under the reducing balance method. The interest rate is 10% per annum compounded semi-annually ($i = 0.05$). [Given: $(1.05)^{-6} = 0.7462$]
(i) Calculate the semi-annual installment payment ($R$).
(ii) Construct a complete 2-period loan amortization schedule showing Opening Balance, Semi-Annual Installment, Interest, Principal Repaid, and Closing Balance for Period 1 and Period 2.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Part (i) Installment Calculation [2 Marks]:
• $P = ₹15,00,000, i = 0.05, n = 6$. $$R = \frac{P \cdot i}{1 - (1+i)^{-6}} = \frac{15,00,000 \times 0.05}{1 - 0.7462} = \frac{75,000}{0.2538} = \mathbf{₹2,95,508}$$

Part (ii) Amortization Schedule (2 Periods) [2 Marks]:
• Period 1:
- Opening Balance $B_0 = ₹15,00,000$
- Installment $R = ₹2,95,508$
- Interest $I_1 = 15,00,000 \times 0.05 = ₹75,000$
- Principal Repayment $P_1 = 2,95,508 - 75,000 = ₹2,20,508$
- Closing Balance $B_1 = 15,00,000 - 2,20,508 = ₹12,79,492$
• Period 2:
- Opening Balance $B_1 = ₹12,79,492$
- Installment $R = ₹2,95,508$
- Interest $I_2 = 12,79,492 \times 0.05 = ₹63,975$
- Principal Repayment $P_2 = 2,95,508 - 63,975 = ₹2,31,533$
- Closing Balance $B_2 = 12,79,492 - 2,31,533 = ₹10,47,959$

Comparative Overview: Sinking Fund vs Amortization

Feature Sinking Fund Loan Amortization (EMI)
Purpose Accumulate a future capital sum Liquidate an existing debt today
Governing Metric Future Value of Ordinary Annuity ($FVA$) Present Value of Ordinary Annuity ($PVA$)
Installment Formula $R = \frac{A \cdot i}{(1+i)^n - 1}$ $\text{EMI} = \frac{P \cdot i}{1 - (1+i)^{-n}}$
Cash Flow Direction Periodic deposits into savings asset Periodic repayments to lender liability

Reducing Balance vs Flat Rate Pitfall

  • Flat Rate Deception: A loan advertised at "8% flat rate" is actually charging an effective reducing rate of almost 14.5% to 15% because the borrower continues paying interest on the full original principal even after paying off half the loan!
  • Always verify whether the question specifies the Reducing Balance Method or Flat Rate Method before selecting your formula.

PART 1: Objective Section (4 Marks)

Q1: In an amortization schedule, as repayment progresses over successive periods:

Solution: (C) Because the outstanding principal balance decreases, the interest component ($I_k = B_{k-1} \cdot i$) decreases, forcing the principal component ($P_k = \text{EMI} - I_k$) to increase.

Q2: A company needs ₹10,00,000 at the end of 5 years. If it deposits ₹$R$ annually into a sinking fund at 10% p.a., the formula to find $R$ is:

Solution: (B) For a sinking fund accumulating future sum $A$: $R = \frac{A}{s_{\overline{n}|i}}$.

Q3: A loan of ₹1,00,000 is to be repaid in 10 equal monthly installments of ₹10,500 under the flat rate method. What is the total interest charged?

Solution: (A) Total paid = $10,500 \times 10 = ₹1,05,000$. Interest = $1,05,000 - 1,00,000 = ₹5,000$.

Q4 (Assertion-Reason):
Assertion (A): The EMI under the reducing balance method is strictly lower than the EMI under the flat rate method for identical loan amount, nominal rate, and term.
Reason (R): Under the reducing balance method, interest is computed only on the unpaid principal balance, whereas under the flat rate method, interest continues to be charged on the entire initial principal throughout the term.

Solution: (A) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason provides the precise mathematical cause of the lower EMI.

Q7 3 Marks

A business firm issues debentures worth ₹50,00,000 redeemable after 8 years. It deposits ₹4,00,000 at the end of each year into a sinking fund at 9% p.a. compounded annually. Find whether the accumulated fund will be sufficient to redeem the debentures, and by how much surplus or deficit. [Given: $(1.09)^8 = 1.9926$]

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Accumulated Sinking Fund Value: $$A = R \left[ \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i} \right] = 4,00,000 \left[ \frac{1.9926 - 1}{0.09} \right] \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$ $$A = 4,00,000 \times \frac{0.9926}{0.09} = 4,00,000 \times 11.0289 = \mathbf{₹44,11,560} \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$ • Target Liability = ₹50,00,000.
• Conclusion: The fund is insufficient by a deficit of $50,00,000 - 44,11,560 = \mathbf{₹5,88,440}$. [1 Mark]

Q8 3 Marks

Prove that in an amortization schedule with periodic interest rate $i$, the principal repayments $P_1, P_2, P_3, \dots, P_n$ form a geometric progression with common ratio $(1 + i)$.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• In period $k$: $\text{EMI} = I_k + P_k = B_{k-1} \cdot i + P_k$.
• In period $k+1$: $\text{EMI} = I_{k+1} + P_{k+1} = B_k \cdot i + P_{k+1}$.
• Since $\text{EMI}$ is constant: $$B_{k-1} \cdot i + P_k = B_k \cdot i + P_{k+1} \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$ • Since $B_k = B_{k-1} - P_k$: $$B_{k-1} \cdot i + P_k = (B_{k-1} - P_k)i + P_{k+1}$$ $$B_{k-1} \cdot i + P_k = B_{k-1} \cdot i - P_k \cdot i + P_{k+1}$$ $$P_k + P_k \cdot i = P_{k+1} \implies P_{k+1} = P_k(1 + i) \quad [1.5 \text{ Marks}]$$ • Hence, the ratio $\frac{P_{k+1}}{P_k} = (1 + i)$ is constant, proving that the principal repayments form a Geometric Progression. [0.5 Mark]

Q9 & Q10 Case Study (4 Marks Total)

Case Context: Home Buyer Housing Loan Decision

An engineer takes a home loan of ₹24,00,000 for 10 years (120 months) from a commercial bank at 9% p.a. compounded monthly ($i = 0.09/12 = 0.0075$). [Given: $(1.0075)^{-120} = 0.4079$, $(1.0075)^{-119} = 0.41095$]

Q9 (2 Marks): Calculate the monthly EMI of the home loan.
Q10 (2 Marks): Calculate the total amount paid by the borrower over the 10-year term and determine the total interest cost incurred.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Solution to Q9 [2 Marks]:
• $P = ₹24,00,000, i = 0.0075, n = 120$. $$\text{EMI} = \frac{24,00,000 \times 0.0075}{1 - (1.0075)^{-120}} = \frac{18,000}{1 - 0.4079} = \frac{18,000}{0.5921} = \mathbf{₹30,400.27}$$
• The required monthly EMI is ₹30,400.

Solution to Q10 [2 Marks]:
• Total Payment over 120 months $= 30,400.27 \times 120 = \mathbf{₹36,48,032.40}$.
• Total Interest Cost $= 36,48,032.40 - 24,00,000 = \mathbf{₹12,48,032.40}$.
• Total interest paid is approximately ₹12.48 Lakhs over the 10-year term.
