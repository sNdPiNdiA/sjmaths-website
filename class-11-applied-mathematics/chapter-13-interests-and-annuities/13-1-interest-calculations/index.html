Comprehensive Concepts & Principles: Interest Calculations

Interest is the compensation paid by a borrower to a lender for the use of borrowed capital over a specified duration. In CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics, interest modeling bridges simple linear growth with exponential compounding, nominal annual percentage rates (APR), continuous compounding, and effective annual rates (EAR).

Key Interest Formulations:
  • Simple Interest: Interest calculated strictly on the initial principal $P$:
    $$SI = \frac{P \cdot R \cdot T}{100}, \quad A = P + SI = P\left(1 + \frac{RT}{100}\right)$$
  • Compound Interest (Compounded $m$ Times per Year): Interest earned is added back to principal at the end of each conversion period:
    $$A = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{m}\right)^{m \cdot t} = P(1 + i)^n$$ where $r = R/100$ is nominal annual rate, $m$ is compounding frequency per year, $i = r/m$ is periodic rate, and $n = m \cdot t$ is total conversion periods.
  • Compounding Frequencies ($m$):
    • Annual: $m = 1, i = r, n = t$
    • Semi-annual (Half-yearly): $m = 2, i = r/2, n = 2t$
    • Quarterly: $m = 4, i = r/4, n = 4t$
    • Monthly: $m = 12, i = r/12, n = 12t$
  • Continuous Compounding: As compounding frequency approaches infinity ($m \to \infty$):
    $$A = P \cdot e^{rt}$$
  • Effective Rate of Interest ($r_{\text{eff}}$): The actual annual rate of return earned when compounding takes place more frequently than once a year:
    $$r_{\text{eff}} = \left(1 + \frac{r}{m}\right)^m - 1$$

Mathematical Framework & Compounding Growth

Higher compounding frequency increases the total accumulated amount because interest is reinvested sooner:

• Compound Interest: $CI = A - P = P\left[(1 + i)^n - 1\right]$
• Effective Annual Rate: $EAR = \left(1 + \frac{r}{m}\right)^m - 1$
• Continuous Effective Rate: $EAR_{\text{cont}} = e^r - 1$
• Rule of 72 (Doubling Time): $t \approx \frac{72}{R}$ years

Worked Benchmark Examples

Example 1 (Compounding Frequency Comparison):

Find the compound interest on ₹1,00,000 for 2 years at 12% per annum compounded: (i) semi-annually, and (ii) quarterly.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

(i) Compounded Semi-Annually ($m = 2$):
$P = ₹1,00,000, r = 0.12, m = 2, t = 2 \implies i = 0.12/2 = 0.06, n = 2 \times 2 = 4$.
$A = 1,00,000(1 + 0.06)^4 = 1,00,000 \times (1.06)^4 = 1,00,000 \times 1.262477 = ₹1,26,248$.
$CI = 1,26,248 - 1,00,000 = \mathbf{₹26,248}$.

(ii) Compounded Quarterly ($m = 4$):
$i = 0.12/4 = 0.03, n = 4 \times 2 = 8$.
$A = 1,00,000(1 + 0.03)^8 = 1,00,000 \times (1.03)^8 = 1,00,000 \times 1.26677 = ₹1,26,677$.
$CI = 1,26,677 - 1,00,000 = \mathbf{₹26,677}$.
Quarterly compounding yields ₹429 more in interest than semi-annual compounding.

Example 2 (Effective Rate of Interest):

A finance company quotes an interest rate of 10% per annum compounded quarterly. Calculate the effective annual rate of interest.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Nominal rate $r = 10\% = 0.10$, compounding frequency $m = 4$.
Periodic rate $i = 0.10 / 4 = 0.025$.
$r_{\text{eff}} = (1 + i)^m - 1 = (1 + 0.025)^4 - 1$
$(1.025)^4 = 1.103813$
$r_{\text{eff}} = 1.103813 - 1 = 0.103813 = \mathbf{10.38\%}$.
Answer: The effective annual rate is 10.38%.

Problem 1 Quarterly Compounding Drill

Calculate the amount and compound interest on ₹50,000 for $1.5$ years at $8\%$ per annum compounded quarterly.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Parameters:
$P = ₹50,000, R = 8\%, t = 1.5 \text{ years}, m = 4$.
Periodic rate $i = \frac{8\%}{4} = 2\% = 0.02$.
Total periods $n = 4 \times 1.5 = 6$.

Amount:
$A = 50,000 \times (1 + 0.02)^6 = 50,000 \times (1.02)^6$.
$(1.02)^6 = 1.126162$.
$A = 50,000 \times 1.126162 = \mathbf{₹56,308.10}$.

Compound Interest:
$CI = 56,308.10 - 50,000 = \mathbf{₹6,308.10}$.

Problem 2 Effective Rate Comparison

Bank A offers 9% per annum compounded monthly, while Bank B offers 9.2% per annum compounded half-yearly. By calculating the effective rate of interest for both banks, determine which bank provides the better return to a depositor.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Bank A (9% compounded monthly, $m=12$):
$i = 0.09 / 12 = 0.0075$.
$r_{\text{eff}} = (1 + 0.0075)^{12} - 1 = (1.0075)^{12} - 1 = 1.093807 - 1 = \mathbf{9.38\%}$.

Bank B (9.2% compounded half-yearly, $m=2$):
$i = 0.092 / 2 = 0.046$.
$r_{\text{eff}} = (1 + 0.046)^2 - 1 = (1.046)^2 - 1 = 1.094116 - 1 = \mathbf{9.41\%}$.

Conclusion: Bank B offers a slightly higher effective return ($9.41\% > 9.38\%$), making it the superior investment for the depositor.

Problem 3 Finding Investment Doubling Time

In how many years will an investment of ₹25,000 double itself if interest is compounded annually at 7% p.a.? (Use $\log 2 = 0.3010, \log 1.07 = 0.0294$). Also verify using the Rule of 72.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Exact Logarithmic Calculation:
$A = 2P \implies P(1 + 0.07)^t = 2P \implies (1.07)^t = 2$.
Taking log on both sides:
$t \cdot \log(1.07) = \log 2$
$t = \frac{\log 2}{\log 1.07} = \frac{0.3010}{0.0294} = \mathbf{10.24 \text{ years}}$ (approximately 10 years and 3 months).

Rule of 72 Approximation:
$t \approx \frac{72}{R} = \frac{72}{7} = \mathbf{10.28 \text{ years}}$.
The Rule of 72 closely approximates the exact logarithmic answer within 0.04 years!

Problem 4 Continuous Compounding Formulation

An investor deposits ₹80,000 in an index growth certificate yielding 6% per annum compounded continuously. Determine the accumulated amount after 3 years. (Given $e^{0.18} = 1.1972$).

Show Step-by-Step Solution

Continuous Compounding Formula:
$A = P \cdot e^{rt}$
$P = ₹80,000, r = 0.06, t = 3 \implies rt = 0.06 \times 3 = 0.18$.
$A = 80,000 \times e^{0.18} = 80,000 \times 1.1972 = \mathbf{₹95,776}$.
$\text{Compound Interest} = 95,776 - 80,000 = \mathbf{₹15,776}$.

Q1. Nominal vs Effective Rate Definition 1 Mark

State why the effective annual rate of interest ($r_{\text{eff}}$) is always greater than or equal to the nominal rate of interest ($r$).

Show Model Solution

When compounding occurs more than once a year ($m > 1$), interest earned in earlier conversion periods itself earns interest in subsequent periods (compounding effect), resulting in an effective rate $r_{\text{eff}} = (1 + r/m)^m - 1 > r$. They are equal only when $m = 1$.

Q2. Continuous Compounding Formula 1 Mark

Write the mathematical formula for the accumulated amount $A$ when principal $P$ is compounded continuously at an annual rate $r$ for $t$ years.

Show Model Solution

$$A = P \cdot e^{rt}$$ where $e \approx 2.71828$ is Euler's mathematical constant.

Q3. Frequency of Compounding Impact 1 Mark

Arrange the following compounding frequencies in increasing order of compound interest yielded on a given principal: Quarterly, Annually, Monthly, Semi-annually.

Show Model Solution

$$\text{Annually } (m=1) < \text{Semi-annually } (m=2) < \text{Quarterly } (m=4) < \text{Monthly } (m=12)$$

Section B: Intermediate Applied Calculations 2 Questions • 2 Marks Each

Q4. Difference Between CI and SI 2 Marks

The difference between compound interest (compounded annually) and simple interest on a certain sum of money for 2 years at 8% per annum is ₹320. Find the principal sum.

Show Model Solution

Standard Formula for 2-Year Difference:
$$CI - SI = P \cdot \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$$ $320 = P \cdot \left(\frac{8}{100}\right)^2 = P \cdot \frac{64}{10,000}$
$P = \frac{320 \times 10,000}{64} = 5 \times 10,000 = \mathbf{₹50,000}$.
Answer: The principal sum is ₹50,000.

Q5. Effective Rate of Monthly Compounding 2 Marks

Find the effective annual rate of interest corresponding to a nominal rate of 12% per annum compounded monthly. (Given $(1.01)^{12} = 1.1268$).

Show Model Solution

Nominal rate $r = 12\% = 0.12, m = 12 \implies i = 0.12/12 = 0.01$.
$r_{\text{eff}} = (1 + i)^{12} - 1 = (1 + 0.01)^{12} - 1$
$r_{\text{eff}} = 1.1268 - 1 = 0.1268 = \mathbf{12.68\%}$.
Answer: The effective rate is 12.68%.

Section C: Comprehensive Board-Level Problem 1 Question • 4 Marks

Q6. Corporate Treasury Investment Optimization 4 Marks

A corporate treasury has an investible surplus of ₹10,00,000 for a 3-year horizon and evaluates three institutional fixed deposit schemes:
• Scheme A: 8.5% p.a. compounded annually.
• Scheme B: 8.25% p.a. compounded quarterly.
• Scheme C: 8.0% p.a. compounded monthly.
(Given: $(1.085)^3 = 1.2773$, $(1.020625)^{12} = 1.2785$, $(1.006667)^{36} = 1.2702$).
Calculate the maturity value under each scheme, and determine which scheme maximizes the treasury's yield.

Show Model Solution

Maturity Value for Scheme A [1 Mark]:
$A_A = 10,00,000 \times (1.085)^3 = 10,00,000 \times 1.2773 = \mathbf{₹12,77,300}$.

Maturity Value for Scheme B [1.5 Marks]:
$i = 0.0825 / 4 = 0.020625, n = 4 \times 3 = 12$.
$A_B = 10,00,000 \times (1 + 0.020625)^{12} = 10,00,000 \times 1.2785 = \mathbf{₹12,78,500}$.

Maturity Value for Scheme C [1 Mark]:
$i = 0.08 / 12 = 0.006667, n = 12 \times 3 = 36$.
$A_C = 10,00,000 \times (1 + 0.006667)^{36} = 10,00,000 \times 1.2702 = \mathbf{₹12,70,200}$.

Conclusion [0.5 Mark]:
Scheme B provides the highest maturity return of ₹12,78,500, surpassing Scheme A by ₹1,200 and Scheme C by ₹8,300.

Essential Exam Traps

  • The Annual vs Periodic Rate Error: If the rate is 10% compounded semi-annually, NEVER plug in $0.10$ into $(1 + i)^n$. You MUST divide by 2: $i = 0.05$, and double the time: $n = 2t$.
  • Effective Rate Confusion: $r_{\text{eff}}$ depends solely on nominal rate $r$ and frequency $m$. It is completely independent of the principal sum $P$ and time $t$.
  • Difference Formula Limitation: The formula $CI - SI = P(R/100)^2$ is strictly valid ONLY for $T = 2 \text{ years}$ under annual compounding. For 3 years, the formula is $P(R/100)^2 \cdot (3 + R/100)$.

Case Background: High-Yield Bank Fixed Deposit Decision
A non-banking financial company (NBFC) offers two retail deposit products for a deposit of ₹2,00,000 for 2 years:
