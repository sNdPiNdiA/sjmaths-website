Comprehensive Concepts & Principles: Valuation of Annuities

Valuation of annuities is the mathematical evaluation of the equivalent lump-sum worth of a series of periodic payments at a specific focal date. In CBSE Class 11 Applied Mathematics, valuation centers on two fundamental metrics: Future Value (Accumulated Sum) and Present Value (Capitalized Lump Sum) across ordinary annuities, annuities due, and perpetuities.

Key Valuation Formulations:
  • Future Value of an Ordinary Annuity ($FVA$): The accumulated terminal worth at the end of $n$ periods:
    $$FVA = R \left[ \frac{(1 + i)^n - 1}{i} \right] = R \cdot s_{\overline{n}|i}$$
  • Present Value of an Ordinary Annuity ($PVA$): The single lump-sum equivalent today required to fund $n$ future periodic withdrawals:
    $$PVA = R \left[ \frac{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}}{i} \right] = R \cdot a_{\overline{n}|i}$$
  • Future Value of an Annuity Due ($FVA_{\text{due}}$): Since each payment earns interest for 1 additional interval:
    $$FVA_{\text{due}} = R \left[ \frac{(1 + i)^n - 1}{i} \right](1 + i) = FVA \times (1 + i)$$
  • Present Value of an Annuity Due ($PVA_{\text{due}}$):
    $$PVA_{\text{due}} = R \left[ \frac{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}}{i} \right](1 + i) = R + R \left[ \frac{1 - (1 + i)^{-(n-1)}}{i} \right]$$
  • Present Value of a Perpetuity ($PV_{\infty}$): As $n \to \infty$, $(1 + i)^{-n} \to 0$:
    $$PV_{\infty} = \frac{R}{i}$$
  • Present Value of a Perpetuity Due:
    $$PV_{\infty, \text{due}} = R + \frac{R}{i} = R\left(1 + \frac{1}{i}\right)$$
  • Present Value of a Deferred Perpetuity (deferred by $k$ periods):
    $$PV_{\infty, \text{def}} = \frac{R}{i}(1 + i)^{-k}$$

Timeline & Cash Flow Equivalence

Every valuation problem can be conceptualized on a geometric progression cash flow timeline. The relationship between Future Value and Present Value over term $n$ is governed by the compound interest discount/accumulation factor:

• $FVA = PVA \times (1 + i)^n$
• $PVA = FVA \times (1 + i)^{-n} = \frac{FVA}{(1 + i)^n}$
• $\frac{1}{a_{\overline{n}|i}} - \frac{1}{s_{\overline{n}|i}} = i \implies \frac{1}{PVA} - \frac{1}{FVA} = \frac{i}{R}$

Worked Benchmark Examples

Example 1 (Future Value of Ordinary Annuity):

An individual deposits ₹10,000 at the end of each year in a savings scheme offering 8% per annum compound interest for 5 years. Calculate the total accumulated amount at maturity. [Given: $(1.08)^5 = 1.4693$]

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Periodic payment $R = ₹10,000$
• Rate $i = 0.08, n = 5$
$$FVA = R \left[ \frac{(1 + i)^n - 1}{i} \right] = 10,000 \left[ \frac{(1.08)^5 - 1}{0.08} \right]$$ $$FVA = 10,000 \left[ \frac{1.4693 - 1}{0.08} \right] = 10,000 \times \frac{0.4693}{0.08} = 10,000 \times 5.86625 = \mathbf{₹58,663}$$
Answer: The accumulated maturity sum is ₹58,663.

Example 2 (Present Value of Ordinary Annuity):

Find the present value of an ordinary annuity of ₹15,000 payable at the end of each half-year for 3 years at 10% per annum compounded semi-annually. [Given: $(1.05)^{-6} = 0.7462$]

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Periodic payment $R = ₹15,000$
• Periodic rate $i = 0.10 / 2 = 0.05$
• Number of periods $n = 3 \times 2 = 6$
$$PVA = R \left[ \frac{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}}{i} \right] = 15,000 \left[ \frac{1 - (1.05)^{-6}}{0.05} \right]$$ $$PVA = 15,000 \left[ \frac{1 - 0.7462}{0.05} \right] = 15,000 \times \frac{0.2538}{0.05} = 15,000 \times 5.076 = \mathbf{₹76,140}$$
Answer: The present value is ₹76,140.

Example 3 (Present Value of Perpetuity):

A philanthropist wishes to establish a perpetual annual scholarship of ₹60,000 to an engineering college. If the endowment fund earns 7.5% per annum interest, how much money must be donated today?

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Annual scholarship payout $R = ₹60,000$
• Annual rate $i = 7.5\% = 0.075$
$$PV_{\infty} = \frac{R}{i} = \frac{60,000}{0.075} = \mathbf{₹8,00,000}$$
Answer: The required initial endowment donation is ₹8,00,000.

Problem 1 Future Value Calculation

A company deposits ₹25,000 at the end of each quarter into a sinking fund earning 8% per annum compounded quarterly. Find the accumulated amount at the end of 4 years. [Use: $(1.02)^{16} = 1.3728$]

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $R = ₹25,000$, $i = 0.08/4 = 0.02$, $n = 4 \times 4 = 16$.
$$FVA = 25,000 \left[ \frac{(1.02)^{16} - 1}{0.02} \right] = 25,000 \times \frac{1.3728 - 1}{0.02}$$ $$FVA = 25,000 \times \frac{0.3728}{0.02} = 25,000 \times 18.64 = \mathbf{₹4,66,000}$$
Answer: The accumulated amount is ₹4,66,000.

Problem 2 Present Value Calculation

Find the present value of an ordinary annuity of ₹8,000 payable annually for 6 years at 9% per annum compounded annually. [Use: $(1.09)^{-6} = 0.5963$]

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• $R = ₹8,000$, $i = 0.09$, $n = 6$.
$$PVA = 8,000 \left[ \frac{1 - (1.09)^{-6}}{0.09} \right] = 8,000 \left[ \frac{1 - 0.5963}{0.09} \right]$$ $$PVA = 8,000 \times \frac{0.4037}{0.09} = 8,000 \times 4.48556 = \mathbf{₹35,884.48}$$
Answer: The present value is ₹35,884.48.

Problem 3 Annuity Due Valuation

A person deposits ₹12,000 at the beginning of every year for 5 years in an account yielding 10% per annum compound interest. Calculate the future value at the end of 5 years. [Use: $(1.10)^5 = 1.6105$]

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Since deposits are made at the beginning, this is an Annuity Due.
• $R = ₹12,000$, $i = 0.10$, $n = 5$.
$$FVA_{\text{due}} = R \left[ \frac{(1 + i)^n - 1}{i} \right](1 + i)$$ $$FVA_{\text{due}} = 12,000 \left[ \frac{1.6105 - 1}{0.10} \right] \times 1.10 = 12,000 \times 6.1051 \times 1.10 = \mathbf{₹80,587.32}$$
Answer: The future value of the annuity due is ₹80,587.32.

Problem 4 Valuation of Perpetuity

What sum of money deposited in a trust paying 6% annual interest compounded quarterly will yield a scholarship of ₹15,000 at the end of every quarter forever?

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Periodic payment $R = ₹15,000$
• Periodic interest rate $i = \frac{0.06}{4} = 0.015$
$$PV_{\infty} = \frac{R}{i} = \frac{15,000}{0.015} = \mathbf{₹10,00,000}$$
Answer: A deposit of ₹10,00,000 must be made today.

Problem 5 Capitalized Cost Application

The initial cost of installing an industrial cooling system is ₹5,00,000. It requires ₹40,000 at the end of each year for indefinite maintenance. If the interest rate is 8% p.a., find the capitalized cost of the equipment.

Show Step-by-Step Solution

• Capitalized Cost = Initial Cost + Present Value of Indefinite Maintenance Perpetuity.
$$PV_{\text{maint}} = \frac{R}{i} = \frac{40,000}{0.08} = ₹5,00,000$$ $$\text{Capitalized Cost} = 5,00,000 + 5,00,000 = \mathbf{₹10,00,000}$$
Answer: The capitalized cost is ₹10,00,000.

Q1: State the mathematical formula for the Present Value of an ordinary perpetuity of ₹$R$ per year at interest rate $i$.

Show Answer & Scheme

$$PV_{\infty} = \frac{R}{i} \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$

Q2: Express the relationship between the Future Value of an Annuity Due ($FVA_{\text{due}}$) and the Future Value of an Ordinary Annuity ($FVA$) of identical parameters.

Show Answer & Scheme

SECTION B: 2-Mark Short Answer Problems

Q3: A machine can be purchased for ₹3,00,000 cash down or for an immediate payment of ₹60,000 followed by 4 equal annual installments of ₹75,000. If the interest rate is 9% per annum compounded annually, calculate the present value of the installment plan and determine which option is cheaper for the buyer. [Given: $(1.09)^{-4} = 0.7084$]

Show Answer & Scheme

• Present value of 4 installments: $$PVA = 75,000 \left[ \frac{1 - 0.7084}{0.09} \right] = 75,000 \times 3.24 = ₹2,43,000 \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$ • Total PV of Installment Plan = $60,000 + 2,43,000 = ₹3,03,000$.
• Cash Down Price = ₹3,00,000.
Decision: Cash down payment is cheaper by ₹3,000. [1 Mark]

Q4: Prove algebraically that $\frac{1}{PVA} - \frac{1}{FVA} = \frac{i}{R}$.

Show Answer & Scheme

$$\frac{1}{PVA} = \frac{i}{R\left[1 - (1+i)^{-n}\right]} = \frac{i(1+i)^n}{R\left[(1+i)^n - 1\right]} \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$ $$\frac{1}{FVA} = \frac{i}{R\left[(1+i)^n - 1\right]}$$ $$\frac{1}{PVA} - \frac{1}{FVA} = \frac{i\left[(1+i)^n - 1\right]}{R\left[(1+i)^n - 1\right]} = \frac{i}{R} \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$

SECTION C: 4-Mark Comprehensive Valuation Applications

Q5: A person plans to accumulate a retirement corpus of ₹50,00,000 over a career spanning 20 years by making equal monthly deposits at the end of each month into an index fund yielding 12% per annum compounded monthly.
(i) Calculate the required monthly deposit $R$. [Given: $(1.01)^{240} = 10.8926$]
(ii) Find the total amount of money directly contributed by the person, and hence compute the total compound interest earned over the 20-year period.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Part (i) Monthly Deposit Calculation [2 Marks]:
• Target Future Value $FVA = ₹50,00,000$
• Periodic rate $i = 0.12/12 = 0.01$
• Number of months $n = 20 \times 12 = 240$
$$FVA = R \left[ \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i} \right] \implies 50,00,000 = R \left[ \frac{(1.01)^{240} - 1}{0.01} \right]$$ $$50,00,000 = R \left[ \frac{10.8926 - 1}{0.01} \right] = R \times 989.26$$ $$R = \frac{50,00,000}{989.26} \approx \mathbf{₹5,054.28} \text{ per month}$$

Part (ii) Direct Contribution & Interest [2 Marks]:
• Total Principal Contributed = $5,054.28 \times 240 = \mathbf{₹12,13,027.20}$.
• Total Compound Interest Earned = $50,00,000 - 12,13,027.20 = \mathbf{₹37,86,972.80}$.
Interest accounts for over 75.7% of the total retirement fund!

High-Yield Quick Revision: Valuation of Annuities

Master reference summary of formulas, valuation rules, and algebraic identities for Chapter 13.3.

Master Formula Sheet

Annuity Type Present Value ($PV$) Future Value ($FV$)
Ordinary Annuity $PVA = R \left[ \frac{1 - (1+i)^{-n}}{i} \right]$ $FVA = R \left[ \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i} \right]$
Annuity Due $PVA_{\text{due}} = PVA \times (1 + i)$ $FVA_{\text{due}} = FVA \times (1 + i)$
Perpetuity $PV = \frac{R}{i}$ Undefined (Infinite)
Perpetuity Due $PV = R + \frac{R}{i}$ Undefined (Infinite)

Critical Exam Shortcuts

  • Connecting PVA and FVA: $FVA = PVA \cdot (1+i)^n$. If you have already calculated the present value, multiply by $(1+i)^n$ to get the future value without recalculating the bracket from scratch!
  • Reciprocal Identity: $\frac{1}{PVA} - \frac{1}{FVA} = \frac{i}{R}$. Useful for quick cross-checking in board exam verification questions.
  • Negative Exponents in PV: $(1+i)^{-n} = \frac{1}{(1+i)^n}$. Ensure proper order of operations when calculating $1 - (1+i)^{-n}$.

10-Question Diagnostic Board Mock Examination: Valuation of Annuities

PART 1: Objective Section (4 Marks)

Q1: The present value of an ordinary perpetuity of ₹18,000 payable annually at 9% per annum is:

Solution: (B) $PV = R / i = 18,000 / 0.09 = ₹2,00,000$.

Q2: If the present value of an ordinary annuity is ₹45,000 and the periodic interest rate is 5%, what is the present value of an annuity due with identical payments, rate, and term?

Solution: (B) $PVA_{\text{due}} = PVA \times (1 + i) = 45,000 \times 1.05 = ₹47,250$.

Q3: The algebraic relationship between $\frac{1}{a_{\overline{n}|i}}$ and $\frac{1}{s_{\overline{n}|i}}$ is given by:

Solution: (B) $\frac{1}{a_{\overline{n}|i}} - \frac{1}{s_{\overline{n}|i}} = i$.

Q4 (Assertion-Reason):
Assertion (A): The present value of a perpetuity is finite even though the payments continue infinitely.
Reason (R): Because money has time value, cash flows in the distant future discount to near zero, causing the infinite geometric series $\sum_{k=1}^\infty (1+i)^{-k}$ to converge to $\frac{1}{i}$.

Solution: (A) Both statements are true and Reason provides the exact infinite GP convergence rationale ($|r| = \frac{1}{1+i} < 1$).

PART 2: Subjective Section (Q5 - Q10)

Q5 2 Marks

Find the amount of an ordinary annuity of ₹5,000 payable half-yearly for 4 years at 8% per annum compounded half-yearly. [Given: $(1.04)^8 = 1.3686$]

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• $R = ₹5,000, i = 0.08/2 = 0.04, n = 4 \times 2 = 8$ [0.5 Mark]
$$FVA = 5,000 \left[ \frac{(1.04)^8 - 1}{0.04} \right] = 5,000 \left[ \frac{1.3686 - 1}{0.04} \right] \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$ $$FVA = 5,000 \times \frac{0.3686}{0.04} = 5,000 \times 9.215 = \mathbf{₹46,075} \quad [0.5 \text{ Mark}]$$

Q6 2 Marks

Calculate the present value of an annuity due of ₹20,000 payable at the beginning of each year for 3 years at 10% p.a. compounded annually. [Given: $(1.10)^{-2} = 0.8264$]

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• In an annuity due: $PVA_{\text{due}} = R + R \cdot a_{\overline{n-1}|i}$
$$PVA_{\text{due}} = 20,000 + 20,000 \left[ \frac{1 - (1.10)^{-2}}{0.10} \right] \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$ $$PVA_{\text{due}} = 20,000 + 20,000 \left[ \frac{1 - 0.8264}{0.10} \right] = 20,000 + 20,000(1.736) = 20,000 + 34,720 = \mathbf{₹54,720} \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$

Q7 3 Marks

A hospital receives an offer of a perpetual annual donation of ₹1,20,000, with the first installment starting 4 years from now. If the interest rate is 6% per annum compounded annually, compute the present value of this deferred perpetuity. [Given: $(1.06)^{-3} = 0.8396$]

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• The perpetuity begins at $t = 4$. In an immediate perpetuity, payment occurs at $t = 1$.
• Deferment period $k = 4 - 1 = 3$ years [1 Mark].
• Value of perpetuity at $t = 3$: $$V_3 = \frac{R}{i} = \frac{1,20,000}{0.06} = ₹20,00,000 \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$ • Discounting back to $t=0$: $$PV = V_3 \times (1 + i)^{-3} = 20,00,000 \times 0.8396 = \mathbf{₹16,79,200} \quad [1 \text{ Mark}]$$

Q8 3 Marks

Show that the present value of an ordinary annuity $P$ and its future value $A$ satisfy the equation $A = P(1 + i)^n$ using their algebraic summation formulas.

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

• Recall: $P = R \left[ \frac{1 - (1+i)^{-n}}{i} \right] = \frac{R}{i} \left[ 1 - \frac{1}{(1+i)^n} \right] = \frac{R}{i} \left[ \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{(1+i)^n} \right]$ [1.5 Marks].
• Multiplying both sides by $(1+i)^n$: $$P(1+i)^n = \frac{R}{i} \left[ (1+i)^n - 1 \right] = R \left[ \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i} \right]$$ • The right-hand side is precisely the formula for Future Value $A$.
• Hence, $A = P(1+i)^n$. [1.5 Marks]

Q9 & Q10 Case Study (4 Marks Total)

Case Context: Corporate Pension Fund Portfolio

A corporate pension fund must pay a retiring employee an ordinary annuity of ₹30,000 at the end of each month for 15 years. The fund's asset portfolio earns a guaranteed annual return of 6% compounded monthly ($i = 0.06/12 = 0.005$). [Given: $(1.005)^{-180} = 0.40748$, $(1.005)^{180} = 2.4541$]

Q9 (2 Marks): Calculate the exact lump sum corpus that the corporation must allocate today into the fund to guarantee these payments.
Q10 (2 Marks): What is the total sum of money the pensioner will receive over the 15-year period, and how much of that total is funded by investment interest earned by the corpus?

Show Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme

Solution to Q9 [2 Marks]:
• $R = ₹30,000, i = 0.005, n = 15 \times 12 = 180$. $$PVA = 30,000 \left[ \frac{1 - (1.005)^{-180}}{0.005} \right] = 30,000 \left[ \frac{1 - 0.40748}{0.005} \right]$$ $$PVA = 30,000 \times \frac{0.59252}{0.005} = 30,000 \times 118.504 = \mathbf{₹35,55,120}$$
• The corporation must allocate ₹35,55,120 today.

Solution to Q10 [2 Marks]:
• Total Payout to Pensioner = $30,000 \times 180 = \mathbf{₹54,00,000}$.
• Interest Earned = Total Payout - Initial Corpus = $54,00,000 - 35,55,120 = \mathbf{₹18,44,880}$.
• Over ₹18.44 Lakhs of the retirement benefits are generated directly through compound interest.
