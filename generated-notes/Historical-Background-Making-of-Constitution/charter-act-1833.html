Charter Act 1833

UPSC Study Notes - Brief and Comprehensive

Charter Act 1833 (Saint Helena Act 1833)

  • Passed by the British Parliament on August 28, 1833.
  • Also known as the Saint Helena Act 1833.
  • It was the final step towards **centralization** in British India.
  • Extended the East India Company's rule for another 20 years.

Key Provisions

1. End of Commercial Functions of EIC

  • The East India Company was deprived of its commercial privileges entirely. It ceased to be a commercial body.
  • It became a purely administrative and political body for British possessions in India.
  • The Company's territories in India were held "in trust for His Majesty, his heirs and successors."

2. Centralization of Administration

  • The Governor-General of Bengal was redesignated as the Governor-General of India.
  • Lord William Bentinck became the first Governor-General of India.
  • The Governor-General of India was vested with all civil and military powers.

3. Legislative Centralization

  • The Governor-General-in-Council was given exclusive legislative powers for the whole of British India.
  • The Governors of Bombay and Madras presidencies were deprived of their power to make laws.
  • Laws made under this Act were called Acts (not Regulations), and they were enforceable throughout British India.

4. Fourth Member (Law Member)

  • A fourth member (Law Member) was added to the Governor-General's Council for legislative purposes only.
  • Lord Macaulay was the first Law Member.

5. Indian Law Commission

  • Provided for the appointment of an Indian Law Commission to codify all Indian laws.
  • The first Law Commission was headed by Lord Macaulay.

6. Attempt at Open Competition for Civil Services

  • The Act attempted to introduce a system of open competition for selection of civil servants.
  • It stated that Indians should not be debarred from holding any office under the Company based on religion, place of birth, descent, or colour (Section 87).
  • However, this provision was negated after opposition from the Court of Directors.

7. Slavery

  • Directed the Governor-General-in-Council to take steps to ameliorate the condition of slaves and ultimately abolish slavery in India.
  • Slavery was finally abolished in India in 1843.

8. Christian Missionaries

  • Allowed Christian missionaries to freely enter India and preach, removing previous restrictions.

Impact & Legacy

  • Marked the end of the East India Company's commercial activities and its transformation into a purely administrative entity.
  • Further strengthened the central administration and legislative authority under the Governor-General of India.
  • Laid the foundation for a unified legal system in India through the Law Commission.
  • The attempt to introduce open competition for civil services, though initially unsuccessful, was a significant step towards meritocracy and non-discrimination in public service.
