Constitutional Government: Study Notes

1. Definition and Core Concept

  • A Constitutional Government is a government defined, established, and limited by a constitution.
  • It operates under the Rule of Law, not the arbitrary will of individuals or groups.
  • Its powers are enumerated and restricted, ensuring that it cannot act beyond the authority granted by the constitution.
  • It embodies the principle of Constitutionalism – adherence to the spirit and letter of the constitution.

2. Key Characteristics and Features

  • Limited Government: The powers of the government are explicitly defined and restricted by the constitution.
  • Supremacy of the Constitution: The constitution is the supreme law of the land, and all governmental actions must conform to it.
  • Rule of Law: All individuals, including those in power, are subject to and accountable under the law. No one is above the law.
  • Separation of Powers: Division of governmental powers into distinct branches (legislative, executive, judicial) to prevent concentration of power.
  • Checks and Balances: Mechanisms by which each branch of government can limit the powers of the other branches, ensuring accountability and preventing abuse.
  • Protection of Fundamental Rights: Guarantees and safeguards for individual liberties and rights, often enshrined in a Bill of Rights.
  • Independent Judiciary: A judiciary free from executive and legislative interference, tasked with interpreting the constitution and upholding the rule of law.
  • Popular Sovereignty: The ultimate authority of the government rests with the people, who delegate power through democratic processes.
  • Accountability and Transparency: Government is answerable to the people and operates with a degree of openness.
  • Democratic Principles: Often associated with democratic forms of government, involving free and fair elections, representation, and citizen participation.

3. Historical Evolution (Brief)

  • Magna Carta (1215): Early document limiting the power of the English monarch.
  • Glorious Revolution (1688) & English Bill of Rights (1689): Further established parliamentary supremacy and individual rights in England.
  • American Revolution (1776) & US Constitution (1787): First modern written constitution establishing a federal republic with explicit limits on government.
  • French Revolution (1789) & Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen: Emphasized popular sovereignty and natural rights.

4. Constitutional Government in India

India is a prime example of a constitutional government, characterized by:

  • Written and Elaborate Constitution: The Constitution of India is the longest written constitution in the world, detailing the structure, powers, and limitations of government.
  • Preamble: Declares India to be a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic, embodying the core ideals of constitutional governance.
  • Fundamental Rights (Part III): Guarantees civil and political rights to citizens, enforceable by courts (Articles 12-35).
  • Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV): Non-justiciable guidelines for the state to promote social and economic justice.
  • Independent Judiciary: Supreme Court and High Courts act as guardians of the constitution and fundamental rights (e.g., Article 124, 214).
  • Judicial Review: The power of the judiciary to examine the constitutionality of legislative enactments and executive orders (Article 13, 32, 226).
  • Separation of Powers with Checks and Balances: Clear demarcation of roles for Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary, with mechanisms for mutual oversight.
  • Basic Structure Doctrine (Kesavananda Bharati case, 1973): The Supreme Court ruled that certain fundamental features of the Constitution cannot be amended by Parliament, reinforcing constitutional limits.
  • Federal System with Unitary Bias: Division of powers between Union and State governments, as outlined in the Seventh Schedule.
