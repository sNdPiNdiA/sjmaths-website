Historical evolution of the Constitution

UPSC Study Notes - Brief and Comprehensive

Company Rule (1773-1858)

  • Regulating Act, 1773
    • First step by British Government to control and regulate the East India Company's affairs in India.
    • Designated the Governor of Bengal as the Governor-General of Bengal (Lord Warren Hastings was the first).
    • Established a Supreme Court at Calcutta in 1774.
    • Prohibited Company servants from engaging in private trade or accepting presents/bribes.
  • Pitt's India Act, 1784
    • Distinguished between the commercial and political functions of the Company.
    • Established a Board of Control for political affairs and allowed the Court of Directors to manage commercial affairs. Introduced a system of dual government.
  • Charter Act, 1813
    • Ended the Company's monopoly over Indian trade, opening it to all British merchants, except for trade in tea and trade with China.
    • Asserted the sovereignty of the British Crown over the Company's territories in India.
  • Charter Act, 1833
    • Final step towards centralization in British India.
    • Designated the Governor-General of Bengal as the Governor-General of India (Lord William Bentinck was the first).
    • Deprived the Governors of Bombay and Madras of their legislative powers.
    • Attempted to introduce an open competition system for the selection of civil servants (failed due to opposition from Court of Directors).
  • Charter Act, 1853
    • Separated the legislative and executive functions of the Governor-General's Council.
    • Introduced an open competition system for civil services, making it open to Indians as well (Macaulay Committee, 1854).
    • Introduced local representation in the Indian (Central) Legislative Council.

Crown Rule (1858-1947)

  • Government of India Act, 1858
    • Ended the East India Company's rule and transferred the powers of government, territories, and revenues to the British Crown.
    • Designated the Governor-General of India as the Viceroy of India (Lord Canning was the first).
    • Created a new office, Secretary of State for India, with a 15-member Council of India, to exercise complete authority over Indian administration.
  • Indian Councils Act, 1861
    • Initiated the process of decentralization by restoring legislative powers to the Bombay and Madras Presidencies.
    • Provided that the Viceroy should nominate some Indians as non-official members to his expanded Legislative Council.
    • Recognized the portfolio system, introduced by Lord Canning in 1859.
  • Indian Councils Act, 1892
    • Increased the number of additional (non-official) members in the Central and Provincial Legislative Councils.
    • Introduced the principle of indirect election for some non-official members.
    • Empowered the legislative councils to discuss the budget and address questions to the executive.
