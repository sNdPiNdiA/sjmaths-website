UPSC Study Notes - Brief and Comprehensive

Amending Act of 1781 (Act of Settlement 1781 / Declaratory Act 1781)

  • Date: Passed by the British Parliament in 1781.
  • Purpose: Enacted to rectify the defects of the Regulating Act of 1773, primarily to clarify the jurisdiction between the Supreme Court at Calcutta and the Governor-General-in-Council.
  • Background: Arose from the conflicts between the Supreme Court (headed by Chief Justice Sir Elijah Impey) and the Governor-General-in-Council (headed by Warren Hastings), notably in cases like the Patna Case and Kasijora Case, which highlighted the ambiguities in the 1773 Act.

Key Provisions

  • Exemption of Governor-General & Council: Exempted the Governor-General and the Council members from the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court for acts done in their official capacity.
  • Exemption of Company Servants: Similarly, exempted the servants of the Company from the Supreme Court's jurisdiction for acts done in their official capacity.
  • Exclusion of Revenue Matters: Explicitly excluded revenue matters and matters arising in the collection of revenue from the Supreme Court's jurisdiction.
  • Appellate Jurisdiction:
    • Established that appeals from the Provincial Courts (Mofussil Diwani Adalats) were to be taken to the Governor-General-in-Council, not the Supreme Court.
    • This made the Governor-General-in-Council the highest court of appeal for provincial civil cases.
  • Jurisdiction over Inhabitants of Calcutta:
    • The Supreme Court was empowered to administer the personal law of the defendant (e.g., Hindu law for Hindus, Muslim law for Muslims) in cases involving personal matters such as inheritance, marriage, and religious usages.
  • Jurisdiction over Provincial Inhabitants: Clarified that the Supreme Court's jurisdiction extended only to the inhabitants of Calcutta, not to the residents of the provinces, unless they were Company servants or had voluntarily submitted to its jurisdiction.
  • Power to Frame Regulations: Empowered the Governor-General-in-Council to frame regulations for the Provincial Courts and Councils. These regulations were not required to be registered with the Supreme Court.

Significance

  • Considered a significant step towards a more coherent judicial system in British India.
  • Reduced the friction and clarified the relationship between the executive (Governor-General-in-Council) and the judiciary (Supreme Court).
  • Strengthened the position of the Governor-General-in-Council by making it the final court of appeal for provincial matters and granting it legislative powers for provincial administration.
  • Recognized the importance of local laws and customs by mandating the application of personal laws in relevant cases.
