Changes by the Independence Act 1947

UPSC Study Notes - Brief and Comprehensive

Key Provisions and Changes

  • Declared India an independent and sovereign state from August 15, 1947.
  • Provided for the partition of India and the creation of two independent dominions: India and Pakistan.
  • Abolished the office of the Viceroy of India.
  • Provided for a Governor-General for each dominion, to be appointed by the British King on the advice of the respective dominion's cabinet.

Legislative and Constitutional Authority

  • Empowered the Constituent Assemblies of both dominions to frame and adopt any constitution for their respective nations.
  • The Constituent Assemblies were granted full power to legislate for their respective dominions.
  • They could repeal any Act of the British Parliament, including the Indian Independence Act itself.
  • No Act of the British Parliament passed after August 15, 1947, would extend to either dominion unless it was extended by the dominion's legislature.
  • Until new constitutions were framed, the Government of India Act, 1935 (with necessary modifications) was to be the provisional constitution for both dominions.

Impact on Princely States

  • Lapse of British paramountcy over the Indian princely states.
  • Princely states were given the freedom to accede to either India or Pakistan, or to remain independent.
  • All treaties and agreements between the British Crown and the princely states automatically lapsed.

Administrative Changes

  • Abolished the office of the Secretary of State for India and transferred his functions to the Secretary of State for Commonwealth Affairs.
  • The British Monarch's title of "Emperor of India" was dropped.
  • Continued the services of the existing civil servants (ICS, etc.) with the same terms and conditions until new arrangements were made.
