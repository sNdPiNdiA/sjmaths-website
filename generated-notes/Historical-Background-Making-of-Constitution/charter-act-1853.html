Charter Act 1853

  • Date: 1853
  • Context: The last of the Charter Acts passed by the British Parliament before the Revolt of 1857. It was a significant step towards asserting parliamentary control over the East India Company's administration in India.

Key Provisions and Features

  • End of Company Rule (Implicit):
    • Did not specify any time period for the Company's rule, unlike previous Charter Acts. This implied that the Company's rule could be terminated by the Parliament at any time.
    • This was a clear indication of the British Parliament's intention to take over the governance of India.
  • Separation of Legislative and Executive Functions:
    • For the first time, it separated the legislative and executive functions of the Governor-General's Council.
    • This laid the foundational stone for a modern parliamentary system.
