Charter Act 1793

  • Date: 1793
  • Context: Enacted by the British Parliament to renew the East India Company's (EIC) charter and introduce administrative reforms, building upon the Regulating Act of 1773 and Pitt's India Act of 1784.

Key Provisions and Features

  • Extension of EIC's Monopoly:
    • The Company's trade monopoly in India and with China was extended for another 20 years.
  • Powers of the Governor-General:
    • The Governor-General (GG) was empowered to override the majority of his council in matters of high importance affecting the safety, tranquility, or interests of British possessions in India. This power, initially granted to Lord Cornwallis, was now generalized to all future GGs.
    • The GG of Bengal was given more powers and control over the Presidencies of Bombay and Madras, especially in matters of war, peace, and revenue.
    • The GG could appoint a Vice-President from among the civilian members of his council to preside over meetings in his absence.
  • Board of Control (BoC):
    • The salaries of the members of the Board of Control and their staff were to be paid out of the Indian revenues. This was a significant financial burden shifted onto India.
    • The BoC was given extensive powers to interpret and amend the Company's regulations and bylaws.
  • Commander-in-Chief:
    • It was no longer mandatory for the Commander-in-Chief to be a member of the Governor-General's Council, unless specifically appointed to it.
  • Judicial and Administrative Reforms:
    • The Act formally separated the revenue administration from the judicial functions, a principle introduced by Lord Cornwallis.
