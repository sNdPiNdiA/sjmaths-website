Government of India Act 1858

UPSC Study Notes - Brief and Comprehensive

Background and Objective

  • **Context**: Passed in the immediate aftermath of the **Revolt of 1857** (also known as the Sepoy Mutiny).
  • **Objective**: To liquidate the **East India Company's** rule and transfer the powers of government, territories, and revenues to the **British Crown**.
  • Also known as the **Act for the Better Government of India**.

Key Provisions

  • **End of Company Rule**: The rule of the East India Company was formally abolished.
  • **Transfer of Power**: All powers relating to the government, territories, and revenues of India were transferred to the **British Crown**.
  • **Abolition of Dual Government**: The **Board of Control** and the **Court of Directors** (established by Pitt's India Act 1784) were abolished, thereby ending the dual system of government.
  • **Secretary of State for India (SOS)**:
    • A new office, the **Secretary of State for India**, was created in the British Cabinet.
    • The SOS was a member of the British Cabinet and was directly responsible to the British Parliament.
    • He was vested with complete authority and control over Indian administration.
  • **Council of India**:
    • The SOS was assisted by a **15-member Council of India**.
    • This council was an advisory body, and its members were mostly former Company officials.
  • **Viceroy of India**:
    • The existing office of **Governor-General of India** was redesignated as the **Viceroy of India**.
    • The Viceroy became the direct representative of the British Crown in India.
    • **Lord Canning** became the first Viceroy of India.
  • **No Change in Administrative Structure**: The Act did not alter the existing administrative and financial structure in India, but placed it under the authority of the Crown.
  • **Queen's Proclamation (1858)**:
    • Issued by **Queen Victoria** on **November 1, 1858**, from Allahabad.
    • Declared an end to the **Doctrine of Lapse**.
    • Promised religious freedom, equal treatment of Indian subjects, and respect for the rights of native princes.
    • Granted pardon to all except those directly involved in the murder of British subjects.

Significance

  • Marked the beginning of **direct rule of the British Crown** over India, replacing the East India Company's rule.
  • Transformed the nature of governance from a commercial enterprise to a direct imperial administration.
  • Centralized control over Indian affairs in London through the Secretary of State, making India a direct dependency of the British Parliament.
  • Laid the foundation for a more centralized and bureaucratic administration in India under the Crown.
