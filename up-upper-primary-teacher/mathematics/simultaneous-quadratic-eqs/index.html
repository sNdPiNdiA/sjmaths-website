1. Fundamental Axioms, Definitions & Foundational Properties

Linear Equation in Two Variables: An equation of the form $ax + by + c = 0$, where $a, b, c$ are real numbers and $a^2 + b^2 \neq 0$. Its graph represents a straight line in the Cartesian coordinate plane, and every point on the line is a solution.
Simultaneous Linear Equations: A system of two linear equations in two variables, expressed as $a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0$ and $a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0$, which must be satisfied simultaneously by the same pair of values $(x, y)$.
Quadratic Equation in One Variable: A second-degree polynomial equation of the standard form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, where $a \neq 0$ and $a, b, c \in \mathbb{R}$. It possesses exactly two roots (real or complex).
Discriminant Property: The expression $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ dictates the nature of roots for a quadratic equation. If $\Delta > 0$, roots are real and distinct; if $\Delta = 0$, roots are real and equal; if $\Delta < 0$, roots are non-real complex conjugates.
Exam Pro-Tip: Always check if a quadratic equation can be solved by factoring before deploying the quadratic formula to save precious seconds in the examination hall.
Memory Mnemonic: C-P-D — Consistent (Parallel vs Intersecting), Product of roots ($c/a$), Discriminant ($b^2 - 4ac$).

Substitution Method: Solve one linear equation for one variable in terms of the other, and substitute that expression into the second equation to reduce it to a single-variable linear equation.
Elimination Method: Multiply one or both equations by suitable non-zero constants so that the coefficients of one variable become additive inverses, then add the equations to eliminate that variable.
Vieta's Formulas: For $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ with roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$, the sum of roots is $\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a}$ and the product of roots is $\alpha \cdot \beta = \frac{c}{a}$.
15-Second Speed-Math Trick: When solving symmetric simultaneous equations like $ax + by = c$ and $bx + ay = d$, immediately add and subtract the two equations to form two trivial equations: $(a+b)(x+y) = c+d$ and $(a-b)(x-y) = c-d$.

3. Word Problem Modeling & Practical Applications

Age Problems Modeling: Translate past and future age statements into simultaneous linear equations. Let current ages be $x$ and $y$. If 5 years ago $x-5 = 3(y-5)$, simplify to $x - 3y = -10$.
Digit Number Problems: A two-digit number is represented as $10x + y$, where $x$ is the tens digit and $y$ is the units digit. The reversed number is $10y + x$. Their sum is $11(x+y)$, and their difference is $9(x-y)$.
Speed, Distance & Time: Modeled via quadratic equations when acceleration or variable speed is involved. Distance $=$ Speed $\times$ Time. If speed increases by $r$ km/h, time decreases, yielding an equation of the form $\frac{d}{v} - \frac{d}{v+r} = t$.
Work & Time Problems: If worker A takes $x$ days and B takes $y$ days, their combined 1-day work is $\frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y} = \frac{1}{T}$, where $T$ is the total working days together.
UP Exam High-Yield Tip: In NCERT/SCERT word problems regarding upstream and downstream speeds, let boat speed in still water be $u$ and stream speed be $v$. Upstream speed is $u - v$ and downstream speed is $u + v$. Set up simultaneous linear equations for $u$ and $v$.

4. Mathematical Edge Cases, Sign Conventions & Common Traps

Critical Distinction: A quadratic equation always has two roots, but those roots may be distinct real numbers, coincident real numbers (multiplicity 2), or complex numbers. Never assume 'roots' implies distinct real numbers unless stated.
Edge Case / Division by Zero: When simplifying rational equations reducible to quadratics (e.g., $\frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{x-2} = 3$), check that extracted root values do not make any denominator zero ($x \neq 0, x \neq 2$).
Common Calculation Trap: Forgetting to distribute the negative sign when subtracting one simultaneous equation from another or when applying the quadratic formula numerator $-b \pm \sqrt{\Delta}$.
Trap Prevention Trick: Always substitute your final solved values of $(x, y)$ back into both original simultaneous equations. If it satisfies one but fails the other, an arithmetic error was made during elimination.
Memory Mnemonic: S-I-G-N — Square roots, Inequality bounds, Greater than zero discriminant, Negative sign distribution.

Question 1 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

1. If the system of linear equations $kx + 3y = k - 3$ and $12x + ky = k$ has infinitely many solutions, what is the value of $k$?

Correct Answer: Option A

For infinitely many solutions, the condition is $\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2}$. Here, $\frac{k}{12} = \frac{3}{k} = \frac{k-3}{k}$. Taking the first equality: $\frac{k}{12} = \frac{3}{k} \implies k^2 = 36 \implies k = \pm 6$. Testing $k = 6$ with the second equality: $\frac{3}{6} = \frac{6-3}{6} \implies \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$, which is true. Testing $k = -6$: $\frac{3}{-6} = \frac{-6-3}{-6} \implies -\frac{1}{2} = \frac{9}{6}$, which is false. Thus, $k = 6$.

Question 2 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

2. What are the roots of the quadratic equation $2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 0$?

Correct Answer: Option A

Using factorization by splitting the middle term: $2x^2 - 6x - x + 3 = 0 \implies 2x(x - 3) - 1(x - 3) = 0 \implies (2x - 1)(x - 3) = 0$. Setting each factor to zero gives $x = 3$ and $x = \frac{1}{2}$. Option A is correct.

Question 3 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

3. If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the roots of the equation $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$, what is the value of $\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta}$?

Correct Answer: Option A

From the quadratic equation $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$, sum of roots $\alpha + \beta = 5$ and product of roots $\alpha \beta = 6$. We need $\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta} = \frac{\alpha + \beta}{\alpha \beta} = \frac{5}{6}$. Option A is correct.

Question 4 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

4. For what value of $m$ will the quadratic equation $x^2 - 2(1 + 3m)x + 7(3 + 2m) = 0$ have real and equal roots?

Correct Answer: Option A

For real and equal roots, discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac = 0$. Here $a = 1$, $b = -2(1+3m)$, $c = 7(3+2m)$. Setting $\Delta = 0$: $[-2(1+3m)]^2 - 4(1)(7)(3+2m) = 0 \implies 4(1 + 6m + 9m^2) - 28(3 + 2m) = 0 \implies 4 + 24m + 36m^2 - 84 - 56m = 0 \implies 36m^2 - 32m - 80 = 0$. Dividing by 4 gives $9m^2 - 8m - 20 = 0 \implies 9m^2 - 18m + 10m - 20 = 0 \implies 9m(m-2) + 10(m-2) = 0 \implies (9m+10)(m-2) = 0$. Thus $m = 2$ or $m = -\frac{10}{9}$.

Question 5 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

5. The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is 9. When 27 is subtracted from the number, the digits are reversed. Find the number.

Correct Answer: Option A

Let the number be $10x + y$. Given $x + y = 9$. Also $(10x + y) - 27 = 10y + x \implies 9x - 9y = 27 \implies x - y = 3$. Adding both equations: $2x = 12 \implies x = 6$, and $y = 3$. Thus the number is $10(6) + 3 = 63$. Checking: $63 - 27 = 36$ (digits reversed).

Question 6 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

6. Solve for $x$ and $y$: $3x + 4y = 10$ and $2x - 2y = 2$. What is the value of $x + y$?

Correct Answer: Option A

Equation 1: $3x + 4y = 10$. Equation 2: $2x - 2y = 2 \implies x - y = 1 \implies x = y + 1$. Substitute into Eq 1: $3(y+1) + 4y = 10 \implies 3y + 3 + 4y = 10 \implies 7y = 7 \implies y = 1$. Then $x = 1 + 1 = 2$. Therefore, $x + y = 2 + 1 = 3$.

Question 7 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

7. If one root of the quadratic equation $x^2 - kx + 8 = 0$ is 4, what is the value of $k$?

Correct Answer: Option A

Since $x = 4$ is a root, substitute $x = 4$ into the equation: $(4)^2 - k(4) + 8 = 0 \implies 16 - 4k + 8 = 0 \implies 24 - 4k = 0 \implies 4k = 24 \implies k = 6$.

Question 8 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

8. A train travels 360 km at a uniform speed. If the speed had been 5 km/h more, it would have taken 1 hour less for the same journey. Find the original speed of the train.

Correct Answer: Option A

Let original speed be $x$ km/h. Time taken = $\frac{360}{x}$. New time = $\frac{360}{x+5}$. Given $\frac{360}{x} - \frac{360}{x+5} = 1 \implies 360\left[\frac{x+5 - x}{x(x+5)}\right] = 1 \implies \frac{1800}{x^2 + 5x} = 1 \implies x^2 + 5x - 1800 = 0$. Factoring: $x^2 + 45x - 40x - 1800 = 0 \implies x(x+45) - 40(x+45) = 0 \implies (x-40)(x+45) = 0$. Since speed cannot be negative, $x = 40$ km/h.

Question 9 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

9. What is the nature of the roots of the quadratic equation $3x^2 - 5x + 7 = 0$?

Correct Answer: Option A

Calculate discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac = (-5)^2 - 4(3)(7) = 25 - 84 = -59$. Since $\Delta < 0$, the roots are non-real complex conjugates.

Question 10 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

10. If $2^x + 3^y = 17$ and $2^{x+2} - 3^{y+1} = 5$, what are the values of $x$ and $y$?

Correct Answer: Option A

Let $A = 2^x$ and $B = 3^y$. Equations become $A + B = 17$ and $4A - 3B = 5$. From first, $B = 17 - A$. Substitute into second: $4A - 3(17 - A) = 5 \implies 4A - 51 + 3A = 5 \implies 7A = 56 \implies A = 8$. Thus $2^x = 8 \implies x = 3$. Then $B = 17 - 8 = 9$, so $3^y = 9 \implies y = 2$. Solution: $x = 3, y = 2$.

High-Yield Rapid Recall Formulas & Laws (Must-Remember Pointers)

#1
A linear equation in two variables $ax + by + c = 0$ represents a straight line on the Cartesian plane.
#2
A system of simultaneous linear equations has a unique solution if $\frac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \frac{b_1}{b_2}$.
#3
A system has infinitely many solutions if $\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2}$ (dependent system).
#4
A system has no solution if $\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \frac{c_1}{c_2}$ (inconsistent parallel lines).
#5
The standard form of a quadratic equation is $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ where $a \neq 0$.
#6
The quadratic formula for finding roots is $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$.
#7
The discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ determines root nature: $\Delta > 0$ (real distinct), $\Delta = 0$ (real equal), $\Delta < 0$ (complex).
#8
Vieta's relations state that for $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, sum of roots is $-b/a$ and product of roots is $c/a$.
