16.1 Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables (युगपत रैखिक समीकरण)

A pair of linear equations in two variables $x$ and $y$ can be represented in standard form as:

$$a_1 x + b_1 y + c_1 = 0$$ $$a_2 x + b_2 y + c_2 = 0$$

The nature of their solutions depends strictly on the ratios of their coefficients:

Ratio Condition Graphical Meaning Algebraic Interpretation Consistency Classification
$$\frac{a_1}{a_2} \ne \frac{b_1}{b_2}$$ Intersecting Lines (प्रतिच्छेदी रेखाएँ) Exactly One Unique Solution Consistent (संगत)
$$\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2}$$ Coincident Lines (संपाती रेखाएँ) Infinitely Many Solutions Consistent & Dependent (आश्रित)
$$\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \ne \frac{c_1}{c_2}$$ Parallel Lines (समांतर रेखाएँ) No Solution (शून्य हल) Inconsistent (असंगत)
Watch Out for Sign Traps in $c_1$ and $c_2$:

Make sure both equations have the constant term on the same side (either both on the LHS: $ax+by+c=0$, or both on the RHS: $ax+by=c$). Mixing $ax+by+c_1=0$ with $ax+by=c_2$ causes sign reversal in ratio comparisons!

16.2 Quadratic Equations & Nature of Roots (द्विघात समीकरण एवं मूलों की प्रकृति)

A second-degree equation of the form:

$$ax^2 + bx + c = 0 \quad (a \ne 0, \; a, b, c \in \mathbb{R})$$

is called a quadratic equation. Its roots are given by the Sridharacharya Formula (श्रीधराचार्य सूत्र):

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

The Discriminant $D = b^2 - 4ac$ (विविक्तकर)

The quantity $D = b^2 - 4ac$ dictates the geometric and algebraic nature of the roots:

Value of Discriminant $D$ Nature of Roots (मूलों की प्रकृति) Roots Formula
$D > 0$ and is a perfect square Real, Rational, and Distinct ($x_1 \ne x_2$) $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a}$ (fractions/integers)
$D > 0$ and NOT a perfect square Real, Irrational Conjugate Pairs ($p \pm \sqrt{q}$) Surd forms: $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a}$
$\mathbf{D = 0}$ Real, Rational, and Equal ($x_1 = x_2$) $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$ (Repeated root)
$D < 0$ No Real Roots (Complex Conjugate Roots) Does not intersect the x-axis

16.3 Vieta's Root Formulas & Symmetric Expressions

If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the roots of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$:

Root Property Mathematical Formula Top-Rank Speed Shortcut
Sum of Roots ($\alpha + \beta$) $\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a}$ $-\frac{\text{Coefficient of } x}{\text{Coefficient of } x^2}$
Product of Roots ($\alpha\beta$) $\alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}$ $\frac{\text{Constant Term}}{\text{Coefficient of } x^2}$
Sum of Squares ($\alpha^2 + \beta^2$) $(\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha\beta$ $\frac{b^2 - 2ac}{a^2}$
Sum of Reciprocals ($\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta}$) $\frac{\alpha + \beta}{\alpha\beta}$ $-\frac{b}{c}$
Difference of Roots ($|\alpha - \beta|$) $\sqrt{(\alpha + \beta)^2 - 4\alpha\beta}$ $\frac{\sqrt{D}}{|a|}$
Two Super Speed Conditions:
  • Condition for Reciprocal Roots ($\beta = \frac{1}{\alpha}$): Product $\alpha\beta = 1 \implies \frac{c}{a} = 1 \implies \mathbf{c = a}$. (Constant term equals $x^2$ coefficient).
  • Condition for Equal & Opposite Roots ($\beta = -\alpha$): Sum $\alpha + \beta = 0 \implies -\frac{b}{a} = 0 \implies \mathbf{b = 0}$. (Coefficient of $x$ is zero).

Type 1: Condition for No Solution in Linear Systems Archetype 1
Q1
For what value of $k$ will the system of equations $2x + ky = 1$ and $3x - 5y = 7$ have NO solution?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $k = -\frac{10}{3}$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. For no solution, the condition is: $$\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \ne \frac{c_1}{c_2}$$
  2. Here $a_1 = 2, b_1 = k, c_1 = 1$ and $a_2 = 3, b_2 = -5, c_2 = 7$.
  3. Equate the first two ratios: $$\frac{2}{3} = \frac{k}{-5} \implies 3k = -10 \implies k = -\frac{10}{3}$$
  4. Verify third ratio: $\frac{k}{-5} = \frac{2}{3} \ne \frac{1}{7}$. Since $\frac{2}{3} \ne \frac{1}{7}$, the condition is satisfied.
Type 2: Infinitely Many Solutions (Coincident Lines) Archetype 2
Q2
For what positive value of $k$ does the pair of linear equations $kx + 3y = k - 3$ and $12x + ky = k$ have infinitely many solutions?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $k = 6$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Condition for infinitely many solutions: $$\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2} \implies \frac{k}{12} = \frac{3}{k} = \frac{k - 3}{k}$$
  2. From first two ratios: $$\frac{k}{12} = \frac{3}{k} \implies k^2 = 36 \implies k = \pm 6$$
  3. Check $k = 6$: $$\frac{3}{6} = \frac{6 - 3}{6} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2} \quad (\text{Valid!})$$
  4. Check $k = -6$: $$\frac{3}{-6} = -\frac{1}{2}, \quad \frac{-6 - 3}{-6} = \frac{-9}{-6} = \frac{3}{2} \ne -\frac{1}{2} \quad (\text{Invalid!})$$
  5. Therefore, only $k = 6$ satisfies all three ratios.

Top-Rank Speed Hack: Always verify candidate values across the constant term ratio $\frac{c_1}{c_2}$ to eliminate extraneous roots!

Type 3: Elimination Method & Slope Substitution Archetype 3
Q3
Solve the system $2x + 3y = 11$ and $2x - 4y = -24$, and hence find the value of $m$ for which $y = mx + 3$.
Correct Answer: Option (B) $m = -1$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Subtract equation (2) from equation (1): $$(2x + 3y) - (2x - 4y) = 11 - (-24)$$ $$7y = 35 \implies y = 5$$
  2. Substitute $y = 5$ into equation (1): $$2x + 3(5) = 11 \implies 2x + 15 = 11 \implies 2x = -4 \implies x = -2$$
  3. Substitute $(x, y) = (-2, 5)$ into $y = mx + 3$: $$5 = m(-2) + 3 \implies 2 = -2m \implies m = -1$$
Type 4: Discriminant & Nature of Quadratic Roots Archetype 4
Q4
What is the nature of the roots of the quadratic equation $3x^2 - 2\sqrt{6}x + 2 = 0$?
Correct Answer: Option (B) Real and Equal

Step-by-Step Discriminant Calculation:

  1. Identify coefficients: $a = 3, b = -2\sqrt{6}, c = 2$.
  2. Compute $D = b^2 - 4ac$: $$D = (-2\sqrt{6})^2 - 4(3)(2)$$ $$D = (4 \times 6) - 24 = 24 - 24 = 0$$
  3. Since $D = 0$, the roots are real and equal (repeated root $x = -\frac{b}{2a} = \frac{2\sqrt{6}}{6} = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{3} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}$).
Type 5: Finding Unknown Parameter for Equal Roots ($D=0$) Archetype 5
Q5
For what non-zero value of $k$ does the quadratic equation $kx(x - 2) + 6 = 0$ have two equal real roots?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $k = 6$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Expand into standard form: $$kx^2 - 2kx + 6 = 0$$
  2. Here $a = k, b = -2k, c = 6$.
  3. For equal roots, $D = b^2 - 4ac = 0$: $$(-2k)^2 - 4(k)(6) = 0$$ $$4k^2 - 24k = 0 \implies 4k(k - 6) = 0$$
  4. Since $k$ cannot be $0$ (otherwise it would not be a quadratic equation), we have $k = 6$.

Examiner Trap: Picking $k = 0$! If $k = 0$, the equation becomes $6 = 0$, which is absurd.

Type 6: Symmetric Expressions of Roots ($\alpha^2 + \beta^2$) Archetype 6
Q6
If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the roots of the quadratic equation $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$, what is the value of $\alpha^2 + \beta^2$?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $13$

Method 1: Vieta's Formulae

$$\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a} = 5, \qquad \alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a} = 6$$ $$\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha\beta = 5^2 - 2(6) = 25 - 12 = 13$$

Method 2: Factoring (When Factors are Obvious)

$$x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3) = 0 \implies \alpha = 2, \beta = 3$$ $$\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = 2^2 + 3^2 = 4 + 9 = 13$$
Type 7: Condition for Reciprocal Roots ($c = a$) Archetype 7
Q7
If one root of the quadratic equation $5x^2 + 13x + k = 0$ is the reciprocal of the other, what is the value of $k$?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $k = 5$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Let the roots be $\alpha$ and $\frac{1}{\alpha}$.
  2. The product of roots is $\alpha \times \frac{1}{\alpha} = 1$.
  3. From Vieta's formula, product of roots $= \frac{c}{a}$: $$\frac{k}{5} = 1 \implies k = 5$$

Top-Rank Speed Hack: Whenever one root is the reciprocal of the other, directly equate the constant term to the leading coefficient: $c = a \implies k = 5$. Solved in 2 seconds!

Type 8: Constructing Quadratic Equation from Roots Archetype 8
Q8
Find the quadratic equation with rational coefficients whose one root is $3 + \sqrt{5}$.
Correct Answer: Option (B) $x^2 - 6x + 4 = 0$

Step-by-Step Solution (Conjugate Surd Theorem):

  1. Irrational roots of a polynomial with rational coefficients always occur in conjugate pairs.
  2. If $\alpha = 3 + \sqrt{5}$, then the other root must be $\beta = 3 - \sqrt{5}$.
  3. $\text{Sum of roots } S = \alpha + \beta = (3 + \sqrt{5}) + (3 - \sqrt{5}) = 6$.
  4. $\text{Product of roots } P = \alpha\beta = (3 + \sqrt{5})(3 - \sqrt{5}) = 3^2 - (\sqrt{5})^2 = 9 - 5 = 4$.
  5. Standard quadratic equation form: $$x^2 - Sx + P = 0 \implies x^2 - 6x + 4 = 0$$
Type 9: Equal in Magnitude but Opposite in Sign ($b = 0$) Archetype 9
Q9
For what value of $p$ are the roots of the quadratic equation $3x^2 + (p^2 - 16)x - 7 = 0$ equal in magnitude but opposite in sign?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $p = \pm 4$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Let roots be $\alpha$ and $-\alpha$.
  2. Sum of roots $= \alpha + (-\alpha) = 0$.
  3. From Vieta's formula: $\text{Sum} = -\frac{b}{a} = -\frac{p^2 - 16}{3} = 0$.
  4. Therefore: $p^2 - 16 = 0 \implies p^2 = 16 \implies p = \pm 4$.

Top-Rank Speed Rule: Equal and opposite roots $\implies$ Middle coefficient $b = 0$ immediately!

Type 10: Word Problem Reducing to Quadratic ($x + 1/x = \text{Fraction}$) Archetype 10
Q10
The sum of a number and its reciprocal is $\frac{13}{6}$. What is the number?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $\frac{2}{3}\text{ or }\frac{3}{2}$

Method 1: Step-by-Step Quadratic Formulation

$$x + \frac{1}{x} = \frac{13}{6} \implies \frac{x^2 + 1}{x} = \frac{13}{6}$$ $$6(x^2 + 1) = 13x \implies 6x^2 - 13x + 6 = 0$$

Split the middle term ($6 \times 6 = 36 \implies -9$ and $-4$):

$$6x^2 - 9x - 4x + 6 = 0 \implies 3x(2x - 3) - 2(2x - 3) = 0$$ $$(2x - 3)(3x - 2) = 0 \implies x = \frac{3}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad x = \frac{2}{3}$$

Method 2: Top-Rank Exam Speed Hack (Decomposition)

$$\frac{13}{6} = \frac{4 + 9}{6} = \frac{4}{6} + \frac{9}{6} = \frac{2}{3} + \frac{3}{2}$$

Notice that $\frac{3}{2}$ is the exact reciprocal of $\frac{2}{3}$! Direct answer without writing any quadratic equation!
