1. Fundamental Axioms, Definitions & Foundational Properties

Definition of Cube: The cube of a number $x$ is defined as $x^3 = x \times x \times x$. If $y = x^3$, then $x$ is the cube root of $y$, denoted as $\sqrt[3]{y} = y^{\frac{1}{3}}$.
Sign Preservation Property: Unlike square roots where negative numbers yield non-real numbers in real domain, cube roots preserve the sign: $\sqrt[3]{-x} = -\sqrt[3]{x}$. For example, $\sqrt[3]{-125} = -5$.
Multiplicative Property: The cube root of a product equals the product of their individual cube roots: $\sqrt[3]{a \times b} = \sqrt[3]{a} \times \sqrt[3]{b}$, provided $a$ and $b$ are real numbers.
Fractional Property: The cube root of a quotient equals the quotient of their individual cube roots: $\sqrt[3]{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt[3]{a}}{\sqrt[3]{b}}$, where $b \neq 0$.
Exam Pro-Tip: Memorize all cube values from $1^3$ to $15^3$ before entering the exam hall to save critical calculation minutes.
Memory Mnemonic: CUBE — Cubes Unlock Basic Exponents.

Prime Factorization Algorithm: Step 1: Find prime factors of given number $N$. Step 2: Group factors in triplets of identical primes. Step 3: If every prime factor appears in a multiple of 3 times, $N$ is a perfect cube.
Estimation Algorithm: Step 1: Group digits in triplets from right. Step 2: Determine units digit of cube root using the last digit of the first block. Step 3: Determine tens digit by finding which two consecutive cubes the second block lies between.
Smallest Multiplier Theorem: To find the smallest number $m$ by which $N$ must be multiplied or divided to make it a perfect cube, factorize $N$ and multiply/divide by leftover primes needed to complete triplets.
15-Second Speed-Math Trick: For estimating $\sqrt[3]{17576}$, split into $(17)$ and $(576)$. Right block $576$ ends in $6$, so unit digit is $6$. Left block $17$ lies between $2^3=8$ and $3^3=27$, so tens digit is $2$. Result is $26$.

3. Word Problem Modeling & Practical Applications

Volume Problem Modeling: If a metallic or wooden solid cube has volume $V$, its edge length $s$ is given by $s = \sqrt[3]{V}$. Essential for SCERT Class 8 mensuration word problems.
Scaling Cubical Containers: When scaling dimensions of similar containers by factor $k$, volume scales by factor $k^3$. Conversely, finding original dimensions from volume ratio requires taking the cube root.
Prime Factorization Word Problems: Finding the minimum number of identical small cubes of side $a$ required to build a large solid cube of volume $V = s^3$, where $s$ is a multiple of $a$.
Compound Growth / Population Models: Problems where quantities grow cubically over three equal time intervals $t$, requiring cube root extraction to find the per-period growth factor.
UP Exam High-Yield Tip: In word problems involving volume of cubical boxes or tanks, always verify if units are consistent (e.g., converting liters to cubic centimeters where $1 \text{ litre} = 1000 \text{ cm}^3$) before extracting cube roots.

4. Mathematical Edge Cases, Sign Conventions & Common Traps

Critical Distinction: $\sqrt[3]{x^3} = x$ for all real numbers $x$, whereas $\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$. Cube roots do not require absolute value bars because signs are preserved.
Edge Case / Exception: Zero has a unique cube root: $\sqrt[3]{0} = 0$. Unity has a cube root of $1$ and two complex cube roots ($\frac{-1 \pm i\sqrt{3}}{2}$) in the complex domain.
Common Calculation Trap: Confusing square root estimation (grouping in pairs of 2 digits) with cube root estimation (grouping in triplets of 3 digits from right).
Trap Prevention Trick: Always double-check your estimated cube root by performing a quick digital root check or testing the last two digits of the cube.
Memory Mnemonic: TRIPLET — Three Repeats In Prime List Ensures Triplet.

Question 1 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

1. What is the exact value of $\sqrt[3]{175616}$?

Correct Answer: Option B

Using estimation or prime factorization: Split $175616$ into blocks $(175)$ and $(616)$. The right block $616$ ends in $6$, so the unit digit of the cube root is $6$. The left block $175$ lies between $5^3 = 125$ and $6^3 = 216$, so the tens digit is $5$. Thus, the cube root is $56$. Verifying: $56^3 = 175616$. Options like $54^3 = 157464$ and $64^3 = 262144$ are common distractor errors.

Question 2 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

2. Find the smallest positive integer by which $3920$ must be multiplied to make the product a perfect cube.

Correct Answer: Option C

Prime factorize 3920=2^4 x 5 x 7^2. To make every exponent a multiple of 3, multiply by 2^2 x 5^2 x 7 = 700. Then 3920 x 700 = 140^3, so the answer is Option C.

Question 3 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

3. Evaluate: $\sqrt[3]{0.000216}$

Correct Answer: Option A

Convert decimal to fraction: $\sqrt[3]{\frac{216}{1000000}}$. Since $\sqrt[3]{216} = 6$ and $\sqrt[3]{1000000} = 100$, the result is $\frac{6}{100} = 0.06$. Distractors like $0.6$ ($\sqrt[3]{0.216}$) and $0.006$ ($0.000216$) arise from incorrect decimal place counting.

Question 4 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

4. If $\sqrt[3]{x} = 15$, what is the value of $\sqrt{x}$?

Correct Answer: Option A

Cubing both sides gives x=15^3=3375. Since 3375=225 x 15, its square root is sqrt(225 x 15)=15*sqrt(15). The answer is Option A.

Question 5 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

5. The volume of a cubical box is $91125 \text{ cm}^3$. Find the length of each side of the box.

Correct Answer: Option B

Side length $s = \sqrt[3]{91125}$. Split into blocks $(91)$ and $(125)$. Right block $125$ ends in $5$, so unit digit is $5$. Left block $91$ lies between $4^3 = 64$ and $5^3 = 125$, so tens digit is $4$. Therefore, $s = 45 \text{ cm}$. Verification: $45^3 = 91125$.

Question 6 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

6. Evaluate the expression: $\sqrt[3]{729} + \sqrt[3]{5832} - \sqrt[3]{2744}$

Correct Answer: Option A

The cube roots are 9, 18, and 14. Therefore, 9+18-14=27-14=13. The answer is Option A.

Question 7 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

7. What is the cube root of $-2197$?

Correct Answer: Option A

By negative sign property of cube roots, $\sqrt[3]{-2197} = -\sqrt[3]{2197}$. For $2197$, split into $(2)$ and $(197)$. Right block ends in $7$, so unit digit is $3$. Left block $2$ lies between $1^3=1$ and $2^3=8$, so tens digit is $1$. Thus $\sqrt[3]{2197} = 13$, and $\sqrt[3]{-2197} = -13$.

Question 8 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

8. If three numbers are in the ratio $1 : 2 : 3$ and the sum of their cubes is $4500$, find the numbers.

Correct Answer: Option B

Let the numbers be $x$, $2x$, $3x$. Sum of their cubes: $x^3 + (2x)^3 + (3x)^3 = x^3 + 8x^3 + 27x^3 = 36x^3$. Given $36x^3 = 4500$. Divide by $36$: $x^3 = \frac{4500}{36} = 125$. Taking cube root: $x = \sqrt[3]{125} = 5$. The numbers are $5(1) = 5$, $5(2) = 10$, $5(3) = 15$. Verification: $5^3 + 10^3 + 15^3 = 125 + 1000 + 3375 = 4500$.

Question 9 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

9. Which of the following numbers is NOT a perfect cube?

Correct Answer: Option D

Testing option 3: $24300 = 243 \times 100 = 3^5 \times 10^2 = 3^5 \times 2^2 \times 5^2$. Since the prime exponents $5, 2, 2$ are not multiples of $3$, $24300$ is not a perfect cube. Options 4096 ($16^3$), 13824 ($24^3$), and 35937 ($33^3$) have prime factors with exponents that are multiples of $3$.

Question 10 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

10. Evaluate: $\sqrt[3]{1.331} \times \sqrt[3]{1000}$

Correct Answer: Option B

Using properties of cube roots: $\sqrt[3]{1.331} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{1331}{1000}} = \frac{11}{10} = 1.1$. Also, $\sqrt[3]{1000} = 10$. Multiplying them: $1.1 \times 10 = 11$. Alternatively, $\sqrt[3]{1.331 \times 1000} = \sqrt[3]{1331} = 11$.

The cube of any positive integer is positive, and the cube of any negative integer is negative.

A number ending in digits 0, 1, 4, 5, 6, 9 has cubes ending in the same respective digits; 2 ends in 8, 8 ends in 2, 3 ends in 7, and 7 ends in 3.
#3
Prime factorization requires grouping prime factors into triplets; if any factor lacks a triplet, the number is not a perfect cube.
#4
Estimation method uses 3-digit grouping from right to left for rapid cube root extraction of large perfect cubes.
#5

#6
