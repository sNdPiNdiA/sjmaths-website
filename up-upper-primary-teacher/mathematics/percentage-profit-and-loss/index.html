9.2 Successive Percentage Change & Price-Consumption Invariance

1. The Net Percentage Change Formula

If a quantity is increased or decreased successively by $a\%$ and then by $b\%$, the net effective percentage change is:

$$\text{Net Change } \% = a + b + \frac{ab}{100}$$

Sign Rule: Use $+a$ or $+b$ for percentage increase/profit, and $-a$ or $-b$ for percentage decrease/loss/discount.

2. Price $\times$ Consumption $=$ Expenditure Invariance

When the price of an article changes, but the total household expenditure remains constant:

$$\text{Price} \times \text{Consumption} = \text{Expenditure (Constant)}$$
  • If price increases by $r\%$, consumption must decrease by: $$\text{Decrease } \% = \left(\frac{r}{100 + r} \times 100\right)\%$$
  • If price decreases by $r\%$, consumption can increase by: $$\text{Increase } \% = \left(\frac{r}{100 - r} \times 100\right)\%$$
Worked Example: Price-Consumption Application

Problem: The price of sugar rises by $25\%$. By what percentage must a family reduce its sugar consumption so that expenditure on sugar does not increase?

Method 1: Direct Formula:

$$\text{Reduction } \% = \frac{25}{100 + 25} \times 100\% = \frac{25}{125} \times 100\% = \frac{1}{5} \times 100\% = 20\%$$

Method 2: Fractional Top-Rank Hack:

$25\% = \frac{1}{4}$ increase. In fractional invariance, if price increases by $\frac{1}{x}$, consumption decreases by $\frac{1}{x+1}$. Here $x=4 \implies \frac{1}{4+1} = \frac{1}{5} = 20\%$. Solved mentally in 5 seconds!

9.3 Profit and Loss: Fundamental Relationships & Faulty Weights

Profit or Loss is ALWAYS calculated on the Cost Price (CP) unless explicitly stated otherwise by the examiner.

Concept Standard Formula Speed Multiplier Form
Profit (Gain) $\text{Profit} = SP - CP$ $\text{Profit } \% = \frac{SP - CP}{CP} \times 100\%$
Loss $\text{Loss} = CP - SP$ $\text{Loss } \% = \frac{CP - SP}{CP} \times 100\%$
Selling Price ($SP$) $$SP = CP \times \left(\frac{100 \pm P/L\%}{100}\right)$$ If Profit $= 20\% \implies SP = 1.20 \times CP$
If Loss $= 15\% \implies SP = 0.85 \times CP$
Cost Price ($CP$) $$CP = \frac{SP \times 100}{100 \pm P/L\%}$$ $CP = \frac{SP}{\text{Multiplier}}$

Classical Rule: Selling Two Articles at Equal SP

When two articles are sold at the same selling price ($SP_1 = SP_2$), one at a profit of $x\%$ and the other at a loss of $x\%$, the transaction always results in a net loss:

$$\text{Net Loss } \% = \left(\frac{x}{10}\right)^2 \% = \frac{x^2}{100}\%$$

Dishonest Merchant & False Weight Models

When a trader professes to sell his goods at cost price but uses a false weight of $w\text{ grams}$ instead of the true weight $T = 1000\text{ grams}$:

$$\text{Gain } \% = \left(\frac{\text{Error}}{\text{True Value} - \text{Error}}\right) \times 100\% = \left(\frac{\text{Error}}{\text{False Weight}}\right) \times 100\%$$

9.4 Marked Price, Discount & The Golden MP-CP Formula

Discount is ALWAYS calculated on the Marked Price (MP) (also called List Price or Printed Price):

$$\text{Discount} = MP - SP, \quad \text{Discount } \% = \frac{\text{Discount}}{MP} \times 100\%$$ $$SP = MP \times \left(\frac{100 - D\%}{100}\right)$$

Successive Discounts Equivalent Formula

Two successive discounts of $d_1\%$ and $d_2\%$ are equivalent to a single discount of:

$$D_{\text{eq}} = \left(d_1 + d_2 - \frac{d_1 d_2}{100}\right)\%$$
The Golden Commercial Formula (Connecting MP, CP, Profit%, Discount%): $$\frac{MP}{CP} = \frac{100 + \text{Profit } \%}{100 - \text{Discount } \%}$$

This formula bypasses the calculation of Selling Price completely, solving marked-price problems in one line!

Examination-Grade Practice MCQs (10 Type-Wise Archetypes)

Type 1: Price, Consumption & Expenditure Invariance Archetype 1
Q1
A reduction of $20\%$ in the price of rice enables a customer to obtain $4\text{ kg}$ more for ₹$160$. What was the original (initial) price of rice per kg?
Correct Answer: Option (B) ₹10 per kg

Method 1: Standard Algebraic Method:

  1. Money saved due to $20\%$ price reduction on ₹$160$: $$\text{Savings} = 20\% \text{ of } 160 = \frac{20}{100} \times 160 = ₹32$$
  2. This ₹$32$ buys the extra $4\text{ kg}$ of rice at the reduced price: $$\text{Reduced Price} = \frac{32}{4} = ₹8\text{ per kg}$$
  3. Since the reduced price is $80\%$ of original price ($CP_0$): $$0.80 \times CP_0 = 8 \implies CP_0 = \frac{8}{0.80} = ₹10\text{ per kg}$$
Top-Rank Speed Hack: $$\text{Original Price} = \frac{\text{Amount} \times \text{Percentage}}{(\text{Quantity}) \times (100 - \text{Percentage})}$$ $$\text{Original Price} = \frac{160 \times 20}{4 \times 80} = \frac{3200}{320} = ₹10\text{ per kg}$$ Done in 10 seconds!
Type 2: Examination Passing Marks & Score Deficits Archetype 2
Q2
In an examination, a candidate must secure $40\%$ marks to pass. A student scores $220$ marks and fails by $20$ marks. What are the maximum total marks of the examination?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $600$ marks

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Passing Marks required $= 220 + 20 = 240\text{ marks}$.
  2. This passing score represents $40\%$ of the maximum marks ($M$): $$40\% \text{ of } M = 240 \implies \frac{40}{100} M = 240$$
  3. Solve for $M$: $$M = \frac{240 \times 100}{40} = 6 \times 100 = 600\text{ marks}$$
Speed Hack: $40\% = 240 \implies 10\% = 60 \implies 100\% = 600$. Solved mentally in 5 seconds!
Type 3: Equal Selling Price with Equal Gain and Loss % Archetype 3
Q3
A shopkeeper sells two bicycles for ₹$4,800$ each. On one he gains $20\%$ and on the other he loses $20\%$. What is his net gain or loss percentage, and what is the absolute loss in rupees on the entire transaction?
Correct Answer: Option (C) $4\%$ loss, ₹400 loss

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Apply the Equal SP Theorem: $$\text{Net Loss } \% = \frac{x^2}{100} = \frac{20^2}{100} = \frac{400}{100} = 4\%$$
  2. Total Selling Price $= 4800 + 4800 = ₹9,600$.
  3. Since there is a $4\%$ loss, this total $SP$ represents $96\%$ of Total $CP$: $$\text{Total } CP = \frac{9600}{0.96} = ₹10,000$$
  4. $\text{Absolute Loss} = \text{Total } CP - \text{Total } SP = 10000 - 9600 = ₹400$.
Critical Exam Trap: Many candidates calculate $4\%$ of the Selling Price ($4\% \text{ of } 9600 = ₹384$) and incorrectly choose Option B! Loss is ALWAYS a percentage of the Cost Price ($4\% \text{ of } 10000 = ₹400$).
Type 4: CP of $m$ Articles Equals SP of $n$ Articles Archetype 4
Q4
If the cost price of $15$ pens is equal to the selling price of $12$ pens, what is the profit percentage?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $25\%$

Method 1: Ratio of CP and SP:

  1. Given: $15 \times CP = 12 \times SP$.
  2. Rearrange as a ratio: $$\frac{SP}{CP} = \frac{15}{12} = \frac{5}{4}$$
  3. Let $CP = 4\text{ units}$, then $SP = 5\text{ units} \implies \text{Profit} = 5 - 4 = 1\text{ unit}$.
  4. $$\text{Profit } \% = \frac{\text{Profit}}{CP} \times 100\% = \frac{1}{4} \times 100\% = 25\%$$
5-Second Formula: $$\text{Gain } \% = \frac{m - n}{n} \times 100\% = \frac{15 - 12}{12} \times 100\% = \frac{3}{12} \times 100\% = 25\%$$
Type 5: Dishonest Merchant & Faulty Weights Archetype 5
Q5
A dishonest dealer claims to sell his goods at cost price, but uses a false weight of $900\text{ grams}$ instead of a $1\text{ kg}$ weight. What is his actual gain percentage?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $11\frac{1}{9}\%$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. True Weight $= 1000\text{ g}$, False Weight $= 900\text{ g}$.
  2. $\text{Error} = 1000 - 900 = 100\text{ g}$.
  3. The trader's actual investment (outflow) is only the cost of $900\text{ g}$, while he collects money for $1000\text{ g}$: $$\text{Gain } \% = \frac{\text{Error}}{\text{False Weight}} \times 100\% = \frac{100}{900} \times 100\% = \frac{1}{9} \times 100\% = 11\frac{1}{9}\%$$
Common Mistake: Dividing by $1000$ ($\frac{100}{1000} \times 100 = 10\%$). The merchant did NOT part with $1000\text{ g}$ of goods, only $900\text{ g}$! The cost incurred is for $900\text{ g}$.
Type 6: Successive Discounts & Equivalent Single Discount Archetype 6
Q6
What is the single discount equivalent to three successive discounts of $20\%, 10\%,$ and $5\%$?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $31.6\%$

Method 1: Formula Step-by-Step:

  1. First combine $20\%$ and $10\%$: $$D_{12} = 20 + 10 - \frac{20 \times 10}{100} = 30 - 2 = 28\%$$
  2. Now combine $28\%$ and $5\%$: $$D_{\text{eq}} = 28 + 5 - \frac{28 \times 5}{100} = 33 - 1.4 = 31.6\%$$
100-Base Top-Rank Hack: Start with $100$: $$100 \xrightarrow{-20\%} 80 \xrightarrow{-10\%} 72 \xrightarrow{-5\%} 72 - 3.6 = 68.4$$ $$\text{Single Discount} = 100 - 68.4 = 31.6\%$$
Type 7: The Golden MP-CP Formula Archetype 7
Q7
A merchant allows a discount of $10\%$ on the marked price of an item and still makes a profit of $17\%$. By what percentage above the cost price did he mark his goods?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $30\%$

Applying the Golden Ratio Formula:

$$\frac{MP}{CP} = \frac{100 + P\%}{100 - D\%} = \frac{100 + 17}{100 - 10} = \frac{117}{90}$$
  1. Simplify $\frac{117}{90}$: $$\frac{117 \div 9}{90 \div 9} = \frac{13}{10}$$
  2. Let $CP = 10$, then $MP = 13$. Markup $= 13 - 10 = 3\text{ units}$.
  3. $$\text{Markup } \% = \frac{3}{10} \times 100\% = 30\%$$
10-Second Calculation: $\frac{117}{90} = 1.30 \implies 30\%$ above CP! No equations needed.
Type 8: Voting & Two-Candidate Election Models Archetype 8
Q8
In an election between two candidates, $10\%$ of the voters did not cast their votes. $10\%$ of the votes polled were declared invalid. The winning candidate secured $54\%$ of the valid votes and won by a majority of $1,620$ votes. Find the total number of voters enrolled in the voters' list.
Correct Answer: Option (B) $25,000$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Let total voters enrolled $= V$.
  2. Votes polled $= 90\% \text{ of } V = 0.90V$.
  3. Valid votes $= 90\% \text{ of polled} = 0.90 \times 0.90V = 0.81V$.
  4. Winning candidate received $54\%$ of valid votes $\implies$ Losing candidate received $46\%$. $$\text{Winning Margin} = 54\% - 46\% = 8\% \text{ of valid votes}$$
  5. Set up equation: $$\frac{8}{100} \times (0.81V) = 1620$$ $$0.0648 V = 1620 \implies V = \frac{1620}{0.0648} = 25,000$$
Fraction Chain Top-Rank Hack: $$V \times \frac{9}{10} \times \frac{9}{10} \times \frac{8}{100} = 1620$$ $$V \times \frac{81 \times 8}{10000} = 1620 \implies V = \frac{1620 \times 10000}{81 \times 8} = \frac{20 \times 10000}{8} = 25,000$$
Type 9: "Buy X, Get Y Free" Discount Calculations Archetype 9
Q9
A shopkeeper displays an offer: "Buy 5 shirts, Get 3 shirts Free". What is the effective discount percentage given to the customer?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $37.5\%$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Let the Marked Price of each shirt be ₹$10$.
  2. Customer takes home $5 + 3 = 8\text{ shirts}$. Total Marked Price $= 8 \times 10 = ₹80$.
  3. Customer pays only for $5\text{ shirts} \implies SP = 5 \times 10 = ₹50$.
  4. $\text{Discount amount} = 80 - 50 = ₹30$.
  5. $$\text{Discount } \% = \frac{\text{Discount}}{MP} \times 100\% = \frac{30}{80} \times 100\% = \frac{3}{8} \times 100\% = 37.5\%$$
Golden Rule: $$\text{Discount } \% = \frac{\text{Free Articles}}{\text{Total Articles}} \times 100\% = \frac{3}{5 + 3} \times 100\% = \frac{3}{8} \times 100\% = 37.5\%$$
Type 10: Profit Calculation On Selling Price Distractor Trap Archetype 10
Q10
A trader calculates his percentage profit on the Selling Price and finds it to be $20\%$. What is his actual profit percentage calculated on Cost Price?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $25\%$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Let $SP = ₹100$.
  2. $\text{Profit} = 20\% \text{ of } SP = ₹20$.
  3. $$CP = SP - \text{Profit} = 100 - 20 = ₹80$$
  4. Actual profit percentage on Cost Price: $$\text{Actual Profit } \% = \frac{\text{Profit}}{CP} \times 100\% = \frac{20}{80} \times 100\% = \frac{1}{4} \times 100\% = 25\%$$
Fraction Shift: Profit on $SP = \frac{1}{5} \implies \text{Profit on } CP = \frac{1}{5-1} = \frac{1}{4} = 25\%$.
