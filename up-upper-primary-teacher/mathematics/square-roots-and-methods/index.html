1. Fundamental Axioms, Definitions & Foundational Properties

Definition of Square Root: If $x^2 = y$ where $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^+$, then $x$ is called the square root of $y$, denoted as $\sqrt{y} = x$. The radical sign $\sqrt{\phantom{x}}$ denotes the principal (non-negative) square root.
Multiplicative Property of Radicals: For any non-negative real numbers $a$ and $b$, $\sqrt{a \times b} = \sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{b}$. This fundamental axiom enables the simplification of large composite numbers during prime factorization.
Divisive Property of Radicals: For $a \ge 0$ and $b > 0$, $\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}$. This property is essential when calculating square roots of fractions and terminating rational numbers.
Unit Digit Property: A perfect square can only end in digits $0, 1, 4, 5, 6,$ or $9$ in base $10$. Numbers ending in $2, 3, 7,$ or $8$ can never be integers when square rooted, which acts as a preliminary check to eliminate incorrect options.
Exam Pro-Tip: Always check the units digit before performing long division. If a number ends in $2, 3, 7,$ or $8$, its square root is irrational or non-terminating decimal unless modified by algebraic fractions in the question.
Memory Mnemonic: SQUARE — Sign positive, Question units, Unify factors, Always pair, Root exact, Examine remainder.

Prime Factorization Algorithm: Express the composite number $N$ as a product of prime powers: $N = 2^{a} \cdot 3^{b} \cdot 5^{c} \dots$. Ensure all exponents are even numbers. If an exponent is odd, the number is not a perfect square, requiring multiplication or division by the deficient prime to make it a perfect square.
Long Division Algorithm (Integer Part): Group digits in pairs from the decimal point moving leftwards (e.g., $1024 \to 10\;24$). Find the greatest integer $x$ whose square is less than or equal to the leftmost pair ($3^2 = 9 \le 10$). Subtract, bring down the next pair, double the quotient for the trial divisor $2x$, and find digit $y$ such that $(20x + y)y \le ext{dividend}$.
Long Division Algorithm (Decimal Part): For decimal numbers like $\sqrt{5.184}$, group digits in pairs after the decimal point from left to right, appending zeros if necessary ($5.18\;40$). Place the decimal point in the quotient exactly when the decimal point in the dividend is crossed.
15-Second Speed-Math Trick: To find the square root of a 4-digit perfect square like $7569$, look at the last digit ($9 \implies 3 \text{ or } 7$). Look at the leading block ($75 o 8^2 = 64 < 75 < 81 o 8$). Test $83 \times 83$ vs $87 \times 87$. Since $85^2 = 7225 < 7569$, the answer is $87$.

3. Word Problem Modeling & Practical Applications

Arrangement in Rows and Columns: If $N$ students or plants are arranged such that the number of rows equals the number of columns, the total number of items is $x^2$. To find items per row, calculate $\sqrt{N}$. If items are left over, subtract the remainder first before taking the square root.
Minimum Addition/Subtraction for Perfect Square: Given non-square number $N$, find smallest integer $k$ to add/subtract so that $N \pm k$ becomes a perfect square. Use long division; if remainder $r \neq 0$, subtraction gives $N - r$. For addition, find next integer square $(q+1)^2 - N$.
SCERT UP Textbook Model: A gardener has $2025$ plants. He wants to plant them in such a way that the number of rows and columns are equal. Find the number of rows. Solution: $\sqrt{2025} = \sqrt{3^4 \cdot 5^2} = 3^2 \cdot 5 = 45$ rows.
Fractional Scaling Problems: If the area of a square field is given as a mixed fraction like $52\frac{1}{16} \text{ m}^2$, convert to improper fraction $\frac{833}{16}$ or use decimal conversion before applying square root algorithms.
UP Exam High-Yield Tip: In questions stating 'least number to be multiplied/divided to make a perfect square', always use Prime Factorization. In questions stating 'least number to be added/subtracted', always use Long Division.

4. Mathematical Edge Cases, Sign Conventions & Common Traps

Critical Distinction: The radical symbol $\sqrt{x}$ strictly represents the principal (positive) square root. Thus, $\sqrt{16} = +4$, NOT $\pm 4$. However, if $x^2 = 16$, then $x = \pm \sqrt{16} = \pm 4$. Distinguish carefully between evaluating a radical and solving a quadratic equation.
Edge Case of Zero and One: $\sqrt{0} = 0$, $\sqrt{1} = 1$. For numbers between $0$ and $1$ (e.g., $0.25$), the square root is greater than the number itself: $\sqrt{0.25} = 0.5 > 0.25$. This counter-intuitive property is a favorite trap in Super TET.
Common Calculation Trap: Confusing $\sqrt{a + b}$ with $\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$. Note that $\sqrt{a + b} \neq \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$. For example, $\sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5$, whereas $\sqrt{9} + \sqrt{16} = 3 + 4 = 7$.
Trap Prevention Trick: When simplifying nested square roots like $\sqrt{7 + \sqrt{48}}$, express $48$ as $2 \cdot \sqrt{12} \cdot \sqrt{4}$ or recognize $(a+b)^2 = a^2+b^2+2ab$ to convert the inner expression into a binomial square $(2 + \sqrt{3})^2$.
Memory Mnemonic: ROOT — Radical is positive, Only pairs get out, Order pairs from right, Test decimals right.

Question 1 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

1. What is the least number by which $1470$ must be divided so that the quotient is a perfect square?

Correct Answer: Option A

Step 1: Perform prime factorization of $1470$. $1470 = 147 \times 10 = (3 \times 7^2) \times (2 \times 5) = 2^1 \times 3^1 \times 5^1 \times 7^2$. Step 2: Identify primes with odd exponents ($2^1, 3^1, 5^1$). Step 3: To make the quotient a perfect square, divide by the product of terms with odd exponents: $2 \times 3 \times 5 = 30$. Thus, dividing by $30$ leaves $7^2$, which is a perfect square.

Question 2 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

2. Evaluate $\sqrt{0.0009} + \sqrt{0.04}$:

Correct Answer: Option A

Step 1: Evaluate the first term: $\sqrt{0.0009} = \sqrt{\frac{9}{10000}} = \frac{3}{100} = 0.03$. Step 2: Evaluate the second term: $\sqrt{0.04} = \sqrt{\frac{4}{100}} = \frac{2}{10} = 0.20$. Step 3: Add both values: $0.03 + 0.20 = 0.23$. Distractor $0.07$ arises from incorrect decimal placement ($\sqrt{0.04} = 0.02$). Higher accuracy ensures zero negative marking.

Question 3 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

3. What is the greatest 4-digit number that is a perfect square?

Correct Answer: Option B

Step 1: The greatest 4-digit number is $9999$. Step 2: Apply long division to find $\sqrt{9999}$. Pairing gives $99\;99$. $9 \times 9 = 81$, remainder $18$. Bring down $99$ to get $1899$. Trial divisor $18y \times y \le 1899 \implies 189 \times 9 = 1701$. Remainder is $198$. Step 3: Subtract remainder from $9999$: $9999 - 198 = 9801$. Step 4: Verify $99^2 = 9801$. Option $9801$ is correct.

Question 4 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

4. Find the square root of $11 + 2\sqrt{30}$:

Correct Answer: Option A

Step 1: Use the surd identity $\sqrt{a + b + 2\sqrt{ab}} = \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$. Step 2: Here, $a + b = 11$ and $ab = 30$. Step 3: Find two numbers whose sum is $11$ and product is $30$; these are $6$ and $5$. Step 4: Therefore, $\sqrt{11 + 2\sqrt{30}} = \sqrt{(\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{5})^2} = \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{5}$. Option 0 is correct.

Question 5 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

5. What least number must be added to $5607$ to make the sum a perfect square?

Correct Answer: Option A

Step 1: Apply long division on $56\;07$. $7 \times 7 = 49$, remainder $7$. Bring down $07$ to get $707$. Trial divisor $14y \times y \le 707 \implies 144 \times 4 = 576$. Remainder is $707 - 576 = 131$. Step 2: Since $74^2 = 5476 < 5607$, the next integer is $75$. Step 3: Calculate $75^2 = 5625$. Step 4: Number to be added = $5625 - 5607 = 18$. Option 0 is correct.

Question 6 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

6. If $\sqrt{1 + \frac{x}{144}} = \frac{13}{12}$, what is the value of $x$?

Correct Answer: Option A

Step 1: Square both sides of the equation: $1 + \frac{x}{144} = \left(\frac{13}{12}\right)^2 = \frac{169}{144}$. Step 2: Simplify: $\frac{x}{144} = \frac{169}{144} - 1 = \frac{169 - 144}{144} = \frac{25}{144}$. Step 3: Equating numerators gives $x = 25$. Option 0 is correct.

Question 7 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

7. Evaluate $\sqrt{24} + \sqrt{216} - \sqrt{96}$:

Correct Answer: Option B

Step 1: Simplify each surd by extracting square factors. $\sqrt{24} = \sqrt{4 \times 6} = 2\sqrt{6}$. Step 2: $\sqrt{216} = \sqrt{36 \times 6} = 6\sqrt{6}$. Step 3: $\sqrt{96} = \sqrt{16 \times 6} = 4\sqrt{6}$. Step 4: Combine terms: $2\sqrt{6} + 6\sqrt{6} - 4\sqrt{6} = (2 + 6 - 4)\sqrt{6} = 4\sqrt{6}$. Option 1 is correct.

Question 8 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

8. A troop of soldiers can be arranged in rows of $12, 15, 18,$ and $20$ soldiers such that each row has an equal number, and they form a solid square. What is the minimum number of soldiers in the troop?

Correct Answer: Option A

Step 1: Find the LCM of $12, 15, 18,$ and $20$. Prime factorizations: $12=2^2\cdot3, 15=3\cdot5, 18=2\cdot3^2, 20=2^2\cdot5$. $\text{LCM} = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 = 4 \times 9 \times 5 = 180$. Step 2: Prime factors of $180$ are $2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5^1$. To make it a square, exponent of $5$ must be even, so multiply by $5$. Step 3: Minimum soldiers = $180 \times 5 = 900$. Option 0 is correct.

Question 9 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

9. What is the square root of $0.9$ up to two decimal places?

Correct Answer: Option A

Step 1: Set up long division for $0.90\;00\;00$. Step 2: First digit after decimal: $9 \times 9 = 81$, remainder $9$. Bring down $00$ to get $90$. Step 3: Double quotient ($0.9 \times 2 = 1.8$, trial divisor $184 \times 4 = 736$). Remainder $164$. Bring down $00$ to get $16400$. Step 4: Trial divisor $1885 \times 5 = 9425$. Thus, $\sqrt{0.9} \approx 0.948$, which rounds to $0.95$. Option 0 is correct.

Question 10 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

10. If $x = \sqrt{5 + \sqrt{5 + \sqrt{5 + \dots \infty}}},$ then which of the following inequalities holds true for $x$?

Correct Answer: Option A

Step 1: Notice that $x = \sqrt{5 + x}$. Step 2: Square both sides: $x^2 = 5 + x \implies x^2 - x - 5 = 0$. Step 3: Using the quadratic formula, $x = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 20}}{2} = \frac{1 + \sqrt{21}}{2}$. Step 4: Since $\sqrt{21}$ lies between $4$ and $5$ (specifically $\approx 4.58$), $x = \frac{1 + 4.58}{2} = 2.79$. Thus, $2 < x < 3$. Option 0 is correct.
