21.1 The Cartesian Coordinate System (कार्तीय निर्देशांक तल)

The Four Quadrants & Distance Formula Right Triangle
Pythagorean distance: $\mathbf{d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}}$.

In a plane, two mutually perpendicular number lines intersecting at origin $(0, 0)$ form the Cartesian Plane:

  • Horizontal line $\implies$ X-axis; its coordinate is called Abscissa (भुज).
  • Vertical line $\implies$ Y-axis; its coordinate is called Ordinate (कोटि).
  • Any point on X-axis has $y = 0 \implies (x, 0)$.
  • Any point on Y-axis has $x = 0 \implies (0, y)$.
Quadrant Abscissa ($x$) Ordinate ($y$) Sign Convention Example Point
Quadrant I (प्रथम चतुर्थांश) Positive ($x > 0$) Positive ($y > 0$) $(+, +)$ $(3, 5)$
Quadrant II (द्वितीय चतुर्थांश) Negative ($x < 0$) Positive ($y > 0$) $(-, +)$ $(-4, 7)$
Quadrant III (तृतीय चतुर्थांश) Negative ($x < 0$) Negative ($y < 0$) $(-, -)$ $(-6, -2)$
Quadrant IV (चतुर्थ चतुर्थांश) Positive ($x > 0$) Negative ($y < 0$) $(+, -)$ $(5, -8)$
Perpendicular Distance Traps:

For any point $P(x, y)$:
- The perpendicular distance of $P$ from the X-axis is $\mathbf{|y|}$ (the absolute value of its ordinate).
- The perpendicular distance of $P$ from the Y-axis is $\mathbf{|x|}$ (the absolute value of its abscissa).
Example: For point $(-3, 4)$, distance from X-axis is $4$, and distance from Y-axis is $3$ (distance is never negative!).

21.2 Master Formulas: Distance, Section & Centroid

Formula Name Standard Formula Key Examination Shortcut
Distance Formula (दूरी सूत्र) $d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$ Distance from origin $(0, 0)$ is $\mathbf{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}$
Section Formula (विभाजन सूत्र) $x = \frac{m_1 x_2 + m_2 x_1}{m_1 + m_2}, \; y = \frac{m_1 y_2 + m_2 y_1}{m_1 + m_2}$ To find ratio, assume $k : 1$
Midpoint Formula (मध्य-बिंदु) $M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$ Used in finding 4th vertex of a parallelogram ($M_{AC} = M_{BD}$)
Centroid of Triangle (केंद्रक) $G = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2 + x_3}{3}, \frac{y_1 + y_2 + y_3}{3}\right)$ Average of coordinates: $\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{3}, \; \bar{y} = \frac{\sum y}{3}$
Area of Triangle & Collinearity $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}|x_1(y_2 - y_3) + x_2(y_3 - y_1) + x_3(y_1 - y_2)|$ Points are collinear $\iff \mathbf{\text{Area} = 0}$
Shortcut for Ratio of Axis Division:
  • The ratio in which the X-axis divides segment joining $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is $\mathbf{-y_1 : y_2}$.
  • The ratio in which the Y-axis divides segment joining $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is $\mathbf{-x_1 : x_2}$.

Example: In what ratio does the X-axis divide segment joining $(2, -3)$ and $(5, 6)$?
Direct formula: $-(-3) : 6 = 3 : 6 = \mathbf{1 : 2}$ internally! Solved in 3 seconds!

21.3 Reflections of a Point Across Axes & Origin (दर्पण प्रतिबिंब)

Mirror / Axis of Reflection Transformation Rule Example Point $(4, -7)$
Reflection in X-axis $(x, y) \to (x, -y)$ (Change sign of $y$) $(4, 7)$
Reflection in Y-axis $(x, y) \to (-x, y)$ (Change sign of $x$) $(-4, -7)$
Reflection in Origin $(0, 0)$ $(x, y) \to (-x, -y)$ (Change sign of both) $(-4, 7)$
Reflection in line $y = x$ $(x, y) \to (y, x)$ (Swap coordinates) $(-7, 4)$

Type 1: Point Location on Axes & Distance to Axis Archetype 1
Q1
What is the perpendicular distance of the point $P(-5, 7)$ from the X-axis?
Correct Answer: Option (C) $7$

Universal Rule:

The perpendicular distance of any point $(x, y)$ from the X-axis is equal to the absolute value of its y-coordinate (ordinate):

$$\text{Distance from X-axis} = |y| = |7| = 7$$

Distance from Y-axis is $|x| = |-5| = 5$.

Type 2: Distance Between Two Coordinates Archetype 2
Q2
What is the distance between the points $A(2, -3)$ and $B(10, 3)$?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $10$

Step-by-Step Distance Calculation:

$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$ $$d = \sqrt{(10 - 2)^2 + (3 - (-3))^2} = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{64 + 36} = \sqrt{100} = 10$$

Speed Hack: Differences are $\Delta x = 8$ and $\Delta y = 6$. Recognize the $(6, 8, 10)$ Pythagorean triplet immediately!

Type 3: Finding Unknown Parameter Given Distance Archetype 3
Q3
If the distance between the points $A(4, p)$ and $B(1, 0)$ is $5\text{ units}$, what are the possible values of $p$?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $p = \pm 4$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. $(4 - 1)^2 + (p - 0)^2 = 5^2 \implies 3^2 + p^2 = 25$.
  2. $9 + p^2 = 25 \implies p^2 = 16 \implies p = \pm 4$.

Common Trap: Forgetting the negative square root and selecting Option C ($p=4$ only)! Distance squared always yields $\pm$ roots.

Type 4: Midpoint Theorem & Parallelogram Fourth Vertex Archetype 4
Q4
If $(1, 2), (4, y), (x, 6),$ and $(3, 5)$ are the vertices of a parallelogram taken in order, find the values of $x$ and $y$.
Correct Answer: Option (B) $x = 6, \; y = 3$

Top-Rank Exam Speed Hack (Parallelogram Diagonal Balance):

In any parallelogram $ABCD$, the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ bisect each other at the same midpoint. Therefore:

$$x_A + x_C = x_B + x_D \implies 1 + x = 4 + 3 \implies 1 + x = 7 \implies x = 6$$ $$y_A + y_C = y_B + y_D \implies 2 + 6 = y + 5 \implies 8 = y + 5 \implies y = 3$$

Solved in 5 seconds without writing midpoint fractions!

Type 5: Ratio of Axis Division (Section Formula Shortcut) Archetype 5
Q5
In what ratio does the Y-axis divide the line segment joining the points $A(-4, 5)$ and $B(3, -7)$?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $4 : 3$

Top-Rank Speed Hack Formula:

The ratio in which the Y-axis divides the segment joining $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is:

$$\text{Ratio} = -\frac{x_1}{x_2} = -\frac{-4}{3} = \frac{4}{3} \implies 4 : 3 \text{ internally}$$

Solved in 2 seconds flat!

Type 6: Centroid of a Triangle Archetype 6
Q6
Two vertices of a triangle are $(3, -5)$ and $(-7, 4)$. If its centroid is at $(2, -1)$, find the coordinates of the third vertex.
Correct Answer: Option (B) $(10, -2)$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Centroid formula: $x_G = \frac{x_1 + x_2 + x_3}{3} \implies 2 = \frac{3 + (-7) + x_3}{3} \implies 6 = -4 + x_3 \implies x_3 = 10$.
  2. $y_G = \frac{y_1 + y_2 + y_3}{3} \implies -1 = \frac{-5 + 4 + y_3}{3} \implies -3 = -1 + y_3 \implies y_3 = -2$.
  3. The third vertex is $(10, -2)$.
Type 7: Area of Triangle in Coordinates Archetype 7
Q7
What is the area of the triangle whose vertices are $A(1, -1), B(-4, 6),$ and $C(-3, -5)$?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $24\text{ sq units}$

Step-by-Step Area Calculation:

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} |x_1(y_2 - y_3) + x_2(y_3 - y_1) + x_3(y_1 - y_2)|$$ $$= \frac{1}{2} |1(6 - (-5)) + (-4)(-5 - (-1)) + (-3)(-1 - 6)|$$ $$= \frac{1}{2} |1(11) + (-4)(-4) + (-3)(-7)| = \frac{1}{2} |11 + 16 + 21| = \frac{1}{2} |48| = 24\text{ sq units}$$
Type 8: Collinearity Condition of Three Points Archetype 8
Q8
For what value of $k$ are the points $A(2, 3), B(4, k),$ and $C(6, -3)$ collinear?
Correct Answer: Option (A) $k = 0$

Top-Rank Speed Hack (Slope Equating):

Three points are collinear $\iff \text{Slope of } AB = \text{Slope of } BC$:

$$\frac{k - 3}{4 - 2} = \frac{-3 - k}{6 - 4} \implies \frac{k - 3}{2} = \frac{-3 - k}{2}$$ $$k - 3 = -3 - k \implies 2k = 0 \implies k = 0$$

Notice that $4$ is the exact midpoint between $2$ and $6$, so $k$ is the average of $3$ and $-3$: $\frac{3 + (-3)}{2} = 0$! Solved in 2 seconds!

Type 9: Point Reflection Across Axes Archetype 9
Q9
What are the coordinates of the image of the point $P(-3, 5)$ after reflection across the origin followed by reflection across the X-axis?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $(3, 5)$

Step-by-Step Transformations:

  1. Start at $P(-3, 5)$.
  2. Reflection across the origin: $(x, y) \to (-x, -y) \implies P'(-(-3), -5) = (3, -5)$.
  3. Reflection across the X-axis: $(x, y) \to (x, -y) \implies P''(3, -(-5)) = (3, 5)$.

Top-Rank Speed Hack: Reflecting across origin followed by X-axis is identical to reflection across the Y-axis: $(-3, 5) \to (3, 5)$ directly!

Type 10: Examination Trap: Equidistant Points from Coordinate Axes Archetype 10
Q10
A point $P$ lies on the line $y = -x$ in Quadrant II and is at a distance of $5\sqrt{2}\text{ units}$ from the origin. What are its coordinates?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $(-5, 5)$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. In Quadrant II, $x < 0$ and $y > 0$.
  2. Since $y = -x$, if $x = -k$ ($k > 0$), then $y = k$. The point is $(-k, k)$.
  3. Distance from origin: $\sqrt{(-k)^2 + k^2} = \sqrt{2k^2} = k\sqrt{2}$.
  4. Given distance is $5\sqrt{2} \implies k\sqrt{2} = 5\sqrt{2} \implies k = 5$.
  5. The point is $(-5, 5)$.
