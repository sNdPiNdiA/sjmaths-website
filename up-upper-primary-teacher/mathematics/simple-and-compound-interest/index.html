1. Fundamental Axioms, Definitions & Foundational Properties

Principal ($P$): The initial amount of money borrowed, lent, or invested, serving as the static base for simple interest and the dynamic base for compound interest.
Rate of Interest ($R$): The percentage charged or earned per unit time, conventionally expressed per annum ($% ext{ p.a.}$), satisfying standard proportionality laws.
Time Period ($T$ or $n$): The duration for which the principal is lent or invested, mandatorily converted into years when using standard annual percentage rates.
Amount ($A$): The total sum of the principal and the accumulated interest at the end of the specified time period, satisfying $A = P + I$.
Exam Pro-Tip: Always check if the time is given in months or days. Convert months to years by dividing by $12$ ($t = rac{ ext{months}}{12}$) and days by dividing by $365$ ($t = rac{ ext{days}}{365}$) unless specified as a leap year.
Memory Mnemonic: PRT — Principal $ imes$ Rate $ imes$ Time divided by $100$ gives Simple Interest!

2. Formulas, Standard Theorems & Algorithmic Procedures

Simple Interest (SI)$SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$$SI = P \times (R \times T\%)$
Compound Interest (CI)$A = P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^n$, $CI = A - P$Effective Rate via Successive Percentage Formula: $x + y + \frac{xy}{100}$
Simple Interest Theorem: Simple interest remains constant for every equal time interval because interest is never added to the principal.
Compound Interest Theorem: Interest is compounded annually, half-yearly, or quarterly. For half-yearly compounding, rate becomes $\frac{R}{2}$ and time becomes $2n$.
Difference Between CI and SI for 2 Years: Given by the exact algebraic formula $CI_2 - SI_2 = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$.
15-Second Speed-Math Trick: For 2 years at $10\%$ per annum, the effective compound interest rate is $10 + 10 + \frac{10 \times 10}{100} = 21\%$. Thus, $CI = 0.21P$ instantly without calculating amount!

3. Word Problem Modeling & Practical Applications

Problem Type 1 (Times of Principal): If a sum becomes $n$ times itself in $T$ years at SI, the rate is given by $R = \frac{100(n-1)}{T}$.
Problem Type 2 (Population Growth): Modeled using compound interest formula $P_n = P_0 \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^n$, where $P_0$ is initial population and $P_n$ is population after $n$ years.
Problem Type 3 (Depreciation of Machinery): Modeled using negative compound growth $V_n = V_0 \left(1 - \frac{R}{100}\right)^n$, where $V_0$ is initial cost and $V_n$ is salvage value.
Problem Type 4 (Installment Problems): Equal annual installments to clear debt under compound interest use the reverse discounting sum series $P = \frac{x}{1+r} + \frac{x}{(1+r)^2} + \dots$.
UP Exam High-Yield Tip: In questions stating "sum becomes 8 times in 3 years at compound interest", use the power template: if it becomes $x$ times in $t_1$ years, it becomes $x^k$ times in $k \times t_1$ years. Match the base powers to solve for time instantly.

4. Mathematical Edge Cases, Sign Conventions & Common Traps

Critical Distinction: Simple interest grows linearly in arithmetic progression, whereas compound interest grows exponentially in geometric progression.
Edge Case (Compounding Frequency): When compounding is quarterly, rate $R$ is divided by $4$ ($R/4$) and time $n$ is multiplied by $4$ ($4n$). Ignoring compounding frequency leads to complete calculation failure.
Common Calculation Trap: Confusing "Amount" with "Interest". Questions asking "Find the sum" require calculating Principal $P$, not just the final Amount $A$ or Interest $I$.
Trap Prevention Trick: Always underline whether the question asks for Simple Interest, Compound Interest, or the Total Amount. Write down $P$, $R$, and $T$ explicitly on your rough sheet before touching any formula.
Memory Mnemonic: PAR — Principal, Amount, Rate; always identify these three before selecting your equation!

Question 1 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

1. If a sum of money becomes 3 times itself in 5 years at simple interest, in how many years will it become 7 times itself at the same rate?

Correct Answer: Option C

At simple interest, if a sum becomes $n$ times in $t_1$ years, the time $t_2$ to become $m$ times is given by $t_2 = t_1 \times \frac{m - 1}{n - 1}$. Substituting values: $t_2 = 5 \times \frac{7 - 1}{3 - 1} = 5 \times \frac{6}{2} = 15 \text{ years}$. Distractor 10 years comes from incorrect proportional scaling ($5 \times \frac{7}{3}$). Option 2 is correct.

Question 2 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

2. What is the difference between compound interest and simple interest on ₹ $8000$ for $2$ years at $5\%$ per annum compounded annually?

Correct Answer: Option B

Using the direct difference formula for 2 years: $\text{Difference} = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$. Here $P = 8000$, $R = 5$. Difference $= 8000 \times \left(\frac{5}{100}\right)^2 = 8000 \times \frac{25}{10000} = \frac{8000 \times 1}{400} = \text{₹ } 20$. Option 1 is correct.

Question 3 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

3. At what rate percent per annum compound interest will ₹ $1000$ amount to ₹ $1331$ in $3$ years?

Correct Answer: Option C

Using the compound amount formula: $A = P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^n$. $1331 = 1000 \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^3$. $\frac{1331}{1000} = \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^3$. $\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^3 = \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^3$. Taking cube root on both sides: $\frac{11}{10} = 1 + \frac{R}{100} \implies \frac{11}{10} - 1 = \frac{R}{100} \implies \frac{1}{10} = \frac{R}{100} \implies R = 10\%$. Option 2 is correct.

Question 4 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

4. A sum is lent at compound interest compounding half-yearly. If the annual rate is $20\%$ p.a. and time is $1$ year, what is the effective annual rate of interest?

Correct Answer: Option B

For half-yearly compounding, rate per half-year $R' = \frac{20}{2} = 10\%$ and time periods $n = 1 \times 2 = 2$. Amount ratio on Re $1$ = $\left(1 + \frac{10}{100}\right)^2 = (1.1)^2 = 1.21$. Effective interest $= 1.21 - 1 = 0.21$ or $21\%$. Distractor $20\%$ is the nominal rate. Option 1 is correct.

Question 5 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

5. The population of a town increases at the rate of $5\%$ per annum. If its current population is $80,000$, what will be its population after $3$ years?

Correct Answer: Option A

Using population growth formula: $P_3 = P_0 \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^n = 80000 \left(1 + \frac{5}{100}\right)^3 = 80000 \left(\frac{21}{20}\right)^3 = 80000 \times \frac{9261}{8000} = 10 imes 9261 = 92,610$. Option 0 is correct.

Question 6 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

6. If the simple interest on a certain sum for $3$ years at $4\%$ per annum is ₹ $600$, what will be the compound interest on the same sum for the same period and rate?

Correct Answer: Option C

First find Principal $P$: $SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100} \implies 600 = \frac{P \times 4 \times 3}{100} \implies P = \frac{600 \times 100}{12} = \text{₹ } 5000$. Now calculate compound amount for 3 years at $4\%$: $A = 5000 \left(1 + \frac{4}{100}\right)^3 = 5000 \left(\frac{26}{25}\right)^3 = 5000 \times \frac{17576}{15625} = \text{₹ } 5624.32$. Thus $CI = 5624.32 - 5000 = \text{₹ } 624.32$. Option 2 is correct.

Question 7 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

7. At a certain rate of simple interest, a principal of ₹ $4000$ amounts to ₹ $5000$ in $4$ years. If the rate of interest is increased by $2\%$ p.a., what will be the new total amount?

Correct Answer: Option B

Extra simple interest due to $2\%$ increase in rate over $4$ years on ₹ $4000$ is $\Delta SI = \frac{4000 \times 2 \times 4}{100} = \text{₹ } 320$. New Amount = Old Amount + Extra Interest = $5000 + 320 = \text{₹ } 5320$. Option 1 is correct.

Question 8 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

8. What sum of money will produce ₹ $140$ as simple interest in $4$ years at $5\%$ per annum?

Correct Answer: Option C

Using $P = \frac{SI \times 100}{R \times T}$: $P = \frac{140 \times 100}{5 \times 4} = \frac{14000}{20} = \text{₹ } 700$. Option 2 is correct.

Question 9 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

9. If the difference between CI and SI on a certain sum of money for $3$ years at $10\%$ per annum is ₹ $31$, find the principal sum.

Correct Answer: Option C

Using the 3-year difference formula: $\text{Diff} = \frac{PR^2(300+R)}{100^3}$. Here $\text{Diff} = 31, R = 10$. $31 = \frac{P \times 10^2 \times (300+10)}{100^3} = \frac{P \times 100 \times 310}{1000000} = \frac{31000 P}{1000000} = \frac{31P}{1000} \implies P = \frac{31 \times 1000}{31} = \text{₹ } 1000$. Option 2 is correct.

Question 10 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

10. A sum of money invested at compound interest doubles itself in $4$ years. In how many years will it become $8$ times itself?

Correct Answer: Option C

Under compound interest, if a sum becomes $2$ times in $4$ years, it becomes $2^k$ times in $k \times 4$ years. We want it to be $8$ times, which is $2^3$. Therefore, required time $= 3 \times 4 = 12 \text{ years}$. Option 2 is correct.
