10.1 Simple Interest (SI): Fundamentals & The Times Principle

Simple Interest (साधारण ब्याज) is the interest calculated uniformly on the original principal sum throughout the loan term, regardless of the period of investment:

$$SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}, \quad A = P + SI = P\left(1 + \frac{RT}{100}\right)$$

Where:

  • $P = \text{Principal (मूलधन)}$ — the initial borrowed or invested sum.
  • $R = \text{Rate of Interest per annum (वार्षिक ब्याज दर \%)}$.
  • $T = \text{Time in years (समय वर्षों में)}$. (If months: $T = \frac{m}{12}$; if days: $T = \frac{d}{365}$).
  • $A = \text{Amount (मिश्रधन)}$ — total money returned ($P + SI$).

The Times-Principle Formulas for Simple Interest

Condition Direct Top-Rank Formula Example
A sum becomes $n$ times itself in $T$ years $$R = \frac{(n - 1) \times 100}{T}\%$$ Doubles ($n=2$) in $8$ years $\implies R = \frac{(2-1) \times 100}{8} = 12.5\%$
A sum becomes $n$ times itself at $R\%$ per annum $$T = \frac{(n - 1) \times 100}{R}\text{ years}$$ Triples ($n=3$) at $10\% \implies T = \frac{(3-1) \times 100}{10} = 20\text{ years}$
Becomes $n_1$ times in $T_1$ years, and $n_2$ times in $T_2$ years $$\frac{T_1}{T_2} = \frac{n_1 - 1}{n_2 - 1}$$ Doubles in $5$ yrs; becomes $4$ times in $T_2$:
$\frac{5}{T_2} = \frac{2-1}{4-1} = \frac{1}{3} \implies T_2 = 15\text{ years}$

10.2 Compound Interest (CI): Compounding Frequencies & The Power Rule

In Compound Interest (चक्रवृद्धि ब्याज), the interest earned at the end of each conversion period is added to the principal, so that the interest itself earns interest in all subsequent periods.

Compounding Frequency Adjusted Rate ($r$) Adjusted Time / Periods ($n$) Amount Formula
Annually (वार्षिक) $R$ $T$ $$A = P\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T$$
Semi-Annually / Half-Yearly (अर्द्धवार्षिक / छमाही) $\frac{R}{2}$ $2T$ $$A = P\left(1 + \frac{R/2}{100}\right)^{2T}$$
Quarterly (त्रैमासिक / तिमाही) $\frac{R}{4}$ $4T$ $$A = P\left(1 + \frac{R/4}{100}\right)^{4T}$$

The CI Power Multiplication Rule

If a sum of money under compound interest multiplies by a factor of $n$ in $T$ years, it will multiply by $n^k$ in $k \times T$ years:

$$\text{If } P \xrightarrow{T \text{ years}} nP \implies P \xrightarrow{k \times T \text{ years}} n^k P$$
Worked Example: CI Power Rule

Problem: A sum of money placed at compound interest doubles itself in $4$ years. In how many years will it amount to $8$ times itself at the same rate of interest?

Solution: Here factor $n = 2$ in $T = 4\text{ years}$. We want $8$ times $= 2^3 \implies k = 3$.
$$\text{Required Time} = k \times T = 3 \times 4 = 12\text{ years}$$ Solved mentally in 5 seconds!

10.3 CI vs SI Difference Shortcuts (2-Year and 3-Year Formulas)

In competitive examinations, the difference between Compound Interest and Simple Interest over 2 and 3 years is the single most frequently asked numerical pattern:

Duration Difference Formula ($D = CI - SI$) Direct Principal ($P$) Formula
2 Years ($D_2$) $$D_2 = P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$$ $$P = D_2 \times \left(\frac{100}{R}\right)^2$$
3 Years ($D_3$) $$D_3 = P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \left(\frac{300 + R}{100}\right)$$ $$D_3 = D_2 \times \left(3 + \frac{R}{100}\right)$$
Ratio $\frac{D_3}{D_2}$ $$\frac{D_3}{D_2} = \frac{300 + R}{100}$$ $$R = \left(\frac{D_3}{D_2} - 3\right) \times 100\%$$
Ratio Shortcut: If the 3-year difference is $D_3$ and 2-year difference is $D_2$, notice that $\frac{D_3}{D_2} = 3 + \frac{R}{100}$. For example, if $\frac{D_3}{D_2} = \frac{25}{8} = 3.125$, then $\frac{R}{100} = 0.125 \implies R = 12.5\%$. Immediate answer without algebra!

10.4 Equal Annual Installments (किस्त भुगतान)

Compound Interest Installments

If a debt of ₹$P$ is repaid in $n$ equal annual installments of ₹$x$ each at $R\%$ per annum compound interest:

$$P = \frac{x}{1 + \frac{R}{100}} + \frac{x}{\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^2} + \dots + \frac{x}{\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^n}$$

For $n = 2$ equal annual installments:

$$P = x \left[\frac{100}{100+R} + \left(\frac{100}{100+R}\right)^2\right]$$

Type 1: Simple Interest Times-Principle Archetype 1
Q1
A sum of money under simple interest triples itself ($3$ times) in $12$ years. In how many years will it become $5$ times itself at the same rate of simple interest?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $24\text{ years}$

Method 1: Step-by-Step Method:

  1. Let principal $= P$. Amount $= 3P \implies SI_1 = 3P - P = 2P$ in $T_1 = 12\text{ years}$.
  2. Rate $R = \frac{SI \times 100}{P \times T} = \frac{2P \times 100}{P \times 12} = \frac{200}{12} = \frac{50}{3}\%$.
  3. For amount to become $5P$: $SI_2 = 5P - P = 4P$.
  4. Time $T_2 = \frac{SI_2 \times 100}{P \times R} = \frac{4P \times 100}{P \times (50/3)} = \frac{400 \times 3}{50} = 24\text{ years}$.
Top-Rank Times Formula (5 Seconds): $$\frac{T_1}{T_2} = \frac{n_1 - 1}{n_2 - 1} \implies \frac{12}{T_2} = \frac{3 - 1}{5 - 1} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2} \implies T_2 = 12 \times 2 = 24\text{ years}$$
Type 2: Sum Split Into Equal Interest Parts Archetype 2
Q2
A sum of ₹$1,750$ is lent in two parts: the first part at $8\%$ per annum for $2$ years and the second part at $6\%$ per annum for $4$ years. If the simple interest received from both parts is equal, find the smaller part of the sum.
Correct Answer: Option (B) ₹700

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Let the two parts be $P_1$ and $P_2$. Since $SI_1 = SI_2$: $$\frac{P_1 \times 8 \times 2}{100} = \frac{P_2 \times 6 \times 4}{100} \implies 16 P_1 = 24 P_2$$
  2. Simplify the ratio: $$\frac{P_1}{P_2} = \frac{24}{16} = \frac{3}{2}$$
  3. Total parts $= 3 + 2 = 5\text{ units}$. Total sum $= ₹1,750$. $$1\text{ unit} = \frac{1750}{5} = ₹350$$
  4. Smaller part $= P_2 = 2 \times 350 = ₹700$.
Speed Hack: Direct inverse ratio of $(R_1 T_1) : (R_2 T_2) = (16) : (24) \implies P_1 : P_2 = 24 : 16 = 3 : 2$.
Smaller part $= \frac{2}{5} \times 1750 = ₹700$.
Type 3: Rate Change & Additional Interest Earned Archetype 3
Q3
A certain sum of money was lent at simple interest for $3$ years. Had it been lent at $2.5\%$ higher rate, it would have fetched ₹$540$ more. Find the principal sum.
Correct Answer: Option (C) ₹7,200

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. The extra interest of ₹$540$ in $3$ years is solely due to the $2.5\%$ per annum rate increase: $$\text{Total extra percentage} = 2.5\% \times 3 = 7.5\% \text{ of Principal}$$
  2. Set up the relation: $$7.5\% \text{ of } P = 540 \implies \frac{7.5}{100} P = 540$$
  3. Solve for $P$: $$P = \frac{540 \times 100}{7.5} = \frac{54000}{7.5} = 72 \times 100 = ₹7,200$$
5-Second Formula: $$P = \frac{\Delta SI \times 100}{\Delta R \times T} = \frac{540 \times 100}{2.5 \times 3} = \frac{54000}{7.5} = ₹7,200$$
Type 4: CI Half-Yearly Compounding Archetype 4
Q4
What is the compound interest on ₹$16,000$ at $20\%$ per annum for $1$ year, compounded half-yearly?
Correct Answer: Option (B) ₹3,360

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. For half-yearly compounding: $$\text{Rate } r = \frac{20\%}{2} = 10\% \text{ per half-year}$$ $$\text{Number of periods } n = 1 \times 2 = 2 \text{ half-years}$$
  2. Amount $A = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{100}\right)^n = 16000 \times \left(1 + \frac{10}{100}\right)^2 = 16000 \times \frac{121}{100} = 160 \times 121 = ₹19,360$.
  3. $\text{Compound Interest} = A - P = 19360 - 16000 = ₹3,360$.
Successive Percentage Hack: Two periods of $10\% \implies \text{Effective Rate} = 10 + 10 + \frac{10 \times 10}{100} = 21\%$.
$$CI = 21\% \text{ of } 16000 = 21 \times 160 = ₹3,360$$
Type 5: 2-Year CI vs SI Difference Shortcut Archetype 5
Q5
The difference between the compound interest and simple interest on a certain sum for $2$ years at $8\%$ per annum is ₹$64$. Find the principal sum.
Correct Answer: Option (C) ₹10,000

Applying the 2-Year Difference Formula:

$$D_2 = P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$$
  1. Substitute $D_2 = 64$ and $R = 8$: $$64 = P \times \left(\frac{8}{100}\right)^2 = P \times \frac{64}{10000}$$
  2. Cancel $64$ from both sides: $$1 = \frac{P}{10000} \implies P = ₹10,000$$
5-Second Mental Math: $$P = D \times \left(\frac{100}{R}\right)^2 = 64 \times \left(\frac{100}{8}\right)^2 = 64 \times \frac{10000}{64} = ₹10,000$$
Type 6: 3-Year CI vs SI Difference Shortcut Archetype 6
Q6
What is the difference between the compound interest and simple interest on a sum of ₹$12,000$ for $3$ years at $10\%$ per annum?
Correct Answer: Option (B) ₹372

Applying the 3-Year Master Difference Formula:

$$D_3 = P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \left(\frac{300 + R}{100}\right)$$
  1. Substitute $P = 12000$ and $R = 10$: $$D_3 = 12000 \times \left(\frac{10}{100}\right)^2 \times \left(\frac{300 + 10}{100}\right)$$
  2. Simplify terms: $$D_3 = 12000 \times \frac{1}{100} \times \frac{310}{100} = 120 \times 3.1 = ₹372$$
The $3A + B$ Tree Hack: Interest for 1st year $= 10\% \text{ of } 12000 = 1200$.
Interest on interest ($A$) $= 10\% \text{ of } 1200 = 120$.
Interest on $A$ ($B$) $= 10\% \text{ of } 120 = 12$.
$$D_3 = 3A + B = (3 \times 120) + 12 = 360 + 12 = ₹372$$ Takes under 10 seconds!
Type 7: The CI Power Multiplication Rule Archetype 7
Q7
A sum of money placed at compound interest doubles itself in $6$ years. In how many years will it amount to $16$ times of itself at the same rate of compound interest?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $24\text{ years}$

Applying the CI Power Rule:

$$\text{If } P \xrightarrow{T\text{ yrs}} 2P \implies P \xrightarrow{k \times T\text{ yrs}} 2^k P$$
  1. Given: $T = 6\text{ years}$ to become $2$ times.
  2. Target multiple: $16\text{ times} = 2^4 \implies k = 4$.
  3. Required Time $= k \times T = 4 \times 6 = 24\text{ years}$.
Critical SI vs CI Confusion: If this were simple interest, doubling in $6$ years would mean $16$ times takes $\frac{16-1}{2-1} \times 6 = 90$ years! In Compound Interest, money grows exponentially by powers, so $16$ times takes only $24$ years.
Type 8: Amounts in Two Successive Years Archetype 8
Q8
A sum of money invested at compound interest amounts to ₹$4,400$ at the end of the first year and to ₹$4,840$ at the end of the second year. Find the rate of interest and the principal sum.
Correct Answer: Option (B) $10\%, ₹4,000$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. In compound interest, the Amount at the end of the 1st year ($A_1$) acts as the Principal for the 2nd year.
  2. Interest earned during the 2nd year: $$\text{Interest} = A_2 - A_1 = 4840 - 4400 = ₹440$$
  3. Rate of interest: $$R = \frac{\text{Interest}}{A_1} \times 100\% = \frac{440}{4400} \times 100\% = 10\%$$
  4. Now find Principal $P$: $$A_1 = P\left(1 + \frac{10}{100}\right) \implies 4400 = 1.10 P \implies P = \frac{4400}{1.10} = ₹4,000$$
Type 9: Equal Annual Installments in Compound Interest Archetype 9
Q9
A person borrows ₹$2,100$ and agrees to repay it in $2$ equal annual installments at $10\%$ per annum compound interest. What is the value of each installment?
Correct Answer: Option (C) ₹1,210

Applying the CI Installment Formula:

$$P = \frac{x}{1 + \frac{R}{100}} + \frac{x}{\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^2}$$
  1. For $R = 10\%$, the multiplier is $\frac{11}{10}$: $$2100 = \frac{x}{11/10} + \frac{x}{(11/10)^2} = x \left(\frac{10}{11} + \frac{100}{121}\right)$$
  2. Add the fractions: $$\frac{10}{11} + \frac{100}{121} = \frac{110 + 100}{121} = \frac{210}{121}$$
  3. Solve for $x$: $$2100 = x \times \frac{210}{121} \implies x = \frac{2100 \times 121}{210} = 10 \times 121 = ₹1,210$$
Option Elimination Secret: At $10\%$, each installment must be divisible by $11^2 = 121$. Only ₹$1,210$ is a multiple of $121$! Direct answer in 3 seconds.
Type 10: Quarterly Compounding Frequency Archetype 10
Q10
What is the compound interest on ₹$10,000$ for $9$ months at $20\%$ per annum, compounded quarterly?
Correct Answer: Option (B) ₹1,576.25

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Convert parameters to quarterly scale: $$\text{Quarterly Rate } r = \frac{20\%}{4} = 5\% \text{ per quarter}$$ $$\text{Number of Quarters } n = \frac{9\text{ months}}{3\text{ months}} = 3\text{ quarters}$$
  2. Amount formula: $$A = P\left(1 + \frac{5}{100}\right)^3 = 10000 \times \left(\frac{21}{20}\right)^3 = 10000 \times \frac{9261}{8000} = \frac{92610}{8} = ₹11,576.25$$
  3. Compound Interest: $$CI = A - P = 11576.25 - 10000 = ₹1,576.25$$
