Mastering equivalent ratios, continued proportions, and proportional distribution is crucial to achieve a top rank.

1. Fundamental Axioms, Definitions & Foundational Properties

Definition of Ratio: A ratio is the comparison of two quantities of the same kind by division, expressed as $a : b$ where $b \neq 0$. It is a dimensionless pure number devoid of units.
Property of Scaling: Multiplying or dividing each term of a ratio by the same non-zero constant $k$ does not alter the ratio, i.e., $a : b = ka : kb$.
Definition of Proportion: An equality of two ratios is called a proportion, denoted as $a : b :: c : d$ or $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$.
Cross-Product Rule: In any valid proportion $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$, the product of the extremes equals the product of the means: $ad = bc$.
Exam Pro-Tip: Always convert all given quantities into identical units (e.g., paise to rupees, grams to kilograms) before formulating any ratio or proportion expression.
Memory Mnemonic: EXTREME — Extremes X Together Relate Equally Means Exactly ($ad = bc$).

2. Formulas, Standard Theorems & Algorithmic Procedures

Category / CaseFormula / Mathematical RuleTopper Calculation Shortcut
Fourth Proportional ($d$)If $a : b :: c : d$, then $d = \frac{b \cdot c}{a}$Multiply middle terms and divide by the first term instantly.
Third Proportional ($c$)If $a : b :: b : c$, then $c = \frac{b^2}{a}$Square the second term and divide by the single given term.
Mean Proportional ($b$)If $a : b :: b : c$, then $b = \sqrt{ac}$Take the geometric mean (square root of the product of outer terms).
Theorem of Compendendo & Dividendo: If $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$, then $\frac{a+b}{a-b} = \frac{c+d}{c-d}$.
Theorem on Equal Ratios: If $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} = \frac{e}{f}$, each ratio equals $\frac{pa + qc + re}{pb + qd + rf}$ for non-zero constants $p, q, r$.
Continued Proportion Rule: Quantities $a, b, c$ are in continued proportion if $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{b}{c} \implies b^2 = ac$.
15-Second Speed-Math Trick: When dividing a total sum $S$ in the ratio $a : b : c$, the individual shares are $\frac{a}{a+b+c} \times S$, $\frac{b}{a+b+c} \times S$, and $\frac{c}{a+b+c} \times S$. Use fractions directly without finding the common variable $x$ to save precious seconds.

3. Word Problem Modeling & Practical Applications

Problem Type 1 (Income & Expenditure): If incomes are in ratio $a : b$ and expenditures in ratio $c : d$ with equal savings $S$, then income of first person is $\frac{S(b-d)}{ad-bc}$.
Problem Type 2 (Mixture & Alloy): Mixing two solutions of concentration $c_1$ and $c_2$ in ratio $q_1 : q_2$ yields a final concentration $C = \frac{c_1 q_1 + c_2 q_2}{q_1 + q_2}$.
Problem Type 3 (Age Related Ratios): Represent ages as $ax$ and $bx$. After $t$ years, the new ratio becomes $\frac{ax+t}{bx+t} = \frac{p}{q}$, solved by cross-multiplication.
Problem Type 4 (Coin / Currency Problems): Total value = (Number of coins $\times$ Denomination value). Convert coin ratios to value ratios before applying total sum.
UP Exam High-Yield Tip: In coin-based word problems, always equate the total given money to the sum of (Number of coins $\times$ individual coin value). Do not equate raw coin counts to total monetary value.

4. Mathematical Edge Cases, Sign Conventions & Common Traps

Critical Distinction: Ratio $a : b$ is strictly distinct from fraction $\frac{a}{b}$ in contextual word problems; ratios represent relative parts of a whole rather than absolute quantities.
Edge Case / Exception: Division by zero is undefined. In proportional equations, denominators $b$, $d$, and term $a$ in denominators must strictly satisfy $a \neq 0, b \neq 0, d \neq 0$.
Common Calculation Trap: Inverting terms in inverse ratio problems. The inverse ratio of $a : b$ is $\frac{1}{a} : \frac{1}{b}$ or simplified as $b : a$, not $-a : -b$.
Trap Prevention Trick: When given a ratio like $2:3:5$ and asked to increase each by $10\%$, do not add $10$ to each ratio term. Convert to absolute variables $2x, 3x, 5x$, apply percentage increase, and simplify back.
Memory Mnemonic: RATIO — Reduce All Terms Into One.

Question 1 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

1. If $x : y = 3 : 4$, what is the value of the expression $\frac{5x + 2y}{3x + 4y}$?

Correct Answer: Option A

Substitute $x = 3k$ and $y = 4k$ into the expression: $\frac{5(3k) + 2(4k)}{3(3k) + 4(4k)} = \frac{15k + 8k}{9k + 16k} = \frac{23k}{25k} = \frac{23}{25}$. Distractors arise from incorrect substitution or adding coefficients wrongly.

2. What is the mean proportional between $0.04$ and $0.36$?

Correct Answer: Option A

The mean proportional $b$ between $a$ and $c$ is given by $b = \sqrt{ac}$. Here, $b = \sqrt{0.04 \times 0.36} = \sqrt{0.0144} = 0.12$. Option 2 (0.20) is a common error from calculating $\frac{0.04+0.36}{2}$. Thus, correct index is 0.

Question 4 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

4. If $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} = \frac{e}{f} = 2.5$, then what is the value of $\frac{2a^2 + 3c^2 + 4e^2}{2b^2 + 3d^2 + 4f^2}$?

Correct Answer: Option A

Given $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} = \frac{e}{f} = k = 2.5$. Then $a = kb, c = kd, e = kf$. Substituting these into the expression gives $\frac{2k^2b^2 + 3k^2d^2 + 4k^2f^2}{2b^2 + 3d^2 + 4f^2} = k^2 = (2.5)^2 = 6.25$. Correct index is 0.

Question 6 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

6. What is the fourth proportional to $4$, $9$, and $12$?

Correct Answer: Option A

Let the fourth proportional be $x$. By definition of proportion, $\frac{4}{9} = \frac{12}{x} \implies 4x = 9 \times 12 = 108 \implies x = \frac{108}{4} = 27$. Distractors like 36 or 24 arise from incorrect cross-multiplication or taking third proportional formulas.

Question 7 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

7. If $x$ is subtracted from each of the numbers $23, 30, 57$, and $78$, the numbers so obtained in this order are in proportion. What is the value of $x$?

Correct Answer: Option A

Given numbers $23-x, 30-x, 57-x, 78-x$ are in proportion. Thus, $\frac{23-x}{30-x} = \frac{57-x}{78-x}$. Solving by cross multiplication or testing options: For $x=4$, $\frac{19}{26}$ and $\frac{53}{74}$. Wait, let's test $x=4$: $(23-4)(78-4) = 19 \times 74 = 1406$, and $(30-4)(57-4) = 26 \times 53 = 1378$ (Not equal). Let's test $x=3$: $(20)(75) = 1500$, $(27)(54) = 1458$. Let's test standard formula for subtracted proportional: $x = \frac{ad - bc}{(a+d) - (b+c)}$. Here $a=23, b=30, c=57, d=78$. $ad = 23 \times 78 = 1794$, $bc = 30 \times 57 = 1710$. $(a+d) = 101$, $(b+c) = 87$. $x = \frac{1794 - 1710}{101 - 87} = \frac{84}{14} = 6$. Therefore, $x=6$ (Option index 1).

Question 8 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

8. The incomes of A and B are in the ratio $3 : 4$ and their expenditures are in the ratio $5 : 7$. If each saves Rs. 5,000, what is the income of A?

Correct Answer: Option A

Let incomes be $3x$ and $4x$, and expenditures be $5y$ and $7y$. Then $3x - 5y = 5000$ and $4x - 7y = 5000$. Equating savings: $3x - 5000 = 5y \implies y = \frac{3x-5000}{5}$. Substituting into second equation: $4x - 7\left(\frac{3x-5000}{5}\right) = 5000 \implies 20x - 21x + 35000 = 25000 \implies x = 10,000$. Income of A = $3x = 3 \times 10,000 = \text{Rs. } 30,000$. Correct index is 0.

Question 9 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

9. What is the duplicate ratio of $5 : 7$ compounded with the inverse ratio of $25 : 42$ and the sub-duplicate ratio of $36 : 49$?

Correct Answer: Option A

Duplicate ratio of $5:7$ is $25:49$. Inverse ratio of $25:42$ is $42:25$. Sub-duplicate ratio of $36:49$ is $6:7$. Compounding them means multiplying: $\frac{25}{49} \times \frac{42}{25} \times \frac{6}{7} = \frac{42 \times 6}{49 \times 7} = \frac{252}{343} = \frac{36}{49}$ (since $252 = 36 \times 7$ and $343 = 49 \times 7$). Correct index is 0.
