18.1 Fundamental Angle & Side Theorems (त्रिभुज के मूलभूत प्रमेय)

Exterior Angle Theorem: $\angle ACD = \angle A + \angle B$
Theorem: The exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of its two interior opposite angles: $\angle ACD = \angle A + \angle B$.
Theorem Name Mathematical Statement Core Exam Implication
Angle Sum Property $\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ$ A triangle cannot have more than one right or obtuse angle.
Exterior Angle Theorem $\angle \text{Ext} = \angle A + \angle B$ Exterior angle is strictly greater than either opposite interior angle.
Triangle Inequality Theorem $a + b > c, \; b + c > a, \; c + a > b$
$|a - b| < c < a + b$		 The third side of a triangle must lie strictly between the difference and sum of the other two sides.
Angle-Side Monotonicity Side opposite to greater angle is longer In $\triangle ABC$, if $\angle A > \angle B > \angle C$, then $a > b > c$.

18.2 The Four Centers of a Triangle (त्रिभुज के चार प्रमुख केंद्र)

Questions on incenter, circumcenter, centroid, and orthocenter angles are guaranteed scoring questions in UP Upper Primary examinations:

Center Definition / Intersection Key Angle / Ratio Formula Location in Right Triangle
Centroid (G / केंद्रक) Intersection of 3 Medians (माध्यिकाएँ) Divides median in ratio $\mathbf{2 : 1}$ from vertex Always strictly inside the triangle
Incenter (I / अंतःकेंद्र) Intersection of 3 Internal Angle Bisectors $\angle BIC = \mathbf{90^\circ + \frac{\angle A}{2}}$ Always strictly inside the triangle
Circumcenter (O / परिकेंद्र) Intersection of 3 Perpendicular Bisectors of sides $\angle BOC = \mathbf{2\angle A}$ At the midpoint of the Hypotenuse ($R = \frac{h}{2}$)
Orthocenter (H / लंबकेंद्र) Intersection of 3 Altitudes (शीर्षलंब) $\angle BHC = \mathbf{180^\circ - \angle A}$ At the vertex with the $90^\circ$ angle
Golden Summary Comparison for $\angle A$:
$$\text{At Incenter } I: \quad \angle BIC = 90^\circ + \frac{\angle A}{2}$$ $$\text{At Circumcenter } O: \quad \angle BOC = 2\angle A$$ $$\text{At Orthocenter } H: \quad \angle BHC = 180^\circ - \angle A$$

Example: If $\angle A = 70^\circ$, then $\angle BIC = 90^\circ + 35^\circ = 125^\circ$, $\angle BOC = 2(70^\circ) = 140^\circ$, and $\angle BHC = 180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ$. Calculate them in 5 seconds flat!

18.3 Congruence Criteria & Midpoint Theorem (सर्वांगसमता एवं मध्य-बिंदु प्रमेय)

Valid Congruence Criteria

  • SAS (Side-Angle-Side): Two sides and the included angle between them.
  • ASA (Angle-Side-Angle): Two angles and the included side between them.
  • AAS (Angle-Angle-Side): Two angles and any non-included corresponding side.
  • SSS (Side-Side-Side): All three corresponding sides are equal.
  • RHS (Right angle - Hypotenuse - Side): Right-angled triangle with equal hypotenuse and one side.
The Famous Congruence Traps:

$\mathbf{AAA}$ is NOT a congruence criterion (it only proves similarity, not congruence).
$\mathbf{SSA}$ or $\mathbf{ASS}$ is NOT a valid congruence criterion (it can produce two non-congruent ambiguous triangles unless the angle is $90^\circ$, which is RHS).

The Mid-Point Theorem (मध्य-बिंदु प्रमेय)

The line segment joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and is half of its length:

$$DE \parallel BC \quad \text{and} \quad DE = \frac{1}{2}BC$$

The medial triangle formed by joining the three midpoints has an area equal to $\frac{1}{4}$ of the original triangle:

$$\text{Area}(\triangle DEF) = \frac{1}{4} \text{Area}(\triangle ABC)$$

Type 1: Exterior Angle & Interior Opposite Angles Archetype 1
Q1
An exterior angle of a triangle is $110^\circ$, and one of its interior opposite angles is $45^\circ$. What is the measure of the other interior opposite angle?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $65^\circ$

Step-by-Step Solution:

By the Exterior Angle Theorem:

$$\angle \text{Ext} = \angle_1 + \angle_2 \implies 110^\circ = 45^\circ + \angle_2 \implies \angle_2 = 110^\circ - 45^\circ = 65^\circ$$
Type 2: Triangle Inequality Theorem (Side Existence) Archetype 2
Q2
Which of the following sets of side lengths CANNOT form a valid triangle?
Correct Answer: Option (C) $3\text{ cm}, 4\text{ cm}, 7\text{ cm}$

Step-by-Step Analysis:

The sum of any two sides must be strictly greater than the third side:

  • In Option C: $3 + 4 = 7$, which is equal to $7$ (not strictly greater!). The two sides flatten out into a line, forming no triangle.
  • In Options A, B, D: Smallest two sides sum strictly greater than the longest side ($6+8=14>10$; $5+7=12>9$; $4+5=9>6$).
Type 3: Incenter Angle Formula ($\angle BIC$) Archetype 3
Q3
In $\triangle ABC$, the internal bisectors of $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ intersect at point $I$ (the incenter). If $\angle A = 70^\circ$, what is the measure of $\angle BIC$?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $125^\circ$

Top-Rank Speed Hack Formula:

$$\angle BIC = 90^\circ + \frac{\angle A}{2} = 90^\circ + \frac{70^\circ}{2} = 90^\circ + 35^\circ = 125^\circ$$

Examiner Trap: Option A ($140^\circ$) is $2\angle A$ (the circumcenter angle $\angle BOC$). Never confuse incenter ($90^\circ + A/2$) with circumcenter ($2A$)!

Type 4: Circumcenter Angle & Right-Triangle Property Archetype 4
Q4
What is the circumradius of a right-angled triangle whose hypotenuse measures $13\text{ cm}$?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $6.5\text{ cm}$

Universal Geometric Theorem:

In any right-angled triangle, the circumcenter lies exactly at the midpoint of the hypotenuse. Therefore:

$$\text{Circumradius } R = \frac{\text{Hypotenuse}}{2} = \frac{13}{2} = 6.5\text{ cm}$$
Type 5: Centroid Median Division Ratio ($2 : 1$) Archetype 5
Q5
In $\triangle ABC$, $AD$ is a median and $G$ is the centroid. If the length of median $AD = 18\text{ cm}$, find the length of $AG$.
Correct Answer: Option (C) $12\text{ cm}$

Step-by-Step Centroid Ratio:

  1. The centroid $G$ divides every median in the ratio $2 : 1$ from the vertex: $$AG : GD = 2 : 1$$
  2. $AG = \frac{2}{2 + 1} \times AD = \frac{2}{3} \times 18 = 12\text{ cm}$.
  3. $GD = \frac{1}{3} \times 18 = 6\text{ cm}$.
Type 6: Congruence Criteria & The Invalid Criteria Trap Archetype 6
Q6
Which of the following is NOT a valid criterion for the congruence of two triangles?
Correct Answer: Option (C) $\text{AAA}$

Geometric Rationale:

$\text{AAA}$ guarantees that two triangles have the exact same shape (they are similar), but NOT necessarily the same size (e.g. an equilateral triangle of side $2\text{ cm}$ and an equilateral triangle of side $10\text{ cm}$ both have angles $60^\circ, 60^\circ, 60^\circ$, but are obviously not congruent!).

Type 7: Mid-Point Theorem & Medial Triangle Area Archetype 7
Q7
$D, E,$ and $F$ are the midpoints of the sides $AB, BC,$ and $CA$ of $\triangle ABC$ respectively. If the area of $\triangle ABC$ is $48\text{ cm}^2$, what is the area of $\triangle DEF$?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $12\text{ cm}^2$

Medial Triangle Area Theorem:

Joining the midpoints of the three sides divides the original triangle into four congruent smaller triangles, each having $\frac{1}{4}$ of the original area:

$$\text{Area}(\triangle DEF) = \frac{1}{4} \times \text{Area}(\triangle ABC) = \frac{1}{4} \times 48 = 12\text{ cm}^2$$
Type 8: Orthocenter Angle Formula ($\angle BHC$) Archetype 8
Q8
In $\triangle ABC$, $H$ is the orthocenter. If $\angle BAC = 55^\circ$, find the measure of $\angle BHC$.
Correct Answer: Option (B) $125^\circ$

Top-Rank Speed Hack Formula:

The angle subtended at the orthocenter $H$ by base $BC$ is supplementary to the opposite vertex angle $A$:

$$\angle BHC = 180^\circ - \angle A = 180^\circ - 55^\circ = 125^\circ$$
Type 9: Isosceles Triangle Base Angle & Altitude Property Archetype 9
Q9
In an isosceles triangle $ABC$ with $AB = AC$, the altitude $AD$ drawn to base $BC$ has length $12\text{ cm}$. If the perimeter of $\triangle ABC$ is $36\text{ cm}$, what is the length of base $BC$?
Correct Answer: Option (A) $10\text{ cm}$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. In an isosceles triangle, the altitude bisects the base: $BD = DC = x \implies BC = 2x$.
  2. Let equal sides be $AB = AC = y$.
  3. In right $\triangle ABD$: $x^2 + 12^2 = y^2 \implies y^2 - x^2 = 144 \implies (y - x)(y + x) = 144$.
  4. Perimeter is $2y + 2x = 36 \implies y + x = 18$.
  5. Substitute $y + x = 18$: $$(y - x)(18) = 144 \implies y - x = 8$$
  6. Subtract the two equations: $(y + x) - (y - x) = 18 - 8 \implies 2x = 10\text{ cm}$.
  7. Base $BC = 2x = 10\text{ cm}$.

Top-Rank Pythagorean Triplet Speed Hack: Notice the altitude is $12$. The standard integer Pythagorean triplet containing $12$ as a leg is $(5, 12, 13)$. Here $x = 5 \implies BC = 10$, and $y = 13$. Perimeter $= 13 + 13 + 10 = 36$ matches perfectly! Solved in 5 seconds!

Type 10: External Angle Bisector Theorem ($\angle B'I'C'$) Archetype 10
Q10
In $\triangle ABC$, the sides $AB$ and $AC$ are produced to points $P$ and $Q$ respectively. The bisectors of the external angles $\angle PBC$ and $\angle QCB$ intersect at point $O$. If $\angle A = 80^\circ$, what is the measure of $\angle BOC$?
Correct Answer: Option (B) $50^\circ$

External Angle Bisector Master Formula:

$$\angle BOC = 90^\circ - \frac{\angle A}{2} = 90^\circ - \frac{80^\circ}{2} = 90^\circ - 40^\circ = 50^\circ$$

Speed Memory Hack:

  • Internal Bisectors (Incenter): $90^\circ \mathbf{+} \frac{A}{2}$
  • External Bisectors (Excenter): $90^\circ \mathbf{-} \frac{A}{2}$
