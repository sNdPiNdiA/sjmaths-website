1. Fundamental Axioms, Definitions & Foundational Properties

Triangle Angle Sum Property: The sum of all interior angles of any triangle is always $180^\circ$. If $\triangle ABC$ has interior angles $\angle A, \angle B, \angle C$, then $\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ$. This holds true for all Euclidean triangles.
Exterior Angle Theorem: If any side of a triangle is produced, the exterior angle so formed is equal to the sum of the two interior opposite angles. Mathematically, if side $BC$ is extended to $D$, then exterior $\angle ACD = \angle A + \angle B$.
Triangle Inequality Theorem: The sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle is always strictly greater than the length of the third side: $a + b > c$, $b + c > a$, and $c + a > b$. Conversely, the difference between any two sides is always less than the third side: $|a - b| < c$.
Side-Angle Relationship: In any triangle, the angle opposite to the longer side is greater than the angle opposite to the shorter side. Conversely, the side opposite to the greater angle is longer. If $a > b$, then $\angle A > \angle B$.
Exam Pro-Tip: In geometry word problems, always draw a neat reference diagram and label given angles and side ratios. Many negative-marking traps in Super TET occur due to misinterpreting interior versus exterior angles in composite figures.
Memory Mnemonic: AST — Angle Sum is Total $180^\circ$.

Congruence Criterion SSS (Side-Side-Side): Two triangles are congruent if three sides of one triangle are respectively equal to the three corresponding sides of the other triangle.
Congruence Criterion SAS (Side-Angle-Side): Two triangles are congruent if two sides and the included angle of one triangle are equal to two sides and the included angle of the other triangle. Note: The angle MUST be included between the two given sides.
Congruence Criterion ASA / AAS (Angle-Side-Angle): Two triangles are congruent if two angles and the included side (or any non-included side paired with AAS) of one triangle are equal to the corresponding parts of the other.
Congruence Criterion RHS (Right-Hypotenuse-Side): Two right-angled triangles are congruent if the hypotenuse and one side of one triangle are respectively equal to the hypotenuse and one side of the other triangle.
15-Second Speed-Math Trick: When checking if three side lengths $a \le b \le c$ form a valid triangle, instantly check if $a + b > c$. If this holds, the triangle is valid. For acute/obtuse checks: if $a^2 + b^2 > c^2$, acute; if $a^2 + b^2 < c^2$, obtuse; if $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, right-angled.

3. Word Problem Modeling & Practical Applications

Ladder and Wall Problems: Modeled as right-angled triangles where the ladder forms the hypotenuse, the wall forms the vertical perpendicular, and the distance from the wall forms the base. Apply Pythagoras theorem: $L^2 = H^2 + B^2$.
Navigation and Distance Problems: Ships or aircraft moving in perpendicular directions form right-angled triangles. Use distance formula $d = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ where $x = \text{speed} \times \text{time}$.
Fencing and Cost Estimation: Calculating perimeter of triangular plots for fencing. Perimeter $P = a + b + c$. Total Cost = $\text{Perimeter} \times \text{Rate per unit length}$.
Height of Trees and Buildings (Shadow Proportions): Utilizing similar triangles ($\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR$) where ratio of corresponding sides is equal: $\frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{BC}{QR} = \frac{AC}{PR}$.
UP Exam High-Yield Tip: SCERT UP Class 7 and 8 textbooks frequently feature word problems on finding unknown interior angles when an exterior angle and one interior opposite angle are given. Always remember: $\text{Exterior Angle} - \text{Known Interior Opposite} = \text{Unknown Interior Opposite}$.

4. Mathematical Edge Cases, Sign Conventions & Common Traps

Critical Distinction (Congruence vs Similarity): Congruence ($\cong$) implies identical shape and identical size (same area, same dimensions). Similarity ($\sim$) implies identical shape but proportional size (equal corresponding angles, proportional corresponding sides). Do not confuse AAA congruence (which does NOT exist; AAA proves similarity, not congruence).
Edge Case (SSA Trap): SSA (Side-Side-Angle) or ASS is NOT a valid congruence criterion in general triangles because it can lead to two different triangles (ambiguous case). Only use RHS for right-angled triangles where the angle is $90^\circ$.
Common Calculation Trap: Forgetting to check triangle inequality before solving perimeter or area problems. For example, side lengths $2, 3, 6$ cannot form a triangle because $2 + 3 = 5 \not> 6$.
Trap Prevention Trick: When naming congruent triangles, order of vertices is paramount! If $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR$, then vertex $A \leftrightarrow P$, $B \leftrightarrow Q$, and $C \leftrightarrow R$. Writing $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PRQ$ is mathematically invalid and will cost negative marks.
Memory Mnemonic: NO-SSA — Never Order SSA for Strict Agreement (SSA is invalid).

Question 1 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

1. The exterior angle of a triangle is $110^\circ$ and one of its interior opposite angles is $40^\circ$. What is the measure of the other interior opposite angle?

Correct Answer: Option A

According to the Exterior Angle Theorem, the exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of the two interior opposite angles. Let the unknown angle be $x$. Then $40^\circ + x = 110^\circ \implies x = 110^\circ - 40^\circ = 70^\circ$. Option 0 is correct.

Question 2 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

2. Which of the following sets of side lengths can form a valid triangle?

Correct Answer: Option B

According to the Triangle Inequality Theorem, the sum of any two sides must be strictly greater than the third side. Testing option 1: $3 + 4 = 7 > 5$, $4 + 5 = 9 > 3$, $3 + 5 = 8 > 4$, which is valid. For option 0: $2+3=5 not > 6$ (invalid). For option 2: $5+8=13 not > 14$ (invalid). For option 3: $1+2=3 not > 3$ (invalid).

Question 3 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

3. In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle PQR$, if $AB = PQ$, $BC = QR$, and $\angle B = \angle Q = 65^\circ$, by which criterion are the two triangles congruent?

Correct Answer: Option C

The given equal parts are two sides ($AB, BC$ and $PQ, QR$) and the angle included between them ($\angle B$ and $\angle Q$). Therefore, the congruence criterion is Side-Angle-Side (SAS). Option 2 is correct.

Question 4 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

4. If the angles of a triangle are in the ratio $2: 3: 4$, what is the measure of the largest angle?

Correct Answer: Option C

Let the angles be $2x, 3x,$ and $4x$. By angle sum property, $2x + 3x + 4x = 180^\circ \implies 9x = 180^\circ \implies x = 20^\circ$. The largest angle is $4x = 4 \times 20^\circ = 80^\circ$. Option 2 is correct.

Question 5 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

5. The perimeter of an equilateral triangle is $48\text{ cm}$. What is its area?

Correct Answer: Option A

Perimeter = $3a = 48 \implies a = 16\text{ cm}$. Area of an equilateral triangle = $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(16)^2 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \times 256 = 64\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}^2$. Option 0 is correct.

Question 6 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

6. In an isosceles triangle, if the vertical angle is $70^\circ$, what is the measure of each base angle?

Correct Answer: Option B

Let each base angle be $x$. In an isosceles triangle, angles opposite to equal sides are equal. The sum of angles is $70^\circ + x + x = 180^\circ \implies 2x = 180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ \implies x = 55^\circ$. Option 1 is correct.

Question 7 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

7. What type of triangle is formed by side lengths $7\text{ cm}, 24\text{ cm},$ and $25\text{ cm}$?

Correct Answer: Option C

Check the Pythagorean relation $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. Here $7^2 + 24^2 = 49 + 576 = 625$. The square of the longest side is $25^2 = 625$. Since $7^2 + 24^2 = 25^2$, it is a right-angled triangle (Pythagorean triple). Option 2 is correct.

Question 8 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

8. If the lengths of two sides of a triangle are $5\text{ cm}$ and $12\text{ cm}$, what is the range of possible integer values for the third side $x$?

Correct Answer: Option B

By the triangle inequality theorem, the third side must satisfy $12 - 5 < x < 12 + 5$, which simplifies to $7 < x < 17$. The possible integer values for $x$ range from $8$ to $16$ inclusive ($8 \le x \le 16$). Option 1 is correct.

Question 9 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

9. The altitudes drawn to the two equal sides of an isosceles triangle are:

Correct Answer: Option B

In an isosceles triangle, the altitudes drawn to the two equal sides are equal in length, a property easily proven using congruent triangles (SAS or AAS criteria involving the halves of the triangle). Option 1 is correct.

Question 10 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

10. In $\triangle ABC$, if $\angle A = 50^\circ$ and $\angle B = 60^\circ$, which side of the triangle is the longest?

Correct Answer: Option B

First find the third angle: $\angle C = 180^\circ - (50^\circ + 60^\circ) = 70^\circ$. The largest angle is $\angle C = 70^\circ$. The side opposite to the largest angle is the longest side. The side opposite to $\angle C$ is $AB$. Wait, let's verify: opposite to $\angle C$ is $AB$, opposite to $\angle B (60^\circ)$ is $AC$, and opposite to $\angle A (50^\circ)$ is $BC$. The largest angle is $\angle C = 70^\circ$, so the longest side is $AB$. Let's re-verify: vertices are $A, B, C$. Side opposite to $A$ is $BC$, side opposite to $B$ is $AC$, side opposite to $C$ is $AB$. Since $\angle C = 70^\circ$ is the largest angle, side $AB$ is the longest side. Option 0 is $AB$. Let's check: $\angle C$ is opposite to $AB$. Yes! Option 0 is correct.
