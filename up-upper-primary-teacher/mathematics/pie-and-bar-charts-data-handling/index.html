1. Fundamental Axioms, Definitions & Foundational Properties

Pie Chart Angle Axiom: The total angle subtended at the center of a circle is exactly $360^\circ$, representing $100\%$ or the aggregate total frequency $N$ of the given dataset.
Bar Chart Proportionality Law: In a standard bar chart or column graph, the height or length of each rectangular bar is strictly proportional to the numerical magnitude it represents, maintaining a constant bar width and uniform spacing.
Percentage-to-Angle Conversion Principle: Any given component percentage $P\%$ of a total quantity corresponds to a central sector angle of $\theta = \frac{P}{100} \times 360^\circ = 3.6^\circ \times P$.
Value-to-Angle Conversion Principle: Given a specific part value $v$ and total aggregate $V$, the sector angle is calculated using the exact ratio formula $\theta = \left(\frac{v}{V}\right) \times 360^\circ$.
Exam Pro-Tip: Always check whether the pie chart total is given in absolute numbers or percentages before calculating angles or missing values.
Memory Mnemonic: CPA — Circle Percent Angle ($360^\circ = 100\%$, so $1\% = 3.6^\circ$).

Bar Height Ratio Theorem: $\frac{h_1}{h_2} = \frac{m_1}{m_2}$, where $h$ represents bar heights and $m$ represents absolute data magnitudes for a fixed vertical scale factor.
Cumulative Frequency Bar Processing: In stacked bar charts, sub-component values are added cumulatively along the vertical axis; individual segment height equals difference between consecutive cumulative totals.
Average Value from Pie Chart: The arithmetic mean of $n$ distinct sectors with equal weight is simply the total aggregate divided by $n$, regardless of individual sector angles.
15-Second Speed-Math Trick: When converting percentages to degrees in a pie chart, never divide by $360$ first. Simply append a decimal point to the percentage and multiply by $3.6$ mentally (e.g., $45\% \to 45 \times 3.6 = 162^\circ$).

3. Word Problem Modeling & Practical Applications

Budget Allocation Modeling: Monthly household expenditure problems allocate a total income (e.g., $\text{₹}36,000$) across food ($120^\circ$), rent ($90^\circ$), education ($60^\circ$), and savings ($90^\circ$). Actual rupee value is found by multiplying $\frac{\theta}{360} \times \text{Income}$.
Comparative School Enrollment Analysis: Bar charts comparing boys and girls across classes V to VIII utilize grouped bars to instantly compute gender ratios, absolute differences, and percentage growths year-over-year.
Agricultural Yield Distribution: Crop production data (wheat, rice, sugarcane, pulses) represented in degrees is converted back to metric tons by setting up unitary method equations anchored on total production.
SCERT UP Textbook Integration: Standard problems from Class 8 'Hamara Ganit' require reconstructing missing tabular frequencies from partial bar chart heights and verifying that sum of all sector angles equals exactly $360^\circ$.
UP Exam High-Yield Tip: In multi-part data interpretation sets, solve sub-questions requiring ratios first before calculating absolute values to save crucial examination time.

4. Mathematical Edge Cases, Sign Conventions & Common Traps

Critical Distinction: A pie chart displays relative proportions (parts of a whole summing to $100\%$ or $360^\circ$), whereas a bar chart displays discrete absolute quantities across independent categories.
Edge Case / Exception: If a sector angle sums to more than $360^\circ$ or percentages exceed $100\%$, the chart is mathematically invalid or represents overlapping categories (multiple-choice survey responses).
Common Calculation Trap: Confusing 'percentage increase between two bars' with 'percentage share of a bar in total'. Percentage increase uses $\frac{\text{New} - \text{Old}}{\text{Old}} \times 100$.
Trap Prevention Trick: Always verify that $\sum_{i=1}^{n} \theta_i = 360^\circ$ before attempting angle-based subtraction or ratio questions in pie charts.
Memory Mnemonic: BASE — Bar is Absolute, Sector is Equivalent.

Question 1 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

1. If the central angle of a sector representing expenditure on food in a pie chart is $108^\circ$, and the total monthly income of the family is $\text{₹}50,000$, what is the exact expenditure on food?

Correct Answer: Option B

Expenditure = $\left(\frac{108^\circ}{360^\circ}\right) \times 50,000 = 0.30 \times 50,000 = \text{₹}15,000$. Distractor \text{₹}12,500 corresponds to $90^\circ$, and \text{₹}18,000 corresponds to $129.6^\circ$.

Question 2 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

2. In a bar chart representing book sales from 2021 to 2024, sales in 2021 were 400 units and in 2024 were 600 units. What is the percentage increase in sales?

Correct Answer: Option C

Percentage increase = $\left(\frac{600 - 400}{400}\right) \times 100 = \left(\frac{200}{400}\right) \times 100 = 50\%$. Distractor 33.33% results from dividing by 600 instead of 400.

Question 3 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

3. A pie chart shows budget distribution where Education is $72^\circ$, Health is $54^\circ$, and Housing is $108^\circ$. What percentage of the total budget is spent on these three combined?

Correct Answer: Option C

Sum of angles = $72^\circ + 54^\circ + 108^\circ = 234^\circ$. Percentage = $\left(\frac{234}{360}\right) \times 100 = 0.65 \times 100 = 65\%$. Option 65% is correct.

Question 4 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

4. If a pie chart sector angle for savings is $54^\circ$ and the savings amount is $\text{₹}9,000$, what is the total aggregate budget represented by the pie chart?

Correct Answer: Option C

Total Budget = $\text{Savings} \times \left(\frac{360^\circ}{\text{Savings Angle}}\right) = 9000 \times \left(\frac{360}{54}\right) = 9000 \times \frac{20}{3} = 3000 \times 20 = \text{₹}60,000$.

Question 5 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

5. In a double bar chart comparing mathematics and science marks of 5 students, if student A scored 80 in math and 60 in science, what is the ratio of math marks to science marks?

Correct Answer: Option B

Ratio = $\frac{\text{Math}}{\text{Science}} = \frac{80}{60} = \frac{8}{6} = \frac{4}{3}$, i.e., $4 : 3$. Distractor $3 : 4$ inverts numerator and denominator.

Question 6 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

6. What is the central angle corresponding to $12.5\%$ in a standard pie chart?

Correct Answer: Option C

Central Angle = $12.5 \times 3.6^\circ = 45^\circ$. Alternatively, $12.5\% = \frac{1}{8}$, and $\frac{360^\circ}{8} = 45^\circ$.

Question 7 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

7. If the total frequency represented by a bar chart across 5 categories is 500, and Category X has a bar height representing 125 units, what percentage does Category X occupy?

Correct Answer: Option B

Percentage = $\left(\frac{125}{500}\right) \times 100 = 0.25 \times 100 = 25\%$. Option 25% is correct.

Question 8 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

8. In a pie chart, expenditures on Item P, Q, and R are in the ratio $2 : 3 : 5$. What is the central angle for Item Q?

Correct Answer: Option C

The ratio has 2+3+5=10 total parts. Item Q accounts for 3 parts, so its angle is (3/10) x 360 degrees = 108 degrees. The answer is Option C.

Question 9 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

9. If the population of a town in a bar chart increases from 25,000 to 30,000 over a decade, what is the absolute bar height difference if scale is $1\text{ cm} = 5,000$ people?

Correct Answer: Option B

Absolute increase = $30,000 - 25,000 = 5,000$ people. Height difference = $\frac{5,000}{5,000} = 1.0\text{ cm}$. Option 1.0 cm is correct.

Question 10 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

10. A pie chart has 4 sectors with angles $x^\circ$, $2x^\circ$, $3x^\circ$, and $4x^\circ$. What is the numerical value of angle $x^\circ$?

Correct Answer: Option C

Sum of angles = $x + 2x + 3x + 4x = 10x = 360^\circ \implies x = \frac{360}{10} = 36^\circ$. Option 36° is correct.
