1. Fundamental Axioms, Definitions & Foundational Properties

Axiom of Probability: The probability $P(E)$ of any event $E$ is a real number bounded strictly between $0$ and $1$, inclusive, expressed as $0 le P(E) le 1$.
Sample Space ($mathcal{S}$): The exhaustive set of all possible distinct outcomes of a random experiment, where the sum of probabilities of all elementary outcomes equals unity ($sum P(omega_i) = 1$).
Complementary Event ($E'$ or $ar{E}$): The event that event $E$ does not occur, governed by the identity $P(E) + P(ar{E}) = 1$, which implies $P(ar{E}) = 1 - P(E)$.
Compound Bar Chart Definition: A graphical data representation using adjacent vertical rectangular bars grouped by categories to compare two or more discrete data series simultaneously across identical horizontal baselines.
Exam Pro-Tip: Always check if a probability question implies 'with replacement' or 'without replacement', as this drastically alters the sample space size in successive draws.
Memory Mnemonic: PEACE — Probability Equals Actual Count Exhaustive.

2. Formulas, Standard Theorems & Algorithmic Procedures

Conditional Probability Formula: The probability of event $A$ given that event $B$ has already occurred is given by $P(A|B) = rac{P(A cap B)}{P(B)}$, provided $P(B) > 0$.
Compound Bar Scaling Algorithm: To determine percentage growth between two comparative bars $A_1$ and $A_2$, use the formula $ ext{Percentage Change} = rac{A_2 - A_1}{A_1} imes 100%$.
Odds in Favor and Against: If $P(E) = rac{a}{a+b}$, the odds in favor of $E$ are $a:b$ and the odds against $E$ are $b:a$.
15-Second Speed-Math Trick: When calculating probability of drawing two balls of the same color without replacement, compute $ rac{inom{n_1}{2} + inom{n_2}{2}}{inom{N}{2}}$ using expanded factorials to bypass lengthy tree diagrams.

3. Word Problem Modeling & Practical Applications

Dice & Coin Combinations: For tossing $n$ coins or rolling $n$ dice, the total sample space is $2^n$ or $6^n$ respectively. For two dice, $n(mathcal{S}) = 36$, and sums of outcomes range from $2$ to $12$ with a symmetric triangular distribution peaking at sum $7$.
Card Deck Probability: A standard pack contains $52$ cards divided equally into 4 suits (Spades $spadesuit$, Hearts $heartsuit$, Diamonds $diamondsuit$, Clubs $clubsuit$). Face cards total $12$ (4 Kings, 4 Queens, 4 Jacks).
Compound Bar Chart Interpretation: When answering comparative questions from grouped bar graphs, first identify the reference scale and key legend, then compute absolute differences or ratios directly from the axis values.
Urn & Marble Models: Problems involving drawing colored marbles from bags without replacement require multiplying conditional probabilities at each successive stage.
UP Exam High-Yield Tip: In UP Junior Super TET exams, questions involving sums of two dice or drawing cards appear frequently. Memorize the 36 outcomes table for dice sums to solve questions in under 30 seconds.

4. Mathematical Edge Cases, Sign Conventions & Common Traps

Critical Distinction: 'Mutually Exclusive' events cannot occur simultaneously ($A cap B = emptyset$), whereas 'Independent' events have no causal effect on each other's probabilities ($P(A cap B) = P(A) cdot P(B)$).
Edge Case of Zero Probability: An impossible event has a probability of exactly $0$, but an event with probability $0$ is not strictly impossible in infinite sample spaces (almost impossible).
Common Calculation Trap: Confusing 'at least one' with 'exactly one'. The phrase 'at least one' means $1 - P( ext{none})$, whereas 'exactly one' means $P(A ext{ and not } B) + P(B ext{ and not } A)$.
Trap Prevention Trick: Whenever you see the phrase 'at least one' in probability word problems, immediately calculate $1 - P( ext{complement})$ to save calculation time and avoid missing intersection terms.
Memory Mnemonic: LEAST — Learn Exactly All Subtracted Total ($1 - P( ext{none})$).

Question 1 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

1. What is the probability of getting a sum of 8 when two unbiased six-faced dice are rolled simultaneously?

Correct Answer: Option A

Total sample space $n(\mathcal{S}) = 6 \times 6 = 36$. Favorable outcomes for a sum of 8 are $(2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3), (6,2)$, giving $n(E) = 5$. Thus, $P(E) = \frac{5}{36}$. Option 1 is correct. Distractors like $\frac{1}{6}$ arise from miscounting favorable pairs.

Question 2 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

2. From a well-shuffled pack of 52 playing cards, one card is drawn at random. What is the probability that it is either a king or a red card?

Correct Answer: Option A

Let $K$ be the event of drawing a king ($n(K) = 4$) and $R$ be the event of drawing a red card ($n(R) = 26$). The intersection $K \cap R$ contains 2 red kings. Using the addition rule: $P(K \cup R) = P(K) + P(R) - P(K \cap R) = \frac{4}{52} + \frac{26}{52} - \frac{2}{52} = \frac{28}{52} = \frac{7}{13}$. Option 0 is correct.

Question 3 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

3. A bag contains 5 red, 4 green, and 3 yellow marbles. If two marbles are drawn at random without replacement, what is the probability that both are red?

Correct Answer: Option A

Total marbles = $5 + 4 + 3 = 12$. Probability of first red = $\frac{5}{12}$. After drawing one red, remaining red = 4, total marbles = 11. Probability of second red given first is red = $\frac{4}{11}$. Total probability = $\frac{5}{12} \times \frac{4}{11} = \frac{20}{132} = \frac{5}{33}$. Option 0 is correct.

Question 4 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

4. If $P(A) = 0.4$, $P(B) = 0.5$, and $P(A \cup B) = 0.7$, what is the value of $P(A|B)$?

Correct Answer: Option B

By inclusion-exclusion, P(A and B)=0.4+0.5-0.7=0.2. Thus P(A given B)=P(A and B)/P(B)=0.2/0.5=0.4=2/5. The answer is Option B.

Question 5 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

5. Three unbiased coins are tossed simultaneously. What is the probability of getting at least two heads?

Correct Answer: Option A

Sample space for 3 coins $n(\mathcal{S}) = 2^3 = 8$. Outcomes with at least two heads are {HHT, HTH, THH, HHH}, which equals 4 outcomes. Thus, $P = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}$. Option 0 is correct.

Question 6 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

6. In a compound bar chart comparing export data of two items (A and B) across years 2021 and 2022, if Item A's bar height goes from 50 to 75, what is the percentage increase?

Correct Answer: Option C

Percentage increase = $\frac{75 - 50}{50} \times 100\% = \frac{25}{50} \times 100\% = 0.5 \times 100\% = 50\%$. Option 2 is correct. Distractor 25% is the absolute difference, and 33.33% uses 75 as the base incorrectly.

Question 7 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

7. A card is drawn from a standard deck of 52 cards. What is the probability that it is neither a heart nor a face card?

Correct Answer: Option A

There are 13 hearts and 12 face cards, with 3 cards counted in both groups. So 22 cards are hearts or face cards, leaving 52-22=30 cards that are neither. The probability is 30/52=15/26. The answer is Option A.

Question 8 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

8. If the probability of a student passing the Super TET Mathematics exam is $0.78$, what is the probability that the student will not pass?

Correct Answer: Option B

Probability of complementary event $P(\bar{E}) = 1 - P(E) = 1 - 0.78 = 0.22$. Option 1 is correct. Distractor 0.28 is a common subtraction error ($1.00 - 0.72$).

Question 9 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

9. Two dice are thrown. What is the probability that the product of the numbers appearing on top is a prime number?

Correct Answer: Option B

A product of two die results is prime only when one result is 1 and the other is 2, 3, or 5. There are 6 ordered outcomes out of 36, so the probability is 6/36=1/6. The answer is Option B.

Question 10 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

10. In a compound bar chart representing school enrollment across years 2020 and 2021 for Classes VI, VII, and VIII, if total enrollment in 2020 was 450 and in 2021 was 540, what is the absolute difference represented as a ratio of 2020 enrollment?

Correct Answer: Option A

Absolute difference = $540 - 450 = 90$. Ratio of absolute difference to 2020 enrollment = $\frac{90}{450} = \frac{1}{5}$. Option 0 is correct.
