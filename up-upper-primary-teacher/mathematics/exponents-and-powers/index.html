1. Fundamental Axioms, Definitions & Foundational Properties

Definition of Exponent: For any non-zero real number $a$ and a positive integer $n$, the expression $a^n$ represents the repeated multiplication of $a$ by itself $n$ times: $a^n = \underbrace{a \times a \times a \times \dots \times a}_{n \text{ times}}$. Here, $a$ is termed the 'base' and $n$ is termed the 'exponent' or 'index'.
Zero Exponent Property: For any non-zero real number $a$, $a^0 = 1$. The case $0^0$ is undefined in standard real analysis and acts as a major trap in competitive screening examinations.
Negative Exponent Property: For any non-zero rational number $a$ and any positive integer $n$, the negative power is defined as the reciprocal raised to the corresponding positive power: $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$ and $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{-n} = \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{n}$.
Fractional Exponent Axiom: For any positive real number $a$ and integers $m, n$ where $n \neq 0$, the rational exponent $a^{\frac{m}{n}}$ represents $\sqrt[n]{a^m} = (\sqrt[n]{a})^m$, bridging radical expressions and exponential laws seamlessly.
Exam Pro-Tip: Whenever bases are not identical during multiplication or division, immediately prime-factorize the composite numbers (e.g., $8 = 2^3$, $27 = 3^3$, $36 = 6^2$ or $2^2 \times 3^2$) to unify bases and apply fundamental index laws.
Memory Mnemonic: BONE — Base stays, Opposite sign for negative index, Negative power flips the numerator and denominator, Every non-zero number to power zero equals one.

2. Formulas, Standard Theorems & Algorithmic Procedures

Product Law$a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$Add exponents directly when bases are equal; combine like bases before expanding.
Quotient Law$\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$ (for $a \neq 0$)Subtract denominator exponent from numerator exponent: $m - n$.
Power of a Power$(a^m)^n = a^{m \times n}$Multiply inner and outer exponents directly without expanding parentheses.
Power of a Product$(ab)^n = a^n \times b^n$Distribute the common exponent to each factor independently.
Power of a Quotient Theorem: For any non-zero rational numbers $a, b$ and integer $n$, $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n = \frac{a^n}{b^n}$, allowing separate exponentiation of numerator and denominator.
Fractional Base Inversion Identity: For any non-zero rational number $\frac{p}{q}$ and integer $n$, $\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{-n} = \left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^n = \frac{q^n}{p^n}$, eliminating negative signs instantly.
Exponential Equation Equivalence: If $a^x = a^y$ and $a \neq 0, \pm 1$, then $x = y$. When bases are equal in an equation, equate the exponents directly to solve for the unknown variable.
15-Second Speed-Math Trick: To solve equations like $2^{x+1} = 32$, convert the right side into the base of the left side ($32 = 2^5$). Thus, $2^{x+1} = 2^5 \implies x + 1 = 5 \implies x = 4$. Never take logarithms manually for standard Super TET integer exponents!

3. Word Problem Modeling & Practical Applications

Standard Form (Scientific Notation): Expressing very large or very small numbers in the format $k \times 10^n$, where $1 \le |k| < 10$ and $n$ is an integer. Example: Mass of Earth $\approx 5.972 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg}$.
Compound Growth & Population Modeling: If initial population is $P_0$ and grows at a constant multiplicative factor $r$ per time period $t$, the population at time $n$ is modeled as $P = P_0 \cdot r^n$.
Microscopic Scale Measurement: Thickness of a paper sheet or radius of a hydrogen atom ($5.3 \times 10^{-11}\text{ m}$) handled via negative powers of 10 in SCERT Class 8 physics-integrated mathematics problems.
Comparison of Magnitude Problems: Comparing two numbers in exponential form by converting them to the same exponent or taking common logarithmic approximations to determine which is greater without full expansion.
UP Exam High-Yield Tip: In standard form conversion questions, shifting the decimal point to the left increases the exponent of 10 by the number of places shifted, whereas shifting the decimal point to the right decreases the exponent of 10 accordingly.

4. Mathematical Edge Cases, Sign Conventions & Common Traps

Critical Distinction: $(-2)^4$ versus $-2^4$. In $(-2)^4$, the negative sign is enclosed within the base, yielding $(-2) \times (-2) \times (-2) \times (-2) = +16$. In $-2^4$, the exponent applies only to $2$, yielding $-(2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2) = -16$.
Edge Case / Exception: Expressions involving $0^n$ (where $n > 0$) equal $0$. However, $0^0$ is indeterminate. Division by zero in exponent denominators (e.g., $a^{-n}$ when $a=0$) is undefined.
Common Calculation Trap: Adding exponents during addition of terms: $a^m + a^n \neq a^{m+n}$. The product law strictly applies to multiplication ($a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$), never to addition or subtraction.
Trap Prevention Trick: Always put parentheses around negative bases before applying even or odd powers. Even power of a negative number is positive; odd power of a negative number is negative.
Memory Mnemonic: PADD — Power Added During Division? No! Power Added During Direct multiplication of same bases ($a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$).

Question 1 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

1. What is the value of $\left[\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{-2} - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-3}\right] \div \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{-2}$?

Correct Answer: Option A

Step 1: Evaluate each term using negative exponent rules: $\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{-2} = 3^2 = 9$. Step 2: $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-3} = 2^3 = 8$. Step 3: $\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{-2} = 4^2 = 16$. Step 4: Substitute into the bracket: $[9 - 8] = 1$. Step 5: Perform division: $1 \div 16 = \frac{1}{16}$. Wait, let's re-verify the terms: $\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{-2} - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-3} = 9 - 8 = 1$. Division by $\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{-2} = 16$. Thus $\frac{1}{16}$. Let's check option A vs C: Option A is $-\frac{1}{16}$ and Option C is $\frac{1}{16}$. Correct answer is $\frac{1}{16}$, which corresponds to index 2.

Question 2 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

2. If $2^{x-1} + 2^{x+1} = 320$, then the value of $x$ is:

Correct Answer: Option C

Step 1: Factor out $2^{x-1}$ from the left-hand side expression: $2^{x-1}(1 + 2^2) = 320$. Step 2: Simplify inside the parenthesis: $1 + 4 = 5$. Step 3: Equation becomes $2^{x-1} \times 5 = 320$. Step 4: Divide both sides by 5: $2^{x-1} = \frac{320}{5} = 64$. Step 5: Express 64 as a power of 2: $64 = 2^6$. Step 6: Equate exponents: $x - 1 = 6 \implies x = 7$. Correct index is 2.

Question 3 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

3. Simplify and find the value of $m$ if $\left(\frac{3}{5} ight)^3 \times \left(\frac{3}{5} ight)^{-6} = \left(\frac{3}{5} ight)^{2m-1}$

Correct Answer: Option A

Step 1: Apply product law of exponents on the left-hand side: $\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^{3 + (-6)} = \left(\frac{3}{5} ight)^{-3}$. Step 2: Equate the exponents of both sides since bases are identical: $-3 = 2m - 1$. Step 3: Solve for $m$: $2m = -3 + 1 = -2 \implies m = -1$. Correct index is 0.

Question 4 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

4. Which of the following numbers is the greatest in magnitude?

Correct Answer: Option A

Step 1: Express all exponents as powers of a common index (HCF of 50, 40, 30, 20 is 10). Step 2: $2^{50} = (2^5)^{10} = 32^{10}$. Step 3: $3^{40} = (3^4)^{10} = 81^{10}$. Step 4: $4^{30} = (4^3)^{10} = 64^{10}$. Step 5: $5^{20} = (5^2)^{10} = 25^{10}$. Step 6: Comparing bases with identical power 10: $81^{10}$ is the largest, which is $3^{40}$. Wait, let's check correct option: $3^{40}$ is option index 1.

Question 5 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

5. What is the standard form of $0.0000729$?

Correct Answer: Option B

Step 1: Shift the decimal point to the right until there is one non-zero digit to its left. Shifting 5 places to the right gives $7.29$. Step 2: Since the decimal moved 5 places to the right, multiply by $10^{-5}$. Thus, $7.29 \times 10^{-5}$. Correct index is 1.

Question 6 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

6. Evaluate: $\left\{({\frac{1}{2}})^{-2} + ({\frac{1}{3}})^{-2} + ({\frac{1}{4}})^{-2}\right}^{\frac{1}{2}}$

Correct Answer: Option A

Step 1: Evaluate each term: $(\frac{1}{2})^{-2} = 2^2 = 4$, $(\frac{1}{3})^{-2} = 3^2 = 9$, $(\frac{1}{4})^{-2} = 4^2 = 16$. Step 2: Sum the values: $4 + 9 + 16 = 29$. Step 3: Apply the outer power $\frac{1}{2}$ (square root): $\sqrt{29}$. Correct index is 0.

Question 7 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

7. If $\frac{3^{x+2} + 3^x}{10} = 81$, then what is the value of $x$?

Correct Answer: Option B

Step 1: Factor out $3^x$ in the numerator: $3^x(3^2 + 1) = 3^x(9 + 1) = 10 \cdot 3^x$. Step 2: Divide by 10: $\frac{10 \cdot 3^x}{10} = 3^x$. Step 3: Equation simplifies to $3^x = 81$. Step 4: Express 81 as $3^4$, so $x = 4$. Wait, let's re-verify: $3^{4+2} + 3^4 = 729 + 81 = 810$. $810 / 10 = 81$. Hence $x = 4$. Option index for 4 is 2.

Question 8 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

8. Find the value of $x$ if $7^{2x} \div 7^{-3} = 7^5$

Correct Answer: Option C

Step 1: Apply quotient/product laws on LHS: $7^{2x} \div 7^{-3} = 7^{2x - (-3)} = 7^{2x+3}$. Step 2: Equate with RHS: $7^{2x+3} = 7^5$. Step 3: Equate exponents: $2x + 3 = 5 \implies 2x = 2 \implies x = 1$. Correct index is 2.

Question 9 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

9. If $a = 2$ and $b = 3$, then the value of $(a^b + b^a)^{-1}$ is:

Correct Answer: Option B

Step 1: Calculate $a^b = 2^3 = 8$. Step 2: Calculate $b^a = 3^2 = 9$. Step 3: Sum them: $8 + 9 = 17$. Wait! Let's check $a=3, b=2$ or $a=2, b=3$. If $a=2, b=3$: $2^3 + 3^2 = 8 + 9 = 17$, so $(17)^{-1} = \frac{1}{17}$. Let's re-read carefully: $(a^b + b^a)^{-1}$ where $a=3, b=2$ gives $3^2 + 2^3 = 9 + 8 = 17$. What if $a=2, b=2$? If option $\frac{1}{17}$ is present (index 0), let's check index 0 as correct.

Question 10 of 10 Super TET Junior Mathematics

10. What is the unit digit of $7^{105}$?

Correct Answer: Option C

Step 1: Identify the cycle of unit digits for powers of 7: $7^1 = 7$, $7^2 = 9$, $7^3 = 3$, $7^4 = 1$. The cycle length is 4. Step 2: Divide the exponent 105 by the cycle length 4: $105 \div 4$ gives a quotient of 26 and a remainder of $1$. Step 3: The unit digit corresponds to $7^1 = 7$. Correct index is 2.

Negative Marking Traps & Common Blunders (-1 Penalty Avoidance)

Negative Marking Alert #1:

Negative Marking Trap 1: Confusing $a^m + a^n$ with $a^{m+n}$. Addition never combines exponents.

Negative Marking Alert #2:

Negative Marking Trap 2: Neglecting parentheses on negative bases, confusing $(-2)^4$ (+16) with $-2^4$ (-16).

Negative Marking Alert #3:

Negative Marking Trap 3: Assuming $0^0 = 1$ or $0$. $0^0$ is strictly undefined in real analysis.

Negative Marking Alert #4:

Negative Marking Trap 4: Careless sign errors when subtracting negative exponents in the quotient law ($m - (-n) = m + n$).
