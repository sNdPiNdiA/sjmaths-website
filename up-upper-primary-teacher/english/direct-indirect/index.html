Direct & Indirect Speech

1. Core Grammatical Rules & Foundations

Fundamental Rule & Definition: Direct speech quotes the exact words spoken by a person (using quotation marks), whereas indirect (or reported) speech conveys the substance of what was said without quoting exact words. (प्रत्यक्ष कथन में वक्ता के मूल शब्दों का ज्यों का त्यों प्रयोग होता है, जबकि परोक्ष कथन में उसका भाव व्यक्त किया जाता है।)
Key Patterns & Structures: When the reporting verb is in the Present or Future tense, the tense inside the reported speech never changes. However, when the reporting verb is in the Past tense, the reported speech undergoes a strict backshift in tense.
Exam Tip: Always check the tense of the reporting verb first before applying any tense backshift rules in the options!

2. Classifications & Practical Applications

Major Categories & Types: Sentences in direct speech are classified into Assertive, Interrogative (Yes/No and Wh-questions), Imperative (commands, requests, advice), and Exclamatory/Optative (wishes, exclamations, curses). Each category follows distinct connector and verb modification rules.
Standard Contextual Sentences: For Interrogative sentences, 'said to' changes to 'asked/inquired', and conjunctions like 'if' or 'whether' are used for Yes/No questions. No auxiliary verb inversion is retained in indirect form.
Quick Identification Trick: In indirect interrogative sentences, the sentence structure must always be Assertive (Subject + Verb), never keep Question-mark style inversion!

3. Key Exceptions & Structural Nuances

Crucial Exceptions: Universal truths, historical facts, habitual actions, and scientific principles do not undergo tense backshifts even if the reporting verb is in the past tense. E.g., He said, 'The earth moves round the sun' becomes He said that the earth moves round the sun.
Commonly Confused Elements: Conditional sentences (Type 2 and Type 3) and past continuous actions ongoing simultaneously often confuse students regarding auxiliary verb retention.
Memory Formula: S-O-N / 1-2-3 1st person -> Subject, 2nd person -> Object, 3rd person -> No change.

4. Exam Insights & Question Analysis

PYQ Trend Analysis: UP Super TET Junior exams frequently test subtle errors in imperative sentences (forgetting to change 'said to' into 'ordered/requested' or retaining 'please' in indirect speech).
Elimination & Speed Strategy: Scan the options specifically for pronoun agreement and tense consistency. Eliminate options that fail to convert time adverbs like 'yesterday' to 'the previous day'.

Question 1 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Choose the correct indirect form of: He said to me, 'Where are you going?'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
In indirect interrogative sentences, the question word ('where') acts as the conjunction, the tense backshifts from present continuous ('are going') to past continuous ('was going'), and the sentence structure becomes assertive ('I was going').
Question 2 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
What is the correct indirect form of: The teacher said, 'The sun rises in the east.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Universal truths do not undergo tense backshift even if the reporting verb is in the past tense. Thus, 'rises' remains 'rises'.
Question 3 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Convert into indirect speech: She said, 'I wrote a letter yesterday.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Past Simple ('wrote') changes to Past Perfect ('had written'), and 'yesterday' changes to 'the previous day'.
Question 4 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Select the correct indirect narration for: 'Don't touch the wire,' the electrician said to us.
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Negative imperative sentences use 'told... not to' or 'forbade... to' (without 'not'). Hence, 'not to touch' is correct.
Question 5 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Identify the indirect speech of: He said, 'Bravo! You have played well.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Exclamatory sentences praising someone ('Bravo!') convert reporting verbs into 'applaud' or 'exclaimed with praise', and 'have played' shifts to 'had played'.
Question 6 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Change into indirect speech: The boy said, 'Hurrah! My team has won the match.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
'Hurrah!' indicates joy ('exclaimed with joy'), 'My' changes to 'his', and 'has won' changes to 'had won'.
Question 7 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Choose the correct indirect form of: Ram said to Sita, 'Please help me with this assignment.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
'Please' is dropped because the reporting verb 'requested' already conveys politeness. 'this' changes to 'that', and 'me' changes to 'him'.
Question 8 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
What is the indirect form of: He said, 'Will you listen to such a man?'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Yes/No interrogative sentence uses 'if/whether', 'Will' changes to 'would', and pronoun 'you' changes to 'they'.
Question 9 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Select the correct indirect narration for: She said, 'May God bless you!'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Optative sentences expressing a prayer change the reporting verb to 'prayed', 'May' changes to 'might', and 'you' changes to 'me' (assuming object).

High-Yield Revision Points (10 Exam Facts)

#1 Reporting verbs in the present or future tense never cause a tense backshift in indirect speech.
#2 The acronym SON stands for Subject (1st person), Object (2nd person), No change (3rd person).
#3 Universal truths, habitual facts, and scientific principles retain their original tense.
#4 Interrogative sentences in indirect speech lose their question mark and take an assertive subject-verb structure.
#5 The word 'please' is always omitted in indirect speech when 'requested' replaces the reporting verb.
#6 'Tomorrow' changes to 'the next day' or 'the following day' in indirect narration.
#7 'Yesterday' changes to 'the previous day' or 'the day before'.
#8 Imperative sentences use infinitives ('to' / 'not to') instead of the conjunction 'that'.
#9 Exclamatory sentences starting with 'Alas!' use 'exclaimed with sorrow'.
#10 Optative sentences expressing blessings or curses use reporting verbs like 'prayed' or 'cursed' with 'might'.

Common Exam Traps & Negative Marking Safeguards (-1 Penalty)

Retaining Question Marks in Indirect Speech Candidates often spot 'asked' but forget to convert the sentence structure from interrogative (Verb + Subject) to assertive (Subject + Verb).
Backshifting Universal Truths The examiner deliberately puts past-tense reporting verbs with historical or scientific facts to tempt candidates into backshifting the tense incorrectly.
Double Negatives in Imperatives Using 'forbade' along with 'not' in negative imperative sentences is a classic trap that violates English syntax rules.
