1. Foundation of the Romantic Age & William Wordsworth

Definition & Launch: The Romantic Age officially commenced in 1798 with the anonymous publication of Lyrical Ballads by William Wordsworth and Samuel Taylor Coleridge. (Hindi Hint: 1798 में 'लिरिकल बैलेड्स' के प्रकाशन से रोमांटिक युग की शुरुआत हुई।)
Wordsworthian Poetics: Poetry is defined in the 1800 Preface to Lyrical Ballads as "the spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings: it takes its origin from emotion recollected in tranquility." Language of common man is used.
Exam Tip: Remember that Lyrical Ballads opened with Coleridge's Ancient Mariner and closed with Wordsworth's Tintern Abbey.

2. S.T. Coleridge & The Power of Supernaturalism

Supernaturalism & Aesthetic Theory: Coleridge introduced the realm of the supernatural into everyday reality, demanding from readers "that willing suspension of disbelief for the moment, which constitutes poetic faith."
Imagination Distinction: He famously bifurcated imagination into Primary Imagination (living power of all human perception) and Secondary Imagination (echo of the primary, co-existing with conscious will, shaping and dissolving to create art).
Quick Identification Trick: Whenever an exam question mentions Primary/Secondary Imagination or Suspension of Disbelief, immediately look for Samuel Taylor Coleridge.

3. The Younger Romantics: Keats & Shelley

John Keats & Negative Capability: Keats coined Negative Capability (1817)—the capacity of a writer to exist in uncertainties, mysteries, and doubts without any irritable reaching after fact and reason. Sensuousness is his hallmark.
P.B. Shelley's Idealism: Shelley was the radical visionary of the movement. Famous works include Ode to the West Wind ("If Winter comes, can Spring be far behind?"), To a Skylark, and the lyrical drama Prometheus Unbound.
Memory Formula: KEATS = Sensuousness + Beauty + Odes; SHELLEY = Radicalism + Skylark + West Wind (Hindi Hint: कीट्स सुंदरता और संवेदना के कवि हैं, शैली क्रांतिकारी आशावाद के।)

4. Lord Byron & The Byronic Hero Archetype

The Byronic Hero: Lord Byron introduced the Byronic Hero—a brooding, rebellious, highly intelligent, fiercely independent outcast haunted by a dark past. Seen in Childe Harold's Pilgrimage.
Satiric Masterpiece: Unlike his contemporaries, Byron excelled in satire and witty narrative verse, best exemplified in his unfinished epic masterpiece, Don Juan.
UP Teaching Exam Trends: UP Super TET Junior exams directly target quotations, character creations, and specific theoretical terms like 'Negative Capability' and 'Spontaneous overflow'.

Exam-Standard Practice MCQs (10 Questions)

Solve UP teacher recruitment pattern questions with instant feedback and pedagogical explanations.

Question 1 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Which year marks the official beginning of the Romantic Age in English Literature with the publication of 'Lyrical Ballads'?
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Lyrical Ballads was published anonymously in 1798 by William Wordsworth and S.T. Coleridge, marking a definitive break from Neoclassical rules and launching the Romantic Revival.
Question 2 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Complete Wordsworth's famous definition of poetry: 'Poetry is the spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings: it takes its origin from...'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Appearing in the 1800 Preface to Lyrical Ballads, Wordsworth famously defined poetry as 'emotion recollected in tranquility'.
Question 3 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Who coined the critical term 'Negative Capability'?
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
John Keats coined 'Negative Capability' in an 1817 letter to his brothers, describing the ability to exist in uncertainty without frantically reaching for logical facts.
Question 4 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Which poem by S.T. Coleridge contains the famous line: 'Water, water, every where, Nor any drop to drink'?
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
These immortal lines appear in Coleridge's supernatural masterpiece, 'The Rime of the Ancient Mariner'.
Question 5 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
The concept of 'willing suspension of disbelief' for the enjoyment of poetry is associated with:
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Coleridge introduced 'suspension of disbelief' in Biographia Literaria (1817) to explain how readers accept supernatural elements in literature.
Question 6 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Which of the following poets created the archetype of the 'Byronic Hero'?
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Lord Byron introduced the brooding, rebellious, and mysterious protagonist known as the Byronic Hero in works like Childe Harold's Pilgrimage.
Question 7 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Whose epitaph, written by himself, reads: 'Here lies one whose name was writ in water'?
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
This poignant epitaph marks the grave of John Keats in the Protestant Cemetery in Rome, reflecting his despair over his unfulfilled literary promise.
Question 8 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Which poem concludes with the famous optimistic line: 'If Winter comes, can Spring be far behind?'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
P.B. Shelley concluded his revolutionary 'Ode to the West Wind' with this iconic line symbolizing political and spiritual rebirth.
Question 9 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Which autobiographical long poem by William Wordsworth charts the 'growth of a poet's mind'?
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
'The Prelude' is Wordsworth's monumental blank-verse autobiographical epic dedicated to S.T. Coleridge, tracing the development of his poetic consciousness.
Question 10 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Which of the following pairs of poets belong to the 'Lake Poets' group?
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Wordsworth, Coleridge, and Robert Southey were collectively known as the Lake Poets because they lived in the Lake District of England.

High-Yield Revision Points (10 Exam Facts)

#1 'Lyrical Ballads' was published in 1798 containing 23 poems (19 by Wordsworth, 4 by Coleridge).
#2 Wordsworth served as England's Poet Laureate from 1843 until his death in 1850.
#3 Coleridge's 'Kubla Khan' was composed in an opium-influenced dream in 1797 and published in 1816.
#4 Keats' great odes were mostly written in the pivotal year of 1819.
#5 P.B. Shelley drowned off the coast of Italy in 1822 with a volume of Keats' poetry in his pocket.
#6 Lord Byron died in 1824 aiding Greek independence in Missolonghi.
#7 'Biographia Literaria' (1817) is Coleridge's masterwork of literary criticism containing his theory of imagination.
#8 The term 'Lake District' refers to Cumberland, Westmorland, and Lancashire in northwestern England.
#9 Wordsworth's 'Lines Written a Few Miles above Tintern Abbey' was composed in 1798.
#10 Shelley's elegy for John Keats is titled 'Adonais'.

Memory Mnemonics & Retention Vault

The Three Lake Poets

W-C-S (Wordsworth, Coleridge, Southey) -> 'We Can Sing'

Easily remember the core Lake Poets by recalling 'We Can Sing' in the Lake District.

Keats' Great Odes

N-G-M-P-A (Nightingale, Grecian, Melancholy, Psyche, Autumn)

Use the acronym NGMPA to quickly check options for Keats' 1819 Odes in matching questions.

Imagination Categories

Primary = Perception; Secondary = Shaping/Art

P comes before S: Primary perceives the world, Secondary creates secondary (artistic) worlds.

Common Exam Traps & Negative Marking Safeguards (-1 Penalty)

Confusing Lake Poets with Younger Romantics Candidates often mix up Lake Poets (Wordsworth, Coleridge) with Younger Romantics (Keats, Shelley, Byron). Remember: Lake poets were older, settled in nature; Younger romantics were rebellious radicals who died young abroad.
Authorship of Lyrical Ballads Many believe Wordsworth wrote Lyrical Ballads alone. Always remember it was a joint venture with Coleridge (1798), though anonymous at first.
Misattributing Negative Capability Examiners trick aspirants by attributing 'Negative Capability' to Coleridge or Shelley. It exclusively belongs to John Keats.
