1. Core Grammatical Rules & Foundations

Fundamental Rule & Definition: A preposition is a word placed before a noun or pronoun to show its relation to some other word in the sentence. Formula: Subject + Verb + Preposition + Object of Preposition (Noun/Pronoun/Gerund). In Hindi: पूर्वसर्ग (Preposition) वह शब्द है जो किसी संज्ञा या सर्वनाम से पहले आकर उसका संबंध वाक्य के अन्य शब्दों से जोड़ता है।
Key Patterns & Structures: Prepositions never take a bare verb as an object; they require a noun, pronoun, or gerund (verb+ing). For instance, 'look forward to meeting' is correct, whereas 'look forward to meet' is a classic trap tested frequently in UP teaching examinations.
Exam Tip: Whenever you see 'to' acting as a preposition (such as in accustomed to, addicted to, with a view to, look forward to), always check if it is followed by a gerund (-ing form), not a base verb.

2. Classifications & Practical Applications

Major Categories & Types: (1) Place/Space: in (large areas/enclosed), at (exact points), on (surfaces). (2) Time: in (centuries, decades, months, years), on (days, dates), at (exact time). (3) Direction/Movement: into (movement inside), across (flat surface/line), through (three-dimensional space).
Standard Contextual Sentences: He lives at Civil Lines in Prayagraj on MG Road. She walked into the classroom during the lecture. Candidates must abstain from unfair means.
Quick Identification Trick: Use the triangle rule for time/place: 'At' is a pinpoint (smallest), 'On' is a line/surface (medium), 'In' is a 3D container/large period (largest). Example: At 5 PM -> On Monday -> In 2026.

3. Key Exceptions & Structural Nuances

Crucial Exceptions: 'Between' is strictly used for two items/persons, while 'among' is used for more than two distinct items. However, 'amongst' is used before vowels. 'Amid' or 'amidst' is used for uncountable or abstract masses (e.g., amidst chaos).
Commonly Confused Elements: 'By' denotes agency or deadline ('by 5 PM'), while 'with' denotes an instrument or tool ('cut with a knife'). 'Since' marks a point of time (exact start), and 'for' marks a period of time (duration).
Memory Formula: B-A-W (Between = 2, Among = >2, With = Instrument/Agent). Remember: Instruments take 'with', living agents take 'by'.

4. Exam Insights & Question Analysis

PYQ Trend Analysis: UP Junior Super TET and TGT/PGT exams heavily test fixed prepositions after adjectives and verbs (e.g., superior to [not superior than], senior to, prefer to) and phrasal verbs like put up with (tolerate) or call off (cancel).
Elimination & Speed Strategy: Read the blank along with the noun that follows it. If the sentence involves motion from outside to inside, instantly eliminate 'in' and select 'into'. Eliminate options containing redundant prepositions (e.g., 'enter into' is incorrect unless entering an agreement/pact).

Question 1 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Choose the correct preposition to fill in the blank: 'He is junior _______ me in the department.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Adjectives ending in '-or' borrowed from Latin (junior, senior, superior, inferior, prior, anterior, posterior) are always followed by the preposition 'to', never 'than'.
Question 2 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Which of the following sentences correctly uses a phrasal verb meaning 'to tolerate'?
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
'Put up with' means to tolerate or endure. 'Put off' means to postpone, 'put out' means to extinguish.
Question 3 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Identify the correct preposition for time: 'The train will arrive _______ 5 o'clock _______ the evening.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Exact clock time takes 'at' (at 5 o'clock), and parts of the day like morning, evening, and afternoon take 'in' (in the evening).

Explanation & Grammar Rule:
'Among' or 'amongst' is used for more than two persons or things. Since there are four brothers, 'among' and 'amongst' are both correct.
Question 5 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Which of the following is an incorrect usage of a fixed preposition?
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
'Enter' meaning to go into a physical place does not take any preposition (enter the classroom). 'Enter into' is only used metaphorically for agreements, discussions, or alliances.
Question 6 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Fill in the blank with the appropriate preposition: 'The cat jumped _______ the table.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
'Onto' expresses movement to the surface of something (dynamic motion). 'On' is static position. 'Upon' is more formal or literary.
Question 7 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
What does the phrasal verb 'call off' mean in the sentence: 'The match was called off due to heavy rain'?
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
'Call off' means to cancel an event entirely. Do not confuse it with 'put off', which means to postpone or delay.
Question 8 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English

Explanation & Grammar Rule:
'Five years' is a period of time (duration), which requires 'for'. 'Since' is used for a specific point in time (e.g., since 2018).
Question 9 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Fill in the blank: 'The river flows _______ the bridge.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
A river flows 'under' a bridge. If referring to movement from one side to another over a surface, 'across' or 'over' is used.
Question 10 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Identify the correct preposition combination: 'He dispensed _______ his dishonest servant.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
'Dispense with' means to manage without or get rid of. In active voice, 'dispense with' takes no extra preposition; in passive, 'is dispensed with'.

High-Yield Revision Points (10 Exam Facts)

#1 'Enter' physically takes no preposition, but 'enter into' applies strictly to agreements or pacts.
#2 Adjectives ending in '-or' (junior, senior, superior, inferior) are followed by 'to', never 'than'.
#3 'Look forward to', 'accustomed to', and 'with a view to' must be followed by a gerund (verb + ing).
#4 'Between' is used for two entities; 'Among' or 'Amongst' is used for more than two entities.
#5 'Since' denotes a point of time (exact start); 'For' denotes a period of time (duration).
#6 'Die of' is used for diseases (die of malaria); 'Die from' is used for causes like hunger, exhaustion, or wounds.
#7 'Angry with' is used for a person; 'Angry at' is used for a thing or behaviour.
#8 'By' indicates agency or deadline; 'With' indicates an instrument or tool used by the agent.
#9 'Put off' means to postpone; 'Put up with' means to tolerate or endure.
#10 'On time' means punctually (at the exact scheduled time); 'In time' means with comfortable margin before the deadline.

Memory Mnemonics & Retention Vault

Latin Comparatives

S-J-S-I-P-A-P: Seniors and Juniors Prefer To be Superior, Inferior, Prior, Anterior, Posterior (all take TO).

Never use 'than' with words ending in '-or' like junior, senior, superior, inferior, prior, anterior, or posterior.

Time Triangle (At, On, In)

Tiny Pinpoint (At), Flat Ribbon (On), Big Box (In).

At = Exact time (At 4 PM), On = Days/Dates (On Monday), In = Months/Years/Centuries (In 2026).

Person vs Thing preposition

Person gets WITH, Thing gets AT (Angry with him, Angry at behavior).

Helps instantly eliminate wrong preposition options in questions testing emotions like angry, annoyed, or displeased.

Common Exam Traps & Negative Marking Safeguards (-1 Penalty)

The 'Enter Into' Fallacy Students often write 'He entered into the room'. This is incorrect because physical entry requires no preposition. 'Enter into' is only valid for agreements and discussions.
Base Verb after 'To' Examiners bait candidates by writing 'Look forward to meet you'. Because 'to' is a preposition here, it must be followed by 'meeting' (gerund).
Confusing 'Since' and 'From' While 'since' is used with perfect tenses for a starting point, 'from' can be used with any tense (Past, Present, Future) indicating a starting point (e.g., 'The exam starts from Monday').
