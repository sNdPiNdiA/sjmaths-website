1. Core Grammatical Rules & Foundations

Fundamental Rule & Definition: Error spotting involves identifying grammatical, syntactical, or semantic errors in a sentence to make it grammatically sound, clear, and logical. It tests your understanding of standard English grammar.
Formulaic Structure: Subject + Verb + Object/Complement + Modifiers (ensure agreement, correct case, proper placement).
Example: "The team is playing well." (Correct S-V agreement)
Key Patterns & Structures:
1. Subject-Verb Agreement (SVA): The verb must agree with its subject in number (singular/plural).
Rule: Singular subject takes a singular verb; plural subject takes a plural verb.
Trap: Intervening phrases often obscure the true subject.
Example: "One of the students (is/are) absent." → "One... is absent."
2. Pronoun Case: Pronouns must be in the correct case (nominative, objective, possessive) depending on their function.
Nominative (Subject): I, he, she, we, they, who.
Objective (Object): me, him, her, us, them, whom.
Example: "Between you and I, this is a secret." → "Between you and me..."
Exam Tip: Always identify the core subject and verb first. This resolves a majority of Subject-Verb Agreement errors. (सबसे पहले वाक्य में मुख्य कर्ता और क्रिया पहचानें। इससे अधिकांश SVA त्रुटियाँ हल हो जाती हैं।)

2. Classifications & Practical Applications

Major Categories & Types:
1. Misplaced Modifiers: A word, phrase, or clause that describes another word but is placed in a way that it seems to modify a different word.
Rule: Modifiers should be placed as close as possible to the word they modify.
Example: "He saw a dog running down the street with one eye." (Incorrect) → "He saw a dog with one eye running down the street."
2. Dangling Modifiers: A modifier that doesn't clearly or logically modify any word in the sentence. The word it's supposed to modify is often missing.
Rule: Ensure the subject being modified is present and clear.
Example: "Walking through the park, the trees were beautiful." (Incorrect - trees don't walk) → "Walking through the park, I found the trees beautiful."
3. Faulty Parallelism: Items in a series or comparison must be in the same grammatical form (e.g., all nouns, all verbs, all gerunds).
Rule: Maintain consistent grammatical structure for elements of equal importance.
Example: "She likes to swim, to hike, and reading." (Incorrect) → "She likes to swim, to hike, and to read."
4. Redundancy & Superfluous Words: Using unnecessary words that repeat meaning.
Rule: Avoid words that add no new meaning.
Example: "He returned back home." (Incorrect) → "He returned home."
Standard Contextual Sentences:
  • The committee has decided to postpone the meeting. (SVA - collective noun as a unit)
  • Having finished the report, the manager reviewed the findings. (Corrected dangling modifier)
  • She is not only intelligent but also hardworking. (Parallelism with correlative conjunctions)
  • The reason why he succeeded was that he worked hard. (Corrected redundancy - 'reason why' and 'because' are redundant)
  • He hardly knows anything about the incident. (Corrected double negative - 'hardly' is negative)
Quick Identification Trick: For modifiers, ask "Who or what is doing the action/being described?" If the answer isn't the closest noun, it's likely a modifier error. For parallelism, check the grammatical form of the first item in a list and ensure subsequent items match. (मॉडिफ़ायर्स के लिए, पूछें "कौन या क्या क्रिया कर रहा है/वर्णित किया जा रहा है?" यदि उत्तर निकटतम संज्ञा नहीं है, तो यह मॉडिफ़ायर त्रुटि हो सकती है। समानांतरता के लिए, सूची में पहले आइटम के व्याकरणिक रूप की जाँच करें और सुनिश्चित करें कि बाद के आइटम मेल खाते हैं।)

3. Key Exceptions & Structural Nuances

Crucial Exceptions:
1. Indefinite Pronouns: Each, every, either, neither, anyone, no one, somebody, everybody are singular and take singular verbs. "Each of the students is present."
2. Phrases like 'as well as', 'along with', 'together with': The verb agrees with the first subject. "The captain, along with his team, is ready."
3. 'Neither...nor', 'Either...or': The verb agrees with the subject closest to it. "Neither the students nor the teacher is ready."
4. 'None': Can be singular or plural depending on context. "None of the money was found." "None of the students are here."
Commonly Confused Elements:
1. Affect vs. Effect: Affect (verb - to influence), Effect (noun - result; verb - to bring about).
Example: "The rain affected the game." "The rain had a bad effect."
2. Lie vs. Lay: Lie (intransitive verb - to recline, no direct object; past: lay, lain). Lay (transitive verb - to place, needs direct object; past: laid, laid).
Example: "I lie down." "I lay the book down."
3. Tense Sequence: If the main verb is past tense, the subordinate verb is usually past tense. "He said that he was ill." (Exception: universal truths: "He said that the Earth is round.")
4. Prepositions: 'Good at' (proficiency), 'good in' (performance within a space). "She is good at English."
Memory Formula: S-P-M-R-T (स्मार्ट)
Subject-Verb Agreement, Pronoun Case, Modifiers, Redundancy, Tense Sequence.
Explanation: This acronym helps quickly recall the most frequent error categories to check in a sentence. (यह संक्षिप्त नाम वाक्य में जाँचने के लिए सबसे लगातार त्रुटि श्रेणियों को तुरंत याद करने में मदद करता है।)

4. Exam Insights & Question Analysis

PYQ Trend Analysis:
  • UP TET/Super TET Junior exams frequently test Subject-Verb Agreement, especially with collective nouns, indefinite pronouns, and phrases like "one of the...".
  • Pronoun case errors (who/whom, I/me) are also common.
  • Redundancy (e.g., "final outcome," "repeat again") and incorrect preposition usage are recurring.
  • Basic tense sequence errors (e.g., simple past with present perfect) are often included.
  • Double negatives are a consistent trap.
Elimination & Speed Strategy:
Step 1: Read the sentence carefully. Identify the subject and verb. Check for SVA.
Step 2: Scan for pronouns. Check their case and agreement with antecedents.
Step 3: Look for modifiers. Ensure they are placed correctly and logically.
Step 4: Check for parallelism. Especially in lists or comparisons.
Step 5: Identify prepositions and conjunctions. Verify their correct usage.
Step 6: Spot redundancy. Look for words that repeat meaning.
Step 7: Check tense consistency and double negatives.
Step 8: If unsure, eliminate obvious incorrect options. Often, one or two options will have glaring errors, making the choice easier.

Question 1 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error: The quality of the products (A) / were not good (B) / and the prices were high (C) / No error (D).
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The error is in part (B). The subject of the sentence is 'quality' (singular), not 'products'. Therefore, the verb should be singular 'was' instead of 'were'. The correct sentence is: 'The quality of the products was not good and the prices were high.'
Question 2 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Choose the part with the error: She is taller (A) / than (B) / him (C) / No error (D).
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The error is in part (C). In comparisons using 'than' or 'as', if the pronoun is the subject of an implied verb, it should be in the nominative case. Here, it implies 'than he is'. So, 'him' should be 'he'. The correct sentence is: 'She is taller than he.'
Question 3 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Find the error in the sentence: He saw a dog (A) / running down the street (B) / with one eye (C) / No error (D).
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The error is a misplaced modifier in part (C). 'With one eye' should modify 'dog', but its placement suggests the street has one eye. The modifier should be placed closer to the noun it describes. The correct sentence is: 'He saw a dog with one eye running down the street.'
Question 4 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Which part of the sentence has an error? She likes (A) / to sing, to dance (B) / and painting (C) / No error (D).
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The error is in part (C) due to faulty parallelism. The items in the list should be in the same grammatical form. Since 'to sing' and 'to dance' are infinitives, the third item should also be an infinitive 'to paint', not a gerund 'painting'. The correct sentence is: 'She likes to sing, to dance, and to paint.'
Question 5 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Identify the incorrect part: The reason why (A) / he was late (B) / was because he missed the bus (C) / No error (D).
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The error is in part (C) due to redundancy. 'The reason why' already implies a cause, so using 'because' is superfluous. It should be replaced with 'that'. The correct sentence is: 'The reason why he was late was that he missed the bus.'
Question 6 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Choose the segment with the grammatical error: He told me (A) / that he has finished (B) / his work (C) / No error (D).
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The error is in part (B) concerning tense sequence. The main verb 'told' is in the past tense. Therefore, the verb in the subordinate clause should also be in a past tense form. 'Has finished' (present perfect) should be 'had finished' (past perfect). The correct sentence is: 'He told me that he had finished his work.'
Question 7 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Find the error: She is good (A) / in English (B) / but weak in Math (C) / No error (D).
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The error is in part (B) with preposition usage. When referring to proficiency in a subject or skill, the correct preposition is 'at', not 'in'. The correct sentence is: 'She is good at English but weak in Math.'
Question 8 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Which part of the sentence contains an error? He hardly (A) / knows nothing (B) / about the matter (C) / No error (D).
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The error is in part (B) due to a double negative. 'Hardly' is already a negative adverb, so it should not be used with another negative word like 'nothing'. 'Nothing' should be replaced with 'anything'. The correct sentence is: 'He hardly knows anything about the matter.'
Question 9 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Identify the error: Each of the students (A) / have submitted (B) / their assignments (C) / No error (D).
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The error is in part (B). 'Each' is an indefinite pronoun and is always singular, even when followed by a plural noun in a prepositional phrase ('of the students'). Therefore, the verb should be singular 'has submitted', not 'have submitted'. The correct sentence is: 'Each of the students has submitted their assignments.'
Question 10 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Choose the part with the error: Every student (A) / must bring (B) / their own book (C) / No error (D).
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The error is in part (C) regarding pronoun-antecedent agreement. 'Every student' is singular, so the possessive pronoun referring to it should also be singular and gender-neutral or specific ('his or her'), not plural 'their'. The correct sentence is: 'Every student must bring his or her own book.'

High-Yield Revision Points (10 Exam Facts)

#1 Subject-verb agreement is the most frequently tested error type; always identify the true subject.
#2 Indefinite pronouns (each, every, either, neither, anyone, no one, somebody, everybody) are always singular and take singular verbs.
#3 Phrases like 'as well as', 'along with', 'together with' do not change the number of the subject; the verb agrees with the first subject.
#4 Pronouns after 'let', 'between', 'but (except)', 'like', 'unlike', and all prepositions are in the objective case (e.g., 'between you and me').
#5 Modifiers must be placed immediately next to the word they modify to avoid ambiguity or illogical meanings.
#6 Ensure parallelism in lists, comparisons, and with correlative conjunctions (e.g., 'not only...but also', 'either...or').
#7 Avoid redundant expressions like 'return back', 'revert back', 'final outcome', 'reason why...because', 'free gift', 'past history'.
#8 If the main verb is in the past tense, the subordinate clause verb is usually also in the past tense (unless it's a universal truth).
#9 Use 'good at' for proficiency in a skill or subject, and 'good for' for benefit. ('Good in' is generally incorrect for proficiency).
#10 Avoid double negatives; words like 'hardly', 'scarcely', 'barely', 'never', 'no', 'not' should not be used together.

Memory Mnemonics & Retention Vault

S-P-M-R-T Scan

S-P-M-R-T: Subject, Pronoun, Modifier, Redundancy, Tense

When spotting errors, mentally scan the sentence for these five common categories in order. This systematic approach ensures you cover the most frequent error types efficiently.

No Double Trouble!

Hardly, Scarcely, Barely, Never, No, Not – Use only ONE to show a negative spot!

This rhyme helps you remember to avoid double negatives. If you see any of these negative words, ensure there isn't another negative word in the same clause that creates redundancy or an incorrect meaning.

Like a List, Keep it Consistent!

For Parallelism's might, keep all parts grammatically right!

Think of a shopping list. If your first item is a noun, all subsequent items should be nouns. If it's an infinitive verb, all should be infinitives. This applies to lists, comparisons, and correlative conjunctions.

Common Exam Traps & Negative Marking Safeguards (-1 Penalty)

The Most Common False Friend Trap: Intervening Phrases in SVA Students often make the verb agree with the noun in the intervening phrase (e.g., 'of the students') instead of the true subject (e.g., 'one'). The examiner deliberately places these phrases to confuse. Always isolate the main subject.
Subtle Agreement / Modifiers Pitfall: Dangling Modifiers The examiner often omits the logical subject of a participial phrase (e.g., 'Having finished the meal,'), making the modifier seem to refer to an incorrect noun in the main clause. Ensure the subject performing the action of the modifier is clearly stated.
Overlooking Negative Phrasing or Exceptions: Double Negatives / 'None' usage Candidates frequently miss subtle negative words like 'hardly', 'scarcely', 'barely' and combine them with 'not' or 'no', leading to an error. Also, remember that 'none' can be singular or plural depending on the context, which is a common trap for SVA.
