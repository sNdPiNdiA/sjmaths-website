1. Core Grammatical Rules & Foundations

Fundamental Rule & Definition: Active voice highlights the subject performing the action (Doer + Action + Object), whereas Passive voice emphasizes the receiver or the action itself (Object + Auxiliary + V3 + by + Doer). Changing voice must never alter the core tense or meaning of the original sentence.
Key Patterns & Structures: Standard formula: Interchange Subject and Object, always use the 3rd form of the main verb (V3), and insert appropriate forms of the 'Be' verb (is/am/are/was/were/be/been/being) according to the active tense. Pronoun cases change dynamically: I -> me, we -> us, he -> him, she -> her, they -> them.
Exam Tip: Always verify the primary auxiliary verb first. If the active sentence is in Simple Past (V2), your passive option MUST contain 'was' or 'were' + V3. Eliminate options showing 'is' or 'has'.

2. Classifications & Practical Applications

Major Categories & Types: Covers Assertive, Interrogative, Imperative, Infinitive/Gerund, and Modal constructions. For Imperative sentences expressing command, use 'Let + object + be + V3' (e.g., 'Open the door' -> 'Let the door be opened'). For suggestions/advice, use 'You are requested/ordered/advised to + base verb'.
Double-Object & Prepositional Passives: Verbs taking two objects (e.g., 'give', 'teach', 'lend') can be passivized using either the direct or indirect object (preferring the indirect/personal object as subject in UP exams). Verbs like 'know', 'oblige' take 'to' instead of 'by'; 'surprise', 'astonish', 'disappoint' take 'at'; 'contain', 'interest' take 'in'.
Quick Identification Trick: For 'Who' questions, instantly look for options starting with 'By whom'. For intransitive verbs (like sleep, die, go, arrive), instantly eliminate any option attempting to make them passive, as they take no direct object!

3. Key Exceptions & Structural Nuances

Crucial Exceptions: Exactly 4 tenses have NO standard passive voice: (1) Present Perfect Continuous, (2) Past Perfect Continuous, (3) Future Continuous, and (4) Future Perfect Continuous. Quasipassive verbs (e.g., 'Rose smells sweet' -> 'Rose is sweet when it is smelled') form another major exception tested in Super TET.
Commonly Confused Elements: Reflexive objects ('He hurt himself') cannot be converted into standard passive because subject and object refer to the same entity. Intransitive verbs followed by prepositional phrases (e.g., 'The car ran over the dog' -> 'The dog was run over by the car') DO form passives because the preposition attaches to the verb phrase.
Memory Formula: NO-PASS-4 -> Present/Past/Future Perfect Continuous + Future Continuous = NEVER passivized!

4. Exam Insights & Question Analysis

PYQ Trend Analysis: UP Education Board junior teacher exams heavily target exceptions: prepositional passive verbs (married to, known to), sentences with modal auxiliaries (should have been, must be), and complex interrogative structures beginning with 'Wh-' words.
Elimination & Speed Strategy: Under negative marking (-1 mark), use the 'Tense & Verb Agreement Scan'. First, check the tense marker. Second, check if V3 is present. Third, verify the correct preposition ('by' vs 'to'/'at'/'in'). This 3-step filter eliminates 3 wrong distractors in under 20 seconds.

Question 1 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
What is the correct passive voice of: 'The police arrested the notorious thief.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The active sentence 'arrested' is in Simple Past tense (V2). The corresponding passive structure requires 'was/were + V3'. Thus, 'was arrested' is correct, and 'by the police' is the appropriate agent.
Question 2 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Choose the correct passive form of: 'Who stole your pen?'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Interrogative sentences starting with 'Who' change to 'By whom' at the beginning, followed immediately by the helping verb according to the tense (Simple Past -> was), then subject ('your pen'), then V3 ('stolen'). Therefore, 'By whom was your pen stolen?' is structurally flawless.
Question 3 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Select the correct passive transformation for: 'Open the window.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Imperative sentences expressing an order/command follow the formula: Let + object + be + V3. Here, object is 'the window', auxiliary is 'be', and V3 of open is 'opened'. Thus, 'Let the window be opened.' is standard.
Question 4 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Which of the following sentences cannot be changed into the passive voice?
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
'Birds fly in the sky' contains an intransitive verb ('fly') which takes no direct object. Passive voice requires a direct object to become the new subject. Hence, intransitive sentences cannot take a standard passive form.
Question 5 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Identify the correct passive form of: 'Everyone knows him.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The verb 'know' takes the fixed preposition 'to' in the passive voice instead of 'by'. Also, the tense is Present Indefinite ('knows'), so 'is known' is correct. Thus, 'He is known to everyone.'
Question 6 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
What is the passive voice of: 'The box contains chocolates.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The verb 'contain' takes the preposition 'in' in the passive voice. The active tense is Present Indefinite ('contains'), so the passive requires 'are + contained + in + the box'.
Question 7 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Choose the correct passive conversion for: 'Please help me.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
When an imperative sentence starts with 'Please' or 'Kindly', it indicates a request. The standard passive conversion formula is 'You are requested to + base verb of active sentence'.
Question 8 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Which tense among the following has NO standard passive voice form?
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Future Continuous tense (will/shall be + V1-ing) has no standard passive voice because combining 'be' with 'being' results in awkward, ungrammatical phrasing in English.
Question 9 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Select the correct passive form of the quasipassive sentence: 'Honey tastes sweet.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Quasipassive verbs (like taste, smell, feel) maintain their adjective in the predicate and are expanded using the clause 'when it is + V3'. Thus, 'Honey is sweet when it is tasted.'
Question 10 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
What is the passive voice of: 'They are building a new bridge.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The active sentence 'are building' is in Present Continuous tense. The passive structure for Present Continuous is 'is/am/are + being + V3'. Therefore, 'A new bridge is being built by them' is correct.

High-Yield Revision Points (10 Exam Facts)

#1 The main verb in EVERY passive voice sentence must be in the Past Participle (V3) form.
#2 There are exactly 4 tenses with no standard passive voice (Present/Past/Future Perfect Continuous & Future Continuous).
#3 Verbs like 'know' and 'oblige' take preposition 'to' in passive voice instead of 'by'.
#4 Verbs like 'surprise', 'astonish', 'disappoint', and 'alarm' take preposition 'at' in passive voice.
#5 Verbs like 'contain' and 'interesse/interest' take preposition 'in' in passive voice.
#6 Intransitive verbs (sleep, go, die, arrive) cannot be converted into standard passive voice.
#7 Imperative sentences of request use 'You are requested to...' while commands use 'Let + obj + be + V3'.
#8 Reflexive pronouns (himself, herself, themselves) remain in their position and cannot become subjects in passive voice.
#9 Quasipassive sentences (e.g., 'Milk tastes sweet') expand into 'Milk is sweet when it is tasted'.
#10 In 'Wh-' interrogative questions, 'Who' transforms to 'By whom' while 'What' usually remains 'What'.

Memory Mnemonics & Retention Vault

The 4 Dead Tenses

No 4-Continuous Passives!

Remember that any tense ending in 'Continuous' that also has 'Perfect' (Present/Past/Future Perfect Continuous) plus Future Continuous (will be doing) cannot be converted into passive.

Fixed Preposition Shield

KNOW TO, SURPRISE AT, CONTAIN IN

Never use 'by' blindly. Memorize the triad: Know takes 'TO', Surprise takes 'AT', Contain takes 'IN'.

Interrogative Shift

WHO becomes BY WHOM

Whenever an active question starts with 'Who', your eyes should instantly scan the options for 'By whom' to eliminate wrong choices in 2 seconds.

Common Exam Traps & Negative Marking Safeguards (-1 Penalty)

The Tense Mismatch Trap Examiners often shift the tense in distractors (e.g., giving Past Indefinite in active but Present Indefinite in passive option). Always verify the auxiliary verb matches the active tense.
Blindly Using 'By' with Prepositional Verbs Candidates instinctively add 'by' after seeing verbs like 'know', 'surprise', or 'content'. Remember these take 'to', 'at, or 'in', leading to a negative marking trap if missed.
Passivizing Intransitive Verbs Setting trick questions with intransitive verbs like 'The baby cried loudly' and offering a fake passive option. Always check if a direct object exists before attempting passivization.
