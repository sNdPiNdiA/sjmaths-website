1. Core Grammatical Rules & Foundations

Fundamental Rule & Definition: Subject-Verb Concord (Agreement) dictates that a finite verb must agree with its subject in person (First, Second, Third) and number (Singular, Plural). Formula: Singular Subject + Singular Verb (-s/-es) vs. Plural Subject + Plural Verb (Base Form). In Shakespearean and classical English literature, this principle governed all metrical prose and formal verse structures.
Key Patterns & Structures: When two singular nouns are joined by 'and' to express a single composite idea or item (e.g., 'Bread and butter is my favorite breakfast'), the verb is singular. However, if they refer to two distinct distinct entities (e.g., 'Time and tide wait for no man'), the verb is strictly plural.
Exam Tip: Always locate the true subject of the sentence by crossing out intervening prepositional phrases before determining the verb form.

2. Classifications & Practical Applications

Major Categories & Types: 1. Additive Connectors: Words like 'as well as', 'with', 'together with', 'along with', and 'in addition to' attach secondary subjects. The verb agrees with the first subject (e.g., 'The captain, along with his crew, was drowned'). 2. Correlative Conjunctions: 'Either...or', 'neither...nor', 'or', and 'not only...but also' follow the Rule of Proximity—the verb agrees with the subject closest to it.
Standard Contextual Sentences: • 'Neither the teacher nor the students were present.' (Nearest subject 'students' is plural). • 'Neither the students nor the teacher was present.' (Nearest subject 'teacher' is singular). • 'Each of the boys has received a prize.' ('Each' triggers singular).
Quick Identification Trick: For 'Either/Or' structures, cover everything up to the second subject; whatever noun remains right before the verb slot dictates its singular/plural form!

3. Key Exceptions & Structural Nuances

Crucial Exceptions: Expressions of measurement, distance, time, and money considered as a single aggregate take a singular verb ('Ten miles is a long walk'; 'Fifty thousand rupees was stolen'). Fractions take singular or plural depending on the noun that follows ('Two-thirds of the book was read' vs. 'Two-thirds of the books were read').
Commonly Confused Elements: Contrasting 'A number of' (plural verb, e.g., 'A number of students are protesting') with 'The number of' (singular verb, e.g., 'The number of accidents is increasing'). Similarly, 'More than one person was injured' vs. 'More than two persons were injured.'
Memory Formula: A = Plural, The = Singular (Remember: 'A number of' takes plural; 'The number of' takes singular).

4. Exam Insights & Question Analysis

PYQ Trend Analysis: UP Super TET Junior and Assistant Teacher exams frequently test edge cases involving indefinite pronouns ('everyone', 'somebody'), collective nouns split in opinion ('The jury were divided in their opinions'), and tricky correlative structures where candidates mistakenly match the verb with the remote subject.
Elimination & Speed Strategy: Scan sentences for distractor phrases placed between commas or modifiers. Cross them out mentally. Check if the subject starts with 'Each', 'Every', or 'Neither'. Eliminate plural verb options instantly if the subject is singular.

Memory Mnemonics & Retention Vault

Proximity Priority

Nearest Neighbor Wins (NNW)

In 'either/or' or 'neither/nor' constructions, ignore the first subject and match the verb strictly with your nearest neighbor.

Article Check for 'And'

One 'The' = One Person; Two 'The's = Two People

'The poet and philosopher is' (same person) vs 'The poet and the philosopher are' (two different people).

Number Article Rule

A = Many (Plural), The = One (Singular)

Remember that 'A number of' means a collection of many items (plural verb), whereas 'The number of' represents a single calculated total (singular verb).

Common Exam Traps & Negative Marking Safeguards (-1 Penalty)

The Intervening Modifier Illusion Candidates often match the verb with the noun immediately preceding it inside a prepositional phrase (e.g., 'A basket of apples <em>were</em> sold'). Always cross out 'of apples' to see the true singular subject 'basket'.
Assuming Collective Nouns are Always Singular Students memorize that 'jury' or 'committee' is singular, failing to notice contextual cues like 'their' or 'divided', which mandate a plural verb.
Confusing 'And' Compound Subjects with Unit Ideas Treating standard paired items like 'Bread and butter' as plural automatically, ignoring whether they represent a single dish or two separate items on a plate.
