1. Core Grammatical Rules & Foundations

Fundamental Rule & Definition: A conjunction is a word used to connect clauses, sentences, words, or phrases (e.g., and, but, because). In UP Super TET Junior exams, questions test your ability to maintain grammatical parallelism across compound and complex structures.
Key Patterns & Structures: Correlative conjunctions must connect parallel grammatical units (nouns with nouns, infinitives with infinitives). Structure: Neither he nor his brother is coming (Noun + Noun). Incorrect: Either you study or to work.
Exam Tip: Always check that correlative pairs (e.g., 'not only' and 'but also') are placed immediately before the words or phrases they intend to link.

2. Classifications & Practical Applications

Major Categories & Types: Divided into Coordinating (joins equals), Subordinating (joins dependent to independent), and Correlative (paired conjunctions). Subordinating conjunctions introduce adverbial, noun, or relative clauses.
Standard Contextual Sentences: Example of 'lest...should': Walk carefully lest you should fall (do not use 'not' after lest because 'lest' has a negative meaning). Example of 'Hardly...when': Hardly had I reached the station when the train left.
Quick Identification Trick: Remember the 'Lest-Should' inseparable bond. The moment you see 'lest' in a fill-in-the-blank, immediately search the options for 'should'.

3. Key Exceptions & Structural Nuances

Crucial Exceptions: 'No sooner' takes 'than', whereas 'Scarcely' and 'Hardly' take 'when' or 'before'. Mixing these up is a favorite trap of UP examination setters.
Commonly Confused Elements: 'Until' is time-oriented (Wait until I return), while 'Unless' is condition-oriented (You will fail unless you work hard).
Memory Formula: UN-TIL = TIME, UN-LESS = LESS CONDITION Use these roots to instantly differentiate time from condition traps.

4. Exam Insights & Question Analysis

PYQ Trend Analysis: UP Teacher exams frequently test error spotting in sentence synthesis (Simple to Complex conversion) and missing auxiliary inversions in sentences beginning with 'Hardly' or 'No sooner'.
Elimination & Speed Strategy: Scan for double negatives (e.g., 'Unless you do not...') which are strictly ungrammatical. Eliminate options with redundant negative markers to lock in the correct answer within 30 seconds.

Choose the correct conjunction pair to complete the sentence: 'Hardly had she entered the room _______ the phone rang.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
Hardly and scarcely are invariably followed by 'when' or 'before', whereas 'no sooner' is followed by 'than'. Hence, option C is correct.
Question 2 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Identify the error in the sentence: 'Work hard lest you may fail.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
The conjunction 'lest' is always followed by the modal auxiliary 'should', never 'may' or 'might'. Therefore, 'you may fail' should be 'you should fail'.
Question 3 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English

Explanation & Grammar Rule:

Question 4 / 10 UP Super TET Junior English
Fill in the blank with the appropriate word: 'You cannot succeed _______ you work sincerely.'
Explanation & Grammar Rule:
'Unless' means 'if not' and is used for conditions. 'Until' is used for time. The sentence sets a conditional barrier for success.

The clause 'that he is honest' acts as the object of the transitive verb 'know', making it a Noun Clause functioning as the object.

High-Yield Revision Points (10 Exam Facts)

#1 FANBOYS (For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So) are the 7 coordinating conjunctions.
#2 'Lest' is invariably followed by 'should' and never takes 'not'.
#3 'No sooner' requires 'than' and inversion of the subject and auxiliary verb.
#4 'Hardly' and 'Scarcely' must be paired with 'when' or 'before'.
#5 'Unless' denotes condition; 'Until' denotes time.
#6 Double negatives with 'unless', 'until', and 'lest' are strictly ungrammatical.
#7 Correlative conjunctions (either...or, neither...nor) demand strict grammatical parallelism.
#8 A Compound sentence has at least two independent clauses joined by coordinating conjunctions.
#9 A Complex sentence contains one independent clause and one or more dependent (subordinate) clauses.
#10 Noun clauses answer the question 'what?' after a verb or preposition.

Common Exam Traps & Negative Marking Safeguards (-1 Penalty)

The Double Negative Pitfall Candidates often write 'Unless you do not study'. Since 'unless' means 'if not', adding 'not' creates a redundant double negative. Examiners love testing this.
Incorrect Correlative Pairings Mixing up 'No sooner...when' instead of 'No sooner...than'. Always check the second half of the sentence pair.
Breaking Parallelism in Correlatives Placing correlative words ('not only...but also') before mismatched parts of speech (e.g., placing one before a noun and the other before a verb).
