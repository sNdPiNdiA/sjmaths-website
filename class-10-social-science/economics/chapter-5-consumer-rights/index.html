Complete Concept Notes

Full chapter coverage plus explicit competency-focused learning activities.

01

Market and Consumer

A market is a place or arrangement where buyers and sellers interact to exchange goods and services. A consumer purchases goods or services for use.

  • Consumers may be weaker than powerful sellers or producers.
  • Rules and regulations are needed to make market transactions fairer.
  • Consumer protection covers both goods and services.
Key concept · Market
02

Consumer Exploitation

Consumer exploitation occurs when buyers are treated unfairly in the marketplace.

  • Under-weighing or supplying less than promised.
  • Overcharging or adding undisclosed charges.
  • Selling adulterated or defective goods.
  • Using false or misleading information.
  • Providing unsafe goods or deficient services.
Key concept · Consumer exploitation
03

Why Rules and Regulations Are Necessary

Rules and regulations protect consumers and create accountability in the marketplace.

  • They protect consumers from unsafe products.
  • They require important product information.
  • They provide mechanisms for complaints and remedies.
  • They help address unequal bargaining power.
Key concept · Consumer protection
04

Consumer Movement in India

The consumer movement developed as a social force against unfair and unethical trade practices.

  • Food shortages, hoarding, black marketing and adulteration contributed to its development.
  • Organisations wrote articles, held exhibitions and formed consumer groups.
  • The movement gradually demanded stronger legal protection.
Key concept · Consumer movement
05

Consumers International and UN Guidelines

Consumers International is an international umbrella body for consumer organisations. The UN Guidelines for Consumer Protection provided an international framework for consumer protection.

  • The UN adopted the guidelines in 1985.
  • International cooperation strengthened consumer advocacy.
  • The movement connects local consumer interests with wider consumer protection efforts.
Key concept · Consumers International
06

COPRA — Consumer Protection Act, 1986

The Consumer Protection Act, 1986 created a legal framework for protecting consumers and seeking redressal.

  • It is popularly known as COPRA.
  • It established a consumer dispute redressal framework.
  • The chapter notes its amendment in 2019.
  • The amended framework includes internet purchases and encourages mediation.
Key concept · COPRA
07

Right to Safety

Consumers have the right to be protected against goods and services that are hazardous to life and property.

  • Safety standards are important for products and services.
  • Negligence causing harm can lead to a consumer grievance.
  • The Reji Mathew case illustrates safety and deficiency in service.
Key concept · Right to Safety
08

Right to Information

Consumers have the right to obtain information needed to make an informed choice.

  • Check ingredients, price, batch number, manufacturing date and expiry date.
  • Medicines provide directions and information about side effects and risks.
  • MRP and package information help consumers judge purchases.
  • The RTI Act, 2005 provides access to information about government functioning.
Key concept · RTI
09

Right to Choose

Consumers should have freedom to select goods and services and should not be forced to accept unwanted purchases.

  • Bundling an unwanted product with a desired service can restrict choice.
  • The Abirami case illustrates the right to choose in a service context.
  • A consumer can seek redressal when choice is unfairly restricted.
Key concept · Right to Choose
10

Right to Seek Redressal

Consumers can seek a remedy when defective goods, deficient services or unfair practices cause harm.

  • A complaint can be supported by bills, receipts and other evidence.
  • The appropriate Consumer Commission examines the matter.
  • The remedy may include refund, replacement, compensation or another appropriate direction.
Key concept · Redressal
11

Right to Representation

Consumers can represent their interests through consumer organisations and consumer protection councils.

  • Consumer organisations can guide consumers.
  • They may help consumers prepare complaints.
  • They can represent consumers before Consumer Commissions.
  • Collective action can strengthen consumer demands.
Key concept · Consumer organisations
12

Right to Consumer Education

Consumer education helps people understand their rights, responsibilities and available remedies.

  • An informed consumer can compare products and identify unfair practices.
  • Awareness programmes and consumer organisations help spread knowledge.
  • Consumer education supports informed decision-making.
Key concept · Consumer education
13

Consumer Protection Councils vs Consumer Commission

These are different parts of the consumer-protection system.

  • Consumer Protection Councils/organisations mainly create awareness, advise and support consumers.
  • Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions hear consumer disputes and can provide formal remedies.
  • A council is primarily supportive/awareness-oriented; a commission performs the formal redressal function.
Key concept · Council vs Commission
14

Three-Tier Consumer Redressal Machinery

The chapter describes District, State and National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions.

  • District Commission: claims up to ₹1 crore in the supplied NCERT text.
  • State Commission: ₹1 crore to ₹10 crore.
  • National Commission: claims exceeding ₹10 crore.
  • Appeal can move from District to State and then National.
Key concept · Consumer Commissions
15

Case Study — Reji Mathew

The medical-negligence case demonstrates how a consumer grievance can be taken through the redressal system.

  • The complaint was initially considered by the State Commission.
  • An appeal was made to the National Commission.
  • The National Commission held the hospital responsible for medical negligence and directed compensation.
Case study · Real-life narrative
16

Case Study — Abirami

Abirami's coaching-institute case demonstrates the right to choose and redressal for deficiency in service.

  • She left a two-year course after one year.
  • The dispute concerned refund of the remaining fee.
  • Consumer commissions considered the complaint and ordered relief.
Case study · Right to choose
17

Case Study — Prakash

Prakash's money-order case demonstrates a step-by-step route to consumer redressal.

  • He first enquired with the post office.
  • He sought advice from a Consumer Protection Council.
  • He filed a complaint before the Consumer Commission.
  • Evidence was examined and both sides were heard before the verdict.
Case study · Complaint process
18

Quality Certification and Logos

Certification marks help consumers identify products that meet specified standards.

  • ISI is associated with standards monitored by the Bureau of Indian Standards.
  • Agmark is associated with agricultural products such as edible oil and cereals in the chapter.
  • Hallmark is associated with jewellery quality certification.
  • +F is used in the chapter in connection with food fortification.
Quality marks · ISI and AGMARK
19

Package Information

Product labels provide information that helps consumers make informed decisions.

  • Check MRP/price.
  • Check quantity and weight.
  • Check manufacturing and expiry dates.
  • Check ingredients and manufacturer details.
  • Keep the bill or proof of purchase.
Consumer awareness
20

Right to Compensation

A consumer who suffers damage can seek compensation according to the degree and nature of the damage.

  • Compensation is one possible form of redressal.
  • Evidence helps establish the consumer's grievance.
  • Consumer Commissions can direct appropriate remedies.
Consumer protection · Redressal
21

Consumer Duties and Consciousness

A conscious consumer is alert, informed and willing to act.

  • Insist on a bill and preserve it.
  • Check price, quantity, expiry and quality marks.
  • Compare information and prices.
  • Question suspicious practices.
  • Complain when rights are violated.
Consumer awareness
22

Progress of Consumer Movement

India's consumer movement has developed through organisations, legal institutions and awareness programmes.

  • Consumer affairs departments exist at central and state levels.
  • Consumer organisations support awareness and complaints.
  • Consumer protection has expanded, although awareness is still spreading slowly.
Consumer awareness
23

Limitations of Redressal

Consumer protection is not automatically effective merely because legal mechanisms exist.

  • The redressal process can be cumbersome, expensive and time-consuming.
  • Evidence may be difficult to collect.
  • Cash memos are often not issued.
  • Weak enforcement and limited awareness can reduce effectiveness.
Consumer awareness · Critical thinking
24

2019 Amendment and Newer Protection

The chapter notes that COPRA was amended in 2019 to reflect newer forms of consumer transactions.

  • Internet purchases are included.
  • Manufacturers and service providers can be held responsible for defective products or deficient services.
  • Mediation is encouraged at all three tiers.
Consumer protection law
25

Fair and Active Consumer

Consumer protection works best when consumers know their rights and use them actively.

  • Awareness should lead to informed decisions.
  • Consumers can work collectively through organisations.
  • Government enforcement and consumer participation are both important.
  • Consumer rights should be used to challenge exploitation.
Well-informed and active consumer

NCERT Exercises + Answers

All 13 end-of-chapter exercise questions with exam-ready answers.

NCERT EXERCISE · Q1

Why are rules and regulations required in the marketplace? Illustrate with a few examples.

Answer:

Rules and regulations are required because individual consumers can be in a weak position compared with sellers and powerful producers. They protect consumers against under-weighing, overcharging, adulteration, defective goods, unsafe products and misleading information. Packaging and safety requirements also help consumers make informed choices and provide evidence when a complaint is made.

NCERT EXERCISE · Q2

What factors gave birth to the consumer movement in India? Trace its evolution.

Answer:

The consumer movement developed because consumers faced food shortages, hoarding, black marketing, adulteration and other unfair practices. It emerged as an organised movement in the 1960s. During the 1970s consumer organisations wrote articles, held exhibitions and formed groups. The movement later gained stronger legal support through the Consumer Protection Act, 1986, with the chapter also noting its 2019 amendment.

NCERT EXERCISE · Q3

Explain the need for consumer consciousness by giving two examples.

Answer:

Consumer consciousness helps people recognise exploitation and make informed choices. For example, checking the expiry date of medicines helps avoid unsafe products. Checking MRP, quantity and the bill helps identify overcharging and provides evidence for a complaint.

NCERT EXERCISE · Q4

Mention a few factors which cause exploitation of consumers.

Answer:

Under-weighing, adulteration, defective goods, overcharging, hidden charges, misleading claims, unsafe products and denial of choice can exploit consumers. Exploitation is easier when sellers or producers are more powerful and consumers lack information or awareness.

NCERT EXERCISE · Q5

What is the rationale behind the enactment of Consumer Protection Act 1986?

Answer:

COPRA was enacted to protect consumers from exploitation and unfair trade practices and to provide a legal mechanism for consumer grievances. It strengthened consumer rights and created a three-tier consumer dispute redressal system.

NCERT EXERCISE · Q6

Describe some of your duties as consumers if you visit a shopping complex in your locality.

Answer:

Check price, quantity, quality, expiry date and product information; look for relevant quality marks; insist on a bill and preserve it; compare products and prices; avoid misleading offers; and complain when unfair practices are noticed.

NCERT EXERCISE · Q7

Suppose you buy a bottle of honey and a biscuit packet. Which logo or mark will you have to look for and why?

Answer:

A consumer should check the relevant quality/certification information and carefully inspect the package. Agmark is associated with agricultural products such as edible oil and cereals in the chapter. The purpose of checking appropriate certification is to obtain assurance about specified standards while also checking ingredients, quantity, dates and other package information.

NCERT EXERCISE · Q8

What legal measures were taken by the government to empower consumers in India?

Answer:

The government enacted COPRA in 1986, created a legal framework for consumer protection and established a three-tier Consumer Disputes Redressal mechanism. Consumer affairs departments and consumer organisations also support awareness. The chapter notes the 2019 amendment, inclusion of internet purchases and encouragement of mediation.

NCERT EXERCISE · Q9

Mention some of the rights of consumers and write a few sentences on each.

Answer:

The six major rights are: Right to Safety — protection from hazardous goods and services; Right to Information — access to information needed for informed choice; Right to Choice — freedom to select goods and services; Right to Seek Redressal — ability to seek remedy or compensation; Right to Representation — ability to represent consumer interests; and Right to Consumer Education — knowledge needed to understand and exercise rights.

NCERT EXERCISE · Q10

By what means can the consumers express their solidarity?

Answer:

Consumers can join or support consumer organisations, participate in awareness campaigns, form consumer groups, raise collective complaints, use class-action mechanisms where appropriate, share information about unfair practices and participate actively in the consumer movement.

NCERT EXERCISE · Q11

Critically examine the progress of consumer movement in India.

Answer:

The movement has expanded consumer awareness, consumer organisations, consumer affairs departments and legal redressal institutions. COPRA strengthened protection and was amended in 2019. However, redressal can be cumbersome, expensive and time-consuming; evidence may be difficult to collect; enforcement can be weak; and awareness is still spreading slowly. Therefore, progress is substantial but incomplete.

NCERT EXERCISE · Q12

Match the following.

Answer:

Correct matches: (i) Availing details of ingredients of a product → Right to information; (ii) Agmark → Certification of edible oil and cereals; (iii) Accident due to faulty engine in a scooter → Right to safety; (iv) District Consumer Commission → Dealing with consumer cases; (v) Food fortification → Addition of key nutrients to staple foods; (vi) Consumers International → Global-level institution of consumer welfare organisations; (vii) Bureau of Indian Standards → Agency that develops standards for goods and services.

NCERT EXERCISE · Q13

Say True or False.

Answer:

(i) False — COPRA is not limited only to goods; services are also covered. (ii) True — India has consumer dispute redressal authorities. (iii) False — a consumer does not have to file every case at the District Commission regardless of the applicable jurisdiction. (iv) False — consumers can seek redressal even for lower-value grievances. (v) True — Hallmark is associated with jewellery certification. (vi) False — the chapter notes that redressal can be cumbersome, expensive and time-consuming. (vii) True — compensation depends on the degree of damage.

Q1

Differentiate between a Consumer Protection Council and a Consumer Commission.

Answer:

A council/organisation mainly creates awareness, guides and supports consumers; a Consumer Commission formally hears consumer disputes and can provide remedies.

Q2

Why should a consumer preserve a bill?

Answer:

It provides evidence of purchase and can support a complaint for refund, replacement or compensation.

Q3

Why is RTI useful in the context of consumer rights?

Answer:

It helps citizens obtain information about government functioning and public services, supporting accountability and informed action.

Q4

Why can a consumer be exploited through misleading information?

Answer:

False or incomplete information can prevent the buyer from making an informed choice.

Q5

Put the complaint process in order: hearing, evidence, contact seller, decision, approach commission.

Answer:

Evidence → Contact seller/service provider → Approach appropriate Consumer Commission → Hearing → Decision/remedy.

Q1

A shopkeeper refuses to provide a bill for a defective product. What should the consumer do?

Answer:

Ask for proof of purchase, preserve any available evidence, seek resolution from the seller, take guidance from a consumer organisation if necessary and approach the appropriate Consumer Commission when the grievance is not resolved.

Q2

A consumer knows the six rights but never checks MRP, expiry or bills. Is the consumer fully conscious? Explain.

Answer:

No. Consumer consciousness requires practical action. A well-informed consumer checks product information, preserves bills, compares choices and acts when rights are violated.

Q3

A coaching institute refuses to refund a student who leaves after poor service. Which right is relevant and why?

Answer:

The Right to Choose and the Right to Seek Redressal are relevant. The consumer should be able to make a choice about continuing the service and can seek a remedy for deficiency in service.

Q4

Why are Consumer Protection Councils and Consumer Commissions not interchangeable?

Answer:

Councils/organisations primarily provide awareness, guidance and support, while Consumer Commissions perform the formal consumer-dispute redressal function and can issue remedies.

Q5

Global consumer awareness survey: only 30% of respondents check bills and 25% check expiry dates. What does this suggest?

Answer:

It suggests low consumer alertness in those areas. The result can be used to plan awareness activities on bills, expiry dates, product information and complaint mechanisms.
