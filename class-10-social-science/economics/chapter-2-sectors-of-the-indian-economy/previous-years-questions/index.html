Short Answer Questions (3 Marks)
2026 — Q38a, 2022, 2019

SA-8. Describe the importance of the Tertiary Sector in the economy.

Answer:
  1. Supports Production: It provides essential trade tools like transport, communication, storage, and banking without which primary/secondary production cannot operate smoothly.
  2. Dominant GDP Contributor: It produces more than half of India's GDP (around 57–60% GVA share).
  3. Creates Quality Employment: It is the fastest-growing job provider for urban workers, administrative professionals, and technology experts (e.g., IT and software).
Long Answer Questions (5 Marks)
2022, 2019

LA-3. How are the three sectors interdependent? Explain with examples.

Answer: All three sectors rely heavily on one another; if one stops, the others suffer:
  1. Primary → Secondary: Sugar mills (secondary) need sugarcane (primary); cotton textile factories need raw cotton. Without farming, these industries cannot operate.
  2. Secondary → Primary: Farmers require manufactured industrial inputs such as tractors, chemical fertilizers, irrigation pumps, and pesticides to run modern farms.
  3. Tertiary → Primary & Secondary: Services transport crops and manufactured goods to cities, provide loans (banking), and manage supply networks (communication/IT).
  4. Primary/Secondary → Tertiary: Without goods produced by farms and factories, transport trucks would sit idle, shops would have nothing to sell, and banking services would experience a downturn.

Q5. Cotton is grown by a farmer → sold to a textile mill → cloth is made. Cotton here is:

Answer: (B) Intermediate good
Explanation: Cotton is a raw material used to produce cloth (final good). Goods used inside the production process to produce other goods are intermediate goods.

Q8. GDP is the value of ___ produced during a year within the domestic territory.

Answer: (C) Final goods and services
Explanation: Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is the value of all final goods and services produced within a country during a particular year. Intermediate goods are excluded.
2022, 2020

Q9. Why are intermediate goods excluded from GDP?

Answer: (B) To avoid double counting
Explanation: The value of intermediate goods is already included in the value of the final good. Adding them separately would count their value multiple times, resulting in double counting.
2021

Q10. Which of the following is a final good?

Answer: (C) Bread bought by a consumer
Explanation: Bread purchased by a consumer is directly consumed and does not undergo further production processing. All other options are intermediate goods.

Very Short Answer Questions (2 Marks)
2023, 2022, 2020, 2016

VSA-1. Define **Gross Domestic Product (GDP)**.

Answer: Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is the total market value of all final goods and services produced within the boundaries of a country during a particular year.
2022, 2020, 2017

VSA-3. Distinguish between final goods and intermediate goods.

Answer:
  • Final Goods: Goods meant for direct use by final consumers. (e.g., Bread bought by a household). Their value is counted in GDP.
  • Intermediate Goods: Goods used as raw material or inputs for further production. (e.g., Flour bought by a bakery). Excluded from GDP to avoid double counting.
Short Answer Questions (3 Marks)
2022, 2020, 2017, 2016

SA-4. How is GDP calculated? Why are only final goods counted?

Answer:
  1. Calculation: The value of final goods and services produced in the Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary sectors are calculated individually (Quantity × Price) and then summed up to give the GDP of a country.
  2. Final Goods only: Intermediate goods' values are already embedded in the final good's price.
  3. Double Counting: Counting raw materials separately counts the same value multiple times, distorting the actual size of the national economy.

Very Short Answer Questions (2 Marks)
2023, 2022, 2019, 2017, 2015, 2014

VSA-2. What is disguised unemployment? Give one example.

Answer: Disguised unemployment is a situation where more people are working than necessary. Since their marginal productivity is zero, removing them does not reduce the total production output. Example: If five members of a family work on a small farm that only requires three workers to run, the extra two are disguisedly unemployed.
2022, 2019, 2018, 2016, 2015

VSA-6. What is NREGA? State its main objective.

Answer: National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (2005) is a social safety program. Its objective is to guarantee 100 days of wage employment in a financial year to rural households willing to do unskilled manual work, reducing rural unemployment and migration.
2019, 2017

VSA-9. Define **underemployment**. How is it different from disguised unemployment?

Answer: Underemployment occurs when a person is working but less than their capacity, or is forced to work in jobs below their qualification level. Disguised unemployment is a specific subset of underemployment where the extra worker's marginal contribution is zero.

SA-1. Why has the **Tertiary Sector** grown rapidly in India? Give three reasons.

Answer:
  1. Basic Services: The government has taken responsibility for providing essential services such as hospitals, educational institutions, post offices, defense, and transport.
  2. Industrial & Agriculture Development: Expansion of primary and secondary sectors creates demand for support services like trade, storage, and transport.
  3. Rising Incomes: As incomes rise, people demand services like eating out, tourism, shopping, private schools, and professional medical services.

SA-3. How can the **government create more employment** in rural areas?

Answer:
  1. Infrastructure Development: Funding canals, dams, and roads creates direct employment and enables year-round multi-cropping.
  2. Promoting Rural Industries: Encouraging agro-processing mills, cold storage facilities, and honey collection centers generates off-farm jobs.
  3. Access to Cheap Credit: Offering affordable bank loans lets farmers buy seeds/equipment or start small cottage businesses.
2022, 2019, 2017, 2016

SA-5. Explain **disguised unemployment** with suitable examples.

Answer:
  • Definition: A situation where more workers are engaged in an activity than required, hiding the fact that some are underemployed. If removed, total production is unaffected.
  • Rural Example: Laxmi is a small farmer with a 2-hectare plot of unirrigated land. All 5 family members work on it because they have nowhere else to go, even though 3 could produce the same yield.
  • Urban Example: Thousands of casual painters, plumbers, and street vendors work all day but earn very little because there are too many people competing for the same jobs.
Long Answer Questions (5 Marks)
2022, 2019, 2018, 2016, 2015

LA-1. How has the government created employment through **NREGA**? Explain in detail.

Answer: MGNREGA has generated significant employment through these key features:
  1. Legal Guarantee: It promises 100 days of guaranteed work in a year. If the government fails to provide it within 15 days, it pays an unemployment allowance.
  2. Village Proximity: Work is provided within 5 kilometers of the worker's home, reducing distress migration to cities.
  3. Asset Creation: The tasks focus on rural development (e.g., land development, water harvesting, canals, roads) which increases farming productivity.
  4. Inclusive Wages: At least one-third of the jobs are reserved for women, and wages are paid directly to bank/post office accounts, cutting out middlemen.
2022, 2019, 2017, 2016

LA-2. Describe the **historical changes in the share of three sectors** in India's GDP and employment.

Answer:
  • GDP Transition: Between 1973-74 and 2013-14, India's GDP grew dramatically. The primary sector's share shrank from ~42% to ~14%, the secondary sector grew slightly to ~27%, and the tertiary sector surged from ~34% to ~59%, becoming the largest producer.
  • Workforce Lag: Employment did not transition at the same rate. The primary sector remains the largest employer, absorbing ~49% of workers in 2011-12. Secondary grew to ~24% and Tertiary to ~27%.
  • Key Finding: Unlike developed nations that moved employment to industries and services, India has a structural gap. Nearly half the workforce is in agriculture generating only 14% of GDP, resulting in severe disguised unemployment.
2019, 2017, 2016

LA-6. Compare the **Primary Sector's contribution** to GDP vs Employment. What does the gap indicate?

Answer:
  • GDP vs Employment: The primary sector accounts for only 13–15% of India's GDP, but it employs around 49% of the country's workforce.
  • Indications:
    1. Disguised Unemployment: There are more people in agriculture than needed, diluting individual labor productivity.
    2. Low Per-Worker Productivity: An agricultural worker produces far less value than a worker in the secondary or tertiary sectors.
    3. Lack of Alternative Jobs: The secondary and tertiary sectors did not generate enough industrial/service jobs to absorb surplus farm labor.

SA-7. Why does the government need to take up activities in the **Public Sector**?

Answer:
  1. Huge Capital Investment: Infrastructure like railways, dams, and highways requires vast funds that private capital cannot or will not assemble.
  2. Non-Excludable / Public Goods: Services must be available to everyone at subsidized, fair prices (like electricity and food grains via ration shops), which conflicts with private profit-seeking.
  3. Regional Development: The government sets up plants in backward regions to reduce disparities and generate regional employment.
Long Answer Questions (5 Marks)
2022, 2019, 2017

LA-4. How can the government help workers in the **Unorganised Sector**?

Answer: The government can support unorganized workers through these policy measures:
  1. Strict Enforcement of Laws: Regularly inspect factories to enforce the Minimum Wages Act and limit excessive daily working hours.
  2. Financial Assistance: Provide subsidized credit to street vendors and small farmers to break their dependence on exploitative local moneylenders.
  3. Social Security Nets: Set up affordable health insurance, pension programs (like PM Shram Yogi Maan-dhan), and provident fund options for casual workers.
  4. Skill and Training: Launch training centers to help rural workers learn new skills, making it easier for them to transition into formal, organized jobs.
  5. Direct Infrastructure Support: Build government-backed markets, warehouses, and storage structures so small producers can sell crops directly without dealers.
2022, 2019, 2014

LA-5. Explain the concept of **Public Sector** and describe its contribution to economic development.

Answer: The public sector covers businesses and assets owned and run directly by the government to provide public services.
Contributions to economic development:
  1. Infrastructure Backbone: It constructed national transportation (railways/highways) and heavy industries (steel/power plants) that private capital avoided.
  2. Balanced Regional Growth: By placing factories in backward districts, it brought infrastructure and income to under-developed areas.
  3. Dignified Employment: It acts as a major employer, offering secure jobs with fair wages, medical support, and pension benefits.
  4. Ensuring Social Equity: It runs subsidized programs (like PDS shops and state-run clinics) to protect low-income households from market pricing.
  5. Economic Stability: By managing strategic sectors like defense, space research, and banking (public sector banks), it keeps national development secure.

Short Answer Questions (3 Marks)
2026 · Q38 [SA - Choice A]

Q38 (a). Describe the importance of the Tertiary Sector in the Indian Economy.

Answer: The tertiary sector is critical for the Indian economy due to:
  1. Highest GDP Share: It is the largest producing sector in India, contributing over 55% of the total Gross Value Added.
  2. Supporting Primary & Secondary Sectors: It provides essential logistics, storage, trading, and financing (banking) systems without which farms and factories cannot trade.
  3. Rise of New Services: It drives innovation and high-value exports via information technology, software parks, call centers, and internet services.
2026 · Q38 [SA - Choice B]

Q38 (b). Describe the features of the Organised Sector.

Answer: Features of the organised sector include:
  1. Government Registration: Enterprises are registered with the government and must follow regulatory laws (like the Factories Act and Shop Act).
  2. Job Security: Employees have assured employment, written contracts, and clear terms of service; they cannot be laid off without legal procedure.
  3. Mandatory Employee Benefits: Employers must provide paid leave, provident fund (PF) contribution, gratuity, medical support, and overtime pay for work beyond standard hours.
