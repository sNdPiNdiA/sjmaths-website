1. Different people, different goals

Development

Development means progress towards a better life. Its meaning changes with a person's situation, needs and aspirations.

Different aspirations

  • Landless workers may seek more work, higher wages, quality schools and freedom from discrimination.
  • Farmers may seek fair crop prices, secure income and reliable irrigation.
  • Women may seek equal freedom, education, safety, respect and shared housework.
  • Unemployed youth may seek dignified, regular work.

Conflicting goals

A large dam may provide electricity and irrigation, but can also displace tribal communities. Development for one group may harm another.

Income and other goals

  • Income matters because it helps people obtain goods and services.
  • Equal treatment, freedom, security, respect and a safe environment also matter.
  • A lower-paid but secure job may be preferred to a high-paid insecure job with no family time.

National development

A fair national path considers different and conflicting interests, asks who benefits, and aims to help a large number of people.

Democratic process

Debate, participation and democratic decision-making help society decide priorities and resolve conflicts fairly.

2. Comparing countries and states

Purpose decides the criterion

We compare people or countries using features relevant to the purpose. For all-round development, more than one important indicator is needed.

Per capita income

Per capita income = Total income ÷ Total population

It shows average income and allows comparison between places with different populations.

World Bank classification

The World Bank uses per capita income to classify countries. The NCERT 2024 figures classify US$66,500 or above as high income and about US$2,300 or below as low income.

Limitation: inequality

Average income hides distribution. Two countries can have the same average even when one has many poor people and one very rich person.

Income and other criteria

Income alone cannot secure clean air, safe medicines, disease prevention or quality schooling. Health and education indicators are essential.

State comparison

Haryana had higher per capita income than Kerala in 2023–24, yet Kerala performed better in infant mortality, literacy and secondary attendance.

3. Public facilities and human development

Public facilities

Schools, healthcare, sanitation, clean water, transport, security and the Public Distribution System are often cheaper and more effective when provided collectively.

Infant Mortality Rate

IMR is the number of children who die before age one per 1,000 live births in a year.

Literacy and attendance

Literacy rate is the proportion of literate people aged seven and above. Net attendance ratio measures 15–17-year-olds in school as a percentage of that age group.

Human Development Index

UNDP's Human Development Report compares countries using health, education and income. HDI puts people and their well-being at the centre.

Key HDI terms

  • Life expectancy: average expected length of life at birth.
  • Mean years of schooling: average years of education for people aged 25 and above.
  • GNI per capita: income per person, compared using purchasing power parity (PPP).
  • Gross enrolment ratio: enrolment at a level of education relative to the official age-group population.

NCERT data insight

In the Human Development Report 2025 data for 2023, Sri Lanka outperformed India in GNI per capita, life expectancy and mean years of schooling. Bangladesh and Nepal had higher life expectancy than India despite lower per capita income.

4. Sustainability of development

Meaning

Development must meet present needs without reducing future generations' ability to meet theirs.

Renewable resources

Resources such as groundwater, crops and plants can be replenished, but are overused when extraction exceeds natural replacement.

Groundwater

Falling water levels show why use must be limited to recharge. Water conservation, rainwater harvesting and efficient irrigation support sustainability.

Non-renewable resources

Crude oil and minerals have a fixed stock and will eventually be exhausted. Discovery can add known reserves but cannot make them endless.

Oil dependence

India imports crude oil. Rising prices can increase costs across transport, production and household life.

Shared future

Environmental harm crosses local, state and national boundaries. Sustainable choices need cooperation among scientists, economists, governments and citizens.
