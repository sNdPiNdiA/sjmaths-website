05 • Democratic Outcomes

Chapter at a Glance

Democracy is a form of government that creates conditions for citizens to pursue goals. Its outcomes must be assessed against reasonable expectations rather than assuming democracy automatically solves every socio-economic and political problem.

First principle

Democracy is only a form of government; it creates conditions, while citizens must use those conditions to achieve goals.

Government outcome

Accountable, responsive and legitimate government with procedures and transparency.

Economic outcome

Democracy is not a guarantee of faster economic development.

Equality outcome

Political equality can coexist with severe economic inequalities.

Social outcome

Democracy can accommodate social differences and reduce the chance of conflict becoming violent.

Human outcome

Democracy is superior in promoting dignity and freedom of individuals.

01 · Assessment

How Do We Assess Democracy's Outcomes?

Democracy is considered better than dictatorship or other alternatives because it promotes equality, enhances individual dignity, improves decision-making, provides a method to resolve conflicts and allows room to correct mistakes. But democracy should not be expected to solve every problem automatically.

KEY DISTINCTION: “Democracy is good in principle” does not mean every democratic government performs equally well in practice.
02 · Government

Accountable, Responsive and Legitimate Government

The most basic expected outcome is a government accountable to citizens and responsive to their needs and expectations. Citizens should have the right to choose rulers, control rulers and participate in decision-making when possible and necessary.

Accountable

Government can be held answerable by citizens through institutions and procedures.

Responsive

Government is attentive to people's needs and demands.

Legitimate

Government is people's own government because rulers are elected by them.

Transparent

Citizens can examine whether decisions were taken through correct procedures.

EFFICIENCY ≠ DEMOCRATIC QUALITY: Non-democratic rulers may decide faster because they need less deliberation. Democracy can take more time because of procedures and negotiation, but those decisions may be more acceptable and effective because people have been involved.
03 · Actual Record

Mixed Performance of Democracies

The chapter reports a mixed record. Democracies have greater success in holding regular, free elections and creating conditions for open public debate, but many fall short of ensuring a fair chance to everyone, subjecting every decision to public debate and sharing information adequately with citizens.

Expected Actual record in chapter
Regular, free elections Greater success
Open public debate Greater success in creating conditions
Fair chance for everyone Many democracies fall short
Public debate on every decision Many democracies fall short
Information to citizens Record is not very good
Responsive, corruption-free government Record is not impressive
STRONGEST CLAIM: Even when democracy is slow, less efficient or not fully responsive, it has one clear advantage: legitimacy.
04 · Legitimacy

Why Is Democratic Government Legitimate?

Democratic government is people's own government. People prefer representatives elected by them and believe democracy is suitable for their country. The chapter treats democracy's ability to generate support for itself as an outcome that cannot be ignored.

05 · Economic Growth

Economic Growth and Development

It is reasonable to ask whether democracy produces development, but the chapter's evidence does not support treating democracy as a guarantee of faster economic growth. For 1950–2000, the cited comparison shows dictatorships had a slightly higher average growth rate overall.

1950–2000 category Growth rate shown in chapter
All democratic regimes 3.95
All dictatorial regimes 4.42
Poor countries under dictatorship 4.34
Poor countries under democracy 4.28

Economic development depends on many factors including population size, global situation, cooperation from other countries and economic priorities. Among less developed countries, the difference between dictatorship and democracy is described as negligible.

EXAM CONCLUSION: Democracy is not a guarantee of economic development, but it can reasonably be expected not to lag behind dictatorships in this respect.
06 · Inequality

Reduction of Inequality and Poverty

Democracies are based on political equality: each individual has equal weight in electing representatives. Yet the chapter observes that growing economic inequalities can exist alongside political equality. A small number of ultra-rich people may enjoy a highly disproportionate share of wealth and income, while people at the bottom may struggle to meet basic needs.

IMPORTANT: The poor are a large proportion of voters, yet democratically elected governments do not always address poverty as strongly as expected. The chapter therefore concludes that democracies do not appear very successful in reducing economic inequalities.
07 · Social Diversity

Accommodation of Social Diversity

Democracy is well suited to accommodate social differences, divisions and conflicts because it develops procedures for political competition. This can reduce the possibility that tensions become explosive or violent. No society can permanently resolve all conflicts, but democracy can help citizens respect differences and negotiate them.

Condition 1

Democracy is not simply rule by majority opinion. The majority must work with the minority so government represents the general view.

Condition 2

Majority rule must not become rule by a majority community based on religion, race, language or another birth-based identity.

Meaning of majority

Different persons and groups can form a majority at different times.

08 · Dignity & Freedom

Dignity and Freedom of Citizens

The chapter presents dignity and freedom as areas where democracy stands much superior to other forms of government. Democratic principles recognise the equal status of individuals and provide a legal and moral basis for struggles against domination and discrimination.

Women

Long struggles have created greater sensitivity to equal treatment and respect for women. Recognition of equality strengthens the basis for continued struggle.

Caste

Democracy in India has strengthened the claims of disadvantaged and discriminated castes for equal status and opportunity.

Citizen identity

People can move from the status of subjects toward citizens who critically examine and challenge power holders.

DEMOCRATIC PARADOX: People having more complaints about democracy can itself indicate success: citizens have awareness, rights and the confidence to question those in power.
09 · Final Insight

Democracy's Examination Never Gets Over

As democracy passes one test, it produces another. Citizens who receive some benefits ask for more and want democracy to improve. The chapter treats public dissatisfaction as evidence that people have developed the awareness and ability to expect more from power holders.

NCERT Exercise

Questions from the supplied Chapter 5 exercise, followed by concise answer frameworks grounded in the chapter.

1 · Accountable, Responsive & Legitimate

How does democracy produce an accountable, responsive and legitimate government?

Answer framework

Through regular, free and fair elections; citizens' right to choose and control rulers; public debate; procedures and norms; transparency; citizens' right to information; and mechanisms for participation and accountability. Democratic government is legitimate because it is people's own elected government.

2 · Social Diversity

What are the conditions under which democracies accommodate social diversities?

Answer

First, majority must work with minority so government represents the general view. Second, majority rule must not become permanent rule by a majority community based on religion, race, language or birth. Different groups must have a chance to form a majority at different times.

3 · Assertion / Argument Questions

(a) Industrialised countries can afford democracy but the poor need dictatorship to become rich.
Answer direction

Oppose. The chapter does not establish dictatorship as a necessary route to development. Democracy is not a guarantee of growth, but among poor countries the difference in growth rates cited is negligible. Development also depends on many other factors.

(b) Democracy can't reduce inequality of incomes between different citizens.
Answer direction

Partly support as an empirical observation: democracies have not been very successful in reducing economic inequalities, and severe inequality can persist. But this does not mean democracy can never influence economic inequality.

(c) Poor countries should spend less on poverty reduction, health and education and more on industries and infrastructure.
Answer direction

Do not accept as a conclusion from this chapter. The chapter asks students to debate priorities and does not support a blanket rule that social spending should be reduced.

(d) One vote for every citizen means absence of domination and conflict.
Answer direction

Oppose. Political equality does not eliminate social or economic inequalities. Democracy's strength lies in accommodating differences and allowing citizens to challenge domination.

4 · Challenges to Democracy

(a) Temple entry for Dalits and non-Dalits through the same door.
Answer direction

Challenge: caste-based discrimination and unequal dignity. Mechanism: equal legal access, enforcement of non-discrimination and institutional protection of equal status.

(b) Farmers committing suicide in different states.
Answer direction

Challenge: economic distress and government responsiveness to citizens' needs. Mechanism: responsive policy, public accountability and effective institutional attention to livelihood concerns.

(c) Enquiry into alleged fake encounter by police.
Answer direction

Challenge: accountability of state institutions and protection of citizens' rights. Mechanism: independent enquiry, transparent procedure and accountability of officials.

5 · What Have Democracies Successfully Eliminated?

Which idea is correct?

Answer

D. The idea of political inequality. Democracy is based on political equality, although economic and social inequalities can continue.

6 · Odd One Out

In assessing democracy, which is the odd one out?

Answer

C. Majority rule. The chapter stresses that democracy must not become simple or permanent majority-community rule; it must accommodate minorities.

7 · Political & Social Inequalities

Studies on political and social inequalities in democracy show that:

Answer

B. Inequalities exist in democracies.

8 · Nannu's RTI Case

Nannu, a daily wage earner, used the Right to Information Act after officials failed to process his duplicate ration card application. What does the example show and what impact did his action have?

Answer framework

The example shows how transparency and the right to information can help ordinary citizens hold government officials accountable. Once Nannu sought information about the progress of his application and officials' responsibility, the matter moved quickly and the officials became responsive.
