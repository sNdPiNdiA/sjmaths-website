01 · Definition

What is Federalism?

Federalism is a system of government in which power is divided between a central authority and various constituent units. Usually there are two levels: a government for the entire country handling some common national interests, and governments at province/state level handling much day-to-day administration. Both levels enjoy power independent of the other.

MEMORY: FEDERAL = “Federal family, separate jobs.” Levels share the country but have their own jurisdictions.
02 · Unitary vs Federal

The Essential Difference

Point Federal Unitary
Levels Two or more levels with own jurisdictions One level, or sub-units subordinate to Centre
State authority State has powers of its own Central government can direct sub-units
Answerability Each level is separately answerable to people Sub-units are subordinate to central authority
Constitution Division of powers constitutionally specified Central authority can generally alter sub-unit powers
EXAM TRICK: If the question asks the main difference, start with “division of power between different levels of government” versus “sub-units subordinate to the central government.”

06 · India

India is a Federal Country

The Constitution declared India a Union of States. Though it did not use the word “federation”, the Indian Union is based on federal principles. Originally there were Union/Central and State governments; later Panchayats and Municipalities added a third tier.

07 · Three Lists

Union List, State List & Concurrent List

MEMORY: U = Uniform nation → defence, foreign affairs, banking, communications, currency. S = State daily life → police, agriculture, irrigation. C = Common → education, forest, trade unions, marriage.
EXAM TRAP: Concurrent does NOT mean “only Centre”. Both Union and State can legislate; if laws conflict, the Union law prevails.
08 · Special Powers

Unequal Powers & Union Territories

India is described as a “holding together” federation. Therefore, all constituent units do not necessarily have identical powers. The chapter mentions special provisions for Assam, Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh and Mizoram under Article 371 because of particular social and historical circumstances, including protection of land rights, culture and preferential government employment. Union Territories such as Chandigarh, Lakshadweep and Delhi have fewer powers than States and the Central Government has special powers in running them.

10 · Practice

Linguistic States

The creation of linguistic States was the first major test for democratic politics. Many old States changed and new ones were created. Some were formed around language; others recognised culture, ethnicity or geography, such as Nagaland, Uttarakhand and Jharkhand.

MEMORY: “Language → Recognition → Unity”, not language → disintegration.
11 · Practice

Language Policy

The Constitution did not give any one language the status of national language. Hindi was identified as the official language; the chapter notes safeguards for other languages and 21 other Scheduled Languages in the relevant discussion. States have their own official languages. The continuation of English along with Hindi followed demands from non-Hindi-speaking States, especially after the Tamil Nadu movement.

IMPORTANT WORDING: “Official language” ≠ “national language”. Also, promotion of Hindi does not mean the Centre can impose Hindi on States where people speak another language.
MNEMONIC: “N-O-S-F” → No national language → Official Hindi → Scheduled/State languages → Flexibility.
12 · Practice

Centre-State Relations

The chapter explains that federal arrangements work in reality depending on how ruling parties and leaders follow them. Earlier, one party often ruled at the Centre and in most States, and State autonomy was weakened; the source notes misuse of constitutional provisions to dismiss rival State governments. After 1990, regional parties rose and coalition governments became common at the Centre, creating a new culture of power sharing and respect for State autonomy. A major Supreme Court judgement also made arbitrary dismissal harder.

MNEMONIC: “1990 = R-C-A” → Regional parties ↑, Coalition era, Autonomy/respect ↑.
13 · Linguistic Diversity

India's Language Diversity

The 2011 Census recorded more than 1300 distinct languages mentioned as mother tongues, grouped into 121 major languages. The chapter says 22 languages were included in the Eighth Schedule and called Scheduled Languages. Hindi was the largest mother tongue at about 44% in the later diversity discussion, still below half of the population.

DON'T OVER-MEMORISE: For exams, remember the hierarchy and the chapter's main argument: India has enormous linguistic diversity and no single language is the mother tongue of a majority of the population.
14 · Decentralisation

Why a Third Tier?

India is vast and States themselves are internally diverse. The chapter therefore explains the need for power sharing within States. Decentralisation means taking power away from Central and State governments and giving it to local government because many local problems are best settled locally, where people know the problems and can participate directly.

“LOCAL” TEST: Local problems → Own knowledge → Citizen participation → Administration closer → Local self-government.
15 · 1992 Amendment

Making Local Government Stronger

Regular elections

Constitutionally mandatory for local government bodies.

Reservation

Seats and executive heads reserved for SCs, STs and OBCs.

Women

At least one-third of all positions reserved for women.

State Election Commission

Independent institution in each State conducts panchayat and municipal elections.

Power + Revenue

State governments are required to share some powers and revenue with local bodies; nature varies by State.

1992 MNEMONIC: “E-R-W-S-P” → Elections, Reservation, Women, SEC, Powers/Revenue.

18 · Outcomes & Limits

Achievements and Remaining Difficulties

The chapter describes local government as the largest experiment in democracy conducted anywhere in the world, with about 36 lakh elected representatives across panchayats and municipalities at the time of the textbook discussion. Constitutional status deepened democracy and increased women's representation and voice. Yet gram sabhas are not held regularly in many places, and most State governments have not transferred significant powers or adequate resources. The ideal of self-government therefore remains incomplete.

19 · Brazil Example

Participatory Democracy in Porto Alegre

Porto Alegre in Brazil combined decentralisation with participative democracy. Local inhabitants could participate in making the city budget through ward-level and city-wide meetings. The source says about 20,000 people participated each year and describes outcomes including better allocation toward poorer colonies and safeguards for slum-dwellers. A similar participatory planning experiment is mentioned in some areas of Kerala.

3 · India & Belgium

Point out one feature in the practice of federalism in India that is similar to and one that is different from Belgium.

Answer direction

Similar: power is shared between national/central and regional governments with constitutional arrangements. Different: India is a holding-together federation with a more powerful Union and unequal powers for some States; Belgium also shifted from unitary to federal to accommodate linguistic communities.

4 · Federal vs Unitary

What is the main difference between a federal form of government and a unitary one? Explain with an example.

Answer framework

Federal: constitutionally divided power between different levels, each with its own jurisdiction. Unitary: one level or subordinate sub-units controlled by the Centre. Use India as federal and Sri Lanka as a largely unitary example from the chapter.

5 · Local Government Before & After 1992

State any two differences between local government before and after the Constitutional Amendment in 1992.

Answer points

Before: elections not regular; local bodies lacked adequate powers/resources and were under State control. After: regular elections constitutionally mandatory; reservations; State Election Commission; some powers and revenue to be shared.

Group Defence, Police, Agriculture, Education, Banking, Forests, Communications, Trade and Marriages under Union, State and Concurrent Lists.
