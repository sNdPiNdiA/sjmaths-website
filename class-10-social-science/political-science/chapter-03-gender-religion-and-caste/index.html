03 • Social Differences

Chapter at a Glance

Social differences can become social divisions—but democratic politics can also express these differences and help disadvantaged groups demand equality.

Core idea

Social diversity itself does not threaten democracy; exclusionary political use of differences can.

Gender

Mostly socially constructed roles, unequal division of labour and unequal political representation.

Religion

Religious expression in politics can be legitimate; communalism is exclusionary and dangerous.

Secularism

Indian secularism treats all religions equally and permits state intervention to prevent discrimination.

Caste

Historical hierarchy and exclusion continue to shape economic and political inequalities.

Political result

Identity politics can empower disadvantaged groups, but exclusive identity politics can create conflict.

Complete Concept Learning

01 · Gender Division

Gender is a Social Division

The chapter describes gender division as a hierarchical social division found widely across societies. It is often treated as natural, but it is based largely on social expectations and stereotypes rather than biology. Boys and girls are commonly socialised into different roles.

EXAM WORD: Sexual division of labour = a system in which work inside the home is assigned to women or organised by them through domestic helpers.
02 · Public / Private

Domestic Work and Political Power

Women do extensive paid and unpaid work, but domestic labour is often undervalued and less visible. The chapter connects this private division of labour to public inequality: if power relations exist inside households, they are relevant to political analysis too.

Dimension Common pattern described Political consequence
Home Cooking, cleaning, childcare, washing, tailoring Unpaid work is undervalued
Outside Jobs, professions, public work Paid work is more visible
Decision-making Male dominance in many households Unequal power can persist
Public life Women historically excluded or underrepresented Political equality becomes a demand
DATA TO REMEMBER: The textbook's time-use survey example shows women spending substantially more time on household-related work, while men's work is more visible because it more often generates income.
03 · Feminist Movements

From Equality Demands to Political Participation

Women organised and agitated for equal rights, voting rights, better legal and political status, education and career opportunities. More radical movements also demanded equality in personal and family life. These are called feminist movements.

FEMINIST = a woman or man who believes in equal rights and opportunities for women and men.
04 · Patriarchy

Patriarchal Society

Patriarchy literally means rule by the father. In political science it refers to a system that values men more and gives them power over women. Gender division is therefore connected to power, not merely to different biological characteristics.

05 · Political Representation

Women in Political Life

Women gained political rights in many democracies, but representation remains unequal. The chapter notes women's under-representation in legislatures and the use of reservation in local government as an important step toward increasing women's participation.

Political equality

Equal voting rights and opportunities to contest elections.

Representation gap

Formal equality does not automatically produce equal representation.

Local reservation

Reservation in Panchayati Raj bodies created a major route for women's political participation.

ANSWER APPROACH: When asked “How is gender division political?”, connect social roles → unequal power → public participation → feminist demands → political representation.
06 · Religion & Politics

When Can Religion Enter Politics?

Religious differences can be expressed in politics. The chapter distinguishes legitimate democratic expression from communalism. Gandhiji's statement that religion cannot be separated from politics referred to moral values that should guide politics, not domination by a particular religion. Human-rights demands to protect minorities and demands to reform discriminatory family laws are also examples of religion-related political issues.

07 · Communalism

What is Communalism?

Communalism is based on the idea that religion is the principal basis of social community. It assumes followers of one religion form one community with identical fundamental interests and that different religious communities necessarily have conflicting interests. The chapter calls this belief fundamentally flawed because people have many identities and interests.

COMMUNALISM MNEMONIC: “P-D-M-V” → Prejudice → Dominance → Mobilisation → Violence.
08 · Secularism

India's Secular State

The chapter explains that the Indian Constitution does not establish any one religion as the official religion. It guarantees freedom to practise and propagate religion, prohibits discrimination on religious grounds, and permits the State to intervene in religious matters when necessary to ensure equality and prevent oppression.

EXAM: Two constitutional provisions you can cite: freedom of religion and no discrimination on grounds of religion.
09 · Caste System

Caste Inequalities in India

Caste division is presented as especially associated with India. Traditionally, occupations were hereditary and caste hierarchy was reinforced by rituals. Members were expected to marry within the caste and observe restrictions on social interaction. The system also excluded and discriminated against outcaste groups through untouchability.

10 · Change in Modern India

Why Old Caste Hierarchies Weakened

Economic development, urbanisation, literacy and education, occupational mobility and the weakening of landlord power in villages have changed caste relations. The old hierarchy has weakened considerably, but caste-based inequalities and discrimination have not disappeared.

Economic development

New occupations and opportunities weaken hereditary work patterns.

Urbanisation

Movement to towns and cities can reduce traditional caste separation.

Education

Literacy and education expand opportunities beyond inherited status.

Occupational mobility

People increasingly enter occupations different from ancestors.

Social reform

Phule, Gandhi, Ambedkar and Periyar challenged caste inequality.

Still unequal

Untouchability is constitutionally prohibited but historical disadvantages continue.

11 · Caste Inequality Today

Caste and Economic Status

Caste remains an important source of economic inequality because historical caste rules affected access to resources, education and land. Although explicit formal inequalities are outlawed, accumulated advantages and disadvantages continue. At the same time, there are rich and poor people in every caste, so caste does not mechanically determine economic status.

Historical pattern Modern change What remains
Some groups denied land Legal equality and mobility Effects of accumulated disadvantage
Education restricted by caste Broader access to education Unequal educational outcomes
Hereditary occupations Occupational mobility Caste-linked economic differences
Untouchability Constitutionally prohibited Not completely eliminated in practice
DO NOT WRITE: “Caste completely determines poverty.” The chapter explicitly stresses that rich and poor people can be found in every caste.
12 · Caste in Politics

How Caste Enters Politics

Caste can influence politics in several ways. Parties consider caste composition while choosing candidates; parties and candidates may appeal to caste sentiments; governments try to include representatives of different castes and tribes. But caste alone cannot determine electoral outcomes because voters have several identities and interests.

3 C's: Candidate selection → Caste appeals → Coalition/representation.
13 · Why Caste Alone Cannot Decide Elections

Multiple Identities Matter

One constituency, many castes

No single caste usually forms a complete majority everywhere.

Different interests

Members of one caste can disagree on economic and political issues.

Candidate popularity

Party image, leadership and performance matter.

Other identities

Religion, region, class, gender and local interests also influence choices.

Party competition

Parties need combinations of groups rather than one caste alone.

Changing alliances

Political coalitions change according to context.

EXAM FORMULA: “Caste matters, but caste alone does not decide.” Explain with multiple castes + multiple identities + candidate/party factors + changing alliances.
14 · Politics in Caste

Politics Also Changes Caste

The relationship is two-way. Politics does not simply become “caste-ridden”; caste identities themselves become politicised. Groups may broaden their caste identity by incorporating neighbouring castes, form coalitions with other communities, and develop new political categories such as backward and forward caste groups.

15 · Positive & Negative

When Identity Politics Helps—and Hurts

Can be democratic / positive Can be unhealthy / negative
Disadvantaged groups demand representation Exclusive identity becomes the only political issue
Dalits and OBCs gain access to decision-making Other important issues are ignored
Demand for dignity and equal opportunities Politics can produce tension and conflict
Pressure for ending discrimination In extreme cases, violence may result
DEMOCRATIC TEST: Identity-based mobilisation is healthier when it expands equality, dignity, representation and rights; it becomes dangerous when it demands exclusive domination.

NCERT Exercise

Chapter 3 exercise coverage with answer directions based on the supplied 2026–27 source.

1 · Gender Discrimination

Mention different aspects of life in which women are discriminated or disadvantaged in India.

Answer points

Unequal division of household labour; undervaluing domestic work; unequal access to education/careers in some contexts; lower political representation; patriarchal decision-making; unequal opportunities and recognition.

2 · Forms of Communal Politics

State different forms of communal politics with one example each.

Answer framework

Everyday prejudice/stereotypes; quest for political dominance; religious mobilisation during elections; communal violence/riots. Give a relevant example for each.

3 · Continuing Caste Inequalities

State how caste inequalities are still continuing in India.

Answer points

Historical educational/resource advantages persist; caste remains linked to economic status; untouchability has not completely disappeared; disadvantaged groups can lag in education and resources.

4 · Caste and Election Results

State two reasons why caste alone cannot determine election results in India.

Answer

Most constituencies contain several caste groups without one complete majority; voters have other identities and interests; party performance, candidates, leadership and alliances also matter.

5 · Women's Representation

What is the status of women's representation in India's legislative bodies?

Answer direction

Women have formal political equality but have historically remained underrepresented in legislatures. Local government reservations have substantially expanded participation.

6 · Secular State

Mention any two constitutional provisions that make India a secular state.

Answer

Freedom to profess/practise/propagate religion and prohibition of discrimination on grounds of religion. You may also mention that the State has no official religion.

7 · Gender Division

When we speak of gender divisions, what do we usually refer to?

Answer

(b) Unequal roles assigned by society to men and women.

8 · Women's Reservation

In India seats are reserved for women in which institution among the given options?

Answer

(d) Panchayati Raj bodies.

9 · Communal Politics

Which statements correctly describe communal politics?

Answer

(c) A and C. Communalism asserts superiority of one religion and treats followers of a religion as one community; it does not support equal plural citizenship.
