01 · Meaning

What Is a Political Party?

A political party is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in government. They agree on policies and programmes for society with a view to promote the collective good. Parties reflect fundamental political divisions in society and involve partisanship.

PARTISAN: A person strongly committed to a party, group or faction; partisanship involves taking a side and may make balanced judgment difficult.
02 · Functions

The Seven Functions of Political Parties

1. Contest elections

Parties select and put forward candidates for elections.

2. Policies & programmes

They group a large variety of views into a few basic positions from which voters choose.

3. Make laws

Party members in legislatures generally follow the direction of party leadership.

4. Form & run governments

Parties recruit and train leaders and make them ministers to run government.

5. Opposition

Parties that lose elections criticise the government and mobilise opposition.

6. Shape public opinion

They raise issues, highlight problems, mobilise activists and sometimes launch movements.

7. Access to government

Citizens can approach party leaders for help with government machinery and welfare schemes.

03 · Necessity

Why Do We Need Political Parties?

Without parties, every candidate would be independent. It would become difficult to make major policy promises, form a coherent government or assign responsibility for how the country is run. Even non-party panchayat elections often develop factions and panels that perform party-like functions.

CONCLUSION: Political parties are described in the chapter as a necessary condition for democracy.
04 · Party Systems

How Many Parties Are Good for Democracy?

System Meaning Textbook example
One-party Only one party is allowed to control/run government; no genuine competition for power. China (as described in the textbook)
Two-party Two main parties have a serious chance of winning a majority and forming government. USA, UK
Multiparty Several parties have a reasonable chance of coming to power alone or in alliances. India

In a multiparty system, parties may form a coalition to form government. An alliance/front is when several parties join hands for contesting elections and winning power.

IMPORTANT: The textbook says there is no single ideal party system for all countries. Party systems evolve according to social and regional divisions, political history and the electoral system.
05 · India's Multiparty System

Why Does India Have Many Parties?

India's social and geographical diversity is large and cannot easily be absorbed by two or three parties. India's multiparty system therefore reflects the country's special circumstances. It may appear messy and can create instability, but it allows a variety of interests and opinions to receive political representation.

Strength

Wide representation of diverse interests.

Challenge

Coalition management can be complex and may appear unstable.

Federal fit

Regional and social diversity encourages state-level and regional parties.

06 · Participation

Popular Participation in Political Parties

The chapter notes that political parties are among the least trusted institutions in many democracies, including South Asia. Yet participation in India has been relatively high: the share reporting party membership was higher than in several advanced countries in the cited survey, and party membership and closeness to parties increased over the preceding decades.

EXAM DISTINCTION: Low trust ≠ low participation. Citizens can criticise parties and still participate actively in party politics.
07 · National Parties

Recognised Political Parties

Every party must register with the Election Commission. Recognised parties receive special facilities; an important facility is a unique election symbol that official candidates of that party can use. The chapter's 2026–27 reprint describes the recognition criteria and lists six recognised national parties according to the Election Commission notification issued in 2023.

Status in supplied text Minimum threshold stated
State party At least 6% of total votes in a State Assembly election + at least 2 seats.
National party At least 6% of total votes in Lok Sabha elections or Assembly elections in four States + at least 4 Lok Sabha seats.
08 · National Parties

Party Profiles in the 2026–27 Reprint

The supplied chapter gives profiles of the recognised national parties it discusses. The descriptions below retain the chapter's framing and historical snapshot rather than replacing it with current political data.

Aam Aadmi Party (AAP)

Formed in 2012 following the 2011 anti-corruption movement; associated with accountability, clean administration, transparency and good governance. The chapter notes governments in Punjab and Delhi in its described snapshot.

Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP)

Formed in 1984 under Kanshi Ram; seeks to represent bahujan samaj including Dalits, Adivasis, OBCs and religious minorities; main base described as Uttar Pradesh.

Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)

Founded in 1980 by reviving the erstwhile Bharatiya Jana Sangh; the chapter describes cultural nationalism/Hindutva, integral humanism, Antyodaya and its positions on national integration, uniform civil code and religious conversions.

Communist Party of India – Marxist (CPI-M)

Founded in 1964; believes in Marxism-Leninism; supports socialism, secularism and democracy and opposes imperialism and communalism.

Indian National Congress (INC)

Founded in 1885; the chapter describes its historic national role, secular democratic orientation, welfare of weaker sections and minorities, centrist orientation and support for economic reforms with a human face.

National People’s Party (NPP)

Formed in 2013 under P.A. Sangma; the chapter describes it as the first party from North East India to attain national-party status, emphasising diversity, education, employment and empowerment.

09 · State Parties

Regional / State Parties

The chapter says State parties are commonly referred to as regional parties, although they need not be regional in ideology or outlook. Some have national-level organisation but succeed mainly in certain states; others consciously emphasise State identity.

Examples in text

Samajwadi Party, Rashtriya Janata Dal, Biju Janata Dal, Sikkim Democratic Front, Mizo National Front and Telangana Rashtra Samithi.

Growth

The number and strength of State parties expanded over the preceding three decades described in the chapter.

Federalism

National parties often need State parties in alliances; this has contributed to strengthening federalism and democracy.

10 · Four Challenges

Challenges to Political Parties

1. Lack of internal democracy

Power becomes concentrated in one or a few leaders; meetings, membership registers and internal elections may be weak or irregular.

2. Dynastic succession

Top positions may be controlled by members of one family, giving unfair advantage and limiting opportunities for ordinary workers.

3. Money & muscle power

Election-focused parties may favour candidates who can raise money; rich donors can influence decisions, and in some cases parties support candidates with criminal backgrounds.

4. No meaningful choice

Ideological differences among parties can shrink, leaving voters without sufficiently different policy alternatives or leadership choices.

11 · Reforms

How Can Political Parties Be Reformed?

The chapter describes recent measures and additional suggestions, while warning that over-regulation can be counter-productive.

Measure / suggestion Purpose / issue addressed Important qualification in chapter
Anti-defection law Discourage elected representatives from changing parties for office/cash. It reduced defection but also made dissent within parties more difficult.
Candidate affidavits Disclose property and pending criminal cases. Information becomes public, but accuracy is not automatically verified.
Organisational elections + income-tax returns Encourage party organisation and transparency. Sometimes treated as mere formality; impact on internal democracy is uncertain.
Internal affairs law Membership registers, party constitution, independent dispute authority and open elections. Suggested reform, not stated as an already accepted solution.
Women's tickets / quotas Increase women's representation in parties. Suggested minimum of about one-third in the chapter.
State funding Reduce dependence on private election money. Suggested support could be cash or in kind.
CAUTION: The textbook warns that legal solutions can have unintended effects. Political reform also depends on citizens, pressure groups, movements and media putting pressure on parties.
12 · Citizens & Reform

More and Better Politics

Citizens can push parties through petitions, publicity and agitations. Pressure groups, movements and the media can make parties take reform seriously when they fear losing public support. The chapter also argues that those who want better politics should join political parties rather than only criticise them from outside.

State the various functions political parties perform in a democracy.

Answer framework

Contest elections; put forward policies and programmes; participate in law-making; form and run governments; play opposition; shape public opinion; provide people access to government machinery and welfare schemes.

2 · Challenges

What are the various challenges faced by political parties?

Answer

Four major challenges: lack of internal democracy, dynastic succession, growing role of money and muscle power, and lack of meaningful choice for voters.

3 · Reforms

Suggest some reforms to strengthen parties so that they perform their functions well.

Answer framework

Internal-party regulation; membership registers and open internal elections; greater representation of women; state funding; transparency through affidavits; anti-defection measures; citizen and media pressure; greater public participation.

11 · Case Study: Muhammad Yunus

The exercise presents Muhammad Yunus launching Nagarik Shakti (Citizens’ Power) in Bangladesh and asks whether he made the right decision, whether the fears expressed were justified, and how such a new party should be organised.

Answer direction

A strong answer should weigh both sides: entering politics can provide a democratic route to improve governance, but political parties require organisation, accountability, grassroots participation, policy clarity and transparent leadership. The new party should be internally democratic and should offer meaningful policy alternatives.
