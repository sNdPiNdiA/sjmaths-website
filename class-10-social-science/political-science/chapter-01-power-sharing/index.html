01 • Power-sharing

Chapter at a Glance

The chapter uses Belgium and Sri Lanka to explain why democracies need power-sharing and then develops four forms of power-sharing.

Belgium

Recognised regional and cultural diversity and created arrangements to live together.

Sri Lanka

Majoritarian policies increased Tamil alienation and eventually contributed to civil war.

Prudential reason

Sharing reduces conflict and political instability.

Moral reason

Power sharing is the spirit of democracy and respects participation.

Four forms

Organs, levels of government, social groups, and political parties/pressure groups.

Core lesson

Power sharing can strengthen unity rather than weaken a country.

01 · Foundation

What is Power-sharing?

In a democracy, power does not rest with any one organ. The chapter begins with the idea that intelligent sharing of power among the legislature, executive and judiciary is important to the design of democracy.

02 · Belgium

Belgium's Ethnic Composition & the Brussels Problem

Belgium has Dutch-speaking, French-speaking and German-speaking communities. The source states that 59% live in the Flemish region and speak Dutch, 40% in Wallonia speak French and 1% speak German. In Brussels, 80% speak French and 20% are Dutch-speaking.

EXAM TRAP: Do not write that Dutch speakers were a minority in Belgium. They were the national majority but a minority in Brussels.
03 · Sri Lanka

Majoritarianism & Its Consequences

Sri Lanka has a Sinhala-speaking majority and Tamil-speaking communities. After independence in 1948, the government adopted measures that sought to establish Sinhala supremacy.

MEMORY: “L-J-B” → Language policy → Jobs/Education preference → Buddhism protection.
TERM: Majoritarianism means the belief that the majority community should rule as it wishes by disregarding the wishes and needs of the minority.
04 · Belgium's Solution

Accommodation: Sharing to Preserve Unity

BELGIUM = “E-S-B-C”: Equal central ministers → State autonomy → Brussels equal representation → Community government.
05 · Compare

Belgium vs Sri Lanka

Point Belgium Sri Lanka
Approach Accommodation and power sharing Majoritarian measures
Language Multiple language communities accommodated Sinhala made only official language in 1956
Political arrangement Equal representation and decentralisation Policies favoured Sinhala majority
Minority response Compromise supported unity Tamil demands for language, autonomy and equality were denied
Outcome Helped avoid civic strife and possible linguistic division Distrust → conflict → civil war; ended in 2009
ONE-LINE DIFFERENCE: Belgium accommodated diversity; Sri Lanka imposed majority dominance.
06 · Why Desirable?

Prudential + Moral Reasons

MNEMONIC: P = Peace/Practical; M = Moral/Meaning of democracy.
07 · Form 1

Horizontal Distribution of Power

Power is shared among organs at the same level: legislature, executive and judiciary. Each checks the others, producing a balance of power. The source calls this a system of checks and balances.

MEMORY: HORIZONTAL = L-E-J → Legislature, Executive, Judiciary + checks and balances.
08 · Form 2

Vertical / Federal Division of Power

Power is shared among governments at different levels: a general/central government and provincial or regional governments. In India these are Union/Central and State Governments. It can extend downward to municipalities and panchayats.

MNEMONIC: V = Vertical ladder → Union → State → local.
09 · Form 3

Power-sharing Among Social Groups

Power may be shared among religious and linguistic groups. Belgium's community government is an example. Constitutional/legal arrangements can also provide representation for socially weaker sections and women, including reserved constituencies.

10 · Form 4

Power-sharing Among Political Parties, Pressure Groups & Movements

Competition among political parties prevents power from remaining in one hand. Two or more parties may form an alliance; if elected, they form a coalition government. Interest groups can influence decision-making through committees and other participation.

MNEMONIC: “P-P-M-C” → Parties, Pressure groups, Movements, Coalition.
11 · Special Example

Lebanon: Community-based Sharing

The chapter's Khalil story describes an arrangement after civil war in which specified top offices are associated with particular communities: President—Maronite Catholic Christian; Prime Minister—Sunni Muslim; Deputy Prime Minister—Orthodox Christian; Speaker—Shi'a Muslim.

12 · Belgium's Three Governments

Central + State + Community Government

Central Government

Dutch and French-speaking ministers are equal; special laws require majority support from each linguistic group.

State Governments

Many central powers are transferred to state governments of the two regions; they are not subordinate to the Central Government.

Brussels Government

Separate government with equal representation of both communities.

Community Government

Elected by Dutch-, French- or German-speaking people regardless of where they live; handles cultural, educational and language issues.

FOUR BELGIAN BOXES: Central → State → Brussels → Community.

Questions reproduced from the supplied Chapter 1 source, with answer guidance where useful.

1 · Forms of Power-sharing

What are the different forms of power sharing in modern democracies? Give an example of each.

Answer framework

1. Horizontal—organs of government; example: legislature/executive/judiciary and checks and balances. 2. Vertical—different levels; example: Union and State Governments. 3. Social groups—religious/linguistic and weaker sections; example: Belgium's community government. 4. Political parties/pressure groups/movements—example: coalition government.

2 · Prudential & Moral

State one prudential reason and one moral reason for power sharing with an example from the Indian context.

Answer framework

Prudential: reduces conflict and instability. Moral: people have a right to participate and be consulted in democratic governance. The source's Panchayati Raj passage can support the participation/efficiency discussion.

Complete Chapter Revision

This revision section deliberately covers every major concept in the supplied chapter—not just a short summary.

Ethnic

A social division based on shared culture; members may share descent or cultural characteristics without necessarily sharing religion or nationality.

Majoritarianism

Majority rules while disregarding the wishes and needs of the minority.

Belgian model

Equal central ministers, state autonomy, equal Brussels representation, community government.

Sri Lankan path

Sinhala-only language policy, preferential policies, Buddhism protected; Tamil alienation followed.

Civil war

Violent conflict within a country between opposing groups; source says Sri Lankan civil war ended in 2009.

Prudential

Calculated benefit: sharing reduces conflict, violence and instability.

Moral

Democratic value: citizens have a right to participate and be consulted.

Horizontal

Same-level organs: legislature, executive, judiciary; checks and balances.

Vertical

Different government levels: Union/Central, State and potentially local.

Social

Religious, linguistic and other social groups; weaker sections and women can receive representation.

Political

Political parties compete; alliances may form coalition governments; pressure groups and movements influence decisions.

Belgium Community Government

Language-community government handles cultural, educational and language-related matters.
