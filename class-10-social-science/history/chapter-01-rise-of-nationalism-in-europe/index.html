The Rise of Nationalism in Europe: Concepts

1. What is a Nation-State?

Nation-state: a state where most citizens develop a sense of common identity and shared history or descent.

Common Identity

People see themselves as part of one political community.

Shared History

Past struggles, memories and achievements create belonging.

Political State

A defined territory is governed by a central sovereign power.

Four visual anchors for understanding the nation-state.
Exam phrase: A nation-state is built around a shared sense of common identity, not merely around a ruler.

2. The French Revolution and the Idea of the Nation

1789 marked the first clear expression of nationalism in the chapter.

French revolutionary measures created a stronger sense of collective identity among citizens.

New Flag

Tricolour replaced the royal standard.

National Assembly

Estates General was renamed.

Uniform Laws

Centralised administration and laws.

Weights & Measures

A uniform system was adopted.

Common Language

Parisian French was promoted.

National Symbols

Hymns, oaths and martyrs reinforced identity.

3. Napoleon and the Spread of Revolutionary Principles

Napoleon restored monarchy in France, but introduced administrative reforms in territories under French control.

Reforms improved administration and helped spread ideas associated with the French Revolution.
Remember: Administrative reform ≠ political freedom. The source notes that taxation, censorship and forced conscription later produced hostility.

4. Liberal Nationalism

The chapter connects liberalism with freedom, equality before law, representative government and economic freedom.

Political Liberalism

  • End of autocracy
  • Constitution
  • Representative government
  • Freedom of the individual
  • Equality before law

Economic Liberalism

  • Freedom of markets
  • Movement of goods
  • Movement of capital
  • Removal of state restrictions
  • Unified economic territory
Important limitation: Equality before law did not automatically mean universal suffrage. Property-owning men held political rights in revolutionary France; women and non-propertied men were excluded.

5. Zollverein: Economic Nationalism

German-speaking regions had many states, currencies and customs barriers. Commercial classes wanted a unified economic territory.

In 1834, the Zollverein abolished tariff barriers among participating German states and reduced the number of currencies.
Memory chain: economic integration → easier movement of goods/capital → shared interests → stronger nationalist sentiment.

6. Conservatism and the Congress of Vienna, 1815

The Vienna settlement aimed to undo many changes of the Napoleonic period and restore a conservative order.

Hosted by

Duke Metternich of Austria.

Main Powers

Britain, Russia, Prussia and Austria.

Core Aim

Restore monarchies and prevent French expansion.

7. Giuseppe Mazzini and Revolutionary Nationalism

Mazzini opposed monarchy and saw nation-states as necessary units of a free Europe.
One-line recall: Mazzini = secret societies + Young Italy + Young Europe + republican nationalism.

9. Romanticism, Culture and National Feeling

Nationalism was shaped not only by wars and politics. Art, poetry, stories, music and folklore helped create a shared cultural past.

Romanticism

Emphasised emotion, intuition and shared cultural heritage.

Herder

Linked German culture to common people — das volk.

Grimm Brothers

Collected folktales and developed German-language work.

Poland

Language and music kept national feeling alive after partition.

Language

Could become a symbol of resistance to foreign domination.

Popular Culture

Made nationalist ideas accessible to wider audiences.

Culture helped people imagine a shared community even when political unity did not yet exist.

10. Hunger, Hardship and the Revolutions of 1848

Population growthMore job seekers.
Industrial competitionSmall producers suffered.
Food shortagesPrices and poverty increased.
Popular revoltBarricades and political demands.
1848 in France: Louis Philippe fled; a Republic was proclaimed; adult male suffrage was granted; the right to work was guaranteed and national workshops were established.

Frankfurt Parliament: 831 elected representatives assembled on 18 May 1848 in the Church of St Paul and drafted a constitution for a German nation under a monarchy subject to a parliament.

Why did it fail? The Prussian king rejected the offered crown; aristocratic and military opposition grew; the middle-class dominated parliament lost worker and artisan support; troops finally dissolved the assembly.

11. German Unification

German unification was driven by Prussian state power under Otto von Bismarck and completed in 1871.
Prussia Bismarck Army + bureaucracy 3 wars 1871 William I

12. Italian Unification

Mazzini supplied an early republican vision; Cavour used diplomacy; Garibaldi led volunteers; Victor Emmanuel II became king of united Italy.
Figure Role in the chapter Giuseppe Mazzini Revolutionary; Young Italy; republican nationalism. Cavour Chief Minister; diplomacy and alliance with France. Garibaldi Led volunteers and the Expedition of the Thousand. Victor Emmanuel II King proclaimed of united Italy in 1861.

13. The Strange Case of Britain

Britain's nation-state emerged through a long process rather than a single revolution.

The chapter presents Britain as a nation-state forged gradually through English political and cultural dominance.

14. Visualising the Nation: Marianne and Germania

Marianne — France

  • Female allegory of the nation.
  • Popular Christian name.
  • Red cap, tricolour and cockade.
  • Represented the Republic and people's nation.

Germania — Germany

  • Female allegory of the German nation.
  • Oak-leaf crown = heroism.
  • Sword = readiness to fight.
  • Olive branch around sword = willingness to make peace.
Artists personified abstract nations as female figures so that the idea of the nation could be given a concrete visual form.

15. Nationalism and Imperialism

After 1871, nationalist aspirations increasingly became intertwined with imperial competition, especially in the Balkans.
Key transition: early nationalism → liberal-democratic aspirations; later nationalism → narrower, competitive and increasingly imperialist aims.

Chapter Exercise & NCERT-Style Questions

Question 1. Explain what is meant by the 1848 Revolution of the Liberals. What political, social and economic ideas did liberals support?5 marks
View Answer

It was a wave of liberal-nationalist revolutions in Europe. Liberals supported constitutionalism, representative government, freedom of the press, freedom of association, individual liberty, equality before law and economic freedom of markets.

Question 2. Choose three examples to show the contribution of culture to the growth of nationalism in Europe.3 marks
View Answer
  • Folk songs, poetry and dances expressed shared cultural heritage.
  • The Grimm Brothers collected folktales and worked on the German language.
  • Polish language and music kept national feelings alive despite foreign domination.
Question 3. Through a focus on any two countries, explain how nations developed over the nineteenth century.5 marks
View Answer

Germany: Prussia led unification under Bismarck through three wars and proclaimed the German Empire in 1871.

Italy: Mazzini's republican vision, Cavour's diplomacy, Garibaldi's volunteers and Victor Emmanuel II's monarchy contributed to unification; Italy was proclaimed united in 1861 and the process was completed in 1870.

Question 4. How was the history of nationalism in Britain unlike the rest of Europe?3 marks
View Answer

Britain's nation-state developed through a long process of parliamentary and political consolidation rather than a sudden revolution. England gradually extended its influence over Scotland, Wales and Ireland, with English culture becoming dominant.

Question 5. Why did nationalist tensions emerge in the Balkans?3 marks
View Answer
  • Different Slavic nationalities sought independence and political rights.
  • The Ottoman Empire was weakening.
  • Balkan states competed for territory.
  • European powers competed for influence, trade and strategic control.
Question 6. Write a note on Giuseppe Mazzini.3 marks
View Answer

Mazzini was an Italian revolutionary who opposed monarchy and supported democratic republics. He joined the Carbonari and founded Young Italy and Young Europe. He believed Italy should become a unified republic.

Question 7. Write a note on Count Camillo de Cavour.3 marks
View Answer

Cavour was Chief Minister of Sardinia-Piedmont. He was not a revolutionary or democrat. He used diplomacy and an alliance with France to defeat Austrian forces in 1859 and advance Italian unification.

Question 8. Write a note on the Greek War of Independence.3 marks
View Answer

The struggle began in 1821 against Ottoman rule. Greek nationalists received support from Greeks abroad and West Europeans sympathetic to ancient Greek culture. The Treaty of Constantinople in 1832 recognised Greece as an independent nation.

Question 9. Write a note on the Frankfurt Parliament.3 marks
View Answer

On 18 May 1848, 831 elected representatives assembled in St Paul's Church in Frankfurt. They drafted a constitution for a German nation headed by a monarchy subject to a parliament. Friedrich Wilhelm IV rejected the offered crown, and the assembly was eventually dissolved.

Question 10. Explain the role of women in nationalist struggles.3 marks
View Answer

Women actively participated in political associations, newspapers, meetings and demonstrations. Yet they were denied suffrage in the Frankfurt Parliament elections and were admitted only as observers. Activists such as Louise Otto-Peters demanded political freedom and equality.

Question 11. What steps did the French revolutionaries take to create a sense of collective identity?3 marks
View Answer
  • Introduced the ideas of la patrie and le citoyen.
  • Adopted the tricolour flag.
  • Renamed Estates General as National Assembly.
  • Created hymns, oaths and national commemorations.
  • Introduced uniform laws, weights and measures.
  • Promoted a common French language.
Question 12. Who were Marianne and Germania? Why were they important?3 marks
View Answer

They were female allegories representing France and Germany. They gave the abstract idea of a nation a concrete visual form and used symbols to communicate ideas of unity, liberty, heroism and national identity.

Question 13. Briefly trace German unification.5 marks
View Answer
  1. Middle-class German nationalists tried to unite Germany in 1848.
  2. The liberal initiative failed.
  3. Prussia then led the movement.
  4. Bismarck used the Prussian army and bureaucracy.
  5. Three wars with Denmark, Austria and France ended in Prussian victories.
  6. William I was proclaimed German Emperor at Versailles in January 1871.
Question 14. What changes did Napoleon introduce to make administration more efficient?3 marks
View Answer
  • Introduced the Napoleonic Code.
  • Removed privileges based on birth.
  • Established equality before law.
  • Secured property rights.
  • Simplified administrative divisions.
  • Abolished feudalism and serfdom-related dues.
  • Improved transport and communication.

  • Broken chains → being freed.

  • Sword → readiness to fight.
  • Olive branch around sword → willingness to make peace.
  • Black-red-gold tricolour → liberal-nationalist flag of 1848.

5-Mark Challenge

Explain how nationalism changed between the first half and the last quarter of the nineteenth century.
View Answer

In the first half of the nineteenth century, nationalism was closely associated with liberalism, democracy, constitutional government and national self-determination. By the last quarter, nationalist groups became increasingly intolerant and nationalist aspirations were manipulated by European powers for imperialist aims. The Balkans became a major centre of conflict, contributing to the tensions that led to the First World War.
