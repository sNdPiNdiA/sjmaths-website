01 · Meaning of Manufacturing

Raw Material → Valuable Product

Production of goods in large quantities after processing raw materials into more valuable products is called manufacturing. Examples in the chapter include paper from wood, sugar from sugarcane, iron and steel from iron ore, and aluminium from bauxite.

02 · Importance

Why Manufacturing Is the Backbone of Development

CORE IDEA: Agriculture and industry are not exclusive; they move hand in hand. Agro-industries supply inputs to farmers and depend on agriculture for raw materials.

05 · Cotton Textile Industry

From Cotton to Value-Added Textile Products

The textile industry contributes significantly to industrial production, employment generation and foreign exchange earnings. The chapter describes it as self-reliant and complete in its value chain from raw material to highest value-added products.

LOCALISATION FACTORS: raw cotton, market, transport/port facilities, labour and moist climate contributed to the early concentration in Maharashtra and Gujarat.
IMPORTANT: Spinning continues to be centralised in Maharashtra, Gujarat and Tamil Nadu; weaving is highly decentralised and occurs in handloom, powerloom and mills.
KHADI: Handspun khadi provides large-scale employment to weavers in their homes as a cottage industry.

07 · Sugar Industry

Bulky Raw Material + Seasonal Nature

India stands second as a world producer of sugar and first in gur and khandsari according to the supplied text. Sugarcane is bulky and its sucrose content reduces during haulage, influencing mill location.

Main states named

Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Punjab, Haryana and Madhya Pradesh.

60% mills

The source states that 60% of mills are in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar.

Shift tendency

Recent tendency toward southern and western states, especially Maharashtra, due to higher sucrose, cooler climate and successful cooperatives.

WHY COOPERATIVE? The industry is seasonal, so the source says it is ideally suited to the cooperative sector.
08 · Iron & Steel

The Basic / Key Industry

Iron and steel is called a basic industry because heavy, medium and light industries depend on it for machinery. Steel is used in engineering goods, construction, defence, medical, telephonic, scientific equipment and consumer goods.

LOCATION: Chhotanagpur Plateau has the maximum concentration of iron and steel industries, with advantages including low-cost/proximate high-grade raw materials, cheap labour and growth potential in the home market.
09 · Aluminium Smelting

Light, Corrosion Resistant & Conductive

PLANTS NAMED: Odisha, West Bengal, Kerala, Uttar Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu.
10 · Chemical Industry

Inorganic + Organic Chemicals

Inorganic

Sulphuric acid, nitric acid, alkalies, soda ash and caustic soda. Sulphuric acid is used for fertilisers, synthetic fibres, plastics, adhesives, paints and dyes; soda ash for glass, soaps, detergents and paper.

Organic

Includes petrochemicals used to manufacture synthetic fibres, synthetic rubber, plastics, dyestuffs, drugs and pharmaceuticals.

Location logic

Organic chemical plants are located near oil refineries or petrochemical plants.

SELF-CONSUMER: The source notes that the chemical industry is its own largest consumer because basic chemicals are processed further into other chemicals.
11 · Fertilizer Industry

N + P + K

MAJOR PRODUCERS: Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab and Kerala contribute towards half of fertiliser production according to the supplied text.
12 · Cement Industry

Heavy Raw Materials → Construction

Cement is essential for houses, factories, bridges, roads, airports, dams and commercial establishments.

13 · Automobile Industry

Transport Manufacturing Network

The automobile industry produces trucks, buses, cars, motorcycles, scooters, three-wheelers and multi-utility vehicles. The source says liberalisation and contemporary models stimulated demand and supported growth.

Manufacturing

Large-quantity production after processing raw materials into more valuable products.

Importance

Agricultural modernisation, employment, poverty reduction, regional development, exports and foreign exchange.

Classification

Raw material; role; capital; ownership; bulk/weight.

Cotton

Early concentration Maharashtra/Gujarat; spinning centralised in Maharashtra/Gujarat/Tamil Nadu; weaving decentralised.

Jute

West Bengal/Hugli belt; Rishra first jute mill in 1855.

Sugar

Bulky cane, sucrose loss during haulage, seasonal, cooperative suitability; 60% mills in UP/Bihar per source.

Steel

Basic industry; iron ore:coking coal:limestone ≈ 4:2:1; Chhotanagpur maximum concentration.

Aluminium

Light, corrosion resistant, conductive, malleable; electricity + raw material are key location factors.

Chemicals

Inorganic + organic; petrochemical plants near refineries/petrochemical centres.

Fertiliser

Nitrogenous, phosphatic, DAP and complex; potash entirely imported per source.

Cement

Limestone, silica, gypsum, coal, electric power and rail transport.

Automobile

Multiple centres around Delhi/Gurugram, Mumbai/Pune, Chennai, Kolkata, Lucknow, Indore, Hyderabad, Jamshedpur and Bengaluru.

IT & Electronics

Bengaluru electronic capital; major concentration also in Noida, Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad and Pune.

Pollution

Air, water, land, noise; thermal pollution and hazardous industrial wastes are discussed.

Water Control

Reuse/recycle, rainwater harvesting, hot-water/effluent treatment; primary, secondary and tertiary treatment.

Air Control

Electrostatic precipitators, fabric filters, scrubbers, inertial separators; oil/gas instead of coal.

Noise Control

Silencers, machinery redesign, noise-absorbing materials and personal ear protection.

Sustainable Development

Efficient equipment, waste reduction, ash utilisation, green belts, pollution control and ecological monitoring.
