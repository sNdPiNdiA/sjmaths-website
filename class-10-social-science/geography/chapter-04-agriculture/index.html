01 · Agriculture in India

Why Agriculture Matters

India is described as an agriculturally important country. Agriculture is a primary activity that produces much of the food consumed and provides raw material for industries. Tea, coffee and spices are also exported.

THINK “FOOD + FACTORY + FOREIGN MARKET”: food for people, raw material for industries, and export products.
02 · Types of Farming

Primitive Subsistence Farming

  • Primitive tools: hoe, dao and digging sticks.
  • Family/community labour.
  • Depends on monsoon and natural fertility.
  • When fertility declines, farmers shift to a fresh patch.
  • Land productivity is low because modern inputs such as fertilisers are not used.
MNEMONIC: “P-S-M-L” — Primitive tools • Small patch • Monsoon • Low productivity.
INDIAN NAMES: Jhumming (North-East), Pamlou (Manipur), Dipa (Bastar), Bewar/Dahiya (Madhya Pradesh), Podu/Penda (Andhra Pradesh), Kumari (Western Ghats), Kuruwa (Jharkhand), etc.
03 · Intensive Subsistence

Maximum Output from Limited Land

Practised in areas of high population pressure on land. It is labour-intensive and uses high doses of biochemical inputs and irrigation for higher production.

WHY THE PRESSURE? The chapter links successive inheritance to division of land holdings, making holdings uneconomical while farmers continue seeking maximum output from limited land because alternative livelihoods are limited.
04 · Commercial Farming

Market-Oriented Production

REGIONAL EXAMPLE: Rice is commercial in Haryana and Punjab but subsistence in Odisha, showing that commercialisation varies by region.
05 · Cropping Pattern

Rabi • Kharif • Zaid

FAST MEMORY: RABI = winter sowing; KHARIF = monsoon; ZAID = summer gap.
PADDY DETAIL: In Assam, West Bengal and Odisha, three rice crops are grown in a year: Aus, Aman and Boro.
06 · Rice

Staple Kharif Crop

Feature Chapter detail
Season Kharif
Temperature Above 25°C
Rainfall Above 100 cm annually
Other requirement High humidity
Low-rainfall areas Grows with irrigation
Major areas North & north-east plains, coastal and deltaic regions; also Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh and parts of Rajasthan through irrigation.
EXAM LINK: High temperature + high humidity + high rainfall → rice. Irrigation explains rice in lower-rainfall areas.
07 · Wheat

Major Rabi Cereal

Wheat requires a cool growing season, bright sunshine during ripening and 50–75 cm annual rainfall evenly distributed over the growing season.

TWO IMPORTANT WHEAT ZONES: Ganga-Satluj plains in the north-west and black-soil region of the Deccan.
MAJOR STATES: Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar and Rajasthan.
08 · Millets & Maize

Jowar • Bajra • Ragi • Maize

Jowar

Rain-fed; moist areas; hardly needs irrigation. Major states: Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh.

Bajra

Grows well on sandy and shallow black soils. Major states: Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Haryana.

Ragi

Dry-region crop; grows on red, black, sandy, loamy and shallow black soils. Karnataka is a major producer.

Maize

Kharif; 21–27°C; old alluvial soil; used as food and fodder. Major states include Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh and Bihar.

NUTRITION: The chapter highlights millets as coarse grains with high nutritional value; ragi is rich in iron, calcium, micronutrients and roughage.
09 · Pulses

Protein + Soil Fertility

India is described as the largest producer and consumer of pulses. Major pulses include tur (arhar), urad, moong, masur, peas and gram.

  • Pulses need less moisture and can survive dry conditions.
  • They are mostly grown in rotation with other crops.
  • All except arhar help restore soil fertility by fixing nitrogen from the air, according to the supplied chapter.
  • Major producing states: Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh and Karnataka.
EXAM TRICK: Pulses = protein + low moisture requirement + crop rotation + nitrogen fixation (except arhar as stated in the chapter).
10 · Sugarcane

Food-Industry Link

Sugarcane is tropical as well as subtropical. It grows well in hot and humid climate, at 21–27°C, with annual rainfall between 75 and 100 cm; irrigation is needed in low-rainfall regions.

USES: Main source of sugar, gur (jaggary), khandsari and molasses.
MAJOR STATES: Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, Punjab and Haryana.
11 · Oilseeds

Groundnut • Mustard • Soyabean and More

Main oilseeds include groundnut, mustard, coconut, sesamum, soyabean, castor seed, cotton seed, linseed and sunflower. Most are edible and used as cooking mediums; some are raw materials for soap, cosmetics and ointments.

Crop Season / fact in chapter
Groundnut Kharif; about half of major oilseeds; Gujarat largest producer in 2019–20, followed by Rajasthan and Tamil Nadu.
Linseed & Mustard Rabi crops.
Sesamum Kharif in north India; rabi in south India.
Castor seed Grown as both rabi and kharif.
12 · Tea

Plantation + Labour-Intensive Beverage Crop

Tea is a plantation crop. It grows well in tropical and sub-tropical climates with deep, fertile, well-drained soil rich in humus and organic matter. Bushes need warm, moist, frost-free climate and frequent showers evenly distributed through the year.

Labour

Tea is labour-intensive and requires abundant, cheap and skilled labour.

Processing

Tea is processed within the tea garden to restore freshness.

Major regions

Assam; Darjeeling & Jalpaiguri in West Bengal; Tamil Nadu; Kerala, plus other producing states.

2020: The chapter states India was the second-largest tea producer after China.
13 · Coffee

Arabica & Southern Hills

Indian coffee is noted in the chapter for good quality. Arabica, initially brought from Yemen, is produced in India. Cultivation was introduced on Baba Budan Hills and is concentrated in Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu.

Fruits & Vegetables

15 · Non-Food Crops

Rubber • Cotton • Jute

Rubber

Equatorial crop; under special conditions tropical/subtropical. Needs moist humid climate, rainfall above 200 cm and temperature above 25°C. Mainly Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andaman & Nicobar and Garo Hills of Meghalaya.

Cotton

Kharif; grows in drier parts of black cotton soil of Deccan Plateau; high temperature, light rainfall/irrigation, 210 frost-free days and bright sunshine; 6–8 months to mature.

Jute

“Golden fibre”; grows on well-drained fertile soils in flood plains renewed annually. Major states: West Bengal, Bihar, Assam, Odisha, Meghalaya. Used for bags, mats, ropes, yarn, carpets and artefacts.

FIBRE CROPS: Cotton, jute, hemp and natural silk. Silk fibre is obtained from silkworm cocoons; rearing silkworms is sericulture.
16 · Technological & Institutional Reforms

How Government Policies Address Agriculture

  • Collectivisation, consolidation of holdings, cooperation and abolition of zamindari were given priority after Independence.
  • Land reform was the main focus of the First Five Year Plan.
  • Green Revolution and White Revolution (Operation Flood) were strategies introduced in the 1960s–70s.
  • A comprehensive land development programme was initiated in the 1980s–90s.
  • Crop insurance against drought, flood, cyclone, fire and disease.
  • Grameen banks, cooperative societies and banks for lower-interest loans.
  • Kisan Credit Card (KCC) and Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS).
  • Weather bulletins and agricultural programmes on radio and television.
  • Minimum support price, remunerative and procurement prices for important crops.
REFORM MEMORY: “LAND → TECH → DEVELOPMENT → CREDIT → PRICE.”
17 · Bhoodan–Gramdan

Vinoba Bhave & the Blood-less Revolution

At Pochampally in Telangana, when landless villagers requested land, Shri Ram Chandra Reddy offered 80 acres to be distributed among 80 landless villagers. This act was known as Bhoodan. Later, some owners offered villages for distribution among landless people, known as Gramdan.

KEY IDEA: The Bhoodan–Gramdan movement initiated by Vinoba Bhave is also called the Blood-less Revolution in the chapter.

Monsoon onset; Sep–Oct harvest; rice, maize, jowar, bajra, tur, cotton, jute, groundnut, soyabean.

Zaid

Short summer season between rabi and kharif; watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, vegetables, fodder.
