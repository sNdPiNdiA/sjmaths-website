01 · Biodiversity

Flora, Fauna & Biodiversity

Biodiversity / Biological Diversity means the immense variety of living organisms, diverse in form and function, but closely integrated through multiple networks of interdependence.

Flora = plant life. Fauna = animal life.

TRICK: “FLORA = FLOWERS/PLANTS; FAUNA = FAUNAL/ANIMALS.”
WHY IMPORTANT? Think A-W-S-F: Air quality • Water • Soil • Food. Forests are primary producers supporting other living beings.
02 · Conservation

Why Conserve Forests & Wildlife?

  • Preserves ecological diversity and life-support systems.
  • Protects water, air and soil systems.
  • Preserves genetic diversity of plants and animals.
  • Supports traditional crop varieties and fisheries.
MNEMONIC: “E-W-A-S-G-A-F” — Ecology • Water • Air • Soil • Genetic diversity • Agriculture • Fisheries.
EXAM LINE: Conservation is not only about saving individual animals; it protects ecological systems and genetic diversity.
03 · Legal Protection

Indian Wildlife Protection Programme

  • Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act: 1972.
  • Protection included banning hunting, legal protection of habitats and restricting wildlife trade.
  • National parks and wildlife sanctuaries were established.
  • Species projects included tiger, one-horned rhinoceros, hangul, crocodiles, Asiatic lion and others.
  • Later protection expanded beyond a few animals towards biodiversity.
DATE TRICK: 72 = Wildlife law; 73 = Tiger. Think “LAW first, TIGER next”.
04 · Project Tiger

Project Tiger — Why Was It Needed?

MNEMONIC: “P-H-P-H” — Poaching • Habitat shrinkage • Prey depletion • Human population growth.
HIGH-YIELD FACT: The chapter states that tiger numbers had fallen to 1,827 in 1973 from an estimated 55,000 at the turn of the century.

Examples of tiger reserves named in the chapter: Corbett, Sunderbans, Bandhavgarh, Sariska, Manas and Periyar.

CONCEPT TIP: Project Tiger is not simply “save the tiger”. The chapter explicitly links conservation with preserving biotypes of sizeable magnitude.
05 · Forest Categories

Reserved, Protected & Unclassed Forests

MEMORY ORDER: R-P-U = Reserved → Protected → Unclassed.
Category Key feature Chapter clue
Reserved More than half Most valuable for conservation
Protected Almost one-third Protected from further depletion
Unclassed Other forests + wastelands Government/private/community
STATE FACT: Madhya Pradesh has the largest area under permanent forests, constituting 75% of its total forest area.
06 · Community Conservation

Communities as Conservation Partners

  • Villagers in Sariska fought against mining using the Wildlife Protection Act.
  • Five Alwar villages declared 1,200 hectares as Bhairodev Dakav ‘Sonchuri’ and banned hunting.
  • Chipko resisted deforestation and demonstrated community afforestation with indigenous species.
  • Beej Bachao Andolan and Navdanya showed diversified production without synthetic chemicals can be viable.
MNEMONIC: “S-C-B-N” — Sariska • Chipko • Beej Bachao • Navdanya.
EXAM TIP: For a 120-word answer, use the structure: community action → example → conservation result → livelihood link.
07 · Traditional Conservation

Sacred Groves

The chapter describes sacred groves as virgin forests preserved in pristine form because nature is revered and interference is banned.

EXAMPLES: Mundas and Santhals worship mahua and kadamba; some communities in Odisha and Bihar worship tamarind and mango during weddings; peepal and banyan are widely considered sacred.
MEMORY: “BELIEF → BAN → FOREST → SPECIES.” A cultural belief creates a practical conservation rule.
08 · Joint Forest Management

JFM — Joint Forest Management

  • Formal existence since 1988, when Odisha passed the first resolution.
  • Local village institutions undertake protection activities.
  • Focus: mostly degraded forest land managed by the Forest Department.
  • Communities receive intermediary benefits such as non-timber forest produce and a share in timber harvested through successful protection.
JFM TRICK: J = Joint → Government + local community. M = Management → protection + restoration.
09 · Core Principle

People-Centric Conservation

3-PILLAR MEMORY: PEOPLE + PLANET + PROSPERITY → People-centric + environment-friendly + economically rewarding.
