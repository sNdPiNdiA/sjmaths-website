What this chapter is teaching

Surface area measures the exposed covering of a solid. Volume measures the space inside it. The questions use formulas, unit conversion, similarity of solids, nets, slicing, filling, melting and estimation.

MeasureIdentify length, breadth, height, radius, diameter and slant height.
ChooseDecide whether the question needs curved, total or lateral surface area.
CalculateUse the correct volume formula and keep all units consistent.
ModelTranslate packing, melting, painting, filling and approximation into geometry.
Study habit: Write the known quantities first, draw or imagine the solid, select the formula, substitute with units, and check whether the answer is reasonable.
Coverage: The notes below provide the formulas and reasoning used in Exercise Sets 14.1–14.4 and the visible end-of-chapter questions supplied for this page.

1Units, dimensions and π

  • Length is measured in units such as cm or m. Area is measured in square units such as cm² or m². Volume is measured in cubic units such as cm³ or m³.
  • Before calculating, convert all lengths to the same unit. For example, 2 m = 200 cm.
  • Use π ≈ 22/7 when the numbers suit it, or π ≈ 3.14 when the question asks for an approximation.
  • Diameter d = 2r and radius r = d/2.
  • For a right circular cone, slant height satisfies l² = h² + r².
1 m = 100 cm   |   1 m² = 10,000 cm²   |   1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³

2Cuboid and cube

  • Volume of a cuboid = lwh.
  • Total surface area of a cuboid = 2(lw + wh + hl).
  • Volume of a cube of side a = a³.
  • Total surface area of a cube = 6a².
  • If a cube is cut into smaller cubes, volume is conserved, but the total surface area usually increases because more faces become exposed.
Cuboid: V = lwh, TSA = 2(lw + wh + hl)   |   Cube: V = a³, TSA = 6a²
Practice14.1End Q1–Q6, Q13–Q15

3Cylinder

  • Curved surface area (CSA) of a cylinder = circumference of base × height = 2πrh.
  • Total surface area (TSA) includes two circular ends: 2πrh + 2πr² = 2πr(h + r).
  • Volume = area of base × height = πr²h.
  • For two cylinders, compare factors rather than calculating decimals: CSA is proportional to rh and volume is proportional to r²h.
  • If the radius changes by a factor k and height stays fixed, volume changes by k².
Cylinder: CSA = 2πrh, TSA = 2πr(h + r), V = πr²h
Practice14.2End Q6, Q15, Q16

4Cone

  • Slant height l is the length along the curved side, not the vertical height.
  • l² = h² + r², so use Pythagoras when two of h, r and l are known.
  • Curved surface area = πrl.
  • Total surface area = πrl + πr² = πr(l + r).
  • Volume = ⅓πr²h. The factor ⅓ is essential.
  • The curved surface of a cone is a sector of a circle of radius l. This explains why CSA is πrl.
Cone: l² = h² + r², CSA = πrl, TSA = πr(l + r), V = ⅓πr²h
Practice14.3End Q7, Q17

5Sphere and hemisphere

  • Surface area of a sphere = 4πr².
  • Volume of a sphere = ⁴⁄₃πr³.
  • Curved surface area of a hemisphere = 2πr².
  • Total surface area of a hemisphere includes its circular base: 2πr² + πr² = 3πr².
  • Volume of a hemisphere = ⅔πr³.
  • Surface area varies as r², while volume varies as r³. Therefore a 10% increase in radius gives a 21% increase in surface area and a 33.1% increase in volume.
Sphere: SA = 4πr², V = ⁴⁄₃πr³   |   Hemisphere: TSA = 3πr², V = ⅔πr³
Practice14.4End Q8–Q12, Q20

6Composite solids, melting and packing

  • When an object is melted and recast without loss, its volume remains the same.
  • For packed objects, volume of empty space or liquid = volume of container − volume occupied by objects.
  • For a rectangular box, V = lwh. For n identical spheres, occupied volume = n × ⁴⁄₃πr³.
  • For estimation questions, state the assumption, calculate an approximate value, and explain that the result depends on the chosen measurements.
  • Check feasibility: the occupied volume cannot be greater than the container volume. If it is, the printed data or the transcription must be checked.
Conservation rule: volume before melting = volume after recasting

Exercise Set 14.1 — Solutions

Cuboids, cubes, packing, painted cubes and integer dimensions.

1The volume of a cube is 64 cm³. What is its total surface area?
Answer / method
Step 1: For a cube, a³ = 64, so a = 4 cm. Step 2: Total surface area = 6a² = 6 × 4² = 96 cm². Answer: 96 cm².
2How many small cubes with side 20 cm can be packed tight in a cubical box with side 2 m?
Answer / method
Step 1: Convert the box side: 2 m = 200 cm. Step 2: Number of small cubes along one edge = 200 ÷ 20 = 10. Step 3: Total cubes = 10 × 10 × 10 = 1000. Answer: 1000 small cubes.
3The dimensions of a godown are 40 m × 25 m × 10 m. If it is filled with cuboidal boxes, each of dimensions 2 m × 1.25 m × 1 m, find the number of boxes.
Answer / method
Step 1: Along the three directions the numbers of boxes are 40 ÷ 2 = 20, 25 ÷ 1.25 = 20 and 10 ÷ 1 = 10. Step 2: Total boxes = 20 × 20 × 10 = 4000. Answer: 4000 boxes.
4Two cubes each of volume 125 cm³ are joined end to end. Find the surface area of the resulting cuboid.
Answer / method
Step 1: Each cube has volume 125 cm³, so its side is 5 cm. Step 2: Joining two cubes end to end gives a cuboid 10 cm × 5 cm × 5 cm. Step 3: Surface area = 2(10×5 + 5×5 + 5×10) = 2(50 + 25 + 50) = 250 cm². Answer: 250 cm².
5A cube of side 4 cm is cut into cubes of side 1 cm. What is the surface area of the original cube and all the smaller cubes? What changes although the volume is unchanged?
Answer / method
Step 1: Original cube surface area = 6 × 4² = 96 cm². Step 2: Number of 1 cm cubes = 4³ = 64. Step 3: Surface area of all small cubes = 64 × 6 × 1² = 384 cm². Step 4: Volume remains 4³ = 64 cm³, but surface area increases because more faces are exposed. Answer: Original 96 cm²; all small cubes 384 cm².
6The surface areas of the three faces of a cuboid that meet at one vertex are 6 cm², 15 cm² and 10 cm² respectively. What is the volume of the cuboid?
Answer / method
Step 1: Let the edges be l, w and h. The three face areas are lw = 6, wh = 15 and hl = 10. Step 2: Multiply: (lw)(wh)(hl) = l²w²h² = (lwh)². Step 3: Therefore V² = 6 × 15 × 10 = 900, so V = 30 cm³. Answer: 30 cm³.
7A cube of side 5 cm is painted on all its faces and then sliced into 1 cm cubes. How many have (i) exactly three faces painted, (ii) exactly two, (iii) exactly one, and (iv) no face painted?
Answer / method
Step 1: There are 5 unit cubes along each edge. Step 2: Exactly 3 painted faces: the 8 corner cubes. Step 3: Exactly 2 painted faces: 12 edges × (5 − 2) = 36. Step 4: Exactly 1 painted face: 6 faces × (5 − 2)² = 54. Step 5: No painted face: (5 − 2)³ = 27. Answer: 8, 36, 54 and 27 respectively.
8Find a cuboid with integer edge lengths in centimetres and total surface area exactly 100 cm². (i) Is there more than one? (ii) Can you find them all? (iii) Show that you have found them all.
Answer / method
Step 1: Let l ≤ w ≤ h be positive integers. TSA = 100 gives lw + wh + hl = 50. Step 2: Since 3l² ≤ 50, l can only be 1, 2, 3 or 4. Step 3: For l = 1, (w + 1)(h + 1) = 51, giving (w,h) = (2,16). Step 4: For l = 2, (w + 2)(h + 2) = 54, giving (w,h) = (4,7). Step 5: l = 3 gives a factorisation of 59, and l = 4 gives no pair with w ≥ l. Answer: The two cuboids are 1×2×16 cm and 2×4×7 cm, apart from rearranging dimensions.

Exercise Set 14.2 — Solutions

Ratios involving cylinders, scaling, and conservation of volume.

1Two cylinders A and B are given. The radius of A is twice the radius of B, and the height of B is half the height of A. Find the ratio of the curved surface area of A to B and the ratio of the volume of A to B.
Answer / method
Step 1: Let rB = r and hB = h. Then rA = 2r and hA = 2h. Step 2: CSA ratio = (2πrA hA):(2πrB hB) = (2r×2h):(r×h) = 4:1. Step 3: Volume ratio = (πrA²hA):(πrB²hB) = (4r²×2h):(r²h) = 8:1. Answer: CSA ratio 4:1; volume ratio 8:1.
2The radii of two cylinders are in the ratio 2:3 and their heights are in the ratio 3:2. Find (a) the ratio of their volumes and (b) the ratio of their curved surface areas.
Answer / method
Step 1: Volume is proportional to r²h. Step 2: Volume ratio = (2²×3):(3²×2) = 12:18 = 2:3. Step 3: Curved surface area is proportional to rh. Step 4: CSA ratio = (2×3):(3×2) = 1:1. Answer: Volumes 2:3; curved surface areas 1:1.
3The edge of a cube measures r cm. The largest possible right circular cylinder is cut out of the cube. What is the volume of the cylinder?
Answer / method
Step 1: The largest cylinder has height r and base diameter r, so its radius is r/2. Step 2: V = π(r/2)²×r = πr³/4. Answer: πr³/4 cm³.
4The radius of the base of a cylinder is increased by 10%. At the same time, the height is decreased by x%. Given that the volume remains unchanged, find x.
Answer / method
Step 1: Original volume = πr²h. Step 2: New radius = 1.1r and new height = (1 − x/100)h. Step 3: Equal volumes give 1.21(1 − x/100) = 1. Step 4: 1 − x/100 = 100/121, so x/100 = 21/121. Answer: x = 2100/121% ≈ 17.36%.
5A solid metallic cube of side 12 cm is melted and recast into solid cylindrical rods, each of radius 2 cm and height 12 cm. Find the volume of the cube, the volume of one rod and the number of complete rods that can be formed.
Answer / method
Step 1: Volume of cube = 12³ = 1728 cm³. Step 2: Volume of one rod = π×2²×12 = 48π cm³ ≈ 150.72 cm³ using π = 3.14. Step 3: Number of complete rods = 1728 ÷ 48π ≈ 11.45. Answer: 1728 cm³; 48π cm³ per rod; 11 complete rods can be made, with material left over.

Exercise Set 14.3 — Solutions

Cones, slant height, curved surface area, total surface area and volume.

1Find the total surface area of a cone if its slant height is 21 m and the diameter of its base is 24 m.
Answer / method
Step 1: Diameter 24 m gives r = 12 m and l = 21 m. Step 2: TSA = πr(l + r) = π×12×(21 + 12) = 396π m². Step 3: Using π = 22/7, TSA = 1244.57 m² approximately. Answer: 396π m² ≈ 1244.57 m².
2Find the curved surface area of a right circular cone whose slant height is 21 cm and base radius is 7 cm.
Answer / method
Step 1: CSA of a cone = πrl. Step 2: CSA = π×7×21 = 147π cm². Step 3: Using π = 22/7, CSA = 462 cm². Answer: 462 cm².
3The height of a cone is 16 cm and its base radius is 12 cm. Find the curved surface area and total surface area of the cone.
Answer / method
Step 1: Slant height l = √(h² + r²) = √(16² + 12²) = √400 = 20 cm. Step 2: CSA = πrl = π×12×20 = 240π cm². Step 3: TSA = πr(l + r) = π×12×(20 + 12) = 384π cm². Answer: CSA = 240π cm²; TSA = 384π cm².
4A cone has height 15 cm and volume 1570 cm³. Find the radius of its base.
Answer / method
Step 1: V = ⅓πr²h = ⅓×3.14×r²×15 = 15.7r². Step 2: 1570 = 15.7r², so r² = 100. Step 3: r = 10 cm. Answer: 10 cm.
5The curved surface area of a cone is 308 cm² and its slant height is 14 cm. Find (i) the radius of its base and (ii) its total surface area.
Answer / method
Step 1: CSA = πrl gives 308 = π×r×14. Step 2: Using π = 22/7, 308 = 44r, so r = 7 cm. Step 3: TSA = πr(l + r) = π×7×(14 + 7) = 147π = 462 cm². Answer: Radius 7 cm; TSA 462 cm².
6A joker’s cap is in the form of a right circular cone of base radius 7 cm and height 24 cm. Find the area of the sheet required to make 10 such caps.
Answer / method
Step 1: Slant height l = √(24² + 7²) = √625 = 25 cm. Step 2: Area of one cap = πrl = π×7×25 = 175π = 550 cm² using π = 22/7. Step 3: For 10 caps, area = 10×550 = 5500 cm². Answer: 5500 cm² of sheet.
7What length of tarpaulin 3 m wide is required to make a conical tent of height 8 m and base radius 6 m? Assume that an extra length of material 20 cm is required for stitching margins and wastage.
Answer / method
Step 1: Slant height l = √(8² + 6²) = 10 m. Step 2: Curved area of tent = πrl = π×6×10 = 60π m². Step 3: With tarpaulin width 3 m, length needed = 60π ÷ 3 = 20π m. Step 4: Add 20 cm = 0.2 m. Total ≈ 20π + 0.2 = 63.06 m. Answer: Approximately 63.1 m.
8A right triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm is rotated through 360° about the side of length 8 cm. Find the volume and curved surface area of the solid formed.
Answer / method
Step 1: Rotation about the 8 cm side gives a cone with h = 8 cm, r = 6 cm and l = 10 cm. Step 2: V = ⅓πr²h = ⅓π×36×8 = 96π cm³. Step 3: CSA = πrl = π×6×10 = 60π cm². Answer: Volume 96π cm³; curved surface area 60π cm².
9A cup has the shape of a right circular cone. It is filled with water to half the depth of the cone. What fraction of the volume of the cup is occupied by water?
Answer / method
Step 1: At half the depth, the radius of a similar smaller cone is half the full radius. Step 2: Volumes of similar solids are in the cube of the linear ratio. Step 3: Water volume : cup volume = (1/2)³ = 1/8. Answer: 1/8 of the cup.

Exercise Set 14.4 — Solutions

Spheres, hemispheres, scaling and approximate volume.

1A ball bearing has radius 0.7 cm. Find its surface area.
Answer / method
Step 1: Surface area of a sphere = 4πr². Step 2: SA = 4×22/7×(0.7)² = 4×22/7×0.49 = 6.16 cm². Answer: 6.16 cm².
*2Two solid spheres made of the same metal have weights 5920 g and 740 g. Determine the radius of the larger sphere if the radius of the smaller one is 5 cm.
Answer / method
Step 1: For equal material, weight ratio equals volume ratio. Step 2: Volume ratio = 5920:740 = 8:1. Step 3: Radius ratio is the cube root: R:r = 2:1. Step 4: The smaller radius is 5 cm, so the larger radius is 10 cm. Answer: 10 cm.
3The diameter of the Moon is approximately one-fourth the diameter of the Earth. Given that the Moon and Earth are approximately spherical, find the ratio of their surface areas.
Answer / method
Step 1: Surface area of a sphere is proportional to the square of its radius. Step 2: Diameter ratio Moon:Earth = 1:4, so radius ratio = 1:4. Step 3: Surface area ratio = 1²:4² = 1:16. Answer: Moon:Earth = 1:16.
4A right triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm is rotated through 360° about the side of length 8 cm. Find the volume and curved surface area of the solid formed.
Answer / method
Step 1: Rotation about the 8 cm side gives h = 8 cm, r = 6 cm and l = 10 cm. Step 2: V = ⅓π×6²×8 = 96π cm³. Step 3: CSA = π×6×10 = 60π cm². Answer: Volume 96π cm³; curved surface area 60π cm².
5Metal spheres, each of radius 2 cm, are packed into a rectangular box of internal dimensions 16 cm × 8 cm × 8 cm. When 36 spheres are packed, the box is filled with preservative liquid. Find the volume of the liquid, rounding to the nearest integer.
The photographed values give 36 spheres of radius 2 cm, whose volume is greater than the stated box volume. The answer therefore exposes an inconsistency in the supplied data.
Answer / method
Step 1: Box volume = 16×8×8 = 1024 cm³. Step 2: Volume of 36 spheres of radius 2 cm = 36×(4/3)π×2³ = 384π cm³ ≈ 1206.4 cm³. Step 3: Liquid volume would be 1024 − 1206.4 = −182.4 cm³, which is impossible. Answer: The supplied figures are inconsistent: 36 spheres of radius 2 cm cannot fit in the stated box. Recheck the printed number or radius before calculating a positive liquid volume.
6The radius of a sphere is increased by 10%. Show that its volume increases by approximately 33.1%.
Answer / method
Step 1: New radius = 1.1r. Step 2: New volume = ⁴⁄₃π(1.1r)³ = 1.331×⁴⁄₃πr³. Step 3: Increase = 1.331 − 1 = 0.331 = 33.1%. Answer: Volume increases by 33.1%.

End-of-Chapter Exercises — Solutions

Estimation, modelling, ratios, composite solids and change in volume.

1Estimate how many scoops of ice cream can be obtained from a cuboidal container of dimensions 10 cm × 15 cm × 20 cm.
Answer / method
Step 1: Make an estimate for one scoop, for example a spherical scoop of radius 2.5 cm. Step 2: Container volume = 10×15×20 = 3000 cm³. Step 3: One scoop volume ≈ ⁴⁄₃π(2.5)³ ≈ 65.45 cm³. Step 4: Estimated number ≈ 3000 ÷ 65.45 ≈ 46. Answer: About 46 scoops under this stated assumption; a different scoop size changes the estimate.
2A cube of integer side length a is made of unit cubes. Write an expression for the number of unit cubes needed to make a cube of side length a + 1.
Answer / method
Step 1: A cube of side a contains a×a×a unit cubes. Step 2: A cube of side a + 1 contains (a + 1)×(a + 1)×(a + 1) unit cubes. Answer: (a + 1)³ unit cubes.
3(i) Could a person drink enough water in a lifetime to fill an entire classroom? (ii) Estimate the number of bricks used to build the walls of your classroom.
Answer / method
Step 1: Estimate classroom volume and average water consumed by one person in a lifetime. Step 2: For example, a room 8×6×3 m has volume 144 m³ = 144,000 litres. Step 3: If one person drinks about 2 litres per day for 70 years, water ≈ 2×365×70 = 51,100 litres. Step 4: Therefore one person cannot fill this example room; the conclusion depends on the chosen dimensions and lifetime estimate. Step 5: For bricks, estimate wall volume and divide by one brick volume, allowing for mortar. Answer: It is an estimation problem; state assumptions and compare volumes.
4You are given two options: (A) one chocolate cube of side 50 mm, or (B) one hundred chocolate cubes of side 10 mm. Which option gives more chocolate?
Answer / method
Step 1: Volume of one 50 mm cube = 50³ = 125,000 mm³. Step 2: Volume of one 10 mm cube = 10³ = 1000 mm³. Step 3: Volume of 100 small cubes = 100×1000 = 100,000 mm³. Step 4: 125,000 > 100,000. Answer: Option A gives more chocolate.
5If all the people in the world are crowded together in one location, how much area would they cover?
Answer / method
Step 1: Estimate the number of people and the area occupied by one person, for example 0.25 m². Step 2: Area ≈ population × area per person. Step 3: Using an assumed population of 8×10⁹ gives approximately 2×10⁹ m², or 2000 km². Answer: About 2000 km² under the stated assumption; the result changes with the population and spacing assumed.
6A school provides milk in cylindrical glasses, each with diameter 7 cm and filled to a height of 12 cm. How many litres of milk are needed to serve 1600 students? Use 1000 cm³ = 1 litre.
Answer / method
Step 1: Radius = 7/2 = 3.5 cm and height = 12 cm. Step 2: Volume of one glass = πr²h = 22/7×3.5²×12 = 462 cm³. Step 3: For 1600 glasses, volume = 462×1600 = 739,200 cm³. Step 4: Convert to litres: 739,200 ÷ 1000 = 739.2 litres. Answer: 739.2 litres.
7The surface area of a sphere of radius 5 cm is five times the curved surface area of a cone of radius 4 cm. Find the height of the cone.
Answer / method
Step 1: Sphere surface area = 4π×5² = 100π. Step 2: Cone curved surface area = π×4×l. Five times this is 20πl. Step 3: 100π = 20πl, so l = 5 cm. Step 4: h² = l² − r² = 5² − 4² = 9, so h = 3 cm. Answer: Height 3 cm.
8Take the Earth, Jupiter and the Sun to be spheres with radii approximately 6379 km, 69,900 km and 695,700 km respectively. Compute the approximate ratios of the volume of Jupiter to Earth and the volume of the Sun to Earth.
Answer / method
Step 1: Volumes of spheres are proportional to the cubes of their radii. Step 2: Jupiter:Earth = (69,900:6379)³ ≈ 1315.75:1. Step 3: Sun:Earth = (695,700:6379)³ ≈ 1,297,204:1. Answer: Jupiter is approximately 1316 Earth-volumes; the Sun is approximately 1.297 million Earth-volumes.
9Show that the volume of a sphere is equal to 2/3 of the volume of the smallest cylinder that encloses it.
Answer / method
Step 1: Volume of a sphere is ⁴⁄₃πR³. Step 2: The smallest enclosing cylinder has radius R and height 2R. Step 3: Cylinder volume = πR²×2R = 2πR³. Step 4: Sphere:cylinder = (⁴⁄₃πR³):(2πR³) = 2:3. Answer: Sphere volume is 2/3 of the cylinder volume.
10A string is wrapped tightly around the equator of a spherical Earth. Another string is 1 m longer and forms a larger circle, staying the same distance above the ground everywhere. How high above the ground is the second string?
Answer / method
Step 1: Let the original radius be R and the new radius be R + h. Step 2: Original circumference = 2πR; new circumference = 2π(R + h). Step 3: Difference is 1 m, so 2πh = 1. Step 4: h = 1/(2π) m ≈ 0.159 m = 15.9 cm. Answer: Approximately 15.9 cm above the ground, independent of Earth’s radius.
11Repeat the string question for (i) the Moon, (ii) Jupiter and (iii) a volleyball. Are you surprised by the answers? Why?
Answer / method
Step 1: The derivation uses only circumference difference 2πh = 1. Step 2: The radius cancels, so the answer is the same for the Moon, Jupiter and a volleyball. Answer: Approximately 15.9 cm in all three cases. The result may seem surprising because it does not depend on the original radius.
12A cylinder has base radius r and height h. A square pyramid is fitted inside it: the square base lies on the cylinder’s base, its corners lie on the circular boundary, and its apex lies on the top of the cylinder. Find the ratio of the volume of the pyramid to the volume of the cylinder.
Answer / method
Step 1: The square base is inscribed in a circle of radius r, so its diagonal is 2r. Step 2: If the square side is s, s√2 = 2r, so s² = 2r². Step 3: Pyramid volume = ⅓×2r²×h = ⅔r²h. Step 4: Cylinder volume = πr²h. Answer: Ratio = (⅔r²h):(πr²h) = 2:3π.
13Find the change in volume when (i) the length of a cuboid with dimensions l, w, h is increased by 1 unit; (ii) the radius of a cylinder with dimensions r, h is increased by 1 unit; (iii) the radius of a sphere is decreased by 1 unit.
Answer / method
Step 1: Cuboid volume changes from lwh to (l + 1)wh. Increase = wh cubic units. Step 2: Cylinder volume changes from πr²h to π(r + 1)²h. Increase = π[(r + 1)² − r²]h = 2πrh + πh. Step 3: Sphere volume decreases from ⁴⁄₃πr³ to ⁴⁄₃π(r − 1)³. Decrease = ⁴⁄₃π[r³ − (r − 1)³] = 4πr² − 4πr + ⁴⁄₃π cubic units. Answer: wh; 2πrh + πh; and the stated sphere decrease.
14Given a cube with total surface area S, express its volume V in terms of S.
Answer / method
Step 1: For a cube, S = 6a². Step 2: a² = S/6, so a = √(S/6). Step 3: V = a³ = (S/6)^(3/2). Answer: V = (S/6)^(3/2) cubic units.
15A cylindrical glass has height 25 cm and radius 4 cm. Water is at a height of 16 cm. How many marbles of radius 1 cm should be dropped into the glass to make the water reach a height of 20 cm?
Answer / method
Step 1: Water rise = 20 − 16 = 4 cm. Step 2: Required displaced volume = cylinder base area × rise = π×4²×4 = 64π cm³. Step 3: One marble volume = ⁴⁄₃π×1³ = ⁴⁄₃π cm³. Step 4: Number = 64π ÷ (⁴⁄₃π) = 48. Answer: 48 marbles.
16Find the volume of ink in a new ball-point pen. Take the necessary measurements and make approximations as needed.
Answer / method
Step 1: Measure or estimate the inner radius r and length h of the ink tube. Step 2: Model the ink as a cylinder, so V = πr²h. Step 3: Convert cm³ to mL using 1 cm³ = 1 mL. Answer: The numerical value depends on the measurements; report the approximation with units and assumptions.
17(i) A ball of chapati dough has radius 6 cm. Estimate how many chapatis can be made from it. (ii) Cut a coconut or muskmelon in half and estimate the volume of edible flesh or fruit by taking necessary measurements.
Answer / method
Step 1: Measure the dough ball radius R and choose an estimated chapati radius r and thickness t. Step 2: Dough volume = ⁴⁄₃πR³. One chapati is approximately πr²t. Step 3: Estimated number = dough volume ÷ one chapati volume. Step 4: For the coconut or muskmelon, estimate the volume of the edible part by a suitable cylinder, sphere or composite shape and subtract the inedible part. Answer: These are measurement-based estimates; write the chosen measurements clearly.
18Looking at the Ganita Manjari Grade 9 Part 2 textbook: (i) If all pages were laid side by side on the floor, would they cover an entire classroom floor? (ii) What is the maximum number of textbooks that can fit in an empty storeroom of dimensions 15 ft × 20 ft × 30 ft?
Answer / method
Step 1: For the pages, estimate total page area and compare it with the classroom floor area. Include the thickness and page count if estimating a stack. Step 2: For textbooks, estimate one book’s volume and divide the storeroom volume 15×20×30 = 9000 ft³ by it. Step 3: Allow for gaps, shelves and movement space. Answer: No unique numerical answer is possible without page, book and room-use measurements.
19If the entire human population decided to climb into one giant cube, how long would one side of the cube have to be?
Answer / method
Step 1: Estimate population N and average volume occupied by one person, v. Step 2: Total volume ≈ Nv. Step 3: If the cube side is s, then s³ = Nv, so s = ∛(Nv). Answer: The numerical side depends on the population and personal-space assumption.
20The Earth’s surface has an estimated volume of 1.38 billion km³ of water. Suppose the Earth is a perfect sphere and all the water forms a uniform layer covering it. Estimate the thickness of this water layer. Take Earth’s radius as 6371 km.
Answer / method
Step 1: Let Earth’s radius be R = 6371 km and water-layer thickness be t. Step 2: Exact shell model: ⁴⁄₃π[(R + t)³ − R³] = 1.38×10⁹ km³. Step 3: Since t is small compared with R, use shell volume ≈ 4πR²t. Step 4: t ≈ 1.38×10⁹ ÷ (4π×6371²) ≈ 2.71 km. Answer: The water layer is approximately 2.7 km thick.
