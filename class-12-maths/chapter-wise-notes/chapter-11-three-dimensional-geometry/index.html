Last 24-Hour Checklist

Direction Cosines & Direction Ratios

Direction Cosines (DCs): If a line makes angles $α, β, γ$ with x, y, z axes respectively, then $l = \cosα, m = \cosβ, n = \cosγ$ are called direction cosines.
Relation: $l^2 + m^2 + n^2 = 1$
Direction Ratios (DRs): Any three numbers $a, b, c$ proportional to direction cosines $l, m, n$.
$la = mb = nc = k$

Finding DCs from DRs: $$l = ± a√a^2+b^2+c^2, \quad m = ± b√a^2+b^2+c^2, \quad n = ± c√a^2+b^2+c^2$$

Direction Ratios of line joining two points $P(x_1, y_1, z_1)$ and $Q(x_2, y_2, z_2)$:

$a = x_2 - x_1, \quad b = y_2 - y_1, \quad c = z_2 - z_1$

Equation of a Line in Space

1. Line through a Point and Parallel to a Vector

Vector Form

$\vec{r} = \vec{a} + λ\vec{b}$

$\vec{a}$: Position vector of point

$\vec{b}$: Parallel vector (Direction)

Cartesian Form

$x-x_1a = y-y_1b = z-z_1c$

$(x_1, y_1, z_1)$: Point

$a, b, c$: Direction Ratios

2. Line Passing Through Two Points

Vector Form

$\vec{r} = \vec{a} + λ(\vec{b} - \vec{a})$

Cartesian Form

$x-x_1x_2-x_1 = y-y_1y_2-y_1 = z-z_1z_2-z_1$

Angle and Distance Between Lines

Angle Between Two Lines:
If lines are parallel to vectors $\vec{b_1}$ and $\vec{b_2}$: $$\cosθ = | \vec{b_1} · \vec{b_2}|\vec{b_1}||\vec{b_2}| |$$ Conditions:
Perpendicular: $\vec{b_1} · \vec{b_2} = 0$ (or $a_1a_2 + b_1b_2 + c_1c_2 = 0$)
Parallel: $\vec{b_1} = λ\vec{b_2}$ (or $a_1a_2 = b_1b_2 = c_1c_2$)

Shortest Distance (Most Important 5M Topic)

Geometry of Skew Lines & Common Perpendicular

The shortest distance is the projection of $(\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1})$ along the unit normal $\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2}|\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2}|$.

Distance between Skew Lines

Vector Form: Lines $\vec{r} = \vec{a_1} + λ\vec{b_1}$ and $\vec{r} = \vec{a_2} + μ\vec{b_2}$

$$d = | (\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2}) · (\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1})|\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2}| |$$

Cartesian Form:

$d = | \begin{vmatrix} x_2-x_1 & y_2-y_1 & z_2-z_1 \\ a_1 & b_1 & c_1 \\ a_2 & b_2 & c_2 \end{vmatrix} √(b_1c_2 - b_2c_1)^2 + (c_1a_2 - c_2a_1)^2 + (a_1b_2 - a_2b_1)^2 |$

Distance between Parallel Lines

Lines: $\vec{r} = \vec{a_1} + λ\vec{b}$ and $\vec{r} = \vec{a_2} + μ\vec{b}$

$$d = | \vec{b} × (\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1})|\vec{b}| |$$

Equation of Plane Through Intersection of Two Planes

If two planes are $P_1 = 0$ and $P_2 = 0$, then the family of planes passing through their intersection line is: $$P_1 + λ P_2 = 0$$
Cartesian Form: $(A_1x + B_1y + C_1z + D_1) + λ(A_2x + B_2y + C_2z + D_2) = 0$
Vector Form: $\vec{r} · (\vec{n_1} + λ\vec{n_2}) = d_1 + λ d_2$

Solved Examples (Board Marking Scheme)

Q1. Find the direction cosines of the line passing through the points $(-2, 4, -5)$ and $(1, 2, 3)$. (2 Marks)

Step 1: Find Direction Ratios 1 Mark

$a = 1 - (-2) = 3$

$b = 2 - 4 = -2$

$c = 3 - (-5) = 8$

Step 2: Calculate Magnitude & DCs 1 Mark

$√a^2+b^2+c^2 = √9 + 4 + 64 = √77$

DCs are $3√77, -2√77, 8√77$

Q2. Find the shortest distance between the lines $\vec{r} = \mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} + λ(2\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k})$ and $\vec{r} = 2\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} - \mathbf{k} + μ(3\mathbf{i} - 5\mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k})$. (5 Marks)

Step 1: Identify Vectors 1 Mark

$\vec{a_1} = \mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j}, \quad \vec{b_1} = 2\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k}$

$\vec{a_2} = 2\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} - \mathbf{k}, \quad \vec{b_2} = 3\mathbf{i} - 5\mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k}$

$\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1} = \mathbf{i} - \mathbf{k}$

Step 2: Calculate $\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2}$ 2 Marks

$\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ 2 & -1 & 1 \\ 3 & -5 & 2 \end{vmatrix}$

$= \mathbf{i}(-2+5) - \mathbf{j}(4-3) + \mathbf{k}(-10+3) = 3\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} - 7\mathbf{k}$

$|\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2}| = √9 + 1 + 49 = √59$

Step 3: Calculate Dot Product 1 Mark

$(\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2}) · (\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1}) = (3\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} - 7\mathbf{k}) · (\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{k})$

$= 3(1) + (-1)(0) + (-7)(-1) = 3 + 7 = 10$

Step 4: Final Formula 1 Mark

$d = | 10√59 | = 10√59$ units.

Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

2023 (1 Mark MCQ): If the direction cosines of a line are $k, k, k$, then:
(A) $k > 0$   (B) $0 < k < 1$   (C) $k=± 1√3$   (D) $k=13$
Ans: (C). Since $l^2+m^2+n^2=1 \Rightarrow 3k^2=1 \Rightarrow k = ± 1√3$.
2022 (3 Marks): Find the equation of the line passing through the point $(1, 2, -4)$ and perpendicular to the two lines $x-83 = y+19-16 = z-107$ and $x-153 = y-298 = z-5-5$.
Hint: Direction of required line is $\vec{b} = \vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2}$.
$\vec{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ 3 & -16 & 7 \\ 3 & 8 & -5 \end{vmatrix}$. Then use point-parallel form.
2020 (5 Marks): Find the shortest distance between the lines whose vector equations are $\vec{r} = (1-t)\mathbf{i} + (t-2)\mathbf{j} + (3-2t)\mathbf{k}$ and $\vec{r} = (s+1)\mathbf{i} + (2s-1)\mathbf{j} - (2s+1)\mathbf{k}$.
Solution:
Convert to standard form:
$\vec{r} = (\mathbf{i}-2\mathbf{j}+3\mathbf{k}) + t(-\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}-2\mathbf{k})$
$\vec{r} = (\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}-\mathbf{k}) + s(\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}-2\mathbf{k})$
Here $\vec{a}_1 = \mathbf{i}-2\mathbf{j}+3\mathbf{k}, \vec{b}_1 = -\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}-2\mathbf{k}$
$\vec{a}_2 = \mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}-\mathbf{k}, \vec{b}_2 = \mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}-2\mathbf{k}$
Calculate $\vec{a}_2-\vec{a}_1 = \mathbf{j}-4\mathbf{k}$ and $\vec{b}_1 × \vec{b}_2 = 2\mathbf{i}-4\mathbf{j}-3\mathbf{k}$.
Distance $d = |8√29|$.

Exam Strategy & Mistake Bank

Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: In Cartesian form $x-x_1a$, ensuring coefficient of $x, y, z$ is 1. If given $2x-13$, rewrite as $x-1/23/2$.
Mistake 2: Forgetting the modulus in the shortest distance formula. Distance cannot be negative!
Mistake 3: Confusing Direction Ratios (DRs) with Direction Cosines (DCs). Remember $l^2+m^2+n^2=1$ for DCs.
Mistake 4: Calculation error in determinant expansion for cross product $\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2}$.

Scoring Tips

Tip 1: Always check if lines are parallel ($\vec{b_1} = λ\vec{b_2}$) before applying the skew lines formula.
Tip 2: For 5-mark questions on Shortest Distance, write the formula clearly at the start. It carries 1 mark.
Tip 3: If finding foot of perpendicular, assume a general point on the line and use dot product = 0.
Tip 4: Convert Cartesian to Vector form if you find vector operations easier (or vice versa).

Formula Sheet (Must Remember!)

Direction Cosines & Lines

1. Relation: $l^2 + m^2 + n^2 = 1$

2. From DRs: $l = ±a√a^2+b^2+c^2$, etc.

3. Vector Eq: $\vec{r} = \vec{a} + λ\vec{b}$

4. Cartesian Eq: $x-x_1a = y-y_1b = z-z_1c$

5. Two Points: $x-x_1x_2-x_1 = y-y_1y_2-y_1 = z-z_1z_2-z_1$

Angle & Distance

6. Angle: $\cosθ = | \vec{b}_1 · \vec{b}_2|\vec{b}_1||\vec{b}_2| |$ or $| a_1a_2+b_1b_2+c_1c_2√a_1^2+b_1^2+c_1^2√a_2^2+b_2^2+c_2^2 |$

7. Perpendicular: $\vec{b_1} · \vec{b_2} = 0$ or $a_1a_2 + b_1b_2 + c_1c_2 = 0$

8. Parallel: $a_1a_2 = b_1b_2 = c_1c_2$

9. Shortest Distance (Skew): $d = |(\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2}) · (\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1})|\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2}||$

10. Distance (Parallel): $d = |\vec{b} × (\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1})|\vec{b}||$

Practice Problems (Self-Assessment)

Level 1: Basic (1-2 Marks Each)

Q1. If a line makes angles $90^°, 135^°, 45^°$ with the x, y and z axes respectively, find its direction cosines.

Answer: $0, -1√2, 1√2$.

Q2. Find the equation of the line passing through $(1, 2, 3)$ and parallel to vector $3\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} - 2\mathbf{k}$.

Answer: $\vec{r} = (\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}+3\mathbf{k}) + λ(3\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}-2\mathbf{k})$.

Level 2: Intermediate (3-4 Marks Each)

Q3. Find the angle between the lines $x+33 = y-15 = z+34$ and $x+11 = y-41 = z-52$.

Answer: $\cosθ = 3(1)+5(1)+4(2)√9+25+16√1+1+4 = 16√50√6 = 1610√3$. $θ = \cos-1(85√3)$.

Q4. Find the coordinates of the foot of the perpendicular drawn from the point $(0, 2, 3)$ to the line $x+35 = y-12 = z+43$.

Hint: General point $P(5λ-3, 2λ+1, 3λ-4)$. Direction of perpendicular is $\vec{AP}$. Dot product with line direction $(5, 2, 3)$ is 0. Solve for $λ$.

Level 3: Advanced (5 Marks Each)

Q5. Find the shortest distance between the lines $\vec{r} = (6\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k}) + λ(\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k})$ and $\vec{r} = (-4\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{k}) + μ(3\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} - 2\mathbf{k})$.

Answer: 9 units.

Chapter 11 Board Mastery Test

CBSE Pattern • Max Marks: 10 • Time: 25 Mins

Q1 (1 Mark): If a line makes angles $90^°, 135^°, 45^°$ with the $x, y$ and $z$ axes respectively, find its direction cosines.
Q2 (2 Marks): Find the vector and cartesian equations of the line that passes through the point $(5, 2, -4)$ and is parallel to the vector $3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 8\hat{k}$.
Q3 (3 Marks): Find the angle between the pair of lines: $\vec{r} = (3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 4\hat{k}) + λ(\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k})$ and $\vec{r} = (5\hat{i} - 2\hat{j}) + μ(3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 6\hat{k})$.
Q4 (4 Marks): Find the shortest distance between the lines: $\vec{r} = (\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + \hat{k}) + λ(\hat{i} - \hat{j} + \hat{k})$ and $\vec{r} = (2\hat{i} - \hat{j} - \hat{k}) + μ(2\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 2\hat{k})$.

Marking Scheme & Solutions

Solution 1:

Direction cosines are $l = \cosα, m = \cosβ, n = \cosγ$.

$l = \cos 90^° = 0$, $m = \cos 135^° = \cos(180^° - 45^°) = -1√2$, $n = \cos 45^° = 1√2$.

$\therefore$ Direction cosines are $\mathbf{(0, -1√2, 1√2)}$. [1 Mark]

Solution 2:

Passing point $\vec{a} = 5\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 4\hat{k}$, parallel vector $\vec{b} = 3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 8\hat{k}$.

Vector Equation: $\vec{r} = \vec{a} + λ\vec{b} \implies \mathbf{\vec{r} = (5\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 4\hat{k}) + λ(3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 8\hat{k})}$. [1 Mark]

Cartesian Equation: $x-x_1a = y-y_1b = z-z_1c \implies \mathbf{x-53 = y-22 = z+4-8}$. [1 Mark]

Solution 3:

Direction vectors: $\vec{b_1} = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$ and $\vec{b_2} = 3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}$.

$\cosθ = |\vec{b_1} · \vec{b_2}||\vec{b_1}||\vec{b_2}|$. [1 Mark]

$\vec{b_1} · \vec{b_2} = (1)(3) + (2)(2) + (2)(6) = 3 + 4 + 12 = 19$.

$|\vec{b_1}| = √1^2 + 2^2 + 2^2 = √9 = 3$, $|\vec{b_2}| = √3^2 + 2^2 + 6^2 = √49 = 7$. [1 Mark]

$\cosθ = 193 × 7 = 1921 \implies \mathbf{θ = \cos-1(1921)}$. [1 Mark]

Solution 4:

$\vec{a_1} = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + \hat{k}$, $\vec{b_1} = \hat{i} - \hat{j} + \hat{k}$

$\vec{a_2} = 2\hat{i} - \hat{j} - \hat{k}$, $\vec{b_2} = 2\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$

$\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1} = (2-1)\hat{i} + (-1-2)\hat{j} + (-1-1)\hat{k} = \hat{i} - 3\hat{j} - 2\hat{k}$. [1 Mark]

$\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = \hat{i}(-2 - 1) - \hat{j}(2 - 2) + \hat{k}(1 + 2) = -3\hat{i} + 3\hat{k}$. [1 Mark]

$|\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2}| = √(-3)^2 + 0^2 + 3^2 = √18 = 3√2$. [0.5 Mark]

$(\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1}) · (\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2}) = (1)(-3) + (-3)(0) + (-2)(3) = -3 - 6 = -9$. [0.5 Mark]

$d = |(\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1}) · (\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2})|\vec{b_1} × \vec{b_2}|| = |-9|3√2 = 93√2 = 3√2 = \mathbf{3√22 units}$. [1 Mark]

Frequently Asked Questions: Class 12 Three-Dimensional Geometry

1. What is the weightage of Chapter 11 Three-Dimensional Geometry in CBSE Class 12? +

Together with Vectors, it forms Unit IV (14 marks). Chapter 11 frequently features a 5-mark subjective question or a 4-mark case study.

2. What is the formula for the shortest distance between two skew lines? +

For lines r = a1 + lambda b1 and r = a2 + mu b2, the shortest distance d = | (b1 x b2) . (a2 - a1) | / |b1 x b2|.

3. What is the relation between direction cosines l, m, n? +

Direction cosines satisfy l^2 + m^2 + n^2 = 1. If direction ratios are a, b, c, then l = a / sqrt(a^2 + b^2 + c^2).

4. Are equations of planes included in the CBSE Class 12 syllabus? +

No, equations of planes have been removed from the current CBSE board syllabus. The syllabus focuses strictly on direction cosines/ratios, equations of lines, and shortest distance between lines.
