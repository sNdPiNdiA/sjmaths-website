5. Three-Dimensional Geometry

Coplanar lines

Coplanar lines are two or more lines lying in the exact same plane, possessing either a single point of intersection or being strictly parallel. Mastery of their coplanar condition via scalar triple products and determinant criteria forms a high-yield scoring zone in UP TGT/PGT, DSSSB, and KVS examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Condition for Coplanarity of Two Lines in Space
Two lines given in vector form by $\vec{r} = \vec{a}_1 + \lambda \vec{b}_1$ and $\vec{r} = \vec{a}_2 + \mu \vec{b}_2$ are coplanar if and only if the shortest distance between them is zero, which algebraically translates to the scalar triple product condition: $$[\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1, \vec{b}_1, \vec{b}_2] = 0$$ In Cartesian form, given lines \(\frac{x - x_1}{l_1} = \frac{y - y_1}{m_1} = \frac{z - z_1}{n_1}\) and \(\frac{x - x_2}{l_2} = \frac{y - y_2}{m_2} = \frac{z - z_2}{n_2}\), the condition is: $$\begin{vmatrix} x_2 - x_1 & y_2 - y_1 & z_2 - z_1 \\ l_1 & m_1 & n_1 \\ l_2 & m_2 & n_2 \end{vmatrix} = 0$$

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, coplanarity implies that the vector joining a fixed point on the first line to a fixed point on the second line, along with the two directional vectors, are linearly dependent and lie in the same flat two-dimensional surface. Thus, they form a parallelopiped of zero volume.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, it reduces the spatial relative positioning of two lines from skew (non-intersecting, non-parallel) to a degenerate system solvable by linear equations in a single plane, yielding the exact equation of the containing plane via det \(\begin{vmatrix} x - x_1 & y - y_1 & z - z_1 \\ l_1 & m_1 & n_1 \\ l_2 & m_2 & n_2 \end{vmatrix} = 0\).

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Determinant Zero Test for Cartesian Lines

$$\begin{vmatrix} x_2 - x_1 & y_2 - y_1 & z_2 - z_1 \\ a_1 & b_1 & c_1 \\ a_2 & b_2 & c_2 \end{vmatrix} = 0$$

Exam Rule: Instantly substitute point differences and direction ratios into a 3x3 determinant and equate to zero to test coplanarity or find unknown parameters in under 30 seconds.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
02

Shortcut 2: Equation of the Containing Plane

$$\begin{vmatrix} x - x_1 & y - y_1 & z - z_1 \\ l_1 & m_1 & n_1 \\ l_2 & m_2 & n_2 \end{vmatrix} = 0$$

Exam Rule: Replacing the point difference row with \((x - x_1, y - y_1, z - z_1)\) directly generates the scalar equation of the unique plane containing both lines.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
03

Shortcut 3: Parallel Lines Coplanarity Check

$$ (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1) \times \vec{b} = \vec{0} $$

Exam Rule: If direction vectors are identical or proportional (\(\vec{b}_1 = \vec{b}_2 = \vec{b}\)), the lines are coplanar if and only if the cross product of the position vector difference and the direction vector vanishes.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
04

Shortcut 4: Intersection Point via Equal Ratios

$$ x_1 + \lambda l_1 = x_2 + \mu l_2, \quad y_1 + \lambda m_1 = y_2 + \mu m_2 $$

Exam Rule: Equate any two coordinate components to solve for parameters \(\lambda\) and \(\mu\), then verify consistency in the third component.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
05

Shortcut 5: Shortest Distance Formula

$$ d = \frac{|[\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1, \vec{b}_1, \vec{b}_2]|}{||\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2||} $$

Exam Rule: If the numerator evaluates to zero, lines are coplanar; otherwise, the ratio gives the exact shortest distance.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
06

Shortcut 6: Symmetry & Option Elimination

$$ \text{Substitute given options for parameters into } \Delta = 0 $$

Exam Rule: When an unknown parameter \(k\) is present in the lines, test the given multiple-choice integer/fraction options directly in the determinant to find the root instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Assuming Intersection Implies Parallelism Failure

Common Pitfall: Candidates confuse skew lines with intersecting lines. Skew lines are non-coplanar, whereas intersecting lines are coplanar.

Counterexample: \text{Lines with } [\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1, \vec{b}_1, \vec{b}_2] \neq 0

Why it fails: Assuming lines in 3D must intersect if they are not parallel leads to severe calculation errors.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check the scalar triple product determinant before attempting to find an intersection point.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Ignoring Parallel Direction Vectors

Common Pitfall: Applying the standard 3x3 determinant when \(\vec{b}_1 = k \vec{b}_2\), which results in a determinant of identically zero \((0 = 0)\) regardless of whether the lines are coplanar or skew.

Counterexample: \text{Parallel lines with different base points and identical direction ratios}

Why it fails: The determinant row-rank drops, yielding a trivial zero without testing point alignment.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check if direction ratios are proportional first. If parallel, use cross-product \((\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1) \times \vec{b} = \vec{0}\).

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the value of \(k\) for which the lines \(\frac{x - 1}{2} = \frac{y - 2}{3} = \frac{z - 3}{4}\) and \(\frac{x - 4}{5} = \frac{y - 1}{2} = \frac{z - k}{1}\) are coplanar.

(A) 3(B) 4(C) 6(D) 7
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Identify points \((1, 2, 3)\) and \((4, 1, k)\) and direction vectors \((2, 3, 4)\) and \((5, 2, 1)\). Step 2: Set up the coplanarity determinant: \(\begin{vmatrix} 4-1 & 1-2 & k-3 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 5 & 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 0\). Step 3: Compute determinant \(\begin{vmatrix} 3 & -1 & k-3 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 5 & 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 3(3 - 8) - (-1)(2 - 20) + (k-3)(4 - 15) = 0\). Step 4: Simplify \(-15 - 18 - 11(k-3) = 0 \implies -33 - 11k + 33 = 0 \implies 11k = 66 \implies k = 6\).
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Substitute option values of \(k\) directly into the 3x3 determinant row \([3, -1, k-3]\) until the determinant evaluates to zero.
💡 Key Takeaway: Be extremely careful with sign conventions when subtracting coordinate points \((x_2 - x_1, y_2 - y_1, z_2 - z_1)\).
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

The equation of the plane containing the lines \(\frac{x}{1} = \frac{y}{2} = \frac{z}{3}\) and \(\frac{x - 1}{2} = \frac{y - 1}{3} = \frac{z - 1}{4}\) is:

(A) x - 2y + z = 0(B) x + y - z = 0(C) 2x - y + z = 0(D) x - y + z = 0
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Points are \((0,0,0)\) and \((1,1,1)\). Difference is \((1,1,1)\). Direction ratios are \((1,2,3)\) and \((2,3,4)\). Step 2: Plane determinant is \(\begin{vmatrix} x & y & z \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 \end{vmatrix} = 0\). Step 3: Expand: \(x(8-9) - y(4-6) + z(3-4) = 0 \implies -x + 2y - z = 0 \implies x - 2y + z = 0\).
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Test the point \((1,1,1)\) from the second line directly in the options. Only \((A)\) gives \(1 - 2(1) + 1 = 0\). Test \((0,0,0)\) as well.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always test both base points in the options to eliminate distractors instantly before full expansion.

Set up the determinant: \(\begin{vmatrix} 3-1 & k-(-1) & 0-1 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 0 \implies \begin{vmatrix} 2 & k+1 & -1 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 0\). MCQ 7: What is the equation of the plane containing \(\frac{x-1}{2} = \frac{y-1}{3} = \frac{z-1}{1}\) and \(\frac{x}{1} = \frac{y-1}{2} = \frac{z}{3}\)?

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

If the determinant is zero, the lines are coplanar. To check if they intersect (rather than being strictly parallel), test whether their direction vectors are non-proportional. If non-proportional, they intersect at a unique point.

Failing to check if the direction vectors are proportional before applying the 3x3 determinant. For parallel lines, the determinant identically vanishes, yielding a false positive for intersection.

Typically 2 to 4 questions appear directly from lines, planes, and coplanarity conditions in every UP PGT Mathematics paper, carrying immense rank-deciding weightage.

The Cartesian determinant method \(\begin{vmatrix} x_2-x_1 & y_2-y_1 & z_2-z_1 \\ l_1 & m_1 & n_1 \\ l_2 & m_2 & n_2 \end{vmatrix} = 0\) is significantly faster and less prone to cross-product arithmetic errors under exam pressure.

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the value of \(k\) for which the lines \(\frac{x - 1}{2} = \frac{y - 2}{3} = \frac{z - 3}{4}\) and \(\frac{x - 4}{5} = \frac{y - 1}{2} = \frac{z - k}{1}\) are coplanar.

(A) 3(B) 4(C) 6(D) 7
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Identify points \((1, 2, 3)\) and \((4, 1, k)\) and direction vectors \((2, 3, 4)\) and \((5, 2, 1)\). Step 2: Set up the coplanarity determinant: \(\begin{vmatrix} 4-1 & 1-2 & k-3 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 5 & 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 0\). Step 3: Compute determinant \(\begin{vmatrix} 3 & -1 & k-3 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 5 & 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 3(3 - 8) - (-1)(2 - 20) + (k-3)(4 - 15) = 0\). Step 4: Simplify \(-15 - 18 - 11(k-3) = 0 \implies -33 - 11k + 33 = 0 \implies 11k = 66 \implies k = 6\).
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Substitute option values of \(k\) directly into the 3x3 determinant row \([3, -1, k-3]\) until the determinant evaluates to zero.
💡 Key Takeaway: Be extremely careful with sign conventions when subtracting coordinate points \((x_2 - x_1, y_2 - y_1, z_2 - z_1)\).
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

The equation of the plane containing the lines \(\frac{x}{1} = \frac{y}{2} = \frac{z}{3}\) and \(\frac{x - 1}{2} = \frac{y - 1}{3} = \frac{z - 1}{4}\) is:

(A) x - 2y + z = 0(B) x + y - z = 0(C) 2x - y + z = 0(D) x - y + z = 0
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Points are \((0,0,0)\) and \((1,1,1)\). Difference is \((1,1,1)\). Direction ratios are \((1,2,3)\) and \((2,3,4)\). Step 2: Plane determinant is \(\begin{vmatrix} x & y & z \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 \end{vmatrix} = 0\). Step 3: Expand: \(x(8-9) - y(4-6) + z(3-4) = 0 \implies -x + 2y - z = 0 \implies x - 2y + z = 0\).
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Test the point \((1,1,1)\) from the second line directly in the options. Only \((A)\) gives \(1 - 2(1) + 1 = 0\). Test \((0,0,0)\) as well.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always test both base points in the options to eliminate distractors instantly before full expansion.

Set up the determinant: \(\begin{vmatrix} 3-1 & k-(-1) & 0-1 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 0 \implies \begin{vmatrix} 2 & k+1 & -1 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 0\). Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

If the determinant is zero, the lines are coplanar. To check if they intersect (rather than being strictly parallel), test whether their direction vectors are non-proportional. If non-proportional, they intersect at a unique point.

Failing to check if the direction vectors are proportional before applying the 3x3 determinant. For parallel lines, the determinant identically vanishes, yielding a false positive for intersection.

Typically 2 to 4 questions appear directly from lines, planes, and coplanarity conditions in every UP PGT Mathematics paper, carrying immense rank-deciding weightage.

The Cartesian determinant method \(\begin{vmatrix} x_2-x_1 & y_2-y_1 & z_2-z_1 \\ l_1 & m_1 & n_1 \\ l_2 & m_2 & n_2 \end{vmatrix} = 0\) is significantly faster and less prone to cross-product arithmetic errors under exam pressure.
