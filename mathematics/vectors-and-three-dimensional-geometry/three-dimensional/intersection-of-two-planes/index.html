5. Three-Dimensional Geometry

Intersection of two planes

The intersection of two non-parallel planes in three-dimensional space always forms a straight line whose direction vector is given by the cross product of their normal vectors. Mastery of plane intersection equations, symmetrical forms, and family of planes is a high-yielding, recurring scoring zone in UP PGT/TGT, DSSSB, and KVS examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Fundamental Principle of Plane Intersection
Let two distinct non-parallel planes be given by their Cartesian equations $\Pi_1: A_1x + B_1y + C_1z + D_1 = 0$ and $\Pi_2: A_2x + B_2y + C_2z + D_2 = 0$. Their intersection forms a unique straight line if and only if their normal vectors $\mathbf{n}_1 = (A_1, B_1, C_1)$ and $\mathbf{n}_2 = (A_2, B_2, C_2)$ are not collinear, i.e., $\frac{A_1}{A_2} \neq \frac{B_1}{B_2}$ or $\frac{B_1}{B_2} \neq \frac{C_1}{C_2}$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, two planes in $\mathbb{R}^3$ can be parallel and distinct (no intersection), parallel and coincident (infinite intersection forming a plane), or intersecting. When they intersect non-coincidentally, their common locus is a linear subspace of dimension 1, representing a unique line in three-dimensional space.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, the intersection corresponds to the solution set of a system of two linear equations in three variables with a rank of 2, possessing a single free parameter that directly generates the symmetric or vector equations of the line of intersection.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Direction Ratios of the Line of Intersection

$$l : m : n = \begin{vmatrix} B_1 & C_1 \\ B_2 & C_2 \end{vmatrix} : \begin{vmatrix} C_1 & A_1 \\ C_2 & A_2 \end{vmatrix} : \begin{vmatrix} A_1 & B_1 \\ A_2 & B_2 \end{vmatrix}$$

Exam Rule: Instantly write down the direction ratios $(l, m, n)$ of the line of intersection by evaluating the 2x2 determinants formed by covering up columns in the normal coefficient matrix.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT and TGT examinations to find symmetrical equations of lines passing through plane intersections.
02

Shortcut 2: Family of Planes Passing Through the Line of Intersection

$$(A_1x + B_1y + C_1z + D_1) + \lambda (A_2x + B_2y + C_2z + D_2) = 0$$

Exam Rule: Any plane passing through the line of intersection of $\Pi_1 = 0$ and $\Pi_2 = 0$ belongs to this pencil of planes parameterized by real scalar $\lambda$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in DSSSB PGT and KVS exams when finding a plane perpendicular to a third plane or passing through a given point.
03

Shortcut 3: Finding a Point on the Line of Intersection

$$z = 0 \implies x = \frac{B_1D_2 - B_2D_1}{A_1B_2 - A_2B_1}, \quad y = \frac{A_2D_1 - A_1D_2}{A_1B_2 - A_2B_1}$$

Exam Rule: To find a specific point on the intersection line, set one coordinate (e.g., $z = 0$) and solve the resulting $2 \times 2$ linear system using Cramer's Rule instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves 60-90 seconds during UP PGT vector geometry problem solving.
04

Shortcut 4: Angle Between Two Intersecting Planes

$$$\cos \theta = \frac{|A_1A_2 + B_1B_2 + C_1C_2|}{\sqrt{A_1^2 + B_1^2 + C_1^2}\sqrt{A_2^2 + B_2^2 + C_2^2}}$$

Exam Rule: The acute angle $\theta$ between two intersecting planes is identical to the angle between their respective normal vectors.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard question type in LT Grade and UP TGT mathematics.
05

Shortcut 5: Condition for Perpendicular Planes

$$A_1A_2 + B_1B_2 + C_1C_2 = 0$$

Exam Rule: Two planes are mutually perpendicular if and only if the dot product of their normal vectors vanishes.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct property checks in multiple-choice screening tests.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Option Elimination via Point Substitution

$$A_1x_0 + B_1y_0 + C_1z_0 + D_1 = 0 \text{ and } A_2x_0 + B_2y_0 + C_2z_0 + D_2 = 0$$

Exam Rule: Any valid point $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$ on the line of intersection must satisfy both plane equations simultaneously. Test options directly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Enables instant elimination of 2 incorrect line equation options in competitive exams.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Parallel Planes with Intersecting Planes

Common Pitfall: Candidates blindly apply cross-product formulas to ratios $\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \frac{B_1}{B_2} = \frac{C_1}{C_2}$, resulting in division by zero or nonsensical direction ratios.

Counterexample: $x + 2y + 3z = 4$ and $2x + 4y + 6z = 10$

Why it fails: The normals are proportional ($\mathbf{n}_2 = 2\mathbf{n}_1$), but $D_1/D_2 \neq 1/2$, meaning the planes are strictly parallel with no intersection.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check normal proportionality $\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \frac{B_1}{B_2} = \frac{C_1}{C_2}$ before attempting to find the line of intersection.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Coincident Planes Error

Common Pitfall: Treating identical plane equations multiplied by a scalar as two distinct intersecting planes forming a line.

Counterexample: $x + y + z = 1$ and $2x + 2y + 2z = 2$

Why it fails: The equations represent the exact same geometric plane; their intersection is the entire plane, not a unique line.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check if $\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \frac{B_1}{B_2} = \frac{C_1}{C_2} = \frac{D_1}{D_2}$. If true, they are coincident.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the symmetric equations of the line of intersection of the planes $x - 2y + z = 0$ and $2x + y - 3z = 5$.

(A) $\frac{x-2}{5} = \frac{y-1}{5} = \frac{z}{5}$(B) $\frac{x}{5} = \frac{y+1}{5} = \frac{z-2}{5}$(C) $\frac{x}{1} = \frac{y}{2} = \frac{z}{3}$(D) $\frac{x-1}{1} = \frac{y-1}{1} = \frac{z-1}{1}$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Compute direction ratios using cross product of normals: $l = (-2)(-3) - (1)(1) = 5$, $m = (1)(2) - (-3)(1) = 5$, $n = (1)(1) - (-2)(2) = 5$. Thus direction ratios are $5:5:5$ or $1:1:1$. Step 2: Put $z = 0$ to find a point: $x - 2y = 0$ and $2x + y = 5 \implies x = 2, y = 1$. Step 3: Line equation is $\frac{x-2}{1} = \frac{y-1}{1} = \frac{z-0}{1}$, which matches option (A) scaled by 5.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Test point $(2, 1, 0)$ in the given planes: $2 - 2(1) + 0 = 0$ (Satisfied). $2(2) + 1 - 3(0) = 5$ (Satisfied). Check option (A) point $(2,1,0)$: $\frac{2-2}{5} = 0$, etc. Only option (A) passes through this point with direction ratios $1:1:1$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always verify the point on the line first by setting one coordinate to zero; it eliminates 3 incorrect options instantly.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

The equation of the plane passing through the intersection of the planes $x + y + z = 1$ and $2x + 3y + 4z = 5$ and parallel to the x-axis is:

(A) $y + 2z = 3$(B) $x + y = 2$(C) $3x + 4y = 7$(D) $z = 1$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write family of planes: $(x + y + z - 1) + \lambda(2x + 3y + 4z - 5) = 0$. Step 2: Rearrange terms: $(1+2\lambda)x + (1+3\lambda)y + (1+4\lambda)z - (1+5\lambda) = 0$. Step 3: Since the plane is parallel to the x-axis, the coefficient of $x$ must be zero. $1 + 2\lambda = 0 \implies \lambda = -1/2$. Step 4: Substitute $\lambda = -1/2$: $0x + (1 - 3/2)y + (1 - 2)z - (1 - 5/2) = 0 \implies -\frac{1}{2}y - z + \frac{3}{2} = 0 \implies y + 2z = 3$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
A plane parallel to the x-axis has no $x$-term. Set the coefficient of $x$ in $(1+2\lambda) = 0 \implies \lambda = -1/2$ and substitute mentally.
💡 Key Takeaway: Remember: Parallel to $x$-axis $\implies A = 0$; parallel to $y$-axis $\implies B = 0$; parallel to $z$-axis $\implies C = 0$.

MCQ 5: The distance between the parallel planes $2x - 2y + z + 3 = 0$ and $4x - 4y + 2z + 5 = 0$ is:

D \text{(D) } \frac{5}{6}
Derivation & Analysis:

Normalize the first equation to match normal coefficients: $4x - 4y + 2z + 6 = 0$. Distance formula between $Ax + By + Cz + D_1 = 0$ and $Ax + By + Cz + D_2 = 0$ is $\frac{|D_1 - D_2|}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2 + C^2}}$. Here $D_1 = 6$, $D_2 = 5$, $A=4, B=-4, C=2$. Distance = $\frac{|6 - 5|}{\sqrt{16 + 16 + 4}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{36}} = \frac{1}{6}$. Correct option is (A).

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): Two distinct planes can intersect in a single point in $\mathbb{R}^3$. Reason (R): The intersection of two non-parallel planes is always a straight line.
A \text{(A) } Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)

Two distinct planes in $\mathbb{R}^3$ can never intersect in a single point; they either do not intersect (parallel) or intersect in a line. Thus Assertion (A) is false and Reason (R) is true. Correct option is (D).

MCQ 9: The projection of the line segment joining $(1, 2, 3)$ and $(3, 5, 7)$ on the plane $x + 2y + 2z = 9$ is:

C \text{(C) } \frac{14}{3}

Derivation & Analysis:

Vector $\mathbf{AB} = (2, 3, 4)$. Normal to plane $\mathbf{n} = (1, 2, 2)$, $|\mathbf{n}| = 3$. Length of $\mathbf{AB} = \sqrt{4+9+16} = \sqrt{29}$. Angle $\theta$ between vector and normal: $\cos \theta = \frac{2(1) + 3(2) + 4(2)}{\sqrt{29}(3)} = \frac{16}{3\sqrt{29}}$. Sine of angle with plane gives projection: $\sin \theta = \sqrt{1 - \cos^2 \theta} = \sqrt{1 - \frac{256}{261}} = \sqrt{\frac{5}{261}}$. Projection of vector on plane is $|\mathbf{AB}| \cos(\text{angle with plane}) = |\mathbf{AB}| \sin \theta = \sqrt{29} \times \frac{\sqrt{5}}{3\sqrt{29}} = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$? MCQ 10: If a plane passes through the point $(1, 1, 1)$ and is perpendicular to the line of intersection of planes $x + y + z = 0$ and $2x - y + 3z = 0$, its equation is:

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Compare the ratios of the coefficients of $x, y, z$ ($A_1/A_2, B_1/B_2, C_1/C_2$). If they are not all equal, the planes intersect in a unique line. If they are equal but unequal to $D_1/D_2$, the planes are strictly parallel. If all four ratios including $D_1/D_2$ are equal, the planes coincide.

Sign errors while calculating 2x2 determinants for the direction ratios of the line of intersection, and forgetting to verify whether the planes are actually parallel before applying cross-product formulas.

Typically, 8 to 12 questions out of 125 are directly or indirectly based on lines, planes, spheres, and their intersections, making this chapter extremely high-yielding for selection.

Use the pencil equation $\Pi_1 + \lambda \Pi_2 = 0$, substitute the given point directly to find $\lambda$ instantly without expanding the entire algebraic expression first.

Shortcut 3: Finding a Point on the Line of Intersection

$$z = 0 \implies x = \frac{B_1D_2 - B_2D_1}{A_1B_2 - A_2B_1}, \quad y = \frac{A_2D_1 - A_1D_2}{A_1B_2 - A_2B_1}$$

Exam Rule: To find a specific point on the intersection line, set one coordinate (e.g., $z = 0$) and solve the resulting $2 \times 2$ linear system using Cramer's Rule instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves 60-90 seconds during UP PGT vector geometry problem solving.
04

Shortcut 4: Angle Between Two Intersecting Planes

$$$\cos \theta = \frac{|A_1A_2 + B_1B_2 + C_1C_2|}{\sqrt{A_1^2 + B_1^2 + C_1^2}\sqrt{A_2^2 + B_2^2 + C_2^2}}$$

Exam Rule: The acute angle $\theta$ between two intersecting planes is identical to the angle between their respective normal vectors.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard question type in LT Grade and UP TGT mathematics.
05

Shortcut 5: Condition for Perpendicular Planes

$$A_1A_2 + B_1B_2 + C_1C_2 = 0$$

Exam Rule: Two planes are mutually perpendicular if and only if the dot product of their normal vectors vanishes.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct property checks in multiple-choice screening tests.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Option Elimination via Point Substitution

$$A_1x_0 + B_1y_0 + C_1z_0 + D_1 = 0 \text{ and } A_2x_0 + B_2y_0 + C_2z_0 + D_2 = 0$$

Exam Rule: Any valid point $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$ on the line of intersection must satisfy both plane equations simultaneously. Test options directly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Enables instant elimination of 2 incorrect line equation options in competitive exams.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Parallel Planes with Intersecting Planes

Common Pitfall: Candidates blindly apply cross-product formulas to ratios $\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \frac{B_1}{B_2} = \frac{C_1}{C_2}$, resulting in division by zero or nonsensical direction ratios.

Counterexample: $x + 2y + 3z = 4$ and $2x + 4y + 6z = 10$

Why it fails: The normals are proportional ($\mathbf{n}_2 = 2\mathbf{n}_1$), but $D_1/D_2 \neq 1/2$, meaning the planes are strictly parallel with no intersection.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check normal proportionality $\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \frac{B_1}{B_2} = \frac{C_1}{C_2}$ before attempting to find the line of intersection.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Coincident Planes Error

Common Pitfall: Treating identical plane equations multiplied by a scalar as two distinct intersecting planes forming a line.

Counterexample: $x + y + z = 1$ and $2x + 2y + 2z = 2$

Why it fails: The equations represent the exact same geometric plane; their intersection is the entire plane, not a unique line.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check if $\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \frac{B_1}{B_2} = \frac{C_1}{C_2} = \frac{D_1}{D_2}$. If true, they are coincident.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the symmetric equations of the line of intersection of the planes $x - 2y + z = 0$ and $2x + y - 3z = 5$.

(A) $\frac{x-2}{5} = \frac{y-1}{5} = \frac{z}{5}$(B) $\frac{x}{5} = \frac{y+1}{5} = \frac{z-2}{5}$(C) $\frac{x}{1} = \frac{y}{2} = \frac{z}{3}$(D) $\frac{x-1}{1} = \frac{y-1}{1} = \frac{z-1}{1}$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Compute direction ratios using cross product of normals: $l = (-2)(-3) - (1)(1) = 5$, $m = (1)(2) - (-3)(1) = 5$, $n = (1)(1) - (-2)(2) = 5$. Thus direction ratios are $5:5:5$ or $1:1:1$. Step 2: Put $z = 0$ to find a point: $x - 2y = 0$ and $2x + y = 5 \implies x = 2, y = 1$. Step 3: Line equation is $\frac{x-2}{1} = \frac{y-1}{1} = \frac{z-0}{1}$, which matches option (A) scaled by 5.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Test point $(2, 1, 0)$ in the given planes: $2 - 2(1) + 0 = 0$ (Satisfied). $2(2) + 1 - 3(0) = 5$ (Satisfied). Check option (A) point $(2,1,0)$: $\frac{2-2}{5} = 0$, etc. Only option (A) passes through this point with direction ratios $1:1:1$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always verify the point on the line first by setting one coordinate to zero; it eliminates 3 incorrect options instantly.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

The equation of the plane passing through the intersection of the planes $x + y + z = 1$ and $2x + 3y + 4z = 5$ and parallel to the x-axis is:

(A) $y + 2z = 3$(B) $x + y = 2$(C) $3x + 4y = 7$(D) $z = 1$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write family of planes: $(x + y + z - 1) + \lambda(2x + 3y + 4z - 5) = 0$. Step 2: Rearrange terms: $(1+2\lambda)x + (1+3\lambda)y + (1+4\lambda)z - (1+5\lambda) = 0$. Step 3: Since the plane is parallel to the x-axis, the coefficient of $x$ must be zero. $1 + 2\lambda = 0 \implies \lambda = -1/2$. Step 4: Substitute $\lambda = -1/2$: $0x + (1 - 3/2)y + (1 - 2)z - (1 - 5/2) = 0 \implies -\frac{1}{2}y - z + \frac{3}{2} = 0 \implies y + 2z = 3$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
A plane parallel to the x-axis has no $x$-term. Set the coefficient of $x$ in $(1+2\lambda) = 0 \implies \lambda = -1/2$ and substitute mentally.
💡 Key Takeaway: Remember: Parallel to $x$-axis $\implies A = 0$; parallel to $y$-axis $\implies B = 0$; parallel to $z$-axis $\implies C = 0$.
