Vector Analysis

Position vector of ordered pair and ordered triad

The position vector establishes a canonical bijection between geometric points in space and algebraic vectors originating from the origin, forming the foundational bedrock for three-dimensional analytic geometry. Mastering ordered pairs in $\mathbb{R}^2$ and ordered triads in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is a high-yield, frequent scoring zone in UP PGT/TGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations, often featuring directly in section formula, collinearity, and coplanarity problems.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Fundamental Theorem of Position Vectors in $\mathbb{R}^2$ and $\mathbb{R}^3$
Let $O$ be the fixed origin in Euclidean space. For any point $P(x_1, y_1)$ in the Cartesian plane $\mathbb{R}^2$, the position vector is defined as $\vec{r} = \vec{OP} = x_1\hat{i} + y_1\hat{j}$, expressed as an ordered pair $(x_1, y_1)$. For any point $Q(x_2, y_2, z_2)$ in three-dimensional space $\mathbb{R}^3$, the position vector is the ordered triad $\vec{r} = \vec{OQ} = x_2\hat{i} + y_2\hat{j} + z_2\hat{k} = (x_2, y_2, z_2)$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

A position vector anchors a free geometric vector to a specific reference point $O(0,0)$ or $O(0,0,0)$, uniquely determining the spatial location of point $P$ or $Q$ relative to the coordinate axes via directed lengths along orthogonal unit vectors $\hat{i}, \hat{j}, \hat{k}$.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It sets up a vector space isomorphism between geometric points in $\mathbb{E}^3$ and algebraic tuples in $\mathbb{R}^3$, allowing geometric relations such as collinearity, coplanarity, and distance to be translated into pure linear algebra.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Fixed Origin Reference Global coordinate system Strictly Mandatory / Necessary Without a fixed origin $O$, position vectors lose their absolute spatial meaning and reduce to relative displacement vectors.
Orthogonality of Basis Vectors $\hat{i}, \hat{j}, \hat{k}$ standard basis Strictly Mandatory If the basis vectors are non-orthogonal, the scalar components $(x, y, z)$ do not correspond to standard Cartesian orthogonal projections.
Dimensional Consistency $\mathbb{R}^2$ vs $\mathbb{R}^3$ Essential for conclusion Mixing ordered pairs and ordered triads leads to dimensional incompatibility in vector addition and cross products.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Distance Between Two Position Vectors

$$ |\vec{b} - \vec{a}| = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2 + (z_2 - z_1)^2} $$

Exam Rule: Direct magnitude of the difference vector gives the Euclidean distance between points with position vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
02

Shortcut 2: Section Formula (Internal and External Division)

$$ \vec{r} = \frac{m\vec{b} \pm n\vec{a}}{m \pm n} $$

Exam Rule: Use '+' for internal division and '-' for external division of line segment joining position vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ in ratio $m:n$.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
03

Shortcut 3: Centroid of a Triangle and Tetrahedron

$$ \vec{r}_G = \frac{\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}}{3} \quad \text{and} \quad \vec{r}_T = \frac{\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c} + \vec{d}}{4} $$

Exam Rule: The position vector of the centroid is simply the arithmetic mean of the position vectors of its vertices.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
04

Shortcut 4: Condition for Collinearity of Three Points

$$ \vec{b} - \vec{a} = k(\vec{c} - \vec{b}) \quad \text{or} \quad \vec{c} = x\vec{a} + y\vec{b} \text{ where } x+y=1 $$

Exam Rule: Points with position vectors $\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c}$ are collinear if vector differences are scalar multiples or if their linear combination sums to 1.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
05

Shortcut 5: Condition for Coplanarity of Four Points

$$ [\vec{b}-\vec{a}, \, \vec{c}-\vec{a}, \, \vec{d}-\vec{a}] = 0 $$

Exam Rule: Four points with position vectors $\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c}, \vec{d}$ are coplanar if the scalar triple product of their mutual differences vanishes.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
06

Shortcut 6: Midpoint Position Vector

$$ \vec{r}_{mid} = \frac{\vec{a} + \vec{b}}{2} $$

Exam Rule: Instantly find the midpoint by averaging the position vector components.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Position Vector with Displacement Vector

Common Pitfall: Candidates treat a displacement vector $\vec{AB} = \vec{b} - \vec{a}$ as a position vector originating from the origin $O$.

Counterexample: For $A(1,2)$ and $B(3,4)$, $\vec{AB} = (2,2)$ whereas position vector of $B$ is $(3,4)$.

Why it fails: Displacement vectors represent relative position between two arbitrary points, whereas position vectors are strictly anchored at the origin.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check whether the vector is stated as $\vec{OP}$ (position vector) or $\vec{PQ}$ (displacement vector).
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Misinterpreting Collinearity Coefficients

Common Pitfall: Assuming points $\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c}$ are collinear if $\vec{c} = x\vec{a} + y\vec{b}$ without checking the auxiliary condition.

Counterexample: $\vec{c} = 2\vec{a} + 3\vec{b}$ where $2 + 3 = 5 \neq 1$.

Why it fails: The correct affine combination for collinearity requires the sum of scalar coefficients to be exactly unity ($x + y = 1$).

🛡️ Exam Defense: Normalize the coefficients by dividing by their sum before concluding collinearity.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If the position vectors of vertices $A, B, C$ of a triangle are $\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$, $2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + \hat{k}$, and $3\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$ respectively, find the position vector of its centroid.

(A) $2\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$(B) $\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}$(C) $3\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$(D) $\frac{3}{2}\hat{i} + \frac{3}{2}\hat{j} + \frac{3}{2}\hat{k}$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Identify position vectors $\vec{a} = (1,2,3)$, $\vec{b} = (2,3,1)$, $\vec{c} = (3,1,2)$. Step 2: Apply centroid formula $\vec{r}_G = \frac{\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}}{3}$. Step 3: Sum components: $x = \frac{1+2+3}{3} = 2$, $y = \frac{2+3+1}{3} = 2$, $z = \frac{3+1+2}{3} = 2$. Step 4: Result is $2\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Average the $i, j, k$ coefficients component-wise in under 15 seconds.
💡 Key Takeaway: Centroid problems are direct arithmetic checks. Never perform full cross products here.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

For what value of $\lambda$ are the points with position vectors $\hat{i} - \hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$, $2\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$, and $\lambda\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 4\hat{k}$ collinear?

(A) $0$(B) $-1$(C) $1$(D) $2$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Let $\vec{a} = (1,-1,3)$, $\vec{b} = (2,1,2)$, $\vec{c} = (\lambda,-2,4)$. Step 2: Form difference vectors $\vec{AB} = \vec{b}-\vec{a} = (1, 2, -1)$ and $\vec{AC} = \vec{c}-\vec{a} = (\lambda-1, -1, 1)$. Step 3: For collinearity, $\vec{AB} = k\vec{AC}$, so $\frac{1}{\lambda-1} = \frac{2}{-1} = \frac{-1}{1}$. Step 4: From $\frac{2}{-1} = -1$, wait, check ratios: $2/(-1) = -2$, so $\lambda-1 = -1/(-2)$ gives consistency. Solving gives $\lambda = -1$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Set the determinant of the augmented coordinate matrix to zero for collinearity in $\mathbb{R}^3$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always cross-verify ratio of components when equating vector multiples.

Q1
MCQ 1: If the position vector of a point $P$ is $2\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}$, its distance from the origin is:
A (A) $5$
B (B) $7$
C (C) $\sqrt{35}$
D (D) $49$
Derivation & Analysis:

$|\vec{OP}| = \sqrt{2^2 + (-3)^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{4 + 9 + 36} = \sqrt{49} = 7$.

Q2
MCQ 2: The position vectors of points $A$ and $B$ are $3\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + \hat{k}$ and $2\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} - 3\hat{k}$. The displacement vector $\vec{AB}$ is:
A (A) $-\hat{i} + 6\hat{j} - 4\hat{k}$
B (B) $\hat{i} - 6\hat{j} + 4\hat{k}$
C (C) $5\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 2\hat{k}$
D (D) $-5\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\vec{AB} = \vec{B} - \vec{A} = (2-3)\hat{i} + (4-(-2))\hat{j} + (-3-1)\hat{k} = -^\hat{i} + 6\hat{j} - 4\hat{k}$.

Q3
MCQ 3: If points with position vectors $a\hat{i} + b\hat{j} + c\hat{k}$ are collinear with origin, then:
A (A) $a=b=c$
B (B) $a, b, c$ are in arithmetic progression
C (C) Direction ratios are proportional to coordinates
D (D) $a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = 1$
Derivation & Analysis:

A point is collinear with the origin and itself if and only if its position vector is a scalar multiple of a fixed direction vector, meaning its coordinates act as proportional direction ratios.

Q4
MCQ 4: The position vector of the point which divides the join of points with position vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ in the ratio $2:3$ internally is:
A (A) $\frac{3\vec{a} + 2\vec{b}}{5}$
B (B) $\frac{2\vec{a} + 3\vec{b}}{5}$
C (C) $\frac{3\vec{a} - 2\vec{b}}{5}$
D (D) $3\vec{a} + 2\vec{b}$
Derivation & Analysis:

By the internal section formula with $m=2, n=3$, $\vec{r} = \frac{m\vec{b} + n\vec{a}}{m+n} = \frac{2\vec{b} + 3\vec{a}}{2+3} = \frac{3\vec{a} + 2\vec{b}}{5}$.

Q5
MCQ 5: If $\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c}$ are position vectors of vertices of an equilateral triangle with circumcenter at the origin, then:
A (A) $\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c} = 0$
B (B) $\vec{a} = \vec{b} = \vec{c}$
C (C) $\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{c}$
D (D) $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 1$
Derivation & Analysis:

For an equilateral triangle centered at the origin, the centroid coincides with the circumcenter (the origin), hence $\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c} = 3\vec{0} = 0$.

Q6
MCQ 6: The position vector of the midpoint of the line segment joining $P(2, 3, 4)$ and $Q(4, -1, 2)$ is:
A (A) $3\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$
B (B) $6\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}$
C (C) $2\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} - 2\hat{k}$
D (D) $\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + \hat{k}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Midpoint position vector = $\frac{(2+4)\hat{i} + (3-1)\hat{j} + (4+2)\hat{k}}{2} = \frac{6\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}}{2} = 3\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$.

Q7
MCQ 7: If the position vectors of $A, B, C$ are $\hat{i} + \hat{j}, \hat{j} + \hat{k}, \hat{k} + \hat{i}$, the position vector of the centroid of $\triangle ABC$ is:
A (A) $\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}$
B (B) $\frac{2}{3}(\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k})$
C (C) $3(\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k})$
D (D) $\frac{1}{2}(\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k})$
Derivation & Analysis:

Sum of vectors = $(\hat{i}+\hat{j}) + (\hat{j}+\hat{k}) + (\hat{k}+\hat{i}) = 2(\hat{i}+\hat{j}+\hat{k})$. Dividing by 3 gives $\frac{2}{3}(\hat{i}+\hat{j}+\hat{k})$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): Position vectors reduce 3D geometric proofs to algebraic manipulation. Reason (R): Every point in $\mathbb{R}^3$ corresponds to a unique ordered triad acting as its position vector.
A (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (C) (A) is true but (R) is false
D (D) (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

The bijective correspondence between geometric points and ordered triads (Reason) is precisely what enables vector algebraic methods in geometry (Assertion).

Q9
MCQ 9: If position vector $\vec{r}$ satisfies $\vec{r} \times \hat{i} = 0$, then the locus of the point is:
A (A) The x-axis
B (B) The y-axis
C (C) The z-axis
D (D) The yz-plane
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $\vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$. Then $\vec{r} \times \hat{i} = -y\hat{k} + z\hat{j} = 0 \implies y=0$ and $z=0$, which defines the x-axis.

Q10
MCQ 10: The position vector of a point dividing the join of $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ externally in ratio $3:2$ is:
A (A) $3\vec{b} - 2\vec{a}$
B (B) $\frac{3\vec{b} - 2\vec{a}}{3 - 2}$
C (C) $\frac{3\vec{b} + 2\vec{a}}{1}$
D (D) $2\vec{b} - 3\vec{a}$
Derivation & Analysis:

External division formula with $m=3, n=2$: $\vec{r} = \frac{3\vec{b} - 2\vec{a}}{3-2} = 3\vec{b} - 2\vec{a}$.

For what value of $\lambda$ are the points with position vectors $\hat{i} - \hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$, $2\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$, and $\lambda\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 4\hat{k}$ collinear?

(A) $0$(B) $-1$(C) $1$(D) $2$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Let $\vec{a} = (1,-1,3)$, $\vec{b} = (2,1,2)$, $\vec{c} = (\lambda,-2,4)$. Step 2: Form difference vectors $\vec{AB} = \vec{b}-\vec{a} = (1, 2, -1)$ and $\vec{AC} = \vec{c}-\vec{a} = (\lambda-1, -1, 1)$. Step 3: For collinearity, $\vec{AB} = k\vec{AC}$, so $\frac{1}{\lambda-1} = \frac{2}{-1} = \frac{-1}{1}$. Step 4: From $\frac{2}{-1} = -1$, wait, check ratios: $2/(-1) = -2$, so $\lambda-1 = -1/(-2)$ gives consistency. Solving gives $\lambda = -1$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Set the determinant of the augmented coordinate matrix to zero for collinearity in $\mathbb{R}^3$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always cross-verify ratio of components when equating vector multiples.
