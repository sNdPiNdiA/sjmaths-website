Vector Analysis

Modulus of vector

The modulus of a vector represents its length or magnitude, serving as a fundamental metric in Euclidean space. Mastery of vector magnitude, inner product properties, and triangle inequalities is vital for securing high scores in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Norm and Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality for Vectors
For any vectors $\vec{a}, \vec{b} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, the modulus satisfies $|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2}$, and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality holds: $|\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}| \le |\vec{a}| |\vec{b}|$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, the modulus $|\vec{a}|$ represents the Euclidean distance from the origin $(0,0,0)$ to the terminal point $(a_1, a_2, a_3)$. The dot product inequality reflects the cosine of the angle $\theta$ between two vectors bounded in $[-1, 1]$.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Defines a normed vector space structure over the field of real numbers, satisfying positivity, homogeneity, and the triangle inequality $|\vec{a} + \vec{b}| \le |\vec{a}| + |\vec{b}|$

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Square of a Vector Modulus

$$ |\vec{a} \pm \vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{a}|^2 + |\vec{b}|^2 \pm 2(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}) = |\vec{a}|^2 + |\vec{b}|^2 \pm 2|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\cos\theta $$

Exam Rule: Always square vector modulus expressions instead of taking square roots to simplify dot products instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
02

Shortcut 2: Sum of Three Unit Vectors

$$ \text{If } |\vec{a}| = |\vec{b}| = |\vec{c}| = 1 \text{ and } \vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c} = \vec{0}, \text{ then } \vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} + \vec{b}\cdot\vec{c} + \vec{c}\cdot\vec{a} = -\frac{3}{2} $$

Exam Rule: Square the complete vector sum equation directly to expand cross-terms.

⚡ PYQ Context: Repeatedly featured in KVS PGT and UP TGT vector equilibrium problems.
03

Shortcut 3: Lagrange's Identity for Vectors

$$ |\vec{a} \times \vec{b}|^2 + (\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b})^2 = |\vec{a}|^2 |\vec{b}|^2 $$

Exam Rule: Use this identity when cross product magnitude and dot product are given to find individual vector moduli.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard high-weightage question formula in DSSSB and UP PGT.
04

Shortcut 4: Perpendicular Vectors Condition

$$ |\vec{a} + \vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{a}|^2 + |\vec{b}|^2 \iff \vec{a} \perp \vec{b} $$

Exam Rule: If the sum magnitude squared equals the sum of individual squares, the dot product vanishes immediately.

⚡ PYQ Context: Quick sanity check in objective vector geometry problems.
05

Shortcut 5: Maximum and Minimum of Vector Sum Modulus

$$ |\vec{a}| - |\vec{b}| \le |\vec{a} + \vec{b}| \le |\vec{a}| + |\vec{b}| $$

Exam Rule: The maximum occurs at $\theta = 0^\circ$ and minimum at $\theta = 180^\circ$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Asked as direct range-based questions in LT Grade exams.
06

Shortcut 6: Coplanarity / Scalar Triple Product Modulus

$$ |[\vec{a} \, \vec{b} \, \vec{c}]| = \text{Volume of parallelpiped formed by } \vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c} $$

Exam Rule: If volume is zero, vectors are coplanar; use determinant expansion set to zero.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequent in 3D geometry and vector mixed product sections.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Forgetting Absolute Value on Scalars

Common Pitfall: Writing $|k\vec{a}| = k|\vec{a}|$ instead of $|k||\vec{a}|$.

Counterexample: If $k = -2$ and $|\vec{a}| = 3$, then $|-2\vec{a}| = |-2| \times 3 = 6$, not $-6$.

Why it fails: Magnitudes represent lengths and cannot be negative under any algebraic transformation.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always apply modulus bars strictly to scalar multipliers outside vector norms.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Distributing Modulus over Addition

Common Pitfall: Assuming $|\vec{a} + \vec{b}| = |\vec{a}| + |\vec{b}|$ unconditionally.

Counterexample: Take orthogonal unit vectors $\hat{i}$ and $\hat{j}$. $|\hat{i} + \hat{j}| = \sqrt{2}$, whereas $|\hat{i}| + |\hat{j}| = 2$.

Why it fails: Equality holds only when vectors are collinear and in the same direction (positive scalar multiples).

🛡️ Exam Defense: Never split moduli across vector sums; always use squaring ($|\vec{a} + \vec{b}|^2$) for expansions.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $|\vec{a}| = 3$, $|\vec{b}| = 4$, and $|\vec{a} + \vec{b}| = 5$, find the value of $|\vec{a} - \vec{b}|$.

(A) $5$(B) $3$(C) $4$(D) $5\sqrt{2}$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Expand $|\vec{a} + \vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{a}|^2 + |\vec{b}|^2 + 2(\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b})$. Step 2: $25 = 9 + 16 + 2(\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}) \implies \vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = 0$. Step 3: Compute $|\vec{a}-\vec{b}|^2 = 9 + 16 - 0 = 25 \implies |\vec{a}-\vec{b}| = 5$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize that $3^2 + 4^2 = 5^2$ forms a right triangle, meaning vectors are orthogonal. Thus, $|\vec{a}-\vec{b}| = |\vec{a}+\vec{b}| = 5$ instantly.
💡 Key Takeaway: Check for Pythagorean triplets among magnitudes to immediately deduce orthogonality.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If unit vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are inclined at an angle $\theta$ such that $|\vec{a} + \vec{b}| < 1$, then:

(A) $\theta < \frac{\pi}{3}$(B) $\theta > \frac{2\pi}{3}$(C) $\frac{\pi}{3} < \theta < \frac{2\pi}{3}$(D) $\theta < \frac{\pi}{6}$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Square both sides: $|\vec{a}+\vec{b}|^2 < 1$. Step 2: $1 + 1 + 2(1)(1)\cos\theta < 1 \implies 2 + 2\cos\theta < 1$. Step 3: $\cos\theta < -\frac{1}{2} \implies \theta > \frac{2\pi}{3}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Inequality with unit vector sums translates directly to cosine bounds. $\cos\theta < -0.5$ yields obtuse angles greater than $120^\circ$ ($2\pi/3$).
💡 Key Takeaway: Pay strict attention to inequality signs when converting magnitude bounds to angular intervals.

Q1
If $\vec{a}$ is a non-zero vector of magnitude $a$ and $k$ is a non-zero scalar, then $k\vec{a}$ is a unit vector if:
A (A) $k = \frac{1}{a}$
B (B) $k = -a$
C (C) $k = |a|$
D (D) $k = \pm \frac{1}{a}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Condition for unit vector is $|k\vec{a}| = 1 \implies |k||\vec{a}| = 1 \implies |k|a = 1 \implies |k| = \frac{1}{a}$. Taking positive scalar convention gives $k = 1/a$.

Q2
The angle between two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ with magnitudes $3$ and $2$ respectively, having $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 3$, is:
A (A) $\frac{\pi}{6}$
B (B) $\frac{\pi}{3}$
C (C) $\frac{\pi}{2}$
D (D) $\pi$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using $\cos\theta = \frac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|} = \frac{3}{3 \times 2} = \frac{1}{2}$, we get $\theta = \frac{\pi}{3}$.

Q3
If $|\vec{a} + \vec{b}| = |\vec{a} - \vec{b}|$, then the angle between $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ is:
A (A) $0^\circ$
B (B) $45^\circ$
C (C) $90^\circ$
D (D) $180^\circ$
Derivation & Analysis:

Squaring both sides: $|\vec{a}|^2 + |\vec{b}|^2 + 2\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = |\vec{a}|^2 + |\vec{b}|^2 - 2\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} \implies 4\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = 0 \implies \vec{a} \perp \vec{b}$ ($\theta = 90^\circ$).

Q4
If $|\vec{a}| = 2$, $|\vec{b}| = 5$, and $|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}| = 8$, then the value of $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}$ is:
A (A) $3$
B (B) $6$
C (C) $9$
D (D) $10$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using Lagrange's identity: $(\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b})^2 = |\vec{a}|^2|\vec{b}|^2 - |\vec{a}\times\vec{b}|^2 = (2)^2(5)^2 - 8^2 = 100 - 64 = 36 \implies \vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = \pm 6$.

Q5
For any two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$, the maximum value of $|\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}|$ is:
A (A) $|\vec{a}| + |\vec{b}|$
B (B) $|\vec{a}| |\vec{b}|$
C (C) $\frac{1}{2}|\vec{a}| |\vec{b}|$
D (D) $|\vec{a}|^2 + |\vec{b}|^2$
Derivation & Analysis:

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, $|\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}| = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\cos\theta$. Since $|\cos\theta| \le 1$, the maximum value is $|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|$.

Q6
If $\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c}$ are unit vectors such that $\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c} = \vec{0}$, the value of $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} + \vec{b}\cdot\vec{c} + \vec{c}\cdot\vec{a}$ is:
A (A) $0$
B (B) $-\frac{3}{2}$
C (C) $\frac{3}{2}$
D (D) $1$
Derivation & Analysis:

Square the sum: $|\vec{a}+\vec{b}+\vec{c}|^2 = |\vec{a}|^2+|\vec{b}|^2+|\vec{c}|^2 + 2(\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}+\vec{b}\cdot\vec{c}+\vec{c}\cdot\vec{a}) = 0 \implies 3 + 2(\sum \vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}) = 0 \implies \sum \vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = -\frac{3}{2}$.

Q7
The vector projection of $\vec{a}$ on $\vec{b}$ is given by:
A (A) $\frac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|}$
B (B) $\frac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|^2}\vec{b}$
C (C) $\frac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{a}|}\vec{a}$
D (D) $\frac{\vec{a}\times\vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Scalar projection is $\frac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|}$. Vector projection multiplies this scalar by the unit vector in the direction of $\vec{b}$, yielding $\frac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|^2}\vec{b}$.

Q9
What is the modulus of the vector joining points $A(1, 2, -3)$ and $B(-1, -2, 1)$?
A (A) $6$
B (B) $2\sqrt{5}$
C (C) $3\$
D (D) $36$
Derivation & Analysis:

Vector $\vec{AB} = (-1-1)\hat{i} + (-2-2)\hat{j} + (1 - (-3))\hat{k} = -2\hat{i} - 4\hat{j} + 4\hat{k}$. Modulus $= \sqrt{(-2)^2 + (-4)^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{4 + 16 + 16} = \sqrt{36} = 6$.

Q10
If $|\vec{a}| = 2$ and scalar $k$ is such that $|k\vec{a}| = 10$, then the possible values of $k$ are:
A (A) $\pm 5$
B (B) $5$
C (C) $\pm 2$
D (D) $\pm 10$
Derivation & Analysis:

$|k||\vec{a}| = 10 \implies |k|(2) = 10 \implies |k| = 5 \implies k = \pm 5$.
