Vector Analysis

Composition of Force Vectors

The composition of force vectors involves combining concurrent or non-concurrent forces into a single resultant vector using vector addition laws, forming a cornerstone of analytical statics and dynamics. Mastering vector addition, parallelogram laws, and Lami's theorem is crucial for securing high scores in competitive examinations like UP TGT/PGT, DSSSB, and KVS Mathematics.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Parallelogram Law of Forces & Resultant Magnitude
If two forces $\vec{P}$ and $\vec{Q}$ acting simultaneously at a point are represented in magnitude and direction by two adjacent sides of a parallelogram drawn from a point, their resultant $\vec{R}$ is represented in magnitude and direction by the diagonal of the parallelogram passing through that point. Mathematically, $R = \sqrt{P^2 + Q^2 + 2PQ \cos\theta}$, where $\theta$ is the angle between $\vec{P}$ and $\vec{Q}$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, vector composition forms a closed triangle or parallelogram where the sides denote vector magnitudes and orientations, and the diagonal represents the net geometrical displacement or force effect in a 3D Euclidean space.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, it translates vector addition into scalar components via dot products, obeying commutative, associative, and distributive laws in vector spaces over $\mathbb{R}$.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Point of Concurrency Single point intersection Strictly Mandatory / Necessary If forces are non-concurrent and non-coplanar, they produce a net torque in addition to a resultant force, reducing the system to a wrench rather than a simple vector sum.
Coplanarity of Vectors Coplanar plane $\mathbb{R}^2$ Mandatory for simple algebraic angle formulas For non-coplanar force systems, components must be resolved along three mutually orthogonal spatial axes ($x, y, z$) utilizing direction cosines.
Linearity of Force Superposition Continuous domain Essential for resultant calculation In non-linear physical regimes, the principle of superposition fails, making simple vector addition invalid.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Equal Forces Resultant

$$R = 2P \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)$$

Exam Rule: When two forces of equal magnitude $P$ act at an angle $\theta$, their resultant bisects the angle between them with this exact magnitude.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT and TGT examinations for quick calculation without expanding full cosines.
02

Shortcut 2: Right-Angled Force Vectors

$$R = \sqrt{P^2 + Q^2}, \quad \tan\alpha = \frac{Q}{P}$$

Exam Rule: When forces are orthogonal ($\theta = 90^\circ$), eliminate the $2PQ \cos\theta$ term instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in KVS/NVS vector mechanics problems.
03

Shortcut 3: Lami's Theorem for Three Concurrent Forces

$$\frac{P}{\sin \alpha} = \frac{Q}{\sin \beta} = \frac{R}{\sin \gamma}$$

Exam Rule: If three coplanar forces are in equilibrium, each force is proportional to the sine of the angle between the other two forces.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard 30-second question format in DSSSB and UP PGT Statics.
04

Shortcut 4: Maximum and Minimum Resultant Bounds

$$P - Q \le R \le P + Q$$

Exam Rule: Maximum resultant occurs at $\theta = 0^\circ$ ($R_{max} = P + Q$) and minimum at $\theta = 180^\circ$ ($R_{min} = |P - Q|$).

⚡ PYQ Context: Used to rule out impossible options in multiple-choice questions.
05

Shortcut 5: Direction of Resultant with Equal Forces

$$\alpha = \frac{\theta}{2}$$

Exam Rule: If $P = Q$, the resultant force bisects the angle $\theta$ between them symmetrically.

⚡ PYQ Context: Enables instant elimination of asymmetrical direction options in exams.
06

Shortcut 6: Resolution into Unit Vectors

$$\vec{R} = \sum F_x \hat{i} + \sum F_y \hat{j} + \sum F_z \hat{k}$$

Exam Rule: For more than two forces, resolve all vectors into orthogonal components and sum them algebraically.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard approach for 3D vector composition problems in LT Grade exams.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Angle Between Forces with Angle of Resultant

Common Pitfall: Candidates substitute the angle of the resultant ($\alpha$) into the resultant magnitude formula instead of the angle between the two forces ($\theta$).

Counterexample: Given $P, Q$ and angle with $P$ as $\alpha$, using $\alpha$ in $R = \sqrt{P^2 + Q^2 + 2PQ \cos\alpha}$.

Why it fails: The formula requires the interior angle $\theta$ between the vector lines of action, not the inclination $\alpha$ of the resultant.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always verify whether the given angle is between the two vectors or between a vector and the resultant.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Equilibrium vs Resultant Magnitude Sign Errors

Common Pitfall: Ignoring the vector direction or assuming vector subtraction is commutative like scalar subtraction.

Counterexample: Assuming $R_{min} = Q - P$ when $P > Q$ without taking absolute value.

Why it fails: Magnitude must be strictly non-negative; failing to use absolute value leads to negative force magnitudes.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always wrap magnitude differences in absolute value bars: $|P - Q|$.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Two forces acting at an angle of $60^\circ$ have a resultant of magnitude $\sqrt{19}$ N. If one force is $3$ N, find the magnitude of the other force.

(A) $2$ N(B) $5$ N(C) $3$ N(D) $4$ N
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write formula $R^2 = P^2 + Q^2 + 2PQ \cos\theta$. Step 2: Substitute $R = \sqrt{19}$, $P = 3$, and $\theta = 60^\circ$. Step 3: Solve quadratic equation $19 = 9 + Q^2 + 2(3)Q(1/2) \implies Q^2 + 3Q - 10 = 0$. Step 4: Factorize $(Q+5)(Q-2) = 0 \implies Q = 2$ or discard negative, giving $5$ N.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Test options directly: For $Q = 5$, $3^2 + 5^2 + 2(3)(5)(1/2) = 9 + 25 + 15 = 49$, wait: $9 + 25 + 15 = 49$, $\sqrt{49} = 7$. Let's check $Q = 3$: $9+9+9 = 27$. Let's test $Q = 5$ with proper calculation: $19 = 9 + Q^2 + 3Q \implies Q^2 + 3Q - 10 = 0 \implies Q = 2$. Option (A) is correct.
💡 Key Takeaway: Be careful with quadratic factorization roots; always check domain bounds.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

The maximum and minimum resultant of two forces are in ratio $3:1$. Find the ratio of the magnitudes of the two forces.

(A) $2:1$(B) $3:1$(C) $1:1$(D) $4:1$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Set up equations $\frac{P+Q}{P-Q} = \frac{3}{1}$. Step 2: Cross multiply $P + Q = 3P - 3Q$. Step 3: Rearrange to $4Q = 2P \implies \frac{P}{Q} = \frac{2}{1}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Apply componendo and dividendo instantly: $\frac{(P+Q)+(P-Q)}{(P+Q)-(P-Q)} = \frac{3+1}{3-1} \implies \frac{2P}{2Q} = \frac{4}{2} = 2 \implies P:Q = 2:1$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Componendo-dividendo saves 40 seconds on ratio problems.

MCQ 1: If the magnitude of the resultant of two equal forces of magnitude $P$ is equal to $P$, the angle between them is:
A (A) $60^\circ$
B (B) $90^\circ$
C (C) $120^\circ$
D (D) $180^\circ$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using $R = 2P \cos(\theta/2)$, we get $P = 2P \cos(\theta/2) \implies \cos(\theta/2) = 1/2 \implies \theta/2 = 60^\circ \implies \theta = 120^\circ$.

Q2
MCQ 2: Two forces $3\vec{i} + 4\vec{j}$ and $4\vec{i} - 3\vec{j}$ act concurrently. The magnitude of their resultant force is:
A (A) $5$
B (B) $7$
C (C) $5\sqrt{2}$
D (D) $10$
Derivation & Analysis:

Resultant vector $\vec{R} = (3+4)\vec{i} + (4-3)\vec{j} = 7\vec{i} + \vec{j}$. Magnitude $= \sqrt{7^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$.

Q3
MCQ 3: If three forces acting in equilibrium are represented by sides of a triangle taken in order, they are:
A (A) Coplanar and concurrent
B (B) Parallel
C (C) Non-coplanar
D (D) Perpendicular to each other
Derivation & Analysis:

By triangle law of equilibrium, three forces forming a closed triangle are coplanar and concurrent at a point.

Q4
MCQ 4: The maximum resultant of two forces is $30$ N and minimum resultant is $10$ N. The forces are:
A (A) $20\text{ N}, 10\text{ N}$
B (B) $25\text{ N}, 5\text{ N}$
C (C) $15\text{ N}, 15\text{ N}$
D (D) $22\text{ N}, 8\text{ N}$
Derivation & Analysis:

$P + Q = 30$ and $P - Q = 10$. Solving gives $2P = 40 \implies P = 20$ N, $Q = 10$ N.

Q5
MCQ 5: If the angle between two forces $P$ and $Q$ is $90^\circ$, their resultant is:
A (A) $P + Q$
B (B) $P - Q$
C (C) $\sqrt{P^2 + Q^2}$
D (D) $\sqrt{P^2 - Q^2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

When $\theta = 90^\circ$, $\cos(90^\circ) = 0$, so $R = \sqrt{P^2 + Q^2 + 2PQ(0)} = \sqrt{P^2 + Q^2}$.

Q6
MCQ 6: Lami's theorem is applicable when how many forces are in equilibrium?
A (A) Two
B (B) Three
C (C) Four
D D) Any number
Derivation & Analysis:

Lami's theorem specifically applies to exactly three concurrent, coplanar forces in equilibrium.

Q7
MCQ 7: If vectors $\vec{A} = 2\hat{i} + \lambda\hat{j} + \hat{k}$ and $\vec{B} = 3\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 4\hat{k}$ are perpendicular, then $\lambda$ is:
A (A) $5$
B (B) $3$
C (C) $-5$
D (D) $10$
Derivation & Analysis:

For perpendicular vectors, dot product is zero: $(2)(3) + (lambda)(-2) + (1)(4) = 0 \implies 6 - 2\lambda + 4 = 0 \implies 2\lambda = 10 \implies \lambda = 5$.

Q8
MCQ 8: The angle between two forces of equal magnitude $P$ when their resultant is also of magnitude $P$ is:
A (A) $0^\circ$
B (B) $60^\circ$
C (C) $90^\circ$
D (D) $120^\circ$
Derivation & Analysis:

Derived from $R = 2P \cos(\theta/2) \implies P = 2P \cos(\theta/2) \implies \theta = 120^\circ$.

Q9
MCQ 9: What is the component of force $\vec{F} = 4\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 5\hat{k}$ along the direction of vector $\hat{i} + \hat{j}$?
A (A) $7\sqrt{2}$
B (B) $\frac{7}{\sqrt{2}}$
C (C) $7$
D (D) $\frac{7}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Scalar projection of $\vec{F}$ along unit vector $\hat{u} = \frac{\hat{i}+\hat{j}}{\sqrt{2}}$ is $\vec{F} \cdot \hat{u} = \frac{4+3}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{7}{\sqrt{2}}$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If the resultant of two forces $P$ and $Q$ is perpendicular to $P$, then:
A (A) $P + Q \cos\theta = 0$
B (B) $P + Q \sin\theta = 0$
C (C) $Q + P \cos\theta = 0$
D (D) $P \tan\theta = Q$
Derivation & Analysis:

The angle $\alpha$ made with $P$ satisfies $\tan\alpha = \frac{Q \sin\theta}{P + Q \cos\theta}$. Since $\alpha = 90^\circ$, $\cot\alpha = 0$, meaning denominator $P + Q \cos\theta = 0$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Vector addition requires vectors to be placed head-to-tail or tail-to-tail. The geometric angle $\theta$ between their lines of action when placed tail-to-tail defines the opening angle for the parallelogram law derivation using the law of cosines.

Candidates often confuse trigonometric identities (like using sine instead of cosine in the magnitude formula) or misinterpret the angle given in statement problems (e.g., angle with resultant vs angle between forces).

On average, 3 to 5 questions directly or indirectly test vector composition, Lami's theorem, and equilibrium of forces in every UP PGT/TGT examination cycle.

Memorize standard symmetric force configurations (like equal forces at $120^\circ$ giving resultant equal to $P$) and utilize component resolution ($\sum F_x$ and $\sum F_y$) to bypass lengthy trigonometric cosine rule expansions.

Scalar projection of $\vec{F}$ along unit vector $\hat{u} = \frac{\hat{i}+\hat{j}}{\sqrt{2}}$ is $\vec{F} \cdot \hat{u} = \frac{4+3}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{7}{\sqrt{2}}$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If the resultant of two forces $P$ and $Q$ is perpendicular to $P$, then:
A (A) $P + Q \cos\theta = 0$
B (B) $P + Q \sin\theta = 0$
C (C) $Q + P \cos\theta = 0$
D (D) $P \tan\theta = Q$
Derivation & Analysis:

The angle $\alpha$ made with $P$ satisfies $\tan\alpha = \frac{Q \sin\theta}{P + Q \cos\theta}$. Since $\alpha = 90^\circ$, $\cot\alpha = 0$, meaning denominator $P + Q \cos\theta = 0$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Vector addition requires vectors to be placed head-to-tail or tail-to-tail. The geometric angle $\theta$ between their lines of action when placed tail-to-tail defines the opening angle for the parallelogram law derivation using the law of cosines.

Candidates often confuse trigonometric identities (like using sine instead of cosine in the magnitude formula) or misinterpret the angle given in statement problems (e.g., angle with resultant vs angle between forces).

On average, 3 to 5 questions directly or indirectly test vector composition, Lami's theorem, and equilibrium of forces in every UP PGT/TGT examination cycle.

Memorize standard symmetric force configurations (like equal forces at $120^\circ$ giving resultant equal to $P$) and utilize component resolution ($\sum F_x$ and $\sum F_y$) to bypass lengthy trigonometric cosine rule expansions.
