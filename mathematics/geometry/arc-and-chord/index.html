Arc and chord of circle

The geometry of arcs and chords forms the foundational backbone of Euclidean circle theorems, frequently yielding high-scoring numerical and conceptual problems in UP TGT/PGT, DSSSB, and KVS examinations. Mastery of perpendicular bisectors, subtended angles, cyclic properties, and length relationships is essential for solving complex multi-circle configurations in under a minute.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Perpendicular Bisector and Equal Chords Theorem
The perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord; conversely, the line drawn through the centre of a circle to bisect a chord is perpendicular to the chord. Furthermore, equal chords are equidistant from the centre and subtend equal angles at the centre.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

A chord divides a circle into two arcs (major and minor). Drawing a perpendicular from the centre creates two congruent right-angled triangles via the Radius-Hypotenuse-Side (RHS) congruence criterion, visually establishing the bisecting property.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Translates Euclidean geometric constraints into coordinate geometry equations using distance formulas, circle equations $(x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2$, and line-intersection parameters.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Chord Length from Radius and Perpendicular Distance

$$ L = 2 \sqrt{r^2 - d^2} $$

Exam Rule: Given radius $r$ and perpendicular distance $d$ from the centre to the chord, directly compute the chord length $L$ using Pythagoras theorem.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP TGT/PGT where numerical values for $r$ and $d$ are given to find the length of the chord.
02

Shortcut 2: Intersecting Chords Theorem (Internal & External)

$$ PA \cdot PB = PC \cdot PD $$

Exam Rule: If two chords $AB$ and $CD$ intersect inside or outside a circle at point $P$, the products of their segment lengths are equal.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in DSSSB PGT and KVS exams for quick calculation of unknown segment lengths.
03

Shortcut 3: Perpendicular Distance Between Parallel Chords

$$ d = \sqrt{r^2 - \left(\frac{L_1}{2}\right)^2} \pm \sqrt{r^2 - \left(\frac{L_2}{2}\right)^2} $$

Exam Rule: For parallel chords on the same side of the centre, use the difference of distances; for opposite sides, use the sum.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard high-weightage question type in UP PGT Mathematics.
04

Shortcut 4: Angle Subtended by Arc at Centre vs Circumference

$$ \theta_{\text{centre}} = 2 \theta_{\text{circumference}} $$

Exam Rule: The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is double the angle subtended by the same arc at any point on the remaining circumference.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used in combination with cyclic quadrilateral properties in geometry problem-solving.
05

Shortcut 5: Length of Common Chord of Two Intersecting Circles

$$ L = \frac{2}{d} \sqrt{s(s-r_1)(s-r_2)(s-d)} $$

Exam Rule: Using Heron's formula for the triangle formed by centres and intersection points, where $s = \frac{r_1 + r_2 + d}{2}$ and $d$ is distance between centres.

⚡ PYQ Context: Advanced coordinate geometry and circle combination problems in LT Grade and UP PGT.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Triangle Inequality

$$ 0 < L \le 2r $$

Exam Rule: Instantly eliminate options where chord length exceeds the diameter or where segment sums violate triangle inequalities.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves crucial time in 125-question examination formats.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Same Side vs Opposite Side of Centre for Parallel Chords

Common Pitfall: Candidates always add or always subtract the perpendicular distances of parallel chords without checking if they lie on the same side or opposite sides of the centre.

Counterexample: Chords of lengths $6$ cm and $8$ cm in a circle of radius $5$ cm.

Why it fails: Distances are $4$ cm and $3$ cm. Distance between them is $4 - 3 = 1$ cm (same side) or $4 + 3 = 7$ cm (opposite sides). Choosing the wrong configuration leads to a wrong option.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Carefully read whether chords are on 'the same side' or 'opposite sides' of the centre.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Assuming Any Line Through Centre Bisects a Chord

Common Pitfall: Assuming a diameter passing through a chord always bisects it regardless of perpendicularity.

Counterexample: A non-perpendicular secant line passing through the centre.

Why it fails: Only the perpendicular dropped from the centre bisects the chord.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Verify the $90^\circ$ orthogonality condition before applying bisection formulas.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the length of a chord which is at a distance of $4$ cm from the centre of a circle of radius $5$ cm.

(A) $6$ cm(B) $8$ cm(C) $10$ cm(D) $3$ cm
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Identify $r = 5$, $d = 4$. Step 2: Use half-chord relation $x = \sqrt{r^2 - d^2} = \sqrt{5^2 - 4^2} = \sqrt{9} = 3$. Step 3: Total chord length $L = 2x = 6$? Wait, $\sqrt{25-16} = 3$, so $2(3) = 6$. Let's check options: (A) $6$ cm.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Pythagorean triple $(3, 4, 5)$. Half chord is $3$, full chord is $2 \times 3 = 6$ cm. Option (A) is correct.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always remember to multiply the half-chord length by $2$.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Two parallel chords of a circle of radius $10$ cm are of lengths $16$ cm and $12$ cm and lie on the same side of the centre. The distance between them is:

(A) $2$ cm(B) $4$ cm(C) $6$ cm(D) $14$ cm
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Distance of first chord $d_1 = \sqrt{10^2 - 8^2} = 6$ cm. Step 2: Distance of second chord $d_2 = \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = 8$ cm. Step 3: Distance between them on the same side = $|d_2 - d_1| = |8 - 6| = 2$ cm.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Use Pythagorean triples $(6, 8, 10)$. Distances from centre are $6$ and $8$. Difference is $2$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Check same side vs opposite side carefully. Same side means difference; opposite side means sum ($6 + 8 = 14$).

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
MCQ 1: If the diameter of a circle is $26$ cm and the length of a chord is $24$ cm, then the distance of the chord from the centre is:
A (A) $5$ cm
B (B) $10$ cm
C (C) $12$ cm
D (D) $13$ cm
Derivation & Analysis:

Radius $r = 13$ cm, half chord $x = 12$ cm. Distance $d = \sqrt{r^2 - x^2} = \sqrt{13^2 - 12^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$ cm. Correct option is (A).

Q2
MCQ 2: The length of a chord of a circle of radius $10$ cm subtending an angle of $90^\circ$ at the centre is:
A (A) $5\sqrt{2}$ cm
B (B) $10\sqrt{2}$ cm
C (C) $10$ cm
D (D) $20$ cm
Derivation & Analysis:

In an isosceles triangle with sides $r, r$ and angle $90^\circ$, the hypotenuse (chord length) is $r\sqrt{2} = 10\sqrt{2}$ cm. Correct option is (B).

Q3
MCQ 3: If two equal chords of a circle intersect within the circle, then the segments of one chord are respectively equal to:
A (A) the segments of the other chord
B (B) twice the segments of the other chord
C (C) half the segments of the other chord
D (D) none of these
Derivation & Analysis:

By symmetry and congruence of triangles formed by joining the centre to the intersection point and chord ends, the corresponding segments are equal.

Q4
MCQ 4: The perpendicular distance from the centre of a circle to a chord of length $16$ cm in a circle of radius $10$ cm is:
A (A) $4$ cm
B (B) $6$ cm
C (C) $8$ cm
D (D) $3$ cm
Derivation & Analysis:

$d = \sqrt{10^2 - 8^2} = \sqrt{100 - 64} = \sqrt{36} = 6$ cm. Correct option is (B).

Q5
MCQ 5: What is the maximum possible length of a chord in a circle of radius $r$?
A (A) $r$
B (B) $\sqrt{2}r$
C (C) $2r$
D (D) $\pi r$
Derivation & Analysis:

The longest chord of a circle is its diameter, which equals $2r$. Correct option is (C).

Q6
MCQ 6: If chords $AB$ and $CD$ of a circle intersect at right angles at point $P$, and $PA = 4$, $PB = 6$, $PC = 3$, then $PD$ is equal to:
A (A) $8$
B (B) $6$
C (C) $4$
D (D) $2$
Derivation & Analysis:

By intersecting chords theorem, $PA \cdot PB = PC \cdot PD \implies 4 \cdot 6 = 3 \cdot PD \implies PD = 24 / 3 = 8$. Correct option is (A).

Q7
MCQ 7: The angle subtended by a minor arc at the centre is $120^\circ$. What is the angle subtended by the corresponding major arc at the centre?
A (A) $120^\circ$
B (B) $240^\circ$
C (C) $180^\circ$
D (D) $60^\circ$
Derivation & Analysis:

The total angle around the centre is $360^\circ$. Major arc angle = $360^\circ - 120^\circ = 240^\circ$. Correct option is (B).

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre. Reason (R): Triangles formed by radii and chords are congruent by SSS criterion.
A (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (C) (A) is true but (R) is false
D (D) (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

Both statements are mathematically rigorous and (R) correctly invokes SSS congruence ($r = r, r = r, L_1 = L_2$).

Q9
MCQ 9: If the distance of a chord from the centre is zero, the chord is:
A (A) a tangent
B (B) a diameter
C (C) a point
D (D) impossible
Derivation & Analysis:

A chord passing through the centre has zero perpendicular distance and is the diameter of the circle.

Q10
MCQ 10: Two parallel chords of length $24$ cm and $10$ cm are on opposite sides of the centre of a circle of radius $13$ cm. The distance between them is:
A (A) $5$ cm
B (B) $12$ cm
C (C) $17$ cm
D (D) $7$ cm
Derivation & Analysis:

Distances from centre are $d_1 = \sqrt{13^2 - 12^2} = 5$ cm and $d_2 = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = 12$ cm. Since they are on opposite sides, total distance = $5 + 12 = 17$ cm. Correct option is (C).
