Segments of chord and formed rectangle

The intersecting chords theorem and its associated rectangle properties form a cornerstone of Euclidean circle geometry, establishing a powerful algebraic product relationship between chord segments. Mastery of this concept allows candidates to swiftly solve intricate geometric configuration problems frequently featured in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Intersecting Chords Theorem (Theorem of Chords / Rectangle Formed by Chord Segments)
If two chords $AB$ and $CD$ of a circle intersect at a point $P$ (either internally inside the circle or externally when extended outside), then the rectangle contained by the segments of one chord is equal in area to the rectangle contained by the segments of the other chord. That is, $$PA \cdot PB = PC \cdot PD$$

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, when two chords intersect, they divide each other into two directed line segments. Constructing rectangles with adjacent sides equal to the respective segments of each chord yields two rectangles of identical area. This is deeply tied to the concept of power of a point with respect to a circle and cyclic quadrilaterals formed by joining the endpoints.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, it establishes a quadratic invariant relationship among the segment lengths. If $P$ divides chord $AB$ into segments of length $x$ and $y$, and chord $CD$ into segments of length $u$ and $v$, then $xy = uv$. This proportionality allows the determination of unknown segment lengths using simple algebraic equations.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Internal Intersection Product Rule

$$AP \cdot PB = CP \cdot PD$$

Exam Rule: If point $P$ lies inside the circle on chords $AB$ and $CD$, simply equate the product of the two parts of the first chord to the product of the two parts of the second chord.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT geometry numerical problems to find an unknown segment length.
02

Shortcut 2: External Secant-Chord (Tangent-Secant) Rule

$$PT^2 = PA \cdot PB$$

Exam Rule: If a tangent segment $PT$ and a secant $PAB$ are drawn from an external point $P$, the square of the tangent length equals the product of the external secant segment and the entire secant length.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequent staple in TGT/PGT exams combining circle geometry and quadratic equations.
03

Shortcut 3: Perpendicular Chord from Center

$$AM = MB = \frac{1}{2}AB = \sqrt{R^2 - d^2}$$

Exam Rule: Perpendicular from the center bisects the chord. Use right triangle formed by radius $R$, distance from center $d$, and half-chord $AM$ to setup quick algebraic rectangles.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard high-yield shortcut for finding circle radius or chord lengths.
04

Shortcut 4: Power of a Point Formula

$$PA \cdot PB = |d^2 - R^2$$

Exam Rule: The power of a point $P$ at distance $d$ from the center of a circle of radius $R$ is constant for any chord passing through $P$, given by $d^2 - R^2$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used to eliminate lengthy chord calculations in advanced geometry questions.
05

Shortcut 5: Equal Chords Equidistant from Center

$$AB = CD \iff d_1 = d_2$$

Exam Rule: Chords of equal length are equidistant from the center; hence their segment rectangles are symmetric.

⚡ PYQ Context: Enables rapid option elimination in structural geometry questions.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Ratio & Area Elimination

$$ \frac{Area(\triangle APC)}{Area(\triangle DPB)} = \frac{AP \cdot CP}{DP \cdot BP} $$

Exam Rule: Use similar triangles $\triangle APC \sim \triangle DPB$ (by subtended angles in same segment) to establish rapid side ratios without full vector/coordinate geometry.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves 60+ seconds in multiple-choice assertion-reason questions.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Internal vs. External Intersection Segments

Common Pitfall: Candidates use the entire chord length instead of the specific segment from the intersection point $P$ to the circle boundary.

Counterexample: For chord $AB$ divided into $AP$ and $PB$, using $AB$ instead of $AP$ or $PB$.

Why it fails: The theorem strictly states that the product of the parts into which the chord is divided by the intersection point are equal, not the whole chords.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always measure segments starting from the intersection point $P$ to the endpoints $A, B, C, D$.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Misapplying External Secant Lengths

Common Pitfall: In external intersection ($P$ outside), taking the product of external part and internal part instead of external part and total secant length.

Counterexample: $PA \cdot AB$ instead of $PA \cdot PB$ where $PB$ is the total length from $P$ to the far end $B$.

Why it fails: The secant theorem involves the external segment and the *entire* secant line segment.

🛡️ Exam Defense: For external points, remember: (External segment) $\times$ (Total secant) = constant.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Two chords $AB$ and $CD$ of a circle intersect internally at a point $P$. If $PA = 4\text{ cm}$, $PB = 6\text{ cm}$, and $PC = 3\text{ cm}$, find the length of $PD$.

(A) $5\text{ cm}$(B) $8\text{ cm}$(C) $6\text{ cm}$(D) $10\text{ cm}$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Apply the internal intersecting chords theorem: $PA \cdot PB = PC \cdot PD$. Step 2: Substitute given values: $4 \times 6 = 3 \times PD$. Step 3: Solve for $PD$: $24 = 3 \cdot PD \implies PD = 8\text{ cm}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Mental math: $4 \times 6 = 24$, divide by $3$ gives $8$. Done in 5 seconds.
💡 Key Takeaway: Verify that $P$ is indeed internal by checking that segment lengths sum to less than diameter if needed, but direct formula applies.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

From an external point $P$, a secant $PAB$ is drawn such that $PA = 4\text{ cm}$ and $AB = 5\text{ cm}$. If a tangent $PT$ is drawn from $P$ to the same circle, find the length of $PT$.

(A) $4\text{ cm}$(B) $5\text{ cm}$(C) $6\text{ cm}$(D) $3\text{ cm}$
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Identify total secant length $PB = PA + AB = 4 + 5 = 9\text{ cm}$. Step 2: Apply tangent-secant theorem: $PT^2 = PA \cdot PB$. Step 3: Substitute values: $PT^2 = 4 \times 9 = 36$. Step 4: Take square root: $PT = 6\text{ cm}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Note total secant $PB = 4+5 = 9$. Then $PT = \sqrt{4 \times 9} = \sqrt{36} = 6$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Examiners frequently trap students by giving $AB = 5$ instead of total $PB = 5$. Always compute total secant length first!

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
MCQ 1: If two chords $AB$ and $CD$ intersect at right angles inside a circle, and the segments of $AB$ are $3$ and $4$, while one segment of $CD$ is $2$, what is the length of the other segment of $CD$?
A $5$
B $6$
C $4$
D $3$
Derivation & Analysis:

By intersecting chords theorem, $3 \times 4 = 2 \times x \implies 12 = 2x \implies x = 6$.

Q2
MCQ 2: A chord $AB$ of length $16\text{ cm}$ is at a distance of $6\text{ cm}$ from the center of the circle. What is the radius of the circle?
A $8\text{ cm}$
B $10\text{ cm}$
C $12\text{ cm}$
D $14\text{ cm}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Perpendicular from center bisects chord, so half-chord $AM = \frac{16}{2} = 8$. Radius $R = \sqrt{AM^2 + d^2} = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{64 + 36} = \sqrt{100} = 10\text{ cm}$.

Q3
MCQ 3: If a point $P$ is at distance $d$ from the center of a circle of radius $R$, the power of the point $P$ with respect to the circle when $P$ is inside is:
A $R^2 - d^2$
B $d^2 - R^2$
C $R^2 + d^2$
D \frac{R^2}{d^2}
Derivation & Analysis:

For an interior point, the power of the point is defined as $R^2 - d^2$, which represents the product of the chord segments passing through $P$.

Q4
MCQ 4: Two intersecting chords $AB$ and $CD$ meet at $P$ inside a circle. If $AP : PB = 2 : 3$ and $CP : PD = 1 : 6$, and $AB = 10$, find $CD$.
A $14$
B $21$
C $28$
D $35$
Derivation & Analysis:

$AP = 4, PB = 6$. Product $AP \cdot PB = 24$. Thus $CP \cdot PD = 24$. Since $CP:PD = 1:6$, let $CP = x, PD = 6x$, so $6x^2 = 24 \implies x^2 = 4 \implies x = 2$. Then $CD = x + 6x = 7x = 7(2) = 14$ ... wait, $CP=2, PD=12$, so $CD = 2 + 12 = 14$. Let's check options: Option A is 14.

Q5
MCQ 5: If the radius of a circle is $13\text{ cm}$ and the length of a chord is $24\text{ cm}$, the distance of the chord from the center is:
A $5\text{ cm}$
B $7\text{ cm}$
C $12\text{ cm}$
D $6\text{ cm}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Half chord $AM = 12$. Distance $d = \sqrt{R^2 - AM^2} = \sqrt{13^2 - 12^2} = \sqrt{169 - 144} = \sqrt{25} = 5\text{ cm}$.

Q6
MCQ 6: In a circle, if two equal chords intersect, the segments of one chord are respectively equal to:
A The segments of the other chord in reverse or direct correspondence
B Half of the chord length
C Double the chord length
D Zero
Derivation & Analysis:

By symmetry, equal chords intersecting at the same angle have corresponding segments equal.

Q7
MCQ 7: A tangent $PT$ of length $12\text{ cm}$ is drawn from an external point $P$ to a circle. If a secant $PAB$ passes through the center or is at distance such that external segment $PA = 8\text{ cm}$, what is the total secant length $PB$?
A $12\text{ cm}$
B $16\text{ cm}$
C $18\text{ cm}$
D $20\text{ cm}$
Derivation & Analysis:

$PT^2 = PA \cdot PB \implies 12^2 = 8 \cdot PB \implies 144 = 8 \cdot PB \implies PB = \frac{144}{8} = 18\text{ cm}$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): The rectangle formed by the segments of two intersecting chords of a circle are equal in area. Reason (R): Triangles formed by joining the endpoints of the chords are similar.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

Similarity of triangles $\triangle APC \sim \triangle DPB$ gives $\frac{AP}{DP} = \frac{PC}{PB} \implies AP \cdot PB = PC \cdot DP$, proving both assertion and reason.

Q9
MCQ 9: If chord $AB$ and chord $CD$ intersect at $90^\circ$ at an interior point $P$, and $AP=2, PB=8, CP=4$, then $PD$ equals:
A $2$
B $4$
C $6$
D $8$
Derivation & Analysis:

$AP \cdot PB = CP \cdot PD \implies 2 \times 8 = 4 \times PD \implies 16 = 4 \cdot PD \implies PD = 4$.

Q10
MCQ 10: Which of the following conditions is strictly necessary for the intersecting chords theorem $PA \cdot PB = PC \cdot PD$ to hold?
A The chords must be equal in length
B The four endpoints must be concyclic
C The intersection point must be the center of the circle
D The chords must be perpendicular
Derivation & Analysis:

Concyclicity of the four endpoints $A, B, C, D$ on a single circle is the fundamental geometric hypothesis required for the theorem.
