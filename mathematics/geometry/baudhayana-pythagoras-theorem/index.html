Baudhayana-Pythagoras theorem and its expansion

The Baudhayana-Pythagoras theorem establishes the fundamental metric relation $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ in right-angled triangles, originating historically in the Sulba Sutras.

Definition and statement

Baudhayana-Pythagoras Theorem & Generalized Cosine Law
In any right-angled triangle with legs of lengths $a$ and $b$ and hypotenuse of length $c$, the area of the square constructed on the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the areas of the squares constructed on the legs: $$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$. Furthermore, in any triangle with sides $a, b, c$ and angle $\theta$ opposite to $c$, $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab \cos\theta$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, the theorem equates the area of the square erected upon the longest side of a right triangle to the combined areas of the squares upon the two perpendicular sides. In inner product spaces, it manifests as the orthogonality condition where vectors $u$ and $v$ satisfy $\|u + v\|^2 = \|u\|^2 + \|v\|^2 \iff \langle u, v \rangle = 0$.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, it provides the foundational distance formula in Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^n$: $d(P, Q) = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - y_i)^2}$. It connects quadratic forms, norm topologies, and Pythagorean triples $(x, y, z)$ satisfying $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$.

Useful results and shortcuts

Use these identities and results when their conditions apply:

01

Pythagorean Triple Generation

$$a = m^2 - n^2, \quad b = 2mn, \quad c = m^2 + n^2$$

Rule: For any integers $m > n > 0$, primitive and non-primitive Pythagorean triples are generated instantly without trial-and-error.

Application: Frequently used in UP PGT to find missing side lengths or perimeter of right triangles given one side.
02

Altitude to Hypotenuse Proportions

$$h^2 = xy, \quad a^2 = xc, \quad b^2 = yc$$

Rule: If $h$ is the altitude to hypotenuse $c$, dividing it into segments $x$ and $y$, use geometric mean relations directly.

03

Diagonal of 3D Cuboid (Extension)

$$d = \sqrt{l^2 + w^2 + h^2}$$

Rule: Successive application of the Pythagorean theorem in three orthogonal dimensions yields the space diagonal instantly.

Application: Directly tested in KVS/NVS PGT vector and 3D coordinate geometry.
04

Apollonius's Theorem (Median Relation)

$$b^2 + c^2 = 2\left(m_a^2 + \left(\frac{a}{2}\right)^2\right)$$

Rule: Relates the lengths of the sides of a triangle to the length of its median, avoiding trigonometric expansion.

Application: Asked in DSSSB PGT Mathematics to find median lengths.
05

Equilateral Triangle on Sides Area Relation

$$A_{\text{hypotenuse}} = A_1 + A_2$$

Rule: If similar geometric figures (squares, semicircles, equilateral triangles) are constructed on the three sides of a right triangle, the area on the hypotenuse equals the sum of areas on the legs.

Application: Conceptual property questions in UP TGT.
06

Coordinate Distance Minimization

$$d(P_1, P_2) = \sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2 + (y_2-y_1)^2 + (z_2-z_1)^2}$$

Rule: Use coordinate vector norms derived from Pythagoras for rapid distance calculation between points in space.

Application: Time-saver in coordinate geometry coordinate-vector problems.

Common errors and counterexamples

These examples show how a conclusion can fail when a condition is missing:

⚠️ COMMON ERROR #01

Assuming Pythagorean Applicability in Non-Right Triangles

Common Pitfall: Applying $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ when the angle opposite to $c$ is not $90^\circ$.

Counterexample: Triangle with sides $3, 4, 6$: $3^2 + 4^2 = 25 \neq 36$

Why it fails: The angle is obtuse, so $c^2 > a^2 + b^2$. Ignoring the cosine correction term leads to incorrect options.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check angle types using $c^2 \lesgtr a^2 + b^2$ before applying metric relations.
⚠️ COMMON ERROR #02

Misidentifying the Hypotenuse

Common Pitfall: Taking one of the perpendicular legs as $c$ in the formula $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$.

Counterexample: Legs $5, 12$: setting hypotenuse as $5$ yields impossible negative leg squared.

Why it fails: The hypotenuse is always the longest side, opposite the right angle.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Verify that $c$ is strictly greater than both $a$ and $b$.

Solved examples

Each problem includes a full solution and a shorter method:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

In a $\triangle ABC$, $\angle B = 90^\circ$. If $AB = 5$ cm and $BC = 12$ cm, find the length of the median drawn from $B$ to $AC$.

(A) $6.5$ cm(B) $6$ cm(C) $13$ cm(D) $5.5$ cm
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Find hypotenuse $AC = \sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = 13$ cm. Step 2: Recall that in a right-angled triangle, the circumcenter is the midpoint of the hypotenuse, and the median to the hypotenuse is equal in length to half the hypotenuse. Step 3: Median $BM = \frac{13}{2} = 6.5$ cm.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
In a right triangle, median to hypotenuse = half of hypotenuse = $13/2 = 6.5$.
💡 Tip: Remember the circumcenter property for right triangles to save 40 seconds.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If the sides of a triangle are $7, 24, 25$, what is the nature of the triangle?

(A) Acute-angled(B) Obtuse-angled(C) Right-angled(D) Equilateral
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Check $7^2 + 24^2 = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25^2$. Since $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ holds strictly, the triangle is right-angled.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize $(7, 24, 25)$ as a standard primitive Pythagorean triple instantly.
💡 Tip: Memorize standard Pythagorean triples like $(3,4,5), (5,12,13), (7,24,25), (8,15,17), (9,40,41)$.ointers.

Practice questions

Q1
The Baudhayana Sulba Sutra states the geometric rule equivalent to which modern theorem?
A Taylor's Theorem
B Pythagoras Theorem
C Intermediate Value Theorem
D De Moivre's Theorem
Derivation & Analysis:

Baudhayana formulated the diagonal relation for rectangles, which is the historical antecedent of the Pythagorean theorem.

Q2
If the legs of a right triangle are $8$ cm and $15$ cm, the length of the perpendicular from the right vertex to the hypotenuse is:
A $\frac{120}{17}$ cm
B $\frac{60}{17}$ cm
C $17$ cm
D $7$ cm
Derivation & Analysis:

Hypotenuse $c = \sqrt{8^2 + 15^2} = 17$. Area $= \frac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 15 = \frac{1}{2} \times 17 \times h \implies h = \frac{120}{17}$.

Q3
In any triangle $ABC$, if $a^2 + b^2 > c^2$, then angle $C$ is:
A Acute
B Obtuse
C Right
D Straight
Derivation & Analysis:

By the law of cosines, $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$. If $a^2 + b^2 > c^2$, then $\cos C > 0$, making angle $C$ acute.

Q4
The length of the longest diagonal of a cuboid with dimensions $3$ cm, $4$ cm, and $12$ cm is:
A $13$ cm
B $19$ cm
C $10$ cm
D $12$ cm
Derivation & Analysis:

Diagonal $d = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13$.

Q5
If $m$ and $n$ ($m > n > 0$) are integers, which of the following represents a Pythagorean triple?
A $(m^2+n^2, mn, m^2-n^2)$
B $(m^2-n^2, 2mn, m^2+n^2)$
C $(2mn, m+n, m-n)$
D $(m^2, n^2, m+n)$
Derivation & Analysis:

Checking $(m^2-n^2)^2 + (2mn)^2 = m^4 - 2m^2n^2 + n^4 + 4m^2n^2 = (m^2+n^2)^2$, which satisfies $a^2+b^2=c^2$.

Q6
If the sides of a triangle are in AP with common difference $d$, and the triangle is right-angled, the sides are in the ratio:
A $3:4:5$
B $1:2:3$
C $5:12:13$
D $2:3:4$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let sides be $x-d, x, x+dx$. By Pythagoras: $(x-d)^2 + x^2 = (x+d)^2 \implies x^2 - 4dx = 0 \implies x = 4d$. Sides are $3d, 4d, 5d$, ratio $3:4:5$.

Q7
In a right-angled triangle $ABC$ with $\angle B = 90^\circ$, $D$ is the midpoint of $AC$. If $BD = 5$ cm, then $AC$ is:
A $5$ cm
B $2.5$ cm
C $10$ cm
D $7.5$ cm
Derivation & Analysis:

The median to the hypotenuse is half of the hypotenuse. Thus $AC = 2 \times BD = 2 \times 5 = 10$ cm.

Q8
What is the distance between the points $(1, 2, 3)$ and $(3, 2, 1)$ in 3D space?
A $2\sqrt{2}$
B $4$
C $2$
D $\sqrt{6}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Distance $d = \sqrt{(3-1)^2 + (2-2)^2 + (1-3)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 0 + 4} = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}$.

Q9
If equilateral triangles are drawn on the three sides of a right-angled triangle, the sum of the areas of the triangles on the two legs equals:
A Half the area on the hypotenuse
B Twice the area on the hypotenuse
C Equal to the area on the hypotenuse
D None of these
Derivation & Analysis:

Since area of an equilateral triangle is proportional to the square of its side, $A = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}s^2$. Thus $A_1 + A_2 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(a^2+b^2) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}c^2 = A_3$.

Q10
Which of the following tuples is NOT a Pythagorean triple?
A $(9, 40, 41)$
B $(11, 60, 61)$
C $(12, 35, 37)$
D $(13, 27, 30)$
Derivation & Analysis:

For $(13, 27, 30)$, $13^2 + 27^2 = 169 + 729 = 898$, whereas $30^2 = 900$. Hence $898 \neq 900$.
