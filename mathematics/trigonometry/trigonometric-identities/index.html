Trigonometry

Trigonometric identities

Trigonometric identities are equations true for all admissible values of the variables involving trigonometric ratios, serving as the cornerstone for algebraic simplification and calculus integration. Mastering fundamental Pythagorean, sum-difference, and multiple-angle identities is vital for securing rapid-fire marks in UP TGT/PGT, DSSSB, and KVS competitive examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Pythagorean Trigonometric Foundation
For all \theta \in \mathbb{R} (with appropriate domains for denominators and tangents), \sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1, \quad 1 + \tan^2 \theta = \sec^2 \theta, \quad 1 + \cot^2 \theta = \csc^2 \theta.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Derived directly from the unit circle equation $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ where $x = \cos \theta$ and $y = \sin \theta$, representing the Euclidean distance from the origin to any point on the circumference.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Provides the primary reduction mechanism for converting high-degree trigonometric polynomials into linear or lower-degree rational forms, essential for calculus transformations.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Domain Restriction for Tangent and Secant \theta \neq \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi, \; k \in \mathbb{Z} Strictly Mandatory Division by zero occurs, rendering the identity undefined and resulting in vertical asymptotes.
Domain Restriction for Cotangent and Cosecant \theta \neq k\pi, \; k \in \mathbb{Z} Strictly Mandatory Leads to undefined expressions due to division by \sin \theta = 0.
Argument Continuity and Periodicity \theta \in \mathbb{R} Essential for functional transformations Ignoring periodicity leads to loss of infinite sets of general solutions in trigonometric equations.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Sum of Sines/Cosines in Arithmetic Progression

$$ \sum_{k=1}^{n} \sin(a + k\cdot d) = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{nd}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)} \sin\left(a + \frac{(n+1)d}{2}\right) $$

Exam Rule: Use the multiplicative angle formula to sum series in seconds without expanding term by term.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT for summing sine and cosine series with common angle differences.
02

Shortcut 2: Maximum and Minimum of $a \cos \theta + b \sin \theta$

$$ -\sqrt{a^2 + b^2} \le a \cos \theta + b \sin \theta \le \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} $$

Exam Rule: Instantly bound expressions of linear combinations of sine and cosine by taking the square root of the sum of squares.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard scoring item in TGT/PGT examinations for range and optimization problems.
03

Shortcut 3: Conditional Trigonometric Identity for Triangles

$$ \text{If } A+B+C = \pi, \text{ then } \tan A + \tan B + \tan C = \tan A \tan B \tan C $$

Exam Rule: Apply immediately when interior angles of a triangle are given, bypassing long algebraic expansions.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested across KVS, NVS, and UP TGT exams.
04

Shortcut 4: Triple Angle Product Identities

$$ \sin \theta \sin(60^\circ - \theta) \sin(60^\circ + \theta) = \frac{1}{4} \sin(3\theta) $$

Exam Rule: Recognize product patterns to instantly substitute into single-term multiple-angle expressions.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used to evaluate numerical trigonometric products rapidly in objective papers.
05

Shortcut 5: Half-Angle Substitution (Weierstrass Substitution)

$$ \sin \theta = \frac{2t}{1+t^2}, \quad \cos \theta = \frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}, \quad \text{where } t = \tan\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) $$

Exam Rule: Convert rational trigonometric functions into algebraic rational functions of $t$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial for evaluating complex integrals and finding extreme values in PGT exams.
06

Shortcut 6: Value Substitution (Testing $\theta = 0^\circ$ or $45^\circ$)

$$ \text{Substitute } \theta = \frac{\pi}{4} \text{ to eliminate variables in identity verification.} $$

Exam Rule: If an identity holds universally, it holds for specific standard angles. Check options by substituting $\theta = 0^\circ$ or $45^\circ$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves 2-3 minutes per complex identity verification question in 125-question exams.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Extraneous Solutions in Squaring Identities

Common Pitfall: Squaring both sides of a trigonometric equation to eliminate radicals introduces extraneous roots that do not satisfy the original equation.

Counterexample: \sin \theta + \cos \theta = \sqrt{2} \implies (\sin \theta + \cos \theta)^2 = 2 \implies 1 + \sin(2\theta) = 2

Why it fails: Squaring changes signs and expands the solution set; solutions where $\sin \theta + \cos \theta = -\sqrt{2}$ are incorrectly included.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always cross-check final angle solutions against the original unsquared equation's domain and sign constraints.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Ignoring Quadrant Signs in Square Roots

Common Pitfall: Writing $\sqrt{\cos^2 \theta} = \cos \theta$ universally without checking the quadrant.

Counterexample: \sqrt{\cos^2 \theta} = |\cos \theta|

Why it fails: In quadrants II and III, $\cos \theta$ is negative, so dropping the absolute value leads to sign errors.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always use absolute value bars when simplifying square roots of squared trigonometric functions: $\sqrt{1-\sin^2 \theta} = |\cos \theta|$.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $\tan \theta + \sec \theta = e^x$, find the value of $\cos \theta$.

(A) \frac{e^x + e^{-x}}{2}(B) \frac{2e^x}{e^{2x} + 1}(C) \frac{e^x - e^{-x}}{e^x + e^{-x}}(D) \frac{1}{e^x + e^{-x}}
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Use the identity $\sec^2 \theta - \tan^2 \theta = 1 \implies (\sec \theta - \tan \theta)(\sec \theta + \tan \theta) = 1$. Step 2: Since $\sec \theta + \tan \theta = e^x$, we have $\sec \theta - \tan \theta = e^{-x}$. Step 3: Add both equations to get $2 \sec \theta = e^x + e^{-x} \implies \sec \theta = \frac{e^x + e^{-x}}{2}$. Step 4: Hence, $\cos \theta = \frac{2}{e^x + e^{-x}} = \frac{2e^x}{e^{2x} + 1}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Use reciprocal property: if $x + \frac{1}{x} = K$, then extraction of cosine via secant reciprocal yields option (B) instantly.
💡 Key Takeaway: Whenever $\sec \theta \pm \tan \theta$ or $\csc \theta \pm \cot \theta$ is given as an exponential, immediately write its reciprocal for the conjugate pair.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

What is the maximum value of $3 \sin \theta + 4 \cos \theta + 5$?

(A) 5(B) 10(C) 12(D) \sqrt{50}
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Identify $a = 3$ and $b = 4$ in $a\cos\theta + b\sin\theta$. Step 2: Calculate maximum value of the trigonometric part as $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$. Step 3: Add the constant 5 to get $5 + 5 = 10$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Max value = $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} + 5 = 5 + 5 = 10$. Done in 5 seconds.
💡 Key Takeaway: Do not forget to add or subtract any constant term appended outside the linear sine-cosine combination.

Q1
MCQ 1: The value of $\sin^2 5^\circ + \sin^2 10^\circ + \sin^2 15^\circ + \dots + \sin^2 85^\circ + \sin^2 90^\circ$ is:
A (A) 8
B (B) 9
C (C) 9.5
D (D) 10
Derivation & Analysis:

Pair terms using complementary angles $\sin^2(90^\circ - \theta) = \cos^2 \theta$. There are 18 terms from $5^\circ$ to $90^\circ$. Pairing $5^\circ$ with $85^\circ$ gives 1, plus $\sin^2 90^\circ = 1$ and $\sin^2 45^\circ = 1/2$, totaling $8 + 1 + 0.5 = 9.5$.

Q2
MCQ 2: If $\cos \alpha + \cos \beta = 0 = \sin \alpha + \sin \beta$, then $\cos(2\alpha - 2\beta)$ is equal to:
A (A) 0
B (B) 1
C (C) -1
D (D) \frac{1}{2}
Derivation & Analysis:

From $\cos \alpha = -\cos \beta$ and $\sin \alpha = -\sin \beta$, squaring and adding gives no direct contradiction, but dividing gives $\tan \alpha = \tan \beta \implies \alpha - \beta = \pi/2 + k\pi$. Thus $2\alpha - 2\beta = \pi + 2k\pi$, so $\cos(2\alpha - 2\beta) = -1$ or check consistency. Wait, $\cos \alpha + \cos \beta = 0 \implies \alpha - \beta = \pi$ or odd multiple of $\pi$. Thus $2\alpha - 2\beta$ is an even multiple of $\pi$, making $\cos(0) = 1$.

Q3
MCQ 3: The expression $\frac{\sin 3\theta}{\sin \theta} - \frac{\cos 3\theta}{\cos \theta}$ is identically equal to:
A (A) 1
B (B) 2
C (C) 4
D (D) \sin \theta \cos \theta
Derivation & Analysis:

Combine fractions over common denominator $\sin \theta \cos \theta$: $\frac{\sin 3\theta \cos \theta - \cos 3\theta \sin \theta}{\sin \theta \cos \theta} = \frac{\sin(3\theta - \theta)}{\sin \theta \cos \theta} = \frac{\sin 2\theta}{\frac{1}{2}\sin 2\theta} = 2$.

Q4
MCQ 4: If $\tan \theta = \frac{1}{2}$ and $\tan \phi = \frac{1}{3}$, then the value of $\theta + \phi$ is:
A (A) \frac{\pi}{6}
B (B) \frac{\pi}{4}
C (C) \frac{\pi}{3}
D (D) \frac{\pi}{2}
Derivation & Analysis:

Use $\tan(\theta + \phi) = \frac{\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3}}{1 - \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{3}} = \frac{5/6}{5/6} = 1$. Since $\theta, \phi \in (0, \pi/2)$, $\theta + \phi = \frac{\pi}{4}$.

Q5
MCQ 5: The maximum value of $\sin^6 \theta + \cos^6 \theta$ is:
A (A) 1
B (B) \frac{3}{4}
C (C) \frac{1}{2}
D (D) \frac{1}{4}
Derivation & Analysis:

Using identity $a^3 + b^3 = (a+b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)$, we get $(\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta)((\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta)^2 - 3\sin^2 \theta \cos^2 \theta) = 1 - \frac{3}{4}\sin^2(2\theta)$. Maximum occurs when $\sin(2\theta) = 0$, giving $1 - 0 = 1$.

Q6
MCQ 6: If $x \sin^3 \theta + y \cos^3 \theta = \sin \theta \cos \theta$ and $x \sin \theta - y \cos \theta = 0$, then $x^2 + y^2$ is equal to:
A (A) 1
B (B) 2
C (C) \frac{1}{2}
D (D) 4
Derivation & Analysis:

From $x \sin \theta = y \cos \theta$, substitute into the first equation: $(y \cos \theta)\sin^2 \theta + (x \sin \theta)\cos^2 \theta = \sin \theta \cos \theta \implies x^2 + y^2 = 1$ after algebraic reduction.

Q7
MCQ 7: General solution of $\sqrt{3} \cos \theta + \sin \theta = \sqrt{2}$ is:
A (A) n\pi + (-1)^n \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{\pi}{3}
B (B) 2n\pi \pm \frac{\pi}{4}
C (C) n\pi + (-1)^n \frac{\pi}{3} + \frac{\pi}{4}
D (D) 2n\pi \pm \frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{\pi}{4}
Derivation & Analysis:

Divide by 2: $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cos \theta + \frac{1}{2}\sin \theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \implies \cos\left(\theta - \frac{\pi}{3}\right) = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)$, yielding $\theta - \frac{\pi}{3} = 2n\pi \pm \frac{\pi}{4}$, or general form equivalent.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): $\sec^2 \theta + \csc^2 \theta$ can never be less than 4. Reason (R): Arithmetic Mean is greater than or equal to Geometric Mean.
A (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (C) (A) is true but (R) is false
D (D) (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

By AM-GM inequality, $\tan^2 \theta + \cot^2 \theta \ge 2$. Adding 2 to both sides gives $\tan^2 \theta + 2 + \cot^2 \theta \ge 4 \implies \sec^2 \theta + \csc^2 \theta \ge 4$. Thus both assertion and reason are correct and linked.

Q9
MCQ 9: If $\cos A = \frac{3}{5}$ and $\cos B = \frac{5}{13}$, where $A$ and $B$ are acute angles, then $\cos(A - B)$ is:
A (A) \frac{33}{65}
B (B) \frac{63}{65}
C (C) \frac{16}{65}
D (D) \frac{56}{65}
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $\cos A = 3/5 \implies \sin A = 4/5$, and $\cos B = 5/13 \implies \sin B = 12/13$. $\cos(A-B) = \cos A \cos B + \sin A \sin B = \frac{3}{5}\cdot\frac{5}{13} + \frac{4}{5}\cdot\frac{12}{13} = \frac{15 + 48}{65} = \frac{63}{65}$.

Q10
MCQ 10: The value of $\tan 9^\circ - \tan 27^\circ - \tan 63^\circ + \tan 81^\circ$ is:
A (A) 1
B (B) 2
C (C) 4
D (D) 8
Derivation & Analysis:

Rewrite using cotangent conversions: $\tan 9^\circ + \cot 9^\circ - (\tan 27^\circ + \cot 27^\circ) = \frac{2}{\sin 18^\circ} - \frac{2}{\sin 54^\circ} = \frac{2}{\sin 18^\circ} - \frac{2}{\cos 36^\circ} = 4$.

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $\tan \theta + \sec \theta = e^x$, find the value of $\cos \theta$.

(A) \frac{e^x + e^{-x}}{2}(B) \frac{2e^x}{e^{2x} + 1}(C) \frac{e^x - e^{-x}}{e^x + e^{-x}}(D) \frac{1}{e^x + e^{-x}}
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Use the identity $\sec^2 \theta - \tan^2 \theta = 1 \implies (\sec \theta - \tan \theta)(\sec \theta + \tan \theta) = 1$. Step 2: Since $\sec \theta + \tan \theta = e^x$, we have $\sec \theta - \tan \theta = e^{-x}$. Step 3: Add both equations to get $2 \sec \theta = e^x + e^{-x} \implies \sec \theta = \frac{e^x + e^{-x}}{2}$. Step 4: Hence, $\cos \theta = \frac{2}{e^x + e^{-x}} = \frac{2e^x}{e^{2x} + 1}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Use reciprocal property: if $x + \frac{1}{x} = K$, then extraction of cosine via secant reciprocal yields option (B) instantly.
💡 Key Takeaway: Whenever $\sec \theta \pm \tan \theta$ or $\csc \theta \pm \cot \theta$ is given as an exponential, immediately write its reciprocal for the conjugate pair.

What is the maximum value of $3 \sin \theta + 4 \cos \theta + 5$?

(A) 5(B) 10(C) 12(D) \sqrt{50}
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation

⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Max value = $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} + 5 = 5 + 5 = 10$. Done in 5 seconds.

A (A) 1
B (B) 2
C (C) 4
D (D) \sin \theta \cos \theta
Derivation & Analysis:

Combine fractions over common denominator $\sin \theta \cos \theta$: $\frac{\sin 3\theta \cos \theta - \cos 3\theta \sin \theta}{\sin \theta \cos \theta} = \frac{\sin(3\theta - \theta)}{\sin \theta \cos \theta} = \frac{\sin 2\theta}{\frac{1}{2}\sin 2\theta} = 2$.

Q4
MCQ 4: If $\tan \theta = \frac{1}{2}$ and $\tan \phi = \frac{1}{3}$, then the value of $\theta + \phi$ is:
A (A) \frac{\pi}{6}
B (B) \frac{\pi}{4}
C (C) \frac{\pi}{3}
D (D) \frac{\pi}{2}
Derivation & Analysis:

Use $\tan(\theta + \phi) = \frac{\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3}}{1 - \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{3}} = \frac{5/6}{5/6} = 1$. Since $\theta, \phi \in (0, \pi/2)$, $\theta + \phi = \frac{\pi}{4}$.

Q5
MCQ 5: The maximum value of $\sin^6 \theta + \cos^6 \theta$ is:
A (A) 1
B (B) \frac{3}{4}
C (C) \frac{1}{2}
D (D) \frac{1}{4}
Derivation & Analysis:

Using identity $a^3 + b^3 = (a+b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)$, we get $(\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta)((\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta)^2 - 3\sin^2 \theta \cos^2 \theta) = 1 - \frac{3}{4}\sin^2(2\theta)$. Maximum occurs when $\sin(2\theta) = 0$, giving $1 - 0 = 1$.

Q6
MCQ 6: If $x \sin^3 \theta + y \cos^3 \theta = \sin \theta \cos \theta$ and $x \sin \theta - y \cos \theta = 0$, then $x^2 + y^2$ is equal to:
A (A) 1
B (B) 2
C (C) \frac{1}{2}
D (D) 4
Derivation & Analysis:

From $x \sin \theta = y \cos \theta$, substitute into the first equation: $(y \cos \theta)\sin^2 \theta + (x \sin \theta)\cos^2 \theta = \sin \theta \cos \theta \implies x^2 + y^2 = 1$ after algebraic reduction.

Q7
MCQ 7: General solution of $\sqrt{3} \cos \theta + \sin \theta = \sqrt{2}$ is:
A (A) n\pi + (-1)^n \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{\pi}{3}
B (B) 2n\pi \pm \frac{\pi}{4}
C (C) n\pi + (-1)^n \frac{\pi}{3} + \frac{\pi}{4}
D (D) 2n\pi \pm \frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{\pi}{4}
Derivation & Analysis:

Divide by 2: $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cos \theta + \frac{1}{2}\sin \theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \implies \cos\left(\theta - \frac{\pi}{3}\right) = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)$, yielding $\theta - \frac{\pi}{3} = 2n\pi \pm \frac{\pi}{4}$, or general form equivalent.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): $\sec^2 \theta + \csc^2 \theta$ can never be less than 4. Reason (R): Arithmetic Mean is greater than or equal to Geometric Mean.
A (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (C) (A) is true but (R) is false
D (D) (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

By AM-GM inequality, $\tan^2 \theta + \cot^2 \theta \ge 2$. Adding 2 to both sides gives $\tan^2 \theta + 2 + \cot^2 \theta \ge 4 \implies \sec^2 \theta + \csc^2 \theta \ge 4$. Thus both assertion and reason are correct and linked.

Q9
MCQ 9: If $\cos A = \frac{3}{5}$ and $\cos B = \frac{5}{13}$, where $A$ and $B$ are acute angles, then $\cos(A - B)$ is:
A (A) \frac{33}{65}
B (B) \frac{63}{65}
C (C) \frac{16}{65}
D (D) \frac{56}{65}
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $\cos A = 3/5 \implies \sin A = 4/5$, and $\cos B = 5/13 \implies \sin B = 12/13$. $\cos(A-B) = \cos A \cos B + \sin A \sin B = \frac{3}{5}\cdot\frac{5}{13} + \frac{4}{5}\cdot\frac{12}{13} = \frac{15 + 48}{65} = \frac{63}{65}$.

Q10
MCQ 10: The value of $\tan 9^\circ - \tan 27^\circ - \tan 63^\circ + \tan 81^\circ$ is:
A (A) 1
B (B) 2
C (C) 4
D (D) 8
Derivation & Analysis:

Rewrite using cotangent conversions: $\tan 9^\circ + \cot 9^\circ - (\tan 27^\circ + \cot 27^\circ) = \frac{2}{\sin 18^\circ} - \frac{2}{\sin 54^\circ} = \frac{2}{\sin 18^\circ} - \frac{2}{\cos 36^\circ} = 4$.

Failure to check domain restrictions (such as denominators becoming zero for tan, cot, sec, csc) and missing general solution periodic bounds.
