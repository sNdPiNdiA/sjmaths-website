10. Trigonometry & Complex Numbers

Height and distance

Height and Distance problems form a cornerstone of applied trigonometry, utilizing right-angled triangles, angles of elevation, and depression to determine inaccessible heights and horizontal spans. Mastering trigonometric ratios, sine/cosine rules, and standard geometrical configurations is essential for scoring high-yield questions in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Principle of Elevation and Depression via Right-Angled Triangles
For any right-angled triangle $\triangle ABC$ with $\angle B = 90^\circ$ and acute angle $\angle BCA = \theta$, the trigonometric ratios define the fundamental relations: $\sin \theta = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Hypotenuse}} = \frac{AB}{AC}$, $\cos \theta = \frac{\text{Adjacent}}{\text{Hypotenuse}} = \frac{BC}{AC}$, and $\tan \theta = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Adjacent}} = \frac{AB}{BC}$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

The angle of elevation is the angle formed by the line of sight with the horizontal upward from the observer, while the angle of depression is formed downward. Geometrically, alternate interior angles equate the angle of depression from an upper point to the angle of elevation from the lower point.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Translates verbal geometric descriptions into a system of simultaneous algebraic equations involving tangents and cotangents of angles, enabling exact analytical solutions for unknown heights and distances.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Tower and Moving Observer (Angle Doubling)

$$ h = d \cdot \frac{\tan \alpha \tan \beta}{\tan \beta - \tan \alpha} \quad \text{or if } \beta = 2\alpha, \, h = x \tan \alpha \text{ (where } x \text{ is distance moved)}$$

Exam Rule: When an observer moves a distance $d$ towards a tower and the angle of elevation changes from $\alpha$ to $\beta$, use the cotangent difference formula: $h = \frac{d}{\cot \alpha - \cot \beta}.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT/TGT where an observer walks towards a building and the angle shifts from $30^\circ$ to $60^\circ$.
02

Shortcut 2: Two Points on Opposite Sides of a Tower

$$ h = \frac{d \tan \alpha \tan \beta}{\tan \alpha + \tan \beta} = d \sin \alpha \cos \beta \text{ (when complementary)}$$

Exam Rule: If two points are at distance $d$ apart on a straight line passing through the base of a tower with elevation angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$, the height is given by the product formula.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard recurring model in DSSSB PGT and KVS Mathematics exams.
03

Shortcut 3: Cloud and Reflection Problem

$$ h = H \left( \frac{\tan \beta + \tan \alpha}{\tan \beta - \tan \alpha} \right) \text{ or } h = x \tan \alpha $$

Exam Rule: If a cloud is at height $h$ above a lake and its reflection in the lake is observed at an angle of depression $\beta$ while the cloud is at elevation $\alpha$, the height of the cloud is calculated directly using the water-surface mirror principle.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct formula substitution questions appear in LT Grade and UP TGT exams.
04

Shortcut 4: Pole on Top of a Building

$$ \frac{H}{h} = \frac{\cot \alpha - \cot \beta_1}{\cot \alpha - \cot \beta_2} $$

Exam Rule: Ratio of heights when a flagpole stands atop a tower, viewed from a common point at varying angles.

⚡ PYQ Context: High-level problem type in UP PGT screening tests.
05

Shortcut 5: Equal Angles from Base to Top

$$ \tan \theta = \frac{h}{d} $$

Exam Rule: Utilize standard Pythagorean triples ($3:4:5$, $5:12:13$) and special angles ($30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ$) to write down answers without solving quadratic equations.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves crucial time in 125-question timed examinations.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Tangent Monotonicity

$$ \alpha < \beta \implies \tan \alpha < \tan \beta \implies d_1 > d_2 $$

Exam Rule: Since $\tan \theta$ is strictly increasing on $(0^\circ, 90^\circ)$, closer distance always corresponds to a larger angle of elevation. Instantly eliminate options violating this monotonicity.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used to discard absurd options in multi-choice questions within 10 seconds.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Ignoring Observer's Eye Height

Common Pitfall: Candidates calculate the height of a tower from the ground level even when the problem states the observer's eye is at a height $h_0$ above the ground.

Counterexample: Observer height = $1.5\text{ m}$, Angle = $45^\circ$, Distance = $10\text{ m}$. Total height $\neq 10\text{ m}$.

Why it fails: The calculated $\tan \theta$ height applies only from the eye level; the total height of the object must include the observer's height ($H = h + h_0$).

🛡️ Exam Defense: Underline statements mentioning 'observer of height $h$' or 'instrument placed at height'.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Confusing Angle of Elevation with Depression

Common Pitfall: Interchanging angles of elevation and depression or misplacing them in non-horizontal baseline scenarios.

Counterexample: Angle of depression from top of cliff to boat = $30^\circ$; candidate takes angle of elevation from cliff to cloud instead.

Why it fails: Results in inverted triangle proportions and completely incorrect trigonometric ratios.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always draw a rough horizontal reference line (line of sight parallel to ground) before marking angles.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point on the ground is $30^\circ$. On walking $50\text{ m}$ towards the tower, the angle of elevation becomes $60^\circ$. Find the height of the tower.

(A) $25\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$(B) $50\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$(C) $25\text{ m}$(D) $\frac{50}{\sqrt{3}}\text{ m}$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Let height be $h$ and initial distance be $x$. $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{h}{x} \implies x = h\sqrt{3}$. After moving $50\text{ m}$, distance is $x - 50$. $\tan 60^\circ = \frac{h}{x - 50} \implies x - 50 = \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}}$. Substituting $x$: $h\sqrt{3} - \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}} = 50 \implies \frac{2h}{\sqrt{3}} = 50 \implies h = 25\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Apply shortcut formula: $h = \frac{d}{\cot \alpha - \cot \beta} = \frac{50}{\cot 30^\circ - \cot 60^\circ} = \frac{50}{\sqrt{3} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}} = \frac{50}{\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}} = 25\sqrt{3}$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Memorize standard ratio values for $30^\circ-60^\circ$ triangle shifts: height is always $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \times \text{distance moved}$.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

The angle of elevation of a cloud from a point $h$ meters above a lake is $\alpha$ and the angle of depression of its reflection in the lake is $\beta$. The height of the cloud above the lake is:

(A) $h \frac{\tan \beta + \tan \alpha}{\tan \beta - \tan \alpha}$(B) $h \frac{\tan \beta - \tan \alpha}{\tan \beta + \tan \alpha}$(C) $h \cot \left(\frac{\alpha + \beta}{2}\right)$(D) $h \tan(\alpha + \beta)$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Let cloud be at height $H$ above water surface. Distance from observation point to cloud level is $H - h$. Depth of reflection below water surface is $H + h$. Using horizontal distance $x = \frac{H-h}{\tan \alpha}$ and $x = \frac{H+h}{\tan \beta}$, equate $x$ to solve for $H$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Direct standard textbook result derivation frequently tested as a direct formula in UP PGT.
💡 Key Takeaway: Note the plus sign in numerator and minus in denominator for cloud-reflection formulas.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
MCQ 1: If the angle of elevation of the sun changes from $30^\circ$ to $60^\circ$, the length of the shadow of a pole decreases by $x$ meters. The height of the pole is:
A (A) $\frac{x}{2}$
B (B) $\frac{x\sqrt{3}}{2}$
C (C) $x\sqrt{3}$
D (D) $2x$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let height be $h$. Initial shadow $h\sqrt{3}$, final shadow $\frac{h}{\sqrt{3}}$. Difference is $h\sqrt{3} - \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{2h}{\sqrt{3}} = x \implies h = \frac{x\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

Q2
MCQ 2: A flagstaff $5\text{ m}$ high stands on a building $25\text{ m}$ high. At an observer on the ground, the flagstaff and building subtend equal angles. The distance of the observer from the building is:
A (A) $5\sqrt{30}$
B (B) $10\sqrt{5}$
C (C) $5\sqrt{5}$
D (D) $15\sqrt{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let distance be $x$. Building height $= 25$, flagstaff $= 5$. Total height $= 30$. Using angle sum tangent properties, $\tan(\theta_1 + \theta_2)$ leads to $x = \sqrt{25 \times 30} = \sqrt{750} = 5\sqrt{30}$.

Q3
MCQ 3: The upper part of a tree broken by the wind makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the ground and the distance from the root to the point where the top touches the ground is $10\text{ m}$. The height of the tree is:
A (A) $10\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$
B (B) $\frac{20}{\sqrt{3}}\text{ m}$
C (C) $10(\sqrt{3} + 1)\text{ m}$
D (D) $10\sqrt{3} \text{ m}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Base $b = 10$, angle $30^\circ$. Height of remaining stump $h = 10 \tan 30^\circ = \frac{10}{\sqrt{3}}$. Broken part (hypotenuse) $l = \frac{10}{\cos 30^\circ} = \frac{20}{\sqrt{3}}$. Total height $= \frac{10}{\sqrt{3}} + \frac{20}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{30}{\sqrt{3}} = 10\sqrt{3}$? Wait: $\frac{10}{\sqrt{3}} + \frac{20}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{30}{\sqrt{3}} = 10\sqrt{3}$. Let's verify option B: $\frac{20}{\sqrt{3}} + \frac{10}{\sqrt{3}} = 10\sqrt{3}$. Correct option is (A) $10\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$.

Q4
MCQ 4: From the top of a cliff $h$ meters high, the angles of depression of the top and bottom of a tower are $\alpha$ and $\beta$ respectively. The height of the tower is:
A (A) $h\left(1 - \frac{\tan \alpha}{\tan \beta}\right)$
B (B) $h\left(1 - \frac{\tan \beta}{\tan \alpha}\right)$
C (C) $h(1 - \tan \alpha \cot \beta)$
D (D) $h(\cot \alpha - \cot \beta)$
Derivation & Analysis:

Standard derivation yields tower height $H = h\left(1 - \frac{\tan \alpha}{\tan \beta}\right)$.

Q5
MCQ 5: A vertical tower is situated on a hill of height $H$. At a point $A$ on the ground, the top and bottom of the tower subtend angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$. The height of the tower is:
A (A) $H(\tan \alpha \cot \beta - 1)$
B (B) $H(\tan \alpha - \tan \beta)$
C (C) $H(\cot \alpha \tan \beta - 1)$
D (D) $\frac{H \sin(\alpha-\beta)}{\cos \alpha \sin \beta}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Standard formula for tower on a hill: $h = H(\tan \alpha \cot \beta - 1)$. Option (A) is correct.

Q6
MCQ 6: If the elevation of the sun is $45^\circ$, then the length of the shadow of a $12\text{ m}$ high tree is:
A (A) $6\text{ m}$
B (B) $12\text{ m}$
C (C) $12\sqrt{2}\text{ m}$
D (D) $24\text{ m}$
Derivation & Analysis:

When $\theta = 45^\circ$, $\tan 45^\circ = 1$, so height equals shadow length $= 12\text{ m}$.

Q7
MCQ 7: Two ships are sailing in the sea on the two sides of a lighthouse. Their angles of depression from the top of the lighthouse are $30^\circ$ and $45^\circ$. If the height of the lighthouse is $100\text{ m}$, the distance between the ships is:
A (A) $100(1 + \sqrt{3})\text{ m}$
B (B) $100(\sqrt{3} - 1)\text{ m}$
C (C) $200\text{ m}$
D (D) $100\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Distances are $h \cot 30^\circ = 100\sqrt{3}$ and $h \cot 45^\circ = 100$. Total distance $= 100(1 + \sqrt{3})$. Option (A).

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): The angle of elevation of an object increases as the observer moves closer to the object. Reason (R): The tangent function is strictly increasing in $(0, \pi/2)$.
A (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation
C (C) (A) is true but (R) is false
D (D) (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

As distance $d$ decreases, $\frac{h}{d}$ increases, and since $\tan \theta = \frac{h}{d}$, $\theta$ increases because $\tan$ is strictly increasing.

Q9
MCQ 9: A pole $10\text{ m}$ high casts a shadow $10\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$ long on the ground. The sun's elevation is:
A (A) $30^\circ$
B (B) $45^\circ$
C (C) $60^\circ$
D (D) $90^\circ$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\tan \theta = \frac{10}{10\sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \implies \theta = 30^\circ$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If a pole $6\text{ m}$ high casts a shadow $2\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$ long on the ground, then the sun's elevation is:
A (A) $30^\circ$
B (B) $45^\circ$
C (C) $60^\circ$
D (D) $75^\circ$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\tan \theta = \frac{6}{2\sqrt{3}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3} \implies \theta = 60^\circ$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Always identify the given side (usually the baseline distance or height) and the required unknown. If the relationship involves perpendicular and base, use tangent ($\tan \theta$). If hypotenuse is involved with perpendicular, use sine ($\sin \theta$).

Calculation errors in radical expressions like $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$ or failing to account for the observer's height when explicitly stated in the problem statement.

Typically 2 to 4 direct or application-based questions appear in every exam cycle, making it a high-return topic given its short solving time.

By memorizing standard coefficient ratios for $30^\circ-60^\circ$ and $45^\circ$ triangles (such as $h = \frac{d}{\cot \alpha - \cot \beta}$) and utilizing option elimination via inequality bounds.
