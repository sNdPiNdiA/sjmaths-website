10. Trigonometry & Complex Numbers

Inverse circular functions

Inverse circular (trigonometric) functions are fundamental mappings that restrict the domain of periodic trigonometric functions to ensure bijectivity, enabling unique angular retrievals from given ratios. Mastering their principal value branches, symmetry relations, and composite identities forms a high-yield scoring zone in UP TGT/PGT, DSSSB, and KVS examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Principal Value Branch Restriction & Inverse Definition
Let $f: [-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}] \to [-1, 1]$ be defined by $f(x) = \sin x$. Since $f$ is strictly monotonic and continuous, it is bijective and possesses a unique inverse function $f^{-1}: [-1, 1] \to [-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}]$ denoted by $f^{-1}(x) = \sin^{-1} x$, satisfying $\sin(\sin^{-1} x) = x$ for all $x \in [-1, 1]$ and $\sin^{-1}(\sin x) = x$ for all $x \in [-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}]$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

The graph of an inverse circular function is obtained by reflecting the restricted parent trigonometric curve across the line $y = x$, swapping the roles of abscissa (ratio) and ordinate (angle).

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Establishes an isomorphism between multiplicative/ratio domains and angular codomains, converting transcendental equations into algebraic or trigonometric systems.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Negative Argument Identities (Parity)

$$ \sin^{-1}(-x) = -\sin^{-1} x, \quad \cos^{-1}(-x) = \pi - \cos^{-1} x, \quad \tan^{-1}(-x) = -\tan^{-1} x $$

Exam Rule: Remember that odd functions (\sin, \tan, \csc) pull out the negative sign directly, while even-codomain co-functions (\cos, \sec, \cot) subtract from \pi.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently tested in UP PGT for evaluating composite inverse sums involving negative inputs.
02

Shortcut 2: Complementary Angle Relations

$$ \sin^{-1} x + \cos^{-1} x = \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad \tan^{-1} x + \cot^{-1} x = \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad \sec^{-1} x + \csc^{-1} x = \frac{\pi}{2} $$

Exam Rule: Whenever matching arguments appear in complementary pairs summing to \frac{\pi}{2}, instantly substitute the constant.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly used to simplify long algebraic sums in TGT examinations within seconds.
03

Shortcut 3: Sum of Two Tangent Inverses

$$ \tan^{-1} x + \tan^{-1} y = \begin{cases} \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x+y}{1-xy}\right) & \text{if } xy < 1 \\ \pi + \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x+y}{1-xy}\right) & \text{if } x > 0, y > 0, xy > 1 \end{cases} $$

Exam Rule: Always check the product $xy$ before applying the standard formula to avoid missing the $\pi$ phase shift.

⚡ PYQ Context: A favorite trap in DSSSB and KVS PGT where $xy > 1$ is explicitly given.
04

Shortcut 4: Conversion to Sine/Cosine Inverse

$$ \tan^{-1} x = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\right) = \cos^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\right) $$

Exam Rule: Draw a mental right triangle with perpendicular $x$ and base $1$ to instantly convert any inverse circular function into another.

⚡ PYQ Context: Essential time-saver for matching options in multiple-choice questions.
05

Shortcut 5: Multiple Angle Inverse Summations

$$ 2\tan^{-1} x = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{2x}{1+x^2}\right) = \cos^{-1}\left(\frac{1-x^2}{1+x^2}\right) = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{2x}{1-x^2}\right) $$

Exam Rule: Use substitution $x = \tan \theta$ to reduce complex algebraic square roots into simple multiple-angle formulas.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard pattern in UP PGT calculus and trigonometry integration.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Domain Checks

$$ \text{Domain of } \sec^{-1} x \text{ is } (-\infty, -1] \cup [1, \infty) $$

Exam Rule: Instantly eliminate options where arguments for $\sin^{-1}$ or $\cos^{-1}$ exceed $[-1, 1]$ or where $\sec^{-1}$ lies inside $(-1, 1)$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Allows candidates to eliminate 2 incorrect options in under 10 seconds.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: The Out-of-Range Principal Value Fallacy

Common Pitfall: Writing $\sin^{-1}(\sin \frac{2\pi}{3}) = \frac{2\pi}{3}$ without checking if it falls inside $[-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}]\text{.}$

Counterexample: \sin^{-1}\left(\sin \frac{2\pi}{3}\right)

Why it fails: Since $\frac{2\pi}{3} \notin [-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}]$, one must use periodicity: $\sin(\frac{2\pi}{3}) = \sin(\frac{\pi}{3})$, yielding $\frac{\pi}{3}\text{.}$

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always reduce the angle into the exact principal domain interval before applying cancellation property.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Ignoring Domain Restrictions in Equation Solving

Common Pitfall: Squaring equations or manipulating $\tan^{-1} x + \tan^{-1} y = \tan^{-1} z$ without verifying that the resulting value satisfies the original domain.

Counterexample: \tan^{-1} x + \tan^{-1}(2x) = \frac{\pi}{4}

Why it fails: Extraneous roots often emerge after taking tangents on both sides and clearing denominators.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Substitute obtained values back into the original inverse equation to ensure both sides match.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $\sin^{-1} x + \sin^{-1} y + \sin^{-1} z = \frac{3\pi}{2}$, find the value of $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - \frac{9}{x+y+z}$.

(A) $0$(B) $1$(C) $-1$(D) $\frac{1}{2}$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Note that the maximum value of $\sin^{-1} x$ is $\frac{\pi}{2}$. Step 2: Since the sum of three principal values equals $\frac{3\pi}{2}$, each must independently achieve its maximum. Step 3: Thus $\sin^{-1} x = \frac{\pi}{2} \implies x = 1$ (and similarly $y=1, z=1$). Step 4: Substitute $x=1, y=1, z=1$ into $1^2 + 1^2 + 1^2 - \frac{9}{1+1+1} = 3 - 3 = 0$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Extreme value saturation principle: when sum equals upper bound, each term equals its individual maximum.
💡 Key Takeaway: Look out for coefficient multipliers or sums equaling boundary values (like $\pi$ or $\frac{3\pi}{2}$).
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

The value of $\tan\left(\frac{1}{2} \cos^{-1} \frac{\sqrt{5}}{3}\right)$ is:

(A) $\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}$(B) $\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}$(C) $3 - \sqrt{5}$(D) $\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{4}$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Let $\theta = \cos^{-1} \frac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$, so $\cos \theta = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$. Step 2: We need $\tan(\theta/2)$. Use half-angle formula $\tan(\theta/2) = \sqrt{\frac{1-\cos \theta}{1+\cos \theta}}$. Step 3: Substitute $\cos \theta = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$ to get $\sqrt{\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{3+\sqrt{5}}}$. Step 4: Rationalize denominator to obtain $\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Direct half-angle substitution for inverse trigonometric ratios.
💡 Key Takeaway: Rationalizing surd denominators is crucial for matching final option formats.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
The principal value of $\cos^{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)$ is:
A -\frac{\pi}{3}
B \frac{2\pi}{3}
C \frac{\pi}{3}
D -\frac{2\pi}{3}
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $\cos^{-1}(-x) = \pi - \cos^{-1}(x)$, we have $\pi - \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{2\pi}{3}$, which lies in $[0, \pi]$.

Q2
If $\tan^{-1} 2 + \tan^{-1} 3 + \theta = \pi$, then $\theta$ equals:
A \frac{\pi}{4}
B -\frac{\pi}{4}
C \frac{3\pi}{4}
D 0
Derivation & Analysis:

Compute $\tan^{-1} 2 + \tan^{-1} 3 = \pi + \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{2+3}{1-6}\right) = \pi + \tan^{-1}(-1) = \pi - \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{3\pi}{4}$. Thus $\frac{3\pi}{4} + \theta = \pi \implies \theta = \frac{\pi}{4}$.

Q3
The domain of the function $f(x) = \sin^{-1}(2x - 1)$ is:
A [-1, 1]
B [0, 1]
C [-\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}]
D (0, 1)
Derivation & Analysis:

For $\sin^{-1}$ argument, $-1 \le 2x - 1 \le 1 \implies 0 \le 2x \le 2 \implies 0 \le x \le 1$.

Q4
The value of $\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3} - \sin^{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)$ is:
A 1
B -1
C 0
D \frac{1}{2}
Derivation & Analysis:

We know $\sin^{-1}(-1/2) = -\pi/6$. The expression becomes $\sin(\pi/3 - (-\pi/6)) = \sin(\pi/2) = 1$.

Q5
If $\cot^{-1} x + \cot^{-1} y + \cot^{-1} z = \frac{\pi}{2}$, then $xy + yz + zx$ is equal to:
A 0
B 1
C -1
D \frac{1}{2}
Derivation & Analysis:

Converting to $\tan^{-1}$, $\tan^{-1}(1/x) + \tan^{-1}(1/y) + \tan^{-1}(1/z) = \pi/2 \implies \sum \frac{1}{x} = \frac{1}{x}\frac{1}{y}\frac{1}{z}$ upon taking $\tan$ of both sides, yielding $xy + yz + zx = 1$.

Q6
The value of $\tan(\cos^{-1} \frac{3}{5} + \tan^{-1} \frac{1}{4})$ is:
A \frac{16}{7}
B \frac{7}{16}
C \frac{19}{8}
D 1
Derivation & Analysis:

Convert $\cos^{-1}(3/5)$ to $\tan^{-1}(4/3)$. Then evaluate $\tan(\tan^{-1}(4/3) + \tan^{-1}(1/4))$ using $\tan(A+B)$ formula to get $16/7$.

Q7
The number of real solutions of equation $\tan^{-1} x + \cot^{-1}(x+1) = \tan^{-1}(1+\frac{1}{x^2})$ is:
A 0
B 1
C 2
D Infinite
Derivation & Analysis:

Simplifying using inverse sum/difference identities yields a unique admissible real solution $x = 1$.

Q8
What is the range of $f(x) = \sec^{-1} x$?
A [-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}]
B [0, \pi]
C [0, \pi] \setminus \{\frac{\pi}{2}\}
D (-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2})
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, the range of $\sec^{-1} x$ is $[0, \pi]$ excluding $\pi/2$ where cosine is zero.

Q9
If $\sin(\sin^{-1} \frac{1}{5} + \cos^{-1} x) = 1$, then the value of $x$ is:
A 1
B \frac{1}{5}
C 0
D -1
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $\sin(\theta) = 1 \implies \theta = \pi/2$. Thus $\sin^{-1}(1/5) + \cos^{-1}(x) = \pi/2$, which implies $\cos^{-1}(x) = \cos^{-1}(1/5) \implies x = 1/5$.

Q10
The value of $\cos\left[2 \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{7}\right)\right] + \sin\left[4 \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)\right]$ is:
A \frac{31}{25}
B 1
C 0
D \frac{14}{25}
Derivation & Analysis:

Apply double-angle formulas for inverse tangents: $2\tan^{-1}(1/7) = \cos^{-1}(24/25)$ and compute terms systematically to yield $31/25$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Because inverse functions are strictly bound to their principal ranges. $\tan^{-1}(\tan x) = x$ holds true only when $x \in (-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2})$. Outside this interval, periodicity must be accounted for.

Failing to check condition ranges when applying sum formulas like $\tan^{-1} x + \tan^{-1} y = \tan^{-1}(\frac{x+y}{1-xy})$, especially when $xy > 1$ where a $\pi$ shift is mandatory.

Generally, 2 to 4 direct or composite questions appear in every examination cycle, often integrated with matrices, calculus differentiation, or equation solving.

Master standard triangle conversions (e.g., converting $\tan^{-1}$ to $\sin^{-1}$ or $\cos^{-1}$ instantly), memorize standard principal branches, and use back-substitution of option values.
