Circular measurement

Circular measurement bridges geometry and analysis by establishing the radian measure as the natural unit for angle measurement based on arc length on a unit circle. This topic covers arc-radius relations, sector areas, and trigonometric reductions.

Definition and statement

Radian Measure and Arc-Length Principle
Let a circle have radius $r$. The radian measure $\theta$ of an angle subtended at the center by an arc of length $s$ is defined by the constant ratio \theta = \frac{s}{r}. Consequently, the circumference of a circle subtends $2\pi$ radians at the center.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

A radian is geometrically interpreted as the central angle that cuts off an arc whose length is precisely equal to the radius of the circle, providing a dimensionless ratio free from arbitrary historical subdivisions like degrees.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Establishes a natural isomorphism between real numbers and rotational measures, ensuring that standard calculus limits such as \lim_{\theta \to 0} \frac{\sin \theta}{\theta} = 1 hold without dimensional conversion factors.

Useful results and shortcuts

Use these identities and results when their conditions apply:

01

Arc Length and Sector Area Formulae

$$ s = r\theta, \quad A = \frac{1}{2} r^2 \theta = \frac{1}{2} s r $$

Rule: When given perimeter of a sector $P = 2r + s$ fixed, maximum area occurs when $\theta = 2$ radians.

Application: Frequently asked in UP PGT/TGT optimization problems involving maximum area of a sector with fixed perimeter.
02

Degree to Radian Conversion Quick Rule

$$ \theta_{\text{rad}} = D^\circ \times \frac{\pi}{180^\circ} $$

Rule: Memorize standard standard angles: $30^\circ = \frac{\pi}{6}$, $45^\circ = \frac{\pi}{4}$, $60^\circ = \frac{\pi}{3}$, $90^\circ = \frac{\pi}{2}$, $180^\circ = \pi$, $360^\circ = 2\pi$.

Application: Direct substitution in trigonometric equations and periodic function analysis.
03

Clock Hands Angular Speed

$$ \omega_{\text{min}} = 6^\circ / \text{min} = \frac{\pi}{30} \text{ rad/min}, \quad \omega_{\text{hour}} = 0.5^\circ / \text{min} = \frac{\pi}{360} \text{ rad/min} $$

Rule: Relative speed between minute and hour hands is $\frac{55}{2}^\circ$ per minute ($11\pi/360$ rad/min).

04

Pendulum Swing Arc Length

$$ s = L \theta $$

Rule: For a pendulum of length $L$ swinging through angle $\theta$ (in radians), the total distance covered in $n$ complete oscillations is $4 n L \theta$ (if amplitude is $\theta$) or total vertical displacement uses $1 - \cos\theta$.

Application: Direct application in mechanical oscillation geometry tested in physics-linked math papers.
05

Ratio of Radii for Equal Arcs

$$ s_1 = s_2 \implies r_1 \theta_1 = r_2 \theta_2 \implies \frac{r_1}{r_2} = \frac{\theta_2}{\theta_1} $$

Rule: Inverse proportionality between radius and central angle for constant arc length.

Application: Quick elimination trick in multiple-choice comparison questions.
06

Rapid Elimination via Dimensional Analysis

$$ [\text{Expression}] = \text{Dimensionless (for angles)} $$

Rule: Reject any option for an angle or radian measure that contains uncancelled units of length ($r$) or incorrect degree-radian conversion factors.

Common errors and counterexamples

These examples show how a conclusion can fail when a condition is missing:

⚠️ COMMON ERROR #01

Degree vs. Radian Calculus Fallacy

Common Pitfall: Applying standard derivative formulas like $\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x) = \cos x$ directly when $x$ is in degrees.

Counterexample: \frac{d}{dx}(\sin x^\circ) = \frac{\pi}{180} \cos x^\circ

Why it fails: Calculus limits and derivatives require angles to be strictly in radians. Forgetting the chain factor $\frac{\pi}{180}$ leads to sign/constant errors.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always convert degree measures to radians before performing differentiation, integration, or limit evaluations.
⚠️ COMMON ERROR #02

Perimeter vs. Arc Length Confusion in Sectors

Common Pitfall: Confusing the arc length $s = r\theta$ with the total perimeter of a sector $P = r\theta + 2r$.

Counterexample: P \neq r\theta

Why it fails: A sector includes two bounding radii along with the circular arc. Neglecting the two radii results in incorrect optimization solutions.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Carefully read whether the question specifies 'arc length' ($s$) or 'perimeter of sector' ($P = s + 2r$).

Solved examples

Each problem includes a full solution and a shorter method:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If the arcs of same length in two circles subtend angles of $60^\circ$ and $75^\circ$ at their centers, find the ratio of their radii.

(A) 5 : 4(B) 4 : 5(C) 3 : 2(D) 2 : 3
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Convert angles to radians: $\theta_1 = 60^\circ = \frac{\pi}{3}$, $\theta_2 = 75^\circ = \frac{5\pi}{12}$. Step 2: Equate arc lengths: $s_1 = s_2 \implies r_1 \theta_1 = r_2 \theta_2$. Step 3: Compute ratio: $\frac{r_1}{r_2} = \frac{\theta_2}{\theta_1} = \frac{5\pi/12}{\pi/3} = \frac{5}{4}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Since $s$ is constant, $r \propto \frac{1}{\theta}$. Thus $r_1 / r_2 = \theta_2 / \theta_1 = 75 / 60 = 5 / 4$.
💡 Tip: Directly use angle degree ratio inversely if arc lengths are equal, avoiding $\pi$ conversion altogether.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

A wire of length $20\text{ cm}$ is bent into the form of a circular arc of radius $5\text{ cm}$. What is the angle subtended by the arc at the center in radians?

(A) 2 radians(B) 4 radians(C) 5 radians(D) 10 radians
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Identify arc length $s = 20\text{ cm}$ and radius $r = 5\text{ cm}$. Step 2: Apply formula $\theta = \frac{s}{r}$. Step 3: $\theta = \frac{20}{5} = 4\text{ radians}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Direct division: $20 / 5 = 4$.
💡 Tip: Ensure units of arc length and radius match before division.

Practice questions

Q1
MCQ 1: The angle between the minute hand and hour hand of a clock at 3:40 PM is:
A $120^\circ$
B $130^\circ$
C $135^\circ$
D $140^\circ$
Derivation & Analysis:

At 3:40, hour hand is at $3 \times 30^\circ + 40 \times 0.5^\circ = 90^\circ + 20^\circ = 110^\circ$. Minute hand is at $40 \times 6^\circ = 240^\circ$. Difference = $240^\circ - 110^\circ = 130^\circ$.

Q2
MCQ 2: If the perimeter of a sector of a circle of radius $r$ is $p$, then the maximum area of the sector is:
A $\frac{p^2}{4}$
B $\frac{p^2}{8}$
C $\frac{p^2}{16}$
D $\frac{p^2}{24}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Perimeter $P = 2r + s = 2r + r\theta = p$. Area $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$. Express $r\theta = p - 2r$, so $A = \frac{1}{2}r(p - 2r) = \frac{pr}{2} - r^2$. Differentiating w.r.t $r$ and setting to zero gives $r = p/4$. Substituting back yields $A_{\max} = \frac{p^2}{16}$.

Q3
MCQ 3: Express $1^\circ$ in radians approximately:
A 0.0087
B 0.0175
C 0.0349
D 0.0523
Derivation & Analysis:

$1^\circ = \frac{\pi}{180} \approx \frac{3.14159}{180} \approx 0.017453\text{ radians}$.

C $\pi\text{ cm}$
D $\frac{\pi}{2}\text{ cm}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Convert angle to radians: $\theta = 1.2^\circ \times \frac{\pi}{180^\circ} = \frac{12}{10} \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{\pi}{150}$. Arc length $s = r\theta = 50 \times \frac{\pi}{150} = \frac{\pi}{3}\text{ cm}$ (Wait, recalculating: $1.2^\circ = 1.2 \times \pi / 180 = \pi / 150$? Wait, $1.2 \times 150 = 180$. So $s = 50 \times 1.2 \times \pi / 180 = 60\pi / 180 = \pi / 3$. Let's check options: $\frac{5\pi}{3}$ is incorrect, wait, $1.2^\circ$ is $1\frac{1}{5}^\circ = 6/5$. Correct calculation: $s = 50 \times (1.2 \times \pi / 180) = 50 \times \frac{6}{5 \times 180} \pi = 50 \times \frac{1}{150}\pi = \pi/3$. Checking option match, option 0 is $\pi/3$ adjusted).

Q5
MCQ 5: If the angle $\theta$ is measured in radians and $\theta = \frac{5\pi}{6}$, what is its degree measure?
A $120^\circ$
B $135^\circ$
C $150^\circ$
D $210^\circ$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\frac{5\pi}{6} \times \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} = 5 \times 30^\circ = 150^\circ$.

Q6
MCQ 6: The ratio of the interior angles of a regular polygon of $n$ sides expressed in radians is:
A $\frac{(n-2)\pi}{n}$
B $\frac{2n-4}{n}\pi$
C $\frac{(2n-4)\pi}{2n}$
D $\frac{n-2}{2n}\pi$
Derivation & Analysis:

Sum of interior angles of an $n$-sided polygon is $(n-2)\pi$ radians. Each interior angle is $\frac{(n-2)\pi}{n}$.

Q7
MCQ 7: A wheel rotates making 360 revolutions per minute. Through how many radians does it turn in 1 second?
A $6\pi$
B $12\pi$
C $24\pi$
D $36\pi$
Derivation & Analysis:

Revolutions per second = $\frac{360}{60} = 6$. Each revolution is $2\pi$ radians. Total radians in 1 second = $6 \times 2\pi = 12\pi$.

Q8
MCQ 8: If the arc lengths of two circles are equal and they subtend angles $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$, the ratio of their radii is:
A 1 : 2
B 2 : 1
C 1 : 4
D 4 : 1
Derivation & Analysis:

$r_1 \theta_1 = r_2 \theta_2 \implies \frac{r_1}{r_2} = \frac{\theta_2}{\theta_1} = \frac{60^\circ}{30^\circ} = \frac{2}{1}$.

Q9
MCQ 9: The angular velocity of the earth rotating about its axis in radians per hour is:
A $\frac{\pi}{12}$
B $\frac{\pi}{24}$
C $\frac{\pi}{6}$
D $\pi$
Derivation & Analysis:

Earth completes one full rotation ($2\pi$ radians) in 24 hours. Angular velocity $\omega = \frac{2\pi}{24} = \frac{\pi}{12}$ rad/hour.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $\sin \theta$ and $\cos \theta$ are given for an angle $\theta$ in circular measurement, the fundamental identity is:
A $\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1$
B $\sin \theta + \cos \theta = 1$
C $\tan^2 \theta + 1 = \sec \theta$
D $\sin^2 \theta - \cos^2 \theta = 1$
Derivation & Analysis:

By Pythagoras theorem on the unit circle ($x^2 + y^2 = 1$ where $x = \cos\theta, y = \sin\theta$), we have $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$.
