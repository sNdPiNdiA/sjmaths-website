Real Analysis

Cauchy's theorem on limits

Cauchy's theorem on limits establishes that if a sequence \{a_n\} converges to a limit $L$, then its arithmetic mean sequence also converges to $L$. This foundational principle bridges sequential limits and average behavior, frequently appearing in competitive examinations like UP PGT/TGT and DSSSB to test advanced limit evaluation techniques.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Cauchy's First Theorem on Limits
If $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = l$, then $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n}{n} = l$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Visually, as the terms of a convergent sequence cluster infinitely close to the limiting value $l$, their running arithmetic average is pulled toward the same limit $l$, smoothing out individual fluctuations.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It provides a powerful analytical tool to evaluate limits of complex summation expressions involving sequences, converting difficult Cesàro sums into straightforward limit computations of the underlying sequence.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Existence of Sequence Limit N/A Strictly Mandatory / Necessary If $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n$ does not exist (e.g., $a_n = (-1)^n$), the Cesàro mean may still exist or oscillate, meaning the converse of Cauchy's theorem is false.
Finiteness of Limit $l$ N/A Strictly Mandatory If $l$ is infinite, specialized extensions apply, but standard formulation assumes a finite limit or requires careful adaptation for improper limits.
Summation Denominator Growth N/A Essential for conclusion If the divisor does not grow unboundedly, the average limit ratio is undefined or indeterminate.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Cesàro Mean for $n^{-p} \sum k^{p-1}$

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n^{p}} \sum_{k=1}^{n} k^{p-1} = \frac{1}{p} $$

Exam Rule: For sums of powers divided by the next higher power, the limit is simply the reciprocal of the exponent $p$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT and KVS PGT examinations for evaluating limits of sums.
02

Shortcut 2: Geometric Sequence Average

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^{n} r^k = 0 \quad (\text{for } |r| < 1) $$

Exam Rule: The arithmetic mean of any geometric progression with common ratio strictly less than 1 vanishes in the limit.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently used in DSSSB PGT Mathematics to quickly evaluate infinite series limits.
03

Shortcut 3: Logarithmic Limit Conversion

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} (n!)^{1/n} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n}{e} $$

Exam Rule: Utilize Cauchy's second theorem on limits ($\lim \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}$) to evaluate factorial-based root limits instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard recurring question pattern in LT Grade and UP TGT exams.
04

Shortcut 4: Bounded Sequence with Null Factor

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n}{n} = l \text{ if } a_n \to l $$

Exam Rule: Do not attempt $\epsilon-N$ proofs in exam; substitute the asymptotic value directly into the general term if convergence is established.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves critical time during 125-question multiple choice tests.
05

Shortcut 5: Stolz-Cesàro Theorem Analogue

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{x_n}{y_n} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{x_n - x_{n-1}}{y_n - y_{n-1}} $$

Exam Rule: For indeterminate forms $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ with monotonic sequences, take differences instead of derivatives (discrete L'Hôpital's rule).

⚡ PYQ Context: Highly favored in premium PGT screening tests for ratio limit evaluations.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Divergent Counterpart

$$ a_n = (-1)^n \implies \text{limit D.N.E., but mean } \to 0 $$

Exam Rule: If the sequence oscillates, check if the mean converges; if the question assumes converse, eliminate immediately.

⚡ PYQ Context: Instant option elimination in assertion-reasoning items.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Assuming the Converse is True

Common Pitfall: Candidates mistakenly assume that if the arithmetic mean $\frac{1}{n}\sum a_n$ converges to $l$, then the sequence $a_n$ must also converge to $l$.

Counterexample: $a_n = (-1)^n$

Why it fails: The sequence $a_n = (-1)^n$ oscillates between $-1$ and $1$ and has no limit, yet its arithmetic mean sequence converges to $0$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Never apply Cauchy's theorem in reverse; the convergence of the mean does not imply the convergence of the sequence.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Misidentifying Cauchy's First and Second Theorems

Common Pitfall: Confusing Cauchy's First Theorem (arithmetic mean) with Cauchy's Second Theorem (geometric mean: if $a_n > 0$ and $\lim \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = l$, then $\lim (a_n)^{1/n} = l$).

Counterexample: $a_n = n$

Why it fails: Applying arithmetic mean rules to geometric mean problems leads to false limit valuations.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check whether the expression involves a sum (First Theorem) or a product/radical (Second Theorem).

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Evaluate the limit: $\lim_{n \to \infty} \left( \frac{1}{n+1} + \frac{1}{n+2} + \dots + \frac{1}{2n} \right)$.

(A) $0$(B) $\log 2$(C) $1$(D) $\infty$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Rewrite the expression as a Riemann sum: $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{r=1}^{n} \frac{1}{1 + r/n} = \int_{0}^{1} \frac{1}{1+x} \, dx = [\log(1+x)]_0^1 = \log 2$. Alternatively, apply Cauchy-Stolz theorem.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize standard definite integral limit form $\frac{1}{n}\sum f(r/n) \to \int_0^1 f(x)dx$ in under 20 seconds.
💡 Key Takeaway: A absolute classic in UP PGT exams. Memorize the result $\log 2$ for this exact series.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $a_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} + \dots + \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \right)$, find $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n$.

(A) $0$(B) $1$(C) $2$(D) Does not exist
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Apply Stolz-Cesàro theorem. Let $x_n = \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}$ and $y_n = \sqrt{n}$. Then $\frac{x_n - x_{n-1}}{y_n - y_{n-1}} = \frac{1/\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{n} - \sqrt{n-1}} = \frac{1/\sqrt{n}}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n} + \sqrt{n-1}}} = \frac{\sqrt{n} + \sqrt{n-1}}{\sqrt{n}} = 1 + \sqrt{1 - 1/n} \to 2$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Use the asymptotic expansion of harmonic/radical sums where $\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}} \sim 2\sqrt{n}$, yielding $\frac{2\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{n}} = 2$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Whenever denominator is $\sqrt{n}$ and numerator is radical sum, Stolz-Cesàro resolves it instantly.

A 3
B 4

Rewrite as $\frac{1}{2} \frac{\sum a_k}{n} + \frac{5}{2}$. By Cauchy's theorem, $\frac{\sum a_k}{n} \to 3$. Thus, $\frac{1}{2}(3) + \frac{5}{2} = \frac{8}{2} = 4$.

Derivation & Analysis:

Stolz-Cesàro theorem is the discrete analogue of L'Hôpital's rule, dealing with indeterminate forms $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ for sequence ratios.
