Real Analysis

Monotonic sequences

A monotonic sequence is one that moves exclusively in a single direction—either non-decreasing or non-increasing—forming the structural bedrock of real analysis and convergence theory. Mastery of monotonic sequences and their boundedness is a highest-yield scoring zone in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations, frequently appearing as direct convergence tests or limit evaluations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Monotone Convergence Theorem (MCT) for Sequences
Every bounded monotonic sequence of real numbers is convergent. Specifically, if $\langle x_n \rangle$ is monotonically increasing and bounded above, then $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = \sup \{x_n : n \in \mathbb{N}\}$. If $\langle x_n \rangle$ is monotonically decreasing and bounded below, then $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = \inf \{x_n : n \in \mathbb{N}\}.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, the terms of the sequence either constantly march upwards or downwards on the real number line while being trapped by a finite barrier. Because they cannot jump past their supremum or infimum and never reverse direction, they must accumulate and settle precisely at their least upper bound or greatest lower bound.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It bridges the algebraic order structure of the real numbers $\mathbb{R}$ with its topological completeness property. It provides a way to establish convergence without ever needing to explicitly compute or guess the limiting value beforehand.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Ratio Test for Monotonicity

$$ \frac{x_{n+1}}{x_n} \ge 1 \implies \text{Increasing (for } x_n > 0\text{)}, \quad \frac{x_{n+1}}{x_n} \le 1 \implies \text{Decreasing (for } x_n > 0\text{)} $$

Exam Rule: For positive term sequences, compute $x_{n+1}/x_n$ directly. If the ratio $\ge 1$ for all $n$, the sequence is monotonically increasing. Avoid tedious subtraction for radical or factorial terms.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT for recursive sequences like $x_{n+1} = \sqrt{c + x_n}$.
02

Shortcut 2: Boundedness via Induction & First Two Terms

$$ \text{If } x_1 < x_2 \text{ and } x_{n+1} = f(x_n) \text{ with } f \text{ increasing, then } \langle x_n \rangle \text{ is increasing.} $$

Exam Rule: Check $x_1$ and $x_2$. If $x_2 \ge x_1$ and $f$ is an increasing function, the sequence is monotonically increasing. The upper bound is usually the positive root of the characteristic equation $x = f(x)$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard pattern in DSSSB and KVS PGT for determining limits of recursive sequences.
03

Shortcut 3: Limit of Monotone Recursive Sequences ($x_{n+1} = \sqrt{k + x_n}$)

$$ L = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4k}}{2} $$

Exam Rule: For sequences defined by $x_1 > 0$ and $x_{n+1} = \sqrt{k + x_n}$, the limit is always the positive root of $L^2 - L - k = 0$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct time-saver formula tested repeatedly in UP TGT/PGT exams.
04

Shortcut 4: Exponential vs. Factorial Growth Monotonicity

$$ \frac{x_{n+1}}{x_n} = \frac{a^{n+1}/(n+1)!}{a^n/n!} = \frac{a}{n+1} \to 0 < 1 $$

Exam Rule: For sequences involving $n!$ and $a^n$, evaluate $x_{n+1}/x_n$. For large $n$, this ratio drops below $1$, proving the sequence is eventually monotonically decreasing and converges to $0$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard convergence and monotonicity MCQ in LT Grade exams.
05

Shortcut 5: Algebraic Difference Test

$$ x_{n+1} - x_n = g(n) $$

Exam Rule: If $x_{n+1} - x_n \ge 0$ for all $n \ge N$, the sequence is eventually monotonically increasing. Check the sign of the difference function directly by treating $n$ as a continuous variable $x$ and finding its derivative.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used for rational polynomial sequence terms in KVS PGT.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Boundedness

$$ \text{Divergent options if unbounded} $$

Exam Rule: If a sequence is monotonically increasing and you spot a term that grows without bound or exceeds the prospective upper bound test, immediately eliminate options suggesting convergence to a finite value.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial for time management across 125 questions in UP PGT.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Monotonicity without Boundedness

Common Pitfall: Assuming every monotonic sequence converges.

Counterexample: $x_n = n$ (Monotonically increasing)

Why it fails: The sequence is strictly increasing, but it is not bounded above; hence it diverges to $+\infty$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always verify both monotonicity AND boundedness before applying the Monotone Convergence Theorem.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Boundedness Alone Implies Convergence

Common Pitfall: Assuming any bounded sequence is convergent without checking monotonicity.

Counterexample: $x_n = (-1)^n$ on $[-1, 1]$

Why it fails: It is bounded between $-1$ and $1$, but it oscillates infinitely and is not monotonic, so it does not converge.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Boundedness guarantees the existence of convergent subsequences (Bolzano-Weierstrass), but not the convergence of the sequence itself unless monotonicity is present.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Determine the limit of the sequence defined recursively by $x_1 = \sqrt{2}$ and $x_{n+1} = \sqrt{2 + x_n}$ for $n \ge 1$.

(A) $1$(B) $2$(C) $\sqrt{2}$(D) $\infty$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Show $x_n < 2$ by mathematical induction. Step 2: Show $x_{n+1} > x_n$ (monotonically increasing). Step 3: By MCT, limit $L$ exists. Step 4: Solve $L = \sqrt{2 + L} \implies L^2 - L - 2 = 0 \implies L = 2$ (discarding negative root).
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Apply Shortcut 3 directly: $k = 2$, solve $L = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4(2)}}{2} = \frac{1 + 3}{2} = 2$. Takes 10 seconds.
💡 Key Takeaway: Look for nested radical recursive patterns. The limit is always the positive root of $L^2 - L - k = 0$.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $x_n = \frac{1}{n+1} + \frac{1}{n+2} + \dots + \frac{1}{2n}$, then the sequence $\langle x_n \rangle$ is:

(A) Monotonically increasing and divergent(B) Monotonically increasing and convergent(C) Monotonically decreasing and convergent(D) Not monotonic
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Compute $x_{n+1} - x_n = \frac{1}{2n+1} + \frac{1}{2n+2} - \frac{1}{n+1} = \frac{1}{2n+1} - \frac{1}{2n+2} > 0$. Thus it is monotonically increasing. Boundedness by $1$ is verified, confirming convergence.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize harmonic sum differences. The difference is positive, establishing strict increase. Bounded above by $\ln 2$, so it converges.
💡 Key Takeaway: Harmonic tail sums of this form are classic UP PGT favorites.

Q1
MCQ 1: Every bounded monotonic sequence in $\mathbb{R}$ is:
A (A) Divergent
B (B) Convergent
C (C) Oscillatory
D (D) Periodic
Derivation & Analysis:

This is the direct statement of the Monotone Convergence Theorem (MCT).

Q2
MCQ 2: The sequence $x_n = \frac{n}{n+1}$ is:
A (A) Monotonically decreasing
B (B) Monotonically increasing and bounded
C (C) Unbounded
D (D) Oscillating
Derivation & Analysis:

$x_{n+1} - x_n = \frac{n+1}{n+2} - \frac{n}{n+1} = \frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)} > 0$, so it is increasing, and $x_n < 1$, so it is bounded.

Q3
MCQ 3: If $x_1 = 1$ and $x_{n+1} = \frac{1}{3}(x_n + 1)$, then $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n$ is equal to:
A (A) $\frac{1}{2}$
B (B) $1$
C (C) $\frac{1}{3}$
D (D) $2$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $L = \frac{1}{3}(L + 1) \implies 3L = L + 1 \implies 2L = 1 \implies L = \frac{1}{2}$.

Q4
MCQ 4: Which of the following sequences is monotonically decreasing?
A (A) $x_n = n^2$
B (B) $x_n = 2^n$
C (C) $x_n = \frac{1}{n}$
D (D) $x_n = \ln(n)$
Derivation & Analysis:

As $n$ increases, $\frac{1}{n}$ decreases strictly, satisfying $x_{n+1} < x_n$.

Q5
MCQ 5: A monotonic sequence which is not bounded above:
A (A) Converges to $0$
B (B) Diverges to $+\infty$
C (C) Converges to $1$
D (D) Oscillates finitely
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, an increasing sequence not bounded above tends to $+\infty$.

Q6
MCQ 6: The sequence $x_n = (-1)^n \cdot n$ is:
A (A) Monotone and bounded
B (B) Monotone and unbounded
C (C) Not monotone and unbounded
D (D) Convergent
Derivation & Analysis:

Terms alternate signs and grow in magnitude, making it non-monotonic and unbounded.

Q7
MCQ 7: If $x_n = \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n$, then the sequence $\langle x_n \rangle$ is:
A (A) Monotonically decreasing
B (B) Monotonically increasing and bounded above by $3$
C (C) Unbounded
D (D) Oscillatory
Derivation & Analysis:

This is Euler's sequence defining $e$, which is strictly increasing and bounded above by $3$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): Every convergent sequence is monotonic. Reason (R): Limit exists for monotonic bounded sequences.
A (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (C) (A) is true but (R) is false
D (D) (A) is false and (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

Assertion (A) is false because convergent sequences (e.g., $\frac{(-1)^n}{n}$) need not be monotonic. Reason (R) is true.

Q9
MCQ 9: The limit of the sequence $x_n = \sqrt{12 + \sqrt{12 + \sqrt{12 + \dots}}}$ is:
A (A) $3$
B (B) $4$
C (C) $6$
D (D) $12$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using Shortcut 3 with $k=12$: $L^2 - L - 12 = 0 \implies (L-4)(L+3) = 0 \implies L = 4$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If a sequence $\langle x_n \rangle$ is bounded below and monotonically decreasing, then its limit is:
A (A) Supremum
B (B) Infimum
C (C) Zero
D (D) First term $x_1$
Derivation & Analysis:

By the Monotone Convergence Theorem, a monotonically decreasing sequence bounded below converges to its greatest lower bound (infimum).
