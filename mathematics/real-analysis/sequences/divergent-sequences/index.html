Real Analysis

Divergent sequences

Divergent sequences are sequences of real numbers that do not converge to any finite limit, either growing without bound, oscillating infinitely, or tending to infinity or negative infinity. Mastering their behavior, asymptotic growth rates, and Cesàro or subsequence properties is a high-yield scoring zone in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Definition and Characterization of Divergence to Infinity ($\pm \infty$)
A sequence $\langle x_n \rangle$ diverges to positive infinity, written as $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = +\infty$ (or $x_n \to \infty$), if for every real number $M > 0$, however large, there exists a positive integer $N = N(M)$ such that $x_n > M$ for all $n \ge N$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, as you move further along the sequence index $n$ on the horizontal axis, the terms $x_n$ eventually rise above any arbitrarily chosen high horizontal barrier $y = M$ and never fall back below it.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Provides the formal arithmetic foundation for manipulating limits involving infinity, reciprocal relations ($x_n \to \infty \iff \frac{1}{x_n} \to 0$ for $x_n > 0$), and growth rate comparisons among polynomials, exponentials, and factorials.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Polynomial Growth Dominance

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_k n^k + a_{k-1}n^{k-1} + \dots + a_0}{b_j n^j + b_{j-1}n^{j-1} + \dots + b_0} = \begin{cases} \pm\infty & k > j \\ \frac{a_k}{b_j} & k = j \\ 0 & k < j \end{cases} $$

Exam Rule: For rational sequences, compare the highest degree of numerator ($k$) and denominator ($j$) instantly by inspecting leading terms.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT and TGT examinations to evaluate limit behavior in under 15 seconds.
02

Shortcut 2: Exponential vs. Polynomial Divergence Rate

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n^k}{a^n} = 0 \quad (a > 1, \, k \in \mathbb{R}) $$

Exam Rule: Exponential growth of base $a > 1$ strictly outpaces any polynomial growth $n^k$; hence $a^n / n^k \to +\infty$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in KVS and NVS PGT assertions regarding the relative speed of divergent sequences.
03

Shortcut 3: Factorial Dominance

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a^n}{n!} = 0 \quad (\forall a \in \mathbb{R}) $$

Exam Rule: Factorial $n!$ grows faster than any exponential $a^n$, meaning $n! / a^n \to +\infty$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard comparative limit test appearing in DSSSB PGT Mathematics.
04

Shortcut 4: Stirling's Asymptotic Divergence Approximation

$$ n! \sim \sqrt{2\pi n} \left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^n $$

Exam Rule: Use Stirling's approximation to evaluate complex factorial ratios in divergent sequence limits.

⚡ PYQ Context: Advanced UP PGT questions involving factorial limits and infinite products.
05

Shortcut 5: Root Test / Ratio Test Link for Sequence Growth

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{x_{n+1}}{x_n}\right| = L > 1 \implies |x_n| \to +\infty $$

Exam Rule: If the successive ratio exceeds $1$, the sequence diverges in magnitude.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently bridges sequence divergence and infinite series radius of convergence problems.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Oscillation Elimination

$$ x_n = (-1)^n n $$

Exam Rule: An unbounded sequence with alternating signs diverges by oscillation (neither $+\infty$ nor $-\infty$), eliminating improper divergence options.

⚡ PYQ Context: Eliminates distractor options in multiple-choice questions regarding unbounded vs. properly divergent sequences.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Unboundedness vs. Divergence to Infinity Confusion

Common Pitfall: Assuming every unbounded sequence diverges to $+\infty$ or $-\infty$.

Counterexample: $x_n = (-1)^n n$

Why it fails: The sequence is unbounded above and below, but it oscillates infinitely between positive and negative values without tending to $+\infty$ or $-\infty$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Unboundedness is necessary but NOT sufficient for proper divergence to $\pm \infty$. Always check sign persistence for large $n$.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Algebraic Operations on Infinity ($\infty - \infty$)

Common Pitfall: Treating $\infty$ as a real number and evaluating indeterminate forms like $\infty - \infty$ or $0 \cdot \infty$ directly.

Counterexample: $x_n = (n+1) - n = 1 \text{ vs } y_n = (2n) - n = n

Why it fails: Expressions like $\infty - \infty$ are indeterminate; the limit depends entirely on the algebraic structure before taking limits.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always rationalize, factor out dominant terms, or apply L'Hôpital's rule (via Stolz-Cesàro theorem) before concluding.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Determine the limit behavior of the sequence $x_n = \frac{n^2 + 3n + 2}{2n^2 - 1}$ as $n \to \infty$.

(A) $1/2$(B) $1$(C) $2$(D) $+\infty$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Divide numerator and denominator by highest power $n^2$: $x_n = \frac{1 + 3/n + 2/n^2}{2 - 1/n^2}$. Taking limit as $n \to \infty$ yields $\frac{1 + 0 + 0}{2 - 0} = \frac{1}{2}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Ratio of leading coefficients: $a_2 = 1, b_2 = 2 \implies \frac{1}{2}$. Takes 5 seconds.
💡 Key Takeaway: Check degrees of numerator and denominator first. If equal, the answer is simply the ratio of leading coefficients.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Identify the nature of the sequence $x_n = n^{1/n}$ as $n \to \infty$.

(A) Diverges to $+\infty$(B) Converges to $1$(C) Oscillates finitely(D) Converges to $0$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Let $x_n = n^{1/n}$. Write $n^{1/n} = e^{\frac{\ln n}{n}}$. Since $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\ln n}{n} = 0$, by continuity of the exponential function, $\lim_{n \to \infty} n^{1/n} = e^0 = 1$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Standard fundamental limit memorization: $\lim n^{1/n} = 1$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Direct standard result frequently asked across UP TGT/PGT exams.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
MCQ 1: Which of the following sequences diverges to $+\infty$?
A $x_n = \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$
B $x_n = \ln(n)$
C $x_n = \sin(n)$
D $x_n = \frac{1}{n^2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

The logarithmic function $\ln(n)$ grows without bound as $n \to \infty$, whereas $\frac{(-1)^n}{n}$ and $\frac{1}{n^2}$ converge to $0$, and $\sin(n)$ oscillates between $-1$ and $1$.

Q2
MCQ 2: The sequence $x_n = \frac{n!}{2^n}$ behaves as follows:
A Converges to $0$
B Converges to $1/2$
C Diverges to $+\infty$
D Oscillates finitely
Derivation & Analysis:

Factorial growth strictly dominates exponential growth. Therefore, $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n!}{2^n} = +\infty$.

Q3
MCQ 3: If a sequence $\langle x_n \rangle$ diverges to $+\infty$, then the sequence $\langle \frac{1}{x_n} \rangle$ (for $x_n \neq 0$):
A Diverges to $+\infty$
B Converges to $1$
C Converges to $0$
D Diverges to $-\infty$
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition of limits to infinity, if $x_n \to +\infty$, then for any $\epsilon > 0$, we can choose $M = 1/\epsilon$, so $x_n > 1/\epsilon \implies 0 < \frac{1}{x_n} < \epsilon$, meaning $\lim \frac{1}{x_n} = 0$.

Q4
MCQ 4: The sequence $x_n = (-1)^n n$ is:
A Convergent
B Divergent to $+\infty$
C Divergent to $-\infty$
D Unbounded and oscillatory divergent
Derivation & Analysis:

The terms alternate in sign and grow in magnitude ($1, -2, 3, -4, \dots$). The sequence is unbounded and does not tend to $+\infty$ or $-\infty$, hence it is oscillatory divergent.

Q5
MCQ 5: What is $\lim_{n \to \infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{n} \right)^{2n}$?
A $e$
B $e^2$
C $1$
D $+\infty$
Derivation & Analysis:

We know $\lim_{n \to \infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{n} \right)^n = e$. Raising the expression to the power of $2$ gives $e^2$.

Q6
MCQ 6: If $x_n > 0$ for all $n$ and $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{x_{n+1}}{x_n} = L > 1$, then $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n$ is:
A $0$
B $1$
C $+\infty$
D Does not exist (oscillatory)
Derivation & Analysis:

By the ratio test corollary for sequences, if successive terms grow by a factor greater than $1$ in the limit, the sequence diverges to $+\infty$.

Q7
MCQ 7: Which sequence satisfies $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = +\infty$ by root test comparison?
A $x_n = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^n$
B $x_n = \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n$
C $x_n = \frac{n}{n+1}$
D $x_n = \frac{\cos n}{n}$
Derivation & Analysis:

For $x_n = (\frac{3}{2})^n$, the base is greater than $1$, so $(3/2)^n \to +\infty$. The root test gives $\lim (x_n)^{1/n} = \frac{3}{2} > 1$, confirming divergence to $+\infty$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): Every bounded sequence is divergent. Reason (R): Boundedness precludes a sequence from tending to infinity.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

Assertion (A) is false because bounded sequences can converge (e.g., $1/n$). Reason (R) is true because a sequence tending to $+\infty$ must be unbounded above.

Q9
MCQ 9: Evaluate $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n^3 - 2n + 1}{3n^3 + 5n^2}$:
A $1/3$
B $0$
C $+\infty$
D $3$
Derivation & Analysis:

Both numerator and denominator are polynomials of degree $3$. The limit is the ratio of their leading coefficients: $\frac{1}{3}$.

Q10
MCQ 10: Stolz-Cesàro Theorem is applicable to evaluate the limit of sequence ratios when the denominator sequence is:
A Bounded and convergent
B Strictly monotonic and divergent to $+\infty$
C Oscillating between finite bounds
D Converging to $0$
Derivation & Analysis:

Stolz-Cesàro theorem (the discrete analogue of L'Hôpital's rule) requires the denominator sequence to be strictly monotonic and divergent to $+\infty$.
