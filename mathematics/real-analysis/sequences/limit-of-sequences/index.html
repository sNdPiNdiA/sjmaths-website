Real Analysis

Limit of sequences

The limit of a sequence is the fundamental analytical concept defining the behavior of terms $x_n$ as $n$ tends to infinity. Mastery of $\epsilon-N$ definitions, algebra of limits, and convergence criteria forms a high-scoring bedrock for UP PGT/PGR, TGT, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Definition of Convergence of a Sequence
A sequence $\langle x_n \rangle$ of real numbers is said to converge to a real number $l$ if for every $\epsilon > 0$, however small, there exists a positive integer $N = N(\epsilon)$ such that $|x_n - l| < \epsilon$ for all $n \ge N$. Symbolically, $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = l$ or $x_n \to l as n \to \infty$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, for any arbitrarily small strip $(l - \epsilon, l + \epsilon)$ around the limit $l$, all terms of the sequence from some point $N$ onwards lie strictly inside this neighborhood.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It translates infinite asymptotic behavior into finite arithmetic bounds using inequalities, enabling rigorous proofs of continuity, series convergence, and bounds.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Algebraic Growth Rate Hierarchy

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{(\ln n)^k}{n^p} = 0 \quad (p > 0, k > 0), \quad \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n^k}{a^n} = 0 \quad (a > 1) $$

Exam Rule: Logarithms grow slower than polynomials, polynomials grow slower than exponentials, and exponentials grow slower than factorials ($n!$).

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly used in UP PGT/TGT to find limits of ratios containing mixed growth terms instantly without L'Hôpital's rule extensions.
02

Shortcut 2: Cauchy's First Theorem on Limits (Cesàro Means)

$$ \text{If } \lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = l, \text{ then } \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n}{n} = l $$

Exam Rule: The limit of the arithmetic mean of sequence terms equals the limit of the terms themselves.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in DSSSB and KVS PGT for complex summation limits.
03

Shortcut 3: Cauchy's Second Theorem on Limits

$$ \text{If } x_n > 0 \text{ and } \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{x_{n+1}}{x_n} = l, \text{ then } \lim_{n \to \infty} (x_n)^{1/n} = l $$

Exam Rule: Convert $n$-th root limits into ratio limits to simplify radical evaluations.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard pattern in UP PGT for evaluating limits like $\lim_{n \to \infty} (n!)^{1/n} / n$.
04

Shortcut 4: Squeeze (Sandwich) Theorem for Sequences

$$ \text{If } a_n \le x_n \le b_n \text{ and } \lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = \lim_{n \to \infty} b_n = l, \text{ then } \lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = l $$

Exam Rule: Bound complicated expressions by two simple sequences sharing the same limit.

⚡ PYQ Context: Tested in every competitive exam for expressions like $\lim_{n \to \infty} \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n^2+1}} + \dots + \frac{1}{\sqrt{n^2+n}}\right)$.
05

Shortcut 5: Monotone Convergence Theorem (MCT) Quick Test

$$ \text{Bounded + Monotone} \implies \text{Convergent} $$

Exam Rule: For recursively defined sequences $x_{n+1} = f(x_n)$, check monotonicity by $x_{n+1} - x_n$ or $x_{n+1}/x_n$, then solve $l = f(l)$ for the limit.

⚡ PYQ Context: Guaranteed 1 question in UP PGT/TGT regarding recursive square root or fractional sequences.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Subsequences

$$ \text{If } \lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = l, \text{ every subsequence } x_{n_k} \text{ also converges to } l. $$

Exam Rule: If even two distinct subsequences converge to different limits, the main sequence diverges.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used to eliminate oscillating options like $(-1)^n$ or $\sin(n\pi/2)$ instantly.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Converse of Boundedness

Common Pitfall: Assuming that every bounded sequence is convergent.

Counterexample: $x_n = (-1)^n$

Why it fails: The sequence is bounded ($|x_n| \le 1$), but it oscillates between $-1$ and $1$, hence it does not converge.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Remember: Boundedness is necessary for convergence, but NOT sufficient. Monotonicity must be present.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Algebraic Operations on Divergent Sequences

Common Pitfall: Applying $\lim(a_n + b_n) = \lim a_n + \lim b_n$ when individual limits do not exist.

Counterexample: Let $a_n = n$ and $b_n = -n$.

Why it fails: $a_n + b_n = 0$, whose limit is $0$, but $\lim a_n$ and $\lim b_n$ do not exist finitely.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Limit laws apply ONLY when individual limits are finite and existent.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Evaluate the limit of the sequence defined recursively by $x_1 = \sqrt{2}$ and $x_{n+1} = \sqrt{2 + x_n}$ for $n \ge 1$.

(A) 1(B) 2(C) \sqrt{2}(D) \infty
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Prove boundedness by induction ($x_n < 2$). Step 2: Prove monotonicity ($x_{n+1} > x_n$). Step 3: Let $\lim x_n = l$. Step 4: Solve $l = \sqrt{2 + l} \implies l^2 - l - 2 = 0 \implies l = 2$ (since $l > 0$).
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Directly set $x = \sqrt{2+x}$ and solve $x^2 - x - 2 = 0 \implies x = 2$.
💡 Key Takeaway: For recursive root problems of type $\sqrt{a + \sqrt{a + \dots}}$, the positive root of $x^2 - x - a = 0$ is always the answer if $x_1 > 0$.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find $\lim_{n \to \infty} \left( \frac{1}{n+1} + \frac{1}{n+2} + \dots + \frac{1}{n+n} \right)$.

(A) 0(B) 1(C) \log 2(D) e
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Rewrite as $\sum_{r=1}^n \frac{1}{n} \frac{1}{1 + r/n}$. Convert to definite integral $\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{1+x} = [\ln(1+x)]_0^1 = \ln 2$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize Riemann sum limit: $\lim \frac{1}{n}\sum f(r/n) = \int_0^1 f(x)dx$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Sum of $n$ terms with denominator $n+r$ always converts to $\int_0^1 \frac{1}{1+x} dx = \ln 2$.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
MCQ 1: The sequence $x_n = \frac{n}{n+1}$ is:
A (A) Monotonic increasing and bounded
B (B) Monotonic decreasing and unbounded
C (C) Not monotonic but bounded
D (D) Divergent
Derivation & Analysis:

Here $x_{n+1} - x_n = \frac{n+1}{n+2} - \frac{n}{n+1} = \frac{1}{(n+2)(n+1)} > 0$, so it is increasing. Also $0 < x_n < 1$, so it is bounded.

Q2
MCQ 2: What is $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n!}{n^n}$?
A (A) 1
B (B) e
C (C) 0
D (D) \infty
Derivation & Analysis:

By the growth hierarchy, $n!$ grows much slower than $n^n$, hence the ratio tends to $0$. Rigorously, use sandwich theorem between $0$ and $\frac{1}{n} \cdot \frac{2}{n} \dots$.

Q3
MCQ 3: The limit of the sequence $x_n = (-1)^n \frac{1}{n}$ is:
A (A) 1
B (B) -1
C (C) Does not exist
D (D) 0
Derivation & Analysis:

By sandwich theorem, $-\frac{1}{n} \le (-1)^n \frac{1}{n} \le \frac{1}{n}$. Since limits of bounds are both $0$, the limit is $0$.

Q4
MCQ 4: If $x_n = \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n$, then $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n$ is:
A (A) 1
B (B) e
C (C) 0
D (D) \infty
Derivation & Analysis:

This is the standard definition of Euler's number $e$ as a sequence limit, converging monotonically to $e$.

Q5
MCQ 5: Which of the following sequences is convergent?
A (A) $x_n = n^{1/n}$
B (B) $x_n = (-1)^n n$
C (C) $x_n = \ln n$
D (D) $x_n = 2^n$
Derivation & Analysis:

$n^{1/n} \to 1$ as $n \to \infty$ (a standard standard limit). Options B, C, D diverge to infinity or oscillate.

Q6
MCQ 6: The sequence $x_n = \sin\left(\frac{n\pi}{2}\right)$ is:
A (A) Convergent to 1
B (B) Convergent to 0
C (C) Oscillatory (divergent)
D (D) Convergent to -1
Derivation & Analysis:

The terms are $1, 0, -1, 0, 1, 0, -1, \dots$, which oscillate between multiple values without settling, hence divergent.

Q7
MCQ 7: If $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = 5$, then $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{2x_n + 3}{x_n - 1}$ equals:
A (A) 13/4
B (B) 5
C (C) 2
D (D) 1
Derivation & Analysis:

Substitute limit directly: $\frac{2(5) + 3}{5 - 1} = \frac{13}{4}$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Cauchy's criterion for convergence of a sequence states that $x_n$ converges if and only if for every $\epsilon > 0$, there exists $N$ such that:
A (A) $|x_n - l| < \epsilon$
B (B) $|x_{n+p} - x_n| < \epsilon \quad \forall n \ge N, p \ge 1$
C (C) $x_n < \epsilon$
D (D) $|x_n| < N$
Derivation & Analysis:

Cauchy's general principle of convergence states $|x_m - x_n| < \epsilon$ for all $m, n \ge N$ without requiring knowledge of the limit $l$.

Q9
MCQ 9: Evaluate $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1^2 + 2^2 + \dots + n^2}{n^3}$:
A (A) 1
B (B) 1/2
C (C) 1/3
D (D) 0
Derivation & Analysis:

Sum of squares is $\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$. Dividing by $n^3$ gives $\frac{2n^3 + 3n^2 + n}{6n^3} \to \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $0 < y < x$, and $x_n = \frac{x^n + y^n}{x^n - y^n}$, then $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n$ is:
A (A) 0
B (B) 1
C (C) -1
D (D) \infty
Derivation & Analysis:

Divide numerator and denominator by $x^n$: $\lim \frac{1 + (y/x)^n}{1 - (y/x)^n}$. Since $y < x$, $(y/x)^n \to 0$, giving $\frac{1+0}{1-0} = 1$.
