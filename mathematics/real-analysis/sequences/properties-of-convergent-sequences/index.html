Real Analysis

Properties of convergent sequences

The properties of convergent sequences establish the fundamental algebraic, order, and topological behavior of limits in the real number system $\mathbb{R}$. Mastering uniqueness, boundedness, algebraic combinations, and inequalities of convergent sequences forms the absolute backbone of real analysis and is a high-scoring conceptual zone in UP PGT/TGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Algebra of Convergent Sequences (Limit Theorems)
Let $\langle a_n \rangle$ and $\langle b_n \rangle$ be sequences of real numbers such that $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = l$ and $\lim_{n \to \infty} b_n = m$. Then: (i) $\lim_{n \to \infty} (a_n \pm b_n) = l \pm m$, (ii) $\lim_{n \to \infty} (c a_n) = c l$ for any constant $c$, (iii) $\lim_{n \to \infty} (a_n b_n) = l m$, and (iv) $\lim_{n \to \infty} \left(\frac{a_n}{b_n}\right) = \frac{l}{m}$, provided $m \neq 0$ and $b_n \neq 0$ for all $n$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, as the index $n$ advances toward infinity, the terms of the combined sequence cluster indefinitely close to a single point determined by the arithmetic combination of their individual limiting targets $l$ and $m$.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

These properties prove that the set of all convergent sequences forms an algebra over $\mathbb{R}$ under termwise operations, preserving limits through continuous algebraic transformations.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Rational Function Limit Ratio

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_k n^k + a_{k-1}n^{k-1} + \dots + a_0}{b_j n^j + b_{j-1}n^{j-1} + \dots + b_0} = \begin{cases} \frac{a_k}{b_j} & \text{if } k = j \\ 0 & \text{if } k < j \\ \pm\infty & \text{if } k > j \end{cases} $$

Exam Rule: Compare highest degrees of numerator and denominator instantly to write down the limit without $\frac{1}{n}$ expansion.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT/TGT and KVS exams for quick algebraic sequences.
02

Shortcut 2: Squeeze (Sandwich) Theorem for Roots

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} (n^k + \dots + n^m)^{1/n} = 1 $$

Exam Rule: Any algebraic polynomial or fixed sum raised to the power $1/n$ converges to $1$ as $n \to \infty$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard recurring question pattern in DSSSB and UP PGT examinations.
03

Shortcut 3: Exponential vs. Factorial Growth Rule

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a^n}{n!} = 0 \quad (\forall a \in \mathbb{R}) $$

Exam Rule: Factorial growth strictly dominates exponential growth, which strictly dominates polynomial growth: $n^k \ll a^n \ll n! \ll n^n$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in comparative limit ranking questions.
04

Shortcut 4: Stolz-Cesaro Theorem (L'Hopital for Sequences)

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n - a_{n-1}}{b_n - b_{n-1}} $$

Exam Rule: Use difference ratio when encountering indeterminate forms $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ with strictly monotonic divergent denominators.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used for solving complex summation limits in high-level PGT papers.
05

Shortcut 5: Bounded Monotone Convergence

$$ \text{Monotone Increasing} + \text{Bounded Above} \implies \text{Converges to } \sup(a_n) $$

Exam Rule: If recurrence relation $a_{n+1} = f(a_n)$ is monotone and bounded, equate $a_{n+1} = a_n = l$ and solve algebraic equation $l = f(l)$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Extremely high yield for recursive sequence limit evaluation.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination Trick for Oscillatory Terms

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\sin(n\theta)}{n} = 0 $$

Exam Rule: Bounded oscillating factor divided by a term growing to infinity instantly evaluates to zero by Squeeze Theorem.

⚡ PYQ Context: Eliminates distracting trigonometric options instantly in exam papers.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Converse of Boundedness

Common Pitfall: Candidates assume that every bounded sequence is convergent.

Counterexample: a_n = (-1)^n

Why it fails: The sequence is bounded ($|a_n| \le 1$) but oscillates infinitely between $-1$ and $1$, hence does not converge.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Remember: Convergent $\implies$ Bounded, but Bounded $\not\implies$ Convergent (need monotonicity or Cauchy property).
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Strict Inequality Limit Fallacy

Common Pitfall: Assuming that if $a_n < b_n$ for all $n$, then $\lim a_n < \lim b_n$ strictly.

Counterexample: a_n = 0, \quad b_n = \frac{1}{n}

Why it fails: Here $0 < \frac{1}{n}$ for all $n$, but both limits are equal to $0$ ($0 \le 0$).

🛡️ Exam Defense: Strict inequalities weaken to non-strict inequalities upon taking limits: $a_n < b_n \implies \lim a_n \le \lim b_n$.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Evaluate the limit of the sequence $\langle a_n \rangle$ given by $a_n = \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n$.

(A) $0$(B) $1$(C) $e$(D) $\infty$
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Recognize standard limit definition of Euler's number $e$. Step 2: Apply logarithmic limit transformation $\lim_{n \to \infty} n \ln(1 + 1/n) = 1$. Step 3: Exponentiate to get $e^1 = e$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Standard textbook result memorization yields the answer in 2 seconds.
💡 Key Takeaway: Direct standard result frequently asked in UP TGT/PGT.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the limit of the recursive sequence defined by $a_1 = \sqrt{2}$ and $a_{n+1} = \sqrt{2 + a_n}$ for $n \ge 1$.

(A) $1$(B) $2$(C) $\sqrt{2}$(D) $\sqrt{3}$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Prove boundedness by induction ($a_n < 2$). Step 2: Prove monotonicity ($a_{n+1} > a_n$). Step 3: Let $\lim a_n = l$ and solve $l = \sqrt{2 + l} \implies l^2 - l - 2 = 0 \implies l = 2$ (discarding negative root).
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Set $a_{n+1} = a_n = l$ directly in the recurrence relation: $l = \sqrt{2 + l} \implies l = 2$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always check initial condition to ensure positive root selection.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
Every convergent sequence in $\mathbb{R}$ is:
A Monotone
B Bounded
C Unbounded
D Geometric progression
Derivation & Analysis:

By fundamental theorems of real analysis, every convergent sequence is bounded, though the converse is false.

Q2
The limit of the sequence $a_n = \frac{2n^2 + 3n}{5n^2 - 1}$ as $n \to \infty$ is:
A $0$
B $3/5$
C $2/5$
D $\infty$
Derivation & Analysis:

Degree of numerator and denominator are equal ($2$). The limit is the ratio of leading coefficients: $\frac{2}{5}$.

Q3
What is $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n!}{n^n}$?
A $1$
B $\infty$
C $e$
D $0$
Derivation & Analysis:

By growth rate hierarchy, $n! \ll n^n$, hence the ratio tends to $0$ as $n \to \infty$.

Q4
If $a_n = \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$, then the sequence $\langle a_n \rangle$ is:
A Convergent to $1$
B Convergent to $0$
C Divergent
D Oscillatory finitely without limit
Derivation & Analysis:

By Squeeze Theorem, $-1/n \le \frac{(-1)^n}{n} \le 1/n$. As $n \to \infty$, both bounds approach $0$, so $\lim a_n = 0$.

Q5
Which of the following sequences is divergent?
A $\langle \frac{1}{n} \rangle$
B $\langle \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n \rangle$
C $\langle (-1)^n \rangle$
D $\langle \frac{n}{n+1} \rangle$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\langle (-1)^n \rangle$ oscillates between $-1$ and $1$ and does not approach a unique limit, hence divergent.

Q6
If $a_n > 0$ for all $n$ and $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = l < 1$, then $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n$ is:
A $1$
B $0$
C $l$
D $\infty$
Derivation & Analysis:

D'Alembert's Ratio Test for sequences: if ratio of successive terms is less than $1$ in limit, the sequence converges to $0$.

Q7
The sequence $a_n = n^{1/n}$ converges to:
A $0$
B $1$
C $e$
D $\infty$
Derivation & Analysis:

Standard limit result: $\lim_{n \to \infty} n^{1/n} = 1$, established using logarithms or Cauchy's First Theorem on Limits.

Q8
Let $a_n = 1 + \frac{1}{1!} + \frac{1}{2!} + \dots + \frac{1}{n!}$. The sequence $\langle a_n \rangle$ is:
A Convergent to $e$
B Divergent to $\infty$
C Oscillatory
D Convergent to $1$
Derivation & Analysis:

This is the standard sequence representation of the number $e$, which is monotone increasing and bounded above by $3$.

Q9
If $a_n \le b_n \le c_n$ for all $n \ge N$ and $\lim a_n = \lim c_n = L$, then $\lim b_n$ equals:
A $0$
B $L$
C Does not exist
D $2L$
Derivation & Analysis:

This is the direct statement of the Squeeze (Sandwich) Theorem for sequences.

Q10
The value of $\lim_{n \to \infty} \left( \frac{1}{n^2} + \frac{2}{n^2} + \dots + \frac{n}{n^2} \right)$ is:
A $0$
B $1$
C $1/2$
D $2$
Derivation & Analysis:

Sum is $\frac{n(n+1)}{2n^2} = \frac{n^2+n}{2n^2}$. Taking limit as $n \to \infty$ gives $\frac{1}{2}$.
