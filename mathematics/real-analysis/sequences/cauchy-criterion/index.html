Real Analysis

Cauchy criterion of convergence

The Cauchy criterion states that a sequence of real numbers converges if and only if it is a Cauchy sequence, providing an internal test for convergence without needing to know the actual limit. In competitive exams like UP PGT/TGT and KVS, it is frequently tested through sequence bounds, tail estimates, and abstract function spaces.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Cauchy's Convergence Criterion for Sequences
A sequence of real numbers $\langle a_n \rangle$ is convergent in $\mathbb{R}$ if and only if for every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a positive integer $N$ such that $|a_m - a_n| < \varepsilon$ for all $m, n \ge N$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, as we move far out along the sequence, the terms cluster arbitrarily close to one another, forming an increasingly tight band of diameter less than any preassigned $\varepsilon > 0$.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It translates the topological property of convergence (which requires knowledge of the limit $L$) into an intrinsic algebraic condition involving only the terms of the sequence themselves.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Completeness of the underlying space ($\mathbb{R}$) Metric space $\mathbb{R}$ Strictly Mandatory / Necessary In incomplete spaces like $\mathbb{Q}$, Cauchy sequences (e.g., decimal approximations of $\sqrt{2}$) fail to converge within the space.
Tail index threshold ($m, n \ge N$) Discrete set of indices $\mathbb{N}$ Strictly Mandatory Failing to hold for all sufficiently large indices invalidates the tail contraction required for convergence.
Arbitrariness of $\varepsilon > 0$ Open ray $(0, \infty)$ Essential for conclusion If the condition holds only for a fixed $\varepsilon$, the sequence need not converge (it may oscillate boundedly).

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Ratio Test for Sequence Tail Bounds

$$ |a_{n+1} - a_n| \le r^n |a_1 - a_0|, \quad 0 < r < 1 $$

Exam Rule: If the consecutive difference satisfies a geometric bound with common ratio $r < 1$, the sequence is automatically Cauchy and convergent.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly applied in UP PGT to determine convergence of recursively defined sequences like $x_{n+1} = \frac{1}{2}(x_n + \frac{a}{x_n})$.
02

Shortcut 2: Bounded Monotonic Convergence Equivalence

$$ \text{Monotonic} + \text{Bounded} \iff \text{Cauchy} \iff \text{Convergent in } \mathbb{R} $$

Exam Rule: For monotonic sequences, never test Cauchy directly; verify boundedness to instantly establish convergence.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard recurring pattern in KVS and NVS PGT mathematics exams.
03

Shortcut 3: Alternating Harmonic & p-Series Tail Estimates

$$ |s_m - s_n| = \left| \sum_{k=n+1}^{m} \frac{1}{k^p} \right| < \varepsilon $$

Exam Rule: Use integral comparison test on tails $\int_{n}^{m} \frac{1}{x^p} dx$ to verify Cauchy property for series.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in DSSSB PGT for series convergence testing.
04

Shortcut 4: Subsequence Isolation Shortcut

$$ \text{If } \langle a_n \rangle \text{ is Cauchy and has a convergent subsequence } a_{n_k} \to L, \text{ then } a_n \to L $$

Exam Rule: If a Cauchy sequence has even one convergent subsequence, the entire sequence converges to that exact limit.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used to eliminate distractor options in multiple-choice assertion questions.
05

Shortcut 5: Cauchy Condensation Test Link

$$ \sum a_n \text{ converges } \iff \sum 2^k a_{2^k} \text{ converges} $$

Exam Rule: Transform heavy monotonic decreasing series into dyadic blocks to test Cauchy criterion instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: High-yield conceptual item in UP TGT/PGT.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Unboundedness

$$ \text{If } \lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = \infty \text{ or oscillates finitely without bound}, \text{ discard Cauchy} $$

Exam Rule: Any Cauchy sequence in $\mathbb{R}$ is bounded. Instantly eliminate any option claiming a divergent or unbounded sequence is Cauchy.

⚡ PYQ Context: Instant 10-second elimination trick for 2 out of 4 options in exam papers.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Assuming Cauchy in Rational Numbers ($\mathbb{Q}$)

Common Pitfall: Candidates assume Cauchy sequences always converge to a number within the same set, forgetting completeness.

Counterexample: $a_n = \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n \text{ or decimal truncations of } \sqrt{2} \text{ in } \mathbb{Q}$

Why it fails: The sequence is Cauchy, but its limit $\sqrt{2}$ or $e$ does not belong to $\mathbb{Q}$, so it diverges within $\mathbb{Q}$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check the underlying metric space domain ($\mathbb{R}$ vs. $\mathbb{Q}$). Real analysis PGT exams explicitly exploit $\mathbb{Q}$ incompleteness.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Confusing 'Infinitely Close' with 'Convergent'

Common Pitfall: Assuming that $\lim_{n \to \infty} (a_{n+1} - a_n) = 0$ implies the sequence is Cauchy or convergent.

Counterexample: $a_n = \sqrt{n}$

Why it fails: Here $a_{n+1} - a_n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, but the sequence is unbounded and diverges to infinity.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Remember that $a_{n+1} - a_n \to 0$ is a necessary condition for convergence of certain series/sequences, but NOT sufficient for Cauchy criterion.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Determine whether the sequence defined by $a_n = 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + \dots + \frac{1}{n}$ is a Cauchy sequence in $\mathbb{R}$.

(A) Yes, because $a_{n+1} - a_n \to 0$(B) No, because it is divergent (Harmonic sequence)(C) Yes, because it is bounded(D) Insufficient data
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Evaluate $|a_{2n} - a_n| = \frac{1}{n+1} + \dots + \frac{1}{2n} \ge n \cdot \frac{1}{2n} = \frac{1}{2}$. Choosing $\varepsilon = \frac{1}{3}$ violates the Cauchy condition for arbitrarily large $n$. Thus, it is not Cauchy.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize $a_n$ as the partial sum of the harmonic series. Harmonic series diverges, so its partial sum sequence is not Cauchy.
💡 Key Takeaway: Never confuse the term vanishing ($\frac{1}{n} \to 0$) with Cauchy property of partial sums.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If a sequence $\langle a_n \n \rangle$ satisfies $|a_{n+1} - a_n| \leq \frac{1}{2^n}$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, then the sequence is:

(A) Divergent(B) Cauchy and convergent(C) Bounded but not Cauchy(D) Oscillatory
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Using triangle inequality for $m > n$: $|a_m - a_n| \le \sum_{k=n}^{m-1} |a_{k+1} - a_k| \le \sum_{k=n}^{m-1} \frac{1}{2^k} < \frac{1}{2^{n-1}} < \varepsilon$ for sufficiently large $N$. Hence it is Cauchy and complete in $\mathbb{R}$ implies convergence.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Geometric bound sum $\sum \frac{1}{2^n} < \infty$ immediately guarantees Cauchy property.
💡 Key Takeaway: Any sequence with exponential consecutive difference bound is strictly Cauchy.

D (D) Unbounded

Linear combinations of Cauchy sequences are Cauchy due to the triangle inequality.
