Types of discontinuity

A discontinuity occurs at a point where a function fails to be continuous, classified rigorously by the behavior of its left-hand and right-hand limits. Mastering removable, jump, and essential discontinuities provides a fundamental framework frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Classification of Discontinuities via Limits
Let $f: D \subset \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ and let $c$ be a limit point of $D$. The function $f$ has a discontinuity at $c$ if $f$ is not continuous at $c$. Discontinuities are categorized into: (1) Removable Discontinuity if $\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$ exists finitely but $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) \neq f(c)$ (or $f(c)$ is undefined); (2) Jump Discontinuity (First Kind) if both left-hand limit $\lim_{x \to c^-} f(x)$ and right-hand limit $\lim_{x \to c^+} f(x)$ exist finitely but are unequal; (3) Essential Discontinuity (Second Kind) if at least one of the one-sided limits fails to exist finitely.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, a removable discontinuity appears as a missing point or a removable 'hole' in the graph. A jump discontinuity manifests as a sudden vertical gap or step where the curve breaks and continues at a different height. An essential discontinuity displays wild oscillations or vertical asymptotic behavior where the graph breaks down completely.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, discontinuity classification exposes the localized limit structure of functions. It demarcates domains where Riemann integration, differentiability, and sequence convergence theorems fail or must be restricted.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Existence of Left-Hand Limit Left neighborhood $(c-\delta, c)$ Strictly Mandatory for First Kind Classification If the left-hand limit does not exist finitely due to oscillation or divergence, the discontinuity cannot be classified as a simple jump and defaults to an essential discontinuity.
Existence of Right-Hand Limit Right neighborhood $(c, c+\delta)$ Strictly Mandatory for First Kind Classification If the right-hand limit fails to exist finitely, jump discontinuity criteria fail, pushing the classification into essential discontinuities.
Finiteness of Limit Values Neighborhood points Essential for removable vs essential distinction If one-sided limits diverge to $\pm\infty$ (e.g., $f(x) = \sin(1/x)$ or $1/x$), the discontinuity is classified as essential rather than removable or jump.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Rational Functions & Removable Discontinuity

$$ f(x) = \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}, \quad Q(c) = 0 \text{ and } P(c) = 0 $$

Exam Rule: If both numerator and denominator vanish at $x = c$, cancel the common linear factor $(x-c)$. If the limit exists after cancellation, $x = c$ is a removable discontinuity.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT to find the value to assign to $f(c)$ to make the function continuous.
02

Shortcut 2: Jump Magnitude Formula

$$ \text{Jump} = | \lim_{x \to c^+} f(x) - \lim_{x \to c^-} f(x) | $$

Exam Rule: For step functions, floor functions, or fractional part functions, the jump at an integer point $c$ is precisely evaluated by taking the absolute difference of the one-sided limits.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in DSSSB and KVS PGT vector/calculus integration limit problems.
03

Shortcut 3: Greatest Integer Function Discontinuities

$$ f(x) = [x] \text{ at } x = n \in \mathbb{Z} $$

Exam Rule: The function $[x]$ has a jump discontinuity at every integer $n$, where $\lim_{x \to n^-} [x] = n-1$ and $\lim_{x \to n^+} [x] = n$, giving a jump magnitude of $1$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard recurring pattern across all State PGT mathematics papers.
04

Shortcut 4: Sine Oscillatory Discontinuity Test

$$ f(x) = \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) \text{ at } x = 0 $$

Exam Rule: When $x \to 0$, the argument diverges to infinity causing rapid bounded oscillations between $-1$ and $1$. Neither limit exists, confirming an essential discontinuity of the second kind.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard conceptual trap in multiple-choice assertion questions.
05

Shortcut 5: Exponential Limit Discontinuity Check

$$ f(x) = \frac{1}{1 + a^{1/x}} $$

Exam Rule: Evaluate left limit ($x \to 0^-$ gives $1/x \to -\infty$) and right limit ($x \to 0^+$ gives $1/x \to +\infty$) to instantly identify jump discontinuity values based on $a > 0$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Appears in advanced UP PGT calculus sorting problems.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Option Elimination via Limit Existence

$$ \text{If } \lim_{x \to c} f(x) \text{ does not exist, eliminate 'Removable'} $$

Exam Rule: If the limit does not exist due to infinite divergence or oscillation, immediately cross out 'removable discontinuity' options to narrow down to jump or essential.

⚡ PYQ Context: Essential time-saving strategy for the 125-question UP TGT/PGT paper.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Removable with Jump Discontinuity

Common Pitfall: Candidates assume any function with a missing single point has a jump discontinuity.

Counterexample: $f(x) = \frac{x^2 - 4}{x - 2}$ for $x \neq 2$, and $f(2) = 5$

Why it fails: The limit as $x \to 2$ is $4$, which exists finitely. Since $\lim_{x \to 2} f(x) \neq f(2)$, it is a removable discontinuity, not a jump.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check if $\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$ exists finitely. If yes, and it doesn't match $f(c)$, it is removable.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Misidentifying Essential Discontinuity as Jump

Common Pitfall: Treating oscillating functions like $\sin(1/x)$ as having jump discontinuities.

Counterexample: $f(x) = \sin(1/x)$ at $x = 0$

Why it fails: Neither left nor right limit exists finitely because the function oscillates infinitely between $-1$ and $1$, failing jump criteria.

🛡️ Exam Defense: If a function oscillates infinitely near a point without settling on finite limits, classify it strictly as an essential discontinuity.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Determine the type of discontinuity for the function $f(x) = \frac{\tan x}{x}$ at $x = 0$, given $f(0) = 2$.

(A) Removable discontinuity(B) Jump discontinuity(C) Essential discontinuity(D) Continuous at $x = 0$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x}{x} = 1$. Step 2: Note that $f(0) = 2$. Step 3: Since the limit exists finitely ($1$) but does not equal $f(0)$ ($2$), the discontinuity is removable.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize standard limit $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x}{x} = 1 \neq f(0)$. Instantly mark removable discontinuity.
💡 Key Takeaway: Check if redefining $f(0)$ to the limit value makes the function continuous.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

What type of discontinuity does $f(x) = e^{1/x}$ possess at $x = 0$?

(A) Removable discontinuity(B) Jump discontinuity(C) Essential discontinuity of second kind(D) Continuous
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Compute $\lim_{x \to 0^-} e^{1/x} = 0$. Step 2: Compute $\lim_{x \to 0^+} e^{1/x} = +\infty$. Step 3: Since the right limit diverges to infinity, the limit does not exist finitely, confirming an essential discontinuity.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Exponential with reciprocal argument $1/x$ blowing up to infinity at $0$ directly indicates an essential discontinuity.
💡 Key Takeaway: Infinite one-sided limits automatically rule out removable and jump discontinuities.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
MCQ 1: The function $f(x) = \frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}$ for $x \neq 1$ and $f(1) = 3$ has at $x = 1$: \n (A) Removable discontinuity \n (B) Jump discontinuity \n (C) Essential discontinuity \n (D) Continuity
A (A) Removable discontinuity
B (B) Jump discontinuity
C (C) Essential discontinuity
D (D) Continuity
Derivation & Analysis:

Limit as $x \to 1$ is $\lim_{x \to 1} (x+1) = 2$. Since $f(1) = 3 \neq 2$, the discontinuity is removable.

Q2
MCQ 2: The function $f(x) = \frac{1}{1 + 2^{1/x}}$ at $x = 0$ exhibits: \n (A) Removable discontinuity \n (B) Jump discontinuity \n (C) Essential discontinuity \n (D) Continuous
A (A) Removable discontinuity
B (B) Jump discontinuity
C (C) Essential discontinuity
D (D) Continuous
Derivation & Analysis:

As $x \to 0^-$, $1/x \to -\infty$, $2^{1/x} \to 0$, so limit is $1/(1+0) = 1$. As $x \to 0^+$, $1/x \to +\infty$, $2^{1/x} \to \infty$, so limit is $0$. Unequal finite limits imply a jump discontinuity.

Q3
MCQ 3: The greatest integer function $f(x) = [x]$ at $x = 3$ has: \n (A) Removable discontinuity \n (B) Jump discontinuity of magnitude $1$ \n (C) Essential discontinuity \n (D) Continuity
A (A) Removable discontinuity
B (B) Jump discontinuity of magnitude $1$
C (C) Essential discontinuity
D (D) Continuity
Derivation & Analysis:

Left limit is $2$ and right limit is $3$. The jump is $3 - 2 = 1$, representing a jump discontinuity.

Q4
MCQ 4: The function $f(x) = \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$ at $x = 0$ has: \n (A) Removable discontinuity \n (B) Jump discontinuity \n (C) Essential discontinuity \n (D) No discontinuity
A (A) Removable discontinuity
B (B) Jump discontinuity
C (C) Essential discontinuity
D (D) No discontinuity
Derivation & Analysis:

Since neither one-sided limit exists due to infinite oscillation between $-1$ and $1$, it is an essential discontinuity.

Q5
MCQ 5: If $\lim_{x \to c^-} f(x) = L_1$ and $\lim_{x \to c^+} f(x) = L_2$ with $L_1 \neq L_2$ both being finite, the discontinuity is: \n (A) Removable \n (B) Jump (First Kind) \n (C) Essential (Second Kind) \n (D) None
A (A) Removable
B (B) Jump (First Kind)
C (C) Essential (Second Kind)
D (D) None
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, unequal finite one-sided limits constitute a jump discontinuity of the first kind.

Q6
MCQ 6: The function $f(x) = \frac{\sin x}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$ and $f(0) = 1$ is: \n (A) Discontinuous (Removable) \n (B) Continuous everywhere \n (C) Discontinuous (Jump) \n (D) Discontinuous (Essential)
A (A) Discontinuous (Removable)
B (B) Continuous everywhere
C (C) Discontinuous (Jump)
D (D) Discontinuous (Essential)
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1 = f(0)$, the function is continuous.

Q7
MCQ 7: Which of the following functions has an essential discontinuity at $x = 0$? \n (A) $f(x) = x \sin(1/x)$ \n (B) $f(x) = \cos(1/x)$ \n (C) $f(x) = \frac{|x|}{x}$ \n (D) $f(x) = \frac{x^2}{x}$
A (A) $f(x) = x \sin(1/x)$
B (B) $f(x) = \cos(1/x)$
C (C) $f(x) = \frac{|x|}{x}$
D (D) $f(x) = \frac{x^2}{x}$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\cos(1/x)$ oscillates infinitely between $-1$ and $1$ as $x \to 0$ without limit, making it an essential discontinuity. Note: $x \sin(1/x)$ has a removable discontinuity at $0$ if defined.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): $f(x) = \frac{1}{x-2}$ has an essential discontinuity at $x = 2$. Reason (R): The one-sided limits as $x \to 2$ are infinite. \n (A) Both A and R are true and R is correct explanation \n (B) Both true but R is not explanation \n (C) A is true, R is false \n (D) A is false, R is true
A (A) Both A and R are true and R is correct explanation
B (B) Both true but R is not explanation
C (C) A is true, R is false
D (D) A is false, R is true
Derivation & Analysis:

Infinite one-sided limits mean the limit does not exist finitely, classifying it as an essential discontinuity of the second kind.

Q9
MCQ 9: The fractional part function $f(x) = \{x\}$ has discontinuities at: \n (A) All integers \n (B) No points \n (C) All rational numbers \n (D) All irrational numbers
A (A) All integers
B (B) No points
C (C) All rational numbers
D (D) All irrational numbers
Derivation & Analysis:

The fractional part function $\{x\} = x - [x]$ jumps at every integer, producing jump discontinuities at all $x \in \mathbb{Z}$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If a function $f$ has a removable discontinuity at $x = c$, then: \n (A) $\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$ does not exist \n (B) $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = f(c)$ \n (C) $\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$ exists finitely but $\neq f(c)$ \n (D) One-sided limits are infinite
A (A) $\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$ does not exist

C (C) $\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$ exists finitely but $\neq f(c)$

Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, a removable discontinuity requires the limit to exist finitely while differing from the function value at that point.
