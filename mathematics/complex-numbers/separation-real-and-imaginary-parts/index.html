Complex Numbers

Separation into real and imaginary parts

Separating a complex expression into its real and imaginary parts involves algebraic manipulation using conjugates, De Moivre's theorem, or logarithmic/trigonometric identities to express it uniquely in the form $u + iv$. Mastering this skill is essential for tackling high-yield computational questions frequently featured in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Fundamental Principle of Complex Decomposition
Let $z = x + iy$ be a complex number where $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$. Any well-defined complex-valued function $f(z)$ can be uniquely decomposed into a real part $u(x, y)$ and an imaginary part $v(x, y)$ such that $f(x + iy) = u(x, y) + iv(x, y)$, where $u, v: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ are real-valued scalar functions.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, separation maps points from the complex z-plane into a two-variable real coordinate system representing orthogonal Cartesian components of the function value.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It establishes a bridge between complex algebra and multivariable real calculus, enabling the application of techniques like partial differentiation, harmonic conjugates, and Cartesian coordinate transformations.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Rationalization of Complex Denominators

$$\frac{a + ib}{c + id} = \frac{(ac + bd) + i(bc - ad)}{c^2 + d^2}$$

Exam Rule: Immediately multiply numerator and denominator by the complex conjugate of the denominator $(c - id)$ to instantly isolate real and imaginary coefficients.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT/TGT to simplify powers of complex fractions within 30 seconds.
02

Shortcut 2: Euler's Form for Powers and Roots

$$(x + iy)^n = r^n (\cos n\theta + i \sin n\theta), \quad \text{where } r = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}, \theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)$$

Exam Rule: Convert algebraic complex numbers into polar/exponential form before applying exponents or extracting roots.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequent in DSSSB PGT for evaluating expressions like $(1 + i)^n + (1 - i)^n$.
03

Shortcut 3: Separation of Logarithmic Complex Numbers

$$\log(x + iy) = \frac{1}{2}\ln(x^2 + y^2) + i \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)$$

Exam Rule: Use the standard logarithmic expansion where the real part is half the natural log of the squared modulus, and the imaginary part is the principal argument.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard scoring item in KVS PGT and UP PGT examinations.
04

Shortcut 4: Trigonometric Functions of Complex Arguments

$$$\sin(x + iy) = \sin x \cosh y + i \cos x \sinh y$$

Exam Rule: Apply hyperbolic identities: $\sin(ix) = i \sinh x$ and $\cos(ix) = \cosh x$ to separate sine and cosine of complex sums instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Tested in LT Grade and UP PGT to find real parts of $\sin(a + ib)$.
05

Shortcut 5: Inverse Trigonometric Real/Imaginary Extraction

$$ \text{If } \sin^{-1}(x + iy) = \alpha + i\beta, \text{ then equate real and imaginary parts after taking sine on both sides.} $$

Exam Rule: Convert inverse trigonometric equations into standard trigonometric forms using expansion formulas for $\sin(A+B)$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT for separation of $\tan^{-1}(x + iy)$.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Modulus Check

$$ |u + iv| = |f(z)| = \frac{|Numerator|}{|Denominator|} $$

Exam Rule: Check the magnitude of options. If an option does not satisfy the computed modulus squared, eliminate it instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves critical time in 125-question PGT papers.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Ignoring Principal Branch Multi-Valuedness

Common Pitfall: Treating logarithmic and fractional power separations as single-valued without considering the $2k\pi$ periodicity of arguments.

Counterexample: \log(1) = 2k\pi i \quad (k \in \mathbb{Z})

Why it fails: Forgetting the general argument adds incorrect imaginary components, leading to wrong option selection.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check whether the question specifies the 'principal value' or general value.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Hyperbolic vs Trigonometric Sign Confusion

Common Pitfall: Mixing up signs when expanding $\cos(x + iy) = \cos x \cosh y - i \sin x \sinh y$, particularly missing the negative sign.

Counterexample: \cos(x + iy) \neq \cos x \cosh y + i \sin x \sinh y

Why it fails: Leads to choosing the sign-inverted distractor deliberately placed by the examiner.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Memorize the fundamental expansion: $\cos(A+B) = \cos A \cos B - \sin A \sin B$ combined with $\cos(iy) = \cosh y$ and $\sin(iy) = i \sinh y$.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $\sin^{-1}(x + iy) = \alpha + i\beta$, where $x, y, \alpha, \beta$ are real, find the value of $x^2 + y^2$ or separate into real and imaginary parts.

(A) $\sin^2 \alpha + \sinh^2 \beta$(B) $\cos^2 \alpha + \cosh^2 \beta$(C) $\sin^2 \alpha - \sinh^2 \beta$(D) $\cosh^2 \alpha + \sinh^2 \beta$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write $x + iy = \sin(\alpha + i\beta) = \sin\alpha \cos(i\beta) + \cos\alpha \sin(i\beta) = \sin\alpha \cosh\beta + i\cos\alpha \sinh\beta$. Step 2: Equate real and imaginary parts: $x = \sin\alpha \cosh\beta$ and $y = \cos\alpha \sinh\beta$. Step 3: Use $\cosh^2\beta - \sinh^2\beta = 1$ to eliminate $\beta$ or compute $x^2 + \dots$
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize the standard result for inverse hyperbolic-trigonometric links: real part squared terms pair with hyperbolic sine/cosine identities.
💡 Key Takeaway: Direct standard result frequently asked verbatim in UP PGT.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Separate $\tan(x + iy)$ into real and imaginary parts.

(A) $\frac{\sin 2x + i \sinh 2y}{\cos 2x + \cosh 2y}$(B) $\frac{\sin 2x - i \sinh 2y}{\cos 2x - \cosh 2y}$(C) $\frac{\sinh 2x + i \sin 2y}{\cosh 2x + \cos 2y}$(D) None of these
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write $\tan(x + iy) = \frac{\sin(x + iy)}{\cos(x + iy)}$. Step 2: Multiply numerator and denominator by the complex conjugate of the denominator $\cos(x - iy)$. Step 3: Simplify using product-to-sum trigonometric and hyperbolic identities to obtain the result.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Remember numerator has $\sin 2x + i \sinh 2y$ and denominator has $\cos 2x + \cosh 2y$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Watch out for sign flips between $\sin$ and $\sinh$ terms in options.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
The real part of $\frac{1}{1 + i}$ is:
A $1$
B $1/2$
C $-1/2$
D $0$
Derivation & Analysis:

Multiply numerator and denominator by $1 - i$: $\frac{1 - i}{(1+i)(1-i)} = \frac{1-i}{2} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{i}{2}$. The real part is $1/2$.

Q2
If $x + iy = \frac{1}{1 + \cos \theta + i \sin \theta}$, then the value of $x$ is:
A $1/2$
B $1$
C $\cos \theta$
D $2 \cos(\theta/2)$
Derivation & Analysis:

Rationalize the denominator using $1 + \cos\theta - i\sin\theta$. Denominator becomes $(1+\cos\theta)^2 + \sin^2\theta = 2 + 2\cos\theta = 4\cos^2(\theta/2)$. Real part works out to $1/2$.

Q3
The imaginary part of $\log(3 + 4i)$ is:
A $\tan^{-1}(3/4)$
B $\tan^{-1}(4/3)$
C $\ln(5)$
D $4/3$
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, $\log(x + iy) = \frac{1}{2}\ln(x^2+y^2) + i\tan^{-1}(y/x)$. Here $x=3, y=4$, so imaginary part is $\tan^{-1}(4/3)$.

Q4
If $\cos(x + iy) = A + iB$, then $A$ is equal to:
A $\cos x \cosh y$
B $-\sin x \sinh y$
C \cos x \cos y
D \sin x \sinh y
Derivation & Analysis:

$\cos(x + iy) = \cos x \cos(iy) - \sin x \sin(iy) = \cos x \cosh y - i \sin x \sinh y$. Thus, real part $A = \cos x \cosh y$.

Q5
The real part of $(1 + i)^n$ when $n = 4$ is:
A $-4$
B $4$
C $0$
D $2$
Derivation & Analysis:

$(1+i)^2 = 2i$, so $(1+i)^4 = (2i)^2 = -4$. The real part is $-4$.

Q6
If $u + iv = e^{x + iy}$, then $v$ is equal to:
A $e^x \cos y$
B $e^x \sin y$
C $e^y \cos x$
D $e^y \sin x$
Derivation & Analysis:

$e^{x+iy} = e^x \cdot e^{iy} = e^x(\cos y + i \sin y) = e^x \cos y + i e^x \sin y$. Hence $v = e^x \sin y$.

Q7
The value of $\left(\frac{1 + i}{1 - i}\right)^3$ is equal to:
A $i$
B $-i$
C $1$
D $-1$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\frac{1+i}{1-i} = i$. Therefore, $\left(\frac{1+i}{1-i}\right)^3 = i^3 = -i$.

Q8
If $\tan(x + iy) = u + iv$, then $u$ is:
A $\frac{\sin 2x}{\cos 2x + \cosh 2y}$
B $\frac{\sinh 2y}{\cos 2x + \cosh 2y}$
C $\frac{\sin 2x}{\cos 2x - \cosh 2y}$
D $\frac{\sinh 2x}{\cos 2x + \cosh 2y}$
Derivation & Analysis:

From standard separation formulas for complex tangent, the real part $u = \frac{\sin 2x}{\cos 2x + \cosh 2y}$.

Q9
The modulus of the complex number $z = 3 - 4i$ is:
A $7$
B $5$
C $25$
D $-5$
Derivation & Analysis:

$|3 - 4i| = \sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.

Q10
If $z = x + iy$ and $z^2 = i$, then the values of $(x, y)$ are:
A $(\pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})$
B $(\pm 1, \pm 1)$
C $(\pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \mp \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})$
D $(1, 1)$
Derivation & Analysis:

$z^2 = x^2 - y^2 + 2ixy = i$. Equating parts: $x^2 - y^2 = 0 \implies x = \pm y$. Also $2xy = 1$. Solving gives $x = \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, y = \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$.
