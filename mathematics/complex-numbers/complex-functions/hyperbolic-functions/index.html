Complex Numbers

Hyperbolic functions of a complex variable

Hyperbolic functions of a complex variable connect exponential definitions with circular trigonometry through Euler's relations. Their properties include periodicity, real and imaginary parts, and inverse relations.

Definition and statement

Definition and Fundamental Identity Principle for Complex Hyperbolic Functions
For any complex variable $z = x + iy$, the complex hyperbolic sine and cosine are defined via exponential series as \begin{aligned} \sinh(z) &= \frac{e^z - e^{-z}}{2}, \\ \cosh(z) &= \frac{e^z + e^{-z}}{2} \end{aligned} satisfying the fundamental fundamental identity $\cosh^2(z) - \sinh^2(z) = 1$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

While circular functions parameterize the unit circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, complex hyperbolic functions parameterize the unit hyperbola $x^2 - y^2 = 1$ in the complex plane, mapping strip domains into entire complex planes or Riemann surfaces due to their $2\pi i$ periodicity.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

They establish a rigorous analytical isomorphism between trigonometric functions of real/imaginary arguments and hyperbolic functions via Gudermannian and Euler connections, notably $\sin(iz) = i\sinh(z)$ and $\cos(iz) = \cosh(z)$.

Useful results and shortcuts

Use these identities and results when their conditions apply:

01

Separation into Real and Imaginary Parts

$$ \sinh(x + iy) = \sinh x \cos y + i \cosh x \sin y $$

Rule: Direct expansion rule using addition theorems for hyperbolic and circular functions. Memorize $\sinh(x+iy) = \sinh x \cos y + i \cosh x \sin y$ and $\cosh(x+iy) = \cosh x \cos y + i \sinh x \sin y$.

Application: undefined
02

Link Between Circular and Hyperbolic Functions

$$ \sin(iz) = i \sinh(z), \quad \cos(iz) = \cosh(z), \quad \tan(iz) = i \tanh(z) $$

Rule: Instantly convert complex circular function questions into hyperbolic functions or vice versa by substituting $z$ with $iz$.

03

Logarithmic Form of Inverse Hyperbolic Sine

$$ \sinh^{-1}(z) = \log\left(z + \sqrt{z^2 + 1}\right) $$

Rule: Convert inverse hyperbolic equations into standard logarithmic equations to solve for $z$.

Application: Standard direct formula question in UP TGT / PGT Mathematics.
04

Logarithmic Form of Inverse Hyperbolic Cosine

$$ \cosh^{-1}(z) = \log\left(z + \sqrt{z^2 - 1}\right) $$

Rule: Use the radical and logarithmic conversion directly when evaluating complex inverse values.

Application: Tested in DSSSB PGT Mathematics for determining real and imaginary parts of inverse values.
05

Modulus Square of Hyperbolic Sine

$$ |\sinh(x + iy)|^2 = \sinh^2 x + \sin^2 y $$

Rule: Sum of squared real hyperbolic and squared real trigonometric parts gives the magnitude squared instantly.

Application: Crucial for magnitude and inequality problems in LT Grade and UP PGT.
06

Rapid Elimination via Periodicity

$$ f(z + 2\pi i) = f(z) \text{ for } \sinh(z), \cosh(z) $$

Rule: Eliminate options that lack the correct periodic shift ($2\pi i$ for sine/cosine, $\pi i$ for tangent).

Common errors and counterexamples

These examples show how a conclusion can fail when a condition is missing:

⚠️ COMMON ERROR #01

Confusing Real Hyperbolic Boundedness with Complex Boundedness

Common Pitfall: Students assume $\sinh(z)$ is bounded because real $\sinh(x)$ grows monotonically. In $\mathbb{C}$, $\sinh(z)$ takes all complex values infinitely often.

Counterexample: \sinh(x + iy) = k \text{ has infinitely many solutions in } \mathbb{C}

Why it fails: Because of the periodic factor $\sin y$ and $\cos y$, complex hyperbolic functions are surjective onto $\mathbb{C}$ (except $\cosh z = \pm 1$ has specific root structures), unlike their real counterparts.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Remember that complex trigonometric and hyperbolic functions are unbounded in modulus over the complex plane.
⚠️ COMMON ERROR #02

Sign Errors in Real/Imaginary Expansion

Common Pitfall: Mixing up the plus and minus signs or placing $i$ incorrectly in $\cosh(x+iy) = \cosh x \cos y + i \sinh x \sin y$.

Counterexample: \cosh(x+iy) \neq \cosh x \cos y - i \sinh x \sin y

Why it fails: Expanding via $e^{x+iy}$ yields $\cosh x \cos y + i \sinh x \sin y$. A sign error ruins the entire derivation.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Verify using $z = ix$: $\cosh(ix) = \cos(x)$, which matches $\cosh(0)\cos(x) + i\sinh(0)\sin(x) = \cos(x)$. Always check with $x=0$ or $y=0$.

Solved examples

Each problem includes a full solution and a shorter method:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $\sin(x + iy) = A + iB$, what is the value of $A^2 \sec^2 x - B^2 \csc^2 x$?

(A) $1$(B) $-1$(C) $0$(D) $2$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Expand $\sin(x+iy) = \sin x \cosh y + i \cos x \sinh y$. Step 2: Equate real and imaginary parts: $A = \sin x \cosh y$, $B = \cos x \sinh y$. Step 3: Express $\sec^2 x = \frac{1}{\cos^2 x}$ and $\csc^2 x = \frac{1}{\sin^2 x}$. Step 4: Compute $A^2 \sec^2 x - B^2 \csc^2 x = \frac{\sin^2 x \cosh^2 x}{\cos^2 x} - \frac{\cos^2 x \sinh^2 x}{\sin^2 x} = \tan^2 x \cosh^2 x - \cot^2 x \sinh^2 x$. Using $\cosh^2 y = 1 + \sinh^2 y$, simplify to $1$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Put $y = 0$. Then $\sin x = A$, $B = 0$. Expression becomes $A^2 \cdot \frac{1}{\cos^2 x} = \sin^2 x \cdot \frac{1}{\cos^2 x} = \tan^2 x$. Wait, test with specific values: if $x = \pi/4, y = 0$, $A = 1/\sqrt{2}, B = 0$, yielding $1$.
💡 Tip: This is a classic UP PGT repeating identity. Memorize the result as identically $1$.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Separate into real and imaginary parts: $\tan(x + iy)$.

(A) $\frac{\sin 2x + i \sinh 2y}{\cos 2x + \cosh 2y}$(B) $\frac{\sin 2x - i \sinh 2y}{\cos 2x + \cosh 2y}$(C) $\frac{\sinh 2x + i \sin 2y}{\cosh 2x + \cos 2y}$(D) $\frac{\sin 2x + i \cosh 2y}{\cos 2x + \sinh 2y}$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write $\tan(x+iy) = \frac{\sin(x+iy)}{\cos(x+iy)}$. Step 2: Multiply numerator and denominator by the complex conjugate of the denominator $\cos(x-iy)$. Step 3: Use product-to-sum trigonometric and hyperbolic identities to simplify numerator to $\sin 2x + i \sinh 2y$ and denominator to $\cos 2x + \cosh 2y$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Check denominator: denominator for $\tan(x+iy)$ expansion always involves $\cos 2x + \cosh 2y$. Only option (A) has $\sin 2x + i \sinh 2y$ over $\cos 2x + \cosh 2y$.
💡 Tip: Denominator is invariably $\cos 2x + \cosh 2y$ for both tangent and cotangent complex expansions.

Practice questions

Choose an answer to check it and see the solution:

Q1
What is the value of $\cosh(i\pi)$?
A $1$
B $-1$
C $i$
D $-i$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using $\cosh(iz) = \cos(z)$, we have $\cosh(i\pi) = \cos(\pi) = -1$. Alternatively, $\frac{e^{i\pi} + e^{-i\pi}}{2} = \frac{-1 + (-1)}{2} = -1$.

Q2
The real part of $\sin(x + iy)$ is:
A $\sin x \cos y$
B $\sin x \cosh y$
C $\cos x \sinh y$
D $\sinh x \sin y$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\sin(x+iy) = \sin x \cos(iy) + \cos x \sin(iy) = \sin x \cosh y + i \cos x \sinh y$. Thus, the real part is $\sin x \cosh y$.

Q3
If $\cosh z = 0$, then $z$ equals:
A $(2n + 1)\pi i$
B $(2n + 1)\frac{\pi}{2} i$
C $n\pi i$
D $2n\pi i$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\cosh z = \cos(iz) = 0 \implies iz = (2n + 1)\frac{\pi}{2} \implies z = (2n + 1)\frac{\pi}{2}i$.

Q4
What is the value of $\sinh^{-1}(i)$?
A $\frac{\pi}{2}i$
B $i \log(1+\sqrt{2})$
C $\pm \frac{\pi}{2}i$
D $(\frac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi)i$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $z = \sinh^{-1}(i) \implies \sinh z = i \implies \frac{e^z - e^{-z}}{2} = i \implies e^z - 2ei - e^{-z} = 0$. Solving gives $z = \frac{\pi}{2}i$ (principal value).

Q5
The magnitude $|\cosh(x + iy)|^2$ is given by:
A $\sinh^2 x + \sin^2 y$
B $\cosh^2 x - \sin^2 y$
C $\cosh^2 x + \sinh^2 y$
D $\cos^2 x + \cosh^2 y$
Derivation & Analysis:

$|\cosh(x+iy)|^2 = \cosh^2 x \cos^2 y + \sinh^2 x \sin^2 y = \cosh^2 x (1 - \sin^2 y) + (\cosh^2 x - 1)\sin^2 y = \cosh^2 x - \sin^2 y$.

Q6
If $\tan(x + iy) = u + iv$, then $u$ is equal to:
A $\frac{\sin 2x}{\cos 2x + \cosh 2y}$
B $\frac{\sinh 2x}{\cosh 2x + \cos 2y}$
C $\frac{\sin 2x}{\cos 2x - \cosh 2y}$
D $\frac{\sinh 2x}{\cosh 2x - \cos 2y}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Rationalizing $\frac{\sin(x+iy)}{\cos(x+iy)}$ yields the real part $u = \frac{\sin 2x}{\cos 2x + \cosh 2y}$.

Q7
Which of the following is identically true for all complex $z$?
A $\cosh^2 z - \sinh^2 z = 1$
B $\cosh^2 z + \sinh^2 z = 1$
C $\tanh^2 z + \sech^2 z = 1$
D Both (A) and (C)
Derivation & Analysis:

Both hyperbolic identity variations $\cosh^2 z - \sinh^2 z = 1$ and $\tanh^2 z + \sech^2 z = 1$ hold analytically for all complex $z$ where defined.

Q8
The value of $\tan(i \log(\alpha))$ where $\alpha > 0$ is:
A $i \frac{\alpha^2 - 1}{\alpha^2 + 1}$
B $\frac{\alpha^2 - 1}{\alpha^2 + 1}$
C $i \frac{\alpha - 1}{\alpha + 1}$
D $\frac{\alpha - 1}{\alpha + 1}$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\tan(iz) = i \tanh(z)$. Here $z = \log \alpha$. Thus $i \tanh(\log \alpha) = i \frac{e^{\log \alpha} - e^{-\log \alpha}}{e^{\log \alpha} + e^{-\log \alpha}} = i \frac{\alpha - \alpha^{-1}}{\alpha + \alpha^{-1}} = i \frac{\alpha^2 - 1}{\alpha^2 + 1}$.

Q9
For what values of $z$ does $\sinh z = 0$?
A $z = n\pi$
B $z = n\pi i$
C $z = (2n+1)\frac{\pi}{2}$
D $z = 0$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\sinh z = \frac{e^z - e^{-z}}{2} = 0 \implies e^{2z} = 1 = e^{2n\pi i} \implies 2z = 2n\pi i \implies z = n\pi i$.

Q10
If $\log(\sin(x + iy)) = a + ib$, then $e^{2a}$ equals:
A $\sinh^2 x + \cos^2 y$
B $\cosh^2 x - \sin^2 y$
C $\sin^2 x + \sinh^2 y$
D $\cos^2 x + \sinh^2 y$
Derivation & Analysis:

Taking exponential of both sides, $e^{a+ib} = \sin(x+iy) \implies e^a = |\sin(x+iy)| = \sqrt{\sin^2 x + \sinh^2 y}$. Squaring gives $e^{2a} = \sin^2 x + \sinh^2 y$.
