Complex Numbers

Circular functions of a complex variable

Circular functions of a complex variable extend standard real trigonometric functions via Euler's formula, establishing deep connections with hyperbolic functions. Mastering identities like $\sin(x+iy) = \sin x \cosh y + i \cos x \sinh y$ is essential for high-scoring performance in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Euler's Definitions & Addition Theorems for Complex Circular Functions
For any complex number $z = x + iy$, where $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$, the complex sine, cosine, and tangent are defined as $^\dagger \sin z = \frac{e^{iz} - e^{-iz}}{2i}$, $\cos z = \frac{e^{iz} + e^{-iz}}{2}$, and $\tan z = \frac{\sin z}{\cos z}$, satisfying fundamental periodicity and hyper-trigonometric transformations.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

While real circular functions map real intervals to bounded ranges $[-1, 1]$, complex circular functions map the complex plane (Cartesian strip domains) to unbounded regions in $\mathbb{C}$, bridging Euclidean rotation with hyperbolic scaling.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

They obey analogous identities to real trigonometry (e.g., $\sin^2 z + \cos^2 z = 1$), but exhibit unbounded moduli for purely imaginary arguments due to the underlying exponential growth of $\sinh y$ and $\cosh y$.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Real and Imaginary Parts of Complex Sine

$$\sin(x + iy) = \sin x \cosh y + i \cos x \sinh y$$

Exam Rule: Memorize the sign and hyperbolic alternation: Sine product uses $\sin x \cosh y + i \cos x \sinh y$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT to find the real or imaginary part of $\sin(x+iy)$ or $\cos(x+iy)$.
02

Shortcut 2: Real and Imaginary Parts of Complex Cosine

$$\cos(x + iy) = \cos x \cosh y - i \sin x \sinh y$$

Exam Rule: Note the minus sign for cosine: $\cos x \cosh y - i \sin x \sinh y$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct matching options in TGT/PGT screening tests.
03

Shortcut 3: Modulus of Complex Sine and Cosine

$$|\sin(x + iy)|^2 = \sin^2 x + \sinh^2 y, \quad |\cos(x + iy)|^2 = \cos^2 x + \sinh^2 y$$

Exam Rule: Use $\cosh^2 y - \sinh^2 y = 1$ to quickly evaluate squared moduli without expanding complex exponentials.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard numerical evaluation questions in KVS/DSSSB PGT.
04

Shortcut 4: Purely Imaginary Argument Reduction

$$\sin(iy) = i \sinh y, \quad \cos(iy) = \cosh y, \quad \tan(iy) = i \tanh y$$

Exam Rule: Circular function of pure imaginary number converts directly into hyperbolic function with an $i$ factor for sine/tangent.

⚡ PYQ Context: Rapid elimination trick for multi-statement assertion questions.
05

Shortcut 5: Magnitude Bounds for Sine/Cosine

$$|\sin z| \ge |\sin x|, \quad |\cos z| \ge |\cos x| \text{ when } y \neq 0$$

Exam Rule: Since $\cosh y \ge 1$ for all real $y$, the modulus can exceed 1 unlike real trigonometry.

⚡ PYQ Context: Tested in conceptual statement-based questions.
06

Shortcut 6: Periodicity of Complex Tangent

$$\tan(z + \pi) = \tan z, \quad \text{Fundamental period is } \pi$$

Exam Rule: Tangent and cotangent maintain period $\pi$, while sine and cosine maintain $2\pi$ in the complex plane.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct factual recall item in teaching recruitment exams.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Boundedness Fallacy

Common Pitfall: Assuming $|\sin z| \le 1$ for all complex numbers $z$, carrying over real trigonometric intuition.

Counterexample: $z = i y$, where $\sin(iy) = i \sinh y \to \infty$ as $y \to \infty$

Why it fails: Hyperbolic sine grows exponentially; hence complex sine and cosine are unbounded in the complex plane.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Never select options stating that complex circular functions are bounded on $\mathbb{C}$.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Hyperbolic Sign Confusion

Common Pitfall: Mixing up the plus and minus signs in $\cos(x+iy) = \cos x \cosh y \pm i \sin x \sinh y$.

Counterexample: Using $+i$ instead of $-i$ in the expansion of $\cos(x+iy)$

Why it fails: Leads to incorrect conjugate matching and wrong sign in options (A) vs (B).

🛡️ Exam Defense: Remember: Sine preserves the inner sign ($+$), while Cosine reverses it ($-$).

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $\sin(x + iy) = \u + iv$, find the value of $u$ and $v$ in terms of $x$ and $y$.

(A) $u = \sin x \cosh y, v = \cos x \sinh y$(B) $u = \sin x \sinh y, v = \cos x \cosh y$(C) $u = \cos x \cosh y, v = \sin x \sinh y$(D) $u = \cos x \sinh y, v = \sin x \cosh y$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write $\sin(x+iy) = \sin x \cos(iy) + \cos x \sin(iy)$. Step 2: Use $\cos(iy) = \cosh y$ and $\sin(iy) = i \sinh y$. Step 3: Equate real ($u$) and imaginary ($v$) parts to get $u = \sin x \cosh y$ and $v = \cos x \sinh y$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recall standard expansion formula: Sine pairs $\sin x$ with $\cosh y$ for real part and $\cos x$ with $\sinh y$ for imaginary part.
💡 Key Takeaway: Direct formula recall item. Do not waste time deriving during exam.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $\cos(x + iy) = A + iB$, what is $A^2 \sec^2 x - B^2 \csc^2 x$?

(A) $0$(B) $1$(C) $-1$(D) $\cosh^2 y$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Here $A = \cos x \cosh y$ and $B = -\sin x \sinh y$. Step 2: Compute $A^2 = \cos^2 x \cosh^2 y$ and $B^2 = \sin^2 x \sinh^2 y$. Step 3: Substitute into expression using $\cosh^2 y - \sinh^2 y = 1$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize standard identity transformation yielding unity.
💡 Key Takeaway: Look for $\cosh^2 y - \sinh^2 y = 1$ hidden inside trigonometric identities.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
MCQ 1: The value of $\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} + i\log 2\right)$ is equal to:
A (A) $\frac{5}{4}$
B (B) $\frac{5}{8}$
C (C) $\frac{3}{4}$
D (D) $2$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using $\sin(\frac{\pi}{2} + iy) = \sin(\frac{\pi}{2}) \cosh y = \cosh y$. Here $y = \log 2$, so $\cosh(\log 2) = \frac{e^{\log 2} + e^{-\log 2}}{2} = \frac{2 + 1/2}{2} = \frac{5}{4}.

Q2
MCQ 2: If $\tan(x + iy) = u + iv$, then $u$ is given by:
A (A) $\frac{\sin 2x}{\cosh 2y - \cos 2x}$
B (B) $\frac{\sin 2x}{\cosh 2y + \cos 2x}$
C (C) $\frac{\cos 2x}{\cosh 2y + \sin 2x}$
D (D) $\frac{\sinh 2y}{\cosh 2y + \cos 2x}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Expanding $\tan(x+iy) = \frac{\sin(x+iy)}{\cos(x+iy)}$ and multiplying numerator and denominator by $\cos(x-iy)$ yields $u = \frac{\sin 2x}{\cosh 2y + \cos 2x}$.

Q3
MCQ 3: The real part of $\cos(i)$ is:
A (A) $\cos 1$
B (B) $\cosh 1$
C (C) $-\cosh 1$
D (D) $i \sinh 1$
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $\cos(iy) = \cosh y$, for $y = 1$, $\cos(i) = \cosh 1$, which is real.

Q4
MCQ 4: Which of the following relations is correct for complex variables?
A (A) $\sin^2 z + \cos^2 z = 1$
B (B) $\sin^2 z - \cos^2 z = 1$
C (C) $1 + \tan^2 z = \csc^2 z$
D (D) $1 - \tan^2 z = \sec^2 z$
Derivation & Analysis:

Standard trigonometric identity $\sin^2 z + \cos^2 z = 1$ holds identically for all complex $z$ due to analytic continuation.

Q5
MCQ 5: If $\sec(x + iy) = u + iv$, then $v$ is equal to:
A (A) $\sec x \sech y$
B (B) $\sin x \tanh y$
C (C) $\frac{2 \sin x \sinh y}{\cos 2x + \cosh 2y}$
D (D) $\frac{2 \cos x \cosh y}{\cos 2x + \cosh 2y}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $\sec(x+iy) = \frac{1}{\cos x \cosh y - i \sin x \sinh y}$, rationalizing gives the imaginary part as $\frac{2 \sin x \sinh y}{\cos 2x + \cosh 2y}$.

Q6
MCQ 6: The value of $|\sin(x+iy)|^2$ is:
A (A) $\sin^2 x + \cos^2 y$
B (B) $\sin^2 x + \sinh^2 y$
C (C) $\cos^2 x + \cosh^2 y$
D (D) $\sinh^2 x + \sin^2 y$
Derivation & Analysis:

$|\sin(x+iy)|^2 = |\sin x \cosh y + i \cos x \sinh y|^2 = \sin^2 x \cosh^2 y + \cos^2 x \sinh^2 y = \sin^2 x(1 + \sinh^2 y) + \cos^2 x \sinh^2 y = \sin^2 x + \sinh^2 y$.

Q7
MCQ 7: If $\alpha + i\beta = \log(\sin(x + iy))$, then $\beta$ equals:
A (A) $\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\cot x}{\tanh y}\right)$
B (B) $\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\tan x}{\tanh y}\right)$
C (C) $\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\tanh y}{\tan x}\right)$
D (D) $\tan^{-1}(\tan x \tanh y)$
Derivation & Analysis:

Taking imaginary parts of $\log(\sin(x+iy))$ gives $\beta = \arg(\sin(x+iy)) = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\cos x \sinh y}{\sin x \cosh y}\right) = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\cot x}{\tanh y}\right)$, wait let's check: $\frac{v}{u} = \frac{\cos x \sinh y}{\sin x \cosh y} = \cot x \tanh y = \frac{\tanh y}{\tan x}$. Therefore $\beta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\tanh y}{\tan x}\right)$.

Q8
MCQ 8: For a complex number $z$, $\sin z = 0$ implies:
A (A) $z = n\pi, n \in \mathbb{Z}$
B (B) $z = i n\pi, n \in \mathbb{Z}$
C (C) $z = \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$
D (D) $z = 0$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\sin z = \frac{e^{iz} - e^{-iz}}{2i} = 0 \implies e^{2iz} = 1 \implies 2iz = 2n\pi i \implies z = n\pi$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. Roots remain purely real.

Q9
MCQ 9: The value of $\cosh(i\pi)$ is:
A (A) $1$
B (B) $-1$
C (C) $0$
D (D) $i$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\cosh(i\pi) = \cos(\pi) = -1$ using the relation $\cosh(iy) = \cos y$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $\sin z = x$, and $x > 1$ is real, then $z$ is:
A (A) Purely real
B (B) Purely imaginary
C (C) Complex with real part $\frac{\pi}{2}$
D (D) Complex with imaginary part $0$
Derivation & Analysis:

Solving $\sin z = x > 1$ yields $z = \frac{\pi}{2} + i y$ or similar branches where the real part is an odd multiple of $\frac{\pi}{2}$.
