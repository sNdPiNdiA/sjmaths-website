10. Trigonometry & Complex Numbers

De Moivre's theorem

De Moivre's theorem bridges complex analysis and trigonometry by stating that $(\cos \theta + i \sin \theta)^n = \cos(n \theta) + i \sin(n \theta)$ for any integer $n$. Its geometric interpretation involves scaling and rotating points on the unit circle in the Argand plane, making it a high-yield scoring zone in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations for finding roots of unity and simplifying trigonometric polynomials.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

De Moivre's Theorem for Rational Indices
For any real number $\theta$ and for any integer $n$, $$(\cos \theta + i \sin \theta)^n = \cos(n \theta) + i \sin(n \theta)$$. Furthermore, if $n$ is a rational number $p/q$ where $q \in \mathbb{N}$ and $\gcd(p, q) = 1$, then $\cos(n \theta) + i \sin(n \theta)$ is one of the values of $(\cos \theta + i \sin \theta)^n$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

In the Argand plane, multiplication of complex numbers corresponds to addition of arguments and multiplication of moduli. Raising a complex number of unit modulus (i.e., on the unit circle) to the power $n$ rotates its position vector by an angle $n \theta$ relative to the positive real axis.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It establishes a fundamental isomorphism between the cyclic group of rotations under multiplication and the additive group of angles modulo $2\pi$. It converts powers of trigonometric binomials into linear combinations of multiple angles.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Real Argument $\theta$ N/A Strictly Mandatory / Necessary If $\theta$ is complex, the theorem in its standard form fails unless generalized via Euler's formula $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$.
Integer or Rational Exponent $n$ N/A Strictly Mandatory If $n$ is irrational or complex, $(\cos \theta + i \sin \theta)^n$ becomes a multi-valued function, and direct application without proper branch cuts leads to paradoxes.
Principal Branch & Periodicity N/A Essential for root extraction Omitting the $2k\pi$ periodicity when extracting roots via $n$-th roots of unity results in missing $n-1$ distinct roots.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Sum of $n$-th Roots of Unity

$$1 + \alpha + \alpha^2 + \dots + \alpha^{n-1} = 0$$ where $\alpha = e^{i 2\pi / n}$

Exam Rule: The sum of all $n$-th roots of unity is always zero for $n \ge 2$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT to evaluate sums like $1 + \omega + \omega^2$ or higher order cyclic sums.
02

Shortcut 2: Product of $n$-th Roots of Unity

$$1 \cdot \alpha \cdot \alpha^2 \dots \alpha^{n-1} = (-1)^{n-1}$$

Exam Rule: The product of all distinct $n$-th roots of unity evaluates directly to $(-1)^{n-1}$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in DSSSB and KVS PGT examinations.
03

Shortcut 3: Reciprocal Sum Simplification

$$(x + \frac{1}{x})^n = 2\cos(n\theta) \text{ when } x = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$$

Exam Rule: Instantly convert binomial powers of trigonometric ratios into multiple-angle sums.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard pattern in UP TGT for evaluating integrals or expansions of $\cos^n \theta$.
04

Shortcut 4: Sum of Angles in Roots

$$\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \cos\left(\theta + \frac{2k\pi}{n}\right) = 0$$

Exam Rule: Symmetrically distributed angles on a circle sum to zero for both sine and cosine components.

⚡ PYQ Context: Appears in advanced trigonometric series summation in UP PGT.
05

Shortcut 5: Modulus-Argument Form Conversion

$$(1 + \cos\theta + i\sin\theta)^n = \left(2\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^n \left(\cos\frac{n\theta}{2} + i\sin\frac{n\theta}{2}\right)$$

Exam Rule: Factor out half-angles immediately to simplify complex binomial expressions.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial time-saver in 125-question PGT papers.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Degree Checking

$$ \text{Degree of resulting polynomial} = n $$

Exam Rule: Eliminate options where trigonometric multiple angles do not match the index $n$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used for rejecting incorrect options instantly in multiple-choice questions.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Ignoring Multi-Valuedness for Rational Indices

Common Pitfall: Treating $(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)^{p/q}$ as having only a single value instead of $q$ distinct values.

Counterexample: $$(1)^{1/3} = 1 \text{ (ignoring } e^{i 2\pi/3} \text{ and } e^{i 4\pi/3})$$

Why it fails: Failing to include $2k\pi$ ($k = 0, 1, \dots, q-1$) causes candidates to lose marks when asked for 'all values'.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always append $+ 2k\pi$ to the argument before applying fractional indices.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Misapplication with Minus Signs

Common Pitfall: Applying De Moivre's theorem directly to $(\cos\theta - i\sin\theta)^n$ without adjusting the sign.

Counterexample: $(\cos\theta - i\sin\theta)^n = [\cos(-\theta) + i\sin(-\theta)]^n = \cos(n\theta) - i\sin(n\theta)$

Why it fails: Directly writing $\cos(n\theta) + i\sin(n\theta)$ for negative sine terms violates the standard form.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Ensure the expression is strictly in the form $(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$ with a plus sign before applying the theorem.

What is the sum of the roots of the equation $z^5 - 1 = 0$?

✓ Correct Answer: (B)

Step 1: The roots are the 5th roots of unity: $1, \alpha, \alpha^2, \alpha^3, \alpha^4$. Step 2: By the theory of equations, the sum of the roots of $z^n - 1 = 0$ is the coefficient of $z^{n-1}$, which is $0$.

Sum of all $n$-th roots of unity for $n \ge 2$ is always zero.

Let $z-1 = y$, then $y^3 = 8 \implies y = 2(1)^{1/3}$. The cube roots of unity are $1, \omega, \omega^2$. Thus $y = 2, 2\omega, 2\omega^2$. Adding $1$ gives $z = 3, 1+2(-1/2 + i\sqrt{3}/2) = 1 - 1 + i\sqrt{3} = 1 + i\sqrt{3}$ and $1 - i\sqrt{3}$.

Q7
If $\omega$ is a complex cube root of unity, then the value of $(1 + \omega)(1 + \omega^2)(1 + \omega^4)(1 + \omega^8)$ is:
A $0$
B $1$
C $-1$
D $3$
Derivation & Analysis:

Simplify terms using $\omega^3 = 1$: $\omega^4 = \omega$ and $\omega^8 = \omega^2$. The product becomes $(1+\omega)(1+\omega^2)(1+\omega)(1+\omega^2) = (-\omega^2)(-\omega)(-\omega^2)(-\omega) = \omega^3 \cdot \omega^3 = 1 \cdot 1 = 1$.

Q8
If $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are the roots of $z^3 - a = 0$, then $\alpha \beta \gamma$ is equal to:
A $a$
B $-a$
C $0$
D $1$
Derivation & Analysis:

By the theory of equations for $z^3 - a = 0$, the product of the roots is $(-1)^3 ( -a ) = a$ (or constant term with proper sign).

Q9
The real part of $(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)^n$ is:
A $\sin(n\theta)$
B $\cos(n\theta)$
C $-\cos(n\theta)$
D $-\sin(n\theta)$
Derivation & Analysis:

By De Moivre's theorem, $(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)^n = \cos(n\theta) + i\sin(n\theta)$. The real part is $\cos(n\theta)$.

Q10
If $x + \frac{1}{x} = 2\cos\theta$, then the value of $x^3 + \frac{1}{x^3}$ is:
A $2\cos(3\theta)$
B $2\sin(3\theta)$
C $\cos(3\theta)$
D $6\cos\theta$
Derivation & Analysis:

Setting $x = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$, we get $x^3 + 1/x^3 = 2\cos(3\theta)$ directly from De Moivre's expansion.
