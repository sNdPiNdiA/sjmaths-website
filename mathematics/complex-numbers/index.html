10. Trigonometry & Complex Numbers

Complex numbers

Complex numbers extend the real number system by introducing the imaginary unit $i$, satisfying $i^2 = -1$, forming an algebraically closed field $\mathbb{C}$ equipped with a rich geometric interpretation in the Argand plane. Mastery of modulus, argument, De Moivre's theorem, and root extraction forms a high-scoring cornerstone for UP TGT/PGT, DSSSB, and KVS examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

De Moivre's Theorem for Rational Indices
For any real number $\theta$ and for any rational number $n$, $(\cos \theta + i \sin \theta)^n = \cos(n\theta) + i \sin(n\theta)$. When $n$ is a rational number $\frac{p}{q}$ (where $p, q \in \mathbb{Z}, q > 0$), $\cos(n\theta) + i \sin(n\theta)$ is one of the values of $(\cos \theta + i \sin \theta)^{p/q}$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Multiplication of two complex numbers in polar form scales their moduli and adds their arguments; raising a complex number to the $n$-th power scales its radius by $r^n$ and rotates its vector in the Argand plane by an angle $n\theta$.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Bridges trigonometric identities with polynomial algebra, enabling efficient computation of powers and roots of unity.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Polar Representation Validity $\theta \in [-\pi, \pi]$ or $[0, 2\pi)$ Strictly Mandatory Failure to use principal arguments leads to multi-valued ambiguity and incorrect root extraction.
Modulus Non-Zero ($r > 0$) $z \neq 0$ Strictly Mandatory for Polar/Exponential forms Argument $\arg(0)$ is undefined, collapsing polar conversions.
Rationality of Index $n$ $n \in \mathbb{Q}$ Essential for finite root sets Irrational exponents yield infinitely many values, requiring complex branch analysis.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Sum of $n$-th Roots of Unity

$$ \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} e^{i 2\pi k/n} = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \prod_{k=0}^{n-1} (1 - \omega^k) = n $$

Exam Rule: The sum of all $n$-th roots of unity is always zero for $n \ge 2$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT and KVS PGT for evaluating polynomial roots and symmetric sums.
02

Shortcut 2: Modulus Property of Complex Fractions

$$ \left| \frac{az + b}{\bar{b}z + \bar{a}} \right| = 1 \quad \text{when } |z| = 1 $$

Exam Rule: If $|z| = 1$, bilinear transformations of this specific conjugate-symmetric form maintain unit modulus.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequent locus questions in TGT/PGT examinations.
03

Shortcut 3: Triangle Inequality Extremes

$$ |z_1| - |z_2| \le |z_1 \pm z_2| \le |z_1| + |z_2| $$

Exam Rule: Maximum distance occurs when vectors are collinear and codirectional; minimum occurs when opposite.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used for finding maximum and minimum values of moduli combinations in competitive tests.
04

Shortcut 4: Rotation of Complex Numbers

$$ z_2 - z_0 = (z_1 - z_0) e^{i \theta} $$

Exam Rule: To rotate a point $z_1$ about center $z_0$ by angle $\theta$ counterclockwise, multiply the shifted vector by $e^{i\theta}$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard geometry problems involving equilateral triangles or squares on the Argand plane.
05

Shortcut 5: Real and Imaginary Power Extraction

$$ (1 \pm i)^n = 2^{n/2} \left( \cos\left(\frac{n\pi}{4}\right) \pm i \sin\left(\frac{n\pi}{4}\right) \right) $$

Exam Rule: Convert binomials like $1+i$ or $\sqrt{3}+i$ instantly to polar form before applying powers.

⚡ PYQ Context: Simplifies tedious binomial expansions in PGT exams within 15 seconds.
06

Shortcut 6: Locus Identification Trick

$$ |z - z_1| = |z - z_2| \implies \text{Perpendicular bisector of segment } z_1 z_2 $$

Exam Rule: Recognize standard distance forms to instantly mark straight lines, circles, or ellipses without algebraic expansion.

⚡ PYQ Context: Time-saving pattern matching for 125-question PGT papers.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Principal Argument Miscalculation

Common Pitfall: Using $\tan^{-1}(y/x)$ blindly without checking the quadrant in which the complex point $(x, y)$ lies.

Counterexample: $z = -1 - i$

Why it fails: $\tan^{-1}((-1)/(-1)) = \tan^{-1}(1) = \pi/4$, but $z$ is in the third quadrant, so $\arg(z) = -3\pi/4$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always sketch the point in the Argand plane before determining its argument.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Power Law Distribution on Non-Real Bases

Common Pitfall: Assuming $(z_1 z_2)^{1/n} = z_1^{1/n} z_2^{1/n}$ without accounting for all branch values.

Counterexample: Roots of $(ab)^{1/n}$ versus $a^{1/n} b^{1/n}$

Why it fails: Radicals are set-valued functions in complex analysis; naive splitting misses $n-1$ other roots.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always use exponential polar form $r e^{i(\theta + 2k\pi)}$ when extracting roots.

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $\omega$ is an imaginary cube root of unity, then the value of $(1 - \omega + \omega^2)^5 + (1 + \omega - \omega^2)^5$ is:

(A) $32$(B) $-32$(C) $64$(D) $-64$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Use $1 + \omega + \omega^2 = 0$. Step 2: Rewrite $1 + \omega^2 = -\omega$ and $1 + \omega = -%\omega^2$. Step 3: Substitute into expression to get $( -2\omega )^5 + ( -2\omega^2 )^5$. Step 4: Simplify using $\omega^3 = 1$ to obtain $-32(\omega^2 + \omega) = -32(-1) = 32$ (Wait, let's recalculate: $(-2\omega)^5 = -32\omega^2$ and $(-2\omega^2)^5 = -32\omega^{10} = -32\omega$. Sum is $-32(\omega^2 + \omega) = -32(-1) = 32$).
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Replace binomial parts instantly using $\omega$ properties, factor out $-32$, and use $\omega+\omega^2=-1$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Keep core identities $1+\omega+\omega^2=0$ and $\omega^3=1$ memorized.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

The locus of complex number $z$ satisfying $\left| \frac{z - i}{z + i} \right| = 1$ in the complex plane is:

(A) A circle(B) The real axis(C) The imaginary axis(D) A straight line parallel to real axis
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Set $|z - i| = |z + i|$. Step 2: Let $z = x + iy$. Step 3: $|x + i(y-1)| = |x + i(y+1)|$. Step 4: Square both sides: $x^2 + (y-1)^2 = x^2 + (y+1)^2 \implies -2y = 2y \implies y = 0$. Step 5: $y=0$ represents the real axis.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
The equation $|z - z_1| = |z - z_2|$ represents the perpendicular bisector of the line joining $z_1 = i$ and $z_2 = -i$. The perpendicular bisector of the imaginary axis segment between $i$ and $-i$ is the real axis ($y=0$).
💡 Key Takeaway: Instantly recognize geometric loci without expanding Cartesian coordinates.

Q1
MCQ 1: If $z = x + iy$ and $|z + 1| = |z - 1|$, then the locus of $z$ is:
A Real axis
B Imaginary axis
C Circle of radius 1
D Parabola
Derivation & Analysis:

$|x+1 + iy| = |x-1 + iy| \implies (x+1)^2 + y^2 = (x-1)^2 + y^2 \implies 4x = 0 \implies x = 0$, which is the imaginary axis.

Q2
MCQ 2: The amplitude of $\frac{1 + i \sqrt{3}}{1 + i}$ is:
A $\frac{\pi}{12}$
B $\frac{\pi}{6}$
C $\frac{\pi}{3}$
D $\frac{5\pi}{12}$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\arg(1+i\sqrt{3}) = \pi/3$, $\arg(1+i) = \pi/4$. Argument of quotient is difference: $\pi/3 - \pi/4 = \pi/12$.

Q3
MCQ 3: If $\omega$ is a non-real cube root of unity, then $(1 - \omega + \omega^2)(1 + \omega - \omega^2)$ equals:
A $1$
B $2$
C $4$
D $8$
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $1+\omega^2 = -\omega$, first factor is $(-2\omega)$. Since $1+\omega = -\omega^2$, second factor is $(-2\omega^2)$. Product is $4\omega^3 = 4$.

Q4
MCQ 4: The value of $\sum_{k=1}^{10} (i^k + i^{k+1})$ where $i = \sqrt{-1}$ is:
A $0$
B $1+i$
C $-1+i$
D $i$
Derivation & Analysis:

Sum is a geometric series of powers of $i$. Simplifying cycles of 4 yields $-1+i$.

Q5
MCQ 5: If $|z| = 4$ and $\arg(z) = \frac{5\pi}{6}$, then $z$ in cartesian form is:
A $-2\sqrt{3} + 2i$
B $2\sqrt{3} - 2i$
C $-2 + 2\sqrt{3}i$
D $2 + 2\sqrt{3}i$
Derivation & Analysis:

$z = 4(\cos(5\pi/6) + i \sin(5\pi/6)) = 4(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + i\frac{1}{2}) = -2\sqrt{3} + 2i$.

Q6
MCQ 6: The number of solutions to the equation $z^2 + \bar{z} = 0$ in $\mathbb{C}$ is:
A $1$
B $2$
C $3$
D $4$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $z = x+iy$. Substituting $z^2 + \bar{z} = 0$ and separating real and imaginary parts gives 4 distinct solutions: $0, 1, -1/2 + i\sqrt{3}/2, -1/2 - i\sqrt{3}/2$.

Q7
MCQ 7: If $z = \frac{1}{1 - \cos\theta + i \sin\theta}$, then $\text{Re}(z)$ is equal to:
A $\frac{1}{2}$
B $\frac{1}{2}\cot(\theta/2)$
C $\frac{1}{2}\tan(\theta/2)$
D $1$
Derivation & Analysis:

Rationalize denominator or use half-angle identities to find $\text{Re}(z) = 1/2$.

Q8
MCQ 8: If $1, \omega, \omega^2$ are the cube roots of unity, then $(1 - \omega)(1 - \omega^2)(1 - \omega^4)(1 - \omega^5)$ equals:
A $3$
B $9$
C $27$
D $81$
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $\omega^3 = 1$, $\omega^4 = \omega$ and $\omega^5 = \omega^2$. The expression evaluates to $(1-\omega)^2(1-\omega^2)^2 = [ (1-\omega)(1-\omega^2) ]^2 = (3)^2 = 9$.

Q9
MCQ 9: The minimum value of $|z| + |z - 3i|$ for $z \in \mathbb{C}$ is:
A $0$
B $2$
C $3$
D $5$
Derivation & Analysis:

By triangle inequality, $|z| + |3i - z| \ge |3i - 0| = 3$. The minimum value is $3$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $\left(\frac{1+i}{1-i}\right)^x = 1$, then the least positive integral value of $x$ is:
A $1$
B $2$
C $4$
D $8$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\frac{1+i}{1-i} = i$. Thus $i^x = 1$. The least positive integer for which $i^x = 1$ is $x = 4$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

The argument function is multi-valued due to the periodicity of trigonometric functions ($2\pi$). Restricting it to a principal interval ensures uniqueness in polar representation.

Sign errors during quadrant placement while finding principal arguments and mishandling branch cuts when applying fractional powers.

Typically 6 to 9 questions appear directly or indirectly, covering roots of unity, loci, moduli properties, and De Moivre's theorem.

Memorize geometric interpretations of standard equations (e.g., perpendicular bisectors, circles, Apollonius circles) rather than expanding into Cartesian coordinates algebraically.

MCQ 9: The minimum value of $|z| + |z - 3i|$ for $z \in \mathbb{C}$ is:
A $0$
B $2$
C $3$
D $5$
Derivation & Analysis:

By triangle inequality, $|z| + |3i - z| \ge |3i - 0| = 3$. The minimum value is $3$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $\left(\frac{1+i}{1-i}\right)^x = 1$, then the least positive integral value of $x$ is:
A $1$
B $2$
C $4$
D $8$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\frac{1+i}{1-i} = i$. Thus $i^x = 1$. The least positive integer for which $i^x = 1$ is $x = 4$.
