Calculus

Differentiation of logarithmic functions

The differentiation of logarithmic functions forms the analytical cornerstone of calculus, allowing the linearization of complex products, quotients, and powers via logarithmic differentiation. Mastering standard forms like $\frac{d}{dx}(\ln x) = \frac{1}{x}$ and logarithmic properties is crucial for scoring high in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Derivative of Natural and General Logarithmic Functions
Let $f(x) = \log_a(x)$, where $a > 0$ and $a \neq 1$, and $x \in (0, \infty)$. Then $f'(x) = \frac{1}{x \ln a}$. In particular, for the natural logarithm where $a = e$, $\frac{d}{dx}(\ln x) = \frac{1}{x}$. For composite functions, if $y = \ln[u(x)]$, then $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{u(x)} \cdot u'(x).$

📐 Geometric Interpretation

The derivative $\frac{1}{x}$ represents the instantaneous rate of change of the logarithmic curve, showing that as $x$ increases, the slope of the tangent line decreases monotonically, reflecting the concave-down nature of the logarithmic graph ($f''(x) = -\frac{1}{x^2} < 0$).

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Logarithms convert multiplicative structures into additive structures ($\log(uv) = \log u + \log v$), which simplifies polynomial powers and variable exponents through logarithmic differentiation before applying standard derivative rules.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Domain Positivity ($x > 0$) (0, \infty) (Open interval) Strictly Mandatory / Necessary The argument of a real logarithm must be strictly positive; non-positive values result in undefined real logarithmic outputs.
Base Constraints ($a > 0, a \neq 1$) Discrete parameter constraint Strictly Mandatory A base of 1 is undefined for division by $\ln(1) = 0$, and negative bases lead to complex multi-valued functions.
Differentiability of Inner Function $u(x) Set where $u(x) > 0$ and $u'(x)$ exists Essential for chain rule application If $u(x)$ fails to be differentiable or becomes zero or negative, the composite derivative $\frac{u'(x)}{u(x)}$ is invalid.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Power-Function Differentiation via Logarithms

$$ \frac{d}{dx}[f(x)]^{g(x)} = [f(x)]^{g(x)} \left[ g'(x) \ln f(x) + g(x) \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)} \right] $$

Exam Rule: Direct formula for functions of the form $y = u(x)^{v(x)}$ without performing full logarithmic differentiation from scratch every time.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT and DSSSB PGT for functions like $x^x$, $(\sin x)^{\cos x}$, etc.
02

Shortcut 2: Logarithmic Differentiation for Products/Quotients

$$ \frac{d}{dx} \ln \left( \prod_{i=1}^{n} f_i(x)^{k_i} \right) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} k_i \frac{f_i'(x)}{f_i(x)} $$

Exam Rule: Taking $\ln$ on both sides transforms products and quotients into sums and differences of simple fractions.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard exam shortcut to differentiate bulky rational functions rapidly.
03

Shortcut 3: Derivative of Infinite Logarithmic Towers

$$ \text{If } y = x^{x^{x^{\dots}}}, \text{ then } \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y^2}{x(1 - y \ln x)} $$

Exam Rule: Substitute $y$ for the infinite tail and differentiate implicitly after taking natural logarithms.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct pattern recognition for UP TGT/PGT repeating tower problems.
04

Shortcut 4: Implicit Logarithmic Derivative Shortcut

$$ \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}}{\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}} $$

Exam Rule: Use partial derivatives for implicit equations containing logarithmic terms like $\ln(xy) + x^2 y = 5$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves 2-3 minutes of algebraic rearrangement in competitive exams.
05

Shortcut 5: Logarithmic Derivative of Modulus Arguments

$$ \frac{d}{dx}(\ln |f(x)|) = \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)} \quad \forall \, f(x) \neq 0 $$

Exam Rule: The absolute value sign inside a logarithm can be ignored during differentiation because the derivative is identical wherever $f(x) \neq 0$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Tested in trick questions involving domains spanning negative numbers.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Log-Slope Testing

$$ \left. \frac{d}{dx} \ln(1+x) \right|_{x=0} = 1 $$

Exam Rule: Test $x=0$ or $x=1$ on options to eliminate incorrect derivative expressions immediately.

⚡ PYQ Context: Essential time-management trick for 125-question PGT papers.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Ignoring Domain Restrictions of Logarithms

Common Pitfall: Applying derivative formulas without checking if the domain of the resulting expression matches the original function.

Counterexample: f(x) = \ln(x^2) \implies f'(x) = \frac{2x}{x^2} = \frac{2}{x}

Why it fails: While $\ln(x^2)$ is defined for all $x \neq 0$, the simplified derivative $\frac{2}{x}$ has the exact same domain, but rewriting it as $2 \ln|x|$ changes the domain perspective if not careful.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always simplify using properties of logarithms ($\ln(x^n) = n \ln|x|$) *before* differentiating to avoid domain discrepancies.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Misapplying Chain Rule to General Logarithms

Common Pitfall: Forgetting the $\ln a$ term in the denominator when differentiating $\log_a x$.

Counterexample: \frac{d}{dx}(\log_{10} x) = \frac{1}{x} \text{ (Incorrect omission of } \ln 10 \text{)}

Why it fails: Base change formula states $\log_a x = \frac{\ln x}{\ln a}$, hence the derivative must include $\frac{1}{\ln a}$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always convert non-natural logs using change of base: $\log_a u = \frac{\ln u}{\ln a}$ before differentiation if unsure.

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the derivative of $y = x^{\sin x}$ with respect to $x$.

(A) $x^{\sin x} \left(\frac{\sin x}{x} + \cos x \ln x\right)$(B) $x^{\sin x} (\cos x \ln x)$(C) $\sin x \cdot x^{\sin x - 1}$(D) $x^{\cos x} (\sin x \ln x)$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Take natural log on both sides: $\ln y = \sin x \ln x$. Step 2: Differentiate implicitly w.r.t $x$: $\frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = \cos x \ln x + \sin x \cdot \frac{1}{x}$. Step 3: Multiply by $y = x^{\sin x}$ to get $\frac{dy}{dx} = x^{\sin x} \left(\frac{\sin x}{x} + \cos x \ln x\right)$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Apply Shortcut 1 directly: $\frac{d}{dx}[u^v] = u^v [v' \ln u + v \frac{u'}{u}]$. Here $u=x, v=\sin x$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Standard variable-power format frequently tested in UP PGT.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $y = \log_7(\log_3 x)$, find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ at $x = 3$.

(A) $\frac{1}{3 \ln 7}$(B) $\frac{1}{3 \ln 3 \ln 7}$(C) $\frac{1}{\ln 21}$(D) $\frac{1}{7 \ln 3}$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Rewrite using change of base: $y = \frac{\ln(\ln x)}{\ln 7}$. Step 2: Differentiate using chain rule: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{\ln 7} \cdot \frac{1}{\ln x} \cdot \frac{1}{x}$. Step 3: Substitute $x = 3$: $\left.\frac{dy}{dx}\right|_{x=3} = \frac{1}{3 \ln 3 \ln 7}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Chain rule from outside in: $\frac{1}{(\ln 3) \ln 7} \times \frac{1}{3}$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Watch out for base conversion constants like $\ln 7$ in denominators.

Q1
MCQ 1: What is the derivative of $\ln(\sec x + \tan x)$ with respect to $x$?
A $\sec x$
B $\csc x$
C $\tan x$
D $\cos x$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $y = \ln(\sec x + \tan x)$. Then $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{\sec x + \tan x}(\sec x \tan x + \sec^2 x) = \frac{\sec x(\tan x + \sec x)}{\sec x + \tan x} = \sec x$.

Q2
MCQ 2: If $y = \log_x(e)$, then $\frac{dy}{dx}$ is equal to:
A $-\frac{1}{x(\ln x)^2}$
B $\frac{1}{x \ln x}$
C $-\frac{1}{x^2}$
D 0
Derivation & Analysis:

Using change of base, $y = \frac{\ln e}{\ln x} = \frac{1}{\ln x} = (\ln x)^{-1}$. Differentiating using chain rule: $\frac{dy}{dx} = -1(\ln x)^{-2} \cdot \frac{1}{x} = -\frac{1}{x(\ln x)^2}.

Q3
MCQ 3: If $y = x^x$, the value of $\frac{dy}{dx}$ at $x = 1$ is:
A 0
B 1
C $e$
D $1 + e$
Derivation & Analysis:

For $y = x^x$, $\frac{dy}{dx} = x^x(1 + \ln x)$. At $x = 1$, $\frac{dy}{dx} = 1^1(1 + \ln 1) = 1(1 + 0) = 1$.

Q4
MCQ 4: If $f(x) = \log_10(5x^2 + 3)$, then $f'(x)$ is:
A $\frac{10x}{(5x^2 + 3)\ln 10}$
B $\frac{10x}{5x^2 + 3}$
C $\frac{1}{(5x^2 + 3)\ln 10}$
D $\frac{5x}{(5x^2 + 3)\ln 10}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Derivative of $\log_a u$ is $\frac{u'}{u \ln a}$. Here $u = 5x^2 + 3$, so $u' = 10x$ and $a = 10$. Thus $f'(x) = \frac{10x}{(5x^2 + 3)\ln 10}$.

Q5
MCQ 5: The derivative of $\ln(\sqrt{x})$ with respect to $x$ is:
A $\frac{1}{x}$
B $\frac{1}{2x}$
C $\frac{2}{x}$
D $\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Simplify first: $\ln(\sqrt{x}) = \frac{1}{2} \ln x$. Differentiating gives $\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{x} = \frac{1}{2x}$.

Q6
MCQ 6: If $y = \sqrt{\ln x + \sqrt{\ln x + \sqrt{\ln x + \dots_\infty}}}$, then $\frac{dy}{dx}$ is:
A $\frac{1}{x(2y - 1)}$
B $\frac{1}{x \ln x}$
C $\frac{y}{x}$
D $\frac{1}{2y}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Equation reduces to $y = \sqrt{\ln x + y} \implies y^2 = \ln x + y$. Differentiating implicitly: $2y y' = \frac{1}{x} + y' \implies y'(2y - 1) = \frac{1}{x} \implies y' = \frac{1}{x(2y - 1)}$.

Q7
MCQ 7: If $y = x^{\ln x}$, then $\frac{dy}{dx}$ at $x = e$ is:
A $2$
B $e$
C $1$
D $2e$
Derivation & Analysis:

Taking $\ln$: $\ln y = (\ln x)^2$. Differentiating: $\frac{1}{y} y' = 2(\ln x) \cdot \frac{1}{x} \implies y' = x^{\ln x} \left(\frac{2 \ln x}{x}\right)$. At $x = e$, $e^{\ln e} \left(\frac{2 \ln e}{e}\right) = e \left(\frac{2(1)}{e}\right) = 2$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): $\frac{d}{dx}(\ln |x|) = \frac{1}{x}$ for all $x \neq 0$. Reason (R): The domain of $\ln |x|$ is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

Both statements are mathematically rigorous and correct. The derivative holds across the entire domain of $\ln|x|$ which is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$.

Q9
MCQ 9: If $y = \ln \left( \frac{e^x}{1 + e^x} \right)$, then $\frac{dy}{dx}$ is:
A $\frac{1}{1 + e^x}$
B $-\frac{1}{1 + e^x}$
C $\frac{1}{1 - e^x}$
D $1$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using log properties: $y = \ln(e^x) - \ln(1 + e^x) = x - \ln(1 + e^x)$. Differentiating: $\frac{dy}{dx} = 1 - \frac{e^x}{1 + e^x} = \frac{1 + e^x - e^x}{1 + e^x} = \frac{1}{1 + e^x}$... Wait, let's re-verify: $1 - \frac{e^x}{1+e^x} = \frac{1}{1+e^x}$. Correct option is A.

Q10
MCQ 10: What is $\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\ln(x + h) - \ln x}{h}$?
A $\frac{1}{x}$
B $\ln x$
C $x$
D $0$
Derivation & Analysis:

This is the first principle definition of the derivative of $\ln x$, which evaluates directly to $\frac{1}{x}$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Logarithmic differentiation is mandatory when dealing with functions of the form $f(x)^{g(x)}$ (variable to the power variable) or when a product/quotient contains more than three algebraic factors, as it converts cumbersome multiplications and divisions into easily manageable sums and differences.

Absolute value signs ensure the argument of the logarithm remains strictly positive, thereby expanding the valid domain of the resulting expression to include negative numbers where the original function or its integral is valid (e.g., $\int \frac{1}{x} dx = \ln|x| + C$).

Candidates can consistently expect 2 to 3 direct or indirect questions touching upon logarithmic differentiation, variable powers ($u^v$), or derivative limits in every UP TGT/PGT examination cycle.

Memorize the direct shortcut formula: $\frac{d}{dx}[u^v] = u^v \left( v' \ln u + v \frac{u'}{u} \right)$. This bypasses taking logs and doing implicit differentiation steps manually, saving precious seconds.

Q4
MCQ 4: If $f(x) = \log_10(5x^2 + 3)$, then $f'(x)$ is:
A $\frac{10x}{(5x^2 + 3)\ln 10}$
B $\frac{10x}{5x^2 + 3}$
C $\frac{1}{(5x^2 + 3)\ln 10}$
D $\frac{5x}{(5x^2 + 3)\ln 10}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Derivative of $\log_a u$ is $\frac{u'}{u \ln a}$. Here $u = 5x^2 + 3$, so $u' = 10x$ and $a = 10$. Thus $f'(x) = \frac{10x}{(5x^2 + 3)\ln 10}$.

Q5
MCQ 5: The derivative of $\ln(\sqrt{x})$ with respect to $x$ is:
A $\frac{1}{x}$
B $\frac{1}{2x}$
C $\frac{2}{x}$
D $\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Simplify first: $\ln(\sqrt{x}) = \frac{1}{2} \ln x$. Differentiating gives $\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{x} = \frac{1}{2x}$.

Q6
MCQ 6: If $y = \sqrt{\ln x + \sqrt{\ln x + \sqrt{\ln x + \dots_\infty}}}$, then $\frac{dy}{dx}$ is:
A $\frac{1}{x(2y - 1)}$
B $\frac{1}{x \ln x}$
C $\frac{y}{x}$
D $\frac{1}{2y}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Equation reduces to $y = \sqrt{\ln x + y} \implies y^2 = \ln x + y$. Differentiating implicitly: $2y y' = \frac{1}{x} + y' \implies y'(2y - 1) = \frac{1}{x} \implies y' = \frac{1}{x(2y - 1)}$.

Q7
MCQ 7: If $y = x^{\ln x}$, then $\frac{dy}{dx}$ at $x = e$ is:
A $2$
B $e$
C $1$
D $2e$
Derivation & Analysis:

Taking $\ln$: $\ln y = (\ln x)^2$. Differentiating: $\frac{1}{y} y' = 2(\ln x) \cdot \frac{1}{x} \implies y' = x^{\ln x} \left(\frac{2 \ln x}{x}\right)$. At $x = e$, $e^{\ln e} \left(\frac{2 \ln e}{e}\right) = e \left(\frac{2(1)}{e}\right) = 2$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): $\frac{d}{dx}(\ln |x|) = \frac{1}{x}$ for all $x \neq 0$. Reason (R): The domain of $\ln |x|$ is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

Both statements are mathematically rigorous and correct. The derivative holds across the entire domain of $\ln|x|$ which is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$.

Q9
MCQ 9: If $y = \ln \left( \frac{e^x}{1 + e^x} \right)$, then $\frac{dy}{dx}$ is:
A $\frac{1}{1 + e^x}$
B $-\frac{1}{1 + e^x}$
C $\frac{1}{1 - e^x}$
D $1$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using log properties: $y = \ln(e^x) - \ln(1 + e^x) = x - \ln(1 + e^x)$. Differentiating: $\frac{dy}{dx} = 1 - \frac{e^x}{1 + e^x} = \frac{1 + e^x - e^x}{1 + e^x} = \frac{1}{1 + e^x}$... Wait, let's re-verify: $1 - \frac{e^x}{1+e^x} = \frac{1}{1+e^x}$. Correct option is A.

Q10
MCQ 10: What is $\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\ln(x + h) - \ln x}{h}$?
A $\frac{1}{x}$
B $\ln x$
C $x$
D $0$
Derivation & Analysis:

This is the first principle definition of the derivative of $\ln x$, which evaluates directly to $\frac{1}{x}$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Logarithmic differentiation is mandatory when dealing with functions of the form $f(x)^{g(x)}$ (variable to the power variable) or when a product/quotient contains more than three algebraic factors, as it converts cumbersome multiplications and divisions into easily manageable sums and differences.

Absolute value signs ensure the argument of the logarithm remains strictly positive, thereby expanding the valid domain of the resulting expression to include negative numbers where the original function or its integral is valid (e.g., $\int \frac{1}{x} dx = \ln|x| + C$).

Candidates can consistently expect 2 to 3 direct or indirect questions touching upon logarithmic differentiation, variable powers ($u^v$), or derivative limits in every UP TGT/PGT examination cycle.

Memorize the direct shortcut formula: $\frac{d}{dx}[u^v] = u^v \left( v' \ln u + v \frac{u'}{u} \right)$. This bypasses taking logs and doing implicit differentiation steps manually, saving precious seconds.
