2. Calculus — Differential Calculus

Leibnitz theorem

Leibnitz's theorem (or Leibniz rule) provides a systematic formula for the $n$-th derivative of the product of two functions, generalizing the product rule through binomial coefficients. It is an indispensable tool in differential calculus frequently tested in UP PGT/TGT, DSSSB, and KVS examinations for finding nth-order derivatives of transcendental-algebraic products.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Leibnitz's Theorem for $n$-th Derivative of a Product
Let $u(x)$ and $v(x)$ be two functions possessing derivatives up to order $n$ in a given interval. Then the $n$-th derivative of their product $y = uv$ is given by $$\frac{d^n}{dx^n}(uv) = uv_n + \binom{n}{1}u_1v_{n-1} + \binom{n}{2}u_2v_{n-2} + \dots + \binom{n}{r}u_rv_{n-r} + \dots + u_nv$$, where subscripts denote the order of differentiation, i.e., $u_r = \frac{d^r u}{dx^r}$ and $v_r = \frac{d^r v}{dx^r}$, with $u_0 = u$ and $v_0 = v$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

While primarily an algebraic formula for successive differentiation, it forms the analytical backbone for establishing asymptotic expansions of curves, curvature vectors, and differential equations governing oscillatory motion.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It establishes a direct isomorphism between the index shift operator in differential rings and the binomial expansion coefficients, facilitating the reduction of linear differential equations with polynomial coefficients into algebraic recurrence relations.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Polynomial-Exponential Product ($x^n e^{ax}$)

$$ y_n = e^{ax} \sum_{r=0}^{n} \binom{n}{r} \frac{d^r}{dx^r}(x^n) a^{n-r} $$

Exam Rule: Choose $u = e^{ax}$ and $v = x^n$ because successive derivatives of polynomials terminate rapidly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT to find $y_n$ at $x=0$.
02

Shortcut 2: Polynomial-Trigonometric Product ($x^n \sin(ax)$ or $x^n \cos(ax)$)

$$ \text{If } y = x^n \sin(ax), \text{ use } v = x^n \text{ and } u = \sin(ax) \text{ where } u_n = a^n \sin\left(ax + \frac{n\pi}{2}\right) $$

Exam Rule: Isolate the trigonometric part as $u$ since its general $n$-th derivative has a closed-form cyclic formula.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard pattern in DSSSB PGT Mathematics.
03

Shortcut 3: Reduction to Second-Order Differential Equations

$$ (1-x^2)y_{n+2} - (2n+1)xy_{n+1} - (n^2 - m^2)y_n = 0 $$

Exam Rule: Applied directly to Chebyshev and Legendre related forms like $y = \cos(m \cos^{-1} x)$ after differentiating twice and applying Leibnitz theorem.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct result tested in UP PGT and KVS PGT examinations.
04

Shortcut 4: Evaluation at Origin ($x = 0$)

$$ \left. \frac{d^n}{dx^n}(uv) \right|_{x=0} = \sum_{r=0}^{n} \binom{n}{r} u_r(0) v_{n-r}(0) $$

Exam Rule: Many terms vanish when $x=0$ due to polynomial properties (e.g., $x^k$ derivatives at $x=0$ are zero for $k < n$).

⚡ PYQ Context: Time-saving trick for high-value coefficient questions.
05

Shortcut 5: Logarithmic and Inverse Trigonometric Products

$$ \text{For } y = (\sin^{-1} x)^2, \text{ show } (1-x^2)y_2 - xy_1 - 2 = 0 \text{ and apply Leibnitz to get } (1-x^2)y_{n+2} - (2n+1)xy_{n+1} - (n^2)y_n = 0 $$

Exam Rule: Differentiate once or twice, clear denominators, and apply Leibnitz theorem immediately to generate recurrence relations.

⚡ PYQ Context: Classic recurring question in LT Grade and UP TGT exams.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Option Elimination via Degree Checking

$$ \text{Degree of polynomial coefficients in Leibnitz recurrence matches highest derivative indices.} $$

Exam Rule: Check the coefficient of $y_{n+2}$ (usually $1-x^2$ or $x^2$) and indices of $y_{n+1}, y_n$ to eliminate 2 incorrect options instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial for saving time in 125-question exams.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Incorrect Assignment of $u$ and $v$

Common Pitfall: Assigning $u$ to a polynomial that does not terminate in $n$ derivatives, making calculation infinitely long.

Counterexample: \text{Let } y = x^n \ln x, \text{ taking } u = \ln x \text{ instead of } v = \ln x

Why it fails: Logarithmic derivatives do not terminate, whereas polynomial derivatives do. Always assign the polynomial to $v$ when its degree is $\le n$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always assign the terminating polynomial to $v$ and the transcendental function to $u$.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Index Shift Error in Recurrence Relations

Common Pitfall: Forgetting that applying Leibnitz theorem to $y_{k}$ increases all derivative subscripts by $n$.

Counterexample: \text{Writing } y_{n+1} \text{ instead of } y_{n+2} \text{ when differentiating } (1-x^2)y_1 - xy = 0 \text{ $n$ times.}

Why it fails: Results in mismatched recurrence indices and wrong answers.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Remember: differentiating $y_1$ $n$ times gives $y_{n+1}$, and differentiating $(1-x^2)y_1$ gives $(1-x^2)y_{n+1} - 2nx y_n - n(n-1)y_{n-1}$.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $y = e^{m \sin^{-1} x}$, prove that $(1-x^2)y_{n+2} - (2n+1)xy_{n+1} - (n^2 - m^2)y_n = 0$. What is the value of $y_n(0)$ when $n$ is even?

(A) $m^2(m^2-2^2)(m^2-4^2)\dots(m^2-(n-2)^2)$(B) $0$(C) $m^n$(D) $m(m^2-1^2)\dots(m^2-(n-1)^2)$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Differentiate $y = e^{m \sin^{-1} x}$ once to get $\sqrt{1-x^2} y_1 = m e^{m \sin^{-1} x}$. Step 2: Square both sides: $(1-x^2)y_1^2 = m^2 y^2$. Step 3: Differentiate again to get $(1-x^2)y_2 - xy_1 - m^2 y = 0$. Step 4: Apply Leibnitz theorem $n$ times to obtain the recurrence relation. Step 5: Substitute $x=0$ to find $y_n(0)$ for even $n$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize the standard Chebyshev-type recurrence relation and immediately extract the product structure for $x=0$ where odd derivatives vanish.
💡 Key Takeaway: Memorize the recurrence relations for $y = \sin(m \sin^{-1}x)$ and $y = \cos(m \cos^{-1}x)$ as they appear verbatim in UP PGT.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $y = (x^2 - 1)^n$, then the value of $y_n(1)$ (the $n$-th derivative at $x=1$) is:

(A) $2^n n!$(B) $n!$(C) $0$(D) $2^n$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write $y = (x-1)^n (x+1)^n$. Step 2: Apply Leibnitz theorem to find the $n$-th derivative at $x=1$. Step 3: All terms containing $(x-1)$ to a positive power vanish at $x=1$. Step 4: The only non-vanishing term corresponds to differentiating $(x-1)^n$ $n$ times and evaluating $(x+1)^n$ at $x=1$, yielding $n! \cdot 2^n$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Rodrigues' formula standard result: for $y = (x^2-1)^n$, $y_n(1) = 2^n n!$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Direct textbook result frequently used in Legendre polynomial properties.

Q1
If $y = x^3 \log x$, then $y_4$ is equal to:
A \frac{6}{x}
B \frac{2}{x}
C \frac{24}{x}
D 6x
Derivation & Analysis:

Using Leibnitz theorem with $u = \log x$ and $v = x^3$. Note that $v_1 = 3x^2, v_2 = 6x, v_3 = 6, v_4 = 0$. Also $u_1 = 1/x, u_2 = -1/x^2, u_3 = 2/x^3, u_4 = -6/x^4$. Summing terms gives $\frac{6}{x}$.

Q2
If $y = \sin(m \sin^{-1} x)$, then $(1-x^2)y_{n+2} - (2n+1)xy_{n+1}$ equals:
A (n^2 - m^2)y_n
B -(n^2 - m^2)y_n
C (m^2 - n^2)y_n
D 0
Derivation & Analysis:

This is the standard Leibnitz reduction formula for trigonometric inverse substitutions, resulting in $(n^2 - m^2)y_n$.

Q3
The $n$-th derivative of $x^n \log x$ at $x=0$ does not exist because:
A Logarithm is undefined at $0$
B Polynomial vanishes
C Derivatives of order $\ge 1$ of $\log x$ blow up at $0$
D It is indeterminate
Derivation & Analysis:

Derivatives of $\log x$ contain negative powers of $x$ (e.g., $-1/x^2, 2/x^3$), which diverge as $x \to 0$.

Q4
If $y = e^{ax} \cos(bx)$, what is $y_1$?
A e^{ax}[a \cos(bx) - b \sin(bx)]
B e^{ax}[a \cos(bx) + b \sin(bx)]
C ae^{ax} \cos(bx)
D be^{ax} \sin(bx)
Derivation & Analysis:

Direct application of product rule / Leibnitz theorem of order 1.

Q5
In Leibnitz formula for $(uv)_n$, the coefficient of $u_{n-1}v_1$ is:
A n
B \binom{n}{2}
C 1
D n!
Derivation & Analysis:

By binomial theorem expansion matching, the coefficient is $\binom{n}{1} = n$.

Q6
If $y = (x^2 - 1)^n$, then $y_{2n}$ is:
A (2n)!
B n!
C 2^n n!
D 0
Derivation & Analysis:

Degree of $y$ is $2n$, so its $(2n)$-th derivative is a constant equal to $(2n)!$.

Q7
If $y = x^n$, then $y_{n+1}$ is:
A n!
B 0
C 1
D x
Derivation & Analysis:

The $(n+1)$-th derivative of a polynomial of degree $n$ is identically zero.

Q8
Leibnitz theorem is an extension of which standard differentiation rule?
A Chain rule
B Product rule
C Quotient rule
D L'Hopital's rule
Derivation & Analysis:

Leibnitz theorem generalizes the product rule $(uv)' = u'v + uv'$ to higher-order derivatives.

Q9
If $y = x e^x$, then $n$-th derivative $y_n(0)$ is:
A n
B n!
C n 2^{n-1}
D 1
Derivation & Analysis:

Using Leibnitz theorem: $y_n = e^x x + n e^x (1) = e^x(x + n)$. At $x = 0$, $y_n(0) = n$.

Q10
If $(1-x^2)y_2 - xy_1 = 0$, then by applying Leibnitz theorem $n$ times, the term corresponding to $y_{n+1}$ has coefficient:
A -(2n+1)x
B -2nx
C -xy
D -(n+1)x
Derivation & Analysis:

Differentiating $-xy_1$ $n$ times gives $-\left[ x y_{n+1} + \binom{n}{1}(1)y_n \right] = -xy_{n+1} - ny_n$, combining with the derivative of $(1-x^2)y_2$ yields total coefficient $-(2n+1)x$ for $y_{n+1}$.
