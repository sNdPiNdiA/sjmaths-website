2. Calculus — Differential Calculus

L'Hospital's rule

L'Hospital's rule provides a powerful algorithmic mechanism for evaluating indeterminate limits of the form $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ by differentiating the numerator and denominator independently. Mastering its precise hypotheses prevents common trap formations and ensures rapid scoring in high-stakes teacher recruitment examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Cauchy's Form / L'Hospital's Rule Theorem
Let $f$ and $g$ be real-valued functions differentiable on an open interval $(a, b)$ containing $c$, except possibly at $c$ itself. If $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = 0$ and $\lim_{x \to c} g(x) = 0$ (or both tend to $\pm\infty$), and if the limit $\lim_{x \to c} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$ exists (say equal to $L$) and $g'(x) \neq 0$ for all $x \neq c$ in a neighborhood of $c$, then $$\lim_{x \to c} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x \to c} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)} = L$$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, as $x$ approaches $c$, the ratio of the ordinates of two curves converging to zero (or infinity) approaches the ratio of their instantaneous rates of change, namely their derivative slopes at the tangent lines.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, it bridges infinitesimal analysis with linear approximations via derivatives, reducing complex rational or transcendental limit evaluations to successive derivative computations.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Indeterminacy Form Point $x \to c$ Strictly Mandatory Applying L'Hospital's rule to determinate forms (e.g., $\frac{1}{2}$) yields catastrophically false limit values.
Differentiability in Neighborhood Open interval $(a, b)$ around $c$ Strictly Mandatory If $f'(x)$ or $g'(x)$ does not exist in a punctured neighborhood of $c$, the ratio of derivatives limit may fail to exist even if the original limit exists.
Non-zero Derivative of Denominator $g'(x) \neq 0$ for $x \neq c$ Essential condition Division by zero occurs in the derivative ratio if $g'(x) = 0$ arbitrarily close to $c$.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Successive Differentiation for Polynomials

$$\lim_{x \to c} \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} = \lim_{x \to c} \frac{P^{(n)}(x)}{Q^{(n)}(x)}$$

Exam Rule: For polynomials yielding $\frac{0}{0}$ up to $n$-th order, differentiate numerator and denominator simultaneously $n$ times until non-zero constant is obtained.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT for rational algebraic expressions involving high-degree roots.
02

Shortcut 2: Exponential-Algebraic Dominance

$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{x^n}{e^{kx}} = 0 \quad (k > 0)$$

Exam Rule: Exponential terms dominate polynomial terms; apply L'Hospital's rule $n$ times to eliminate the polynomial numerator instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently appears in KVS/NVS limits involving growth rates.
03

Shortcut 3: Logarithmic Conversion for Exponents

$$\lim f(x)^{g(x)} \implies \exp\left( \lim g(x) \ln f(x) \right)$$

Exam Rule: Convert $0^0$, $\infty^0$, or $1^\infty$ indeterminate forms into $0 \cdot \infty$ or $\frac{0}{0}$ via natural logarithm before applying L'Hospital's rule.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard scoring zone in DSSSB PGT Mathematics.
04

Shortcut 4: Series Expansion vs. L'Hospital's Rule

$$\sin x = x - \frac{x^3}{3!} + \dots$$

Exam Rule: When multiple derivatives are tedious, replace transcendental functions with their Maclaurin series expansions up to the leading non-zero term.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves up to 90 seconds in competitive exams compared to repeated differentiation.
05

Shortcut 5: Parametric Limit Evaluation

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x - x}{x^3} = \frac{1}{3}$$

Exam Rule: Memorize standard standard limits like $\frac{x - \sin x}{x^3} = \frac{1}{6}$ to bypass triple differentiation.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly asked in UP TGT/PGT examinations.
06

Shortcut 6: Option Elimination via Parameter Testing

$$a = ?, b = ?$$

Exam Rule: Substitute convenient test values or boundary values into options to eliminate incorrect choices before full derivation.

⚡ PYQ Context: Essential time-saving trick for 125-question papers.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Blind Application on Determinate Forms

Common Pitfall: Applying L'Hospital's rule blindly to limits that are not indeterminate (e.g., $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x + 1} = \frac{0}{1} = 0$).

Counterexample: $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\cos x}{1} = 1 \quad (\text{Incorrect, actual limit is } 0)$$

Why it fails: The initial limit is already determinate ($0/1 = 0$), and derivative ratio gives $1/1 = 1$, which is completely false.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always evaluate the limit by direct substitution first to confirm $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ form.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Ignoring Non-Existent Derivative Ratio Limits

Common Pitfall: Assuming L'Hospital's rule fails only when indeterminate forms are absent, forgetting that if $\lim \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$ does not exist, the rule gives no conclusion.

Counterexample: $$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{x + \sin x}{x}$$

Why it fails: Derivative ratio is $\frac{1 + \cos x}{1} = 1 + \cos x$, which oscillates and has no limit as $x \to \infty$, yet original limit is $1$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check if $\lim \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$ actually exists before declaring the limit nonexistent.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Evaluate the limit: $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x \cos x - \sin x}{x^3}$$

(A) $-\frac{1}{3}$(B) $-\frac{1}{6}$(C) $\frac{1}{3}$(D) $\frac{1}{6}$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Check form: numerator $\to 0$, denominator $\to 0$ (Form $\frac{0}{0}$). Step 2: Apply L'Hospital's rule: derivative of numerator is $-x \sin x$, denominator is $3x^2$. Still $\frac{0}{0}$. Step 3: Differentiate again: numerator $-(\sin x + x \cos x)$, denominator $6x$. Still $\frac{0}{0}$. Step 4: Differentiate third time: numerator $- (2\cos x - x \sin x)$, denominator $6$. Step 5: Substitute $x = 0$ to get $\frac{-2}{6} = -\frac{1}{6}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Use standard expansion: $\sin x = x - \frac{x^3}{6} + \dots$, so $x \cos x - \sin x = x(1 - x^2/2) - (x - x^3/6) = -\frac{x^3}{3}$. Dividing by $x^3$ yields $-\frac{1}{3}$, wait, careful: $-x^3/2 + x^3/6 = -x^3/3$. Thus limit is $-\frac{1}{3}$? Let's check: $x(1 - x^2/2 + \dots) - (x - x^3/6 + \dots) = -x^3/2 + x^3/6 = -x^3/3$. Dividing by $x^3$ gives $-\frac{1}{3}$. Correct option is (A).
💡 Key Takeaway: Always cross-verify triple L'Hospital differentiation with series expansion to avoid arithmetic sign errors.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the value of $k$ if $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^{kx} - 1 - kx}{x^2} = 5$$

(A) $5$(B) $10$(C) $\pm 5$(D) $\pm \sqrt{10}$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Form is $\frac{0}{0}$ at $x=0$. Step 2: Apply L'Hospital's rule: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{k e^{kx} - k}{2x}$. Still $\frac{0}{0}$. Step 3: Apply L'Hospital's rule again: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{k^2 e^{kx}}{2} = \frac{k^2}{2}$. Step 4: Equate to $5$: $\frac{k^2}{2} = 5 \implies k^2 = 10 \implies k = \pm \sqrt{10}$? Wait, let's re-evaluate: series expansion for $e^{kx}$ is $1 + kx + \frac{k^2 x^2}{2} + \dots$. Subtracting $1 + kx$ leaves $\frac{k^2 x^2}{2}$. Dividing by $x^2$ gives $\frac{k^2}{2} = 5 \implies k^2 = 10 \implies k = \pm \sqrt{10}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Instantly use Taylor series quadratic coefficient: coefficient of $x^2$ in numerator is $\frac{k^2}{2}$, divided by $1$ from denominator gives $\frac{k^2}{2} = 5 \implies k = \pm \sqrt{10}$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Parameter determination problems frequently appear in UP PGT; equating Taylor coefficients is 3x faster than repeated differentiation.

Q1
MCQ 1: Evaluate $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x - x}{x - \sin x}$$
A (A) $1$
B (B) $2$
C (C) $3$
D (D) $4$
Derivation & Analysis:

Applying L'Hospital's rule three times or using series expansions $\tan x \approx x + \frac{x^3}{3}$ and $\sin x \approx x - \frac{x^3}{6}$ yields $\frac{x^3/3}{x^3/6} = 2$.

Q2
MCQ 2: The value of $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\log_e(1 + x) - x}{x^2}$$ is
A (A) $0$
B (B) $-\frac{1}{2}$
C (C) $1$
D (D) $\frac{1}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Form is $\frac{0}{0}$. Differentiate numerator: $\frac{1}{1+x} - 1$. Differentiate denominator: $2x$. Limit becomes $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{-x}{(1+x)2x} = -\frac{1}{2}$.

Q3
MCQ 3: Evaluate $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{a^x - b^x}{x}$$
A (A) $\log(a/b)$
B (B) $\log(ab)$
C (C) $\log(a^b)$
D (D) $\log(b/a)$
Derivation & Analysis:

By L'Hospital's rule, derivative of numerator is $a^x \ln a - b^x \ln b$, denominator is $1$. At $x=0$, result is $\ln a - \ln b = \ln(a/b)$.

Q4
MCQ 4: Evaluate $$\lim_{x \to \infty} x \left( \arctan\left(\frac{x+1}{x}\right) - \frac{\pi}{4} \right)$$
A (A) $\frac{1}{2}$
B (B) $1$
C (C) $-\frac{1}{2}$
D (D) $0$
Derivation & Analysis:

Rewrite as $\frac{\arctan(1 + 1/x) - \pi/4}{1/x}$. Let $h = 1/x \to 0$. Limit becomes $\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\arctan(1+h) - \arctan(1)}{h}$, which is the derivative of $\arctan(x)$ at $x=1$, equal to $\frac{1}{1+1^2} = \frac{1}{2}$.

Q5
MCQ 5: The limit $$\lim_{x \to 0} (1 + x)^{1/x}$$ evaluates to
A (A) $1$
B (B) $e$
C (C) $e^2$
D (D) $\infty$
Derivation & Analysis:

This is $1^\infty$ form. Let $y = (1+x)^{1/x}$, $\ln y = \frac{\ln(1+x)}{x}$. As $x \to 0$, limit is $\frac{1}{1+0} = 1$, so $y \to e^1 = e$.

Q6
MCQ 6: Evaluate $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(a+x) - \sin(a-x)}{x}$$
A (A) $2 \cos a$
B (B) $2 \sin a$
C (C) $\cos a$
D (D) $0$
Derivation & Analysis:

Differentiate w.r.t $x$: numerator derivative is $\cos(a+x) + \cos(a-x)$, denominator is $1$. At $x=0$, result is $2 \cos a$.

Q7
MCQ 7: If $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{sin 2x + a \sin x}{x^3}$$ exists finitely, then the values of $a$ and the limit are
A (A) $a = -2$, limit $= 1$
B (B) $a = -2$, limit $= -1$
C (C) $a = 2$, limit $= 0$
D (D) $a = -1$, limit $= 2$
Derivation & Analysis:

For numerator to vanish to third order, use expansion: $\sin 2x + a \sin x = (2x - \frac{8x^3}{6}) + a(x - \frac{x^3}{6}) = (2+a)x - \frac{8+a}{6}x^3$. For $x$ term to vanish, $2+a = 0 \implies a = -2$. Then coefficient of $x^3$ is $-\frac{6}{6} = -1$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Which of the following limits CANNOT be evaluated directly using L'Hospital's rule without prior algebraic transformation?
A (A) $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x}$
B (B) $\lim_{x \to 0^+} x \ln x$
C (C) $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^x - 1}{x}$
D (D) $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{x}{e^x}$
Derivation & Analysis:

$x \ln x$ is of form $0 \cdot \infty$, not $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$. It must be rewritten as $\frac{\ln x}{1/x}$ or $\frac{x}{1/\ln x}$ before L'Hospital's rule applies.

Q9
MCQ 9: Evaluate $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\cosh x - 1}{x^2}$$
A (A) $1$
B (B) $\frac{1}{2}$
C (C) $0$
D (D) $-\frac{1}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $\cosh x = 1 + \frac{x^2}{2} + \dots$, the expression simplifies to $\frac{x^2/2}{x^2} = \frac{1}{2}$. Alternatively, apply L'Hospital twice: $\frac{\sinh x}{2x} \to \frac{\cosh x}{2} \to \frac{1}{2}$.

Q10
MCQ 10: Evaluate $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x - \arctan x}{x^3}$$
A (A) $\frac{1}{3}$
B (B) $\frac{1}{6}$
C (C) $-\frac{1}{3}$
D (D) $1$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using series expansion $\arctan x = x - \frac{x^3}{3} + \frac{x^5}{5} - \dots$, numerator $x - (x - \frac{x^3}{3} + \dots) = \frac{x^3}{3}$. Dividing by $x^3$ gives $\frac{1}{3}$. Triple L'Hospital also yields $\frac{1}{3}$.
