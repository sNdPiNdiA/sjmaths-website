Rolle's theorem

Rolle's theorem is a foundational pillar of differential calculus guaranteeing the existence of at least one critical point with a horizontal tangent under smooth boundary conditions. In competitive examinations like UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB, it serves as a high-yield scoring zone for root isolation, derivative vanishing properties, and parameter identification.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Rolle's Theorem (Theorem of the Vanishing Derivative)
Let $f: [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}$ be a real-valued function satisfying three hypotheses: (1) $f$ is continuous on the closed interval $[a, b]$, (2) $f$ is differentiable on the open interval $(a, b)$, and (3) $f(a) = f(b)$. Then there exists at least one real number $c \in (a, b)$ such that $$f'(c) = 0$$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

If the graph of a continuous, smooth curve starts and ends at the exact same height ($f(a) = f(b)$), the curve must peak or trough at least once in the interior. Geometrically, this means there is at least one point $c$ in the open interval $(a, b)$ where the tangent line to the curve is perfectly parallel to the $x$-axis, i.e., its slope is zero.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, Rolle's theorem bridges the gap between function values and derivative values, acting as the stepping stone for Lagrange's Mean Value Theorem and Taylor's Theorem. It implies that between any two distinct real roots of a differentiable function, there lies at least one real root of its derivative.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Continuity on $[a, b]$ $[a, b]$ (Closed interval) Strictly Mandatory / Necessary If the function has a jump or removable discontinuity in the interval, the Extreme Value Theorem fails, and the curve may not attain local extrema.
Differentiability on $(a, b)$ $(a, b)$ (Open interval) Strictly Mandatory A sharp corner or cusp (like $f(x) = |x|$ at $x=0$) breaks the smooth flow, making the derivative undefined at that point, violating the conclusion.
Boundary Equality $f(a) = f(b)$ Boundary values $a$ and $b$ Essential for horizontal tangent If $f(a) \neq f(b)$, the secant line is tilted, which shifts the theorem into Lagrange's Mean Value Theorem rather than guaranteeing a horizontal tangent.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Root Isolation & Polynomial Derivatives

$$ \text{If } f(x) \text{ has } n \text{ real roots, } f'(x) \text{ has at least } n-1 \text{ real roots in between.} $$

Exam Rule: Between any two consecutive roots of a differentiable function, there is at least one root of its derivative. Use this to count roots instantly without solving higher-degree equations.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT to find the minimum number of roots for derivatives of polynomials like $x^n + ax + b = 0$.
02

Shortcut 2: Symmetric Intervals & Parity

$$ \text{For } f(x) \text{ even on } [-a, a], \; f'(0) = 0 \text{ automatically by Rolle's / Fermat's principle.} $$

Exam Rule: If a function is even and differentiable on a symmetric interval, its derivative at the origin is strictly zero. If odd, examine symmetry properties for rapid root spotting.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard appearance in TGT/PGT matching columns for function properties and critical points.
03

Shortcut 3: Algebraic Derivative Roots

$$ \text{If } f(x) = (x-a)^m (x-b)^n, \text{ then } c = \frac{mb + na}{m+n} \in (a, b) $$

Exam Rule: For factored algebraic polynomials, the value of $c$ where $f'(c) = 0$ is simply the weighted arithmetic mean of the roots.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct result tested in examination to compute exact value of $c$ in under 15 seconds.
04

Shortcut 4: Trigonometric & Exponential Bounds

$$ \text{For } f(x) = e^x \sin x \text{ on } [0, \pi], \; c = \frac{3\pi}{4} $$

Exam Rule: Recognize standard product combinations of trigonometric and exponential functions where $f(0) = f(\pi) = 0$, leading to $c$ at standard angle offsets like $\frac{3\pi}{4}$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct exam application in DSSSB and KVS PGT calculus sections.
05

Shortcut 5: Determinant / Wronskian / Matrix Shortcut

$$ \text{If } \begin{vmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{vmatrix} \text{ structures arise, apply auxiliary function } F(x) = f(x)e^{\lambda x}. $$

Exam Rule: Construct auxiliary function $F(x) = f(x)g(x)$ or exponential shifts to satisfy $F(a) = F(b)$ and apply Rolle's theorem to prove existence of roots for linear combinations.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard advanced PGT question format for differential equations and root existence.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination Trick

$$ c \in (a, b) \implies \text{Eliminate options where } c \le a \text{ or } c \ge b. $$

Exam Rule: Instantly discard any option where the value of $c$ lies outside the open interval $(a, b)$ or equals the endpoints.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial time management strategy in 125-question exams where options test interval bounds.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: The Sharp Corner / Modulus Fallacy

Common Pitfall: Candidates apply the theorem to piecewise or absolute value functions without verifying differentiability at interior points.

Counterexample: $f(x) = |x|$ on $[-1, 1]$

Why it fails: Continuous everywhere and $f(-1) = f(1) = 1$, but $f'(0)$ does not exist, so no $c \in (-1, 1)$ satisfies $f'(c) = 0$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always test derivative from left and right ($f'_-(x_0) = f'_+(x_0)$) at points where argument changes sign.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Open vs. Closed Interval Confusion

Common Pitfall: Confusing where continuity vs differentiability is required, or asserting that $c$ can be an endpoint $a$ or $b$.

Counterexample: $c \in (a, b)$ strictly, never $c = a$ or $c = b$

Why it fails: If $f'(x) = 0$ only at endpoints, the theorem is violated because $c$ must be in the open interior $(a, b)$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Instantly discard any option that offers $c = a$ or $c = b$.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If Rolle's theorem is applicable to $f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6$ on $[1, 3]$, find the value of $c \in (1, 3)$ such that $f'(c) = 0$.

(A) $\frac{3 + \sqrt{3}}{3}$(B) $2 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$(C) $2 + \sqrt{3}$(D) $\frac{3 - \sqrt{3}}{2}$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Verify $f(1) = 1 - 6 + 11 - 6 = 0$ and $f(3) = 27 - 54 + 33 - 6 = 0$, so $f(1) = f(3)$. Polynomial is continuous and differentiable everywhere. Step 2: Compute $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12x + 11$. Step 3: Set $f'(c) = 0 \implies 3c^2 - 12c + 11 = 0$. Step 4: Solve using quadratic formula $c = \frac{12 \pm \sqrt{144 - 132}}{6} = \frac{12 \pm \sqrt{12}}{6} = 2 \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$. Step 5: Since $2 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 2.577 \in (1,3)$, both roots are in range, but check specific option mapping ($2 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ is also valid; option B matches standard printing).
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Use shortcut $c = \frac{a+b}{2} \pm \frac{b-a}{2\sqrt{3}}$ for cubic expansions, yielding $2 \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Check interval membership immediately before locking the option.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Let $f(x) = (x-a)^m (x-b)^n$ where $m, n > 0$ on $[a, b]$. The value of $c$ where $f'(c) = 0$ is:

(A) $\frac{ma + nb}{m+n}$(B) $\frac{mb + na}{m+n}$(C) $\frac{a+b}{2}$(D) $\sqrt{ab}$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: $f(a) = 0$ and $f(b) = 0$, satisfying Rolle's theorem. Step 2: Apply logarithmic differentiation: $\ln f(x) = m \ln(x-a) + n \ln(x-b)$. Step 3: Differentiate: $\frac{f'(x)}{f(x)} = \frac{m}{x-a} + \frac{n}{x-b}$. Step 4: Set $f'(c) = 0 \implies \frac{m}{c-a} + \frac{n}{c-b} = 0 \implies m(c-b) + n(c-a) = 0 \implies c(m+n) = mb + na \implies c = \frac{mb + na}{m+n}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Direct formula application from Shortcut 3.
💡 Key Takeaway: Memorize this weighted mean result as it appears directly in UP PGT exams without numerical changes.

Q1
MCQ 1: Which of the following functions satisfies all conditions of Rolle's theorem on $[-1, 1]$?
A (A) $f(x) = |x|$
B (B) $f(x) = x^{2/3}$
C (C) $f(x) = x^2 - 1$
D (D) $f(x) = \tan x$
Derivation & Analysis:

$f(x) = x^2 - 1$ is continuous on $[-1, 1]$, differentiable on $(-1, 1)$, and $f(-1) = f(1) = 0$. $f(x) = |x|$ and $x^{2/3}$ fail differentiability at $0$, and $\tan x$ is undefined at $\pm \pi/2$ or has singularities.

Q2
MCQ 2: If $f(x) = e^x(\sin x - \cos x)$ satisfies Rolle's theorem on $[\pi/4, 5\pi/4]$, then the value of $c$ is:
A (A) $\pi$
B (B) $\frac{\pi}{2}$
C (C) $\frac{3\pi}{4}$
D (D) $\frac{3\pi}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Compute $f(\pi/4) = e^{\pi/4}(0) = 0$ and $f(5\pi/4) = e^{5\pi/4}(0) = 0$. Differentiating gives $f'(x) = e^x(\sin x - \cos x) + e^x(\cos x + \sin x) = 2e^x \sin x$. Setting $f'(c) = 0 \implies \sin c = 0 \implies c = \pi \in (\pi/4, 5\pi/4)$.

Q3
MCQ 3: If $f(x) = a x^2 + b x + c$ satisfies the conditions of Rolle's theorem on $[x_1, x_2]$, then the value of $c$ is:
A (A) $x_1 + x_2$
B (B) $\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}$
C (C) $\sqrt{x_1 x_2}$
D (D) $\frac{2x_1 x_2}{x_1 + x_2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

For any quadratic polynomial satisfying Rolle's theorem, the critical point $c$ is precisely the midpoint of the interval: $c = -b / (2a) = (x_1 + x_2)/2$.

Q4
MCQ 4: Between two consecutive roots of the equation $e^x \sin x = 1$, the derivative $e^x(\sin x + \cos x)$ has:
A (A) exactly one root
B (B) no root
C (C) at least two roots
D (D) infinite roots
Derivation & Analysis:

By Rolle's theorem, between any two roots of a differentiable function, there lies at least one root of its derivative.

Q5
MCQ 5: The function $f(x) = x(x-1)(x-2)$ on $[0, 2]$ has how many values of $c$ satisfying Rolle's theorem?
A (A) 0
B (B) 1
C (C) 2
D (D) 3
Derivation & Analysis:

$f(0) = f(2) = 0$. $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 2x \implies f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x + 2 = 0 \implies c = \frac{6 \pm \sqrt{36 - 24}}{6} = 1 \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, both of which lie in $(0, 2)$. Thus there are 2 values.

Q6
MCQ 6: Which hypothesis of Rolle's theorem fails for $f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$ on $[-1, 1]$?
A (A) $f(a) = f(b)$
B (B) Continuity on $[-1, 1]$
C (C) Differentiability on $(-1, 1)$
D (D) Both continuity and differentiability
Derivation & Analysis:

The function $\frac{1}{x}$ is discontinuous at $x = 0$, violating the closed-interval continuity requirement.

Q7
MCQ 7: If $f(x)$ is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$ and $f'(x) \neq 1$ for all $x$, then $f(1) - f(0)$ can be:
A (A) 1
B (B) 2
C (C) any real number
D (D) not equal to 1
Derivation & Analysis:

By Lagrange's MVT (consequence of Rolle's), $\frac{f(1)-f(0)}{1-0} = f'(c) \neq 1$, hence $f(1) - f(0) \neq 1$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): Rolle's theorem cannot be applied to $f(x) = \tan x$ on $[0, \pi]$. Reason (R): $f(x)$ is not continuous at $x = \pi/2$.
A (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation
C (C) (A) is true but (R) is false
D (D) (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

Discontinuity at $x = \pi/2$ violates the closed interval continuity requirement, making Rolle's theorem inapplicable.

Q9
MCQ 9: If $f(x) = (x-1)(x-2)(x-3)$, then roots of $f'(x) = 0$ lie in:
A (A) $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 3)$
B (B) $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 2)$
C (C) $(2, 3)$ and $(3, 4)$
D (D) outside $(1, 3)$
Derivation & Analysis:

Roots of $f(x)$ are $1, 2, 3$. By Rolle's theorem, one root of $f'(x)$ lies in $(1, 2)$ and another in $(2, 3)$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $f(x) = x^n$ on $[a, b]$ where $0 < a < b$, then the value of $c$ given by Rolle's theorem does not exist because:
A (A) $f(a) \neq f(b)$
B (B) $f$ is not continuous
C (C) $f$ is not differentiable
D (D) $f'(x) = 0$ has no root
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $a > 0$ and $b > 0$, $f(a) = a^n \neq b^n = f(b)$, violating the boundary equality condition $f(a) = f(b)$.

Step 4: Set $f'(c) = 0 \implies \frac{m}{c-a} + \frac{n}{c-b} = 0 \implies m(c-b) + n(c-a) = 0 \implies c(m+n) = mb + na \implies c = \frac{mb + na}{m+n}$.

Q2
MCQ 2: If $f(x) = e^x(\sin x - \cos x)$ satisfies Rolle's theorem on $[\pi/4, 5\pi/4]$, then the value of $c$ is:
A (A) $\pi$
B (B) $\frac{\pi}{2}$
C (C) $\frac{3\pi}{4}$
D (D) $\frac{3\pi}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Compute $f(\pi/4) = e^{\pi/4}(0) = 0$ and $f(5\pi/4) = e^{5\pi/4}(0) = 0$. Differentiating gives $f'(x) = e^x(\sin x - \cos x) + e^x(\cos x + \sin x) = 2e^x \sin x$. Setting $f'(c) = 0 \implies \sin c = 0 \implies c = \pi \in (\pi/4, 5\pi/4)$.

Q3

B (B) $\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}$
C (C) $\sqrt{x_1 x_2}$
D (D) $\frac{2x_1 x_2}{x_1 + x_2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

For any quadratic polynomial satisfying Rolle's theorem, the critical point $c$ is precisely the midpoint of the interval: $c = -b / (2a) = (x_1 + x_2)/2$.

Q4
MCQ 4: Between two consecutive roots of the equation $e^x \sin x = 1$, the derivative $e^x(\sin x + \cos x)$ has:
A (A) exactly one root
B (B) no root
C (C) at least two roots
D (D) infinite roots
Derivation & Analysis:

By Rolle's theorem, between any two roots of a differentiable function, there lies at least one root of its derivative.

Q5
MCQ 5: The function $f(x) = x(x-1)(x-2)$ on $[0, 2]$ has how many values of $c$ satisfying Rolle's theorem?
A (A) 0
B (B) 1
C (C) 2
D (D) 3
Derivation & Analysis:

$f(0) = f(2) = 0$. $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 2x \implies f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x + 2 = 0 \implies c = \frac{6 \pm \sqrt{36 - 24}}{6} = 1 \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, both of which lie in $(0, 2)$. Thus there are 2 values.

The function $\frac{1}{x}$ is discontinuous at $x = 0$, violating the closed-interval continuity requirement.

By Lagrange's MVT (consequence of Rolle's), $\frac{f(1)-f(0)}{1-0} = f'(c) \neq 1$, hence $f(1) - f(0) \neq 1$.

Discontinuity at $x = \pi/2$ violates the closed interval continuity requirement, making Rolle's theorem inapplicable.
