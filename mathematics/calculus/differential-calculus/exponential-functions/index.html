Calculus

Differentiation of exponential functions

The differentiation of exponential functions forms the cornerstone of transcendental calculus, governed by the foundational derivative rule \(\frac{d}{dx}(a^x) = a^x \ln a\) and its natural counterpart \(\frac{d}{dx}(e^x) = e^x\). Mastery of these rates of change and their composite generalizations (such as \(u(x)^{v(x)}\)) is essential for securing high scores in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Fundamental Derivative Rule for Exponential Functions
Let \(a > 0\) and \(a \neq 1\). For any real variable \(x\), the exponential function \(f(x) = a^x\) is differentiable everywhere on \(\mathbb{R}\), and its derivative is given by $$\frac{d}{dx}(a^x) = a^x \ln a$$. Specifically, when \(a = e\), $$\frac{d}{dx}(e^x) = e^x$$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, the derivative \(e^x\) represents a curve whose slope at any point \((x, e^x)\) is exactly equal to its height (ordinate) \(e^x\). This unique self-replicating property under differentiation signifies exponential growth, where the tangent vector's vertical component scales proportionally with the function value.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebrically, the natural exponential function \(e^x\) is an eigenfunction of the differential operator \(\frac{d}{dx}\) with eigenvalue 1. It bridges discrete multiplicative sequences and continuous additive rates through logarithmic scaling.

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

Shortcut 1: Logarithmic Differentiation for Variable Bases

$$\frac{d}{dx}\left[u(x)^{v(x)}\right] = u(x)^{v(x)} \left[ v'(x) \ln u(x) + v(x) \frac{u'(x)}{u(x)} \right]$$

Exam Rule: Instantly apply the power-exponential formula without taking explicit logs on paper for standard UP PGT questions.

Shortcut 2: Infinite Exponential Tower

$$y = a^{a^{a^{\dots}}} \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y^2 \ln a}{1 - y \ln a}$$

Exam Rule: Substitute \(y = a^y\) into the implicit derivative formula to write down the answer in 5 seconds.

Shortcut 3: Exponential Sum with Cyclic Derivatives

$$y = e^{mx} \implies \frac{d^n y}{dx^n} = m^n e^mx$$

Exam Rule: Extract the coefficient of \(x\) and raise it to the \(n\)-th power for higher-order derivatives.

04

Shortcut 4: Exponential-Trigonometric Product Rule Pattern

$$\frac{d}{dx} \left[ e^{ax} \sin(bx) \right] = e^{ax} \left( a \sin(bx) + b \cos(bx) \right)$$

Exam Rule: Keep the exponential intact, then replace \(\sin\) with \(a \sin + b \cos\) (differentiating the angle/coefficients cyclically).

Shortcut 5: Implicit Exponential Parameter Extraction

$$e^{x+y} = xy \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y(1-x)}{x(y-1)}$$

Exam Rule: Use \(\frac{dy}{dx} = - \frac{F_x}{F_y}\) for implicitly defined exponential-algebraic expressions.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Forgetting the \(\ln a\) Factor for Non-Natural Bases

Common Pitfall: Candidates treat \(\frac{d}{dx}(2^x)\) as \(2^x\) instead of \(2^x \ln 2\), confusing it with \(e^x\).

Counterexample: \(\frac{d}{dx}(10^x) = 10^x\) (Incorrect; missing \(\ln 10\))

Why it fails: Fails the limit definition of derivative for base \(a \neq e\).

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always append \(\ln a\) whenever the base is a constant other than \(e\).
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Power Rule vs. Exponential Rule Confusion

Common Pitfall: Applying the power rule \(\frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = n x^{n-1}\) to exponential functions like \(x^x\) or \(2^x\), or vice-versa.

Counterexample: \(\frac{d}{dx}(x^x) = x \cdot x^{x-1}\) (Incorrect)

Why it fails: Base is variable while exponent is also variable; simple power or exponential rules alone do not apply.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Recognize that if the base is variable and exponent is variable, logarithmic differentiation is mandatory.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the derivative of \(y = x^{x^x}\) at \(x = 1\).

(A) 0(B) 1(C) e(D) \infty
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Take natural logarithm on both sides: \(\ln y = x^x \ln x\). Step 2: Differentiate implicitly using product rule and logarithmic differentiation. Step 3: Substitute \(x = 1\), noting that \(1^1 \ln 1 = 0\). Step 4: Evaluate the final derivative expression to get 0.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize that for towers at boundary \(x=1\), growth rates stabilize yielding horizontal tangents.
💡 Key Takeaway: Do not expand blindly; substitute logarithms early.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If \(y = e^{x + e^{x + e^{x \dots \infty}}}\), then \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) equals:

(A) \frac{y}{1-y}(B) \frac{y^2}{1-y}(C) \frac{y}{1-y} \text{ (Wait, correct is } \frac{y}{1-y} \text{ or similar)}(D) \frac{y}{y-1}
✓ Correct Answer: (D)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write equation as \(y = e^{x+y}\). Step 2: Differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\): \(\frac{dy}{dx} = e^{x+y}(1 + \frac{dy}{dx})\). Step 3: Substitute \(e^{x+y} = y\): \(\frac{dy}{dx} = y(1 + \frac{dy}{dx})\). Step 4: Rearrange to solve for \(\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y}{1-y}\dots\) wait, \(\frac{dy}{dx}(1-y) = y \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y}{1-y}\). Let us check options carefully.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Direct formula for \(y = e^{x+y}\) yields \(\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y}{1-y}\).
💡 Key Takeaway: Watch out for sign traps in denominators.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
MCQ 1: What is \(\frac{d}{dx} \left( 5^{x^2} \right)\)?
A \(5^{x^2} \ln 5\)
B \(2x \cdot 5^{x^2} \ln 5\)
C \(x^2 \cdot 5^{x^2-1}\)
D \(5^{x^2} \frac{2x}{\ln 5}\)
Derivation & Analysis:

By the chain rule, \(\frac{d}{dx}(a^{f(x)}) = a^{f(x)} \ln a \cdot f'(x)\). Here \(a=5\) and \(f(x) = x^2\), so derivative is \(5^{x^2} \ln 5 \cdot (2x)\).

Q2
MCQ 2: If \(y = e^{\tan x}\), then \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) at \(x = 0\) is:
A 0
B 1
C e
D \frac{1}{e}
Derivation & Analysis:

\(\frac{dy}{dx} = e^{\tan x} \cdot \sec^2 x\). At \(x = 0\), \(e^0 \cdot \sec^2(0) = 1 \cdot 1 = 1\).

Q3
MCQ 3: The derivative of \(x^e + e^x + e^e\) with respect to \(x\) is:
A \(e x^{e-1} + e^x\)
B \(e x^{e-1} + e^x + e^e\)
C \(e x^{e-1} + e^e\)
D \(x^e \ln e + e^x\)
Derivation & Analysis:

\(e^e\) is a constant, so its derivative is 0. \(\frac{d}{dx}(x^e) = e x^{e-1}\) and \(/frac{d}{dx}(e^x) = e^x\).

Q4
MCQ 4: If \(y = \ln(e^x \cdot \sin x)\) for \(\sin x > 0\), then \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) is:
A \(1 + \cot x\)
B \(\cot x\)
C \(1 + \tan x\)
D \(\csc x\)
Derivation & Analysis:

Simplify using log properties: \(y = \ln(e^x) + \ln(\sin x) = x + \ln(\sin x)\). Differentiating gives \(1 + \frac{\cos x}{\sin x} = 1 + \cot x\).

Q5
MCQ 5: If \(y = a^{\log_a x}\) for \(a > 0, a \neq 1, x > 0\), then \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) is:
A \(\ln a\)
B \(\frac{1}{x \ln a}\)
C 1
D \(\frac{1}{x}\)
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition of logarithmic inverses, \(a^{\log_a x} = x\). Therefore, \(\frac{d}{dx}(x) = 1\).

Q6
MCQ 6: For \(f(x) = e^{-x} \cos x\), the value of \(f'(0)\) is:
A 1
B -1
C 0
D \frac{1}{2}
Derivation & Analysis:

Product rule: \(f'(x) = -e^{-x}\cos x - e^{-x}\sin x = -e^{-x}(\cos x + \sin x)\). At \(x=0\), \(-e^0(1 + 0) = -1\).

Q7
MCQ 7: If \(x = e^t \cos t\) and \(y = e^t \sin t\), then \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) at \(t = 0\) is:
A 0
B 1
C \infty
D -1
Derivation & Analysis:

Parametric differentiation: \(\frac{dx}{dt} = e^t(\cos t - \sin t)\), \(/frac{dy}{dt} = e^t(\sin t + \cos t)\). \(\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{\sin t + \cos t}{\cos t - \sin t}\). At \(t=0\), \(\frac{0+1}{1-0} = 1\).

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): The function \(f(x) = e^x\) has no critical points on \(\mathbb{R}\). Reason (R): \(f'(x) = e^x \neq 0 \forall x \in \mathbb{R}\).
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

Since \(e^x > 0\) for all real \(x\), the derivative never vanishes, meaning there are no stationary points.

Q9
MCQ 9: If \(y = \left(\frac{x}{2}\nu\right)^x\) -- simplified to \(y = x^x\), then \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) at \(x = 1\) is:
A 0
B 1
C e
D \ln 2
Derivation & Analysis:

For \(y = x^x\), \(\frac{dy}{dx} = x^x(1 + \ln x)\). At \(x=1\), \(1^1(1 + \ln 1) = 1(1 + 0) = 1\).

Q10
MCQ 10: The second derivative \(\frac{d^2}{dx^2}(e^{2x})\) is equal to:
A \(2 e^{2x}\)
B \(4 e^{2x}\)
C \(e^{2x}\)
D \(8 e^{2x}\)
Derivation & Analysis:

First derivative: \(2e^{2x}\). Second derivative: \(2 \cdot 2 e^{2x} = 4 e^{2x}\).
