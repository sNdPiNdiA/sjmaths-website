Limit of a function

The limit of a function forms the foundational bedrock of all differential and integral calculus, capturing the precise behavior of a function as its independent variable approaches a specific point. Mastering left-hand limits, right-hand limits, indeterminate forms, and expansion techniques is essential for securing high scores in UP PGT/TGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Cauchy's / $\epsilon$-$\delta$ Definition of Limit
Let $f: A \subset \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ and let $c$ be a limit point of $A$. We say that \(\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L\) if for every \(\epsilon > 0\), there exists a \(\delta > 0\) such that for all $x \in A$, if \(0 < |x - c| < \delta\), then \(|f(x) - L| < \epsilon\).

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, as $x$ is chosen within a horizontal strip of radius $\delta$ around $c$ (excluding $c$ itself), the corresponding function values $f(x)$ are forced into a vertical strip of radius $\epsilon$ around $L$.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Establishes the rigorous topological foundation of real analysis, guaranteeing that algebraic limit laws (sum, product, quotient rules) hold securely without circular reasoning.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Existence of Limit Point Domain Neighborhood $(c-\delta, c+\delta) \setminus \{c\}$ Strictly Mandatory / Necessary If $c$ is an isolated point of the domain, the limit concept becomes vacuous or undefined.
Equality of One-Sided Limits Left interval $(c-\delta, c)$ and Right interval $(c, c+\delta)$ Strictly Mandatory If $\lim_{x \to c^-} f(x) \neq \lim_{x \to c^+} f(x)$, the two-sided limit $\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$ fails to exist.
Finiteness of Limit Value Real number codomain $L \in \mathbb{R}$ Essential for standard limit existence If values grow unboundedly (diverge to $\pm\infty$), the limit does not exist in the real number system.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: L'Hôpital's Rule for $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$

$$\lim_{x \to c} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x \to c} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$$

Exam Rule: Directly differentiate numerator and denominator separately until the indeterminate form is resolved, provided the quotient of derivatives exists.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly applied in over 40% of UP PGT/TGT calculus limit problems.
02

Shortcut 2: Standard Algebraic Limits Form

$$\lim_{x \to a} \frac{x^n - a^n}{x - a} = n a^{n-1}$$

Exam Rule: Instantly substitute $n$ and $a$ when encountering binomial rational limits, saving algebraic factorization time.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequent direct question pattern in DSSSB and KVS PGT exams.
03

Shortcut 3: Exponential / Logarithmic Standard Limits

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^x - 1}{x} = 1, \quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\ln(1+x)}{x} = 1$$

Exam Rule: Memorize standard exponential and logarithmic limits to bypass full series expansions during tight exam time constraints.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard matching and numerical evaluation items in LT Grade exams.
04

Shortcut 4: Trigonometric Ratio Limits

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1, \quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} = \frac{1}{2}$$

Exam Rule: Replace $\sin(kx) \approx kx$ and $1 - \cos(kx) \approx \frac{1}{2}k^2x^2$ for rapid asymptotic approximations.

⚡ PYQ Context: Essential speed tool for multi-step calculus problems.
05

Shortcut 5: The $1^\infty$ Indeterminate Form Shortcut

$$\text{If } \lim f(x) = 1 \text{ and } \lim g(x) = \infty, \text{ then } \lim [f(x)]^{g(x)} = e^{\lim (f(x)-1)g(x)}$$

Exam Rule: Convert $1^\infty$ problems into exponential products instantly without cumbersome logarithmic transformations.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently featured as a high-difficulty differentiator in UP PGT.
06

Shortcut 6: Series Expansion (Maclaurin) Elimination

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x - \sin x}{x^3} = \frac{1}{6}$$

Exam Rule: Use leading non-zero terms of Maclaurin series for rational functions involving trigonometric or exponential terms.

⚡ PYQ Context: Enables elimination of incorrect options within 15 seconds.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Misapplying L'Hôpital's Rule on Non-Indeterminate Forms

Common Pitfall: Candidates blindly differentiate numerator and denominator when the expression is not in $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ form.

Counterexample: $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x + 1} = \frac{0}{1} = 0$$

Why it fails: Applying L'Hôpital gives $\lim \frac{\cos x}{1} = 1$, which is completely incorrect because direct substitution yields $0$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always evaluate by direct substitution first before applying any limit rule.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Ignoring Left-Hand and Right-Hand Limit Discrepancies

Common Pitfall: Assuming a limit exists simply because algebraic manipulation yields a single formula, ignoring absolute values or step functions.

Counterexample: $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{|x|}{x}$$

Why it fails: Left-hand limit is $-1$ and right-hand limit is $+1$, hence the two-sided limit does not exist.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check one-sided limits for modulus, greatest integer, or signum functions.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Evaluate the limit: $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x - \sin x}{x^3}$$

(A) $0$(B) $\frac{1}{2}$(C) $1$(D) $\frac{1}{3}$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Write $\tan x = x + \frac{x^3}{3} + \dots$ and $\sin x = x - \frac{x^3}{6} + \dots$. Then numerator is $\left(x + \frac{x^3}{3}\right) - \left(x - \frac{x^3}{6}\right) = \frac{x^3}{2}$. Dividing by $x^3$ gives $\frac{1}{2}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Apply L'Hôpital's rule three times successively or use standard series expansion.
💡 Key Takeaway: Series expansion is faster than three rounds of L'Hôpital's rule.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the value of $k$ if $f(x) = \frac{\sqrt{1+kx} - \sqrt{1-kx}}{x}$ has a limit of $2$ as $x \to 0$.

(A) $1$(B) $2$(C) $3$(D) $4$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Rationalize the numerator by multiplying by $(\sqrt{1+kx} + \sqrt{1-kx})$. This simplifies to $\frac{2kx}{x(\sqrt{1+kx} + \sqrt{1-kx})} = \frac{2k}{\sqrt{1+kx} + \sqrt{1-kx}}$. Taking $x \to 0$ gives $\frac{2k}{2} = k$. Given limit is $2$, so $k = 2$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Use binomial approximation $(1+u)^n \approx 1 + nu$: numerator becomes $1 + \frac{1}{2}kx - (1 - \frac{1}{2}kx) = kx$. Thus limit is $k = 2$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Binomial approximation cuts down algebraic steps by 70%.

Q1
MCQ 1: Evaluate \(\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(ax)}{\sin(bx)}\).
A (A) $\frac{a}{b}$
B (B) $\frac{b}{a}$
C (C) $1$
D (D) $ab$
Derivation & Analysis:

Rewrite as $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(ax)}{ax} \cdot \frac{bx}{\sin(bx)} \cdot \frac{a}{b} = 1 \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{a}{b} = \frac{a}{b}$.

Q2
MCQ 2: Evaluate \(\lim_{x \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{1}{x}\right)^{2x}\).
A (A) $e$
B (B) $e^2$
C (C) $1$
D (D) $\infty$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using standard limit \(\lim_{x \to \infty} (1 + \frac{a}{x})^{bx} = e^{ab}\), here $a=1, b=2$, so result is $e^{1 \cdot 2} = e^2$.

Q3
MCQ 3: The limit \(\lim_{x \to 0} x \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\) is equal to:
A (A) $1$
B (B) $-1$
C (C) $0$
D (D) Does not exist
Derivation & Analysis:

By Sandwich Theorem, $-|x| \le x \sin(1/x) \le |x|$. Since $\lim_{x \to 0} -|x| = \lim_{x \to 0} |x| = 0$, the limit is $0$.

Q4
MCQ 4: Evaluate \(\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{a^x - 1}{x}\) ($a > 0$).
A (A) $1$
B (B) $a$
C (C) $\ln a$
D (D) $0$
Derivation & Analysis:

This is the standard definition of derivative of $a^x$ at $x=0$, which evaluates directly to $\ln a$.

Q5
MCQ 5: If \(f(x) = \frac{|x|}{x} for x \neq 0\), then \(\lim_{x \to 0^+} f(x) - \lim_{x \to 0^-} f(x)\) is:
A (A) $0$
B (B) $1$
C (C) $-2$
D (D) $2$
Derivation & Analysis:

Right-hand limit is $+1$ and left-hand limit is $-1$. Difference is $1 - (-1) = 2$.

Q6
MCQ 6: Evaluate \(\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\log(1+x)}{x}\).
A (A) $0$
B (B) $1$
C (C) $e$
D (D) $\infty$
Derivation & Analysis:

Standard limit formula yielding $1$.

Q7
MCQ 7: Evaluate \(\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^3 - 8}{x^2 - 4}\).
A (A) $2$
B (B) $3$
C (C) $4$
D (D) $6$
Derivation & Analysis:

Factorize numerator: $(x-2)(x^2+2x+4)$ and denominator: $(x-2)(x+2)$. Cancel $(x-2)$ and substitute $x=2$ to get $\frac{4+4+4}{4} = 3$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Which of the following limits does NOT exist?
A (A) $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x}$
B (B) $\lim_{x \to 0} e^{1/x}$
C (C) $\lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \cos(1/x)$
D (D) $\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^2-1}{x-1}$
Derivation & Analysis:

For $\lim_{x \to 0} e^{1/x}$, the left-hand limit is $0$ and right-hand limit is $\infty$, so it does not exist.

Q9
MCQ 9: Evaluate \(\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos(2x)}{x^2}\).
A (A) $1$
B (B) $2$
C (C) $4$
D (D) $0$
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $1 - \cos(2x) = 2\sin^2 x$, expression becomes $\lim \frac{2\sin^2 x}{x^2} = 2(1)^2 = 2$.

Q10
MCQ 10: Find \(\lim_{x \to \infty} \left(\frac{x+2}{x-1}\right)^x\).
A (A) $e$
B (B) $e^2$
C (C) $e^3$
D (D) $1$
Derivation & Analysis:

Rewrite as $\left(1 + \frac{3}{x-1}\right)^x = \left(1 + \frac{3}{x-1}\right)^{x-1} \cdot \left(1 + \frac{3}{x-1}\right)^1 \to e^3 \cdot 1 = e^3$.
