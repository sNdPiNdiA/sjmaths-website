2. Calculus — Integration

Integration by substitution

Integration by substitution (the change of variable method) transforms complex integrals into standard forms by leveraging the chain rule of differentiation in reverse. Mastery of this core calculus technique is vital for securing high scores in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations, where integrals involving composite functions frequently appear.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Theorem of Integration by Substitution (Change of Variable)
Let $g: [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}$ be a differentiable function whose derivative $g'$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, and let $f$ be continuous on the range of $g$. Then, $$\int_{a}^{b} f(g(x)) g'(x)\,dx = \int_{g(a)}^{g(b)} f(u)\,du$ where $u = g(x)$ and $du = g'(x)\,dx$. For indefinite integrals: $$\int f(g(x)) g'(x)\,dx = F(g(x)) + C$$ where $F'(u) = f(u)$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, substitution rescales the domain of integration by matching the infinitesimal differential element $dx$ with the local magnification factor given by the slope $g'(x)$, thereby preserving the net accumulated area under the transformed curve.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, it acts as the inverse operation to the chain rule of differentiation. It linearizes or simplifies integrands containing a function and its derivative product factor, reducing non-standard integrals into elementary antiderivatives.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Continuity of $f$ on the range Range of $g(x)$ (Connected interval) Strictly Mandatory / Necessary If $f$ is discontinuous within the range of integration, the antiderivative $F$ may not exist or Riemann integrability fails.
Continuity and Differentiability of $g$ [a, b] (Closed interval) Strictly Mandatory If $g'(x)$ has jump discontinuities or fails to exist at interior points, the differential transformation $du = g'(x)dx$ becomes invalid.
Monotonicity / Bijectivity of $g$ (for definite integrals) [a, b] Essential for unique inverse mapping If $g$ is not injective, multiple domains map to the same range value, leading to overlapping accumulation of area and incorrect definite integral evaluation unless broken into monotonic sub-intervals.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Logarithmic Derivative Pattern

$$\int \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\,dx = \ln|f(x)| + C$$

Exam Rule: If the numerator is the exact derivative of the denominator, the answer is immediately the natural logarithm of the denominator.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT and TGT examinations with trigonometric ratios like $\frac{\sec^2 x}{\tan x}$ or $\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}$.
02

Shortcut 2: Power-Chain Formula

$$\int [f(x)]^n f'(x)\,dx = \frac{[f(x)]^{n+1}}{n+1} + C \quad (n \neq -1)$$

Exam Rule: When a composite function raised to a power is multiplied by its base's derivative, ignore the derivative and increment the power by 1.

⚡ PYQ Context: Extensively used in algebraic-trigonometric mixed expressions in DSSSB and KVS PGT exams.
03

Shortcut 3: Exponential Composite Rule

$$\int e^{f(x)} f'(x)\,dx = e^{f(x)} + C$$

Exam Rule: Whenever an exponential function has an exponent whose derivative multiplies the term, the integral is simply the exponential term itself.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently features in 10-second matching questions in LT Grade exams.
04

Shortcut 4: Inverse Trigonometric Differential Forms

$$\int \frac{f'(x)}{\sqrt{1 - [f(x)]^2}}\,dx = \sin^{-1}(f(x)) + C$$

Exam Rule: Identify standard derivatives inside standard inverse trigonometric denominators to instantly write standard inverse trig outputs.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard scoring item across all state board PGT tests.
05

Shortcut 5: Linear Scaling Substitution

$$ \int f(ax + b)\,dx = \frac{1}{a} F(ax + b) + C $$

Exam Rule: For linear inner functions, perform standard integration with respect to the linear expression and divide by the coefficient of $x$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used repeatedly to simplify long expressions in preliminary exam sections.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Domain & Limits

$$ \int_{a}^{b} f(g(x))g'(x)\,dx = \int_{g(a)}^{g(b)} f(u)\,du $$

Exam Rule: For definite integrals with substitution, always transform limits $u_1 = g(a)$ and $u_2 = g(b)$ instantly. Options failing to change limits can be eliminated at a glance.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves up to 45 seconds per question in competitive math papers.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Forgetting to Change Limits in Definite Integrals

Common Pitfall: Candidates substitute $u = g(x)$, integrate with respect to $u$, and then plug in the original limits of $x$ without back-substituting or changing limits.

Counterexample: $$\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin x \cos x \, dx$$

Why it fails: Plugging $x$-limits into a $u$-expression yields completely erroneous values because $u$ spans a different interval than $x$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Either change the lower and upper limits immediately upon substitution ($u = g(a)$ and $u = g(b)$) or completely revert to $x$ before applying limits.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Non-Injective Substitution Pitfall

Common Pitfall: Using a non-monotonic substitution (such as $u = \sin x$ over an interval spanning multiple quadrants) in a definite integral without splitting the domain.

Counterexample: $$\int_{0}^{\pi} \cos x \, dx$$

Why it fails: If $u = \sin x$, $du = \cos x \, dx$, but $\sin x$ is not one-to-one on $[0, \pi]$, causing sign cancellation errors and division-by-zero anomalies in inverse mappings.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Ensure the substitution function $g(x)$ is strictly monotonic (one-to-one) on the interval of integration.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Evaluate the definite integral $$\int_{0}^{1} \frac{x^3}{1 + x^8} \, dx$$.

(A) $\frac{\pi}{8}$(B) $\frac{\pi}{4}$(C) $\frac{\pi}{16}$(D) $\frac{1}{4}\ln 2$
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Notice $x^8 = (x^4)^2$ and the numerator contains $x^3$, which is proportional to the derivative of $x^4$. Step 2: Put $u = x^4$, so $du = 4x^3 dx \implies x^3 dx = \frac{1}{4} du$. Step 3: Change limits: when $x=0$, $u=0$; when $x=1$, $u=1$. Step 4: The integral becomes $\frac{1}{4} \int_{0}^{1} \frac{1}{1 + u^2} du = \frac{1}{4} [\tan^{-1} u]_{0}^{1} = \frac{1}{4} \left(\frac{\pi}{4} - 0\right) = \frac{\pi}{16}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Spot $x^3$ as $\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{dx}(x^4)$. Immediately write $\frac{1}{4}[\tan^{-1}(x^4)]_0^1 = \frac{1}{4}\frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\pi}{16}$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always check if the power in the denominator is the square of a variable whose derivative sits in the numerator.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the value of $$\int \frac{\dx}{x(\ln x)^3}$$.

(A) $-\frac{1}{2(\ln x)^2} + C$(B) $\frac{1}{2(\ln x)^2} + C$(C) $-\frac{1}{(\ln x)^2} + C$(D) $\ln|\ln x| + C$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Recognize that $\frac{1}{x} dx$ is the differential of $\ln x$. Step 2: Let $u = \ln x$, then $du = \frac{1}{x} dx$. Step 3: Integral transforms to $\int u^{-3} du$. Step 4: Integrate using power rule: $\frac{u^{-2}}{-2} + C = -\frac{1}{2(\ln x)^2} + C$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Rewrite as $\int (\ln x)^{-3} d(\ln x)$ and apply reverse power rule instantly.
💡 Key Takeaway: Logarithmic denominators with powers are frequently asked in UP PGT to test substitution fluency.

Let $u = \sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$, then $du = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} dx \implies \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}} = 2 du$. Integral becomes $2 \int \sec^2(u) du = 2 \tan(\sqrt{x}) + C$.

MCQ 3: The value of $$\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^5 x \cos x \, dx$$ is:
A (A) $\frac{1}{6}$

Let $u = \sin x$, $du = \cos x dx$. Limits: $x=0 \implies u=0$, $x=\pi/2 \implies u=1$. Integral: $\int_{0}^{1} u^5 du = \left[\frac{u^6}{6}\right]_{0}^{1} = \frac{1}{6}$.

A (A) $\frac{1}{(\ln x)^2}$
B (B) $\ln|x| + C$

D (D) $\frac{1}{x} + C$

Let $u = \ln x$, $du = \frac{1}{x} dx$. Integral becomes $\int \frac{1}{u} du = \ln|u| + C = \ln|\ln x| + C$.

Q5
MCQ 5: Evaluate $$\int \frac{e^{x} - e^{-x}}{e^{x} + e^{-x}} \, dx$$.
A (A) $\ln|e^x + e^{-x}| + C$
B (B) $\ln|e^x - e^{-x}| + C$
C (C) $e^x + e^{-x} + C$
D (D) $-\ln|e^x + e^{-x}| + C$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $u = e^x + e^{-x}$, then $du = (e^x - e^{-x}) dx$. The integral is of the form $\int \frac{du}{u} = \ln|u| + C = \ln|e^x + e^{-x}| + C$.

Q6
MCQ 6: Evaluate $$\int \frac{dx}{1 + e^x}$$.
A (A) $x - \ln(1 + e^x) + C$
B (B) $\ln(1 + e^x) + C$
C (C) $-\ln(1 + e^x) + C$
D (D) $\ln(e^x - 1) + C$
Derivation & Analysis:

Rewrite as $\int \frac{e^{-x} dx}{e^{-x} + 1}$. Let $u = e^{-x} + 1$, then $du = -e^{-x} dx$. Integral becomes $-\int \frac{du}{u} = -\ln|e^{-x} + 1| + C = x - \ln(1 + e^x) + C$.

MCQ 7: Evaluate $$\int \frac{\sin(\tan^{-1} x)}{1 + x^2} \, dx$$.
A (A) $\cos(\tan^{-1} x) + C$
B (B) $-\cos(\tan^{-1} x) + C$
C (C) $\sin(\tan^{-1} x) + C$
D (D) $\frac{1}{1+x^2} + C$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $u = \tan^{-1} x$, then $du = \frac{dx}{1 + x^2}$. Integral becomes $\int \sin u \, du = -\cos u + C = -\cos(\tan^{-1} x) + C$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): $$\int \frac{2x}{(1+x^2)^2} dx = -\frac{1}{1+x^2} + C$$. Reason (R): $\int [f(x)]^n f'(x) dx = \frac{[f(x)]^{n+1}}{n+1} + C$.
A (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (C) (A) is true but (R) is false
D (D) (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $u = 1+x^2$, $du = 2x dx$. Integral is $\int u^{-2} du = -u^{-1} + C = -\frac{1}{1+x^2} + C$. Both assertion and reason are true, and reason directly justifies the substitution formula used.

Q9
MCQ 9: Evaluate $$\int \frac{\sin x}{\sqrt{1 + \cos x}} \, dx$$.
A (A) $-2\sqrt{1+\cos x} + C$
B (B) $2\sqrt{1+\cos x} + C$
C (C) $-\sqrt{1+\cos x} + C$
D (D) $\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+\cos x} + C$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $u = 1 + \cos x$, then $du = -\sin x dx$. Integral becomes $-\int u^{-1/2} du = -2u^{1/2} + C = -2\sqrt{1+\cos x} + C$.

Q10
MCQ 10: Evaluate $$\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x} + x}$$.
A (A) $2 \ln(1 + \sqrt{x}) + C$
B (B) $\ln(1 + \sqrt{x}) + C$
C (C) $2 \ln|x| + C$
D (D) $\frac{1}{2}\ln(1+\sqrt{x}) + C$
Derivation & Analysis:

Factor denominator: $\sqrt{x}(1 + \sqrt{x})$. Let $u = \sqrt{x}$, then $du = \frac{dx}{2\sqrt{x}} \implies \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}} = 2 du$. Integral becomes $2 \int \frac{du}{1+u} = 2 \ln|1+u| + C = 2 \ln(1+\sqrt{x}) + C$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Look for the inner function of a composite expression, the argument of a transcendental function (trig, exponential, logarithmic), or a denominator whose derivative (or a constant multiple of it) appears elsewhere in the integrand.

While back-substituting into the antiderivative and using original $x$-limits yields the correct answer, changing limits immediately prevents algebraic errors, eliminates redundant back-substitution steps, and saves crucial time in timed examinations like UP PGT.

Integration forms the backbone of calculus in UP exams. Typically, 3 to 5 direct or indirect questions test substitution techniques either as standalone integrals or within differential equations and area under curves.

Memorize standard derivative-integral pairs (like $\frac{f'}{f}$, $f'e^f$, $[f]^n f'$), perform mental substitutions for linear scalings ($ax+b$), and leverage option elimination by checking domain boundaries and derivatives of options.

D (D) $\sec(\sqrt{x}) + C$

Let $u = \sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$, then $du = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} dx \implies \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}} = 2 du$. Integral becomes $2 \int \sec^2(u) du = 2 \tan(\sqrt{x}) + C$.

Q3
MCQ 3: The value of $$\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^5 x \cos x \, dx$$ is:
A (A) $\frac{1}{6}$
B (B) $\frac{1}{3}$
C (C) $\frac{1}{5}$
D (D) $\frac{1}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $u = \sin x$, $du = \cos x dx$. Limits: $x=0 \implies u=0$, $x=\pi/2 \implies u=1$. Integral: $\int_{0}^{1} u^5 du = \left[\frac{u^6}{6}\right]_{0}^{1} = \frac{1}{6}$.

Q4
MCQ 4: Evaluate $$\int \frac{1}{x \ln x} \, dx$$.
A (A) $\frac{1}{(\ln x)^2}$
B (B) $\ln|x| + C$
C (C) $\ln|\ln x| + C$
D (D) $\frac{1}{x} + C$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $u = \ln x$, $du = \frac{1}{x} dx$. Integral becomes $\int \frac{1}{u} du = \ln|u| + C = \ln|\ln x| + C$.

Q5
MCQ 5: Evaluate $$\int \frac{e^{x} - e^{-x}}{e^{x} + e^{-x}} \, dx$$.
A (A) $\ln|e^x + e^{-x}| + C$
B (B) $\ln|e^x - e^{-x}| + C$
C (C) $e^x + e^{-x} + C$
D (D) $-\ln|e^x + e^{-x}| + C$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $u = e^x + e^{-x}$, then $du = (e^x - e^{-x}) dx$. The integral is of the form $\int \frac{du}{u} = \ln|u| + C = \ln|e^x + e^{-x}| + C$.

Q6
MCQ 6: Evaluate $$\int \frac{dx}{1 + e^x}$$.
A (A) $x - \ln(1 + e^x) + C$
B (B) $\ln(1 + e^x) + C$
C (C) $-\ln(1 + e^x) + C$
D (D) $\ln(e^x - 1) + C$
Derivation & Analysis:

Rewrite as $\int \frac{e^{-x} dx}{e^{-x} + 1}$. Let $u = e^{-x} + 1$, then $du = -e^{-x} dx$. Integral becomes $-\int \frac{du}{u} = -\ln|e^{-x} + 1| + C = x - \ln(1 + e^x) + C$.

Q7
MCQ 7: Evaluate $$\int \frac{\sin(\tan^{-1} x)}{1 + x^2} \, dx$$.
A (A) $\cos(\tan^{-1} x) + C$
B (B) $-\cos(\tan^{-1} x) + C$
C (C) $\sin(\tan^{-1} x) + C$
D (D) $\frac{1}{1+x^2} + C$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $u = \tan^{-1} x$, then $du = \frac{dx}{1 + x^2}$. Integral becomes $\int \sin u \, du = -\cos u + C = -\cos(\tan^{-1} x) + C$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): $$\int \frac{2x}{(1+x^2)^2} dx = -\frac{1}{1+x^2} + C$$. Reason (R): $\int [f(x)]^n f'(x) dx = \frac{[f(x)]^{n+1}}{n+1} + C$.
A (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (C) (A) is true but (R) is false
D (D) (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $u = 1+x^2$, $du = 2x dx$. Integral is $\int u^{-2} du = -u^{-1} + C = -\frac{1}{1+x^2} + C$. Both assertion and reason are true, and reason directly justifies the substitution formula used.

Q9
MCQ 9: Evaluate $$\int \frac{\sin x}{\sqrt{1 + \cos x}} \, dx$$.
A (A) $-2\sqrt{1+\cos x} + C$
B (B) $2\sqrt{1+\cos x} + C$
C (C) $-\sqrt{1+\cos x} + C$
D (D) $\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+\cos x} + C$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $u = 1 + \cos x$, then $du = -\sin x dx$. Integral becomes $-\int u^{-1/2} du = -2u^{1/2} + C = -2\sqrt{1+\cos x} + C$.

Q10
MCQ 10: Evaluate $$\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x} + x}$$.
A (A) $2 \ln(1 + \sqrt{x}) + C$
B (B) $\ln(1 + \sqrt{x}) + C$
C (C) $2 \ln|x| + C$
D (D) $\frac{1}{2}\ln(1+\sqrt{x}) + C$
Derivation & Analysis:

Factor denominator: $\sqrt{x}(1 + \sqrt{x})$. Let $u = \sqrt{x}$, then $du = \frac{dx}{2\sqrt{x}} \implies \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}} = 2 du$. Integral becomes $2 \int \frac{du}{1+u} = 2 \ln|1+u| + C = 2 \ln(1+\sqrt{x}) + C$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Look for the inner function of a composite expression, the argument of a transcendental function (trig, exponential, logarithmic), or a denominator whose derivative (or a constant multiple of it) appears elsewhere in the integrand.

While back-substituting into the antiderivative and using original $x$-limits yields the correct answer, changing limits immediately prevents algebraic errors, eliminates redundant back-substitution steps, and saves crucial time in timed examinations like UP PGT.

Integration forms the backbone of calculus in UP exams. Typically, 3 to 5 direct or indirect questions test substitution techniques either as standalone integrals or within differential equations and area under curves.

Memorize standard derivative-integral pairs (like $\frac{f'}{f}$, $f'e^f$, $[f]^n f'$), perform mental substitutions for linear scalings ($ax+b$), and leverage option elimination by checking domain boundaries and derivatives of options.
