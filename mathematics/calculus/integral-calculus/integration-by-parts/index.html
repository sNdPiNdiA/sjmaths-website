2. Calculus — Integration

Integration by parts

Integration by parts is the foundational integral calculus counterpart to the product rule for differentiation, converting the integral of a product into alternative forms. Mastering its recursive application and the LIATE rule is vital for high accuracy and rapid scoring in competitive exams like UP PGT/TGT, DSSSB, and KVS.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Integration by Parts Formula
If $u = f(x)$ and $v = g(x)$ are two differentiable functions of $x$, then the integral of their product is given by $$\int f(x)g(x)\,dx = f(x)\int g(x)\,dx - \int \left[f'(x)\int g(x)\,dx\right]dx$$, or in compact notation, $$\int u \, dv = u v - \int v \, du$$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, integration by parts relates the area under a product curve to complementary rectangular sub-regions and the area under the transformed derivative curve, underpinning transformations in coordinate geometry.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It stems directly from the differential product rule $d(uv) = u\,dv + v\,du$, serving as an indispensable tool for reducing the algebraic complexity of transcendental and polynomial-algebraic mixed integrands.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Differentiability of the first factor (a, b) (Open interval) Strictly Mandatory The derivative $f'(x)$ must exist on the interval, otherwise the secondary integral containing $f'(x)$ cannot be formed.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: The LIATE Priority Rule for Choosing $u$

$$ \text{LIATE: } \text{Logarithmic} \to \text{Inverse Trig} \to \text{Algebraic} \to \text{Trigonometric} \to \text{Exponential} $$

Exam Rule: Always assign $u$ to the function appearing earlier in the LIATE acronym to ensure algebraic simplification upon differentiation.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly applied in UP PGT/TGT to integrate $\int x^n \ln x \, dx$ or $\int x^2 e^x \, dx$ within 30 seconds.
02

Shortcut 2: Bernoulli's Tabular Integration (DI Method)

$$ \int P(x)e^{ax}\,dx = P(x)\frac{e^{ax}}{a} - P'(x)\frac{e^{ax}}{a^2} + P''(x)\frac{e^{ax}}{a^3} - \cdots $$

Exam Rule: For polynomials multiplied by exponentials or sines/cosines, differentiate the polynomial successively to zero while integrating the other part in parallel with alternating signs.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial time-saver for degree-3 or degree-4 polynomial integrals frequently asked in DSSSB PGT and UP PGT.
03

Shortcut 3: Exponential-Trigonometric Cyclic Integral

$$ \int e^{ax}\sin(bx)\,dx = \frac{e^{ax}}{a^2+b^2}[a\sin(bx) - b\cos(bx)] + C $$

Exam Rule: Recognize the cyclic loop after applying parts twice and solve algebraically without recalculating.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard recurring question type in KVS PGT and UP TGT mathematics.
04

Shortcut 4: Reduction Formula for Powers of Sines and Cosines

$$ \int \sin^n x \, dx = -\frac{\sin^{n-1}x \cos x}{n} + \frac{n-1}{n}\int \sin^{n-2}x \, dx $$

Exam Rule: Use recursive reduction steps to drop high integer powers down to standard forms instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently featured in LT Grade and UP PGT definitive calculus problems.
05

Shortcut 5: Integral of $e^x[f(x) + f'(x)]$

$$ \int e^x [f(x) + f'(x)]\,dx = e^x f(x) + C $$

Exam Rule: Spot the function and its derivative combined with $e^x$ to write the answer instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct scoring question in almost every state teaching recruitment exam.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Logarithmic Arguments

$$ \int \ln(x^2 + a^2)\,dx = x\ln(x^2+a^2) - 2x + 2a\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{a}\right) + C $$

Exam Rule: Memorize standard inverse and logarithmic integral expansion structures to eliminate incorrect sign options immediately.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used to eliminate 2 distractor options in multi-term logarithmic integrals.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Forgetting the Integration Constant in Intermediate Steps

Common Pitfall: Omitting $+ C$ during intermediate integration of $dv$ can lead to compounding algebraic errors in definite or cyclic integrals.

Counterexample: $$\int x \cdot 0 \, dx \neq \text{incomplete forms}$$

Why it fails: In cyclic integrals like $\int e^x \cos x \, dx$, missing or mishandling constants leads to self-cancelling identity traps ($0 = 0$).

🛡️ Exam Defense: Combine or properly account for constants only at the final stage of indefinite integration.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Wrong Choice of $u$ and $dv$

Common Pitfall: Choosing an algebraic function as $dv$ when it increases in complexity upon integration (e.g., trying to integrate $\ln x$ by setting $u = x$).

Counterexample: $$\int \ln x \, dx \text{ with } u = x, dv = \frac{1}{x}dx \text{ leads to non-termination}$$

Why it fails: The resulting integral becomes more complex rather than simpler.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always follow the LIATE hierarchy strictly.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Evaluate the integral $I = \int x e^{2x} \, dx$.

(A) \frac{1}{2}x e^{2x} - \frac{1}{4}e^{2x} + C(B) x e^{2x} - e^{2x} + C(C) \frac{1}{4}x e^{2x} - \frac{1}{2}e^{2x} + C(D) \frac{1}{2}x e^{2x} + \frac{1}{4}e^{2x} + C
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Let $u = x$ and $dv = e^{2x}dx$. Then $du = dx$ and $v = \frac{1}{2}e^{2x}$. Applying parts: $\int x e^{2x}dx = x \left(\frac{1}{2}e^{2x}\right) - \int \frac{1}{2}e^{2x}dx = \frac{1}{2}x e^{2x} - \frac{1}{4}e^{2x} + C$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Use Bernoulli's table: Differentiate $x \to 1 \to 0$; Integrate $e^{2x} \to \frac{1}{2}e^{2x} \to \frac{1}{4}e^{2x}$. Combine with signs $(+) (-)$: $x(\frac{1}{2}e^{2x}) - 1(\frac{1}{4}e^{2x}) + C$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Check coefficients of exponential denominators carefully to avoid sign traps.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Evaluate $I = \int \ln x \, dx$.

(A) x \ln x + x + C(B) x \ln x - x + C(C) \frac{\ln x}{x} + C(D) \ln x - x + C
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Rewrite as $\int (\ln x) \cdot 1 \, dx$. Let $u = \ln x, dv = dx$. Then $du = \frac{1}{x}dx, v = x$. Formula gives $x \ln x - \int x \cdot \frac{1}{x}dx = x \ln x - \int 1 \, dx = x \ln x - x + C$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Direct standard result memorization for logarithmic integrals.
💡 Key Takeaway: Frequently asked as a direct formula in UP TGT exams.

Q1
What is the value of $\int x \sin x \, dx$?
A -x \cos x + \sin x + C
B x \cos x - \sin x + C
C -x \sin x + \cos x + C
D x \sin x + \cos x + C
Derivation & Analysis:

Set $u = x, dv = \sin x dx$. Then $du = dx, v = -\cos x$. Integral = $-x\cos x - \int(-\cos x)dx = -x\cos x + \sin x + C$.

Q2
Evaluate $\int x^2 e^x \, dx$.
A (x^2 - 2x + 2)e^x + C
B (x^2 + 2x + 2)e^x + C
C (x^2 - 2x - 2)e^x + C
D None of these
Derivation & Analysis:

Using tabular integration: Polynomial $x^2 \to 2x \to 2 \to 0$. Exponential $e^x \to e^x \to e^x \to e^x$. Signs: $+ - +$. Result: $x^2(e^x) - 2x(e^x) + 2(e^x) + C = (x^2 - 2x + 2)e^x + C$.

Q3
The integral $\int e^x (\tan x + \sec^2 x) \, dx$ equals:
A e^x \tan x + C
B e^x \sec x + C
C e^x \ln(\sec x) + C
D e^x \cot x + C
Derivation & Analysis:

Here $f(x) = \tan x$ and $f'(x) = \sec^2 x$. By the standard property $\int e^x [f(x)+f'(x)]dx = e^x f(x) + C$, the answer is $e^x \tan x + C$.

Q4
Evaluate $\int \tan^{-1} x \, dx$.
A x \tan^{-1} x + \frac{1}{2}\ln(1+x^2) + C
B x \tan^{-1} x - \frac{1}{2}\ln(1+x^2) + C
C \tan^{-1} x - x + C
D \frac{x}{1+x^2} + C
Derivation & Analysis:

Take $u = \tan^{-1}x, dv = dx$. Then $du = \frac{1}{1+x^2}dx, v = x$. Result is $x\tan^{-1}x - \int \frac{x}{1+x^2}dx = x\tan^{-1}x - \frac{1}{2}\ln(1+x^2) + C$.

Q5
Evaluate $\int e^{2x} \cos(3x) \, dx$.
A \frac{e^{2x}}{13}(2\cos 3x + 3\sin 3x) + C
B \frac{e^{2x}}{13}(2\cos 3x - 3\sin 3x) + C
C \frac{e^{2x}}{13}(3\cos 3x + 2\sin 3x) + C
D None of these
Derivation & Analysis:

Using formula $\int e^{ax}\cos(bx)dx = \frac{e^{ax}}{a^2+b^2}[a\cos(bx)+b\sin(bx)]+C$ with $a=2, b=3$: $\frac{e^{2x}}{4+9}[2\cos 3x + 3\sin 3x] + C$.

Q6
What is $\int \sec^3 x \, dx$?
A \frac{1}{2}\sec x \tan x + \frac{1}{2}\ln|\sec x + \tan x| + C
B \sec x \tan x + \ln|\sec x + \tan x| + C
C \frac{1}{2}\sec^2 x + C
D None
Derivation & Analysis:

Writing as $\int \sec x \cdot \sec^2 x dx$, take $u = \sec x, dv = \sec^2 x dx$. Reduction yields $\frac{1}{2}\sec x \tan x + \frac{1}{2}\ln|\sec x + \tan x| + C$.

Q7
Evaluate $\int x \sec^2 x \, dx$.
A x \tan x + \ln|\cos x| + C
B x \tan x - \ln|\cos x| + C
C x \tan x + \tan x + C
D x \sec x + C
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $u = x, dv = \sec^2 x dx \implies v = \tan x$. Integral is $x\tan x - \int \tan x dx = x\tan x - \ln|\sec x| + C = x\tan x + \ln|\cos x| + C$.

Q8
Find $\int (x^2 + 1)\ln x \, dx$.
A \left(\frac{x^3}{3} + x\)\ln x - \frac{x^3}{9} - x + C
B \left(\frac{x^3}{3} + x\)\ln x + \frac{x^3}{9} + x + C
C \frac{x^3}{3}\ln x + C
D None of these
Derivation & Analysis:

LIATE dictates $u = \ln x, dv = (x^2+1)dx \implies v = \frac{x^3}{3}+x$. Applying parts gives the expanded algebraic form.

Q9
If $I_n = \int \tan^n x \, dx$, then $I_n + I_{n-2}$ equals:
A \frac{\tan^{n-1}x}{n-1}
B \frac{\tan^{n-1}x}{n-1} + C
C \tan^{n-1}x
D 0
Derivation & Analysis:

Standard reduction relation for tangent powers gives $I_n = \frac{\tan^{n-1}x}{n-1} - I_{n-2}$, hence $I_n + I_{n-2} = \frac{\tan^{n-1}x}{n-1}$.

Q10
Evaluate $\int x^3 \ln x \, dx$.
A \frac{x^4}{4}\ln x - \frac{x^4}{16} + C
B \frac{x^4}{4}\ln x + \frac{x^4}{16} + C
C x^4 \ln x - x^4 + C
D \frac{1}{4}x^3 \ln x + C
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $u = \ln x, dv = x^3 dx \implies v = \frac{x^4}{4}$. Formula: $\frac{x^4}{4}\ln x - \int \frac{x^4}{4}\cdot \frac{1}{x}dx = \frac{x^4}{4}\ln x - \frac{x^4}{16} + C$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Use substitution when an explicit function-derivative pair ($f(x)$ and $f'(x)$) exists within the integrand. Use integration by parts when the integrand is a product of two completely different elementary functions (such as algebraic and trigonometric or logarithmic).

Sign errors during the second integral step—especially when the derivative of the first term or the integral of the second term introduces negative signs—and forgetting the arbitrary constant $+ C$ in indefinite integrals.

Direct and indirect applications account for 2 to 4 questions in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB examinations, often embedded within definite integrals or differential equations.

Mastering Bernoulli's Tabular method for polynomial-exponential products and memorizing standard cyclic integral results saves 60 to 90 seconds per question.
