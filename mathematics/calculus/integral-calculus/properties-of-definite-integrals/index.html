2. Calculus — Integration

Properties of definite integrals

The properties of definite integrals form the analytical backbone of integral calculus, enabling rapid simplification and evaluation of complex area-under-curve problems. Mastery of symmetry, interval splitting, and shifting properties provides decisive shortcuts heavily favored in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Fundamental Integration Properties & King's Property
$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \int_a^b f(a+b-x)\,dx$$ and for symmetric intervals, $$\int_{-a}^{a} f(x)\,dx = \begin{cases} 2 \int_{0}^{a} f(x)\,dx & \text{if } f(-x) = f(x) \\ 0 & \text{if } f(-x) = -f(x) \end{cases}$$

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Reflecting the region of integration horizontally across the midpoint of the interval $(a+b)/2$ leaves the net accumulated area invariant. For symmetric intervals, even functions double the positive-side area due to mirror symmetry about the y-axis, whereas odd functions cancel out perfectly across the origin.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Converts intractable integrals into symmetrical counterparts whose sum eliminates complex polynomial or trigonometric denominators instantly through variable substitution.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Continuity on $[a, b]$ $[a, b]$ (Closed interval) Strictly Mandatory / Necessary Discontinuities (especially infinite or jump discontinuities) render standard Riemann integration properties invalid without improper integral limits.
Piecewise Continuous on Subdivisions $[a, c]$ and $[c, b]$ Mandatory for Interval Additivity Property Failure to check domain boundaries when splitting integrals across points of definition change leads to divergent or incorrect evaluations.
Symmetry Domain for Parity Rules $[-a, a]$ Essential for Even/Odd Reduction Applying even/odd shortcuts on asymmetric intervals like $[-a, b]$ where $a \neq b$ yields completely erroneous results.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: King's Property (Interval Summation)

$$\int_a^b \frac{f(x)}{f(x) + f(a+b-x)}\,dx = \frac{b-a}{2}$$

Exam Rule: If the integrand takes the form $f(x)/(f(x) + f(a+b-x))$, the answer is always half the length of the interval, $(b-a)/2$, bypassing integration entirely.

⚡ PYQ Context: Extensively tested in UP PGT and KVS with trigonometric and logarithmic functions.
02

Shortcut 2: Symmetric Bounds Reduction

$$\int_{-a}^{a} \frac{x^2 \sin^3 x}{1 + \cos^2 x}\,dx = 0$$

Exam Rule: Instantly check the parity of $f(-x)$. If $f(-x) = -f(x)$ over $[-a, a]$, immediately mark $0$ without calculation.

⚡ PYQ Context: Appears in every second TGT/PGT exam as a 10-second scoring question.
03

Shortcut 3: Periodic Function Property

$$\int_0^{nT} f(x)\,dx = n \int_0^T f(x)\,dx$$

Exam Rule: For a periodic function with period $T$, integer multiples of the period scale the integral linearly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard recurring pattern with fractional part functions and modulated trig functions.
04

Shortcut 4: Integration by Shifting Bounds

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \int_a^b f(a+b-x)\,dx$$

Exam Rule: Replace every $x$ with $a+b-x$ and add the original integral to eliminate complex variable terms.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial for inverse trigonometric and logarithmic definite integrals.
05

Shortcut 5: Wallis's Formula for Definite Integrals

$$\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^n x \cos^m x \, dx$$

Exam Rule: Use the factorial reduction product formula when limits are $0$ to $\pi/2$ and powers are integers.

⚡ PYQ Context: High-frequency appearance in DSSSB and UP PGT calculus sections.
06

Shortcut 6: Leibniz Rule for Integral Differentiation

$$\frac{d}{dx} \int_{g(x)}^{h(x)} f(t)\,dt = f(h(x))h'(x) - f(g(x))g'(x)$$

Exam Rule: Differentiate under the integral sign with variable limits instantly by substituting limits and multiplying by their derivatives.

⚡ PYQ Context: Favorite tool for finding extrema of integral functions in PGT exams.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Blindly Applying Even/Odd Property on Non-Symmetric Intervals

Common Pitfall: Applying $\int_{-a}^a f(x)\,dx = 0$ when the lower limit is not the negative of the upper limit (e.g., $\int_{-1}^2 x^3 \, dx$).

Counterexample: $$\int_{-1}^{2} x^3 \, dx \neq 0$$

Why it fails: The symmetry property requires exact balance $[-a, a]$. Asymmetric intervals leave unbalanced remnants.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check if the lower and upper limits are exact opposites before checking function parity.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Ignoring Domain Restrictions in Logarithmic/Trigonometric Integrands

Common Pitfall: Using King's property or substitution when the integrand has undefined points (asymptotes) inside the interval of integration.

Counterexample: $$\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx$$

Why it fails: The function blows up at $x=0$, violating continuity on $[-1, 1]$. Standard definite integral properties do not apply directly.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check for vertical asymptotes or points of infinite discontinuity within $[a, b]$ before evaluating.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Evaluate the definite integral: $$\int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin x}{\sin x + \cos x}\,dx$$

(A) $\frac{\pi}{4}$(B) $\frac{\pi}{2}$(C) $\pi$(D) $0$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Let $I = \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin x}{\sin x + \cos x}\,dx$. Step 2: Apply property $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \int_a^b f(a+b-x)\,dx$ to get $I = \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\cos x}{\cos x + \sin x}\,dx$. Step 3: Add both equations to get $2I = \int_0^{\pi/2} 1\,dx = \pi/2$. Step 4: Thus $I = \pi/4$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize King's Property standard template where $f(x) + f(a+b-x)$ matches the denominator. Answer is instantly $(b-a)/2 = (\pi/2 - 0)/2 = \pi/4$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Memorize this standard result; examiners frequently change $\sin x$ to $\tan x$ or $\sec x$ with identical outcomes.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Evaluate: $$\int_{-3}^{3} x \sqrt{9-x^2}\,dx$$

(A) $18$(B) $9\pi$(C) $0$(D) $36$
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Identify interval as $[-3, 3]$. Step 2: Let $f(x) = x \sqrt{9-x^2}$. Step 3: Test parity: $f(-x) = (-x)\sqrt{9-(-x)^2} = -x\sqrt{9-x^2} = -f(x)$. Step 4: Since $f(x)$ is an odd function on a symmetric interval, the integral is $0$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Spot odd power of $x$ multiplied by even function on symmetric limits $[-a, a]$. Mark $0$ in 5 seconds.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always check for odd functions when limits are symmetric (e.g., $-a$ to $a$).

Q1
The value of $\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \sin^7 x \cos^2 x \, dx$ is:
A $\frac{2}{63}$
B $0$
C $\frac{\pi}{4}$
D $1$
Derivation & Analysis:

The integrand $f(x) = \sin^7 x \cos^2 x$ satisfies $f(-x) = \sin^7(-x)\cos^2(-x) = -\sin^7 x \cos^2 x = -f(x)$. Thus, by the odd function property on a symmetric interval $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$, the integral is $0$.

Q2
Evaluate $$\int_0^1 \log \left(\frac{1-x}{x}\right)\,dx$$
A $1$
B $\pi$
C $0$
D $-1$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $I = \int_0^1 \log(\frac{1-x}{x})\,dx$. Using property $\int_0^a f(x)\,dx = \int_0^a f(a-x)\,dx$, we get $I = \int_0^1 \log(\frac{x}{1-x})\,dx = -I$. Therefore $2I = 0 \implies I = 0$.

Q3
What is the value of $\int_0^{\pi/2} \log(\tan x)\,dx$?
A $\pi$
B $1$
C $-\pi$
D $0$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using King's property, $\int_0^{\pi/2} \log(\tan x)\,dx = \int_0^{\pi/2} \log(\cot x)\,dx = -\int_0^{\pi/2} \log(\tan x)\,dx$. Thus $2I = 0 \implies I = 0$.

Q4
If $f(x)$ is continuous and $\int_0^a f(x)\,dx = \int_0^a f(a-x)\,dx$, then $\int_0^a x f(x)\,dx$ is equal to:
A $$\frac{a}{2} \int_0^a f(x)\,dx$$
B $$a \int_0^a f(x)\,dx$$
C $$0$$
D $$\int_0^a f(x)\,dx$$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using the property $\int_0^a x f(x)\,dx = \frac{a}{2} \int_0^a f(x)\,dx$, which is derived by replacing $x$ with $a-x$ and adding.

Q5
The value of $\int_{-1}^{1} |x|\,dx$ is:
A $0$
B $1$
C $2$
D $\frac{1}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

$|x|$ is an even function on $[-1, 1]$. Therefore, $\int_{-1}^1 |x|\,dx = 2 \int_0^1 x\,dx = 2 \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^1 = 1$.

Q6
If $f(x) = a_0 + a_1 x + a_2 x^2 + \dots + a_n x^n$, then $\int_{-1}^{1} f(x)\,dx$ depends only on:
A Odd powers of $x$
B Even powers of $x$
C All powers of $x$
D Constant term only
Derivation & Analysis:

On a symmetric interval $[-1, 1]$, odd powers integrate to $0$. Only even powers contribute $2 \int_0^1 a_{2k} x^{2k} \, dx$.

Q7
Evaluate $$\int_0^2 [x] \, dx$$ where $[x]$ is the greatest integer function:
A $0$
B $1$
C $2$
D $3$
Derivation & Analysis:

Split the interval: $\int_0^1 0\,dx + \int_1^2 1\,dx = 0 + 1(2-1) = 1$.

Q8
The derivative of $\int_0^{x^2} \cos(t^2)\,dt$ with respect to $x$ is:
A $\cos(x^2)$
B $2x \cos(x^4)$
C $x^2 \cos(x^2)$
D $2x \cos(x^2)$
Derivation & Analysis:

By Leibniz rule: $\frac{d}{dx} \int_0^{x^2} \cos(t^2)\,dt = \cos((x^2)^2) \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(x^2) = 2x \cos(x^4)$.

Q9
The value of $\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \frac{x^2 \sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x}\,dx$ is:
A $\pi$
B $\frac{\pi^2}{2}$
C $0$
D $\frac{\pi}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $f(x) = \frac{x^2 \sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x}$. Then $f(-x) = \frac{(-x)^2 \sin(-x)}{1 + \cos^2(-x)} = \frac{-x^2 \sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x} = -f(x)$. Since the integrand is odd and limits are symmetric, the integral is $0$.

Q10
If $f(x)$ is a periodic function with period $T$, then $\int_a^{a+T} f(x)\,dx$ is:
A Independent of $a$
B Dependent on $a$
C Always zero
D Equal to $T \int_0^T f(x)\,dx$
Derivation & Analysis:

A fundamental property of periodic functions states that $\int_a^{a+T} f(x)\,dx = \int_0^T f(x)\,dx$, making it independent of the starting point $a$.

Q9
The value of $\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \frac{x^2 \sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x}\,dx$ is:
A $\pi$
B $\frac{\pi^2}{2}$
C $0$
D $\frac{\pi}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $f(x) = \frac{x^2 \sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x}$. Then $f(-x) = \frac{(-x)^2 \sin(-x)}{1 + \cos^2(-x)} = \frac{-x^2 \sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x} = -f(x)$. Since the integrand is odd and limits are symmetric, the integral is $0$.

Q10
If $f(x)$ is a periodic function with period $T$, then $\int_a^{a+T} f(x)\,dx$ is:
A Independent of $a$
B Dependent on $a$
C Always zero
D Equal to $T \int_0^T f(x)\,dx$
Derivation & Analysis:

A fundamental property of periodic functions states that $\int_a^{a+T} f(x)\,dx = \int_0^T f(x)\,dx$, making it independent of the starting point $a$.
