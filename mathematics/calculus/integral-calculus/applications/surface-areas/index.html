Surface area of cylinder, cone and sphere by integration

This section covers rigorous calculation of lateral and total surface areas of cylinders, cones, and spheres using definite integrals and arc-length elements of revolution. This module bridges intuitive geometric mensuration with differential calculus, providing high-speed computational frameworks.

Definition and statement

Theorem of Surface Area of Revolution
Let $y = f(x)$ be a non-negative, smooth function possessing a continuous derivative $f'(x)$ on the closed interval $[a, b]$. The area $S$ of the surface generated by revolving the curve about the $x$-axis is given by the definite integral $$S = 2\pi \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2} \, dx = 2\pi \int_{a}^{b} y \, ds$$ where $ds = \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2} \, dx$ represents the differential arc length element.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

The surface area is conceived as the limiting sum of frustums of right circular cones. When an infinitesimal arc length $ds$ at distance $y$ from the axis of rotation is revolved around the axis, it sweeps out a elemental conical band of radius $y$ and slant height $ds$, yielding a lateral strip area $dA = 2\pi y \, ds$.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It unifies single-variable integral calculus with differential geometry, transforming the geometric summation of non-flat conical elements into a Riemann integral of the composite function $2\pi f(x)\sqrt{1+[f'(x)]^2}$.

Conditions and hypotheses

Check each condition and what changes when it is omitted:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Continuity of the generating curve $f(x)$ [a, b] (Closed interval) Strictly Mandatory / Necessary If $f(x)$ is discontinuous, the surface tears or forms open boundaries, making the integration domain undefined and Riemann sums divergent.
Continuous differentiability ($f'(x)$ continuous) [a, b] (Closed interval with continuous derivative) Strictly Mandatory If $f'(x)$ has jump discontinuities or vertical tangents, the differential arc element $ds = \sqrt{1+(dy/dx)^2}$ becomes undefined or infinite at those points, invalidating the integral.
Non-negativity ($f(x) \ge 0$) [a, b] Essential for geometric surface area If $f(x)$ changes sign, the radius of revolution becomes negative or self-intersecting surfaces cancel out algebraically, failing to represent a true geometric area.

Useful results and shortcuts

Use these identities and results when their conditions apply:

01

Cylinder Lateral Surface Area via Integration

$$S = 2\pi \int_{0}^{h} r \, dz = 2\pi r h$$

Rule: For a cylinder of radius $r$ and height $h$, the generator is $f(x) = r$ (constant), so $f'(x) = 0$. The integral collapses instantly to $2\pi r \times \text{height}.

Application: Frequently tested as a foundational calculus proof or quick verification in UP TGT/PGT.
02

Right Circular Cone Surface Area via Integration

$$S = \pi r \ell = \pi r \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$$

Rule: Revolving the line $y = \frac{r}{h}x$ from $x=0$ to $h$ about the $x$-axis yields the exact lateral area without evaluating complex radicals.

03

Sphere Surface Area via Parametric / Radial Integration

$$S = 4\pi R^2$$

Rule: For a sphere generated by $x^2 + y^2 = R^2$, use $y = \sqrt{R^2 - x^2}$. The integral $2\pi \int_{-R}^{R} \sqrt{R^2-x^2} \frac{R}{\sqrt{R^2-x^2}} \, dx$ reduces to $2\pi R \int_{-R}^{R} dx = 4\pi R^2$.

Application: Standard derivation question in UP PGT Mathematics.
04

Zone of a Sphere (Spherical Cap)

$$S = 2\pi R h$$

Rule: The surface area of a spherical zone cut by two parallel planes at distance $h$ depends only on the radius of the sphere $R$ and the height $h$ of the zone, independent of its position.

05

Pappus's Centroid Theorem for Surface Area

$$S = 2\pi \bar{y} L$$

Rule: The surface area generated by rotating a plane curve of arc length $L$ about an axis is equal to the product of $L$ and the distance traveled by its geometric centroid $\bar{y}$.

Application: Saves 3 minutes of integration time for complex arcs.
06

Symmetry Doubling Rule for Even Functions

$$S = 4\pi \int_{0}^{a} f(x) \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2} \, dx$$

Rule: When generating symmetric surfaces (like spheres or ellipsoids) about an axis, integrate over half the domain $[0, a]$ and multiply by 2 to eliminate negative bounds.

Common errors and counterexamples

These examples show how a conclusion can fail when a condition is missing:

⚠️ COMMON ERROR #01

Forgetting the Radius Term $y$ in the Integrand

Common Pitfall: Candidates mistakenly use the arc-length formula $\int \sqrt{1+(y')^2}dx$ instead of the surface area formula $2\pi \int y \sqrt{1+(y')^2}dx$.

Counterexample: Using $\int_{-R}^{R} \frac{R}{\sqrt{R^2-x^2}}dx$ computes arc length (or circumference), not surface area.

Why it fails: Omitting the radius factor $y$ misses the circular sweeping radius of revolution, resulting in an incorrect dimension (length instead of area).

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always prefix the integral with $2\pi y$ (or $2\pi x$ depending on the axis of revolution).
⚠️ COMMON ERROR #02

Sign Flaws with Implicit or Lower-Half Curves

Common Pitfall: Revolving a curve where $f(x) < 0$ without taking the absolute value or proper positive branch.

Counterexample: Revolving $y = -\sqrt{R^2-x^2}$ and integrating directly without accounting for orientation.

Why it fails: Produces negative surface area values which are geometrically inadmissible.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always ensure the radius function $y$ in $2\pi \int y \, ds$ is expressed as its non-negative distance from the axis of rotation.

Solved examples

Each problem includes a full solution and a shorter method:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the lateral surface area of a right circular cone of base radius $r$ and height $h$ using integration.

(A) $\pi r \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$(B) $2\pi r \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$(C) $\pi r h$(D) $\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Equation of the slant line generator is $y = \frac{r}{h}x$ for $0 \le x \le h$. Step 2: Derivative $y' = \frac{r}{h}$. Step 3: Apply $S = 2\pi \int_{0}^{h} y \sqrt{1 + (y')^2} \, dx = 2\pi \int_{0}^{h} \frac{r}{h}x \sqrt{1 + \frac{r^2}{h^2}} \, dx$. Step 4: Evaluate to get $\pi r \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize that the lateral area of a cone is $\pi r \ell$, where $\ell = \sqrt{r^2+h^2}$ is the slant height.
💡 Tip: Standard derivation asked directly in UP PGT exams.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the surface area of a sphere of radius $R$ generated by revolving the circle $x^2 + y^2 = R^2$ about the $x$-axis.

(A) $2\pi R^2$(B) $\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3$(C) $4\pi R^2$(D) $\pi R^2$
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Consider upper semi-circle $y = \sqrt{R^2 - x^2}$. Step 2: $y' = \frac{-x}{\sqrt{R^2 - x^2}}$. Step 3: $1 + (y')^2 = \frac{R^2}{R^2-x^2}$. Step 4: $S = 2\pi \int_{-R}^{R} \sqrt{R^2-x^2} \frac{R}{\sqrt{R^2-x^2}} \, dx = 2\pi R \int_{-R}^{R} dx = 4\pi R^2$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recall Archimedes' theorem: surface area of a sphere equals the lateral area of its circumscribed cylinder ($2\pi R \times 2R = 4\pi R^2$).
💡 Tip: Memorize the sphere integral result to save 45 seconds.

Practice questions

Choose an answer to check it and see the solution:

Q1
What is the surface area generated by revolving the line segment $y = x$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 2$ about the $x$-axis?
A $4\sqrt{2}\pi$
B $8\sqrt{2}\pi$
C $2\sqrt{2}\pi$
D $4\pi$
Derivation & Analysis:

Here $f(x) = x$, $f'(x) = 1$. Then $S = 2\pi \int_{0}^{2} x \sqrt{1 + 1^2} \, dx = 2\pi \sqrt{2} \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{0}^{2} = 4\sqrt{2}\pi$.

Q2
The lateral surface area of a cylinder of radius $r$ and height $h$ obtained via integration is:
A $\pi r h$
B $2\pi r h$
C $4\pi r h$
D \pi r^2 h
Derivation & Analysis:

For a cylinder, $y = r$ (constant), $y' = 0$. $S = 2\pi \int_{0}^{h} r \sqrt{1+0} \, dz = 2\pi r [z]_{0}^{h} = 2\pi r h$.

Q3
The surface area of a sphere of radius $R$ is equal to:
A \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3
B 2\pi R^2
C 4\pi R^2
D \pi R^2
Derivation & Analysis:

Standard integral result: $S = 4\pi R^2$.

Q4
If a curve $y = f(x)$ is rotated about the $y$-axis instead of the $x$-axis, the formula for the surface area is:
A 2\pi \int x \sqrt{1 + (y')^2} \, dx
B 2\pi \int y \sqrt{1 + (y')^2} \, dx
C \pi \int y^2 \, dx
D 2\pi \int x^2 \, dx
Derivation & Analysis:

When revolving about the $y$-axis, the radius of revolution is $x$, hence $S = 2\pi \int x \, ds = 2\pi \int x \sqrt{1 + (y')^2} \, dx$.

Q5
What is the surface area of the zone of a sphere of radius $R$ cut by two planes at distance $h$?
A \pi R h
B 2\pi R h
C 4\pi R h
D \pi R^2 h
Derivation & Analysis:

By integrating the surface element over the corresponding arc interval, the area of a spherical zone depends only on the radius and height: $S = 2\pi R h$.

Q6
Which of the following conditions is strictly required for the validity of the surface area integral formula $S = 2\pi \int y \sqrt{1+(y')^2}dx$?
A $f(x)$ must be linear
B $f'(x)$ must be continuous on $[a, b]$
C $f(x)$ must be negative
D $f(x)$ must be discontinuous
Derivation & Analysis:

The derivative $f'(x)$ must be continuous (smooth curve) so that arc length $ds$ is well-defined.

Q7
The integral expression for the surface area of revolution of $y = \sqrt{x}$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 1$ about the $x$-axis is:
A 2\pi \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{x} \sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{4x}} \, dx
B 2\pi \int_{0}^{1} x \sqrt{1 + x} \, dx
C \pi \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{x} \, dx
D 2\pi \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{1 + x} \, dx
Derivation & Analysis:

Here $y = \sqrt{x}$, $y' = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$. Substituting into $2\pi \int y \sqrt{1+(y')^2}dx$ gives $2\pi \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{x} \sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{4x}} \, dx$.

Q8
Pappus's Centroid Theorem states that the surface area generated by rotating a plane curve of length $L$ about an axis is:
A \pi \bar{y} L
B 2\pi \bar{y} L
C 4\pi \bar{y} L
D \frac{1}{2}\pi \bar{y} L
Derivation & Analysis:

Pappus's theorem for surface area of revolution states $S = 2\pi \bar{y} L$, where $\bar{y}$ is the distance of the centroid from the axis of rotation.

Q9
When calculating the surface area of a cone generated by $y = 2x$ from $x=0$ to $x=3$, what is the differential arc length element $ds$?
A \sqrt{3} \, dx
B \sqrt{5} \, dx
C 2 \, dx
D 3 \, dx
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $y' = 2$, $ds = \sqrt{1 + (2)^2} \, dx = \sqrt{5} \, dx$.

Q10
If the curve $y=f(x)$ has a corner point in $(a,b)$, how should the standard surface-area formula be applied?
A (A) Use the formula unchanged across the corner
B (B) Split the integral at the corner and apply the formula on each smooth segment
C (C) The surface area is necessarily zero
D (D) The integral must diverge
Derivation & Analysis:

The usual formula assumes a differentiable curve. For a piecewise-smooth curve, split the interval at the corner and evaluate the surface area on each smooth segment.

Common questions

Integrating $ds$ alone gives the arc length (1D measure) of the curve. Multiplying by $2\pi y$ accounts for the circular perimeter $2\pi y$ swept out at radius $y$ as the arc revolves around the axis, transforming the 1D arc into a 2D surface area.

Forgetting the radius term $y$ in the integrand or failing to simplify the radical expression $\sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2}$ correctly before integration.

Memorize standard results for cylinders, cones, and spheres ($2\pi rh$, $\pi r \ell$, $4\pi R^2$), and utilize Pappus's centroid theorem or symmetry properties to bypass lengthy integration steps.
