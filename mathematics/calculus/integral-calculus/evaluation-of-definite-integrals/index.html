2. Calculus — Integration

Evaluation of definite integrals

Definite integrals represent the net accumulated area under a curve between two fixed limits, rigorously defined via Riemann sums and evaluated using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus. Mastery of definite integral evaluation, along with its powerful symmetry and reduction properties, is a high-yield scoring zone in UP TGT/PGT, DSSSB, and KVS examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (Parts I and II) & Definite Integral Definition
Let $f: [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuous, and let $F(x) = \int_a^x f(t)\,dt$ for $x \in [a, b]$. Then $F$ is differentiable on $(a, b)$ and $F'(x) = f(x)$. Furthermore, if $G$ is any antiderivative of $f$ on $[a, b]$, then $$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = G(b) - G(a)$$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ computes the net signed area bounded by the curve $y = f(x)$, the $x$-axis, and the vertical lines $x = a$ and $x = b$, where areas above the $x$-axis are positive and those below are negative.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It bridges infinitesimal accumulation (sums of infinite terms) with anti-differentiation, transforming complex limit-of-sum problems into straightforward algebraic evaluations.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: King's Property (Symmetric Integration)

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \int_a^b f(a + b - x)\,dx$$

Exam Rule: Instantly eliminates cumbersome denominators by replacing $x$ with $a+b-x$ and adding the original integral to the transformed integral.

⚡ PYQ Context: Extremely frequent in UP PGT and TGT examinations for integrals involving trigonometric ratios like $\frac{\sin^n x}{\sin^n x + \cos^n x}$.
02

Shortcut 2: Even and Odd Function Parity

$$\int_{-a}^{a} f(x)\,dx = \begin{cases} 2 \int_{0}^{a} f(x)\,dx & \text{if } f(-x) = f(x) \\ 0 & \text{if } f(-x) = -f(x) \end{cases}$$

Exam Rule: Check parity immediately when limits are symmetric $[-a, a]$. If odd, write $0$ instantly without integration.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct 5-second questions in KVS, NVS, and UP PGT exams featuring polynomial-trigonometric products.
03

Shortcut 3: Wallis's Formula (Reduction for Trig Powers)

$$\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^n x \cos^m x \, dx = \frac{(n-1)(n-3)\cdots \times (m-1)(m-3)\cdots}{(n+m)(n+m-2)\cdots} \times k$$

Exam Rule: Where $k = \frac{\pi}{2}$ if both $n$ and $m$ are even integers; otherwise $k = 1$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard time-saver for definite integrals of powers of $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ from $0$ to $\frac{\pi}{2}$.
04

Shortcut 4: Periodic Property

$$\int_0^{nT} f(x)\,dx = n \int_0^T f(x)\,dx \quad \text{if } f(x+T) = f(x)$$

Exam Rule: Pull out the multiple $n$ if the interval length is an exact integer multiple of the fundamental period $T$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently tested with fractional parts, modulus of periodic functions, and trigonometric combinations.
05

Shortcut 5: Integral Inequality & Estimation

$$\left| \int_a^b f(x)\,dx \right| \le \int_a^b |f(x)| \, dx$$

Exam Rule: Use bounds $m \le f(x) \le M$ to bound the integral by $m(b-a) \le \int_a^b f(x)\,dx \le M(b-a)$ without full evaluation.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used in option elimination for difficult non-integrable expressions in DSSSB PGT.
06

Shortcut 6: Differentiation Under the Integral Sign (Leibniz Rule)

$$\frac{d}{dx} \left[ \int_{u(x)}^{v(x)} f(t)\,dt \right] = f(v(x))v'(x) - f(u(x))u'(x)$$

Exam Rule: Differentiate parameter limits directly without evaluating the integral first.

⚡ PYQ Context: Appears in advanced calculus portions of UP PGT and LT Grade exams.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Ignoring Discontinuities within the Interval

Common Pitfall: Applying fundamental theorem blindly across a point where the integrand is undefined or discontinuous (e.g., $\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx$).

Counterexample: $$\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx$$

Why it fails: The integrand is discontinuous at $x = 0$. Using $\left[ -\frac{1}{x} \right]_{-1}^1 = -1 - (1) = -2$ is catastrophically false because the integral diverges to $+\infty$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check if the denominator vanishes or if the function has vertical asymptotes inside $[a, b]$. If so, split the integral and test for convergence.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Modulus Function Blind Splitting

Common Pitfall: Integrating $|f(x)|$ without breaking the interval at the critical points where $f(x) = 0$.

Counterexample: $$\int_{-2}^{2} |x|\,dx$$

Why it fails: Failing to split into $\int_{-2}^{0} (-x)\,dx + \int_{0}^{2} x\,dx$ leads to sign errors.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always locate roots of the expression inside absolute value symbols within the integration domain before applying integration formulas.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Evaluate the definite integral $I = \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin x}{\sin x + \cos x} \, dx$.

(A) $\frac{\pi}{4}$(B) $\frac{\pi}{2}$(C) $\pi$(D) $0$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Let $I = \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin x}{\sin x + \cos x} \, dx$. Using King's property $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \int_a^b f(a+b-x)\,dx$, we get $I = \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}-x)}{\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}-x) + \cos(\frac{\pi}{2}-x)} \, dx = \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\cos x}{\cos x + \sin x} \, dx$. Adding both equations: $2I = \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin x + \cos x}{\sin x + \cos x} \, dx = \int_0^{\pi/2} 1\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}$. Hence, $I = \frac{\pi}{4}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Standard King's property result for $\int_a^b \frac{f(x)}{f(x)+f(a+b-x)}\,dx$ is always $\frac{b-a}{2}$. Here $\frac{\pi/2 - 0}{2} = \frac{\pi}{4}$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Recognize this template instantly in UP TGT/PGT to write the answer in 2 seconds.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Evaluate the value of $\int_{-1}^{1} x^5 \cos^4 x \, dx$.

(A) $2$(B) $\frac{\pi}{2}$(C) $0$(D) $1$
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Let $f(x) = x^5 \cos^4 x$. Then $f(-x) = (-x)^5 \cos^4(-x) = -x^5 \cos^4 x = -f(x)$. Thus, the integrand is an odd function. Since the integration limits are symmetric $[-1, 1]$, the integral is identically $0$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Check power of $x$: odd power ($5$) multiplied by even power function ($\cos^4 x$) yields an odd function over symmetric limits $\Rightarrow$ Answer is $0$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Do not attempt integration by parts. Check parity first whenever limits are symmetric.

Q1
MCQ 1: The value of $\int_0^{\pi} x \sin^2 x \, dx$ is:
A (A) $\frac{\pi^2}{4}$
B (B) $\frac{\pi^2}{2}$
C (C) $\frac{\pi}{4}$
D (D) $\pi^2$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using King's property, $I = \int_0^{\pi} (\pi - x)\sin^2(\pi-x)\,dx = \pi \int_0^{\pi} \sin^2 x \, dx - I \implies 2I = \pi \int_0^{\pi} \sin^2 x \, dx = \pi \left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi^2}{2}$. Thus $I = \frac{\pi^2}{4}$.

Q2
MCQ 2: Evaluate $\int_{-1}^{1} |x| \, dx$.
A (A) $0$
B (B) $1$
C (C) $2$
D (D) $\frac{1}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $|x|$ is an even function on $[-1, 1]$, $\int_{-1}^{1} |x|\,dx = 2 \int_0^1 x\,dx = 2 \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_0^1 = 1$.

Q3
MCQ 3: The value of $\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^6 x \, dx$ is:
A (A) $\frac{5\pi}{32}$
B (B) $\frac{5\pi}{16}$
C (C) $\frac{3\pi}{16}$
D (D) $\frac{\pi}{32}$
Derivation & Analysis:

By Wallis's formula: $\frac{5 \times 3 \times 1}{6 \times 4 \times 2} \times \frac{\pi}{2} = \frac{15}{48} \times \frac{\pi}{2} = \frac{5}{16} \times \frac{\pi}{2} = \frac{5\pi}{32}$.

Q4
MCQ 4: What is the value of $\int_0^{\pi/2} \log(\tan x) \, dx$?
A (A) $\pi$
B (B) $\frac{\pi}{2}$
C (C) $1$
D (D) $0$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let $I = \int_0^{\pi/2} \log(\tan x)\,dx$. Using $\int_0^a f(x)dx = \int_0^a f(a-x)dx$, $I = \int_0^{\pi/2} \log(\cot x)\,dx = -\int_0^{\pi/2} \log(\tan x)\,dx = -I$. Therefore, $2I = 0 \implies I = 0$.

Q5
MCQ 5: The value of $\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{1 + x^2}$ is:
A (A) $\frac{\pi}{2}$
B (B) $\frac{\pi}{4}$
C (C) $\frac{\pi}{3}$
D (D) $\frac{\pi}{6}$
Derivation & Analysis:

$$\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{1+x^2} = [\tan^{-1} x]_0^1 = \tan^{-1}(1) - \tan^{-1}(0) = \frac{\pi}{4} - 0 = \frac{\pi}{4}$$.

Q6
MCQ 6: If $f(x)$ is a continuous function such that $f(a+b-x) = f(x)$, then $\int_a^b x f(x) \, dx$ is equal to:
A (A) $\frac{a+b}{2} \int_a^b f(x) \, dx$
B (B) $(b-a) \int_a^b f(x) \, dx$
C (C) $0$
D (D) $\int_a^b f(x) \, dx$
Derivation & Analysis:

Standard property of definite integrals: $\int_a^b x f(x)\,dx = \frac{a+b}{2} \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ whenever $f(a+b-x) = f(x)$.

Q7
MCQ 7: The value of $\int_{-a}^{a} \sin^7 x \, dx$ is:
A (A) $2$
B (B) $\pi$
C (C) $0$
D (D) $1$
Derivation & Analysis:

The integrand $\sin^7 x$ is an odd function because $\sin^7(-x) = -\sin^7 x$. Since the interval $[-a, a]$ is symmetric, the integral is $0$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Evaluate $\int_0^1 x e^x \, dx$.
A (A) $1$
B (B) $e$
C (C) $0$
D (D) $e-1$
Derivation & Analysis:

Integration by parts: $\int_0^1 x e^x dx = [x e^x]_0^1 - \int_0^1 e^x dx = (1\cdot e^1 - 0) - [e^x]_0^1 = e - (e - 1) = 1$.

Q9
MCQ 9: The value of $\int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\cos x - \sin x}{1 + \sin x \cos x} \, dx$ is:
A (A) $1$
B (B) $\frac{\pi}{2}$
C (C) $\pi$
D (D) $0$
Derivation & Analysis:

Substitute $t = \sin x + \cos x$, or apply symmetry properties observing that the numerator is the derivative of the denominator structure or anti-symmetric under $x \to \pi/2 - x$, evaluating directly to $0$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $I_n = \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^n x \, dx$, then the recurrence relation is:
A (A) $I_n = \frac{n-1}{n} I_{n-2}$
B (B) $I_n = \frac{n}{n-1} I_{n-2}$
C (C) $I_n = n I_{n-1}$
D (D) $I_n = \frac{n-1}{n+1} I_{n-2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Standard reduction formula for powers of sine is $I_n = \frac{n-1}{n} I_{n-2}$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Use King's Property ($\int_a^b f(x)dx = \int_a^b f(a+b-x)dx$) whenever the denominator is symmetric under the transformation $x \to a+b-x$, such as trigonometric sums involving $\sin(x)$ and $\cos(x)$, or logarithmic expressions.

Failing to change the limits of integration when substituting a new variable. Always convert lower and upper limits using $t = g(x)$ rather than substituting back to $x$ at the end.

Definite integrals and their properties (King's property, parity, inequalities) contribute 3 to 5 direct questions in UP PGT and TGT examinations, making them essential for securing a top rank.

Memorize standard results like $\int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin x}{\sin x + \cos x}dx = \frac{\pi}{4}$, Wallis formula coefficients, and quick parity checks for symmetric intervals to solve problems in under 30 seconds.
