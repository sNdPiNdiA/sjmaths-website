3. Ordinary Differential Equations

Order of differential equations

The order of an ordinary differential equation is defined strictly as the order of the highest-order derivative appearing in the differential equation, provided the equation can be expressed as a polynomial in its derivatives. This foundational concept forms the starting classification gate for all differential equations in UP PGT/TGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Definition of the Order of an Ordinary Differential Equation
Let an ordinary differential equation be given in a form where it is algebraic (or can be made rational and integral) in terms of its derivatives. The order of the differential equation is the exact positive integer $n$ corresponding to the highest-order derivative $\frac{d^n y}{dx^n}$ or $y^{(n)}$ present in the relation $$F\left(x, y, \frac{dy}{dx}, \frac{d^2 y}{dx^2}, \dots, \frac{d^n y}{dx^n}\right) = 0$$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, the order $n$ of a differential equation dictates the dimension of the family of curves represented by its general solution, specifically requiring $n$ independent arbitrary constants of integration. For instance, a first-order ODE represents a single-parameter family of tangent slopes, while an order $n$ ODE represents an $n$-parameter family of curves.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, determining the order requires complete freedom from radical signs and fractional powers affecting any derivative term. The expression must be cleared of radicals and rationalized with respect to all derivative operators before the order is legitimately read.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Radical Elimination Rule

$$ \left[ \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} \right]^{p/q} = f(x, y, y') \implies \left[ \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} \right]^p = [f(x, y, y')]^q $$

Exam Rule: Raise both sides to the power of the denominator of any fractional exponent on the highest-order derivative *before* declaring the order and degree.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently tested in UP PGT/TGT where equations are disguised with fractional powers on $y''$ or $y'''$.
02

Shortcut 2: Transcendental Argument Inspection

$$ \sin\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right) = x \implies \text{Order} = 1, \text{Degree = Not Defined} $$

Exam Rule: If a derivative is trapped inside a non-algebraic function (sin, cos, log, exp), the order remains validly equal to the highest derivative present, but the degree is strictly undefined.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard trap question in DSSSB and KVS PGT examinations.
03

Shortcut 3: Sum of Orders in Coupled Systems

$$ \max\left( \text{Order of } x(t), \text{Order of } y(t) \right) $$

Exam Rule: For simultaneous differential equations, the total order of the system is the order of the highest derivative present across all constituent equations.

⚡ PYQ Context: Asked in elite tier PGT mathematics papers to test depth of understanding.
04

Shortcut 4: Implicit Derivative Expansion

$$ \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2} = k \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} $$

Exam Rule: Square both sides to eliminate the radical radicalizing the first derivative, ensuring all derivative powers are positive integers.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard curve-radius and curvature-based differential equations in UP LT Grade exams.
05

Shortcut 5: Direct Inspection of Constants

$$ y = c_1 e^x + c_2 e^{2x} \implies \text{Order} = 2 $$

Exam Rule: The order of the differential equation obtained by eliminating $n$ independent arbitrary constants from a relation is always equal to $n$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Extremely high frequency in UP TGT/PGT matching equations with arbitrary constants.
06

Shortcut 6: Elimination of Redundant Parameters

$$ y = a \cos(x + b) \implies y'' + y = 0 \quad (\text{Order } 2) $$

Exam Rule: Count the number of *essential* (independent) arbitrary constants. If constants can be combined (e.g., $c_1 + c_2$), simplify first before counting order.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct scoring questions in all state board teacher recruitment exams.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Order with Degree

Common Pitfall: Candidates mix up the highest derivative (order) with the exponent of that highest derivative after rationalization (degree).

Counterexample: $$\left(\frac{d^3y}{dx^3}\vphantom{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^2 + \frac{dy}{dx} = 0$$

Why it fails: Order is 3 (highest derivative is third), Degree is 2 (power of the highest derivative). Candidates frequently swap these numbers.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Order = How many times you differentiate. Degree = Power of the heaviest derivative box.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Ignoring Transcendental Traps on Degree

Common Pitfall: Stating the degree of an equation where a derivative is inside a Taylor expansion or transcendental function.

Counterexample: $$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + \sin\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right) = 0$$

Why it fails: Order is clearly 2, but because $\sin(y')$ expands into an infinite polynomial in $y'$, the degree is not defined.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check if any derivative argument is non-polynomial (sin, cos, log, exp). If yes, degree is undefined; order remains completely unaffected.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the order and degree of the differential equation: $$\left[1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2\right]^{3/2} = k \frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$$

(A) Order = 2, Degree = 1(B) Order = 2, Degree = 2(C) Order = 1, Degree = 3(D) Order = 2, Degree = 3
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Square both sides to eliminate the fractional power $3/2$. We get $\left[1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2\right]^3 = k^2 \left(\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right)^2$. Step 2: Identify the highest derivative, which is $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ (Order = 2). Step 3: Note its exponent after squaring, which is 2 (Degree = 2).
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Square the fractional power $3/2 \to$ power becomes 3 on LHS, power becomes 2 on RHS derivative $y''$. Thus Order = 2, Degree = 2.
💡 Key Takeaway: Do not read the degree as 3 directly from the fractional power before squaring!
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

What is the order of the differential equation satisfied by all circles passing through the origin and having their centres on the x-axis?

(A) 1(B) 2(C) 3(D) 4
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write equation of circle passing through origin with centre $(a, 0)$: $(x-a)^2 + y^2 = a^2 \implies x^2 - 2ax + y^2 = 0$. Step 2: There is exactly 1 essential arbitrary constant ($a$). Wait, let's check: $x^2 + y^2 - 2ax = 0 \implies 2x + 2yy' - 2a = 0 \implies a = x + yy'$. Substitute $a$ back: $x^2 + y^2 - 2x(x + yy') = 0$. Step 3: This elimination involves 1 parameter, so order is 1? Let's re-verify: Equation is $(x-a)^2 + y^2 = a^2$. The center is on the x-axis, so radius is $|a|$. There is 1 parameter $a$. Hence order is 1.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Count essential parameters. A circle with fixed centre locus (on x-axis) has 1 parameter $a$. Order of resulting ODE is 1.
💡 Key Takeaway: Be extremely careful with geometric families. Here centre is on x-axis, so coordinates are $(a, 0)$, radius is $a$. Only one parameter $a$ exists.

Q1
What is the order of the differential equation $y = x \frac{dy}{dx} + \sqrt{a^2 \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 + b^2}$?
A 1
B 2
C 3
D Not defined
Derivation & Analysis:

Clearing the radical gives a polynomial expression in derivatives where the highest derivative is $\frac{dy}{dx}$. Thus, the order is 1.

Q2
The order of the differential equation representing the family of curves $y = c_1 e^{2x} + c_2 e^{-2x}$ is:
A 1
B 2
C 3
D 4
Derivation & Analysis:

There are 2 independent arbitrary constants ($c_1, c_2$). Eliminating them requires differentiating twice, yielding a second-order differential equation.

Q3
What is the degree of the differential equation $\frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} + \cos\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right) = 0$?
A 1
B 2
C 0
D Not defined
Derivation & Analysis:

The derivative $\frac{dy}{dx}$ is inside a transcendental cosine function, making it impossible to express the ODE as a finite polynomial in its derivatives. Hence, degree is not defined (though order is 2).

Q4
Find the order of $x \frac{d^3y}{dx^3} + \left(\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right)^4 - y = 0$.
A 1
B 2
C 3
D 4
Derivation & Analysis:

The highest-order derivative present in the equation is $\frac{d^3y}{dx^3}$. Therefore, the order is 3.

Q5
The order of the differential equation of all parabolas whose axis is parallel to the y-axis is:
A 1
B 2
C 3
D 4
Derivation & Analysis:

General equation of parabola with axis parallel to y-axis is $y = ax^2 + bx + c$, which contains 3 arbitrary constants ($a, b, c$). Differentiating 3 times eliminates all constants, giving an ODE of order 3? Wait! Standard equation has 3 parameters, so order is 3.

Q6
What is the order of $\left(\frac{d^3y}{dx^3}\right)^{2/3} - 3\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 5\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$?
A 1
B 2
C 3
D Not defined
Derivation & Analysis:

Cube both sides to clear the fractional exponent $2/3$. The highest derivative remains $\frac{d^3y}{dx^3}$, so the order is 3.

Q7
If $y = A \sin x + B \cos x$, the corresponding differential equation is:
A $y'' + y = 0$
B $y'' - y = 0$
C $y' + y = 0$
D $y'' + yy' = 0$
Derivation & Analysis:

Differentiating twice eliminates $A$ and $B$: $y' = A\cos x - B\sin x$, $y'' = -A\sin x - B\cos x = -y \implies y'' + y = 0$ (Order 2).

Q8
The order of the differential equation of the family of circles touching the x-axis at the origin is:
A 1
B 2
C 3
D 4
Derivation & Analysis:

Equation of circles touching x-axis at origin is $x^2 + y^2 - 2ay = 0$ (1 parameter $a$). Wait, centre is $(0, a)$, radius $a$. Equation: $x^2 + (y-a)^2 = a^2 \implies x^2 + y^2 - 2ay = 0$. One parameter $a$, so order is 1? Let's check: $2x + 2yy' - 2ay' = 0 \implies a = \frac{x + yy'}{y'}$. Substituting gives an ODE of order 1.

Q9
Which of the following differential equations has order 2 and degree 1?
A $\frac{dy}{dx} + \left(\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right)^2 = x$
B $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + x\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^3 = y$
C $\left(\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 = 0$
D $y = x y' + (y'')^3$
Derivation & Analysis:

In option B, the highest derivative is $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ with exponent 1, and no fractional powers or transcendental traps exist. Hence Order = 2, Degree = 1.

Q10
The number of arbitrary constants in the general solution of a differential equation of fourth order is:
A 2
B 3
C 4
D Infinite
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, the general solution of an ordinary differential equation of order $n$ contains exactly $n$ independent arbitrary constants.
