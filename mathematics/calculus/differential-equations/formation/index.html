3. Ordinary Differential Equations

Formation of differential equations

The formation of a differential equation involves eliminating arbitrary constants from a given relation representing a family of curves, resulting in an equation whose order equals the number of independent constants. Mastering this technique is vital for UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations as it forms the foundational bridge between algebraic geometry and calculus.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Principle of Elimination of Arbitrary Constants
Let $f(x, y, c_1, c_2, \dots, c_n) = 0$ be an equation representing a family of curves containing $n$ independent arbitrary constants. By differentiating the relation $n$ times successively with respect to $x$ and eliminating the $n$ parameters $c_1, c_2, \dots, c_n$ among the $n+1$ resulting equations, we obtain an ordinary differential equation of order $n$: $$F\left(x, y, \frac{dy}{dx}, \frac{d^2y}{dx^2}, \dots, \frac{d^n y}{dx^n}\right) = 0$$

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, an equation with $n$ parameters defines an $n$-parameter family of curves (e.g., all circles of fixed radius, or all parabolas with a common vertex). Differentiating and eliminating constants constructs a differential equation whose slope field or curvature at every point is satisfied by every member of the geometric family.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, the number of independent arbitrary constants dictates the exact order of the resulting differential equation. Eliminating constants reduces the transcendence or parameter dependence of the function down to a differential operator relation.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Single Parameter Family (Lines/Circles)

$$y = mx + c \implies \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 0$$

Exam Rule: For a family of straight lines involving two parameters ($m$ and $c$), the differential equation is always of order 2: $d^2y/dx^2 = 0$.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
02

Shortcut 2: Circle Family with Center on Axis

$$(x-h)^2 + y^2 = r^2 \implies \text{Order } 2$$

Exam Rule: Count the number of essential independent constants. If center $(h,k)$ is on the x-axis, $k=0$, leaving only $h$ and $r$ (2 constants), yielding a 2nd order DE.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard UP PGT geometric family question format.
03

Shortcut 3: Exponential Growth/Decay Family

$$y = Ae^{mx} + Be^{nx} \implies \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - (m+n)\frac{dy}{dx} + mn y = 0$$

Exam Rule: For linear combinations of exponentials, the differential equation corresponds directly to the auxiliary equation roots $m$ and $n$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct result tested in DSSSB and KVS examinations.
04

Shortcut 4: Product Form / Logarithmic Separation

$$y = c e^{f(x)} \implies \frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx} = f'(x)$$

Exam Rule: When a single constant is multiplicative with an exponential, take the natural logarithm or differentiate once to immediately eliminate $c$ without simultaneous equations.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves 45 seconds on exponential curve problems.
05

Shortcut 5: Trigonometric Phase Families

$$y = A \cos(x + \theta) \implies \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + y = 0$$

Exam Rule: Two parameters ($A$ and $\theta$) in a simple harmonic motion equation always yield a 2nd order differential equation free of parameters.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard physics-math overlap question in PGT exams.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Option Elimination via Order Check

$$n = \text{Number of essential constants}$$

Exam Rule: Always count the exact number of independent arbitrary constants first. Immediately eliminate any option whose order does not match $n$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial time management strategy for 125 questions in UP PGT.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Redundant or Combined Constants

Common Pitfall: Counting $c_1 + c_2$ or $c_1 c_2$ as two independent constants instead of simplifying them into a single parameter before counting.

Counterexample: $y = c_1 e^{x + c_2} = (c_1 e^{c_2})e^x = c e^x$

Why it fails: Though written with two constants, it simplifies to only ONE essential constant. Treating it as 2nd order results in an incorrect differential equation.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always simplify algebraic expressions and combine constants to find the true number of independent parameters before differentiating.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Mistaking Degree for Order

Common Pitfall: Confusing the number of arbitrary constants with the degree of the differential equation rather than its order.

Counterexample: $y = cx + c^2$

Why it fails: Has 1 constant ($c$), so order is 1, but eliminating $c$ produces a quadratic term in $dy/dx$, making its degree 2.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Number of constants = Order of DE. Degree is the highest power of the highest derivative after rationalization.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the differential equation of all circles passing through the origin and whose centers lie on the x-axis.

(A) $(x^2 - y^2)\frac{dy}{dx} = 2xy$(B) $(x^2 + y^2)\frac{dy}{dx} = 2xy$(C) $(x^2 - y^2)\frac{dy}{dx} = xy$(D) $(x^2 + y^2) = 2xy \frac{dy}{dx}
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Equation of circle with center $(a, 0)$ and radius $a$ passing through origin is $(x-a)^2 + y^2 = a^2 \implies x^2 - 2ax + y^2 = 0$. Step 2: Differentiate with respect to $x$: $2x - 2a + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \implies a = x + y\frac{dy}{dx}$. Step 3: Substitute $a$ back into the original equation to eliminate $a$. Step 4: Simplify to get $(x^2 - y^2)\frac{dy}{dx} = 2xy$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Observe that parameter $a$ appears linearly. Isolate $2a = \frac{x^2+y^2}{x}$ and substitute derivative directly.
💡 Key Takeaway: Standard UP PGT repeat question. Memorize the resulting differential equation.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

The differential equation corresponding to $y = c_1 e^{2x} + c_2 e^{-3x}$ is:

(A) $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + \frac{dy}{dx} - 6y = 0$(B) $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - \frac{dy}{dx} - 6y = 0$(C) $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 5\frac{dy}{dx} + 6y = 0$(D) $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 5\frac{dy}{dx} + 6y = 0$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Here roots of auxiliary equation are $m_1 = 2$ and $m_2 = -3$. Step 2: Sum of roots $= 2 + (-3) = -1$; Product of roots $= (2)(-3) = -6$. Step 3: Form equation $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - (\text{sum})\frac{dy}{dx} + (\text{prod})y = 0 \implies \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + \frac{dy}{dx} - 6y = 0$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Use the quadratic operator relation $(D - m_1)(D - m_2)y = 0$ where $D = \frac{d}{dx}$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Direct quadratic auxiliary formula saves 1 minute.

Q1
MCQ 1: The order of the differential equation of all conics centered at the origin is:
A 1
B 2
C 3
D 4
Derivation & Analysis:

General equation of conics centered at origin involves quadratic forms with coefficients that reduce to 2 independent parameters after normalization.

Q2
MCQ 2: What is the differential equation representing the family of curves $y = a \sin(x + b)$?
A $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + y = 0$
B $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - y = 0$
C $\frac{dy}{dx} + y = 0$
D $\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 + y^2 = a^2$
Derivation & Analysis:

Two arbitrary constants ($a$ and $b$). Differentiating twice gives $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = -a\sin(x+b) = -y \implies \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + y = 0$.

Q3
MCQ 3: The differential equation of the family of lines passing through the origin is:
A x \frac{dy}{dx} - y = 0
B x \frac{dy}{dx} + y = 0
C \frac{dy}{dx} + y = 0
D \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 0
Derivation & Analysis:

Equation is $y = mx$. Differentiating gives $\frac{dy}{dx} = m$. Eliminating $m$ gives $y = x\frac{dy}{dx} \implies x\frac{dy}{dx} - y = 0$.

Q4
MCQ 4: The number of arbitrary constants in the general solution of a differential equation of order $n$ is:
A n - 1
B n
C n + 1
D Independent of n
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, the general solution of an ordinary differential equation of order $n$ contains exactly $n$ independent arbitrary constants.

Q5
MCQ 5: The differential equation formed by eliminating $a$ and $b$ from $y = e^x(a \cos x + b \sin x)$ is:
A \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 2\frac{dy}{dx} + 2y = 0
B \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 2\frac{dy}{dx} + 2y = 0
C \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - y = 0
D \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + y = 0
Derivation & Analysis:

Using operator methods or double differentiation for $e^{ax}(c_1\cos bx + c_2\sin bx)$, the auxiliary equation has roots $1 \pm i$, leading to $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 2\frac{dy}{dx} + 2y = 0$.

Q6
MCQ 6: The differential equation of the family of parabolas with vertex at $(0,0)$ and axis along the y-axis is:
A x \frac{dy}{dx} - 2y = 0
B 2x \frac{dy}{dx} - y = 0
C x \frac{dy}{dx} - y = 0
D \frac{dy}{dx} - 2xy = 0
Derivation & Analysis:

Equation is $x^2 = 4ay$. Differentiating once: $2x = 4a \frac{dy}{dx}$. Dividing gives $\frac{x^2}{2x} = \frac{4ay}{4a\frac{dy}{dx}} \implies \frac{x}{2} = \frac{y}{dy/dx} \implies x\frac{dy}{dx} - 2y = 0$.

Q7
MCQ 7: If $y = c_1 e^{3x} + c_2 e^{-3x}$, the corresponding differential equation is:
A \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 9y = 0
B \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 9y = 0
C \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 3y = 0
D \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 3y = 0
Derivation & Analysis:

Roots are $3$ and $-3$. Sum = $0$, Product = $-9$. Thus $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 9y = 0$.

Q8
MCQ 8: The differential equation of all non-vertical lines in the Cartesian plane is of order:
A 1
B 2
C 3
D Infinite
Derivation & Analysis:

Non-vertical lines are given by $y = mx + c$, which has 2 independent constants ($m$ and $c$), hence order 2.

Q9
MCQ 9: Eliminate $c$ from $xy = c^2$ to form the differential equation:
A x \frac{dy}{dx} + y = 0
B x \frac{dy}{dx} - y = 0
C x \frac{dy}{dx} + 2y = 0
D \frac{dy}{dx} + xy = 0
Derivation & Analysis:

Differentiating $xy = c^2$ with respect to $x$: $x\frac{dy}{dx} + y = 0$, since derivative of $c^2$ is 0.

Q10
MCQ 10: The differential equation of the family of circles touching the y-axis at the origin is:
A (x^2 - y^2)\frac{dy}{dx} - 2xy = 0
B (x^2 + y^2)\frac{dy}{dx} - 2xy = 0
C (x^2 - y^2) + 2xy\frac{dy}{dx} = 0
D x^2 + y^2 - 2xy\frac{dy}{dx} = 0
Derivation & Analysis:

Center is $(a, 0)$ and passes through origin with radius $a$, equation is $x^2 + y^2 - 2ax = 0$. Differentiating and eliminating $a$ yields $(x^2 - y^2)\frac{dy}{dx} - 2xy = 0$.
