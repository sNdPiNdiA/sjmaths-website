Harmonic progression

A Harmonic Progression (H.P.) is a sequence of non-zero real numbers whose terms have their reciprocals in Arithmetic Progression (A.P.). Because direct algebraic manipulations of H.P. are cumbersome, mastery of the reciprocal bridge to A.P. and standard mean relations forms a high-scoring, recurring zone in UP TGT/PGT, DSSSB, and KVS examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Principle of Harmonic Progression and Harmonic Mean
A sequence $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots, a_n$ of non-zero real numbers is said to form a harmonic progression if the sequence of reciprocals $\frac{1}{a_1}, \frac{1}{a_2}, \frac{1}{a_3}, \dots, \frac{1}{a_n}$ forms an arithmetic progression. Consequently, the general term is given by $a_n = \frac{1}{a_1 + (n-1)d'}$, where $d'$ is the common difference of the corresponding A.P.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Harmonic progressions appear naturally in projective geometry, optics (lens and mirror formulas), and musical acoustics (string lengths), representing points whose cross-ratios are harmonic.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

H.P. provides a fundamental bridge between nonlinear algebraic sequences and linear arithmetic progressions, allowing the reduction of complex fractional equations to standard linear systems.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: General Term Retrieval from Given Terms

$$ \text{If } a_m = x \text{ and } a_n = y \text{ in an H.P., then } a_{mn} = \frac{xy}{nx + (m-n)y} \text{ (depending on standard indexing)} $$

Exam Rule: Translate the given H.P. terms into A.P. terms via reciprocals, find the common difference $d'$, and reconstruct.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT to find specific terms like $a_{10}$ when $a_5$ and $a_7$ are given.
02

Shortcut 2: Harmonic Mean (H) between Two Numbers

$$ H = \frac{2ab}{a+b} $$

Exam Rule: For any two positive numbers $a$ and $b$, the harmonic mean is twice their product divided by their sum.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequent foundational MCQ in UP TGT and DSSSB PGT exams.
03

Shortcut 3: AM, GM, HM Fundamental Chain

$$ G^2 = AH \quad \text{and} \quad A \ge G \ge H $$

Exam Rule: For positive numbers, the geometric mean is the geometric mean of the arithmetic and harmonic means.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used in optimization and inequality problems across KVS and NVS exams.
04

Shortcut 4: Inserting $n$ Harmonic Means

$$ \frac{1}{H_1}, \frac{1}{H_2}, \dots, \frac{1}{H_n} \text{ form an A.P. between } \frac{1}{a} \text{ and } \frac{1}{b} with \, d' = \frac{\frac{1}{b} - \frac{1}{a}}{n+1} $$

Exam Rule: Work entirely in the reciprocal A.P. space, then invert the resulting terms back.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard long-form question converted to 30-second execution.
05

Shortcut 5: Condition for Three Numbers to be in H.P.

$$ a, b, c \text{ are in H.P.} \iff b = \frac{2ac}{a+c} \iff \frac{1}{a}, \frac{1}{b}, \frac{1}{c} \text{ are in A.P.} \iff \frac{a-b}{b-c} = \frac{a}{c} $$

Exam Rule: Instantly cross-multiply or check the A.P. condition on reciprocals to verify options.

⚡ PYQ Context: Eliminates incorrect options in parameter-dependent multiple-choice questions.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Symmetry

$$ \text{If } a, b, c \text{ are in H.P., test with } a = 1, b = \frac{2}{3}, c = \frac{1}{2} $$

Exam Rule: Substitute simple numerical values satisfying the hypothesis into algebraic option expressions.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial time-saving technique for 125-question exams like UP PGT.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Direct Arithmetic Operations on H.P. Terms

Common Pitfall: Candidates attempt to find the common difference by subtracting consecutive terms of the H.P. directly (i.e., computing $a_2 - a_1$).

Counterexample: $1, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{3}$

Why it fails: H.P. terms do not have a constant difference; their reciprocals have a constant difference. Subtracting H.P. terms directly yields variable values.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always invert the sequence to A.P. before applying common difference formulas.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Misapplication of AM-GM-HM Equality Condition

Common Pitfall: Assuming $A = G = H$ holds for distinct numbers.

Counterexample: $a = 2, b = 8$

Why it fails: $A = 5, G = 4, H = \frac{32}{10} = 3.2$. Equality $A = G = H$ holds if and only if $a = b$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check equality constraints strictly; distinct numbers always satisfy strict inequality $A > G > H$.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If the 7th term of an H.P. is $\frac{1}{10}$ and the 12th term is $\frac{1}{25}$, find its 20th term.

(A) $\frac{1}{49}$(B) $\frac{1}{51}$(C) $\frac{1}{45}$(D) $\frac{1}{55}$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Let the corresponding A.P. have first term $A$ and common difference $D'$. Then $A_7 = 10 \implies A + 6D' = 10$. Also $A_{12} = 25 \implies A + 11D' = 25$. Step 2: Subtracting gives $5D' = 15 \implies D' = 3$, and $A = -8$. Step 3: $A_{20} = A + 19D' = -8 + 19(3) = 49$. Step 4: Thus, the 20th term of H.P. is $\frac{1}{49}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Use linear interpolation on indices of the reciprocal A.P.: $D' = \frac{\frac{1}{a_{12}} - \frac{1}{a_7}}{12 - 7} = \frac{25 - 10}{5} = 3$. Then $a_{20}^{-1} = a_7^{-1} + (20-7)D' = 10 + 13(3) = 49$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Quickly jump to A.P. space, solve linear equations in two variables, and invert at the end.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $a, b, c$ are in H.P., then the expression $\frac{1}{b-a} + \frac{1}{b-c}$ is equal to:

(A) $\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{c}$(B) $\frac{2}{b}$(C) $\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{c}$(D) $\frac{1}{a} - \frac{1}{c}$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: $a, b, c$ in H.P. means $b = \frac{2ac}{a+c}$ or $\frac{1}{a}, \frac{1}{b}, \frac{1}{c}$ in A.P., so $\frac{2}{b} = \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{c}$. Step 2: Simplify the expression: $\frac{(b-c) + (b-a)}{(b-a)(b-c)} = \frac{2b - (a+c)}{b^2 - ab - bc + ac}$. Step 3: Substitute $b = \frac{2ac}{a+c}$ to evaluate the numerator and denominator.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Assign values: let $a = 1, b = \frac{2}{3}, c = \frac{1}{2}$ (which form an H.P.). Compute $\frac{1}{\frac{2}{3}-1} + \frac{1}{\frac{2}{3}-\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{1}{-1/3} + \frac{1}{1/6} = -3 + 6 = 3$. Test options with $a=1, c=1/2$: Option (A) $\frac{1}{1} + \frac{1}{1/2} = 1 + 2 = 3$. Matches!
💡 Key Takeaway: Substitution with clean fractions (like $1, \frac{2}{3}, \frac{1}{2}$) saves 2 minutes of algebraic expansion.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
MCQ 1: If $a, b, c$ are in H.P., then $\frac{a}{b+c-a}, \frac{b}{c+a-b}, \frac{c}{a+b-c}$ are in:
A A.P.
B G.P.
C H.P.
D None of these
Derivation & Analysis:

Add 1 to each term or use reciprocal properties. The transformed terms $\frac{b+c}{b+c-a}, \dots$ form an A.P., proving the original sequence is in A.P.

Q2
MCQ 2: The harmonic mean of two numbers is 4, and their arithmetic mean $A$ and geometric mean $G$ satisfy $2A + G^2 = 27$. The numbers are:
A 1 and 4
B 3 and 6
C 2 and 6
D 3 and 12
Derivation & Analysis:

Given $H = 4$ and $G^2 = AH$. Thus $G^2 = 4A$. Substitute into $2A + 4A = 27 \implies 6A = 27 \implies A = 4.5$, so $G = \sqrt{4(4.5)} = \sqrt{18}$ (Wait, check numbers 3 and 6: $A = \frac{9}{2} = 4.5$, $H = \frac{2(3)(6)}{9} = 4$, $G = \sqrt{18}$, $2(4.5) + 18 = 27$. Correct option is 3 and 6).

Q3
MCQ 3: If $p$-th, $q$-th, and $r$-th terms of an H.P. are $x, y, z$ respectively, then the value of $(q-r)yz + (r-p)zx + (p-q)xy$ is:
A 1
B 0
C $xyz$
D None of these
Derivation & Analysis:

Convert to the corresponding A.P. where terms are $\frac{1}{x}, \frac{1}{y}, \frac{1}{z}$. Standard identity yields 0.

Q4
MCQ 4: If $\frac{1}{a}, \frac{1}{b}, \frac{1}{c}$ are in A.P., then $a, b, c$ are in:
A A.P.
B G.P.
C H.P.
D Arithmetico-Geometric Progression
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, if reciprocals of a sequence form an A.P., the sequence itself forms a Harmonic Progression (H.P.).

Q5
MCQ 5: The 4th term of an H.P. is $\frac{3}{5}$ and the 8th term is $\frac{1}{3}$. The 1st term is:
A $1$
B $2$
C $3$
D $5$
Derivation & Analysis:

Reciprocals give $A_4 = \frac{5}{3}$ and $A_8 = 3$. Common difference $D' = \frac{3 - 5/3}{4} = \frac{4/3}{4} = \frac{1}{3}$. Then $A_1 = A_4 - 3D' = \frac{5}{3} - 3(\frac{1}{3}) = \frac{2}{3}$. Thus $a_1 = \frac{3}{2}$... Wait, recalculate: $A_1 = A_4 - 3D' = 5/3 - 1 = 2/3$, so $a_1 = 3/2$ (Option C).

Q6
MCQ 6: If $a, b, c$ are in positive H.P., then which of the following is true?
A $b^2 = ac$
B $b = \frac{a+c}{2}$
C $b = \frac{2ac}{a+c}$
D $a > b > c$
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition of Harmonic Mean, $b$ is the harmonic mean of $a$ and $c$, giving $b = \frac{2ac}{a+c}$.

Q7
MCQ 7: If $H$ is the harmonic mean between $a$ and $b$, then the value of $\frac{H}{a} + \frac{H}{b}$ is:
A 1
B 2
C $\frac{ab}{a+b}$
D $\frac{2ab}{a+b}$
Derivation & Analysis:

$H = \frac{2ab}{a+b}$. Then $\frac{H}{a} + \frac{H}{b} = H(\frac{a+b}{ab}) = \frac{2ab}{a+b} \cdot \frac{a+b}{ab} = 2$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): The sum of an infinite H.P. cannot be determined by elementary algebraic formulas. Reason (R): The terms of an H.P. do not decrease rapidly enough for convergence in simple closed form.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

Unlike G.P., infinite series formed by H.P. terms (like the harmonic series $\sum \frac{1}{n}$) diverge, and general finite or infinite sums lack simple closed-form expressions.

Q9
MCQ 9: If $a, b, c, d$ are in H.P., then $\frac{ab+bc+cd}{ad}$ is equal to:
A $3$
B $2$
C $1$
D $\frac{3}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using standard properties of four terms in H.P., the ratio simplifies to 3.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $a, b, c$ are in A.P., $b, c, d$ are in G.P., and $c, d, e$ are in H.P., then $a, c, e$ are in:
A A.P.
B G.P.
C H.P.
D Arithmetic-Geometric Progression
Derivation & Analysis:

Given $2b = a+c$, $c^2 = bd$, and $d = \frac{2ce}{c+e}$. Substituting $d = \frac{c^2}{b}$ and solving yields $c^2 = ae$, confirming $a, c, e$ are in G.P.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Unlike A.P. and G.P., the sum of the reciprocals forms an A.P., but summing the reciprocals themselves ($\sum \frac{1}{a + (k-1)d'}$) involves harmonic numbers which do not yield a simple elementary closed-form algebraic expression.

Candidates frequently attempt to compute common differences directly using H.P. terms or forget to invert the sequence back to H.P. after performing calculations in the A.P. reciprocal space.

Progressions (A.P., G.P., H.P., and special series) consistently account for 4 to 7 questions out of 125 in UP TGT/PGT, making it a high-yield, direct scoring module.

Master the reciprocal transformation to A.P. immediately upon reading the question, and use standard numerical test values ($1, \frac{2}{3}, \frac{1}{2}$) to verify options without full algebraic expansion.
