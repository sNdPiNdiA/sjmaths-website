1. Algebra — Equations, Progressions and Series

Theory of equations of degree \ge 2

The theory of algebraic equations of degree $\ge 2$ forms the cornerstone of polynomial algebra, governing root distributions, symmetric functions, and transformations. Mastering relations between roots and coefficients, synthetic division, and Sturm's theorem is essential for scoring high in UP TGT/PGT, DSSSB, and KVS examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Fundamental Theorem of Algebra & Vieta's Relations
Every non-zero, single-variable, degree $n$ polynomial with complex coefficients has exactly $n$ complex roots, counted with multiplicity. If $P(x) = a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0 = 0$ ($a_n \neq 0$) has roots $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n$, then the sum of products of roots taken $k$ at a time is given by $\sum_{1 \le i_1 < \dots < i_k \le n} \alpha_{i_1} \dots \alpha_{i_k} = (-1)^k \frac{a_{n-k}}{a_n}$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

A polynomial curve of degree $n$ intersects the real $x$-axis at most $n$ times. Complex roots correspond to local extrema that do not cross the axis, reflecting turning points in the Cartesian plane.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Establishes the algebraic closure of the complex number field $\mathbb{C}$ and links the coefficients directly to elementary symmetric polynomials of the roots.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Degree constraint n \ge 2 Strictly Mandatory / Necessary For $n = 1$, the equation is linear with a single unique root, trivializing symmetric coefficient relations.
Non-zero leading coefficient a_n \neq 0 Strictly Mandatory If $a_n = 0$, the actual degree collapses, invalidating the count of $n$ roots.
Domain of coefficients a_i \in \mathbb{R} \text{ or } \mathbb{C} Essential for root multiplicity Without fixed coefficient fields, complex conjugate root pairings for real polynomials fail.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Sum of Powers of Roots ($S_k$)

$$a_n S_k + a_{n-1} S_{k-1} + \dots + a_{n-k} k = 0 \quad (k \le n)$$

Exam Rule: Use Newton's Sums of Powers recurrence relation to find $\sum \alpha^k$ without calculating individual roots.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT to find sums of squares, cubes, or reciprocal powers of roots.
02

Shortcut 2: Reciprocal Equations

$$x^n P\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = \pm P(x)$$

Exam Rule: For standard reciprocal equations of even degree with positive coefficients, substitute $z = x + \frac{1}{x}$ to drop the degree by half.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in KVS PGT and UP TGT polynomial simplification problems.
03

Shortcut 3: Cardan's Cubic Transformation

$$x^3 + 3Hx + G = 0$$

Exam Rule: To eliminate the second-highest degree term $x^{n-1}$, substitute $x = y - \frac{a_{n-1}}{n a_n}$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard approach for reducing cubic and quartic equations in competitive teaching tests.
04

Shortcut 4: Rational Root Theorem

$$p \text{ divides } a_0, \quad q \text{ divides } a_n$$

Exam Rule: Potential rational roots are restricted to factors of the constant term divided by factors of the leading coefficient.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial for instantly testing options in cubic equations during time-constrained exams.
05

Shortcut 5: Descarte's Rule of Signs

n^+ \le \text{Sign changes in } P(x)

Exam Rule: Count sign changes in coefficients of $P(x)$ for positive roots and $P(-x)$ for negative roots to bound real roots.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used for quick elimination of options regarding the exact number of positive/negative roots.
06

Shortcut 6: Symmetric Root Translation

f(x \pm h) = 0

Exam Rule: To increase or decrease roots by a constant $h$, apply synthetic division successively or replace $x$ by $y \mp h$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Favorite pattern in UP PGT for finding transformed equations.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Ignoring Complex Conjugate Pairing

Common Pitfall: Assuming a polynomial with real coefficients can have an odd number of complex roots.

Counterexample: P(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 4x - 2

Why it fails: Complex roots of polynomials with real coefficients always occur in conjugate pairs; hence, odd-degree real polynomials must have at least one real root.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check degree parity and coefficient reality before concluding root nature.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Misapplying Vieta's Signs

Common Pitfall: Forgetting the alternating sign factor $(-1)^k$ in Vieta's formulas.

Counterexample: x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6 = 0 \implies \alpha\beta\gamma = -(-6)/1 = 6

Why it fails: Dropping the alternating negative sign leads to sign inversions in product sum terms.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Memorize: Coefficient ratio sign starts negative for odd power sums ($a_{n-1}/a_n$) and positive for even power sums ($a_{n-2}/a_n$).

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are the roots of $x^3 - 3x + 2 = 0$, find the value of $\sum \alpha^3 \beta^3$.

(A) 3(B) 6(C) -3(D) 0
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write Vieta's relations: $\sum \alpha = 0$, $\sum \alpha\beta = -3$, $\alpha\beta\gamma = -2$. Step 2: Use identity $\alpha\beta = -2/\gamma$. Step 3: Compute transformed symmetric sum.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Use roots inspection: $x^3 - 3x + 2 = (x-1)^2(x+2) = 0$. Roots are $1, 1, -2$. Then calculate $1^3(1) + 1^3(-2) + (-2)^3(1) = 1 - 2 - 8 = -9$ or apply direct power sums.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always check if polynomial factors by inspection before applying lengthy Newton's sums.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

The condition that the roots of $x^3 + px^2 + qx + r = 0$ are in arithmetic progression is:

(A) $2p^3 - 9pq + 27r = 0$(B) $p^3 - 4pq + 8r = 0$(C) $9pqr = 2p^3 + 27r^3$(D) $p^2 = 3q$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Let roots be $a-d, a, a+d$. Sum of roots gives $3a = -p \implies a = -p/3$. Substitute $x = -p/3$ into equation and apply condition for AP.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Standard derived identity for cubic in AP: $2p^3 - 9pq + 27r = 0$. Memorize standard results for AP, GP, and HP roots.
💡 Key Takeaway: Direct formula recall saves 2 minutes in UP PGT.

Q9
MCQ 9: The condition that the cubic equation $x^3 - px^2 + qx - r = 0$ has its roots in geometric progression is:
A $p^3 r = q^3$
B $q^3 r = p^3$
C $p^3 q = r$
D $r^3 p = q$
Derivation & Analysis:

Let roots be $a/r, a, ar$. Product of roots = $a^3 = r \implies a = r^{1/3}$. Substituting into condition yields $p^3 r = q^3$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are roots of $x^3 - 7x + 6 = 0$, the value of $\sum \alpha^2$ is:
A 7
B 14
C 21
D 0
Derivation & Analysis:

Here $\sum \alpha = 0$ and $\sum \alpha\beta = -7$. Thus $\sum \alpha^2 = (\sum \alpha)^2 - 2\sum \alpha\beta = 0 - 2(-7) = 14$.
