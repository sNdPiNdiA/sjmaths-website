7. Linear Algebra

Transpose of a Linear Transformation

The transpose (or dual) of a linear transformation maps the dual space of the codomain back to the dual space of the domain, preserving algebraic structure and reversing direction. In matrix representations, it corresponds precisely to the matrix transpose, making it a high-yield conceptual scoring zone in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Definition and Existence of the Transpose Transformation
Let $U$ and $V$ be vector spaces over a field $\mathbb{F}$, and let $T: U \to V$ be a linear transformation. The transpose (or dual) of $T$ is the map $T^*: V^* \to U^*$ defined on the dual spaces $V^*$ and $U^*$ such that for every functional $\varphi \in V^*$ and every vector $u \in U$, the action of $T^*(\varphi)$ on $u$ is given by $$(T^*(\varphi))(u) = \varphi(T(u))$$

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, while $T$ moves vectors forward from the domain space $U$ to the codomain space $V$, its transpose $T^*$ pulls linear functionals (hyperplanes or measurements) backward from the dual space $V^*$ to $U^*$. This duality preserves the inner product pairing or contraction evaluation: $\langle \varphi, T(u) \rangle = \langle T^*(\varphi), u \rangle$.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, the transpose map establishes a canonical contravariant functor between vector spaces and their duals. If $T$ is represented by a matrix $A$ relative to ordered bases, then $T^*$ is represented by the exact matrix transpose $A^T$ relative to the corresponding dual bases, intertwining null spaces and ranges via annihilators.

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Matrix Representation Rule

$$[T^*]_{\mathcal{B}^*} = ([T]_\mathcal{B})^T$$

Exam Rule: The matrix of the transpose transformation relative to dual bases is precisely the transpose of the matrix of the original transformation relative to the primal bases.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT and DSSSB PGT to find the matrix of $T^*$ without computing dual basis functionals explicitly.
02

Shortcut 2: Rank Preservation Property

$$\text{rank}(T^*) = \text{rank}(T)$$

Exam Rule: A linear transformation and its transpose always have identical ranks, as row rank equals column rank for matrices.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used to answer quick conceptual questions regarding dimensions of ranges and null spaces in exam papers.
03

Shortcut 3: Nullity and Annihilator Dimension Relation

$$\text{nullity}(T^*) = \dim(V) - \text{range}(T)$$

Exam Rule: The nullity of $T^*$ equals the dimension of the annihilator of the range of $T$, directly related to the corank of $T$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in KVS PGT vector space dimension calculation problems.
04

Shortcut 4: Composition Reversal Rule

$$(S \circ T)^* = T^* \circ S^*$$

Exam Rule: The transpose of a composite linear transformation reverses the order of the maps, identical to matrix transposition $(AB)^T = B^T A^T$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Tests property-based multiple-choice questions in LT Grade and UP TGT exams.
05

Shortcut 5: Inverse Transpose Relation

$$(T^{-1})^* = (T^*)^{-1}$$

Exam Rule: If a linear transformation $T$ is invertible, its transpose is also invertible, and the transpose of the inverse is the inverse of the transpose.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard verification question in competitive teaching tests.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Options Elimination via Dimension Check

$$\text{If } T: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m, \text{ then } T^*: (\mathbb{R}^m)^* \to (\mathbb{R}^n)^*$$

Exam Rule: Always check domain and codomain dimensions. If $T$ goes from dimension $n$ to $m$, $T^*$ goes from $m$ to $n$. Eliminate options reversing this.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves 30 seconds on matching matrix dimension questions.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Domain and Codomain Direction Reversal Confusion

Common Pitfall: Candidates assume that if $T: U \to V$, then $T^*$ also maps from $U$ to $V$ or from $V$ to $U$ without dual spaces.

Counterexample: If $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^3$, then $T^*: (\mathbb{R}^3)^* \to (\mathbb{R}^2)^^*$, not $\mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^2$.

Why it fails: The transpose acts on linear functionals, reversing the direction of mapping between dual spaces.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always remember: Primal goes $U \to V$, Transpose dual goes $V^* \to U^*$.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Assuming Transpose Equals Adjoint in Non-Inner Product Spaces

Common Pitfall: Confusing the algebraic transpose $T^*$ with the Hermitian adjoint $T^\dagger$ or metric adjoint over general vector spaces.

Counterexample: Over vector spaces without a specified inner product, $T^*$ is defined purely algebraically via dual spaces, whereas adjoint requires an inner product structure.

Why it fails: Using inner product properties on spaces that only have a field structure leads to incorrect algebraic conclusions.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check if an inner product is explicitly given; if not, use pure dual-space definition $(T^*\varphi)(u) = \varphi(Tu)$.

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Let $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be the linear transformation defined by $T(x, y) = (x + 2y, 3x - y)$. Find the matrix of the transpose transformation $T^*$ relative to the standard dual bases.

(A) $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$(B) $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$(C) $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 2 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$(D) $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ -3 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write down the standard matrix representation of $T$, which is $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$. Step 2: Use the theorem that the matrix of $T^*$ is the transpose of $A$. Step 3: Compute $A^T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Instantly write the coefficients of $x$ and $y$ as columns for $T$, then transpose rows and columns to get $A^T$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Do not compute dual functionals from scratch; matrix transposition is algebraically equivalent and takes 10 seconds.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $T: V \to W$ is a linear transformation between finite-dimensional vector spaces, which of the following is always true regarding $\text{rank}(T)$ and $\text{rank}(T^*)$?

(A) $\text{rank}(T^*) > \text{rank}(T)$(B) $\text{rank}(T^*) < \text{rank}(T)$(C) $\text{rank}(T^*) = \text{rank}(T)$(D) $\text{rank}(T^*) = \text{nullity}(T)$
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
By definition, the rank of a matrix is equal to the rank of its transpose ($\text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}(A^T)$). Since the matrix of $T^*$ is the transpose of the matrix of $T$, their ranks must be equal.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recall the fundamental matrix property: row rank equals column rank, hence rank of transpose equals rank of original map.
💡 Key Takeaway: undefined

Reversal law for transpose of compositions: $(S \circ T)^* = T^* \circ S^*$, exactly analogous to matrix multiplication $(AB)^T = B^T A^T$.
