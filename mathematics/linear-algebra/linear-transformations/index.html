7. Linear Algebra

Linear transformation

A linear transformation is a structure-preserving map between vector spaces that respects vector addition and scalar multiplication, forming the foundational core of linear algebra. Because they can be uniquely represented by matrices under fixed bases, mastering their rank-nullity properties and matrix representations is essential for scoring high in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Rank-Nullity Theorem (Dimension Theorem)
Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces over a field $\mathbb{F}$, and let $T: V \to W$ be a linear transformation. If $V$ is finite-dimensional, then $\dim(\ker(T)) + \dim(\text{range}(T)) = \dim(V)$, where $\ker(T)$ is the null space and $\text{range}(T)$ (or $\text{image}(T)$) is the range space of $T$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, the theorem states that the dimension of the domain vector space $V$ is partitioned into two parts: the dimension of the subspace that gets collapsed to the zero vector (the nullity) and the dimension of the subspace that is successfully mapped into the codomain (the rank).

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It links the structural dimensions of domain, kernel, and image, allowing us to determine invertibility and injectivity/surjectivity instantly in matrix representations.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Linearity (Additivity and Homogeneity) Entire Domain $V$ Strictly Mandatory / Necessary If $T(x + y) \neq T(x) + T(y)$ or $T(cx) \neq cT(x)$, the mapping is non-linear, and matrix representation theorems or the Rank-Nullity theorem completely fail.
Finite-Dimensionality of Domain Domain Space $V$ Mandatory for the standard Rank-Nullity dimension equality In infinite-dimensional spaces (such as polynomial spaces without degree bounds), dimensions may not be well-defined or finite, requiring functional analytic generalizations.
Vector Space Field Consistency Base Field $\mathbb{F}$ Essential for scalar multiplication consistency If scalars do not belong to the same underlying field, scalar homogeneity fails.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Rank-Nullity Dimension Check

$$\text{Nullity}(T) = \dim(V) - \text{rank}(A)$$

Exam Rule: Always find the rank of the associated matrix $A$ via row reduction or determinant non-vanishing order, then subtract from domain dimension to get nullity instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly asked in UP PGT and KVS PGT to find the dimension of the null space of matrix transformations.
02

Shortcut 2: Matrix of Linear Transformation relative to Standard Bases

$$[T]_{B} = \begin{bmatrix} T(e_1) & T(e_2) & \cdots & T(e_n) \end{bmatrix}$$

Exam Rule: Place the images of the standard basis vectors as the *columns* of the matrix representation.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial for quick matrix construction in DSSSB and UP TGT vector space problems.
03

Shortcut 3: Change of Basis Formula

$$[T]_{B'} = P^{-1} [T]_{B} P$$

Exam Rule: Use the transition matrix $P$ formed by writing new basis elements in terms of the old basis to transform linear operators rapidly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequent in LT Grade and UP PGT examinations for diagonalizability checks.
04

Shortcut 4: Invertibility & Determinant Condition

$$T \text{ is invertible} \iff \det(A) \neq 0 \iff \text{nullity}(T) = 0$$

Exam Rule: An operator on a finite-dimensional space is one-to-one if and only if it is onto, which holds if and only if the matrix determinant is non-zero.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard option-elimination tool in multiple-choice questions.
05

Shortcut 5: Eigenvalues of Polynomial Transformations

$$if \; T(v) = \lambda v, \; \text{then} \; P(T)(v) = P(\lambda)v$$

Exam Rule: To find eigenvalues of a polynomial operator $P(T)$, simply substitute the eigenvalue $\lambda$ into the polynomial expression.

⚡ PYQ Context: High-yield shortcut for advanced PGT questions.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Dimension Exclusion

$$m < n \implies \text{rank} \le m, \; \text{nullity} \ge n - m$$

Exam Rule: For a transformation from $\mathbb{R}^n$ to $\mathbb{R}^m$ where $n > m$, the nullity can never be zero (non-zero kernel always exists, i.e., non-injective).

⚡ PYQ Context: Instantaneous elimination of incorrect options in qualitative theory questions.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Domain and Codomain Dimensions

Common Pitfall: Candidates subtract rank from the codomain dimension instead of the domain dimension in the Rank-Nullity theorem.

Counterexample: $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^5$ with $\text{rank}(T) = 2$. Nullity is $3 - 2 = 1$, NOT $5 - 2 = 3$.

Why it fails: The kernel is a subspace of the domain $V$, so its dimension is bounded by $\dim(V)$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Remember: Dimension of domain = Nullity + Rank. Always start with the domain dimension.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Assuming Homogeneity Implies Linearity

Common Pitfall: Checking only $T(cv) = cT(v)$ or $T(u+v) = T(u) + T(v)$ alone, or testing with zero vector properties.

Counterexample: $T(x, y) = (x^2, y)$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$

Why it fails: $T(cx, cy) = (c^2x^2, cy) \neq c(x^2, y)$ for $c \neq 1$. It fails homogeneity despite passing some scalar checks.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always verify both conditions simultaneously using the combined test: $T(au + bv) = aT(u) + bT(v)$.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Let $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be a linear transformation defined by $T(x, y, z) = (x+y+z, y+z, z)$. Find the nullity of $T$.

(A) 0(B) 1(C) 2(D) 3
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Set $T(x, y, z) = (0, 0, 0)$. Step 2: Solve $x+y+z=0, y+z=0, z=0$. Step 3: Backward substitution gives $z=0, y=0, x=0$. Step 4: $\ker(T) = \{(0,0,0)\}$, so $\dim(\ker(T)) = 0$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Write the matrix of $T$: $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$. Since $\det(A) = 1 \neq 0$, the matrix is non-singular, rank = 3, and nullity = $3 - 3 = 0$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Upper triangular matrices with non-zero diagonal entries have full rank and zero nullity instantly.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ is a linear transformation such as $T(1, 0) = (2, 3)$ and $T(0, 1) = (1, 4)$, then $T(5, 2)$ is equal to:

(A) (12, 23)(B) (11, 23)(C) (10, 20)(D) (12, 19)
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Express $(5, 2)$ as a linear combination of standard basis: $(5, 2) = 5(1, 0) + 2(0, 1)$. By linearity, $T(5, 2) = 5T(1, 0) + 2T(0, 1) = 5(2, 3) + 2(1, 4) = (10+2, 15+8) = (12, 23)$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Form the matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$ and multiply by column vector $\begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$: $\begin{bmatrix} 2(5) + 1(2) \\ 3(5) + 4(2) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 12 \\ 23 \end{bmatrix}$.
💡 Key Takeaway: When standard basis images are given, matrix multiplication is faster than linear combination expansion.

A (A) 5
B (B) 4
C (C) 1
D (D) 9
Derivation & Analysis:

The range of $T$ is a subspace of $W$, so $\dim(\text{range}(T)) \le \dim(W) = 4$. Also, $\dim(\text{range}(T)) \le \dim(V) = 5$. Thus, the maximum possible rank is $\min(5, 4) = 4$.

A (A) 3
B (B) 2
C (C) 1
D (D) 0
Derivation & Analysis:

By the Rank-Nullity Theorem, $\dim(\ker(T)) + \dim(\text{range}(T)) = \dim(\mathbb{R}^3) \implies 2 + \text{rank} = 3 \implies \text{rank} = 1$.

Q4
MCQ 4: Let $T: P_n(\mathbb{R}) \to P_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})$ be the differentiation operator $T(p(x)) = p'(x)$. The nullity of $T$ is:
A (A) 0
B (B) 1
C (C) $n$
D (D) $n-1$
Derivation & Analysis:

The derivative of a polynomial is zero if and only if the polynomial is a constant. The space of constant polynomials has dimension 1, so $\dim(\ker(T)) = 1$.

Q5
MCQ 5: A linear transformation $T: V \to W$ is one-to-one if and only if:
A (A) $\ker(T) = V$
B (B) $\ker(T) = \{0\}$
C (C) $\text{range}(T) = \{0\}$
D (D) $\dim(\text{range}(T)) = 0$
Derivation & Analysis:

A linear transformation is injective (one-to-one) if and only if its kernel contains only the zero vector, i.e., $\ker(T) = \{0}$. This is a fundamental theorem in linear algebra.

Q6
MCQ 6: If $A$ is an $m \times n$ matrix, the rank of $A$ plus the nullity of $A$ equals:
A (A) $m$
B (B) $n$
C (C) $m+n$
D (D) $|m-n|
Derivation & Analysis:

By the Rank-Nullity theorem applied to the matrix transformation $T(x) = Ax$ where $A: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$, the sum of rank and nullity equals the dimension of the domain, which is $n$.

Q7
MCQ 7: Which of the following is NOT a linear transformation?
A (A) Zero transformation
B (B) Identity transformation
C (C) Translation by a non-zero vector $v_0$
D (D) Scaling transformation $T(x) = c x$
Derivation & Analysis:

Translation $T(x) = x + v_0$ where $v_0 \neq 0$ maps the zero vector to $v_0 \neq 0$, failing $T(0) = 0$ which is required for all linear transformations.

Q8
MCQ 8: Let $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be a reflection across the x-axis. Its matrix relative to the standard basis is:
A (A) $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$
B (B) $\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$
C (C) $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$
D (D) $\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Reflection across the x-axis maps $(1, 0) \to (1, 0)$ and $(0, 1) \to (0, -1)$. Placing these as columns gives $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$.

D (D) No relation between dimensions

An isomorphism is bijective, which implies that finite-dimensional vector spaces $V$ and $W$ must have the exact same dimension.

Q10
MCQ 10: Let $T$ be a linear operator on $\mathbb{R}^3$ whose matrix is $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$. The nullity of $T$ is:
A (A) 0
B (B) 1
C (C) 2
D (D) 3
Derivation & Analysis:

The determinant of the diagonal matrix is $2 \times 3 \times 5 = 30 \neq 0$. Thus, $T$ is invertible and full rank ($3$), making its nullity $3 - 3 = 0$.

C (C) $m+n$
D (D) $|m-n|
Derivation & Analysis:

By the Rank-Nullity theorem applied to the matrix transformation $T(x) = Ax$ where $A: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$, the sum of rank and nullity equals the dimension of the domain, which is $n$.

Q7
MCQ 7: Which of the following is NOT a linear transformation?
A (A) Zero transformation
B (B) Identity transformation
C (C) Translation by a non-zero vector $v_0$
D (D) Scaling transformation $T(x) = c x$
Derivation & Analysis:

Translation $T(x) = x + v_0$ where $v_0 \neq 0$ maps the zero vector to $v_0 \neq 0$, failing $T(0) = 0$ which is required for all linear transformations.

Q8
MCQ 8: Let $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be a reflection across the x-axis. Its matrix relative to the standard basis is:
A (A) $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$
B (B) $\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$
C (C) $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$
D (D) $\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Reflection across the x-axis maps $(1, 0) \to (1, 0)$ and $(0, 1) \to (0, -1)$. Placing these as columns gives $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$.

Q9
MCQ 9: If $T: V \to W$ is an isomorphism, then:
A (A) $\dim(V) < \dim(W)$
B (B) $\dim(V) > \dim(W)$
C (C) $\dim(V) = \dim(W)$
D (D) No relation between dimensions
Derivation & Analysis:

An isomorphism is bijective, which implies that finite-dimensional vector spaces $V$ and $W$ must have the exact same dimension.

Q10
MCQ 10: Let $T$ be a linear operator on $\mathbb{R}^3$ whose matrix is $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$. The nullity of $T$ is:
A (A) 0
B (B) 1
C (C) 2
D (D) 3
Derivation & Analysis:

The determinant of the diagonal matrix is $2 \times 3 \times 5 = 30 \neq 0$. Thus, $T$ is invertible and full rank ($3$), making its nullity $3 - 3 = 0$.
