7. Linear Algebra

Kernel of linear transformation

The kernel (or null space) of a linear transformation is the subspace of the domain that maps to the zero vector, serving as a fundamental measure of the transformation's non-injectivity. Mastering the kernel, nullity, and its relation with rank via the Rank-Nullity Theorem is a high-scoring, repeatedly tested conceptual zone in UP PGT/TGT, DSSSB, and KVS examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Definition of Kernel & The Rank-Nullity Theorem (Sylvester's Law of Nullity)
Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces over a field $F$, and let $T: V \to W$ be a linear transformation. The kernel of $T$, denoted as $\ker(T)$ or $\mathcal{N}(T)$, is defined by $\ker(T) = \{v \in V \mid T(v) = 0_W\}$. Furthermore, if $V$ is finite-dimensional, then $\dim(\ker(T)) + \dim(\text{range}(T)) = \dim(V)$, where $\dim(\ker(T))$ is the nullity and $\dim(\text{range}(T))$ is the rank of $T$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, the kernel represents all vectors in the domain space $V$ that are collapsed, squashed, or projected directly onto the origin of the codomain $W$. For instance, a projection from $\mathbb{R}^3$ onto the $xy$-plane has a kernel consisting of the entire $z$-axis.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, $\ker(T)$ is always a subspace of the domain $V$. It dictates the injectivity (one-to-one nature) of $T$: $T$ is injective if and only if $\ker(T) = \{0_V\}$, providing an immediate algebraic test for invertibility when $\dim(V) = \dim(W)$.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Injectivity via Trivial Kernel Test

$$ \ker(T) = \{0_V\} \iff T \text{ is one-to-one (injective)} $$

Exam Rule: To test if a linear operator is injective, set the general transformation expression equal to zero and solve. If only the zero vector emerges as a solution, nullity is $0$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly asked in UP PGT and KVS to determine whether given matrix transformations or differential operators are injective.
02

Shortcut 2: Rank-Nullity Dimension Balancing

$$ \text{Nullity}(T) = \dim(V) - \text{rank}(T) $$

Exam Rule: Whenever asked for nullity of a matrix or transformation, compute the rank (number of non-zero rows in row-echelon form or linearly independent columns) and subtract it from the dimension of the domain.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard scoring question in UP TGT/PGT where a matrix of order $m \times n$ is given and nullity is requested.
03

Shortcut 3: Homogeneous System Equivalence

$$ \ker(T_A) = \{X \in F^n \mid AX = 0} $$

Exam Rule: For a matrix transformation $T_A(X) = AX$, finding the kernel is identical to solving the homogeneous linear system $AX = 0$. The nullity equals the number of free variables (dimension of null space).

⚡ PYQ Context: Appears in almost every exam cycle involving matrices over fields like $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{Z}_p$.
04

Shortcut 4: Differential Operator Kernel (Annihilator Approach)

$$ \text{For } D: P_n(\mathbb{R}) \to P_n(\mathbb{R}), \; \ker(D) = \text{Span}\{1} \implies \text{Nullity} = 1 $$

Exam Rule: For differentiation operators on polynomial spaces, the kernel consists strictly of constant polynomials. The nullity equals the order of the highest derivative annihilated or $1$ for a single derivative.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequent in DSSSB PGT Mathematics questions involving polynomial differential spaces.
05

Shortcut 5: Matrix Nullity via Row Reduction

$$ \text{Nullity}(A) = n - \text{rank}(A) \quad (A \text{ is } m \times n) $$

Exam Rule: Do not find the basis of the kernel unless explicitly asked. Simply reduce $A$ to row echelon form, count non-zero pivot rows to get the rank, and subtract from column count $n$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves 2 minutes per matrix question in UP PGT exams.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Option Elimination via Rank-Nullity Bounds

$$ \max(0, \dim(V) - \dim(W)) \le \text{nullity}(T) \le \dim(V) $$

Exam Rule: Instantly eliminate options where nullity exceeds the dimension of the domain space or violates lower bounds imposed by codomain dimensions.

⚡ PYQ Context: Powerful mental check for theoretical multiple-choice questions.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Range Dimension with Kernel Dimension

Common Pitfall: Candidates calculate the rank (number of pivot columns) and mistakenly report it as the nullity of the transformation.

Counterexample: For a $3 \times 3$ matrix of rank $2$, nullity is $3 - 2 = 1$, NOT $2$.

Why it fails: Forgetting to subtract the rank from the total dimension of the domain vector space $\dim(V)$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always write down $\dim(\ker) + \dim(\text{range}) = \dim(V)$ on your scratchpad before selecting the final numerical option.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Assuming Non-Zero Kernel Implies Non-Linearity

Common Pitfall: Believing that if $\ker(T)$ contains non-zero vectors, the transformation cannot be linear.

Counterexample: The zero transformation $T(v) = 0$ has $\ker(T) = V$, yet it is perfectly linear.

Why it fails: Confusing injectivity with linearity. Linearity requires preservation of vector addition and scalar multiplication, not a zero kernel.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Linearity is a structural property of the mapping rule itself; kernel size only dictates injectivity.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Let $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be a linear transformation defined by $T(x, y, z) = (x + y + z, 2x + y - z, x + 2y + 3z)$. Find the dimension of the kernel of $T$ (nullity of $T$).

(A) 0(B) 1(C) 2(D) 3
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write the matrix $A$ associated with $T$: $$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$$. Step 2: Perform elementary row operations to find the rank. $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$, $R_3 \to R_3 - R_1$: $$A \sim \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & -3 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$$. Step 3: $R_3 \to R_3 + R_2$: $$A \sim \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$$. Step 4: There are 3 non-zero pivot rows, so $\text{rank}(T) = 3$. Step 5: By Rank-Nullity, $\text{Nullity}(T) = \dim(\mathbb{R}^3) - \text{rank}(T) = 3 - 3 = 0$. Wait, recalculate $R_3$: row 3 is $(1, 2, 3)$, minus row 1 $(1, 1, 1)$ gives $(0, 1, 2)$. Adding row 2 $(0, -1, -3)$ gives $(0, 0, -1)$. Let's check determinant: $\det(A) = 1(3 - (-2)) - 1(6 - (-1)) + 1(4 - 1) = 5 - 7 + 3 = 1 \neq 0$. Thus $\text{rank} = 3$, nullity $= 0$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Since the determinant of the transformation matrix is non-zero ($\det A = 1 \neq 0$), the transformation is invertible and non-singular, meaning $\ker(T) = \{0_V\}$ and nullity is instantly $0$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always check the determinant for square matrices before embarking on full row reduction.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Let $T: \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be a linear transformation. If the rank of $T$ is $2$, what is the nullity of $T$?

(A) 1(B) 2(C) 3(D) 4
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Identify the domain dimension $\dim(\mathbb{R}^4) = 4$. Step 2: Apply the Rank-Nullity Theorem: $\dim(\ker T) + \text{rank}(T) = \dim(\text{domain})$. Step 3: $\text{Nullity}(T) + 2 = 4 \implies \text{Nullity}(T) = 2$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Direct subtraction: $4 - 2 = 2$. Takes 5 seconds.
💡 Key Takeaway: Watch out whether the domain or codomain dimension is given in the problem statement.

Q1
If $T: V \to W$ is a linear transformation and $\dim(V) = 5$, $\dim(W) = 7$, what is the maximum possible value for the nullity of $T$?
A (A) 3
B (B) 5
C (C) 7
D (D) 2
Derivation & Analysis:

The kernel of $T$ is a subspace of the domain $V$. Therefore, $\dim(\ker(T)) \le \dim(V) = 5$. The maximum possible nullity is the dimension of the domain, which is $5$.

Q2
Let $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be defined by $T(x, y, z) = (x - y, y - z, z - x)$. The dimension of $\ker(T)$ is:
A (A) 0
B (B) 1
C (C) 2
D (D) 3
Derivation & Analysis:

Set $T(x,y,z) = (0,0,0) \implies x - y = 0, y - z = 0, z - x = 0 \implies x = y = z$. Thus, $\ker(T) = \{(t, t, t) \mid t \in \mathbb{R}\} = \text{Span}\{(1, 1, 1)\}$. The dimension of this subspace is $1$.

Q3
Which of the following conditions is equivalent to a linear transformation $T: V \to W$ being one-to-one?
A (A) $\ker(T) = V$
B (B) $\ker(T) = \{0_V\}$
C (C) $\text{range}(T) = \{0_W\}$
D (D) $\dim(\ker(T)) = \dim(V)$
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, $T(u) = T(v) \implies T(u - v) = 0 \implies u - v \in \ker(T)$. For $T$ to be injective, $u - v = 0 \implies u = v$, which requires $\ker(T) = \{0_V\}$.

Q4
If $A$ is a $4 \times 6$ matrix of rank $3$, what is the nullity of the corresponding linear transformation?
A (A) 1
B (B) 2
C (C) 3
D (D) 4
Derivation & Analysis:

The domain dimension for a $4 \times 6$ matrix transformation is the number of columns, which is $6$. By Rank-Nullity, $\text{Nullity} = 6 - \text{rank}(A) = 6 - 3 = 3$.

Q5
Let $P_n(\mathbb{R})$ be the vector space of polynomials of degree at most $n$. If $D: P_3(\mathbb{R}) \to P_3(\mathbb{R})$ is the differentiation operator $D(p(x)) = p'(x)$, then the nullity of $D$ is:
A (A) 0
B (B) 1
C (C) 2
D (D) 3
Derivation & Analysis:

The kernel of the derivative operator consists of polynomials whose derivative is identically zero, i.e., constant polynomials $p(x) = c$. The space of constants has dimension $1$.

Q6
Let $T: V \to W$ be a linear transformation between two vector spaces of equal finite dimension $\dim(V) = \dim(W) = n$. Then $T$ is onto if and only if:
A (A) $\ker(T) \neq \{0\}$
B (B) $\ker(T) = \{0\}$
C (C) $\text{rank}(T) = 0$
D (D) $\text{nullity}(T) = n$
Derivation & Analysis:

By the Rank-Nullity Theorem, $\text{rank}(T) + \text{nullity}(T) = n$. For $T$ to be onto, $\text{rank}(T) = n$, which forces $\text{nullity}(T) = 0$, meaning $\ker(T) = \{0\}$.

Q7
What is the kernel of the zero transformation $T: V \to W$ defined by $T(v) = 0_W$ for all $v \in V$?
A (A) $\{0_V\}$
B (B) $\emptyset$
C (C) $V$
D (D) $W$
Derivation & Analysis:

Every vector in $V$ is mapped to $0_W$ by definition of the zero transformation. Therefore, $\ker(T) = \{v \in V \mid T(v) = 0_W\} = V$.

Q8
If $T_1: U \to V$ and $T_2: V \to W$ are linear transformations, then for their composition $T_2 \circ T_1$, which of the following holds for their kernels?
A (A) $\ker(T_1) \subseteq \ker(T_2 \circ T_1)$
B (B) $\ker(T_2 \circ T_1) \subseteq \ker(T_1)$
C (C) $\ker(T_1) = \ker(T_2 \circ T_1)$
D (D) $\ker(T_2) \subseteq \ker(T_2 \circ T_1)$
Derivation & Analysis:

If $u \in \ker(T_1)$, then $T_1(u) = 0$. Then $(T_2 \circ T_1)(u) = T_2(T_1(u)) = T_2(0) = 0$, which implies $u \in \ker(T_2 \circ T_1)$. Thus, $\ker(T_1) \subseteq \ker(T_2 \circ T_1)$.

Q9
Let $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be a reflection about the x-axis. What is the dimension of its kernel?
A (A) 0
B (B) 1
C (C) 2
D (D) Undefined
Derivation & Analysis:

Reflection is an invertible linear isomorphism ($T^{-1} = T$). Invertible operators are injective, so their kernel is trivial: $\ker(T) = \{(0,0)\}$ with dimension $0$.

Q10
If the nullity of a linear transformation $T: \mathbb{R}^5 \to \mathbb{R}^4$ is $2$, what is the rank of $T$?
A (A) 1
B (B) 2
C (C) 3
D (D) 4
Derivation & Analysis:

Using the Rank-Nullity Theorem: $\text{rank}(T) + \text{nullity}(T) = \dim(\text{domain}) \implies \text{rank}(T) + 2 = 5 \implies \text{rank}(T) = 3$.
