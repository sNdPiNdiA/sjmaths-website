7. Linear Algebra

Rank of linear transformation

The rank of a linear transformation $T: V \to W$ is defined as the dimension of its range space (or image space), denoted by $\text{rank}(T) = \dim(\text{Im}(T))$. Mastering the rank-nullity theorem, matrix representations, and dimension formulas provides an essential scoring edge in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Rank-Nullity Theorem (Dimension Theorem for Linear Transformations)
Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces over a field $\mathbb{F}$, with $V$ being finite-dimensional. Let $T: V \to W$ be a linear transformation. Then $\dim(V) = \dim(\ker(T)) + \dim(\text{Im}(T))$, which can also be written as $\text{nullity}(T) + \text{rank}(T) = \dim(V)$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, the transformation $V$ collapses a subspace of dimension $\text{nullity}(T)$ to the zero vector in $W$, while the remaining dimensions of $V$ are faithfully mapped to an isomorphic subspace in $W$ of dimension $\text{rank}(T)$.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It bridges abstract vector space dimensions with concrete matrix rank computations, guaranteeing that the column rank equals the row rank of the corresponding matrix representation.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Sylvester's Inequality for Matrix/Transformation Rank

$$\text{rank}(AB) \ge \text{rank}(A) + \text{rank}(B) - n$$

Exam Rule: For composite transformations $S \circ T$, the rank of the composition is bounded below by the sum of individual ranks minus the dimension of the intermediate space.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT and DSSSB PGT when finding minimum possible rank of a product.
02

Shortcut 2: Frobenius Inequality

$$\text{rank}(ABC) \ge \text{rank}(AB) + \text{rank}(BC) - \text{rank}(B)$$

Exam Rule: Direct inequality for triple compositions or matrix chains tested in high-level screening exams.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard PYQ appearance in NVS and KVS PGT Mathematics.
03

Shortcut 3: Rank of Transpose

$$\text{rank}(T^*) = \text{rank}(T)$$

Exam Rule: The rank of the dual/transpose linear transformation equals the rank of the original transformation.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct property question tested in LT Grade exams.
04

Shortcut 4: Dimension Bound by Codomain

$$\text{rank}(T) \le \min(\dim(V), \dim(W))$$

Exam Rule: The rank can never exceed the dimension of the domain or the codomain.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used for instant elimination of impossible options in rank questions.
05

Shortcut 5: Matrix Rank via Echelon Form

$$\text{rank}(T) = \text{Number of non-zero rows in RREF}$$

Exam Rule: Convert the standard matrix representation $[T]_B$ to row echelon form; the non-zero rows give the rank instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard computational question format in all teaching exams.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Nullity

$$\text{rank}(T) = \dim(V) - \text{nullity}(T)$$

Exam Rule: If finding kernel dimension is easier than image dimension, calculate nullity first and subtract from $\dim(V)$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Time management strategy in 125-question exams.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Domain Dimension with Codomain Dimension

Common Pitfall: Candidates subtract nullity from $\dim(W)$ instead of the domain dimension $\dim(V)$.

Counterexample: For $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^4$, $\dim(V) = 2$, so $\text{rank}(T) \le 2$ regardless of codomain dimension 4.

Why it fails: The Rank-Nullity theorem explicitly states $\dim(V) = \text{nullity}(T) + \text{rank}(T)$, where $V$ is the domain.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check whether the domain or codomain dimension is given in the question stem.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Assuming Rank of Product equals Product of Ranks

Common Pitfall: Assuming $\text{rank}(ST) = \text{rank}(S) \cdot \text{rank}(T)$ like scalar multiplication.

Counterexample: Take two nilpotent matrices of rank 1 whose product is the zero matrix of rank 0.

Why it fails: Rank satisfies submultiplicativity: $\text{rank}(ST) \le \min(\text{rank}(S), \text{rank}(T))$, never equality in general.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Never multiply ranks; use Sylvester's inequality instead.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Let $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be a linear transformation defined by $T(x, y, z) = (x+y+z, y+z, z)$. Find the rank of $T$.

(A) 1(B) 2(C) 3(D) 0
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write matrix representation relative to standard basis: $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Step 2: Since $A$ is upper triangular with non-zero diagonal entries, its determinant is non-zero ($\det(A) = 1$). Step 3: Thus $\text{rank}(A) = 3$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
An upper triangular matrix with all diagonal elements non-zero has full rank equal to its order ($n=3$).
💡 Key Takeaway: Look at the matrix representation diagonal entries if triangular.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Let $T: P_2(\mathbb{R}) \to P_3(\mathbb{R})$ be a linear transformation defined by $T(p(x)) = x p(x)$. What is the rank of $T$?

(A) 2(B) 3(C) 4(D) 1
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: $\dim(P_2(\mathbb{R})) = 3$. Step 2: Find the kernel of $T$: $T(p(x)) = x p(x) = 0 \implies p(x) = 0$, so $\ker(T) = \{0}$, meaning $\text{nullity}(T) = 0$. Step 3: By Rank-Nullity theorem, $\text{rank}(T) = \dim(P_2) - \text{nullity}(T) = 3 - 0 = 3$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Multiplication by non-zero polynomial $x$ is injective, so nullity is 0 and rank equals domain dimension.
💡 Key Takeaway: For polynomial derivative or multiplication operators, check injectivity directly to determine nullity.

Q1
MCQ 1: If $T: V \to W$ is a linear transformation and $\dim(V) = 5$, $\dim(W) = 7$, what is the maximum possible rank of $T$?
A 5
B 7
C 2
D 12
Derivation & Analysis:

The rank cannot exceed the dimension of the domain $V$. Thus $\max(\text{rank}(T)) = \min(5, 7) = 5$.

Q2
MCQ 2: Let $T: \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be a linear transformation. What is the minimum possible nullity of $T$?
A 0
B 1
C 3
D 4
Derivation & Analysis:

By Rank-Nullity theorem, $\text{rank}(T) + \text{nullity}(T) = 4$. Since $\text{rank}(T) \le \dim(\mathbb{R}^3) = 3$, we have $\text{nullity}(T) = 4 - \text{rank}(T) \ge 4 - 3 = 1$.

Q3
MCQ 3: The rank of the zero transformation $T: V \to W$ where $\dim(V) = n$ is:
A $n$
B $1$
C $0$
D Undefined
Derivation & Analysis:

The zero transformation maps every vector to the zero vector, so its image space contains only the zero vector, giving rank 0.

Q4
MCQ 4: Let $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be given by $T(x,y,z) = (x+y, y+z, x+z)$. The nullity of $T$ is:
A 0
B 1
C 2
D 3
Derivation & Analysis:

The matrix of $T$ is $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ with determinant $2 \neq 0$. Thus $\text{rank} = 3$ and nullity $= 3 - 3 = 0$.

Q5
MCQ 5: If $S$ and $T$ are two linear transformations on a finite-dimensional space $V$ such that $ST = 0$, then:
A $\text{rank}(S) + \text{rank}(T) \le \dim(V)$
B $\text{rank}(S) = \text{rank}(T)$
C $S = 0$ or $T = 0$
D $\text{nullity}(S) = \text{nullity}(T)$
Derivation & Analysis:

By Sylvester's rank inequality or properties of null spaces, $\text{Im}(T) \subseteq \ker(S)$, which implies $\text{rank}(T) \le \text{nullity}(S) = \dim(V) - \text{rank}(S)$.

Q6
MCQ 6: Let $V$ be the vector space of all $3 \times 3$ skew-symmetric matrices. The dimension of the range of a non-trivial linear transformation on $V$ cannot exceed:
A 9
B 3
C 6
D 1
Derivation & Analysis:

The dimension of the space of $3 \times 3$ skew-symmetric matrices is $\frac{3(2)}{2} = 3$. Hence any rank is bounded by 3.

Q7
MCQ 7: Which of the following cannot be the rank of a $4 \times 4$ real matrix?
A 0
B 2
C 4
D 5
Derivation & Analysis:

The rank of a $4 \times 4$ matrix cannot exceed its dimension 4. Therefore, 5 is impossible.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): If $\text{rank}(T) = \dim(V)$, then $T$ is one-to-one. Reason (R): $\text{nullity}(T) = 0$ when rank equals domain dimension.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation
C (A) is true, (R) is false
D (A) is false, (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

By Rank-Nullity theorem, $\text{rank}(T) + \text{nullity}(T) = \dim(V)$. If $\text{rank}(T) = \dim(V)$, then $\text{nullity}(T) = 0$, meaning $\ker(T) = \{0}$, so $T$ is injective.

Q9
MCQ 9: Let $T: \mathbb{R}^5 \to \mathbb{R}^5$ be a linear transformation with $\text{rank}(T) = 3$. Then the dimension of $\ker(T - I)$ is at most:
A 3
B 5
C 2
D 0
Derivation & Analysis:

The dimension of the eigenspace corresponding to eigenvalue 1 is bounded by the algebraic/geometric constraints, but directly nullity is 2, and rank-nullity principles apply.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $A$ is an $m \times n$ matrix and $\text{rank}(A) = r$, then the dimension of the null space of $A^T$ is:
A $n - r$
B $m - r$
C $r$
D $m + n - r$
Derivation & Analysis:

The null space of $A^T$ is the left null space of $A$, whose dimension is $m - \text{rank}(A) = m - r$.
