7. Linear Algebra

Rank-nullity theorem

The Rank-Nullity Theorem establishes a fundamental structural bridge between the dimension of the domain, the rank (dimension of the image/range), and the nullity (dimension of the kernel) of a linear transformation between finite-dimensional vector spaces. Mastering this identity is a high-scoring prerequisite for clearing competitive examinations like UP PGT/TGT, KVS, and DSSSB Mathematics.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Rank-Nullity Theorem (Sylvester's Law of Nullity)
Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces over a field $F$, and let $T: V \to W$ be a linear transformation. If $V$ is finite-dimensional, then the kernel (null space) $\ker(T)$ and the range (image) $\text{Im}(T)$ are finite-dimensional, and: $$\dim(V) = \dim(\ker(T)) + \dim(\text{Im}(T))$$ or equivalently, $$\text{nullity}(T) + \text{rank}(T) = \dim(V)$$

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, when a linear transformation maps a vector space $V$ to $W$, the domain space $V$ is partitioned into two complementary subspaces: the part that collapses to the zero vector (the null space, whose dimension is the nullity) and the part that is mapped injectively into the codomain (the range, whose dimension is the rank). The theorem shows that the sum of the degrees of freedom lost (nullity) and the degrees of freedom preserved (rank) precisely equals the total dimension of the domain.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, this theorem is the cornerstone for solving homogeneous and non-homogeneous linear equations, studying matrix transformations, determining invertibility, and analyzing the structural properties of linear operators without needing explicit bases.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Matrix Rank-Nullity Dimension Check

$$ \text{Rank}(A) + \text{Nullity}(A) = n \quad \text{for an } m \times n \text{ matrix } A $$

Exam Rule: If you know the rank of a matrix (number of non-zero rows in row echelon form) and the size of the matrix columns $n$, instantly find nullity as $n - \text{rank}$.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
02

Shortcut 2: Invertibility & Full Rank Condition

$$ \text{nullity}(T) = 0 \iff T \text{ is injective (one-to-one)} $$

Exam Rule: For a linear operator $T: V \to V$ where $\dim(V) = n$, $\text{rank}(T) = n \iff \text{nullity}(T) = 0 \iff T$ is invertible (non-singular).

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently used in KVS and NVS to quickly determine if a linear transformation is an isomorphism.
03

Shortcut 3: Dimension Inequality for Composite Maps

$$ \text{rank}(ST) \le \min(\text{rank}(S), \text{rank}(T)) $$

Exam Rule: Use Sylvester's rank inequalities to bound the rank and nullity of composed transformations without matrix multiplication.

⚡ PYQ Context: Asked in DSSSB PGT to eliminate options involving composite linear transformations.
04

Shortcut 4: Homogeneous System Free Variables

$$ \text{Nullity} = \text{Number of variables } (n) - \text{Rank of coefficient matrix } (r) $$

Exam Rule: The dimension of the solution space (null space) of a homogeneous system $AX = 0$ is equal to the number of free variables, which is $n - r$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard question format across all Indian teacher recruitment exams.
05

Shortcut 5: Transformation via Basis Evaluation

$$ \dim(\text{Im}(T)) = \text{Number of linearly independent columns of standard matrix representation} $$

Exam Rule: Do not compute the entire range space; simply construct the standard matrix and find its column rank using elementary row/column operations.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves 2 minutes in UP TGT matrix transformation problems.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Option Elimination via Parity/Divisibility

$$ \text{rank}(A) + \text{nullity}(A) = n $$

Exam Rule: If an option gives a nullity greater than the column dimension $n$ or non-integer rank, eliminate it instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: High-speed tactical elimination for 125-question time-crunch papers.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Domain Dimension with Codomain Dimension

Common Pitfall: Candidates subtract rank from the dimension of the codomain $W$ instead of the domain $V$.

Counterexample: $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^5$

Why it fails: The formula is $\dim(V) = \text{rank} + \text{nullity}$, where $\dim(V) = 2$. Using $\dim(W) = 5$ will result in a completely incorrect nullity calculation.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check the domain space $V$ dimension. Nullity is bounded by $\dim(V)$, not $\dim(W)$.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Assuming Rank Equals Codomain Dimension

Common Pitfall: Assuming $\text{rank}(T) = \dim(W)$ for any arbitrary linear transformation.

Counterexample: Zero transformation $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$, where $T(v) = 0$ $\forall v$

Why it fails: Here, $\text{rank}(T) = 0$ and $\text{nullity}(T) = 3$, even though the codomain dimension is 3. Rank is only the dimension of the *image*, not the codomain.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Remember that $\text{rank}(T) \le \min(\dim(V), \dim(W))$. Equality holds if and only if the map is surjective.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Let $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be a linear transformation defined by $T(x, y, z) = (x + y + z, 2x + y - z, 4x + 3y + z)$. What is the rank of $T$?

(A) $0$(B) $1$(C) $2$(D) $3$
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write the standard matrix representation of $T$: $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & -1 \\ 4 & 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Step 2: Apply elementary row operations to reduce $A$ to row echelon form. $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$, $R_3 \to R_3 - 4R_1$ gives $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & -3 \\ 0 & -1 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$. Step 3: $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$ gives $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. Step 4: The number of non-zero rows is 2, hence $\text{rank}(T) = 2$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Compute the determinant of matrix $A$. $\det(A) = 1(1 - (-3)) - 1(2 - (-4)) + 1(6 - 4) = 4 - 6 + 2 = 0$. Since $\det(A) = 0$, the rank is strictly less than 3. By inspection, the first two rows are linearly independent, so rank is 2.
💡 Key Takeaway: If the determinant of the matrix of a linear operator on $\mathbb{R}^n$ is zero, immediately rule out rank $n$.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Let $V$ be a vector space of all polynomials of degree at most $3$. Let $D: V \to V$ be the differentiation operator $D(p(x)) = p'(x)$. What is the nullity of $D$?

(A) $0$(B) $1$(C) $2$(D) $3$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: The dimension of $V$ (polynomials of degree $\le 3$) is $\dim(V) = 4$ (basis: ${1, x, x^2, x^3}$). Step 2: The kernel of $D$ consists of polynomials $p(x)$ such that $p'(x) = 0$. Step 3: The derivative is zero only for constant polynomials $p(x) = c$. Step 4: The space of constant polynomials is 1-dimensional (basis: ${1}$). Therefore, $\text{nullity}(D) = \dim(\ker(D)) = 1$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Derivative of a polynomial is zero if and only if it is a constant. A constant space is spanned by ${1}$, dimension = 1. Done in 5 seconds.
💡 Key Takeaway: For differential operators, the nullity is always equal to the order of constant terms / polynomials mapped to zero (here, constants have dimension 1).

Q1
If $T: V \to W$ is a linear transformation and $\dim(V) = 7$, $\dim(W) = 5$, what is the minimum possible value of $\text{nullity}(T)$?
A 0
B 2
C 5
D 7
Derivation & Analysis:

By Rank-Nullity Theorem, $\text{rank}(T) + \text{nullity}(T) = 7$. Also, $\text{rank}(T) \le \dim(W) = 5$. Thus, $\text{nullity}(T) = 7 - \text{rank}(T) \ge 7 - 5 = 2$. Minimum nullity is 2.

Q2
Let $A$ be a $4 \times 6$ matrix of rank 3. What is the dimension of the null space of $A$?
A 1
B 2
C 3
D 4
Derivation & Analysis:

Using Sylvester's Rank-Nullity Law for matrices, $\text{rank}(A) + \text{nullity}(A) = n$, where $n$ is the number of columns ($n = 6$). Hence, $3 + \text{nullity}(A) = 6 \implies \text{nullity}(A) = 3$.

Q3
Which of the following statements is ALWAYS true for a linear transformation $T: V \to W$ where $\dim(V) = \dim(W) = n$?
A $T$ is one-to-one $\iff T$ is onto
B $T$ is always invertible
C $\text{rank}(T) = 0$
D $\text{nullity}(T) = n$
Derivation & Analysis:

For finite-dimensional vector spaces of equal dimension, a linear map is injective (one-to-one) if and only if it is surjective (onto), which is equivalent to being bijective (invertible).

Q4
If $T: \mathbb{R}^5 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ is a linear transformation, what is the maximum possible rank of $T$?
A 2
B 3
C 5
D 8
Derivation & Analysis:

The rank of $T$ is bounded by $\min(\dim(V), \dim(W)) = \min(5, 3) = 3$. Therefore, the maximum possible rank is 3.

Q5
Let $W_1$ and $W_2$ be subspaces of a finite-dimensional vector space $V$. If $\dim(W_1) = 3$, $\dim(W_2) = 4$, and $\dim(W_1 \cap W_2) = 1$, what is $\dim(W_1 + W_2)$?
A 5
B 6
C 7
D 8
Derivation & Analysis:

Using the dimension formula for sums of subspaces: $\dim(W_1 + W_2) = \dim(W_1) + \dim(W_2) - \dim(W_1 \cap W_2) = 3 + 4 - 1 = 6$.

Q6
What is the nullity of the zero transformation $T: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$ defined by $T(x) = 0$?
A 0
B 1
C $m$
D $n$
Derivation & Analysis:

The zero transformation maps every vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$ to 0. Thus, $\ker(T) = \mathbb{R}^n$, so its dimension (nullity) is $n$.

Q7
If a square matrix $A$ of order $n$ is invertible, what is its nullity?
A 0
B 1
C $n$
D Depends on $n$
Derivation & Analysis:

An invertible matrix has $\text{rank}(A) = n$. By Rank-Nullity, $\text{nullity}(A) = n - \text{rank}(A) = n - n = 0$.

Q8
Assertion (A): If $\text{rank}(T) = \dim(V)$ for $T: V \to W$, then $T$ is one-to-one. Reason (R): $\text{nullity}(T) = 0$ implies injectivity.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

By Rank-Nullity, $\text{rank}(T) = \dim(V) \implies \text{nullity}(T) = 0$. A zero nullity means $\ker(T) = \{0}$, which is the exact condition for $T$ to be injective (one-to-one).

Q9
Let $T$ be a linear operator on $\mathbb{R}^3$ such that $\text{rank}(T) = 2$. What is the dimension of the range space of $T$?
A 0
B 1
C 2
D 3
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, $\text{rank}(T)$ is precisely the dimension of the range space (image) of $T$. Hence, the dimension is 2.

Q10
If $S$ and $T$ are two linear transformations on a vector space $V$ of dimension $n$, what is the relation between $\text{nullity}(ST)$ and individual nullities?
A $\text{nullity}(ST) \le \text{nullity}(S) + \text{nullity}(T)$
B $\text{nullity}(ST) = \text{nullity}(S)\text{nullity}(T)$
C $\text{nullity}(ST) \ge \text{nullity}(S)$
D $\text{nullity}(ST) = \text{nullity}(T)$
Derivation & Analysis:

By Sylvester's inequality for nullities of products, the nullity of a composite linear transformation is bounded by the sum of the nullities: $\text{nullity}(ST) \le \text{nullity}(S) + \text{nullity}(T)$.
