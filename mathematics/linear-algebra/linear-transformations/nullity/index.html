7. Linear Algebra

Nullity of linear transformation

The nullity of a linear transformation $T: V \to W$ is defined as the dimension of its null space (or kernel), denoted as $\text{nullity}(T) = \dim(\ker(T))$. Governed fundamentally by the Rank-Nullity Theorem, it represents the dimension of the subspace collapsed to the zero vector and serves as a premier scoring area in UP PGT/TGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Rank-Nullity Theorem (Dimension Theorem for Linear Transformations)
Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces over a field $\mathbb{F}$, with $V$ being finite-dimensional. Let $T: V \to W$ be a linear transformation. Then the sum of the rank of $T$ and the nullity of $T$ is equal to the dimension of the domain space $V$: $$\text{rank}(T) + \text{nullity}(T) = \dim(V)$$ or equivalently, $$\dim(\text{range}(T)) + \dim(\ker(T)) = \dim(V)$$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, the nullity measures the 'degrees of freedom' lost when vectors are mapped under $T$. If $T$ projects 3D space onto a 2D plane, the kernel is a 1D line (the direction of projection), so the nullity is 1 and the rank is 2, summing to the domain dimension 3.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, it connects the solution space of homogeneous linear equations ($AX = 0$) with the column space of matrices. It dictates injectivity and surjectivity: $T$ is injective if and only if $\text{nullity}(T) = 0$.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Injectivity Test via Nullity

$$ T \text{ is injective} \iff \ker(T) = \{0\} \iff \text{nullity}(T) = 0 $$

Exam Rule: To check if a linear transformation is one-to-one, instantly set $T(x) = 0$ and find the dimension of the solution space. If dimension is zero, it is injective.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT and KVS PGT to eliminate options for non-singular transformations.
02

Shortcut 2: Matrix Rank-Nullity Calculation

$$ \text{nullity}(A) = \text{number of columns of } A - \text{rank}(A) $$

Exam Rule: For a matrix transformation $T(x) = Ax$, find the rank using row echelon form (number of non-zero rows) and subtract from total columns.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequent numerical questions in DSSSB and UP TGT asking for nullity of a given $3 \times 4$ or $4 \times 3$ matrix.
03

Shortcut 3: Dimension Inequality for Composition

$$ \text{nullity}(ST) \le \text{nullity}(S) + \text{nullity}(T) $$

Exam Rule: Use this inequality to instantly bound the nullity of composite transformations without computing matrix multiplication.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard theoretical multiple-choice question in NVS PGT examinations.
04

Shortcut 4: Dimension of Null Space of Homogeneous System

$$ \text{nullity} = n - r $$

Exam Rule: For a homogeneous system $AX = 0$ of $m$ equations in $n$ variables with rank $r$, the number of linearly independent solutions (nullity) is always $n - r$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Extremely high yield in UP PGT Mathematics for system of linear equations.
05

Shortcut 5: Transformation on Polynomial Spaces

$$ \text{nullity}(D) = 1 \text{ for derivative operator } D: P_n(\mathbb{R}) \to P_n(\mathbb{R}) $$

Exam Rule: The derivative operator on polynomials of degree $\le n$ annihilates constant polynomials, hence its nullity is always 1.

⚡ PYQ Context: Asked repeatedly in LT Grade and UP PGT vector space modules.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Domain Dimension

$$ \text{nullity}(T) \le \dim(V) $$

Exam Rule: Eliminate any option where nullity exceeds the dimension of the domain space $V$. Also ensure rank + nullity equals $\dim(V)$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Time-saving elimination trick for 125-question PGT papers.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Domain Dimension with Codomain Dimension

Common Pitfall: Candidates subtract rank from $\dim(W)$ instead of $\dim(V)$ when applying the Rank-Nullity theorem.

Counterexample: $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^5$ with $\text{rank}(T) = 2$. Nullity is $2 - 2 = 0$, NOT $5 - 2 = 3$.

Why it fails: The Rank-Nullity theorem strictly states $\text{rank} + \text{nullity} = \dim(\text{Domain})$, regardless of the codomain dimension.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always verify that you are subtracting rank from the dimension of the source space (domain), not the target space.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Assuming Non-Zero Nullity Implies Non-Linearity

Common Pitfall: Believing that if a transformation maps multiple distinct vectors to zero, it cannot be linear.

Counterexample: Zero transformation $T(x) = 0$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$

Why it fails: The zero transformation is perfectly linear, and its nullity equals the entire dimension of the domain $\dim(V)$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Linear transformations can have large nullities; linearity only requires preservation of vector addition and scalar multiplication.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Let $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be a linear transformation defined by $T(x, y, z) = (x + y + z, 2x + y - z, 4x + 3y + z)$. Find the nullity of $T$.

(A) 0(B) 1(C) 2(D) 3
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write the matrix of $T$ with respect to the standard basis: $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & -1 \\ 4 & 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Step 2: Apply row operations to find the rank. $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$, $R_3 \to R_3 - 4R_1$ gives $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & -3 \\ 0 & -1 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$. Step 3: Further reduction gives 2 non-zero rows, so $\text{rank}(T) = 2$. Step 4: By Rank-Nullity Theorem, $\text{nullity}(T) = \dim(\mathbb{R}^3) - \text{rank}(T) = 3 - 2 = 1$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Compute the determinant of matrix $A$. $\det(A) = 1(1 - (-3)) - 1(2 - (-4)) + 1(6 - 4) = 4 - 6 + 2 = 0$. Since $\det(A) = 0$, rank $< 3$. Checking $2 \times 2$ minor $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = -1 \neq 0$, so $\text{rank} = 2$. Nullity $= 3 - 2 = 1$.
💡 Key Takeaway: If the determinant of the transformation matrix is zero, the nullity is at least 1. Always check determinants first for square matrices!
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Let $V$ be the vector space of all polynomials of degree at most 3 over $\mathbb{R}$. Let $T: V \to V$ be the linear transformation defined by $T(p(x)) = \frac{d}{dx}p(x)$. What is the nullity of $T$?

(A) 0(B) 1(C) 2(D) 3
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: The dimension of $V = P_3(\mathbb{R})$ is $3 + 1 = 4$. Step 2: The range of $T$ consists of polynomials of degree at most 2, whose dimension is 3. Step 3: By Rank-Nullity Theorem, $\text{rank}(T) + \text{nullity}(T) = 4 \implies 3 + \text{nullity}(T) = 4 \implies \text{nullity}(T) = 1$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
The derivative operator annihilates constant polynomials. The space of constant polynomials in $P_3(\mathbb{R})$ has dimension 1. Therefore, $\text{nullity} = 1$.
💡 Key Takeaway: For differentiation operators on polynomial spaces $P_n$, the nullity is always 1 (corresponding to constant polynomials).

Q1
MCQ 1: If $T: V \to W$ is a linear transformation and $\dim(V) = 5$, $\dim(W) = 7$, and $\text{rank}(T) = 3$, then the nullity of $T$ is:
A (A) 2
B (B) 4
C (C) 5
D (D) 0
Derivation & Analysis:

By the Rank-Nullity Theorem, $\text{rank}(T) + \text{nullity}(T) = \dim(V)$. Here $\dim(V) = 5$ and $\text{rank}(T) = 3$. Thus, $\text{nullity}(T) = 5 - 3 = 2$. Note that codomain dimension 7 is extra information not needed.

Q2
MCQ 2: Let $T: \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be a linear transformation. What is the minimum possible nullity of $T$?
A (A) 0
B (B) 1
C (C) 2
D (D) 3
Derivation & Analysis:

The maximum possible rank of $T$ is $\min(\dim(\mathbb{R}^4), \dim(\mathbb{R}^3)) = \min(4, 3) = 3$. Since $\text{nullity} = 4 - \text{rank}$, the minimum nullity occurs when rank is maximum ($3$). Thus, minimum nullity $= 4 - 3 = 1$.

Q3
MCQ 3: A linear transformation $T: V \to W$ is one-to-one if and only if:
A (A) $\text{nullity}(T) = \dim(V)$
B (B) $\text{nullity}(T) = 0$
C (C) $\text{rank}(T) = 0$
D (D) $\text{rank}(T) = \dim(W)$
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, $T$ is one-to-one (injective) if and only if $\ker(T) = \{0}$, which means the dimension of the kernel (nullity) is 0.

Q4
MCQ 4: Let $A$ be a $5 \times 8$ matrix of rank 3. What is the nullity of the corresponding linear transformation?
A (A) 2
B (B) 3
C (C) 5
D (D) 5
Derivation & Analysis:

The nullity of a matrix transformation is given by $n - \text{rank}(A)$, where $n$ is the number of columns. Here columns $n = 8$ and $\text{rank} = 3$, so nullity $= 8 - 3 = 5$. Q5
MCQ 5: If $T: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$ is a linear transformation such that $\text{nullity}(T) = 0$, then $T$ is:
A (A) Non-invertible
B (B) Singular
C (C) Invertible (Non-singular)
D (D) The zero transformation
Derivation & Analysis:

When $\text{nullity}(T) = 0$ on equal-dimensional spaces, $\text{rank}(T) = n$, making $T$ onto as well as one-to-one, hence invertible (non-singular).

Q6
MCQ 6: Let $W_1$ and $W_2$ be subspaces of a finite-dimensional vector space $V$. Consider the transformation $T: V \to V/W_1 \times V/W_2$. The nullity of this canonical projection map is:
A (A) $\dim(W_1 \cap W_2)$
B (B) $\dim(W_1 + W_2)$
C (C) $\dim(W_1) + \dim(W_2)$
D (D) $0$
Derivation & Analysis:

The kernel of the map $x \mapsto (x + W_1, x + W_2)$ consists of vectors $x$ that belong to both $W_1$ and $W_2$, which is $W_1 \cap W_2$. Thus, nullity equals $\dim(W_1 \cap W_2)$.

Q7
MCQ 7: If $S$ and $T$ are linear transformations on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$, which of the following is always true for the composition $ST$?
A (A) $\text{nullity}(ST) = \text{nullity}(S) + \text{nullity}(T)$
B (B) $\text{nullity}(ST) \le \text{nullity}(S) + \text{nullity}(T)$
C (C) $\text{nullity}(ST) \ge \text{nullity}(S)$
D (D) $\text{nullity}(ST) = \text{nullity}(T)$
Derivation & Analysis:

By Sylvester's law of nullity or fundamental rank inequalities for compositions, $\text{nullity}(ST) \le \text{nullity}(S) + \text{nullity}(T)$ is always satisfied.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): The nullity of the identity operator on $\mathbb{R}^n$ is 0. Reason (R): The identity operator is one-to-one.
A (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A)
C (C) (A) is true but (R) is false
D (D) (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

The identity operator maps every vector to itself, so its kernel contains only the zero vector (nullity = 0), which directly stems from the fact that it is one-to-one.

Q9
MCQ 9: Let $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be defined by $T(x, y, z) = (x - y, y - z, z - x)$. The nullity of $T$ is:
A (A) 0
B (B) 1
C (C) 2
D (D) 3
Derivation & Analysis:

Setting $T(x,y,z) = (0,0,0)$ gives $x = y$, $y = z$, so $x = y = z$. Thus, solutions are of the form $(c, c, c) = c(1, 1, 1)$. The null space is spanned by $(1, 1, 1)$, giving dimension (nullity) equal to 1.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $A$ is a $4 \times 4$ matrix and $\text{nullity}(A) = 2$, then the system $AX = 0$ has how many linearly independent solutions?
A (A) 1
B (B) 2
C (C) 3
D (D) 4
Derivation & Analysis:

The number of linearly independent solutions to the homogeneous system $AX = 0$ is exactly equal to the nullity of the matrix $A$, which is given as 2.
