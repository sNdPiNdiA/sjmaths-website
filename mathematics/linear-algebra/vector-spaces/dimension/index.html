7. Linear Algebra

Dimension of a vector space

The dimension of a vector space is defined as the cardinality of any of its bases, representing the maximal number of linearly independent vectors or the minimal number of generators. Mastering dimension formulas for function spaces, polynomial spaces, and matrix spaces is essential for securing high scores in UP TGT/PGT, DSSSB, and KVS mathematics examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Invariance of Dimension Theorem
If $V$ is a finitely generated vector space over a field $\mathbb{F}$, then any two bases of $V$ have the exact same number of elements. Furthermore, if $\dim(V) = n$, then any linearly independent set of $n$ vectors is a basis, and any spanning set of $n$ vectors is a basis.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, dimension describes the degrees of freedom or independent directions available in a space (e.g., a line is 1-dimensional, a plane is 2-dimensional, and physical space is 3-dimensional).

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, it establishes that vector spaces over the same field are isomorphic if and only if their dimensions are equal, reducing structural problems to scalar dimension counting.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Dimension of Polynomial Spaces

$$\dim(P_n) = n + 1$$

Exam Rule: The space of polynomials of degree at most $n$, denoted $P_n$ or $\mathbb{R}_n[x]$, has dimension $n+1$ because the standard basis is $\{1, x, x^2, \dots, x^n\}.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT and KVS PGT to find dimensions of restricted polynomial subspaces.
02

Shortcut 2: Dimension of Matrix Spaces

$$\dim(M_{m \times n}(\mathbb{F})) = m \times n$$

Exam Rule: The vector space of all $m \times n$ matrices has dimension $mn$. For square matrices of order $n$: Symmetric matrices have dimension $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$, Skew-symmetric matrices have dimension $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$, and Diagonal matrices have dimension $n$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct memory-based question in DSSSB and UP TGT exams.
03

Shortcut 3: Dimension of Subspace Intersections and Sums

$$\dim(W_1 + W_2) = \dim(W_1) + \dim(W_2) - \dim(W_1 \cap W_2)$$

Exam Rule: Use the modular identity to instantly compute the missing dimension when three of the four values are provided.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard numerical problem in UP PGT and LT Grade exams.
04

Shortcut 4: Complex vs. Real Dimensions

$$\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(V_{\mathbb{C}}) = 2 \times \dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{\mathbb{C}})$$

Exam Rule: When a complex vector space of dimension $n$ is viewed as a real vector space, its dimension is doubled.

⚡ PYQ Context: Tricky conceptual question appearing in PGT screening tests.
05

Shortcut 5: Rank-Nullity Theorem Shortcut

$$\dim(V) = \text{Rank}(T) + \text{Nullity}(T)$$

Exam Rule: For a linear transformation $T: V \to W$, the dimension of the domain equals the rank plus the nullity.

⚡ PYQ Context: Core scoring zone for linear algebra in all state PGT exams.
06

Shortcut 6: Trace Zero Matrix Subspace

$$\dim(\{A \in M_{n \times n}(\mathbb{F}) : \text{tr}(A) = 0\}) = n^2 - 1$$

Exam Rule: Imposing a single homogeneous linear constraint (like trace zero) reduces the dimension of the space by exactly 1.

⚡ PYQ Context: Asked to test deeper understanding of subspace constraints in UP PGT.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Field Dependence Fallacy

Common Pitfall: Assuming the dimension of a vector space is absolute regardless of the scalar field.

Counterexample: $V = \mathbb{C}$, $\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}) = 1$ vs $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C}) = 2$

Why it fails: The choice of the underlying scalar field dictates scalar multiplication rules and consequently the basis set.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always read the subscript of the vector space notation carefully: $V(\mathbb{F})$ or $\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(V)$.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Dimension of Union Subspace Mistake

Common Pitfall: Writing $\dim(W_1 \cup W_2) = \dim(W_1) + \dim(W_2)$.

Counterexample: $W_1$ and $W_2$ are intersecting subspaces

Why it fails: The union of two subspaces is generally not a subspace unless one is contained in the other. The dimension formula applies to the sum $W_1 + W_2$, not the union.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Never add dimensions for unions; always use the sum formula for vector spaces.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the dimension of the vector space of all symmetric matrices of order $n$ with trace equal to zero over the field $\mathbb{R}$.

(A) $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$(B) $\frac{n(n+1)}{2} - 1$(C) $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$(D) $n^2 - 1$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: The dimension of symmetric matrices of order $n$ is $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$. Step 2: The trace condition $\text{tr}(A) = 0$ imposes 1 independent linear constraint on the diagonal elements. Step 3: Subtract 1 from the dimension, yielding $\frac{n(n+1)}{2} - 1$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Start with symmetric dimension $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$ and subtract 1 for the trace condition.
💡 Key Takeaway: Watch out for combined constraints in matrix subspaces.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $W_1$ and $W_2$ are two subspaces of a vector space $V$ such that $\dim(W_1) = 3$, $\dim(W_2) = 4$, and $\dim(W_1 \cap W_2) = 1$, then what is $\dim(W_1 + W_2)$?

(A) 7(B) 6(C) 5(D) 8
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Apply modular identity $\dim(W_1 + W_2) = \dim(W_1) + \dim(W_2) - \dim(W_1 \cap W_2)$. Step 2: Substitute values: $3 + 4 - 1 = 6$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Direct arithmetic: $3 + 4 - 1 = 6$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Standard formula substitution question; solve in under 10 seconds.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
What is the dimension of the vector space $\mathbb{C}$ over the field $\mathbb{R}$?
A 1
B 2
C 0
D Infinite
Derivation & Analysis:

The basis for $\mathbb{C}$ over $\mathbb{R}$ is $\{1, i\}$, which contains 2 elements. Hence, the dimension is 2.

Q2
What is the dimension of the vector space of all diagonal matrices of order $n$?
A $n^2$
B $n$
C $n(n-1)/2$
D $n(n+1)/2$
Derivation & Analysis:

A diagonal matrix has only its diagonal entries non-zero, yielding $n$ independent free parameters.

Q3
If $V$ is a vector space of dimension $n$, then any subset of $V$ containing more than $n$ vectors is:
A Linearly independent
B A basis
C Linearly dependent
D A spanning set
Derivation & Analysis:

By fundamental dimension theorems, any set containing more than $n$ vectors in an $n$-dimensional space must be linearly dependent.

Q4
What is the dimension of the zero vector space $\{0}$?
A 1
B 0
C Not defined
D Infinity
Derivation & Analysis:

The zero vector space has the empty set as its basis. The cardinality of the empty set is 0.

Q5
What is the dimension of the vector space of all polynomials of degree at most $n$?
A $n$
B $n-1$
C $n+1$
D $2n$
Derivation & Analysis:

The standard basis is $\{1, x, x^2, \dots, x^n\}$, which has $n+1$ elements.

Q6
Let $W$ be the subspace of $M_{3 \times 3}(\mathbb{R})$ consisting of skew-symmetric matrices. What is $\dim(W)$?
A 9
B 6
C 3
D 4
Derivation & Analysis:

For a skew-symmetric matrix of order $n=3$, the dimension is $\frac{n(n-1)}{2} = \frac{3(2)}{2} = 3$.

Q7
If $W_1$ and $W_2$ are subspaces of dimensions 3 and 5 respectively, what is the minimum possible dimension of $W_1 \cap W_2$?
A 0
B 3
C 2
D 1
Derivation & Analysis:

Using modular identity and maximal possible sum dimension $\dim(W_1 + W_2) \le \dim(V)$, the intersection can be 0 if they are direct sums.

Q8
What is the dimension of $\mathbb{R}$ considered as a vector space over the field of rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$?
A 1
B Countable
C Uncountable (Infinite)
D Zero
Derivation & Analysis:

$\mathbb{R}$ is an uncountable dimensional vector space over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Q9
If $T: \mathbb{R}^5 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ is a linear transformation, what is the maximum possible rank of $T$?
A 5
B 3
C 2
D 8
Derivation & Analysis:

By rank-nullity theorem, $\text{Rank}(T) + \text{Nullity}(T) = 5$, and image is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$, so $\text{Rank}(T) \le 3$.

Q10
Which of the following sets forms a basis for $\mathbb{R}^2$?
A $\{(1, 2), (2, 4)\}$
B $\{(1, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1)\}$
C $\{(1, 1), (1, -1)\}$
D $\{(0, 0), (1, 1)\}$
Derivation & Analysis:

The vectors $(1, 1)$ and $(1, -1)$ are linearly independent and span $\mathbb{R}^2$ as there are 2 vectors with non-zero determinant.
