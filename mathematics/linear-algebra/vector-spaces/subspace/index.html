7. Linear Algebra

Subspace

A subspace is a subset of a vector space that is itself a vector space under the inherited operations of addition and scalar multiplication. Mastering subspaces is essential for clearing algebraic structure tests and forms a core high-scoring zone in UP PGT/TGT, DSSSB, and KVS examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Fundamental Subspace Characterization Theorem
Let $V$ be a vector space over a field $\mathbb{F}$, and let $W$ be a non-empty subset of $V$. Then $W$ is a subspace of $V$ if and only if for all $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{F}$ and for all $x, y \in \mathbb{W}$, we have $\alpha x + \beta y \in W$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, a subspace represents a flat geometric object passing through the origin (such as lines through the origin in $\mathbb{R}^2$, or planes and lines through the origin in $\mathbb{R}^3$). It can never be a translated affine space that misses the zero vector.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It condenses the eight vector space axioms down into closedness under linear combinations, ensuring that the algebraic operations do not take vectors outside the subset.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: The Zero Vector Quick-Test

$$ 0_V \notin W \implies W \text{ is NOT a subspace} $$

Exam Rule: Always check if the zero vector of the parent space belongs to the given subset $W$. If $0$ is missing, immediately reject the option without checking closure.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently used in UP PGT/TGT to eliminate 2 out of 4 options within 5 seconds.
02

Shortcut 2: Homogeneous vs Non-Homogeneous Equations

$$ A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0} \implies \text{Subspace} $$

Exam Rule: Solution sets of homogeneous linear systems are always subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^n$, whereas non-homogeneous systems are affine varieties and never subspaces.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in DSSSB and KVS PGT exams.
03

Shortcut 3: Non-Linearity Elimination

$$ x^2 + y^2 \le 1 \text{ or } xy = 0 \implies \text{Not a Subspace} $$

Exam Rule: Subspaces are strictly defined by linear homogeneous equations. Any degree $\ge 2$ terms, products of variables ($xy$), or non-linear constraints instantly rule out a subspace.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard pattern in UP PGT exam questions involving polynomial or coordinate subsets.
04

Shortcut 4: Intersection of Subspaces

$$ W_1, W_2 \le V \implies W_1 \cap W_2 \le V $$

Exam Rule: The intersection of any collection of subspaces is always a subspace. (Note: Union is generally not a subspace unless one is contained in the other).

⚡ PYQ Context: Tested as theoretical assertion-reason questions in PGT screening tests.
05

Shortcut 5: Dimension Constraint of Subspaces

$$ W \le V \implies \dim(W) \le \dim(V) $$

Exam Rule: If a purported subspace has a dimension greater than the parent space, or fails to satisfy rank-nullity bounds, it is invalid.

⚡ PYQ Context: Applied in vector space mapping and matrix subspace problems.
06

Shortcut 6: Symmetric and Skew-Symmetric Matrices

$$ \text{Sym}_n(\mathbb{R}) \le M_n(\mathbb{R}), \quad \text{Skew}_n(\mathbb{R}) \le M_n(\mathbb{R}) $$

Exam Rule: Symmetric matrices form a subspace of $M_n(\mathbb{R})$, and skew-symmetric matrices form a subspace. However, their union or general invertible matrices do not.

⚡ PYQ Context: High-frequency question type in UP TGT/PGT linear algebra.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: The Zero Vector Fallacy in Transformed Sets

Common Pitfall: Candidates check addition and scalar multiplication but forget that $0_V$ must be in the subset.

Counterexample: W = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid x + y = 1\}

Why it fails: Here $(0,0)$ does not satisfy $0 + 0 = 1$. Thus $0_V \notin W$, so $W$ is not a subspace despite looking linear.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Substitute $(0,0, \dots, 0)$ into the defining equation first before doing any closure tests.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Union of Subspaces Confusion

Common Pitfall: Assuming that the union of two subspaces is also a subspace.

Counterexample: W_1 = \{(x, 0) \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}, \; W_2 = \{(0, y) \mid y \in \mathbb{R}\} \text{ in } \mathbb{R}^2

Why it fails: (1,0) \in W_1$ and $(0,1) \in W_2$, but their sum $(1,1)$ is not in $W_1 \cup W_2$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Remember: $W_1 \cup W_2$ is a subspace $\iff W_1 \subset W_2$ or $W_2 \subset W_1$.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Which of the following subsets of $\mathbb{R}^3$ is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$?

(A) \{(x, y, z) \mid x + y + z = 1\}(B) \{(x, y, z) \mid x = y = z\}(C) \{(x, y, z) \mid xyz = 0\}(D) \{(x, y, z) \mid x \ge 0\}
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Check zero vector. For (B), $0=0=0$ holds, so $(0,0,0) \in W$. Step 2: Check closure: if $x_1=y_1=z_1$ and $x_2=y_2=z_2$, then $(x_1+x_2)=(y_1+y_2)=(z_1+z_2)$, which holds. Thus (B) is a subspace.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Option (A) misses $(0,0,0)$ because $0+0+0 \neq 1$. Option (C) has non-linear product $xyz$. Option (D) fails scalar multiplication by negative numbers. Only (B) represents a homogeneous linear system.
💡 Key Takeaway: Look for homogeneous linear equations to instantly spot the correct subspace.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Let $V = M_n(\mathbb{R})$. Which of the following is NOT a subspace of $V$?

(A) Diagonal matrices(B) Symmetric matrices(C) Invertible matrices(D) Upper triangular matrices
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
The zero matrix $0$ is not invertible, hence the zero vector of $M_n(\mathbb{R})$ does not belong to the set of invertible matrices. Also, the sum of two invertible matrices may be singular.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recall the golden rule: Invertible matrices do NOT contain the zero matrix, so they can never form a subspace.
💡 Key Takeaway: Examiners frequently include 'invertible matrices' or 'singular matrices' as distractors.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
MCQ 1: Which of the following subsets of $\mathbb{R}^2$ is a subspace?
A (A) \{(x, y) \mid y = 2x\}
B (B) \{(x, y) \mid y = 2x + 1\}
C (C) \{(x, y) \mid xy = 0\}
D (D) \{(x, y) \mid x^2 + y^2 \le 1\}
Derivation & Analysis:

$y = 2x$ passes through the origin ($0,0$) and represents a homogeneous linear equation, defining a line through the origin which is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$.

Q2
MCQ 2: Let $W_1$ and $W_2$ be subspaces of a vector space $V$. Then $W_1 \cup W_2$ is a subspace of $V$ if and only if:
A (A) $W_1 \cap W_2 = \{0\}$
B (B) $W_1 \subseteq W_2$ or $W_2 \subseteq W_1$
C (C) $W_1 + W_2 = V$
D (D) $W_1$ and $W_2$ are disjoint
Derivation & Analysis:

Standard theorem in linear algebra: The union of two subspaces is a subspace if and only if one is contained in the other.

Q3
MCQ 3: The dimension of the subspace of all symmetric matrices of order $n$ over $\mathbb{R}$ is:
A (A) $n^2$
B (B) $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$
C (C) $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$
D (D) $n^2 - n$
Derivation & Analysis:

A symmetric matrix has independent entries on and above the main diagonal, numbering $1 + 2 + \dots + n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$.

Q4
MCQ 4: The dimension of the subspace of all skew-symmetric matrices of order $n$ over $\mathbb{R}$ is:
A (A) $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$
B (B) $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$
C (C) $n^2 - n$
D (D) $\frac{n^2}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Skew-symmetric matrices have zeros on the main diagonal and negated entries below, giving $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ independent parameters.

Q5
MCQ 5: Which of the following is a subspace of the vector space $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}$ of all real-valued functions?
A (A) \{f \mid f(0) = 1\}
B (B) \{f \mid f(1) = 0\}
C (C) \{f \mid f is differentiable and f'(x) = 1\}
D (D) \{f \mid f(x) \ge 0 \forall x\}
Derivation & Analysis:

The zero function $f(x)=0$ satisfies $f(1)=0$, and linear combinations of functions vanishing at $x=1$ also vanish at $x=1$. Thus it forms a subspace.

Q6
MCQ 6: If $W_1$ and $W_2$ are two subspaces of dimension $3$ and $5$ respectively in a vector space of dimension $7$, what is the minimum possible dimension of $W_1 \cap W_2$?
A (A) $0$
B (B) $1$
C (C) $2$
D (D) $3$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using modular identity for dimensions: $\dim(W_1 + W_2) = \dim(W_1) + \dim(W_2) - \dim(W_1 \cap W_2)$. Since $\dim(W_1 + W_2) \le 7$, we have $3 + 5 - \dim(W_1 \cap W_2) \le 7 \implies \dim(W_1 \cap W_2) \ge 1$.

Q7
MCQ 7: The set of all polynomials of degree at exact degree $n$ along with the zero polynomial:
A (A) Forms a subspace of $P(\mathbb{R})$
B (B) Does not form a subspace because of closure under addition failure
C (C) Forms a subspace of dimension $n$
D (D) Forms a subspace of dimension $n+1$
Derivation & Analysis:

The sum of polynomials of exact degree $n$, such as $(x^n + x) + (-x^n + 1) = x + 1$, has degree $1 < n$, failing addition closure.

Q8
MCQ 8: Consider the subset $W$ of $\mathbb{R}^3$ given by $x - 2y + 3z = 0$. The dimension of $W$ is:
A (A) $0$
B (B) $1$
C (C) $2$
D (D) $3$
Derivation & Analysis:

The equation $x - 2y + 3z = 0$ is 1 independent homogeneous linear equation in 3 variables, so $\dim(W) = 3 - 1 = 2$.

Q9
MCQ 9: Which of the following is a subspace of the vector space of all $n \times n$ matrices?
A (A) Matrices with trace equal to $1$
B (B) Matrices with determinant equal to $0$
C (C) Matrices with trace equal to $0$
D (D) Orthogonal matrices
Derivation & Analysis:

Trace is a linear map, and the kernel of a linear map (matrices with trace zero) is always a subspace.

Q10
MCQ 10: Let $W$ be the set of all symmetric matrices of order $n$. What is the codimension of $W$ in $M_n(\mathbb{R})$?
A (A) $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$
B (B) $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$
C (C) $n^2$
D (D) $\frac{n(n-2)}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Codimension is $\dim(M_n(\mathbb{R})) - \dim(\text{Sym}_n) = n^2 - \frac{n(n+1)}{2} = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$.
