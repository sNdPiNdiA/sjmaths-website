6. Abstract Algebra

Normal subgroups

Normal subgroups are foundational substructures in abstract algebra that allow the construction of well-defined quotient groups through the preservation of conjugation. Mastery of normal subgroups, their equivalent characterizations, and tests is a high-yield, frequently tested scoring zone in UP PGT/TGT, DSSSB, and KVS Mathematics examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade

○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Definition and Fundamental Characterizations of Normal Subgroups
A subgroup $N$ of a group $G$ is called a normal subgroup (denoted by $N \trianglelefteq G$) if it is invariant under inner automorphisms of $G$, i.e., \forall g \in G, \forall n \in N, gng^{-1} \in N. Equivalently, left cosets and right cosets coincide: \forall g \in G, gN = Ng.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Symmetry preservation under conjugation; visualizing group actions where internal substructures are left invariant by external transformations.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Normal subgroups are precisely the kernels of group homomorphisms and form the exact algebraic condition required to define a valid group structure on the set of cosets (quotient group $G/N$).

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Index 2 Subgroup Rule

$$\text{If } H \le G \text{ and } [G:H] = 2, \text{ then } H \trianglelefteq G.$$

Exam Rule: Any subgroup of index 2 in a group $G$ is automatically a normal subgroup.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT and KVS to instantly identify normal subgroups of symmetric groups $S_n$ (like $A_n$) or dihedral groups.
02

Shortcut 2: Center of a Group

$$Z(G) = \{z \in G \mid zg = gz, \forall g \in G\} \implies Z(G) \trianglelefteq G$$

Exam Rule: The center of any group $G$ is always a normal subgroup of $G$. Furthermore, every subgroup of an abelian group is normal.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct theoretical questions in DSSSB PGT and UP PGT regarding abelian groups and centers.
03

Shortcut 3: Kernel of Homomorphism

$$\text{If } \phi: G \to H \text{ is a group homomorphism, then } \ker(\phi) \trianglelefteq G.$$

Exam Rule: Every normal subgroup arises as the kernel of some group homomorphism, and vice versa.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used to solve structural quotient group mapping questions in LT Grade and UP PGT exams.
04

Shortcut 4: Sylow $p$-Subgroup Uniqueness

$$\text{If } P \text{ is the unique Sylow } p\text{-subgroup of } G, \text{ then } P \trianglelefteq G.$$

Exam Rule: A unique Sylow $p$-subgroup for a given prime $p$ is always normal in $G$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard high-level PGT question format for finite groups.
05

Shortcut 5: Direct Product Components

$$G = G_1 \times G_2 \implies G_1 \times \{e_2\} \trianglelefteq G$$

Exam Rule: Direct product factors are normal subgroups of the direct product group.

⚡ PYQ Context: Appears in structural classification of finite abelian groups.
06

Shortcut 6: Simple Groups Elimination

$$|G| = p \text{ (prime) } \implies \text{ only normal subgroups are } \{e} \text{ and } G$$

Exam Rule: Groups of prime order have no proper non-trivial normal subgroups (they are simple).

⚡ PYQ Context: Rapid elimination trick for options involving cyclic groups of prime order.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Subgroup of Normal Subgroup is Normal

Common Pitfall: Candidates assume that if $H \trianglelefteq N$ and $N \trianglelefteq G$, then $H \trianglelefteq G (normality is transitive).

Counterexample: Alternating group $A_4$ has a normal subgroup $V_4$ (Klein 4-group), and $V_4$ has subgroups of order 2 which are normal in $V_4$ but not normal in $A_4$.

Why it fails: Normality is not a transitive relation in general.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Never assume transitivity for normal subgroups unless specified (e.g., characteristic subgroups).
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Every Subgroup of Abelian Group vs Non-Abelian Group

Common Pitfall: Assuming non-abelian groups have no normal subgroups other than trivial ones, or forgetting that all subgroups of abelian groups are normal.

Counterexample: Dihedral group $D_4$ has non-normal subgroups of order 2.

Why it fails: Non-abelian groups possess a mix of normal and non-normal subgroups.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always verify conjugation $gng^{-1} \in N$ or index for non-abelian groups.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $H$ is a subgroup of $G$ of index 2, then which of the following is always true?

(A) $H$ is not a subgroup(B) $H$ is a normal subgroup of $G$(C) $H$ is equal to $G$(D) $H$ is abelian
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Note index $[G:H] = 2$. Step 2: Left and right cosets partition $G$ into two sets: $H$ and $G \setminus H$. Step 3: Since $G = H \cup gH = H \cup Hg$ for any $g \notin H$, left and right cosets coincide. Step 4: By definition, $H \trianglelefteq G$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Apply Shortcut 1 directly: Index 2 implies normality instantly.
💡 Key Takeaway: Direct theorem statement frequently asked in UP TGT/PGT.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

What is the number of proper non-trivial normal subgroups of the symmetric group $S_3$?

(A) 0(B) 1(C) 2(D) 3
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: List all subgroups of $S_3$ (orders 1, 2, 3, 6). Step 2: Subgroups of order 2 have index $6/2 = 3$ (not index 2, hence not normal). Step 3: Subgroup of order 3 is $A_3$, which has index $6/3 = 2$, hence normal. Step 4: Only $A_3$ is a proper non-trivial normal subgroup.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Use index 2 rule: $A_3$ has index 2 in $S_3$, so it is normal. Subgroups of order 2 are not normal because they are not unique in their conjugacy class.
💡 Key Takeaway: Remember $A_n$ is always normal in $S_n$ with index 2.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
MCQ 1: A subgroup $N$ of $G$ is normal if and only if for every $g \in G$:
A $gN \neq Ng$
B $gN = Ng$
C $gN \subset Ng$
D $Ng \subset gN$
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, a subgroup $N$ is normal in $G$ if and only if its left cosets and right cosets coincide, i.e., $gN = Ng$ for all $g \in G$.

Q2
MCQ 2: If $G$ is an abelian group, then every subgroup of $G$ is:
A Not a subgroup
B Normal
C Infinite
D Cyclic
Derivation & Analysis:

In an abelian group, $gh = hg$ for all $g, h \in G$. Thus $gNg^{-1} = gg^{-1}N = N$, so every subgroup is normal.

Q3
MCQ 3: The index of the alternating group $A_n$ in the symmetric group $S_n$ ($n \ge 2$) is:
A $n$
B $n!$
C 2
D $n/2$
Derivation & Analysis:

$A_n$ consists of all even permutations, which has order $n!/2$. Thus the index $[S_n : A_n] = \frac{n!}{n!/2} = 2$.

Q4
MCQ 4: Which of the following is always a normal subgroup of any group $G$?
A Any subgroup of prime order
B The center $Z(G)$
C Any cyclic subgroup
D Any subgroup of order 2
Derivation & Analysis:

The center $Z(G) = \{z \in G \mid zg = gz, \forall g \in G\}$ commutes with every element of $G$ and is always a normal subgroup.

Q5
MCQ 5: If $H$ is a subgroup of index 2 in $G$, then $H$ is:
A Always normal
B Never normal
C Abelian only
D Trivial
Derivation & Analysis:

Any subgroup of index 2 in any group $G$ is automatically a normal subgroup of $G$.

Q6
MCQ 6: Kernel of any group homomorphism $\phi: G \to H$ is always a:
A Non-normal subgroup
B Normal subgroup of $G$
C Normal subgroup of $H$
D Cyclic group
Derivation & Analysis:

The kernel of a homomorphism $\phi: G \to H$ satisfies the subgroup property and normality condition, hence $\ker(\phi) \trianglelefteq G$.

Q7
MCQ 7: If $N$ and $M$ are normal subgroups of $G$, then their intersection $N \cap M$ is:
A Not a subgroup
B Always a normal subgroup of $G$
C Always trivial
D Always of index 2
Derivation & Analysis:

The intersection of any family of normal subgroups is a normal subgroup.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): Every subgroup of a cyclic group is normal. Reason (R): Every cyclic group is abelian.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation
C (A) is true but (R) is false
D (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

Every cyclic group is abelian, and every subgroup of an abelian group is normal. Thus both are true and (R) explains (A).

Q9
MCQ 9: In the symmetric group $S_3$, the subgroup $H = \{(1), (1 2)\}$ is:
A A normal subgroup
B Not a normal subgroup
C equal to $A_3$
D The center of $S_3$
Derivation & Analysis:

H has order 2 and index 3 in $S_3$. Conjugating $(1 2)$ by $(1 3)$ yields $(2 3) \notin H$, hence $H$ is not normal.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $G$ is a simple group of order 60, its proper non-trivial normal subgroups number is:
A 0
B 1
C 2
D 5
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, a simple group has no proper non-trivial normal subgroups, so the count is 0.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Without normality ($gN = Ng$), coset multiplication $(aN)(bN) = abN$ is not well-defined, as the product would depend on the choice of coset representatives.

Assuming normality is transitive (i.e., $H \trianglelefteq N$ and $N \trianglelefteq G \implies H \trianglelefteq G$), which is false in general.

Abstract algebra generally contributes 3 to 5 questions in UP PGT and 2 to 3 questions in UP TGT, with normal subgroups and quotient groups forming a core portion.

Memorize high-yield shortcuts such as the index 2 rule, center normality, and homomorphism kernel properties to bypass lengthy conjugation tests.
