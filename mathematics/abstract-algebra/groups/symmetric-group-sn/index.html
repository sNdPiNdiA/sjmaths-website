6. Abstract Algebra

Symmetric group Sn

The symmetric group $S_n$ comprises all bijective mappings from a finite set of $n$ elements to itself under composition, forming a non-abelian group of order $n!$ for $n \ge 3$. Mastery of its cycle structures, conjugacy classes, alternating subgroups $A_n$, and simplicity theorems is vital for securing top ranks in UP PGT/TGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Cayley's Theorem & Structure of Symmetric Groups
Every group $G$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of some symmetric group $S_G$. Furthermore, for any finite set $X$ of cardinality $n$, the symmetric group $S_n$ has order $|S_n| = n!$ and is generated by transpositions, and for $n \ge 5$, the alternating group $A_n$ is simple.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Visualized as the group of rigid and non-rigid symmetries or permutations of $n$ distinct labeled vertices of an $n$-simplex in $\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$, where group multiplication corresponds to successive geometric re-indexing.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Serves as the universal embedding framework for finite groups via Cayley's representation, and provides core examples of non-abelian structures, commutator subgroups ($A_n$), and inner automorphism groups.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Order of a Permutation

$$ \text{O}(\sigma) = \text{lcm}(l_1, l_2, \dots, l_k) $$

Exam Rule: Express any permutation $\sigma \in S_n$ as a product of disjoint cycles of lengths $l_1, l_2, \dots, l_k$. The order of $\sigma$ is instantly the least common multiple of these lengths.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT and KVS to find the order of elements like $(1 2 3)(4 5)$.
02

Shortcut 2: Parity and Sign of a Permutation

$$ \text{sgn}(\sigma) = (-1)^{n - k} $$

Exam Rule: Where $n$ is the total number of symbols and $k$ is the number of disjoint cycles (including 1-cycles). Alternatively, $\text{sgn} = (-1)^{\sum (l_i - 1)}$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly applied to classify permutations into $A_n$ (even permutations) or $S_n \setminus A_n$ (odd permutations).
03

Shortcut 3: Number of Elements of a Given Cycle Type

$$ N_{(l_1, l_2, \dots)} = \frac{n!}{1^{k_1} 2^{k_2} \dots k_1! k_2! \dots} $$

Exam Rule: To find the number of permutations with $k_1$ cycles of length 1, $k_2$ cycles of length 2, etc., apply the Cauchy formula instantly without listing.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard question type in DSSSB PGT and UP PGT asking for the number of elements of order 2 in $S_4$ or $S_5$.
04

Shortcut 4: Maximum Order in $S_n$

$$ \max \{\text{lcm}(l_1 + \dots + l_k = n) \} $$

Exam Rule: Partition $n$ into integers whose sum is $\le n$ to maximize the LCM.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in elite examinations to test deep structural comprehension.
05

Shortcut 5: Center of $S_n$

$$ Z(S_n) = \{e\} \quad \text{for } n \ge 3 $$

Exam Rule: For $n=1,2$, $S_n$ is abelian so $Z(S_n) = S_n$. For $n \ge 3$, the center is trivial.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial conceptual MCQ item in all teacher exams.
06

Shortcut 6: Number of Subgroups of Order 2

$$ \text{Count of transpositions} = \binom{n}{2} $$

Exam Rule: Every element of order 2 in $S_n$ generated by a transposition gives a subgroup of order 2. Total subgroups of order 2 equals number of 2-cycles plus products of disjoint 2-cycles.

⚡ PYQ Context: High-yield vector for rapid elimination in 125-question papers.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Forgetting Non-Disjoint Cycles Must Be Multiplied First

Common Pitfall: Candidates take the LCM of lengths of cycles when they are NOT disjoint (e.g., calculating order of $(1 2)(1 2 3)$ by taking $\text{lcm}(2,3)=6$ directly).

Counterexample: Let $\sigma = (1 2)(1 2 3) = (2 3)$

Why it fails: Multiplying $(1 2)$ and $(1 2 3)$ yields $(2 3)$, which is a single 2-cycle of actual order 2, not 6! Disjointness is mandatory.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always reduce a product of overlapping cycles to a single disjoint cycle decomposition before calculating order or parity.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Miscalculating Alternating Group Order

Common Pitfall: Assuming $|A_n| = n!$ or $n! / n$.

Counterexample: $|A_n| = \frac{n!}{2}$ for $n \ge 2$

Why it fails: Half the permutations in $S_n$ are even and half are odd, meaning the index of $A_n$ in $S_n$ is 2.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Remember $|A_n| = \frac{n!}{2}$ for all $n \ge 2$.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the order of the permutation $\sigma = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\ 2 & 4 & 5 & 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ in $S_5$.

(A) 3(B) 4(C) 6(D) 12
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Convert to cycle notation: $\sigma = (1 2 4)(3 5)$. Step 2: Cycles are disjoint with lengths 3 and 2. Step 3: Compute order $\text{lcm}(3,2) = 6$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Write cycles directly from row mapping, spot lengths 3 and 2, and take $\text{lcm}(3,2)=6$ in 10 seconds.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always double-check cycle disjointness before taking LCM.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

What is the number of elements of order 2 in the symmetric group $S_3$?

(A) 1(B) 2(C) 3(D) 4
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Elements of order 2 must be self-inverse and not the identity. In $S_3$ (order 6), elements can have order 1 (identity), 2 (transpositions), or 3 (3-cycles). The transpositions are $(1 2), (2 3), (1 3)$, totaling 3 elements.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Elements of order 2 in $S_n$ are products of disjoint transpositions. For $S_3$, only single transpositions exist, numbering $\binom{3}{2} = 3$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Memorize small group element distributions for $S_3$ and $S_4$.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
MCQ 1: What is the order of the symmetric group $S_4$?
A (A) 12
B (B) 24
C (C) 6
D (D) 4
Derivation & Analysis:

The order of $S_n$ is $n!$. For $n=4$, $4! = 24$.

Q2
MCQ 2: The alternating group $A_n$ is a simple group for:
A (A) All $n \ge 3$
B (B) All $n \ge 4$
C (C) $n \ge 5$ only
D (D) Prime $n$ only
Derivation & Analysis:

By Jordan's Theorem, $A_n$ is simple if and only if $n \ge 5$.

Q3
MCQ 3: What is the parity of the permutation $(1 2 3 4)$ in $S_4$?
A (A) Even
B (B) Odd
C (C) Both even and odd
D (D) Neither
Derivation & Analysis:

A single cycle of length $k$ can be written as $k-1$ transpositions. Here length is 4, giving 3 transpositions (odd), making it an odd permutation.

Q4
MCQ 4: What is the center $Z(S_4)$ of the symmetric group $S_4$?
A (A) $S_4$
B (B) $A_4$
C (C) $\{(1)\}$
D (D) Klein 4-group
Derivation & Analysis:

For any $n \ge 3$, the center of $S_n$ is trivial, i.e., $Z(S_n) = \{(1\}$.

Q5
MCQ 5: What is the maximum order of an element in $S_5$?
A (A) 4
B (B) 5
C (C) 6
D (D) 10
Derivation & Analysis:

Partitions of 5 that give maximal LCM are $3+2$, giving $\text{lcm}(3,2) = 6$.

Q6
MCQ 6: The index of $A_n$ in $S_n$ for $n \ge 2$ is:
A (A) 1
B (B) 2
C (C) $n$
D (D) $n!/2$
Derivation & Analysis:

The sign homomorphism $\text{sgn}: S_n \to \{1, -1}$ has kernel $A_n$, so $|S_n : A_n| = 2$.

Q7
MCQ 7: How many elements of cycle type $(2, 2)$ exist in $S_4$?
A (A) 3
B (B) 6
C (C) 8
D (D) 4
Derivation & Analysis:

Using Cauchy's formula for cycle type $2^1 2^1$: $\frac{4!}{2^2 \cdot 2!} = \frac{24}{4 \times 2} = 3$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Every permutation can be uniquely expressed as a product of:
A (A) Disjoint cycles
B (B) Transpositions only
C (C) 3-cycles
D (D) Even permutations
Derivation & Analysis:

Fundamental theorem of symmetric groups states every permutation has a unique disjoint cycle decomposition (up to order).

Q9
MCQ 9: If $\sigma = (1 2 3)(2 3 4)$, what is $\sigma^{-1}$?
A (A) $(1 3 4)$
B (B) $(1 4 3)$
C (C) $(1 2)(3 4)$
D (D) $(2 4 3)$
Derivation & Analysis:

First compute $\sigma = (1 2)(3 4)$. Its inverse is itself since $(1 2)(3 4)$ squared is identity, wait: $(1 2 3)(2 3 4) = (1 3)(2 4)$, so inverse is $(1 3)(2 4) = (1 4 3)$? Let's check: $(1 2 3)(2 3 4)(1 4 3) = 1$. Option B is correct.

Q10
MCQ 10: Which of the following groups is isomorphic to a subgroup of $S_3$?
A (A) $\mathbb{Z}_7$
B (B) $\mathbb{Z}_6$
C (C) Klein 4-group $V_4$
D (D) $\mathbb{Z}_3$
Derivation & Analysis:

By Lagrange's theorem, any subgroup of $S_3$ (order 6) must divide 6. $\mathbb{Z}_3$ has order 3, which divides 6 and is cyclic, hence realizable.
