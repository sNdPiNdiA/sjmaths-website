Set Theory & Elementary Group Theory

Coset decomposition

Coset decomposition partitions a group into disjoint, equal-sized subsets using a subgroup, forming the cornerstone for proving Lagrange's Theorem and constructing quotient groups. Mastering left and right cosets, index of a subgroup, and partition mechanics is a frequent high-scoring zone in UP PGT/TGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Partition Theorem via Left/Right Cosets
Let $H$ be a subgroup of a group $G$. For any $a, b \in G$, the left coset $aH = \{ah \mid h \in H\}$ either is identical to $bH$ or is disjoint from $bH$. Furthermore, $G$ can be expressed as a disjoint union of distinct left cosets of $H$ in $G$, i.e., $G = \bigcup_{i \in I} a_i H$, where $|a_i H| = |H|$ for all $i$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Visually, think of $G$ as a large geometric space and $H$ as a linear subspace passing through the origin. Left cosets $aH$ act as parallel affine translates that tile the entire space without overlapping, each having identical cardinality and structure.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Coset decomposition establishes an equivalence relation on $G$ modulo $H$, proving that subgroups partition groups into equal partitions, which directly yields Lagrange's Theorem: $|G| = [G:H] \cdot |H|$.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Subgroup property of $H$ N/A Strictly Mandatory / Necessary If $H$ is merely a subset or subgroupoid without identity or closure, $aH = bH$ equivalence relation properties collapse, and subsets fail to partition $G$ uniformly.
Group operation associativity and inverses N/A Strictly Mandatory Without inverses, mapping $h \mapsto ah$ is not a bijection, meaning cosets $aH$ and $bH$ can have unequal cardinalities, destroying the index counting argument.
Choice of representative elements N/A Essential for partition completeness Failing to select representatives from outside previously generated cosets leads to redundant overlapping sets instead of a strict disjoint partition.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Index Calculation via Lagrange's Theorem

$$ [G:H] = \frac{|G|}{|H|} $$

Exam Rule: For finite groups, the number of distinct left (or right) cosets of $H$ in $G$ is precisely the ratio of their orders.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT to find the number of distinct left cosets of a cyclic subgroup in dihedral or symmetric groups.
02

Shortcut 2: Coset Equality Condition

$$ aH = bH \iff b^{-1}a \in H \iff a \in bH $$

Exam Rule: Two left cosets are identical if and only if the representative of one belongs to the other.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used in TGT/PGT to instantly verify whether two algebraic expressions generate the same coset.
03

Shortcut 3: Order of Coset equals Order of Subgroup

$$ |aH| = |H| \quad \forall a \in G $$

Exam Rule: Every left or right coset has the exact same cardinality as the subgroup $H$, regardless of the choice of $a$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial for counting elements satisfying specific equations or verifying finite group structures.
04

Shortcut 4: Self-Inverse / Index 2 Subgroups

$$ [G:H] = 2 \implies aH = Ha \text{ and } a^2 \in H \; \forall a \in G $$

Exam Rule: Every subgroup of index 2 is automatically normal, meaning left and right cosets coincide entirely.

⚡ PYQ Context: Instant elimination rule in questions involving alternating groups $A_n$ inside $S_n$ or index 2 subgroups.
05

Shortcut 5: Lagrange Divisibility Rule for Elements

$$ o(a) \mid |G| \quad \forall a \in G $$

Exam Rule: The order of any element in a finite group must divide the order of the group, derived directly from cyclic subgroup coset decomposition.

⚡ PYQ Context: Extensively used in UP PGT to rule out impossible element orders.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Coprime Orders

$$ \gcd(|H|, [G:H]) = 1 \implies \text{Unique structural properties} $$

Exam Rule: Use prime factorization of group orders to eliminate options that violate coset product cardinalities.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard time-saving trick in 125-question PGT screening.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Left Coset with Right Coset in Non-Abelian Groups

Common Pitfall: Assuming $aH = Ha$ for all groups, which is only true if $H$ is a normal subgroup.

Counterexample: Let $G = S_3, H = \{(1), (1 2)\}, a = (1 3)$

Why it fails: Left coset $aH = \{(1 3), (1 2 3)\}$ while right coset $Ha = \{(1 3), (1 3 2)\}$. They are distinct because $H$ is not normal in $S_3$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Never assume $aH = Ha$ unless the group is abelian or $H$ is explicitly stated to be normal.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Assuming Every Subgroup has a Complementary Subgroup

Common Pitfall: Believing that if $[G:H] = k$, there exists another subgroup $K$ of order $k$ such that $G = HK$.

Counterexample: Subgroup $H = \{(1), (1 2)\}$ in $S_3$ has index 3, but there is no subgroup of order 3 normal complement in $S_3$ for that specific $H$.

Why it fails: Index measures the number of cosets, not necessarily the existence of a transversal subgroup.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Distinguish carefully between coset spaces $G/H$ (which are sets of cosets) and quotient groups (which require normality).

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the number of distinct left cosets of the subgroup $H = 4\mathbb{Z}$ in the additive group $G = \mathbb{Z}$.

(A) 2(B) 4(C) Infinite(D) 8
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Identify group $G = \mathbb{Z}$ and subgroup $H = 4\mathbb{Z} = \{..., -8, -4, 0, 4, 8, ...\}$. Step 2: Form distinct left cosets by adding representatives $0, 1, 2, 3$: $0+4\mathbb{Z}, 1+4\mathbb{Z}, 2+4\mathbb{Z}, 3+4\mathbb{Z}$. Step 3: Total number of distinct cosets is 4.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize that $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_4$, which has cardinality 4. Instant tick.
💡 Key Takeaway: For infinite groups like $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$, finite index occurs when the subgroup is formed by multiples $n\mathbb{Z}$, yielding index $n$.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Let $H$ be a subgroup of a finite group $G$ such that $|G| = 48$ and $|H| = 8$. What is the index $[G:H]$?

(A) 4(B) 6(C) 8(D) 384
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: State Lagrange's Theorem formula $|G| = [G:H] \cdot |H|$. Step 2: Substitute values: $48 = [G:H] \cdot 8$. Step 3: Solve for $[G:H] = 48 / 8 = 6$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Direct division: $48 / 8 = 6$. Takes 3 seconds in UP PGT.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always verify whether the question asks for group order, subgroup order, or index to avoid reciprocal errors.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Left and right cosets coincide if and only if the subgroup is normal ($gHg^{-1} = H$ for all $g$). In non-abelian groups, arbitrary subgroups lack this symmetry, resulting in distinct left and right partition structures.

Candidates often confuse the index $[G:H]$ with the order of the subgroup or incorrectly assume that every subgroup of index $k$ has elements of order $k$.

Abstract algebra constitutes about 15-20% of the mathematics paper in UP PGT, with at least 2 to 3 direct questions on Lagrange's theorem, index calculation, and coset properties.

Memorize standard group orders (like $S_n, A_n, \mathbb{Z}_n, D_n$) and apply Lagrange's divisibility and index formulas directly without constructing full Cayley tables or explicit coset lists.

Abstract algebra constitutes about 15-20% of the mathematics paper in UP PGT, with at least 2 to 3 direct questions on Lagrange's theorem, index calculation, and coset properties.

Memorize standard group orders (like $S_n, A_n, \mathbb{Z}_n, D_n$) and apply Lagrange's divisibility and index formulas directly without constructing full Cayley tables or explicit coset lists.
