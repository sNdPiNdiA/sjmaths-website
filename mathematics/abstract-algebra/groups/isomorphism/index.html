6. Abstract Algebra

Group isomorphism

A group isomorphism is a bijective group homomorphism that preserves the binary operation, proving that two algebraically identical groups are structurally indistinguishable. In competitive examinations like UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB, isomorphism problems test rapid structural classification of finite and cyclic groups using order properties and generators.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Fundamental Theorem of Group Isomorphism (Cayley's Theorem / Isomorphism Theorems)
Let $(G, \cdot)$ and $(G', *)$ be two groups. A mapping $\phi: G \to G'$ is called a group isomorphism if: (1) $\phi$ is one-to-one (injective), (2) $\phi$ is onto (surjective), and (3) \phi(a \cdot b) = \phi(a) * \phi(b) \text{ } \forall \text{ } a, b \in G$. If such a map exists, $G$ and $G'$ are isomorphic, denoted $G \cong G'$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, an isomorphism acts as a rigid transformation or a relabeling of vertices in a Cayley graph without altering adjacency or path relations between group elements.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

It establishes an equivalence relation among groups, implying that all algebraic properties—such as element orders, center size, subgroup lattices, and solvability—are completely preserved.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Bijectivity (Injectivity + Surjectivity) Domain $G$ to Codomain $G'$ Strictly Mandatory If not injective, multiple elements collapse (kernel is non-trivial), destroying structural uniqueness. If not surjective, the image is a proper subgroup, not the full codomain.
Operation Preservation (Homomorphism property) Binary operations $\cdot$ and $*$ Strictly Mandatory Dropping this condition reduces the mapping to a mere set bijection, ignoring algebraic structure completely.
Identity and Inverse Preservation Group axioms Derived / Necessary consequence Fails to map the neutral element of $G$ to the neutral element of $G'$, violating the definition of a group homomorphism.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Finite Cyclic Groups

$$ \text{Every cyclic group of order } n \text{ is isomorphic to } \mathbb{Z}_n (+) $$

Exam Rule: Never check homomorphisms element-by-element for cyclic groups. If two cyclic groups have the same finite order, they are instantly isomorphic.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT to match cyclic groups with residue classes modulo $n$.
02

Shortcut 2: Infinite Cyclic Groups

$$ \text{Every infinite cyclic group is isomorphic to } (\mathbb{Z}, +) $$

Exam Rule: Any group generated by a single element of infinite order is isomorphic to the additive group of integers.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard conceptual check in KVS PGT and DSSSB.
03

Shortcut 3: Direct Product Isomorphism

$$ \mathbb{Z}_m \times \mathbb{Z}_n \cong \mathbb{Z}_{mn} \iff \gcd(m, n) = 1 $$

Exam Rule: Decompose direct products of cyclic groups instantly by checking coprimality to find isomorphic cyclic counterparts.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct numerical questions in UP TGT/PGT regarding structure of abelian groups.
04

Shortcut 4: Number of Elements of Order $k$

$$ \text{If } G \cong G', \text{ then } \forall k, \text{ number of elements of order } k \text{ in } G \text{ equals that in } G'. $$

Exam Rule: Count element orders. If two groups have a mismatch in the count of elements of any specific order, they cannot be isomorphic.

⚡ PYQ Context: Used to prove $\mathbb{Z}_8$ is not isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_4 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$.
05

Shortcut 5: Center and Inner Structure Invariance

$$ G \cong G' \implies Z(G) \cong Z(G') \text{ and } |G| = |G'| $$

Exam Rule: Compare orders, abelian nature, and sizes of centers to rapidly eliminate non-isomorphic candidate options.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial time-saver in 4-option multiple-choice elimination.
06

Shortcut 6: Klein 4-Group vs Cyclic Group of Order 4

$$ V_4 \not\cong \mathbb{Z}_4 $$

Exam Rule: V_4 has three elements of order 2 and one of order 1, whereas \mathbb{Z}_4 has two elements of order 4. They are never isomorphic despite having equal order 4.

⚡ PYQ Context: A classic trap question in almost all state PGT examinations.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Equal Order Implies Isomorphism

Common Pitfall: Assuming that if two groups have the same finite order $n$, they must be isomorphic.

Counterexample: $|V_4| = 4$ and $|\mathbb{Z}_4| = 4$

Why it fails: Order is only a necessary condition, not a sufficient condition. $\mathbb{Z}_4$ is cyclic, while $V_4$ is non-cyclic.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check element order distributions before declaring groups of the same order isomorphic.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Abelian Property Confusion

Common Pitfall: Assuming homomorphic images preserve non-abelian structure without checking injectivity.

Counterexample: Mapping a non-abelian group $G$ to the trivial group ${e}$ via $\phi(x) = e$.

Why it fails: This is a valid homomorphism but not an isomorphism because it is neither injective nor surjective.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check bijectivity strictly; homomorphisms can collapse large non-abelian groups into abelian or trivial groups.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Determine which of the following groups is isomorphic to the additive group $\mathbb{Z}_6$.

(A) $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_3$(B) $S_3$(C) $\mathbb{Z}_6$ under multiplication modulo 7 (non-zero)(D) Both (A) and (C)
✓ Correct Answer: (D)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Note that $\mathbb{Z}_6$ is cyclic of order 6. Step 2: $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_3$ is cyclic because $\gcd(2,3) = 1$, so it is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_6$. Step 3: Non-zero integers modulo 7 form a cyclic group of order 6 under multiplication, isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_6$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Use $\mathbb{Z}_m \times \mathbb{Z}_n \cong \mathbb{Z}_{mn}$ for coprime moduli, and recall multiplicative group of prime fields $\mathbb{Z}_p^* \cong \mathbb{Z}_{p-1}$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always check coprimality in direct products of cyclic groups.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

How many non-isomorphic abelian groups of order 36 exist?

(A) 3(B) 4(C) 5(D) 6
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Prime factorization of $36 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2$. Step 2: Write partitions of exponents: for 2, partitions are $2$ and $1+1$ (2 partitions); for 3, partitions are $2$ and $1+1$ (2 partitions). Step 3: Multiply partition counts: $2 \times 2 = 4$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Number of abelian groups of order $p^a q^b$ is $P(a) \times P(b)$ where $P(n)$ is the partition function. Here $P(2) \times P(2) = 2 \times 2 = 4$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Master the partition function formula for quick abelian group counting.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
MCQ 1: Which of the following groups is isomorphic to the Klein 4-group $V_4$?
A (A) $\mathbb{Z}_4$
B (B) $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$
C (C) $\mathbb{U}(5)$
D (D) $\mathbb{Z}_5$
Derivation & Analysis:

The Klein 4-group $V_4$ has elements of order 1 and 2. $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ also consists entirely of elements of order 1 and 2 (except identity), making them isomorphic.

Q2
MCQ 2: Let $\phi: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$ be a group homomorphism such that $\phi(1) = 3$. Then $\phi(x)$ is given by:
A (A) $3x$
B (B) $x^3$
C (C) $3^x$
D (D) $x+3$
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $\phi$ is a homomorphism on additive groups, $\phi(nx) = n\phi(x)$. Thus $\phi(x) = \phi(x \cdot 1) = x \phi(1) = 3x$.

Q3
MCQ 3: The number of non-isomorphic groups of order 4 is:
A (A) 1
B (B) 2
C (C) 3
D (D) 4
Derivation & Analysis:

Groups of order 4 must be abelian (since every group of order $p^2$ is abelian). The only possible structures are $\mathbb{Z}_4$ and $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$.

Q4
MCQ 4: If $G$ is a cyclic group of order $n$, then the number of generators of $G$ is given by:
A (A) $n$
B (B) $\phi(n)$ (Euler's totient)
C (C) $n-1$
D (D) $\frac{n}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

A cyclic group of order $n$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_n$, and the number of elements of order $n$ (generators) is $\phi(n)$.

Q5
MCQ 5: Which of the following statements is true for any two isomorphic groups $G$ and $G'$?
A (A) They must be abelian
B (B) They must have the same number of subgroups of order $k$
C (C) One must be cyclic if the other is not
D (D) Their operation tables must be identical without relabeling
Derivation & Analysis:

Isomorphism preserves all structural properties, including the exact lattice of subgroups and number of elements of any given order.

Q6
MCQ 6: The multiplicative group $\mathbb{U}(8) = \{1, 3, 5, 7}$ modulo 8 is isomorphic to:
A (A) $\mathbb{Z}_4$
B (B) $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$
C (C) $\mathbb{Z}_8$
D (D) $S_2$
Derivation & Analysis:

In $\mathbb{U}(8)$, $3^2 \equiv 1$, $5^2 \equiv 1$, $7^2 \equiv 1$. Every non-identity element has order 2. Thus it is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ (Klein 4-group).

Q7
MCQ 7: According to the First Isomorphism Theorem, if $\phi: G \to G'$ is a homomorphism, then $G / \ker(\phi)$ is isomorphic to:
A (A) $G$
B (B) $G'$
C (C) $\text{Im}(\phi)$
D (D) $\ker(\phi)$
Derivation & Analysis:

The First Isomorphism Theorem states that $G / \ker(\phi) \cong \text{Im}(\phi)$.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): Every group of prime order is cyclic. Reason (R): Every cyclic group of prime order $p$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p$.
A (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C (C) (A) is true but (R) is false
D (D) (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

By Lagrange's theorem, elements other than identity must generate the entire group if order is prime, making it cyclic. All cyclic groups of order $p$ are isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p$.

Q9
MCQ 9: The quotient group $\mathbb{Z} / 5\mathbb{Z}$ is isomorphic to which of the following?
A (A) $5\mathbb{Z}$
B (B) $\mathbb{Z}_5$
C (C) $\mathbb{Z}$
D (D) $\mathbb{Z}_{10}$
Derivation & Analysis:

The quotient of the additive group of integers by the subgroup of multiples of 5 produces the cyclic group of order 5, denoted $\mathbb{Z}_5$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $G$ is a group of order $p^2$ where $p$ is prime, then $G$ is necessarily:
A (A) Non-abelian
B (B) Isomorphic to $S_p$
C (C) Abelian
D (D) Simple
Derivation & Analysis:

A classical theorem in group theory states that every group of order $p^2$ is abelian (either $\mathbb{Z}_{p^2}$ or $\mathbb{Z}_p \times \mathbb{Z}_p$).
