Commercial Mathematics

Compound interest

Compound interest models exponential growth of capital where interest is reinvested periodically, governed by discrete exponential sequences or continuous differential equations. Mastery of its algebraic formulation and logarithmic manipulation forms a high-yield scoring pillar in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Compound Interest Growth Principle
If a principal $P$ is invested at an annual nominal rate of interest $r$ compounded $n$ times per year for $t$ years, the accumulated amount $A$ is given by the discrete exponential function $$A = P \left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}$$

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, compound interest represents an exponential curve $y = P(1 + r/n)^{nt}$ that exhibits monotonic non-linear growth with a strictly positive and increasing slope, contrasting with the linear growth characteristic of simple interest.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, the relationship forms a geometric progression where the growth factor per conversion period is $1 + \frac{r}{n}$. Taking logarithms reduces multiplicative compounding to linear equations, facilitating quick analytical solutions for time or rate unknowns.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Doubling Time Rule of 72 / 69.3

$$t \approx \frac{72}{\text{Rate}(\%)} \quad \text{or} \quad t \approx \frac{0.693}{\ln(1+r)}$$

Exam Rule: To find approximate time for money to double at compound interest, divide 72 (or 69.3 for continuous compounding) by the percentage interest rate.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently tested in UP PGT/TGT to bypass tedious logarithmic calculations when estimating doubling periods.
02

Shortcut 2: Multi-Year Amount Ratio Property

$$\frac{A_{t+1}}{A_t} = 1 + r, \quad \frac{A_{t+k}}{A_t} = (1 + r)^k$$

Exam Rule: Amounts accumulated at successive equal time intervals form a geometric progression. The ratio of amounts after $(t+k)$ years to $t$ years depends solely on $k$, independent of $P$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard pattern in KVS and DSSSB where a sum becomes $x$ times in $a$ years and $y$ times in $b$ years.
03

Shortcut 3: Difference Between CI and SI for 2 Years

$$CI - SI = P \left(\frac{r}{100}\right)^2$$

Exam Rule: Direct formula to find principal or rate when the 2-year compound interest and simple interest difference is given.

⚡ PYQ Context: Extremely common 10-second question format in UP TGT and LT Grade exams.
04

Shortcut 4: Difference Between CI and SI for 3 Years

$$CI - SI = P \left(\frac{r}{100}\right)^2 \left(3 + \frac{r}{100}\right)$$

Exam Rule: Immediate extraction of principal or rate for 3-year differential problems without expanding full binomials.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT examinations to differentiate between well-prepared candidates.
05

Shortcut 5: Fractional Time Compounding

$$A = P \left(1 + \frac{r}{100}\right)^a \left(1 + \frac{b}{c} \frac{r}{100}\right)$$

Exam Rule: For time $t = a \frac{b}{c}$ years (where $a$ is integer and $b/c$ is fraction), compound for $a$ years at full rate and simple interest for the fractional remainder.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in recruitment exams to test handling of mixed time periods.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Unit Digits / Modulo Arithmetic

$$A \equiv P(1 + r)^t \pmod{10}$$

Exam Rule: Examine the last digit of the calculated amount using modular arithmetic to eliminate 2 incorrect options instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial time-saving technique for 125-question exams where calculation speed is paramount.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Nominal vs. Effective Annual Rate Confusion

Common Pitfall: Candidates use the nominal rate $r$ directly in annual formulas when compounding occurs semi-annually, quarterly, or monthly without adjusting rate to $r/n$ and time to $nt$.

Counterexample: $r = 12\%$ compounded quarterly for $1$ year calculated as $P(1+0.12)^1$ instead of $P(1+0.03)^4$.

Why it fails: Failing to adjust conversion frequency ignores the higher effective yield generated by more frequent compounding.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always scale the rate by dividing by conversion frequency $n$ and multiply the time exponent by $n$ before calculation.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Simple vs. Compound Interest Formula Overlap

Common Pitfall: Applying linear simple interest formulas ($A = P(1 + rt)$) to compound interest problems or vice versa.

Counterexample: Calculating 3-year compound growth by multiplying rate by 3 ($3r$) instead of compounding factor $(1+r)^3$.

Why it fails: Compound interest incorporates interest-on-interest, making linear approximations underestimate true growth significantly for $t > 1$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check the keyword carefully: 'compounded annually/half-yearly' strictly demands exponential factors.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

A sum of money placed at compound interest triples itself in 4 years. In how many years will it become 27 times itself?

(A) 8 years(B) 12 years(C) 16 years(D) 24 years
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Let principal be $P$. Given $3P = P(1+r)^4 \implies (1+r)^4 = 3$. We need amount to be $27P$: $27P = P(1+r)^t \implies 3^3 = (1+r)^t$. Substituting $(1+r)^4 = 3$: $((1+r)^4)^3 = (1+r)^t \implies (1+r)^{12} = (1+r)^t \implies t = 12$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Since $27 = 3^3$, multiply the base time of 4 years by 3 to get $4 \times 3 = 12$ years instantly.
💡 Key Takeaway: Look for powers of the multiplier: if it becomes $x^k$ times, the time is $k \times$ original time.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

The difference between compound interest and simple interest on a certain sum of money at $5\%$ per annum for 2 years is Rs. 25. Find the principal.

(A) Rs. 5,000(B) Rs. 10,000(C) Rs. 20,000(D) Rs. 25,000
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Using the 2-year difference formula: $CI - SI = P \left(\frac{r}{100}\right)^2$. Given $25 = P \left(\frac{5}{100}\right)^2 = P \left(\frac{1}{20}\right)^2 = \frac{P}{400}$. Thus $P = 25 \times 400 = 10,000$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Apply shortcut $P = \frac{\text{Diff} \times 100^2}{r^2} = \frac{25 \times 10000}{25} = 10,000$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Memorize the 2-year and 3-year difference formulas to solve in under 15 seconds.

Q1
MCQ 1: If a sum amounts to \( \text{Rs. } 4500 \) in 2 years and \( \text{Rs. } 6750 \) in 4 years at compound interest, what is the principal?
A Rs. 2,000
B Rs. 3,000
C Rs. 3,500
D Rs. 4,000
Derivation & Analysis:

Using geometric property \( \frac{A_4}{A_2} = \frac{6750}{4500} = 1.5 = 1+r \). Principal \( P = \frac{A_2}{(1+r)^2} = \frac{4500}{(1.5)^2} = \frac{4500}{2.25} = \text{Rs. } 3,000 \).

Q2
MCQ 2: The compound interest on \( \text{Rs. } 10,000 \) at \( 10\% \) per annum compounded half-yearly for 1 year is:
A Rs. 1,000
B Rs. 1,025
C Rs. 1,050
D Rs. 1,100
Derivation & Analysis:

Half-yearly rate \( r' = 5\% \), time periods \( n = 2 \). Amount \( A = 10000 (1 + \frac{5}{100})^2 = 10000 (1.05)^2 = 10000 \times 1.1025 = 11025 \). Interest \( CI = 11025 - 10000 = \text{Rs. } 1025 \).

Q3
MCQ 3: At what annual rate of compound interest will \( \text{Rs. } 1600 \) amount to \( \text{Rs. } 2500 \) in 2 years?
A 15\%
B 20\%
C 25\%
D 30\%
Derivation & Analysis:

Formula: \( 2500 = 1600 (1 + \frac{r}{100})^2 \implies \frac{25}{16} = (1 + \frac{r}{100})^2 \implies \frac{5}{4} = 1 + \frac{r}{100} \implies \frac{r}{100} = \frac{1}{4} \implies r = 25\% \).

Q4
MCQ 4: A machine depreciates in value at the rate of \( 10\% \) per annum. If its present value is \text{Rs. } 81,000, what was its value 2 years ago?
A Rs. 90,000
B Rs. 95,000
C Rs. 1,00,000
D Rs. 1,05,000
Derivation & Analysis:

Value 2 years ago \( P \) satisfies \( 81000 = P (1 - \frac{10}{100})^2 = P (0.9)^2 = 0.81 P \implies P = \frac{81000}{0.81} = \text{Rs. } 1,00,000 \).

Q5
MCQ 5: The difference between simple and compound interest on a sum for 3 years at \( 10\% \) per annum is \text{Rs. } 31. The sum is:
A Rs. 500
B Rs. 800
C Rs. 1,000
D Rs. 1,200
Derivation & Analysis:

Formula for 3 years: \( CI - SI = P \left(\frac{r}{100}\right)^2 (3 + \frac{r}{100}) \implies 31 = P (\frac{1}{10})^2 (3 + \frac{1}{10}) = P (\frac{1}{100}) (\frac{31}{10}) = \frac{31 P}{1000} \implies P = \text{Rs. } 1,000 \).

Q6
MCQ 6: In how many years will \( \text{Rs. } 8,000 \) amount to \( \text{Rs. } 9,261 \) at \( 5\% \) per annum compound interest?
A 2 years
B 3 years
C 4 years
D 5 years
Derivation & Analysis:

\( 9261 = 8000 (1 + \frac{5}{100})^t \implies \frac{9261}{8000} = (\frac{21}{20})^t \implies (\frac{21}{20})^3 = (\frac{21}{20})^t \implies t = 3 \) years.

Q7
MCQ 7: If the effective rate of interest is \( 10.25\% \) per annum corresponding to a nominal rate compounded semi-annually, the annual nominal rate is:
A 5\%
B 10\%
C 20\%
D 10.25\%
Derivation & Analysis:

Effective rate \( r_{eff} = (1 + \frac{r}{2})^2 - 1 = 0.1025 \implies (1 + \frac{r}{2})^2 = 1.025^2 \implies 1 + \frac{r}{2} = 1.05 \implies \frac{r}{2} = 0.05 \implies r = 10\% \).

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): Compound interest grows faster than simple interest after the first conversion period. Reason (R): Compound interest is calculated on the accumulated amount of previous periods.
A Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
C Assert (A) is true but Reason (R) is false
D Assert (A) is false but Reason (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, compound interest reinvests prior interest, generating exponential growth which always outpaces linear simple interest growth for \( t > 1 \).

Q9
MCQ 9: A sum amounts to \( \text{Rs. } 2400 \) in 3 years and \( \text{Rs. } 3000 \) in 4 years at compound interest. The annual rate of interest is:
A 15\%
B 20\%
C 25\%
D 33.33\%
Derivation & Analysis:

Interest for the 4th year \( = 3000 - 2400 = 600 \). This interest accrued on the amount at year 3 \( (2400) \). Rate \( r = \frac{600}{2400} \times 100 = \frac{1}{4} \times 100 = 25\% \).

Q10
MCQ 10: Under continuous compounding, a principal \( P \) doubles in 10 years. What is the approximate continuous interest rate \( r \)?
A 5\%
B 6.93\%
C 7.2\%
D 10\%
Derivation & Analysis:

Formula: \( 2P = P e^{10r} \implies \ln 2 = 10r \implies 0.6931 = 10r \implies r = 0.06931 \text{ or } 6.93\% \).

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Compound interest exhibits exponential growth because interest is added to the principal at each conversion period, earning further interest. Simple interest remains strictly linear since interest is computed solely on the fixed initial principal.

Failing to adjust the nominal rate and time period when compounding frequency is semi-annual, quarterly, or monthly (e.g., forgetting to divide rate by 2 and multiply time by 2 for semi-annual compounding).

Typically 3 to 5 questions appear across Commercial Mathematics (including compound interest, installment buying, and annuities), serving as reliable, high-scoring areas if shortcuts are applied correctly.

Candidates should leverage the ratio property \( A_{t+k} / A_t = (1+r)^k \), the Rule of 72 for doubling periods, and modular arithmetic to eliminate incorrect options rapidly without full logarithmic expansion.
