Work and time

Work and time problems rely on inverse proportionality between the number of workers, time taken, and the total quantum of work completed. Mastery of unit-work rates ($1/n$) and LCM methods transforms tedious fractions into rapid integer arithmetic, providing a high-scoring edge in UP TGT/PGT examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Principle of Inverse Proportionality and Unit Work Rate
If $n$ persons can complete a task in $d$ days working $h$ hours per day with efficiency $\eta$, then the total work $W$ is invariant and governed by the relation \frac{n_1 d_1 h_1 \eta_1}{W_1} = \frac{n_2 d_2 h_2 \eta_2}{W_2}.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Representing work as a rectangular area where length corresponds to time and height corresponds to rate of working. The total area remains constant for a fixed task.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Establishes a joint variation model $W = k \cdot n \cdot d \cdot h \cdot \eta$, allowing linear and inverse proportion equations to be solved systematically.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Two-Worker Combined Time

$$T = \frac{xy}{x + y}$$

Exam Rule: If A can do work in $x$ days and B in $y$ days, together they finish it in the product divided by the sum.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
02

Shortcut 2: LCM Unit Work Method

$$Total Work = \text{LCM}(x, y, z), \quad Rate_A = \frac{W}{x}$$

Exam Rule: Assume total work is the LCM of given days to avoid fractional arithmetic completely.

⚡ PYQ Context: Extensively used to solve 3-worker and pipe-cistern problems within 30 seconds.
03

Shortcut 3: Men-Days-Hours Chain Rule

$$\frac{M_1 D_1 H_1}{W_1} = \frac{M_2 D_2 H_2}{W_2}$$

Exam Rule: Equate the man-hour-day product divided by respective work units when parameters change.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard recurring question type in KVS, NVS, and UP PGT exams.
04

Shortcut 4: Alternate Days Work Completion

$$2k \text{ days} \implies 2k \times (R_A + R_B) \text{ work}$$

Exam Rule: Calculate work done in a 2-cycle period, then multiply to approach total work before adding remainder days.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently featured in UP TGT exams to test careful tracking of sequence boundaries.
05

Shortcut 5: Efficiency Ratio to Time Ratio

$$Efficiency_A : Efficiency_B = b : a \implies Time_A : Time_B = a : b$$

Exam Rule: Time taken is inversely proportional to efficiency; use proportional parts directly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial for advanced PGT questions involving percentage efficiency comparisons.
06

Shortcut 6: Pipes and Cisterns Negative Work

$$Net Rate = \frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y} - \frac{1}{z}$$

Exam Rule: Treat leakages or outlet pipes as negative rates of work ($-\frac{1}{z}$) in the LCM setup.

⚡ PYQ Context: High-yield application in LT Grade and UP TGT mathematics papers.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Reciprocal Addition for Alternate Days

Common Pitfall: Candidates simply take the combined rate and apply it directly when workers alternate days, ignoring the specific order of who starts.

Counterexample: A takes 10 days, B takes 15 days, working on alternate days starting with A.

Why it fails: The work does not distribute evenly in fractional days; discrete cycles of 2 days must be computed integer-wise.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always map out complete 2-day cycles and calculate remaining work explicitly before assigning single-day increments.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Confusing 'More Efficient' with 'More Time'

Common Pitfall: Assuming a worker who is $50\%$ more efficient takes $50\%$ more time.

Counterexample: Efficiency $\eta_2 = 1.5 \eta_1$

Why it fails: Time is inversely proportional to efficiency: $T_2 = \frac{T_1}{1.5} = \frac{2}{3} T_1$, not $1.5 T_1$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Invert efficiency ratios immediately when converting to time domains.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

A can do a piece of work in 12 days and B in 15 days. They work together for 3 days, then A leaves. How long will B take to finish the remaining work?

(A) 7 days(B) 8 \frac{1}{4} days(C) 9 days(D) 8 days
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Let total work = LCM(12, 15) = 60 units. Step 2: Rate of A = 5 units/day, Rate of B = 4 units/day. Step 3: Combined work in 3 days = $3(5+4) = 27$ units. Step 4: Remaining work = $60 - 27 = 33$ units. Step 5: Time taken by B = $\frac{33}{4} = 8\frac{1}{4}$ days.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
LCM method instantly yields integer rates (5 and 4), avoiding fractions until the final division.
💡 Key Takeaway: Pay close attention to whether the question asks for 'remaining work time' or 'total time elapsed'.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If 12 men or 18 women can reap a field in 14 days, in how many days can 8 men and 16 women reap the same field?

(A) 9 days(B) 10 days(C) 9 \frac{9}{11} days(D) 12 days
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Equate capacities: $12M = 18W \implies 2M = 3W$. Step 2: Convert total work into man-days: Total Work = $12 \times 14$ man-days = 168 man-days. Step 3: Convert target group ($8M + 16W$) into equivalent men: $16W = \frac{16}{3}M$. Total men = $8 + \frac{16}{3} = \frac{40}{3}$ men. Step 4: Time = $\frac{168}{40/3} = \frac{504}{40} = \frac{63}{5} = 12.6$ or check options. Wait, recalculate: $8M + 16W = 8M + 16(2/3M) = (24+32)/3 = 56/3 M$. Days = $12 \times 14 / (56/3) = \frac{12 \times 14 \times 3}{56} = \frac{3 \times 14 \times 3}{14} = 9$ days? Wait, $12 \times 14 = 168$. Equivalent men = $8 + 16 \times (2/3)$? Wait, $12M = 18W \implies M/W = 18/12 = 3/2 \implies W = (2/3)M$. Target: $8M + 16W = 8M + 16(2/3)M = (24+32)/3 M = (56/3)M$. Total Work = $12 \times 14 = 168$. Days = $168 \times 3 / 56 = 3 \times 3 = 9$ days.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Use the equivalence formula: Days = $\frac{\text{Given Men} \times \text{Given Days}}{\text{Target Men} + \text{Target Women} \times \frac{M}{W}}$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always reduce the 'or' condition to a single gender unit before evaluating the 'and' condition.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
MCQ 1: If A and B together can complete a work in 15 days and B alone in 20 days, in how many days can A alone complete the work?
A (A) 45 days
B (B) 60 days
C (C) 35 days
D (D) 50 days
Derivation & Analysis:

Rate of A = $\frac{1}{15} - \frac{1}{20} = \frac{4-3}{60} = \frac{1}{60}$. Thus, A takes 60 days.

Q2
MCQ 2: A is twice as efficient as B, and together they finish a work in 14 days. How many days will A alone take?
A (A) 21 days
B (B) 42 days
C (C) 28 days
D (D) 14 days
Derivation & Analysis:

Efficiencies are $2:1$. Combined units = $2+1 = 3$. Total work = $3 \times 14 = 42$ units. A's time = $\frac{42}{2} = 21$ days.

Q3
MCQ 3: 10 men working 8 hours a day can complete a work in 18 days. How many hours a day must 15 men work to complete the same work in 12 days?
A (A) 6 hours
B (B) 8 hours
C (C) 10 hours
D (D) 12 hours
Derivation & Analysis:

Using $\frac{M_1 D_1 H_1}{W_1} = \frac{M_2 D_2 H_2}{W_2}$: $10 \times 18 \times 8 = 15 \times 12 \times H_2 \implies 1440 = 180 H_2 \implies H_2 = 8$ hours.

Q4
MCQ 4: Two pipes A and B can fill a tank in 20 minutes and 30 minutes respectively. If both pipes are opened simultaneously, find the time taken to fill the tank.
A (A) 12 minutes
B (B) 15 minutes
C (C) 10 minutes
D (D) 25 minutes
Derivation & Analysis:

Using $\frac{xy}{x+y} = \frac{20 \times 30}{20 + 30} = \frac{600}{50} = 12$ minutes.

Q5
MCQ 5: Pipe A fills a tank in 12 hours, while pipe B empties it in 15 hours. If both are opened together in an empty tank, when will the tank be full?
A (A) 50 hours
B (B) 60 hours
C (C) 45 hours
D (D) 55 hours
Derivation & Analysis:

Net rate = $\frac{1}{12} - \frac{1}{15} = \frac{5-4}{60} = \frac{1}{60}$. Time = 60 hours.

Q6
MCQ 6: A contractor undertakes to finish a road in 40 days and employs 100 men. After 24 days, he finds that only $\frac{1}{3}$ of the work has been completed. How many additional men must he employ to finish the work on time?
A (A) 150
B (B) 200
C (C) 100
D (D) 250
Derivation & Analysis:

Remaining work = $\frac{2}{3}$, remaining time = $40 - 24 = 16$ days. Using $\frac{100 \times 24}{1/3} = \frac{M_2 \times 16}{2/3} \implies M_2 = 300$. Additional men = $300 - 100 = 200$.

Q7
MCQ 7: A can do a work in 20 days and B in 30 days. They start together, but A leaves 5 days before the work is completed. In how many days is the total work finished?
A (A) 12 days
B (B) 14 days
C (C) 15 days
D (D) 16 days
Derivation & Analysis:

Let total days be $t$. Work done by A in $(t-5)$ days + Work done by B in $t$ days = 1. $\frac{t-5}{20} + \frac{t}{30} = 1 \implies 3(t-5) + 2t = 60 \implies 5t = 75 \implies t = 15$ days.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): If 5 persons complete a task in 10 days, 10 persons will complete it in 5 days. Reason (R): Number of persons and time taken are inversely proportional when work is constant.
A (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation
C (C) (A) is true but (R) is false
D (D) (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

By inverse variation $M_1 D_1 = M_2 D_2 \implies 5 \times 10 = 10 \times 5 = 50$. Both assertion and reason are mathematically sound.

Q9
MCQ 9: Three men A, B, C can complete a work in 10, 12, and 15 days respectively. They start working together, but A leaves 2 days after the start and B leaves 3 days before the completion of work. How long does the work last?
A (A) 6 days
B (B) 7 days
C (C) 8 days
D (D) 5 days
Derivation & Analysis:

Let total days be $x$. A works for 2 days, B works for $x-3$ days, C works for $x$ days. $\frac{2}{10} + \frac{x-3}{12} + \frac{x}{15} = 1 \implies \frac{1}{5} + \frac{x-3}{12} + \frac{x}{15} = 1$. LCM = 60: $12 + 5(x-3) + 4x = 60 \implies 12 + 5x - 15 + 4x = 60 \implies 9x - 3 = 60 \implies 9x = 63 \implies x = 7$ days.

Q10
MCQ 10: A pump can be used to fill a tank with water in 2 hours. Because of a leak, it took 2 hours and 20 minutes to fill the tank. What is the time taken by the leak to empty the full tank?
A (A) 12 hours
B (B) 14 hours
C (C) 10 hours
D (D) 15 hours
Derivation & Analysis:

Pump rate = $\frac{1}{2}$. Net rate with leak = $\frac{1}{7/3} = \frac{3}{7}$. Leak rate = $\frac{1}{2} - \frac{3}{7} = \frac{7-6}{14} = \frac{1}{14}$. Thus, leak empties in 14 hours.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Fractions introduce calculation fatigue and higher probability of arithmetic errors under exam stress. Assuming total work as the LCM of given days converts all rates into clean integers, speeding up problem-solving by at least 40%.

Students mistakenly average the rates or assume continuous fractional distribution. Because work is completed in discrete blocks (cycles of 2 or more days), remainder work must always be calculated step-by-step.
