8. Vector Calculus

Vector identities

Vector identities form the algebraic bedrock of vector calculus, relating gradient, divergence, and curl through powerful differential operator expansions. Mastery of these identities is essential for solving high-weightage vector differential equations and line/surface/volume integrals frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Fundamental Vector Differential Operator Identities
Let $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ be differentiable vector fields, and let $\phi$ and $\psi$ be differentiable scalar fields in $\mathbb{R}^3$. Then the fundamental differential identities involving $\nabla$ are given by: (1) $\nabla (\phi \psi) = \phi \nabla \psi + \psi \nabla \phi$, (2) $\nabla \cdot (\phi \vec{v}) = \phi (\nabla \cdot \vec{v}) + (\nabla \phi) \cdot \vec{v}$, (3) $\nabla \times (\phi \vec{v}) = \phi (\nabla \times \vec{v}) + (\nabla \phi) \times \vec{v}$, (4) $\nabla \cdot (\vec{u} \times \vec{v}) = \vec{v} \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{u}) - \vec{u} \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{v})$, and (5) $\nabla \times (\vec{u} \times \vec{v}) = (\vec{v} \cdot \nabla)\vec{u} - \vec{v}(\nabla \cdot \vec{u}) - (\vec{u} \cdot \nabla)\vec{v} + \vec{u}(\nabla \cdot \vec{v})$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Vector identities describe how directional derivatives, fluxes, and circulations interact under scalar scaling and cross/dot products. For instance, the curl of a scalar multiple of a vector field decomposes into a circulation scaled by the scalar plus a twist induced by the spatial variation (gradient) of the scalar.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

They act as distributive and associative-like laws for differential operators, allowing complex vector differential expressions to be simplified, transformed, or evaluated without reverting to tedious Cartesian component expansions.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Continuity of First Partial Derivatives ($C^1$ smoothness) Open domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ Strictly Mandatory / Necessary If partial derivatives are discontinuous, mixed partials may fail to commute (Clairaut's theorem fails), invalidating standard curl and divergence identities.
Differentiability of Scalar and Vector Fields Open domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ Strictly Mandatory Application of differential operators $\nabla$, $\nabla \cdot$, and $\nabla \times$ becomes undefined at points where fields possess corner singularities or non-differentiable boundaries.
Twice Differentiability ($C^2$) for Second-Order Identities Open domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ Essential for second-order identities like $\nabla \times (\nabla \phi) = \mathbf{0}$ and $\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{v}) = 0$ Without $C^2$ continuity, mixed partial derivatives fail to be equal, meaning $\frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial x \partial y} \neq \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial y \partial x}$, causing second-order identities to collapse.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Curl of Gradient Vanishing Identity

$$ \nabla \times (\nabla \phi) = \mathbf{0} $$

Exam Rule: The curl of any gradient field is identically zero. Instantly recognize that conservative vector fields have zero curl.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT to test if a given force field is conservative or irrotational.
02

Shortcut 2: Divergence of Curl Vanishing Identity

$$ \nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{v}) = 0 $$

Exam Rule: The divergence of any curl field (solenoidal field) is identically zero. Any vector field expressed as a curl has zero divergence.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in TGT/PGT matching questions regarding solenoidal and irrotational properties.
03

Shortcut 3: Position Vector $\vec{r}$ Differential Rules

$$ \nabla \cdot \vec{r} = 3, \quad \nabla \times \vec{r} = \mathbf{0}, \quad \nabla r = \frac{\vec{r}}{r} $$

Exam Rule: Memorize standard position vector derivatives: $\nabla r^n = n r^{n-2} \vec{r}$, $\nabla \cdot (r^n \vec{r}) = (n+3)r^n$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Extremely high yield in UP PGT and KVS PGT for rapid evaluation of integrals and differential expressions involving $r = |\vec{r}|$.
04

Shortcut 4: Laplacian of Power of $r$

$$ \nabla^2 (r^n) = n(n+1)r^{n-2} $$

Exam Rule: Direct formula for Laplacian of $r^n$. For harmonic functions in 3D, $n = -1$, yielding $\nabla^2 (r^{-1}) = 0$ for $r \neq 0$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly asked in DSSSB and UP PGT examinations.
05

Shortcut 5: Triple Vector Product Expansion (BAC-CAB Rule for Operators)

$$ \nabla \times (\nabla \times \vec{v}) = \nabla (\nabla \cdot \vec{v}) - \nabla^2 \vec{v} $$

Exam Rule: Relates curl of curl to gradient of divergence and vector Laplacian. Essential for electromagnetic wave equations and fluid dynamics.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard theoretical question in PGT vector calculus sections.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Dimension Substitution for Radial Fields

$$ \nabla \cdot (f(r)\vec{r}) = r f'(r) + 3f(r) $$

Exam Rule: Replace $\nabla \cdot (f(r)\vec{r})$ instantly using the formula where 3 corresponds to spatial dimension in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves 2 minutes of component-wise differentiation in competitive exams.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Misapplying Vector Product Order

Common Pitfall: Writing $\nabla \times (\phi \vec{v}) = (\nabla \phi) \times (\nabla \times \vec{v})$ or forgetting that $\nabla$ acts on both terms.

Counterexample: $(\nabla \phi) \times \vec{v} \neq \phi (\nabla \times \vec{v})$

Why it fails: The product rule for vector differential operators distributes across components; missing a term leads to wrong evaluation.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Treat $\nabla$ as both a vector and a differential operator; use index notation ($\epsilon_{ijk}$) if in doubt.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Ignoring Singularity at the Origin for Radial Powers

Common Pitfall: Assuming $\nabla^2 (r^{-1}) = 0$ everywhere without checking $r = 0$.

Counterexample: $\nabla^2 \left(\frac{1}{r}\right) = -4\pi \delta(\vec{r})$

Why it fails: At $r = 0$, $1/r$ is undefined, and its Laplacian gives a Dirac delta distribution, not zero everywhere.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always note whether the domain includes the origin $r = 0$ when dealing with negative powers of $r$.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If $\vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$ and $r = |\vec{r}|$, find the value of $\nabla \cdot (r^5 \vec{r})$.

(A) $5r^5$(B) $8r^5$(C) $3r^5$(D) $5r^3$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Use the identity $\nabla \cdot (f(r)\vec{r}) = r f'(r) + 3f(r)$. Step 2: Set $f(r) = r^5$, so $f'(r) = 5r^4$. Step 3: Substitute: $r(5r^4) + 3(r^5) = 5r^5 + 3r^5 = 8r^5$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Apply direct shortcut formula $\nabla \cdot (r^n \vec{r}) = (n+3)r^n$. Here $n=5$, so $(5+3)r^5 = 8r^5$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Memorize $\nabla \cdot (r^n \vec{r}) = (n+3)r^n$ to solve in under 10 seconds.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Evaluate $\nabla \times (\phi \nabla \phi)$ where $\phi(x,y,z)$ is a differentiable scalar field.

(A) $\phi \nabla \phi$(B) $\mathbf{0}$(C) $\nabla^2 \phi$(D) $(\nabla \phi) \times \vec{r}$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Expand using curl product rule: $\nabla \times (\phi \nabla \phi) = \phi \nabla \times (\nabla \phi) + (\nabla \phi) \times (\nabla \phi)$. Step 2: Since $\nabla \times (\nabla \phi) = \mathbf{0}$ and cross product of a vector with itself $(\nabla \phi) \times (\nabla \phi) = \mathbf{0}$, the result is $\mathbf{0}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Curl of any gradient is zero, and cross product of identical vectors is zero.
💡 Key Takeaway: Watch for self-cross products in vector operator expressions.

A (A) $\nabla^2 \phi$

Derivation & Analysis:

The curl of the gradient of any scalar field $\phi$ is identically zero vector $\mathbf{0}$.

Q4
MCQ 4: If $\vec{a}$ is a constant vector and $\vec{r}$ is the position vector, then $\nabla \cdot (\vec{a} \times \vec{r})$ is:
A (A) $0$
B (B) $\vec{a}$
C (C) $2\vec{a}$
D (D) $-\vec{a}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using identity $\nabla \cdot (\vec{u} \times \vec{v}) = \vec{v} \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{u}) - \vec{u} \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{v})$, here $\vec{u} = \vec{a}$ (constant, so curl is zero) and $\vec{v} = \vec{r}$ (curl is zero). Thus $\nabla \cdot (\vec{a} \times \vec{r}) = 0$.

Q5
MCQ 5: The value of $\nabla^2 (\ln r)$ where $r = |\vec{r}|$ in 3 dimensions is:
A (A) $\frac{1}{r^2}$
B (B) $\frac{1}{r}$
C (C) $0$
D (D) $-\frac{1}{r^2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using $\nabla^2 (r^n) = n(n+1)r^{n-2}$ is for power functions. For $\ln r$, $\nabla (\ln r) = \frac{\vec{r}}{r^2}$, and its divergence gives $\nabla \cdot \left(\frac{\vec{r}}{r^2}\right) = \frac{1}{r^2}$.

Q6
MCQ 6: For any vector field $\vec{v}$, the vector identity for $\nabla \times (\nabla \times \vec{v})$ is:
A (A) $\nabla (\nabla \cdot \vec{v}) - \nabla^2 \vec{v}$
B (B) $\nabla^2 \vec{v} - \nabla (\nabla \cdot \vec{v})$
C (C) $\nabla \times \vec{v} - \nabla^2 \vec{v}$
D (D) $\mathbf{0}$
Derivation & Analysis:

This is the standard vector triple product expansion for differential operators: $\nabla \times (\nabla \times \vec{v}) = \nabla (\nabla \cdot \vec{v}) - \nabla^2 \vec{v}$.

Q7
MCQ 7: If $\vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$, then $\nabla \cdot \vec{r}$ equals:
A (A) $0$
B (B) $1$
C (C) $2$
D (D) $3$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\nabla \cdot \vec{r} = \frac{\partial x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial z}{\partial z} = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3$.

Q8
MCQ 8: If $\phi$ and $\psi$ are scalar fields, then $\nabla \cdot (\phi \nabla \psi)$ is equal to:
A (A) $\phi \nabla^2 \psi + \nabla \phi \cdot \nabla \psi$
B (B) $\phi \nabla \psi + \psi \nabla \phi$
C (C) $\nabla \phi \cdot \nabla \psi$
D (D) $\phi \nabla^2 \psi$
Derivation & Analysis:

Applying product rule for divergence: $\nabla \cdot (\phi \nabla \psi) = \phi (\nabla \cdot \nabla \psi) + (\nabla \phi) \cdot \nabla \psi = \phi \nabla^2 \psi + \nabla \phi \cdot \nabla \psi$.

Q9
MCQ 9: The curl of a constant vector field $\vec{c}$ is:
A (A) $\vec{c}$
B (B) $3\vec{c}$
C (C) $\mathbf{0}$
D (D) Undefined
Derivation & Analysis:

Since all partial derivatives of a constant vector field are zero, its curl is identically the zero vector $\mathbf{0}$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $\vec{v}$ is a solenoidal vector field, then:
A (A) $\nabla \cdot \vec{v} = 0$
B (B) $\nabla \times \vec{v} = \mathbf{0}$
C (C) $\nabla^2 \vec{v} = 0$
D (D) $\vec{v} = \mathbf{0}$
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, a solenoidal vector field has zero divergence, i.e., $\nabla \cdot \vec{v} = 0$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Do not memorize them by rote. Relate them to standard scalar calculus product rules (e.g., derivative of product, chain rule) and vector algebra cross/dot product expansions. Treating $\nabla$ as a formal vector greatly aids in recalling BAC-CAB and product rules.

An irrotational field has zero curl ($\nabla \times \vec{v} = \mathbf{0}$) and can be expressed as the gradient of a scalar potential. A solenoidal field has zero divergence ($\nabla \cdot \vec{v} = 0$) and can be expressed as the curl of a vector potential.

B (B) $\mathbf{0}$
C (C) $1$
D (D) $\nabla \phi$
Derivation & Analysis:

The curl of the gradient of any scalar field $\phi$ is identically zero vector $\mathbf{0}$.

Q4
MCQ 4: If $\vec{a}$ is a constant vector and $\vec{r}$ is the position vector, then $\nabla \cdot (\vec{a} \times \vec{r})$ is:
A (A) $0$
B (B) $\vec{a}$
C (C) $2\vec{a}$
D (D) $-\vec{a}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using identity $\nabla \cdot (\vec{u} \times \vec{v}) = \vec{v} \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{u}) - \vec{u} \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{v})$, here $\vec{u} = \vec{a}$ (constant, so curl is zero) and $\vec{v} = \vec{r}$ (curl is zero). Thus $\nabla \cdot (\vec{a} \times \vec{r}) = 0$.

Q5
MCQ 5: The value of $\nabla^2 (\ln r)$ where $r = |\vec{r}|$ in 3 dimensions is:
A (A) $\frac{1}{r^2}$
B (B) $\frac{1}{r}$
C (C) $0$
D (D) $-\frac{1}{r^2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using $\nabla^2 (r^n) = n(n+1)r^{n-2}$ is for power functions. For $\ln r$, $\nabla (\ln r) = \frac{\vec{r}}{r^2}$, and its divergence gives $\nabla \cdot \left(\frac{\vec{r}}{r^2}\right) = \frac{1}{r^2}$.

Q6
MCQ 6: For any vector field $\vec{v}$, the vector identity for $\nabla \times (\nabla \times \vec{v})$ is:
A (A) $\nabla (\nabla \cdot \vec{v}) - \nabla^2 \vec{v}$
B (B) $\nabla^2 \vec{v} - \nabla (\nabla \cdot \vec{v})$
C (C) $\nabla \times \vec{v} - \nabla^2 \vec{v}$
D (D) $\mathbf{0}$
Derivation & Analysis:

This is the standard vector triple product expansion for differential operators: $\nabla \times (\nabla \times \vec{v}) = \nabla (\nabla \cdot \vec{v}) - \nabla^2 \vec{v}$.

Q7
MCQ 7: If $\vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$, then $\nabla \cdot \vec{r}$ equals:
A (A) $0$
B (B) $1$
C (C) $2$
D (D) $3$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\nabla \cdot \vec{r} = \frac{\partial x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial z}{\partial z} = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3$.

Q8
MCQ 8: If $\phi$ and $\psi$ are scalar fields, then $\nabla \cdot (\phi \nabla \psi)$ is equal to:
A (A) $\phi \nabla^2 \psi + \nabla \phi \cdot \nabla \psi$
B (B) $\phi \nabla \psi + \psi \nabla \phi$
C (C) $\nabla \phi \cdot \nabla \psi$
D (D) $\phi \nabla^2 \psi$
Derivation & Analysis:

Applying product rule for divergence: $\nabla \cdot (\phi \nabla \psi) = \phi (\nabla \cdot \nabla \psi) + (\nabla \phi) \cdot \nabla \psi = \phi \nabla^2 \psi + \nabla \phi \cdot \nabla \psi$.

Q9
MCQ 9: The curl of a constant vector field $\vec{c}$ is:
A (A) $\vec{c}$
B (B) $3\vec{c}$
C (C) $\mathbf{0}$
D (D) Undefined
Derivation & Analysis:

Since all partial derivatives of a constant vector field are zero, its curl is identically the zero vector $\mathbf{0}$.

Q10
MCQ 10: If $\vec{v}$ is a solenoidal vector field, then:
A (A) $\nabla \cdot \vec{v} = 0$
B (B) $\nabla \times \vec{v} = \mathbf{0}$
C (C) $\nabla^2 \vec{v} = 0$
D (D) $\vec{v} = \mathbf{0}$
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, a solenoidal vector field has zero divergence, i.e., $\nabla \cdot \vec{v} = 0$.
