4. Coordinate Geometry of Two Dimensions

Classification as hyperbola

The general equation of second degree in two variables represents a hyperbola if and only if the discriminant determinant $\Delta \neq 0$ and the invariant condition $h^2 > ab$ holds. Mastering this algebraic classification is a high-yield scoring zone in UP TGT/PGT, DSSSB, and KVS examinations.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Discriminant Condition for Hyperbola
The general homogeneous and non-homogeneous second-degree equation given by $$ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$$ represents a hyperbola if the determinant $$\Delta = \begin{vmatrix} a & h & g \\ h & b & f \\ g & f & c \end{vmatrix} \neq 0$$ and the second-degree coefficient condition satisfies $h^2 > ab$. Furthermore, if $h^2 > ab$ and $a + b \neq 0$, it represents a non-rectangular hyperbola; if $h^2 > ab$ and $a + b = 0$, it represents a rectangular hyperbola.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, the condition $h^2 > ab$ ensures that the cutting plane intersects both nappes of a double right-circular cone, producing two unbounded, disjoint open branches opening in opposite directions. The asymptotes of the hyperbola have slopes given by the quadratic equation in slope form $bm^2 + 2hm + a = 0$.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebriacally, the transformation of coordinates via rotation and translation eliminates the cross-product term $2hxy$, converting the general quadratic form into its canonical diagonalized principal-axis form where the squared terms have opposite signs.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Non-Zero Determinant ($\Delta \neq 0$) Global Determinant Condition Strictly Mandatory / Necessary If $\Delta = 0$, the second-degree equation degenerates into a pair of intersecting straight lines, representing a degenerate conic rather than a proper hyperbola.
Discriminant Inequality ($h^2 > ab$) Coefficient Inequality Strictly Mandatory If $h^2 = ab$, the conic becomes a parabola; if $h^2 < ab$, it becomes an ellipse (or circle/imaginary ellipse).
Real Coefficients ($a, b, h, g, f, c \in \mathbb{R}$) Real Domain Essential for real locus If coefficients are complex or violate real locus constraints, the hyperbola may become imaginary (locus of no real points).

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Rapid Hyperbola Test via Determinant Expansion

$$\Delta = abc + 2fgh - af^2 - bg^2 - ch^2 \neq 0 \quad \text{and} \quad h^2 > ab$$

Exam Rule: Quickly evaluate $\Delta$ and $h^2 - ab$. If $\Delta \neq 0$ and $h^2 > ab$, instantly mark hyperbola.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT to identify conic sections from a general degree-2 equation.
02

Shortcut 2: Asymptote Slopes Formula

$$m_1, m_2 = \frac{-h \pm \sqrt{h^2 - ab}}{b} \quad (b \neq 0)$$

Exam Rule: The slopes of the two asymptotes of the hyperbola are immediately found using the roots of $bm^2 + 2hm + a = 0$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly applied in DSSSB PGT and KVS PGT questions asking for angle between asymptotes or asymptote equations.
03

Shortcut 3: Rectangular Hyperbola Condition

$$a + b = 0 \quad \text{with} \quad h^2 > ab$$

Exam Rule: If the sum of coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ is zero ($a + b = 0$), the hyperbola is rectangular (asymptotes perpendicular).

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard recurring question type in UP TGT/PGT.
04

Shortcut 4: Center of the Hyperbola

$$x_0 = \frac{hf - bg}{ab - h^2}, \quad y_0 = \frac{gh - af}{ab - h^2}$$

Exam Rule: Obtained by partially differentiating the general equation with respect to $x$ and $y$ and solving the linear system $\frac{\partial S}{\partial x} = 0$ and $\frac{\partial S}{\partial y} = 0$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves 2 minutes of cumbersome substitution during exam time.
05

Shortcut 5: Eccentricity Formula from Coefficients

$$e = \sqrt{\frac{2\sqrt{(a-b)^2 + 4h^2}}{(a+b) \pm \sqrt{(a-b)^2 + 4h^2}}}$$

Exam Rule: Direct relation to compute eccentricity directly from general equation parameters without shifting origin.

⚡ PYQ Context: Asked in high-level PGT screening tests.
06

Shortcut 6: Elimination via Degeneracy Check

$$\Delta = 0 \implies \text{Degenerate Conic (Lines)}$$

Exam Rule: Always check $\Delta = 0$ first. If zero, eliminate all proper conic options (ellipse, parabola, hyperbola) instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Eliminates two options in under 10 seconds.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Forgetting the Determinant Non-Zero Condition

Common Pitfall: Candidates check only $h^2 > ab$ and conclude it is a hyperbola, ignoring whether $\Delta = 0$.

Counterexample: $xy = 0$

Why it fails: Here $a=0, b=0, h=1/2$, so $h^2 > ab$ ($1/4 > 0$), but $\Delta = 0$, meaning it represents a pair of intersecting lines (axes), not a hyperbola.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always compute $\Delta$ alongside $h^2 - ab$ before concluding.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Mistaking Rectangular Hyperbola Definition

Common Pitfall: Assuming $xy = c^2$ is the only form of rectangular hyperbola and missing general rotated forms where $a+b=0$.

Counterexample: $x^2 - y^2 + 4xy - 2x + 4y - 5 = 0$

Why it fails: Here $a=1, b=-1$, so $a+b=0$. $h=2$, $h^2 > ab$ ($4 > -1$), and $\Delta \neq 0$. This is a rotated rectangular hyperbola.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check $a+b=0$ and $h^2 > ab$ together.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

If $\Delta = 0$, the second-degree equation factors into two linear equations representing intersecting straight lines. Although $h^2 > ab$ holds for intersecting lines, they form a degenerate conic, not a proper hyperbola.

A rectangular hyperbola requires both $h^2 > ab$ and $a + b = 0$, which implies that its asymptotes intersect at right angles ($90^\circ$). A general hyperbola only requires $h^2 > ab$ without the orthogonal coefficient sum restriction.

Typically 2 to 4 questions directly test second-degree equation classification, asymptote properties, or eccentricity in UP TGT and PGT examinations.

First calculate $h^2 - ab$. If it is positive, check if the determinant $\Delta$ is non-zero. This eliminates parabolas and ellipses immediately and distinguishes proper hyperbolas from intersecting lines.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

If $\Delta = 0$, the second-degree equation factors into two linear equations representing intersecting straight lines. Although $h^2 > ab$ holds for intersecting lines, they form a degenerate conic, not a proper hyperbola.

A rectangular hyperbola requires both $h^2 > ab$ and $a + b = 0$, which implies that its asymptotes intersect at right angles ($90^\circ$). A general hyperbola only requires $h^2 > ab$ without the orthogonal coefficient sum restriction.

Typically 2 to 4 questions directly test second-degree equation classification, asymptote properties, or eccentricity in UP TGT and PGT examinations.

First calculate $h^2 - ab$. If it is positive, check if the determinant $\Delta$ is non-zero. This eliminates parabolas and ellipses immediately and distinguishes proper hyperbolas from intersecting lines.
