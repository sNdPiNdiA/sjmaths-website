4. Coordinate Geometry of Two Dimensions

Chord of contact

The chord of contact is the straight line joining the points of contact of two tangents drawn from an external point to a conic section. Its equation ($T = 0$) is identical in form to the tangent equation, serving as a vital high-scoring algebraic tool in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Equation of Chord of Contact
Let $P(x_1, y_1)$ be an external point relative to a conic section represented by the general second-degree equation $S = ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$. The equation of the chord of contact joining the points of contact of the tangents drawn from $P$ to the conic is given by $T = 0$, where $$T = xx_1 + yy_1 + g(x + x_1) + f(y + y_1) + c = 0$$ (for a circle $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$, it reduces to $xx_1 + yy_1 - r^2 = 0$).

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, the chord of contact is a unique linear chord whose endpoints are precisely the points where the tangent lines originating from the external point $M(x_1, y_1)$ touch the conic boundary. If the point $M$ lies outside the conic, two real tangents can be drawn, making the chord of contact a real line intersecting the conic at two distinct points.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebrically, the expression $T = 0$ provides a linear polar-like relation derived from the polarity property of conics. It establishes a dual correspondence between points in the plane and lines (poles and polars), where an external point acts as the pole whose polar is the chord of contact.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Chord of Contact for Standard Circle

$$xx_1 + yy_1 = r^2$$

Exam Rule: For any circle $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ and point $(x_1, y_1)$, the chord of contact is instantly written by replacing $x^2$ with $xx_1$, $y^2$ with $yy_1$, $x$ with $\frac{x+x_1}{2}$, and $y$ with $\frac{y+y_1}{2}$.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
02

Shortcut 2: Length of Chord of Contact

$$L = \frac{2 \cdot \sqrt{S_1} \cdot r}{\sqrt{r^2 + d^2}} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{2L_t \cdot r}{\sqrt{r^2 + L_t^2}}$$

Exam Rule: Where $S_1 = x_1^2 + y_1^2 - r^2$ (power of the point) and $r$ is the radius. For a parabola $y^2 = 4ax$, length is $\frac{\sqrt{(y_1^2 - 4ax_1)(y_1^2 + 4a^2)}}{a}$ simplified via normal forms.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequent numerical problems in UP TGT asking for the exact length of the chord intercepted on a circle or parabola.
03

Shortcut 3: Area of Triangle Formed by Chord of Contact and Tangents

$$ \text{Area} = \frac{(S_1)^{3/2}}{2r} \quad \text{(for circle } x^2 + y^2 = r^2\text{)} $$

Exam Rule: The area enclosed between the two tangents from $(x_1, y_1)$ and their chord of contact is given directly by the power of the point raised to the power $3/2$ divided by twice the radius.

⚡ PYQ Context: High-yield formula for TGT/PGT questions where lengthy vertex-finding is bypassed.
04

Shortcut 4: Equation of the Pair of Tangents

$$SS_1 = T^2$$

Exam Rule: The combined equation of the two tangents drawn from $(x_1, y_1)$ to any conic $S = 0$ is always given by $SS_1 = T^2$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in KVS and DSSSB PGT mathematics to find joint equations without finding individual slopes.
05

Shortcut 5: Pole-Polar Reciprocity for Conics

$$xx_1 + yy_1 = a^2 \quad \text{(Ellipse)}$$

Exam Rule: If a line $lx + my + n = 0$ touches the conic, or if we need the pole of a given line with respect to a conic, use the comparison of coefficients: $\frac{x_1}{l/a^2} = \frac{y_1}{m/b^2} = \frac{-1}{n}$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard matching and calculation questions in UP PGT coordinate geometry sections.
06

Shortcut 6: Midpoint Chord shortcut

$$T = S_1$$

Exam Rule: The equation of a chord of a conic bisected at a given point $(x_1, y_1)$ is always $T = S_1$. Do not confuse this with the chord of contact ($T = 0$), which uses an external point.

⚡ PYQ Context: A classic trap in UP TGT/PGT exams where options feature both $T=0$ and $T=S_1$ for midpoint vs contact problems.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Chord of Contact ($T=0$) with Midpoint Chord ($T=S_1$)

Common Pitfall: Candidates apply the formula $T = 0$ when the problem states that $(x_1, y_1)$ is the *midpoint* of the chord, instead of the point from which tangents are drawn.

Counterexample: \text{For } x^2 + y^2 = a^2 \text{ and midpoint } (x_1, y_1), \text{ equation is } xx_1 + yy_1 = x_1^2 + y_1^2.

Why it fails: Using $T = 0$ for a midpoint chord yields an incorrect line that does not pass through the given midpoint correctly.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Read the phrasing carefully: 'tangents drawn from' $\implies T = 0$; 'bisected at' $\implies T = S_1$.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Applying $T=0$ for Internal Points

Common Pitfall: Assuming $T = 0$ represents a chord of contact even when the point $(x_1, y_1)$ is inside the conic ($S_1 < 0$).

Counterexample: \text{Point } (0,0) \text{ inside } x^2 + y^2 = r^2

Why it fails: No tangents can be drawn from an internal point, hence no points of contact exist. However, $T=0$ still produces a valid line (the polar line), but it does not intersect the curve at points of contact.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check $S_1 > 0$ before interpreting $T=0$ as the physical chord joining tangent points.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the equation of the chord of contact of tangents drawn from the point $(2, 5)$ to the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 9$.

(A) $2x + 5y = 9$(B) $2x + 5y = 3$(C) $5x + 2y = 9$(D) $2x - 5y = 9$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Identify circle equation $S = x^2 + y^2 - 9 = 0$ and point $(x_1, y_1) = (2, 5)$. Step 2: Apply chord of contact formula $T = xx_1 + yy_1 - r^2 = 0$. Step 3: Substitute values: $x(2) + y(5) - 9 = 0 \implies 2x + 5y = 9$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Direct substitution into $xx_1 + yy_1 = r^2$: $2x + 5y = 9$. Match with option (A) in 10 seconds.
💡 Key Takeaway: Check if the point is external: $2^2 + 5^2 - 9 = 4 + 25 - 9 = 20 > 0$. Valid external point!
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If the chord of contact of tangents from a point $P$ to the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$ passes through the fixed point $(x_2, y_2)$, then the point $P$ lies on:

(A) A straight line(B) A circle(C) A parabola(D) An ellipse
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Let $P = (h, k)$. Chord of contact with respect to $y^2 = 4ax$ is $yk = 2a(x + h)$. Since it passes through $(x_2, y_2)$, we have $yk_2 = 2a(x_2 + h)$, which is linear in $h$ and $k$. Thus, the locus of $P(h, k)$ is a straight line.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Linearity of the chord of contact equation with respect to pole coordinates establishes a dual linear relationship (Polarity), proving the locus is a straight line.
💡 Key Takeaway: Remember: The pole of a moving line is a point, and the locus of the pole of a line passing through a fixed point is a straight line (polar).

Q1
MCQ 1: The equation of the chord of contact of the circle $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$ from the origin $(0,0)$ is:
A (A) $gx + fy + c = 0$
B (B) $gx + fy = 0$
C (C) $gx + fy - c = 0$
D (D) $xx_1 + yy_1 + c = 0$
Derivation & Analysis:

Substitute $(x_1, y_1) = (0,0)$ into $T = xx_1 + yy_1 + g(x+x_1) + f(y+y_1) + c = 0$, yielding $gx + fy + c = 0$.

Q2
MCQ 2: For the ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$, the chord of contact from $(x_1, y_1)$ is given by:
A (A) $\frac{xx_1}{a^2} + \frac{yy_1}{b^2} = 0$
B (B) $\frac{xx_1}{a^2} + \frac{yy_1}{b^2} = 1$
C (C) $\frac{xx_1}{a^2} - \frac{yy_1}{b^2} = 1$
D (D) $xx_1 + yy_1 = a^2b^2$
Derivation & Analysis:

Standard application of $T = 0$ for an ellipse yields $\frac{xx_1}{a^2} + \frac{yy_1}{b^2} = 1$.

Q3
MCQ 3: If the chord of contact of tangents from $(h, k)$ to the circle $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$ subtends a right angle at the origin, then:
A (A) $h^2 + k^2 = 2a^2$
B (B) $h^2 + k^2 = a^2$
C (C) $h^2 + k^2 = 4a^2$
D (D) $h^2 + k^2 = \sqrt{2}a^2$
Derivation & Analysis:

Homogenizing the circle equation using the chord of contact $hx + ky = a^2$ leads to the condition for perpendicular lines at the origin, giving $h^2 + k^2 = 2a^2$.

Q4
MCQ 4: The length of the chord of contact of tangents drawn from $(5, 4)$ to the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 16$ is:
A (A) $\frac{40}{\sqrt{41}}$
B (B) $\frac{20}{\sqrt{41}}$
C (C) $\frac{10}{\sqrt{41}}$
D (D) $\frac{5}{\sqrt{41}}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using formula $L = \frac{2r\sqrt{S_1}}{\sqrt{r^2 + d^2}}$ or geometric triangle relations. Here $r=4$, $d = \sqrt{5^2+4^2} = \sqrt{41}$, $S_1 = 25+16-16 = 25$. Length $L = \frac{2(4)(5)}{\sqrt{41}} = \frac{40}{\sqrt{41}}$.

Q5
MCQ 5: What is the chord of contact of the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$ with respect to the focus $(a, 0)$?
A (A) $x + a = 0$
B (B) $x - a = 0$
C (C) $y = 0$
D (D) $x = 0$
Derivation & Analysis:

For the focus $(a,0)$, $y(0) = 2a(x + a) \implies x + a = 0$ (which is the directrix of the parabola).

Q6
MCQ 6: If the line $lx + my = n$ is the chord of contact of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$ with respect to the point $(x_1, y_1)$, then $(x_1, y_1)$ is:
A (A) $\left(\frac{-a^2l}{n}, \frac{-a^2m}{n}\right)$
B (B) $\left(\frac{a^2l}{n}, \frac{a^2m}{n}\right)$
C (C) $\left(\frac{n}{al}, \frac{n}{bm}\right)$
D (D) $(la^2, ma^2)$
Derivation & Analysis:

Comparing $xx_1 + yy_1 = a^2$ with $lx + my = n \implies \frac{lx}{n} + \frac{my}{n} = 1$, we get $x_1 = \frac{a^2 l}{n}$ and $y_1 = \frac{a^2 m}{n}$.

Q7
MCQ 7: The chord of contact of a point $P$ with respect to a hyperbola $x^2 - y^2 = a^2$ passes through $(x_0, y_0)$. The locus of $P$ is:
A (A) A circle
B (B) A parabola
C (C) A straight line
D (D) A hyperbola
Derivation & Analysis:

Since the chord of contact equation $T = 0$ is linear in the coordinates of the pole $P$, the locus of $P$ when the chord passes through a fixed point is always a straight line.

Q8
MCQ 8: Assertion (A): The chord of contact from any point on the directrix of a circle to the circle subtends a right angle at the centre. Reason (R): The angle between tangents from any point on the directrix is $90^\circ$.
A (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
B (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation
C (C) (A) is true but (R) is false
D (D) (A) is false but (R) is true
Derivation & Analysis:

Direct geometric property of circles: director circle of $x^2+y^2=r^2$ is $x^2+y^2=2r^2$. Points on the director circle have orthogonal tangents, making the chord of contact subtend $90^\circ$ at the center.

Q9
MCQ 9: The area of the triangle formed by the tangents drawn from $(3, 4)$ to the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ and their chord of contact is:
A (A) $\frac{128 \sqrt{2}}{25}$
B (B) $\frac{64 \sqrt{2}}{25}$
C (C) $\frac{32}{25}$
D (D) $\frac{256}{25}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using formula $\text{Area} = \frac{r (S_1)^{3/2}}{r^2 + d^2}$ or $\frac{RL^3}{2(R^2+L^2)}$. Here $r=1$, $S_1 = 3^2+4^2-1 = 24$, giving $\frac{1(24 \sqrt{24})}{2(1+25)} = \frac{48\sqrt{6}}{52}$ (using standard triangle area derivation for conics).

Q10
MCQ 10: If the chord of contact of tangents from $(x_1, y_1)$ to $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$ touches the circle $x^2 + y^2 = b^2$, then $(x_1, y_1)$ lies on:
A (A) $xx_1 + yy_1 = \frac{a^2}{b}$
B (B) $x^2 + y^2 = \frac{a^4}{b^2}$
C (C) $x^2 + y^2 = a^2 b^2$
D (D) $x^2 + y^2 = \frac{b^4}{a^2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

The chord of contact is $xx_1 + yy_1 = a^2$. Its distance from the origin is $\frac{a^2}{\sqrt{x_1^2 + y_1^2}}$. Since it touches $x^2+y^2=b^2$, this distance equals $b$. Thus $\frac{a^2}{\sqrt{x_1^2+y_1^2}} = b \implies x_1^2 + y_1^2 = \frac{a^4}{b^2}$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

A tangent ($T=0$) applies when the point lies *on* the curve ($S_1=0$). A chord of contact ($T=0$) applies when the point lies *outside* the curve ($S_1>0$) and represents the line joining the points of tangency. A chord bisected at a point ($T=S_1$) applies when the given point is the *midpoint* of the chord itself.

Examiners deliberately test algebraic memory by phrasing questions similarly ('chord with respect to point' vs 'chord bisected at point'). Memorizing the condition $S_1=0$ (on), $S_1>0$ (external), and midpoint property prevents this trap.

Coordinate geometry constitutes roughly 20-25% of the total 125 questions in UP PGT/TGT Mathematics. Tangents, normals, polars, and chords of contact account for 3 to 5 direct questions every year.

Avoid finding the intersection points explicitly (which leads to messy quadratic equations). Instead, use the perpendicular distance from the center to the chord of contact and apply Pythagoras theorem in the right-angled triangle formed by the radius, distance to point, and half-chord.
