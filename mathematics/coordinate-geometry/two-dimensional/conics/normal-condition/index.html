Condition for a Line to be Normal to a Conic

The condition for a given line to act as a normal to a conic section is derived by enforcing orthogonality between the line vector and the tangent vector at the point of contact. Mastery of these algebraic conditions forms a high-yield scoring zone in UP TGT/PGT, DSSSB, and KVS examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Condition for Line to be Normal to Conic
Let the conic be given by the general second-degree equation $$ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$$ and let the given line be $$lx + my + n = 0$$. The line is normal to the conic if and only if the pole of the line with respect to the conic lies on the polar line, or equivalently, when the equation of the normal at any point $(x_1, y_1)$ matches the given line under proportional comparison.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

A normal to a conic at a given point is a straight line passing through that point perpendicular to the tangent line of the conic at the same point. Geometrically, the condition ensures that the directional vector of the given line is parallel to the outward or inward gradient vector of the conic surface at the foot of the normal.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, matching the coefficients of the computed normal equation with the given line $lx + my + n = 0$ yields a system of simultaneous equations in coordinates of the point of contact and parameters of the conic, leading to condition determinants.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Normal to Parabola $y^2 = 4ax$

$$c = -am - 2am^3$$

Exam Rule: The line $y = mx + c$ is a normal to the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$ if and only if $c = -am - am^3$ (or in general form $al^3m + 2al^2n + m^3n = 0$).

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT for finding the equation or parameter of normals.
02

Shortcut 2: Normal to Ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$

$$ \frac{a^2}{l^2} + \frac{b^2}{m^2} = \frac{(a^2 - b^2)^2}{n^2} $$

Exam Rule: The line $lx + my + n = 0$ is a normal to the ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ if the condition $\frac{a^2}{l^2} + \frac{b^2}{m^2} = \frac{(a^2-b^2)^2}{n^2}$ is satisfied.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequent appearance in KVS PGT and DSSSB math papers for parameter elimination.
03

Shortcut 3: Normal to Rectangular Hyperbola $xy = c^2$

$$ct^4 - x_0 t^3 + y_0 t - c = 0$$

Exam Rule: The normals from a point $(x_0, y_0)$ to $xy = c^2$ give a 4th-degree polynomial in parameter $t$, implying up to 4 concurrent normals.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard question in UP TGT regarding the sum of parameters of concurrent normals.
04

Shortcut 4: Concurrency of Normals

$$ \sum t_i = 0 $$

Exam Rule: For a parabola, if three normals drawn from a point $(h, k)$ meet the curve at parameters $t_1, t_2, t_3$, then $t_1 + t_2 + t_3 = 0$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly asked in UP PGT to find locus or properties of orthogonal normals.
05

Shortcut 5: Slope Form for Ellipse Normal

$$ y = mx \mp \frac{(a^2 - b^2)m}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 m^2}} $$

Exam Rule: The line $y = mx + c$ is normal to $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ when $c^2 = \frac{(a^2 - b^2)^2 m^2}{a^2 + b^2 m^2}$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Time-saving formula for matching options in multiple-choice questions.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Dimensional Elimination

$$ [l]^2 [n]^{-2} \text{ scaling check} $$

Exam Rule: Check homogeneity of parameters in options. Conditions for normality must be homogeneous in coefficients of the line when scaled appropriately.

⚡ PYQ Context: Eliminate 2 incorrect options instantly in exam.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Tangent and Normal Conditions

Common Pitfall: Applying the tangent condition $c^2 = a^2m^2 + b^2$ directly to normal problems.

Counterexample: Ellipse condition $c^2 = \frac{(a^2-b^2)^2 m^2}{a^2 + b^2 m^2}$ vs $c^2 = a^2m^2 + b^2$

Why it fails: Tangency deals with intersection multiplicity of 2, whereas normality incorporates perpendicularity of slope $-a^2/b^2 \cdot m = -1$.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Memorize the squared difference $(a^2 - b^2)^2$ numerator for ellipse/hyperbola normals.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Ignoring Sign Conventions in Parabola Normals

Common Pitfall: Writing the normal condition for $y^2 = -4ax$ using formulas derived for $y^2 = 4ax$.

Counterexample: Replacing $a$ with $-a$ in $c = -am - am^3$

Why it fails: Failing to adjust orientation signs leads to incorrect sign matching in options (A) and (B).

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always derive or verify via parametric coordinates $(at^2, 2at)$ if unsure about standard forms.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the condition that the straight line $lx + my + n = 0$ may be a normal to the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$.

(A) al^3 + 2alm^2 + ml^2n = 0(B) al^3 + 2alm^2 + m^2n = 0(C) al^3 + 2al^2m + mn^2 = 0(D) al^3 + 2alm^2 + mn^2 = 0
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write parametric normal equation $y = -tx + 2at + at^3$. Step 2: Compare with $lx + my + n = 0$ to get $t = -l/m$ and $n = -2al - a(l^3/m^2)$. Step 3: Simplify to get $al^3 + 2alm^2 + m^2n = 0$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Substitute standard slope form or use dimensional homogeneity check on options.
💡 Key Takeaway: Standard bookwork result; memorize coefficient structures for parabs/ellipses.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If the line $x - y + c = 0$ is a normal to the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$, then the value of $c$ is:

(A) $3a$(B) $-3a$(C) $2a$(D) $-2a$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Here $m = -1$ and $l = 1, n = c$. Using the normal condition $al^3 + 2alm^2 + m^2n = 0$, substitute $l=1, m=-1$: $a(1)^3 + 2a(1)(-1)^2 + (-1)^2 c = 0 \implies a + 2a + c = 0 \implies c = -3a$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Direct application of $c = -am - am^3$ with $m = -1$ gives $c = -a(-1) - a(-1)^3 = a + a = 2a$? Wait, using slope-intercept form $y = x + c$, $m=1$, so $c = -a(1) - a(1)^3 = -2a$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Be extremely careful with line coefficients $l, m, n$ versus slope-intercept form $y = mx + c$.

Q2
MCQ 2: If $y = 2x + c$ is a normal to $y^2 = 8x$, here $a = 2, m = 2$. The value of $c$ is:
A (A) $-10$
B (B) $-12$
C (C) $-18$
D (D) $-20$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using $c = -am - am^3$ with $a=2, m=2$: $c = -2(2) - 2(2^3) = -4 - 2(8) = -4 - 16 = -20$. Wait, checking: formula $c = -am - 2am^3$ or $c = -am - am^3$? For $y^2 = 4ax$, normal is $y = mx - am - am^3$. Here $4a = 8 \implies a = 2$. $c = -2(2) - 2(2)^3 = -4 - 16 = -20$.

Q3
MCQ 3: The number of normals that can be drawn from an external point to a parabola is:
A (A) 1
B (B) 2
C (C) 3
D (D) 4
Derivation & Analysis:

The normal equation in $t$ is cubic ($at^3 + (2a-x)t + y = 0$), yielding at most 3 real normals.

Q5
MCQ 5: If the normals at three points $t_1, t_2, t_3$ on a parabola meet at a point, then $t_1 + t_2 + t_3$ equals:
A (A) 1
B (B) -1
C (C) 0
D (D) $2a$
Derivation & Analysis:

From the cubic in $t$, the coefficient of $t^2$ is zero, hence the sum of roots $t_1 + t_2 + t_3 = 0$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Parametric coordinates (like $(at^2, 2at)$ for parabolas or $(a \cos \theta, b \sin \theta)$ for ellipses) reduce a two-variable implicit curve equation into a single parameter $t$ or $\theta$, making algebraic comparisons and degree determinations much simpler.

Confusing the condition of tangency with the condition of normality. Candidates frequently use $c^2 = a^2m^2 + b^2$ when the question asks for a normal line, ignoring the required orthogonal slope transformation.

Coordinate geometry typically accounts for 20% to 25% of the total 125 questions in UP PGT Mathematics, with direct applications of tangents, normals, and poles/polars appearing in 3 to 5 questions.

Check for dimensional homogeneity and degree matching of the line coefficients ($l, m, n$) and conic parameters ($a, b$). Conditions for conics like ellipses must be symmetric or correctly weighted by $a^2$ and $b^2$.
