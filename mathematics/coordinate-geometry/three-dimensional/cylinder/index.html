5. Three-Dimensional Geometry

Cylinder

A cylinder in three-dimensional coordinate geometry is generated by a moving straight line (generatrix) that remains parallel to a fixed direction and intersects a given fixed curve (guiding curve). Mastering equations of right circular cylinders, enveloping cylinders, and tangent planes forms a high-weightage scoring zone in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Equation of a Right Circular Cylinder
Let the axis of a right circular cylinder be given by the symmetric equations \frac{x-x_1}{l} = \frac{y-y_1}{m} = \frac{z-z_1}{n} where l^2+m^2+n^2=1$, and let $r$ be its constant radius. The perpendicular distance from any point $P(x,y,z)$ on the surface of the cylinder to the axis is identically equal to $r$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

The surface is traced in 3D space by revolving a line parallel to the axis at a fixed constant perpendicular distance $r$. Cross-sections perpendicular to the axis are congruent circles of radius $r$.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Translates spatial geometric locus conditions into a second-degree algebraic equation in three variables $(x, y, z)$ lacking constant term and mixed linear-quadratic anomalies when properly centered.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Right Circular Cylinder with Axis along Coordinate Axes

$$x^2 + y^2 = r^2 \quad (\text{Axis } z\text{-axis})$$

Exam Rule: If the axis coincides with a coordinate axis, the equation simply drops the coordinate corresponding to the axis and equates the sum of squares of the other two coordinates to $r^2$.

⚡ PYQ Context: undefined
02

Shortcut 2: Radius from General Equation of Right Circular Cylinder

$$a(x^2+y^2+z^2) + 2ux + 2vy + 2wz + d = 0 \implies \text{Conditions: } u^2+v^2+w^2 > ad, \text{ and coefficients of } x^2, y^2, z^2 \text{ must be equal}.$$

Exam Rule: Equate coefficients of $x^2, y^2, z^2$ first. If unequal, it cannot represent a right circular cylinder. Use symmetry to extract radius rapidly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard identification problem in DSSSB and KVS PGT exams.
03

Shortcut 3: Cylinder with Given Generatrix and Guiding Curve

$$\text{Eliminate } (a, b) \text{ from } \frac{x-x_1}{l} = \frac{y-y_1}{m} = \frac{z-z_1}{n} \text{ and guiding curve } f(a,b)=0, g(a,b)=0$$

Exam Rule: Use point-direction form of the moving generator line passing through $(a,b,0)$ or guiding curve points, then eliminate parameters.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct locus problems in UP PGT Mathematics.
04

Shortcut 4: Enveloping Cylinder Equation

$$S S_1 = T^2$$

Exam Rule: For a sphere $S = 0$ and parallel rays with direction ratios $(l, m, n)$, the enveloping cylinder is found using standard tangent conditions or polar plane relations.

⚡ PYQ Context: Scoring questions in senior grade recruitment tests (LT Grade / UP PGT).
05

Shortcut 5: Axis Length and Foot of Perpendicular

$$d = \frac{|Ax_1 + By_1 + Cz_1 + D|}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2 + C^2}}$$

Exam Rule: Distance from external point to axis gives radius $r$ instantly when constructing cylinder through a given point parallel to a line.

⚡ PYQ Context: Numerical calculation shortcuts in NVS/KVS exams.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Option Elimination via Homogeneity

$$Degree of second degree terms = 2$$

Exam Rule: A cylinder equation representing lines parallel to a fixed line has its highest degree homogeneous part factorizable into linear factors corresponding to parallel planes or degenerate forms.

⚡ PYQ Context: Eliminating incorrect options in 125-question PGT papers.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Unequal Coefficients of Quadratic Terms

Common Pitfall: Assuming any second-degree equation in $x, y, z$ with $x^2, y^2, z^2$ terms represents a right circular cylinder.

Counterexample: $2x^2 + 3y^2 + 2z^2 = 5$

Why it fails: The coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ or $z^2$ must be strictly equal for a right circular cylinder. Here it represents an ellipsoid.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check coefficient equality of $x^2, y^2, z^2$ before testing radius conditions.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Ignoring Axis Direction Cosine Normalization

Common Pitfall: Using raw direction ratios $(l, m, n)$ instead of actual direction cosines when calculating perpendicular distance $r$.

Counterexample: \text{Using } l=2, m=3, n=6 \text{ without dividing by } \sqrt{2^2+3^2+6^2}=7

Why it fails: Results in dimensional scaling error, yielding incorrect radius calculations.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always ensure $l^2+m^2+n^2 = 1$ when applying perpendicular distance formulas involving line directions.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Find the equation of the right circular cylinder whose axis is the line $\frac{x}{1} = \frac{y}{2} = \frac{z}{2}$ and whose radius is $3$.

(A) $4x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 4xy - 4yz - 2dx + \dots = 45$(B) $5x^2 + 5y^2 + 5z^2 - 4xy - 4yz - 4zx = 27$(C) $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 9$(D) $3x^2 + 4y^2 = 25$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Let $P(x,y,z)$ be any point on the cylinder. Step 2: Compute the length of the projection of vector joining origin to $P$ on the axis line. Step 3: Use Pythagoras theorem: $r^2 = OP^2 - \text{(projection)}^2$. Step 4: Simplify to get $5(x^2+y^2+z^2) - 4(xy+yz+zx) = 27$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Apply distance from point to line formula: $d^2 = \frac{(y_2-y_1)^2 + \dots}{\sum l^2} = 3^2 = 9$, expand and match coefficients.
💡 Key Takeaway: Be extremely careful with cross-product expansions in distance formulas to avoid sign slips.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

The radius of the circular cylinder $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - yz - zx - xy = 1$ whose axis is given by $x = y = z$ is:

(A) $1$(B) $\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}$(C) $\sqrt{2}$(D) $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Identify the axis line $x=y=z$ with direction ratios $(1,1,1)$. Step 2: Normalize direction cosines to $\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)$. Step 3: Compute distance from any point on surface to axis. Step 4: Alternatively, compare with standard right circular cylinder forms to find $r=\sqrt{2}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize that for symmetric axis $x=y=z$, the quadratic form reduces cleanly yielding $r^2 = 2$, hence $r=\sqrt{2}$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Symmetric axes simplify cross-product terms significantly in examination settings.

Q1
The equation $x^2 + 2y^2 + z^2 - 2xy - 2yz = 7$ represents:
A A sphere
B A right circular cylinder
C A cone
D An ellipsoid
Derivation & Analysis:

Checking the determinant of the second-degree homogeneous part and evaluating rank and cofactor conditions confirms it represents a cylinder.

Q2
What is the radius of the right circular cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 9$?
A $3$
B $9$
C $6$
D $3\sqrt{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

The equation $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ represents a right circular cylinder along the z-axis with radius $r = \sqrt{9} = 3$.

Q3
The axis of the right circular cylinder given by $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 2xy - 2yz - 2zx + 9 = 0$ is parallel to:
A x-axis
B y-axis
C The line $x = y = z$
D z-axis
Derivation & Analysis:

Symmetric appearance of cross terms $-2xy - 2yz - 2zx$ indicates the axis is aligned symmetrically along the line $x = y = z$.

Q4
If the guiding curve of a cylinder is $x^2 + y^2 = a^2, z = 0$ and generators are parallel to the z-axis, its equation is:
A $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$
B $x^2 + z^2 = a^2$
C $y^2 + z^2 = a^2$
D $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = a^2$
Derivation & Analysis:

Since generators are parallel to the z-axis, the coordinate $z$ is entirely free, yielding $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$.

Q5
The enveloping cylinder of the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = a^2$ with generators parallel to the x-axis has the equation:
A $y^2 + z^2 = a^2$
B $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$
C $x^2 + z^2 = a^2$
D $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 2a^2$
Derivation & Analysis:

Generators parallel to the x-axis mean $x$ is eliminated or free in the tangent cylinder projection, resulting in $y^2 + z^2 = a^2$.

Q6
The degree of the homogeneous equation representing a general cylinder in 3D Cartesian coordinates is:
A 1
B 2
C 3
D Depends on guiding curve
Derivation & Analysis:

A general cylinder formed by second-degree surfaces or lines results in a second-degree equation in $x, y, z$.

Q7
A cylinder whose generators are perpendicular to its guiding plane is called:
A Oblique cylinder
B Right cylinder
C Enveloping cylinder
D Cone
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, a cylinder whose generatrix is perpendicular to the plane of the base/guiding curve is a right cylinder.

Q8
The number of arbitrary constants in the general equation of a right circular cylinder is:
A 5
B 6
C 7
D 4
Derivation & Analysis:

Determined by direction ratios of axis (2 independent), point on axis (2 independent), radius (1), and orientation constraints.

Q9
If $l, m, n$ are direction cosines of the axis of a right circular cylinder, then the relation satisfied by them is:
A $l + m + n = 1$
B $l^2 + m^2 + n^2 = 1$
C $lm + mn + nl = 0$
D $l = m = n$
Derivation & Analysis:

The fundamental property of direction cosines in 3D geometry is that the sum of their squares equals unity ($l^2 + m^2 + n^2 = 1$).

Q10
The projection of a cylinder on a plane parallel to its axis is:
A A circle
B A pair of parallel lines
C A point
D An ellipse
Derivation & Analysis:

Projecting parallel lines along the generator direction onto a parallel plane yields a pair of parallel bounding lines.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

A sphere has equal coefficients of $x^2, y^2, z^2$ and no product terms ($xy, yz, zx$). A right circular cylinder has equal coefficients of squared terms with specific discriminant relations among product terms. A cone is a purely homogeneous second-degree equation in $x, y, z$.

Failing to normalize direction ratios $(l, m, n)$ to direction cosines when computing perpendicular distances, leading to incorrect radius calculations.

Typically 3 to 5 questions are directly asked from the sub-topics of cones and cylinders, making this a high-yield area for securing top rank.

Memorize standard equations where axes align with coordinate axes and use symmetry properties to eliminate incorrect options instantly.
