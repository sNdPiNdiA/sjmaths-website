Statistics

Index Numbers

Index numbers are specialized statistical measures designed to quantify relative changes in a variable or a group of variables over time or across geographical regions. Mastering price and quantity relatives, consistency tests (time reversal, factor reversal, and circular tests), and weighted aggregates (Laspeyres, Paasche, Fisher, and Marshall-Edgeworth) is crucial for scoring high in UP TGT/PGT, DSSSB, and KVS competitive examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Fisher's Ideal Index Number and Consistency Tests
Fisher's Ideal Index is defined as the geometric mean of Laspeyres' and Paasche's index numbers, expressed as $$P_{01}^F = \sqrt{P_{01}^L \times P_{01}^P} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum p_1 q_0}{\sum p_0 q_0} \times \frac{\sum p_1 q_1}{\sum p_0 q_1}}$$. It uniquely satisfies both the Time Reversal Test and the Factor Reversal Test.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

It represents a balanced geometric averaging of base-weighted and current-weighted price movements, minimizing both upward and downward proportional biases inherent in unilateral index formulations.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, it satisfies the Time Reversal Test ($P_{01} \times P_{10} = 1$) and the Factor Reversal Test ($P_{01} \times Q_{01} = \frac{\sum p_1 q_1}{\sum p_0 q_0}$), making it the supreme algebraic standard among all index number formulas.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Fisher from Laspeyres and Paasche

$$P_{01}^F = \sqrt{P_{01}^L \times P_{01}^P}$$

Exam Rule: If Laspeyres index is given as $120$ and Paasche index as $108$, Fisher index is simply $\sqrt{120 \times 108} = \sqrt{12960} \approx 113.84$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked direct numerical evaluation in UP PGT and KVS PGT exams.
02

Shortcut 2: Time Reversal Test Condition

$$P_{01} \times P_{10} = 1$$

Exam Rule: To check if an index satisfies the Time Reversal Test, interchange $0$ and $1$ in the formula and multiply; if the product is identically $1$, the test passes.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard theoretical multiple-choice question in DSSSB PGT Mathematics.
03

Shortcut 3: Factor Reversal Test Condition

$$P_{01} \times Q_{01} = \frac{\sum p_1 q_1}{\sum p_0 q_0}$$

Exam Rule: Multiplying the price index by the corresponding quantity index (interchanging $p$ and $q$) must equal the true value ratio.

⚡ PYQ Context: Tested in UP TGT assertions and reasons.
04

Shortcut 4: Marshall-Edgeworth Weighting Structure

$$P_{01}^{ME} = \frac{\sum p_1(q_0 + q_1)}{\sum p_0(q_0 + q_1)}$$

Exam Rule: Uses arithmetic mean of base and current quantities as weights. Acts as a close approximation to Fisher's Ideal Index with simpler computation.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct formula identification in LT Grade examinations.
05

Shortcut 5: Cost of Living Index (Family Budget Method)

$$I = \frac{\sum R W}{\sum W}, \quad \text{where } R = \frac{p_1}{p_0} \times 100, \, W = p_0 q_0$$

Exam Rule: When price relatives $R$ and base expenditures $W = p_0 q_0$ are provided, weighted average of relatives yields the exact Laspeyres cost of living index in fewer steps.

⚡ PYQ Context: Numerical problem-solving in UP PGT.
06

Shortcut 6: Circular Test Property

$$P_{01} \times P_{12} \times P_{20} = 1$$

Exam Rule: Laspeyres, Paasche, and Fisher indices do NOT satisfy the circular test. Simple geometric mean of price relatives and Walsh index satisfy it.

⚡ PYQ Context: High-yield conceptual trap in competitive teaching exams.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Confusing Laspeyres and Paasche Weighting Baselines

Common Pitfall: Candidates mix up base quantities ($q_0$) with current quantities ($q_1$) when recalling formula denominators.

Counterexample: Laspeyres uses $\sum p_0 q_0$, whereas Paasche uses $\sum p_0 q_1$.

Why it fails: Using current weights for base index or vice versa results in completely wrong numerical values and incorrect option selection.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Mnemonic: 'L' for Laspeyres uses 'L'ast year quantities ($q_0$); 'P' for Paasche uses 'P'resent year quantities ($q_1$).
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Assuming Fisher Index Satisfies Circular Test

Common Pitfall: Assuming that because Fisher's Ideal Index satisfies Time and Factor Reversal tests, it must also satisfy the Circular Test.

Counterexample: $P_{01}^F \times P_{12}^F \times P_{20}^F \neq 1$

Why it fails: Fisher index depends on fixed base periods in its component structures and fails transitiveness across multiple sequential periods.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Remember: Only the simple geometric mean of price relatives and fixed-weight index with constant weights satisfy the circular test.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

If the Laspeyres index number is $110$ and the Paasche index number is $121$, find Fisher's Ideal Index number.

(A) $110$(B) $115$(C) $121$(D) $132$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Write Fisher's formula $P_F = \sqrt{P_L \times P_P}$. Step 2: Substitute given values $P_L = 110$ and $P_P = 121$. Step 3: Compute $P_F = \sqrt{110 \times 121} = \sqrt{13310} \approx 115.37$, closest to $115$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Recognize that Fisher is the geometric mean lying between 110 and 121; check square roots: $115^2 = 13225$, which aligns closely with $13310$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Direct formula application accounts for 40% of index number questions in UP PGT.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

Which of the following index numbers satisfies both the Time Reversal Test and the Factor Reversal Test?

(A) Laspeyres' Index(B) Paasche's Index(C) Fisher's Ideal Index(D) Marshall-Edgeworth Index
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Evaluate each index against test criteria. Laspeyres and Paasche fail both reversal tests due to weight asymmetry. Fisher's index satisfies both identically.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Direct theoretical recall: Fisher = Ideal = Reversal Test satisfaction.
💡 Key Takeaway: Frequent theoretical question in DSSSB and KVS PGT exams.
