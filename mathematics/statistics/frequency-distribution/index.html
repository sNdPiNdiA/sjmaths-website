Statistics

Frequency distribution

A frequency distribution systematically organizes raw statistical data by assigning frequencies to distinct values or class intervals, forming the bedrock of descriptive statistics. Mastery of discrete and continuous frequency distributions, cumulative frequencies, and class boundaries is vital for scoring high in UP TGT/PGT, KVS, and DSSSB examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Principle of Grouped Frequency Distribution & Cumulative Aggregation
Let a set of $N$ observations be partitioned into $k$ mutually exclusive and exhaustive classes $[l_i, u_i)$ with respective frequencies $f_i$ such that $\sum_{i=1}^{k} f_i = N$. The cumulative frequency $F_i$ corresponding to the $i$-th class is defined as $F_i = \sum_{j=1}^{i} f_j$ for "less than" type and $F_i^* = \sum_{j=i}^{k} f_j$ for "more than" type distributions.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Represented graphically via histograms, frequency polygons, and ogives (cumulative frequency curves), where the intersection of the 'less than' and 'more than' ogives precisely determines the median of the distribution.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Facilitates compressed algebraic computation of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and dispersion (variance, standard deviation) without processing raw data arrays.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Median Calculation via Ogive Intersection

$$ M_d = L + \left( \frac{\frac{N}{2} - C_f}{f} \right) \times h $$

Exam Rule: Identify median class where cumulative frequency exceeds $N/2$, then apply direct proportional interpolation.

⚡ PYQ Context: Directly tested in UP PGT/TGT to find missing frequencies given the median.
02

Shortcut 2: Mode Calculation from Modal Class

$$ M_o = L + \left( \frac{f_1 - f_0}{2f_1 - f_0 - f_2} \right) \times h $$

Exam Rule: Locate maximum frequency $f_1$, preceding $f_0$, succeeding $f_2$, and plug into the ratio.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequent numerical questions in KVS and DSSSB PGT Mathematics.
03

Shortcut 3: Step-Deviation Method for Mean

$$ \bar{x} = A + \left( \frac{\sum f_i u_i}{\sum f_i} \right) \times h, \quad u_i = \frac{x_i - A}{h} $$

Exam Rule: Choose assumed mean $A$ at the mid-frequency class to simplify arithmetic coefficients to small integers.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves calculation time in lengthy data sets during time-bound exams.
04

Shortcut 4: Combined Mean of Frequency Distributions

$$ \bar{x}_{12} = \frac{N_1\bar{x}_1 + N_2\bar{x}_2}{N_1 + N_2} $$

Exam Rule: Weighted average of subgroup means weighted by their respective total frequencies $N_1$ and $N_2$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard recurring pattern in UP TGT exams.
05

Shortcut 5: Empirical Relationship between Mean, Median, and Mode

$$ \text{Mode} = 3(\text{Median}) - 2(\text{Mean}) $$

Exam Rule: For moderately skewed distributions, use this exact linear identity to find one missing central tendency measure when two are known.

⚡ PYQ Context: Extremely high yield; appears in almost every alternate UP PGT paper.
06

Shortcut 6: Change of Origin and Scale for Variance

$$ \sigma_u^2 = \frac{1}{h^2} \sigma_x^2 \implies \sigma_x^2 = h^2 \sigma_u^2 $$

Exam Rule: Variance is independent of change of origin ($A$) but scales quadratically with change of scale ($h$).

⚡ PYQ Context: Trick questions testing conceptual clarity in property-based MCQs.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Inclusive vs. Exclusive Class Intervals

Common Pitfall: Failing to convert inclusive series (e.g., $10-19, 20-29$) into exclusive series ($9.5-19.5, 19.5-29.5$) before calculating median, mode, or continuity-dependent statistics.

Counterexample: \text{Classes: } 10-19, 20-29 \implies \text{True limits: } 9.5-19.5, 19.5-29.5, \text{with } h = 10, \text{not } 9.

Why it fails: Using upper limit of inclusive class directly as boundary corrupts the lower-limit $L$ and class width $h$ in interpolation formulas.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always check if the upper limit of one class matches the lower limit of the next. If not, apply continuity correction factor $\pm 0.5$.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Confusing Cumulative Frequency with Class Frequency

Common Pitfall: Using cumulative frequency values $C_f$ directly as $f_i$ in arithmetic mean or variance summation formulas.

Counterexample: \text{Given } C_f: 5, 12, 25 \implies \text{Actual } f: 5, 7, 13.

Why it fails: Summing cumulative frequencies double-counts previous observations, leading to grossly inflated mean values.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Check table headers. If terms are increasing monotonically up to $N$, they are cumulative frequencies; subtract consecutive terms to recover $f_i$.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

In a frequency distribution, if the mean is $26.8$ and the median is $27.9$, find the approximate mode using the empirical relation.

(A) $30.1$(B) $28.5$(C) $25.6$(D) $29.2$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Recall empirical formula $\text{Mode} = 3(\text{Median}) - 2(\text{Mean})$. Step 2: Substitute values: $\text{Mode} = 3(27.9) - 2(26.8)$. Step 3: Compute $83.7 - 53.6 = 30.1$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Mental calculation: $3 \times 28 \approx 84$, minus $53.6 \approx 30.1$. Match option (A).
💡 Key Takeaway: Direct formula substitution question; do not waste time deriving.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

For a continuous frequency distribution, the lower limit of the median class is $20$, frequency of median class is $10$, cumulative frequency of preceding class is $12$, and total frequency $N = 50$, with class width $h = 10$. Find the median.

(A) $23$(B) $25$(C) $27$(D) $29$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Identify parameters: $L = 20, N/2 = 25, C_f = 12, f = 10, h = 10$. Step 2: Apply formula $M_d = L + \frac{\frac{N}{2} - C_f}{f} h$. Step 3: $M_d = 20 + \frac{25 - 12}{10} \times 10 = 20 + 13 = 33$. Wait, let's re-verify: $N=50 \implies N/2 = 25$. If $C_f = 12$, $25-12 = 13$, $13/10 \times 10 = 13$, $20+13 = 33$. Let's adjust options: (A) 31, (B) 33, (C) 35, (D) 37. Correct option (B) 33.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Cancel $h$ and $f$ directly when class width equals frequency denominator magnitude.
💡 Key Takeaway: Ensure $N/2$ is computed correctly from total frequency sum.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Click on your selected answer to receive immediate feedback and unlock the step-by-step mathematical derivation:

Q1
If the sum of frequencies $\sum f_i = 50$ and $\sum f_i x_i = 1250$, the mean $\bar{x}$ is:
A $20$
B $25$
C $30$
D $35$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum f_i x_i}{\sum f_i} = \frac{1250}{50} = 25$. Option (B) is correct.

Q2
The curve obtained by plotting cumulative frequencies against upper class boundaries is called:
A Histogram
B Frequency polygon
C Ogive
D Frequency curve
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, the cumulative frequency curve is termed an Ogive.

Q3
In a moderately skewed distribution, if Mean = $20$ and Mode = $26$, then Median is:
A $21$
B $22$
C $23$
D $24$
Derivation & Analysis:

Using $\text{Mode} = 3\text{Median} - 2\text{Mean} \implies 26 = 3M_d - 2(20) \implies 26 + 40 = 3M_d \implies 66 = 3M_d \implies M_d = 22$.

Q4
If each observation in a frequency distribution is multiplied by $3$, the variance of the new distribution:
A Remains unchanged
B Gets multiplied by $3$
C Gets multiplied by $9$
D Gets divided by $3$
Derivation & Analysis:

Variance scales quadratically with scale change: $\sigma_{new}^2 = 3^2 \sigma_{old}^2 = 9 \sigma_{old}^2$.

Q5
Class marks of a frequency distribution are given as $15, 25, 35, 45$. The class interval corresponding to class mark $25$ with uniform width $10$ is:
A $20 - 30$
B $15 - 25$
C $25 - 35$
D $10 - 20$
Derivation & Analysis:

Class interval is $[x_i - h/2, x_i + h/2] = [25 - 5, 25 + 5] = [20, 30]$.

Q6
The sum of deviations of all values $x_i$ from their arithmetic mean $\bar{x}$, weighted by frequencies $f_i$, is always:
A One
B Positive
C Zero
D Negative
Derivation & Analysis:

By algebraic property of mean, $\sum f_i(x_i - \bar{x}) = 0$.

Q7
For a symmetrical frequency distribution, which of the following holds true?
A $\text{Mean} < \text{Median} < \text{Mode}$
B $\text{Mean} > \text{Median} > \text{Mode}$
C $\text{Mean} = \text{Median} = \text{Mode}$
D $\text{Median} = \frac{\text{Mean} + \text{Mode}}{2}$
Derivation & Analysis:

In a perfectly symmetrical distribution, all three measures of central tendency coincide at the center.

Q8
If the total frequency $N = 100$, the median corresponds to the cumulative frequency value of:
A $25$
B $50$
C $75$
D $100$
Derivation & Analysis:

Median divides the total frequency into two equal halves, corresponding to $N/2 = 50$.

Q9
Which graphical representation is best suited for determining the Mode visually?
A Less than ogive
B More than ogive
C Histogram
D Frequency polygon
Derivation & Analysis:

The mode can be located graphically from the highest rectangle in a histogram using cross-diagonal lines.

Q10
If the coefficient of variation is $50$ and mean is $20$, the standard deviation is:
A $5$
B $10$
C $15$
D $20$
Derivation & Analysis:

$\text{CV} = \frac{\sigma}{\bar{x}} \times 100 \implies 50 = \frac{\sigma}{20} \times 100 \implies \sigma = 10$.

Pillar 8 Frequently Asked Questions

Inclusive series have gaps between the upper limit of one class and the lower limit of the next (e.g., $10-19$ and $20-29$). Converting them to exclusive series ($9.5-19.5$ and $19.5-29.5$) ensures proper class boundaries and accurate median/mode interpolation.

Candidates often forget that variance scales with the square of the class width ($h^2$) and is unaffected by the choice of assumed mean (change of origin).
