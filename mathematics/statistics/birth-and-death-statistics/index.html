Statistics

Statistics of birth and death

The statistics of birth and death involve vital demographic indices such as crude birth and death rates, age-specific fertility rates, and mortality tables, which are fundamental for population projection. In competitive examinations like UP PGT/TGT Mathematics, questions frequently test analytical formulae involving expectation of life, central mortality rates, and force of mortality. Mastering these relations provides a high-scoring edge under strict time constraints.

Target Exams: UP PGT Mathematics UP TGT Mathematics DSSSB PGT / TGT KVS / NVS PGT LT Grade
📖 Self-Study Mastery Status
○ Mark Topic as Mastered

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Demographic Vital Rates and Force of Mortality Principle
Let $N(t)$ denote the population size at time $t$, $B(t)$ the number of births, and $D(t)$ the number of deaths in $[t, t+dt)$. The force of mortality $\mu_x$ at age $x$ is defined as the instantaneous rate of mortality per unit of population at exact age $x$, given by $l_x \mu_x = -\frac{d}{dx} l_x$, where $l_x$ represents the number of survivors at age $x$ out of an initial cohort $l_0$.

📐 Geometric Interpretation

The survivorship curve $l_x$ versus age $x$ represents a strictly non-increasing function starting at $l_0$ and terminating at $l_w = 0$, where $w$ is the limiting age. The area under the survivorship curve yields the total number of person-years lived by the cohort.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Establishes differential equation models for population dynamics and actuarial mathematics, connecting probability density functions of human lifetime with empirical mortality tables.

Pillar 2 Hypotheses & Condition Checklist

Every hypothesis is strictly non-negotiable. Inspect the domain type and the exact consequence if a condition fails:

Hypothesis / Condition Domain / Interval Necessity What Fails If Condition Dropped
Survivorship Continuity and Differentiability [0, w] (Cohort lifespan interval) Strictly Mandatory / Necessary Discontinuities in $l_x$ imply instantaneous catastrophic cohort losses which violate standard demographic smoothness assumptions.
Non-negativity of Force of Mortality \mu_x \ge 0 \quad \forall x \in [0, w] Strictly Mandatory Negative mortality rates violate probability axioms and imply population resurrection.
Initial Cohort Normalization l_0 > 0 Essential for conclusion If $l_0 = 0$, the entire cohort analysis is trivialized as a null set.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Probability of Survival

$$ _t p_x = \frac{l_{x+t}}{l_x} = \exp\left(-\int_0^t \mu_{x+s} ds\right) $$

Exam Rule: Instantly convert cumulative survivorship ratios into exponential integrals of the force of mortality.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT actuarial-statistics sections for rapid calculation.
02

Shortcut 2: Crude Death Rate (CDR) Formula

$$ \text{CDR} = \frac{D}{P} \times 1000 $$

Exam Rule: Direct ratio of total deaths $D$ to mid-year total population $P$, expressed per thousand.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard direct-formula question in TGT/PGT exams.
03

Shortcut 3: General Fertility Rate (GFR)

$$ \text{GFR} = \frac{\text{Total Births}}{\text{Female Population aged 15-49}} \times 1000 $$

Exam Rule: Always check denominator bounds: strictly women in childbearing age (15-49 years).

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct evaluation of demographic definitions.
04

Shortcut 4: Net Reproduction Rate (NRR) Threshold

$$ \text{NRR} = \int_{15}^{49} l_a \cdot m_a \cdot \frac{L_a}{l_0} da $$

Exam Rule: If $\text{NRR} > 1$, population grows; if $\text{NRR} < 1$, population declines; if $\text{NRR} = 1$, replacement level.

⚡ PYQ Context: Conceptual trick questions testing demographic equilibrium.
05

Shortcut 5: Expectation of Life ($e_x$)

$$ e_x = \frac{1}{l_x} \int_0^{w-x} l_{x+t} dt = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} _k p_x $$

Exam Rule: Average remaining lifetime is equal to the integral of future survival probabilities.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct calculation in numerical problems.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Units

$$ \text{Rates per } 1000 \implies \text{Multiply raw decimal fractions by } 1000 $$

Exam Rule: Eliminate options failing magnitude or scaling checks instantly.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves 30 seconds per question in 125-question papers.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: CDR vs STDR Confusion

Common Pitfall: Using Crude Death Rate to compare mortality across countries with different age structures without age-adjustment (Standardized Death Rate).

Counterexample: Country A (older population) having higher CDR than Country B despite better healthcare.

Why it fails: CDR is confounded by age distribution; direct comparison is invalid.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always look for age-standardization when comparing populations.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: GFR Denominator Misidentification

Common Pitfall: Including total population or males in the denominator of General Fertility Rate.

Counterexample: Dividing total annual births by total mid-year population.

Why it fails: This yields Crude Birth Rate, not General Fertility Rate.

🛡️ Exam Defense: GFR denominator must exclusively comprise women aged 15-49.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

In a population, the total number of live births in a year is $15,000$ and the female population in the age group 15-49 years is $60,000$. Find the General Fertility Rate (GFR).

(A) $125$(B) $250$(C) $300$(D) $150$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Identify formula $GFR = \frac{\text{Births}}{\text{Female Pop (15-49)}} \times 1000$. Step 2: Substitute values: $\frac{15000}{60000} \times 1000 = \frac{1}{4} \times 1000 = 250$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Compute ratio $\frac{15}{60} = \frac{1}{4}$, multiply by 1000 to get 250.
💡 Key Takeaway: Verify denominator is strictly 15-49 female population, not total population.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

If the force of mortality $\mu_x$ is constant and equal to $m$, what is the probability that a person aged $x$ will survive for $t$ years?

(A) $e^{-mt}$(B) $1 - e^{-mt}$(C) $mt$(D) $e^{mt}$
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Use $_t p_x = \exp\left(-\int_0^t \mu_{x+s} ds\right)$. Step 2: Since $\mu_{x+s} = m$, the integral evaluates to $\int_0^t m ds = mt$. Step 3: Result is $e^{-mt}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Constant force of mortality directly leads to exponential survival law $e^{-mt}$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Remember constant force analogy with radioactive decay.

Q1
MCQ 1: The Crude Death Rate (CDR) is expressed per:
A 100
B 1,000
C 10,000
D 1,000,000
Derivation & Analysis:

By definition, CDR is calculated per thousand of the mid-year population: $\frac{D}{P} \times 1000$.

Q2
MCQ 2: If $l_0 = 100000$ and $l_x$ is the number of survivors at age $x$, the probability of dying between age $x$ and $x+1$ is given by:
A $\frac{l_x - l_{x+1}}{l_x}$
B $\frac{l_{x+1}}{l_x}$
C $\frac{l_x}{l_{x+1}}$
D $l_x - l_{x+1}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Probability of dying in the interval is the ratio of deaths in $[x, x+1]$, which is $l_x - l_{x+1}$, divided by the cohort size $l_x$.

Q3
MCQ 3: General Fertility Rate (GFR) uses which of the following as its denominator?
A Total population
B Total female population
C Female population aged 15-49 years
D Total male population
Derivation & Analysis:

GFR is specifically restricted to women in the reproductive age span of 15 to 49 years.

Q4
MCQ 4: Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) has which denominator?
A Total population
B Total live births
C Total deaths
D Female population
Derivation & Analysis:

IMR is defined as $\frac{\text{Deaths of infants under 1 year}}{\text{Total live births}} \times 1000$.

Q5
MCQ 5: If Net Reproduction Rate (NRR) is equal to 1, then the population is:
A Increasing
B Decreasing
C Stationary / Replacement level
D Extinct
Derivation & Analysis:

An NRR of exactly 1 means each generation of mothers replaces itself exactly, leading to zero long-term population growth.

Q6
MCQ 6: The force of mortality $\mu_x$ in terms of survivorship $l_x$ is expressed as:
A $-\frac{1}{l_x} \frac{dl_x}{dx}$
B $\frac{1}{l_x} \frac{dl_x}{dx}$
C $-\frac{dl_x}{dx}$
D $\frac{dl_x}{dx}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $l_x \mu_x = -\frac{dl_x}{dx}$, dividing by $l_x$ yields $\mu_x = -\frac{1}{l_x} \frac{dl_x}{dx}$.

Q7
MCQ 7: Which demographic measure is least affected by age structure variations?
A Crude Birth Rate
B Crude Death Rate
C Age-Specific Death Rate
D Total Population Growth Rate
Derivation & Analysis:

Age-Specific Death Rates (ASDR) isolate specific age cohorts, eliminating age distribution confounding effects.

Q8
MCQ 8: If survivorship function $l_x = 100 - x$ for $0 \le x \le 100$, what is the limiting age $w$?
A 50
B 100
C 75
D Infinity
Derivation & Analysis:

Setting $l_w = 100 - w = 0$ gives $w = 100$.

Q9
MCQ 9: The expectation of life at birth $e_0$ is given by:
A $\int_0^w l_x dx$
B $\frac{1}{l_0} \int_0^w l_x dx$
C $\int_0^w x l_x dx$
D $\frac{1}{l_0} \int_0^w x l_x dx$
Derivation & Analysis:

Expectation of life is total person-years lived divided by initial cohort $l_0$, i.e., $\frac{1}{l_0} \int_0^w l_x dx$.

Q10
MCQ 10: In vital statistics, a life table column denoted by $L_x$ represents:
A Number of survivors at age $x$
B Number of person-years lived between age $x$ and $x+1$
C Deaths between age $x$ and $x+1$
D Force of mortality
Derivation & Analysis:

Standard actuarial notation $L_x = \int_0^1 l_{x+t} dt$ represents person-years lived in the age interval $[x, x+1]$.PILLAR_7_COMPLETE
