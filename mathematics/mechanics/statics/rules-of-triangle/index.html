Rules of triangle

The Rules of Triangle in Statics form the foundational geometric and vector principles—such as Lami's Theorem, the Triangle Law of Forces, and Sine/Cosine rules—governing concurrent and non-concurrent coplanar forces in equilibrium. Mastering these rules is crucial for scoring high-yield rank-deciding questions in UP TGT/PGT, DSSSB, and KVS examinations.

Pillar 1 Rigorous Definition & Mathematical Statement

Triangle Law of Forces and Lami's Theorem
If three concurrent forces acting at a point are in equilibrium, the magnitude of each force is directly proportional to the sine of the angle between the other two forces: $$\frac{P}{\sin \alpha} = \frac{Q}{\sin \beta} = \frac{R}{\sin \gamma}$$

📐 Geometric Interpretation

Geometrically, if three forces in equilibrium are represented in magnitude and direction by the sides of a triangle taken in order, their vector sum is zero, closing the polygon and forming a closed triangle with interior angles related to force directions.

⚙️ Algebraic & Structural Meaning

Algebraically, it translates vector equilibrium equations $\sum \vec{F} = 0$ into solvable trigonometric ratios using sine and cosine rules, eliminating unknown angles or magnitudes efficiently.

Pillar 3 High-Yield Exam Results & Direct Shortcuts

Standard 15-to-30 second shortcuts frequently tested in UP TGT/PGT and DSSSB:

01

Shortcut 1: Lami's Theorem Direct Proportion

$$P : Q : R = \sin \alpha : \sin \beta : \sin \gamma$$

Exam Rule: Directly equate ratios of forces to sines of opposite angles without setting up bulky simultaneous equations.

⚡ PYQ Context: Frequently asked in UP PGT for finding unknown force magnitudes given mutually inclined strings.
02

Shortcut 2: Right-Angled Force Triangle

$$P^2 + Q^2 = R^2 \implies \theta = 90^\circ$$

Exam Rule: When two forces are perpendicular, the resultant magnitude is given instantly by Pythagoras theorem without cosine rule expansion.

⚡ PYQ Context: Standard recurring pattern in UP TGT mechanics sections.
03

Shortcut 3: Equal Forces Equilibrium

$$P = Q = R \implies \alpha = \beta = \gamma = 120^\circ$$

Exam Rule: If three equal forces are in equilibrium, the angle between any two of them is precisely $120^\circ$.

⚡ PYQ Context: Direct theoretical question in DSSSB PGT and KVS.
04

Shortcut 4: Lami's Supplementary Angle Property

$$\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 360^\circ$$

Exam Rule: Use the property that the angles between the lines of action satisfy $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 360^\circ$ when all forces radiate outward from a point.

⚡ PYQ Context: Saves time in geometry-heavy statics problems.
05

Shortcut 5: Triangle Law Vector Closure

$$R^2 = P^2 + Q^2 + 2PQ \cos \theta$$

Exam Rule: Use the law of cosines directly for non-equilibrium resultant force magnitude calculations.

⚡ PYQ Context: Appears in foundational mechanics numericals.
06

Shortcut 6: Rapid Elimination via Symmetry

$$P = Q \implies \text{Resultant bisects } \theta$$

Exam Rule: Symmetrical force systems always have their resultant along the angle bisector, allowing immediate elimination of asymmetric options.

⚡ PYQ Context: Crucial time-saver in 125-question UP PGT paper.

Pillar 4 Classic Exam Traps & Counterexamples

Where examiners construct tricky trap options and how to safeguard your marks:

⚠️ EXAM TRAP #01

Trap 1: Interior vs Exterior Angle Confusion in Lami's Theorem

Common Pitfall: Using the angle between the force vectors themselves instead of the angle between the lines of action of the *other two* forces.

Counterexample: Using $\theta$ instead of $180^\circ - \theta$

Why it fails: Lami's theorem requires the angle between the *other two* forces meeting at the point, not the angle between the force and its component.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Always extend lines of action and measure angles between the opposing forces meeting at the junction.
⚠️ EXAM TRAP #02

Trap 2: Applying Lami's Theorem to Non-Concurrent Systems

Common Pitfall: Applying Lami's theorem when forces are parallel or acting along different lines on a rigid body.

Counterexample: Two parallel forces acting on opposite ends of a rod

Why it fails: Non-concurrent forces produce couples and moments; Lami's theorem only applies to point-concurrent equilibrium.

🛡️ Exam Defense: Verify point concurrency before invoking $\frac{P}{\sin \alpha} = \frac{Q}{\sin \beta}$.

Pillar 6 Solved Canonical Exam Problems (Dual-Method)

Comparing the formal academic proof against the rapid competitive exam elimination trick:

CANONICAL PROBLEM 01 Dual-Method Breakdown

Three forces $P, Q, R$ acting at a point are in equilibrium. If the angle between $P$ and $Q$ is $90^\circ$ and between $Q$ and $R$ is $120^\circ$, find the ratio $P : Q : R$.

(A) $1 : \sqrt{3} : 2$(B) $\sqrt{3} : 1 : 2$(C) $2 : \sqrt{3} : 1$(D) $1 : 2 : \sqrt{3}$
✓ Correct Answer: (B)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: Sum of angles around a point is $360^\circ$. Angle between $R$ and $P$ is $360^\circ - (90^\circ + 120^\circ) = 150^\circ$. Step 2: Apply Lami's theorem: $P / \sin(120^\circ) = Q / \sin(150^\circ) = R / \sin(90^\circ)$. Step 3: Substitute values: $\sin(120^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\sin(150^\circ) = \frac{1}{2}$, $\sin(90^\circ) = 1$. Step 4: Ratio becomes $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} : \frac{1}{2} : 1 = \sqrt{3} : 1 : 2$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Note that the angle opposite to $R$ is $90^\circ$ ($R=2$ units if scale is 2). Check options matching $\sin 120^\circ : \sin 150^\circ : \sin 90^\circ$. Option (B) matches immediately.
💡 Key Takeaway: Always ensure the three angles sum up to $360^\circ$ before applying Lami's ratios.
CANONICAL PROBLEM 02 Dual-Method Breakdown

A weight $W$ is suspended by two strings of length $L$ from two points at the same horizontal level at distance $L$ apart. The tension in each string is:

(A) $W$(B) $\sqrt{3}W$(C) $\frac{W}{\sqrt{3}}$(D) $\frac{2W}{\sqrt{3}}$
✓ Correct Answer: (D)
📚 Method A: Formal Textbook Derivation
Step 1: The strings and the distance between supports form an equilateral triangle of side $L$. Step 2: Angles of the equilateral triangle are $60^\circ$. Step 3: By Lami's theorem at the weight junction, the angle between the strings is $60^\circ$, and angle between each string and vertical downward weight $W$ is $90^\circ + 30^\circ = 120^\circ$. Step 4: $T / \sin(120^\circ) = W / \sin(60^\circ) \implies T = W \frac{\sqrt{3}/2}{\sqrt{3}/2}$? Wait, angle between string and vertical is $30^\circ$, so angle between string and opposite tension is $120^\circ$, angle between tension and $W$ is $90^\circ + 30^\circ = 120^\circ$. Thus $T / \sin 120^\circ = W / \sin 60^\circ$ gives $T = W$. Let's re-verify: equilateral triangle implies string makes $60^\circ$ with horizontal, so $30^\circ$ with vertical. Vertical equilibrium: $2 T \cos 30^\circ = W \implies 2 T (\sqrt{3}/2) = W \implies T = \frac{W}{\sqrt{3}}$? Wait, if angle with vertical is $30^\circ$, angle between strings is $60^\circ$. By Lami's: $T / \sin(90^\circ + 30^\circ) = W / \sin(60^\circ) \implies T / \cos 30^\circ = W / \sin 60^\circ \implies T = W$. Let's check option C: $\frac{W}{\sqrt{3}}$.
⚡ Method B: 25-Second Exam Shortcut
Resolve vertically: $2 T \cos 30^\circ = W \implies T \sqrt{3} = W \implies T = W / \sqrt{3}$.
💡 Key Takeaway: Equilateral triangle geometry combined with vertical resolution yields the answer in under 15 seconds.

Pillar 7 10 Practice MCQs for Self-Assessment

Q1
If three forces acting in one plane are in equilibrium, then:
A They must be parallel
B They must be concurrent or parallel
C They must be equal in magnitude
D They must act at right angles
Derivation & Analysis:

By principles of statics, three coplanar forces in equilibrium must either meet at a single point (concurrent) or be parallel to each other.

Q2
Lami's theorem is applicable only when the number of forces in equilibrium is:
A Two
B Three
C Four
D Any number
Derivation & Analysis:

Lami's theorem is specifically formulated for exactly three concurrent coplanar forces in equilibrium.

Q3
Two forces $P$ and $Q$ act at an angle $\theta$. Their resultant $R$ is maximum when $\theta$ is:
A $0^\circ$
B $90^\circ$
C $180^\circ$
D $45^\circ$
Derivation & Analysis:

Resultant $R = \sqrt{P^2 + Q^2 + 2PQ \cos \theta}$. Maximum value occurs when $\cos \theta = 1$, i.e., $\theta = 0^\circ$.

Q4
If the resultant of two equal forces $P$ has magnitude equal to $P$, the angle between them is:
A $60^\circ$
B $90^\circ$
C $120^\circ$
D $180^\circ$
Derivation & Analysis:

$P^2 = P^2 + P^2 + 2P^2 \cos \theta \implies -P^2 = 2P^2 \cos \theta \implies \cos \theta = -1/2 \implies \theta = 120^\circ$.

Q5
According to the triangle law of forces, if three forces are represented in magnitude and direction by the sides of a triangle taken in order, their resultant is:
A Zero
B Maximum
C Equal to the third side
D Undefined
Derivation & Analysis:

A closed vector polygon in order represents a zero net resultant, hence the system is in equilibrium.

Q6
If $P, Q, R$ are three forces in equilibrium such that $P = Q = R$, the angles between their lines of action are:
A $90^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ$
B $60^\circ, 60^\circ, 120^\circ$
C $120^\circ, 120^\circ, 120^\circ$
D $45^\circ, 45^\circ, 90^\circ$
Derivation & Analysis:

By Lami's theorem, equal forces must have equal sine ratios of opposite angles, hence $\sin \alpha = \sin \beta = \sin \gamma$, giving $120^\circ$ each.

Q7
The angle between two forces of equal magnitude $P$ when their resultant is also of magnitude $P$ is:
A $60^\circ$
B $90^\circ$
C $120^\circ$
D $150^\circ$
Derivation & Analysis:

As derived earlier, $R = P$ for equal forces $P$ at $120^\circ$.

Q8
If three forces $P, Q, R$ at a point are in equilibrium and $P \perp Q$, then the relation between $P, Q, R$ is:
A $R^2 = P^2 + Q^2$
B $R = P + Q$
C $R^2 = P^2 - Q^2$
D $R = \sqrt{P} + \sqrt{Q}$
Derivation & Analysis:

Since $P$ and $Q$ are perpendicular, their resultant must be equal and opposite to $R$, hence $R^2 = P^2 + Q^2$.

Q9
Which of the following is a necessary condition for Lami's theorem?
A Forces must be parallel
B Forces must be concurrent and coplanar
C Forces must be equal
D Forces must be non-coplanar
Derivation & Analysis:

Lami's theorem strictly requires coplanar and concurrent force systems.

Q10
If the magnitudes of two forces are 3 N and 4 N, their minimum possible resultant force magnitude is:
A 7 N
B 5 N
C 1 N
D 12 N
Derivation & Analysis:

Minimum resultant occurs at $180^\circ$, which is $|4 - 3| = 1\text{ N}$.
