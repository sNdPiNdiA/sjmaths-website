Q1 1. Differentiate between the following pairs of terms based on the clues given in parentheses:
(i) Cell membrane and cell wall (permeability)
(ii) RER and SER (structure)
(iii) Chloroplasts and chromoplasts (pigments)
Show Ideal Answer

(i) Cell Membrane and Cell Wall (Permeability):

  • Cell Membrane: Selectively permeable; it allows only specific molecules (such as oxygen, carbon dioxide, water, and essential nutrients) to pass through while restricting others.
  • Cell Wall: Freely permeable; it allows all molecules of appropriate size to pass through without selective restriction.

(ii) RER and SER (Structure):

  • Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER): Has ribosomes attached to the outer surface of its membranes, giving it a rough, granular appearance under the microscope.
  • Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum (SER): Lacks ribosomes on its surface, giving it a smooth, tubular appearance.

(iii) Chloroplasts and Chromoplasts (Pigments):

  • Chloroplasts: Contain the green pigment chlorophyll (along with accessory carotenoids) required for photosynthesis.
  • Chromoplasts: Contain colored pigments other than chlorophyll, such as carotenoids (yellow, orange, red) that impart colors to flowers and fruits.
Q2 2. Two similar animal cells are placed in two different solutions:
• Cell X is placed in pure water.
• Cell Y is placed in a concentrated salt solution. Cells are observed after some time. Cell X swells, and Cell Y shrinks.
Which statement provides the correct explanation for the above observations?
(i) Salt molecules moved into Cell Y, causing it to shrink.
(ii) Water moved into Cell X and more water moved out of Cell Y than the salt solution entered in it.
(iii) Water moved into Cell X and moved out of Cell Y through the cell membrane.
(iv) Solute movement caused osmosis in both cells.
Show Ideal Answer

Correct Option: (iii) Water moved into Cell X and moved out of Cell Y through the cell membrane.

Detailed Explanation:

  • Cell X in pure water: Pure water is a hypotonic solution (higher water concentration outside than inside the cell). Water enters the cell via endosmosis, causing it to swell.
  • Cell Y in concentrated salt solution: Concentrated salt solution is a hypertonic solution (lower water concentration outside). Water moves out of the cell via exosmosis, causing it to shrink.

Q4 4. Which of the following option(s) of the pairs of cell organelles are correctly placed under the given categories Option Present in the plant cells Absent in the animal cells
(i) Leucoplast Cell wall
(ii) Mitochondria Ribosome
(iii) Cell wall Golgi apparatus
(iv) Lysosome Endoplasmic reticulum
Show Ideal Answer

Correct Option: (i) Leucoplast | Cell wall

Explanation: Both leucoplasts (non-green storage plastids) and cell walls (rigid outer boundary) are present in plant cells and completely absent in animal cells. Other options contain organelles like mitochondria, ribosomes, Golgi apparatus, and endoplasmic reticulum which are present in both plant and animal cells.

Q5 5. Two students, Renu and Rohit, were having a discussion on the plastids. Renu emphasised that all parts of the plants, even roots, contain plastids. However, Rohit did not agree with the statement and told her that plastids are absent in plant roots since the roots are underground and do not need to perform photosynthesis. Who is correct? Justify your answer.
Show Ideal Answer

Renu is correct.

Justification: Plastids are present in all plant cells, though not all plastids are green. While chloroplasts are found in green aerial parts for photosynthesis, underground parts like roots contain non-green plastids called leucoplasts, which store starch, oils, and protein granules. Therefore, plastids are indeed present in plant roots.

Q6 6. Mitochondria and chloroplasts are two important organelles in a plant cell. Discuss how these two organelles are structurally and functionally similar to each other, and different from each other.
Show Ideal Answer

Similarities:

  • Double Membrane: Both possess outer and inner membrane envelopes.
  • Genetic Material: Both contain their own circular DNA and ribosomes, making them semi-autonomous organelles.
  • Energy Conversion: Both are central to energy metabolism (ATP production).

Differences:

  • Function: Mitochondria perform cellular respiration (oxidizing food to release ATP energy), whereas chloroplasts perform photosynthesis (capturing solar energy to synthesize glucose).
  • Occurrence: Mitochondria are found in both plant and animal cells, whereas chloroplasts are restricted to plant cells and certain photosynthetic algae.
Q7 7. Which of the following pairs of cell organelles contains DNA?
(i) Chloroplasts, Ribosomes
(ii) Mitochondria, Nucleus
(iii) Golgi bodies, Ribosomes
(iv) Nucleus, Lysosomes
Show Ideal Answer

Correct Option: (ii) Mitochondria, Nucleus

Explanation: The nucleus houses primary genomic DNA, while mitochondria (and chloroplasts) possess their own extra-nuclear circular DNA.

Q8 8. A researcher carried out an experiment in which she took two carrots of similar size. She placed one carrot in plain water and the other carrot in concentrated salt solution (Fig. 2.21). After 24 hours she recorded her observations.
(i) What hypothesis does she want to test through this experiment?
(ii) What would you suggest for the improvement of this experiment?
(iii) Why does the carrot in plain water stay stiff and crunchy, but the carrot in concentrated salt solution become rubbery and limp?
Show Ideal Answer

(i) Hypothesis: To test how different external water concentrations (hypotonic vs hypertonic solutions) affect plant tissue via osmosis.

(ii) Improvement: Use carrots of identical initial mass, length, and freshness. Measure initial and final weight/dimensions accurately for quantitative comparison.

(iii) Mechanism: Plain water is hypotonic, so water enters carrot cells by endosmosis, maintaining turgidity (stiff and crunchy). Concentrated salt solution is hypertonic, causing water to leave carrot cells by exosmosis (plasmolysis), resulting in loss of turgor pressure (rubbery and limp).

Q10 10. Carry out the following experiment:
Take four peeled potato halves and scoop each one out to make potato cups. One of these potato cups should be made from a boiled potato. Place each of the potato cups in a beaker containing water (Fig. 2.22). Now, set up the experiment as follows:
(a) Keep Cup A empty.
(b) Add one teaspoon sugar in Cup B.
(c) Add one teaspoon salt in Cup C.
(d) Add one teaspoon sugar in the boiled potato in Cup D.
Observe the four potato cups at least two hours and answer the following questions:
(i) Explain why water gathers in the hollowed portion of Cup B and Cup C.
(ii) Why is Cup A necessary for this experiment?
(iii) Explain why water does not gather in the hollowed portions of Cups A and D.
Show Ideal Answer

(i) Water gathers in Cup B (sugar) and Cup C (salt) because the solute creates a hypertonic environment inside the cup. Water moves from the beaker through the living potato cells into the cup via osmosis.

(ii) Cup A acts as a control to prove that hollowed potato alone without concentrated solute does not cause water accumulation.

(iii) Cup A lacks a solute concentration gradient. Cup D is made from a boiled potato, which kills and denatures the cell membranes, destroying their selective permeability and stopping osmosis.

Q11 11. Identify the pair that incorrectly matches the cell organelle with its function.
(i) Ribosome — Protein synthesis
(ii) SER — Lipid and cellulose synthesis
(iii) Lysosome — Digestion of foreign agents
Show Ideal Answer

Incorrect Pair: (ii) SER — Lipid and cellulose synthesis

Correction: Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum (SER) synthesizes lipids/fats, but plant cell wall cellulose is synthesized by specialized enzymes at the plasma membrane, not by the SER.

Q12 12. What outcome do you expect, if all the mitochondria are removed from a eukaryotic cell?
Show Ideal Answer

Without mitochondria, the cell would be unable to produce ATP through cellular respiration. Energy-dependent cellular activities such as active transport, synthesis of biomolecules, and maintenance would immediately stop, leading to rapid cell death.

Q13 13. Which phenomenon inhibits the formation of tumors in the human body? Can plants also develop tumors? Explain.
Show Ideal Answer

Human Body: Programmed cell death (apoptosis), DNA repair mechanisms, and tumor-suppressor genes (like p53) inhibit uncontrolled cell division and tumor formation.

Plants: Yes, plants can develop tumors (such as crown galls) caused by bacterial infections (e.g., Agrobacterium tumefaciens) or viral/insect attacks leading to abnormal proliferation of plant cells.

Q14 14. The cell membrane of a cell is made up of proteins and lipids. Which cell organelles help in the synthesis of cell membrane? Write the path of these compounds from their site of synthesis to the cell membrane and show this through a labelled diagram.
Show Ideal Answer

Organelles involved: Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER synthesizes proteins, SER synthesizes lipids) via membrane biogenesis.

Path: Proteins and lipids synthesized in the ER are transported to the Golgi apparatus for modification and packaging into transport vesicles. These vesicles travel to the cell membrane and fuse with it, integrating the newly synthesized lipids and proteins into the plasma membrane.

Q15 15. What would happen if gametes are formed by mitotic divisions?
Show Ideal Answer

Mitosis maintains the diploid chromosome number ($2n$). If gametes were formed by mitosis instead of meiosis, they would be diploid ($2n$). Upon fertilization ($2n + 2n$), the resulting zygote would become tetraploid ($4n$), doubling the chromosome number every generation and causing genetic instability and inviability.

Q16 16. A farmer, Deepa, was very happy with the harvest of amla (Indian Gooseberry) and lemons on her farm. However, she could sell only one-fourth of the produce in the local market. Recognising that a significant amount of produce may be lost post-harvest, she employed a traditional yet scientifically sound method to extend the shelf life of amla and lemons. She turned perishable produce into profitable products, such as pickles and sharbat. She used the excess produce to prepare pickles, murabbas, and sharbat by adding appropriate amounts of salt, sugar, or jaggery to small pieces of fruit and their juices. These were then stored in small glass bottles for sale, helping her prevent the wastage of post-harvest produce. This shift from farming to agro-processing would strengthen food security and boost the local economy, creating a sustainable model that cuts waste while increasing her income. Based on the above passage answer the following questions:
(i) Which scientific concept has the farmer applied in the preservation of the farm produce?
(ii) How does the addition of high concentrations of salt and sugar create an environment that prevents the growth of spoilage-causing bacteria and fungi?
(iii) Suggest a healthy recipe of this kind for food preservation.
(iv) What are the scientific values addressed in this case?
Show Ideal Answer

(i) Scientific Concept: Osmosis and plasmolysis / dehydration using hypertonic solutions.

(ii) Mechanism: High concentrations of salt and sugar create a hypertonic extracellular environment, causing water to leave spoilage-causing bacteria and fungi through osmosis, dehydrating and killing them.

(iii) Healthy Recipe: Low-sugar natural amla-ginger squash or sun-dried salted amla candies using minimal jaggery.

(iv) Scientific Values: Resourcefulness, minimizing post-harvest food waste, food security, sustainable agro-processing, and eco-friendly entrepreneurship.

Q-Thinking 1 What is the future of the development of synthetic cells using non-living chemicals?
Show Ideal Answer

Synthetic biology holds immense potential for creating custom artificial cells designed to synthesize target medicines, capture carbon dioxide, clean environmental pollutants, and produce renewable biofuels cleanly and efficiently.

Q-Thinking 2 If a synthetic cell is developed, what may be the related ethical issues?
Show Ideal Answer

Ethical concerns include potential biosafety risks (uncontrolled release of engineered synthetic organisms), biosecurity threats (misuse for harmful agents), and profound philosophical questions regarding the definition and creation of artificial life.
