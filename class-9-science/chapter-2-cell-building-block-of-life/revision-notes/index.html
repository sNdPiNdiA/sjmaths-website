Overview

Origin of Life & Cell Study

  • Origin of Life: Accepted to have started in water pools under fluctuating conditions. Modern hot springs (like Puga Valley in Ladakh, remaining near boiling in freezing climates) resemble conditions on Earth 3.5 billion years ago.
  • Thermophiles: Heat-loving bacteria living in hot springs. Rapid formation of calcium carbonate around them shielded organic molecules from radiation and helped form early protective cell membranes.
  • Limit of Resolution: The minimum distance at which two points are seen as distinct.
    • Human Eye limit = 0.1 mm (from 25 cm away).
    • Light Microscope limit = ~200 nm.
    • Electron Microscope limit = Nanometer scale (using electron beams).
  • Size Estimation Formula:
    Estimated Cell Size (µm) = Diameter of visible field (µm) / Number of cells in straight line
Mnemonic for Levels of Biological Organization:

"Cute Tiny Organisms Smile Often"
• Cute → Cell
• Tiny → Tissue
• Organisms → Organ
• Smile → Organ System
• Often → Organism

Timeline

Cell Discoveries & Classic Cell Theory

  • Robert Hooke (1665): Observed thin cork slices under a basic microscope, naming the box-like structures "cells".
  • Matthias Schleiden (1838): Declared all plants are composed of cells.
  • Theodor Schwann (1839): Concluded all animals are composed of cells.
  • Rudolf Virchow (1855): Expanded cell theory stating new cells arise from pre-existing cells ("Omnis cellula-e cellula").
Mnemonic for Cell Theory Pioneers:

"Silently Study Viruses"
• Silently → Schleiden (1838 - Plants)
• Study → Schwann (1839 - Animals)
• Viruses → Virchow (1855 - Pre-existing cells)

Boundaries

Cell Membrane & Cell Wall

  • Cell Membrane (Plasma Membrane): Thin (7 to 10 nm), selectively permeable structure composed of lipids and proteins.
    • Fluid-Mosaic Model: Lipids form a bilayer (hydrophilic heads outwards, hydrophobic tails inwards) that is fluid (molecules move sideways/rotate). Proteins act as gatekeepers.
  • Cell Wall (Plants, Fungi, Bacteria): Rigid, permeable outer layer made of cellulose. Maintains shape and protects cells from mechanical stresses.
    • Plasmolysis: Shrinkage of plant protoplasm away from the cell wall when placed in a hypertonic solution. Animal cells shrivel completely because they lack a cell wall.
Mnemonic for Fluid-Mosaic Structure:

"Fat Molecules Protect"
• Fat → Fluid properties (lipid bilayer flips and rotates)
• Molecules → Mosaic arrangement (tile-like proteins/lipids layout)
• Protect → Proteins embedded acting as transport gatekeepers

Transport

Osmosis, Diffusion & Tonicity

  • Diffusion: Net movement of particles from high to low concentration.
  • Osmosis: Diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane.
  • Potato Osmometer (Activity 2.2):
    • Beaker A (Water) → Water moves in → Potato swells (Endosmosis).
    • Beaker B (20% salt/sugar solution) → Water moves out → Potato shrinks (Exosmosis).
HYPOtonic → "HIPPOtonic"

Extracellular concentration is lower. Water enters the cell. The cell swells and gets fat like a Hippo.

ISOtonic → "ISO-equal"

Equal concentration outside and inside. No net water flow. Cell size remains unchanged.

HYPERtonic → "HYPERactive"

Extracellular concentration is higher. Water exits the cell. Like hyperactive kids running outside, water leaves, and the cell shrinks.

Feature Prokaryotic Cell Eukaryotic Cell
Nucleus Absent (Primitive Nucleoid) Present (Double-membrane bound)
Organelles Lacks membrane-bound organelles Organelles present (Mito, Plastids, etc.)

Acellular Entities (Outliers):

• Viruses: Genetic material + Protein coat.
• Viroids: Naked genetic material (no protein coat).
• Prions: Misfolded infectious protein strands containing no genetic material.

Organelle Functions & Memory Hooks

1. Nucleus (The Control Room):

Double membrane, nuclear pores, nucleolus. DNA + proteins form chromatin which condenses into chromosomes and genes during division.
• RBC Exception: Mature human Red Blood Cells lose their nucleus to pack more oxygen-carrying hemoglobin (120-day short lifespan).
• Mnemonic: "Nucleus = Network Center"

2. Endoplasmic Reticulum (The Factory):

Spreads from nuclear envelope.
• Rough ER (RER): studded with ribosomes, makes and secretes proteins (e.g., in pancreas).
• Smooth ER (SER): lacks ribosomes, synthesizes lipids, fats, and hormones.
• Mnemonic: "Rough has Ribosomes for Rapid proteins; Smooth makes Steroids & fats."

3. Golgi Apparatus (The Post Office):

Stacks of flattened sacs. Modifies, sorts, and packages ER products.
• History: Discovered by Camillo Golgi (1898) in barn owl nerve cells.
• Mnemonic: "Golgi = Global Shipping Center"

4. Lysosomes (The Clean-up System):

Single-membrane digestive sacs. Break down cell waste.
• Fertilization: Sperm tips carry lysosomal enzymes to dissolve the egg's outer barrier.
• Mnemonic: "Lysosome = Lysol spray (cleans up/disposes waste)"

5. Mitochondria (The Powerhouse):

Double membrane. Inner folded into cristae. Synthesizes ATP. Contains own circular DNA and ribosomes.
• Mnemonic: "Mighty Mitochondria Make Money (ATP)"

6. Plastids (Plant Only Food Centers):

Double-membraned with own DNA & ribosomes.
• Chloroplasts: Contain chlorophyll in stroma for photosynthesis.
• Chromoplasts: Red/yellow/orange color pigments to attract pollinators.
• Leucoplasts: Colorless storage (e.g. starch in potato/Colocasia roots).
• Mnemonic: "Color = Chromo, Chlorophyll = Chloro, Lacks pigment = Leuco"

7. Vacuoles (The Water Reservoir):

Large central cell-sap filled vacuole in plant cells. Exerts turgor pressure to maintain cell turgidity (loss of water causes wilting).
• Mnemonic: "Vacuole = Vast storage tank"

Reproduction

Cell Division, Totipotency & Synthetic Biology

  • Mitosis: One division forming two genetically identical diploid daughter cells. Essential for growth, tissue repair, and asexual reproduction.
  • Meiosis: Two consecutive divisions forming four genetically distinct haploid gametes (sperm/egg in animals, pollen/egg in plants). Generates genetic diversity for sexual reproduction.
  • Errors: Mitosis errors lead to tumours (benign/malignant) and cancer (loss of contact inhibition). Meiosis errors lead to genetic developmental disorders.
  • Synthetic Biology (Craig Venter, 2010): First synthetic DNA sequence transplanted into a host cell with its own DNA removed, verifying DNA coordinates all cell actions.
  • Totipotency (Gottlieb Haberlandt, 1902): The ability of a single mature plant cell to develop into a complete plant. Laid foundations for Plant Tissue Culture.
  • PCD (Programmed Cell Death): Genetically regulated cell destruction (e.g. removing webbing cells between fingers in embryos).
Mitosis: "My Two"

Makes Two identical twins (normal body cells).

Meiosis: "Make Me"

Makes gametes (sperm/egg) to reproduce and Make Me.
