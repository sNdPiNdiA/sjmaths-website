Q1 Which of the following mixtures are correctly classified as homogeneous (Hm) and heterogeneous (Ht)? Choose the correct option.
(i) Air — Hm, Milk — Ht, Sugar solution — Hm, Smoke — Hm
(ii) Brass — Ht, Fog — Ht, Vinegar — Ht, Muddy water — Hm
(iii) Copper sulfate solution — Hm, Salt solution — Hm, Milk — Hm, Bronze — Hm
(iv) Muddy water — Ht, Milk — Ht, Blood — Ht, Brass — Hm
Show Ideal Answer

Correct Option: (iv) Muddy water — Ht, Milk — Ht, Blood — Ht, Brass — Hm

Explanation:

  • Muddy water: Suspension (Heterogeneous) as clay/mud particles settle at the bottom.
  • Milk and Blood: Colloidal systems (Heterogeneous) because they consist of distinct phases (fat/blood cells suspended in plasma/liquid) though they appear homogeneous to the naked eye.
  • Brass: Solid solution/alloy (Homogeneous) as copper and zinc are uniformly mixed at atomic scales.
Q2 Which among the following mixtures show the Tyndall Effect?
A mixture of: (a) air and dust particles, (b) copper sulfate and water, (c) starch and water, (d) acetone and water.
Options: (i) a and b, (ii) b and d, (iii) a and c, (iv) c and d
Show Ideal Answer

Correct Option: (iii) a and c

Explanation:

  • Tyndall Effect is the scattering of light by suspended particles in a heterogeneous mixture (suspensions and colloids).
  • (a) Air and dust particles: Represents a suspension/colloid (aerosol) that scatters light beams.
  • (c) Starch and water: A colloidal mixture containing particle diameters ($1\text{ nm}$ to $1000\text{ nm}$) large enough to scatter a light path.
  • (b) and (d) are true solutions with particles smaller than $1\text{ nm}$, which do not scatter light.

Q4 Solve the following problems:
(i) A cake recipe uses dry ingredients, namely 75 g of sugar for 420 g of all-purpose flour and 5 g of sodium hydrogencarbonate. Express the concentration of each component in the mixture.
(ii) A brass alloy contains 70% copper by mass. Calculate the quantities of copper and zinc present in 120 g of brass.
Show Ideal Answer

(i) Concentration of Cake Components:

  • Total mass of the mixture (solution) $= 75\text{ g (sugar)} + 420\text{ g (flour)} + 5\text{ g (sodium hydrogencarbonate)} = 500\text{ g}$.
  • Concentration of Sugar (% m/m): $$\text{Sugar Conc.} = \frac{75\text{ g}}{500\text{ g}} \times 100 = 15\%$$
  • Concentration of Flour (% m/m): $$\text{Flour Conc.} = \frac{420\text{ g}}{500\text{ g}} \times 100 = 84\%$$
  • Concentration of Sodium Hydrogencarbonate (% m/m): $$\text{NaHCO}_3\text{ Conc.} = \frac{5\text{ g}}{500\text{ g}} \times 100 = 1\%$$

(ii) Quantities in 120 g of Brass (70% Copper, 30% Zinc):

  • Mass of Copper: $$\text{Mass of Copper} = 70\% \text{ of } 120\text{ g} = \frac{70}{100} \times 120 = 84\text{ g}$$
  • Mass of Zinc: $$\text{Mass of Zinc} = 30\% \text{ of } 120\text{ g} = \frac{30}{100} \times 120 = 36\text{ g}$$
Q5 The label on a cooking oil pack says one litre (910 g). If this oil is mixed with water, will it form a separate layer? If so, which substance will be on top? How will you separate the two layers? Also, draw the diagram of the apparatus used.
Show Ideal Answer

Yes, they will form separate layers.

  • Density Analysis: Water has a density of $1.0\text{ g/mL}$ (or $1000\text{ g/L}$). The cooking oil has a density of $910\text{ g/L}$ ($0.91\text{ g/mL}$). Since oil is less dense than water and they are immiscible, the oil will form the top layer.
  • Method of Separation: The mixture can be separated using a separating funnel.
Q6 Assertion (A): Solutions do not exhibit the Tyndall effect.
Reason (R): The particles in solutions are larger than 100 nm, so they cannot scatter light.
Choose the correct option: (i) Both A and R are true, and R is correct explanation. (ii) Both A and R are true, but R is not correct explanation. (iii) A is true, but R is false. (iv) A is false, but R is true.
Show Ideal Answer

Correct Option: (iii) A is true, but R is false.

Explanation:

  • Assertion (A) is True: Solute particles in a true solution are extremely small (less than $1\text{ nm}$ or $10^{-9}\text{ m}$ in diameter) and cannot scatter light rays, hence they do not exhibit the Tyndall effect.
  • Reason (R) is False: Particles in solutions are smaller than 1 nm, not larger than 100 nm.

Q8 Two miscible liquids, A and B, are present in a mixture. The boiling point of A is 60 °C and the boiling point of B is 90 °C. Suggest a method to separate them. Also, draw a labelled diagram of the method suggested.
Show Ideal Answer

Suggested Method: Simple Distillation

  • Justification: The difference in boiling points of the two miscible liquids is $30^\circ\text{C}$ ($90^\circ\text{C} - 60^\circ\text{C}$), which is greater than the $25^\circ\text{C}$ threshold required for simple distillation. Liquid A (lower boiling point) will vaporize first, pass through the condenser, and be collected.
Q9 Compare evaporation, crystallization and distillation. In which situation, would you prefer each of these over the others?
Show Ideal Answer
  • Evaporation:
    • Principle: Heating to remove volatile solvent to recover a solid solute. Solvent is lost.
    • Preference: Preferred when the solvent is not needed and the solid solute does not decompose on direct heating (e.g., getting crude salt from sea water).
  • Crystallization:
    • Principle: Slow cooling of a hot saturated solution to form pure crystals.
    • Preference: Preferred over simple evaporation because it removes soluble impurities and prevents thermal decomposition of solids (e.g., purifying copper sulfate crystals).
  • Distillation:
    • Principle: Boiling a mixture and condensing the vapor back to liquid.
    • Preference: Preferred when the solvent itself needs to be recovered or when separating miscible liquids with different boiling points.
Q10 Blood is an example of a colloidal mixture.
(i) What would happen if blood behaved like a true suspension inside the body?
(ii) In a blood sample, identify the dispersed phase and the dispersion medium.
Show Ideal Answer
  • (i) Suspension behavior inside the body:
    If blood behaved like a true suspension, the blood cells (RBCs, WBCs, platelets) would settle down at the bottom of the blood vessels whenever the body remains resting or stationary. This settling would block veins and arteries, causing circulatory failure and death.
  • (ii) Colloidal Phases in Blood:
    • Dispersed Phase: Blood cells (Red Blood Cells, White Blood Cells, and Platelets).
    • Dispersion Medium: Liquid Plasma (primarily water containing dissolved proteins and salts).
Q11 You are given a mixture of sand, common salt and naphthalene. Identify and write down the correct sequence of separation techniques.
Show Ideal Answer

Correct Sequence of Steps:

  1. Step 1: Sublimation
    Heat the dry mixture. Naphthalene undergoes sublimation (sublimes directly from solid to vapor) and deposits on the cooler inner walls of the funnel. Sand and salt remain in the dish.
  2. Step 2: Dissolution and Filtration
    Add water to the remaining sand-salt mixture and stir. Salt dissolves in water while sand remains insoluble. Filter the solution: sand stays as residue on the filter paper, while the salt solution passes through as filtrate.
  3. Step 3: Evaporation or Crystallization
    Heat the salt filtrate. Water evaporates, leaving behind pure crystals of common salt.
Q12 Why is distillation an effective method for separating a mixture of water and acetone?
Show Ideal Answer

Distillation is effective due to two key properties:

  1. Miscibility: Water and acetone mix completely (miscible liquids), meaning filtration or separating funnels cannot separate them.
  2. Boiling Point Difference: Acetone boils at a low temperature ($56^\circ\text{C}$), while water boils at a much higher temperature ($100^\circ\text{C}$). This large boiling point difference of $44^\circ\text{C}$ (which is $\ge 25^\circ\text{C}$) ensures acetone vaporizes completely and condenses before water starts boiling.
Q13 Answer the following questions based on Table 5.4 (Solubility data):
(i) What mass of potassium nitrate would be needed to prepare its saturated solution in 50 g of water at 40 °C?
(ii) A student makes a saturated solution of potassium chloride in water at 80 °C and leaves the solution to cool at room temperature (25 °C). What would she observe as the solution cools? Explain.
(iii) Compare the changes in the solubility of the four given salts with increasing temperature from 10 °C to 80 °C.
Show Ideal Answer
  • (i) Potassium Nitrate Mass calculation:
    At $40^\circ\text{C}$, the solubility of potassium nitrate is $62\text{ g}$ per $100\text{ g}$ of water. $$\text{Mass needed for } 50\text{ g of water} = \frac{62}{100} \times 50\text{ g} = 31\text{ g}$$
  • (ii) Cooling KCl saturated solution:
    At $80^\circ\text{C}$, solubility of $\text{KCl} = 54\text{ g}$ per $100\text{ g}$ of water.
    At room temperature ($25^\circ\text{C}$), the solubility lies between $37.4\text{ g}$ and $40\text{ g}$ (approx $38.5\text{ g}$).
    Observation: As the solution cools, the excess solute ($54 - 38.5 = 15.5\text{ g}$ of $\text{KCl}$) can no longer remain dissolved and will separate out (precipitate) as solid crystals at the bottom of the beaker.
  • (iii) Solubility Trends:
    • Potassium Nitrate: Increases drastically with temperature (from $21\text{ g}$ to $167\text{ g}$).
    • Sodium Chloride: Shows almost negligible change in solubility with temperature (remains static around $36\text{-}37\text{ g}$).
    • Potassium Chloride and Ammonium Chloride: Show moderate increases as temperature rises.
Q14 Three students A, B and C prepare sugar solutions:
• Student A dissolves 20 g of sugar in 80 g of water.
• Student B dissolves 20 g of sugar in 100 g of water.
• Student C dissolves 30 g of sugar in 80 g of water.
(i) Calculate the mass percentage concentration of each solution.
(ii) Whose solution is the most concentrated? Explain.
Show Ideal Answer

(i) Mass Percentage (% m/m) Calculations:

  • Student A Solution: $$\text{Total Mass} = 20\text{ g (solute)} + 80\text{ g (solvent)} = 100\text{ g}$$ $$\text{Mass \%} = \frac{20}{100} \times 100 = 20\%$$
  • Student B Solution: $$\text{Total Mass} = 20\text{ g (solute)} + 100\text{ g (solvent)} = 120\text{ g}$$ $$\text{Mass \%} = \frac{20}{120} \times 100 \approx 16.67\%$$
  • Student C Solution: $$\text{Total Mass} = 30\text{ g (solute)} + 80\text{ g (solvent)} = 110\text{ g}$$ $$\text{Mass \%} = \frac{30}{110} \times 100 \approx 27.27\%$$

(ii) Conclusion:

Student C's solution is the most concentrated because it contains the highest proportion of solute relative to the total mass of the solution ($27.27\%$).
