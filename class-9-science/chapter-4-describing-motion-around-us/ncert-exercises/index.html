Q1 My father went to a shop from home which is located at a distance of 250 m on a straight road. On reaching there, he discovered that he forgot to carry a cloth bag. He came home to take it, went to the shop again, bought provisions and came back home. How much was the total distance travelled by him? What was his displacement from home?
Show Ideal Answer

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Path Analysis:
    • Home to Shop = $250\text{ m}$
    • Shop back to Home = $250\text{ m}$
    • Home to Shop again = $250\text{ m}$
    • Shop back to Home = $250\text{ m}$
  • Total Distance Travelled: $$\text{Total Distance} = 250\text{ m} + 250\text{ m} + 250\text{ m} + 250\text{ m} = 1000\text{ m } (1\text{ km})$$
  • Displacement from Home:
    Since the starting position (Home) and the final position (Home) are the same, the net change in position is zero. $$\text{Displacement} = 0\text{ m}$$
Q2 A student runs from the ground floor to the fourth floor of a school building to collect a book and then comes down to their classroom on the second floor. If the height of each floor is 3 m, find:
(i) the total vertical distance travelled, and
(ii) their displacement from the starting point.
Show Ideal Answer

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • (i) Total vertical distance travelled:
    Let ground floor height be $0\text{ m}$.
    • Distance to 4th floor: $4 \times 3\text{ m} = 12\text{ m}$ (Upward).
    • Distance from 4th to 2nd floor: $2 \times 3\text{ m} = 6\text{ m}$ (Downward). $$\text{Total Distance} = 12\text{ m} + 6\text{ m} = 18\text{ m}$$
  • (ii) Displacement from starting point:
    The starting point is the Ground floor ($0\text{ m}$). The final point is the 2nd floor Classroom ($2 \times 3\text{ m} = 6\text{ m}$). $$\text{Displacement} = 6\text{ m (pointing in the upward direction)}$$
Q3 A girl is riding her scooter and finds that its speedometer reading is constant. Is it possible for her scooter to be accelerating and if so, how?
Show Ideal Answer

Yes, it is possible for her scooter to be accelerating.

Explanation:

  • A constant speedometer reading means the speed (magnitude of velocity) remains constant.
  • However, velocity is a vector quantity that has both magnitude and direction. If the girl changes the direction of her scooter (for example, by negotiating a curve or moving in a circular path), her velocity vector changes.
  • Since acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, any change in direction represents an acceleration (specifically, centripetal acceleration), despite the constant speed.
Q4 A car starts from rest and its velocity reaches 24 m s-1 in 6 s. Find the average acceleration and the distance travelled in these 6 s.
Show Ideal Answer

Step-by-Step Solution:

Given parameters: Initial velocity $u = 0\text{ m/s}$ (starts from rest), final velocity $v = 24\text{ m/s}$, time $t = 6\text{ s}$.

  • Average Acceleration (a): $$a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{24 - 0}{6} = 4\text{ m s}^{-2}$$
  • Distance Travelled (s):
    Using the second equation of motion ($s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$): $$s = (0 \times 6) + \frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times (6)^2$$ $$s = 0 + 2 \times 36 = 72\text{ m}$$
Q5 A motorbike moving with initial velocity 28 m s-1 and constant acceleration stops after travelling 98 m. Find the acceleration of the motorbike and the time taken to come to a stop.
Show Ideal Answer

Step-by-Step Solution:

Given parameters: Initial velocity $u = 28\text{ m s}^{-1}$, final velocity $v = 0\text{ m s}^{-1}$ (stops), displacement $s = 98\text{ m}$.

  • 1. Acceleration (a):
    Using the third equation of motion ($v^2 = u^2 + 2as$): $$0^2 = (28)^2 + 2a(98)$$ $$0 = 784 + 196a \implies 196a = -784$$ $$a = \frac{-784}{196} = -4\text{ m s}^{-2}$$ The negative sign indicates deceleration.
  • 2. Time taken (t):
    Using the first equation of motion ($v = u + at$): $$0 = 28 + (-4)t \implies 4t = 28$$ $$t = 7\text{ s}$$
Q6 Fig. 4.27 shows a position-time graph of two objects A and B that are moving along the parallel tracks in the same direction. Do objects A and B ever have equal velocity? Justify your answer.
Show Ideal Answer

Yes, objects A and B have equal velocity at one instant of time.

Justification:

  • The slope of a position-time ($s-t$) graph represents the instantaneous velocity of the object.
  • Object A moves with a constant positive slope, indicating constant velocity.
  • Object B starts with a flatter slope (lower velocity) and curves upwards with a progressively steeper slope (accelerating).
  • By geometry, there must exist a point on B's curve where the tangent line is exactly parallel to line A. At this specific point, their slopes—and thus their velocities—are equal.
Q7 A graph in Fig. 4.28 shows the change in position with time for two objects A and B moving in a straight line from 0 to 10 seconds. Choose the correct option(s).
(i) The average velocity of both over the 10 s time interval is equal since they have the same initial and final positions.
(ii) The average speeds of both over the 10 s time interval are equal since both cover equal distance in equal time.
(iii) The average speed of A over the 10 s time interval is lower than that of B since it covers a shorter distance than B in 10 seconds.
(iv) The average speed of A over the 10 s time interval is greater than that of B since B’s speed is lower than A’s in some segments.
Show Ideal Answer

Correct Options: (i) and (iii)

Explanation:

  • Option (i) is correct: Both objects start at the same position at $t = 0\text{ s}$ and end at the same position at $t = 10\text{ s}$. Since their net displacements are equal, their average velocities ($\text{displacement}/\text{time}$) must be equal.
  • Option (iii) is correct: Curve B curves outwards and reverses directions (backtracks), indicating B travels a longer actual path length (distance) than the straight-line path of A. Since average speed is $\text{distance}/\text{time}$, A's average speed is lower than B's.
Q8 A truck driver driving at the speed of 54 km h-1 notices a road sign with a speed limit of 40 km h-1 for trucks. He slows down to 36 km h-1 in 36 s. What was the distance travelled by him during this time? Assume the acceleration to be constant while slowing down.
Show Ideal Answer

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Convert initial and final velocities to SI units ($m/s$):
    Initial speed $u = 54\text{ km/h} = 54 \times \frac{5}{18} = 15\text{ m/s}$
    Final speed $v = 36\text{ km/h} = 36 \times \frac{5}{18} = 10\text{ m/s}$
    Time $t = 36\text{ s}$
  • Calculate deceleration (a): $$a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{10 - 15}{36} = -\frac{5}{36}\text{ m/s}^2$$
  • Calculate distance (s):
    Using $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$: $$s = (15 \times 36) + \frac{1}{2} \times \left(-\frac{5}{36}\right) \times (36)^2$$ $$s = 540 - \frac{5}{72} \times 1296 = 540 - 90 = 450\text{ m}$$
Q9 A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly to 20 m s-1 in 5 seconds. It then travels at 20 m s-1 for 10 seconds and finally applies the brake (with uniform acceleration) to stop in 6 seconds. Find the total distance travelled.
Show Ideal Answer

Step-by-Step Solution:

We divide the motion into three consecutive stages:

  • Stage 1: Acceleration ($0\text{ s}$ to $5\text{ s}$):
    Initial speed $u = 0$, final speed $v = 20\text{ m/s}$, duration $t_1 = 5\text{ s}$. $$s_1 = \frac{1}{2} \times t_1 \times v = \frac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 20 = 50\text{ m}$$
  • Stage 2: Constant Speed ($5\text{ s}$ to $15\text{ s}$):
    Speed $v = 20\text{ m/s}$, duration $t_2 = 10\text{ s}$. $$s_2 = v \times t_2 = 20 \times 10 = 200\text{ m}$$
  • Stage 3: Braking to a Stop ($15\text{ s}$ to $21\text{ s}$):
    Initial speed $u = 20\text{ m/s}$, final speed $v = 0$, duration $t_3 = 6\text{ s}$. $$s_3 = \frac{1}{2} \times t_3 \times u = \frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 20 = 60\text{ m}$$
  • Total Distance: $$s = s_1 + s_2 + s_3 = 50\text{ m} + 200\text{ m} + 60\text{ m} = 310\text{ m}$$
Q10 A bus is travelling at 36 km h-1 when the driver sees an obstacle 30 m ahead. The driver takes 0.5 seconds to react before pressing the brake. Once the brake is applied, the velocity of the bus reduces with constant acceleration of 2.5 m s-2. Will the bus be able to stop before reaching the obstacle?
Show Ideal Answer

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Convert speed to SI units: $$u = 36\text{ km/h} = 36 \times \frac{5}{18} = 10\text{ m/s}$$
  • 1. Reaction Time Distance ($s_1$):
    During the $0.5\text{ s}$ reaction time, the bus continues at a constant speed of $10\text{ m/s}$: $$s_1 = u \times t_{react} = 10 \times 0.5 = 5\text{ m}$$
  • 2. Braking Distance ($s_2$):
    Deceleration $a = -2.5\text{ m/s}^2$, final velocity $v = 0$.
    Using $v^2 = u^2 + 2as_2$: $$0^2 = 10^2 + 2(-2.5)s_2 \implies 0 = 100 - 5s_2 \implies 5s_2 = 100 \implies s_2 = 20\text{ m}$$
  • 3. Total stopping distance: $$\text{Total Distance} = s_1 + s_2 = 5\text{ m} + 20\text{ m} = 25\text{ m}$$
  • Conclusion:
    Since the total stopping distance of $25\text{ m}$ is less than the distance to the obstacle ($30\text{ m}$), the bus will successfully stop $5\text{ m}$ before reaching the obstacle.
Q11 A student said, “The Earth moves around the Sun”. In this context, discuss whether an object kept on the Earth can be considered to be at rest.
Show Ideal Answer

Yes, the object can be considered at rest, depending on the frame of reference.

Explanation:

  • Rest and motion are relative terms and depend on the chosen reference frame.
  • Earth-bound reference frame: If the table or ground on Earth is selected as the origin/reference point, the object's position does not change over time. Thus, relative to Earth, the object is at rest.
  • Astronomical reference frame: If the Sun or stars are taken as the reference frame, the Earth is revolving around the Sun. Relative to the Sun, the object is in motion.
Q12 The velocity-time graph from 0 s to 120 s for a cyclist is shown in Fig. 4.30. Shade the areas (in different colours) representing the displacement of the cyclist (i) while cyclist is moving with constant velocity, and (ii) when the velocity of cyclist is decreasing. Also, calculate the displacement and average acceleration in the 120 s time interval.
Show Ideal Answer

Calculations:

  • (i) Constant Velocity Region (40 s to 80 s): Shaded green.
    Displacement $s_2 = (80 - 40) \times 6 = 240\text{ m}$.
  • (ii) Decreasing Velocity Region (80 s to 120 s): Shaded red.
    Displacement $s_3 = \frac{1}{2} \times (120 - 80) \times 6 = 120\text{ m}$.
  • Total Displacement (0 to 120 s): $$\text{Total Area} = \text{Area}_1 + \text{Area}_2 + \text{Area}_3$$ $$\text{Area}_1 (0\text{ to } 40\text{ s}) = \frac{1}{2} \times 40 \times 6 = 120\text{ m}$$ $$\text{Total Displacement} = 120 + 240 + 120 = 480\text{ m}$$
  • Average Acceleration:
    Initial velocity $u = 0$, final velocity $v = 0$ at $t = 120\text{ s}$. $$a_{av} = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{0 - 0}{120} = 0\text{ m/s}^2$$
Q13 A girl is preparing for her first marathon by running on a straight road. She uses a smartwatch to calculate her running speed at different intervals. The graph (Fig. 4.31) depicts her velocity versus time. Estimate the distance she ran based on the graph.
Show Ideal Answer

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • The area under the velocity-time graph represents the total distance. The shape is a trapezium.
  • Parallel sides are $a = 6\text{ h}$ (from $0$ to $6\text{ h}$) and $b = 2\text{ h}$ (from $2$ to $4\text{ h}$).
  • Height $h = 7.5\text{ km/h}$.
  • Distance: $$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times (a + b) \times h = \frac{1}{2} \times (6 + 2) \times 7.5 = 4 \times 7.5 = 30\text{ km}$$
Q14 On entering a state highway, a car continues to move with a constant velocity of 6 m s-1 for 2 minutes and then accelerates with a constant acceleration 1 m s-2 for 6 seconds. Find the displacement of the car on the state highway in the 2 min 6 s time interval by drawing a velocity-time graph for its motion.
Show Ideal Answer

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Identify stages of motion:
    • Stage 1: Constant speed $u = 6\text{ m/s}$ for $t_1 = 2\text{ min} = 120\text{ s}$.
    • Stage 2: Accelerates at $a = 1\text{ m/s}^2$ for $t_2 = 6\text{ s}$.
    Final velocity $v = u + at_2 = 6 + (1 \times 6) = 12\text{ m/s}$.
  • Calculate Displacement from Area:
    • **Displacement in Stage 1 ($s_1$):** Rectangle area $$s_1 = 6\text{ m/s} \times 120\text{ s} = 720\text{ m}$$ • **Displacement in Stage 2 ($s_2$):** Trapezium area (from $6\text{ m/s}$ to $12\text{ m/s}$ over $6\text{ s}$) $$s_2 = \frac{1}{2} \times (u + v) \times t_2 = \frac{1}{2} \times (6 + 12) \times 6 = 18 \times 3 = 54\text{ m}$$ $$\text{Total Displacement} = 720\text{ m} + 54\text{ m} = 774\text{ m}$$
Q15 Two cars A and B start moving with a constant acceleration from rest, in a straight line. Car A attains a velocity of 5 m s-1 in 5 s. Car B attains a velocity of 3 m s-1 in 10 s. Plot the velocity-time graphs for both the cars in the same graph. Using the graph, calculate the displacement in the two time intervals mentioned.
Show Ideal Answer

Displacement Calculations:

  • Car A (displacement in 5 s): Area of triangle under Car A slope. $$s_A = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 5 = 12.5\text{ m}$$
  • Car B (displacement in 10 s): Area of triangle under Car B slope. $$s_B = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 3 = 15\text{ m}$$
Q16 Rohan studies science from 6 PM to 7:30 PM at home. Consider the tip of the minute’s hand of the wall clock. During the given time interval, what is its: (i) distance travelled, (ii) displacement, (iii) speed, and (iv) velocity? The length of the minute’s hand is 7 cm.
Show Ideal Answer

Given Parameters: Length of hand $R = 7\text{ cm} = 0.07\text{ m}$. Time interval = $1.5\text{ hours} = 90\text{ min} = 5400\text{ s}$.

In 1.5 hours, the minute hand completes exactly 1.5 revolutions.

  • (i) Distance Travelled: $$\text{Distance} = 1.5 \times \text{Circumference} = 1.5 \times 2\pi R = 1.5 \times 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 7 = 66\text{ cm } (0.66\text{ m})$$
  • (ii) Displacement:
    After 1.5 revolutions, the hand starts at 12 and ends at the 6 position. The straight-line distance is the diameter: $$\text{Displacement} = 2 \times R = 14\text{ cm } (0.14\text{ m}) \text{ pointing downwards}$$
  • (iii) Speed: $$\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}} = \frac{66\text{ cm}}{5400\text{ s}} \approx 0.0122\text{ cm/s } (1.22 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m/s})$$
  • (iv) Velocity: $$\text{Velocity} = \frac{\text{Displacement}}{\text{Time}} = \frac{14\text{ cm}}{5400\text{ s}} \approx 0.00259\text{ cm/s } (2.59 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m/s}) \text{ pointing downwards}$$

Spinning cardboard disc observation (radius ~ 8 cm, numbers at 7 cm, letters at 4 cm). Why do the numbers fade or disappear while the letters remain visible? Are the speeds the same?
Show Ideal Answer

Justification:

  • Angular speed ($\omega$) of all points on the rigid disc is equal.
  • However, linear speed ($v = \omega r$) is proportional to the distance ($r$) from the center.
  • The outer numbers ($r = 7\text{ cm}$) travel at a higher linear speed than the inner letters ($r = 4\text{ cm}$).
  • When spun rapidly, the outer numbers sweep past the eye faster, exceeding the human eye's motion integration threshold and causing them to fade/blur. The inner letters, moving slower, remain resolvable by the eye.

Derive the remaining two equations of motion:
(i) $s = vt - \frac{1}{2}at^2$
(ii) $s = \frac{1}{2}(u + v)t$ using the trapezium area formula.
Show Ideal Answer

(i) Derivation of $s = vt - \frac{1}{2}at^2$:

Start with the second equation: $$s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$$ From the first equation $v = u + at$, we substitute $u = v - at$: $$s = (v - at)t + \frac{1}{2}at^2$$ $$s = vt - at^2 + \frac{1}{2}at^2 \implies s = vt - \frac{1}{2}at^2$$

(ii) Derivation of $s = \frac{1}{2}(u + v)t$ (Area of trapezium):

Under a velocity-time graph, the displacement is the area under the trapezoidal shape bounded by initial velocity $u$ and final velocity $v$ over time interval $t$: $$\text{Area of Trapezium} = \frac{1}{2} \times (\text{sum of parallel sides}) \times \text{height}$$ $$\text{Displacement } (s) = \frac{1}{2} \times (u + v) \times t$$
