Tissues in Action: Revision Notes & Mnemonics

Overview

60-Second Chapter Summary

Master the foundational core of tissues and biological organization in under a minute:

  • Tissue Definition: A group of cells (usually similar in structure and origin) coordinating to perform a specialized function, enabling division of labour in multicellular organisms.
  • Divergent Evolution: Plants are stationary, autotrophic, and grow continuously via localized meristems; animals are mobile, heterotrophic, and exhibit uniform, determinate growth.
  • Meristems: Active growth zones lacking vacuoles. Apical (tips/length), Lateral (girth/thickness), and Intercalary (internodes/branching).
  • Simple Permanent: Parenchyma (storage/buoyancy), Collenchyma (pectin corners/flexibility), Sclerenchyma (lignified dead cells/coconut husk).
  • Complex Permanent: Xylem (unidirectional water transport; dead vessels/tracheids) and Phloem (bidirectional food transport; living sieve tubes/companion cells).
  • Animal Linings: Epithelial tissue sheets (Squamous, Cuboidal, Columnar, Ciliated, Glandular).
  • Animal Bindings: Connective tissues (Fluid Blood, Hard Calcium Bone, Flexible Cartilage, Muscle-to-Bone Tendons, Bone-to-Bone Ligaments).
  • Animal Movement & Control: Striated voluntary Skeletal muscles, unstriated involuntary Smooth muscles, and branched Cardiac muscles, under the rapid electrical control of **Neurons**.

Cheat Sheet

Plant Tissues Breakdown

I. Meristematic Tissues

  • Activity 3.1 (Onion Experiment): Cutting root tips stops division, indicating growth is localized at the apices containing apical meristems.
  • Cell Structure: Small cells, thin cellulose walls, dense cytoplasm, large prominent nucleus. Vacuoles are absent because actively dividing cells require no storage space.
  • Differentiation: The biological process where newly formed meristematic cells mature, lose their division ability, and take up permanent shapes, sizes, and functions.

II. Permanent Tissues

Tissue Class Cellular Features Key Chemical / Components Core Functions
Parenchyma Living, thin-walled, loosely packed. Cellulose Storage, photosynthesis (Chlorenchyma), floating (Aerenchyma).
Collenchyma Living, thickened at cell corners. Pectin Flexible support, bending without snapping.
Sclerenchyma Dead, uniformly thick rigid walls. Lignin (natural cement) Rigidity, strength (husk of coconut, seed coats).
Xylem Tracheids, Vessels, Fibres (dead); Parenchyma (living). Lignified tubes Unidirectional water/mineral transport upward; strength.
Phloem Sieve tubes, Companion cells, Parenchyma (living); Fibres (dead). Perforated sieve plates Bidirectional organic food translocation. Sieve tube functions are monitored by companion cells.

III. Plant Tissue Systems

  • Dermal System: Outermost protector. Epidermis, covered by a waxy cuticle (cutin) to reduce water loss. Includes stomata (flanked by Guard cells) and root hairs. Older stems form cork/bark from dividing cork cambium, coated with impermeable suberin.
  • Ground System: Forms the main body between dermal and vascular tissue. Includes parenchyma, collenchyma, and sclerenchyma.
  • Vascular System: Conducting channels composed of xylem and phloem.
Animal Tissues

Animal Tissues Cheat Sheet

I. Epithelial Tissues (Protection & Boundary)

  • Simple Squamous: Single layer of flat cells (blood vessels, lung alveoli) for rapid diffusion.
  • Stratified Squamous: Layers of cells (skin, mouth) to protect against wear and tear.
  • Glandular: Folded epithelium specialized for secreting enzymes, mucus, hormones, sweat, and saliva.
  • Ciliated / Sensory: Cells with cilia/hair (taste buds, nostrils, inner ear) for reception and sweeping particles.
  • Columnar: Tall, pillar-like cells (small intestine) optimizing nutrient absorption.

II. Connective Tissues (Support & Binding)

  • Blood: Fluid connective tissue containing a matrix called plasma. Cells include RBCs (red due to hemoglobin, live ~4 months, carry oxygen), WBCs (accumulate at infection sites, forming pus), and Platelets (blood clotting).
  • Bone: Hard, rigid matrix rich in calcium and phosphorus compounds.
  • Cartilage: Soft, flexible matrix cushioning bone joints, outer ear, and nose tip.
  • Tendons: Connect muscle to bone, transmitting force.
  • Ligaments: Highly elastic tissue connecting bone to bone, preventing joint dislocation.

III. Muscular Tissues (Movement & Locomotion)

Muscular Type Control Type Cell Shape & Layout Nuclei Count Locations
Skeletal (Striated) Voluntary Long, cylindrical, unbranched, showing dark/light bands. Multinucleated Attached to bones/skeleton.
Smooth (Unstriated) Involuntary Spindle-shaped, tapering ends, no bands. Uninucleated Stomach, intestine, blood vessels.
Cardiac Involuntary Cylindrical, branched, faint bands, intercalated discs. Uninucleated Heart walls (tireless beating).
Locomotion

The Musculoskeletal System

  • Overview: Composed of bones, muscles, joints, cartilage, tendons, and ligaments working under nervous command. Skeleton accounts for 12-15% of body weight.
  • Joints: Junctions between bones.
    • Ball and Socket Joint: Rounded bone top fits into hollow socket, allowing multi-directional movement (e.g., shoulder, hip).
    • Hinge Joint: Allows motion in a single plane only (e.g., elbow, knee with patella).
    • Pivot Joint: Permits side-to-side turning (e.g., skull articulating on the vertebral spine).
    • Fixed Joints: Locked sutures allowing no movement (e.g., skull plates safeguarding the brain).
  • Vertebral Column (Spine): Extending from the skull base, made of vertebrae cushioned by cartilage discs to permit flexible bending and protect the spinal cord.
  • Rib Cage: 12 pairs of ribs joined to the spine and sternum (breast bone) by flexible cartilage, expanding during breathing.
Application

Applied Science & Frontier Biology

  • Totipotency (F. C. Steward, 1958): The capacity of mature somatic cells (like phloem cells of a carrot) to dedifferentiate, divide, and redifferentiate into an entire adult plant.
    • Table 3.6 Insight: Cells cultured in aerated liquid mediums grow fastest, showing a 20% biomass increase compared to solid agar mediums or stagnant, dark conditions.
  • Genetic Tool: Agrobacterium tumefaciens causes crown gall disease by inserting genes into plants. Scientists customized it to transfer disease-resistant traits into crops.
  • Medical Stem Cells: Bone marrow contains hematopoietic stem cells. Bone marrow transplants are used to cure blood cancers (Leukemia) and Thalassemia.
  • Scientists in Focus:
    • B. G. L. Swamy: Renowned botanist; author of the Kannada classic Hasuru Honnu (Kendra Sahitya Akademi Award, 1978) blending science and culture.
    • Sipra Guha Mukherjee & S. C. Maheshwari: Pioneered haploid plant development through artificial anther tissue cultures.
  • Wellness: Yoga (including the 12 physical poses of Surya Namaskar) maintains synovial joint flexibility, bone mineralization, and cardiovascular circulation.
