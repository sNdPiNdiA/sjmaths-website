Chapter Overview

What You'll Learn in This Chapter

In earlier chapters you learnt how forces change the motion of objects. But when forces change with time or act in complicated ways, applying Newton's laws directly can become difficult. In this chapter, you will explore the ideas of work, energy and power, which often allow us to analyse motion and interactions more easily. You will also learn about simple machines — pulley, inclined plane, and lever — which help us perform tasks with less effort. Energy, the capacity to do work, lies at the heart of all these ideas.

Chapter Roadmap
  • Work Done by a Constant Force — W = F × s, positive & negative work
  • Work-Energy Theorem — work done equals change in energy
  • Forms of Energy — mechanical, kinetic, potential, and others
  • Kinetic Energy — KE = ½mv² and its variation with velocity
  • Potential Energy — gravitational PE = mgh
  • Conservation of Mechanical Energy — energy transforms but total stays constant
  • Power — rate of doing work, P = W/t
  • Simple Machines — pulley, inclined plane, lever, and mechanical advantage
Prerequisites

Before starting this chapter, ensure you are comfortable with force, Newton's laws, and kinematic equations from Chapter 6. You will also use the concept of mass and acceleration due to gravity (g ≈ 10 m/s²).

Quick Facts

Key Concepts at a Glance

Work Done

W = F × s (when force and displacement are in the same direction)

Kinetic Energy

KE = ½mv² — energy due to motion; doubles with velocity, quadruples when velocity doubles

Potential Energy

PE = mgh — energy due to position; directly proportional to height

Conservation

In a closed system, total mechanical energy remains constant (ignoring friction)

Power

P = W/t — rate of doing work; SI unit is watt (W)

Mechanical Advantage

MA = Load / Effort — how much a machine multiplies your applied force
