7.1
NCERT Concept

Work Done by a Constant Force

Work is said to be done on an object when a force acting on it produces displacement in the direction of the force. For a constant force, work depends on the force and the displacement in the direction of that force.

\[ W=F\times s \]

The scientific definition is important: while describing work, always identify the force/agency doing the work and the object on which the work is done.

The SI unit of work is the joule (J)

For a force–displacement graph, work done is represented by the area under the graph. This remains useful even when the force is not constant.

NCERT Example 7.1
Example 7.1 · Positive and Negative Work

While exercising, a girl lifts a dumbbell and slowly lowers it down. Identify when the girl does positive work on the dumbbell and when she does negative work on it.

Show Answer
NCERT Answer

While moving the dumbbell up, the applied force and displacement are in the same direction, so the work done by the girl is positive. While lowering it, the force applied by the girl to hold it is opposite to the displacement, so her work on the dumbbell is negative.

NCERT Example 7.2
Example 7.2 · Numerical

A goalkeeper's hand moved back by \(15\,\mathrm{cm}\) while stopping a ball with a force of \(200\,\mathrm{N}\). How much work did the goalkeeper do on the ball?

Show Answer
NCERT Answer

The force is opposite to the ball's displacement, so the displacement in the direction of the force is \(-0.15\,\mathrm{m}\).

\[ W=200(-0.15)=-30\,\mathrm{J} \]
Concept Check

Three identical bags are lifted through the same height. Explain why the work done is three times the work needed for one bag.

7.1.1
NCERT Concept

When Is Work Done Equal to Zero?

From \(W=Fs\), work is zero in several important situations.

  • No force: \(F=0\).
  • No displacement: \(s=0\), even if a force is applied.
  • Force perpendicular to displacement: there is no displacement in the direction of the force.

For example, pushing a rigid wall does not produce displacement of the wall, so no work is done on the wall in the scientific sense. A person may still feel tired because the body uses internal energy while maintaining force.

Similarly, when a girl carries a box horizontally, the upward force she applies to balance the weight is perpendicular to the horizontal displacement, so that force does zero work on the box.

NCERT · Pause and Ponder
  1. A weightlifter is holding a barbell steady. Is she doing any work on the barbell while holding it steady?
  2. Is the work done by friction on a stack of coins travelling on a rough surface positive, negative or zero?
Show Answer
Answer

For the stationary barbell, displacement is zero, so the work done on the barbell by the weightlifter is zero.

Friction opposes the motion of the coins, so its work is negative.

Concept Check

A student pushes a wall with a large force but the wall does not move. Distinguish between the student's physical effort and scientific work done on the wall.

7.1.2
NCERT Concept

Positive and Negative Work Done

Work can be positive or negative depending on the relative directions of force and displacement. Work itself has no direction; its sign indicates the energy effect of the force.

  • Positive work: force and displacement are in the same direction.
  • Negative work: force and displacement are in opposite directions.

Pushing a wheelchair forward gives positive work by the applied force. Stopping a moving ball involves negative work by the stopping force.

Remember that different forces acting during the same event can do different amounts and signs of work.

Concept Check

During upward motion of a thrown ball, compare the signs of the work done by gravity and by an upward applied force.

7.2
NCERT Concept

The Work–Energy Theorem

An object having the capacity to do work is said to possess energy. When positive work is done on an object, it gains energy. That energy can subsequently be transferred to another object.

\[ \text{Work done on an object}=\text{change in its energy} \]

This is the work–energy theorem. It also applies to a system of objects and remains useful even when the forces involved are not constant. The SI unit of energy is the joule (J).

Energy can be transferred by mechanical work, heat, radiation, electric circuits, sound waves and nuclear processes. Mechanical work is therefore one important mode of energy transfer, not the only one.

Example 7.3 · Carrom Collision

In a game of carrom, a player plays a shot to pocket the black coin. Identify who does work and describe the changes in energy at each collision.

Show Answer
NCERT Answer

The moving striker does positive work on the white coin, increasing its energy. The white coin does negative work on the striker, decreasing its energy. The white coin then does positive work on the black coin, increasing its energy, while the black coin does negative work on the white coin.

NCERT · Pause and Ponder

When you pedal a bicycle on a flat road, your muscles supply energy. In what forms does this muscular energy appear as you ride?

Show Answer
Answer

Chemical energy stored in the body is converted into mechanical energy of the moving bicycle and rider, with some energy also appearing as thermal energy and other losses such as sound.

Concept Check

If a force does negative work on a moving object, explain what happens to the object's kinetic energy when that force is responsible for slowing it.

7.3
NCERT Concept

Forms of Energy

Energy is the capacity to do work and can exist in many forms. The chapter introduces mechanical, thermal, light, sound, nuclear, chemical and electrical energy.

FormIdea / Example
MechanicalEnergy due to motion or position.
ThermalEnergy associated with temperature and heating.
LightEnergy that enables vision.
SoundEnergy associated with vibrations.
NuclearEnergy stored in atomic nuclei.
ChemicalEnergy stored in chemical bonds, such as in food and fuels.
ElectricalEnergy associated with electric charges and circuits.

Energy can be transformed from one form into another. For example, electrical energy can become light and thermal energy in a bulb; chemical energy in food can power muscles and become mechanical energy; and mechanical energy in a ringing bell becomes sound energy.

7.4
NCERT Concept

Mechanical Energy

Mechanical energy is the energy an object possesses due to its motion or position. In this chapter it is studied through kinetic energy and potential energy.

\[ E_{\text{mechanical}}=K+U \]

Kinetic energy is associated with motion, while potential energy is associated with deformation or the relative positions of objects in a system.

Concept Check

A moving object is brought to rest by friction. Which part of its mechanical energy changes first, and where does the transferred energy go?

7.4.1
NCERT Concept

Kinetic Energy

The energy possessed by an object due to its motion is called kinetic energy. Starting from the work–energy theorem and using the kinematic relation for constant acceleration gives the general work expression

\[ W=\frac12m(v^2-u^2) \]

For an object starting from rest, \(u=0\), so its kinetic energy is

\[ K=\frac12mv^2 \]

Kinetic energy is a scalar quantity and its SI unit is joule. Positive work that increases speed increases kinetic energy; negative work that decreases speed decreases kinetic energy.

Example 7.4 · Velocity Scaling

If the velocity of a vehicle doubles in magnitude, what will its kinetic energy be compared to its original value?

Show Answer
NCERT Answer
\[ K_1=\frac12mv^2,\qquad K_2=\frac12m(2v)^2=4K_1 \]

The new kinetic energy is four times the original.

Example 7.5 · Cricket Ball

A cricket ball of approximate mass \(0.2\,\mathrm{kg}\) is bowled at about \(154.8\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}\). Calculate its kinetic energy.

Show Answer
NCERT Answer

\(154.8\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}=43\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).

\[ K=\frac12(0.2)(43)^2\approx184.9\,\mathrm{J} \]
Example 7.6 · Jet Aircraft

A \(15000\,\mathrm{kg}\) jet is stopped within \(100\,\mathrm{m}\) by a wire exerting an approximately constant backward force of \(367500\,\mathrm{N}\). Find its velocity just before the wire catches it.

Show Answer
NCERT Answer

The wire does negative work: \[ W=367500(-100)=-36,750,000\,\mathrm{J}. \] By the work–energy theorem this equals the change in kinetic energy: \[ -36,750,000=0-\frac12(15000)v^2. \] Hence \[ v=70\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}. \]

NCERT · Pause and Ponder
  1. Two objects A and B of masses \(m\) and \(4m\) have the same kinetic energy. What is the ratio of their speeds?
  2. Does the kinetic energy of an object moving with constant velocity change with its position?
Show Answer
Answer

Since \(K=\frac12mv^2\), equal kinetic energies give \(mv^2=\text{constant}\). Thus \[ v_A:v_B=2:1. \] For constant velocity and constant mass, kinetic energy remains unchanged regardless of position.

Concept Check

If the mass is doubled and the speed is halved, compare the new kinetic energy with the original kinetic energy.

7.4.2
NCERT Concept

Potential Energy

Potential energy is the energy stored by an object because of its deformation or by a system because of the relative positions of its objects.

A stretched rubber band, a bent bow and a compressed spring can store energy because work has been done to deform them. A system of separated magnets or electric charges can store energy because of their relative positions. Similarly, the Earth–ball system stores gravitational potential energy when the ball is raised.

Gravitational Potential Energy

Near the Earth's surface, potential energy usually refers to gravitational potential energy. Taking the ground as the zero reference level, raising an object of mass \(m\) through height \(h\) requires work against gravity:

\[ W=mgh \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad U=mgh \]

The expression \(U=mgh\) is valid near the Earth's surface where \(g\) can be treated as approximately constant.

Activity 7.1 · Investigating Potential Energy

Raise a heavy ball above loose sand and drop it. Repeat from a greater height. The deeper depression produced from the greater height shows that the ball has more potential energy at greater height.

Example 7.7 · Gravitational Potential Energy

A fielder throws a \(200\,\mathrm{g}\) cricket ball about \(10\,\mathrm{m}\) above the ground. How much potential energy does it have at maximum height? Take \(g=10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).

Show Answer
NCERT Answer
\[ U=mgh=(0.2)(10)(10)=20\,\mathrm{J} \]
NCERT · Pause and Ponder

Does the potential energy of an object near the Earth's surface change if it moves with constant velocity horizontally? What if it is gradually raised vertically?

Show Answer
Answer

Horizontal motion at constant height does not change gravitational potential energy. Raising the object vertically increases its gravitational potential energy because its height increases.

Concept Check

Explain why gravitational potential energy belongs naturally to the Earth–object system rather than being treated as a property of the object alone.

7.4.3
NCERT Concept

Conservation of Mechanical Energy

The sum of kinetic energy and potential energy is called mechanical energy. For an object moving under gravity, if no other external forces act and energy losses such as friction are absent, mechanical energy remains constant.

\[ E_{\text{mechanical}}=K+U=\text{constant} \]

During free fall, potential energy decreases while kinetic energy increases by an equal amount. Thus, potential energy is converted into kinetic energy while the total mechanical energy remains \(mgh\) for an object released from height \(h\).

Activity 7.2 · Simple Pendulum

At one extreme position the bob has mainly potential energy. At the lowest point, potential energy is minimum and kinetic energy is maximum. At the other extreme, kinetic energy again becomes zero and potential energy is restored. In a real pendulum, friction and air resistance gradually convert mechanical energy into other forms, so the oscillations die out.

Example 7.8 · Child on a Slide

What will be the magnitude of velocity of a child on reaching the bottom of a frictionless slide of height \(h\)?

Show Answer
NCERT Answer
\[ mgh=\frac12mv^2 \Rightarrow v=\sqrt{2gh} \]

The result depends on height \(h\), not on the mass of the child or the shape of the frictionless slide.

Example 7.9 · Escape Ramp

A \(10000\,\mathrm{kg}\) truck moving at \(72\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}\) enters a \(30^\circ\) escape ramp. Sand exerts \(50000\,\mathrm{N}\) opposite to motion. Find the minimum ramp length needed to stop it. Take \(g=10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).

Show Answer
NCERT Answer

\(72\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}=20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), so the initial kinetic energy is \[ K_i=\frac12(10000)(20)^2=2,000,000\,\mathrm{J}. \] For a \(30^\circ\) ramp, height gained is \(d/2\), giving final gravitational potential energy \(50000d\). Sand does work \(-50000d\).

\[ -50000d=50000d-2000000 \] \[ d=20\,\mathrm{m} \]
NCERT · Pause and Ponder
  1. For the situation in Fig. 7.19, calculate the mechanical energy of the ball just before it hits the ground and show that it is \(mgh\).
  2. For the roller-coaster exhibit in Fig. 7.22, describe how kinetic and potential energy change at A, B and C. Why are later points such as C, D and E usually lower because of frictional energy loss?
Show Answer
Answer

Just before reaching the ground, the potential energy is zero if the ground is chosen as the zero level, while the kinetic energy has increased by the same amount by which potential energy decreased. Therefore the total mechanical energy is still \(mgh\).

At higher points the ball has more potential and less kinetic energy. As it moves downward, potential energy decreases and kinetic energy increases. Friction and air resistance convert part of the mechanical energy into thermal energy and sound, so later maximum heights are generally lower.

Concept Check

What additional condition is required for mechanical energy to remain constant when an object is moving under gravity?

7.5
NCERT Concept

Power

Power describes how quickly work is done. Doing the same work in a shorter time requires greater power.

\[ P=\frac{W}{t} \]

The SI unit of power is the watt: \[ 1\,\mathrm{W}=1\,\mathrm{J\,s^{-1}}. \] Another unit used for engines and pumps is horsepower: \[ 1\,\mathrm{hp}=746\,\mathrm{W}. \]

Example 7.10 · Weightlifter

A weightlifter lifts a \(75\,\mathrm{kg}\) mass by \(2\,\mathrm{m}\) in \(5\,\mathrm{s}\). How much power is required? Take \(g=10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).

Show Answer
NCERT Answer
\[ W=mgh=(75)(10)(2)=1500\,\mathrm{J} \] \[ P=\frac{1500}{5}=300\,\mathrm{W} \]
Example 7.11 · Car Engine

A \(1000\,\mathrm{kg}\) car starts from rest and reaches \(72\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}\) in \(10\,\mathrm{s}\). Calculate the power required to achieve this start.

Show Answer
NCERT Answer

\(72\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}=20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).

\[ W=\Delta K=\frac12(1000)(20)^2=200000\,\mathrm{J} \] \[ P=\frac{200000}{10}=20000\,\mathrm{W} \]
Concept Check

Two students do the same work, but one takes half the time. Compare their powers.

7.6
NCERT Concept

Simple Machines

Simple machines make tasks easier by changing the magnitude or direction of the force that needs to be applied. They do not reduce the total work required when friction is ignored.

The force applied to a machine is called the effort, and the force that must be overcome is called the load. Mechanical advantage is

\[ \mathrm{MA}=\frac{\text{Load}}{\text{Effort}} \]

This chapter studies three simple machines: pulley, inclined plane and lever.

7.6.1
NCERT Concept

Pulley

A pulley is a wheel with a groove that guides a rope. A fixed pulley does not reduce the magnitude of effort; it changes the direction of the effort. For an ideal fixed pulley:

\[ \mathrm{MA}=1 \]

A movable pulley or a system of pulleys can have mechanical advantage greater than 1, allowing a heavier load to be lifted with smaller effort. Such systems are used in devices such as elevators and cranes.

Key comparison
  • Fixed pulley → mainly changes direction of effort.
  • Movable/system pulley → can provide mechanical advantage greater than 1.
  • Ideal machine → does not create energy.
Concept Check

Why is pulling downward with a fixed pulley more convenient than directly lifting the same load upward, even though the ideal mechanical advantage is 1?

7.6.2
NCERT Concept

Inclined Plane

An inclined plane allows a heavy load to be moved to a higher or lower level with a smaller effort applied over a larger distance.

Activity 7.3

Compare lifting a cart vertically with pulling it along an inclined plank. Then reduce the steepness of the plank. The required force decreases as the plank becomes less steep, but the distance over which the force acts increases.

For a load \(mg\), effort \(F'\), height \(h\) and inclined-plane length \(L\), ignoring friction:

\[ F'L=mgh \] \[ \mathrm{MA} =\frac{mg}{F'} =\frac{L}{h} \]

Since \(L>h\), the ideal mechanical advantage is greater than 1. Increasing the length of the ramp for the same height reduces the effort further.

Example 7.12 · Inclined Ramp

A ramp raises an object over a \(30\,\mathrm{cm}\) step and has a width of \(40\,\mathrm{cm}\). Find its mechanical advantage.

Show Answer
NCERT Answer

The ramp length is \(50\,\mathrm{cm}\) by the right-angled triangle property.

\[ \mathrm{MA}=\frac{L}{h} =\frac{50}{30} \approx1.67 \]
NCERT · Pause and Ponder
  1. Why are roads on hills built to wind around in gentle slopes?
  2. Why is an inclined ladder easier to climb than a vertical ladder to reach the same floor?
Show Answer
Answer

A longer, gentler slope reduces the effort required by increasing the distance over which the force is applied. The same principle makes an inclined ladder easier than a vertical climb.

Concept Check

If the ramp length is doubled while the height remains the same, what happens to its ideal mechanical advantage and the required effort?

7.6.3
NCERT Concept

Lever

A lever is a rigid bar that can rotate about a fixed point called the fulcrum. Its three main parts are fulcrum, load and effort. The distance of the load from the fulcrum is the load arm; the distance of the effort from the fulcrum is the effort arm.

\[ F_1d_1=F_2d_2 \] \[ \mathrm{MA} = \frac{\text{effort arm}}{\text{load arm}} \]

Increasing the effort arm allows a smaller effort to produce a larger force on the load. The effort moves through a larger distance, so the lever does not reduce total work when friction is ignored.

Activity 7.4 · Lever Investigation

Using a scale over a pencil as a fulcrum, place a heavy stapler near the fulcrum and lighter objects farther away. A small effort can lift a larger load when the geometry gives sufficient mechanical advantage.

Activity 7.5 · Beam Balance

A beam balance demonstrates the lever principle. Balance occurs when

\[ \text{effort}\times\text{effort arm} = \text{load}\times\text{load arm} \]
Example 7.13 · Seesaw

For a seesaw with fulcrum at C, \(AC=EC=2\,\mathrm{m}\) and \(BC=DC=1\,\mathrm{m}\), where should children of masses \(15\,\mathrm{kg}\) and \(30\,\mathrm{kg}\) sit to balance it?

Show Answer
NCERT Answer

Let the \(15\,\mathrm{kg}\) child sit at A. If the \(30\,\mathrm{kg}\) child is at distance \(L\):

\[ 15\times2=30\times L \Rightarrow L=1\,\mathrm{m} \]

Therefore the \(30\,\mathrm{kg}\) child should sit at D.

NCERT · Pause and Ponder
  1. Why is it easier to open the lid of a can using a spoon?
  2. Why do you push an object closer to the scissors' fulcrum when cutting a hard object?
  3. Why do real machines eventually slow down and stop? Explain in terms of work and energy.
Show Answer
Answer

A spoon can provide a long effective effort arm, so a relatively small effort produces a larger turning effect on the lid.

Moving a hard object closer to the fulcrum reduces the load arm. For the same load, this reduces the effort needed.

Real machines experience friction and other energy losses. Mechanical energy is transferred mainly into thermal energy and sometimes sound, so the machine cannot continue doing useful work indefinitely without an energy input.

A lever has an effort arm twice the load arm. What ideal mechanical advantage does it provide?
