Earth as a System: Energy, Matter, and Life

Revise, Reflect, Refine

Exact textbook questions with expandable solutions

1.
1 Marks
Choose the most appropriate option to describe the role of biogeochemical cycles in an ecosystem.
  1. (i) To provide food directly to all organisms.
  2. (ii) To recycle essential nutrients between biotic and abiotic components.
  3. (iii) To create new elements for use by living things.
  4. (iv) To remove pollutants and toxins from the organism.
View Solution & Marking Scheme

Answer: (ii) To recycle essential nutrients between biotic and abiotic components.

2.
1 Marks
Which of the following is primarily responsible for warming of the Earth?
  1. (i) Solar radiation is immediately absorbed by carbon dioxide, which then releases it as heat.
  2. (ii) The atmosphere’s tiny particles absorb incoming solar radiation, which directly heats the Earth.
  3. (iii) The Earth’s surface absorbs solar radiation, which is then re-radiated and trapped by greenhouse gases.
  4. (iv) The Earth’s environment is heated only by the solar radiation reflected by the clouds.
View Solution & Marking Scheme

Answer: (iii) The Earth’s surface absorbs solar radiation, which is then re-radiated and trapped by greenhouse gases.

3.
3 Marks
Explain how climate change affects the water cycle. Illustrate with examples.
View Solution & Marking Scheme

Climate change can make the atmosphere hold more moisture, causing heavier rainfall in some places and drought in others. Glacier melting changes river water and contributes to long-term sea-level rise. Intense rainfall can increase runoff and soil erosion while reducing infiltration and groundwater recharge.

4.
3 Marks
Describe how albedo affects the Earth’s surface temperature and its climate.
View Solution & Marking Scheme

Albedo is the fraction of incoming solar radiation reflected by a surface. High-albedo surfaces reflect more and absorb less, tending to remain cooler; low-albedo surfaces absorb more and warm more. Therefore changes in albedo can influence surface temperature and climate.

5.
4 Marks
How are mountain and valley breezes formed? Suppose there are two mountains, one covered with grass and another covered with barren rocks; would the temperature of the two mountain breezes be different? If so, how?
View Solution & Marking Scheme

During the day, slopes heat and warm air rises, producing a valley breeze as cooler valley air moves upslope. After sunset, slopes cool and denser air flows downslope as a mountain breeze. Grass and barren rock surfaces can heat and cool differently because of their surface properties, so the resulting breeze temperatures can differ.

6.
3 Marks
You have witnessed weather phenomena, such as winds, storms, rainfall, etc. Which atmospheric layer is mainly responsible for such phenomena and what is the primary reason for its occurrence?
View Solution & Marking Scheme

Troposphere. Most weather occurs there because it is heated from the Earth’s surface. Warm air rises, producing convection, pressure differences and atmospheric circulation.

7.
4 Marks
Explain the processes involved in the nitrogen cycle. How would life on Earth be affected if nitrogen were not cycled?
View Solution & Marking Scheme

The processes are nitrogen fixation, assimilation, ammonification, nitrification and denitrification. Without cycling, usable nitrogen would not be continually replenished, disrupting plant growth, food webs and ecosystem productivity.

8.
4 Marks
What are the impacts of deforestation on the Earth’s oxygen and carbon cycles? What are the other consequences of deforestation?
View Solution & Marking Scheme

Deforestation reduces photosynthesis, so less CO2 is removed and less O2 is restored. It can release stored carbon and weaken carbon sinks. Other consequences include reduced transpiration, altered rainfall, changed albedo, increased soil erosion and habitat loss.

9.
4 Marks
Explain with suitable diagram the path that carbon takes to go back to the atmosphere. You may start from plants using CO2 from the atmosphere.
View Solution & Marking Scheme

Plants take in CO2 by photosynthesis. Carbon moves through food chains. Respiration and decomposition return CO2 to the atmosphere. Over long periods, buried carbon can become fossil fuels; combustion returns it rapidly as CO2.

10.
3 Marks
Why is an excess of CO2 in the atmosphere considered undesirable even though it is required by plants?
View Solution & Marking Scheme

CO2 is necessary for photosynthesis and the natural greenhouse effect, but excess CO2 intensifies greenhouse warming, contributing to global warming, ice melt, sea-level rise and changing rainfall.

11.
3 Marks
How is heat lost from the surface of the Earth? What is its significance?
View Solution & Marking Scheme

The surface re-radiates energy mainly as infrared radiation. Greenhouse gases absorb some outgoing heat. This is significant because it keeps Earth warm enough for life; excess greenhouse gases enhance heat retention.

12.
3 Marks
If the Earth were a flat disc instead of a sphere, how would the patterns of solar radiation and temperature be different?
View Solution & Marking Scheme

Earth’s spherical shape makes sunlight strike different latitudes at different angles and spread over different areas. A flat disc would have a different distribution of solar radiation, so the present equator-to-pole temperature pattern and associated wind and climate patterns would be altered.

13.
3 Marks
Suppose there is a rise in atmospheric temperature on Earth. How would this affect the cryosphere, hydrosphere and biosphere?
View Solution & Marking Scheme

Higher temperature can accelerate glacier and polar-ice melting in the cryosphere. Meltwater changes the hydrosphere and contributes to long-term sea-level rise. Changes in temperature, water availability, rainfall and habitats affect the biosphere.

14.
3 Marks
Explain how the Earth’s atmosphere helps in maintaining a suitable temperature for life to survive on the Earth.
View Solution & Marking Scheme

The atmosphere absorbs part of incoming radiation, while greenhouse gases absorb some outgoing infrared radiation. The natural greenhouse effect prevents Earth from becoming too cold. Excess greenhouse gases, however, cause excessive warming.

15.
4 Marks
Describe the interrelationship between different spheres of the Earth. Illustrate with example how these spheres function in a delicate balance.
View Solution & Marking Scheme

The geosphere, hydrosphere, cryosphere, atmosphere and biosphere exchange matter and energy. For example, reduced snowfall can lower summer lake levels, reducing grass available to animals; warming can accelerate glacier melt, alter rainfall and affect habitats.

The Journey Beyond

Investigations and real-world exploration from the textbook

  • Consider two hypothetical Earth-sized planets that have an atmosphere. Assume that one planet is entirely covered by oceans and the other is entirely by land. Knowing that the Sun heats the equator more than the poles, how would the wind patterns on these planets compare with the wind systems we observe on Earth, with its combination of land and sea?
  • Choose any one meal you ate recently (it could be anything, for example, roti and dal, rice and sambar, idli and chutney, and so on). For each main item in the meal, find out and explain: (i) how the carbon in it originally came from carbon dioxide in the air through photosynthesis, and (ii) how the nitrogen in it likely came from the atmosphere into the soil (for example, by bacteria or through the Haber-Bosch process and fertilisers) and then into the plant. Further, list other human activities involved in producing or cooking this meal that add extra carbon dioxide or nitrogen to the environment.
  • Using data from the India Meteorological Department (IMD) or newspaper records, find the average monsoon rainfall (June–September, or the local season) for your city or district for 5 years during two decades, such as the 1980s and 2020s. Note any trend you may find (increasing or decreasing, or the total number of days) with heavy rain (>50 mm). How can this be connected to the warmer Arabian Sea temperatures or changes in land use (forests to farms to cities) as discussed in the chapter?
