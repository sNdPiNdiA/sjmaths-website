Patterns in Life: Diversity and Classification

Master Revision: Diversity & Classification

Complete recall sheet — concepts, comparisons, mnemonics, traps and exam reasoning

Biodiversity = variety of living organisms and their forms/habitats. Classification = systematic grouping using meaningful similarities, differences and relationships.

5. Chordates and Vertebrates

Protochordates: notochord present at least once. Vertebrates: vertebral column protects vital organs and supports larger, more complex bodies.

Five vertebrate groups: Fish → Amphibians → Reptiles → Birds → Mammals.

Feature → function

Fish: fins + gills → aquatic movement/respiration. Birds: feathers + hollow bones → flight. Camels: fat storage → survival in harsh conditions. Polar bears: thick fur → cold adaptation. Mammals: mammary glands → survival of young.

6. Classification Hierarchy

Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species

Move downward → group size generally decreases → common features increase.

Tiger

Animalia → Chordata → Vertebrata → Mammalia → Carnivora → Felidae → Panthera → P. tigris

7. Binomial Nomenclature

  • Two parts: genus + species.
  • Genus begins with capital letter.
  • Species begins with lowercase letter.
  • Printed name italicised; handwritten name underlined.

Examples: Panthera tigris, Mangifera indica.

Mnemonic: G-S-C-L

Genus first, Species second, Capital genus, Lowercase species.

8. Timeline of Classification

Aristotle → Two Kingdom → Three Kingdom → Four Kingdom → Whittaker Five Kingdom → Woese Three Domains.

Memory: 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 3 domains. The system changes because scientific evidence changes.

9. Fossils, Biodiversity Threats & Conservation

  • Fossils preserve evidence of past life.
  • Older rock layers generally contain simpler forms; newer layers show more complex forms as described in the chapter.
  • Threats: pollution, deforestation, overuse of resources, climate change.
  • Species loss can affect dependent organisms and ecological balance.
  • Conservation protects habitats as well as individual species.

Chapter case: phumdis of Loktak Lake support endemic Sangai deer; habitat degeneration threatens the population.

10. High-Frequency Exam Traps

  • Endemic ≠ endangered: endemic means naturally restricted to a region.
  • Habitat ≠ classification: organisms sharing habitat can be unrelated.
  • Monera ≠ Protista: prokaryotic vs eukaryotic.
  • Fungi ≠ plants: fungi are heterotrophic and have chitin walls.
  • Bryophyta ≠ Pteridophyta: vascular tissue is the major distinction in this chapter.
  • Gymnosperm ≠ Angiosperm: naked seeds vs seeds enclosed in fruits.
  • Arthropoda: jointed appendages + exoskeleton.
  • Echinodermata: internal calcium-carbonate skeleton.
  • Genus is more specific than class: lower rank → fewer organisms, more common features.
  • Five kingdoms are not the final word: genetic evidence led to the three-domain system.

11. 60-Second Recall

Biodiversity → classify → compare → relate → conserve.

Five kingdoms: Monera | Protista | Fungi | Plantae | Animalia.

Plants: T-B-P-G-A.

Invertebrates: P-C-P-N-A-A-M-E.

Hierarchy: K-P-C-O-F-G-S.

Binomial: Genus capital + species lowercase + italics/underline.

Domains: Bacteria | Archaea | Eukarya.

Science rule: new evidence can change classification.
