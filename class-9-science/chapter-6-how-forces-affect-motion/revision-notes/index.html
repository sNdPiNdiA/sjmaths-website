How Forces Affect Motion: Revision Notes & Mnemonics

Summary

60-Second Chapter Summary

Master the core of force and motion in under a minute:

  • Force: A vector quantity needing direction + magnitude + unit (SI unit: newton (N)). It can start, stop, speed up, slow down, or change the direction/shape of motion.
  • Net Force: Same-direction forces add; opposite-direction forces subtract. Motion depends only on the net force.
  • Friction: Always opposes motion, depends on the surface, and usually needs a counter-force to maintain motion.
  • Newton's First Law (Inertia): Object stays at rest or moves with constant velocity unless a net force acts.
  • Newton's Second Law: $F = ma$; acceleration is in the direction of the net force and inversely proportional to mass. Weight: $W = mg$ ($g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$).
  • Newton's Third Law: Every action has an equal and opposite reaction acting on a different object (walking, rockets, recoil).
  • Systems: Treat connected objects as one system; acceleration $a = F/(m_1 + m_2)$.
Memory Hooks

Custom Mnemonics

Use these quick brain hacks to ensure you never confuse the laws or formulas in exams:

Mnemonic 1: The Three Laws — "Behave Like-Nice Person"
  • 1st — Behave: an object keeps doing its thing (rest or constant velocity) unless disturbed.
  • 2nd — Like Net: F = m × a links net force, mass, acceleration.
  • 3rd — Pairs: every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
Mnemonic 2: Opposite forces "Subtract-Away"
  • Opposite-direction forces → Subtract their magnitudes.
  • Same-direction forces → Add their magnitudes.
  • Watch the win: net force points along the larger force.
Mnemonic 3: "g-grab the mass" for Weight
  • Weight W = m × g. Remember g = 9.8 m/s² (≈ 10 for quick checks).
  • The acceleration due to gravity g does not depend on the mass of the object.

Formulas

Essential Formula Sheet

Newton's Second Law $$F = ma \qquad a = \frac{F}{m}$$
Weight (Gravitational Force) $$W = mg \qquad (g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2} \approx 10\text{ m s}^{-2})$$
Acceleration of a Connected System $$a = \frac{F}{m_1 + m_2}$$
Useful Kinematic Equation $$v^2 = u^2 + 2as \qquad s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2 \qquad v = u + at$$
Unit Conversions to Remember

km/h → m/s: multiply by 5/18 (e.g., 108 km/h = 30 m/s).
1 N = 1 kg m s⁻². A force of ~1 N is felt by holding a 100 g mass in your palm.

Concepts

Must-Know Concepts & Diagram Checklist

  • Force as a vector: always state direction, magnitude, and unit (newton).
  • Net force: add same-direction, subtract opposite-direction forces.
  • Free-body forces on a pushed box: applied force, friction, weight (down), normal force (up).
  • Newton's three laws with statement and one daily example each.
  • Action–reaction: forces act on two different objects, so they do not cancel.
  • Position-time & velocity-time graphs for rest and constant velocity (First Law).
  • Velocity-time graph + F = ma to find force (e.g., sports car Example 6.6).
