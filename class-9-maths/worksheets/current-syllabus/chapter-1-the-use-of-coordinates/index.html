Question 6

Explain how assigning coordinates to a geometric point connects geometry with algebra. On a grid, mark A(2,1) and B(−1,3), and describe B relative to A.

Show step-wise answer

B is 3 units left and 2 units above A; coordinates allow geometric properties to be expressed algebraically.

  1. Two perpendicular axes and a common origin fix the reference system.
  2. An ordered pair records signed horizontal and vertical displacements; equations can then describe sets of geometric points.
  3. B − A = (−1 − 2, 3 − 1) = (−3,2). Plot using these displacements.

Question 7

Find the midpoint of A(−2,5) and B(6,−1). Verify it by comparing the displacements from each endpoint.

Show step-wise answer

M = (2,2).

  1. Average x-coordinates: (−2 + 6)/2 = 2.
  2. Average y-coordinates: (5 − 1)/2 = 2.
  3. M − A = (4,−3) and B − M = (4,−3), so M is equally placed along the segment.

Question 8

A rectangular classroom has grid vertices (0,0), (8,0), (8,6), (0,6), with 1 grid unit = 1 metre. Mark the centre and find the straight-line distance from (0,0) to the opposite corner.

Show step-wise answer

Centre (4,3); diagonal 10 m.

  1. Draw a rectangle 8 units wide and 6 units high using the given coordinates.
  2. The centre is the midpoint of opposite corners: ((0 + 8)/2, (0 + 6)/2) = (4,3).
  3. The diagonal length is √(8² + 6²) = √100 = 10 m.

Question 9

Use distances to decide whether A(0,0), B(2,2) and C(5,5) are collinear.

Show step-wise answer

They are collinear, with B between A and C.

  1. AB = √8 = 2√2; BC = √18 = 3√2.
  2. AC = √50 = 5√2.
  3. AB + BC = AC, so equality in the triangle inequality places B on segment AC.

Question 10

Check whether A(0,0), B(4,0), C(0,3) form a right-angled triangle. Identify the right-angle vertex.

Show step-wise answer

The triangle is right-angled at A.

  1. AB² = 16 and AC² = 9.
  2. BC² = (4 − 0)² + (0 − 3)² = 25.
  3. AB² + AC² = BC²; by the converse of Pythagoras the angle opposite BC, at A, is 90°.

Question 11

M(3,−1) is the midpoint of A(−2,4) and B. Find B and state its quadrant.

Show step-wise answer

B = (8,−6), in Quadrant IV.

  1. (−2 + xB)/2 = 3 gives xB = 8.

  3. A positive x and negative y place B in Quadrant IV.

Question 12

Explain why an ordered pair must be ordered. Plot P(2,5) and Q(5,2) and explain how coordinate geometry lets us compare their positions without relying on an approximate picture.

Show step-wise answer

The first coordinate is horizontal and the second vertical; P and Q are different points.

  1. An ordered pair assigns different roles to its first and second entries.
  2. P is 2 units right and 5 up; Q is 5 right and 2 up.
  3. Subtracting coordinates gives Q − P = (3,−3). Algebra makes this comparison exact and independent of drawing accuracy.

Distance and midpoint · 3 practice marks

Question 13

Find the distance and midpoint of P(2,−3) and Q(8,5). Explain the different roles of differences and averages.

Show step-wise answer

PQ = 10 units; midpoint (5,1).

  1. The changes are 8 − 2 = 6 and 5 − (−3) = 8.
  2. Pythagoras gives PQ = √(6² + 8²) = 10.
  3. Average the respective coordinates: ((2 + 8)/2, (−3 + 5)/2) = (5,1). Differences measure displacement; averages locate the halfway point.

Question 14

Derive the distance formula for A(x₁,y₁) and B(x₂,y₂) using a right triangle. Apply it to A(−3,−2) and B(5,4).

Show step-wise answer

AB = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²); the example distance is 10.

  1. Draw horizontal and vertical segments between the two points to form a right triangle.
  2. Their lengths are |x₂ − x₁| and |y₂ − y₁|.
  3. Pythagoras gives the squared distance as the sum of the two squared coordinate differences.
  4. For the example, the differences are 8 and 6; √(64 + 36) = 10.

Question 15

Describe a systematic algorithm to check if three distinct coordinate points form a triangle, and then whether that triangle is right-angled. Test A(1,1), B(4,1), C(4,5).

Show step-wise answer

The points form a triangle right-angled at B.

  1. First test collinearity, for example whether (xB − xA)(yC − yA) = (yB − yA)(xC − xA). If equal, there is no non-degenerate triangle.
  2. For the given points the products are 12 and 0, so they are not collinear.
  3. Compute the three squared distances: AB² = 9, BC² = 16, AC² = 25.
  4. Sort these values. The two smaller sum to the largest, so the angle at B is right.

Question 16

On a floor-plan grid, a straight corridor joins P(−1,2) to Q(7,8), with each unit representing 0.5 m. Find its actual length and the coordinates of a camera placed halfway along it.

Show step-wise answer

Length 5 m; camera at (3,5).

  1. The grid displacement is (8,6), so the grid length is √100 = 10.
  2. Multiply by the scale 0.5 m per unit to get 5 m.
  3. The halfway coordinates are ((−1 + 7)/2, (2 + 8)/2) = (3,5).
  4. Coordinates remain in grid units; do not multiply them by the physical length scale unless explicitly converting the coordinate system.

Question 17

P(1,3), Q(4,9) and R(t,2t + 1) are given. Prove R lies on the line PQ for every real t. When is R strictly between P and Q?

Show step-wise answer

R lies on y = 2x + 1; it is strictly between P and Q when 1 < t < 4.

  1. P and Q both satisfy y = 2x + 1.
  2. Their x-coordinates differ, so they determine this one line.
  3. R also satisfies the equation by construction.
  4. Along this non-vertical line the x-coordinate orders the points. Endpoints correspond to t = 1 and t = 4.
