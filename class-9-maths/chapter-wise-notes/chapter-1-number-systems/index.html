Rational Numbers on the Number Line

Every rational number sits at a clear point. Use equal parts of an interval to plot fractions precisely.

  1. To plot \( \frac{3}{4} \), divide the segment from 0 to 1 into 4 equal parts and count 3 parts.
  2. To plot \( -\frac{2}{3} \), divide the segment from 0 to -1 into 3 equal parts and count 2 parts to the left.

Finding a rational number between two rationals: Use the midpoint or a common denominator. For example, between \( \frac{1}{3} \) and \( \frac{1}{2} \), the midpoint is \( \frac{\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{2}}{2} = \frac{5}{12} \). This method always gives one rational between any two different rationals.

🧩 Problem Decomposition: Rational Density

To find \(n\) rational numbers between \(x\) and \(y\), don't just guess. Break it down:

  1. Make denominators same.
  2. Multiply by \((n+1)\) if needed to create a large enough numerator gap.
  3. List the integers in between.
The Density Property (Formal Proof)

Property: Between any two rational numbers, there are infinitely many rational numbers.

1. Let \(a\) and \(b\) be two rational numbers such that \(a < b\).
2. Their average \(m = \frac{a+b}{2}\) is also rational because the sum of two rationals is rational and dividing by 2 gives another rational.
3. Proof of \(a < m < b\): Since \(a < b\), adding \(a\) to both sides gives \(2a < a+b \Rightarrow a < \frac{a+b}{2}\). Adding \(b\) to both sides gives \(a+b < 2b \Rightarrow \frac{a+b}{2} < b\).
4. Example: Between \( \frac{2}{5} \) and \( \frac{3}{5} \), the midpoint is \( \frac{5}{10} = 0.5 \), which is rational.
5. Algorithm: For \( \frac{p}{q} < \frac{r}{s} \), rewrite both with denominator \( qs \), then choose any integer between the adjusted numerators to get a new rational number.
6. Repeat the midpoint step again and again to produce infinitely many rationals between \(a\) and \(b\).

Proof of Irrationality (Contradiction Method)

This is a core Algorithmic Thinking tool. We prove \( \sqrt{2} \) is irrational by showing that assuming it is rational leads to a logical disaster.

S1 Assume \( \sqrt{2} = \frac{p}{q} \) where \( p, q \) are co-prime integers (\( q \neq 0 \)).
S2 Square both sides: \( 2 = \frac{p^2}{q^2} \Rightarrow p^2 = 2q^2 \). This means \( p^2 \) is even, so \( p \) is even (\( p = 2k \)).
S3 Substitute \( p = 2k \): \( (2k)^2 = 2q^2 \Rightarrow 4k^2 = 2q^2 \Rightarrow q^2 = 2k^2 \). This means \( q^2 \) is even, so \( q \) is even.
S4 If both \( p \) and \( q \) are even, they have 2 as a common factor. This **contradicts** our assumption that they are co-prime.
S5 Therefore, our assumption was wrong, and \( \sqrt{2} \) is irrational.

Proof that \( \sqrt{3} \) is Irrational

The same contradiction method works. Assume \( \sqrt{3} = \frac{p}{q} \) in lowest terms.

S1Square both sides: \(3 = \frac{p^2}{q^2}\), so \(p^2 = 3q^2\).
S2Then \(p^2\) is divisible by 3, so \(p\) is divisible by 3. Write \(p = 3k\).
S3Substitute: \(9k^2 = 3q^2 \Rightarrow q^2 = 3k^2\). So \(q\) is also divisible by 3.
S4Both \(p\) and \(q\) share factor 3, contradicting lowest terms.
S5Conclusion: \( \sqrt{3} \) must be irrational.

The Square Root Spiral

The square root spiral builds each new root using the previous one. Start with a right triangle of sides 1 and 1, then add another right triangle using the last hypotenuse as one leg and 1 as the other leg.

1.Begin with triangle OAB where OA = 1 and AB = 1; OB = \( \sqrt{2} \).
2.From B draw BC = 1 perpendicular to OB. Then OC = \( \sqrt{3} \).
3.From C draw CD = 1 perpendicular to OC. Then OD = \( \sqrt{4} = 2 \).
4.Repeat to generate future lengths like \( \sqrt{5}, \sqrt{6}, \sqrt{7} \).

5. Rationalisation

Rationalisation means removing the square root from the denominator by multiplying the numerator and denominator by a suitable factor.

Standard Forms:
  • For \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}\), multiply by \(\frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{a}}\).
  • For \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{a} \pm \sqrt{b}}\), multiply by the conjugate \(\frac{\sqrt{a} \mp \sqrt{b}}{\sqrt{a} \mp \sqrt{b}}\).
Identity: \( (\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}) = a - b \)

This identity creates a rational number from two irrationals. We use the conjugate (change the sign in the middle) to rationalise.

Example: Rationalise \( \frac{1}{2 + \sqrt{3}} \)

Multiply num & den by \( 2 - \sqrt{3} \)

Denominator becomes \( (2)^2 - (\sqrt{3})^2 = 4 - 3 = 1 \)

Result: \( 2 - \sqrt{3} \)

6. Laws of Exponents

For Real number \( a > 0 \) and rational numbers \( p, q \):

Rational Exponents:
\( a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a^m} = (\sqrt[n]{a})^m \)

(where \(n\) is a positive integer and \(a\) is a positive real number)

Rule Formula Example
Product \( a^p \cdot a^q = a^{p+q} \) \( 2^3 \cdot 2^2 = 2^5 \)
Power \( (a^p)^q = a^{pq} \) \( (2^3)^2 = 2^6 \)
Quotient \( \frac{a^p}{a^q} = a^{p-q} \) \( \frac{7^5}{7^3} = 7^2 \)
Negative \( a^{-p} = \frac{1}{a^p} \) \( 2^{-3} = \frac{1}{8} \)

Chapter Summary

Let's recap the key concepts before the quiz!

  • Rational Numbers (\(Q\)): Can be written as \( \frac{p}{q} \). Decimals are either terminating or recurring.
  • Irrational Numbers (\(T\)): Cannot be written as \( \frac{p}{q} \). Decimals are non-terminating and non-recurring (e.g., \( \sqrt{2}, \pi \)).
  • Real Numbers (\(R\)): The collection of all Rational and Irrational numbers. The **Density Property** ensures infinite points between any two rationals.
  • Irrationality Proof: We use the **Contradiction Method** (logical reversal) to prove \( \sqrt{2} \) and \( \sqrt{3} \) are irrational.
  • Rationalisation: To remove a root from the denominator of \( \frac{1}{\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}} \), multiply by the conjugate \( \sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b} \).
  • Laws of Exponents: Algorithmic rules: \( a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n} \), \( (a^m)^n = a^{mn} \), and \( a^0 = 1 \).
