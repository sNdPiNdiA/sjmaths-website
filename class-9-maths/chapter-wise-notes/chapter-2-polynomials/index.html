Foundation

1. Algebraic Expressions and the Meaning of a Polynomial

An algebraic expression is made using numbers, variables, and operations. For example, \( 3x + 5 \), \( 7 - 2n \), and \( 4a^2 + a - 9 \) are algebraic expressions.

Variable

A letter whose value can change. Example: \( x \), \( n \), \( y \).

Constant

A fixed number. Example: \( 5 \), \( -3 \), \( \frac{1}{2} \).

Coefficient

The number multiplying a variable. In \( 8x \), the coefficient of \( x \) is 8.

\( p(x) = 4x^2 - 3x + 7 \) Polynomial because the powers of \( x \) are whole numbers: 2, 1, and 0.
  • A polynomial has variables with whole number powers.
  • A polynomial can have one term, two terms, or many terms.
  • If a variable is in the denominator, or has a negative or fractional power, the expression is not a polynomial.

Terms and Degree

2. Terms, Coefficients, and Degree of a Polynomial

Each part separated by a plus or minus sign is called a term. The degree of a polynomial is the highest power of the variable.

\( 6x - 11 \)
Degree 1

Terms: \( 6x \), \( -11 \)

Coefficient of \( x \): 6

Constant term: -11

\( 3x^3 + 2x^2 - x + 5 \)
Degree 3

Terms: \( 3x^3 \), \( 2x^2 \), \( -x \), \( 5 \)

Coefficient of \( x^2 \): 2

Highest power: 3

\( 9 \)
Degree 0

This is a constant polynomial.

There is no variable term.

Degree Name Example
0 Constant polynomial \( 7 \)
1 Linear polynomial \( 4x - 5 \)
2 Quadratic polynomial \( x^2 + 3x + 1 \)
3 Cubic polynomial \( 2x^3 - x \)

Fast Rule

In a polynomial written in one variable, the highest power tells you the degree. If the highest power is 1, it is a linear polynomial. That is the main focus of the new chapter.

Linear Polynomials

3. Introduction to Linear Polynomials and Applications

A linear polynomial in one variable has degree 1. Its usual form is \( ax + b \), where \( a \neq 0 \).

\( p(x) = ax + b \) The output changes by a fixed amount whenever \( x \) changes by 1.

Linear Growth

If the coefficient of \( x \) is positive, the value keeps increasing at a steady rate.

Example: Savings after \( x \) weeks: \( S(x) = 200x + 500 \)

Linear Decay

If the coefficient of \( x \) is negative, the value decreases at a steady rate.

Example: Water left after \( x \) minutes: \( W(x) = 60 - 4x \)

Initial Value

The constant term tells you where the situation starts when \( x = 0 \).

Example: In \( y = 5x + 12 \), the starting value is 12.

Taxi Fare

\( F(x) = 15x + 40 \)

Base fare 40, then 15 rupees for each kilometer.

Cooling Drink

\( T(x) = 32 - 2x \)

The temperature drops by 2 degrees every minute.

Notebook Pages

\( P(x) = 24x + 12 \)

Each pack adds 24 pages and there are already 12 pages in use.

Relationships and Graphs

5. Linear Relationships, Slope, y-intercept, and Graphs

A linear relationship can be shown in three connected ways: equation, table, and graph. If the equation is \( y = ax + b \), then:

Slope \( a \)

The slope tells us how much \( y \) changes when \( x \) increases by 1.

y-intercept \( b \)

The line cuts the y-axis at \( (0, b) \).

Graph

The graph of a linear equation in two variables is a straight line.

Computational Thinking

6. How to Build a Linear Rule Step by Step

The syllabus explicitly asks you to use computational thinking. That means you should be able to follow a clear process instead of guessing.

  1. Observe the pattern. Write the first few values in a table.
  2. Check the change. If the difference is constant, suspect a linear rule.
  3. Find the starting value. What is the output when \( x = 0 \)? That is \( b \).
  4. Find the rate. How much does the output change for each step of \( x \)? That is \( a \).
  5. Write the equation. Combine them to get \( y = ax + b \).
  6. Test the rule. Substitute two or three values to verify it.

Example

A plant is 8 cm tall now and grows 3 cm every week. Let \( x \) be the number of weeks and \( y \) be the height. Then the rule is \( y = 3x + 8 \).

At \( x = 0 \), \( y = 8 \). Each week the height increases by 3, so the slope is 3.
