Indian Independence Act, 1947

Enacted by the British Parliament on July 18, 1947, based on the Mountbatten Plan (June 3 Plan), this Act brought an end to British rule in India and established two independent dominions.

Key Provisions

  • End of British Rule: Declared India as an independent and sovereign state from August 15, 1947.
  • Partition: Provided for the partition of India into two independent dominions: India and Pakistan, with the right to secede from the British Commonwealth.
  • Abolition of Viceroy: Abolished the office of the Viceroy and provided for a Governor-General for each dominion, to be appointed by the British King on the advice of the dominion cabinet.
  • Constituent Assemblies: Empowered the Constituent Assemblies of the two dominions to frame and adopt any constitution and to repeal any act of the British Parliament, including the Independence Act itself.
  • Legislative Sovereignty: Until the new constitutions were framed, the governance of the dominions was to be carried on in accordance with the Government of India Act, 1935 (with necessary modifications).
  • Termination of Suzerainty: Lapsed the British paramountcy over the Princely States and treaty relations with tribal areas. States were free to join either India or Pakistan or remain independent.
  • Secretary of State: Abolished the office of the Secretary of State for India and transferred his functions to the Secretary of State for Commonwealth Affairs.
  • Civil Services: Discontinued the appointment to civil services and reservation of posts by the Secretary of State. Existing officers were entitled to receive the same benefits as before.

Key Implications

  • Dominion Status: India remained a dominion until the commencement of the Constitution on January 26, 1950.
  • Legal Continuity: Ensured legal continuity by allowing the 1935 Act to function as the interim constitutional framework.
  • Royal Titles: Dropped the title of 'Emperor of India' from the royal titles of the British monarch.
