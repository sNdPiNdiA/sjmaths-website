Amending Act of 1781 (The Declaratory Act)

The Amending Act of 1781 was enacted by the British Parliament to rectify the defects and practical difficulties created by the Regulating Act of 1773. It is also known as the Declaratory Act.

Key Objectives

  • To remove the ambiguities and conflicts between the Supreme Court of Calcutta and the Governor-General-in-Council.
  • To define the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and protect the servants of the Company from its interference.

Major Provisions

  • Exemption for Company Officials: It exempted the Governor-General and the Council from the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court for acts done in their official capacity.
  • Jurisdiction Limitation: The Supreme Court’s jurisdiction was restricted to Calcutta. It was mandated to administer the personal laws of the defendants (Hindus according to Hindu law, Muslims according to Mohammedan law).
  • Revenue Matters: The Supreme Court was stripped of its jurisdiction over matters concerning revenue collection or acts done in the collection of revenue.
  • Appellate Authority: It mandated that appeals from the Provincial Courts should be taken to the Governor-General-in-Council, not the Supreme Court.
  • Legislative Power: The Governor-General-in-Council was empowered to frame regulations for the Provincial Courts and Councils.

Significance for UPSC

  • Constitutional Evolution: It marked the first attempt by the British Parliament to define the relationship between the executive and the judiciary in India.
  • Shift in Power: It clearly established the supremacy of the Governor-General-in-Council over the Supreme Court in administrative and revenue matters.
  • Legal Pluralism: It formally recognized the importance of local customs and religious laws in the judicial process.
