Amending Act of 1786

The Amending Act of 1786 was enacted to facilitate the appointment of Lord Cornwallis as the Governor-General of Bengal. Cornwallis insisted on specific powers before accepting the position, leading to this legislative adjustment.

Key Provisions

  • Power to Override Council: The Governor-General was empowered to override the decision of his Council and act on his own responsibility in special cases.
  • Dual Role: The Act combined the offices of the Governor-General of Bengal and the Commander-in-Chief.
  • Purpose of Appointment: These provisions were specifically tailored to ensure that Lord Cornwallis had absolute authority to implement administrative and judicial reforms in India.

Significance for UPSC

  • Precedent: The power to override the Council became a standard feature for subsequent Governor-Generals, strengthening the executive authority of the office.
  • Administrative Centralization: This Act marked a significant step toward centralizing power within the British administration in India.
  • Context: It reflects the British government's shift toward appointing high-profile, trusted individuals to manage the growing complexities of the East India Company's territories.
