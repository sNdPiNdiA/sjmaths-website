Decline of the Mauryan Empire

A critical analysis of the disintegration of India's first pan-Indian empire, examining the interplay of internal instability and external pressures.

Syllabus Core Study Notes (Deep-Dive)

Master the key concepts and dimensions of this topic.

1. Core Study Notes

Core Regions

  • North-Western frontiers lost first.
  • Core Magadha remained central.
  • Dakshinapatha became autonomous.

Key Figures

  • Ashoka: The last great king.
  • Brihadratha: The last monarch.
  • Pushyamitra: The usurper.

The Structural Decay

  • Administrative Centralization: The inability of weak successors to manage a vast, diverse empire from Pataliputra.
  • Economic Strain: High cost of maintaining a massive bureaucracy and standing army leading to tax exploitation.
  • Brahminical Reaction: Theory proposed by H.P. Sastri; claim that Ashoka's pro-Buddhist/anti-sacrifice stance alienated the priestly class.
  • Succession Crisis: Absence of a clear primogeniture law led to civil wars.
