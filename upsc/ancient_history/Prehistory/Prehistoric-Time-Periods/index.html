Evolution of Prehistoric Cultures

Paleolithic Age
Old Stone Age · c. 2.6 MYA – 10,000 BCE
Earliest & longest phase. Humans were nomadic hunter-gatherers; used crude stone tools (hand-axes, choppers, cleavers); discovered fire; lived in caves & rock shelters; no knowledge of agriculture/pottery. Divided into Lower, Middle & Upper Paleolithic. Tools made of quartzite — hence called "Quartzite Man".

Key sites: Soan Valley (Pakistan) — oldest; Bhimbetka (MP) — UNESCO site, rock art; Attirampakkam (TN) — Acheulian; Hunsgi (Karnataka); Belan Valley (UP); Didwana (Rajasthan).

Mesolithic Age
Middle Stone Age · c. 10,000 – 8,000 BCE
Transitional phase between Paleolithic & Neolithic. Marked by microliths (tiny geometric stone tools — triangles, crescents, trapezes). Incipient domestication of animals began; semi-nomadic lifestyle; first human burials with grave goods. Rock art continued with group hunting & dancing scenes.

Key sites: Bagor (Rajasthan) — largest Mesolithic site in India; Adamgarh (MP) — earliest evidence of animal domestication; Langhnaj (Gujarat); Sarai Nahar Rai (UP); Bhimbetka (continues from Paleolithic).
Neolithic Age
New Stone Age · c. 8,000 – 2,000 BCE
Neolithic Revolution — the greatest turning point in human history. Shift from food-gathering → food-producing. Characterised by settled agriculture, polished stone tools, wheel-made pottery, domestication of animals, and village life. Crops: wheat, barley, rice (regional). Animals: sheep, goat, cattle. Invention of the wheel.

Key sites: Mehrgarh (Balochistan) — earliest Neolithic (c. 7000 BCE), earliest evidence of agriculture & pastoralism in South Asia; Burzahom (J&K) — pit-dwellings, bone tools, dog burial; Gufkral (J&K); Chirand (Bihar); Koldihwa (UP) — earliest rice; Daojali Hading (Assam) — shouldered axes.

2K
Chalcolithic Age
Copper-Stone Age · c. 2,000 – 1,500 BCE
First use of copper (metal) alongside stone. Hallmark pottery: Black & Red Ware (BRW). Agriculture-based village settlements; rice, cattle-herding, fishing. Mostly pre-urban and non-Harappan.

Regional Chalcolithic cultures:
• Malwa Culture (MP) — Navdatoli, Maheshwar; BRW, rice-wheat
• Jorwe Culture (Maharashtra) — Inamgaon, Daimabad; most excavated site = Inamgaon; Daimabad = bronze chariot, elephants, rhinos
• Kayatha Culture (MP) — earliest Chalcolithic in MP; Kayatha, Eran
• Ahar/Banas Culture (Rajasthan) — Ahar; copper smelting evidence
• Prabhas & Rangpur (Gujarat) — Late Harappan-Chalcolithic overlap
Early Iron Age
c. 1,500 BCE onward
Iron smelting technology revolutionised agriculture (iron ploughshare → surplus) & warfare. Led to Second Urbanisation (6th cent. BCE). Associated with megalithic burials (dolmens, menhirs, stone circles) across South India — Brahmagiri, Maski, Adichanallur. Pottery: Painted Grey Ware (PGW) → Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW).

Key sites: Hallur (Karnataka) — earliest iron in South India (c. 1000 BCE); Malhar (UP) — early iron smelting; Dadupur (UP); Jakhera (UP). Megalithic sites: Brahmagiri (Karnataka), Maski (Karnataka), Adichanallur (TN), Junapani (Maharashtra). PGW sites: Hastinapur, Ahichchhatra — associated with early Vedic period & Mahabharata.

Period-wise Detailed Notes

🪨

Paleolithic Age (Old Stone Age)

  • Time: 2.6 MYA – 10,000 BCE (99% of human history)
  • Climate: Ice Age / Pleistocene — severe glaciation & inter-glacial phases
  • Sub-divisions:
    • Lower Paleolithic: Hand-axes, cleavers, bifaces — Abbevillian & Acheulian traditions; sites: Soan Valley, Attirampakkam, Bhimbetka, Hunsgi
    • Middle Paleolithic: Flake tools, scrapers, points — Nevasan tradition (Nevasa, Maharashtra); sites: Nevasa, Pushkar, Belan Valley
    • Upper Paleolithic: Blade tools, burins, bone tools — sites: Bhimbetka, Belan Valley, Renigunta (AP), Patne (Maharashtra)
  • Tools: Quartzite hand-axes, cleavers, choppers, flakes, blades, burins — called "Quartzite Man"
  • Key sites: Soan Valley (Pakistan) — oldest; Attirampakkam (TN) — oldest Acheulian (1.5 MYA); Bhimbetka (MP) — UNESCO; Hunsgi-Baichbal Valley (Karnataka) — dense Acheulian; Belan Valley (UP) — continuous sequence; Didwana (Rajasthan); Nevasa (Maharashtra); Patne (Maharashtra) — ostrich eggshell beads
  • Society: Nomadic hunter-gatherers; lived in caves & rock shelters; use of fire (Kurnool caves, Bhimbetka)
  • Art: Bhimbetka rock paintings — hunting scenes, animals, stylised humans; UNESCO World Heritage
  • Key UPSC point: Oldest human traces in Indian subcontinent = Soan Valley & Attirampakkam; ostrich eggshell beads found at Patne indicate symbolic behaviour
🔹

Mesolithic Age (Middle Stone Age)

  • Time: c. 10,000 – 8,000 BCE; Post-Pleistocene / Holocene — warmer climate
  • Tools: Microliths — tiny (1-5 cm) geometric stone tools (triangles, crescents, trapezes, lunates); composite tools (hafted on wood/bone)
  • Subsistence: Hunting-fishing-gathering + incipient domestication — transition stage; first evidence of animal domestication at Adamgarh (MP)
  • Society: Semi-nomadic; first evidence of human burials with grave goods — indicates belief in after-life (Sarai Nahar Rai, Langhnaj)
  • Art: Rock art continued from Paleolithic — group hunting, communal dances, ritual scenes; Bhimbetka, Adamgarh
  • Key sites:
    • Bagor (Rajasthan) — largest Mesolithic site in India; on river Kothari; earliest radiocarbon date c. 5500 BCE; three cultural phases
    • Adamgarh (MP) — earliest evidence of animal domestication (cattle, sheep, goat)
    • Langhnaj (Gujarat) — microliths, human burials
    • Sarai Nahar Rai (UP) — earliest radiocarbon-dated Mesolithic site (c. 8000 BCE); burials with skeletons in extended position
    • Bhimbetka — rock shelters with Mesolithic paintings
  • Key UPSC point: Mesolithic is the bridge between Paleolithic hunting-gathering and Neolithic farming; microliths indicate greater efficiency; Bagor is a crucial site (radiocarbon dated, three phases)
🌾

Neolithic Age (New Stone Age)

  • Time: c. 8,000 – 2,000 BCE; varies regionally; Holocene
  • Neolithic Revolution: Term coined by V. Gordon Childe — shift from food-gathering → food-producing; most significant turning point in human history
  • Key features:
    • Settled agriculture: Wheat, barley (NW India); rice (eastern India — Koldihwa, c. 6000 BCE)
    • Domestication: Sheep, goat, cattle, dog (Burzahom — dog burial)
    • Polished & ground stone tools: Axes, adzes, chisels, celts
    • Pottery: Wheel-made; grey ware, black-burnished ware, cord-impressed ware
    • Housing: Mud-brick houses (Mehrgarh); pit-dwellings (Burzahom)
    • Invention of wheel — revolutionised pottery & transport
  • Key sites:
    • Mehrgarh (Balochistan, Pakistan) — earliest Neolithic (c. 7000 BCE); aceramic phase → ceramic phase; earliest evidence of agriculture & pastoralism in South Asia; dental drill evidence
    • Burzahom (J&K) — pit-dwellings (subterranean); bone tools; dog burial with master; grey ware; radical difference from other Neolithic sites
    • Gufkral (J&K) — similar to Burzahom; pit-dwellings, polished stone tools, cattle domestication
    • Chirand (Bihar) — Neolithic on Ganga plains; rice cultivation; bone & stone tools
    • Koldihwa (UP) — earliest evidence of rice (c. 6000 BCE)
    • Daojali Hading (Assam) — shouldered axes, cord-impressed pottery; links with SE Asia
  • Key UPSC point: Mehrgarh is the earliest farming site in South Asia; Neolithic laid foundation for civilisation; regional variation is important (NW wheat-barley vs. E rice); Burzahom's pit-dwellings are unique
⛏️

Chalcolithic Age (Copper-Stone Age)

  • Time: c. 2,000 – 1,500 BCE (overlaps with & post-dates Neolithic in most regions)
  • Characteristics: First use of metal (copper) alongside stone tools; Black & Red Ware (BRW) — hallmark pottery; painted designs in white; agriculture-based village settlements; pre-urban, non-Harappan
  • Regional Chalcolithic cultures (very important for UPSC):
    • Malwa Culture: Navdatoli, Maheshwar, Eran (MP); BRW with white paintings; wheat, rice, cattle; Daimabad bronzes (chariot, elephant, rhinoceros, buffalo) — found in Malwa context but may be Harappan
    • Jorwe Culture (Maharashtra): Inamgaon, Daimabad (most extensively excavated Chalcolithic site = Inamgaon); four phases; houses of mud & wattle-and-daub; evidence of irrigation (canal); burial practices (urn burials, pot burials); Daimabad bronzes
    • Kayatha Culture (MP): Kayatha, Eran — earliest Chalcolithic in MP (c. 2000 BCE); white-painted BRW; copper objects; buttons, rings
    • Ahar/Banas Culture (Rajasthan): Ahar, Balathal, Gilund — earliest copper smelting in India; BRW; rice cultivation; rectangular & circular houses
    • Prabhas Culture (Saurashtra): Somnath — Harappan-Chalcolithic overlap
  • Economy: Agriculture (wheat, barley, rice — rice at Inamgaon, Lothal), cattle-herding, fishing, hunting; trade in copper (Khetri mines, Rajasthan)
  • Decline: Chalcolithic cultures declined due to flood, climate change, soil exhaustion, and Iron Age transition
  • Key UPSC point: Inamgaon is the most excavated Chalcolithic site (HD Sankalia); Daimabad bronzes are unique; distinction between Chalcolithic cultures is frequently asked; overlap with late Harappan in Gujarat
⚔️

Early Iron Age

  • Time: c. 1,500 BCE onward; iron appears in India by c. 1,800 BCE (earliest at Malhar, c. 1800 BCE)
  • Technology: Iron smelting & forging — harder & more abundant than copper/bronze; iron ploughshare → agricultural surplus → urbanisation
  • Key developments:
    • Megalithic Culture: Large stone burials (dolmens, menhirs, stone circles, cists, cairns) — predominantly South India (Karnataka, Kerala, TN, Andhra, Maharashtra); types: dolmenoid cists, port-holed cists, urn burials (Adichanallur); associated with iron implements & Black & Red Ware
    • Painted Grey Ware (PGW): c. 1100-600 BCE; associated with early Vedic period & Mahabharata sites (Hastinapur, Ahichchhatra, Kurukshetra); fine grey pottery with black paintings
    • Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW): c. 600 BCE onward; associated with Second Urbanisation & Mahajanapadas; luxurious, glossy, black-slipped ware
  • Key sites:
    • Hallur (Karnataka) — earliest iron in South India (c. 1000 BCE)
    • Malhar (UP) — earliest iron in India (c. 1800 BCE)
    • Dadupur (UP) — early iron (c. 1100 BCE)
    • Brahmagiri (Karnataka) — megalithic site; iron & BRW
    • Maski (Karnataka) — megalithic & Ashokan
    • Adichanallur (TN) — urn burials; iron weapons
    • Hastinapur (UP) — PGW site; associated with Mahabharata
  • Key UPSC point: Iron → agricultural surplus → Second Urbanisation (6th cent. BCE); distinction between PGW (early Vedic) & NBPW (Mahajanapada period); megaliths are primarily a South Indian Iron Age phenomenon but found in Maharashtra & Rajasthan too
🗿

Key UPSC Takeaways

  • Oldest human traces: Soan Valley (Pakistan) / Attirampakkam (TN) — Lower Paleolithic
  • Oldest Acheulian site: Attirampakkam (1.5 MYA, dated by cosmogenic nuclide burial dating)
  • Largest Mesolithic site: Bagor (Rajasthan)
  • Earliest animal domestication: Adamgarh (MP) — Mesolithic
  • Earliest agriculture: Mehrgarh (Balochistan) — Neolithic, c. 7000 BCE
  • Earliest rice: Koldihwa (UP) — Neolithic, c. 6000 BCE
  • First metal used: Copper — Chalcolithic Age
  • Most excavated Chalcolithic site: Inamgaon (Maharashtra)
  • Earliest iron: Malhar (UP) — c. 1800 BCE
  • Earliest iron in South India: Hallur (Karnataka) — c. 1000 BCE
  • UNESCO World Heritage: Bhimbetka rock shelters (all periods)
  • Sequence: Paleolithic → Mesolithic → Neolithic → Chalcolithic → Iron Age
  • Key scholars: HD Sankalia (excavated Inamgaon, Navdatoli); V. Gordon Childe (Neolithic Revolution); Robert Bruce Foote (discovered first Paleolithic tool in India — Pallavaram, 1863)

Sources of Information on Pre-History

🪦

Archaeological Sources

Primary sources — stone tools, pottery, bones, burials, cave paintings, house remains. Excavations at sites like Bhimbetka, Inamgaon, Burzahom reveal material culture, technology, and lifestyle. Lithic analysis (tool typology) & pottery typology help determine cultural sequences.

🧬

Fossil & Skeletal Remains

Human & animal bones help understand physical characteristics, diet, disease, and evolution. Rare in India due to acidic soil & tropical climate. Notable: Sarai Nahar Rai skeletons, Langhnaj burials, Burzahom dog burial. Palaeopathology reveals disease patterns.

⛰️

Geological & Geo-archaeology

Stratigraphy, river terrace analysis, and soil studies help date & contextualise sites. Belan Valley (UP) & Son Valley (MP) provide continuous Quaternary sequences. Pollen analysis reveals climate & vegetation change. Ostrich eggshells (Patne) indicate palaeoenvironment.

🔬

Scientific Dating Methods

Radiocarbon (C-14) — most common; dates organic material up to 50,000 years. Thermoluminescence (TL) — dates pottery & burnt clay. Potassium-Argon (K-Ar) — dates volcanic rock (older sites). Cosmogenic nuclide burial dating — used for Attirampakkam (1.5 MYA). Dendrochronology — tree-ring dating.

Geographical Distribution of Prehistoric Sites

🏔️

Northwest (Soan Valley)

Oldest Paleolithic — Soan Valley (Pakistan); Lower Paleolithic bifaces & pebble tools. Also Acheulian sites in Potwar Plateau.

🏞️

North & Central India

Belan & Son Valleys — continuous Paleolithic to Neolithic sequences. Bagor (Rajasthan) — largest Mesolithic site. Koldihwa — earliest rice.

🌄

Ganga Plains

Chirand (Bihar) — Neolithic; Sarai Nahar Rai — Mesolithic; Hastinapur — PGW/Iron Age; Malhar — earliest iron.

⛰️

Southern India

Attirampakkam (TN) — oldest Acheulian; Hunsgi-Baichbal (Karnataka) — dense Acheulian; Hallur — earliest South Indian iron. Megaliths at Brahmagiri, Maski, Adichanallur.

🏔️

Himalayan Region

Burzahom & Gufkral (J&K) — Neolithic pit-dwellings; bone tools; unique cultural tradition distinct from plains. Dog burial at Burzahom.

🌋

Deccan Plateau

Inamgaon, Daimabad, Navdatoli — Chalcolithic settlements. Jorwe & Malwa cultures. Nevasa — Middle Paleolithic type-site.

🏝️

Western India

Gujarat: Langhnaj (Mesolithic), Lothal & Prabhas (Chalcolithic-Harappan overlap). Rajasthan: Ahar, Balathal (Chalcolithic), Didwana (Paleolithic).

🗺️

Eastern & NE India

Odisha: Kuchai — Neolithic. Assam: Daojali Hading — shouldered axes, cord-impressed pottery, links with SE Asia. Bihar: Chirand — Neolithic rice.
