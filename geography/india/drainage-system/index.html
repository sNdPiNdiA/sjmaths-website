India

Drainage System: Comprehensive Geographic Analysis, Physical & Human Mechanisms, and Regional Distribution

High-yield conceptual breakdown and physical/human geographical foundations of Indian Drainage Systems tailored for UP TGT/PGT Geography and UGC-NET mastery.

UP PGT Geography UP TGT Social Science B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

The drainage system of India is structurally controlled by major tectonic and geomorphic epochs, prominently divided into the Himalayan (antecedent and superimposed) and Peninsular (sequent and superimposed) river systems. Originating from distinct geological basements, Indian rivers showcase diverse evolutionary histories, water divides, and spatial regimes influenced by monsoon climatology and structural tilting of the Indian landmass. Understanding drainage patterns—such as dendritic, trellis, rectangular, and radial—provides crucial insights into underlying lithology, structural weaknesses, and regional geomorphology essential for competitive examinations.

1. Evolution and Classification of Indian Drainage Systems

Geological antiquity and structural genesis differentiating Himalayan and Peninsular river architectures.

  • Himalayan rivers are antecedent, meaning they existed before the Himalayan orogeny, cutting deep gorges across rising ranges.
  • Indus, Brahmaputra, and Sutlej are classic examples of antecedent drainage originating beyond the Great Himalayas.
  • Peninsular rivers exhibit mature, graded profiles with broad, shallow valleys due to long-term base-level stabilization.
  • Water divide of India is marked by the Western Ghats, Aravallis, Vindhyas, and Satpuras, separating Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal drainages.
  • Approximately 77% of Indian drainage is oriented towards the Bay of Bengal, while 23% drains into the Arabian Sea.
Comparative Analysis: Himalayan systems are youthful, snow-fed, perennial, and possess high erosive capacity, whereas Peninsular systems are older, rain-fed, seasonal, and have attained near base-level profiles.
  • Himalayan rivers form extensive meanders and floodplains in plains; Peninsular rivers flow through rigid tectonic troughs and rift valleys.
  • Bay of Bengal receives larger volume due to major river systems like Ganga, Brahmaputra, Godavari, and Mahanadi.

2. The Himalayan Drainage System: Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra

Complex network of perennial snow-fed rivers featuring massive sediment loads and complex basin morphology.

  • Indus System includes left-bank tributaries (Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) and right-bank tributaries (Shyok, Gilgit, Kabul).
  • Ganga System originates from Gangotri glacier (Bhagirathi) and Alaknanda at Devprayag; major left-bank tributaries include Gomti, Ghaghra, Gandak, Kosi.
  • Right-bank tributaries of Ganga include Yamuna and Son, the latter originating from the Amarkantak plateau.
  • Brahmaputra originates from Chemayungdung glacier, enters India as Dihang through the Namcha Barwa gorge in Arunachal Pradesh.
  • Majuli in Assam is the world's largest riverine island formed by the Brahmaputra system.
Comparative Analysis: Kosi is notorious as the 'Sorrow of Bihar' due to frequent channel shifting, contrasting with the stable structural course of the Peninsular Narmada.
  • Indus Waters Treaty (1960) allocates Western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) to Pakistan and Eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India.
  • Brahmaputra is known as Tsangpo in Tibet, Jamuna in Bangladesh, and exhibits massive tidal bores in its lower reaches.

3. The Peninsular Drainage System and Drainage Patterns

Tectonically stable ancient plateau drainage exhibiting structural control and diverse morphological patterns.

  • East-flowing Peninsular rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery) form large deltas on the east coast.
  • West-flowing Peninsular rivers (Narmada, Tapti, Mahi) flow through rift valleys and form estuaries instead of deltas.
  • Godavari is the largest river in Peninsular India, often termed 'Dakshina Ganga'.
  • Dendritic pattern resembles tree branches, developing on uniform lithology like the Indo-Gangetic plains.
  • Trellis pattern develops in folded mountain regions where main streams are joined by right-angle tributaries.
Comparative Analysis: East-flowing rivers traverse a greater gradient across the plateau slope, whereas west-flowing rift valley rivers are short, swift, and sediment-deficient.
  • Narmada flows between Vindhya and Satpura ranges in a classic rift valley fault zone.
  • Radial pattern (e.g., Amarkantak plateau) directs streams outward from a central dome or highland.

Spatial Patterns & Regional Distribution

🌐 Global Geographic Belts & Spatial Variation

Major global drainage basins are controlled by continental divides, major structural trenches, and climatic zones, separating exoreic (ocean-draining) from endoreic (inland drainage) basins.

🇮🇳 Indian Subcontinent Context & Regional Corridors

India features major river basins classified by the Central Water Commission: 12 major basins (drainage area > 20,000 sq. km), 46 medium basins, and numerous minor basins. The Ganga basin is the largest, supporting over 40% of India's population.

📐 Key Geographic Formulas, Scales & Classifications
Horton's Law of Stream Numbers and Bifurcation Ratio
Rb = N_u / N_(u+1)
Parameters: Rb = Bifurcation ratio, N_u = Number of streams of order u, N_(u+1) = Number of streams of the next higher order.
Exam Focus: Frequently tested in UGC-NET quantitative geography and geomorphology sections to evaluate basin morphometry and hydrological response.
Drainage Density (Horton)
Dd = L_sum / A
Parameters: Dd = Drainage density (km/sq. km), L_sum = Total length of all streams in the basin, A = Total area of the basin.
Exam Focus: Indicates landscape permeability, climate, and vegetation cover; high drainage density implies impermeable sub-soils and high runoff.

📚 High-Yield Geographic Terminology & Definitions

Antecedent Drainage UGC-NET Core Concept

A drainage system that established its course before the tectonic uplift of the land across which it flows, maintaining its path through deep gorges.

Superimposed Drainage UP PGT Frequently Asked

A drainage system that develops on a former geologic surface and cuts across structural features unconcerned with underlying rock hardness or attitude.

Consequent Rivers Geomorphic Fundamentals

Rivers whose course is directly determined by the general slope of the land, usually following the initial regional uplift.

Subsequent Rivers Drainage Patterns

Tributaries that develop along strike valleys after the major consequent streams have established themselves, eroding weaker rock layers.

Dendritic Pattern Pattern Recognition

A tree-like branching drainage pattern that develops in areas of homogeneous rock resistance and uniform slope.

Trellis Pattern Fold Mountain Feature

A rectangular drainage pattern where primary tributaries flow parallel along valleys and secondary streams join them nearly at right angles.

Radial Pattern Landform Mapping

Streams flowing outwards in all directions from a central high point, characteristic of domes, volcanic cones, or isolated uplands.

Centripetal Pattern Interior Drainage

Streams converging into a central low point or depression, such as an inland lake, basin, or calderas.

Barachhain and Oxbow Lakes Fluvial Landform

Crescent-shaped lakes formed when meandering rivers abandon loops during high floods, common in the middle and lower Gangetic plains.

Periyar River South Indian Rivers

The longest river of Kerala, flowing westward into the Arabian Sea, utilized heavily for hydro-electric projects like Idukki.

Ambala Water Divide Regional Geography

A topographic high separating the Indus river drainage basin from the Ganga river drainage basin on the Indo-Gangetic plain.

Tsangpo Himalayan Systems

The Tibetan name for the Brahmaputra river upstream of its entry into Arunachal Pradesh through the Namcha Barwa gorge.

Pennar Basin Peninsular Drainage

A medium-sized east-flowing peninsular river basin situated between the Krishna and Cauvery river basins in southern India.

Bara Lacha La Himalayan Passes

A high mountain pass in Zanskar range acting as the source region for the Chenab river via its headwaters Chandra and Bhaga.

🧭 Prescribed Scholars, Laws & Spatial Models

W.M. Davis (Cycle of Erosion & Drainage Evolution) (1899)

Proposed that drainage patterns evolve through stages of youth, maturity, and old age, modifying antecedent and consequent systems through stream capture and valley widening.

R.E. Horton (Laws of Drainage Composition) (1945)

Formulated quantitative laws relating stream order to stream number (Law of Stream Numbers) and stream length (Law of Stream Lengths) in watershed basins.

J.T. Hack (Dynamic Equilibrium Theory) (1960)

Argued that landscapes and drainage basins adjust rapidly to rock resistance, maintaining a dynamic steady state rather than passing through sequential Davisian evolutionary stages.

A.D. Howard (Drainage Analysis & Genetic Classification) (1967)

Classified drainage anomalies and patterns into genetic categories based on structural control, regional slope, and tectonic history.

Question 1 of 18
Which of the following rivers is classified as an antecedent river cutting across the Greater Himalayas?

Rationale: Sutlej, along with Indus, Brahmaputra, and Kosi, is an antecedent river that originated before the Himalayan uplift and carved deep gorges through the ranges.

Question 2 of 18
The drainage pattern developed on a dome-like structure where streams diverge from a central high point is called:

Rationale: A radial drainage pattern resembles the spokes of a wheel, radiating outward from a central dome or volcanic cone.

Question 3 of 18
What is the primary reason why Peninsular rivers flow predominantly from west to east?

Rationale: The Western Ghats act as a water divide, and the overall tilt of the Peninsular block is from west to east, causing major rivers like Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Cauvery to empty into the Bay of Bengal.

Question 4 of 18
Which river forms the famous Jog Falls (Gersoppa Falls), India's one of the highest plunge waterfalls?

Rationale: Jog Falls is created by the Sharavathi River in Karnataka, descending 253 meters in four distinct cascades (Raja, Rani, Roarer, and Rocket).

Question 5 of 18
The Ambala water divide separates which two major river systems?

Rationale: The Ambala water divide stands between the Indus river basin and the Ganga river basin on the northern plains of India.

Question 6 of 18
Which among the following is a left-bank tributary of the Ganga river?

Rationale: Gandak is a major left-bank tributary joining the Ganga, whereas Yamuna and Son join from the right bank.

Question 7 of 18
The Brahmaputra river enters India through which gorge in Arunachal Pradesh?

Rationale: After taking a hairpin turn around Namcha Barwa, the Tsangpo enters India in Arunachal Pradesh as the Siang/Brahmaputra.

Question 8 of 18
Which of the following east-flowing rivers forms a delta that is often referred to as the 'Rice Bowl of Andhra Pradesh'?

Rationale: The fertile deltaic region formed by the Krishna and Godavari rivers (often associated with Krishna-Godavari delta complex) is known for extensive paddy cultivation.

Question 9 of 18
What type of drainage pattern is typically found in regions characterized by alternating bands of hard and soft rocks folded into ridges and valleys?

Rationale: Trellis drainage pattern develops where hard and soft rocks exist parallel to each other, with primary streams running in valleys and tributaries joining at right angles.

Question 10 of 18
Which river flows through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges?

Rationale: The Narmada river flows westward through a rift valley created by faulting between the Vindhyan and Satpura mountain ranges.

Question 11 of 18
Which of the following is the longest river in Peninsular India?

Rationale: Godavari, with a length of about 1,465 km, is the longest river in Peninsular India, often called Dakshina Ganga.

Question 12 of 18
Loktak Lake, famous for its 'phumdis' (floating circular swamps), is located in the drainage basin of which river system/state?

Rationale: Loktak Lake is situated in Manipur and represents a classic centripetal interior drainage wetland ecosystem.

Question 13 of 18
Which of the following rivers originates from the Mahabaleshwar in the Western Ghats?

Rationale: The Krishna River rises near Mahabaleshwar in the Western Ghats and flows into the Bay of Bengal.

Question 14 of 18
The 'Lonar Lake' in Maharashtra, formed by a meteorite impact, is an example of what type of drainage feature?

Rationale: Lonar Lake is an endoreic soda lake located in a basaltic impact crater with no outlet to the sea.

Question 15 of 18
Which river is known as the 'Sorrow of Bihar' due to its frequent shifting of courses and devastating floods?

Rationale: The Kosi river is notorious for lateral channel shifting and flooding in North Bihar plains.

Question 16 of 18
The meeting point of the Alaknanda and Bhagirathi rivers, where it officially takes the name 'Ganga', is at:

Rationale: Devaprayag is the holy confluence where Alaknanda meets Bhagirathi to form the holy Ganga.

Question 17 of 18
Which of the following west-flowing rivers of India has its source in the Multai plateau of Betul district, Madhya Pradesh?

Rationale: The Tapti river originates near Multai in the Satpura range of Madhya Pradesh and flows westward into the Gulf of Khambhat.

Question 18 of 18
Which tributary of the Indus system originates near Verinag at the foot of the Pir Panjal range?

Rationale: The Jhelum river springs from Verinag in the Kashmir valley before flowing through Wular Lake into Pakistan.

Previous Years Questions & Exam Benchmarks

Real and benchmark questions aligned with UP PGT, TGT, and National Eligibility tests.

UP PGT Geography (Previous Year Pattern)
Match List-I (Rivers) with List-II (Origin points): A. Godavari (1. Trimbakeshwar), B. Mahanadi (2. Sihawa), C. Tapi (3. Multai), D. Kaveri (4. Brahmagiri). Select the correct code.

Official Benchmark Explanation: Godavari originates at Trimbakeshwar (Nashik), Mahanadi at Sihawa (Chhattisgarh), Tapi at Multai (Madhya Pradesh), and Kaveri at Brahmagiri hills (Karnataka).

UGC-NET Geography (Previous Year Pattern)
Assertion (A): Drainage density is generally high in areas with impermeable subsurface materials and sparse vegetation cover. Reason (R): Impermeable rocks and sparse vegetation reduce infiltration and promote surface run-off, generating numerous rills and streamlets.

Official Benchmark Explanation: High drainage density is a function of high surface runoff, which occurs when infiltration is low due to impermeable lithology and lack of vegetative canopy.

UP TGT Geography (Previous Year Pattern)
Which of the following river basins is the largest in terms of catchment area in India?

Official Benchmark Explanation: The Ganga basin is the largest river basin in India, covering over 26% of the country's total geographical area.

UP PGT Geography (Previous Year Pattern)
The 'Teesta' river is a tributary of which major river system?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Teesta is a major right-bank tributary of the Brahmaputra river, originating from Phedang glacier in Sikkim and flowing through West Bengal into Bangladesh.

UGC-NET Geography (Previous Year Pattern)
Which scholar is credited with proposing the morphometric stream ordering system subsequently modified by Robert Horton?

Official Benchmark Explanation: While Horton introduced quantitative basin analysis, A.N. Strahler modified the stream-ordering method (1952) most widely used in modern geomorphology today.

Question 1 of 10
Consider the following statements regarding Himalayan and Peninsular drainage systems: 1. Himalayan rivers are perennial, whereas most Peninsular rivers are seasonal. 2. Himalayan rivers feature meandering courses in plains, while Peninsular rivers flow in graded, broad valleys. 3. Himalayan rivers exhibit antecedent and superimposed characteristics, which are absent in Peninsular rivers. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Solution: Statement 3 is incorrect because Peninsular rivers also exhibit antecedent features (e.g., Subarnarekha, Damodar, Chambal cutting through plateaus).

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following sequences of Panch Prayag (confluences of Alaknanda) from north to south is correct?

Solution: As Alaknanda flows down from Badrinath, it meets Dhauli Ganga at Vishnuprayag, Nandakini at Nandaprayag, Pindar at Karnaprayag, Mandakini at Rudraprayag, and Bhagirathi at Devaprayag.

Question 3 of 10
An isolated circular pattern of streams draining outward from a central depression or crater is incorrectly termed unless it refers to:

Solution: A centripetal pattern converges into a central depression or basin, whereas radial diverges outward.

Question 4 of 10
Which river system forms the 'Lonar Crater' inflow or internal drainage dynamics?

Solution: Lonar Lake is fed by small local seasonal streams draining the immediate crater rim with no surface outlet.

Question 5 of 10
Identify the incorrect pair of river and its major left/right bank tributary:

Solution: Subansiri originates in Tibet and joins the Brahmaputra from the north, making it a right-bank tributary, not a left-bank one.

Question 6 of 10
What is a 'Barbed Drainage Pattern' indicative of?

Solution: A barbed pattern, where tributaries flow in a hook-like upstream direction, indicates that the main stream has captured a tributary originally flowing in the opposite direction.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following West-flowing rivers drains into the Gulf of Kutch rather than the Gulf of Khambhat?

Solution: Luni flows southwest through Rajasthan and ultimately dissipates into the marshy lands of the Rann of Kutch.

Question 8 of 10
Which among the following parameters is directly proportional to stream order in Horton's Law of Drainage Composition?

Solution: According to Horton's laws, as stream order increases, the average length of streams of that order also increases geometrically (Law of Stream Lengths).

Question 9 of 10
The 'Sardar Sarovar Dam' is constructed across which river?

Solution: Sardar Sarovar Dam is a massive gravity dam built on the Narmada River in Navagam, Gujarat.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following drainage basins in India experiences the highest percentage of groundwater utilization relative to available recharge?

Solution: The Indian portion of the Indus basin (Punjab, Haryana, western UP) features intense agricultural irrigation, leading to high groundwater extraction rates.
