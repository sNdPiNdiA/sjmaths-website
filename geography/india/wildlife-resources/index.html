India

Wildlife Resources: Comprehensive Geographic Analysis, Physical & Human Mechanisms, and Regional Distribution

High-yield conceptual breakdown and physical/human geographical foundations of Indian wildlife resources tailored for UP TGT/PGT Geography and UGC-NET mastery.

UP PGT Geography UP TGT Social Science B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

India is one of the world's 17 megadiverse countries, harboring nearly 7-8% of all recorded species on just 2.4% of the global land area. This immense biodiversity is a direct function of its unique latitudinal spread, complex physiographic heterogeneity, and confluence of three distinct biogeographic realms (Afrotropical, Indo-Malayan, and Palaearctic). Geomorphologically, the altitudinal gradients of the Himalayas, the peninsular stable block, and extensive coastal-insular systems create diverse micro-climatic niches. Anthropogenically, wildlife resources represent critical biotic components of natural capital, supporting ecological balance, tribal livelihoods, and gene pools, though increasingly threatened by habitat fragmentation, biotic pressure, and anthropogenic edge effects.

1. Biogeographic Classification and Spatial Zoogeography of India

Rodier and Rodgers & Panwar's 10 Biogeographic Zones framework mapping spatial faunal distribution based on climatic and physiographic parameters.

  • Trans-Himalayan zone: Characterized by high-altitude cold deserts, supporting snow leopard, wild yak, and Tibetan argali.
  • Himalayan zone: Encompasses sub-tropical to alpine zones; home to the red panda, musk deer, and Himalayan tahr.
  • Indian Desert zone: Thar desert region featuring the desert fox, Indian wild ass, and great Indian bustard.
  • Semi-Arid and Western Ghats zones: Transition zones and biodiversity hotspots hosting endemic species like the Nilgiri tahr and lion-tailed macaque.
  • Deccan Peninsula, Gangetic Plain, North-East India, Coasts, and Islands complete the 10-zone matrix.
Comparative Analysis: Contrasts with Wallace's Zoogeographic Realms by localizing micro-habitats of the Indian subcontinent (transition zone between Palearctic and Indo-Malayan realms).
  • Rodgers and Panwar (1988) classification is the standard for protected area network planning in India.
  • The Deccan Peninsula constitutes the largest biogeographic zone by area.
  • North-East India and Western Ghats are global biodiversity hotspots.

2. Wildlife Conservation Legislation, Policy, and Protected Area Network

Institutional frameworks and legal instruments governing in-situ and ex-situ conservation in India.

  • Enactment of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972, establishing strict schedules for flora and fauna protection.
  • Establishment of Project Tiger (1973) as a flagship species-based conservation model leveraging core-buffer strategies.
  • Creation of Protected Area (PA) categories: National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Community Reserves, and Conservation Reserves.
  • Launch of Project Elephant (1992) to protect elephant corridors and mitigate human-elephant conflict.
  • Constitution of the National Board for Wildlife (NBWL) chaired by the Prime Minister.
Comparative Analysis: Compares in-situ conservation (National Parks with strict anthropogenic restrictions) vs. ex-situ conservation (Zoological parks, botanical gardens, and gene banks).
  • India has over 100 National Parks and 500+ Wildlife Sanctuaries.
  • Wildlife (Protection) Amendment Act 2022 aligns Indian law with CITES provisions.
  • Critical Tiger Habitats (CTHs) enjoy the highest legal protection.

3. Anthropogenic Pressures, Habitat Fragmentation, and Biogeography of Extinction

Island Biogeography principles applied to continental habitat islands and anthropogenic landscape modifications.

  • Habitat fragmentation driven by linear infrastructure (railways, highways, canals) bisecting migration corridors.
  • Human-Wildlife Conflict (HWC) escalation due to agricultural encroachment into buffer zones and ecotones.
  • Poaching and illegal wildlife trade fueled by transnational trafficking networks.
  • Invasive alien species (e.g., Lantana camara, Prosopis juliflora) altering native vegetative structure and food webs.
  • Climate change impacts shifting altitudinal and latitudinal bioclimatic envelopes for endemic fauna.
Comparative Analysis: Contrasts natural extinction rates (background extinction) with anthropogenic mass extinction rates driven by land-use changes.
  • Edge effects degrade the ecological integrity of fragmented forest patches.
  • Corridor ecology is vital for maintaining genetic viability via metapopulation connectivity.
  • Buffer zone management is essential to mitigate edge-associated conflicts.

Spatial Patterns & Regional Distribution

🌐 Global Geographic Belts & Spatial Variation

Wildlife resources are globally concentrated in tropical rainforests, coral reefs, and savannah biomes, adhering to a latitudinal gradient of increasing biodiversity from poles to the equator, governed by solar energy input, moisture availability, and evolutionary time stability.

🇮🇳 Indian Subcontinent Context & Regional Corridors

In India, wildlife resources concentrate heavily along the Himalayan foothills (Terai-Duar arc), the Western Ghats escarpment, the central Indian forested belt (Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha), and the mangrove ecosystems of the Sundarbans. The Indo-Gangetic plain exhibits lower wildlife density due to intense agrarian transformation.

📐 Key Geographic Formulas, Scales & Classifications
Shannon-Wiener Biodiversity Index (H')
H' = - sum from i=1 to S (p_i * ln(p_i))
Parameters: H' = species diversity index; p_i = proportion of total individuals made up of species i; S = total number of species (species richness)
Exam Focus: Frequently tested in UGC-NET Geography for measuring community ecology and spatial biodiversity distribution within protected areas.
Edge-to-Interior Ratio (EIR)
EIR = A_edge / A_total
Parameters: A_edge = Area affected by microclimatic and biotic edge effects; A_total = Total area of the wildlife reserve or habitat patch
Exam Focus: Appears in PGT Geography questions related to reserve design, geometric shape (circular vs linear reserves), and habitat fragmentation analysis.

📚 High-Yield Geographic Terminology & Definitions

Wildlife Sanctuary UP TGT/PGT Factual

An area designated for the protection of wild animals and their habitat, where limited human activities like grazing may be permitted under statutory control.

National Park UGC-NET Core Concept

A protected area strictly reserved for the betterment of wildlife and biodiversity, where private human activity, grazing, and forestry are completely prohibited.

Biosphere Reserve Advanced Geography

Large areas of representative ecosystems internationally recognized under UNESCO's MAB program, divided into core, buffer, and transition zones.

Project Tiger High-Yield Factual

A centrally sponsored tiger conservation program launched in April 1973 from Jim Corbett National Park to ensure a viable population of Bengal tigers.

Project Elephant Conservation Geography

Launched in 1992 by the Ministry of Environment and Forests to financially and technically support state efforts in managing and protecting wild elephants and their corridors.

Endemic Species Biogeography

Plant and animal species that are native to and restricted to a specific geographic region and found nowhere else on Earth.

Biodiversity Hotspot UGC-NET Frequently Asked

A biogeographic region characterized both by exceptional levels of plant endemism and by serious levels of habitat loss, conceptualized by Norman Myers.

Wildlife Corridor Spatial Ecology

Strips of natural habitat connecting wildlife populations separated by human-developed infrastructure or agricultural land, vital for genetic diversity.

Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species (CITES) Global Environmental Law

An international agreement between governments ensuring that international trade in specimens of wild animals and plants does not threaten their survival.

Red Data Book Standard Exam Term

The document established by the IUCN that provides public records and state evaluations of rare and endangered plant and animal species of a country or state.

Community Reserve Wildlife Protection Act

Protected areas in India that act as buffer zones to or connectors and migration corridors between established national parks and sanctuaries, managed by local communities.

Core Zone Protected Area Network

The inner, legally protected zone of a biosphere reserve where human activity is strictly prohibited to preserve undisturbed ecosystems.

Eco-Sensitive Zone (ESZ) Environmental Governance

Areas notified by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change around Protected Areas to act as a shock absorber for the protected habitats.

Critical Tiger Habitat (CTH) UP PGT Special

Core areas of tiger reserves mandated under the Wildlife Protection Amendment Act to be kept inviolate for tiger conservation purposes.

🧭 Prescribed Scholars, Laws & Spatial Models

Wildlife Protection Act (1972)

Provides the legal framework for the protection of wild animals, birds, and plants, establishing schedules of protected species and the creation of sanctuaries and national parks across India.

Forest (Conservation) Act (1980)

Regulates the indiscriminate diversion of forest land for non-forest uses, ensuring central government approval for de-reservation of forests to safeguard wildlife habitats.

Environment (Protection) Act (1986)

An umbrella legislation enacted in the wake of the Bhopal disaster empowering the central government to coordinate actions of state agencies, set standards, and protect environmental quality.

Biological Diversity Act (2002)

Aims at the conservation of biological diversity, sustainable use of its components, and fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising out of the use of biological resources through Biodiversity Management Committees.

Island/Island Biogeography Theory (MacArthur & Wilson) (1967)

Explains biodiversity dynamics in fragmented habitats and protected areas, establishing that species richness on islands or habitat patches depends on immigration and extinction rates relative to size and isolation.

Question 1 of 18
Which of the following is India's first National Park established in 1936 as Hailey National Park?

Rationale: Jim Corbett National Park in Uttarakhand was established in 1936 as Hailey National Park and was renamed Jim Corbett in 1957. It was also the first park to come under Project Tiger in 1973.

Question 2 of 18
Project Tiger was launched in India in which year?

Rationale: Project Tiger was launched on April 1, 1973, to ensure a maintenance-viable population of tigers in India for scientific, economic, aesthetic, cultural, and ecological values.

Question 3 of 18
Which Indian state has the maximum number of National Parks as of recent estimates?

Rationale: Madhya Pradesh has the highest number of National Parks (11 parks), earning it the moniker 'Tiger State' and a leading position in wildlife conservation infrastructure.

Question 4 of 18
The Kaziranga National Park is globally famous and recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage site primarily for protecting which animal?

Rationale: Kaziranga National Park in Assam hosts two-thirds of the world's great one-horned rhinoceroses and is a world-renowned success story in anti-poaching and wildlife management.

Question 5 of 18
Where is the only natural habitat of the Asiatic Lion located in India?

Rationale: Gir National Park and Wildlife Sanctuary in Gujarat is the sole home of the pure Asiatic Lion (Panthera leo leo).

Question 6 of 18
Which of the following biosphere reserves in India was the first to be included in the UNESCO World Network of Biosphere Reserves?

Rationale: Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve, spanning parts of Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Karnataka, was the first biosphere reserve in India established in 1986 and included in UNESCO's network.

Question 7 of 18
The Dachigam National Park is famous for sheltering which critically endangered species?

Rationale: Dachigam National Park, located in Jammu and Kashmir, is renowned as the primary natural home of the Hangul or Kashmir stag, a subspecies of red deer.

Question 8 of 18
Project Elephant was launched by the Government of India in the year:

Rationale: Project Elephant was launched in February 1992 as a centrally sponsored scheme to protect elephants, their habitats, and corridors, and address human-elephant conflicts.

Question 9 of 18
Which scheduling category under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972 affords absolute protection to species with penalties for violation being the highest?

Rationale: Schedule I and Part II of Schedule II provide absolute protection to endangered species against hunting, trapping, or trading, carrying the highest penalties.

Question 10 of 18
The Keibul Lamjao National Park, famous for the endangered Sangai deer, is located on which water body?

Rationale: Keibul Lamjao National Park is situated on Loktak Lake in Manipur and is recognized as the world's only floating national park, famous for the dancing deer (Sangai).

Question 11 of 18
Which biogeographic zone of India covers the largest geographic area?

Rationale: The Deccan Peninsula is the largest biogeographic zone in India, covering about 43% of the total landmass and hosting immense biodiversity.

Question 12 of 18
Silent Valley National Park, known for its tropical rain forests and endemic lion-tailed macaque, is located in:

Rationale: Silent Valley National Park is located in the Nilgiri hills of Palakkad district, Kerala, and became famous for the fierce environmental movement against the Silent Valley Hydroelectric Project.

Question 13 of 18
The Sundarbans National Park is renowned for being the largest contiguous mangrove forest and is famous for which unique adaptation of tigers?

Rationale: Royal Bengal Tigers in the Sundarbans are unique for their ability to swim in saline estuarine waters and hunt marine prey like fish and crabs.

Question 14 of 18
Which species was officially declared extinct in India in 1952?

Rationale: The Asiatic Cheetah was declared extinct in India in 1952 due to hunting, habitat loss, and depletion of prey base. Reintroduction efforts began recently in Kuno National Park.

Question 15 of 18
The Nilgiri Tahr is endemic to which mountain range in India?

Rationale: The Nilgiri Tahr is an endangered wild goat species endemic to the southern portion of the Western Ghats in Tamil Nadu and Kerala.

Question 16 of 18
Which of the following organizations maintains the Red List of Threatened Species globally?

Rationale: The International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) maintains the IUCN Red List, which evaluates the conservation status of plant and animal species worldwide.

Question 17 of 18
Which state houses the Periyar Tiger Reserve, famous for elephant and tiger conservation around an artificial lake?

Rationale: Periyar Tiger Reserve is situated in the Cardamom Hills of the Western Ghats in Kerala, centered around the Periyar Lake formed by the Mullaperiyar Dam.

Question 18 of 18
The Snow Leopard is the state animal of which Indian Union Territory/State?

Rationale: Ladakh declared the Snow Leopard (Panthera uncia) as its state animal, reflecting its commitment to high-altitude conservation.

Previous Years Questions & Exam Benchmarks

Real and benchmark questions aligned with UP PGT, TGT, and National Eligibility tests.

UP PGT Geography
Which of the following regions is recognized as a biodiversity hotspot in India?

Official Benchmark Explanation: The Western Ghats along with the Eastern Himalayas, Indo-Burma region, and Sundaland are the designated biodiversity hotspots located partly or wholly within India.

UGC-NET Geography
Match List-I (National Park) with List-II (State) and select the correct code: List-I: A. Manas B. Bandhavgarh C. Bandipur D. Pin Valley List-II: 1. Karnataka 2. Assam 3. Himachal Pradesh 4. Madhya Pradesh Codes:

Official Benchmark Explanation: Manas is in Assam (A-2), Bandhavgarh is in Madhya Pradesh (B-4), Bandipur is in Karnataka (C-1), and Pin Valley is in Himachal Pradesh (D-3). Thus option A is correct.

UP TGT Geography
In which Indian state is Jim Corbett National Park situated?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Jim Corbett National Park is located in the Nainital district and Pauri Garhwal district of Uttarakhand. It was carved out of UP during state bifurcation in 2000.

UGC-NET Geography
Consider the following statements regarding Biosphere Reserves in India: 1. Nilgiri was the first biosphere reserve established in India. 2. All biosphere reserves are part of the UNESCO World Network. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Statement 1 is correct as Nilgiri was set up in 1986 as India's first biosphere reserve. Statement 2 is incorrect because out of 18 designated biosphere reserves in India, only a specific subset are included in UNESCO's MAB world network.

UP PGT Geography
The Gir forest is famous for which of the following animals?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Gir National Park in Gujarat is famous worldwide as the last refuge of the Asiatic Lion.

UGC-NET Geography
Which biogeographic classification of India given by Rodgers and Panwar (1988) divides the country into how many major biogeographic provinces?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Rodgers and Panwar classified India into 10 biogeographic zones and 25 biogeographic provinces, which remains the standard framework for wildlife planning in India.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following wetlands of international importance under the Ramsar Convention is located in Keoladeo National Park?

Solution: Keoladeo National Park, formerly known as Bharatpur Bird Sanctuary in Rajasthan, is a famous Ramsar site and UNESCO World Heritage site known for hosting migratory waterfowl like Siberian cranes.

Question 2 of 10
The concept of 'Wildlife Corridors' is critical in conservation geography because it primarily addresses:

Solution: Wildlife corridors counteract habitat fragmentation by allowing gene flow between isolated populations, preventing inbreeding depression and maintaining genetic health.

Question 3 of 10
Which amendment to the Indian Constitution introduced the protection of forests and wildlife as a Directive Principle of State Policy (DPSP) and a Fundamental Duty?

Solution: The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act of 1976 added Article 48A (DPSP for environment and wildlife protection) and Article 51A(g) (Fundamental Duty to protect wildlife).

Question 4 of 10
Which Indian state shares its boundary with Nepal and Bhutan and holds world-famous wildlife reserves like Manas and Kaziranga?

Solution: Assam is located in northeastern India bordering Bhutan and Arunachal Pradesh, hosting iconic reserves like Kaziranga, Manas, Nameri, and Orang.

Question 5 of 10
The 'Great Indian Bustard' is critically endangered and its primary stronghold in India is found in which arid state?

Solution: The Great Indian Bustard's main population is found in Rajasthan (particularly in Desert National Park, Jaisalmer), along with fragmented populations in Gujarat and southern states.

Question 6 of 10
Under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972, which authority is responsible for the approval of management plans for Tiger Reserves?

Solution: The National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA), statutory body established under the Wildlife Protection Act, oversees tiger conservation and approves tiger reserve plans.

Question 7 of 10
Which biosphere reserve spans across the border between India and Bangladesh in the deltaic region?

Solution: The Sundarbans Biosphere Reserve encompasses the vast deltaic mangrove forests of the Ganges-Brahmaputra river system shared between West Bengal (India) and Bangladesh.

Question 8 of 10
The Valmiki National Park and Tiger Reserve is located in which district of Bihar, representing the terai ecosystem?

Solution: Valmiki National Park is situated in the West Champaran district of Bihar, bordering Nepal, and is the sole national park in the state.

Question 9 of 10
What is the primary objective of establishing Eco-Sensitive Zones (ESZs) around protected areas in India?

Solution: Eco-Sensitive Zones act as shock absorbers by regulating and minimizing negative impacts of developmental activities around fragile national parks and sanctuaries.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following protected areas is located in the cold desert biogeographic zone of India?

Solution: Hemis National Park in Ladakh is located in the cold high-altitude desert region of India, famous for its high density of snow leopards.
