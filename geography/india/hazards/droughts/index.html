India — Hazards

Droughts: Comprehensive Geographic Analysis, Physical & Human Mechanisms, and Regional Distribution

High-yield conceptual breakdown and physical/human geographical foundations of Droughts tailored for competitive exam mastery in UP TGT/PGT Geography and UGC-NET.

UP PGT Geography UP TGT Social Science B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Drought is an extended period of deficient precipitation resulting in water shortage, causing severe hydrological imbalances and ecosystem stress. Unlike sudden natural hazards like earthquakes or floods, droughts are creeping phenomena with cumulative impacts across agricultural, meteorological, and hydrological domains. In the Indian context, droughts are fundamentally tied to the spatiotemporal variability of the Indian Summer Monsoon (ISM), El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) anomalies, Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) phases, and anthropogenic triggers such as unsustainable groundwater extraction and land-use changes. Mastery of this topic requires analyzing the tripartite classification of droughts, spatial vulnerability zones mapped by the National Commission on Agriculture, and standard quantitative drought indices critical for competitive examinations.

1. Typology and Mechanisms of Droughts

Drought progression follows a cascading temporal sequence from atmospheric deficit to agricultural stress and hydrological depletion.

  • Meteorological drought: Defined by a significant deficit in precipitation compared to the climatological normal over a specific duration.
  • Agricultural drought: Characterized by soil moisture depletion leading to plant root-zone water stress and crop failure.
  • Hydrological drought: Manifests as below-normal surface and subsurface water levels in rivers, lakes, reservoirs, and aquifers.
  • Socio-economic drought: Occurs when the demand for economic goods exceeds supply due to weather-induced water shortages.
Comparative Analysis: Unlike flash floods or tropical cyclones that manifest sudden catastrophic energy release, droughts evolve slowly over seasons, making precise onset and termination points difficult to demarcate.
  • Meteorological drought acts as the primary trigger for all subsequent drought types.
  • Groundwater depletion delays recovery even after normal monsoon rains resume.

2. Physical and Synoptic Drivers of Droughts in India

Indian monsoon failures are structurally modulated by teleconnections, sea surface temperature anomalies, and regional land-atmosphere feedbacks.

  • El Niño episodes suppress convective activity over the Indo-Pacific warm pool, weakening the South-West monsoon winds.
  • Positive Southern Oscillation Index (SOI) is associated with normal monsoons, while negative SOI often correlates with drought years.
  • Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD): Negative IOD phases suppress rainfall over the Indian subcontinent, inducing drought conditions.
  • Northern Hemisphere snow cover: Excessive winter snow cover over the Eurasian landmass delays the seasonal heating of the Tibetan Plateau.
Comparative Analysis: El Niño acts as a global macro-scale disruptor, whereas regional features like the Western Ghats create strong local rain-shadow zones independent of global ocean-atmospheric cycles.
  • Not all El Niño years result in droughts in India (e.g., 1997 had a strong El Niño but normal monsoon), highlighting internal monsoon variability.
  • Shift in the Monsoon Trough position northwards towards the foothills of the Himalayas causes prolonged 'breaks in monsoon'.

3. Human Dimensions and Anthropogenic Amplification

Anthropogenic pressures convert natural climatic variability into severe structural socio-economic disasters.

  • Over-extraction of groundwater for intensive irrigation in semi-arid tracts accelerates hydrological drought severity.
  • Deforestation and land degradation reduce soil infiltration capacity, lowering local water table recharge rates.
  • Cropping pattern mismatch: Cultivating high-water-demand crops (paddy, sugarcane) in drought-prone agro-climatic zones.
  • Urban heat island effects and wetland reclamation impair micro-climatic moisture recycling.
Comparative Analysis: Natural drought is a climatic hazard, whereas desertification and acute agrarian distress are anthropogenic vulnerabilities compounding natural deficits.
  • Watershed management and micro-irrigation (drip/sprinkler) are critical adaptive responses to human-induced water stress.
  • Policy interventions must align minimum support prices (MSP) with agro-climatic suitability.

Spatial Patterns & Regional Distribution

🌐 Global Geographic Belts & Spatial Variation

Global drought belts closely align with subtropical high-pressure descending air masses (Subtropical anticyclones), interior continental landmasses far from oceanic moisture sources (continental interiors of Eurasia and North America), and cold ocean current coastal margins (Atacama and Namib deserts).

🇮🇳 Indian Subcontinent Context & Regional Corridors

India's drought-prone regions are classified by the National Commission on Agriculture into chronically drought-prone areas (encompassing Western Rajasthan, Kutch, Saurashtra, Rayalaseema, interior Karnataka, and the Telangana plateau) and semi-arid drought-prone zones (covering parts of Madhya Pradesh, eastern Uttar Pradesh, and the Vidarbha region of Maharashtra). Rain-shadow zones of the Western Ghats (e.g., districts like Solapur, Ahmednagar, and Bijapur) experience severe agricultural drought despite proximity to the coast due to descending dry air masses.

📐 Key Geographic Formulas, Scales & Classifications
Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI)
SPI = (X - X_mean) / Sigma
Parameters: X = precipitation for a given time scale, X_mean = climatological mean precipitation, Sigma = standard deviation of precipitation derived from a fitted probability distribution (typically Gamma distribution).
Exam Focus: Widely used in UGC-NET questions regarding meteorological drought quantification. Evaluators test standardized deviation thresholds (e.g., SPI <= -1.5 indicates severe drought).
Palmer Drought Severity Index (PDSI)
PDSI = Z * 0.304 / (1.91 * log(N) + 1.9) + 0.5 (Empirical water balance accounting model)
Parameters: Z = moisture anomaly index; N = duration factor incorporating antecedent precipitation, evapotranspiration, and soil recharge capacity.
Exam Focus: Frequent conceptual questions in UP PGT Geography regarding indices that account for both soil moisture and temperature-based evapotranspiration.

Mnemonics, Memory Shortcuts & Exam Traps

💡 Classification of Drought Types
MAHS (Meteorological, Agricultural, Hydrological, Socio-economic)

M - Meteorological (Rain), A - Agricultural (Soil/Crop), H - Hydrological (Water bodies/Aquifers), S - Socio-economic (Supply/Demand).

⚠️ Exam Trap: Examiners often reverse the chronological sequence of drought manifestation. Remember: Meteorological drought always precedes Agricultural and Hydrological droughts.
💡 Major Drought-Prone States in Peninsular India
RAM-K (Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh/Rayalaseema, Maharashtra, Karnataka)

R - Rajasthan, A - Andhra Pradesh, M - Maharashtra, K - Karnataka. These four states account for the largest share of chronically drought-affected districts in India.

⚠️ Exam Trap: Do not confuse drought-prone zones with flood-prone basins like the Brahmaputra or lower Ganga plains.

📚 High-Yield Geographic Terminology & Definitions

Meteorological Drought UP PGT 2021

A deficit of precipitation over a pre-determined period compared to the long-term average for a specific region.

Hydrological Drought UGC-NET

Deficiencies in surface and subsurface water supplies (streamflow, reservoir and lake levels, groundwater) following prolonged meteorological drought.

Agricultural Drought UP TGT

A condition where soil moisture is insufficient to maintain crop growth and maturity, leading to extreme crop stress and yield reduction.

Socio-economic Drought UGC-NET

The association of the supply and demand of some economic good with elements of meteorological, hydrological, and agricultural drought.

Palmer Drought Severity Index (PDSI) Standard Index

An index that uses precipitation and temperature data to estimate relative dryness and has been used extensively to determine moisture adequacy.

Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI) IMD Standard

A probability-based index focused solely on precipitation deficits across multiple time scales, widely used for drought monitoring in India.

Drought Prone Area Programme (DPAP) UP PGT

The earliest area development programme initiated by the Government of India in 1973-74 to mitigate drought impacts in chronically affected areas.

Desertification and Drought UGC-NET

Land degradation in arid, semi-arid, and dry sub-humid areas resulting from various factors, including climatic variations and human activities.

Rainshadow Area Geography Core

A dry region on the leeward side of a mountainous barrier, which receives significantly less precipitation, making it prone to meteorological droughts.

El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) UP PGT Geography

A periodic warming of sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern tropical Pacific that disrupts Indian monsoon rainfall, often triggering widespread droughts.

Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) UGC-NET Advanced

An ocean-atmosphere interaction in the Indian Ocean; a positive phase can counteract weak El Niño impacts and prevent Indian droughts.

National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) Guidelines on Drought Policy Framework

Comprehensive guidelines issued in 2010 focusing on proactive drought management, early warning systems, and watershed development.

🧭 Prescribed Scholars, Laws & Spatial Models

Indian Meteorological Department (IMD) Drought Criterion (Standardized over decades)

A meteorological sub-division is considered drought-affected when the seasonal rainfall deficiency is equal to or greater than 19% of the long-term normal.

Thornthwaite's Moisture Index (Im) (1948 / 1955)

Classifies climates based on potential evapotranspiration (PET) and precipitation (P), foundational for calculating moisture deficiency and identifying arid/drought-prone zones.

Walker Circulation and ENSO Teleconnection (1923 / Revised periodically)

Explains the seesaw pattern of atmospheric pressure between the eastern and western tropical Pacific, linking Pacific sea surface temperatures to the failure of the Indian Summer Monsoon.

Dry Farming Research and Isohyet Concept (ICAR Framework)

Identifies areas receiving less than 750 mm of annual rainfall as chronic drought-prone zones requiring specialized dryland agricultural techniques and moisture conservation.

Question 1 of 18
According to the Indian Meteorological Department (IMD), a drought year is declared for the country when the deficiency of the all-India summer monsoon rainfall is below normal by:

Rationale: IMD defines a drought year when the seasonal (June-September) rainfall for the country as a whole is deficient by 10% or more, and when more than 20% of the geographical area of the country is affected. However, at a sub-divisional level, a rainfall deficiency of 19% or more is categorized as drought.

Question 2 of 18
Which of the following Indian states has the maximum geographical area under chronic drought-prone classification?

Rationale: Rajasthan, due to its arid and semi-arid terrain coupled with erratic monsoon rainfall and location in the western part of the country, has the largest geographical area classified as drought-prone.

Question 3 of 18
The Drought Prone Area Programme (DPAP) was launched by the Government of India during which Five-Year Plan?

Rationale: The DPAP was initiated as the Drought-Prone Area Works Programme in 1970-71 in chronically drought-affected areas and was renamed DPAP during the Fourth Five-Year Plan (1969-74) in 1973.

Question 4 of 18
Which ocean phenomenon is most directly correlated with the suppression of the Indian summer monsoon and subsequent drought events?

Rationale: El Niño, characterized by the warming of sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific, increases atmospheric pressure over the Indian Ocean, weakening monsoon winds and often causing droughts.

Question 5 of 18
Which index is widely used globally and by the IMD to monitor meteorological drought based strictly on precipitation probabilities across multiple time scales?

Rationale: The Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI) is designed to quantify precipitation deficits on multiple time scales, making it versatile for monitoring meteorological, agricultural, and hydrological droughts.

Question 6 of 18
Rayalaseema region in Andhra Pradesh and Vidarbha in Maharashtra are notorious for agricultural distress primarily due to being located in:

Rationale: Rayalaseema lies in the rainshadow zone of the Western Ghats, and Vidarbha is in the interior plateau region; both experience high variability in rainfall and poor irrigation cover, making them prime agricultural drought zones.

Question 7 of 18
In the sequential progression of droughts, which type of drought occurs first?

Rationale: Meteorological drought is the initial trigger, characterized by a lack of precipitation. This leads to a deficit in soil moisture (agricultural drought), followed by reduced river flows and groundwater depletion (hydrological drought).

Question 8 of 18
Desert Development Programme (DDP) was launched in India in the year:

Rationale: The Desert Development Programme (DDP) was launched in 1977-78 to mitigate the adverse effects of desertification and drought in hot desert areas of Rajasthan, Haryana, Gujarat, and cold desert areas of Jammu & Kashmir and Himachal Pradesh.

Question 9 of 18
Which of the following is NOT a standard objective of watershed management programs implemented for drought proofing in India?

Rationale: Promoting water-intensive crops in arid tracts exacerbates drought vulnerability. Watershed management focuses on water conservation, soil protection, and sustainable cropping patterns.

Question 10 of 18
The classification of drought vulnerability in India by the National Commission on Agriculture (1976) categorizes areas receiving annual rainfall less than what amount as chronic drought areas?

Rationale: Areas receiving less than 750 mm of annual rainfall, with high variability (over 30%), are classified by the National Commission on Agriculture as chronically drought-prone.

Question 11 of 18
What role does a Positive Indian Ocean Dipole (Positive IOD) play regarding Indian monsoons?

Rationale: A positive IOD brings warmer sea surface temperatures to the western Indian Ocean, increasing moisture transport toward India and often acting as a buffer against negative monsoon impacts caused by El Niño.

Question 12 of 18
Which Ministry at the Central Government level is the nodal ministry for overall coordination of drought management in India?

Rationale: The Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare (specifically the Drought Management division) is the nodal agency at the central level for coordinating drought assessment, monitoring, and relief assistance.

Question 13 of 18
Which of the following states contains the largest absolute area under semi-arid drought-prone zones in peninsular India?

Rationale: Karnataka features extensive interior districts (such as Kolar, Chitradurga, and Bellary) that lie in the rainshadow of the Western Ghats, giving it one of the largest semi-arid drought-prone areas in peninsular India.

Question 14 of 18
The term 'Aridity Index' (AI) is expressed as a function of:

Rationale: The Aridity Index is typically calculated as the ratio of precipitation (P) to potential evapotranspiration (PET). Lower values indicate higher aridity and drought susceptibility.

Question 15 of 18
Which remote sensing index is most commonly used for monitoring agricultural drought severity by assessing vegetation health and greenness anomalies?

Rationale: NDVI is widely used in satellite remote sensing to monitor vegetation stress, density, and health, serving as a primary indicator for detecting agricultural drought conditions in near-real time.

Question 16 of 18
Which committee or commission introduced the 'Hill Area Development Programme' and emphasized watershed approaches for drought mitigation in India?

Rationale: The Hanumantha Rao Committee (1994) reviewed the DPAP and DDP and recommended a decentralized watershed development approach managed by local communities for effective drought-proofing.

Question 17 of 18
Kutch and Saurashtra regions of Gujarat frequently suffer from drought conditions primarily due to:

Rationale: Kutch and Saurashtra experience high inter-annual rainfall variability, high summer temperatures leading to rapid evaporation, and vulnerability to breaks in the monsoon current.

Question 18 of 18
What is the primary distinguishing feature between 'arid zones' and 'droughts' in geographical studies?

Rationale: Aridity is a permanent climatic feature characterized by low average rainfall, whereas drought is a temporary, recurring deviation from normal or average moisture conditions in any climatic zone.

Previous Years Questions & Exam Benchmarks

Real and benchmark questions aligned with UP PGT, TGT, and National Eligibility tests.

UP PGT Geography (Previous Year Pattern)
Which of the following Indian meteorological sub-divisions is classified as chronically drought-prone due to rainshadow location?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Madhya Maharashtra (including districts like Pune, Satara, Solapur, Ahmednagar) lies directly on the leeward rainshadow side of the Western Ghats and receives deficient and highly variable monsoon rainfall.

UGC-NET Geography
Assertion (A): Agricultural drought can occur even when meteorological drought is absent if unseasonal dry spells coincide with critical crop growth stages. Reason (R): Inadequate irrigation infrastructure and improper crop choice amplify soil moisture stress during dry spells.

Official Benchmark Explanation: Agricultural drought is driven by soil moisture deficiency during critical crop stages. Even with normal seasonal totals (no meteorological drought), a prolonged dry spell at flowering stage causes crop failure, exacerbated by poor irrigation and water-intensive cropping.

UP TGT Geography
The term 'El Niño' originally referred to:

Official Benchmark Explanation: El Niño (Spanish for 'little boy' or 'Christ child') originally referred to a weak warm ocean current that appeared along the coasts of Peru and Ecuador around Christmas time.

UP PGT Geography
Which committee report led to the comprehensive restructuring of watershed development programs for drought mitigation in India during the mid-1990s?

Official Benchmark Explanation: The High Level Committee headed by C.H. Hanumantha Rao (1994) evaluated drought-prone area programs and recommended the Guidelines for Watershed Development, emphasizing people's participation.

UGC-NET Geography
Match List-I (Drought Type) with List-II (Indicator/Parameter): List-I: a. Meteorological Drought b. Agricultural Drought c. Hydrological Drought d. Socio-economic Drought List-II: 1. Streamflow and reservoir levels 2. Precipitation departure from normal 3. Shortage of economic goods due to water scarcity 4. Soil moisture deficit and crop stress

Official Benchmark Explanation: Meteorological drought is measured by precipitation departure (a-2); Agricultural drought by soil moisture and crop stress (b-4); Hydrological drought by streamflow and reservoir levels (c-1); Socio-economic drought by shortage of economic goods (d-3).

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following regions in India experiences the highest co-efficient of variation (%) in annual rainfall, making it exceptionally drought-prone?

Solution: Western Rajasthan and Kachchh experience annual rainfall below 300-500 mm with a coefficient of variation exceeding 60-70%, making rainfall extremely erratic and highly unpredictable.

Question 2 of 10
The National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) guidelines emphasize that drought management plans should primarily transition from a relief-centric approach to a:

Solution: NDMA guidelines mandate shifting from reactive post-disaster relief to proactive risk management, preparedness, mitigation, and long-term drought-proofing.

Question 3 of 10
What is the primary reason why the eastern part of Rajasthan (East Rajasthan) is less drought-prone compared to Western Rajasthan?

Solution: Eastern Rajasthan receives rainfall from the Bay of Bengal monsoon branch as well as Arabian Sea influences, resulting in higher average annual rainfall compared to the arid Western Rajasthan.

Question 4 of 10
In the context of drought assessment, what does the term 'Decile Index' measure?

Solution: The Decile Index divides the historical rainfall distribution into tenths (deciles) to determine whether current precipitation falls into exceptionally dry, average, or wet categories.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following governmental interventions in India directly addresses livelihood security during severe agricultural droughts in rural areas?

Solution: MGNREGA provides a legal guarantee for 100 days of wage employment in rural areas, serving as a crucial social safety net and livelihood security mechanism during drought-induced agricultural distress.

Question 6 of 10
Why are peninsular river basins such as the Krishna and Cauvery frequently embroiled in inter-state water disputes during drought years?

Solution: Peninsular rivers depend entirely on southwest monsoon rainfall. During drought years, diminished streamflows fail to meet irrigation and drinking water demands across riparian states, sparking severe disputes.

Question 7 of 10
The concept of 'Agro-climatic Zones' formulated by the Planning Commission of India in 1989 aimed to address regional agricultural imbalances and drought vulnerabilities by grouping the country into how many zones?

Solution: The Planning Commission divided India into 15 agro-climatic zones based on homogeneity in agro-climatic characteristics such as soil, rainfall, temperature, and water resources to plan region-specific drought strategies.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following drought management strategies focuses on capturing surface runoff during peak monsoon downpours for lean-season agricultural use?

Solution: Traditional and modern watershed techniques, including farm ponds, check dams, and Johads, capture and store surface runoff, recharging groundwater and providing protective irrigation during dry spells.

Question 9 of 10
What is the primary limitation of using solely 'meteorological drought' data for formulating agricultural relief policies?

Solution: Meteorological drought only measures precipitation deficits. It does not account for soil type, evaporation rates, or irrigation availability, which determine whether a crop actually suffers agricultural drought.

Question 10 of 10
How does global climate change impact the frequency and intensity of droughts in the Indian subcontinent according to recent climatic assessments?

Solution: Climate change intensifies the hydrological cycle, increasing monsoon variability. This results in longer dry spells (triggering droughts) alongside short bursts of heavy, flood-inducing rainfall.
