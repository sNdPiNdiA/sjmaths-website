India

Physiography: Comprehensive Geographic Analysis, Physical & Human Mechanisms, and Regional Distribution

High-yield conceptual breakdown and physical/human geographical foundations of Physiography tailored for competitive exam mastery.

UP PGT Geography UP TGT Social Science B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

The physiography of India is a direct consequence of complex geodynamic evolutions, marked by the northward drift of the Indian Plate and its subduction beneath the Eurasian Plate initiated during the Cretaceous-Eocene epochs. This tectonic collision built the youngest and loftiest fold mountains—the Himalayas—while concurrent subsidence of the foreland led to the alluvium-filled Indo-Gangetic trough, flanked to the south by the stable, ancient Gondwanaland remnant, the Peninsular Plateau. Examining Indian physiography requires an integrated approach that connects endogenic structural foundations with exogenic fluvial, glacial, and aeolian geomorphic processes. For aspirants of UP TGT/PGT Geography and UGC-NET, mastery over the spatial arrangement, structural lithology, and drainage integration of the six major physiographic divisions—Northern Mountains, Northern Plains, Peninsular Plateau, Indian Desert, Coastal Plains, and Islands—is indispensable for tackling advanced conceptual, map-based, and analytical queries.

1. Tectonic Structure and Geological Evolution of India

Plate tectonics, continental drift, and the structural partitioning of the Indian subcontinent.

  • India is structurally divided into three major units: the Peninsular Shield, the Extra-Peninsula (Himalayas), and the Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra Trough.
  • The Peninsular Shield is composed of ancient Archean gneisses and schists, representing a stable block largely unaffected by major folding since the Paleozoic.
  • The Himalayas formed through convergent plate tectonics involving the Indian Plate and the Eurasian Plate, producing a series of parallel thrusts like the MBT, MFT, and MCT.
  • The Indo-Gangetic Plain is a vast foredeep created by crustal downwarping in front of the advancing Himalayan wedge, subsequently filled by Pleistocene to Recent fluvial sediments.
  • Volcanic activity during the Cretaceous-Eocene boundary produced the Deccan Traps through fissure eruptions of basaltic lava over the western Peninsular block.
Comparative Analysis: Contrasts the rigid, fault-block dominated stable shield of the Peninsula with the ductile, thrust-faulted, seismically active orogenic belt of the extra-peninsular Himalayas.
  • Peninsula = Stable shield of Archean rocks.
  • Himalayas = Young fold mountains of Tertiary origin.
  • Indo-Gangetic Plain = Alluvial foredeep depression.

2. Relief and Major Physiographic Regions: Himalayas and Northern Plains

Altitudinal zonation, longitudinal/transverse valleys, and depositional landforms of northern India.

  • Trans-Himalayas (Tethys Himalayas): Contains the Karakoram, Ladakh, and Zaskar ranges; rain shadow zone with arid conditions.
  • Greater Himalayas (Himadri): Continuous snow-clad range averaging 6,000m elevation; composed of crystalline rocks (granites and gneisses).
  • Lesser Himalayas (Himachal): Rugged topography with ranges like Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, and Mahabharat; famous for valleys like Kashmir, Kullu, and Kangra.
  • Outer Himalayas (Shivaliks): Foothill zone composed of unconsolidated fluvial sediments; features longitudinal valleys called Duns (Dehra Dun) and Duars.
  • Northern Plains Divisions: Bhabar (porous gravel belt), Terai (marshy resurgence zone), Bhangar (older alluvial terraces), and Khadar (flood-prone newer alluvium).
Comparative Analysis: Compares the high-altitude, glacial-carved relief of the Greater Himalayas with the low-relief, fluvial-deposition dominated topography of the Indo-Gangetic plains.
  • Himalayas run west-northwest to east-southeast in an arcuate shape.
  • Bhabar is porous and dry; Terai is marshy and biologically rich.
  • Khadar is more fertile than Bhangar due to annual silt deposition.

3. Relief and Major Physiographic Regions: Peninsular Plateau, Deserts, and Coasts

Block uplifts, rift valleys, coastal geomorphology, and insular configurations.

  • Peninsular Plateau is characterized by broad, shallow valleys and rounded hills, subdivided into the Central Highlands and the Deccan Plateau.
  • Major plateaus within the Peninsular block include Malwa, Chota Nagpur (mineral heartland), Meghalaya Plateau, and Dandakaranya.
  • Western Ghats (Sahyadri) are block mountains higher and more continuous than the dissected, lower-elevation Eastern Ghats.
  • Indian Desert (Thar) exhibits sand dunes (barchans, seifs), saline lakes (playas/dhands), and an inland drainage system.
  • Coastal Plains are divided into Western Coastal Plains (narrow, subdivided into Konkan, Kanara, and Malabar) and Eastern Coastal Plains (wider, featuring deltas of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Cauvery).
Comparative Analysis: Contrasts the submergent, estuarine-friendly Western Coast with the emergent, delta-forming, broader Eastern Coast.
  • Western Ghats act as a true water divide; Eastern Ghats are discontinuous.
  • Andaman and Nicobar are tectonic/sub-marine mountain extensions; Lakshadweep is coral-atoll based.
  • Thar Desert represents an arid biome with relict hills and ancient drainage traces.

Spatial Patterns & Regional Distribution

🌐 Global Geographic Belts & Spatial Variation

Major physiographic belts correspond to global tectonic plate boundaries, such as the Alpine-Himalayan seismic belt, the Circum-Pacific ring of fire, continental shield areas (cratons like the Canadian and Brazilian Shields), and major rift valley systems like the East African Rift.

🇮🇳 Indian Subcontinent Context & Regional Corridors

India's physiography spans from the snow-capped Himalayan ranges in the north (Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Arunachal Pradesh) through the fertile agricultural expanse of the Indo-Gangetic Plains (Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal) to the mineral-rich Peninsular Plateau (Madhya Pradesh, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka, Maharashtra) and the extensive coastal-insular domains.

📐 Key Geographic Formulas, Scales & Classifications
Hypsometric Integral (HI)
HI = (Mean Elevation - Minimum Elevation) / (Maximum Elevation - Minimum Elevation)
Parameters: Mean, minimum, and maximum elevations of a given drainage basin or physiographic unit, expressed as dimensionless ratios between 0 and 1.
Exam Focus: Frequently tested in UGC-NET Geomorphology to assess the erosion stage of a landscape (youthful, mature, or old stage).
Ruggedness Index (Strahler/Melton)
R = (H * D) / 1000
Parameters: H = Total relief (maximum minus minimum elevation in meters), D = Drainage density (km/km²).
Exam Focus: Used in quantitative geomorphology and regional physiographic analysis to measure the steepness and dissection intensity of mountain terrains.

Mnemonics, Memory Shortcuts & Exam Traps

💡 Himalayan Ranges from North to South
TR-GRE-LE-OUT

TR = Trans Himalayas, GRE = Greater Himalayas, LE = Lesser Himalayas, OUT = Outer Himalayas (Shivaliks).

⚠️ Exam Trap: Examiners often mix up Lesser and Outer Himalayas or place Trans-Himalayas south of Greater Himalayas.
💡 Northern Plain Soil Belts from North to South
B-T-B-K (Baby Tries Breaking Keys)

B = Bhabar, T = Terai, B = Bhangar, K = Khadar.

⚠️ Exam Trap: Remember that Bhabar is immediately south of the Shivaliks where rivers deposit coarse gravel, making it waterless at the surface.

📚 High-Yield Geographic Terminology & Definitions

Bhabar UP PGT Geography

A narrow belt of 8-16 km width lying parallel to the slopes of the Shiwaliks where streams deposit heavy loads of boulders and pebbles, causing rivers to disappear underground.

Terai UGC-NET

A marshy and damp tract lying south of the Bhabar belt where underground streams re-emerge, historically characterized by thick forests and rich wildlife.

Bangar UP TGT

The older alluvium plains of the Northern Plains, lying above the flood levels of rivers and often containing calcareous formations known as kankar.

Khadar UP PGT

The newer and younger alluvium deposits of the floodplains, enriched every year by fresh silt deposition, making it extremely fertile.

Kayals UGC-NET

Shallow lagoons or backwaters found along the Malabar Coast of Kerala, famous for boat racing and tourism.

Dun UP TGT

Longitudinal valleys lying between the Lesser Himalayas (Himachal) and the outer Himalayas (Shiwaliks), such as Dehra Dun and Patli Dun.

Palghat Gap UGC-NET

A major break in the Western Ghats mountain range located between the Nilgiri Hills and the Anaimalai Hills, facilitating connectivity between Kerala and Tamil Nadu.

Ten Degree Channel UP PGT

A maritime channel that separates the Andaman Islands from the Nicobar Islands in the Bay of Bengal.

Lakshadweep Islands UP TGT

An archipelago of coral islands situated off the southwestern coast of India, entirely of atoll origin.

Dhodap UP PGT

One of the highest peaks in the Northern Sahyadris, standing as a prominent landmark in the Western Ghats of Maharashtra.

Karewas UGC-NET

Glacio-lacustrine deposits found in the Valley of Kashmir, highly suitable for the cultivation of saffron (zafran).

Cutch Peninsula UP TGT

A vast expanse of saline mud flats and arid terrain in Gujarat, representing an uplifted shelf of the Arabian Sea.

Palk Strait UP PGT

A strait between the Tamil Nadu state of India and the Mannar district of the Northern Province of the island nation of Sri Lanka.

🧭 Prescribed Scholars, Laws & Spatial Models

Suess and Burrard (Tectonic Origin of Himalayas) (Late 19th - Early 20th Century)

Postulated that the Himalayas were formed due to the compression of Tethys Sea sediments caused by the southward movement of the Angaraland against Gondwanaland.

Wadia (Structure of the Syntaxial Bends) (1931)

Explained the hairpin-like syntaxial bends of the Himalayas at Nanga Parbat in the west and Namcha Barwa in the east as a result of rigid blocks obstructing the northward drift of the Indian plate.

D.N. Wadia (Peninsular Block and Bundelkhand Gneiss) (1943)

Established the Peninsular Plateau as an ancient stable shield composed primarily of Archean gneisses and schists, unaffected by major orogeny since the Precambrian.

S.P. Chatterjee (Physiographic Subdivisions of India) (1965)

Categorized India into major macro, meso, and micro physiographic regions based on relief, structure, and geomorphic history, laying the foundation for modern regional geography.

Question 1 of 18
Which physiographic division of India is considered the oldest stable landmass formed of ancient gneisses and granites?

Rationale: The Peninsular Plateau is part of the ancient Gondwanaland and is composed of old crystalline, igneous, and metamorphic rocks, making it the most stable geological block in India.

Question 2 of 18
The longitudinal valleys lying between the Lesser Himalayas and the Shiwaliks are known as:

Rationale: Longitudinal valleys lying between the Lesser Himalayas (Himachal) and the Shiwaliks are called Duns (e.g., Dehra Dun) in the west and Duars in the east.

Question 3 of 18
Which of the following channels separates the Andaman Islands from the Nicobar Islands?

Rationale: The Ten Degree Channel is a waterway that separates the Andaman Islands from the Nicobar Islands in the Bay of Bengal.

Question 4 of 18
Karewas, famous for saffron cultivation, are thick deposits of glacial clay and other materials embedded in:

Rationale: Karewas are lacustrine (lake-deposited) formations found in the Kashmir Valley, highly prized for saffron (zafran) cultivation.

Question 5 of 18
Which part of the Himalayas is bounded by the Indus and Satluj rivers?

Rationale: Regional division of the Himalayas by river valleys designates the stretch between the Indus and Satluj rivers as the Kashmir or Punjab Himalayas.

Question 6 of 18
The highest peak of the Eastern Ghats is:

Rationale: Arma Konda (Sitamma Konda) located in Andhra Pradesh is widely recognized as the highest peak of the Eastern Ghats.

Question 7 of 18
Which of the following mountain ranges lies parallel to the west coast of India?

Rationale: The Western Ghats (Sahyadris) run parallel to the western coast of the Indian peninsula from the Tapi river down to Kanyakumari.

Question 8 of 18
The fertile floodplains formed by newer alluvium along river courses are known as:

Rationale: Khadar represents the newer alluvium of floodplains, renewed every year by river floods, rendering it extremely fertile.

Question 9 of 18
Palghat Gap connects which of the following states?

Rationale: Palghat Gap is a major pass in the southern Western Ghats between the Nilgiri Hills and Anaimalai Hills, linking Kerala to Tamil Nadu.

Question 10 of 18
The Lakshadweep islands are primarily of which origin?

Rationale: Lakshadweep islands consist of coral islands and atolls built up by the accumulation of coral polyps over submarine plateaus.

Question 11 of 18
Guru Shikhar, the highest peak of the Aravalli Range, is located in which hill range?

Rationale: Guru Shikhar is situated in the Abu Hills (Mt. Abu) of the Aravalli Range in Rajasthan, rising to an elevation of 1,722 meters.

Question 12 of 18
The Northern Plains of India are formed by the interplay of three major river systems: Indus, Ganga, and:

Rationale: The Great Northern Plains of India have been formed by the alluvial deposits brought down by the Indus, the Ganga, and the Brahmaputra river systems.

Question 13 of 18
Which of the following passes links Srinagar with Leh?

Rationale: Zoji La pass is located in the Zanskar range of the Himalayas and provides vital road linkage between Srinagar and Leh in Ladakh.

Question 14 of 18
The Chota Nagpur Plateau is largely drained by which of the following rivers?

Rationale: The Damodar river flows through the rift valley of the Chota Nagpur Plateau, often referred to as the 'Ruhr of India'.

Question 15 of 18
Anamudi, the highest peak in South India, is located on which hill group?

Rationale: Anamudi (2,695 m) is situated in the Anaimalai Hills of Kerala and represents the highest elevation in the entire Western Ghats and Peninsular India.

Question 16 of 18
The eastern coastal plain is characterized by extensive deltas formed by major rivers. Which of the following is NOT one of them?

Rationale: The Narmada river drains westward into the Arabian Sea through an estuary, not an eastern delta, and is located on the western side of the peninsula.

Question 17 of 18
The southern tip of the Indian Union, Indira Point, is submerged under the sea and is located in:

Rationale: Indira Point, formerly known as Pygmalion Point, is located at the southernmost tip of the Great Nicobar Island.

Question 18 of 18
Which physiographic unit of India features barking deer, rich biodiversity, and swampy conditions south of the Bhabar belt?

Rationale: The Terai zone is a wet, swampy, and marshy tract south of the Bhabar, supporting dense forests and diverse fauna.

Previous Years Questions & Exam Benchmarks

Real and benchmark questions aligned with UP PGT, TGT, and National Eligibility tests.

UP PGT Geography (Previous Year Pattern)
Which of the following mountain passes connects Kullu and Spiti valleys in Himachal Pradesh?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Rohtang Pass (and nearby Atal Tunnel) connects the Kullu valley with the Lahaul and Spiti valleys of Himachal Pradesh, holding high strategic and geographic significance.

UGC-NET Geography
Match List-I (Himalayan Sub-division) with List-II (Extent by Rivers) and select the correct code: List-I: A. Punjab Himalayas B. Kumaon Himalayas C. Nepal Himalayas D. Assam Himalayas List-II: 1. Indus and Satluj 2. Satluj and Kali 3. Kali and Teesta 4. Teesta and Dihang Options: (a) A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4 (b) A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3 (c) A-1, B-3, C-2, D-4 (d) A-4, B-3, C-2, D-1

Official Benchmark Explanation: Sir Sidney Burrard's regional classification of the Himalayas is based on river valleys: Punjab Himalayas (Indus-Satluj), Kumaon (Satluj-Kali), Nepal (Kali-Teesta), and Assam (Teesta-Dihang).

UP TGT Geography
The western coastal plain of India is narrow and characterized by backwaters. What are these backwaters locally known as in Kerala?

Official Benchmark Explanation: In Kerala, the shallow lagoons or backwaters along the Malabar Coast are locally known as 'Kayals', famous for inland navigation and boat races.

UGC-NET Geography
Consider the following statements regarding the Peninsular Plateau: 1. It is composed of ancient igneous and metamorphic rocks. 2. The general slope of the plateau is from west to east. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Both statements are correct. The Peninsular Plateau is part of ancient Gondwanaland with ancient crystalline rocks, and the general drainage pattern (flowing into Bay of Bengal) indicates a west-to-east slope.

UP PGT Geography
Which of the following islands of India is of volcanic origin?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Barren Island in the Andaman and Nicobar group is India's only active volcano, formed entirely through volcanic activity.

Question 1 of 10
Which mountain range acts as a major watershed between the Ganga river system and the Narmada river system?

Solution: The Vindhyan Range forms the southern boundary of the Great Plains and serves as a major geographical divide and watershed between northern river systems (Ganga) and central peninsular rivers.

Question 2 of 10
The Dandakaranya region is primarily spread across which of the following states?

Solution: Dandakaranya is a physiographic and cultural region spread primarily across Chhattisgarh and Odisha, extending into parts of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following passes connects Sikkim with Tibet (China)?

Solution: Nathula Pass is located in the Chumbi Valley of the Himalayas in Sikkim, connecting India with the Tibet Autonomous Region of China.

Question 4 of 10
The Nilgiri Hills meet the Eastern Ghats and Western Ghats at which prominent knot?

Solution: The Nilgiri Hills act as the crucial junction or meeting point where the Western Ghats and Eastern Ghats converge.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following regions in India receives rainfall from Western Disturbances during winter?

Solution: North-West India (Punjab, Haryana, Western UP) receives winter precipitation (Mahawat) from temperate cyclones originating in the Mediterranean region known as Western Disturbances.

Question 6 of 10
The Shillong Plateau is structurally an extension of which of the following?

Solution: The Shillong Plateau (Meghalaya Plateau) is separated from the main Peninsular block by the Malda Fault, proving it is structurally an detached extension of the Indian Peninsular Shield.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following coastal plains is known as the Coromandel Coast?

Solution: The southern part of the Eastern Coastal Plain, stretching roughly from the Krishna delta down to Kanyakumari, is known as the Coromandel Coast.

Question 8 of 10
Which river cuts through the Pir Panjal range to form a gorge near Baramulla in Kashmir?

Solution: The Jhelum river flows through the Kashmir valley and cuts across the Pir Panjal range through a deep gorge near Baramulla before entering Pakistan.

Question 9 of 10
The Malwa Plateau is bounded in the north-west by which mountain range?

Solution: The Malwa Plateau is bounded by the Aravalli range in the northwest, the Vindhyan range in the south, and the Bundelkhand plateau in the east.

Question 10 of 10
The Eight Degree Channel separates which of the following?

Solution: The Eight Degree Channel separates the island of Minicoy (part of Lakshadweep, India) from the independent nation of Maldives to the south.
