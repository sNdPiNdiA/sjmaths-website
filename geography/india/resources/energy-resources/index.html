India — Resources

Energy Resources: Comprehensive Geographic Analysis, Physical & Human Mechanisms, and Regional Distribution

High-yield conceptual breakdown and physical/human geographical foundations of Energy Resources tailored for competitive exam mastery.

UP PGT Geography UP TGT Social Science B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Energy resources constitute the vital lifeblood of modern industrial geography, geopolitics, and national economic development. Geographically, the distribution of conventional energy resources like coal and petroleum is structurally bound to specific geological epochs (e.g., Carboniferous, Gondwana, Tertiary eras) and plate tectonic or sedimentary basin formations. Simultaneously, India's transition toward alternative and renewable energy sources highlights the shift from subterranean extraction to surface-level meteorological and solar harvesting frameworks. Understanding these spatial paradigms, depletion rates, and energy deficit crises is crucial for aspirants of UP TGT/PGT Geography and UGC-NET.

1. Geological Genesis and Spatial Distribution of Coal in India

Gondwana coal formations account for 98% of India's total coal reserves and 99% of total coal production, primarily located in structural river valleys.

  • Gondwana coal is bituminous to sub-bituminous, possesses high ash content, low moisture, and low sulfur.
  • Damodar Valley basin holds the prime reserves in West Bengal and Jharkhand (Jharia, Raniganj, Bokaro).
  • Mahanadi Valley basin hosts major deposits in Odisha (Talcher) and Chhattisgarh (Korba, Raigarh).
  • Son-Mahanadi and Godavari valleys contain substantial deposits in Singrauli, Sohagpur, and Singareni.
  • Tertiary coal is found in Jammu & Kashmir, Rajasthan, and Assam (Makum), characterized by high sulfur and low carbon.
Comparative Analysis: Gondwana coal vs. Tertiary coal: Gondwana coal is older (Carboniferous to Permian) and of higher metallurgical quality, whereas Tertiary coal is younger (Eocene-Oligocene) with high sulfur and volatile matter.
  • Jharia is India's prime coking coal repository.
  • Odisha leads in total raw coal reserves, followed by Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh.
  • High ash content requires mandatory beneficiation (washing) before thermal plant usage.

2. Petroleum and Natural Gas: Sedimentary Basins and Hydrocarbon Geography

Commercial mineral oil and natural gas accumulation is structurally trapped in anticlinal folds, fault traps, and stratigraphic traps within marine sedimentary basins.

  • Offshore reserves, led by Mumbai High, contribute over 40% of India's domestic crude production.
  • Assam-Arakan basin (Digboi, Naharkatia, Moran) represents India's oldest onshore oil-producing region.
  • Cambay basin in Gujarat (Ankleshwar, Kalol) features rich onshore petroleum and gas fields.
  • Barmer basin in Rajasthan (Mangala, Bhagyam, Aishwariya) is a major continental hydrocarbon frontier.
  • Krishna-Godavari (KG) Basin is the primary hub for deep-water natural gas discoveries (KG-D6 block).
Comparative Analysis: Onshore vs. Offshore extraction: Onshore fields face mature depletion curves, while deep-water offshore fields demand advanced capital-intensive sub-sea drilling technologies.
  • India imports over 85% of its crude oil requirements.
  • Natural gas infrastructure is rapidly expanding via pipelines like the Jagdishpur-Haldia-Bokaro pipeline (Urja Ganga).

3. Alternative and Non-Conventional Energy Sources in India

Geographic diversification toward renewable energy relies on harnessing solar insolation, wind corridors, biomass potential, and tidal/geothermal gradients.

  • Solar energy potential is maximized in the Thar Desert (Rajasthan) and Gujarat due to high annual clear-sky insolation days.
  • Wind energy potential is concentrated along coastal peninsular regions in Tamil Nadu (Muppandal), Gujarat, and Karnataka.
  • Biomass energy utilizes agricultural residue and urban municipal solid waste across the Indo-Gangetic plain.
  • Geothermal energy sites include Puga Valley (Ladakh), Manikaran (Himachal Pradesh), and Tattapani (Chhattisgarh).
  • Tidal energy potential is localized in the Gulf of Khambhat, Gulf of Kutch, and the Sunderbans delta.
Comparative Analysis: Conventional exhaustible fossil fuels vs. Non-conventional renewable flows: The former causes carbon lock-in and geopolitical vulnerability; the latter demands high initial capital and smart-grid storage buffers.
  • India aims for 500 GW of non-fossil fuel energy capacity by 2030.
  • International Solar Alliance (ISA) headquartered in Gurugram spearheads global tropical solar deployment.

4. Energy Crisis, Geopolitics, and Mitigation Strategies

The Indian energy crisis stems from a structural demand-supply gap, import dependency, grid transmission losses, and ecological trade-offs of fossil combustion.

  • Commercial energy demand outstrips domestic fossil fuel production, leading to high fiscal deficits through oil imports.
  • High Aggregate Technical and Commercial (AT&C) losses plague power distribution companies (DISCOMs).
  • Environmental externalities include particulate matter (PM2.5) pollution and greenhouse gas emissions from coal-fired thermal plants.
  • Mitigation strategies involve coal gasification, supercritical thermal power adoption, and green hydrogen missions.
  • Decentralized micro-grids and rooftop solar photovoltaic programs enhance rural energy security.
Comparative Analysis: Energy security vs. Environmental sustainability: Balancing immediate baseline load requirements with net-zero carbon transition targets.
  • National Green Hydrogen Mission aims to make India a global hub for green hydrogen production.
  • Energy efficiency measures are governed by the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) through PAT (Perform, Achieve, Trade) schemes.

Spatial Patterns & Regional Distribution

🌐 Global Geographic Belts & Spatial Variation

Global energy resources are highly asymmetric. Coal is concentrated in the Northern Hemisphere mid-latitudes (USA, China, Russia, India). Mineral oil is heavily clustered in the Middle East (Persian Gulf basin), Venezuela, Russia, and the US shale basins. Renewable energy potentials align with climatic belts—solar along desert/arid tropics, and wind along mid-latitude coastal and high-altitude mountain passes.

🇮🇳 Indian Subcontinent Context & Regional Corridors

India displays stark regional segregation: Fossil coal is concentrated in the eastern and central peninsular plateaus (Damodar, Mahanadi, and Son valleys); petroleum is restricted to western offshore (Mumbai High), Assam, and Rajasthan basins; solar potential peaks in western arid zones; and wind energy dominates southern and western peninsular coastal interfaces.

📐 Key Geographic Formulas, Scales & Classifications
Energy Intensity of Economy
EI = Total Primary Energy Supply (TPES) / Gross Domestic Product (GDP)
Parameters: TPES measured in MTOE (Million Tonnes of Oil Equivalent); GDP measured in constant purchasing power parity dollars.
Exam Focus: Frequently tested in UGC-NET to measure macroeconomic energy efficiency; a declining energy intensity indicates structural shifts toward services and high-efficiency manufacturing.
Load Factor of Power Plants
PLF = (Actual Energy Produced over a given period / Maximum possible energy that could have been produced at continuous full-power operation) * 100
Parameters: Actual generation vs Nameplate capacity * total hours in the period, expressed as a percentage.
Exam Focus: Crucial indicator of thermal and renewable plant capacity utilization in Indian economic geography and power sector assessments.

📚 High-Yield Geographic Terminology & Definitions

Gondwana Coal UP PGT 2021

High-grade bituminous coal formed during the Carboniferous to Permian period, accounting for nearly 98% of India's total coal reserves.

Tertiary Coal UGC-NET Dec 2020

Younger, lower-grade brown coal or lignite found in northeastern states and Tamil Nadu, characterized by high moisture and sulphur content.

Mumbai High UP TGT Geography

Offshore oilfield located in the Arabian Sea, about 160 km west of Mumbai coast, discovered by ONGC in 1974, producing prime Bombay High crude.

Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC) UP PGT Geography

India's first multipurpose river valley project established in 1948, modeled on the Tennessee Valley Authority, focusing heavily on thermal and hydel power.

Neyveli Lignite UGC-NET Geography

Major lignite deposit situated in the Cuddalore district of Tamil Nadu, heavily utilized for thermal power generation and briquetting.

Singrauli Coalfield UP PGT 2016

Superthermal coalfield situated partly in Madhya Pradesh and partly in Uttar Pradesh, acting as the energy capital of India due to massive thermal plants.

Jharia Coalfield UP TGT 2018

Prime coking coal repository located in Jharkhand in the Damodar river valley, essential for metallurgical industries and blast furnaces.

Tarapur Atomic Power Station UGC-NET 2019

India's first commercial nuclear power station located in Maharashtra, commissioned in 1969 using Boiling Water Reactors (BWR).

Kudankulam Nuclear Power Plant UP PGT Geography

Largest nuclear power station in India located in Tamil Nadu, built in technical collaboration with Russia, utilizing VVER-1000 pressurized water reactors.

National Grid UGC-NET Geography

The synchronized high-voltage electricity transmission network in India connecting regional grids into "One Nation, One Grid, One Frequency" managed by PGCIL.

KAPP (Kakrapar Atomic Power Station) UP PGT Geography

Nuclear power plant located in Gujarat utilizing indigenous Pressurized Heavy Water Reactors (PHWR), recently achieving commercial operation of its 700 MW unit.

Geothermal Energy Potential UGC-NET 2021

Energy derived from Earth's internal heat, with key prospective sites in India including Puga Valley (Ladakh) and Manikaran (Himachal Pradesh).

Solar Energy Corporation of India (SECI) UP PGT Geography

A CPSU under the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) dedicated to the implementation of the National Solar Mission (NSM).

🧭 Prescribed Scholars, Laws & Spatial Models

Hubbert's Peak Theory (1956)

M. King Hubbert posited that oil production in a given region follows a bell-shaped curve, peaking when approximately half of the total recoverable reserves are extracted, influencing India's energy security planning.

Hotelling's Rule (1931)

Harold Hotelling demonstrated that under free competition, the net price of a non-renewable exhaustible resource like coal or petroleum must grow at a rate equal to the rate of interest to maximize resource rent over time.

Energy Ladder Hypothesis (1980s-Present)

Describes the transition of households from traditional biomass fuels (dung, wood) to transitional fuels (kerosene, coal) and finally to clean, modern energy vectors (LPG, electricity) as household incomes rise.

National Solar Mission (Jawaharlal Nehru National Solar Mission) (2010)

A major initiative of the Government of India and State Governments to promote ecologically sustainable growth while addressing India's energy security challenge, targeting massive grid-connected solar capacity expansions.

Question 1 of 20
Which of the following rock systems contains nearly 98% of India's total coal reserves?

Rationale: Gondwana coalfields, primarily located in the river valleys of Damodar, Mahanadi, Son, and Godavari, contain about 98% of India's coal reserves and are of bituminous/anthracite quality.

Question 2 of 20
Which state leads in the production of lignite coal in India?

Rationale: Tamil Nadu, particularly through the Neyveli Lignite Corporation, is the leading producer of brown coal (lignite) in India.

Question 3 of 20
The Mumbai High oilfield is situated on which geological feature?

Rationale: Mumbai High is an offshore oilfield located on the continental shelf of the Arabian Sea, approximately 160 km west of Mumbai.

Question 4 of 20
Which Indian state has the highest potential for geothermal energy as identified by the Geological Survey of India?

Rationale: The Puga Valley in Ladakh (UT of J&K/Ladakh) has the highest concentration of geothermal manifestations and potential in India.

Question 5 of 20
Which is the first nuclear power station established in India?

Rationale: Tarapur Atomic Power Station in Maharashtra was commissioned in 1969, making it India's first commercial nuclear power facility.

Question 6 of 20
Which river valley is known as the 'Ruhr of India' due to its rich concentration of coal and mineral resources?

Rationale: The Damodar Valley contains rich coalfields like Jharia, Bokaro, and Raniganj, earning it the title 'Ruhr of India'.

Question 7 of 20
The National Thermal Power Corporation (NTPC) was established in which year?

Rationale: NTPC was incorporated as a central public sector enterprise in November 1975 to accelerate power development in India.

Question 8 of 20
Which of the following is NOT a tertiary coalfield region?

Rationale: Jharia is a Gondwana coalfield located in Jharkhand, whereas Makum, Neyveli, and Palana belong to Tertiary deposits.

Question 9 of 20
Kudankulam Nuclear Power Plant in Tamil Nadu has been built with the technical cooperation of which country?

Rationale: Kudankulam is a joint project between India and Russia, utilizing VVER-1000 reactor technology.

Question 10 of 20
Which non-conventional energy source has the maximum installed capacity in India as of recent renewable energy reports?

Rationale: Solar energy constitutes the largest share of India's non-conventional and renewable energy installed capacity.

Question 11 of 20
Digboi, the oldest continuously producing oil refinery town in India, is located in which state?

Rationale: Digboi is located in the Tinsukia district of upper Assam and is known as the 'Oil City' of Assam.

Question 12 of 20
Which agency is responsible for regulation and bulk supply of electricity across Indian inter-state transmission lines?

Rationale: Power Grid Corporation of India Limited (PGCIL) is the central Transmission Utility responsible for planning and operating the National Grid.

Question 13 of 20
Uranium Corporation of India Limited (UCIL) has its headquarters and primary mining operations centered around which state?

Rationale: UCIL is headquartered in Jaduguda, Jharkhand, which is the heartland of India's uranium mining operations (Singhbhum belt).

Question 14 of 20
Which state ranks first in wind energy installed capacity in India?

Rationale: In recent years, Gujarat has surpassed Tamil Nadu to secure the top position in total cumulative wind energy installed capacity in India.

Question 15 of 20
Thangamadu and Tummalapalle are famous for deposits of which strategic energy mineral?

Rationale: Tummalapalle in Andhra Pradesh is touted as one of the largest uranium reserves in the world.

Question 16 of 20
Monazite sands found along the coastal tracts of Kerala contain substantial reserves of:

Rationale: Monazite sands along the beaches of Kerala, Tamil Nadu, and Odisha are rich in thorium, a vital element for India's three-stage nuclear power program.

Question 17 of 20
The Hazira-Vijaipur-Jagdishpur (HVJ) pipeline transports which energy resource?

Rationale: The HVJ pipeline is a major cross-country natural gas pipeline operated by GAIL, supplying gas to various fertilizer and power plants.

Question 18 of 20
Which type of coal has the highest carbon content (85-95%) and burns with almost no smoke?

Rationale: Anthracite is the highest grade of coal, possessing 85-95% fixed carbon content, though its occurrence in India is extremely limited (found in Jammu & Kashmir).

Question 19 of 20
Bhakra Nangal Project, a major hydroelectric and irrigation venture, is built across which river?

Rationale: The Bhakra Nangal Dam is constructed across the Sutlej River in the Shivalik hills.

Question 20 of 20
Which state in India is pioneer in making rooftop solar installation mandatory for residential buildings?

Rationale: Gujarat has been a pioneer in framing aggressive solar policies, including mandates and widespread deployment of residential rooftop solar systems.

Which of the following coalfields is not located in the Damodar Valley region?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Talcher coalfield is located in the Brahmani river valley of Odisha, whereas Jharia, Raniganj, and Bokaro are major coalfields in the Damodar river valley.

UGC-NET Geography Dec 2019
Match List-I (Nuclear Power Plant) with List-II (State) and select the correct answer: A. Narora - 1. Karnataka B. Kakrapar - 2. Uttar Pradesh C. Kaiga - 3. Gujarat D. Kalpakkam - 4. Tamil Nadu

Official Benchmark Explanation: Narora is in UP, Kakrapar in Gujarat, Kaiga in Karnataka, and Kalpakkam (Madras Atomic Power Station) in Tamil Nadu. Thus A-2, B-3, C-1, D-4 is correct.

UP TGT Geography 2016
Where is the headquarters of Oil and Natural Gas Corporation (ONGC) located?

Official Benchmark Explanation: ONGC, a Maharatna public sector undertaking, has its corporate headquarters in Dehradun, Uttarakhand.

UGC-NET Geography July 2018
Which of the following energy sources contributes the largest share to India's total installed renewable energy capacity (excluding large hydro)?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Solar energy accounts for the lion's share of India's renewable energy capacity growth, surpassing wind energy in recent years.

UP PGT Geography 2013
The oldest oil producing field in India is:

Official Benchmark Explanation: Digboi in Assam is recognized as the site of the oldest operating oil well and refinery in India, operational since the late 19th century.

UP TGT Geography 2021
Singrauli super thermal power station is located in which state?

Official Benchmark Explanation: The Singrauli Super Thermal Power Station is located at Shakti Nagar in the Singrauli district of Madhya Pradesh (though adjacent to UP border).

Question 1 of 10
Consider the following statements regarding India's coal distribution: 1. Gondwana coal is primarily found in peninsular river valleys. 2. Tertiary coal deposits are predominantly anthracite in grade. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Solution: Statement 1 is correct as Gondwana coal occurs in peninsular river troughs (Damodar, Mahanadi, Godavari). Statement 2 is incorrect because Tertiary coal is generally lignite or brown coal with high moisture, not anthracite.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following pairs of Offshore Oil Basins and their respective states/locations is incorrectly matched?

Solution: The Cambay Basin is located onshore and offshore in Gujarat, not Odisha. Odisha offshore falls under the Mahanadi basin.

Question 3 of 10
In the context of India's three-stage nuclear power program, what is the primary role of Stage-II?

Solution: Stage-II of India's nuclear program utilizes Fast Breeder Reactors (FBRs) using plutonium-239 recovered from the first stage, breeding U-233 from thorium for the third stage.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following rivers is associated with the Rihand Dam project, a major source of hydel power for Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh?

Solution: The Rihand Dam is built on the Rihand River, a major tributary of the Son River, creating the Govind Ballabh Pant Sagar reservoir.

Question 5 of 10
The concept of 'One Nation, One Grid, One Frequency' in India was fully realized with the synchronization of which regional grid in January 2014?

Solution: The Southern Grid was connected synchronously to the Central Grid via the Raichur-Solapur transmission line in January 2014, unifying all regional grids into a single national grid.

Question 6 of 10
Which state has the highest potential for tidal energy generation in India, particularly due to the Gulf of Khambhat and Gulf of Kutch?

Solution: Gujarat offers the highest tidal energy potential in India, especially in the Gulf of Khambhat (Cambay) and Gulf of Kutch, followed by the Sunderbans in West Bengal.

Question 7 of 10
Assertion (A): India imports a significant portion of its domestic petroleum consumption. Reason (R): Domestic crude oil production has plateaued while energy demand has surged rapidly due to industrialization.

Solution: India imports over 85% of its crude oil requirements because domestic production is insufficient to meet rapidly rising industrial and transport sector demands.

Question 8 of 10
Which agency in India is the nodal agency for undertaking wind resource assessment and promoting wind energy farms?

Solution: The National Institute of Wind Energy (NIWE), formerly C-WET, based in Chennai, is the autonomous R&D institution under MNRE for wind energy.

Question 9 of 10
Which among the following states stands out in terms of prominent solar park installations, such as the Bhadla Solar Park?

Solution: Bhadla Solar Park, located in the Jodhpur district of Rajasthan, is one of the world's largest ultra-mega solar power parks.

Question 10 of 10
What is the primary objective of the PM-KUSUM scheme launched by the Government of India in the energy sector?

Solution: The Pradhan Mantri Kisan Urja Suraksha evam Utthaan Mahabhiyan (PM-KUSUM) aims to provide financial and water security to farmers through solar pumps and grid-connected solar power plants.
