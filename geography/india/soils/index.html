India

Soils: Comprehensive Geographic Analysis, Physical & Human Mechanisms, and Regional Distribution

High-yield conceptual breakdown and physical/human geographical foundations of Indian Soils tailored for UP TGT/PGT and UGC-NET mastery.

UP PGT Geography UP TGT Social Science B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Soil geography in the Indian subcontinent is an intricate function of lithological variation, monsoonal megathermal climates, diverse relief features, and prolonged biotic interference. Classified fundamentally by the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) on the lines of the US Soil Taxonomy, Indian soils exhibit striking spatial dichotomies between the ancient peninsular in-situ residual formations and the dynamic, allochthonous aggradational plains of the extra-peninsular river basins. This module provides a rigorous academic dissection of pedogenesis, major soil groups, morphological and chemical properties, anthropogenic degradation vectors, and conservation frameworks essential for competitive geographic examinations.

1. Pedogenesis and Major Soil Types of India (ICAR Classification)

Soil formation (pedogenesis) governed by the Dokuchaev equation (S = f(cl, o, r, p, t)) across diverse Indian morphoclimatic units.

  • Alluvial Soils (Entisols, Inceptisols, Alfisols): Cover ~43% of total land area, divided into Khadar (newer, fertile, non-calcareous) and Banger (older, higher terraces, contains kankar nodules).
  • Black Soils / Regur (Vertisols): Derived from Deccan basalt parent material; self-mulching clay minerals (montmorillonite) exhibiting high shrink-swell capacity and deep vertical cracks.
  • Red and Yellow Soils (Alfisols, Ultisols): Formed over ancient crystalline and metamorphic rocks of the Peninsular plateau under moderate to high rainfall; red due to ferric oxide diffusion, yellow due to hydration.
  • Laterite Soils (Oxisols, Ultisols): Developed in regions of high tropical temperature and intense alternating wet/dry monsoonal leaching; desilicated, rich in iron and aluminium oxides, poor in nitrogen and humus.
  • Arid and Desert Soils (Aridisols): High soluble salts, calcareous nature, alkaline reaction, low moisture retention, and high sand fraction with aeolian influence.
  • Saline and Alkaline Soils (Aridisols, Entisols): Known as Reh, Usar, or Thur; concentrated in semi-arid zones due to capillary action drawing sodium, magnesium, and calcium salts to the surface.
  • Forest and Mountain Soils (Inceptisols, Entisols): Highly heterogeneous, acidic, rich in organic carbon under coniferous canopies, prone to podzolization in higher Himalayan altitudes.
Comparative Analysis: Contrasts sharply with temperate podzols and chernozems; Indian soils are predominantly mature tropical/sub-tropical forms heavily influenced by the seasonal monsoonal rhythm rather than glacial activity.
  • Alluvium dominates the Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra trough.
  • Regur (Black soil) is auto-ploughing due to smectite clay.
  • Laterite soils are extensively used for brick-making after drying.

2. Physical and Chemical Characteristics of Indian Soils

Spatial variation in mineralogical composition, nutrient status, pH balances, and textural triangular parameters.

  • Textural variation spans from coarse sandy loams in western Rajasthan to heavy clay vertisols in Maharashtra and Madhya Pradesh.
  • Nitrogen and organic matter deficiencies are universally prevalent across nearly all Indian soil orders due to high decomposition rates in tropical heat.
  • Phosphorus deficiency is widespread, requiring continuous synthetic NPK (Nitrogen-Phosphorus-Potassium) fertilization.
  • Potash status is generally moderate to high, especially in alluvial and black soils derived from potassium-bearing feldspars and micas.
  • Soil reaction (pH) ranges from acidic in heavy rainfall belts (North-East India, Western Ghats) to alkaline/sodic in arid and canal-irrigated tracts (Punjab, Haryana).
Comparative Analysis: Unlike European soils that suffer from continuous leaching of bases and soil acidification, Indian semi-arid soils face severe alkalization and salinization induced by intensive canal irrigation without adequate drainage.
  • Universal deficiency in Nitrogen; variable status in Phosphorus and Potassium.
  • Black soils have high cation exchange capacity (CEC); laterite soils have exceptionally low CEC.

3. Soil Erosion Mechanisms and Geomorphic Dynamics

Accelerated soil removal by exogenous agents (water and wind) disrupting the pedosphere-lithosphere equilibrium.

  • Sheet Erosion: Uniform removal of a thin soil layer over gently sloping agricultural lands during heavy monsoon downpours; insidious and difficult to notice immediately.
  • Rill Erosion: Progression of sheet erosion where small, well-defined finger-like parallel channels (rills) develop on unprotected slopes.
  • Gully Erosion: Advanced stage of rill erosion creating deep, steep-sided ravines (e.g., Chambal and Yamuna ravines cutting through alluvial deposits in Madhya Pradesh/Uttar Pradesh).
  • Stream Bank Erosion: Lateral cutting and scouring of river banks during peak flood stages in Himalayan rivers (e.g., Brahmaputra in Assam).
  • Wind Erosion: Deflation, saltation, and surface creep of loose sandy particles in the Thar Desert and western arid plains during dry summer months.
  • Anthropogenic drivers: Deforestation, shifting cultivation (Jhum) in North-East, overgrazing in semi-arid pastoral tracts, and faulty contour-plowing techniques.
Comparative Analysis: Water erosion is dominant in high-relief monsoonal zones (Himalayas and Western Ghats), whereas wind erosion is restricted to the hyper-arid and arid western marches.
  • Chambal ravines are classic geomorphic expressions of severe alluvial gully erosion.
  • Sheet erosion is often termed 'the thief of agriculture' because it strips topsoil imperceptibly.

4. Soil Conservation and Management Strategies in India

Integrated watershed management, agronomic practices, and engineering structures designed to arrest degradation and restore fertility.

  • Agronomic Measures: Contour cultivation, strip cropping, cover cropping, mulching, and mixed cropping to break the kinetic energy of raindrops and reduce runoff velocity.
  • Biological Measures: Afforestation, reforestation, shelterbelt creation, and windbreaks in arid fringes (e.g., Indira Gandhi Canal command area greening).
  • Engineering/Mechanical Measures: Contour bunding, terracing of hill slopes, construction of check dams, gully plugs, and vegetative wattling.
  • Institutional Interventions: Soil Health Card Scheme providing customized fertilizer recommendations based on micro-nutrient testing; Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana (PKVY) promoting organic farming.
  • Reclamation of Problem Soils: Application of gypsum and phosphogypsum for sodic/alkaline soils; lime application for acidic laterite soils.
Comparative Analysis: Traditional indigenous conservation methods (e.g., Zabo system in Nagaland, bench terracing in Himalayas) integrated with modern remote-sensing-based watershed mapping yield higher survival rates than isolated engineering projects.
  • Gypsum ($ ext{CaSO}_4cdot2 ext{H}_2 ext{O}$) is the primary chemical amendment for sodic/alkaline soil reclamation.
  • Watershed management is the nodal paradigm for sustainable rainfed agriculture in India.

Spatial Patterns & Regional Distribution

🌐 Global Geographic Belts & Spatial Variation

Soil orders globally follow climatic and vegetation zonality (Zonal soils), transitioning from tundra gelisols and boreal spodosols to temperate mollisols/alfisols and tropical oxisols/ultisols. Azonal soils (entisols, inceptisols) and intrazonal soils (vertisols, histosols) are determined by parent rock or local hydrological conditions rather than climate.

🇮🇳 Indian Subcontinent Context & Regional Corridors

Alluvial soils blanket the Great Northern Plains, coastal deltas (Cauvery, Krishna, Godavari, Mahanadi), and lower river valleys. Regur soils are concentrated in the Malwa plateau, Maharashtra, and parts of Gujarat and MP. Red and yellow soils dominate the Chota Nagpur plateau, eastern MP, and Tamil Nadu uplands. Laterite soils spot the summits of the Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats, and parts of Odisha and Meghalaya. Arid soils form an uninterrupted belt across western Rajasthan and Kutch.

📐 Key Geographic Formulas, Scales & Classifications
Universal Soil Loss Equation (USLE)
A = R \times K \times L \times S \times C \times P
Parameters: A = Computed spatial soil loss per unit area; R = Rainfall-runoff erosivity factor; K = Soil erodibility factor; L = Slope length factor; S = Slope steepness factor; C = Cover-management factor; P = Support practice factor.
Exam Focus: Frequently tested in UGC-NET and PGT Geography for quantitative evaluation of water erosion vulnerability and watershed planning parameters.
Soil pH Scale (Acidity and Alkalinity Index)
pH = -\log_{10}[\text{H}^^+]
Parameters: pH = Negative logarithm of hydrogen ion activity in moles per litre; values < 7 indicate acidity, 7 neutrality, and > 7 alkalinity/sodicity.
Exam Focus: Appears in matching questions regarding soil types, crop suitability, and chemical reclamation agents (e.g., optimum pH for general crops is 6.0 to 7.5).

Mnemonics, Memory Shortcuts & Exam Traps

💡 Major Soil Types of India Mnemonic
ART-BLAS (Alluvial, Red/Yellow, Laterite - Black, Laterite/Desert, Alkaline/Saline/Forest)

A = Alluvial, R = Red & Yellow, T = Laterite, B = Black (Regur), L = Laterite/Desert, A = Arid/Saline. Helps recall the primary ICAR groupings instantly.

⚠️ Exam Trap: Do not confuse Laterite with Alluvial; remember Laterite is formed by intense leaching under high temperature and alternating wet-dry conditions, not river deposition.
💡 Soil Erosion Sequence Mnemonic
S-R-G (Sheet to Rill to Gully)

S = Sheet erosion (invisible film), R = Rill erosion (small channels), G = Gully erosion (deep ravines/badlands).

⚠️ Exam Trap: Examiners often invert the progression order in multiple-choice questions. Always remember: Sheet precedes Rill, and Rill intensifies into Gully.

📚 High-Yield Geographic Terminology & Definitions

Regur UP PGT 2021

The traditional local name for Indian black soils, which are highly argillaceous and well-suited for cotton cultivation due to high moisture retention.

Laterite Soil UGC NET Dec 2023

A residual soil formed under conditions of high temperature and heavy rainfall with alternate wet and dry periods, causing intense leaching of silica.

Bhangar UP TGT Geography

Older alluvial deposits found above the flood plains of the rivers, characterized by calcareous concretions known as kankar.

Khadar UGC NET

Newer, younger alluvial soils found along river beds, enriched every year by fresh deposits of silt during annual floods.

Pedogenesis Core Concept

The natural process of soil formation involving weathering, humification, translocation, and eluviation/illuviation under specific climatic and biotic controls.

Eluviation UGC NET Geography

The washing out or downward leaching of mineral matter and organic matter from the upper A-horizon of the soil profile.

Illuviation UP PGT Geography

The accumulation or deposition of leached mineral and organic materials in the lower B-horizon of the soil profile.

Usar Soil UP TGT

Alkaline and saline soils characterized by high sodium, potassium, and magnesium content, rendering them infertile and barren.

Terra Rossa UGC NET

A reddish clay soil produced by the weathering of limestone, typically found in Mediterranean-type climatic zones.

Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC) Advanced Edaphology

The total capacity of a soil to hold exchangeable cations, vital for assessing soil fertility and nutrient retention capacity.

Humus General Geomorphology

The dark, organic component of soil formed by the decomposition of plant and animal matter by microorganisms.

Inceptisol UGC NET Pattern

One of the prominent soil orders in India according to USDA soil taxonomy, representing moderately developed young soils widely distributed in river valleys.

Vertisol UGC NET

USDA soil order corresponding to black cotton soils, rich in expanding clay minerals like montmorillonite that cause deep cracks during dry seasons.

🧭 Prescribed Scholars, Laws & Spatial Models

V.V. Dokuchaev (Father of Soil Science) (1883)

Postulated that soil is an independent natural-historical body formed as a function of five prime factors: Parent material, Climate, Organisms, Relief, and Time (S = f[cl, o, r, p, t]).

Hans Jenny (Factors of Soil Formation) (1941)

Expanded Dokuchaev's concept into a systematic state equation of soil formation, defining quantitative relationships between soil properties and environmental state factors.

Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) Soil Classification (1953 (Revised))

Classified Indian soils into 8 major groups based on genesis, composition, and location, led by eminent soil scientists like J.S. Kanwar and S.P. Raychaudhuri.

USDA Soil Taxonomy System (1975 (Present))

Hierarchical soil classification based on diagnostic horizons and soil moisture/temperature regimes, classifying Indian soils primarily into Inceptisols, Entisols, Alfisols, Vertisols, and Ultisols.

Question 1 of 18
Which of the following soil orders covers the largest geographical area in India according to USDA Soil Taxonomy?

Rationale: Inceptisols are the most widely distributed soil order in India, covering nearly 39.7% of the total geographical area, followed by Entisols and Alfisols.

Question 2 of 18
Black soils (Regur) in India derive their characteristic dark color primarily from:

Rationale: The dark color of Regur soil is attributed to the presence of titaniferous magnetite, compounds of iron and aluminium, and humic-grewing complexes.

Question 3 of 18
Which phenomenon is primarily responsible for the formation of Laterite soils?

Rationale: Laterization occurs under heavy seasonal rainfall and high temperatures where silica is leached away, leaving behind sesquioxides of iron and aluminium.

Question 4 of 18
Which of the following state pairs has the maximum concentration of alkaline (Usar/Reh) soils in India?

Rationale: Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, and Haryana have extensive tracts of alkaline and saline soils due to canal irrigation, high evaporation, and capillary action bringing salts upward.

Question 5 of 18
Bhangar is distinct from Khadar because Bhangar represents:

Rationale: Bhangar is older alluvium located above flood levels, often containing lime nodules (kankar), whereas Khadar is newer, flood-renewed soil.

Question 6 of 18
Red soils appear red primarily due to:

Rationale: Red soils develop on crystalline igneous rocks and get their red color from the diffusion of iron oxides, though they appear yellow when occurring in hydrated forms.

Question 7 of 18
According to ICAR classification, how many major groups of soils are found in India?

Rationale: ICAR classifies Indian soils into 8 major groups: Alluvial, Black, Red and Yellow, Laterite, Arid, Saline/Alkaline, Peaty/Marshy, and Forest/Hill soils.

Question 8 of 18
Which clay mineral dominates the Indian Black Cotton soils, causing high swelling and shrinking characteristics?

Rationale: Montmorillonite is a 2:1 expanding lattice clay mineral that absorbs water readily, causing black soils to swell when wet and shrink/crack heavily when dry.

Question 9 of 18
Desert (Arid) soils are characterized by:

Rationale: Arid soils are sandy, low in nitrogen and organic matter, but high in soluble salts and phosphates, with calcium carbonate accumulation forming kankar pans at lower depths.

Question 10 of 18
Laterite soils are generally deficient in which of the following nutrients?

Rationale: While rich in iron and aluminium oxides, laterite soils are heavily leached, making them severely deficient in nitrogen, potassium, lime, and organic matter.

Question 11 of 18
Saline soils in India are locally known by all the following names EXCEPT:

Rationale: Bhur refers to an elevated tract of sandy soil found along the Ganga-Yamuna doab, whereas Reh, Kallar, and Chopan denote saline/alkaline soils.

Question 12 of 18
In the soil profile, which horizon is known as the zone of eluviation (leaching)?

Rationale: The A-horizon (or E-horizon when specifically differentiated) is the topsoil zone where eluviation (washing out of minerals/clay) takes place.

Question 13 of 18
Which of the following soils is most suitable for tea and coffee cultivation in South India and Assam?

Rationale: Laterite soils, after adequate manuring, are extensively used for plantation crops like tea, coffee, rubber, and cashew nuts, particularly in high-rainfall undulating terrain.

Question 14 of 18
Peaty and marshy soils are formed due to:

Rationale: Peaty and marshy soils develop in humid regions due to the accumulation of large amounts of organic matter under waterlogged conditions, resulting in heavy, highly acidic soils.

Question 15 of 18
Which state in India has the largest area under Laterite soil development?

Rationale: Kerala, along with Maharashtra (Konkan coast) and parts of Odisha and Assam, contains significant tracts of well-developed laterite soils due to tropical monsoonal climate.

Question 16 of 18
The process of accumulation of calcium carbonate in lower soil horizons in arid and semi-arid regions is known as:

Rationale: Calcification is the soil-forming process where evaporation exceeds precipitation, leading to the precipitation and accumulation of calcium carbonate (calcic horizon).

Question 17 of 18
Which of the following regions exhibits the maximum intensity of soil erosion by water in India?

Rationale: The Chambal river valley (badland topography/ravines) and the undulating terrain of the Chota Nagpur plateau experience severe sheet and gully erosion by water.

Question 18 of 18
Which organization is presently responsible for nationwide soil mapping and land use planning in India?

Rationale: Headquartered in Nagpur, NBSS&LUP (under ICAR) is the premier institute for soil mapping, classification, and database generation in India.

Previous Years Questions & Exam Benchmarks

Real and benchmark questions aligned with UP PGT, TGT, and National Eligibility tests.

UP PGT Geography (Previous Year Pattern)
Which type of soil covers the maximum area in the Indo-Gangetic plain?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Alluvial soils deposited by river systems (Ganga, Brahmaputra, Indus) form the most extensive and fertile soil group across the Indo-Gangetic plain, covering about 43% of India's total land area.

UGC NET Geography
Match List-I (Soil Type) with List-II (Characteristic Feature): A. Regur - 1. Leached soil rich in iron and aluminium B. Laterite - 2. Self-ploughing clay soil with high moisture retention C. Bhangar - 3. Older alluvium with kankar nodules D. Khadar - 4. New flood plain alluvium Correct Code:

Official Benchmark Explanation: Regur is self-ploughing black cotton soil (A-2). Laterite is leached tropical soil rich in sesquioxides (B-1). Bhangar is older alluvium with kankar (C-3). Khadar is fresh flood plain silt (D-4).

UP TGT Geography
The term 'Regur' is associated with which of the following?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Regur is the vernacular and regional term for black cotton soil, derived mainly from the weathering of Deccan basalt traps.

UGC NET Dec 2021/2022
Which of the following soil orders according to USDA classification is predominantly associated with high fertility and volcanic or alluvial parent material in river valleys of India?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Inceptisols represent young to moderately developed soils with significant fertility found extensively in alluvial plains and river valleys across India.

UP PGT Geography
Which of the following states experiences severe land degradation and soil erosion due to 'Chambal Ravines'?

Official Benchmark Explanation: The badland topography and deep ravines formed by gully erosion along the Chambal river heavily impact the tri-junction of Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, and Uttar Pradesh.

Question 1 of 10
Consider the following statements regarding Indian soils: 1. Alluvial soils are deficient in nitrogen but generally adequate in potash. 2. Black soils require frequent irrigation because they have very low moisture-holding capacity. 3. Laterite soils develop in regions experiencing high temperatures and heavy rainfall with alternating dry periods. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Solution: Statement 1 is correct (alluvial soils are low in nitrogen/humus, adequate in potash). Statement 2 is incorrect because black soils possess exceptionally high moisture-retention capacity. Statement 3 is correct (classic condition for lateritization).

Question 2 of 10
In the context of soil profile development, which soil horizon represents the parent rock or unconsidated bedrock from which the soil profile develops?

Solution: The R horizon denotes the underlying hard bedrock, whereas the C horizon consists of weathered parent material lying just above the bedrock.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following agricultural practices is most effective for mitigating soil erosion on steep sloping hillsides in the Himalayan region?

Solution: Contour farming and bench terracing break the slope length, reducing runoff velocity and controlling soil erosion in mountainous regions.

Question 4 of 10
What is the primary geochemical reason for the extreme infertility of Laterite soils unless heavily amended?

Solution: Leaching washes away silica, plant nutrients, and bases, leaving a residual matrix of iron and aluminium oxides devoid of essential plant nutrients and organic matter.

Question 5 of 10
Which soil group is characterized by high proportion of sodium salts, making the soil structure columnar and impermeable to water?

Solution: Sodic and alkaline soils contain high exchangeable sodium percentages, leading to dispersion of soil colloids, poor aeration, and impermeable subsoil structures.

Question 6 of 10
Assertion (A): Black cotton soils swell immensely when wet and develop deep polygonal cracks during the dry season. Reason (R): They contain a high proportion of kaolinite clay minerals which have fixed crystal lattices. Select the correct option:

Solution: Assertion is true. Reason is false because black soils owe their swelling/shrinking behavior to expanding 2:1 clay minerals like montmorillonite, not kaolinite (which is a 1:1 non-expanding clay).

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following is NOT a major factor of soil formation as formulated by V.V. Dokuchaev and Hans Jenny?

Solution: The Earth's magnetic field is not a state factor in soil formation. The five classic factors are Parent material, Climate, Relief, Organisms, and Time.

Question 8 of 10
Which Indian state has witnessed significant land reclamation projects through gypsum application to neutralize sodic/alkaline soils?

Solution: Gypsum (calcium sulphate) is extensively applied in the sodic plains of Uttar Pradesh (such as in districts like Rae Bareli, Sultanpur, and Aligarh) to replace exchangeable sodium with calcium.

Question 9 of 10
Red and Yellow soils are predominantly formed over which type of geological formations in the Deccan peninsula?

Solution: Red and yellow soils develop extensively on ancient crystalline and metamorphic rocks of the eastern and southern Deccan plateau due to low rainfall weathering.

Question 10 of 10
In USDA Soil Taxonomy, which soil order is typical of cold, waterlogged, or organic-rich marshy environments characterized by massive accumulation of partially decayed plant debris?

Solution: Histosols are organic soils (peat and muck soils) that form in wetlands, marshes, and bogs where restricted oxygen prevents rapid decomposition of organic residues.
