Physical Geography — Lithosphere

Plateaus: Comprehensive Geographic Analysis, Physical & Human Mechanisms, and Regional Distribution

High-yield conceptual breakdown and physical/human geographical foundations of Plateaus tailored for competitive exam mastery.

UP PGT Geography UP TGT Social Science B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Plateaus are elevated, flat-topped landforms that rise sharply above the surrounding terrain on at least one side, covering roughly 45 percent of the Earth's land surface. Often referred to as 'tablelands', they are shaped by endogenic forces such as tectonic uplift, volcanism, and crustal warping, as well as exogenic forces like denudation, glacial scouring, and fluvial dissection. Their genesis is fundamentally tied to dynamic mantle plume activities, orogenic compression, and continental rifting. From a climatic and economic perspective, plateaus act as formidable barriers to atmospheric circulation, creating rain-shadow deserts on their leeward sides while housing rich mineral wealth due to ancient cratonic roots and exposed shield rocks. Understanding their types—intermontane, continental, piedmont, and dissected—along with spatial patterns like the Tibetan Plateau and the Deccan Trap, is critical for candidates preparing for UP TGT/PGT Geography and UGC-NET examinations.

1. Morphogenetic Classification & Types of Plateaus

Classification of plateaus based on their mode of origin, tectonic environment, and topographic evolution.

  • Intermontane Plateaus: Highest and most extensive, enclosed by fold mountains (e.g., Tibetan Plateau, Bolivian Plateau).
  • Continental Plateaus: Formed by extensive continental uplift or massive basaltic lava flows, bordered by plains or oceans (e.g., Deccan Plateau, Canadian Shield).
  • Piedmont Plateaus: Located at the base of high mountain ranges, bounded by plains on one side (e.g., Malwa Plateau, Piedmont Plateau of USA).
  • Dissected Plateaus: Formed by vertical fluvial erosion and uplift of ancient tablelands, creating rugged relief (e.g., Chota Nagpur Plateau, Scottish Highlands).
  • Volcanic Plateaus: Built by fissure eruptions of fluid basic lava accumulating in thick horizontal layers (e.g., Columbia Plateau, Antrim Plateau).
Comparative Analysis: Contrast intermontane plateaus (formed via tectonic compression and crustal thickening) with volcanic plateaus (formed via effusive mantle melting and fissure flooding).
  • Tibetan Plateau is the highest and largest intermontane plateau globally.
  • Deccan Plateau represents a classic flood basalt province associated with the Réunion hotspot.
  • Dissected plateaus exhibit youthful-to-mature river valleys cutting through ancient horizontal strata.

2. Endogenic & Exogenic Formative Mechanisms

Geomorphic processes responsible for the creation, evolution, and destruction of plateau topography.

  • Epeirogenic Movements: Broad vertical uplift of continental crust without significant folding or faulting.
  • Orogenic Compression: Crustal shortening and tectonic thickening leading to high-altitude intermontane basins and plateaus.
  • Fissure Volcanism: Upwelling of low-viscosity basaltic magma through deep crustal fractures, forming horizontal trappean topography.
  • Fluvial Dissection: Downcutting by rivers transforming smooth tablelands into deeply incised plateau-and-valley systems.
  • Glacial Scouring: Plucking and abrasion by continental ice sheets smoothing high-latitude shield surfaces (e.g., Laurentian Plateau).
Comparative Analysis: Compare epeirogenic uplift (broad, vertical, continent-scale) with orogenic uplift (narrow, lateral, mountain-building scale).
  • Plateaus act as geological archives preserving ancient cratonic roots and shield structures.
  • Exogenic denudation continuously reduces plateau surface areas, eventually converting them into peneplains.

3. Major Global Plateaus & Tectonic Settings

Spatial distribution of world plateaus in relation to lithospheric plates and global climatic belts.

  • Tibetan Plateau: Formed by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates (Cenozoic Orogeny).
  • Colorado Plateau: Broad epeirogenic uplift cut by the entrenched meanders of the Colorado River, showcasing horizontal sedimentary strata.
  • Patagonian Plateau: Classic rain-shadow desert plateau east of the Andes Mountains, shaped by arid denudation.
  • Anatolian Plateau: Intermontane plateau nestled between the Pontic and Taurus mountain ranges in Turkey.
  • Azn/African Shield Plateaus: Ancient Precambrian platforms rich in metallic minerals and surrounded by narrow coastal plains.
Comparative Analysis: Contrast tectonic-collision plateaus (Tibetan) with thermal-anomaly plateaus (Colorado/Deccan).
  • Most high plateaus coincide with active or inactive convergent plate boundaries.
  • Rain-shadow effect is the dominant climatic control on arid plateaus like Patagonia and Colorado.

Spatial Patterns & Regional Distribution

🌐 Global Geographic Belts & Spatial Variation

Global plateaus are primarily concentrated in two structural belts: the Alpine-Himalayan belt (featuring the Tibetan, Anatolian, and Iranian plateaus formed by plate convergence) and the stable Precambrian cratonic shields (such as the Laurentian, Brazilian, and African plateaus formed by long-term epeirogenesis and denudation). Volcanic plateaus cluster around major mantle hotspots and continental flood basalt provinces like the Deccan, Columbia, and Siberian Traps.

🇮🇳 Indian Subcontinent Context & Regional Corridors

In India, plateaus dominate the peninsular shield region. The Deccan Plateau spans Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Andhra Pradesh, formed by Cretaceous-Eocene basaltic volcanism. The Chota Nagpur Plateau in Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh is rich in metallic minerals (iron, coal, mica) and dissected by the Damodar River. The Malwa Plateau lies between the Aravalli and Vindhyan ranges, while the Shillong Plateau represents an outlying detached fragment of the Indian Peninsula separated from the Rajmahal Hills by the Malda Gap.

📐 Key Geographic Formulas, Scales & Classifications
Hypsometric Integral (HI)
HI = (Mean Elevation - Min Elevation) / (Max Elevation - Min Elevation)
Parameters: Mean Elevation = average height of the plateau basin; Min Elevation = lowest point at valley floor; Max Elevation = highest peak on the plateau rim. Dimensionless ratio ranging from 0 to 1.
Exam Focus: Appears in UGC-NET geomorphology questions to determine the erosional stage of plateaus and mountainous terrain (youthful > 0.6, mature 0.3-0.6, senile < 0.3).
Drainage Density (Dd)
Dd = Σ L / A
Parameters: Σ L = total length of all stream channels within the plateau basin (km); A = total area of the plateau basin (sq km). Units: km^-1.
Exam Focus: Frequently tested in UP PGT to assess the degree of fluvial dissection and lithological resistance across different plateau surfaces.

Mnemonics, Memory Shortcuts & Exam Traps

💡 Classification of Plateaus by Origin
I-V-C-P (Indus Valley Civilization... Plateaus!)

I = Intermontane (Tibetan), V = Volcanic/Lava (Deccan), C = Continental (Shields), P = Piedmont (Malwa/Piedmont).

⚠️ Exam Trap: Examiners often confuse Piedmont plateaus with Intermontane plateaus; remember Piedmont is at the foot of mountains, while Intermontane is enclosed by them.
💡 Major Mineral-Rich Indian Plateaus
CCM (Carbon, Copper, Minerals)

C = Chota Nagpur (Ruhr of India, Coal/Iron), C = Chhattisgarh (Bauxite/Iron), M = Malwa (Agricultural, Black soil).

⚠️ Exam Trap: Do not confuse the mineral-heavy Chota Nagpur plateau with the agricultural-heavy Malwa plateau when answering economic geography questions.

📚 High-Yield Geographic Terminology & Definitions

Intermontane Plateau UP PGT 2021

Plateaus surrounded by fold mountains on all or most sides, typically formed during major orogeny.

Piedmont Plateau UGC-NET

Plateaus situated at the base of extensive mountain ranges, bordered by plains or seas on the other side.

Continental Plateau UP TGT

Vast tablelands raised abruptly above surrounding lowlands or the ocean, formed by stable cratonic uplift or basaltic flows.

Lava Plateau UGC-NET

Extensive elevated surfaces formed by successive fissure eruptions of fluid basaltic lava, such as the Deccan Traps.

Dissected Plateau UP PGT

A plateau that has been severely eroded by rivers into a rugged topography with steep slopes and deep valleys.

Tibet Plateau UGC-NET

The highest and largest intermontane plateau in the world, often termed the 'Roof of the World'.

Colorado Plateau UP PGT

A classic structural plateau in western USA known for deep canyon cutting like the Grand Canyon.

Anatolian Plateau UP TGT

A major intermontane plateau located between the Pontic and Taurus mountain ranges in Turkey.

Bolivian Plateau UGC-NET

Also known as the Altiplano, this is a high intermontane plateau nestled within the Andes mountain ranges.

Patagonian Plateau UP PGT

A rain-shadow arid piedmont plateau located in southern Argentina, bounded by the Andes and the Atlantic.

Chota Nagpur Plateau UP TGT

A mineral-rich continental plateau in eastern India characterized by a sequence of stepped erosion surfaces.

Meseta UGC-NET

The vast central internal plateau of the Iberian Peninsula (Spain), surrounded by mountain systems.

Dome Plateau UP PGT

Plateaus formed by the upward warping of the Earth's crust due to underground magma or tectonic forces.

🧭 Prescribed Scholars, Laws & Spatial Models

W.M. Davis (Cycle of Erosion) (1899)

Explains the evolution of plateaus through youth (dissection of plateau top), maturity (maximum relief and valley deepening), and old age (reduction to peneplain).

L.C. King (Peneplanation and Pediplanation) (1962)

Suggests that plateau retreat occurs primarily through scarp retreat and pedimentation, prevalent in semi-arid and arid continental plateaus.

Isostasy (Airy and Pratt Models) (1855)

Explains the gravitational equilibrium of the Earth's crust; high plateaus like Tibet possess deep crustal roots to maintain isostatic balance.

The Deccan Plateau of India is primarily an example of which type of plateau?

Which plateau is known as the 'Roof of the World'?

Rationale: The Pamir Plateau, connecting the Himalayas, Tian Shan, Karakoram, and Hindu Kush ranges, is famously called the 'Roof of the World'.

The Colorado Plateau is globally famous for which geomorphic feature?

Rationale: The Colorado Plateau is a high structural plateau intensely incised by rivers, creating dramatic features like the Grand Canyon.

Question 5 of 20
Which of the following is a piedmont plateau located at the base of the Andes?

Question 6 of 20
What primary geological process leads to the formation of continental plateaus?

Rationale: Continental plateaus are formed either by broad epeirogenic uplift of stable cratonic blocks or accumulation of extensive flood basalts.

Question 7 of 20
The Meseta is the central plateau of which country?

Rationale: The Meseta Central is the vast elevated plateau dominating the interior of the Iberian Peninsula (Spain).

Question 8 of 20
Which factor is primarily responsible for the arid climate of the Patagonian Plateau?

Rationale: The moisture-bearing westerly winds drop their precipitation on the western slopes of the Andes, creating a severe rain-shadow desert on the Patagonian Plateau.

Question 9 of 20
The Altiplano is a high-altitude plateau situated in which continent?

Rationale: The Altiplano is a broad intermontane plateau located in the central Andes of South America, spanning Peru and Bolivia.

Question 10 of 20
Which term describes a plateau that has been heavily eroded by running water into broken, rugged relief?

Rationale: A dissected plateau features steep slopes and deeply incised valleys created by aggressive fluvial erosion over geological time.

Question 11 of 20
The Shaanxi and Shanxi plateaus of China are well known for extensive deposits of:

Rationale: These plateaus in northern China are blanketed with thick accumulations of wind-blown silt known as loess.

Question 12 of 20
Which of the following plateaus is rich in mineral resources, particularly coal and iron ore, in India?

Rationale: The Chota Nagpur Plateau is often referred to as the 'Ruhr of India' due to its vast mineral wealth.

Question 13 of 20
What is the typical topographic characteristic of a plateau compared to a plain?

Rationale: Plateaus are defined as extensive upland areas characterized by a relatively flat or gently undulating top standing noticeably above adjacent lowlands.

Question 14 of 20
Which of the following mountain systems bounds the Anatolian Plateau on the south?

Rationale: The Anatolian Plateau in Turkey is bounded by the Pontic Mountains to the north and the Taurus Mountains to the south.

Question 15 of 20
In terms of plate tectonics, the Tibetan Plateau was formed as a result of:

Rationale: The collision of the Indian tectonic plate with the Eurasian plate resulted in massive crustal thickening and the uplift of the Tibetan Plateau.

Question 16 of 20
Which plateau is located between the Greater and Lesser Khingan ranges in Northeast China?

Rationale: The Mongolian Plateau extends across Mongolia and northern China, bordered by major mountain systems including the Altai and Khingan ranges.

Question 17 of 20
What is a 'mesa' in geomorphology?

Rationale: A mesa (Spanish for table) is a flat-topped elevation with steep cliffs, representing a remnant of a dissected plateau.

Question 18 of 20
The Kimberely Plateau is situated in which country?

Rationale: The Kimberley Plateau is located in the northern part of Western Australia, composed of ancient Precambrian sandstone.

Question 19 of 20
Which plateau is famous for its extensive bauxite reserves and is located in peninsular India?

Question 20 of 20
The Columbia Plateau in North America is formed by:

Rationale: The Columbia Plateau is a major flood basalt plateau located in the Pacific Northwest of the United States.

Question 1 of 10
Which geomorphic agent is primarily responsible for transforming a young flat plateau into a dissected plateau with deep gorges?

Solution: Fluvial erosion by rivers cutting down into elevated plateau surfaces creates deep canyons, gorges, and dissected topography.

Question 2 of 10
Consider the following statements regarding lava plateaus: 1. They are formed by fluid basic lava spreading over vast areas. 2. They typically exhibit step-like or terraced topography. 3. The Columbia Plateau and Deccan Plateau are prime examples. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Solution: All statements are correct. Lava plateaus form from fluid basaltic fissure eruptions, often display stair-like 'trap' topography, and include the Deccan and Columbia plateaus.

Question 3 of 10
What distinguishes a structural plateau from a volcanic plateau?

Solution: Structural plateaus (like the Colorado Plateau) consist of relatively flat-lying sedimentary beds uplifted by earth movements, unlike volcanic plateaus formed by magma/lava flows.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following plateaus is NOT situated in Asia?

Solution: The Abyssinian (Ethiopian) Plateau is located in East Africa, whereas the others are prominent Asian plateaus.

Question 5 of 10
The process of scarp retreat leading to the formation of extensive horizontal surfaces in arid regions was heavily emphasized by which geomorphologist?

Solution: L.C. King championed the concept of pediplanation and parallel scarp retreat in the evolution of continental landscapes, including plateaus.

Question 6 of 10
Which plateau is famous for being bordered by the Elburz and Zagros mountain ranges?

Solution: The Iranian Plateau is an intermontane plateau enclosed by the Zagros Mountains to the west/south and the Elburz and Kopet Dag ranges to the north.

Question 7 of 10
In the context of plateau classification, what is a 'Dome Plateau'?

Solution: Dome plateaus are created when crustal warping or subsurface intrusive activity (like laccoliths) arches the surface upward into a broad dome-like upland.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following pairs of Plateau and Country is INCORRECTLY matched?

Solution: The Ozark Plateau is located in the central United States (Missouri, Arkansas, Oklahoma), not Australia.

Question 9 of 10
How does the climate of high intermontane plateaus like Tibet differ typically from low-lying coastal plains at similar latitudes?

Solution: High intermontane plateaus experience thin air, rapid heat loss at night (high diurnal range), low temperatures, and strong solar radiation.

Question 10 of 10
Which feature represents an isolated, steep-sided flat-topped remnant of a plateau whose width is less than its height?

Solution: A butte is an isolated hill with steep sides and a small flat top, smaller than a mesa, formed by the continued erosion of a plateau.
