Physical Geography — Lithosphere

Major Landforms: Comprehensive Geographic Analysis, Physical & Human Mechanisms, and Regional Distribution

High-yield conceptual breakdown and physical/human geographical foundations of Major Landforms tailored for competitive exam mastery.

UP PGT Geography UP TGT Social Science B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Geomorphology examines the systematic evolution of Earth's surface through the interplay of endogenetic forces (diastrophism, volcanism) and exogenetic agents (weathering, mass wasting, erosion, and deposition). The lithosphere's relief features are sculpted dynamically by moving water, glacial ice, groundwater in soluble rocks, coastal wave action, and wind in hyper-arid zones. Each geomorphic agent leaves distinct morphogenetic signatures, creating assemblages of erosional and depositional landforms governed by base levels, climatic regimes, and lithological resistances. In competitive examinations such as UP TGT/PGT Geography and UGC-NET, mastery over minor and major landforms requires distinguishing between structural, erosional, and depositional origins. Questions frequently test Davisian cycle stages, specific depositional features like drumlins, eskers, yardangs, and barchans, and regional Indian geomorphology. Understanding energy-load balances and hydrodynamic equations enables candidates to evaluate regional landscape evolution precisely.

1. Fluvial Landforms: Dynamics of Running Water

Fluvial processes operate through hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, and corrosion, sculpting landscapes along graded river profiles from youth to old age.

  • V-shaped valleys, gorges, and canyons dominate youth stages through vertical downcutting.
  • Pot holes, rapids, and waterfalls (e.g., plunge pools) develop in high-energy headwater reaches.
  • Meanders, oxbow lakes (horseshoe lakes), and alluvial fans characterize mature lateral erosion zones.
  • Floodplains, natural levees, deltaic distributaries, and bird's-foot or arcuate deltas form in old age zones.
  • Piedmont alluvial zones feature bajadas, pediments, and bolson features in arid transition environments.
Comparative Analysis: Contrast Davis's geographical cycle (youth, maturity, old age stages based on structure, process, and time) with Penck's morphological system (waxing, uniform, and waning development based on crustal movement rates).
  • Waterfalls represent nick points resulting from differential rock resistance or rejuvenation.
  • Bird's-foot deltas (e.g., Mississippi) form when river sediment deposition outpaces wave and tidal redistribution.

2. Glacial and Karst Landforms: Cryospheric and Solutional Processes

Glacial systems sculpt through plucking and abrasion, while karst topographies evolve via chemical solution of calcium carbonate by carbonation.

  • Glacial erosional features include U-shaped glacial troughs, hanging valleys, cirques (corries), arêtes, and horn peaks.
  • Glacial depositional features include ground moraines, recessional moraines, drumlins, eskers, kames, and erratic boulders.
  • Karst surface features include lapies (karren), sinkholes (swallow holes), dolines, uvalas, and massive poljes.
  • Karst subterranean features include stalactites, stalagmites, column pillars, and cavern networks formed by groundwater precipitation.
  • Periglacial features like nivation hollows, patterned ground, and pingos characterize permafrost margins.
Comparative Analysis: Glacial erosion creates steep U-shaped valleys with hanging tributaries, whereas fluvial erosion produces sharp V-shaped river valleys.
  • Drury drumlins indicate ice-flow direction with steep stoss sides and gentle lee slopes.
  • Karst topography requires massive, jointed limestone or dolomite and high rainfall combined with organic acids.

3. Marine and Aeolian Landforms: Coastal and Desert Geomorphology

Marine landforms are sculpted by wave action, littoral currents, and tidal forces, whereas aeolian landforms are driven by wind deflation, abrasion, and saltation.

  • Marine erosional features include sea cliffs, wave-cut platforms, sea caves, arches, stacks, and stumps.
  • Marine depositional features include beaches, spits, bars, tombolos, barrier islands, and coastal lagoons.
  • Aeolian erosional features include deflation hollows, ventifacts, zeugen, yardangs, and mushroom rocks (demoiseaux).
  • Aeolian depositional features include barchans (crescentic dunes), transverse dunes, longitudinal dunes (seifs), and extensive loess plains.
  • Coastal emergence creates raised beaches and marine terraces, while submergence produces rias, fiords, and estuarine coasts.
Comparative Analysis: Yardangs are carved parallel to dominant winds by differential wind abrasion, whereas zeugen form perpendicular to winds in alternating hard and soft strata.
  • Tombolos connect offshore islands to the mainland via sand spits built by wave refraction.
  • Loess deposits represent silt accumulated by wind deflation far from glacial outwash plains or arid source deserts.

Spatial Patterns & Regional Distribution

🌐 Global Geographic Belts & Spatial Variation

Glacial landforms concentrate in high-latitude polar regions and high-altitude alpine belts (Himalayas, Andes, Alps, Rockies). Karst topography occurs extensively in the Dinaric Alps (Balkans), southern China (Guangxi karst), the Caribbean (Yucatan Peninsula), and the Kentucky karst region of North America. Aeolian landforms dominate subtropical desert belts (Sahara, Kalahari, Atacama, Great Australian, Thar) aligned with descending limbs of Hadley cells. Coastal marine landforms fringe all continental margins, with fjords restricted to high-latitude glaciated coasts (Norway, Chile, New Zealand, Alaska).

🇮🇳 Indian Subcontinent Context & Regional Corridors

In India, glacial landforms are prominent in the Greater Himalayas, Karakoram, and Zanskar ranges (e.g., Siachen, Gangotri cirques, and moraines). Fluvial landforms dominate the vast Indo-Gangetic Alluvial Plain with mature meanders, oxbow lakes, and the massive Sundarbans delta. Karst landforms occur in isolated limestone tracts such as the Gupteshwar caves (Odisha), Bastar region (Chhattisgarh), and Robbers Cave (Dehradun). Aeolian landforms are localized in the Thar Desert of western Rajasthan (barchans, dhian, playas/ranns). Marine features characterize the Konkan, Malabar, and Coromandel coastal zones, including backwaters (kayals) of Kerala and barrier spits.

📐 Key Geographic Formulas, Scales & Classifications
Stream Gradient and Slope Calculation
S = (H_up - H_down) / L
Parameters: S = Stream gradient (dimensionless or m/km); H_up = Elevation of upstream point (meters); H_down = Elevation of downstream point (meters); L = Horizontal channel distance between points (kilometers or meters).
Exam Focus: Frequently tested in UGC-NET map-interpretation and quantitative geomorphology sections to determine stream energy and erosional capacity.
Stream Sinuosity Index (SSI)
SSI = P / C
Parameters: P = Channel length along the meanders (actual distance); C = Straight-line valley distance between the same two points (displacement distance). SSI > 1.5 indicates a meandering river.
Exam Focus: Used in UP PGT questions to classify river channel patterns into straight, transitional, and meandering categories based on threshold values.

Mnemonics, Memory Shortcuts & Exam Traps

💡 Glacial vs Fluvial Depositional Features
D.E.K.M. (Drumlins, Eskers, Kames, Moraines = DE Khelega Morchai)

Memorizes primary glacial depositional landforms quickly to avoid confusion with river-borne (alluvial) depositional features.

⚠️ Exam Trap: Examiners often mix up Eskers (glacial meltwater ridges) with Levees (fluvial riverbank ridges).
💡 Karst Subterranean Features Differentiation
Stalactite has 'C' for Ceiling; Stalagmite has 'G' for Ground

Stalactites hang from cave ceilings, while stalagmites grow upward from cave floors. When they join, they form columns or pillars.

⚠️ Exam Trap: A classic trick question in TGT exams swaps the definitions of stalactites and stalagmites to test spatial visualization.

📚 High-Yield Geographic Terminology & Definitions

Peneplain UP PGT Geography

An extensive low-relief landform surface produced by subaerial erosion near base level, classically theorized by W.M. Davis in his geographical cycle of erosion.

Monadnock UGC-NET

An isolated resistant hill or mountain standing above a surrounding peneplain, named after Mount Monadnock in New Hampshire.

Barchan UP TGT

A crescent-shaped sand dune with tips pointing downwind, formed in areas of limited sand supply and unidirectional wind.

Zeugen UP PGT Geography

Table-shaped desert rock masses formed by differential wind erosion where soft strata underlie hard horizontal cap rock.

Yardang UGC-NET

Ridge and furrow landscape carved by wind abrasion in arid regions, aligned parallel to the prevailing wind direction.

Bajada UP TGT

A continuous apron of sediment formed by the coalescence of multiple alluvial fans along the base of a mountain range in arid or semi-arid environments.

Esker UGC-NET

A long, winding ridge of stratified sand and gravel associated with subglacial meltwater streams beneath stagnant or retreating ice sheets.

Drumlin UP PGT Geography

An oval or spoon-shaped hill of glacial till aligned parallel to the direction of ice flow, often called 'basket of eggs' topography.

Cirque UP TGT

An amphitheatre-shaped glacial headwall hollow carved out by nivation and glacial plucking at the source of a glacier.

Uvala UGC-NET

A large karst depression formed by the coalescence of multiple smaller sinkholes or dolines in limestone terrain.

Polje UP PGT Geography

A vast, flat-floored closed karst depression with internal drainage, bounded by steep limestone walls.

Stacks UP TGT

Isolated pillars of rock standing in the sea near a headland, formed by the wave erosion and collapse of sea arches.

Tombolo UGC-NET

A depositional landform in which an island is attached to the mainland by a narrow spit of sand or shingle.

🧭 Prescribed Scholars, Laws & Spatial Models

W.M. Davis (1899)

Geographical Cycle of Erosion ('Landscape is a function of structure, process, and time'). Stages include Youth, Maturity, and Old Age leading to peneplain formation.

Walther Penck (1924)

Morphological Analysis (Morphologische Analyse). Emphasized 'Endogen/Exogen' balance, proposing parallel retreat of slopes and dynamic crustal uplift rates (Aufsteigen).

Lester C. King (1953)

Cycle of Pediplanation. Applicable primarily to semi-arid and savanna regions, emphasizing scarp retreat and the formation of extensive pediplains rather than Davisian peneplains.

John Hack (1960)

Concept of Dynamic Equilibrium. Argued that landscapes adjust continuously to tectonic and lithological inputs without necessarily passing through distinct evolutionary stages.

Interactive Concept Practice

Instant evaluation with detailed spatial & physical explanations for each question.

Question 1 of 18
Which of the following landforms is produced by aeolian deposition rather than erosion?

Rationale: Loess is an accumulation of wind-blown silt deposits. Zeugen, yardangs, and Inselbergs are landforms predominantly shaped by wind erosion and weathering.

Question 2 of 18
What is a 'Uvala' in geomorphology?

Rationale: Uvalas are large karst depressions formed by the coalescence of several adjacent dolines (sinkholes) in limestone regions.

Question 3 of 18
The 'Geographical Cycle of Erosion' was propounded by:

Rationale: W.M. Davis formulated the Geographical Cycle in 1899, dividing landscape evolution into youth, maturity, and old age.

Question 4 of 18
Which landform is known as 'basket of eggs topography'?

Rationale: Drumlins are streamlined hills of glacial till that often occur in extensive swarms, referred to as 'basket of eggs' topography.

Question 5 of 18
Zeugen are formed primarily due to:

Rationale: Zeugen are table-like desert landforms formed when soft rock strata underlying a hard rock cap are eroded faster by wind action.

Question 6 of 18
A narrow ridge of sand connecting an island to the mainland is called a:

Rationale: A tombolo is a depositional landform where an island becomes attached to the mainland by a narrow spit or bar.

Question 7 of 18
Who proposed the theory of 'Morphological Analysis' focusing on parallel retreat of slopes?

Rationale: Walther Penck challenged Davisian theory by introducing 'Morphological Analysis' and stressing parallel slope retreat.

Question 8 of 18
Which of the following is a depositional feature of a glacier?

Rationale: Eskers are long, winding ridges of stratified drift deposited by meltwater streams flowing within or beneath glacial ice.

Question 9 of 18
In karst regions, caves and caverns are formed primarily by:

Rationale: Karst landforms are created by chemical weathering (solution and carbonation) of soluble rocks like limestone and dolomite by acidic groundwater.

Question 10 of 18
An isolated, steep-sided residual hill rising abruptly from a peneplain is called a:

Rationale: A monadnock is an isolated resistant hill standing above a surrounding peneplain surface.

Question 11 of 18
Which wind-blown deposit consists predominantly of silt-sized particles spread over vast areas?

Rationale: Loess is an aeolian sediment composed mostly of silt-sized grains, often originating from glacial outwash plains or deserts.

Question 12 of 18
What is a 'Bajada'?

Rationale: A bajada is a depositional feature formed when multiple adjacent alluvial fans coalesce along a mountain base in arid/semi-arid regions.

Question 13 of 18
Hanging valleys in glaciated mountains typically give rise to which feature when they meet the main valley?

Rationale: Hanging valleys are left suspended high above the deepened main glacial trough, frequently resulting in spectacular waterfalls.

Question 14 of 18
Which term describes a steep-sided, flat-topped hill common in arid landscapes with caprocks?

Rationale: A mesa is a broad, flat-topped elevation capped by a resistant rock layer, wider than it is high.

Question 15 of 18
The concept of 'Dynamic Equilibrium' in geomorphology was advocated by:

Rationale: John T. Hack proposed the concept of dynamic equilibrium, emphasizing that landforms reflect a balance among competing processes and lithologies.

Question 16 of 18
Which coastal feature is formed by longshore drift blocking the mouth of a bay?

Rationale: A baymouth bar is a depositional ridge of sand that completely or partially crosses the mouth of a bay, formed by longshore drift.

Question 17 of 18
Sinkholes are most commonly developed in which type of rock formation?

Rationale: Limestone (calcium carbonate) is highly susceptible to chemical dissolution, leading to the formation of sinkholes, dolines, and karst topography.

Question 18 of 18
Which desert landform is shaped like a crescent with horns pointing downwind?

Rationale: Barchans are crescent-shaped dunes where horns point in the direction of the wind due to lower sand supply and unidirectional wind.

Previous Years Questions & Exam Benchmarks

Real and benchmark questions aligned with UP PGT, TGT, and National Eligibility tests.

UP PGT Geography (Previous Year Pattern)
Which of the following landforms is a result of glacial deposition?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Moraines are accumulations of unsorted glacial till deposited directly by ice. Cirques, U-shaped valleys, and horns are erosional glacial landforms.

UGC-NET Geography (Previous Year Pattern)
Match List-I (Landform) with List-II (Process): A. Zeugen (Wind), B. Cirque (Glacial), C. Sinkhole (Karst), D. Stack (Marine). Which combination is correct?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Zeugen is aeolian erosion, Cirque is glacial erosion, Sinkhole is solution/karst action, and Stack is marine erosion. All mappings correspond directly to their formative agents.

UP TGT Geography (Previous Year Pattern)
An extensive plain formed by the coalescence of pediments in arid regions is known as:

Official Benchmark Explanation: A pediplain is a broad, low-relief plain formed by the coalescence of multiple pediments in arid or semi-arid environments, as emphasized by L.C. King.

UP PGT Geography (Previous Year Pattern)
According to W.M. Davis, what is the prime determinant of landform evolution?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Davis famously postulated that landscape is a function of three physical factors: structure, process, and time (the Davisian triad).

UGC-NET Geography (Previous Year Pattern)
Which of the following depositional coastal features has one end attached to the mainland and the other terminating in open water?

Official Benchmark Explanation: A spit is a depositional ridge extending from the coast into the sea, with one end attached to the mainland and the other free in open water.

UP PGT Geography (Previous Year Pattern)
Which of the following is NOT an erosional landform of underground water in karst regions?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Stalactites are depositional features hanging from cave roofs formed by the precipitation of calcium carbonate. Lapies, sinkholes, and caverns are erosional/solution forms.

Question 1 of 10
In the Davisian cycle of erosion, which stage is characterized by maximum relief, vigorous downcutting, and active valley deepening?

Solution: In the youthful stage of Davis's cycle, vertical downcutting is dominant, resulting in steep-sided V-shaped valleys and maximum relative relief.

Question 2 of 10
Consider the following statements regarding Yardangs: 1. They are ridges carved parallel to the prevailing wind. 2. They consist of alternating bands of hard and soft rock strata. Which statement(s) is/are correct?

Solution: Yardangs are ridge and furrow features sculpted by wind abrasion parallel to wind direction in regions with alternating resistant and weak rock layers.

Question 3 of 10
Which glacial depositional feature marks the maximum extent of an ice sheet or glacier?

Solution: A terminal moraine forms at the snout (terminus) of a glacier, delineating its maximum advance before retreat or stagnation.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following landforms is specifically associated with marine submergence coasts?

Solution: A ria coast is a drowned river valley system formed by relative sea-level rise (submergence). Raised beaches, platforms, and stacks are emergence/erosional features.

Question 5 of 10
What is a 'Playa' in arid geomorphology?

Solution: A playa is a dry, flat lake bed located in the lowest part of an arid interior drainage basin (bolson), occasionally filling with water to form ephemeral lakes.

Question 6 of 10
The process of 'Nivation' is most critical in the formation of which of the following?

Solution: Nivation involves frost shattering, thawing, and meltwater removal beneath snowbanks, initiating the hollows that develop into glacial cirques.

Question 7 of 10
Which theorist proposed that slopes retreat parallel to themselves during landscape evolution?

Solution: Walther Penck's model of slope evolution emphasizes parallel retreat (parallelvergleich), maintaining constant angle slopes as the hillslope recedes.

Question 8 of 10
A steep, narrow ridge separating two adjacent glacial valleys (cirques) is called:

Solution: An arete is a sharp-edged, knife-like mountain ridge formed when two adjacent cirques erode backwards toward one another.

Question 9 of 10
Which depositional feature forms in arid regions where a mountain stream emerges onto a lowland plain?

Solution: An alluvial fan is a cone-shaped deposit of sediment formed where a high-gradient stream emerges from a mountain canyon onto a flatter plain.

Question 10 of 10
What is the primary geomorphic agent responsible for creating 'Zeugen' and 'Yardangs'?

Solution: Zeugen and yardangs are classic aeolian erosional landforms sculpted by wind abrasion and deflation in arid deserts.
