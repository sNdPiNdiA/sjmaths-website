Physical Geography — Lithosphere

Earthquakes: Comprehensive Geographic Analysis, Physical & Human Mechanisms, and Regional Distribution

High-yield conceptual breakdown and physical/human geographical foundations of Earthquakes tailored for competitive exam mastery.

UP PGT Geography UP TGT Social Science B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Earthquakes represent the sudden release of accumulated strain energy in the Earth's lithosphere, resulting in seismic wave propagation and catastrophic geomorphic transformation. From a rigorous physical geography standpoint, seismicity is inextricably linked to plate boundary dynamics, subduction zones, transform faults, and intraplate stress anomalies. Beyond natural tectonic origins, contemporary geography increasingly analyzes anthropogenic seismicity induced by reservoir impoundment, hydraulic fracturing, deep-well waste fluid injection, and rapid urban mass loading.

1. Tectonic Mechanics and Elastic Rebound Theory

H.F. Reid’s Elastic Rebound Theory governing strain accumulation and instantaneous rupture along pre-existing fault planes.

The generation of tectonic earthquakes is governed by the gradual accumulation of tectonic stress within rock masses adjacent to plate boundaries. As continuous plate motion exerts frictional resistance along fault interfaces, rocks undergo elastic deformation, storing potential energy. Once the internal shear stress exceeds the ultimate frictional shear strength of the fault zone, brittle failure occurs. The rock masses abruptly snap back (rebound) toward an unstressed state, radiating energy hemispherically and spherically via seismic waves from the hypocenter (focus), while the point directly above on the surface is termed the epicenter. This mechanism explains shallow-focus earthquakes at divergent and transform boundaries, and deep-focus earthquakes descending down Benioff zones.

Comparative Analysis: Contrasts sharply with volcanic earthquakes (magma movement, restricted to volcanic arcs) and collapse/explosion earthquakes (localized, shallow, anthropogenic or gravitational cavern collapses).
  • Focus (Hypocenter) is the subsurface point of origin; Epicenter is the surface projection.
  • Elastic strain energy is converted into kinetic seismic wave energy upon brittle failure.
  • Deep-focus earthquakes occur exclusively in subduction zones up to 700 km depth.

2. Seismic Waves: Typology, Propagation, and Shadow Zones

Differential velocity and state-dependent propagation of Body waves (P and S) and Surface waves (Love and Rayleigh).

Seismic waves are categorized into body waves, which travel through the Earth's interior, and surface waves, which propagate along the Earth's crust. Body waves comprise Primary (P) waves, which are compressional/longitudinal waves capable of passing through both solids and liquids, and Secondary (S) waves, which are shear/transverse waves restricted entirely to solid media. Surface waves, including Love waves (horizontal particle motion transverse to propagation) and Rayleigh waves (elliptical retrograde motion), are slower but cause the most structural devastation due to their prolonged amplitudes. The refraction and attenuation of P and S waves through Earth's heterogeneous interior create distinct 'shadow zones' (P-wave shadow zone from 103° to 142° and S-wave shadow zone beyond 103°), proving the liquid nature of the Earth's outer core.

Comparative Analysis: P-waves travel 1.7 times faster than S-waves, serving as the primary arrival for early warning systems, whereas Surface waves dominate low-frequency, high-amplitude ground roll.
  • P-waves travel through solids, liquids, and gases; S-waves cannot travel through liquids.
  • Surface waves (Rayleigh and Love) are confined to the crust and decay exponentially with depth.
  • S-wave shadow zones confirm the outer core is molten.

3. Anthropogenic and Induced Seismicity

Human alteration of crustal stress regimes via Reservoir-Induced Seismicity (RIS), mining, and fluid injection.

Modern geographic analysis extends beyond natural tectonics to anthropogenic or induced seismicity. Large-scale anthropogenic interventions alter pore fluid pressure and effective normal stress on pre-existing fault systems. Reservoir-Induced Seismicity (RIS), exemplified by the Koyna Dam earthquake (Maharashtra, 1967), occurs due to immense hydrostatic water loading combined with increased pore-fluid pressure lubricating fault planes and reducing frictional resistance. Similarly, hydraulic fracturing ('fracking') and deep-well wastewater disposal inject high-pressure fluids into impermeable basement rocks, elevating pore pressure and triggering slip on critically stressed faults in stable intraplate regions like the mid-continental United States.

Comparative Analysis: Contrasts with natural tectonic quakes by demonstrating localized spatial clustering linked directly to engineering projects rather than plate boundary interactions.
  • Pore-fluid pressure increases reduce effective normal stress, facilitating fault slip.
  • Koyna Dam (India) is a classic textbook case of Reservoir-Induced Seismicity.
  • Wastewater injection has transformed stable intraplate zones into seismically active areas.

Spatial Patterns & Regional Distribution

🌐 Global Geographic Belts & Spatial Variation

Global seismicity is highly concentrated along three major continuous belts: 1) The Circum-Pacific Belt (Ring of Fire), accounting for nearly 80% of all energy release, associated with active subduction and convergent boundaries; 2) The Alpide Belt, stretching from the Mediterranean through the Himalayas to the East Indies, formed by continental collision between the African/Arabian and Eurasian plates; and 3) Mid-Oceanic Ridges, featuring shallow, divergent boundary earthquakes along spreading centers.

🇮🇳 Indian Subcontinent Context & Regional Corridors

India's seismicity is characterized by high vulnerability, with over 59% of the national landmass prone to moderate to severe earthquakes. The Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) categorizes India into four seismic zones (Zone II, III, IV, and V). Zone V represents the highest hazard level, encompassing the entire Northeast India, the Himalayan belt (Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand), the Kachchh region of Gujarat, and the Andaman-Nicobar archipelago. The seismic risk is driven primarily by the ongoing northward collision of the Indian Plate with the Eurasian Plate at an approximate rate of 40-50 mm/year.

📐 Key Geographic Formulas, Scales & Classifications
Richter Magnitude Scale ($M_L$)
M_L = \log_{10}(A) - \log_{10}(A_0)
Parameters: A = maximum amplitude of seismic wave recorded on a standard Wood-Anderson torsion seismograph; A_0 = correction factor accounting for distance from epicenter. Logarithmic base-10 scale where each whole number increase represents a 32-fold increase in energy release.
Exam Focus: Frequently tested in conceptual calculation questions. Remember: Magnitude increase of 1.0 = 32x energy; Magnitude increase of 2.0 = 1000x ($32^2$) energy.
Modified Mercalli Intensity (MMI) Scale
Qualitative Scale: Roman Numerals I to XII
Parameters: I = Imperceptible to people; XII = Total structural destruction, objects thrown into the air, ground surface visibly waved. Based on observed human reaction, structural damage, and environmental effects.
Exam Focus: Exam questions frequently contrast Mercalli (intensity, subjective, varies with distance) with Richter/Moment Magnitude (magnitude, objective, single value per event).

Mnemonics, Memory Shortcuts & Exam Traps

💡 Indian Seismic Zones (High to Low Risk)
V-N-H-S (Very Naughty Himalayan Students)

V = Zone V (Very Severe), N = Zone IV (Severe), H = Zone III (Moderate), S = Zone II (Low).

⚠️ Exam Trap: Examiners often trick candidates by stating Zone II is the highest risk. Always remember Zone V is the maximum hazard zone in India.
💡 Seismic Wave Velocity & State Behavior
P-Primary-Passes-All / S-Secondary-Solids-Only

P-waves pass through All states of matter (Solid, Liquid, Gas) and are Primary (fastest). S-waves pass through Solids only and are Secondary (slower).

⚠️ Exam Trap: Do not confuse S-waves with Surface waves; S-waves are body waves, whereas Love and Rayleigh are surface waves.

📚 High-Yield Geographic Terminology & Definitions

Focus (Hypocenter) UP PGT 2021

The exact point of origin of seismic waves beneath the Earth's surface.

Epicenter UGC NET

The point on the Earth's surface directly vertically above the seismic focus.

Seismograph UP TGT

An instrument used to detect and record the intensity, direction, and duration of ground vibrations.

Richter Scale UGC NET

A logarithmic scale ranging from 0 to 10 used to measure the total energy released by an earthquake.

Mercalli Scale UP PGT

A 12-point qualitative scale based on the observed destructive effects and human reactions to an earthquake.

Primary Waves (P-Waves) UP TGT

Longitudinal or compressional seismic waves that travel fastest through solids, liquids, and gases.

Secondary Waves (S-Waves) UGC NET

Transverse or shear seismic waves that can travel exclusively through solid media.

Surface Waves (L-Waves) UP PGT

Long-period seismic waves that travel along the Earth's outer layer, causing maximum structural devastation.

Shadow Zone UGC NET

The specific belt on the Earth's surface (103° to 142°) where seismic waves are not recorded due to refraction and absorption.

Benioff Zone UGC NET

A dipping planar zone of seismicity corresponding with the subducting slab in subduction zones.

Tectonic Earthquakes UP TGT

The most common class of earthquakes caused by the sudden slippage of rock masses along fault planes.

Plutonic Earthquakes UP PGT

Deep-focus earthquakes originating at depths greater than 300 km within the mantle.

Isoseismal Lines UP TGT

Lines drawn on a map connecting points that experience equal intensity of earthquake shaking.

🧭 Prescribed Scholars, Laws & Spatial Models

H.F. Reid (Elastic Rebound Theory) (1910)

Postulates that tectonic stress builds up strain within crustal rocks until the frictional resistance is exceeded, causing rocks to snap back and release energy as seismic waves.

Hugo Benioff (Benioff Seismic Zone) (1949)

Demonstrated that deep-focus earthquakes align along inclined zones dipping away from oceanic trenches beneath island arcs and active continental margins, mapping subducting plates.

Charles Richter (Magnitude Scale) (1935)

Established an open-ended logarithmic scale calculating the maximum amplitude of seismic waves recorded on a standard Wood-Anderson seismograph corrected for distance.

Gutenberg and Richter (Energy-Magnitude Relation) (1954)

Formulated the empirical relationship linking earthquake magnitude with the logarithm of radiated seismic energy, showing a 32-fold energy increase per magnitude unit.

Instant evaluation with detailed spatial & physical explanations for each question.

Question 1 of 18
Which of the following seismic waves is responsible for maximum damage on the Earth's surface?

Rationale: Surface waves (Rayleigh and Love waves) travel along the Earth's crust and have the longest duration and amplitude, causing the most structural damage.

Question 2 of 18
The Gutenberg seismic shadow zone for P-waves extends between which angular distances from the epicenter?

Rationale: P-waves are refracted by the Earth's core, creating a shadow zone spanning from 103 degrees to 142 degrees away from the epicenter.

Question 3 of 18
Who propounded the famous Elastic Rebound Theory of earthquakes?

Rationale: H.F. Reid propounded the Elastic Rebound Theory in 1910 based on his study of the 1906 San Francisco earthquake.

Question 4 of 18
What is the primary cause of tectonic earthquakes?

Rationale: Tectonic earthquakes are caused by sudden sliding and displacement of rock strata along geological faults due to plate tectonic forces.

Question 5 of 18
Which scale measures the intensity of earthquakes based on observed structural damage and human reaction?

Rationale: The Modified Mercalli Scale is a qualitative 12-point scale measuring earthquake intensity based on observed damage and human perception.

Question 6 of 18
S-waves cannot pass through which layer of the Earth?

Rationale: S-waves are transverse waves that cannot propagate through fluids. Since the outer core is liquid, S-waves fail to pass through it.

Question 7 of 18
The point within the Earth where seismic energy is first released is called:

Rationale: The focus or hypocenter is the subterranean point of origin of an earthquake.

Question 8 of 18
What is the approximate increase in energy release for every whole number increase on the Richter scale?

Rationale: Due to the logarithmic nature of the Richter scale, each unit increase represents approximately 32 times more energy release.

Question 9 of 18
Which of the following zones is most seismically active globally?

Rationale: The Circum-Pacific Belt (Ring of Fire) accounts for nearly 70-80% of all major global earthquakes.

Question 10 of 18
Deep-focus earthquakes are primarily associated with which tectonic setting?

Rationale: Deep-focus earthquakes (up to 700 km deep) occur exclusively in subduction zones where lithospheric plates plunge into the mantle.

Question 11 of 18
Lines connecting places experiencing earthquake shocks at the same time are called:

Rationale: Homoseismal lines connect places where earthquake waves arrive at the exact same time, whereas isoseismal lines connect points of equal intensity.

Question 12 of 18
Which type of seismic wave travels fastest and is recorded first on a seismograph?

Rationale: Primary (P) waves are compressional waves with the highest velocity, thus arriving at seismic stations before any other wave.

Question 13 of 18
Which instrument is used to record earthquake waves?

Rationale: A seismograph is the standard scientific instrument used to detect and record seismic waves.

Question 14 of 18
What kind of plate boundary is associated with the San Andreas Fault?

Rationale: The San Andreas Fault is a classic conservative or transform fault boundary where the Pacific plate and North American plate slide past each other.

Question 15 of 18
Collapse earthquakes are usually triggered by:

Rationale: Collapse earthquakes are minor local tremors caused by the collapse of underground roofs in mines or limestone caverns.

Question 16 of 18
Reservoir-induced seismicity is primarily triggered by:

Rationale: The massive weight of water in large reservoirs increases crustal stress and pore water pressure, triggering local earthquakes.

Question 17 of 18
The Benioff zone dips downward beneath which feature?

Rationale: Benioff zones dip downward from oceanic trenches toward island arcs along subduction zones.

Question 18 of 18
Which seismic wave motion is analogous to sound waves in air?

Rationale: P-waves are longitudinal/compressional waves that push and pull the ground, similar to sound waves traveling through air.

Real and benchmark questions aligned with UP PGT, TGT, and National Eligibility tests.

UP PGT Geography
Which one of the following earthquake waves is completely absorbed when it enters the Earth's outer core?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Secondary (S) waves are shear waves incapable of passing through liquids. Since the Earth's outer core is in a molten liquid state, S-waves are completely absorbed.

UGC NET Geography
Match List-I (Scale) with List-II (Parameter Measured): A. Richter Scale - 1. Earthquake Intensity B. Mercalli Scale - 2. Earthquake Magnitude C. Rossi-Forel Scale - 3. Early Intensity Scale D. Moment Magnitude Scale - 4. Seismic Moment and Energy Select the correct code:

Official Benchmark Explanation: Richter scale measures magnitude, Mercalli measures intensity, Rossi-Forel is a historical intensity scale, and Moment Magnitude measures total energy via seismic moment.

UP TGT Geography
The Alpine-Himalayan earthquake belt accounts for what percentage of world earthquakes?

Official Benchmark Explanation: The Alpine-Himalayan seismic belt accounts for approximately 21% of the world's earthquakes, while the Circum-Pacific belt accounts for nearly 68%.

UP PGT Geography
What is the shadow zone for S-waves?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Because S-waves cannot travel through the liquid outer core, no S-waves are recorded anywhere beyond an angular distance of 103 degrees from the epicenter, forming a shadow zone from 103° to 180°.

UGC NET Geography
Consider the following statements regarding earthquakes: 1. P-waves travel through solid, liquid, and gas. 2. S-waves are transverse waves. 3. Epicenter is always located in the ocean basins. Which of the above statements are correct?

Official Benchmark Explanation: Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is incorrect because epicenters can be located on continents as well as ocean floors.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following describes the correct chronological order of arrival of seismic waves at a distant recording station?

Solution: P-waves have the highest velocity and arrive first, followed by S-waves, and finally surface waves (L-waves) which travel along the crust.

Question 2 of 10
What causes the S-wave shadow zone to be much larger than the P-wave shadow zone?

Solution: S-waves cannot propagate through the liquid outer core, causing the entire hemisphere opposite to the focus beyond 103° to receive no direct S-waves.

Question 3 of 10
Which seismic wave exhibits particle motion perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation?

Solution: S-waves (Secondary/Shear waves) are transverse waves where particle displacement is perpendicular to the direction of wave travel.

Question 4 of 10
In terms of plate tectonics, shallow-focus earthquakes are common in all of the following EXCEPT:

Solution: Stable continental cratons (shields) lie far away from plate boundaries and experience very few, highly infrequent earthquakes.

Question 5 of 10
The depth of the focus for intermediate earthquakes typically ranges between:

Solution: Earthquakes are classified by depth: shallow (0-70 km), intermediate (70-300 km), and deep (300-700 km).

Question 6 of 10
Which scale replaced the Richter scale for accurate measurement of very large earthquakes due to its basis on seismic moment?

Solution: The Moment Magnitude Scale (Mw) measures the total energy released based on seismic moment (rigidity of rock x area of fault slip x slip distance), avoiding saturation issues of the Richter scale.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following regions in India falls under Seismic Zone V (Very High Damage Risk Zone)?

Solution: Zone V represents the highest seismic hazard zone in India, covering the entire Northeast, parts of Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, and the Andaman & Nicobar Islands.

Question 8 of 10
What is the primary reason why P-waves undergo refraction when entering the Earth's core?

Solution: The core-mantle boundary (Gutenberg discontinuity) marks a sharp contrast in density and seismic velocity, causing P-waves to bend (refract).

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following phenomena is often a precursor to volcanic eruptions and tectonic earthquakes?

Solution: Anomalous increases in radon gas concentration in groundwater are widely studied as short-term earthquake precursors due to micro-fracturing of rocks.

Question 10 of 10
Why are building structures founded on soft alluvial deposits generally more damaged during earthquakes than those on solid bedrock?

Solution: Soft alluvial sediments undergo seismic wave amplification and resonance, causing significantly stronger ground shaking compared to solid bedrock.
