Physical Geography — Lithosphere

Plains: Comprehensive Geographic Analysis, Physical & Human Mechanisms, and Regional Distribution

High-yield conceptual breakdown and physical/human geographical foundations of Plains tailored for competitive exam mastery.

UP PGT Geography UP TGT Social Science B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Plains constitute extensive, low-relief tracts of land characterized by gentle gradients and low absolute elevation relative to sea level, forming the cradles of human civilization. Geomorphologically, they are classified based on their genesis into structural, erosional, depositional, and glacio-fluvial plains, with fluvial deposition acting as the dominant sculpturing agent across the globe. From a tectonic and structural viewpoint, they frequently occupy cratonic interiors, foredeeps of collisional mountain chains, or coastal margins, integrating complex processes of subsidence, sedimentation, and eustatic sea-level fluctuations. For competitive exams like UP TGT/PGT Geography and UGC-NET, mastery requires dissecting the micro-relief of alluvial plains (Bhabar, Terai, Bhangar, Khadar), deltaic morphology, structural classifications, and anthropogenic carrying capacities.

1. Geomorphic Genesis and Genetic Classification of Plains

Plains are formed through diverse endogenetic and exogenetic processes operating across varying temporal and spatial scales.

  • Structural plains are formed by tectonic stability, epeirogenic uplift of continental shelves, or cratonic downwarping.
  • Depositional plains include alluvial plains (floodplains, meander belts), deltaic plains, loess plains, glacial outwash plains, and lacustrine plains.
  • Erosional plains (peneplains and pediplains) are produced by subaerial denudation, weather-mass wasting, and lateral planation over prolonged geological eras.
  • Glacio-fluvial or outwash plains (sandurs) form via meltwater deposition of stratified drift beyond terminal moraines during glacial retreats.
  • Coastal plains form through marine sedimentation, wave-built platform emergence, or relative drop in sea levels.
Comparative Analysis: Contrasts structural plains (cratonic stability, e.g., Russian Platform) with depositional plains (foreland basins, e.g., Indo-Gangetic Plain) and erosional peneplains (Davisian cycle of erosion terminal stages).
  • Alluvial depositional plains represent the highest concentration of global agrarian and demographic settlement.
  • Loess plains (e.g., Loess Plateau of China) represent aeolian accumulation of fine wind-blown silt particles.

2. Alluvial Micro-Morphology of the Indo-Gangetic Plain

North Indian Plains exhibit systematic longitudinal and transverse zonation based on sediment load, stream competence, and permeability.

  • Bhabar zone lies parallel to the Shiwalik foothills, characterized by high porosity, coarse boulders, and disappearing streams.
  • Terai zone features marshy, water-releasing subterranean streams, high biodiversity, and dense fine-grained sedimentary accumulation.
  • Bhangar represents older alluvial terraces above flood limits, often containing calcareous concretion nodules known as kankar.
  • Khadar represents newer, fertile alluvial floodplains replenished annually by monsoon river flooding.
  • Reh or Kalar refers to saline-alkaline efflorescences developing in canal-irrigated semi-arid tracts of the western Gangetic plain.
Comparative Analysis: Compares Bhangar (pedologically mature, leached, higher elevation) with Khadar (immature, highly fertile, seasonally flooded lowlands).
  • Bhabar is non-arable due to boulder fragmentation; Khadar is agriculturally prime but prone to fluvial shifting.
  • Canal irrigation without adequate drainage converts productive Bhangar into sterile Reh wasteland.

3. Structural, Tectonic, and Climatic Controls on Plain Formation

Plains are profoundly influenced by foreland subsidence, crustal flexure, and climatic regimes governing weathering and sediment yield.

  • Foredeep plains (e.g., Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra trough) develop in flexural downwarping zones ahead of collision-induced orogenic belts.
  • Pediplains form in arid and semi-arid environments through parallel retreat of slopes and sheetwash action.
  • Coastal plains are heavily controlled by eustatic sea-level changes and continental shelf gradients.
  • Glacial depositional plains reflect Pleistocene glacial advances, till deposition, and drumlinized till plains.
  • Climatic zones dictate the mechanical vs chemical weathering balance, directly impacting sediment calibre reaching depositional basins.
Comparative Analysis: Contrasts Suess and Argand's geosynclinal foredeep subsidence models with modern plate tectonic foreland basin dynamics.
  • Foreland basins undergo continuous subsidence, accommodating thick sequences of continental clastic sediments.
  • Arid plains show extensive pedimentation, whereas humid tropical plains are dominated by chemical weathering and thick alluvium.

Spatial Patterns & Regional Distribution

🌐 Global Geographic Belts & Spatial Variation

Major global plain belts include the Great Plains of North America (interior continental deposition/glacial outwash), the East European and Russian Platform Plains (cratonic stability), the Amazon Basin (tropical equatorial structural/alluvial plain), the Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra Plain (foredeep tectonic plain), and the North China Plain (alluvial-loessic plain).

🇮🇳 Indian Subcontinent Context & Regional Corridors

The Great Plains of India span from the Indus river system in the west to the Brahmaputra valley in the east, wedged between the Peninsular Plateau and the Himalayan orogen. It is sub-divided into the Rajasthan Plain (Thar desert, Luni basin, inland drainage), Punjab-Haryana Plain (Sutlej-Beas-Ravi interfluves or doabs), Ganga Plain (Upper, Middle, Lower sub-regions), and the Brahmaputra (Assam) Valley plain.

📐 Key Geographic Formulas, Scales & Classifications
Stream Gradient Index (SL Index)
SL = (ΔH / ΔL) * L
Parameters: ΔH = change in elevation of reach; ΔL = length of reach; L = total stream length from watershed divide to midpoint of reach.
Exam Focus: Frequently used in UGC-NET geomorphology questions to evaluate active tectonic uplift, knickpoints, and longitudinal profile adjustments in plain-edge transition zones.
Roughness Index (Relief Amplitude)
R = H_max - H_min
Parameters: H_max = maximum elevation within a given grid cell; H_min = minimum elevation within the same grid cell, measured in meters.
Exam Focus: Helps quantitatively differentiate true plains (very low R values, <50m) from plateau margins and dissected uplands in regional physiographic mapping.

📚 High-Yield Geographic Terminology & Definitions

Peneplain UP PGT Geography

An extensive lowland area of relatively low relief formed by the long-term erosion of a landsurface almost down to base level, as conceptualized by W.M. Davis.

Bhabar UP TGT

A narrow belt ranging from 8 to 16 km lying parallel to the slopes of the Shiwaliks where rivers deposit heavy loads of coarse pebbles and boulders, rendering streams underground.

Terai UGC-NET

A marshy and swampy region lying south of the Bhabar belt where underground streams reappear on the surface, supporting thick forests and diverse wildlife.

Bangar UP PGT Geography

The older alluvial plain found above the floodplains of the rivers, characterized by calcareous deposits locally known as kankar.

Khadar UP TGT

The newer, younger alluvial deposits found along river beds which are enriched by fresh silt floods almost every year, making them extremely fertile.

Piedmont Plain UGC-NET

A gentle slope plain formed at the foot of mountain ranges through the coalescing of multiple alluvial fans (also called pediments when formed in arid zones).

Loess Plain UP PGT Geography

An extensive upland plain formed by the accumulation of wind-blown fine silt or dust particles, famously developed in the Loess Plateau of Northwest China.

Panplain UGC-NET

A low-relief plain formed by the lateral erosion and shifting of meandering rivers across a valley floor, coalescing floodplains as described by C.A. Cotton.

Esker UP TGT

Long, winding ridges of stratified sand and gravel associated with glaciation, often traversing outwash plains.

Outwash Plain UP PGT Geography

A broad plain formed in front of the terminal moraine of a glacier by the action of meltwater streams carrying stratified drift.

Deltaic Plain UP TGT

Low-lying flatlands formed at the mouth of major rivers through the extensive deposition of sediment load as the river velocity drops upon entering a standing body of water.

Structural Plain UGC-NET

Relatively undisturbed horizontal sedimentary strata forming extensive flat surfaces, such as the Great Plains of the USA or the Russian Platform.

Reh UP PGT Geography

Saline and alkaline efflorescences found in the drier parts of the Ganga plain, particularly on the Bangar uplands due to intensive canal irrigation.

🧭 Prescribed Scholars, Laws & Spatial Models

W.M. Davis's Cycle of Erosion (1899)

Landforms evolve through a sequential cycle of Youth, Maturity, and Old Age, culminating in the ultimate formation of a low-relief erosional surface termed a 'peneplain'.

Penck's Morphological Analysis (1924)

Landscape development depends on the ratio of crustal uplift (Endogenetic forces) to denudation (Exogenous forces), leading to parallel retreat of slopes and the formation of 'Primrumpf' (initial plain) and 'Endrumpf' (final plain).

L.C. King's Pediplanation Cycle (1953)

Focusing on semiarid and savanna landscapes, King argued that landforms evolve through scarp retreat, coalescing pediments to form extensive desert plains known as 'pediplains'.

Playfair's Law of Uniformity / Dynamic Equilibrium (1802)

Rivers cut their own valleys and establish graded profiles, maintaining a dynamic balance between sediment load and energy, leading to the creation of alluvial plains and floodplains.

Interactive Concept Practice

Instant evaluation with detailed spatial & physical explanations for each question.

Question 1 of 18
Which of the following depositional landforms is formed by the coalescence of alluvial fans along the foothills?

Rationale: Piedmont alluvial plains (also called bajadas when continuous) are formed when multiple alluvial fans merge together along the base of a mountain range.

Question 2 of 18
In the Northern Plains of India, streams disappear into which belt due to high porosity of heavy boulders and pebbles?

Rationale: The Bhabar belt is composed of porous pebble and boulder deposits dropped by rivers descending from the Shiwaliks, causing small streams to flow underground.

Question 3 of 18
Who introduced the concept of 'Peneplain' to denote the end-product of normal fluvial cycle of erosion?

Rationale: W.M. Davis coined the term 'peneplain' (almost a plain) to describe the low-relief featureless landscape produced in the old age stage of his geographical cycle.

Question 4 of 18
Which type of plain is exemplified by the Russian Platform and the Great Plains of Central North America?

Rationale: Structural plains are formed by relatively undisturbed horizontal rock strata resting on stable continental shields or platforms, like the Russian and North American interior plains.

Question 5 of 18
The older alluvium of the Indo-Gangetic plain, which lies above the flood level and contains kankar nodules, is known as:

Rationale: Bangar represents the older alluvial terrace systems containing calcium carbonate nodules (kankar), whereas Khadar is the newer, annually flooded alluvium.

Question 6 of 18
Which term did L.C. King use for the desert plain formed by the coalescence of pediments?

Rationale: L.C. King used 'pediplain' to describe extensive low-relief plains formed by pedimentation and backwearing of slopes in arid and semi-arid environments.

Question 7 of 18
Loess plains are primarily formed by the depositional action of:

Rationale: Loess consists of fine-grained wind-blown dust (silt) accumulated over large areas, creating fertile loess plateaus and plains (e.g., in North China).

Question 8 of 18
Which of the following is a classic example of an erosional plain formed by glacial action?

Rationale: The Canadian Shield contains extensive ice-scoured plains (glacial drift and scoured bedrock plains) carved out by continental ice sheets.

Question 9 of 18
Saline efflorescences developing on the Bangar plains of India due to excessive canal irrigation are called:

Rationale: Reh or Kallar refers to the white saline-alkaline efflorescence that accumulates on the surface of older alluvial plains due to rising water tables induced by canal irrigation.

Question 10 of 18
What is the primary geomorphic agent responsible for the formation of an 'outwash plain'?

Rationale: Outwash plains are formed by glacial meltwater depositing stratified gravel, sand, and silt beyond the terminal moraine.

Question 11 of 18
C.A. Cotton used which of the following terms to denote a plain formed by the lateral shifting and coalescing of river floodplains?

Rationale: C.A. Cotton coined 'panplain' to describe plains produced by the lateral corrasion and meandering of rivers across valley floors.

Question 12 of 18
Which Indian physiographic division is characterized by marshy conditions, dense forests, and the reappearance of underground streams?

Rationale: The Terai region lies immediately south of the Bhabar, where subterranean streams emerge to create a wet, swampy environment.

Question 13 of 18
Marine plains are formed by:

Rationale: Marine plains are formed either by the uplift of continental shelf areas (plain of marine emergence) or wave-built depositional platforms along coasts.

Question 14 of 18
The Pampas of Argentina and the Downs of Australia are notable examples of:

Rationale: The Pampas and Downs are extensive flat to gently rolling depositional plains characterized by deep soil mantles and temperate grasslands.

Question 15 of 18
Walther Penck termed the initial undulating plain before tectonic uplift as:

Rationale: Primrumpf ('primary crust') refers to the initial low-relief land surface in Penck's geomorphic model prior to major tectonic uplift.

Question 16 of 18
Which plain in India is formed entirely by basaltic lava flows filling pre-existing topography?

Rationale: Volcanic or lava plains (such as parts of the Deccan Trap region) are formed by fissure eruptions spreading fluid basaltic lava to form extensive flat surfaces.

Question 17 of 18
Which factor is primarily responsible for the high fertility of Khadar plains?

Rationale: Khadar plains are low-lying floodplains that receive fresh layers of nutrient-rich alluvial silt during annual monsoon floods.

Question 18 of 18
An extensive plain formed on the sea floor that has been uplifted above sea level is called a:

Rationale: A plain of marine emergence is created when a shallow continental shelf is uplifted tectonically above sea level, e.g., the Coromandel Coastal plains.

Question 1 of 10
Consider the following statements regarding alluvial plains: 1. Khadar soils are richer in organic nutrients compared to Bangar soils due to annual replenishment. 2. Bhabar is composed of fine clay particles suitable for extensive paddy cultivation. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Solution: Statement 1 is correct because Khadar receives annual fresh silt. Statement 2 is incorrect because Bhabar is composed of coarse boulders and pebbles, making it unsuitable for cultivation.

Question 2 of 10
Which geomorphological term corresponds to the initial undulating low-relief surface before tectonic uplift in Walther Penck's model?

Solution: Penck designated the baseline low-relief landscape prior to uplift as Primrumpf.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following pairs of Plain Types and Agents of Formation is incorrectly matched?

Solution: A panplain is formed by fluvial lateral erosion and shifting river floodplains, not marine waves.

Question 4 of 10
The uplift of a continental shelf forms which of the following?

Solution: When a shallow sea floor or continental shelf is uplifted above sea level, it results in a plain of marine emergence.

Question 5 of 10
What is the primary reason for the underground flow of rivers in the Bhabar region?

Solution: Rivers drop heavy coarse loads of boulders and pebbles at the mountain base, creating a highly porous medium where water sinks below the surface.

Question 6 of 10
In the context of erosional plains, which scholar argued against Davis's concept of downwasting and emphasized parallel slope retreat leading to pediplanation?

Solution: Both Walther Penck and L.C. King rejected Davis's downwasting model in favor of slope retreat mechanisms (waxing/waning development and pediplanation).

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following regions in India is most prone to the formation of 'Reh' (saline efflorescence) due to intense irrigation?

Solution: Intensive canal irrigation in the drier Bangar tracts of Western UP and Punjab raises the water table, drawing dissolved salts to the surface through capillary action (Reh).

Question 8 of 10
Which geomorphic process creates a floodplain?

Solution: Floodplains are built up through lateral accretion and vertical deposition of sediments when rivers overflow their banks during floods.

Question 9 of 10
The Great Plains of the United States, stretching from the Gulf of Mexico to Canada, owe their origin primarily to:

Solution: The North American Great Plains are extensive structural and depositional plains shaped by sedimentary infilling, glacial meltwater outwash, and fluvial action.

Question 10 of 10
Which term describes a flat, low-lying alluvial tract formed at the mouth of a river distributary network entering the sea?

Solution: Deltaic plains are formed at river mouths where distributary channels deposit suspended sediment load in low-energy marine or lacustrine settings.
