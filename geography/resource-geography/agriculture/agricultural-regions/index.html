Resource Geography — Agriculture

Agricultural Regions: Comprehensive Geographic Analysis, Physical & Human Mechanisms, and Regional Distribution

High-yield conceptual breakdown and physical/human geographical foundations of Agricultural Regions tailored for competitive exam mastery.

UP PGT Geography UP TGT Social Science B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

The systematic demarcation of agricultural regions constitutes a foundational paradigm in Resource Geography and economic landscape analysis. Pioneered systematically by D. Whittlesey in 1936, the spatial classification of agriculture relies on the synthesis of physical determinants—such as thermal regimes, precipitation gradients, edaphic conditions, and topographic variations—with human factors, including agrarian technologies, capital intensity, land tenure systems, market accessibility, and cultural traditions. Understanding these complexes allows geographers to map functional agricultural typologies that reflect distinct operational strategies and resource-use efficiencies across diverse global and national landscapes. For aspirants of UP TGT/PGT Geography and UGC-NET, mastery over agricultural regions requires linking macro-climatic realms with micro-economic decision-making frameworks. Questions frequently target Whittlesey's 13 primary agricultural types, Von Thünen's spatial-economic land-use modeling, and contemporary indices of agricultural productivity and diversification. Rigorous preparation demands an analytical grip on how physiographic divisions, river basins, and institutional mechanisms shape cropping patterns, intensity gradients, and the spatial transition from subsistence systems to highly commercialized, capital-intensive agribusiness complexes.

1. Whittlesey's Classification and Empirical Criteria of Agricultural Regions

Whittlesey's 1936 empirical division of the earth's surface into distinct agricultural realms based on functional and operational criteria.

  • Based on five principal criteria: crop and livestock association, operational methods, labor/capital intensity, product destination, and housing/equipment utilization.
  • Identifies 13 major agricultural regions globally, categorized broadly into subsistence, mixed, and commercial enterprise types.
  • Nomadic Herding dominates arid/semi-arid latitudinal belts where extreme aridity precludes sedentary crop cultivation.
  • Shifting Cultivation (Swidden/Milpa/Jhum) persists in tropical rainforest realms under low population pressure and nutrient-deficient latosols.
  • Subsistence Crop and Livestock (Intensive Subsistence without paddy) dominates monsoon Asia interior dry zones, focusing on wheat, sorghum, and millets.
  • Intensive Subsistence with Rice dominant occupies alluvial lowlands of East, South, and Southeast Asia with high labor input per unit area.
  • Commercial Plantation agriculture is localized in tropical lowlands, specializing in monoculture cash crops (tea, rubber, coffee, oil palm).
  • Mediterranean Agriculture adapts to dry-summer subtropical climates, featuring viticulture, olive cultivation, and citrus orchards.
  • Commercial Grain Farming characterizes sub-humid temperate grasslands (Great Prairies, Eurasian Steppes) with high mechanization and low labor input.
Comparative Analysis: Contrasts sharply with purely environmental determinist classifications by integrating economic intent and technological inputs, moving beyond mere climate-vegetation matching.
  • Whittlesey established 13 major regions.
  • Intensive subsistence rice dominates monsoon lowlands.
  • Commercial grain farming relies heavily on mechanized monoculture.

2. Von Thünen's Isolated State Model and Spatial-Economic Optimization

Land-use intensity and agricultural zoning are governed by transport costs and economic rent relative to urban market centers.

  • Assumes an isolated city within a uniform plain, surrounded by isotropic soil and equal transport accessibility in all directions.
  • Economic Rent (Locational Rent) decreases with increasing distance from the central market due to rising transport costs.
  • Ring 1 (Closest to Market): Intensive farming and dairying due to high perishability and high transport costs for bulky products.
  • Ring 2: Forestry (Timber was heavy and crucial for fuel and construction in the 19th-century baseline model).
  • Ring 3: Crop rotation without fallow, intensive grain farming.
  • Ring 4: Three-field system combining crops and fallow.
  • Ring 5: Livestock farming and ranching situated at the outermost periphery due to self-transportability (animals walking to market).
  • Modifying factors include navigable rivers (which stretch zones outward linearly) and secondary urban market nodes.
Comparative Analysis: Compares with Alonso's Urban Land Use Model and Weber's Industrial Location Theory, operating on the foundational principle of transport-cost minimization versus material index.
  • Rent declines uniformly with distance from the central market.
  • Perishable and bulky items occupy inner rings.
  • Extensive livestock occupies peripheral zones.

3. Agro-Climatic and Agro-Ecological Zoning in India

Hierarchical spatial partitioning of India based on physiography, soils, climate, and growing periods for optimal resource planning.

  • Planning Commission (now NITI Aayog) divided India into 15 distinct Agro-Climatic Zones for decentralized agricultural macro-planning.
  • ICAR expanded this framework into 60 Agro-Ecological Regions (AERs) based on pedological, meteorological, and length of growing period (LGP) criteria.
  • Zone 1 (Western Himalayan Region) emphasizes temperate horticulture, terraced farming, and transhumance.
  • Zone 4 (Middle Gangetic Plain) exhibits high agricultural intensity, rich alluvial soils, and dominance of rice-wheat cropping systems.
  • Zone 10 (Southern Plateau and Hills) focuses on dryland farming, rainfed millets, cotton, and oilseeds.
  • Agro-ecological zoning directly dictates irrigation requirement indexes and drought vulnerability mapping across peninsular tracts.
Comparative Analysis: Shifts perspective from monolithic national planning to micro-regional physiographic customization, contrasting with generalized global agricultural belts.
  • 15 macro Planning Commission zones.
  • 60 ICAR Agro-Ecological Regions based on LGP.
  • Tailors cropping systems to regional soil-water dynamics.

4. Agricultural Typology and Intensity Indices

Quantitative metrics used to evaluate cropping intensity, specialization, and diversification across spatial units.

  • Cropping Intensity Index measures the proportion of net sown area cropped more than once in an agricultural year.
  • Jasbir Singh and Weaver's Crop Combination analysis determines structural dominance of crops in regional agricultural profiles.
  • Bhatia's Method of Agricultural Efficiency correlates crop yield per hectare with regional acreage proportions.
  • Index of Agricultural Diversification highlights risk-minimizing polyculture versus export-oriented monoculture.
Comparative Analysis: Compares qualitative descriptive regionalization with rigorous quantitative spatial analysis tools utilized in modern economic geography.
  • Cropping intensity reflects multiple cropping capabilities.
  • Weaver's method is standard for crop combination regions.
  • Efficiency indices balance yield and acreage.

Spatial Patterns & Regional Distribution

🌐 Global Geographic Belts & Spatial Variation

Global agricultural systems form distinct latitudinal and continental belts. Nomadic herding concentrates in the tundra and arid deserts of Central Eurasia and North Africa. Mediterranean agriculture forms micro-thermal belts along coastal Southern Europe, California, Central Chile, the Cape Province of South Africa, and Southwest Australia. Commercial grain farming dominates the interior mid-latitude steppes of North America, Ukraine, and the Argentine Pampas. Intensive subsistence rice agriculture is bounded by monsoonal East and Southeast Asia, while dairy farming clusters heavily around urban industrial clusters in Northeastern North America and Northwestern Europe.

🇮🇳 Indian Subcontinent Context & Regional Corridors

India's agricultural landscape is partitioned by its major physiographic units and river basins. The Indo-Gangetic Alluvial Plain functions as the granary of India, dominated by the intensive Rice-Wheat cropping system across Punjab, Haryana, Western Uttar Pradesh, and Bihar. The Deccan Trap (Black Cotton Soil/Regur) region of Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, and Gujarat specializes in cotton, soybean, and pulses. The coastal strips and deltas of the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Cauvery rivers represent high-intensity paddy belts. The rain-prone Western Ghats and Northeast India host plantation economies (tea, coffee, rubber, and spices), while the arid Western Thar region (Rajasthan) relies on pearl millet and livestock ranching.

📐 Key Geographic Formulas, Scales & Classifications
Cropping Intensity Index (CII)
CII = (Gross Cropped Area / Net Sown Area) * 100
Parameters: Gross Cropped Area (GCA) = Total area sown once and more than once in a year; Net Sown Area (NSA) = Total physical land area sown with crops.
Exam Focus: Frequently tested in UGC-NET and UP PGT numerical problems to assess irrigation efficiency and double-cropping capacity of Indian states.
Weaver's Crop Combination Method Variance Formula
σ² = Σ(d²) / n
Parameters: d = difference between the actual percentage of a crop and the theoretical percentage in a given combination; n = number of crops in the combination.
Exam Focus: Used to determine the exact number of dominant crops that constitute a regional agricultural profile by selecting the combination that yields the minimum variance (σ²).

Mnemonics, Memory Shortcuts & Exam Traps

💡 Whittlesey's Major Commercial Agricultural Types
M-D-C-L-G (My Dairy Cow Loves Grass)

M = Mediterranean Agriculture, D = Dairy Farming, C = Commercial Grain Farming, L = Livestock Ranching, G = Grain and Livestock (Mixed Farming).

⚠️ Exam Trap: Do not confuse Whittlesey's commercial types with purely subsistence-based swidden or nomadic categories when answering multiple-choice matching questions.
💡 Von Thünen Rings Order (Inside to Outside)
F-D-F-G-L (Fresh Dairy Forests Grain Livestock)

F = Farming/Market Gardening, D = Dairying, F = Forest (Timber), G = Grain/Crop Rotation, L = Livestock/Ranching.

⚠️ Exam Trap: Examiners often swap Forest and Dairying positions; remember that in the 19th-century context, heavy timber transport necessitated inner-ring proximity alongside perishables.

📚 High-Yield Geographic Terminology & Definitions

Agricultural Regions UGC-NET Fundamental

Contiguous spatial areas exhibiting relative homogeneity in agricultural characteristics such as crop combinations, livestock, and farming techniques.

Crop Combination Region UP PGT Geography

A delimitation technique introduced by J.C. Weaver to determine the dominance of individual crops in a given areal unit based on variance analysis.

Nomadic Herding TGT/PGT Core

A primitive subsistence form of agriculture where pastoralists move livestock continuously or seasonally in search of fresh pastures and water.

Commercial Grain Farming UGC-NET Concept

Extensive, highly mechanized cereal production primarily for export and market sale, typical of mid-latitude interior steppes and prairies.

Mediterranean Agriculture UP PGT Map-based

A specialized farming system characterized by viticulture, olive cultivation, and citrus orchards adapted to dry summers and wet winters.

Plantation Agriculture Economic Geography

A large-scale, capital-intensive tropical enterprise specializing in the mono-cropping of cash crops like tea, coffee, rubber, and sugarcane.

Intensity of Cropping UP TGT Quantitative

The ratio of net sown area to the total cropped area expressed as a percentage, reflecting the frequency of land use within an agricultural year.

Truck Farming UGC-NET Terminology

Horticultural production specializing in perishable vegetables and fruits transported rapidly by motor vehicles to urban markets.

Shifting Cultivation Resource Geography

A rotational bush-fallow system of subsistence farming wherein forest plots are cleared, burned, farmed temporarily, and abandoned.

Mixed Farming UGC-NET High-Yield

An integrated agricultural system combining crop cultivation and livestock rearing on the same farm to diversify income and maintain soil fertility.

Agro-forestry Modern Concept

A land-use management system in which trees or shrubs are grown around or among crops or pastureland to enhance biodiversity and sustainability.

Von Thünen's Rings Core Agricultural Model

Concentric zones of agricultural land use surrounding a central market town, determined by transport costs and perishability.

Dairy Farming UP PGT Specific

A specialized branch of agriculture focused on the long-term production of milk and milk products, heavily concentrated near urban consumer centers.

🧭 Prescribed Scholars, Laws & Spatial Models

D. Whittlesey (1936)

Identified 13 major agricultural regions of the world based on five functional criteria: crop-livestock association, methods, labor intensity, output disposal, and infrastructural association.

J.C. Weaver (1954)

Developed the minimum variance method using standard deviation to mathematically delimit crop combination regions in the United States.

Johann Heinrich von Thünen (1826)

Formulated the Isolated State model demonstrating that agricultural land use patterns form concentric rings around a central market based on transport costs and economic rent.

O. Bhatia (1965)

Modified crop combination techniques by introducing a ranking method based on percentage deviations to study agricultural efficiency and regional disparities in India.

Question 1 of 18
Who among the following geographers is credited with the classic classification of the world's agricultural regions into 13 distinct types?

Rationale: D. Whittlesey published his monumental classification of world agricultural regions in 1936 in the Annals of the Association of American Geographers, establishing 13 main types.

Question 2 of 18
Which mathematical technique was primarily employed by J.C. Weaver in 1954 to delimit crop combination regions?

Rationale: J.C. Weaver used the standard deviation formula to measure the variance between actual crop percentages and theoretical distributions to determine crop combinations.

Question 3 of 18
In Von Thünen's agricultural land use model, which activity is located in the innermost ring closest to the central market?

Rationale: Dairying and market gardening occupy the first ring because their products are highly perishable and transport costs to the central market are exceptionally high.

Question 4 of 18
Shifting cultivation is known as 'Milpa' in which of the following regions?

Rationale: Shifting cultivation is known as Milpa in Central America and Mexico, Roca in Brazil, Ladang in Indonesia, and Jhum in Northeast India.

Question 5 of 18
Which of the following agricultural systems is characterized by the production of wine grapes, olives, and figs?

Rationale: Mediterranean agriculture is uniquely adapted to dry-summer subtropical climates and specializes in viticulture, arboriculture (olives, figs, citrus), and horticulture.

Question 6 of 18
What is the primary characteristic of extensive commercial grain farming?

Rationale: Extensive commercial grain farming takes place on vast tracts of land (e.g., prairies, steppes) with heavy machinery, low population density, and low labor input per hectare.

Question 7 of 18
Which Indian geographer modified Weaver's crop combination method by using percentage deviations to study agricultural regions in Rajasthan?

Rationale: S.S. Bhatia (1965) proposed a modified method of crop combination analysis that avoided the cumbersome calculations of standard deviation used by Weaver.

Question 8 of 18
Truck farming is most closely associated with which type of agricultural activity?

Rationale: Truck farming refers to the commercial production of vegetables on a truck-farm scale, historically transported by trucks to urban centers.

Question 9 of 18
Which of the following climatic conditions is most critical for Mediterranean agriculture?

Rationale: Mediterranean climate features hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters, which favor drought-tolerant crops like olives, grapes, and citrus fruits.

Question 10 of 18
Plantation agriculture requires which of the following combinations of factors?

Rationale: Plantations are large-scale corporate or colonial enterprises featuring heavy capital investment, specialized tropical cash crops, and managerial hierarchies.

Question 11 of 18
What does 'Jhum' cultivation refer to in the context of Indian agriculture?

Rationale: Jhum or Jhuming is the local term for slash-and-burn shifting cultivation practiced predominantly by tribal communities in Northeast India.

Question 12 of 18
Which of the following is NOT one of Whittlesey’s functional criteria for classifying world agricultural regions?

Rationale: Whittlesey used crop-livestock combination, farming methods, labor/capital intensity, product disposal, and housing/infrastructural association; political ideology is excluded.

Question 13 of 18
Nomadic herding is typically practiced in which geographic environments?

Rationale: Nomadic herding thrives in regions with sparse natural vegetation where crop farming is impossible due to extreme aridity, cold, or rugged terrain.

Question 14 of 18
Mixed farming is most prominently developed in which continent?

Rationale: Mixed farming—combining crops and livestock—is a hallmark of humid temperate regions, especially in Northwest Europe and parts of North America.

Question 15 of 18
Which crop combination technique uses the standard deviation to find the minimum variance?

Rationale: J.C. Weaver’s method (1954) uses standard deviation to test the fit of actual crop percentages against theoretical distributions.

Question 16 of 18
What is the primary objective of commercial dairy farming?

Rationale: Commercial dairy farming is specialized in producing milk, butter, and cheese, requiring proximity to urban consumer markets or advanced refrigeration transport.

Question 17 of 18
Which index measures the number of crops grown on a given piece of land in a single agricultural year?

Rationale: Cropping intensity measures how frequently land is cropped within a year, calculated as (Gross Cropped Area / Net Sown Area) × 100.

Question 18 of 18
Which region is known as the 'Granary of the World' due to extensive commercial grain farming?

Rationale: The North American Prairies are widely termed the granary of the world because of massive surplus wheat production through extensive farming.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following assumptions is fundamental to Von Thünen's agricultural land use model?

Solution: Von Thünen assumed an isolated state consisting of an isotropic flat plain with uniform soil fertility, uniform transport costs in all directions, and a single central market.

Question 2 of 10
What is the primary reason for the intensive nature of paddy rice farming in Monsoon Asia?

Solution: Intensive subsistence rice farming dominates Monsoon Asia due to high population pressure, requiring maximum caloric output per unit of land through intensive labor.

Question 3 of 10
How does Weaver’s method differ from Bhatia’s method of crop combination?

Solution: Weaver's method relies on standard deviation to compute variance against theoretical models, whereas Bhatia uses the sum of absolute deviations of crop percentages.

Question 4 of 10
Which agricultural region is characterized by intensive cultivation of fruits, vegetables, and flowers specifically for nearby metropolitan markets?

Solution: Truck farming and market gardening are specialized horticultural activities designed for rapid transport and sale in nearby urban consumer markets.

Question 5 of 10
In the context of agricultural typology, what does 'Transhumance' signify?

Solution: Transhumance is pastoral nomadism characterized by seasonal vertical migration of herdsmen and their livestock between alpine pastures and lowlands.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following crops is a classic example of Mediterranean arboriculture?

Solution: Olive trees, along with grapes and figs, are quintessential woody perennial crops of the Mediterranean climatic region.

Question 7 of 10
What is the principal limitation of shifting cultivation in modern ecological contexts?

Solution: Shifting cultivation is sustainable only under low population densities with long fallow periods; population growth shortens fallows, causing soil exhaustion and deforestation.

Question 8 of 10
Which economist or geographer expanded Von Thünen's model by introducing the effect of a navigable river acting as a transport cost modifier?

Solution: Von Thünen himself extended his isolated state model to incorporate the modifying effect of a navigable river, which reduces transport costs and distorts the circular rings into river-parallel zones.

Question 9 of 10
What does 'Net Sown Area' signify in agricultural statistics?

Solution: Net Sown Area represents the actual physical land area cultivated with crops; if sown more than once, it is counted only once in net area calculations.

Question 10 of 10
Why is mixed farming considered economically stable compared to specialized single-crop farming?

Solution: Mixed farming diversifies risk; farmers do not depend entirely on a single crop, as livestock and secondary crops provide alternative revenue streams during market fluctuations.
