Human Geography — Tribes

India: Human Geography — Comprehensive Spatial Analysis of Major Tribes

Rigorous academic framework mapping the anthropological distribution, linguistic classification, and socio-spatial characteristics of major Indian tribes for UP TGT/PGT and UGC-NET Geography.

UP PGT Geography UP TGT Social Science B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

The tribal population of India, designated constitutionally as Scheduled Tribes (Scheduled Tribes Order, 1950), represents the autochthonous inhabitants (Adivasis) of the subcontinent, constituting approximately 8.6% of India's total population as per the 2011 Census. Geographically, their spatial distribution is strongly correlated with rugged terrains, forested domains, mineral-rich plateaus, and peripheral borderlands, largely avoiding the densely populated Indo-Ganga-Brahmaputra alluvial plains. Anthropologically, Indian tribes are classified by linguistic families (Austro-Asiatic, Dravidian, Indo-Aryan, and Tibeto-Burman) and racial strains (Proto-Australoid, Mongoloid, Negrito, and Dravidian). For competitive examinations like UP PGT Geography and UGC-NET, mastery requires precise correlation between specific tribes, their geographical habitats, cultural traits, and livelihood strategies.

1. Linguistic and Racial Classification of Indian Tribes

The multi-layered ethnolinguistic stratification of tribal groups reflecting historical migrations and isolations.

  • Austro-Asiatic (Munda sub-branch): Spoken by Santhal, Munda, Ho, and Khasi; primarily concentrated in the Chota Nagpur Plateau and Meghalaya Plateau.
  • Dravidian linguistic family: Comprises Gond, Oraon, Toda, Chenchu, and Kui; dominant in the Central Indian belt and Nilgiri-Southern uplands.
  • Tibeto-Burman (Sino-Tibetan): Spoken by Naga, Mizo, Garo, Bodo, and Adi; distributed across the Himalayan and North-Eastern hill states.
  • Indo-Aryan linguistic assimilation: Groups like Bhil, Mina, and Katkari have largely adopted regional Indo-Aryan dialects.
  • Racial typology (Guha's classification): Dominated by Proto-Australoid in central/southern hills and Mongoloid in the Northeast.
Comparative Analysis: Contrast with the linguistic dominance of Indo-Aryan and Dravidian populations in the mainstream agricultural plains; tribal languages represent relict linguistic isolates protected by geographic barriers.
  • Santhal and Munda belong to the Austro-Asiatic family.
  • Gond and Toda belong to the Dravidian language group.
  • North-Eastern tribes are predominantly Tibeto-Burman.

2. Spatial Distribution and Physiographic Zoning of Major Tribes

The spatial segregation of tribal communities into distinct geographic zones driven by relief and ecological niches.

  • North and North-Eastern Zone: Encompasses the Himalayan ranges and Purvanchal hills; home to Naga, Mizo, Khasi, Jaintia, Garo, and Apa Tani.
  • Central Zone (Chota Nagpur/Vindhya/Satpura): The largest tribal concentration; hosts Bhil, Gond, Santhal, Oraon, Baiga, and Ho.
  • Southern Zone (Nilgiri and Western Ghats): Contains primitive, food-gathering groups like Toda, Irula, Paniya, Kadar, and Chenchu.
  • Western Zone (Aravalli and Gujarat Plains): Dominated numerically by the Bhils (India's largest tribe) and Minas.
  • Island Zone (Andaman and Nicobar): Houses vulnerable, isolated Negrito and Mongoloid tribes like Sentinelese, Jarawa, Onge, and Great Andamanese.
Comparative Analysis: Compare the high density of tribal populations in the Central Indian Mineral Belt (Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Odisha) with the sparse, highly specialized tribal enclaves of the Andaman Islands.
  • Central India holds the highest absolute tribal population.
  • Madhya Pradesh ranks first in total Scheduled Tribe population in absolute terms.
  • Mizoram and Lakshadweep record the highest percentage of tribal population to total state population.

3. Socio-Economic Typology and Livelihood Strategies

Adaptation of tribal groups to ecological constraints through diverse economic activities ranging from foraging to pastoralism.

  • Food Gatherers and Hunters: Primitive groups like Chenchu (Nallamala hills), Birhor (Jharkhand), and Cholanaikayan (Kerala).
  • Shifting Cultivators: Practiced under local names like Jhum (Northeast), Bewar/Dahia (Madhya Pradesh), and Podu (Odisha/Andhra Pradesh).
  • Pastoral Nomads: Transhumant pastoralism practiced by Toda (buffalo herding in Nilgiris), Gujjar, and Bakarwal (Himalayas).
  • Settled Agriculturists: Major groups like Gond, Santhal, Bhil, and Oraon engaged in terrace and plain agriculture.
  • Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs): 75 officially designated groups identified by stagnant population, pre-agricultural technology, and extremely low literacy.
Comparative Analysis: Contrast the ecologically destructive aspects of unmanaged Jhum cultivation with the sustainable forest-dependent economy of forest-dwelling PVTGs.
  • 75 groups are officially categorized as PVTGs by the Ministry of Home Affairs/Tribal Affairs.
  • Jhum cultivation is the primary driver of soil erosion in the North-Eastern hill tracts.

Spatial Patterns & Regional Distribution

🌐 Global Geographic Belts & Spatial Variation

Indigenous and tribal populations globally occupy marginal lands, tropical rainforests (Amazon, Congo basin), high-altitude plateaus (Andes, Tibetan plateau), and arid margins (Kalahari, Australian outback), representing relict distributions pushed away from fertile river basins by state-level agrarian societies.

🇮🇳 Indian Subcontinent Context & Regional Corridors

Concentrated heavily along the 'Central Tribal Belt' spanning Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Jharkhand, and Maharashtra, as well as the international borderlands of the North-East (Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Mizoram, Meghalaya) and the forested ridges of the Western and Eastern Ghats.

📐 Key Geographic Formulas, Scales & Classifications
Scheduled Tribe Concentration Index (STCI)
STCI = (Tribal Population in District / Total District Population) / (Total State Tribal Population / Total State Population) * 100
Parameters: Measures the relative concentration of tribal population in a micro-region compared to the macro-regional average.
Exam Focus: Frequently utilized in UGC-NET regional planning questions to identify tribal core regions versus peripheral dispersion zones.
Areal Density of Primitive Tribal Groups (AD-PTG)
AD-PTG = Total PVTG Population / Total Forested Geographic Area (sq. km)
Parameters: Quantifies the ecological pressure and habitat saturation of vulnerable foraging tribes within protected forest ecosystems.
Exam Focus: Important for PGT questions relating to environmental geography, carrying capacity, and wildlife sanctuary management.

Mnemonics, Memory Shortcuts & Exam Traps

💡 Major Central Indian Tribes Mnemonic
BSG-O (Bhil, Santhal, Gond, Oraon)

Stands for the four most populous major tribes of the Central Indian belt: Bhil, Santhal, Gond, and Oraon.

⚠️ Exam Trap: Examiners often trick students by misidentifying Bhils as the largest tribe in terms of percentage (Mizoram has highest percentage, but MP has highest absolute numbers).
💡 Andaman Primitive Tribes Mnemonic
JOS-GA (Jarawa, Onge, Sentinelese, Great Andamanese)

Covers the four major indigenous Negrito/Mongoloid tribes inhabiting the Andaman Islands.

⚠️ Exam Trap: Do not confuse Andaman tribes with Nicobarese (who are Mongoloid, horticulturalists, and not classified as PVTGs in the same extreme isolation category as Sentinelese).

📚 High-Yield Geographic Terminology & Definitions

Scheduled Tribes (ST) Constitutional Provision

Indigenous communities in India notified under Article 342 of the Constitution, characterized by primitive traits, distinct culture, shyness of contact, and economic backwardness.

PVTGs (Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups) High-Yield Welfare

A sub-classification of tribal groups identified by declining or stagnant population, pre-agricultural level of technology, and extremely low literacy; formerly known as Primitive Tribal Groups.

Bonda Tribe Regional Ethnography

A particularly vulnerable tribal group of Odisha living in the Malkangiri district, known for their isolated hilltop settlements and adherence to a traditional matrilineal social structure.

Todas South India Tribes

A pastoral tribal community inhabiting the Nilgiri Hills of Southern India, famous for their unique barrel-shaped huts and ritualistic buffalo-herding economy.

Gond Demographic Giants

One of the largest central Indian tribal groups, distributed across Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, and Maharashtra, historically renowned for their kingdoms and shifting cultivation practices (Dahia/Penda).

Santhal Tribal Uprisings

The largest tribal community in Jharkhand and West Bengal, belonging to the Proto-Australoid racial stock and known for their historical participation in the Santhal Hul rebellion of 1855.

Bhil Western India

A major Indo-Aryan speaking tribal group inhabiting the Western Satpura and Aravalli ranges across Rajasthan, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, and Maharashtra.

Jarawa Island Tribes

An indigenous Negrito tribe inhabiting the South and Middle Andaman Islands, who maintained strict isolation until recent decades.

Sentinelese Isolated Groups

An uncontacted Negrito hunter-gatherer tribe living on North Sentinel Island in the Andaman archipelago, protected by strict government exclusion zones.

Jhum Cultivation Agricultural Economy

A local term for slash-and-burn or shifting agriculture widely practiced by the hill tribes of North-East India, leading to forest clearing and short-term soil fertility use.

Gaddi Transhumance

A pastoralist community residing primarily in the western Himalayas of Himachal Pradesh (Bharmour region), practicing transhumance between the high alpine meadows and the lower valleys.

Bakarwal Himalayan Nomads

A nomadic pastoralist tribe of Jammu and Kashmir, primarily engaged in goat and sheep rearing, migrating seasonally with their herds across the Pir Panjal range.

Chenchu Southern PVTGs

A designated Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group living in the Nallamala forests of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, traditionally relying entirely on hunting and gathering forest produce.

🧭 Prescribed Scholars, Laws & Spatial Models

B.S. Guha's Racial Classification of India (1931 (Census of India))

Classified the population of India into 6 major racial groups and 9 sub-types, establishing the foundation for linking indigenous Indian tribes to Proto-Australoid, Negrito, and Mongoloid physical stocks.

Verrier Elwin's Tribal Policy (National Park Policy) (1940s - 1950s)

Proposed that tribes should be protected from rapid, disruptive mainstream assimilation by creating a protective 'National Park' environment, heavily influencing early post-independence tribal welfare policy.

Jawaharlal Nehru's Tribal Panchsheel (1950s)

A five-point framework for tribal development emphasizing development along lines of their own genius, respect for tribal land rights, training tribal people in administration, avoiding over-administration, and judging results by quality of human character.

Ghurye's Assimilation vs Isolation Thesis (1943 (The Aborigines - So-Called and Their Future))

Argued that Indian tribes are essentially 'backward Hindus' and favored gradual acculturation and integration into mainstream Indian society rather than anthropological isolation.

Question 1 of 18
Which of the following is the largest tribal group in India in terms of total population?

Rationale: According to Census data, the Bhils constitute the largest tribal group in India, followed by Gonds and Santhals.

Question 2 of 18
The Toda tribe, known for their pastoral economy and barrel-shaped houses, resides primarily in which of the following mountain ranges?

Rationale: The Todas are indigenous to the Nilgiri plateau of Tamil Nadu in Southern India.

Question 3 of 18
Jhum cultivation, a form of shifting agriculture, is most extensively practiced by tribal communities in which region of India?

Rationale: Jhum cultivation is deeply rooted in the socio-agricultural practices of the hill tribes of North-East India.

Question 4 of 18
Who formulated the 'Tribal Panchsheel' for the development and integration of tribal communities in India?

Rationale: Jawaharlal Nehru formulated the Tribal Panchsheel in consultation with anthropologists like Verrier Elwin.

Question 5 of 18
The Sentinelese tribe, known for total isolation and resistance to external contact, inhabits which island group?

Rationale: The Sentinelese inhabit North Sentinel Island within the Andaman and Nicobar archipelago.

Question 6 of 18
B.S. Guha classified the Indian population, including indigenous tribes, into how many major racial categories?

Rationale: B.S. Guha's classic classification divided the Indian population into 6 main racial groups.

Question 7 of 18
Which article of the Indian Constitution empowers the President to specify the tribes or tribal communities to be deemed as Scheduled Tribes?

Rationale: Article 342 grants the President the power to notify Scheduled Tribes in consultation with the Governor of the respective state.

Question 8 of 18
The Gaddi tribe, which practices transhumance between the high Himalayan pastures and lower valleys, is primarily found in:

Rationale: Gaddis are pastoralists mainly residing in the Chamba district (Bharmour region) of Himachal Pradesh.

Question 9 of 18
Which of the following tribes belongs to the Union Territory of Lakshadweep?

Rationale: The native inhabitants of the Lakshadweep islands, such as those on Aminidivi, Kavaratti, and Minicoy, are ethnically and culturally distinct, often classified under Scheduled Tribes.

Question 10 of 18
The Santhal Hul (rebellion) took place in which historical year against British oppression and zamindari exploitation?

Rationale: The Santhal rebellion (Hul) occurred in 1855 under the leadership of Sidhu and Kanhu Murmu.

Question 11 of 18
Which anthropologist argued that Indian tribes should be viewed as 'backward Hindus' rather than isolated entities?

Rationale: G.S. Ghurye advocated for assimilation, viewing tribes as Hindu society members who failed to attain full integration.

Question 12 of 18
The Chenchu tribe, noted for food gathering and hunting, is chiefly located in the forests of:

Rationale: Chenchus are a prominent PVTG residing in the Nallamala hill forests.

Question 13 of 18
Which state in India has the highest absolute population of Scheduled Tribes according to Census data?

Rationale: Madhya Pradesh records the highest absolute population of Scheduled Tribes in India.

Question 14 of 18
The Baiga tribe of central India is particularly known for practicing which type of traditional shifting cultivation?

Rationale: Bewar is the traditional local term for shifting cultivation practiced by the Baiga tribe in Madhya Pradesh.

Question 15 of 18
Which state has the highest percentage of Scheduled Tribe population relative to its total state population?

Rationale: Mizoram records the highest percentage of Scheduled Tribe population (over 94%) in India.

Question 16 of 18
The Onges and Great Andamanese are indigenous tribal groups belonging to which racial stock?

Rationale: The indigenous tribes of the Andaman Islands, including Onges, Jarawas, and Sentinelese, belong to the Negrito racial stock.

Question 17 of 18
Which Indian state has zero notified Scheduled Tribes population according to the Constitution and Census?

Rationale: Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Chandigarh, and Puducherry have no notified Scheduled Tribe population.

Question 18 of 18
The Warli tribe, famous for their unique ritualistic mud-wall paintings, lives primarily near the borders of which states?

Rationale: The Warlis are an indigenous tribe inhabiting the mountainous and coastal areas of Maharashtra and Gujarat border.

UP TGT Social Science / Geography

Official Benchmark Explanation: Bhumij is an Austro-Asiatic speaking tribal community concentrated in the Chota Nagpur region, primarily Jharkhand and West Bengal.

UGC-NET Geography
Who among the following propounded the theory of racial classification of Indian tribes identifying the Proto-Australoid element as fundamental to central Indian indigenous groups?

Official Benchmark Explanation: B.S. Guha detailed the Proto-Australoid physical type as the foundational strain among many peninsular Indian tribes.

UP PGT Geography
Shifting cultivation is known as 'Bewar' among which of the following tribes?

Official Benchmark Explanation: The Baiga tribe of Madhya Pradesh practices shifting agriculture locally termed as Bewar.

UGC-NET Geography

Official Benchmark Explanation: Bakarwals are transhumant pastoralists migrating across the Pir Panjal mountain ranges in Jammu and Kashmir.
