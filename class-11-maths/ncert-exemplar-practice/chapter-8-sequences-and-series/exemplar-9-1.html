Exercise 9.1

Multiple Choice Questions

Q1
A line cutting off intercept – 3 from the y-axis and the tangent at angle to the x-axis is 3/5, its equation is
Given, intercept c = – 3 and slope m = tan(θ) = 3/5. The equation of the line is y = mx + c, which is y = (3/5)x - 3. This simplifies to 5y = 3x - 15, or 5y - 3x + 15 = 0.
$5y – 3x + 15 = 0$
Q2
Slope of a line which cuts off intercepts of equal lengths on the axes is
The intercept form of a line is x/a + y/b = 1. If intercepts are of equal length, a = b (or a = -b). If a = b, x + y = a, slope is -1. If a = -b, x - y = a, slope is 1. Usually, 'equal lengths' implies the numerical value, but the most common interpretation leading to one of the options is that the intercepts themselves are equal or opposite. If the intercepts are non-zero and equal, say 'k', the line passes through (k, 0) and (0, k). The slope is (k-0)/(0-k) = -1.
$-1$
Q3
The equation of the straight line passing through the point (3, 2) and perpendicular to the line y = x is
The slope of the line y = x is 1. The slope of a line perpendicular to it is -1/1 = -1. The equation of the required line passing through (3, 2) is y - 2 = -1(x - 3), which simplifies to y - 2 = -x + 3, or x + y = 5.
$x + y = 5$
Q4
The equation of the line passing through the point (1, 2) and perpendicular to the line x + y + 7 = 0 is
The slope of the line x + y + 7 = 0 is -1. The slope of the perpendicular line is -1/(-1) = 1. The equation of the line passing through (1, 2) with slope 1 is y - 2 = 1(x - 1), which simplifies to y - 2 = x - 1, or y - x - 1 = 0.
$y – x – 1 = 0$
Q5
The distance of the point P (1, –3) from the line 2y – 3x = 4 is
The line is 3x - 2y + 4 = 0. The distance from (1, -3) is |3(1) - 2(-3) + 4| / √(3² + (-2)²) = |3 + 6 + 4| / √(9 + 4) = 13 / √13 = √13. None of the options are correct. The correct distance is √13.
$None of these$
Q6
The coordinates of the foot of the perpendicular from the point (2, 3) on the line x + y – 11 = 0 are
Let the foot of the perpendicular be (h, k). It lies on the line, so h + k - 11 = 0. The slope of the given line is -1. The slope of the perpendicular line joining (2, 3) and (h, k) is (k-3)/(h-2). So, (k-3)/(h-2) = 1, which means k - 3 = h - 2, or h - k = -1. Solving h + k = 11 and h - k = -1, we get 2h = 10, so h = 5. Then k = 6. The coordinates are (5, 6).
$(5, 6)$
Q7
The intercept cut off by a line from y-axis is twice than that from x-axis, and the line passes through the point (1, 2). The equation of the line is
Let the x-intercept be 'a'. Then the y-intercept is '2a'. The equation is x/a + y/(2a) = 1. It passes through (1, 2), so 1/a + 2/(2a) = 1, which gives 1/a + 1/a = 1, so 2/a = 1, and a = 2. The equation is x/2 + y/4 = 1. Multiplying by 4 gives 2x + y = 4.
$2x + y = 4$
Q8
A line passes through the point P (1, 2) such that its intercept between the axes is bisected at P. The equation of the line is
Let the intercepts be (a, 0) and (0, b). P(1, 2) is the midpoint. So, (a+0)/2 = 1 and (0+b)/2 = 2. This gives a = 2 and b = 4. The equation of the line in intercept form is x/a + y/b = 1, so x/2 + y/4 = 1. This simplifies to 2x + y = 4.
$2x + y = 4$
Q9
The equation of the line passing through the point (2, 3) with slope 2 is
Using the point-slope form y - y1 = m(x - x1), we have y - 3 = 2(x - 2). This simplifies to y - 3 = 2x - 4, or 2x - y - 1 = 0.
$2x - y - 1 = 0$
Q10
The distance between the lines y = mx + c1 and y = mx + c2 is
The lines are mx - y + c1 = 0 and mx - y + c2 = 0. The distance between two parallel lines Ax + By + C1 = 0 and Ax + By + C2 = 0 is |C1 - C2| / √(A² + B²). Here A = m, B = -1. So the distance is |c1 - c2| / √(m² + (-1)²) = |c1 - c2| / √(1 + m²).
$|c1 - c2| / √(1 + m²)$
Q11
The coordinates of the foot of perpendiculars from the point (2, 3) on the line y = 3x + 4 is given by
The line is 3x - y + 4 = 0. Let the foot be (h, k). The slope of the given line is 3. The slope of the perpendicular is -1/3. So, (k-3)/(h-2) = -1/3 => 3k - 9 = -h + 2 => h + 3k = 11. Also, (h, k) lies on the line, so 3h - k + 4 = 0 => k = 3h + 4. Substituting this into the first equation: h + 3(3h + 4) = 11 => h + 9h + 12 = 11 => 10h = -1 => h = -1/10. Then k = 3(-1/10) + 4 = -3/10 + 40/10 = 37/10. The point is (-1/10, 37/10).
$(-1/10, 37/10)$
Q12
If the coordinates of the middle point of the portion of a line intercepted between the coordinate axes is (3, 2), then the equation of the line will be
Let the intercepts be (a, 0) and (0, b). The midpoint is (a/2, b/2) = (3, 2). So a = 6 and b = 4. The equation is x/6 + y/4 = 1. Multiplying by 12 (LCM of 6 and 4) gives 2x + 3y = 12.
$2x + 3y = 12$
Q13
Equation of the line passing through (1, 2) and parallel to the line y = 3x – 1 is
The slope of the given line is 3. A parallel line will have the same slope. Using the point-slope form with point (1, 2) and slope 3, we get y - 2 = 3(x - 1).
$y – 2 = 3(x – 1)$
Q14
Equations of diagonals of the square formed by the lines x = 0, y = 0, x = 1 and y = 1 are
The vertices of the square are (0, 0), (1, 0), (0, 1), and (1, 1). The first diagonal connects (0, 0) and (1, 1), its equation is y = x. The second diagonal connects (1, 0) and (0, 1), its equation is (y-0) = (1-0)/(0-1) * (x-1), which is y = -1(x-1), or x + y = 1.
$y = x, y + x = 1$
Q15
For specifying a straight line, how many geometrical parameters should be known?
To uniquely define a straight line, two independent parameters are required. For example: two points, or a point and a slope, or a slope and an intercept.
$2$
Q16
The point (4, 1) undergoes two successive transformations: (i) Reflection about the line y = x. (ii) Translation through a distance 2 units along the positive x-axis. Then the final coordinates of the point are
(i) Reflection of (4, 1) about y = x gives (1, 4). (ii) Translation of (1, 4) through 2 units along the positive x-axis means adding 2 to the x-coordinate. The new point is (1+2, 4) = (3, 4).
$(3, 4)$
Q17
A point equidistant from the lines 4x + 3y + 10 = 0, 5x – 12y + 26 = 0 and 7x + 24y – 50 = 0 is
The locus of points equidistant from two lines is the angle bisector. We need to find a point that lies on the bisectors of all three pairs of lines. A simpler way is to test the given points. For (0, 0): d1 = |10|/√(16+9) = 10/5 = 2. d2 = |26|/√(25+144) = 26/13 = 2. d3 = |-50|/√(49+576) = 50/25 = 2. Since the distances are equal, (0, 0) is the point.
$(0, 0)$
Q18
A line passes through (2, 2) and is perpendicular to the line 3x + y = 3. Its y-intercept is
The slope of 3x + y = 3 is -3. The slope of the perpendicular line is 1/3. The equation of the line is y - 2 = (1/3)(x - 2). To find the y-intercept, set x = 0: y - 2 = (1/3)(-2) => y = 2 - 2/3 = 4/3.
$4/3$
Q19
The ratio in which the line 3x + 4y + 2 = 0 divides the distance between the lines 3x + 4y + 5 = 0 and 3x + 4y – 5 = 0 is
Let the lines be L1: 3x+4y+5=0, L2: 3x+4y-5=0, and L: 3x+4y+2=0. The distance between L1 and L is |5-2|/√(9+16) = 3/5. The distance between L and L2 is |2-(-5)|/√(9+16) = 7/5. The ratio of the distances is (3/5) : (7/5), which is 3:7.
$3:7$
Q20
One vertex of the equilateral triangle with centroid at the origin and one side as x + y – 2 = 0 is
Let the vertex be (h, k). The centroid is the origin (0, 0). The centroid lies between the vertex and the opposite side. Thus, the vertex and the centroid must be on the same side of the line x + y - 2 = 0. For the centroid (0,0), the value of the expression is 0 + 0 - 2 = -2 (negative). For the vertex (-2, -2), the value is -2 - 2 - 2 = -6 (negative). Since both are negative, they are on the same side. The other possible vertex (2,2) gives 2+2-2=2 (positive), which is on the opposite side. Therefore, (-2, -2) is the correct vertex.
$(-2, -2)$
