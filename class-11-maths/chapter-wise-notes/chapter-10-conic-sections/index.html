1. What are Conic Sections?

Conic sections are curves obtained by intersecting a right circular cone with a plane.

Definition by Eccentricity (\(e\)): The locus of a point which moves such that the ratio of its distance from a fixed point (Focus) to its perpendicular distance from a fixed line (Directrix) is constant.

2. Circle

A circle is the set of all points in a plane equidistant from a fixed point (center).

Equation: \( (x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2 \)

\( x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0 \)
Center: \( (-g, -f) \)
Radius: \( \sqrt{g^2 + f^2 - c} \)

3. Parabola (e=1)

Locus of a point equidistant from focus and directrix.

Equation Axis Focus Directrix
\( y^2 = 4ax \) X-axis (Right) \( (a, 0) \) \( x = -a \)
\( y^2 = -4ax \) X-axis (Left) \( (-a, 0) \) \( x = a \)
\( x^2 = 4ay \) Y-axis (Up) \( (0, a) \) \( y = -a \)
\( x^2 = -4ay \) Y-axis (Down) \( (0, -a) \) \( y = a \)

?? Latus Rectum Extremities

Conic Extremities of Latus Rectum
Parabola (\(y^2 = 4ax\)) \( (a, \pm 2a) \)
Ellipse \( (\pm ae, \pm \frac{b^2}{a}) \)
Hyperbola \( (\pm ae, \pm \frac{b^2}{a}) \)

?? Condition of Tangency

A line \( y = mx + c \) touches the conic if:

  • Parabola (\(y^2 = 4ax\)): \( c = \frac{a}{m} \)
  • Ellipse: \( c = \pm \sqrt{a^2m^2 + b^2} \)
  • Hyperbola: \( c = \pm \sqrt{a^2m^2 - b^2} \)

So, equations of tangents to ellipse/hyperbola are \( y = mx \pm \sqrt{a^2m^2 \pm b^2} \).

?? Director Circle (Board Favourite)

Ellipse:

\[ \text{Director Circle: } x^2 + y^2 = a^2 + b^2 \]

Hyperbola:

\[ \text{Director Circle: } x^2 + y^2 = a^2 - b^2 \]

?? Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing a and b in ellipse
  • Using wrong sign in parabola equations
  • Forgetting square root while finding c
  • Writing wrong latus rectum formula
  • Mixing ellipse & hyperbola relations
