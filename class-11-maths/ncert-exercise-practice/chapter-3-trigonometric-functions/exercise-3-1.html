Find the radian measure corresponding to the following degree measures:
(i) $25^\circ$
(ii) $-47^\circ 30'$
(i) We know that $180^\circ = \pi$ radian.
$25^\circ = 25 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{5\pi}{36}$ radian.
(ii) $-47^\circ 30' = -(47 + \frac{30}{60})^\circ = -47\frac{1}{2}^\circ = -\frac{95}{2}^\circ$.
Radian measure = $-\frac{95}{2} \times \frac{\pi}{180} = -\frac{19\pi}{72}$ radian.
(i) $\frac{5\pi}{36}$, (ii) $-\frac{19\pi}{72}$

Find the degree measure corresponding to the following radian measures (Use $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$):
(i) $\frac{11}{16}$
(ii) $-4$
(i) $\frac{11}{16}$ rad = $\frac{11}{16} \times \frac{180}{\pi}^\circ = \frac{11}{16} \times \frac{180}{22/7}^\circ = \frac{11 \times 180 \times 7}{16 \times 22}^\circ = \frac{315}{8}^\circ$.
$= 39^\circ + \frac{3}{8}^\circ = 39^\circ + (\frac{3}{8} \times 60)' = 39^\circ + 22.5' = 39^\circ 22' 30''$.
(ii) $-4$ rad = $-4 \times \frac{180}{\pi}^\circ = -4 \times \frac{180}{22/7}^\circ = -\frac{2520}{11}^\circ$.
$= -229^\circ + \frac{1}{11}^\circ = -229^\circ + (\frac{1}{11} \times 60)' = -229^\circ + 5' + \frac{5}{11}'$.
$= -229^\circ 5' + (\frac{5}{11} \times 60)'' \approx -229^\circ 5' 27''$.
(i) $39^\circ 22' 30''$, (ii) $-229^\circ 5' 27''$

A wheel makes 360 revolutions in one minute. Through how many radians does it turn in one second?
Number of revolutions in 1 minute = 360.
Number of revolutions in 1 second = $\frac{360}{60} = 6$.
Angle turned in 1 revolution = $2\pi$ radian.
Angle turned in 6 revolutions = $6 \times 2\pi = 12\pi$ radian.
$12\pi$ radians

Find the degree measure of the angle subtended at the centre of a circle of radius 100 cm by an arc of length 22 cm (Use $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$).
We use $\theta = \frac{l}{r}$. Here $l = 22$ cm, $r = 100$ cm.
$\theta = \frac{22}{100}$ radian.
In degrees: $\frac{22}{100} \times \frac{180}{\pi} = \frac{22}{100} \times \frac{180}{22/7} = \frac{22}{100} \times \frac{180 \times 7}{22} = \frac{126}{10}^\circ = 12.6^\circ$.
$12.6^\circ = 12^\circ + 0.6 \times 60' = 12^\circ 36'$.
$12^\circ 36'$

00:00
In a circle of diameter 40 cm, the length of a chord is 20 cm. Find the length of the minor arc of the chord.
Diameter = 40 cm $\Rightarrow$ Radius $r = 20$ cm.
Chord length = 20 cm.
Since radius = chord = 20 cm, the triangle formed by the radii and the chord is equilateral.
Angle subtended at centre $\theta = 60^\circ = \frac{\pi}{3}$ radian.
Arc length $l = r\theta = 20 \times \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{20\pi}{3}$ cm.
$\frac{20\pi}{3}$ cm

If in two circles, arcs of the same length subtend angles $60^\circ$ and $75^\circ$ at the centre, find the ratio of their radii.
Let radii be $r_1$ and $r_2$. Let arc length be $l$.
$\theta_1 = 60^\circ = \frac{\pi}{3}$ rad. $\theta_2 = 75^\circ = \frac{5\pi}{12}$ rad.
$l = r_1 \theta_1 = r_2 \theta_2 \Rightarrow r_1 \frac{\pi}{3} = r_2 \frac{5\pi}{12}$.
$\frac{r_1}{r_2} = \frac{5\pi/12}{\pi/3} = \frac{5}{12} \times \frac{3}{1} = \frac{5}{4}$.
