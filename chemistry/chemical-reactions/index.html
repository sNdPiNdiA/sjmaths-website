Chemical Reactions and Stoichiometry रासायनिक अभिक्रियाएँ और रससमीकरणमिति (Chemical Reactions and Stoichiometry)

Chemical reactions involve the breaking of existing chemical bonds and the formation of new bonds to transform reactants into products. Stoichiometry applies the law of conservation of mass to calculate quantitative relationships between reactants and products. रासायनिक अभिक्रियाओं में विद्यमान रासायनिक बंधों का टूटना और नए बंधों का निर्माण शामिल होता है, जिससे अभिकारक (reactants) उत्पादों (products) में परिवर्तित हो जाते हैं। रससमीकरणमिति (Stoichiometry) अभिकारकों और उत्पादों के बीच मात्रात्मक संबंधों की गणना करने के लिए द्रव्यमान संरक्षण के नियम (law of conservation of mass) का उपयोग करती है।

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Stoichiometric Relationships and Limiting Reagents रससमीकरणमितीय संबंध और सीमांत अभिकर्मक (Stoichiometric Relationships and Limiting Reagents)

  • The limiting reagent is completely consumed in a reaction and dictates the maximum amount of product formed.
  • Stoichiometric coefficients in a balanced chemical equation represent molar ratios, not mass ratios.
  • Calculations involving gaseous reactants must account for Gay-Lussac's Law of combining volumes at constant temperature and pressure.
  • सीमांत अभिकर्मक (limiting reagent) अभिक्रिया में पूरी तरह से उपभोग हो जाता है और उत्पाद की अधिकतम मात्रा को निर्धारित करता है।
  • एक संतुलित रासायनिक समीकरण में रससमीकरणमितीय गुणांक (stoichiometric coefficients) मोलर अनुपात (molar ratios) को दर्शाते हैं, न कि द्रव्यमान अनुपात को।
  • गैसीय अभिकारकों से संबंधित गणनाओं में स्थिर ताप और दाब पर गे-लुसाक के गैसीय आयतन के नियम (Gay-Lussac's Law of combining volumes) का ध्यान रखना आवश्यक है।

Concentration Units and Solution Stoichiometry सांद्रता इकाइयाँ और विलयन रससमीकरणमिति (Concentration Units and Solution Stoichiometry)

  • Molarity (M) is temperature-dependent because volume changes with thermal expansion or contraction.
  • Molality (m) and mole fraction (x) are temperature-independent as they are based on mass.
  • Parts per million (ppm) is utilized for trace concentrations, defined as milligrams of solute per kilogram of solution.
  • मोलरता (Molarity) ताप पर निर्भर करती है क्योंकि तापीय प्रसार या संकुचन के साथ आयतन बदल जाता है।
  • मोललता (Molality) और मोल अंश (mole fraction) ताप से स्वतंत्र होते हैं क्योंकि ये द्रव्यमान पर आधारित होते हैं।
  • पार्ट्स प्रति मिलियन (ppm) का उपयोग सूक्ष्म सांद्रता के लिए किया जाता है, जिसे प्रति किलोग्राम विलयन में विलेय के मिलीग्राम के रूप में परिभाषित किया जाता है।

Types of Chemical Reactions रासायनिक अभिक्रियाओं के प्रकार (Types of Chemical Reactions)

  • Redox reactions involve the simultaneous transfer of electrons, characterized by changes in oxidation numbers.
  • Disproportionation reactions involve a single element undergoing both oxidation and reduction concurrently.
  • Acid-base neutralization reactions follow the principle of equivalence where equivalents of acid equal equivalents of base.
  • रेडॉक्स अभिक्रियाओं (Redox reactions) में इलेक्ट्रॉनों का एक साथ स्थानांतरण होता है, जो ऑक्सीकरण संख्या में परिवर्तन द्वारा पहचाने जाते हैं।
  • विषमानुपातन अभिक्रियाओं (Disproportionation reactions) में एक ही तत्व का एक साथ ऑक्सीकरण और अपचयन होता है।
  • अम्ल-क्षार उदासीनीकरण (Acid-base neutralization) अभिक्रियाएं तुल्यांक के सिद्धांत का पालन करती हैं जहाँ अम्ल के तुल्यांक क्षार के तुल्यांकों के बराबर होते हैं।

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Combustion of hydrocarbons yields carbon dioxide and water exclusively.हाइड्रोकार्बन का दहन केवल कार्बन डाइऑक्साइड और जल उत्पन्न करता है।

Observation: Incomplete combustion yields carbon monoxide (CO) or elemental carbon (soot). — Insufficient supply of oxygen prevents complete oxidation of the carbon atoms. अवलोकन: अपूर्ण दहन से कार्बन मोनोऑक्साइड (CO) या कालिख (कार्बन) प्राप्त होती है। कारण: ऑक्सीजन की अपर्याप्त आपूर्ति कार्बन परमाणुओं के पूर्ण ऑक्सीकरण को रोकती है।

Exception Strong acids completely dissociate in aqueous solution.प्रबल अम्ल जलीय विलयन में पूर्णतः वियोजित हो जाते हैं।

Observation: Phosphoric acid (H3PO4) and sulfuric acid in their second dissociation steps are weak. — Electrostatic attraction between sulfate/phosphate ions and hydronium ions hinders complete secondary deprotonation. अवलोकन: फॉस्फोरिक अम्ल (H3PO4) और सल्फ्यूरिक अम्ल के द्वितीय वियोजन चरण दुर्बल होते हैं। कारण: सल्फेट/फॉस्फेट आयनों और हाइड्रोनियम आयनों के बीच स्थिरवैद्युत आकर्षण द्वितीयक विप्रोटोनन (deprotonation) में बाधा डालता है।

Exception Stoichiometric coefficients are always whole numbers.रससमीकरणमितीय गुणांक हमेशा पूर्णांक होते हैं।

Observation: Fractional coefficients are permitted in thermochemical equations representing enthalpy of formation for one mole of a substance. — To maintain the definition of standard enthalpy of formation per single mole of product. अवलोकन: ऊष्मारसायन समीकरणों में भिन्नात्मक गुणांक स्वीकार्य हैं जो एक मोल पदार्थ के निर्माण की एन्थैल्पी को दर्शाते हैं। कारण: उत्पाद के प्रति मोल मानक निर्माण एन्थैल्पी की परिभाषा बनाए रखने के लिए।

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • One mole of any ideal gas occupies 22.414 liters at Standard Temperature and Pressure (STP, 0 °C and 1 atm).
  • Avogadro's number equals 6.022 × 1023 entities per mole.
  • The sum of mole fractions of all components in a solution is always equal to unity (∑ xi = 1).
  • Equivalent weight of an oxidizing agent equals its molar mass divided by the total change in oxidation number per molecule.
  • Law of Conservation of Mass was formulated by Antoine Lavoisier in 1789.
  • Law of Definite Proportions states that a given chemical compound always contains its component elements in fixed ratio by mass.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing volume of solution with volume of solvent when calculating molarity.
  • Failing to balance redox reactions in acidic or basic medium before applying stoichiometric calculations.
  • Assuming volume is additive when mixing two different aqueous solutions.
  • Using mass percentage directly as molality without factoring in molar mass and density.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Reactions and Stoichiometry

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Conservation Laws

Mass and atoms are conserved in all chemical reactions, forming the mathematical foundation for balancing chemical equations.

Limiting Reactants

The reactant present in the lesser stoichiometric amount dictates product yield and must be used for all theoretical calculations.

Solution Concentration

Quantitative concentration terms connect mass and volume measurements to stoichiometric molar amounts in liquid phases.

Redox Stoichiometry

Electron transfer processes require the conservation of both mass and charge, managed via ion-electron or oxidation number methods.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Mole (mol)
Amount of substance containing as many elementary entities as there are atoms in 0.012 kg of carbon-12.
Molarity (M)
Number of moles of solute dissolved in one liter of solution.
Molality (m)
Number of moles of solute dissolved in one kilogram of solvent.
Equivalent Weight (E)
Mass of one equivalent of a substance, reacting with or supplying one mole of hydrogen ions or electrons.
Mole Fraction (x)
Ratio of the number of moles of a given component to the total number of moles in the mixture.
Limiting Reagent (LR)
Reactant that is fully consumed and limits the extent of the reaction.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Standard temperature and pressure: T = 273.15 K, P = 1 bar (or 1 atm depending on convention).
  • Number of equivalents = Moles × n-factor.
  • Density = Mass / Volume.
  • Mass percentage = (Mass of solute / Mass of solution) × 100.
  • Parts per billion (ppb) = (Mass of solute / Mass of solution) × 109.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Stoichiometry relates masses, moles, and volumes in chemical reactions using balanced equations.
  • Concentration expressions like molarity and molality quantify solution compositions.
  • Limiting reactant calculations determine maximum product yield.
  • Redox balancing requires tracking electron transfer and charge conservation.

⚖️ Key Differences

Molarity VS Molality

Molarity uses volume of solution (affected by temperature), while molality uses mass of solvent (independent of temperature).

Question 1 of 18

What is the molarity of a solution containing 40 g of NaOH in 250 mL of solution?

Explanation: Molar mass of NaOH = 40 g/mol. Moles = 40/40 = 1 mol. Volume = 0.250 L. Molarity = 1 / 0.250 = 4.0 M.

Question 2 of 18

Which concentration term is independent of temperature?

Explanation: Molality is defined using mass of solvent, which does not change with temperature variation.

Question 3 of 18

In the reaction N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3, what volume of ammonia gas is produced from 60 L of hydrogen gas at constant temperature and pressure?

Explanation: According to Gay-Lussac's law, 3 volumes of H2 give 2 volumes of NH3. Therefore, 60 L of H2 yields (2/3) × 60 = 40 L of NH3.

Question 4 of 18

What is the oxidation number of sulfur in H2SO4?

Explanation: Let oxidation state of S be x. 2(+1) + x + 4(-2) = 0 ⇒ 2 + x - 8 = 0 ⇒ x = +6.

Question 5 of 18

How many moles of atoms are present in 0.5 mole of propane (C3H8)?

Explanation: Each molecule of C3H8 contains 3 + 8 = 11 atoms. Total moles of atoms = 0.5 mol × 11 = 5.5 mol.

Question 6 of 18

What is the equivalent weight of KMnO4 when it acts as an oxidizing agent in an acidic medium?

Explanation: In acidic medium, MnO4- reduces to Mn2+ (change of 5 electrons). Equivalent weight = Molar mass / 5 = 158 / 5 = 31.6 g/eq.

Question 8 of 18

Which of the following contains the maximum number of molecules?

Explanation: Number of molecules is proportional to moles (1/Molar mass). H2 has the lowest molar mass (2 g/mol), hence highest moles and molecules per gram.

Question 9 of 18

What is the mass of 1 mole of electrons?

Explanation: Mass of 1 electron = 9.109 × 10-31 kg = 9.109 × 10-28 g. Multiply by Avogadro's number (6.022 × 1023) ⇒ 5.48 × 10-4 g.

Question 10 of 18

Calculate the molality of 20% (w/w) aqueous solution of KI (Molar mass = 166 g/mol).

Explanation: 20 g KI in 80 g water. Molality = (20 / 166) / (80 / 1000) = 0.12048 / 0.08 = 1.505 mol/kg.

Question 11 of 18

What is the n-factor of H3PO4 when it reacts with excess NaOH to form Na3PO4?

Explanation: H3PO4 replaces all three replaceable hydrogen atoms when forming Na3PO4, so its base-neutralizing capacity (n-factor) is 3.

Question 12 of 18

Which law states that equal volumes of all gases under the same conditions of temperature and pressure contain equal number of molecules?

Explanation: Avogadro's hypothesis/law states that equal volumes of gases at identical T and P contain equal numbers of molecules.

Question 13 of 18

What volume of 0.1 M HCl is required to completely neutralize 50 mL of 0.2 M NaOH solution?

Explanation: Using M1V1 = M2V2 (for monobasic acid and monoacidic base): 0.1 × V1 = 0.2 × 50 ⇒ V1 = 100 mL.

Question 14 of 18

What is the oxidation state of iron in Fe3O4?

Explanation: Fe3O4 is a mixed oxide (FeO · Fe2O3) containing Fe2+ and Fe3+ ions in a 1:2 ratio.

Question 15 of 18

What is the percentage composition of carbon in carbon dioxide (CO2)?

Explanation: Molar mass of CO2 = 44 g/mol. Mass of C = 12 g/mol. Percentage = (12 / 44) × 100 = 27.27%.

Question 16 of 18

Which reactant acts as a reducing agent in the reaction: Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu?

Explanation: Zinc (Zn) undergoes oxidation from 0 to +2, donating electrons and thus acting as the reducing agent.

Question 17 of 18

How many significant figures are present in 0.00250?

Explanation: Leading zeros are not significant, but trailing zeros after a decimal point are significant. Thus, 250 gives 3 significant figures.

Question 18 of 18

What is the empirical formula of a compound containing 40% carbon, 6.7% hydrogen, and 53.3% oxygen by mass?

Explanation: Moles of C = 40/12 = 3.33; Moles of H = 6.7/1 = 6.7; Moles of O = 53.3/16 = 3.33. Ratio C:H:O = 1:2:1 ⇒ CH2O.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the mass of anhydrous sodium carbonate required to prepare 250 mL of 0.1 M solution.

Solution: Molar mass of Na2CO3 = 106 g/mol. Mass = M × Molar mass × V(L) = 0.1 × 106 × 0.250 = 2.65 g.

Question 2 of 10
In a reaction, 4 moles of electrons are transferred to 1 mole of an oxidant. What is the equivalent weight of the oxidant if its molar mass is M?

Solution: Equivalent weight equals molar mass divided by the number of electrons gained per molecule (n-factor = 4), i.e., M / 4.

Question 3 of 10
What is the molality of an aqueous solution of glucose (C6H12O6) having a mole fraction of glucose equal to 0.1?

Solution: Mole fraction xsolute = 0.1, xsolvent = 0.9. Molality = [xsolute / (xsolvent × Msolvent)] × 1000 = [0.1 / (0.9 × 18)] × 1000 = 6.17 m.

Question 5 of 10
What is the oxidation state of chromium in K2Cr2O7?

Solution: 2(+1) + 2(x) + 7(-2) = 0 ⇒ 2 + 2x - 14 = 0 ⇒ 2x = 12 ⇒ x = +6.

Question 6 of 10
A gas mixture contains 2 moles of He and 3 moles of CH4. What is the mole fraction of methane?

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following concentration expressions varies with temperature?

Solution: Molarity is defined per unit volume of solution, which expands or contracts with temperature fluctuations.

Question 8 of 10
What volume of 0.2 M H2SO4 is completely neutralized by 20 mL of 0.5 M KOH?

Question 9 of 10
If the percentage yield of a reaction is 75% and the theoretical yield is 40 g, what is the actual yield?

Solution: Actual yield = (Percentage Yield / 100) × Theoretical Yield = (75 / 100) × 40 = 30 g.

Question 10 of 10
What is the stoichiometric coefficient of water when the redox equation: MnO4- + I- + H+ → Mn2+ + I2 + H2O is balanced?

Solution: Balancing the acidic redox equation yields 2MnO4- + 10I- + 16H+ → 2Mn2+ + 5I2 + 8H2O, giving a stoichiometric coefficient of 8 for water.
