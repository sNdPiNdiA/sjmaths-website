Inorganic Chemistry

Organometallic Chemistry

Organometallic chemistry is the branch of science dealing with compounds containing at least one direct metal-carbon bond. These species bridge classical inorganic and organic chemistry through unique metal-ligand bonding modes.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification and Nomenclature of Organometallic Compounds

  • Compounds possessing a direct sigma or pi bond between a metal atom and a carbon atom are defined as organometallics.
  • Metal carbonyls, metallocenes, and metal alkyls/aryls represent the primary structural classes of organometallic compounds.
  • Hapticity (ηn) denotes the number of contiguous carbon atoms coordinated to the metal center.
  • Bridging ligands are designated by the prefix μ with a superscript indicating the number of metal atoms bridged.

The 18-Electron Rule and Electron Counting

  • Transition metal complexes achieve electronic stability analogous to noble gases when their valence shell contains 18 electrons.
  • In the neutral atom method, neutral ligands donate an even number of electrons corresponding to their lone pair contributions.
  • Metallocenes such as ferrocene [Fe(η5-C5H5)2] satisfy the 18-electron rule completely.
  • Early transition metals often violate the 18-electron rule due to steric unsaturation or higher oxidation states, forming 16-electron or 12-electron complexes.

Metal Carbonyls and Synergistic Bonding

  • Metal-carbon monoxide bonding involves σ-donation from the CO HOMO to an empty metal d-orbital combined with π-back-donation from filled metal d-orbitals to the CO LUMO.
  • Synergistic back-bonding strengthens the metal-carbon bond while simultaneously weakening and lengthening the internal carbon-oxygen bond.
  • Increased electron density on the metal center enhances back-bonding, lowering the infrared stretching frequency (νCO).
  • Bridging carbonyls absorb at lower infrared frequencies compared to terminal carbonyl ligands due to increased metal-to-ligand electron delocalization.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
18-Electron Rule (Neutral Atom Method)
Total Electrons = Valence Electrons of Metal + Electrons donated by ligands
Conditions: Stable low-spin transition metal organometallic complexes.
Units: Count in integers equal to noble gas configuration
Synergistic Bonding Contribution (Back-bonding)
Δ(νCO) ∝ kCO ∝ π*-back-donation density
Conditions: Metal-carbonyl complexes with varying electron densities at the metal center.
Units: Stretching frequency in cm-1
Wade's Rules (Polyhedral Skeletal Electron Pairs)
PSEP = (Total valence electrons - 2n) / 2 or n + k
Conditions: Cluster compounds containing boron, transition metals, or main group elements.
Units: n = number of vertices, k = integer value indicating structural type
Effective Atomic Number (EAN) Rule
EAN = Z - OX + 2(CN)
Conditions: Applicable to mononuclear and dinuclear transition metal complexes obeying the 18-electron rule.
Units: Z = Atomic number, OX = Oxidation state, CN = Coordination number (Total electrons)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Transition metal organometallic complexes strictly follow the 18-electron rule for maximum stability.

Observation: Square planar d8 complexes (e.g., Vaska's complex, [IrCl(CO)(PPh3)2], [PtCl4]2-) are exceptionally stable with 16 electrons. — The vacant pz orbital is too high in energy to accept electrons effectively without causing severe steric crowding.

Exception Increasing negative charge on a metal carbonylates lowers the CO stretching frequency due to enhanced back-bonding.

Observation: [Mn(CO)6]+ exhibits a higher CO stretching frequency than neutral [Cr(CO)6], which is higher than [V(CO)6]-. — Higher positive charge withdraws electron density from the metal, reducing back-donation into the π* orbitals of CO.

Exception Metallocenes exhibit parallel cyclopentadienyl rings.

Observation: Titanocene dichloride [Cp2TiCl2] and similar early transition metal metallocenes possess a bent sandwich structure. — The presence of non-bonding valence electrons and orbital geometry preferences forces a bent geometry with an angle less than 180°.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Ferrocene was discovered by Pauson and Kealy, and independently by Miller, Tebboth, and Tremaine in 1951.
  • Zeise's salt, K[PtCl3(η2-C2H4)], is the oldest known organometallic complex containing an alkene ligand.
  • The 18-electron rule is obeyed primarily by low-spin complexes involving strong-field ligands like CO, CN-, and phosphines.
  • Grignard reagents (RMgX) and organolithium compounds (RLi) contain polar sigma metal-carbon bonds and serve as primary alkylating agents.
  • Fluxional molecules undergo rapid intramolecular rearrangements that average out distinct spectroscopic signals at room temperature.
  • Octahedral metal hexacarbonyls like [Cr(CO)6], [Mo(CO)6], and [W(CO)6] are diamagnetic and thermally stable.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing hapticity (η) with coordination number; hapticity specifies contiguous carbon atoms bound to the metal.
  • Forgetting to include the two electrons contributed by the metal-metal bond in dinuclear metal carbonyl complexes.
  • Assuming all transition metal complexes must strictly follow the 18-electron rule, ignoring stable 16-electron square planar systems.
  • Incorrectly assigning positive stretching frequency shifts when metal electron density increases via anionic charges.

⚡ Quick Revision: Organometallic Chemistry

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition and Scope

Organometallic compounds are defined by the presence of at least one metal-carbon covalent or coordinate bond. They encompass diverse classes including main group alkyls, transition metal carbonyls, and pi-complexes.

Electron Counting Rules

The 18-electron rule dictates electronic saturation for transition metal complexes, analogous to the octet rule in main group chemistry. Both neutral atom and ionic methods are utilized for electron counting.

Metal Carbonyls & Synergistic Bonding

Metal-CO bonding relies on concurrent σ-donation and π-back-donation. This synergistic mechanism strengthens the metal-carbon interaction while weakening the carbon-oxygen triple bond.

Structural Variations & Fluxionality

Complexes such as metal clusters follow electron counting frameworks like Wade's rules, while fluxional species exhibit rapid degenerate rearrangements detectable via NMR spectroscopy.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Hapticity (ηn)
Number of contiguous carbon atoms bonded to a metal center.
Synergistic Bonding (σ-donation + π-back-bonding)
Mutually reinforcing electronic interactions between metal and ligand.
Metallocene ([M(η5-C5H5)2])
Sandwich compound containing a transition metal between two cyclopentadienyl rings.
Fluxional Molecule (Dynamic Equilibrium)
Complex undergoing rapid intramolecular isomerization without bond cleavage.
Agostic Interaction (M···H-C)
Three-center two-electron interaction between a metal center and a C-H bond.
Vaska's Complex ([IrCl(CO)(PPh3)2])
Stable 16-electron square planar iridium(I) complex active in oxidative addition.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ferrocene has 18 electrons and a sandwich geometry.
  • Bridging CO ligands absorb at lower infrared frequencies (~1750 cm-1) than terminal CO ligands (~1950 cm-1).
  • Zeise's salt contains an ethylene ligand bonded with η2 hapticity.
  • Square planar d8 complexes typically possess 16 valence electrons.
  • Wade's rules classify cluster structures into closo, nido, and arachno based on skeletal electron pairs.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Organometallic chemistry focuses on direct metal-carbon bonds.
  • The 18-electron rule governs the stability of transition metal complexes.
  • Synergistic back-bonding explains the unique spectral properties of metal carbonyls.
  • Structural variations include sandwich compounds, metal clusters, and fluxional systems.

⚖️ Key Differences

Coordination Number VS Hapticity

Coordination number counts total ligand attachment points, whereas hapticity specifically counts contiguous carbon atoms bonded in pi-systems.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following compounds contains a direct metal-carbon bond?

Explanation: Ferrocene is a classic organometallic sandwich compound containing direct iron-carbon bonds with cyclopentadienyl rings.

Question 2 of 18

What is the hapticity (η) of a cyclopentadienyl ring when it acts as a pentahapto ligand?

Explanation: A pentahapto ligand coordinates through all five adjacent carbon atoms of the ring, denoted as η5.

Question 3 of 18

How many valence electrons does the central metal atom possess in [Cr(CO)6] according to the neutral atom method?

Explanation: Chromium contributes 6 valence electrons, and each of the six CO ligands donates 2 electrons, yielding a total of 18 electrons.

Question 4 of 18

Which of the following is a 16-electron complex?

Explanation: Vaska's complex, [IrCl(CO)(PPh3)2], is a square planar d8 iridium(I) complex possessing 16 valence electrons.

Question 5 of 18

In metal carbonyl complexes, what is the effect of increased negative charge on the metal atom on the C-O stretching frequency?

Explanation: Negative charge increases d-electron density on the metal, enhancing π-back-donation into CO π* orbitals, which weakens the C-O bond and lowers its stretching frequency.

Question 6 of 18

What is the formula for Zeise's salt?

Explanation: Zeise's salt is potassium trichloro(ethylene)platinate(II), formulated as K[PtCl3(η2-C2H4)].

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following molecules exhibits fluxional behavior at room temperature?

Explanation: Iron pentacarbonyl [Fe(CO)5] undergoes rapid intramolecular Berry pseudorotation, rendering it fluxional in solution at room temperature.

Question 8 of 18

What type of bonding is primarily responsible for the stability of metal-alkene complexes?

Explanation: The Dewar-Chatt-Duncanson model explains metal-alkene bonding through alkene-to-metal σ-donation and metal-to-alkene π-back-donation.

Question 9 of 18

According to Wade's rules, what skeletal electron pair count corresponds to a closo-borane or cluster structure with n vertices?

Explanation: Closo clusters possess (n + 1) skeletal electron pairs, corresponding to 2n + 2 skeletal electrons.

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following ligands acts exclusively as a one-electron donor in the neutral atom electron counting method?

Explanation: Chlorine atom contributes 1 electron in the neutral radical method (or acts as a 2-electron donor in the ionic method as Cl-).

Question 11 of 18

Which metal carbonyl dimer exhibits both bridging and terminal carbonyl ligands in the solid state?

Explanation: [Fe2(CO)9] contains three bridging carbonyl ligands and six terminal carbonyl ligands.

Question 12 of 18

What is the oxidation state of iron in ferrocene, [Fe(η5-C5H5)2], using the ionic model?

Explanation: In the ionic model, each cyclopentadienyl ring is treated as a monoanion (C5H5-), giving iron an oxidation state of +2.

Question 13 of 18

Which term describes an interaction where a metal center coordinates to a C-H sigma bond?

Explanation: An agostic interaction involves the coordination of a carbon-hydrogen single bond to an electron-deficient metal center via a 3-center 2-electron bond.

Question 14 of 18

What is the total electron count of [Mn2(CO)10] using the neutral atom method?

Explanation: Each manganese contributes 7 electrons, five CO ligands contribute 10 electrons, and the Mn-Mn bond contributes 1 electron, totaling 18 electrons per metal center.

Question 15 of 18

Which spectroscopic technique is most diagnostic for identifying terminal versus bridging carbonyl groups?

Explanation: Infrared spectroscopy clearly distinguishes terminal (~1900-2120 cm-1) and bridging (~1700-1850 cm-1) carbonyl stretching frequencies.

Question 16 of 18

What is the hapticity of benzene in dibenzenechromium, [Cr(η6-C6H6)2]?

Explanation: Benzene coordinates to the chromium center through all six of its carbon atoms, giving a hapticity of η6.

Question 17 of 18

Which of the following compounds violates the 18-electron rule?

Explanation: Vanadium hexacarbonyl, [V(CO)6], possesses 17 valence electrons and is a rare stable radical monomeric metal carbonyl.

Question 18 of 18

Which class of organometallic compounds is extensively used as homogeneous catalysts in hydrogenation reactions?

Explanation: Wilkinson's catalyst, [RhCl(PPh3)3], is a prominent organometallic complex widely used for homogeneous alkene hydrogenation.

Question 1 of 10
Determine the total valence electron count for [Co2(CO)8] using the neutral atom method.

Solution: Each cobalt contributes 9 electrons (18 total), eight CO ligands contribute 16 electrons, and the Co-Co bond contributes 2 electrons, summing to 36 total electrons.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following ligands exhibits both terminal and bridging modes in metal carbonyl chemistry?

Solution: Carbon monoxide (CO) frequently adopts both terminal and bridging (μ2, μ3) bonding modes in dinuclear and polynuclear metal carbonyls.

Question 3 of 10
What is the stable coordination number and electron count for Wilkinson's catalyst, [RhCl(PPh3)3]?

Solution: Rhodium(I) in Wilkinson's catalyst has a coordination number of 4 and contains 16 valence electrons, making it coordinatively unsaturated.

Question 4 of 10
In the infrared spectrum of a metal carbonyl complex, a sharp absorption band appearing around 1780 cm-1 is indicative of:

Solution: Stretching frequencies in the region of 1700-1850 cm-1 are characteristic of bridging carbonyl ligands.

Question 5 of 10
Which metal complex obeys the 18-electron rule and is mononuclear?

Solution: Iron pentacarbonyl [Fe(CO)5] is mononuclear and possesses exactly 18 valence electrons (8 from Fe + 5×2 from CO).

Question 6 of 10
What happens to the carbon-oxygen bond length in CO upon coordination to an electron-rich transition metal?

Solution: Back-donation populates the anti-bonding π* orbitals of CO, decreasing the bond order and increasing the C-O bond length.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following compounds is an example of an organometallic catalyst used in the polymerization of alkenes?

Solution: The Ziegler-Natta catalyst (e.g., TiCl4 + Al(C2H5)3) is an organometallic catalyst system used for the stereospecific polymerization of alkenes.

Question 8 of 10
What is the expected structure of [Os6(CO)18] based on skeletal electron count and Wade's rules?

Solution: Calculation of skeletal electron pairs for [Os6(CO)18] gives 84 valence electrons, corresponding to 14 skeletal electron pairs (n+1 where n=6), indicating a closo octahedral cluster.

Question 9 of 10
Which reagent is classified as a main group organometallic compound containing a polar sigma bond?

Solution: Methylmagnesium bromide is a Grignard reagent, representing a polar main group organometallic compound with a sigma metal-carbon bond.

Question 10 of 10
What is the primary driving force behind synergistic back-bonding in metal carbonyls?

Solution: Back-bonding relieves excess negative charge accumulation on the metal center resulting from initial ligand-to-metal sigma donation.
