Inorganic Chemistry

Organometallic Chemistry

Organometallic chemistry investigates chemical compounds containing at least one bond between a carbon atom of an organic moiety and a metal. These complexes serve as key homogeneous catalysts in industrial synthesis and exhibit unique structural bonding motifs such as synergic backbonding.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification and Hapticity of Ligands

  • Hapticity (η) denotes the number of contiguous carbon atoms bonded to the metal center.
  • Monohapto allyl ($eta^1$-C3H5) contributes 2 electrons, whereas trihapto allyl ($eta^3$-C3H5) contributes 4 electrons.
  • Ferrocene ([Fe(η5-C5H5)2]) features two cyclopentadienyl rings each contributing 6 electrons.
  • Arene ligands such as benzene in [Cr(η6-C6H6)2] act as 6-electron donors.

Synergic Bonding in Metal Carbonyls

  • Metal-carbonyl bonding involves a two-component synergic model consisting of σ-donation and π-backdonation.
  • σ-donation proceeds via overlap of the lone pair on the carbon atom of CO with an empty metal dz2 orbital.
  • π-backdonation involves electron transfer from a filled metal d-orbital into the empty antibonding π* orbital of CO.
  • Stronger backbonding weakens the C-O bond, lowering its IR stretching frequency below free CO (2143 cm-1).

Catalytic Reactions in Organometallic Chemistry

  • Oxidative addition increases both the formal oxidation number and the coordination number of the metal center by 2.
  • Reductive elimination is the microscopic reverse of oxidative addition, decreasing oxidation and coordination numbers by 2.
  • Migratory insertion involves movement of an anionic ligand to an unsaturated neutral ligand on the same metal center.
  • The Wilkinson's catalyst [RhCl(PPh3)3] facilitates the homogeneous hydrogenation of alkenes.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Transition metal carbonyls strictly obey the 18-electron rule for maximum stability.

Observation: V(CO)6 has 17 valence electrons and does not dimerize to V2(CO)12. — Steric hindrance prevents the vanadium atoms from approaching closely enough to form the metal-metal bond.

Exception Increasing negative charge on a metal carbonylate anion increases backbonding and lowers νCO.

Observation: Neutral carbonyls with strongly electron-donating phosphine ligands can exhibit lower νCO than some anions. — Phosphines are superior σ-donors compared to CO, significantly raising the energy of metal d-electrons and enhancing π-backdonation.

Exception Terminal CO stretching frequencies are higher than bridging CO stretching frequencies.

Observation: Certain highly strained or electropositive metal clusters exhibit overlapping ranges. — Increased bridging delocalization and lower force constants typically place bridging CO (1700-1850 cm-1) well below terminal CO (1850-2120 cm-1).

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Neutral Atom Electron Counting Method
Total Valence = Group Number of Metal + ∑(Ligand contributions as neutral radicals)

Application: Avoids formal oxidation state calculations; count neutral CO as 2, Cl as 1, H as 1, η5-Cp as 5.

IR Stretching Frequency Trend Mnemonic
Backbonding ↑ → C-O bond order ↓ → νCO ↓

Application: Use when comparing metal carbonyl complexes with negative charges, lower oxidation states, or electron-donating ancillary ligands.

Oxidative Addition Delta Count
ΔOX = +2, ΔCN = +2, ΔVE = 0

Application: Instantly determines changes in oxidation number (OX), coordination number (CN), and valence electron count (VE) during oxidative addition.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Zeise's salt contains a Pt-C bond and is formulated as K[PtCl3(η2-C2H4)]·H2O.
  • Ferrocene exhibits sandwich geometry with a 18-electron stable configuration for iron(II).
  • Wilkinson's catalyst [RhCl(PPh3)3] is a 16-electron square planar complex used in alkene hydrogenation.
  • Hydroformylation (Oxo process) converts alkenes, CO, and H2 into aldehydes using cobalt or rhodium catalysts.
  • Ziegler-Natta catalysts (e.g., TiCl4 + Al(C2H5)3) polymerize alkenes stereospecifically.
  • Cobalt octacarbonyl Co2(CO)8 exists in equilibrium between bridged and non-bridged isomers in solution.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing hapticity (η) with coordination number; a single η5-Cp ring occupies one coordination site but donates 6 electrons.
  • Assuming all metal carbonyls obey the 18-electron rule; V(CO)6 has 17 electrons and Mn(CO)5 has 17 electrons.
  • Forgetting that bridging carbonyls absorb at significantly lower wavenumbers in IR spectroscopy compared to terminal carbonyls.
  • Incorrectly assigning electron counts by mixing neutral and ionic ligand counting methods in the same calculation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Organometallic Chemistry

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

18-Electron Rule Fundamentals

Transition metal complexes achieve noble gas configurations by utilizing (n-1)d, ns, and np orbitals, totaling 18 valence electrons.

Synergic Backbonding Mechanics

Metal-to-ligand π-backdonation strengthens the metal-carbon bond while weakening the internal ligand bond through electron density shift into π* orbitals.

Fundamental Reaction Types

Catalytic cycles rely on oxidative addition, reductive elimination, migratory insertion, and β-hydrogen elimination elementary steps.

Industrial Applications

Organometallic complexes drive large-scale industrial processes including alkene polymerization, hydrogenation, and hydroformylation.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Hapticity (η)
Number of contiguous carbon atoms bonded to the metal center.
Synergic Bonding (σ-donation + π-backdonation)
Mutual reinforcement of metal-ligand bonding through dual electron transfer.
Oxidative Addition (M + XY → LnM(X)(Y))
Reaction increasing metal oxidation and coordination numbers by 2.
Reductive Elimination (LnM(X)(Y) → M + XY)
Reaction decreasing metal oxidation and coordination numbers by 2.
Agostic Interaction (C-H→M)
Three-center two-electron bond involving a C-H sigma bond and metal d-orbital.
Metallocene ((C5H5)2M)
Sandwich compound containing a transition metal between two cyclopentadienyl rings.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Terminal CO IR frequency: ~1850 - 2120 cm-1
  • Bridging CO IR frequency: ~1700 - 1850 cm-1
  • Ferrocene total valence electrons: 18 (Fe2+ d6 + two η5-Cp rings donating 12 electrons)
  • Wilkinson's catalyst electron count: 16 electrons for [RhCl(PPh3)3]
  • Zeise's salt Pt hapticity: η2-ethylene ligand

📌 Quick Recall

  • Organometallic compounds feature direct metal-carbon bonds.
  • The 18-electron rule parallels the octet rule for main group elements in transition metal chemistry.
  • IR spectroscopy is a primary tool for diagnosing the extent of π-backbonding in metal carbonyls.
  • Homogeneous catalysis cycles alternate between coordinatively saturated and unsaturated active species.

⚖️ Key Differences

Oxidative Addition VS Electrophilic Attack

Oxidative addition inserts the metal into a covalent bond with an increase in metal oxidation state; electrophilic attack transfers a positive species without changing metal oxidation state.

Question 1 of 19

What is the total valence electron count of Fe(CO)5 based on the neutral atom method?

Explanation: Fe is in Group 8 (8 electrons) and five CO ligands each contribute 2 electrons, giving 8 + 5(2) = 18 electrons.

Question 2 of 19

Which of the following metal carbonyls does not obey the 18-electron rule?

Explanation: V(CO)6 has 5 (V) + 6(2) = 17 valence electrons.

Question 3 of 19

In Zeise's salt, K[PtCl3(η2-C2H4)], what is the hapticity of the ethylene ligand?

Explanation: Ethylene coordinates through both carbon atoms of its π-bond, making it a dihapto (η2) ligand.

Question 4 of 19

How does the C-O stretching frequency in metal carbonyls change upon increasing π-backdonation?

Explanation: Backdonation populates the anti-bonding π* orbital of CO, weakening the C-O bond and lowering its stretching frequency.

Question 5 of 19

What is the change in oxidation number of the metal during oxidative addition?

Explanation: Oxidative addition formally oxidizes the metal center, increasing its oxidation state by 2.

Question 6 of 19

Which catalyst is used for the homogeneous hydrogenation of alkenes?

Explanation: Wilkinson's catalyst, [RhCl(PPh3)3], is the classic homogeneous hydrogenation catalyst.

Question 7 of 19

What is the electron contribution of an η5-cyclopentadienyl (η5-Cp) ligand in the neutral atom counting method?

Explanation: An η5-Cp ring coordinates through 5 carbon atoms and contributes 5 electrons as a neutral radical.

Question 8 of 19

Which complex exhibits a sandwich structure?

Explanation: Ferrocene [Fe(η5-C5H5)2] has a classic sandwich structure with the iron atom between two parallel cyclopentadienyl rings.

Question 9 of 19

What is the coordination number of cobalt in Co2(CO)8 in its non-bridged isomer?

Explanation: In the non-bridged isomer of Co2(CO)8, each cobalt atom is bonded to 4 terminal CO ligands and 1 Co-Co bond, giving a coordination number of 5.

Question 10 of 19

Which ligand acts solely as a 2-electron σ-donor and does not engage in significant π-backbonding?

Explanation: Ammonia (NH3) is a pure σ-donor ligand lacking low-lying π* orbitals for backbonding.

Question 11 of 19

In the hydroformylation reaction, an alkene is converted into what functional group?

Explanation: Hydroformylation adds CO and H across a double bond to yield an aldehyde with one more carbon atom.

Question 12 of 19

What type of interaction is defined as a 3-center 2-electron bond involving a C-H sigma bond and a transition metal?

Explanation: An agostic interaction involves coordination of a C-H single bond to an electron-deficient metal center.

Question 13 of 19

What is the electron contribution of a bridging carbonyl ((μ-CO)) ligand when counted per metal atom in the neutral method?

Explanation: A bridging carbonyl donates 1 electron to each of the two metal centers it bridges (total 2 electrons).

Question 14 of 19

Which metal complex is diamagnetic and possesses a sandwich structure with 18 valence electrons?

Explanation: Ferrocene is diamagnetic and strictly obeys the 18-electron rule with a sandwich structure.

Question 15 of 19

What is the role of Al(C2H5)3 in the Ziegler-Natta catalyst system?

Explanation: Triethylaluminum acts as an alkylating agent and cocatalyst with TiCl4 in Ziegler-Natta polymerization.

Question 16 of 19

Which species is isoelectronic with CO?

Explanation: CN-, N2, and NO+ all possess 14 valence electrons and are isoelectronic with CO.

Question 17 of 19

What occurs during the reductive elimination step in a catalytic cycle?

Explanation: Reductive elimination eliminates two ligands as a neutral molecule, decreasing both metal oxidation and coordination numbers by 2.

Question 18 of 19

Which of the following is an example of a π-acid ligand?

Explanation: CO possesses empty π* orbitals that can accept metal d-electrons, classifying it as a π-acid ligand.

Question 19 of 19

What is the formal oxidation state of iron in Fe(CO)5?

Explanation: Since CO is a neutral ligand and the complex is neutral, iron has a formal oxidation state of 0.

Question 1 of 10
Determine the number of metal-metal bonds in Co2(CO)8.

Solution: Each Co (Group 9) needs 9 electrons. In Co2(CO)8, total valence electrons = 2(9) + 8(2) = 34. To achieve 18 electrons per Co (36 total), a deficit of 2 electrons requires one Co-Co bond.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following ligands acts as a 3-electron donor in the neutral atom counting method?

Solution: An η3-allyl group acts as a 3-electron radical donor (or 4-electron ionic donor).

Question 3 of 10
What is the coordination geometry of Ni(CO)4?

Solution: Ni(CO)4 is a 4-coordinate complex adopting a tetrahedral geometry.

Question 4 of 10
Which reaction sequence describes alkene hydrogenation using Wilkinson's catalyst?

Solution: The catalytic cycle involves ligand dissociation to create a vacant site, oxidative addition of H2, alkene coordination, migratory insertion, and reductive elimination.

Question 5 of 10
In metal carbonyls, the σ-donation from CO to the metal utilizes which orbital of CO?

Solution: CO donates electron density via the Highest Occupied Molecular Orbital (HOMO), which is a σ lone pair primarily localized on the carbon atom.

Question 6 of 10
What is the formal oxidation state of manganese in Mn2(CO)10?

Solution: In homonuclear metal carbonyl dimers like Mn2(CO)10, the metal-metal bond is homolytic, assigning an oxidation state of 0 to manganese.

Question 7 of 10
Which complex is known to undergo fluxional behavior (stereochemical non-rigidity) in solution at room temperature?

Solution: Complexes like [Fe(η5-Cp)(η1-Cp)(CO)2] undergo rapid intramolecular rearrangements (fluxionality) observed in NMR spectroscopy.

Question 8 of 10
What happens to the metal oxidation state during β-hydride elimination?

Solution: β-hydride elimination formally oxidizes the metal center, increasing its oxidation state by 2.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following is a mononuclear 18-electron complex?

Solution: Fe(CO)5 has 8 + 5(2) = 18 electrons. The others are 17-electron radicals.

Question 10 of 10
The synergic effect in metal carbonyls results in:

Solution: π-backdonation populates the anti-bonding π* orbital of CO, which simultaneously strengthens the metal-carbon bond and weakens the carbon-oxygen bond.
