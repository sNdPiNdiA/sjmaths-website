Nomenclature of Coordination and Organometallic Compounds

Organometallic and coordination nomenclature follows systematic IUPAC rules established to unambiguously define complex molecular architecture, ligand arrangements, and metal oxidation states. The naming convention prioritizes ligands alphabetically before the central metal atom, with bridging ligands explicitly designated by the prefix μ.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

IUPAC Rules for Coordination Sphere Naming

  • The cation is always named before the anion, regardless of whether the complex is cationic or anionic.
  • Ligands are listed in alphabetical order before the central metal atom, ignoring numerical prefixes like di-, tri-, or tetrakis-.
  • Anionic ligands end with the suffix -o (e.g., chloro, cyano, hydroxo), whereas neutral ligands retain their common molecular names with exceptions like aquam, ammmine, carbonyl, and nitrosyl.
  • If the complex entity is anionic, the central metal name ends with the suffix -ate, often derived from its Latin root (e.g., ferrate, plumbate, cuprate).

Organometallic Nomenclature and Hapticity

  • The symbol η (hapticity) indicates the number of contiguous carbon atoms bonded to the metal center, as seen in η5-cyclopentadienyl.
  • Ligands bonded through multiple non-contiguous donor atoms or bridging centers use the μ prefix, superscripted with the number of metal atoms bridged if greater than two.
  • Sandwich complexes such as ferrocene are systematically named as bis(η5-cyclopentadienyl)iron(II).
  • Metal-metal bonds within polynuclear clusters are indicated explicitly using the italicized metal symbol enclosed in parentheses, such as (Fe-Fe).

Oxidation State and Stoichiometry Designation

  • The oxidation state of the central metal atom is written in Roman numerals inside parentheses immediately following the metal name without any space.
  • Geometric isomerism is designated using cis- and trans- prefixes for square planar and octahedral complexes, or fac- and mer- for facial and meridional octahedral isomers.
  • Ambidentate ligands use specific IUPAC names or directional suffixes such as -κN or -κO to explicitly denote the donor atom binding to the metal.
  • Crystallization water molecules outside the coordination sphere are appended at the end of the name as tetrahydrate, hexahydrate, etc., following stoichiometric multipliers.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Anionic metal names should use standard Latin suffixes (-ate)

Observation: Mercury in anionic complexes uses 'mercurate', but iron uses 'ferrate' rather than 'ironate'. — Historical persistence of Latin roots in IUPAC nomenclature standards for traditional metal salts.

Exception Neutral ligands retain their molecular names

Observation: H2O is named 'aqua', NH3 is named 'ammine' (with double 'm'), CO is 'carbonyl', and NO is 'nitrosyl'. — Special historical conventions maintained by IUPAC inorganic nomenclature commissions.

Exception Prefixes are ignored for alphabetizing ligands

Observation: Derived complex ligand prefixes like 'bis-', 'tris-', 'tetrakis-' are alphabetized based on the first letter of the ligand name inside the parenthesis. — To prevent alphabetization ambiguities when complex or substituted ligands are bracketed.

Application: Quickly arrange ligands by discarding numerical multipliers (di, tri, bis, tris) and sorting strictly by the first letter of the ligand name.

Facial vs Meridional Identification
fac = 3 identical ligands occupy one triangular face; mer = 3 identical ligands occupy a meridian plane

Application: Examine octahedral geometry: if three identical ligands form a face triangle, it is fac; if they bisect the octahedron in a T-shape plane, it is mer.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • In IUPAC nomenclature, the cation is named before the anion in coordination compounds.
  • Ligands are always arranged in alphabetical order regardless of their charge or number.
  • Neutral ligands like NH3 are termed 'ammine' with a double 'm'.
  • Hapticity is denoted by the Greek letter η with a superscript indicating carbon atom count.
  • Bridging ligands between two or more metal atoms are designated with the prefix μ.
  • The oxidation state of the metal is denoted by Roman numerals in parentheses without spaces.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting to double the 'm' in 'ammine' when naming ammine complexes.
  • Alphabetizing based on multiplicative prefixes like 'di-' or 'tris-' instead of the core ligand name.
  • Placing a space between the metal name and the Roman numeral oxidation state parenthesis.
  • Confusing the hapticity (η) symbol with the bridging (μ) symbol in organometallic clusters.

⚡ Quick Revision: Nomenclature of Coordination and Organometallic Compounds

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Coordination Sphere Order

Name the cation first, then the anion. Inside the complex ion, list ligands alphabetically before naming the central metal.

Anionic Metal Suffixes

When the coordination entity carries a net negative charge, the metal name must end with the suffix -ate appended to its Latin or English root.

Hapticity and Bridging

Organometallic descriptors include η for delocalized π-systems indicating contact atoms, and μ for shared bridging ligands.

Isomerism Prefixes

Geometric configurations are identified using cis-, trans-, fac-, and mer- prefixes placed prior to the coordination entity name.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Hapticity (η)
Number of contiguous carbon atoms bound to a metal center.
Bridging Ligand (μ)
A ligand bonded to two or more metal atoms simultaneously.
Ambidentate (Ambidentate Ligand)
A ligand capable of coordinating through two different donor atoms.
Denticity (Donor Atom Count)
Number of donor atoms a single ligand uses to bind the metal.
Coordination Number (CN)
Total number of ligand donor atoms directly bonded to the central metal.
Ammine (NH3)
Neutral ammonia ligand in coordination complexes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Alphabetical sorting ignores prefixes: di, tri, tetra, bis, tris, tetrakis.
  • Anionic complexes end in -ate.
  • Neutral ligands: aqua, ammine, carbonyl, nitrosyl.
  • Oxidation state written as Roman numerals in parentheses.
  • Bridging ligands prefixed with μ.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Systematic nomenclature provides unambiguous identification of complexes.
  • Organometallic nomenclature incorporates η and μ notations.
  • Alphabetical ordering governs multi-ligand complexes.
  • Oxidation state calculation balances outer-sphere and inner-sphere charges.

⚖️ Key Differences

Amine (R-NH2) VS Ammine (NH3)

Ammine has two 'm's and denotes coordinated ammonia, whereas amine denotes organic derivatives.
