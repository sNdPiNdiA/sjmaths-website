Inorganic Chemistry

Metal–carbon bond

Metal–carbon bonds characterize organometallic compounds, bridging inorganic and organic chemistry through covalent interactions between metal centers and carbon atoms. These bonds exhibit varying degrees of ionic and covalent character depending on the electronegativity of the metal and the hybridization of the carbon atom.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Nature and Polarity of Metal-Carbon Bonds

  • The polarity of the metal-carbon bond is dictated by the electronegativity difference between the metal and carbon atom.
  • Main group alkyls (e.g., Grignard reagents R-Mg-X, organolithium R-Li) possess significant ionic character with a nucleophilic carbon center.
  • Transition metal carbon bonds feature high covalent character due to comparable electronegativities and favorable orbital overlap.
  • Organometallic compounds are classified as σ-bonded, π-bonded, or σ-π mixed complexes based on ligand hapticity (η).

Synergistic Bonding in Metal Carbonyls

  • Metal carbonyls feature synergistic bonding involving forward donation from the filled ligand σ-orbitals to the empty metal d-orbitals.
  • Back-donation occurs from the filled metal d-orbitals into the empty **π* antibonding orbitals** of the carbon monoxide ligand.
  • Synergistic back-donation strengthens the metal-carbon bond while simultaneously weakening the C-O bond, lowering its stretching vibrational frequency (νCO).
  • An increase in negative charge on the metal center enhances back-donation, further decreasing the νCO IR absorption frequency.

Stability and Structural Rules (18-Electron Rule)

  • Stable transition metal organometallic complexes typically attain a valence electron configuration analogous to the next noble gas, totaling 18 electrons.
  • The EAN rule helps predict stability, though complexes with 16 electrons (typically d8 square planar complexes like [PtCl4]2- or Vaska's complex) are also kinetically stable.
  • Metallocenes such as ferrocene [Fe(η5-C5H5)2] satisfy the 18-electron rule and exhibit high thermal and chemical stability.
  • Bridging metal-carbon bonds commonly utilize 3-center 2-electron (3c-2e) bonds, as observed in aluminum alkyl dimers like Al2(CH3)6.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception All stable transition metal complexes strictly obey the 18-electron rule.

Observation: Many d8 square planar complexes (e.g., Ir(I), Pt(II), Rh(I)) are exceptionally stable with 16 valence electrons. — The dz2 orbital is non-bonding or weakly anti-bonding and unpopulated or doubly occupied without accepting extra ligands due to steric and crystal field splitting effects.

Exception Metal-carbon bond strength increases symmetrically with higher oxidation states.

Observation: High oxidation state metals reduce the availability of d-electrons for π-backbonding in metal carbonyls. — Positive charge on the metal contracts the d-orbitals, worsening the overlap with ligand π* orbitals.

Exception Bridging alkyl groups donate one electron per metal atom.

Observation: In trimethylaluminum dimer, bridging methyl groups form 3-center 2-electron bonds. — Electron deficiency forces carbon to share its electron pair between two aluminum centers simultaneously.

🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing neutral fragment electron counting with ionic fragment counting methods.
  • Assuming all transition metal complexes strictly follow the 18-electron rule while ignoring stable 16-electron square planar systems.
  • Forgetting that back-donation strengthens the metal-carbon bond while weakening the carbon-oxygen bond in metal carbonyls.
  • Incorrectly assigning hapticity (η value) by counting non-contiguous atoms in cyclic polyenes.

⚡ Quick Revision: Metal–carbon bond

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Classification of Organometallic Compounds

Organometallic compounds are classified by the nature of the metal-carbon bond, encompassing ionic main group alkyls and covalent transition metal complexes.

Synergistic Backbonding Mechanism

Metal-π-acid complexes utilize forward σ-donation and backward π-donation to form stable multiple-bond character metal-ligand linkages.

The 18-Electron Rule

Transition metal complexes achieve electronic saturation when their valence shell contains 18 electrons, mirroring noble gas configurations.

Electron Deficient Bridging Structures

Alkyl bridges in compounds like aluminum and beryllium dimers rely on multi-center electron-deficient bonding frameworks.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Hapticity (η (Eta))
The number of contiguous carbon atoms of a ligand bonded to a metal center.
Synergistic Effect (M(d) → CO(π*))
Mutually reinforcing forward donation and back-donation in metal carbonyls.
EAN Rule (Effective Atomic Number)
Total electrons around a metal including inner core and ligand donations.
Metallocene (Sandwich Complex)
Organometallic coordination compound consisting of a transition metal sandwiched between two cyclopenta-dienyl rings.
Agostic Interaction (C-H→M)
Three-center two-electron interaction between a metal center and a C-H σ-bond.
Oxidative Addition (Metal Insertion)
A reaction where a metal inserts itself into a covalent bond, increasing both oxidation state and coordination number.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ferrocene has 18 valence electrons and a sandwich structure.
  • CO is a strong field π-acid ligand that promotes extensive backbonding.
  • Trimethylaluminum forms a bridged dimer with 3c-2e bonds.
  • Square planar complexes of Pt(II), Pd(II), and Ir(I) typically possess 16 valence electrons.
  • Grignard reagents formula: R-Mg-X.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Metal-carbon bonds range from ionic (main group) to covalent (transition metals).
  • Synergistic bonding explains the stability and IR spectral shifts of metal carbonyls.
  • The 18-electron rule serves as a fundamental guideline for predicting transition metal complex stability.
  • Hapticity dictates the ligand electron contribution to the metal center.

⚖️ Key Differences

Terminal Carbonyl VS Bridging Carbonyl

Terminal CO absorbs at higher IR frequencies (~2000 cm-1) compared to bridging CO (~1850 cm-1).

Question 1 of 20

What is the valence electron count of Fe in [Fe(CO)₅] based on the neutral atom method?

Explanation: Fe has 8 valence electrons. Each of the 5 CO ligands donates 2 electrons. Total = 8 + 5(2) = 18 electrons.

Question 2 of 20

Which of the following compounds exhibits 3-center 2-electron bonds?

Explanation: Trimethylaluminum dimer contains bridging methyl groups held by 3-center 2-electron (3c-2e) bonds.

Question 3 of 20

What is the hapticity (η) of the cyclopentadienyl ligand in ferrocene?

Explanation: In ferrocene, each cyclopentadienyl ring uses all 5 carbon atoms to bond with the iron center, denoting η⁵.

Question 4 of 20

In metal carbonyls, back-donation from metal d-orbitals to ligand π* orbitals results in:

Explanation: Electron density entering the antibonding π* orbital of CO weakens and lengthens the C-O bond while strengthening the metal-carbon bond.

Question 5 of 20

Which complex is a classic example of a 16-electron square planar stable system?

Explanation: Vaska's complex is a 16-electron d⁸ square planar iridium(I) complex that is remarkably stable.

Question 6 of 20

What is the expected IR stretching frequency trend for free CO compared to coordinated CO in a neutral metal carbonyl?

Explanation: Due to metal-to-ligand backbonding into antibonding orbitals, the C-O bond weakens, causing its stretching frequency to drop.

Question 7 of 20

Grignard reagents are represented by the general formula:

Explanation: Grignard reagents are organomagnesium halides with the general formula R-Mg-X.

Question 8 of 20

What type of bond exists between the metal and carbon in main-group alkyls like organolithium?

Explanation: Main group metal-carbon bonds have high ionic character due to the large electronegativity difference between carbon and the alkali metal.

Question 9 of 20

What is the electron contribution of a bridging carbonyl ligand (μ-CO) to each metal center in a dinuclear complex?

Explanation: A bridging carbonyl ligand donates one pair of electrons shared between two metal atoms, contributing effectively 1 electron to each metal's valence tally.

Question 10 of 20

Which transition metal complex does NOT obey the 18-electron rule?

Explanation: Vanadium hexacarbonyl [V(CO)₆] has 17 valence electrons (6 + 6(2) = 17) due to steric hindrance preventing further coordination.

Question 11 of 20

What is the IUPAC name for Zeise's salt?

Explanation: Zeise's salt is potassium trichloro(η²-ethylene)platinate(II), K[PtCl₃(C₂H₄)].

Question 12 of 20

Which ligand acts as a pure σ-donor without significant π-acceptor properties?

Explanation: Alkyl ligands like CH₃⁻ act primarily as strong σ-donors with negligible π-acidity.

Question 13 of 20

The oxidation state of iron in ferrocene, [Fe(C₅H₅)₂], assuming ionic ligand splitting is:

Explanation: Each cyclopentadienyl ring carries a single negative charge (C₅H₅⁻), giving iron an oxidation state of +2.

Question 14 of 20

Which structural feature is characteristic of an agostic interaction?

Explanation: An agostic interaction is a 3c-2e interaction where a C-H σ-bond coordinates to an electron-deficient metal center.

Question 15 of 20

What is the total valence electron count for [Mn(CO)₅]• radical?

Explanation: Mn has 7 valence electrons, and five CO ligands contribute 10 electrons. Total = 7 + 10 = 17 electrons.

Question 16 of 20

Which of the following metal carbonyls has the lowest CO stretching frequency?

Explanation: An anionic metal carbonyl like [V(CO)₆]⁻ has excess electron density, maximizing back-donation and giving the lowest νCO.

Question 17 of 20

What role does the metal center play in synergistic bonding with carbon monoxide?

Explanation: The metal accepts ligand lone pairs into empty σ-type orbitals while simultaneously donating d-electron density into ligand π* orbitals.

Question 18 of 20

How many electrons does an η³-allyl group formally donate to a metal in neutral counting mode?

Explanation: An η³-allyl group binds through three contiguous carbons and donates 3 electrons in the neutral radical counting method.

Question 19 of 20

Which reagent is commonly used to prepare organolithium compounds from alkyl halides?

Explanation: Organolithium compounds are synthesized by reacting alkyl halides with lithium metal in an inert organic solvent like dry ether.

Question 20 of 20

What is the primary driving force for oxidative addition reactions of metal complexes?

Explanation: Oxidative addition typically increases the metal's electron count by 2, allowing unsaturated complexes to reach the stable 18-electron configuration.

Solution: Carbon monoxide is a classic π-acid ligand capable of strong σ-donation and π-backbonding.

Solution: Neutral carbon monoxide ligands impart no charge, leaving nickel in the zero oxidation state.

Question 6 of 10
In metal carbonyl complexes, as the negative charge on the complex increases, the C-O stretching frequency:
