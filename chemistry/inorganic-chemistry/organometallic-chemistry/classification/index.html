Inorganic Chemistry

Classification of Organometallic Compounds

Organometallic compounds are classified based on the nature of the metal-carbon bond, including ionic, covalent, and delocalized multicenter bonds. Compounds are systematically categorized according to metal electronegativity, ligand hapticity, and the 18-electron rule.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification Based on Metal-Carbon Bond Polarity

  • Main group organometallics containing electropositive metals like lithium, sodium, and magnesium form predominantly ionic or polar covalent bonds due to large electronegativity differences.
  • Transition metal organometallics exhibit covalent bonds characterized by significant back-bonding involving metal d-orbitals and ligand π*-orbitals.
  • Organolanthanides and actinides display highly ionic metal-carbon interactions due to large atomic radii and electropositive character.

Classification Based on Ligand Hapticity and Unsaturation

  • σ-bonded complexes involve single carbon-metal covalent bonds, such as in alkyl and aryl derivatives like methyllithium (LiCH3).
  • π-complexes involve delocalized carbon π-systems bonded to transition metals, designated by hapticity (ηn) representing the number of contiguous carbons interacting with the metal.
  • Examples include η2-C2H4 in Zeise's salt and η5-C5H5 in ferrocene [Fe(η5-C5H5)2].

Classification Based on Electron Count and Stability

  • 18-Electron complexes achieve noble gas electron configurations and exhibit high kinetic and thermodynamic stability.
  • Electron-deficient complexes possess fewer than 18 electrons (frequently seen in early transition metals) and act as strong Lewis acids.
  • Electron-rich complexes exceeding 18 electrons (such as d10 square planar complexes) remain stable due to ligand steric hindrance or occupation of non-bonding orbitals.

Hapticity (ηn) Calculation
n = Number of contiguous carbon atoms bonded to the metal center
Conditions: π-bonded unsaturated hydrocarbon ligands
Units: Integer representing carbon atom count
Effective Atomic Number (EAN)
EAN = Z - Oxidation State + 2(Coordination Number)
Conditions: Mononuclear and polynuclear metal carbonyl complexes
Units: Total electron count around metal
Metal-Carbon Bond Ionic Character
Percentage Ionic Character = 16(χC - χM) + 3.5(χC - χM)2
Conditions: Pauling electronegativity values χ
Units: Percentage (%)
18-Electron Rule (Neutral Atom Method)
Total Electrons = Group Number of Metal + 2(∑L) + 1(∑X) + charge
Conditions: Transition metal complexes obeying effective atomic number (EAN) rule
Units: Dimensionless integer (18 for stable valence shell configuration)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Transition metal alkyls are generally unstable and undergo rapid β-hydride elimination.

Observation: Metal alkyls lacking β-hydrogens (e.g., methyl, neopentyl, benzyl complexes) are remarkably stable. — Absence of β-hydrogens prevents the cyclic four-center transition state required for β-hydride elimination.

Exception All stable transition metal complexes must strictly obey the 18-electron rule.

Observation: Bulkier d8 complexes (e.g., Ir(I), Rh(I), Pt(II)) form stable 16-electron square planar configurations. — Steric crowding around the metal center prevents the addition of two more ligands to reach 18 electrons.

Exception Metallocenes containing iron and similar metals exhibit symmetrical sandwich structures.

Observation: Titanocene dichloride ([Cp2TiCl2]) adopts a bent sandwich structure. — Presence of additional ligands and specific d-electron counts distort the parallel ring arrangement.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Neutral Atom vs Ionic Method for 18-Electron Count
Metal Group Number + Ligand Donated Electrons + Charge Correction

Application: Use the neutral atom method to avoid oxidation state ambiguity: CO = 2e-, halide = 1e-, Cp (η5) = 5e-.

Bridge vs Terminal Carbonyl Identification
IR stretching frequency: Terminal CO (>1850 cm-1), Bridging CO (1700-1850 cm-1)

Application: Quickly identify bridging carbonyls in polynuclear metal carbonyls using infrared spectroscopy data in exam questions.

Hapticity Counting Shortcut
(ηn) donation count equals the number of unsaturated carbons bonded to metal.

Application: Allyl = 3, Benzene = 6, Cyclopentadienyl = 5; multiply by respective electron contribution per carbon or treat the entire ring as a single donor unit.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Ferrocene [Fe(η5-C5H5)2] contains iron in the +2 oxidation state and obeys the 18-electron rule.
  • Zeise's salt K[PtCl3(η2-C2H4)] contains an ethylene ligand with a hapticity of 2.
  • Grignard reagents (RMgX) are classified as polar covalent main group organometallic compounds.
  • Metal carbonyls like Ni(CO)4 and Fe(CO)5 are zero-valent transition metal complexes obeying the 18-electron rule.
  • Bridging carbonyl ligands lower the stretching frequency in IR spectra due to increased back-bonding from multiple metal centers.
  • Metallocenes of early transition metals (e.g., titanocene) often deviate from the 18-electron rule due to steric and electronic factors.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing hapticity (η) with coordination number; hapticity specifically denotes contiguous carbon atoms bound to the metal.
  • Assuming all transition metal complexes strictly follow the 18-electron rule; square planar d8 complexes possess 16 electrons.
  • Miscalculating electron contributions where carbonyl acts as a bridging ligand instead of a terminal 2-electron donor.
  • Forgetting to account for overall complex charge when applying the electron counting formula.

⚡ Quick Revision: Classification of Organometallic Compounds

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Classification Parameters

Organometallic compounds are classified by metal electronegativity, bond polarity, ligand hapticity, and electron count.

Main Group vs Transition Metal Systems

Main group elements form ionic/polar covalent bonds obeying the octet rule, while transition metals form covalent complexes obeying the 18-electron rule.

Hapticity and π-Coordination

Unsaturated hydrocarbons coordinate via π-systems denoted by hapticity (ηn), ranging from η1 to η8.

Structural Stability Rules

Stability is governed by the 18-electron rule, absence of β-hydrogens to prevent elimination, and adequate ligand stabilization.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Hapticity (ηn)
Number of contiguous carbon atoms of a ligand bonded to a metal center.
18-Electron Rule (Valence Electron Count)
Stable electron configuration analogous to noble gases for transition metals.
Metallocene ((C5H5)2M)
Sandwich compound containing a transition metal between two cyclopentadienyl rings.
Synergistic Effect (σ-donor π-acceptor)
Reinforcing mechanism of metal-ligand bonding involving forward donation and back-donation.
β-Hydride Elimination (Decomposition Pathway)
Intramolecular abstraction of a β-hydrogen by the metal center.
EAN (Effective Atomic Number)
Total number of electrons owned by the metal including ligand donations.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ferrocene has 18 valence electrons and a sandwich geometry.
  • Zeise's salt contains an η2-ethylene ligand coordinated to Platinum(II).
  • Ni(CO)4 is tetrahedral with 18 electrons.
  • Square planar complexes of Ir(I), Rh(I), and Pt(II) are stable 16-electron systems.
  • Grignard reagents form Schlenk equilibrium in solution.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Organometallics feature direct metal-carbon bonds.
  • Classification depends on bond polarity and ligand type (σ vs π).
  • Transition metals achieve stability via the 18-electron rule.
  • Hapticity defines multi-atom ligand bonding modes.

⚖️ Key Differences

Coordination Number VS Hapticity

Coordination number counts total ligand attachment points, whereas hapticity counts contiguous carbons on a single π-ligand.

Question 1 of 19

What is the hapticity (η) of the cyclopentadienyl ring in ferrocene, [Fe(η5-C5H5)2]?

Explanation: In ferrocene, all five carbon atoms of the cyclopentadienyl ring interact equally with the iron center, making its hapticity η5.

Question 2 of 19

Which of the following complexes obeys the 18-electron rule?

Explanation: Cr(CO)6 has 6 (Cr) + 6×2 (CO) = 18 electrons. V(CO)6 has 17, Mn(CO)5 has 17, and Fe(CO)4 has 16 electrons.

Question 3 of 19

Zeise's salt is classified as which type of organometallic compound?

Explanation: Zeise's salt, K[PtCl3(η2-C2H4)], contains an alkene (π-system) bonded to platinum.

Question 4 of 19

What is the total electron count for Ni(CO)4 using the neutral atom method?

Explanation: Ni has 10 valence electrons (Group 10) and each CO contributes 2 electrons: 10 + 4(2) = 18 electrons.

Question 5 of 19

Which transition metal complex typically forms a stable 16-electron square planar geometry?

Explanation: d8 metal centers like Pt(II), Ir(I), and Rh(I) commonly form stable 16-electron square planar complexes.

Question 6 of 19

Organometallic compounds of main group alkali metals are predominantly:

Explanation: Alkali metal organometallics like organolithium compounds feature highly ionic or polar covalent bonds due to low metal electronegativity.

Question 7 of 19

What is the hapticity of benzene in Cr(η6-C6H6)2?

Explanation: Benzene coordinates via all six of its carbon atoms, giving a hapticity of η6.

Question 8 of 19

Which reaction is primarily responsible for the decomposition of transition metal alkyls containing β-hydrogens?

Explanation: β-hydride elimination involves transfer of a hydrogen atom from the β-carbon to the metal center, leading to decomposition.

Question 9 of 19

In the neutral atom method, how many electrons does a terminal carbonyl (CO) ligand donate to the metal?

Explanation: A carbonyl ligand acts as a two-electron donor through a lone pair on the carbon atom.

Question 10 of 19

Which compound is an example of a sandwich complex?

Explanation: Ferrocene consists of an iron atom sandwiched between two parallel cyclopentadienyl rings.

Question 11 of 19

What is the oxidation state of iron in ferrocene, [Fe(η5-C5H5)2]?

Explanation: Treating each cyclopentadienyl ring as Cp- (monoanionic), iron is in the +2 oxidation state.

Question 12 of 19

Which metal carbonyl dimer, Mn2(CO)10, contains what type of metal-metal interaction?

Explanation: Mn2(CO)10 possesses a single Mn-Mn bond, satisfying the 18-electron rule for each manganese center.

Question 13 of 19

Which of the following ligands acts as a 3-electron donor in its usual coordination mode?

Explanation: An allyl ligand coordinated via three contiguous carbons (η3) donates 3 electrons to the metal center.

Question 14 of 19

What is the effective atomic number (EAN) of Fe in Fe(CO)5?

Explanation: Fe has atomic number 26, oxidation state 0, and 5 CO ligands contributing 10 electrons: 26 - 0 + 10 = 36 (Krypton configuration).

Question 15 of 19

Organometallic chemistry strictly requires which of the following bonds?

Explanation: The defining criterion of organometallic compounds is the presence of at least one direct bond between a metal and a carbon atom.

Question 16 of 19

Which class of compounds typically exhibits electron deficiency and forms polymeric structures in the solid state?

Explanation: Light main group metal alkyls like methyllithium and dimethylberyllium are electron-deficient and form multi-center two-electron bonds resulting in polymers.

Question 17 of 19

What is the contribution of a bridging halide (μ-X) ligand to a single metal center when counting electrons in the neutral method?

Explanation: A bridging halide donates one electron pair (2 electrons) to be shared between the two metal centers, contributing 1 electron to each.

Question 18 of 19

Which of the following statements about metal carbene complexes (Fischer carbenes) is correct?

Explanation: Fischer carbenes typically feature low-valent, electron-rich metals and electrophilic carbene carbons attached to heteroatom substituents.

Question 19 of 19

What is the classification of methylcobalamin (Coenzyme B12 derivative) based on its metal-carbon bond?

Explanation: Methylcobalamin contains a cobalt-carbon bond, classifying it as a transition metal organometallic compound.

Question 2 of 10
What is the electron count of [Fe(η5-C5H5)(CO)2]2?

Solution: Each iron center has 8 (Fe) + 5 (Cp) + 2×2 (CO) + 1 (Fe-Fe bond) = 18 electrons.

Question 3 of 10
Which ligand acts as a 4-electron donor when bonding via a conjugated diene system like butadiene?

Solution: A conjugated diene coordinates through all four carbons (η4), donating 4 electrons to the metal center.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following is classified as an ionic organometallic compound?

Solution: Phenyllithium contains a highly polar bond with significant ionic character due to lithium's low electronegativity.

Question 5 of 10
What happens to the C-O stretching frequency in metal carbonyls when back-bonding increases?

Solution: Back-donation populates the anti-bonding π* orbital of CO, weakening the C-O bond and lowering its stretching frequency.

Question 6 of 10
Which metal complex violates the 18-electron rule by possessing 19 electrons?

Solution: V(CO)6 has 5 (V) + 12 (CO) = 17 electrons. Wait, vanadium has 5 valence electrons, plus 12 from CO gives 17 electrons (electron deficient).

Question 7 of 10
The coordination number of platinum in Zeise's salt is:

Solution: Platinum is coordinated to three chloride ions and one η2-ethylene ligand (counted as a bidentate or single coordination site depending on convention, total coordinate sites = 4 giving square planar geometry).

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following reagents is a classic organo-main group nucleophile used in carbonyl additions?

Solution: Grignard reagents are quintessential main group organometallic nucleophiles.

Question 9 of 10
What is the hapticity of cyclooctatetraene (COT) in uranocene, U(η8-C8H8)2?

Solution: In uranocene, each cyclooctatetraene ring is planar and bonds via all eight carbon atoms, giving η8 hapticity.

Question 10 of 10
Which factor stabilizes transition metal alkyls against β-hydride elimination?

Solution: Steric hindrance or absence of vacant coordination sites prevents the approach necessary for β-hydride elimination.
