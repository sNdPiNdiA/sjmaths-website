Inorganic Chemistry

Ionic Radii

Ionic radius refers to the effective distance from the nucleus of an ion up to which it exerts its electrostatic influence on neighboring ions. It is fundamentally determined by the balance between nuclear charge and the number of surrounding electrons.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Periodic Trends in Cations and Anions

  • Cations are significantly smaller than their parent neutral atoms due to the removal of the valence shell and increased net positive electrostatic pull.
  • Anions are always larger than their parent neutral atoms because electron-electron repulsion expands the electron cloud.
  • Down any group in the periodic table, ionic radii increase consistently due to the addition of principal quantum shells.
  • Across any period from left to right, isoelectronic ionic radii decrease due to increasing nuclear charge ($Z$).
  • Transition metal series exhibit a more gradual decrease in radii compared to s-block elements due to the poor shielding of d-electrons.

Isoelectronic Series and Screening Effect

  • Isoelectronic species contain the same number of electrons but varying nuclear charges ($Z$).
  • Within an isoelectronic series (e.g., N3-, O2-, F-, Na+, Mg2+, Al3+), ionic radius decreases sharply as atomic number increases.
  • The screening effect (or shielding effect) inner-shell electrons diminish the full nuclear charge experienced by valence electrons.
  • Slater's rules provide empirical coefficients ($S$) to calculate the screening constant and determine effective nuclear charge ($Z_{eff}$).
  • d- and f-orbitals exhibit poor shielding capability, resulting in the lanthanide contraction and unexpected ionic radius similarities.

Coordination Number and Radius Ratios

  • The ionic radius of a specific ion increases as its coordination number (CN) increases within a crystal lattice.
  • Higher coordination numbers force more counter-ions into the primary coordination sphere, increasing internuclear repulsion.
  • Radius ratio limits dictate crystal geometries: values between 0.225 and 0.414 typically correspond to tetrahedral coordination.
  • Octahedral coordination geometries are stabilized when the radius ratio falls within the range of 0.414 to 0.732.
  • Cubic coordination (CN = 8) requires a minimum radius ratio threshold of 0.732 to 1.000.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Pauling Ionic Radius Relation
r+ / r- = d / (Zeff*)n
Conditions: For isoelectronic ions sharing noble gas configurations
Units: Picometers (pm) or Angstroms (Å)
Radius Ratio Rule
ρ = rcation / ranion
Conditions: Determines crystal coordination number in ionic solids
Units: Dimensionless ratio
Landé Equation for Interionic Distance
d0 = r+ + r-
Conditions: Additive property in touching hard-sphere ionic lattices
Units: pm
Effective Nuclear Charge
Zeff = Z - S
Conditions: Applicable to multi-electron systems for estimating shielding
Units: Dimensionless (units of elementary charge e)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Ionic radius decreases smoothly down a main group.

Observation: Post-lanthanide elements (e.g., Hf vs Zr) — Lanthanide contraction cancels out the expected size increase due to poor 4f-electron shielding.

Exception Cations are uniformly smaller than anions.

Observation: Large diffuse complex cations compared to small compact anions (e.g., bulky organic onium ions vs F-) — Spatial volume of substituted organic ligands or extremely high principal quantum number overshadows nuclear charge.

Exception Transition metal radii decrease steadily across a period.

Observation: Irregularities in the middle of 3d series (Fe, Co, Ni radii are nearly constant) — Balance between increasing nuclear charge and increased interelectronic repulsion in partially filled d-orbitals.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • In an isoelectronic series, the ion with the highest positive charge possesses the smallest radius.
  • Fluoride ion (F-, 133 pm) is smaller than oxide ion (O2-, 140 pm) due to lower nuclear charge in oxygen.
  • The radius of Li+ is 76 pm, whereas Cs+ is 167 pm, demonstrating group 1 size expansion.
  • Lanthanide contraction results in nearly identical atomic and ionic radii for Zr (160 pm) and Hf (159 pm).
  • Crystal radii scales devised by Shannon and Prewitt account for coordination number and spin state.
  • Polarizing power of a cation increases as its ionic radius decreases (Fajans' rules).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming atomic and ionic radii are identical; ionic radii depend strictly on crystal coordination and oxidation state.
  • Forgetting that isoelectronic species do not follow normal periodic group trends.
  • Neglecting the effect of high spin versus low spin states on transition metal ionic radii.
  • Treating ionic radii as fixed, absolute hard-sphere constants rather than environmentally dependent values.

⚡ Quick Revision: Ionic Radii

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition and Scale Foundations

Ionic radii depend on partitioning interionic distances measured via X-ray diffraction. Pauling and Shannon-Prewitt scales provide standard numerical references.

Periodic and Group Variations

Radii increase down a group due to shell addition and decrease across a period due to rising effective nuclear charge.

Isoelectronic Species Mechanics

For ions with identical electron counts, higher atomic number increases Zeff, shrinking the electron cloud rapidly.

Coordination Number Impacts

Higher coordination number increases electrostatic repulsion and spatial requirements, expanding the effective ionic radius.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Ionic Radius (rion)
Effective distance from nucleus to ion boundary in a lattice, measured in pm.
Isoelectronic (Same e- count)
Species possessing identical total electron counts with varying nuclear charge.
Effective Nuclear Charge (Zeff)
Net positive charge experienced by valence electrons after shielding correction.
Screening Effect (S)
Reduction of true nuclear charge by inner core electrons.
Lanthanide Contraction (4f-shielding failure)
Gradual decrease in atomic/ionic radii across the lanthanide series.
Radius Ratio (ρ = r+/r-)
Ratio determining stable crystal coordination geometry.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Anions > Neutral Atoms > Cations (for the same element).
  • In isoelectronic series, size decreases as nuclear charge increases.
  • Lanthanide contraction causes 4d and 5d series elements to have nearly identical radii.
  • Higher coordination number implies larger effective ionic radius.
  • Polarizing power increases with smaller cation radius and higher charge.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Ionic radius governs physical and thermodynamic properties of salts.
  • Periodic trends mirror atomic trends with specific isoelectronic modifications.
  • Shielding inefficiencies cause contractions like the lanthanide and actinide phenomena.
  • Coordination geometry directly correlates with ionic radius ratios.

⚖️ Key Differences

Atomic Radius VS Ionic Radius

Atomic radius measures neutral single atoms, whereas ionic radius measures charged species in electrostatic fields.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following is the correct order of ionic radii for the isoelectronic species N3-, O2-, F-, Na+, and Mg2+?

Explanation: In an isoelectronic series, size decreases as nuclear charge (Z) increases. N3- has the lowest Z (7) and largest size, while Mg2+ has the highest Z (12) and smallest size.

Question 2 of 19

Why is a cation always smaller than its parent atom?

Explanation: Loss of valence electrons often results in the loss of an entire principal shell, and the remaining electrons experience a higher net positive pull from the nucleus.

Question 3 of 19

Which ion has the largest radius?

Explanation: Down group 1, ionic radius increases with principal quantum number. Cesium is at the bottom of the group.

Question 4 of 19

The phenomenon of lanthanide contraction is primarily due to:

Explanation: 4f electrons provide poor screening, causing Zeff to increase effectively across the series, resulting in a contraction of atomic and ionic radii.

Question 5 of 19

What is the effect of increasing coordination number on ionic radius in a crystal lattice?

Explanation: Higher coordination number means more counter-ions surround the central ion, increasing interionic repulsion and expanding the effective radius.

Question 6 of 19

Which of the following pairs has nearly identical radii due to lanthanide contraction?

Explanation: Zirconium (4d) and Hafnium (5d) exhibit virtually identical radii because the lanthanide contraction precedes hafnium, compensating for the expected shell increase.

Question 7 of 19

In the isoelectronic series Cl-, K+, and Ca2+, the correct order of increasing radius is:

Explanation: All have 18 electrons. Ca2+ has Z=20 (smallest), K+ has Z=19, and Cl- has Z=17 (largest).

Question 8 of 19

An anion is always larger than its parent neutral atom because:

Explanation: Adding electrons to form an anion increases interelectronic repulsion without increasing nuclear charge, causing the electron cloud to expand.

Question 9 of 19

According to radius ratio rules, a radius ratio (ρ) between 0.225 and 0.414 indicates which coordination geometry?

Explanation: A radius ratio of 0.225 to 0.414 corresponds to a coordination number of 4 with a tetrahedral arrangement.

Question 10 of 19

Which transition metal ion possesses the smallest ionic radius in high-spin octahedral coordination?

Explanation: Sc3+ has a higher charge (+3) and lower electron count compared to divalent transition metal ions, resulting in a significantly smaller ionic radius.

Question 11 of 19

Slater's rules are utilized to calculate:

Explanation: Slater's rules provide empirical values for the screening constant (S), allowing estimation of effective nuclear charge Zeff = Z - S.

Question 12 of 19

Which group 2 ion has the smallest ionic radius?

Explanation: Beryllium is at the top of group 2, possessing the fewest electron shells and the smallest ionic radius (31 pm).

Question 13 of 19

Which factor does NOT affect the ionic radius of an ion in a solid lattice?

Explanation: Standard ionic radii tables are compiled at standard conditions; atmospheric pressure does not directly alter internal lattice ionic radii under normal conditions.

Question 14 of 19

Comparing Fe2+ and Fe3+, which statement is correct?

Explanation: Fe3+ has one less electron than Fe2+, resulting in a higher effective nuclear charge per electron and a smaller radius.

Question 15 of 19

The Pauling scale of ionic radii is based on the assumption that:

Explanation: Pauling derived his scale by assuming that for isoelectronic noble-gas-like ions, crystal radii are inversely proportional to effective nuclear charge acting on the outermost shell.

Question 16 of 19

Which halogen anion has the largest ionic radius?

Explanation: Iodide (I-) is at the bottom of group 17, possessing the greatest number of principal shells and largest radius.

Question 17 of 19

The crystal radius of potassium (K+) is larger than that of sodium (Na+) because:

Explanation: Potassium is in period 4 (valence shell n=3), whereas sodium is in period 3 (valence shell n=2), adding a complete quantum shell.

Question 18 of 19

What is the coordination number predicted when the radius ratio is 0.750?

Explanation: A radius ratio between 0.732 and 1.000 corresponds to cubic coordination with a coordination number of 8 (e.g., CsCl structure).

Question 19 of 19

Which of the following has the maximum flocculating power according to Hardy-Schulze rule, relating to ionic size and charge?

Explanation: Al3+ has the highest charge and highest charge-to-radius ratio (polarizing power), giving it maximum flocculating power for negatively charged sols.

Question 1 of 10
Identify the correct statement regarding ionic radii in the periodic table.

Solution: In any isoelectronic series, higher atomic number increases effective nuclear charge, causing cations to decrease in size.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following sets represents correctly ordered ionic radii?

Solution: Halide anions increase in size down the group (I- > F-), and isoelectronic positive ions decrease in size with increasing charge (Na+ > Al3+). Thus both A and C are correct.

Question 3 of 10
The crystal radius of Cl- is 181 pm and of Na+ is 95 pm. What is the approximate internuclear distance (d0) in NaCl crystal lattice?

Solution: According to the additive rule, d0 = rNa+ + rCl- = 95 + 181 = 276 pm.

Question 4 of 10
Why does Zn2+ have a smaller ionic radius than Mg2+ despite having more principal electron shells?

Solution: The 3d electrons in Zn provide poor shielding, so the valence electrons experience a higher effective nuclear charge, compressing the radius.

Question 5 of 10
What is the minimum radius ratio required for a stable octahedral coordination geometry?

Solution: The lower limit for octahedral coordination (CN = 6) in the radius ratio rule is 0.414.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following ions is most effective at polarizing anions according to Fajans' rules?

Solution: Al3+ has a high charge and small ionic radius, resulting in the highest charge-to-radius ratio and maximum polarizing power.

Question 7 of 10
In a given period, transition metal ionic radii show a less marked decrease than s-block ions because:

Solution: The addition of d-electrons provides imperfect screening, but mutual repulsion among d-electrons moderates the net decrease in size across the transition series.

Question 8 of 10
Which species has the largest radius?

Solution: Se2- (selenium with a 2- charge) is a large chalcogenide anion with multiple quantum shells, surpassing the cations and even lighter halide anions.

Question 9 of 10
The unit of ionic radius commonly used in crystallographic literature is:

Solution: Ionic radii are conventionally expressed in picometers (pm) or Angstroms (Å), where 1 Å = 100 pm.

Question 10 of 10
How does hydration enthalpy change with a decrease in alkali metal cation radius?

Solution: Smaller cations have higher charge density, enabling stronger ion-dipole interactions with water molecules, leading to higher (more negative) hydration enthalpy.
