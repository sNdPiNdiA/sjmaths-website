Inorganic Chemistry

p-Block Elements

The p-block elements occupy groups 13 to 18 of the periodic table, where the differentiating electron enters the outermost p-subshell. Their chemistry is characterized by a wide variation in physical and chemical properties, showing clear trends from metallic to non-metallic behavior down the groups.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Group 13 and 14 Anomalous Behavior

  • Boron exhibits diagonal relationship with Silicon due to comparable charge-to-radius ratios.
  • Inert pair effect becomes progressively more prominent down group 13, 14, and 15, stabilizing lower oxidation states (+1 for Tl, +2 for Pb, +3 for Bi).
  • Catenation capacity drops sharply down Group 14 from carbon to lead due to decreasing E-E bond dissociation enthalpies.

Oxoacids and Hydrides of p-Block

  • Acid strength of oxoacids of halogens increases with the oxidation state of the central halogen atom (e.g., HClO < HClO2 < HClO3 < HClO4).
  • Reducing character of group 15 hydrides increases down the group from NH3 to BiH3 due to decreasing E-H bond dissociation enthalpy.
  • Lewis acidity of boron trihalides follows the order BF3 < BCl3 < BBr3 < BI3, contrary to simple electronegativity expectations due to pπ-pπ back bonding.

Noble Gas Compounds and Structures

  • XeF2 adopts a linear geometry with sp3d hybridization and three lone pairs on the equatorial positions.
  • XeF4 exhibits square planar geometry with sp3d2 hybridization and two lone pairs trans to each other.
  • XeO3 possesses a pyramidal structure with sp3 hybridization and one lone pair on the xenon atom.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Electron gain enthalpy becomes more negative down a group

Observation: Fluorine has a less negative electron gain enthalpy than chlorine — Small atomic size of fluorine creates high interelectronic repulsion within its compact 2p orbitals.

Exception Hydride bond angle decreases monotonically down a group as central atom size increases

Observation: NH3 has a bond angle of 107° while PH3 drops sharply to 93.6° — Drago's rule states that in heavier hydrides, central atoms use pure p-orbitals for bonding, leaving s-character in the lone pair.

Exception Boron trifluoride acts as a strong Lewis acid

Observation: BF3 is a weaker Lewis acid towards certain donors compared to BCl3 — Effective intramolecular pπ-pπ overlap between filled 2p orbitals of fluorine and vacant 2p orbital of boron reduces Lewis acidity.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Maximum covalency of boron is 4 due to the absence of d-orbitals in its valence shell.
  • Boric acid (H3BO3) is a Lewis acid that accepts OH- ions rather than acting as a proton donor.
  • Carbon monoxide (CO) forms coordinate bonds with transition metals due to its lone pair on carbon and vacant π* orbitals.
  • Silicates are structured around the fundamental tetrahedral [SiO4]4- building block.
  • White phosphorus consists of discrete tetrahedral P4 molecules with strained bond angles of 60°.
  • Sulfuric acid is manufactured industrially via the Contact process, using V2O5 as a catalyst.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming NH3 is a weaker base than PH3; remember nitrogen's high electronegativity and small size concentrates electron density, making ammonia a stronger Lewis base.
  • Confusing the oxidation state of sulfur in Caro's acid (+6) and Marshall's acid (+6) with peroxide linkages.
  • Forgetting that fluorine exhibits only -1 oxidation state due to the absence of vacant d-orbitals for promotion.
  • Miscalculating the hybridisation of xenon in coordination complexes by ignoring non-bonding electron pairs.

⚡ Quick Revision: p-Block Elements

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Periodic Trends in p-Block

Ionisation enthalpy and electronegativity decrease down the groups, while metallic character increases, leading to metalloids in groups 13-16.

Boron and Carbon Families

Group 13 features electron-deficient hydrides (boranes) and Lewis acidity, whereas Group 14 is dominated by catenation and stable tetravalent compounds.

Pnictogens and Chalcogens

Nitrogen and oxygen form strong multiple bonds (pπ-pπ), whereas heavier congeners form weaker multiple bonds and prefer single covalent networks.

Halogens and Noble Gases

Halogens exhibit high oxidising power and variable oxoacids, while heavier noble gases form stable fluorides and oxides due to low ionisation energies.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Catenation (Self-linkage)
Ability of an element to form chains of identical atoms via covalent bonds.
Inert Pair Effect (s-orbital persistence)
Reluctance of ns2 electrons to participate in bonding down heavier p-block groups.
Back Bonding (pπ-pπ overlap)
Donor interaction from a filled p-orbital of one atom to a vacant p-orbital of an adjacent atom.
Allotropy (Structural forms)
Existence of an element in two or more different physical forms in the same phase.
Drago's Rule (Hybridisation restriction)
Elements of group 15 and 16 in period 3 and below form hydrides using pure p-orbitals.
Disproportionation (Self-redox)
A single chemical species is simultaneously oxidized and reduced.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Boron trihalide Lewis acidity: BF3 < BCl3 < BBr3 < BI3
  • Hydride reducing power: NH3 < PH3 < AsH3 < SbH3 < BiH3
  • Thermal stability of carbonates: Increases down Group 2 and Group 1
  • Hydrolysis of XeF6 yields XeOF4, XeO2F2, and finally XeO3
  • Concentrated HNO3 passivates iron and chromium by forming a protective oxide layer.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Anomalous behavior of period 2 elements is due to small size, high electronegativity, and absence of d-orbitals.
  • Lewis acidic character of group 13 halides is dictated by steric and back-bonding factors.
  • Group 16 elements show extensive allotropy, with oxygen and sulfur being most prominent.
  • Noble gases are no longer chemically inert; Xe and Kr form stable fluorides and oxides.

⚖️ Key Differences

Electronegativity VS Electron Gain Enthalpy

Electronegativity is a tendency to attract shared electrons in a bond, whereas electron gain enthalpy is the energy released when an isolated gaseous atom gains an electron.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following boron halides is the strongest Lewis acid?

Explanation: BI3 is the strongest Lewis acid because iodine has the poorest pπ-pπ overlap with boron, leaving the boron atom electron-deficient and reactive towards donors.

Question 2 of 18

The shape of XeF4 according to VSEPR theory is:

Explanation: XeF4 has 4 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs on xenon, adopting an octahedral electronic geometry and square planar molecular geometry.

Question 3 of 18

Which hydride of group 15 has the lowest boiling point?

Explanation: PH3 lacks intermolecular hydrogen bonding, giving it a significantly lower boiling point than NH3, while AsH3 and SbH3 have higher boiling points due to increased molecular mass and van der Waals forces.

Question 4 of 18

The oxidation state of sulfur in peroxodisulfuric acid (Marshall's acid) is:

Explanation: In H2S2O8, sulfur atoms are bonded in a +6 oxidation state, featuring a peroxy linkage (-O-O-) between the two sulfur centers.

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following haloids does not exist?

Explanation: Trichloride of chlorine (Cl3) does not exist as a stable neutral molecule due to steric crowding and electronic constraints.

Question 6 of 18

White phosphorus reacts with caustic soda (NaOH) to form phosphine and:

Explanation: P4 undergoes disproportionation in alkaline medium to yield PH3 and sodium hypophosphite (NaH2PO2).

Question 7 of 18

The correct order of acidic strength of halogen acids is:

Explanation: Acid strength increases down the group as H-X bond dissociation enthalpy decreases, making HI the strongest acid.

Question 8 of 18

Which element shows maximum catenation property in Group 14?

Explanation: Carbon shows maximum catenation due to its small size and high C-C bond enthalpy.

Question 9 of 18

Silica (SiO2) dissolves in which of the following reagents?

Explanation: HF reacts with silica to form volatile silicon tetrafluoride and hexafluorosilicic acid: SiO2 + 4HF → SiF4 + 2H2O.

Question 10 of 18

The hybridization of Xe in XeO3 is:

Explanation: XeO3 has 3 sigma bonds and 1 lone pair, leading to sp3 hybridization and a pyramidal structure.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following oxides is neutral?

Explanation: Carbon monoxide (CO) is a neutral oxide, whereas CO2, SO2, and NO2 are acidic oxides.

Question 12 of 18

The gas evolved on heating ammonium dichromate is:

Explanation: Thermal decomposition of ammonium dichromate yields nitrogen gas, chromium(III) oxide, and water vapor.

Question 13 of 18

Which halogen has the highest electron gain enthalpy (most negative)?

Explanation: Chlorine has the most negative electron gain enthalpy because fluorine's small 2p orbitals suffer from interelectronic repulsion upon accepting an extra electron.

Question 14 of 18

The shape of ClF3 is:

Explanation: ClF3 has 3 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs, resulting in a T-shaped molecular geometry due to lone pair-lone pair repulsions.

Question 15 of 18

Which allotrope of phosphorus is thermodynamically most stable at room temperature?

Explanation: Black phosphorus has a layered puckered structure and is the most thermodynamically stable allotrope of phosphorus.

Question 16 of 18

The highest oxidation state exhibited by osmium and ruthenium in p-block or transition elements is:

Explanation: Osmium and ruthenium reach an oxidation state of +8 in tetroxides (OsO4, RuO4).

Question 17 of 18

Which of the following acids is known as oil of vitriol?

Explanation: Sulfuric acid was historically prepared from green vitriol and is commonly referred to as oil of vitriol.

Question 18 of 18

The number of bridging oxygen atoms in P4O10 is:

Explanation: P4O10 contains 6 P-O-P bridging oxygen atoms and 4 terminal P=O bonds in its cage structure.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary reason for the inert pair effect in heavier p-block elements?

Solution: Poor screening by intervening d and f orbitals contracts and holds the valence ns2 electrons tightly, preventing them from participating in bonding.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following oxoacids of chlorine is the strongest?

Solution: HClO4 has the highest oxidation state (+7) and maximum number of oxygen atoms, stabilising the resulting perchlorate anion through charge dispersal.

Question 3 of 10
The number of pπ-dπ bonds in SO3 molecule is:

Solution: Sulfur uses its d-orbitals to overlap with the p-orbitals of oxygen, forming three pπ-dπ bonds in addition to three σ bonds in SO3.

Question 4 of 10
Which element forms an amphoteric oxide?

Solution: Tin (Sn) and lead (Pb) form amphoteric oxides (SnO, SnO2), dissolving in both acids and bases.

Question 5 of 10
The hydrolysis of PCl3 yields:

Solution: PCl3 undergoes hydrolysis to produce phosphorous acid (H3PO3) and hydrochloric acid (HCl).

Question 6 of 10
Which interhalogen compound has a pentagonal bipyramidal structure?

Solution: IF7 possesses 7 bonding pairs and 0 lone pairs around iodine, resulting in pentagonal bipyramidal geometry with sp3d3 hybridization.

Question 7 of 10
What is the color and physical state of fluorine at room temperature?

Solution: Fluorine is a pale yellow to greenish-yellow toxic gas at standard temperature and pressure.

Question 8 of 10
Which compound is formed when bleaching powder reacts with dilute acids?

Solution: Action of dilute acids on bleaching powder releases free chlorine gas, which acts as the bleaching and oxidizing agent.

Question 9 of 10
The structure of white phosphorus consists of:

Solution: White phosphorus is composed of discrete tetrahedral P4 molecules held together by weak van der Waals forces.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following species acts both as an oxidizing and a reducing agent?

Solution: Sulfur in SO2 has an intermediate oxidation state of +4, allowing it to be oxidized to +6 or reduced to lower states.
