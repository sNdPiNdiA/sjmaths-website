Inorganic Chemistry

Phosphorus Compounds

Phosphorus forms numerous hydrides, oxides, oxoacids, and halides exhibiting variable oxidation states from -3 to +5. The chemistry of phosphorus is dominated by its tendency to form strong P=O bonds and stable tetrahedral structures.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Allotropes and Hydrides of Phosphorus

  • White phosphorus exists as discrete tetrahedral P4 molecules with strained bond angles of 60°, rendering it highly reactive and chemiluminescent.
  • Red phosphorus is polymeric with a chain-like structure, possessing higher stability and lower solubility in carbon disulfide than white phosphorus.
  • Phosphine (PH3) is a colorless, highly toxic gas with a rotten fish odor, prepared by the action of water or dilute acids on metal phosphides.
  • Phosphine acts as a weak Lewis base and forms coordination complexes, but it is less basic than ammonia due to low s-character of the lone pair orbital.

Halides and Oxides of Phosphorus

  • Phosphorus trichloride (PCl3) has a pyramidal geometry (sp3 hybridization) and fumes in moist air due to hydrolysis yielding HCl.
  • Phosphorus pentachloride (PCl5) exhibits a trigonal bipyramidal geometry in the gaseous and liquid state (sp3d hybridization).
  • In the solid state, PCl5 exists as an ionic crystal lattice composed of [PCl4]+ (tetrahedral) and [PCl6]- (octahedral) ions.
  • Phosphorus trioxide (P4O6) and phosphorus pentoxide (P4O10) are acidic anhydrides corresponding to phosphorous and phosphoric acids.

Oxoacids and Structure of Phosphorus Acids

  • Phosphorus oxoacids contain tetrahedral phosphorus surrounded by oxo groups, terminal hydroxyl (-OH) groups, and direct P-H bonds.
  • The reducing property of phosphorus oxoacids is directly proportional to the number of hydrogen atoms directly bonded to phosphorus (P-H bonds).
  • Hypophosphorous acid (H3PO2) is a monoprotic acid with two P-H bonds, whereas phosphorous acid (H3PO3) is diprotic with one P-H bond.
  • Orthophosphoric acid (H3PO4) is triprotic with no P-H bonds, meaning it possesses zero reducing capability.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Halides of group 15 elements should all be stable penta-halides.

Observation: Nitrogen does not form NF5, and phosphorus forms PCl5, PBr5 (ionic), but does not form PI5. — Nitrogen lacks vacant d-orbitals for expansion of octet. Iodine is too large and bulky, and its weak oxidizing character cannot polarize the large iodide ion to form stable pentahalide bonds.

Exception Hydrides of group 15 elements should show regular increase in boiling points down the group due to increasing molecular mass.

Observation: Ammonia (NH3) has an anomalously high boiling point compared to PH3, AsH3, and SbH3. — Presence of extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding in liquid ammonia, which is absent in the other heavier hydrides.

Exception Basicity of group 15 hydrides should increase down the group as size increases and charge density decreases.

Observation: Ammonia is a stronger Lewis base than phosphine (PH3). — The lone pair in PH3 resides in an orbital with high s-character, making it less available for donation compared to the sp3 hybridized lone pair of ammonia.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
P-H Bond Counting for Reducing Power
Reducing power ∝ Number of P-H bonds

Application: Quickly identify the number of P-H bonds from the chemical formula: H3PO2 has 2 (strong reducing agent), H3PO3 has 1 (moderate), H3PO4 has 0 (non-reducing).

Basicity of Oxoacids Rule
Basicity = Number of ionizable P-OH groups

Application: Count the number of -OH groups attached directly to phosphorus; ignore hydrogens attached directly to phosphorus (P-H) when determining basicity.

PCl5 Solid State Ionization
2PCl5(s) → [PCl4]+ + [PCl6]-

Application: Remember that solid PCl5 is not molecular; it consists of tetrahedral cation and octahedral anion.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • White phosphorus is kept under water to prevent its spontaneous combustion in air, as its ignition temperature is only 30°C.
  • Phosphine gas (PH3) spontaneously catches fire in air due to the presence of impurity diphosphine (P2H4).
  • PCl3 is liquid at room temperature while PCl5 is a covalent solid that sublimes upon heating.
  • Hypophosphorous acid (H3PO2) contains one P-OH, two P-H, and one P=O bond, making it a monobasic acid.
  • Orthophosphoric acid on strong heating at 600°C gives metaphosphoric acid (HPO3)n.
  • P4O10 is an extremely powerful dehydrating agent and reacts vigorously with water to form H3PO4.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming all H atoms in H3PO2 or H3PO3 are acidic; only H atoms attached to oxygen are ionizable.
  • Believing that PCl5 maintains its trigonal bipyramidal molecular structure in the solid state.
  • Confusing white and red phosphorus solubilities; white phosphorus is soluble in CS2 while red is not.
  • Forgetting that pure PH3 is non-flammable; spontaneous ignition is entirely due to P2H4 traces.

⚡ Quick Revision: Phosphorus Compounds

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Allotropy and Structure

Phosphorus exhibits extensive allotropy, with white phosphorus consisting of discrete tetrahedral P4 units with strained bonds, whereas red phosphorus has a stable polymeric linked-tetrahedral network.

Hydrides and Basicity

Phosphine (PH3) is produced via metal phosphide hydrolysis or white phosphorus disproportionation, acting as a weaker base than ammonia due to high s-character of its lone pair.

Halide Structures

Phosphorus forms trihalides (pyramidal) and pentahalides (trigonal bipyramidal in gas, ionic [PCl4]+[PCl6]- in solid), showing versatile bonding capabilities via vacant d-orbitals.

Oxoacids and Reducing Property

Oxoacids of phosphorus feature tetrahedral central atoms with varying numbers of P-OH, P=O, and P-H bonds; the presence of P-H bonds imparts strong reducing character.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Phosphine (PH3)
Hydride of phosphorus with rotten fish odor and weak basic properties.
White Phosphorus (P4)
Waxy, toxic, highly reactive allotrope with 60° bond angles.
Red Phosphorus ((P4)n)
Polymeric, non-toxic, stable allotrope of phosphorus.
Phosphorus Pentachloride (PCl5)
Chlorinating agent existing as an ionic solid [PCl4]+[PCl6]-.
Orthophosphoric Acid (H3PO4)
Triprotic oxoacid of phosphorus containing no P-H bonds.
Hypophosphorous Acid (H3PO2)
Monoprotic oxoacid with two P-H bonds acting as a strong reducing agent.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • White P4 molecule has bond angle of 60° and high ring strain.
  • Solid PCl5 is ionic: [PCl4]+ [PCl6]-.
  • H3PO2 is monobasic, H3PO3 is dibasic, H3PO4 is tribasic.
  • Reducing power order: H3PO2 > H3PO3 > H3PO4.
  • White phosphorus reacts with caustic soda to give PH3 and NaH2PO2.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Phosphorus displays variable oxidation states from -3 to +5.
  • Allotrope stability follows Red > White in thermodynamic terms.
  • Halides readily hydrolyze to yield corresponding acids and HCl.
  • Oxoacids contain tetrahedral P centers with P=O, P-OH, and P-H linkages.

⚖️ Key Differences

H3PO3 VS H3PO4

H3PO3 has one P-H bond and is dibasic/reducing; H3PO4 has zero P-H bonds and is tribasic/non-reducing.

Explanation: H3PO3 has two ionizable -OH groups attached to phosphorus, making it dibasic.

Explanation: White phosphorus undergoes disproportionation with NaOH to form phosphine (PH3) and sodium hypophosphite (NaH2PO2).

Explanation: PCl3 has sp3 hybridization with three bond pairs and one lone pair, resulting in a pyramidal molecular geometry.

Explanation: Hypophosphoric acid (H4P2O6) contains a direct P-P covalent linkage in its structure.

Question 6 of 19

The hybridization of phosphorus in gaseous PCl5 is:

Explanation: In gaseous and liquid states, PCl5 molecules possess trigonal bipyramidal geometry corresponding to sp3d hybridization.

Question 7 of 19

Why does white phosphorus glow in the dark?

Explanation: White phosphorus undergoes slow oxidation in air (chemiluminescence), emitting a greenish glow in the dark.

Question 8 of 19

Solid PCl5 exists as:

Explanation: In the solid state, PCl5 rearranges into an ionic lattice containing tetrahedral [PCl4]+ cations and octahedral [PCl6]- anions.

Question 9 of 19

Which of the following is a strong reducing agent?

Explanation: H3PO2 contains two P-H bonds, imparting strong reducing properties.

Question 10 of 19

Phosphine is prepared in the laboratory by the action of water or dilute HCl on:

Explanation: Both calcium phosphide (Ca3P2) and aluminum phosphide (AlP) react with water or dilute acids to liberate phosphine.

Question 11 of 19

What is the coordination number of phosphorus in P4O10?

Explanation: In P4O10, each phosphorus atom is bonded to four oxygen atoms in a tetrahedral arrangement, giving a coordination number of 4.

Question 12 of 19

Which of the following hydrides has the lowest boiling point?

Explanation: PH3 lacks hydrogen bonding and has a lower molecular mass than AsH3 and SbH3, resulting in the lowest boiling point.

Question 13 of 19

The number of P-O-P bridges in pyrophosphoric acid (H4P2O7) is:

Explanation: Pyrophosphoric acid has the structure (OH)2P(=O)-O-P(=O)(OH)2, containing exactly one P-O-P linkage.

Question 14 of 19

Which phosphorus halide is thermally most stable?

Explanation: PF3 has the highest thermal stability due to strong P-F bond energy.

Question 15 of 19

The bond angle in white phosphorus P4 molecule is:

Explanation: The P4 molecule forms a regular tetrahedron with strained bond angles of 60°.

Question 16 of 19

Cyclic metaphosphoric acid (HPO3)n where n = 3 is known as:

Explanation: Cyclic trimer of metaphosphoric acid, (HPO3)3, is called trimetaphosphoric acid.

Question 17 of 19

Spontaneous ignition of phosphine gas in air is caused by traces of:

Explanation: Diphosphine (P2H4) impurity present in phosphine is spontaneously flammable in air and triggers the ignition of PH3.

Question 18 of 19

Which oxoacid has a +1 oxidation state for phosphorus?

Explanation: In H3PO2, assigning oxidation numbers (H = +1, O = -2) yields an oxidation state of +1 for phosphorus.

Question 19 of 19

Phosphorus pentoxide (P4O10) is widely used as:

Explanation: P4O10 has a very high affinity for moisture and serves as a powerful dehydrating agent.
