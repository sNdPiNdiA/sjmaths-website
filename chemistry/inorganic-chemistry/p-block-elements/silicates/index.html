Inorganic Chemistry

Silicates

Silicates are chemical compounds consisting of the fundamental structural unit [SiO4]4- tetrahedra, where silicon is sp3 hybridized. They form the primary constituent of the Earth's crust and mantle, exhibiting diverse structural classifications based on oxygen sharing.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Fundamental Structural Unit and Classification

  • The building block of all silicates is the [SiO4]4- tetrahedral unit with silicon at the center and four oxygen atoms at the corners.
  • Silicates are classified into nesosilicates, sorosilicates, cyclosilicates, inosilicates, phyllosilicates, and tectosilicates based on the number of shared corner oxygens.
  • In orthosilicates (e.g., olivine, phenacite), independent tetrahedra are linked exclusively by ionic bonds with interstitial metal cations.
  • In sorosilicates (e.g., thortveitite), two tetrahedra share a single bridging oxygen atom, yielding the [Si2O7]6- unit.

Chain, Ring, and Sheet Silicate Architectures

  • Cyclosilicates (e.g., beryl) are formed by linking $n$ number of tetrahedra into closed rings with the general formula [(SiO3)n]2n-.
  • Inosilicates include pyroxenes (single chains sharing two oxygens, Si:O = 1:3) and amphiboles (double chains sharing alternating two and three oxygens, Si:O = 4:11).
  • Phyllosilicates (e.g., talc, micas, kaolinite) share three oxygen atoms per tetrahedron, producing infinite two-dimensional silicate sheets with perfect basal cleavage.
  • The negative charge of sheet silicates is balanced by interlayer cations such as K+, Mg2+, or Al3+.

Three-Dimensional Frameworks and Industrial Silicates

  • Tectosilicates (e.g., quartz, feldspatoids, zeolites) involve sharing all four corner oxygen atoms of every [SiO4]4- tetrahedron.
  • Isomorphous substitution of Si4+ by Al3+ in tectosilicates generates a net negative charge, compensated by alkali or alkaline earth metal ions like Na+, K+, or Ca2+.
  • Zeolites are hydrated aluminosilicates possessing a microporous 3D framework utilized extensively for shape-selective catalysis and water softening.
  • Glass is an amorphous silicate network modified by fluxes like Na2O and stabilizers like CaO to lower melting points and increase chemical durability.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Silicon typically maintains a strict coordination number of 4 with oxygen in normal silicates.

Observation: High-pressure polymorphs of silica (e.g., stishovite) — Extreme lithostatic pressure forces silicon into a 6-coordinate octahedral geometry with oxygen.

Exception Pure silica (SiO2) network consists entirely of neutral corner-sharing tetrahedra.

Observation: Aluminosilicates (Feldspars) — Substitution of Si4+ by Al3+ introduces a localized negative charge requiring balancing framework cations.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The fundamental structural unit of all silicates is the tetrahedral [SiO4]4- ion.
  • Olivine belongs to nesosilicates and contains discrete [SiO4]4- units linked by Mg2+/Fe2+ ions.
  • Beryl is a cyclosilicate featuring rings composed of six [SiO4]4- tetrahedra sharing two corners each ([Si6O18]12-).
  • Talc and mica possess sheet (phyllosilicate) structures with a Si:O stoichiometric ratio of 2:5.
  • Ultramarine is a complex framework aluminosilicate containing trapped sulfur anions.
  • Stishovite is a high-pressure form of silica where silicon is hexacoordinated.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the Si:O ratio of pyroxenes (1:3) with that of amphiboles (4:11).
  • Assuming all oxygen atoms in a sorosilicate are shared; only 1 oxygen per 2 tetrahedra is shared.
  • Forgetting that isomorphous substitution of Si4+ by Al3+ in tectosilicates requires extra-framework cations for electrical neutrality.
  • Treating glass as a crystalline silicate rather than an amorphous supercooled liquid.

⚡ Quick Revision: Silicates

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Tetrahedral Building Block

Silicates are built from [SiO4]4- units where silicon exhibits sp3 hybridization and tetrahedral geometry.

Polymerization Degree

The structural classification depends strictly on the number of corner oxygen atoms shared between adjacent silicate tetrahedra.

Layered and Fibrous Mechanics

Sheet silicates exhibit cleavage due to weak interlayer bonding, whereas chain silicates confer fibrous habits.

Framework Modification

Substitution of silicon by aluminum creates active sites in framework aluminosilicates crucial for catalytic and ion-exchange behavior.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Nesosilicate ([SiO4]4-)
Ortho-silicate containing isolated independent tetrahedra.
Sorosilicate ([Si2O7]6-)
Pyrosilicate containing two tetrahedra sharing one oxygen atom.
Cyclosilicate ([(SiO3)n]2n-)
Ring silicate formed by closed loops of tetrahedra.
Inosilicate ((SiO3)n or (Si4O11)n6n-)
Chain silicate forming single or double infinite ribbons.
Phyllosilicate ((Si2O5)n2n-)
Sheet silicate with three shared oxyhedra per tetrahedron.
Tectosilicate ((SiO2)n)
Framework silicate with all four oxygens shared.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Basic unit: [SiO4]4- tetrahedron
  • Si:O ratio in orthosilicates: 1:4
  • Si:O ratio in tectosilicates: 1:2
  • Pyroxenes possess single chains; Amphiboles possess double chains
  • Stishovite has 6-coordinate silicon

📌 Quick Recall

  • Silicates dominate the Earth's crust composition.
  • Polymerization increases from discrete units to 3D networks.
  • Shared oxygens dictate mechanical and chemical properties.
  • Aluminosilicates incorporate charge-balancing cations.

⚖️ Key Differences

Pyroxenes VS Amphiboles

Pyroxenes are single-chain silicates (Si:O = 1:3), whereas amphiboles are double-chain silicates (Si:O = 4:11).

What is the structural formula of the anion in orthosilicates?

Question 2 of 18

How many oxygen atoms are shared per tetrahedron in a sheet silicate (phyllosilicate)?

Explanation: In phyllosilicates, three corner oxygen atoms of each tetrahedron are shared with adjacent tetrahedra, forming 2D sheets.

Question 3 of 18

Which of the following is an example of a cyclic silicate?

Explanation: Beryl (Be3Al2Si6O18) is a cyclosilicate containing rings of six tetrahedra.

Question 4 of 18

The Si : O stoichiometric ratio in tectosilicates is:

Explanation: In tectosilicates, all four oxygens are shared, yielding the composition SiO2, hence a 1:2 ratio.

Question 5 of 18

Which silicate mineral class exhibits a double chain structure?

Explanation: Amphiboles are double-chain inosilicates where two single chains link by sharing additional oxygen atoms.

Question 6 of 18

What is the coordination number of silicon in stishovite, a high-pressure form of silica?

Explanation: Under extreme lithostatic pressure, silica polymorph stishovite adopts a rutile-like structure where silicon is hexacoordinated (CN = 6).

Question 7 of 18

In pyroxenes, the general formula of the silicate chain anion is:

Explanation: Pyroxenes are single-chain silicates with the repeating unit (SiO3)n2n-.

Question 8 of 18

Which metal ions are typically found in olivine?

Explanation: Olivine is an orthosilicate with the formula (Mg, Fe)2SiO4 containing magnesium and iron(II) cations.

Question 9 of 18

What structural feature accounts for the perfect basal cleavage observed in micas?

Explanation: Micas are sheet silicates (phyllosilicates) where individual sheets are bound together by weak electrostatic interactions with interlayer cations like K+.

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following is a pyrosilicate containing the [Si2O7]6- anion?

Explanation: Thortveitite (Sc2Si2O7) is a classic example of a sorosilicate containing the pyrosilicate ion.

Question 11 of 18

What is the hybridization of silicon in the fundamental [SiO4]4- unit?

Explanation: Silicon forms four sigma bonds with oxygen in a tetrahedral arrangement, corresponding to sp3 hybridization.

Question 12 of 18

Zeolites are widely used as catalysts because of their:

Explanation: Zeolites possess regular porous frameworks that allow molecular sieving and shape-selective catalysis.

Question 13 of 18

What is the Si : O ratio in sorosilicates?

Explanation: Sorosilicates feature two tetrahedra sharing one oxygen, giving the formula [Si2O7]6-, which corresponds to a 2:7 ratio.

Question 14 of 18

Which mineral belongs to the tectosilicate family?

Explanation: Feldspars (such as orthoclase and albite) are framework tectosilicates.

Question 15 of 18

What happens when Si4+ is replaced by Al3+ in a tectosilicate framework?

Explanation: Substitution of a tetravalent silicon by a trivalent aluminum introduces a deficit of positive charge, balanced by extra-framework alkali cations.

Question 16 of 18

Asbestos minerals belong to which silicate category?

Explanation: Asbestos minerals are fibrous amphibole or serpentine inosilicates.

Question 17 of 18

Which of the following has zero shared oxygen atoms per tetrahedron?

Explanation: Olivine is an orthosilicate containing completely isolated [SiO4]4- tetrahedra with zero shared oxygens.

Question 18 of 18

The formula of kaolinite, a clay mineral, is typically represented as:

Explanation: Kaolinite is a hydrous aluminum phyllosilicate with the formula Al2Si2O5(OH)4.

Question 1 of 10
What is the structural classification of a silicate having the anion formula [Si2O7]6-?

Solution: The [Si2O7]6- anion consists of two tetrahedra sharing one oxygen atom, defining a sorosilicate (pyrosilicate).

Question 2 of 10
Which silicate class contains continuous infinite two-dimensional sheets?

Solution: Phyllosilicates share three oxygen atoms per tetrahedron, forming infinite 2D sheets.

Question 3 of 10
What is the Si : O ratio in a cyclic silicate ring composed of six tetrahedra ([Si6O18]12-)?

Solution: For [Si6O18]12-, the ratio of Si to O is 6 : 18, which simplifies to 1 : 3.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following is a characteristic property of amphibole silicates?

Solution: Amphiboles are characterized by double-chain silicate structures.

Question 5 of 10
In which mineral does silicon exhibit an octahedral coordination number (CN = 6)?

Solution: Stishovite is a high-pressure polymorph of SiO2 featuring hexacoordinated silicon.

Question 6 of 10
What type of linkage exists between tetrahedra in ultramarines and zeolites?

Solution: Zeolites and ultramarines are tectosilicates possessing an open three-dimensional framework.

Question 7 of 10
The number of shared oxygen atoms per tetrahedron in an inosilicate single chain is:

Solution: In single-chain inosilicates (pyroxenes), each tetrahedron shares two oxygen atoms with neighboring tetrahedra.

Question 8 of 10
Which ion is responsible for balancing the charge when aluminum substitutes for silicon in feldspars?

Solution: Monovalent alkali metal ions such as Na+ and K+ or divalent alkaline earth ions like Ca2+ balance the negative charge introduced by Al3+ substitution.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following minerals is classified under nesosilicates?

Solution: Zircon (ZrSiO4) contains discrete [SiO4]4- units, classifying it as a nesosilicate.

Question 10 of 10
What is the general stoichiometric formula representing amphibole chain silicate anions?

Solution: Amphiboles feature double chains with the characteristic anion stoichiometry of (Si4O11)n6n-.
