Inorganic Chemistry

Hydrides

Hydrides are binary compounds formed by hydrogen with elements of lower or similar electronegativity across the periodic table. They are classified into ionic, covalent, and metallic hydrides based on bonding characteristics and electronegativity differences.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification and Bonding of Hydrides

  • Saline or ionic hydrides are formed by s-block elements (except Be and Mg) and possess high melting points as crystalline solids.
  • Covalent or molecular hydrides are formed by p-block elements and are further subdivided into electron-precise, electron-deficient, and electron-rich categories.
  • Metallic or interstitial hydrides are formed by d-block and f-block transition metals where hydrogen occupies interstitial sites in the metal lattice.
  • Polymeric hydrides such as BeH2 and MgH2 exhibit bridge bonding through hydrogen atoms.

Periodic Trends in Hydride Properties

  • Thermal stability of group 17 hydrides decreases down the group due to a decrease in M-H bond dissociation enthalpy.
  • Acidic strength of group 17 hydrides increases down the group (HF < HCl < HBr < HI) owing to decreasing bond strength.
  • Basic strength of group 15 hydrides decreases down the group (NH3 > PH3 > AsH3 > SbH3) as the electron density on the central atom decreases.
  • Boiling points of hydrides of groups 15, 16, and 17 show anomalous high values for the first element (NH3, H2O, HF) due to intermolecular hydrogen bonding.

Chemical Reactivity and Stoichiometry

  • Ionic hydrides react violently with water to liberate dihydrogen gas and form metal hydroxides.
  • Electron-deficient hydrides like diborane (B2H6) act as Lewis acids and react readily with Lewis bases like amines to form adducts.
  • Transition metal interstitial hydrides often exhibit variable stoichiometry and are non-stoichiometric in nature.
  • Saline hydrides act as strong reducing agents and find extensive use in organic synthesis for generating complex reducing species like LiAlH4.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkaline earth metals form ionic hydrides

Observation: Beryllium hydride (BeH2) and magnesium hydride (MgH2) are covalent and polymeric — High charge density and polarizing power of Be2+ and Mg2+ cause partial covalent character through orbital polarization.

Exception Boiling points increase down a group as molecular weight increases due to van der Waals forces

Observation: NH3, H2O, and HF have abnormally high boiling points compared to subsequent hydrides in their respective groups — Presence of strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding in the first-row hydrides.

Exception Hydrides of group 13 are electron-precise or electron-rich

Observation: Group 13 hydrides like diborane are electron-deficient — Central boron atoms possess fewer valence electrons than required for conventional two-center two-electron bonds, leading to 3c-2e banana bonds.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Saline hydrides conduct electricity in the molten state and evolve hydrogen gas at the anode.
  • Beryllium hydride is prepared by the reaction of beryllium chloride with lithium aluminum hydride.
  • Diborane contains two terminal B-H bonds and two bridging B-H-B banana bonds.
  • Water has a maximum boiling point in group 16 hydrides due to an extensive three-dimensional hydrogen-bonded network.
  • Transition metal hydrides are often non-stoichiometric, historically termed metallic hydrides.
  • Hydrazine (N2H4) and hydrogen peroxide are examples of covalent hydrides with functional heteroatom linkages.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming all s-block hydrides are purely ionic; beryllium and magnesium form polymeric covalent hydrides.
  • Confusing the acidic trend of group 17 hydrides with their thermal stability trend.
  • Forgetting that transition metal hydrides often show non-stoichiometry and variable composition.
  • Failing to recognize that diborane has bridging hydrogens holding 3-center 2-electron bonds.

⚡ Quick Revision: Hydrides

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Ionic Hydrides

Formed by highly electropositive s-block metals, displaying salt-like properties and high melting points with hydrogen present as hydride ion (H-).

Covalent Hydrides

Formed by p-block elements featuring molecular structures categorized into electron-precise, electron-deficient, and electron-rich types based on valence octets.

Metallic Hydrides

Formed by d- and f-block transition metals where hydrogen occupies interstitial sites, exhibiting metallic luster and variable stoichiometry.

Hydrogen Bonding in Hydrides

Significant association through intermolecular hydrogen bonding in NH3, H2O, and HF leads to anomalous physical properties like high boiling points.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Saline Hydride (MH)
Ionic compound containing hydride ion.
Interstitial Hydride (MHx)
Metallic hydride with hydrogen in lattice voids.
Banana Bond (3c-2e Bond)
Three-center two-electron bond found in diborane.
Electron-Deficient Hydride (e.g., B2H6)
Hydride with fewer electrons than needed for Lewis octets.
Electron-Rich Hydride (e.g., NH3, H2O)
Hydride possessing lone pairs of electrons on the central atom.
Electron-Precise Hydride (e.g., CH4)
Hydride with exact number of electrons for covalent bonds.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Thermal stability of group 17 hydrides decreases from HF to HI.
  • Acidic strength increases down group 17 and group 16.
  • BeH2 and MgH2 are polymeric and covalent.
  • Diborane features 3-center 2-electron bonds.
  • Saline hydrides react with water to give H2 gas.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Hydrides are classified into ionic, covalent, and metallic types.
  • Periodic trends govern thermal stability, acidic strength, and boiling points.
  • Anomalous properties of first-row hydrides stem from hydrogen bonding.
  • Electron deficiency in group 13 hydrides results in bridging structures.

⚖️ Key Differences

Thermal Stability VS Acidic Strength

Thermal stability decreases down a group, whereas acidic strength increases down the same group.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following hydrides is ionic in nature?

Explanation: Sodium is an alkali metal and forms an ionic (saline) hydride, NaH, whereas BeH2 is covalent/polymeric, and CH4 and NH3 are covalent molecular hydrides.

Question 2 of 19

Which hydride exhibits electron-deficient bonding?

Explanation: Diborane (B2H6) is an electron-deficient hydride possessing three-center two-electron (3c-2e) banana bonds.

Question 3 of 19

What is the correct order of acidic strength for group 17 hydrides?

Explanation: Acidic strength increases down group 17 due to a decrease in H-X bond dissociation enthalpy.

Question 4 of 19

Which of the following elements does NOT form a metallic hydride?

Explanation: Group 7, 8, and 9 transition metals (except Cr) do not form hydrides (the 'hydride gap'). Copper also does not form stable interstitial hydrides.

Question 5 of 19

The unusual high boiling point of water is due to:

Explanation: Water molecules associate extensively through intermolecular hydrogen bonding, causing an abnormally high boiling point.

Question 6 of 19

What type of hydride is beryllium hydride (BeH2)?

Explanation: Due to high polarizing power, beryllium forms a polymeric covalent hydride containing hydrogen bridges.

Question 7 of 19

When saline hydrides react with water, the gas evolved is:

Explanation: Saline hydrides react with water to yield metal hydroxide and dihydrogen gas (H2).

Question 8 of 19

Which group 15 hydride has the highest basic strength?

Explanation: Ammonia (NH3) has the highest basic strength because nitrogen has a high electron density and localized lone pair.

Question 9 of 19

In diborane (B2H6), how many terminal hydrogen atoms are present?

Explanation: Diborane has 4 terminal hydrogen atoms and 2 bridging hydrogen atoms.

Question 10 of 19

Which of the following hydrides is electron-rich?

Explanation: Ammonia (NH3) contains a lone pair of electrons on the central nitrogen atom, making it an electron-rich hydride.

Question 11 of 19

Which element forms stoichiometric interstitial hydrides?

Explanation: Palladium forms nearly stoichiometric hydrides under specific conditions, whereas many other d-block hydrides are non-stoichiometric.

Question 12 of 19

The thermal stability of group 16 hydrides follows the order:

Explanation: Thermal stability decreases down the group as the M-H bond strength decreases with increasing atomic size.

Question 13 of 19

Which of the following represents an electron-precise hydride?

Explanation: Methane (CH4) has carbon bonded with four electron pairs, fulfilling the octet rule without lone pairs or electron deficiency, making it electron-precise.

Question 14 of 19

What is the hybridization of boron in diborane?

Explanation: Boron atoms in diborane are sp3 hybridized to accommodate the bridging and terminal bonds.

Question 15 of 19

Which metal hydride is commonly used as a reducing agent in organic synthesis?

Explanation: Lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4) contains complex hydride ions and serves as a powerful reducing agent.

Question 16 of 19

The bridging bonds in diborane are referred to as:

Explanation: The B-H-B bonds involve three atoms sharing two electrons, commonly known as banana bonds or 3c-2e bonds.

Question 17 of 19

Which group does NOT form metallic hydrides readily?

Explanation: Elements of groups 7, 8, and 9 (with the exception of Cr in group 6) do not form metallic hydrides, a phenomenon known as the hydride gap.

Question 18 of 19

What is the oxidation state of hydrogen in ionic hydrides like NaH?

Explanation: In ionic hydrides, hydrogen exists as the hydride ion (H-) with an oxidation state of -1.

Question 19 of 19

Which hydride has the lowest boiling point in group 16?

Explanation: H2S has a lower boiling point than H2Se and H2Te due to weaker van der Waals forces, while H2O is anomalously high due to hydrogen bonding.

Question 1 of 10
Which hydride is polymeric and covalent among alkaline earth metals?

Solution: Beryllium hydride (BeH2) is polymeric and covalent due to the high charge-to-size ratio of Be2+.

Question 2 of 10
What is the role of hydrogen in metallic hydrides?

Solution: In metallic hydrides, hydrogen occupies interstitial sites within the metallic lattice as neutral atoms or protons with localized electrons.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following represents an electron-rich hydride?

Solution: Phosphine (PH3) possesses a lone pair on the phosphorus atom, making it an electron-rich hydride.

Question 4 of 10
The thermal stability order for group 17 hydrides is:

Solution: Thermal stability decreases down the halogen group as bond dissociation enthalpy decreases from HF to HI.

Question 5 of 10
Which substance is formed when boron trifluoride reacts with lithium hydride?

Solution: Reaction of boron trifluoride with lithium hydride yields diborane and lithium fluoride: 2BF3 + 6LiH → B2H6 + 6LiF.

Question 6 of 10
Why do transition metal hydrides show non-stoichiometric behavior?

Solution: Transition metal hydrides are interstitial, where hydrogen atoms occupy metal lattice voids variably without altering the crystal structure type.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following hydrides has the highest melting point?

Solution: Lithium hydride (LiH) is an ionic solid with a crystal lattice, giving it a very high melting point compared to molecular covalent hydrides.

Question 8 of 10
What is the geometry around boron atoms in diborane?

Solution: Each boron atom in diborane is surrounded by four pairs of electrons, resulting in a distorted tetrahedral geometry.

Question 9 of 10
Which hydride is a gas at room temperature and exhibits strong hydrogen bonding?

Solution: Hydrogen fluoride (HF) is a gas/low-boiling liquid that exhibits very strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding.

Question 10 of 10
The hydrides of group 13 elements are generally known as:

Solution: Group 13 hydrides, such as boranes (boron hydrides) and alanes (aluminum hydrides), are characteristic electron-deficient compounds.
