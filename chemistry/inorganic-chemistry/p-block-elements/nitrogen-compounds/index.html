Inorganic Chemistry

Nitrogen Compounds

Nitrogen compounds encompass hydrides, oxides, oxoacids, and halides exhibiting variable oxidation states from -3 to +5. They demonstrate diverse chemical behavior due to the high electronegativity and small size of the nitrogen atom.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation and Properties of Ammonia

  • Ammonia is industrially prepared via the Haber Process using an iron catalyst promoted with potassium and aluminum oxides.
  • It acts as a strong Lewis base due to the presence of a lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom.
  • Aqueous ammonia precipitates metal hydroxides from salt solutions, such as forming a blue gelatinous precipitate of Cu(OH)2 with copper salts.
  • Excess ammonia dissolves copper hydroxide to form a deep blue complex ion, [Cu(NH3)4(H2O)2]2+.

Oxides and Oxoacids of Nitrogen

  • Nitrogen forms multiple oxides ranging from +1 to +5 oxidation states, including N2O, NO, N2O3, NO2, N2O4, and N2O5.
  • Nitric acid (HNO3) is manufactured by the Ostwald Process, involving catalytic oxidation of ammonia followed by absorption in water.
  • Concentrated nitric acid acts as a powerful oxidizing agent, passivating metals like iron and chromium due to the formation of an oxide film.
  • Phosphorus pentoxide dehydrates nitric acid to form dinitrogen pentoxide (N2O5).

Organic Nitrogen Compounds and Amines

  • Aliphatic amines are stronger bases than ammonia due to the +I electron-donating effect of alkyl groups increasing electron density on nitrogen.
  • Primary amines react with chloroform and alcoholic potassium hydroxide to yield foul-smelling carbylamines (isocyanides).
  • The Hinsberg test utilizes benzenesulfonyl chloride to distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary amines.
  • Diazonium salts undergo coupling reactions with phenols and aromatic amines to yield brightly colored azo dyes.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aliphatic amine basicity order: Tertiary > Secondary > Primary in aqueous phase

Observation: Secondary amines are often the strongest bases (e.g., diethylamine > ethylamine > triethylamine) — Steric hindrance of bulky alkyl groups and solvation effects counterbalance the inductive electron-donating effect in aqueous solution.

Exception Nitrogen forms pentahalides analogous to phosphorus

Observation: Nitrogen does not form pentahalides like NF5 — Absence of vacant d-orbitals in the valence shell of nitrogen prevents expansion of its octet.

Exception All nitrogen oxides are acidic

Observation: Nitrous oxide (N2O) and nitric oxide (NO) are neutral — They do not react with acids or bases to form salts under standard conditions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Dinitrogen is relatively inert at room temperature due to the high bond dissociation enthalpy of the N≡N triple bond (946 kJ mol-1).
  • Ammonia forms hydrogen bonds, resulting in unusually high melting and boiling points compared to heavier group 15 hydrides.
  • Bismuth is the only group 15 element that forms exclusively basic oxides.
  • White phosphorus reacts with caustic soda to give phosphine, whereas nitrogen compounds do not undergo analogous disproportionation easily.
  • Aniline undergoes diazotization with nitrous acid at 273-278 K to form benzene diazonium chloride.
  • Nitrobenzene undergoes reduction in neutral medium (Zn/NH4Cl) to form N-phenylhydroxylamine.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the basicity order of amines in the gaseous phase with the aqueous phase.
  • Assuming nitrogen can expand its octet to form pentavalent compounds like NCl5.
  • Failing to maintain low temperature (0-5°C) during diazotization reactions, leading to phenol formation instead of diazonium salt.
  • Mistaking neutral oxides like NO and N2O for acidic or basic anhydrides.

⚡ Quick Revision: Nitrogen Compounds

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Hydrides of Nitrogen

Ammonia is prepared industrially via the Haber process and exhibits strong hydrogen bonding, high boiling points, and excellent Lewis base character.

Oxides and Acids

Nitrogen exhibits oxidation states from +1 to +5 in its oxides. Nitric acid is prepared by the Ostwald process and serves as a potent oxidizing agent.

Aliphatic and Aromatic Amines

Amines are derivatives of ammonia categorized by alkyl or aryl substitution. Basicity is governed by inductive effects, steric hindrance, and solvation.

Diazonium Salts

Aromatic diazonium salts are versatile intermediates undergoing replacement and coupling reactions to synthesize diverse aryl derivatives and azo dyes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • N≡N bond dissociation energy is 946 kJ mol-1
  • Ammonia boiling point is anomalously high due to intermolecular H-bonding
  • HNO3 acts as a strong oxidizing agent yielding NO2 or NO
  • Aniline is a weaker base than ammonia due to resonance delocalization of lone pair
  • Primary amines give carbylamine test; secondary and tertiary do not

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nitrogen anomalies stem from small size, high electronegativity, and absence of d-orbitals.
  • Ammonia is a key industrial chemical acting as a nucleophile and base.
  • Nitric acid oxidizes most metals except noble metals like gold and platinum.
  • Amines exhibit complex basicity trends dependent on solvent and substituent effects.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nitrous Oxide (N2O) VS Nitric Oxide (NO)

N2O is laughing gas (neutral, produced by NH4NO3 pyrolysis), while NO is a neutral free radical gas containing an odd electron.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following nitrogen oxides is paramagnetic?

Explanation: Nitric oxide (NO) contains 15 valence electrons (an odd-electron molecule), making it paramagnetic.

Question 2 of 18

The catalyst used in the industrial Haber process for ammonia synthesis is:

Explanation: Finely divided iron with molybdenum or potassium/aluminum oxides as promoters is used in the Haber process.

Question 3 of 18

What is the product of thermal decomposition of ammonium nitrate at 250°C?

Explanation: Ammonium nitrate decomposes upon gentle heating to form dinitrogen monoxide (nitrous oxide) and water.

Question 4 of 18

Which amine does not react with benzenesulfonyl chloride?

Explanation: Tertiary amines lack a hydrogen atom attached to nitrogen and do not react with benzenesulfonyl chloride.

Question 5 of 18

The correct basicity order of methylamines in aqueous solution is:

Explanation: In aqueous solution, secondary methylamines are most basic due to a balance between inductive effect, solvation, and steric hindrance.

Question 6 of 18

Carbylamine test is given by:

Explanation: Only primary aliphatic and aromatic amines react with chloroform and alcoholic KOH to form foul-smelling isocyanides.

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following oxoacids of nitrogen is unstable in pure form?

Explanation: Nitrous acid (HNO2) is unstable and disproportionates into nitric acid, nitric oxide, and water upon warming.

Question 8 of 18

When concentrated nitric acid is heated with copper, the gas evolved is:

Explanation: Reaction of copper with concentrated HNO3 produces brown nitrogen dioxide (NO2) gas.

Question 9 of 18

Aniline is converted to benzene diazonium chloride by treatment with:

Explanation: Diazotization requires nitrous acid generated in situ from sodium nitrite and hydrochloric acid at 0-5°C.

Question 10 of 18

Which hydride of group 15 elements has the lowest boiling point?

Explanation: Phosphine (PH3) has the lowest boiling point due to lack of intermolecular hydrogen bonding, unlike ammonia.

Question 11 of 18

The hybridization of nitrogen in ammonia is:

Explanation: Nitrogen in ammonia forms three sigma bonds and holds one lone pair, resulting in sp3 hybridization and trigonal pyramidal geometry.

Question 12 of 18

Which compound is formed when nitrobenzene is reduced with Sn/HCl?

Explanation: Reduction of nitrobenzene with tin and hydrochloric acid yields aniline as the primary product.

Question 13 of 18

Bredt's rule is related to which class of compounds?

Explanation: Bredt's rule restricts the formation of double bonds at bridgehead positions in bicyclic systems, relevant in nitrogen heterocycles and bridgehead amines.

Question 14 of 18

Which nitrogen compound is used as a standard fertilizer and explosive precursor?

Explanation: Ammonium nitrate is widely used as a nitrogenous fertilizer and in industrial explosives.

Question 15 of 18

The brown ring test for nitrates detects the presence of which species?

Explanation: The brown ring test confirms the presence of nitrate ions (NO3-) by forming a nitroiron(I) complex.

Question 16 of 18

Why is nitrogen gas chemically inert at room temperature?

Explanation: The strong triple covalent bond between nitrogen atoms requires exceptionally high energy to break.

Question 17 of 18

Coupling reaction of benzene diazonium chloride with phenol takes place in:

Explanation: Azo coupling with phenols requires a mildly alkaline medium to activate the phenol ring towards electrophilic attack.

Question 18 of 18

Which of the following is a tertiary amine?

Explanation: Triethylamine has three alkyl groups attached to the nitrogen atom, making it a tertiary amine.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following compounds gives a foul smell when heated with chloroform and alcoholic KOH?

Solution: Primary amines like ethylamine undergo the carbylamine reaction to form isocyanides with an offensive odor.

Question 2 of 10
The oxidation state of nitrogen in ammonium nitrate (NH4NO3) is:

Solution: In NH4+, nitrogen is in -3 oxidation state; in NO3-, nitrogen is in +5 oxidation state.

Question 3 of 10
Which nitrogen oxide is obtained by the thermal decomposition of ammonium nitrate?

Solution: Controlled heating of ammonium nitrate yields nitrous oxide (N2O) and water vapor.

Question 4 of 10
Which reagent converts nitrobenzene directly to aniline in acidic medium?

Solution: Tin and hydrochloric acid (Sn/HCl) is the standard reagent for reducing nitrobenzene to aniline.

Question 5 of 10
Which among the following is the strongest base in aqueous solution?

Solution: Dimethylamine ((CH3)2NH) is the strongest base among methylamines in water due to optimized inductive, steric, and solvation effects.

Question 6 of 10
What is the product formed when benzene diazonium chloride reacts with H3PO2 and water?

Solution: Hypophosphorous acid (H3PO2) reduces benzene diazonium chloride to benzene with the elimination of N2 and HCl.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following species acts as an electrophile in aromatic nitration by concentrated HNO3 and H2SO4?

Solution: Protonated nitric acid loses water to generate the nitronium ion (NO2+), which serves as the active electrophile.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following compounds does not contain a nitrogen-nitrogen bond?

Solution: Hydroxylamine (NH2OH) contains only one nitrogen atom bonded to oxygen and hydrogen, lacking an N-N bond.

Question 9 of 10
During the diazotization of aniline, excess nitrous acid is destroyed using:

Solution: Urea is added to destroy excess nitrous acid after diazotization to prevent side reactions.

Question 10 of 10
The geometry of the ammonia molecule according to VSEPR theory is:

Solution: With three bond pairs and one lone pair, ammonia adopts a trigonal pyramidal molecular geometry.
