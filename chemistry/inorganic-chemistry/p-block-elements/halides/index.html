Inorganic Chemistry

Halides

Halides are binary compounds formed between halogen elements and less electronegative elements or metals. They exhibit diverse structural, thermal, and hydrolytic properties depending on the polarizing power of the associated cation and the size of the halide ion.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermal Stability and Volatility of Metal Halides

  • Ionic halides possessing high lattice energy exhibit high melting and boiling points.
  • Covalent halides (e.g., AlCl3, SiCl4) are usually low-melting solids, liquids, or gases due to weak van der Waals forces.
  • Anhydrous halides of transition metals with high oxidation states (e.g., TiCl4, VCl5) exist as covalent liquids with high volatility.
  • Thermal stability of alkali metal halides increases down the group as lattice energy stabilizes with matching ionic radii.

Hydrolysis Behavior of Non-Metal and Metalloid Halides

  • Halides like CCl4 do not undergo hydrolysis because carbon lacks vacant d-orbitals to coordinate water molecules.
  • SiCl4, PCl3, and SF6 readily undergo hydrolysis due to the availability of vacant d-orbitals for nucleophilic attack by H2O.
  • Hydrolysis of PCl3 yields phosphorous acid (H3PO3) and HCl.
  • Hydrolysis of SiCl4 yields silicic acid (H4SiO4) which condenses to silica gel upon heating.

Oxidizing Power and Interhalogen Interactions

  • Fluorine (F2) is the strongest oxidizing agent due to its low bond dissociation energy and high hydration energy of F-.
  • Halogens can displace halides below them in the electrochemical series from their aqueous solutions (e.g., Cl2 + 2Br- → Br2 + 2Cl-).
  • Interhalogen compounds (e.g., ClF3, IF7) are more reactive than parent halogens because heteronuclear bonds are generally weaker than homonuclear bonds.
  • Polyhalide formation (e.g., I3-) occurs readily due to the large size and high polarizability of heavier halogens.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher oxidation state metal halides are generally ionic.

Observation: MnF4 and UF6 show significant covalent character, and transition metal halides in highest oxidation states (e.g., CrO2Cl2, OsF6) are covalent molecules. — Extreme polarizing power of highly charged cations polarizes the electron cloud of the halide anion.

Exception Group 14 tetrachlorides undergo rapid hydrolysis.

Observation: Carbon tetrachloride (CCl4) is completely inert toward hydrolysis. — Absence of vacant d-orbitals on the central carbon atom prevents coordination of water molecules in the transition state.

Exception Hydrohalic acids increase in acidity down the group (HF to HI).

Observation: HF is a weak acid in aqueous solution compared to HCl, HBr, and HI. — Strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding in HF and high H-F bond dissociation enthalpy restrict complete dissociation.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Fluorine exhibits only a -1 oxidation state in its compounds due to the absence of d-orbitals.
  • Silver halides (AgCl, AgBr, AgI) show characteristic colors from white to pale yellow and distinct solubilities in ammonia.
  • Aluminum chloride exists as a covalent dimer (Al2Cl6) in the vapor phase to complete its octet.
  • Beryllium chloride exists as a chain polymer in the solid state involving electron-deficient chlorine bridges.
  • Silicon tetrafluoride reacts with excess HF to form fluorosilicic acid (H2SiF6).
  • Bleaching powder is a mixed halide-hypochlorite salt with the formula CaOCl2·H2O.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming CCl4 undergoes hydrolysis because other group 14 tetrachlorides do.
  • Confusing the thermal stability trend of hydrides (HF > HCl > HBr > HI) with their acidic strength trend (HI > HBr > HCl > HF).
  • Forgetting that HF can be stored in wax-lined or plastic containers because it reacts with glass (SiO2).
  • Assuming all metal halides are soluble in water without considering lattice and hydration enthalpy balances.

⚡ Quick Revision: Halides

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Polarization and Covalent Character

Fajans' rules dictate that small cations and large anions promote covalency by polarizing electron density.

Hydrolysis Mechanisms

Halides of elements with vacant d-orbitals readily undergo nucleophilic attack by water, yielding oxyacids and halogen acids.

Acid-Base Properties of HX

Acid strength in aqueous media increases down the halogen group due to decreasing bond dissociation enthalpy.

Redox Behavior

Halogens act as oxidizing agents, with standard reduction potentials decreasing from fluorine to iodine.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Polarizability (Ability of an electron cloud to distort)
Tendency of an anion to undergo distortion under the influence of a cation's electric field.
Interhalogen (XYn)
Binary compounds containing two different halogen atoms.
Polyhalide (X3-)
Anions formed by the addition of a halogen molecule to a halide ion.
Lattice Energy (U)
Energy released when gaseous ions combine to form one mole of a crystal lattice (kJ mol-1).
Hydration Enthalpy (ΔHhyd)
Energy released when gaseous ions are hydrated by water molecules.
Anomalous Behavior (First element anomaly)
Unique chemical properties of fluorine due to small size, high electronegativity, and absence of d-orbitals.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • HF is a weak acid, whereas HCl, HBr, and HI are strong acids.
  • CCl4 does not hydrolyze due to lack of d-orbitals.
  • AlCl3 dimerizes to Al2Cl6 in the vapor phase.
  • Fluorine is the strongest oxidizing agent among all halogens.
  • Silica reacts with HF to produce volatile SiF4.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Halides range from strongly ionic to strongly covalent based on cation-anion size ratios.
  • Thermal stability and volatility are governed by lattice and intermolecular forces.
  • Hydrolysis requires accessible vacant orbitals on the central atom.
  • Oxidizing power decreases down the halogen group.

⚖️ Key Differences

Thermal Stability of Hydrides VS Acidic Strength of Hydrides

Thermal stability decreases down the group (HF > HCl > HBr > HI), while acidic strength increases down the group (HI > HBr > HCl > HF).

According to Fajans' rule, covalent character is favored by:

Explanation: High positive charge density on a small cation combined with a polarizable large anion maximizes covalent character.

Which halogen has the highest standard reduction potential?

Explanation: Fluorine has the highest positive reduction potential, making it the strongest oxidizing agent.

Question 5 of 18

Why can fluorine not show positive oxidation states?

Explanation: Fluorine has no vacant d-orbitals in its valence shell to promote electrons for higher oxidation states.

Question 6 of 18

Aluminum chloride in the vapor phase exists as:

Explanation: AlCl3 exists as a covalent dimer (Al2Cl6) in the vapor phase to achieve an octet around aluminum.

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following compounds is used to etch glass?

Explanation: HF reacts with silica (SiO2) in glass to form water-soluble silicon tetrafluoride and hexafluorosilicic acid.

Question 8 of 18

The hybridization of iodine in IF7 is:

Explanation: IF7 has 7 bond pairs and 0 lone pairs, resulting in pentagonal bipyramidal geometry with sp3d3 hybridization.

Question 9 of 18

Which metal halide is predominantly covalent due to high polarizability?

Explanation: Sn(IV) has a high charge-to-size ratio, making SnCl4 a covalent liquid.

Question 10 of 18

Which polyhalide ion is exceptionally stable in aqueous solution?

Explanation: I3- is stable because iodine is large and highly polarizable, allowing effective association with iodide ions.

Question 11 of 18

The melting point of alkali metal halides generally follows which trend?

Explanation: Lattice energy decreases as cation and anion radii increase, leading to lower melting points for iodides.

Question 12 of 18

Which species acts as a Lewis acid in Friedel-Crafts reactions?

Explanation: Anhydrous AlCl3 has an incomplete octet and acts as a strong Lewis acid by accepting electron pairs.

Question 13 of 18

Explanation: PCl3 + 3H2O → H3PO3 + 3HCl.

Question 14 of 18

Which of the following is an example of an interhalogen compound of the AB5 type?

Explanation: IF5 contains one iodine atom bonded to five fluorine atoms, representing an AB5 square pyramidal structure.

Question 15 of 18

Beryllium chloride in the solid state has a:

Explanation: Solid BeCl2 consists of chlorine-bridged polymeric chains to reduce electron deficiency.

Question 16 of 18

Why is HF a liquid while other hydrogen halides are gases at room temperature?

Explanation: Strong hydrogen bonding between HF molecules results in association and a higher boiling point.

Question 17 of 18

Which reagent can convert potassium iodide to iodine?

Explanation: Chlorine is a stronger oxidizing agent and displaces iodine from potassium iodide solution.

Question 18 of 18

The shape of ClF3 molecule according to VSEPR theory is:

Explanation: ClF3 has 3 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs, adopting a T-shape configuration.

Solution: Sulfur cannot expand its coordination number to 6 with small hydrogen atoms due to steric overcrowding and insufficient energy.
