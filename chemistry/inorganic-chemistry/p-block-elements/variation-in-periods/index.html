Inorganic Chemistry

Variation in Periods

Periodic variations across a period reflect the progressive filling of valence shells and the corresponding increase in effective nuclear charge. This systematic shift dictates physical and chemical properties such as atomic radius, ionization enthalpy, electron gain enthalpy, and electronegativity.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Atomic and Ionic Radius Trends Across a Period

  • Atomic radii decrease progressively from left to right across a period due to increasing effective nuclear charge (Zeff) pulling the electron cloud closer.
  • The addition of electrons to the same principal shell fails to provide effective mutual shielding, accentuating nuclear attraction.
  • Noble gases exhibit an apparent anomaly, showing abnormally large van der Waals radii because they lack covalent bond formation and possess fully paired valence shells.

Ionization Enthalpy and Electron Gain Enthalpy Variations

  • Successive removal of electrons requires higher energy, and primary ionization enthalpy generally increases across a period as atomic size decreases.
  • Deviations from this trend occur at Group 2/13 and Group 15/16 boundaries due to the extra stability of fully-filled and half-filled subshells.
  • Electron gain enthalpy becomes increasingly negative across a period, reflecting a higher thermodynamic tendency to accept electrons and achieve noble gas configuration.

Electronegativity and Metallic-Nonmetallic Character

  • Electronegativity values rise steadily from alkali metals up to halogens as the capacity of bonded atoms to attract shared electron pairs intensifies.
  • Metallic character decreases across a period, transitioning from highly electropositive s-block metals to amphoteric/metalloid elements, and finally to strongly electronegative p-block non-metals.
  • Maximum non-metallic character concentrates in the upper-right corner of the periodic table, excluding noble gases.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Ionization energy should increase steadily across a period.

Observation: Group 2 (Be, Mg) has a higher ionization enthalpy than Group 13 (B, Al). — Removal of a p-electron from Group 13 elements requires less energy than removing an s-electron from the completely filled ns2 configuration of Group 2.

Exception Ionization energy should increase from Group 15 to Group 16.

Observation: Group 15 (N, P) has a higher ionization enthalpy than Group 16 (O, S). — Half-filled np3 orbitals in Group 15 provide extra exchange energy stability, making electron removal more difficult than from the np4 configuration.

Exception Electron gain enthalpy should become more negative continuously across a period.

Observation: Period 2 elements (e.g., F) have less negative electron gain enthalpy than Period 3 elements (e.g., Cl). — Compact 2p orbitals suffer from severe inter-electronic repulsion when an extra electron enters, whereas larger 3p orbitals accommodate the incoming electron more readily.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Zeff Shortcut for Isoelectronic Species
Radius ∝ 1Z

Application: For isoelectronic ions (e.g., N3-, O2-, F-, Na+, Mg2+), the species with the highest nuclear charge (Z) has the smallest ionic radius.

Diagonal Relationship Mnemonic
Li → Mg, Be → Al, B → Si

Application: Diagonal neighbors share comparable charge-to-radius ratios (ionic potential), leading to strikingly similar chemical properties and hydrolysis behaviors.

Metallic Character Trend Trick
Metal ← Left | Right → Non-metal

Application: Remember that metallic behavior is inversely proportional to electronegativity; both trends move in exact opposition across any horizontal row.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Atomic radii decrease across a period due to increased effective nuclear charge with constant principal quantum number.
  • First ionization enthalpy shows local dips at Group 13 and Group 16 due to subshell symmetry.
  • Electron gain enthalpy of fluorine is less negative than chlorine due to high electron-electron repulsion in compact 2p orbitals.
  • The maximum oxidation state exhibited by an element across a period generally equals its group number up to group 7 or 8.
  • Electronegativity values dictate bond polarity, increasing from left to right.
  • Diagonal relationship pairs (Li-Mg, Be-Al, B-Si) exhibit similar electronegativities and polarizing powers.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming noble gases have the smallest atomic radii in a period; van der Waals radii make them significantly larger than preceding halogens.
  • Confusing electron gain enthalpy sign conventions; more negative values signify a greater thermodynamic affinity for electrons.
  • Believing that Period 2 elements can expand their octets beyond an octet limit; they lack accessible low-energy d-subshells.
  • Treating shielding constants (S) as constant across a period; S increases due to additional electrons in the same shell.

⚡ Quick Revision: Variation in Periods

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Effective Nuclear Charge Progression

Zeff increases steadily across a period because protons are added to the nucleus while screening electrons remain in the same shell, driving down atomic radii.

Ionization Energy Irregularities

Anomalies in ionization enthalpy trends across periods stem directly from the energetic stability associated with fully-filled and half-filled subshells.

Anomalous Behavior of Period 2

First-row elements exhibit distinct chemical behaviors due to small atomic size, high electronegativity, and complete absence of vacant d-orbitals for bonding expansion.

Periodic Redox Properties

Oxidizing power increases from left to right across a period as electron gain enthalpies become more negative, making non-metals strong oxidizing agents.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Effective Nuclear Charge (Zeff)
Net positive charge experienced by valence electrons after shielding correction.
Ionization Enthalpy (ΔiH)
Energy required to remove an electron from an isolated gaseous atom in ground state (kJ mol-1).
Electron Gain Enthalpy (egH)
Enthalpy change when an electron is added to an isolated gaseous atom (kJ mol-1).
Electronegativity (χ)
Measure of an atom's ability to attract shared bonding electrons.
Covalency (Valency bond limit)
Number of covalent bonds formed by an atom based on available orbitals.
Diagonal Relationship (Diagonal similarity)
Similar chemical properties exhibited by diagonally adjacent elements in adjacent periods.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Zeff increases across any period from left to right.
  • Atomic radii decrease across a period.
  • Ionization energy shows local dips at groups 13 and 16.
  • Period 2 elements cannot expand octet due to lack of d-orbitals.
  • Electronegativity is highest for fluorine in the p-block (excluding noble gases).

📌 Quick Recall

  • Periodic trends correlate directly with valence shell filling and effective nuclear charge.
  • Exceptions to general trends are explained by stable half-filled or fully-filled subshell configurations.
  • Period 2 elements diverge from group norms due to extreme small size and high charge density.
  • Metallic character drops and non-metallic character rises moving right across a period.

⚖️ Key Differences

Electron Gain Enthalpy VS Electronegativity

Electron gain enthalpy is an energetic property measured for an isolated gaseous atom, whereas electronegativity is a dimensionless relative property of a bonded atom.

Question 1 of 20

Which of the following elements has the highest first ionization enthalpy in Period 3?

Explanation: Argon is a noble gas with a stable octet configuration and highest Zeff in period 3, requiring maximum energy to remove an electron.

Question 2 of 20

Why is the first ionization energy of Mg greater than that of Al?

Explanation: Magnesium has its valence electrons in a fully-filled 3s2 orbital, which imparts extra stability and requires higher energy to ionize than removing a p-electron from aluminium.

Question 3 of 20

Which set represents isoelectronic species?

Explanation: Na+, Mg2+, and Al3+ all possess 10 electrons (neon core configuration), making them isoelectronic.

Question 4 of 20

In a given period, atomic radius decreases from left to right because:

Explanation: As protons are added across a period without increasing the principal shell, Zeff increases, pulling the electron cloud inward.

Question 5 of 20

The electron gain enthalpy of fluorine is less negative than that of chlorine due to:

Explanation: Fluorine's small 2p orbitals concentrate electron density, causing high repulsion when an incoming electron enters.

Question 6 of 20

Which diagonal pair shows a striking similarity in chemical behavior?

Explanation: Lithium and magnesium exhibit a diagonal relationship due to comparable ionic radii and polarizing power.

Question 7 of 20

What is the maximum covalency of boron (Period 2)?

Explanation: Boron belongs to Period 2 and lacks vacant d-orbitals, restricting its maximum covalency to 4 (e.g., [BF4]-).

Question 8 of 20

Across Period 3, the metallic character:

Explanation: Metallic character drops steadily from sodium (metal) through metalloids to chlorine (non-metal) across the period.

Question 9 of 20

Which species has the smallest radius among O2-, F-, Na+, and Mg2+?

Explanation: For isoelectronic species, radius decreases as nuclear charge (Z) increases. Magnesium has the highest atomic number (Z = 12), hence the smallest radius.

Question 10 of 20

The electronegativity scale based on bond energies was formulated by:

Explanation: Linus Pauling developed the electronegativity scale using thermochemical bond energy data.

Question 11 of 20

Which element in Period 2 forms multiple bonds (pπ-pπ) most readily?

Explanation: Carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen readily form pπ-pπ multiple bonds due to effective lateral overlap of compact 2p orbitals.

Question 12 of 20

First ionization energy of oxygen is less than that of nitrogen because:

Explanation: Nitrogen's half-filled 2p3 subshell provides extra stability, requiring more energy for electron removal than oxygen's 2p4 configuration.

Question 13 of 20

Which property generally decreases across a period?

Explanation: Atomic radius decreases across a period due to increasing Zeff.

Question 14 of 20

The correct order of increasing covalent character in chlorides of Period 3 is:

Explanation: According to Fajan's rules, as ionic charge increases and size decreases across a period, polarizing power and covalent character increase.

Question 15 of 20

Which element shows anomalous behavior compared to the rest of its group?

Explanation: Lithium, as the first member of Group 1, shows anomalous behavior due to its extremely small size and high polarizing power.

Question 16 of 20

The tendency to attract shared electrons is known as:

Explanation: Electronegativity is defined as the capability of a bonded atom to attract shared bonding electrons towards itself.

Question 17 of 20

Which group 15 element has the lowest ionization enthalpy?

Explanation: Ionization enthalpy decreases down a group due to increasing atomic size and shielding; Bismuth at the bottom has the lowest value.

Question 18 of 20

Slater's rule calculates which of the following?

Explanation: Slater's rules provide empirical formulas to calculate the screening constant (S) and effective nuclear charge (Zeff).

Question 19 of 20

Which oxide of Period 3 is amphoteric?

Explanation: Aluminium oxide (Al2O3) displays amphoteric behavior, reacting with both acids and bases.

Question 20 of 20

The general trend for electron gain enthalpy values across a period becomes:

Explanation: Electron gain enthalpy becomes increasingly negative from left to right as non-metallic character and electron affinity intensify.

Which of the following factors causes a decrease in atomic radii down a group but an increase across a period?

Solution: Zeff increases across a period (decreasing radius) while remaining relatively constant down a group where principal shell number dominates.

Solution: Beryllium shows a diagonal relationship with aluminium, exhibiting similar covalent tendencies and amphoteric oxide behavior.

Question 5 of 10
The hydration energy of ions across a period from left to right generally:

Solution: Hydration energy increases as ionic radius decreases and charge density increases across a period.

Question 6 of 10
Why do noble gases possess unusually high values of ionization enthalpy?

Solution: Stable octet/duplet configurations in noble gases resist electron removal, resulting in maximum ionization energy.

Question 7 of 10
Which chloride in Period 3 is predominantly ionic?

Solution: Sodium chloride (NaCl) is formed by complete electron transfer between an alkali metal and halogen, making it ionic.

Question 8 of 10
The second ionization enthalpy is always greater than the first ionization enthalpy because:

Solution: Removing an electron from a cation requires overcoming greater electrostatic attraction because the remaining electron cloud is held more tightly.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following properties increases progressively across a period?

Solution: Non-metallic character increases from left to right as electronegativity and electron affinity rise.

Question 10 of 10
What is the oxidation state of chlorine in its highest oxide, Cl2O7?

Solution: Chlorine utilizes all 7 of its valence electrons (3s23p5) to exhibit a maximum oxidation state of +7 in Cl2O7.
