Oxyacids of p-Block Elements

Oxyacids are chemical compounds containing hydrogen, oxygen, and at least one other element, where at least one hydrogen atom is bound to oxygen and ionizable as a proton. Their acid strength is governed by the electronegativity of the central atom and the number of terminal oxo groups.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structural Features and Basicity of Phosphorus Oxyacids

  • Phosphorus oxyacids contain tetrahedral phosphorus centers bonded to terminal P=O, hydroxyl P–OH, and direct P–H bonds.
  • Directly bonded hydrogen atoms (P–H) are non-acidic and do not contribute to basicity.
  • H3PO2 (hypophosphorous acid) is monobasic, H3PO3 (phosphorous acid) is dibasic, and H3PO4 (phosphoric acid) is tribasic.
  • The presence of reducing P–H bonds makes lower phosphorus oxyacids strong reducing agents.

Periodic Trends in Acid Strength of Halogen Oxyacids

  • For a series of oxyacids of the same halogen with varying oxidation states (e.g., HClO to HClO4), acid strength increases with an increase in oxidation state.
  • Higher oxidation states of the central halogen withdraw electron density more effectively from the O–H bond via inductive effect.
  • For oxyacids of different halogens in the same oxidation state (e.g., HClO vs HBrO vs HIO), acid strength decreases down the group due to decreasing electronegativity.
  • HClO4 is one of the strongest known acids because the negative charge in the perchlorate anion is stabilized across four equivalent oxygen atoms by resonance.

Sulfur Oxyacids and Peroxo Linkages

  • Sulfuric acid (H2SO4) and sulfurous acid (H2SO3) form extensive series of condensed acids including peroxodisulfuric acid (Marshall's acid, H2S2O8).
  • Peroxodisulfuric acid contains a characteristic –O–O– peroxo linkage where sulfur is in the +6 oxidation state.
  • Thiosulfuric acid (H2S2O3) contains a sulfur-sulfur bond where one sulfur replaces an oxygen atom.
  • Dithionic acid (H2S2O6) contains a direct S–S covalent bond linking two sulfonic acid groups.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Pauling's Rule for Oxyacid Strength (First Constant)
pKa ≈ 8 - 5n
Conditions: Where n is the number of non-protonated oxo groups (O atoms without H)
Units: Dimensionless
Oxidation State Calculation
OS = ∑(Charges of other atoms) / (Number of atoms)
Conditions: Applies to the central atom in neutral or ionic oxyacids
Units: Integer oxidation number
Basicity of Oxyacids
Basicity = Number of ionizable X–O–H bonds
Conditions: Determined by structural analysis (NMR or X-ray diffraction)
Units: Integer count per molecule
Acid Dissociation Constant
Ka = ([H+][A-]) / [HA]
Conditions: Dilute aqueous solutions at equilibrium
Units: Dimensionless or mol L-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher central atom oxidation state always yields greater acid strength.

Observation: H3PO3 has phosphorus in +3 state, yet it is a stronger acid than some carboxylic acids, while H3PO2 (+1 state) is stronger than H3PO3 due to tautomeric forms and electron-releasing effects of P-H bonds. — Anion stabilization and specific crystal lattice hydration energies override simple inductive arguments in lower phosphorus oxidation states.

Exception All hydrogen atoms in an oxyacid molecule are acidic.

Observation: In H3PO2 and H3PO3, hydrogens attached directly to phosphorus (P-H) are non-ionizable. — Hydrogen attached to phosphorus has a low electronegativity difference compared to hydrogen attached to oxygen (O-H).

Exception Chlorine oxyacids increase in thermal stability with oxidation number.

Observation: Hypochlorous acid (HClO) and chloric acid (HClO3) disproportionate readily upon heating into chlorides, perchlorates, and oxygen. — Kinetic instability of intermediate oxidation states toward disproportionation redox pathways.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Pauling's Rule Shortcut for pKa
pKa ≈ 8 - 5(number of terminal =O groups)

Application: Use to instantly estimate the relative acid strength of oxyacids like HNO3, H2SO4, and HClO4 without calculation.

Basicity Count via Structures
Basicity = Count of –OH groups attached to central atom

Application: Draw the central atom surrounded by =O and –OH groups to avoid counting non-acidic P-H or C-H hydrogens.

Halogen Oxyacid Acidity Sequence
HOCl < HOClO < HOClO2 < HOClO3

Application: Remember that each extra oxygen atom adds roughly 2 to 3 orders of magnitude to the Ka value.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • H3PO2 contains two P-H bonds, one P-OH bond, and one P=O bond, making it monobasic.
  • H3PO3 contains one P-H bond, two P-OH bonds, and one P=O bond, making it dibasic.
  • Orthophosphoric acid (H3PO4) yields three series of salts: dihydrogen phosphates, hydrogen phosphates, and phosphates.
  • Perchloric acid (HClO4) has chlorine in the +4 tetrahedral environment with three double-bonded oxygen atoms and one hydroxyl group.
  • Carbonic acid (H2CO3) exists in equilibrium with dissolved carbon dioxide in aqueous solution with a very small Ka.
  • Nitric acid (HNO3) functions both as a strong monoprotic acid and a powerful oxidizing agent due to N in the +5 state.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming all hydrogens in H3PO3 are acidic; remember P-H hydrogens do not ionize.
  • Confusing the oxidation state of sulfur in H2SO5 (Caro's acid, +6 with peroxo link) with standard sulfate (+6).
  • Writing incorrect structures for pyrophosphoric acid (H4P2O7) by omitting the central P-O-P bridge.
  • Forgetting that periodic acid exists as H5IO6 (paraperiodic acid) rather than HIO4 in aqueous solution.

⚡ Quick Revision: Oxyacids of p-Block Elements

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Acidity and Electronegativity

Acid strength of oxyacids HmXOn increases with the electronegativity of the central atom X and the number of terminal oxygen atoms (n - m).

Reducing Property of Phosphorus Oxyacids

Phosphorus oxyacids containing at least one P-H bond act as strong reducing agents, reducing Tollens' reagent and Mercuric chloride.

Peroxo Oxyacids

Sulfur and phosphorus form peroxo oxyacids containing –O–O– linkages, which release active oxygen and exhibit high oxidizing power.

Polyphosphoric Acids

Condensation of orthophosphoric acid molecules by elimination of water yields linear and cyclic polyphosphoric acids with P-O-P bridges.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Oxyacid (HmXOn)
An acid that contains oxygen, hydrogen, and at least one other element.
Basicity (Proton-donating capacity)
The number of ionizable hydrogen atoms per molecule of acid.
Peroxo Acid (–O–O– linkage)
An oxyacid containing a peroxide linkage between oxygen atoms.
Hypo Acid (Lowest oxidation state)
Prefix denoting an oxyacid with the lowest positive oxidation state for the halogen series (+1).
Per Acid (Highest oxidation state)
Prefix denoting the highest oxidation state or presence of peroxo groups (+7 for halogens).
Orthophosphoric Acid (H3PO4)
Standard tribasic phosphoric acid derived from phosphorus pentoxide.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • H3PO2 is monobasic and has two P-H bonds.
  • H3PO3 is dibasic and has one P-H bond.
  • HClO4 is the strongest halogen oxyacid due to extensive resonance stabilization.
  • Pyrophosphoric acid (H4P2O7) contains one P-O-P bond and four P-OH groups.
  • Caro's acid is H2SO5 (peroxomonosulfuric acid).

📌 Quick Recall

  • Oxyacid strength depends directly on central atom electronegativity and formal oxidation state.
  • Non-bridging terminal oxygen atoms withdraw electron density, facilitating proton release from O-H bonds.
  • Phosphorus oxyacids exhibit variable basicity corresponding directly to the number of P-OH hydroxyl groups.
  • Halogen oxyacids span oxidation states from +1 to +7, with stability and acidity increasing with oxygen count.

⚖️ Key Differences

H3PO2 VS H3PO4

H3PO2 is monobasic and a strong reducing agent; H3PO4 is tribasic and non-reducing.

Question 3 of 18

Which halogen oxyacid is the strongest?

Explanation: HClO4 (perchloric acid) is the strongest due to the +7 oxidation state of chlorine and maximum resonance stabilization of the perchlorate anion.

Question 4 of 18

What is the oxidation state of sulfur in Caro's acid (H2SO5)?

Explanation: In H2SO5, sulfur is in the +6 oxidation state, accounting for two hydroxyl groups, one peroxo linkage, and one terminal oxo group.

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following contains a direct S-S single bond?

Explanation: Dithionic acid (H2S2O6) contains a direct sulfur-sulfur bond linking two sulfonic groups.

Question 6 of 18

Which acid contains a peroxo linkage?

Explanation: Peroxodisulfuric acid (H2S2O8, Marshall's acid) contains an –O–O– peroxo linkage.

Question 7 of 18

The number of P-OH bonds in pyrophosphoric acid (H4P2O7) is:

Explanation: Pyrophosphoric acid has structure (OH)2P(=O)-O-P(=O)(OH)2, containing four P-OH bonds and one P-O-P bridge.

Question 8 of 18

What is the hybridization of the central chlorine atom in HClO4?

Explanation: Chlorine in HClO4 is bonded to four oxygen atoms with zero lone pairs, resulting in sp3 tetrahedral hybridization.

Question 9 of 18

Which phosphorus oxyacid acts as a powerful reducing agent due to the presence of two P-H bonds?

Explanation: H3PO2 contains two P-H bonds which readily donate electrons to reduce metal cations.

Question 10 of 18

Among hypohalous acids (HOX), the strongest acid is:

Explanation: Chlorine is the most electronegative halogen among fluorine (which doesn't form HOF stably in water this way), making HOCl the strongest hypohalous acid.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following oxyacids of chlorine has chlorine in the +3 oxidation state?

Explanation: In chlorous acid (HClO2), chlorine exhibits an oxidation state of +3.

Question 12 of 18

The basicity of orthophosphoric acid (H3PO4) is:

Explanation: H3PO4 has three P-OH hydroxyl groups, making it tribasic.

Question 13 of 18

Which of the following compounds is known asoleum?

Explanation: Oleum is a solution of sulfur trioxide in sulfuric acid, represented as H2S2O7 or H2SO4·SO3.

Question 14 of 18

Which nitrogen oxyacid is unstable as a pure liquid at room temperature and decomposes to give NO2?

Explanation: Nitrous acid (HNO2) is unstable and disproportionates into nitric acid, nitric oxide, and water.

Question 15 of 18

The oxidation state of nitrogen in nitric acid (HNO3) is:

Explanation: Using standard oxidation numbers (+1 for H, -2 for O), nitrogen in HNO3 is in the +5 state.

Question 16 of 18

Which oxyacid of sulfur contains sulfur in the +2 oxidation state?

Explanation: Dithionous acid (H2S2O4) features sulfur in the +3 state, but wait, let's check thiosulfuric acid where outer S is -2 and inner S is +6 (average +2). For dithionous acid, average OS is +3.

Question 17 of 18

Which of the following is metaphosphoric acid formula?

Explanation: Metaphosphoric acid has the empirical formula HPO3 and typically exists as cyclic trimers or polymers.

Question 18 of 18

Which halogen does not form a perhalic acid of the type HXO4?

Explanation: Fluorine is the most electronegative element and does not form oxyacids of the type HFO4 due to lack of d-orbitals and high electronegativity.
