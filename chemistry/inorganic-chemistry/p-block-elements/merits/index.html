Inorganic Chemistry

p-Block Elements

The p-block elements comprise groups 13 to 18 of the periodic table, where the last electron enters the outermost p-subshell. They exhibit diverse chemical behavior encompassing metals, metalloids, non-metals, and noble gases.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Group Trends and Anomalous Behavior of First Element

  • The first member of each p-block group exhibits anomalous behavior due to small size, high electronegativity, and absence of d-orbitals.
  • Maximum covalency of the first row elements is restricted to 4 (octet rule), whereas heavier congeners expand their octet utilizing vacant d-orbitals.
  • p-Block elements show a wide range of oxidation states, typically decreasing by two units down the group due to the inert pair effect.

Group 13 and Group 14 Chemistry

  • Group 13 elements (Boron family) show +3 and +1 oxidation states; Thallium (Tl) is most stable in the +1 state.
  • Boron forms electron-deficient compounds like diborane (B2H6) featuring three-center two-electron (3c-2e) banana bonds.
  • Group 14 elements (Carbon family) show +4 and +2 oxidation states; Lead (Pb) exhibits stable +2 state due to relativistic stabilization of the 6s2 electrons.

Group 15 to Group 18 Halogens and Noble Gases

  • Group 15 elements form hydrides whose reducing character and basic strength follow the order BiH3 > SbH3 > AsH3 > PH3 > NH3.
  • Interhalogen compounds are more reactive than halogens because X-X' bonds are weaker than homonuclear X-X bonds.
  • Xenon reacts with fluorine to form stable fluorides like XeF2, XeF4, and XeF6 acting as powerful fluorinating agents.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Electron gain enthalpy becomes more negative down a group

Observation: Fluorine has a less negative electron gain enthalpy than Chlorine — Small size of fluorine atom causes high interelectronic repulsion within its compact 2p orbitals.

Exception Catenation decreases down a group

Observation: Carbon shows maximum catenation while silicon shows significantly lower catenation — C-C bond enthalpy (348 kJ mol-1) is much higher than Si-Si bond enthalpy (297 kJ mol-1).

Exception Acidity of hydrides increases down the group with increasing bond length

Observation: NH3 is a stronger Lewis base than BiH3 due to high charge density — Localized lone pair on the small nitrogen atom provides high electron donor capacity despite lower bond length.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Boron has an anomalously high melting point due to its extremely strong icosahedral crystal lattice structure.
  • Diborane (B2H6) contains two 3c-2e bonds and four regular 2c-2e bonds.
  • Boric acid (H3BO3) acts as a weak monobasic Lewis acid by accepting a hydroxyl ion from water.
  • Concentrated nitric acid oxidizes non-metals to their respective highest oxoacids (e.g., I2 to HIO3, C to CO2).
  • White phosphorus is reactive, poisonous, and exists as discrete tetrahedral P4 molecules with bond angles of 60°.
  • Silicones are organosilicon polymers containing R2SiO repeating units, possessing high thermal stability and water-repellent properties.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming BF3 undergoes complete hydrolysis like other boron trihalides without forming [BF4]-.
  • Confusing the oxidation state of sulfur in Caro's acid (+6) with its average oxidation state or incorrect peroxy linkage identification.
  • Forgetting that nitrogen cannot form pentahalides like NF5 due to the complete absence of vacant d-orbitals in its valence shell.
  • Treating interhalogen compounds as mixtures of halogens rather than distinct chemical species with unique polar covalent bonds.

⚡ Quick Revision: p-Block Elements

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Group 13 General Chemistry

Group 13 elements transition from metalloid boron to metallic thallium, displaying stable +1 and +3 oxidation states governed by the inert pair effect.

Group 14 Allotropy and Catenation

Carbon exhibits extensive catenation and multiple allotropic forms (diamond, graphite, fullerenes) while heavier elements show metallic character and decreasing catenation.

Nitrogen and Phosphorus Compounds

Nitrogen forms stable pπ-pπ multiple bonds and inert N2 gas, whereas phosphorus forms reactive single-bonded chains, rings, and oxoacids.

Noble Gas Chemistry

Xenon reacts directly with fluorine under specific thermodynamic conditions to form well-characterized covalent fluorides and oxofluorides possessing distinct molecular geometries.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Inert Pair Effect (ns2 reluctance)
Reluctance of s-electrons to participate in chemical bonding due to poor shielding by d and f electrons.
Banana Bond (3c-2e bond)
Electron-deficient three-center two-electron bond present in hydrides like diborane.
Catenation (Self-linking)
Ability of an element to form long chains or rings through covalent bonds with itself.
Interhalogen (XX'<sub>n</sub>)
Compounds formed by the combination of two different halogen atoms.
Allotropy (Structural forms)
Existence of an element in two or more different physical forms in the same physical state.
Silicone ((R2SiO)n)
Synthetic organosilicon polymers containing alternate silicon-oxygen backbones.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Boron is electron-deficient with a maximum covalency of 4.
  • Thallium and Lead show predominant +1 and +2 oxidation states respectively.
  • White phosphorus is tetrahedral with 60° bond angle strain.
  • HF is a weak acid compared to other halogen acids due to strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
  • Xenon hexafluoride cannot be stored in glass vessels because it reacts with silica to form explosive XeO3.

📌 Quick Recall

  • p-Block properties depend strongly on periodic trends and orbital availability.
  • Inert pair effect dominates the heavier elements of groups 13, 14, and 15.
  • Halogens exhibit high reactivity and oxidizing power, decreasing from fluorine to iodine.
  • Noble gases are largely inert but form stable fluorides under high pressure and temperature.

⚖️ Key Differences

White Phosphorus VS Red Phosphorus

White is reactive, luminescent, and soluble in CS2; red is polymeric, stable, and insoluble.

Which of the following elements shows maximum catenation capacity?

Explanation: Carbon has the highest catenation capacity due to its small size and very high C-C bond enthalpy.

Question 2 of 19

The hybridization of boron in diborane (B2H6) is:

Explanation: Each boron atom in diborane uses sp3 hybrid orbitals for bonding with terminal and bridging hydrogen atoms.

Question 3 of 19

Which halogen has the most negative electron gain enthalpy?

Explanation: Chlorine has the most negative electron gain enthalpy because fluorine's small 2p orbitals experience high interelectronic repulsion.

Question 4 of 19

The stable oxidation state of Thallium (Tl) is:

Explanation: Due to the inert pair effect, the +1 oxidation state of thallium is thermodynamically more stable than +3.

Question 5 of 19

Which hydride of Group 15 is the strongest reducing agent?

Explanation: BiH3 has the longest and weakest E-H bond, making it the least stable and strongest reducing agent.

Question 6 of 19

The shape of XeF4 according to VSEPR theory is:

Explanation: XeF4 has 4 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs, resulting in a square planar geometry.

Question 7 of 19

Which of the following oxoacids of chlorine is the strongest acid?

Explanation: Acid strength increases with the oxidation state of chlorine due to greater dispersal of negative charge on the conjugate base; HClO4 has chlorine in +7 state.

Question 8 of 19

Which boron halide is a Lewis acid that readily forms addition compounds and does not hydrolyze completely?

Explanation: BF3 hydrolyzes to form fluoroboric acid (HBF4) because of strong B-F back-bonding and complex formation.

Question 9 of 19

Concentrated nitric acid oxidizes sulfur to:

Explanation: Concentrated HNO3 is a strong oxidizing agent that converts non-metals like sulfur directly into sulfuric acid.

Question 10 of 19

Which allotrope of carbon is a good electrical conductor?

Explanation: Graphite possesses free delocalized π-electrons within its parallel layers, enabling electrical conduction.

Question 11 of 19

The number of bridging hydrogen atoms in diborane (B2H6) is:

Explanation: Diborane features two 3c-2e B-H-B bridging hydrogen atoms and four terminal hydrogen atoms.

Question 12 of 19

Which noble gas does not form clathrate compounds readily?

Explanation: Helium atoms are extremely small and escape easily through the crystal lattice cavities of host lattices.

Question 13 of 19

The geometry of XeF2 is:

Explanation: XeF2 has 2 bond pairs and 3 lone pairs arranged in a trigonal bipyramidal electron geometry resulting in a linear molecular shape.

Question 14 of 19

Which element in group 14 forms maximum number of stable oxides with acidic nature?

Explanation: Carbon forms distinct acidic oxides like CO2 and neutral/acidic species in high variety.

Question 15 of 19

Which phosphorus allotrope is thermodynamically the most stable at room temperature?

Explanation: Black phosphorus has a layered puckered structure and is the most stable allotrope of phosphorus.

Question 16 of 19

The bleaching action of chlorine is due to:

Explanation: Chlorine bleaches colored substances permanently through an oxidation reaction in the presence of moisture.

Question 17 of 19

Which gas is liberated when concentrated sulfuric acid reacts with sodium chloride?

Explanation: NaCl reacts with concentrated H2SO4 to produce hydrogen chloride gas via a displacement reaction.

Question 18 of 19

The maximum covalency of nitrogen is limited to:

Explanation: Nitrogen lacks vacant d-orbitals in its valence shell, restricting its maximum covalency to 4 (octet rule).

Question 19 of 19

Which interhalogen compound has a pentagonal bipyramidal geometry?

Explanation: IF7 has 7 bond pairs and 0 lone pairs, resulting in a pentagonal bipyramidal molecular geometry.

Solution: Hydrogen sulfide (H2S) is a gas due to the absence of intermolecular hydrogen bonding, unlike water.

Solution: Both XeO3 and BrO3- have 26 valence electrons and pyramidal geometry with 3 bond pairs and 1 lone pair.

Solution: Boron is the first member of group 13 and exhibits distinctly anomalous behavior compared to aluminum and heavier congeners.

Question 6 of 10
The correct thermal stability order of group 16 hydrides is:

Solution: Thermal stability decreases down the group as E-H bond energy decreases with increasing atomic size.

Question 7 of 10
Which oxide of nitrogen is neutral?

Solution: Nitrous oxide (N2O) and nitric oxide (NO) are neutral oxides; others are acidic.

Question 8 of 10
Which halogen exhibits only a single oxidation state (-1) in its compounds?

Solution: Fluorine is the most electronegative element and exhibits only -1 oxidation state because it lacks d-orbitals for higher oxidation states.

Question 9 of 10

Solution: Strong heating of orthophosphoric acid eliminates water to form metaphosphoric acid.
