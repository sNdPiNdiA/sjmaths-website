Chemical Reactivity of p-Block Elements

The chemical reactivity of p-block elements is governed by their valence shell configurations, ranging from strongly metallic to non-metallic behavior. Periodic trends dictate variations in oxidation states, catenation, and bonding across groups 13 through 18.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Oxidation States and Inert Pair Effect

  • The inert pair effect becomes increasingly prominent down groups 13, 14, and 15 due to poor shielding by intervening d and f orbitals.
  • Lower oxidation states become thermodynamically more stable than the group oxidation state in heavier p-block elements (e.g., Tl(I), Pb(II), Bi(III)).
  • Higher oxidation states are strongly oxidizing for heavier elements, driven by the instability of retaining all valence electrons in bonding.

Anomalous Behavior of Second-Period Elements

  • Elements of the second period (B, C, N, O, F) exhibit anomalous behavior due to their small atomic radius, high electronegativity, and absence of vacant d-orbitals.
  • Maximum covalency for second-period elements is strictly four, whereas heavier congeners expand their octet utilizing vacant d-orbitals (e.g., [SiF6]2- exists, but [CF6]2- does not).
  • Second-period elements form strong multiple bonds (pπ-pπ) with themselves and other second-row elements, unlike heavier congeners.

Hydrides, Halides, and Oxides Reactivity Trends

  • Thermal stability of p-block hydrides decreases down the group due to a decrease in M-H bond enthalpy.
  • Lewis acidity of group 13 trihalides follows the order BI3 > BBr3 > BCl3 > BF3 due to π-back-bonding strength (pπ-pπ overlap).
  • Acidic character of oxides transitions systematically from basic (lower left) to amphoteric and finally acidic (upper right of the p-block).

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Electron gain enthalpy becomes more negative down the group.

Observation: Fluorine has a less negative electron gain enthalpy than chlorine. — Extreme small size of the F atom leads to high interelectronic repulsion within the compact 2p orbitals.

Exception Catenation capacity decreases down a group.

Observation: Carbon shows maximum catenation, but sulfur and phosphorus show significant catenation compared to adjacent elements. — High strength of C-C single bonds and appropriate single-bond energies facilitate extensive open-chain and cyclic structures.

Exception Trihalides of group 13 act as strong Lewis acids in the order BI3 > BBr3 > BCl3 > BF3.

Observation: BF3 is the weakest Lewis acid among boron trihalides. — Effective overlap of lone pair from 2p of fluorine into vacant 2p of boron forms a stable pπ-pπ back bond.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Thallium primarily exhibits a +1 oxidation state due to the inert pair effect, making Tl(III) a strong oxidizing agent.
  • Silicon forms stable polymers (silicones) containing Si-O-Si linkages, unlike carbon which forms simple oxides like CO2.
  • Nitrogen exists as a diatomic gas with a strong triple bond (N≡N), whereas phosphorus exists as tetrahedral P4 molecules.
  • Sulfur shows allotropy, with rhombic and monoclinic sulfur existing as puckered S8 rings.
  • Interhalogen compounds (e.g., ClF3, IF7) are more reactive than constituent halogens because element-halogen bonds are weaker than homonuclear X-X bonds.
  • Noble gases, previously thought inert, form stable fluorides and oxides (e.g., XeF2, XeF4, XeO3) due to high electronegativity and comparable ionization energy to oxygen.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming boron trifluoride is the strongest Lewis acid because fluorine is the most electronegative element.
  • Forgetting that carbon forms gaseous CO2 with discrete molecules, whereas silicon dioxide forms an extensive 3D covalent network.
  • Confusing the thermal stability order of hydrides with their acidic strength down a group.
  • Neglecting the tendency of group 13 and 14 elements to form cationic complexes or coordinate polymers in lower oxidation states.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Reactivity of p-Block Elements

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Group 13 Reactivity Trends

Group 13 elements show +3 and +1 oxidation states, with +1 stability increasing down the group due to the inert pair effect. Boron is distinctly non-metallic and forms electron-deficient compounds.

Group 14 Catenation and Allotropy

Carbon exhibits maximum catenation and multiple bonding capability. Metallic character increases down the group from carbon to lead, with Ge, Sn, and Pb displaying both +4 and +2 oxidation states.

Group 15 Hydrides and Oxides

Group 15 elements form hydrides MH3 whose basicity decreases and reducing character increases down the group. Nitrogen forms multiple stable oxides ranging from N2O to N2O5.

Halogens and Interhalogens

Halogens exhibit high reactivity as oxidizing agents, decreasing from fluorine to iodine. Interhalogen compounds are extremely reactive covalent species that undergo ready hydrolysis.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Inert Pair Effect (ns2 retention)
Reluctance of s-electrons to participate in chemical bonding due to poor shielding by d and f electrons.
Catenation (Self-linking property)
Ability of an element to form long chains or rings via covalent bonds with itself.
Back Bonding (pπ-pπ donation)
Donation of electron density from a filled p-orbital of a ligand into a vacant p- or d-orbital of a central atom.
Interhalogen (XX'n)
Compounds formed by the combination of two different halogen elements.
Disproportionation (Self-redox)
A chemical reaction where a single substance is simultaneously oxidized and reduced.
Amphoteric (Dual acid-base property)
Ability of an oxide or hydroxide to react with both acids and bases.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Order of Lewis acidity: BI3 > BBr3 > BCl3 > BF3
  • Thermal stability of hydrides: NH3 > PH3 > AsH3 > SbH3 > BiH3
  • Oxidizing power of halogens: F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2
  • Maximum covalency of period 2 elements is 4.
  • Inert pair effect stabilization order: Tl(I) > In(I) > Ga(I)

📌 Quick Recall

  • p-Block chemical reactivity is dictated by valence shell configurations and periodic trends.
  • Anomalous behavior of period 2 elements arises from small size, high electronegativity, and absence of d orbitals.
  • Heavier elements exhibit lower stable oxidation states due to the relativistic inert pair effect.
  • Bond strengths, catenation tendencies, and acid-base characteristics vary systematically across groups 13 to 18.

⚖️ Key Differences

Hydride thermal stability VS Hydride acidic strength

Thermal stability decreases down the group, whereas acidic strength increases due to decreasing M-H bond dissociation enthalpy.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following boron trihalides is the strongest Lewis acid?

Explanation: BI3 is the strongest Lewis acid because the tendency for pπ-pπ back-bonding is minimal compared to BF3, leaving the boron atom electron-deficient.

Question 2 of 18

The stability of the +1 oxidation state increases in the sequence:

Explanation: Due to the inert pair effect, the lower oxidation state (+1) becomes increasingly stable down Group 13, making Tl(I) most stable.

Question 3 of 18

Which element does not form pentafluoride?

Explanation: Nitrogen lacks vacant d-orbitals in its valence shell and cannot expand its octet to form pentahalides like NF5.

Question 4 of 18

Which of the following hydrides is the most thermally stable?

Explanation: NH3 has the highest M-H bond dissociation enthalpy due to small size and high overlap, making it the most thermally stable group 15 hydride.

Question 5 of 18

The maximum covalency of silicon in [SiF6]2- is:

Explanation: Silicon utilizes vacant d-orbitals to expand its octet, achieving a maximum covalency of 6 in species like [SiF6]2-.

Question 6 of 18

Which halogen has the highest negative electron gain enthalpy?

Explanation: Chlorine has the highest negative electron gain enthalpy because fluorine's small 2p orbitals experience high interelectronic repulsion upon accepting an extra electron.

Question 7 of 18

The acidic character of group 16 dioxides follows which order?

Explanation: Acidic character of non-metal oxides decreases down the group as metallic character increases.

Question 8 of 18

Which of the following is an amphoteric oxide?

Explanation: Aluminium oxide (Al2O3) dissolves in both acids and bases, classifying it as an amphoteric oxide.

Question 9 of 18

White phosphorus reacts with caustic soda to form phosphine and:

Explanation: White phosphorus undergoes disproportionation with NaOH to yield PH3 and sodium hypophosphite (NaH2PO2).

Question 10 of 18

Which interhalogen compound has a pentagonal bipyramidal geometry?

Explanation: IF7 has 7 bond pairs and 0 lone pairs on iodine, resulting in sp3d3 hybridization and a pentagonal bipyramidal geometry.

Question 11 of 18

The property of catenation is strongest in:

Explanation: Carbon has the highest catenation ability due to its small size and high C-C single bond enthalpy.

Question 12 of 18

Which noble gas forms the maximum number of compounds?

Explanation: Xenon has the lowest ionization enthalpy among stable noble gases, allowing it to react readily with highly electronegative fluorine and oxygen.

Question 13 of 18

The shape of XeF4 according to VSEPR theory is:

Explanation: XeF4 has 4 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs, resulting in sp3d2 hybridization and a square planar molecular geometry.

Question 14 of 18

Which of the following acids is the strongest?

Explanation: Acid strength of oxoacids increases with an increase in oxidation number of the central chlorine atom; HClO4 has chlorine in the +7 oxidation state.

Question 15 of 18

Red phosphorus is less reactive than white phosphorus because:

Explanation: Red phosphorus consists of a polymeric chain of P4 tetrahedral units linked together, requiring more energy to break down than discrete tetrahedral white phosphorus.

Question 16 of 18

Which element exhibits allotropy in group 13?

Explanation: Boron exhibits multiple allotropic forms, primarily amorphous and crystalline (α- and β-rhombohedral) modifications.

Question 17 of 18

The hydrolysis of SiCl4 yields:

Explanation: Hydrolysis of silicon tetrachloride proceeds via nucleophilic attack of water on silicon, yielding silicic acid [Si(OH)4] or hydrated silica.

Question 18 of 18

Which of the following compounds does not exist?

Explanation: SH6 does not exist because hydrogen is not sufficiently electronegative to promote sulfur into a high oxidation state required for hexacoordination.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following species is hydrolysed most easily?

Solution: SiCl4 is hydrolysed readily because silicon has vacant d-orbitals that coordinate water molecules for nucleophilic attack, unlike carbon in CCl4.

Question 2 of 10
The correct decreasing order of electron gain enthalpy with negative sign for group 17 elements is:

Solution: Chlorine has the highest electron gain enthalpy, followed by bromine, fluorine (due to small size repulsions), and iodine.

Question 3 of 10
What is the product formed when concentrated nitric acid reacts with iodine?

Solution: Concentrated HNO3 oxidizes iodine to iodic acid (HIO3) along with the evolution of NO2 gas.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following noble gas compounds is tetrahedral?

Solution: XeO4 has 4 double bonds (8 bonding electrons) and 0 lone pairs, giving a regular tetrahedral geometry with sp3 hybridization.

Question 5 of 10
The brown ring test for nitrates depends on the formation of a coordination complex whose formula is:

Solution: The brown ring complex is pentaaquanitrosyliron(II) sulfate, [Fe(H2O)5(NO)]SO4.

Question 6 of 10
Which element of group 15 exhibits metallic character predominantly?

Solution: Metallic character increases down group 15, making bismuth the most metallic element in the group.

Question 7 of 10
In graphite, carbon atoms are hybridized as:

Solution: Graphite has layered hexagonal sheets where each carbon is sp2 hybridized, forming a delocalized π-electron system.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following oxoacids of chlorine is the weakest acid?

Solution: HClO (hypochlorous acid) has the lowest oxidation state (+1) for chlorine and is the weakest acid among chlorine oxoacids.

Question 9 of 10
The geometry of ClF3 molecule is:

Solution: ClF3 has 3 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs, resulting in sp3d hybridization with a distorted T-shaped geometry.

Question 10 of 10
Which allotrope of phosphorus is thermodynamically most stable at room temperature?

Solution: Black phosphorus has a layered puckered structure and is the most thermodynamically stable allotrope of phosphorus under normal conditions.
