Inorganic Chemistry

Inorganic Polymers

Inorganic polymers are macromolecules possessing backbones consisting of alternating or contiguous atoms other than carbon, such as silicon, phosphorus, boron, and nitrogen. They exhibit distinct thermal stability, mechanical flexibility, and electrical properties compared to conventional carbon-based organic polymers.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Silicones and Siloxane Polymers

  • Silicones are synthesized via the hydrolysis of alkylchlorosilanes followed by condensation polymerization.
  • Chain termination is achieved using trimethylchlorosilane ((CH3)3SiCl) which caps the growing siloxane chain.
  • The Si-O-Si bond angle is exceptionally wide (approx. 140-180°) due to pπ-dπ back bonding between oxygen and silicon.
  • Cross-linking in silicone resins is controlled by the inclusion of trichlorosilanes (RSiCl3) during synthesis.

Polyphosphazenes and Borazynes

  • Polyphosphazenes feature a backbone of alternating phosphorus and nitrogen atoms with side substituents attached to phosphorus.
  • Trimeric phosphonitrilic chloride undergoes thermal ring-opening at 250°C to yield high molecular weight poly(dichlorophosphazene).
  • Borazine (B3N3H6) is known as inorganic benzene due to its planar ring structure and isoelectronic nature.
  • Unlike benzene, borazine undergoes addition reactions readily because B-N bonds are polarized with partial positive charge on B and negative charge on N.

Silicates and Isoelectronic Analogues

  • Silicate structures are classified based on the degree of sharing of oxygen atoms per [SiO4]4- tetrahedron.
  • Orthosilicates contain isolated [SiO4]4- units, whereas pyrosilicates share one oxygen vertex.
  • Cyclic and chain silicates share two oxygen vertices, forming rings or infinite single chains with empirical formula (SiO3)n2n-.
  • Sheet silicates share three oxygen atoms per tetrahedron, yielding layered structures responsible for cleavage properties in micas and clays.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Organohalosilanes undergo complete hydrolysis to yield stable silanols.

Observation: Mono-functional trialkylchlorosilanes (R3SiCl) yield dimers (R3Si-O-SiR3) and cannot form long polymer chains. — They lack functional sites for further condensation, acting solely as chain terminators.

Exception Inorganic ring systems analogous to aromatic hydrocarbons undergo typical electrophilic aromatic substitution.

Observation: Borazine undergoes rapid hydrolysis and addition reactions across B-N bonds instead of standard substitution. — The large electronegativity difference between boron and nitrogen polarizes the π-cloud, localizing electron density near nitrogen.

Exception Higher analogues of carbon chains form stable homcaters easily.

Observation: Polysilanes (R2Si)n are prone to photochemical and thermal oxidation. — The Si-Si bond energy (approx. 226 kJ mol-1) is significantly lower than the C-C bond energy, making catenated silicon more labile.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Silicones are water-repellent organosilicon polymers with the general formula (R2SiO)n.
  • Trimeric phosphonitrilic chloride ((NPCl2)3) is synthesized from phosphorus pentachloride and ammonium chloride.
  • Borazine (B3N3H6) is prepared by reacting boron trichloride with ammonium chloride in boiling chlorobenzene.
  • Ultraphosphates are condensed inorganic polymers containing highly cross-linked P-O-P networks.
  • The coordination number of silicon in monomeric silicates is 4, adopting a tetrahedral geometry.
  • Polyphosphazenes exhibit extreme low-temperature flexibility due to the low barrier to torsional rotation around the P-N bond.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the structural role of (R3SiCl) as a chain extender instead of a chain terminator.
  • Assuming borazine undergoes electrophilic aromatic substitution identically to benzene without considering bond polarity.
  • Mistaking the Si-O-Si bond angle for 109.5°; it is actually much wider (140-180°) due to pπ-dπ interactions.
  • Failing to account for the exact oxygen-to-silicon ratio when identifying sheet versus chain silicates.

⚡ Quick Revision: Inorganic Polymers

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Synthesis and Structure of Silicones

Silicones are synthesized via hydrolysis of chlorosilanes and subsequent condensation of silanols, yielding linear or cross-linked polysiloxanes.

Polyphosphazenes Chemistry

Polyphosphazenes feature alternating phosphorus and nitrogen atoms, formed by thermal ring-opening of chlorophosphazene trimers followed by nucleophilic substitution.

Silicate Mineral Classification

Silicates are classified into ortho, pyro, cyclic, chain, sheet, and framework types based on the degree of sharing of vertex oxygen atoms in [SiO4]4- units.

Inorganic Aromaticity in Borazine

Borazine is isoelectronic and isostructural with benzene, yet exhibits distinct chemical reactivity due to polarized B-N bonds.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Inorganic benzene analogue containing B and N.
Polyphosphazene ((NPX2)n)
Inorganic polymer with a P-N backbone.
Orthosilicate ([SiO4]4-)
Monomeric silicate unit containing isolated tetrahedra.
Silanol (R3SiOH)
Silicon analogue of an alcohol.
Back Bonding (pπ-dπ)
Overlap between filled p-orbital of O and vacant d-orbital of Si.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • General formula of silicones: (R2SiO)n
  • Structure of borazine: planar six-membered ring of alternating B and N
  • Chain terminating agent in silicones: (CH3)3SiCl
  • Basic structural unit of all silicates: [SiO4]4-
  • Hybridization of silicon in silicates: sp3

📌 Quick Recall

  • Inorganic polymers possess heteroatomic backbones providing high thermal stability.
  • Silicones offer exceptional water repellency, flexibility, and thermal resistance.
  • Silicates exhibit structural diversity ranging from discrete units to complex 3D frameworks.
  • Polyphosphazenes allow versatile functionalization through side-group substitution.

⚖️ Key Differences

Silicone VS Silicon

Silicon is a chemical element (Si), whereas silicone is a synthetic organosilicon polymer.

Question 1 of 20

Which compound acts as a chain terminator in the preparation of silicones?

Explanation: R3SiCl has only one reactive chlorine atom, allowing it to cap the growing siloxane chain and stop polymerization.

Question 2 of 20

What is the general formula of cyclic silicates?

Explanation: Cyclic and chain silicates share two oxygen atoms per tetrahedron, leading to the empirical formula (SiO3)n2n-.

Question 3 of 20

Borazine is commonly known as:

Explanation: Borazine (B3N3H6) is known as inorganic benzene due to its ring size, electron count, and structural similarity to benzene.

Question 4 of 20

What is the hybridization of silicon in the [SiO4]4- tetrahedron?

Explanation: Silicon forms four single covalent bonds with oxygen in a tetrahedral arrangement, corresponding to sp3 hybridization.

Question 5 of 20

Which of the following properties is characteristic of polyphosphazenes?

Explanation: Polyphosphazenes possess low barriers to torsional rotation around the P-N backbone, conferring remarkable low-temperature flexibility.

Question 6 of 20

The Si-O-Si bond angle in siloxanes is unusually large due to:

Explanation: Overlap between the filled p-orbital of oxygen and the vacant d-orbital of silicon creates partial double bond character, widening the bond angle.

Question 7 of 20

Which silicate mineral contains discrete, isolated [SiO4]4- tetrahedra?

Explanation: Olivine (e.g., (Mg, Fe)2SiO4) is an orthosilicate containing isolated tetrahedra bound by metal cations.

Question 8 of 20

What is the starting material for synthesizing poly(dichlorophosphazene)?

Explanation: Trimeric phosphonitrilic chloride is synthesized from PCl5 and NH4Cl, which then thermally polymerizes.

Question 9 of 20

Which reagent is used to introduce cross-linking in silicone polymers?

Explanation: Trichlorosilanes (RSiCl3) have three functional groups, allowing branching and cross-linking during condensation.

Question 10 of 20

The empirical formula of sheet silicates (phyllosilicates) is:

Explanation: Sheet silicates share three oxygen atoms per tetrahedron, giving the characteristic stoichiometry (Si2O5)n2n-.

Question 11 of 20

Why does borazine readily undergo addition reactions compared to benzene?

Explanation: The electronegativity difference between B and N polarizes the ring bonds, facilitating nucleophilic attack at boron and electrophilic attack at nitrogen.

Question 12 of 20

What is the coordination number of silicon in quartz?

Explanation: Quartz is a framework silicate where each silicon atom is tetrahedrally coordinated to four oxygen atoms (CN = 4).

Question 13 of 20

Which of the following is an example of an inorganic polymer?

Explanation: Polyphosphazene has a backbone of phosphorus and nitrogen atoms, classifying it as an inorganic polymer.

Question 14 of 20

What is the product formed when dimethyldichlorosilane undergoes complete hydrolysis?

Explanation: Hydrolysis of (CH3)2SiCl2 yields dimethylsilanediol, which condenses into linear chains and cyclic siloxanes.

Question 15 of 20

In pyrosilicates, how many oxygen atoms are shared per tetrahedron?

Explanation: Pyrosilicates (e.g., [Si2O7]6-) share one oxygen atom between two tetrahedra.

Question 16 of 20

Which gas is evolved during the moisture-cure process of acetoxy silicones?

Explanation: Acetoxysilanes react with atmospheric moisture to form siloxanes and release acetic acid as a byproduct.

Question 17 of 20

The structure of beryl is an example of which type of silicate?

Explanation: Beryl (Be3Al2Si6O18) contains six-membered rings of [SiO4]4- tetrahedra, making it a cyclic silicate.

Question 18 of 20

Which element replaces carbon in carboranes?

Explanation: Carboranes are cluster compounds containing boron and carbon atoms within a polyhedral cage.

Question 19 of 20

What is the commercial name for polymers based on tetrafluoroethylene?

Explanation: Teflon is polytetrafluoroethylene, an organic fluoropolymer, distinct from inorganic silicones.

Question 20 of 20

Polysilanes are characterized by backbones composed of:

Explanation: Polysilanes possess catenated silicon-silicon single bond backbones, analogous to alkanes.
