Inorganic Chemistry

p-Block Elements

The p-block elements occupy groups 13 to 18 of the periodic table where the last electron enters the outermost p-orbital. Their general valence electronic configuration is ns2np1-6 (except helium).

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Group 13 and Group 14 Trends

  • Group 13 elements exhibit oxidation states of +3 and +1, with the stability of the +1 state increasing down the group due to the inert pair effect.
  • Boron is a non-metal with an icosahedral structure in its crystalline allotropes, displaying high melting points and covalent bonding.
  • Group 14 elements show +4 and +2 oxidation states, where carbon and silicon form predominantly covalent compounds while lead stabilizes in the +2 state.
  • Catelanation is maximum in carbon and decreases down group 14 due to decreasing M-M bond dissociation enthalpy.

Group 15 to Group 17 Chemistry

  • Group 15 elements (pnictogens) form hydrides whose reducing character and basic strength increase from NH3 to BiH3.
  • Group 16 elements (chalcogens) show variable oxidation states of -2, +2, +4, and +6, with oxygen strictly lacking d-orbitals to exceed a covalency of four.
  • Group 17 elements (halogens) possess the highest negative electron gain enthalpy in their respective periods, decreasing in magnitude from chlorine to iodine.
  • Fluorine exhibits an anomalously low F-F bond dissociation enthalpy due to high lone-pair repulsion among small atoms.

Noble Gases and Interhalogens

  • Group 18 elements are chemically inert due to stable octet configurations, though heavier elements like xenon form fluorides and oxides under specialized conditions.
  • Xenon fluorides (XeF2, XeF4, XeF6) are powerful fluorinating agents and hydrolyze readily upon contact with moisture.
  • Interhalogen compounds are more reactive than halogens because A-B bonds are weaker than homonuclear A-A or B-B bonds.
  • The structure of XeF4 is square planar with sp3d2 hybridization and two lone pairs.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Electron gain enthalpy becomes less negative down a group.

Observation: Chlorine has a more negative electron gain enthalpy than fluorine. — Small atomic size of fluorine creates high interelectronic repulsion within its compact 2p orbitals.

Exception Hydride boiling points increase regularly with molecular mass down a group.

Observation: Water (H2O), Ammonia (NH3), and Hydrogen Fluoride (HF) have unusually high boiling points. — Presence of extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding.

Exception Maximum covalency of group 2 elements is limited to four.

Observation: Boron and carbon cannot expand their octets, whereas aluminum and silicon form six-coordinate species like [AlF6]3-. — Availability of vacant d-orbitals in the third period and beyond.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Boric acid acts as a Lewis acid by accepting a hydroxyl ion, not by donating a proton.
  • Carbon monoxide forms coordinate covalent bonds with transition metals to yield metal carbonyls.
  • Silicones are organosilicon polymers containing R2SiO repeating units.
  • White phosphorus is reactive, toxic, and consists of discrete tetrahedral P4 molecules.
  • Ozone is a powerful oxidizing agent that quantitatively liberates iodine from neutral potassium iodide solution.
  • Bleaching powder is chemically represented as Ca(OCl)2·CaCl2·Ca(OH)2·2H2O.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming boron trifluoride acts as a Brønsted-Lowry acid instead of recognizing it as a Lewis acid.
  • Confusing the oxidation state of sulfur in Caro's acid (+6) and Marshall's acid (+6) with peroxide linkages.
  • Neglecting the lone pairs when predicting geometries of interhalogen compounds using VSEPR theory.
  • Assuming nitrogen can expand its octet to form pentachloride analogous to phosphorus.

⚡ Quick Revision: p-Block Elements

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Group 13 Anomalous Boron

Boron exhibits diagonal relationship with silicon and forms electron-deficient hydrides like diborane (B2H6) featuring 3-center 2-electron (3c-2e) bonds.

Group 14 Allotropy and Catenation

Carbon shows extensive catenation and forms multiple bonds (pπ-pπ overlap), whereas heavier group 14 elements form single bonds and exhibit metallic character.

Group 15 Hydrides and Oxides

Nitrogen forms stable dinitrogen with a triple bond, while heavier pnictogens form polymeric structures and hydrides with decreasing thermal stability and increasing basic strength.

Group 18 Noble Gas Reactivity

Neils Bartlett synthesized the first noble gas compound (O2+[PtF6]-), leading to the preparation of xenon fluorides and oxides.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Inert Pair Effect (ns2 reluctance)
Non-participation of ns electrons in bonding due to poor shielding by d and f electrons.
Catelanation (Self-linking)
Ability of an element to form chains of identical atoms through covalent bonds.
Allotropy (Structural variation)
Existence of an element in two or more different physical forms in the same phase.
Interhalogen (ABn)
Binary compounds formed by two different halogen elements.
Banana Bond (3c-2e bond)
A three-center two-electron bond present in electron-deficient hydrides like diborane.
Oxoacid (HnXOm)
An acid containing hydrogen, oxygen, and at least one other element.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • B2H6 contains two terminal B-H bonds and two bridged 3c-2e B-H-B bonds.
  • HNO3 acts as a strong oxidizing agent, reacting with metals to evolve NO or NO2.
  • Sulfuric acid is manufactured via the Contact Process involving V2O5 catalyst.
  • Chlorine gas is prepared by the Deacon process via oxidation of HCl with O2 over CuCl2.
  • Xenon fluorides require nickel vessels and strict anhydrous conditions for synthesis.

📌 Quick Recall

  • p-block properties transition from non-metallic to metallic down every group.
  • Oxidation state ranges span from group number minus 10 to group number.
  • Anomalous behavior of second-period elements stems from small size, high electronegativity, and lack of d-orbitals.
  • Reactivity of halogens decreases from fluorine to iodine.

⚖️ Key Differences

White Phosphorus VS Red Phosphorus

White phosphorus is discrete P4 tetrahedral molecules, reactive and luminescent; red phosphorus is a polymeric chain network and stable in air.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following hydrides has the highest boiling point?

Explanation: Ammonia forms intermolecular hydrogen bonds, leading to an abnormally high boiling point compared to heavier group 15 hydrides.

Question 2 of 19

The hybridization of xenon in XeF4 is:

Explanation: XeF4 has 4 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs, totaling 6 electron pairs, which corresponds to sp3d2 hybridization and square planar geometry.

Question 3 of 19

Which halogen has the highest electron gain enthalpy?

Explanation: Chlorine has the highest (most negative) electron gain enthalpy because fluorine's small 2p orbitals experience high interelectronic repulsion.

Question 4 of 19

Maximum coordination number of boron is:

Explanation: Due to the absence of vacant d-orbitals in its valence shell, boron cannot expand its octet beyond a coordination number of 4.

Question 5 of 19

Which of the following is a pseudo-halogen?

Explanation: The cyanide ion (CN-) dimerizes to cyanogen (CN)2, mimicking the chemical behavior of halogens.

Question 6 of 19

Boric acid is:

Explanation: Boric acid accepts a hydroxyl ion from water to act as a weak monobasic Lewis acid.

Question 7 of 19

The shape of SF4 molecule according to VSEPR theory is:

Explanation: SF4 has 4 bond pairs and 1 lone pair, resulting in a seesaw shape based on trigonal bipyramidal electron geometry.

Question 8 of 19

Which element does not form pentahalide?

Explanation: Nitrogen lacks vacant d-orbitals in its valence shell and cannot expand its octet to form pentahalides.

Question 9 of 19

The reddish-brown gas evolved when concentrated nitric acid reacts with copper is:

Explanation: Reaction of copper with concentrated HNO3 produces nitrogen dioxide (NO2), which is a reddish-brown gas.

Question 10 of 19

Which allotrope of carbon is thermodynamically most stable at room temperature and pressure?

Explanation: Graphite is the standard reference state and most stable allotrope of carbon at 1 atm and 298 K.

Question 11 of 19

Which of the following is an amphoteric oxide?

Explanation: Aluminum oxide (Al2O3) reacts with both acids and bases, classifying it as amphoteric.

Question 12 of 19

The number of bridging hydrogen atoms in diborane (B2H6) is:

Explanation: Diborane has 4 terminal hydrogen atoms and 2 bridging hydrogen atoms held by 3c-2e bonds.

Question 13 of 19

Which noble gas is radioactive?

Explanation: Radon is a radioactive noble gas formed as a decay product of radium.

Question 14 of 19

The strongest oxidizing agent among the halogens is:

Explanation: Fluorine has the highest standard reduction potential and acts as the strongest oxidizing agent.

Question 15 of 19

Which gas is evolved when sodium thiosulfate reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid?

Explanation: Acidification of thiosulfate yields sulfur dioxide gas along with elemental sulfur precipitate.

Question 16 of 19

The geometry of ClF3 is:

Explanation: ClF3 has 3 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs, resulting in a T-shaped molecular geometry.

Question 17 of 19

Which of the following acids contains a peroxide linkage?

Explanation: Peroxodisulfuric acid (Marshall's acid, H2S2O8) contains a -O-O- peroxide linkage.

Question 18 of 19

The correct order of acidic strength of hydrogen halides is:

Explanation: Acidic strength increases down the group due to decreasing bond dissociation enthalpy from HF to HI.

Question 19 of 19

Commercial production of nitric acid is carried out by:

Explanation: The Ostwald process involves catalytic oxidation of ammonia to produce nitric acid.

What is the oxidation state of sulfur in sodium tetrathionate (Na2S4O6)?

Solution: In Na2S4O6, two sulfur atoms have an oxidation state of +5 (terminal) and two have 0 (central).

Question 2 of 10
Which gas is liberated when concentrated sulfuric acid is heated with sodium chloride?

Solution: Reaction of NaCl with concentrated H2SO4 produces hydrogen chloride gas.

Question 3 of 10
The number of P-O-P bonds in cyclic metaphosphoric acid (HPO3)3 is:

Solution: Cyclic trimetaphosphoric acid has a six-membered ring containing alternating phosphorus and oxygen atoms with 3 P-O-P linkages.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following compounds does not exist?

Solution: Nitrogen cannot form pentahalides like NCl5 because it lacks vacant d-orbitals.

Question 5 of 10
What happens when white phosphorus is heated with concentrated NaOH solution in an inert atmosphere?

Solution: White phosphorus undergoes disproportionation with aqueous alkali to give phosphine (PH3) and sodium hypophosphite.

Question 6 of 10
The total number of lone pairs on xenon in XeF6 is:

Solution: XeF6 has 6 bonding electrons and 1 lone pair on the xenon atom, giving distorted octahedral geometry.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following is the strongest base?

Solution: Ammonia has the highest electron density on nitrogen and is the strongest base among group 15 hydrides.

Question 8 of 10
The catalyst used in the manufacture of sulfuric acid by the Contact Process is:

Solution: Vanadium pentoxide (V2O5) is used to catalyze the oxidation of SO2 to SO3.

Question 9 of 10
Which halogen does not exhibit positive oxidation states like +1, +3, +5, +7?

Solution: Fluorine is the most electronegative element and exhibits only a -1 oxidation state in its compounds.

Question 10 of 10
The correct order of bond angles in group 15 hydrides is:

Solution: Bond angles decrease down the group from ~107° in NH3 to ~91° in BiH3 due to decreasing electronegativity of the central atom.
