Inorganic Chemistry

Atomic Radii

Atomic radius represents the distance from the center of the nucleus to the outermost shell of electrons. It generally decreases across a period and increases down a group in the periodic table.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Periodic Trends Across Periods and Groups

  • Atomic radii decrease across a period due to an increase in effective nuclear charge (Zeff) while principal quantum number remains constant.
  • Atomic radii increase down a group due to the addition of successive principal shell levels (n) and increased inner-core electron shielding.
  • The outermost electrons experience higher electrostatic attraction from the nucleus as atomic number increases in a given period.

Types of Atomic Radii Definitions

  • Covalent radius is defined as one-half of the distance between the nuclei of two identical covalently bonded atoms in a homonuclear molecule.
  • Van der Waals radius is half of the distance between two non-bonded isolated atoms of the same element in solid state and is significantly larger than covalent radius.
  • Ionic radius determines the size of ions, where cations are smaller than their parent atoms due to loss of electrons and increased Zeff.
  • Anions are larger than their parent atoms due to interelectronic repulsion expanding the electron cloud.

Transition Metal Contractions and Lanthanide Contraction

  • Lanthanide contraction is the progressive decrease in atomic and ionic radii of elements in the lanthanide series from La to Lu.
  • It is caused by the poor shielding of nuclear charge by 4f electrons, which fail to effectively offset the added positive charge.
  • Due to lanthanide contraction, the atomic radii of second and third transition series elements (e.g., Zr and Hf) become nearly identical.
  • Scandide contraction (d-block contraction) occurs due to poor shielding by 3d electrons, impacting post-transition elements like Ga.

Conditions: Schomaker-Stevenson equation for heteronuclear diatomic molecules AB
Units: Angstroms (Å) or picometers (pm)
Metallic Radius Relation
rmetallic = d / 2
Conditions: Half of the internuclear distance between two adjacent metal atoms in a metallic crystal lattice
Units: pm or Å
Ionic Radius Ratio
ρ = r+ / r-
Conditions: Used for predicting coordination numbers and crystal geometry
Units: Dimensionless
Slater's Effective Nuclear Charge
Zeff = Z - S
Conditions: Applicable for estimating shielding effects of multi-electron systems
Units: Dimensionless (Z and S are integers/decimals)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Atomic radius increases monotonically down every group in the periodic table.

Observation: Post-lanthanide elements like Hf have radii nearly identical to their second-row congeners like Zr. — Lanthanide contraction compensates for the addition of an extra electron shell due to poor 4f shielding.

Exception Noble gases have smaller atomic radii than preceding halogens due to increased Zeff.

Observation: Noble gases possess the largest atomic radius in their respective periods. — Radii for noble gases are measured as van der Waals radii rather than covalent radii, making them artificially larger.

Exception Size of isoelectronic species decreases with increasing nuclear charge.

Observation: Anions show a sudden jump in size relative to cations despite equal electron counts. — Higher electron-to-proton ratio in anions expands the electron cloud via increased interelectronic repulsion.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Isoelectronic Series Rule
Radius ∝ 1 / Z

Application: For species with identical electron counts (e.g., N3-, O2-, F-, Na+, Mg2+, Al3+), order by nuclear charge Z; highest Z gives smallest radius.

Slater's Shielding Shortcut
S = 0.35(n-1 electrons) + 0.85(n-2 electrons)

Application: Quickly estimate shielding constant for s and p valence electrons to compare effective nuclear charge.

Transition Metal Pair Mnemonic
Zr ≈ Hf and Nb ≈ Ta

Application: Remember that elements in the 4d and 5d series following the lanthanides have almost identical radii due to lanthanide contraction.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Covalent radius is always less than the corresponding van der Waals radius for any given element.
  • Cation radius is smaller than neutral atom radius; anion radius is larger than neutral atom radius.
  • Zeff increases across a period from left to right, causing a progressive drop in atomic radii.
  • Group 13 elements show anomalous size trends due to d- and f-block contractions.
  • Lanthanide contraction causes the radii of Zr (160 pm) and Hf (159 pm) to be virtually identical.
  • Atomic radii reach a maximum value at alkali metals in each period.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing covalent radii with van der Waals radii when comparing non-metals like chlorine.
  • Assuming down-group atomic radii remain constant; always account for the principal quantum shell addition.
  • Neglecting the effect of interelectronic repulsion when sizing isoelectronic anions.
  • Forgetting that noble gas radii are measured using van der Waals forces.

⚡ Quick Revision: Atomic Radii

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition and Measurement

Atomic radius defines atomic size, categorized into covalent, metallic, and van der Waals radii depending on bonding.

Periodic Variations

Size decreases across periods due to increasing Zeff and increases down groups due to principal shell additions.

Ionic Radii

Cations are smaller and anions are larger than their neutral precursors based on electron-proton imbalances.

Contractions in d and f Blocks

Poor shielding by d and f electrons results in transition and lanthanide contractions, equalizing radii in heavier rows.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Covalent Radius (rcov)
Half of the internuclear distance between two singly bonded identical atoms (Å).
Van der Waals Radius (rvdw)
Half of the distance between closest approach of non-bonded atoms in a crystal (Å).
Metallic Radius (rmet)
Half of the internuclear distance between two adjacent metal ions in a lattice (Å).
Effective Nuclear Charge (Zeff)
Net positive charge experienced by valence electrons (Dimensionless).
Lanthanide Contraction (d-f shielding)
Steady decrease in atomic size across the lanthanide series (pm).
Isoelectronic Species (Same e- count)
Atoms or ions possessing identical numbers of electrons.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • rvdw > rmetallic > rcovalent for any given element.
  • Zeff = Z - S (Slater's rule).
  • Cation < Neutral Atom < Anion for a given element.
  • Lanthanide contraction results in Zr ≈ Hf size equivalence.
  • Atomic radii decrease from left to right across a period.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Atomic radius is determined by balance between nuclear pull and electron shielding.
  • Periodic trends are governed by Zeff and principal quantum number n.
  • Irregularities in group trends stem from d- and f-orbital contractions.
  • Ionic size dictates crystal lattice structures and coordination numbers.

⚖️ Key Differences

Covalent Radius VS Van der Waals Radius

Covalent radius involves overlapping orbitals in chemical bonds; van der Waals radius involves non-bonded contact distances.
