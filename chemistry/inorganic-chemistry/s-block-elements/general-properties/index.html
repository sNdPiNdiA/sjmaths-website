Inorganic Chemistry

General Properties of s-Block Elements

The s-block elements comprise Groups 1 and 2 of the periodic table, characterized by their outermost s1 and s2 valence electron configurations. They exhibit low ionization enthalpies, high electropositive character, and strong reducing properties that intensify down the group.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Atomic and Physical Properties

  • Alkali and alkaline earth metals possess the largest atomic and ionic radii in their respective periods due to the addition of a new shell.
  • First ionization enthalpy decreases down the group as the valence electron gets progressively further from the nucleus and is shielded by inner core electrons.
  • Melting and boiling points are exceptionally low compared to d-block elements, decreasing down the group due to weaker metallic bonding as atomic size increases.
  • Group 1 metals display characteristic flame coloration because thermal energy promotes valence electrons to higher energy levels, emitting visible light upon relaxation.

Chemical Reactivity and Reducing Nature

  • All s-block elements are strong reducing agents, with lithium exhibiting the highest negative standard reduction potential in aqueous solution due to its exceptionally high hydration energy.
  • Alkali metals react vigorously with oxygen to form oxides, peroxides, or superoxides, depending on the size of the metal cation and lattice stabilization.
  • Alkaline earth metals burn in dinitrogen to form stable ionic nitrides (M3N2), a unique property distinguishing them from Group 1 elements (except lithium).
  • Group 2 metals are less reactive than Group 1 counterparts owing to higher nuclear charge and smaller ionic radii.

Solubility and Thermal Stability Trends

  • Thermal stability of alkali metal carbonates and nitrates increases down the group as the larger cation stabilizes larger polyatomic anions via lattice energy effects.
  • Solubility of Group 2 hydroxides increases down the group because lattice enthalpy decreases more rapidly than hydration enthalpy.
  • Solubility of Group 2 sulfates decreases down the group due to a sharp drop in hydration energy as the cation size increases.
  • Hydration energy of alkali metal ions decreases in the order Li+ > Na+ > K+ > Rb+ > Cs+.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkali metals form normal oxides exclusively upon reaction with oxygen.

Observation: Sodium forms peroxide (Na2O2) and potassium, rubidium, and cesium form superoxides (MO2). — Large cations stabilize larger, highly charged anions via lattice energy effects (stabilization of large anions by large cations).

Exception Lithium shares the closest chemical resemblance with elements down its own group.

Observation: Lithium exhibits a diagonal relationship with magnesium, showing structural and chemical similarities. — Similar charge-to-radius ratios (ionic potential) between Li+ and Mg2+ ions.

Exception All alkaline earth metal sulfates are sparingly soluble in water.

Observation: Beryllium sulfate (BeSO4) is readily soluble in water. — High hydration enthalpy of the small Be2+ ion strongly overcomes the lattice energy.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Lithium has the lowest melting point and highest standard reduction potential among alkali metals.
  • Beryllium hydroxide is amphoteric, whereas other Group 2 hydroxides are strictly basic.
  • Cations of s-block elements impart characteristic colors to bunsen flames due to low excitation energy requirements.
  • Superoxides contain the paramagnetic [O2]- ion with a bond order of 1.5.
  • Beryllium does not impart any color to the flame because its ionization energy is too high.
  • Sodium metal is stored under kerosene or paraffin oil to prevent rapid oxidation and reaction with moisture.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the order of reducing power in water with ionization enthalpy values; Li is strongest reducing agent in water due to high hydration energy.
  • Assuming all alkali metals form superoxides; only K, Rb, and Cs form superoxides, while Na forms peroxide and Li forms normal oxide.
  • Neglecting the diagonal relationship between Lithium and Magnesium in periodic property questions.
  • Forgetting that beryllium shares chemical similarities with aluminum rather than barium.

⚡ Quick Revision: General Properties of s-Block Elements

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electronic Configuration and Periodicity

Group 1 and Group 2 elements have outer configurations of ns1 and ns2. Atomic and ionic radii increase down the groups while ionization enthalpies decrease.

Electropositive and Reducing Character

High electropositivity dictates strong metallic bonding and robust reducing behavior, with lithium acting as the most powerful reducing agent in aqueous solution.

Oxide and Salt Formation Trends

Alkali metals react with oxygen yielding oxides, peroxides, or superoxides. Thermal stability of carbonates and nitrates increases down the group due to lattice energy stabilization.

Anomalous Behavior of Lithium and Beryllium

Li and Be display anomalous behavior due to extremely small atomic size and high charge-to-radius ratio, resulting in pronounced covalent character and diagonal relationships.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Hydration Enthalpy (ΔHhyd)
Energy released when one mole of gaseous ions combines with water to form hydrated ions, measured in kJ mol-1.
Superoxide (MO2)
Compound containing the [O2]- radical anion where oxygen has an apparent oxidation state of -1/2.
Diagonal Relationship (Periodic diagonal trend)
Similarities in chemical properties between diagonally adjacent elements in adjacent groups and periods (e.g., Li and Mg).
Lattice Energy (U)
Energy released when gaseous ions are brought together to form one mole of a solid ionic crystal lattice.
Ionization Enthalpy (ΔHi)
Energy required to remove an outermost electron from an isolated gaseous atom.
Standard Reduction Potential (E°)
Voltage associated with a reduction reaction relative to the Standard Hydrogen Electrode.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Lithium is the strongest reducing agent in aqueous medium.
  • Beryllium is amphoteric; magnesium reacts with boiling water.
  • Sodium peroxide is yellowish-white and diamagnetic.
  • Potassium superoxide is paramagnetic and used in life support systems.
  • Solubility of Group 2 sulfates decreases down the group.

📌 Quick Recall

  • s-Block elements occupy Groups 1 and 2 of the periodic table.
  • They have low ionization energies and high electropositive character.
  • Reactivity with oxygen, water, and halogens increases down the group.
  • Anomalous behavior of Li and Be arises from high charge density.

⚖️ Key Differences

Oxide stability VS Carbonate stability

Oxides of small cations are stable, whereas large anions require large cations for maximum lattice stability.
