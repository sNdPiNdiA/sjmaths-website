Diagonal Relationship

Diagonal relationship refers to the similarity in chemical and physical properties exhibited by certain pairs of diagonally adjacent elements in the second and third periods of the periodic table. This phenomenon arises due to comparable values of ionic radii, electronegativity, and charge-to-radius (ionic potential) ratios.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Periodic Trends and Ionic Potential

  • Diagonal pairs share similar charge-to-radius ratios (φ = Z/r), leading to comparable polarizing power and covalent character in their compounds.
  • Moving down a group, ionic radius increases, while moving across a period, ionic radius decreases; diagonal movement balances these opposite trends.
  • Electronegativity values of diagonally related elements (e.g., Li = 1.0, Mg = 1.2; Be = 1.5, Al = 1.5) are remarkably close.
  • Similar polarizing powers result in comparable hydration energies and solubilities of corresponding salts in organic solvents.

Lithium and Magnesium Resemblance

  • Both Lithium and Magnesium react directly with nitrogen gas to form ionic nitrides: 6Li + N2 → 2Li3N and 3Mg + N2 → Mg3N2.
  • Their carbonates decompose upon heating to yield corresponding metal oxides and carbon dioxide gas: Li2CO3 → Li2O + CO2.
  • Both form normal oxides rather than peroxides or superoxides when burned in excess oxygen.
  • The hydroxides and carbonates of both Li and Mg have relatively low solubility in water compared to other Group 1 and Group 2 members.

Beryllium and Aluminium Resemblance

  • Both Beryllium and Aluminium form amphoteric oxides and hydroxides that dissolve in excess sodium hydroxide to form soluble hydroxo complexes.
  • Their carbides (Be2C and Al4C3) undergo hydrolysis upon treatment with water to yield pure methane gas.
  • The chlorides of both elements (BeCl2 and AlCl3) exist as chlorine-bridged polymeric structures in the solid state and act as strong Lewis acids.
  • Passive protective oxide layers form on the surfaces of both metals, rendering them resistant to nitric acid attack.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Group 1 metals react with oxygen to form peroxides (Na) or superoxides (K, Rb, Cs).

Observation: Lithium forms exclusively the normal oxide (Li2O) upon reaction with oxygen. — Small size and high charge density of Li+ stabilize the small oxide ion (O2-) through high lattice energy.

Exception Group 2 sulfates (CaSO4, SrSO4, BaSO4) show decreasing solubility down the group.

Observation: Beryllium sulfate (BeSO4) is readily soluble in water, mirroring magnesium sulfate. — High hydration enthalpy of the small Be2+ ion compensates for the lattice energy during dissolution.

Exception Group 13 elements typically form trivalent cationic species and ionic compounds.

Observation: Boron forms exclusively covalent compounds and exhibits a diagonal relationship with Silicon. — High ionization energy and small size of boron prevent the formation of B3+ ions.

Diagonal Neighbor Mnemonic
Li → Mg, Be → Al, B → Si

Application: Remember the stair-step shift: start at Period 2 (Li, Be, B) and shift one column right and one row down.

Carbide Hydrolysis Product Rule
Be2C & Al4C3 → CH4; CaC2 → C2H2

Application: Metals sharing diagonal relationships (Be and Al) yield methane upon carbide hydrolysis, unlike heavier alkaline earth carbides which yield acetylene.

Polarizing Power Shortcut
φ = Zr

Application: Higher charge and smaller radius mean higher covalent character in ionic lattices (Fajans' rule application).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Lithium and magnesium both form stable nitrides upon direct heating with nitrogen gas.
  • Beryllium hydroxide and aluminium hydroxide are both amphoteric in chemical character.
  • The diagonal relationship is primarily attributed to similar ionic potentials (charge-to-radius ratio).
  • Carbides of both beryllium (Be2C) and aluminium (Al4C3) yield methane on hydrolysis.
  • Both lithium carbonate and magnesium carbonate decompose on heating to release carbon dioxide.
  • Beryllium chloride (BeCl2) exists as a polymeric chain with chlorine bridges in the solid state.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming diagonal elements belong to the same group; they belong to adjacent groups in different periods.
  • Confusing the nitride-forming property of lithium with other alkali metals like sodium or potassium.
  • Neglecting the polymeric nature of beryllium chloride and assuming simple discrete BeCl2 molecules.
  • Forgetting that boron and silicon share a diagonal relationship despite boron being a non-metal.

⚡ Quick Revision: Diagonal Relationship

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Origin of Diagonal Relationship

Diagonal relationships occur because periodic properties vary in opposite directions across periods and down groups, causing diagonal pairs to achieve nearly identical ionic radii and electronegativities.

Lithium-Magnesium Analogies

Li and Mg display similar thermal stabilities of carbonates, nitride formation, and low solubility of fluorides and carbonates due to comparable charge densities.

Beryllium-Aluminium Analogies

Be and Al exhibit similar amphoteric behavior, formation of covalent chlorides acting as Lewis acids, and passivation layers on metal surfaces.

Boron-Silicon Analogies

B and Si both form covalent hydrides (boranes and silanes) and acidic oxides (B2O3 and SiO2) with network covalent structures.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Ionic Potential (φ = Z/r)
Ratio of ionic charge to ionic radius determining polarizing power.
Amphoteric (M(OH)n)
Species capable of reacting with both acids and strong bases.
Polarization (Fajans' Rule)
Distortion of anion electron cloud by a cation.
Nitride (M3Nn)
Binary compound containing the N3- ion.
Passivation (Oxide film)
Formation of a thin inert oxide layer protecting the metal.
Lewis Acid (Electron pair acceptor)
Species with vacant orbitals capable of accepting electron pairs.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Li → Mg diagonal pair forms nitrides directly with N2.
  • Be & Al show amphoteric oxide/hydroxide behavior.
  • Be2C and Al4C3 liberate methane gas on hydrolysis.
  • Ionic potential φ = Z/r dictates diagonal similarities.
  • B and Si form network covalent oxides (B2O3, SiO2).

📌 Quick Recall

  • Diagonal relationships bridge groups 1-2 and 2-13 across periods 2 and 3.
  • Similar ionic radii and electronegativities drive chemical resemblances.
  • Covalent character in halides increases down diagonal pairs.
  • Thermal decomposition pathways of oxo-salts match within diagonal pairs.

⚖️ Key Differences

Lithium Nitrate Decomposition VS Sodium Nitrate Decomposition

Lithium nitrate decomposes to oxide, NO2, and O2 (like magnesium nitrate); sodium nitrate decomposes to nitrite and O2.
