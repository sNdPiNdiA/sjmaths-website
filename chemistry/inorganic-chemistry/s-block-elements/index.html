Inorganic Chemistry

s-Block Elements

The s-block elements comprise Group 1 (alkali metals) and Group 2 (alkaline earth metals) of the periodic table, characterized by ns1 and ns2 outermost electronic configurations. They exhibit highly electropositive character, low ionization enthalpies, and strong reducing properties.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Periodic Trends and Physical Properties of Alkali Metals

  • Group 1 elements possess a general valence configuration of ns1, resulting in large atomic and ionic radii within their respective periods.
  • Ionization enthalpies decrease down the group from Li to Cs due to increasing atomic size and shielding effect.
  • Standard reduction potentials (E°) are uniformly negative, reflecting their strong reducing nature in aqueous solution.
  • Alkali metals impart characteristic colors to an oxidizing flame owing to the low excitation energy of the valence electron.

Chemical Reactivity and Compound Formation of Group 1

  • Lithium uniquely forms a normal oxide (Li2O) upon reaction with oxygen, whereas sodium forms peroxide (Na2O2), and heavier congeners form superoxides (MO2).
  • Alkali metals dissolve in liquid ammonia to form deep blue solutions that are conducting and strongly reducing due to the presence of ammonated electrons.
  • Carbonates and bicarbonates are thermally stable, with thermal stability increasing down the group as the electropositive character increases.
  • Halides of alkali metals are ionic crystalline solids with high melting points, with the exception of lithium iodide which exhibits covalent character.

Properties and Anomalous Behavior of Alkaline Earth Metals

  • Group 2 elements feature an ns2 valence configuration, rendering them harder, denser, and less reactive than corresponding Group 1 metals.
  • Beryllium exhibits anomalous behavior and a diagonal relationship with aluminum due to its high charge-to-radius ratio and polarizing power.
  • Solubility of alkaline earth metal sulfates and hydroxides decreases and increases down the group respectively, governed by the relative magnitudes of hydration and lattice enthalpies.
  • Be(OH)2 and Al(OH)3 are distinctly amphoteric, dissolving in strong alkali to form beryllates and aluminates.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Size and hydration energy trends down a group

Observation: Lithium ion (Li+) has the smallest ionic size but the highest hydration enthalpy. — Extreme charge density of the tiny Li+ ion causes extensive coordination of water molecules.

Exception Reaction of alkali metals with oxygen to form superoxides

Observation: Lithium forms only the normal oxide (Li2O) and traces of peroxide. — The small Li+ cation stabilizes the small O2- anion through high lattice energy; larger superoxides cannot be accommodated.

Exception Thermal stability of carbonates of Group 2 metals

Observation: BeCO3 is extremely unstable and decomposes readily even at room temperature. — High polarizing power of Be2+ distorts the carbonate ion, lowering the activation energy for decomposition to BeO and CO2.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Plaster of Paris has the chemical formula CaSO4·½H2O or (CaSO4)2·H2O.
  • Solvay process is used for the industrial manufacture of sodium carbonate (Na2CO3).
  • Caustic soda (NaOH) is produced commercially by the Castner-Kellner cell using mercury cathode.
  • BeSO3 and MgSO3 are soluble in water, whereas BaSO4 is notoriously insoluble due to high lattice energy.
  • Quicklime is CaO, slaked lime is Ca(OH)2, and milk of lime is an aqueous suspension of Ca(OH)2.
  • Superoxides contain the [O2]- ion and are colored paramagnetic species.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the formulas of washing soda (Na2CO3·10H2O) and baking soda (NaHCO3).
  • Assuming all alkali metals react with oxygen to form superoxides; lithium strictly forms the normal oxide.
  • Forgetting that beryllium hydroxide is amphoteric while magnesium hydroxide is purely basic.
  • Neglecting water of crystallization when calculating molecular weights of group 2 salt hydrates like Epsom salt (MgSO4·7H2O).

⚡ Quick Revision: s-Block Elements

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Alkali Metal Reactivity

Group 1 elements are soft, highly reactive metals with low melting points and single valence electrons that readily form M+ ions.

Anomalous Properties of Lithium

Lithium differs from other alkali metals due to its extremely small atomic radius and high polarizing power, showing similarities to magnesium.

Alkaline Earth Metal Compounds

Group 2 elements form ionic compounds that are generally less soluble than corresponding Group 1 salts due to higher lattice enthalpies.

Biological Significance

Sodium and potassium ions maintain transmembrane electrochemical gradients, while magnesium is essential for chlorophyll and calcium forms bone matrices.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Solvay Process (Na2CO3 synthesis)
Industrial method to produce sodium carbonate using ammonia, carbon dioxide, and brine.
Down's Cell (Electrolytic cell)
Industrial apparatus used for the extraction of metallic sodium from molten NaCl and CaCl2.
Hydration Enthalpy (ΔhydH°)
Energy released when one mole of gaseous ions dissolves in water to form hydrated ions.
Lattice Enthalpy (ΔlatticeH°)
Energy required to completely separate one mole of a solid ionic compound into gaseous ions.
Dead Burnt Plaster (Anhydrous CaSO4)
Formed when gypsum is heated above 400 K, losing all water of crystallization.
Milk of Magnesia (Mg(OH)2 suspension)
Aqueous suspension of magnesium hydroxide used as an antacid.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Lithium reacts with nitrogen directly at room temperature to form Li3N.
  • Beryllium does not impart any color to the bunsen flame.
  • The reducing power sequence in aqueous solution is: Li > K > Cs > Na.
  • Gypsum formula is CaSO4·2H2O.
  • Epsom salt formula is MgSO4·7H2O.

📌 Quick Recall

  • s-Block elements comprise Groups 1 and 2 with ns1 and ns2 electronic configurations.
  • Reactivity with air, water, and hydrogen increases down the groups.
  • Diagonal relationships link Li-Mg and Be-Al due to comparable charge-to-radius ratios.
  • Industrial preparations include Solvay, Castner-Kellner, and Down's processes.

⚖️ Key Differences

Washing Soda VS Baking Soda

Washing soda is Na2CO3·10H2O while baking soda is NaHCO3.

The aqueous solution of which alkali metal ion conducts electricity best at infinite dilution?

Explanation: Cs+ is least hydrated and has the largest effective size in water, but its bare ion mobility combined with hydration radius makes Cs+ highly mobile compared to heavily hydrated Li+.

Question 5 of 18

Which alkaline earth metal does not impart color to the flame?

Explanation: Magnesium and beryllium have high ionization enthalpies; their electrons cannot be easily excited by flame energy.

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following hydroxides is amphoteric?

Question 8 of 18

The stability of alkaline earth metal carbonates increases in the order:

Explanation: Thermal stability of group 2 carbonates increases down the group as lattice stabilization of larger cations increases.

Question 9 of 18

Which metal dissolves in liquid ammonia to give a blue, conducting solution?

Explanation: Alkali metals dissolve in liquid ammonia to produce ammonated electrons and cations, yielding a blue conducting solution.

Question 10 of 18

What is the oxidation state of potassium in potassium superoxide (KO2)?

Explanation: Potassium exhibits a fixed +1 oxidation state in all its compounds, while the superoxide ion [O2]- has a net -1 charge.

Question 11 of 18

Explanation: Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) is commonly known as caustic soda.

Question 14 of 18

Explanation: Magnesium reacts slowly with cold water, forming magnesium hydroxide and hydrogen gas.

Question 15 of 18

The correct order of density for alkali metals is:

Explanation: Potassium is anomalously lighter than sodium due to an unusual increase in atomic volume.

Question 16 of 18

Which metal is stored under kerosene oil?

Explanation: Sodium is highly reactive with moisture and oxygen in air and is stored under kerosene.

Question 17 of 18

Explanation: Castner-Kellner cell employs brine electrolysis with a mercury cathode to form sodium amalgam.
