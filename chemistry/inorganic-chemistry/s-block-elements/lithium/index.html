Inorganic Chemistry

Lithium chemistry

Lithium is the first member of group 1 with the smallest atomic radius and highest charge density among alkali metals. It exhibits pronounced diagonal relationship with magnesium due to similar ionic and charge-to-radius ratios.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Anomalous Behavior and Diagonal Relationship

  • Lithium differs from other alkali metals due to its exceptionally small atomic and ionic radii and high electronegativity.
  • It shows a strong diagonal relationship with Magnesium owing to comparable polarizing powers and charge-to-radius ratios.
  • Unlike other alkali metals, lithium reacts directly with nitrogen gas at room temperature to form a stable ionic nitride, Li3N.
  • Lithium hydroxide and lithium carbonate are thermally less stable than those of heavier alkali metals, decomposing upon heating to oxides.

Hydration Enthalpy and Solution Properties

  • Lithium ion possesses the highest hydration energy among all alkali metal ions due to its extreme charge density.
  • High hydration energy leads to extensive solvation, making the hydrated lithium ion exceptionally large with a low ionic mobility in aqueous solutions.
  • Lithium salts often crystallize as hydrates (e.g., LiCl·2H2O), unlike heavier group 1 halides which separate as anhydrous crystals.
  • Due to high hydration energy compensating for sublimation and ionization energy, lithium exhibits the most negative standard reduction potential (E° = -3.04 V) making it the strongest reducing agent in aqueous solution.

Thermal Stability and Compound Characteristics

  • Lithium forms only the normal oxide (Li2O) upon burning in oxygen, whereas heavier alkali metals form peroxides and superoxides.
  • Lithium carbonate (Li2CO3) readily decomposes into lithium oxide and carbon dioxide upon heating, unlike K2CO3 or Na2CO3.
  • Lithium fluoride is sparingly soluble in water due to its exceptionally high lattice energy compared to other alkali metal halides.
  • Lithium alkyl and aryl compounds possess significant covalent character due to high polarizing power, allowing them to dissolve in organic solvents.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkali metals do not form stable nitrides under normal conditions.

Observation: Lithium reacts directly with N2 to form Li3N. — High lattice energy of Li3N compensates for the endothermic dissociation of dinitrogen and ionization of lithium.

Exception Alkali metals burn in oxygen to form peroxides or superoxides.

Observation: Lithium forms strictly the normal oxide (Li2O). — The small Li+ ion stabilizes the small O2- anion via high lattice energy, preventing larger peroxide or superoxide formation.

Exception Alkali metal halides are highly soluble in water.

Observation: Lithium fluoride (LiF) has relatively low water solubility. — Both Li+ and F- are small ions with exceptionally high lattice energy that outweighs their hydration energies.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Lithium has the lowest density (0.534 g/cm3) among all known solid elements.
  • Lithium standard reduction potential E° is -3.04 V, the lowest among elements.
  • Lithium nitrate on heating decomposes to lithium oxide, nitrogen dioxide, and oxygen gas.
  • Lithium chloride is deliquescent and often separates out from aqueous solutions as a hydrate (LiCl·H2O).
  • Due to high polarizing power, lithium salts of anions with large polarizability possess considerable covalent character.
  • Lithium is kept immersed in paraffin oil, but due to low density it tends to float on it; hence it is typically stored in vaseline or sealed tubes.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming lithium forms superoxides like potassium; lithium only forms the normal oxide.
  • Confusing the order of ionic mobility in water; lithium has the lowest mobility due to maximum hydration shell size.
  • Forgetting that lithium reacts with nitrogen at room temperature to form a nitride.
  • Confusing thermal stability of carbonates; lithium carbonate decomposes easily, whereas sodium and potassium carbonates do not.

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Anomalous Character

Lithium exhibits anomalous properties due to its extremely small size and high charge-to-radius ratio.

Hydration Dynamics

High hydration energy of Li+ dictates its anomalous aqueous behavior, large hydrated radius, and low ionic mobility.

Diagonal Affinity

Diagonal relationship with magnesium explains shared characteristics such as direct nitride formation and covalent tendencies.

Thermal instability of lithium nitrate, carbonate, and hydroxide stems from high lattice stabilization of Li2O.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Polarizing Power (phi = q/r^2)
Ability of a cation to distort an anion electron cloud.
Hydration Energy (Delta H_hyd)
Energy released when gaseous ions are hydrated in water.
Diagonal Relationship (Li - Mg)
Similar chemical properties between diagonally adjacent elements.
Lithium Nitride (Li3N)
Ionic nitride formed by direct union of lithium and nitrogen.
Standard Reduction Potential (E_red^o)
Measure of tendency to acquire electrons in standard aqueous state (-3.04 V for Li).
Normal Oxide (Li2O)
Metal oxide containing the O^2- oxide ion.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Lithium is the only alkali metal forming a stable nitride (Li3N).
  • Lithium has the most negative E° value (-3.04 V) among all elements.
  • Lithium burns in oxygen to form only the normal oxide (Li2O).
  • Lithium ion has the largest hydrated radius among alkali metals.
  • Lithium carbonate decomposes upon heating, unlike Na2CO3 or K2CO3.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Lithium is distinct from group 1 due to high polarizing power.
  • Displays strong diagonal similarities with magnesium.
  • Exhibits highest hydration enthalpy and lowest ionic mobility in water.
  • Forms covalent organometallic compounds readily.

⚖️ Key Differences

Hydrated Radius VS Ionic Radius

Lithium has the smallest bare ionic radius but the largest hydrated radius due to maximum water molecule coordination.

Solution: Smaller ionic radius results in tighter packing and higher lattice energy for lithium salts.

Question 3 of 10
The decreasing order of standard reduction potential (E°) in water for group 1 metals is:

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following halides has the highest melting point?

Solution: Lithium fluoride has the smallest anion, leading to maximum lattice energy and highest melting point among lithium halides.

Question 5 of 10
Lithium differs from other alkali metals in that:

Solution: High polarizing power enables lithium to form covalent bonds readily compared to heavier alkali metals.

What is observed when lithium nitrate is strongly heated?

Question 7 of 10
The effective hydrated radius of lithium ion is largest because:
