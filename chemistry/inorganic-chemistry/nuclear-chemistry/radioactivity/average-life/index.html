Average Life of Radioactive Decay

The average life of a radioactive isotope represents the mathematical mean of the lifetimes of all individual radioactive atoms in a given sample. It is reciprocally related to the radioactive decay constant and serves as a fundamental metric for evaluating long-term nuclear stability.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mathematical Derivation and Statistical Interpretation

  • The average life (τ) is defined as the sum of the total life spans of all radioactive atoms divided by the total number of atoms initially present.
  • Mathematically integrated as τ = ∫0∞ t dN / N0, yielding the reciprocal of the decay constant (λ).
  • It denotes the time during which the number of undecayed nuclei decreases to 1/e or approximately 36.8% of its initial value.

Comparison with Half-Life and Decay Constant

  • While half-life (t1/2) measures the time required for exactly 50% decay, average life accounts for the extended tail of the exponential decay curve.
  • The constant 1.442 factor links half-life to average life (τ = 1.442 t1/2), meaning average life is always longer than half-life.
  • Neither average life nor half-life is affected by external physical conditions such as temperature, pressure, or chemical combination.

Applications in Radiometric Dating and Kinetics

  • Radioactive disintegration strictly obeys first-order kinetics, making average life an invariant characteristic for a specific radioisotope.
  • Used in conjunction with disintegration rates to evaluate geological time scales and isotopic abundances.
  • Provides direct estimation of the mean persistence time of unstable nuclei inside stellar environments or geological formations.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Radioactive decay rates are completely independent of external thermodynamic conditions.

Observation: Electron capture (EC) and internal conversion decay modes. — Electron capture rates can show extremely minute variations if the electron density near the nucleus is altered by extreme chemical bonding or high pressures.

Exception Decay constant remains strictly invariant across all states.

Observation: Bound-state β- decay. — Fully ionized atoms can exhibit modified decay constants because the emitted electron is captured directly into a bound atomic orbital.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Average life is defined as the reciprocal of the decay constant (τ = 1/λ).
  • The relation between average life and half-life is τ = 1.442 t1/2.
  • At t = τ, the fraction of undecayed radioactive atoms remaining is 1/e or 36.8%.
  • Radioactive decay follows first-order kinetics, meaning average life is independent of initial concentration.
  • External factors such as temperature, pressure, and catalyst do not alter the average life of a radioisotope.
  • The units of average life are time units (seconds, minutes, years).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing half-life with average life by using 0.693 instead of 1.442.
  • Assuming average life is shorter than half-life; remember average life is always greater (τ > t1/2).
  • Applying radioactive decay formulas to zero-order or second-order chemical reactions.
  • Forgetting that units of λ must match the time units of τ.

⚡ Quick Revision: Average Life of Radioactive Decay

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition of Average Life

Average life represents the statistical mean life span of all radioactive nuclei in a sample, expressed mathematically as τ = 1/λ.

Conversion Factors

Average life is strictly linked to half-life via the relation τ = t1/2 / 0.693 = 1.442 t1/2.

Decay Characteristics

At a time equal to one average life, exactly 36.8% of the original radioactive substance remains undecayed.

Kinetic Independence

Being a first-order nuclear phenomenon, average life is completely unaffected by changes in external physical or chemical environments.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Average Life (τ)
Reciprocal of decay constant representing mean lifetime. Unit: s or years.
Decay Constant (λ)
Probability of decay per unit time. Unit: s-1.
Half-Life (t1/2)
Time required for activity to reduce to half. Unit: s or years.
Initial Nuclei (N0)
Number of radioactive nuclei at t = 0. Dimensionless.
Remaining Nuclei (N)
Number of undecayed radioactive nuclei at time t. Dimensionless.
Activity (A)
Rate of disintegration (-dN/dt). Unit: Becquerel (Bq) or Curie (Ci).

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • τ = 1 / λ
  • τ = 1.442 × t1/2
  • N = N_0 e-t/τ
  • At t = τ, N = 0.368 N_0
  • Radioactive decay is independent of external conditions

📌 Quick Recall

  • Average life is the inverse of the decay constant.
  • It is always 1.442 times longer than the half-life.
  • Represents the time when 36.8% of sample remains.
  • Follows exponential decay laws.

⚖️ Key Differences

Half-Life VS Average Life

Half-life is time for 50% decay (0.693/λ), whereas average life is mean life (1.442 t1/2).

Question 1 of 18

What is the correct mathematical relationship between average life (τ) and decay constant (λ)?

Explanation: Average life is defined as the reciprocal of the decay constant, τ = 1 / λ.

Question 2 of 18

What is the relationship between average life (τ) and half-life (t1/2)?

Explanation: Since t1/2 = 0.693 / λ and τ = 1 / λ, dividing gives τ = t1/2 / 0.693 = 1.442 t1/2.

Question 3 of 18

What percentage of a radioactive substance remains undecayed after a time equal to its average life?

Explanation: Substituting t = τ in N = N_0 e-t/τ gives N = N_0 / e, which is approximately 36.8%.

Question 4 of 18

If the decay constant of a radioactive element is 0.02 year-1, what is its average life?

Explanation: τ = 1 / λ = 1 / 0.02 = 50 years.

Question 5 of 18

Radioactive decay follows which order of reaction kinetics?

Explanation: Radioactive disintegration is strictly a first-order kinetic process where rate is proportional to the concentration of undecayed nuclei.

Question 6 of 18

How does an increase in temperature affect the average life of a radioactive isotope?

Explanation: Radioactive decay is a nuclear phenomenon and is independent of external physical factors like temperature and pressure.

Question 7 of 18

What is the fraction of radioactive nuclei decayed when time equals one average life?

Explanation: The fraction decayed is 1 - (N/N_0) = 1 - (1/e) = 1 - 0.368 = 0.632 or 63.2%.

Question 8 of 18

If the half-life of a radioactive isotope is 69.3 days, what is its average life?

Explanation: τ = 1.442 × t1/2 = 1.442 × 69.3 = 100 days (approx).

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following units is appropriate for the average life of a radioisotope?

Explanation: Average life is a measure of time, hence measured in seconds, minutes, days, or years.

Question 10 of 18

Which term represents the sum of the lifetimes of all atoms divided by the total number of atoms?

Explanation: This is the fundamental definition of average life.

Question 11 of 18

If the average life of a nuclide is τ, the time in which the activity drops to 1/e of its initial value is:

Explanation: Activity is proportional to N, so A = A_0 e-t/τ. When t = τ, A = A_0 / e.

Question 12 of 18

Which constant links half-life to the decay constant?

Explanation: t1/2 = 0.693 / λ.

Question 13 of 18

Can average life be determined graphically from a ln(N) versus t plot?

Explanation: Slope of ln(N/N_0) vs t is -λ. Thus, average life is the negative inverse of the slope.

Question 14 of 18

What is the decay constant if the average life is 200 seconds?

Explanation: λ = 1 / τ = 1 / 200 = 0.005 s-1.

Question 15 of 18

Does the average life depend on the initial amount of the radioactive element present?

Explanation: First-order kinetics ensures that half-life and average life are independent of initial concentration.

Question 16 of 18

Which decay mode can occasionally show minute environmental sensitivity?

Explanation: Electron capture involves inner-shell electrons, whose density can be slightly altered by chemical bonding.

Question 17 of 18

What is the value of 1 / ln(2)?

Explanation: 1 / 0.69315 = 1.442, which is the conversion multiplier between half-life and average life.

Question 18 of 18

The radioactive disintegration rate at any instant is proportional to:

Explanation: Rate = -λN, which is directly proportional to the number of remaining undecayed nuclei N.

Question 2 of 10
What fraction of a radioactive sample has decayed when the elapsed time equals two average lives?

Solution: Remaining fraction is N/N_0 = e-2. Decayed fraction is 1 - e-2 = 1 - (1 / e2).

Question 3 of 10
The unit of the radioactive decay constant λ is:

Solution: Decay constant represents probability of decay per unit time, hence unit is time-1.

Question 4 of 10
Which mathematical expression correctly relates remaining nuclei N to average life τ and time t?

Solution: Substituting λ = 1/τ into N = N_0 e-lambda t gives N = N_0 e-t/τ.

Question 5 of 10
If average life is 500 years, what is the half-life of the radioisotope?

Solution: t1/2 = τ / 1.442 = 500 / 1.442 ≈ 346.7 years.

Question 6 of 10
Why is average life always greater than half-life?

Solution: Mathematically τ = 1.442 t1/2, originating from the integration over the infinite exponential tail of the decay curve.

Question 7 of 10
What is the decay rate (activity) of a sample containing 106 nuclei with an average life of 104 seconds?

Solution: Activity A = N / τ = 106 / 104 = 100 disintegrations per second (Bq).

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following statements is incorrect regarding average life?

Solution: Average life is independent of the initial mass or concentration of the radioactive sample.

Question 9 of 10
If the half-life of Radium is 1620 years, what is its decay constant?

Question 10 of 10
In a plot of ln(Activity) versus time for a radioactive sample, what does the intercept on the y-axis represent?

Solution: From ln(A) = ln(A_0) - λt, the y-intercept at t = 0 is ln(A_0).
