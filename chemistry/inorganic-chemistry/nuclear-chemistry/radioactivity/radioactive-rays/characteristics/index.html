Characteristics of Radioactive Rays

Radioactive disintegration emits three distinct types of rays—α, β, and γ—characterized by differing mass, charge, and interaction with electric and magnetic fields. Their penetration, ionization, and deflection properties are governed by the conservation of mass-energy and angular momentum.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Corpuscular Nature and Deflection of Alpha and Beta Particles

  • Alpha ( α ) particles are doubly ionized helium nuclei (2He4) carrying a charge of +2e and mass of 4.0015 u.
  • Beta ( β ) particles are high-speed electrons (-1e0) or positrons (+1e0) originating from nuclear beta decay.
  • Both α and β rays are deflected by electric and magnetic fields due to their net electrical charge, obeying the Lorentz force law.
  • The magnitude of deflection is inversely proportional to their momentum and proportional to their charge-to-mass ratio.

Electromagnetic Nature and Penetration of Gamma Rays

  • Gamma ( γ ) rays are high-energy photons (electromagnetic radiation) with wavelengths typically less than 0.01 nm.
  • Gamma photons carry zero rest mass and zero electrical charge; hence, they remain undeflected in electric and magnetic fields.
  • Ionizing power follows the order α > β > γ, where alpha particles create dense tracks of ion pairs over short distances.
  • Penetrating power follows the exact inverse order: γ > β > α, with gamma rays requiring dense shielding like thick lead plates.

Radioactive Disintegration Kinetics and Secondary Effects

  • Alpha emission decreases the atomic number Z by 2 and mass number A by 4 according to Soddy-Fajans displacement law.
  • Beta-minus emission increases atomic number Z by 1 while keeping mass number A constant, via the conversion of a neutron to a proton.
  • Radioactive rays induce fluorescence when striking zinc sulfide (ZnS) screens, generating discrete scintillation flashes.
  • Passage of these rays through gases causes extensive ionization, transforming neutral atoms into positive ions and free electrons.

r = mv / (qB)

Units: r (m), m (kg), v (m/s), q (C), B (Tesla)

Q = [m(ZXA) - m(Z-2YA-4) - m(2He4)] · c2
Conditions: Rest mass values measured in atomic mass units (u)
Units: Q (Joules or MeV)
Absorption Law (Beer-Lambert / Gamma Rays)
I = I0e-μx
Conditions: Collimated monoenergetic gamma radiation passing through absorber of thickness x
Units: I (Intensity), μ (linear attenuation coefficient, cm-1), x (cm)
Deflection in Electric Field
y = ½ · (qE / mv2) · x2
Conditions: Particle of mass m and charge q entering a uniform electric field E
Units: y (m), x (m), v (m/s), q (C), E (N/C), m (kg)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Charged particles deflect in electric and magnetic fields

Observation: Gamma rays show zero deflection — Gamma rays are uncharged electromagnetic photons with zero rest mass, experiencing no Lorentz force.

Exception Beta particles originate from orbital electron shells during ionization

Observation: Nuclear beta particles originate from inside the nucleus — Beta emission results from the weak interaction converting a nuclear neutron into a proton (or vice versa), not from stripping valence electrons.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Penetration vs Ionization Power Mnemonic
PIE: Penetration (Gamma > Beta > Alpha), Ionization (Alpha > Beta > Gamma)

Application: Whenever asked about penetration or ionization, remember they are exact opposites.

Charge-to-Mass Ratio Deflection Rule
Deflection ∝ q / m

Application: Beta particles deflect much more than alpha particles in the same electric field because their mass is nearly 7000 times smaller.

Soddy-Fajans Quick Balance
α → (-2, -4) | β- → (+1, 0) | γ → (0, 0)

Application: Quickly determine daughter nuclei atomic (Z) and mass (A) changes without writing full nuclear equations.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Alpha particles are deflected towards the negative plate in an electric field.
  • Beta particles have a specific charge-to-mass ratio much higher than alpha particles.
  • Gamma rays travel with the speed of light (3 × 108 m/s) in vacuum.
  • Alpha emission reduces the mass number by 4 units.
  • Zinc sulfide (ZnS) screen glows (scintillation) when struck by alpha particles.
  • Internal conversion is a process competing with gamma emission where an orbital electron is ejected.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming gamma rays have mass because they carry momentum; remember photons have relativistic momentum p = h/λ without rest mass.
  • Confusing beta-minus particles with orbital cathode rays; nuclear beta particles possess continuous energy spectra due to neutrino emission.
  • Forgetting that alpha particles acquire two electrons from the surrounding medium to become neutral helium atoms.
  • Mistaking the ionizing power order with the penetrating power order.

⚡ Quick Revision: Characteristics of Radioactive Rays

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Corpuscular Emissions

Alpha and beta rays consist of actual matter particles possessing rest mass, momentum, and discrete electrical charge.

Electromagnetic Waves

Gamma rays represent discrete wave packets of energy (photons) emitted during nuclear de-excitation from excited energy states.

Field Interactions

Electric and magnetic fields split radioactive beams into three distinct components based on charge-to-mass ratio.

Interaction with Matter

Energy dissipation occurs primarily through electrostatic interactions, producing intense gas ionization and excitation.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Alpha Particle (2He4)
A doubly ionized helium ion possessing two protons and two neutrons.
Beta Particle (-1e0)
A high-velocity electron emitted from the atomic nucleus during radioactive decay.
Gamma Ray (hν)
Short-wavelength high-frequency electromagnetic radiation emitted by excited nuclei.
Ionizing Power (Ion pairs / cm)
The ability of radiation to strip electrons from gas atoms along its trajectory.
Penetrating Power (Range in matter)
The depth to which radiation can penetrate before complete absorption.
Scintillation (Light flash)
Flash of light produced when ionizing radiation strikes a phosphor like ZnS.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Order of Ionization: α > β > γ
  • Order of Penetration: γ > β > α
  • Charge of Alpha particle: +2e
  • Rest mass of Gamma photon: 0
  • Speed of Gamma rays in vacuum: c = 3 × 108 m/s

📌 Quick Recall

  • Radioactive rays comprise α corpuscles, β corpuscles, and γ photons.
  • Deflection in electromagnetic fields separates charged corpuscles from uncharged photons.
  • Ionization and penetration capabilities are inversely related.
  • Radioactive decay strictly follows conservation laws of mass-energy, charge, and momentum.

⚖️ Key Differences

Alpha Particle VS Helium Atom

An alpha particle is a bare 2He4 nucleus lacking two orbital electrons, whereas a neutral helium atom possesses two orbital electrons.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary reason why gamma rays are not deflected by magnetic fields?

Solution: Magnetic deflection requires moving electrical charge (Lorentz force q(v x B)). Gamma photons are neutral and massless.

Question 2 of 10
Which species is produced when an alpha particle captures two electrons?

Solution: Capturing two electrons neutralizes the +2 charge of an alpha particle, turning it into a stable helium-4 atom.

Question 3 of 10
If an isotope undergoes beta-plus decay, what is the change in atomic number Z?

Solution: Beta-plus decay (positron emission) converts a proton into a neutron, reducing atomic number Z by 1.

Question 4 of 10
What is the velocity range of alpha particles emitted from radioactive sources?

Solution: Alpha particles are massive corpuscles traveling at velocities between 0.05c and 0.1c.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following correctly describes the nature of beta-minus particles?

Solution: Beta-minus particles are high-energy electrons originating from nuclear neutron decay.

Question 6 of 10
In terms of stopping thickness, gamma rays require:

Solution: High penetrating power requires dense, thick absorbers like lead to attenuate gamma photons.

Question 7 of 10
Which conservation law is fundamentally violated if a gamma ray is emitted without a change in nuclear energy state?

Solution: Gamma emission carries away excitation energy; emitting a photon without a drop in nuclear energy violates energy conservation.

Question 8 of 10
What characterizes the energy spectrum of beta particles compared to alpha particles?

Solution: Alpha particles have discrete kinetic energies, whereas beta particles show a continuous energy spectrum due to simultaneous neutrino emission.

Question 9 of 10
Which ray causes the maximum fogging on photographic plates?

Solution: Gamma rays penetrate deep into photographic cassettes, causing widespread fogging due to high penetration.

Question 10 of 10
What is the charge of a beta-plus particle (positron)?

Solution: A positron is the antiparticle of an electron and carries a positive elementary charge (+1e).
