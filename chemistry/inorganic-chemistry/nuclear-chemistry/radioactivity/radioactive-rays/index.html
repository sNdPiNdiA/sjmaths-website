Inorganic Chemistry

Radioactive Rays

Radioactive rays consist of ionizing radiations emitted spontaneously from unstable atomic nuclei during radioactive decay. These emissions primarily comprise alpha particles, beta particles, and gamma photons.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Nature and Properties of Alpha Rays

  • Alpha rays are a stream of doubly ionized helium ions (2He4) carrying a charge of +2e.
  • They possess high ionizing power due to their large mass and charge, but have very low penetration depth, stopped by a thin sheet of paper.
  • Alpha particles are deflected by both electric and magnetic fields, curving towards the negative plate or pole.
  • Their velocity ranges from 1/10th to 1/20th of the speed of light.

Nature and Properties of Beta Rays

  • Beta rays consist of high-speed electrons (β-) or positrons (β+) emitted from the nucleus.
  • They exhibit intermediate ionization power, higher than gamma rays but lower than alpha rays.
  • Beta particles possess greater penetration power than alpha rays and can be stopped by a thin sheet of aluminum.
  • They undergo severe deflection in electric and magnetic fields due to their small mass-to-charge ratio.

Nature and Properties of Gamma Rays

  • Gamma rays are electromagnetic radiation of extremely short wavelength (10-11 m to 10-14 m) and high frequency.
  • They carry no electrical charge and therefore suffer no deflection in electric or magnetic fields.
  • Gamma photons have the lowest ionizing power among the three, but possess the highest penetration power, requiring thick lead or concrete blocks for attenuation.
  • Gamma emission usually accompanies alpha or beta decay to rid the daughter nucleus of excess excitation energy.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Beta-Minus Decay Law
ZXA → Z+1YA + -1e0 + νe¯
Conditions: Neutron-rich nuclei conversion of neutron to proton
Units: -1e0 = Beta particle, νe¯ = Electron antineutrino
Beta-Plus Decay Law
ZXA → Z-1YA + +1e0 + νe
Conditions: Proton-rich nuclei conversion of proton to neutron
Units: +1e0 = Positron, νe = Electron neutrino
Gamma Emission
ZXA* → ZXA + γ
Conditions: De-excitation of an excited nuclear state
Units: γ = High-energy photon
Radioactive Decay Law
N(t) = N0e-λt
Conditions: First-order nuclear disintegration kinetics
Units: N(t) = Remaining nuclei, N0 = Initial nuclei, λ = Decay constant, t = Time
Alpha Decay Law
ZXA → Z-2YA-4 + 2He4
Conditions: Emission of a helium nucleus from heavy unstable nuclides
Units: A = Mass number, Z = Atomic number
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alpha particles are pure helium nuclei without electrons.

Observation: Upon losing their kinetic energy, alpha particles capture two electrons from the surrounding medium to become neutral helium atoms. — Charge neutralization stability in matter.

Exception Beta particles originate from orbital electron clouds.

Observation: Beta particles originate directly from the nucleus via the conversion of a neutron into a proton or vice versa. — Nuclear weak interaction processes.

Exception Electromagnetic radiation always causes mass number or atomic number changes upon emission.

Observation: Gamma-ray emission does not alter the mass number (A) or atomic number (Z) of the nuclide. — Gamma rays are massless, chargeless energy packets emitted from nuclear energy levels.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Deflection Mnemonic (LEDA)
Lighter particles Experience Deflection Amplified (β > α > γ)

Application: Use to quickly determine relative deflection magnitudes in electric and magnetic fields.

Penetration vs Ionization Rule
Penetration power ∝ 1 / Ionization power

Application: Gamma has maximum penetration and minimum ionization; alpha has minimum penetration and maximum ionization.

Soddy-Fajans Displacement Shortcut
α loss: (Z-2, A-4); β loss: (Z+1, A); γ loss: (Z, A)

Application: Instantly calculate the product nuclide in nuclear decay reactions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Alpha particles have a mass of 4 amu and a charge of +2 units.
  • Beta-minus decay involves the transformation of a neutron into a proton: n → p + e- + ν¯.
  • Beta-plus decay involves the transformation of a proton into a neutron: p → n + e+ + ν.
  • Gamma rays travel at the speed of light (3 × 108 m/s) in a vacuum.
  • The ionizing power ratio of α : β : γ is approximately 10000 : 100 : 1.
  • The penetrating power ratio of α : β : γ is approximately 1 : 100 : 10000.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming beta particles are extracted from the K-shell electrons; they are actually created inside the nucleus via weak nuclear forces.
  • Confusing gamma rays with X-rays based on their high penetration; X-rays originate from electronic transitions, while gamma rays originate from nuclear transitions.
  • Neglecting the emission of neutrinos or antineutrinos in beta decay balance equations.
  • Assuming gamma radiation changes the elemental identity of the emitting atom.

⚡ Quick Revision: Radioactive Rays

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Nature of Alpha Emissions

Alpha rays are particulate radiations consisting of He2+ ions with high kinetic energy and strong ionizing capability.

Mechanics of Beta Emissions

Beta rays are high-velocity electrons or positrons ejected due to intra-nuclear neutron-proton interconversions.

Origin of Gamma Photons

Gamma rays are electromagnetic wave packets released when a daughter nucleus drops from an excited state to its ground state.

Radiation Interactions

Penetration and ionization capacities vary inversely, governed by the mass, charge, and velocity of the radioactive rays.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Alpha Particle (2He4)
A doubly ionized helium nucleus emitted during radioactive decay.
Beta Particle (-1e0)
A high-speed electron emitted from the nucleus during beta decay.
Gamma Ray (γ)
High-frequency electromagnetic radiation emitted from atomic nuclei.
Ionization Power (Ions/cm)
The ability of radiation to knock electrons out of atoms along its path.
Penetrating Power (Range in matter)
The depth to which radiation can penetrate through various shielding materials.
Positron (+1e0)
The antiparticle of the electron, carrying a positive unit charge.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Alpha charge = +2e, Mass = 4 amu
  • Beta charge = -1e (or +1e), Mass = 0.00055 amu
  • Gamma charge = 0, Mass = 0
  • Ionization order: α > β > γ
  • Penetration order: γ > β > α

📌 Quick Recall

  • Radioactive rays include alpha, beta, and gamma emissions.
  • Alpha and beta rays are corpuscular, whereas gamma rays are electromagnetic waves.
  • Alpha and beta particles are deflected by electric and magnetic fields; gamma rays are not.
  • Conservation of mass number and atomic number applies strictly to all radioactive decay equations.

⚖️ Key Differences

Cathode Rays VS Beta Rays

Cathode rays are extra-nuclear high-speed electrons from a discharge tube, whereas beta rays are intra-nuclear electrons.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following radioactive rays has the highest ionizing power?

Explanation: Alpha particles have the largest mass and charge among the three, making them interact strongly with matter and possess the highest ionizing power.

Question 2 of 19

Gamma rays are deflected by:

Explanation: Gamma rays are neutral electromagnetic photons and carry no charge, hence they are not deflected by electric or magnetic fields.

Question 3 of 19

The emission of an alpha particle from a nuclide results in:

Explanation: Alpha decay follows ZXA → Z-2YA-4 + 2He4, decreasing mass number by 4 and atomic number by 2.

Question 4 of 19

What is the nature of beta-minus particles?

Explanation: Beta-minus particles are high-speed electrons ejected from the nucleus during the conversion of a neutron into a proton.

Question 5 of 19

Which rays possess the maximum penetrating power?

Explanation: Due to their high energy and zero mass/charge, gamma rays can penetrate thick layers of lead or concrete.

Question 6 of 19

During beta-plus decay, which of the following occurs within the nucleus?

Explanation: Beta-plus decay involves the conversion of a proton into a neutron, emitting a positron and an electron neutrino.

Question 7 of 19

Which of the following is true regarding alpha particles?

Explanation: Alpha particles are stripped helium atoms, i.e., helium nuclei carrying a +2 charge.

Question 8 of 19

What happens to the atomic number when a nuclide emits a beta-minus particle?

Explanation: In beta-minus decay, a neutron becomes a proton, thereby increasing the atomic number Z by 1 while mass number A remains constant.

Question 9 of 19

The velocity of alpha particles is approximately:

Explanation: Alpha particles are relatively massive and travel at speeds ranging from 1/10th to 1/20th of the speed of light.

Question 10 of 19

Which type of radiation leaves no change in the mass or atomic number of the parent nucleus?

Explanation: Gamma emission involves only the release of excitation energy, keeping both mass number and atomic number unchanged.

Question 11 of 19

What is required to stop beta particles effectively?

Explanation: Beta particles have moderate penetration power and are typically stopped by a thin sheet of aluminum.

Question 12 of 19

The ratio of ionizing power of α, β, and γ rays is roughly:

Explanation: Alpha rays have the highest ionization (~10000), beta rays moderate (~100), and gamma rays the lowest (~1).

Question 13 of 19

Antineutrinos are emitted along with which type of radioactive ray?

Explanation: Beta-minus decay is accompanied by the emission of an electron antineutrino to conserve lepton number and energy.

Question 14 of 19

Which particle is represented by +1e0?

Explanation: +1e0 denotes a positron, the antimatter counterpart of an electron.

Question 15 of 19

Why do gamma rays have high penetrating power?

Explanation: Lack of charge and mass minimizes electrostatic interactions with surrounding matter, allowing deep penetration.

Question 16 of 19

If a radioactive element emits one alpha and two beta particles, the resulting daughter element is an:

Explanation: Alpha loss reduces A by 4 and Z by 2. Two beta gains increase Z by 2, returning Z to its original value while A becomes A-4. Same Z means an isotope.

Question 17 of 19

Which radioactive emission can blacken a photographic plate kept wrapped in black paper?

Explanation: All three types of radioactive radiations can affect photographic emulsions.

Question 18 of 19

The source of gamma radiation in radioactive decay is:

Explanation: Gamma rays originate from transitions between nuclear energy states.

Question 19 of 19

Which conservation law is violated if neutrino emission is ignored in beta decay?

Explanation: Continuous energy spectrum in beta decay necessitated the inclusion of neutrinos to conserve energy and angular momentum.

Question 1 of 10
Which radioactive radiation has the lowest penetration power?

Solution: Alpha particles have the lowest penetration power due to their large mass and high charge, stopping after a few centimeters in air or a sheet of paper.

Question 2 of 10
What is the charge on an alpha particle in coulombs?

Solution: An alpha particle carries two positive elementary charges: 2 × 1.602 × 10-19 C = +3.2 × 10-19 C.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following is true for beta rays?

Solution: Beta rays are corpuscular streams of high-speed electrons (β-) or positrons (β+).

Question 4 of 10
The mass number of a nucleus decreases by how much during one alpha emission?

Solution: Since an alpha particle is 2He4, its emission decreases the mass number by 4.

Question 5 of 10
Which rays are essentially high-energy electromagnetic waves?

Solution: Gamma rays are high-frequency electromagnetic radiation originating from the nucleus.

Question 6 of 10
In a cloud chamber, tracks of alpha particles are:

Solution: Alpha particles cause dense ionization along their trajectory, producing thick, straight tracks in a cloud chamber.

Question 7 of 10
When an element undergoes beta-minus decay, its position in the periodic table moves:

Solution: Atomic number Z increases by 1 during beta-minus decay, shifting the element one group to the right in the periodic table.

Question 8 of 10
Which type of ray is not deflected by a magnetic field?

Solution: Gamma rays are neutral photons and experience zero Lorentz force in magnetic fields.

Question 9 of 10
The wavelength range of gamma rays is typically:

Solution: Gamma rays occupy the highest frequency and shortest wavelength region of the electromagnetic spectrum, typically 10-11 m to 10-14 m.

Question 10 of 10
What particle is emitted when 90Th232 decays to 88Ra228?

Solution: Mass number decreases by 4 (232 to 228) and atomic number decreases by 2 (90 to 88), which corresponds to the emission of an alpha particle.
