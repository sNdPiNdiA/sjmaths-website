Electrolytic Conductance

Electrolytic conductance measures the ease with which electric current flows through an electrolytic solution due to the migration of dissolved ions. It obeys Ohm's law and depends on factors such as ion concentration, ionic mobility, and temperature.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Fundamental Definitions and Cell Constants

  • Conductance (G) is the reciprocal of resistance and represents the ability of a solution to conduct electricity.
  • Specific conductance or conductivity (κ) is the conductance of a 1 cm3 volume of solution.
  • Cell constant (G*) is defined as the ratio of distance between electrodes to the cross-sectional area (l / A).

Variation of Conductivity and Molar Conductivity with Dilution

  • Conductivity (κ) decreases upon dilution because the number of current-carrying ions per unit volume decreases.
  • Molar conductivity (Λm) increases with dilution due to increased ionic mobility and decreased interionic electrostatic attractions.
  • Weak electrolytes show a sharp increase in molar conductivity upon dilution due to a higher degree of dissociation (α).

Kohlrausch's Law and its Applications

  • Kohlrausch's Law states that limiting molar conductivity of an electrolyte is the sum of contributions from its constituent cations and anions.
  • It is used to calculate the limiting molar conductivity of weak electrolytes which cannot be obtained by direct extrapolation.
  • It permits the determination of the degree of dissociation (α = Λm / Λm°) and dissociation constant (Ka) of weak acids.

Conditions: C in mol L-1

Conditions: N in g equiv L-1

Conditions: Infinite dilution

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Molar conductivity increases monotonically upon infinite dilution for all electrolytes.

Observation: Strong electrolytes approach a limiting value (Λm°) asymptotically, whereas weak electrolytes show a steep, near-vertical rise at extreme dilution. — Weak electrolytes undergo continuous increase in degree of dissociation toward 100% as dilution increases.

Exception Conductivity of an electrolytic solution generally increases with temperature.

Observation: Viscosity effects can sometimes dominate if hydration shells are unusually large, though thermal agitation normally increases ionic mobility. — Decreased solvent viscosity and increased kinetic energy augment ionic velocities.

Exception Proton and hydroxide ions move via conventional hydrodynamic migration.

Observation: H+ (H3O+) and OH- possess exceptionally high limiting ionic mobilities compared to other ions. — They conduct electricity via the Grotthuss proton-jump mechanism through the hydrogen-bonded water network.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Unit Conversion Guard
κ (S m-1) = 100 × κ (S cm-1)

Application: Always convert cm to m carefully when dealing with SI units in numerical problems to avoid magnitude errors.

Degree of Dissociation Shortcut
α = Λm / Λm°

Application: Directly find the fraction dissociated by taking the ratio of molar conductivity at concentration c to that at infinite dilution.

Weak Acid Dissociation Constant
Ka = (c α2) / (1 - α) = (c Λm2) / [Λm°(Λm° - Λm)]

Application: Substitute α in Ostwald's dilution law formula for rapid calculation of Ka from conductance data.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Units of conductivity (κ) are S m-1 or dS m-1 or ohm-1 cm-1.
  • Cell constant units are cm-1 or m-1.
  • Limiting molar conductivity for NaCl can be found using λ°(Na+) + λ°(Cl-).
  • Ostwald's dilution law applies strictly to weak electrolytes.
  • Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation is valid only for dilute solutions of strong electrolytes.
  • Specific conductance decreases with dilution, whereas molar and equivalent conductivities increase.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing specific conductance (κ) with molar conductivity (Λm); remember κ decreases on dilution while Λm increases.
  • Forgetting the stoichiometric coefficients (ν+, ν-) in Kohlrausch's law calculations.
  • Mixing up units of concentration (mol L-1 vs mol m-3) when multiplying by 1000.
  • Assuming weak electrolytes obey the linear Debye-Hückel-Onsager extrapolation to zero concentration.

⚡ Quick Revision: Electrolytic Conductance

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrical Resistance and Conductance

Resistance follows Ohm's law, and conductance is its inverse. Conductivity normalizes conductance to physical cell dimensions.

Concentration Dependence

Molar conductivity scales with volume containing one mole of electrolyte, showing distinct profiles for strong and weak electrolytes.

Kohlrausch's Principle

Independent migration of ions allows algebraic summation of individual ionic conductivities to find total electrolyte conductivity at infinite dilution.

Applications of Conductance Measurement

Conductometric titrations and determination of solubility product of sparingly soluble salts rely directly on precise conductivity data.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Conductance (G = 1/R)
Ease of current flow, measured in Siemens (S).
Conductivity (κ)
Conductance of a unit cube of solution, S m-1.
Molar Conductivity (Λm)
Conductance of solution containing 1 mole of electrolyte, S cm2 mol-1.
Equivalent Conductivity (Λeq)
Conductance of solution containing 1 gram equivalent of electrolyte, S cm2 equiv-1.
Cell Constant (G* = l/A)
Ratio of electrode separation to area, cm-1.
Degree of Dissociation (α = Λm / Λm°)
Fraction of electrolyte ionized in solution.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • κ decreases on dilution; Λm increases on dilution.
  • Units of Λm usually involve cm2 mol-1.
  • Kohlrausch's law is used for weak electrolyte limiting conductivity.
  • Grotthuss mechanism explains anomalously high mobility of H+.
  • Cell constant = Measured Resistance × Specific Conductance.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Electrolytic conduction involves physical movement of ions under electric fields.
  • Interionic attractions retard ionic movement in concentrated solutions.
  • Extrapolation to zero concentration gives limiting molar conductivity for strong electrolytes.
  • Sparingly soluble salt solubility is calculated using measured conductivity and limiting molar conductivity.

⚖️ Key Differences

Conductivity (κ) VS Molar Conductivity (Λm)

κ is per unit volume and drops on dilution, whereas Λm is per mole and rises on dilution.

Question 1 of 18

What happens to the specific conductivity of an electrolyte solution upon dilution?

Explanation: Specific conductivity is the conductance of unit volume of solution. On dilution, the number of ions per unit volume decreases, hence conductivity decreases.

Question 2 of 18

What are the units of molar conductivity in standard physical chemistry convention?

Explanation: Molar conductivity is defined as Λm = (κ × 1000)/C, yielding units of S cm2 mol-1.

Question 3 of 18

Whose law states that limiting molar conductivity of an electrolyte is the sum of ionic conductivities?

Explanation: Kohlrausch's law of independent migration of ions states that Λm° is the sum of cationic and anionic contributions.

Question 4 of 18

Which of the following ions has the highest ionic mobility in aqueous solution?

Explanation: H+ (or H3O+) has the highest mobility due to the Grotthuss proton-jump mechanism.

Question 5 of 18

How is cell constant (G*) related to resistance (R) and specific conductance (κ)?

Explanation: Since R = (ρ × l)/A and κ = 1/ρ, R = (l/A)/κ = G / κ, which means G = R × κ.

Question 6 of 18

For a weak electrolyte, the degree of dissociation (α) is given by:

Explanation: Degree of dissociation α is the ratio of molar conductivity at concentration c to that at infinite dilution.

Question 7 of 18

What is the effect of increasing temperature on electrolytic conductance?

Explanation: Temperature increase reduces solvent viscosity and increases ionic kinetic energy, thereby increasing electrolytic conductance.

Question 8 of 18

Which equation represents the variation of molar conductivity with concentration for strong electrolytes?

Explanation: The Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation relates Λm to Λm° and the square root of concentration.

Question 9 of 18

Why can limiting molar conductivity of weak electrolytes not be obtained by graphical extrapolation?

Explanation: At infinite dilution, the curve for weak electrolytes becomes parallel to the y-axis, making accurate extrapolation impossible.

Question 10 of 18

What is the unit of cell constant?

Explanation: Cell constant is l / A, where l is in cm and A is in cm2, giving units of cm-1.

Question 11 of 18

If the resistance of a 0.1 M solution is 100 ohms and the cell constant is 1.2 cm-1, what is the conductivity (κ)?

Explanation: κ = Cell Constant / Resistance = 1.2 / 100 = 0.012 S cm-1.

Question 12 of 18

Which salt solution will have the highest molar conductivity at equivalent concentrations assuming complete dissociation?

Explanation: AlCl3 yields 4 moles of ions per mole of electrolyte, giving a higher number of charge carriers per mole.

Question 13 of 18

Which of the following is a non-electrolyte?

Explanation: Glucose does not dissociate into ions in aqueous solution and thus acts as a non-electrolyte.

Question 14 of 18

In conductometric titrations of a strong acid with a strong base, the conductance initially:

Explanation: Fast-moving H+ ions are replaced by slower Na+ ions, causing conductance to decrease until the equivalence point.

Question 15 of 18

What is the relationship between equivalent conductivity (Λeq) and molar conductivity (Λm) for Al2(SO4)3?

Explanation: For Al2(SO4)3, total positive charge per formula unit is 6. Molar conductivity = Equivalent conductivity × (Valency factor = 6), wait: n-factor is 6, so Λm = 6 Λeq.

Question 16 of 18

Which phenomenon is responsible for the decrease in ionic velocity at high concentrations of strong electrolytes?

Explanation: Asymmetry effect and electrophoretic effect retard ionic motion in concentrated ionic atmospheres.

Question 17 of 18

The SI unit of conductance is:

Explanation: Conductance is the reciprocal of resistance, measured in ohm-1 or Siemens (S).

Question 18 of 18

What is the value of molar conductivity when κ = 0.02 S cm-1 and C = 0.5 mol L-1?

Explanation: Λm = (0.02 × 1000) / 0.5 = 20 / 0.5 = 40 S cm2 mol-1.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the cell constant if a solution with specific conductivity 0.005 S cm-1 exhibits a resistance of 200 ohms.

Solution: Cell Constant = κ × R = 0.005 × 200 = 1.0 cm-1.

Question 2 of 10
The equivalent conductance of 0.1 N NaCl solution is 100 S cm2 equiv-1. What is its specific conductance?

Solution: Λeq = (κ × 1000) / N → 100 = (κ × 1000) / 0.1 → κ = 0.01 S cm-1.

Question 3 of 10
What is the primary reason for the sharp increase in molar conductivity of weak electrolytes upon infinite dilution?

Solution: Weak electrolytes dissociate more completely as concentration approaches zero, raising total ion count.

Question 4 of 10
Which expression represents Kohlrausch's law for acetic acid (CH3COOH)?

Solution: Limiting molar conductivity is the sum of constituent ionic conductivities at infinite dilution.

Question 5 of 10
In the Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation, the slope of the plot of Λm versus √c depends upon:

Solution: The slope depends on the charges on ions (valency type) and properties of the medium such as dielectric constant and viscosity.

Question 6 of 10
If the resistance of a conductivity cell filled with 0.1 mol L-1 KCl is 100 Ω and conductivity is 1.29 S m-1, what is the cell constant in cm-1?

Solution: κ = 1.29 S m-1 = 0.0129 S cm-1. G* = κ × R = 0.0129 × 100 = 1.29 cm-1 (Wait: 0.0129 × 100 = 1.29 cm-1). Let's verify: 1.29 S m-1 = 0.0129 S cm-1. Cell constant = κ × R = 0.0129 × 100 = 1.29 cm-1.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following statements is correct regarding electrolytic solutions?

Solution: Molar conductance always increases with dilution for both strong and weak electrolytes.

Question 8 of 10
The dissociation constant Ka of a weak acid can be expressed in terms of molar conductivity as:

Solution: Substituting α = Λm/Λm° into Ostwald's dilution law gives Ka = cΛm2 / [Λm°(Λm° - Λm)].

Question 9 of 10
Why do ions migrate at a slower rate in concentrated solutions compared to dilute solutions?

Solution: Strong interionic electrostatic forces and drag from ionic atmospheres retard ionic movement in concentrated solutions.

Question 10 of 10
Which apparatus is used to measure the resistance of an electrolytic solution accurately?

Solution: A Wheatstone bridge circuit (using an alternating current source to prevent polarization) measures solution resistance.
