Physical Chemistry

Types of Single Electrodes

Single electrodes constitute half-cells where oxidation or reduction reactions occur at the phase boundary between a metal and its ion solution or a gas and its ions. Their electrode potentials depend on the activities of the participating species according to the Nernst equation.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Metal-Metal Ion and Gas Electrodes

  • A metal-metal ion electrode consists of a metal rod dipped in a solution containing its own metal cations, such as Zn2+/Zn.
  • The electrode potential is directly proportional to the logarithm of the metal ion activity as dictated by the Nernst equation.
  • A gas electrode involves a gas in equilibrium with its respective ions in solution, requiring an inert conductor like platinum for electron transfer.
  • The Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) serves as the primary reference electrode with an assigned potential of exactly 0.00 V at all temperatures.

Metal-Metal Insoluble Salt and Amalgam Electrodes

  • A metal-metal insoluble salt electrode comprises a metal covered with its sparingly soluble salt immersed in a solution containing the same anion.
  • The Calomel electrode (Hg2Cl2/Hg, Cl-) and Silver-Silver Chloride electrode (AgCl/Ag, Cl-) are primary examples of electrodes of the second kind.
  • The potential of a metal-metal insoluble salt electrode depends exclusively on the activity of the anions in the solution.
  • An amalgam electrode utilizes a metal dissolved in mercury, lowering the metal's activity and preventing undesirable side reactions with aqueous solvents.

Redox and Oxidation-Reduction Electrodes

  • A redox electrode consists of an inert metal, typically platinum or gold, immersed in a solution containing ions of the same element in two different oxidation states.
  • An example is the quinhydrone electrode or the Pt | Fe2+, Fe3+ half-cell used for pH and potential measurements.
  • Electron transfer occurs directly across the inert metal surface without dissolving or depositing solid metal phases.
  • The potential is governed by the ratio of activities of the oxidized and reduced forms in the homogeneous solution phase.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Metal-Metal Ion Electrode Potential
EMn+/M = E°Mn+/M + (RT / nF) ln(aMn+)
Conditions: Standard temperature and unit activity of metal ions
Units: E in Volts, aMn+ is dimensionless activity
Gas Electrode Potential (Hydrogen Electrode)
EH+/H2 = E°H+/H2 - (RT / F) ln(aH+ / PH21/2)
Conditions: Involves gas phase pressure P and proton activity a
Units: P in atmospheres or bar, E in Volts
Metal-Metal Insoluble Salt Electrode Potential
ECl-/AgCl/Ag = E°Cl-/AgCl/Ag - (RT / F) ln(aCl-)
Conditions: Anion activity determines the potential of second-type electrodes
Units: E in Volts, aCl- is halide ion activity
Redox Electrode Potential
E = E° - (RT / nF) ln([ared] / [aox])
Conditions: All species present in solution phase at an inert conductor
Units: Concentrations or activities in mol L-1
Nernst Equation for Single Electrode Potential
E = E° - (RT / nF) ln(ared / aox)
Conditions: Applicable for any general half-cell reaction at equilibrium
Units: E and E° in Volts, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin, F in C mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Metal electrodes function via direct cation deposition and dissolution

Observation: Active alkali metals like sodium or potassium react violently with aqueous solutions — They form amalgams or utilize non-aqueous solvents to prevent hydrogen gas evolution and secondary reactions

Exception Standard reduction potentials are independent of external pressure

Observation: Gas electrodes such as the hydrogen electrode show strong pressure dependence — Gaseous species terms appear explicitly in the reaction quotient via gas fugacity or partial pressure

Exception Electrode potentials remain constant under varying ionic strengths

Observation: Real solutions deviate significantly from unit activity at high concentrations — Activity coefficients depart from unity due to interionic electrostatic forces described by the Debye-Hückel theory

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Second Kind Anion Dependency
Potential ∝ - log(aanion)

Application: Remember that for metal-insoluble salt electrodes, increasing the anion concentration decreases the reduction potential.

Inert Conductor Identification
Redox & Gas Electrodes → Pt or Au

Application: Quickly identify that half-cells lacking a solid metal reactant must employ an inert platinum wire to complete the circuit.

Nernst Sign Convention Shortcut
E = E° - (0.0591 / n) log([Reduced] / [Oxidized]) at 298K

Application: Use base-10 log and the 0.0591 constant at room temperature to rapidly compute single electrode potentials.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The standard hydrogen electrode has an assigned potential of 0.00 V at all temperatures.
  • Calomel electrode potential with saturated KCl is +0.2415 V at 25°C.
  • Silver-silver chloride electrodes are widely used as secondary reference electrodes due to their temperature stability.
  • Quinhydrone electrode is a redox electrode used for rapid pH determination in acidic and mildly basic media.
  • Oxidation occurs at the anode, which acts as the negative pole in a galvanic cell.
  • Reduction occurs at the cathode, which acts as the positive pole in a galvanic cell.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing oxidation potential sign with reduction potential sign; IUPAC convention exclusively uses standard reduction potentials.
  • Neglecting the number of electrons (n) transferred in the logarithmic term of the Nernst equation.
  • Assuming gas partial pressure units must be in Pascals instead of atmospheres or bars in the potential calculation.
  • Treating concentration directly as activity in concentrated solutions without applying activity coefficients.

⚡ Quick Revision: Types of Single Electrodes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Metal-Metal Ion Half-Cells

Represents the simplest electrode type where a metal strip dips into a solution of its ions, establishing an equilibrium governed by electron transfer and ion hydration energy.

Gas Reference Systems

Involves gaseous reactants like hydrogen or chlorine adsorbed on platinum surfaces, maintaining constant gas fugacity to establish reproducible reference potentials.

Sparingly Soluble Salt Electrodes

Utilizes a metal coated with its salt (e.g., AgCl or Hg2Cl2) to maintain a fixed anion activity, providing stable potentials independent of minor cation fluctuations.

Oxidation-Reduction Assemblies

Comprises homogeneous solutions of multi-oxidation state ions interacting electronically via inert noble metal conductors without net phase consumption.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Half-Cell (Single Electrode)
A single metallic or gas phase contact with an ionic solution capable of supporting an electrode reaction.
Reference Electrode (Standard Electrode)
An electrode with a stable, known potential used to measure other unknown single electrode potentials.
Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE / NHE)
Platinum wire in 1 M H+ solution under 1 bar H2 gas, assigned 0 V.
Calomel Electrode (Hg2Cl2 / Hg / Cl-)
A secondary reference electrode based on mercury, mercurous chloride, and potassium chloride.
Quinhydrone Electrode (Redox pH Electrode)
An organic redox system composed of quinone and hydroquinone used for pH sensing.
Nernst Equation (E = E° - (RT/nF)ln Q)
Mathematical relation connecting cell or electrode potential to chemical activities.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • SHE potential is 0.00 V at all temperatures.
  • Calomel electrode contains metallic mercury, paste of Hg2Cl2, and KCl solution.
  • Nernst constant (RT/F) equals 0.0591 V at 298 K using base-10 logarithms.
  • Second kind electrodes depend inversely on anion activity.
  • Inert platinum is mandatory for gas and redox electrodes.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Single electrodes are categorized into metal-metal ion, gas, metal-insoluble salt, and redox types.
  • All single electrode potentials are measured relative to the standard hydrogen electrode.
  • The Nernst equation quantitatively describes the effect of concentration and pressure on electrode potential.
  • Reference electrodes must maintain constant potential invariant to current passing through them.

⚖️ Key Differences

Oxidation Potential VS Reduction Potential

Oxidation potential has the exact same magnitude as reduction potential but opposite sign (Eox = -Ered).

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following is classified as a metal-metal insoluble salt electrode?

Explanation: The calomel electrode consists of mercury in contact with mercurous chloride (calomel) and a chloride solution, representing a typical metal-insoluble salt electrode.

Question 2 of 18

What is the standard reduction potential of the Standard Hydrogen Electrode at 298 K?

Explanation: By international convention, the standard hydrogen electrode potential is arbitrarily assigned a value of exactly 0.00 V at all temperatures.

Question 3 of 18

In a quinhydrone electrode, the electrode potential depends upon:

Explanation: The quinhydrone electrode reaction involves protons, making its potential directly dependent on the pH of the solution.

Question 4 of 18

The potential of a metal-metal ion electrode depends on:

Explanation: According to the Nernst equation for metal-metal ion electrodes, the potential varies logarithmically with the activity of metal ions in the solution.

Question 5 of 18

Which metal is commonly used as an inert conductor in gas and redox electrodes?

Explanation: Platinum is chemically inert, highly conductive, and provides a catalytic surface for electron exchange without dissolving in the test solution.

Question 6 of 18

The calomel electrode is reversible with respect to which ion?

Explanation: The potential of a calomel electrode is determined exclusively by the activity of chloride ions in the potassium chloride solution.

Question 7 of 18

What is the value of the constant (2.303 RT / F) at 298 K in the Nernst equation?

Explanation: Multiplying (RT/F) by 2.303 to convert natural log to base-10 at 298 K yields 0.0591 V.

Question 8 of 18

Which of the following half-cells represents a redox electrode?

Explanation: A redox electrode consists of an inert conductor (Pt) immersed in a solution containing ions of an element in two different oxidation states (Fe^2+ and Fe^3+).

Question 9 of 18

What effect does increasing the concentration of metal ions have on the reduction potential of a metal-metal ion electrode?

Explanation: According to the Nernst equation E = E° + (RT/nF)ln(aMn+), increasing metal ion activity increases the reduction potential.

Question 10 of 18

Amalgam electrodes are primarily used to:

Explanation: Amalgamating active metals like sodium or alkali metals with mercury reduces their chemical activity, moderating their reaction rate in aqueous media.

Question 11 of 18

In the silver-silver chloride electrode, the solid phase in contact with silver metal is:

Explanation: The silver-silver chloride electrode consists of a silver wire coated with sparingly soluble silver chloride immersed in a chloride solution.

Question 12 of 18

Why can single electrode potentials not be measured in absolute terms?

Explanation: A complete electrical circuit requires two half-cells (anode and cathode); thus, only cell potential differences can be measured experimentally.

Question 13 of 18

Which reference electrode is secondary and utilizes KCl solutions of 0.1 M, 1.0 M, or saturated concentration?

Explanation: The calomel electrode is prepared with varying concentrations of potassium chloride, yielding decinormal, normal, or saturated calomel electrodes.

Question 14 of 18

For a gas electrode involving chlorine, the potential depends on:

Explanation: Chlorine gas electrode potential depends on the partial pressure of Cl2 gas and the activity of Cl- ions in solution.

Question 15 of 18

Which species acts as the oxidant in a standard Zn2+/Zn half-cell when operating as a reduction half-reaction?

Explanation: In the reduction half-reaction Zn2+ + 2e- → Zn, the zinc ion accepts electrons and acts as the oxidizing agent.

Question 16 of 18

The IUPAC convention mandates that all standard electrode potentials are listed as:

Explanation: IUPAC officially adopts standard reduction potentials as the standard convention for tabulating electrode potentials.

Question 17 of 18

What is the role of the porous plug or salt bridge in electrochemical cells involving single electrodes?

Explanation: A salt bridge allows ion migration to prevent charge accumulation while minimizing liquid junction potentials between half-cells.

Question 18 of 18

Which of the following electrodes is classified as an electrode of the first kind?

Explanation: Electrodes of the first kind are metal-metal ion electrodes, such as copper immersed in copper sulfate solution.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the reduction potential of a zinc electrode where [Zn2+] = 0.01 M at 298 K, given E°Zn2+/Zn = -0.76 V.

Solution: Using E = E° - (0.0591/2)log(1 / 0.01) = -0.76 - 0.02955(2) = -0.76 - 0.0591 = -0.819 V.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following single electrode systems does not involve a solid metal phase participating directly in the redox reaction?

Solution: The standard hydrogen electrode uses an inert platinum wire where hydrogen gas and protons react, without solid metal participation.

Question 3 of 10
The unit of the factor (RT/F) in the Nernst equation is:

Solution: Dividing the gas constant times temperature (J mol-1) by the Faraday constant (C mol-1) yields Joules per Coulomb, which is Volts.

Question 4 of 10
What is the primary function of the mercury in a calomel electrode?

Solution: Liquid mercury provides a stable, conductive electrical interface between the platinum wire lead and the Hg2Cl2 paste.

Question 5 of 10
If the activity of metal ions in a metal-metal ion half-cell is increased by a factor of 10, the reduction potential at 298 K changes by:

Solution: By the Nernst equation, ΔE = (0.0591 / n) log(10) = +0.0591 / n V.

Question 6 of 10
Which electrode system is best suited for measuring the pH of a solution without requiring external gas supplies?

Solution: The quinhydrone electrode is a redox electrode that directly measures hydrogen ion activity (pH) without needing pressurized hydrogen gas.

Question 7 of 10
In an electrochemical cell, a single electrode acting as an anode undergoes:

Solution: By definition, oxidation always occurs at the anode in both galvanic and electrolytic cells.

Question 8 of 10
What is the temperature coefficient (dE/dT) of a standard hydrogen electrode?

Solution: The standard hydrogen electrode potential and its temperature coefficient are both arbitrarily assigned a value of zero by convention.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following expressions correctly represents the electrode potential of a gas electrode (H+/H2)?

Solution: Applying the Nernst equation to H+ + e- → 1/2 H2 yields E = E° - (RT/F) ln(aH+ / PH21/2).

Question 10 of 10
Why is potassium chloride (KCl) preferred over sodium chloride in preparing salt bridges and calomel electrodes?

Solution: Potassium and chloride ions have virtually identical transport numbers (ionic mobilities), effectively minimizing liquid junction potentials.
