Physical Chemistry

Electrochemistry

Electrochemistry describes the interconversion of chemical energy and electrical energy through redox reactions occurring at electrode-electrolyte interfaces. It governs the relationship between cell potential, Gibbs free energy, and the concentration of chemical species in solution.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrochemical Cells and Potentials

  • The Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) is assigned a potential of 0.00 V by convention.
  • Galvanic cells convert chemical energy to electrical energy via spontaneous redox reactions (ΔG < 0).
  • Electrolytic cells require an external power source to drive non-spontaneous reactions (ΔG > 0).
  • The Cell Potential (E°cell) is calculated as E°cathode - E°anode.

Conductance and Ionic Mobility

  • Molar conductivity (Λm) increases with dilution as inter-ionic attractions decrease.
  • Specific conductance (κ) decreases with dilution due to the reduction in the number of ions per unit volume.
  • Transport number (ti) represents the fraction of total current carried by a specific ion.
  • Kohlrausch's Law allows the determination of limiting molar conductivity for weak electrolytes.

Electrolysis and Faraday's Laws

  • Faraday's Second Law states that the mass of different substances deposited by the same quantity of electricity is proportional to their equivalent weights.
  • Overpotential is the extra voltage required to overcome kinetic barriers during gas evolution at electrodes.
  • Electrochemical series predicts the order of discharge of ions based on their reduction potentials.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Gibbs Free Energy Relation
ΔG° = -nFE°cell
Conditions: Standard state conditions
Units: ΔG in Joules, n (dimensionless), F = 96485 C/mol
Kohlrausch Law
Λ°m = ν+λ°+ + ν-λ°-
Conditions: Infinite dilution of strong electrolytes
Units: Λ in S·cm²/mol
Faraday's First Law
m = ZIt
Conditions: Constant current flow
Units: m in grams, I in Amperes, t in seconds
Debye-Hückel-Onsager Equation
Λm = Λ°m - (A + BΛ°m)√c
Conditions: Strong electrolytes at low concentration
Units: c in mol/L
Nernst Equation
Ecell = E°cell - (RT/nF) ln Q
Conditions: Non-standard concentration conditions
Units: E in Volts, R in J/mol·K, T in Kelvin, F in C/mol
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher reduction potential ions discharge first

Observation: Overpotential effects — Kinetic factors (e.g., H2 evolution on Hg) require higher energy than thermodynamic predictions.

Exception Strong electrolytes follow Debye-Hückel-Onsager

Observation: Very high concentrations — Ion-ion interactions become too complex for the simple square-root dependence.

Exception Metals conduct via electrons

Observation: Ionic solids — Conductivity occurs via ion migration only when in molten or dissolved states.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • 1 Faraday = 96485 Coulombs per mole of electrons.
  • Cell potential is an intensive property; it does not depend on the amount of substance.
  • Gibbs free energy is an extensive property; it depends on the stoichiometry of the reaction.
  • Conductivity (κ) units are S·cm-1.
  • Molar conductivity (Λm) units are S·cm2·mol-1.
  • The salt bridge maintains electrical neutrality and minimizes liquid junction potential.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the sign of the anode/cathode in galvanic vs electrolytic cells.
  • Forgetting to convert temperature to Kelvin when using the Nernst equation.
  • Using molarity instead of activity in the reaction quotient (Q) for precise calculations.
  • Neglecting the stoichiometric coefficient 'n' in the Nernst equation exponent.

⚡ Quick Revision: Electrochemistry

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrochemical Thermodynamics

The relationship between cell potential and Gibbs free energy (ΔG = -nFE) links macroscopic electrical work to microscopic chemical potential.

Ionic Conductance

Conductivity depends on ion concentration, mobility, and charge; it is measured using a Wheatstone bridge setup.

Nernstian Behavior

The Nernst equation accounts for concentration variations, showing that cell potential decreases as the reaction approaches equilibrium.

Faraday's Laws

These laws quantify the mass of substance transformed during electrolysis based on the total charge passed through the circuit.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Electromotive Force (EMF)
The potential difference between electrodes when no current flows.
Molar Conductivity (Λm)
Conductivity of a solution containing one mole of electrolyte.
Cell Constant (G*)
Ratio of distance between electrodes to area (l/A).
Redox (Reduction-Oxidation)
Simultaneous electron transfer processes.
Equivalent Weight (Eq. Wt.)
Molar mass divided by the number of electrons transferred.
Electrode Potential (E)
Tendency of an electrode to lose or gain electrons.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Galvanic cells produce electricity.
  • Electrolytic cells consume electricity.
  • Conductivity decreases with dilution.
  • Molar conductivity increases with dilution.

⚖️ Key Differences

Conductivity (κ) VS Molar Conductivity (Λm)

κ is per unit volume, Λm is per mole of solute.
