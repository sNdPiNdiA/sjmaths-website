Streaming Potential

Streaming potential is an electrokinetic phenomenon generated when a liquid is forced to flow through a porous plug or capillary under a pressure gradient. The hydrodynamic shear of the mobile layer of the electrical double layer creates a macroscopic potential difference along the flow axis.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Origin and Mechanism of Streaming Potential

  • At the solid-liquid interface, preferential adsorption of ions forms an electrical double layer consisting of a fixed Stern layer and a diffuse mobile layer.
  • Applied hydrodynamic pressure ΔP drags the mobile ions in the diffuse layer toward the low-pressure end of the capillary.
  • Accumulation of charge creates an opposing conduction current through the bulk liquid until a steady-state streaming potential is established.

Factors Affecting Streaming Potential

  • Streaming potential Es is directly proportional to the applied pressure gradient ΔP and the electrokinetic zeta potential ζ.
  • Increasing the bulk electrolyte concentration increases solution conductivity κ, which consequently decreases the observed streaming potential.
  • The magnitude of Es depends heavily on the dielectric constant εr and dynamic viscosity η of the permeating liquid.

Experimental Measurement and Applications

  • Streaming potential is measured using non-polarizable reference electrodes (such as Ag/AgCl) placed upstream and downstream of the porous plug.
  • It provides a direct experimental method to determine the zeta potential of mineral surfaces, membranes, and synthetic polymers.
  • Used extensively in assessing surface charge modifications in nanofiltration and reverse osmosis membranes.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Streaming Current Equation
Is = (εr ε0 ζ π r2 ΔP) / (η L)
Conditions: Capillary tube of radius r and length L under hydrostatic pressure ΔP.
Units: Is in Amperes, r and L in meters
Electrokinetic Potential (Zeta Potential) Relation
ζ = (Es η κ) / (εr ε0 ΔP)
Conditions: Derived directly from the rearranged Quincke-Smoluchowski expression assuming constant bulk permittivity and viscosity.
Units: ζ in Volts
Conductivity Correction in Porous Plugs (Fairbrother-Mastin)
κeff = κbulk + (2 Ks) / r
Conditions: Accounts for surface conductance contributions in narrow capillaries.
Units: κeff and κbulk in S m-1, surface conductivity Ks in S
Quincke-Smoluchowski Equation for Streaming Potential
Es / ΔP = (εr ε0 ζ) / (η κ)
Conditions: Valid for thin electrical double layers where the capillary radius is much larger than the Debye length (κr >> 1).
Units: Es in Volts, ΔP in Pascals, εr (dimensionless), ε0 in F m-1, ζ in Volts, η in Pa s, κ in S m-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Streaming potential increases linearly with electrolyte concentration.

Observation: High concentrations suppress streaming potential drastically. — High ionic strength compresses the electrical double layer (smaller Debye length) and increases bulk conductivity κ, reducing the potential.

Exception Streaming potential is independent of capillary radius in wide pores.

Observation: Deviations occur in ultra-narrow nanochannels. — Surface conductance becomes comparable to bulk conductance, necessitating Fairbrother-Mastin corrections.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Electrokinetic Mnemonic
SPE = Streaming Pressure Electrokinetic

Application: Remember that Streaming Potential involves Pressure causing Electricity (Potential).

Proportionality Shortcut
Es ∝ (ζ × ΔP) / κ

Application: Quickly determine that potential increases with zeta and pressure, but decreases with conductivity.

Sign Convention Rule
Sign(Es) = Sign(ζ)

Application: The polarity of the streaming potential matches the sign of the zeta potential at the shear plane.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Streaming potential is the reverse phenomenon of electroosmosis.
  • The Quincke-Smoluchowski equation assumes laminar flow (Poiseuille flow) inside the capillary.
  • Units of zeta potential ζ are typically expressed in milliVolts (mV).
  • Surface conductivity corrections are essential when measuring low-conductivity organic liquids.
  • The zeta potential represents the electric potential at the hydrodynamic shear plane.
  • Streaming current is independent of the bulk solution conductivity κ.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing streaming potential with sedimentation potential (Dorn effect), which uses gravity instead of applied pressure.
  • Neglecting the effect of temperature on liquid viscosity η when calculating zeta potential.
  • Assuming the diffuse layer extends infinitely into the bulk without accounting for double layer compression.
  • Using polarizable electrodes instead of reversible electrodes, leading to undesired redox potentials.

⚡ Quick Revision: Streaming Potential

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrical Double Layer

The interface between a charged solid surface and a polar liquid contains a compact Stern layer and a diffuse mobile layer where counter-ions predominate.

Quincke-Smoluchowski Relation

Relates the generated streaming potential directly to pressure gradient, liquid permittivity, viscosity, zeta potential, and electrical conductivity.

Electrokinetic Coupling

Demonstrates the direct thermodynamic coupling between hydrodynamic fluxes and electrical fluxes via Onsager reciprocal relations.

Surface Conductance

Surface charge mobility contributes significantly to total current in narrow pores, requiring conductivity corrections for accurate zeta potential extraction.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Streaming Potential (Es)
Potential generated by forced liquid flow through a porous medium in Volts.
Zeta Potential (ζ)
Electrokinetic potential at the shear plane in Volts.
Pressure Gradient (ΔP)
Applied hydrostatic pressure difference in Pascals.
Solution Conductivity (κ)
Bulk electrical conductivity in S m-1.
Dynamic Viscosity (η)
Resistance of fluid to flow in Pa s.
Streaming Current (Is)
Convective ionic current carried by moving liquid in Amperes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Es / ΔP = (εζ) / (ηκ)
  • Streaming potential is inversely proportional to electrolyte conductivity.
  • Zeta potential calculation requires accurate viscosity and dielectric constant values.
  • Non-polarizable Ag/AgCl electrodes prevent polarization errors.
  • Valid primarily when double layer thickness is much smaller than pore radius.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Hydrodynamic flow shears the diffuse electrical double layer.
  • Charge separation generates a macroscopic potential difference.
  • Quincke-Smoluchowski equation is the foundational mathematical model.
  • Widely applied in colloid science and membrane characterization.

⚖️ Key Differences

Streaming Potential VS Electroosmosis

Streaming potential uses pressure to generate electricity; electroosmosis uses electricity to generate fluid flow.

Question 8 of 18

What happens to streaming current when bulk electrolyte concentration increases?

Explanation: Streaming current increases with concentration because more counter-ions are available in the mobile diffuse layer to be transported by flow.

Explanation: Measuring Es as a function of ΔP is a standard experimental technique for calculating zeta potential.

What type of fluid flow is assumed in deriving electrokinetic equations in capillaries?

Question 14 of 18

If the zeta potential of a capillary wall is negative, what is the sign of the streaming potential?

Explanation: The polarity of the streaming potential matches the sign of the zeta potential because mobile counter-ions are carried downstream.

Question 15 of 18

Which correction accounts for surface conduction in narrow pores?

Explanation: The Fairbrother-Mastin correction accounts for the excess conductivity near charged surfaces in narrow capillaries.

Question 16 of 18

Streaming potential is classified under which category of phenomena?

Explanation: Streaming potential is a classical electrokinetic phenomenon involving coupled hydrodynamic and electrical transport.

Question 17 of 18

What is the effect of increasing temperature on dynamic viscosity η and streaming potential?

Explanation: Since viscosity is in the denominator, a decrease in viscosity with temperature leads to an increase in streaming potential.

Question 18 of 18

Explanation: Counter-ions populate the diffuse mobile layer to neutralize the fixed surface charge.
