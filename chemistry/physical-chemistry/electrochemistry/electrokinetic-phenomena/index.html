Electrokinetic Phenomena

Electrokinetic phenomena encompass a class of physicochemical processes where relative motion occurs between a charged surface and the surrounding liquid phase under the influence of an electric field or pressure gradient. These phenomena are governed by the properties of the electrical double layer formed at the solid-liquid interface.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Origin and Structure of the Electrical Double Layer

  • The Electrical Double Layer (EDL) consists of an immobile Stern layer of specifically adsorbed counterions and a diffuse Gouy-Chapman layer extending into the bulk solution.
  • The zeta potential (ζ) is defined as the electric potential at the shear plane (slip plane) relative to the bulk fluid, distinct from the total surface potential.
  • Increasing electrolyte concentration compresses the diffuse double layer, reducing the effective thickness of the EDL (Debye length, 1/κ) and decreasing the magnitude of ζ.
  • Potential-determining ions adsorbed onto the colloid surface determine the sign of the surface charge and consequently the sign of the zeta potential.

Primary Electrokinetic Manifestations

  • Electrophoresis involves the migration of charged colloidal particles through a stationary liquid medium under an applied external electric field.
  • Electroosmosis describes the bulk flow of liquid through a porous plug, membrane, or capillary tube when an electric potential gradient is applied across it.
  • Streaming potential is the generation of an electric potential difference when a liquid is forced to flow through a porous plug or capillary under hydrostatic pressure.
  • Sedimentation potential (Dorn effect) is the electric field generated when charged particles settle under the influence of gravity through a liquid medium.

Factors Influencing Zeta Potential and Electrokinetic Mobility

  • The addition of polyvalent counterions (e.g., Al3+ to negatively charged sols) drastically lowers the zeta potential and induces coagulation per the Hardy-Schulze rule.
  • pH variation alters the surface charge of amphoteric colloids (such as proteins or metal oxides), shifting the zeta potential through the isoelectric point (IEP) where ζ = 0.
  • Viscosity (η) is inversely proportional to electrophoretic mobility and electroosmotic velocity; raising temperature decreases solvent viscosity and increases mobility.
  • Surface-active agents (surfactants) alter the shear plane location and hydrophobic interactions, shifting the measured zeta potential significantly.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Streaming Potential Equation
Es / ΔP = (εr ε0 ζ) / (η λ)
Conditions: Laminar flow through a capillary or porous plug under pressure difference ΔP
Units: Es (streaming potential in V), ΔP (pressure in Pa), λ (solution conductivity in S m-1)
Electroosmotic Velocity Equation
veo = - (εr ε0 ζ E) / η
Conditions: Uniform electric field E applied across a capillary or porous plug
Units: veo (velocity in m s-1), E (electric field strength in V m-1)
Sedimentation Potential (Dorn Effect) Relation
Esed ∝ g Δρ r2 ζ / (η λ)
Conditions: Free sedimentation of charged colloidal particles under gravity or centrifugal force
Units: Esed (sedimentation potential in V), g (acceleration due to gravity), Δρ (density difference), r (particle radius)
Debye-Hückel Parameter
κ = [ (2 e2 I NA) / (εr ε0 kB T) ]1/2
Conditions: Dilute electrolyte solutions, 1:1 electrolyte
Units: κ in m-1, I (ionic strength), NA (Avogadro constant), T (temperature in K)
Helmholtz-Smoluchowski Equation for Electrophoretic Mobility
μ = (εr ε0 ζ) / η
Conditions: Valid for thin electrical double layers where the particle radius >> Debye length (κa >> 1)
Units: μ in m2 V-1 s-1, εr (dielectric constant), ε0 (permittivity of vacuum), ζ (zeta potential in V), η (dynamic viscosity in Pa s)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher electrolyte concentration always increases solution conductivity and should enhance electrical effects

Observation: High electrolyte concentration compresses the double layer and sharply reduces zeta potential — Excess screening ions restrict the diffuse layer thickness, reducing the net electrostatic potential at the slipping plane.

Exception Electrophoretic mobility is independent of particle size for very large particles

Observation: Mobility depends heavily on the ratio of particle radius to Debye length (κa) — When particles are comparable in size to the Debye length, relaxation and retardation effects complicate the simple Helmholtz-Smoluchowski limit.

Exception Colloidal particles migrate strictly toward the electrode of opposite charge

Observation: Charge reversal can occur at high surfactant or multivalent ion concentrations — Specific chemical adsorption of multivalent counterions inside the slip plane reverses the sign of the zeta potential.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Paired Electrokinetic Duals
Electric Field ↔ Mechanical Motion

Application: Electrophoresis and electroosmosis are direct conversions (Electric Field → Motion), whereas streaming potential and sedimentation potential are inverse conversions (Motion → Electric Field).

Viscosity-Temperature Inverse Rule
μ ∝ 1 / η

Application: In numerical questions, remember that liquid viscosity drops with rising temperature, leading to a linear increase in electrophoretic mobility for a constant zeta potential.

Zeta Potential Sign Mnemonic
Shear Plane Potential

Application: Always remember that zeta potential is measured at the slipping plane, NOT at the solid surface; therefore, structural changes outside the Stern layer directly alter zeta potential.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Zeta potential is the potential drop across the diffuse part of the electrical double layer, specifically at the hydrodynamic shear plane.
  • The Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation assumes that the electrical double layer is very thin compared to the particle radius.
  • Electroosmosis is used commercially in dewatering clay soils and muds in civil engineering.
  • Streaming potential measurements provide a rapid route to determine zeta potentials of flat membranes and capillary inner walls.
  • The Hardy-Schulze rule states that the coagulating power of an electrolyte depends primarily on the valence of the counterion.
  • At the isoelectric point, the zeta potential is zero and colloidal particles exhibit zero electrophoretic mobility.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the Nernst potential (total potential difference across the interface) with the zeta potential (potential at the shear plane).
  • Assuming electrokinetic phenomena occur only in aqueous media; they also occur in non-polar organic solvents with appropriate charging agents.
  • Neglecting the sign of viscosity or dielectric constant terms when computing absolute velocities.
  • Forgetting that the diffuse layer thickness (Debye length) decreases as ionic strength increases.

⚡ Quick Revision: Electrokinetic Phenomena

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrical Double Layer Foundation

The interface between a charged solid surface and an electrolyte solution develops an electrical double layer comprising a fixed Stern layer and a mobile diffuse layer.

Zeta Potential Significance

Zeta potential (ζ) quantifies the electrostatic potential at the slipping plane and serves as a master variable for colloidal stability and electrokinetic transport.

Coupled Transport Processes

Electrokinetic phenomena couple mechanical flow and electrical fields via the mobile ions within the diffuse layer, categorized into forward and inverse effects.

Quantitative Modeling

Equations derived by Helmholtz, Smoluchowski, and Debye-Hückel link observable velocities and potentials to fundamental interfacial properties.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Zeta Potential (ζ)
Electric potential at the hydrodynamic shear plane. Unit: Volts (V).
Electrophoretic Mobility (μ)
Velocity per unit electric field. Unit: m2 V-1 s-1.
Debye Length (1/κ)
Characteristic thickness of the electrical double layer. Unit: meters (m).
Streaming Potential (Es)
Potential generated by fluid flow under pressure. Unit: Volts (V).
Electroosmotic Velocity (veo)
Fluid velocity driven by an applied electric field. Unit: m s-1.
Slip Plane (Shear Plane)
Boundary separating immobile fluid/ions from mobile diffuse layer fluid.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Zeta potential is measured at the shear plane, not the solid surface.
  • Electrophoresis and electroosmosis are electric-field-driven processes.
  • Streaming and sedimentation potentials are pressure- or gravity-driven electrical responses.
  • Increasing electrolyte concentration compresses the diffuse double layer.
  • At the isoelectric point, electrophoretic mobility drops to zero.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Electrokinetic phenomena depend on relative motion at charged interfaces.
  • The electrical double layer structure dictates the magnitude of electrokinetic coefficients.
  • Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation connects mobility, dielectric constant, zeta potential, and viscosity.
  • Colloidal stability is directly correlated with the magnitude of the zeta potential.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nernst Potential VS Zeta Potential

Nernst potential is the total potential across the interface, whereas zeta potential is measured only at the slipping plane.

Question 1 of 18

What is the physical location where zeta potential is defined?

Explanation: Zeta potential is specifically defined as the potential at the slipping (shear) plane relative to the bulk solution.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following is an example of an electrokinetic phenomenon where fluid motion generates an electric potential?

Explanation: Streaming potential is generated when fluid is forced to flow through a porous medium under pressure.

Question 3 of 18

How does an increase in electrolyte concentration affect the thickness of the diffuse double layer?

Explanation: Higher ionic strength increases screening, compressing the diffuse double layer and decreasing its thickness (Debye length).

Question 4 of 18

What is the driving force in electrophoresis?

Explanation: Electrophoresis involves the migration of charged particles under the influence of an applied electric field.

Question 5 of 18

In the Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation for electrophoretic mobility, what is in the denominator?

Explanation: The mobility μ = (εr ε0 ζ) / η, showing dynamic viscosity (η) in the denominator.

Question 6 of 18

What is the zeta potential value at the isoelectric point of a colloidal suspension?

Explanation: At the isoelectric point, the net surface charge at the shear plane is zero, making the zeta potential zero.

Question 7 of 18

Which phenomenon describes the movement of liquid through a porous plug under an applied electric field?

Explanation: Electroosmosis is the bulk flow of liquid through a stationary porous matrix induced by an electric field.

Question 8 of 18

The Dorn effect is another name for which electrokinetic phenomenon?

Explanation: Sedimentation potential, generated by particles settling under gravity, is commonly referred to as the Dorn effect.

Question 9 of 18

If the viscosity of the liquid medium decreases while keeping other parameters constant, how does electrophoretic mobility change?

Explanation: Since mobility is inversely proportional to viscosity (μ ∝ 1 / η), a decrease in viscosity leads to an increase in mobility.

Question 10 of 18

Which ions are most effective in reducing the zeta potential of a negatively charged hydrophobic sol according to the Hardy-Schulze rule?

Explanation: According to the Hardy-Schulze rule, coagulating power increases sharply with the valence of the counterion; hence Al3+ is most effective for negative sols.

Question 11 of 18

What represents the inverse of the Debye length (κ)?

Explanation: The parameter 1/κ represents the characteristic thickness of the diffuse double layer.

Question 12 of 18

Which parameters are related by the streaming potential equation?

Explanation: Streaming potential relates the generated electric potential Es directly to the applied pressure difference ΔP.

Question 13 of 18

What is the primary cause of charge on colloidal particles in aqueous media?

Explanation: Colloidal particles acquire charge primarily through selective adsorption of potential-determining ions from solution.

Question 14 of 18

In microelectrophoresis experiments, stationary levels exist in a flat capillary because:

Explanation: Electroosmotic flow at the charged glass walls creates a parabolic velocity profile, necessitating measurement at specific stationary levels to obtain true electrophoretic velocity.

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following is true regarding the Stern layer?

Explanation: The Stern layer is the inner, tightly bound layer of specifically adsorbed counterions that remain immobile with respect to the solid surface.

Question 16 of 18

How does temperature affect zeta potential in general?

Explanation: Temperature changes affect dielectric permittivity, viscosity, and ion adsorption equilibria, causing complex variations in zeta potential.

Question 17 of 18

Which electrokinetic property is best suited for measuring the surface charge characteristics of flat macroscopic plates?

Explanation: Streaming potential is uniquely suited for studying macroscopic surfaces, membranes, and capillary channels.

Question 18 of 18

What is the sign of the electroosmotic velocity vector relative to the electric field for a negatively charged capillary wall?

Explanation: Excess positive counterions in the diffuse layer near a negative wall move toward the cathode, meaning electroosmotic flow goes in the direction of the electric field while particle migration depends on particle charge.

Question 1 of 10
Which equation correctly relates electrophoretic mobility (μ) to zeta potential (ζ) under thin double layer conditions?

Solution: The Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation is μ = (εr ε0 ζ) / η.

Question 2 of 10
What happens to the zeta potential when an indifferent electrolyte is added to a hydrophobic sol?

Solution: Addition of indifferent electrolytes increases ionic strength, compresses the double layer, and decreases the magnitude of the zeta potential.

Question 3 of 10
Which phenomenon is the exact inverse of electroosmosis in terms of energy conversion?

Solution: Electroosmosis converts electrical energy into mechanical liquid flow, whereas streaming potential converts mechanical pressure flow into electrical potential.

Question 4 of 10
What is the primary condition for the validity of the Smoluchowski equation for electrophoresis?

Solution: The Smoluchowski limit assumes a thin electrical double layer compared to particle radius, which translates to κa >> 1.

Question 5 of 10
Which term describes the potential at the outer Helmholtz plane?

Solution: The potential at the outer Helmholtz plane corresponds to the Stern potential, which differs from the zeta potential at the shear plane.

Question 6 of 10
In sedimenting colloidal suspensions, the potential difference created between the top and bottom of the liquid column is called:

Solution: Sedimentation potential (Dorn effect) is generated when charged particles settle under gravity.

Question 7 of 10
How does raising the pH affect the zeta potential of a metal oxide surface starting from an acidic medium?

Solution: Raising pH increases OH- adsorption (or deprotonates surface sites), lowering the zeta potential and eventually passing through the isoelectric point.

Question 8 of 10
What is the unit of electrophoretic mobility in SI units?

Solution: Mobility is velocity per unit electric field: (m s-1) / (V m-1) = m2 V-1 s-1.

Question 9 of 10
Why is microelectrophoresis performed at specific stationary levels in flat cells?

Solution: Electroosmotic flow along the cell walls creates a parabolic velocity profile; measuring at stationary levels corrects for this bulk liquid movement.

Question 10 of 10
Which colloidal property is most directly inferred from measurements of electrophoretic mobility?

Solution: Electrophoretic mobility is directly converted to zeta potential using electrokinetic equations like Helmholtz-Smoluchowski.
