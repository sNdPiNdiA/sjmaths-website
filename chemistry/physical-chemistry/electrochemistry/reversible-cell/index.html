Reversible Cell

A reversible cell is an electrochemical system where the chemical reaction proceeds in the reverse direction upon the application of an external opposing electromotive force (EMF) slightly greater than the cell's own EMF. The system remains in thermodynamic equilibrium at every stage of the process, ensuring no net change in the chemical composition of the electrodes or electrolyte when the current is zero.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Requirements for Reversibility

  • The cell reaction must be chemically reversible, meaning the products can be converted back to reactants by reversing the current.
  • The external opposing EMF must be infinitesimally greater than the cell EMF to drive the reaction in reverse.
  • No irreversible side reactions or chemical changes (e.g., gas evolution) should occur at the electrodes.
  • The system must satisfy the thermodynamic criteria where ΔG = -nFE.

Electrode Potential and EMF

  • The EMF of a reversible cell is the potential difference between electrodes when no current flows.
  • The Nernst equation defines the dependence of cell potential on the concentration of active species.
  • Reversible cells act as potentiometric sensors because they provide a stable, reproducible potential.

Temperature Dependence of Cell EMF

  • The Gibbs-Helmholtz equation relates the EMF to the enthalpy change of the cell reaction.
  • The term (∂E/∂T)P represents the temperature coefficient of the cell.
  • If (∂E/∂T)P > 0, the cell absorbs heat from the surroundings during operation.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Gibbs Free Energy Relation
ΔG = -nFEcell
Conditions: Standard conditions for ΔG°.
Units: ΔG in Joules, n (dimensionless), F = 96485 C/mol.
Temperature Coefficient of EMF
ΔH = nF[T(∂E/∂T)P - E]
Conditions: Constant pressure P.
Units: ΔH in Joules, (∂E/∂T)P in V/K.
Equilibrium Constant Relation
ln Keq = (nFE°cell) / RT
Conditions: At chemical equilibrium.
Units: Keq is dimensionless.
Nernst Equation
Ecell = E°cell - (RT/nF) ln Q
Conditions: Applicable for any cell reaction at temperature T.
Units: E in Volts, R in J/K·mol, T in Kelvin, F in C/mol.
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Spontaneous cells produce work.

Observation: Irreversible cells (e.g., Daniell cell with high current). — Internal resistance and polarization lead to heat dissipation, violating the reversible work condition.

Exception Nernst equation assumes activity.

Observation: Concentrated solutions. — Inter-ionic attractions cause deviations from ideal activity, requiring activity coefficients.

Exception Reversible cells are stable.

Observation: Cells with gas electrodes (e.g., H2/H+). — Adsorption kinetics and surface poisoning can introduce irreversibility.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • A reversible cell operates under thermodynamic equilibrium.
  • The EMF of a reversible cell is a state function.
  • Reversible cells do not involve irreversible chemical changes.
  • The temperature coefficient (∂E/∂T)P determines the entropy change.
  • ΔS = nF(∂E/∂T)P.
  • Reversible cells are used in standard reference electrodes.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing cell potential with EMF; EMF is measured at zero current.
  • Ignoring the temperature coefficient in enthalpy calculations.
  • Miscalculating n in the Nernst equation for complex redox reactions.
  • Assuming standard conditions (1M) for non-standard cell potentials.

⚡ Quick Revision: Reversible Cell

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Thermodynamic Equilibrium

A reversible cell maintains chemical equilibrium at the electrode-electrolyte interface. The potential is defined by the Nernst equation.

Work and Energy

The electrical work done by a reversible cell is equal to the decrease in Gibbs free energy. ΔG = -nFE.

Temperature Effects

The temperature coefficient of EMF relates the entropy and enthalpy changes of the cell reaction to the potential.

Reversibility Criteria

A cell is reversible if the reaction can be reversed by an infinitesimal change in external potential without side reactions.

Measure of disorder, J/K·mol.
Enthalpy (ΔH)
Heat content change, J/mol.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔG = -nFE
  • ΔS = nF(∂E/∂T)P
  • ΔH = nF[T(∂E/∂T)P - E]
  • Ecell = Ecathode - Eanode
  • n is the number of moles of electrons transferred.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Reversible cells maintain equilibrium.
  • EMF is the maximum potential difference.
  • Thermodynamic parameters are derived from EMF.
  • Nernst equation is the fundamental governing law.

⚖️ Key Differences

Cell Potential VS EMF

EMF is measured at zero current; potential is measured under load.
