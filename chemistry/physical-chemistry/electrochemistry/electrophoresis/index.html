Physical Chemistry

Electrophoresis

Electrophoresis is the directed migration of charged colloidal particles or ions under the influence of an external electric field. This electrokinetic phenomenon serves as a primary method for separating and characterizing biomolecules and lyophobic sols based on their charge-to-mass ratio.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrokinetic Potential and Double Layer

  • The electrical double layer consists of a fixed inner Stern layer and a mobile diffuse Gouy-Chapman layer surrounding dispersed colloidal particles.
  • The potential difference between the shear plane of the moving boundary and the electroneutral bulk solution is defined as the zeta potential (ζ).
  • Colloidal stability is directly proportional to the magnitude of the zeta potential due to electrostatic repulsion preventing particle aggregation.

Mechanism and Experimental Setup

  • When direct current electrodes are immersed in a colloidal sol, positively charged particles migrate toward the cathode, known as cataphoresis.
  • Negatively charged particles migrate toward the anode, a process termed anaphoresis.
  • The U-tube method of Tiselius electrophoresis enables direct visualization of boundary migration using optical Schlieren scanning systems.

Factors Affecting Migration Velocity

  • The magnitude of the net charge on the dispersed phase determines both the direction and force of electrostatic migration.
  • Increasing medium viscosity (η) directly retards electrophoretic velocity according to hydrodynamic drag principles.
  • Electrolyte concentration alters the thickness of the electrical double layer, compressing the diffuse layer and reducing mobility at high ionic strengths.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Electrophoretic Mobility
μ = \varepsilon ζ6π η
Conditions: Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation for large flat particles in terms of zeta potential
Units: μ in m2·V-1·s-1, ϵ in F·m-1, ζ in V, η in Pa·s
Hückel Equation for Mobility
μ = 2\varepsilon ζ3π η
Conditions: Applicable for small non-conducting rigid spherical particles in media of high ionic strength
Units: μ in m2·V-1·s-1, ϵ in F·m-1, ζ in V, η in Pa·s
Zeta Potential Definition
ζ = 4π η μ\varepsilon
Conditions: Relates electrokinetic potential to mobility across the diffuse double layer
Units: ζ in Volts, μ in m2·V-1·s-1, η in Pa·s, ϵ in F·m-1
Electrophoretic Velocity
v = μ × E
Conditions: Applied uniform electric field E in a medium of viscosity η
Units: v in m·s-1, μ in m2·V-1·s-1, E in V·m-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Particles with higher net charge migrate faster in an electric field

Observation: High ionic strength buffers severely reduce migration speed despite high net charge — High electrolyte concentration causes severe compression of the diffuse double layer, lowering the zeta potential and increasing retarding forces.

Exception Positively charged particles always migrate to the cathode

Observation: Electroosmotic flow can reverse net liquid movement past fixed charges on solid support walls — Charged surfaces of silica or glass capillary walls develop a streaming potential that drags bulk fluid in the opposite direction.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Charge Direction Mnemonic
CAT-ode attracts CAT-ions & Cathaphoresis; AN-ode attracts AN-ions & Anaphoresis

Application: Instantly remember that positively charged sols undergo cataphoresis toward the cathode.

Mobility vs Viscosity Shortcut
μ ∝ 1η

Application: Quickly determine that doubling the solvent viscosity halves the electrophoretic mobility.

Zeta Potential Sign Test
Direction of boundary shift = Sign of particle charge

Application: Determine sol charge sign by observing which electrode boundary moves downward.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Electrophoresis is used to determine the sign of the charge on colloidal particles.
  • The Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation applies to thick particles where particle radius is much greater than double layer thickness.
  • Proteins migrate as zwitterions and possess zero net mobility at their isoelectric point.
  • Zone electrophoresis uses porous supporting media such as paper, cellulose acetate, or polyacrylamide gels.
  • Colloidal ferric hydroxide [Fe(OH)3] sol is positively charged and moves toward the cathode during electrophoresis.
  • Arsenic sulfide [As2S3] sol is negatively charged and migrates toward the anode.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming electrophoresis only applies to macromolecules; it applies to all lyophobic and lyophilic colloidal dispersions.
  • Confusing electrophoresis with electrodialysis, where ions pass through semipermeable membranes driven by a concentration and potential gradient.
  • Neglecting temperature effects; Joule heating during runs changes buffer viscosity and alters migration rates.
  • Forgetting that pH changes modify protein charges via protonation and deprotonation equilibria.

⚡ Quick Revision: Electrophoresis

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrokinetic Basis

Electrophoresis relies on the interaction between an external electric field and the net electrical charge of suspended colloidal particles.

Double Layer Dynamics

The potential at the slipping plane, termed zeta potential, dictates the velocity of particle transport through the liquid medium.

Analytical Separations

Variations in charge-to-mass ratios allow high-resolution separation of complex mixtures in gel matrices.

Isoelectric Point Control

Adjusting buffer pH to match a molecule's isoelectric point halts migration entirely due to zero net charge.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Electrophoresis (v = μE)
Migration of charged particles under an electric field.
Zeta Potential (ζ)
Electrokinetic potential at the shear plane of a double layer (Volts).
Cataphoresis (Positive sol migration)
Migration of positively charged colloidal particles toward the cathode.
Anaphoresis (Negative sol migration)
Migration of negatively charged colloidal particles toward the anode.
Isoelectric Point (pI)
pH value at which net electrophoretic mobility is zero.
Electroosmosis (Fluid transport)
Movement of liquid relative to a stationary charged surface under an electric field.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Fe(OH)3 sol moves to the cathode (+ charge)
  • As2S3 sol moves to the anode (- charge)
  • Mobility is inversely proportional to medium viscosity
  • Zeta potential governs electrokinetic stability
  • Isoelectric point gives zero migration velocity

📌 Quick Recall

  • Electrophoresis separates species based on charge and size differences.
  • Buffer pH determines the magnitude and sign of amphoteric molecules.
  • Gel supports eliminate convection currents during analytical runs.
  • Viscosity and ionic strength are key environmental control parameters.

⚖️ Key Differences

Electrophoresis VS Electrodialysis

Electrophoresis moves particles through a fluid, whereas electrodialysis transports ions through selective membranes.

Question 1 of 20

Which of the following colloidal solutions moves toward the anode during electrophoresis?

Explanation: Arsenic sulfide [As2S3] is a negatively charged hydrophobic sol and therefore migrates toward the anode.

Question 2 of 20

What is the term for the migration of positively charged colloidal particles toward the cathode?

Explanation: Cataphoresis specifically describes the movement of positive colloidal particles toward the cathode.

Question 3 of 20

The zeta potential is measured at which boundary layer region?

Explanation: Zeta potential is the potential drop across the diffuse part of the double layer, specifically at the plane of shear.

Question 4 of 20

How does an increase in solvent viscosity affect electrophoretic mobility?

Explanation: According to mobility equations like Helmholtz-Smoluchowski, μ is inversely proportional to viscosity (η).

Question 5 of 20

At its isoelectric point, a protein's electrophoretic mobility is:

Explanation: At the isoelectric point, the net charge on the protein is zero, resulting in zero electrophoretic mobility.

Question 6 of 20

Which equation relates electrophoretic mobility to zeta potential for large flat particles?

Explanation: The Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation is used for large particles where the double layer is thin compared to particle radius.

Question 7 of 20

Hydrated ferric oxide sol migrates toward which electrode in a U-tube experiment?

Explanation: Hydrated ferric oxide carries a positive charge due to preferential adsorption of Fe3+ ions and moves to the cathode.

Question 8 of 20

What primary purpose does a supporting gel matrix serve in zone electrophoresis?

Explanation: Porous gels stabilize the liquid medium against thermal convection and mechanical disturbance during separation.

Question 9 of 20

Which parameter has the most direct influence on the thickness of the electrical double layer?

Explanation: Debye length (double layer thickness) decreases as the ionic strength of the electrolyte solution increases.

Question 10 of 20

Which movement describes the flow of liquid relative to a stationary charged surface under an electric field?

Explanation: Electroosmosis involves the movement of the liquid phase past a stationary charged solid boundary.

Question 11 of 20

Starch, gold, and platinum sols are classified based on their charge origin as:

Explanation: Gold and platinum sols are classic lyophobic colloids that acquire charges via preferential ion adsorption.

Question 12 of 20

In capillary electrophoresis, electroosmotic flow is primarily generated by ionized silanol groups on:

Explanation: Ionization of silanol groups (Si-OH → Si-O- + H+) on fused-silica walls creates the zeta potential driving electroosmosis.

Question 13 of 20

If the applied voltage across an electrophoresis cell is doubled, the migration velocity of a particle will:

Explanation: Velocity v = μE, where E is electric field strength (voltage divided by distance). Doubling voltage doubles E and thus doubles v.

Question 14 of 20

Sulphide sols such as As2S3 acquire negative charge primarily through:

Explanation: Lyophobic sulphide sols preferentially adsorb sulfide ions from the dispersing medium, yielding a negative surface charge.

Question 15 of 20

The SI unit of electrophoretic mobility (μ) is:

Explanation: Mobility is velocity per unit electric field: (m·s-1) / (V·m-1) = m2·V-1·s-1.

Question 16 of 20

Which analytical technique couples electrophoresis with mass spectrometry?

Explanation: Capillary Electrophoresis-Mass Spectrometry (CE-MS) combines high-efficiency separation with structural identification.

Question 17 of 20

What happens to the rate of electrophoresis when electrolytes are added in excess to a colloidal sol?

Explanation: Excess electrolyte compresses the double layer, reducing zeta potential and often inducing coagulation (Schulze-Hardy rule).

Question 18 of 20

Amphoteric macromolecules such as proteins migrate in an electric field because their net charge depends on:

Explanation: Buffer pH dictates the protonation state of amino acid residues, directly determining the net charge of the protein.

Question 19 of 20

Which of the following is true regarding lyophilic sols in electrophoresis?

Explanation: Lyophilic sols like proteins and starch undergo electrophoresis, with mobility governed by amphoteric ionization.

Question 20 of 20

The direction of electrophoretic movement directly confirms:

Explanation: Migration toward the cathode proves a positive charge, while migration toward the anode proves a negative charge.

Question 1 of 10
Which factor primarily determines the direction of particle migration in electrophoresis?

Solution: The sign of the net charge dictates whether particles move toward the anode (+) or cathode (-).

Question 2 of 10
What is the primary effect of increasing buffer ionic strength on electrophoretic separation efficiency?

Solution: High ionic strength increases current, leading to Joule heating, thermal convection, and band broadening.

Question 3 of 10
The Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation assumes which of the following conditions?

Solution: The Helmholtz-Smoluchowski model applies when the Debye length is very small relative to particle dimensions.

Question 4 of 10
Which colloid carries a negative charge in aqueous solution?

Solution: Eosin is an anionic dye that forms negatively charged colloidal dispersions.

Question 5 of 10
In moving boundary electrophoresis, the velocity of the migrating boundary is determined by tracking:

Solution: Tiselius apparatus utilized Schlieren optical techniques to track refractive index gradients at boundaries.

Question 6 of 10
If a protein has a pI of 6.0, in a buffer at pH 8.0 it will migrate toward:

Solution: At pH 8.0 (above its pI), the protein is deprotonated and carries a net negative charge, moving toward the anode.

Question 7 of 10
What is electroosmosis?

Solution: Electroosmosis is bulk fluid movement past fixed surface charges when an electric field is applied.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following supports is commonly used in zonal electrophoresis of proteins?

Solution: Polyacrylamide gels provide a molecular sieve matrix for high-resolution protein electrophoresis.

Question 9 of 10
The phenomenon of electrophoresis was first systematically studied and demonstrated by:

Solution: F. F. Reuss first observed electroosmosis and electrophoresis using clay suspensions in water in 1809.

Question 10 of 10
In electrophoresis, if viscosity is doubled and voltage is halved, what is the net change in velocity?

Solution: v = μE ∝ (1/η) × (V). Doubling η halves velocity; halving V halves velocity again, giving a 4-fold reduction.
