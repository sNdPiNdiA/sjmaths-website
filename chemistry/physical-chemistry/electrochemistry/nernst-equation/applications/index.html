Physical Chemistry

Applications of Nernst Equation

The Nernst equation quantifies the dependence of electrode potential on the concentration of electroactive species and temperature. It provides the mathematical framework to calculate cell potentials under non-standard conditions and determine equilibrium constants.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Calculation of Electrode and Cell Potentials

  • The Nernst equation allows for the determination of Ecell when concentrations deviate from the standard 1 M.
  • For a half-cell reaction Mn+ + ne- → M(s), the potential is E = E° - (0.0591/n) log(1/[Mn+]).
  • The reaction quotient Q includes only aqueous species or gases, excluding pure solids and liquids.
  • A decrease in reactant concentration or increase in product concentration lowers the cell potential.

Determination of Equilibrium Constants

  • At chemical equilibrium, the net cell potential Ecell becomes zero.
  • The relationship log Kc = nE°cell / 0.0591 links electrochemical data to thermodynamic equilibrium constants.
  • Large positive values of E°cell correspond to very large values of Kc, indicating spontaneous reactions.
  • This method is highly precise for measuring Kc values that are otherwise difficult to determine via titration.

Potentiometric Titrations and pH Measurement

  • The Glass Electrode utilizes the Nernstian dependence of potential on the H+ ion concentration (pH).
  • In potentiometric titrations, the equivalence point is identified by the sharp change in potential (dE/dV).
  • The solubility product (Ksp) of sparingly soluble salts can be calculated using a concentration cell arrangement.
  • The potential of a hydrogen electrode is given by E = -0.0591 pH at 298 K.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Nernst Equation at 298K
Ecell = E°cell - (0.0591/n) log10 Q
Conditions: Standard temperature of 298 K.
Units: E in Volts, n is moles of electrons transferred.
Equilibrium Constant Relation
log Kc = (nE°cell) / 0.0591
Conditions: At equilibrium where Ecell = 0.
Units: Kc is dimensionless, E° in Volts.
Gibbs Free Energy Relation
ΔG° = -nFE°cell
Conditions: Standard state conditions.
Units: ΔG° in Joules, F = 96485 C/mol.
Reaction Quotient (Q)
Q = [Products]x / [Reactants]y
Conditions: Activities of pure solids/liquids are taken as unity.
Units: Concentrations in mol/L.
Nernst Equation (General)
Ecell = E°cell - (RT/nF) ln Q
Conditions: Applicable to any electrochemical cell at temperature T.
Units: E in Volts, R in J/mol·K, T in Kelvin, F in C/mol.
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Nernst equation assumes ideal behavior.

Observation: High concentration solutions. — Activity coefficients deviate significantly from unity in concentrated electrolytes.

Exception Solids have unit activity.

Observation: Finely divided or surface-active solids. — Surface energy effects can alter the chemical potential of the solid phase.

Exception Temperature dependence is linear.

Observation: Extreme temperature ranges. — Entropy and enthalpy changes of the reaction are temperature-dependent (Kirchhoff's Law).

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
The 0.0591 Rule
0.0591 / n

Application: Use 0.0591 for log10 at 25°C; use 0.0257 for ln at 25°C.

Sign Convention Mnemonic
E = E° - (RT/nF) ln(P/R)

Application: Remember 'P over R' (Products over Reactants) to avoid sign errors in the log term.

Equilibrium Shortcut
E° = 0.0591/n * log K

Application: Quickly estimate K by multiplying E° by n and dividing by 0.06 for mental math.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • E°cell is an intensive property.
  • ΔG is an extensive property.
  • At equilibrium, Q = Kc.
  • F = 96485 Coulombs per mole of electrons.
  • The Nernst equation applies to both half-cells and full cells.
  • Concentration cells have E° = 0.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting to divide by n (number of electrons) in the log term.
  • Using natural log (ln) instead of log10 when using the 0.0591 constant.
  • Including pure solids or liquids in the expression for Q.
  • Incorrectly identifying the anode and cathode, leading to an inverted Q value.

⚡ Quick Revision: Applications of Nernst Equation

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrochemical Potential

The potential of a cell is determined by the standard potential and the reaction quotient. E = E° - (RT/nF)lnQ.

Thermodynamic Linkage

Gibbs free energy change is related to cell potential by ΔG = -nFE. Spontaneity requires ΔG < 0.

Equilibrium State

At equilibrium, E = 0 and ΔG = 0. The relation log K = nE°/0.0591 holds.

Concentration Cells

Cells with identical electrodes but different electrolyte concentrations. E = -(0.0591/n)log([Mn+]anode/[Mn+]cathode).

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

E° (Standard Potential)
Potential at 1M concentration and 298K (Volts).
Q (Reaction Quotient)
Ratio of product to reactant activities.
n (Stoichiometric Electrons)
Number of moles of electrons transferred.
F (Faraday Constant)
Charge of one mole of electrons (96485 C/mol).
Kc (Equilibrium Constant)
Ratio of concentrations at equilibrium.
R (Gas Constant)
8.314 J/mol·K.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • E = E° - 0.0591/n log Q
  • ΔG° = -nFE°
  • log K = nE°/0.0591
  • E° = 0 for concentration cells
  • Q = [Products]/[Reactants]

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nernst equation links concentration to potential.
  • Equilibrium constant is derived from E°.
  • Gibbs energy determines spontaneity.
  • Concentration cells depend only on concentration ratios.

⚖️ Key Differences

E° VS E

E° is a constant for a reaction; E varies with concentration.
