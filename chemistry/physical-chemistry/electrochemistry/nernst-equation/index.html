Physical Chemistry

Nernst Equation

The Nernst equation quantitatively relates the reduction potential of an electrochemical reaction to the standard electrode potential and the activities of chemical species. It is derived from the variation of Gibbs free energy with reaction quotient under non-standard conditions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Derivation and Thermodynamic Foundation

  • The variation of Gibbs free energy is expressed as ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q.
  • Substituting ΔG = -nFE and ΔG° = -nFE° yields the foundational Nernst relation.
  • At equilibrium, ΔG = 0 and Q = Keq, leading directly to the standard potential-equilibrium relation.

Application to Half-Cells and Full Cells

  • For a reduction half-reaction Mn+ + n e- → M(s), the potential depends inversely on the logarithm of metal ion concentration.
  • Solids and pure liquids are assigned an activity of unity and are omitted from the reaction quotient expression Q.
  • The overall cell potential Ecell is calculated as Ecathode - Eanode using respective Nernst expressions.

Effect of Concentration, pH, and Temperature

  • Increasing reactant concentration or decreasing product concentration shifts the equilibrium forward and increases cell potential.
  • When hydrogen ions (H+) or hydroxide ions (OH-) participate in redox steps, the potential becomes strictly dependent on pH.
  • The temperature coefficient (∂E/∂T)p is directly related to the standard reaction entropy change ΔS°.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Nernst Equation at 298 K (Base-10)
E = E° - (0.0591 / n) log Q
Conditions: Temperature T = 298 K
Units: E in Volts, n is moles of electrons, Q is reaction quotient
Relationship with Equilibrium Constant
E°cell = (0.0591 / n) log Keq
Conditions: At chemical equilibrium where Ecell = 0 at 298 K
Units: E° in Volts, Keq is dimensionless
Gibbs Free Energy and Cell Potential
ΔG = -nFEcell
Conditions: Constant temperature and pressure
Units: ΔG in Joules, F in C mol-1, E in Volts
Concentration Cell Potential
Ecell = (0.0591 / n) log (C2 / C1)
Conditions: Same electrode materials, differing ionic concentrations C1 and C2
Units: Concentrations in mol L-1, E in Volts
General Nernst Equation
E = E° - (RT / nF) ln Q
Conditions: Any temperature, non-standard concentrations
Units: E and E° in Volts, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin, F in C mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Activity of pure solids and liquids is taken as unity in the Nernst equation.

Observation: High pressure or extremely concentrated solutions deviate from unit activity. — Inter-ionic or intermolecular forces cause significant departures from ideal dilute solution behavior.

Exception Standard reduction potentials are strictly defined at 1 M concentration and 1 bar pressure.

Observation: Gases behave non-ideally at high pressures. — Fugacity coefficients must replace partial pressures in precise thermodynamic treatments.

Anode on top, Cathode on bottom for concentration cells

log Keq = n E° / 0.0591

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The standard cell potential E°cell is an intensive property and does not depend on stoichiometric coefficients.
  • When Q < 1, the cell potential E is greater than the standard cell potential E°.
  • At chemical equilibrium, the net cell potential Ecell drops exactly to zero.
  • The Faraday constant F represents the total charge carried by one mole of electrons, approximately 96500 C mol-1.
  • Concentration cells generate electricity solely due to a difference in concentration between identical half-cells.
  • The temperature dependence of cell potential yields the standard reaction enthalpy via the Gibbs-Helmholtz equation.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Including pure solids like Cu(s) or Zn(s) in the reaction quotient Q expression.
  • Failing to multiply individual half-cell potentials by stoichiometric coefficients when calculating overall Gibbs free energy.
  • Confusing the anode and cathode positions when writing the logarithmic term in the Nernst equation.
  • Using natural logarithm (ln) constants instead of base-10 logarithm (log) when utilizing the 0.0591 factor.

⚡ Quick Revision: Nernst Equation

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Thermodynamic Basis

The Nernst equation stems from the isothermal expansion work equivalent to free energy changes in redox processes.

Reaction Quotient Impact

Deviations of ionic concentrations from standard state values alter cell electromotive force predictably via Q.

Equilibrium Relations

Equilibrium constants are directly computable from standard cell potentials through logarithmic proportionality.

Concentration Cells

Spontaneous electron transfer occurs exclusively via concentration gradients when standard potentials cancel out.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Cell Potential (Ecell)
Electromotive force driving electrons through an external circuit, measured in Volts.
Standard Potential (E°)
Potential under standard state conditions of 1 M concentration and 1 bar pressure.
Reaction Quotient (Q)
Ratio of product activities to reactant activities raised to their stoichiometric coefficients.
Faraday Constant (F)
Magnitude of charge per mole of electrons, approximately 96485 C mol-1.
Equilibrium Constant (Keq)
Ratio of concentrations of products to reactants at chemical equilibrium.
Gibbs Free Energy (ΔG)
Thermodynamic potential measuring maximum reversible work obtainable, in Joules.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • E = E° - (0.0591 / n) log Q at 298 K
  • ΔG° = -nFE°cell
  • Log Keq = n E° / 0.0591
  • Solids and pure liquids have activity = 1
  • At equilibrium, Ecell = 0

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nernst equation connects non-standard potentials to standard values and composition.
  • Temperature and concentration variations alter electrochemical cell output.
  • Equilibrium constant and standard potential are mutually interconvertible.
  • Concentration cells rely purely on activity gradients.

⚖️ Key Differences

Intensive property (E°) VS Extensive property (ΔG°)

E° does not change when multiplying reactions, whereas ΔG° scales directly with stoichiometry.

Which of the following species is omitted from the reaction quotient Q expression in electrochemical cells?

What is the relationship between standard free energy change and standard cell potential?

In a concentration cell, what drives the flow of current?

Question 8 of 18

If E°cell is positive, what can be inferred about the equilibrium constant Keq?

Explanation: A positive E°cell implies negative ΔG°, corresponding to Keq > 1.

Question 9 of 18

What is the unit of the Faraday constant F?

Explanation: The Faraday constant represents electrical charge per mole of electrons, with units C mol-1.

Question 10 of 18

Which equation correctly represents the Nernst equation for a general half-reaction Mn+ + n e- → M(s)?

Explanation: The reduction potential follows E = E° - (RT/nF) ln(1/[Mn+]) which is equivalent to E = E° + (RT/nF) ln[Mn+].

Question 11 of 18

If the temperature increases, how does the Nernst term (RT/nF) behave?

Explanation: Since temperature T is in the numerator, an increase in T increases the magnitude of the RT/nF prefactor.

Question 12 of 18

What is the value of Keq for a cell where E°cell = 0 V at 298 K?

Explanation: When E°cell = 0, log Keq = 0, meaning Keq = 1.

Question 13 of 18

Which factor makes the standard reduction potential an intensive property?

Explanation: Intensive properties do not depend on the quantity of matter present in the system.

Question 14 of 18

In the Nernst equation, what does Q represent?

Explanation: Q stands for the reaction quotient, defined as the ratio of product activities to reactant activities.

Question 15 of 18

What happens to the cell potential if the concentration of products is increased?

Explanation: Increasing product concentration increases Q, subtracting a larger term and decreasing Ecell.

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following is true for a spontaneous electrochemical process?

Explanation: A spontaneous electrochemical cell has a positive cell potential and negative Gibbs free energy change.

Question 17 of 18

What is the electron stoichiometry n for the oxidation of solid zinc to zinc ions?

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the reduction potential of a half-cell containing 0.1 M Fe3+ and 0.01 M Fe2+. Given E°(Fe3+/Fe2+) = +0.77 V.

Solution: E = 0.77 - (0.0591 / 1) log(0.01 / 0.1) = 0.77 - 0.0591(-1) = 0.77 + 0.0591 = +0.829 V.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following conditions yields a cell potential greater than the standard cell potential E°cell?

Solution: When Q < 1, log Q is negative, making the subtraction term positive and yielding E > E°.

Question 3 of 10
For the redox reaction involving 3 electrons with E°cell = 0.295 V at 298 K, what is log Keq?

Solution: log Keq = (n E°) / 0.0591 = (3 * 0.295) / 0.0591 = 0.885 / 0.0591 = 15.

Question 4 of 10
What is the effect of doubling the stoichiometric coefficients of a half-reaction on its standard reduction potential E°?

Solution: E° is an intensive property and does not change when the stoichiometric coefficients are multiplied by a factor.

Question 5 of 10
In the Nernst equation, temperature T is always expressed in which scale?

Solution: Thermodynamic equations require absolute temperature measured in Kelvin (K).

Question 6 of 10
What is the EMF of a concentration cell where both compartments have equal ionic concentrations?

Solution: When concentrations are equal, Q = 1, log(1) = 0, and the cell potential Ecell = 0.

Question 7 of 10
If ΔG° for a reaction is positive, the value of E°cell is:

Solution: Since ΔG° = -nFE°, a positive ΔG° requires a negative E°cell.

Question 8 of 10
What is the potential of a standard hydrogen electrode (SHE) under standard conditions?

Solution: By international convention, the standard hydrogen electrode potential is defined as exactly 0.00 V at all temperatures.

Question 9 of 10
Which variable in the Nernst equation accounts for non-unit activities of reactants and products?

Solution: The reaction quotient Q accounts for the actual activities of all participating species.

Question 10 of 10
What happens to the cell potential if the pressure of a reactant gas is increased in a half-cell?

Solution: Increasing reactant gas pressure increases its activity in Q, shifting equilibrium forward and increasing potential.
