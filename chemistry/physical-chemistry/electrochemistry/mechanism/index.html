Physical Chemistry

Electrode Reaction Mechanisms

Electrode reaction mechanisms involve the sequence of elementary steps including mass transport, electron transfer, and chemical reactions at the electrode-electrolyte interface. The overall rate is governed by the slowest step, typically either mass transfer or the heterogeneous electron transfer process.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mass Transport Processes

  • Diffusion occurs due to concentration gradients according to Fick's laws.
  • Migration involves the movement of charged species in an electric field.
  • Convection is forced or natural fluid motion affecting the diffusion layer thickness.
  • The Nernst Diffusion Layer model assumes a stagnant layer of thickness δ where only diffusion occurs.

Electron Transfer Kinetics

  • Outer-sphere electron transfer involves no bond breaking or formation.
  • Inner-sphere electron transfer requires a bridging ligand between the electrode and the reactant.
  • The Symmetry Factor (α) represents the fraction of electrical energy used to lower the activation barrier.
  • Overpotential (η) is the difference between the applied potential and the equilibrium potential.

Coupled Chemical Reactions

  • EC Mechanism involves an electron transfer (E) followed by a chemical reaction (C).
  • CE Mechanism involves a chemical reaction (C) preceding the electron transfer (E).
  • ECE Mechanism describes an electron transfer followed by a chemical reaction and another electron transfer.
  • Reaction mechanisms are determined using Cyclic Voltammetry peak current ratios.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Tafel Equation
η = a + b log(j)
Conditions: High overpotential, irreversible electron transfer
Units: η in Volts, j in A/m2
Nernst-Planck Equation
Ji = -Di ∇Ci - (ziF/RT)DiCi ∇φ
Conditions: Mass transport via diffusion and migration
Units: Ji in mol/m2·s, Di in m2/s
Cottrell Equation
i(t) = nFAC√(D/πt)
Conditions: Chronoamperometry, planar electrode, diffusion-controlled
Units: i in Amperes, t in seconds
Exchange Current Density
j0 = nFk0C
Conditions: Equilibrium potential, reversible system
Units: j0 in A/m2, k0 in m/s
Butler-Volmer Equation
j = j0 [exp(αnFη/RT) - exp(-(1-α)nFη/RT)]
Conditions: Single-step electron transfer, activation-controlled
Units: j in A/m2, η in Volts, R in J/mol·K
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Butler-Volmer kinetics

Observation: Very high exchange current density — System behaves as electrochemically reversible with negligible overpotential.

Exception Diffusion-limited current

Observation: Microelectrodes — Spherical diffusion dominates over linear diffusion, leading to steady-state currents.

Exception Tafel slope of 59 mV/decade

Observation: Multi-step mechanisms — Rate-determining step involves different electron transfer coefficients.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Tafel Slope Rule
b = 2.303RT/αnF

Application: Use to determine the transfer coefficient α from the slope of η vs log(j) plots.

Reversibility Check
ΔEp = 59/n mV

Application: If peak separation in CV is ~59/n mV, the process is Nernstian (reversible).

Diffusion Layer Estimation
δ ≈ √(Dt)

Application: Estimate the thickness of the diffusion layer at time t for a given diffusion coefficient D.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The exchange current density j0 is directly proportional to the standard rate constant k0.
  • Tafel plots are linear only at high overpotentials where one direction of the reaction dominates.
  • The symmetry factor α typically ranges between 0.3 and 0.7 for most electrode reactions.
  • In a CE mechanism, the chemical step is the rate-limiting step at high scan rates.
  • Migration is suppressed in electrochemical cells by adding a large excess of supporting electrolyte.
  • The limiting current iL is independent of potential in the mass-transfer controlled region.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the symmetry factor α with the transfer coefficient.
  • Assuming all electrode reactions follow Butler-Volmer kinetics regardless of potential.
  • Neglecting the effect of supporting electrolyte on migration currents.
  • Incorrectly applying the Cottrell equation to non-planar or non-diffusion-controlled systems.

⚡ Quick Revision: Electrode Reaction Mechanisms

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Mass Transport

Mass transport is defined by the Nernst-Planck equation, combining diffusion, migration, and convection. It sets the upper limit for the current in electrochemical cells.

Activation Control

Activation control occurs when electron transfer is the slowest step, described by the Butler-Volmer equation. It dominates at low overpotentials.

Coupled Reactions

Chemical reactions coupled to electron transfer (EC, CE, ECE) alter the electrochemical response. They are analyzed via diagnostic criteria in voltammetry.

Diagnostic Voltammetry

Cyclic voltammetry peak potentials and currents provide evidence for mechanism. Peak separation and scan rate dependence are key indicators.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Overpotential (η)
Excess potential required to drive a reaction.
Exchange Current (j0)
Current at equilibrium potential.
Transfer Coefficient (α)
Fraction of potential affecting activation energy.
Diffusion Coefficient (D)
Rate of mass transport by diffusion.
Supporting Electrolyte (None)
Salt added to eliminate migration.
Tafel Slope (b)
Sensitivity of overpotential to current density.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Electrode reactions are multi-step processes.
  • Mass transport limits current at high potentials.
  • Electron transfer kinetics are potential-dependent.
  • Coupled reactions change the electrochemical signature.

⚖️ Key Differences

Diffusion VS Migration

Diffusion is concentration-driven; migration is field-driven.
