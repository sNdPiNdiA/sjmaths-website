Physical Chemistry

Dry Cell

A dry cell is a primary electrochemical cell that utilizes a moist electrolyte paste to facilitate ion transport between electrodes. It functions as a Leclanché cell, converting chemical energy into electrical energy through irreversible redox reactions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrode Composition and Structure

  • The anode consists of a zinc container that undergoes oxidation.
  • The cathode is a central graphite rod surrounded by a mixture of powdered MnO2 and carbon.
  • The electrolyte is a moist paste of NH4Cl and ZnCl2.

Redox Mechanism and Byproducts

  • Ammonia gas produced at the cathode reacts with Zn2+ to form the complex ion [Zn(NH3)2Cl2].
  • The presence of ZnCl2 prevents the cell from drying out too quickly and manages ammonia accumulation.
  • The cell is irreversible because the chemical products cannot be easily converted back to reactants.

Operational Limitations

  • The cell potential drops over time due to the accumulation of reaction products.
  • The acidic nature of NH4Cl causes the zinc container to corrode even when the cell is not in use.
  • High current drain leads to polarization, reducing the effective voltage output.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Standard galvanic cells are reversible

Observation: Dry cell (Leclanché) — The chemical products (NH3, Mn2O3) diffuse or react in ways that prevent reversal.

Exception Electrolytes are typically aqueous solutions

Observation: Dry cell electrolyte — Uses a moist paste to prevent leakage and allow portability.

Exception Constant voltage output

Observation: Dry cell voltage — Voltage declines steadily as the electrolyte is consumed and internal resistance increases.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The dry cell is a primary battery.
  • The zinc container acts as the anode.
  • The graphite rod acts as the cathode.
  • MnO2 acts as the depolarizer.
  • The initial voltage is approximately 1.5 V.
  • The cell reaction is irreversible.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the anode and cathode polarity; remember the zinc shell is the negative terminal.
  • Assuming the cell is rechargeable; it is a primary cell.
  • Neglecting the role of NH4Cl in the electrolyte paste.
  • Misidentifying the depolarizer as the cathode itself.

⚡ Quick Revision: Dry Cell

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Primary Cell Definition

A primary cell is a non-rechargeable electrochemical device where the chemical reaction proceeds in one direction until reactants are exhausted.

Role of Depolarizer

MnO2 prevents the accumulation of hydrogen gas at the cathode, which would otherwise cause polarization and increase internal resistance.

Electrolyte Function

The NH4Cl paste provides the ions necessary for charge balance and completes the internal circuit.

Corrosion Factor

The acidic NH4Cl electrolyte causes the zinc casing to corrode, limiting the shelf life of the cell.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Anode (Zn)
Negative electrode where oxidation occurs.
Cathode (Graphite)
Positive electrode where reduction occurs.
Depolarizer (MnO2)
Substance that prevents polarization.
Electrolyte (NH4Cl)
Paste providing ionic conductivity.
Primary Cell (Non-rechargeable)
Cell that cannot be reversed.
Polarization (Voltage drop)
Decrease in potential due to gas accumulation.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Anode: Zn
  • Cathode: Graphite rod
  • Depolarizer: MnO2
  • Voltage: 1.5 V
  • Electrolyte: NH4Cl paste

📌 Quick Recall

  • Dry cell is a Leclanché cell.
  • It is a primary cell.
  • Voltage is not constant.
  • Zinc casing corrodes over time.

⚖️ Key Differences

Primary Cell VS Secondary Cell

Primary cells are non-rechargeable; secondary cells are rechargeable.
