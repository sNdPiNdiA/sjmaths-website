Electro-osmosis

Electro-osmosis is the phenomenon of fluid migration through a porous diaphragm or capillary tube under the influence of an applied external electric field. It arises from the presence of an electrical double layer at the solid-liquid interface.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Origin and Electrical Double Layer

  • The electrical double layer (EDL) forms at the interface between a charged solid surface and a liquid electrolyte.
  • The fixed layer of ions is held firmly on the solid surface, while the diffuse layer of mobile counter-ions extends into the liquid phase.
  • The electric potential at the plane of shear between the fixed and mobile layers is defined as the zeta potential (ζ).

Mechanism of Electro-osmotic Flow

  • When a potential gradient is applied across a porous plug, the mobile ions in the diffuse layer migrate toward the oppositely charged electrode.
  • As these hydrated counter-ions move, they drag the surrounding bulk liquid along through viscous coupling.
  • If the liquid flow is blocked, it generates a measurable hydrostatic pressure known as electro-osmotic pressure.

Factors Affecting Electro-osmosis

  • The rate of electro-osmotic flow is directly proportional to the applied electric field strength and the zeta potential of the interface.
  • Increasing the concentration of simple electrolyte solutions compresses the diffuse double layer and reduces electro-osmotic velocity.
  • The solvent viscosity (η) is inversely proportional to the flow velocity; higher viscosity restricts fluid motion.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Electro-osmotic Volume Flow Rate
V = (ε ζ A I) / (4 π η κ)
Conditions: In terms of current I and cross-sectional area A
Units: V in m3 s-1, A in m2, I in A, κ in S m-1
Streaming Potential Relation
Es / P = (ε ζ) / (4 π η κ)
Conditions: Direct reciprocal phenomenon to electro-osmosis
Units: Es in V, P in Pa
Zeta Potential Expression
ζ = (4 π η u) / ε
Conditions: Where u is the electrophoretic mobility
Units: ζ in V, u in m2 V-1 s-1
Helmholtz-Smoluchowski Velocity Equation
v = (ε ζ E) / η
Conditions: For fluid flow in a capillary or porous medium under electric field E
Units: v in m s-1, ε in F m-1, ζ in V, E in V m-1, η in Pa s
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher electrolyte concentration increases solution conductivity and should increase current.

Observation: Electro-osmotic flow rate decreases at high electrolyte concentrations. — High ionic strength compresses the electrical double layer (smaller Debye length), reducing the net charge in the mobile diffuse layer.

Exception Direction of liquid flow is always toward the cathode.

Observation: Liquid can flow toward the anode in systems with positively charged solid surfaces. — When the surface acquires a positive charge (e.g., basic metal oxides in acidic media), the diffuse layer contains excess anions, reversing the net directional migration.

Exception Electro-osmotic velocity is independent of capillary radius for wide channels.

Observation: Flow deviates significantly in nanofluidic channels where double layers overlap. — When the channel radius approaches the Debye length, electroneutrality fails across the entire channel cross-section.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Reciprocal Pair Mnemonic
Electro-osmosis ↔ Streaming Potential | Electrophoresis ↔ Sedimentation Potential

Application: Remember that electro-kinetic phenomena come in reciprocal pairs: one applies an electrical field to cause mechanical movement, and the reverse applies mechanical force to generate an electrical potential.

Proportionality Quick Check
v ∝ ζ × E / η

Application: Use this direct proportionality for quick elimination in numerical MCQs regarding velocity changes when solvent or field is altered.

Sign of Zeta Potential
Positive electrode accumulation = Negative Zeta Potential

Application: Liquid moving toward the cathode implies a negative charge on the capillary wall and a positive diffuse layer.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Electro-osmosis is the movement of liquid through a porous membrane under an electric field.
  • The Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation relates velocity to zeta potential, dielectric constant, and viscosity.
  • The slipping plane potential is known as the zeta potential.
  • Addition of multivalent coagulating ions drastically reduces electro-osmotic flow.
  • Electro-osmosis is a type of electrokinetic phenomenon associated with colloids and interfaces.
  • The reciprocal phenomenon of electro-osmosis is streaming potential.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing electro-osmosis (liquid moves, solid stationary) with electrophoresis (solid particles move, liquid stationary).
  • Assuming zeta potential is equal to the Nernst (thermodynamic) potential.
  • Neglecting the effect of electrolyte concentration on double-layer thickness.
  • Using incorrect units for permittivity (ε) and viscosity (η) in numerical calculations.

⚡ Quick Revision: Electro-osmosis

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrical Double Layer

Consists of a fixed Stern layer and a mobile diffuse Gouy-Chapman layer at the phase boundary.

Zeta Potential

The potential drop across the diffuse part of the double layer, governing electrokinetic behavior.

Electro-osmotic Velocity

Determined by the Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation, directly proportional to applied field and zeta potential.

Reciprocal Effects

Coupled electrokinetic phenomena linking mechanical and electrical transport coefficients via Onsager reciprocal relations.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Zeta Potential (ζ)
Electrokinetic potential at the shear plane of the double layer (Volts).
Electro-osmosis (v = εζE/η)
Fluid motion driven by an electric field through a stationary porous medium.
Streaming Potential (Es)
Potential generated by forced liquid flow through a porous plug.
Electrophoresis (u)
Migration of charged colloidal particles under an electric field.
Debye Length (κ-1)
Measure of the thickness of the diffuse electrical double layer.
Permittivity (ε)
Ability of a substance to store electrical energy in an electric field (F m-1).

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation for electro-osmotic velocity.
  • Electro-osmosis and streaming potential are reciprocal electrokinetic phenomena.
  • Zeta potential depends on the composition of the liquid medium and wall surface groups.
  • Increased ionic strength suppresses electro-osmotic flow due to double-layer compression.
  • Liquid moves toward the cathode if the capillary wall is negatively charged.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Electro-osmosis involves liquid flow through stationary capillaries via double layer sliding.
  • Governed by electrostatic force balanced against viscous drag.
  • Directly proportional to the zeta potential and applied electric field.
  • Crucial mechanism in capillary electrophoresis and microfluidic pumping.

⚖️ Key Differences

Electro-osmosis VS Electrophoresis

In electro-osmosis, the solid phase is fixed and liquid moves; in electrophoresis, liquid is stationary and solid particles move.

What is the driving force in electro-osmosis?

Explanation: Electro-osmosis is the motion of liquid induced by an applied external electric field across a porous plug or capillary.

Question 2 of 18

Which potential is responsible for electro-osmotic flow?

Explanation: The zeta potential (ζ) at the shear plane of the electrical double layer dictates the electro-osmotic velocity.

Question 3 of 18

According to the Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation, electro-osmotic velocity is independent of which of the following?

Explanation: For wide capillaries where the double layer is thin compared to the radius, velocity is independent of capillary radius.

Question 4 of 18

What is the phenomenon reciprocal to electro-osmosis?

Explanation: Streaming potential is the generation of a potential difference when a liquid is forced through a porous plug under pressure.

Question 5 of 18

If the inner wall of a glass capillary is negatively charged, in which direction does the liquid move during electro-osmosis?

Explanation: A negatively charged wall creates a positive diffuse layer which migrates toward the negative electrode (cathode).

Question 6 of 18

How does an increase in electrolyte concentration affect electro-osmotic flow?

Explanation: Higher ionic strength compresses the double layer, reducing the net mobile charge and lowering the flow rate.

Question 7 of 18

What is the unit of zeta potential (ζ)?

Explanation: Zeta potential is an electrical potential difference measured in volts (or millivolts).

Question 8 of 18

Which parameter appears in the denominator of the Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation?

Explanation: The velocity formula is v = (εζE)/η, making viscosity inversely proportional to flow velocity.

Question 9 of 18

What happens to electro-osmotic pressure if fluid flow is completely blocked?

Explanation: Blocking the flow generates a counterbalancing hydrostatic pressure known as the electro-osmotic pressure.

Question 10 of 18

Which component constitutes the mobile part of the electrical double layer?

Explanation: The diffuse layer contains mobile counter-ions that move under an electric field, dragging solvent molecules with them.

Question 11 of 18

In capillary electrophoresis, electro-osmotic flow (EOF) is often utilized. What is a key characteristic of EOF profile?

Explanation: Electro-osmosis generates a plug-like (flat) velocity profile because the driving force acts uniformly across the diffuse layer.

Question 12 of 18

Which factor directly increases the zeta potential of a silica surface in contact with water?

Explanation: Higher pH causes increased ionization of surface silanol groups (Si-OH → Si-O-), increasing surface charge and zeta potential.

Question 13 of 18

What is sedimentation potential also known as?

Explanation: Sedimentation potential, generated by settling charged particles, is known as the Dorn effect.

Question 14 of 18

Which electrokinetic phenomenon involves the movement of dispersed solid particles through a liquid under an electric field?

Explanation: Electrophoresis is defined as the migration of charged colloidal particles under the influence of an electric field.

Question 15 of 18

If the dielectric constant of the liquid medium increases, what happens to the electro-osmotic velocity?

Explanation: Velocity is directly proportional to the dielectric constant (ε) in the Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation.

Question 16 of 18

What term describes the potential difference set up when a liquid is forced through a capillary?

Explanation: Streaming potential is created by mechanical pressure driving liquid through a capillary or porous plug.

Question 17 of 18

Which equation correctly represents the Helmholtz-Smoluchowski electro-osmotic velocity?

Explanation: The standard derived equation is v = (εζE)/η.

Question 18 of 18

Why does multivalent electrolyte addition suppress electro-osmosis more effectively than univalent electrolytes?

Explanation: According to the Schulze-Hardy rule, multivalent ions compress the electrical double layer much more effectively.
