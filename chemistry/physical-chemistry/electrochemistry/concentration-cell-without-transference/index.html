Concentration Cell Without Transference

A concentration cell without transference consists of two identical electrodes dipping into solutions of the same electrolyte at different concentrations, but without any liquid junction boundary between them. The electromotive force (EMF) is generated solely due to the difference in chemical potential of the electrolyte or gas across the two half-cells.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Foundation and Absence of Liquid Junction

  • These cells eliminate the liquid junction potential by utilizing two distinct chemical cells connected in series, effectively combining them into a single circuit.
  • The overall cell reaction involves the transfer of electrolyte from a region of higher activity to a region of lower activity.
  • The driving force of the cell is the decrease in Gibbs free energy associated with the dilution of the more concentrated electrolyte.
  • Because there is no direct physical contact between the two differing electrolyte solutions, transport numbers do not appear in the EMF equation.

Cell Construction Using Chemical Cells in Series

  • Constructed by coupling two chemical cells without transference, such as combining a silver-silver chloride electrode in NaCl(a1) with another in NaCl(a2).
  • For a cell configuration like Pt | H2(g) | HCl(a1) | AgCl(s) | Ag(s) - Ag(s) | AgCl(s) | HCl(a2) | H2(g) | Pt, the net reaction is HCl(a2) → HCl(a1).
  • The EMF expression contains terms involving mean ionic activities (a±) rather than individual ionic activities which cannot be measured independently.
  • At extreme dilutions, activities can be approximated by molalities or concentrations multiplied by the mean activity coefficient.

Comparison with Concentration Cells With Transference

  • Cells with transference contain a liquid junction boundary across which ions migrate at different speeds, introducing a liquid junction potential.
  • Cells without transference evaluate pure chemical potential differences without any contribution from individual ionic transport numbers (t+ or t-).
  • Measurement of EMF without transference combined with transference data allows experimental determination of ionic transport numbers.
  • These cells yield thermodynamically rigorous values for activity coefficients because no unmeasurable single-ion properties are isolated.

Conditions: Isothermal and isobaric reversible electrochemical processes

Conditions: Cell without transference involving two electrolyte concentrations with amalgam or gas electrodes
Units: E in Volts, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin, F in C mol-1, a± is mean ionic activity
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Activity equals concentration in very dilute solutions

Observation: Deviations occur even at low concentrations for unsymmetrical electrolytes (like CaCl2 or LaCl3) — Interionic electrostatic forces described by the Debye-Hückel theory are much stronger for multivalent ions due to higher ionic charge products.

Exception EMF depends solely on activity ratio for ideal systems

Observation: Activity coefficients vary non-linearly with ionic strength in concentrated solutions — Solvation shell effects and ion-pair formation alter the effective number of free ions contributing to the chemical potential.

Exception Transport numbers do not appear in EMF equations of concentration cells without transference

Observation: If calculated by subtracting cells with and from transference, transport numbers reappear as difference parameters — Mathematical coupling of cells with and without transference isolates the liquid junction contribution containing ion transport velocities.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Activity Ratio Rule for EMF Sign
E = -(RT/nF) ln(aright / aleft)

Application: Always ensure the logarithm term has the higher activity in the denominator or adjust the negative sign based on oxidation-reduction placement to maintain positive spontaneous EMF (ΔG < 0).

Valency Factor Quick Check
Multiply RT/F by ν (stoichiometric coefficient sum)

Application: For binary electrolytes like CaCl2 (ν = 3), include the total number of ions per formula unit in the pre-logarithmic multiplier.

Gas Pressure Mnemonic
Phigh to Plow

Application: In gas concentration cells, the gas always tends to escape from the higher pressure electrode compartment to the lower pressure compartment, dictating the spontaneous electron flow.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Concentration cells without transference do not contain a liquid junction boundary.
  • The EMF expression for an electrolyte concentration cell without transference involves mean ionic activities: E = -(RT/F) ln(a±,2 / a±,1).
  • Transport numbers of ions do not appear in the EMF equation of cells without transference.
  • These cells are thermodynamically reversible and allow accurate determination of activity coefficients.
  • The net cell reaction corresponds to the transfer of electrolyte from a solution of higher activity to one of lower activity.
  • Combination of cells with and without transference enables the calculation of ionic transport numbers.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing concentration cells with transference and without transference regarding the inclusion of transport numbers in the Nernst equation.
  • Omitting the stoichiometric number of ions (ν) when calculating the EMF of multivalent electrolytes.
  • Using concentrations directly instead of mean ionic activities in non-ideal, concentrated electrolyte solutions.
  • Failing to account for the sign convention of EMF, leading to negative voltage values for spontaneous electrochemical cells.

⚡ Quick Revision: Concentration Cell Without Transference

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Thermodynamic Basis

Concentration cells without transference operate via the spontaneous transfer of solute from high to low chemical potential without liquid junction mixing.

Absence of Liquid Junction

By pairing two chemical cells in series, physical contact between differing electrolyte solutions is prevented, eliminating junction potentials.

Mean Ionic Activity

EMF equations rely strictly on mean ionic activities (a±) to account for non-ideal ionic interactions in solution.

Analytical Utility

These cells provide precise experimental data for determining activity coefficients and evaluating standard thermodynamic properties.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Concentration Cell (Electrochemical Cell)
Cell whose EMF arises solely from chemical potential differences.
Transference (Ion Migration)
Movement of ions across a liquid boundary.
Activity (a)
Effective concentration in thermodynamics.
Mean Ionic Activity (a±)
Geometric mean of cation and anion activities.
Junction Potential (Ej)
Potential difference at the boundary of two solutions.
Transport Number (ti)
Fraction of total current carried by an ion.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • No liquid junction potential exists in concentration cells without transference.
  • EMF equation: E = -(RT/F) ln(a2 / a1).
  • Transport numbers do not appear in the EMF equations of these cells.
  • Net cell reaction is the transfer of electrolyte from high to low activity.
  • Activity coefficients can be precisely determined using these cells.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Eliminates liquid junction boundaries through series-coupled chemical cells.
  • Relies on mean ionic activities for accurate thermodynamic treatment.
  • Provides rigorous values free from single-ion ambiguity.
  • Serves as a vital tool for determining electrolyte activity coefficients.

⚖️ Key Differences

Cell with transference VS Cell without transference

Cells with transference include a liquid junction potential and transport numbers; cells without transference eliminate both.

Explanation: Electroneutrality principle dictates that cations and anions cannot be separated in macroscopic quantities, preventing measurement of single-ion activities.

Question 2 of 10
Calculate the EMF of a concentration cell without transference for a univalent electrolyte where a2/a1 = 100 at 298 K.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following is NOT a feature of a concentration cell without transference?

Solution: Transport numbers are absent from the EMF calculation of cells without transference.

Question 4 of 10
In amalgam concentration cells, if a1 > a2, electrons flow from:

Solution: Oxidation occurs at the amalgam with higher activity (higher chemical potential), releasing electrons toward the lower activity amalgam.

Question 5 of 10
What is the effect of dilution on the mean activity coefficient (γ±) in very dilute solutions according to Debye-Hückel theory?

Solution: As concentration approaches zero (infinite dilution), activity coefficients approach 1.

Question 6 of 10
The auxiliary electrodes used in constructing electrolyte concentration cells without transference must be:

Solution: Auxiliary electrodes like Ag/AgCl are reversible to one of the electrolyte ions (chloride).

Question 7 of 10
How are transport numbers evaluated experimentally using concentration cells?

Solution: Subtracting the EMF of a cell without transference from that of a cell with transference isolates the liquid junction potential containing transport numbers.

Question 8 of 10
For a binary electrolyte (1:1 type), the mean ionic activity a± equals:

Solution: For x=1, y=1, a± = (a_+1 a_-1)1/2 = (a_+ a_-)1/2.

Question 9 of 10
What is the stoichiometric coefficient sum ν for MgCl2 used in activity calculations?

Solution: MgCl2 dissociates into 1 Mg2+ and 2 Cl- ions, giving ν = 1 + 2 = 3.

Question 10 of 10
Under what condition does the Nernst equation for concentration cells reduce to concentration ratios instead of activities?

Solution: At infinite dilution or in ideal solutions, activity coefficients approach 1, allowing activities to be replaced by concentrations or molalities.
