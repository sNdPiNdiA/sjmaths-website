Concentration Cell with Transference

A concentration cell with transference allows the direct transfer of matter between two electrolyte solutions of different concentrations through a liquid junction. The electromotive force of this cell depends on both the activities of the ions and their respective transport numbers.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Basis and Liquid Junction Potential

  • A liquid junction potential (Ej) develops at the interface between two solutions of different concentrations due to unequal rates of ionic migration.
  • The total emf of a cell with transference includes both the electrode potential difference and the liquid junction potential.
  • Elimination of the liquid junction potential is typically achieved using a concentrated KCl salt bridge because the transport numbers of K+ and Cl- are nearly identical (t+ ≈ t- ≈ 0.5).

Derivation and Transport Number Determination

  • For a concentration cell reversible to cations, the emf with transference is given by Ecell = t- (RT / F) ln(a2 / a1) for a 1:1 electrolyte.
  • Comparing the emf of a cell with transference to that of a cell without transference allows the experimental determination of transport numbers.
  • The ratio of the emf with transference to the emf without transference directly yields the transport number of the ion to which the electrodes are non-reversible.

Activity Coefficients and Boundary Conditions

  • In dilute solutions, mean ionic activities (a = m±γ±) are substituted for concentrations to account for non-ideal ionic interactions.
  • When concentrations c1 and c2 are very close, the logarithmic term can be approximated using molalities.
  • Temperature dependence of the EMF follows standard thermodynamic relations where (∂E / ∂T)P corresponds to the entropy change of the cell reaction.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Liquid Junction Potential Formula
Ej = (t+ - t-) (RT / F) ln(a2 / a1)
Conditions: Potential difference arising across the boundary of two differing electrolyte solutions.
Units: Ej in Volts, t+ and t- are transport numbers
EMF without Transference
Ewithout = 2 (RT / F) ln(a2 / a1)
Conditions: Cell involving transference eliminated via a salt bridge.
Units: E in Volts, activities a1 and a2
Transport Number Relation to EMF
t- = Ewith / Ewithout
Conditions: Applicable for symmetrical electrolytes where activity coefficients are assumed equal.
Units: Dimensionless transport number ratio
EMF of Concentration Cell with Transference
Ecell = 2t- (RT / F) ln(a2 / a1)
Conditions: For a concentration cell of a unibivalent electrolyte with transference at constant temperature.
Units: E in Volts, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin, F in C mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Transport numbers must sum up to unity in a binary electrolyte (t+ + t- = 1).

Observation: In concentrated solutions, transport numbers vary significantly with concentration. — Ion-pairing, hydration changes, and specific ionic interactions alter the relative mobility and solvation spheres of the ions.

Exception Liquid junction potential is zero when two identical solutions meet.

Observation: Ej can be non-zero even at equal concentrations if solvent composition differs. — Differences in solvation energies and dielectric constants across mixed-solvent boundaries generate unequal ionic mobilities.

Exception Salt bridges completely eliminate liquid junction potentials.

Observation: Residual junction potentials of a few millivolts always persist. — Complete equality of ionic mobilities (t+ = t- = 0.5) is never exact for any single electrolyte.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Transport Number Extraction Mnemonic
tion = Ewith / Ewithout

Application: Always remember that the cell with transference has a smaller EMF than the cell without transference because the liquid junction potential opposes or reduces the net electrical work.

Salt Bridge Choice Rule
t(K+) ≈ t(Cl-)

Application: Select KCl for salt bridges in aqueous cells because potassium and chloride ions possess virtually identical ionic mobilities in water.

Logarithmic Concentration Ratio Shortcut
2.303 RT / F ≈ 0.0591 V at 298 K

Application: Convert natural logarithms to base-10 logarithms immediately by multiplying by 0.0591 / n during numerical calculations at room temperature.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The EMF of a concentration cell with transference for a 1:1 electrolyte is given by E = 2t-(RT/F)ln(a2/a1).
  • The liquid junction potential arises due to the difference in the speeds of cations and anions across the boundary.
  • A saturated solution of potassium chloride (KCl) is universally chosen for salt bridges to minimize junction potentials.
  • The ratio of the EMF with transference to that without transference gives the transport number of the ion carrying current across the boundary.
  • Concentration cells with transference are spontaneous when transferring from a region of higher activity to lower activity.
  • At 298 K, the factor 2.303 RT / F evaluates to approximately 0.0591 V.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the EMF formula for concentration cells with transference with that of cells without transference.
  • Neglecting the sign convention of activities where a2 > a1 for spontaneous cell operation.
  • Assuming transport numbers remain constant across all concentration ranges.
  • Forgetting that transport numbers are dimensionless fractions whose sum equals 1.

⚡ Quick Revision: Concentration Cell with Transference

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Liquid Junction Potential Origin

Ej originates from the faster diffusion of one ion over another across the concentration boundary, creating a local electrical double layer.

Role of Salt Bridge

A salt bridge suppresses the liquid junction potential by providing an equitransferent electrolyte that dominates the current carriage across the junction.

Measurement of Transport Numbers

Electromotive force measurements of concentration cells with and without transference provide an accurate electrochemical method for determining ionic transport numbers.

Thermodynamic Activity Correction

Real solutions require mean ionic activities instead of concentrations in the Nernst-derived logarithmic terms to maintain thermodynamic rigor.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Transport Number (ti)
Fraction of total current carried by a specific ionic species.
Liquid Junction Potential (Ej)
Potential difference developed across the boundary of two differing electrolyte solutions.
Concentration Cell (Ecell)
Cell whose EMF arises solely from a difference in concentration or activity between compartments.
Mean Ionic Activity (a±)
Effective thermodynamic concentration of ions in solution.
Equitransferent Ion (t+ = t-)
An ion pair having equal mobilities and transport numbers (e.g., KCl).
EMF with Transference (Ewith)
Cell voltage incorporating both electrode potentials and liquid junction potential.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ewith = 2t- (RT/F) ln(a2/a1) for 1:1 electrolyte.
  • t+ + t- = 1 in any binary electrolyte solution.
  • KCl salt bridge minimizes Ej due to equal ionic mobilities.
  • Transport number can be found from the ratio Ewith / Ewithout.
  • Activity replaces concentration in rigorous thermodynamic expressions.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Concentration cells with transference involve liquid junction boundaries.
  • Transport numbers dictate the magnitude of the junction potential.
  • Salt bridges effectively eliminate junction potentials in standard cells.
  • Comparison of cells with and without transference yields ionic transport numbers.

⚖️ Key Differences

Cell with Transference VS Cell without Transference

Cell with transference permits direct mixing and develops a liquid junction potential, whereas cell without transference uses a salt bridge to eliminate it.

Question 1 of 19

What is the primary cause of liquid junction potential in a concentration cell with transference?

Explanation: Liquid junction potential arises because cations and anions migrate at different speeds across the boundary between two solutions of different concentrations.

Question 2 of 19

Which electrolyte is most commonly used in a salt bridge to eliminate liquid junction potential?

Explanation: Both KCl and NH4NO3 have ions with nearly identical transport numbers (t+ ≈ t-), making them ideal for salt bridges.

Question 3 of 19

For a 1:1 electrolyte concentration cell with transference, the EMF is proportional to:

Explanation: The EMF formula for a concentration cell reversible to cations with transference is E = 2t-(RT/F)ln(a2/a1).

Question 4 of 19

If the transport number of the cation is 0.4, what is the transport number of the anion in a binary electrolyte?

Explanation: The sum of transport numbers of cation and anion in a binary electrolyte is always unity (t+ + t- = 1).

Question 5 of 19

What happens to the liquid junction potential if a very effective salt bridge is employed?

Explanation: An effective salt bridge suppresses the junction potential, reducing it to a negligible value (usually less than 1-2 mV).

Question 6 of 19

The ratio of the EMF of a concentration cell with transference to that without transference gives:

Explanation: For a symmetrical electrolyte, Ewith / Ewithout equals the transport number of the ion to which the electrodes are not reversible.

Question 7 of 19

Which of the following conditions is required for a concentration cell to generate spontaneous electrical work?

Explanation: Spontaneous operation requires transfer from a higher activity (a2) to a lower activity (a1).

Question 8 of 19

In the expression for EMF with transference, activity is used instead of concentration to account for:

Explanation: Activities replace concentrations to correct for interionic electrostatic forces in non-ideal solutions.

Question 9 of 19

What is the value of (RT / F) at 298 K in volts?

Explanation: The term RT/F at 298 K is (8.314 J K-1 mol-1 × 298 K) / 96500 C mol-1 ≈ 0.0257 V.

Question 10 of 19

Why does a concentration cell with transference have a lower EMF than one without transference?

Explanation: The liquid junction potential acts in opposition to the thermodynamic potential difference between the electrodes, lowering the net measured EMF.

Question 11 of 19

Which thermodynamic property change (ΔG) is associated with the operation of a concentration cell?

Explanation: All spontaneous electrochemical cells operate with a decrease in Gibbs free energy, hence ΔG is negative.

Question 12 of 19

If t+ = 0.5 in a 1:1 electrolyte, what is the liquid junction potential contribution relative to transport numbers?

Explanation: Since t+ = t- = 0.5, the term (t+ - t-) becomes zero, making the liquid junction potential zero.

Question 13 of 19

Concentration cells are classified into two main types based on:

Explanation: Concentration cells are broadly divided into electrode concentration cells and electrolyte concentration cells.

Question 14 of 19

What is the unit of transport number?

Explanation: Transport number is a fraction representing current carried by an ion relative to total current, making it dimensionless.

Question 15 of 19

In a concentration cell with transference, matter is transferred from:

Explanation: Diffusion and ionic migration occur spontaneously from the higher concentration compartment to the lower concentration compartment.

Question 16 of 19

The Nernst equation for a concentration cell without transference across activities a1 and a2 involves which logarithmic term?

Explanation: The Nernst equation yields a logarithmic ratio of higher activity to lower activity, ln(a2 / a1).

Question 17 of 19

Which of the following scientists derived the equation for liquid junction potential?

Explanation: Nernst and Planck derived classical expressions for liquid junction potentials based on diffusion and concentration gradients.

Question 18 of 19

What is the effect of increasing temperature on the EMF of a concentration cell, assuming concentrations remain constant?

Explanation: The Nernst equation explicitly contains the temperature T as a multiplicative factor, causing EMF to scale directly with T.

Question 19 of 19

In a concentration cell with transference, ions cross the liquid junction via:

Explanation: Transport across the liquid junction occurs through both concentration-driven diffusion and potential-driven migration.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following expressions represents the liquid junction potential (Ej) for a unibivalent electrolyte?

Solution: Liquid junction potential is given by Ej = (t+ - t-) (RT/F) ln(a2/a1) for univalent electrolytes.

Question 3 of 10
In a concentration cell with transference, if t+ = t-, then the liquid junction potential is:

Solution: When t+ = t-, the term (t+ - t-) vanishes, making the liquid junction potential zero.

Question 4 of 10
The sum of transport numbers of all ions in a ternary electrolyte solution equals:

Solution: By definition, the sum of transport numbers of all ions present in any electrolyte solution is always equal to 1.

Question 5 of 10
What is the effect of dilution on the transport number of an ion in a strong electrolyte?

Solution: Transport numbers approach a constant limiting value at infinite dilution due to complete dissociation and independent ionic migration.

Question 6 of 10
Which cell setup inherently includes a liquid junction potential?

Solution: A concentration cell with transference features direct liquid-liquid contact, generating a liquid junction potential.

Question 7 of 10
For a concentration cell without transference, the EMF formula for a 1:1 electrolyte is:

Solution: Cell without transference eliminates junction effects and doubles the concentration term factor: E = 2 (RT/F) ln(a2/a1).

Question 8 of 10
Why is NH4NO3 also used in salt bridges alongside KCl?

Solution: NH4NO3 has nearly equal ionic mobilities and prevents insoluble chloride precipitation when silver or mercury ions are present.

Question 9 of 10
If a2 = 0.1 M and a1 = 0.01 M in a 1:1 concentration cell, the logarithmic ratio ln(a2/a1) equals:

Solution: ln(0.1 / 0.01) = ln(10).

Question 10 of 10
Which physical factor directly influences ionic mobility and hence transport numbers?

Solution: Viscosity determines hydrodynamic drag on solvated ions, directly affecting ionic mobility and transport numbers.
