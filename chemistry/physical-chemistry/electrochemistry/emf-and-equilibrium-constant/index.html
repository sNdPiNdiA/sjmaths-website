EMF and Equilibrium Constant

The electromotive force (EMF) of a galvanic cell is fundamentally related to the standard Gibbs free energy change and the thermodynamic equilibrium constant of the cell reaction. At electrochemical equilibrium, the cell potential becomes zero, enabling the direct calculation of equilibrium constants from standard reduction potentials.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Foundation of Cell Potential

  • The Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) represents the maximum non-expansion work obtainable from a galvanic cell.
  • The relation ΔG = -nFEcell links macroscopic electrical work to microscopic electrochemical driving force.
  • A positive E°cell corresponds to a negative ΔG°, denoting a spontaneous chemical process under standard conditions.

Equilibrium Constant Derivation from Nernst Equation

  • When a galvanic cell discharges completely, it reaches chemical equilibrium, where the net cell potential Ecell = 0.
  • Substituting Ecell = 0 and the reaction quotient Q = Kc into the Nernst equation yields E°cell = (RT / nF) ln Kc.
  • At 298 K, converting to base-10 logarithm gives E°cell = (0.0591 / n) log Kc for aqueous solutions.

Thermodynamic Functions from EMF Temperature Dependence

  • The temperature dependence of EMF, (∂E / ∂T)P, is known as the temperature coefficient of the cell.
  • Reaction entropy is calculated via ΔS = nF (∂E / ∂T)P, connecting electrical measurements directly to entropy change.
  • Enthalpy change is derived using the combined equation ΔH = -nFE + nFT (∂E / ∂T)P.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Standard EMF and Equilibrium Constant (at 298 K)
log Kc = (n × E°cell) / 0.0591
Conditions: Standard temperature 298 K, aqueous systems
Units: E° in Volts, Kc is dimensionless
Gibbs Free Energy and Standard EMF
ΔG° = -n F E°cell
Conditions: Standard state conditions (1 bar, 1 M, 298 K)
Units: ΔG° in J mol-1, F in C mol-1, E° in Volts
Temperature Coefficient of EMF
ΔS = n F (∂E / ∂T)P
Conditions: Constant pressure, reversible cell operation
Units: ΔS in J K-1 mol-1, (∂E / ∂T) in V K-1
Nernst Equation at Equilibrium
Ecell = E°cell - (RT / nF) ln Kc
Conditions: At equilibrium when Ecell = 0 and Q = Kc
Units: E in Volts, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin, F in C mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher standard reduction potential indicates a stronger oxidizing agent

Observation: Kinetic overpotential anomalies — Certain reactions possess high activation energy barriers, making them thermodynamically feasible (positive E°) but kinetically sluggish.

Exception Equilibrium constant increases exponentially with standard EMF

Observation: Precipitation and complex formation side reactions — Secondary equilibria alter free ion concentrations, causing deviations from theoretical Nernstian equilibrium calculations.

Exception Cell EMF is independent of stoichiometric coefficients

Observation: Activity coefficient variations at high ionic strength — In concentrated solutions, ion-ion interactions cause effective concentrations (activities) to diverge from molarities, altering observed EMF.

Rapid Kc Calculation Rule
log Kc ≈ (n × E°cell) / 0.0591

Application: Use this simplified relation at 298 K to rapidly estimate the equilibrium constant when E°cell and n are known.

Spontaneity Check Mnemonic
+ E° → - ΔG° → K > 1

Application: A positive standard EMF always guarantees a negative standard Gibbs free energy and an equilibrium constant greater than unity.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • At electrochemical equilibrium, the cell potential Ecell becomes exactly zero.
  • The standard Gibbs free energy change ΔG° is directly proportional to standard cell EMF via -nFE°cell.
  • At 298 K, the relation between standard EMF and equilibrium constant is log Kc = nE° / 0.0591.
  • If E°cell > 0, the equilibrium constant Kc > 1, favoring product formation.
  • The temperature coefficient of a cell (∂E / ∂T)P allows direct determination of reaction entropy ΔS.
  • Enthalpy of reaction in an electrochemical cell is obtained from both EMF and its temperature derivative.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the reaction quotient Q with the equilibrium constant Kc when applying the Nernst equation.
  • Failing to multiply half-cell reactions to equalize electrons, leading to incorrect values of n in thermodynamic calculations.
  • Assuming Ecell = 0 at standard conditions instead of recognizing it applies strictly at equilibrium.
  • Neglecting temperature conversion from Celsius to Kelvin when using the gas constant R and Faraday constant F.

⚡ Quick Revision: EMF and Equilibrium Constant

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrochemical Equilibrium

Chemical systems in galvanic cells evolve until net electrical potential drops to zero, achieving dynamic equilibrium where forward and reverse rates balance.

Gibbs Energy Link

Electrochemical potential acts as the electrical analogue of chemical potential, driving non-expansion work quantified through Gibbs free energy changes.

Temperature Dependence

The variation of cell EMF with temperature provides direct access to reaction entropy and enthalpy without requiring calorimetric measurements.

Equilibrium Constant Magnitude

The exponential relationship between standard EMF and equilibrium constant dictates whether a reaction goes essentially to completion.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Electromotive Force (Ecell / Volts)
Maximum potential difference between two half-cells when zero current flows.
Equilibrium Constant (Kc / Dimensionless)
Ratio of product activities to reactant activities at chemical equilibrium.
Faraday Constant (F ≈ 96485 C mol-1)
Electrical charge carried by one mole of electrons.
Standard Gibbs Energy (ΔG° / J mol-1)
Gibbs free energy change under standard state conditions.
Number of Electrons (n / Integer)
Moles of electrons transferred in the balanced redox reaction.
Temperature Coefficient ((∂E / ∂T)P / V K-1)
Rate of change of cell EMF with temperature at constant pressure.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ecell = 0 at equilibrium
  • ΔG° = -nFE°cell
  • log Kc = (nE°) / 0.0591 (at 298 K)
  • ΔS = nF(∂E / ∂T)P
  • Kc > 1 when E°cell > 0

📌 Quick Recall

  • EMF measures the driving force of redox reactions.
  • Nernst equation bridges non-standard potentials to equilibrium constants.
  • Spontaneity requires positive standard cell potential.
  • Thermal properties of cells are accessible via potential-temperature derivatives.

⚖️ Key Differences

Ecell VS E°cell

Ecell varies with concentration and conditions, whereas E°cell is a constant standard property.

Explanation: Derived from E° = (RT/nF) ln Kc by substituting R, T, F, and converting natural log to base-10.

If E°cell is positive, what can be deduced about the equilibrium constant Kc?

Explanation: A positive E°cell implies a negative ΔG°, which corresponds to Kc greater than 1.

What is the unit of the Faraday constant (F)?

Question 6 of 19

For a redox reaction with n = 2 and E°cell = 0.295 V at 298 K, what is log Kc?

Explanation: log Kc = (2 × 0.295) / 0.0591 = 0.590 / 0.0591 ≈ 10.

Question 7 of 19

Which thermodynamic property is directly obtained from standard cell EMF?

Explanation: ΔG° = -nFE°cell provides the direct link between standard EMF and Gibbs free energy.

Question 8 of 19

What happens to the cell potential when reactant concentrations are increased?

Explanation: According to the Nernst equation, increasing reactant concentration reduces the reaction quotient Q, increasing Ecell.

Question 9 of 19

Which condition must be met for a galvanic cell reaction to be spontaneous?

Explanation: A positive standard cell potential ensures spontaneous reaction progression under standard conditions.

Question 10 of 19

The temperature coefficient of a reversible cell is given by:

Explanation: The temperature coefficient is defined as the partial derivative of EMF with respect to temperature at constant pressure.

Question 11 of 19

If log Kc = 0, what is the value of E°cell?

Explanation: If log Kc = 0, Kc = 1, meaning the system is already at standard equilibrium where E°cell = 0.

Question 12 of 19

What is the relationship between standard cell potential and equilibrium constant when n = 1 at 298 K?

Explanation: Both base-10 (using 0.0591) and natural logarithm (using RT/F ≈ 0.0257) forms are correct.

Question 13 of 19

Which of the following does NOT affect the value of standard cell potential (E°)?

Explanation: Standard potential is defined at unit activity (1 M concentration), so concentration changes do not alter E°.

Question 14 of 19

Calculate ΔG° for a cell with n = 1 and E° = 1.10 V.

Explanation: ΔG° = -1 × 96485 C mol-1 × 1.10 V = -106,133.5 J mol-1 ≈ -106.1 kJ mol-1.

Question 15 of 19

When Q < Kc, the cell potential Ecell is:

Explanation: When Q < Kc, the forward reaction is favored, resulting in a positive cell potential driving the system toward equilibrium.

Question 16 of 19

The term 'n' in the Nernst equation represents:

Explanation: 'n' denotes the stoichiometric number of electrons exchanged in the balanced redox equation.

Question 17 of 19

What is the unit of Gibbs free energy change (ΔG)?

Question 18 of 19

Which expression gives the enthalpy of a cell reaction in terms of EMF?

Explanation: From thermodynamic combination ΔH = ΔG + TΔS, substituting ΔG and ΔS yields ΔH = -nFE + nFT(∂E / ∂T)P.

Question 19 of 19

If Kc > 1, the position of equilibrium lies largely towards:

Explanation: A large equilibrium constant (Kc > 1) signifies high product yield at equilibrium.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the equilibrium constant at 298 K for a cell with E°cell = 0.118 V and n = 2.

Solution: log Kc = (2 × 0.118) / 0.0591 = 0.236 / 0.0591 ≈ 4, so Kc = 104.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following statements is correct regarding a cell at equilibrium?

Solution: At equilibrium, the system has reached minimum free energy, hence ΔG = 0.

Question 3 of 10
If ΔG° for a redox reaction is -212 kJ mol-1 and n = 2, what is E°cell?

Solution: E° = -ΔG° / (nF) = 212,000 / (2 × 96485) ≈ 1.10 V.

Question 4 of 10
What is the effect on the equilibrium constant if the standard cell potential is increased?

Solution: Since log Kc is directly proportional to E°cell, an increase in standard EMF increases Kc.

Question 5 of 10
The Nernst equation expression for log Kc at 298 K assumes which value for RT / F (converted to base 10)?

Solution: The factor (2.303 RT / F) evaluates to approximately 0.0591 at 298.15 K.

Question 6 of 10
If a cell reaction has Kc < 1, the value of standard cell potential is:

Solution: An unfavorable equilibrium (Kc < 1) corresponds to a positive ΔG° and thus a negative E°cell.

Question 7 of 10
In the measurement of EMF temperature coefficient, the pressure is maintained as:

Solution: The temperature coefficient (∂E / ∂T)P strictly requires constant pressure.

Question 8 of 10
What is the cell potential of a Daniell cell at chemical equilibrium?

Solution: Any galvanic cell at chemical equilibrium exhibits a cell potential of zero.

Question 9 of 10
Which combination correctly describes a non-spontaneous electrochemical process?

Solution: Non-spontaneous reactions have negative standard EMF, positive standard free energy, and equilibrium constants less than one.

Question 10 of 10
Calculate the reaction entropy (ΔS) if n = 2, F = 96485 C mol-1, and (∂E / ∂T)P = 0.0002 V K-1.

Solution: ΔS = 2 × 96485 × 0.0002 = 38.594 J K-1 mol-1.
