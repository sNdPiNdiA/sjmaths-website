Physical Chemistry

Fuel Cells

Fuel cells are galvanic cells that continuously convert the chemical energy of a fuel and an oxidant directly into electrical energy through electrochemical reactions. They operate indefinitely as long as reactants are supplied continuously to the electrodes.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Hydrogen-Oxygen Fuel Cell Architecture

  • The H2-O2 fuel cell (Bacon cell) uses porous carbon electrodes impregnated with catalysts like platinum, silver, or metal oxides.
  • An aqueous solution of KOH or NaOH is typically employed as the liquid electrolyte separating the anode and cathode compartments.
  • Hydrogen gas is fed continuously into the anode compartment, while oxygen gas is fed into the cathode compartment.
  • Water is formed as the sole byproduct, making it an environmentally clean energy conversion system.

Electrode Half-Reactions and Mechanisms

  • At the anode in alkaline medium, hydrogen undergoes oxidation: 2H2(g) + 4OH-(aq) → 4H2O(l) + 4e-.
  • At the cathode, oxygen undergoes reduction: O2(g) + 2H2O(l) + 4e- → 4OH-(aq).
  • The overall cell reaction is the combination of both half-reactions: 2H2(g) + O2(g) → 2H2O(l).
  • Catalysts are strictly required to lower the activation energy for the sluggish oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) at the cathode.

Thermodynamics and Efficiency Advantage

  • Unlike internal combustion engines limited by the Carnot cycle efficiency, fuel cells are not heat engines and bypass thermal limitations.
  • The theoretical efficiency of a hydrogen fuel cell exceeds 83% under standard conditions, whereas practical efficiencies range between 40% and 60%.
  • The entropy change (ΔS) for the formation of liquid water is negative, meaning ΔG is slightly less negative than ΔH.
  • Waste heat generated during operation can be captured in cogeneration systems to enhance overall thermal-electrical facility efficiency.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Galvanic cells deplete their reactants and eventually stop producing current.

Observation: Fuel cells do not undergo internal reactant depletion during operation. — Reactants (fuel and oxidant) are continuously supplied from external storage tanks, maintaining a steady-state non-equilibrium condition.

Exception Standard electrochemical cells convert chemical energy to electrical energy with efficiencies bounded by Carnot limitations.

Observation: Fuel cell efficiency can theoretically exceed 100% based on enthalpy if heat is absorbed from the surroundings (ΔH < ΔG). — Because ΔG = ΔH - TΔS, if TΔS is positive and large, the electrical work output can surpass the enthalpy of reaction by absorbing ambient thermal energy.

Application: Anode is always Oxidation (electrons released, fuel fed here), Cathode is always Reduction (electrons consumed, oxidant fed here) in all electrochemical devices including fuel cells.

Electron Count Rule for H2-O2 Cells
n = 4 per mole of O2

Application: Always use n = 4 when calculating ΔG = -nFE° for the standard hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell reaction consuming 1 mole of O2 or 2 moles of H2.

Efficiency Quick Estimation
η ≈ 1.23 V / 1.48 V (at standard conditions)

Application: Ratio of the reversible cell voltage (1.23 V) to the thermodulal voltage based on enthalpy (1.48 V) quickly yields the maximum theoretical efficiency (~83%).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The standard emf of a hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell is approximately 1.23 V at 298 K.
  • Water formed in the H2-O2 fuel cell is pure enough to be used as drinking water by astronauts in space missions (Apollo program).
  • Phosphoric Acid Fuel Cells (PAFC) use concentrated H3PO4 and operate at around 200 °C.
  • Proton Exchange Membrane Fuel Cells (PEMFC) utilize a solid polymer electrolyte and operate at lower temperatures (60-100 °C).
  • Solid Oxide Fuel Cells (SOFC) operate at high temperatures (500-1000 °C) using a ceramic solid electrolyte like yttria-stabilized zirconia.
  • Molten Carbonate Fuel Cells (MCFC) employ a molten alkali carbonate mixture as the electrolyte and require CO2 recycling at the cathode.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the polarity of electrodes: In fuel cells, the anode is negative and the cathode is positive, identical to electrolytic/galvanic cells, but powered by external gas feeds.
  • Assuming fuel cells are 100% efficient due to direct energy conversion; thermodynamic and kinetic overpotentials reduce real-world efficiency to 40-60%.
  • Forgetting that water produced in high-temperature fuel cells (like SOFC) is in vapor form, changing the reaction entropy values.
  • Applying standard conditions when calculating operating voltages under load without accounting for activation, ohmic, and mass transport overpotentials.

⚡ Quick Revision: Fuel Cells

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Fundamental Definition

Fuel cells are open electrochemical systems where reactants are fed continuously, bypassing the thermodynamic limitations of combustion heat engines.

Thermodynamic Basis

The maximum electrical work obtainable is equal to the decrease in Gibbs free energy (-ΔG), directly linked to standard cell potential via ΔG = -nFE°.

Classification by Electrolyte

Fuel cells are categorized based on their electrolyte type into PEMFC, PAFC, AFC, MCFC, and SOFC, dictating their operating temperature and catalyst requirements.

Environmental Significance

Offering high energy density and zero direct emissions when fueled by pure hydrogen, fuel cells represent a cornerstone technology for green energy transition.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Overall H2-O2 reaction: 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O
  • Standard EMF of H2-O2 fuel cell = 1.23 V
  • Number of electrons transferred per mole of oxygen = 4
  • Efficiency formula: η = ΔG / ΔH
  • Fuel cells are not subject to Carnot cycle limitations

📌 Quick Recall

  • Continuous reactant feed prevents thermodynamic dead-ends typical of standard batteries.
  • Catalysts are essential at both electrodes to minimize activation overpotentials.
  • Different electrolytes determine optimal operating temperatures ranging from 80 °C to 1000 °C.
  • Water is the primary byproduct, ensuring zero toxic emissions when using pure hydrogen.

⚖️ Key Differences

Combustion Engine VS Fuel Cell

Combustion converts chemical energy to heat then work (Carnot limited); fuel cells convert chemical energy directly to electricity electrochemically.

Question 1 of 19

What is the primary byproduct of a hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell?

Explanation: The overall reaction in a hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell combines hydrogen and oxygen to form water: 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O.

Question 2 of 19

In an alkaline hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell, what species is oxidized at the anode?

Explanation: Hydrogen gas is fed to the anode where it undergoes oxidation to form water by reacting with hydroxide ions.

Question 3 of 19

How many moles of electrons are transferred in the overall redox reaction when one mole of oxygen is consumed in a fuel cell?

Explanation: The reduction of one mole of O2 requires 4 electrons: O2 + 2H2O + 4e- → 4OH-.

Question 4 of 19

Which of the following describes the efficiency limit of fuel cells?

Explanation: Since fuel cells convert chemical energy directly to electrical energy without a thermal intermediate, they are not constrained by the Carnot efficiency limit.

Question 5 of 19

What is the standard emf (E°) of a typical hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell at 298 K?

Explanation: The standard electromotive force of a hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell is calculated from ΔG° = -nFE°, yielding approximately 1.23 V.

Question 6 of 19

Which type of fuel cell operates at the highest temperature?

Explanation: Solid Oxide Fuel Cells use ceramic electrolytes and operate at high temperatures ranging from 500 °C to 1000 °C.

Question 7 of 19

What is the electrolyte used in Proton Exchange Membrane Fuel Cells (PEMFC)?

Explanation: PEMFCs use a solid polymer electrolyte membrane that selectively conducts protons (H+) from anode to cathode.

Question 8 of 19

Why are platinum catalysts commonly used in fuel cells?

Explanation: Catalysts like platinum accelerate the sluggish kinetics of the oxygen reduction and hydrogen oxidation reactions by lowering activation energy.

Question 9 of 19

Which thermodynamic property is directly related to the maximum electrical work of a fuel cell?

Explanation: The maximum non-expansion work obtainable from a system at constant temperature and pressure equals the negative change in Gibbs free energy (-ΔG).

Question 10 of 19

What is the cathode reaction in an acidic fuel cell?

Explanation: In acidic media, oxygen is reduced at the cathode by reacting with protons and electrons to produce water.

Question 11 of 19

Which fuel cell was famously utilized in the Apollo space missions?

Explanation: Francis Bacon's alkaline hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell design was successfully adapted for electricity and potable water generation in the Apollo spacecraft.

Question 12 of 19

What happens to the theoretical efficiency of a hydrogen fuel cell if the operating temperature is increased, assuming ΔS is negative?

Explanation: Since ΔG = ΔH - TΔS, an increase in temperature when ΔS is negative makes ΔG less negative, reducing the available electrical work and theoretical efficiency.

Question 13 of 19

Which species acts as the mobile charge carrier inside a Solid Oxide Fuel Cell (SOFC)?

Explanation: SOFCs operate via the migration of oxide ions (O2-) through the solid ceramic crystal lattice from cathode to anode.

Question 14 of 19

What is the main advantage of Direct Methanol Fuel Cells (DMFC) over hydrogen fuel cells?

Explanation: Methanol is a liquid at room temperature, simplifying storage and distribution compared to cryogenic or high-pressure gaseous hydrogen.

Question 15 of 19

In the calculation ΔG = -nFE°, what units are typically used for Faraday constant (F)?

Explanation: The Faraday constant represents the electric charge carried by one mole of electrons and is expressed in Coulombs per mole (C mol-1).

Question 16 of 19

What is the primary cause of voltage loss (polarization) in an operating fuel cell?

Explanation: Real fuel cells experience voltage drop due to activation losses at electrodes, ohmic resistance of the electrolyte, and mass transport limitations.

Question 17 of 19

Which substance is added to zirconia to stabilize its crystal structure for use in SOFCs?

Explanation: Yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) is the standard ceramic electrolyte used in SOFCs because yttria stabilizes the cubic fluorite structure and creates oxygen vacancies.

Question 18 of 19

What is the role of the electrolyte in a fuel cell?

Explanation: The electrolyte permits the selective passage of ions between electrodes to complete the internal circuit while preventing direct mixing of fuel and oxidant.

Question 19 of 19

Which of the following fuels can be used in a high-temperature fuel cell without an external reformer?

Explanation: High-temperature fuel cells like SOFCs can internally reform hydrocarbons such as methane or utilize carbon monoxide directly as fuel.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) for a hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell operating with n = 4 and E° = 1.23 V (F = 96485 C mol-1).

Solution: ΔG° = -nFE° = -(4 mol) × (96485 C mol-1) × (1.23 V) = -474826 J mol-1 ≈ -474.8 kJ mol-1.

Question 2 of 10
What is the primary electrolyte used in Proton Exchange Membrane Fuel Cells (PEMFC)?

Solution: PEMFCs utilize a hydrated solid polymer proton-conducting membrane, commonly known by the trade name Nafion.

Question 3 of 10
Which electrode process occurs at the cathode of all fuel cells?

Solution: By definition, reduction always takes place at the cathode in any electrochemical cell, consuming electrons supplied from the external circuit.

Question 4 of 10
What is the main reason why fuel cells are not universally used for all power generation currently?

Solution: Economic barriers including expensive noble metal catalysts (platinum) and technical difficulties in hydrogen storage and transport limit widespread adoption.

Question 5 of 10
In a molten carbonate fuel cell (MCFC), what ion is transported across the electrolyte from cathode to anode?

Solution: MCFCs operate using molten alkali carbonate salts where carbonate ions (CO32-) migrate from the cathode to the anode.

Question 6 of 10
If the enthalpy of combustion of hydrogen (ΔH) is -285.8 kJ mol-1 and ΔG is -237.2 kJ mol-1 at 298 K, what is the thermodynamic efficiency (η)?

Solution: η = ΔG / ΔH = (-237.2) / (-285.8) × 100% ≈ 83.0%.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following statements regarding Direct Methanol Fuel Cells (DMFC) is correct?

Solution: In a DMFC, liquid methanol is fed directly to the anode where it undergoes electro-oxidation in the presence of water to form CO2, protons, and electrons.

Question 8 of 10
What role does porous carbon play when used as electrode material in fuel cells?

Solution: Porous carbon structures ensure high triple-phase boundary areas where gaseous reactants, liquid/solid electrolytes, and solid catalysts meet.

Question 9 of 10
Why does the actual cell potential of a fuel cell drop below its theoretical Nernst potential when current is drawn?

Solution: Polarization phenomena such as activation energy barriers, internal electrolyte resistance (ohmic), and reactant diffusion limits (mass transport) cause voltage drops under load.

Question 10 of 10
What is the primary difference between a primary battery and a fuel cell?

Solution: Primary batteries contain a finite quantity of sealed reactants which get depleted, whereas fuel cells maintain continuous operation via external reactant feeds.
