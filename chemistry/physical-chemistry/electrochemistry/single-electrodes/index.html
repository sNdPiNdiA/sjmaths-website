Physical Chemistry

Single Electrodes

A single electrode potential arises at the interface between a metal electrode and an electrolyte solution containing its ions due to charge separation. Absolute single electrode potentials cannot be measured experimentally, requiring the Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) as a universal reference.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Origin and Types of Single Electrode Potentials

  • The electrode potential develops when metal atoms lose electrons to form ions in solution (oxidation) or metal ions gain electrons from the electrode (reduction).
  • Metal-Metal Ion Electrodes consist of a metal rod dipped in a solution of its own ions, represented as Mn+ | M.
  • Gas Electrodes involve a gas in contact with its ions in solution in the presence of an inert conductor like platinum, such as the Hydrogen Electrode (Pt | H2 (g) | H+ (aq)).
  • Metal-Metal Insoluble Salt Electrodes feature a metal coated with its sparingly soluble salt in contact with a solution containing the same anion, such as the Calomel Electrode (Hg | Hg2Cl2 | Cl-).

Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) and Reference Systems

  • The Standard Hydrogen Electrode has an arbitrarily assigned standard reduction potential of exactly 0.00 V at all temperatures.
  • SHE configuration: Pt(s) | H2(g, 1 bar) | H+(aq, a = 1).
  • Secondary reference electrodes like the Saturated Calomel Electrode (SCE) and Silver-Silver Chloride electrode are used due to the experimental difficulty of handling hydrogen gas.
  • The potential of an unknown single electrode is determined by coupling it with SHE or SCE and measuring the net cell emf.

Thermodynamics and IUPAC Sign Conventions

  • According to IUPAC Stockholm convention, all electrode potentials are expressed as reduction potentials.
  • A positive standard reduction potential ($E^°$) indicates that the species is more easily reduced than H+ and acts as a stronger oxidizing agent.
  • A negative standard reduction potential indicates that the reduced form is a stronger reducing agent than hydrogen gas.
  • The relationship between equilibrium constant $K$ and standard cell potential is given by $\ln K = nFE^°RT$.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Nernst Equation at 298 K (Base-10)
E = E° - (0.0591 / n) log([Reduced species] / [Oxidized species])
Conditions: Standard temperature T = 298 K, dilute aqueous solutions
Units: E in Volts, concentration in mol L-1
Gibbs Free Energy and Electrode Potential
ΔG = -nFE
Conditions: Isothermal and isobaric electrochemical cell process
Units: ΔG in Joules, F in C mol-1, E in Volts
Standard Cell Potential
E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode
Conditions: Standard conditions: 298 K, 1 bar pressure, 1 M activity
Units: E° in Volts
Nernst Equation for Single Electrode
E = E° - (RT / nF) ln(aRed / aOx)
Conditions: Applicable for any temperature and non-unit activities
Units: E in Volts, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin, F in C mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Standard reduction potentials are generally independent of total pressure except for gas electrodes.

Observation: Hydrogen electrode potential varies significantly with H2 gas pressure. — Derived directly from the Nernst equation term involving the partial pressure of H2 gas ($PH_21/2$).

Exception Concentration cells with transference have zero liquid junction potential.

Observation: Liquid junction potential develops between two solutions of different concentrations or compositions. — Unequal rates of ionic migration (transport numbers) of cations and anions across the boundary create a localized potential.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The standard reduction potential of SHE is set to 0.00 V at all temperatures.
  • A negative $E^°$ value means the metal is more reactive than hydrogen and displaces $H_2$ gas from dilute acids.
  • Electrode potential is an intensive property; multiplying the half-reaction by a stoichiometric coefficient does not change its $E^°$ value.
  • The Nernst equation accounts for the effect of concentration and temperature on electrode potential.
  • Calomel electrode utilizes mercury, mercurous chloride ($Hg_2Cl_2$), and potassium chloride solution.
  • Gibbs free energy change ($Δ G$) is an extensive property and is directly additive when combining half-cells via $Δ G = -nFE$.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Multiplying the standard electrode potential $E^°$ when balancing half-reactions (remember $E^°$ is intensive and does not scale with stoichiometry).
  • Confusing oxidation potentials with reduction potentials under the IUPAC sign convention.
  • Ignoring the activity coefficients and using raw molar concentrations in high ionic strength solutions.
  • Reversing the anode and cathode positions in the $E^°cell = Ecathode - Eanode$ formula.

⚡ Quick Revision: Single Electrodes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrode Potential Genesis

Single electrode potentials originate from phase boundary equilibria between metal atoms and solvated ions, establishing an electrical double layer.

Reference Electrodes

Primary standards like SHE and secondary standards like calomel and Ag/AgCl electrodes enable reproducible measurement of single electrode potentials.

Nernst Quantification

The Nernst equation links chemical activities of redox species directly to electrical potential differences under non-standard conditions.

Thermodynamic Functions

Electromotive force measurements provide direct access to thermodynamic quantities including standard free energy, enthalpy, and entropy changes.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Electrode Potential (E)
Measure of tendency of an electrode to lose or gain electrons, measured in Volts.
Standard Reduction Potential (E°)
Potential of a half-cell under standard state conditions (1 bar, 1 M, 298 K).
Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)
Primary reference half-cell with assigned potential of 0.00 V.
Activity (a)
Effective concentration of a species in a thermodynamic system, dimensionless.
Faraday Constant (F)
Charge carried by one mole of electrons, approximately 96485 C mol-1.
Electromotive Force (EMF)
Potential difference between two half-cells when no current flows, measured in Volts.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • SHE potential = 0.00 V at all temperatures.
  • IUPAC convention uses reduction potentials exclusively.
  • E° is an intensive property; ΔG° is an extensive property.
  • Nernst factor at 298 K = 0.0591 / n.
  • Spontaneous cell reaction requires E°cell > 0 and ΔG° < 0.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Single electrode potentials cannot be measured in isolation without a reference.
  • The Nernst equation quantitatively describes concentration and temperature dependence.
  • Standard potentials dictate oxidizing and reducing power trends across the electrochemical series.
  • Thermodynamic parameters are readily extracted from electrochemical cell measurements.

⚖️ Key Differences

Oxidation Potential VS Reduction Potential

Oxidation potential has the exact same magnitude as reduction potential but opposite sign ($Eox = -Ered$).

Question 1 of 18

What is the assigned standard reduction potential of the Standard Hydrogen Electrode at 298 K?

Explanation: By international convention, the standard hydrogen electrode potential is arbitrarily set to 0.00 V.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following represents the correct Nernst equation for a general reduction half-reaction Mn+ + ne- → M(s)?

Explanation: The activity of pure solid metal is unity, giving E = E° - (RT/nF)ln(1/[Mn+]) which simplifies to E = E° + (RT/nF)ln[Mn+].

Question 3 of 18

Why is the standard electrode potential considered an intensive property?

Explanation: Electrode potential measures the escaping tendency of electrons, which does not depend on the total quantity of material present.

Question 4 of 18

Which of the following is a secondary reference electrode?

Explanation: The Saturated Calomel Electrode (SCE) is widely used as a convenient secondary reference electrode.

Question 5 of 18

If the standard reduction potential of Zn2+/Zn is -0.76 V, what is its standard oxidation potential?

Explanation: Oxidation potential is the exact negative of reduction potential; hence, -(-0.76 V) = +0.76 V.

Question 6 of 18

What is the relationship between standard cell potential (E°cell) and equilibrium constant (K)?

Explanation: Since ΔG° = -nFE° and ΔG° = -RT ln K, combining them gives nFE° = RT ln K, which rearranges to E° = (2.303RT / nF) log K.

Question 7 of 18

Which metal can displace hydrogen gas from dilute hydrochloric acid?

Explanation: Metals with negative standard reduction potentials relative to hydrogen (like zinc) can displace H2 from acids.

Question 8 of 18

What happens to the reduction potential of a single electrode when the concentration of its metal ions is increased?

Explanation: According to the Nernst equation, increasing the reactant ion concentration shifts potential in the positive direction.

Question 9 of 18

What is the unit of molar Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) in electrochemical calculations?

Explanation: Gibbs free energy is a thermodynamic energy term expressed in J mol-1 or kJ mol-1.

Question 10 of 18

In the cell representation Pt(s) | H2(g) | H+(aq) || Cu2+(aq) | Cu(s), which electrode acts as the anode?

Explanation: By convention, the anode is written on the left side of the cell diagram.

Question 11 of 18

How does temperature affect the standard electrode potential of a half-cell?

Explanation: The variation of electrode potential with temperature is given by the temperature coefficient (dE/dT), related to reaction entropy.

Question 12 of 18

Which species is the strongest oxidizing agent among those with standard reduction potentials: F2 (+2.87 V), Cl2 (+1.36 V), Br2 (+1.07 V), I2 (+0.54 V)?

Explanation: The species with the highest positive standard reduction potential (+2.87 V for F2) is the strongest oxidizing agent.

Question 13 of 18

What is the value of the Faraday constant approximately equal to?

Explanation: The Faraday constant F is approximately 96485 C mol-1, commonly rounded to 96500 C mol-1.

Question 14 of 18

Under what condition is the cell potential (Ecell) equal to the standard cell potential (E°cell)?

Explanation: Standard conditions require unit activity (1 M for solutions, 1 bar for gases).

Question 15 of 18

Which half-cell reaction occurs at the cathode during the electrolysis of aqueous NaCl using inert electrodes?

Explanation: Reduction of water occurs more readily than reduction of Na+ ions at the cathode in aqueous solution due to standard potential values.

Question 16 of 18

What is the effect of adding a complexing agent that binds strongly to metal ions on the reduction potential of that metal electrode?

Explanation: Complexation lowers the free metal ion concentration, which shifts the electrode potential to a more negative (lower) value according to the Nernst equation.

Question 17 of 18

Which of the following is true for a galvanic cell at chemical equilibrium?

Explanation: At equilibrium, the cell potential drops to zero and no net work is done (ΔG = 0).

Question 18 of 18

Which reference electrode is sensitive to pH changes?

Explanation: The potential of the Standard Hydrogen Electrode depends directly on the hydrogen ion activity (pH).

UP PGT 2021
The standard reduction potential for Fe3+/Fe2+ is +0.77 V and for Fe2+/Fe is -0.44 V. The standard reduction potential for Fe3+/Fe is:
Answer: Option B (-0.04 V) Use ΔG° additivity: ΔG°3 = ΔG°1 + ΔG°2 → (-3)FE°3 = (-1)FE°1 + (-2)FE°2. Thus E°3 = (1(0.77) + 2(-0.44)) / 3 = (0.77 - 0.88) / 3 = -0.11 / 3 = -0.036 V ≈ -0.04 V.
UP PGT 2016
Which of the following expressions correctly gives the Nernst equation for a metal electrode Mn+ + ne- → M at 298 K?
Answer: Option A (E = E° + (0.0591 / n) log[Mn+]) Substituting natural log to base 10 gives E = E° - (0.0591/n) log(1/[Mn+]) = E° + (0.0591/n) log[Mn+].
UP PGT 2013
Standard reduction potentials of three metals X, Y, and Z are -1.2 V, +0.5 V, and -3.0 V respectively. The reducing power order is:
Answer: Option A (Z > X > Y) More negative reduction potential corresponds to a stronger reducing agent. Z (-3.0 V) > X (-1.2 V) > Y (+0.5 V).
UP PGT 2009
The EMF of a cell corresponding to the reaction Zn + 2H+ → Zn2+ + H2 is 0.28 V at 298 K with [H+] = 1 M. What is the concentration of Zn2+ if E°cell = 0.76 V?
Answer: Option B (10-25.7 M) Ecell = E°cell - (0.0591/2) log[Zn2+]. 0.28 = 0.76 - 0.02955 log[Zn2+] → log[Zn2+] = (0.28 - 0.76) / 0.02955 = -16.24 → [Zn2+] ≈ 10-25.7.
Model Question
A negative value of standard reduction potential of an electrode indicates that:
Answer: Option B (It is less easily reduced than hydrogen ion.) A negative standard reduction potential means the reduction is less favorable than that of H+, making it easier to oxidize.

Question 1 of 10
What is the potential of a hydrogen electrode immersed in a solution of pH = 3 at 298 K under 1 bar H2 pressure?

Solution: E = E° - (0.0591/1) log(1 / [H+]) = 0 - 0.0591 × pH = -0.0591 × 3 = -0.177 V.

Question 2 of 10
Which thermodynamic property is directly proportional to the cell EMF?

Solution: ΔG = -nFE links Gibbs free energy directly to cell potential.

Question 3 of 10
If the equilibrium constant for a 2-electron redox reaction is 104 at 298 K, what is the standard cell potential?

Solution: E° = (0.0591 / n) log K = (0.0591 / 2) × 4 = 0.0591 × 2 = 0.118 V.

Question 4 of 10
Why can absolute single electrode potential not be measured experimentally?

Solution: Complete circuits require both oxidation and oxidation-reduction coupling, preventing direct measurement of a single phase boundary potential.

Question 5 of 10
Calculate the cell potential for Cu | Cu2+ (0.01 M) || Cu2+ (0.1 M) | Cu at 298 K.

Solution: Concentration cell formula: E = -(0.0591 / 2) log(0.01 / 0.1) = -0.02955 log(0.1) = +0.02955 V.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following metals has the highest standard reduction potential?

Solution: Gold has a highly positive standard reduction potential (~ +1.50 V), making it extremely noble.

Question 7 of 10
What is the effect of increasing temperature on the cell potential of a reaction where ΔS is positive?

Solution: Since (∂E/∂T)P = ΔS / nF, a positive ΔS means potential increases with temperature.

Question 8 of 10
In the shorthand notation of an electrochemical cell, a single vertical line (|) represents:

Solution: A single vertical line denotes a phase boundary (e.g., solid metal in contact with aqueous solution), while a double vertical line represents a salt bridge.

Question 9 of 10
What is the value of n in the Nernst equation for the reduction of dichromate ion in acidic medium: Cr2O72- + 14H+ + 6e- → 2Cr3+ + 7H2O?

Solution: The balanced half-reaction involves the transfer of 6 moles of electrons, so n = 6.

Question 10 of 10
Which reference electrode is secondary, maintenance-free in terms of gas supply, and uses potassium chloride paste?

Solution: The Saturated Calomel Electrode uses Hg, Hg2Cl2, and KCl solution without requiring pressurized gas.
