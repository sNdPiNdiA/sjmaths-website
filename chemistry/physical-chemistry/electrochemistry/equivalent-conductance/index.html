Physical Chemistry

Equivalent Conductance

Equivalent conductance is defined as the conducting power of all the ions produced by one gram equivalent of an electrolyte dissolved in a specific volume of solution. It is quantitatively related to specific conductance and normality through the expression Λeq = (κ × 1000) / N.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Fundamental Definition and Concentration Dependence

  • Equivalent conductance (Λeq) decreases with an increase in concentration due to interionic forces of attraction.
  • Upon infinite dilution (C → 0), the ions are far apart, eliminating interionic hindrance and reaching maximum limiting equivalent conductance (Λeq°).
  • For strong electrolytes, the variation of Λeq with concentration follows the Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation: Λeq = Λeq° - (A + BΛeq°)c1/2.

Behavior of Strong versus Weak Electrolytes

  • Strong electrolytes exhibit a gradual increase in Λeq with dilution because complete dissociation already exists; only ionic mobilities alter slightly.
  • Weak electrolytes show a sharp increase in Λeq upon dilution owing to a substantial rise in the degree of dissociation (α).
  • The limiting equivalent conductance Λeq° for weak electrolytes cannot be obtained by direct extrapolation of the Λeq versus c1/2 curve and requires Kohlrausch's Law.

Applications of Equivalent Conductance

  • It permits the calculation of the degree of dissociation (α) of weak electrolytes at any given concentration C.
  • It enables the determination of the dissociation constant (Ka or Kb) of weak acids and bases.
  • It is utilized in calculating the solubility of sparingly soluble salts such as AgCl or BaSO4 using the relation S = (κ × 1000) / Λeq°.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Equivalent conductance increases continuously upon dilution for all electrolytes.

Observation: Very concentrated solutions of certain multi-valent electrolytes — Ion pair formation or complex ion association can temporarily reduce the effective number of independent conducting species before complete dissociation occurs upon further dilution.

Exception Strong electrolytes have constant molar and equivalent conductance ratios.

Observation: Polyvalent electrolytes like Al2(SO4)3 — The n-factor varies with concentration regimes due to incomplete stepwise dissociation or hydrolysis reactions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Equivalent conductance unit is S cm2 eq-1 or Ω-1 cm2 eq-1.
  • For monovalent electrolytes like NaCl or KCl, Molar Conductance equals Equivalent Conductance.
  • For divalent electrolytes like MgSO4, Λm = 2 × Λeq.
  • The Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation is strictly valid only for dilute solutions of strong electrolytes.
  • Kohlrausch's law is based on the independent migration of ions at infinite dilution.
  • The degree of dissociation α approaches 1 only at infinite dilution for weak electrolytes.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing normality with molarity when calculating equivalent conductance, leading to incorrect n-factor application.
  • Forgetting the factor of 1000 when converting cm3 to liters in numerical formulas.
  • Applying Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation directly to weak electrolytes where it fails completely.
  • Mixing up SI units (S m2 eq-1) and cgs units (S cm2 eq-1) in options during calculations.

⚡ Quick Revision: Equivalent Conductance

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition and Dimensions

Equivalent conductance measures the conducting capacity per gram equivalent of electrolyte. Its dimensions are S cm2 eq-1.

Concentration Effects

Both strong and weak electrolytes show an increase in equivalent conductance upon dilution, approaching a limiting value Λeq°.

Kohlrausch's Application

Limiting equivalent conductances are additive properties of the constituent cations and anions as governed by Kohlrausch's Law.

Electrolyte Strength Evaluation

The ratio of equivalent conductance at concentration C to that at infinite dilution yields the degree of dissociation for weak electrolytes.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Equivalent Conductance (Λeq)
Conductance of 1 gram equivalent of electrolyte in solution. Unit: S cm2 eq-1.
Specific Conductance (κ)
Conductance of a 1 cm cube of electrolyte solution. Unit: S cm-1.
Normality (N)
Number of gram equivalents of solute per liter of solution. Unit: eq L-1.
Limiting Equivalent Conductance (Λeq°)
Equivalent conductance at zero concentration or infinite dilution.
Degree of Dissociation (α)
Fraction of total electrolyte molecules dissociated into ions.
Cell Constant (G* = l/A)
Ratio of distance between electrodes to cross-sectional area. Unit: cm-1.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Λeq = (κ × 1000) / N
  • Λm = nf × Λeq
  • α = Λeq / Λeq°
  • Kc = Cα2 / (1 - α)
  • Kohlrausch's Law: Λeq° = λ_+° + λ_-°

📌 Quick Recall

  • Equivalent conductance measures equivalent ion transport capacity.
  • Dilution increases equivalent conductance by reducing interionic forces or increasing dissociation.
  • Strong electrolytes plot linearly with c1/2, whereas weak electrolytes show sharp non-linear rises.
  • Kohlrausch law allows calculation of equivalent conductance at infinite dilution for weak electrolytes.

⚖️ Key Differences

Molar Conductance (Λm) VS Equivalent Conductance (Λeq)

Molar conductance refers to 1 mole of substance, whereas equivalent conductance refers to 1 gram equivalent of substance.

Question 1 of 18

What is the SI unit of equivalent conductance?

Explanation: The SI unit for equivalent conductance is Siemens meter squared per equivalent (S m2 eq-1).

Question 2 of 18

How is equivalent conductance (Λeq) related to molar conductance (Λm) for BaCl2?

Explanation: For BaCl2, the n-factor (valency of barium or total positive charge) is 2. Therefore, Λm = 2 Λeq.

Question 3 of 18

Which of the following equations represents the Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation for equivalent conductance?

Explanation: The Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation accounts for electrophilic and relaxation effects: Λeq = Λeq° - (A + BΛeq°)c1/2.

Question 4 of 18

What happens to the equivalent conductance of a weak electrolyte on infinite dilution?

Explanation: Upon infinite dilution, dissociation of weak electrolytes becomes 100%, and equivalent conductance reaches its maximum limiting value (Λeq°).

Question 5 of 18

The equivalent conductance of a 0.1 N KCl solution is 120 S cm2 eq-1. What is its specific conductance (κ)?

Explanation: Using Λeq = (κ × 1000) / N, we get κ = (120 × 0.1) / 1000 = 0.012 S cm-1.

Question 6 of 18

Kohlrausch's law is applicable to:

Explanation: Kohlrausch's law applies universally to find limiting molar or equivalent conductances for both strong and weak electrolytes.

Question 7 of 18

For a weak electrolyte, the degree of dissociation (α) is given by:

Explanation: The degree of dissociation is the ratio of equivalent conductance at concentration C to that at infinite dilution: α = Λeq / Λeq°.

Question 8 of 18

Why does equivalent conductance increase with dilution for strong electrolytes?

Explanation: Strong electrolytes are fully dissociated; dilution increases the distance between ions, reducing interionic electrostatic attraction and increasing ionic mobility.

Question 9 of 18

What is the unit of specific conductance (κ) used in equivalent conductance calculations?

Explanation: Specific conductance is expressed in Siemens per centimeter (S cm-1) when using cgs units.

Question 10 of 18

If the equivalent conductance of AlCl3 is related to its molar conductance, what is the correct relation?

Explanation: AlCl3 dissociates into Al3+ and 3Cl-, giving a total positive charge (n-factor) of 3. Thus, Λm = 3 Λeq.

Question 11 of 18

Which graphical plot is linear for strong electrolytes in equivalent conductance studies?

Explanation: According to the Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation, Λeq varies linearly with c1/2 for strong electrolytes.

Question 12 of 18

The dissociation constant (K) of a weak electrolyte in terms of equivalent conductance is:

Explanation: Substituting α = Λeq / Λeq° into K = cα2 / (1 - α) yields K = cΛeq2 / [Λeq°(Λeq° - Λeq)].

Question 13 of 18

What is the effect of temperature on equivalent conductance?

Explanation: An increase in temperature decreases solvent viscosity and increases ionic mobility, thereby increasing equivalent conductance.

Question 14 of 18

For a sparingly soluble salt, solubility (S) in terms of equivalent conductance at infinite dilution is:

Explanation: At saturation, concentration equals solubility S (eq L-1), giving S = (κ × 1000) / Λeq°.

Question 15 of 18

Why cannot the limiting equivalent conductance of a weak electrolyte be found by direct extrapolation?

Explanation: The curve for weak electrolytes shoots up steeply near zero concentration, making accurate extrapolation impossible.

Question 16 of 18

What is the equivalent conductance of 0.05 M H2SO4 if its specific conductance is 0.022 S cm-1?

Explanation: Normality of 0.05 M H2SO4 is 0.05 × 2 = 0.1 N. Then Λeq = (0.022 × 1000) / 0.1 = 220 S cm2 eq-1.

Question 17 of 18

Which ion possesses the highest ionic mobility in aqueous solution at infinite dilution?

Explanation: Hydrated hydrogen ion (H+) exhibits exceptional mobility via the Grotthuss proton-jump mechanism.

Question 18 of 18

In the measurement of equivalent conductance, what does the term '1000' represent?

Explanation: The factor '1000' converts cubic centimeters (cm3) to liters in the denominator of the concentration term.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the equivalent conductance of a 0.02 N solution of an electrolyte having specific conductance 2.4 × 10-2 S cm-1.

Solution: Λeq = (2.4 × 10-2 × 1000) / 0.02 = 24 / 0.02 = 1200 S cm2 eq-1.

Question 2 of 10
If the limiting equivalent conductance of CH3COOH is 390.5 S cm2 eq-1 and its equivalent conductance at 0.01 M is 48.1 S cm2 eq-1, what is the degree of dissociation?

Solution: α = Λeq / Λeq° = 48.1 / 390.5 = 0.123.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following expressions correctly links solubility S of AgCl with equivalent conductance at infinite dilution (Λeq°) and specific conductance (κ)?

Solution: Solubility in eq L-1 is given by S = (κ × 1000) / Λeq°.

Question 4 of 10
What is the unit conversion factor from S cm2 eq-1 to SI units (S m2 eq-1)?

Solution: 1 S cm2 eq-1 = 10-4 S m2 eq-1.

Question 5 of 10
Equivalent conductance of a weak acid HA at concentration C is 10 S cm2 eq-1 and Λeq° is 200 S cm2 eq-1. What is the dissociation constant Ka if C = 0.01 eq L-1?

Solution: α = 10 / 200 = 0.05. Ka = Cα2 / (1 - α) = (0.01 × 0.052) / 0.95 = 2.63 × 10-5 ≈ 2.5 × 10-5.

Question 6 of 10
How does equivalent conductance change upon addition of excess solvent to a strong electrolyte solution?

Solution: For strong electrolytes, equivalent conductance increases slightly with dilution and approaches a limiting maximum value (Λeq°).

Question 7 of 10
For which electrolyte is the ratio Λm / Λeq equal to 3?

Solution: AlCl3 has an n-factor of 3, so Λm = 3 Λeq.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following factors causes a decrease in equivalent conductance?

Solution: Increasing concentration increases interionic attractions, retarding ionic movement and decreasing equivalent conductance.

Question 9 of 10
What is the equivalent conductance of 0.005 M CaSO4 if its specific conductance is 1.1 × 10-3 S cm-1?

Solution: Normality = 0.005 × 2 = 0.01 N. Λeq = (1.1 × 10-3 × 1000) / 0.01 = 110 S cm2 eq-1.

Question 10 of 10
According to Kohlrausch's law, the limiting equivalent conductance of an electrolyte AB is equal to:

Solution: Kohlrausch's law states that limiting equivalent conductance is the sum of individual ionic equivalent conductances: λA+° + λB-°.
