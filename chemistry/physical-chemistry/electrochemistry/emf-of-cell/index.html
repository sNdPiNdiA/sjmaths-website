Physical Chemistry

EMF of a Cell

The electromotive force (EMF) of a galvanic cell is the potential difference between two electrodes when no current flows through the circuit. It represents the maximum potential work obtainable from a chemical reaction occurring within the cell.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Basis of Cell EMF

  • The electromotive force (EMF) drives electrons from the anode (oxidation) to the cathode (reduction) through an external circuit.
  • The spontaneity of a cell reaction requires a positive EMF (Ecell > 0) and a negative Gibbs free energy change (ΔG < 0).
  • Standard cell potential is an intensive property, meaning its value does not depend on the stoichiometric coefficients used to balance the half-reactions.
  • The temperature coefficient of a cell ((∂E/∂T)P) relates cell EMF changes to the reaction entropy change via ΔS = nF(∂E/∂T)P.

Concentration Dependence and the Nernst Equation

  • The Nernst equation quantifies how cell potential varies with the concentration of reactant and product species in solution.
  • Reaction quotient Q includes dissolved species and gases, while pure solids and liquids are assigned an activity of unity.
  • At chemical equilibrium, the cell potential drops to zero (Ecell = 0) and the reaction quotient equals the equilibrium constant (Q = Kc).
  • Concentration cells generate EMF solely due to a difference in concentration between the two compartments, with Ecell° equal to zero.

Measurement and Calculation of Standard Potentials

  • Standard electrode potentials are measured relative to the Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE), which is arbitrarily assigned a potential of exactly 0.00 V.
  • A positive reduction potential indicates that the species is a stronger oxidizing agent than H+ and gets reduced more easily than H+.
  • A negative reduction potential indicates that the species is a weaker oxidizing agent than H+ and acts as a reducing agent.
  • The overall cell representation follows the IUPAC left-hand oxidation, right-hand reduction (or anode-salt bridge-cathode) convention.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Nernst Equation for Cell Potential
Ecell = Ecell° - (RT / nF) ln Q
Conditions: Non-standard temperature, pressure, or concentration
Units: R in J K-1 mol-1, T in K, F in C mol-1, E in V
Gibbs Free Energy and Cell EMF
ΔG = -nFEcell
Conditions: Constant temperature and pressure
Units: ΔG in J mol-1, F in C mol-1, E in V
Equilibrium Constant and Standard EMF
log Kc = (n Ecell°) / 0.0591
Conditions: At 298 K when equilibrium is reached (Ecell = 0)
Units: Kc is dimensionless, E in V
Nernst Equation at 298 K (Base 10)
Ecell = Ecell° - (0.0591 / n) log([Product] / [Reactant])
Conditions: Temperature = 298 K
Units: Concentrations in mol L-1, E in V
Cell EMF from Standard Electrode Potentials
Ecell° = Ecathode° - Eanode°
Conditions: Standard conditions: 298 K, 1 bar pressure, 1 M concentration
Units: E in Volts (V)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Cell potential is independent of the stoichiometric coefficients of the balanced equation.

Observation: Multiplying half-reactions changes ΔG and n, but standard reduction potential (E°) remains unaltered. — E° is an intensive property representing work per unit charge, whereas ΔG is an extensive property dependent on total moles reacted.

Exception Higher concentration of reactants always increases cell potential.

Observation: If the reactant is a product in the Nernst log term denominator, increasing its concentration decreases the cell potential. — The logarithmic term shifts according to Le Chatelier's principle, lowering the net driving force when product concentration builds up.

Exception Standard hydrogen electrode operates at 1 atm pressure.

Observation: Real gases deviate from ideal behavior at high pressures, requiring fugacity instead of partial pressure in exact calculations. — Intermolecular forces at high pressures cause deviations from the idealized gas laws assumed in standard potential derivations.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Standard reduction potential of SHE is defined as 0.00 V at all temperatures.
  • The unit of electrical work output from a cell is Joules (J), calculated as wmax = nFEcell.
  • Standard cell potential does not change when the stoichiometric equation is multiplied by an integer.
  • Equilibrium constant Kc for a cell reaction can exceed 1 when Ecell° is positive.
  • In a concentration cell, the standard EMF (Ecell°) is always zero.
  • The potential of a half-cell depends on temperature, pressure of gases, and ionic concentration.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Using extensive values of ΔG directly to calculate standard reduction potential without accounting for the number of electrons (n).
  • Subtracting standard potentials incorrectly instead of using Ecell° = Ereduction(cathode)° - Ereduction(anode)°.
  • Including pure solids, pure liquids, or solvents in the expression for the reaction quotient Q in the Nernst equation.
  • Confusing the signs of electrode potentials when reversing a half-reaction; oxidation potential is the negative of reduction potential.

⚡ Quick Revision: EMF of a Cell

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electromotive Force and Cell Representation

A galvanic cell is represented by placing the anode on the left and the cathode on the right, separated by a salt bridge represented by a double vertical line (||).

Thermodynamic Functions in Electrochemistry

Cell EMF links electrical energy to thermodynamic state functions through ΔG = -nFEcell, ΔH, and ΔS relationships.

The Nernst Equation Applications

The Nernst equation allows calculation of cell potentials under non-standard conditions and determination of unknown ion concentrations or pH values.

Electrochemical Series

Arranging elements by standard reduction potential yields the electrochemical series, predicting the feasibility of metal displacement and redox reactions.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

EMF (Ecell)
Maximum potential difference between electrodes when no current flows. Unit: Volt (V).
Faraday Constant (F)
Charge carried by one mole of electrons, approximately 96485 C mol-1.
Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)
Reference electrode with a potential defined as 0.00 V. Unit: Volt (V).
Reaction Quotient (Q)
Ratio of product activities to reactant activities at any given time. Unit: Dimensionless.
Gibbs Free Energy (ΔG)
Maximum non-expansion work available from a system. Unit: J mol-1.
Equilibrium Constant (Kc)
Ratio of concentrations at chemical equilibrium. Unit: Dimensionless.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ecell° = Ecathode° - Eanode° using reduction potentials.
  • At chemical equilibrium, Ecell = 0 and Q = Kc.
  • ΔG° = -nFEcell° governs reaction spontaneity.
  • Faraday constant F = 96485 C mol-1.
  • Concentration cells have Ecell° = 0.

📌 Quick Recall

  • EMF measures the driving force of redox reactions in galvanic cells.
  • Nernst equation connects cell potential to concentration and temperature.
  • Intensive property E° does not depend on reaction stoichiometry.
  • Electrochemical series predicts oxidizing and reducing strengths.

⚖️ Key Differences

Oxidation Potential VS Reduction Potential

Oxidation potential is the negative of reduction potential for the same half-reaction according to IUPAC conventions.

Question 1 of 18

What is the standard potential of the Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)?

Explanation: By international convention, the standard reduction potential of the SHE is assigned an arbitrary value of exactly 0.00 V at all temperatures.

Question 2 of 18

For a spontaneous cell reaction, which of the following conditions must be met?

Explanation: A spontaneous electrochemical process requires a positive cell EMF (Ecell > 0) which corresponds to a negative Gibbs free energy change (Delta G < 0).

Question 3 of 18

How does multiplying a half-reaction by 2 affect its standard reduction potential (E°)?

Explanation: Standard reduction potential is an intensive property and does not depend on the stoichiometric coefficients used to balance the half-reaction.

Question 4 of 18

What is the value of Ecell for a concentration cell when equilibrium is reached?

Explanation: At equilibrium, concentrations in both compartments become equal or the net driving force ceases, resulting in a cell potential of zero (Ecell = 0).

Question 5 of 18

Which equation represents the correct relation between standard cell EMF and equilibrium constant at 298 K?

Explanation: Rearranging Delta G° = -nFE° = -2.303 RT log Kc at 298 K yields E° = (0.0591 / n) log Kc.

Question 6 of 18

In the cell notation Zn(s) | Zn2+(aq) || Cu2+(aq) | Cu(s), which species undergoes oxidation?

Explanation: Oxidation always occurs at the anode, which is written on the left side of the salt bridge in standard cell notation. Thus, solid zinc (Zn) oxidizes to Zn2+.

Question 7 of 18

What happens to the cell potential if the concentration of products is increased in a galvanic cell?

Explanation: According to the Nernst equation, increasing product concentration increases the logarithmic term, which subtracts a larger value from E°, thereby decreasing Ecell.

Question 8 of 18

What is the unit of the Faraday constant (F)?

Explanation: The Faraday constant represents the total electrical charge carried by one mole of electrons, expressed in Coulombs per mole (C mol-1).

Question 9 of 18

What is the standard cell EMF of a concentration cell?

Explanation: In a concentration cell, both electrodes are made of the same material dipping into solutions of the same ions at different concentrations, making Ecell° = 0 V.

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following is an intensive property?

Explanation: Standard electrode potential is independent of the amount of substance reacting, making it an intensive property, unlike Delta G which is extensive.

Question 11 of 18

If the standard reduction potentials of X2+/X and Y2+/Y are -0.76 V and +0.34 V respectively, what is the standard cell potential?

Explanation: Ecell° = Ecathode° - Eanode° = 0.34 V - (-0.76 V) = +1.10 V.

Question 12 of 18

Which metal can displace copper from copper sulfate solution based on standard reduction potentials (Cu = +0.34 V, Zn = -0.76 V, Ag = +0.80 V)?

Explanation: A metal with a more negative reduction potential can displace a metal with a more positive reduction potential. Zinc (-0.76 V) displaces copper (+0.34 V).

Question 13 of 18

The temperature coefficient of a reversible cell is given by:

Explanation: The temperature coefficient of a cell EMF is defined as the rate of change of EMF with temperature at constant pressure, represented as (dE/dT)P.

Question 14 of 18

What is the maximum work obtainable from a galvanic cell equal to?

Explanation: The maximum non-expansion work obtainable from a galvanic cell is equal to the decrease in Gibbs free energy (-Delta G = nFEcell).

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following conditions represents standard state conditions for electrochemistry?

Explanation: Standard states are strictly defined at 298.15 K, 1 bar pressure for gases, and 1 M concentration for dissolved species.

Question 16 of 18

In the Nernst equation, what does 'n' represent?

Explanation: In both the Nernst equation and the Gibbs-EMF relation, 'n' denotes the total number of moles of electrons exchanged in the balanced redox equation.

Question 17 of 18

What is the sign of Ecell for an electrolytic cell?

Explanation: An electrolytic cell drives non-spontaneous reactions, requiring an applied external potential greater than the negative cell EMF of the reaction.

Question 18 of 18

Which half-reaction takes place at the cathode of a galvanic cell?

Explanation: Reduction always occurs at the cathode in both galvanic and electrolytic cells (remember Red Cat).

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the standard EMF of a cell where the cathode reduction potential is +0.80 V and the anode reduction potential is -0.76 V.

Solution: Ecell° = Ecathode° - Eanode° = 0.80 - (-0.76) = +1.56 V.

Question 2 of 10
What happens to the cell EMF when a cell reaches chemical equilibrium?

Solution: At chemical equilibrium, the forward and reverse reaction rates are equal, resulting in zero potential difference (Ecell = 0).

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following ions has the highest standard reduction potential in the electrochemical series?

Solution: Fluorine gas (F2 / F-) possesses the highest standard reduction potential (+2.87 V), making it the strongest oxidizing agent.

Question 4 of 10
If Delta G° for a cell reaction is -212.3 kJ mol-1 and n = 2, what is the standard cell potential?

Solution: E° = -Delta G° / nF = 212300 J / (2 * 96485 C) = 1.10 V.

Question 5 of 10
Which species acts as the strongest reducing agent among standard reduction potentials: Li+ (-3.05 V), Zn2+ (-0.76 V), Cu2+ (+0.34 V), F2 (+2.87 V)?

Solution: Lithium metal (Li) corresponds to the most negative reduction potential (-3.05 V), making it the strongest reducing agent.

Question 6 of 10
What is the effect of increasing temperature on an endothermic cell reaction with a positive temperature coefficient?

Solution: According to the thermodynamic relation dE/dT = Delta S / nF, a positive entropy change (endothermic reaction) results in increased EMF with rising temperature.

Question 7 of 10
In a Daniel cell, electrons flow from:

Solution: Zinc acts as the anode (oxidation) and copper as the cathode (reduction), so electrons flow externally from zinc to copper.

Question 8 of 10
What is the value of reaction quotient Q when reactant and product concentrations are all equal to 1 M?

Solution: When all concentrations are 1 M, Q = 1 / 1 = 1. Since log(1) = 0, the Nernst equation simplifies to Ecell = Ecell°.

Question 9 of 10
Which material is commonly used as a salt bridge electrolyte because its cation and anion transport numbers are nearly identical?

Solution: Potassium chloride (KCl) is preferred in salt bridges because K+ and Cl- have nearly equal ionic mobilities and transport numbers.

Question 10 of 10
The equilibrium constant Kc of a redox reaction is 104 at 298 K. What is the approximate standard cell potential (Ecell°) for n = 2?

Solution: E° = (0.0591 / n) log Kc = (0.0591 / 2) 4 = 0.02955 4 = 0.118 V (~0.12 V).
