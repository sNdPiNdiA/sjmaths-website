Chemical Cells Without Transference

Chemical cells without transference are galvanic cells designed to eliminate liquid junction potential by bringing the reacting electrolytes into direct contact or using a common ion species. Their electromotive force is determined solely by the thermodynamic activities of the reactants and products without any contribution from ionic transference numbers.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Basis and Cell Construction

  • Cells without transference contain no liquid junction boundary, thereby eliminating liquid junction potentials entirely.
  • The overall cell reaction is obtained by summing the individual electrode reactions without any intermediate diffusion layer.
  • The measured electromotive force E depends strictly on the initial and final thermodynamic states of the system.
  • Such cells typically employ a common ion or a single electrolyte solution surrounding both electrodes to avoid mixing boundaries.

Harned Cell and Mean Ionic Activity Determination

  • The classic example is the Harned cell: Pt | H2 (g, 1 atm) | HCl (m) | AgCl (s) | Ag.
  • The cell reaction is given by: 1/2 H2 (g) + AgCl (s) → Ag (s) + H+ (aq) + Cl- (aq).
  • The EMF expression contains the product of molality and mean ionic activity coefficient: m2γ±2.
  • This configuration permits the precise experimental determination of mean activity coefficients of strong electrolytes without transference number data.

Elimination of Liquid Junction Potential

  • In cells with transference, the junction potential arises from differing ionic mobilities across the phase boundary.
  • Cells without transference bypass this error by eliminating the physical boundary between two different electrolyte solutions.
  • They provide more accurate thermodynamic data for calculating Gibbs free energy, enthalpy, and entropy changes of reactions.
  • Electrode reversibility to both cations and anions in the same compartment is a prerequisite for successful operation.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Gibbs Free Energy Change
ΔG = -nFE
Conditions: Constant temperature and pressure, reversible electrochemical process
Units: ΔG in Joules or kJ mol-1, F in C mol-1, E in Volts
Harned Cell Equation (Activity Coefficient)
E = E° - (2RT / F) ln(m ± γ ±)
Conditions: For cells like Pt | H2(g) | HCl(m) | AgCl(s) | Ag
Units: m in mol kg-1, γ ± is mean ionic activity coefficient
Standard Cell Potential and Equilibrium Constant
ln K = (nFE°) / (RT)
Conditions: At electrochemical equilibrium when Ecell = 0
Units: K is dimensionless, E° in Volts
Temperature Coefficient of EMF
ΔS = nF (∂E / ∂T)p
Conditions: At constant pressure
Units: ΔS in J K-1 mol-1, (∂E / ∂T) in V K-1
Electromotive Force of Cell Without Transference
E = E° - (RT / nF) ∑ νi ln(ai)
Conditions: Applicable for any reversible cell reaction at constant temperature and pressure
Units: E in Volts, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin, F in C mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Cell EMF depends solely on total electrolyte concentration in ideal solutions

Observation: Real concentrated solutions deviate significantly due to interionic forces — Activity coefficients depart from unity at high ionic strengths, requiring Debye-Hückel limiting law corrections.

Exception Hydrogen gas electrodes maintain constant potential at 1 atm pressure

Observation: Catalyst poisoning occurs readily in the presence of trace impurities like sulfur or arsenic — Platinized platinum surface loses its active adsorption sites, destroying reversible hydrogen oxidation/reduction.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Cells without transference completely eliminate the liquid junction potential.
  • The Harned cell is represented as Pt | H2(g) | HCl(m) | AgCl(s) | Ag.
  • The cell reaction for the Harned cell involves the formation of aqueous HCl from hydrogen gas and silver chloride.
  • Mean ionic activity coefficients (γ±) are accurately determined using cells without transference.
  • The Nernst equation for these cells includes the mean activity term squared for a 1:1 electrolyte.
  • Temperature dependence of EMF in these cells yields the standard entropy change (ΔS) directly.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming liquid junction potential exists in cells without transference; remember it is identically zero.
  • Omitting the stoichiometric coefficients when writing the reaction quotient for activity calculations.
  • Confusing molality (m) with molarity (M) when applying Harned cell activity equations.
  • Neglecting gas fugacity corrections at high pressures for the hydrogen electrode.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Cells Without Transference

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Principle of Cells Without Transference

These cells operate without any physical boundary or salt bridge between different electrolyte solutions, completely avoiding liquid junction potentials.

Thermodynamic Evaluation

By measuring cell EMF as a function of molality, accurate mean ionic activity coefficients and equilibrium constants are evaluated without transference numbers.

The Harned Cell Design

The standard hydrogen-silver chloride cell represents the benchmark system for measuring thermodynamic properties of hydrochloric acid solutions.

Advantages Over Transference Cells

Absence of diffusion potentials yields cleaner thermodynamic data that directly reflect pure chemical potentials of the electrolyte components.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Liquid Junction Potential (Ej)
Potential difference arising across the boundary between two different electrolyte solutions in Volts.
Mean Ionic Activity Coefficient (γ±)
Effective concentration correction factor for ions in solution, dimensionless.
Harned Cell (Pt | H2 | HCl | AgCl | Ag)
Standard chemical cell without transference used for thermodynamic studies.
Molality (m)
Number of moles of solute per kilogram of solvent, mol kg-1.
Standard EMF (E°)
Electromotive force of a cell under standard state conditions in Volts.
Reaction Quotient (Q)
Ratio of activities of products to reactants at any given point, dimensionless.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Liquid junction potential is zero in cells without transference.
  • Harned cell reaction: 1/2 H2 + AgCl → Ag + H+ + Cl-.
  • ΔG = -nFE is the fundamental link between electrochemistry and thermodynamics.
  • Mean ionic activity for 1:1 electrolyte is a product of ion activities: a±2 = a+a-.
  • Faraday constant F is approximately 96485 C mol-1.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Cells without transference avoid junction mixing errors.
  • Activity coefficients of strong electrolytes are derived precisely.
  • Electrode reactions must be reversible to both ionic species.
  • Temperature coefficients give direct entropy and enthalpy changes.

⚖️ Key Differences

Cell with transference VS Cell without transference

Cells with transference contain a liquid junction boundary with a diffusion potential, whereas cells without transference do not.

For a 1:1 electrolyte like HCl in a Harned cell, the Nernst equation involves the mean activity term raised to what power?

Question 10 of 19

Explanation: Salt bridges are used to minimize liquid junction potentials in cells with transference; cells without transference eliminate this requirement.

Question 1 of 10
In a cell without transference, what eliminates the liquid junction potential?

Solution: Liquid junction potential is eliminated because there is no physical boundary or junction between two distinct electrolyte solutions.

Question 2 of 10
Calculate the free energy change (ΔG°) for a cell reaction where n = 2 and E° = 1.10 V (F = 96485 C mol-1).

Solution: ΔG° = -nFE° = -(2 mol)(96485 C mol-1)(1.10 V) = -212,267 J mol-1 = -212.3 kJ mol-1.

Question 3 of 10
The Harned cell configuration utilizes which of the following electrodes?

Solution: The Harned cell consists of a hydrogen gas electrode and a silver-silver chloride electrode.

Question 4 of 10
Which thermodynamic parameter cannot be directly obtained from temperature dependence of cell EMF?

Solution: Temperature coefficient of EMF yields entropy (ΔS) and via combination with EMF, enthalpy (ΔH) and free energy (ΔG). Activation energy requires kinetic measurements.

Question 5 of 10
What is the primary reason for studying cells without transference in physical chemistry?

Solution: Their primary utility is the rigorous thermodynamic evaluation of activity coefficients free from transference number uncertainties.

Question 6 of 10
If the EMF of a cell without transference is independent of concentration, what is the value of n? (Assume standard conditions)

Solution: EMF dependency on concentration is dictated by the Nernst equation; independence implies standard conditions or zero reaction quotient change, but n represents electrons transferred.

Question 7 of 10
In the Nernst equation for a cell without transference, how does the activity of a pure solid (like AgCl or Ag) appear?

Solution: Activities of pure solids and pure liquids in their standard states are taken as unity.

Question 8 of 10
What is the relationship between the equilibrium constant K and standard cell potential E°?

Solution: From ln K = (nFE°) / (RT), K is related exponentially to E°.

Question 9 of 10
Which electrolyte is most commonly investigated using the standard Harned cell?

Solution: Hydrochloric acid (HCl) is the standard electrolyte investigated using the Harned cell.

Question 10 of 10
What happens to the measured EMF when a cell without transference reaches thermodynamic equilibrium?

Solution: At electrochemical equilibrium, the net cell potential is zero (Ecell = 0).
