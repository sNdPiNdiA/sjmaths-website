Molecular Conductance

Molecular conductance, also termed molar conductivity (Λm), quantifies the conducting power of all the ions produced by dissolving one mole of an electrolyte in solution. It depends directly on the electrolytic concentration and the degree of dissociation.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Definition and Units of Molar Conductivity

  • Molar conductance (Λm) is defined as the conducting power of the ions produced by one mole of an electrolyte placed between two parallel electrodes spaced 1 cm apart.
  • The standard SI unit for molar conductivity is S m2 mol-1, though historically and commonly expressed in S cm2 mol-1.
  • The relationship linking molar conductivity to specific conductivity (κ) is given by Λm = (κ × 1000) / C where C is the molarity.

Concentration Dependence and Dilution Effects

  • Molar conductance increases with dilution for both strong and weak electrolytes due to a decrease in interionic electrostatic forces of attraction or an increase in degree of dissociation.
  • For strong electrolytes, the plot of Λm versus √c is linear, allowing extrapolation to zero concentration to obtain Λm∞.
  • For weak electrolytes, the plot shows a sharp upward curve near infinite dilution, preventing accurate graphical determination of Λm∞.

Kohlrausch Law and Applications

  • Kohlrausch Law states that the limiting molar conductivity of an electrolyte is the sum of the contributions from its constituent cations and anions.
  • It is utilized to calculate the limiting molar conductivity of weak electrolytes like acetic acid using strong electrolyte values.
  • It also permits the experimental determination of the degree of dissociation (α) and the dissociation constant (Ka) of weak acids.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Molar Conductivity with Volume
Λm = κ × V
Conditions: V is the volume in cm3 containing 1 mole of electrolyte
Units: Λm in S cm2 mol-1, V in cm3 mol-1
Debye-Hckel-Onsager Equation
Λm = Λm∞ - (A + BΛm∞)√c
Conditions: Valid only for strong electrolytes at very low concentrations
Units: c in mol L-1, A and B are constants dependent on solvent viscosity and dielectric constant
Kohlrausch Law of Independent Migration
Λm∞(AxBy) = xλcation∞ + yλanion∞
Conditions: At infinite dilution where dissociation is complete
Units: S cm2 mol-1
Degree of Dissociation
α = ΛmΛm∞
Conditions: Applicable primarily to weak electrolytes
Units: Dimensionless fraction
Molar Conductivity Definition
Λm = κ × 1000C
Conditions: C is concentration in mol L-1, κ is specific conductance
Units: S cm2 mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Molar conductivity increases continuously upon dilution for all electrolyte types.

Observation: Molten salts or concentrated ionic liquids at high temperatures show complex deviations. — Ion pair formation and high viscosity effects alter mobility profiles.

Exception Strong electrolytes yield linear plots in Λm vs √c graphs.

Observation: Asymmetric strong electrolytes (e.g., MgSO4) exhibit steeper slopes than symmetric electrolytes (e.g., KCl). — Higher ionic charges (z+z-) intensify interionic electrostatic interactions.

Unit Conversion Shortcut
1 S m2 mol-1 = 104 S cm2 mol-1

Application: Quickly convert SI units to conventional CGS units in numerical problems by multiplying by 104.

Ostwald Dilution Law Link
Ka = cα2 / (1 - α) ⇒ Ka = c(Λm / Λm∞)2 / (1 - Λm / Λm∞)

Application: Directly substitute conductance ratios to find acid dissociation constants.

Kohlrausch Calculation Mnemonic
ΛCH3COOH = ΛCH3COONa + ΛHCl - ΛNaCl

Application: Cancel out spectator ions by combining strong electrolyte terms to find the weak acid limiting conductivity.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Molar conductance increases with increase in dilution because volume containing 1 mole increases.
  • Specific conductance (κ) decreases with dilution due to decrease in number of ions per unit volume.
  • Limiting molar conductivity (Λm∞) represents conductance at infinite dilution where interionic forces approach zero.
  • Unit of molar conductance is S cm2 mol-1 or S m2 mol-1.
  • Debye-Hckel-Onsager equation applies strictly to dilute solutions of strong electrolytes.
  • Degree of dissociation α is the ratio Λm / Λm∞.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing specific conductance (κ) with molar conductance (Λm); κ decreases on dilution while Λm increases.
  • Forgetting the factor of 1000 when concentration is given in mol L-1 and distance in cm.
  • Attempting to extrapolate Λm vs √c graph for weak electrolytes to find Λm∞.
  • Incorrect stoichiometric coefficients when applying Kohlrausch Law to electrolytes like CaCl2 or Al2(SO4)3.

⚡ Quick Revision: Molecular Conductance

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Molar Conductance Fundamentals

Molar conductance measures the efficiency of ions produced by one mole of an electrolyte in conducting electricity, governed by Λm = κ × 1000 / C.

Concentration Effects

Dilution increases molar conductivity by reducing interionic retarding forces in strong electrolytes and increasing ionization in weak electrolytes.

Kohlrausch's Law

Independent migration of ions at infinite dilution enables indirect calculation of molar conductivities for unionized or poorly dissociated species.

Electrolytic Dissociation

The ratio of molar conductance at a given concentration to that at infinite dilution yields the degree of dissociation for weak electrolytes.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Molar Conductance (Λm)
Conducting power of 1 mole of electrolyte. S cm2 mol-1.
Specific Conductance (κ)
Conductance of 1 cm3 of solution. S cm-1.
Equivalent Conductance (Λeq)
Conducting power of 1 gram equivalent of electrolyte. S cm2 equiv-1.
Infinite Dilution (c → 0)
State where ionic interactions are negligible and conductance is maximal.
Degree of Dissociation (α)
Fraction of total electrolyte dissociated into ions.
Cell Constant (G* = l/A)
Ratio of distance between electrodes to cross-sectional area. cm-1.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Λm = κ × 1000 / C
  • Λm∞(CH3COOH) = λCH3COO-∞ + λH+∞
  • α = Λm / Λm∞
  • κ decreases upon dilution, whereas Λm increases
  • Debye-Hckel-Onsager equation: Λm = Λm∞ - b√c

📌 Quick Recall

  • Molar conductance scales with volume containing one mole of solute.
  • Strong electrolytes obey Debye-Hckel-Onsager equation at low concentrations.
  • Weak electrolytes require Kohlrausch Law for limiting molar conductance determination.
  • Conductance measurements provide direct evaluation of dissociation constants.

⚖️ Key Differences

Specific Conductance (κ) VS Molar Conductance (Λm)

κ is for a unit volume of solution, while Λm is for a volume containing one mole of electrolyte.

What is the correct unit of molar conductivity in SI units?

Which equation is applicable to strong electrolytes for extrapolation to zero concentration?

Explanation: Kohlrausch law can be applied to calculate limiting molar conductivities of both strong and weak electrolytes.

Question 6 of 20

The degree of dissociation (α) of a weak electrolyte is given by:

Explanation: Degree of dissociation is the ratio of molar conductance at concentration c to that at infinite dilution.

Question 7 of 20

Why does specific conductance decrease with dilution?

Explanation: Specific conductance is the conductance of 1 cm3 of solution. On dilution, the number of ions per unit volume decreases.

Question 8 of 20

Which of the following has the highest molar conductance at infinite dilution?

Explanation: Cs+ has the smallest hydrated radius due to lowest charge density, thus moving fastest and exhibiting higher mobility.

Question 10 of 20

On diluting a weak electrolyte solution, its molar conductance:

Explanation: Dilution increases the degree of dissociation of weak electrolytes, producing more ions and increasing molar conductance.

Question 11 of 20

The constant 'A' in the Debye-Hckel-Onsager equation depends upon:

Explanation: Constant 'A' depends on physical properties of the solvent such as viscosity and dielectric constant, as well as temperature.

Question 12 of 20

The molar conductance of NaCl at infinite dilution can be obtained by:

Explanation: NaCl is a strong electrolyte, so its limiting molar conductance is obtained by linear extrapolation of the Λm vs √c plot.

Question 13 of 20

Which factor does NOT affect molar conductance?

Explanation: Molar conductance is an intensive property of the solution and does not depend on the size of the container.

Question 14 of 20

The unit of specific conductance is:

Explanation: Specific conductance (κ) is the reciprocal of resistivity, having units of S cm-1 or Ω-1 cm-1.

Question 15 of 20

In the Debye-Hckel-Onsager equation, the slope of the plot Λm vs √c is:

Explanation: The slope is negative because molar conductance decreases as concentration increases due to interionic forces.

Question 16 of 20

For a solution of CH3COOH, the degree of dissociation is 0.04. If Λm is 8 S cm2 mol-1, what is Λm∞?

Explanation: Λm∞ = Λm / α = 8 / 0.04 = 200 S cm2 mol-1.

Question 17 of 20

Which of the following electrolyte solutions shows maximum molar conductance at equivalent concentrations?

Explanation: K4[Fe(CN)6] yields the highest number of ions per formula unit (5 cations + 1 anion = 5 ions total), giving maximum molar conductance.

Question 18 of 20

The dimension of cell constant is:

Explanation: Cell constant is l/A, having dimensions of length-1 (typically cm-1).

Question 19 of 20

With an increase in temperature, molar conductance of an electrolyte solution generally:

Explanation: Higher temperature reduces solvent viscosity and increases ionic mobility, thereby increasing molar conductance.

Question 20 of 20

At infinite dilution, the mobilities of cations and anions:

Explanation: At infinite dilution, interionic interactions are zero, allowing ions to move with maximum limiting mobilities.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the molar conductance of 0.05 M NaOH solution if its specific conductance is 0.021 S cm-1.

Solution: Λm = (κ × 1000) / C = (0.021 × 1000) / 0.05 = 21 / 0.05 = 420 S cm2 mol-1.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following plots is linear for strong electrolytes according to Debye-Hckel-Onsager theory?

Solution: The plot of Λm versus √c is linear for strong electrolytes.

Question 3 of 10
Limiting molar conductivities for BaCl2, H2SO4, and HCl are x, y, and z respectively. The limiting molar conductivity of BaSO4 is:

Solution: Λm∞(BaSO4) = λBa2+∞ + λSO42-∞ = [ Λm(BaCl2) + Λm(H2SO4) - 2 Λm(HCl) ] = x + y - 2z.

Question 4 of 10
What is the unit of cell constant?

Solution: Cell constant is length divided by area (l / A), giving units of cm-1 or m-1.

Question 5 of 10
The molar conductance of a weak monobasic acid is 12.0 S cm2 mol-1 at concentration c and 400 S cm2 mol-1 at infinite dilution. The dissociation constant Ka of the acid is:

Solution: α = 12 / 400 = 0.03. Ka = cα2 / (1 - α). Assuming c = 0.04 M (standard test value): Ka = 0.04 × (0.03)2 / 0.97 ≈ 3.6 × 10-4.

Question 6 of 10
Why can limiting molar conductivity of acetic acid not be determined directly by extrapolation?

Solution: Weak electrolytes show a steep exponential rise in molar conductance near infinite dilution, making graphical extrapolation impossible.

Question 7 of 10
Which ion has the lowest ionic mobility in aqueous solution at infinite dilution?

Solution: Li+ has the smallest crystallographic radius but highest charge density, resulting in maximum hydration, a large hydrated radius, and lowest mobility.

Question 8 of 10
If conductivity of 0.001 M acetic acid is 4 × 10-5 S cm-1, its molar conductance is:

Solution: Λm = (4 × 10-5 × 1000) / 0.001 = 4 × 10-2 / 10-3 = 40 S cm2 mol-1.

Question 9 of 10
Ostwald's dilution law is applicable to:

Solution: Ostwald's dilution law applies exclusively to weak electrolytes that follow the law of mass action upon dissociation.

Question 10 of 10
How does temperature affect the specific conductance and molar conductance of an electrolyte solution?

Solution: Increasing temperature increases both specific and molar conductance due to lowered solvent viscosity and enhanced ionic velocities.
