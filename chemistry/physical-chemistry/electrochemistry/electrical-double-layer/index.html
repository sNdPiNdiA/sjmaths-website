Electrical Double Layer

The electrical double layer describes the structure that forms on a charged surface when it is exposed to an electrolyte solution, separating charges by a molecular distance. It consists of an inner region of specifically adsorbed ions and an outer diffuse region of thermally agitated counter-ions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Models of the Electrical Double Layer

  • The Helmholtz model treats the double layer as a parallel-plate capacitor, neglecting ionic thermal motion.
  • The Gouy-Chapman model introduces a diffuse layer where counter-ions follow a Boltzmann distribution away from the surface.
  • The Stern model combines both approaches by defining an inner Compact layer (Helmholtz plane) and an outer Diffuse layer (Gouy plane).

Diffuse Layer and Debye Length

  • The Debye length (κ-1) characterizes the thickness of the diffuse double layer and decreases with increasing electrolyte concentration.
  • Higher ionic strength compresses the diffuse double layer, restricting electrostatic repulsion to shorter distances from the charged surface.
  • Valency of ions significantly impacts double layer thickness; divalent and trivalent counter-ions reduce the Debye length more effectively than monovalent ions.

Electrokinetic Phenomena and Zeta Potential

  • The zeta potential (ζ) is the electrical potential at the slipping plane or hydrodynamic shear plane relative to the bulk solution.
  • Electrokinetic properties like electrophoresis and streaming potential directly depend on the magnitude of the zeta potential.
  • Colloidal stability is maintained when absolute zeta potential values exceed ±30 mV, preventing coagulation through high electrostatic repulsion.

Gouy-Chapman Diffuse Layer Potential Decay
ψ(x) = ψ0 exp(-κx)
Conditions: Low potential approximation (Debye-Hückel limit)
Units: ψ in Volts, x in meters
Debye Length
κ-1 = [ (εr ε0 kB T) / (2 e2 I NA) ]1/2
Conditions: Symmetrical 1:1 electrolyte at absolute temperature T
Units: κ-1 in meters, I in mol L-1
Stern Model Potential Drop
ψ0 = ψStern + ψDiffuse
Conditions: Combined compact and diffuse layer representation
Units: Potentials in Volts
Electrokinetic Potential (Zeta Potential)
ζ = (η u) / (εr ε0)
Conditions: Electrophoretic mobility measurements at the slipping plane
Units: ζ in Volts, η in Pa s, u in m2 V-1 s-1
Helmholtz Model Capacitance
C = εr ε0 / d
Conditions: Compact rigid layer approximation without thermal diffusion
Units: C in F m-2, εr (dielectric constant), ε0 (permissivity, F m-1), d (distance, m)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher electrolyte concentration reduces double layer thickness for all ionic types.

Observation: Specific ion adsorption (Stern specific adsorption) — De-solvated ions can penetrate the inner Helmholtz plane directly, sometimes reversing the charge of the surface regardless of bulk electrolyte valency rules.

Exception Zeta potential matches the total surface potential (ψ0) of the charged interface.

Observation: High surface charge densities — The hydrodynamic slipping plane is located some distance outward from the Stern plane, meaning zeta potential is always lower in magnitude than the total surface potential.

Debye Length Concentration Shortcut
κ-1 ∝ (1 / √I)

Application: If electrolyte concentration (ionic strength I) increases by a factor of 100, the Debye length decreases by a factor of 10.

Zeta Potential Stability Rule
|ζ| > 30 mV

Application: Instantly identify stable colloids in MCQ options by checking if the absolute zeta value exceeds 30 millivolts.

Valency Effect (Schulze-Hardy Rule)
Coagulating power ∝ z6

Application: Use the valency power rule to quickly rank coagulating ions: Al3+ > Ba2+ > Na+.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The electrical double layer forms at any interface between a charged solid surface and a liquid electrolyte.
  • The outer Helmholtz plane (OHP) passes through the centers of solvated counter-ions closest to the surface.
  • The inner Helmholtz plane (IHP) passes through the centers of specifically adsorbed un-solvated ions.
  • Debye length is inversely proportional to the square root of ionic strength (I).
  • Zeta potential is measured experimentally via microelectrophoresis or streaming current techniques.
  • Addition of inert electrolytes compresses the electrical double layer and lowers colloid stability.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the Stern potential with the total surface potential; Stern potential is measured at the OHP, whereas surface potential is at the exact solid-liquid boundary.
  • Assuming Debye length increases with ionic strength; remember it decreases as electrolyte concentration rises.
  • Equating the slipping plane with the outer Helmholtz plane; the slipping plane lies slightly further out into the solution.
  • Forgetting that non-electrolyte additives alter dielectric constants and thus modify double layer thickness.

⚡ Quick Revision: Electrical Double Layer

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Interfacial Charge Separation

Surface charging occurs via ion adsorption, dissociation, or lattice substitution, balanced by counter-ions in the liquid phase.

Capacitative Nature of Interfaces

The electrical double layer acts as a capacitor storing electrical charge across nanometer-scale dimensions.

Thermal vs Electrostatic Forces

The Gouy-Chapman diffuse layer represents the dynamic equilibrium between electrostatic attraction of counter-ions and thermal diffusion.

Electrokinetic Applications

Zeta potential bridges microscopic surface chemistry with macroscopic colloidal phenomena like flocculation and electrophoresis.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Inner Helmholtz Plane (IHP) (IHP)
Plane passing through specifically adsorbed de-solvated ions.
Outer Helmholtz Plane (OHP) (OHP)
Plane passing through centers of closest solvated counter-ions.
Debye Length (κ-1)
Characteristic thickness of the diffuse double layer in meters.
Zeta Potential (ζ)
Electrokinetic potential at the hydrodynamic slipping plane in Volts.
Ionic Strength (I)
Measure of electrical intensity of ions in solution, mol L-1.
Electroosmosis (Electro-osmotic flow)
Movement of liquid relative to a stationary charged surface under an electric field.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Helmholtz Capacitance formula: C = εr ε0 / d
  • Debye length decreases with increase in electrolyte concentration
  • Stern model merges Helmholtz and Gouy-Chapman models
  • Zeta potential magnitude > 30 mV indicates stable colloidal suspension
  • Schulze-Hardy rule governs ion valency effect on coagulation

📌 Quick Recall

  • Double layer models explain interfacial charge distribution.
  • Thermal agitation prevents complete collapse of counter-ions onto the surface.
  • Electrolyte addition compresses the diffuse layer thickness.
  • Electrokinetic measurements yield zeta potential values crucial for colloid chemistry.

⚖️ Key Differences

IHP VS OHP

IHP holds specifically adsorbed un-solvated ions; OHP holds closest hydrated ions.

Question 1 of 18

Which model of the electrical double layer completely ignores thermal motion of ions?

Explanation: The Helmholtz model treats the double layer purely as a rigid parallel-plate capacitor, omitting thermal agitation effects.

Question 2 of 18

The thickness of the diffuse double layer is represented by:

Explanation: Debye length (κ-1) characterizes the spatial decay of electrical potential in the diffuse double layer.

Question 3 of 18

How does the Debye length change when the concentration of a 1:1 electrolyte is increased tenfold?

Explanation: Since κ-1 is inversely proportional to the square root of ionic strength (I), a tenfold increase in I decreases Debye length by √10.

Question 4 of 18

The plane of shear where zeta potential is measured is known as:

Explanation: Zeta potential is defined as the electrical potential at the hydrodynamic slipping plane relative to the bulk solution.

Question 5 of 18

According to the Stern model, the total potential drop across the interface is divided into:

Explanation: The Stern model combines the Helmholtz inner compact layer potential drop and the Gouy-Chapman diffuse layer drop.

Question 6 of 18

Which of the following conditions ensures high stability of a hydrophobic colloidal suspension?

Explanation: Colloidal particles remain stably suspended when electrostatic repulsion (high absolute zeta potential) overcomes van der Waals attraction.

Question 7 of 18

Specifically adsorbed ions are located at:

Explanation: Specifically adsorbed ions shed part of their hydration shell and reside at the Inner Helmholtz Plane (IHP).

Question 8 of 18

The Gouy-Chapman model assumes ion distribution follows which statistical law?

Explanation: The diffuse layer assumes ions distribute themselves according to the Boltzmann distribution under electrostatic potential.

Question 9 of 18

What is the primary limitation of the Gouy-Chapman model at high surface potentials?

Explanation: Treating ions as point charges causes the Gouy-Chapman model to predict excessively high counter-ion concentrations near highly charged surfaces.

Question 10 of 18

Which electrolyte ion has the highest coagulating power for a negatively charged sol according to the Schulze-Hardy rule?

Explanation: The Schulze-Hardy rule states that coagulating power increases rapidly with valency; Al3+ has the highest valency among the cations.

Question 11 of 18

In electrokinetics, streaming potential arises due to:

Explanation: Streaming potential is generated when an electrolyte solution is forced to flow through a charged capillary or porous plug.

Question 12 of 18

The dielectric constant of the medium (εr) appears in the Debye length formula in the:

Explanation: Debye length formula: κ-1 = [ (εr ε0 kB T) / (2 e2 I NA) ]1/2, showing εr in the numerator.

Question 13 of 18

Which model introduces a boundary separating the compact inner region from the diffuse outer region?

Explanation: Otto Stern integrated Helmholtz's compact layer with Gouy-Chapman's diffuse layer by introducing the Stern plane.

Question 14 of 18

What is the typical unit of electrical double layer capacitance per unit area (C)?

Explanation: Capacitance per unit area is expressed in Farads per square meter (F m-2).

Question 15 of 18

Electrophoresis refers to the movement of:

Explanation: Electrophoresis is the migration of charged dispersed particles relative to the liquid under the influence of an electric field.

Question 16 of 18

When ionic strength (I) approaches zero, the Debye length approaches:

Explanation: Since Debye length is inversely proportional to the square root of ionic strength, as I → 0, the Debye length approaches infinity.

Question 17 of 18

The potential drop across the diffuse layer in the Gouy-Chapman low potential limit decays:

Explanation: In the Debye-Hückel approximation (low potential limit), the potential decays exponentially as ψ(x) = ψ0 exp(-κx).

Question 18 of 18

Specific adsorption of ions generally occurs when ions:

Explanation: Ions that shed their primary hydration shell can bind directly to the electrode surface via specific chemical or electrostatic interactions.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary assumption of the Helmholtz model that makes it invalid at very low electrolyte concentrations?

Solution: Helmholtz treated counter-ions as a rigid sheet, completely ignoring thermal diffusion which creates a diffuse cloud in dilute solutions.

Question 2 of 10
Calculate the factor by which Debye length changes if the ionic strength is increased by a factor of 16.

Solution: Since Debye length is proportional to 1/√I, multiplying I by 16 reduces the Debye length by a factor of √16 = 4.

Question 3 of 10
Which plane separates specifically adsorbed un-solvated ions from solvated ions in the Stern double layer?

Solution: The Inner Helmholtz plane (IHP) passes through the centers of specifically adsorbed ions that have lost their hydration shells.

Question 4 of 10
Electroosmosis is best described as the movement of:

Solution: Electroosmosis is the electrokinetic phenomenon where liquid moves through a charged porous medium when an electric potential gradient is applied.

Question 5 of 10
The potential at the outer Helmholtz plane in the Stern model is denoted as:

Solution: The potential at the boundary between the compact Stern layer and the diffuse layer is termed the Stern potential (ψStern).

Question 6 of 10
Why are multivalent ions extremely effective at coagulating colloidal sols?

Solution: Higher valency ions increase ionic strength contribution quadratically (I = 0.5 ∑ ci zi2), sharply reducing Debye length and zeta potential.

Question 7 of 10
In microelectrophoresis experiments, velocity of a colloidal particle is directly proportional to:

Solution: Electrophoretic mobility is directly proportional to the zeta potential through the Smoluchowski or Hückel equations.

Question 8 of 10
What causes the negative charge on many oxide surfaces when immersed in water?

Solution: Oxide surfaces typically acquire charge via potential-determining ions like H+ and OH- through dissociation or adsorption.

Question 9 of 10
The capacitance of the diffuse double layer according to Gouy-Chapman theory depends on:

Solution: Diffuse layer capacitance varies non-linearly with applied potential and depends directly on electrolyte concentration and temperature.

Question 10 of 10
Which electrokinetic phenomenon involves measuring the potential difference created by forcing liquid through a plug?

Solution: Streaming potential is generated by the hydrodynamic flow of liquid carrying excess mobile charges past a stationary charged plug.
