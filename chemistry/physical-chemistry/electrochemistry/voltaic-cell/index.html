Physical Chemistry

Voltaic Cell

A voltaic cell is an electrochemical device that converts chemical energy from spontaneous redox reactions into electrical energy. It consists of two half-cells connected by an external circuit and a salt bridge to maintain electrical neutrality.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrode Processes and Half-Cells

  • The anode is the site of oxidation and is assigned a negative polarity in a voltaic cell.
  • The cathode is the site of reduction and is assigned a positive polarity.
  • Electrons flow through the external wire from the anode to the cathode.
  • The salt bridge prevents liquid junction potential and maintains charge balance via ion migration.

Thermodynamics of Electrochemical Cells

  • A spontaneous reaction occurs only when ΔG < 0, which corresponds to Ecell > 0.
  • The Faraday constant (F) represents the charge of one mole of electrons, approximately 96,485 C/mol.
  • The cell potential is an intensive property, meaning it does not depend on the amount of substance present.
  • Maximum work obtainable from the cell is equal to the decrease in Gibbs free energy.

Cell Notation and Conventions

  • The IUPAC convention dictates writing the anode on the left and the cathode on the right.
  • A single vertical line (|) represents a phase boundary, while a double vertical line (||) represents the salt bridge.
  • Concentrations of species are typically included in parentheses for aqueous ions.
  • Inert electrodes like Platinum (Pt) are used when the redox couple does not involve a solid metal.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Gibbs Free Energy Relation
ΔG° = -nFE°cell
Conditions: Standard conditions
Units: Joules (J), n=moles of electrons, F=Faraday constant
Nernst Equation
Ecell = E°cell - (RT/nF) ln Q
Conditions: Non-standard concentrations
Units: E in Volts, Q=reaction quotient
Equilibrium Constant Relation
log Kc = (nE°cell) / 0.0591
Conditions: At 298 K
Units: Dimensionless Kc
Cell Potential
E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode
Conditions: Standard state (1 M concentration, 298 K, 1 bar pressure)
Units: Volts (V)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) potential is 0.00 V

Observation: Potential varies with pH — The Nernst equation shows dependence on [H+] concentration.

Exception Metals displace hydrogen from acids

Observation: Copper (Cu) does not displace H2 from HCl — Copper has a positive standard reduction potential (E°Cu2+/Cu > 0).

Exception Concentration cells have E° = 0

Observation: Ecell is non-zero — Potential arises solely from the difference in ion concentrations between half-cells.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • E°cell is independent of the stoichiometric coefficients in the balanced equation.
  • The salt bridge contains inert electrolytes like KCl or KNO3 in agar-agar.
  • At equilibrium, Ecell = 0 and ΔG = 0.
  • Standard reduction potentials are measured relative to the SHE.
  • The Nernst equation accounts for the effect of concentration on cell potential.
  • A cell with a larger E°cell value is a stronger oxidizing agent.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the sign of the anode in voltaic versus electrolytic cells.
  • Forgetting to convert log to ln or using the wrong factor (0.0591 vs 0.0592) in Nernst calculations.
  • Assuming E°cell changes with the number of moles of electrons (it is intensive).
  • Neglecting to include the activity of solids and liquids as unity in the reaction quotient Q.

⚡ Quick Revision: Voltaic Cell

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Spontaneity Criteria

Spontaneity is determined by the sign of E°cell. Positive values indicate spontaneous processes.

Nernst Application

The Nernst equation allows calculation of potential under non-standard conditions using the reaction quotient Q.

Salt Bridge Function

Maintains electrical neutrality by allowing ion flow, preventing the buildup of charge in half-cells.

Electrode Potentials

Standard reduction potentials measure the tendency of a species to gain electrons relative to hydrogen.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • E°cell = E°red(cathode) - E°red(anode)
  • ΔG° = -nFE°cell
  • Ecell = E°cell - (0.0591/n) log Q
  • F = 96500 C/mol (approx)
  • Spontaneous if Ecell > 0

📌 Quick Recall

  • Voltaic cells convert chemical energy to electrical energy.
  • Electrons flow from anode to cathode.
  • Salt bridge maintains charge neutrality.
  • Thermodynamic feasibility is linked to E°cell.

⚖️ Key Differences

Oxidation VS Reduction

Oxidation is loss of electrons; Reduction is gain of electrons.
