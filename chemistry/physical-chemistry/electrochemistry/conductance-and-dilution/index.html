Variation of Conductance with Dilution

Electrolytic conductance measures the ease of charge transport through an ionic solution under an applied electrical field. Dilution alters specific conductance, molar conductance, and equivalent conductance differently due to changes in interionic interactions and degree of dissociation.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Effect of Dilution on Specific Conductance

  • Specific conductance (κ) decreases upon dilution for both strong and weak electrolytes.
  • The decrease is caused by a reduction in the number of current-carrying ions per unit volume of the solution.
  • As solvent is added, ions are spaced further apart, diminishing the conductance of a fixed 1 cm3 volume.

Effect of Dilution on Molar and Equivalent Conductance

  • Molar conductance (Λm) increases with dilution because the volume containing one mole of electrolyte increases significantly.
  • For strong electrolytes, Λm increases gradually and linearly with √c according to the Debye-Huckel-Onsager equation.
  • For weak electrolytes, Λm rises sharply at high dilutions due to a rapid increase in the degree of dissociation (α).

Limiting Molar Conductance and Kohlrausch's Law

  • Limiting molar conductance (Λm∞) represents the conductance at infinite dilution where interionic forces approach zero.
  • For strong electrolytes, Λm∞ is obtained by extrapolating the Λm versus √c plot to zero concentration.
  • For weak electrolytes, extrapolation is impossible; thus Kohlrausch's Law of Independent Migration of Ions is used to determine Λm∞.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Relation between Molar Conductance and Molarity
Λm = (κ × 1000) / M
Conditions: Molarity M in mol L-1
Units: Λm in S cm2 mol-1, κ in S cm-1
Debye-Huckel-Onsager Equation
Λm = Λm∞ - B√c
Conditions: Valid only for strong electrolytes at very low concentrations
Units: Λm∞ in S cm2 mol-1, B is constant depending on solvent viscosity and dielectric constant
Degree of Dissociation for Weak Electrolytes
α = Λm / Λm∞
Conditions: Applicable for weak electrolytes obeying Ostwald's dilution law
Units: α is dimensionless, Λm and Λm∞ in S cm2 mol-1
Cell Constant
G* = l / A
Conditions: Where l is distance between electrodes and A is cross-sectional area
Units: G* in cm-1, l in cm, A in cm2
Molar Conductance
Λm = κ / c
Conditions: Applicable for solutions of concentration c
Units: Λm in S cm2 mol-1, κ in S cm-1, c in mol cm-3
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Molar conductance increases continuously upon dilution for all electrolytes.

Observation: Very high dilutions of extremely weak electrolytes or impure water contributions. — At extreme dilutions, the contribution of H+ and OH- ions from self-ionization of water becomes comparable to the solute.

Exception Strong electrolytes show a linear variation of Λm with √c.

Observation: At very high concentrations, deviations from linearity occur. — Interionic attraction equations break down due to ion-ion and ion-solvent structural overlapping at close range.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Unit Conversion Guard for Molarity
Λm = (κ × 1000) / M

Application: Always multiply κ (in S cm-1) by 1000 when molarity is expressed in mol L-1 to keep units consistent in cm2.

Strong vs Weak Graph Shape
Steep curve = Weak; Gentle straight line = Strong

Application: In identification MCQs, a sharp asymptotic jump in Λm at low concentration denotes a weak electrolyte.

Ostwald's α Estimation
α = Λc / Λ0

Application: Use the ratio of molar conductance at concentration c to infinite dilution to find the degree of dissociation quickly.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Specific conductance (κ) always decreases with dilution due to fewer ions per unit volume.
  • Molar conductance (Λm) increases with dilution due to increased volume containing one mole of electrolyte.
  • Debye-Huckel-Onsager equation applies to strong electrolytes: Λm = Λm∞ - B√c.
  • Limiting molar conductance cannot be found graphically for weak electrolytes because the curve becomes parallel to the y-axis.
  • Kohlrausch's Law enables the calculation of molar conductance at infinite dilution for weak electrolytes.
  • The unit of molar conductance is S cm2 mol-1 or Ω-1 cm2 mol-1.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing specific conductance with molar conductance; remember specific decreases while molar increases upon dilution.
  • Forgetting the factor of 1000 when calculating molar conductance from molarity and specific conductance.
  • Attempting to extrapolate the curve of a weak electrolyte to zero concentration to find Λm∞.
  • Ignoring temperature dependence; conductance values change significantly with temperature variations.

⚡ Quick Revision: Variation of Conductance with Dilution

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Specific Conductance Trend

Specific conductance (κ) decreases on dilution because the number of ions per unit volume decreases.

Molar Conductance Trend

Molar conductance (Λm) increases on dilution as the total volume containing one mole of electrolyte increases.

Strong Electrolyte Behavior

Strong electrolytes are completely dissociated; dilution only reduces interionic forces, leading to a small linear increase in Λm.

Weak Electrolyte Behavior

Weak electrolytes have low initial dissociation; dilution increases α significantly, causing a sharp rise in Λm.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Specific Conductance (κ (Kappa))
Conductance of a 1 cm cube of solution. SI unit: S m-1.
Molar Conductance (Λm)
Conductance of a solution containing 1 mole of electrolyte. Unit: S cm2 mol-1.
Equivalent Conductance (Λeq)
Conductance of a solution containing 1 gram equivalent of electrolyte.
Limiting Molar Conductance (Λm∞)
Molar conductance at infinite dilution where ionic interactions are zero.
Cell Constant (G*)
Ratio of distance between electrodes to surface area (l/A). Unit: cm-1.
Degree of Dissociation (α)
Fraction of total electrolyte dissociated into ions in solution.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Λm = (κ × 1000) / M
  • Debye-Huckel-Onsager: Λm = Λm∞ - B√c
  • α = Λm / Λm∞
  • Specific conductance decreases with dilution; molar conductance increases with dilution.
  • Kohlrausch's law is essential for weak electrolyte limiting conductance.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Dilution decreases specific conductance due to reduced ionic concentration per unit volume.
  • Dilution increases molar conductance due to expanded volume per mole.
  • Strong electrolytes follow DHO equation; weak electrolytes follow Ostwald's dilution law.
  • Infinite dilution removes all interionic interactions.

⚖️ Key Differences

Specific Conductance VS Molar Conductance

Specific conductance is per unit volume, whereas molar conductance is for a fixed amount (one mole) contained in a variable volume.

What happens to the specific conductance of an electrolyte solution upon dilution?

Explanation: Specific conductance decreases on dilution because the number of ions per unit volume decreases.

Question 2 of 18

Why does molar conductance increase with dilution?

Explanation: Molar conductance increases because the volume of solution containing one mole of the electrolyte increases much faster than the decrease in specific conductance.

Question 3 of 18

Which equation represents the variation of molar conductance with concentration for strong electrolytes?

Explanation: The Debye-Huckel-Onsager equation (Λm = Λm∞ - B√c) governs strong electrolytes.

Question 4 of 18

The unit of molar conductance is:

Explanation: Molar conductance is expressed in S cm2 mol-1 or Ohm-1 cm2 mol-1.

Question 5 of 18

For a weak electrolyte, the plot of molar conductance versus the square root of concentration (√c) is:

Explanation: Weak electrolytes show a sharp exponential-like rise in molar conductance at low concentrations due to increased dissociation.

Question 6 of 18

How is the degree of dissociation (α) of a weak electrolyte related to molar conductances?

Explanation: The degree of dissociation is given by the ratio of molar conductance at concentration c to that at infinite dilution.

Question 7 of 18

What is the effect of dilution on the equivalent conductance of a strong electrolyte?

Explanation: Equivalent conductance of strong electrolytes increases slightly with dilution because interionic attractions are reduced.

Question 8 of 18

Which of the following terms represents conductance per unit volume?

Explanation: Specific conductance (κ) is the conductance of a unit volume (1 cm3) of solution.

Question 9 of 18

Why can limiting molar conductance of weak electrolytes not be determined by direct extrapolation?

Explanation: The graph rises too steeply and becomes almost parallel to the y-axis, making accurate graphical extrapolation impossible.

Question 10 of 18

Which law is used to calculate the limiting molar conductance of weak electrolytes?

Explanation: Kohlrausch's Law of independent migration of ions is used to calculate Λm∞ for weak electrolytes.

Question 11 of 18

What is the dimension of cell constant (G*)?

Explanation: Cell constant is l/A, where l is in cm and A is in cm2, giving units of cm-1.

Question 12 of 18

If molarity of a solution is M and specific conductance is κ, the formula for molar conductance in standard cgs units is:

Explanation: Λm = (κ × 1000) / M when M is in mol L-1 and κ in S cm-1.

Question 13 of 18

As concentration approaches zero, the molar conductance approaches:

Explanation: At infinite dilution (c → 0), molar conductance reaches its maximum limiting value, Λm∞.

Question 14 of 18

In the Debye-Huckel-Onsager equation, the constant 'B' depends on:

Explanation: Constant B depends on experimental parameters like solvent viscosity, dielectric constant, and temperature.

Question 15 of 18

Which electrolyte shows the maximum increase in molar conductance upon extreme dilution?

Explanation: CH3COOH is a weak electrolyte, so dilution causes a massive increase in dissociation and thus molar conductance.

Question 16 of 18

What is the relationship between molar conductance (Λm) and equivalent conductance (Λeq) for a trivalent-trivalent electrolyte like AlPO4?

Explanation: For AlPO4, total positive/negative charge per formula unit is 3, so n-factor = 3, giving Λm = 3 Λeq.

Question 17 of 18

The SI unit of specific conductance (κ) is:

Explanation: The SI unit for specific conductance is Siemens per meter (S m-1).

Question 18 of 18

What causes the slight increase in molar conductance of strong electrolytes on dilution?

Explanation: Strong electrolytes are fully dissociated already; dilution increases spacing, reducing interionic friction and drag.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary reason for the sharp rise in molar conductance of acetic acid upon dilution?

Solution: Weak electrolytes dissociate more completely at higher dilutions, causing a sharp rise in the number of active ions.

Question 2 of 10
If the specific conductance of 0.02 M KCl solution is 2.48 × 10-3 S cm-1, its molar conductance is:

Solution: Λm = (κ × 1000) / M = (2.48 × 10-3 × 1000) / 0.02 = 2.48 / 0.02 = 124 S cm2 mol-1.

Question 3 of 10
Which term in the Debye-Huckel-Onsager equation represents the slope of the Λm versus √c line?

Solution: In Λm = Λm∞ - B√c, B is the slope of the linear plot.

Question 4 of 10
For which of the following electrolytes is graphical extrapolation of Λm to zero concentration valid?

Solution: KCl is a strong electrolyte, so its Λm vs √c plot is linear and can be extrapolated to zero concentration.

Question 5 of 10
What happens to equivalent conductance when a solution of an electrolyte is diluted 100 times?

Solution: Equivalent conductance increases with dilution, but not proportionally for strong electrolytes due to interionic drag.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following expressions correctly defines cell constant (G*) in terms of resistance (R) and specific conductance (κ)?

Solution: Conductance = 1/R = κ × (A/l). Since G = l/A, 1/R = κ / G, giving G* = κ × R.

Question 7 of 10
The unit of equivalent conductance is:

Solution: Equivalent conductance is expressed in S cm2 eq-1.

Question 8 of 10
Which factor does NOT affect the molar conductance of a solution?

Solution: The material of the container holding the solution has no effect on the electrolytic conductance.

Question 9 of 10
At infinite dilution, the mobilities of cations and anions:

Solution: At infinite dilution, ions are far apart and migrate independently, reaching their maximum characteristic mobilities.

Question 10 of 10
How does temperature affect the molar conductance of an electrolyte solution?

Solution: Raising temperature decreases solvent viscosity and increases ionic mobilities, thereby increasing molar conductance.
