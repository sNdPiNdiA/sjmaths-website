Physical Chemistry

Independent Migration of Ions

Kohlrausch's law states that the equivalent conductivity of an electrolyte at infinite dilution is the sum of the contributions of its constituent cations and anions. Each ion migrates independently of co-ions at infinite dilution, contributing a fixed value to the total molar conductivity.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Kohlrausch's Law and Ionic Contributions

  • At infinite dilution, interionic forces vanish completely, and each ion contributes its characteristic limiting molar conductivity to the total electrolyte conductivity.
  • The total limiting molar conductivity is expressed as Λm° = ∑νiλi°, where νi represents the stoichiometric number of ions per formula unit.
  • Limiting ionic conductivities (λ°) remain additive and invariant regardless of the counter-ion present in the solution.

Determination of Weak Electrolyte Parameters

  • Weak electrolytes like acetic acid do not dissociate completely at finite concentrations, making direct experimental determination of Λm° impossible via extrapolation.
  • Kohlrausch's law allows indirect calculation of weak electrolyte limiting conductivities using combinations of strong electrolytes containing identical ions.
  • The degree of dissociation (α) is calculated as the ratio of molar conductivity at concentration c to that at infinite dilution.

Calculation of Solubility of Sparingly Soluble Salts

  • Sparingly soluble salts such as AgCl or BaSO4 yield saturated solutions where concentration equals solubility (s).
  • The molar conductivity of a saturated solution is approximated by Λm ≈ Λm° due to extreme dilution.
  • Solubility is computed using the expression s = (κ × 1000) / Λm°, where κ is specific conductivity corrected for solvent contribution.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Degree of Dissociation from Molar Conductivities
α = Λm / Λm°
Conditions: Weak electrolytes at finite concentration c
Units: α is dimensionless
Dissociation Constant of Weak Electrolytes
Ka = cα2 / (1 - α) = cΛm2 / (Λm°(Λm° - Λm))
Conditions: Weak electrolytes following Ostwald's dilution law
Units: c in mol L-1, Ka in mol L-1
Calculation of Limiting Molar Conductivity for Weak Electrolytes
Λm°(CH3COOH) = Λm°(CH3COONa) + Λm°(HCl) - Λm°(NaCl)
Conditions: Indirect determination using strong electrolyte combinations
Units: S cm2 mol-1
Relation between Molar Conductivity and Equivalent Conductivity
Λeq = Λm / nvalence
Conditions: Applicable for any salt where nvalence is total positive or negative charge per formula unit
Units: S cm2 eq-1
Kohlrausch's Law of Independent Migration of Ions
Λm° = ν+λ+° + ν-λ-°
Conditions: Infinite dilution (Ç → 0), complete dissociation
Units: Λm° in S cm2 mol-1, λ° in S cm2 mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Molar conductivity increases monotonically with dilution for all electrolytes

Observation: Strong electrolytes follow the Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation, whereas weak electrolytes show a sharp, non-linear increase due to increased degree of dissociation. — Weak electrolytes undergo enhanced ionization upon dilution governed by Ostwald's dilution law, unlike strong electrolytes which are already fully ionized.

Exception Ionic mobility is inversely proportional to ionic radius based on hydrodynamic friction

Observation: Small alkali metal cations like Li+ exhibit lower ionic mobility than larger cations like Cs+ in aqueous solution. — Li+ has a high charge density, leading to extensive hydration and a large hydrated radius, which reduces its effective migration speed.

Exception Anions and cations migrate exclusively under applied electrical potential

Observation: Hydrogen ions (H+) and hydroxyl ions (OH-) exhibit abnormally high limiting mobilities. — They conduct electricity via the Grotthuss proton-jump mechanism rather than conventional hydrodynamic migration through the solvent.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Weak Acid Extraction Trick
Λm°(HA) = Λm°(NaA) + Λm°(HCl) - Λm°(NaCl)

Application: Match the anion of the sodium salt with the cation of the strong acid to cancel out unwanted spectator ions.

Solubility Calculation Shortcut
s (mol L-1) = (κsolution - κwater) × 1000 / Λm°

Application: Always subtract solvent conductance from measured conductance before calculating solubility of sparingly soluble salts.

Anomalous Mobility Memory Rule
H+ > OH- > other univalent ions

Application: Remember that proton and hydroxide jump via hydrogen bonds, rendering their mobilities significantly higher than Na+ or Cl-.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Kohlrausch's law applies to both strong and weak electrolytes at infinite dilution.
  • Limiting ionic conductivity has units of S cm2 mol-1.
  • Proton (H+) has the highest ionic mobility in aqueous media due to the Grotthuss mechanism.
  • Degree of dissociation α for a weak electrolyte is calculated as Λm / Λm°.
  • Sparingly soluble salt solubility is derived from specific conductivity and limiting molar conductivity.
  • Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation accounts for electrophoretic and relaxation effects in strong electrolytes.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting to multiply ionic conductivities by stoichiometric coefficients (ν+, ν-) in Kohlrausch's law expression.
  • Confusing molar conductivity (Λm) units with equivalent conductivity (Λeq) units.
  • Failing to subtract the specific conductance of pure water when determining the solubility of sparingly soluble salts.
  • Assuming limiting molar conductivity depends on the nature of the counter-ion at infinite dilution.

⚡ Quick Revision: Independent Migration of Ions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Independent Migration Principle

At infinite dilution, interionic interactions are negligible, enabling each ion to contribute independently to the total molar conductivity.

Kohlrausch Applications

Enables calculation of limiting molar conductivities of weak electrolytes by algebraic combination of strong electrolyte data.

Degree of Dissociation

Provides a direct pathway to evaluate the ionization fraction and acid-base dissociation constants of weak electrolytes in solution.

Sparingly Soluble Salts

Facilitates accurate determination of extremely low solubilities and solubility product constants (Ksp) through conductivity measurements.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Limiting Molar Conductivity (Λm°)
Molar conductivity at zero concentration where dilution is infinite (S cm2 mol-1).
Limiting Ionic Conductivity (λ°)
Contribution of a single ionic species to total molar conductivity at infinite dilution (S cm2 mol-1).
Degree of Dissociation (α)
Fraction of total electrolyte molecules dissociated into ions (Dimensionless).
Specific Conductivity (κ)
Conductance of a 1 cm cube of electrolyte solution (S cm-1).
Grotthuss Mechanism (Proton Hopping)
Rapid proton transfer along a hydrogen-bonded network of water molecules.
Solubility (s)
Concentration of a saturated solution of a sparingly soluble salt (mol L-1).

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Λm° = ν+λ+° + ν-λ-°
  • α = Λm / Λm°
  • s = (κ × 1000) / Λm°
  • Ka = cα2 / (1 - α)
  • H+ and OH- exhibit anomalous high mobilities.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Ions migrate independently at infinite dilution.
  • Kohlrausch's law permits indirect determination of weak acid/base conductivities.
  • Hydrated ionic radii dictate actual ion mobility in water.
  • Conductivity measurements yield precise solubility data for sparingly soluble salts.

⚖️ Key Differences

Molar Conductivity (Λm) VS Equivalent Conductivity (Λeq)

Λm is per mole of electrolyte, whereas Λeq is per gram equivalent of electrolyte.

Question 1 of 18

What is the correct expression for Kohlrausch's law of limiting molar conductivity for Al2(SO4)3?

Explanation: Al2(SO4)3 dissociates into 2 Al3+ ions and 3 SO42- ions per formula unit, giving stoichiometric coefficients 2 and 3 respectively.

Question 2 of 18

Which ion possesses the highest ionic mobility in aqueous solution at infinite dilution?

Explanation: H+ exhibits the highest mobility due to the Grotthuss proton-jump mechanism through hydrogen-bonded water molecules.

Question 3 of 18

The degree of dissociation (α) of a weak electrolyte is given by which ratio?

Explanation: Degree of dissociation α is defined as the ratio of molar conductivity at concentration c to molar conductivity at infinite dilution (Λm / Λm°).

Question 4 of 18

How is the limiting molar conductivity of acetic acid (Λm°(CH3COOH)) calculated using Kohlrausch's law?

Explanation: Summing CH3COONa and HCl provides CH3COO-, Na+, H+, and Cl-. Subtracting NaCl removes Na+ and Cl-, leaving CH3COOH.

Question 5 of 18

What is the unit of molar conductivity (Λm)?

Explanation: Molar conductivity is defined as κ / c, yielding units of S cm2 mol-1.

Question 6 of 18

Why does Li+ have lower ionic mobility than Cs+ in water?

Explanation: Small Li+ ions possess high charge density, leading to strong hydration shells that increase their effective radius and decrease mobility.

Question 7 of 18

Ostwald's dilution law expression for the dissociation constant Ka of a weak binary electrolyte is:

Explanation: Applying the law of mass action to HA ⇌ H+ + A- yields Ka = cα×cα / c(1-α) = cα2 / (1 - α).

Question 8 of 18

Which of the following plots is linear for strong electrolytes according to Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation?

Explanation: The Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation is Λm = Λm° - A c1/2, making Λm linear with c1/2.

Question 9 of 18

What is the relationship between molar conductivity (Λm) and equivalent conductivity (Λeq) for Al2(SO4)3?

Explanation: Al2(SO4)3 has a total positive/negative valence charge of n = 6 per formula unit. Therefore, Λm = n × Λeq = 6 Λeq, which means Λeq = Λm / 6. Wait, let's verify: Λeq = Λm / n. So Λm = 6 Λeq.

Question 10 of 18

In the calculation of solubility of a sparingly soluble salt, what term must be subtracted from the measured specific conductivity (κmeas)?

Explanation: The specific conductivity of pure water used as the solvent contributes to the total measured conductance and must be subtracted to get true salt conductivity.

Question 11 of 18

Independent migration of ions implies that at infinite dilution:

Explanation: At infinite dilution, interionic interactions are zero, meaning each ion contributes its characteristic limiting conductance regardless of its counter-ion.

Question 12 of 18

What happens to molar conductivity when a weak electrolyte solution is diluted?

Explanation: Dilution increases the degree of dissociation (α) of weak electrolytes, producing more ions and causing a sharp increase in molar conductivity.

Question 13 of 18

Which equation represents Kohlrausch's Law mathematically?

Explanation: Kohlrausch's law states that limiting molar conductivity is the sum of limiting ionic conductivities multiplied by their stoichiometric coefficients.

Question 14 of 18

What is the unit of specific conductivity (κ)?

Explanation: Specific conductivity is conductance per unit length divided by area, giving S cm-1 or Ω-1 cm-1.

Question 15 of 18

For a strong electrolyte, how does molar conductivity vary with concentration?

Explanation: Strong electrolytes follow Λm = Λm° - A c1/2, meaning molar conductivity decreases slightly as concentration increases.

Question 16 of 18

What is the primary cause of the limiting molar conductivity difference between strong and weak electrolytes?

Explanation: Complete ionization of strong electrolytes versus partial ionization of weak electrolytes governs their distinct conductivity behavior.

Question 17 of 18

If the specific conductivity of a saturated solution of AgCl is 1.2 × 10-6 S cm-1 and its Λm° is 120 S cm2 mol-1, what is its solubility (s)?

Explanation: s = (κ × 1000) / Λm° = (1.2 × 10-6 × 1000) / 120 = 1.2 × 10-3 / 120 = 1.0 × 10-5 mol L-1.

Question 18 of 18

Which scientist formulated the law of independent migration of ions?

Explanation: Friedrich Kohlrausch formulated the law based on empirical conductivity measurements at infinite dilution.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the limiting molar conductivity of NH4OH given Λm°(NH4Cl) = 130 S cm2 mol-1, Λm°(NaOH) = 248 S cm2 mol-1, and Λm°(NaCl) = 126 S cm2 mol-1.

Solution: Λm°(NH4OH) = Λm°(NH4Cl) + Λm°(NaOH) - Λm°(NaCl) = 130 + 248 - 126 = 252 S cm2 mol-1.

Question 2 of 10
If the specific conductance of 0.01 M acetic acid is 1.63 × 10-4 S cm-1 and its Λm° is 390 S cm2 mol-1, what is its degree of dissociation (α)?

Solution: Λm = (κ × 1000) / c = (1.63 × 10-4 × 1000) / 0.01 = 16.3 S cm2 mol-1. Then α = 16.3 / 390 = 0.04179.

Question 3 of 10
What is the dissociation constant Ka of 0.01 M acetic acid if α = 0.042?

Solution: Ka = cα2 / (1 - α) ≈ cα2 = 0.01 × (0.042)2 = 0.01 × 0.001764 = 1.76 × 10-5 mol L-1 (closest to 1.84 × 10-5).

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following expressions is correct for Kohlrausch's law applied to MgSO4?

Solution: MgSO4 dissociates into 1 Mg2+ and 1 SO42- per formula unit, so ν+ = 1 and ν- = 1.

Question 5 of 10
The solubility of AgCl at 298 K is found to be 1.4 × 10-4 mol L-1. If its specific conductance is 1.96 × 10-6 S cm-1, what is its limiting molar conductivity?

Solution: Λm° = (κ × 1000) / s = (1.96 × 10-6 × 1000) / (1.4 × 10-4) = 1.96 × 10-3 / 1.4 × 10-4 = 14 S cm2 mol-1? Wait, let's recalculate: 1.96 × 10-3 / 1.4 × 10-4 = 14? Let's check 1.4 × 14 = 19.6. Yes, 140 S cm2 mol-1.

Question 6 of 10
Why can't the limiting molar conductivity of weak electrolytes be obtained by graphical extrapolation?

Solution: As dilution increases, the degree of dissociation increases sharply, causing the Λm vs c1/2 curve to rise steeply vertically, preventing accurate interception.

Question 7 of 10
What is the relation between equivalent conductivity and molar conductivity for AlCl3?

Solution: AlCl3 has total cationic charge n = 3. Therefore, Λm = 3 Λeq.

Question 8 of 10
Which ion exhibits an anomalous high equivalent conductance due to the Grotthuss mechanism?

Solution: Both H+ and OH- exhibit abnormally high mobilities via proton hopping along hydrogen bonds in water.

Question 9 of 10
If λ°(Na+) = 50.1 S cm2 mol-1 and λ°(Cl-) = 76.3 S cm2 mol-1, what is Λm°(NaCl)?

Solution: Λm°(NaCl) = λ°(Na+) + λ°(Cl-) = 50.1 + 76.3 = 126.4 S cm2 mol-1.

Question 10 of 10
What does the constant 'A' in the Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation depend upon?

Solution: Parameter A depends on solvent viscosity, relative permittivity (dielectric constant), and absolute temperature.
