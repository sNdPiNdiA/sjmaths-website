Lead Storage Battery

The lead storage battery is a secondary cell that functions as a galvanic cell during discharge and an electrolytic cell during recharging. It utilizes a lead anode and a lead dioxide cathode submerged in an aqueous sulfuric acid electrolyte.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrochemical Cell Components

  • The anode consists of a spongy lead (Pb) grid.
  • The cathode consists of a lead grid packed with lead dioxide (PbO2).
  • The electrolyte is an aqueous solution of sulfuric acid (H2SO4) typically at 38% concentration by mass.
  • The cell potential for a single unit is approximately 2.0 Volts.

Discharge and Recharge Mechanisms

  • During discharge, both electrodes are converted into lead sulfate (PbSO4) and water is produced, diluting the acid.
  • During recharge, an external DC source reverses the current, converting PbSO4 back into Pb and PbO2.
  • The density of the electrolyte increases during the charging process as H2SO4 is regenerated.
  • The reaction is reversible, allowing the battery to be used for multiple cycles.

Operational Characteristics

  • The battery is a secondary cell because it can be recharged by applying an external potential.
  • The specific gravity of the sulfuric acid solution is a direct indicator of the battery's state of charge.
  • Internal resistance increases as PbSO4 crystals accumulate on the electrode surfaces.
  • The cell is typically connected in series to achieve higher voltages (e.g., 12V batteries contain 6 cells).

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Standard galvanic cell electrolyte

Observation: H2SO4 concentration change — Unlike Daniell cells, the electrolyte in lead storage batteries is a reactant/product, causing the voltage to drop as the acid is consumed.

Exception Electrode material consumption

Observation: Both electrodes form the same product — Both the lead anode and lead dioxide cathode are converted into insoluble lead sulfate (PbSO4) during discharge.

Exception Recharge efficiency

Observation: Sulfation — If left in a discharged state, PbSO4 forms large, hard crystals that are difficult to convert back to Pb and PbO2.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The lead storage battery is a secondary cell.
  • The electrolyte is 38% H2SO4.
  • The cathode is PbO2.
  • The anode is spongy Pb.
  • The cell voltage is approximately 2.0 V.
  • PbSO4 is formed at both electrodes during discharge.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the anode and cathode materials; remember Pb is the anode.
  • Assuming the electrolyte concentration remains constant; it changes significantly.
  • Forgetting that water is produced during discharge, which lowers the acid density.
  • Neglecting the role of external DC current in the recharging process.

⚡ Quick Revision: Lead Storage Battery

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Cell Construction

Consists of Pb and PbO2 electrodes in H2SO4. Acts as a secondary cell.

Discharge Chemistry

Pb and PbO2 react with H2SO4 to form PbSO4 and water. Energy is released.

Recharge Chemistry

External current reverses the discharge reaction. PbSO4 is converted back to Pb and PbO2.

State of Charge

Monitored by measuring the density of the sulfuric acid electrolyte.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Anode (Pb)

Cathode (PbO2)
Positive electrode where reduction occurs.
Electrolyte (H2SO4)
Sulfuric acid solution providing ions.
Secondary Cell (Rechargeable)
Battery that can be restored by reverse current.
Sulfation (PbSO4)
Formation of lead sulfate crystals on plates.
Cell Potential (Ecell)
Voltage produced by the cell, approx 2V.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Anode: Pb + SO42- → PbSO4 + 2e-
  • Cathode: PbO2 + SO42- + 4H+ + 2e- → PbSO4 + 2H2O
  • Overall: Pb + PbO2 + 2H2SO4 → 2PbSO4 + 2H2O
  • Electrolyte density decreases during discharge.
  • Cell is a secondary battery.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Lead storage battery is a secondary cell.
  • Discharge produces PbSO4 and water.
  • Recharge reverses the chemical process.
  • Density of electrolyte indicates charge.

⚖️ Key Differences

Primary Cell VS Secondary Cell

Primary cannot be recharged; secondary can.
