Specific Conductance

Specific conductance, or conductivity, is the conducting power of ions present in a unit volume of solution placed between two electrodes of unit cross-sectional area separated by unit distance. It is the reciprocal of specific resistance and is fundamentally dependent on the concentration and mobility of ions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Fundamental Definition and Units

  • Specific conductance (κ) is defined as the conductance of a 1 cm3 (or 1 m3) volume of an electrolytic solution.
  • The SI unit of specific conductance is S m-1 (Siemens per meter), though S cm-1 is widely used in chemical literature.
  • It directly reflects the ability of all ions contained in a specific unit volume to conduct an electrical current under an applied electric field.

Dependence on Concentration and Temperature

  • Specific conductance decreases with dilution because the number of current-carrying ions per unit volume of the solution decreases.
  • It increases with temperature due to a decrease in solvent viscosity and an increase in the kinetic energy and ionic mobilities.
  • For strong electrolytes, κ increases almost linearly at low concentrations, while weak electrolytes show a much slower increase due to partial dissociation.

Experimental Measurement via Conductivity Cell

  • Direct measurement of resistance is performed using a Wheatstone bridge powered by an alternating current (AC) to prevent electrolysis and polarization.
  • The cell constant (G* = l/A) is determined prior to measurement by calibrating the cell with a standard KCl solution of known specific conductance.
  • The measured resistance of the standard KCl solution yields the exact geometrical factor for subsequent unknown solutions in the same cell.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Definition of Specific Conductance (κ)
κ = (1R) × (lA)
Conditions: Valid for any electrolytic cell
Units: κ in S m-1 or S cm-1
Relation with Cell Constant
κ = G* × (1R)
Conditions: G* is the cell constant (l/A)
Units: G* in m-1 or cm-1
Relation with Molar Conductivity
Λm = (κ × 1000C)
Conditions: C in mol L-1 (Molarity)
Units: Λm in S cm2 mol-1
Ohm's Law for Electrolytic Solutions
R = ρ (lA)
Conditions: Uniform cross-section conductor
Units: R in Ω, ρ in Ω m, l in m, A in m2
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Specific conductance increases monotonically with increasing concentration of strong electrolytes.

Observation: At extremely high concentrations, ion-ion interactions and ion atmosphere relaxation effects cause κ to reach a plateau or deviate from linearity. — Inter-ionic attractive forces restrict independent ionic motion and effective charge transport at high molarities.

Exception Solutions with higher solute content always exhibit greater conductivity.

Observation: Pure strong acids like 100% H2SO4 or pure water have extremely low specific conductance. — Lack of auto-ionization or sufficient ion-pairs in the pure state restricts free charge carriers.

Exception Conductivity increases uniformly across all temperature intervals for aqueous solutions.

Observation: Beyond the boiling point or in highly viscous non-aqueous media, hydration shell stabilization alters the standard thermal coefficient. — Structural breakdown of solvent cage configurations at elevated temperatures changes activation energy for ionic jump mechanisms.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Specific conductance (κ) decreases on dilution because the number of ions per unit volume decreases.
  • The unit of cell constant is m-1 or cm-1.
  • AC current is used in conductivity measurements to prevent polarization and composition changes at electrodes.
  • Specific conductance of 0.1 M KCl solution is standardly used to determine the cell constant.
  • With a rise in temperature, specific conductance increases due to lowered solvent viscosity.
  • Pure water has a very small specific conductance due to self-ionization into H+ and OH- ions.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing specific conductance with molar conductivity; remember specific is per unit volume, molar is per mole.
  • Using direct current (DC) in conductivity meters, which leads to electrolytic deposition and erroneous resistance values.
  • Forgetting the factor of 1000 when converting concentrations from mol L-1 to cm-3 units.
  • Assuming specific conductance increases upon addition of solvent; dilution actually lowers κ.

⚡ Quick Revision: Specific Conductance

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Nature of Electrolytic Conduction

Electrolytic conduction involves the physical movement of ions through a medium under an applied electrical potential gradient.

Role of Specific Conductance

It acts as the fundamental intensity property representing local charge-carrying capacity per unit physical volume.

Experimental Determination

Measured using a Wheatstone bridge circuit with a Kohlrausch flask and platinum electrodes coated with platinum black.

Concentration Dependency

Unlike molar conductivity which rises asymptotically on dilution, specific conductance drops continuously as ion density per unit volume falls.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Specific Conductance (κ (Kappa))
Conductance of unit volume of solution; unit S m-1.
Resistance (R)
Opposition to current flow; unit Ohm (Ω).
Resistivity (ρ (Rho))
Resistance of a conductor of unit length and unit cross-sectional area; unit Ω m.
Cell Constant (G*)
Ratio of distance between electrodes to cross-sectional area (l/A).
Conductance (G)
Reciprocal of resistance; unit Siemens (S) or ohm-1.
Molar Conductivity (Λm)
Conducting power of all ions produced by 1 mole of electrolyte.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • κ = (1/R) × (l/A)
  • Specific conductance decreases with dilution.
  • AC current prevents electrode polarization.
  • Standard KCl solutions calibrate conductivity cells.
  • Units must be carefully matched (cm vs m).

📌 Quick Recall

  • Specific conductance measures local ionic conductivity.
  • It depends directly on ion concentration, mobility, and temperature.
  • Cell constant bridges measured resistance to absolute conductivity.
  • Dilution decreases specific conductance while increasing molar conductivity.

⚖️ Key Differences

Specific Conductance (κ) VS Molar Conductivity (Λm)

Specific conductance is per unit volume; molar conductivity accounts for all ions from one mole of solute.

Explanation: Higher temperature lowers viscosity and increases ion mobility, raising specific conductance.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the specific conductance if resistance is 50 ohm and cell constant is 0.5 cm-1.

Solution: κ = G* / R = 0.5 / 50 = 0.01 S cm-1.

Question 2 of 10
Why does specific conductance decrease on dilution while molar conductivity increases?

Solution: Specific conductance is per unit volume; as volume increases, ions per unit volume decrease, whereas molar conductivity accounts for all ions from one mole.

Question 3 of 10
What is the cell constant if l = 2 cm and A = 4 cm2?

Solution: Cell constant = l / A = 2 / 4 = 0.5 cm-1.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following conditions is mandatory when measuring electrolytic conductivity?

Solution: Alternating current (AC) is required to avoid polarization and electrolytic decomposition.

Question 5 of 10
The SI unit of resistivity is:

Solution: Resistivity (ρ) in SI units is expressed in ohm meter (Ω m).

Question 6 of 10
If molarity is 0.01 M and specific conductance is 0.00141 S cm-1, what is molar conductivity?

Solution: Λm = (κ × 1000) / C = (0.00141 × 1000) / 0.01 = 141 S cm2 mol-1.

Question 7 of 10
Specific conductance of a solution depends on:

Solution: It depends directly on the concentration and mobility of ions present in the solution.

Question 8 of 10
Which aqueous solution exhibits the highest specific conductance at 0.1 M?

Solution: HCl is a strong electrolyte yielding high concentrations of highly mobile H+ and Cl- ions.

Question 9 of 10
What is the effect of increasing temperature on the resistivity of an electrolyte solution?

Solution: Resistivity is the inverse of specific conductance. Since specific conductance increases with temperature, resistivity decreases.

Question 10 of 10
Which quantity remains invariant when a solution is diluted inside a specific conductivity cell?

Solution: The cell constant depends solely on the physical dimensions of the cell (l/A), which remain constant.
