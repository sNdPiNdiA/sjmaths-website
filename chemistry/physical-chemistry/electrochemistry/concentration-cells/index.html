Concentration Cells

Concentration cells are electrochemical cells in which the electromotive force is generated solely due to a difference in concentration or activity of the same chemical species between the two compartments. The standard cell potential (E°cell) for a concentration cell is always zero because the same electrode reaction occurs in both compartments.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamics and EMF of Concentration Cells

  • The standard EMF (ΔE°) of any concentration cell is identically zero because the anode and cathode reactions are identical in standard states.
  • Gibbs free energy change for a concentration cell is given by ΔG = -nFEcell, which is strictly negative for spontaneous dilution processes.
  • Spontaneity dictates that matter flows from high concentration to low concentration, causing the more concentrated half-cell to dilute and the dilute half-cell to concentrate.
  • At equilibrium, the concentrations in both half-cells become equal, resulting in Ecell = 0 V and ΔG = 0.

Classification of Concentration Cells

  • Electrode concentration cells utilize two identical electrodes of different activities or gas pressures dipping into the same electrolyte solution.
  • In gas electrode concentration cells like the hydrogen electrode, EMF depends on the ratio of gas pressures at the two electrodes: E = (RT / 2F) ln (P1 / P2).
  • Electrolyte concentration cells feature electrodes of the same material dipping into solutions of the same electrolyte at different concentrations or activities.
  • Electrolyte cells are further divided into those with transference (having a direct liquid-liquid junction) and without transference (using salt bridges or chemical coupling).

Liquid Junction Potential and Transference

  • Liquid junction potential arises at the boundary of two electrolyte solutions of different concentrations due to unequal rates of ionic diffusion.
  • The magnitude of liquid junction potential is suppressed by introducing a concentrated salt bridge containing ions of nearly equal mobilities like KCl.
  • For a concentration cell with transference, the measured potential includes the contribution from the transference numbers of the ions.
  • Eliminating the liquid junction potential allows direct calculation of mean ionic activity coefficients.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Electrolyte Concentration Cell with Transference
Ecell = 2 t+ (RT / F) ln (a2 / a1)
Conditions: Electrolyte concentration cell where liquid junction is maintained with transference
Units: t+ is transference number of cation, a1 and a2 are activities
Electrolyte Concentration Cell without Transference
Ecell = (2RT / F) ln (a2 / a1)
Conditions: Two cells combined via a salt bridge eliminating liquid junction potential
Units: a1 and a2 are mean ionic activities
Amalgam Concentration Cell Potential
Ecell = (0.0591 / n) log (C2 / C1) at 298 K
Conditions: Two metal amalgams of different concentrations dipped in the same electrolyte solution
Units: C1 and C2 are concentrations of metal in amalgams in mol L-1
Nernst Equation for Electrode Concentration Cell
Ecell = (RT / nF) ln (C2 / C1)
Conditions: T = 298 K, dilute solutions where activity coefficients approach unity
Units: E in Volts, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin, F in C mol-1, C in mol L-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Cell potential increases with increasing concentration difference

Observation: Very high concentrations where activity coefficients deviate sharply from unity — Interionic electrostatic interactions invalidate the Debye-Hückel limiting law, substituting concentration with non-ideal activity terms.

Exception Standard cell potential E° is zero for all concentration cells

Observation: Apparent non-zero E° observed when junction potentials are not accounted for experimentally — Uncompensated boundary potentials introduce an extraneous electromotive force not predicted by the simple Nernst expression.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Spontaneity Rule for Concentration Cells
Coxidation < Creduction

Application: Always place the lower concentration in the anode compartment (oxidation) and the higher concentration in the cathode compartment (reduction) to ensure a positive cell potential.

Logarithm Factor at 298 K
E = (0.0591 / n) log (Chigher / Clower)

Application: Directly substitute natural log to base-10 log by multiplying by 0.0591 for quick numerical calculations at room temperature.

Pressure Ratio Trick for Gas Cells
E = (0.0591 / n) log (Panode / Pcathode)

Application: For hydrogen or halogen gas concentration cells, treat gas pressures identically to solution concentrations.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The standard cell potential E° for any concentration cell is always equal to 0 V.
  • Concentration cells operate by converting free energy of dilution directly into electrical energy.
  • For an amalgam concentration cell, metal transfers from the dilute amalgam to the concentrated amalgam until equilibrium is attained.
  • Liquid junction potential can be effectively minimized by employing a saturated KCl salt bridge.
  • The EMF of a concentration cell depends on temperature directly via the factor T in the Nernst equation.
  • In a concentration cell without transference, thermodynamic activity coefficients must be used for rigorous calculations at high ionic strengths.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming E° can be non-zero for concentration cells based on different standard reduction potentials (which are identical).
  • Placing the higher concentration solution in the anode compartment, yielding a negative cell potential.
  • Confusing activity with molar concentration in concentrated solutions where activity coefficients deviate significantly from 1.
  • Neglecting the transference number factor when calculating cells with liquid junction.

⚡ Quick Revision: Concentration Cells

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Fundamental Basis

Concentration cells derive electrical work purely from the entropy of mixing and concentration gradients between identical chemical species.

Nernst Derivation

The general Nernst equation simplifies drastically because standard reduction potentials cancel out, leaving only concentration or activity ratios.

Transference Effects

Ionos transport numbers dictate the boundary potentials in cells with transference, requiring salt bridges for accurate thermodynamic measurements.

Analytical Applications

Concentration cells serve as primary tools for determining solubility products, activity coefficients, and thermodynamic equilibrium constants.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Concentration Cell (Ecell)
Electrochemical cell driven solely by concentration gradients between identical half-cells.
Liquid Junction Potential (Ej)
Potential difference developed across the boundary of two different electrolyte solutions.
Transference Number (t+, t-)
Fraction of total current carried by a specific ionic species.
Amalgam Cell (M(Hg))
Concentration cell utilizing metal amalgams of varying weight percentages.
Mean Ionic Activity (a±)
Effective concentration of an electrolyte used in thermodynamic equations.
Salt Bridge (KCl / NH4NO3)
Device used to eliminate liquid junction potential and complete electrical circuit.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • E°cell = 0 for all concentration cells
  • E = (0.0591 / n) log (C2 / C1) at 298 K
  • Salt bridge eliminates liquid junction potential
  • Amalgam cells have metal migrating from dilute to concentrated phase
  • Dilution free energy supplies cell EMF

📌 Quick Recall

  • Concentration cells require identical electrodes or electrolytes differing only in activity.
  • Spontaneity favors equalization of concentrations between compartments.
  • Standard cell potentials are zero; only concentration ratios drive the voltage.
  • Liquid junction potentials must be eliminated for exact thermodynamic measurements.

⚖️ Key Differences

Electrode Concentration Cell VS Electrolyte Concentration Cell

Electrode cells vary electrode activity in the same electrolyte; electrolyte cells vary solution concentration with identical electrodes.

Question 1 of 19

What is the standard cell potential (E°cell) of a concentration cell?

Explanation: Since both anode and cathode involve the same half-reaction, their standard reduction potentials are identical, making E°cell = 0.

Question 2 of 19

For a hydrogen gas concentration cell operating at 298 K, the EMF is given by:

Explanation: For 2 electrons per mole of H2, the Nernst factor uses n = 2 resulting in (0.0591 / 2) log (P1 / P2).

Question 3 of 19

In an amalgam concentration cell, metal transfers from:

Explanation: Spontaneous transfer occurs from the higher chemical potential (dilute amalgam in terms of metal activity or standard convention depending on definition, but typically metal moves from lower concentration in amalgam or vice versa to equalize chemical potential).

Question 4 of 19

What is the primary purpose of a salt bridge in electrolyte concentration cells?

Explanation: A salt bridge minimizes junction potentials by providing equitransferent ions like K+ and Cl-.

Question 5 of 19

The free energy change (ΔG) for a working concentration cell is:

Explanation: Concentration cells are spontaneous galvanic cells, hence ΔG = -nFEcell is negative.

Question 6 of 19

Which of the following represents an electrode concentration cell?

Explanation: A gas electrode concentration cell uses identical electrolyte solutions with different gas pressures at the electrodes.

Question 7 of 19

If C1 = C2 in a concentration cell, the EMF of the cell is:

Explanation: When concentrations are equal, log(1) = 0, so the EMF is 0 V.

Question 8 of 19

The EMF of an electrolyte concentration cell without transference is given by:

Explanation: For a 1:1 electrolyte without transference, the expression includes a factor of 2: E = (2RT / F) ln (a2 / a1).

Question 9 of 19

Which condition must be met for a concentration cell to have a positive potential?

Explanation: To get log(Ccathode / Canode) > 0, the cathode concentration must be higher than the anode concentration.

Question 10 of 19

What happens to the cell potential as a concentration cell discharges spontaneously?

Explanation: As the cell discharges, the concentrations in the two compartments approach each other, causing Ecell to drop to zero.

Question 11 of 19

Liquid junction potential arises due to:

Explanation: Different mobilities of cations and anions across the boundary create a charge separation and potential.

Question 12 of 19

In a concentration cell with transference, the EMF expression contains:

Explanation: The liquid junction contribution explicitly involves the transference number of the ions.

Question 13 of 19

Calculate the EMF of a cell: Ag | AgNO3 (0.01 M) || AgNO3 (0.1 M) | Ag at 298 K.

Explanation: E = (0.0591 / 1) log (0.1 / 0.01) = 0.0591 log(10) = 0.0591 V.

Question 14 of 19

Which salt is ideally used in a salt bridge to minimize liquid junction potential?

Explanation: KCl is chosen because K+ and Cl- have nearly identical ionic mobilities.

Question 15 of 19

Concentration cells are examples of:

Explanation: They produce electrical energy spontaneously from chemical potential differences, categorizing them as galvanic cells.

Question 16 of 19

The temperature coefficient of a concentration cell (dE/dT) is related to:

Explanation: The temperature dependence of EMF relates directly to the entropy change associated with transferring species between concentrations.

Question 17 of 19

In an electrolyte concentration cell, reduction occurs at:

Explanation: Reduction occurs at the cathode, which is the compartment with the higher concentration (higher activity).

Question 18 of 19

What is the value of n in the Nernst equation for a copper ion concentration cell Cu | Cu2+ (a1) || Cu2+ (a2) | Cu?

Explanation: The electrode reaction is Cu2+ + 2e- → Cu, so n = 2.

Question 19 of 19

If activities are replaced by concentrations in dilute solutions, the error introduced is:

Explanation: In dilute solutions, activity coefficients approach 1, making activity approximately equal to concentration.

Question 1 of 10
What is the EMF of an amalgam concentration cell where two amalgams of concentrations 0.05 M and 0.5 M of sodium are dipped in NaOH solution at 298 K?

Solution: For n = 1, E = (0.0591 / 1) log (0.5 / 0.05) = 0.0591 log(10) = 0.0591 V.

Question 2 of 10
Why is the salt bridge packed with KCl or NH4NO3?

Solution: Equitransferent ions ensure that the liquid junction potential is reduced to near zero.

Question 3 of 10
For a concentration cell without transference formed by combining two cells with transference of opposite polarity:

Solution: Combining cells appropriately cancels out the liquid junction potential contributions.

Question 4 of 10
The decrease in Gibbs free energy for a concentration cell is equal to:

Solution: The maximum non-expansion work available from a cell is equal to -ΔG.

Question 5 of 10
In the concentration cell Zn | ZnSO4 (a1) || ZnSO4 (a2) | Zn, if a2 > a1, which electrode acts as the cathode?

Solution: Reduction occurs in the more concentrated compartment (a2), making it the cathode.

Question 6 of 10
What is the formula for the EMF of a concentration cell with transference for a 1:1 electrolyte?

Solution: For a 1:1 electrolyte with transference, E = 2 t- (RT / F) ln (a2 / a1) or using cation transference number depending on which ion's transference number is specified.

Question 7 of 10
If the concentration ratio C2 / C1 is 100 for a divalent metal ion concentration cell at 298 K, the EMF is:

Solution: E = (0.0591 / 2) log(100) = (0.0591 / 2) × 2 = 0.0591 V.

Question 8 of 10
Concentration cells cannot function if:

Solution: When concentrations equalize, the driving chemical potential gradient disappears and Ecell drops to 0.

Question 9 of 10
The transference number t+ plus t- for a binary electrolyte always equals:

Solution: The sum of transference numbers of all ions carrying current in an electrolyte equals unity.

Question 10 of 10
Which concentration cell will have the highest potential among the following (same C1, different C2)?

Solution: EMF is proportional to log(C2 / C1); hence a ratio of 1000 gives the largest logarithmic value and highest EMF.
