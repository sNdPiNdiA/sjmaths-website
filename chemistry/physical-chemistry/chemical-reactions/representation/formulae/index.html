Physical Chemistry

Chemical Formulae and Stoichiometry

Chemical formulae represent the elemental composition and atomic ratios within a substance using symbols and subscripts. Empirical formulae denote the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms, while molecular formulae indicate the actual number of atoms in a discrete molecule.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Stoichiometric Representation

  • The Empirical Formula is derived by dividing the mass percentage of each element by its respective atomic mass.
  • Molecular Formula requires the knowledge of the molar mass of the compound to determine the integer multiplier 'n'.
  • Structural formulae provide connectivity information, distinguishing between isomers with identical molecular formulae.

Concentration Units

  • Molarity (M) is defined as the number of moles of solute per liter of solution, sensitive to thermal expansion.
  • Molality (m) is defined as the number of moles of solute per kilogram of solvent, preferred in colligative property studies.
  • Mole fraction (x) is the ratio of moles of one component to the total moles in the mixture.

Reaction Stoichiometry

  • The Limiting Reagent is the reactant that is completely consumed first, dictating the theoretical yield of the reaction.
  • Stoichiometric coefficients in a balanced chemical equation represent the molar ratios of reactants and products.
  • Percentage yield is calculated as (Actual Yield / Theoretical Yield) × 100.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Molecular Formula Relation
Molecular Formula = n × (Empirical Formula)
Conditions: n = Molar Mass / Empirical Formula Mass.
Units: Molar mass in g/mol.
Percentage Composition
% Element = (Total mass of element / Molar mass of compound) × 100
Conditions: Applicable to any chemical compound.
Units: Mass in grams.
Molarity
M = nsolute / Vsolution(L)
Conditions: Temperature dependent due to volume expansion.
Units: mol/L or M.
Molality
m = nsolute / masssolvent(kg)
Conditions: Temperature independent.
Units: mol/kg or m.
Empirical Formula Calculation
nelement = (mass % / atomic mass)
Conditions: Used to determine the simplest atomic ratio.
Units: Mass in grams, atomic mass in g/mol.
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Ionic compounds have discrete molecular formulas.

Observation: Ionic lattices like NaCl. — They exist as continuous 3D networks, so only empirical formulas are valid.

Exception All gases follow ideal behavior.

Observation: Real gases at high pressure/low temperature. — Intermolecular forces and finite molecular volume deviate from ideal gas laws.

Exception Empirical formula equals molecular formula.

Observation: Polymers and macromolecules. — The molecular weight is a multiple of the monomer unit weight.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
The 100g Assumption
Assume 100g of sample.

Application: Converts percentage composition directly into grams for empirical formula calculation.

Molarity-Molality Bridge
m = (1000 × M) / (1000 × d - M × Msolute)

Application: Quickly convert molarity to molality using density (d) and molar mass of solute.

Limiting Reagent Shortcut
Divide moles by coefficient.

Application: The lowest value identifies the limiting reagent instantly.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • One mole of any gas at STP occupies 22.4 L.
  • Avogadro's number is 6.022 × 1023 particles/mol.
  • Empirical formula is the simplest ratio of atoms.
  • Molecular formula = n × Empirical formula.
  • Molarity is temperature dependent due to volume change.
  • Molality is preferred for boiling point elevation calculations.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing molarity with molality; remember molarity is per liter of solution.
  • Forgetting to balance the chemical equation before calculating stoichiometry.
  • Using atomic mass instead of molar mass for molecular formula calculations.
  • Ignoring the density of the solution when converting between concentration units.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Formulae and Stoichiometry

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Atomic Ratios

Empirical formulas represent the simplest whole-number ratio of elements in a compound, calculated via mass percentage division by atomic weight.

Molar Relationships

Molecular formulas are integer multiples of empirical formulas, determined by the ratio of molar mass to empirical formula mass.

Solution Concentration

Molarity (M) relates moles to solution volume, while molality (m) relates moles to solvent mass, with molality being temperature-invariant.

Stoichiometric Yield

Theoretical yield is determined by the limiting reagent, which is the reactant that limits the extent of the chemical reaction.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Mole (n)
Amount of substance containing 6.022 × 1023 entities.
Molar Mass (M)
Mass of one mole of a substance in g/mol.
Molarity (M)
Moles of solute per liter of solution.
Molality (m)
Moles of solute per kilogram of solvent.
Mole Fraction (x)
Ratio of moles of one component to total moles.
STP (Standard Temp/Pressure)
0 °C and 1 bar pressure.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • n = mass / molar mass
  • Molarity = n / V(L)
  • Molality = n / mass(kg)
  • Density = mass / volume
  • Percent Yield = (Actual/Theoretical) × 100

📌 Quick Recall

  • Empirical formula is the simplest ratio.
  • Molecular formula is the actual count.
  • Molarity changes with temperature.
  • Molality is constant with temperature.

⚖️ Key Differences

Molarity VS Molality

Molarity uses solution volume; Molality uses solvent mass.
