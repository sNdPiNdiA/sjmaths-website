Physical Chemistry

Chemical Symbols and Reaction Notation

Chemical symbols represent elements using IUPAC-defined atomic notation, while reaction equations utilize stoichiometric coefficients to satisfy the Law of Conservation of Mass. Physical state symbols and thermodynamic indicators provide necessary context for reaction conditions and energy changes.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

IUPAC Elemental Representation

  • Elements are denoted by 1 or 2 letter symbols, where the first letter is capitalized and the second is lowercase.
  • The mass number (A) is written as a left superscript, and the atomic number (Z) as a left subscript.
  • Isotopes of the same element share the same Z but differ in A due to neutron count variation.

Reaction State Indicators

  • Physical states are indicated by subscripts: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, and (aq) aqueous solution.
  • The symbol Δ placed above the reaction arrow signifies the application of heat.
  • Catalysts are typically written above the arrow to indicate they participate without being consumed.

Thermodynamic Sign Conventions

  • The ° symbol denotes standard state conditions, typically defined at 1 bar pressure.
  • Exothermic reactions are represented by a negative ΔH value, indicating energy release to surroundings.
  • Endothermic reactions require a positive ΔH value, signifying energy absorption from the environment.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Group 1 elements always exhibit +1 oxidation state

Observation: Alkali metals in electride salts — Presence of trapped electrons in cavities of crown ethers

Exception Noble gases are chemically inert

Observation: Xenon fluorides (XeF2, XeF4) — Low ionization energy of Xenon allows bonding with highly electronegative Fluorine

Exception Mass number is the sum of protons and neutrons

Observation: Fractional atomic masses — Weighted average of naturally occurring isotopic abundances

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Atomic number Z defines the identity of the element.
  • Mass number A is the sum of protons and neutrons.
  • Standard pressure is defined as 1 bar (105 Pa).
  • Aqueous solutions are denoted by (aq) indicating hydration.
  • Catalysts lower activation energy without changing ΔH.
  • Reversible reactions are denoted by the ⇌ symbol.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing mass number with atomic mass; mass number is an integer.
  • Omitting state symbols in thermochemical equations leads to errors in enthalpy calculations.
  • Incorrectly assuming standard temperature is 273 K; it is 298 K.
  • Misinterpreting the Δ symbol as a variable rather than a change operator.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Atomic Number (Z)
Number of protons in the nucleus.
Mass Number (A)
Sum of protons and neutrons.
Enthalpy (H)
Heat content of a system.
Standard State (°)
Reference state at 1 bar.
Aqueous (aq)
Dissolved in water.
Reversible (⇌)
Reaction proceeds in both directions.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Z = Protons
  • A = Z + N
  • ΔH < 0 (Exothermic)
  • ΔH > 0 (Endothermic)
  • Standard Pressure = 1 bar

📌 Quick Recall

  • Symbols define chemical identity.
  • Equations must be balanced.
  • States are crucial for thermodynamics.
  • Standard conditions are fixed.

Isotope VS Isobar
