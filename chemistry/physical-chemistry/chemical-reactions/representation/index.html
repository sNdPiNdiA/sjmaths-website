Physical Chemistry

Representation of Chemical Reactions

Chemical reactions are represented quantitatively using balanced stoichiometric equations that conserve mass, charge, and energy. In physical chemistry, these representations extend to thermochemical equations, differential rate expressions, and electrochemical cell notations to define the thermodynamic and kinetic state of the system.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Stoichiometric and State Representations

  • Stoichiometric coefficients (νi) represent the molar ratios of reactants and products, where reactants are assigned negative values and products positive values in thermodynamic formulations (∑ νi Bi = 0).
  • State symbols (s, l, g, aq) are mandatory in thermochemical representations because enthalpy changes (ΔH) depend directly on the physical state of the species.
  • Limiting reagent identification is determined by comparing the mole-to-coefficient ratio (ni / |νi|) for all reactants; the reactant with the minimum ratio is completely consumed.

Kinetic and Rate Representations

  • The differential rate of reaction (r) is represented as r = -1/a · d[A]/dt = 1/p · d[P]/dt for a general reaction aA → pP, ensuring a unique, positive rate value independent of the chosen species.
  • Rate laws represent the empirical relationship between reaction rate and reactant concentrations, r = k[A]x[B]y, where exponents x and y represent partial orders and are not necessarily equal to stoichiometric coefficients.
  • Elementary reaction representations directly equate the molecularity of the step to the stoichiometric coefficients in the rate law, unlike complex multi-step reactions.

Electrochemical and Thermodynamic Representations

  • IUPAC Cell Notation represents galvanic cells by placing the anode (oxidation half-cell) on the left and the cathode (reduction half-cell) on the right, separated by a double vertical line (||) representing the salt bridge.
  • Phase boundaries in cell notation are represented by a single vertical line (|), and concentration or partial pressure values are written in parentheses next to the respective species.
  • Reaction Quotient (Q) representation is formulated as the product of activities of products raised to their stoichiometric coefficients divided by those of reactants, where pure solids and liquids have activities defined as unity (a = 1).
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Differential Rate of Reaction Representation
r = -1/a · d[A]/dt = 1/c · d[C]/dt
Conditions: For a general reaction: aA → cC
Units: r in mol L-1 s-1
Standard Enthalpy of Reaction Representation
ΔH°rxn = ∑ νpΔH°f(products) - ∑ νrΔH°f(reactants)
Conditions: Standard state conditions (1 bar, 298.15 K)
Units: ΔH° in kJ mol-1
Reaction Quotient Representation
Q = Π (aproducts)νp / Π (areactants)νr
Conditions: Any non-equilibrium or equilibrium state
Units: Dimensionless (using activities)
Electrochemical Cell Potential Representation
Ecell = E°cell - (RT / nF) ln Q
Conditions: Reversible electrochemical cell
Units: E in Volts (V), T in Kelvin (K), F in C mol-1
Stoichiometric Conservation and Extent of Reaction
Δξ = Δni / νi
Conditions: Closed system, single reaction pathway
Units: ξ in moles (mol), νi is dimensionless
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Stoichiometric coefficients in balanced chemical equations are typically integers.

Observation: Fractional coefficients in thermochemical equations. — Thermochemical equations represent molar quantities (e.g., 1 mole of H2O), allowing fractional coefficients like H2(g) + 1/2 O2(g) → H2O(l) to maintain a 1-mole product basis.

Exception Rate law exponents match stoichiometric coefficients.

Observation: Complex/multi-step reactions. — The rate-determining step (slowest step) governs the overall rate law, making the empirical order different from the overall stoichiometric coefficients.

Exception All species in a chemical equation appear in the equilibrium constant expression.

Observation: Pure solids, pure liquids, and solvents in dilute solutions. — Their chemical activities remain virtually constant during the reaction and are conventionally defined as 1.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Limiting Reagent Quick Finder
Ratio = Given Moles / Stoichiometric Coefficient

Application: The reactant with the lowest ratio is the limiting reagent; use its ratio multiplied by other coefficients to find product moles.

Cell Notation Mnemonic 'LOAN'
Left, Oxidation, Anode, Negative

Application: Instantly write or interpret the left side of any electrochemical cell representation.

Rate Expression Sign Rule
Reactants get a negative sign (disappearance), products get a positive sign (appearance)

Application: Avoid sign errors when equating rates of consumption and formation: -1/a · Δ[A]/Δt = +1/b · Δ[B]/Δt.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The extent of reaction (ξ) is a state-independent quantity defined as Δni / νi, measured in moles.
  • In thermochemical equations, reversing a reaction changes the sign of ΔH but keeps its magnitude constant (Laplace's Law).
  • For gas-phase reactions, the reaction quotient Qp is represented using partial pressures, where Pi = Xi · Ptotal.
  • In electrochemical cell representation, active gas electrodes (like SHE) require an inert conductor (e.g., Pt) represented at the outer boundary: Pt(s) | H2(g, 1 bar) | H+(aq, 1 M).
  • The rate of a reaction is always a positive quantity, regardless of whether it is expressed in terms of reactants or products.
  • Stoichiometric calculations involving gases at STP use the molar volume representation of 22.7 L/mol (IUPAC standard) or 22.4 L/mol (classical standard).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Omitting physical state symbols in thermochemical equations, which leads to incorrect enthalpy calculations since ΔH of H2O(g) differs from H2O(l).
  • Confusing reaction order with stoichiometric coefficients in complex reactions; always use experimental rate laws rather than balanced equations to write rate expressions.
  • Incorrectly placing the inert electrode (like Pt) in the middle of electrochemical cell notations; it must always be written on the extreme outer ends.
  • Forgetting to divide the rate of consumption/formation of a species by its stoichiometric coefficient when calculating the overall rate of reaction.

⚡ Quick Revision: Representation of Chemical Reactions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Stoichiometric Representation

Balanced equations represent the conservation of atoms and charge, serving as the quantitative foundation for mass-mass, mass-volume, and volume-volume calculations.

Thermochemical Representation

Equations that include physical states and the associated enthalpy change (ΔH), where ΔH is directly proportional to the stoichiometric coefficients.

Kinetic Representation

Differential and integrated rate laws that express the velocity of a reaction as a function of reactant concentrations and stoichiometric coefficients.

Electrochemical Representation

IUPAC cell diagrams that systematically represent phase boundaries, concentrations, and half-cell reactions without drawing the physical cell.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Stoichiometric Coefficient (νi)
Dimensionless number indicating the relative moles of a species participating in a chemical reaction.
Extent of Reaction (ξ)
Quantity representing the progress of a reaction, defined as dξ = dni / νi in moles.
Rate of Reaction (r)
The change in concentration of a reactant or product per unit time divided by its stoichiometric coefficient, in mol L-1 s-1.
Reaction Quotient (Q)
The ratio of the product of activities of products to reactants, each raised to their stoichiometric coefficients at any non-equilibrium state.
Cell Diagram (IUPAC Notation)
A shorthand notation representing the components and phases of an electrochemical cell.
Limiting Reagent (LR)
The reactant that is completely consumed first in a chemical reaction, limiting the amount of product formed.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔH°rxn = ∑ νpΔH°f(products) - ∑ νrΔH°f(reactants)
  • Rate of reaction r = -1/a · d[A]/dt is always positive.
  • Cell notation: Anode (Oxidation) || Cathode (Reduction).
  • Pure solids and liquids have activity a = 1 in equilibrium and rate expressions.
  • Extent of reaction Δξ = Δni / νi.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Balanced chemical equations represent mass conservation and provide stoichiometric ratios for quantitative calculations.
  • Thermochemical equations must specify physical states because phase transitions involve latent heat changes.
  • Rate expressions normalize the rate of consumption/formation of individual species using their stoichiometric coefficients.
  • Electrochemical cell representation follows the IUPAC convention (left anode, right cathode) with phase boundaries clearly marked.

⚖️ Key Differences

Reaction Rate (r) VS Rate of Consumption of Reactant (rA)

Reaction rate is normalized by the stoichiometric coefficient (r = 1/a · rA), whereas the rate of consumption is simply -d[A]/dt.

Question 1 of 18

For the reaction 2A + 3B → 4C, the rate of reaction in terms of disappearance of B is represented as:

Explanation: The rate of reaction is normalized by dividing the rate of change of concentration of any species by its stoichiometric coefficient. For reactant B, it is -1/3 · d[B]/dt.

Question 2 of 18

In the thermochemical equation: H2(g) + 1/2 O2(g) → H2O(l), ΔH = -285.8 kJ/mol. If the equation is written as 2H2(g) + O2(g) → 2H2O(l), what is the value of ΔH?

Explanation: Enthalpy change is an extensive property and is directly proportional to the stoichiometric coefficients. Doubling the coefficients doubles the enthalpy change: -285.8 × 2 = -571.6 kJ/mol.

Question 3 of 18

Which of the following is the correct IUPAC representation for a cell consisting of a zinc anode and a copper cathode?

Explanation: According to IUPAC convention, the anode (oxidation half-cell) is written on the left: Zn(s) | Zn2+(aq), and the cathode (reduction half-cell) on the right: Cu2+(aq) | Cu(s), separated by a salt bridge (||).

Question 4 of 18

For a gas-phase reaction aA(g) &rightleftharpoons bB(g), the relation between Kp and Kc is represented as Kp = Kc(RT)Δn. What does Δn represent?

Explanation: Δn represents the difference between the sum of stoichiometric coefficients of gaseous products and gaseous reactants.

Question 5 of 18

In the representation of a hydrogen electrode, why is Platinum (Pt) included?

Explanation: Platinum is an inert metal that does not take part in the chemical reaction but provides a surface for the adsorption of hydrogen gas and acts as an electrical conductor.

Question 6 of 18

For the reaction N2(g) + 3H2(g) → 2NH3(g), if the rate of disappearance of N2 is 2.0 × 10-4 M s-1, what is the rate of appearance of NH3?

Explanation: From the stoichiometry, -d[N2]/dt = 1/2 · d[NH3]/dt. Therefore, d[NH3]/dt = 2 × (-d[N2]/dt) = 2 × (2.0 × 10-4) = 4.0 × 10-4 M s-1.

Question 7 of 18

In a balanced chemical equation, the sum of stoichiometric coefficients of reactants is always equal to that of products. This statement is:

Explanation: Stoichiometric coefficients represent moles, which are not conserved in chemical reactions (only total mass and atoms are conserved). In isomerization (e.g., A → B), the coefficients on both sides are equal (1 = 1).

Question 8 of 18

Which representation of the reaction quotient Q is correct for the heterogeneous reaction: CaCO3(s) &rightleftharpoons CaO(s) + CO2(g)?

Explanation: The activities of pure solids like CaCO3(s) and CaO(s) are taken as unity (1). Thus, the reaction quotient expression simplifies to Q = [CO2] or PCO2.

Question 9 of 18

The extent of reaction (ξ) has the SI unit of:

Explanation: The extent of reaction is defined as dξ = dni / νi. Since ni is in moles and νi is dimensionless, the unit of ξ is mol.

Question 10 of 18

For the reaction A + 2B → C, if 5 moles of A and 8 moles of B are mixed, the limiting reagent is:

Explanation: Comparing the mole-to-coefficient ratios: for A, 5/1 = 5; for B, 8/2 = 4. Since B has the lower ratio, B is the limiting reagent.

Question 11 of 18

In the electrochemical cell representation Pt(s) | Cl2(g, 1 atm) | Cl-(aq, 1 M) || Ag+(aq, 1 M) | Ag(s), which species undergoes oxidation?

Explanation: The anode is on the left, where oxidation occurs. The anode reaction is 2Cl-(aq) → Cl2(g) + 2e-, meaning Cl-(aq) is oxidized.

Question 12 of 18

Why must the physical states of reactants and products be specified in a thermochemical equation?

Explanation: Enthalpy is a state function. The heat content of a substance varies with its physical state (solid, liquid, gas) due to phase transition energies.

Question 13 of 18

For the reaction 2NO2(g) → 2NO(g) + O2(g), the rate of reaction is represented as r = k[NO2]2. What is the overall order of this reaction?

Explanation: The overall order of the reaction is the sum of the exponents in the rate law. Here, the exponent of [NO2] is 2, so the order is 2.

Question 14 of 18

In a cell diagram, what does a double vertical line (||) represent?

Explanation: By IUPAC convention, a single vertical line (|) represents a phase boundary, and a double vertical line (||) represents a salt bridge or liquid junction.

Question 15 of 18

For the reaction 3A → 2B, the rate of disappearance of A is related to the rate of appearance of B by:

Explanation: The rate of reaction is r = -1/3 · d[A]/dt = 1/2 · d[B]/dt. Multiplying by 3 gives -d[A]/dt = 3/2 · d[B]/dt.

Question 16 of 18

In representing the standard hydrogen electrode (SHE), the concentration of H+ ions is maintained at:

Explanation: By definition, the standard state for solute species in electrochemical cells is a concentration of 1.0 M (or more precisely, unit activity).

Question 17 of 18

If the reaction quotient Q is greater than the equilibrium constant K (Q > K), the reaction representation indicates that:

Explanation: When Q > K, there are excess products relative to reactants compared to the equilibrium state. The reaction will shift in the reverse direction to reach equilibrium.

Question 18 of 18

In the representation of the rate law r = k[A]m[B]n, if doubling the concentration of A quadruples the rate, the value of m is:

Explanation: Since rate ∝ [A]m, doubling [A] results in 2m times the rate. Since the rate quadruples (4 = 22), m must be 2.

UP PGT 2021
For the reaction N2O5(g) → 2NO2(g) + 1/2 O2(g), the rate of disappearance of N2O5 is written as -d[N2O5]/dt = k1[N2O5]. The rates of formation of NO2 and O2 are written as d[NO2]/dt = k2[N2O5] and d[O2]/dt = k3[N2O5]. What is the relationship between k1, k2, and k3?
Answer: Option D (k1 = k2/2 = 2k3) The rate of reaction is r = -d[N2O5]/dt = 1/2 · d[NO2]/dt = 2 · d[O2]/dt. Substituting the rate expressions: k1[N2O5] = 1/2 · k2[N2O5] = 2 · k3[N2O5]. Thus, k1 = k2/2 = 2k3.
UP PGT 2016
Which of the following represents the correct cell notation for the reaction: Mg(s) + 2Ag+(aq) → Mg2+(aq) + 2Ag(s)?
Answer: Option A (Mg(s) | Mg2+(aq) || Ag+(aq) | Ag(s)) Mg is oxidized to Mg2+ (anode, left side), and Ag+ is reduced to Ag (cathode, right side). The phases are separated by single vertical lines, and the half-cells by a double vertical line.
UP PGT 2013
In a thermochemical equation, C(graphite) + O2(g) → CO2(g), ΔH = -393.5 kJ. What does this representation imply about the stability of the product compared to the reactants?
Answer: Option B (Product is more stable than reactants) A negative ΔH (exothermic reaction) indicates that the product CO2 lies at a lower potential energy level than the reactants, meaning it is thermodynamically more stable.
Model Question
For the reaction 2A + B → 3C, if the rate of disappearance of A is 0.4 mol L-1 s-1, what is the rate of the reaction?
Answer: Option B (0.2 mol L-1 s-1) The rate of reaction is r = -1/2 · d[A]/dt. Given -d[A]/dt = 0.4 mol L-1 s-1, the rate of reaction is 0.4 / 2 = 0.2 mol L-1 s-1.
UP PGT 2011
How is the standard electrode potential of a half-cell represented when coupled with a Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)?
Answer: Option C (By measuring the cell EMF under standard conditions with SHE as the reference) The standard electrode potential of any electrode is measured by coupling it with a SHE (whose potential is defined as 0.00 V) under standard conditions of concentration (1 M) and pressure (1 bar).

Question 1 of 10
For the reaction aA → bB, the rate of reaction is represented as r = -1/a · d[A]/dt = 1/b · d[B]/dt. If a=2 and b=3, and the rate of consumption of A is 0.06 M s-1, what is the rate of formation of B?

Solution: r = 1/2 · (0.06) = 0.03 M s-1. Therefore, d[B]/dt = 3 · r = 3 × 0.03 = 0.09 M s-1.

Question 2 of 10
In the representation of the Nernst equation for the cell reaction Zn(s) + Cu2+(aq) → Zn2+(aq) + Cu(s), the reaction quotient Q is:

Solution: Pure solids Zn(s) and Cu(s) have activities of 1. Therefore, the reaction quotient Q is represented solely by the ratio of the active dissolved ions: [Zn2+]/[Cu2+].

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following representations correctly shows the relationship between standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) and the standard equilibrium constant (K)?

Solution: The thermodynamic relationship is ΔG° = -RT ln K. If using base-10 logarithm, it is ΔG° = -2.303 RT log K.

Question 4 of 10
For the reaction H2(g) + I2(g) &rightleftharpoons 2HI(g), the equilibrium constant is K. What is the equilibrium constant representation for the reaction 1/2 H2(g) + 1/2 I2(g) &rightleftharpoons HI(g)?

Solution: When the stoichiometric coefficients of a balanced reaction are multiplied by a factor 'n' (here, n = 1/2), the new equilibrium constant is raised to the power of 'n'. Thus, K' = K1/2.

Question 5 of 10
In the cell representation Pt(s) | Fe2+(aq), Fe3+(aq) || Ce4+(aq), Ce3+(aq) | Pt(s), what separates the Fe2+ and Fe3+ ions in the anode half-cell?

Solution: When two chemical species in the same half-cell are in the same phase (both are aqueous ions), they are separated by a comma (,) rather than a vertical line.

Question 6 of 10
In a stoichiometric calculation, if 22.4 L of H2(g) at STP reacts with 11.2 L of O2(g) at STP to form H2O(l), the mass of water formed is:

Solution: 22.4 L of H2 = 1 mol; 11.2 L of O2 = 0.5 mol. The reaction is H2 + 1/2 O2 → H2O. Both reactants are in exact stoichiometric proportions, yielding 1 mol of H2O, which has a mass of 18 g.

Question 7 of 10
For the reaction A + 3B → 2C, the rate of reaction r is represented as:

Solution: The rate of reaction is obtained by dividing the rate of change of each species by its stoichiometric coefficient, with a negative sign for reactants: r = -d[A]/dt = -1/3 · d[B]/dt = 1/2 · d[C]/dt.

Question 8 of 10
In the thermochemical representation of combustion of methane: CH4(g) + 2O2(g) → CO2(g) + 2H2O(l), ΔHc° = -890 kJ/mol. What is the enthalpy change for the formation of 9 g of H2O(l)?

Solution: The reaction produces 2 moles of H2O (36 g) with an enthalpy change of -890 kJ. For 9 g of H2O (0.5 mol), the enthalpy change is -890 × (0.5 / 2) = -222.5 kJ.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following is the correct representation of the rate constant unit for a second-order reaction?

Solution: The general unit for a rate constant is (mol L-1)1-n s-1. For n = 2, the unit is (mol L-1)-1 s-1 = L mol-1 s-1.

Question 10 of 10
In the cell representation Zn(s) | Zn2+(1.0 M) || Cu2+(0.01 M) | Cu(s), if the concentration of Cu2+ is decreased, the cell potential (Ecell) will:

Solution: According to the Nernst equation: Ecell = E°cell - (0.0591/2) log([Zn2+]/[Cu2+]). Decreasing [Cu2+] increases the value of the log term, which is subtracted, thereby decreasing Ecell.
