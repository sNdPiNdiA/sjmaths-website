Chemical Kinetics and Reaction Rates

Chemical kinetics describes the rate at which chemical reactions proceed and the molecular mechanisms by which they occur. It relates the change in concentration of reactants or products over time to the reaction rate constant and order of reaction.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Reaction Order and Molecularity

  • Order of reaction is the sum of powers of concentration terms in the rate law expression.
  • Molecularity refers to the number of reacting species colliding simultaneously in an elementary step.
  • Molecularity is always a whole number, whereas order can be fractional or zero.
  • For complex reactions, the rate-determining step dictates the overall reaction order.

Collision Theory and Activation Energy

  • Reactions occur when molecules collide with energy exceeding the activation energy (Ea).
  • The steric factor (P) accounts for the requirement of proper molecular orientation during collision.
  • Catalysts increase reaction rate by providing an alternative pathway with lower activation energy.
  • The transition state is the high-energy configuration at the peak of the reaction coordinate.

Temperature Dependence

  • Reaction rates typically double or triple with a 10°C rise in temperature.
  • The Arrhenius plot of ln k versus 1/T yields a straight line with slope -Ea/R.
  • The pre-exponential factor (A) represents the frequency of collisions.
  • High Ea values result in greater sensitivity of the rate constant to temperature changes.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Arrhenius Equation
k = Ae-Ea/RT
Conditions: Temperature dependence of reaction rate
Units: Ea in J mol-1, R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1
First-Order Integrated Rate Law
ln[A]t = -kt + ln[A]0
Conditions: Single reactant, first-order kinetics
Units: k in s-1
Half-life of First-Order Reaction
t1/2 = 0.693 / k
Conditions: Independent of initial concentration
Units: t1/2 in seconds
Zero-Order Integrated Rate Law
[A]t = -kt + [A]0
Conditions: Rate independent of concentration
Units: k in mol L-1 s-1
Rate Law
Rate = k[A]m[B]n
Conditions: Empirical relation determined by experiment
Units: k in (mol L-1)1-(m+n) s-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Rate increases with concentration

Observation: Zero-order reactions — The reaction rate is independent of reactant concentration, often due to surface catalysis saturation.

Exception Rate increases with temperature

Observation: Some enzyme-catalyzed reactions — Protein denaturation at high temperatures leads to a decrease in reaction rate.

Exception Molecularity equals order in elementary steps

Observation: Complex reactions — The overall order is determined by the slowest elementary step, not the stoichiometry of the balanced equation.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Zero-order reactions have units of mol L-1 s-1.
  • First-order reactions have units of s-1.
  • Second-order reactions have units of L mol-1 s-1.
  • Catalysts do not change the equilibrium constant.
  • The rate-determining step is the slowest step in a mechanism.
  • Activation energy is always positive.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing molecularity with order; molecularity is for elementary steps only.
  • Forgetting to convert temperature to Kelvin in Arrhenius calculations.
  • Assuming the rate law matches the stoichiometric coefficients of the balanced equation.
  • Neglecting the negative sign in the rate expression for reactants.

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Rate Law Expression

The rate law relates reaction rate to reactant concentrations using the rate constant k and reaction orders.

Integrated Rate Equations

These equations provide concentration as a function of time for specific reaction orders.

Temperature Effects

The Arrhenius equation quantifies how temperature and activation energy influence the rate constant.

Catalysis

Catalysts lower activation energy, increasing the rate without being consumed in the net reaction.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Rate Constant (k)
Proportionality constant in the rate law.
Activation Energy (Ea)
Minimum energy for reaction.
Half-life (t1/2)
Time for concentration to reach half its initial value.
Elementary Step (Single step)
A reaction occurring in one collision event.
Complex Reaction (Multi-step)
Reaction occurring via multiple elementary steps.
Transition State (Activated Complex)
Transient high-energy state.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • First order: ln[A] = -kt + ln[A]0
  • Zero order: [A] = -kt + [A]0
  • Arrhenius: k = Ae-Ea/RT
  • Rate = -d[R]/dt = d[P]/dt
  • R = 8.314 J/K mol

📌 Quick Recall

  • Order is experimental.
  • Molecularity is theoretical.
  • Catalysts lower Ea.
  • Temperature increases k.
