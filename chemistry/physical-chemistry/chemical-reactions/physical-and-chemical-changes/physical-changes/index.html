Physical Chemistry

Physical Changes

Physical changes involve alterations in the state or appearance of matter without changing its chemical composition or molecular identity. These processes are typically reversible and involve the rearrangement of intermolecular forces rather than the breaking or formation of covalent or ionic bonds.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamics of Phase Transitions

  • Phase transitions occur at constant temperature and pressure where the Gibbs Free Energy change (ΔG) is zero.
  • The entropy (ΔS) increases during melting and vaporization as molecular disorder increases.
  • Intermolecular forces like Hydrogen bonding or London dispersion forces are overcome without altering the internal molecular structure.

Physical Properties and State Functions

  • Physical changes affect intensive properties like density, refractive index, and boiling point.
  • Mass and total energy are conserved in accordance with the Law of Conservation of Mass and the First Law of Thermodynamics.
  • Solubility is a physical property defined by the maximum amount of solute that dissolves in a solvent at a specific temperature.

Classification of Physical Processes

  • Processes include phase changes (melting, freezing, sublimation, condensation) and mechanical changes (crushing, grinding).
  • Formation of mixtures or solutions is considered a physical change as components retain their individual chemical properties.
  • Separation techniques like distillation, chromatography, and filtration rely on differences in physical properties.

ln(P2/P1) = (ΔHvap/R) * (1/T1 - 1/T2)
Conditions: Phase transition between liquid and vapor
Units: P in Pa, T in Kelvin, R = 8.314 J/mol·K
Heat of Sublimation
ΔHsub = ΔHfus + ΔHvap
Conditions: Hess's Law application for phase transitions
Units: kJ/mol
Density Relation
ρ = m/V
Conditions: Mass remains constant during physical change
Units: g/cm³
Enthalpy of Fusion
ΔHfus = Hliquid - Hsolid
Conditions: At constant pressure and melting point
Units: kJ/mol
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Density increases upon solidification

Observation: Water (H2O) — Hydrogen bonding creates an open hexagonal lattice structure in ice, making it less dense than liquid water.

Exception Substances expand upon heating

Observation: Invar (Fe-Ni alloy) — Low coefficient of thermal expansion due to magnetic properties counteracting lattice vibrations.

Exception Melting point increases with pressure

Observation: Ice — The solid phase is less dense than the liquid phase, causing the melting point to decrease as pressure increases (Le Chatelier's Principle).

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
The Reversibility Test
Can I recover the original substance by simple physical separation?

Application: If yes, it is a physical change; if no (e.g., burning, rusting), it is a chemical change.

Composition Check
Check the molecular formula before and after

Application: If the formula remains unchanged (e.g., H2O(s) → H2O(l)), it is physical.

Energy Indicator
Compare ΔHphysical vs ΔHchemical

Application: Physical changes involve significantly lower enthalpy changes compared to bond-breaking chemical reactions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Melting of ice is an endothermic physical process.
  • Sublimation is the direct transition from solid to gas phase.
  • Distillation separates components based on boiling point differences.
  • Dissolution of sugar in water is a physical change.
  • Magnetization of iron is a physical change.
  • Phase changes occur at constant temperature (isothermal).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing dissolution with chemical reaction; dissolution is physical if no new species are formed.
  • Assuming all color changes are chemical; physical changes like heating a metal can cause color changes.
  • Ignoring the role of pressure in phase transitions for substances like water.
  • Misidentifying the formation of alloys as chemical compounds; alloys are physical mixtures.

⚡ Quick Revision: Physical Changes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Phase Equilibrium

At the phase transition temperature, the chemical potentials of the two phases are equal.

μsolid = μliquid at the melting point.

Latent Heat

Energy absorbed or released during a phase change without a temperature change.

q = m × L, where L is the specific latent heat.

Mixture Formation

Mixing substances creates a physical combination where each component retains its chemical identity.

Properties of mixtures are often weighted averages of components.

Separation Principles

Physical separation relies on differences in boiling points, solubility, or particle size.

No chemical bonds are broken during these processes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔG = 0 at phase transition
  • Water density anomaly
  • Physical changes are reversible
  • No new chemical bonds form
  • Conservation of mass holds

📌 Quick Recall

  • Physical changes alter state, not identity.
  • Phase transitions are isothermal.
  • Energy is required to overcome intermolecular forces.
  • Mixtures are physical combinations.

⚖️ Key Differences

Dissolution VS Reaction

Dissolution is physical; reaction creates new chemical species.
