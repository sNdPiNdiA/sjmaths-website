Physical Chemistry

Chemical Changes

Chemical changes involve the transformation of one or more substances into new chemical species through the breaking and forming of chemical bonds. These processes are characterized by permanent changes in chemical composition and are typically accompanied by energy exchange in the form of heat, light, or electricity.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamics of Chemical Transformation

  • Chemical changes are governed by the Gibbs Free Energy criterion where ΔG < 0 indicates a spontaneous process.
  • Exothermic reactions release energy (ΔH < 0), while endothermic reactions absorb energy (ΔH > 0).
  • The Entropy change (ΔS) of the universe must be positive for any spontaneous chemical change to occur.

Kinetics and Reaction Mechanism

  • The Rate Determining Step (RDS) is the slowest elementary step that dictates the overall reaction velocity.
  • Activation energy (Ea) represents the minimum energy barrier required for reactants to form the transition state.
  • Catalysts increase reaction rates by providing an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy without being consumed.

Stoichiometry and Chemical Equilibrium

  • Balanced chemical equations must satisfy the Law of Conservation of Mass and charge balance.
  • Chemical equilibrium is reached when the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction.
  • Le Chatelier's Principle predicts the shift in equilibrium position upon changes in concentration, pressure, or temperature.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Reaction Enthalpy
ΔH°rxn = ∑ ΔH°f(products) - ∑ ΔH°f(reactants)
Conditions: Standard state (1 bar, 298 K)
Units: kJ/mol
Arrhenius Equation
k = Ae-Ea/RT
Conditions: Elementary reactions
Units: k (s-1 or M-1s-1), Ea (J/mol)
Gibbs Free Energy Change
ΔG = ΔH - TΔS
Conditions: Constant temperature and pressure
Units: Joules (J)
Rate Law
Rate = k[A]x[B]y
Conditions: Empirical determination
Units: mol L-1 s-1
Law of Conservation of Mass
∑ massreactants = ∑ massproducts
Conditions: Closed system
Units: grams (g)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Increased temperature increases reaction rate

Observation: Explosive decomposition or enzyme denaturation — High temperatures can destroy the catalyst or reactant structure, leading to rate inhibition.

Exception Noble gases are chemically inert

Observation: XeF2, XeF4, XeF6 formation — High electronegativity of fluorine allows excitation of electrons into d-orbitals of Xenon.

Exception Bond energy increases with bond order

Observation: F2 has lower bond dissociation energy than Cl2 — High inter-electronic repulsion between lone pairs in the small F-F bond.

Application: Oxidation Is Loss of electrons, Reduction Is Gain of electrons.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Chemical changes involve breaking and forming of covalent or ionic bonds.
  • Combustion is a rapid chemical change involving oxygen as an oxidant.
  • A catalyst alters the reaction path but not the equilibrium constant Keq.
  • The transition state is a high-energy, short-lived species in a reaction mechanism.
  • Standard enthalpy of formation for elements in their stable state is zero.
  • Chemical changes are always accompanied by a change in internal energy.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing physical state changes (melting) with chemical reactions.
  • Ignoring the temperature dependence of the equilibrium constant K.
  • Assuming all reactions with negative ΔG are fast (kinetics vs thermodynamics).
  • Miscalculating stoichiometry by failing to balance atoms and charges.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Changes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Chemical Kinetics

Focuses on reaction rates and mechanisms. Governed by the Arrhenius equation and collision theory.

Chemical Thermodynamics

Determines the spontaneity of reactions using ΔG, ΔH, and ΔS. Governed by the Laws of Thermodynamics.

Chemical Equilibrium

Describes the state where forward and reverse rates are equal. Governed by the Law of Mass Action.

Redox Chemistry

Involves electron transfer between species. Governed by Nernst equation and oxidation state changes.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Enthalpy (H)
Heat content of a system at constant pressure.
Entropy (S)
Measure of disorder or randomness in a system.
Activation Energy (Ea)
Energy barrier for a reaction.
Catalyst (cat)
Substance that increases rate without being consumed.
Equilibrium Constant (Keq)
Ratio of product to reactant concentrations at equilibrium.
Spontaneity (ΔG)
Ability of a process to occur without external work.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔG = -nFE°cell
  • ln(k2/k1) = (Ea/R)(1/T1 - 1/T2)
  • Kp = Kc(RT)Δn
  • pH = -log[H+]
  • Rate = k[A]n

📌 Quick Recall

  • Chemical changes create new substances.
  • Thermodynamics predicts spontaneity.
  • Kinetics predicts reaction speed.
  • Equilibrium defines the extent of reaction.

⚖️ Key Differences

Endothermic VS Exothermic

Endothermic absorbs heat (ΔH > 0), Exothermic releases heat (ΔH < 0).
