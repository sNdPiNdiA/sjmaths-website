Physical Chemistry

Physical and Chemical Changes

Physical changes involve alterations in the state or appearance of matter without changing its chemical composition. Chemical changes involve the formation of new substances through the breaking and making of chemical bonds.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Characteristics of Physical Changes

  • Physical changes are reversible processes where the chemical identity of the substance remains constant.
  • Changes in state (melting, boiling, sublimation) are physical changes involving only intermolecular force adjustments.
  • No new chemical species are formed; the molecular formula remains unchanged.
  • Energy changes are typically small, corresponding to enthalpy of fusion or vaporization.

Characteristics of Chemical Changes

  • Chemical changes involve the rearrangement of atoms to form new substances with distinct chemical properties.
  • These processes are generally irreversible under normal conditions and involve significant energy release or absorption.
  • Evidence includes gas evolution, precipitate formation, or permanent color change.
  • The reaction follows the Law of Definite Proportions and stoichiometry.

Thermodynamic Distinctions

  • Chemical changes involve the breaking of covalent or ionic bonds, requiring high activation energy (Ea).
  • Physical changes involve overcoming van der Waals forces or hydrogen bonding.
  • The entropy change (ΔS) is typically larger in chemical reactions due to changes in molecular structure.
  • Chemical reactions are governed by chemical kinetics and equilibrium constants (Keq).

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Dissolution of salts is physical

Observation: Hydration of anhydrous salts — Formation of coordinate covalent bonds with water molecules constitutes a chemical change.

Exception Phase changes are physical

Observation: Sublimation of ammonium chloride — Often accompanied by thermal dissociation into NH3 and HCl gas.

Exception Chemical changes are irreversible

Observation: Reversible chemical reactions — Dynamic equilibrium allows reactants and products to coexist and interconvert.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
The 'New Substance' Test
Can you recover the original substance by simple physical separation?

Application: If yes, it is physical; if no, it is chemical.

Energy Signature
High energy change = Chemical; Low energy change = Physical

Application: Used to distinguish between phase transitions and combustion/redox reactions.

Bonding Rule
Intramolecular bond change = Chemical; Intermolecular force change = Physical

Application: Analyze if the structural formula of the molecule is altered.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Melting of ice is a physical change.
  • Rusting of iron is a chemical change (Fe2O3·xH2O).
  • Combustion of fuels is an exothermic chemical change.
  • Distillation is a physical separation method.
  • Electrolysis of water is a chemical change.
  • Sublimation of iodine is a physical change.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing dissolution of sugar (physical) with reaction of sugar with H2SO4 (chemical).
  • Assuming all color changes are chemical; some are physical (e.g., heating CoCl2).
  • Ignoring the role of catalysts in accelerating chemical changes.
  • Misidentifying phase transitions as chemical reactions.

⚡ Quick Revision: Physical and Chemical Changes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Matter Identity

Physical changes preserve molecular identity, while chemical changes alter the internal atomic arrangement.

Energy Dynamics

Chemical changes involve breaking/forming bonds, requiring activation energy (Ea) and releasing/absorbing enthalpy (ΔH).

Reversibility

Physical changes are easily reversed by physical means; chemical changes require specific chemical processes to reverse.

Stoichiometry

Chemical reactions obey the Law of Conservation of Mass and definite proportions.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Physical changes: No new substance.
  • Chemical changes: New substance formed.
  • Bond breaking is endothermic.
  • Bond formation is exothermic.
  • Mass is conserved in all reactions.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Physical = State change.
  • Chemical = Composition change.
  • Reversibility is the key indicator.
  • Energy magnitude distinguishes the two.

⚖️ Key Differences

Dissolving VS Reacting

Dissolving is physical; reacting forms new bonds.
