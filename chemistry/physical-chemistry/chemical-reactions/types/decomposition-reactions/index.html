Physical Chemistry

Decomposition Reactions

Decomposition reactions involve the breakdown of a single chemical compound into two or more simpler substances, typically requiring an input of energy. These processes are generally endothermic, represented by the general form AB → A + B.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermal Decomposition Mechanisms

  • Thermal decomposition involves the cleavage of chemical bonds via heat energy.
  • Metal carbonates decompose to metal oxides and carbon dioxide: MCO3 → MO + CO2.
  • Metal nitrates decompose to metal nitrites and oxygen, or metal oxides for heavier metals.
  • The stability of oxoanions increases with the size of the central metal cation.

Photolytic and Electrolytic Pathways

  • Photodecomposition occurs when molecules absorb photons of sufficient energy to reach an excited state.
  • Silver halides (AgCl, AgBr) undergo photolysis: 2AgX(s) → 2Ag(s) + X2(g).
  • Electrolytic decomposition uses direct current to drive non-spontaneous redox reactions.
  • Water electrolysis: 2H2O(l) → 2H2(g) + O2(g) at electrodes.

Kinetics of Decomposition

  • Most unimolecular decompositions follow first-order kinetics.
  • The rate is proportional to the concentration of the reactant: Rate = k[A].
  • Decomposition reactions are characterized by a positive entropy change (ΔS > 0) due to increased disorder.
  • High activation energy (Ea) often dictates the thermal stability of the compound.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
First-Order Integrated Rate Law
ln[A]t = -kt + ln[A]0
Conditions: Unimolecular decomposition
Units: [A] in mol/L, k in s-1
Half-life of First-Order Reaction
t1/2 = 0.693/k
Conditions: First-order kinetics
Units: t1/2 in seconds
Gibbs Free Energy Change
ΔG° = ΔH° - TΔS°
Conditions: Spontaneity at constant T and P
Units: ΔG, ΔH in J/mol, ΔS in J/mol·K
Arrhenius Equation for Rate Constant
k = Ae-Ea/RT
Conditions: Temperature dependence of decomposition rate
Units: k in s-1, Ea in J/mol, T in K
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Group 1 carbonates are thermally stable

Observation: Li2CO3 decomposes upon heating — High charge density of Li+ polarizes the carbonate ion.

Exception Nitrates decompose to nitrites

Observation: LiNO3 and heavy metal nitrates decompose to oxides — Strong polarizing power of small or highly charged cations.

Exception Thermal decomposition is endothermic

Observation: Ozone decomposition (2O3 → 3O2) is exothermic — Formation of stronger O=O double bonds releases significant energy.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Thermal decomposition of CaCO3 requires temperatures above 800°C.
  • Ammonium nitrate decomposition can be explosive: NH4NO3 → N2O + 2H2O.
  • Hydrogen peroxide decomposition is catalyzed by MnO2.
  • Decomposition reactions are inherently entropy-driven at high temperatures.
  • The rate constant k increases exponentially with temperature per the Arrhenius equation.
  • Metal chlorates decompose to metal chlorides and oxygen gas.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing metal nitrate decomposition products; remember Li and heavy metals yield oxides.
  • Assuming all decomposition reactions are first-order; some are complex or second-order.
  • Neglecting the role of catalysts in lowering activation energy for decomposition.
  • Incorrectly balancing redox equations in electrolytic decomposition.

⚡ Quick Revision: Decomposition Reactions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Thermodynamics of Decomposition

Decomposition is spontaneous when ΔG < 0, which is favored at high temperatures due to the positive ΔS term.

Kinetic Control

The rate of decomposition is governed by the Arrhenius equation, where Ea represents the energy barrier for bond cleavage.

Cation Polarization

Fajans' rules explain why small, highly charged cations destabilize oxoanions, leading to easier decomposition.

Catalytic Effects

Catalysts provide an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy, significantly increasing the decomposition rate.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Activation Energy (Ea)
Minimum energy required for reaction.
Rate Constant (k)
Proportionality constant in rate law.
Half-life (t1/2)
Time for 50% reactant consumption.
Endothermic (ΔH > 0)
Heat absorbed from surroundings.
Photolysis (hν)
Decomposition induced by light.
Electrolysis (I)
Decomposition induced by electricity.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • 2KClO3 → 2KCl + 3O2
  • CaCO3 → CaO + CO2
  • 2H2O2 → 2H2O + O2
  • ln(k2/k1) = (Ea/R)(1/T1 - 1/T2)
  • ΔG = ΔH - TΔS

📌 Quick Recall

  • Decomposition is the reverse of synthesis.
  • Energy input is mandatory.
  • Cation size dictates thermal stability.
  • Kinetics follow Arrhenius behavior.

⚖️ Key Differences

Thermal Decomposition VS Dissociation

Dissociation is often reversible; decomposition is typically irreversible.
