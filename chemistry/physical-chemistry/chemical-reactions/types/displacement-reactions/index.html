Physical Chemistry

Displacement Reactions

Displacement reactions involve the substitution of one element or ion in a compound by another more reactive species. These processes are fundamentally governed by the relative standard reduction potentials of the chemical species involved.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrochemical Series and Reactivity

  • A metal with a more negative standard reduction potential displaces a metal with a more positive potential from its salt solution.
  • The Electrochemical Series ranks elements by their ability to act as reducing agents.
  • Fluorine (F2) is the strongest oxidizing agent and displaces other halides from their salts.
  • Metals above Hydrogen in the series displace H2 gas from dilute acids.

Single Displacement Mechanisms

  • General form: A + BC → AC + B, where A is a more reactive metal than B.
  • These reactions are inherently redox processes involving electron transfer.
  • The species being displaced undergoes reduction, while the displacing agent undergoes oxidation.
  • Non-metal displacement involves the substitution of a less electronegative non-metal by a more electronegative one.

Thermodynamics of Displacement

  • A displacement reaction is spontaneous only if the total cell potential E°cell is positive.
  • The value of ΔG° must be negative for the reaction to proceed in the forward direction.
  • Temperature dependence is governed by the Van't Hoff equation if E° varies with T.
  • Surface area of the solid reactant influences the kinetics but not the thermodynamic feasibility.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Gibbs Free Energy Relation
ΔG° = -nFE°cell
Conditions: Spontaneous reaction at standard state
Units: ΔG in Joules, n in moles, F in Coulombs/mol
Electrochemical Series Ranking
E°red(A) > E°red(B)
Conditions: Standard conditions (298K, 1M concentration)
Units: E° in Volts
Equilibrium Constant Relation
ln Keq = (nFE°cell) / RT
Conditions: At chemical equilibrium
Units: Keq dimensionless, R in J/mol·K
Nernst Equation
Ecell = E°cell - (RT/nF)lnQ
Conditions: Non-standard conditions, aqueous solution
Units: E in Volts, R in J/mol·K, T in Kelvin
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkali metals displace H from water

Observation: Beryllium (Be) does not react with water — High ionization energy and small ionic radius prevent spontaneous reaction.

Exception More reactive metal displaces less reactive

Observation: Gold (Au) and Platinum (Pt) are resistant to acid displacement — Extremely high positive standard reduction potentials.

Exception Halogens displace heavier halides

Observation: Astatine (At) chemistry is highly radioactive and unstable — Relativistic effects and short half-life complicate standard displacement behavior.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Zinc displaces Copper from CuSO4 solution.
  • Iron displaces Copper from Copper(II) sulfate.
  • The standard potential of the SHE is defined as 0.00 V.
  • Displacement reactions are always redox reactions.
  • Alkali metals react violently with water to displace hydrogen.
  • Displacement of a metal by a more reactive metal is a common metallurgical process.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming all metals displace hydrogen from water (only active metals do).
  • Ignoring concentration effects in the Nernst equation for non-standard cells.
  • Confusing displacement with double decomposition (precipitation) reactions.
  • Miscalculating E°cell by reversing the cathode/anode signs.

⚡ Quick Revision: Displacement Reactions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Redox Basis

Displacement reactions are driven by the transfer of electrons from a more electropositive metal to a less electropositive metal ion.

Electrochemical Series

The series provides a quantitative hierarchy of reduction potentials, predicting the direction of spontaneous displacement.

Thermodynamic Feasibility

Spontaneity is strictly determined by ΔG° < 0, which corresponds to E°cell > 0.

Halogen Displacement

Non-metal displacement follows the trend of electronegativity, where higher electronegativity elements displace lower ones.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • E°cell > 0 for spontaneous reactions
  • F2 is the strongest oxidizing agent
  • Li is the strongest reducing agent
  • Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu
  • Cl2 + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br2

📌 Quick Recall

  • Displacement requires a more reactive species.
  • Redox is the underlying mechanism.
  • Standard potentials predict feasibility.
  • Concentration affects actual potential.

⚖️ Key Differences

Displacement VS Double Displacement

Displacement involves redox; double displacement involves ion exchange.
