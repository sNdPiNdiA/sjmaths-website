Physical Chemistry

Dissociation Reactions

Dissociation is the reversible process where a chemical compound breaks down into simpler constituents such as atoms, ions, or radicals. It is governed by the law of mass action and is typically characterized by an equilibrium constant Kc or Kp.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamics of Dissociation

  • Dissociation reactions are generally endothermic (ΔH > 0) as energy is required to break chemical bonds.
  • According to Le Chatelier's Principle, increasing temperature shifts the equilibrium toward the products for endothermic dissociation.
  • The Gibbs Free Energy change ΔG° = -RT ln K relates the spontaneity of the dissociation process to the equilibrium constant.

Equilibrium Constants and Stoichiometry

  • The value of Kc depends solely on temperature and is independent of initial concentrations or pressure.
  • For a reaction A → nB, the equilibrium concentration of B is given by cα and A by c(1-α).
  • The reaction quotient (Q) determines the direction of the reaction by comparing it to the equilibrium constant K.

Electrolytic Dissociation

  • Ostwald's Dilution Law states that Ka = (cα2) / (1 - α) for weak electrolytes.
  • Dissociation of strong electrolytes is considered complete in aqueous solution, represented by a single arrow.
  • The common ion effect suppresses the dissociation of a weak electrolyte by increasing the concentration of a product ion.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Dissociation Equilibrium Constant (Kc)
Kc = [Products]n / [Reactants]m
Conditions: Constant temperature, closed system
Units: molΔn L-Δn
Van't Hoff Factor (i)
i = 1 + (n - 1)α
Conditions: For solutes undergoing dissociation
Units: Dimensionless
Relationship between Kp and Kc
Kp = Kc(RT)Δn
Conditions: Ideal gas behavior
Units: Kp in atmΔn, Kc in molΔn L-Δn
Degree of Dissociation
α = (Number of moles dissociated) / (Total initial moles)
Conditions: Applicable to weak electrolytes and gaseous equilibria
Units: Dimensionless
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Increased pressure shifts equilibrium to fewer moles

Observation: Dissociation of N2O4 → 2NO2 — Pressure increase shifts equilibrium toward the side with fewer moles of gas.

Exception Strong acids dissociate completely

Observation: HF (Hydrofluoric acid) — Strong hydrogen bonding and high bond dissociation energy make it a weak acid.

Exception Dissociation is endothermic

Observation: Exothermic dissociation of some complex ions — Lattice energy or hydration energy factors can override bond breaking energy.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
The α Approximation
α = √(Ka/c)

Application: Use when α < 0.05 to simplify quadratic equations in weak acid/base dissociation.

Delta n calculation
Δn = Σnproducts - Σnreactants

Application: Only count gaseous species when calculating Δn for Kp expressions.

ICE Table Method
Initial, Change, Equilibrium

Application: Systematically track moles for complex dissociation reactions to avoid stoichiometric errors.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Dissociation of PCl5 is favored by low pressure.
  • The unit of Kc is (mol/L)Δn.
  • Van't Hoff factor i > 1 for dissociation.
  • Temperature is the only factor that changes the value of K.
  • Ostwald's law applies to weak electrolytes only.
  • Dissociation of HI is exothermic in reverse, endothermic forward.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Including solids or liquids in the Kc expression; only gases and aqueous species are included.
  • Confusing α (degree of dissociation) with % dissociation (100α).
  • Forgetting to square or cube stoichiometric coefficients in the Kc expression.
  • Assuming Δn = 0 for all reactions.

⚡ Quick Revision: Dissociation Reactions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Equilibrium Constant

Kc is the ratio of product concentrations to reactant concentrations at equilibrium, raised to their stoichiometric coefficients.

Degree of Dissociation

α represents the fraction of total moles that have dissociated at equilibrium.

Le Chatelier's Principle

Systems at equilibrium adjust to counteract changes in pressure, temperature, or concentration.

Van't Hoff Factor

i accounts for the increase in particle count due to dissociation in colligative property calculations.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Kc (Equilibrium Constant)
Ratio of product to reactant concentrations.
α (Degree of Dissociation)
Fraction of substance dissociated.
Δn (Change in moles)
Difference in gaseous moles.
i (Van't Hoff Factor)
Ratio of observed to theoretical property.
Q (Reaction Quotient)
Ratio at any time t.
Kp (Pressure Constant)
Equilibrium constant using partial pressures.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Kp = Kc(RT)Δn
  • i = 1 + (n-1)α
  • α = √(K/c)
  • ΔG° = -RT ln K
  • K is temperature dependent

📌 Quick Recall

  • Dissociation is reversible.
  • K depends on T.
  • Pressure affects gaseous dissociation.
  • α increases with dilution.

⚖️ Key Differences

Kc VS Q

Kc is at equilibrium; Q is at any time.
