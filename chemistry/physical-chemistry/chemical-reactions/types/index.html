Physical Chemistry

Classification of Chemical Reactions

Chemical reactions are categorized based on the nature of atomic rearrangement, electron transfer, or energy exchange. These classifications include combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement, and redox processes.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Stoichiometric Reaction Types

  • Combination reactions involve the synthesis of a single product from multiple reactants (A + B → AB).
  • Decomposition reactions require energy input to break a single reactant into multiple products (AB → A + B).
  • Displacement reactions occur when a more reactive element replaces a less reactive one in a compound (A + BC → AC + B).
  • Double displacement reactions involve the exchange of ions between two compounds (AB + CD → AD + CB).

Redox and Electron Transfer

  • Oxidation is defined as the loss of electrons, while reduction is the gain of electrons.
  • Disproportionation reactions occur when the same element in a reactant is simultaneously oxidized and reduced.
  • The oxidation number change method is used to balance complex redox equations in acidic or basic media.
  • Reducing agents are substances that provide electrons and are themselves oxidized.

Reaction Energetics and Kinetics

  • Exothermic reactions release heat (ΔH < 0), whereas endothermic reactions absorb heat (ΔH > 0).
  • Catalysts increase reaction rates by providing an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy (Ea).
  • Elementary reactions occur in a single step, while complex reactions proceed through a series of elementary steps.
  • The rate-determining step is the slowest elementary step in a reaction mechanism.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Arrhenius Equation
k = Ae-Ea/RT
Conditions: Temperature dependence of reaction rate
Units: Ea in J/mol, R = 8.314 J/K·mol
First Order Integrated Rate Law
ln[A]t = -kt + ln[A]0
Conditions: For reactions where rate is proportional to concentration of one reactant
Units: k in s-1
Nernst Equation (Redox)
Ecell = E°cell - (RT/nF)lnQ
Conditions: Non-standard state electrochemical cells
Units: E in Volts, F = 96485 C/mol
Gibbs Free Energy Change
ΔG° = -nFE°cell
Conditions: Spontaneity of redox reactions
Units: ΔG° in Joules
Rate Law Expression
Rate = k[A]x[B]y
Conditions: Applicable to elementary and complex reactions
Units: k units depend on overall order (n = x+y)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher oxidation states are more stable in p-block elements

Observation: Inert pair effect in Tl, Pb, Bi — Poor shielding of s-electrons by d and f orbitals makes them less available for bonding.

Exception Half-filled orbitals are stable

Observation: Cr (3d5 4s1) vs Cr2+ — Exchange energy stabilization in d-subshells.

Exception Electronegativity increases across a period

Observation: Noble gases — Stable octet configuration prevents electron attraction.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Redox reactions involve simultaneous oxidation and reduction.
  • Catalysts do not change the equilibrium constant (Keq).
  • Molecularity is always a whole number, whereas order can be fractional.
  • The sum of stoichiometric coefficients in a balanced equation must be integers.
  • Activation energy is independent of temperature.
  • Standard electrode potential (E°) is an intensive property.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing molecularity with reaction order; molecularity applies only to elementary steps.
  • Ignoring the sign of ΔH; exothermic reactions have negative values.
  • Forgetting to balance oxygen atoms using H2O in acidic redox reactions.
  • Assuming all reactions are elementary; most are complex mechanisms.

⚡ Quick Revision: Classification of Chemical Reactions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Chemical Kinetics

Study of reaction rates and mechanisms. Governed by the Arrhenius equation and rate laws.

Redox Processes

Electron transfer reactions involving changes in oxidation states. Balanced using half-reaction methods.

Thermodynamics of Reactions

Energy changes associated with chemical transformations. Defined by ΔH, ΔS, and ΔG.

Reaction Mechanisms

The sequence of elementary steps. The slowest step determines the overall rate.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Rate = k[A]n
  • ΔG = ΔH - TΔS
  • E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode
  • ln(k2/k1) = (Ea/R)(1/T1 - 1/T2)
  • n-factor for redox = change in oxidation number

📌 Quick Recall

  • Reactions are classified by mechanism and stoichiometry.
  • Redox involves electron transfer.
  • Kinetics describes the speed of reaction.
  • Thermodynamics describes the feasibility.
