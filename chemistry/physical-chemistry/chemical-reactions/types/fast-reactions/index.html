Physical Chemistry

Fast Reactions

Fast reactions are chemical processes characterized by extremely high rate constants, typically occurring on timescales of 10-3 to 10-15 seconds. These reactions are generally diffusion-controlled or involve minimal activation energy barriers.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Experimental Techniques for Fast Kinetics

  • Stopped-flow spectrophotometry measures reactions with half-lives down to 1 ms by rapid mixing of reactants.
  • Flash photolysis utilizes high-intensity light pulses to generate reactive intermediates for sub-microsecond analysis.
  • Temperature-jump (T-jump) relaxation perturbs chemical equilibria by rapid heating to observe the return to equilibrium.
  • NMR line broadening allows determination of exchange rates in the 10-1 to 10-5 s range.

Diffusion-Controlled Reaction Dynamics

  • Diffusion-controlled reactions occur at every collision because the activation energy is negligible.
  • The rate constant is determined by the viscosity of the solvent and the diffusion coefficients of the reactants.
  • The Debye equation modifies the Smoluchowski limit to account for electrostatic attraction or repulsion between ions.
  • Reaction rates in this regime are independent of the chemical nature of the reactants, depending only on transport properties.

Relaxation Kinetics Principles

  • Relaxation methods study systems at equilibrium by applying a small external perturbation.
  • The system returns to a new equilibrium state following first-order kinetics regardless of the reaction order.
  • The relaxation time (τ) is the time taken for the concentration deviation to decrease to 1/e of its initial value.
  • Multiple relaxation times are observed if the reaction mechanism involves multiple intermediate steps.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher ionic strength increases reaction rate for ions of same charge

Observation: Reactions between neutral molecules — Primary salt effect is negligible for neutral species as activity coefficients remain near unity.

Exception Diffusion-controlled rates increase with temperature

Observation: Viscosity-dependent rate decrease — If solvent viscosity increases significantly with temperature, the diffusion coefficient may decrease, slowing the reaction.

Exception Reaction rate is proportional to concentration

Observation: Diffusion-limited regime — At the diffusion limit, the rate is limited by the frequency of encounters, not the probability of reaction upon collision.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Relaxation Time Shortcut
1/τ = kf + kb

Application: Use for any reversible A ↔ B system to find the rate constant sum instantly.

Ionic Strength Rule
log(k/k0) ∝ zAzB

Application: Predict rate increase if zAzB > 0, decrease if zAzB < 0.

Diffusion Limit Check
k ≈ 1010 M-1s-1

Application: If calculated k exceeds 1010, the reaction is likely diffusion-controlled.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Fast reactions have half-lives typically less than 1 second.
  • Stopped-flow technique is limited by the dead time of the mixing apparatus.
  • Relaxation methods are ideal for studying fast reversible equilibria.
  • The primary salt effect is a hallmark of ionic reactions in solution.
  • Diffusion-controlled reactions are limited by the solvent viscosity.
  • Flash photolysis is used to study free radical kinetics.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing relaxation time with half-life; remember τ = t1/2 / ln(2).
  • Assuming all fast reactions are diffusion-controlled; some are simply low-barrier reactions.
  • Ignoring the solvent viscosity in diffusion-controlled rate calculations.
  • Applying the Debye-Hückel limiting law at high ionic strengths where it fails.

⚡ Quick Revision: Fast Reactions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Relaxation Kinetics

Small perturbations to equilibrium systems result in exponential return to equilibrium defined by the relaxation time τ.

Diffusion Control

When the reaction rate exceeds the rate of molecular encounters, the reaction is limited by diffusion coefficients.

Primary Salt Effect

The rate constant of ionic reactions depends on the ionic strength of the medium due to changes in activity coefficients.

Experimental Constraints

Techniques like stopped-flow and flash photolysis are chosen based on the required temporal resolution.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Relaxation Time (τ)
Time for a system to return to equilibrium after perturbation.
Dead Time (td)
Minimum time required to mix reactants in stopped-flow.
Ionic Strength (I)
Measure of total ion concentration in solution.
Diffusion Coefficient (D)
Rate of particle transport due to concentration gradients.
Flash Photolysis (hν)
Use of light pulses to initiate fast reactions.
Transition State (TS)
High-energy configuration during a reaction.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Fast reactions require specialized physical techniques.
  • Relaxation methods measure equilibrium shifts.
  • Diffusion limits the maximum possible rate.
  • Ionic strength influences ionic reaction rates.

⚖️ Key Differences

Relaxation Time VS Half-life

Relaxation time is the time to reach 1/e of the initial value; half-life is time to reach 1/2.
