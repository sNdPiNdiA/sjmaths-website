Physical Chemistry

Exothermic Reactions

Exothermic reactions are chemical processes that release energy in the form of heat or light to the surroundings. The enthalpy change (ΔH) for these reactions is always negative, indicating that the products have lower potential energy than the reactants.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Sign Conventions

  • Exothermic processes are characterized by a negative enthalpy change (ΔH < 0).
  • Heat (q) is evolved to the surroundings, resulting in a negative sign for heat transfer in the system.
  • The internal energy of the system decreases as energy is dissipated as thermal radiation or kinetic energy.

Reaction Coordinate and Activation Energy

  • The potential energy diagram shows the reactant energy level higher than the product energy level.
  • Activation energy (Ea) is the barrier that must be overcome for the reaction to proceed.
  • The net energy released is the difference between the bond dissociation energy of reactants and the energy released during bond formation of products.

Equilibrium and Temperature Effects

  • According to Le Chatelier's Principle, increasing temperature shifts the equilibrium of an exothermic reaction toward the reactants.
  • The equilibrium constant (K) decreases as the temperature increases for exothermic reactions.
  • Exothermic reactions are generally spontaneous at low temperatures if the entropy change (ΔS) is negative.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Standard Enthalpy of Reaction
ΔH°rxn = ∑nΔH°f(products) - ∑mΔH°f(reactants)
Conditions: Standard state (1 bar, 298 K)
Units: kJ/mol
First Law of Thermodynamics
ΔU = q + w
Conditions: Closed system
Units: Joules (J)
Temperature Dependence (Van't Hoff)
ln(K2/K1) = (ΔH°/R) * (1/T1 - 1/T2)
Conditions: Ideal gas behavior
Units: R = 8.314 J/mol·K
Gibbs Free Energy
ΔG = ΔH - TΔS
Conditions: Constant T and P
Units: kJ/mol
Enthalpy Change
ΔH = Hproducts - Hreactants
Conditions: Constant pressure
Units: kJ/mol
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Exothermic reactions are always spontaneous

Observation: Non-spontaneous exothermic reactions — Occurs when ΔS is highly negative and TΔS > ΔH, making ΔG positive.

Exception Bond formation is always exothermic

Observation: Formation of certain unstable intermediates — High steric strain or electronic repulsion can offset the energy released during bond formation.

Exception Combustion is always exothermic

Observation: Endothermic combustion of nitrogen — Requires high energy input to break the N≡N triple bond, often requiring plasma or high-temperature arcs.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
The Sign Mnemonic
EXO = EXIT

Application: Heat EXITS the system, therefore ΔH is negative.

Equilibrium Shift Rule
EXO = COLD

Application: Exothermic reactions prefer cold conditions to shift forward; heat acts as a product.

Energy Level Check
R > P

Application: If Reactants are higher than Products on the Y-axis, the reaction is exothermic.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Exothermic reactions release energy to the surroundings.
  • The enthalpy of products is less than the enthalpy of reactants.
  • Le Chatelier's principle predicts a shift to reactants upon heating.
  • Combustion reactions are classic examples of exothermic processes.
  • The equilibrium constant K decreases as temperature increases.
  • Bond formation is an exothermic process.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing ΔH sign with ΔG; a reaction can be exothermic but non-spontaneous.
  • Assuming all exothermic reactions occur rapidly; they may have high activation energy.
  • Ignoring the effect of temperature on the equilibrium constant K.
  • Misinterpreting the heat term in thermochemical equations.

⚡ Quick Revision: Exothermic Reactions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Enthalpy Definition

Enthalpy is the heat content of a system at constant pressure. For exothermic reactions, ΔH is negative.

Thermodynamic Stability

Products are more stable than reactants in exothermic reactions due to lower potential energy.

Temperature Sensitivity

The equilibrium constant K is inversely proportional to temperature for exothermic reactions.

Spontaneity

Spontaneity is determined by ΔG = ΔH - TΔS; exothermic reactions are favored by low T.

Exothermic VS Exergonic

Exothermic refers to heat (ΔH), Exergonic refers to free energy (ΔG).
