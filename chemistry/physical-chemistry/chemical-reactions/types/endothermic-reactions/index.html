Physical Chemistry

Endothermic Reactions

Endothermic reactions are chemical processes that absorb thermal energy from the surroundings, resulting in a positive change in enthalpy (ΔH > 0). These reactions typically involve the breaking of chemical bonds, which requires an input of energy exceeding the energy released during bond formation.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Sign Conventions

  • For endothermic processes, the enthalpy change (ΔH) is always positive.
  • The system gains heat from the surroundings, making the heat capacity (q) positive.
  • The potential energy of the products is higher than that of the reactants.
  • The activation energy (Ea) for the forward reaction is greater than the reverse reaction.

Equilibrium and Temperature Effects

  • According to Le Chatelier's Principle, increasing the temperature shifts the equilibrium toward the products.
  • The equilibrium constant (K) increases as the temperature rises for endothermic reactions.
  • Endothermic reactions are non-spontaneous at low temperatures if ΔS is negative.
  • High temperatures favor the formation of products in endothermic systems.

Molecular Energy Profiles

  • The reaction coordinate diagram shows a net energy increase from reactants to products.
  • Bond breaking is the rate-determining step in many endothermic processes.
  • The entropy change (ΔS) can be positive or negative depending on the molecular disorder.
  • Spontaneity is achieved when TΔS > ΔH at high temperatures.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Bond Enthalpy Relation
ΔH° = ΣBE(reactants) - ΣBE(products)
Conditions: Gas phase reactions
Units: kJ/mol
Temperature Dependence (Van't Hoff)
ln(K2/K1) = (ΔH°/R) * (1/T1 - 1/T2)
Conditions: Constant ΔH° over temperature range
Units: R = 8.314 J/mol·K
Gibbs-Helmholtz Equation
ΔG = ΔH - TΔS
Conditions: Constant pressure and temperature
Units: Joules (J)
Enthalpy of Reaction
ΔH°rxn = ΣnΔH°f(products) - ΣmΔH°f(reactants)
Conditions: Standard state conditions (1 bar, 298 K)
Units: kJ/mol
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Endothermic reactions are non-spontaneous

Observation: Spontaneous endothermic reactions — Occur when ΔS is sufficiently positive such that TΔS > ΔH, making ΔG negative.

Exception Bond breaking requires energy

Observation: Endothermic dissolution of salts — Lattice energy required to break the crystal lattice exceeds the hydration energy released.

Exception Temperature increase speeds up all reactions

Observation: Temperature-independent rate — Some complex mechanisms involve pre-equilibrium steps where the net rate is insensitive to temperature.

🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing ΔH sign with ΔG sign; ΔH can be positive while ΔG is negative.
  • Assuming all endothermic reactions are non-spontaneous at all temperatures.
  • Ignoring the role of entropy in determining spontaneity.
  • Misinterpreting the effect of temperature on the equilibrium constant K.

⚡ Quick Revision: Endothermic Reactions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Enthalpy Definition

Enthalpy is the total heat content of a system. For endothermic reactions, the heat absorbed is defined by ΔH = Hproducts - Hreactants > 0.

Spontaneity Conditions

A reaction is spontaneous if ΔG = ΔH - TΔS < 0. Endothermic reactions require high T to ensure TΔS > ΔH.

Equilibrium Constant

The Van't Hoff equation relates the change in K to temperature. For endothermic reactions, d(ln K)/dT > 0.

Bond Energy

The net energy change is the difference between energy required to break bonds and energy released during bond formation.

If ΔH is 50 kJ/mol and ΔS is 100 J/mol·K, at what temperature does it become spontaneous?

Solution: Set ΔG = 0: 50000 - T(100) = 0, so T = 500 K. Spontaneous when T > 500 K.

Question 4 of 10

What is the relationship between ΔH and bond energies?

Solution: Enthalpy is the energy to break bonds minus energy released in forming them.

Question 7 of 10
Why does K increase with T for endothermic reactions?

Solution: Le Chatelier's principle states that adding heat (increasing T) shifts the equilibrium to consume the heat.

Question 8 of 10
Which state function is enthalpy?

Solution: Enthalpy is a state function, depending only on the initial and final states.

Question 10 of 10
What happens to the internal energy of a system in an endothermic reaction?

Solution: Since heat is absorbed, internal energy increases assuming no work is done.
