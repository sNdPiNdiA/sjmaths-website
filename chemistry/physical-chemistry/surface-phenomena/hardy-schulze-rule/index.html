Hardy–Schulze Rule

The Hardy–Schulze rule states that the precipitating power of an electrolyte increases rapidly with an increase in the valency of the ion whose charge is opposite to that of the colloidal particles. Flocculation value is inversely proportional to the coagulating power of the active ion.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mechanism of Coagulation and Electrical Double Layer

  • Colloidal particles adsorb specific ions preferentially from the dispersion medium, establishing a fixed Stern layer and a diffuse Gouy-Chapman layer.
  • Addition of an electrolyte introduces counter-ions that compress the diffuse double layer and reduce the zeta potential (ζ).
  • When the zeta potential drops below the critical threshold, repulsive forces fail to overcome van der Waals attractions, leading to flocculation.

Valency Dependence and Ion Efficiency

  • Coagulating power is determined exclusively by the ion carrying a charge opposite to that of the dispersed colloidal sol.
  • For positively charged sols (e.g., Fe(OH)3), anions follow the coagulating power order: [Fe(CN)6]4- > SO42- > Cl-.
  • For negatively charged sols (e.g., As2S3), cations follow the coagulating power order: Al3+ > Ba2+ > Na+.

Flocculation Value and Quantitative Metrics

  • Flocculation value is defined as the minimum concentration of an electrolyte in millimoles per litre required to cause complete precipitation of a sol in 2 hours.
  • Lower flocculation value corresponds to higher precipitating efficiency of the active ion.
  • The effectiveness ratio of mono-, di-, and trivalent ions frequently approximates 1 : 1/32 : 1/729 based on the inverse sixth-power law derivation.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher valency ions always show standard predicted low flocculation values.

Observation: Organic ions and large polyatomic complexes can deviate due to specific adsorption and hydration energies. — Strong hydration shells or specific chemical adsorption can alter the effective charge density and distance of closest approach.

Exception Coagulation power depends strictly on electrical valency.

Observation: Transition metal cations with variable coordination tendencies cause anomalous precipitation at unexpectedly low concentrations. — Formation of coordination complexes or surface chemical bonds overrides pure electrostatic double-layer compression.

Exception Sols remain stable indefinitely in the absence of electrolytes.

Observation: Mutually interacting oppositely charged sols cause mutual coagulation upon mixing. — Neutralization of zeta potential occurs directly between the positive and negative surface charges of the two distinct colloidal species.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Opposite Charge First Mnemonic
O-C-P (Opposite Charge Precipitates)

Application: Always identify the charge of the sol first (e.g., metal sulfides are negative, metal hydroxides are positive) and then select the ion with the highest opposite valency.

Valency Power Rule Shortcut
Power ∝ Z3 or Z6

Application: When comparing precipitation concentration ratios between Na+, Ca2+, and Al3+, use the inverse cube or sixth power to quickly estimate relative efficiency.

Flocculation Value Inversion
High Power = Low Value

Application: Never confuse coagulating power with flocculation value; if a question asks for the minimum concentration needed, pick the electrolyte with the highest ionic charge.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The Hardy–Schulze rule applies to lyophobic colloids undergoing electrolyte-induced coagulation.
  • Coagulating power is directly proportional to the valency of the active ion raised to a power between 2 and 6.
  • For As2S3 sol (negative), the coagulating power order is Al3+ > Ba2+ > Na+.
  • For Fe(OH)3 sol (positive), the coagulating power order is [Fe(CN)6]4- > PO43- > SO42- > Cl-.
  • Flocculation value is measured in millimoles per litre (mmol L-1).
  • Mutual coagulation occurs when two oppositely charged sols are mixed in equimolar or equivalent proportions.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the charge of the sol with the active coagulating ion; remember that the ion with the opposite charge causes precipitation.
  • Assuming flocculation value is directly proportional to coagulating power, whereas it is inversely proportional.
  • Neglecting the sign of the sol when evaluating multiple-choice options involving complex anions like [Fe(CN)6]4-.
  • Treating lyophilic colloids as obeying the Hardy–Schulze rule strictly without considering solvation layers.

⚡ Quick Revision: Hardy–Schulze Rule

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Colloidal Stability

Lyophobic sols derive stability from preferential ion adsorption and electrical double layer repulsion preventing particle coalescence.

Hardy–Schulze Principle

Precipitating power increases exponentially with the valency of the counter-ion according to empirical power laws.

Flocculation Value Metric

Quantitative measure defined as millimoles of electrolyte per litre required to induce complete precipitation.

Mutual Coagulation

Neutralization and precipitation resulting from the direct combination of oppositely charged colloidal dispersions.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Hardy–Schulze Rule (Valency Coagulation Law)
Principle stating coagulating power increases with ion valency of opposite charge.
Flocculation Value (Minimum Coagulation Concentration)
Millimoles of electrolyte per litre needed to precipitate a sol. Unit: mmol L-1.
Zeta Potential (ζ-potential)
Electrokinetic potential at the shear plane of the double layer. Unit: mV.
Lyophobic Sol (Solvent-repelling Colloid)
Colloid system requiring stabilizing electrolytes or charges to prevent aggregation.
Mutual Coagulation (Opposite Sol Neutralization)
Precipitation occurring upon mixing two sols with opposite electrical charges.
Counter-Ion (Oppositely Charged Ion)
Ion in electrolyte solution possessing a charge sign opposite to the colloidal core.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Coagulating power ∝ Valencyn (where n = 2 to 6)
  • Flocculation value ∝ 1 / Coagulating power
  • As2S3 is a negatively charged sol; Fe(OH)3 is a positively charged sol
  • Al3+ is more effective than Ba2+, which is more effective than Na+ for negative sols
  • Phosphates and ferrocyanides are exceptionally powerful coagulants for positive sols

📌 Quick Recall

  • Valency of counter-ion is the primary governing factor in sol coagulation.
  • Inverse relationship exists between flocculation value and precipitating power.
  • Stern and Gouy-Chapman double layer compression explains the reduction of zeta potential.
  • Lyophilic systems possess solvation stability in addition to charge stability.

⚖️ Key Differences

Coagulating Power VS Flocculation Value

Power is higher for higher valency ions, whereas flocculation value is lower for higher valency ions.

Question 8 of 18

The Hardy–Schulze rule primarily applies to which type of colloidal systems?

Explanation: Lyophobic colloids are thermodynamically unstable and depend on electrical charge for stability, making them subject to the Hardy–Schulze rule.

Question 9 of 18

What happens to the zeta potential of a colloidal particle upon addition of an effective coagulating electrolyte?

Explanation: Electrolyte addition compresses the diffuse electrical double layer, reducing the zeta potential below the critical coagulation threshold.

Which of the following anions has the lowest flocculation value for Fe(OH)3 sol?

Explanation: PO43- carries a 3- charge, giving it the highest coagulating power and therefore the lowest flocculation value for the positive Fe(OH)3 sol.

Question 11 of 18

According to empirical observations, coagulating power is proportional to Zn where n is typically:

Explanation: Empirical studies on the Hardy–Schulze rule show that the power varies with the valency raised to a power n ranging between 2 and 6.

Question 12 of 18

Question 13 of 18

If the flocculation value of NaCl for a negative sol is 50 mmol L-1, which electrolyte would likely have a much lower flocculation value?

Explanation: MgCl2 provides Mg2+ ions (divalent), which have higher coagulating power and thus a lower flocculation value than monovalent sodium salts.

Question 14 of 18

The stabilization of lyophobic colloids is primarily attributed to:

Explanation: Lyophobic sols are stabilized by the presence of identical electrical charges on all dispersed particles, causing mutual electrostatic repulsion.

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following compounds produces a negative sol in aqueous medium?

Explanation: Arsenic sulfide (As2S3) adsorbs sulfide ions preferentially, resulting in a negatively charged colloidal particle surface.

Question 16 of 18

In the context of the diffuse double layer, the Stern layer represents:

Explanation: The Stern layer is the inner layer containing tightly bound counter-ions adsorbed directly onto the charged particle surface.

Question 17 of 18

Why are multivalent ions vastly more effective at coagulation than monovalent ions?

Explanation: Multivalent ions provide higher charge density per particle, compressing the double layer more effectively and lowering the zeta potential.

Question 18 of 18

Which factor does NOT directly alter the flocculation value of a given electrolyte for a specific sol?

Explanation: The container material shape has no bearing on the surface chemistry and electrostatic interactions governing coagulation.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following colloidal solutions is negatively charged?
