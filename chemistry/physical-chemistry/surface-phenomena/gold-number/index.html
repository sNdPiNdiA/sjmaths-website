Physical Chemistry

Gold Number

Gold number is defined as the minimum mass of a protective colloid in milligrams required to prevent the coagulation of 10 mL of a standard gold sol when 1 mL of a 10% sodium chloride solution is added. It serves as a quantitative measure of the protective efficiency of lyophilic colloids against lyophobic sols.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Definition and Experimental Measurement

  • Gold number specifically measures the protective power of lyophilic colloids added to a standard red gold sol.
  • The coagulation threshold is reached by adding exactly 1 mL of 10% NaCl solution to 10 mL of gold sol.
  • Starch, gelatin, gum arabic, and albumin exhibit vastly different gold numbers ranging from 0.005 to 25.

Relationship Between Gold Number and Protective Power

  • A lower gold number corresponds to a higher protective power because less mass is required to prevent coagulation.
  • Gelatin has an extremely low gold number (0.005 - 0.01), making it an exceptionally effective protective colloid.
  • Potato starch has a high gold number (approx. 20 - 25), indicating poor protective efficiency relative to proteins.

Colloidal Stability and Mechanism

  • Protective colloids form a hydration layer around the lyophobic sol particles, preventing direct particle-particle contact.
  • Lyophilic colloids are strongly solvated in dispersion media, imparting stability against electrolyte flocculation.
  • The addition of electrolytes neutralizes the electrical double layer, but the protective coating physically obstructs aggregation.

Schulze-Hardy Rule (Coagulation Value)
C ∝ 1z^6
Conditions: Coagulating power increases rapidly with the valency (z) of the active flocculating ion.
Units: C is coagulation concentration in mmol L-1.
Protective Efficiency Relation
Protective Power ∝ 1Gold Number
Conditions: Lower gold number indicates higher protective action.
Units: Dimensionless inverse measure.
Gold Number Definition Formula
Gold Number = W × 1000V
Conditions: W is the mass of protective colloid in grams, V is the volume of the gold sol in mL (typically 10 mL).
Units: Gold number is expressed in milligrams (mg).
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher molecular weight polymers always have lower gold numbers.

Observation: Certain synthetic polymers with high molecular weights show erratic protective behavior due to coiling conformations. — Steric hindrance and chain folding can bury hydrophilic functional groups, reducing effective surface coverage.

Exception Proteins are uniformly excellent protective agents.

Observation: Denatured proteins or proteins at their isoelectric point show severely diminished protective action. — At the isoelectric point, net charge and solubility decrease, causing self-aggregation rather than sol stabilization.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Inverse Power Mnemonic
Power ←→ 1 / Gold Number

Application: Remember 'LESS gold number means MORE power'. Think of it as a grading scale where '1' is top tier.

Standard Volumes Rule
10 mL Sol + 1 mL 10% NaCl

Application: Always look for 10 mL and 1 mL in numerical problems involving the definition of gold number.

Units Conversion Shortcut
mg = g × 1000

Application: If mass is given in milligrams directly, do not multiply by 1000 in the numerator when computing standard values.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Gold number is defined in terms of milligrams of protective colloid per 10 mL of gold sol.
  • The standard electrolyte used in the gold number determination is 1 mL of 10% sodium chloride solution.
  • Gelatin possesses the lowest gold number among common hydrophilic colloids, signifying maximum protective action.
  • Protective power is inversely proportional to the gold number of the substance.
  • Lyophilic colloids protect lyophobic colloids by forming a physical sheath and through strong solvation.
  • Schulze-Hardy rule governs the coagulation values of electrolytes based on the valency of the counter-ion.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing gold number units: it is expressed in milligrams (mg), not grams.
  • Assuming a high gold number means high protective efficiency; remember the relationship is strictly inverse.
  • Forgetting the exact volumes used in the test definition (10 mL sol and 1 mL 10% NaCl).
  • Confusing the coagulation value (mmol/L) with the gold number (mg).

⚡ Quick Revision: Gold Number

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition of Gold Number

Gold number quantifies the milligrams of a protective colloid needed to stop 10 mL of gold sol from coagulating upon adding 1 mL of 10% NaCl.

Inverse Relationship

Protective power is inversely related to the gold number; smaller numerical values indicate superior protective stabilization.

Mechanism of Protection

Lyophilic colloids coat lyophobic particles, creating a protective envelope that resists electrolyte-induced flocculation.

Experimental Parameters

Standardized conditions dictate exact volumes of gold sol and concentration of sodium chloride to ensure reproducibility.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Gold Number (GN)
Minimum mass in mg of protective colloid protecting 10 mL gold sol from 1 mL 10% NaCl.
Lyophilic Colloid (Solvent-attracting)
Colloid having high affinity for the dispersion medium.
Lyophobic Colloid (Solvent-repelling)
Colloid having low affinity for the dispersion medium, easily coagulated.
Protective Action (Stabilization)
Ability of lyophilic sol to prevent precipitation of lyophobic sol.
Coagulation Value (Flocculation concentration)
Minimum concentration of electrolyte in mmol/L required to cause coagulation.
Schulze-Hardy Rule (Valency rule)
Flocculating power is proportional to the sixth power of the valency of the active ion.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Gold number ∝ 1 / Protective Power
  • Gelatin has the lowest gold number (0.005 - 0.01)
  • Starch has a high gold number (20 - 25)
  • Standard test uses 10 mL gold sol and 1 mL 10% NaCl
  • Units of gold number are milligrams (mg)

📌 Quick Recall

  • Gold number is a standardized metric for lyophilic colloid efficiency.
  • Lower gold number implies stronger protection against electrolyte coagulation.
  • Proteins like gelatin and hemoglobin are highly efficient protectors.
  • Starch and dextrin exhibit relatively weak protective capacities.

⚖️ Key Differences

Gold Number VS Coagulation Value

Gold number measures protective colloid efficiency (lower is better); coagulation value measures electrolyte concentration needed to cause precipitation (lower means stronger electrolyte power).
