Langmuir adsorption isotherm

The Langmuir adsorption isotherm quantitatively relates the fractional surface coverage to the pressure or concentration of an adsorbate at constant temperature. It is derived by assuming dynamic equilibrium between adsorption and desorption rates on a localized, energetically uniform surface containing a fixed number of identical sites.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Derivation and Kinetic Postulates

  • Adsorption is treated as a dynamic equilibrium between free gas molecules striking vacant sites and adsorbed molecules desorbing from occupied sites.
  • The rate of adsorption is proportional to the gas pressure P and the fraction of vacant sites (1 - θ), expressed as Ra = kaP(1 - θ).
  • The rate of desorption is proportional to the fraction of occupied sites θ, expressed as Rd = kdθ.
  • At adsorption equilibrium, Ra = Rd, yielding the standard Langmuir relation θ = (kaP/kd) / (1 + kaP/kd).

Limiting Behavior and Pressure Regimes

  • At low pressure regimes (KP << 1), the term KP in the denominator is neglected, making coverage directly proportional to pressure (θ = KP).
  • At high pressure regimes (KP >> 1), the surface approaches saturation where θ ≈ 1, making the adsorption independent of pressure (zero-order kinetics).
  • The volume of gas adsorbed v is directly proportional to θ, such that v = vmaxKP / (1 + KP), where vmax is the volume for complete monolayer coverage.
  • Plotting P/v versus P yields a straight line with a slope of 1/vmax and an intercept of 1/(vmaxK), allowing graphical determination of surface area.

Underlying Assumptions and Limitations

  • The model assumes adsorption is strictly localized and restricted to a monomolecular layer, preventing multi-layer condensation.
  • All adsorption sites are assumed to be energetically equivalent and uniform, with no surface heterogeneity.
  • There are assumed to be no lateral interactions between adjacent adsorbed molecules, meaning the heat of adsorption remains constant regardless of coverage.
  • The model fails at high pressures and low temperatures where multilayer adsorption occurs, as described by the BET isotherm instead.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Langmuir isotherm assumes constant heat of adsorption with coverage

Observation: Heat of adsorption often drops significantly as surface coverage increases — Surface heterogeneity and lateral repulsive interactions between adjacent polar or charged adsorbates destabilize the adsorbed state at high coverages.

Exception Langmuir isotherm strictly restricts adsorption to a single monolayer

Observation: Physical adsorption systems routinely transition into multilayer formation near saturation vapor pressure — Weak van der Waals forces allow condensation of subsequent molecular layers over already adsorbed molecules, violating the single-site monolayer restriction.

Exception Adsorption rate is directly proportional to vacant sites (1 - θ)

Observation: Dissociative adsorption of diatomic gases like H2 or O2 scales with (1 - θ)2 — Molecules dissociate into two distinct atoms upon adsorption, requiring two adjacent vacant surface sites for successful attachment.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Intercept-Slope Method for Langmuir Constants
Slope = 1 / vmax, Intercept = 1 / (vmax K)

Application: In linear plots of P/v vs P, divide the intercept by the slope to directly find the adsorption coefficient K.

Dissociative Adsorption Modification
θ = K1/2 P1/21 + K1/2 P1/2

Application: Whenever a diatomic gas dissociates upon adsorption, replace P with P1/2 and K with K1/2 in the standard Langmuir equation.

Fractional Coverage from Volume Ratios
θ = vvmax

Application: Substitute volume of gas adsorbed per gram at a given pressure divided by the calculated monolayer capacity volume to get θ instantly.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The Langmuir adsorption isotherm is strictly applicable to monolayer formation.
  • At very low pressures, Langmuir adsorption follows first-order kinetics with respect to pressure.
  • At very high pressures, Langmuir adsorption follows zero-order kinetics with respect to pressure.
  • The constant K in the Langmuir expression represents the ratio of the adsorption rate constant to the desorption rate constant.
  • A plot of P/v against P yields a straight line with a positive slope for Type I isotherms.
  • Dissociative adsorption of gases introduces a square-root dependence on pressure in the Langmuir isotherm.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming Langmuir isotherm accounts for multilayer formation; remember it applies exclusively to monolayer adsorption.
  • Confusing the units of the equilibrium constant K in pressure form (Pa-1 or atm-1) with concentration form (dm3 mol-1).
  • Failing to square root the pressure term when dealing with dissociative gas adsorption like hydrogen or nitrogen on metal surfaces.
  • Interpreting the Freundlich empirical constant as having the exact theoretical foundation found in Langmuir's kinetic derivation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Langmuir adsorption isotherm

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Kinetic Derivation

Derived by equating the rate of adsorption to the rate of desorption under dynamic equilibrium conditions on uniform sites.

Limiting Laws

Reduces to Henry's law (θ = KP) at low pressures and approaches saturation (θ = 1) at high pressures.

Linear Transformation

Expressed linearly as P/v = 1/(vmaxK) + P/vmax to extract monolayer capacity and binding affinity constants.

Surface Limitations

Fails on heterogeneous surfaces and at high pressures where lateral interactions and multilayers become dominant.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Fractional Coverage (θ)
Ratio of occupied adsorption sites to total available sites (dimensionless).
Adsorption Coefficient (K)
Ratio of adsorption rate constant to desorption rate constant (Pa-1).
Monolayer Capacity (vmax)
Volume of gas required to completely cover the surface with a single molecular layer (cm3).
Adsorption Rate Constant (k_a)
Rate constant governing attachment of gas molecules to vacant sites (Pa-1 s-1).
Desorption Rate Constant (k_d)
Rate constant governing departure of molecules from surface sites (s-1).
Heat of Adsorption (ΔHads)
Enthalpy change associated with the adsorption process (kJ mol-1).

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Langmuir equation: θ = KP / (1 + KP)
  • Low pressure limit: θ = KP
  • High pressure limit: θ = 1
  • Linear form: P/v = 1/(vmaxK) + P/vmax
  • Dissociative form uses P1/2

📌 Quick Recall

  • Assumes localized, non-interacting sites forming a single monolayer.
  • Yields linear plots for P/v vs P in physical gas adsorption analysis.
  • Accounts for dynamic equilibrium between surface attachment and detachment.
  • Serves as the foundational theoretical model for heterogeneous catalysis.

⚖️ Key Differences

Langmuir Isotherm VS Freundlich Isotherm

Langmuir assumes energetic uniformity and monolayer saturation, whereas Freundlich assumes surface heterogeneity and lacks a saturation limit.

Question 1 of 19

What is the value of fractional coverage (θ) at extremely high pressures according to the Langmuir isotherm?

Explanation: At very high pressures, KP >> 1, making θ = KP / (1 + KP) ≈ KP / KP = 1.

Question 2 of 19

In the linear form of the Langmuir adsorption isotherm (P/v vs P), what does the slope of the line represent?

Explanation: The linear equation is P/v = (1 / vmaxK) + (1 / vmax)P, meaning the slope of P/v versus P is 1 / vmax.

Question 3 of 19

Which assumption is fundamentally required in the derivation of the Langmuir adsorption isotherm?

Explanation: Langmuir derivation strictly assumes that all adsorption sites are energetically identical and independent.

Question 4 of 19

At very low pressures, the Langmuir adsorption isotherm predicts that the amount of gas adsorbed is:

Explanation: At low pressures, KP << 1, reducing the equation to θ = KP, showing a linear direct proportionality.

Question 5 of 19

What is the correct modification of the Langmuir isotherm for a diatomic gas that undergoes dissociation upon adsorption?

Explanation: Dissociation into two atoms requires two sites, making the adsorption rate dependent on P1/2 and the site term proportional to (1 - θ).

Question 6 of 19

The adsorption coefficient K in the Langmuir isotherm is equal to:

Explanation: At equilibrium, k_a P (1 - θ) = k_d θ, which rearranges to θ / ((1 - θ)P) = k_a / k_d = K.

Question 7 of 19

What is the order of adsorption reaction at very high pressures according to Langmuir theory?

Explanation: At high pressures, the surface is fully saturated (θ ≈ 1), so the adsorption rate becomes independent of pressure, indicating zero-order kinetics.

Question 8 of 19

Which plot gives a straight line for the Langmuir adsorption isotherm?

Explanation: Rearranging v = vmaxKP / (1 + KP) into P/v = 1/(vmaxK) + P/vmax shows that plotting P/v versus P produces a linear graph.

Question 9 of 19

What are the units of the adsorption equilibrium constant K when pressure is expressed in Pascals?

Explanation: Since KP is dimensionless in the denominator (1 + KP), K must have units inverse to pressure, i.e., Pa-1 or atm-1.

Question 10 of 19

Under what conditions does the Langmuir isotherm fail significantly?

Explanation: At high pressures and low temperatures, molecules tend to form multilayers and experience lateral interactions, violating Langmuir postulates.

Question 11 of 19

In the Langmuir equation for adsorption from solution, concentration C replaces which variable from the gas-phase equation?

Explanation: For liquid-phase adsorption, partial pressure P is replaced by equilibrium solution concentration C.

Question 12 of 19

Which of the following is NOT an assumption of the Langmuir model?

Explanation: Langmuir explicitly restricts adsorption strictly to a single monolayer, rejecting multilayer formation.

Question 13 of 19

What does the intercept of the P/v versus P Langmuir plot equal?

Explanation: From P/v = (1 / vmax)P + 1 / (vmaxK), the y-intercept is equal to 1 / (vmaxK).

Question 14 of 19

If the adsorption of a gas is physical and exothermic, what happens to the value of K as temperature increases?

Explanation: Exothermic physical adsorption processes have negative enthalpy of adsorption, so increasing temperature shifts equilibrium backward, decreasing K.

Question 15 of 19

What is the physical meaning of vmax in the Langmuir adsorption isotherm?

Explanation: vmax represents the maximum adsorption capacity corresponding to complete monolayer coverage of the solid surface.

Question 16 of 19

Why does the Langmuir isotherm fail to explain the Freundlich observation at intermediate pressures?

Explanation: Real surfaces are energetically heterogeneous, which causes deviations from the uniform-surface Langmuir model at intermediate coverages.

Question 17 of 19

In the Langmuir kinetic derivation, the rate of desorption is proportional to:

Explanation: Desorption occurs only from occupied sites, making its rate directly proportional to the fraction of occupied surface coverage (θ).

Question 18 of 19

Which equation represents the Langmuir isotherm in terms of volume adsorbed v?

Explanation: Since v is proportional to θ (v = vmaxθ), multiplying θ = KP / (1 + KP) by vmax yields v = vmaxKP / (1 + KP).

Question 19 of 19

When KP << 1, what is the apparent order of adsorption with respect to gas pressure?

Explanation: At low pressures where θ = KP, coverage depends linearly on pressure, corresponding to first-order kinetics.
