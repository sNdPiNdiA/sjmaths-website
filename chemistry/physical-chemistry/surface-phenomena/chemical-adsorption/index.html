Physical Chemistry

Chemical Adsorption

Chemical adsorption involves strong chemical bond formation between adsorbate molecules and the surface atoms of the adsorbent. It exhibits high activation energy and leads to unimolecular or multimolecular layers depending on surface sites.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamics and Energetics

  • Chemisorption is strongly exothermic with enthalpy values ranging from -80 to -240 kJ mol-1.
  • Entropy decreases (ΔS < 0) as molecules lose translational degrees of freedom upon surface binding.
  • Spontaneity is driven by a large negative enthalpy change (ΔG < 0).

Activation Energy and Temperature Effects

  • Chemisorption requires an activation energy, causing the rate to initially increase with temperature (activated adsorption).
  • Elevated temperatures enhance the rate of chemisorption up to an optimal threshold.
  • Surface compounds formed are stable, making the process largely irreversible.

Specificity and Surface Coverage

  • Chemisorption is highly specific and occurs only when chemical bonds can form between specific adsorbate-adsorbent pairs.
  • It forms strictly a monolayer because surface valencies become saturated once primary bonds are established.
  • Presence of active centers and surface defects dictates the extent of chemisorption.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Chemisorption generally increases with temperature due to activation energy.

Observation: At very high temperatures, chemisorption decreases. — High thermal energy favors the reverse endothermic desorption process.

Exception Chemisorption is strictly irreversible.

Observation: Certain systems undergo desorption under ultra-high vacuum with extreme heating. — Thermal input supplies the high activation energy required to break the surface-adsorbate bond.

Exception Chemisorption forms only a monolayer.

Observation: Dissociative chemisorption can sometimes mimic multilayer effects if secondary reactions occur. — Subsequent chemical reactions on top of the first layer create complex multi-layered surface products.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Chemisorption is characterized by high enthalpy of adsorption (-80 to -240 kJ mol-1).
  • It requires an activation energy barrier, classifying it as activated adsorption.
  • The process is highly specific and depends on the chemical nature of both adsorbate and adsorbent.
  • Chemisorption results in the formation of surface chemical bonds and is irreversible.
  • It is restricted to monolayer coverage because chemical valencies of surface atoms become saturated.
  • High pressure has little effect on chemisorption once the monolayer is fully saturated.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing chemisorption with physisorption temperature profiles; chemisorption increases initially with temperature.
  • Assuming chemisorption forms multilayers; it is strictly a monolayer phenomenon.
  • Neglecting the activation energy requirement in kinetic analyses of chemisorption.
  • Confusing the reversibility of physical and chemical adsorption processes.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Adsorption

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Chemical Bond Formation

Adsorbate molecules form true chemical bonds with adsorbent surface atoms, releasing substantial energy.

Activation Energy Requirements

An initial energy barrier must be overcome, making chemisorption slow at low temperatures.

Monolayer Limitation

Surface saturation restricts uptake strictly to a single monolayer of bound species.

Irreversibility and Specificity

High bond strength imparts chemical specificity and irreversible retention of the adsorbate.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Chemisorption (Chemical Adsorption)
Adsorption involving chemical bond formation between adsorbate and adsorbent.
Activation Energy (Ea)
Energy barrier required to initiate chemical adsorption in J mol-1.
Monolayer (θ = 1)
Single layer of adsorbed species covering all active surface sites.
Enthalpy of Adsorption (ΔH)
Heat released when adsorbate binds to surface, measured in kJ mol-1.
Active Centers (Surface Sites)
Unsaturated atoms on the solid surface where chemisorption occurs.
Desorption (Surface Release)
Process of removing adsorbed species from the surface.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔH for chemisorption is -80 to -240 kJ mol-1.
  • Chemisorption is irreversible and specific.
  • Activation energy is required for chemisorption to proceed.
  • Coverage is restricted to a unimolecular layer.
  • Temperature increase initially increases chemisorption rate.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Chemical adsorption involves electron transfer or electron sharing.
  • It resembles a chemical reaction occurring at a phase boundary.
  • Surface area and active sites dictate the extent of chemisorption.
  • High temperatures eventually cause desorption due to thermal breaking of bonds.

⚖️ Key Differences

Physisorption VS Chemisorption

Physisorption uses Van der Waals forces with low enthalpy; chemisorption uses chemical bonds with high enthalpy.
