Physical Chemistry

Surface Phenomena

Surface phenomena involve the physical and chemical changes occurring at the interface of two phases, typically solid-gas or solid-liquid. These processes are governed by surface energy, adsorption, and the formation of colloidal systems.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Adsorption Dynamics

  • Adsorption is an exothermic process where ΔH and ΔS are negative.
  • Physisorption involves weak van der Waals forces, while chemisorption involves strong chemical bonds.
  • Chemisorption is highly specific and often irreversible, whereas physisorption is reversible.
  • The extent of adsorption increases with surface area of the adsorbent.

Colloidal Systems

  • Colloids are classified as lyophilic (solvent-attracting) or lyophobic (solvent-repelling).
  • Tyndall effect is the scattering of light by colloidal particles.
  • Brownian motion arises from the unbalanced bombardment of particles by dispersion medium molecules.
  • Hardy-Schulze rule states that the coagulating power of an ion increases with its valency.

Catalysis and Micelles

  • Homogeneous catalysis involves reactants and catalysts in the same phase.
  • Heterogeneous catalysis involves reactants and catalysts in different phases, typically solid-gas.
  • Micelles are aggregated surfactant molecules formed above the Critical Micelle Concentration (CMC).
  • Kraft temperature is the minimum temperature required for micelle formation.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Langmuir Adsorption Isotherm
x/m = (aP) / (1 + bP)
Conditions: Monolayer adsorption, uniform surface
Units: a, b are constants
Gibbs Adsorption Isotherm
Γ = -(1/RT) * (dγ / dlnC)
Conditions: Constant temperature, dilute solutions
Units: Γ in mol/m², γ in J/m²
BET Equation
P / [v(P0 - P)] = 1 / (vmc) + [(c-1) / (vmc)] * (P / P0)
Conditions: Multilayer adsorption
Units: v, vm in volume units
Freundlich Adsorption Isotherm
x/m = kP1/n
Conditions: Moderate pressure range, n > 1
Units: x/m in g/g, P in atm or bar
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Adsorption decreases with temperature

Observation: Chemisorption — Chemisorption requires an activation energy barrier to form chemical bonds.

Exception Surface tension decreases with solute

Observation: Inorganic electrolytes — Strong hydration of ions increases the surface tension of water.

Exception Colloids are stable

Observation: Lyophobic sols — Lack of solvation shell makes them susceptible to coagulation by small amounts of electrolytes.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Hardy-Schulze Rule Mnemonic
Power ∝ Valency

Application: Higher charge on the coagulating ion leads to faster precipitation of the sol.

Freundlich Log Plot
log(x/m) = log k + (1/n)log P

Application: Slope of the linear plot gives 1/n; intercept gives log k.

Adsorption Sign Convention
ΔG = ΔH - TΔS

Application: Since ΔS is negative, ΔH must be sufficiently negative to ensure ΔG < 0.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Adsorption is a surface phenomenon involving accumulation of molecular species.
  • Freundlich isotherm fails at high pressures.
  • Gold number measures the protective power of lyophilic colloids.
  • Electrophoresis is the movement of colloidal particles under an electric field.
  • Dialysis is used to purify colloidal solutions.
  • Catalysts provide an alternative reaction path with lower activation energy.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing adsorption with absorption; adsorption is surface-only, absorption is bulk.
  • Assuming all adsorption is exothermic; while true, chemisorption may show an initial endothermic rise.
  • Misapplying the Hardy-Schulze rule by ignoring the charge of the counter-ion.
  • Neglecting the effect of temperature on the CMC of surfactants.

⚡ Quick Revision: Surface Phenomena

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Adsorption Isotherms

Mathematical models relating the amount of adsorbate to the pressure or concentration at constant temperature.

Colloidal Stability

Maintained by electrostatic repulsion or solvation; disrupted by electrolyte addition (coagulation).

Surface Tension

The force acting per unit length on the surface of a liquid, influenced by intermolecular forces.

Catalysis Mechanism

Adsorption theory of catalysis explains how reactants bind to active sites on solid surfaces.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Adsorbate (Substance adsorbed)
The species that accumulates on the surface.
Adsorbent (Surface material)
The material providing the surface for adsorption.
Desorption (Removal)
The process of removing an adsorbed substance.
Sorption (Combined)
When both adsorption and absorption occur simultaneously.
Sol (Colloidal suspension)
Solid dispersed in a liquid medium.
Emulsion (Liquid-liquid colloid)
Dispersion of one liquid in another.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔH < 0 for adsorption
  • ΔS < 0 for adsorption
  • Hardy-Schulze: Coagulation power ∝ Valency^n
  • Tyndall effect is for colloids, not true solutions
  • CMC is the concentration where micelles form

📌 Quick Recall

  • Adsorption is surface-specific.
  • Colloids are intermediate between solutions and suspensions.
  • Catalysts lower activation energy.
  • Surface tension is a result of unbalanced cohesive forces.

⚖️ Key Differences

Adsorption VS Absorption

Adsorption is surface, absorption is bulk.
