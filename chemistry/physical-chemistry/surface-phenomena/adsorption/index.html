Physical Chemistry

Adsorption

Adsorption is the accumulation of molecular species at the surface rather than in the bulk of a solid or liquid. It is a spontaneous surface phenomenon driven by a decrease in Gibbs free energy (ΔG < 0).

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamics and Energetics of Adsorption

  • Adsorption is invariably an exothermic process because attractive forces are established between adsorbate and adsorbent surface, making ΔH negative.
  • The movement of gas molecules onto a surface restricts their translational degrees of freedom, resulting in a decrease in entropy (ΔS < 0).
  • For adsorption to occur spontaneously, the Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) must be negative, satisfied when the magnitude of ΔH exceeds TΔS.

Classification: Physical versus Chemical Adsorption

  • Physisorption involves weak van der Waals forces, is reversible, and has low enthalpy of adsorption (20-40 kJ mol-1).
  • Chemisorption involves strong chemical bonds, is largely irreversible, and exhibits high enthalpy of adsorption (80-240 kJ mol-1).
  • Physisorption decreases continuously with increasing temperature, whereas chemisorption initially increases with temperature due to an activation energy barrier.

Adsorption Isotherms and Surface Area

  • The Freundlich isotherm fails at high pressures where x/m becomes independent of pressure.
  • The BET theory extends Langmuir's model to account for multi-layer adsorption and is used for determining specific surface area of solids.
  • Factors affecting adsorption include surface area of the adsorbent, nature of the gas (easily liquefiable gases adsorb more), and activation of the solid.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Langmuir Adsorption Isotherm
x/m = (a · P) / (1 + b · P)
Conditions: Monolayer adsorption, constant temperature
Units: a and b are empirical constants, P in atm
Gibbs Adsorption Isotherm
Γ = (- c / RT) · (dγ / dc)
Conditions: Solution-solid or solution-gas interface at equilibrium
Units: Γ in mol m-2, c in mol L-1, γ in N m-1
Thermodynamics of Adsorption
ΔG = ΔH - TΔS
Conditions: At constant temperature and pressure
Units: ΔG, ΔH in kJ mol-1, ΔS in J K-1 mol-1
Freundlich Adsorption Isotherm
x/m = k · P1/n
Conditions: Intermediate pressure range, constant temperature
Units: x/m in g g-1 or mol g-1, P in atm or kPa
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Physisorption decreases uniformly with an increase in temperature.

Observation: Chemisorption first increases and then decreases with temperature. — Chemisorption requires a specific activation energy; thus, it behaves like an activated chemical reaction.

Exception Adsorption is always exothermic with a negative enthalpy change.

Observation: Endothermic adsorption of certain polymers and proteins from solution. — Desorption of structured solvent molecules from the surface provides a large positive entropy change that drives the overall process.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Freundlich Logarithmic Linear Form
log(x/m) = log k + (1/n) log P

Application: Use this straight-line equation (y = mx + c) where the slope gives 1/n and the y-intercept gives log k.

Gas Liquefaction Correlation
Higher Critical Temperature (Tc) → Greater Adsorption

Application: Gases with higher boiling points or critical temperatures liquefy easily and show significantly higher extent of physisorption.

Entropy Sign Rule
ΔS is always negative for adsorption

Application: Remember that confinement of mobile gas molecules to a fixed surface always restricts degrees of freedom.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Adsorption is a surface phenomenon while absorption is a bulk phenomenon.
  • The extent of adsorption x/m is directly proportional to the surface area of the adsorbent.
  • Critical temperature of gases follows the order SO2 > HCl > NH3 > CO2 > CH4 > N2.
  • In Freundlich isotherm, the value of 1/n lies between 0 and 1 under normal pressure ranges.
  • Charcoal adsorbs gases like Cl2 and NH3 much more effectively than permanent gases like H2 or He.
  • Langmuir isotherm assumes dynamic equilibrium between adsorbed and free gas molecules.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing adsorption with absorption; remember adsorption is strictly restricted to the surface interface.
  • Assuming chemisorption is favored by very low temperatures; it requires thermal activation energy.
  • Treating 1/n as a constant in Freundlich isotherm across all pressure ranges.
  • Overlooking the fact that ΔS is negative despite spontaneity, compensated by a large negative ΔH.

⚡ Quick Revision: Adsorption

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Surface vs Bulk Accumulation

Adsorption localizes chemical species at the phase boundary. The concentration on the surface is significantly higher than in the bulk phase.

Thermodynamic Driver

The spontaneity of adsorption is dictated by ΔG = ΔH - TΔS. Since ΔS is negative, ΔH must be sufficiently negative to keep ΔG < 0.

Adsorption Isotherms

Mathematical expressions relating gas pressure (P) or solution concentration (c) to the amount adsorbed per unit mass (x/m) at constant temperature.

Catalytic Applications

Heterogeneous catalysis relies heavily on adsorption to concentrate reactants and lower activation energy barriers via intermediate surface complexes.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Adsorbate (Species adsorbed)
The substance that gets concentrated at the surface.
Adsorbent (Surface provider)
The solid or liquid surface on which adsorption occurs.
Desorption (Removal process)
The reverse of adsorption where species leave the surface.
Sorption (Combined process)
A process where both adsorption and absorption occur simultaneously.
Occlusion (Gas in metals)
Adsorption of gases on the surface of transition metals.
Isotherm (Constant T relation)
Equation relating adsorption amount to pressure at fixed temperature.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔH < 0, ΔS < 0, ΔG < 0 for spontaneous adsorption.
  • Freundlich equation: x/m = kP1/n.
  • Langmuir equation: x/m = aP / (1 + bP).
  • Physisorption decreases with temperature while chemisorption increases initially.
  • Easily liquefiable gases show higher adsorption.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Surface chemistry studies interfacial phenomena including adsorption.
  • Energetics are governed by enthalpy-entropy compensation.
  • Isotherms provide mathematical descriptions of surface coverage.
  • Industrial applications include gas masks, heterogeneous catalysis, and chromatography.

⚖️ Key Differences

Adsorption VS Absorption

Adsorption is surface-only, whereas absorption is uniform bulk penetration.

Which adsorption isotherm equation is applicable for multi-layer adsorption?

Solution: BET (Brunauer-Emmett-Teller) theory accounts for multi-layer adsorption.

Question 2 of 10
What is the slope of the straight line in the plot of x/m versus P at very low pressure for Langmuir isotherm?

Solution: At low pressure, 1 + bP ≈ 1, so x/m = aP, giving slope a.

Question 3 of 10
Enthalpy of physical adsorption is usually in the range of:

Solution: Weak van der Waals forces in physisorption release 20 to 40 kJ mol-1.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following processes is not accompanied by adsorption?

Solution: Diffusion into the bulk is absorption, not adsorption.

Question 5 of 10
Why does chemisorption first increase and then decrease with temperature?

Solution: Initial rise is due to overcoming activation energy; subsequent decrease is due to the exothermic nature favoring desorption.

Question 6 of 10
In Gibbs adsorption isotherm, Γ represents:

Solution: Γ denotes the excess concentration of solute per unit surface area.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following conditions favors maximum physical adsorption of a gas on a solid?

Solution: Low temperature favors exothermic physisorption, and high pressure increases gas concentration at the surface.

Question 8 of 10
Activated charcoal is prepared by:

Solution: Destructive distillation of wood in the absence of air opens up micropores, yielding activated charcoal.

Question 9 of 10
Which property is shared by both physisorption and chemisorption?

Solution: Both phenomena involve attractive interactions and are exothermic (ΔH < 0).

Question 10 of 10
In the BET equation, what does Vm signify?

Solution: Vm is the volume of gas needed to form a complete monolayer on the adsorbent surface.
