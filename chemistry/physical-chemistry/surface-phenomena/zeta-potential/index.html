Physical Chemistry

Zeta Potential

Zeta potential is the electrical potential at the hydrodynamic plane of shear relative to the bulk liquid phase. It serves as a primary indicator of the stability of colloidal dispersions by quantifying the magnitude of electrostatic repulsion between particles.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrical Double Layer Structure

  • The Stern layer comprises strongly adsorbed counter-ions fixed tightly to the charged particle surface.
  • The diffuse layer extends into the bulk solution where ions are mobile and influenced by both thermal motion and electrostatic forces.
  • The slipping plane (hydrodynamic plane of shear) marks the boundary dividing fluid that moves with the particle from fluid that remains stationary.

Electrophoretic Mobility and Zeta Potential

  • Electrophoretic mobility (μ) is defined as the velocity of a colloidal particle per unit electric field strength.
  • Zeta potential (ζ) cannot be measured directly but is calculated from experimentally determined electrophoretic mobility using hydrodynamic models.
  • The magnitude of zeta potential reflects the net electrical charge contained within the region bounded by the slipping plane.

Factors Affecting Zeta Potential

  • pH changes alter surface charge density by modulating the protonation or deprotonation of surface functional groups.
  • Addition of electrolyte concentration compresses the electrical double layer, shifting the potential drop and reducing absolute zeta potential values.
  • Valency of counter-ions follows the Schulze-Hardy rule, where higher charge ions drastically decrease zeta potential and induce flocculation.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Huckel Equation
μ = (2 εr ε0 ζ) / (3 η)
Conditions: Thick electrical double layer where particle radius a << Debye length κ-1
Units: μ in m2s-1V-1, ζ in Volts
Henry Equation
μ = (2 εr ε0 ζ / 3 η) f(κa)
Conditions: General case encompassing all ratios of particle radius to double layer thickness
Units: f(κa) ranges from 1.0 (Huckel) to 1.5 (Smoluchowski)
Debye Length
κ-1 = [(εr ε0 kB T) / (2 e2 I)]1/2
Conditions: Aqueous electrolyte solution at absolute temperature T
Units: κ-1 in meters, I in mol·m-3, kB in J·K-1
Helmholtz-Smoluchowski Equation
μ = (εr ε0 ζ) / η
Conditions: Thin electrical double layer where particle radius a >> Debye length κ-1
Units: μ in m2s-1V-1, εr (dimensionless), ε0 in Fm-1, ζ in Volts, η in Pa·s
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher electrolyte concentration always compresses the double layer and lowers zeta potential.

Observation: Specific ion adsorption (potential-determining ions) — Ions that chemically bind to the Stern layer can reverse the sign of the zeta potential entirely, regardless of ionic strength.

Exception Colloidal stability is maintained at high absolute zeta potential values (> |30 mV|).

Observation: Steric stabilization mechanisms — Polymeric coatings provide physical barriers preventing aggregation even when zeta potentials are near zero.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Zeta potential is measured in millivolts (mV).
  • A threshold of ±30 mV separates stable and unstable colloidal suspensions.
  • The slipping plane lies at an unknown distance slightly outside the Stern plane.
  • Electrophoresis is the primary experimental technique used to determine zeta potential.
  • Polyvalent counter-ions are extremely effective at reducing zeta potential due to charge neutralization.
  • Isoelectric point (IEP) is the pH at which the zeta potential equals exactly zero.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the Nernst potential (surface potential) with the zeta potential (shear plane potential).
  • Assuming zeta potential is equal to the total surface potential without accounting for the Stern layer potential drop.
  • Neglecting temperature effects on medium viscosity (η) when calculating mobility.
  • Applying the Smoluchowski equation to very small nanoparticles suspended in high-purity deionized water.

⚡ Quick Revision: Zeta Potential

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrical Double Layer

The interface between a charged solid surface and a liquid medium consists of a fixed Stern layer and a mobile diffuse layer.

Slipping Plane

The hydrodynamic plane of shear separates the stationary fluid bound to the particle from the moving bulk solution.

Electrophoretic Mobility

Mobility measures particle velocity per unit electric field and forms the experimental basis for zeta potential calculations.

Colloidal Stability

High electrostatic repulsion prevents particle aggregation, quantified by an absolute zeta potential exceeding 30 mV.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Zeta Potential (ζ)
Potential at the hydrodynamic plane of shear in mV.
Nernst Potential (E0)
Potential difference between solid surface and bulk solution.
Electrophoretic Mobility (μ)
Velocity per unit electric field in m2s-1V-1.
Debye Length (κ-1)
Thickness of the electrical double layer in meters.
Stern Layer (Inner EDL)
Layer of tightly bound counter-ions on the particle surface.
Isoelectric Point (IEP)
pH value where zeta potential is zero.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Stable suspension threshold: |ζ| > 30 mV
  • Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation applies to thick double layers where κa >> 1
  • Schulze-Hardy rule: flocculating power increases with ion valency
  • Isoelectric point corresponds to zero electrophoretic mobility
  • Zeta potential is dependent on pH, ionic strength, and additives

📌 Quick Recall

  • Zeta potential measures electrokinetic potential at the shear plane.
  • It dictates colloidal stability through electrostatic repulsion.
  • Calculated from electrophoretic mobility using Henry or Smoluchowski equations.
  • Altered significantly by pH and electrolyte concentration.

⚖️ Key Differences

Surface Charge VS Zeta Potential

Surface charge is the total charge on the particle, whereas zeta potential is the potential at the slipping plane distance away.

Question 1 of 19

What does the zeta potential measure?

Explanation: Zeta potential is defined as the electrical potential at the hydrodynamic plane of shear (slipping plane) relative to the bulk liquid.

Question 2 of 19

What is the typical threshold value in millivolts for a stable colloidal dispersion?

Explanation: Dispersions with zeta potentials more positive than +30 mV or more negative than -30 mV are normally considered stable against aggregation.

Question 3 of 19

Which equation applies when the electrical double layer is very thin compared to the particle radius?

Explanation: The Helmholtz-Smoluchowski equation is valid for systems where particle radius is much greater than the Debye length (κa >> 1).

Question 4 of 19

What happens to the zeta potential of a negatively charged sol when NaCl is added?

Explanation: Adding an electrolyte compresses the diffuse double layer and reduces the magnitude of the zeta potential.

Question 5 of 19

The pH at which the zeta potential of a colloidal particle is zero is called:

Explanation: The isoelectric point (IEP) is the specific pH where electrophoretic mobility and zeta potential are zero.

Question 6 of 19

According to the Schulze-Hardy rule, which ion is most effective in coagulating a negatively charged sol?

Explanation: The coagulating power increases rapidly with the valency of the active ion; Al3+ has the highest valency among the cations listed.

Question 7 of 19

Electrophoretic mobility is measured in which of the following units?

Explanation: Mobility is velocity per unit electric field, yielding units of m2 s-1 V-1.

Question 8 of 19

Which layer of the electrical double layer contains strongly bound counter-ions?

Explanation: The Stern layer consists of ions adsorbed directly and tightly to the charged particle surface.

Question 9 of 19

Can zeta potential be directly measured with a standard potentiometer?

Explanation: Zeta potential cannot be measured directly; it is calculated from electrokinetic measurements such as electrophoresis.

Question 10 of 19

How does increasing temperature affect zeta potential measurements?

Explanation: Temperature changes the viscosity of the liquid medium, which directly influences electrophoretic mobility and calculated zeta potential.

Question 11 of 19

What is the primary force preventing aggregation in a stable colloidal system with high zeta potential?

Explanation: High like-charges on particles generate strong electrostatic repulsion that overcomes attractive Van der Waals forces.

Question 12 of 19

In the Henry equation, what is the value of the function f(κa) in the Huckel limit?

Explanation: For non-conducting particles in non-polar media (Huckel limit, κa << 1), f(κa) equals 1.0.

Question 13 of 19

Which of the following parameters does NOT directly affect the zeta potential of a suspension?

Explanation: Container volume does not influence interfacial electrochemistry, whereas pH, ionic strength, and potential-determining ions do.

Question 14 of 19

What occurs at the slipping plane?

Explanation: The slipping plane defines the hydrodynamic boundary where liquid begins to move relative to the moving particle.

Question 15 of 19

If a metal oxide sol has an isoelectric point at pH 8, what is its surface charge at pH 5?

Explanation: At pH values below the isoelectric point, excess protons protonate the surface, conferring a net positive charge.

Question 16 of 19

Which technique is commonly used to measure particle mobility for zeta potential determination?

Explanation: Laser Doppler Electrophoresis measures the velocity of particles in an electric field via scattered light frequency shifts.

Question 17 of 19

The thickness of the electrical double layer is inversely proportional to:

Explanation: Debye length (κ-1) is inversely proportional to the square root of ionic strength (I).

Question 18 of 19

What is the sign of zeta potential for a typical silica nanoparticle dispersed in water at neutral pH?

Explanation: Silanol groups (Si-OH) deprotonate at neutral pH, giving silica particles a negative surface charge and negative zeta potential.

Question 19 of 19

Which of the following statements regarding the Stern layer is correct?

Explanation: Ions in the Stern layer are strongly adsorbed and held rigidly to the charged surface.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the approximate electrophoretic mobility of a particle if its zeta potential is 30 mV in water at 25°C (η = 0.89 mPa·s, εr = 78.5, ε0 = 8.85 × 10-12 Fm-1) using the Smoluchowski limit.

Solution: Using μ = (εr ε0 ζ) / η = (78.5 × 8.85 × 10-12 × 0.030) / (0.89 × 10-3) ≈ 2.3 × 10-8 m2s-1V-1.

Question 2 of 10
Which ion is least effective in coagulating a negatively charged arsenic trisulfide sol?

Solution: Arsenic trisulfide sol is negatively charged; therefore, anions like PO43- have negligible coagulating power compared to cations due to charge repulsion.

Question 3 of 10
What is the effect of increasing electrolyte concentration on the thickness of the electrical double layer?

Solution: Higher ionic strength increases counter-ion concentration, which compresses and reduces the Debye length (κ-1).

Question 4 of 10
Why does lowering the pH below the isoelectric point cause a protein or metal oxide suspension to stabilize?

Solution: Moving away from the isoelectric point increases net surface charge, raising the absolute zeta potential and enhancing electrostatic repulsion.

Question 5 of 10
In Henry's equation, what parameter determines whether the Huckel or Smoluchowski limit applies?

Solution: The product κa (ratio of particle radius to Debye length) dictates which limiting approximation applies.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following describes the potential drop across the Stern layer?

Solution: Potential drops relatively linearly across the compact Stern layer before decaying exponentially in the diffuse layer.

Question 7 of 10
A suspension has a zeta potential of -12 mV. How would you classify its physical stability?

Solution: Absolute zeta potentials between 0 and ±20 mV typically indicate rapid agglomeration and poor stability.

Question 8 of 10
What is the primary role of surfactant addition in controlling zeta potential?

Solution: Surfactants adsorb onto particle surfaces, modifying the Stern layer charge and shifting the zeta potential.

Question 9 of 10
If electrophoretic mobility is zero, what can be inferred about the zeta potential?

Solution: Electrophoretic mobility is directly proportional to zeta potential; zero mobility corresponds to zero zeta potential.

Question 10 of 10
Which factor is NOT accounted for in the Huckel equation for mobility?

Solution: The Huckel equation assumes a very thick double layer where retardation and relaxation effects of the diffuse cloud are negligible.
