Physical Chemistry

Physical Adsorption

Physical adsorption involves the accumulation of gases or liquids on the surface of a solid through weak van der Waals forces. The process is reversible and characterized by low enthalpies of adsorption.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Nature of Forces and Reversibility

  • Physisorption is driven by weak van der Waals forces between adsorbate and adsorbent.
  • The process is completely reversible in nature; lowering pressure desorbs the gas.
  • No new chemical bonds are formed, maintaining the chemical identity of the adsorbate.

Thermodynamics and Temperature Dependence

  • Adsorption is invariably exothermic with negative enthalpy values (ΔH < 0).
  • Entropy decreases during adsorption (ΔS < 0) as translational degrees of freedom are lost.
  • Physisorption decreases continuously with a rise in temperature following Le Chatelier's principle.

Surface Area and Activation Energy

  • The extent of adsorption is directly proportional to the specific surface area of the adsorbent.
  • Physisorption requires zero or negligible activation energy due to non-specific interactions.
  • Multimolecular layers can form on the adsorbent surface at pressures approaching saturation vapor pressure.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Freundlich Isotherm (Logarithmic Form)
log(x / m) = log k + (1 / n) log P
Conditions: Straight-line plot analysis
Units: x/m in g g-1, P in atm
Langmuir Adsorption Isotherm
x / m = (a · P) / (1 + b · P)
Conditions: Monolayer adsorption, constant temperature
Units: a and b are constants, P in atm
Enthalpy of Adsorption
ΔHad = -20 to -40 kJ mol-1
Conditions: Physisorption standard conditions
Units: ΔH in kJ mol-1
Freundlich Adsorption Isotherm
x / m = k · P1/n
Conditions: Moderate pressure range
Units: x/m in g g-1, P in atm, k and n are empirical constants
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Adsorption decreases with increase in temperature for all gases

Observation: Initial heating at very low temperatures can sometimes increase rate — Overcoming minor kinetic bottlenecks to reach equilibrium state

Exception Easily liquefiable gases show low adsorption

Observation: Easily liquefiable gases show higher physisorption — Higher critical temperature and stronger intermolecular van der Waals forces

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Critical Temperature Rule
Tc ∝ Extent of Adsorption

Application: Gases with higher critical temperatures (like SO2, NH3) liquefy easily and show greater physisorption than H2 or He.

Freundlich Slope Test
0 ≤ 1/n ≤ 1

Application: The value of 1/n derived from the log(x/m) vs log P plot usually lies between 0 and 1 for valid physisorption.

Enthalpy Sign Check
ΔHad = Negative

Application: Always remember physical adsorption releases heat; therefore, ΔH is negative and ranges between -20 and -40 kJ/mol.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Physical adsorption is non-specific and occurs with all gases on all solids.
  • The enthalpy of physical adsorption ranges between -20 to -40 kJ/mol.
  • Freundlich adsorption isotherm fails at high pressures.
  • High surface area solids like silica gel and activated charcoal are effective physical adsorbents.
  • Physisorption leads to the formation of multimolecular layers at high pressures.
  • Entropy change (ΔS) for adsorption is always negative.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming physisorption involves chemical bond formation; it only involves van der Waals forces.
  • Confusing the sign of enthalpy; adsorption is always exothermic (ΔH < 0).
  • Applying Freundlich isotherm at excessively high pressures where it deviates.
  • Forgetting that entropy decreases (ΔS < 0) when gas molecules are confined to a surface.

⚡ Quick Revision: Physical Adsorption

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Surface Phenomenon Fundamentals

Adsorption is a surface phenomenon where the concentration of adsorbate is higher at the surface than in the bulk phase.

Thermodynamics of Adsorption

Spontaneous adsorption is driven by a negative Gibbs free energy change (ΔG = ΔH - TΔS < 0), combining exothermic enthalpy and negative entropy.

Adsorption Isotherms

Freundlich and Langmuir isotherms mathematically relate the extent of adsorption (x/m) to the equilibrium pressure (P) or concentration.

Factors Affecting Adsorption

Nature of gas (critical temperature), surface area of adsorbent, temperature, and pressure govern the extent of physical adsorption.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Adsorbate (Substance adsorbed)
The substance that gets accumulated on the surface.
Adsorbent (Surface material)
The solid or liquid surface on which adsorption occurs.
Desorption (Removal process)
The reverse of adsorption where gas is released from the surface.
Occlusion (Gas in metals)
Adsorption of gases specifically on metal surfaces.
Isotherm (Constant T plot)
Variation of adsorption with pressure at constant temperature.
Multilayer (Stacked adsorption)
Formation of multiple layers of adsorbate molecules via van der Waals forces.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Physisorption is reversible and exothermic.
  • Enthalpy of physisorption: -20 to -40 kJ/mol.
  • Freundlich equation: x/m = kP^(1/n).
  • Easily liquefiable gases adsorb more extensively.
  • Surface area directly increases adsorption capacity.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Physical adsorption relies on weak van der Waals forces.
  • Temperature increase causes desorption in physisorption.
  • No activation energy barrier exists for physical adsorption.
  • Freundlich isotherm represents moderate pressure behavior well.

⚖️ Key Differences

Physisorption VS Chemisorption

Physisorption forms multimolecular layers with low enthalpy, whereas chemisorption forms unimolecular layers with high enthalpy.
