Physical Chemistry

Hydrolysis of Salts

Salt hydrolysis is the chemical interaction of salt ions with water molecules to produce an acidic, basic, or neutral aqueous solution. It represents the reverse process of a neutralization reaction between an acid and a base.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamics and Equilibrium of Salt Hydrolysis

  • Hydrolysis constant Kh is related to ionic product of water Kw and dissociation constants by Kh = Kw / Ka for salts of weak acids.
  • The degree of hydrolysis h increases with dilution because the forward reaction is favored by higher water concentration.
  • Hydrolysis reactions are generally endothermic, meaning the hydrolysis constant increases with a rise in temperature.

Types of Salt Hydrolysis and pH Characteristics

  • Salts of strong acid and strong base do not undergo hydrolysis and maintain a neutral pH of 7 at 298 K.
  • Salts of weak acid and strong base yield alkaline solutions due to the formation of excess OH- ions.
  • Salts of weak acid and weak base determine solution acidity entirely by the relative magnitudes of Ka and Kb.

Buffer Action and Mathematical Approximations

  • A salt of a weak acid and strong base hydrolyzes to form a mixture acting as a basic buffer system.
  • For salts of weak acids and weak bases, the degree of hydrolysis h is independent of the initial concentration of the salt.
  • The pH of a salt of a weak acid and weak base is independent of concentration when h << 1.

Units: Kh, Kw, Ka, Kb are dimensionless equilibrium constants

Units: pH is dimensionless, C in mol L-1

Conditions: Independent of concentration C
Units: h is dimensionless

Conditions: Valid when Ka ≈ Kb or general approximation
Units: pH is dimensionless

Conditions: Applicable for dilute solutions where hydrolysis constant h << 1
Units: h is dimensionless, Kw and Ka are dissociation constants, C is molar concentration (mol L-1)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Salts of weak acids and weak bases always form neutral solutions.

Observation: They form acidic, basic, or neutral solutions depending on Ka and Kb. — If Ka > Kb, the solution is acidic; if Kb > Ka, it is basic; if Ka = Kb, it is neutral.

Exception Degree of hydrolysis depends on the concentration of the salt solution.

Observation: For salts of weak acids and weak bases, h is independent of concentration. — Concentration terms cancel out in the mathematical expression for h of a weak acid-weak base salt.

Exception Hydrolysis constants remain constant at all conditions.

Observation: Kh varies significantly with temperature. — Since hydrolysis is an endothermic process, Van 't Hoff equation dictates an increase in Kh with temperature.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Quick pH Rule for Salts
pH = 7 + ½(pKa - pKb)

Application: Use this direct formula for weak acid-weak base salts without calculating h.

Nature Determination Shortcut
Weak Acid + Strong Base → Basic

Application: The stronger parent component always dictates the pH nature of the salt solution.

Dilution Effect Trick
h ∝ 1 / √C

Application: Instantly deduce that dilution increases hydrolysis degree for univalent-univalent salts.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The pH of ammonium acetate (CH3COONH4) is independent of its concentration in aqueous solution.
  • For CH3COONa, the hydrolysis constant expression is Kh = Kw / Ka.
  • Salts derived from strong acids and strong bases do not undergo hydrolysis.
  • The unit of hydrolysis constant Kh for a salt of a weak acid and weak base is dimensionless.
  • An increase in temperature increases the degree of hydrolysis because hydrolysis is endothermic.
  • The pH of a 0.1 M solution of NH4Cl is less than 7 at 298 K.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming salts of strong acids and strong bases undergo hydrolysis.
  • Confusing the expression for degree of hydrolysis h with dissociation constant α.
  • Neglecting the effect of temperature on Kw when calculating pH at non-standard temperatures.
  • Applying the concentration-dependent formula of h to weak acid-weak base salts.

⚡ Quick Revision: Hydrolysis of Salts

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition and Principle

Salt hydrolysis is the reverse of neutralization, where ions react with water to alter pH. It depends on the relative strengths of parent acids and bases.

Mathematical Relations

Kh relates Kw with Ka and Kb. The degree of hydrolysis h and pH equations are derived using chemical equilibrium laws.

Effect of Concentration and Temperature

Dilution increases hydrolysis degree for single-ion hydrolyzing salts. Temperature rise increases Kh due to endothermic nature.

Buffer Characteristics

Hydrolyzed salt solutions often behave as buffers, stabilizing pH changes upon addition of small amounts of strong acids or bases.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Hydrolysis Constant (Kh)
Equilibrium constant for salt hydrolysis reaction, dimensionless.
Degree of Hydrolysis (h)
Fraction of total salt hydrolyzed at equilibrium, dimensionless.
Ionic Product of Water (Kw)
Product of molar concentrations of H+ and OH- in water, 10-14 at 298 K.
Acid Dissociation Constant (Ka)
Measure of acid strength in water, mol L-1.
Base Dissociation Constant (Kb)
Measure of base strength in water, mol L-1.
Buffer Solution (pH Buffer)
Solution resisting pH change upon addition of small amounts of acid/base.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Kh = Kw / (Ka × Kb) for weak acid-weak base salts.
  • pH = 7 + ½ pKw + ½ pKa + ½ log C for weak acid-strong base salts.
  • Degree of hydrolysis is independent of concentration for weak acid-weak base salts.
  • Hydrolysis is an endothermic process.
  • Salts of strong acids and strong bases have pH = 7.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Hydrolysis is ionic reaction with water producing acidic or basic solutions.
  • Strong acid-strong base salts do not hydrolyze.
  • Weak component determines the pH nature of the salt solution.
  • Temperature increases hydrolysis constant.

⚖️ Key Differences

Hydrolysis Constant (Kh) VS Dissociation Constant (Ka/Kb)

Kh applies to salt-water reaction, whereas Ka/Kb applies to acid/base dissociation.

Question 1 of 18

What is the pH range of an aqueous solution of ammonium chloride (NH4Cl) at 298 K?

Explanation: NH4Cl is a salt of a strong acid (HCl) and a weak base (NH4OH), making its aqueous solution acidic with pH < 7.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following salts does not undergo hydrolysis?

Explanation: NaCl is formed from a strong acid (HCl) and a strong base (NaOH); neither ion hydrolyzes.

Question 3 of 18

The hydrolysis constant Kh for a salt of a weak acid and weak base is expressed as:

Explanation: For a salt of a weak acid and weak base, both ions hydrolyze, giving Kh = Kw / (Ka × Kb).

Question 4 of 18

How does the degree of hydrolysis (h) of CH3COONa vary with dilution?

Explanation: Since h = √(Kw / (Ka × C)), h is inversely proportional to the square root of concentration C, meaning dilution increases h.

Question 5 of 18

The aqueous solution of which salt is alkaline?

Explanation: CH3COONa is derived from a weak acid (CH3COOH) and a strong base (NaOH), yielding an alkaline solution.

Question 6 of 18

What is the unit of the degree of hydrolysis (h)?

Explanation: The degree of hydrolysis is a fraction or ratio of hydrolyzed moles to total moles, making it dimensionless.

Question 7 of 18

For a salt of a weak acid and weak base, the degree of hydrolysis is independent of:

Explanation: The equation h = √(Kw / (Ka × Kb)) does not contain concentration C.

Question 8 of 18

Which of the following expressions represents the pH of a salt of a weak acid and strong base?

Explanation: The standard derivation for weak acid-strong base salt pH yields pH = 7 + ½ pKw + ½ pKa + ½ log C.

Question 9 of 18

When ammonium acetate is dissolved in water, the solution is nearly neutral because:

Explanation: For ammonium acetate, Ka of acetic acid and Kb of ammonium hydroxide are nearly equal (~1.8 × 10-5), resulting in a neutral pH.

Question 10 of 18

What is the effect of increasing temperature on the hydrolysis constant Kh?

Explanation: Hydrolysis is an endothermic process, so according to Le Chatelier's principle and the Van 't Hoff equation, Kh increases with temperature.

Question 11 of 18

Which salt solution will have the highest pH?

Explanation: CH3COONa produces a basic solution, whereas NaCl is neutral, NH4Cl is acidic, and CH3COONH4 is nearly neutral.

Question 12 of 18

The hydrolysis of which ion causes Na2CO3 solution to be alkaline?

Explanation: CO32- is the conjugate base of the weak acid HCO3-, and its hydrolysis produces OH- ions.

Question 13 of 18

What is the relationship between Kh, Ka, and Kw for a salt of a weak acid and strong base?

Explanation: Kh = Kw / Ka is the standard equilibrium relation for salts of weak acids and strong bases.

Question 14 of 18

Calculate the degree of hydrolysis if Kh is 10-9 and salt concentration is 10-3 M.

Explanation: h = √(Kh / C) = √(10-9 / 10-3) = √(10-6) = 10-3.

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following salts produces an acidic aqueous solution?

Explanation: FeCl3 is a salt of a weak base Fe(OH)3 and a strong acid HCl, resulting in an acidic solution due to cation hydrolysis.

Question 16 of 18

What happens to the pH of a CuSO4 solution when hydrolyzed?

Explanation: CuSO4 is formed from a weak base Cu(OH)2 and a strong acid H2SO4, making the solution acidic.

Question 17 of 18

The expression for hydrolysis constant of a strong acid-weak base salt is:

Explanation: For a strong acid-weak base salt, Kh = Kw / Kb.

Question 18 of 18

Which of the following factors affects the degree of hydrolysis?

Explanation: Temperature, concentration, and the dissociation constants of the parent acid and base all affect the degree of hydrolysis.

Question 1 of 10
What is the pH of a 0.1 M solution of CH3COONa given Ka = 1.8 × 10-5 and Kw = 10-14?

Solution: Using pH = 7 + ½ pKw + ½ pKa + ½ log C, substituting values yields approximately 8.87.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following salts undergoes cationic hydrolysis?

Solution: NH4+ from NH4Cl reacts with water to produce H+ ions, exhibiting cationic hydrolysis.

Question 3 of 10
If the dissociation constant of a weak acid is 10-5, what is the hydrolysis constant of its sodium salt at 298 K?

Solution: Kh = Kw / Ka = 10-14 / 10-5 = 10-9.

Question 4 of 10
Which salt solution is most acidic among the following?

Solution: AlCl3 is formed from a very weak base Al(OH)3 and a strong acid HCl, resulting in high acidity upon hydrolysis.

Question 5 of 10
What is the degree of hydrolysis of 0.01 M NaCN if Kh = 2.0 × 10-5?

Solution: h = √(Kh / C) = √(2.0 × 10-5 / 10-2) = √(2.0 × 10-3) ≈ 0.045.

Question 6 of 10
The pH of a salt of a weak acid and weak base is independent of:

Solution: pH = 7 + ½ pKa - ½ pKb shows no dependence on concentration C.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following ions does not undergo hydrolysis in water?

Solution: Cl- is the conjugate base of a strong acid (HCl) and has negligible affinity for protons in water.

Question 8 of 10
In the hydrolysis of ammonium chloride, the expression for hydrolysis constant is:

Solution: For a strong acid-weak base salt like NH4Cl, Kh = Kw / Kb.

Question 9 of 10
What is the pH of a 0.01 M solution of a salt of weak acid and weak base where pKa = 4.8 and pKb = 4.8?

Solution: pH = 7 + ½ pKa - ½ pKb = 7 + ½(4.8) - ½(4.8) = 7.0.

Question 10 of 10
Why does dilution increase the degree of hydrolysis?

Solution: Water acts as a reactant in hydrolysis; adding water (dilution) shifts the equilibrium forward, increasing h.
