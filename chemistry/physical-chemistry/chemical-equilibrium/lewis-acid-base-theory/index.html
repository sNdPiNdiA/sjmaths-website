Physical Chemistry

Lewis Acid-Base Theory

The Lewis theory defines acids as electron-pair acceptors and bases as electron-pair donors, expanding acid-base chemistry beyond proton transfer. This electronic framework rationalizes coordinate bond formation and the reactivity of numerous metal ions and organic intermediates.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Fundamental Definitions and Classifications

  • A Lewis acid is any chemical species containing a vacant orbital capable of accepting an electron pair.
  • A Lewis base is any species containing at least one unshared pair of electrons available for donation.
  • Molecules with incomplete octets like BF3, AlCl3, and transition metal cations function strictly as Lewis acids.
  • Species with lone pairs such as NH3, H2O, and halide ions (Cl-) function as Lewis bases.

Classification of Acids and Bases (HSAB Principle)

  • Hard acids are small, highly charged, and possess low polarizability (e.g., H+, Li+, Ti4+).
  • Soft acids are large, carry low formal charge, and have high polarizability (e.g., Ag+, Hg2+, Pt2+).
  • Hard bases feature electronegative donor atoms with low polarizability (e.g., F-, OH-, NH3).
  • Soft bases contain donor atoms of higher polarizability and lower electronegativity (e.g., I-, CN-, CO).
  • The HSAB principle states that hard acids preferentially bind to hard bases, and soft acids preferentially bind to soft bases through ionic and covalent interactions respectively.

Factors Affecting Lewis Acidity and Basicity

  • Increasing positive charge on a central atom enhances its Lewis acidity due to increased electrostatic attraction for incoming electron pairs.
  • Electron-withdrawing substituents on a Lewis acid increase its strength, while electron-donating groups decrease it.
  • Steric hindrance can drastically alter coordination ability, as observed in the diminished basicity of 2,6-dimethylpyridine toward bulky acids compared to pyridine.
  • Back-bonding (such as pπ-dπ interactions in boron trihalides) decreases Lewis acidity by increasing electron density at the central atom.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Boron trifluoride (BF3) should be a stronger Lewis acid than BI3 based on halogen electronegativity.

Observation: BI3 is a stronger Lewis acid than BF3 toward most common bases. — Effective 2p-2p π-back-bonding in BF3 fills the vacant boron p-orbital, whereas large iodine atoms cannot efficiently overlap with boron 2p orbitals.

Exception Aliphatic amines are universally stronger bases than ammonia due to inductive electron donation.

Observation: Tertiary amines often show lower apparent basicity than secondary amines in aqueous solution. — Steric hindrance prevents effective solvation of the protonated cation by water molecules, destabilizing the conjugate acid.

Exception Proton (H+) is classified as a hard acid.

Observation: H+ binds very strongly to soft bases like hydride (H-) and carbon in organometallic carbanions. — The extreme charge density of the naked proton overrides standard HSAB size-polarizability matching in specific covalent hydride transfers.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Octet Expansion Check
Central atom has vacant d-orbitals → expands octet → Lewis Acid

Application: Quickly identify SiCl4, SnCl4, and PCl5 as Lewis acids even though they possess complete octets.

HSAB Matching Rule
Hard + Hard = Ionic; Soft + Soft = Covalent

Application: Predict stability of mineral ores and coordination complexes (e.g., HgS is stable because Hg2+ is soft and S2- is soft).

Boron Halide Acidity Trend
BI3 > BBr3 > BCl3 > BF3

Application: Remember that Lewis acidity of boron halides is the exact reverse of their bond dissociation energies and electronegativity trends due to back-bonding.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • BF3 acts as a Lewis acid by accepting an electron pair to form adducts like F3B-NH3.
  • Proton (H+) is the simplest Lewis acid, lacking any electrons and possessing a vacant 1s orbital.
  • CO and CN- function as Lewis bases because they donate carbon lone pairs via coordinate covalent bonds.
  • Metal aqua complexes like [Fe(H2O)6]3+ act as Brønsted acids due to electron withdrawal by the Lewis acidic Fe3+ center.
  • Soft-soft interactions are primarily covalent, whereas hard-hard interactions are predominantly electrostatic.
  • Silicon tetrachloride (SiCl4) undergoes rapid hydrolysis because silicon acts as a Lewis acid toward water molecules.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Brønsted-Lowry basicity with Lewis basicity; all Brønsted bases are Lewis bases, but not all Lewis bases are Brønsted bases (e.g., BF3 is not a Brønsted acid/base).
  • Assuming smaller halogens make boron halides stronger Lewis acids by ignoring back-bonding stabilization.
  • Forgetting that transition metal cations in aqueous solutions exist as hydrated Lewis acid complexes.
  • Treating steric hindrance as irrelevant in Lewis acid-base adduct formation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Lewis Acid-Base Theory

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electronic Theory of Acids and Bases

G.N. Lewis generalized acid-base behavior based on orbital interactions and electron pair sharing, encompassing systems without protons.

Coordinate Covalent Bonding

Adduct formation results from the donation of a non-bonding electron pair from a Lewis base into a vacant low-lying orbital of a Lewis acid.

Hard and Soft Acids and Bases (HSAB)

Pearsons HSAB concept correlates polarizability and charge density to predict reaction thermodynamics and thermodynamic stability of complexes.

Substituent Electronic Effects

Inductive and resonance effects modify electron density at the reactive center, directly altering Lewis acid-base equilibrium constants.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Lewis Acid (Electron-pair acceptor)
A chemical species with a vacant orbital that accepts an electron pair.
Lewis Base (Electron-pair donor)
A chemical species possessing an available unshared electron pair.
Adduct (Coordination complex)
The product formed by the combination of a Lewis acid and a Lewis base.
Hard Acid (Non-polarizable acceptor)
High charge density, small radius, hard metal cation.
Soft Base (Polarizable donor)
Low electronegativity, large radius, highly polarizable donor atom.
Back-bonding (pπ-dπ interaction)
Donation of electron density from a filled ligand orbital to an empty metal orbital.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Lewis acid = electron pair acceptor (vacant orbital)
  • Lewis base = electron pair donor (lone pair)
  • Boron halide acidity order: BI3 > BBr3 > BCl3 > BF3
  • HSAB rule: Hard prefers hard, soft prefers soft
  • All cations are potential Lewis acids

📌 Quick Recall

  • Lewis theory expands acid-base chemistry to non-aqueous and non-protonic systems.
  • Adduct formation is driven by electrostatic and covalent orbital interactions.
  • Polarizability determines soft-soft covalent binding preferences.
  • Steric and electronic factors tune acid-base strength in organic and inorganic molecules.

⚖️ Key Differences

Lewis Acid VS Brønsted Acid

A Lewis acid accepts an electron pair, whereas a Brønsted acid specifically donates a proton (H+).

Which of the following is classified strictly as a Lewis acid but not a Brønsted-Lowry acid?

Explanation: BF3 accepts an electron pair via a vacant p-orbital, making it a Lewis acid, but it possesses no ionizable protons to act as a Brønsted-Lowry acid.

Question 2 of 19

What is the correct order of Lewis acidity for boron trihalides?

Explanation: Due to decreasing pπ-pπ back-bonding efficiency from F to I, the vacant orbital on boron becomes more accessible, making BI3 the strongest Lewis acid.

Question 3 of 19

According to the HSAB principle, which ion pair forms the most stable thermodynamic combination?

Explanation: Ag+ is a soft acid and I- is a soft base; soft-soft interactions are highly favorable and covalent.

Question 4 of 19

Which of the following molecules acts as a Lewis base?

Explanation: NH3 possesses a lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom which it can donate, classifying it as a Lewis base.

Question 5 of 19

In the reaction BF3 + NH3 → F3B-NH3, the species BF3 functions as:

Explanation: BF3 accepts the electron pair from NH3 into its vacant boron orbital, acting as a Lewis acid.

Question 6 of 19

Which species is a hard acid according to Pearson's HSAB classification?

Explanation: Al3+ is a small, highly charged cation with low polarizability, categorizing it as a hard acid.

Question 7 of 19

Why does SiCl4 act as a Lewis acid while CCl4 does not?

Explanation: Silicon can expand its octet by utilizing vacant 3d orbitals to accept electron pairs from nucleophiles, whereas carbon lacks accessible d-orbitals.

Question 8 of 19

Which of the following is considered a soft base?

Explanation: Iodide (I-) is large, has low electronegativity, and high polarizability, making it a classic soft base.

Question 9 of 19

The coordinate covalent bond in a Lewis acid-base adduct is formed by:

Explanation: A coordinate covalent bond (dative bond) is characterized by both bonding electrons originating from the Lewis base.

Question 10 of 19

Which metal cation is classified as a borderline acid in the HSAB principle?

Explanation: Fe2+, Co2+, Ni2+, Cu2+, and Pb2+ are classified as borderline acids lying between hard and soft categories.

Question 11 of 19

What is the primary factor determining the Lewis acidity of simple metal cations in water?

Explanation: A higher charge-to-radius ratio increases electrostatic pull on incoming electron pairs, enhancing Lewis acidity.

Question 12 of 19

Which compound does NOT act as a Lewis acid?

Explanation: Methane (CH4) has a complete octet, no vacant low-lying orbitals, and no lone pairs, so it is neither a Lewis acid nor base.

Question 13 of 19

In terms of Lewis acid strength, how do AlCl3 and BCl3 compare?

Explanation: Aluminum lacks effective 3p-3p back-bonding compared to boron's 2p-2p overlap, leaving the vacant orbital on aluminum more available.

Question 14 of 19

Which solvent system is most appropriate for measuring very strong Lewis acid-base interactions without interference?

Explanation: Non-coordinating solvents or weakly coordinating media like liquid SO2 prevent solvent competition with the Lewis base.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following behaves as both a Lewis acid and a Lewis base?

Explanation: H2O has lone pairs on oxygen (Lewis base) and can accept electron density or release H+ acting as an acid, plus has vacant antibonding orbitals.

Question 16 of 19

The Drago-Wayland equation is used to calculate:

Explanation: Drago-Wayland parameters (E and C) quantify electrostatic and covalent contributions to enthalpy of adduct formation.

Question 17 of 19

Which ligand is a classic π-acceptor Lewis base in transition metal chemistry?

Explanation: Carbon monoxide (CO) donates a lone pair from carbon while accepting metal d-electron density into its vacant π* orbitals.

Question 18 of 19

What happens to the Lewis basicity of amines upon alkylation from primary to tertiary in the gas phase?

Explanation: Inductive electron donation by alkyl groups increases electron density on nitrogen, steadily increasing gas-phase basicity.

Question 19 of 19

Which of the following is a neutral Lewis acid containing a complete octet?

Explanation: BF3 has an incomplete octet, while SiF4 and SnCl4 have complete octets but can accept electron pairs via vacant d-orbitals.
