Physical Chemistry

Brønsted–Lowry Acid–Base Theory

The Brønsted–Lowry theory defines acids as proton donors and bases as proton acceptors, establishing equilibrium systems of conjugate acid-base pairs. This model extends acid-base behavior to non-aqueous media and systems without hydroxyl groups.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Proton Transfer Mechanism and Conjugate Pairs

  • A Brønsted–Lowry acid is defined as any chemical species capable of donating a proton (H+).
  • A Brønsted–Lowry base is defined as any chemical species capable of accepting a proton (H+).
  • A conjugate acid-base pair differs by exactly one proton (H+).
  • The stronger an acid is, the weaker its conjugate base, governed by the inverse relation Ka × Kb = Kw.

Amphiprotic Behavior and Solvent Systems

  • Amphiprotic species such as HCO3- and H2PO4- can act as either Brønsted acids or Brønsted bases depending on the reaction partner.
  • Water exhibits autoprotolysis, acting simultaneously as an acid and a base: 2H2O(l) ⇄ H3O+(aq) + OH-(aq).
  • The leveling effect of water limits the strongest acid in aqueous solution to H3O+ and the strongest base to OH-.

Factors Governing Acid Strength

  • Acid strength increases across a period due to increasing electronegativity of the central atom bonded to hydrogen.
  • Acid strength increases down a group due to decreasing bond dissociation enthalpy resulting from larger atomic radii.
  • Inductive effects and resonance stabilization of the conjugate base anion significantly enhance acid dissociation constants (Ka).

pH = pKa + log10(&frac;[A-];[HA])

Units: Dimensionless pH scale
Ion Product of Water
pKw = pKa + pKb = 14.0
Conditions: Pure water at 298 K

Conjugate Acid-Base Relationship
Ka × Kb = Kw
Conditions: Dilute aqueous solutions at 298 K
Units: Dimensionless equilibrium constants
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher electronegativity atoms always form stronger binary acids

Observation: HF is a weak acid compared to HCl, HBr, and HI — Extremely high H-F bond dissociation enthalpy outweighs the electronegativity factor in aqueous solution.

Exception Anions are generally basic

Observation: HSO4-, H2PO4-, and CH3COO- exhibit varied acid-base strengths — Presence of residual ionizable protons or specific charge delocalization creates amphiprotic or weakly acidic properties.

Exception Binary acid strength increases down a group

Observation: NH3, H2O, and HF periodic reversals — Polarity and hydrogen bonding stabilization dominate in the second period compared to heavier congeners.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Water undergoes autoprotolysis with Kw = 1.0 × 10-14 at 298 K.
  • The conjugate base of a strong acid is a very weak (spectator) base.
  • Amphiprotic species can both accept and donate protons.
  • HI is the strongest binary acid among the hydrogen halides in aqueous solution.
  • Phenol is a stronger acid than ethanol due to resonance stabilization of the phenoxide ion.
  • Acetic acid is partially dissociated in water, possessing a Ka value of approximately 1.8 × 10-5.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Lewis acids/bases with Brønsted-Lowry proton transfer definitions.
  • Failing to change the sign when converting between Ka and pKa.
  • Assuming all hydrogen-containing compounds are Brønsted acids (e.g., CH4 is not acidic).
  • Neglecting the stoichiometry of conjugate pairs when applying the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Brønsted–Lowry Acid–Base Theory

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Proton Transfer Fundamentals

Brønsted-Lowry theory frames acidity strictly around proton exchange, independent of solvent identity.

Conjugate Acid-Base Pairs

Every acid-base reaction establishes two conjugate pairs linked by proton gain or loss.

Periodic Trends in Acidity

Bond strength, electronegativity, and anion stabilization dictate acid strength across the periodic table.

Amphoterism and Autoprotolysis

Molecules like water and amphiprotic ions display dual acidic and basic properties.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Brønsted Acid (H+ Donor)
Species that releases a proton.
Brønsted Base (H+ Acceptor)
Species that accepts a proton.
Conjugate Acid (HA / BH+)
Formed when a base accepts a proton.
Conjugate Base (A- / B)
Formed when an acid donates a proton.
Amphiprotic (Dual Nature)
Can act as either proton donor or acceptor.
Autoprotolysis (Self-ionization)
Proton transfer between identical solvent molecules.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ka × Kb = 1.0 × 10-14 at 298 K
  • Stronger acid gives weaker conjugate base
  • HF is a weak acid despite high electronegativity
  • Water is amphiprotic and undergoes autoprotolysis
  • Protons (H+) exist as hydronium ions (H3O+) in aqueous solution

📌 Quick Recall

  • Brønsted-Lowry theory expands acid-base chemistry beyond aqueous hydroxide systems.
  • Conjugate acid-base pairs differ by a single proton.
  • Anion stability dictates relative acid strength in periodic trends.
  • Amphiprotic substances maintain chemical equilibrium in diverse media.

⚖️ Key Differences

Arrhenius Base VS Brønsted-Lowry Base

Arrhenius bases must produce OH- in water, whereas Brønsted bases only need to accept a proton.

What is the conjugate base of HSO4-?

Explanation: Removing a proton (H+) from HSO4- yields the sulfate ion, SO42-.

Question 2 of 19

Which of the following species is amphiprotic in aqueous solution?

Explanation: HCO3- can accept a proton to form H2CO3 or donate a proton to form CO32-.

Question 3 of 19

According to the Brønsted-Lowry theory, water acts as:

Explanation: Water is amphiprotic and acts as a base with acids (like HCl) and as an acid with bases (like NH3).

Question 4 of 19

What is the conjugate acid of NH3?

Explanation: Adding a proton (H+) to ammonia (NH3) yields the ammonium ion (NH4+).

Question 5 of 19

Which halogen acid is the weakest in aqueous solution?

Explanation: HF has an exceptionally high bond dissociation enthalpy, making it a weak acid compared to other hydrogen halides.

Question 6 of 19

If Ka of an acid is 1.0 × 10-5, what is the pKa?

Explanation: pKa = -log(1.0 × 10-5) = 5.0.

Question 7 of 19

Which species is formed when a Brønsted acid donates a proton?

Explanation: Loss of a proton from a Brønsted acid produces its conjugate base.

Question 8 of 19

In the reaction CH3COOH + H2O ⇄ CH3COO- + H3O+, which is a conjugate acid-base pair?

Explanation: CH3COOH and CH3COO- differ by a single proton (H+), forming a conjugate pair.

Question 9 of 19

What is the product of Ka and Kb for a conjugate acid-base pair at 25 °C?

Explanation: The product Ka × Kb equals Kw, which is 1.0 × 10-14 at 298 K.

Question 10 of 19

Which of the following is NOT a Brønsted-Lowry base?

Explanation: NH4+ is a proton donor (acid), not a proton acceptor.

Question 11 of 19

Which factor primarily explains why HI is a stronger acid than HF?

Explanation: The larger atomic radius of iodine leads to poor orbital overlap and a weak H-I bond, releasing H+ easily.

Question 12 of 19

What is the conjugate base of water (H2O)?

Explanation: Removal of H+ from H2O produces the hydroxide ion (OH-).

Question 13 of 19

Which statement correctly describes the leveling effect of water?

Explanation: Water levels the strength of strong acids so that the strongest acid present in aqueous solution is H3O+.

Question 14 of 19

Which of the following ions is expected to form a basic aqueous solution?

Explanation: CH3COO- is the conjugate base of a weak acid and hydrolyzes to produce OH- ions.

Question 15 of 19

What is the conjugate acid of H2PO4-?

Explanation: Adding a proton to H2PO4- yields phosphoric acid (H3PO4).

Question 16 of 19

In the Brønsted-Lowry system, a substance that donates an electron pair is classified as:

Explanation: Electron pair donation is defined by Lewis theory, not Brønsted-Lowry theory.

Question 17 of 19

Which acid has the highest Ka value?

Explanation: HCl is a strong acid that dissociates completely, giving it a very large Ka value compared to weak organic or inorganic acids.

Question 18 of 19

What is the pH of a neutral aqueous solution at 298 K?

Explanation: At 298 K, [H3O+] = 1.0 × 10-7 M, resulting in pH = 7.

Question 19 of 19

Which species acts as a Brønsted acid in the reaction: NH3 + H2O ⇄ NH4+ + OH-?

Explanation: Water donates a proton to ammonia in this reaction, acting as a Brønsted acid.
