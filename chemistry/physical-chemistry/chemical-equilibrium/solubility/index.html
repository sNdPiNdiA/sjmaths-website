Solubility and Solubility Product

Solubility is the maximum amount of a solute that can dissolve in a given amount of solvent at a specific temperature. The solubility product constant quantifies the equilibrium between a sparingly soluble ionic compound and its dissolved ions in a saturated solution.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Solubility Equilibrium and Ksp Expressions

  • A saturated solution of a sparingly soluble salt establishes a dynamic equilibrium between the undissolved solid and its constituent ions.
  • The solubility product constant (Ksp) depends only on temperature and remains constant regardless of the individual ion concentrations as long as the solid phase is present.
  • Activity coefficients are assumed to be unity in dilute solutions, allowing molar concentrations to substitute for activities in the Ksp expression.

Common Ion Effect and Fractional Precipitation

  • The addition of a common ion suppresses the dissociation and solubility of a sparingly soluble salt due to Le Chatelier's principle shifting the equilibrium backward.
  • Fractional precipitation separates two ions by adding a precipitating reagent whose anion forms a salt with vastly different Ksp values; the less soluble salt precipitates first.
  • Quantitative precipitation is achieved when the concentration of the residual ion is reduced such that its Qsp drops below Ksp.

Effect of pH and Complex Formation on Solubility

  • The solubility of salts containing basic anions (such as CH3COO-, C2O42-, or OH-) increases significantly in acidic medium due to protonation of the anion.
  • Formation of stable coordination complexes with ligands like NH3 or CN- drastically enhances the solubility of sparingly soluble metal salts (e.g., AgCl dissolving in ammonia).
  • Amphoteric hydroxides like Al(OH)3 and Zn(OH)2 dissolve in excess strong base to form soluble hydroxy complexes.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Relation Between Solubility (s) and Ksp for AB Type
Ksp = s2
Conditions: 1:1 electrolyte (e.g., AgCl, NaCl)
Units: s in mol L-1, Ksp in mol2 L-2
Relation Between Solubility (s) and Ksp for AB2 Type
Ksp = 4s3
Conditions: 1:2 electrolyte (e.g., CaF2, BaCl2)
Units: s in mol L-1, Ksp in mol3 L-3
Relation Between Solubility (s) and Ksp for A2B3 Type
Ksp = 108s5
Conditions: 2:3 electrolyte (e.g., As2S3, Al2(SO4)3)
Units: s in mol L-1, Ksp in mol5 L-5
Ionic Product (Qsp) and Precipitation Criterion
Qsp > Ksp ⇒ Precipitation occurs
Conditions: Any instant in solution; if Qsp = Ksp, solution is saturated; if Qsp < Ksp, unsaturated
Units: Molar concentration terms
Solubility Product Constant ($K_{sp}$)
Ksp = [Ay+]x [Bx-]y
Conditions: For a sparingly soluble salt AxBy in a saturated solution at constant temperature
Units: Mx+y (dimensionless or molx+y L-(x+y))
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Addition of a common ion always decreases solubility.

Observation: High concentrations of strong electrolytes containing non-common ions — The 'salt effect' or ionic strength increase reduces activity coefficients of ions, temporarily increasing apparent solubility.

Exception Metal sulfides are insoluble across all pH ranges.

Observation: Metal sulfides like ZnS dissolve readily in dilute mineral acids (HCl), while CuS and HgS do not. — The conjugate base S2- shifts the equilibrium strongly when protonated to form weak acid H2S, provided the Ksp is high enough.

Common Ion Suppression Shortcut
s' ≈ Ksp / [Ccommon]

Application: Quickly estimate the new solubility s' of a sparingly soluble salt in the presence of a large concentration of a common ion.

Precipitation Check Rule
Compare calculated Qsp with tabulated Ksp

Application: If volumes are mixed, remember to recalculate molarities of ions using V1M1 = V2M2 before finding Qsp.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The solubility of AgCl in 0.1 M NaCl is drastically lower than in pure water due to the common ion effect.
  • Units of Ksp vary depending on the stoichiometric coefficients of the dissociation reaction.
  • Precipitation occurs when ionic product Qsp exceeds the solubility product Ksp.
  • Barium sulfate (BaSO4) is used in medicine as a contrast medium because of its extremely low solubility, preventing toxic Ba2+ ion release.
  • Complex formation equilibria can shift solubility limits by tying up metal cations into soluble complex ions.
  • Salts of weak acids (e.g., CaF2, AgCN) show enhanced solubility at lower pH values due to anion protonation.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting to account for stoichiometry coefficients as exponents in the Ksp expression (e.g., writing [Ag+][Cl2-] instead of [Ag+]2[S2-] for Ag2S).
  • Failing to recalculate ion concentrations (M1V1 = M2V2) when two solutions are mixed to test for precipitation.
  • Confusing solubility (mol L-1 or g L-1) with solubility product constant (Ksp), which is dimensionless or concentration-unit dependent.
  • Assuming all metal hydroxides have the same pH dependence regardless of their Ksp values.

⚡ Quick Revision: Solubility and Solubility Product

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Solubility Equilibrium

Sparingly soluble salts establish a heterogeneous equilibrium where the rate of dissolution equals the rate of precipitation. The equilibrium constant for this process is designated as Ksp.

Common Ion Effect

The addition of an ion common to the solute suppresses ionization and decreases solubility. This principle is widely utilized in quantitative gravimetric analysis to ensure complete precipitation.

Precipitation Criteria

By comparing Qsp with Ksp, one determines whether a solution is unsaturated, saturated, or supersaturated. Precipitation occurs exclusively when Qsp > Ksp.

pH Influence on Solubility

Solubility of salts derived from weak acids increases as pH decreases. Conversely, metal hydroxides exhibit increased solubility at lower pH and decreased solubility at higher pH.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Solubility (s)
Maximum concentration of solute in a saturated solution at a given temperature, mol L-1.
Solubility Product (Ksp)
Product of molar concentrations of constituent ions raised to their stoichiometric powers in a saturated solution.
Ionic Product (Qsp)
Product of ion concentrations at any arbitrary condition in solution.
Common Ion Effect (Suppression of s)
Reduction in solubility of a salt by adding an electrolyte containing an ion in common.
Fractional Precipitation (Selective separation)
Sequential precipitation of different ions from solution by gradually increasing precipitant concentration.
Salt Effect (Activity adjustment)
Slight increase in solubility due to presence of non-common inert electrolytes increasing ionic strength.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • For AB → Ksp = s2
  • For AB2 → Ksp = 4s3
  • For A2B3 → Ksp = 108s5
  • Precipitation condition: Qsp > Ksp
  • Temperature change alters the value of Ksp.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Ksp is constant at constant temperature for a given salt.
  • Common ion effect reduces solubility.
  • Acidic medium increases solubility of salts of weak acids.
  • Complexing agents increase solubility by lowering free metal ion concentration.

⚖️ Key Differences

Solubility (s) VS Solubility Product (Ksp)

Solubility is the actual concentration of dissolved solute (mol L-1), whereas Ksp is an equilibrium constant calculated from ion activities.

Question 1 of 18

What is the relationship between solubility (s) and solubility product (Ksp) for silver chromate (Ag2CrO4)?

Explanation: Ag2CrO4 dissociates into 2 Ag+ and 1 CrO42-. Thus Ksp = (2s)2(s) = 4s3.

Question 2 of 18

If the solubility product of CaF2 is 3.2 × 10-11, what is its solubility in pure water?

Explanation: Ksp = 4s3 = 3.2 × 10-11. Therefore s3 = 8.0 × 10-12, giving s = 2.0 × 10-4 M.

Question 3 of 18

Precipitation of a sparingly soluble salt occurs when:

Explanation: Precipitation occurs when the ionic product Qsp exceeds the solubility product Ksp.

Question 4 of 18

Which of the following will have the lowest solubility in 0.1 M NaCl?

Explanation: AgCl shares the common chloride ion (Cl-) with NaCl, which heavily suppresses its solubility due to the common ion effect.

Question 5 of 18

The units of solubility product for Al(OH)3 are:

Explanation: Al(OH)3 → Al3+ + 3 OH-. Ksp = [Al3+][OH-]3, so units are (mol L-1)(mol L-1)3 = mol4 L-4.

Question 6 of 18

Addition of HCl to a saturated solution of ZnS will:

Explanation: H+ combines with S2- to form weak acid H2S, removing sulfide ions and shifting equilibrium right, increasing ZnS solubility.

Question 7 of 18

What is the solubility of AgCl in terms of its Ksp?

Explanation: For AgCl (1:1 salt), Ksp = s2, so s = (Ksp)1/2.

Question 8 of 18

Which factor affects the value of the solubility product (Ksp)?

Explanation: Equilibrium constants like Ksp are dependent only on temperature.

Question 9 of 18

If Qsp = Ksp, the solution is:

Explanation: When Qsp = Ksp, the solution is saturated and dynamic equilibrium exists.

Question 10 of 18

Solubility of which of the following salts is independent of pH?

Explanation: BaSO4 is the salt of a strong acid (H2SO4) and strong base (Ba(OH)2); its ions do not hydrolyze significantly with pH changes.

Question 11 of 18

In qualitative analysis, group II cations are precipitated as sulfides using H2S in the presence of dilute HCl to:

Explanation: HCl provides H+ ions which suppress the dissociation of H2S via common ion effect, keeping [S2-] low enough to precipitate only group II sulfides.

Question 12 of 18

The solubility of calcium oxalate in acidic buffer is:

Explanation: Oxalate ion (C2O42-) is a weak acid conjugate, so adding acid protonates it into HCGS/oxalic acid, driving dissolution forward.

Question 13 of 18

If Ksp of Mg(OH)2 is 1.0 × 10-11, what is the pH of a saturated solution of Mg(OH)2?

Explanation: Ksp = 4s3 = 1.0 × 10-11 ⇒ s = 1.35 × 10-4 M ⇒ [OH-] = 2.7 × 10-4 M ⇒ pOH = 3.57 ⇒ pH ≈ 10.4 (close to 10.3).

Question 14 of 18

Which of the following expressions correctly represents Ksp for bismuth sulfide (Bi2S3)?

Explanation: Bi2S3 → 2 Bi3+ + 3 S2-, giving Ksp = [Bi3+]2[S2-]3.

Question 15 of 18

Addition of excess ammonia to silver chloride precipitate results in:

Explanation: Ag+ forms a stable complex [Ag(NH3)2]+, reducing free Ag+ concentration and dissolving solid AgCl.

Question 16 of 18

Solubility product expression is applicable to:

Explanation: Ksp applies specifically to saturated solutions of sparingly soluble electrolytes in equilibrium with their undissolved solids.

Question 17 of 18

If 10 mL of 10-4 M CaCl2 is mixed with 10 mL of 10-4 M Na2SO4, will CaSO4 precipitate? (Given Ksp of CaSO4 = 2.4 × 10-5)

Explanation: After mixing, [Ca2+] = 5 × 10-5 M and [SO42-] = 5 × 10-5 M. Qsp = 2.5 × 10-9, which is much less than Ksp (2.4 × 10-5). No precipitation.

Question 18 of 18

The solubility of a gas in liquid generally decreases with:

Explanation: Gas dissolution is an exothermic process; increasing temperature decreases gas solubility according to Le Chatelier's principle.

Question 1 of 10
The solubility of AgCl in water is 1.0 × 10-5 mol L-1. Its solubility in 0.01 M CaCl2 solution will be:

Solution: Ksp of AgCl = (1.0 × 10-5)2 = 1.0 × 10-10. In 0.01 M CaCl2, [Cl-] = 2(0.01) = 0.02 M. s' = Ksp / [Cl-] = 1.0 × 10-10 / 0.02 = 5.0 × 10-8 M.

Question 2 of 10
Which metal sulfide is soluble in dilute hydrochloric acid?

Solution: ZnS has a relatively high Ksp compared to CuS or HgS, allowing it to dissolve in dilute HCl.

Question 3 of 10
What is the concentration of Ag+ ions in a saturated solution of AgCl if Ksp = 1.6 × 10-10?

Solution: [Ag+] = √Ksp = √(1.6 × 10-10) = 1.26 × 10-5 M.

Question 4 of 10
If the Ksp of ferric hydroxide Fe(OH)3 is 3.8 × 10-38, what is the expression for its solubility s?

Solution: Fe(OH)3 → Fe3+ + 3OH-. Ksp = (s)(3s)3 = 27s4 ⇒ s = (Ksp / 27)1/4.

Question 5 of 10
Solubility of carbon dioxide gas in water increases with:

Solution: According to Henry's Law, gas solubility is directly proportional to partial pressure above the solution.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following pairs shows correct relation between Ksp and s for a 2:3 electrolyte?

Solution: For A2B3, Ksp = (2s)2(3s)3 = 4s2 × 27s3 = 108s5.

Question 7 of 10
Why does NaCl precipitate when HCl gas is passed through a saturated aqueous solution of NaCl?

Solution: Passing HCl introduces high concentration of Cl- ions, exceeding Ksp of NaCl and causing precipitation.

Question 8 of 10
Buffer solutions are used in group separation of cations in qualitative analysis to:

Solution: Buffers control specific pH ranges to regulate the concentration of counter-ions (like S2- or OH-) for selective precipitation.

Question 9 of 10
The solubility product of a salt AB2 is 3.2 × 10-11. Its solubility is:

Solution: Ksp = 4s3 = 3.2 × 10-11 ⇒ s3 = 8 × 10-12 ⇒ s = 2 × 10-4 mol L-1.

Question 10 of 10
Which compound has its solubility decreased by the addition of dilute HNO3?

Solution: Adding HNO3 increases solubility of salts of weak acids (like Ag2CO3) by protonating the anion. For BaSO4 and AgCl, acid has negligible effect. Thus, none have solubility decreased.
