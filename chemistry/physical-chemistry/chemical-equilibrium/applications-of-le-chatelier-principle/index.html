Physical Chemistry

Applications of Le Chatelier Principle

Le Chatelier's principle states that if a dynamic equilibrium is disturbed by changing the conditions, the position of equilibrium shifts to counteract the change. It quantitatively governs the response of chemical systems to alterations in concentration, pressure, temperature, and inert gases.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Effect of Concentration and Pressure Changes

  • Increasing the concentration of reactants shifts the equilibrium in the forward direction to consume the added reactant.
  • Increasing the total pressure on a gaseous equilibrium shifts the system toward the side with fewer moles of gas (Δng < 0).
  • Addition of an inert gas at constant volume has no effect on the equilibrium position because individual partial pressures remain unchanged.
  • Addition of an inert gas at constant pressure increases the total volume, shifting equilibrium toward the side with a larger number of moles of gas.

Effect of Temperature and Catalysts

  • Raising the temperature shifts an equilibrium in the endothermic direction, absorbing the excess thermal energy (ΔH > 0).
  • Lowering the temperature favors the exothermic direction, releasing thermal energy to compensate for the drop (ΔH < 0).
  • A catalyst increases both forward and backward reaction rates equally, leaving the equilibrium position and K unchanged.
  • Catalysts only accelerate the rate at which equilibrium is attained in a reversible reaction.

Industrial Applications in Synthesis

  • In the Haber process for ammonia (N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3, ΔH = -92.4 kJ mol-1), high pressure and low temperature favor high yield.
  • In the Contact process for sulfur trioxide (2SO2 + O2 ⇌ 2SO3, ΔH = -197 kJ mol-1), excess oxygen and high pressure drive conversion forward.
  • The dissociation of water is endothermic, meaning autoionization constant Kw increases at elevated temperatures.
  • Solubility of gases in liquids decreases with rising temperature due to exothermic dissolution behavior.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Reaction Quotient Expression
Qc = [C]c[D]d / [A]a[B]b
Conditions: Any point in time for a homogeneous system
Units: Concentrations in mol L-1
Equilibrium Constant Relation with Pressure
Kp = Kc(RT)Δng
Conditions: Ideal gas behavior
Units: Kp in (atm)Δng, Kc in (mol L-1)Δng
Change in Free Energy at Equilibrium
ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q
Conditions: General non-standard conditions
Units: ΔG in J mol-1, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin
Van 't Hoff Equation
ln(K2 / K1) = (ΔH° / R) × [(T2 - T1) / (T1T2)]
Conditions: Standard conditions, constant ΔH°
Units: K is dimensionless, ΔH° in J mol-1, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Increasing pressure shifts equilibrium to fewer moles of gas.

Observation: Reactions with Δng = 0 — When total stoichiometric moles of gaseous reactants equal gaseous products, pressure changes exert no net shift on equilibrium.

Exception Solid-liquid equilibrium for ice-water system under pressure.

Observation: Melting point of ice decreases with increasing pressure. — Ice has a larger volume than liquid water due to open cage-like hydrogen-bonded structures, making the forward process volume-decreasing.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Temperature Shift Mnemonic
Endo = Heat as Reactant, Exo = Heat as Product

Application: Treat heat like a chemical species. Adding heat shifts away from the side containing 'heat'.

Pressure Shift Rule
High P → Less Moles

Application: Instantly identify the side of the reaction equation with fewer gaseous moles when pressure is increased.

Van 't Hoff Sign Check
T ↑ → K ↑ (ΔH > 0)

Application: If temperature and equilibrium constant move in the same direction, the reaction is strictly endothermic.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Le Chatelier's principle applies exclusively to systems at dynamic chemical equilibrium.
  • The value of the equilibrium constant K depends solely on temperature.
  • Addition of an inert gas at constant volume does not alter partial pressures or equilibrium concentrations.
  • For exothermic reactions, high temperature decreases the equilibrium constant.
  • In the synthesis of ammonia, optimum industrial yield requires moderate temperature coupled with high pressure (200-300 atm).
  • Solubility of calcium acetate decreases with increasing temperature because its dissolution is exothermic.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming catalysts change the equilibrium yield; catalysts only alter the rate to reach equilibrium.
  • Confusing inert gas addition at constant volume with addition at constant pressure.
  • Applying Le Chatelier's principle to systems that are not at equilibrium.
  • Assuming pressure changes affect condensed phases (pure liquids and solids) significantly.

⚡ Quick Revision: Applications of Le Chatelier Principle

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Qualitative Perturbation Response

Systems respond to stress by shifting equilibrium to minimize the applied disturbance.

Temperature and K

Temperature changes alter the magnitude of the equilibrium constant via the Van 't Hoff relation.

Inert Gas Effects

Constant volume inert gas additions have zero effect; constant volume reduction increases total pressure shifting to more moles.

Industrial Optimization

Compromise conditions of temperature and pressure are selected balancing kinetic rate and thermodynamic yield.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Le Chatelier Principle (Equilibrium Shift)
Qualitative rule predicting shift in equilibrium upon environmental disturbance.
Reaction Quotient (Q)
Ratio of product concentrations to reactant concentrations at any instant.
Equilibrium Constant (K)
Constant ratio of activities at chemical equilibrium.
Van 't Hoff Equation (d(ln K)/dT)
Quantitative expression of temperature dependence of K.
Inert Gas (Non-reacting Gas)
Gas that does not participate in the chemical equilibrium reaction.
Endothermic (ΔH > 0)
Process absorbing heat from the surroundings.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • K changes only with temperature.
  • Catalysts do not shift equilibrium positions.
  • Constant volume inert gas additions have no effect.
  • High pressure favors fewer moles of gas.
  • Endothermic reactions have increased K at higher temperatures.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Concentration adjustments consume added species.
  • Pressure shifts favor lower gas stoichiometry.
  • Temperature shifts depend on sign of enthalpy change.
  • Equilibrium constant remains invariant to concentration and pressure.

⚖️ Key Differences

Q < K VS Q > K

Q < K favors forward reaction; Q > K favors reverse reaction.

Explanation: Increasing pressure shifts equilibrium toward the side with fewer moles of gas (2 moles of NH3 vs 4 moles of reactants).

Question 2 of 19

Which factor changes the value of the equilibrium constant K?

Explanation: Only temperature changes the thermodynamic equilibrium constant K.

Question 3 of 19

Addition of an inert gas at constant volume to a gaseous equilibrium results in:

Explanation: At constant volume, partial pressures of reacting gases remain unchanged, causing no shift.

Question 4 of 19

For an endothermic reaction, raising the temperature causes:

Explanation: Endothermic reactions absorb heat; raising temperature favors the forward reaction, increasing K.

Question 5 of 19

What is the effect of a catalyst on a reversible reaction at equilibrium?

Explanation: Catalysts increase forward and backward rates equally, leaving the equilibrium position unaltered.

Question 6 of 19

In the reaction PCl5(g) ⇌ PCl3(g) + Cl2(g), addition of Cl2 gas will:

Explanation: Adding product Cl2 shifts equilibrium in the reverse direction to consume the excess Cl2.

Question 7 of 19

The melting of ice is accompanied by absorption of heat and decrease in volume. High pressure will:

Explanation: According to Le Chatelier's principle, increased pressure favors the volume-decreasing process (melting of ice).

Question 8 of 19

If Qc > Kc for a reaction, the net reaction will proceed in:

Explanation: When Qc > Kc, there is an excess of products, so the system proceeds in the reverse direction.

Question 9 of 19

Which of the following equilibria is not affected by pressure change?

Explanation: H2 + I2 ⇌ 2HI has Δng = 0, so pressure changes have no effect.

Question 10 of 19

The solubility of a gas in liquid generally decreases with:

Explanation: Dissolution of gases in liquids is typically exothermic, so increasing temperature decreases gas solubility.

Question 11 of 19

For the exothermic reaction 2SO2 + O2 ⇌ 2SO3, maximum yield of SO3 is obtained at:

Explanation: Exothermic reactions are favored by low temperatures, and fewer gas moles are favored by high pressures.

Question 12 of 19

Addition of argon at constant pressure to the dissociation of PCl5 will:

Explanation: Constant pressure addition of inert gas increases volume, shifting equilibrium toward more moles of gas (forward).

Question 13 of 19

According to Van 't Hoff equation, if ΔH is positive, ln K increases when:

Explanation: For endothermic reactions (ΔH > 0), increasing temperature raises ln K.

Question 14 of 19

In the dissociation of calcium carbonate (CaCO3(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO2(g)), the equilibrium pressure of CO2 depends on:

Explanation: Equilibrium vapor pressure of CO2 depends solely on temperature, independent of solid amounts.

Question 15 of 19

When sodium chloride is added to a saturated solution of NaCl, precipitation occurs due to:

Explanation: The common ion effect is a direct application of Le Chatelier's principle involving concentration shifts.

Question 16 of 19

Which reaction experiences a shift to the left when volume is decreased?

Explanation: Decreasing volume increases pressure, shifting PCl5 dissociation backward toward fewer moles.

Question 17 of 19

What is the sign of ΔH for a reaction whose equilibrium constant decreases with increasing temperature?

Explanation: Inverse relation between temperature and K indicates an exothermic reaction (ΔH < 0).

Question 18 of 19

For the system Fe3+(aq) + SCN-(aq) ⇌ [Fe(SCN)]2+(aq), adding oxalic acid binds Fe3+, causing:

Explanation: Removing Fe3+ shifts equilibrium backward, reducing red complex concentration.

Question 19 of 19

Le Chatelier's principle is applicable to:

Explanation: The principle applies universally to any system in dynamic equilibrium.

When NaHCO3 is heated in a closed vessel, equilibrium is established. The effect of adding more NaHCO3 is:

Solution: Adding pure solids does not change their active mass, hence no effect on gas pressure.

Question 3 of 10
If the equilibrium constant for a reaction is 105, the system at equilibrium contains:

Solution: A very large K (> 103) indicates that products predominate at equilibrium.

Question 4 of 10
What is the effect of increasing pressure on the boiling point of a liquid?

Solution: Vaporization is endothermic and increases volume; higher pressure opposes vaporization, raising boiling point.

Question 5 of 10
For the gas-phase reaction A + B ⇌ C + D, ΔH = -40 kJ, lowering the temperature will:

Solution: Lowering temperature for an exothermic reaction shifts the equilibrium forward.

Question 6 of 10
In the reaction CO(g) + 2H2(g) ⇌ CH3OH(g), high yield of methanol is favored by:

Solution: Synthesis is exothermic (Δng = -2), requiring low temperature and high pressure.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following perturbations will alter the value of K?

Solution: Temperature is the only factor that alters the magnitude of K.

Question 8 of 10
If Q < K, the Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) for the reaction is:

Solution: When Q < K, the reaction is spontaneous in the forward direction, meaning ΔG < 0.

Question 9 of 10

Solution: Chloride ions from HCl suppress NaCl solubility via the common ion effect.
