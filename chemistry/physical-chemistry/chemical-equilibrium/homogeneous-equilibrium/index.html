Homogeneous Equilibrium

Homogeneous equilibrium describes reversible chemical systems where all reactants and products exist in a single, uniform phase, predominantly gas or liquid solution. The thermodynamic state of the system is governed by the Law of Mass Action and quantified via equilibrium constants dependent exclusively on temperature.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Formulation of Equilibrium Constants

  • The Law of Mass Action states that the rate of a chemical reaction is proportional to the active masses of reactants.
  • For a general gas-phase equilibrium aA + bB → cC + dD, the equilibrium constant expression omits pure solids and liquids if heterogeneous, but homogeneous systems include all species.
  • The active mass is expressed as molar concentration [X] in mol L-1 for solutions or partial pressure PX for gases.
  • The value of the equilibrium constant is dimensionless when calculated using standard state activities.

Characteristics of Homogeneous Gaseous Systems

  • Change in moles of gas (Δng) dictates whether total pressure affects the equilibrium position according to Le Chatelier's Principle.
  • When Δng = 0, changes in pressure or volume have no effect on the equilibrium composition (e.g., H2(g) + I2(g) ⇄ 2HI(g)).
  • When Δng > 0, an increase in volume or decrease in pressure shifts equilibrium toward the product side.
  • Inert gas addition at constant volume has no effect on the equilibrium position because partial pressures of reactants and products remain unaltered.

Thermodynamic Control of Equilibrium

  • The reaction quotient Q determines the direction of net reaction by comparison with K; if Q < K, the forward reaction is favored.
  • A positive standard enthalpy change (ΔH° > 0) for an endothermic reaction causes K to increase with rising temperature.
  • Catalysts alter the forward and reverse reaction rates equally by lowering activation energy, thus leaving the equilibrium constant unchanged.
  • The standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) must be negative for a spontaneous equilibrium process favoring products at standard state.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Pressure Equilibrium Constant (Kp)
Kp = (PCc PDd) / (PAa PBb)
Conditions: Applicable specifically to homogeneous gaseous equilibria using partial pressures
Units: Pressure units (atm)Δn or (bar)Δn
Relation Between Kp and Kc
Kp = Kc(RT)Δn
Conditions: Ideal gas behavior assumed for all gaseous components
Units: Δn = (c + d) - (a + b), R = 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1
Van 't Hoff Equation for Temperature Dependence
ln(K2 / K1) = (ΔH° / R) × [(T2 - T1) / (T1T2)]
Conditions: Standard enthalpy of reaction (ΔH°) assumed constant over the temperature range
Units: ΔH° in J mol-1, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin
Standard Gibbs Free Energy and Equilibrium Constant
ΔG° = -RT ln K
Conditions: System at constant temperature and pressure
Units: ΔG° in J mol-1, R in J K-1 mol-1, K is dimensionless activity quotient
Law of Mass Action Equilibrium Constant (Kc)
Kc = ([C]c[D]d) / ([A]a[B]b)
Conditions: Applicable to homogeneous systems at constant temperature in terms of molar concentrations
Units: Molar concentration units (mol L-1)Δn
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Equilibrium constant depends only on temperature and is independent of initial concentrations.

Observation: Apparent equilibrium shifts in open systems — Open systems allow escape of gaseous products, preventing the establishment of true closed homogeneous equilibrium.

Exception Addition of an inert gas at constant pressure shifts equilibrium toward the side with more moles of gas.

Observation: Inert gas addition at constant volume — Constant volume addition increases total pressure without altering individual molar concentrations or partial pressures, leaving equilibrium undisturbed.

Delta n G Shortcut
Δng = (Moles of gaseous products) - (Moles of gaseous reactants)

Application: Quickly determine unit of Kp/Kc or predict pressure dependence without writing full derivations.

Temperature-K Direction Mnemonic
Endo = Hotter favors products (K increases); Exo = Cooler favors products (K increases)

Application: Immediately answer qualitative Le Chatelier temperature effects on K.

Q vs K Comparison Rule
Alphabetical order: Q → K (Left to Right means Q < K goes Forward)

Application: Remember that if Q < K, the reaction proceeds forward to reach equilibrium.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The equilibrium constant K is independent of initial concentrations, catalyst presence, and presence of inert gases at constant volume.
  • For the dissociation of HI, 2HI ⇄ H2 + I2, the value of Kp equals Kc because Δng = 0.
  • Units of Kc for N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇄ 2NH3(g) are L2 mol-2.
  • A catalyst accelerates both forward and reverse reactions to the same extent, reducing the time required to attain equilibrium.
  • The standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) is zero when the equilibrium constant K equals 1.
  • Temperature is the only factor that alters the numerical value of the equilibrium constant K.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Including pure solids or liquids in the homogeneous equilibrium constant expression.
  • Confusing the reaction quotient Q with the equilibrium constant K when calculating non-equilibrium states.
  • Failing to account for stoichiometry coefficients as powers in the equilibrium expression.
  • Assuming inert gas addition always shifts equilibrium regardless of whether volume or pressure is kept constant.

⚡ Quick Revision: Homogeneous Equilibrium

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Law of Mass Action

The rate of a chemical reaction at a constant temperature is proportional to the product of the active masses of reactants raised to powers equal to their stoichiometric coefficients.

Equilibrium Constant Relations

Kp and Kc are mathematically linked via the ideal gas law relationship Kp = Kc(RT)Δn.

Le Chatelier's Principle

A system at equilibrium subjected to a stress responds in a manner that counteracts or neutralizes the imposed stress to re-establish equilibrium.

Thermodynamics of Equilibrium

The standard Gibbs free energy change dictates spontaneity via ΔG° = -RT ln K, coupling chemical affinity directly with equilibrium position.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Equilibrium Constant (K)
Ratio of product activities to reactant activities at equilibrium at constant temperature.
Reaction Quotient (Q)
Ratio of product activities to reactant activities at any arbitrary point in time.
Active Mass (Molar Concentration / Partial Pressure)
Effective concentration of a species participating in a chemical reaction.
Van 't Hoff Factor (i)
Ratio of actual moles of particles in solution to formula moles.
Standard Enthalpy (ΔH°)
Heat change of a reaction at 1 bar pressure and specified temperature in Joules.
Delta n g (Δng)
Difference between total moles of gaseous products and gaseous reactants.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Kp = Kc(RT)Δn
  • ΔG° = -RT ln K
  • If Q < K, reaction proceeds in forward direction
  • Catalyst changes rate, not position of equilibrium
  • Temperature changes K; concentration does not change K

📌 Quick Recall

  • Homogeneous equilibria involve reactants and products in a single phase.
  • Pressure changes affect gaseous homogeneous equilibria only when Δng ≠ 0.
  • Endothermic reactions have positive ΔH° and K increases with temperature.
  • Exothermic reactions have negative ΔH° and K decreases with temperature.

⚖️ Key Differences

Equilibrium Constant (K) VS Reaction Quotient (Q)

K is calculated using equilibrium concentrations only; Q can be calculated at any point during the reaction.

Question 1 of 18

For which of the following reactions is Kp equal to Kc?

Explanation: Kp = Kc(RT)Δn. For H2(g) + Cl2(g) ⇄ 2HCl(g), Δn = 2 - 2 = 0, making Kp = Kc.

Question 2 of 18

What is the unit of Kc for the reaction N2O4(g) ⇄ 2NO2(g)?

Explanation: Kc = [NO2]2 / [N2O4] = (mol L-1)2 / (mol L-1) = mol L-1.

Question 3 of 18

If the equilibrium constant for a reaction is 105, what can be inferred about the reaction?

Explanation: A very large equilibrium constant (K >> 1) indicates that the forward reaction proceeds nearly to completion, favoring products.

Question 4 of 18

What happens to the value of the equilibrium constant when a catalyst is added to a homogeneous gaseous reaction?

Explanation: A catalyst lowers the activation energy of both forward and reverse reactions equally, affecting the rate of attainment of equilibrium but not the equilibrium constant itself.

Question 5 of 18

In an exothermic homogeneous gaseous reaction, an increase in temperature will:

Explanation: According to the Van 't Hoff equation, for an exothermic reaction (ΔH° < 0), increasing temperature causes the equilibrium constant K to decrease.

Question 6 of 18

What is the relationship between standard free energy change (ΔG°) and equilibrium constant (K) at constant temperature?

Explanation: The fundamental thermodynamic relation is ΔG° = -RT ln K.

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following conditions favors maximum yield of ammonia in the Haber process: N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇄ 2NH3(g) (ΔH = -92.4 kJ)?

Explanation: The forward reaction is exothermic, so low temperature favors products. Δn = 2 - 4 = -2, so high pressure shifts equilibrium toward products.

Question 8 of 18

For a reversible reaction, if the reaction quotient Q exceeds the equilibrium constant K, the reaction will proceed in:

Explanation: When Q > K, the system has an excess of products compared to equilibrium, so the backward reaction is favored to consume products until Q = K.

Question 9 of 18

What is the effect of adding an inert gas at constant pressure to the equilibrium system PCl5(g) ⇄ PCl3(g) + Cl2(g)?

Explanation: Addition of inert gas at constant pressure increases total volume, shifting equilibrium toward the side with more moles of gas (Δn = +1), which is the forward direction.

Question 10 of 18

The equilibrium constant for the reaction A + B ⇄ C + D is K. What is the equilibrium constant for the reverse reaction C + D ⇄ A + B?

Explanation: Reversing a chemical equation inverts its equilibrium constant expression, making the new constant 1 / K.

Question 11 of 18

If the stoichiometric coefficients of a chemical equation are multiplied by 2, how does the new equilibrium constant (K') relate to the original (K)?

Explanation: Multiplying the equation by 2 raises the concentration terms in the equilibrium expression to the power of 2, so K' = K2.

Question 12 of 18

Which of the following is a homogeneous equilibrium?

Explanation: In 2CO(g) + O2(g) ⇄ 2CO2(g), all reactants and products are gases, constituting a single gaseous phase.

Question 13 of 18

In the dissociation of N2O4 to NO2, if α is the degree of dissociation, the total number of moles at equilibrium starting with 1 mole of N2O4 is:

Explanation: N2O4 ⇄ 2NO2. Initial = 1, 0. At eq = 1 - α, 2α. Total moles = 1 - α + 2α = 1 + α.

Question 14 of 18

What is the unit of Kp for the reaction N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇄ 2NH3(g)?

Explanation: Δn = 2 - 4 = -2. The unit of Kp is (atm)-2.

Question 15 of 18

Le Chatelier's Principle is applicable to:

Explanation: Le Chatelier's Principle predicts how a system already at dynamic equilibrium responds to external stress.

Question 16 of 18

If ΔG° for a reaction is positive, the value of K is:

Explanation: Since ΔG° = -RT ln K, a positive ΔG° requires ln K to be negative, meaning K < 1.

Question 17 of 18

Which factor will shift the equilibrium N2(g) + O2(g) ⇄ 2NO(g) (ΔH = +180 kJ) to the right?

Explanation: The reaction is endothermic (ΔH > 0). Increasing temperature shifts equilibrium toward the products.

Question 18 of 18

The equilibrium constant expression for a homogeneous gas reaction depends on:

Explanation: The equilibrium constant expression is derived directly from the stoichiometric coefficients of the balanced chemical equation.

Question 1 of 10
For the gas-phase reaction A(g) + 2B(g) ⇄ C(g), the expression for Kp in terms of partial pressures is:

Solution: Products are in the numerator raised to stoichiometric coefficients, and reactants are in the denominator.

Question 2 of 10
If Δng = 0 for a homogeneous gaseous reaction, the relation between Kp and Kc is:

Solution: When Δng = 0, (RT)0 = 1, so Kp = Kc.

Question 3 of 10
What is the effect of increasing pressure on the equilibrium: N2O4(g) ⇄ 2NO2(g)?

Solution: Δn = 2 - 1 = +1. Increasing pressure shifts equilibrium toward the side with fewer moles of gas, which is the backward direction.

Question 4 of 10
The standard free energy change ΔG° for a reaction with K = 1 is:

Solution: ΔG° = -RT ln(1) = 0 since ln(1) = 0.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following is true when a chemical system has reached homogeneous equilibrium?

Solution: Equilibrium is dynamic; forward and reverse reaction rates become equal while concentrations remain constant.

Question 6 of 10
For an endothermic homogeneous reaction, how does an increase in temperature affect the equilibrium constant K?

Solution: Endothermic reactions absorb heat (ΔH > 0); raising temperature increases the equilibrium constant K.

Question 7 of 10
What are the units of Kp for the reaction 2NO2(g) ⇄ 2NO(g) + O2(g)?

Solution: Δn = (2 + 1) - 2 = +1. The unit of Kp is atm.

Question 8 of 10
In the synthesis of hydrogen iodide from hydrogen and iodine, doubling the volume of the reaction vessel will:

Solution: For H2 + I2 ⇄ 2HI, Δn = 0, so volume changes do not shift equilibrium.

Question 9 of 10
Which parameter determines whether a reaction mixture is at equilibrium?

Solution: Comparing Q with K determines equilibrium status (Q = K means equilibrium).

Question 10 of 10
The Van 't Hoff equation relates which two quantities?

Solution: The Van 't Hoff equation quantifies the temperature dependence of the equilibrium constant K using enthalpy.
