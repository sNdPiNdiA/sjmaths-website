Physical Chemistry

Relationship between Kp and Kc

Chemical equilibrium constants can be expressed in terms of partial pressures (Kp) or molar concentrations (Kc). The mathematical relationship between them is derived using the ideal gas law equation.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Derivation of Kp and Kc Interconversion

  • The equilibrium constant Kc uses molar concentration terms [X] while Kp uses partial pressure terms PX.
  • Using the ideal gas equation PXV = nXRT, partial pressure is expressed as PX = (nX/V)RT = CXRT.
  • Substituting concentration expressions into the pressure equilibrium constant expression yields Kp = Kc(RT)Δng.

Significance of Delta ng Values

  • When Δng = 0 (e.g., H2(g) + I2(g) ↔ 2HI(g)), the relation simplifies to Kp = Kc.
  • When Δng > 0 (e.g., PCl5(g) ↔ PCl3(g) + Cl2(g)), the value of Kp is greater than Kc.
  • When Δng < 0 (e.g., N2(g) + 3H2(g) ↔ 2NH3(g)), the value of Kp is less than Kc.

Units and Dimensional Analysis

  • The unit of Kc is (mol L-1)Δng, whereas the unit of Kp is (atm)Δng or (bar)Δng.
  • The gas constant R must be chosen in appropriate units, typically 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1 when pressure is in atmospheres.
  • When Δng = 0, both Kp and Kc are completely dimensionless quantities.

General Relationship Equation
Kp = Kc(RT)Δng
Conditions: Ideal gas behavior for all gaseous reactants and products
Units: Kp in (atm)Δng or (bar)Δng; Kc in (mol L-1)Δng
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Gaseous moles are counted for all species in the reaction container

Observation: Pure solids and pure liquids are excluded — Their active masses or concentrations remain constant and are incorporated into the equilibrium constant expression

Exception Kp and Kc depend directly on the stoichiometric coefficients written in the equation

Observation: Multiplying a chemical equation by a factor n scales the equilibrium constant to the power of n — Equilibrium constants are proportional to the stoichiometric coefficients raised as powers in the law of mass action

Exception Ideal gas law is assumed for all gaseous mixtures at equilibrium

Observation: Real gases at very high pressures or very low temperatures deviate significantly — Intermolecular forces and molecular volumes alter the direct proportionality between pressure and molar concentration

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • For the reaction N2O4(g) ↔ 2NO2(g), Δng = 1, hence Kp = KcRT.
  • For the synthesis of ammonia (N2 + 3H2 ↔ 2NH3), Δng = -2, making Kc > Kp.
  • Units of Kp/Kc depend entirely on the change in stoichiometric gaseous moles (Δng).
  • Temperature is the only factor that alters the numerical value of equilibrium constants Kp and Kc.
  • Pure liquids and solids like CaCO3(s) or H2O(l) do not appear in Kp or Kc expressions.
  • The value of R used in Kp = Kc(RT)Δng with pressure in atm is 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Including pure solid or liquid reactants/products in the calculation of Δng.
  • Failing to convert Celsius temperature to Kelvin when substituting into the RT term.
  • Assuming Kp and Kc have identical numerical values regardless of the balanced chemical equation.
  • Incorrectly reversing the sign of Δng when converting Kp to Kc instead of Kc to Kp.

⚡ Quick Revision: Relationship between Kp and Kc

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Law of Mass Action

At constant temperature, the rate of a chemical reaction is proportional to the active masses of reactants raised to their stoichiometric coefficients.

Expression for Kc

Kc is the ratio of product molar concentrations to reactant molar concentrations at equilibrium, each raised to its stoichiometric power.

Expression for Kp

Kp replaces molar concentrations with partial pressures for all participating gaseous species in the equilibrium mixture.

Interconversion Bridge

The equation Kp = Kc(RT)Δng connects pressure-based and concentration-based equilibrium constants using ideal gas behavior.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Equilibrium Constant (Kc) (Kc)
Ratio of product concentrations to reactant concentrations at equilibrium in mol L-1.
Pressure Equilibrium Constant (Kp) (Kp)
Ratio of product partial pressures to reactant partial pressures in atm or bar.
Change in Gaseous Moles (Δng) (Δng)
Difference between total moles of gaseous products and gaseous reactants.
Universal Gas Constant (R) (R)
Gas constant equal to 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1.
Absolute Temperature (T) (T)
Temperature measured in Kelvin scale.
Mole Fraction (x) (xi)
Ratio of moles of component i to total moles in the mixture.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Kp = Kc(RT)Δng
  • When Δng = 0, Kp = Kc
  • When Δng > 0, Kp > Kc
  • When Δng < 0, Kp < Kc
  • Solids and liquids have unit activity and are excluded from Δng calculation

📌 Quick Recall

  • Kp and Kc are linked through ideal gas laws.
  • Temperature changes alter K values according to van 't Hoff equation.
  • Units of Kp and Kc depend on Δng.
  • Catalysts do not alter the values of Kp or Kc.

Kp VS Kc

Kp uses partial pressure terms while Kc uses molar concentration terms.

Question 1 of 18

For which of the following reactions is Kp equal to Kc?

Explanation: For H2(g) + Cl2(g) ↔ 2HCl(g), Δng = 2 - 2 = 0. Therefore, Kp = Kc(RT)0 = Kc.

Question 2 of 18

What is the unit of Kp for the reaction N2(g) + 3H2(g) ↔ 2NH3(g)?

Explanation: For this reaction Δng = 2 - 4 = -2. The unit of Kp is (atm)-2 or atm-2.

Question 3 of 18

In the reaction PCl5(g) ↔ PCl3(g) + Cl2(g), how are Kp and Kc related?

Explanation: Here Δng = 2 - 1 = +1. Since Δng is positive, Kp = KcRT, meaning Kp > Kc.

Question 4 of 18

What is the value of Δng for the dissociation of calcium carbonate: CaCO3(s) ↔ CaO(s) + CO2(g)?

Explanation: Only gaseous moles are counted. Products have 1 mole of CO2, reactants have 0 moles of gas. Thus Δng = 1 - 0 = 1.

Question 5 of 18

If Kc for a reaction is 4.0 × 10-2 at 300 K with Δng = -1, what is Kp? (R = 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1)

Explanation: Kp = Kc(RT)-1 = (4.0 × 10-2) / (0.0821 × 300) = (0.04) / (24.63) ≈ 1.62 × 10-3 atm.

Question 6 of 18

Which factor affects the numerical value of both Kp and Kc?

Explanation: Equilibrium constants Kp and Kc are strictly temperature-dependent.

Question 7 of 18

For which reaction is Kp less than Kc?

Explanation: For 2NO2(g) ↔ N2O4(g), Δng = 1 - 2 = -1. Therefore, Kp = Kc(RT)-1, meaning Kp < Kc.

Question 8 of 18

What are the units of Kc for the reaction N2O4(g) ↔ 2NO2(g)?

Explanation: For this reaction Δng = 2 - 1 = 1. The unit of Kc is (mol L-1)1 = mol L-1.

Question 9 of 18

If an equilibrium reaction is reversed, what happens to Kp?

Explanation: Reversing a chemical reaction inverts the equilibrium constant expression, making the new equilibrium constant equal to 1/Kp.

Question 10 of 18

What is the dimension of Kp when Δng = 0?

Explanation: When Δng = 0, any unit raised to the power zero equals 1, making both Kp and Kc dimensionless.

Question 11 of 18

In the relation Kp = Kc(RT)Δng, what does R represent?

Explanation: R is the universal gas constant used in ideal gas equations.

Question 12 of 18

Which of the following expressions relates Kp and Kx (equilibrium constant in mole fraction)?

Explanation: Since partial pressure Pi = xi × Ptotal, substituting gives Kp = Kx(Ptotal)Δng.

Question 13 of 18

If a reaction is multiplied by 2, the equilibrium constant Kc becomes:

Explanation: Multiplying stoichiometric coefficients by a factor n raises the equilibrium constant to the power n. Here n = 2, so Kc2.

Question 1 of 10
For the reaction CO(g) + 1/2O2(g) ↔ CO2(g), what is Δng?

Solution: Δng = 1 - (1 + 0.5) = 1 - 1.5 = -0.5.

Question 2 of 10
Which equation correctly represents Kc in terms of Kp?

Solution: Rearranging Kp = Kc(RT)Δng yields Kc = Kp(RT)-Δng.

Question 3 of 10
What is the unit of Kc when Δng = 2?

Solution: Unit of Kc is (mol L-1)Δng. For Δng = 2, it is (mol L-1)2 = mol2 L-2.

Question 4 of 10
For the thermal dissociation of PCl5 into PCl3 and Cl2, if temperature is increased, what happens to Kp?

Solution: Dissociation of PCl5 is endothermic; according to van 't Hoff equation, raising temperature increases the equilibrium constant Kp.

Question 5 of 10
If Kp for a reaction with Δng = 0 is 50 at 500 K, what is Kp at 600 K if the reaction is exothermic?

Solution: For an exothermic reaction, increasing the temperature decreases the equilibrium constant.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following is correct for the reaction NH4HS(s) ↔ NH3(g) + H2S(g)?

Solution: NH4HS is a solid, so its active mass is unity. Kp = PNH3 × PH2S and Δng = 2, so Kp = Kc(RT)2.

Question 7 of 10
What happens to the ratio Kp/Kc if temperature approaches absolute zero?

Solution: As T → 0, (RT)Δng approaches 0 for positive Δng.

Question 8 of 10
For a gaseous reaction, if Kp = Kc, the stoichiometric coefficients must satisfy:

Solution: Kp = Kc requires Δng = 0, which means total moles of gaseous products equal total moles of gaseous reactants.

Question 9 of 10
Which species is omitted when writing the Kc expression for the reaction Fe3O4(s) + 4H2(g) ↔ 3Fe(s) + 4H2O(g)?

Solution: Pure solid phases (Fe3O4 and Fe) have constant activity and are excluded from the equilibrium expression.

Question 10 of 10
If Δng = -2, the expression relating Kp and Kc is:

Solution: Substituting Δng = -2 into Kp = Kc(RT)Δng gives Kp = Kc(RT)-2.
