Physical Chemistry

Solubility product

Solubility product constant (Ksp) is the equilibrium constant for the dissolution of a solid ionic compound in an aqueous solution. It defines the maximum product of ion concentrations at a given temperature in a saturated solution.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Equilibrium in Sparingly Soluble Salts

  • A sparingly soluble salt establishes a dynamic equilibrium between the undissolved solid and its constituent ions in aqueous solution.
  • The concentration of the pure solid phase is treated as unity and incorporated into the solubility product constant (Ksp).
  • Ksp is strictly constant at a specified temperature and changes when the temperature is altered according to van 't Hoff equation.

Common Ion Effect on Solubility

  • The addition of a common ion from a strong electrolyte shifts the dissolution equilibrium in the backward direction.
  • The solubility (s) of a sparingly soluble salt decreases significantly in the presence of a common ion due to suppression of dissociation.
  • Fractional precipitation can be achieved by selectively controlling the concentration of precipitating ions using the common ion effect.

Applications in Qualitative Analysis

  • Group II cations (Cu2+, Cd2+, etc.) precipitate as sulfides in acidic medium because their Ksp values are extremely low.
  • Group III cations (Fe3+, Al3+, Cr3+) precipitate as hydroxides in alkaline buffer medium containing NH4OH and NH4Cl.
  • The addition of NH4Cl suppresses the high concentration of OH- ions from NH4OH, preventing premature precipitation of Group IV hydroxides.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Relationship between Solubility (s) and Ksp (Binary Electrolyte AB)
Ksp = s2
Conditions: 1:1 type sparingly soluble salt (e.g., AgCl)
Units: s in mol L-1, Ksp in mol2 L-2
Relationship between Solubility (s) and Ksp (Ternary Electrolyte AB2 or A2B)
Ksp = 4s3
Conditions: 1:2 or 2:1 type sparingly soluble salt (e.g., PbI2)
Units: s in mol L-1, Ksp in mol3 L-9
Relationship between Solubility (s) and Ksp (Electrolyte A3B2)
Ksp = 108s5
Conditions: 3:2 type sparingly soluble salt (e.g., Ca3(PO4)2)
Units: s in mol L-1, Ksp in mol5 L-5
Ionic Product (Qip) vs Ksp Criterion
Qip > Ksp (Precipitation occurs), Qip = Ksp (Saturated), Qip < Ksp (Unsaturated)
Conditions: Any aqueous ionic solution containing constituent ions
Units: Dimensionless comparison
General Solubility Product Expression
Ksp = [Ay+]x [Bx-]y
Conditions: Saturated solution of electrolyte AxBy at constant temperature
Units: Mx+y (dimensionless thermodynamic activity)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Addition of a common ion always decreases solubility of a sparingly soluble salt.

Observation: High concentrations of common ions can sometimes increase solubility. — Formation of soluble complex ions or changes in ionic strength altering activity coefficients.

Exception Salts of strong acids and weak bases have higher solubility in neutral water.

Observation: Certain salts show anomalous pH dependence due to hydrolysis. — Protonation of the weak acid anion removes ions from solution, driving further dissolution.

Qip vs Ksp

Common Ion Solubility Calculation
s' = Ksp / [Ccommon]y

Application: When a common ion is present in large excess (Ccommon >> s), neglect s in the salt contribution term to simplify calculations.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The unit of solubility product depends entirely on the stoichiometric coefficients of the ions in the dissociation equation.
  • For a 1:1 electrolyte like AgCl, solubility s = √Ksp.
  • For a 1:2 electrolyte like CaF2, solubility s = (Ksp / 4)1/3.
  • Precipitation occurs only when the ionic product exceeds the solubility product (Qip > Ksp).
  • Addition of NH4Cl to NH4OH reduces [OH-] concentration via the common ion effect.
  • H2S gas is passed in acidic medium to precipitate Group II basic radicals by maintaining a low [S2-] concentration.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Failing to account for volume changes when mixing two solutions before calculating the ionic product.
  • Using molarity directly instead of accounting for stoichiometric coefficients as powers in the Ksp expression.
  • Confusing solubility (mol L-1) with solubility product constant (dimensionless activity product).
  • Assuming Ksp depends on the amounts of undissolved solid present in the container.

⚡ Quick Revision: Solubility product

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Solubility Equilibrium

Heterogeneous equilibrium established between solid ionic solute and aqueous solvated ions.

Solubility Product Constant

Product of molar concentrations of constituent ions raised to their stoichiometric powers in a saturated solution.

Common Ion Effect

Suppression of ionic salt dissociation through the addition of an electrolyte sharing a common ion.

Precipitation Condition

Precipitation initiates precisely when the ionic product Qip exceeds the thermodynamic limit Ksp.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Solubility (s)
Maximum amount of solute dissolved per unit volume of saturated solution at a given temperature, mol L-1.
Solubility Product (Ksp)
Equilibrium constant governing the precipitation and dissolution of sparingly soluble salts.
Ionic Product (Qip)
Product of ion concentrations raised to stoichiometric powers at any arbitrary instant.
Common Ion Effect (Le Chatelier application)
Shift in equilibrium position caused by introducing an ion common to the equilibrium system.
Saturated Solution (Equilibrium state)
Solution containing maximum possible dissolved solute in dynamic equilibrium with undissolved solid.
Supersaturated Solution (Unstable state)
Solution containing higher solute concentration than the saturation limit, Qip > Ksp.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ksp = s2 for 1:1 salts
  • Ksp = 4s3 for 1:2 or 2:1 salts
  • Ksp = 108s5 for 3:2 salts
  • Condition for precipitation: Qip > Ksp
  • Solubility decreases in the presence of a common ion

📌 Quick Recall

  • Ksp is constant at constant temperature and independent of individual ion amounts.
  • Common ion effect reduces solubility by shifting equilibrium backward.
  • Qualitative inorganic analysis relies on selective precipitation via Ksp control.
  • Ionic product determines whether a solution is unsaturated, saturated, or supersaturated.

⚖️ Key Differences

Solubility (s) VS Solubility Product (Ksp)

Solubility is the concentration of dissolved solute in a saturated solution (mol L-1), whereas Ksp is the equilibrium constant composed of ion concentration products.

Question 1 of 18

What is the relationship between solubility (s) and solubility product (Ksp) for Ag2CrO4?

Explanation: Ag2CrO4 dissociates into 2Ag+ and CrO42-. Thus, Ksp = (2s)2(s) = 4s3.

Question 2 of 18

If the solubility of PbSO4 is 1.5 × 10-4 mol L-1, what is its Ksp?

Explanation: For a 1:1 salt, Ksp = s2 = (1.5 × 10-4)2 = 2.25 × 10-8.

Question 3 of 18

Precipitation of an ionic compound occurs when:

Explanation: Precipitation takes place only when the ionic product exceeds the solubility product.

Question 4 of 18

The addition of HCl to a saturated solution of NaCl decreases the solubility of NaCl due to:

Explanation: Cl- acts as a common ion, suppressing the dissociation of NaCl and causing precipitation.

Question 5 of 18

What is the unit of Ksp for Al(OH)3?

Explanation: Al(OH)3 → Al3+ + 3OH-. Ksp = [Al3+][OH-]3, giving units of (mol L-1)4 = mol4 L-4.

Question 6 of 18

Group II basic radicals are precipitated as sulfides by passing H2S in:

Explanation: Acidic medium maintains a very low [S2-] concentration, allowing only highly insoluble Group II sulfides to precipitate.

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following salts has the highest solubility if all have the same Ksp value of 10-10?

Explanation: For AB, s = √Ksp = 10-5, which is larger than the solubility calculated for higher stoichiometric types with the same Ksp.

Question 8 of 18

If the solubility product of CaSO4 is 9 × 10-6, its solubility in mol L-1 is:

Explanation: s = √Ksp = √(9 × 10-6) = 3 × 10-3 mol L-1.

Question 9 of 18

The solubility of a sparingly soluble salt in the presence of a common ion is:

Explanation: The common ion effect shifts the equilibrium to the left, decreasing solubility.

Question 10 of 18

What is the expression for Ksp of Bi2S3 in terms of solubility s?

Explanation: Bi2S3 → 2Bi3+ + 3S2-. Ksp = (2s)2(3s)3 = 4s2 × 27s3 = 108s5.

Question 11 of 18

Buffer solution of NH4Cl and NH4OH is used in group III precipitation to:

Explanation: NH4Cl suppresses [OH-] via the common ion effect so that only Group III hydroxides precipitate.

Question 12 of 18

If Qip < Ksp, the solution is:

Explanation: When the ionic product is less than Ksp, the solution is unsaturated and capable of dissolving more solute.

Question 13 of 18

Solubility product constant depends upon:

Explanation: Like all equilibrium constants, Ksp varies only with temperature.

Question 14 of 18

Which salt will have maximum solubility in 0.1 M NaCl solution?

Explanation: AgCl has the highest Ksp among common silver halides, yielding higher solubility at the same common ion concentration.

Question 15 of 18

The unit of solubility (s) is always:

Explanation: Solubility is standardly expressed in molarity (mol L-1).

Question 16 of 18

In qualitative analysis, Group IV cations are precipitated as sulfides in:

Explanation: Alkaline medium increases [S2-] concentration, enabling precipitation of more soluble Group IV sulfides like ZnS and CoS.

Question 17 of 18

What is the Ksp expression for Ag3PO4?

Explanation: Ag3PO4 → 3Ag+ + PO43-, giving Ksp = [Ag+]3[PO43-].

Question 18 of 18

If Ksp of Mg(OH)2 is 4 × 10-12, the concentration of OH- ions in a saturated solution is:

Explanation: Ksp = s(2s)2 = 4s3 = 4 × 10-12 ⇒ s = 1 × 10-4 M. [OH-] = 2s = 2 × 10-4 M.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following conditions represents a saturated solution without precipitation?

Solution: At saturation, the ionic product exactly equals the solubility product constant.

Question 3 of 10
If Ksp of CaF2 is 3.2 × 10-11, what is the solubility in mol L-1?

Solution: Ksp = 4s3 ⇒ s3 = (3.2 × 10-11)/4 = 8 × 10-12 ⇒ s = 2 × 10-4 mol L-1.

Question 4 of 10
Addition of ammonium chloride to ammonium hydroxide solution:

Solution: NH4Cl provides common NH4+ ions, suppressing the dissociation of NH4OH and reducing [OH-].

Question 5 of 10
The solubility of Ag2CrO4 is 1.0 × 10-4 mol L-1. Its Ksp is:

Solution: Ksp = 4s3 = 4 × (1.0 × 10-4)3 = 4 × 10-12.

Question 6 of 10
Why is HCl added before passing H2S in Group II cation analysis?

Solution: HCl provides H+ ions that combine with S2- from H2S, suppressing [S2-] to prevent premature precipitation of Group IV.

Question 7 of 10
What is the relationship between solubility s and Ksp for a salt of type A2B3?

Solution: A2B3 → 2A3+ + 3B2- ⇒ Ksp = (2s)2(3s)3 = 108s5.

Question 8 of 10
Which salt is least soluble in water among the following given similar Ksp orders?

Solution: Solubility comparisons require specific numerical values of Ksp or direct experimental solubility data.

Question 9 of 10
If 50 mL of 0.002 M CaCl2 is mixed with 50 mL of 0.002 M Na2SO4, will CaSO4 precipitate? (Ksp of CaSO4 = 2.4 × 10-5)

Solution: After mixing, concentrations are halved ([Ca2+] = 0.001 M, [SO42-] = 0.001 M). Qip = 10-6, which is less than Ksp (2.4 × 10-5).

Question 10 of 10
The unit of Ksp for lead iodide (PbI2) is:

Solution: PbI2 → Pb2+ + 2I-. Ksp = [Pb2+][I-]2, giving units of (mol L-1)3 = mol3 L-3.
