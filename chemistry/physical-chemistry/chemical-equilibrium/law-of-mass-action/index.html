Law of Mass Action

The law of mass action states that the rate of a chemical reaction is directly proportional to the active masses of the reactants raised to the power of their stoichiometric coefficients. Formulated by Cato Maximilian Guldberg and Peter Waage, it establishes the quantitative foundation for chemical equilibrium.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Formulation and Active Mass

  • Active mass is expressed as molar concentration in dilute solutions or partial pressure in gaseous systems.
  • For non-ideal systems, activities replace concentrations to account for interionic and intermolecular forces.

Equilibrium Constant Characteristics

  • The equilibrium constant (K) is independent of initial concentrations, catalysts, and presence of inert gases at constant volume.
  • K varies strictly with temperature according to the van't Hoff equation.

Reaction Quotient and Direction

  • The reaction quotient (Q) uses non-equilibrium concentrations to predict the direction of net reaction.
  • When Q < K, the reaction proceeds in the forward direction; when Q > K, it proceeds backward.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Equilibrium Constant Expression (Concentration)
Kc = ([C]c[D]d) / ([A]a[B]b)
Conditions: Reversible reaction at equilibrium in solution or gas phase
Units: Dimensionless or (mol L-1)Δn
Equilibrium Constant Expression (Pressure)
Kp = (PCc PDd) / (PAa PBb)
Conditions: Gaseous homogeneous equilibria
Units: Dimensionless or (bar)Δn or (atm)Δn
Relation between Kp and Kc
Kp = Kc(RT)Δng
Conditions: Ideal gas behavior assumed
Units: R in L atm K-1 mol-1 or J K-1 mol-1
Rate Law Expression
r = k[A]a[B]b
Conditions: Elementary reaction at constant temperature
Units: r in mol L-1 s-1, [A], [B] in mol L-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Active mass of pure solids and pure liquids is included in the equilibrium expression.

Observation: Pure solids and pure liquids are assigned an activity of unity (1) and omitted from K expressions. — Their concentration or density remains constant throughout the reaction course at constant temperature.

Exception Equilibrium constant is independent of initial concentrations and catalysts.

Observation: Catalysts alter the rate of forward and backward reactions equally without changing K. — Catalysts provide an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy, leaving initial and final state thermodynamics unchanged.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Delta n G Sign Shortcut
Δng = (Moles of gaseous products) - (Moles of gaseous reactants)

Application: Quickly determine whether Kp is greater than, less than, or equal to Kc.

Reversed Reaction Inversion
Kreverse = 1 / Kforward

Application: Immediately find the equilibrium constant when a chemical equation is written in the reverse direction.

Stoichiometric Coefficient Multiplier
Knew = (Koriginal)n

Application: Multiply K by power n when the entire balanced equation is multiplied by a factor n.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The unit of Kc is (mol L-1)Δn where Δn is the change in gaseous moles.
  • For reactions where Δng = 0, Kp equals Kc.
  • The value of the equilibrium constant depends exclusively on temperature and the stoichiometry of the reaction.
  • Addition of an inert gas at constant volume has no effect on the equilibrium position.
  • Addition of an inert gas at constant pressure shifts equilibrium toward the side with more moles of gas.
  • Heterogeneous equilibrium expressions omit terms for pure solids and pure liquids.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Including pure solids or liquids in the denominator or numerator of the K expression.
  • Confusing the reaction quotient Q with the equilibrium constant K when calculating non-equilibrium states.
  • Failing to convert units of pressure or concentration to standard SI units before substitution.
  • Assuming catalysts alter the equilibrium yield of a chemical reaction.

⚡ Quick Revision: Law of Mass Action

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Law of Mass Action Principle

Relates reaction rate directly to active masses of reacting species with stoichiometric coefficients as powers.

Equilibrium Constant Expression

Ratio of product activities to reactant activities raised to stoichiometric powers at dynamic equilibrium.

Reaction Quotient (Q)

Instantaneous concentration ratio used alongside K to determine net reaction direction.

Thermodynamic Link

Relates standard Gibbs free energy change to equilibrium constant via ΔG° = -RT ln K.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Active Mass ([A] or PA)
Effective concentration expressed as molarity or partial pressure.
Equilibrium Constant (K)
Ratio of rate constants or concentration ratios at equilibrium.
Reaction Quotient (Q)
Concentration ratio at any arbitrary point in time.
Heterogeneous Equilibrium (Multi-phase system)
Equilibrium involving reactants and products in more than one phase.
Homogeneous Equilibrium (Single-phase system)
Equilibrium where all species are in the same phase.
Degree of Dissociation (α)
Fraction of total moles dissociated at equilibrium.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Kp = Kc(RT)Δng
  • Q < K : Forward reaction favored
  • Q > K : Backward reaction favored
  • Q = K : System is at equilibrium
  • ΔG° = -RT ln K

📌 Quick Recall

  • Law of mass action forms the mathematical basis for chemical equilibrium.
  • K is constant at a fixed temperature regardless of initial amounts.
  • Pure solids and liquids do not appear in equilibrium expressions.
  • Temperature is the only factor that alters the numerical value of K.

⚖️ Key Differences

K (Equilibrium Constant) VS Q (Reaction Quotient)

K applies strictly at equilibrium; Q applies at any arbitrary stage of reaction.

Question 1 of 19

What is the relation between Kp and Kc for the reaction N2(g) + 3H2(g) → 2NH3(g)?

Explanation: For N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3, Δng = 2 - 4 = -2. Thus, Kp = Kc(RT)-2.

Question 2 of 19

Which of the following remains constant when a catalyst is added to a reversible reaction at equilibrium?

Explanation: A catalyst lowers activation energy and increases forward and backward rates equally, leaving the equilibrium constant K unchanged.

Question 3 of 19

For a reaction where Qc > Kc, what is the behavior of the system?

Explanation: When Qc > Kc, the concentration of products is higher than at equilibrium, so the system proceeds in the reverse (backward) direction.

Question 4 of 19

What is the unit of Kc for the dissociation of HI: 2HI(g) → H2(g) + I2(g)?

Explanation: For 2HI → H2 + I2, Δng = 2 - 2 = 0. Therefore, Kc is dimensionless.

Question 5 of 19

Who formulated the Law of Mass Action?

Explanation: Cato Maximilian Guldberg and Peter Waage formulated the law of mass action between 1864 and 1879.

Question 6 of 19

In the dissociation of CaCO3(s) → CaO(s) + CO2(g), the equilibrium constant Kp is expressed as:

Explanation: Pure solids CaCO3 and CaO have unit activity and are excluded, leaving Kp = PCO2.

Question 7 of 19

How does an increase in temperature affect an exothermic equilibrium reaction?

Explanation: For exothermic reactions (ΔH < T), increasing temperature decreases the value of the equilibrium constant K.

Question 8 of 19

If the equilibrium constant for A → B is K1 and for B → C is K2, what is the equilibrium constant for A → C?

Explanation: When chemical equations are added, their respective equilibrium constants are multiplied.

Question 9 of 19

Which factor alters the numerical value of the equilibrium constant?

Explanation: The equilibrium constant is strictly temperature-dependent.

Question 10 of 19

What is Δng for the reaction PCl5(g) → PCl3(g) + Cl2(g)?

Explanation: Δng = (1 + 1) - 1 = 1.

Question 11 of 19

Addition of an inert gas at constant pressure to a gaseous equilibrium mixture where Δng > 0 will:

Explanation: At constant pressure, adding inert gas increases total volume, shifting equilibrium toward the side with more moles of gas (Δng > 0, forward).

Question 12 of 19

The relationship between standard free energy change (ΔG°) and equilibrium constant (K) is:

Explanation: Standard thermodynamic relation is ΔG° = -RT ln K.

Question 13 of 19

If K for a reaction is 105, the reaction will:

Explanation: A very large value of K (> 103) indicates that products strongly predominate at equilibrium, meaning the reaction goes almost to completion.

Question 14 of 19

Active mass of a substance is conventionally expressed in units of:

Explanation: Active mass is taken as molar concentration expressed in moles per liter (mol L-1).

Question 15 of 19

For which of the following reactions is Kp equal to Kc?

Explanation: For H2 + I2 → 2HI, Δng = 2 - 2 = 0, so Kp = Kc(RT)0 = Kc.

Question 16 of 19

What happens to Q when reactants are added to a system at equilibrium?

Explanation: Adding reactants increases the denominator in the quotient expression Q = [Products] / [Reactants], causing Q to decrease.

Question 17 of 19

Which of the following statements about the law of mass action is incorrect?

Explanation: The law of mass action applies to both homogeneous and heterogeneous systems, not just heterogeneous ones.

Question 18 of 19

The unit of Kp for the reaction N2(g) + 3H2(g) → 2NH3(g) is:

Explanation: Unit is (atm)-2 since Δng = -2.

Question 19 of 19

If a reaction is reversed and its coefficients are halved, the new equilibrium constant K' is related to original K as:

Explanation: Reversing the reaction inverts K (1/K), and halving coefficients takes the square root (√), giving K' = 1 / √K.

Answer: Option B (L mol-1) Here Δng = 2 - 3 = -1. The unit is (mol L-1)-1 = L mol-1.
UP PGT 2013
If K1 and K2 are equilibrium constants for the reactions SO2 + 1/2 O2 → SO3 and 2SO3 → 2SO2 + O2 respectively, what is the relation between them?
Answer: Option B (K2 = 1 / K12) The second reaction is the reverse of the first multiplied by 2. Thus, K2 = (1 / K1)2 = 1 / K12.

In which of the following equilibria does the change in pressure fail to affect the equilibrium position?
Answer: Option C (H2(g) + I2(g) → 2HI(g)) Pressure has no effect when Δng = 0. For H2 + I2 → 2HI, Δng = 0.
Model Question
What is the value of Δng for the thermal decomposition of potassium chlorate: 2KClO3(s) → 2KCl(s) + 3O2(g)?
Answer: Option A (3) Only gaseous moles are counted. Products have 3 moles of O2(g), reactants have 0 moles of gas. Δng = 3 - 0 = 3.

Question 1 of 10
For the gaseous reaction A + 3B → 4C, the equilibrium expression for Kc is:

Solution: Kc is the product of product concentrations raised to stoichiometric coefficients divided by reactant concentrations.

Question 2 of 10
At 500 K, the equilibrium constant Kc for the reaction is 10. What is Kc for the reverse reaction at the same temperature?

Solution: The equilibrium constant for a reversed reaction is the reciprocal of the original: 1 / 10 = 0.1.

Question 3 of 10
Which condition favors maximum yield of products in an exothermic equilibrium reaction according to Le Chatelier and mass action principles?

Solution: Exothermic reactions are favored by low temperatures; high pressure favors reactions with a decrease in gaseous moles.

Question 4 of 10
Calculate Kp for a system where Kc = 0.40 at 300 K for the reaction CO(g) + Cl2(g) → COCl2(g) (R = 0.0821 L atm mol-1 K-1).

Solution: Δng = 1 - 2 = -1. Kp = 0.40 × (0.0821 × 300)-1 = 0.40 / 24.63 ≈ 0.016.

Question 5 of 10
What is the equilibrium constant expression for the dissolution of silver chloride in water: AgCl(s) ⇄ Ag+(aq) + Cl-(aq)?

Solution: Pure solid AgCl has unit activity and is omitted from the denominator, yielding Ksp = [Ag+][Cl-].

Question 6 of 10
If Qc < Kc for a reaction mixture, the net reaction will proceed to:

Solution: When Qc < Kc, there are too few products relative to reactants, so the reaction proceeds to the right (forward).

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following equations represents the correct form of the van't Hoff equation relating temperature and equilibrium constant?

Solution: The integrated form of the van't Hoff equation is ln(K2/K1) = (ΔH°/R) [(T2 - T1)/(T1T2)].

Question 8 of 10
In the synthesis of ammonia, N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇄ 2NH3(g), what is the effect of increasing the volume of the reaction vessel at equilibrium?

Solution: Increasing volume decreases pressure, shifting equilibrium toward the side with more moles of gas (reactants, 4 moles vs 2 moles).

Question 9 of 10
What is the relationship between Kp and Kc when Δng = 1?

Solution: Substituting Δng = 1 into Kp = Kc(RT)Δng gives Kp = KcRT.

Question 10 of 10
At chemical equilibrium, the rate of the forward reaction is:

Solution: Chemical equilibrium is dynamic; the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the backward reaction.
