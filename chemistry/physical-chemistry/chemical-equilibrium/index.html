Physical Chemistry

Chemical Equilibrium

Chemical equilibrium is a dynamic state in which the rates of the forward and reverse reactions are equal, resulting in constant macroscopic concentrations of reactants and products. It is thermodynamically characterized by a minimum in Gibbs free energy and a net entropy change of zero for the system and surroundings at constant temperature and pressure.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Foundations and Equilibrium Constants

  • The dynamic nature of chemical equilibrium is established when the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction (rf = rb).
  • The equilibrium constant (Kc or Kp) is a temperature-dependent quantity; its value is independent of initial concentrations, volume, pressure, or the presence of a catalyst.
  • The reaction quotient (Q) determines the direction of spontaneous change: if Q < K, the reaction proceeds forward; if Q > K, it proceeds backward; if Q = K, the system is at equilibrium.
  • Pure solids and liquids have constant activities defined as unity (a = 1), meaning their concentrations are omitted from the equilibrium constant expression.

Le Chatelier's Principle and System Perturbations

  • Le Chatelier's Principle states that if a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change in concentration, temperature, or pressure, the system shifts to counteract the imposed change.
  • Effect of pressure depends on Δng: an increase in pressure shifts the equilibrium toward the side with fewer moles of gas; if Δng = 0, pressure changes have no effect.
  • Effect of temperature is governed by the sign of ΔH: increasing temperature shifts endothermic reactions (ΔH > 0) forward (increasing K) and exothermic reactions (ΔH < 0) backward (decreasing K).
  • Inert gas addition at constant volume has no effect on equilibrium; at constant pressure, it shifts the equilibrium toward the side with more moles of gas (where Δng > 0).

Dissociation and Vapor Density Measurements

  • The degree of dissociation (α) is the fraction of a reactant that dissociates into products, varying from 0 to 1.
  • The vapor density method relates the initial vapor density (D, theoretical) and equilibrium vapor density (d, experimental) to calculate α via α = (D - d)/((n - 1)d).
  • Heterogeneous equilibria involve multiple phases, where the equilibrium state depends only on the active masses of gaseous or dissolved species (e.g., thermal decomposition of CaCO3).
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Van 't Hoff Equation (Integrated Form)
ln(K2 / K1) = (ΔH° / R) * (1/T1 - 1/T2)
Conditions: Constant standard enthalpy change (ΔH°) over the temperature range
Units: ΔH° in J mol-1, R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin
Degree of Dissociation from Vapor Density
α = (D - d) / ((n - 1)d)
Conditions: Gas-phase dissociation reaction yielding 'n' moles of gaseous products from one mole of reactant
Units: α is dimensionless, D (theoretical vapor density) and d (observed vapor density) in g L-1 or relative units
Gibbs Free Energy and Equilibrium Constant
ΔG° = -RT ln Keq = -2.303 RT log Keq
Conditions: Standard state conditions, constant temperature and pressure
Units: ΔG° in J mol-1, R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin
Reaction Quotient and Gibbs Free Energy
ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q
Conditions: Non-equilibrium states at constant temperature and pressure
Units: ΔG and ΔG° in J mol-1, Q is dimensionless reaction quotient
Relation between Kp and Kc
Kp = Kc(RT)Δng
Conditions: Ideal gas behavior, constant temperature
Units: Kp in atmΔng or barΔng, Kc in (mol L-1)Δng, R = 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Adding a reactant shifts the equilibrium to the product side.

Observation: Adding a pure solid reactant to a heterogeneous equilibrium has no effect on the equilibrium position. — The activity of a pure solid is constant (unity) and does not alter the reaction quotient Q.

Exception Increasing pressure shifts the equilibrium to the side with fewer gas moles.

Observation: Melting of ice to water shifts the equilibrium forward (towards liquid water) despite being a solid-to-liquid transition. — Water has a higher density than ice, so increasing pressure favors the phase with smaller volume (liquid water).

Exception Catalysts speed up chemical reactions.

Observation: Catalysts do not alter the equilibrium constant (K) or the equilibrium composition. — A catalyst lowers the activation energy of both forward and reverse reactions by the same amount, increasing both rates equally.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Inert Gas Addition Mnemonic
Constant Volume = No Change; Constant Pressure = Shift to More Gas Moles (V-N-P-M)

Application: Quickly solve conceptual MCQs on inert gas addition without writing down mathematical expressions.

Vapor Density Shortcut for α
α = (Mt - Mo) / ((n - 1)Mo)

Application: Use molar masses directly (Mt = theoretical molar mass, Mo = observed molar mass) instead of converting to vapor densities first.

Kp vs Kc Sign Rule
If Δng > 0 → Kp > Kc; if Δng < 0 → Kp < Kc; if Δng = 0 → Kp = Kc

Application: Instantly eliminate incorrect options in relation-based multiple-choice questions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The unit of Kp is (atm)Δng and Kc is (mol L-1)Δng.
  • For the reaction N2(g) + 3H2(g) &rightleftharpoons 2NH3(g), Δng = -2, hence Kp = Kc(RT)-2.
  • Le Chatelier's principle is a direct consequence of the second law of thermodynamics.
  • The value of the equilibrium constant is independent of the direction from which equilibrium is approached.
  • If a reaction is multiplied by a factor n, the new equilibrium constant K' = Kn.
  • If two reactions are added, their equilibrium constants are multiplied: K3 = K1 × K2.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Using total pressure instead of partial pressures when calculating Kp without multiplying by mole fractions.
  • Including the concentration of liquid water in aqueous solutions where water acts as the solvent (its concentration is constant at ~55.5 M and incorporated into K).
  • Confusing the sign of Δng by including solid or liquid phases in the calculation (only gaseous species must be counted).
  • Assuming that ΔG = 0 at standard state equilibrium; actually, ΔG = 0 at equilibrium, whereas ΔG° is only zero when K = 1.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Equilibrium

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Dynamic Equilibrium

The state where forward and backward reaction rates are equal, resulting in constant concentrations of reactants and products over time.

Law of Mass Action

The rate of a chemical reaction is directly proportional to the product of the active masses of the reacting substances raised to their stoichiometric coefficients.

Le Chatelier's Principle

A system at equilibrium responds to external perturbations (temperature, pressure, concentration) by shifting in a direction that minimizes the effect of that perturbation.

Thermodynamic Link

The equilibrium state represents the minimum of the Gibbs free energy curve (dG = 0) at constant T and P, directly linking K to standard free energy change via ΔG° = -RT ln K.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Equilibrium Constant (K)
The ratio of the mathematical product of concentrations of products to reactants, each raised to the power of its stoichiometric coefficient at equilibrium.
Reaction Quotient (Q)
The ratio of concentrations of products to reactants at any point in time during a reaction.
Degree of Dissociation (α)
The fraction of a mole of reactant that dissociates at equilibrium; dimensionless.
Le Chatelier's Principle (N/A)
A qualitative rule predicting the direction of shift of a system in response to external changes.
Van 't Hoff Equation (d(ln K)/dT = ΔH°/RT2)
Equation describing the variation of the equilibrium constant with temperature.
Active Mass ([A] or pA)
The effective concentration of a substance in a chemical reaction, taken as molarity for solutes or partial pressure for gases.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Kp = Kc(RT)Δng
  • ΔG° = -2.303 RT log K
  • α = (D - d) / ((n - 1)d)
  • Units of R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1 or 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1 depending on units of P and V.
  • Catalysts do not change the position of equilibrium, only the rate at which it is attained.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Equilibrium is dynamic, not static.
  • K depends solely on temperature for a given reaction.
  • Δng determines the sensitivity of Kp/Kc relations and pressure effects.
  • Exothermic reactions are favored by low temperatures, while endothermic reactions are favored by high temperatures.

⚖️ Key Differences

Reaction Gibbs Free Energy (ΔG) VS Standard Gibbs Free Energy (ΔG°)

ΔG determines the spontaneity of a reaction at any given composition and is zero at equilibrium, whereas ΔG° is a constant for a reaction at standard state and determines the magnitude of the equilibrium constant K.

Question 1 of 18

For the reaction PCl5(g) &rightleftharpoons PCl3(g) + Cl2(g), what is the relation between Kp and Kc?

Explanation: Here, Δng = (moles of gaseous products) - (moles of gaseous reactants) = (1 + 1) - 1 = 1. Using Kp = Kc(RT)Δng, we get Kp = Kc(RT)1.

Question 2 of 18

In which of the following reactions does an increase in pressure favor the forward reaction?

Explanation: An increase in pressure shifts the equilibrium to the side with fewer moles of gas. For N2(g) + 3H2(g) &rightleftharpoons 2NH3(g), Δng = 2 - 4 = -2. The product side has fewer moles, so the forward reaction is favored.

Question 3 of 18

What is the effect of adding helium gas at constant volume to the equilibrium N2(g) + 3H2(g) &rightleftharpoons 2NH3(g)?

Explanation: Adding an inert gas at constant volume does not change the partial pressures or molar concentrations of the reacting gases. Therefore, there is no effect on the equilibrium.

Question 4 of 18

If the equilibrium constant for A + B &rightleftharpoons C + D is K, what is the equilibrium constant for 2C + 2D &rightleftharpoons 2A + 2B?

Explanation: Reversing a reaction changes the equilibrium constant to 1/K. Multiplying the reaction by a factor of 2 raises the equilibrium constant to the power of 2. Thus, the new constant is (1/K)2 = 1/K2.

Question 5 of 18

For an exothermic reaction, how does the equilibrium constant change with an increase in temperature?

Explanation: According to the Van 't Hoff equation, for an exothermic reaction (ΔH < 0), increasing the temperature (T2 > T1) results in ln(K2/K1) < 0, meaning K2 < K1. Thus, the equilibrium constant decreases.

Question 6 of 18

Under what condition does Kp = Kc?

Explanation: Since Kp = Kc(RT)Δng, when Δng = 0, (RT)0 = 1, which yields Kp = Kc.

Question 7 of 18

What is the degree of dissociation (α) of N2O4 if its observed vapor density is d and theoretical vapor density is D?

Explanation: For N2O4(g) &rightleftharpoons 2NO2(g), the number of product moles from one mole of reactant is n = 2. Using the formula α = (D - d)/((n - 1)d), we get α = (D - d)/((2 - 1)d) = (D - d)/d.

Question 8 of 18

For the reaction C(s) + CO2(g) &rightleftharpoons 2CO(g), what is the expression for Kp?

Explanation: The active mass of pure solid carbon, C(s), is taken as unity (1). Therefore, it is omitted from the equilibrium expression, giving Kp = p2CO / pCO2.

Question 9 of 18

If ΔG° < 0 for a reaction, what can be inferred about the equilibrium constant K?

Explanation: Since ΔG° = -RT ln K, a negative ΔG° requires ln K to be positive, which means K must be greater than 1.

Question 10 of 18

In the synthesis of ammonia: N2(g) + 3H2(g) &rightleftharpoons 2NH3(g) + 92.4 kJ, what conditions favor the maximum yield of ammonia?

Explanation: The reaction is exothermic (ΔH < 0), so low temperature favors the forward reaction. The reaction also proceeds with a decrease in the number of gaseous moles (Δng = -2), so high pressure favors the forward reaction.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following will not affect the value of the equilibrium constant?

Explanation: The equilibrium constant (K) is a function of temperature only. Changes in pressure or the addition of a catalyst do not alter the value of K.

Question 12 of 18

For the reaction H2(g) + I2(g) &rightleftharpoons 2HI(g), if the volume of the container is doubled, what happens to the equilibrium constant?

Explanation: The equilibrium constant is independent of the volume of the container; it changes only with temperature.

Question 13 of 18

The equilibrium constant for a reaction is 1 × 1020 at 298 K. What is the value of ΔG° for this reaction?

Explanation: Since K is extremely large (1 × 1020), ln K is a large positive value. Because ΔG° = -RT ln K, ΔG° must be highly negative.

Question 14 of 18

For the physical equilibrium Ice &rightleftharpoons Water, what is the effect of increasing pressure?

Explanation: Water has a higher density (smaller volume) than ice. Increasing pressure favors the state with smaller volume, shifting the equilibrium toward the liquid water phase.

Question 15 of 18

If Qc > Kc for a reaction mixture, in which direction will the reaction proceed to attain equilibrium?

Explanation: When Qc > Kc, there is an excess of products relative to reactants compared to the equilibrium state. To reach equilibrium, the reaction must shift in the backward direction.

Question 16 of 18

For the reaction N2O4(g) &rightleftharpoons 2NO2(g), the value of α at a total pressure P is related to Kp by which expression?

Explanation: Starting with 1 mole of N2O4, at equilibrium we have (1-α) moles of N2O4 and 2α moles of NO2. Total moles = 1 + α. Partial pressures: p(N2O4) = [(1-α)/(1+α)]P, p(NO2) = [2α/(1+α)]P. Kp = p2(NO2)/p(N2O4) = [4α2/(1+α)2]P2 / [(1-α)/(1+α)]P = 4α2P / (1 - α2).

Question 17 of 18

In a reaction A + B &rightleftharpoons C, if the initial concentrations of A and B are 1 M each, and at equilibrium the concentration of C is 0.5 M, what is Kc?

Explanation: At equilibrium: [C] = 0.5 M. [A] = 1 - 0.5 = 0.5 M, [B] = 1 - 0.5 = 0.5 M. Kc = [C] / ([A][B]) = 0.5 / (0.5 * 0.5) = 2.0.

Question 18 of 18

Which thermodynamic quantity is zero at chemical equilibrium?

Explanation: At equilibrium, the Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) is zero. The standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) is not necessarily zero unless K = 1.

Question 2 of 10

Solution: The equilibrium constant is independent of volume changes; it remains 2.4 × 10-3.

Question 3 of 10
If the standard free energy change (ΔG°) for a reaction is zero, the equilibrium constant is:

Solution: Using ΔG° = -RT ln K, if ΔG° = 0, then ln K = 0, which means K = e0 = 1.

Question 4 of 10
For the reaction SO2(g) + 1/2 O2(g) &rightleftharpoons SO3(g), if Kp = Kc(RT)x, then x is:

Solution: Δng = 1 - (1 + 0.5) = -0.5. Therefore, x = -0.5.

Question 6 of 10
For the reaction N2O4(g) &rightleftharpoons 2NO2(g), if the degree of dissociation at equilibrium is 0.2 and total pressure is 1 atm, the partial pressure of NO2 is:

Solution: Total moles at equilibrium = 1 + α = 1 + 0.2 = 1.2. Moles of NO2 = 2α = 0.4. Partial pressure of NO2 = (0.4 / 1.2) * 1 atm = 0.33 atm.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following statements is correct regarding the addition of a catalyst to a system at equilibrium?

Solution: A catalyst increases the rates of both forward and backward reactions equally, thereby helping the system reach equilibrium faster without changing the equilibrium composition.

Question 8 of 10
For an endothermic reaction, the slope of the plot of ln K versus 1/T is:

Solution: The integrated van 't Hoff equation is ln K = -ΔH°/RT + constant. For an endothermic reaction, ΔH° > 0, so the slope (-ΔH°/R) is negative.

Question 9 of 10
In the heterogeneous equilibrium NH4HS(s) &rightleftharpoons NH3(g) + H2S(g), if the total pressure at equilibrium is 2 atm, the value of Kp is:

Solution: Since NH4HS is solid, Kp = pNH3 pH2S. Since they are produced in a 1:1 ratio, pNH3 = pH2S = Ptotal / 2 = 2 / 2 = 1 atm. Thus, Kp = 1 1 = 1 atm2.

Question 10 of 10
If the concentration of a reactant is doubled in a first-order reversible reaction, the equilibrium constant:

Solution: The equilibrium constant is a constant at a given temperature and does not change when the concentration of reactants or products is altered.
