Physical Chemistry

Application of Law of Mass Action

The law of mass action states that the rate of a chemical reaction is directly proportional to the active masses of the reactants. It enables the formulation of equilibrium constants for homogeneous and heterogeneous systems.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Derivation and Expression of Equilibrium Constants

  • The law of mass action was formulated by C.M. Guldberg and P. Waage in 1864.
  • Active mass is expressed as molar concentration in mol L-1 or partial pressure for gases.
  • For a general reversible reaction aA + bB → cC + dD, the equilibrium constant Kc is the ratio of product concentrations to reactant concentrations raised to their stoichiometric coefficients.
  • Pure solids and pure liquids are assigned an activity of unity (1) and are omitted from the equilibrium constant expression.

Heterogeneous Equilibria and Phase Rules

  • Heterogeneous equilibria involve reactants and products in more than one phase, such as solids and gases.
  • In the thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate, CaCO3(s) → CaO(s) + CO2(s), Kp = PCO2.
  • The concentration or pressure of pure solid phases remains constant at a given temperature, hence excluded from Kc and Kp.
  • The addition of more solid reactant or product does not alter the position of equilibrium.

Reaction Quotient and Equilibrium Prediction

  • The reaction quotient (Qc) uses non-equilibrium concentrations to determine the net direction of a reversible reaction.
  • When Qc < Kc, the reaction proceeds in the forward direction to form more products.
  • When Qc > Kc, the reaction proceeds in the backward direction to regenerate reactants.
  • When Qc = Kc, the system is at chemical equilibrium with zero net change.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Equilibrium Constant in Terms of Concentration (Kc)
Kc = ([C]c[D]d) / ([A]a[B]b)
Conditions: Applicable to ideal solutions or dilute gases at equilibrium
Units: (mol L-1)Δn
Equilibrium Constant in Terms of Partial Pressure (Kp)
Kp = (PCc PDd) / (PAa PBb)
Conditions: Applicable to gaseous equilibria behaving ideally
Units: (atm)Δn or (bar)Δn
Relation Between Kp and Kc
Kp = Kc(RT)Δng
Conditions: Ideal gas behavior assumed
Units: R = 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin
Reaction Quotient (Qc)
Qc = ([C]t[D]t) / ([A]t[B]t)
Conditions: At any given instant of time t
Units: Dimensionless or concentration units matching Kc
Law of Mass Action Rate Equation
Rate = k[A]a[B]b
Conditions: Constant temperature for elementary reactions
Units: Rate in mol L-1 s-1, k depends on reaction order
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Equilibrium constant depends only on temperature.

Observation: Catalysts alter reaction rates but do not change Kc or Kp. — A catalyst lowers the activation energy equally for both forward and reverse reactions, leaving the equilibrium position unchanged.

Exception Active mass equals molar concentration for all solutions.

Observation: In concentrated or non-ideal solutions, activity coefficients (γ) must be applied instead of concentrations. — Interionic and intermolecular forces cause deviations from ideal solution behavior at high concentrations.

Exception Partial pressures are used directly in Kp for all gases.

Observation: At very high pressures, gases deviate from ideal behavior and fugacity (f) must be used instead of pressure. — Real gases experience significant intermolecular attractions and repulsions at high pressures.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Delta n Gas Shortcut
Δng = (Moles of gaseous products) - (Moles of gaseous reactants)

Application: Use directly in Kp = Kc(RT)Δng without writing full derivations.

Reversed Reaction Rule
K' = 1 / K

Application: If a chemical equation is reversed, take the reciprocal of the original equilibrium constant.

Stoichiometric Coefficient Multiplier
K'' = (K)n

Application: If the entire reaction equation is multiplied by a factor n, raise the original equilibrium constant to the power n.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The units of Kc depend strictly on the change in stoichiometric moles (Δn).
  • For reactions where Δn = 0, both Kp and Kc are completely dimensionless.
  • The equilibrium constant for the dissociation of weak acids is known as the acid dissociation constant (Ka).
  • Adding an inert gas at constant volume has no effect on the equilibrium position.
  • Adding an inert gas at constant pressure shifts equilibrium toward the side with more moles of gas.
  • The value of the equilibrium constant changes with temperature according to the van't Hoff equation.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Including pure solids and pure liquids in the equilibrium constant expression.
  • Confusing the reaction quotient Q with the equilibrium constant K when calculating shifts.
  • Failing to convert grams or volumes into proper molar concentrations before applying Kc.
  • Neglecting temperature units (must always use Kelvin scale in RT terms).

⚡ Quick Revision: Application of Law of Mass Action

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Law of Mass Action Foundation

Relates reaction rate directly to the active masses of reacting species raised to their stoichiometric powers at constant temperature.

Equilibrium Constant Expressions

Formulated as Kc for concentrations and Kp for partial pressures, omitting pure condensed phases.

Thermodynamic Link via Delta n

Interconverted via the relation Kp = Kc(RT)Δng for ideal gas systems.

Reaction Quotient Diagnostics

Compares Q with K to quantitatively predict whether a system will shift forward, backward, or remain at equilibrium.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Active Mass (Molar Concentration)
Effective concentration expressed in mol L-1.
Equilibrium Constant (Kc / Kp)
Ratio of product activities to reactant activities at equilibrium.
Reaction Quotient (Qc)
Ratio of active masses at any arbitrary point in time.
Heterogeneous Equilibrium (Multi-phase System)
Equilibrium system involving more than one physical phase.
Degree of Dissociation (α)
Fraction of total moles dissociated at equilibrium.
van't Hoff Equation (d(ln K)/dT = ΔH°/(RT2))
Relates temperature dependence of K to standard enthalpy change.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Kp = Kc(RT)Δng
  • Pure solids and liquids have an activity of 1.
  • Q < K implies forward shift.
  • Q > K implies backward shift.
  • Catalysts do not alter K.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Law of mass action forms the basis of chemical equilibrium calculations.
  • Equilibrium constants are temperature dependent parameters.
  • Heterogeneous equilibria exclude condensed pure phases.
  • Q versus K comparison dictates reaction direction.

⚖️ Key Differences

Kc VS Kp

Kc uses molar concentrations whereas Kp uses partial pressures of gases.

Question 1 of 18

What is the unit of Kc for the reaction N2(g) + 3H2(g) ↔ 2NH3(g)?

Explanation: Δng = 2 - (1 + 3) = -2. Therefore, unit is (mol L-1)-2 = mol-2 L2.

Question 2 of 18

For which of the following reactions is Kp equal to Kc?

Explanation: For H2 + I2 ↔ 2HI, Δng = 2 - 2 = 0. Hence Kp = Kc(RT)0 = Kc.

Question 3 of 18

If Qc > Kc for a reversible reaction, what happens?

Explanation: When Qc exceeds Kc, there is an excess of products, so the reaction shifts backward to reach equilibrium.

Question 4 of 18

Which factor changes the value of the equilibrium constant?

Explanation: The equilibrium constant is strictly a function of temperature. Concentration, pressure, and catalysts do not alter K.

Question 5 of 18

In the dissociation of solid ammonium chloride, NH4Cl(s) ↔ NH3(g) + HCl(g), the equilibrium constant Kp is expressed as:

Explanation: Solid NH4Cl has an activity of 1 and is omitted, leaving Kp = PNH3 × PHCl.

Question 6 of 18

What is the relation between Kp and Kc when Δng = +1?

Explanation: Substituting Δng = 1 into Kp = Kc(RT)Δng yields Kp = KcRT.

Question 7 of 18

Who formulated the law of mass action?

Explanation: C.M. Guldberg and P. Waage formulated the law of mass action in 1864.

Question 8 of 18

If the equilibrium constant for A ↔ B is K, what is the equilibrium constant for 2B ↔ 2A?

Explanation: Reversing the reaction gives 1/K, and multiplying by 2 raises it to the power 2, resulting in 1/K2.

Question 9 of 18

For a gas-phase reaction, if total pressure is increased at constant temperature, the equilibrium constant Kp:

Explanation: The equilibrium constant depends only on temperature and is unaffected by changes in pressure.

Question 10 of 18

Active mass for dilute solutions is numerically approximately equal to:

Explanation: Active mass in dilute solutions is taken as molar concentration (molarity).

Question 11 of 18

In the reaction PCl5(g) ↔ PCl3(g) + Cl2(g), the units of Kc are:

Explanation: Δng = 2 - 1 = 1. Thus, units are mol L-1.

Question 12 of 18

Which of the following does not affect a chemical equilibrium system?

Explanation: A catalyst speeds up both forward and backward reactions equally without shifting the equilibrium.

Question 13 of 18

If Kc for a reaction is very large (> 103), it indicates that:

Explanation: A large Kc means the numerator (products) is much larger than the denominator (reactants) at equilibrium.

Question 14 of 18

What is the value of Δng for the reaction 2HI(g) ↔ H2(g) + I2(g)?

Explanation: Δng = (1 + 1) - 2 = 0.

Question 15 of 18

The equilibrium constant for an exothermic reaction typically ________ with an increase in temperature.

Explanation: According to the van't Hoff equation, for exothermic reactions (ΔH < T), K decreases as temperature increases.

Question 16 of 18

What is the active mass of 8 grams of oxygen gas contained in a 2-liter vessel?

Explanation: Moles of O2 = 8 / 32 = 0.25 mol. Molarity = 0.25 mol / 2 L = 0.125 mol L-1.

Question 17 of 18

If Qc = Kc, the system is:

Explanation: Qc = Kc defines the exact condition of chemical equilibrium.

Question 18 of 18

Which species is omitted when writing the equilibrium expression for the dissolution of silver chloride in water?

Explanation: Solid AgCl is a pure solid phase, so its activity is taken as 1 and it is omitted from the expression.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate Kp for the reaction 2NOCl(g) ↔ 2NO(g) + Cl2(g) if Kc = 1.6 × 10-5 at 400 K (R = 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1).

Solution: Δng = 3 - 2 = 1. Kp = 1.6 × 10-5 × (0.0821 × 400)1 = 5.24 × 10-4.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following systems will have Kp = Kc?

Solution: For CO + H2O ↔ CO2 + H2, Δng = (1 + 1) - (1 + 1) = 0.

Question 3 of 10
In the reaction A(g) + B(g) ↔ C(g), active masses at equilibrium are [A] = 2 M, [B] = 3 M, [C] = 12 M. What is Kc?

Solution: Kc = [C] / ([A][B]) = 12 / (2 × 3) = 12 / 6 = 2.

Question 4 of 10
If Qc < Kc, the reaction shifts in which direction?

Solution: Qc < Kc means reactant concentration is higher than equilibrium ratio, favoring the forward reaction.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following is true for an endothermic reaction when temperature is increased?

Solution: For endothermic reactions (ΔH > 0), raising temperature increases the equilibrium constant K.

Question 7 of 10
What is the active mass of 4g of hydrogen gas in a 1-liter container?

Solution: Moles of H2 = 4 / 2 = 2 mol. Molarity = 2 mol / 1 L = 2 mol L-1.

Question 8 of 10
For the equilibrium 2SO2(g) + O2(g) ↔ 2SO3(g), if the volume of the system is halved, what happens to the equilibrium?

Solution: Halving the volume doubles the pressure, shifting the equilibrium toward the side with fewer moles of gas (forward, 2 moles vs 3 moles).

Question 9 of 10
The equilibrium constant for the reaction H2 + I2 ↔ 2HI is 64. What is the equilibrium constant for HI ↔ 0.5H2 + 0.5I2?

Solution: Reversing and halving the coefficients gives K' = (1 / 64)0.5 = 1 / 8 = 0.125.

Question 10 of 10
Which expression represents the equilibrium constant Kc for the reaction 4NH3 + 5O2 ↔ 4NO + 6H2O?

Solution: Products raised to their stoichiometric coefficients divided by reactants raised to their coefficients.
