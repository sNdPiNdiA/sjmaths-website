Physical Chemistry

Le Chatelier's Principle

Le Chatelier's principle states that if a dynamic equilibrium is disturbed by changing the conditions, the position of equilibrium shifts to counteract the change to re-establish equilibrium. It applies to chemical systems at equilibrium undergoing alterations in concentration, pressure, volume, or temperature.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Effect of Concentration and Pressure on Equilibrium

  • Increasing the concentration of reactants shifts the equilibrium in the forward direction to consume the added reactant.
  • Increasing the total pressure by decreasing volume shifts the equilibrium toward the side with fewer moles of gas.
  • Addition of an inert gas at constant volume has no effect on the equilibrium position because partial pressures remain unchanged.

Effect of Temperature on Equilibrium Constant

  • An increase in temperature favors the endothermic reaction (ΔH > 0), increasing the value of the equilibrium constant K.
  • A decrease in temperature favors the exothermic reaction (ΔH < 0), shifting equilibrium to release heat.
  • The equilibrium constant K changes only with a change in temperature, unlike concentration or pressure modifications.

Role of Catalysts in Equilibrium Systems

  • A catalyst lowers the activation energy for both the forward and reverse reactions equally.
  • Addition of a catalyst does not alter the position of equilibrium or the value of the equilibrium constant K.
  • A catalyst only helps the system reach equilibrium faster by accelerating both rates equally.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Reaction Quotient Expression
Qc = ([C]c[D]d) / ([A]a[B]b)
Conditions: For general reaction aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD at any given time
Units: Concentrations in mol L-1
Equilibrium Constant Relation
Kp = Kc(RT)Δng
Conditions: For gaseous equilibria where Δng = moles of gaseous products - moles of gaseous reactants
Units: R = 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1 or 8.314 J K-1 mol-1
Gibbs Free Energy and Equilibrium
ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q
Conditions: Relates non-standard free energy change to reaction quotient
Units: ΔG in kJ mol-1
Van 't Hoff Equation
ln(K2 / K1) = (ΔH° / R) * [(T2 - T1) / (T1T2)]
Conditions: Applies to temperature dependence of equilibrium constant K
Units: R in J mol-1 K-1, T in Kelvin, ΔH° in J mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Increasing pressure shifts equilibrium to fewer moles of gas

Observation: H2(g) + I2(g) ⇌ 2HI(g) — When Δng = 0, change in pressure has zero effect on the equilibrium composition.

Exception Adding an inert gas at constant volume changes total pressure

Observation: Inert gas addition at constant volume — Partial pressures of reactants and products remain constant, hence no shift occurs despite total pressure increase.

Exception Catalysts do not affect equilibrium

Observation: Enzyme-catalyzed biochemical reactions — Catalysts alter the pathway but thermodynamic state functions and final equilibrium concentrations remain identical.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Temperature Shift Rule (HOT)
Heat On Temperature (Endothermic): T↑ → K↑

Application: Treat heat as a reactant in endothermic reactions and as a product in exothermic reactions.

Pressure Shift Rule (P-V)
P↑ → fewer gas moles

Application: Count gaseous stoichiometric coefficients on both sides to quickly predict the direction of shift under compression.

Q vs K Comparison Mnemonic
Q eats K to reach equilibrium

Application: If Q < K, reaction proceeds forward (left to right). If Q > K, reaction proceeds backward.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Le Chatelier's principle applies exclusively to systems already at dynamic equilibrium.
  • The equilibrium constant K depends solely on temperature and is independent of initial concentrations, pressure, or catalysts.
  • For the synthesis of ammonia (Haber process), high pressure and low temperature favor maximum yield, but moderate temperature is used for optimal reaction rate.
  • In the contact process for H2SO4, oxidation of SO2 is exothermic, so lower temperatures favor high yield.
  • Addition of liquid water to a system involving ice-water equilibrium at 0 °C shifts the equilibrium to favor melting.
  • The solubility of sparingly soluble salts decreases in the presence of a common ion due to Le Chatelier's principle.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming catalysts change the equilibrium yield of a chemical reaction.
  • Forgetting that adding an inert gas at constant pressure increases volume, thus shifting equilibrium toward more gas moles.
  • Confusing the reaction quotient Q with the equilibrium constant K when determining spontaneous direction.
  • Applying pressure changes to condensed phases (liquids and solids) where volume changes are negligible.

⚡ Quick Revision: Le Chatelier's Principle

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Dynamic Equilibrium and Stress

Systems at equilibrium respond to external constraints by shifting to minimize the imposed stress and restore equilibrium.

Quantitative Effects of Temperature

The Van 't Hoff equation mathematically connects temperature changes to shifts in the equilibrium constant based on enthalpy.

Pressure and Gas Stoichiometry

Only reactions with a net change in gaseous moles (Δng ≠ 0) experience shifts due to pressure variations.

Inert Gas Dynamics

Constant volume inert gas addition causes no shift, whereas constant pressure addition expands volume and favors more gas moles.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Le Chatelier's Principle (Equilibrium Shift)
Qualitative rule predicting equilibrium response to stress.
Reaction Quotient (Q)
Ratio of product concentrations to reactant concentrations at any moment.
Equilibrium Constant (K)
Ratio of activities at equilibrium, constant at a fixed temperature.
Van 't Hoff Equation (ln(K2/K1))
Equation relating temperature variation to equilibrium constant.
Gaseous Mole Change (Δng)
Difference between moles of gaseous products and gaseous reactants.
Common Ion Effect (Precipitation Shift)
Suppression of dissociation by adding a common ion.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • K changes only with temperature.
  • Catalysts do not change K or position.
  • Δng = 0 means pressure has no effect.
  • Endothermic reactions have K increase with temperature.
  • Exothermic reactions have K decrease with temperature.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Equilibrium shifts to counteract applied changes.
  • Concentration changes alter Q relative to K.
  • Pressure shifts favor fewer moles of gas.
  • Temperature shifts depend on reaction enthalpy.

⚖️ Key Differences

Inert gas at constant volume VS Inert gas at constant pressure

Constant volume causes no shift; constant pressure increases volume and shifts toward more gaseous moles.

Which factor changes the value of the equilibrium constant K?

Explanation: The equilibrium constant K is strictly a function of temperature for a given reaction.

Question 2 of 19

For the exothermic reaction N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g), what favors maximum yield of ammonia?

Explanation: Since the forward reaction is exothermic, low temperature favors the product; high pressure favors the side with fewer gas moles (2 vs 4).

Question 3 of 19

What is the effect of adding an inert gas at constant volume to the equilibrium PCl5(g) ⇌ PCl3(g) + Cl2(g)?

Explanation: At constant volume, adding an inert gas increases total pressure but does not alter the partial pressures of the reacting species.

Question 4 of 19

If Qc > Kc for a reversible reaction, in which direction does the reaction proceed?

Explanation: When Qc exceeds Kc, the concentration of products is too high, so the reaction shifts backward to reach equilibrium.

Question 5 of 19

Which reaction will not be affected by a change in pressure?

Explanation: For N2 + O2 ⇌ 2NO, Δng = 2 - 2 = 0, so pressure changes have no effect.

Question 6 of 19

What happens to the solubility of NaCl when concentrated HCl is added to its saturated solution?

Explanation: Addition of HCl introduces Cl- ions, causing a common ion effect that precipitates NaCl and decreases its solubility.

Question 7 of 19

In an endothermic equilibrium reaction, an increase in temperature causes:

Explanation: For endothermic reactions (ΔH > 0), raising temperature increases the rate of the forward reaction more, raising K.

Question 8 of 19

What is the effect of adding a catalyst on the position of chemical equilibrium?

Explanation: Catalysts accelerate both forward and reverse rates equally, leaving the equilibrium position and K unchanged.

Question 9 of 19

For the equilibrium 2NO2(g) ⇌ N2O4(g), ΔH = -57.2 kJ, increasing the volume of the container will:

Explanation: Increasing volume decreases pressure, causing the system to shift toward the side with more moles of gas (left, 2 moles vs 1 mole).

Question 10 of 19

Which of the following describes Le Chatelier's principle correctly?

Explanation: The core tenet is that systems adjust to counteract any applied external stress on an equilibrium system.

Question 11 of 19

How does lowering the temperature affect an exothermic equilibrium system?

Explanation: Lowering temperature removes heat, shifting the exothermic reaction in the forward direction to generate heat.

Question 12 of 19

What is the value of Δng for the dissociation of calcium carbonate: CaCO3(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO2(g)?

Explanation: Δng = moles of gaseous products - moles of gaseous reactants = 1 - 0 = 1.

Question 13 of 19

Addition of argon at constant pressure to PCl5(g) ⇌ PCl3(g) + Cl2(g) will:

Explanation: At constant pressure, adding argon increases the total volume, lowering partial pressures and shifting equilibrium toward more moles of gas (right).

Question 14 of 19

The dissociation of weak acid CH3COOH is suppressed by adding HCl due to:

Explanation: Excess H+ from HCl shifts the acetate equilibrium backward according to Le Chatelier's principle.

Question 15 of 19

If the equilibrium constant K is very large (K > 103), the equilibrium mixture contains:

Explanation: A large K indicates that the numerator (products) is much larger than the denominator (reactants) at equilibrium.

Question 16 of 19

Which stress will shift the equilibrium N2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2NO(g) (ΔH = +180 kJ) to the right?

Explanation: Since the reaction is endothermic, increasing the temperature shifts the equilibrium to the right.

Question 17 of 19

What is the unit of the equilibrium constant Kc for the reaction H2(g) + I2(g) ⇌ 2HI(g)?

Explanation: Since Δng = 0, concentrations cancel out, making Kc dimensionless.

Question 18 of 19

According to Van 't Hoff equation, if ΔH° is positive and temperature T2 > T1, then:

Explanation: For an endothermic reaction (ΔH° > 0), raising the temperature increases the equilibrium constant (K2 > K1).

Question 19 of 19

Which of the following equilibria will shift toward the products with an increase in pressure?

Explanation: N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3 has 4 moles of reactants and 2 moles of products, so high pressure favors products.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following reactions will have its equilibrium shifted to the left by increasing pressure?

Solution: PCl5 ⇌ PCl3 + Cl2 has 1 mole of reactant and 2 moles of products; increased pressure shifts it left.

Question 2 of 10
If the temperature of an exothermic reaction is increased, the equilibrium constant K:

Solution: For exothermic reactions, increasing temperature shifts equilibrium left, decreasing the value of K.

Question 3 of 10
What is the relationship between Kp and Kc for the reaction CO(g) + H2O(g) ⇌ CO2(g) + H2(g)?

Solution: Here Δng = (1 + 1) - (1 + 1) = 0, so Kp = Kc(RT)0 = Kc.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following statements about Le Chatelier's principle is incorrect?

Solution: Le Chatelier's principle predicts shifts, but it does not alter the equilibrium constant K, which only changes with temperature.

Question 5 of 10
In the dissociation of N2O4(g) ⇌ 2NO2(g), color deepens on heating because:

Solution: The dissociation of N2O4 is endothermic; heating shifts equilibrium to the right, producing more brown NO2.

Question 6 of 10
When a catalyst is added to a reversible reaction at equilibrium, the rate of the forward reaction:

Solution: A catalyst increases the rates of both the forward and reverse reactions by the exact same factor.

Question 7 of 10
What happens to the degree of dissociation of PCl5 if pressure is decreased?

Solution: Decreasing pressure shifts the equilibrium toward more moles of gas (products), increasing dissociation.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following changes will shift the equilibrium 2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g) to the right?

Solution: Removing SO3 as it forms shifts the equilibrium forward to replace the lost product.

Question 9 of 10
For a gas-phase reaction, if Δng = -2, the relation between Kp and Kc is:

Solution: Substituting Δng = -2 into Kp = Kc(RT)Δng gives Kp = Kc(RT)-2.

Question 10 of 10
What is the primary purpose of applying Le Chatelier's principle in industrial chemistry?

Solution: Industrial processes use Le Chatelier's principle to optimize reaction conditions for maximum economic yield.
