Heterogeneous Equilibrium

Heterogeneous equilibrium involves systems in which reactants and products exist in two or more distinct phases. The equilibrium constant expression excludes the active masses of pure solids and pure liquids because their concentration remains constant at a given temperature.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Phase Rule and Active Mass of Condensed Phases

  • The active mass of a pure solid or pure liquid is taken as unity (1) because its density and molar concentration remain constant at a constant temperature.
  • Omitting pure solids and liquids simplifies the equilibrium expression to include only gaseous or dissolved aqueous species.
  • Adding more of a pure solid or pure liquid at equilibrium does not shift the position of equilibrium, provided the phase remains present.

Thermal Dissociation of Solids

  • For the thermal dissociation of calcium carbonate: CaCO3(s) → CaO(s) + CO2(g), the equilibrium constant is simply Kp = PCO2.
  • The dissociation pressure of a pure solid producing a gas depends only on temperature, not on the initial amounts of solid reactants or products.
  • If the volume of the container enclosing a decomposing solid is changed at constant temperature, the equilibrium pressure of the gas remains invariant once equilibrium is re-established.

Thermodynamics of Heterogeneous Phase Changes

  • At phase equilibrium, the Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) is zero, establishing the foundation for vapor pressure and solubility equilibria.
  • The temperature dependence of heterogeneous equilibria is governed by the sign of ΔH°; endothermic dissociation increases K with temperature rise.
  • Phase equilibria like liquid-vapor or solid-liquid systems are special cases where chemical potential is equal across all coexisting phases.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Pressure Equilibrium Constant
Kp = (PC)c(PD)d / (PA)a(PB)b
Conditions: Applicable only to gaseous components in the heterogeneous mixture
Units: atmΔn or PaΔn
Relation Between Kp and Kc
Kp = Kc(RT)Δng
Conditions: Ideal gas behavior for gaseous species; Δng counts only moles of gases
Units: Δng in moles, R in L atm K-1 mol-1
Van 't Hoff Equation for Temperature Dependence
ln(K2 / K1) = (ΔH° / R) [(T2 - T1) / (T1T2)]
Conditions: Assumes standard enthalpy of reaction (ΔH°) remains constant over the temperature range
Units: T in Kelvin, ΔH° in J mol-1
Equilibrium Constant for Heterogeneous Systems
Kc = [C]c[D]d / [A]a[B]b
Conditions: Excludes pure solids and pure liquids from the expression
Units: Dimensionless or (mol dm-3)Δn
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Concentration of pure solids is omitted from K expression

Observation: Solid solutions and alloys — In solid solutions, components do not maintain constant activity; their mole fractions must be included in the equilibrium quotient.

Exception Dissociation pressure of a solid is independent of solid quantity

Observation: Microcrystalline or nanoparticle sized solids — Extremely small particles have higher surface free energy, altering the vapor pressure according to the Kelvin equation.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Solid Cancellation Rule
Cross out (s) and (l), keep (g) and (aq)

Application: Instantly write the correct Kc or Kp expression by ignoring all terms marked with solid or pure liquid phases.

Delta n Counting Shortcut
Δng = Σnproducts(g) - Σnreactants(g)

Application: Only count coefficients of gases when calculating Δng for Kp = Kc(RT)Δn conversions.

Decomposition Pressure Trick
Kp = Pgas

Application: For any solid decomposition yielding a single gas, the equilibrium constant is numerically equal to the partial pressure of that gas.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The active mass of a pure solid is constant and convention assigns it a value of unity.
  • For CaCO3(s) → CaO(s) + CO2(g), Kp = PCO2.
  • Units of Kp depend directly on the change in gaseous moles (Δng).
  • Temperature increase favors endothermic heterogeneous reactions, raising the value of K.
  • Adding excess solid reactant to a heterogeneous system does not alter the yield of products.
  • Phase equilibrium between ice and liquid water is a classic example of a heterogeneous system.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Including the concentration or partial pressure of a pure solid in the equilibrium constant expression.
  • Forgetting that units of Kc and Kp change depending on stoichiometric coefficients.
  • Assuming that changing the amount of solid reactant will shift the equilibrium position.
  • Failing to convert Celsius to Kelvin when applying the Van 't Hoff equation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Heterogeneous Equilibrium

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Active Mass Convention

Pure solids and liquids maintain constant density and molar concentration. Their activities are therefore standardized to 1 in all equilibrium calculations.

Pressure Equilibrium in Solids

The equilibrium constant for solid-gas systems like NH4HS(s) ↔ NH3(g) + H2S(g) depends exclusively on the partial pressures of the gases: Kp = PNH3PH2S.

Temperature Dependence

Endothermic heterogeneous decompositions experience an increase in equilibrium constant with temperature, whereas exothermic reactions show a decrease.

Application of Le Chatelier Principle

Removing a gaseous product continuously from a heterogeneous equilibrium system forces the solid to dissociate completely.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Heterogeneous Equilibrium (Equilibrium in multi-phase systems)
A chemical equilibrium state involving reactants and products in two or more distinct phases.
Active Mass (Molar concentration [A])
Effective concentration of a substance participating in a chemical reaction, measured in mol dm-3.
Dissociation Pressure (Pgas)
The partial pressure exerted by the gas in equilibrium with a dissociating solid at a given temperature.
Phase Rule (F = C - P + 2)
Gibbs phase rule relating degrees of freedom (F), components (C), and phases (P).
Standard Enthalpy (ΔH°)
Enthalpy change of a reaction at standard state conditions, measured in kJ mol-1.
Gas Constant (R)
Universal gas constant equal to 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1 or 8.314 J K-1 mol-1.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Pure solids and liquids have unit activity ([solid] = 1).
  • For CaCO3(s) ↔ CaO(s) + CO2(g), Kp = PCO2.
  • Kp = Kc(RT)Δng applies strictly to gaseous moles.
  • Vapor pressure of a liquid in a closed container is a heterogeneous equilibrium.
  • Adding solid reactant does not shift equilibrium.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Heterogeneous equilibria span multiple phases with simplified expression terms.
  • Pure condensed phases are omitted from equilibrium constant equations.
  • Pressure constants (Kp) depend solely on gaseous components.
  • Temperature shifts equilibrium according to Van 't Hoff relation.

⚖️ Key Differences

Kc VS Kp

Kc uses concentration terms for gases and aqueous species, whereas Kp uses partial pressures for gases only.

Question 1 of 18

What is the correct expression for Kc for the reaction: C(s) + CO2(g) ↔ 2CO(g)?

Explanation: Pure solid carbon [C(s)] has a constant activity of 1 and is omitted from the equilibrium constant expression.

Question 2 of 18

For the reaction CaCO3(s) ↔ CaO(s) + CO2(g), what is the relationship between Kp and PCO2?

Explanation: Both CaCO3 and CaO are pure solids with activities equal to 1, leaving Kp equal to the partial pressure of CO2.

Question 3 of 18

What happens to the decomposition pressure of CaCO3(s) if the amount of solid CaCO3 in the container is doubled at constant temperature?

Explanation: The dissociation pressure of a solid producing a gas depends only on temperature and is independent of the amount of solid present.

Question 4 of 18

For the heterogeneous equilibrium NH4HS(s) ↔ NH3(g) + H2S(g), if Kp = 9 atm2 at 400 K, what is the equilibrium partial pressure of NH3?

Explanation: Kp = PNH3 × PH2S. Since PNH3 = PH2S = x, x2 = 9, so x = 3.0 atm.

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following is a heterogeneous equilibrium?

Explanation: The reaction involves solid zinc, aqueous ions, and gaseous hydrogen, spanning multiple phases.

Question 6 of 18

What is the value of Δng for the reaction C(s) + H2O(g) ↔ CO(g) + H2(g)?

Explanation: Δng = moles of gaseous products - moles of gaseous reactants = 2 - 1 = 1.

Question 7 of 18

How does an increase in temperature affect the decomposition of an endothermic solid carbonate?

Explanation: According to the Van 't Hoff equation, endothermic reactions (ΔH > 0) have equilibrium constants that increase with temperature.

Question 8 of 18

In the dissociation of solid ammonium carbamate: NH2COONH4(s) ↔ 2NH3(g) + CO2(g), if total pressure at equilibrium is P, what is Kp?

Explanation: Let PCO2 = p, then PNH3 = 2p. Total pressure P = 3p, so p = P/3. Kp = (2p)2(p) = 4p3 = 4(P/3)3 = 4P3 / 27.

Question 9 of 18

Why are pure liquids and solids omitted from equilibrium constant expressions?

Explanation: Pure condensed phases maintain constant density and molar concentration throughout the reaction, so their activity is incorporated into the equilibrium constant value.

Question 10 of 18

If Kc for a heterogeneous reaction is 4.0 at a certain temperature, what is Kp if Δng = 2 and RT = 10 L atm mol-1?

Explanation: Kp = Kc(RT)Δng = 4.0 × (10)2 = 4 × 100 = 400.

Question 11 of 18

Which factor will shift the equilibrium position of Fe(s) + 4H2O(g) ↔ Fe3O4(s) + 4H2(g) to the right?

Explanation: Removing a gaseous product (H2) shifts equilibrium forward according to Le Chatelier's principle.

Question 12 of 18

What are the units of Kp for the reaction CaCO3(s) ↔ CaO(s) + CO2(g)?

Explanation: Kp = PCO2, which has units of pressure (atm).

Question 13 of 18

Liquid water in equilibrium with water vapor in a closed vessel represents which type of equilibrium?

Explanation: H2O(l) ↔ H2O(g) involves two distinct phases (liquid and gas) in a physical phase change.

Question 14 of 18

In the thermal dissociation of solid mercuric oxide: 2HgO(s) ↔ 2Hg(g) + O2(g), the expression for Kp is:

Explanation: Solid HgO is excluded, leaving the product of the partial pressures raised to their stoichiometric coefficients: (PHg)2(PO2).

Question 15 of 18

What is the effect of adding a catalyst on a heterogeneous equilibrium?

Explanation: Catalysts affect forward and reverse reaction rates equally, speeding up the approach to equilibrium without altering K or product yields.

Question 16 of 18

For a heterogeneous system, if the pressure is increased at constant temperature, what happens to the equilibrium of BaO2(s) ↔ BaO(s) + O2(g)?

Explanation: Increasing pressure favors the side with fewer gaseous moles. The forward reaction produces 1 mole of gas, so higher pressure shifts equilibrium to the left.

Question 17 of 18

Which condition is essential for establishing heterogeneous equilibrium in a closed vessel?

Explanation: Heterogeneous equilibrium inherently requires coexisting phases (e.g., solid-gas or liquid-gas) exchanging matter at equal rates.

Question 18 of 18

What is the relationship between Kp and Kc when Δng = 0 in a heterogeneous reaction?

Explanation: When Δng = 0, (RT)0 = 1, so Kp = Kc.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following expressions is correct for the heterogeneous equilibrium established when liquid water vaporizes in a closed vessel at constant temperature?

Solution: Liquid water has unit activity, so the equilibrium constant depends solely on the concentration of water vapor.

Question 2 of 10
Calculate Δng for the heterogeneous reaction: 3Fe(s) + 4H2O(g) ↔ Fe3O4(s) + 4H2(g).

Solution: Gaseous products = 4 moles of H2; gaseous reactants = 4 moles of H2O. Δng = 4 - 4 = 0.

Question 3 of 10
In the thermal dissociation of CaCO3(s) in a closed container, what happens to the partial pressure of CO2 if the temperature is increased?

Solution: CaCO3 decomposition is endothermic, so raising the temperature increases Kp, which equals PCO2.

Question 4 of 10
What is the unit of Kc for the reaction C(s) + CO2(g) ↔ 2CO(g)?

Solution: Kc = [CO]2 / [CO2], which has units of (mol L-1)2 / (mol L-1) = mol L-1.

Question 5 of 10
If Kp for NH4COONH4(s) ↔ 2NH3(g) + CO2(g) is 2.9 × 10-5 atm3 at 300 K, what is the partial pressure of ammonia at equilibrium?

Solution: Kp = 4p3 = 2.9 × 10-5. Solving gives p (PCO2) = 0.019 atm. PNH3 = 2p = 0.038 atm.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following statements is incorrect regarding heterogeneous equilibrium?

Solution: Changing the amount of pure solid does not affect the equilibrium position as long as some solid remains.

Question 7 of 10
For the reaction BaO2(s) ↔ BaO(s) + O2(g), ΔH = +117 kJ. What causes an increase in the yield of oxygen gas?

Solution: Because the reaction is endothermic, increasing the temperature shifts the equilibrium to the right, increasing O2 yield.

Question 8 of 10
What is the relationship between Kp and Kc for the reaction C(s) + H2O(g) ↔ CO(g) + H2(g)?

Solution: For this reaction, Δng = 2 - 1 = 1, so Kp = Kc(RT)1 = Kc(RT).

Question 9 of 10
In a saturated solution of NaCl in contact with excess solid NaCl, the heterogeneous equilibrium is represented by NaCl(s) ↔ Na+(aq) + Cl-(aq). The solubility product Ksp is given by:

Solution: Solid NaCl has unit activity, so Ksp equals the product of the aqueous ion concentrations: [Na+][Cl-].

Question 10 of 10
What is the effect of decreasing the container volume on the equilibrium of CaCO3(s) ↔ CaO(s) + CO2(g) at constant temperature?

Solution: At constant temperature, the equilibrium partial pressure of CO2 is fixed by Kp. Compressing the volume causes some CO2 to recombine with CaO until PCO2 returns to its original equilibrium value.
