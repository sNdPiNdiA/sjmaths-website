Physical Chemistry

Sparingly Soluble Salts

Sparingly soluble salts dissolve to a very small extent in water, establishing a dynamic equilibrium between the undissolved solid and its constituent ions in a saturated solution. The extent of their dissolution is quantitatively expressed by the solubility product constant (Ksp).

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Solubility Equilibrium and Ksp

  • A sparingly soluble salt establishes a heterogeneous equilibrium between the solid phase and aqueous ions.
  • The solubility product constant (Ksp) is the product of molar concentrations of ions raised to their stoichiometric coefficients at equilibrium.
  • Ksp depends strictly on temperature and remains constant regardless of individual ion concentrations until phase equilibrium is disturbed.

Common-Ion Effect on Solubility

  • The addition of a strong electrolyte sharing a common ion suppresses the solubility of a sparingly soluble salt.
  • Le Chatelier's principle dictates that excess common ions shift the equilibrium backward toward the solid precipitate.
  • Quantitative calculation of solubility in the presence of a common ion requires substituting the foreign ion concentration directly into the Ksp expression.

Condition for Precipitation and Fractional Precipitation

  • Precipitation takes place only when the ionic product (IP) exceeds the solubility product constant (IP > Ksp).
  • If IP = Ksp, the solution is exactly saturated and no precipitation occurs.
  • Fractional precipitation separates two ions by gradual addition of a precipitating reagent, where the salt with the smaller Ksp precipitates first.

Relationship Between Solubility (s) and Ksp for AB type
Ksp = s2 ⇒ s = √(Ksp)
Conditions: 1:1 electrolyte (e.g., AgCl, BaSO4)
Units: s in mol dm-3, Ksp in mol2 dm-6
Relationship Between Solubility (s) and Ksp for AB2 type
Ksp = 4s3 ⇒ s = (Ksp / 4)1/3
Conditions: 1:2 electrolyte (e.g., CaF2, PbCl2)
Units: s in mol dm-3, Ksp in mol3 dm-9
Relationship Between Solubility (s) and Ksp for A2B3 type
Ksp = 108s5 ⇒ s = (Ksp / 108)1/5
Conditions: 2:3 electrolyte (e.g., As2S3)
Units: s in mol dm-3, Ksp in mol5 dm-15
Ionic Product and Precipitation Condition
IP > Ksp
Conditions: Precipitation occurs; supersaturated solution
Units: Dimensionless comparison of concentrations
Solubility Product Expression for AxBy
Ksp = [Ay+]x [Bx-]y
Conditions: Saturated solution at constant temperature
Units: mol(x+y) dm-3(x+y)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Salts of strong acids and weak bases dissolve easily in water.

Observation: Heavy metal halides like AgCl, AgBr, AgI, and Hg2Cl2 are extremely sparingly soluble. — High lattice energy due to strong covalent character and polarization (Fajan's rules) overcomes hydration energy.

Exception Solubility of all ionic salts increases uniformly with temperature.

Observation: Certain salts like CaSO4 and Ca(OH)2 exhibit anomalous decreases in solubility at elevated temperatures. — Dissolution of these salts is exothermic (ΔH is negative), shifting equilibrium unfavorably at higher temperatures.

Exception Adding a common ion always reduces solubility predictably.

Observation: High concentrations of common ions can sometimes increase solubility via complex ion formation (e.g., AgCl in concentrated NaCl forms [AgCl2]-). — Formation of soluble complex species consumes free metal ions, driving more solid salt to dissolve.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The solubility of AgCl in pure water is approximately 1.34 × 10-5 mol dm-3 at 298 K.
  • Units of Ksp vary depending on the stoichiometric coefficients of the dissociated ions.
  • H2S precipitation in qualitative analysis utilizes pH control to adjust sulfide ion concentration via common-ion effect with HCl.
  • Alkaline earth metal sulfates (MgSO4 to BaSO4) show decreasing solubility down the group due to rapid decrease in hydration energy compared to lattice energy.
  • Silver halides (AgCl, AgBr, AgI) show decreasing solubility from chloride to iodide due to increasing covalent character.
  • The addition of NH4OH to a solution containing Mg2+ and NH4Cl prevents precipitation of Mg(OH)2 by suppressing [OH-].
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing solubility (expressed in g L-1 or mol L-1) with solubility product (Ksp, which is a dimensionless equilibrium constant based on standard states).
  • Failing to multiply the ion concentration by its stoichiometric coefficient as an exponent in the Ksp expression.
  • Ignoring the effect of hydrolysis of anions (like CO32- or S2-) when calculating theoretical solubility.
  • Assuming Ksp changes with the addition of a common ion; Ksp is strictly constant at constant temperature.

⚡ Quick Revision: Sparingly Soluble Salts

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Solubility Product Principle

The solubility product constant Ksp governs the equilibrium between solid salt and solvated ions in saturated solutions.

Common-Ion Effect Application

Introduction of a common ion decreases solubility by shifting equilibrium toward the undissolved salt according to Le Chatelier's principle.

Precipitation Criteria

Precipitation occurs only when the ionic product exceeds the solubility product constant (IP > Ksp).

Qualitative Analysis Significance

Group separation of cations relies on selective precipitation by controlling reagent concentrations and pH.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Solubility (s)
Maximum amount of solute dissolved per unit volume in a saturated solution at a given temperature; mol dm-3.
Solubility Product (Ksp)
Equilibrium constant for the dissolution of a sparingly soluble salt; variable units.
Ionic Product (IP)
Product of ionic concentrations at any non-equilibrium condition; dimensionless-based.
Common-Ion Effect (Suppression of s)
Reduction of solubility of a salt by adding an electrolyte with a common ion.
Supersaturated Solution (IP > Ksp)
Metastable solution containing more dissolved solute than equilibrium saturation.
Fractional Precipitation (Selective separation)
Sequential precipitation of different ions from solution by controlled reagent addition.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ksp for AB type = s2
  • Ksp for AB2 type = 4s3
  • Ksp for A2B3 type = 108s5
  • Condition for precipitation: IP > Ksp
  • Ksp depends only on temperature

📌 Quick Recall

  • Sparingly soluble salts maintain heterogeneous equilibrium in saturated solutions.
  • Ksp expression is derived from equilibrium ion concentrations raised to stoichiometric powers.
  • Common ions suppress solubility via equilibrium shift.
  • Precipitation requires ionic product to exceed Ksp.

⚖️ Key Differences

Solubility (s) VS Solubility Product (Ksp)

Solubility is the actual concentration of dissolved solute (mol L-1), whereas Ksp is the product of ion concentrations raised to stoichiometric powers.

Question 1 of 18

What is the correct relationship between solubility (s) and solubility product (Ksp) for Ag2CrO4?

Explanation: Ag2CrO4 dissociates into 2Ag+ and CrO42- (1:2 electrolyte). Thus, Ksp = (2s)2(s) = 4s3.

Question 2 of 18

Precipitation of a sparingly soluble salt occurs when:

Explanation: Precipitation takes place only when the ionic product (IP) exceeds the solubility product (Ksp).

Question 3 of 18

The solubility of which salt is most affected by the addition of 0.1 M HCl?

Explanation: AgCl contains Cl-, which is a common ion with HCl, drastically reducing its solubility due to the common-ion effect.

Question 4 of 18

If the solubility product of CaF2 is 3.2 × 10-11, its solubility in mol L-1 is:

Explanation: Ksp = 4s3 ⇒ s = (3.2 × 10-11 / 4)1/3 = (8 × 10-12)1/3 = 2.0 × 10-4 mol L-1.

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following factors affects the value of the solubility product (Ksp)?

Explanation: Ksp is an equilibrium constant and depends exclusively on temperature.

Question 6 of 18

The units of solubility product for Al(OH)3 are:

Explanation: Al(OH)3 ⇒ Al3+ + 3OH-. Ksp = [Al3+][OH-]3, giving units of (mol L-1)4 = mol4 L-4.

Question 7 of 18

Why is H2S gas passed through an acidic solution of copper sulfide for precipitation?

Explanation: Adding HCl provides H+ ions, suppressing the dissociation of H2S and lowering [S2-] so only highly insoluble sulfides precipitate.

Question 8 of 18

What happens to the solubility of BaSO4 when Na2SO4 is added to its saturated solution?

Explanation: Addition of Na2SO4 provides common SO42- ions, shifting equilibrium backward and decreasing BaSO4 solubility.

Question 9 of 18

The solubility product of AgCl is 1.8 × 10-10. What is its solubility in pure water?

Explanation: For AgCl, Ksp = s2 ⇒ s = √(1.8 × 10-10) = 1.34 × 10-5 mol L-1.

Question 10 of 18

In fractional precipitation, which salt precipitates first?

Explanation: The salt with the lowest Ksp requires the minimum concentration of the precipitating ion to exceed Ksp, hence precipitating first.

Question 11 of 18

What is the relationship between solubility (s) and Ksp for Bi2S3?

Explanation: Bi2S3 ⇒ 2Bi3+ + 3S2-. Ksp = (2s)2(3s)3 = 4s3 × 27s3 = 108s5.

Question 12 of 18

Which of the following compounds is least soluble in water?

Explanation: AgI has the smallest solubility product and lowest solubility among silver halides due to maximum covalent character.

Question 13 of 18

The addition of NH4Cl to NH4OH solution decreases the concentration of:

Explanation: Common ion NH4+ from NH4Cl suppresses the dissociation of weak base NH4OH, reducing [OH-].

Question 14 of 18

If IP = Ksp, the solution is:

Explanation: When IP = Ksp, the system is at equilibrium in a saturated solution.

Question 15 of 18

Which salt's solubility increases with an increase in temperature due to an endothermic dissolution process?

Explanation: KNO3 dissolution is endothermic (ΔH > 0), so its solubility increases with temperature according to Le Chatelier's principle.

Question 16 of 18

What is the solubility of CaSO4 if its Ksp is 9 × 10-6?

Explanation: For CaSO4 (1:1), Ksp = s2 ⇒ s = √(9 × 10-6) = 3 × 10-3 mol L-1.

Question 17 of 18

The concept of solubility product is applicable to:

Explanation: Ksp is specifically applied to sparingly soluble salts where equilibrium is established between solid and ions.

Question 18 of 18

What is the unit of solubility (s)?

Explanation: Solubility represents molar concentration in a saturated solution, expressed in mol L-1 (or mol dm-3).

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the solubility of AgCl in 0.01 M CaCl2 solution given Ksp of AgCl is 1.8 × 10-10.

Solution: [Cl-] from 0.01 M CaCl2 is 2 × 0.01 = 0.02 M. Ksp = [Ag+][Cl-] ⇒ 1.8 × 10-10 = [Ag+](0.02) ⇒ [Ag+] = 9.0 × 10-9 mol L-1 (approx 1.8 × 10-8 considering fractional contributions).

Question 2 of 10
What is the maximum concentration of Ag+ ions possible in a solution where [Cl-] = 0.1 M and Ksp of AgCl = 1.8 × 10-10?

Solution: [Ag+] = Ksp / [Cl-] = (1.8 × 10-10) / 0.1 = 1.8 × 10-9 M.

Question 3 of 10
The solubility product of Ca3(PO4)2 is given in terms of solubility (s) as:

Solution: Ca3(PO4)2 ⇒ 3Ca2+ + 2PO43-. Ksp = (3s)3(2s)2 = 27s3 × 4s2 = 108s5.

Question 4 of 10
Which salt will have the highest solubility in pure water among the following given their Ksp values?

Solution: Assuming identical 1:1 stoichiometry, a higher Ksp directly indicates higher solubility.

Question 5 of 10
A solution contains 0.08 M Ni2+ and 0.02 M Cu2+. Can they be separated by selective precipitation with sulfide if Ksp(CuS) = 8 × 10-36 and Ksp(NiS) = 1 × 10-21?

Solution: CuS has a much smaller Ksp than NiS, allowing CuS to precipitate selectively at lower sulfide concentrations.

Question 6 of 10
What is the pH of a saturated solution of Mg(OH)2 given Ksp = 1.0 × 10-11 and s = 1.4 × 10-4 mol L-1?

Solution: [OH-] = 2s = 2 × 1.4 × 10-4 = 2.8 × 10-4 M. pOH = -log(2.8 × 10-4) ≈ 3.55. pH = 14 - 3.55 = 10.45.

Question 7 of 10
The solubility of AgBr in water is 7.1 × 10-7 mol L-1. Its Ksp is:

Solution: Ksp = s2 = (7.1 × 10-7)2 = 5.04 × 10-13 ≈ 5.0 × 10-13.

Question 8 of 10
Addition of excess NH3 to AgCl precipitate results in:

Solution: AgCl dissolves in aqueous ammonia because it forms a soluble complex ion, [Ag(NH3)2]+.

Question 9 of 10
If the ionic product of BaSO4 is 2.5 × 10-9 and its Ksp is 1.1 × 10-10, the solution is:

Solution: Since IP (2.5 × 10-9) > Ksp (1.1 × 10-10), the solution is supersaturated and precipitation occurs.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following sulfides is precipitated in neutral or alkaline medium (Group IV)?

Solution: ZnS has a relatively higher Ksp among metal sulfides, requiring a higher [S2-] achieved in alkaline medium (Group IV) to precipitate.
