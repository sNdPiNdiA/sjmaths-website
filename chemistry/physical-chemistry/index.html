Physical Chemistry

Chemical Thermodynamics

Chemical thermodynamics describes the energy changes and spontaneity of chemical and physical processes based on the laws of conservation of energy and entropy. It relates macroscopic properties such as enthalpy, entropy, and Gibbs free energy to determine the equilibrium state of a system.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

State Functions and Path Functions

  • State functions like Pressure (P), Volume (V), Temperature (T), and Internal Energy (U) depend only on the initial and final states.
  • Path functions such as Work (w) and Heat (q) depend on the specific path taken between states.
  • The cyclic integral of any state function over a closed path is exactly zero.
  • Exact differentials represent state functions, while inexact differentials represent path functions.

Spontaneity and Equilibrium

  • A process is spontaneous if ΔG < 0 at constant temperature and pressure.
  • At equilibrium, ΔG = 0, meaning the forward and reverse reaction rates are equal.
  • The relationship ΔG° = -RT ln K relates the standard Gibbs energy change to the equilibrium constant.
  • Entropy of the universe (ΔSuniv) must be > 0 for any spontaneous process.

Thermochemistry Principles

  • Hess's Law states that the total enthalpy change for a reaction is the same regardless of the number of steps.
  • Standard enthalpy of formation (ΔHf°) is defined for the formation of one mole of a substance from its elements in their standard states.
  • Kirchhoff's Law describes the temperature dependence of enthalpy: ΔHT2 = ΔHT1 + ΔCp(T2 - T1).

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Entropy increases in all spontaneous processes

Observation: Formation of ordered crystals from solution — The entropy of the surroundings increases more than the system's entropy decreases, keeping ΔStotal > 0.

Exception Ideal gas internal energy depends only on temperature

Observation: Real gases — Intermolecular forces in real gases cause internal energy to depend on volume (pressure).

Exception Heat capacity is constant

Observation: Temperature-dependent heat capacity — Vibrational modes of molecules become active at higher temperatures, increasing Cp.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Gibbs Sign Convention
G = H - TS

Application: If ΔH is (-) and ΔS is (+), the reaction is spontaneous at all temperatures.

Work Done Mnemonic
w = -PextΔV

Application: Work done ON the system is positive (+), work done BY the system is negative (-).

Hess's Law Shortcut
ΔHrxn = ΣΔHf°(products) - ΣΔHf°(reactants)

Application: Always multiply by stoichiometric coefficients before subtracting.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • For an adiabatic process, q = 0.
  • For an isothermal reversible expansion of an ideal gas, ΔU = 0.
  • The third law of thermodynamics states that the entropy of a perfect crystal at 0 K is zero.
  • Standard state pressure is defined as 1 bar.
  • Enthalpy of combustion is always negative for exothermic reactions.
  • Joule-Thomson coefficient μJT = (∂T/∂P)H.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing ΔH and ΔU; remember ΔH = ΔU + Δ(PV).
  • Forgetting to convert temperature to Kelvin in thermodynamic equations.
  • Assuming ΔG = ΔG° at non-standard conditions; use ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q.
  • Incorrect sign convention for work in physics vs chemistry textbooks.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Thermodynamics

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

First Law

Energy is conserved; ΔU = q + w. It defines internal energy as a state function.

Second Law

Entropy of the universe increases for spontaneous processes. ΔSuniv = ΔSsys + ΔSsurr ≥ 0.

Third Law

Entropy of a pure crystalline substance at absolute zero is zero. Provides a reference for absolute entropy.

Gibbs Energy

Combines enthalpy and entropy to predict spontaneity. ΔG = ΔH - TΔS.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Enthalpy (H)
Total heat content of a system.
Entropy (S)
Measure of molecular disorder.
Gibbs Energy (G)
Available energy for work.
Internal Energy (U)
Sum of all microscopic energies.
Heat Capacity (C)
Energy to raise temperature by 1K.
Standard State (P° = 1 bar)
Reference state for thermodynamic data.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • R = 8.314 J/K·mol
  • ΔG = -nFE°cell
  • ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT
  • Cp - Cv = R
  • ΔS = nCpln(T2/T1)

📌 Quick Recall

  • Energy is conserved in all processes.
  • Entropy drives spontaneity.
  • Gibbs energy determines equilibrium.
  • Hess's law allows enthalpy calculation.

Isothermal VS Adiabatic

Isothermal implies ΔT=0, Adiabatic implies q=0.
