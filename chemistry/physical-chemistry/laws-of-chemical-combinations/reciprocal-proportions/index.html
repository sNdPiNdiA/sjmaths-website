Law of Reciprocal Proportions

The Law of Reciprocal Proportions states that if two different elements combine separately with a fixed mass of a third element, the ratio of their masses in these combinations is either the same or a simple multiple of the ratio in which they combine with each other. This law serves as a fundamental principle for determining equivalent weights of elements.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Fundamental Principles of Reciprocal Proportions

  • The law is also known as the Law of Equivalent Proportions.
  • It provides a basis for the concept of equivalent weight.
  • It applies to three elements A, B, and C where A and B react with C to form AC and BC, and with each other to form AB.
  • The ratio of masses of A and B that react with a fixed mass of C is related to the ratio of masses of A and B in compound AB.

Quantitative Verification

  • Consider H2S, H2O, and SO2; the ratio of S:O in SO2 is consistent with the ratio of S:O derived from their respective reactions with H.
  • The law holds true for covalent and ionic compounds alike.
  • It is a direct consequence of the Law of Definite Proportions.
  • Verification requires accurate determination of mass percentages in binary compounds.

Scope and Limitations

  • The law is strictly applicable to binary compounds.
  • It does not apply to complex mixtures or non-stoichiometric compounds.
  • It is essential for calculating equivalent masses of elements that do not react directly with each other.
  • The integer 'n' must be a small whole number, typically 1, 2, or 3.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Equivalent Weight Relation
Eq. Wt. = Atomic Weight / Valency
Conditions: Applicable for elements reacting to form compounds.
Units: Dimensionless (relative scale).
Law of Combining Weights
Mass A / Mass B = Eq. Wt. A / Eq. Wt. B
Conditions: Valid for any chemical reaction between two elements.
Units: Mass in grams (g).
Stoichiometric Ratio
n = (Mass A / Mass B)1 / (Mass A / Mass B)2
Conditions: Where n is a small integer (1, 2, 3...).
Units: Dimensionless.
Mass Ratio Equality
Ratio1 = (Mass A / Mass B)in compound AB = n × (Mass A / Mass B)in separate compounds with C
Conditions: Applicable to three elements forming binary compounds.
Units: Mass in grams (g).
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Fixed integer ratio

Observation: Non-stoichiometric compounds — Defects in crystal lattice structure lead to variable composition.

Exception Binary compound formation

Observation: Isotopes — Different atomic masses of isotopes can lead to slight variations in mass ratios.

Exception Chemical combination

Observation: Noble gases — Lack of chemical reactivity prevents formation of standard binary compounds.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
The Triangle Method
Place elements at vertices of a triangle; sides represent binary compounds.

Application: Verify mass ratios along the sides to ensure the product of ratios equals the third side ratio.

Equivalent Weight Shortcut
Mass A / Mass B = Eq. Wt. A / Eq. Wt. B

Application: Use this to bypass complex stoichiometry when equivalent weights are known.

Integer Check
Ratio 1 / Ratio 2 = Integer

Application: Always divide the larger ratio by the smaller one to find the integer 'n'.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The law was proposed by Jeremias Richter in 1792.
  • It is also known as the Law of Equivalent Proportions.
  • It helps in calculating the equivalent weight of elements.
  • The ratio of masses is always a simple multiple.
  • It is valid for binary compounds only.
  • It is a corollary of the Law of Definite Proportions.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the Law of Reciprocal Proportions with the Law of Multiple Proportions.
  • Failing to convert mass ratios to the same fixed mass of the third element.
  • Assuming the integer 'n' must always be 1.
  • Ignoring the requirement for binary compounds.

⚡ Quick Revision: Law of Reciprocal Proportions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Equivalent Weight

The mass of an element that combines with or displaces 1.008g of Hydrogen or 8g of Oxygen.

Binary Compounds

Compounds consisting of exactly two different elements, essential for the application of this law.

Mass Ratios

The ratio of masses of two elements reacting with a fixed mass of a third element.

Stoichiometric Verification

The process of comparing experimental mass ratios to theoretical integer multiples.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Law of Reciprocal Proportions = Law of Equivalent Proportions
  • Applicable to binary compounds
  • Ratio of masses is a simple multiple
  • Basis for equivalent weight
  • Proposed by Richter

📌 Quick Recall

  • Three elements involved
  • Fixed mass of one element
  • Simple integer ratio
  • Binary compound constraint

⚖️ Key Differences

Reciprocal Proportions VS Multiple Proportions

Reciprocal involves 3 elements; Multiple involves 2 elements forming multiple compounds.
