Law of Multiple Proportions

The Law of Multiple Proportions states that when two elements combine to form more than one compound, the masses of one element that combine with a fixed mass of the other are in the ratio of small whole numbers. This law, proposed by John Dalton, serves as a fundamental principle for understanding stoichiometry and atomic theory.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Fundamental Stoichiometric Principles

  • The law applies only when two elements form multiple distinct compounds.
  • It provides evidence for the atomic nature of matter by requiring discrete integer ratios.
  • It is a direct consequence of the Law of Conservation of Mass and Dalton's atomic theory.

Quantitative Analysis of Binary Compounds

  • To verify the law, calculate the mass of element X that reacts with 1 gram of element Y in each compound.
  • The resulting values for element X must form a simple integer ratio (e.g., 1:2, 2:3).
  • Non-stoichiometric compounds (e.g., Fe0.95O) do not follow this law due to lattice defects.

Experimental Verification

  • Common examples include oxides of nitrogen (N2O, NO, NO2, N2O5).
  • Carbon oxides (CO and CO2) demonstrate a mass ratio of 1:2 for oxygen.
  • Sulfur oxides (SO2 and SO3) show oxygen mass ratios of 2:3 for a fixed mass of sulfur.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Stoichiometric Ratio
n1 : n2 = x : y
Conditions: Applicable to binary compounds of elements A and B.
Units: Dimensionless ratio
Dalton's Atomic Postulate
A + B → AB or AB2
Conditions: Conservation of mass applies to all chemical reactions.
Units: Atomic mass units (amu)
Equivalent Mass Relation
EA = (Mass of A / Mass of B) × EB
Conditions: Based on fixed mass of reference element B.
Units: g/equivalent
Mass Ratio Calculation
Ratio = (mA1 / mB) : (mA2 / mB)
Conditions: Mass of element B is kept constant in both compounds.
Units: Mass in grams (g)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Fixed integer ratios in compounds

Observation: Non-stoichiometric compounds (Bertollides) — Presence of metal deficiency or interstitial defects in the crystal lattice.

Exception Discrete molecular compounds

Observation: Polymeric substances — Variable chain lengths prevent fixed mass ratios between constituent elements.

Exception Simple whole number ratios

Observation: Isotopically enriched samples — Variation in average atomic mass alters the mass ratio calculations.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Fixed Mass Method
Divide mass of element A by mass of element B in each compound.

Application: Normalize the mass of one element to 1g to see the ratio of the second element immediately.

The Oxide Check
Compare oxygen content for fixed metal mass.

Application: Quickly identify if two metal oxides follow the law by checking the ratio of oxygen atoms.

Valency Shortcut
Ratio = Valency1 : Valency2

Application: For binary compounds, the mass ratio of the variable element is often the inverse ratio of its valencies.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Proposed by John Dalton in 1803.
  • Applies to compounds like H2O and H2O2.
  • Requires fixed mass of one element for comparison.
  • Ratio of masses must be small integers.
  • Does not apply to mixtures or solutions.
  • Validates the existence of atoms.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Law of Multiple Proportions with Law of Definite Proportions.
  • Failing to fix the mass of one element before calculating the ratio.
  • Assuming the law applies to compounds with more than two elements.
  • Ignoring non-stoichiometric compounds in advanced questions.

⚡ Quick Revision: Law of Multiple Proportions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Dalton's Contribution

Dalton used this law to support the idea that elements combine in discrete atomic units.

Mass Normalization

The core procedure involves dividing the mass of the variable element by the fixed mass of the reference element.

Integer Ratios

The resulting ratios must be simple integers like 1:1, 1:2, or 2:3.

Scope of Application

Limited to binary compounds formed by the same two elements.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Law applies to CO and CO2.
  • Ratio of Oxygen in CO/CO2 is 1:2.
  • Law supports atomic theory.
  • Requires two elements only.
  • Mass ratio is always simple.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Two elements form multiple compounds.
  • Fixed mass of one element.
  • Variable element in simple ratio.
  • Supports discrete atomic nature.

⚖️ Key Differences

Multiple Proportions VS Definite Proportions

Multiple involves >1 compound; Definite involves 1 compound.
