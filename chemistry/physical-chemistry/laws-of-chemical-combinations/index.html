Physical Chemistry

Laws of Chemical Combinations

The laws of chemical combinations govern the quantitative relationships between reactants and products in chemical reactions. These fundamental principles provide the basis for stoichiometry and the calculation of mass and volume changes during chemical transformations.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mass and Definite Proportions

  • The Law of Conservation of Mass states that matter is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.
  • The Law of Definite Proportions (Proust's Law) dictates that a given compound always contains elements in a fixed mass ratio.
  • Experimental verification requires high-purity samples to avoid deviation from stoichiometric ratios.

Multiple and Reciprocal Proportions

  • The Law of Multiple Proportions applies when two elements combine to form more than one compound, resulting in simple integer mass ratios.
  • The Law of Reciprocal Proportions states that if two elements combine separately with a third, their ratio of combination is the same or a simple multiple of their direct combination ratio.
  • These laws are foundational for determining equivalent weights of elements.

Gaseous Volume Relations

  • Gay-Lussac's Law relates the volumes of reacting gases as small whole numbers at constant T and P.
  • Avogadro's Law bridges the gap by stating equal volumes of all gases at the same T and P contain equal numbers of molecules.
  • These laws are strictly applicable to ideal gases and approximate for real gases at low pressure.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Law of Definite Proportions
mA / mB = constant
Conditions: Pure chemical compound
Units: Mass in grams (g)
Law of Multiple Proportions
mA1 / mA2 = n1 / n2
Conditions: Two elements forming multiple compounds
Units: Mass ratios as integers
Gay-Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes
V1 / V2 = n1 / n2
Conditions: Constant temperature and pressure
Units: Volume in Liters (L) or mL
Law of Conservation of Mass
Massreactants = Massproducts
Conditions: Closed system, non-nuclear reactions
Units: Mass in grams (g)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Law of Conservation of Mass

Observation: Nuclear reactions — Mass-energy equivalence (E=mc²) results in measurable mass loss converted to energy.

Exception Law of Definite Proportions

Observation: Non-stoichiometric compounds (e.g., Fe0.95O) — Defects in crystal lattice structures lead to variable elemental composition.

Exception Law of Multiple Proportions

Observation: Isotopically enriched samples — Varying isotopic abundance changes the average atomic mass, affecting mass ratios.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Lavoisier proposed the Law of Conservation of Mass in 1789.
  • Proust proposed the Law of Definite Proportions.
  • Dalton proposed the Law of Multiple Proportions.
  • Richter proposed the Law of Reciprocal Proportions.
  • Gay-Lussac's Law is only valid for gaseous reactants and products.
  • Avogadro's hypothesis explains Gay-Lussac's law using molecular theory.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Applying Law of Multiple Proportions to compounds with different elements; it only applies to the same two elements.
  • Ignoring the physical state of reactants in Gay-Lussac's Law; it applies only to gases.
  • Assuming non-stoichiometric compounds follow the Law of Definite Proportions.
  • Confusing Law of Multiple Proportions with Law of Reciprocal Proportions.

⚡ Quick Revision: Laws of Chemical Combinations

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Mass Conservation

Total mass of reactants equals total mass of products in a closed system. Fundamental for balancing chemical equations.

Definite Proportions

Compounds have a constant elemental composition by mass regardless of the source or method of preparation.

Multiple Proportions

When two elements form multiple compounds, the masses of one element combining with a fixed mass of the other are in simple ratio.

Gaseous Volumes

Gases react in volumes that bear a simple ratio to one another and to the product if gaseous, at constant T and P.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Stoichiometry (Greek: stoicheion (element))
Quantitative study of reactants and products.
Mole (n)
Amount of substance containing 6.022 × 1023 particles.
Atomic Mass (u)
Mass of an atom relative to 1/12th of C-12.
Molecular Mass (M)
Sum of atomic masses of atoms in a molecule.
Equivalent Mass (E)
Mass of element displacing 1.008g of Hydrogen.
Closed System (Isolated)
System where no matter enters or leaves.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Conservation of Mass: Massin = Massout
  • Definite Proportions: Fixed mass ratio
  • Multiple Proportions: Simple integer ratio
  • Gay-Lussac: Volume ratio = Mole ratio
  • Avogadro: V ∝ n

📌 Quick Recall

  • Laws provide the quantitative framework for chemistry.
  • Conservation of mass is the basis for balancing equations.
  • Definite and multiple proportions define compound identity.
  • Gaseous laws link volume to molecular count.

⚖️ Key Differences

Multiple Proportions VS Reciprocal Proportions

Multiple involves two elements; Reciprocal involves three elements.

Which law states that matter is neither created nor destroyed?

Who proposed the Law of Multiple Proportions?

Explanation: John Dalton proposed the Law of Multiple Proportions in 1803.

Which law is applicable to gaseous reactions?

Explanation: Gay-Lussac's Law specifically relates the volumes of gases in chemical reactions.

Question 4 of 19

What is the ratio of masses of oxygen that combine with a fixed mass of carbon in CO and CO2?

Explanation: In CO, 12g C reacts with 16g O. In CO2, 12g C reacts with 32g O. Ratio is 16:32 = 1:2.

Question 5 of 19

Which law is the basis for the concept of equivalent weight?

Explanation: The Law of Reciprocal Proportions allows for the determination of equivalent weights.

Question 6 of 19

Does the Law of Conservation of Mass hold in nuclear reactions?

Explanation: In nuclear reactions, mass is converted to energy according to E=mc².

Question 7 of 19

What does Avogadro's law state?

Explanation: Avogadro's law states that equal volumes of gases at constant T and P contain equal numbers of molecules.

Question 8 of 19

Which compound is non-stoichiometric?

Question 9 of 19

If 12g of Carbon reacts with 32g of Oxygen to form CO2, what law is demonstrated?

Explanation: This demonstrates the fixed mass ratio required for a specific compound.

Question 10 of 19

What is the simple ratio of volumes of H2 and O2 to form H2O?

Which law is also known as the Law of Constant Composition?

Explanation: Proust's Law of Definite Proportions is also called the Law of Constant Composition.

Question 12 of 19

Which of the following is not a law of chemical combination?

Question 14 of 19

What is the mass ratio of H to O in water?

Question 15 of 19

Who formulated the Law of Reciprocal Proportions?

Explanation: Jeremias Richter formulated the Law of Reciprocal Proportions.

Question 16 of 19

Which law relates to the volumes of gases?

Explanation: Gay-Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes.

Question 17 of 19

If 10L of H2 reacts with 5L of O2, how much H2O vapor is formed?

Explanation: 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O. 10L H2 produces 10L H2O.

Explanation: Isotopes do not violate these laws; they are accounted for in average atomic mass.
