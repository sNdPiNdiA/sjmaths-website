Mitscherlich's Law of Isomorphism

Mitscherlich's law states that substances which crystallize in the same geometric form possess similar chemical compositions and analogous atomic ratios. It provides a method for determining atomic weights by comparing the composition of isomorphous compounds.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Principles of Isomorphism

  • Isomorphous substances exhibit identical crystal lattice structures despite different chemical identities.
  • The law requires the atomic ratio of constituent elements to be identical in the compounds.
  • It serves as a tool to verify atomic weights when the valency of an element is uncertain.

Structural Requirements

  • Compounds must have the same stoichiometric proportions of atoms.
  • The ionic radii of the substituting ions must be sufficiently similar to fit into the same crystal lattice.
  • Common examples include Alums (KAl(SO4)2·12H2O) and Tutton's salts.

Analytical Applications

  • Used to determine the valency of an element by comparing its oxide with a known isomorphous oxide.
  • Helps in identifying solid solutions where one ion replaces another in a crystal structure.
  • The law is limited to crystalline solids and does not apply to amorphous substances.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Isomorphous Ratio
n1 / n2 = constant
Conditions: Ratio of atoms in the empirical formula must be identical.
Units: n = number of atoms
Equivalent Weight Calculation
E = Atomic Weight / Valency
Conditions: Applicable to elements forming isomorphous oxides.
Units: E in g/eq
Molecular Formula Relation
M = n × E
Conditions: Where n is the valency of the element.
Units: M in g/mol
Atomic Weight Determination
W1 / W2 = E1 / E2
Conditions: Applicable to isomorphous salts with known equivalent weights.
Units: W = Atomic weight, E = Equivalent weight (dimensionless ratio)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Isomorphous substances have similar formulas.

Observation: Polymorphism — The same substance can crystallize in different forms under different temperature or pressure conditions.

Exception Similar ionic radii lead to isomorphism.

Observation: Size mismatch — If the difference in ionic radii exceeds 15%, the lattice stability is compromised, preventing isomorphism.

Exception Isomorphous compounds have similar chemical properties.

Observation: Chemical reactivity — Structural similarity does not guarantee identical chemical reactivity, as electronic configurations may differ.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Mitscherlich's law was proposed in 1819.
  • Isomorphism is observed in compounds with similar atomic ratios.
  • Alums are the most common examples of isomorphous series.
  • The law helps in calculating atomic weights of elements.
  • Ionic radii similarity is a prerequisite for isomorphism.
  • Polymorphism is the opposite phenomenon of isomorphism.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing isomorphism with polymorphism; remember isomorphism involves different substances.
  • Assuming all compounds with similar formulas are isomorphous; crystal structure must also match.
  • Ignoring the role of water of crystallization in alums.
  • Applying the law to amorphous substances where no crystal lattice exists.

⚡ Quick Revision: Mitscherlich's Law of Isomorphism

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Atomic Ratio Principle

Compounds with identical atomic ratios and similar chemical formulas exhibit isomorphism. This allows for the determination of unknown atomic weights.

Crystal Lattice Stability

Isomorphism depends on the ability of ions to substitute each other in a lattice without distorting the structure significantly.

Valency Determination

By comparing the mass of an element in an isomorphous oxide with a known standard, the valency can be calculated using equivalent weights.

Structural Constraints

The law is strictly limited to crystalline solids and does not account for variations in bonding types like covalent vs ionic.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Different substances having the same crystal structure.
Polymorphism (Many forms)
One substance existing in multiple crystal forms.
Alum (MIMIII(SO4)2·12H2O)
A class of isomorphous double salts.
Atomic Ratio (n1:n2)
The ratio of atoms in a chemical formula.
Equivalent Weight (E)
Atomic weight divided by valency.
Crystal Lattice (Lattice)
The ordered 3D arrangement of atoms in a solid.

  • Ionic radii must be similar for substitution.
  • Law is used for atomic weight determination.
  • Polymorphism is the inverse of isomorphism.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Mitscherlich's law links crystal form to chemical composition.
  • It is a key tool for verifying atomic weights.
  • Structural similarity is the core requirement.
  • It applies only to crystalline substances.

⚖️ Key Differences

Isomorphism VS Isomerism

Isomorphism relates to crystal structure of different substances; Isomerism relates to molecular structure of the same formula.
