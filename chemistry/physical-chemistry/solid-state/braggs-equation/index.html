Bragg's Equation

Bragg's equation relates the wavelength of electromagnetic radiation to the lattice spacing and the angle of incidence for constructive interference in crystalline solids. It is expressed mathematically as nλ = 2d sin(θ).

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Derivation and Geometric Principles of X-Ray Diffraction

  • Path difference between waves reflected from adjacent crystal planes of spacing d is given by 2d sin(θ).
  • Constructive interference occurs when the path difference is an integral multiple of the wavelength (nλ).
  • The angle θ is measured between the incident beam and the crystal plane, known as the Bragg angle.
  • The order of diffraction n represents the integer multiples of the wavelength for a given set of planes.

Interplanar Spacing and Miller Indices Relations

  • In a cubic crystal system, the interplanar spacing d depends on the unit cell dimension a and Miller indices (h k l).
  • For a simple cubic lattice, all combinations of (h k l) are permitted.
  • For a Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) lattice, reflections are observed only when the sum of Miller indices (h + k + l) is even.
  • For a Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) lattice, Miller indices h, k, l must be all odd or all even for constructive interference.

Experimental Limitations and Applications

  • The maximum possible value of sin(θ) is 1, which sets a theoretical limit for detecting diffraction where λ ≤ 2d.
  • X-rays are typically used because their wavelength is comparable to interatomic distances (1 to 2 Å).
  • Bragg's equation is fundamental for determining crystal structures, bond lengths, and atomic radii using X-ray crystallography.

Bragg's Law

Units: n (integer), λ (wavelength, m or Å), d (interplanar distance, m or Å), θ (Bragg angle, degrees)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception All crystal planes should yield diffraction peaks for any integer n.

Observation: Systematic absences occur in centered lattices like BCC and FCC. — Destructive interference caused by scattering from lattice-centering atoms cancels specific (h k l) reflections.

Exception Bragg angle θ can take any value between 0 and 90 degrees.

Observation: Practical diffraction is limited by the condition nλ ≤ 2d. — Since sin(θ) cannot exceed 1, wavelengths greater than 2d cannot be diffracted at any angle.

Exception Interplanar spacing depends solely on crystal density.

Observation: Different crystallographic planes have unique d-spacings based on orientation. — Planes with higher Miller indices have smaller interplanar distances due to tighter geometric packing.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Bragg's equation is expressed as nλ = 2d sin(θ).
  • X-rays are used in diffraction because their wavelength matches interatomic spacings in crystals.
  • For first-order diffraction, n = 1, simplifying the equation to λ = 2d sin(θ).
  • In BCC lattices, reflections where (h + k + l) is odd are systematically absent.
  • In FCC lattices, mixed Miller indices (some odd, some even) result in zero intensity peaks.
  • The maximum value of the Bragg angle θ is 90 degrees, where sin(θ) = 1.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the Bragg angle θ with the angle between the incident and reflected beam (2θ).
  • Assuming all Miller index combinations produce peaks in BCC and FCC lattices, ignoring systematic absences.
  • Using inconsistent units for wavelength (λ) and interplanar spacing (d).
  • Treating the diffraction order 'n' as a variable without realizing it must be an integer.

⚡ Quick Revision: Bragg's Equation

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Bragg's Law Fundamentals

Bragg's equation nλ = 2d sin(θ) governs the condition for constructive interference of X-rays scattered by parallel planes in a crystal.

Interplanar Spacing and Unit Cell Geometry

The distance between adjacent planes (d) is geometrically linked to the unit cell parameter (a) and Miller indices (h k l) for cubic systems.

Systematic Absences in XRD

Centered lattices exhibit destructive interference for specific Miller index combinations, enabling identification of crystal lattice types.

Experimental Constraints

Diffraction requires wavelengths comparable to interplanar distances, restricted by the mathematical limit where sin(θ) cannot exceed unity.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Bragg Angle (θ)
Angle between the incident X-ray beam and the scattering crystal plane.
Interplanar Spacing (d)
Perpendicular distance between parallel crystallographic planes, measured in meters or Ångstroms.
Order of Diffraction (n)
An integer representing the multiple of wavelengths in path difference.
Miller Indices ((h k l))
Reciprocals of fractional intercepts that a crystal plane makes with crystal axes.
X-ray Diffraction (XRD)
Technique used to determine atomic and molecular structure of crystals.
Systematic Absence (Absent Reflection)
Specific diffraction peaks missing due to destructive interference in centered lattices.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Bragg Equation: nλ = 2d sin(θ)
  • Cubic d-spacing formula: d = a / √(h2 + k2 + l2)
  • BCC allowed reflection condition: h + k + l = even
  • FCC allowed reflection condition: h, k, l all odd or all even
  • Maximum diffraction limit: λ ≤ 2d

📌 Quick Recall

  • Bragg's law equates path difference to integral multiples of X-ray wavelength.
  • Constructive interference produces sharp diffraction maxima.
  • Miller indices determine both d-spacing and systematic absences.
  • Crystal structures are definitively solved using diffraction angle measurements.

⚖️ Key Differences

Bragg Angle (θ) VS Scattering Angle (2θ)

Bragg angle is measured relative to the crystal plane, whereas 2θ is the deviation angle between incident and detector beams.

Question 1 of 19

What is the condition for constructive interference in Bragg's equation?

Explanation: Bragg's law states that constructive interference occurs when the path difference equals an integer multiple of wavelengths, expressed as nλ = 2d sin(θ).

Question 2 of 19

In Bragg's equation nλ = 2d sin(θ), what does 'n' represent?

Explanation: 'n' is an integer representing the order of diffraction (1, 2, 3...).

Question 3 of 19

For first-order diffraction (n = 1) at θ = 90°, what is the relationship between λ and d?

Explanation: Substituting n = 1 and sin(90°) = 1 into nλ = 2d sin(θ) yields λ = 2d.

Question 4 of 19

Which Miller index reflection is absent in a Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) lattice?

Explanation: For BCC lattices, reflections occur only when h + k + l is even. For (1 1 1), the sum is 1 + 1 + 1 = 3 (odd), so it is systematically absent.

Question 5 of 19

Which condition applies to allowed reflections in a Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) lattice?

Explanation: In FCC lattices, constructive interference occurs only when Miller indices h, k, and l are either all odd or all even.

Question 6 of 19

What is the interplanar spacing 'd' for the (1 0 0) planes in a simple cubic unit cell of edge length 'a'?

Explanation: Using d = a / √(h^2 + k^2 + l^2), for (1 0 0), d = a / √(1^2 + 0 + 0) = a.

Question 7 of 19

What is the maximum possible value of sin(θ) in Bragg's law?

Explanation: The sine function has a maximum value of 1 at an angle of 90 degrees.

Question 8 of 19

Why are X-rays used for crystal diffraction instead of visible light?

Explanation: X-ray wavelengths are in the range of 1 to 2 Å, which matches the typical interatomic distances in crystals required for observable diffraction.

Question 9 of 19

If the wavelength of X-rays is 1.54 Å and the Bragg angle for first-order diffraction is 30°, what is the interplanar spacing 'd'?

Explanation: Using d = nλ / (2 sin(θ)) = (1 × 1.54) / (2 × sin(30°)) = 1.54 / (2 × 0.5) = 1.54 Å.

Question 10 of 19

What happens to the diffraction angle θ if the wavelength of radiation λ is increased for a constant d-spacing?

Explanation: Since sin(θ) = nλ / (2d), increasing λ increases sin(θ), which in turn increases the angle θ.

Question 11 of 19

In a powder diffraction pattern, the first reflection for a simple cubic lattice corresponds to which plane?

Explanation: For simple cubic, (1 0 0) has the largest d-spacing (a), yielding the smallest sin(θ) and thus appearing first at the lowest angle.

Question 12 of 19

What is the interplanar spacing for (1 1 0) planes in a simple cubic unit cell of edge length 'a'?

Explanation: d = a / √(1^2 + 1^2 + 0^2) = a / √2.

Question 13 of 19

Which of the following represents a forbidden reflection for an FCC lattice?

Explanation: (1 0 0) has mixed indices (one odd, two even), which violates the FCC condition that all indices must be either all odd or all even.

Question 14 of 19

If n = 2, what is the term used for the diffraction order?

Explanation: n = 2 corresponds to second-order diffraction.

Question 15 of 19

What is the physical significance of the angle 2θ in an X-ray diffractometer?

Explanation: In standard diffractometers, the detector measures the beam deflected by an angle of 2θ relative to the incident beam path.

Question 16 of 19

Calculate the wavelength of X-rays if d = 2.0 Å and the first-order diffraction peak occurs at θ = 30°.

Explanation: λ = (2 × 2.0 × sin(30°)) / 1 = 4.0 × 0.5 = 2.0 Å.

Question 17 of 19

Which cubic lattice has its first allowed reflection at (1 1 0)?

Explanation: For BCC, (1 0 0) is forbidden because 1+0+0 = 1 (odd). The first allowed plane is (1 1 0) where 1+1+0 = 2 (even).

Question 18 of 19

What is the value of sin(θ) when nλ = 2d?

Explanation: From nλ = 2d sin(θ), if nλ = 2d, then 2d = 2d sin(θ), meaning sin(θ) = 1.

Question 19 of 19

How does interplanar spacing 'd' change as the Miller indices (h k l) increase for a given cubic unit cell?

Explanation: Since d = a / √(h^2 + k^2 + l^2), larger Miller indices increase the denominator, thereby decreasing the interplanar spacing 'd'.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the wavelength of X-rays that produce a first-order diffraction peak at 30° from a crystal plane with d = 1.732 Å.

Solution: λ = 2d sin(θ) / n = 2 × 1.732 × sin(30°) / 1 = 2 × 1.732 × 0.5 = 1.732 Å.

Question 2 of 10
What is the Bragg angle for second-order diffraction (n = 2) when X-rays of wavelength 1.0 Å strike a plane with d = 2.0 Å?

Solution: sin(θ) = (2 × 1.0) / (2 × 2.0) = 2/4 = 0.5. Therefore, θ = sin^-1(0.5) = 30°.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following crystal systems has the interplanar spacing formula d = a / √(h^2 + k^2 + l^2)?

Solution: This simplified formula applies exclusively to cubic systems where a = b = c.

Question 4 of 10
What is the maximum wavelength that can be diffracted by a set of planes with spacing d = 1.5 Å?

Solution: Maximum wavelength for first-order diffraction occurs at sin(θ) = 1, giving λ_max = 2d = 2 × 1.5 = 3.0 Å.

Question 5 of 10
In X-ray diffraction, if the order of diffraction 'n' is doubled while keeping λ and d constant, what happens to sin(θ)?

Solution: sin(θ) = nλ / (2d), so sin(θ) is directly proportional to 'n'.

Question 6 of 10
For a simple cubic crystal, what is the ratio of d-spacings for (1 0 0), (1 1 0), and (1 1 1) planes?

Solution: d(100) : d(110) : d(111) = (a/1) : (a/√2) : (a/√3) = 1 : 1/√2 : 1/√3.

Question 7 of 10
Which diffraction order corresponds to n = 3?

Solution: n = 3 explicitly denotes third-order diffraction.

Question 8 of 10
If the edge length of a BCC unit cell is 4.0 Å, what is the interplanar spacing for (2 0 0) planes?

Solution: d = 4.0 / √(2^2 + 0 + 0) = 4.0 / 2 = 2.0 Å.

Question 9 of 10
Why are higher-order reflections (n > 1) less intense in X-ray powder diffraction?

Solution: Atomic scattering efficiency drops at higher scattering angles, causing higher-order peaks to have diminished intensity.

Question 10 of 10
What is the angle between the incident beam and the reflected beam when the Bragg angle is 25°?

Solution: The angle between the incident and reflected beam is the scattering angle, which is 2θ = 2 × 25° = 50°.
