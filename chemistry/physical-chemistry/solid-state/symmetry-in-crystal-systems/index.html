Physical Chemistry

Symmetry in Crystal Systems

Crystalline solids possess characteristic internal order defined by macroscopic and microscopic symmetry elements including axes of rotation, planes of symmetry, and centers of inversion. The combination of these symmetry operations generates the 32 crystallographic point groups and 230 space groups.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Elements of Symmetry

  • A plane of symmetry (σ) is an imaginary plane that divides a crystal into two identical halves which are mirror images of each other.
  • A center of inversion (i) is a point within the crystal such that any line drawn from a point on the surface passes through the center and meets an identical point at an equal distance on the opposite side.
  • An axis of rotation (Cn) is an imaginary line about which the crystal can be rotated by an angle of 360°/n to yield an indistinguishable configuration.

Crystallographic Point Groups and Systems

  • All known crystals are classified into 7 crystal systems based on their unit cell axis lengths (a, b, c) and interaxial angles (α, β, γ).
  • The combination of rotational axes, reflection planes, and inversion centers generates 32 crystallographic point groups (crystal classes).
  • The cubic system is the most symmetric, containing 3 axes of fourfold symmetry (C4), 4 axes of threefold symmetry (C3), and 6 axes of twofold symmetry (C2).

Bravais Lattices and Space Groups

  • Translational symmetry combined with point symmetry elements yields the 14 Bravais lattices distributed across the 7 crystal systems.
  • Screw axes and glide planes involve translation combined with rotation or reflection, expanding point groups into 230 unique space groups.
  • The triclinic system has the lowest symmetry, possessing only a center of inversion (or C1) and no constraints on axis lengths or angles.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Cubic systems generally possess a center of inversion.

Observation: Tetartohedral classes of the cubic system (e.g., class 23) — Absence of center of inversion imparts optical activity and piezoelectric properties to crystals like sodium chlorate.

Exception Higher crystal systems possess high rotational symmetry.

Observation: Hexagonal system — Contains a unique hexad axis (C6) along the c-axis but lacks fourfold axes, restricting cubic symmetry.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Total number of macroscopic symmetry elements in a cubic crystal is 23 (9 planes, 13 axes, 1 center).
  • The 7 crystal systems are Triclinic, Monoclinic, Orthorhombic, Tetragonal, Hexagonal, Rhombohedral (Trigonal), and Cubic.
  • There are exactly 14 Bravais lattices in three-dimensional space.
  • Triclinic is the crystal system with the lowest symmetry (point group 1 or ī).
  • Cubic system possesses 4 diagonal threefold axes directed along the body diagonals.
  • Space groups combine point symmetry operations with pure translations, screw axes, and glide planes.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing crystal systems (7) with Bravais lattices (14) or point groups (32).
  • Assuming all crystal systems have 90-degree interaxial angles (monoclinic and triclinic do not).
  • Forgetting that rhombohedral and hexagonal systems both utilize hexagonal axes descriptions.
  • Confusing proper rotational axes (Cn) with improper rotation-reflection axes (Sn).

⚡ Quick Revision: Symmetry in Crystal Systems

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Macroscopic Symmetry Elements

Macroscopic symmetry includes planes of symmetry (σ), centers of inversion (i), and proper axes of rotation (Cn where n = 1, 2, 3, 4, 6).

Crystal Systems and Parameters

Crystals are categorized into 7 systems based on axial lengths (a, b, c) and interaxial angles (α, β, γ).

Bravais Lattices

The 14 Bravais lattices describe all possible ways lattice points can be arranged in three-dimensional space without changing crystal point symmetry.

Space Groups

Inclusion of translation-dependent symmetry elements (screw axes and glide planes) expands the 32 point groups into 230 unique space groups.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Axis of Symmetry (Cn)
Line of rotation yielding identical appearance n times per 360°.
Plane of Symmetry (σ)
Imaginary plane dividing crystal into two mirror-image halves.
Center of Inversion (i)
Point through which every line drawn meets equivalent points at equal distances.
Bravais Lattice (14 Lattices)
Distinct lattice arrangements in 3D crystal systems.
Point Group (32 Classes)
Collection of symmetry operations leaving at least one point fixed.
Space Group (230 Groups)
Complete description of spatial symmetry including translation.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Total crystal systems = 7
  • Total Bravais lattices = 14
  • Total point groups = 32
  • Total space groups = 230
  • Cubic symmetry elements = 23

📌 Quick Recall

  • Symmetry operations define internal order and physical properties of crystals.
  • Cubic system has the highest symmetry; Triclinic has the lowest.
  • Translational periodicity creates space lattices.
  • Point groups govern tensorial properties like piezoelectricity and optical activity.

⚖️ Key Differences

Point Group VS Space Group

Point groups include only operations leaving one point fixed; space groups incorporate translational symmetry.

Explanation: A glide plane combines reflection across a plane with translation parallel to that plane.

Question 16 of 18

Question 17 of 18

What is the characteristic feature of a centrosymmetric crystal?

Solution: A centrosymmetric crystal contains an inversion center (i).

Question 2 of 10
How many planes of symmetry are present in a cubic crystal?

Solution: Cubic crystals have 3 rectangular planes and 6 diagonal planes, totaling 9 planes of symmetry.

Question 3 of 10
Which lattice type is NOT found in the tetragonal crystal system?

Solution: Tetragonal system only exhibits Primitive (P) and Body-centered (I) Bravais lattices.

Question 4 of 10
What is the relationship between lattice angles in the rhombohedral system?

Solution: Rhombohedral system has equal axes (a = b = c) and equal angles (α = β = γ ≠ 90°).

Question 5 of 10
Screw axes involve which combination of operations?

Solution: A screw axis combines a rotation about an axis with a translation parallel to that axis.

Question 6 of 10
Which crystal class does NOT possess a center of inversion?

Solution: Certain lower symmetry classes in the cubic system lack a center of inversion.

Question 7 of 10
What is the total number of symmetry elements in a triclinic system (class 1)?

Solution: Triclinic class 1 possesses only one symmetry element, the identity operation (E) or C1.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following is an improper axis of symmetry?

Solution: Sn represents an improper axis of rotation (rotation-reflection axis).

Question 9 of 10
The crystal system with parameters a = b ≠ c and α = β = 90°, γ = 90° is:

Solution: Tetragonal system has two equal axes and one unequal axis at 90° angles.

Question 10 of 10
Why are there only 32 crystallographic point groups?

Solution: The crystallographic restriction theorem limits valid rotational symmetries in a periodic lattice to 1, 2, 3, 4, and 6-fold axes.
