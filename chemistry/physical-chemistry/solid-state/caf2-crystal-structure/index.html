Physical Chemistry

CaF₂ Crystal Structure

The fluorite structure consists of a face-centered cubic (fcc) arrangement of calcium ions with fluoride ions occupying all the tetrahedral interstitial sites. The stoichiometric ratio enforces a coordination number of 8 for Ca2+ and 4 for F-.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Lattice Geometry and Ion Distribution

  • Ca2+ ions form a face-centered cubic (fcc) lattice, contributing 8 × (1/8) + 6 × (1/2) = 4 ions per unit cell.
  • F- ions occupy all 8 tetrahedral voids present inside the cubic unit cell.
  • The total number of formula units (Z) per unit cell for CaF2 is 4.

Coordination Number and Stoichiometry

  • Each Ca2+ ion is surrounded by 8 F- ions at the corners of a cube, giving a coordination number of 8.
  • Each F- ion is surrounded by 4 Ca2+ ions located at the vertices of a regular tetrahedron.
  • The coordination number ratio (8:4) strictly reflects the chemical stoichiometry of AB2.

Density and Interatomic Distance Relationships

  • The distance between nearest-neighbor Ca2+ and F- ions is equal to √3 a / 4, where 'a' is the edge length.
  • The closest approach between fluoride ions occurs along the edge of the unit cell, equal to a / √2.
  • Defects such as Frenkel defects are common in the fluorite lattice due to the vacant interstitial sites available for smaller anions or cations.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher radius ratio dictates higher coordination number

Observation: Coordination numbers are 8:4 despite radius ratio falling in the 6:6 range for some antifluorite systems — Stoichiometric requirement of AB2 forces twice as many anions as cations, altering the local packing constraints.

Exception Anions typically form the close-packed host framework in ionic crystals

Observation: In CaF2, the larger Ca2+ cations form the fcc host lattice while smaller F- anions occupy tetrahedral voids — The stoichiometry requires twice as many interstitial sites as cations, requiring the larger ion to form the primary lattice.

Exception Ionic crystals exhibit Schottky defects predominantly when ions are of similar size

Observation: CaF2 predominantly exhibits Frenkel defects rather than Schottky defects — The presence of large vacant tetrahedral spaces allows smaller ions to easily leave their lattice sites and occupy interstitial positions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Ca2+ ions form a face-centered cubic (fcc) arrangement.
  • F- ions occupy 100% of the tetrahedral voids.
  • The coordination number of Ca2+ is 8 and of F- is 4.
  • The number of formula units per unit cell (Z) is 4.
  • Antifluorite structure (like Na2O) reverses the positions of cations and anions.
  • CaF2 exhibits Frenkel defects due to mobile ions in interstitial spaces.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming anions form the fcc lattice in CaF2; remember that Ca2+ forms the fcc lattice.
  • Confusing the coordination number ratio 8:4 with 6:6 or 4:4.
  • Using Z = 1 or Z = 2 when calculating density; always use Z = 4 for CaF2.
  • Forgetting that all tetrahedral sites are occupied in CaF2, unlike zinc blende where only half are occupied.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Fluorite Structure (CaF₂ type)
Crystal lattice with AB₂ stoichiometry where cations are fcc and anions occupy all tetrahedral sites.
Coordination Number (C.N.)
Number of nearest neighbor oppositely charged ions surrounding a central ion.
Tetrahedral Void (Interstitial site)
Space surrounded by 4 spheres at the corners of a regular tetrahedron.
Unit Cell Z-value (Z)
Number of formula units present per unit cell, equal to 4 for CaF₂.
Antifluorite Structure (M₂X type)
Structure where positions of anions and cations are reversed relative to fluorite.
Frenkel Defect (Point defect)
Vacancy created when an ion leaves its regular site and occupies an interstitial position.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ca2+ coordination number = 8
  • F- coordination number = 4
  • Formula units per unit cell (Z) = 4
  • Cations form the fcc lattice framework
  • All tetrahedral voids are occupied by anions

📌 Quick Recall

  • CaF2 crystallizes in a face-centered cubic lattice of calcium ions.
  • Fluoride ions occupy all tetrahedral holes, resulting in an 8:4 coordination network.
  • The density calculation requires substituting Z = 4 into the standard unit cell equation.
  • Antifluorite structures invert the cation and anion lattice positions.

⚖️ Key Differences

Fluorite Structure (CaF₂) VS Zinc Blende Structure (ZnS)

In CaF₂, cations are fcc and all tetrahedral voids are filled (8:4). In ZnS, anions are fcc and only half the tetrahedral voids are filled (4:4).
