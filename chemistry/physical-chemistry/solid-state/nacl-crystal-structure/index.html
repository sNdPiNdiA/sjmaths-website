NaCl Crystal Structure

Sodium chloride crystallizes in a face-centered cubic (FCC) lattice where chloride ions form the cubic close-packed array and sodium ions occupy all octahedral voids. The unit cell contains 4 formula units of NaCl with a coordination number ratio of 6:6.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Lattice Architecture and Ionic Positions

  • The Cl- ions form a face-centered cubic (FCC) lattice array, contributing 4 ions per unit cell.
  • The smaller Na+ ions occupy all octahedral voids located at the body center and edge centers of the unit cell.
  • The total number of octahedral voids per unit cell equals the number of closed-packed spheres, yielding 4 Na+ and 4 Cl- per unit cell.
  • Each ion is surrounded by 6 counter-ions, establishing a 6:6 coordination geometry.

Interatomic Distances and Unit Cell Parameters

  • The edge length of the unit cell (a) is equal to twice the sum of the radii of sodium and chloride ions: a = 2(rNa+ + rCl-).
  • Contact between adjacent ions occurs along the edges of the unit cell, not across the face diagonals.
  • The distance between nearest like-charged ions (e.g., Na+ to Na+) is a / √2.
  • The limiting radius ratio (rNa+ / rCl-) is approximately 0.525, which falls comfortably within the stability range for octahedral coordination.

Symmetry Elements and Density Relations

  • The NaCl unit cell possesses a total of 23 symmetry elements, including planes of symmetry, axes of rotation, and a center of inversion.
  • The crystal system belongs to the cubic system with lattice parameters a = b = c and α = β = γ = 90°.
  • Macroscopic density calculations utilize Z = 4 formula units per unit cell for stoichiometric sodium chloride.
  • Defects such as Schottky defects maintain electrical neutrality without altering the stoichiometric ratio of 1:1.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkali halides adopt the 6:6 coordination NaCl structure

Observation: CsCl, CsBr, and CsI adopt the 8:8 coordination BCC structure — The larger radius ratio of cesium halides (r+ / r- > 0.732) favors cubic coordination over octahedral coordination.

Exception Cations occupy octahedral voids in FCC lattices when radius ratio is 0.414-0.732

Observation: AgCl exhibits slight deviations in lattice energy and solubility behavior compared to pure alkali halides — Ag+ possesses an 18-electron outer shell configuration (pseudo-inert gas core), imparting significant covalent character to the Ag-Cl bond.

Exception Stoichiometric ionic crystals have equal numbers of cations and anions

Observation: Non-stoichiometric metal excess defects (F-centers) yield colored crystals like yellow NaCl — Trapped electrons in anionic vacancies absorb visible light, altering stoichiometric balance while maintaining macroscopic electrical neutrality.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Sodium chloride exhibits a 6:6 coordination number with an FCC lattice.
  • The number of formula units (Z) per unit cell for NaCl is exactly 4.
  • Chloride ions occupy FCC lattice points while sodium ions occupy octahedral voids.
  • The unit cell edge length relation is given by a = 2(rNa+ + rCl-).
  • Schottky defects are prevalent in NaCl crystals due to high ionic character and coordination number.
  • The radius ratio (rNa+ / rCl-) for NaCl lies in the range 0.414 to 0.732.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Na+ positions with Cl- positions; remember Cl- forms the FCC host lattice while Na+ occupies octahedral voids.
  • Assuming ion-ion contact occurs across the face diagonal; contact actually occurs along the edge of the unit cell.
  • Using Z = 1 or Z = 2 when calculating density for NaCl instead of the correct value of Z = 4.
  • Incorrectly stating that coordination numbers change with temperature or pressure without noting phase transitions.

⚡ Quick Revision: NaCl Crystal Structure

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Lattice Geometry

NaCl crystallizes in an FCC arrangement where anions form the close-packed framework and cations fill interstitial octahedral sites.

Stoichiometry & Z

Each unit cell contains 4 Na+ and 4 Cl- ions, preserving the 1:1 stoichiometry and yielding Z = 4.

Coordination Number

Both sodium and chloride ions maintain a coordination number of 6, dictated by the limiting radius ratio rules for octahedral geometry.

Density Calculations

Macroscopic density is evaluated using the formula ρ = (Z × M) / (NA × a3) with Z = 4.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Coordination Number (C.N.)
Number of nearest neighbor counter-ions surrounding a central ion; 6 for NaCl.
Octahedral Void (Interstitial Site)
Void surrounded by six equidistant ions located at the vertices of an octahedron.
Face-Centered Cubic (FCC)
Lattice with points at all corners and center of all six faces of the unit cell.
Unit Cell Edge Length (a)
Linear dimension of the cube edge in cm or pm.
Formula Units per Unit Cell (Z)
Number of empirical formula units contained inside a single unit cell; Z = 4 for NaCl.
Radius Ratio (r+ / r-)
Ratio of cation radius to anion radius determining coordination geometry.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Coordination number = 6:6
  • Edge length relation: a = 2(rNa+ + rCl-)
  • Chloride ions form FCC lattice
  • Sodium ions occupy octahedral voids

📌 Quick Recall

  • NaCl has an FCC crystal lattice with 4 formula units per unit cell.
  • Octahedral coordination results in a 6:6 coordination number.
  • Density calculations require Z = 4 and molar mass of NaCl.

⚖️ Key Differences

NaCl Structure VS CsCl Structure

NaCl has 6:6 coordination in an FCC lattice, whereas CsCl has 8:8 coordination in a simple cubic lattice.

Question 2 of 10
Which defect in NaCl crystals causes a decrease in density?

Solution: Schottky defects involve missing ions from the lattice, which decreases overall density.

Question 3 of 10
What happens to the coordination number of NaCl when subjected to high pressure?

Solution: High pressure favors higher coordination numbers, converting the 6:6 NaCl structure into the 8:8 CsCl structure.

Question 4 of 10
In the calculation of density for NaCl, what are the standard units for edge length 'a' when using cm?

Solution: Edge length is typically given in pm (1 pm = 10-10 cm) or Å (1 Å = 10-8 cm).

Question 5 of 10
How many octahedral voids are located on the edges of a single FCC unit cell?

Solution: There are 12 edges in a cube, and each edge center contributes 1/4 to the unit cell (12 × 1/4 = 3 edge voids, plus 1 at the body center = 4 total).

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following ions occupies the body center of the NaCl unit cell?

Solution: The body center of an FCC unit cell is an octahedral void, which is occupied by Na+.

Question 7 of 10
What is the total contribution of all corner Cl- ions to a single unit cell?

Solution: There are 8 corners, and each corner contributes 1/8 to the unit cell (8 × 1/8 = 1).

Question 8 of 10
What is the total contribution of all face-centered Cl- ions to a single unit cell?

Solution: There are 6 faces, and each face contributes 1/2 to the unit cell (6 × 1/2 = 3).

Question 9 of 10
Why does NaCl exhibit Schottky defects rather than Frenkel defects?

Solution: Schottky defects occur in ionic solids where cations and anions are of comparable size and coordination numbers are high.

Question 10 of 10
If the radius of Na+ is 95 pm and Cl- is 181 pm, what is the theoretical radius ratio?

Solution: Radius ratio = 95 / 181 = 0.525, which lies in the octahedral range (0.414 - 0.732).
