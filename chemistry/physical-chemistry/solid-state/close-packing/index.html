Physical Chemistry

Close Packing

Close packing of identical hard spheres defines the spatial arrangement within crystal lattices to maximize space utilization. The efficiency of packing determines the ultimate density and coordination number of the crystalline solid.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

One-Dimensional and Two-Dimensional Close Packing

  • In one-dimensional close packing, spheres are arranged in a single linear row touching each other with a coordination number of 2.
  • Square close packing (AAAA type) in two dimensions aligns adjacent rows directly above one another, yielding a packing efficiency of 78.5% and a coordination number of 4.
  • Hexagonal close packing (ABAB type) in two dimensions offsets alternate rows into the depressions of preceding rows, yielding a denser packing efficiency of 90.6% and a coordination number of 6.

Three-Dimensional Close Packing and Void Formation

  • Three-dimensional hexagonal close packing (hcp, ABAB pattern) stacks alternating layers directly over the tetrahedral voids of the first layer, achieving an overall packing efficiency of 74% with a coordination number of 12.
  • Three-dimensional cubic close packing (ccp or fcc, ABCABC pattern) places the third layer over octahedral voids, also achieving 74% packing efficiency and a coordination number of 12.
  • Tetrahedral voids are formed at the junction of four spheres (three in one layer, one in the adjacent) and have a limiting radius ratio of 0.225 - 0.414.
  • Octahedral voids are formed by the intersection of six spheres (three in one layer, three inverted in the adjacent) with a limiting radius ratio of 0.414 - 0.732.

Packing Efficiency Calculations and Lattice Densities

  • Simple cubic (sc) lattices possess only one atom per unit cell with a packing efficiency of 52.4% and a coordination number of 6.
  • Body-centered cubic (bcc) lattices contain two atoms per unit cell with a packing efficiency of 68% and a coordination number of 8.
  • The theoretical density (ρ) of a crystal is computed using the expression ρ = (Z × M) / (NA × a3).
  • Defects in close packing disrupt ideal translational symmetry, yet local coordination often remains preserved in stoichiometric crystals.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Equal number of octahedral and tetrahedral voids in close packing

Observation: Tetrahedral voids are always double the number of close-packed spheres (2N), whereas octahedral voids equal the number of spheres (N). — Geometrical derivation dictates that each sphere contributes to multiple tetrahedral spaces relative to its coordination geometry.

Exception Solid spheres touch along unit cell edges in simple cubic systems

Observation: In body-centered cubic systems, spheres touch along the body diagonal, not the edge length. — The central atom forces corner atoms apart, making the body diagonal equal to 4r/√3.

Exception All metal crystals adopt maximum density structures

Observation: Alkali metals crystallize in the less efficient bcc lattice (68%) instead of ccp or hcp (74%) under normal conditions. — Weak metallic bonding and valence electron configurations favor directional body-centered overlap over dense closed packed spheres.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The coordination number of an atom in both hcp and ccp arrangements is 12.
  • Simple cubic packing efficiency is exactly 52.4%, leaving 47.6% empty space.
  • Body-centered cubic packing efficiency is 68% with a coordination number of 8.
  • The total number of tetrahedral voids in a crystal lattice is twice the number of close-packed atoms.
  • In a face-centered cubic lattice, the relation between edge length 'a' and atomic radius 'r' is a = 2√2 r.
  • Density calculation requires proper unit conversion between picometers (pm) and centimeters (cm).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the number of tetrahedral voids with octahedral voids (tetrahedral = 2N, octahedral = N).
  • Assuming bcc atoms touch along the edge length 'a' instead of the body diagonal.
  • Forgetting to multiply Avogadro's number when calculating molar mass or density from unit cell dimensions.
  • Equating coordination number in 2D square close packing (4) with 3D simple cubic packing (6).

⚡ Quick Revision: Close Packing

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Dimensional Close Packing Hierarchy

Close packing builds from 1D linear rows (CN 2) to 2D sheets (square CN 4, hexagonal CN 6), culminating in 3D structures.

Three-Dimensional Lattices

3D close packing yields hcp (ABAB) and ccp/fcc (ABCABC) arrangements, both achieving the theoretical maximum 74% packing efficiency.

Interstitial Voids

Interstitial spaces form tetrahedral and octahedral geometries, dictating stoichiometry and ion placement in complex crystals.

Quantitative Cell Parameters

Mathematical relations link edge length (a), atomic radius (r), density (ρ), and Avogadro's number across all four fundamental unit cells.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Coordination Number (CN)
Number of nearest neighbor atoms touching a given sphere in a lattice.
Packing Fraction (PF)
Fraction of total volume occupied by constituent spheres.
Tetrahedral Void (V_tet)
Interstitial site surrounded by 4 spheres in tetrahedral geometry.
Octahedral Void (V_oct)
Interstitial site surrounded by 6 spheres in octahedral geometry.
Unit Cell (a, b, c)
Smallest repeating structural unit in a crystal lattice.
Radius Ratio (r_+ / r_-)
Ratio of cation to anion radii determining ionic coordination.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • SC Packing Efficiency = 52.4%, CN = 6
  • BCC Packing Efficiency = 68%, CN = 8
  • FCC/HCP Packing Efficiency = 74%, CN = 12
  • Tetrahedral Voids = 2N, Octahedral Voids = N
  • Density equation: ρ = (Z × M) / (N_A × a^3)

📌 Quick Recall

  • Close packing maximizes space filling using rigid sphere models.
  • Stacking sequences differentiate hcp (ABAB) from ccp (ABCABC).
  • Voids provide structural templates for ionic and interstitial alloys.
  • Geometric formulas link radius, edge length, and density.

⚖️ Key Differences

FCC VS CCP

FCC refers to the lattice point centering geometry, whereas CCP refers to the ABCABC stacking sequence; structurally they are identical.

Question 1 of 20

What is the coordination number of an atom in a hexagonal close-packed (hcp) 3D structure?

Explanation: In both hcp and ccp 3D close-packed structures, each sphere is surrounded by 12 nearest neighbors (6 in its own plane, 3 in the layer above, and 3 in the layer below).

Question 2 of 20

What percentage of free space exists in a body-centered cubic (bcc) unit cell?

Explanation: The packing efficiency of a bcc unit cell is 68%. Therefore, the empty space (void percentage) is 100% - 68% = 32%.

Question 3 of 20

If the number of close-packed spheres in a crystal lattice is N, what is the total number of octahedral voids?

Explanation: In any close-packed arrangement of N spheres, the number of octahedral voids is equal to N, and the number of tetrahedral voids is 2N.

Question 4 of 20

What is the relationship between edge length (a) and radius (r) in a face-centered cubic lattice?

Explanation: In an fcc unit cell, atoms touch along the face diagonal. The face diagonal length is 4r and equals √2 a, yielding a = 4r / √2 = 2√2 r.

Question 5 of 20

Which of the following crystal systems has a packing efficiency of 52.4%?

Explanation: Simple cubic (primitive cubic) lattice has only 1 atom per unit cell and a packing efficiency of 52.4%.

Question 6 of 20

What is the limiting radius ratio (r+/r-) for a coordination number of 6 (octahedral geometry)?

Explanation: The stability range for an octahedral void coordination number of 6 is 0.414 to 0.732.

Question 7 of 20

What stacking sequence characterizes cubic close packing (ccp)?

Explanation: Cubic close packing corresponds to an ABCABC... stacking sequence, which is identical to a face-centered cubic (fcc) lattice.

Question 8 of 20

How many effective atoms are present in a face-centered cubic (fcc) unit cell?

Explanation: An fcc unit cell has 8 corner atoms (8 × 1/8 = 1) and 6 face-centered atoms (6 × 1/2 = 3), totaling 4 atoms.

Question 9 of 20

What is the coordination number in a two-dimensional square close-packed layer?

Explanation: In 2D square close packing, each sphere touches four neighboring spheres in the same plane.

Question 10 of 20

Which alkali metal crystallizes in a body-centered cubic lattice under normal conditions?

Explanation: All alkali metals (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs) crystallize in body-centered cubic structures under standard conditions.

Question 11 of 20

What is the number of tetrahedral voids per atom in a close-packed arrangement?

Explanation: There are 2 tetrahedral voids for every constituent atom (2N for N atoms).

Question 12 of 20

In a bcc unit cell, atoms touch along which geometric feature?

Explanation: In a bcc lattice, the central atom touches the eight corner atoms along the body diagonal of the cube.

Question 13 of 20

What is the density formula for a cubic crystal unit cell?

Explanation: Density ρ is given by mass of unit cell (Z × M / N_A) divided by volume of unit cell (a^3).

Question 14 of 20

What is the coordination number of sodium ions (Na+) in the NaCl crystal lattice?

Explanation: In NaCl (rock salt structure), both Na+ and Cl- ions possess a coordination number of 6, adopting an fcc arrangement of anions with cations in octahedral voids.

Question 15 of 20

What fraction of a corner atom belongs to a single cubic unit cell?

Explanation: A corner atom in a cubic lattice is shared among eight adjacent unit cells, so its contribution is 1/8.

Question 16 of 20

Which of the following has the highest packing efficiency?

Explanation: Hexagonal close packing (hcp) and cubic close packing (ccp) both share the maximum packing efficiency of 74%.

Question 17 of 20

What is the radius ratio range for a tetrahedral void?

Explanation: The stability limit for tetrahedral coordination (CN 4) is 0.225 to 0.414.

Question 18 of 20

What is the total number of atoms per unit cell in a body-centered cubic (bcc) crystal?

Explanation: A bcc unit cell has 8 corners (8 × 1/8 = 1) and 1 body center (1 × 1 = 1), totaling 2 atoms.

Question 19 of 20

What is the void space percentage in a face-centered cubic (fcc) arrangement?

Explanation: Since fcc packing efficiency is 74%, the empty space (void percentage) is 100% - 74% = 26%.

Question 20 of 20

Which type of void is larger in a close-packed structure of equal-sized spheres?

Explanation: Octahedral voids are larger than tetrahedral voids, having a critical radius ratio limit of 0.414 compared to 0.225 for tetrahedral voids.

Question 1 of 10
What is the effective number of atoms per unit cell in a hexagonal close-packed unit cell?

Solution: An hcp unit cell contains 6 effective atoms derived from contributions of corner, face-center, and interior atoms.

Question 2 of 10
Calculate the packing fraction of a simple cubic lattice.

Solution: PF = (1 × (4/3)πr^3) / (2r)^3 = (π/6) ≈ 0.524 or 52.4%.

Question 3 of 10
Which crystal defect does not alter the stoichiometry of the solid?

Solution: Both Schottky and Frenkel defects maintain electrical neutrality and stoichiometric ratio within the ionic crystal.

Question 4 of 10
What happens to the coordination number when pressure is applied to an ionic crystal like NaCl?

Solution: High pressure favors denser packing and higher coordination numbers (e.g., NaCl coordination increases from 6:6 to 8:8 under high pressure).

Question 5 of 10
In a compound formed by elements X and Y, Y forms ccp and X occupies 1/3rd of tetrahedral voids. What is the formula?

Solution: If Y = N (atoms), tetrahedral voids = 2N. X occupies (1/3)(2N) = 2N/3. Ratio X:Y = (2N/3):N = 2:3 → X_2Y_3.

Question 6 of 10
What is the distance between nearest neighbor octahedral voids in an fcc lattice with edge length 'a'?

Solution: Octahedral voids are located at the edge centers and the body center. The distance between an edge center void and the body center void is a/2.

Question 7 of 10
What is the empty space percentage in hexagonal close packing?

Solution: HCP packing efficiency is 74%, leaving 26% empty space (void volume).

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following anions forms the close-packed lattice in fluorite (CaF2) structure?

Solution: In CaF2, calcium ions (Ca^2+) form a ccp (fcc) lattice, and fluoride ions (F^-) occupy all tetrahedral voids.

Question 9 of 10
What is the radius ratio (r+/r-) threshold separating coordination number 4 (tetrahedral) from coordination number 6 (octahedral)?

Solution: The radius ratio of 0.414 is the exact boundary threshold where coordination transitions from tetrahedral (0.225-0.414) to octahedral (0.414-0.732).

Question 10 of 10
If edge length of an FCC unit cell is 400 pm, what is the atomic radius of the constituent element?

Solution: Using a = 2√2 r, r = 400 / (2 × 1.414) = 400 / 2.828 ≈ 141.4 pm.
