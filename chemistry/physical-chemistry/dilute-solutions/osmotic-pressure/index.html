Physical Chemistry

Osmotic Pressure

Osmotic pressure is the excess hydrostatic pressure that must be applied to a solution to prevent the net flow of solvent into the solution through a semipermeable membrane. It is a colligative property directly proportional to the molar concentration of solute particles and absolute temperature.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Origin and Semi-Permeable Membranes

  • Osmosis involves the spontaneous net movement of solvent molecules from a region of lower solute concentration to higher solute concentration across a semipermeable membrane.
  • The semipermeable membrane allows the passage of solvent molecules while selectively blocking hydrated solute particles.
  • Osmotic pressure (π) counterbalances the chemical potential gradient of the solvent across the membrane, achieving equilibrium.
  • It is treated as a colligative property because it depends exclusively on the number of solute entities rather than their chemical identity.

Quantitative Laws and Ideal Solution Behavior

  • Pioneered by Jacobus Henricus van 't Hoff, the osmotic pressure equation mirrors the ideal gas law: πV = nRT.
  • Osmotic pressure is particularly favored for determining the molar masses of macromolecules (proteins, polymers) because even dilute solutions yield measurable heights.
  • Solutions having the same osmotic pressure at a given temperature are defined as isotonic solutions and possess identical molar concentrations.
  • A solution with a higher osmotic pressure relative to another is termed hypertonic, while the one with lower pressure is hypotonic.

Abnormal Molecular Masses and Electrolyte Solutions

  • Electrolytes exhibit higher osmotic pressures than predicted due to dissociation into multiple ions in aqueous media.
  • The van 't Hoff factor (i) corrects the theoretical equation: π = iCRT.
  • For solutes undergoing association (e.g., benzoic acid in benzene), the van 't Hoff factor drops below 1.
  • Degree of dissociation (α) is calculated using relation α = i - 1n - 1, where n represents total moles of ions produced per mole of electrolyte.

Units: i is dimensionless, π in atm, C in mol L-1, R, T standard units

Conditions: For macromolecular solutes of mass W2 in volume V
Units: M2 in g mol-1, W2 in grams, V in liters

Van 't Hoff Equation for Osmotic Pressure
π = CRT
Conditions: Dilute ideal solutions at constant temperature
Units: π in atm, C in mol L-1, R in L atm K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Colligative properties depend solely on the total number of moles of solute particles.

Observation: Strong electrolytes in concentrated solutions deviate significantly from ideal osmotic calculations. — Interionic electrostatic forces of attraction reduce the effective number of independent particles (activity coefficient effects).

Exception Osmotic pressure increases linearly with molar concentration.

Observation: Polymeric macromolecules deviate at higher concentrations due to chain entanglement and excluded volume effects. — Macromolecules exhibit non-ideal thermodynamic behavior requiring virial expansion coefficients.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Gas Law Analogy
π = (n/V)RT = CRT

Application: Treat osmotic pressure (π) identically to pressure (P) in the ideal gas equation, replacing concentration with molarity.

Macromolecule Molar Mass Shortcut
M = (w × R × T) / (π × V)

Application: Use this specific rearranged form when given height of liquid column or direct pressure for large biological polymers.

Isotonic Condition Trick
C1 = C2 (or i1C1 = i2C2)

Application: For isotonic solutions (like blood cells and saline), equate molar concentrations directly without recalculating R and T.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Osmotic pressure has the largest magnitude among all colligative properties, making it ideal for high-precision molecular weight determination.
  • The unit of osmotic pressure in standard SI is Pascals (Pa), but atmospheres (atm) or millimeters of mercury (mmHg) are frequently used in UP PGT examinations.
  • Reverse osmosis is applied in water desalination by applying a pressure exceeding the osmotic pressure to force pure solvent out.
  • Van 't Hoff factor (i) for complete dissociation of K4[Fe(CN)6] is 5.
  • Isotonic solutions like 0.9% mass/volume NaCl solution are isotonic with human red blood cells.
  • Osmotic pressure is a colligative property because it depends on the number of solute particles, not their chemical nature.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing mass concentration (g/L) with molar concentration (mol/L) when substituting into π = CRT.
  • Forgetting to include the van 't Hoff factor (i) for ionic salts like NaCl or CaCl2.
  • Mistaking hypertonic solutions for hypotonic solutions when analyzing biological cell shrinkage (crenation).
  • Assuming osmotic pressure is dependent on the chemical nature of the solute rather than particle count.

⚡ Quick Revision: Osmotic Pressure

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition and Membrane Dynamics

Osmotic pressure is the equilibrium pressure stopping solvent influx through a semipermeable membrane.

Van 't Hoff Mathematical Law

Expressed as π = iCRT, relating pressure directly to molarity, temperature, and ionization factor.

Biological and Technical Applications

Governs cell turgor pressure, hemolysis, crenation, and reverse osmosis purification processes.

Macromolecular Analysis

Primary method for finding high molecular weights due to large, easily measurable pressure signals.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Osmotic Pressure (π)
Excess pressure stopping osmosis; SI unit Pascal (Pa).
Semipermeable Membrane (SPM)
Membrane selectively permeable to solvent molecules only.
Van 't Hoff Factor (i)
Ratio of experimental colligative property to calculated property.
Isotonic Solutions (π1 = π2)
Solutions possessing equal osmotic pressures.
Crenation (Cell Shrinkage)
Shrinkage of red blood cells in hypertonic saline solutions.
Reverse Osmosis (RO)
Application of external pressure exceeding π to reverse solvent flow.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Formula: π = iCRT
  • Universal Gas Constant R = 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1
  • Isotonic solutions have equal molar concentrations at same temperature
  • Van 't Hoff factor i > 1 for dissociation, i < 1 for association
  • Osmosis moves solvent from low solute concentration to high solute concentration

📌 Quick Recall

  • Osmotic pressure is a colligative property proportional to molar concentration.
  • It provides accurate molar masses for delicate macromolecules without thermal degradation.
  • Electrolytes require incorporation of the van 't Hoff factor i.
  • Reverse osmosis is the foundational principle for modern water desalination units.

⚖️ Key Differences

Hypertonic Solution VS Hypotonic Solution

Hypertonic has higher osmotic pressure causing cell shrinkage; hypotonic has lower pressure causing cell swelling.

What happens to red blood cells when placed in a hypertonic solution?

Question 10 of 19

Explanation: Since π is directly proportional to T (π ∝ T), doubling T doubles the osmotic pressure.

Question 14 of 19

Question 15 of 19

Question 16 of 19

Which of the following expressions correctly gives the degree of dissociation (α) of an electrolyte?

Explanation: Osmosis is the net movement of solvent from dilute solution (lower solute concentration) to concentrated solution.

Question 5 of 10
What will happen to a plant cell placed in a hypertonic solution?

Question 6 of 10
The relation between osmotic pressure (π) and absolute temperature (T) at constant volume is:

If an electrolyte dissociates into 4 ions with 80% degree of dissociation, its van 't Hoff factor (i) is:
