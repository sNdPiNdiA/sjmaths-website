Physical Chemistry

Abnormal Colligative Properties

Abnormal colligative properties arise when solutes undergo association or dissociation in solution, resulting in molar masses that differ from the theoretical values. The van 't Hoff factor corrects colligative property equations to account for these actual particle counts.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrolyte Dissociation and Particle Multiplication

  • Strong electrolytes such as NaCl and KNO3 completely dissociate in aqueous solution, increasing the total number of solute particles.
  • An increase in the total number of particles leads to higher colligative properties (osmotic pressure, boiling point elevation) than expected.
  • The van 't Hoff factor i is greater than 1 for complete dissociation (i = n for n ions).

Solute Association and Dimerization

  • Carboxylic acids like acetic acid and benzoic acid undergo intermolecular hydrogen bonding in non-polar solvents, forming dimers.
  • Association decreases the total number of independent solute particles in solution, lowering experimental colligative property values.
  • The van 't Hoff factor i is less than 1 for associating systems (e.g., i ≈ 0.5 for complete dimerization).

Mathematical Treatment of Non-Ideal Solutions

  • All colligative property formulas are multiplied by the van 't Hoff factor i to yield accurate thermodynamic results.
  • The degree of dissociation α links the van 't Hoff factor to the stoichiometric ion count n via i = 1 + α(n - 1).
  • Abnormal molar masses determined experimentally are inversely proportional to the van 't Hoff factor.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Binary electrolytes should exhibit a van 't Hoff factor of exactly 2.

Observation: Strong electrolytes like NaCl show i < 2 at higher concentrations. — Interionic attractions and formation of ionic pairs reduce the effective number of independent free ions in concentrated solutions.

Exception Carboxylic acids dissolved in non-polar solvents (benzene) have a normal molar mass.

Observation: Carboxylic acids exhibit double the normal molar mass in non-polar media. — Strong hydrogen bonding induces complete dimerization, reducing particle concentration by half.

Exception Non-electrolytes like glucose and urea have a van 't Hoff factor equal to 1.

Observation: Concentrated solutions of urea show slight deviations from ideal behavior. — Solute-solute interactions and hydration shells affect the effective chemical potential at high molalities.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The van 't Hoff factor i was introduced in 1886 to account for anomalies in colligative properties.
  • For complete dissociation of Al2(SO4)3, the theoretical van 't Hoff factor i is 5.
  • Colligative properties are colligative because they depend solely on the number of solute particles, not their chemical nature.
  • Degree of dissociation α for a binary electrolyte is calculated as (i - 1).
  • Association of benzoic acid in benzene results in an experimental molar mass of approximately 244 g/mol instead of 122 g/mol.
  • Osmotic pressure measurements are preferred over freezing point depression for high molecular mass polymers due to larger numerical magnitudes.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming strong electrolytes like NaCl have i = 2 under all concentration regimes without considering interionic attraction.
  • Confusing the stoichiometric coefficient 'n' with the van 't Hoff factor 'i' when the degree of dissociation is less than 100%.
  • Applying association formulas to aqueous solutions of carboxylic acids, ignoring their ionization in water.
  • Failing to multiply the boiling point elevation constant (Kb) by the van 't Hoff factor during numerical problem-solving.

⚡ Quick Revision: Abnormal Colligative Properties

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Van 't Hoff Factor Definition

The van 't Hoff factor quantifies the deviation of colligative properties from ideal values, defined as the ratio of observed to calculated colligative properties.

Electrolyte Dissociation

Electrolytes dissociate into multiple ions in polar solvents, increasing particle count, raising boiling point and osmotic pressure, and depressing freezing point.

Solute Association

Molecules capable of hydrogen bonding associate into larger aggregates in non-polar media, lowering the effective particle concentration and colligative values.

Quantitative Relationships

Degrees of dissociation and association are mathematically related to the van 't Hoff factor 'i' and the number of participating species 'n'.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Van 't Hoff Factor (i)
Ratio of normal molar mass to abnormal molar mass. Dimensionless.
Degree of Dissociation (α)
Fraction of total moles of electrolyte that dissociate into ions. Dimensionless.
Degree of Association (α)
Fraction of total moles of solute that associate into larger molecular clusters. Dimensionless.
Colligative Properties (Properties depending on particle count)
Physical properties of solutions dependent on the number of solute particles rather than their identity.
Normal Molar Mass (Mnormal)
Calculated molecular mass from chemical formula in g/mol.
Abnormal Molar Mass (Mabnormal)
Experimentally determined molecular mass from colligative properties in g/mol.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • i = (Normal Molar Mass) / (Abnormal Molar Mass)
  • For dissociation: i = 1 + α(n - 1)
  • For association: i = 1 + α((1/n) - 1)
  • ΔT_f = i · K_f · m
  • π = i · C · R · T

📌 Quick Recall

  • Association decreases colligative properties and increases apparent molar mass.
  • Dissociation increases colligative properties and decreases apparent molar mass.
  • Non-electrolytes maintain an ideal van 't Hoff factor of 1.
  • Strong electrolytes approach limiting theoretical 'i' values only at infinite dilution.

⚖️ Key Differences

Degree of Dissociation (α) VS Van 't Hoff Factor (i)

α is the fraction of molecules dissociated, whereas 'i' is the total effective particle multiplication factor.

Question 1 of 18

What is the van 't Hoff factor for a dilute aqueous solution of K3}[Fe(CN)6] assuming complete dissociation?

Explanation: K3}[Fe(CN)6] dissociates into 3 K+ ions and 1 [Fe(CN)6]3- ion, totaling 4 particles. Thus, i = 4.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following aqueous solutions will have the highest freezing point depression?

Explanation: Freezing point depression is directly proportional to the van 't Hoff factor. Al2(SO4)3 yields 5 ions (i = 5), giving the highest value.

Question 3 of 18

The van 't Hoff factor 'i' for a compound that undergoes association in solution is:

Explanation: Association reduces the total number of particles in solution, making the experimental colligative property smaller than expected, hence i < 1.

Question 4 of 18

What is the degree of dissociation (α) of a weak electrolyte yielding n ions if its van 't Hoff factor is i?

Explanation: From the equation i = 1 + α(n - 1), rearranging gives α = (i - 1) / (n - 1).

Question 5 of 18

Benzoic acid dissolved in benzene exhibits an abnormal molar mass due to:

Explanation: Benzoic acid forms stable dimers in non-polar solvents like benzene through intermolecular hydrogen bonding, doubling its apparent molar mass.

Question 6 of 18

If the experimental molar mass of NaCl is found to be half of its theoretical molar mass, the van 't Hoff factor is:

Explanation: i = Mnormal / Mabnormal = M / (M / 2) = 2.

Question 7 of 18

Which solute pair will produce isotonic solutions at the same molar concentration?

Explanation: Urea and glucose are both non-electrolytes with i = 1, so equal molar concentrations produce identical osmotic pressures (isotonic).

Question 8 of 18

What is the theoretical van 't Hoff factor for BaCl2 assuming 100% dissociation?

Explanation: BaCl2 → Ba2+ + 2Cl-, yielding a total of 3 ions.

Question 9 of 18

When acetic acid is dissolved in water, its van 't Hoff factor 'i' is:

Explanation: Acetic acid is a weak electrolyte that partially dissociates in water, giving a value between 1 and 2.

Question 10 of 18

Which colligative property is most suitable for determining the molecular mass of polymers?

Explanation: Osmotic pressure can be measured at room temperature and uses dilute solutions where readings are large and easily measurable, avoiding polymer degradation.

Question 11 of 18

If 10% of a binary electrolyte dissociates in solution, the van 't Hoff factor is:

Explanation: i = 1 + α(n - 1) = 1 + 0.10(2 - 1) = 1.1.

Question 12 of 18

What happens to the boiling point of water when sucrose is dissolved in it?

Explanation: Addition of a non-volatile solute elevates the boiling point of the solvent.

Question 13 of 18

For a solute undergoing trimerization (3 molecules forming 1), the van 't Hoff factor for complete association is:

Explanation: 3 molecules combine to form 1 particle, so the particle count is reduced to 1/3, making i = 1/3.

Question 14 of 18

Which of the following has the maximum van 't Hoff factor?

Explanation: Na3PO4 dissociates into 4 ions (3 Na+ + PO43-), yielding the highest i value of 4.

Question 15 of 18

The abnormal colligative property ratio (Observed value / Calculated value) is known as:

Explanation: By definition, the van 't Hoff factor is the ratio of observed colligative property to the calculated normal colligative property.

Question 16 of 18

Why do concentrated solutions of electrolytes show deviations from ideal colligative behavior?

Explanation: Strong electrostatic forces of attraction and repulsion between ions in concentrated solutions reduce effective particle activity.

Question 17 of 18

What is the expected van 't Hoff factor for K2SO4 undergoing complete dissociation?

Explanation: K2SO4 → 2K+ + SO42-, producing 3 ions per formula unit.

Question 18 of 18

If a solute has a van 't Hoff factor equal to 1.5, the percentage dissociation of the binary electrolyte is:

Explanation: i = 1 + α(2 - 1) ⇒ 1.5 = 1 + α ⇒ α = 0.50 or 50%.

Question 3 of 10
If the observed osmotic pressure of a 0.1 M NaCl solution is twice that of a 0.1 M glucose solution, the van 't Hoff factor for NaCl is:

Solution: π = iCRT. Since concentration and temperature are identical, ratio of osmotic pressures equals the ratio of van 't Hoff factors. i(NaCl) / i(glucose) = 2 / 1 = 2.

Question 4 of 10
The freezing point of a 0.05 molal solution of a non-electrolyte in water is -0.093 °C. If Kf = 1.86 K kg mol-1, what is the van 't Hoff factor?

Solution: ΔT_f = i K_f m ⇒ 0.093 = i × 1.86 × 0.05 ⇒ i = 0.093 / 0.093 = 1.0.
