Dilute Solutions

Dilute solutions are homogeneous mixtures where the solute concentration is significantly lower than the solvent concentration. Colligative properties of these solutions depend solely on the number of solute particles rather than their chemical identity.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Colligative Properties

  • Colligative properties include relative lowering of vapor pressure, boiling point elevation, freezing point depression, and osmotic pressure.
  • These properties are directly proportional to the molality (m) or molarity (C) of the solute particles.
  • For electrolytes, the Van't Hoff factor (i) must be included in the calculation: ΔT = iKfm.

Ideal and Non-Ideal Solutions

  • Ideal solutions obey Raoult's Law at all concentrations and exhibit ΔHmix = 0 and ΔVmix = 0.
  • Positive deviation from Raoult's law occurs when A-B interactions are weaker than A-A or B-B interactions.
  • Negative deviation occurs when A-B interactions are stronger, leading to a decrease in total volume and vapor pressure.

Abnormal Molar Masses

  • Association of solute particles in solution leads to a decrease in the number of particles, resulting in i < 1.
  • Dissociation of solute particles increases the total particle count, resulting in i > 1.
  • The degree of dissociation (α) is related to i by α = (i - 1) / (n - 1), where n is the number of ions per formula unit.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Elevation in Boiling Point
ΔTb = Kbm
Conditions: Dilute solution, non-volatile solute
Units: ΔTb in K, m in mol/kg
Depression in Freezing Point
ΔTf = Kfm
Conditions: Dilute solution, non-volatile solute
Units: ΔTf in K, m in mol/kg
Osmotic Pressure
Π = CRT
Conditions: Dilute solution, semi-permeable membrane
Units: Π in atm, C in mol/L, T in K
Van't Hoff Factor
i = (Observed Colligative Property) / (Calculated Colligative Property)
Conditions: Electrolytic solutes undergoing dissociation or association
Units: Dimensionless
Raoult's Law
PA = XAP°A
Conditions: Ideal solutions at constant temperature
Units: P in Pa or atm, X is mole fraction
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Raoult's Law behavior

Observation: Azeotropic mixtures — Constant boiling mixtures that distill without change in composition due to extreme deviations from ideal behavior.

Exception Colligative property magnitude

Observation: High concentration solutions — Inter-ionic attractions and solute-solvent interactions become significant, causing deviations from the dilute limit.

Exception Van't Hoff factor i = 1

Observation: Strong electrolytes like NaCl — Complete dissociation into ions increases the effective particle count, making i ≈ 2.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Van't Hoff Factor Shortcut
i = 1 + (n-1)α

Application: Use for any electrolyte where n is the number of ions and α is the degree of dissociation.

Boiling Point Elevation Mnemonic
ΔTb = iKbm

Application: Remember 'i' is always included for salts; ignore for non-electrolytes like glucose or urea.

Osmotic Pressure Comparison
Π1 = Π2 (Isotonic)

Application: Set molar concentrations equal for isotonic solutions to solve for unknown molar mass.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Relative lowering of vapor pressure is (P° - P) / P° = Xsolute.
  • Kb (Ebullioscopic constant) depends only on the nature of the solvent.
  • Osmotic pressure is the most sensitive colligative property for determining molar mass of macromolecules.
  • For a 0.1 M solution of NaCl, i is approximately 2.
  • Isotonic solutions have the same osmotic pressure at a given temperature.
  • Reverse osmosis occurs when external pressure exceeds osmotic pressure.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing molality (m) with molarity (M) in freezing point calculations.
  • Forgetting to multiply by the Van't Hoff factor 'i' for ionic solutes.
  • Assuming ΔHmix = 0 for non-ideal solutions.
  • Applying Raoult's law to concentrated solutions where it fails.

⚡ Quick Revision: Dilute Solutions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Vapor Pressure Lowering

The vapor pressure of a solvent decreases upon addition of a non-volatile solute, defined by Raoult's Law.

Boiling Point Elevation

The increase in boiling point is proportional to the molality of the solute particles.

Freezing Point Depression

The decrease in freezing point is proportional to the molality of the solute particles.

Osmotic Pressure

The pressure required to stop the flow of solvent into a solution through a semi-permeable membrane.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Molality (m)
Moles of solute per kg of solvent.
Molarity (M)
Moles of solute per liter of solution.
Mole Fraction (X)
Ratio of moles of one component to total moles.
Ebullioscopic Constant (Kb)
Boiling point elevation per unit molality.
Cryoscopic Constant (Kf)
Freezing point depression per unit molality.
Semi-permeable Membrane (SPM)
Allows only solvent molecules to pass.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔTb = iKbm
  • ΔTf = iKfm
  • Π = iCRT
  • Ptotal = PA + PB
  • i = 1 + (n-1)α

📌 Quick Recall

  • Colligative properties depend on particle count.
  • Raoult's law is the basis for ideal solutions.
  • Van't Hoff factor corrects for dissociation/association.
  • Osmotic pressure is ideal for large molecules.

⚖️ Key Differences

Elevation of BP VS Depression of FP

BP increases with solute; FP decreases with solute.
