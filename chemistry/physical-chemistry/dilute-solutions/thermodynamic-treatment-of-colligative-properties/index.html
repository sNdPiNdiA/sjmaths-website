Thermodynamic Treatment of Colligative Properties

Colligative properties depend exclusively on the number of solute particles rather than their chemical nature in dilute solutions. The thermodynamic foundation relies on the equality of chemical potentials of a solvent in both solution and pure liquid or vapor phases at equilibrium.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Chemical Potential and Equilibrium Foundations

  • The chemical potential of a solvent in solution drops below that of the pure solvent due to increased configurational entropy.
  • At phase equilibrium, the chemical potential of the solvent vapor equals the chemical potential of the solvent in the liquid solution.
  • Raoult's law is derived by equating μA*(l) to μA(g) and utilizing the ideal gas approximation for the vapor phase.

Cryoscopy and Ebullioscopy Derivations

  • The ebullioscopic constant (Kb) is thermodynamically related to the enthalpy of vaporization (ΔHvap) and the gas constant by Kb = RTb2MA / (ΔHvap · 1000).
  • The cryoscopic constant (Kf) is similarly derived from the enthalpy of fusion (ΔHfus) via Kf = RTf2MA / (ΔHfus · 1000).
  • Both boiling point elevation and freezing point depression depend strictly on the latent heats of phase change of the pure solvent.

Osmotic Pressure and van 't Hoff Factor

  • Osmotic pressure arises because solvent chemical potential is equalized across a semipermeable membrane via hydrostatic pressure rather than vapor phase transport.
  • The van 't Hoff factor i corrects colligative equations for electrolytes undergoing dissociation or association in solution.
  • Abnormal molar masses observed experimentally are reconciled using i = (Experimental Molar Mass) / (Calculated Molar Mass).
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Elevation of Boiling Point Thermodynamic Derivation
ΔTb = Kb · m
Conditions: Dilute nonelectrolyte solutions where xB << 1
Units: ΔTb in K, Kb in K kg mol-1, m in mol kg-1
Depression of Freezing Point Thermodynamic Derivation
ΔTf = Kf · m
Conditions: Dilute solutions where solute does not form solid solution with solvent
Units: ΔTf in K, Kf in K kg mol-1, m in mol kg-1
Osmotic Pressure van 't Hoff Equation
Π = c · R · T
Conditions: Very dilute solutions behaving ideally
Units: Π in atm or Pa, c in mol L-1, R in L atm K-1 mol-1
van 't Hoff Factor Correction
i = 1 + α(n - 1)
Conditions: Dissociation or association of solute in solution
Units: i is dimensionless, α is degree of dissociation/association, n is number of particles
Chemical Potential Equilibrium for Vapor Pressure Lowering
μA(T, P) = μA(T, P, xA) = μA(T, P) + RT ln(xA)
Conditions: Ideal dilute solution at constant temperature and pressure
Units: μ in J mol-1, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Colligative properties depend solely on total particle concentration.

Observation: Strong electrolytes at high concentrations deviate significantly from theoretical values. — Interionic electrostatic attractions reduce the effective particle concentration, quantified by the mean ionic activity coefficient (γ±).

Exception Solutes do not enter the solid lattice upon freezing.

Observation: Formation of solid solutions (mixed crystals) where solute replaces solvent in the crystal lattice. — Freezing point depression is diminished because solute lowers chemical potential of both liquid and solid phases simultaneously.

Exception Osmotic pressure obeys the ideal gas law analogy Π = cRT.

Observation: Concentrated solutions exhibit severe positive deviations from the linear concentration dependence. — Finite molecular volume of hydrated solute particles and hydration shell interactions invalidate the ideal osmotic pressure equation.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Enthalpy to Constant Shortcut
Kb ∝ 1 / ΔHvap and Kf ∝ 1 / ΔHfus

Application: Directly compare solvents for freezing or boiling point depression experiments without lengthy calculations.

van 't Hoff Factor Quick Count
i = Total ions per formula unit for strong electrolytes (α = 1)

Application: Instantly determine i for salts like K4[Fe(CN)6] (i = 5) or Al2(SO4)3 (i = 5).

Osmotic Pressure vs Other Properties
Π achieves measurable values at ultra-low concentrations compared to ΔTb or ΔTf.

Application: Use osmotic pressure to determine extremely high molar masses of polymers and biological macromolecules.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Colligative properties are thermodynamic properties of solutions dependent on the number of solute entities.
  • The ebullioscopic constant Kb is a property characteristic of the solvent, independent of the solute identity.
  • Cryoscopic constant Kf depends on the molar mass and latent heat of fusion of the pure solvent.
  • Osmotic pressure equation Π = cRT is analogous to the ideal gas law PV = nRT.
  • Association of benzoic acid in benzene leads to a van 't Hoff factor i < 1.
  • Thermodynamic derivation of freezing point depression assumes pure solid solvent separates at the freezing temperature.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming Kb and Kf depend on the nature of the solute; they depend exclusively on the solvent properties.
  • Failing to account for incomplete dissociation (degree of dissociation α) when calculating the van 't Hoff factor.
  • Confusing molality (m) used in boiling and freezing point equations with molarity (M) used in osmotic pressure.
  • Neglecting temperature dependence of solvent chemical potential when integrating the Gibbs-Helmholtz equation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Thermodynamic Treatment of Colligative Properties

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Chemical Potential and Raoult's Law

Solvent chemical potential lowering in solution dictates all colligative behaviors via thermodynamic equilibrium criteria.

Boiling Point Elevation

ΔTb = Kbm connects microscopic composition to macroscopic boiling point changes using latent heat of vaporization.

Freezing Point Depression

ΔTf = Kfm relies on the separation of pure solid solvent crystals at thermodynamic equilibrium.

Osmotic Pressure and van 't Hoff Correction

ΠV = nRT provides precise molar mass determination for macromolecules, corrected via the van 't Hoff factor i.

Molal boiling point elevation constant in K kg mol-1.
Cryoscopic Constant (Kf)
Molal freezing point depression constant in K kg mol-1.

Ratio of experimental colligative property to normal calculated value.

Number of moles of solute per kilogram of solvent (mol kg-1).

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Kb = RTb2MA / (ΔHvap · 1000)
  • Kf = RTf2MA / (ΔHfus · 1000)
  • ΔTb = i · Kb · m
  • ΔTf = i · Kf · m
  • Π = i · c · R · T

📌 Quick Recall

  • Colligative properties stem from entropy of mixing and chemical potential reduction of the solvent.
  • Thermodynamic constants Kb and Kf are strictly properties of the solvent.
  • Electrolytes require the van 't Hoff factor i to account for particle count modification.
  • Osmotic pressure measurements are ideal for determining high molecular weights.

⚖️ Key Differences

Kb (Molal Boiling Point Elevation Constant) VS Boiling point of solvent

Kb is a thermodynamic constant characteristic of the solvent, whereas boiling point is a physical state property.

Question 1 of 19

What is the primary thermodynamic basis for all colligative properties in dilute solutions?

Explanation: Colligative properties arise because the chemical potential of the solvent in solution is lower than that of the pure solvent due to increased entropy.

Question 2 of 19

Which of the following parameters determines the value of the ebullioscopic constant (Kb) for a solvent?

Explanation: Kb is given by RTb2MA / (ΔHvap · 1000), depending entirely on solvent properties.

Question 3 of 19

If a solute undergoes dimerization in benzene, what is the expected van 't Hoff factor (i)?

Explanation: Dimerization reduces the total number of free particles in solution, resulting in a van 't Hoff factor less than 1.

Question 4 of 19

Which concentration unit is preferred in thermodynamic treatments of boiling point elevation and freezing point depression?

Explanation: Molality is temperature-independent, making it thermodynamically robust for temperature-dependent phase changes.

Question 5 of 19

The osmotic pressure of a dilute solution is directly proportional to:

Explanation: The van 't Hoff equation for osmotic pressure is Π = cRT, where c is the molar concentration (molarity).

Question 6 of 19

How does the formation of a solid solution affect the freezing point depression?

Explanation: If solute enters the solid crystal lattice, it lowers the chemical potential of the solid phase as well, reducing the depression (ΔTf).

Question 7 of 19

What is the van 't Hoff factor for a completely dissociated aqueous solution of Al2(SO4)3?

Explanation: Al2(SO4)3 dissociates into 2 Al3+ and 3 SO42- ions, giving a total of 5 particles (i = 5).

Question 8 of 19

The cryoscopic constant (Kf) is thermodynamically related to which property of the solvent?

Explanation: Kf is derived from the freezing point equilibrium, depending on the enthalpy of fusion (ΔHfus) and freezing point of the solvent.

Question 9 of 19

Why is osmotic pressure preferred over boiling point elevation for determining the molar mass of proteins?

Explanation: Osmotic pressure produces significant, easily measurable heights (Π) even at low concentrations, avoiding thermal degradation of delicate macromolecules.

Question 10 of 19

According to Raoult's law, the relative lowering of vapor pressure of a dilute solution is equal to:

Explanation: The relative lowering of vapor pressure (ΔP / P*) equals the mole fraction of the solute (xB).

Question 11 of 19

For a dilute aqueous solution of glucose, the van 't Hoff factor (i) is approximately:

Explanation: Glucose is a nonelectrolyte that does not dissociate or associate in water, so i = 1.

Question 12 of 19

Which of the following colligative properties is most directly tied to vapor phase equilibrium?

Explanation: Boiling point elevation occurs when the vapor pressure of the solution equals atmospheric pressure, directly involving vapor-liquid equilibrium.

Question 13 of 19

In the thermodynamic equation ΔTb = Kb · m, the unit of Kb is:

Explanation: Kb has units of Kelvin kilogram per mole (K kg mol-1).

Question 14 of 19

What happens to the chemical potential of a solvent when a non-volatile solute is added at constant T and P?

Explanation: Addition of a solute increases entropy and lowers the chemical potential of the solvent (μA < μA*).

Question 15 of 19

Which equation correctly represents the degree of dissociation (α) in terms of the van 't Hoff factor (i) for a binary electrolyte (n = 2)?

Explanation: From i = 1 + α(n - 1), substituting n = 2 yields α = i - 1.

Question 16 of 19

What is the primary reason for deviations from ideal behavior in colligative property measurements of ionic solutions?

Explanation: Electrostatic interactions between ions in solution reduce effective particle concentration, causing deviations.

Question 17 of 19

Which property is NOT a colligative property?

Explanation: Optical rotation depends on chiral molecular structure, not solely on the number of solute particles.

Question 18 of 19

If 1 mole of NaCl is dissolved in 1 kg of water, the freezing point depression is roughly doubled compared to 1 mole of glucose. Why?

Explanation: NaCl dissociates (i ≈ 2), producing twice the number of particles as nonelectrolyte glucose (i = 1).

Question 19 of 19

In the thermodynamic derivation of boiling point elevation, what assumption is made regarding the vapor phase?

Explanation: The vapor phase is assumed to follow the ideal gas law to simplify integration of the Clapeyron equation.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the van 't Hoff factor for acetic acid in benzene where it dimerizes with degree of association α = 0.8.

Solution: For dimerization (n = 2), i = 1 + α(1/n - 1) = 1 + 0.8(0.5 - 1) = 1 - 0.4 = 0.6.

Question 2 of 10
Which thermodynamic property is equated for the derivation of freezing point depression?

Solution: Equilibrium at freezing point requires μA(solution) = μA*(solid).

Question 3 of 10
If the latent heat of vaporization of water is 540 cal g-1 at its normal boiling point (373 K), what is its molal boiling point constant Kb? (R = 2 cal K-1 mol-1)

Solution: Kb = RTb2MA / (ΔHvap · 1000) = (2 · 3732 · 18) / (540 · 1000) ≈ 0.51 K kg mol-1.

Question 4 of 10
Which colligative property measurement is best suited for determining the molar mass of polymers up to 106 g mol-1?

Solution: Osmotic pressure generates measurable millimeter column heights even at extremely low molar concentrations characteristic of high polymers.

Question 5 of 10
A 0.01 molal aqueous solution of K3[Fe(CN)6] freezes at -0.0744 °C. Given Kf = 1.86 K kg mol-1, what is the apparent van 't Hoff factor?

Solution: i = ΔTf / (Kf · m) = 0.0744 / (1.86 · 0.01) = 4.0.

Question 6 of 10
What is the relationship between the ebullioscopic constant Kb and cryoscopic constant Kf for the same solvent?

Solution: Kb involves Tb2 and ΔHvap, whereas Kf involves Tf2 and ΔHfus.

Question 7 of 10
For an electrolyte AxBy undergoing complete dissociation, the total number of ions produced per formula unit is n = x + y. The van 't Hoff factor i equals:

Solution: Complete dissociation yields x + y moles of ions per mole of electrolyte, hence i = x + y.

Question 8 of 10
Under what condition does Raoult's law become an exact thermodynamic law?

Solution: Raoult's law is a limiting law that holds strictly as the mole fraction of the solvent approaches 1 (infinite dilution).

Question 9 of 10
Why does addition of salt to ice cause melting on winter roads?

Solution: Freezing point depression lowers the solution freezing point below ambient temperature, causing ice to melt.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following statements is thermodynamically true for the vapor pressure of a solution containing a non-volatile solute?

Solution: Lower chemical potential of solvent in solution results in a lower escaping tendency, manifesting as reduced vapor pressure.
