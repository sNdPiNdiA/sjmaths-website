First Law of Thermodynamics

The first law of thermodynamics is a statement of the law of conservation of energy applied to thermodynamic systems. It establishes that the internal energy of an isolated system is constant and relates heat, work, and internal energy changes.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Energy as a State Function and Work Sign Conventions

  • Internal energy (ΔU) is a state function, depending only on the initial and final states of the system.
  • Heat (q) and work (w) are path functions, dependent on the specific manner in which the process is carried out.
  • According to IUPAC sign conventions, heat absorbed by the system is positive (+q) and work done on the system is positive (+w).
  • Work done by the system against external pressure carries a negative sign (-w = -PexΔV).

Enthalpy and Heat Capacities

  • Enthalpy (H) is defined as the sum of internal energy and the pressure-volume product, H = U + PV.
  • The heat capacity at constant volume is defined as the partial derivative of internal energy with respect to temperature: Cv = (∂U/∂T)v.
  • The heat capacity at constant pressure is defined as the partial derivative of enthalpy with respect to temperature: Cp = (∂H/∂T)p.
  • For an ideal gas, the difference between molar heat capacities is given by Mayer's relation: Cp,m - Cv,m = R.

Thermodynamic Processes and Isothermal/Adiabatic Changes

  • In an isothermal process at constant temperature, ΔT = 0, which implies ΔU = 0 for an ideal gas, so q = -w.
  • In an adiabatic process, no heat enters or leaves the system (q = 0), resulting in ΔU = w.
  • For a reversible adiabatic expansion of an ideal gas, the relationship between pressure and volume is PVγ = constant, where γ = Cp/Cv.
  • Free expansion of a gas into a vacuum involves zero external pressure (Pex = 0), resulting in w = 0 and, for ideal gases, ΔU = 0 and ΔT = 0.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on temperature (ΔU = nCvΔT)

Observation: Real gases at high pressures and low temperatures — Intermolecular forces become significant, making internal energy a function of volume as well as temperature (∂U/∂V)T ≠ 0.

Exception Work done in a cyclic process is zero since state functions return to initial values

Observation: Net work in a cyclic thermodynamic path enclosing an area on a P-V diagram — Work and heat are path functions; their cyclic integrals (∮ dw and ∮ dq) are non-zero, even though ∮ dU = 0.

Exception Temperature remains constant in an isothermal process (ΔT = 0)

Observation: Joule-Thomson expansion of real gases below the inversion temperature — Real gases undergo cooling or heating during adiabatic throttling due to non-ideal intermolecular forces.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Internal energy and enthalpy are state functions, whereas heat and work are path functions.
  • For any cyclic process, the net change in internal energy is zero (∮ dU = 0).
  • In an isothermal expansion of an ideal gas, ΔU = 0 and ΔH = 0.
  • For an ideal gas, Cp - Cv = R, where R is the universal gas constant.
  • During free expansion of an ideal gas into a vacuum, q = 0, w = 0, and ΔT = 0.
  • The adiabatic index (γ = Cp/Cv) is always greater than 1 for all gases.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing path functions (q, w) with state functions (U, H, P, V, T). State functions have exact differentials (dU), while path functions do not (dq).
  • Ignoring the negative sign in the expansion work formula w = -PexΔV.
  • Assuming ΔU = 0 for isothermal processes involving real gases or chemical reactions.
  • Forgetting to convert units of pressure and volume to standard SI units when calculating work in Joules (1 L·atm = 101.3 J).

⚡ Quick Revision: First Law of Thermodynamics

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Conservation of Energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another, expressed quantitatively as ΔU = q + w.

State vs Path Functions

State properties depend solely on the current state, while path properties depend on the specific historical pathway taken between states.

Enthalpy and Heat Capacities

Enthalpy measures heat flow at constant pressure, linked to temperature changes via heat capacities Cv and Cp.

Adiabatic Transitions

Adiabatic processes occur without heat transfer, resulting in temperature variations driven entirely by expansion or compression work.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Internal Energy (U)
Total microscopic energy contained within a thermodynamic system, measured in Joules.
Enthalpy (H = U + PV)
Heat content of a system at constant pressure, measured in Joules.
Heat (q)
Energy transferred due to a temperature difference, measured in Joules.
Work (w)
Energy transferred through macroscopic displacement against a force, measured in Joules.
Heat Capacity (C = dq/dT)
Heat required to raise the temperature of a system by one Kelvin, measured in J K-1.
Adiabatic Index (γ = Cp/Cv)
Ratio of heat capacities, dimensionless quantity greater than 1.

📌 Quick Recall

  • The first law enforces energy conservation in thermodynamic systems.
  • Internal energy and enthalpy changes depend strictly on initial and final states.
  • Heat and work are non-zero path-dependent quantities.
  • Work formulas vary significantly between reversible and irreversible pathways.

⚖️ Key Differences

Heat (q) VS Temperature (T)

Heat is energy in transit due to thermal gradients, whereas temperature is a state property reflecting average kinetic energy.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the work done when 2 moles of an ideal gas expand reversibly and isothermally from 5 L to 50 L at 300 K.

Solution: w = -2 × 8.314 × 300 × ln(50/5) = -5744 × 2.303 = -13228 J ≈ -11.48 kJ (using R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1).

Question 2 of 10
If a system absorbs 500 J of heat and does 200 J of work on the surroundings, what is the change in internal energy (ΔU)?

Solution: ΔU = q + w = (+500 J) + (-200 J) = +300 J.

Question 3 of 10
For a gaseous reaction: C3H8(g) + 5O2(g) → 3CO2(g) + 4H2O(l), what is Δng?

Solution: Δng = moles of gaseous products - moles of gaseous reactants = 3 - (1 + 5) = -3 (H2O is liquid).

Question 4 of 10
What is the internal energy change when 1 mole of an ideal monoatomic gas is heated from 25°C to 100°C? (Cv,m = 1.5 R)

Solution: ΔU = n Cv ΔT = 1 × (1.5 × 8.314) × 75 = 1870.6 J ≈ 1871 J.

Question 5 of 10
In an adiabatic process, if work done on the gas is 250 J, what is the change in internal energy?

Solution: Since q = 0 in adiabatic processes, ΔU = w. Work done on the gas is +250 J, so ΔU = +250 J.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following thermodynamic quantities is NOT a state function?

Solution: Work (w) depends on the path taken by the system, so it is a path function.

Question 7 of 10
For an ideal gas, (∂U/∂V)T is equal to:

Solution: Internal energy of an ideal gas is independent of volume at constant temperature; thus (∂U/∂V)T = 0.

Question 8 of 10
The unit of heat capacity is:

Solution: Heat capacity is heat absorbed per unit temperature change, having units of J K-1.

Question 9 of 10
Calculate ΔH - ΔU for the vaporization of 1 mole of water at 100°C, assuming water vapor behaves ideally. (R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1)

Solution: Δng = 1 (steam), ΔH - ΔU = ΔngRT = 1 × 8.314 × 373 = 3101 J = 3.10 kJ.

Question 10 of 10
A system undergoes a process in which q = +300 J and w = -300 J. The change in internal energy is:

Solution: ΔU = q + w = (+300 J) + (-300 J) = 0 J.
