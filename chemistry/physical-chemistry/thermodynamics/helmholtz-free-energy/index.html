Helmholtz Free Energy

Helmholtz free energy is a thermodynamic potential that measures the useful work obtainable from a closed thermodynamic system at a constant temperature and volume. Its decrease during any isothermal process represents the maximum amount of work performable by the system.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Definition and State Functions

  • Helmholtz energy is defined strictly as A = U - TS, combining internal energy, temperature, and entropy.
  • It is an extensive property and a state function, meaning its change depends only on initial and final equilibrium states.
  • The total differential dA = -S dT - p dV links Helmholtz energy to thermal and mechanical variables.

Criteria of Spontaneity and Equilibrium

  • At constant temperature and volume (dT = 0, dV = 0), any spontaneous process proceeds with a decrease in Helmholtz energy, dA < 0.
  • At equilibrium under the same constraints, the Helmholtz energy reaches a minimum, yielding dA = 0.
  • If a process increases Helmholtz energy at constant T and V (dA > 0), the process is non-spontaneous.

Relation to Maximum Work

  • The decrease in Helmholtz energy during an isothermal process equals the maximum total work obtainable from the system: -ΔA = wmax.
  • At constant volume, the maximum work is exclusively expansion-free or mechanical work, where -ΔA = w.
  • Maxwell relations derived from dA yield (∂S/∂V)T = (∂p/∂T)V.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Spontaneity is determined exclusively by ΔStotal > 0

Observation: System-specific criteria utilize ΔA or ΔG — Helmholtz and Gibbs free energies incorporate surroundings implicitly by transforming entropy criteria into state functions of the system.

Exception -ΔA equals maximum work for any isothermal process

Observation: Holds true strictly for reversible pathways — Irreversible processes dissipate energy as heat due to finite gradients, making actual work less than -ΔA.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Helmholtz free energy is denoted by A (or F in older literature).
  • The SI unit of Helmholtz free energy is Joules (J) or Joules per mole (J mol-1).
  • For an isothermal, isochoric process, ΔA = wmax.
  • Maxwell relation from Helmholtz energy: (∂A/∂V)T = -p.
  • At absolute zero (T = 0 K), Helmholtz free energy equals internal energy (A = U).
  • Helmholtz energy is particularly useful in treating gases and liquids at constant volume, as well as systems in celestial mechanics or statistical thermodynamics.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Helmholtz energy constraints (constant T, V) with Gibbs energy constraints (constant T, p).
  • Assuming ΔA = w for irreversible processes; it only gives maximum work under reversible conditions.
  • Ignoring the negative sign in -ΔA = wmax during numerical calculations.
  • Treating A as a conserved quantity; it is a thermodynamic potential, not a conserved conservation law like energy.

⚡ Quick Revision: Helmholtz Free Energy

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition and Physical Significance

Helmholtz free energy (A = U - TS) quantifies the capacity of a system to perform work at constant temperature and volume.

Criteria for Spontaneity

A decrease in Helmholtz energy (ΔA < 0) indicates a spontaneous process under isothermal-isochoric conditions.

Exact Differentials and Maxwell Relations

The total differential dA = -S dT - p dV yields powerful Maxwell relations connecting thermal and mechanical properties.

Statistical Thermodynamic Link

Microscopically, Helmholtz energy links directly to the canonical partition function via A = -kBT ln Q.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Internal Energy (U)
Total microscopic energy of a system. SI Unit: J.
Entropy (S)
Measure of thermal unavailability or dispersion. SI Unit: J K-1.
Isothermal (dT = 0)
Process occurring at constant temperature.
Isochoric (dV = 0)
Process occurring at constant volume.
Maximum Work (wmax)
Work obtained under reversible path conditions. SI Unit: J.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • A = U - TS
  • dA = -S dT - p dV
  • (dA)T,V ≤ 0 for spontaneity
  • -ΔA = wmax at constant T and V
  • (∂A/∂V)T = -p

📌 Quick Recall

  • Helmholtz free energy combines energy, temperature, and entropy.
  • It determines spontaneity at constant T and V.
  • Its drop measures maximum available work.
  • It forms the bridge between macroscopic thermodynamics and microscopic partition functions.

⚖️ Key Differences

Helmholtz Energy (A) VS Gibbs Energy (G)

A is used for constant T and V processes, whereas G is used for constant T and p processes.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the change in Helmholtz energy when an ideal gas isothermally expands from 2 L to 10 L at 300 K, given 1 mole of gas (R = 8.314 J mol-1 K-1, ln 5 = 1.609).

Solution: For an isothermal expansion of an ideal gas, ΔU = 0, so ΔA = -TΔS = -nRT ln(V2/V1) = -(1)(8.314)(300)ln(5) = -4015 J.

Question 2 of 10
What is the value of (∂A/∂V)T for a Van der Waals gas?

Solution: Regardless of gas equation of state, the Maxwell/exact differential relation gives (∂A/∂V)T = -p.

Question 3 of 10
Which property is NOT a natural variable for Helmholtz free energy?

Solution: Pressure is a natural variable for Gibbs energy, not Helmholtz energy (which uses volume).

Question 4 of 10
At absolute zero, what does the rate of change of Helmholtz energy with temperature approach?

Solution: Since (∂A/∂T)V = -S, and by Nernst Heat Theorem entropy approaches zero at T=0, the derivative approaches zero.

Question 5 of 10
If a system is at equilibrium at constant temperature and volume, which of the following is true?

Solution: At equilibrium under constant T and V, Helmholtz energy is at a minimum, so dA = 0.

Question 6 of 10
How does Helmholtz energy behave for a closed system undergoing an irreversible isothermal-isochoric process?

Solution: Any spontaneous or irreversible process at constant T and V proceeds with a decrease in Helmholtz energy (ΔA < 0).

Question 7 of 10
The relation between Helmholtz energy (A) and Gibbs energy (G) for a fluid system is:

Solution: Since G = H - TS and H = U + pV, substituting yields G = U + pV - TS = A + pV.

Question 8 of 10
In terms of partition function Q, the internal energy U can be expressed as:

Solution: Internal energy relates to partition function via U = kT2(∂ln Q/∂T)V.

Question 9 of 10
What is the unit of the term (-p dV) in the Helmholtz differential equation?

Solution: Pressure times volume (p dV) has dimensions of energy, measured in Joules.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following is correct for Helmholtz free energy change in a cyclic isothermal process?

Solution: Since Helmholtz energy is a state function, its net change over any complete cyclic process is zero.
