Physical Chemistry

Free Energy

Free energy functions represent the thermodynamic potentials that measure maximum reversible work obtainable from a thermodynamic system at constant temperature and pressure or volume. They serve as fundamental criteria for determining the spontaneity of chemical and physical processes.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Criteria of Spontaneity and Equilibrium

  • A process is spontaneous at constant temperature and pressure if ΔG < 0.
  • A system is at equilibrium when ΔG = 0 and ΔG > 0 indicates a non-spontaneous process.
  • At constant temperature and volume, spontaneity is governed by Helmholtz free energy where ΔA < 0.
  • The total entropy change of the universe (ΔSuniv > 0) is fundamentally equivalent to -ΔG / T > 0 at constant T and P.

Temperature Dependence of Free Energy

  • The variation of Gibbs free energy with temperature is expressed through the relation (∂G / ∂T)P = -S.
  • The Gibbs-Helmholtz equation relates ΔG to ΔH at different temperatures: (∂(ΔG/T) / ∂T)P = -ΔH / T2.
  • Endothermic reactions with positive ΔS become spontaneous only at high temperatures where TΔS > ΔH.
  • Exothermic reactions with negative ΔS are spontaneous only at low temperatures.

Pressure and Concentration Dependence

  • For an ideal gas, the change in Gibbs free energy with pressure at constant temperature is given by dG = V dp.
  • Integrating yields G = G° + RT ln P for a single gas component.
  • For a general reaction, ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q, where Q is the reaction quotient.
  • In electrochemical cells, standard free energy relates to cell potential via ΔG° = -nFE°.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Spontaneous processes always have negative enthalpy change (ΔH < 0)

Observation: Endothermic spontaneous processes (ΔH > 0) — If ΔS is sufficiently positive such that TΔS > ΔH, then ΔG becomes negative, driving spontaneity.

Exception Reactions with positive standard free energy (ΔG° > 0) never proceed

Observation: Coupled reactions in biological systems — Spontaneity is achieved by coupling an unfavorable endergonic reaction with a highly favorable exergonic reaction (like ATP hydrolysis).

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
HOT and COLD Spontaneity Mnemonic
H(+), S(+) → High T; H(-), S(-) → Low T

Application: Quickly determine whether a reaction becomes spontaneous at high or low temperatures by checking the signs of ΔH and ΔS.

Sign of Equilibrium Constant from ΔG°
ΔG° = -ve → K > 1; ΔG° = +ve → K < 1

Application: Instantly deduce whether products or reactants are favored at equilibrium without calculating values.

Electrochemical Cell Work Shortcut
ΔG° = -n F E°

Application: Quickly convert standard cell potential to standard free energy change by multiplying moles of electrons by Faraday constant (96500 C/mol) and E°.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • At constant temperature and pressure, the condition for spontaneity is ΔG < 0.
  • The standard Gibbs free energy of formation for an element in its standard state is zero.
  • The relationship between standard cell potential and equilibrium constant is ln K = nFE° / RT.
  • Gibbs free energy is an extensive property, but molar Gibbs free energy is an intensive property.
  • The maximum non-expansion work obtainable from a system at constant T and P equals -ΔG.
  • At absolute zero, the entropy change for a reaction between pure crystalline solids is zero (Third Law).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing ΔG with ΔG°; ΔG applies to any set of conditions, whereas ΔG° applies strictly to standard states.
  • Neglecting temperature units when substituting into ΔG = ΔH - TΔS (temperature must always be in Kelvin).
  • Assuming ΔH and ΔS are strictly independent of temperature over wide ranges (Kirchhoff's law applies).
  • Using pressure units in atm without converting when standard state pressure is 1 bar.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Gibbs Energy (G)
Thermodynamic potential measuring maximum non-expansion work at constant T and P. Unit: J
Helmholtz Energy (A)
Thermodynamic potential measuring maximum total work at constant T and V. Unit: J
Standard Free Energy (G°)
Free energy change at standard state conditions (1 bar, 298 K). Unit: kJ mol-1
Reaction Quotient (Q)
Ratio of product activities to reactant activities at any given moment.
Equilibrium Constant (K)
Value of reaction quotient at chemical equilibrium.
Faraday Constant (F)
Charge of one mole of electrons, approximately 96485 C mol-1.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔG = ΔH - TΔS
  • ΔG° = -RT ln K
  • ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q
  • ΔG° = -nFE°
  • (∂G / ∂T)P = -S

📌 Quick Recall

  • Free energy functions define limits of thermodynamic work and chemical spontaneity.
  • Negative ΔG guarantees spontaneity at constant temperature and pressure.
  • Standard free energy connects thermodynamics with chemical equilibrium and electrochemistry.
  • Temperature variation of free energy is governed by enthalpy and entropy changes.

⚖️ Key Differences

ΔG VS ΔG°

ΔG is for arbitrary conditions while ΔG° is strictly for reactants and products in standard states.

Question 1 of 19

What is the condition for a process to be spontaneous at constant temperature and pressure?

Explanation: A process is spontaneous at constant T and P if the change in Gibbs free energy is negative (ΔG < 0).

Question 2 of 19

Which equation correctly relates standard Gibbs free energy change to the equilibrium constant?

Explanation: The standard relation is ΔG° = -RT ln K.

Question 3 of 19

What is the derivative (∂G / ∂T)P equal to?

Explanation: From the Maxwell relation derived from dG = V dp - S dT, (∂G / ∂T)P = -S.

Question 4 of 19

For a reaction to be non-spontaneous at all temperatures, what must be the signs of ΔH and ΔS?

Explanation: When ΔH > 0 and ΔS < 0, ΔG = ΔH - TΔS is positive at all temperatures, making it non-spontaneous.

Question 5 of 19

What does Helmholtz free energy (A) represent at constant temperature and volume?

Explanation: Helmholtz free energy change (ΔA) equals the maximum total work done on or by the system at constant T and V.

Question 6 of 19

If the equilibrium constant K of a reaction is greater than 1, what is the sign of ΔG°?

Explanation: Since ΔG° = -RT ln K, if K > 1, ln K is positive, making ΔG° negative.

Question 7 of 19

What is the unit of molar Gibbs free energy?

Explanation: Molar Gibbs free energy is expressed in Joules per mole (J mol-1 or kJ mol-1).

Question 8 of 19

What is the value of ΔG at chemical equilibrium under constant T and P?

Explanation: At equilibrium, the system is at minimum free energy, so ΔG = 0.

Question 9 of 19

Which relation connects cell potential E° with standard free energy change ΔG°?

Explanation: The maximum electrical work is given by ΔG° = -nFE°.

Question 10 of 19

An endothermic reaction (ΔH > 0) with a positive entropy change (ΔS > 0) is spontaneous at:

Explanation: For ΔG = ΔH - TΔS to be negative when both ΔH and ΔS are positive, T must be large (high temperatures).

Question 11 of 19

What is the expression for the Gibbs-Helmholtz equation involving Gibbs free energy?

Explanation: The Gibbs-Helmholtz equation is (∂(ΔG/T)/∂T)P = -ΔH/T2.

Question 12 of 19

Which of the following thermodynamic quantities is a measure of capacity for doing useful work?

Explanation: Gibbs free energy represents the maximum amount of non-expansion work that can be extracted from a system.

Question 13 of 19

For a pure substance, the chemical potential (μ) is equal to:

Explanation: For a pure component, the chemical potential is identically equal to its molar Gibbs free energy.

Question 14 of 19

How does Gibbs free energy change with an increase in pressure for an isothermal ideal gas process?

Explanation: Since dG = V dp, and V is positive, increasing pressure increases Gibbs free energy (G = G° + RT ln P).

Question 15 of 19

Which property is extensive?

Explanation: Gibbs free energy depends on the amount of substance present, making it an extensive property.

Question 16 of 19

What is the standard Gibbs free energy of formation of an element in its reference state?

Explanation: By convention, the standard Gibbs free energy of formation of any element in its stable reference state is zero.

Question 17 of 19

If ΔG for a reaction is +50 kJ mol-1, the reaction is:

Explanation: A positive ΔG indicates that the forward reaction is non-spontaneous.

Question 18 of 19

Which condition defines standard states for gases in thermodynamics?

Explanation: The IUPAC standard pressure for thermodynamic standard states is 1 bar.

Question 19 of 19

What is the relationship between Helmholtz free energy (A) and internal energy (U)?

Explanation: Helmholtz free energy is defined as A = U - TS.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate ΔG° for a reaction with an equilibrium constant K = 10 at 298 K (R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1).

Solution: ΔG° = -RT ln K = -(8.314)(298) ln(10) ≈ -5705 J mol-1 = -5.7 kJ mol-1.

Question 2 of 10
For a given reaction, ΔH = -30 kJ mol-1 and ΔS = -100 J K-1 mol-1. Below what temperature will the reaction become spontaneous?

Solution: For spontaneity, ΔG < 0 → T < ΔH / ΔS = (-30000 J) / (-100 J K-1) = 300 K.

Question 3 of 10
What is the physical significance of Helmholtz free energy (A)?

Solution: -ΔA represents the maximum work obtainable from a system at constant temperature and volume.

Question 4 of 10
Which condition must be met for a reaction to be spontaneous at all temperatures?

Solution: When ΔH < 0 and ΔS > 0, ΔG = ΔH - TΔS is always negative regardless of temperature.

Question 5 of 10
The variation of Gibbs free energy with pressure at constant temperature for 1 mole of an ideal gas is:

Solution: Integrating dG = V dp = (RT/p) dp for finite pressure change gives ΔG = RT ln(p2/p1).

Question 6 of 10
If standard cell potential E° for a 2-electron redox reaction is +1.10 V, what is ΔG°?

Solution: ΔG° = -nFE° = -(2)(96500 C mol-1)(1.10 V) = -212,300 J mol-1 = -212.3 kJ mol-1.

Question 7 of 10
What is the slope of the plot of G/T versus 1/T at constant pressure according to the Gibbs-Helmholtz equation?

Solution: Since (∂(ΔG/T)/∂(1/T))P = ΔH, the slope equals ΔH.

Question 8 of 10
For a spontaneous process in an isolated system, which of the following is true?

Solution: An isolated system has no constant temperature/pressure constraint with surroundings, so spontaneity is dictated by total entropy change (ΔSuniv > 0).

Question 9 of 10
What is the unit of the temperature derivative of Gibbs free energy at constant pressure, (∂G/∂T)P?

Solution: Since (∂G / ∂T)P = -S, its unit is the same as molar entropy, J K-1 mol-1.

Question 10 of 10
In a reversible isothermal expansion of an ideal gas, what is the change in Gibbs free energy (ΔG)?

Solution: For an isothermal expansion of an ideal gas, ΔG = nRT ln(p2/p1). Since p2 < p1, ln(p2/p1) is negative, so ΔG < 0.
