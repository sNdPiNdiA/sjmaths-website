Carnot Cycle

The Carnot cycle is an idealized thermodynamic cycle operating between two thermal reservoirs, establishing the theoretical maximum limit for the efficiency of any heat engine. It consists of two reversible isothermal processes and two reversible adiabatic processes.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Sequence of Thermodynamic Processes in Carnot Cycle

  • Step 1 involves reversible isothermal expansion at temperature TH where heat QH is absorbed from the hot reservoir.
  • Step 2 involves reversible adiabatic expansion where the system cools from TH to TC without heat exchange.
  • Step 3 involves reversible isothermal compression at temperature TC where heat QC is rejected to the cold reservoir.
  • Step 4 involves reversible adiabatic compression where the system temperature rises from TC back to TH.

Efficiency and Temperature Dependency

  • The efficiency of a Carnot engine depends exclusively on the temperatures of the hot and cold reservoirs, independent of the working substance.
  • Maximum theoretical efficiency is achieved only when the temperature of the cold reservoir approaches absolute zero (0 K).
  • No real heat engine can exceed the Carnot efficiency operating between the same two thermal reservoirs due to inherent irreversibilities like friction and thermal dissipation.
  • The coefficient of performance (COP) for a Carnot refrigerator is given by COP = TC / (TH - TC).

Carnot Theorem and Entropy Implications

  • Carnot's theorem states that all reversible engines operating between the same two temperatures have the same efficiency, regardless of the working fluid.
  • For any complete Carnot cycle, the cyclic integral of reduced heat ∮ (dq / T)rev is exactly equal to zero.
  • Entropy change ΔS for a complete reversible cyclic process is zero, establishing entropy as a state function.
  • Irreversible cycles operating between the same reservoirs show a net positive entropy generation, satisfying the Clausius inequality ∮ (dq / T) < 0.

Units: η is dimensionless, TH and TC in Kelvin
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Carnot efficiency approaches 100% as cold reservoir temperature approaches zero

Observation: Third Law of Thermodynamics violation — It is experimentally impossible to attain absolute zero (0 K) in a finite number of steps, rendering 100% efficiency unattainable.

Exception Efficiency depends only on reservoir temperatures

Observation: Real working fluids deviate from ideal gas behavior at high pressures — Real gases exhibit intermolecular forces and compressibility factors Z ≠ 1, causing minor deviations from idealized Carnot work output.

Exception Isothermal steps involve constant internal energy (ΔU = 0) for ideal gases

Observation: Real gases undergoing isothermal expansion/compression show ΔU ≠ 0 — Internal energy of real gases depends on volume/pressure due to potential energy terms arising from intermolecular attractions.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Kelvin Temperature Rule
Always convert Celsius to Kelvin: T(K) = t(°C) + 273.15

Application: Directly substitute Kelvin temperatures into the efficiency formula η = 1 - (TC / TH); using Celsius leads to calculation errors.

Efficiency Shortcut for 50% Rule
TH = 2 TC

Application: Whenever an MCQ states efficiency is 50% (0.5), immediately deduce that the hot reservoir temperature is twice the cold reservoir temperature.

Refrigerator to Engine Conversion
COPmax = TC / (TH - TC)

Application: To find work required by a refrigerator, use QC / W = COP; do not confuse engine efficiency with refrigerator COP.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Carnot efficiency depends solely on the temperatures of the source and sink.
  • The cyclic integral of entropy change for a reversible Carnot cycle is zero (ΔS = 0).
  • Isothermal steps in a Carnot cycle are accompanied by heat transfer, while adiabatic steps are heat-isolated (q = 0).
  • For an ideal gas undergoing a Carnot cycle, PVγ = constant holds true during adiabatic paths.
  • The efficiency of a reversible engine is always greater than or equal to an irreversible engine operating between identical temperatures.
  • The maximum possible efficiency for an engine operating between 500 K and 300 K is 40%.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Using Celsius temperatures instead of Kelvin temperatures in the efficiency formula.
  • Assuming Carnot efficiency depends on the nature of the working fluid or engine design.
  • Confusing the heat absorbed from the hot reservoir (QH) with the net work done (W).
  • Applying ideal gas adiabatic relations (PVγ = constant) to isothermal steps.

⚡ Quick Revision: Carnot Cycle

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Idealized Reversibility

The Carnot cycle employs strictly reversible isothermal and adiabatic transformations to eliminate all sources of mechanical and thermal dissipation.

Universal Upper Limit

Carnot's theorem establishes that no thermodynamic engine can surpass the efficiency of a reversible engine operating between the same temperature bounds.

Temperature Scaling

Absolute thermodynamic temperature scale is defined independently of thermometric substance properties using the Carnot efficiency ratio QC / QH = TC / TH.

Entropy State Function

The reversibility of the Carnot cycle proves that ∫ (dq / T) is independent of path, leading directly to the definition of entropy.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Carnot Cycle (Cyclic Process)
An idealized thermodynamic cycle consisting of two isothermal and two adiabatic reversible processes.
Thermal Efficiency (η)
Ratio of net work output to heat absorbed from the hot reservoir.
Hot Reservoir (Source (TH))
Infinite thermal capacity body supplying heat at constant high temperature.
Cold Reservoir (Sink (TC))
Infinite thermal capacity body absorbing waste heat at constant low temperature.
Coefficient of Performance (COP)
Measure of refrigerator or heat pump effectiveness defined as desired output divided by work input.
Adiabatic Process (q = 0)
Thermodynamic process occurring without heat exchange with the surroundings.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • η = 1 - (TC / TH)
  • QC / QH = TC / TH
  • ∮ (dq / T)rev = 0
  • COPmax = TC / (TH - TC)
  • For adiabatic ideal gas expansion: T1V1γ-1 = T2V2γ-1

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carnot cycle sets the theoretical maximum efficiency limit for heat engines.
  • Efficiency depends only on source and sink absolute temperatures.
  • All four steps of the Carnot cycle are completely reversible.
  • Entropy change over a complete reversible Carnot cycle is zero.

⚖️ Key Differences

Engine Efficiency (η) VS Coefficient of Performance (COP)

Efficiency is always less than 1 (or < 100%), whereas COP for refrigerators can be greater than 1.

Question 1 of 18

The efficiency of a Carnot engine operating between 400 K and 300 K is:

Explanation: η = 1 - (300 / 400) = 1 - 0.75 = 0.25 or 25%.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following processes does not occur during a Carnot cycle?

Explanation: A Carnot cycle consists of two isothermal and two adiabatic processes; isobaric processes are not involved.

Question 3 of 18

If the temperature of the sink is decreased, the efficiency of a Carnot engine:

Explanation: Since η = 1 - (TC / TH), decreasing TC decreases the ratio TC / TH, thereby increasing efficiency.

Question 4 of 18

The entropy change for a complete reversible Carnot cycle is:

Explanation: Entropy is a state function; for any complete cyclic reversible process, ΔS = 0.

Question 5 of 18

What is the efficiency of a Carnot engine operating between 0 °C and 100 °C?

Explanation: TH = 373.15 K, TC = 273.15 K. η = 1 - (273.15 / 373.15) = 1 - 0.732 = 0.268 or 26.8%.

Question 6 of 18

The work done by an ideal gas in an isothermal reversible expansion is given by:

Explanation: For isothermal reversible expansion of an ideal gas, w = -nRT ln(V2 / V1).

Question 7 of 18

Carnot's theorem states that:

Explanation: Carnot's theorem establishes that no engine can be more efficient than a reversible engine operating between the same two temperatures.

Question 8 of 18

For an adiabatic process involving an ideal gas, the relation between pressure and volume is:

Explanation: For a reversible adiabatic process of an ideal gas, PVγ = constant, where γ = CP / CV.

Question 9 of 18

If a Carnot engine absorbs 1000 J of heat from the source and rejects 400 J to the sink, its efficiency is:

Explanation: η = (QH - QC) / QH = (1000 - 400) / 1000 = 600 / 1000 = 0.60 or 60%.

Question 10 of 18

The coefficient of performance (COP) of a Carnot refrigerator operating between 250 K and 300 K is:

Explanation: COP = TC / (TH - TC) = 250 / (300 - 250) = 250 / 50 = 5.

Question 11 of 18

In a Carnot cycle, heat is absorbed during:

Explanation: Heat QH is absorbed exclusively during the isothermal expansion step at TH.

Question 12 of 18

Can a Carnot engine have 100% efficiency?

Explanation: Achieving 100% efficiency requires TC = 0 K, which violates the Third Law of Thermodynamics.

Question 13 of 18

Which thermodynamic property is constant in both adiabatic expansion and adiabatic compression steps?

Explanation: Reversible adiabatic processes are isentropic, meaning entropy (S) remains constant (ΔS = 0).

Question 14 of 18

The ratio of heat absorbed to heat rejected in a Carnot cycle is equal to:

Explanation: QH / QC = TH / TC for a reversible Carnot cycle.

Question 15 of 18

If the efficiency of a Carnot engine is 50% and the sink temperature is 300 K, the source temperature is:

Explanation: 0.5 = 1 - (300 / TH) ⇒ 300 / TH = 0.5 ⇒ TH = 600 K.

Question 16 of 18

Which plot represents a Carnot cycle on a T-S (Temperature-Entropy) diagram?

Explanation: On a T-S diagram, a Carnot cycle forms a simple rectangle bounded by two isotherms and two adiabats.

Question 17 of 18

The working substance in an idealized Carnot engine is typically:

Explanation: Theoretical derivations of Carnot cycle parameters typically assume an ideal gas as the working substance.

Question 18 of 18

Clausius inequality is expressed mathematically as:

Explanation: For any cyclic process, ∮ (dq / T) ≤ 0, where equality holds for reversible cycles.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the work done by a Carnot engine that absorbs 2000 kJ of heat from a reservoir at 600 K and rejects heat to a sink at 300 K.

Solution: η = 1 - (300 / 600) = 0.5. W = η × QH = 0.5 × 2000 kJ = 1000 kJ.

Question 2 of 10
The P-V diagram of a Carnot cycle consists of how many curves?

Solution: A Carnot cycle is bounded by two isothermal curves and two adiabatic curves on a P-V plot.

Question 3 of 10
What is the maximum theoretical work obtainable from a Carnot engine operating between 500 K and 250 K with 5 moles of an ideal gas expanding isothermally from 10 L to 20 L at the higher temperature?

Solution: Work in isothermal expansion w = -nRTH ln(V2 / V1) = -5 × 8.314 × 500 × ln(2) ≈ -14.4 kJ (work done by system).

Question 4 of 10
If two Carnot engines A and B are connected in series such that heat rejected by A is absorbed by B, what is the net efficiency of the combined system?

Solution: Combined efficiency η = 1 - (TC / TH) = ηA + ηB - ηAηB.

Question 5 of 10
In a Carnot refrigerator, if 500 J of heat is removed from the cold body at 200 K and transferred to surroundings at 300 K, the work required is:

Solution: COP = TC / (TH - TC) = 200 / (300 - 200) = 2. COP = QC / W ⇒ W = 500 / 2 = 250 J.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following statements regarding the Carnot cycle is incorrect?

Solution: Carnot efficiency is independent of the working substance, making statement 2 incorrect.

Question 7 of 10
An ideal gas undergoes Carnot cycle where volume doubles during isothermal expansion. The entropy change during this isothermal expansion step is:

Solution: ΔS = nR ln(V2 / V1) = nR ln 2.

Question 8 of 10
For a Carnot engine, the ratio of heat rejected (QC) to work done (W) is given by:

Solution: W = QH - QC ⇒ QC / W = TC / (TH - TC).

Question 9 of 10
If the absolute temperature of both the source and the sink of a Carnot engine are increased by 100 K each, the efficiency of the engine:

Solution: Increasing both TH and TC by the same additive constant decreases the ratio TC / TH, thereby decreasing efficiency.

Question 10 of 10
The area enclosed by a Carnot cycle on a P-V diagram represents:

Solution: The closed area enclosed by any cyclic process on a P-V diagram represents the net work done during one complete cycle.
