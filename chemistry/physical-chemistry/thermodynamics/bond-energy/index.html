Physical Chemistry

Bond Energy

Bond energy is defined as the measure of bond strength in a chemical bond, quantified by the standard enthalpy change required to break a specific bond in 1 mole of gaseous molecules. It depends directly on atomic size, bond multiplicity, hybridization, and electronegativity differences between the bonded atoms.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Factors Influencing Bond Dissociation Enthalpy

  • Bond multiplicity directly increases bond energy; triple bonds are stronger and shorter than double bonds, which are stronger than single bonds.
  • Atomic size is inversely proportional to bond energy; smaller atoms form shorter, overlapping orbitals yielding stronger bonds.
  • Hybridization state dictates s-character; higher s-character (sp > sp2 > sp3) pulls electrons closer to the nucleus, increasing bond strength.
  • Lone pair repulsion decreases bond energy in molecules containing adjacent atoms with lone pairs (e.g., F2, HO-OH).

Thermochemical Calculations Using Bond Energies

  • Enthalpy calculations treat reactants as bonds broken (endothermic, positive sign) and products as bonds formed (exothermic, negative sign).
  • Mean bond energies are utilized when exact localized bond values are unavailable, averaged across various molecular environments.
  • Bond polarity adds ionic resonance stabilization energy, increasing experimental bond energy beyond pure covalent predictions per Pauling's equation.
  • Phase considerations restrict direct bond energy calculations strictly to gaseous state reactants and products to avoid confounding enthalpy of vaporization.

Bond Energy and Thermodynamic Stability

  • Thermodynamic stability correlates with total atomization energy; higher aggregate bond energy reflects a deeper potential energy well.
  • Kinetic stability versus thermodynamic stability must be distinguished; high bond energy often confers chemical inertness under ambient conditions.
  • Bond length shows an inverse empirical correlation with bond energy across homologous series of diatomic halogen and hydride molecules.
  • Thermochemical cycles like Born-Haber utilize bond dissociation energies to determine lattice energies and electron affinities.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Bond energy decreases down a group due to increasing atomic radius.

Observation: Cl-Cl bond energy is greater than F-F bond energy. — Extreme lone pair-lone pair repulsion between small, highly compact fluorine atoms with high electron density weakens the F-F single bond.

Exception Higher bond multiplicity consistently yields proportionally higher bond energies.

Observation: The N-N single bond energy is anomalously lower than expected relative to P-P or other group 15 hydrides. — Strong lone pair-lone pair repulsions on adjacent small nitrogen atoms in hydrazine-like frameworks reduce bond stability.

Exception C-C bond energy remains relatively constant in aliphatic chains.

Observation: Terminal versus internal bond dissociation energies in branched alkanes vary significantly. — Formation of more stable tertiary or secondary alkyl radicals upon homolytic cleavage lowers the specific bond dissociation energy.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Reactants minus Products Sign Rule
ΔH = Bonds Broken (-) - Bonds Formed (+)

Application: Remember 'Broken requires energy (+), Formed releases energy (-)' to instantly fix calculation signs in numericals.

Hybridization s-Character Rule
BE ∝ % s-character

Application: Use when comparing C-H or C-C bond strengths in alkynes (sp, 50%), alkenes (sp2, 33%), and alkanes (sp3, 25%).

Electronegativity Difference Correction
Δ = BE(A-B) - [BE(A-A) × BE(B-B)]1/2

Application: Use Pauling's empirical relation to quickly estimate ionic contribution to bond energy from electronegativity differences.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Bond dissociation energy is always an endothermic process with a positive enthalpy change (ΔH > 0).
  • Bond formation is always an exothermic process with a negative enthalpy change (ΔH < 0).
  • Carbon-carbon bond energy follows the order: C≡C > C=C > C-C.
  • Fluorine has an anomalously low bond dissociation enthalpy of 158 kJ mol-1 compared to chlorine (242 kJ mol-1).
  • Hybridization controls bond strength: sp carbon-hydrogen bonds are stronger than sp3 carbon-hydrogen bonds.
  • Resonance stabilization increases the actual bond energy compared to calculated purely localized single/double bond values.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing bond dissociation energy (specific to a step) with average bond energy (averaged across a molecule).
  • Reversing the reactant and product signs in enthalpy calculations (ΔH = Bonds Broken - Bonds Formed).
  • Assuming bond energy values apply directly to condensed liquid or solid states without accounting for vaporization/sublimation enthalpies.
  • Neglecting lone pair repulsions when evaluating halogen bond energies.

⚡ Quick Revision: Bond Energy

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition and Physical Basis

Bond energy measures the strength of chemical binding by quantifying the energy required for homolytic cleavage in the gas phase.

Periodic Trends

Bond energy decreases down a group with increasing atomic radii, but exceptions occur in period 2 elements due to intense lone pair repulsions.

Thermochemical Applications

Reaction enthalpies are estimated by subtracting energy released in bond formation from energy absorbed in bond breaking.

Hybridization and Multiplicity Effects

Higher s-character and greater bond multiplicity shorten bond lengths and substantially increase bond energies.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Bond Dissociation Enthalpy (D(A-B))
Energy required to cleave a specific bond homolytically in the gas phase.
Average Bond Energy (BE)
Mean enthalpy of a bond type derived from various polyatomic molecules.
Homolytic Cleavage (Free Radical Formation)
Bond breaking where each bonded atom retains one electron.
Heterolytic Cleavage (Ionic Formation)
Bond breaking where one atom retains both bonding electrons.
Atomization Energy (ΔH°atom)
Enthalpy change when a compound is completely broken into gaseous atoms.
Resonance Energy (RE)
Energy stabilization conferred by delocalized electron systems.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔH°rxn = ∑BE(broken) - ∑BE(formed)
  • Bond energy ∝ Bond multiplicity
  • Bond energy ∝ s-character
  • F-F bond energy is anomalously lower than Cl-Cl bond energy
  • All bond breaking processes are endothermic (ΔH > 0)

📌 Quick Recall

  • Bond energy measures covalent bond strength.
  • Calculations require gaseous state reactants and products.
  • Atomic radius and hybridization are primary structural determinants.
  • Thermochemical calculations rely on summation of bond energies.

⚖️ Key Differences

Bond Dissociation Energy VS Average Bond Energy

Dissociation energy applies to specific sequential steps in polyatomic molecules, whereas average bond energy is a mean value across all identical bonds.

Which of the following bonds has the highest bond dissociation energy?

Explanation: Triple bonds possess the highest bond multiplicity and strongest orbital overlap, resulting in maximum bond dissociation energy.

Question 2 of 18

The process of bond breaking is always:

Explanation: Energy must be supplied to overcome attractive forces between nuclei and shared electrons, making bond breaking endothermic (ΔH > 0).

Question 3 of 18

Why is the F-F bond energy lower than the Cl-Cl bond energy?

Explanation: The extremely small atomic radius of fluorine concentrates lone pairs closely together, generating severe electrostatic repulsion that weakens the single covalent bond.

Question 4 of 18

Which C-H bond has the highest bond energy?

Explanation: Carbon in acetylene (C2H2) is sp hybridized, possessing 50% s-character, which holds electrons closer to the nucleus and strengthens the C-H bond.

Question 5 of 18

Calculate ΔH for H2(g) + Cl2(g) → 2HCl(g) given BE(H-H) = 436 kJ/mol, BE(Cl-Cl) = 242 kJ/mol, and BE(H-Cl) = 431 kJ/mol.

Explanation: ΔH = ∑BE(broken) - ∑BE(formed) = (436 + 242) - (2 × 431) = 678 - 862 = -184 kJ/mol.

Question 6 of 18

Homolytic bond cleavage results in the formation of:

Explanation: Homolytic cleavage involves symmetrical splitting of a bond where each atom retains one bonding electron, producing free radicals.

Question 7 of 18

Which group 17 element has the highest bond dissociation energy?

Explanation: Chlorine (Cl2) has the highest bond dissociation energy (242 kJ/mol) because fluorine suffers from lone pair repulsion and bromine/iodine have larger, weaker overlaps.

Question 8 of 18

Which factor decreases bond dissociation energy?

Explanation: Lone pair-lone pair repulsions destabilize the molecule, lowering the energy required to break the bond.

Question 9 of 18

When calculating reaction enthalpy from bond energies, reactants are treated as:

Explanation: Reactant bonds must be broken to form isolated atoms, which is an endothermic process requiring energy input.

Question 10 of 18

The unit of bond energy is typically expressed as:

Explanation: Bond energy is quantified per mole of bonds in standard thermodynamic units of kilojoules per mole.

Question 11 of 18

What is the relationship between bond length and bond energy?

Explanation: Shorter bonds have stronger orbital overlap and higher electrostatic attraction, leading to higher bond energies (inverse relationship).

Question 12 of 18

In a polyatomic molecule like methane (CH4), the four C-H bond dissociation energies are:

Explanation: Each sequential removal of a hydrogen atom changes the electronic and steric environment of the remaining radical, altering individual step dissociation energies.

Question 13 of 18

Which species has the shortest and strongest nitrogen-nitrogen bond?

Explanation: N2 possesses a nitrogen-nitrogen triple bond (≡), making it the shortest and strongest nitrogen-nitrogen bond.

Question 14 of 18

Heterolytic cleavage of a covalent bond A-B produces:

Explanation: Heterolytic cleavage transfers both bonding electrons to the more electronegative atom, generating an ion pair (cation and anion).

Question 15 of 18

Resonance stabilization of a molecule generally causes its experimental atomization energy to be:

Explanation: Delocalization of electrons through resonance provides extra thermodynamic stability, meaning more energy is required to atomize the molecule than calculated from isolated localized bond energies.

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following carbon-carbon single bonds is shortest?

Explanation: An sp-sp carbon-carbon single bond has the highest s-character (50%), pulling nuclei closer and producing the shortest, strongest single bond.

Question 17 of 18

Pauling's equation relating ionic character to bond energy utilizes:

Explanation: Pauling correlated extra ionic resonance energy with the electronegativity difference between bonded atoms.

Question 18 of 18

For an exothermic reaction calculated via bond energies:

Explanation: When more energy is released in bond formation than absorbed in bond breaking (∑BE(formed) > ∑BE(broken)), the overall reaction enthalpy is negative (exothermic).

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the reaction enthalpy for C2H4 + H2 → C2H6 given average bond energies: C=C (614 kJ/mol), C-C (348 kJ/mol), C-H (413 kJ/mol), H-H (436 kJ/mol).

Solution: Bonds broken: 1 C=C (614) + 1 H-H (436) + 4 C-H (1652) = 2702. Bonds formed: 1 C-C (348) + 6 C-H (2478) = 2826. ΔH = 2702 - 2826 = -124 kJ/mol (approx -128 kJ/mol depending on exact dataset).

Question 2 of 10
Which molecule possesses the lowest bond dissociation energy?

Solution: Iodine (I2) has the longest bond length and weakest overlap, resulting in the lowest bond dissociation energy among halogens.

Question 3 of 10

Solution: O2+ has a bond order of 2.5, higher than O2 (2.0), O2- (1.5), and O22- (1.0), leading to the highest bond energy.

Question 4 of 10
Heterolytic fission requires the presence of:

Solution: Heterolytic cleavage is facilitated by polar solvents that stabilize the resulting ionic species (cation and anion).

Question 5 of 10

Question 6 of 10
If the bond energy of X-X is x, Y-Y is y, and X-Y is z, and z > (x + y)/2, the bond X-Y has:

Solution: According to Pauling, when experimental bond energy exceeds the geometric mean of homonuclear bond energies, the bond possesses ionic resonance stabilization.

Question 7 of 10

Solution: Ethane contains a single C-C bond, which has the lowest multiplicity and greatest bond length.

Question 8 of 10

Solution: Bond energies apply to isolated gaseous molecules to eliminate intermolecular forces and vaporization enthalpy contributions.

Question 9 of 10

Solution: HF has the smallest atomic size and highest orbital overlap, resulting in the strongest H-F bond.

Question 10 of 10

Solution: When more energy is released in bond formation than absorbed in bond breaking, the reaction is exothermic.
