Physical Chemistry

Entropy

Entropy is a state function that measures the degree of randomness or thermal disorder of a thermodynamic system. Mathematically, it is defined through statistical probability and thermodynamic reversibility.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Definition and Clausius Inequality

  • Entropy is an extensive property and a state function whose value depends only on the initial and final states.
  • For any cyclic process, the cyclic integral of dq/T is less than or equal to zero: ∮ (dq / T) ≤ 0.
  • The equality holds for a reversible process, whereas the inequality holds for an irreversible process.
  • Entropy change depends solely on the path-independent state change, evaluated via any arbitrary reversible path.

Statistical Interpretation and Microstates

  • Ludwig Boltzmann defined entropy through the number of accessible microstates (Ω) corresponding to a macroscopic state.
  • The Boltzmann constant kB is equal to R / NA, bridging macroscopic gas constants with microscopic probability.
  • According to the Third Law of Thermodynamics, the entropy of a perfectly crystalline substance approaches zero at absolute zero (T = 0 K).
  • Residual entropy arises in crystals with frozen-in orientational disorder, such as carbon monoxide (CO) or ice crystals at 0 K.

Entropy Changes in Chemical and Physical Processes

  • Standard entropy of reaction (ΔS°) is calculated from the difference between absolute standard molar entropies of products and reactants.
  • Processes involving an increase in moles of gas generally exhibit a positive entropy change (ΔS > 0).
  • Trouton's Rule states that most normal liquids have a standard entropy of vaporization of approximately 85 to 88 J K-1 mol-1.
  • The spontaneity criterion requires that the total entropy of the universe must increase for any spontaneous process (ΔSuniv > 0).
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Boltzmann Statistical Entropy
S = kB ln(Ω)
Conditions: Microscopic states and probability distributions
Units: J K-1
Entropy Change of Ideal Gas (T and V)
ΔS = n Cv ln(T2 / T1) + n R ln(V2 / V1)
Conditions: Isothermal or non-isothermal expansion/compression of ideal gases
Units: J K-1
Total Entropy Change (Universe)
ΔSuniv = ΔSsys + ΔSsurr
Conditions: Any spontaneous or non-spontaneous process
Units: J K-1
Phase Transition Entropy
ΔStrans = ΔHtrans / Ttrans
Conditions: At equilibrium phase change (e.g., melting, boiling)
Units: J K-1 mol-1
Clausius Entropy Change
ΔS = ∫ (dqrev / T)
Conditions: Reversible process at constant temperature or variable T
Units: J K-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Dissolution of ionic salts is universally expected to increase entropy due to destruction of crystal lattice.

Observation: Dissolution of certain multivalent ions like Mg2+ or Al3+ can result in a net negative ΔS. — Strong electrostriction and ordering of water hydration spheres around highly charged ions outweigh the lattice breakdown.

Exception Cooling a liquid always decreases its entropy.

Observation: Supercooled liquids undergoing structural glass transition exhibit complex non-equilibrium relaxation. — Trapping of configurational microstates prevents the expected drop in entropy predicted by equilibrium thermodynamics.

Exception Gas phase reactions with fewer product moles than reactant moles have negative entropy changes.

Observation: Reactions involving very large molar masses and high vibrational degrees of freedom can exhibit anomalous entropy trends. — Soft vibrational modes in complex polyatomic molecules contribute heavily to the partition function, increasing entropy despite fewer moles.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Phase Change Trick
ΔS = ΔHvap / Tb

Application: Use Trouton's rule value (~88 J K-1 mol-1) for quick estimations of boiling entropy without complete integral data.

Gas Moles Inspection Rule
Δng = np(gas) - nr(gas)

Application: If Δng > 0, ΔS is almost always positive; if Δng < 0, ΔS is negative.

Isothermal Ideal Gas Shortcut
ΔS = n R ln(V2 / V1) = -n R ln(P2 / P1)

Application: Immediately calculate isothermal expansion entropy without temperature terms when T is constant.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Entropy is an extensive property, whereas molar entropy is an intensive property.
  • The third law of thermodynamics permits the calculation of absolute entropies.
  • The entropy of vaporization for most unassociated liquids is roughly 88 J K-1 mol-1 (Trouton's Rule).
  • For an adiabatic reversible process, entropy change of the system is exactly zero (isentropic process).
  • Spontaneity in an isolated system is dictated entirely by ΔSuniv > 0.
  • Expansion of an ideal gas into a vacuum (free expansion) yields zero work, zero heat, but positive entropy change for the system.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing system entropy change with universe entropy change when evaluating spontaneity.
  • Applying the formula ΔS = q/T to irreversible processes using irreversible heat instead of finding an equivalent reversible path.
  • Ignoring the temperature dependence of heat capacity when calculating non-isothermal entropy changes over wide temperature ranges.
  • Assuming entropy is conserved in all adiabatic processes (it is conserved only in reversible adiabatic processes).

⚡ Quick Revision: Entropy

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Clausius Theorem

Establishes that dq/T for a cyclic process is less than or equal to zero, proving the existence of the state function entropy.

Statistical Entropy

Connects macroscopic thermodynamics to microscopic probability via S = kB ln(Ω).

Third Law and Absolute Entropy

Fixes the entropy reference point to zero at 0 K for perfect crystalline substances, enabling absolute entropy tables.

Criteria for Spontaneity

Dictates that irreversible real processes always increase the net entropy of the isolated universe system.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Entropy (S)
Measure of thermal disorder and energy dispersal, unit J K-1.
Microstate (Ω)
Specific microscopic configuration corresponding to a macroscopic state.
Boltzmann Constant (kB)
Gas constant per molecule, R / NA = 1.38 × 10-23 J K-1.
Isentropic (dS = 0)
Process occurring at constant entropy.
Residual Entropy (S0)
Entropy remaining at T = 0 K due to configurational disorder.
Trouton's Constant (ΔSvap)
Standard entropy of vaporization, ~88 J K-1 mol-1.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔSuniv = ΔSsys + ΔSsurr > 0 for spontaneity
  • S = kB ln(Ω)
  • ΔS = n Cp ln(T2 / T1) - n R ln(P2 / P1)
  • At absolute zero, S = 0 for perfect crystals
  • Free expansion of ideal gas: ΔSsys = n R ln(V2 / V1), ΔSsurr = 0

📌 Quick Recall

  • Entropy measures unavailable energy and molecular randomization.
  • State function evaluated via reversible paths.
  • Statistical mechanics provides the molecular basis for entropy.
  • Third law allows absolute entropy evaluation.

⚖️ Key Differences

System Entropy Change VS Universe Entropy Change

System entropy can decrease (e.g., freezing), but universe entropy must always increase or remain zero for real processes.

Question 1 of 18

What is the unit of molar entropy?

Explanation: Entropy has units of energy divided by temperature, and molar entropy divides this by moles, yielding J K-1 mol-1.

Question 2 of 18

For a reversible adiabatic process, what is the entropy change of the system?

Explanation: A reversible adiabatic process is isentropic, meaning dqrev = 0, so dS = 0.

Question 3 of 18

Which of the following processes has a negative entropy change?

Explanation: Condensation transitions a gas to a liquid, decreasing the spatial randomness and lowering the entropy.

Question 4 of 18

Boltzmann's entropy equation is expressed as:

Explanation: Boltzmann relation connects macroscopic entropy directly to the natural logarithm of microstates: S = kB ln(Ω).

Question 5 of 18

What is the entropy change when 1 mole of an ideal gas expands isothermally from V to 2V?

Explanation: For isothermal expansion of an ideal gas, ΔS = n R ln(V2 / V1) = R ln(2V / V) = R ln 2.

Question 6 of 18

According to the Third Law of Thermodynamics, the entropy of a pure crystalline solid at 0 K is:

Explanation: At absolute zero, a perfect crystal has only one microstate (Ω = 1), so S = kB ln(1) = 0.

Question 7 of 18

Trouton's rule applies to which of the following?

Explanation: Trouton's rule states that the entropy of vaporization of normal unassociated liquids is nearly constant at about 88 J K-1 mol-1.

Question 8 of 18

In a spontaneous process, the total entropy change of the universe (ΔSuniv) must be:

Explanation: The Second Law of Thermodynamics dictates that ΔSuniv > 0 for all spontaneous processes.

Question 9 of 18

Why does ice melting have a positive entropy change?

Explanation: Transitioning from a rigid crystal lattice to a mobile liquid phase increases molecular motion and disorder.

Question 10 of 18

What is the entropy change for the free expansion of an ideal gas into a vacuum?

Explanation: Free expansion is irreversible, but since entropy is a state function, we use a reversible isothermal expansion path giving ΔS = n R ln(V2 / V1).

Question 11 of 18

Which property of entropy is correct?

Explanation: Entropy depends on the quantity of substance (extensive) and depends only on initial and final states (state function).

Question 12 of 18

What is the Clausius inequality for an irreversible thermodynamic cycle?

Explanation: For any irreversible cycle, the cyclic integral of dq/T is strictly less than zero.

Question 13 of 18

Which of the following substances would you expect to have the highest standard molar entropy at 298 K?

Explanation: Gaseous phases possess significantly higher translational freedom and microstates than condensed liquid or solid phases.

Question 14 of 18

When solid iodine sublimes into iodine vapor, the sign of ΔS is:

Explanation: Going from solid to gas drastically increases volume and disorder, resulting in a positive entropy change.

Question 15 of 18

Calculate the entropy change when 2 moles of an ideal gas expand reversibly and isothermally to 10 times its initial volume.

Explanation: ΔS = n R ln(V2 / V1) = 2 × R × ln(10V / V) = 2 R ln 10.

Question 16 of 18

Which factor increases the entropy of a gas sample?

Explanation: Increasing volume provides more microstates for gas molecules to occupy, increasing configurational entropy.

Question 17 of 18

The second law of thermodynamics introduces which important thermodynamic property?

Explanation: The Second Law is fundamentally based on the definition and behavior of entropy.

Question 18 of 18

For a phase transition at equilibrium, the entropy change is given by:

Explanation: At equilibrium phase transition temperatures, ΔS = ΔHtrans / Ttrans.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the entropy change for the fusion of 1 mole of ice at 273 K, given that the enthalpy of fusion is 6.0 kJ mol-1.

Solution: ΔSfus = ΔHfus / Tf = 6000 J mol-1 / 273 K = 21.98 J K-1 mol-1.

Question 2 of 10
For an irreversible isothermal expansion of an ideal gas, which of the following is correct?

Solution: All irreversible processes result in a net increase in the entropy of the universe (ΔSuniv > 0).

Question 3 of 10
What is the residual entropy of a crystal according to Boltzmann if it possesses 3 equivalent orientations at 0 K?

Solution: S0 = kB ln(Ω) = kB ln 3.

Question 4 of 10
Which substance has the lowest molar entropy at 298 K?

Solution: Diamond is extremely rigid with tightly bound carbon atoms and low vibrational degrees of freedom, giving it very low entropy.

Question 5 of 10
In a cyclic reversible process, the total entropy change of the system is:

Solution: Entropy is a state function, so its change over any complete cyclic process is identically zero.

Question 6 of 10
When liquid water freezes to ice at 260 K (below its normal freezing point), what are the signs of ΔSsys and ΔSsurr respectively?

Solution: Freezing decreases system entropy (ΔSsys < 0), while heat released to surroundings increases surroundings entropy (ΔSsurr > 0).

Question 7 of 10
Calculate ΔS when 2 moles of an ideal gas double their volume at constant temperature.

Solution: ΔS = n R ln(V2 / V1) = 2 R ln(2V / V) = 2 R ln 2.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following statements about entropy is incorrect?

Solution: Entropy change depends only on the initial and final states, not on the path taken.

Question 9 of 10
What is the change in entropy when a gas expands adiabatically and reversibly?

Solution: Reversible adiabatic processes are isentropic (dS = 0).

Question 10 of 10
What is the driving thermodynamic force for gas mixing in an isolated container?

Solution: Spontaneous mixing of ideal gases is entirely driven by the increase in configurational entropy.
