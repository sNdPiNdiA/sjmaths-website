Enthalpy of a System

Enthalpy (H) is a thermodynamic state function defined as the sum of internal energy (U) and the product of pressure (P) and volume (V). It represents the total heat content of a system at constant pressure.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Definition and State Functions

  • Enthalpy H is an extensive property and a state function, depending only on the initial and final states of the system.
  • Absolute enthalpy cannot be measured experimentally; only changes in enthalpy (ΔH) can be determined.
  • For an infinitesimal change, the differential form is given by dH = dU + P dV + V dP.

Enthalpy Change in Chemical Reactions

  • The enthalpy of reaction (ΔHrxn) is defined as the difference between the sum of enthalpies of products and reactants: ΔH = ∑Hproducts - ∑Hreactants.
  • Exothermic reactions release heat and have a negative ΔH value, whereas endothermic reactions absorb heat and have a positive ΔH.
  • The relation between enthalpy change and internal energy change involves the change in moles of gas: ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT.

Heat Capacity and Temperature Dependence

  • Heat capacity at constant pressure is defined as Cp = (∂H / ∂T)P.
  • For ideal gases, internal energy and enthalpy depend only on temperature, making (∂U / ∂V)T = 0 and (∂H / ∂P)T = 0.
  • Kirchhoff's Law dictates how the heat of reaction varies with temperature based on the difference in heat capacities between products and reactants.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Enthalpy Change at Constant Pressure
ΔH = qp
Conditions: Closed system doing only pressure-volume work at constant pressure
Units: Joules (J)
Enthalpy and Internal Energy Relation for Ideal Gases
ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT
Conditions: Ideal gas behavior, constant temperature
Units: Δng in moles, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin
Kirchhoff's Equation
ΔH°(T2) = ΔH°(T1) + ΔCp(T2 - T1)
Conditions: Constant heat capacity over the temperature range T1 to T2
Units: ΔCp in J K-1 mol-1
Temperature Dependence of Enthalpy Change (Differential)
(∂ΔH / ∂T)P = ΔCp
Conditions: Constant pressure
Units: J K-1 mol-1
Definition of Enthalpy
H = U + PV
Conditions: Applicable to any thermodynamic system in equilibrium
Units: Joules (J) or kJ mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception For condensed phases (liquids and solids), ΔH is approximately equal to ΔU.

Observation: Under extremely high external pressures, the PV term becomes significant even for condensed phases. — At ultra-high pressures, the volume term multiplied by pressure yields a non-negligible energy contribution.

Exception Internal energy and enthalpy of an ideal gas depend solely on temperature.

Observation: Real gases at high pressure or low temperature show dependence on volume and pressure. — Intermolecular forces and finite molecular volumes cannot be neglected in real gases.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Delta n(g) Sign Shortcut
Δng = (moles of gaseous products) - (moles of gaseous reactants)

Application: Quickly determine if ΔH > ΔU, ΔH < ΔU, or ΔH = ΔU without full calculations.

Reverse Reaction Rule
ΔHreverse = - ΔHforward

Application: Invert the sign of enthalpy when a chemical equation is reversed.

Hess's Law Addition
ΔHnet = ∑(ΔHi)

Application: Add or multiply reaction equations and their corresponding ΔH values algebraically to find target reaction enthalpy.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Enthalpy H is defined mathematically as H = U + PV.
  • At constant pressure, the heat absorbed or released equals the enthalpy change: qp = ΔH.
  • For reactions involving only solids and liquids, ΔH ≈ ΔU because Δng = 0.
  • When moles of gaseous products exceed gaseous reactants (Δng > 0), ΔH is greater than ΔU.
  • Kirchhoff's equation relates reaction enthalpy at two different temperatures using ΔCp.
  • The enthalpy of formation of an element in its standard state is defined as zero.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing internal energy change (ΔU) with enthalpy change (ΔH) during constant volume processes.
  • Forgetting to convert units of R (8.314 J K-1 mol-1) when energies are given in kJ.
  • Ignoring the phase (s, l, g) of reactants and products when calculating Δng.
  • Treating real gases under extreme conditions as ideal gases while applying ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT.

⚡ Quick Revision: Enthalpy of a System

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition and State Function Nature

Enthalpy is an extensive state function combining internal energy and pressure-volume work terms.

Enthalpy and Heat Transfer

At constant pressure, enthalpy change directly measures heat absorbed or evolved by the system.

Ideal Gas Relationships

The relation ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT bridges internal energy and enthalpy changes for gaseous systems.

Temperature Dependence

Kirchhoff's law governs the variation of reaction enthalpy with temperature via heat capacity changes.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Enthalpy (H = U + PV)
Total heat content of a system at constant pressure. SI unit: Joule (J).
Internal Energy (U)
Total microscopic energy of a system. SI unit: Joule (J).
Enthalpy of Reaction (ΔHrxn)
Enthalpy change accompanying a chemical reaction. SI unit: kJ mol-1.
Standard Enthalpy of Formation (ΔHf°)
Enthalpy change when one mole of a substance is formed from its elements in standard states.
Heat Capacity at Constant Pressure (Cp)
Heat required to raise the temperature of a system by 1 K at constant pressure.
Kirchhoff's Equation (d(ΔH)/dT = ΔCp)
Relates rate of change of reaction enthalpy with temperature to heat capacities.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • H = U + PV
  • ΔH = qp
  • ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT
  • (∂H / ∂T)P = Cp
  • ΔHf° of elements in standard state = 0

📌 Quick Recall

  • Enthalpy combines internal energy and flow work (PV).
  • Changes in enthalpy equal heat transferred at constant pressure.
  • Gaseous mole changes dictate the difference between ΔH and ΔU.
  • Kirchhoff's law permits calculating reaction enthalpies at varied temperatures.

⚖️ Key Differences

Enthalpy (H) VS Internal Energy (U)

Enthalpy includes PV work energy accounting for boundary displacement at constant pressure, whereas internal energy does not.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following expressions defines enthalpy (H) correctly?

Explanation: Enthalpy is defined as the sum of internal energy and the product of pressure and volume: H = U + PV.

Question 2 of 19

For a reaction, if Δng = 0, what is the relationship between ΔH and ΔU?

Explanation: Since ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT, when Δng = 0, the term ΔngRT becomes zero, making ΔH = ΔU.

Question 3 of 19

What is the enthalpy change equal to at constant pressure in a closed system doing only expansion work?

Explanation: By definition, at constant pressure, the heat transferred equals the change in enthalpy (ΔH = qp).

Question 4 of 19

In an exothermic reaction, the sign of ΔH is:

Explanation: Exothermic reactions release heat to the surroundings, resulting in a decrease in system enthalpy (ΔH < 0).

Question 5 of 19

Which of the following is an intensive property?

Explanation: Specific heat capacity is heat capacity per unit mass, making it an intensive property, whereas H, U, and Cp are extensive.

Question 6 of 19

For the reaction N2(g) + 3H2(g) → 2NH3(g), what is Δng?

Explanation: Δng = (moles of gaseous products) - (moles of gaseous reactants) = 2 - (1 + 3) = 2 - 4 = -2.

Question 7 of 19

Kirchhoff's equation gives the variation of reaction enthalpy with:

Explanation: Kirchhoff's equation expresses how the enthalpy of reaction changes as a function of temperature.

Question 8 of 19

What is the standard enthalpy of formation of graphite at 298 K?

Explanation: Graphite is the most stable standard reference state of carbon, so its standard enthalpy of formation is defined as zero.

Question 9 of 19

Which thermodynamic property is a state function?

Explanation: Enthalpy is a state function, whereas heat and work are path functions.

Question 10 of 19

If ΔH for a reaction is positive, the reaction is:

Explanation: A positive ΔH indicates heat absorption, characteristic of endothermic processes.

Question 11 of 19

The units of molar enthalpy are:

Explanation: Molar enthalpy is expressed in kilojoules per mole (kJ mol-1).

Question 12 of 19

For an ideal gas, (∂H / ∂P)T is equal to:

Explanation: The enthalpy of an ideal gas depends solely on temperature, so its variation with pressure at constant temperature is zero.

Question 13 of 19

What is the relationship between Cp and Cv for an ideal gas?

Explanation: For one mole of an ideal gas, Mayer's relation states that Cp - Cv = R.

Question 14 of 19

When a gas expands against a constant external pressure, the PV work done by the system is:

Explanation: By IUPAC sign convention, work done by the system expansion is positive (w = PΔV).

Question 15 of 19

The enthalpy change of a reaction does not depend on:

Explanation: Since enthalpy is a state function, its change is independent of the reaction path.

Question 16 of 19

Hess's Law is a direct consequence of:

Explanation: Hess's Law of constant heat summation is based on the first law of thermodynamics (conservation of energy and state function property of enthalpy).

Question 17 of 19

If a reaction is reversed, the value of ΔH:

Explanation: Reversing a reaction reverses the sign of its enthalpy change (ΔHrev = - ΔHfwd).

Question 18 of 19

Which of the following phases has the highest standard entropy at a given temperature?

Explanation: Gases possess higher molecular disorder and spatial freedom, leading to higher entropy compared to liquids and solids.

Question 19 of 19

For the combustion of methane: CH4(g) + 2O2(g) → CO2(g) + 2H2O(l), what is Δng?

Explanation: Δng = (gaseous moles of products) - (gaseous moles of reactants) = 1 - (1 + 2) = 1 - 3 = -2 (water is liquid).

Question 1 of 10
Calculate ΔH for a process where internal energy changes by 500 J and the system performs 200 J of expansion work at constant pressure.

Solution: ΔH = ΔU + PΔV. Here ΔU = 500 J and work done by system (PΔV) = 200 J, so ΔH = 500 + 200 = 700 J.

Question 2 of 10
What is the value of Δng for the vaporization of water: H2O(l) → H2O(g)?

Solution: Δng = (moles of gaseous products) - (moles of gaseous reactants) = 1 - 0 = +1.

Question 3 of 10
If Cp for an ideal gas is 29.1 J K-1 mol-1, what is Cv?

Solution: Cv = Cp - R = 29.1 - 8.314 = 20.786 ≈ 20.8 J K-1 mol-1.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following statements about enthalpy is incorrect?

Solution: Absolute enthalpy cannot be measured directly; only changes in enthalpy can be measured.

Question 5 of 10
For a cyclic process, what is the change in enthalpy (ΔH)?

Solution: Enthalpy is a state function, so its net change over any cyclic process is always zero.

Question 6 of 10
The standard enthalpy of formation (ΔHf°) of which of the following is NOT zero at 298 K?

Solution: Ozone (O3) is an allotrope of oxygen, but its standard stable reference allotrope is O2(g). Thus, ΔHf° of O3(g) is not zero.

Question 7 of 10
If ΔH = -120 kJ mol-1 and ΔS = -40 J K-1 mol-1 for a reaction at 300 K, what is ΔG?

Solution: ΔG = ΔH - TΔS = -120,000 J - (300)(-40) = -120,000 + 12,000 = -108,000 J mol-1 = -108 kJ mol-1.

Question 8 of 10
Which equation correctly represents the standard enthalpy of formation of methane, CH4(g)?

Solution: Standard enthalpy of formation involves forming 1 mole of a substance from its constituent elements in their reference standard states (C as graphite, H as H2 gas).

Question 9 of 10
In an adiabatic process at constant pressure, if a gas expands, what happens to its enthalpy?

Solution: In an adiabatic expansion, work is done at the expense of internal energy, and at constant pressure, enthalpy decreases as temperature drops.

Question 10 of 10
If the temperature dependence of heat of reaction is given by ΔCp = 0, then ΔH of the reaction:

Solution: According to Kirchhoff's law, d(ΔH)/dT = ΔCp. If ΔCp = 0, ΔH is independent of temperature.
