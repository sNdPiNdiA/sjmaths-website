Applications of Clapeyron-Clausius Equation

The Clapeyron-Clausius equation quantitatively relates the temperature dependence of vapor pressure to the enthalpy of phase transition. It is derived from the exact thermodynamic equilibrium condition where chemical potentials of coexisting phases are equal.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Derivation and Approximations for Vaporization

  • The general Clapeyron equation applies to any phase transition of a pure component without approximation.
  • To obtain the Clausius-Clapeyron equation for liquid-vapor equilibria, the molar volume of the liquid Vl is neglected compared to Vv.
  • The vapor phase is assumed to follow the ideal gas law, giving Vv = RT / P.
  • Integration assumes ΔHvap is independent of temperature over moderate intervals.

Phase Transitions: Vaporization, Sublimation, and Melting

  • For liquid-vapor and solid-vapor transitions, ΔH is positive, so vapor pressure always increases with increasing temperature.
  • For ice-water equilibrium, ΔV (ΔV = Vliquid - Vsolid) is negative because ice floats and is less dense than liquid water.
  • Consequently, the melting point of ice decreases with an increase in external pressure.
  • For most substances where Vliquid > Vsolid, the melting point increases with increased pressure.

Boiling Point Elevation and Altitude Variations

  • Atmospheric pressure decreases with altitude, lowering the boiling point of liquids according to the integrated Clausius-Clapeyron relation.
  • The molar enthalpy of vaporization can be experimentally determined by measuring vapor pressures at two different temperatures.
  • Trouton's Rule states that many normal liquids have an entropy of vaporization of approximately 88 J K-1 mol-1.
  • Deviations from Trouton's Rule occur due to strong specific intermolecular interactions such as hydrogen bonding in water, ethanol, or HF.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Clausius-Clapeyron Equation (Vaporization/Sublimation)
d(ln P) / dT = ΔHvap / (R T2)
Conditions: Liquid-Vapor or Solid-Vapor equilibrium, assuming ideal gas behavior and negligible liquid volume
Units: P in atm/torr/Pa, T in K, R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1
Integrated Clausius-Clapeyron Equation (Two-Point Form)
ln(P2 / P1) = (ΔHvap / R) × [(T2 - T1) / (T1 T2)]
Conditions: Assumes constant enthalpy of vaporization over the temperature range T1 to T2
Units: P1, P2 in same units; T1, T2 in Kelvin
Solid-Liquid Melting Pressure Dependence
dP / dT = ΔHfus / [T (Vliquid - Vsolid)]
Conditions: Solid-Liquid phase equilibrium under applied external pressure
Units: P in Pa, T in K, ΔHfus in J mol-1, V in m3 mol-1
Exact Clapeyron Equation
dP / dT = ΔH / (T ΔV)
Conditions: Any two-phase equilibrium in a pure substance
Units: P in Pa, T in K, ΔH in J mol-1, ΔV in m3 mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Melting points of most substances increase with increasing pressure (ΔV > 0)

Observation: Water, Bismuth, Gallium, and Antimony — The solid phase is less dense than the liquid phase (ΔV < 0), causing the melting point to decrease with increased pressure.

Exception Enthalpy of vaporization is treated as constant over wide temperature ranges

Observation: High temperature intervals approaching the critical point — Heat capacities of liquid and vapor differ significantly (Cp,v ≠ Cp,l), making ΔH temperature-dependent via Kirchhoff's equation.

Exception Vapor phase obeys the ideal gas law at low pressures

Observation: High pressures or near critical temperatures — Intermolecular attractive forces and finite molecular volumes cause significant deviations from ideality, requiring compressibility factor corrections.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The exact Clapeyron equation applies to any single-component, two-phase equilibrium system.
  • Clausius-Clapeyron equation assumes vapor behaves as an ideal gas and liquid volume is negligible.
  • For water-ice equilibrium, dP/dT is negative due to anomalous expansion upon freezing.
  • The slope of ln P versus 1/T yields -ΔHvap / R.
  • Trouton's rule gives an approximate constant entropy of vaporization of ~88 J K-1 mol-1 for non-associated liquids.
  • Units must be strictly consistent: pressure in atm/torr/Pa, temperature in Kelvin, enthalpy in Joules.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Using Celsius instead of Kelvin temperature in Clausius-Clapeyron calculations.
  • Assuming liquid volume is negligible in solid-liquid equilibrium (melting/fusion).
  • Forgetting that dV = V2 - V1 where V1 is the initial phase and V2 is the final phase.
  • Confusing the slope of P vs T with the slope of ln P vs 1/T.

⚡ Quick Revision: Applications of Clapeyron-Clausius Equation

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Thermodynamic Foundation

Derived from equality of chemical potentials (μA = μB) along a coexistence curve, yielding exact derivatives for phase boundaries.

Vaporization Dynamics

Neglecting condensed phase volume and utilizing ideal gas laws transforms the exact Clapeyron relation into the integrated linear form for vapor pressure.

Anomalous Phase Behavior

Water exhibits a negative melting point slope with pressure because ice is less dense than liquid water, a cornerstone concept for competitive exams.

Graphical Analysis

Plotting ln P against 1/T produces a straight line whose gradient directly determines the latent heat of vaporization.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Clapeyron Equation (dP/dT = ΔH/(TΔV))
Exact thermodynamic relation for phase equilibria.
Clausius-Clapeyron Equation (d(ln P)/dt = ΔH/(RT2))
Approximate relation for liquid-vapor and solid-vapor systems.
Latent Heat of Vaporization (ΔHvap)
Energy required to vaporize one mole of liquid at constant pressure.
Trouton's Rule (ΔHvap/Tb ≈ 88 J K-1 mol-1)
Empirical rule for normal boiling points of non-associated liquids.
Triple Point (Ttr, Ptr)
Unique point where solid, liquid, and vapor phases coexist in equilibrium.
Coexistence Curve (Phase Boundary)
Line on a P-T diagram separating two phases in thermodynamic equilibrium.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Clausius-Clapeyron assumes ideal gas behavior for the vapor phase.
  • Melting point of ice decreases under pressure (ΔV < 0).
  • Slope of ln P vs 1/T plot is -ΔHvap / R.
  • Units of R must match units of ΔH (8.314 J K-1 mol-1).
  • Trouton's rule fails for liquids with strong hydrogen bonding.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Clapeyron-Clausius connects pressure, temperature, and enthalpy of phase change.
  • Vapor pressure always increases with temperature for vaporization and sublimation.
  • Solid-liquid boundaries depend strictly on whether the solid expands or contracts upon melting.
  • Graphical integration allows rapid estimation of enthalpy changes from vapor pressure data.

⚖️ Key Differences

Exact Clapeyron Equation VS Clausius-Clapeyron Equation

Exact version includes ΔV and applies to all phase changes; Clausius-Clapeyron applies specifically to vaporization/sublimation with volume and ideal gas approximations.

Question 1 of 18

What is the sign of dP/dT for the melting of ice under high pressure?

Explanation: Ice contracts upon melting (ΔV < 0), making ΔHfus / (T ΔV) negative.

Question 2 of 18

Which assumption is NOT made in deriving the Clausius-Clapeyron equation from the Clapeyron equation?

Explanation: Enthalpy of vaporization (ΔHvap) is definitely not zero for a vaporization process.

Question 3 of 18

The slope of a plot of ln P versus 1/T for a liquid-vapor equilibrium is equal to:

Explanation: From d(ln P)/d(1/T) = -ΔHvap / R when using natural logarithm.

Question 4 of 18

Trouton's rule states that the entropy of vaporization for most normal liquids is approximately:

Explanation: Normal non-associated liquids show a vaporization entropy close to 88 J K-1 mol-1.

Question 5 of 18

Why does water have an abnormally high entropy of vaporization compared to Trouton's rule prediction?

Explanation: Extensive hydrogen bonding in liquid water requires extra energy to disrupt, raising ΔSvap.

Question 6 of 18

If the pressure on a liquid is increased, what happens to its boiling point under normal conditions (ΔVvap > 0)?

Explanation: Higher external pressure requires a higher temperature for the vapor pressure to equal the external pressure.

Question 7 of 18

The Clapeyron equation is applicable to which type of system?

Explanation: The Clapeyron equation is a general thermodynamic relation for any pure substance undergoing a phase transition.

Question 8 of 18

In the integrated Clausius-Clapeyron equation, if T2 > T1 and ΔHvap > 0, then P2 is:

Explanation: Vapor pressure increases exponentially with temperature for endothermic vaporization.

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following substances exhibits a negative dP/dT slope for melting?

Explanation: Water is famously known for having ice denser than liquid water at the melting point.

Question 10 of 18

What are the SI units of the term (dP/dT) in the Clapeyron equation?

Explanation: Pressure divided by temperature has units of Pascals per Kelvin.

Question 11 of 18

When applying the Clausius-Clapeyron equation, temperature must always be expressed in:

Explanation: Absolute thermodynamic temperature in Kelvin is mandatory for all gas law and thermodynamic equations.

Question 12 of 18

The vapor pressure of a liquid at two different temperatures T1 and T2 is used to calculate:

Explanation: The two-point Clausius-Clapeyron equation directly relates P1, P2, T1, T2, and ΔHvap.

Question 13 of 18

For sublimation (solid to vapor), the sign of ΔH is:

Explanation: Sublimation is an endothermic process requiring energy input, so ΔHsub > 0.

Question 14 of 18

If ΔV for a phase transition is zero, what is the value of dP/dT according to Clapeyron?

Explanation: Division by zero in the denominator (ΔV = 0) makes dP/dT approach infinity (characteristic of lambda transitions).

Question 15 of 18

Which equation correctly represents the differential Clausius-Clapeyron relation?

Explanation: d(ln P)/dT = ΔHvap / (RT2) is the standard differential form.

Question 16 of 18

At the critical point of a pure substance, the latent heat of vaporization (ΔHvap) becomes:

Explanation: At the critical point, liquid and vapor phases become identical, so ΔHvap = 0 and ΔV = 0.

Question 17 of 18

Cooking food at high altitude takes longer because:

Explanation: Lower atmospheric pressure reduces the boiling point of water, meaning water boils below 100 °C.

Question 18 of 18

The Clausius-Clapeyron equation can be integrated assuming ΔHvap is constant, yielding an equation analogous to:

Explanation: Both the Arrhenius rate constant temperature dependence and the Van 't Hoff equilibrium constant temperature dependence share the exact same mathematical form.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the change in vapor pressure when temperature changes if ΔHvap = 40 kJ/mol and T changes from 300 K to 310 K (approximate).

Solution: Endothermic vaporization (ΔH > 0) dictates that vapor pressure increases with temperature.

Question 2 of 10
What is the primary thermodynamic condition required for deriving the Clapeyron equation?

Solution: Phase equilibrium requires μphase 1 = μphase 2.

Question 3 of 10
In the Clausius-Clapeyron equation, the molar volume of the liquid is neglected because:

Solution: At temperatures well below the critical point, gas volume exceeds liquid volume by factors of 1000 or more.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following has a positive dP/dT for melting?

Solution: Metals like copper contract upon freezing (solid is denser than liquid), so their melting point increases with pressure.

Question 5 of 10
The integration of d(ln P) / dT = ΔH / (RT2) assumes which parameter is constant?

Solution: Standard integration treats ΔHvap as temperature-independent over the integration interval.

Question 6 of 10
If the normal boiling point of a liquid is 350 K and ΔHvap = 35 kJ/mol, what is the approximate entropy of vaporization according to Trouton's rule?

Solution: ΔS = ΔH / Tb = 35000 / 350 = 100 J K-1 mol-1, which is close to Trouton's constant.

Question 7 of 10
What happens to the sublimation pressure of dry ice (solid CO2) when temperature increases?

Solution: Sublimation pressure follows the Clausius-Clapeyron relation with positive ΔHsub, increasing with T.

Question 8 of 10
Which physical property is represented by the numerator in the Clapeyron equation dP/dT = ΔH / (T ΔV)?

Solution: The numerator is ΔH, the latent heat or enthalpy change of the phase transition.

Question 9 of 10
Why does the Clausius-Clapeyron equation fail near the critical temperature?

Solution: Near the critical point, Vliquid approaches Vgas, violating the assumption that liquid volume is negligible.

Question 10 of 10
If dP/dT is positive for a liquid-vapor system, the sign of ΔV must be:

Solution: Since T and ΔHvap are positive, dP/dT being positive requires ΔV to be positive.
