Relationship between Cp and Cv

The heat capacity at constant pressure (&Cp) exceeds the heat capacity at constant volume (&Cv) by the work done by the system during expansion. For an ideal gas, this difference is equal to the universal gas constant R per mole.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Derivation of Mayer's Relation for Ideal Gases

  • Enthalpy is defined as H = U + PV, where U is internal energy, P is pressure, and V is volume.
  • Differentiating enthalpy with respect to temperature at constant pressure yields (∂H/∂T)P = Cp.
  • For one mole of an ideal gas, substituting PV = RT into the enthalpy definition gives H = U + RT.
  • Differentiating with respect to T at constant pressure yields Cp = Cv + R, confirming Cp > Cv.

Thermodynamic Equation of State for Real Systems

  • Internal energy of a real gas depends on both temperature and volume due to intermolecular forces of attraction.
  • The partial derivative (∂U/∂V)T represents internal pressure and is positive for real gases.
  • For liquids and solids, the volume change with temperature is extremely small, making Cp ≈ Cv.
  • Water exhibits a negative thermal expansion coefficient below 4°C, affecting its heat capacity relationships.

Atomicity and Heat Capacity Ratios (γ)

  • For monoatomic ideal gases (e.g., He, Ne), Cv = (3/2)R and Cp = (5/2)R, resulting in γ = 1.67.
  • For diatomic ideal gases (e.g., O2, N2) at room temperature, Cv = (5/2)R and Cp = (7/2)R, giving γ = 1.40.
  • For non-linear polyatomic gases (e.g., H2O, CO2), Cv = 3R and Cp = 4R, yielding γ = 1.33.
  • Vibrational degrees of freedom contribute to heat capacities significantly at higher temperatures.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
General Heat Capacity Difference
Cp - Cv = [P + (∂U/∂V)T] (∂V/∂T)P
Conditions: Any substance or real gas
Units: Cp, Cv in J K-1 mol-1; P in Pa; V in m3; T in K
Ratio of Heat Capacities
γ = Cp / Cv
Conditions: Adiabatic processes and gas atomicity determination
Units: γ is dimensionless
Volume Expansion and Compressibility Relation
Cp - Cv = TVα2 / κT
Conditions: Real gases, liquids, and solids where α is thermal expansion coefficient and κT is isothermal compressibility
Units: T in K, V in m3, α in K-1, κT in Pa-1
Mayer's Relation
Cp - Cv = R
Conditions: One mole of an ideal gas
Units: Cp, Cv, R in J K-1 mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Cp - Cv = R for all gases

Observation: Real gases deviate significantly at high pressure and low temperature — Intermolecular forces and finite molecular volume invalidate the ideal gas equation PV = RT

Exception Cp is always greater than Cv

Observation: Liquid water between 0°C and 4°C — The thermal expansion coefficient α is negative in this range, making Cp less than Cv

Exception Solids have negligible difference between Cp and Cv

Observation: High-temperature crystalline solids — Thermal expansion increases at high temperatures, causing a measurable divergence between Cp and Cv

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Equipartition Theorem Shortcut
Cv = (f/2)R

Application: Count the total degrees of freedom (f) for translational, rotational, and active vibrational modes to find Cv instantly.

Gamma Calculation Mnemonic
γ = 1 + (2/f)

Application: Directly calculate the heat capacity ratio using only the total degrees of freedom (f) without computing individual Cp and Cv.

Mayer's Relation Unit Check
Cp,m - Cv,m = R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1

Application: Always confirm molar heat capacities are used when applying the simple R difference to avoid calorie-to-joule conversion errors.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The difference Cp - Cv represents the external work done by one mole of gas when heated through 1 Kelvin at constant pressure.
  • For any ideal gas, the molar heat capacity difference Cp,m - Cv,m equals the universal gas constant R.
  • For solids and liquids, (∂U/∂V)T is extremely large compared to P, meaning Cp is primarily greater due to volumetric expansion work.
  • Monoatomic gases possess 3 translational degrees of freedom, giving Cv = 1.5R and γ = 1.67.
  • Diatomic gases possess 3 translational and 2 rotational degrees of freedom at room temperature, giving Cv = 2.5R.
  • At extremely low temperatures, vibrational degrees of freedom are frozen out for most diatomic gases.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing specific heat capacity (per gram) with molar heat capacity (per mole) when applying Mayer's relation.
  • Assuming Cp - Cv = R holds true for liquids and solids under standard conditions.
  • Forgetting that γ decreases as atomicity and molecular complexity increase.
  • Applying ideal gas relationships directly to van der Waals gases without incorporating internal pressure correction terms.

⚡ Quick Revision: Relationship between Cp and Cv

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

First Law Foundation

Heat capacities measure the sensitivity of thermal energy storage to temperature changes under defined thermodynamic constraints.

Enthalpy and Expansion Work

Cp exceeds Cv because additional thermal energy is required to perform boundary work during isobaric expansion.

Degrees of Freedom

Molecular structure dictates energy partitioning across translational, rotational, and vibrational modes as defined by the equipartition theorem.

Real Gas Corrections

Intermolecular forces in real gases add an internal pressure component to the heat capacity difference equation.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Heat Capacity at Constant Pressure (Cp)
Quantity of heat required to raise the temperature of a system by 1 K at constant pressure. Unit: J K-1.
Heat Capacity at Constant Volume (Cv)
Quantity of heat required to raise the temperature of a system by 1 K at constant volume. Unit: J K-1.
Mayer's Relation (Cp - Cv = R)
The exact thermodynamic difference between molar heat capacities for an ideal gas.
Heat Capacity Ratio (γ = Cp/Cv)
Dimensionless adiabatic index used in gas expansion calculations.
Thermal Expansion Coefficient (α = (1/V)(∂V/∂T)P)
Fractional change in volume per degree change in temperature at constant pressure.
Isothermal Compressibility (κT = -(1/V)(∂V/∂P)T)
Fractional reduction in volume per unit increase in pressure at constant temperature.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Cp - Cv = R for 1 mole of an ideal gas.
  • Cp,m - Cv,m = nR for n moles of an ideal gas.
  • For monoatomic ideal gases, γ = 1.67.
  • For diatomic ideal gases, γ = 1.40.
  • Cp is always greater than Cv for ideal gases.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Heat capacities quantify energy absorption relative to temperature increments.
  • Mayer's relation bridges microscopic degrees of freedom with macroscopic work terms.
  • Real substances require partial derivative corrections based on internal pressure.
  • Adiabatic processes rely heavily on the heat capacity ratio γ.

⚖️ Key Differences

Cp VS Cv

Cp includes enthalpy changes and expansion work, whereas Cv measures internal energy changes alone.

What is the value of Cp - Cv for 2 moles of an ideal gas?

Explanation: For n moles of an ideal gas, Cp - Cv = nR. For n = 2, the difference is 2R.

Question 2 of 19

Which of the following relations is correct for a real gas?

Explanation: For real gases, intermolecular attractive forces require extra energy to overcome internal pressure, making Cp - Cv greater than R.

Question 3 of 19

What is the heat capacity ratio (γ) for a monoatomic ideal gas?

Explanation: For a monoatomic gas, Cv = 1.5R and Cp = 2.5R, so γ = 2.5/1.5 = 1.67.

Question 4 of 19

Why is Cp greater than Cv for gases?

Explanation: At constant pressure, a gas expands, performing work against the surroundings, which requires extra heat input.

Question 5 of 19

For a diatomic gas at room temperature, what is Cv?

Explanation: A diatomic gas has 3 translational and 2 rotational degrees of freedom, giving Cv = (5/2)R = 2.5R.

Question 6 of 19

What happens to Cp - Cv as an ideal gas approaches zero pressure (infinite dilution)?

Explanation: At very low pressures, all real gases behave ideally, and Cp - Cv approaches R.

Question 7 of 19

For liquid water between 0°C and 4°C, which of the following is true?

Explanation: Water has a negative thermal expansion coefficient between 0°C and 4°C, making Cp less than Cv.

Question 8 of 19

What is the relation between Cp and Cv for a non-linear triatomic gas like SO2 at room temperature?

Explanation: Mayer's relation Cp - Cv = R holds for one mole of any ideal gas regardless of its atomicity.

Question 9 of 19

Which thermodynamic property is equal to Cp?

Explanation: By definition, heat capacity at constant pressure is the partial derivative of enthalpy with respect to temperature at constant pressure.

Question 10 of 19

What is the value of γ for a diatomic gas vibrating actively at high temperatures?

Explanation: Active vibrational modes add 2 degrees of freedom (1 kinetic, 1 potential), making f = 7, so γ = 9/7 ≈ 1.29.

Question 11 of 19

In the general expression Cp - Cv = [P + (∂U/∂V)T] (∂V/∂T)P, what does (∂U/∂V)T represent?

Explanation: (∂U/∂V)T is termed internal pressure, reflecting the change in internal energy with volume at constant temperature.

Question 12 of 19

For an ideal gas, what is the value of internal pressure (∂U/∂V)T?

Explanation: Internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on temperature, so internal pressure (∂U/∂V)T is zero.

Question 13 of 19

Which gas has the highest value of γ?

Explanation: Monoatomic gases like helium have the lowest degrees of freedom (3), resulting in the highest heat capacity ratio γ (1.67).

Question 14 of 19

If Cv of a gas is 20 J K-1 mol-1, what is its Cp assuming ideal behavior?

Explanation: Cp = Cv + R = 20 + 8.314 = 28.314 J K-1 mol-1.

Question 15 of 19

What is the ratio Cp/Cv designated as in thermodynamics?

Explanation: The ratio Cp/Cv is termed the adiabatic index or heat capacity ratio (γ).

Question 16 of 19

Which of the following expressions correctly defines thermal expansion coefficient (α)?

Explanation: Thermal expansion coefficient α is defined as (1/V)(∂V/∂T)P.

Question 17 of 19

For solids at room temperature, Cp and Cv are:

Explanation: Solids have very small thermal expansion coefficients, making Cp and Cv nearly identical at room temperature.

Question 18 of 19

What units are standard for molar heat capacities Cp,m and Cv,m in SI?

Explanation: Molar heat capacities are expressed per mole and per kelvin, yielding J K-1 mol-1.

Question 19 of 19

According to the equipartition theorem, what is the total energy per mole for a monoatomic gas at temperature T?

Explanation: A monoatomic gas has 3 translational degrees of freedom, each contributing (1/2)RT, totaling (3/2)RT.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate Cp for an ideal gas if Cv = 3R.

Solution: Using Mayer's relation, Cp = Cv + R = 3R + R = 4R.

Question 2 of 10
What is the degrees of freedom for a gas if its adiabatic index γ is 1.40?

Solution: Using γ = 1 + (2/f), 1.40 = 1 + (2/f) ⇒ 0.40 = 2/f ⇒ f = 5.

Question 3 of 10
Which expression represents the general thermodynamic identity for Cp - Cv?

Solution: The general thermodynamic relation derived from Maxwell relations is Cp - Cv = TVα2 / κT.

Question 4 of 10
For 3 moles of an ideal monoatomic gas, what is Cv?

Solution: For n moles, Cv = n(3/2)R = 3(1.5R) = 4.5R.

Question 5 of 10
Why does Cp equal Cv for incompressible fluids theoretically?

Solution: If a substance is completely incompressible, α = 0, which makes Cp - Cv = 0.

Question 6 of 10
What is the molar heat capacity at constant volume for a linear triatomic gas like CO2 at high temperature including active vibrations?

Solution: Linear triatomic gas has 3 translational, 2 rotational, and 4 vibrational modes (8 degrees of freedom). With active vibrations, Cv = (f/2)R = 4R or higher depending on temperature excitation.

Question 7 of 10
If the temperature of 1 mole of an ideal gas is increased by 10 K at constant pressure, what is the work done by expansion?

Solution: Work done in expansion at constant pressure is P ΔV = R ΔT = 10R.

Question 8 of 10
Which gas molecule has the lowest ratio of Cp / Cv among the following?

Solution: Polyatomic non-linear molecules like CH4 have the highest degrees of freedom and thus the lowest γ value (1.31).

Question 9 of 10
In an adiabatic expansion of an ideal gas, what is the relation between P and V involving γ?

Solution: The adiabatic reversible expansion of an ideal gas obeys the polytropic relation PVγ = constant.

Question 10 of 10
What is the physical significance of the difference Cp - Cv being positive for all common gases?

Solution: A positive difference reflects the mechanical work executed by the system as it expands under constant pressure.
