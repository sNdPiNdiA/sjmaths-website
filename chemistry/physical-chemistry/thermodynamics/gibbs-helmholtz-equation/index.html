Gibbs–Helmholtz Equation

The Gibbs–Helmholtz equation expresses the temperature dependence of the Gibbs free energy or Helmholtz free energy of a thermodynamic system. It relates the change in free energy of a process to the enthalpy or internal energy change at constant pressure or volume.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Derivation and Thermodynamic Basis

  • The fundamental definition of Gibbs free energy is G = H - TS, which differentiates to dG = VdP - SdT under variable conditions.
  • At constant pressure (dP = 0), dividing by T2 yields the standard temperature differential form linking ΔG and ΔH.
  • The relation confirms that if ΔH is independent of temperature over a small range, ΔG varies linearly with temperature.

Application to Electrochemical Cells

  • In electrochemistry, the maximum electrical work is related to Gibbs free energy via ΔG = -nFE.
  • Substituting into the Gibbs–Helmholtz equation allows the determination of reaction entropy change as ΔS = nF(∂ E∂ T)_P.
  • The temperature coefficient of the cell emf (∂ E∂ T)_P directly yields the reaction enthalpy ΔH through combined equations.

Integral Forms and Van 't Hoff Connection

  • Integrating the Gibbs–Helmholtz equation between two temperatures T1 and T2 allows calculation of ΔG2 if ΔH is known.
  • When applied to chemical equilibria, the equation connects the temperature dependence of the equilibrium constant K to the standard enthalpy change ΔH°.
  • This integration forms the thermodynamic basis for the van 't Hoff equation in chemical kinetics and equilibrium thermodynamics.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Temperature Derivative Form for Gibbs Energy
\left[ ∂ (Δ G / T)∂ T \right]_P = - Δ HT^2
Conditions: Constant pressure, closed system
Units: T in Kelvin (K), ΔH in J mol-1
Gibbs-Helmholtz Equation in terms of A and U
ΔA = ΔU + T \left( ∂ ΔA∂ T \right)_V
Conditions: Constant volume process
Units: ΔA and ΔU in Joules (J), T in Kelvin (K)
Temperature Derivative Form for Helmholtz Energy
\left[ ∂ (Δ A / T)∂ T \right]_V = - Δ UT^2
Conditions: Constant volume, closed system
Units: T in Kelvin (K), ΔU in J mol-1
Gibbs-Helmholtz Equation in terms of G and H
ΔG = ΔH + T \left( ∂ ΔG∂ T \right)_P
Conditions: Constant pressure process
Units: ΔG and ΔH in Joules (J), T in Kelvin (K)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Enthalpy change (ΔH) is generally assumed constant over wide temperature ranges.

Observation: Significant heat capacity changes (ΔCP ≠ 0) — Kirchhoff's law dictates that ΔH varies with temperature according to ΔCP, requiring integration involving heat capacities for precise calculations.

Exception Gibbs free energy always decreases with an increase in temperature for exothermic spontaneous processes.

Observation: Entropy change (ΔS) is negative and dominant — The sign of \left( ∂ ΔG∂ T \right)_P is -ΔS; thus, if ΔS is strongly negative, ΔG increases with temperature.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The Gibbs-Helmholtz equation relates ΔG to ΔH at constant pressure.
  • The differential form is \left[ ∂ (Δ G / T)∂ T \right]_P = - Δ HT^2.
  • For Helmholtz energy, the corresponding energy term is internal energy (ΔU) at constant volume.
  • The temperature coefficient of an electrochemical cell is equal to the entropy change divided by nF.
  • If ΔH is zero, ∂ (Δ G / T)∂ T = 0, meaning ΔG/T is independent of temperature.
  • The integration of the Gibbs-Helmholtz equation leads directly to the van 't Hoff equation for equilibrium constants.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting the negative sign in the temperature derivative expression \left[ ∂ (Δ G / T)∂ T \right]_P = - Δ HT^2.
  • Confusing constant pressure conditions (using ΔH) with constant volume conditions (using ΔU).
  • Assuming ΔH is independent of temperature when precise heat capacity data (ΔCP) is provided.
  • Using incorrect units for temperature (Celsius instead of Kelvin) in logarithmic or exponential integration steps.

⚡ Quick Revision: Gibbs–Helmholtz Equation

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Core Concept 1: Thermodynamic Foundations

The Gibbs-Helmholtz equation bridges macroscopic energy states (enthalpy and internal energy) with free energy changes across temperature gradients.

Core Concept 2: Pressure vs. Volume Control

Constant pressure processes utilize enthalpy (ΔH), whereas constant volume systems rely on internal energy change (ΔU) in the respective formulations.

Core Concept 3: Electrochemical Applications

Coupling thermodynamics with electrochemistry enables the direct determination of reaction enthalpy and entropy from cell potential measurements.

Core Concept 4: Equilibrium Integration

Integrating the differential form yields expressions that dictate how spontaneity and equilibrium constants shift with temperature changes.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Gibbs Free Energy (G)
Maximum non-expansion work obtainable at constant P and T; SI unit J.
Helmholtz Free Energy (A)
Maximum work obtainable at constant V and T; SI unit J.
Enthalpy Change (ΔH)
Heat absorbed or released at constant pressure; SI unit J mol-1.
Internal Energy Change (ΔU)
Heat absorbed or released at constant volume; SI unit J mol-1.
Temperature Coefficient ((∂ E∂ T)_P)
Rate of change of cell potential with temperature; SI unit V K-1.
Entropy Change (ΔS)
Measure of system randomness change; SI unit J K-1 mol-1.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔG = ΔH + T(∂ ΔG∂ T)_P
  • \left[ ∂ (Δ G / T)∂ T \right]_P = - Δ HT^2
  • ΔA = ΔU + T(∂ ΔA∂ T)_V
  • ΔS = nF(∂ E∂ T)_P
  • ΔH = -nFE + nFT(∂ E∂ T)_P

📌 Quick Recall

  • The Gibbs-Helmholtz equation governs the temperature dependence of free energy functions.
  • Pressure constraints apply to Gibbs energy via enthalpy; volume constraints apply to Helmholtz energy via internal energy.
  • Electrochemical cells provide an experimental pathway to evaluate thermodynamic parameters using these equations.
  • Integration of the master differential equation leads to temperature-dependent equilibrium constants.

⚖️ Key Differences

(∂ ΔG∂ T)_P VS \left[ ∂ (Δ G / T)∂ T \right]_P

The former equals -ΔS, while the latter equals -ΔH/T2.

Which thermodynamic property replaces enthalpy (ΔH) when formulating the Gibbs-Helmholtz equation at constant volume?

Explanation: At constant volume, Helmholtz free energy (ΔA) is related to internal energy change (ΔU) rather than enthalpy.

Question 3 of 19

If ΔH for a reaction is positive, how does ΔG/T behave as temperature increases?

Explanation: Since ∂ (Δ G / T)∂ T = -Δ HT^2, a positive ΔH makes the derivative negative, meaning ΔG/T decreases as T increases.

Question 4 of 19

In an electrochemical cell, the temperature coefficient of the emf (∂ E∂ T)_P is directly proportional to:

Explanation: From ΔG = -nFE, differentiating with respect to T gives -nF(∂ E∂ T)_P = -ΔS, hence it is proportional to ΔS.

Question 5 of 19

What is the relation between Gibbs free energy and enthalpy at constant temperature according to Gibbs-Helmholtz?

Explanation: The standard Gibbs-Helmholtz equation is ΔG = ΔH + T(∂ ΔG∂ T)_P.

Question 6 of 19

Which of the following equations represents the Helmholtz energy version of the Gibbs-Helmholtz equation?

Explanation: For Helmholtz energy at constant volume, ΔA = ΔU + T(∂ ΔA∂ T)_V.

Question 7 of 19

If ∂ (Δ G / T)∂ T = 0 for a process, what can be inferred about ΔH?

Explanation: Since the derivative equals -Δ HT^2, a zero derivative implies ΔH must be zero.

Question 8 of 19

What is the SI unit of the Gibbs free energy change (ΔG)?

Explanation: Gibbs free energy is an energy term, expressed in Joules or Joules per mole.

Question 9 of 19

The integration of the Gibbs-Helmholtz equation assuming constant enthalpy yields an equation analogous to:

Explanation: Integration of Gibbs-Helmholtz with respect to temperature leads directly to the van 't Hoff equation for equilibrium constants.

Question 10 of 19

What does the term (∂ ΔG∂ T)_P physically represent?

Explanation: From the total differential dG = VdP - SdT, at constant pressure dG = -SdT, meaning (∂ ΔG∂ T)_P = -ΔS.

Question 11 of 19

In an electrochemical cell, if the emf increases with an increase in temperature, what is the sign of ΔS?

Explanation: Since ΔS = nF(∂ E∂ T)_P, a positive temperature coefficient (∂ E∂ T)_P results in a positive ΔS.

Question 12 of 19

Which condition is mandatory for the validity of the Gibbs-Helmholtz equation relating ΔG and ΔH?

Explanation: The Gibbs-Helmholtz equation for G and H is derived and applied under constant pressure conditions.

Question 13 of 19

What is the value of \left[ ∂ (Δ A / T)∂ T \right]_V?

Explanation: The Helmholtz temperature derivative form is \left[ ∂ (Δ A / T)∂ T \right]_V = - Δ UT^2.

Question 14 of 19

If ΔH is independent of temperature, the plot of ΔG/T versus 1/T is:

Explanation: Integrating ∂ (Δ G / T)∂ (1/T) = Δ H shows that plotting ΔG/T against 1/T gives a straight line with slope ΔH (or -ΔH depending on axis definition).

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following thermodynamic quantities is a state function?

Explanation: Gibbs free energy, enthalpy, and entropy are all state functions whose changes depend only on initial and final states.

Question 16 of 19

For a spontaneous process at constant temperature and pressure, the change in Gibbs free energy (ΔG) must be:

Explanation: The spontaneity criterion at constant P and T is ΔG < 0.

Question 17 of 19

What is the physical significance of the Helmholtz free energy (ΔA)?

Explanation: Helmholtz free energy measures the maximum work obtainable from a closed system at constant temperature and volume.

Question 18 of 19

If the temperature coefficient of a cell is zero, how do ΔH and ΔG relate?

Explanation: If (∂ E∂ T)_P = 0, then ΔS = 0, which means TΔS = 0, leading to ΔG = ΔH.

Question 19 of 19

Which mathematical operation is fundamental in deriving the Gibbs-Helmholtz equation from basic definitions?

Explanation: Partial differentiation of free energy functions with respect to temperature at constant pressure or volume forms the core derivation.

When is ΔG equal to ΔH for a chemical reaction at constant pressure?

What is the primary utility of the Gibbs-Helmholtz equation in chemical thermodynamics?

Solution: The primary utility is finding free energy changes at varying temperatures using enthalpy data.

Which of the following equations links the equilibrium constant K to enthalpy change via Gibbs-Helmholtz integration?
