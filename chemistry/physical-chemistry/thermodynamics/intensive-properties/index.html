Physical Chemistry

Intensive Properties

Intensive properties are thermodynamic variables whose values are independent of the total quantity or mass of matter present in the system. Examples include temperature, pressure, density, and refractive index.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Fundamental Definition and Characteristics

  • An intensive property does not scale with the size of the system and remains constant upon subdivision.
  • The ratio of two extensive properties always yields an intensive property (e.g., mass divided by volume yields density).
  • Intensive properties define the state of equilibrium of a thermodynamic system uniformly throughout its bulk phases.

Thermal and Mechanical Intensive Variables

  • Temperature (T) and Pressure (P) are classic examples of intensive properties that equalize at thermal and mechanical equilibrium.
  • Surface tension and viscosity are intensive properties characteristic of fluid phases independent of total volume.
  • Chemical potential (μ) of a component is an intensive property representing the partial molar Gibbs free energy.

Electrical and Optical Intensive Parameters

  • Refractive index and specific conductance are intensive properties independent of the sample dimensions.
  • Cell potential (E) and pH are intensive electrochemical variables that do not depend on the size of the electrodes.
  • Molar absorptivity (ε) is an intensive spectroscopic property governing light absorption by a chemical species.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Density
ρ = m / V
Conditions: Homogeneous system at constant temperature and pressure.
Units: ρ in kg m-3, m in kg, V in m-3
Molar Heat Capacity
Cm = C / n
Conditions: Pure substance or constant composition mixture.
Units: Cm in J K-1 mol-1
Specific Heat Capacity
c = C / m
Conditions: Mass-independent intensive normalization of heat capacity.
Units: c in J K-1 kg-1
Ratio of Two Extensive Properties
Xintensive = Yextensive / Zextensive
Conditions: Where Y and Z are homogeneous extensive properties like mass and volume.
Units: Derived units depending on the ratio (e.g., kg m-3 for density)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Extensive properties add together when two identical systems are combined.

Observation: Intensive properties do not add; their values remain unchanged upon combination. — Intensive properties represent local state conditions or normalized ratios rather than total system capacity.

Exception Macroscopic bulk properties are generally extensive.

Observation: Properties evaluated per unit quantity (molar, specific, or density-based) become intensive. — Division by mass or moles eliminates the size dependency.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
The Division Rule Mnemonic
Extensive / Extensive = Intensive

Application: Use this ratio test in MCQs to instantly classify ambiguous variables like heat capacity divided by mass.

Subdivision Test
Cut system in half

Application: If the value changes (like volume or total energy), it is extensive. If it stays the same (like temperature or density), it is intensive.

Specific and Molar Prefix Rule
Specific X or Molar X = Intensive

Application: Any extensive property prefixed with 'specific' (per unit mass) or 'molar' (per mole) transforms into an intensive property.

ExtensiveProportional to mass or volumeValues are additive (e.g., V1 + V2 = Vtotal)
🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Temperature is an intensive property.
  • Pressure is an intensive property.
  • Density is an intensive property formed by mass/volume.
  • Specific heat capacity is an intensive property, whereas total heat capacity is extensive.
  • Molar volume is an intensive property.
  • Refractive index and viscosity are intensive properties.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing total internal energy (extensive) with temperature (intensive).
  • Treating molar enthalpy as an extensive property because it contains enthalpy; molar quantities are intensive.
  • Assuming density varies with system size in a homogeneous phase.
  • Forgetting that electric potential is intensive while electrical resistance is extensive.

Heat Capacity (C) VS Specific Heat Capacity (c)
