Second Law of Thermodynamics

The second law of thermodynamics establishes that all spontaneous processes result in an increase in the total entropy of the universe. It sets the directional limit for thermodynamic transformations and dictates that heat cannot spontaneously flow from a colder body to a warmer body without external work.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Entropy as a State Function

  • Entropy (S) is an extensive state function that measures the degree of randomness or disorder in a thermodynamic system.
  • The change in entropy depends only on the initial and final states, given mathematically as ΔS = ∫ (dqrev / T).
  • For any cyclical process operating reversibly, the cyclic integral of dq/T is zero (∮ dqrev/T = 0).

Spontaneity Criteria and the Universe

  • A process is spontaneous if and only if the total entropy change of the system and its surroundings is positive (ΔSuniv > 0).
  • At equilibrium, the total entropy change of the universe is zero (ΔSuniv = 0).
  • Surrounding entropy change is calculated using the heat exchanged with the system: ΔSsurr = -qsys / Tsurr.

Carnot Theorem and Heat Engines

  • The Carnot engine establishes the maximum theoretical efficiency achievable when converting heat into work between two temperatures.
  • No heat engine operating between two given temperatures can be more efficient than a reversible Carnot engine operating between the same temperatures.
  • The efficiency of a Carnot engine depends exclusively on the temperatures of the hot and cold reservoirs, η = 1 - (T2 / T1).
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Entropy Change for an Ideal Gas
ΔS = n Cv,m \ln(T_2T_1) + n R \ln(V_2V_1)
Conditions: Change of state of n moles of an ideal gas.
Units: ΔS in J K-1, Cv,m in J K-1 mol-1, R in J K-1 mol-1
Efficiency of a Carnot Engine
η = 1 - T_2T_1 = WQ_1
Conditions: Operating between two thermal reservoirs at temperatures T1 (source) and T2 (sink).
Units: η is dimensionless, temperatures in Kelvin, work and heat in Joules
Total Entropy Change of the Universe
ΔSuniv = ΔSsys + ΔSsurr > 0
Conditions: Irreversible (spontaneous) process in an isolated system.
Units: ΔS in J K-1
Entropy Change for Isothermal Expansion of Ideal Gas
ΔS = n R \ln(V_2V_1) = n R \ln(P_1P_2)
Conditions: Constant temperature expansion.
Units: ΔS in J K-1
Clausius Inequality
∮ dqrevT ≤ 0 \quad or \quad dS ≥ dqT
Conditions: Any thermodynamic cycle; equality holds for reversible processes, inequality for irreversible processes.
Units: S in J K-1, q in Joules, T in Kelvin
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Entropy of a system always increases during any physical or chemical change

Observation: Local decrease in system entropy (ΔSsys < 0) — Freezing of liquids or condensation of gases decreases system entropy, but this is compensated by a larger increase in surrounding entropy (ΔSsurr > 0), maintaining ΔSuniv > 0.

Exception Entropy of a perfectly crystalline solid is zero at absolute zero

Observation: Substances with residual entropy at 0 K — Molecules with configurational degeneracies or orientational isomerism in the crystal lattice (such as CO, H2O ice) retain non-zero entropy at T = 0 K.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Entropy is an extensive property, whereas specific entropy is intensive.
  • The third law of thermodynamics states that the entropy of a pure, perfectly crystalline substance approaches zero as temperature approaches absolute zero.
  • For an isothermal reversible expansion of an ideal gas, ΔSsys = nR ln(V2/V1) and ΔSsurr = -nR ln(V2/V1), making ΔSuniv = 0.
  • In any spontaneous natural process, the entropy of the universe is continuously increasing.
  • Clausius statement of the second law: Heat cannot spontaneously flow from a cooler body to a warmer body without the intervention of external work.
  • Kelvin-Planck statement: It is impossible to construct an engine that, operating in a complete cycle, will produce no other effect than the absorption of heat from a single reservoir and the performance of an equivalent amount of work.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing system entropy change (ΔSsys) with universe entropy change (ΔSuniv) when determining spontaneity.
  • Forgetting to convert Celsius temperatures to Kelvin when computing entropy or Carnot efficiency.
  • Applying the ideal gas entropy formula ΔS = n Cv ln(T2/T1) + n R ln(V2/V1) to non-ideal systems or condensed phases without modification.
  • Assuming an endothermic process cannot be spontaneous; it can be if the entropy increase of the system overcomes the negative surrounding entropy change.

⚡ Quick Revision: Second Law of Thermodynamics

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Limitation of the First Law

The first law accounts for energy conservation but fails to predict the direction of spontaneous physical and chemical processes, necessitating the second law.

Mathematical Statement of Entropy

Entropy is defined quantitatively via Clausius inequality where dS = dqrev/T, serving as the quantitative metric for unavailable energy.

Spontaneity and the Second Law

All spontaneous processes are accompanied by a net increase in the entropy of the universe (ΔSuniv > 0).

Carnot Cycle Limitations

The Carnot cycle sets the fundamental upper limit on the efficiency of converting thermal energy into mechanical work.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Entropy (S)
A measure of molecular disorder or randomness in a system; units J K-1.
Clausius Inequality (∮ dq/T ≤ 0)
Mathematical criterion distinguishing reversible from irreversible cycles.
Carnot Efficiency (η = 1 - T2/T1)
Maximum theoretical efficiency of a heat engine.
Spontaneous Process (ΔSuniv > 0)
A process that occurs naturally under given conditions without external intervention.
Thermal Reservoir (T)
A body of infinite heat capacity whose temperature remains constant when heat is added or removed.
Trouton's Rule (ΔSvap ≈ 88 J K-1mol-1)
Empirical observation for normal liquids at their boiling points.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔSuniv = ΔSsys + ΔSsurr > 0 for spontaneity
  • ΔS = n Cp,m ln(T2/T1) + n R ln(P1/P2)
  • For an adiabatic reversible process, ΔS = 0 (isentropic process)
  • Carnot efficiency depends only on source and sink temperatures
  • Entropy is a state function; cyclic integral of dS over any cycle is zero

📌 Quick Recall

  • Second law introduces entropy as the directional arrow of time for thermodynamic systems.
  • Spontaneity requires universe entropy to rise.
  • Reversible processes maintain constant universe entropy.
  • Carnot engine defines the upper bound of heat-to-work conversion efficiency.

⚖️ Key Differences

System Entropy (ΔSsys) VS Universe Entropy (ΔSuniv)

Only ΔSuniv must be positive for spontaneity; ΔSsys can be negative if ΔSsurr is sufficiently positive.

Question 1 of 18

What is the entropy change for a reversible adiabatic expansion of an ideal gas?

Explanation: A reversible adiabatic process is isentropic, meaning dq = 0, which results in ΔS = 0.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following conditions is mandatory for a spontaneous process in an isolated system?

Explanation: In an isolated system, surroundings do not exchange heat or matter, so ΔSuniv = ΔSsys > 0.

Question 3 of 18

The efficiency of a Carnot engine working between 400 K and 300 K is:

Explanation: η = 1 - (300 / 400) = 1 - 0.75 = 0.25 or 25%.

Question 4 of 18

Entropy is defined mathematically as:

Explanation: Entropy change is defined exactly as the reversible heat divided by absolute temperature: dS = dqrev / T.

Question 5 of 18

According to the Clausius inequality, for any irreversible cyclic process:

Explanation: For any irreversible cyclic process, the cyclic integral of dq/T is strictly less than zero.

Question 6 of 18

What is the unit of entropy?

Explanation: Molar entropy has units of Joules per Kelvin per mole (J K-1 mol-1).

Question 7 of 18

When liquid water freezes to ice at 260 K (below its normal freezing point), what are the signs of ΔSsys and ΔSuniv?

Explanation: Freezing decreases system entropy (ΔSsys < 0), but since the process is spontaneous below 273 K, ΔSuniv must be positive.

Question 8 of 18

Which thermodynamic property acts as the quantitative measure of unavailable energy for doing work?

Explanation: The product of temperature and entropy (TΔS) represents the energy unavailable for doing work.

Question 9 of 18

For a cyclic heat engine, the net work output is equal to:

Explanation: From energy conservation in a cycle, net work W equals heat absorbed from source minus heat rejected to sink (Q1 - Q2).

Question 10 of 18

Trouton's rule states that the entropy of vaporization for most normal liquids is approximately:

Explanation: Trouton's constant is approximately 88 to 85 J K-1 mol-1 for unassociated normal liquids at their boiling points.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following is an intensive property?

Explanation: Specific entropy (entropy per unit mass) is intensive, whereas total entropy is extensive.

Question 12 of 18

What is the entropy change when 1 mole of an ideal gas expands isothermally to double its volume?

Explanation: ΔS = nR ln(V2/V1) = (1)R ln(2V1/V1) = R ln 2.

Question 13 of 18

Which law of thermodynamics introduced the concept of entropy?

Explanation: The second law of thermodynamics formally introduces entropy as a state function.

Question 14 of 18

A Carnot engine absorbs 500 kJ of heat from a source at 500 K and rejects heat to a sink at 300 K. What is the work done?

Explanation: Efficiency η = 1 - (300/500) = 0.4. Work = η × Q1 = 0.4 × 500 kJ = 200 kJ.

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following processes has a negative entropy change (ΔS < 0)?

Explanation: Condensation goes from gas to liquid, decreasing molecular randomness and therefore lowering entropy.

Question 16 of 18

At chemical equilibrium, the Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) and total entropy change (ΔSuniv) are respectively:

Explanation: At equilibrium, ΔG = 0 and ΔSuniv = 0.

Question 17 of 18

Why is it impossible to construct a perpetual motion machine of the second kind?

Explanation: The Kelvin-Planck statement forbids converting 100% of absorbed heat into work in a cycle, preventing PMM-2.

Question 18 of 18

If the temperature of a thermal reservoir is doubled while keeping the heat exchange constant, how does the entropy change of that reservoir alter?

Explanation: ΔS = q / T. Doubling T while keeping q constant halves the entropy change.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the entropy change for the vaporization of 1 mole of a liquid with a boiling point of 350 K, given its enthalpy of vaporization is 35 kJ mol-1.

Solution: ΔSvap = ΔHvap / Tb = (35000 J mol-1) / (350 K) = 100 J K-1 mol-1.

Question 2 of 10
A refrigerator removes heat from a cold freezer at 250 K and rejects it to a room at 300 K. What is the maximum coefficient of performance (COP)?

Solution: COPmax = Tcold / (Thot - Tcold) = 250 / (300 - 250) = 250 / 50 = 5.

Question 3 of 10
In an irreversible process, the total entropy of the system and surroundings:

Solution: All irreversible/spontaneous processes result in an increase in the total entropy of the universe (ΔSuniv > 0).

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following conditions ensures that a non-spontaneous process at low temperatures becomes spontaneous at high temperatures?

Solution: When ΔH > 0 and ΔS > 0, ΔG = ΔH - TΔS becomes negative only when TΔS > ΔH, which occurs at high temperatures.

Question 5 of 10
The entropy of a substance increases upon heating because:

Solution: Heating populates higher energy microstates, increasing the thermal dispersal of energy and entropy.

Question 6 of 10
What is the entropy change of 1 mole of an ideal gas when it undergoes isobaric heating from 300 K to 600 K? (Cp,m = 29.1 J K-1 mol-1)

Solution: ΔS = n Cp,m ln(T2/T1) = 1 × 29.1 × ln(600/300) = 29.1 × 0.693 = 20.17 J K-1.

Question 7 of 10
Which statement is incorrect regarding entropy?

Solution: The entropy of the universe increases for spontaneous processes, it is only constant for reversible processes.

Question 8 of 10
If a heat engine operates between 1000 K and 500 K with 40% efficiency, how does its performance compare with a Carnot engine?

Solution: Carnot efficiency = 1 - (500/1000) = 50%. Since the engine has 40% efficiency, it is less efficient than a Carnot engine.

Question 9 of 10
The cyclic integral of a state function like entropy over any closed path is always:

Solution: By definition, state functions return to their initial value in a cyclic process, making their cyclic integral zero (∮ dS = 0).

Question 10 of 10
What happens to the entropy of a gas when it is compressed isothermally?

Solution: Isothermal compression decreases the volume, restricting translational freedom and decreasing system entropy (ΔS < 0).
