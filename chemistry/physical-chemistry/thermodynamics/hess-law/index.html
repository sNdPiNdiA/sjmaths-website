Hess's Law of Constant Heat Summation

Hess's law states that the total enthalpy change of a chemical reaction is independent of the pathway taken between the initial and final states. It is a direct consequence of enthalpy being a state function governed by the first law of thermodynamics.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

State Function and Thermodynamic Path Independence

  • Enthalpy (H) is a state function, meaning its change depends solely on initial and final thermodynamic states.
  • Because pathway independence holds true, intermediate thermochemical equations can be algebraically manipulated.
  • Reversing a chemical equation multiplies its corresponding ΔH value by -1.
  • Multiplying a thermochemical equation by a stoichiometric coefficient n scales the ΔH value by the same factor n.

Thermochemical Equations and Algebraic Manipulation

  • Individual reaction steps must cancel out common intermediate species when summing equations to yield the target reaction.
  • Standard enthalpy of formation (ΔHf°) for any element in its standard reference state is defined as exactly zero.
  • Combustion enthalpies (ΔHc°) are always negative (exothermic) as bonds in CO2 and H2O form.
  • Lattice energies and hydration enthalpies can be combined via Born-Haber cycles, which represent an application of Hess's law.

Applications and Limitations of Hess's Law

  • Enthalpy changes for extremely slow or experimentally inaccessible reactions are calculated directly using Hess's law.
  • Theoretical calculations assume reactants and products exist under identical standard states of pressure and temperature.
  • Phase changes must be explicitly accounted for if reactants or products transition between solid, liquid, or gas states across steps.
  • Calculations using mean bond enthalpies provide approximations because local electronic environments affect individual bond strengths.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Enthalpy from Combustion Enthalpies
ΔHrxn = ∑ ΔHc,reactants° - ∑ ΔHc,products°
Conditions: Complete combustion in excess oxygen
Units: kJ mol-1
Bond Enthalpy Calculation
ΔHrxn = ∑ BEbonds broken - ∑ BEbonds formed
Conditions: Gas-phase species at 298 K
Units: kJ mol-1
Temperature Dependence (Kirchhoff's Law)
ΔH(T2) = ΔH(T1) + ∫ ΔCp dT
Conditions: Constant pressure across temperature range
Units: kJ mol-1 K-1
Hess's Law Enthalpy Summation
ΔHrxn = ∑ ΔHf,products° - ∑ ΔHf,reactants°
Conditions: Standard state conditions (1 bar pressure, 298.15 K)
Units: kJ mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Standard enthalpy of formation of elements in their reference state is zero.

Observation: Allotropes not chosen as the standard reference state have non-zero enthalpies of formation. — Graphite is the standard reference state for carbon, whereas diamond has a standard enthalpy of formation of +1.9 kJ mol-1.

Exception Sum of bond energies yields accurate reaction enthalpies.

Observation: Molecules with significant resonance or delocalization show discrepancies. — Resonance stabilization energy lowers actual ground-state enthalpy compared to localized bond energy sums.

Application: Set the sum of all upward energy arrows equal to the sum of all downward energy arrows to solve for missing lattice or electron gain enthalpies.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Enthalpy is an extensive property; doubling reaction stoichiometry doubles ΔH.
  • The standard state pressure is precisely 1 bar (approximately 0.986 atm).
  • Standard enthalpy of formation of O2(g) is 0 kJ mol-1, while O3(g) is +142.7 kJ mol-1.
  • Graphite is the thermodynamically stable reference allotrope of carbon at 298 K and 1 bar.
  • Hess's law is a direct mathematical consequence of the first law of thermodynamics applied to state functions.
  • Phase changes must be included as separate thermochemical steps if temperatures cross melting or boiling points.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting to reverse the sign of ΔH when a thermochemical equation is written in reverse.
  • Failing to multiply ΔH by stoichiometric coefficients when balancing intermediate equations.
  • Using standard enthalpy of formation values for non-reference allotropes assuming they are zero.
  • Confusing the reactant-product subtraction order when using combustion data versus formation data.

⚡ Quick Revision: Hess's Law of Constant Heat Summation

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

State Function Principle

Enthalpy change depends only on initial and final states, making reaction pathways irrelevant to total ΔH.

Algebraic Addition

Thermochemical equations can be added, subtracted, and scaled like algebraic equations to determine unknown reaction enthalpies.

Standard Formation States

ΔHf° values of pure elements in their most stable standard states are assigned a value of zero.

Born-Haber Cycle Application

Ionic crystal lattice energies are calculated by applying Hess's law across atomization, ionization, and electron gain steps.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Enthalpy (H)
Heat content of a system at constant pressure, measured in J.
State Function (State Property)
A property whose value depends only on the current state of the system.
Standard Enthalpy of Formation (ΔHf°)
Enthalpy change when one mole of a compound is formed from its elements in standard states.
Standard Enthalpy of Combustion (ΔHc°)
Enthalpy change when one mole of a substance undergoes complete combustion in oxygen.
Bond Dissociation Energy (BE)
Energy required to break one mole of a specific covalent bond in the gas phase.
Lattice Enthalpy (ΔHlattice)
Energy released when gaseous ions combine to form one mole of an ionic solid.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Reversing equation: ΔH → -ΔH
  • Scaling equation by n: ΔH → nΔH
  • ΔHrxn = ∑ ΔHf(Products) - ∑ ΔHf(Reactants)
  • Reference state of carbon at 298 K is graphite
  • Enthalpy of formation of elements in reference state = 0

📌 Quick Recall

  • Hess's law relies on enthalpy being a state function.
  • Equations combine algebraically to yield overall reaction enthalpy.
  • Combustion and formation data are standard inputs for calculation.
  • Born-Haber cycles apply Hess's law to ionic crystals.

⚖️ Key Differences

Formation Enthalpy Calculation VS Combustion Enthalpy Calculation

Formation uses Products minus Reactants, whereas combustion uses Reactants minus Products.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the reaction enthalpy for 4Fe(s) + 3O2(g) → 2Fe2O3(s) given ΔHf° of Fe2O3(s) is -824 kJ mol-1.

Solution: ΔH = 2(-824) - [4(0) + 3(0)] = -1648 kJ for 2 moles, but for 4 moles it is 4 × (-824) / 2 = -1648 kJ. Wait, 2 moles of Fe2O3 is formed: 2 × (-824) = -1648 kJ.

Question 2 of 10
Which condition is necessary for applying Hess's law?

Solution: Enthalpy changes depend on temperature and pressure, so intermediate steps must share standard conditions.

Question 3 of 10
If ΔHc of CH4 is -890 kJ mol-1, what is the heat evolved when 8 g of CH4 undergoes combustion?

Solution: Molar mass of CH4 is 16 g mol-1. 8 g is 0.5 mole. 0.5 × 890 = 445 kJ.

Question 4 of 10
The enthalpy change for a reaction does not depend on:

Solution: Path independence means intermediate steps do not affect the overall enthalpy change.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following compounds has a standard enthalpy of formation equal to zero?

Solution: Bromine is a liquid (Br2(l)) in its standard reference state at 298 K.

Question 6 of 10
Calculate ΔH for CO(g) + 1/2 O2(g) → CO2(g) given ΔHf(°CO) = -110.5 kJ mol-1 and ΔHf(°CO2) = -393.5 kJ mol-1.

Solution: ΔH = (-393.5) - (-110.5 + 0) = -283.0 kJ mol-1.

Question 7 of 10
What is the standard enthalpy change when a substance burns completely in oxygen?

Solution: Complete combustion in excess oxygen defines the standard enthalpy of combustion.

Question 8 of 10
If reaction 1 has ΔH1 and reaction 2 (the reverse of reaction 1) has ΔH2, then:

Solution: Reversing a reaction negates its enthalpy value, so ΔH1 + (-ΔH1) = 0.

Question 9 of 10
Which thermodynamic property is directly utilized in formulating Hess's law?

Solution: Hess's law specifically deals with the summation of enthalpy changes.

Question 10 of 10
When calculating reaction enthalpy from bond energies, the formula uses:

Solution: Bond energy calculation is unique: ∑ BE(bonds broken, reactants) - ∑ BE(bonds formed, products).
