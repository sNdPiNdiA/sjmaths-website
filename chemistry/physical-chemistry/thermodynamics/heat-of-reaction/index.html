Physical Chemistry

Heat of Reaction

Heat of reaction represents the amount of heat absorbed or evolved during a chemical reaction at a specified temperature and pressure or volume. It is fundamentally equal to the change in enthalpy (ΔH) at constant pressure or internal energy (ΔU) at constant volume.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Functions and State Relations

  • The heat of reaction at constant volume equals the internal energy change, ΔU = qv.
  • The heat of reaction at constant pressure equals the enthalpy change, ΔH = qp.
  • The term Δng denotes the change in the number of moles of gaseous products minus gaseous reactants.
  • For reactions involving only solids and liquids, Δng = 0, making ΔH = ΔU.

Standard Enthalpies and Hess's Law

  • The standard state refers to a substance at 1 bar pressure and a specified temperature (usually 298.15 K).
  • The standard enthalpy of formation of any element in its most stable allotropic form is defined as zero.
  • Hess's Law states that the total enthalpy change of a reaction is independent of the path taken, depending only on initial and final states.
  • Reaction enthalpies are additive; if a chemical equation is reversed, the sign of ΔH is inverted.

Temperature Dependence and Bond Energies

  • Kirchhoff's equation dictates how enthalpy of reaction varies with temperature using heat capacity differences (ΔCp or ΔCv).
  • Bond dissociation enthalpy is always a positive quantity as energy must be supplied to break covalent bonds.
  • Resonance energy is calculated as the difference between the experimental heat of formation and the calculated heat of formation from bond energies.
  • Combustion reactions are invariably exothermic, yielding negative ΔH values.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Kirchhoff's Equation
ΔHT2 = ΔHT1 + ΔCp(T2 - T1)
Conditions: Constant pressure, temperature independent heat capacity over interval
Units: ΔCp in J K-1 mol-1
Hess's Law of Constant Heat Summation
ΔHreaction = ∑ΔHf⁰(products) - ∑ΔHf⁰(reactants)
Conditions: Standard states, any multi-step reaction
Units: ΔH in kJ mol-1
Bond Enthalpy Relation
ΔH = ∑BE(reactants broken) - ∑BE(products formed)
Conditions: Gaseous state reactants and products
Units: BE in kJ mol-1
Integral Heat of Solution
ΔHs = Hsolution - (Hsolute + n Hsolvent)
Conditions: Specified moles of solvent (n)
Units: kJ mol-1
Enthalpy and Internal Energy Relation
ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT
Conditions: Ideal gas behavior, constant temperature
Units: ΔH, ΔU in kJ mol-1; R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Enthalpy of formation of elements in standard state is zero

Observation: Ozone (O3) or diamond allotropes — Graphite and O2(g) are the designated standard reference states; diamond and ozone have non-zero standard enthalpies of formation.

Exception Bond enthalpies are constant across different molecular environments

Observation: C-H bond in methane vs methanol or benzene — Local electronic environment, hybridization, and inductive effects alter individual bond strengths.

Exception Neutralization of strong acid and strong base is constant (-57.1 kJ)

Observation: Weak acids or weak bases — Part of the heat evolved is consumed in the dissociation of the weak electrolyte.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Delta Ng Sign Trick
ΔH - ΔU = ΔngRT

Application: If products have more moles of gas than reactants, ΔH > ΔU. If fewer, ΔH < ΔU.

Combustion Enthalpy Rule
ΔHc⁰ = ∑ΔHf⁰(products) - ∑ΔHf⁰(reactants)

Application: Always write balanced combustion with 1 mole of the combustible substance using O2.

Hess Law Reverse-Multiply
Multiply equation by 'x' → multiply ΔH by 'x'. Reverse equation → multiply ΔH by -1.

Application: Quickly manipulate thermo equations without recalculating individual states.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The standard heat of combustion of carbon (graphite) is -393.5 kJ mol-1.
  • Enthalpy of neutralization of strong acid with strong base is always constant at -57.1 kJ mol-1.
  • For the reaction H2(g) + Cl2(g) → 2HCl(g), Δng = 0, hence ΔH = ΔU.
  • Standard enthalpy of formation (ΔHf⁰) of a compound is the enthalpy change when 1 mole is formed from its constituent elements in standard states.
  • Kirchhoff's equation at constant volume uses ΔCv instead of ΔCp.
  • Calorimeter constant represents the heat capacity of the entire calorimeter assembly including water.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting that standard states require gases at 1 bar, not 1 atm, in strict IUPAC definitions.
  • Failing to account for the physical state (s, l, g) of reactants and products when computing Δng.
  • Confusing bond dissociation energy with mean bond enthalpy.
  • Applying Hess's law without ensuring all intermediary equations are balanced correctly.

⚡ Quick Revision: Heat of Reaction

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

First Law Foundation

Heat of reaction relates directly to internal energy and enthalpy changes under constant volume and pressure conditions.

Thermochemical Equations

Equations must specify physical states and temperature to accurately reflect the heat of reaction value.

Calorimetry Measurement

Bomb calorimeters measure constant volume heats (ΔU), while coffee-cup calorimeters measure constant pressure heats (ΔH).

Bond Energy Calculations

Enthalpy changes can be estimated accurately from the difference between reactant bond breaking and product bond forming energies.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Enthalpy of Formation (ΔHf⁰)
Heat change when 1 mole of substance is formed from elements. Unit: kJ mol-1.
Enthalpy of Combustion (ΔHc⁰)
Heat evolved when 1 mole of substance is burnt in excess oxygen. Unit: kJ mol-1.
Enthalpy of Neutralization (ΔHneut)
Heat evolved when 1 gram equivalent of acid is neutralized by base. Unit: kJ mol-1.
Enthalpy of Solution (ΔHsol)
Heat change when 1 mole of solute is dissolved in specified solvent. Unit: kJ mol-1.
Bond Enthalpy (BE)
Energy required to break 1 mole of gaseous bonds. Unit: kJ mol-1.
Resonance Energy (ΔHres)
Difference between experimental and calculated resonance structures enthalpy. Unit: kJ mol-1.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT
  • Neutralization of strong acid/base = -57.1 kJ mol-1
  • Standard enthalpy of elemental reference state = 0
  • Kirchhoff's equation: ΔH2 - ΔH1 = ΔCp(T2 - T1)
  • Combustion reactions are always exothermic (ΔH < 0)

📌 Quick Recall

  • Heat of reaction is state function dependent on T and P.
  • Hess's law permits calculation of unmeasurable heats via algebraic summation.
  • Phase transitions contribute specific latent heats to overall thermochemical pathways.
  • Calorimetric methods provide experimental validation for theoretical enthalpy values.

⚖️ Key Differences

Enthalpy of Atomization VS Bond Dissociation Enthalpy

Atomization involves breaking all bonds in 1 mole of substance to form isolated gaseous atoms, whereas bond dissociation is specific to a single bond in a specific molecule.

Question 1 of 18

For which of the following reactions is ΔH equal to ΔU?

Explanation: In 2HI(g) → H2(g) + I2(g), moles of gaseous products = 2 and moles of gaseous reactants = 2, so Δng = 0 and ΔH = ΔU.

Question 2 of 18

What is the standard enthalpy of formation of graphite at 298 K?

Explanation: Graphite is the most stable allotrope of carbon, hence its standard enthalpy of formation is defined as zero.

Question 3 of 18

The heat of neutralization of a strong acid and a strong base is always:

Explanation: The neutralization of any strong acid by any strong base involves the same net ionic reaction: H+(aq) + OH-(aq) → H2O(l), releasing 57.1 kJ per mole of water formed.

Question 4 of 18

According to Hess's Law, the heat of a reaction depends on:

Explanation: Enthalpy is a state function; therefore, the heat of reaction depends solely on the initial and final states, independent of the pathway.

Question 5 of 18

If Δng is positive for a reaction, then:

Explanation: From ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT, when Δng > 0, the term ΔngRT is positive, making ΔH greater than ΔU.

Question 6 of 18

The units of standard enthalpy of reaction are typically:

Explanation: Enthalpy change per mole of reaction is conventionally expressed in kilojoules per mole (kJ mol-1).

Question 7 of 18

Kirchhoff's equation relates the variation of enthalpy of reaction with:

Explanation: Kirchhoff's equation gives the mathematical dependence of ΔH on temperature using heat capacity changes.

Question 8 of 18

Which of the following processes is always exothermic?

Explanation: Combustion reactions involve bond formation and oxidation of fuels, which consistently release heat, making them exothermic.

Question 9 of 18

In a bomb calorimeter, the heat measured is:

Explanation: A bomb calorimeter operates at constant volume, meaning the measured heat change corresponds directly to internal energy change (ΔU).

Question 10 of 18

What is the value of Δng for the combustion of 1 mole of methane: CH4(g) + 2O2(g) → CO2(g) + 2H2O(l)?

Explanation: Gaseous products = 1 (CO2). Gaseous reactants = 1 (CH4) + 2 (O2) = 3. Δng = 1 - 3 = -2. (Note H2O is liquid).

Question 11 of 18

The enthalpy of atomization of diatomic chlorine gas (Cl2) is related to its bond enthalpy by:

Explanation: Atomization of Cl2(g) → 2Cl(g) breaks 1 mole of Cl-Cl bonds, which is equal to the bond dissociation enthalpy.

Question 12 of 18

Why is the heat of neutralization of acetic acid with NaOH less than -57.1 kJ?

Explanation: Acetic acid is a weak electrolyte; part of the heat released is absorbed in ionizing the un-ionized acid molecules.

Question 13 of 18

The standard enthalpy of formation of an element in its standard state is:

Explanation: By international convention, the standard enthalpy of formation for any element in its reference standard state is zero.

Question 14 of 18

Which instrument is used to determine heats of combustion accurately?

Explanation: Bomb calorimeters are specially designed high-pressure vessels used to measure combustion heats at constant volume.

Question 15 of 18

What is ΔH for a cyclic thermodynamic process?

Explanation: Enthalpy is a state function; therefore, the total change over any complete cyclic process is zero.

Question 16 of 18

The standard state pressure agreed upon by IUPAC is:

Explanation: IUPAC redefined the standard pressure condition to exactly 1 bar (105 Pa) in 1982.

Question 17 of 18

If bond energies of reactants exceed products, the reaction is:

Explanation: More energy is required to break reactant bonds than is released forming product bonds, resulting in a positive ΔH (endothermic).

Question 18 of 18

The enthalpy of solution of anhydrous copper sulfate is negative, meaning the process is:

Explanation: A negative enthalpy of solution indicates heat is evolved during dissolution.

UP PGT 2021
The relation between enthalpy change (ΔH) and internal energy change (ΔU) for the reaction C2H5OH(l) + 3O2(g) → 2CO2(g) + 3H2O(l) at temperature T is:
Answer: Option A (ΔH = ΔU - RT) Gaseous moles of products = 2 (CO2). Gaseous moles of reactants = 3 (O2). Δng = 2 - 3 = -1. Thus ΔH = ΔU - RT.
UP PGT 2016
Standard enthalpy of formation values of species A, B, and C are -400 kJ mol-1, -200 kJ mol-1, and 0 respectively. For the reaction A + B → 2C, the standard enthalpy change is:
Answer: Option A (+600 kJ mol-1) ΔH° = ∑ΔHf⁰(products) - ∑ΔHf⁰(reactants) = 2(0) - [(-400) + (-200)] = +600 kJ mol-1.
UP PGT 2013
The heat of neutralization of NaOH with HCl is -57.1 kJ mol-1. What will be the heat of neutralization of 1 mole of HCN with NaOH?
Answer: Option A (Less than -57.1 kJ mol-1) HCN is a weak acid, so energy is absorbed to dissociate it, making the net heat of neutralization less negative than -57.1 kJ mol-1.
Model Question
If the bond enthalpies of H-H, Cl-Cl, and H-Cl are 435, 242, and 431 kJ mol-1 respectively, the enthalpy of formation of HCl is:
Answer: Option A (-92.5 kJ mol-1) For 1/2H2 + 1/2Cl2 → HCl, ΔH = sum(BE reactants broken) - sum(BE products formed) = [1/2(435) + 1/2(242)] - [431] = [217.5 + 121] - 431 = 338.5 - 431 = -92.5 kJ mol-1.
UP PGT 2009
Which expression gives Kirchhoff's equation for heat of reaction at constant pressure?
Answer: Option A (d(ΔH)/dT = ΔCp) Kirchhoff's law in differential form states that the rate of change of reaction enthalpy with temperature equals the heat capacity change at constant pressure, d(ΔH)/dT = ΔCp.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate ΔU for the formation of 1 mole of CO from carbon and oxygen if the heat of combustion of carbon to CO2 is -393.5 kJ and CO to CO2 is -283.0 kJ.

Solution: C + 1/2O2 → CO. C + O2 → CO2 (ΔH = -393.5). CO + 1/2O2 → CO2 (ΔH = -283.0). Subtracting gives -393.5 - (-283.0) = -110.5 kJ.

Question 2 of 10
The enthalpy of combustion of glucose (C6H12O6) is -2800 kJ mol-1. How much energy is evolved when 90 g of glucose is burned?

Solution: Molar mass of glucose = 180 g mol-1. 90 g corresponds to 0.5 moles. Energy evolved = 0.5 × 2800 = 1400 kJ.

Question 3 of 10
Given ΔH for combustion of methane is -890 kJ mol-1, what is ΔU at 298 K?

Solution: CH4(g) + 2O2(g) → CO2(g) + 2H2O(l). Δng = 1 - 3 = -2. ΔU = ΔH - ΔngRT = -890000 - (-2 × 8.314 × 298) = -890000 + 4955.15 = -885.0 kJ mol-1 approx.

Question 4 of 10
The heat of solution of anhydrous CuSO4 is -66.5 kJ mol-1 and that of CuSO4·5H2O is +11.7 kJ mol-1. The heat of hydration of CuSO4 is:

Solution: Enthalpy of hydration = ΔHsol(anhydrous) - ΔHsol(hydrated) = -66.5 - (+11.7) = -78.2 kJ mol-1.

Question 5 of 10
For the reaction N2(g) + 3H2(g) → 2NH3(g), ΔH is -92.2 kJ at 298 K. What is ΔU at this temperature?

Solution: Δng = 2 - 4 = -2. ΔU = ΔH - ΔngRT = -92200 - (-2 × 8.314 × 298) = -92200 + 4955 = -87245 J = -87.2 kJ.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following has a standard enthalpy of formation equal to zero?

Solution: Bromine is liquid at standard conditions (298 K, 1 bar), making Br2(l) the stable reference state form with zero enthalpy of formation.

Question 7 of 10
If the enthalpy of formation of ammonia is -46.0 kJ mol-1, calculate the enthalpy change for the dissociation of 2 moles of ammonia into its elements.

Solution: Formation: N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3 (ΔH = -92 kJ). Dissociation is the reverse process, so ΔH = +92 kJ for 2 moles.

Question 8 of 10
The integral heat of solution depends upon:

Solution: Integral heat of solution varies with the dilution level, i.e., the amount of solvent per mole of solute.

Question 9 of 10
Bond energy values are strictly applicable to:

Solution: Bond enthalpies are defined and measured in the gaseous state to eliminate intermolecular forces of attraction.
