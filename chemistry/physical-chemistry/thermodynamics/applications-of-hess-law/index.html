Physical Chemistry

Applications of Hess's Law

Hess's Law of Constant Heat Summation states that the total enthalpy change of a chemical reaction is independent of the pathway between initial and final states. It functions as a direct consequence of enthalpy being a state function obeying the first law of thermodynamics.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Calculation of Enthalpy of Formation for Inaccessible Compounds

  • Enthalpies of formation for unstable or intermediate species like CO(g) or CH3COOH(l) are calculated indirectly using combustion data.
  • Reactions are manipulated algebraically by reversing equations and multiplying by stoichiometric coefficients to cancel intermediate species.

Lattice Enthalpy and Born-Haber Cycle Integration

  • The Born-Haber cycle applies Hess's law to ionic solid formation, splitting the process into sublimation, ionization, dissociation, electron affinity, and lattice energy steps.
  • Lattice energy (U) cannot be measured directly but is evaluated by equating the sum of step enthalpies to the standard enthalpy of formation.

Bond Dissociation and Resonance Energy Determination

  • Resonance energy is quantified as the difference between the experimentally measured enthalpy of formation and the theoretical value calculated from standard bond energies.
  • Average bond enthalpies derived from tabulated data can be utilized via Hess's law to estimate gas-phase reaction enthalpies without direct calorimetry.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Standard Enthalpy of Reaction from Enthalpies of Combustion
ΔH°rxn = ∑ νr ΔH°c(reactants) - ∑ νp ΔH°c(products)
Conditions: Complete combustion in excess oxygen at standard conditions
Units: kJ mol-1
Standard Enthalpy of Reaction from Bond Enthalpies
ΔH°rxn = ∑ ΔHbonds broken(reactants) - ∑ ΔHbonds formed(products)
Conditions: Gas-phase covalent species at 298.15 K
Units: kJ mol-1
Temperature Dependence of Enthalpy (Kirchhoff's Equation)
ΔH°(T2) - ΔH°(T1) = ∫T1T2 ΔCp dT
Conditions: Constant pressure, assuming ΔCp is independent of temperature over a narrow range
Units: J mol-1 or kJ mol-1
Lattice Energy Calculation via Born-Haber Cycle
ΔH°f = ΔH°sub + I.E. + 12ΔH°diss + E.A. + U
Conditions: Ionic crystal formation from elements in standard states
Units: kJ mol-1
Standard Enthalpy of Reaction from Enthalpies of Formation
ΔH°rxn = ∑ νp ΔH°f(products) - ∑ νr ΔH°f(reactants)
Conditions: Standard state conditions (298.15 K, 1 bar)
Units: kJ mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Enthalpy of formation of any element in its standard state is zero.

Observation: Allotrope modifications not stable at 298.15 K and 1 bar possess non-zero standard enthalpies of formation. — Graphite is the reference state for carbon (ΔH°f = 0), whereas diamond has a standard enthalpy of formation of +1.90 kJ mol-1.

Exception Bond enthalpies are additive and constant across all molecular environments.

Observation: Bond enthalpies vary significantly depending on the local electronic environment and neighboring functional groups. — C-H bond energy in methane differs from the C-H bond energy in chloroform due to inductive and steric polarization effects.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Combustion Sign Reversal Rule
ΔH°rxn = ∑(ΔH°c, reactants) - ∑(ΔH°c, products)

Application: When given combustion data, always subtract product combustion enthalpies from reactant combustion enthalpies, keeping reactant signs intact.

Born-Haber Energy Balance
ΔH°f = Sum of endothermic steps + Sum of exothermic steps + U

Application: Assign positive signs to Sublimation, Ionization Energy, and Dissociation Enthalpy; assign negative signs to Electron Affinity.

Equation Reversal Vector Method
Reverse eq → multiply ΔH by (-1); Multiply eq by n → multiply ΔH by n

Application: Treat thermochemical equations as vectors to quickly find net enthalpy changes without rewriting entire mechanisms.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Hess's law is a direct consequence of the first law of thermodynamics and the fact that enthalpy is a state function.
  • Enthalpy of combustion values are always negative (ΔH°c < 0) because combustion reactions are exothermic.
  • The standard enthalpy of formation of an element in its standard reference state is defined as exactly zero.
  • Bond dissociation enthalpy is always an endothermic process (ΔH > 0), whereas bond formation is exothermic (ΔH < 0).
  • Kirchhoff's equation evaluates the change in reaction enthalpy as a function of temperature using heat capacities at constant pressure.
  • Lattice energy increases with higher ionic charges and smaller ionic radii according to Coulombic attraction.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Failing to reverse the sign of ΔH when a thermochemical equation is reversed during algebraic manipulation.
  • Using bond enthalpies for species in liquid or solid states instead of strictly correcting for vaporization or sublimation.
  • Forgetting to multiply standard enthalpies of formation by the correct stoichiometric coefficients from the balanced equation.
  • Confusing the sign convention for electron affinity when assembling a Born-Haber cycle calculation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Applications of Hess's Law

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

State Function Principle

Enthalpy change depends only on initial and final states, permitting alternative pathways to calculate inaccessible reaction heats.

Thermochemical Equations

Chemical equations balanced with physical states and corresponding enthalpy changes can be added, subtracted, and multiplied.

Born-Haber Cycle

Application of Hess's law to ionic crystals relates lattice energy to sublimation, ionization energy, dissociation, and electron affinity.

Bond Energy Calculations

Net reaction enthalpy is estimated by subtracting total bond enthalpies of products formed from total bond enthalpies of reactants broken.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Hess's Law (ΔHtotal = ∑ΔHi)
Total enthalpy change is independent of the reaction pathway.
Standard Enthalpy of Formation (ΔH°f)
Enthalpy change when 1 mole of a compound is formed from its elements in standard states (kJ mol-1).
Lattice Energy (U)
Energy released when gaseous ions combine to form 1 mole of an ionic solid (kJ mol-1).
Bond Enthalpy (ΔHbond)
Energy required to break 1 mole of covalent bonds in the gas phase (kJ mol-1).
Enthalpy of Combustion (ΔH°c)
Enthalpy change when 1 mole of a substance is completely burned in excess oxygen (kJ mol-1).
Kirchhoff's Equation (d(ΔH)/dT = ΔCp)
Relates the variation of reaction enthalpy with temperature to heat capacity changes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Enthalpy is a state function; pathway does not affect total ΔH.
  • ΔH°f of elements in standard states = 0.
  • Reversing a reaction multiplies its ΔH by -1.
  • Combustion reactions are always exothermic (ΔH < 0).
  • Born-Haber cycles equate formation energy to the sum of all constituent atomic and ionic energy steps.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Hess's law allows calculation of unknown reaction enthalpies using known thermodynamic data.
  • Thermochemical equations obey algebraic rules when combined.
  • Standard enthalpies of formation and combustion are primary data sources.
  • Bond energy estimations provide insight into molecular stability.

⚖️ Key Differences

Enthalpy of Formation VS Enthalpy of Combustion

Formation refers to synthesis of 1 mole of compound from elements; combustion refers to oxidation of 1 mole of substance in oxygen.

What thermodynamic property guarantees the validity of Hess's Law?

Explanation: Enthalpy is a state function, meaning its change depends solely on the initial and final states, regardless of the path taken.

Question 2 of 18

If reaction A → B has ΔH = +50 kJ mol-1, what is the enthalpy change for B → A?

Explanation: Reversing a chemical reaction reverses the sign of its enthalpy change.

Question 3 of 18

Which of the following has a standard enthalpy of formation (ΔH°f) equal to zero at 298.15 K and 1 bar?

Explanation: O2(g) is an element in its standard reference state, so its standard enthalpy of formation is defined as zero.

Question 4 of 18

How is the standard enthalpy of reaction calculated from standard enthalpies of formation?

Explanation: The standard enthalpy of a reaction equals the sum of standard enthalpies of formation of products minus the sum of standard enthalpies of formation of reactants.

Question 5 of 18

When an equation is multiplied by a coefficient n, what happens to its enthalpy change (ΔH)?

Explanation: Enthalpy is an extensive property; multiplying a chemical equation by n multiplies its enthalpy change by n.

Question 6 of 18

In a Born-Haber cycle for NaCl, which step corresponds to the ionization energy of sodium?

Explanation: Ionization energy is the energy required to remove an electron from a gaseous atom, represented by Na(g) → Na+(g) + e-.

Question 7 of 18

What is the relationship between bond enthalpy and reaction enthalpy?

Explanation: Reaction enthalpy from bond energies is calculated as total bond enthalpy of bonds broken (reactants) minus total bond enthalpy of bonds formed (products).

Question 8 of 18

Which of the following processes is always exothermic?

Explanation: Combustion reactions release heat to the surroundings, making their enthalpy changes negative (exothermic).

Question 9 of 18

Kirchhoff's equation expresses the variation of reaction enthalpy with respect to:

Explanation: Kirchhoff's equation gives the derivative of enthalpy of reaction with respect to temperature in terms of heat capacity changes.

Question 10 of 18

What term represents the energy released when gaseous ions form 1 mole of an ionic solid?

Explanation: Lattice energy is defined as the energy released when gaseous ions coalesce to form one mole of a crystalline ionic compound.

Question 11 of 18

Why is graphite used as the standard reference state for carbon instead of diamond?

Explanation: Graphite is the thermodynamically stable allotrope of carbon at 298.15 K and 1 bar, hence its ΔH°f is assigned as zero.

Question 12 of 18

If ΔH°c values are used to find reaction enthalpy, what is the correct formula structure?

Explanation: When using combustion data, reaction enthalpy is calculated by subtracting product combustion enthalpies from reactant combustion enthalpies.

Question 13 of 18

Which law serves as the theoretical foundation for Hess's Law?

Explanation: Hess's Law is a direct application of the First Law of Thermodynamics, establishing conservation of energy for chemical transformations.

Question 14 of 18

What is the standard enthalpy of formation of graphite?

Explanation: Graphite is the reference allotrope of carbon, so its standard enthalpy of formation is defined as zero.

Question 15 of 18

Which factor increases lattice energy in ionic crystals?

Explanation: According to Coulomb's law, lattice energy increases when ionic radii are smaller and ionic charges are higher.

Question 16 of 18

Calculate ΔH° for A + 2B → C given ΔH°f (A) = -50, ΔH°f (B) = -30, ΔH°f (C) = -140 kJ mol-1.

Explanation: ΔH° = ΔH°f(C) - [ΔH°f(A) + 2ΔH°f(B)] = -140 - [-50 + 2(-30)] = -140 - [-110] = -30 kJ mol-1.

Question 17 of 18

In the context of thermochemical equations, what does a positive sign for ΔH signify?

Explanation: A positive ΔH indicates that the system absorbs heat from the surroundings, characterizing an endothermic process.

Question 18 of 18

Which property is classified as an intensive property among the following?

Explanation: Temperature is independent of the amount of substance, making it an intensive property, whereas H, U, and C are extensive.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the enthalpy of formation of carbon monoxide (CO) given: C(graphite) + O2(g) → CO2(g), ΔH = -393.5 kJ mol-1 and CO(g) + 12O2(g) → CO2(g), ΔH = -283.0 kJ mol-1.

Solution: Subtracting the second equation from the first yields C(graphite) + 12O2(g) → CO(g) with ΔH = -393.5 - (-283.0) = -110.5 kJ mol-1.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following thermodynamic quantities is NOT a state function?

Solution: Work (w) and heat (q) are path functions, whereas H, U, and G are state functions.

Question 3 of 10
What is the unit of standard enthalpy of reaction in SI?

Solution: Standard enthalpy of reaction is conventionally expressed in kilojoules per mole (kJ mol-1).

Question 4 of 10
In applying Hess's Law, if an equation is reversed, what happens to the sign of ΔH?

Solution: Reversing a chemical reaction changes the sign of its enthalpy change (ΔH → -ΔH).

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following compounds has a non-zero standard enthalpy of formation at 298.15 K?

Solution: H2O(g) is a compound, not an element in its standard state, so its standard enthalpy of formation is non-zero (-241.8 kJ mol-1).

Question 6 of 10
According to Kirchhoff's law, the rate of change of reaction enthalpy with temperature (&partial;ΔH / &partial;T)p is equal to:

Solution: Kirchhoff's equation defines (&partial;ΔH / &partial;T)p = ΔCp, the difference in heat capacities between products and reactants.

Question 7 of 10
What does a Born-Haber cycle primarily calculate?

Solution: The Born-Haber cycle uses Hess's law to determine the lattice energy of ionic solids.

Question 8 of 10
If bond enthalpies for A-A, B-B, and A-B are 436, 242, and 431 kJ mol-1, calculate ΔH for A2 + B2 → 2AB.

Solution: ΔH = (436 + 242) - 2(431) = 678 - 862 = -184 kJ mol-1.

Question 9 of 10
Which condition is specified for standard states in thermochemistry?

Solution: Standard state conditions are defined at 298.15 K and 1 bar (105 Pa) pressure.

Question 10 of 10
Why is the enthalpy of combustion always negative?

Solution: Combustion reactions release thermal energy to the surroundings, resulting in an exothermic process with a negative enthalpy change.
