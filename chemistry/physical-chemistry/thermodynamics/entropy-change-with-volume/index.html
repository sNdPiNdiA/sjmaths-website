Entropy change with volume

Entropy change as a function of volume describes the variation of system disorder during expansion or compression processes. For an ideal gas, this property is mathematically derived using the combined first and second laws of thermodynamics.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Derivation from Combined First and Second Laws

  • Starting from dS = (dU + pdV) / T, substitute dU = n Cv,m dT for an ideal gas.
  • Substitute p = nRT / V into the expression to obtain dS = n Cv,m (dT / T) + n R (dV / V).
  • Integration between initial and final states yields the general entropy change equation involving both temperature and volume variables.

Isothermal Volume Expansion

  • During isothermal expansion where V2 > V1, the entropy change is positive (ΔS > 0) due to increased spatial microstates.
  • For isothermal compression where V2 < V1, the entropy change is negative (ΔS < 0) as volume restriction decreases disorder.
  • The magnitude depends solely on the logarithmic ratio of the final to initial volumes.

Adiabatic Free Expansion

  • In a free expansion into vacuum (Joule expansion), external pressure is zero, hence q = 0 and w = 0.
  • Since internal energy remains constant for an ideal gas, temperature does not change, making ΔS = n R ln(V2 / V1) > 0.
  • Free expansion is an irreversible process, confirming that the entropy of the universe increases during unhindered volume expansion.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Entropy Change for Ideal Gas (V and T)
ΔS = n Cv,m ln(T2 / T1) + n R ln(V2 / V1)
Conditions: Ideal gas undergoing any state change
Units: n in moles, Cv,m in J K-1 mol-1, R in J K-1 mol-1, V in m3 or L
Entropy Change for Isothermal Ideal Gas
ΔS = n R ln(V2 / V1) = n R ln(P1 / P2)
Conditions: Constant temperature process (T1 = T2)
Units: ΔS in J K-1, R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1
Entropy Change for Isochoric Process
ΔS = n Cv,m ln(T2 / T1)
Conditions: Constant volume process (V1 = V2)
Units: ΔS in J K-1
Entropy Change with Volume (General)
ΔS = ∫ (dqrev / T)
Conditions: Applicable to any reversible process
Units: ΔS in J K-1, q in Joules, T in Kelvin
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Entropy increases during volume expansion for all systems

Observation: Certain rubber bands and elastomers — Stretching decreases polymer chain conformation entropy; volume remains nearly constant while conformational microstates decrease.

Exception Entropy change for an ideal gas depends on volume and temperature

Observation: Isothermal process of ideal gas — Internal energy remains constant, causing the temperature contribution term to vanish entirely, leaving only the volume-dependent term.

Exception Real gases obey ideal gas entropy-volume logarithmic equations

Observation: Gases at high pressures and low temperatures — Intermolecular forces and finite molecular volumes cause significant deviations from ideal behavior, requiring fugacity and virial corrections.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Volume Ratio Logarithm Rule
ΔS = 2.303 n R log(V2 / V1)

Application: Convert natural logarithm to base-10 logarithm quickly when solving numericals involving liters or cubic meters.

Pressure-Volume Swap Trick
ln(V2 / V1) = ln(P1 / P2)

Application: Substitute pressure terms directly into the entropy equation for isothermal processes when volume data is missing.

Sign Inspection Shortcut
V2 > V1 → +ΔS

Application: Immediately determine the sign of entropy change by checking whether the gas expands or compresses.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Entropy change for an ideal gas depends on both temperature and volume variations.
  • For isothermal expansion of 1 mole of an ideal gas, ΔS = R ln(V2/V1).
  • Entropy is an extensive property; doubling the amount of gas doubles the entropy change for the same volume ratio.
  • During adiabatic reversible expansion, entropy change of the system is zero (ΔSsys = 0).
  • Free expansion of an ideal gas into a vacuum yields ΔS = n R ln(V2/V1) > 0.
  • The SI unit of entropy and entropy change is J K-1.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing reversible isothermal expansion where ΔSuniv = 0 with irreversible expansion where ΔSuniv > 0.
  • Forgetting to convert natural logarithm to base-10 logarithm when using log10 tables.
  • Applying the ideal gas volume entropy formula directly to condensed phases (liquids and solids) where volume changes have negligible impact on entropy.
  • Incorrectly assuming that adiabatic means constant entropy in irreversible processes; only reversible adiabatic processes are isentropic.

⚡ Quick Revision: Entropy change with volume

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Thermodynamic Definition of Entropy

Entropy measures the dispersal of energy and microscopic disorder within a system, defined via reversible heat transfer divided by absolute temperature.

Volume Dependency in Ideal Gases

An increase in volume expands the available spatial coordinates for gas molecules, raising the number of accessible microstates and increasing entropy.

Isothermal Entropy Change

At constant temperature, the energy input equals the expansion work, and entropy change depends exclusively on the logarithm of the volume ratio.

Irreversibility and Universe Entropy

Spontaneous volume changes such as free expansion into a vacuum create positive total entropy changes in accordance with the second law of thermodynamics.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Heat capacity at constant volume per mole; J K-1 mol-1.
Universal Gas Constant (R)
Constant equal to 8.314 J K-1 mol-1.
Isothermal Process (ΔT = 0)
Process occurring at constant temperature.
Adiabatic Process (q = 0)
Process with no heat exchange between system and surroundings.
Microstate (Ω)
Specific microscopic configuration of a thermodynamic system.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔS = n R ln(V2 / V1) for isothermal ideal gas expansion.
  • General ideal gas entropy equation: ΔS = n Cv,m ln(T2/T1) + n R ln(V2/V1).
  • For free expansion into vacuum, q = 0, w = 0, but ΔS > 0.
  • Entropy is a state function; its change depends only on initial and final states.
  • Total entropy of the universe increases for all spontaneous processes.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Volume expansion increases positional entropy of gas molecules.
  • Isothermal entropy changes depend strictly on volume or pressure ratios.
  • Adiabatic reversible expansion maintains constant entropy (isentropic).
  • Irreversible expansions always generate positive entropy change for the universe.

⚖️ Key Differences

Isothermal expansion VS Adiabatic free expansion

Both maintain constant temperature for an ideal gas, but isothermal involves heat exchange (q > 0) whereas adiabatic free expansion involves zero heat exchange (q = 0).

Question 1 of 19

What is the entropy change when 2 moles of an ideal gas expand isothermally to double its initial volume?

Explanation: ΔS = n R ln(V2 / V1) = 2 × R × ln(2V1 / V1) = 2R ln 2.

Question 2 of 19

Which of the following expressions represents the entropy change of an ideal gas with respect to volume and temperature?

Explanation: Integrating dS = (n Cv,m dT / T) + (n R dV / V) yields n Cv,m ln(T2/T1) + n R ln(V2/V1).

Question 3 of 19

For an isothermal reversible expansion of an ideal gas, the entropy change of the universe (ΔSuniv) is:

Explanation: In a reversible process, ΔSsys + ΔSsurr = 0, so ΔSuniv = 0.

Question 4 of 19

During the adiabatic free expansion of an ideal gas into a vacuum, the entropy change of the system is:

Explanation: Free expansion is irreversible with V2 > V1, hence ΔS = n R ln(V2/V1) > 0.

Question 5 of 19

What is the entropy change of 1 mole of an ideal gas undergoing isobaric heating from T1 to T2?

Explanation: At constant pressure, dS = dqrev / T = Cp,m dT / T, which integrates to Cp,m ln(T2/T1).

Question 6 of 19

If the volume of an ideal gas is compressed to half its original volume isothermally, the entropy change is:

Explanation: ΔS = R ln(V2/V1) = R ln(V1/2 / V1) = R ln(1/2) = -R ln 2.

Question 7 of 19

Why does entropy increase when a gas expands into a larger volume?

Explanation: Larger volume increases the spatial positions available to molecules, enhancing positional probability and microstates.

Question 8 of 19

Which thermodynamic property is zero for a cyclic isothermal process involving an ideal gas?

Explanation: Entropy is a state function; for any complete cycle, the total change in entropy (ΔS) is zero.

Question 9 of 19

In a reversible adiabatic process, the entropy change of the system (ΔSsys) is:

Explanation: A reversible adiabatic process is isentropic, meaning dqrev = 0 and ΔS = 0.

Question 10 of 19

What is the unit of molar entropy?

Explanation: Entropy per mole has units of energy divided by temperature and moles, i.e., J K-1 mol-1.

Question 11 of 19

When an ideal gas undergoes a Joule-Thomson expansion, the entropy change of the gas is:

Explanation: Joule-Thomson expansion is an irreversible expansion (throttling), hence entropy increases (ΔS > 0).

Question 12 of 19

For a pure crystalline solid at absolute zero (0 K), the entropy is zero according to:

Explanation: The Third Law of Thermodynamics states that the entropy of a perfect crystal at 0 K is zero.

Question 13 of 19

Which of the following variables does entropy change NOT depend on for an isothermal ideal gas process?

Explanation: In an isothermal process, temperature is constant and does not contribute to the magnitude of ΔS.

Question 14 of 19

Calculate ΔS for 1 mole of an ideal gas expanding from 10 L to 100 L isothermally (R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1, ln 10 = 2.303):

Explanation: ΔS = 1 × 8.314 × ln(100/10) = 8.314 × 2.303 = 19.14 J K-1.

Question 15 of 19

If the volume of a given mass of gas is tripled at constant temperature, the entropy change is:

Explanation: ΔS = n R ln(3V1 / V1) = n R ln 3.

Question 16 of 19

Which relation is correct for entropy change with pressure at constant temperature?

Explanation: Since P1V1 = P2V2, V2/V1 = P1/P2, giving ΔS = n R ln(P1/P2) = -n R ln(P2/P1).

Question 17 of 19

Entropy is classified as which type of thermodynamic property?

Explanation: Entropy depends on the size of the system, making it an extensive state property.

Question 18 of 19

What is the sign of ΔS when a gas is compressed?

Explanation: Compression decreases volume (V2 < V1), resulting in a negative entropy change (ΔS < 0).

Question 19 of 19

The integration of dS = dqrev / T for a reversible isothermal expansion of an ideal gas yields:

Explanation: Since T is constant, ΔS = (1/T) ∫ dqrev = qrev / T.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the entropy change when 0.5 moles of an ideal gas expand from 2 L to 20 L at constant temperature (R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1).

Solution: ΔS = 0.5 × 8.314 × ln(20 / 2) = 0.5 × 8.314 × 2.303 = 9.57 J K-1.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following statements is true for any spontaneous process in an isolated system?

Solution: According to the second law of thermodynamics, entropy of the universe increases for all spontaneous processes.

Question 3 of 10
An ideal gas undergoes isothermal expansion. Which statement is correct regarding entropy?

Solution: In reversible isothermal expansion, system entropy increases while surrounding entropy decreases by an equal amount.

Question 4 of 10
What is the entropy change for a reversible isothermal compression of 2 moles of an ideal gas to 1/4th of its initial volume?

Solution: ΔS = n R ln(V2/V1) = 2 × R × ln(1/4) = -2 R ln 4.

Question 5 of 10
The entropy of a system depends on:

Solution: For a single-component ideal gas system, entropy is a function of temperature and volume (or temperature and pressure).

Question 6 of 10
If 1 mole of an ideal gas expands isothermally from V to 2V, and then compresses isothermally back to V, the total entropy change of the gas is:

Solution: Entropy is a state function. For a complete cyclic process, the net entropy change is zero.

Question 7 of 10
Which equation correctly represents entropy change in terms of initial and final pressures at constant temperature?

Solution: Since P1V1 = P2V2, V2/V1 = P1/P2, yielding ΔS = n R ln(P1/P2).

Question 8 of 10
In an adiabatic irreversible expansion of an ideal gas, the entropy change of the system is:

Solution: Even though q = 0, the process is irreversible and involves volume expansion (V2 > V1), so ΔS = n R ln(V2/V1) > 0.

Question 9 of 10
What happens to the molecular disorder when a gas is compressed?

Solution: Compression restricts molecular motion to a smaller volume, thereby decreasing molecular disorder and entropy.

Question 10 of 10
The condition for a process to be isentropic is:

Solution: An isentropic process is one that is both reversible and adiabatic, resulting in zero entropy change.
