Physical Chemistry

Heat Capacity

Heat capacity is the quantity of heat required to raise the temperature of a given system by one degree Celsius or Kelvin. It is an extensive property that depends on the amount and nature of the substance present in the system.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Definitions and Thermodynamic Relations

  • Heat capacity (C) is defined as the ratio of heat absorbed by the system to the resulting temperature change.
  • At constant volume, heat added equals the change in internal energy (qv = ΔU).
  • At constant pressure, heat added equals the change in enthalpy (qp = ΔH).

Temperature Dependence and Debye Model

  • According to Kirchhoff's equation, the variation of enthalpy change with temperature is given by ΔCp.
  • At extremely low temperatures near absolute zero, the heat capacity of solids approaches zero according to the Debye T3 law.
  • At high temperatures, the molar heat capacity of monoatomic solid elements approaches the classical limit of 3R according to the Dulong-Petit law.

Extensive vs Intensive Heat Capacities

  • Specific heat capacity (c) is an intensive property defined as heat capacity per unit mass (J g-1 K-1).
  • Molar heat capacity (Cm) is an intensive property defined as heat capacity per mole (J mol-1 K-1).
  • The ratio of Cp to Cv is denoted as adiabatic index (γ), which varies depending on atomicity of the gas.

Mayer's Relation for Ideal Gases
Cp,m - Cv,m = R
Conditions: Ideal gas behavior, 1 mole of gas
Units: R in J K-1 mol-1, Cp,m and Cv,m in J K-1 mol-1

C = qΔ T
Conditions: Any closed system undergoing a non-phase change process
Units: C in J K-1, q in J, ΔT in K
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Mayer's relation Cp - Cv = R applies to all gases.

Observation: Real gases and liquids/solids deviate significantly from this relation. — Real gases possess intermolecular forces, and condensed phases have negligible volume expansion coefficients.

Exception Heat capacities of solids are constant at room temperature.

Observation: Heat capacities drop sharply at very low temperatures. — Quantum mechanical quantization of lattice vibrations freezes out vibrational modes at low temperatures.

Exception Dulong-Petit law predicts Cv,m = 3R for all solid elements.

Observation: Light elements like diamond, beryllium, and boron show much lower values at room temperature. — High characteristic Debye temperatures cause vibrational modes to freeze out even at standard temperatures.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Cp is always greater than Cv for any substance due to work done during expansion at constant pressure.
  • For an ideal gas, Cp,m - Cv,m = R exactly.
  • The Dulong-Petit law sets the classical molar heat capacity limit of solid elements at approximately 25 J K-1 mol-1.
  • At absolute zero (0 K), both Cp and Cv approach zero for all substances.
  • For a monoatomic ideal gas, Cv,m = 1.5 R and Cp,m = 2.5 R.
  • For a diatomic ideal gas at room temperature, Cv,m = 2.5 R and Cp,m = 3.5 R.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming Cp - Cv = R holds true for liquids and solids.
  • Confusing intensive specific heat capacity with extensive total heat capacity.
  • Forgetting that ΔU = n Cv ΔT applies to all processes for ideal gases, not just constant volume.
  • Neglecting the temperature dependence of heat capacity when calculating entropy or enthalpy changes over wide temperature ranges.

⚡ Quick Revision: Heat Capacity

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Fundamental Definition

Heat capacity measures a system's capacity to store thermal energy as temperature increases.

Path Dependence of Heat

While heat is path-dependent, heat capacities Cv and Cp are state properties tied to U and H.

Ideal Gas Relations

Mayer's relation links Cp and Cv through the universal gas constant R for ideal gases.

Temperature Limits

Quantum mechanics explains low-temperature anomalies via Debye's T3 law and high-temperature limits via Dulong-Petit.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Heat Capacity (C = q / ΔT)
Heat required to change system temperature by 1 K. SI unit: J K-1.
Specific Heat (c = C / mass)
Heat capacity per unit mass. SI unit: J g-1 K-1.
Molar Heat Capacity (Cm = C / n)
Heat capacity per mole. SI unit: J mol-1 K-1.
Cv ((&partial;U / &partial;T)v)
Heat capacity at constant volume.
Cp ((&partial;H / &partial;T)p)
Heat capacity at constant pressure.
Adiabatic Index (γ = Cp / Cv)
Ratio of heat capacities, always greater than 1.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Cp - Cv = R for ideal gases
  • Cv,m = 1.5 R for monoatomic ideal gas
  • Cp,m = 2.5 R for monoatomic ideal gas
  • Debye T3 law applies at temperatures near 0 K
  • Dulong-Petit limit is 3R for solid elements at high temperatures

📌 Quick Recall

  • Heat capacity relates temperature change to energy input.
  • Cp exceeds Cv because of expansion work.
  • Intensive forms include specific and molar heat capacities.
  • Quantum effects dominate heat capacities at cryogenic temperatures.

⚖️ Key Differences

Specific Heat VS Heat Capacity

Specific heat is intensive (per gram), whereas heat capacity is extensive (for the whole body).

Question 1 of 19

What is the relation between Cp and Cv for one mole of an ideal gas?

Explanation: Mayer's relation for an ideal gas states that Cp,m - Cv,m = R.

Question 2 of 19

For a monoatomic ideal gas, what is the value of molar heat capacity at constant volume (Cv,m)?

Explanation: According to the equipartition theorem, a monoatomic gas has 3 translational degrees of freedom, giving Cv,m = (3/2)R = 1.5 R.

Question 3 of 19

Which of the following is an intensive property?

Explanation: Specific heat capacity is defined per unit mass, making it independent of total quantity (intensive).

Question 4 of 19

At absolute zero temperature, the heat capacity of a crystalline solid approaches:

Explanation: According to the third law of thermodynamics and Debye's law, heat capacity approaches zero as temperature approaches 0 K.

Question 5 of 19

The heat capacity at constant pressure (Cp) is equal to the derivative of which thermodynamic property with respect to temperature?

Explanation: By definition, Cp = (&partial;H / &partial;T)p.

Question 6 of 19

What is the Dulong-Petit law limiting value for molar heat capacity of solid elements at high temperature?

Explanation: Dulong-Petit law states that the molar heat capacity of solid elements at high temperatures is approximately 3R (~25 J K-1 mol-1).

Question 7 of 19

For a diatomic ideal gas at room temperature, what is Cp,m?

Explanation: A diatomic gas at room temperature has 3 translational and 2 rotational degrees of freedom, so Cv,m = 2.5 R, and Cp,m = Cv,m + R = 3.5 R.

Question 8 of 19

What does the ratio Cp / Cv represent?

Explanation: The ratio Cp/Cv is defined as the adiabatic index or Poisson's ratio (γ).

Question 9 of 19

Why is Cp greater than Cv for gases?

Explanation: At constant pressure, part of the heat supplied is used to do work of expansion against external pressure, requiring more heat to raise temperature.

Question 10 of 19

Which mathematical law describes heat capacity of solids at very low temperatures?

Explanation: At temperatures near 0 K, solid heat capacity follows Debye's T3 law: Cv = aT3.

Question 11 of 19

What is the SI unit of molar heat capacity?

Explanation: Molar heat capacity is heat capacity per mole, expressed in J K-1 mol-1.

Question 12 of 19

For an ideal gas, the change in internal energy (ΔU) for any process is given by:

Explanation: For any ideal gas process, ΔU = n Cv ΔT because internal energy depends solely on temperature.

Question 13 of 19

Which of the following is an extensive property?

Explanation: Heat capacity depends on the total mass of the system, making it an extensive property.

Question 14 of 19

What is the value of Cp - Cv for a van der Waals gas under moderate conditions?

Explanation: Real gases deviate from ideal behavior, so Cp - Cv deviates from R due to intermolecular forces and finite molecular volumes.

Question 15 of 19

If 50 J of heat raises the temperature of a system by 5 K, what is its heat capacity?

Explanation: C = q / ΔT = 50 J / 5 K = 10 J K-1.

Question 16 of 19

The variation of reaction enthalpy with temperature is given by:

Explanation: Kirchhoff's equation relates the change in enthalpy of a reaction to temperature using ΔCp.

Question 17 of 19

Which phase of matter generally has the highest heat capacity under standard conditions?

Explanation: Liquid water has an exceptionally high specific heat capacity (~4.18 J g-1 K-1) compared to most solids and gases.

Question 18 of 19

What happens to Cv of a polyatomic gas as temperature increases and vibrational modes become active?

Explanation: Higher temperatures activate vibrational degrees of freedom, increasing the molar heat capacity.

Question 19 of 19

Which term best describes Cv in terms of partial derivatives?

Explanation: Cv is strictly defined as the partial derivative of internal energy with respect to temperature at constant volume.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the heat required to raise the temperature of 2 moles of an ideal monoatomic gas from 200 K to 300 K at constant volume (R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1).

Solution: qv = ΔU = n Cv,m ΔT = 2 × (1.5 × 8.314) × (300 - 200) = 2 × 12.471 × 100 = 2494.2 J.

Question 2 of 10
What is the molar heat capacity at constant pressure for a diatomic ideal gas at room temperature?

Solution: Cp,m = Cv,m + R = 2.5 R + R = 3.5 R.

Question 3 of 10
If Cp of a gas is 29.1 J K-1 mol-1 and Cv is 20.8 J K-1 mol-1, calculate R.

Solution: R = Cp - Cv = 29.1 - 20.8 = 8.3 J K-1 mol-1.

Question 4 of 10
At very low temperatures, solid heat capacity is proportional to:

Solution: Debye's law states that Cv ∝ T3 at temperatures approaching absolute zero.

Question 5 of 10
What is the adiabatic index (γ) for a monoatomic ideal gas?

Solution: γ = Cp / Cv = (2.5 R) / (1.5 R) = 5 / 3 = 1.67.

Question 6 of 10
Which thermodynamic property remains constant during a process where heat capacity is measured at constant volume?

Solution: By definition, Cv is measured at constant volume.

Question 7 of 10
If 100 J of heat is added to a system and its temperature rises by 2 K at constant pressure, what is Cp?

Solution: Cp = qp / ΔT = 100 J / 2 K = 50 J K-1.

Question 8 of 10
Kirchhoff's equation for the variation of reaction enthalpy with temperature utilizes which quantity?

Solution: d(ΔH) / dT = ΔCp according to Kirchhoff's law.

Question 9 of 10
For a non-linear triatomic gas at room temperature, what is Cv,m?

Solution: A non-linear triatomic molecule has 3 translational and 3 rotational degrees of freedom, giving Cv,m = 3R.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following describes the nature of heat capacity?

Solution: Heat capacity (C) is extensive because it scales with the size of the system.
