Thermodynamic Criteria of Equilibrium

Thermodynamic equilibrium in a system is established when the total entropy change of the isolated system is maximized and its thermodynamic driving potentials reach minimum values under specified constraints. Spontaneous processes proceed in the direction that decreases free energy until equilibrium is attained.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Entropy Criteria and The Second Law

  • The universal criterion for any spontaneous process is that the total entropy change must be strictly greater than zero (ΔStotal > 0).
  • At thermodynamic equilibrium, the total entropy of the system and its surroundings attains its maximum possible value (ΔStotal = 0).
  • For an isolated system, internal processes occur spontaneously only when system entropy increases (dSsys > 0).
  • Entropy changes in the surroundings are evaluated using the relation dSsurr = -dqsys / T under constant pressure or volume.

Gibbs and Helmholtz Free Energy Functions

  • Helmholtz free energy (A) acts as the thermodynamic potential for processes occurring at constant temperature and volume, where (dA)V,T ≤ 0.
  • Gibbs free energy (G) serves as the primary criterion for chemical reactions and phase transitions occurring at constant temperature and pressure.
  • At constant T and P, a system is at equilibrium when the Gibbs free energy change is zero (ΔGT,P = 0) and differential dG = 0.
  • Spontaneous processes at constant T and P are characterized by a negative Gibbs free energy change (ΔG < 0).

Chemical Potential and Equilibrium Constants

  • The chemical potential (μ_i) of component i represents the partial molar Gibbs free energy, μ_i = (∂G / ∂n_i)_{T,P,nj≠i}.
  • At chemical equilibrium, the sum of the chemical potentials multiplied by stoichiometric coefficients equals zero: ∑ ν_i μ_i = 0.
  • The variation of reaction Gibbs energy with composition is linked to the reaction quotient (Q) via ΔG = ΔG° + RT \ln Q.
  • Temperature dependence of the equilibrium constant is governed by the Van't Hoff equation: d(\ln K)dT = Δ H°RT^2.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Spontaneous processes always have ΔSsys > 0

Observation: Phase transitions like freezing of water below 0°C or condensation of gases — The decrease in system entropy is offset by a larger increase in surroundings entropy due to released latent heat (ΔSsurr > |ΔSsys|).

Exception Reactions with positive ΔH° never proceed spontaneously

Observation: Endothermic reactions with large positive ΔS° values at high temperatures — The temperature-dependent entropy term (-TΔS°) dominates the Gibbs equation (ΔG = ΔH° - TΔS°), making ΔG negative.

Exception Equilibrium implies absolute rest at the molecular level

Observation: Dynamic equilibrium in chemical and phase systems — Forward and reverse reaction rates are precisely equal, yielding a net zero macroscopic change while microscopic particle exchange continues actively.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The condition for thermal equilibrium is equality of temperatures between subsystems: T_1 = T_2.
  • Mechanical equilibrium requires equality of internal and external pressures: Psys = Pext.
  • Chemical equilibrium requires equality of chemical potentials of a component across all phases: μ^(α) = μ^(β).
  • For an irreversible spontaneous process in an isolated system, dS > 0.
  • At absolute zero, the entropy of a pure crystalline substance approaches zero (Third Law of Thermodynamics).
  • The standard Gibbs free energy of formation for an element in its standard state is defined as zero.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing system entropy change (ΔSsys) with total entropy change (ΔStotal) when evaluating spontaneity.
  • Applying the Gibbs free energy criterion (ΔG ≤ 0) to conditions other than constant temperature and pressure.
  • Assuming that a reaction with a negative standard Gibbs energy (ΔG° < 0) occurs infinitely fast without considering activation energy kinetics.
  • Omitting the stoichiometric coefficients when writing the relation between standard Gibbs energy and the equilibrium constant.

⚡ Quick Revision: Thermodynamic Criteria of Equilibrium

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Second Law and Entropy

The direction of spontaneous change is dictated by the requirement that total entropy increases. Isolated systems evolve toward a state of maximum entropy at equilibrium.

Auxiliary Thermodynamic Functions

Helmholtz (A) and Gibbs (G) free energies combine internal energy, entropy, and boundary constraints to provide direct indicators of spontaneity under laboratory conditions.

Chemical Potential

Chemical potential governs matter transfer between phases and chemical reaction progression, driving systems toward uniform potential values at equilibrium.

Equilibrium Constants and Free Energy

The mathematical link between standard reaction Gibbs energy and the equilibrium constant establishes whether reactants or products are favored at equilibrium.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Entropy (S)
Measure of thermal energy dispersion per unit temperature, measured in J K^-1.
Gibbs Free Energy (G = H - TS)
Maximum non-expansion work obtainable at constant T and P, measured in Joules.
Helmholtz Energy (A = U - TS)
Thermodynamic potential at constant temperature and volume, measured in Joules.
Chemical Potential (μ)
Partial molar Gibbs free energy per mole of species, measured in J mol^-1.
Reaction Quotient (Q)
Ratio of activities of products to reactants at any given point.
Equilibrium Constant (K)
Value of the reaction quotient at chemical equilibrium under specific conditions.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔStotal = ΔSsys + ΔSsurr > 0 for spontaneity
  • (dG)T,P = 0 at equilibrium
  • (dA)T,V = 0 at equilibrium
  • ΔG° = -RT \ln Keq
  • ∑ ν_i μ_i = 0 at chemical equilibrium

📌 Quick Recall

  • Equilibrium corresponds to the maximum entropy state for isolated systems.
  • Free energy functions incorporate system properties and environmental constraints.
  • Chemical potential gradients drive mass transfer and chemical reactions.
  • Equilibrium constants are temperature-dependent thermodynamic quantities.

⚖️ Key Differences

ΔG VS ΔG°

ΔG applies under arbitrary conditions, while ΔG° strictly applies when all species are in their standard states.

Question 1 of 19

What is the thermodynamic criterion for equilibrium in a closed system at constant temperature and pressure?

Explanation: At constant temperature and pressure, the Gibbs free energy is minimized at equilibrium, making dG = 0.

Question 2 of 19

Which of the following conditions correctly defines a spontaneous process in an isolated system?

Explanation: The Second Law dictates that any spontaneous change in an isolated system must result in an increase in total entropy (ΔStotal > 0).

Question 3 of 19

For a system at constant temperature and volume, spontaneity is determined by which thermodynamic function?

Explanation: Helmholtz free energy (A) is the potential whose decrease governs spontaneous changes at constant T and V.

Question 4 of 19

What is the value of ΔG at chemical equilibrium?

Explanation: At equilibrium, the Gibbs free energy reaches a minimum, meaning the differential change dG is zero.

Question 5 of 19

Which equation relates standard Gibbs free energy change directly to the equilibrium constant?

Explanation: The standard relation is ΔG° = -RT \ln Keq.

Question 6 of 19

What is the chemical potential of a substance in a mixture defined as?

Explanation: Chemical potential μ_i is defined mathematically as the partial molar Gibbs free energy μ_i = (∂G / ∂n_i)T,P,n_j.

Question 7 of 19

If ΔG° for a reaction is positive, what can be inferred about the equilibrium constant K?

Explanation: Since ΔG° = -RT \ln K, a positive ΔG° yields a negative natural logarithm for K, meaning K < 1 (reactants favored).

Question 8 of 19

Thermal equilibrium between two systems requires which condition to be met?

Explanation: Thermal equilibrium is characterized by a uniform temperature across the interacting subsystems.

Question 9 of 19

Mechanical equilibrium requires which of the following?

Explanation: Mechanical equilibrium is attained when opposing mechanical forces balance out, corresponding to equality of pressure.

Question 10 of 19

What happens to the total entropy during a reversible cyclic process?

Explanation: For any reversible cycle, the net entropy change of the universe (ΔStotal) is zero.

Question 11 of 19

Which law of thermodynamics establishes the absolute reference zero for entropy?

Explanation: The Third Law of Thermodynamics states that the entropy of a pure crystalline substance is zero at absolute zero (T = 0 K).

Question 12 of 19

In a phase transition at the normal transition temperature, what is the value of ΔG?

Explanation: At a phase transition temperature, the two phases are in equilibrium, so ΔG = 0.

Question 13 of 19

What is the sign of ΔSsurr when heat is absorbed by the system from the surroundings at constant temperature?

Explanation: Heat lost by the surroundings results in a decrease in surroundings entropy (ΔSsurr = -qsys/T < 0).

Question 14 of 19

For a reaction where ΔH° < 0 and ΔS° > 0, the reaction is:

Explanation: With negative enthalpy and positive entropy, ΔG = ΔH° - TΔS° is negative at all temperatures.

Question 15 of 19

What condition must be satisfied for mass transfer equilibrium between two phases α and β?

Explanation: Equilibrium with respect to transfer of matter requires identical chemical potentials in all coexisting phases.

Question 16 of 19

Which thermodynamic quantity is constant during a Joule-Thomson expansion?

Explanation: Joule-Thomson expansion is an isenthalpic process, meaning enthalpy remains constant (H = constant).

Question 17 of 19

If the reaction quotient Q is greater than the equilibrium constant K (Q > K), the reaction will:

Explanation: When Q > K, there is an excess of products, so the system shifts in the reverse direction to reach equilibrium.

Question 18 of 19

Which of the following is an intensive property derived from Gibbs free energy?

Explanation: Chemical potential is a partial molar property, which is intensive and independent of system size.

Question 19 of 19

What does the Clausius inequality (dS > dq/T) signify?

Explanation: The strict inequality sign represents irreversible or spontaneous heat transfer processes in closed systems.

Question 2 of 10
If a reaction has ΔH° = +50 kJ mol^-1 and ΔS° = +100 J K^-1 mol^-1, above what temperature does the reaction become spontaneous?

Solution: Setting ΔG = 0 gives T = ΔH° / ΔS° = 50000 / 100 = 500 K. Spontaneity occurs above 500 K.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following statements regarding entropy and equilibrium is correct?

Solution: At equilibrium, the total entropy of the system and surroundings reaches its maximum possible value.

Question 4 of 10
The van 't Hoff equation describes the temperature dependence of:

Solution: The van 't Hoff equation links the derivative of the equilibrium constant with respect to temperature to standard enthalpy change.

Question 5 of 10
What is the value of dSsys + dSsurr for a spontaneous process in a closed system?

Solution: The total entropy change for any spontaneous process must be positive (ΔStotal > 0).

Question 6 of 10
Which thermodynamic property is a measure of capacity to do non-expansion work at constant T and P?

Solution: Decrease in Gibbs free energy at constant T and P equals maximum non-expansion work.

Question 7 of 10
For a reaction in equilibrium, if Q < K, what happens to the Gibbs free energy change (ΔG)?

Solution: When Q < K, the forward reaction is spontaneous, which corresponds to a negative Gibbs energy change (ΔG < 0).

Question 8 of 10
What is the differential form of the fundamental thermodynamic relation combining first and second laws?

Solution: The combined first and second law relation for a closed system is dU = TdS - PdV.

Question 9 of 10
Under what condition is the decrease in Helmholtz free energy equal to maximum work done by the system?

Solution: -ΔA equals the maximum total work (expansion and non-expansion) at constant temperature and volume.

Question 10 of 10
Why is the entropy change of the universe zero at equilibrium?

Solution: Equilibrium represents a reversible state where driving forces balance and the universe's total entropy remains constant.
