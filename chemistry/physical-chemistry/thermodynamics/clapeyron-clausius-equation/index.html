Clapeyron-Clausius Equation

The Clapeyron-Clausius equation quantitatively describes the variation of equilibrium pressure with temperature for a single-component phase transition. It is derived from the equality of chemical potentials between two coexisting phases in a closed system.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Derivation and Thermodynamic Basis

  • The exact Clapeyron equation is derived from the equality of chemical potentials (μA = μB) along a phase coexistence curve.
  • Applying the Gibbs-Duhem relation yields dP⁄dT = ΔS⁄ΔV, which transforms into dP⁄dT = ΔH⁄(T·ΔV) using ΔS = ΔH⁄T.
  • The equation defines the slope of the phase boundary line in a pressure-temperature (P-T) phase diagram for a pure substance.

Approximations for Vaporization and Sublimation

  • For liquid-vapor and solid-vapor transitions, the molar volume of the condensed phase is neglected because Vgas >> Vcondensed.
  • Assuming the vapor phase exhibits ideal gas behavior allows the substitution Vgas = RT⁄P, yielding the differential Clausius-Clapeyron equation.
  • Integration assumes the enthalpy of vaporization (ΔHvap) remains constant over the specific temperature range.

Application to Solid-Liquid Equilibria

  • For melting (fusion), both Vliquid and Vsolid are retained because their volume differences are extremely small.
  • For most substances, Vliquid > Vsolid, making ΔV positive and resulting in a positive slope (ΔHfus > 0, dP⁄dT > 0).
  • Water is a notable anomaly where Vice > Vliquid water, resulting in a negative slope (dP⁄dT < 0), meaning an increase in pressure lowers the melting point.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Clausius-Clapeyron Equation (Liquid-Vapor)
d(ln P)⁄dT = ΔHvap / (R · T2)
Conditions: Vaporization or sublimation equilibria where vapor behaves as an ideal gas and Vliquid << Vgas
Units: P in atm/Torr, T in K, ΔHvap in J·mol-1, R = 8.314 J·K-1·mol-1
Integrated Clausius-Clapeyron Equation (Two-Point Form)
ln(P2⁄P1) = (-ΔHvap / R) · (1⁄T2 - 1⁄T1)
Conditions: Assuming ΔHvap is independent of temperature over the given interval
Units: P in any units, T in Kelvin, R = 8.314 J·K-1·mol-1
Solid-Liquid Phase Transition Form
dP⁄dT = ΔHfus / (T · (Vliquid - Vsolid))
Conditions: Melting or fusion process under applied external pressure
Units: ΔHfus in J·mol-1, V in m3·mol-1
General Clapeyron Equation
dP⁄dT = ΔH / (T · ΔV)
Conditions: Any phase transition involving a single component
Units: P in Pa or atm, T in K, ΔH in J·mol-1, ΔV in m3·mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Increase in pressure increases the melting point of solids because higher density solid forms are favored.

Observation: Water, bismuth, and antimony exhibit a decrease in melting point with an increase in pressure. — The liquid phase is denser than the solid phase (Vliquid < Vsolid), yielding a negative ΔVfus.

Exception Enthalpy of vaporization (ΔHvap) is constant across wide temperature ranges.

Observation: ΔHvap varies noticeably with temperature, approaching zero at the critical temperature. — Heat capacities of the gas and liquid phases differ (ΔCp ≠ 0), causing temperature dependence according to Kirchhoff's law.

Exception Vapor phase behaves ideally under all low-pressure conditions.

Observation: Polar vapors like water, ammonia, or associating vapors like acetic acid deviate significantly from ideality. — Intermolecular hydrogen bonding and non-ideal gas compressibility factors cause deviations from the simplified Clausius-Clapeyron equation.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Base-10 Logarithm Conversion
log(P2⁄P1) = (-ΔHvap / 2.303 R) · (1⁄T2 - 1⁄T1)

Application: Use this form when solving numerical problems involving standard base-10 logarithms to save computation time.

Sign Analysis for Melting Slopes
If ΔV > 0 → dP⁄dT > 0; If ΔV < 0 → dP⁄dT < 0

Application: Quickly determine whether a solid sinks or floats in its own liquid and how pressure affects the melting point.

Boiling Point Estimation Shortcut
ln P = -A⁄T + B

Application: Recognize that plotting ln P versus 1/T yields a straight line with a slope equal to -ΔHvap⁄R, bypassing extensive calculations.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The Clapeyron equation applies to any phase equilibrium of a single-component system.
  • The Clausius-Clapeyron equation assumes vapor behaves as an ideal gas and liquid volume is negligible.
  • The slope of the ln P versus 1/T plot gives -ΔHvap⁄R.
  • The melting point of ice decreases with an increase in external pressure due to negative ΔV.
  • Units must be consistent: R = 8.314 J·K-1·mol-1 and ΔH in J·mol-1.
  • At the critical point, ΔHvap approaches zero and the phase boundary terminates.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Failing to convert Celsius temperatures to Kelvin before substituting into the Clausius-Clapeyron equation.
  • Using the simplified Clausius-Clapeyron equation for solid-liquid equilibria where volume changes cannot be ignored.
  • Forgetting the negative sign in front of ΔHvap⁄R in the integrated equation.
  • Mixing up units of Joules and Calories for the gas constant R and enthalpy change ΔH.

⚡ Quick Revision: Clapeyron-Clausius Equation

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Thermodynamic Foundation of Clapeyron Equation

The exact Clapeyron equation relates pressure, temperature, enthalpy changes, and volume changes during phase transitions in a pure substance based on chemical potential equality.

Clausius-Clapeyron Approximations

By assuming ideal gas behavior for the vapor and neglecting condensed phase volume, the exact equation simplifies to evaluate liquid-vapor and solid-vapor equilibria.

Temperature Dependence of Vapor Pressure

The integrated form allows calculation of vapor pressure at a second temperature given the enthalpy of vaporization and vapor pressure at a primary temperature.

Anomalous Behavior of Water

Water's higher solid density causes a negative melting curve slope on the P-T diagram, distinguishing it from most other substances.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Clapeyron Equation (dP⁄dT = ΔH⁄(T·ΔV))
Exact thermodynamic relation for phase transitions in a single component.
Clausius-Clapeyron Equation (d(ln P)⁄dT = ΔHvap⁄(RT2))
Approximated differential equation for liquid-vapor equilibria.
Enthalpy of Vaporization (ΔHvap)
Heat absorbed when one mole of liquid converts to vapor at constant pressure, J·mol-1.
Molar Volume Change (ΔV)
Difference in molar volumes between two phases, m3·mol-1.
Triple Point (Tt, Pt)
Unique temperature and pressure where solid, liquid, and vapor coexist in equilibrium.
Phase Boundary (P-T Coexistence Curve)
Line on a phase diagram satisfying the Clapeyron equation.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • dP⁄dT = ΔH / (T · ΔV)
  • ln(P2⁄P1) = (-ΔHvap / R) · (1⁄T2 - 1⁄T1)
  • Slope of ln P vs 1/T plot = -ΔHvap / R
  • Ice melting point decreases with increasing pressure due to negative ΔV
  • Vliquid is negligible compared to Vgas in Clausius-Clapeyron derivation

📌 Quick Recall

  • Clapeyron equation connects pressure and temperature for any phase transition.
  • Clausius-Clapeyron applies specifically to vaporization and sublimation.
  • Integration assumes constant ΔH over the temperature interval.
  • Water shows negative solid-liquid slope due to higher ice volume.

⚖️ Key Differences

Clapeyron Equation VS Clausius-Clapeyron Equation

Clapeyron is exact for all phase changes with no approximations; Clausius-Clapeyron assumes ideal gas vapor and negligible condensed volume for vaporization/sublimation.

Question 1 of 18

What does the slope of a plot of ln P versus 1/T represent for a liquid-vapor equilibrium?

Explanation: From the integrated Clausius-Clapeyron equation, ln P = (-ΔHvap/R)(1/T) + C, so the slope is -ΔHvap/R.

Question 2 of 18

Which assumption is made in deriving the Clausius-Clapeyron equation from the exact Clapeyron equation?

Explanation: The Clausius-Clapeyron equation assumes Vliquid << Vgas and that the vapor obeys the ideal gas law.

Question 3 of 18

How does an increase in external pressure affect the melting point of ice?

Explanation: Water expands upon freezing (Vsolid > Vliquid), making ΔV negative; hence, increasing pressure lowers the melting point.

Question 4 of 18

What is the unit of the slope in the Clapeyron equation (dP/dT)?

Explanation: Pressure divided by temperature has units of Pa·K-1 or atm·K-1.

Question 5 of 18

The Clapeyron equation is applicable to which of the following systems?

Explanation: The Clapeyron equation specifically describes equilibrium between two phases of a single component.

Question 6 of 18

If the enthalpy of vaporization of a liquid is 40 kJ·mol-1, what is its approximate behavior in the integrated Clausius-Clapeyron equation?

Explanation: The exponential form P ∝ exp(-ΔH/RT) shows that vapor pressure increases exponentially with temperature.

Question 7 of 18

For a sublimation process (solid → gas), what is ΔV approximately equal to?

Explanation: Since solid volume is negligible compared to gas volume, ΔV ≈ Vgas.

Question 8 of 18

Which substance has a negative slope on its solid-liquid phase boundary line?

Explanation: Water, along with bismuth and antimony, expands upon freezing, giving a negative solid-liquid slope.

Question 9 of 18

What is the primary condition required to integrate the Clausius-Clapeyron equation assuming ΔHvap is constant?

Explanation: ΔHvap varies with temperature according to Kirchhoff's law, so it can only be treated as constant over narrow temperature intervals.

Question 10 of 18

In the Clapeyron equation dP⁄dT = ΔH / (T · ΔV), what does ΔS represent?

Explanation: By definition of entropy change at phase transition equilibrium, ΔS = ΔH / T.

Question 11 of 18

What happens to the vapor pressure of a liquid when temperature increases?

Explanation: Vaporization is endothermic (ΔHvap > 0), so increasing temperature increases vapor pressure according to Le Chatelier's principle and Clausius-Clapeyron.

Question 12 of 18

Which of the following equations represents the integrated form of the Clausius-Clapeyron equation for two temperatures?

Explanation: Integration between T1 and T2 yields ln(P2/P1) = (-ΔHvap/R)(1/T2 - 1/T1).

Question 13 of 18

What is the sign of ΔV for a vaporization process?

Explanation: Gas volume is always significantly larger than liquid volume, making ΔV = Vgas - Vliquid positive.

Question 14 of 18

Why does the melting curve of ice have a negative slope?

Explanation: Liquid water has a higher density than ice, meaning Vliquid < Vsolid, resulting in a negative ΔVfus.

Question 15 of 18

If ΔHvap = 30 kJ·mol-1 and boiling point is 300 K, what is the approximate entropy of vaporization (ΔSvap)?

Explanation: ΔSvap = ΔHvap / T = 30,000 J·mol-1 / 300 K = 100 J·K-1·mol-1.

Question 16 of 18

Which phase transition has the largest molar volume change (ΔV)?

Explanation: Liquid-vapor transitions involve the creation of a gaseous phase, leading to a massive increase in molar volume.

Question 17 of 18

What physical property does the Clapeyron equation fail to calculate directly without additional data?

Explanation: The Clapeyron equation relates changes (ΔH, ΔV, dP, dT) but cannot determine absolute entropy values without third-law integration.

Question 18 of 18

At what point does the liquid-vapor line on a phase diagram terminate?

Explanation: The liquid-vapor coexistence curve terminates at the critical point, above which distinct liquid and vapor phases cease to exist.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the vapor pressure of a liquid at 350 K if its vapor pressure at 300 K is 100 mmHg and ΔHvap = 40 kJ·mol-1 (R = 8.314 J·K-1·mol-1).

Solution: Using ln(P2/100) = (-40,000/8.314) (1/350 - 1/300) = 1.69; P2 = 100 exp(1.69) ≈ 542 mmHg.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following conditions must be satisfied for the direct applicability of the Clausius-Clapeyron equation?

Solution: Clausius-Clapeyron applies to single-component equilibria where one phase is a gas (vaporization or sublimation).

Question 3 of 10
What is the effect of increasing pressure on the boiling point of a liquid?

Solution: Since ΔHvap > 0 and ΔVvap > 0, dP⁄dT is always positive for vaporization, meaning boiling point increases with pressure.

Question 4 of 10
If the boiling point of a liquid at 1 atm is 350 K and its ΔHvap is 30 kJ·mol-1, what is dP⁄dT at this temperature?

Solution: Using Clausius-Clapeyron: dP/dT = (P · ΔH)/(R · T2) = (101325 Pa 30,000 J) / (8.314 3502) ≈ 3100 Pa·K-1 ≈ 3.1 kPa·K-1.

Question 5 of 10
Why does the Clausius-Clapeyron equation work well for vaporization at temperatures far below the critical point?

Solution: Far below the critical point, gas density is extremely low (ideal gas behavior) and liquid volume is negligible compared to gas volume.

Question 6 of 10
What is the slope of the coexistence curve for sublimation in a P-T diagram compared to vaporization?

Solution: Since ΔHsub = ΔHfus + ΔHvap, the enthalpy change for sublimation is larger, resulting in a steeper slope.

Question 7 of 10
Which thermodynamic property is continuous across a first-order phase transition boundary?

Solution: At equilibrium, the chemical potential of the two coexisting phases must be equal (μA = μB).

Question 8 of 10
In the derivation of the Clapeyron equation, what does the term ΔS represent?

Solution: ΔS is the difference in molar entropy between the two phases in equilibrium.

Question 9 of 10
If ΔHvap is 45 kJ·mol-1, calculate the boiling point elevation when pressure changes such that ln(P2/P1) = 0.1.

Solution: Using integrated form with T1 ≈ 350 K: ΔT ≈ (R · T2 · ln(P2/P1)) / ΔHvap ≈ (8.314 3502 0.1) / 45,000 ≈ 2.26 K.

Question 10 of 10
What does a negative value of dP⁄dT indicate for a solid-liquid phase line?

Solution: A negative slope means ΔV < 0, implying Vliquid < Vsolid, so the solid is less dense than the liquid.
