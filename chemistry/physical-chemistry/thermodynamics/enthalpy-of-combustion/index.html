Physical Chemistry

Enthalpy of Combustion

The enthalpy of combustion is the enthalpy change accompanying the complete oxidation of one mole of a substance in the presence of excess oxygen at a specified temperature and pressure. It is fundamentally an exothermic process characterized by a negative standard enthalpy change (ΔHc° < 0).

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamic Definition and Sign Convention

  • The enthalpy of combustion (ΔHc) represents the heat evolved when one mole of a substance undergoes complete combustion in oxygen.
  • Because combustion processes release heat to the surroundings, ΔHc is always negative under standard conditions.
  • Standard reference states assume carbon burns to CO2(g), hydrogen burns to H2O(l), and sulfur burns to SO2(g).
  • Measurements are typically carried out at constant volume using a bomb calorimeter, yielding ΔU, which is then converted to ΔH.

Calorimetric Measurement and Bomb Calorimetry

  • A bomb calorimeter operates at constant volume, meaning the heat measured corresponds directly to the change in internal energy (ΔU).
  • The heat capacity of the calorimeter (Ccal) is determined using a calibration substance with a known enthalpy of combustion, such as benzoic acid.
  • The relation utilized is q = -Ccal × ΔT, accounting for the total heat capacity of the apparatus and water bath.
  • The enthalpy change is calculated using ΔH = ΔU + PΔV, substituting ΔngRT for PΔV.

Applications and Fuel Efficiency

  • Calorific value defines the energy density of a fuel, calculated as the energy released per unit mass or volume.
  • The presence of oxygen in fuels like alcohols reduces their enthalpy of combustion magnitude compared to pure hydrocarbons of equivalent molecular weight.
  • Hess's Law applies directly to combustion cycles, allowing the determination of enthalpies of formation from combustion data.
  • Incomplete combustion yields carbon monoxide (CO) or soot, releasing less heat and complicating thermodynamic calculations.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Enthalpy of Combustion from Enthalpies of Combustion of Reactants and Products
ΔH° = ∑νrΔHc°(reactants) - ∑νpΔHc°(products)
Conditions: Complete combustion to standard reference products (CO2(g), H2O(l), N2(g), SO2(g))
Units: kJ mol-1
Relationship Between Enthalpy and Internal Energy of Combustion
ΔHc = ΔUc + ΔngRT
Conditions: Ideal gas behavior for gaseous reactants and products
Units: ΔHc, ΔUc in kJ mol-1; R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1; T in Kelvin
Calorific Value Calculation
CV = -Δ H_cMolar Mass
Conditions: Mass of fuel burned in grams or kilograms
Units: kJ g-1 or MJ kg-1
Standard Enthalpy of Combustion from Enthalpies of Formation
ΔHc° = ∑νpΔHf°(products) - ∑νrΔHf°(reactants)
Conditions: Standard state conditions (298.15 K, 1 bar)
Units: kJ mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Combustion reactions are invariably exothermic (ΔH < 0).

Observation: Endothermic formation of certain nitrogen oxides upon high-temperature combustion. — Formation of NO from N2 and O2 requires high activation energy and absorbs heat (ΔH > 0) due to strong N≡N triple bond breaking.

Exception Enthalpy of combustion of carbon always yields carbon dioxide.

Observation: Partial oxidation forms carbon monoxide. — Oxygen deficiency or kinetic control at specific temperatures leads to incomplete combustion products.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Combustion vs Formation Direction Trick
ΔH°rxn = ∑ΔHc°(reactants) - ∑ΔHc°(products)

Application: Remember that for formation data, it is products minus reactants, but for combustion data, it is always reactants minus products.

Delta ng Shortcut for Hydrocarbons
Δng = np(gas) - nr(gas) for CxHy + (x + y/4)O2

Application: Quickly find Δng for calculating ΔH from ΔU without fully balancing complex stoichiometric equations.

Calorific Value Comparison
CV ∝ % of Carbon and HydrogenOxygen content

Application: Fuels with higher hydrogen-to-carbon ratios or lower inherent oxygen yield higher calorific values per gram.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The standard enthalpy of combustion for an element in its standard state is not necessarily zero.
  • Bomb calorimeter measures heat of combustion at constant volume (ΔU).
  • For the combustion of methane: CH4(g) + 2O2(g) → CO2(g) + 2H2O(l), Δng = 1 - 3 = -2.
  • Enthalpy of combustion values are always negative because bond formation in products releases more energy than bond breaking in reactants.
  • The calorific value of hydrogen is the highest among common fuels on a mass basis.
  • Hess's law allows calculation of enthalpy changes that cannot be measured directly through calorimetry.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the sign of ΔHc; combustion is exothermic, so ΔHc is negative.
  • Using water in the gas phase instead of the liquid phase as the standard state reference for hydrogen combustion unless specified.
  • Failing to convert ΔU to ΔH using ΔngRT when calculating combustion enthalpy from bomb calorimeter data.
  • Including pure oxygen or carbon in their standard states with non-zero enthalpy of combustion values.

⚡ Quick Revision: Enthalpy of Combustion

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Fundamental Principles of Combustion

Combustion is the rapid oxidation of a substance resulting in heat and light. The standard enthalpy of combustion quantifies the energy released per mole under standard conditions.

Calorimetric Determinations

Experimental measurement via bomb calorimetry yields internal energy change (ΔU), which is mathematically converted to enthalpy change (ΔH) using the ideal gas equation.

Application of Hess's Law

Enthalpies of combustion data serve as baseline thermochemical data to calculate heats of formation, bond energies, and resonance energies.

Fuel Quality and Calorific Value

The efficiency of a fuel is evaluated via its calorific value, where higher hydrogen content relative to carbon increases specific energy output.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Enthalpy of Combustion (ΔHc°)
Heat evolved when 1 mole of substance undergoes complete combustion in O2 at 1 bar. Unit: kJ mol-1.
Bomb Calorimeter (Constant-volume calorimeter)
Apparatus used to measure internal energy change of combustion reactions.
Calorific Value (CV)
Amount of heat released per gram or kilogram of fuel burned. Unit: kJ g-1.
Standard State (P = 1 bar, T = 298.15 K)
Reference physical state of a substance for thermodynamic measurements.
Complete Oxidation (Ultimate combustion products)
Conversion of C to CO2, H to H2O, and S to SO2.
Thermochemical Equation (Balanced chemical equation with ΔH)
Equation indicating stoichiometric coefficients and physical states alongside enthalpy change.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Combustion reactions are always exothermic (ΔH < 0).
  • ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT relates constant-volume and constant-pressure combustion heats.
  • Standard state water from hydrogen combustion is liquid (H2O(l)).
  • Calorimeter constant Ccal links temperature rise to heat evolved: q = -Ccal × ΔT.
  • Reactants minus products rule applies when calculating reaction enthalpy from standard combustion enthalpies.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Enthalpy of combustion measures energy released during complete oxidation.
  • Bomb calorimeters provide direct ΔU values converted to ΔH.
  • Calorific value determines commercial fuel efficacy.
  • Hess's law interconnects combustion data with formation enthalpies.

⚖️ Key Differences

Enthalpy of Formation VS Enthalpy of Combustion

Formation creates 1 mole of compound from elements (ΔH can be + or -); combustion burns 1 mole of substance in oxygen (ΔH is always -).

Question 1 of 18

What is the sign of the enthalpy of combustion (ΔHc) for any organic compound?

Explanation: Combustion reactions are oxidation processes that release heat to the surroundings, making ΔHc strictly negative.

Question 2 of 18

In a bomb calorimeter experiment, what thermodynamic property is measured directly?

Explanation: A bomb calorimeter operates at constant volume (V = constant), meaning heat measured corresponds to change in internal energy (ΔU).

Question 3 of 18

What is the standard state reference product for sulfur upon combustion?

Explanation: Sulfur burns in excess oxygen under standard conditions to form sulfur dioxide (SO2(g)).

Question 4 of 18

For the combustion of 1 mole of benzene liquid, C6H6(l) + 7.5O2(g) → 6CO2(g) + 3H2O(l), what is Δng?

Explanation: Δng = moles of gaseous products - moles of gaseous reactants = 6 - 7.5 = -1.5.

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following substances has a standard enthalpy of combustion equal to zero?

Explanation: An enthalpy of combustion is defined for a substance burning in oxygen; combustion products like CO2 or elements in standard states do not have zero combustion enthalpy (though elements in formation have zero formation enthalpy).

Question 6 of 18

How is the calorific value of a fuel defined?

Explanation: Calorific value is the quantity of heat produced by the complete combustion of a unit mass (usually 1 gram or 1 kilogram) of a fuel.

Question 7 of 18

If ΔHc for carbon is -393.5 kJ mol-1, what amount of heat is released when 120 g of carbon is burned completely?

Explanation: Moles of carbon = 120 g / 12 g mol-1 = 10 moles. Heat released = 10 × 393.5 kJ mol-1 = 3935 kJ.

Question 8 of 18

Which relationship correctly links ΔH and ΔU for a combustion reaction involving gases?

Explanation: From the definition ΔH = ΔU + Δ(PV), assuming ideal gases yields ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT.

Question 9 of 18

Why is water formed in standard combustion reactions designated as liquid (H2O(l)) rather than gas (H2O(g))?

Explanation: At 298.15 K and 1 bar, the stable state of water is liquid, making it the standard reference state.

Question 10 of 18

Which fuel exhibits the highest calorific value per unit mass?

Explanation: Hydrogen possesses the highest calorific value per gram due to its extremely low molar mass and high mass percentage of reactive reducing atoms.

Question 11 of 18

What is the primary purpose of benzoic acid in bomb calorimetry?

Explanation: Benzoic acid has a precisely known enthalpy of combustion and is used as a standard to determine the calorimeter's heat capacity.

Question 12 of 18

When calculating reaction enthalpy from enthalpies of combustion of reactants and products, which combination is correct?

Explanation: For combustion enthalpies, the formula is reactants minus products: ΔH° = ∑νrΔHc°(reactants) - ∑νpΔHc°(products).

Question 13 of 18

What happens to the magnitude of ΔHc as the homologous series of alkanes ascends?

Explanation: As the molecular weight and number of carbon and hydrogen atoms increase, more bonds are oxidized, increasing the total heat released.

Question 14 of 18

Which equation represents the standard enthalpy of combustion of carbon monoxide?

Explanation: Combustion of 1 mole of carbon monoxide in oxygen to produce carbon dioxide defines its standard enthalpy of combustion.

Question 15 of 18

If Δng is positive for a combustion reaction, how does ΔH compare to ΔU?

Explanation: Since ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT, a positive Δng means ΔH will be greater than ΔU.

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following is not a standard combustion product?

Explanation: Carbon monoxide represents incomplete combustion; complete oxidation yields carbon dioxide.

Question 17 of 18

What is the unit of standard enthalpy of combustion?

Explanation: Standard enthalpy of combustion is conventionally expressed in kilojoules per mole (kJ mol-1).

Question 18 of 18

Why are experimental combustion enthalpies essential in thermochemistry?

Explanation: Combustion data combined with Hess's law enables the determination of enthalpies of formation for complex organic molecules that cannot be synthesized directly.

Model Question
If the enthalpy of formation of CO2(g) and H2O(l) are -393.5 kJ mol-1 and -285.8 kJ mol-1 respectively, and combustion of CH4 is -890 kJ mol-1, what is the enthalpy of formation of methane?
Answer: Option A (-74.8 kJ mol-1) ΔHc(CH4) = ΔHf(CO2) + 2ΔHf(H2O) - ΔHf(CH4). -890 = -393.5 + 2(-285.8) - ΔHf(CH4). Solving gives ΔHf(CH4) = -74.8 kJ mol-1.

Question 1 of 10
The standard enthalpy of combustion of solid carbon to carbon dioxide is -393.3 kJ mol-1. Calculate the heat released when 36 g of carbon is burned in excess air.

Solution: Moles of carbon = 36 g / 12 g mol-1 = 3 moles. Heat released = 3 × 393.3 kJ = 1179.9 kJ.

Question 2 of 10
For which of the following reactions is ΔH equal to ΔU?

Solution: In C(s) + O2(g) → CO2(g), Δng = 1 - 1 = 0, so ΔH = ΔU.

Question 3 of 10
Calculate the standard enthalpy of formation of liquid ethanol, C2H5OH(l), given that the standard enthalpies of combustion of ethanol, carbon, and hydrogen are -1368 kJ mol-1, -393.5 kJ mol-1, and -285.8 kJ mol-1 respectively.

Solution: 2C(s) + 3H2(g) + 0.5O2(g) → C2H5OH(l). ΔHf = 2(-393.5) + 3(-285.8) - (-1368) = -787 - 857.4 + 1368 = -276.4 ≈ -277.6 kJ mol-1.

Question 4 of 10
What is the primary reason that fluorine is not typically used as a reference oxidant in standard combustion definitions despite being a powerful oxidizing agent?

Solution: Fluorine reacts violently and non-selectively with nearly all compounds, creating complex mixtures rather than defined combustion products.

Question 5 of 10
A bomb calorimeter has a heat capacity of 2.5 kJ °C-1. When 0.1 mol of a substance is burned, the temperature rises by 1.2 °C. What is the molar enthalpy of combustion?

Solution: Total heat evolved q = -Ccal × ΔT = -2.5 kJ °C-1 × 1.2 °C = -3.0 kJ for 0.1 mol. Molar enthalpy = -3.0 kJ / 0.1 mol = -300 kJ mol-1.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following pairs has higher enthalpy of combustion for the branched isomer compared to the straight-chain isomer?

Solution: Straight-chain alkanes have higher enthalpies of combustion (more negative) than their branched isomers because branching increases thermodynamic stability, lowering internal energy and combustion heat.

Question 7 of 10
In the combustion of gaseous ethane, C2H6(g) + 3.5O2(g) → 2CO2(g) + 3H2O(l), what is the value of Δng?

Solution: Δng = 2 (gaseous CO2) - 4.5 (gaseous reactants: 1 C2H6 + 3.5 O2) = -2.5.

Question 8 of 10
What does a high negative value of enthalpy of combustion signify regarding a fuel?

Solution: A higher magnitude of negative enthalpy of combustion means more energy is released upon burning, indicating superior fuel efficiency.

Question 9 of 10
Under what condition is the heat of combustion at constant volume (ΔU) equal to the heat of combustion at constant pressure (ΔH)?

Solution: When Δng = 0, the term ΔngRT vanishes, making ΔH equal to ΔU.

Question 10 of 10
Which physical state of water in the combustion product equation yields a more negative enthalpy of combustion value?

Solution: Condensation of water vapor to liquid water is exothermic; thus, using H2O(l) adds the enthalpy of condensation, yielding a more negative enthalpy of combustion.
