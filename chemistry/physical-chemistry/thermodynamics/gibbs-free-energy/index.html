Physical Chemistry

Gibbs Free Energy

Gibbs free energy is a thermodynamic potential that measures the maximum reversible work obtainable from a closed system at constant temperature and pressure. It serves as the primary criterion for determining the spontaneity of chemical processes and phase transitions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Spontaneity Criteria and Thermodynamic Sign Conventions

  • A process is spontaneous when ΔG < 0, at equilibrium when ΔG = 0, and non-spontaneous when ΔG > 0 under constant temperature and pressure.
  • Processes with negative ΔH and positive ΔS are spontaneous at all temperatures.
  • Processes with positive ΔH and negative ΔS are non-spontaneous at all temperatures.
  • Processes dependent on temperature switch spontaneity signs at T = ΔH / ΔS.

Standard Gibbs Free Energy and Equilibrium Relations

  • The standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) relates to the reaction quotient via ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q.
  • At dynamic equilibrium, Q = K and ΔG = 0, simplifying the equation to ΔG° = -RT ln K.
  • A large positive value of K (> 1) yields a negative ΔG°, indicating a product-favored equilibrium position.
  • Standard molar Gibbs free energy of formation (ΔGf°) for an element in its standard reference state is defined as zero.

Temperature Dependence and Electrochemical Cells

  • The variation of Gibbs free energy with temperature is governed by the Gibbs-Helmholtz equation: (∂(ΔG/T)/∂T)P = -ΔH / T2.
  • Electrical work derived from a galvanic cell links to Gibbs free energy through ΔG = -nFEcell.
  • Standard cell potential connects to the equilibrium constant via E°cell = (RT / nF) ln K.
  • The temperature coefficient of cell potential (∂Ecell/∂T)P equals ΔS / nF.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Spontaneous processes always have negative ΔG under all conditions.

Observation: ΔG applies strictly to systems at constant temperature and pressure. — For systems at constant internal energy and volume, entropy change (ΔSuniv) dictates spontaneity instead of Gibbs free energy.

Exception Exothermic reactions (ΔH < 0) are universally spontaneous.

Observation: Exothermic reactions with large negative entropy changes (ΔS < 0) become non-spontaneous above a specific threshold temperature. — The TΔS term outweighs ΔH in the equation ΔG = ΔH - TΔS at elevated temperatures.

Exception Standard Gibbs free energy of formation for pure elements is zero.

Observation: Allotropes not chosen as the standard reference state at 298.15 K and 1 bar possess non-zero ΔGf° values (e.g., graphite = 0, diamond = 2.9 kJ mol-1). — Thermodynamic stability scales relative to the most stable crystalline allotrope of the element.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
HOT and COLD Spontaneity Rule
ΔH < 0, ΔS > 0 (All T); ΔH > 0, ΔS < 0 (None T)

Application: Remember 'HOT' for High T when ΔH > 0 and ΔS > 0 (spontaneous when T > ΔH/ΔS) and 'COLD' for Low T when ΔH < 0 and ΔS < 0 (spontaneous when T < ΔH/ΔS).

Faraday Cell Work Shortcut
ΔG° = -nFE°

Application: Convert cell potentials directly to kJ mol-1 by multiplying n × E° by 96.5 when calculating standard free energy.

Equilibrium Constant Sign Check
K > 1 → ΔG° < 0; K < 1 → ΔG° > 0

Application: Instantly eliminate incorrect multiple-choice options by checking if a product-favored reaction matches a negative standard free energy change.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) represents the maximum net work obtainable from a closed system at constant temperature and pressure.
  • At chemical equilibrium under constant T and P, ΔG equals zero.
  • The standard Gibbs free energy change relates to the equilibrium constant via ΔG° = -2.303 RT log K.
  • Electrochemical cell electromotive force relates to free energy through ΔG = -nFE.
  • The entropy change of the universe (ΔSuniv) relates to Gibbs free energy by ΔSuniv = -ΔG / T.
  • Phase transitions at normal boiling or melting points occur at equilibrium where ΔG = 0.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing ΔG with ΔG°; ΔG applies to any arbitrary composition of reaction mixture, whereas ΔG° corresponds strictly to standard states.
  • Forgetting to convert units of ΔH (usually kJ) and ΔS (usually J) when substituting into ΔG = ΔH - TΔS.
  • Assuming a positive standard cell potential (E°) implies a positive ΔG° (remember ΔG° = -nFE°, so positive E° yields negative ΔG°).
  • Applying Gibbs free energy criteria to systems that are not at constant temperature and pressure.

⚡ Quick Revision: Gibbs Free Energy

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition and Physical Significance

Gibbs free energy combines enthalpy and entropy terms into G = H - TS, serving as the definitive measure of reaction feasibility under atmospheric conditions.

Criteria of Spontaneity

A negative sign of ΔG indicates a spontaneous forward process, while positive values dictate non-spontaneity, and zero denotes chemical equilibrium.

Temperature Dependence

The interplay between ΔH and ΔS dictates temperature-dependent spontaneity thresholds calculated via T = ΔH / ΔS.

Electrochemical and Equilibrium Coupling

Gibbs free energy bridges thermodynamics and electrochemistry through exact quantitative links with equilibrium constants and standard cell potentials.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Gibbs Free Energy (G)
Thermodynamic potential measuring maximum non-expansion work; unit is Joules (J).
Standard Free Energy Change (ΔG°)
Free energy change when reactants and products are in standard states; unit kJ mol-1.
Reaction Quotient (Q)
Ratio of activities of products to reactants at any given point; dimensionless.
Equilibrium Constant (K)
Value of reaction quotient at chemical equilibrium; dimensionless.
Faraday Constant (F)
Electric charge carried by one mole of electrons; approximately 96485 C mol-1.
Helmholtz Free Energy (A)
Thermodynamic potential for constant T and V systems defined as U - TS; unit Joules (J).

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔG = ΔH - TΔS
  • ΔG° = -RT ln K
  • ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q
  • ΔG = -nFEcell
  • Spontaneous condition: ΔG < 0 (constant T, P)

📌 Quick Recall

  • Gibbs free energy identifies spontaneity exclusively at constant temperature and pressure.
  • Enthalpy-driven versus entropy-driven processes dictate the temperature dependence of ΔG.
  • Equilibrium constants are directly calculable from standard Gibbs free energy changes.
  • Electrochemical cells interconvert electrical energy and chemical free energy.

⚖️ Key Differences

ΔG VS ΔG°

ΔG applies to any reaction mixture composition, whereas ΔG° refers strictly to standard state conditions.

Question 1 of 18

What is the condition for spontaneity of a chemical reaction at constant temperature and pressure?

Explanation: A process is spontaneous at constant temperature and pressure if the change in Gibbs free energy (ΔG) is negative.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following relations correctly connects standard Gibbs free energy change with the equilibrium constant?

Explanation: The standard Gibbs free energy change is related to the equilibrium constant by the equation ΔG° = -RT ln K.

Question 3 of 18

For a reaction where both ΔH and ΔS are positive, the reaction will be spontaneous at:

Explanation: When ΔH > 0 and ΔS > 0, the term TΔS exceeds ΔH only at high temperatures, making ΔG negative.

Question 4 of 18

What is the value of ΔG at equilibrium under constant temperature and pressure?

Explanation: At dynamic equilibrium under constant temperature and pressure, the Gibbs free energy of the system is at a minimum, making ΔG = 0.

Question 5 of 18

The relationship between Gibbs free energy change and electrical work in a galvanic cell is given by:

Explanation: The maximum electrical work obtainable from a cell is related to Gibbs free energy by ΔG = -nFEcell.

Question 6 of 18

If the reaction quotient Q is greater than the equilibrium constant K, what is the sign of ΔG?

Explanation: When Q > K, the reaction proceeds spontaneously in the reverse direction, resulting in a positive ΔG.

Question 7 of 18

What is the standard Gibbs free energy of formation (ΔGf°) of pure graphite at 298.15 K and 1 bar?

Explanation: By definition, the standard Gibbs free energy of formation of an element in its standard reference state (graphite for carbon) is zero.

Question 8 of 18

Which thermodynamic property is minimized at constant temperature and volume for a spontaneous process?

Explanation: Helmholtz free energy (A) is minimized for spontaneous processes occurring at constant temperature and volume.

Question 9 of 18

Calculate ΔG° for a reaction with an equilibrium constant K = 1 at 298 K.

Explanation: Since ΔG° = -RT ln K and ln(1) = 0, the standard free energy change is zero when K = 1.

Question 10 of 18

An endothermic reaction with a negative entropy change (ΔH > 0, ΔS < 0) will be:

Explanation: Both terms work against spontaneity (ΔH is positive, -TΔS is positive), making ΔG positive at all temperatures.

Question 11 of 18

The units of Gibbs free energy (G) in the SI system are:

Explanation: Gibbs free energy is an extensive thermodynamic property expressed in Joules (J) or kilojoules (kJ).

Question 12 of 18

What is the effect of increasing temperature on the spontaneity of an exothermic reaction with a negative entropy change?

Explanation: For ΔH < 0 and ΔS < 0, raising the temperature increases the magnitude of the positive TΔS term, eventually making ΔG positive (non-spontaneous).

Question 13 of 18

The Gibbs-Helmholtz equation expresses the variation of which quantity with temperature?

Explanation: The Gibbs-Helmholtz equation relates (∂(ΔG/T)/∂T)P to -ΔH / T2.

Question 14 of 18

If a cell reaction has a positive standard cell potential (E°cell > 0), the reaction is:

Explanation: A positive E°cell results in a negative ΔG° (ΔG° = -nFE°), confirming forward spontaneity.

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following is an intensive property?

Explanation: While total G, H, and S are extensive properties, their molar or specific counterparts are intensive properties.

Question 16 of 18

What does a very large positive value of ΔG° imply about the reverse reaction?

Explanation: A large positive ΔG° means reactants are favored at equilibrium, implying the reverse reaction is spontaneous and equilibrium lies to the left.

Question 17 of 18

In the equation ΔG = ΔH - TΔS, temperature T must always be expressed in:

Explanation: Absolute thermodynamic temperature in thermodynamic equations must always be in Kelvin (K).

Question 18 of 18

The maximum non-expansion work obtainable from a closed system at constant temperature and pressure is equal to:

Explanation: The decrease in Gibbs free energy (-ΔG) equals the maximum non-expansion work available from the system.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the standard Gibbs free energy change for a reaction where K = 10 at 298 K (R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1).

Solution: ΔG° = -2.303 × 8.314 × 298 × log(10) = -5705 J mol-1 ≈ -5.7 kJ mol-1.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following conditions guarantees a non-spontaneous reaction at all temperatures?

Solution: When ΔH > 0 and ΔS < 0, ΔG = ΔH - TΔS is always positive at any temperature.

Question 3 of 10
The standard free energy of formation of an element in its standard state is:

Solution: By international convention, the standard Gibbs free energy of formation for any element in its reference state is defined as zero.

Question 4 of 10
For a particular reaction, ΔH = -30 kJ mol-1 and ΔS = -100 J K-1 mol-1. At what temperature does the reaction reach equilibrium?

Solution: At equilibrium, ΔG = 0, so T = ΔH / ΔS = (-30000 J mol-1) / (-100 J K-1 mol-1) = 300 K.

Question 5 of 10
What is the maximum electrical work from 2 moles of electrons passing through a cell with E° = 1.10 V?

Solution: ΔG° = -nFE° = -2 × 96485 C mol-1 × 1.10 V = -212267 J = -212.3 kJ.

Question 6 of 10
If Q < K for a given reaction mixture, the reaction will:

Solution: When Q < K, ΔG is negative, indicating spontaneous progression in the forward direction until Q = K.

Question 7 of 10
Which equation correctly represents the pressure dependence of Gibbs free energy for an ideal gas?

Solution: For an isothermal expansion of an ideal gas, G = G° + RT ln(P / P°).

Question 8 of 10
In an isolated system, spontaneity is determined by:

Solution: Isolated systems cannot exchange heat or matter, so spontaneity is governed entirely by the total entropy increase (ΔStotal > 0).

Question 9 of 10
The units of the molar entropy term (ΔS) when calculating ΔG in kJ are typically:

Solution: Standard entropy values are reported in J K-1 mol-1, requiring division by 1000 when combined with kJ enthalpy values.

Question 10 of 10
What does a negative sign of ΔG° signify about an equilibrium constant?

Solution: Since ΔG° = -RT ln K, a negative ΔG° requires ln K to be positive, meaning K > 1.
