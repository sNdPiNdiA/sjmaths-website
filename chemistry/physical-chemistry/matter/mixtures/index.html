Physical Chemistry

Mixtures and Solutions

A mixture is a physical combination of two or more substances where each component retains its individual chemical identity. Solutions are homogeneous mixtures characterized by a uniform composition at the molecular level.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification of Dispersed Systems

  • True solutions consist of particles smaller than 1 nm, forming a homogeneous phase.
  • Colloidal dispersions contain particles between 1 nm and 1000 nm, exhibiting the Tyndall effect.
  • Suspensions contain particles larger than 1000 nm, which settle under gravity over time.
  • Classification is based primarily on the particle size of the dispersed phase.

Thermodynamics of Ideal Solutions

  • An ideal solution obeys Raoult's Law across the entire concentration range.
  • Mixing of ideal components results in ΔHmix = 0 and ΔVmix = 0.
  • The entropy of mixing is always positive, ΔSmix > 0, driving the spontaneous formation of the mixture.
  • Intermolecular forces between A-A, B-B, and A-B are identical in magnitude.

Colligative Properties

  • Colligative properties depend solely on the number of solute particles, not their chemical nature.
  • Includes relative lowering of vapor pressure, elevation of boiling point, depression of freezing point, and osmotic pressure.
  • The van't Hoff factor (i) accounts for the dissociation or association of solute particles in the solvent.
  • For non-electrolytes, i = 1; for electrolytes, i > 1 (dissociation) or i < 1 (association).
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Molality
m = nsolute / msolvent(kg)
Conditions: Temperature independent
Units: mol kg-1
Mole Fraction
xi = ni / Σntotal
Conditions: Sum of all mole fractions equals 1
Units: Dimensionless
Raoult's Law
Pi = xiP°i
Conditions: Ideal solutions
Units: P in Pa or atm
Henry's Law
P = KH χ
Conditions: Dilute solutions of gases in liquids
Units: KH in Pa or atm
Molarity
M = nsolute / Vsolution(L)
Conditions: Temperature dependent due to volume expansion
Units: mol L-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Raoult's Law behavior

Observation: Negative deviation — Stronger A-B interactions than A-A or B-B, leading to lower vapor pressure.

Exception Raoult's Law behavior

Observation: Positive deviation — Weaker A-B interactions than A-A or B-B, leading to higher vapor pressure.

Exception Solubility of gases in liquids

Observation: Temperature increase — Dissolution of gas is usually exothermic; higher T favors the reverse (endothermic) process.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Osmotic pressure (π) is defined by πV = nRT.
  • Azeotropes are constant boiling mixtures that cannot be separated by fractional distillation.
  • Maximum boiling azeotropes show large negative deviations from Raoult's Law.
  • Minimum boiling azeotropes show large positive deviations from Raoult's Law.
  • Molality is the preferred unit for temperature-dependent studies.
  • The van't Hoff factor i = (observed colligative property) / (calculated colligative property).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Molarity (M) with Molality (m) in temperature-dependent calculations.
  • Forgetting the van't Hoff factor (i) for ionic solutes in colligative property calculations.
  • Assuming all mixtures are ideal; real solutions almost always deviate.
  • Misinterpreting the sign of ΔHmix for positive vs negative deviations.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Solute (Dispersed phase)
Component present in smaller amount.
Solvent (Dispersion medium)
Component present in larger amount.
Azeotrope (Constant boiling mixture)
Mixture with same composition in liquid and vapor.
Osmosis (Solvent flow)
Movement of solvent through semi-permeable membrane.
Kb (Ebullioscopic constant)
Boiling point elevation per unit molality.
Kf (Cryoscopic constant)
Freezing point depression per unit molality.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • ΔTb = i Kb m
  • ΔTf = i Kf m
  • π = iCRT
  • Ptotal = PA + PB
  • i = 1 for non-electrolytes

📌 Quick Recall

  • Mixtures are physical, not chemical.
  • Ideal solutions follow Raoult's Law.
  • Colligative properties are particle-count dependent.
  • Azeotropes have fixed boiling points.

⚖️ Key Differences

Molarity VS Molality

Molarity is volume-based (T-dependent); Molality is mass-based (T-independent).
