Physical Chemistry

Gaseous State Behaviour

The gaseous state is governed by the kinetic molecular theory, which assumes particles are in constant random motion with negligible intermolecular forces. Real gases deviate from ideal behaviour due to finite molecular volume and interparticle attractions, quantified by the compressibility factor Z.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Kinetic Molecular Theory Postulates

  • Gas molecules are considered point masses with negligible volume compared to the container.
  • Collisions between molecules and container walls are perfectly elastic, conserving total kinetic energy.
  • Average kinetic energy of gas molecules is directly proportional to the absolute temperature (K).
  • Intermolecular forces of attraction are assumed to be zero in the ideal gas model.

Real Gas Deviations

  • At high pressure, the finite volume of molecules (b) becomes significant, leading to Z > 1.
  • At low temperature, intermolecular attractions (a) dominate, causing the gas to be more compressible (Z < 1).
  • The Boyle temperature (TB = a/Rb) is the temperature where a real gas behaves ideally over a wide pressure range.
  • Liquefaction is easier for gases with higher Van der Waals constants 'a' due to stronger attractive forces.

Molecular Speed Distributions

  • The Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution shows the fraction of molecules possessing specific speeds at a given temperature.
  • The relationship between speeds is urms > uavg > ump.
  • Increasing temperature shifts the distribution curve to the right and flattens the peak.
  • The most probable speed (ump) is given by √(2RT/M).
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Van der Waals Equation
(P + an2/V2)(V - nb) = nRT
Conditions: Applicable to real gases accounting for molecular volume and attraction.
Units: a in Pa m6 mol-2, b in m3 mol-1
Compressibility Factor
Z = PV / nRT
Conditions: Measure of deviation from ideal behaviour.
Units: Z is dimensionless; Z=1 for ideal, Z≠1 for real gases.
Root Mean Square Velocity
urms = √(3RT/M)
Conditions: Average speed of gas molecules at temperature T.
Units: urms in m/s, M in kg/mol
Critical Constants
Tc = 8a / 27Rb; Pc = a / 27b2
Conditions: Conditions at which gas and liquid phases become indistinguishable.
Units: Tc in K, Pc in Pa
Ideal Gas Law
PV = nRT
Conditions: Applicable to ideal gases at low pressure and high temperature.
Units: P in Pa, V in m3, n in mol, R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1, T in K
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Ideal gases follow PV=nRT at all conditions.

Observation: Real gases deviate significantly at high pressure and low temperature. — Molecular volume and intermolecular forces cannot be ignored under these conditions.

Exception Compressibility factor Z = 1 for all gases.

Observation: H2 and He show Z > 1 at all pressures. — Attractive forces are negligible, and the repulsive volume effect dominates even at low pressures.

Exception Gases liquefy upon cooling.

Observation: Gases above their critical temperature (Tc) cannot be liquefied by pressure alone. — Kinetic energy exceeds the potential energy of intermolecular attractions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The SI unit of pressure is Pascal (Pa) or N/m2.
  • Boyle's Law: P ∝ 1/V at constant T.
  • Charles's Law: V ∝ T at constant P.
  • Avogadro's Law: V ∝ n at constant P and T.
  • Dalton's Law: Ptotal = ΣPi for non-reacting gases.
  • Graham's Law of Diffusion: Rate ∝ 1/√M.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the units of R; always check if using 0.0821 (L atm) or 8.314 (J).
  • Forgetting to convert Celsius to Kelvin when using gas laws.
  • Assuming 'a' represents volume; 'a' represents attractive forces, 'b' represents volume.
  • Applying ideal gas laws to gases near their critical point.

⚡ Quick Revision: Gaseous State Behaviour

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Gas Laws

Boyle, Charles, and Gay-Lussac laws describe the relationship between P, V, and T. Combined, they form the Ideal Gas Equation.

Kinetic Theory

Relates macroscopic properties (P, T) to microscopic motion (urms). Kinetic energy is solely a function of temperature.

Real Gas Deviations

Van der Waals equation corrects for molecular size (b) and attraction (a). Deviations are measured by Z.

Molecular Speeds

Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution defines the spread of molecular velocities. Speeds depend on temperature and molar mass.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Boyle Temperature (TB)
Temperature where a real gas behaves ideally.
Critical Pressure (Pc)
Minimum pressure to liquefy gas at Tc.
Diffusion (Rate)
Spreading of gas molecules.
Effusion (Rate)
Gas escape through a small orifice.
Molar Volume (Vm)
Volume of 1 mole of gas at STP (22.4 L).
Ideal Gas (PV=nRT)
Hypothetical gas obeying all gas laws.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • R = 8.314 J/mol K
  • STP = 273.15 K and 1 bar
  • urms = √(3RT/M)
  • Z = PV/nRT
  • TB = a/Rb

📌 Quick Recall

  • Real gases behave ideally at low P and high T.
  • Attractive forces dominate at low T.
  • Excluded volume dominates at high P.
  • Kinetic energy is proportional to T.

⚖️ Key Differences

Diffusion VS Effusion

Diffusion is mixing; Effusion is escape through a hole.
