Physical Chemistry

Chemical Compounds

A chemical compound is a substance formed by the chemical combination of two or more elements in a fixed mass ratio. These substances possess unique chemical and physical properties distinct from their constituent elements.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Stoichiometric Principles

  • Compounds follow the Law of Conservation of Mass in all closed chemical reactions.
  • The Law of Multiple Proportions applies when two elements form more than one compound.
  • Stoichiometry relies on the Mole Concept to relate mass, volume, and particle count.
  • Chemical formulas represent the stoichiometric composition of the substance.

Bonding and Structural Classification

  • Ionic compounds are formed by electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.
  • Covalent compounds involve electron sharing between non-metal atoms.
  • Coordinate covalent bonds occur when one atom provides both electrons for the bond.
  • Lattice energy determines the stability of ionic crystal structures.

Compositional Analysis

  • Empirical formulas provide the simplest ratio of atoms in a compound.
  • Molecular formulas define the actual number of atoms present in one molecule.
  • Hydrated compounds contain water molecules within their crystal lattice structure.
  • Percentage composition is calculated using atomic weights from the periodic table.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Empirical Formula Calculation
ni = massi / Mi
Conditions: Used to determine the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms.
Units: n in moles, M in g/mol
Molecular Formula Relation
Molecular Formula = n × (Empirical Formula)
Conditions: n = Molar Mass / Empirical Formula Mass
Units: n is a dimensionless integer
Mass Percent Composition
% element = (mass of element in compound / molar mass of compound) × 100
Conditions: Based on total molar mass of the compound.
Units: Percentage (%)
Law of Definite Proportions
mA / mB = constant
Conditions: Applies to all pure chemical compounds regardless of source.
Units: Mass in grams (g)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Fixed stoichiometry in compounds

Observation: Non-stoichiometric compounds (Bertollides) — Defects in crystal lattices, such as metal deficiency in Fe1-xO.

Exception Ionic compounds are solids

Observation: Ionic liquids — Large, asymmetric ions prevent efficient packing, lowering the melting point.

Exception Noble gases are inert

Observation: XeF2, XeF4, XeF6 — High electronegativity of fluorine allows excitation of Xe electrons.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Avogadro's number is 6.022 × 1023 mol-1.
  • The sum of oxidation numbers in a neutral compound is zero.
  • Isomers have the same molecular formula but different structures.
  • Hydrates release water upon heating to form anhydrous salts.
  • Molecular mass is the sum of atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule.
  • The limiting reagent determines the theoretical yield of a reaction.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing empirical formula with molecular formula; always check the molar mass ratio.
  • Ignoring the water of crystallization when calculating the molar mass of hydrates.
  • Incorrectly assigning oxidation states to elements in peroxides (e.g., O is -1).
  • Assuming all compounds follow the law of definite proportions (ignoring non-stoichiometric solids).

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Compounds

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Law of Definite Proportions

A chemical compound always contains the same elements in the same proportion by mass, regardless of the method of preparation.

Molar Mass Calculation

The molar mass is the sum of the atomic masses of all atoms in the formula unit, expressed in g/mol.

Stoichiometric Ratios

Chemical equations must be balanced to reflect the conservation of atoms, ensuring the mole ratios are consistent.

Crystal Lattice Stability

Lattice energy is the energy released when gaseous ions form an ionic solid, directly influencing compound stability.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Mole (mol)
Amount of substance containing 6.022 × 1023 particles.
Molar Mass (M)
Mass of one mole of a substance in g/mol.
Valency (V)
Combining capacity of an element.
Hydrate (MX·nH2O)
Compound with fixed water molecules in its structure.
Isomer (CxHy)
Compounds with same formula but different arrangement.
Stoichiometry (n)
Quantitative relationship between reactants and products.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • 1 mol = 22.4 L at STP for ideal gases
  • Sum of oxidation states = 0 for neutral molecules
  • Empirical formula is the simplest ratio
  • Molecular formula = n × empirical formula
  • Ionic bonds form between metal and non-metal

📌 Quick Recall

  • Compounds have fixed composition.
  • Formulas represent atom ratios.
  • Bond type dictates physical properties.
  • Stoichiometry governs reaction yields.
