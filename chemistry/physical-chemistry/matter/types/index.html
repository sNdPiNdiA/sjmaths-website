Physical Chemistry

Classification of Matter

Matter is defined as any substance that possesses mass and occupies space, existing primarily in solid, liquid, gaseous, or plasma states. It is classified chemically into pure substances, including elements and compounds, and mixtures, which are categorized as homogeneous or heterogeneous.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Physical States of Matter

  • Solids possess definite shape and volume due to strong intermolecular forces.
  • Liquids exhibit definite volume but take the shape of the container, characterized by fluidity.
  • Gases lack definite shape or volume, with particles moving randomly at high velocities.
  • Plasma consists of ionized gas particles, occurring at extremely high temperatures.

Classification of Pure Substances

  • Elements are fundamental substances consisting of only one type of atom, represented by chemical symbols.
  • Compounds are chemically bonded substances composed of two or more elements in a fixed ratio.
  • The Law of Definite Proportions dictates that a compound always contains elements in fixed mass ratios.
  • Compounds can only be separated into elements via chemical reactions.

Mixtures and Separation Techniques

  • Homogeneous mixtures, or solutions, exhibit uniform composition throughout the phase.
  • Heterogeneous mixtures possess non-uniform composition with visible phase boundaries.
  • Separation methods include distillation, filtration, chromatography, and sublimation based on physical properties.
  • Mixtures retain the individual properties of their constituent components.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Molarity
M = nsolute / Vsolution(L)
Conditions: Temperature dependent due to volume expansion
Units: mol/L or M
Mole Fraction
xA = nA / (nA + nB)
Conditions: Sum of all mole fractions in a mixture equals 1
Units: Dimensionless
Density
ρ = m / V
Conditions: Constant pressure and temperature
Units: kg/m3 or g/cm3
Molality
m = nsolute / masssolvent(kg)
Conditions: Temperature independent
Units: mol/kg or m
Mass Percent Composition
Mass % = (Mass of solute / Mass of solution) × 100
Conditions: Applicable to mixtures and solutions
Units: Percentage (%)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Solids are generally denser than their liquid phase

Observation: Water (Ice) — Hydrogen bonding creates an open cage-like structure in ice, increasing volume and decreasing density.

Exception Elements are typically monatomic or diatomic

Observation: Sulfur (S8) and Phosphorus (P4) — These elements form stable polyatomic allotropes to satisfy valency requirements.

Exception Gases are highly compressible

Observation: Ideal gas behavior at high pressure — At very high pressures, the volume of gas molecules becomes significant relative to the container volume.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Temperature Independence
Molality (m) and Mole Fraction (x) are T-independent

Application: Use these for calculations involving temperature changes as they do not involve volume.

Homogeneous vs Heterogeneous
One phase = Homogeneous; Multiple phases = Heterogeneous

Application: Check for phase boundaries (e.g., oil in water) to identify heterogeneous mixtures.

Element Identification
Look for chemical formulas with one capital letter

Application: Distinguish elements (e.g., He, O2) from compounds (e.g., CO, H2O).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Matter is classified into pure substances and mixtures.
  • Elements contain atoms of the same type.
  • Compounds are formed by chemical combination in fixed ratios.
  • Solutions are homogeneous mixtures.
  • Colloids are intermediate between true solutions and suspensions.
  • Physical changes do not alter chemical identity.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing compounds with mixtures; compounds have fixed ratios, mixtures do not.
  • Assuming all solutions are liquid; gaseous and solid solutions exist.
  • Neglecting temperature effects on molarity calculations.
  • Misidentifying alloys as compounds; they are solid solutions.

⚡ Quick Revision: Classification of Matter

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Pure Substances

Substances with constant chemical composition and distinct properties. Includes elements and compounds.

Mixtures

Physical combinations of substances where components retain individual chemical identities.

States of Matter

Defined by kinetic energy of particles vs. intermolecular forces of attraction.

Chemical Change

A process resulting in the formation of new substances with different chemical properties.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Element (Pure substance)
Cannot be broken down by chemical means.
Compound (Chemical combination)
Two or more elements in fixed ratio.
Solution (Homogeneous mixture)
Solute dissolved in solvent.
Suspension (Heterogeneous mixture)
Particles settle over time.
Colloid (Intermediate mixture)
Particles do not settle, show Tyndall effect.
Allotrope (Structural variation)
Different forms of the same element.

Physical Change VS Chemical Change
