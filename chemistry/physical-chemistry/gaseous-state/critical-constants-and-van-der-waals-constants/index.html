Relation of Critical Constants with van der Waals Constants

Real gases deviate from ideal behavior due to intermolecular attractions and finite molecular volumes, corrected in the van der Waals equation. Critical constants define the state parameters at the critical point where liquid and vapor phases merge into a single homogeneous phase.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Derivation of Critical Constants from van der Waals Equation

  • The van der Waals equation for one mole is expanded into a cubic equation in terms of molar volume V.
  • At the critical point, the isotherm shows an inflection point where the first and second derivatives of pressure with respect to volume equal zero.
  • Equating the coefficients of the expanded van der Waals equation with the standard critical state identity (V - Vc)3 = 0 yields the relations.
  • Solving the simultaneous polynomial equations expresses a and b strictly in terms of Tc, Pc, and R.

Calculation of van der Waals Constants from Critical Data

  • The constant a representing intermolecular attraction is calculated using the formula a = 27R2Tc2 / (64Pc).
  • The excluded volume constant b is determined directly from the critical volume using the relation b = Vc / 3.
  • Alternatively, b can be evaluated using critical temperature and pressure via b = R · Tc / (8 · Pc).
  • Experimental measurement of critical constants provides the most reliable route to finding unique molecular parameters a and b for real gases.

Critical Constants and Reduced Equation of State

  • Reduced properties are defined as the ratio of actual property to its critical counterpart: Pr = P / Pc, Vr = V / Vc, and Tr = T / Tc.
  • Substituting reduced variables into the van der Waals equation yields the law of corresponding states: (Pr + 3 / Vr2)(3Vr - 1) = 8Tr.
  • The reduced equation contains no specific constants (a or b), proving that all gases at the same reduced temperature and pressure exhibit the same compressibility factor.
  • The theoretical van der Waals value of Zc = 0.375 frequently overestimates experimental observations for real gases, which typically range between 0.20 and 0.30.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Critical Temperature (Tc)
Tc = 8a / (27 · R · b)
Conditions: Derived by setting (∂P/∂V)T = 0 and (∂2P/∂V2)T = 0
Units: Tc in Kelvin (K)
Critical Pressure (Pc)
Pc = a / (27 · b2)
Conditions: Derived from the inflection point of the isothermal curve at Tc
Units: Pc in atmospheres (atm) or Pascals (Pa)
Critical Volume (Vc)
Vc = 3 · b
Conditions: Molar volume of the gas at the critical point
Units: Vc in Liters per mole (L·mol-1)
Critical Compressibility Factor (Zc)
Zc = Pc · Vc / (R · Tc) = 3 / 8 = 0.375
Conditions: Universal constant derived from van der Waals parameters
Units: Dimensionless
van der Waals Equation of State
(P + a · n2 / V2)(V - n · b) = n · R · T
Conditions: For 1 mole of a real gas, n = 1
Units: P in atm or Pa, V in L or m3, T in K, a in atm·L2·mol-2, b in L·mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Critical compressibility factor Zc is predicted to be a universal constant of 0.375 by the van der Waals equation.

Observation: Real gases show experimental Zc values ranging from 0.20 to 0.30 (e.g., hydrogen is ~0.30, water is ~0.23). — The van der Waals equation fails to account for temperature-dependent variations in molecular interactions and complex molecular geometries.

Exception Constant 'a' should remain strictly invariant across all temperature ranges for a given gas.

Observation: Experimental 'a' values derived from compressibility data vary slightly across different temperature ranges. — Intermolecular potential energy functions deviate from the simple inverse-square attractive assumptions built into the basic van der Waals model.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
The 27-8-3 Rule for van der Waals Constants
Tc = 8a/27Rb, Pc = a/27b2, Vc = 3b

Application: Remember the numerator/denominator coefficients as 8, 27, and 3 to rapidly derive any critical constant formula during calculations.

Calculating 'a' and 'b' from Tc and Pc
b = R·Tc / (8·Pc) and a = 27R2Tc2 / (64·Pc)

Application: Use these direct equations when critical volume Vc is missing from the problem statement.

Boyle Temperature and Critical Temperature Shortcut
TB = 27 / 8 · Tc = 3.375 · Tc

Application: Instantly calculate Boyle temperature (TB) by multiplying the given critical temperature (Tc) by 3.375.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The critical temperature Tc of a gas depends directly on constant a and inversely on constant b.
  • Critical pressure Pc is independent of the absolute gas constant R in its direct representation using constants a and b.
  • Critical volume Vc depends exclusively on the excluded volume constant b and equals 3b.
  • The compressibility factor at the critical point (Zc) is a universal constant equal to 3/8 (0.375) according to van der Waals theory.
  • Boyle temperature (TB) is always higher than the critical temperature (Tc) by a factor of 27/8.
  • The law of corresponding states utilizes reduced variables to establish behavior uniformity across diverse real gases.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the formulas for Tc and Pc by misplacing the exponent on constant b (remember Pc has b2 in the denominator).
  • Assuming Zc varies from gas to gas within the ideal van der Waals framework, whereas it is predicted to be a constant 0.375.
  • Forgetting that the unit of 'a' involves volume squared and pressure (atm·L2·mol-2 or Pa·m6·mol-2).
  • Using inconsistent units for the universal gas constant R when calculating critical constants.

⚡ Quick Revision: Relation of Critical Constants with van der Waals Constants

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Critical Point Phenomena

At the critical temperature and pressure, the meniscus separating liquid and vapor phases disappears, creating a supercritical fluid phase where densities of both phases become identical.

Mathematical Derivation from Inflection Point

Setting the first and second partial derivatives of pressure with respect to volume equal to zero at the critical isotherm allows exact evaluation of critical parameters.

Intermolecular Force Parameter (a)

Constant 'a' dictates the magnitude of cohesive forces holding molecules together, directly controlling the values of critical temperature and critical pressure.

Excluded Volume Parameter (b)

Constant 'b' represents four times the actual proper volume of gas molecules per mole, serving as the direct proportional factor for critical molar volume (Vc = 3b).

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Critical Temperature (Tc = 8a / (27Rb))
Temperature above which gas cannot be liquefied. SI Unit: Kelvin.
Critical Pressure (Pc = a / (27b2))
Minimum pressure required for liquefaction at Tc. SI Unit: Pascal.
Critical Volume (Vc = 3b)
Volume occupied by one mole at Tc and Pc. SI Unit: m3·mol-1.
Compressibility Factor (Z = PV / (nRT))
Ratio of actual molar volume to ideal molar volume. Dimensionless.
Boyle Temperature (TB = a / (Rb))
Temperature where real gas obeys ideal gas law over low-to-moderate pressures.
Inversion Temperature (Ti = 2a / (Rb))
Temperature below which a gas cools upon Joule-Thomson expansion.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Tc = 8a / (27Rb)
  • Pc = a / (27b2)
  • Vc = 3b
  • Zc = 3/8 = 0.375
  • TB = 27/8 Tc

📌 Quick Recall

  • Critical constants depend entirely on van der Waals constants 'a' and 'b' and the gas constant R.
  • The critical volume is independent of both temperature and pressure parameters, depending solely on 'b'.
  • Real gases fail to yield the exact theoretical Zc of 0.375, generally showing experimental values near 0.27.
  • The law of corresponding states expresses gas equations using dimensionless reduced variables.

⚖️ Key Differences

Boyle Temperature (TB) VS Critical Temperature (Tc)

TB is 3.375 times Tc; at TB the second virial coefficient is zero, whereas Tc is the upper limit of liquefaction.

Question 1 of 19

What is the correct expression for critical temperature (Tc) in terms of van der Waals constants 'a' and 'b'?

Explanation: Critical temperature is derived as Tc = 8a / (27Rb) from the van der Waals equation.

Question 2 of 19

The critical volume (Vc) of a van der Waals gas is equal to:

Explanation: Setting the second derivative of pressure with respect to volume to zero yields Vc = 3b.

Question 3 of 19

What is the theoretical value of the critical compressibility factor (Zc) derived from van der Waals equation?

Explanation: Zc = PcVc / (RTc) = (a / 27b2)(3b) / (R · 8a / 27Rb) = 3/8 = 0.375.

Question 4 of 19

Which critical constant is independent of the universal gas constant R?

Explanation: Pc = a / (27b2) and Vc = 3b do not contain the gas constant R.

Question 5 of 19

If the critical temperature of a gas is Tc, what is its Boyle temperature (TB) approximately equal to?

Explanation: Boyle temperature TB = a / (Rb) = (27/8) Tc = 3.375 Tc.

Question 6 of 19

The dimensions of van der Waals constant 'a' are:

Explanation: From the pressure correction term P = a·n2/V2, 'a' has units of pressure multiplied by volume squared per mole squared.

Question 7 of 19

The dimensions of van der Waals constant 'b' are:

Explanation: Constant 'b' represents excluded volume per mole, having units of L·mol-1 or m3·mol-1.

Question 8 of 19

What does the law of corresponding states state?

Explanation: The law of corresponding states asserts that real gases at corresponding states (same Tr, Pr, Vr) exhibit identical properties.

Question 9 of 19

Which relation correctly expresses van der Waals constant 'a' using critical temperature and critical pressure?

Explanation: Substituting b = RTc / (8Pc) into Pc = a / (27b2) yields a = 27R2Tc2 / (64Pc).

Question 10 of 19

In terms of critical constants, constant 'b' can be expressed as:

Explanation: From Tc = 8a / (27Rb) and Pc = a / (27b2), solving for b gives b = RTc / (8Pc).

Question 11 of 19

What is the reduced pressure (Pr) formula?

Explanation: Reduced property is defined as the ratio of the actual property to its critical value, hence Pr = P / Pc.

Question 12 of 19

For a real gas, as temperature increases far above Tc, its behavior approaches:

Explanation: At high temperatures, kinetic energy overshadows intermolecular attractive forces, causing real gases to approach ideal behavior.

Question 13 of 19

Why do real gases deviate from the theoretical Zc value of 0.375?

Explanation: The van der Waals model is an approximation that assumes constant molecular volume and simple spherical interaction potentials.

Question 14 of 19

The units of critical pressure (Pc) in the SI system are:

Explanation: Standard SI unit for pressure is Pascal (Pa) or N·m-2.

Question 15 of 19

If constant 'a' for a gas is zero, what can be inferred about the gas?

Explanation: Constant 'a' represents attractive forces; a = 0 signifies absence of intermolecular attractions (ideal gas character regarding forces).

Question 16 of 19

Which gas would you expect to have the highest critical temperature Tc?

Explanation: Water (H2O) has strong hydrogen bonding resulting in a very large 'a' parameter, leading to a high critical temperature (~647 K).

Question 17 of 19

What is the physical condition at the critical point on a P-V isotherm?

Explanation: The critical point is the point of inflection on the critical isotherm, satisfying both first and second derivative conditions equal to zero.

Question 18 of 19

The reduced temperature (Tr) is given by:

Explanation: Reduced temperature is the ratio of the absolute temperature to the critical temperature, Tr = T / Tc.

Question 19 of 19

If two different gases have the same reduced temperature and reduced pressure, they will also have:

Explanation: According to the law of corresponding states, gases at identical reduced states share the same compressibility factor Z.
