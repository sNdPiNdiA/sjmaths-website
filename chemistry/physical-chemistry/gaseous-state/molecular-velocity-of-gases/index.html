Physical Chemistry

Molecular velocity of gases

The kinetic molecular theory of gases relates macroscopic properties to microscopic molecular velocities via statistical distribution laws. Gas molecules exhibit a range of speeds categorized into root-mean-square, average, and most probable velocities.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Maxwell-Boltzmann Velocity Distribution

  • The fraction of molecules possessing a specific velocity depends on both temperature and molar mass.
  • The distribution curve broadens and shifts to higher velocity values as the absolute temperature increases.
  • The area under the Maxwell-Boltzmann curve represents the total number of gas molecules present in the system.
  • At higher temperatures, the proportion of molecules possessing higher kinetic energies increases significantly.

Types of Molecular Velocities

  • The Root-Mean-Square velocity (vrms) accounts for the square root of the mean of squared velocities and directly relates to absolute temperature.
  • The Most Probable velocity (vmp) represents the speed possessed by the maximum fraction of gas molecules at a given temperature.
  • The quantitative ratio of most probable, average, and root-mean-square velocities is vmp : vav : vrms = √2 : √(8/π) : √3 which simplifies to 1 : 1.128 : 1.224.
  • Root-mean-square velocity can also be expressed using pressure P and density d via the relation vrms = √(3P/d).

Dependence on Temperature and Molar Mass

  • Molecular velocities are inversely proportional to the square root of the molar mass (M) of the gas.
  • Lighter gases such as H2 and He possess significantly higher molecular velocities than heavier gases like CO2 and SO2 at the same temperature.
  • Kinetic energy depends exclusively on temperature, meaning different gases at the identical temperature possess the same average translational kinetic energy.
  • Absolute zero (0 K) corresponds to the theoretical state where all molecular translational motion ceases completely.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Average Velocity
vav = √(8RT / πM)
Conditions: Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution integration
Units: R in J K-1 mol-1, T in K, M in kg mol-1, v in m s-1
Most Probable Velocity
vmp = √(2RT / M)
Conditions: Velocity corresponding to maximum fraction of molecules
Units: R in J K-1 mol-1, T in K, M in kg mol-1, v in m s-1
Kinetic Energy of Translation
Ek = (3/2)RT per mole = (3/2)kBT per molecule
Conditions: Applies to ideal monoatomic gases
Units: E in Joules, R in J K-1 mol-1, kB in J K-1
Graham's Law of Effusion
r1 / r2 = √(M2 / M1) = √(d2 / d1)
Conditions: Constant pressure and temperature conditions
Units: r in mol s-1 or volume/time, M in g mol-1
Root Mean Square Velocity
vrms = √(3RT / M) = √(3P / d)
Conditions: Valid for ideal gases at absolute temperature T
Units: R in J K-1 mol-1, T in K, M in kg mol-1, v in m s-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception RMS velocity depends on molar mass exclusively for ideal gases

Observation: Real gases deviate under high pressure and low temperature — Intermolecular attractive forces and finite molecular volume alter effective pressure and volume terms.

Exception Average kinetic energy is strictly a function of temperature

Observation: Polyatomic gases distribute energy across vibrational and rotational degrees of freedom — Translational kinetic energy remains (3/2)RT, but total internal energy includes internal molecular modes.

Velocity Ratio Mnemonic
RAM : Average : RMS = √2 : √8/π : √3

Application: Remember alphabetical order of velocities matches numerical magnitude: MP (lowest) < Average < RMS (highest).

Temperature Scaling Shortcut
v2 / v1 = √(T2 / T1)

Application: Quickly find the factor by which velocity changes when absolute temperature is scaled by a factor.

Graham's Law Molecular Mass Relation
Rate ∝ 1 / √M

Application: Directly compare effusion rates or diffusion times by inverting the square root of molecular mass ratios.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The ratio of vmp : vav : vrms is always 1 : 1.128 : 1.224.
  • Root mean square velocity is directly proportional to the square root of absolute temperature in Kelvin.
  • At absolute zero, molecular motion ceases and vrms becomes zero.
  • Different gases at the same temperature have the same average kinetic energy per mole.
  • Graham's law of effusion is inversely proportional to the square root of gas density or molar mass.
  • RMS velocity has units of meters per second (m s-1) when SI units are utilized.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Using Celsius temperature instead of Kelvin absolute temperature in velocity calculations.
  • Confusing molar mass in grams with kilograms when calculating RMS velocity using SI value of R (8.314 J K-1 mol-1).
  • Assuming average velocity is equal to the root mean square velocity.
  • Forgetting that kinetic energy depends only on temperature, not on the nature of the gas.

⚡ Quick Revision: Molecular velocity of gases

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Kinetic Gas Equation

Relates macroscopic pressure and volume to molecular mass and mean square speed via PV = (1/3)MNvrms2.

Maxwell Distribution Curves

Graphical representation showing the fraction of molecules corresponding to various speeds at distinct thermal energies.

Translational Kinetic Energy

Determined solely by temperature, equal to (3/2)RT per mole for all ideal gases.

Effusion and Diffusion

Governed by molecular velocities where lighter molecules effuse faster inversely proportional to square root of molar mass.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Root Mean Square Velocity (vrms)
Square root of the arithmetic mean of squared molecular speeds in m s-1.
Most Probable Velocity (vmp)
Speed possessed by the maximum fraction of gas molecules at temperature T.
Average Velocity (vav)
Arithmetic mean of the speeds of all molecules in a gas sample.
Effusion (Rate of escape)
Process where gas molecules escape through a tiny orifice into an evacuated chamber.
Diffusion (Intermixing)
Spontaneous intermixing of gases due to molecular motion and velocity.
Maxwell-Boltzmann Law (f(v) dv)
Statistical distribution law describing molecular speeds in an ideal gas.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • vrms = √(3RT / M)
  • vav = √(8RT / πM)
  • vmp = √(2RT / M)
  • Ratio: vmp : vav : vrms = 1 : 1.128 : 1.224
  • Kinetic Energy per mole = (3/2)RT

📌 Quick Recall

  • Molecular velocity distributions depend on temperature and molar mass.
  • RMS velocity is always greater than average velocity, which is greater than most probable velocity.
  • Temperature is a direct measure of average translational kinetic energy.
  • Graham's law relates effusion rates inversely to square root of molar masses.

⚖️ Key Differences

vrms VS vmp

vrms accounts for root mean square energy calculations, whereas vmp is the peak of the Maxwell distribution curve.

Question 11 of 19

If molar mass of a gas is quadrupled at constant temperature, its RMS velocity will:

Explanation: vrms ∝ 1 / √M. When M is multiplied by 4, velocity is divided by √4 = 2.

Question 13 of 19

Which velocity is mathematically the largest among the three molecular velocities?

Explanation: vrms has a factor of √3 (~1.732), compared to √2 (~1.414) for vmp and √(8/π) (~1.596).

Explanation: Low pressure and high temperature minimize intermolecular interactions and molecular volume effects.

Question 1 of 10
The root mean square velocity of SO2 at a certain temperature is equal to the most probable velocity of oxygen (O2) at 300 K. What is the temperature of SO2?

Solution: vrms(SO2) = vmp(O2). √(3RT1 / 64) = √(2R(300) / 32). Solving yields T1 = 600 K.

Question 2 of 10
What is the average translational kinetic energy of a single molecule of an ideal gas at temperature T?

Solution: Per molecule kinetic energy is (3/2)kBT where kB is the Boltzmann constant.

Question 3 of 10
At what temperature will the root mean square velocity of hydrogen be double its value at 27 °C?

Solution: Initial T = 300 K. To double vrms, temperature must increase by a factor of 22 = 4. 4 × 300 = 1200 K.

Question 4 of 10
The ratio of rates of diffusion of two gases A and B with molar masses 16 and 64 g mol-1 respectively is:

Solution: rA / rB = √(MB / MA) = √(64 / 16) = √4 = 2 : 1.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following expressions correctly represents most probable speed?

Solution: Most probable speed is √(2RT / M).

Question 6 of 10
In Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution, as temperature increases, the fraction of molecules with higher speeds:

Solution: Higher temperature increases thermal energy, shifting the distribution curve rightward and increasing the fraction of faster molecules.

Question 7 of 10
The root-mean-square velocity of an ideal gas molecule depends upon:

Solution: vrms = √(3RT / M), showing direct dependence on temperature and inverse dependence on molar mass.

Question 8 of 10
If gas molecules have a root mean square velocity of 500 m s-1, their average velocity will be approximately:

Solution: vav = vrms / 1.224 × 1.128 = 500 × (1.128 / 1.224) ≈ 460 m s-1 or calculated via exact ratio.

Question 9 of 10
Which property is independent of the molecular mass of the gas at a given temperature?

Solution: Average kinetic energy per mole is (3/2)RT, which is independent of molar mass.

Question 10 of 10
The root-mean-square velocity of molecules of a gas is V. If the temperature is doubled and nitrogen gas dissociates into atomic nitrogen, the new RMS velocity becomes:

Solution: T becomes 2T. Molar mass M becomes M/2. vnew = √(3R(2T)/(M/2)) = √(4 × 3RT/M) = 2√2 V.
