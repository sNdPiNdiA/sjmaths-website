Critical Constants

Critical constants define the specific thermodynamic state parameters where liquid and vapor phases of a substance become identical and indistinguishable. These parameters include critical temperature (Tc), critical pressure (Pc), and critical volume (Vc), which are derived directly from the real gas equations of state.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Derivation from Van der Waals Equation

  • The Van der Waals equation is given by (P + a/V2)(V - b) = RT for one mole of gas.
  • At the critical point, the isotherm shows an inflection point where the first and second derivatives of pressure with respect to volume equal zero: (∂P/∂V)T = 0 and (∂2P/∂V2)T = 0.
  • Solving these simultaneous partial differential equations yields the expressions for Tc, Pc, and Vc in terms of van der Waals constants a and b.

Reduced Equation of State

  • Reduced variables are defined as dimensionless ratios of the actual property to its critical value: Tr = T/Tc, Pr = P/Pc, and Vr = V/Vc.
  • Substituting these variables into the Van der Waals equation yields the law of corresponding states: (Pr + 3/Vr2)(3Vr - 1) = 8Tr.
  • Gases at the same reduced temperature and reduced pressure occupy the same reduced volume and exhibit the same deviation from ideal behavior.

Significance of Van der Waals Constants

  • Constant a accounts for the magnitude of intermolecular attractive forces and has units of atm L2 mol-2.
  • Constant b represents the excluded volume or effective incompressible volume of the gas molecules per mole, with units of L mol-1.
  • The ratio of critical temperature to critical pressure is directly proportional to b/a, establishing a link between molecular size, attraction, and liquefaction difficulty.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Van der Waals compressibility factor Zc is predicted to be a constant 0.375 for all gases.

Observation: Experimental Zc values for real gases typically range between 0.20 and 0.30. — The simple Van der Waals model fails to accurately predict molecular behavior and repulsive-attractive interactions precisely at high densities near the critical point.

Exception Boyle temperature is always lower than critical temperature.

Observation: For certain polar gases and complex polyatomic molecules, TB can be significantly higher than Tc. — Strong directional intermolecular forces and dipole-dipole interactions alter the balance between attractive and repulsive forces.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The critical temperature (Tc) of a gas is given by 8a / (27Rb).
  • The critical pressure (Pc) of a gas is given by a / (27b2).
  • The critical volume (Vc) of a gas is equal to 3b.
  • The compressibility factor at the critical point (Zc) is 3/8 (0.375) according to Van der Waals equation.
  • The unit of van der Waals constant 'a' is atm L2 mol-2 or N m4 mol-2.
  • The unit of van der Waals constant 'b' is L mol-1 or m3 mol-1.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the units of 'a' and 'b'; remember 'a' has pressure-volume squared dimensions per mole squared, while 'b' is simply volume per mole.
  • Assuming Zc is universally 0.375 experimentally; real gases show deviations due to non-ideal molecular interactions.
  • Applying ideal gas laws near the critical region where P-V-T behavior deviates maximally from ideality.
  • Forgetting that critical volume Vc is for one mole of gas (3b), not individual molecules.

⚡ Quick Revision: Critical Constants

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Critical Phenomenon

At the critical temperature, the meniscus between liquid and vapor disappears, and the surface tension becomes zero.

Van der Waals Parameters

Constants 'a' and 'b' can be evaluated experimentally using critical constants Tc and Pc.

Law of Corresponding States

Gases under identical reduced states share identical compressibility factors regardless of their chemical identity.

Liquefaction of Gases

Gases with higher critical temperatures (such as NH3 and CO2) are easier to liquefy than gases with low Tc (such as H2 and He).

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Critical Temperature (Tc)
Temperature above which a gas cannot be liquefied. Unit: Kelvin (K).
Critical Pressure (Pc)
Minimum pressure required to liquefy a gas at its critical temperature. Unit: atm.
Critical Volume (Vc)
Volume occupied by one mole of a substance at its critical temperature and pressure. Unit: L mol-1.
Boyle Temperature (TB)
Temperature at which real gas obeys PV = RT over a pressure range. Unit: Kelvin (K).
Inversion Temperature (Ti)
Temperature at which Joule-Thomson coefficient is zero. Unit: Kelvin (K).
Reduced Volume (Vr)
Ratio of actual volume to critical volume (V/Vc). Dimensionless.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Tc = 8a / (27Rb)
  • Pc = a / (27b2)
  • Vc = 3b
  • Zc = 3/8 = 0.375
  • TB = (27/8) Tc

📌 Quick Recall

  • Critical constants depend entirely on van der Waals constants 'a' and 'b'.
  • Zc is a constant 0.375 for all gases obeying the Van der Waals equation.
  • Higher intermolecular attraction ('a') leads to higher critical temperature and easier liquefaction.
  • Law of corresponding states allows generalized compressibility behavior for all real gases.

⚖️ Key Differences

Critical Temperature (Tc) VS Boyle Temperature (TB)

Tc is the limit for liquefaction, whereas TB is the temperature where real gas acts ideally over a moderate pressure range.

What is the expression for critical temperature (Tc) according to the Van der Waals equation?

Explanation: Critical temperature is derived from the Van der Waals equation by setting the first and second pressure derivatives with respect to volume to zero, yielding Tc = 8a / (27Rb).

Question 2 of 19

What is the value of the critical compressibility factor (Zc) derived from the Van der Waals equation?

Explanation: Zc = PcVc / (RTc) = [a/(27b2) 3b] / [R 8a/(27Rb)] = 3/8 = 0.375.

Question 3 of 19

What is the critical volume (Vc) for one mole of a Van der Waals gas in terms of constant 'b'?

Explanation: Solving the inflection point equations for volume yields Vc = 3b for one mole of gas.

Question 4 of 19

Which gas is expected to have the highest critical temperature?

Explanation: Water (H2O) exhibits extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding, resulting in a very large 'a' constant and consequently a high critical temperature.

Question 5 of 19

What are the units of the Van der Waals constant 'a'?

Explanation: Constant 'a' is added to pressure term (P + a/V2), so its units are atm * (L/mol)2 = atm L2 mol-2.

Question 6 of 19

What are the units of the Van der Waals constant 'b'?

Explanation: Constant 'b' is subtracted from volume (V - b), representing molar excluded volume with units of L mol-1.

Question 7 of 19

The ratio TB / Tc (Boyle temperature to critical temperature) according to Van der Waals theory is:

Explanation: TB = a / (Rb) and Tc = 8a / (27Rb). Therefore, TB / Tc = 27 / 8 = 3.375.

Question 8 of 19

At the critical point, the liquid and vapor phases:

Explanation: At the critical point, liquid and vapor densities become identical, and the meniscus disappears, making the phases indistinguishable.

Question 9 of 19

Which of the following represents the law of corresponding states?

Explanation: Substituting reduced variables into the Van der Waals equation yields the universal law of corresponding states: (Pr + 3/Vr2)(3Vr - 1) = 8Tr.

Question 10 of 19

If the critical temperature of a gas is Tc, above this temperature the gas:

Explanation: Above the critical temperature, thermal kinetic energy overcomes intermolecular attractions, making liquefaction impossible regardless of applied pressure.

Question 11 of 19

What is the relation between critical pressure (Pc) and Van der Waals constants 'a' and 'b'?

Explanation: Critical pressure is derived as Pc = a / (27b2).

Question 12 of 19

Reduced temperature (Tr) is defined as:

Explanation: Reduced temperature is the ratio of the absolute temperature to the critical temperature (T / Tc).

Question 13 of 19

Which gas is easiest to liquefy among the following based on typical intermolecular forces?

Explanation: Sulfur dioxide (SO2) has strong dipole-dipole interactions and a large 'a' value, giving it the highest critical temperature and making it the easiest to liquefy.

Question 14 of 19

The compressibility factor Z is defined as:

Explanation: Compressibility factor Z is the ratio of actual molar volume of a gas to the molar volume of an ideal gas at the same temperature and pressure: Z = PV / nRT.

Question 15 of 19

At the critical point, the slope of the PV isotherm (∂P/∂V)T is:

Explanation: The critical point is an inflection point on the critical isotherm where both the first and second derivatives of pressure with respect to volume are zero.

Question 16 of 19

What is the relationship between the inversion temperature (Ti) and critical temperature (Tc) in Van der Waals gases?

Explanation: Since Ti = 2 TB and TB = 3.375 Tc, the inversion temperature is 2 * 3.375 Tc = 6.75 Tc.

Question 17 of 19

If 'b' is the excluded volume per mole, what is the actual volume of the gas molecules themselves per mole?

Explanation: According to kinetic theory and Van der Waals derivation, the excluded volume b is four times the actual volume of the molecules (b = 4Vm), so actual molecular volume is b / 4.

Question 18 of 19

Gases obey the law of corresponding states when they have the same:

Explanation: The law of corresponding states states that all gases have the same compressibility factor when compared at the same reduced temperature and reduced pressure.

Question 19 of 19

Which of the following constants is a direct measure of molecular size in the Van der Waals equation?

Explanation: Van der Waals constant 'b' represents the excluded volume, which is directly proportional to the actual volume occupied by the gas molecules.

Question 1 of 10
What is the critical volume Vc in terms of constant 'b' for 2 moles of a Van der Waals gas?

Solution: Critical volume for 1 mole is Vc = 3b. For 2 moles, it is 2 * 3b = 6b.

Question 2 of 10
If the critical pressure Pc of a gas is 40 atm, what is the value of constant 'a' if 'b' = 0.05 L mol-1?

Solution: Pc = a / (27b2) ⇒ a = Pc 27b2 = 40 27 (0.05)2 = 40 27 * 0.0025 = 2.7 atm L2 mol-2.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following relations correctly links critical constants with Van der Waals constant 'b'?

Solution: Critical volume Vc equals 3b per mole.

Question 4 of 10
The reduced pressure Pr of a gas at 50 atm given its critical pressure is 25 atm equals:

Solution: Pr = P / Pc = 50 / 25 = 2.0.

Question 5 of 10
What is the physical state of matter when temperature exceeds Tc and pressure exceeds Pc?

Solution: Above Tc and Pc, the substance exists as a supercritical fluid possessing properties of both gas and liquid without phase boundaries.

Question 6 of 10
If constants 'a' and 'b' for a gas are 4.0 atm L2 mol-2 and 0.04 L mol-1 respectively, what is Tc (take R = 0.0821 atm L mol-1 K-1)?

Solution: Tc = 8a / (27Rb) = (8 4.0) / (27 0.0821 * 0.04) = 32 / 0.088668 ≈ 360-475 K range calculation gives ~475 K.

Question 7 of 10
Why do real gases deviate from the ideal gas law at high pressures?

Solution: At high pressures, molecules are forced close together, making the excluded volume significant and repulsive forces prominent, causing Z > 1.

Question 8 of 10
The ratio of Boyle temperature to inversion temperature for a Van der Waals gas is:

Solution: TB = 3.375 Tc and Ti = 6.75 Tc. Therefore, TB / Ti = 3.375 / 6.75 = 1 / 2.

Question 9 of 10
In terms of reduced variables, what is the value of Vr when actual volume equals critical volume?

Solution: Vr = V / Vc. When V = Vc, Vr = 1.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following gases has the lowest critical temperature?

Solution: Helium (He) has extremely weak London dispersion forces and a very small 'a' constant, leading to the lowest critical temperature (~5.2 K).
