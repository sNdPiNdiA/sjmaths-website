Mean Free Path

Mean free path represents the average distance traveled by a gas molecule between successive collisions with other molecules. It is inversely proportional to the pressure of the gas and the collision cross-section area.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Molecular Parameters and Collision Cross-Section

  • The collision diameter (d) is defined as the closest distance between the centers of two colliding molecules.
  • The collision cross-section (σ) equals πd2, representing the effective area intercepted by a molecule.
  • Mean free path increases directly with temperature and decreases directly with pressure at constant volume.

Dependence on Pressure, Temperature, and Density

  • Since number density n is proportional to pressure p, λ is inversely proportional to p at constant temperature.
  • At constant pressure, λ is directly proportional to absolute temperature T due to increased molar volume.
  • Molecules with larger molecular radii have significantly smaller mean free path values under identical physical conditions.

Relationship with Viscosity and Thermal Conductivity

  • Coefficient of viscosity (η) is directly related to mean free path via the relation η = 13 ρ cav λ.
  • Thermal conductivity of a gas depends directly on mean free path and average molecular speed.
  • Mean free path calculations help bridge microscopic molecular properties with macroscopic transport phenomena.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Mean free path approaches infinity as pressure approaches absolute zero

Observation: Vacuum chambers with finite dimensions — Container walls begin to restrict molecular travel, making the mean free path comparable to or larger than vessel dimensions (Knudsen gas regime).

Exception Molecular diameters remain constant at all thermal energies

Observation: High-temperature plasmas or extreme shock waves — Molecules dissociate into atoms or ions, altering the effective collision cross-section and changing the mean free path.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Mean free path formula includes the factor &sqrt;2 in the denominator.
  • Viscosity of gases is independent of pressure because λ increases as pressure decreases, maintaining product constancy.
  • At standard temperature and pressure (STP), the mean free path of air molecules is approximately 60 to 100 nm.
  • Mean free path increases when volume increases at constant temperature.
  • Collision frequency is directly proportional to average molecular speed and inversely proportional to mean free path.
  • Unit of mean free path in SI is the meter (m).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing mean free path with average distance between stationary molecules; mean free path accounts for moving target molecules.
  • Omitting the &sqrt;2 factor in the denominator of the Maxwell mean free path expression.
  • Assuming gas viscosity depends on pressure due to density changes, ignoring the counter-effect of mean free path.
  • Forgetting to convert temperatures to Kelvin when applying proportionalities.

⚡ Quick Revision: Mean Free Path

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition of Mean Free Path

Mean free path (λ) is the average distance traversed by a molecule between two successive collisions. It depends on molecular diameter and number density.

Pressure and Temperature Dependence

Exhibits inverse proportionality with pressure and direct proportionality with temperature. Higher pressures reduce free travel distance.

Transport Properties

Connects microscopic molecular collisions to macroscopic transport coefficients like viscosity and thermal conductivity.

Collision Parameters

Involves collision cross-section (πd2) and root-mean-square or average speeds to quantify collision frequencies.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Mean Free Path (λ)
Average distance between molecular collisions in meters.
Collision Diameter (d)
Closest distance of approach between centers of two colliding molecules.
Collision Cross-Section (σ)
Effective area πd2 presented by a molecule for collision.
Number Density (n)
Number of molecules per unit volume (m-3).
Collision Frequency (z)
Number of collisions experienced by a single molecule per second.
Total Collision Frequency (Z11)
Total binary collisions per unit volume per second.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • λ = 1 / (&sqrt;2 π n d2)
  • λ ∝ T / p
  • Viscosity of gases is independent of pressure
  • Collision frequency z = cav / λ
  • Unit of λ is meters (m)

📌 Quick Recall

  • Mean free path measures intermolecular spacing dynamics.
  • Inversely proportional to pressure and molecular diameter squared.
  • Directly proportional to temperature at constant pressure.
  • Vital parameter linking kinetic theory of gases with transport phenomena.

⚖️ Key Differences

Mean Free Path VS Average Distance Between Molecules

Mean free path is distance traveled between actual collisions, whereas average distance depends solely on cube root of number density.

Question 1 of 19

What is the dependence of mean free path (λ) on pressure (p) at constant temperature?

Explanation: Mean free path is inversely proportional to pressure because higher pressure increases number density and collision frequency.

Question 2 of 19

Which factor appears in the denominator of the exact Maxwell formula for mean free path?

Explanation: The Maxwell-Boltzmann derivation introduces a factor of &sqrt;2 in the denominator to account for the relative velocity distribution of colliding molecules.

Question 3 of 19

How does the mean free path of a gas change when the temperature is doubled at constant pressure?

Explanation: At constant pressure, mean free path is directly proportional to absolute temperature (λ ∝ T), so doubling T doubles λ.

Question 4 of 19

What happens to the mean free path if the collision diameter of molecules is doubled?

Explanation: Since λ is inversely proportional to d2, doubling d reduces the mean free path by a factor of 22 = 4.

Question 5 of 19

The unit of mean free path in the SI system is:

Explanation: Mean free path is a distance, so its SI unit is the meter (m).

Question 6 of 19

Viscosity of a gas is found to be independent of:

Explanation: Gas viscosity is independent of pressure because the increase in mean free path exactly offsets the decrease in density.

Question 7 of 19

What is the expression for collision frequency (z) of a single molecule?

Explanation: Collision frequency z is the average speed divided by the mean free path (z = cav / λ).

Question 8 of 19

If the pressure of a gas is decreased by a factor of 3 at constant temperature, the mean free path:

Explanation: Mean free path is inversely proportional to pressure; reducing pressure by 3 increases λ by 3.

Question 9 of 19

Collision cross-section is represented by which mathematical expression?

Explanation: Collision cross-section σ is the effective target area equal to πd2.

Question 10 of 19

Which of the following conditions promotes a larger mean free path?

Explanation: Low pressure (low density) and high temperature increase the average distance between collisions.

Question 11 of 19

In the kinetic theory of gases, the mean free path is directly proportional to:

Explanation: At constant pressure, λ ∝ T. It is inversely proportional to number density and collision diameter squared.

Question 12 of 19

If number density 'n' of a gas is doubled, the mean free path becomes:

Explanation: Mean free path is inversely proportional to number density (n), so doubling n halves λ.

Question 13 of 19

What is the dimension of the collision cross-section (σ)?

Explanation: Collision cross-section has units of area (m2), corresponding to dimension [L2].

Question 14 of 19

Which transport property of gases is directly proportional to mean free path?

Explanation: Both gas viscosity and thermal conductivity are directly proportional to the mean free path of gas molecules.

Question 15 of 19

When does the classical mean free path formula fail?

Explanation: In ultra-high vacuums (Knudsen regime), molecules collide with container walls more often than with each other.

Question 16 of 19

The average speed of gas molecules (cav) used in mean free path calculations is proportional to:

Explanation: According to kinetic theory, average molecular speed is proportional to the square root of absolute temperature (&sqrt;T).

Question 17 of 19

If two different gases have the same pressure and temperature, the gas with the larger molecular diameter will have:

Explanation: Larger molecular diameter increases collision cross-section, resulting in a smaller mean free path.

Question 18 of 19

The term 'n' in the mean free path equation (λ = 1 / (&sqrt;2 π n d2)) stands for:

Explanation: In microscopic kinetic theory equations, 'n' denotes the number density of molecules per unit volume.

Question 19 of 19

Which statement is correct regarding total collision frequency (Z11)?

Explanation: Total binary collision frequency Z11 is proportional to the square of number density (n2).

Solution: Mean free path is inversely proportional to pressure. Tripling the pressure reduces λ to 1/3 of its initial value.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following parameters does NOT affect the mean free path of an ideal gas?

Solution: Container volume alone does not determine λ; number density (molecules per unit volume) and molecular size dictate the mean free path.

Question 3 of 10
What is the physical significance of the collision diameter (d)?

Solution: Collision diameter is defined as the closest distance of approach between the centers of two colliding molecules.

Question 4 of 10
If the absolute temperature of a gas is increased four times at constant volume, what happens to the mean free path?

Solution: At constant volume, number density 'n' is constant. Since λ depends only on n and d in the standard formula (λ = 1 / (&sqrt;2 π n d2)), it remains unchanged.

Question 5 of 10
The dimensions of mean free path are equivalent to:

Solution: Mean free path is a length, so its dimension is [L].

Question 6 of 10
For a gas, if both pressure and absolute temperature are doubled simultaneously, the mean free path:

Solution: Since λ ∝ T/p, doubling both T and p leaves their ratio unchanged, so the mean free path remains constant.

Question 7 of 10
Collision frequency (z) has which of the following units?

Solution: Collision frequency measures the number of collisions per unit time for a single molecule, with SI units of s-1.

Question 8 of 10
In a Knudsen gas regime:

Solution: In the Knudsen regime, mean free path exceeds container dimensions, so molecules collide more frequently with walls than with each other.

Question 9 of 10
How is thermal conductivity (K) of a gas related to mean free path (λ)?

Solution: Thermal conductivity is directly proportional to mean free path, average molecular speed, and heat capacity.
