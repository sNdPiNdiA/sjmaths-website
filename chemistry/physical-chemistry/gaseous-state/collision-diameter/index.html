Collision Diameter

Collision diameter is defined as the closest distance between the centers of two colliding gas molecules when they undergo a binary collision. It is denoted by the symbol σ and is directly related to the actual physical size of the gas molecules.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Geometric Definition and Molecular Dimensions

  • The collision diameter (σ) represents the minimum separation distance between molecular centers at the instant of impact.
  • For spherical molecules of radius r, the collision diameter is exactly twice the molecular radius: σ = 2r.
  • The value of σ is typically on the order of a few angstroms (Å) for common atmospheric gases.
  • Molecules are treated as rigid elastic spheres in the elementary kinetic theory of gases when defining σ.

Relation to Transport Properties and van der Waals Constant

  • The van der Waals excluded volume 'b' per mole is four times the actual total volume of the molecules: b = 4Vm.
  • Substituting molecular volume yields b = (2/3) π σ3 NA, linking macroscopic non-ideality to microscopic collision diameter.
  • The mean free path (λ) is inversely proportional to the collision cross-section (πσ2) and number density.
  • Viscosity and thermal conductivity coefficients of gases depend directly on the collision diameter through kinetic theory derivations.

Temperature Dependence and Experimental Determination

  • While treated as a constant for rigid spheres, experimental collision diameters show slight decreases at higher temperatures due to molecular interpenetration during energetic collisions.
  • Collision diameters are experimentally determined from viscosity measurements or van der Waals 'b' parameters derived from critical constants.
  • Gases with larger electron clouds or higher molecular weights generally exhibit larger collision diameters.
  • The collision frequency increases quadratically with an increase in collision diameter.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Collision diameter remains constant across all temperature ranges.

Observation: Real gas collision diameters decrease slightly at elevated temperatures. — Higher kinetic energy at elevated temperatures allows molecules to approach closer before repulsive forces turn them away, effectively reducing the dynamic σ.

Exception Molecules behave strictly as rigid spheres with fixed collision diameters.

Observation: Polar molecules exhibit temperature-dependent effective collision diameters. — Anisotropic electrostatic dipole-dipole interactions alter the effective collision cross-section depending on orientation and kinetic energy.

σ = 2r

Excluded Volume Shortcut
b = 4 × (Volume of 1 mole of molecules)

Mean Free Path Proportionality
λ ∝ 1σ^2

Application: If the collision diameter is doubled, the mean free path decreases by a factor of four.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Collision diameter σ has dimensions of length, commonly expressed in meters or picometers.
  • The van der Waals constant 'b' is directly proportional to σ3.
  • Collision cross-section σc equals πσ2.
  • Mean free path is inversely proportional to the square of the collision diameter (σ2).
  • In a gas mixture, the collision diameter between dissimilar molecules A and B is given by σAB = (σA + σB)/2.
  • Collision frequency is directly proportional to the square of the collision diameter.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing collision diameter (σ) with molecular radius (r); remember σ = 2r.
  • Forgetting to cube the collision diameter when calculating van der Waals constant 'b'.
  • Assuming collision diameter is identical to bond length; collision diameter is a non-bonded hard-sphere interaction parameter.
  • Neglecting units conversion when converting angstroms to meters in transport property equations.

⚡ Quick Revision: Collision Diameter

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition of Collision Diameter

Collision diameter (σ) is the distance between centers of two colliding spherical molecules at the point of closest approach.

Relation with van der Waals Constant

The excluded volume parameter 'b' in the van der Waals equation is directly proportional to the cube of the collision diameter.

Transport Properties Impact

Collision diameter dictates collision frequency and mean free path, thereby controlling gas viscosity and diffusion coefficients.

Temperature Variation

Although treated as a constant for ideal rigid spheres, real molecular softness causes σ to decrease slightly at higher temperatures.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Collision Diameter (σ)
Distance of closest approach between molecular centers.
Collision Cross-Section (πσ2)
Effective area presented by a molecule for collision.
Mean Free Path (λ)
Average distance traveled by a molecule between two successive collisions.
Collision Frequency (Z)
Number of collisions taking place per unit time per unit volume.
Excluded Volume (b)
Volume unavailable to free translational motion of gas molecules per mole.
Molecular Radius (r)
Half of the collision diameter for spherical molecules.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • σ = 2r for spherical molecules
  • b = (2/3) π σ3 NA
  • λ = 1 / (√2 π σ2 n)
  • Z11 ∝ σ2
  • σAB = (σA + σB) / 2 for binary mixtures

📌 Quick Recall

  • Collision diameter represents molecular spatial extension in kinetic theory.
  • Links microscopic molecular size to macroscopic non-ideal gas behavior ('b').
  • Inversely affects mean free path quadratically.
  • Essential parameter in calculating gas viscosity and thermal conductivity.

⚖️ Key Differences

Collision Diameter (σ) VS Bond Length

Collision diameter applies to non-bonded van der Waals interactions between independent molecules, whereas bond length is the internuclear distance within a bonded molecule.

Question 1 of 18

What is the relationship between collision diameter (σ) and molecular radius (r) for a spherical gas molecule?

Explanation: Collision diameter is the distance between centers of two touching spherical molecules, which equals twice the radius (σ = 2r).

Question 2 of 18

The unit of collision diameter (σ) is:

Explanation: Collision diameter represents a distance of closest approach, so its SI unit is the meter (m).

Question 3 of 18

How does the mean free path (λ) vary with collision diameter (σ)?

Explanation: Mean free path is inversely proportional to the collision cross-section (πσ2), hence λ ∝ 1 / σ2.

Question 4 of 18

The collision cross-section (σc) of a gas molecule with collision diameter σ is given by:

Explanation: Collision cross-section is the effective area swept out by a molecule, represented as a circle of radius σ, giving an area of πσ2.

Question 5 of 18

The van der Waals constant 'b' is related to collision diameter σ by:

Explanation: Excluded volume b = 4 × (4/3 π r3 NA). Substituting r = σ/2 yields b = (2/3) π σ3 NA.

Question 6 of 18

For a binary mixture of two different gases A and B, the collision diameter σAB is:

Explanation: The collision diameter between unlike molecules is the arithmetic mean of their individual collision diameters: σAB = (σA + σB) / 2.

Question 7 of 18

If the collision diameter of a gas is doubled, its collision frequency (under identical conditions) will:

Explanation: Collision frequency is directly proportional to σ2. Doubling σ increases the collision frequency by 22 = 4 times.

Question 8 of 18

What effect does increasing temperature have on the effective collision diameter of real gas molecules?

Explanation: At higher temperatures, molecules possess greater kinetic energy, allowing them to overcome repulsive forces slightly and approach closer, reducing effective σ.

Question 9 of 18

Which property of gases does NOT directly depend on the collision diameter?

Explanation: Ideal gas behavior at 0 K or general ideal pressure relations are derived without molecular volume or collision diameter considerations.

Question 10 of 18

If the radius of a gas molecule is 2 Å, its collision diameter is:

Explanation: Collision diameter σ = 2r = 2 × 2 Å = 4 Å.

Question 11 of 18

In the kinetic theory of gases, molecules are assumed to be:

Explanation: Elementary kinetic theory treats gas molecules as rigid, elastic spheres possessing a finite collision diameter.

Question 12 of 18

The dimension of collision cross-section is:

Explanation: Collision cross-section is an area (πσ2), so its dimensional formula is [L2].

Question 13 of 18

Which of the following units is appropriate for collision frequency Z11?

Explanation: Collision frequency Z11 measures the number of collisions per unit volume per unit time, having units of m-3 s-1.

Question 14 of 18

If the mean free path of a gas is doubled at constant pressure and temperature, the collision diameter must have:

Explanation: Since λ ∝ 1 / σ2, doubling λ requires σ to decrease by a factor of √2.

Question 15 of 18

The van der Waals constant 'b' has units of:

Explanation: The parameter 'b' represents excluded volume per mole, expressed in L mol-1 or m3 mol-1.

Question 16 of 18

When calculating transport properties, the collision diameter is obtained primarily from experimental measurements of:

Explanation: Gas viscosity coefficients depend directly on molecular mass and collision diameter, making viscosity measurements the primary source for determining σ.

Question 17 of 18

Which gas would you expect to have the largest collision diameter?

Explanation: Xenon (Xe) has the largest atomic radius and electron cloud among the given noble gases, resulting in the largest collision diameter.

Question 18 of 18

If collision diameter is expressed in picometers (pm), 1 pm is equivalent to:

Explanation: 1 picometer (pm) is defined as 10-12 meters.

Calculate the collision cross-section if the collision diameter is 400 pm.

Solution: σ = 400 pm = 4 × 10-10 m. Area = π × (4 × 10-10)2 = 3.1416 × 1.6 × 10-19 = 5.03 × 10-19 m2.

Solution: Number density n is proportional to pressure; since λ = 1 / (√2 π σ2 n), λ is inversely proportional to pressure.

Solution: Collision frequency per unit volume per unit time has spatial units of inverse volume (cm-3) and inverse time (s-1).
