Physical Chemistry

Gaseous State

The gaseous state is characterized by high kinetic energy and negligible intermolecular forces compared to solids and liquids. Gases conform to the ideal gas law under conditions of high temperature and low pressure.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Kinetic Molecular Theory

  • Gas particles are treated as point masses with negligible volume compared to the container.
  • Collisions between particles and container walls are perfectly elastic.
  • Average kinetic energy is directly proportional to absolute temperature (K).
  • No intermolecular forces of attraction or repulsion exist between gas molecules.

Real Gas Deviations

  • At high pressure, the volume of gas molecules is not negligible compared to container volume.
  • At low temperature, intermolecular forces become significant, causing Z < 1.
  • The Boyle temperature (TB) is the temperature where a real gas behaves ideally over a range of pressure.
  • Van der Waals constant 'a' represents intermolecular attraction; 'b' represents excluded volume.

Critical Phenomena

  • Critical temperature (Tc) is the temperature above which a gas cannot be liquefied by pressure alone.
  • At the critical point, the density of the liquid and gas phases becomes identical.
  • Critical constants are related to Van der Waals constants: Tc = 8a/27Rb, Pc = a/27b2, Vc = 3b.
  • Reduced variables are defined as Pr=P/Pc, Vr=V/Vc, and Tr=T/Tc.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Van der Waals Equation
(P + an2/V2)(V - nb) = nRT
Conditions: Real gases at moderate pressures
Units: a in atm L2 mol-2, b in L mol-1
Root Mean Square Velocity
urms = √(3RT/M)
Conditions: Ideal gas behavior
Units: R in J K-1 mol-1, M in kg mol-1
Graham's Law of Diffusion
r1/r2 = √(M2/M1)
Conditions: Constant pressure and temperature
Units: r = rate of diffusion, M = molar mass
Compressibility Factor
Z = PV/nRT
Conditions: Measure of deviation from ideality
Units: Dimensionless
Ideal Gas Law
PV = nRT
Conditions: Applicable to ideal gases
Units: P in Pa, V in m3, n in mol, T in K
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Ideal gases follow PV=nRT

Observation: Real gases show Z ≠ 1 — Presence of intermolecular forces and finite molecular volume.

Exception Z < 1 for all gases at low pressure

Observation: H2 and He show Z > 1 at all pressures — Extremely weak intermolecular forces make 'a' negligible; repulsive forces dominate.

Exception Liquefaction occurs at high pressure

Observation: Gases above Tc cannot be liquefied — Kinetic energy exceeds the potential energy of intermolecular attraction.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Average kinetic energy = 3/2 RT.
  • Boyle temperature TB = a/Rb.
  • Inversion temperature Ti = 2a/Rb.
  • At low pressure, Z = 1 - a/RTV.
  • At high pressure, Z = 1 + Pb/RT.
  • Root mean square speed is proportional to √T.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Using Celsius instead of Kelvin in gas laws.
  • Confusing the units of 'a' and 'b' in Van der Waals equation.
  • Assuming Z=1 for all gases at STP.
  • Neglecting the molar mass unit conversion in velocity calculations.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • R = 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1
  • R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1
  • 1 atm = 101325 Pa
  • STP = 273.15 K and 1 bar
  • ump : uavg : urms = √2 : √(8/π) : √3

📌 Quick Recall

  • Gases are ideal at high T and low P.
  • Real gases deviate due to 'a' and 'b'.
  • Kinetic energy depends only on T.
  • Diffusion rate is inversely proportional to √M.

⚖️ Key Differences

Diffusion VS Effusion

Diffusion is mixing; Effusion is escaping through a pinhole.

Question 1 of 20

What is the value of Z for an ideal gas?

Explanation: For an ideal gas, PV = nRT, so Z = PV/nRT = 1.

Question 2 of 20

Which gas deviates most from ideal behavior?

Explanation: NH3 has the strongest intermolecular forces (hydrogen bonding), leading to the largest 'a' value.

Question 3 of 20

What is the unit of Van der Waals constant 'a'?

Explanation: Constant 'a' corrects for pressure, units are derived from P = an2/V2.

Question 4 of 20

At what temperature does a real gas behave ideally?

Explanation: Boyle temperature is where the compressibility factor Z is unity for a range of pressure.

Question 5 of 20

What is the relationship between urms and temperature?

Explanation: urms = √(3RT/M), so it is proportional to the square root of T.

Question 6 of 20

Which molecular speed is the highest?

Explanation: The order is urms > uavg > ump.

Question 7 of 20

What is the value of Vc in terms of 'b'?

Explanation: The critical volume Vc is defined as 3b.

Question 8 of 20

How does pressure affect the volume of a gas at constant T?

Explanation: Boyle's Law states P ∝ 1/V at constant T.

Question 9 of 20

What happens to the kinetic energy of gas molecules at 0 K?

Explanation: Since KE ∝ T, at T = 0 K, kinetic energy is zero.

Question 10 of 20

Which gas will diffuse fastest?

Explanation: Diffusion rate is inversely proportional to √M. CH4 (16) has the lowest molar mass.

Question 11 of 20

What is the compressibility factor Z at the critical point?

Explanation: For Van der Waals gases, Zc = PcVc/RTc = 3/8 = 0.375.

Question 12 of 20

Which law relates volume and temperature?

Explanation: Charles's Law states V ∝ T at constant pressure.

Question 13 of 20

What does the constant 'b' represent?

Explanation: The constant 'b' accounts for the finite volume occupied by gas molecules.

Question 14 of 20

What is the SI unit for pressure?

Explanation: The SI unit for pressure is the Pascal (Pa).

Question 15 of 20

Which gas is most easily liquefied?

Explanation: Cl2 has the highest boiling point and strongest intermolecular forces among the options.

Question 16 of 20

What is the effect of increasing temperature on the Maxwell-Boltzmann curve?

Explanation: Higher temperature increases the most probable speed and spreads the distribution.

Question 17 of 20

Dalton's Law of partial pressures applies to?

Explanation: It applies to a mixture of non-reacting gases in a container.

Question 18 of 20

What is the value of R in L atm K-1 mol-1?

Explanation: The universal gas constant R is approximately 0.0821 in L atm units.

Question 19 of 20

The excluded volume 'b' is equal to?

Explanation: Excluded volume per mole is 4 times the actual volume of the gas molecules.

Question 20 of 20

What is the relationship between Pc, Vc, and Tc?

Explanation: Using critical constants, PcVc/RTc = 3/8.
