Law of Corresponding States

The law of corresponding states expresses that all gases at the same reduced temperature and reduced pressure possess the same compressibility factor. Consequently, they deviate from ideal gas behavior to the same extent under identical reduced conditions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Derivation of Reduced Equation of State

  • Substitute P = PrPc, V = VrVc, and T = TrTc into the van der Waals equation.
  • Substitute critical constants in terms of van der Waals parameters: Pc = a / (27b2), Vc = 3b, and Tc = 8a / (27Rb).
  • Eliminate parameters a and b to yield an equation devoid of specific gas constants.
  • The resulting expression depends solely on Pr, Vr, and Tr.

Significance of the Compressibility Factor

  • The compressibility factor is defined as Z = PVm / (RT).
  • According to the law of corresponding states, Z is a universal function of Pr and Tr.
  • Gases sharing identical Pr and Tr values exhibit nearly identical Z values regardless of their chemical identity.
  • This principle forms the theoretical basis of the generalized compressibility chart.

Limitations and Scope of Application

  • The law is an approximation derived primarily from the van der Waals and Dieterici equations of state.
  • It holds best for spherical, non-polar, and moderately polar gases.
  • Large deviations occur for strongly hydrogen-bonded liquids or gases such as HF, H2O, and NH3.
  • Gases with significantly different Zc values do not conform strictly to corresponding states.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Reduced Pressure
Pr = P / Pc
Conditions: Applicable to any real gas at pressure P and critical pressure Pc
Units: P and Pc in atm or Pa (dimensionless ratio)
Reduced Volume
Vr = Vm / Vc
Conditions: Applicable to any real gas at molar volume Vm and critical molar volume Vc
Units: Vm and Vc in L mol-1 (dimensionless ratio)
Generalized van der Waals Equation
(Pr + 3 / Vr2)(3Vr - 1) = 8Tr
Conditions: Expressed in terms of reduced variables using van der Waals constants
Units: Dimensionless universal equation
Compressibility Factor at Critical Point
Zc = PcVc / (RTc)
Conditions: At critical temperature, pressure, and volume
Units: Dimensionless constant (approximately 3/8 for van der Waals gases)
Reduced Temperature
Tr = T / Tc
Conditions: Applicable to any real gas at absolute temperature T and critical temperature Tc
Units: T and Tc in Kelvin (dimensionless ratio)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Gases at identical reduced temperature and pressure should have identical compressibility factors.

Observation: Polar and hydrogen-bonded gases (e.g., water vapor, ammonia) — Strong directional intermolecular forces and association cause deviations from the generalized corresponding states behavior.

Exception Critical compressibility factor Zc is constant for all gases.

Observation: Zc varies between 0.20 and 0.30 for different chemical substances — Real molecules deviate from the ideal van der Waals spherical rigid sphere model due to varying molecular geometries and polarizabilities.

Exception Quantum gases like helium and hydrogen follow classical corresponding states.

Observation: Helium-3, Helium-4, and Hydrogen at very low temperatures — Quantum mechanical effects (zero-point energy and wave-particle duality) dominate over classical thermodynamic behavior.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Reduced Variable Substitution
Xr = X / Xc

Application: Always divide the experimental property (P, T, or V) by its respective critical property value to find the dimensionless reduced parameter.

Critical Constants Memory Rule
Pc = a/27b2, Vc = 3b, Tc = 8a/27Rb

Application: Remember '27' appears in the denominator of Pc and Tc, while '3' appears in Vc.

Zc Estimation
Zc ≈ 0.375 (van der Waals)

Application: Use 3/8 as the theoretical van der Waals critical compressibility factor for quick estimation in numerical problems.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The law of corresponding states was formulated by Johannes van der Waals.
  • Reduced temperature Tr = T / Tc is a dimensionless quantity.
  • Reduced pressure Pr = P / Pc lacks units.
  • The generalized compressibility chart plots Z versus Pr at constant Tr.
  • The critical compressibility factor Zc = PcVc / (RTc) is approximately 3/8 for van der Waals gases.
  • Non-polar gases obey the law of corresponding states with high accuracy.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Using Celsius instead of Kelvin temperature when calculating reduced temperature Tr.
  • Confusing critical molar volume Vc with standard molar volume (22.4 L mol-1).
  • Assuming all real gases have the exact same critical compressibility factor Zc.
  • Applying the law directly to highly quantum or strongly hydrogen-bonded systems without correction.

⚡ Quick Revision: Law of Corresponding States

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition of Reduced States

Reduced variables normalize pressure, temperature, and volume relative to their critical point values, creating a universal scale for comparing diverse gases.

Universal Equation of State

By substituting reduced variables into the van der Waals equation, material-specific constants a and b are eliminated, yielding a universal reduced equation.

Compressibility Factor Principle

Gases at the same reduced temperature and reduced pressure exhibit equal compressibility factors, confirming the validity of corresponding states.

Deviations and Limits

Substances with anomalous intermolecular interactions, such as hydrogen bonding or strong dipole moments, deviate systematically from the law.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Reduced Temperature (Tr = T / Tc)
Ratio of absolute temperature to critical temperature (dimensionless).
Reduced Pressure (Pr = P / Pc)
Ratio of pressure to critical pressure (dimensionless).
Reduced Volume (Vr = Vm / Vc)
Ratio of molar volume to critical molar volume (dimensionless).
Critical Temperature (Tc)
Temperature above which a gas cannot be liquefied (Kelvin).
Critical Pressure (Pc)
Minimum pressure required to liquefy a gas at its critical temperature (atm or Pa).
Critical Compressibility Factor (Zc)
Compressibility factor evaluated at the critical point (dimensionless).

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Tr = T / Tc
  • Pr = P / Pc
  • Vr = Vm / Vc
  • Zc = PcVc / (RTc) ≈ 0.375
  • Universal reduced van der Waals equation eliminates specific gas constants a and b.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Normalizes gas properties using critical constants.
  • Demonstrates that real gases behave similarly under equivalent reduced conditions.
  • Forms the foundation of generalized compressibility charts.
  • Fails for highly polar or quantum gases.

⚖️ Key Differences

Reduced Temperature (Tr) VS Absolute Temperature (T)

Tr is a dimensionless ratio relative to Tc, whereas T has dimensions of temperature (Kelvin).
