Physical Chemistry

van der Waals Equation of State

The van der Waals equation modifies the ideal gas law by accounting for finite molecular volumes and intermolecular attractive forces. It expresses the real gas behavior across wider ranges of temperature and pressure than the simple ideal model.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Volume Correction Term

  • Real gas molecules possess finite, non-zero volume, unlike point masses assumed in ideal gases.
  • The excluded volume b represents 4 times the actual volume of one mole of gas molecules: b = 4 Vm.
  • The corrected volume available for free movement is (V - b) per mole.

Pressure Correction Term

  • Molecules experience intermolecular forces of attraction, reducing the inward momentum change upon wall collision.
  • Observed pressure is lower than ideal pressure by a factor proportional to the square of molar density: Pobs = Pideal - a / V2.
  • The constant a is a measure of the magnitude of intermolecular attractive forces characteristic of a specific gas.

Critical Constants and Compressibility Factor

  • At the critical point, the isotherm shows a point of inflection where (∂P/∂V)T = 0 and (∂2P/∂V2)T = 0.
  • The compressibility factor at the critical point is defined as Zc = PcVc / RTc = 3/8 = 0.375 for all van der Waals gases.
  • The gas constant can be expressed in terms of critical constants as R = 8PcVc / 3Tc.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
van der Waals Equation of State (for n moles)
(P + n2a / V2)(V - nb) = nRT
Conditions: Valid for n moles of a real gas.
Units: V is total volume in liters or m3, n is number of moles.
Critical Temperature (Tc)
Tc = 8a / 27Rb
Conditions: Derived from the point of inflection on the critical isotherm.
Units: Tc in Kelvin.
Critical Pressure (Pc)
Pc = a / 27b2
Conditions: Derived from the point of inflection on the critical isotherm.
Units: Pc in atm or Pa.
Critical Volume (Vc)
Vc = 3b
Conditions: Molar volume of the gas at the critical point.
Units: Vc in L/mol or m3/mol.
Boyle Temperature (TB)
TB = a / Rb
Conditions: Temperature at which a real gas behaves like an ideal gas over a wide pressure range.
Units: TB in Kelvin.
van der Waals Equation of State (for 1 mole)
(P + a / V2)(V - b) = RT
Conditions: Valid for one mole of a real gas at moderate to high pressures.
Units: P in atm or Pa, V in L/mol or m3/mol, T in Kelvin, R is universal gas constant.
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Compressibility factor Z equals 1 for ideal gases at all conditions.

Observation: Real gases deviate significantly from Z = 1 at high pressures and low temperatures. — At high pressure, volume of molecules (b) dominates making Z > 1; at intermediate pressure, attractive forces (a) dominate making Z < 1.

Exception van der Waals constants a and b are universally constant for all states of matter.

Observation: Constants a and b can show slight variations over extreme ranges of temperature and pressure. — Molecular geometry, polarizability, and effective collision diameters change under extreme thermodynamic compression.

Exception Critical compressibility factor Zc is predicted as 0.375 by van der Waals equation.

Observation: Experimental Zc values for most real gases fall between 0.20 and 0.30. — The van der Waals equation overestimates the critical volume and does not accurately account for multi-body interactions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The constant a has dimensions of atm L2 mol-2 or N m4 mol-2.
  • The constant b has dimensions of L mol-1 or m3 mol-1.
  • Higher value of a indicates greater ease of liquefaction for the gas.
  • At very low pressures, the van der Waals equation reduces to the ideal gas equation because V >> b and P >> a/V2.
  • At very high pressures, the volume correction b cannot be neglected, and the equation simplifies to P(V - b) = RT.
  • The inversion temperature of a gas is related to van der Waals constants by Ti = 2a / Rb.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting that the constant b is 4 times the actual molar volume of molecules, not just the volume of one molecule.
  • Confusing units of constant a when using SI units (Pa m6 mol-2) versus liter-atm units.
  • Assuming the van der Waals equation holds perfectly across the liquid-vapor coexistence region without applying Maxwell's equal area rule.
  • Incorrectly scaling the pressure correction term when substituting multiple moles without multiplying by n2.

⚡ Quick Revision: van der Waals Equation of State

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Failure of Ideal Gas Law

Real gases deviate from PV = nRT due to finite molecular size and intermolecular forces at high pressures and low temperatures.

van der Waals Corrections

Pressure is corrected upward by adding a / V2, and volume is corrected downward by subtracting b.

Critical Phenomena

The critical point defines the state where liquid and vapor phases merge, expressible entirely through constants a and b.

Corresponding States Principle

Gases under the same reduced pressure, reduced temperature, and reduced volume have the same compressibility factor.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

van der Waals Constant a (a)
Measure of intermolecular attraction magnitude. SI unit: N m4 mol-2.
van der Waals Constant b (b)
Excluded volume per mole of gas. SI unit: m3 mol-1.
Critical Temperature (Tc)
Temperature above which a gas cannot be liquefied regardless of pressure.
Critical Pressure (Pc)
Minimum pressure required to liquefy a gas at its critical temperature.
Critical Volume (Vc)
Molar volume of a substance at its critical temperature and pressure.
Boyle Temperature (TB)
Temperature at which a real gas exhibits ideal behavior over a finite pressure range.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • (P + a/V2)(V - b) = RT
  • Tc = 8a / 27Rb
  • Pc = a / 27b2
  • Vc = 3b
  • TB = a / Rb = 3.375 Tc

📌 Quick Recall

  • Real gases deviate from ideality due to molecular volume and attractive forces.
  • Parameter 'a' accounts for cohesion, 'b' accounts for repulsion/volume.
  • Critical constants link macroscopic state variables to molecular parameters.
  • At Boyle temperature, real gas behaves ideally in low-to-moderate pressure ranges.

⚖️ Key Differences

Excluded volume (b) VS Actual molecular volume

Excluded volume 'b' is four times the actual volume of molecules per mole.

Question 1 of 19

What are the dimensions of the van der Waals constant 'a'?

Explanation: From the pressure correction term P = a / V2, the unit of 'a' is P × V2, which gives atm L2 mol-2.

Question 2 of 19

What is the physical significance of the van der Waals constant 'b'?

Explanation: Constant 'b' represents the excluded volume, which is four times the actual volume occupied by the molecules in one mole of gas.

Question 3 of 19

What is the expression for critical temperature (Tc) in terms of van der Waals constants 'a' and 'b'?

Explanation: Solving the condition of inflection for the van der Waals equation yields Tc = 8a / 27Rb.

Question 4 of 19

At the Boyle temperature, a real gas behaves like an ideal gas over which pressure range?

Explanation: At Boyle temperature, the effects of 'a' and 'b' cancel each other out, leading to ideal behavior at low to moderate pressures.

Question 5 of 19

What is the value of the critical compressibility factor (Zc) predicted by the van der Waals equation?

Explanation: Zc = PcVc / RTc = (a / 27b2)(3b) / R(8a / 27Rb) = 3/8 = 0.375.

Question 6 of 19

Which gas is expected to have the highest value of van der Waals constant 'a'?

Explanation: Ammonia (NH3) exhibits strong hydrogen bonding and dipole-dipole interactions, resulting in the largest attractive constant 'a' among the choices.

Question 7 of 19

Under what condition does a real gas most closely approach ideal behavior?

Explanation: High temperature increases kinetic energy minimizing attractive forces, and low pressure maximizes intermolecular distance minimizing molecular volume effects.

Question 8 of 19

What is the unit of van der Waals constant 'b'?

Explanation: Since 'b' is subtracted from molar volume V, its units must be the same as molar volume, i.e., L mol-1.

Question 9 of 19

How is Boyle temperature (TB) related to critical temperature (Tc)?

Explanation: TB = a / Rb and Tc = 8a / 27Rb, therefore TB = (27/8)Tc = 3.375 Tc.

Question 10 of 19

What happens to the van der Waals equation at very low pressures?

Explanation: At low pressure, V is very large, so 'b' can be neglected compared to V, and a/V2 can be neglected compared to P, yielding PV = RT.

Question 11 of 19

The compressibility factor Z for a real gas is defined as:

Explanation: Compressibility factor is the ratio of the molar volume of a gas to the molar volume of an ideal gas at the same temperature and pressure, Z = PV / nRT.

Question 12 of 19

If Z < 1 for a gas at a given temperature and pressure, what is the dominant factor?

Explanation: When Z < 1, the molar volume is less than ideal due to dominant intermolecular attractive forces pulling molecules together.

Question 13 of 19

What is the critical volume (Vc) in terms of the van der Waals constant 'b'?

Explanation: At the critical point, the molar volume Vc is derived as 3b from the partial derivative conditions.

Question 14 of 19

Which of the following gases has the lowest value of van der Waals constant 'a'?

Explanation: Helium (He) is a monoatomic noble gas with only weak London dispersion forces, giving it the smallest 'a' value.

Question 15 of 19

What is the critical pressure (Pc) formula derived from van der Waals equation?

Explanation: Critical pressure is given by Pc = a / 27b2.

Question 16 of 19

In the van der Waals equation, the term (V - nb) corrects for:

Explanation: The term nb accounts for the excluded volume, correcting the total container volume for the physical space occupied by the gas molecules.

Question 17 of 19

What is the slope of Z versus P curve at very low pressure for real gases?

Explanation: At low to moderate pressures, attractive forces dominate making Z < 1 and the initial slope negative.

Question 18 of 19

Which parameter among van der Waals constants depends directly on molecular size?

Explanation: Constant 'b' directly represents the effective excluded volume and size of the gas molecules.

Question 19 of 19

At high pressure, the van der Waals equation for 1 mole simplifies to:

Explanation: At high pressure, V is small so a/V2 is very large compared to P, but V cannot be smaller than b, yielding P(V - b) = RT.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the Boyle temperature of a gas given that its van der Waals constants are a = 4.0 L2 atm mol-2 and b = 0.04 L mol-1 (R = 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1).

Solution: TB = a / (Rb) = 4.0 / (0.0821 × 0.04) = 4.0 / 0.003284 = 1218 K.

Question 2 of 10
If the critical temperature of a gas is 300 K, what is its approximate Boyle temperature?

Solution: TB = 3.375 × Tc = 3.375 × 300 = 1012.5 K.

Question 3 of 10
What is the excluded volume for 2 moles of a gas if the constant b is 0.030 L mol-1?

Solution: Excluded volume for n moles is nb = 2 mol × 0.030 L mol-1 = 0.060 L.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following gases has the highest critical temperature based on molecular properties?

Solution: Water has extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding leading to a very high attractive constant 'a' and consequently the highest critical temperature among the options.

Question 5 of 10
The critical pressure Pc of a gas is 45 atm and critical volume Vc is 0.15 L mol-1. What is constant 'b'?

Solution: Vc = 3b, therefore b = Vc / 3 = 0.15 / 3 = 0.05 L mol-1.

Question 6 of 10
In terms of reduced variables (Pr = P/Pc, Tr = T/Tc, V_r = V/V_c), the van der Waals equation takes the reduced form:

Solution: Substituting P = PrPc, V = VrVc, T = TrTc into the van der Waals equation with critical constants yields (Pr + 3 / Vr2)(3Vr - 1) = 8 Tr.

Question 7 of 10
Which condition favors maximum deviation from ideal gas behavior?

Solution: High pressure forces molecules close together (making volume b significant) and low temperature reduces kinetic energy (making attractive forces 'a' dominant).

Question 8 of 10
The ratio of Boyle temperature to critical temperature (TB / Tc) is:

Solution: TB / Tc = (a/Rb) / (8a/27Rb) = 27/8 = 3.375.

Question 9 of 10
If constant 'a' for a gas is zero, what does the van der Waals equation reduce to?

Solution: Setting a = 0 removes the pressure correction term, leaving P(V - b) = RT.

Question 10 of 10
What is the physical meaning of the point of inflection on a real gas isotherm at the critical point?

Solution: At the critical point, the isotherm has a point of inflection where both (∂P/∂V)T = 0 and (∂2P/∂V2)T = 0.
