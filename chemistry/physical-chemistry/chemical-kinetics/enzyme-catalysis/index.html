Physical Chemistry

Enzyme Catalysis

Enzyme catalysis involves the acceleration of specific biochemical reactions by highly specialized protein catalysts known as enzymes. The kinetics of these reactions typically follow the Michaelis-Menten mechanism, characterized by rapid formation of an enzyme-substrate complex.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mechanism and Kinetics of Enzyme Action

  • Enzyme catalysis proceeds via the reversible binding of substrate [S] to enzyme [E] to form an enzyme-substrate complex [ES].
  • The overall reaction mechanism is represented as E + S ⇄[k_1][k-1] ES →[k_2] E + P.
  • The steady-state approximation assumes the rate of formation of [ES] equals the rate of its consumption.
  • At low substrate concentrations ([S] << K_m), the reaction rate is first order with respect to [S].
  • At high substrate concentrations ([S] >> K_m), the reaction rate becomes zero order as all active sites are saturated.

Graphical Linearizations and Parameters

  • The Lineweaver-Burk plot plots 1/v versus 1/[S], yielding a straight line with slope K_m/V_max and y-intercept 1/V_max.
  • K_m represents the substrate concentration at which the reaction velocity reaches half of V_max.
  • A lower K_m value indicates a higher affinity of the enzyme for its specific substrate.
  • The turnover number (k_cat) defines the maximum number of substrate molecules converted to product per active site per unit time.
  • The ratio k_cat / K_m measures catalytic efficiency and approaches the diffusion-controlled limit.

Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity

  • Enzyme activity exhibits a sharp optimum temperature, above which rapid thermal denaturation of the protein tertiary structure occurs.
  • Changes in pH alter the ionization states of amino acid residues in the active site, creating a bell-shaped activity profile.
  • Competitive inhibitors bind to the active site, increasing apparent K_m without altering V_max.
  • Non-competitive inhibitors bind to allosteric sites, decreasing V_max without changing K_m.
  • Uncompetitive inhibitors bind exclusively to the [ES] complex, decreasing both V_max and K_m simultaneously.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Michaelis Constant
K_m = k-1 + k_2k_1
Conditions: Single intermediate enzyme-substrate complex formation
Units: k_1 in mol-1 L s-1, k-1 and k_2 in s-1, K_m in mol L-1
Lineweaver-Burk Equation
1v = K_mVmax[S] + 1Vmax
Conditions: Double reciprocal plot linear transformation
Units: v in mol L-1 s-1, [S] in mol L-1
Turnover Number
kcat = Vmax[E]_0
Conditions: Maximum catalytic efficiency per active site
Units: kcat in s-1, Vmax in mol L-1 s-1, [E]_0 in mol L-1
Arrhenius Equation for Enzyme Inactivation
k = A e-E_aRT
Conditions: Applies across operational temperature range before thermal denaturation
Units: k in s-1, E_a in J mol-1, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in Kelvin
Michaelis-Menten Rate Equation
v = Vmax[S]K_m + [S]
Conditions: Steady-state approximation, [S] >> [E]0
Units: v in mol L-1 s-1, Vmax in mol L-1 s-1, K_m and [S] in mol L-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Reaction rate increases indefinitely with increasing substrate concentration

Observation: Substrate Inhibition — At exceptionally high concentrations, a second substrate molecule binds to the active site or an allosteric site of the ES complex, forming an inactive SES complex.

Exception Enzymes follow standard hyperbolic Michaelis-Menten kinetics

Observation: Allosteric Enzymes — Multimeric enzymes exhibit cooperative binding, yielding sigmoidal v versus [S] plots instead of standard rectangular hyperbolas.

Exception Temperature increase always increases reaction rates according to Arrhenius law

Observation: Thermal Inactivation above Optimum — Protein denaturation disrupts hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions, causing an abrupt drop in catalytic activity above the optimum temperature.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Lineweaver-Burk Intercepts
x-intercept = -1/K_m, y-intercept = 1/V_max

Application: Quickly determine kinetic constants from double-reciprocal plots by taking negative inverse of x-intercept for K_m.

Inhibition Effect Mnemonic
COMPETitive = Competes for active site (K_m changes, V_max constant)

Application: Recall that competitive inhibition shifts the x-intercept while keeping the y-intercept constant on a Lineweaver-Burk plot.

Catalytic Efficiency Upper Limit
Diffusion limit ≈ 10^8 to 10^9 M^-1 s^-1

Application: Identify catalytically perfect enzymes when k_cat/K_m reaches the diffusion-controlled rate of substrate encounter.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Enzymes reduce the activation energy of a reaction while leaving the standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) unaltered.
  • The Michaelis constant K_m has the dimension of concentration (mol L-1).
  • Competitive inhibitors can be overcome by adding excess substrate.
  • The turnover number k_cat has units of time inverse (s-1).
  • Optimum pH for most intracellular enzymes is near neutral (pH 6.5 to 7.5).
  • Enzymes are highly specific catalysts exhibiting stereospecificity and reaction specificity.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming K_m is a measure of enzyme-substrate dissociation constant under all conditions; it equals K_s only when k_2 << k-1.
  • Confusing non-competitive inhibition with uncompetitive inhibition regarding their effect on K_m.
  • Forgetting that V_max depends on total enzyme concentration [E]_0.
  • Stating that enzymes alter the equilibrium constant of a chemical reaction.

⚡ Quick Revision: Enzyme Catalysis

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Michaelis-Menten Kinetics

Relates reaction velocity to substrate concentration using Vmax and K_m under steady-state conditions.

Lineweaver-Burk Transformation

Provides a linear double-reciprocal plot to accurately extract kinetic parameters from experimental velocity data.

Enzyme Inhibition

Classifies inhibitors into competitive, non-competitive, and uncompetitive based on their binding modes and kinetic effects.

Environmental Effects

Describes the dependence of catalytic rates on temperature and pH via ionization changes and thermal denaturation.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Active Site (Catalytic Pocket)
Specific region on the enzyme surface where substrate binding and catalysis occur.
Turnover Number (k_cat)
Maximum number of substrate molecules converted per active site per second.
Michaelis Constant (K_m)
Substrate concentration at which reaction velocity is half of V_max.
Lineweaver-Burk Plot (1/v vs 1/[S])
Double-reciprocal linear plot used to analyze enzyme kinetics and inhibition.
Allosteric Site (Regulatory Site)
Site other than the active site where effector molecules bind to modulate activity.
Coenzymes (Cofactors)
Non-protein organic molecules required by certain enzymes for catalytic activity.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • v = (V_max[S]) / (K_m + [S])
  • K_m = (k-1 + k_2) / k_1
  • k_cat = V_max / [E]_0
  • Competitive inhibition: V_max unchanged, K_m increased
  • Non-competitive inhibition: V_max decreased, K_m unchanged

📌 Quick Recall

  • Enzymes function by lowering activation energy barriers.
  • Michaelis-Menten kinetics relies on the steady-state assumption for [ES].
  • Inhibition types are distinguished by their kinetic signatures on double-reciprocal plots.
  • Temperature and pH extremes cause irreversible loss of enzyme activity via denaturation.

⚖️ Key Differences

K_m VS K_s

K_m is the kinetic Michaelis constant encompassing all rate constants, whereas K_s is the thermodynamic dissociation constant of the ES complex.

Question 1 of 18

What is the order of an enzyme-catalyzed reaction when the substrate concentration is extremely high ([S] >> K_m)?

Explanation: At very high substrate concentrations, all enzyme active sites are saturated, making the rate independent of [S], which corresponds to zero-order kinetics.

Question 2 of 18

In a Lineweaver-Burk plot, what does the y-intercept represent?

Explanation: The Lineweaver-Burk equation is 1/v = (K_m/V_max)(1/[S]) + 1/V_max. Setting 1/[S] = 0 yields a y-intercept of 1/V_max.

Question 3 of 18

Which kinetic parameter is unaltered in the presence of a competitive inhibitor?

Explanation: Competitive inhibitors compete with the substrate for the active site, which can be overcome at high substrate concentrations, leaving V_max unchanged.

Question 4 of 18

What are the units of the Michaelis constant (K_m)?

Explanation: K_m has the same units as substrate concentration [S], which is mol L-1 (or molar).

Question 5 of 18

How does an enzyme affect the overall Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) of a chemical reaction?

Explanation: Catalysts alter only the reaction rate and activation energy; they do not alter thermodynamic state functions such as ΔG° or the equilibrium constant.

Question 6 of 18

What type of inhibition is characterized by a decrease in both V_max and K_m?

Explanation: Uncompetitive inhibitors bind only to the ES complex, reducing both V_max and K_m proportionally, maintaining a constant slope on a Lineweaver-Burk plot.

Question 7 of 18

The turnover number (k_cat) of an enzyme is calculated as:

Explanation: Turnover number k_cat represents the maximum moles of product formed per mole of enzyme active site per unit time, given by V_max / [E]_0.

Question 8 of 18

Under what condition does K_m closely approximate the dissociation constant (K_s) of the ES complex?

Explanation: K_m = (k-1 + k_2)/k_1. If product formation rate constant k_2 is much smaller than dissociation rate constant k-1, K_m reduces to k-1/k_1, which is K_s.

Question 9 of 18

What is the typical effect of increasing temperature above the optimum temperature on enzyme activity?

Explanation: Elevating temperature beyond the optimum disrupts non-covalent interactions maintaining protein structure, causing thermal denaturation and loss of activity.

Question 10 of 18

In non-competitive inhibition, where does the inhibitor bind?

Explanation: Non-competitive inhibitors bind reversibly to an allosteric site on both the free enzyme and the enzyme-substrate complex with equal affinity.

Question 11 of 18

Which plot is most commonly used to linearize Michaelis-Menten kinetic data?

Explanation: The Lineweaver-Burk double-reciprocal plot (1/v vs 1/[S]) is the classical linearization method used in enzyme kinetics.

Question 12 of 18

What does a lower K_m value signify regarding enzyme-substrate interaction?

Explanation: A smaller K_m indicates that a lower substrate concentration is required to reach half-maximal velocity, signifying higher binding affinity.

Question 13 of 18

What are coenzymes?

Explanation: Coenzymes are complex organic molecules, often derived from vitamins, that assist enzymes in catalysis.

Question 14 of 18

Which amino acid residue in an enzyme active site often acts as a general acid-base catalyst?

Explanation: Histidine has a pK_a near physiological pH (approx. 6.0), making its imidazole ring an efficient proton donor and acceptor in catalytic mechanisms.

Question 15 of 18

What is the primary reason enzymes accelerate reaction rates compared to uncatalyzed pathways?

Explanation: Enzymes stabilize the transition state, effectively lowering the activation energy barrier for the reaction.

Question 16 of 18

If an enzyme has a k_cat of 500 s-1 and [E]_0 = 2 μM, what is V_max?

Explanation: V_max = k_cat × [E]_0 = 500 s-1 × 2 μM = 1000 μM s-1.

Question 17 of 18

What is the kinetic effect of a competitive inhibitor on the Lineweaver-Burk plot?

Explanation: Since V_max remains unchanged, all lines for different competitive inhibitor concentrations intersect at the same point on the y-axis (1/V_max).

Question 18 of 18

Which term describes the catalytic efficiency of an enzyme operating at low substrate concentrations?

Explanation: At low substrate concentrations ([S] << K_m), the rate equation simplifies to v = (k_cat/K_m)[E]_0[S], making k_cat/K_m the second-order rate constant for catalytic efficiency.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the velocity of an enzyme-catalyzed reaction when V_max = 50 μmol L-1 s-1, K_m = 5 mM, and substrate concentration [S] = 20 mM.

Solution: v = (50 × 20) / (5 + 20) = 1000 / 25 = 40 μmol L-1 s-1.

Question 2 of 10
Which parameter in the Lineweaver-Burk plot changes when a non-competitive inhibitor is added?

Solution: Non-competitive inhibitors decrease V_max while keeping K_m constant, which alters both the slope (K_m/V_max) and the y-intercept (1/V_max) while leaving the x-intercept (-1/K_m) unchanged.

Question 3 of 10
What is the fractional saturation (θ) of an enzyme when [S] = 3 K_m?

Solution: θ = [S] / (K_m + [S]) = 3K_m / (K_m + 3K_m) = 3/4 = 0.75.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following statements regarding enzymes is incorrect?

Solution: Enzymes do not alter the overall free energy change (ΔG) of a reaction; they only affect the rate of approach to equilibrium.

Question 5 of 10
If the K_m of enzyme A for substrate S is 2 × 10-4 M and enzyme B has K_m = 4 × 10-6 M, which enzyme has higher affinity?

Solution: A lower K_m value denotes a higher substrate binding affinity; hence Enzyme B (4 × 10-6 M) has higher affinity.

Question 6 of 10
In uncompetitive inhibition, the inhibitor binds to:

Solution: Uncompetitive inhibitors bind exclusively to the conformational state of the enzyme-substrate complex (ES).

Question 7 of 10
The steady-state assumption in enzyme kinetics implies that:

Solution: The steady-state approximation assumes that the concentration of the intermediate enzyme-substrate complex remains constant (d[ES]/dt = 0) during the initial linear phase.

Question 8 of 10
What is the effect of doubling the total enzyme concentration [E]_0 on V_max, assuming substrate is saturating?

Solution: Since V_max = k_cat × [E]_0, V_max is directly proportional to the total enzyme concentration.

Question 9 of 10
Which type of inhibition can be completely reversed by adding a large excess of substrate?

Solution: Competitive inhibition is reversible and surmountable by increasing substrate concentration to outcompete the inhibitor.

Question 10 of 10
What does a high k_cat / K_m ratio indicate?

Solution: The specificity constant k_cat / K_m measures overall catalytic efficiency; high values indicate extremely efficient enzymes.
