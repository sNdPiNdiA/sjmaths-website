Physical Chemistry

Activation Energy

Activation energy represents the minimum kinetic energy reacting species must possess to undergo a chemical reaction. It defines the height of the potential energy barrier between reactants and products on a reaction coordinate diagram.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Potential Energy Profiles and Transition States

  • The transition state or activated complex corresponds to the maximum on the potential energy barrier along the reaction coordinate.
  • The reaction coordinate represents the concerted progress of bond-breaking and bond-forming events during transformation.
  • The difference between the potential energy of the activated complex and the reactants defines the activation energy.
  • Exothermic and endothermic reactions differ in relative reactant and product energy levels while maintaining positive activation barriers.

Temperature Dependence and Collision Dynamics

  • According to Arrhenius theory, rate constants increase exponentially with temperature because a larger fraction of molecules exceed Ea.
  • The frequency factor (A) incorporates collision frequency and spatial orientation requirements via the steric factor.
  • A plot of ln k versus 1/T yields a straight line whose slope directly determines the numerical value of activation energy.
  • Near room temperature, an increase of 10°C approximately doubles the reaction rate for many common processes.

Catalysis and Mechanistic Pathways

  • A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway characterized by a lower activation energy barrier.
  • Catalysts accelerate both forward and reverse reactions equally, leaving the overall equilibrium constant (Keq) unchanged.
  • Enzymes and heterogeneous catalysts minimize transition state energy by stabilizing intermediates through electrostatic or coordination interactions.
  • Reactions with exceptionally high activation energies proceed at negligible rates at ambient temperatures without thermal or catalytic assistance.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Arrhenius Equation (Two-Temperature Form)
log(k2 / k1) = (Ea / 2.303 R) [ (T2 - T1) / (T1 T2) ]
Conditions: Assumes activation energy Ea remains constant between temperatures T1 and T2
Units: k1, k2 dimensionless ratio, Ea in J mol-1, R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1, T in K
Activation Energy from Graphical Slope
Slope = -Ea / (2.303 R)
Conditions: Linear form obtained by plotting ln k or log k versus 1 / T
Units: Slope in Kelvin, R in J K-1 mol-1, Ea in J mol-1
Activation Energy Relation to Threshold Energy
Ea = Eth - Eaverage(reactants)
Conditions: Applicable in collision theory framework for reacting molecules
Units: Energies in J mol-1 or kJ mol-1
Activation Energy for Reverse Reaction
Ea(rev) = Ea(fwd) - ΔH
Conditions: Valid for elementary reactions involving enthalpy change ΔH
Units: Energies in kJ mol-1
Arrhenius Equation (Differential / Rate Constant Form)
k = A e-Ea / RT
Conditions: Valid for elementary and complex reactions over a limited temperature range where Ea and A are temperature-independent
Units: k in units of rate constant, A in same units as k, Ea in J mol-1, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in K
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Reaction rate increases monotonically with temperature for all reactions.

Observation: Explosions and enzyme-catalyzed denaturation — At very high temperatures, enzymes undergo thermal denaturation, destroying active sites, and explosive chain branched reactions can self-terminate or suffer thermal decomposition.

Exception Activation energy is strictly positive for all elementary reactions.

Observation: Certain radical recombination and ion-molecule reactions — Reactions involving radical recombinations often proceed with zero or slightly negative activation energy because no activation barrier exists for radical coupling.

Exception Arrhenius parameters Ea and A are independent of temperature.

Observation: Complex reactions and gas-phase radical mechanisms over wide temperature ranges — Collision cross-sections and partition functions possess slight temperature dependencies, causing experimental Ea to vary with T.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Double Rate 10-Degree Rule Shortcut
k2 / k1 ≈ 2(ΔT / 10)

Application: Quickly estimate rate constant changes when temperature changes by multiples of 10°C without logs.

Activation Energy Sign Convention for Slopes
Ea = -2.303 × R × Slope

Application: If plotting log k vs 1/T, remember the slope is negative (-Ea / 2.303R), so cancel the negative sign to get positive Ea.

Endo vs Exo Barrier Mnemonic
Ea(fwd) < Ea(rev) (Exo) ; Ea(fwd) > Ea(rev) (Endo)

Application: Instantly sketch potential energy profiles: products lower than reactants means forward barrier is smaller than reverse barrier.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The units of activation energy in standard SI representation are J mol-1 or kJ mol-1.
  • A catalyst alters the mechanism of a reaction to decrease activation energy, thereby increasing reaction rate.
  • The frequency factor A has the same dimensions and units as the rate constant k for the specific reaction order.
  • Temperature coefficient is defined as the ratio of rate constants separated by a 10°C temperature interval, typically ranging between 2 and 3.
  • According to collision theory, effective collisions require both proper spatial orientation and energy equal to or exceeding activation energy.
  • The difference between forward and reverse activation energies equals the standard enthalpy change of the reaction (ΔH).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting to convert Celsius to Kelvin when substituting temperature values into the Arrhenius equation.
  • Confusing activation energy with overall reaction enthalpy change on potential energy profiles.
  • Assuming catalysts change the equilibrium constant or standard Gibbs free energy of a reaction.
  • Neglecting the conversion factor 2.303 when using base-10 logarithms instead of natural logarithms.

⚡ Quick Revision: Activation Energy

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Activation Energy Definition

Ea is the energetic hurdle separating reactants from products, determining sensitivity to temperature variations.

Arrhenius Temperature Dependence

The exponential term e-Ea/RT quantifies the fraction of reactant molecules possessing sufficient energy.

Role of Catalysts

Catalysts lower Ea through intermediate complex formation, accelerating rates without altering thermodynamic state functions.

Graphical Evaluation

Arrhenius plots of ln k versus 1/T yield slopes equal to -Ea/R, permitting direct experimental calculation of Ea.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Activation Energy (Ea)
Minimum excess energy required by reactants to form products in J mol-1.
Frequency Factor (A)
Pre-exponential constant related to collision frequency and molecular orientation.
Arrhenius Constant (R)
Universal gas constant equal to 8.314 J K-1 mol-1.
Activated Complex (Transition State)
High-energy unstable configuration at the peak of the potential energy barrier.
Temperature Coefficient (μ)
Ratio of rate constants at temperatures differing by 10°C.
Threshold Energy (Eth)
Total minimum energy required for a successful collision leading to reaction.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • k = A exp(-Ea / RT)
  • log(k2 / k1) = (Ea / 2.303 R) [(ΔT) / (T1 T2)]
  • Slope of Arrhenius plot = -Ea / 2.303 R
  • Ea(fwd) - Ea(rev) = ΔH
  • Catalysts lower Ea but do not change ΔH or Keq

📌 Quick Recall

  • Activation energy governs reaction rate sensitivity to temperature changes.
  • Higher activation energy implies a more temperature-sensitive reaction rate.
  • Catalysts provide alternative low-energy pathways to speed up kinetics.
  • Arrhenius equation forms the mathematical bridge between kinetics and thermodynamics.

⚖️ Key Differences

Activation Energy (Ea) VS Enthalpy of Reaction (ΔH)

Ea is the kinetic barrier height from reactants to transition state, whereas ΔH is the thermodynamic energy difference between products and reactants.

Question 1 of 19

What is the physical significance of the activation energy in a chemical reaction?

Explanation: Activation energy represents the energy barrier that reacting molecules must overcome to transform into products.

Question 2 of 19

In the Arrhenius equation k = A exp(-Ea / RT), what does the term 'A' represent?

Explanation: A is the Arrhenius pre-exponential factor representing collision frequency and spatial orientation probability.

Question 3 of 19

If a plot of ln k versus 1/T is constructed, what does the slope of the straight line equal?

Explanation: Taking natural log of k = A exp(-Ea/RT) gives ln k = ln A - (Ea/R)(1/T), so the slope of ln k vs 1/T is -Ea/R. For base-10 log, it is -Ea/(2.303 R).

Question 4 of 19

How does a catalyst increase the rate of a chemical reaction?

Explanation: Catalysts lower the activation energy barrier, allowing a larger fraction of molecules to react at a given temperature.

Question 5 of 19

What is the effect of a catalyst on the equilibrium constant of a reversible reaction?

Explanation: A catalyst accelerates both forward and reverse rates equally, leaving the equilibrium constant Keq unchanged.

Question 6 of 19

For an endothermic reaction with enthalpy change ΔH, what is the relation between forward and reverse activation energies?

Explanation: In an endothermic reaction, products are at higher energy than reactants, meaning the forward barrier is larger than the reverse barrier: Ea(fwd) = Ea(rev) + ΔH.

Question 7 of 19

The temperature coefficient for most reactions lies within which range?

Explanation: For most chemical reactions, the rate roughly doubles or triples when temperature is raised by 10°C, giving a temperature coefficient between 2 and 3.

Question 8 of 19

Which of the following equations correctly expresses the two-temperature form of the Arrhenius equation?

Explanation: Integrating between T1 and T2 yields log(k2/k1) = (Ea / 2.303 R)[(T2 - T1)/(T1 T2)].

Question 9 of 19

If activation energy of a reaction is zero, how does the rate constant vary with temperature?

Explanation: When Ea = 0, the term exp(-0/RT) = 1, making k = A, which is independent of temperature.

Question 10 of 19

What does the transition state represent on a potential energy profile?

Explanation: The transition state is the highest energy point (maximum) on the reaction coordinate pathway.

Question 11 of 19

Which factor affects the rate constant k according to the Arrhenius equation?

Explanation: The Arrhenius equation explicitly relates the rate constant k to activation energy Ea, temperature T, and frequency factor A.

Question 12 of 19

What are the standard units of activation energy (Ea) in SI?

Explanation: Activation energy is expressed as energy per mole of reactant, typically J mol^-1 or kJ mol^-1.

Question 13 of 19

If the activation energy of a reaction is very high, the reaction is typically:

Explanation: A high activation energy means very few molecules possess sufficient energy, resulting in a very slow reaction rate at ambient temperatures.

Question 14 of 19

Which theory provides a detailed physical basis for the pre-exponential factor A in terms of collision frequency and orientation?

Explanation: Collision theory relates A to collision frequency and steric requirements of reacting molecules.

Question 15 of 19

What is the relationship between threshold energy (Eth) and activation energy (Ea) for reactants with average kinetic energy (Eavg)?

Explanation: Activation energy is the additional energy needed over the average energy of reactants to reach the threshold energy: Ea = Eth - Eavg.

Question 16 of 19

In an exothermic reaction, how do potential energies of reactants and products compare?

Explanation: Exothermic reactions release heat, meaning products lie lower in potential energy than reactants.

Question 17 of 19

Why do biological enzymes catalyze biochemical reactions so effectively?

Explanation: Enzymes stabilize transition states, drastically reducing activation energy and allowing rapid physiological rates.

Question 18 of 19

If temperature approaches infinity (T → ∞) in the Arrhenius equation, what does the rate constant k approach?

Explanation: As T → ∞, exp(-Ea/RT) → exp(0) = 1, so k equals A.

Question 19 of 19

Which of the following statements about activation energy is incorrect?

Explanation: Activation energy is independent of reactant concentrations; it is a characteristic energetic property of the reaction path.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary factor that determines the magnitude of the activation energy for an elementary bimolecular gas-phase reaction?

Solution: Activation energy primarily reflects the energy required to distort and break bonds leading to the transition state.

Question 2 of 10
If the activation energy of a reaction is 60 kJ mol^-1 and temperature is increased from 300 K to 310 K, the rate constant approximately increases by a factor of:

Solution: Using the 10-degree rule or Arrhenius equation with Ea = 60 kJ/mol around 300K yields roughly a doubling of the rate constant.

Question 3 of 10
In a potential energy diagram for a multi-step reaction, the rate-determining step is generally identified as the step with:

Solution: The step with the highest activation energy barrier offers the greatest resistance and dictates the overall rate of the reaction.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following expressions represents the temperature dependence of the rate constant according to Arrhenius?

Solution: Differentiating ln k = ln A - Ea/RT with respect to T gives d(ln k)/dT = Ea / (RT^2).

Question 5 of 10
How does the addition of a negative catalyst (inhibitor) affect the activation energy?

Solution: An inhibitor typically functions by directing the reaction through a pathway with a higher activation energy, slowing down the rate.

Question 6 of 10
Calculate the activation energy if the rate constant doubles when temperature rises from 298 K to 308 K (R = 8.314 J K^-1 mol^-1).

Solution: log(2) = 0.3010. Using log(k2/k1) = (Ea / 2.303 R) [10 / (298 * 308)], solving gives Ea ≈ 52.9 kJ mol^-1.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following statements is true regarding collision theory and activation energy?

Solution: Collision theory requires both energy ≥ Ea and proper steric orientation for successful reaction.

Question 8 of 10
What is the slope value when plotting log(k) versus 1/T on a graph?

Solution: The slope of log k vs 1/T is -Ea / (2.303 R) because log = ln / 2.303.

Question 9 of 10
If Ea1 and Ea2 are activation energies of a forward and reverse reaction respectively, and the reaction is exothermic (ΔH = -x kJ/mol), then:

Solution: For an exothermic reaction, Ea(fwd) = Ea(rev) - |ΔH|, hence Ea1 = Ea2 - x.

Question 10 of 10
Which parameter in the Arrhenius equation is dimensionless or has units depending on reaction order?

Solution: The frequency factor A matches the units of the rate constant k, which vary according to the overall reaction order.
