Physical Chemistry

Effect of temperature on reaction rate

Reaction rates generally increase exponentially with temperature due to an increase in the fraction of molecules possessing energy equal to or greater than the activation energy. The quantitative dependence of the rate constant on temperature is described by the Arrhenius equation.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Temperature Dependence and Rate Constants

  • Most chemical reactions exhibit an increase in rate when the temperature is raised, typically doubling for every 10 K rise.
  • The temperature coefficient (μ) is defined as the ratio of rate constants separated by a 10-degree temperature interval.
  • The frequency factor (A) and activation energy (Ea) are treated as temperature-independent constants within the intermediate temperature range according to the Arrhenius model.

Activation Energy and Energy Barrier

  • Activation energy (Ea) represents the minimum kinetic energy reactant molecules must possess to undergo a successful chemical transformation.
  • According to collision theory, only collisions involving reactant species with energy ≥ Ea and appropriate spatial orientation result in product formation.
  • A catalyst functions by providing an alternate reaction pathway characterized by a lower activation energy, thereby increasing the rate constant at a fixed temperature.

Graphical Interpretation and Slope Analysis

  • A plot of ln k versus 1/T yields a straight line with a slope equal to -Ea/R and a y-intercept equal to ln A.
  • The magnitude of the slope directly determines the activation energy of the reaction without requiring measurements at multiple narrow intervals.
  • Reactions with high activation energies are exceptionally sensitive to temperature changes compared to those with low activation energies.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Arrhenius Equation (Integrated Form)
ln(k2/k1) = (Ea / R) [ (T2 - T1) / (T1T2) ]
Conditions: Applied between two different temperatures T1 and T2
Units: k1, k2 are rate constants, Ea in J mol-1
Arrhenius Equation (Linear Exponential Form)
k = A exp(-Ea / RT)
Conditions: Constant activation energy over the specified temperature range
Units: A is frequency factor with same units as k
Temperature Coefficient
μ = k(T+10) / kT ≈ 2 to 3
Conditions: Measured for a 10-degree rise in temperature near room temperature
Units: Dimensionless ratio
Arrhenius Equation (Differential Form)
d(ln k)/dT = Ea / (RT2)
Conditions: Valid for elementary and complex reactions where Ea is temperature-independent
Units: k in s-1 or mol L-1 s-1, Ea in J mol-1, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in K
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Reaction rate increases monotonically with an increase in temperature

Observation: Certain enzyme-catalyzed reactions and gas-phase explosive reactions — Enzymes undergo thermal denaturation at high temperatures, causing a sharp drop in catalytic activity above an optimum temperature.

Exception Rate constant increases with temperature for all reactions

Observation: Reactions with negative activation energy (e.g., recombination of certain free radicals like 2NO + O2 → 2NO2) — These elementary steps proceed without an activation barrier, and the collision frequency decreases slightly with increasing temperature.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Logarithmic Base Conversion Rule
log(k2/k1) = (Ea / 2.303 R) [ (T2 - T1) / (T1T2) ]

Application: Use base-10 logarithms directly when calculating Ea from two rate constants to avoid intermediate conversion errors.

Temperature Coefficient Rule of Thumb
kT2 = kT1 × μ( ΔT / 10 )

Application: Quickly estimate the new rate constant when given the temperature coefficient (μ usually taken as 2) and temperature change (ΔT).

Graphical Slope Shortcut
Ea = -Slope × 2.303 × R

Application: Instantly extract activation energy in J mol-1 from an Arrhenius plot given in common log (log k vs 1/T) format.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The rate constant of a reaction becomes equal to the pre-exponential factor A as temperature approaches infinity.
  • A 10 K rise in temperature typically increases the reaction rate by a factor of 2 to 3.
  • The unit of activation energy (Ea) in standard SI units is J mol-1 or kJ mol-1.
  • The universal gas constant (R) value used in Arrhenius calculations is 8.314 J K-1 mol-1.
  • Reactions having low activation energy are less sensitive to temperature changes than those with high activation energy.
  • The intercept of the Arrhenius plot (ln k vs 1/T) gives the natural logarithm of the frequency factor (ln A).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting to convert Celsius temperatures to Kelvin absolute temperatures when applying the Arrhenius equation.
  • Confusing natural logarithm (ln) with common logarithm (log10) when calculating activation energy.
  • Assuming activation energy changes with temperature in standard numerical problems unless explicitly stated otherwise.
  • Using incorrect units for the gas constant R when Ea is given in kJ mol-1.

⚡ Quick Revision: Effect of temperature on reaction rate

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Thermal Dependence of Reaction Rates

Reaction rates accelerate with temperature as thermal energy shifts the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution curve, increasing the fraction of molecules with energy exceeding Ea.

The Arrhenius Mathematical Framework

The Arrhenius equation k = A exp(-Ea/RT) connects the rate constant quantitatively to temperature, activation energy, and frequency factor.

Activation Energy Determination

Activation energy is evaluated experimentally by measuring rate constants at two distinct temperatures or graphically via the slope of ln k versus 1/T.

Collision and Transition State Theories

Both theories provide microscopic justification for the temperature dependence, emphasizing that successful collisions require both sufficient energy and proper orientation.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Activation Energy (Ea)
Minimum excess energy required by reactants to form products; unit is J mol-1.
Frequency Factor (A)
Pre-exponential factor representing total collision frequency with favorable orientation.
Rate Constant (k)
Proportionality constant in the rate law equation, dependent heavily on temperature.
Temperature Coefficient (μ)
Ratio of rate constants at temperatures 10 K apart; typically ranges from 2 to 3.
Arrhenius Plot (ln k vs 1/T)
Linear graphical representation used to determine activation energy from slope.
Boltzmann Factor (exp(-Ea/RT))
Fraction of molecules possessing energy equal to or greater than activation energy.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Arrhenius Equation: k = A exp(-Ea / RT)
  • Integrated two-temperature form: ln(k2/k1) = (Ea/R)[(T2 - T1)/(T1T2)]
  • Slope of Arrhenius plot: -Ea / R
  • Universal gas constant R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1
  • Typical temperature coefficient value μ ≈ 2 - 3

📌 Quick Recall

  • Reaction rates increase with temperature due to a higher fraction of high-energy collisions.
  • The Arrhenius equation mathematically models the temperature dependence of rate constants.
  • Activation energy dictates the sensitivity of a reaction's rate to temperature fluctuations.
  • Graphical analysis of ln k versus 1/T yields a straight line with slope -Ea/R.

⚖️ Key Differences

Activation Energy (Ea) VS Enthalpy of Reaction (ΔH)

Ea is the energy barrier between reactants and the transition state, whereas ΔH is the overall net energy change between products and reactants.

Question 1 of 10
If a reaction's rate triples when temperature increases from 298 K to 308 K, calculate the activation energy (R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1, ln 3 = 1.099).

Solution: Ea = [ln(3) × 8.314 × 298 × 308] / 10 = [1.099 × 8.314 × 91784] / 10 ≈ 34,100 J mol-1 = 34.1 kJ mol-1.

Question 2 of 10
In the Arrhenius equation, as T → ∞, the rate constant k approaches:

Solution: As T approaches infinity, exp(-Ea/RT) → exp(0) = 1, so k → A.

Question 3 of 10
Which statement is incorrect regarding the effect of temperature on reaction rate?

Solution: Activation energy is assumed constant in the Arrhenius equation and does not decrease as temperature increases.

Question 4 of 10
The activation energy of a reaction can be determined from the rate constants at two temperatures using:

Solution: This is the standard integrated two-temperature Arrhenius relation using base-10 logarithms.

Question 5 of 10
For two reactions with activation energies Ea1 and Ea2 where Ea1 > Ea2, which reaction is more sensitive to temperature?

Solution: Higher activation energy results in a larger temperature dependence and greater sensitivity to temperature changes.

Question 6 of 10
What is the temperature coefficient (μ) if the rate constant doubles when temperature rises by 10 K?

Solution: By definition, the temperature coefficient is the ratio k(T+10) / kT, which equals 2.

Question 7 of 10
If ln A = 20 and Ea = 50 kJ mol-1, what is ln k at 300 K? (R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1)

Solution: ln k = ln A - (Ea / RT) = 20 - [50,000 / (8.314 × 300)] = 20 - 20.03 = -0.03 (approx 9.95 with correct numerical substitution).

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following conditions ensures that a collision is effective?

Solution: Effective collisions require both energy ≥ Ea and appropriate spatial orientation.

Question 9 of 10
The rate constant of a reaction is 1.5 × 10-4 s-1 at 300 K and 3.0 × 10-4 s-1 at 310 K. The activation energy is approximately:

Solution: Since the rate constant doubles for a 10 K rise from 300 K to 310 K, the activation energy is approximately 53.6 kJ mol-1.

Question 10 of 10
In the differential form of the Arrhenius equation, d(ln k)/dT = Ea / RT2, the term RT2 represents:

Solution: The RT2 term emerges from the differentiation of the exponential term with respect to absolute temperature T.
