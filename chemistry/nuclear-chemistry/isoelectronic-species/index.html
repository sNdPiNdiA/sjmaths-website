Nuclear Chemistry and Radioactivity

Isoelectronic Species

Isoelectronic species are atoms, ions, or molecules that possess an identical number of total electrons. Their physical and chemical properties vary significantly due to differences in nuclear charge despite sharing the same electronic configuration.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Definition and Electron Configuration

  • Species with the identical number of electrons are termed isoelectronic.
  • Isoelectronic atoms and ions share the same ground-state electronic configuration (e.g., Ne, Na+, F- all possess 10 electrons: 1s22s22p6).
  • Molecules or polyatomic ions can also be isoelectronic if their total valence or core electron sums match (e.g., CO and N2 both have 14 electrons).

Periodic Trends in Ionic Radius

  • Within an isoelectronic series, the nuclear charge (Z) increases progressively with atomic number.
  • As atomic number increases, the attractive force exerted by the nucleus on the constant electron cloud increases.
  • Consequently, ionic radius decreases monotonically as positive charge increases and negative charge decreases.

Impact on Physical Properties

  • Ionization energy increases across an isoelectronic series as the higher nuclear charge holds electrons more tightly.
  • Hydration energy increases for smaller cations within an isoelectronic series due to higher charge density.
  • Lattice energies of ionic crystals containing isoelectronic ions depend primarily on interionic distance and ionic charges.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher nuclear charge always results in a smaller ionic radius in isoelectronic series.

Observation: D-block metal ions (transition metal ions) with pseudo-noble gas configurations deviate slightly due to poor shielding by d-electrons. — Diffuse d-orbitals provide inefficient shielding, leading to higher effective nuclear charge felt by outer electrons than predicted by standard Slater rules.

Exception Isoelectronic diatomic molecules should have identical bond lengths.

Observation: CO and N2 exhibit slightly different bond strengths and force constants despite having 14 electrons. — Polarity differences and permanent dipole moments alter electron distribution and internuclear repulsion.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Radius Order Trick
Anion (-) > Neutral (0) > Cation (+)

Application: For any isoelectronic set, arrange in order of increasing atomic number Z; radius decreases as Z increases.

Electron Counting Shortcut
e- = Atomic Number - Charge

Application: Quickly verify isoelectronic sets by calculating total electrons including sign (e.g., O2- = 8 - (-2) = 10).

Valence Isoelectronic Rule
Group number sum rule for molecules

Application: Molecules from the same periodic groups (like CO2 and N2O) have equal total valence electrons and similar structures.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Isoelectronic species contain the exact same number of electrons.
  • For the series N3-, O2-, F-, Na+, Mg2+, and Al3+, all possess 10 electrons.
  • Al3+ has the smallest ionic radius (53 pm) due to its highest nuclear charge (Z = 13).
  • N3- has the largest ionic radius (171 pm) due to its lowest nuclear charge (Z = 7).
  • Isoelectronic diatomic molecules like N2 and CO both possess 14 electrons and are termed isostructural/isoelectronic.
  • Hydration energy of isoelectronic divalent cations increases with atomic number.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming isoelectronic species have the same physical and chemical properties; they differ widely due to varying nuclear charge.
  • Confusing atomic radius with isoelectronic ionic radius trends.
  • Failing to account for the sign of the charge when calculating total electrons for ions.
  • Assuming all species with the same mass number are isoelectronic.

⚡ Quick Revision: Isoelectronic Species

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electron Equivalence

Species having identical total electron counts form isoelectronic sets, sharing common ground-state electron configurations.

Size Contraction

Radius decreases sharply across an isoelectronic series as nuclear charge Z increases, concentrating the electron cloud.

Effective Nuclear Charge

Zeff dictates the pull on the constant electron cloud, scaling directly with atomic number.

Molecular Isoelectronic Systems

Molecules with matching total electron counts often display analogous bonding paradigms and molecular geometries.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Isoelectronic (Equal e-)
Species containing the same number of electrons.
Nuclear Charge (Z)
Total number of protons in the atomic nucleus.
Effective Nuclear Charge (Zeff)
Net positive charge experienced by valence electrons.
Ionic Radius (r)
Distance from the nucleus to the outer boundary of the ion's electron cloud.
Screening Effect (S)
Reduction in electrostatic attraction between nucleus and outer electrons.
Valence Electrons (Outer e-)
Electrons in the outermost principal energy level.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Radius ∝ 1 / Z for isoelectronic ions.
  • Ne, Na+, Mg2+, F-, O2- are isoelectronic with 10 electrons.
  • Anions are always larger than their parent neutral atom.
  • Cations are always smaller than their parent neutral atom.
  • Zeff increases with increasing atomic number in an isoelectronic series.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Isoelectronic species share electron counts but differ in protons.
  • Higher nuclear charge contracts the electron cloud.
  • Radius decreases as positive charge increases in ions.
  • Properties such as ionization energy and hydration enthalpy track with Zeff.

⚖️ Key Differences

Isoelectronic VS Isotopes

Isoelectronic species have the same number of electrons, whereas isotopes have the same number of protons but different neutrons.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following sets of species is isoelectronic with Argon (Z = 18)?

Explanation: Argon has 18 electrons. Cl- (17+1), K+ (19-1), and Ca2+ (20-2) all possess 18 electrons.

Question 2 of 18

Arrange the following isoelectronic ions in order of increasing ionic radius: O2-, F-, Na+, Mg2+.

Explanation: As nuclear charge (Z) increases from Mg (12) to O (8), radius decreases. Thus Mg2+ is smallest and O2- is largest.

Question 3 of 18

How many electrons are present in the isoelectronic species N3-, O2-, and F-?

Explanation: N (7+3=10), O (8+2=10), F (9+1=10); all contain 10 electrons.

Question 4 of 18

Which species is isoelectronic with CO?

Explanation: CO has 6 + 8 = 14 electrons. N2 has 7 + 7 = 14 electrons.

Question 5 of 18

In an isoelectronic series, effective nuclear charge (Zeff) ____.

Explanation: Zeff increases with atomic number Z because nuclear charge increases while the shielding electron count remains constant.

Question 6 of 18

Which of the following ions has the smallest radius?

Explanation: All are isoelectronic with 10 electrons. Al3+ has the highest nuclear charge (Z = 13), resulting in the smallest radius.

Question 8 of 18

Which molecule is isoelectronic with NO+?

Explanation: NO+ has 7 + 8 - 1 = 14 electrons. N2 has 14 electrons.

Question 9 of 18

The ionic radii of isoelectronic ions follow which proportionality with atomic number Z?

Explanation: Ionic radius is inversely proportional to nuclear charge Z in an isoelectronic series.

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following contains 18 electrons?

Explanation: Ca2+ (20-2=18), K+ (19-1=18), and Cl- (17+1=18) all have 18 electrons.

Question 11 of 18

Which species is isoelectronic with the hydronium ion (H3O+)?

Explanation: H3O+ has 3(1) + 8 - 1 = 10 electrons. NH3 has 7 + 3(1) = 10 electrons.

Question 12 of 18

Which ion has the largest radius in the series S2-, Cl-, K+, Ca2+?

Explanation: S2- has the lowest nuclear charge (Z = 16) among the 18-electron isoelectronic series, giving it the largest radius.

Question 13 of 18

Between Na+ and F-, which has a smaller radius?

Explanation: Na+ has Z = 11 while F- has Z = 9. Higher nuclear charge in Na+ pulls the 10 electrons closer.

Question 14 of 18

Which of the following pairs represents isoelectronic species with 36 electrons?

Explanation: Kr has atomic number 36. Br- has 35 + 1 = 36 electrons.

Question 15 of 18

What is the total number of electrons in the sulfate ion (SO42-)?

Explanation: S (16) + 4 × O (4 × 8 = 32) + 2 (charge) = 16 + 32 + 2 = 50 electrons.

Question 16 of 18

Which property generally decreases across an isoelectronic series from anion to cation?

Explanation: Ionic radius decreases progressively from anion to cation in an isoelectronic series.

Question 17 of 18

Which of the following is isoelectronic with cyanide ion (CN-)?

Explanation: CN- has 6 + 7 + 1 = 14 electrons. Both N2 (14) and CO (14) are isoelectronic with it.

Question 18 of 18

Which of the following sets contains only isoelectronic species?

Explanation: N3- (10), O2- (10), and Ne (10) all have exactly 10 electrons.
