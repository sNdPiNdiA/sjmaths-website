Average Life in Radioactivity

Average life is defined as the sum of the life spans of all radioactive nuclei divided by the total number of nuclei initially present. It represents the reciprocal of the radioactive decay constant (λ).

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mathematical Derivation and Definition

  • Average life (τ) is mathematically expressed as the integral of time multiplied by the rate of decay from t = 0 to t = ∞ divided by initial nuclei N0.
  • Substitution of the first-order rate differential yields τ = ∫0∞ t(λN0e-lambda t)dt / N0.
  • Integration by parts yields the standard reciprocal decay constant relation: τ = 1 / λ.
  • It denotes the time during which the number of undecayed nuclei decreases to 1/e (approximately 36.8%) of its original value.

Interconversion with Half-Life

  • Half-life (t1/2) is related to the decay constant via t1/2 = ln 2 / λ ≈ 0.693 / λ.
  • Combining t1/2 and τ expressions gives τ = t1/2 / 0.693 = 1.443 t1/2.
  • Average life is always longer than the half-life by a constant factor of approximately 1.443.
  • Radioactive decay constants are additive for branched decay pathways, affecting both effective half-life and effective average life.

Physical Significance and Decay Statistics

  • Unlike half-life, which measures the time required for 50% decay, average life represents the statistical expectation value of the lifetime of a radioactive nucleus.
  • In one average life span, 63.2% of the initial radioactive atoms undergo disintegration.
  • Average life remains completely independent of temperature, pressure, and chemical combination, adhering strictly to pure nuclear statistics.
  • It is extensively used in radioactive dating calculations and tracer kinetics to determine mean residence times.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Radioactive decay rate is constant over time for a given sample.

Observation: Apparent variation of decay constant in electron capture or internal conversion under extreme high pressures — Changes in inner-shell electron density alter electron capture probabilities slightly, though average life remains invariant under standard conditions.

Exception Average life is strictly constant for a specific radionuclide.

Observation: Mössbauer isotopes or isomeric transitions with environmental chemical state dependency — Chemical environment alters the chemical shift or electron density at the nucleus, marginally affecting internal conversion coefficients.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Average life is mathematically defined as the reciprocal of the decay constant (λ).
  • The relationship between average life (τ) and half-life (t1/2) is τ = 1.443 t1/2.
  • During a time interval equal to one average life, 63.2% of the radioactive substance disintegrates.
  • The fraction of radioactive nuclei remaining undecayed after time t equal to average life is 1/e.
  • Average life is independent of initial concentration, temperature, and pressure.
  • Units of average life are reciprocal to the units of the decay constant (λ).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing average life with half-life by omitting the 1.443 multiplication factor.
  • Assuming average life represents the time when 50% of the sample has decayed (which applies to half-life).
  • Mixing up units of time for τ and λ in numerical calculations.
  • Applying first-order average life formulas to second-order or complex consecutive decay kinetics without proper effective constants.

⚡ Quick Revision: Average Life in Radioactivity

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition of Average Life

Average life (τ) represents the statistical mean lifetime of radioactive atoms, calculated as the reciprocal of the decay constant λ.

Relation with Half-Life

The average life is always 1.443 times the half-life of the radioactive nuclide, derived from the integration of exponential decay laws.

Decay Statistics

At t = τ, the remaining undecayed fraction of radioactive nuclei falls to 1/e or 36.8% of the initial amount N0.

Independence of External Factors

Nuclear decay parameters like average life are strictly nuclear properties unaffected by physical and chemical conditions.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Average Life (τ)
Reciprocal of the radioactive decay constant. Unit: seconds or years.
Decay Constant (λ)
Fraction of nuclei decaying per unit time. Unit: s-1.
Half-Life (t1/2)
Time required for half of the radioactive nuclei to disintegrate.
Initial Nuclei (N0)
Number of radioactive nuclei present at time t = 0.
Remaining Nuclei (N)
Number of undecayed radioactive nuclei at time t.
Exponential Decay (N = N0e-λt)
Governing mathematical law for first-order radioactive disintegration.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • τ = 1 / λ
  • τ = 1.443 × t1/2
  • N / N0 = 1/e at t = τ
  • Percentage decayed in one average life = 63.2%
  • Percentage remaining in one average life = 36.8%

📌 Quick Recall

  • Average life is the mean life of all nuclei in a radioactive sample.
  • It is numerically equal to 1.443 times the half-life.
  • It follows first-order kinetics and remains constant for a given isotope.
  • Calculations involving τ utilize exponential decay equations.

⚖️ Key Differences

Average Life VS Half-Life

Average life is 1.443 times longer than half-life; 36.8% remains at average life versus 50% at half-life.
