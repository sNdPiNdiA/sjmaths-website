Nuclear Chemistry and Radioactivity

Nuclear Fusion

Nuclear fusion is the process by which multiple light atomic nuclei join together to form a heavier nucleus, accompanied by the release of substantial binding energy. This reaction powers stellar bodies and requires extremely high temperatures to overcome electrostatic repulsion between positively charged nuclei.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Energetics and Mass Defect in Fusion

  • Nuclear fusion is exothermic for nuclei lighter than iron-56 (2656Fe) due to an increase in binding energy per nucleon.
  • The mass defect (Δm) between the reactants and products is converted directly into energy according to Einstein's relation.
  • Light nuclei such as isotopes of hydrogen possess low binding energies per nucleon, making their fusion energetically favorable.

Coulomb Barrier and Ignition Temperature

  • Positively charged nuclei experience strong electrostatic repulsion (Coulomb barrier) that must be overcome for fusion to occur.
  • Extremely high thermal kinetic energies—corresponding to temperatures of 107 to 108 K—are required to achieve sufficient quantum mechanical tunneling.
  • In stellar interiors, gravitational confinement maintains the high density and temperature necessary to sustain the proton-proton chain and CNO cycle.

Controlled Fusion Reactions and Terrestrial Fuels

  • The primary terrestrial fusion reaction combines deuterium and tritium to yield helium-4 and a high-energy neutron.
  • Terrestrial confinement methods include magnetic confinement (tokamaks, stellarators) and inertial confinement (high-power lasers).
  • Deuterium is abundant in seawater, while tritium is bred in situ using lithium blankets via the reaction 36Li + 01n → 24He + 13H.

Units: mp, mn, mnucleus in atomic mass units (u)

Conditions: Stellar core conditions (T > 107 K)

Mass-Energy Equivalence
ΔE = Δm × c2
Conditions: Applies to all nuclear reactions involving mass defect
Units: ΔE in Joules, Δm in kg, c in m s-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Fusion of all atomic nuclei is exothermic and releases energy.

Observation: Fusion of nuclei heavier than iron-56 is endothermic. — Iron-56 has the highest binding energy per nucleon; fusing heavier nuclei decreases the average binding energy per nucleon, consuming energy.

Exception Higher temperatures always increase nuclear reaction rates linearly.

Observation: At extremely high temperatures, Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distributions shift, but plasma instabilities can cause losses. — Turbulent transport and Bremsstrahlung radiation losses can exceed the fusion power output if the Lawson criterion is not met.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Binding Energy Curve Rule
A < 56 → Fusion releases energy; A > 56 → Fission releases energy

Application: Quickly determine whether a given nuclide undergoes fission or fusion spontaneously based on mass number.

D-T Energy Yield Mnemonic
D + T = He (3.5 MeV) + n (14.1 MeV) = 17.6 MeV Total

Application: Instantly recall energy partitioning in the deuterium-tritium fusion reaction for numerical questions.

Lawson Criterion Memory
n × τ × T > Threshold

Application: Remember that density (n), confinement time (τ), and temperature (T) must simultaneously exceed critical values for a self-sustaining fusion reactor.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The total energy released in the Deuterium-Tritium fusion reaction is 17.6 MeV.
  • Iron-56 (2656Fe) represents the peak of the binding energy curve per nucleon.
  • Quantum mechanical tunneling allows nuclei to penetrate the Coulomb barrier at temperatures lower than classical calculations predict.
  • The proton-proton chain is the dominant energy source for stars of the mass of the Sun or smaller.
  • Magnetic confinement in tokamaks utilizes toroidal magnetic fields to isolate high-temperature plasma from reactor walls.
  • Bremsstrahlung radiation losses increase with plasma temperature and place a lower limit on ignition temperatures.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the direction of the binding energy curve: fusion occurs for low A, fission occurs for high A.
  • Assuming nuclear fusion produces long-lived radioactive waste comparable to nuclear fission reactors.
  • Neglecting the kinetic energy carried away by the neutron in the D-T reaction (14.1 MeV out of 17.6 MeV).
  • Forgetting that high temperature is specifically needed to overcome electrostatic Coulomb repulsion, not nuclear forces.

⚡ Quick Revision: Nuclear Fusion

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Energetics of Fusion

Nuclear fusion combines light nuclides into heavier ones, releasing energy via mass defect according to ΔE = Δm c2. It is the primary energy source of stellar interiors.

Coulomb Barrier & Temperature

Extremely high thermal energy (107-108 K) is mandatory to overcome electrostatic repulsion between positively charged nuclei and enable nuclear interaction.

Stellar Nucleosynthesis

Stars generate energy primarily through the proton-proton chain and the carbon-nitrogen-oxygen (CNO) cycle, fusing hydrogen into helium continuously.

Controlled Terrestrial Fusion

Terrestrial reactors utilize deuterium-tritium fuels requiring advanced magnetic or inertial confinement systems to achieve the Lawson criterion for net energy gain.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Nuclear Fusion (N/A)
Reaction where light nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus with mass loss.
Mass Defect (Δm)
Difference between rest mass of nucleus and sum of constituent nucleons.
Binding Energy (BE)
Energy required to disassemble a nucleus into constituent free nucleons.
Coulomb Barrier (Vc)
Electrostatic potential energy barrier between two interacting charged nuclei.
Lawson Criterion (n τ T)
Figure of merit for achieving a self-sustaining controlled thermonuclear fusion reactor.
Proton-Proton Chain (p-p chain)
Set of fusion reactions by which stars convert hydrogen to helium.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • D + T → 4He + n + 17.6 MeV
  • Peak binding energy per nucleon occurs at A = 56 (Iron).
  • Fusion is exothermic for A < 56.
  • Ignition temperature for D-T fusion is approximately 108 K.
  • Mass-energy relation: 1 u is equivalent to 931.5 MeV.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Fusion requires overcoming the electrostatic Coulomb barrier via extreme thermal energy.
  • The D-T reaction is the most viable candidate for controlled terrestrial energy generation.
  • Stellar energy relies on continuous proton-proton fusion cycles.
  • Fusion produces clean energy with minimal radioactive byproducts compared to fission.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nuclear Fusion VS Nuclear Fission

Fusion combines light nuclei to form heavier ones; fission splits heavy nuclei into lighter ones.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following nuclei has the highest binding energy per nucleon?

Explanation: Iron-56 (2656Fe) possesses the highest binding energy per nucleon among stable nuclides, making it the most stable nucleus.

Question 2 of 18

In the deuterium-tritium fusion reaction, what is the energy carried away by the neutron?

Explanation: The D-T reaction releases 17.6 MeV total, partitioned as 3.5 MeV to the alpha particle (helium-4) and 14.1 MeV to the neutron.

Question 3 of 18

Why is an extremely high temperature required for nuclear fusion to take place?

Explanation: High thermal kinetic energy is necessary for nuclei to approach each other closely enough to overcome the Coulomb electrostatic repulsion.

Question 4 of 18

Which reaction represents the primary initial step of the proton-proton chain in stellar cores?

Explanation: The first step of the p-p chain involves two protons combining to form a deuteron, a positron, and an electron neutrino.

Question 5 of 18

Nuclear fusion is exothermic for nuclei with mass number (A):

Explanation: For nuclei with A < 56, combining them results in an increase of binding energy per nucleon, which releases energy (exothermic).

Question 6 of 18

What is the primary technical challenge in achieving controlled thermonuclear fusion in a laboratory?

Explanation: Maintaining plasma confinement at temperatures exceeding 108 K without damaging reactor walls is the primary engineering hurdle.

Question 7 of 18

In mass-energy equivalence calculations, 1 atomic mass unit (u) is equivalent to approximately:

Explanation: Multiplying 1 u (1.6605 × 10-27 kg) by c2 yields approximately 931.5 MeV.

Question 8 of 18

Which confinement method uses powerful magnetic fields shaped in a torus?

Explanation: Tokamaks and stellarators use toroidal magnetic fields to confine high-temperature plasma.

Question 9 of 18

Tritium used in D-T fusion reactors is commonly bred from which element?

Explanation: Tritium is bred in reactor blankets by bombarding lithium-6 with neutrons: 36Li + 01n → 24He + 13H.

Question 10 of 18

What does the Lawson criterion relate in fusion reactor physics?

Explanation: The Lawson criterion establishes the minimum product of plasma density, confinement time, and temperature required for ignition.

Question 11 of 18

Why does the fusion of iron-56 require an input of energy rather than releasing it?

Explanation: Because iron-56 sits at the peak of the binding energy curve, any further combination decreases binding energy per nucleon, making the process endothermic.

Question 12 of 18

Which subatomic particle is emitted alongside helium-4 in the standard D-T fusion reaction?

Explanation: The D-T reaction produces a helium-4 nucleus and a free neutron (12H + 13H → 24He + 01n).

Question 13 of 18

Quantum mechanical tunneling assists nuclear fusion at lower temperatures because it allows particles to:

Explanation: Tunneling permits nuclei to penetrate the Coulomb barrier even when their kinetic energy is lower than the barrier peak.

Question 14 of 18

In stellar nucleosynthesis, the CNO cycle acts catalytically using which nuclei?

Explanation: The CNO cycle uses carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen nuclei as catalysts to convert hydrogen into helium in massive stars.

Question 15 of 18

What is the primary advantage of nuclear fusion over nuclear fission as an energy source?

Explanation: Fusion utilizes abundant light isotopes (like deuterium from water) and does not yield long-lived actinides characteristic of fission waste.

Question 16 of 18

What is the mass defect (Δm) formula for a nucleus of mass mnucleus containing Z protons and (A-Z) neutrons?

Explanation: Mass defect is the total mass of the individual separated nucleons minus the mass of the bound nucleus.

Question 17 of 18

Which isotope of hydrogen is radioactive with a half-life of about 12.3 years?

Explanation: Tritium (13H) is a radioactive isotope of hydrogen decaying via beta-minus emission with a half-life of 12.3 years.

Question 18 of 18

In inertial confinement fusion, how is the fuel pellet compressed?

Explanation: Inertial confinement uses high-power lasers or particle beams to rapidly heat and compress a tiny fuel pellet symmetrically.

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the mass defect when a helium-4 nucleus is formed from 2 protons and 2 neutrons. (Mass of 11H = 1.007825 u, Mass of 01n = 1.008665 u, Mass of 24He = 4.00150 u)

Solution: Mass = 2(1.007825) + 2(1.008665) = 4.03298 u. Mass defect = 4.03298 u - 4.00150 u = 0.03048 u (approx 0.03038 u).

Question 2 of 10
Which condition is NOT required by the Lawson criterion for a sustained fusion reactor?

Solution: Lawson criterion involves density, confinement time, and temperature; fission fragments are irrelevant to fusion.

Question 3 of 10
What is the role of lithium in deuterium-tritium fusion reactors?

Solution: Lithium blankets surrounding the reactor absorb neutrons to breed tritium fuel (36Li + 01n → 24He + 13H).

Question 4 of 10
In stellar nucleosynthesis, what happens after the core exhausts its hydrogen supply?

Solution: Core hydrogen exhaustion leads to gravitational contraction, increasing core temperature until helium fusion (triple-alpha process) ignites.

Question 5 of 10
The process by which two protons fuse to form a deuteron in the p-2p chain involves the simultaneous conversion of a proton into a neutron. Which particle is emitted to conserve charge and lepton number?

Solution: p + p → 2H + e+ + νe. A positron (e+) and electron neutrino (νe) are emitted.

Question 6 of 10
Why is deuterium considered an attractive fuel for nuclear fusion compared to normal hydrogen (protium)?

Solution: The reaction cross-section for deuterium fusion is significantly higher than that of the initial proton-proton reaction, making it practical for terrestrial applications.

Question 7 of 10
What accounts for the enormous binding energy released during nuclear fusion of light elements?

Solution: Energy release in fusion stems directly from the mass defect converted according to Einstein's mass-energy equivalence.

Question 8 of 10
In a tokamak reactor, what is the primary purpose of the toroidal and poloidal magnetic fields?

Solution: Magnetic fields create a magnetic bottle that prevents high-temperature plasma from touching and destroying the reactor walls.

Question 9 of 10
Which nuclear process dominates the energy production of stars significantly more massive than the Sun?

Solution: In stars heavier than the Sun, higher core temperatures make the Carbon-Nitrogen-Oxygen (CNO) cycle the dominant fusion mechanism.

Question 10 of 10
If 1.0 microgram of mass is completely converted into energy in a fusion reaction, how much energy is released? (c = 3 × 108 m s-1)

Solution: E = Δm × c2 = (1.0 × 10-9 kg) × (3 × 108 m s-1)2 = 9 × 1010 J.
