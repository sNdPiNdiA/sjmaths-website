Nuclear Chemistry and Radioactivity

Isobars

Isobars are nuclides of different chemical elements that share the exact same mass number but possess different atomic numbers. They differ in both their proton-to-neutron ratios and their chemical properties due to varying nuclear charge.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Atomic and Nuclear Characteristics of Isobars

  • Isobars have identical mass numbers (A) but distinct atomic numbers (Z) and neutron numbers (N).
  • Because their nuclear charges (Z) differ, isobars belong to different chemical elements and exhibit completely different chemical behavior.
  • The nuclear binding energies of isobars vary, often leading to beta decay transitions between them to achieve minimum energy states.

Radioactive Interconversion via Beta Decay

  • Beta-minus decay converts a neutron into a proton, transforming a nuclide into its isobar with Z + 1.
  • Positron emission or electron capture converts a proton into a neutron, transforming a nuclide into its isobar with Z - 1.
  • Isobaric chains terminate at the most stable isobar, which represents the minimum of the mass parabola for a given mass number A.

Mirror Nuclei and Nuclear Symmetry

  • Mirror nuclei are a special subclass of isobars where the number of protons in one nucleus equals the number of neutrons in the other and vice versa (e.g., 13H and 23He).
  • Mirror nuclei provide direct experimental evidence for the charge independence and isotopic spin symmetry of strong nuclear forces.
  • Mass differences in mirror nuclei are primarily attributed to Coulomb electrostatic repulsion between protons and the neutron-proton mass discrepancy.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Isobars are atoms of different chemical elements.

Observation: Nuclear isomers — Nuclear isomers share identical Z and A but differ only in their nuclear energy states, whereas isobars differ in Z.

Exception Isobars exhibit completely different chemical properties.

Observation: Biochemical behavior of radio-isobaric tracers — In certain medical applications, specific isobaric decay products mimic metabolic pathways if chemically bonded to carrier molecules, though elemental identities remain distinct.

Exception All isobars have identical mass numbers (A).

Observation: Nuclidic mass defect variations — While mass numbers (sum of nucleons) are identical, actual atomic masses (in amu) differ slightly due to differences in total nuclear binding energy.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Isobars have the same mass number but different atomic numbers.
  • Mirror nuclei are a specific type of isobars with swapped proton and neutron counts.
  • Isobars belong to different chemical elements with distinct electronic configurations.
  • Beta decay processes interconvert one isobar into another within the same mass chain.
  • Actual isotopic masses differ among isobars due to variations in mass defect and binding energy.
  • The nuclear mass parabola predicts beta stability for isobars of odd and even mass numbers.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing isobars with isotopes; remember isobars have different atomic numbers while isotopes have the same.
  • Assuming isobars have identical atomic masses in atomic mass units (amu); only their mass numbers (A) are identical.
  • Failing to account for conservation of nucleon number during isobaric radioactive transformations.
  • Confusing mirror nuclei with isobars in general; mirror nuclei represent a strict symmetric subset.

⚡ Quick Revision: Isobars

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition of Isobars

Nuclides with identical mass numbers (A) but different atomic numbers (Z) are termed isobars. They represent distinct chemical elements with unique physical and chemical characteristics.

Radioactive Decay Pathways

Beta decay processes (β-, β+, and electron capture) facilitate transitions between isobars along an isobaric mass chain until the most energetically stable isobar is reached.

Mirror Nuclei

Mirror nuclei are isobars where the neutron number of one equals the proton number of the other. They serve as fundamental tools for studying charge symmetry in nuclear forces.

Mass Defect and Stability

Although mass numbers are integer values, exact rest masses of isobars differ based on nuclear binding energy differences dictated by the semi-empirical mass formula.

Nuclides with identical atomic number Z but different mass number A.
Isotones (N = A - Z)
Nuclides with identical neutron number N.

Isobars with interchanged numbers of protons and neutrons.
Mass Number (A)
Total sum of protons and neutrons in a nucleus.
Atomic Number (Z)
Total number of protons in a nucleus.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Isobars belong to different chemical elements.
  • Mass number (A) remains constant across isobars.
  • Beta-minus decay increases atomic number by 1 within isobaric chains.
  • Positron emission decreases atomic number by 1.
  • Mirror nuclei confirm the charge independence of nuclear forces.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Isobars share mass numbers but differ in chemical identity.

  • Mirror nuclei illustrate nuclear symmetry principles.
  • Binding energy differences account for exact mass variations.

⚖️ Key Differences

Isobars VS Isotopes

Isobars have the same mass number (A), whereas isotopes have the same atomic number (Z).

What remains constant for any given set of isobars?

Explanation: By definition, isobars are nuclides that have the same mass number (A).

Question 3 of 19

Do isobars exhibit similar chemical properties?

Explanation: Chemical properties depend on the atomic number (number of protons/electrons), which differs among isobars.

Question 4 of 19

Which decay process converts a nuclide into its isobar with Z + 1?

Explanation: In beta-minus decay, a neutron converts into a proton, increasing Z by 1 while keeping A constant.

Question 5 of 19

Which pair represents mirror nuclei?

Explanation: In 13H (Z=1, N=2) and 23He (Z=2, N=1), the proton and neutron numbers are swapped.

Question 6 of 19

What is the change in atomic number during positron emission?

Explanation: Positron emission converts a proton into a neutron, decreasing the atomic number Z by 1.

Question 7 of 19

Which nuclear property is identical in isobars?

Explanation: The total number of nucleons is the mass number A, which is identical for all isobars.

Question 8 of 19

Electron capture results in which of the following changes?

Explanation: Electron capture combines an inner shell electron with a proton to form a neutron, decreasing Z by 1 while keeping A constant.

Question 9 of 19

Why do isobars have different atomic masses in amu despite having the same mass number?

Explanation: Mass defect varies because different isobars have different binding energies, and mass numbers are integers while actual masses are measured in amu.

Question 10 of 19

Which of the following is true regarding isobaric mass chains?

Explanation: In an isobaric chain, unstable nuclei undergo beta decays to reach the most stable isobar (minimum mass).

Question 11 of 19

What is the neutron number of 2656Fe?

Explanation: N = A - Z = 56 - 26 = 30.

Question 12 of 19

Which of the following nuclide pairs are isotones rather than isobars?

Explanation: 614C has N = 8 and 816O has N = 8, making them isotones.

Question 13 of 19

Mirror nuclei provide evidence for which nuclear force property?

Explanation: Similar binding energy characteristics in mirror nuclei demonstrate that nuclear forces are charge-independent.

Question 14 of 19

Which element is isobaric with 714N?

Explanation: 614C has mass number 14, matching 714N.

Question 15 of 19

In the context of nuclear chemistry, what does 'A' represent?

Explanation: A represents the mass number, which is the sum of protons and neutrons.

Question 16 of 19

Can two stable isobars exist for the same odd mass number A?

Explanation: Mattauch's isobar rule states that two stable isobars differing by one atomic number do not exist; for odd A, there is normally only one stable isobar.

Question 17 of 19

What happens to the mass number during alpha decay?

Explanation: Alpha decay emits an alpha particle (24He), reducing the mass number by 4.

Question 18 of 19

Which particle is emitted during beta-minus decay?

Explanation: Beta-minus decay involves the emission of an electron (-10e).

Question 19 of 19

What is the primary factor determining nuclear stability among isobars?

Explanation: The optimal neutron-to-proton ratio determines the most stable isobar within a mass chain.
