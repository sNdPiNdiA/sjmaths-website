Nuclear Chemistry and Radioactivity

Nuclear Fission

Nuclear fission is the fragmentation of a heavy atomic nucleus into two lighter nuclei of comparable mass accompanied by the release of a massive amount of energy. The process is initiated by the absorption of a neutron and yields excess neutrons that propagate chain reactions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mechanism of Induced Nuclear Fission

  • Absorption of a slow (thermal) neutron by 92U235 forms an unstable compound nucleus 92U236*.
  • The compound nucleus undergoes severe electrostatic repulsion between protons, causing it to deform into a dumbbell shape and rupture.
  • Fission fragments follow a asymmetric mass distribution curve, typically peaking around mass numbers 95 (light group) and 137 (heavy group).
  • An average of 2.5 neutrons are emitted instantaneously per fission event of 92U235.

Energy Balances and Mass Defect

  • The total binding energy per nucleon increases from about 7.6 MeV in uranium to about 8.5 MeV in the fission products.
  • The mass defect (Δm) translates directly into kinetic energy of fission fragments, neutrons, and subsequent gamma radiation.
  • Approximately 200 MeV of energy is released per individual 92U235 fission reaction.
  • Prompt gamma rays and radioactive decay of neutron-rich fission fragments account for the remainder of the energy dissipation.

Nuclear Chain Reactions and Control

  • A critical mass is the minimum mass of fissile material required to sustain a self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction where k = 1.
  • Control rods composed of neutron absorbers such as cadmium (Cd) or boron (B) regulate the neutron flux in nuclear reactors.
  • Moderators like heavy water (D2O) or graphite thermalize fast neutrons to increase the fission cross-section of 92U235.
  • Uncontrolled chain reactions form the operational basis of nuclear weapons based on fast fission.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception All heavy elements undergo spontaneous fission readily

Observation: Spontaneous fission half-lives are extremely long for most nuclei except very heavy transuranic elements — Alpha decay is kinetically favored over fission for lighter actinides due to high Coulomb barrier requirements for splitting into two massive fragments.

Exception All uranium isotopes are fissile with thermal neutrons

Observation: 92U238 is fissionable only by fast neutrons (>1 MeV), not thermal neutrons — Even-even nuclei require the pairing energy contribution supplied only by high-energy neutron impact to cross the activation fission barrier.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Fission of one 92U235 nucleus yields approximately 200 MeV of energy.
  • Thermal neutrons have kinetic energies around 0.025 eV corresponding to room temperature.
  • Cadmium and boron act as effective neutron poisons due to their high neutron capture cross-sections.
  • Heavy water (D2O) acts as both a moderator and a coolant in pressurized heavy-water reactors.
  • Mass number conservation and atomic number conservation apply strictly in all balanced nuclear fission reactions.
  • Plutonium-239 (94Pu239) is a synthetic fissile material produced by neutron capture on 92U238.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing nuclear fission with nuclear fusion; remember fission splits heavy nuclei, fusion combines light nuclei.
  • Assuming 92U238 undergoes fission with thermal neutrons, whereas it only undergoes fission with fast neutrons.
  • Forgetting that mass defect units must be in atomic mass units (amu) when applying the 931.5 MeV conversion factor.
  • Assuming the fission products are always identical elements; fission yields a broad spectrum of unequal fragment pairs.

⚡ Quick Revision: Nuclear Fission

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Compound Nucleus Formation

Thermal neutron capture creates an excited compound nucleus whose internal excitation energy exceeds the fission activation energy barrier.

Asymmetric Mass Distribution

Nuclear fission preferentially splits heavy nuclei into two unequal fragments with mass numbers centered around 95 and 137.

Neutron Economy

Secondary neutrons sustain the chain reaction, requiring moderators to slow them down and control rods to absorb excess flux.

Energy Yield Calculation

The mass defect between reactants and products is converted into kinetic and radiant energy via Einstein's relation ΔE = Δm · c2.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Fissile Material (Nuclide that undergoes fission with thermal neutrons)
Examples include 92U235, 94Pu239, and 92U233.
Fissionable Material (Nuclide that undergoes fission with fast neutrons)
Includes 92U238 and 90Th232.
Moderator (Substance used to slow down neutrons)
Common examples include graphite, heavy water (D2O), and ordinary water (H2O).
Control Rods (Neutron-absorbing rods)
Regulate reactor power by capturing excess neutrons using boron or cadmium.
Critical Mass (Minimum mass for self-sustaining chain reaction)
Mass where neutron production equals neutron loss (k = 1).
Mass Defect (Δm)
Difference between rest mass of nucleus and sum of individual constituent nucleons.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Energy released per 92U235 fission = ~200 MeV
  • Conversion factor: 1 amu = 931.5 MeV
  • Average secondary neutrons per 92U235 fission = ~2.5
  • Critical condition for chain reaction: k = 1
  • Thermal neutron energy = ~0.025 eV

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nuclear fission splits heavy nuclei into lighter fission fragments and excess neutrons.
  • Mass defect converts directly into high kinetic energy via ΔE = Δm · c2.
  • Chain reactions are regulated using moderators and control rods in nuclear reactors.
  • Asymmetric mass splitting yields products with mass numbers near 95 and 137.

⚖️ Key Differences

Fissile VS Fissionable

Fissile materials undergo fission with low-energy thermal neutrons, whereas fissionable materials require high-energy fast neutrons.
