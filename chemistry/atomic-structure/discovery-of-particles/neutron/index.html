Neutron न्यूट्रॉन (Neutron)

The neutron is a subatomic nucleon with zero net electric charge and a mass slightly greater than that of a proton. It was discovered by James Chadwick in 1932 by bombarding beryllium with alpha particles. न्यूट्रॉन एक उप-परमाण्विक न्यूक्लियॉन (subatomic nucleon) है जिस पर कोई शुद्ध विद्युत आवेश नहीं होता है और इसका द्रव्यमान प्रोटॉन के द्रव्यमान से थोड़ा अधिक होता है। इसकी खोज 1932 में जेम्स चैडविक ने बेरिलियम पर अल्फा कणों की बमबारी करके की थी।

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Discovery and Fundamental Properties खोज और मूलभूत गुण

  • Discovered by James Chadwick in 1932 using alpha particle bombardment of 9Be yielding carbon and penetrating radiation.
  • Has a rest mass of 1.6749 × 10-27 kg (1.00866 u), which is approximately 0.14% heavier than a proton.
  • Possesses zero electrical charge and an intrinsic magnetic dipole moment due to internal quark spin distributions.
  • Classified as a hadron and specifically a baryon, composed of one up quark and two down quarks (udd).
  • 1932 में जेम्स चैडविक (James Chadwick) द्वारा 9Be पर अल्फा कणों की बमबारी से कार्बन और भेदी विकिरण (penetrating radiation) प्राप्त करके खोजा गया।
  • इसका विराम द्रव्यमान (rest mass) 1.6749 × 10^-27 kg (1.00866 u) है, जो प्रोटॉन से लगभग 0.14% अधिक भारी है।
  • इसमें शून्य विद्युत आवेश होता है और आंतरिक क्वार्क स्पिन वितरण के कारण एक आंतरिक चुंबकीय द्विध्रुव आघूर्ण (magnetic dipole moment) होता है।
  • इसे एक हैड्रॉन (hadron) और विशेष रूप से एक बेरियॉन (baryon) के रूप में वर्गीकृत किया गया है, जो एक अप क्वार्क (up quark) और दो डाउन क्वार्क (down quarks) (udd) से बना है।

Nuclear Stability and N/Z Ratio नाभिकीय स्थिरता और N/Z अनुपात

  • The stability of an atomic nucleus depends critically on the neutron-to-proton (N/Z) ratio within the valley of stability.
  • For light nuclei (Z ≤ 20), the stable N/Z ratio is approximately 1:1.
  • For heavier nuclei, electrostatic repulsion requires an excess of neutrons, driving the N/Z ratio up to approximately 1.5:1 for bismuth.
  • Nuclei with magic numbers of neutrons (2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126) exhibit exceptional nuclear stability.
  • परमाणु नाभिक की स्थिरता स्थिरता की घाटी (valley of stability) के भीतर न्यूट्रॉन-प्रोटॉन (N/Z) अनुपात पर महत्वपूर्ण रूप से निर्भर करती है।
  • हल्के नाभिकों (Z <= 20) के लिए, स्थिर N/Z अनुपात लगभग 1:1 होता है।
  • भारी नाभिकों के लिए, स्थिर वैद्युत प्रतिकर्षण (electrostatic repulsion) के कारण अतिरिक्त न्यूट्रॉन की आवश्यकता होती है, जिससे बिस्मथ के लिए N/Z अनुपात लगभग 1.5:1 तक बढ़ जाता है।
  • न्यूट्रॉन की जादुई संख्याओं (magic numbers) (2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126) वाले नाभिक असाधारण नाभिकीय स्थिरता प्रदर्शित करते हैं।

Interactions and Applications अन्योन्यक्रिया और अनुप्रयोग

  • Free neutrons are unstable outside the nucleus, undergoing beta-minus decay with a half-life of roughly 879 seconds.
  • Because they lack charge, neutrons do not experience Coulombic repulsion, making them ideal probes for nuclear fission and chain reactions.
  • Thermal neutrons (kinetic energy ~0.025 eV) are highly effective at inducing fission in fissile isotopes like 235U.
  • Neutron activation analysis utilizes neutron capture to transform stable isotopes into radioactive ones for elemental composition determination.
  • मुक्त न्यूट्रॉन नाभिक के बाहर अस्थिर होते हैं, जो लगभग 879 सेकंड के अर्ध-आयु काल (half-life) के साथ बीटा-माइनस क्षय (beta-minus decay) से गुजरते हैं।
  • आवेश न होने के कारण, न्यूट्रॉन कूलम्ब प्रतिकर्षण का अनुभव नहीं करते हैं, जो उन्हें नाभिकीय विखंडन (nuclear fission) और श्रृंखला अभिक्रियाओं के लिए आदर्श प्रोब बनाता है।
  • तापीय न्यूट्रॉन (thermal neutrons) (गतिज ऊर्जा ~0.025 eV) 235U जैसे विखंडनीय समस्थानिकों (fissile isotopes) में विखंडन प्रेरित करने में अत्यधिक प्रभावी होते हैं।
  • न्यूट्रॉन सक्रियण विश्लेषण (neutron activation analysis) स्थिर समस्थानिकों को रेडियोधर्मी में बदलने के लिए न्यूट्रॉन प्रग्रहण (neutron capture) का उपयोग करता है ताकि तात्विक संरचना का निर्धारण किया जा सके।

Observation: Protium (1H) — The most common hydrogen isotope consists solely of one proton and one electron with zero neutrons. अवलोकन: प्रोटियम (1H) — सबसे सामान्य हाइड्रोजन समस्थानिक में केवल एक प्रोटॉन और एक इलेक्ट्रॉन होता है, जिसमें शून्य न्यूट्रॉन होते हैं।

Exception Free subatomic particles remain stable indefinitely.मुक्त उप-परमाण्विक कण अनिश्चित काल तक स्थिर रहते हैं।

Observation: Free neutron — Unbound neutrons undergo spontaneous radioactive beta decay because the neutron mass exceeds the combined mass of a proton, electron, and antineutrino. अवलोकन: मुक्त न्यूट्रॉन — अबद्ध न्यूट्रॉन स्वतः रेडियोधर्मी बीटा क्षय से गुजरते हैं क्योंकि न्यूट्रॉन का द्रव्यमान प्रोटॉन, इलेक्ट्रॉन और एंटीन्यूट्रिनो के संयुक्त द्रव्यमान से अधिक होता है।

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The neutron was discovered by James Chadwick in 1932.
  • The nuclear reaction for neutron discovery is 94Be + 42α → 126C + 10n.
  • The rest mass of a neutron is 1.6749 × 10-27 kg.
  • Free neutrons decay via beta emission into protons, electrons, and electron antineutrinos.
  • Thermal neutrons possess kinetic energies around 0.025 eV.
  • The quark composition of a neutron is udd (one up, two down).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming neutrons have a positive or negative charge; neutrons are strictly neutral particles.
  • Confusing the mass of a neutron with that of a proton; neutrons are slightly heavier.
  • Believing free neutrons are completely stable; they decay with a half-life of ~10 minutes.
  • Forgetting that ordinary hydrogen (protium) contains zero neutrons.

⚡ Quick Revision: Neutron

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Discovery Mechanics

Chadwick bombarded beryllium foil with alpha particles, observing highly penetrating, neutral radiation capable of ejecting protons from paraffin.

Mass and Charge Characteristics

Neutrons carry zero electrical charge and possess a mass slightly exceeding the proton, contributing significantly to nuclear mass without altering atomic number.

Radioactive Decay

Free neutrons are unstable and decay spontaneously into protons, electrons, and antineutrinos with a well-defined half-life.

Nuclear Reactions

Due to lack of charge, thermal neutrons penetrate nuclear potential barriers easily, driving fission chain reactions in heavy elements.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Neutron (10n)
Neutral subatomic particle found in atomic nuclei.
Isotopes (Nuclide variants)
Atoms with identical atomic number Z but differing neutron numbers N.
Thermal Neutrons (Slow neutrons)
Neutrons in thermal equilibrium with their surroundings (~0.025 eV).
Fast Neutrons (High-energy neutrons)
Neutrons with kinetic energies greater than 1 MeV.
Magic Numbers (Nuclear shell numbers)
Neutron counts yielding exceptionally stable closed nuclear shells.
Baryon (Hadron sub-class)
Composite subatomic particle made of three quarks.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Discovery year: 1932 by James Chadwick
  • Mass: 1.6749 × 10-27 kg
  • Quark structure: udd
  • Half-life of free neutron: ~879 seconds
  • Element with zero neutrons: Protium (1H)

📌 Quick Recall

  • Neutrons provide the strong nuclear force glue without adding electrostatic repulsion.
  • N/Z ratio dictates nuclear stability across the periodic table.
  • Thermal neutrons initiate nuclear fission in heavy reactors.
  • Free neutrons decay via beta-minus emission.

⚖️ Key Differences

Fast Neutron VS Thermal Neutron

Fast neutrons have high kinetic energy (>1 MeV), whereas thermal neutrons are slowed to room temperature energies (~0.025 eV).

Question 1 of 10
What experimental observation led Chadwick to conclude that the neutral radiation consisted of particles with a mass comparable to a proton?

Solution: Chadwick measured the recoil velocities of protons in paraffin wax bombarded by the neutral radiation, using conservation of momentum to deduce the neutron mass.

Question 2 of 10
Why are thermal neutrons more effective than fast neutrons in inducing fission in 235U?

Solution: The fission cross-section for 235U is inversely proportional to velocity, making slow thermal neutrons much more likely to be captured.

Question 3 of 10
Calculate the approximate nuclear radius of an aluminum nucleus (A = 27) given R0 = 1.2 fm.

Solution: R = R0 A^(1/3) = 1.2 × (27)^(1/3) = 1.2 × 3 = 3.6 fm.

Question 4 of 10
Which isotope falls outside the standard stability belt and typically undergoes beta-minus decay?

Solution: Neutron-rich nuclides lie above the valley of stability and convert excess neutrons to protons via beta-minus decay.

Question 5 of 10
What is the baryon number of a single neutron?

Solution: Since a neutron is composed of three quarks (baryon), its baryon number is +1.

Question 6 of 10
In terms of quark flavor, what change occurs during the beta-minus decay of a neutron?

Solution: Beta decay of a neutron involves a down quark converting into an up quark via weak interaction (udd → uud + e- + ν_e).

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following describes the magnetic moment of a neutron despite its zero net charge?

Solution: Although neutral overall, the spatial distribution of charged quarks inside the neutron generates a non-zero magnetic dipole moment.

Question 8 of 10
What is the role of a moderator in a nuclear reactor containing neutrons?

Solution: Moderators like heavy water or graphite slow down fast fission neutrons to thermal energies to sustain the chain reaction.

Question 9 of 10
If a nucleus has an excess of protons relative to neutrons, which decay mode is most probable?

Solution: Proton-rich nuclei increase stability by converting a proton into a neutron via positron emission or electron capture.

Question 10 of 10
What is the approximate density of nuclear matter based on the constant nuclear radius parameter?

Solution: Nuclear matter is extremely dense, on the order of 2.3 × 10^17 kg/m^3, because mass is packed into the tiny volume defined by R = R0 A^(1/3).
