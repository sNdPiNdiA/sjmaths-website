Characteristics of Fundamental Particles and Atomic Structure मूलभूत कणों के लक्षण और परमाणु संरचना (Characteristics of Fundamental Particles and Atomic Structure)

Atoms consist of a dense central nucleus composed of protons and neutrons, surrounded by extranuclear electrons in discrete quantum states. The fundamental characteristics of these subatomic particles define the mass, charge, and chemical behavior of all elements. परमाणु एक सघन केंद्रीय नाभिक (nucleus) से बने होते हैं, जिसमें प्रोटॉन और न्यूट्रॉन होते हैं, जो असतत क्वांटम अवस्थाओं (discrete quantum states) में बाह्य-नाभिकीय इलेक्ट्रॉनों से घिरे होते हैं। इन उप-परमाणु कणों के मूलभूत लक्षण सभी तत्वों के द्रव्यमान, आवेश और रासायनिक व्यवहार को निर्धारित करते हैं।

Fundamental Subatomic Particles मूलभूत उप-परमाणु कण (Fundamental Subatomic Particles)

  • The electron was discovered by J.J. Thomson via cathode ray tube experiments, possessing a specific charge-to-mass ratio (e/m) of 1.758 × 1011 C/kg.
  • Protons were identified via anode rays, having a unit positive charge of 1.602 × 10-19 C and a rest mass of 1.672 × 10-27 kg.
  • Neutrons were discovered by James Chadwick via beryllium bombardment with alpha particles, carrying zero electrical charge and a mass nearly equal to that of a proton.
  • Specific charge is highest for the electron among all subatomic particles due to its extremely small rest mass (9.109 × 10-31 kg).
  • इलेक्ट्रॉन (electron) की खोज जे.जे. थॉमसन द्वारा कैथोड किरण ट्यूब प्रयोगों के माध्यम से की गई थी, जिसका विशिष्ट आवेश-द्रव्यमान अनुपात (e/m) 1.758 × 1011 C/kg है।
  • प्रोटॉन (protons) की पहचान एनोड किरणों के माध्यम से की गई, जिनका इकाई धनात्मक आवेश 1.602 × 10-19 C और विराम द्रव्यमान (rest mass) 1.672 × 10-27 kg है।
  • न्यूट्रॉन (neutrons) की खोज जेम्स चैडविक द्वारा अल्फा कणों के साथ बेरिलियम की बमबारी के माध्यम से की गई थी, जिन पर शून्य विद्युत आवेश होता है और द्रव्यमान लगभग प्रोटॉन के बराबर होता है।
  • अत्यधिक कम विराम द्रव्यमान (9.109 × 10-31 kg) के कारण सभी उप-परमाणु कणों में इलेक्ट्रॉन के लिए विशिष्ट आवेश (specific charge) सबसे अधिक होता है।

Atomic Models and Electromagnetic Radiation परमाणु मॉडल और विद्युत चुंबकीय विकिरण (Atomic Models and Electromagnetic Radiation)

  • Rutherford's alpha-particle scattering experiment established the nuclear model, proving the atom is predominantly empty space with a dense positive nucleus.
  • Bohr's model introduced quantized angular momentum, stating electrons revolve only in orbits where angular momentum is an integral multiple of h / (2π).
  • Atomic emission spectra of hydrogen exhibit distinct series (Lyman, Balmer, Paschen, Brackett, Pfund) corresponding to electronic transitions between principal shells.
  • The Rydberg formula calculates transition wavelengths using 1 / λ = RH Z2 (1 / n12 - 1 / n22).
  • रदरफोर्ड के अल्फा-कण प्रकीर्णन प्रयोग ने परमाणु मॉडल की स्थापना की, जिससे यह सिद्ध हुआ कि परमाणु का अधिकांश भाग रिक्त स्थान है और इसमें एक सघन धनात्मक नाभिक होता है।
  • बोर के मॉडल ने क्वांटाइज्ड कोणीय संवेग (quantized angular momentum) का परिचय दिया, जिसके अनुसार इलेक्ट्रॉन केवल उन्हीं कक्षाओं में घूमते हैं जहाँ कोणीय संवेग h / (2π) का एक पूर्णांक गुणज होता है।
  • हाइड्रोजन के परमाणु उत्सर्जन स्पेक्ट्रम (atomic emission spectra) में विशिष्ट श्रेणियाँ (लाइमन, बामर, पाश्चन, ब्रैकेट, फंड) दिखाई देती हैं, जो मुख्य कोशों के बीच इलेक्ट्रॉनिक संक्रमण (electronic transitions) के अनुरूप होती हैं।
  • रिडबर्ग सूत्र (Rydberg formula) संक्रमण तरंगदैर्ध्य (wavelengths) की गणना 1 / λ = RH Z2 (1 / n12 - 1 / n22) का उपयोग करके करता है।

Wave-Particle Duality and Quantum Mechanics तरंग-कण द्वैतता और क्वांटम यांत्रिकी (Wave-Particle Duality and Quantum Mechanics)

  • Wave-particle duality, proposed by de Broglie, dictates that all moving material particles exhibit wave characteristics inherently linked to their momentum.
  • Heisenberg's uncertainty principle limits simultaneous exact determination of canonically conjugate variables like position and momentum.
  • Schrödinger's wave equation &Hahat; ψ = E ψ treats electrons as three-dimensional stationary waves within electrostatic potentials of nuclei.
  • Quantum numbers (n, l, ml, ms) completely characterize the energy, shape, spatial orientation, and spin of an electron in an atomic orbital.
  • डी ब्रोग्ली द्वारा प्रस्तावित तरंग-कण द्वैतता (wave-particle duality) यह निर्धारित करती है कि सभी गतिशील पदार्थ कण तरंग लक्षण प्रदर्शित करते हैं जो उनके संवेग से जुड़े होते हैं।
  • हाइजेनबर्ग का अनिश्चितता सिद्धांत (Heisenberg's uncertainty principle) स्थिति और संवेग जैसे विहित संयुग्मी चरों (canonically conjugate variables) के एक साथ सटीक निर्धारण को सीमित करता है।
  • श्रोडिंगर का तरंग समीकरण &Hahat; ψ = E ψ इलेक्ट्रॉनों को नाभिक के इलेक्ट्रोस्टैटिक विभव के भीतर त्रि-आयामी स्थिर तरंगों के रूप में मानता है।
  • क्वांटम संख्याएँ (n, l, ml, ms) परमाणु कक्षक (atomic orbital) में इलेक्ट्रॉन की ऊर्जा, आकार, स्थानिक अभिविन्यास और चक्रण (spin) को पूर्णतः परिभाषित करती हैं।

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aufbau principle filling order (n + l rule)आफबाऊ सिद्धांत (Aufbau principle) भरने का क्रम (n + l नियम)

Observation: Chromium (Z = 24) and Copper (Z = 29) — Extra stability is conferred by completely half-filled (d5) and fully filled (d10) subshells due to symmetrical charge distribution and higher exchange energy. अवलोकन: क्रोमियम (Z = 24) और कॉपर (Z = 29) — सममित आवेश वितरण और उच्च विनिमय ऊर्जा के कारण पूर्णतः अर्ध-पूरित (d5) और पूर्ण-पूरित (d10) उपकोशों द्वारा अतिरिक्त स्थिरता प्राप्त होती है।

Exception Monotonic decrease in ionization energy across periodsआवर्त में आयनन ऊर्जा (ionization energy) में एकसमान कमी

Observation: Group 2 to Group 13 (e.g., Be to B) and Group 15 to Group 16 (e.g., N to O) — Removal of an electron from a stable fully-filled s-orbital (Be) or half-filled p-orbital (N) requires anomalously higher energy than removing from partially filled p-orbitals. अवलोकन: समूह 2 से समूह 13 (जैसे Be से B) और समूह 15 से समूह 16 (जैसे N से O) — एक स्थिर पूर्ण-पूरित s-कक्षक (Be) या अर्ध-पूरित p-कक्षक (N) से इलेक्ट्रॉन निकालने के लिए आंशिक रूप से भरे p-कक्षक की तुलना में असामान्य रूप से अधिक ऊर्जा की आवश्यकता होती है।

Exception Electron gain enthalpy becomes progressively more negative across a periodआवर्त में इलेक्ट्रॉन लब्धि एन्थैल्पी (electron gain enthalpy) का अधिक ऋणात्मक होना

Observation: Fluorine (F) has a less negative electron gain enthalpy than Chlorine (Cl) — Extremely small size of the F atom leads to intense interelectronic repulsion in its compact 2p orbitals, hindering incoming electron accommodation. अवलोकन: फ्लोरीन (F) की इलेक्ट्रॉन लब्धि एन्थैल्पी क्लोरीन (Cl) की तुलना में कम ऋणात्मक होती है — F परमाणु का अत्यंत छोटा आकार इसके सघन 2p कक्षकों में तीव्र अंतर-इलेक्ट्रॉनिक प्रतिकर्षण पैदा करता है, जो आने वाले इलेक्ट्रॉन के समायोजन में बाधा डालता है।

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Cathode rays consist of negatively charged particles called electrons, independent of the nature of the gas or electrodes used.
  • The e/m ratio of cathode ray particles is constant, whereas anode rays show varying e/m ratios depending on the residual gas inside the discharge tube.
  • Neutrons are absent only in the common isotope of hydrogen, protium (1H).
  • The angular momentum of an electron in the n-th Bohr orbit is given by L = nh / (2π).
  • The radius of the nucleus is proportional to A1/3, where A is the mass number, indicating a constant nuclear density.
  • The energy required to remove an electron from the ground state of a hydrogen atom is 13.6 eV.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing angular nodes (l) with radial nodes (n - l - 1); always remember total nodes equal n - 1.
  • Assuming de Broglie wavelength applies only to charged particles; it applies to any matter in motion regardless of charge.
  • Forgetting that Bohr's postulates and equations strictly apply only to single-electron (hydrogenoid) systems.
  • Treating the principal quantum number 'n' as determinative of orbital shape; 'l' determines the angular shape (s, p, d, f).

⚡ Quick Revision: Characteristics of Fundamental Particles and Atomic Structure

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Subatomic Discovery and Properties

Subatomic investigations established electrons, protons, and neutrons as the building blocks of matter, quantified by precise charge-to-mass measurements.

Quantization in Atomic Spectra

Bohr's model accounted for quantized atomic energy states and spectral line emissions via electronic transitions governed by integer quantum numbers.

Wave-Particle Duality and Uncertainty

Matter waves and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle superseded classical deterministic orbits with probabilistic quantum mechanical wavefunctions.

Quantum Numbers and Orbitals

Four quantum numbers (n, l, ml, ms) uniquely define the energy, angular momentum, spatial orientation, and spin states of atomic electrons.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Isotopes (Same Z, different A)
Atoms of the same element having identical atomic numbers but different mass numbers.
Isobars (Same A, different Z)
Atoms of different elements having the same mass number but different atomic numbers.
Isotones (Same neutron count (A - Z))
Nuclides containing the identical number of neutrons.
Isoelectronic (Same electron count)
Species possessing the exact same total number of electrons.
Node (Probability = 0)
Region in space where the probability density of finding an electron is zero.
Aufbau Principle (Building up rule)
Orbitals are filled in order of increasing energy based on (n + l) values.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Atomic structure is governed by quantum mechanics replacing classical mechanics.
  • Dual character of matter is defined by de Broglie's wavelength equation.
  • Uncertainty principle restricts simultaneous exact momentum and position tracking.
  • Quantum numbers define electronic configurations and periodic properties.

⚖️ Key Differences

Atomic Number (Z) VS Mass Number (A)

Z represents proton count (identifies element), whereas A represents total nucleons (protons plus neutrons).

Question 1 of 19

Which subatomic particle has the highest specific charge (e/m)?

Explanation: The electron possesses the smallest rest mass (9.11 × 10-31 kg), yielding the highest e/m ratio.

Question 2 of 19

The de Broglie wavelength of a particle is inversely proportional to its:

Explanation: From de Broglie's relation λ = h/p = h/(mv), wavelength is inversely proportional to momentum p.

Question 3 of 19

Heisenberg's uncertainty principle is theoretically significant for:

Explanation: Uncertainty is negligible for macroscopic bodies due to the large value of mass, but critical for subatomic particles like electrons.

Question 4 of 19

What is the maximum number of electrons that can be accommodated in a shell with principal quantum number n = 3?

Explanation: Maximum electron capacity per shell is given by 2n2. For n = 3, capacity is 2(32) = 18.

Question 5 of 19

The number of radial nodes for a 4s orbital is:

Explanation: Radial nodes = n - l - 1. For 4s, n = 4 and l = 0, so radial nodes = 4 - 0 - 1 = 3.

Question 6 of 19

Which of the following electronic configurations represents an excited state?

Explanation: In 1s2 2s1 2p1, an electron from the 2s orbital has been promoted to the 2p orbital, representing an excited state of carbon or boron-like systems.

Question 7 of 19

The orbital with angular momentum quantum number l = 2 is designated as:

Explanation: l = 0 corresponds to s, l = 1 to p, l = 2 to d, and l = 3 to f orbitals.

Question 8 of 19

Cathode rays are deflected by:

Explanation: Being streams of negatively charged electrons, cathode rays experience deflection in both electric and magnetic fields.

Question 9 of 19

The energy of an electron in the ground state of a hydrogen atom is -13.6 eV. What is the energy of the electron in the second excited state?

Explanation: Ground state corresponds to n = 1. First excited state is n = 2, and second excited state is n = 3. Energy E3 = -13.6 / (32) = -13.6 / 9 = -1.51 eV.

Question 10 of 19

Which quantum number specifies the orientation of an orbital in space?

Explanation: The magnetic quantum number (ml) determines the spatial orientation of orbitals within a subshell.

Question 11 of 19

The total number of nodes for a 3p orbital is:

Explanation: Total nodes = n - 1. For n = 3, total nodes = 3 - 1 = 2 (one angular node and one radial node).

Question 12 of 19

Pauli exclusion principle states that:

Explanation: Pauli's exclusion principle restricts any two electrons in the same atom from sharing identical sets of all four quantum numbers (n, l, ml, ms).

Question 13 of 19

Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity governs the filling of:

Explanation: Hund's rule dictates that electron pairing in degenerate (equal energy) orbitals does not occur until each orbital is singly occupied.

Question 14 of 19

What is the wavelength of a photon associated with a frequency of 5 × 1014 Hz?

Explanation: λ = c / ν = (3 × 108 m/s) / (5 × 1014 s-1) = 6 × 10-7 m = 600 nm.

Question 15 of 19

Which species is isoelectronic with Ne (Z = 10)?

Explanation: Na+ (11 - 1 = 10), Mg2+ (12 - 2 = 10), and F- (9 + 1 = 10) all contain 10 electrons, matching Neon.

Question 16 of 19

The correct set of four quantum numbers for the outermost electron of Na (Z = 11) is:

Explanation: Sodium configuration is [Ne] 3s1. For the 3s electron: n = 3, l = 0 (s), ml = 0, ms = +1/2.

Question 17 of 19

Who measured the charge of an electron experimentally using oil drop apparatus?

Explanation: Robert A. Millikan determined the charge of an electron via his famous oil drop experiment.

Question 18 of 19

The ratio of frequencies of ultraviolet light of wavelength 200 nm and infrared light of wavelength 1000 nm is:

Explanation: Frequency is inversely proportional to wavelength (ν ∝ 1/λ). Thus, νUV / νIR = 1000 / 200 = 5 : 1.

Question 19 of 19

In a multi-electron atom, which orbital fills first according to the (n + l) rule?

Explanation: For 4s: n+l = 4+0 = 4. For 3d: n+l = 3+2 = 5. Lower (n+l) fills first, so 4s fills before 3d.

Question 1 of 10
What is the de Broglie wavelength of an electron accelerated through a potential difference of 100 volts?

Solution: For an electron, λ = 1.227 / √V nm. For V = 100 V, λ = 1.227 / 10 = 0.1227 nm ≈ 0.123 nm.

Question 2 of 10
The ground state electronic configuration of Cr (Z = 24) is:

Solution: Chromium exhibits an exceptional configuration [Ar] 3d5 4s1 to achieve symmetrical half-filled d-subshell stability.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following sets represents species that are isoelectronic with each other?

Solution: O2- (10), F- (10), and Na+ (10) all have 10 electrons.

Question 4 of 10
The energy of a photon of wavelength 4000 Å is approximately:

Solution: E = hc / λ = (12400 eV·Å) / 4000 Å = 3.10 eV.

Question 5 of 10
The maximum number of emission lines observed when an electron falls from n = 4 to n = 1 in atomic hydrogen is:

Solution: Number of spectral lines = n(n - 1) / 2 = 4(3) / 2 = 6.

Question 6 of 10
What is the orbital angular momentum of an electron in a 3d orbital?

Solution: Orbital angular momentum = √[l(l + 1)] (h / 2π). For d orbital, l = 2, so angular momentum = √[2(3)] (h / 2π) = √6 (h / 2π).

Question 7 of 10
Which transition in the hydrogen spectrum gives spectral lines in the visible region?

Solution: The Balmer series corresponds to electron transitions ending at n = 2, falling within the visible spectrum.

Question 8 of 10
The wave-mechanical model of the atom is based on:

Solution: Quantum mechanical model integrates de Broglie's wave-particle duality, Heisenberg's uncertainty principle, and Schrödinger's wave equation.

Question 9 of 10
An electron has a kinetic energy of 3.0 × 10-25 J. Its de Broglie wavelength is (mass of electron = 9.1 × 10-31 kg):

Solution: λ = h / √(2mE) = (6.626 × 10-34) / √(2 × 9.1 × 10-31 × 3.0 × 10-25) ≈ 8.98 × 10-7 m = 898 nm.

Question 10 of 10
The penetration power of orbitals in a multi-electron atom decreases in the order:

Solution: Penetration power towards the nucleus is highest for s orbitals due to lack of directional nodes at the nucleus, following s > p > d > f.
