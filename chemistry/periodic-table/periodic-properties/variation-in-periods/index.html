Periodic Classification and Periodicity

Variation in Periodic Properties Across Periods

Periodic properties across a period are governed by the progressive increase in effective nuclear charge (Zeff) while the principal quantum number remains constant. This leads to a systematic contraction in atomic radii and a corresponding increase in ionization energy and electronegativity.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Atomic and Ionic Radii Trends

  • Atomic radius decreases across a period from left to right due to increasing Zeff.
  • The screening effect of inner electrons remains relatively constant as electrons are added to the same shell.
  • Cationic radius is always smaller than the parent atom due to increased Z/e ratio.
  • Anionic radius is larger than the parent atom due to increased inter-electronic repulsion.

Ionization Energy and Electron Affinity

  • First Ionization Energy (IE1) generally increases across a period due to tighter nuclear hold.
  • Discontinuities occur at Group 2 (ns2) and Group 15 (np3) due to extra stability of filled/half-filled subshells.
  • Electron Affinity becomes more negative across a period, reflecting higher energy release upon electron gain.
  • Noble gases exhibit positive electron gain enthalpy due to stable octet configuration.

Electronegativity and Metallic Character

  • Electronegativity increases across a period as the ability to attract shared electron pairs rises.
  • Metallic character decreases across a period, transitioning from alkali metals to non-metals.
  • Oxides transition from basic (Group 1) to amphoteric (Group 13) to acidic (Group 17).
  • Non-metallic character is highest at the extreme right of the period.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Coulombic Force of Attraction
F ∝ (Zeff × e) / r2
Conditions: Applicable for valence electrons in a period.
Units: Newtons (N)
Ionization Energy Trend
IE ∝ Zeff / r
Conditions: Valid for neutral gaseous atoms in ground state.
Units: kJ/mol or eV/atom
Electronegativity (Pauling Scale)
χA - χB = 0.1017 √Δ
Conditions: Where Δ is the ionic resonance energy.
Units: Dimensionless
Slater's Rule for Zeff
Zeff = Z - σ
Conditions: Applicable for calculating shielding constant σ.
Units: Dimensionless
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception IE increases across a period

Observation: Group 13 < Group 2 and Group 16 < Group 15 — Stability of fully filled ns2 and half-filled np3 orbitals.

Exception Electron Affinity becomes more negative

Observation: Group 15 < Group 14 — Extra stability of half-filled p-orbitals in Group 15 resists electron addition.

Exception Atomic radius decreases

Observation: Noble gases have larger van der Waals radii — Lack of covalent bonding results in larger measured van der Waals radius compared to covalent radius.

Application: Quickly compare effective nuclear charge for elements in the same period.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Across a period, Zeff increases by 0.65 per unit increase in atomic number.
  • The noble gas of each period has the highest ionization energy.
  • Fluorine has the highest electronegativity on the Pauling scale.
  • Isoelectronic species radius is inversely proportional to atomic number Z.
  • Metallic character is inversely related to electronegativity.
  • The second period elements (Li to F) show anomalous properties due to small size.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing electron affinity with ionization energy; remember EA is energy released.
  • Ignoring the half-filled p-orbital stability in Group 15.
  • Assuming noble gases have zero electron affinity; they are positive.
  • Applying periodic trends to transition metals without considering d-orbital shielding.

⚡ Quick Revision: Variation in Periodic Properties Across Periods

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Effective Nuclear Charge

The net positive charge experienced by valence electrons, calculated as Z - σ.

Periodic Contraction

The reduction in atomic size due to increased Zeff pulling electrons closer.

Ionization Energy Anomalies

Deviations in IE trends caused by stable electronic configurations (ns2, np3).

Electronegativity

The relative tendency of an atom to attract a shared pair of electrons in a bond.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Zeff (Effective Nuclear Charge)
Net charge felt by valence electrons.
IE (Ionization Energy)
Energy to remove an electron (kJ/mol).
EA (Electron Affinity)
Energy change on electron gain.
EN (Electronegativity)
Attraction for shared electrons.
Isoelectronic (Same electrons)
Species with identical electron count.
Shielding (Screening effect)
Inner electrons reducing nuclear pull.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • IE order: Be > B and N > O
  • EA order: Cl > F
  • Radius order: N3- > O2- > F-
  • EN order: F > O > N
  • Metallic character: Cs > Fr

📌 Quick Recall

  • Zeff increases left to right.
  • Atomic size decreases left to right.
  • IE increases left to right with exceptions.
  • EN increases left to right.

⚖️ Key Differences

Electron Affinity VS Electronegativity

EA is for isolated atoms; EN is for bonded atoms.
