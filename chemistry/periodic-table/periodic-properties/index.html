Periodic Classification and Periodicity

Periodic Properties

Periodic properties are chemical and physical characteristics of elements that recur at regular intervals when elements are arranged by increasing atomic number. These properties are governed by effective nuclear charge, shielding effect, and outer electronic configurations across periods and groups.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Atomic and Ionic Radii Trends

  • Covalent, van der Waals, and metallic radii decrease across a period due to increasing effective nuclear charge (Z*).
  • Atomic radii increase down a group as new principal quantum shells (n) are added, dominant over the rise in nuclear charge.
  • Isoelectronic species size decreases with increasing atomic number; cation size is smaller than parent atom, while anion size is larger due to electron repulsion.
  • d-block and f-block contractions cause elements in Period 5 and 6 of groups 4-12 to have almost identical atomic radii (e.g., Zr ≈ Hf).

Ionization Enthalpy and Electron Gain Enthalpy

  • First Ionization Enthalpy (ΔiH) generally increases across a period but dips at Group 2 → 13 (s-electron penetration) and Group 15 → 16 (half-filled stability).
  • Electron Gain Enthalpy (ΔegH) becomes more negative across a period and less negative down a group.
  • Chlorine has a more negative ΔegH than Fluorine (-349 kJ/mol vs -328 kJ/mol) due to inter-electronic repulsions in the compact 2p subshell of Fluorine.
  • Noble gases possess positive ΔegH values because incoming electrons must enter a higher energy s-orbital.

Electronegativity and Chemical Reactivity

  • Electronegativity increases across a period and decreases down a group; Fluorine is the most electronegative element (χP = 4.0).
  • Oxide nature correlates with electronegativity difference: Δχ > 2.3 yields basic oxides, Δχ < 1.0 yields acidic oxides, and intermediate values yield amphoteric oxides.
  • Diagonal relationships arise between Period 2 and Period 3 elements (Li-Mg, Be-Al, B-Si) due to similar ionic potential (φ = charge/radius).
  • The inert pair effect causes higher oxidation states to become less stable down Groups 13-16 (e.g., Tl+ is more stable than Tl3+).
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Mulliken Electronegativity
χM = (IE + EA) / 2
Conditions: Both Ionization Energy (IE) and Electron Affinity (EA) expressed in electron-volts (eV).
Units: eV
Pauling Electronegativity Difference
|χA - χB| = 0.208 × &sqrt;(Ed(A-B) - &sqrt;(Ed(A-A) × Ed(B-B)))
Conditions: Bond dissociation energies Ed measured in kcal/mol.
Units: Dimensionless
Allred-Rochow Electronegativity
χAR = 0.359 × (Z* / r2) + 0.744
Conditions: r is the covalent radius of the atom in Å.
Units: Dimensionless
Effective Nuclear Charge (Slater's Rule)
Z* = Z - σ
Conditions: Applies to valence or inner shell electrons to compute net nuclear attraction.
Units: Dimensionless (represented in atomic charge units)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Ionization enthalpy increases across a period

Observation: IE1 of Nitrogen is greater than Oxygen, and IE1 of Beryllium is greater than Boron — Nitrogen has a stable half-filled 2p3 configuration; Beryllium has a fully-filled 2s2 configuration with high penetration power.

Exception Electron affinity increases up a group

Observation: Fluorine has lower electron affinity than Chlorine; Oxygen has lower electron affinity than Sulfur — Compact 2p subshell of F and O experiences high electron-electron repulsion upon addition of an incoming electron.

Exception Atomic radius increases significantly down a group

Observation: Zr (4d, 160 pm) and Hf (5d, 159 pm) have nearly identical radii — Lanthanide contraction caused by poor shielding of the 4f subshell in the 6th period.

Exception Atomic radius increases down Group 13

Observation: Gallium (135 pm) is smaller than Aluminium (143 pm) — Transition state contraction caused by weak shielding of 10 d-electrons in Gallium.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Isoelectronic Species Radius Rule
Radius ∝ 1 / Z (Atomic Number)

Application: For species with identical electron counts (e.g., N3-, O2-, F-, Na+, Mg2+, Al3+), order ionic radii purely by inverse atomic number.

Pauling and Mulliken Scale Conversion
χPauling = χMulliken / 2.8

Application: Converts Mulliken electronegativity values (in eV) directly to the Pauling scale.

Oxide Character Prediction
High Charge/Radius Ratio (φ) → Acidic Oxide

Application: Higher oxidation state oxides of same metal are more acidic (e.g., CrO3 is acidic, Cr2O3 amphoteric, CrO basic).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Fluorine is the most electronegative element with Pauling value 4.0, while Cs and Fr have the lowest value (~0.7).
  • Chlorine has the most negative electron gain enthalpy (ΔegH = -349 kJ/mol) in the periodic table.
  • Helium has the highest first ionization enthalpy (ΔiH1 = 2372 kJ/mol).
  • Ionic radii sequence for 10-electron system: N3- (1.71 Å) > O2- (1.40 Å) > F- (1.36 Å) > Na+ (1.02 Å) > Mg2+ (0.72 Å) > Al3+ (0.54 Å).
  • Slater's shielding constant contribution per electron: 0.35 for same principal shell (0.30 for 1s), 0.85 for (n-1) shell, and 1.00 for (n-2) or inner shells.
  • Metals with amphoteric oxides include Be, Al, Ga, Sn, Pb, Zn, and Sb.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Electron Affinity (EA) sign convention with Electron Gain Enthalpy (ΔegH); EA = -ΔegH.
  • Assuming Fluorine has the highest electron affinity; Chlorine actually has the highest electron affinity.
  • Ignoring d-orbital contraction when predicting the ionic radius of Ga compared to Al.
  • Applying Slater's rules for d and f electrons using identical shielding parameters as s and p electrons; d and f electrons receive a contribution of 1.00 from all lower shells.

⚡ Quick Revision: Periodic Properties

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Atomic and Ionic Size Variations

Atomic size decreases across a period due to increasing Z* and increases down a group due to new shell addition. Isoelectronic species radii decrease with increasing Z.

Ionization and Penetration Effects

Ionization enthalpy measures electron removal energy, affected by nuclear charge, shielding, orbital penetration (s > p > d > f), and configuration stability.

Electron Gain Enthalpy Mechanics

Exothermic electron addition corresponds to negative ΔegH. Anomaly in Period 2 (F, O, N) occurs due to inter-electronic repulsion in small valence shells.

Electronegativity and Acid-Base Trends

Electronegativity quantifies electron-attracting power in a covalent bond. Oxides transition from basic to acidic across a period and acidic to basic down a group.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Effective Nuclear Charge (Z*)
Net positive nuclear charge experienced by an electron in a polyelectronic atom.
Shielding Effect (σ)
Reduction in nuclear attraction on outer electrons caused by inner shell electron repulsions.
Ionization Enthalpy (ΔiH)
Minimum energy required to remove the most loosely bound electron from an isolated gaseous atom.
Electron Gain Enthalpy (ΔegH)
Enthalpy change when a neutral gaseous atom accepts an electron to form a uninegative ion.
Electronegativity (χ)
Tendency of an atom in a covalent molecule to attract shared pairs of electrons towards itself.
Ionic Potential (φ)
Ratio of ionic charge to ionic radius, determining polarizing power.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Slater calculation for s/p: σ = 0.35(n) + 0.85(n-1) + 1.00(n-2 and lower).
  • Chlorine has higher EA than Fluorine; Sulfur has higher EA than Oxygen.
  • First IE: Be > B and N > O.
  • Zr and Hf have identical radii due to Lanthanide Contraction.
  • Relationship: χMulliken = 2.8 × χPauling.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Periodic trends reflect balance between increasing Z and electronic shielding.
  • Group 2, Group 15, and noble gases exhibit anomaly spikes in ionization enthalpy due to subshell stability.
  • Periodic trends in oxide acidity are directly proportional to element electronegativity.
  • Isoelectronic radii scale strictly with 1/Z.

⚖️ Key Differences

Electron Affinity VS Electronegativity

Electron affinity measures energy involved in isolated atom taking an electron; electronegativity measures bonded atom electron attraction capability.

Shielding Constant for s/p VS Shielding Constant for d/f

In s/p orbitals, (n-1) shell electrons contribute 0.85; in d/f orbitals, electrons in (n-1) and lower shells contribute 1.00.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following orders is correct for the first ionization enthalpy (ΔiH1)?

Explanation: Be (1s22s2) has higher IE than B (1s22s22p1) due to filled s-shell and penetration. N (2p3) has higher IE than O (2p4) due to half-filled p-subshell stability.

Question 2 of 18

Which element has the maximum negative electron gain enthalpy?

Explanation: Chlorine has the highest negative electron gain enthalpy (-349 kJ/mol) because Fluorine experiences stronger electron repulsions in its smaller 2p orbital.

Question 3 of 18

The correct order of ionic radii for N3-, O2-, F-, and Na+ is:

Explanation: For isoelectronic species, ionic size decreases as atomic number Z increases. Z values: N(7), O(8), F(9), Na(11).

Question 4 of 18

The shielding constant (σ) for the 4s electron of Zinc (Z = 30) calculated using Slater's rule is:

Explanation: Config: (1s)2(2s,2p)8(3s,3p)8(3d)10(4s)2. For 1 electron in 4s: σ = 1×0.35 + 18×0.85 + 10×1.00 = 0.35 + 15.30 + 10.00 = 25.65.

Question 5 of 18

Which set of elements shows a diagonal relationship?

Explanation: Li and Mg share a diagonal relationship due to similar ionic radii and similar charge-to-radius ratio.

Question 6 of 18

Why are the ionic radii of Zr4+ (0.72 Å) and Hf4+ (0.71 Å) nearly identical?

Explanation: Lanthanide contraction due to poor shielding of 4f electrons causes 5d elements to have nearly identical ionic radii to 4d elements.

Question 7 of 18

On the Pauling scale, if the electronegativity of element X is 3.0 and Y is 1.0, the nature of the X-Y bond is:

Explanation: Δχ = 3.0 - 1.0 = 2.0. A difference greater than 1.7 indicates more than 50% ionic character.

Question 8 of 18

Which oxide is amphoteric in nature?

Explanation: Al2O3 reacts with both acids and bases, making it amphoteric. Na2O and CaO are basic; SO3 is acidic.

Question 9 of 18

The second ionization enthalpy of Sodium is significantly higher than that of Magnesium because:

Explanation: Removing an electron from Na+ requires breaking a stable octet (2p6), requiring massive energy input.

Question 10 of 18

According to Allred-Rochow scale, electronegativity depends directly on:

Explanation: The formula is χAR = 0.359 (Z / r2) + 0.744, so it depends directly on Z / r2.

Question 11 of 18

Which property decreases down a halogen group?

Explanation: Electronegativity decreases down a group due to increasing atomic size and reduced nuclear attraction.

Question 12 of 18

The pair of elements exhibiting the lowest and highest electron affinities respectively are:

Explanation: Noble gases have effectively zero or positive electron affinities (lowest/least negative), while halogens have highest negative electron affinities.

Question 13 of 18

What is the order of electronegativity on the Pauling scale for C, N, O, F?

Explanation: Electronegativity increases across Period 2: C (2.5) < N (3.0) < O (3.5) < F (4.0).

Question 14 of 18

Which of the following transitions requires the maximum energy?

Explanation: Na+ has a noble gas configuration (2p6); removing an electron from it requires highest energy.

Question 15 of 18

Gallium has a smaller covalent radius than Aluminium due to:

Explanation: The 10 d-electrons in Gallium poorly shield the nuclear charge, leading to an increased effective nuclear charge and smaller size.

Question 16 of 18

Electronegativity of an atom on the Mulliken scale is 5.6 eV. Its Pauling electronegativity value is approximately:

Explanation: χP = χM / 2.8 = 5.6 / 2.8 = 2.0.

Question 17 of 18

Which species has the largest size?

Explanation: All are isoelectronic (18 electrons). S2- has the smallest atomic number (Z = 16), hence the largest radius.

Question 18 of 18

The general electronic configuration of d-block elements is:

Explanation: Standard IUPAC representation for d-block elements is (n-1)d1-10 ns0-2 to account for Pd (4d105s0).

Effective nuclear charge (Z*) for a valence electron in Carbon (Z = 6) according to Slater's rules is:

The acidic strength of oxides decreases in the order:

Question 1 of 10
Calculate effective nuclear charge (Z*) on a 3d electron of Copper (Z = 29).

Solution: Cu config: (1s)2(2s,2p)8(3s,3p)8(3d)10(4s)1. For a 3d electron: σ = 9 × 0.35 + 18 × 1.00 = 3.15 + 18.00 = 21.15. Z = 29 - 21.15 = 7.85? Wait: electrons in shells higher than 3d do not contribute. For 3d: 9 electrons in same group (9 × 0.35) + 18 in inner shells (18 × 1.0) = 3.15 + 18 = 21.15. Z = 29 - 21.15 = 7.85. Option correction: 7.85 or for 4s electron: σ = 0 (4s ignored in 3d test) -> let's compute for 4s electron: σ = 0 × 0.35 + 18 × 0.85 + 10 × 1.00 = 25.30; Z* = 29 - 25.30 = 3.70.

Question 2 of 10
Which element has the highest ionization energy among the following?

Solution: Neon has the smallest atomic radius among noble gases except Helium, giving it the highest ionization energy among the choices.

Question 3 of 10
The electronegativity values of H, C, N, O, and F on Pauling scale are respectively:

Solution: Standard Pauling values: H (2.1), C (2.5), N (3.0), O (3.5), F (4.0).

Question 4 of 10
The correct sequence of ionic radius for the ions P3-, S2-, Cl- is:

Solution: Isoelectronic ions with 18 electrons: smaller nuclear charge yields larger radius. P(15) < S(16) < Cl(17).

Question 5 of 10
The first ionization enthalpy of Be is greater than B because:

Solution: The 2s electrons in Be penetrate closer to the nucleus than the 2p electron in B, making 2s electrons harder to remove.

Question 6 of 10
The valence shell configuration of element with atomic number 114 (Flerovium) is:

Solution: Element 114 belongs to Group 14, Period 7. Electronic config: [Rn] 5f14 6d10 7s2 7p2.

Question 7 of 10
Which element has the lowest electronegativity?

Solution: Caesium (Cs) has one of the lowest electronegativities (~0.79) among non-radioactive elements.

Question 8 of 10
According to Pauling's formula, a bond between A and B is 50% ionic when |χA - χB| equals:

Solution: By Pauling's empirical relation, an electronegativity difference of 1.7 corresponds to 50% ionic and 50% covalent character.

Question 9 of 10
The basicity of the oxides Na2O, MgO, Al2O3, SiO2 decreases in the order:

Solution: Across Period 3, oxides transition from strongly basic (Na2O) to basic (MgO), amphoteric (Al2O3), and weakly acidic (SiO2).

Question 10 of 10
The term 'Inert Pair Effect' refers to the reluctance of which electrons to participate in bond formation?

Solution: Inert pair effect is the reluctance of the outermost s-electrons (ns2) to participate in chemical bonding in heavier p-block elements.
