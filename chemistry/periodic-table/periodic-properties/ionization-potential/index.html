Periodic Classification and Periodicity

Ionization Potential

Ionization potential, or ionization energy, is the minimum energy required to remove the most loosely bound electron from an isolated gaseous atom in its ground state. It is a measure of the strength of the electrostatic attraction between the nucleus and the valence electrons.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Periodic Trends and Shielding

  • Ionization energy increases across a period due to increasing Zeff and decreasing atomic radius.
  • Ionization energy decreases down a group due to the addition of new shells and increased shielding effect.
  • The shielding effect (σ) reduces the effective nuclear charge felt by valence electrons, lowering IE.

Electronic Configuration Stability

  • Atoms with half-filled (p3, d5, f7) or fully-filled (s2, p6, d10) orbitals exhibit higher IE than expected.
  • Exchange energy and symmetry contribute to the extra stability of these configurations.
  • Removing an electron from a stable configuration requires significantly higher energy.

Successive Ionization Energies

  • Successive ionization energies always follow the order: IE1 < IE2 < IE3.
  • A large jump in successive IE values indicates the removal of an electron from a noble gas core.
  • The magnitude of IE is inversely proportional to the atomic radius.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception IE increases across a period

Observation: Be (2s2) > B (2s2 2p1) — Removal of p-electron from B is easier than s-electron from Be due to better penetration.

Exception IE increases across a period

Observation: N (2p3) > O (2p4) — N has a stable half-filled p-subshell configuration.

Exception IE decreases down a group

Observation: Tl (Group 13) > In — Poor shielding of 4f electrons (lanthanide contraction) increases Zeff.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • IE is measured in kJ/mol or eV/atom.
  • Noble gases have the highest IE in their respective periods.
  • Alkali metals have the lowest IE in their respective periods.
  • IE1 of Nitrogen is higher than Oxygen.
  • IE1 of Beryllium is higher than Boron.
  • Lanthanide contraction causes anomalous IE in 6th period elements.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing IE with Electron Affinity; IE is energy required to remove, EA is energy released to add.
  • Ignoring the penetration effect when comparing s and p orbitals.
  • Assuming IE decreases linearly down a group without considering d/f block contractions.
  • Miscalculating the jump in successive IE values.

⚡ Quick Revision: Ionization Potential

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Periodic Variation

IE increases left to right due to Zeff increase and decreases top to bottom due to shell addition.

Electronic Stability

Half-filled and fully-filled subshells possess higher stability, requiring more energy for ionization.

Successive Ionization

Removing electrons from a cation is harder than from a neutral atom due to increased electrostatic attraction.

Shielding and Penetration

Inner electrons shield valence electrons; s-orbitals penetrate closer to the nucleus, increasing their IE.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

IE1 (First Ionization Energy)
Energy to remove first electron.
Zeff (Effective Nuclear Charge)
Net positive charge felt by valence electrons.
Shielding (Screening Effect)
Reduction of nuclear attraction by inner electrons.
Penetration (Orbital Proximity)
Ability of orbital to be near the nucleus.
Noble Gas (Group 18)
Elements with stable octet configuration.
Lanthanide Contraction (Size Reduction)
Poor shielding of 4f electrons.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • N > O
  • Be > B
  • Mg > Al
  • IE1 < IE2 < IE3
  • Noble gases have highest IE

📌 Quick Recall

  • IE increases across period.
  • IE decreases down group.
  • Stable configurations increase IE.
  • Successive IE increases.

⚖️ Key Differences

IE VS EA

IE is energy input for removal; EA is energy change for addition.
