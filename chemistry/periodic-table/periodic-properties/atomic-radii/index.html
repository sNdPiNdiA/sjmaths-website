Periodic Classification and Periodicity

Atomic Radii

Atomic radius is defined as the distance from the center of the nucleus to the outermost shell containing electrons. It is determined by the balance between nuclear attraction and electron-electron repulsion.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Periodic Trends in Atomic Size

  • Atomic radius decreases across a period due to increasing effective nuclear charge (Zeff).
  • Atomic radius increases down a group due to the addition of new principal energy shells (n).
  • The lanthanoid contraction causes the radii of 5d elements to be nearly identical to 4d elements.

Ionic Radii Variations

  • Cations are always smaller than their parent atoms due to the loss of an electron shell and increased Zeff.
  • Anions are always larger than their parent atoms due to increased electron-electron repulsion and decreased Zeff.
  • In isoelectronic species, radius decreases as the nuclear charge (Z) increases.

Factors Influencing Radii

  • Shielding effect (screening) by inner electrons reduces the effective pull of the nucleus on valence electrons.
  • Bond multiplicity decreases bond length; triple bonds are shorter than double or single bonds.
  • Coordination number affects metallic radius; higher coordination numbers result in larger observed radii.

rcov = dA-A / 2
Conditions: For homonuclear diatomic molecules
Units: Picometers (pm) or Angstroms (Å)
Metallic Radius
rmet = dM-M / 2
Conditions: For metallic crystal lattices
Units: Picometers (pm)
Van der Waals Radius
rvdW = dnon-bonded / 2
Conditions: For non-bonded adjacent atoms in solid state
Units: Picometers (pm)
Slater's Rule for Effective Nuclear Charge
Zeff = Z - σ
Conditions: Used to estimate shielding constant σ
Units: Dimensionless
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Size increases down a group

Observation: Zr (160 pm) and Hf (159 pm) — Lanthanoid contraction due to poor shielding of 4f electrons.

Exception Size decreases across a period

Observation: Noble gases have larger radii than preceding halogens — Van der Waals radius is measured for noble gases, which is inherently larger than covalent radii.

Exception Size increases with shell number

Observation: Ga (135 pm) vs Al (143 pm) — Poor shielding by 3d electrons in Gallium leads to higher Zeff.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Isoelectronic Order
Radius ∝ 1 / Z

Application: For species with same electrons, higher atomic number (Z) means smaller size.

Cationic Charge Rule
Charge ↑ → Size ↓

Application: Fe3+ < Fe2+ < Fe.

Anionic Charge Rule
Charge ↑ → Size ↑

Application: O2- > F- > Ne.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Atomic radius is measured in pm or Å.
  • Noble gases possess the largest atomic radii in their respective periods.
  • Lanthanoid contraction affects the entire 5d transition series.
  • The radius of F- is larger than the radius of Na+.
  • Effective nuclear charge Zeff = Z - S.
  • Atomic radius generally decreases from left to right in the periodic table.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing covalent radius with ionic radius.
  • Ignoring the effect of the 4f orbital shielding in lanthanoids.
  • Assuming noble gases follow the same trend as main group elements.
  • Miscalculating Zeff by ignoring the contribution of electrons in the same shell.

⚡ Quick Revision: Atomic Radii

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Effective Nuclear Charge

The net positive charge experienced by valence electrons, calculated by subtracting the screening constant from the atomic number.

Lanthanoid Contraction

The steady decrease in ionic and atomic radii of lanthanoid elements due to poor shielding by 4f electrons.

Isoelectronic Series

Atoms or ions having the same number of electrons; size is inversely proportional to the nuclear charge.

Periodic Trends

Atomic size decreases across periods and increases down groups due to shell addition and Zeff changes.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Atomic Radius (r)
Distance from nucleus to valence shell.
Zeff (Effective Nuclear Charge)
Net charge felt by valence electrons.
Shielding (S)
Reduction of nuclear pull by inner electrons.
Isoelectronic (Same e-)
Species with identical electron counts.
Cation (M+)
Positively charged ion, smaller than atom.
Anion (X-)
Negatively charged ion, larger than atom.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Noble gases have the largest radii in periods.
  • Zr ≈ Hf due to lanthanoid contraction.
  • Cations are smaller than parent atoms.
  • Anions are larger than parent atoms.
  • Zeff increases left to right.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Size decreases across periods.
  • Size increases down groups.
  • Isoelectronic size depends on Z.
  • Lanthanoids affect transition metal sizes.

⚖️ Key Differences

Covalent Radius VS Van der Waals Radius

Covalent involves bonding; Van der Waals involves non-bonded interactions.
