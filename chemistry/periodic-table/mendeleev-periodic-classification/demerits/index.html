Periodic Classification and Periodicity

Demerits of Mendeleev's Periodic Classification

Mendeleev's periodic table arranged elements based on atomic mass and chemical property similarities. However, several fundamental flaws existed, including atomic mass inversions, the ambiguous position of hydrogen, inability to accommodate isotopes, and grouping of chemically dissimilar elements.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Anomalous Pairs of Elements (Mass Inversion)

  • Mendeleev placed certain elements with higher atomic masses prior to elements with lower atomic masses to preserve chemical similarity.
  • Argon and Potassium: Argon (atomic mass 39.9 amu) precedes Potassium (atomic mass 39.1 amu).
  • Cobalt and Nickel: Cobalt (atomic mass 58.9 amu) precedes Nickel (atomic mass 58.7 amu).
  • Tellurium and Iodine: Tellurium (atomic mass 127.6 amu) precedes Iodine (atomic mass 126.9 amu).
  • Thorium and Protactinium: Thorium (atomic mass 232.0 amu) precedes Protactinium (atomic mass 231.0 amu).

Position of Hydrogen and Isotopes

  • Hydrogen Ambiguity: Hydrogen resembles Alkali Metals (Group I) in electropositive nature, forming H+ ions and oxides like H2O, but also resembles Halogens (Group VII) by forming diatomic molecule H2 and univalent hydrides like NaH.
  • Isotope Placement Defect: Isotopes possess identical chemical properties but different atomic masses. Arranging by atomic mass requires separate slots for isotopes, which Mendeleev failed to accommodate.
  • No separate positions were assigned to isotopes such as 1H, 2H, and 3H, or 35Cl and 37Cl.

Grouping of Dissimilar Elements and Separation of Similar Elements

  • Grouping Dissimilar Elements: Highly reactive alkali metals (Li, Na, K) were placed in Subgroup IA alongside unreactive coinage metals (Cu, Ag, Au) in Subgroup IB.
  • Separation of Similar Elements: Chemically similar elements like Platinum (Pt) and Gold (Au) or Copper (Cu) and Mercury (Hg) were placed in different groups.
  • Inner Transition Elements: Lanthanides and Actinides were not allocated specific places within the main grid of the periodic table.
  • Cause of Periodicity: Mendeleev could not explain the fundamental underlying physical cause for the periodic repetition of properties.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Modern Periodic Law (Moseley's Law)
ν = a(Z - b)2
Conditions: Relates characteristic X-ray frequency (ν) to atomic number (Z).
Units: Frequency ν in s-1, Z is dimensionless atomic number.
Atomic Weight Inversion Index
ΔA = Apreceding - Afollowing > 0
Conditions: Identifies anomalous pairs where heavier element precedes lighter element.
Units: amu or g/mol
Isotopic Mass Difference Principle
A = Z + N
Conditions: Identifies isotopes having identical Z but different N and mass number A.
Units: Dimensionless mass number
Mendeleev's Periodic Law
Properties of elements = f(Atomic Mass)
Conditions: Applies strictly to Mendeleev's classification framework.
Units: Atomic mass unit (amu or u)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Elements arranged in strictly increasing order of atomic mass.

Observation: Anomalous Pairs (Ar/K, Co/Ni, Te/I, Th/Pa) — Higher atomic mass elements were placed before lower atomic mass elements to match chemical valence and reactivity patterns.

Exception Elements in the same group exhibit uniform chemical properties.

Observation: Subgroups IA (Alkali metals) and IB (Coinage metals) — Mendeleev forced alkali metals (Li, Na, K) and coinage metals (Cu, Ag, Au) into Group I despite extreme differences in reactivity and electronic behavior.

Exception Distinct atomic mass mandates distinct positions in the periodic table.

Observation: Isotopes of elements (e.g., 35Cl and 37Cl) — Isotopes were assigned a single location based on chemical similarity, directly violating the primary periodic criterion of atomic mass.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Argon (39.9 amu) precedes Potassium (39.1 amu) in Mendeleev's table.
  • Cobalt (58.9 amu) precedes Nickel (58.7 amu) in Mendeleev's table.
  • Tellurium (127.6 amu) precedes Iodine (126.9 amu) in Mendeleev's table.
  • Thorium (232.0 amu) precedes Protactinium (231.0 amu) in Mendeleev's table.
  • Group I included both soft alkali metals (Li, Na, K) and hard coinage metals (Cu, Ag, Au).
  • Mendeleev's periodic law fails for isotopes because isotopes possess different atomic weights but identical chemical properties.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming Mendeleev used atomic number: Mendeleev used atomic mass; Henry Moseley later introduced atomic number.
  • Confusing the order of anomalous pairs: Argon precedes Potassium (not K before Ar); Cobalt precedes Nickel (not Ni before Co).
  • Believing isotopes were given separate columns: Isotopes were not given separate positions, creating a major logical inconsistency.
  • Thinking Mendeleev predicted noble gases initially: Noble gases were completely unknown when Mendeleev introduced his original table in 1869.

⚡ Quick Revision: Demerits of Mendeleev's Periodic Classification

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Anomalous Pairs Defect

Elements with higher atomic weights were placed before elements with lower atomic weights (Ar before K, Co before Ni, Te before I, Th before Pa) to align chemical characteristics, violating the fundamental law.

Position of Hydrogen

Hydrogen exhibits dual affinity: forming H+ cations like alkali metals and H- hydride anions like halogens. Mendeleev could not justify placing it exclusively in Group I.

Isotopic Inconsistency

If atomic mass determines position, isotopes must occupy distinct slots. Mendeleev placed isotopes in the same position, contradicting his core physical premise.

Irregular Grouping of Dissimilar Elements

Chemically distinct elements (e.g., alkali metals Li, Na, K and coinage metals Cu, Ag, Au) were forced into Subgroups IA and IB, while similar elements (Pt and Au) were separated.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Anomalous Pair (Mass Inversion Pair)
A pair of elements in Mendeleev's table where the element of higher atomic mass precedes the element of lower atomic mass.
Isotopes (Same Z, Different A)
Atoms of the same element having identical atomic numbers but different atomic masses.
Coinage Metals (Group IB Elements)
Transition metals Cu, Ag, and Au placed in Subgroup IB of Mendeleev's Group I.
Alkali Metals (Group IA Elements)
Highly reactive s-block metals Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs placed in Subgroup IA.
Atomic Mass (A)
The mass of an atom expressed in atomic mass units, used as Mendeleev's sorting key.
Periodicity Cause (Electronic Configuration)
The underlying reason for repeating properties, which Mendeleev could not explain.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ar (39.9) precedes K (39.1)
  • Co (58.9) precedes Ni (58.7)
  • Te (127.6) precedes I (126.9)
  • Th (232) precedes Pa (231)
  • Mendeleev's classification was based on atomic mass, not atomic number.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Mendeleev's table successfully predicted new elements but suffered from mass inversions.
  • Hydrogen's dual resemblance to alkali metals and halogens made its position questionable.
  • The discovery of isotopes highlighted the limitation of using atomic mass as the periodic criterion.
  • Moseley's X-ray experiments showed atomic number (Z) is the true fundamental property, resolving Mendeleev's demerits.

⚖️ Key Differences

Mendeleev's Periodic Basis VS Modern Periodic Basis

Mendeleev used atomic mass; Modern table uses atomic number (Z).

Cobalt vs Nickel Position VS Argon vs Potassium Position

Co (58.9) precedes Ni (58.7) due to valence similarity; Ar (39.9) precedes K (39.1) due to noble gas core similarity.

Subgroup IA VS Subgroup IB

Subgroup IA contains reactive alkali metals (Li, Na, K); Subgroup IB contains inert coinage metals (Cu, Ag, Au).

Question 3 of 19

Why could hydrogen NOT be assigned a single definitive position in Mendeleev's periodic table?

Explanation: Hydrogen forms H+ like alkali metals and H- or diatomic H2 like halogens, creating position ambiguity.

Question 4 of 19

Which set of metals was artificially placed in Group I alongside alkali metals in Mendeleev's classification?

Explanation: Coinage metals (Cu, Ag, Au) were placed in Subgroup IB of Group I alongside alkali metals (Subgroup IA).

Question 5 of 19

The discovery of which atomic species created the greatest contradiction to Mendeleev's atomic mass criterion?

Explanation: Isotopes have different atomic masses but identical chemical properties. If arranged strictly by mass, they require separate positions.

Question 6 of 19

Explanation: Tellurium (127.6 amu) was placed before Iodine (126.9 amu).

Question 7 of 19

Mendeleev could not offer a satisfactory explanation for which fundamental periodic aspect?

Explanation: Mendeleev could not explain why properties repeat periodically; this was later explained by electronic configuration.

Question 8 of 19

Which actinide pair demonstrates an atomic mass inversion in Mendeleev's system?

Explanation: Thorium (atomic mass 232.0) was placed before Protactinium (atomic mass 231.0).

Question 9 of 19

Which pair of chemically similar elements was placed in different groups in Mendeleev's table?

Explanation: Copper (Group I) and Mercury (Group II) exhibit strong chemical similarities but were separated.

Question 10 of 19

In Mendeleev's periodic table, Lanthanides and Actinides were:

Explanation: Lanthanides and Actinides could not be accommodated in the main grid of Mendeleev's periodic table.

Question 11 of 19

What happens to the mass inversion problem when elements are arranged by atomic number (Z)?

Explanation: When arranged by atomic number, Ar (Z=18) naturally precedes K (Z=19), and Co (Z=27) naturally precedes Ni (Z=28).

Question 12 of 19

Which property of hydrogen aligns it with Group VIIA (Halogens)?

Explanation: Hydrogen forms hydride anions (H-) in metallic hydrides like NaH, resembling halogens forming halide anions (X-).

Question 13 of 19

Which of the following elements has a lower atomic mass than Cobalt but was placed AFTER Cobalt by Mendeleev?

Explanation: Nickel has an atomic mass of 58.7 amu, which is lower than Cobalt's 58.9 amu, yet Nickel was placed after Cobalt.

Question 14 of 19

Why did Mendeleev place Tellurium (127.6) before Iodine (126.9)?

Explanation: To maintain chemical similarity in groups (Te with oxygen family, I with halogen family), Mendeleev inverted their mass order.

Question 15 of 19

Which fundamental defect of Mendeleev's table was resolved by Henry Moseley's work in 1913?

Explanation: Moseley showed that atomic number, not atomic mass, is the fundamental property, resolving mass inversions, isotopic placement, and periodic basis.

Question 16 of 19

Which of the following is NOT a demerit of Mendeleev's periodic table?

Explanation: Predicting undiscovered elements (like Eka-Aluminium / Gallium) was a major merit, not a demerit.

Question 17 of 19

Protactinium has an atomic mass of 231 amu and Thorium has an atomic mass of 232 amu. In Mendeleev's table:

Explanation: Thorium (232 amu) was placed before Protactinium (231 amu) as an anomalous pair.

Question 18 of 19

How did isotopes create a dilemma for Mendeleev's Periodic Law?

Explanation: Mendeleev's law states properties depend on atomic mass. Different atomic masses of isotopes require different positions, which contradicted experimental observation.

Question 19 of 19

Group IB in Mendeleev's table contains Cu, Ag, and Au. These elements differ significantly from Group IA metals in being:

Explanation: Coinage metals (Cu, Ag, Au) are transition elements that are hard, noble, and less reactive, unlike soft and highly reactive alkali metals.
