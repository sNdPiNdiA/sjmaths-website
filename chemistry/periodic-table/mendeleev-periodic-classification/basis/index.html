Periodic Classification and Periodicity

Basis of Mendeleev's Periodic Classification

Mendeleev's periodic law postulates that the physical and chemical properties of chemical elements are periodic functions of their atomic masses. The original classification relied on arranging elements in increasing order of atomic mass while grouping them based on empirical formulas of their hydrides and oxides.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Fundamental Principles of Mendeleev's System

  • Dmitri Mendeleev arranged the 63 known elements in order of increasing atomic mass while aligning elements with similar chemical properties in vertical columns (Groups).
  • The Periodic Law states that the physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic weights.
  • Mendeleev prioritized chemical similarity over strict adherence to increasing atomic weight when placement conflicts arose.
  • The table consisted of eight vertical groups (I to VIII) and seven horizontal rows termed periods.

Oxide and Hydride Stoichiometry as Classification Criteria

  • Mendeleev selected hydrogen and oxygen as key reactive elements because they form compounds with almost all known elements.
  • Basic stoichiometry of oxides (R2O to RO4) and hydrides (RH to RH4) served as the primary test for placing an element in a specific group.
  • Group I elements form oxides of formula R2O and hydrides of formula RH, whereas Group IV elements form oxides of RO2 and hydrides of RH4.
  • Group VIII elements consist of transition triads (e.g., Fe, Co, Ni) forming high-oxidation-state oxides such as RO4.

Atomic Mass Correction and Eka-Elements

  • Mendeleev corrected the atomic mass of Beryllium from 13.5 to 9.0 by re-evaluating its valency from 3 (trivalent like Al) to 2 (bivalent like Mg).
  • Atomic mass corrections were also successfully applied to Indium (In), Uranium (U), Gold (Au), and Platinum (Pt).
  • Gaps were deliberately left in the table for undiscovered elements, predicting their properties accurately as Eka-Boron (Scandium), Eka-Aluminium (Gallium), and Eka-Silicon (Germanium).
  • Eka-Manganese was later discovered as Technetium (Tc), validating the predictive utility of Mendeleev's periodic framework.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Elements arranged in strictly increasing order of atomic mass

Observation: Anomalous pairs placed in inverted atomic mass order: Argon (39.9) before Potassium (39.1), Cobalt (58.9) before Nickel (58.7), Tellurium (127.6) before Iodine (126.9), Thorium (232.0) before Protactinium (231.0). — Mendeleev prioritized chemical group similarity over strict atomic weight progression.

Exception Isotopes have different atomic masses and should occupy separate positions

Observation: Isotopes of elements (e.g., 35Cl and 37Cl) are assigned a single position in Mendeleev's periodic table. — Mendeleev's classification had no knowledge of atomic number or nuclear structure.

Exception Elements in the same group possess similar chemical properties

Observation: Coinage metals (Cu, Ag, Au) placed in Group IB alongside highly reactive alkali metals (Li, Na, K) in Group IA. — Grouping was based strictly on formal stoichiometry (e.g., formation of R2O) rather than electronic configuration.

Exception Chemically similar elements should be placed in the same group

Observation: Chemically similar pairs like Copper (Cu) and Mercury (Hg), or Platinum (Pt) and Gold (Au), were separated into different groups. — Rigid adherence to oxide stoichiometry forced separation of elements with varying valencies.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Mendeleev's periodic table was published in 1869 based on 63 known elements.
  • Beryllium atomic mass was corrected from 13.5 to 9.0 based on its bivalent nature and placement in Group II.
  • Eka-Aluminium was later named Gallium (Ga); Eka-Silicon was named Germanium (Ge); Eka-Boron was named Scandium (Sc).
  • Tellurium (atomic weight 127.6) was placed before Iodine (atomic weight 126.9) to preserve group chemical similarity.
  • Mendeleev's system contained 8 groups and 7 periods; Group VIII consisted of transition triads.
  • Inert gases (Group 0) were not present in Mendeleev's original 1869 table and were added later by Ramsay without disturbing main groups.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Eka-Aluminium with Germanium instead of Gallium (Remember: Al -> Ga, Si -> Ge).
  • Assuming Mendeleev used atomic number; he strictly used atomic mass and chemical properties.
  • Believing noble gases were part of the 1869 original table; they were discovered later (1890s) and placed in Group 0.
  • Mistaking the atomic mass correction of Beryllium as being based on spectral lines rather than equivalent weight and valency revision.

⚡ Quick Revision: Basis of Mendeleev's Periodic Classification

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Atomic Mass as Fundamental Criterion

Mendeleev organized elements in order of increasing atomic masses. He stated that the physical and chemical properties of elements periodically recur when arranged by mass.

Role of Hydrides and Oxides

The chemical formulas of hydrides (RH to RH4) and oxides (R2O to RO4) formed the core basis for categorizing elements into eight distinct groups.

Correction of Atomic Mass

Using the relation Atomic Mass = Equivalent Weight × Valency, Mendeleev rectified incorrect atomic masses of Be, In, Au, Pt, and U based on periodic placement.

Predictive Power (Eka-Elements)

Mendeleev left intentional gaps in his periodic table and successfully predicted the atomic masses and chemical properties of undiscovered elements like Scandium, Gallium, and Germanium.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Periodic Law (Mendeleev's Law)
Statement that physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of atomic mass.
Eka-Element (Prefix 'Eka')
Sanskrit prefix meaning 'one' used by Mendeleev to name undiscovered elements below a known element.
Anomalous Pair (Inverted Mass Pair)
Pairs of elements placed in reverse order of atomic mass to maintain group similarity.
Equivalent Weight (Eq. Wt.)
Mass of an element combining with 1 g of hydrogen or 8 g of oxygen; Eq Wt = Atomic Mass / Valency.
Transition Triad (Group VIII Triad)
Set of three chemically similar metals (e.g., Fe-Co-Ni) placed together in Group VIII.
Group 0 Elements (Noble Gases)
Group added to Mendeleev's modified table after the discovery of inert gases by William Ramsay.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Atomic Mass of Be corrected from 13.5 (valency 3) to 9 (valency 2).
  • Eka-Boron = Scandium (Sc), Eka-Aluminium = Gallium (Ga), Eka-Silicon = Germanium (Ge).
  • Anomalous pairs: Ar (39.9) & K (39.1); Co (58.9) & Ni (58.7); Te (127.6) & I (126.9); Th (232) & Pa (231).
  • Group I to VII elements are subdivided into sub-groups A and B.
  • Original Mendeleev table (1869) contained 63 elements and zero noble gases.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Mendeleev classified 63 elements based on increasing atomic mass and oxide/hydride formulas.
  • Chemical similarity was prioritized over atomic mass order leading to anomalous pairs.
  • Atomic masses of several elements were corrected using Equivalent Weight × Valency.
  • Unfilled slots successfully predicted existence and properties of Sc, Ga, and Ge.

⚖️ Key Differences

Eka-Aluminium VS Eka-Silicon

Eka-Aluminium corresponds to Gallium (Ga, Group 13), whereas Eka-Silicon corresponds to Germanium (Ge, Group 14).

Explanation: Eka-Silicon was discovered in 1886 by Clemens Winkler and named Germanium (Ge).

Question 8 of 18

What is the general hydride formula for Group IV elements in Mendeleev's periodic table?

Explanation: Group IV elements (like Carbon and Silicon) form hydrides of the general formula RH4.

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following elements was predicted by Mendeleev as 'Eka-Boron'?

Explanation: Eka-Boron was discovered by Lars Fredrik Nilson in 1879 and named Scandium (Sc).

Question 10 of 18

Why did Mendeleev place Tellurium (127.6) before Iodine (126.9)?

Explanation: Mendeleev placed Iodine after Tellurium so that Iodine aligned with other halogens (F, Cl, Br) exhibiting similar chemical properties.

Question 11 of 18

Which group of elements was completely missing from Mendeleev's original 1869 periodic table?

Explanation: Inert/noble gases were not discovered in 1869 and were added later as Group 0.

The atomic weight correction formula utilized by Mendeleev was:

Explanation: Atomic Mass equals Equivalent Weight multiplied by Valency.

Question 13 of 18

Which element's atomic weight was corrected by Mendeleev alongside Beryllium?

Explanation: Mendeleev corrected the atomic weights of Beryllium, Indium, Gold, Platinum, and Uranium.

Question 14 of 18

In Mendeleev's periodic table, transition triads like Fe, Co, Ni are placed in which group?

Explanation: Group VIII contained nine transition elements arranged in three triads: (Fe, Co, Ni), (Ru, Rh, Pd), and (Os, Ir, Pt).

Question 15 of 18

What compound types did Mendeleev primarily study to determine the chemical properties of elements?

Explanation: Hydrogen and Oxygen form compounds with almost all elements, making hydrides and oxides the fundamental chemical criteria for Mendeleev's grouping.

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following is NOT an anomalous pair in Mendeleev's classification?

Explanation: Sodium (23) and Potassium (39) are arranged in increasing order of atomic mass; they are not an anomalous pair.

Question 17 of 18

Eka-Manganese predicted by Mendeleev corresponds to which synthesized transition element?

Explanation: Eka-Manganese was later identified as Technetium (Tc), element 43.

Question 18 of 18

Which major limitation of Mendeleev's table stems from placing isotopes of an element?

Explanation: Since Mendeleev's table was based on atomic mass, isotopes of the same element (different mass) should have different positions, which contradicted their identical chemical behavior.

What is the equivalent weight of Beryllium if its atomic mass is 9.0 and valency is 2?

Which pair of elements has inverted atomic masses in Mendeleev's periodic table?

Solution: Tellurium (127.6) comes before Iodine (126.9), which is an anomalous pair.

Question 3 of 10
The formula of the hydride formed by Group III elements in Mendeleev's table is:

Solution: Group III elements exhibit a valency of 3, yielding hydrides with formula RH3 (e.g., BH3, AlH3).

Question 4 of 10
Which feature of Mendeleev's table best highlights its predictive utility?

Solution: Leaving empty slots and accurately predicting properties of undiscovered elements (Eka-elements) was Mendeleev's main success.

Question 5 of 10
Gold (Au) and Platinum (Pt) had their atomic weights revised by Mendeleev because:

Solution: Mendeleev revised the accepted atomic masses of Pt and Au so their placement aligned with chemical group trends.

Question 6 of 10
Why were coinage metals (Cu, Ag, Au) grouped with alkali metals (Li, Na, K) in Mendeleev's table?

Solution: Both Cu, Ag, Au (Group IB) and Li, Na, K (Group IA) form monovalent oxides of formula R2O.

Question 7 of 10
The atomic weight of Argon is 39.9 and Potassium is 39.1. In Mendeleev's modified periodic table:

Solution: In the modified Mendeleev table, Argon (Group 0) precedes Potassium (Group I) despite having a higher atomic mass.

Question 8 of 10
The total number of vertical groups in Mendeleev's original 1869 periodic table was:

Solution: Mendeleev's original table contained 8 vertical groups (I to VIII).

Question 9 of 10
Which property of Eka-Aluminium predicted by Mendeleev matched Gallium closely?

Question 10 of 10
What primary limitation prevented Mendeleev from correctly placing isotopes?

Solution: Because isotopes have different atomic masses, a classification based strictly on atomic mass would require multiple slots for a single element.
