Periodic Classification and Periodicity

Merits of Mendeleev's Periodic Classification

Mendeleev's periodic law states that the physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic weights. His classification organized 63 known elements into groups and periods while successfully predicting undiscovered elements and correcting erroneous atomic masses.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Systematic Classification and Grouping

  • Organized 63 known elements into 8 vertical columns called groups and 7 horizontal rows called periods.
  • Elements in the same group exhibit similar chemical properties and valencies based on oxide and hydride formulas.
  • Divided groups I to VII into sub-groups A and B to separate representative elements and transition elements.
  • Group VIII contained nine transition elements arranged in three triads (Fe-Co-Ni, Ru-Rh-Pd, Os-Ir-Pt).

Prediction of Undiscovered Elements

  • Left intentional gaps in the periodic table for undiscovered elements based on periodic trends.
  • Predicted properties of Eka-boron, which was later discovered as Scandium (Sc).
  • Predicted properties of Eka-aluminium, which matched the later discovered Gallium (Ga).
  • Predicted properties of Eka-silicon, which perfectly corresponded to Germanium (Ge).
  • Predicted Eka-manganese, later identified as the synthetic element Technetium (Tc).

Correction of Doubtful Atomic Masses

  • Corrected atomic mass of Beryllium (Be) from 13.5 to 9.0 based on its valency of 2 instead of 3.
  • Beryllium equivalent weight (4.5) multiplied by correct valency (2) yielded atomic mass 9.0 (BeO formula).
  • Corrected atomic mass of Indium (In) from 76 to 114 by establishing trivalent nature (In2O3).
  • Revised atomic masses of Gold (Au) and Platinum (Pt) to align with periodic placement.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Dulong and Petit's Law
Atomic Weight × Specific Heat ≈ 6.4
Conditions: Applies to solid metallic elements at room temperature.
Units: cal/g·°C for specific heat
Equivalent Mass Formula
Equivalent Mass = Mass of Element / Mass of Hydrogen combined (× 1.008)
Conditions: Standard stoichiometric combination method.
Units: g/equiv
Moseley's Law (Modern Revision)
√ν = a(Z - b)
Conditions: Relates X-ray frequency (ν) to atomic number (Z), proving Z is more fundamental than atomic mass.
Units: ν in Hz, Z is dimensionless
Atomic Weight - Valency Relation
Atomic Weight = Equivalent Weight × Valency
Conditions: Used by Mendeleev to correct atomic weights of elements like Be and In.
Units: g/mol or amu
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Elements arranged strictly in increasing order of atomic mass

Observation: Anomalous pairs: Ar (39.9) before K (39.1), Co (58.9) before Ni (58.7), Te (127.6) before I (126.9) — Mendeleev prioritized chemical property similarities over strict atomic weight sequence.

Exception Grouping elements solely by chemical similarity

Observation: Dissimilar elements placed in same group (e.g., alkali metals Li, Na, K grouped with coinage metals Cu, Ag, Au) — Sub-groups A and B were forced into a single main group due to identical primary valencies.

Exception Every element occupies a distinct position based on atomic weight

Observation: Isotopes of elements have different atomic weights but occupy the same position — Mendeleev's periodic law was based on atomic mass rather than atomic number.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Mendeleev's periodic law was published in 1869 based on 63 known elements.
  • Eka-boron corresponds to Scandium (Sc, Z=21).
  • Eka-aluminium corresponds to Gallium (Ga, Z=31).
  • Eka-silicon corresponds to Germanium (Ge, Z=32).
  • Beryllium atomic mass was corrected from 13.5 to 9.0 amu because its valency is 2.
  • Noble gases were added later to Group 0 without altering the original table layout.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Eka-boron with Gallium instead of Scandium.
  • Assuming Mendeleev used atomic number instead of atomic weight as the classification basis.
  • Thinking noble gases were present in Mendeleev's original 1869 table; they were added in 1890s.
  • Believing Beryllium's equivalent mass was wrong; only its assumed valency was incorrect.

⚡ Quick Revision: Merits of Mendeleev's Periodic Classification

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Mendeleev's Periodic Law

Physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic masses. Chemical similarities are reflected in structural formulas of oxides and hydrides.

Predictive Capability

By leaving vacant spaces, Mendeleev successfully predicted properties of undiscovered elements (Sc, Ga, Ge, Tc) with remarkable quantitative accuracy.

Correction of Valency and Mass

Utilized periodic trends to recalculate valencies of elements like Be, In, Au, and Pt, leading to correct atomic mass determinations.

Group and Period Framework

Provided a systematic foundation featuring 8 groups and 7 periods that eventually enabled the development of the modern periodic table.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Atomic Mass = Equivalent Mass × Valency.
  • Be equivalent mass = 4.5, corrected valency = 2, corrected atomic mass = 9.0.
  • Eka-boron = Scandium (Sc).
  • Eka-aluminium = Gallium (Ga).
  • Eka-silicon = Germanium (Ge).

📌 Quick Recall

  • Mendeleev organized 63 elements by atomic mass and chemical stoichiometry.
  • He correctly predicted properties of unfound elements using group trends.
  • He corrected erroneous atomic weights by reassessing valencies.
  • The table accommodated noble gases later without destroying its core arrangement.

⚖️ Key Differences

Eka-Aluminium VS Eka-Silicon

Eka-Aluminium is Gallium (Group 13); Eka-Silicon is Germanium (Group 14).

Mendeleev Periodic Law VS Modern Periodic Law

Mendeleev's law is based on atomic weight; Modern law is based on atomic number (Z).

Question 1 of 18

Which element was predicted by Mendeleev as Eka-aluminium?

Explanation: Eka-aluminium was predicted by Mendeleev and later discovered as Gallium (Ga) by Lecoq de Boisbaudran in 1875.

Question 2 of 18

The atomic weight of Beryllium was corrected by Mendeleev from 13.5 to 9.0 by changing its assumed valency from:

Explanation: Beryllium's equivalent weight is 4.5. It was previously assumed trivalent (4.5 × 3 = 13.5), but Mendeleev reassigned it as bivalent (4.5 × 2 = 9.0).

Question 3 of 18

Eka-silicon predicted by Mendeleev corresponds to which modern element?

Explanation: Eka-silicon corresponds to Germanium (Ge), discovered by Clemens Winkler in 1886.

Question 4 of 18

Which of the following pair of elements is an anomalous pair in Mendeleev's periodic table?

Explanation: Cobalt (58.9) was placed before Nickel (58.7) to keep Cobalt with Rh and Ir, violating strict increasing atomic mass sequence.

Question 5 of 18

Eka-boron was later named as:

Explanation: Eka-boron predicted by Mendeleev was discovered in 1879 by Lars Fredrik Nilson and named Scandium.

Question 6 of 18

How many elements were known when Mendeleev presented his periodic table?

Explanation: Mendeleev formulated his periodic table in 1869 based on the 63 elements known at that time.

Question 7 of 18

What basis did Mendeleev use to group elements together in the same column?

Explanation: Mendeleev grouped elements sharing similar general chemical formulas for oxides (e.g., R2O, RO) and hydrides (e.g., RH, RH2).

Question 8 of 18

Indium's atomic weight was corrected by Mendeleev by considering its valency to be:

Explanation: Indium was considered divalent giving atomic weight ~76. Mendeleev determined it was trivalent (In2O3), fixing the mass to ~114.

Question 9 of 18

Which group was added later to Mendeleev's periodic table without disturbing the existing structure?

Explanation: The Zero Group was added by William Ramsay after noble gases were discovered, leaving the rest of the table intact.

Question 10 of 18

Eka-manganese predicted by Mendeleev is modern-day:

Explanation: Eka-manganese was predicted by Mendeleev and later synthesized as Technetium (Tc, Z=43).

Question 11 of 18

In Mendeleev's periodic table, transition elements were placed in group:

Explanation: Group VIII contained nine transition metals grouped into three triads (Fe-Co-Ni, Ru-Rh-Pd, Os-Ir-Pt).

Question 12 of 18

The formula of oxide predicted for Eka-aluminium was:

Explanation: Since Eka-aluminium belonged to Group III, its predicted oxide formula was E2O3, matching Ga2O3.

Question 13 of 18

Which of the following elements had its atomic mass corrected by Mendeleev?

Explanation: Mendeleev corrected the atomic mass sequence of Platinum (Pt) along with Gold (Au) and Beryllium (Be).

Question 14 of 18

Which chemical property was primary in Mendeleev's periodic law?

Explanation: Mendeleev focused on the compositions of hydrides and oxides as primary criteria for chemical periodicity.

Question 15 of 18

In Mendeleev's periodic table, Tellurium (127.6) was placed before Iodine (126.9). Why?

Explanation: Tellurium was placed in Group VI and Iodine in Group VII to align Iodine with other halogens based on chemical reactivity.

Question 16 of 18

Explanation: Dulong and Petit's law (Atomic Weight × Specific Heat ≈ 6.4) helped confirm approximate atomic weights.

Question 17 of 18

What formula did Mendeleev predict for the hydride of Eka-silicon?

Explanation: Eka-silicon (Group IV) was predicted to form a hydride of formula EH4, matching Germanium hydride (GeH4).

Question 18 of 18

Which merit of Mendeleev's table directly assisted in the discovery of new elements?

Explanation: Leaving deliberate vacant gaps with predicted physical/chemical properties guided experimentalists to discover new elements.

Question 1 of 10
An element has an equivalent weight of 4.5. If its oxide has formula MO, what is its correct atomic weight?

Solution: In MO oxide, valency of metal M is 2. Atomic weight = Equivalent weight × Valency = 4.5 × 2 = 9.0.

Question 2 of 10
Which pair of elements was placed in the same group in Mendeleev's original table despite distinct properties?

Solution: Group I contained sub-group A (alkali metals like K) and sub-group B (coinage metals like Cu), which differ greatly in properties.

Question 3 of 10
What is the density predicted for Eka-aluminium compared to Gallium?

Solution: Mendeleev predicted density of 5.9 g/cm³ for Eka-aluminium, which agreed remarkably with Gallium's measured 5.94 g/cm³.

Question 4 of 10
Which group in Mendeleev's periodic table did not have sub-groups A and B?

Solution: Group VIII contained nine elements in triads without sub-group A and B divisions.

Question 5 of 10
Scandium was discovered by Nilson in 1879. Which predicted element's properties did it fulfill?

Solution: Scandium fulfilled the properties predicted for Eka-boron.

Question 6 of 10
Mendeleev's correction of Indium's atomic weight depended on changing its valency from:

Solution: Indium was previously assumed divalent (giving 38 × 2 = 76). Reassigning valency 3 gave 38 × 3 = 114.

Question 7 of 10
Why did Mendeleev place Cobalt before Nickel?

Solution: Cobalt was grouped with Rhodium and Iridium due to similar chemical behavior, placing chemical property agreement above atomic mass sequence.

Question 8 of 10
Noble gases were easily incorporated into Mendeleev's periodic table later because:

Solution: Zero valency of noble gases enabled placement in a dedicated 'Zero Group' without shifting other elements.

Question 9 of 10
The total number of vertical columns (groups) in Mendeleev's revised periodic table (including Zero group) was:

Solution: The revised table contained 9 groups: Group 0 and Groups I through VIII.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following is NOT a merit of Mendeleev's periodic table?

Solution: Inability to explain isotope positions was a defect, not a merit, of Mendeleev's periodic table.
