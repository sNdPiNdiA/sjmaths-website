Periodic Classification and Periodicity

Mendeleev Periodic Classification

Mendeleev's periodic law states that the physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic weights. The classification organized 63 known elements into vertical groups and horizontal periods based on atomic weights and stoichiometry of their oxides and hydrides.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structural Organization of Mendeleev Periodic Table

  • Organized 63 known elements into 8 vertical Groups (I to VIII) and 7 horizontal Periods.
  • Groups I to VII were split into Subgroup A (main/representative elements) and Subgroup B (transition elements).
  • Group VIII contained nine transition elements arranged in three triads: (Fe, Co, Ni), (Ru, Rh, Pd), and (Os, Ir, Pt).
  • Classification relied on formulas of oxides (R2O to RO4) and hydrides (RH to RH4) formed by elements.

Prediction of Undiscovered Elements and Mass Corrections

  • Left deliberate gaps in the table for undiscovered elements and accurately predicted their properties.
  • Eka-Aluminium was later discovered as Gallium (Ga); Eka-Silicon was discovered as Germanium (Ge).
  • Eka-Boron was identified as Scandium (Sc), and Eka-Manganese as Technetium (Tc).
  • Corrected the atomic weight of Beryllium from 13.5 (assuming valency 3) to 9 (with correct valency 2: 4.5 × 2 = 9).

Anomalies and Anomalous Pairs in Mendeleev Classification

  • Inverted Atomic Mass Pairs: Placed heavier elements before lighter elements to preserve chemical periodicity.
  • Key anomalous pairs: Ar (39.9) before K (39.1), Co (58.9) before Ni (58.7), Te (127.6) before I (126.9), Th (232.0) before Pa (231.0).
  • Position of Hydrogen: Remained ambiguous due to dual chemical behavior resembling Group IA alkali metals and Group VIIA halogens.
  • Position of Isotopes: Failed to accommodate isotopes since elements were classified strictly by atomic mass.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Elements must be arranged in strict increasing order of atomic weights.

Observation: Argon (39.9) precedes Potassium (39.1); Cobalt (58.93) precedes Nickel (58.69). — Mendeleev prioritized chemical similarity and group properties over absolute atomic weight order.

Exception Elements in the same subgroup must exhibit similar chemical reactivity.

Observation: Group IB coinage metals (Cu, Ag, Au) placed alongside Group IA alkali metals (Li, Na, K). — Both groups shared an oxidation state of +1 despite vast differences in chemical reactivity and metallic nature.

Exception Isotopes of an element have different atomic weights and should occupy different positions.

Observation: Isotopes like 1H, 2H, 3H were given a single position in the table. — Chemical properties of isotopes are identical, revealing a fundamental flaw in mass-based classification.

Exception Elements of Group VIII exhibit uniform group valency of 8.

Observation: Fe, Co, Ni exhibit principal oxidation states of +2 and +3, rarely reaching +8. — Transition elements possess d-orbital interactions that do not conform to standard main-group valency rules.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Dmitri Mendeleev published his Periodic Law and Table in 1869 based on 63 known elements.
  • Beryllium's atomic mass was corrected from 13.5 to 9.0 by assigning its true valency of 2 instead of 3.
  • Eka-Aluminium (predicted density 6.0 g/cm3) corresponds to Gallium (experimental density 5.9 g/cm3).
  • Eka-Silicon (predicted atomic weight 72) corresponds to Germanium (experimental atomic weight 72.6).
  • Noble gases were absent in the original 1869 table and were later added into a separate Group 0 by Ramsay and Mendeleev.
  • The 4 major anomalous mass pairs are Argon-Potassium, Cobalt-Nickel, Tellurium-Iodine, and Thorium-Protactinium.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Eka-Boron with Gallium: Eka-Boron is Scandium (Sc), whereas Eka-Aluminium is Gallium (Ga).
  • Assuming noble gases were part of Mendeleev's original 1869 table: They were discovered in the late 1890s and added later as Group 0.
  • Misapplying atomic mass correction: Forgetting that Atomic Weight = Equivalent Weight × Valency, not Equivalent Weight divided by Valency.
  • Confusing Subgroup A and B: Subgroup A contained normal (representative) elements while Subgroup B contained transition elements.

⚡ Quick Revision: Mendeleev Periodic Classification

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Mendeleev Periodic Law and Table Layout

Mendeleev organized 63 elements by increasing atomic weight into 8 groups and 7 periods. Groups I-VII divided into Subgroups A and B, while Group VIII contained transition element triads.

Predictive Power and Gap Filling

Mendeleev left empty spaces for undiscovered elements, naming them with the prefix 'Eka-'. He correctly predicted properties of Eka-Aluminium (Ga), Eka-Silicon (Ge), and Eka-Boron (Sc).

Correction of Atomic Masses

Atomic mass determination errors were rectified using Equivalent Weight × Valency. Beryllium's valency was corrected from 3 to 2, correcting its atomic weight from 13.5 to 9.

Limitations of Atomic Mass Criterion

Inverted mass pairs (Ar-K, Co-Ni, Te-I), lack of positions for isotopes, uncertain placement of Hydrogen, and grouping alkali metals with coinage metals led to the transition to atomic numbers.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Periodic Law (Mendeleev's Law)
Statement that physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of atomic weights.
Eka-Element (Eka-prefix)
Sanskrit prefix meaning 'one', used by Mendeleev to name undiscovered elements below a known element.
Anomalous Pair (Inverted Pair)
A pair of elements placed contrary to increasing atomic weight order to preserve group homology.
Subgroup (Subgroup A / B)
Divisions within main Groups I to VII separating representative elements (A) from transition elements (B).
Transition Triad (Group VIII Triad)
A set of three chemically similar transition metals placed together in Group VIII (e.g., Fe, Co, Ni).
Equivalent Mass (Eq. Wt.)
Mass of an element that combines with or displaces 1.008 g of hydrogen or 8 g of oxygen.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Eka-Boron = Scandium (Sc)
  • Eka-Aluminium = Gallium (Ga)
  • Eka-Silicon = Germanium (Ge)
  • Eka-Manganese = Technetium (Tc)
  • Be Corrected Mass = 4.5 × 2 = 9.0 g/mol

📌 Quick Recall

  • Mendeleev classified 63 elements into 8 groups based on atomic mass and chemical formulas of oxides/hydrides.
  • Predicted vacant elements: Ga, Ge, Sc, Tc with high quantitative accuracy.
  • Corrected atomic masses of Be, In, Au, Pt using correct valencies.
  • Failed to place isotopes and hydrogen properly due to reliance on atomic mass instead of atomic number.

⚖️ Key Differences

Subgroup A Elements VS Subgroup B Elements

Subgroup A consists of representative s-block and p-block elements; Subgroup B consists of transition d-block elements.

According to Mendeleev's Periodic Law, the physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their:

Question 2 of 18

How many elements were known when Mendeleev presented his periodic table in 1869?

Explanation: There were 63 known elements when Mendeleev formulated his periodic table in 1869.

Question 3 of 18

The element predicted by Mendeleev as 'Eka-Aluminium' is:

Explanation: Eka-Aluminium was later discovered by Lecoq de Boisbaudran in 1875 and named Gallium (Ga).

Question 4 of 18

Which element corresponds to Mendeleev's predicted 'Eka-Silicon'?

Explanation: Eka-Silicon was discovered by Clemens Winkler in 1886 and named Germanium (Ge).

Question 5 of 18

Mendeleev corrected the atomic weight of Beryllium from 13.5 to 9.0 by revising its valency from:

Explanation: Beryllium was previously thought to be trivalent (Valency 3, Eq. Wt = 4.5 → Mass = 13.5). Mendeleev established it is divalent (Valency 2, Eq. Wt = 4.5 → Mass = 9.0).

Question 6 of 18

Which of the following constitutes an anomalous pair in Mendeleev's periodic table?

Explanation: Cobalt (58.93) was placed before Nickel (58.69) despite having a higher atomic weight, forming an anomalous pair.

Question 7 of 18

Eka-Boron predicted by Mendeleev was later named:

Explanation: Eka-Boron corresponds to Scandium (Sc), discovered by Lars Fredrik Nilson in 1879.

Question 8 of 18

In Mendeleev's periodic table, total number of main vertical groups was:

Explanation: Mendeleev's original table contained 8 vertical groups designated by Roman numerals I to VIII.

Question 9 of 18

Which group in Mendeleev's original 1869 table contained transition element triads like Fe, Co, Ni?

Explanation: Group VIII contained nine transition elements arranged in three triads: (Fe, Co, Ni), (Ru, Rh, Pd), and (Os, Ir, Pt).

Question 10 of 18

Noble gases were inserted into Mendeleev's periodic table as:

Explanation: Upon discovery of inert gases by Ramsay, a new group called Group 0 was added to the periodic table without disturbing existing positions.

Question 11 of 18

The general formula of oxides of Group III elements in Mendeleev's table is:

Explanation: Group III elements form oxides of general formula R2O3, matching the valency of 3.

Question 12 of 18

Which of the following pairs is another example of an inverted atomic mass order in Mendeleev's table?

Explanation: Tellurium (127.6) was placed before Iodine (126.9) to ensure Iodine remained in Group VII with other halogens.

Question 13 of 18

What formula did Mendeleev assign to the hydride of Group IV elements?

Explanation: Group IV elements exhibit valency 4 toward hydrogen, giving the general hydride formula RH4 (e.g., CH4, SiH4).

Question 14 of 18

Which element's predicted formula for chloride was ECl3, later verified as GaCl3?

Explanation: Eka-Aluminium was predicted to form a chloride ECl3, which matched Gallium trichloride (GaCl3).

Question 15 of 18

Why could isotopes not be properly placed in Mendeleev's periodic table?

Explanation: Isotopes have different atomic weights, requiring separate positions under mass classification, yet their chemical properties are identical.

Question 16 of 18

Which coin metal was placed in Subgroup IB along with alkali metals in Mendeleev's table?

Explanation: Copper (Cu), along with Ag and Au, was placed in Subgroup IB, while alkali metals were in Subgroup IA.

Question 17 of 18

What is the equivalent weight of Beryllium if its corrected atomic mass is 9.0 and valency is 2?

Explanation: Equivalent weight = Atomic Mass / Valency = 9.0 / 2 = 4.5.

Question 18 of 18

The element Technetium (Tc) was predicted by Mendeleev under the name:

Explanation: Eka-Manganese was the name given by Mendeleev to the element below manganese, later isolated as Technetium (Tc).

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following statement is FALSE regarding Mendeleev's Periodic Table?

Solution: Mendeleev's periodic table could NOT assign separate positions for isotopes despite their different atomic weights, which was one of its primary limitations.

Question 2 of 10
The predicted atomic weight of Eka-Silicon by Mendeleev was 72. The actual atomic weight of Germanium (Ge) is approximately:

Solution: Mendeleev predicted atomic mass 72 for Eka-Silicon; Germanium was found to have an atomic mass of 72.6 g/mol.

Question 3 of 10
Which set of transition metals constituted the third triad of Group VIII in Mendeleev's table?

Solution: The three triads in Group VIII were 1st: Fe-Co-Ni, 2nd: Ru-Rh-Pd, and 3rd: Os-Ir-Pt.

Question 4 of 10
The valency of an element belonging to Group V in Mendeleev's periodic table with respect to hydrogen is:

Solution: For Groups V to VII, valency with respect to hydrogen is (8 - Group Number). For Group V, Valency = 8 - 5 = 3 (e.g., NH3, PH3).

Question 5 of 10
Thorium (Th, Z=90, Mass=232) and Protactinium (Pa, Z=91, Mass=231) form an anomalous pair because:

Solution: Thorium (232.0) has a higher atomic weight than Protactinium (231.0), yet Thorium precedes Protactinium in Mendeleev's arrangement.

Question 6 of 10
An element forms a hydride RH3 and an oxide R2O5. To which group does it belong in Mendeleev's table?

Solution: Oxide R2O5 indicates a valency of 5 toward oxygen, which corresponds to Group V.

Question 7 of 10
Indium's atomic weight was corrected by Mendeleev using its correct valency of:

Solution: Indium was initially considered divalent (Eq wt 38 × 2 = 76), but Mendeleev corrected its valency to 3, giving an atomic weight of 38 × 3 = 114.

Question 8 of 10
Which alkali metal was placed in Subgroup IA along with Sodium and Potassium?

Solution: Lithium (Li), Sodium (Na), and Potassium (K) belong to Subgroup IA. Cu, Ag, and Au belong to Subgroup IB.

Question 9 of 10
What was the density predicted by Mendeleev for Eka-Aluminium, and what is the density of Gallium?

Solution: Mendeleev predicted a density of ~6.0 g/cm3 for Eka-Aluminium; Gallium was found to have a density of 5.9 g/cm3.

Question 10 of 10
Why was the position of Hydrogen considered a major drawback in Mendeleev's Periodic Table?

Solution: Hydrogen forms H+ ions like alkali metals (Group IA) and H- ions/diatomic molecules like halogens (Group VIIA), making its position ambiguous.
