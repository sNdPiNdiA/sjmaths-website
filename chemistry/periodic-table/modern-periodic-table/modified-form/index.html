Periodic Classification and Periodicity

Modified Form of Mendeleev's Periodic Table

The modified form of Mendeleev's periodic table arranges elements in order of increasing atomic number following Moseley's law, rather than atomic weights. It consists of nine vertical columns called groups (0, I to VIII) and seven horizontal rows called periods, eliminating several anomalies of the original table.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structural Layout and Grouping

  • Consists of 9 vertical groups: Group 0 (noble gases) and Groups I through VIII.
  • Groups I to VII are subdivided into subgroups A and B representing representative and transition elements respectively.
  • Group VIII contains 9 transition elements arranged in three triads (Fe-Co-Ni, Ru-Rh-Pd, Os-Ir-Pt) without subgroup division.
  • Elements in subgroup A possess electrons entering s or p orbitals, while subgroup B elements involve d orbitals.

Periods and Electronic Shell Filling

  • Contains 7 horizontal periods corresponding to principal shell numbers n = 1 to 7.
  • Period 1 contains 2 elements (Very Short Period), Periods 2 and 3 contain 8 elements each (Short Periods).
  • Periods 4 and 5 contain 18 elements each (Long Periods), divided into two series (even and odd series).
  • Period 6 contains 32 elements (Very Long Period), including 14 lanthanides placed separately at the bottom.

Improvements over Original Mendeleev Table

  • Inclusion of Group 0 accommodates inert gases discovered by Ramsay without disturbing existing elements.
  • Atomic number Z replaces atomic weight as the fundamental periodicity property according to Moseley's experiments.
  • Separation of main group elements (Subgroup A) from transition metals (Subgroup B) resolved major chemical disparities.
  • Explains the position of isotopes under a single atomic number, resolving a major defect of the original table.

Maximum Shell Electron Capacity
Nmax = 2n2
Conditions: Principal quantum number n ≥ 1
Units: Dimensionless electron count
Period Capacity for Even Periods
N = ½(n + 2)2
Conditions: n is an even period number (n = 2, 4, 6)
Units: Number of elements
Period Capacity for Odd Periods
N = ½(n + 1)2
Conditions: n is an odd period number (n = 1, 3, 5)
Units: Number of elements
Slater's Effective Nuclear Charge Rule
Z* = Z - σ
Conditions: Calculates net attraction experienced by an electron in a multi-electron atom
Units: Z* and Z are unitless charge units
Moseley's Law
√ν = a(Z - b)
Conditions: Applies to characteristic X-ray emission spectra of elements
Units: ν in Hz, Z is atomic number, a and b are constants
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Elements are arranged in strictly increasing order of atomic mass in Mendeleev's framework

Observation: Anomalous pairs like Ar (39.9) preceding K (39.1), Co (58.9) preceding Ni (58.7), and Te (127.6) preceding I (126.9) — Moseley proved atomic number Z governs periodic properties, making Ar (Z=18) correctly placed before K (Z=19).

Exception Group assignment correlates directly with highest positive valence state

Observation: Copper, Silver, and Gold exhibit oxidation states of +2 and +3 despite belonging to Group IB — Transition elements involve (n-1)d electrons in bonding, leading to variable valencies exceeding group number.

Exception Lanthanides and Actinides belong to Group IIIB in the main periodic body

Observation: 14 Lanthanides and 14 Actinides are segregated below the main body of the periodic table — Accommodating 14 f-block elements inside the main matrix would severely disrupt the periodicity of d-block elements.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Subgroup A vs Subgroup B Identification
Subgroup A = Normal/Representative (s & p block); Subgroup B = Transition Metals (d block)

Application: If the outer electronic configuration is ns1-2 or ns2np1-5, place in Subgroup A. If (n-1)d1-10ns1-2, place in Subgroup B.

Determining Group Number in Modified Table
Group = Valence Electrons (for s & p) or ( Valence e- in (n-1)d + ns )

Application: Quickly assigns elements to Groups I to VIII based on total outer valency electrons.

Moseley's Frequency Shortcut
√ν ∝ Z

Application: A plot of √ν vs Z yields a straight line, confirming Z as the fundamental atomic property.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Moseley's Law √ν = a(Z - b) proved that atomic number Z, not atomic weight, dictates chemical properties.
  • Group 0 was introduced in the modified table by Sir William Ramsay to accommodate noble gases.
  • Groups I to VII are subdivided into Subgroup A (Representative/Normal elements) and Subgroup B (Transition elements).
  • Group VIII consists of 9 elements grouped in 3 triads: (Fe, Co, Ni), (Ru, Rh, Pd), and (Os, Ir, Pt).
  • The 6th period is the longest complete period in the modified table, containing 32 elements.
  • Hydrogen remains in an anomalous position, displaying properties of both Group IA (alkali metals) and Group VIIA (halogens).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Subgroup A with Subgroup B: Subgroup A contains s and p block elements, whereas Subgroup B contains d block transition elements.
  • Assuming Group VIII has A and B subgroups: Group VIII is undivided and contains three sets of triads.
  • Mishandling atomic weight inversion: Believing Ar-K anomaly violates Modern Periodic Law when it actually follows increasing atomic numbers (18 and 19).
  • Misidentifying the position of Lanthanides: Lanthanides belong to Group IIIB (or Group 3) and Period 6, despite being written at the bottom.

⚡ Quick Revision: Modified Form of Mendeleev's Periodic Table

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Moseley's Discovery and Modern Periodic Law

Henry Moseley studied X-ray spectrum lines (&Kalpha; lines) of various elements and discovered that √ν is linearly proportional to atomic number Z. This led to the Modern Periodic Law: physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.

Group Architecture and Subgrouping

The modified table contains 9 groups (0, I-VIII). Groups I to VII split into A and B subgroups based on representative versus transition character, while Group 0 holds unreactive monoatomic gases and Group VIII holds transitional metal triads.

Period Capacity and Shell Filling

Periods correspond to principal energy levels n = 1 to 7. The filling order following Aufbau principle determines period lengths: 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, and incomplete 7th period.

Persistent Defects of the Modified Table

Despite structural improvements, the modified table fails to fix the dual position of Hydrogen, cannot integrate lanthanides and actinides inside the main table, and groups chemically dissimilar metals like Cu and K together under same main group I.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Atomic Number (Z)
Number of protons in the nucleus of an atom, defining its position in the periodic table.
Group 0 (Inert Gases)
Vertical column containing He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn with stable ns2np6 valence shell.
Subgroup A (Main Group)
Subgroups containing s-block and p-block representative elements.
Subgroup B (Transition Group)
Subgroups containing d-block transition elements.
Transition Triads (Group VIII)
Clusters of three chemically similar elements in Period 4, 5, and 6 of Group VIII.
Lanthanides (4f Series)
14 elements from Ce (Z=58) to Lu (Z=71) placed at the bottom of the periodic table.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Moseley's equation: √ν = a(Z - b)
  • Total number of groups in Modified Mendeleev Table = 9
  • Triads in Group VIII: (Fe, Co, Ni), (Ru, Rh, Pd), (Os, Ir, Pt)
  • Subgroup A = Representative elements, Subgroup B = Transition elements
  • Position of Lanthanides: Period 6, Group IIIB

📌 Quick Recall

  • The modified Mendeleev periodic table is based on atomic numbers following Moseley's experimental findings.
  • It expands Mendeleev's 8 groups to 9 groups by incorporating Group 0 for zero-valent noble gases.
  • Groups I to VII are subdivided into Subgroups A and B, effectively separating s/p elements from d-block metals.
  • Group VIII retains 9 elements in 3 central triads without subgroup splitting.

⚖️ Key Differences

Original Mendeleev Table VS Modified Mendeleev Table

Original table uses Atomic Weight with 8 groups; Modified table uses Atomic Number with 9 groups (includes Group 0).

The modified form of Mendeleev's periodic table is based on which fundamental property?

How many total vertical groups are present in the modified Mendeleev periodic table?

Group 0 was added to Mendeleev's periodic table to accommodate which class of elements?

Explanation: Subgroup A contains main group or representative elements (s-block and p-block).

Which group in the modified Mendeleev table contains triads of elements without sub-classification into A and B?

Moseley's law relating X-ray frequency (ν) to atomic number (Z) is given by which expression?

Question 8 of 20

The anomalous pair problem of Argon (39.9) and Potassium (39.1) was resolved by arranging elements according to:

Explanation: Argon has Z = 18 and Potassium has Z = 19. When arranged by atomic number, Argon naturally precedes Potassium.

Question 9 of 20

Coinage metals (Cu, Ag, Au) are placed in which group and subgroup of the modified table?

Explanation: Coinage metals are transition elements placed in Group IB of the modified periodic table.

How many elements are present in the 1st period of the modified Mendeleev periodic table?

Explanation: The 1st period contains only 2 elements: Hydrogen (H) and Helium (He).

Question 11 of 20

Which element triad belongs to Group VIII in the 4th period?

Explanation: The triad Fe, Co, Ni belongs to Group VIII in the 4th period.

Question 12 of 20

Lanthanides consist of elements with atomic numbers ranging from:

Explanation: Lanthanides consist of 14 elements from Cerium (Z = 58) to Lutetium (Z = 71).

Question 13 of 20

Why are Lanthanides and Actinides placed separately at the bottom of the periodic table?

Explanation: Including 14 f-block elements inside the main body would disrupt the periodicity and width of d-block elements.

Question 14 of 20

In the modified Mendeleev table, halogens are placed in which subgroup?

Explanation: Halogens (F, Cl, Br, I, At) are representative p-block elements placed in Group VIIA.

Question 15 of 20

Which property remains an unresolved defect in the modified Mendeleev periodic table?

Explanation: Hydrogen shows similarities with both alkali metals (Group IA) and halogens (Group VIIA), leaving its position ambiguous.

Question 16 of 20

The screening constant (σ) in Slater's rule for effective nuclear charge Z* = Z - σ measures:

Explanation: The screening constant σ quantifies the shielding or repulsive influence of inner electrons on the valence electron.

Question 17 of 20

Group IB elements (Cu, Ag, Au) and Group IA elements (Li, Na, K) share which common feature in Mendeleev's modified table?

Explanation: Both alkali metals (IA) and coinage metals (IB) are placed under main Group I due to exhibiting a principal oxidation state of +1.

Question 18 of 20

What is the maximum number of electrons that can be accommodated in the 4th energy level (n = 4)?

Question 1 of 10
Which fundamental principle explains why the second and third periods of the periodic table contain 8 elements each?

Solution: In periods 2 and 3, only the ns and np subshells are filled (2 + 6 = 8 electrons), as d-orbitals do not exist for n=2 and 3d is higher in energy than 4s for n=3.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following elements belongs to Group VIII, 5th period triad in the modified Mendeleev table?

Solution: The 5th period Group VIII triad comprises Ruthenium (Ru), Rhodium (Rh), and Palladium (Pd).

Question 3 of 10
In Moseley's equation √ν = a(Z - b), the term 'b' represents the:

Solution: In Moseley's law, 'b' is the screening constant (b = 1 for K-series transitions), accounting for shielding by inner electrons.

Question 4 of 10
Elements belonging to Subgroup VIIA in the modified table possess which valence shell electronic configuration?

Solution: Subgroup VIIA consists of halogens with outer electronic configuration ns2 np5.

Question 5 of 10
Which period is classified as an 'Incomplete Period' in classic modified periodic table descriptions?

Solution: Historically, the 7th period was labeled incomplete as synthetic transuranic elements were continuously being discovered.

Question 6 of 10
What is the primary reason why alkali metals (IA) and coinage metals (IB) were placed in the same main Group I by Mendeleev?

Solution: Mendeleev grouped IA and IB under Group I because both sets of elements form monovalent compounds (e.g., NaCl and CuCl).

Question 7 of 10
An element has atomic number Z = 26. Its position in the modified Mendeleev periodic table is:

Solution: Z = 26 is Iron (Fe), configuration [Ar] 3d6 4s2, which belongs to Period 4 and the first triad of Group VIII.

Question 8 of 10
The element with atomic number Z = 57 (Lanthanum) belongs to which group in the modified table?

Solution: Lanthanum (Z = 57) is a d-block element belonging to Period 6 and Group IIIB.

Question 9 of 10
How many total d-block elements are accommodated in Period 4 of the periodic table?

Solution: Period 4 contains 10 d-block elements corresponding to the 3d transition series from Sc (Z=21) to Zn (Z=30).

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following statements correctly describes a major limitation of the modified Mendeleev table?

Solution: The main matrix cannot accommodate 14 lanthanides and 14 actinides without breaking the continuous flow of d-block elements, requiring them to be placed below.
