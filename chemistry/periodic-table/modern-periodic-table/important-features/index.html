Periodic Classification and Periodicity

Important Features of Modern Periodic Table

The modern periodic table organizes elements in increasing order of their atomic numbers, reflecting the periodic repetition of electronic configurations. Conceived via Moseley's X-ray spectroscopic law, it resolves major anomalies of Mendeleev's table by correlating physical and chemical properties directly to valence shell electron structures.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Moseley's Atomic Number Law and Basis of Modern Table

  • Henry Moseley analyzed X-ray spectra of elements and established that frequency square root (ν1/2) correlates linearly with atomic number (Z), not atomic weight.
  • Modern Periodic Law: Physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.
  • Atomic number equals nuclear charge (number of protons), rendering it the fundamental property determining an element's ground-state electronic configuration.
  • The periodic recurrence of identical outer shell electronic configurations at regular intervals (magic numbers 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32) causes periodic recurrence of properties.

Structural Architecture of Long Form Periodic Table

  • Consists of 7 horizontal rows called periods and 18 vertical columns called groups (IUPAC recommended 1 to 18 notation).
  • The Period number directly represents the highest principal quantum number (n) of valence shell electrons present in the elements of that period.
  • Period 1 (Very Short: 2 elements), Period 2 & 3 (Short: 8 elements each), Period 4 & 5 (Long: 18 elements each), Period 6 (Very Long: 32 elements including 14 Lanthanoids), Period 7 (Incomplete/Long: 32 elements including 14 Actinoids).
  • Group classification: Group 1 (Alkali metals), Group 2 (Alkaline earth metals), Groups 13-16 (Chalcogens/Pnictogens/Icosagens), Group 17 (Halogens), Group 18 (Noble gases).

Blockwise Division and IUPAC Nomenclature of Z > 100

  • s-Block: General outer configuration ns1-2; includes Group 1 and 2, highly electropositive metals with low ionization energies.
  • p-Block: General outer configuration ns2 np1-6; includes Groups 13 to 18, containing metals, metalloids, and non-metals.
  • d-Block: General outer configuration (n-1)d1-10 ns1-2; includes Groups 3 to 12 (Transition elements), forming colored ions and multiple oxidation states.
  • f-Block: General outer configuration (n-2)f1-14 (n-1)d0-1 ns2; comprises Lanthanoids (Ce, Z=58 to Lu, Z=71) and Actinoids (Th, Z=90 to Lr, Z=103), placed separately at the bottom.
  • Elements with Z > 100 are assigned systematic names using roots: 0=nil, 1=un, 2=bi, 3=tri, 4=quad, 5=pent, 6=hex, 7=sept, 8=oct, 9=enn, ending with suffix -ium.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Maximum Electron Capacity of a Shell
Nmax = 2n2
Conditions: Applies to principal quantum shell n
Units: Dimensionless count of electrons
Total Orbitals in a Shell
Norb = n2
Conditions: Applies to principal shell with principal quantum number n
Units: Dimensionless count of degenerate and non-degenerate atomic orbitals
Maximum Electron Capacity of a Subshell
Nsub = 2(2l + 1)
Conditions: Applies to subshell characterized by azimuthal quantum number l
Units: Dimensionless count of electrons (l = 0 for s, 1 for p, 2 for d, 3 for f)
Slater's Rule for Effective Nuclear Charge
Z* = Z - σ
Conditions: Applies to multi-electron atoms to estimate actual nuclear pull felt by a valence electron
Units: Z and Z* in atomic units of charge, σ is shielding constant
Moseley's Law
ν1/2 = a(Z - b)
Conditions: Applies to characteristic X-ray emission spectra of elements
Units: ν in Hz, Z is atomic number, a and b are constants for a specific X-ray spectral line series
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Elements with 1s2 outer configuration belong to s-block.

Observation: Helium (1s2) is placed in Group 18 (p-block position). — Helium exhibits completely filled valence shell stability and inert non-reactive behavior matching Group 18 noble gases.

Exception Elements are classified into blocks according to the subshell receiving the last differential electron.

Observation: Lanthanum (Z=57) and Actinium (Z=89) are d-block elements, but Lanthanoids begin at Cerium (Z=58). — In La, the 5d orbital receives the electron before 4f fill starts; thus La belongs strictly to group 3, d-block.

Exception d-Block transition elements follow regular ns2 (n-1)dx configuration.

Observation: Chromium (Z=24: 3d54s1) and Copper (Z=29: 3d104s1). — Symmetrical electron distribution and extra exchange energy of exactly half-filled (d5) and fully-filled (d10) subshells.

Exception Hydrogen has ns1 valence configuration, so it is an Alkali metal.

Observation: Hydrogen exhibits dual characteristics resembling both Group 1 alkali metals and Group 17 halogens. — Can lose one electron to form H+ (like Na+) or gain one electron to form H- achieving Helium configuration (like F-).

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Group Number Determination Formula
s-block = ns e-; p-block = 12 + np e-; d-block = (n-1)d e- + ns e-; f-block = Group 3

Application: Quickly identify the group number of any element from its ground state valence electronic configuration.

Magic Numbers for Atomic Numbers in Groups
Group 1, 18: +2, +8, +8, +18, +18, +32; Group 2, 13-17: +8, +8, +18, +18, +32; Group 3: +18, +18, +32

Application: Rapidly calculate atomic numbers of successive congeners in any vertical column without memorization.

IUPAC Name Generator for Z > 100
Digits to Latin numerical roots + suffix '-ium'

Application: For Z=104: 1(un) + 0(nil) + 4(quad) + ium = Unnilquadium (Unq).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Moseley's equation ν1/2 = a(Z - b) proved Z is more fundamental than atomic weight.
  • Total elements per period: 1st=2, 2nd=8, 3rd=8, 4th=18, 5th=18, 6th=32, 7th=32.
  • All f-block elements (14 Lanthanoids and 14 Actinoids) belong strictly to Group 3 (III B) and 6th/7th periods.
  • Representative elements consist of s-block and p-block elements excluding Group 18 noble gases.
  • Transuranic elements are artificial synthetic elements with Z > 92 (beyond Uranium).
  • Diagonal relationship is shown by Period 2 elements (Li, Be, B) with Period 3 elements (Mg, Al, Si) due to similar ionic radii and polarising power.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Mistaking Group number of p-block elements as just np electrons instead of 10 + valence electrons (or 12 + np electrons).
  • Assuming f-block elements are distributed across multiple groups; all 28 f-block elements are in Group 3.
  • Counting Helium as a p-block element based on its position in Group 18 (it is an s-block element with 1s2 configuration).
  • Confusing period length calculation with 2n2 formula; 2n2 gives max electrons per shell, not maximum capacity of elements in period n.

⚡ Quick Revision: Important Features of Modern Periodic Table

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Moseley's Discovery & Modern Periodic Law

High-energy X-ray spectral emissions confirmed that frequency square root varies linearly with atomic number Z. Modern Periodic Law states that properties of elements are periodic functions of Z, resolving Mendeleev's isotope and anomalous pair defects.

Subshell Filling & Period Architecture

Periods correspond to the principal quantum number n being filled according to Aufbau principle. Period lengths (2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32) reflect available orbitals in each shell energy band.

Four-Block Categorization

Elements are divided into s, p, d, and f blocks based on the outer subshell receiving the differentiating electron. This demarcates reactive metals, non-metals, transition metals, and inner-transition metals clearly.

IUPAC System for Superheavy Elements

Elements beyond Z=100 are systematically named using Latin roots for digits 0 to 9 followed by suffix '-ium'. Official discovery priority is determined by IUPAC/IUPAP joint working party before naming rights.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Atomic Number (Z)
Number of protons present in the nucleus of an atom.
Representative Elements (Main Group Elements)
Elements of s-block and p-block (Groups 1, 2, 13 to 17).
Transition Elements (d-Block Elements)
Elements having partially filled d-orbitals in atomic or stable oxidation state (Groups 3 to 11).
Inner-Transition Elements (f-Block Elements)
Elements having partially filled (n-2)f subshell; Lanthanoids and Actinoids.
Transuranic Elements (Z > 92)
Synthetic radioactive elements occurring after Uranium in the periodic table.
Diagonal Relationship (Period 2 & 3 Similarity)
Similarity in chemical properties of diagonally adjacent Period 2 and Period 3 elements due to equal ionic potential.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Moseley's Law: ν1/2 = a(Z - b)
  • Group number for d-block = (n-1)d electrons + ns electrons
  • Group number for p-block = 12 + np electrons
  • All f-block elements belong to Group 3
  • Magic numbers for periodicity: 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32

📌 Quick Recall

  • The Modern Periodic Table is constructed based on increasing atomic number Z.
  • 7 Periods correspond to filling of principal energy shell n.
  • 18 Groups group together elements with identical outer shell electron configurations.
  • Properties repeat periodically due to valence electronic configuration recurrence.

⚖️ Key Differences

Transition Elements VS d-Block Elements

All transition elements are d-block elements, but Zn, Cd, Hg (Group 12) are d-block elements that are NOT transition elements because they have completely filled d10 configurations.

Lanthanum (La, Z=57) VS Lanthanoids (Z=58 to 71)

Lanthanum is a d-block element (5d16s2), whereas Lanthanoids are 14 f-block elements starting from Cerium (Z=58) to Lutetium (Z=71).

Question 1 of 18

In Moseley's law ν1/2 = a(Z - b), the term 'b' represents:

Explanation: In Moseley's equation ν1/2 = a(Z - b), 'Z' is the atomic number and 'b' is the screening constant or nuclear shielding constant for a given spectral line series.

Question 2 of 18

The total number of elements present in the 6th period of the long form of the periodic table is:

Explanation: The 6th period involves filling of 6s (1 orbital), 4f (7 orbitals), 5d (5 orbitals), and 6p (3 orbitals). Total 16 orbitals × 2 electrons = 32 elements.

Question 3 of 18

An element with atomic number Z = 37 belongs to which block and period?

Explanation: Z = 37 has electronic configuration [Kr] 5s1. The highest principal quantum number n=5 (5th period) and last electron enters s subshell (s-block, Group 1).

Question 4 of 18

Which group of the long form of periodic table contains all f-block elements?

Explanation: All 28 f-block elements (Lanthanoids Z=58-71 and Actinoids Z=90-103) are assigned to Group 3 (III B).

Question 5 of 18

According to IUPAC nomenclature, what is the systematic symbol for element with atomic number 118?

Explanation: For Z=118: 1=un, 1=un, 8=oct. Name = Ununoctium, symbol = Uuo.

Question 6 of 18

Which of the following d-block elements is NOT considered a transition element?

Explanation: Zinc (Zn, Z=30) has completely filled d-orbitals (3d104s2) in its elemental state as well as in its common oxidation state (Zn2+: 3d10), so it is not a transition element.

Question 7 of 18

The general electronic configuration of d-block elements is:

Explanation: The valence electronic configuration characteristic of d-block elements is (n-1)d1-10 ns1-2.

Question 8 of 18

Elements showing diagonal relationship possess similar:

Explanation: Diagonal relationship between Period 2 and Period 3 elements arises due to nearly equal ionic size and similar ionic potential (φ = charge / radius).

Question 9 of 18

The total number of orbitals present in the n=4 shell is:

Explanation: Total number of orbitals in a shell with principal quantum number n is given by n2. For n=4, Norb = 42 = 16.

Question 10 of 18

Which period of the periodic table is known as the shortest period?

Explanation: Period 1 contains only 2 elements (Hydrogen and Helium) and is called the shortest period.

Question 11 of 18

What is the group number of an element having ground state electronic configuration [Ar] 3d5 4s1?

Explanation: For d-block elements, Group number = (n-1)d electrons + ns electrons = 5 + 1 = 6 (Group 6, Chromium).

Question 12 of 18

The electronic configuration of an element is 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p5. Its group number in the periodic table is:

Explanation: The valence shell configuration is 3s2 3p5 (p-block). Group number for p-block = 12 + np electrons = 12 + 5 = 17 (Halogens).

Question 13 of 18

The transuranic elements are elements with atomic number:

Explanation: Transuranic elements are man-made synthetic radioactive elements having atomic number Z > 92 (elements after Uranium).

Question 14 of 18

Cerium (Z = 58) is a member of:

Explanation: Cerium (Z=58) is the first element of the Lanthanoid series (4f series, Z=58 to 71).

Question 15 of 18

The IUPAC name for element with atomic number Z = 104 is:

Explanation: Z = 104: 1 (un) + 0 (nil) + 4 (quad) + ium = Unnilquadium (Symbol: Unq, official name Rutherfordium).

Question 16 of 18

How many elements are present in the 4th period of the periodic table?

Explanation: Period 4 fills 4s (1), 3d (5), and 4p (3) orbitals. Total 9 orbitals × 2 = 18 elements.

Question 17 of 18

Which element exhibits diagonal relationship with Beryllium (Be)?

Explanation: Beryllium (Period 2, Group 2) shows a diagonal relationship with Aluminum (Period 3, Group 13).

Question 18 of 18

The maximum number of electrons that can be accommodated in a subshell with l = 3 is:

Explanation: Subshell with l = 3 is the f subshell. Maximum capacity = 2(2l + 1) = 2(2(3) + 1) = 2 × 7 = 14 electrons.

Question 1 of 10
An element has valence shell electronic configuration 4s2 4p3. Its atomic number and block are:

Solution: Configuration: [Ar] 3d10 4s2 4p3. Z = 18 + 10 + 2 + 3 = 33 (Arsenic, p-block).

Question 2 of 10
What is the ground state electronic configuration of Actinium (Z = 89)?

Solution: Actinium (Z=89) is a d-block element in Group 3, Period 7 with electron configuration [Rn] 6d1 7s2.

Question 3 of 10
In the 7th period of the modern periodic table, the orbitals filled sequentially are:

Solution: According to Aufbau (n+l) rule, filling order for Period 7 is 7s (n+l=7) → 5f (n+l=8) → 6d (n+l=8) → 7p (n+l=8).

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following elements has the highest screening constant (σ) value for its outermost electron?

Solution: Cesium (Cs) has the largest number of inner shell electrons (54 inner electrons), producing maximum shielding constant σ for its 6s electron.

Question 5 of 10
The correct sequence of elements showing diagonal relationship with Li, Be, and B respectively is:

Solution: Li relates diagonally to Mg, Be relates diagonally to Al, and B relates diagonally to Si.

Question 6 of 10
What is the IUPAC systematic name of the element with atomic number 120?

Solution: 1 (un) + 2 (bi) + 0 (nil) + ium = Unbinilium (Ubn).

Question 7 of 10
Which quantum number determines the period of an element in the long form periodic table?

Solution: The period of an element corresponds to the maximum principal quantum number (n) of its outer valence shell.

Question 8 of 10
The maximum number of elements in 5th period of periodic table is 18 because orbitals filled are:

Solution: In Period 5, subshells filled in order of energy are 5s (1 orbital), 4d (5 orbitals), and 5p (3 orbitals) = 9 orbitals total, accommodating 18 elements.

Question 9 of 10
An element with Z = 25 belongs to which block, group, and period respectively?

Solution: Z = 25 (Manganese) has configuration [Ar] 3d5 4s2. Period = 4, Block = d-block, Group = 5 + 2 = 7.

Question 10 of 10
Group 16 elements of the modern periodic table are collectively termed as:

Solution: Group 16 elements (O, S, Se, Te, Po) are known as Chalcogens (ore-forming elements).
