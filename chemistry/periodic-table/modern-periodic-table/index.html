Periodic Classification and Periodicity

Modern Periodic Table

The Modern Periodic Law states that the physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers (Z). The modern long form of the periodic table organizes elements into 7 horizontal periods and 18 vertical groups based on their ground-state electronic configurations.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Moseley's Law and Basis of Modern Classification

  • Henry Moseley analyzed characteristic X-ray spectra (&Kalpha; lines) of elements and observed that √ν is directly proportional to Z, not atomic weight.
  • The Modern Periodic Law modified Mendeleev's concept by establishing atomic number (Z) as the fundamental property governing elemental behavior.
  • Elements are arranged in order of increasing atomic number, which directly corresponds to total nuclear charge and electronic configuration.
  • Horizontal rows are called periods (7 total), and vertical columns are called groups (18 total under IUPAC system).

Structural Architecture and Block Classification

  • s-Block Elements: Groups 1 and 2, valence configuration ns1-2; includes reactive alkali and alkaline earth metals.
  • p-Block Elements: Groups 13 to 18, valence configuration ns2 np1-6; contains metals, metalloids, non-metals, and noble gases.
  • d-Block Elements: Groups 3 to 12, general configuration (n-1)d1-10 ns0-2; four series (3d, 4d, 5d, 6d) of transition elements.
  • f-Block Elements: Placed separately at bottom, configuration (n-2)f1-14 (n-1)d0-1 ns2; consists of Lanthanoids (Z = 58-71) and Actinoids (Z = 90-103).

IUPAC Nomenclature for Elements with Z > 100

  • Systematic names are derived directly from atomic number digits using numerical roots: 0=nil, 1=un, 2=bi, 3=tri, 4=quad, 5=pent, 6=hex, 7=sept, 8=oct, 9=enn.
  • The suffix -ium is appended at the end of the root combination (e.g., Z = 104 is Unnilquadium, symbol Unq).
  • If 'enn' is followed by 'nil', one 'n' is dropped (ennil); if 'bi' or 'tri' is followed by 'ium', the 'i' is dropped (bium, trium).
  • Official IUPAC names are assigned post-discovery approval (e.g., Z = 118 is Oganesson, Og).

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Expected d-block filling order following Aufbau Principle (ns2 (n-1)dx)

Observation: Chromium (Z = 24: [Ar] 3d5 4s1) and Copper (Z = 29: [Ar] 3d10 4s1) — Extra stability associated with half-filled (d5) and fully-filled (d10) subshells due to high exchange energy and spherical symmetry.

Exception Expected progressive 4f shell filling in Lanthanoids

Observation: Gadolinium (Z = 64: [Xe] 4f7 5d1 6s2) and Lu (Z = 71: [Xe] 4f14 5d1 6s2) — Electron enters 5d orbital instead of 4f to maintain half-filled 4f7 and completely filled 4f14 configurations.

Exception Placement of s-block elements based on valence shell configuration

Observation: Helium (1s2 configuration placed in p-block / Group 18) — Placed in Group 18 due to its completely filled valence shell, inert chemical behavior, and noble gas characteristics.

Exception Palladium ground state configuration (n-1)d8 ns2

Observation: Palladium (Z = 46: [Kr] 4d10 5s0) — Complete transfer of two 5s electrons to 4d subshell creates a exceptionally stable closed-shell 4d10 configuration.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Moseley's equation √ν = a(Z - b) proved Z is more fundamental than atomic weight; 'b' is the screening constant.
  • The 6th period is the longest complete period, containing 32 elements including the 4f lanthanoid series.
  • All elements with Z > 92 (beyond Uranium) are synthetic and termed Transuranic elements.
  • Lanthanoids span Z = 58 (Cerium) to Z = 71 (Lutetium); Lanthanum (Z = 57) itself belongs to the d-block in Group 3.
  • Actinoids span Z = 90 (Thorium) to Z = 103 (Lawrencium); all actinoids are radioactive.
  • Group 11 elements (Cu, Ag, Au) are historically called Coinage metals.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Lanthanum (Z=57) and Actinium (Z=89) as f-block elements; they are d-block elements (Group 3).
  • Writing incorrect period capacity using 2n2 directly; 2n2 gives shell electron capacity, not necessarily the number of elements in period n (e.g., Period 4 has 18 elements, not 32).
  • Mistaking screening constant σ for s-electrons as identical to p or d electrons; Slater's rules assign different weighting values based on quantum numbers.
  • Forgetting that Group number of all f-block elements (Lanthanoids and Actinoids) is strictly Group 3.

⚡ Quick Revision: Modern Periodic Table

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Modern Periodic Law & Moseley's Experiment

Moseley demonstrated that the square root of characteristic X-ray frequency (√ν) correlates linearly with atomic number (Z). This established atomic number as the fundamental organizing criterion for periodic property variation.

Long Form Architecture & Subshell Filling

Elements are divided into s, p, d, and f blocks based on the final subshell being filled according to the Aufbau principle. The table comprises 7 periods corresponding to principal quantum number n and 18 vertical groups.

IUPAC Superheavy Element Nomenclature

Systematic names for elements with Z > 100 utilize Latin/Greek numerical roots ending with '-ium'. Symbols consist of three letters derived from the initials of each numerical root.

Electronic Configuration Anomalies

Deviations from standard Aufbau sequence arise due to half-filled and fully-filled subshell stability (e.g., Cr, Cu, Mo, Pd, Gd), driven by enhanced exchange energy and symmetrical charge distribution.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Atomic Number (Z)
Total number of protons in the atomic nucleus of an element.
Modern Periodic Law (Law of Periodicity)
States physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of atomic number Z.
Screening Constant (σ)
Measure of inner-shell electron shielding that reduces nuclear pull on valence electrons.
Transuranic Elements (Synthetic Elements)
Elements with atomic number Z > 92, synthesized via artificial nuclear reactions.
Lanthanoid Contraction (4f Shielding Effect)
Steady decrease in atomic and ionic radii across 4f series due to poor shielding by 4f electrons.
Representative Elements (Main Group Elements)
Elements belonging to s-block and p-block (excluding Group 18 noble gases).

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Moseley's formula: √ν = a(Z - b)
  • Total elements in periods 1 through 7: 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32
  • All f-block elements belong to Group 3
  • Electronic configuration of Pd (Z = 46): [Kr] 4d10 5s0
  • IUPAC root for 0 is 'nil', 1 is 'un', 9 is 'enn'

📌 Quick Recall

  • Modern periodic table arranges 118 elements by atomic number Z across 7 periods and 18 groups.
  • Division into s, p, d, and f blocks reflects the orbital receiving the differentiating electron.
  • Exceptions in configuration (Cr, Cu, Pd, Gd) stem from subshell stability and exchange energy.
  • Elements Z > 100 follow standardized three-letter IUPAC nomenclature prior to official naming.

⚖️ Key Differences

Lanthanum (La, Z=57) VS Lanthanoids (Z=58 to 71)

Lanthanum is a d-block element, whereas Lanthanoids are 14 f-block elements following Lanthanum.

Period Number VS Principal Quantum Number

Period number directly corresponds to the highest principal quantum number (n) of the valence shell.

Question 1 of 18

Which physical parameter was plotted against square root of frequency (√ν) by Moseley to establish Modern Periodic Law?

Explanation: Moseley plotted √ν against atomic number (Z) and obtained a straight line, establishing Z as the fundamental periodic property.

Question 2 of 18

What is the systematic IUPAC name for the element with atomic number Z = 114?

Explanation: 1 = un, 1 = un, 4 = quad, suffix = ium. Hence, Ununquadium (Symbol: Uuq, now officially Flerovium).

Question 3 of 18

How many elements are present in the 6th period of the modern periodic table?

Explanation: The 6th period fills 6s (2), 4f (14), 5d (10), and 6p (6) subshells, giving a total of 2 + 14 + 10 + 6 = 32 elements.

Question 4 of 18

Which of the following elements has the ground-state electronic configuration [Kr] 4d10 5s0?

Explanation: Palladium (Z = 46) has a completely filled 4d subshell with zero electrons in 5s: [Kr] 4d10 5s0.

Question 5 of 18

An element has atomic number Z = 35. To which block and group does it belong?

Explanation: Z = 35 is Bromine ([Ar] 3d10 4s2 4p5). The last electron enters 4p, so it belongs to p-block and Group 10 + 7 = 17.

Question 6 of 18

To which group do all Lanthanoids and Actinoids belong in the modern periodic table?

Explanation: All f-block elements (Lanthanoids and Actinoids) are formally placed in Group 3 of the periodic table.

Question 7 of 18

What is the IUPAC symbol for the element with atomic number 108?

Explanation: 1 = un (u), 0 = nil (n), 8 = oct (o). The symbol is Unh or Uno; using roots u-n-o gives Uno (Unniloctium).

Question 8 of 18

The electronic configuration of Gadolinium (Z = 64) is:

Explanation: Gadolinium preserves a half-filled 4f subshell, giving configuration [Xe] 4f7 5d1 6s2.

Question 9 of 18

What is the correct order of filling of subshells in the 4th period?

Explanation: According to Aufbau principle ((n+l) rule), filling order in Period 4 is 4s (n+l=4) → 3d (n+l=5) → 4p (n+l=5).

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following pairs of atomic numbers represents elements in the same group?

Explanation: Z = 13 (Al) and Z = 31 (Ga) both belong to Group 13 (Boron family).

Question 11 of 18

The general electronic configuration of d-block elements is:

Explanation: The standard general valence configuration for d-block elements is (n-1)d1-10 ns0-2 (allowing for Pd with ns0).

Question 12 of 18

What is the number of gaseous elements in the modern periodic table at STP?

Explanation: There are 11 gaseous elements: H2, N2, O2, F2, Cl2, and 6 noble gases (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn).

Question 13 of 18

An element has configuration [Rn] 5f14 6d10 7s2 7p2. Its atomic number and group are:

Explanation: Total electrons = 86 (Rn) + 14 + 10 + 2 + 2 = 114. Group number for p-block = 10 + 4 valence electrons = Group 14.

Question 14 of 18

According to Slater's rules, the shielding contribution (σ) of an electron in the same shell (n) as a valence s or p electron is:

Explanation: Under Slater's rules, each other electron in the same (n) group contributes 0.35 to the shielding constant σ (except 1s where it is 0.30).

Question 15 of 18

Which element was named as Eka-Silicon by Mendeleev?

Explanation: Eka-Silicon was the provisional name given by Mendeleev to Germanium (Ge, Z = 32).

Question 16 of 18

The atomic number of the element placed in Period 6 and Group 3 is:

Explanation: Lanthanum (Z = 57) is located in Period 6 and Group 3 of the modern periodic table.

Question 17 of 18

What is the constant 'b' in Moseley's equation √ν = a(Z - b) for &Kalpha; X-rays?

Explanation: For K-alpha X-ray lines, the screening constant b equals 1.0 due to shielding by the remaining 1s electron.

Question 18 of 18

Which block of the periodic table contains metals, non-metals, and metalloids?

Explanation: The p-block is the only block containing all three categories: metals (e.g., Al, Pb), metalloids (e.g., Si, Ge), and non-metals (e.g., C, N, O).

Question 1 of 10
Calculate the effective nuclear charge (Zeff) experienced by a 3p electron in Silicon (Z = 14) using Slater's rules.

Solution: Silicon configuration: (1s2)(2s2 2p6)(3s2 3p2). For a 3p electron: 3 electrons in n=3 contribute 3 × 0.35 = 1.05; 8 electrons in n=2 contribute 8 × 0.85 = 6.80; 2 electrons in n=1 contribute 2 × 1.00 = 2.00. Total σ = 1.05 + 6.80 + 2.00 = 9.85. Zeff = 14 - 9.85 = 4.15.

Question 2 of 10
Which element has the highest atomic number among naturally occurring elements?

Solution: Uranium (Z = 92) is the heaviest naturally occurring element found in significant quantities on Earth.

Question 3 of 10
What is the correct IUPAC symbol for the element with atomic number 120?

Solution: 1 = un (u), 2 = bi (b), 0 = nil (n). Three letter symbol is Ubn (Unbinilium).

Question 4 of 10
Which subshell shielding effect follows the correct order of screening efficiency?

Solution: Due to spatial radial distribution and penetration effect, shielding efficiency decreases in the order s > p > d > f.

Question 5 of 10
If a hypothetical periodic table had 4 quantum states per orbital instead of 2, how many elements would be in the 1st period?

Solution: In 1st period (n=1), there is 1 orbital (1s). If each orbital holds 4 electrons, the first period would contain 4 elements.

Question 6 of 10
What is the screening constant (σ) for a 1s electron in Helium (Z = 2) according to Slater's rules?

Solution: For a 1s electron in Helium, the other 1s electron contributes 0.30 to the screening constant σ.

Question 7 of 10
The element Cerium (Z = 58) belongs to which block of the modern periodic table?

Solution: Cerium (Z = 58) is the first element of the 4f lanthanoid series in the f-block.

Question 8 of 10
The formula for the total number of orbitals in a shell with principal quantum number n is:

Solution: The number of orbitals in a shell with principal quantum number n is n2.

Question 9 of 10
An element has valence configuration 5f7 6d1 7s2. Its atomic number is:

Solution: Configuration corresponds to Curium (Cm): [Rn] 5f7 6d1 7s2. Z = 86 + 7 + 1 + 2 = 96.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following elements is classified as a metalloid?

Solution: Silicon (Si, Z = 14) exhibits intermediate properties between metals and non-metals and is classified as a metalloid.
