Chemical Properties of Alkanes

Alkanes are saturated aliphatic hydrocarbons characterized by strong, non-polar σ-bonds between sp3-hybridized carbon atoms. Due to this robust bonding, they exhibit general chemical inertness toward polar reagents under standard conditions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Free Radical Halogenation Mechanism

  • Halogenation proceeds via a three-step chain mechanism: initiation, propagation, and termination.
  • Order of halogen reactivity is F2 > Cl2 > Br2 >> I2, with fluorination being violently explosive and iodination reversible.
  • Selectivity for hydrogen abstraction follows the stability of intermediate carbon radicals: 3° > 2° > 1°.

Combustion and Thermal Oxidation

  • Alkanes undergo highly exothermic combustion yielding carbon dioxide and water with large negative standard enthalpies of formation.
  • Incomplete combustion in limited oxygen produces carbon monoxide (CO) and carbon soot.
  • Controlled catalytic oxidation of alkanes yields diverse oxygenated derivatives such as alcohols, aldehydes, and carboxylic acids.

Isomerization, Pyrolysis, and Reforming

  • Straight-chain alkanes isomerize into branched-chain alkanes in the presence of anhydrous aluminum chloride and hydrogen chloride.
  • Thermal decomposition or pyrolysis of higher alkanes breaks high molecular weight hydrocarbons into smaller alkenes and alkanes.
  • Catalytic reforming converts straight-chain alkanes into aromatic hydrocarbons via cyclization and dehydrogenation.

Halogenation Propagation Step 1
X• + R-H → H-X + R•
Conditions: Thermal or photochemical homolytic cleavage (hν)
Units: X = Cl, Br; R = Alkyl radical
Halogenation Propagation Step 2
R• + X2 → R-X + X•
Conditions: Exothermic chain propagation step
Units: X = Halogen atom
Pyrolysis (Cracking) Equation
CnH2n+2 → CxH2n+2-y + CzH2z + ...
Conditions: High temperature (∼700-900 K) in the absence of oxygen
Units: x, z = smaller hydrocarbon fragments
Aromatization Reaction
CH3(CH2)4CH3 → C6H6 + 4 H2
Conditions: Cr2O3 / V2O5 / Mo2O3 on Al2O3, 773 K, 10-20 atm
Units: n-Hexane yields Benzene
General Combustion Equation
CnH2n+2 + [(3n+1)/2] O2 → n CO2 + (n+1) H2O
Conditions: Complete combustion in excess oxygen with high temperature
Units: n = integer representing number of carbon atoms
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Iodination of alkanes is expected to proceed spontaneously like chlorination and bromination.

Observation: Iodination is extremely slow and fully reversible. — Hydrogen iodide (HI) formed as a byproduct is a strong reducing agent and reduces the alkyl iodide back to the alkane; concentrated nitric or iodic acid must be added to oxidize HI.

Exception Fluorination of alkanes yields pure alkyl fluorides under controlled lighting.

Observation: Fluorination is violently explosive with rupture of carbon-carbon chains. — The reaction is extremely exothermic due to the high bond dissociation energy of the H-F bond compared to the C-F bond.

Exception Tertiary hydrogen abstraction is always the exclusive pathway in free radical halogenation.

Observation: Chlorination shows low regioselectivity compared to bromination at room temperature. — Chlorine radical attack is energetically early (Hammond's postulate) and exothermic, making it less sensitive to radical stability differences than the endothermic bromine radical attack.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Halogen Reactivity Sequence
F > Cl > Br > I

Application: Remember explosives are fast (Fluorine), practical with light are middle (Chlorine/Bromine), and lazy/reversible is last (Iodine).

Radical Stability Ranking
3° > 2° > 1° > CH3•

Application: Always substitute the hydrogen at the most substituted carbon atom to find the major monohalogenated product.

Aromatization Carbon Count Rule
n ≥ 6

Application: Only alkanes containing six or more carbon atoms can undergo cyclization to form aromatic benzene rings under catalytic reforming conditions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Alkanes are known as paraffin coefficients meaning little affinity because of non-polar C-C and C-H single bonds.
  • The standard enthalpy of combustion increases regularly with each incremental -CH2- group added to the homologous series.
  • Nitration of alkanes occurs only in the vapor phase at 673-773 K via free radical mechanisms yielding nitroalkane mixtures.
  • Sulfonation of higher normal alkanes (n ≥ C6H14) with fuming sulfuric acid yields alkyl sulfonic acids.
  • Wurtz reaction involves coupling of two alkyl halides with sodium metal in dry ether to form symmetrical higher alkanes.
  • Kolbe's electrolytic decarboxylation of sodium or potassium salts of carboxylic acids yields alkanes at the anode.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming alkanes react readily with aqueous acids, bases, or strong oxidizing agents at room temperature.
  • Confusing the role of HI in iodination as a catalyst rather than a reducing agent that reverses the reaction.
  • Forgetting that methane cannot undergo Wurtz reaction to give a higher alkane directly from a single methyl halide source.
  • Neglecting stereochemistry when radical abstraction generates a new chiral center, resulting in racemic mixtures.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Properties of Alkanes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Chemical Inertness

Alkanes lack functional groups and possess strong, non-polar covalent bonds, rendering them stable toward ionic reagents, acids, and bases under normal ambient conditions.

Free Radical Substitution

Substitution of hydrogen by halogens proceeds via homolytic bond cleavage driven by heat or ultraviolet light through propagation chain mechanisms.

Thermal Oxidation

Combustion yields carbon dioxide and water exothermically, serving as the primary source of energy generation and industrial fuel applications.

Pyrolysis and Reforming

Thermal cracking breaks heavy petroleum fractions into smaller alkenes, while catalytic reforming converts straight chains to aromatics for high-octane fuels.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Homolytic Cleavage (Homolysis)
Equal splitting of a covalent bond producing two free radicals.
Pyrolysis (Thermal Cracking)
Thermal breakdown of hydrocarbons at high temperatures in the absence of air.
Aromatization (Catalytic Dehydrocyclization)
Conversion of acyclic alkanes into aromatic compounds.
Combustion (Oxidation)
Rapid reaction of alkanes with oxygen producing heat, CO2, and H2O.
Octane Number (Anti-knock Rating)
Measure of the resistance of a motor fuel to knock, referenced against isooctane and heptane.
Free Radical (R•)
Neutral reactive intermediate possessing an unpaired electron.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Halogen reactivity order: F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2
  • Free radical stability order: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3•
  • Minimum carbons required for aromatization: 6 carbons (n-hexane)
  • Combustion is always exothermic with negative ΔH values
  • Iodination requires an oxidizing agent (HNO3 or HIO3) to consume byproduct HI

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkanes undergo substitution rather than addition due to saturation.
  • Halogenation follows free radical chain reaction pathways.
  • Higher temperatures promote cracking and skeletal isomerization.
  • Branched alkanes have higher octane numbers than straight-chain isomers.

⚖️ Key Differences

Substitution Reaction VS Addition Reaction

Alkanes undergo substitution of H atoms by reagents, whereas unsaturated hydrocarbons (alkenes/alkynes) undergo addition across multiple bonds.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following alkanes cannot be prepared in good yield by the Wurtz reaction?

Explanation: Propane requires a mixture of chloromethane and chloroethane in Wurtz reaction, which yields a statistical mixture of propane, ethane, and butane, making separation difficult.

Question 2 of 18

What is the order of reactivity of halogens towards alkanes?

Explanation: Fluorine reacts explosively due to low F-F bond dissociation energy, followed by chlorine, bromine, and finally iodine which is sluggish.

Question 3 of 18

Which intermediate is formed during the halogenation of alkanes?

Explanation: Halogenation of alkanes proceeds via homolytic fission of bonds, generating alkyl free radicals as reactive intermediates.

Question 4 of 18

In the free radical chlorination of propane, the major product is:

Explanation: Abstraction of a secondary hydrogen yields a more stable 2° free radical, leading predominantly to 2-chloropropane.

Question 5 of 18

Catalytic oxidation of methane with MoO3 at high temperature and pressure yields:

Explanation: Controlled catalytic oxidation of methane using copper or molybdenum oxide catalysts yields methanal (formaldehyde).

Question 6 of 18

Which of the following compounds gives a single monochloro derivative upon photochlorination?

Explanation: 2,2-Dimethylpropane (neopentane) has 12 equivalent primary hydrogen atoms, yielding only one monochloro derivative.

Question 7 of 18

Pyrolysis of higher alkanes to yield lower alkenes and alkanes is known as:

Explanation: Cracking or pyrolysis refers to thermal decomposition of large hydrocarbon molecules into smaller fragments at high temperatures.

Question 8 of 18

What catalyst is used for the aromatization of n-hexane to benzene?

Explanation: Aromatization (dehydrocyclization) of n-hexane to benzene is achieved using Cr2O3, V2O5, or Mo2O3 supported on alumina at 773 K.

Question 9 of 18

Vapor phase nitration of propane yields a mixture of how many nitroalkane isomers?

Explanation: Vapor phase nitration involves C-C bond cleavage as well as substitution, yielding 1-nitropropane, 2-nitropropane, nitroethane, and nitromethane.

Question 10 of 18

Why is direct iodination of alkanes not feasible under normal conditions?

Explanation: HI produced during iodination is a strong reducing agent that converts the alkyl iodide back to the starting alkane.

Question 11 of 18

Which alkane has the highest boiling point among the following isomers of hexane?

Explanation: Straight-chain n-hexane has a larger surface area and stronger Van der Waals forces, resulting in a higher boiling point than branched isomers.

Question 12 of 18

Kolbe's electrolytic decarboxylation of sodium propanoate yields:

Explanation: Kolbe electrolysis of sodium propanoate produces ethyl radicals at the anode, which combine to form butane.

Question 13 of 18

Which of the following free radicals is the most stable?

Explanation: Tertiary free radicals are stabilized by hyperconjugation and inductive effect from three adjacent alkyl groups, making them the most stable.

Question 14 of 18

What happens when methane reacts with excess chlorine in the presence of sunlight?

Explanation: Excess chlorine leads to successive substitution of all hydrogen atoms, culminating in carbon tetrachloride (CCl4).

Question 15 of 18

Sulfonation of alkanes requires which of the following reagents?

Explanation: Lower alkanes are unreactive towards sulfonation, but higher alkanes (n ≥ 6) react with fuming sulfuric acid (oleum) to form sulfonic acids.

Question 16 of 18

In the mechanism of chlorination of methane, the termination step involves:

Explanation: Termination steps involve the combination of two free radicals to form a neutral molecule, halting the chain reaction.

Question 17 of 18

Isomerization of alkanes is catalyzed by:

Explanation: Straight-chain alkanes convert into branched-chain isomers in the presence of Lewis acids like anhydrous aluminium chloride and hydrogen chloride.

Question 18 of 18

Which reagent can be used to oxidize HI back to I2 during alkane iodination?

Explanation: Strong oxidizing agents like iodic acid (HIO3) or concentrated nitric acid (HNO3) oxidize byproduct HI to iodine, preventing the reverse reaction.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary product formed when propane is heated with oxygen in the presence of manganese acetate catalyst?

Solution: Controlled catalytic oxidation of alkanes with specific metal acetate catalysts cleaves carbon chains, yielding lower carboxylic acids such as acetic acid.

Question 2 of 10
In free radical bromination of isobutane, what is the major organic product?

Solution: Bromination is highly selective for tertiary hydrogen abstraction, yielding 2-bromo-2-methylpropane as the exclusive major product.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following compounds has the lowest heat of combustion per mole?

Solution: Highly branched isomers are more thermodynamically stable and possess lower potential energy, resulting in lower heats of combustion.

Question 4 of 10
During the Kolbe electrolysis of sodium acetate, gases evolved at the anode are:

Solution: Kolbe electrolysis generates ethane and carbon dioxide at the anode, while hydrogen gas is liberated at the cathode.

Question 5 of 10
Which halogen exhibits explosive reaction kinetics with methane?

Solution: Fluorination is extremely exothermic and explosive, tearing apart the carbon skeleton of the alkane.

Question 6 of 10
What is the product of thermal cracking of decane at 773 K?

Solution: Pyrolysis or cracking of higher alkanes results in random C-C bond cleavage yielding a mixture of smaller alkanes and alkenes.

Question 7 of 10
Which catalyst is used for the isomerization of straight-chain alkanes to branched-chain alkanes?

Solution: Anhydrous aluminum chloride in the presence of dry hydrogen chloride is the standard Lewis acid catalyst for alkane isomerization.

Question 8 of 10
How many monochloro derivatives (including stereoisomers) are possible for 2-methylbutane upon radical chlorination?

Solution: 2-Methylbutane has 4 different carbon positions for substitution, and two of these positions yield chiral centers giving a pair of enantiomers each, totaling 6 isomeric monochloro products.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following reactions is used to step-up the carbon chain in alkanes?

Solution: The Wurtz reaction couples two alkyl halide molecules to form a symmetrical alkane with a doubled carbon chain.

Question 10 of 10
What is the general formula for cycloalkanes which are functional isomers of alkenes?

Solution: Cycloalkanes share the general formula CnH2n, making them functional isomers of straight-chain or branched alkenes.
