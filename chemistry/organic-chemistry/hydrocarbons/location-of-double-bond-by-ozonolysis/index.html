Organic Chemistry

Location of Double Bond by Ozonolysis

Ozonolysis involves the addition of ozone to alkenes to form unstable molozonides, which rearrange to ozonides before cleavage into carbonyl compounds. The structural identity of the resulting aldehydes and ketones establishes the exact position of the original carbon-carbon double bond.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Reaction Mechanism and Intermediates

  • Ozone undergoes a 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition across the carbon-carbon double bond to generate an initial 1,2,3-trioxolane, known as the molozonide.
  • The molozonide is thermally unstable and rapidly cleaves into a carbonyl compound and a carbonyl oxide dipole via a cycloreversion process.
  • Recombination of the carbonyl oxide and the carbonyl fragment via a 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition forms the stable 1,2,4-trioxolane (ozonide) ring.
  • The skeletal structure of the resulting ozonide dictates the precise cleavage products obtained upon subsequent workup.

Reductive versus Oxidative Workup Conditions

  • Reductive workup employs reagents such as zinc dust in water, dimethyl sulfide (DMS), or triphenylphosphine to yield aldehydes and ketones without further oxidation.
  • Zinc dust acts as a scavenger for hydrogen peroxide generated during hydrolysis, preventing the oxidation of aldehyde products into carboxylic acids.
  • Oxidative workup utilizes hydrogen peroxide or peracids during decomposition, converting any resulting aldehyde functions directly into carboxylic acids.
  • Terminal double bonds treated with oxidative ozonolysis yield carbon dioxide and water alongside the respective carbonyl fragment.

Structural Determination and Retro-Analysis

  • To locate the double bond in an unknown alkene, replace the oxygen atoms of all isolated carbonyl fragments with a carbon-carbon double bond (the 'de-oxygenation' trick).
  • Symmetrical alkenes yield a single type of carbonyl compound, whereas unsymmetrical alkenes yield a distinct mixture of two different aldehydes or ketones.
  • Cyclic alkenes undergo ozonolysis followed by ring-opening to yield dicarbonyl compounds (dialdehydes, diketones, or keto-aldehydes).
  • The position of substituents on the resulting carbonyl fragments directly corresponds to the substitution pattern and position of the original double bond.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Criegee Rearrangement to Ozonide
Molozonide → [R2C+−O−O−] + R2C=O → 1,2,4-Trioxolane (Ozonide)
Conditions: Intramolecular dipolar cycloaddition rearrangement
Units: Dimensionless structural conversion
Reductive Cleavage of Ozonide
Ozonide + Zn / H2O (or (CH3)2S) → 2 R2C=O + ZnO + H2O
Conditions: Presence of reducing metal (Zn) or dimethyl sulfide to prevent over-oxidation
Units: Stoichiometric reaction coefficients
Oxidative Cleavage of Ozonide
Ozonide + H2O2 (or RCOOOH) → Carboxylic acids / Ketones + H2O
Conditions: Presence of oxidizing agents converting aldehydes directly to carboxylic acids
Units: Stoichiometric reaction coefficients
Structural Reconstruction Equation
R1R2C=O + O=CR3R4 → R1R2C=CR3R4 + H2O
Conditions: Removal of oxygen atoms between the two carbonyl fragments
Units: Structural retro-synthesis mapping
Ozone Addition (Molozonide Formation)
R2C=CR2 + O3 → R2C−O−O−O−CR2 (Molozonide)
Conditions: Inert solvent such as CH2Cl2 at low temperatures (-78°C)
Units: Stoichiometric molar ratio 1:1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkenes yield carbonyl compounds upon reductive ozonolysis.

Observation: Tetrasubstituted alkenes with bulky groups or specific electron-deficient systems can form stable peroxides that resist standard cleavage. — Steric hindrance prevents the initial approach of ozone or stabilizes the intermediate peroxide against decomposition.

Exception Terminal alkenes yield formaldehyde (HCHO) upon reductive workup.

Observation: Formaldehyde can undergo secondary hydration and polymerization reactions if not immediately trapped or complexed. — High reactivity and solubility of formaldehyde in aqueous reaction media.

Exception Ozonolysis cleaves all carbon-carbon double bonds.

Observation: Sterically congested double bonds in polycyclic systems show significantly reduced reaction rates or require elevated pressures. — Inaccessible π-electron cloud due to steric shielding by adjacent bulky ring frameworks.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
The 'Erase and Connect' Shortcut
R1R2C=O + O=CR3R4 → Erase 'O's and join carbons with '='

Application: Given the structures of the cleavage products, simply face the carbonyl oxygens toward each other, remove both 'O' atoms, and fuse the carbon atoms with a double bond to find the parent alkene.

Counting Carbons Check
∑ Carbons(Alkene) = ∑ Carbons(Products)

Application: Always verify that the total number of carbon atoms in the starting alkene matches the exact sum of carbon atoms in all isolated carbonyl cleavage products.

Ring Opening Rule for Cyclic Alkenes
Cyclic Alkene + O3 → Open-chain Dicarbonyl Compound

Application: For a monocyclic alkene with n carbons, ozonolysis breaks the ring to form a straight-chain dicarbonyl compound containing the exact same number of carbon atoms (n).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Ozone is an allotrope of oxygen with a bent structure and acts as a potent electrophilic agent toward alkenes.
  • The primary intermediate in ozonolysis is the unstable 1,2,3-trioxolane (molozonide).
  • Reductive cleavage using zinc and water prevents the further oxidation of aldehyde functionalities.
  • Dimethyl sulfide (DMS) is widely preferred as a reducing agent because its byproduct (dimethyl sulfoxide) is easily separated.
  • Ozonolysis of 2-butene yields two equivalents of acetaldehyde.
  • Cyclohexene undergoes ozonolysis to form hexane-1,6-dial (adipic aldehyde).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting that terminal double bonds produce formaldehyde, which can be further oxidized if water is not managed.
  • Confusing the products of reductive workup (aldehydes/ketones) with those of oxidative workup (acids/ketones).
  • Failing to account for all carbon atoms when reconstructing the parent alkene from cleavage fragments.
  • Assuming aromatic rings undergo standard ozonolysis under mild conditions; benzene is remarkably resistant compared to isolated alkenes.

⚡ Quick Revision: Location of Double Bond by Ozonolysis

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrophilic Addition of Ozone

Ozone undergoes 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition across the carbon-carbon double bond to yield an unstable molozonide intermediate.

Criegee Mechanism

The molozonide fragments into a carbonyl oxide and a carbonyl compound, which recombine to form a stable 1,2,4-trioxolane ozonide.

Workup Selectivity

Reductive workup (Zn/H2O or DMS) yields aldehydes and ketones, while oxidative workup (H2O2) yields carboxylic acids.

Structure Determination

The number, identity, and substitution pattern of the resulting carbonyl compounds unambiguously establish the position of the original double bond.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Ozonolysis (Alkene Cleavage)
Cleavage of an alkene by ozone followed by reductive or oxidative decomposition.
Molozonide (1,2,3-Trioxolane)
Primary unstable five-membered ring intermediate formed by ozone addition.
Ozonide (1,2,4-Trioxolane)
Stable cyclic rearrangement product formed via the Criegee mechanism.
Criegee Mechanism (Ozonide Formation Pathway)
The accepted stepwise pathway involving cycloaddition, fragmentation, and recombination.
Reductive Workup (Zn/H2O or DMS)
Decomposition method preserving aldehyde and ketone functional groups.
Oxidative Workup (H2O2)
Decomposition method converting aldehydes into carboxylic acids.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ozone adds specifically across carbon-carbon double bonds.
  • Molozonide → Ozonide is a 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition rearrangement.
  • Zinc dust scavenges H2O2 during reductive workup.
  • Terminal double bonds yield formaldehyde upon reductive cleavage.
  • Cyclic alkenes yield open-chain dicarbonyl compounds.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Ozonolysis is the premier degradative method for locating double bonds.
  • Products depend entirely on the substitution of the alkene and the choice of workup.
  • Retro-analysis allows direct deduction of alkene structure from carbonyl fragments.
  • Symmetrical cleavage indicates a symmetrical starting alkene.

⚖️ Key Differences

Reductive Ozonolysis VS Oxidative Ozonolysis

Reductive workup stops at aldehydes and ketones, whereas oxidative workup further oxidizes aldehydes to carboxylic acids.

Question 1 of 20

What is the primary intermediate formed immediately after ozone adds to an alkene?

Explanation: The initial 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition of ozone across the double bond yields the unstable 1,2,3-trioxolane, known as the molozonide.

Question 2 of 20

Which reagent is commonly used for reductive workup in ozonolysis to prevent aldehyde oxidation?

Explanation: Zinc dust in water (or dimethyl sulfide) reduces the ozonide and destroys hydrogen peroxide, preventing the oxidation of aldehydes to acids.

Question 3 of 20

Ozonolysis of 2-methylpropene followed by reductive workup yields which of the following products?

Explanation: 2-Methylpropene (CH2=C(CH3)2) cleavage yields HCHO (formaldehyde) and CH3COCH3 (acetone).

Question 4 of 20

What is the stable cyclic intermediate formed during ozonolysis via the Criegee rearrangement?

Explanation: The molozonide rearranges into 1,2,4-trioxolane, which is the stable ozonide isolated prior to workup.

Question 5 of 20

Which compound is formed when cyclohexene undergoes reductive ozonolysis?

Explanation: Cleavage of the ring double bond in cyclohexene yields the dialdehyde hexane-1,6-dial under reductive conditions.

Question 6 of 20

What is the function of dimethyl sulfide (DMS) in ozonolysis?

Explanation: Dimethyl sulfide acts as a mild reducing agent that reacts with the ozonide to produce carbonyl compounds and dimethyl sulfoxide.

Question 7 of 20

If an alkene yields only acetone upon reductive ozonolysis, what is the structure of the alkene?

Explanation: 2,3-Dimethyl-2-butene ((CH3)2C=C(CH3)2) splits into two identical molecules of acetone (CH3COCH3).

Question 8 of 20

What happens when an alkene undergoes oxidative ozonolysis using H2O2?

Explanation: Oxidative workup with hydrogen peroxide oxidizes any aldehyde groups generated during ozonolysis into carboxylic acids.

Question 9 of 20

Terminal alkenes upon ozonolysis and reductive workup always produce which compound alongside another carbonyl?

Explanation: The =CH2 group of a terminal alkene is cleaved to form formaldehyde (HCHO) during reductive workup.

Question 10 of 20

Which of the following describes the nature of the ozone molecule in the initial step of ozonolysis?

Explanation: Ozone acts as an electrophilic 1,3-dipole that attacks electron-rich carbon-carbon double bonds.

Question 11 of 20

How many moles of carbonyl products are formed from one mole of a symmetrical acyclic monoalkene?

Explanation: Cleavage of an acyclic monoalkene invariably produces two fragments (moles) of carbonyl compounds per mole of alkene.

Question 12 of 20

Which solvent is standardly employed for carrying out low-temperature ozonolysis?

Explanation: Dichloromethane at low temperatures (-78°C) provides an inert, non-reactive medium for controlled ozonide formation.

Question 13 of 20

What is the IUPAC name of the ozonide ring system?

Explanation: The stable ozonide ring contains three oxygen atoms and two carbon atoms, designated systematically as 1,2,4-trioxolane.

Question 14 of 20

If an unknown alkene gives equimolar amounts of formaldehyde and acetone upon reductive ozonolysis, the alkene is:

Explanation: 2-Methylpropene has a =CH2 group (giving formaldehyde) and a =(CH3)2 group (giving acetone).

Question 15 of 20

Why is ozonolysis considered a superior method for locating double bonds compared to simple halogenation?

Explanation: Ozonolysis cleaves the exact bonds of unsaturation, yielding predictable carbonyl fragments whose structures reveal the original double bond position.

Question 16 of 20

What is the product of oxidative ozonolysis of a terminal alkene at the terminal carbon?

Explanation: Oxidative workup of a terminal alkene converts the terminal =CH2 carbon fully into carbon dioxide and water.

Question 17 of 20

Which of the following alkanes cannot be analyzed by ozonolysis?

Explanation: Saturated alkanes lack carbon-carbon multiple bonds and therefore do not react with ozone under standard ozonolysis conditions.

Question 18 of 20

In the Criegee mechanism, what species acts as the 1,3-dipole?

Explanation: The fragmentation of the molozonide yields a carbonyl oxide dipole and a carbonyl compound.

Question 19 of 20

What is the stereochemical outcome at existing chiral centers outside the double bond during ozonolysis?

Explanation: Chiral centers remote from the reaction site remain unaffected, maintaining their configuration (retention).

Question 20 of 20

Ozonolysis of an acyclic alkene yields pentan-3-one and ethanal. What is the alkene?

Explanation: Combining pentan-3-one (CH3CH2C(=O)CH2CH3) and ethanal (CH3CH=O) by removing oxygen atoms yields 3-ethylpent-2-ene.
