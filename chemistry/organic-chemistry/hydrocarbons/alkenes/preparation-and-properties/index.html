Preparation and Properties of Alkenes

Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-carbon double bond with the general molecular formula CnH2n. Their chemical behavior is dominated by electrophilic addition reactions across the sp2 hybridized π-bond.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthetic Routes for Alkene Preparation

  • Dehydrohalogenation of Alkyl Halides: Treatment with alcoholic KOH undergoes E2 elimination following Zaitsev's rule to yield the most substituted alkene.
  • Dehydration of Alcohols: Heating alcohols with concentrated H2SO4 or H3PO4 produces alkenes via an E1 mechanism involving carbocation intermediates.
  • Partial Hydrogenation of Alkynes: Reduction using Lindlar's catalyst (Pd/BaSO4 poisoned with quinoline) yields cis-alkenes (syn-addition).
  • Birch Reduction: Treatment of alkynes with Na in liquid NH3 yields trans-alkenes via single-electron transfer steps.

Stereochemical Outcomes of Addition Reactions

  • Catalytic Hydrogenation: Proceeds via syn-addition where hydrogen atoms deliver from the same catalyst face.
  • Halogenation: Addition of Cl2 or Br2 to alkenes involves a cyclic halonium ion intermediate, resulting exclusively in anti-addition and trans-vicinal dihalides.
  • Hydroboration-Oxidation: Involves simultaneous B and H addition from the same face (syn-addition) leading to anti-Markovnikov alcohol formation.
  • Oxymercuration-Demercuration: Proceeds via a cyclic mercurinium ion with Markovnikov orientation and no skeletal rearrangement.

Oxidative Cleavage and Polymerization

  • Ozonolysis: Reaction with O3 followed by reductive workup (Zn/H2O) cleaves the C=C bond to form aldehydes or ketones.
  • KMnO4 Oxidation: Cold alkaline KMnO4 (Baeyer's reagent) converts alkenes into vicinal cis-diols with discharge of purple color.
  • Epoxidation: Peroxyacids (e.g., mCPBA) transfer an oxygen atom to the alkene π-system yielding oxiranes (epoxides).
  • Addition Polymerization: Alkenes polymerize under radical or ionic initiation to form high-molecular-weight polymers like polyethene and polypropene.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Dehydration of alcohols yields the most substituted alkene as the major product.

Observation: Bulky bases or specific primary alcohol substrates can give Hofmann products or undergo Wagner-Meerwein rearrangements. — Steric hindrance or exceptionally stable alternative carbocation intermediates redirect the kinetic versus thermodynamic product ratio.

Exception Electrophilic addition follows Markovnikov's rule strictly.

Observation: Addition of HBr to terminal alkenes in the presence of peroxides yields anti-Markovnikov products. — Presence of organic peroxides shifts the reaction mechanism from ionic electrophilic addition to a free-radical chain mechanism.

Exception Halogenation of alkenes yields chiral racemic mixtures via anti-addition.

Observation: Addition to symmetrical cis- or trans-alkenes yields specific meso or d,l-stereoisomers depending on alkene geometry. — Stereospecific nature of the cyclic halonium intermediate coupled with molecular symmetry determines the optical activity of the product.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Baeyer's reagent is cold, dilute, alkaline KMnO4 which acts as a test for unsaturation by turning purple to a brown MnO2 precipitate.
  • Zaitsev's rule states that during elimination, the major alkene contains the maximum number of alkyl substituents on the double bond carbons.
  • The stability of alkenes increases with alkyl substitution due to hyperconjugation and inductive effect.
  • Ozonolysis followed by Zn/H2O prevents further oxidation of aldehydes to carboxylic acids.
  • Hydroboration-oxidation gives syn-addition with anti-Markovnikov regioselectivity.
  • Electrophilic addition to alkenes is initiated by the attack of the electron-rich π-cloud on an electrophile.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the regioselectivity of acid-catalyzed hydration (Markovnikov) with hydroboration-oxidation (anti-Markovnikov).
  • Forgetting that carbocation intermediates in E1 or electrophilic addition can undergo 1,2-shifts for rearrangement.
  • Assuming peroxide effect works with HCl and HI as well as it does with HBr (it is thermodynamically unfavorable for HCl and HI).
  • Failing to account for stereochemistry (syn vs anti addition) when determining meso or racemic product pairs.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation and Properties of Alkenes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Preparation Methods

Alkenes are synthesized via elimination reactions (dehydration of alcohols, dehydrohalogenation of alkyl halides) and reduction of alkynes using stereospecific catalysts like Lindlar's reagent or dissolving metals.

Electrophilic Addition Mechanism

The high electron density of the C=C π-bond makes alkenes susceptible to electrophiles, generating carbocation or cycliconium ion intermediates followed by nucleophilic attack.

Regioselectivity and Stereoselectivity

Addition reactions follow Markovnikov rule unless peroxides are present, and exhibit specific stereochemical pathways such as syn-addition via catalytic hydrogenation or anti-addition via halogenation.

Oxidative Degradation

Oxidative cleavage via ozonolysis or KMnO4 breaks the carbon-carbon double bond, yielding carbonyl compounds or carboxylic acids depending on reaction conditions.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Lindlar Catalyst (Pd/BaSO_4 + quinoline)
Poisoned palladium catalyst used for partial hydrogenation of alkynes to cis-alkenes.
Baeyer's Reagent (Cold alkaline KMnO4)
Oxidizing agent used to test for unsaturation by forming vicinal diols.
Markovnikov's Rule (Regioselectivity rule)
Addition of unsymmetrical reagents places the negative part on the more substituted carbon.
Zaitsev's Rule (Elimination rule)
Elimination yields the most substituted, most stable alkene as the major product.
Hyperconjugation (No-bond resonance)
Stabilization of alkenes through delocalization of σ-electrons of C-H bonds into the empty π* orbital.
Epoxide (Oxirane)
Three-membered cyclic ether formed by peroxyacid oxidation of alkenes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Cis-alkenes have higher dipole moments than trans-alkenes.
  • Hydroboration-oxidation results in syn-addition and anti-Markovnikov alcohol.
  • Ozone cleavage (O3 followed by Zn/H2O) splits C=C into two carbonyl fragments.
  • Bromine water test discharges its reddish-brown color in the presence of alkenes.
  • Trans-alkenes are thermodynamically more stable than cis-alkenes due to lower steric hindrance.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons prepared via elimination or alkyne reduction.
  • Electrophilic addition is the characteristic chemical property governed by carbocation stability.
  • Stereochemistry of addition reactions is strictly controlled (syn vs anti).
  • Oxidative cleavage methods help determine the position of double bonds in unknown structures.

⚖️ Key Differences

Hydroboration-Oxidation VS Oxymercuration-Demercuration

Hydroboration gives anti-Markovnikov syn-hydration without rearrangement; oxymercuration gives Markovnikov anti-hydration without rearrangement.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following catalysts is used to convert alkynes to cis-alkenes?

Explanation: Lindlar's catalyst (palladium on calcium carbonate or barium sulfate poisoned with quinoline) reduces alkynes stereospecifically to cis-alkenes via syn-addition.

Question 2 of 18

Dehydration of 2-butanol with concentrated sulfuric acid primarily yields:

Explanation: Dehydration follows Zaitsev's rule via an E1 mechanism, yielding 2-butene as the major thermodynamic product.

Question 3 of 18

What is the product formed when propene reacts with HBr in the presence of benzoyl peroxide?

Explanation: In the presence of peroxides, addition of HBr to unsymmetrical alkenes proceeds via a free-radical mechanism yielding the anti-Markovnikov product, 1-bromopropane.

Question 4 of 18

Which reagent causes syn-dihydroxylation of alkenes?

Explanation: Cold dilute alkaline potassium permanganate (Baeyer's reagent) converts alkenes into cis-1,2-diols via syn-addition.

Question 5 of 18

The addition of bromine to trans-2-butene yields which stereoisomer?

Explanation: Halogenation of trans-2-butene via an anti-addition cyclic bromonium ion intermediate produces the meso-stereoisomer due to internal plane of symmetry.

Question 6 of 18

Which of the following alkenes is most stable?

Explanation: 2,3-dimethyl-2-butene has six α-hydrogen atoms attached to sp2 carbons, providing maximum hyperconjugative stabilization.

Question 7 of 18

Ozonolysis of 2-methyl-2-butene followed by Zn/H2O gives:

Explanation: Cleaving (CH3)2C=CHCH3 at the double bond yields acetone ((CH3)2C=O) and acetaldehyde (CH3CHO).

Question 8 of 18

Birch reduction of alkynes yields:

Explanation: Reduction of alkynes with sodium in liquid ammonia (Birch reduction) proceeds via electron transfer to yield trans-alkenes stereoselectively.

Question 9 of 18

What intermediate is formed during the electrophilic addition of halogen to an alkene?

Explanation: Electrophilic addition of halogens like Br2 proceeds through a three-membered cyclic halonium ion intermediate, accounting for anti-addition stereochemistry.

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following compounds does not show geometrical isomerism?

Explanation: 1-butene has two identical hydrogen atoms attached to terminal sp2 carbon (C1), hence it cannot exhibit geometrical isomerism.

Question 11 of 18

Hydration of propene using dilute H2SO4 gives:

Explanation: Acid-catalyzed hydration proceeds via a secondary carbocation intermediate following Markovnikov's rule to yield 2-propanol.

Question 12 of 18

Epoxidation of alkenes is typically carried out using:

Explanation: Peroxyacids such as meta-chloroperoxybenzoic acid (mCPBA) transfer an oxygen atom directly to the alkene to form epoxides.

Question 13 of 18

Which reaction sequence converts an alkene to an alkane?

Explanation: Catalytic hydrogenation adds hydrogen across the double bond over Pt, Pd, or Ni to form saturated alkanes.

Question 14 of 18

In E2 elimination of alkyl halides to form alkenes, the orientation is predominantly:

Explanation: Standard base-induced E2 elimination of simple alkyl halides yields the more substituted, more stable alkene following Zaitsev's rule.

Question 15 of 18

What is the hybridization of carbon atoms involved in the double bond of an alkene?

Explanation: Each carbon atom forming the double bond in an alkene is sp2 hybridized with one unhybridized p-orbital forming the π-bond.

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following has the highest boiling point?

Explanation: cis-2-butene possesses a net dipole moment resulting in stronger intermolecular dipole-dipole attractions and a slightly higher boiling point than trans-2-butene.

Question 17 of 18

When 2-methyl-2-butanol is heated with concentrated H2SO4, the major product is:

Explanation: Dehydration proceeds via a tertiary carbocation and yields the tetrasubstituted alkene 2-methyl-2-butene as the major product.

Question 18 of 18

The addition of water to acetylene (alkyne) in the presence of HgSO4 and H2SO4 yields acetaldehyde. What similar hydration of propene yields?

Explanation: Hydration of propene yields 2-propanol (isopropyl alcohol) via Markovnikov addition.

Question 1 of 10
What is the major product obtained when 3-methyl-2-butanol undergoes acid-catalyzed dehydration?

Solution: Protonation and loss of water from 3-methyl-2-butanol yields a secondary carbocation which undergoes a 1,2-methyl shift to form a more stable tertiary carbocation, ultimately yielding 2-methyl-2-butene as the major product.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following reagents converts an alkyne directly into a trans-alkene?

Solution: Dissolving metal reduction using sodium in liquid ammonia reduces internal alkynes stereoselectively to trans-alkenes via radical-anion intermediates.

Question 3 of 10
Which alkene on reductive ozonolysis yields only glyoxal (OHC-CHO)?

Solution: Ozonolysis of cyclobutene cleaves the single ring double bond to yield succinaldehyde derivative glyoxal-type dialdehyde (OHC-CH2-CH2-CHO).

Question 4 of 10
What stereochemical outcome is expected from the hydroxylation of cis-2-butene with osmium tetroxide (OsO4)?

Solution: Syn-dihydroxylation of cis-2-butene via a cyclic osmate ester intermediate yields meso-2,3-butanediol due to molecular symmetry.

Question 5 of 10
Which alkyl halide undergoes E2 elimination most rapidly with alcoholic KOH?

Solution: Tertiary alkyl halides bearing good leaving groups like iodide react fastest in E2 elimination due to formation of a highly stable transition state with increased substitution.

Question 6 of 10
In electrophilic addition of HX to an unsymmetrical alkene, the initial slow step involves:

Solution: The rate-determining step of electrophilic addition is the attack of the alkene π-electrons on the proton (electrophile) to generate a carbocation intermediate.

Question 7 of 10
Which compound does not decolorize bromine water?

Solution: Ethane is a saturated alkane lacking π-bonds, hence it does not undergo electrophilic addition to decolorize bromine water.

Question 8 of 10
Which intermediate is isolated or implicated during oxymercuration-demercuration?

Solution: Oxymercuration proceeds through a three-membered cyclic mercurinium ion, preventing carbocation rearrangements.

Question 9 of 10
What is the main reason trans-alkenes have higher melting points than cis-alkenes?

Solution: Trans-alkenes have a linear, symmetrical geometry that fits into crystal lattices more efficiently than bent cis-alkenes, resulting in higher melting points.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following reactions is stereospecific and gives syn-addition?

Solution: Catalytic hydrogenation delivers both hydrogen atoms from the metal surface to the same face of the alkene, representing a stereospecific syn-addition.
