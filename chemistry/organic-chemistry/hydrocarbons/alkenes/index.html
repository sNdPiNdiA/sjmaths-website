Organic Chemistry

Alkenes

Alkenes are unsaturated acyclic hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-carbon double bond with the general formula CnH2n. They undergo characteristic electrophilic addition reactions due to the high electron density of the π-bond.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structure, Bonding and Isomerism in Alkenes

  • The carbon-carbon double bond consists of one σ-bond formed by sp2-sp2 overlap and one π-bond formed by lateral overlap of unhybridized 2p orbitals.
  • The bond angle is approximately 120° with trigonal planar geometry around each sp2 hybridized carbon atom.
  • Alkenes exhibit geometrical isomerism (cis-trans or E-Z notation) due to restricted rotation about the double bond.
  • E-Z nomenclature uses Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priority rules where high priority groups on the same side give the Z-isomer and on opposite sides give the E-isomer.

Preparation Methods of Alkenes

  • Dehydration of alcohols uses concentrated H2SO4 or H3PO4 via an E1 mechanism involving a carbocation intermediate.
  • Dehydrohalogenation of alkyl halides proceeds via an E2 mechanism using strong bases like alcoholic KOH, yielding Zaitsev's rule products predominantly.
  • Partial hydrogenation of alkynes using Lindlar's catalyst (palladized calcium carbonate deactivated with quinoline) yields cis-alkenes exclusively.
  • Birch reduction of alkynes using sodium in liquid ammonia yields trans-alkenes via a dissolving metal reduction pathway.

Chemical Reactivity and Addition Reactions

  • Electrophilic addition is the characteristic reaction of alkenes, initiated by attack of the electron-rich π-cloud on an electrophile to form a cyclic halonium or open carbocation intermediate.
  • Markovnikov's rule dictates that in the ionic addition of unsymmetrical reagents to unsymmetrical alkenes, the electropositive part attaches to the carbon with more hydrogen atoms.
  • Anti-Markovnikov addition (Kharasch effect / peroxide effect) is observed only with HBr in the presence of peroxides, proceeding via a free-radical chain mechanism.
  • Hydroboration-oxidation yields anti-Markovnikov alcohol products with syn-stereochemistry via a concerted concerted concerted concerted hydroboration step.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Addition of HX to alkenes follows Markovnikov's rule yielding more substituted halides.

Observation: Addition of HBr in the presence of organic peroxides yields anti-Markovnikov products. — Peroxides generate bromine radicals via homolytic cleavage, changing the mechanism from ionic electrophilic addition to a free-radical chain propagation pathway.

Exception Dehydration of alcohols follows Zaitsev's rule, favoring the most substituted and stable alkene.

Observation: Bulky bases with poor leaving groups or extreme steric hindrance in specific substrates can yield Hofmeister (less substituted) alkenes predominantly. — Steric hindrance prevents the base from abstracting the internal more sterically blocked proton, favoring abstraction of the more accessible terminal proton.

Exception Electrophilic addition of halogens (Cl2, Br2) to alkenes gives anti-addition products exclusively.

Observation: In the presence of solvent nucleophiles or neighboring group participation, some syn-addition components can be detected. — Formation of open carbocations instead of cyclic halonium ions allows bond rotation prior to nucleophilic attack.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Alkenes possess a carbon-carbon double bond with a bond length of 1.34 Å and a bond energy of approximately 610 kJ mol-1.
  • The stability of alkenes increases with alkyl substitution following hyperconjugation rules: Tetrasubstituted > Trisubstituted > Disubstituted > Monosubstituted.
  • Bayer's reagent (cold alkaline dilute KMnO4) oxidizes alkenes to vicinal cis-diols with concomitant color change from purple to brown MnO2 precipitate.
  • Epoxidation of alkenes using peroxycarboxylic acids (RCO3H) yields oxiranes (epoxides) via a concerted syn-addition mechanism.
  • Ozonlysis of alkenes cleaves both σ and π bonds of the alkene functionality to yield aldehydes, ketones, or carboxylic acids.
  • Polymerization of alkenes proceeds via chain-growth mechanisms (free-radical, cationic, or anionic) to form high molecular weight polymers like polythene.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the stereochemical outcome of catalytic hydrogenation (syn-addition) with Birch reduction (anti-addition).
  • Applying anti-Markovnikov addition to HCl and HI in the presence of peroxides; the peroxide effect is strictly limited to HBr.
  • Forgetting that carbocation rearrangements (hydride or methyl shifts) can occur during alcohol dehydration or electrophilic addition to alkenes.
  • Assuming geometrical isomers are optical isomers; cis-trans isomers are diastereomers, not enantiomers.

⚡ Quick Revision: Alkenes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

Alkenes contain sp2 hybridized carbon atoms forming planar systems with localized π-electron clouds vulnerable to electrophilic attack.

Methods of Preparation

Synthesized via elimination reactions (dehydration, dehydrohalogenation) or partial reduction of alkynes using specific stereoselective reagents.

Electrophilic Addition Reactions

Reactions proceed via carbocation or cyclic intermediates obeying Markovnikov regioselectivity unless specific free-radical conditions apply.

Oxidation and Cleavage

Oxidative cleavage by ozone or acidic potassium permanganate breaks the double bond entirely to generate distinct carbonyl fragments.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Olefin (Alkene class name)
Historical term meaning oil-forming, referring to oily products formed on halogenation.
Hyperconjugation (No-bond resonance)
Stabilization of π-systems through overlap of adjacent C-H σ-bonds with empty p-orbitals.
Zaitsev's Rule (Elimination rule)
Elimination reactions yield the highly substituted and stable alkene as the major product.
Ozonolysis (O3 cleavage)
Cleavage of unsaturation bonds using ozone followed by reductive or oxidative workup.
Lindlar Catalyst (Pd/CaCO3/quinoline)
Poisoned palladium catalyst used for selective cis-hydrogenation of alkynes.
Markovnikov Rule (Regioselective addition)
Addition rule where the electrophile adds to yield the more stable carbocation intermediate.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • General formula: CnH2n
  • Hybridization of C=C carbons: sp2
  • Bayer's test: Purple KMnO4 decolorized with brown precipitate
  • Peroxide effect applies only to HBr addition
  • Cis-dihydroxylation: Cold alkaline KMnO4 or OsO4

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons featuring C=C double bonds.
  • Electrophilic addition is their hallmark chemical property.
  • Stability is governed by hyperconjugation and steric factors.
  • Stereoselective synthesis allows control over cis and trans products.

⚖️ Key Differences

Cis-Addition VS Anti-Addition

Cis-addition adds groups from the same face of the double bond (e.g., catalytic hydrogenation), whereas anti-addition adds groups from opposite faces (e.g., halogenation via halonium ion).

Question 1 of 19

What is the hybridization of carbon atoms involved in the double bond of an alkene?

Explanation: Carbon atoms sharing a double bond are bonded to three other atoms in a trigonal planar geometry, corresponding to sp2 hybridization.

Question 2 of 19

Which of the following catalysts is used for the stereoselective cis-hydrogenation of alkynes to alkenes?

Explanation: Lindlar's catalyst (poisoned palladium on calcium carbonate) yields cis-alkenes from alkynes via syn-addition.

Question 3 of 19

The peroxide effect is observed during the addition of which reagent to alkenes?

Explanation: The free-radical addition mechanism (peroxide effect) works exclusively with HBr; HCl and HI do not undergo this radical chain addition readily.

Question 4 of 19

What is the major product formed when 2-methylpropene reacts with HCl?

Explanation: Addition follows Markovnikov's rule forming a tertiary carbocation intermediate, yielding 2-chloro-2-methylpropane.

Question 5 of 19

Cold dilute alkaline KMnO4 reacts with alkenes to form:

Explanation: Cold alkaline KMnO4 (Bayer's reagent) hydroxylates alkenes to give vicinal cis-diols (glycols).

Question 6 of 19

Which alkene has the highest stability among the following?

Explanation: 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene is tetrasubstituted and possesses the maximum number of α-hydrogens (12), rendering it most stable.

Question 7 of 19

What is the degree of unsaturation for a compound with molecular formula C5H8?

Explanation: DBE = 5 + 1 - (8/2) = 6 - 4 = 2. This indicates two double bonds or a ring plus a double bond.

Question 8 of 19

Ozonolysis of 2-methylbut-2-ene followed by reductive workup with Zn/H2O yields:

Explanation: Cleavage of (CH3)2C=CH-CH3 yields acetone ((CH3)2C=O) and acetaldehyde (CH3CHO).

Question 9 of 19

Which of the following reactions proceeds via a carbocation intermediate?

Explanation: Acid-catalyzed dehydration of alcohols proceeds via protonation of the hydroxyl group followed by loss of water to form a carbocation.

Question 10 of 19

Birch reduction of an alkyne produces:

Explanation: Birch reduction using Na in liquid NH3 reduces alkynes stereoselectively to trans-alkenes via radical-anion intermediates.

Question 11 of 19

What color change is observed when an alkene is treated with bromine water?

Explanation: The reddish-brown color of bromine water is discharged (decolorized) as Br2 adds across the alkene double bond.

Question 12 of 19

Which intermediate is formed during the addition of bromine to an alkene?

Explanation: Bromine addition proceeds via a cyclic halonium ion intermediate, accounting for anti-stereochemistry.

Question 13 of 19

Dehydrohalogenation of 2-bromobutane with alcoholic KOH primarily gives:

Explanation: According to Zaitsev's rule, elimination preferentially yields the more substituted and stable 2-butene.

Question 14 of 19

Hydroboration-oxidation of propene yields:

Explanation: Hydroboration-oxidation adds water in an anti-Markovnikov and syn-stereochemical fashion, converting propene to propan-1-ol.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following compounds exhibits geometrical isomerism?

Explanation: But-2-ene has different groups attached to each sp2 carbon of the double bond (CH3 and H), allowing cis-trans isomerism.

Question 16 of 19

Polyethylene is manufactured from ethene via which type of polymerization?

Explanation: Polyethylene is produced via addition polymerization of ethene using free-radical initiators or coordination catalysts (Ziegler-Natta).

Question 17 of 19

What is the product when propene reacts with NBS in the presence of light?

Explanation: NBS (N-bromosuccinimide) specifically brominates alkenes at the allylic position via free-radical substitution.

Question 18 of 19

The π-bond in alkenes is formed by the lateral overlap of:

Explanation: The π-bond results from sideways (lateral) overlap of unhybridized 2p atomic orbitals perpendicular to the nodal plane.

Question 19 of 19

Which reagent converts an alkene directly into a vicinal diol with syn-stereochemistry?

Explanation: Both cold alkaline KMnO4 and OsO4 effect syn-dihydroxylation of alkenes to form cis-1,2-diols.

Question 1 of 10
What is the main product of the reaction between propene and HBr in the absence of peroxides?

Solution: Electrophilic addition follows Markovnikov's rule, producing 2-bromopropane via the more stable secondary carbocation.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following compounds has the highest boiling point?

Solution: Cis-isomers have a net dipole moment, leading to stronger intermolecular dipole-dipole interactions and higher boiling points than trans-isomers.

Question 3 of 10
Reaction of cyclohexene with cold alkaline KMnO4 produces:

Solution: Cold alkaline KMnO4 brings about syn-hydroxylation, yielding cis-cyclohexane-1,2-diol.

Question 4 of 10
What is the intermediate formed during the acid-catalyzed hydration of ethene?

Solution: Protonation of ethene generates the ethyl carbocation intermediate before water attacks.

Question 5 of 10
Which reducing agent converts alkynes specifically to trans-alkenes?

Solution: Sodium in liquid ammonia reduces internal alkynes to trans-alkenes via a dissolving metal reduction mechanism.

Question 6 of 10
Allylic bromination of propene can be successfully carried out using:

Solution: N-bromosuccinimide (NBS) in the presence of light or heat provides low steady-state concentrations of Br2 for allylic substitution.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following alkenes cannot show geometrical isomerism?

Solution: 2-methylbut-2-ene has two identical methyl groups attached to one of the sp2 carbon atoms, preventing geometrical isomerism.

Question 8 of 10
What happens when ethene is passed through cold concentrated sulfuric acid?

Solution: Ethene absorbs into concentrated H2SO4 via electrophilic addition to form ethyl hydrogen sulfate.

Question 9 of 10
In the mechanism of electrophilic addition to alkenes, the rate-determining step is:

Solution: The initial attack of the π-electrons on the electrophile to form the carbocation or halonium intermediate is the slow, rate-determining step.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following compounds does not decolorize bromine water?

Solution: Ethane is a saturated alkane and lacks unsaturation to react with bromine water, unlike alkenes and alkynes.
