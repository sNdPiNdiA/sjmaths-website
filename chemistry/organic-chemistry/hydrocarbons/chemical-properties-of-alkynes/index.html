Chemical Properties of Alkynes

Alkynes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing a carbon-carbon triple bond characterized by high electron density and acidic terminal hydrogen atoms. Their chemical behavior is dominated by electrophilic addition, nucleophilic addition, oxidation, and metal-acetylide formation.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrophilic Addition Reactions

  • Alkynes undergo electrophilic addition reactions sequentially, forming vinyl halides or gem-dihalides.
  • The addition follows Markovnikov's rule, proceeding through a relatively unstable vinyl cation intermediate.
  • Halogens (Cl2, Br2) and hydrogen halides (HX) add across the triple bond to yield tetrahalides or geminal dihalides.
  • Addition of HX follows the order: HI > HBr > HCl due to decreasing bond dissociation enthalpy of the hydrogen halide.

Acidity of Terminal Alkynes and Metal Acetylides

  • Terminal alkynes possess an sp hybridized carbon atom, imparting significant s-character (50%) and high electronegativity.
  • The acidic proton is readily abstracted by strong bases like NaNH2, Grignard reagents, or ammoniacal Cu(I) and Ag(I) solutions.
  • Reaction with ammoniacal AgNO3 yields a white precipitate of silver acetylide, while Cu2Cl2 yields a red precipitate.
  • Internal alkynes lack acidic hydrogen atoms and do not react with metal salt solutions or sodamide.

Reduction, Oxidation, and Polymerization

  • Complete catalytic hydrogenation over Pt, Pd, or Ni yields alkanes via alkane intermediates.
  • Ozonolysis of alkynes yields diketones or carboxylic acids upon oxidative cleavage of both σ and π bonds.
  • Acetylene undergoes cyclic polymerization at high temperature over red-hot iron tube to produce benzene.
  • Terminal alkynes undergo cuprous chloride-catalyzed dimerization to form vinylacetylene.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Acidity of Terminal Alkynes
R-C≡C-H + NaNH2 → R-C≡C-Na+ + NH3
Conditions: Strong base in liquid ammonia solvent
Units: pKa ≈ 25
Lindlar Reduction (cis-Alkene Formation)
R-C≡C-R + H2 + Pd/CaCO3 (Quinoline) → cis-RCH=CHR
Conditions: Catalytic hydrogenation with poisoned catalyst
Units: Stereospecific syn-addition
Birch Reduction (trans-Alkene Formation)
R-C≡C-R + Na + liquid NH3 → trans-RCH=CHR
Conditions: Dissolving metal reduction
Units: Stereospecific anti-addition
Kishner-like Hydration of Alkynes
R-C≡CH + H2O + H2SO4/HgSO4 → R-CO-CH3
Conditions: Markovnikov addition via enol tautomerism
Units: Ketone formation (except acetylene yielding acetaldehyde)
General Formula of Alkynes
CnH2n-2
Conditions: Acyclic, open-chain alkyne with one triple bond
Units: n ≥ 2 (Integer)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Hydration of terminal alkynes typically yields ketones following Markovnikov's rule.

Observation: Hydration of acetylene (ethyne) — Acetylene yields acetaldehyde (ethanal) as the sole product because it is the only terminal alkyne lacking an alkyl group to dictate a higher ketone.

Exception Addition of HBr to alkenes in the presence of peroxides yields anti-Markovnikov products.

Observation: Addition of HBr to alkynes with peroxides — Peroxide effect is generally not observed with alkynes because the radical addition chain mechanism is less favorable compared to ionic addition pathways.

Exception Alkynes react readily with electrophiles due to high electron density.

Observation: Reaction rate of electrophilic addition to alkynes is slower than alkenes — The vinyl cation intermediate formed during alkyne addition is less stable than the alkyl carbocation formed during alkene addition.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Acetylene on passing through red-hot iron tube at 873 K polymerizes to benzene.
  • Terminal alkynes react with NaNH2 to form sodium acetylides and release hydrogen gas.
  • Hydration of propyne with H2SO4/HgSO4 gives acetone.
  • Lindlar catalyst is poisoned palladium deposited on calcium carbonate.
  • Birch reduction uses sodium in liquid ammonia to yield trans-alkenes.
  • Ozonolysis of 2-butyne yields biacetyl (2,3-butanedione).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the stereochemical outcome of Lindlar reduction (cis) with Birch reduction (trans).
  • Assuming internal alkynes react with ammoniacal silver nitrate to form silver acetylides.
  • Forgetting that hydration of acetylene produces acetaldehyde, not a ketone.
  • Assuming alkynes undergo electrophilic addition faster than alkenes due to having two pi bonds.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Properties of Alkynes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Acidity

The sp hybridization of alkynes provides 50% s-character, making terminal C-H bonds sufficiently acidic to react with strong bases and metal ions.

Electrophilic Addition

Addition reactions occur via vinyl cation intermediates, following Markovnikov orientation to yield gem-dihalides and tetrahalides.

Stereoselective Reduction

Catalytic hydrogenation with poisoned catalysts yields cis-alkenes, while dissolving metal reductions yield trans-alkenes.

Oxidation and Polymerization

Strong oxidizers cleave the triple bond to form carboxylic acids, and trimerization yields aromatic benzene rings.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Lindlar Catalyst (Pd/CaCO3 + Quinoline)
Poisoned palladium catalyst for syn-addition of H2 to alkynes.
Birch Reduction (Na + liq. NH3)
Dissolving metal reduction yielding trans-alkenes.
Vinyl Cation (R-C+=CH2)
Unstable reactive intermediate in alkyne electrophilic addition.
Metal Acetylide (R-C≡C-M)
Organometallic derivative formed by terminal alkyne deprotonation.
Markovnikov's Rule (Regioselectivity)
Addition rule where electrophile bonds to carbon with more hydrogens.
Tautomerism (Enol-Keto)
Rapid interconversion between enol and stable keto forms.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Terminal alkynes have pKa ≈ 25.
  • Acetylene + Red-hot Fe tube → Benzene.
  • Lindlar catalyst gives cis-alkene.
  • Birch reduction gives trans-alkene.
  • Hydration of ethyne gives ethanal.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkynes contain sp hybridized carbon atoms.
  • Terminal alkynes exhibit acidic properties.
  • Addition reactions follow Markovnikov rule through vinyl cations.
  • Reduction methods control alkene stereochemistry.

⚖️ Key Differences

Lindlar Reduction VS Birch Reduction

Lindlar yields cis-alkenes (syn-addition), whereas Birch yields trans-alkenes (anti-addition).

Question 3 of 18

Which of the following compounds will give a white precipitate with ammoniacal AgNO3?

Question 4 of 18

What is the product when acetylene is passed through a red-hot iron tube at 873 K?

Explanation: Cyclic trimerization of acetylene over red-hot iron tube produces benzene.

Question 5 of 18

The hybridization of carbon atoms in an alkyne triple bond is:

Explanation: Carbon atoms involved in a triple bond are sp hybridized with linear geometry.

Question 6 of 18

Terminal alkynes are acidic because:

Explanation: An sp hybridized carbon has 50% s-character, holding electrons closer to the nucleus and stabilizing the negative charge on the conjugate base.

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following reagents can distinguish between 1-butyne and 2-butyne?

Explanation: Ammoniacal cuprous chloride reacts only with terminal alkynes like 1-butyne to form a red precipitate.

Question 8 of 18

Addition of HCl to propyne in the presence of peroxide proceeds via:

Explanation: Addition of hydrogen halides to alkynes proceeds via ionic electrophilic addition even in the presence of peroxides.

Question 9 of 18

The intermediate formed during electrophilic addition of HX to alkynes is a:

Explanation: Protonation of the triple bond generates an sp2 hybridized vinyl cation intermediate.

Question 10 of 18

Ozonolysis of 2-butyne followed by hydrolysis yields:

Explanation: Oxidative cleavage of 2-butyne by ozone breaks both sigma and pi bonds of the triple bond, yielding biacetyl.

Question 11 of 18

Which alkyne does not react with sodium metal in liquid ammonia to give a sodium acetylide?

Explanation: 2-Butyne is an internal alkyne lacking an acidic terminal hydrogen, hence it does not react with sodium metal to form acetylides.

Question 12 of 18

What catalyst is used in Lindlar's reduction?

Explanation: Lindlar catalyst consists of palladium on calcium carbonate, deactivated with quinoline to stop reduction at the cis-alkene stage.

Question 13 of 18

The pKa value of terminal alkynes is approximately:

Explanation: Terminal alkynes have a pKa around 25, making them much more acidic than alkenes (pKa ~ 44) or alkanes (pKa ~ 50).

Question 14 of 18

Reaction of acetylene with excess hydrogen chloride gives:

Explanation: Addition of two equivalents of HCl to acetylene follows Markovnikov's rule on the second addition, yielding 1,1-dichloroethane (gem-dihalide).

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following compounds has the shortest carbon-carbon bond length?

Explanation: Ethyne contains a carbon-carbon triple bond, resulting in the highest bond order and shortest bond length (120 pm).

Question 16 of 18

When propyne reacts with hypochlorous acid (HOCl), the major product is:

Explanation: Addition follows Markovnikov rule where OH- attacks the more substituted carbon and Cl+ attacks the terminal carbon, leading to 1-chloro-2-propanone.

Question 17 of 18

Polymerization of acetylene with nickel cyanide at high pressure yields:

Explanation: Tetramerization of acetylene in the presence of nickel cyanide catalyst yields cyclooctatetraene.

Question 18 of 18

Which functional group is formed when an alkyne undergoes hydration?

Explanation: Hydration of alkynes yields enols that spontaneously tautomerize to carbonyl compounds (aldehydes or ketones).

The conversion of propyne to acetone involves which type of reaction mechanism?

Solution: Hydration proceeds via electrophilic addition of water across the triple bond, followed by keto-enol tautomerism.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following compounds is most acidic?

Solution: Ethyne has sp hybridized carbon atoms with 50% s-character, making its protons significantly more acidic than those of ethene or ethane.

Question 3 of 10
What is the principal organic product formed when 1-butyne reacts with 2 equivalents of HBr?

Solution: Addition follows Markovnikov's rule twice, resulting in both bromine atoms bonding to carbon-2, forming 2,2-dibromobutane.

Question 4 of 10
Which metal acetylide is explosive when dry?

Solution: Silver and copper acetylides are shock-sensitive and explosive when dry.

Question 5 of 10
Ozonolysis of an alkyne R-C≡C-R' followed by Zn/H2O gives:

Solution: Reductive ozonolysis of alkynes yields alpha-diketones (RCOCOR').

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following reagents reduces alkynes to alkanes directly?

Solution: Hydrogen over platinum, palladium, or nickel catalysts completely hydrogenates alkynes to alkanes.

Question 7 of 10
What is the intermediate in the addition of electrophiles to alkynes?

Solution: Electrophilic addition to alkynes generates a vinyl carbocation as the key reactive intermediate.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following reactions is used for chain elongation in alkynes?

Solution: Nucleophilic displacement of alkyl halides by metal acetylides constructs longer carbon chains.

Question 9 of 10
Cuprous chloride in ammonium hydroxide reacts with 1-butyne to give a precipitate of color:

Solution: Terminal alkynes react with ammoniacal cuprous chloride to yield a characteristic red precipitate of copper(I) acetylide.

Question 10 of 10
Why is the electrophilic addition of halogens to alkynes slower than to alkenes?

Solution: The vinyl cation intermediate formed during alkyne halogenation is higher in energy and less stable than the alkyl carbocation formed from alkenes.
