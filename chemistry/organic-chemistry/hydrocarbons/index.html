Organic Chemistry

Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons are organic compounds composed exclusively of carbon and hydrogen atoms, classified into aliphatic, alicyclic, and aromatic systems based on their structural frameworks. Their chemical reactivity is governed by carbon hybridization (sp3, sp2, sp) and the cleavage of robust C-C and C-H sigma bonds.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Alkanes and Cycloalkanes: Structure and Conformation

  • Alkanes exhibit sp3 hybridization with a tetrahedral geometry and bond angles of approximately 109.5°.
  • Conformational isomerism in ethane yields stable staggered conformers and less stable eclipsed conformers due to torsional strain.
  • Baeyer strain theory explains that cyclopropane and cyclobutane possess high angular strain, leading to enhanced ring-opening reactivity.
  • Chair conformation of cyclohexane is completely strain-free, with equatorial positions more stable for bulky substituents.

Alkenes and Alkynes: Electrophilic Addition and Acidity

  • Alkenes undergo electrophilic addition reactions via carbocation intermediates, governed by Markovnikov's rule.
  • Anti-Markovnikov addition occurs in the presence of peroxides via a free-radical chain mechanism for HBr only.
  • Terminal alkynes exhibit weakly acidic behavior due to the high % s-character (50%) of sp-hybridized carbon atoms.
  • Reduction of alkynes with Birch reduction (Na/liquid NH3) yields trans-alkenes, whereas catalytic hydrogenation with Lindlar's catalyst yields cis-alkenes.

Aromatic Hydrocarbons: Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution

  • Aromatic systems obey Hückel's Rule of aromaticity containing (4n + 2) π-electrons in a planar conjugated ring.
  • Electrophilic aromatic substitution (EAS) proceeds via a resonance-stabilized Wheland intermediate or sigma complex.
  • Activating groups (e.g., -OH, -NH2, -CH3) are ortho/para-directing due to resonance or inductive electron donation.
  • Deactivating groups (e.g., -NO2, -CN, -COOH) are meta-directing due to electron withdrawal from the ring.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Peroxide effect applies uniformly to all hydrogen halides (HCl, HBr, HI) during addition to alkenes.

Observation: Peroxide effect is exclusively observed with HBr. — The propagation step for HCl is endothermic because of high H-Cl bond dissociation energy, while HI step 1 is endothermic because the I-I bond is weak and iodine radicals readily recombine.

Exception Tertiary carbocations are always formed as the exclusive intermediate in electrophilic additions.

Observation: Rearrangements can occur to form more stable carbocations via 1,2-hydride or 1,2-methyl shifts. — Driving force is the thermodynamic stability of secondary to tertiary or bridged carbocation structures.

Exception Electrophilic aromatic substitution on nitrobenzene yields ortho and para substituted products predominantly.

Observation: Nitrobenzene yields exclusively meta-substituted products. — The nitro group is strongly electron-withdrawing via -M and -I effects, drastically decreasing electron density at ortho and para positions.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Hückel Rule Counting
Total π electrons = 2, 6, 10, 14...

Application: Set (4n + 2) equal to total number of π-electrons. If n is a whole integer (0, 1, 2...), the system is aromatic.

Markovnikov Addition Shortcut
Rich get richer

Application: In unsymmetrical alkenes, the electrophile (H+) attaches to the carbon with more hydrogen atoms to form the most stable carbocation.

Degree of Unsaturation Quick Count
DU = (Σ Vn(n - 2) / 2) + 1

Application: Sum the valency contributions of each atom to quickly assess rings and pi-bonds from complex structures without full formula generation.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The C-C bond length in ethane is 1.54 Å, whereas in ethene it is 1.34 Å and in ethyne it is 1.20 Å.
  • Wurtz reaction couples two alkyl halides using sodium metal in dry ether to form higher symmetrical alkanes.
  • Kolbe's electrolytic decarboxylation of sodium or potassium salts of carboxylic acids yields alkanes at the anode.
  • Ozonolysis of alkenes followed by reductive workup (Zn/H2O) yields aldehydes and/or ketones.
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation of benzene often suffers from carbocation rearrangement and polyalkylation.
  • Benzene undergoes electrophilic substitution rather than addition because addition destroys its stable aromatic stabilization energy (~150 kJ/mol).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming cyclopropane is stable due to single bonds; it actually undergoes ring-opening reactions easily due to high angle strain.
  • Forgetting that anti-Markovnikov addition via peroxides works exclusively for HBr and fails for HCl and HI.
  • Confusing Birch reduction products (trans-alkene) with Lindlar catalyst products (cis-alkene) from alkynes.
  • Treating meta-directing groups as activating; all meta-directing groups are strongly deactivating in EAS.

⚡ Quick Revision: Hydrocarbons

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Alkane Preparation and Properties

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons prepared via hydrogenation of alkenes/alkynes, reduction of alkyl halides, Wurtz coupling, and Kolbe electrolysis. They undergo free-radical halogenation via chain initiation, propagation, and termination steps.

Alkene Reactivity and Stereochemistry

Alkenes contain a weak π-bond making them susceptible to electrophilic addition. Reactions include halogenation, hydrohalogenation, hydration, ozonolysis, and hydroxylation yielding distinct stereochemical outcomes.

Alkyne Acidity and Reduction

Terminal alkynes possess acidic protons removable by strong bases like NaNH2 to form metal acetylides. Reduction pathways allow stereospecific synthesis of cis- or trans-alkenes using tailored reducing conditions.

Aromaticity and Benzene Chemistry

Aromatic hydrocarbons possess cyclic delocalized (4n + 2) π-electron clouds providing exceptional thermodynamic stability. Electrophilic aromatic substitution proceeds via a sigma complex without destroying the aromatic ring permanently.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Aromaticity (Hückel's Rule (4n + 2))
Special thermodynamic stability of planar cyclic conjugated systems with (4n + 2) π-electrons.
Markovnikov's Rule (Regioselectivity)
Addition of protic acids to unsymmetrical alkenes places the hydrogen on the carbon with more hydrogens.
Ozonolysis (O3 cleavage)
Oxidative cleavage of unsaturated carbon-carbon multiple bonds into carbonyl compounds.
Wurtz Reaction (2R-X + 2Na → R-R + 2NaX)
Coupling of alkyl halides using sodium metal in dry ether to form symmetrical alkanes.
Birch Reduction (Na / liq. NH3)
Stereoselective reduction of alkynes to trans-alkenes via dissolved metal electron transfer.
Lindlar Catalyst (Pd/BaSO4, quinoline)
Poisoned palladium catalyst used for stereospecific cis-hydrogenation of alkynes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Benzene resonance energy is approximately 150 kJ/mol.
  • Stability order of free radicals and carbocations: 3° > 2° > 1° > Methyl.
  • Stability order of alkenes: Tetrasubstituted > Trisubstituted > Disubstituted > Monosubstituted.
  • Angle strain in cyclopropane is 24° 44' (tetrahedral angle minus internal angle).
  • All meta-directing groups in aromatic substitution are electron-withdrawing deactivated groups.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkanes exhibit sp3 hybridization and undergo free radical substitution.
  • Alkenes possess sp2 hybridization and undergo electrophilic addition.
  • Alkynes possess sp hybridization, exhibit terminal proton acidity, and undergo addition reactions.
  • Aromatic systems undergo electrophilic aromatic substitution while preserving the stable ring system.

⚖️ Key Differences

Inductive Effect (-I) VS Mesomeric Effect (-M)

Inductive effect acts through sigma bonds and distance-dependently, whereas mesomeric effect acts through pi-bonds via resonance across the conjugated system.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following compounds is aromatic according to Hückel's rule?

Explanation: Benzene has 6 π-electrons, fitting the 4n + 2 rule where n = 1, making it planar and aromatic.

Question 2 of 18

What is the principal product formed when propene reacts with HBr in the presence of benzoyl peroxide?

Explanation: In the presence of peroxides, HBr adds to unsymmetrical alkenes via free radical mechanism yielding anti-Markovnikov product, 1-bromopropane.

Question 3 of 18

Which reagent converts an alkyne directly into a trans-alkene?

Explanation: Birch reduction using sodium in liquid ammonia reduces alkynes stereospecifically to trans-alkenes via radical anion intermediates.

Question 4 of 18

What is the hybridization of carbon atoms in acetylene (ethyne)?

Explanation: Ethyne contains a triple bond, making each carbon sp-hybridized with linear geometry.

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following alkyl halides undergoes Wurtz reaction most efficiently to give a pure product?

Explanation: Wurtz reaction with a single primary alkyl halide (like CH3Cl) yields a single symmetrical alkane (ethane), whereas mixtures yield statistical product mixtures.

Question 6 of 18

The conversion of hexane into benzene in the presence of Cr2O3/Al2O3 at 773 K is called:

Explanation: Aromatization involves cyclization and dehydrogenation of alkanes containing 6 or more carbons into aromatic hydrocarbons.

Question 7 of 18

Which group is strongly activating and ortho/para-directing in electrophilic aromatic substitution?

Explanation: The hydroxyl group (-OH) donates electrons via resonance (+M effect), activating the benzene ring and directing incoming electrophiles to ortho and para positions.

Question 8 of 18

What is the degree of unsaturation for a molecule with formula C6H10?

Explanation: DU = C + 1 - H/2 = 6 + 1 - 10/2 = 7 - 5 = 2. This corresponds to two double bonds, one triple bond, or one ring and one double bond.

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following cycloalkanes exhibits the highest angular strain?

Explanation: Cyclopropane has a bond angle of 60° compared to the tetrahedral 109.5°, resulting in maximum deviation and highest angular strain according to Baeyer strain theory.

Question 10 of 18

Ozonolysis of 2-methylbut-2-ene followed by zinc dust and water yields:

Explanation: Cleavage of (CH3)2C=CH-CH3 at the double bond yields acetone (CH3COCH3) and acetaldehyde (CH3CHO).

Question 11 of 18

Which catalyst is used for Friedel-Crafts alkylation of benzene?

Explanation: Anhydrous aluminium chloride (AlCl3) acts as a Lewis acid catalyst to generate the carbocation electrophile from alkyl halides.

Question 12 of 18

What happens when ethyne is passed through a red-hot iron tube at 873 K?

Explanation: Cyclic polymerization of three molecules of ethyne over red-hot iron tube yields benzene.

Question 13 of 18

The acidic character of alkynes is due to:

Explanation: The sp hybridized carbon has 50% s-character, making it highly electronegative and capable of stabilizing the negative charge on the conjugate base acetylide ion.

Question 14 of 18

Kolbe's electrolytic method produces alkanes at which electrode?

Explanation: During electrolysis of aqueous sodium carboxylates, alkyl radicals combine at the anode to form alkanes, while hydrogen gas is liberated at the cathode.

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following is most reactive towards electrophilic substitution?

Explanation: Phenol contains a strongly activating -OH group that increases electron density in the benzene ring via resonance, making it highly reactive.

Question 16 of 18

The most stable conformation of cyclohexane is:

Explanation: The chair conformation is completely free of angle strain and torsional strain, making it the most stable conformer.

Question 17 of 18

Addition of water to acetylene in the presence of HgSO4 and dilute H2SO4 gives:

Explanation: Hydration of ethyne forms unstable vinyl alcohol which tautomerizes instantly to stable ethanal (acetaldehyde).

Question 18 of 18

Which compound does not decolourize alkaline KMnO4 solution (Baeyer's reagent)?

Explanation: Alkanes like ethane lack unsaturation and do not react with or decolourize cold dilute alkaline KMnO4.

Question 1 of 10
What is the product formed when sodium propanoate is electrolyzed in concentrated aqueous solution?

Solution: Kolbe electrolysis of sodium propanoate yields ethyl radicals at the anode which couple to form butane (C4H10).

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following is non-aromatic?

Solution: Cyclooctatetraene has 8 π-electrons (4n system) and adopts a non-planar tub shape to avoid antiaromatic instability, making it non-aromatic.

Question 3 of 10
In the free-radical chlorination of methane, which propagation step is exothermic?

Solution: The reaction of methyl radical with chlorine molecule to form chloromethyl and chlorine radical is highly exothermic and fast.

Question 4 of 10
What is the primary product of acid-catalyzed hydration of 2-methylpropene?

Solution: Protonation yields a tertiary carbocation which is attacked by water to form 2-methylpropan-2-ol (tert-butyl alcohol).

Question 5 of 10
Which reaction can be used to prepare methane in the laboratory?

Solution: Heating anhydrous sodium acetate with sodalime (NaOH + CaO) eliminates CO2 (decarboxylation) to produce pure methane gas.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following dienes has the most stable conjugated system?

Solution: Buta-1,3-diene has alternating single and double bonds, allowing effective delocalization of pi-electrons (conjugation) conferring extra thermodynamic stability.

Question 7 of 10
Benzene reacts with acetyl chloride in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3 to give:

Solution: Friedel-Crafts acylation of benzene with acetyl chloride yields acetophenone (phenyl methyl ketone).

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following compounds has the lowest boiling point?

Solution: Isobutane is more branched and compact than n-butane, leading to smaller surface area and weaker intermolecular forces, resulting in lower boiling point.

Question 9 of 10
What is the major product when 3-methylbut-1-ene reacts with HCl?

Solution: Addition of HCl via secondary carbocation followed by a 1,2-hydride shift yields the more stable tertiary carbocation, forming 2-chloro-2-methylbutane.

Question 10 of 10
Which hydrocarbon gives glyoxal on ozonolysis followed by Zn/H2O?

Solution: Ozonolysis of acetylene (ethyne) cleaves the triple bond to yield glyoxal (OHC-CHO) upon reductive workup.
