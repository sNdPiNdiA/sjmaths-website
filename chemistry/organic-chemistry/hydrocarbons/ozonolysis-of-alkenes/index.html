Ozonolysis of Alkenes

Ozonolysis is an organic cleavage reaction where ozone reacts with alkenes to form primary ozonides, which subsequently decompose into carbonyl compounds. The reaction pathway depends on whether reductive or oxidative workup conditions are applied.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mechanism and Intermediate Formation

  • Ozone adds across the carbon-carbon double bond via a 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition to form an unstable molozonide (1,2,3-trioxolane).
  • The molozonide rapidly rearranges through a cycloreversion and re-cycloaddition sequence to yield the stable ozonide (1,2,4-trioxolane).

Reductive Workup Pathways

  • Treatment of the ozonide with Zinc and water or Dimethyl sulfide (DMS) cleaves it reductively to prevent further oxidation.
  • Monosubstituted alkenes yield aldehydes, while disubstituted alkenes yield ketones or a mixture depending on substitution.

Oxidative Workup and Structural Determination

  • When hydrogen peroxide or peracids are present during workup, any intermediate aldehydes are further oxidized to carboxylic acids.
  • Ozonolysis serves as a definitive structural proof for locating the position of double bonds in unknown unsaturated hydrocarbons.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Reductive Ozonolysis (Zinc)
RCH=CHR + O3 + Zn + H2O → 2 RCHO + ZnO + H2O2
Conditions: Addition of zinc dust to destroy hydrogen peroxide
Units: Yields aldehydes or ketones without over-oxidation
Oxidative Ozonolysis
RCH=CHR + O3 → [Ozonide] → H2O2/H2O → 2 RCOOH
Conditions: Presence of an oxidizing agent or allowing H2O2 to react
Units: Yields carboxylic acids from terminal aldehydes
Dimethyl Sulfide (DMS) Workup
R2C=CR2 + O3 + (CH3)2S → 2 R2C=O + (CH3)2SO
Conditions: Mild reductive cleavage preventing acid formation
Units: Stoichiometric consumption of dimethyl sulfide
General Cleavage Reaction
R2C=CR2 + O3 → Ozonide → 2 R2C=O
Conditions: Reductive workup (Zn / H2O or DMS)
Units: Stoichiometric reaction of 1 mole alkene per 1 mole ozone
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Ozonolysis of alkenes yields aldehydes or ketones upon reductive workup.

Observation: Terminal aldehydes produced during oxidative workup are oxidized to carboxylic acids or carbon dioxide. — In the presence of hydrogen peroxide or oxidizing workup conditions, the aldehyde hydrogen is abstracted to form carboxyl groups.

Exception Symmetrical internal alkenes yield two equivalents of a single carbonyl compound.

Observation: Unsymmetrical cyclic alkenes yield a single dicarbonyl compound instead of two separate fragments. — The ring structure keeps the two cleaved carbonyl units tethered within the same molecular framework.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
The 'Erase and Insert' Shortcut
C=C → C=O + O=C

Application: Simply erase the double bond in the alkene structure and attach an oxygen atom (O=) to each of the two previously bonded carbons.

Ring Opening Rule
Cyclic Alkene → Open-chain Dicarbonyl

Application: Cleaving a cyclic alkene breaks the ring open at the double bond site, generating a single open-chain molecule containing two terminal carbonyl groups.

Oxidative vs Reductive Check
Zn/H2O = Stop at Aldehyde; H2O2 = Push to Acid

Application: Immediately convert any formed aldehyde to a carboxylic acid if the reagent list includes oxidative conditions like H2O2 or KMnO4.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Ozone is a 1,3-dipole that adds to alkenes via 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition.
  • The primary intermediate formed immediately after ozone addition is a 1,2,3-trioxolane (molozonide).
  • Molozonide rearranges to 1,2,4-trioxolane, commonly known as the ozonide.
  • Zinc dust in reductive workup reacts with H2O2 to prevent unwanted secondary oxidations.
  • Dimethyl sulfide (DMS) is oxidized to dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) during reductive workup.
  • Ozonolysis of ethene (CH2=CH2) exclusively produces formaldehyde (HCHO).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting that terminal aldehydes can be oxidized further under oxidative conditions.
  • Confusing molozonide (1,2,3-trioxolane) structure with the stable ozonide (1,2,4-trioxolane) structure.
  • Failing to break cyclic rings into open-chain dicarbonyl compounds during structural elucidation problems.
  • Assuming ozone acts as a free radical halogenation reagent rather than a cycloaddition reactant.

⚡ Quick Revision: Ozonolysis of Alkenes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Cycloaddition and Molozonide

Ozone reacts with the π-bond of an alkene via concerted 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition to form an unstable primary ozonide (molozonide).

Ozonide Rearrangement

The molozonide undergoes retro-1,3-dipolar cycloaddition followed by recombination to form the stable secondary ozonide (1,2,4-trioxolane).

Reductive Workup

Cleavage of the ozonide using Zn/H2O or DMS yields aldehydes and ketones without permitting oxidative side reactions.

Oxidative Workup

Workup with hydrogen peroxide converts aldehydes into carboxylic acids, altering the final isolated product distribution.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Ozonolysis (O3 Cleavage)
Cleavage of an alkene double bond using ozone.
Molozonide (1,2,3-Trioxolane)
Unstable primary cyclic addition intermediate.
Ozonide (1,2,4-Trioxolane)
Stable secondary cyclic rearrangement product.
Reductive Workup (Zn/H2O or DMS)
Chemical treatment yielding aldehydes and ketones.
Oxidative Workup (H2O2)
Chemical treatment yielding carboxylic acids.
Cycloaddition ([3+2] Addition)
Reaction uniting two unsaturated molecules into a ring.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Alkenes react with O3 to form molozonides then ozonides.
  • Zn/H2O prevents over-oxidation of aldehydes.
  • Dimethyl sulfide gets converted to DMSO upon workup.
  • Cyclic alkenes yield open-chain dicarbonyls.
  • Ozonolysis determines the exact position of carbon-carbon double bonds.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Ozone adds across C=C bonds to form 5-membered ring ozonides.
  • Reductive cleavage stops at aldehydes and ketones.
  • Oxidative workup pushes aldehydes to carboxylic acids.
  • Shortcut: Cut the double bond and attach oxygen to each carbon.

⚖️ Key Differences

Molozonide VS Ozonide

Molozonide contains three contiguous oxygen atoms (1,2,3-trioxolane), whereas the ozonide has an ether linkage interrupting the peroxide chain (1,2,4-trioxolane).

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following alkenes yields only formaldehyde upon reductive ozonolysis?

Solution: Ethene has only two carbons (CH2=CH2), both of which become formaldehyde molecules.

Question 3 of 10
In the mechanism of ozonolysis, what is the role of ozone?

Solution: Ozone functions as a 1,3-dipole undergoing cycloaddition with the alkene π bond.

Question 4 of 10
What byproduct is formed when dimethyl sulfide is used for reductive workup of an ozonide?

Solution: Dimethyl sulfide reduces the ozonide and gets oxidized to dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO).

Question 5 of 10
An unknown alkene on reductive ozonolysis gives one mole of formaldehyde and one mole of acetone. The structure of the alkene is:

Solution: Acetone ((CH3)2C=O) plus formaldehyde (H2C=O) corresponds to 2-methylpropene ((CH3)2C=CH2).

Question 6 of 10
Which ring transformation occurs during the conversion of molozonide to ozonide?

Solution: The unstable 1,2,3-trioxolane (molozonide) rearranges into the stable 1,2,4-trioxolane (ozonide).

Question 7 of 10
What happens to terminal alkyne or alkene carbon atoms bonded to hydrogen during oxidative ozonolysis?

Solution: Oxidative workup oxidizes formyl hydrogens to hydroxyl groups, forming carboxylic acids or carbon dioxide.

Question 8 of 10
How many moles of ozone are required to completely react with one mole of 1,3-butadiene?

Solution: Since 1,3-butadiene contains two double bonds, two moles of ozone are consumed per mole of diene.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following statements regarding ozonolysis is incorrect?

Solution: Molozonide is extremely unstable and rapidly rearranges to the more stable ozonide.

Question 10 of 10
What is the ultimate structural utility of ozonolysis in classical organic chemistry?

Solution: By identifying the carbonyl fragments produced, the exact position of the double bond in the parent alkene can be deduced.
