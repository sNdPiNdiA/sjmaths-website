Chemical Properties of Alkenes

Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing a carbon-carbon double bond (>C=C<) characterized by electrophilic addition reactions. The pi-bond is electron-rich and readily attacks electrophiles to form stable carbocation intermediates.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrophilic Addition Reactions

  • The π-bond acts as a nucleophile, attacking electrophiles such as protons, halogens, and metal cations to generate a carbocation intermediate.
  • Markovnikov's rule dictates that the electrophile adds to the less substituted carbon to yield the more stable tertiary or secondary carbocation.
  • Anti-Markovnikov addition occurs exclusively with HBr in the presence of peroxides via a free-radical chain mechanism.

Oxidation and Hydroxylation Reactions

  • Baeyer's reagent (cold, dilute alkaline KMnO4) converts alkenes into syn-1,2-diols accompanied by a color change from purple to brown.
  • Peroxycarboxylic acids (RCO3H) react with alkenes to yield epoxides (oxiranes) through stereospecific syn-addition.
  • Ozonolysis cleaves both σ and π-bonds of the alkene, producing aldehydes, ketones, or carboxylic acids depending on substituents and oxidative workup.

Reduction and Polymerization

  • Catalytic hydrogenation using Pt, Pd, or Ni catalysts adds hydrogen across the double bond in a syn-stereospecific manner to yield alkanes.
  • Alkenes undergo addition polymerization under high pressure and temperature via free-radical, cationic, or anionic pathways.
  • Terminal alkenes undergo hydroboration-oxidation to yield anti-Markovnikov alcohols via concerted syn-addition of H and OH.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Addition of HX to alkenes always follows Markovnikov's rule

Observation: Addition of HBr in the presence of organic peroxides yields anti-Markovnikov products — Peroxide-induced homolytic cleavage generates bromine radicals, shifting the reaction mechanism from electrophilic addition to a free-radical chain propagation pathway.

Exception Halogenation of alkenes yields vicinal dihalides with anti-stereochemistry

Observation: Addition of halogens in the presence of strong nucleophiles like water yields halohydrins — Water acts as a superior nucleophile compared to halide ions, trapping the cyclic halonium ion intermediate preferentially.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Ozonolysis Shortcut Mnemonic
Cut C=C → Replace with two C=O groups

Application: Directly predict aldehyde and ketone products by erasing the double bond and attaching oxygen atoms to each formerly bonded carbon.

Carbocation Rearrangement Rule
1,2-Shift for 2° to 3° stability

Application: Always check for hydride or methide shifts whenever a secondary carbocation is formed adjacent to a tertiary or quaternary carbon during electrophilic addition.

Syn vs Anti Addition Trick
KMnO4/OsO4/H2 → Syn; X2/CCl4 → Anti

Application: Instantly determine stereochemical outcome based on the reagent: cyclic intermediates (bromonium, mercurinium) lead to anti products, while concerted cyclic transition states (osmate esters, permanganate esters) lead to syn products.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The electrophilic addition to alkenes is initiated by the attack of the π-electrons on an electrophile.
  • Carbocation stability order follows 3° > 2° > 1° due to hyperconjugation and inductive effects.
  • Baeyer's test using alkaline KMnO4 is a standard chemical test for unsaturation.
  • Catalytic hydrogenation is exothermic, with heat of hydrogenation decreasing as alkene stability increases.
  • Ozonolysis followed by zinc dust reduction prevents further oxidation of aldehydes to carboxylic acids.
  • Anti-Markovnikov addition is exclusively observed for HBr in peroxide; HCl and HI do not exhibit this anomaly.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting that carbocation rearrangements can occur during acid-catalyzed hydration or hydrohalogenation.
  • Assuming peroxide effect applies to HCl and HI as well as HBr.
  • Confusing the products of reductive ozonolysis (Zn/H2O) with oxidative ozonolysis (H2O2).
  • Neglecting stereochemistry (syn vs anti addition) when determining geometrical isomers of products.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Properties of Alkenes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrophilic Addition Mechanism

Alkenes react via a two-step electrophilic addition where the π-bond forms a carbocation or cyclic intermediate followed by nucleophilic attack.

Oxidative Cleavage

Strong oxidizing agents like acidic KMnO4 or O3 cleave the carbon-carbon double bond completely to form carbonyl or carboxylic derivatives.

Stereospecific Hydroxylation

Hydroxylation agents like OsO4 or cold KMnO4 deliver two hydroxyl groups from the same face, yielding cis-diols.

Free Radical Addition

In the presence of peroxides, HBr adds to unsymmetrical alkenes against Markovnikov's rule via a radical chain mechanism.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Markovnikov Rule (Regioselectivity rule)
Electrophile adds to the carbon with more hydrogen atoms.
Ozonolysis (C=C Cleavage)
Cleavage of double bonds using ozone followed by reduction.
Baeyer Reagent (Cold alkaline KMnO4)
Reagent used to detect unsaturation via syn-hydroxylation.
Hydroboration (BH3 THF addition)
Method for anti-Markovnikov hydration of alkenes.
Halonium Ion (Cyclic intermediate)
Three-membered intermediate formed during halogen addition.
Hydrogenation (H2 / Catalyst)
Addition of hydrogen across a double bond to form an alkane.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carbocation stability: 3° > 2° > 1°
  • Peroxide effect works only for HBr.
  • Baeyer's reagent turns from purple to brown.
  • Ozonolysis with Zn/H2O stops at aldehydes/ketones.
  • Catalytic hydrogenation is always syn-addition.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkenes undergo characteristic electrophilic addition reactions due to electron-rich π-bonds.
  • Regioselectivity is governed by Markovnikov's rule unless peroxides are present.
  • Oxidation reactions provide valuable tools for structural determination via bond cleavage.
  • Stereochemical outcomes depend strictly on the cyclic or open-chain nature of reaction intermediates.

⚖️ Key Differences

Acid-Catalyzed Hydration VS Hydroboration-Oxidation

Acid hydration gives Markovnikov alcohol with possible rearrangements; hydroboration gives anti-Markovnikov alcohol with syn-addition and no rearrangements.

Question 1 of 19

What is the major product formed when propene reacts with HBr in the presence of benzoyl peroxide?

Explanation: In the presence of peroxides, HBr adds to unsymmetrical alkenes via a free-radical mechanism yielding the anti-Markovnikov product, 1-bromopropane.

Question 2 of 19

Which reagent is used for syn-dihydroxylation of alkenes?

Explanation: Cold dilute alkaline KMnO4 (Baeyer's reagent) hydroxylates alkenes stereospecifically to form syn-1,2-diols.

Question 3 of 19

What intermediate is formed during the addition of bromine to ethene in CCl4?

Explanation: Halogenation proceeds via a cyclic halonium ion intermediate, which is subsequently opened by halide attack with anti-stereochemistry.

Question 4 of 19

Ozonolysis of 2-methylbut-2-ene followed by Zn/H2O yields:

Explanation: Cleaving (CH3)2C=CH-CH3 at the double bond yields acetone (CH3)2CO and acetaldehyde CH3CHO.

Question 5 of 19

Which of the following carbocations is most stable?

Explanation: Tertiary carbocations ((CH3)3C+) are stabilized by maximum hyperconjugative structures and inductive release from three alkyl groups.

Question 6 of 19

Hydration of 2-methylpropene with dilute H2SO4 gives mainly:

Explanation: Protonation forms a stable tertiary carbocation, which is attacked by water to yield tert-butyl alcohol via Markovnikov orientation.

Question 7 of 19

Which alkene does not show geometrical isomerism?

Explanation: 1-butene has two identical hydrogen atoms attached to C-1 terminal carbon, failing the structural requirement for cis-trans isomerism.

Question 8 of 19

The conversion of an alkene to an alkane using hydrogen gas and nickel catalyst is termed:

Explanation: Catalytic addition of molecular hydrogen across the double bond is called catalytic hydrogenation.

Question 9 of 19

What is the product of acid-catalyzed hydration of ethene?

Explanation: Ethene reacts with water in the presence of concentrated H2SO4 to form ethanol.

Question 10 of 19

Which of the following compounds gives only formaldehyde upon ozonolysis?

Explanation: Ethene (CH2=CH2) yields two moles of formaldehyde (HCHO) upon ozonolysis.

Question 11 of 19

The peroxide effect is applicable to which of the following hydrogen halides?

Explanation: The peroxide effect is exclusive to HBr; HCl bond is too strong to break homolytically, and HI radical step is endothermic.

Question 12 of 19

Reaction of propene with hypochlorous acid (HOCl) yields:

Explanation: HOCl acts as OH- and Cl+, where Cl+ attacks the double bond to form a chloronium ion, yielding 1-chloro-2-propanol following Markovnikov rule.

Question 13 of 19

What type of reaction is the addition of bromine to ethene?

Explanation: Alkenes possess electron-rich pi bonds and undergo electrophilic addition reactions.

Question 14 of 19

Which reagent converts alkenes into epoxides?

Explanation: Peroxy acids (e.g., mCPBA) transfer an oxygen atom to the alkene pi bond, forming epoxides.

Question 15 of 19

In electrophilic addition to alkenes, the rate-determining step is:

Explanation: The slow, rate-determining step of electrophilic addition is the initial electrophilic attack forming the carbocation intermediate.

Question 16 of 19

Which alkene on vigorous oxidation with hot acidic KMnO4 gives acetic acid and CO2?

Explanation: Propene oxidative cleavage breaks the terminal CH2 group into CO2 and H2O, and the CH3-CH= fragment into acetic acid.

Question 17 of 19

What is the hybridization of carbon atoms forming the double bond in alkenes?

Explanation: Double-bonded carbons in alkenes are sp2 hybridized with trigonal planar geometry.

Question 18 of 19

Which of the following catalysts is used in the commercial hydrogenation of oils?

Explanation: Finely divided nickel is widely used for catalytic hydrogenation of vegetable oils to saturated fats.

Question 19 of 19

Hydroboration-oxidation of propene yields:

Explanation: Hydroboration-oxidation is an anti-Markovnikov hydration yielding primary alcohol propan-1-ol from propene.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary intermediate formed in the acid-catalyzed hydration of alkenes?

Solution: Acid-catalyzed hydration proceeds via protonation of the alkene to form a carbocation intermediate, which is then attacked by water.

Question 2 of 10
Which reagent achieves syn-dihydroxylation of alkenes via a cyclic osmate ester intermediate?

Solution: Osmium tetroxide (OsO4) reacts with alkenes in a concerted pericyclic cycloaddition to form a cyclic osmate ester, yielding cis-diols upon hydrolysis.

Question 3 of 10
What products are formed when 2-methylbut-2-ene undergoes ozonolysis with oxidative workup (O3 / H2O2)?

Solution: Oxidative workup with H2O2 oxidizes any resulting aldehydes to carboxylic acids, converting 2-methylbut-2-ene into acetone and acetic acid.

Question 4 of 10
Why does addition of iodine to alkenes not take place readily under normal conditions?

Solution: The addition of I2 is thermodynamically unfavorable because the carbon-iodine bond energy is low and the reaction is endothermic.

Question 5 of 10
Which reaction sequence converts propene into propan-1-ol?

Solution: Hydroboration-oxidation hydrates alkenes in an anti-Markovnikov, syn-addition manner to yield primary alcohols like propan-1-ol.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following species acts as the initial electrophile in bromination of ethene in CCl4?

Solution: Polarization of Br2 by the alkene pi cloud generates an electrophilic positive bromine species (Br+) that attacks the double bond.

Question 7 of 10
In the free-radical addition of HBr to propene, the propagating radical is:

Solution: Bromine radical adds to terminal carbon to generate a more stable secondary carbon radical (CH3-CH* -CH2Br).

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following compounds gives carbon dioxide and water upon complete combustion?

Solution: All hydrocarbons, including alkenes, undergo complete combustion in excess oxygen to yield carbon dioxide and water.

Question 9 of 10
What is the stereochemical outcome of catalytic hydrogenation of alkenes over palladium catalyst?

Solution: Catalytic hydrogenation delivers both hydrogen atoms from the catalyst surface to the same face of the alkene, resulting in stereospecific syn-addition.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following alkenes is most reactive towards electrophilic addition?

Solution: Electron-donating alkyl groups increase electron density in the pi bond and stabilize the intermediate carbocation, making 2-methylbut-2-ene the most reactive.
