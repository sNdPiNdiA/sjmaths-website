Organic Chemistry

Alkanes

Alkanes are saturated acyclic hydrocarbons possessing the general molecular formula CnH2n+2 with sp3 hybridized carbon atoms. They exhibit single covalent bonds characterized by tetrahedral geometry and restricted conformational rotation.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structure, Hybridization, and Isomerism

  • All carbon atoms in alkanes are sp3 hybridized, forming four equivalent σ-bonds with a tetrahedral bond angle of 109.5°.
  • Alkanes exhibit chain isomerism starting from butane (C4H10), where straight-chain and branched isomers possess different physical properties.
  • Rotation about the carbon-carbon single bond is largely unobstructed, generating infinite conformational isomers including staggered and eclipsed forms.

Methods of Preparation

  • Catalytic hydrogenation of alkenes or alkynes yields alkanes using finely divided metal catalysts such as Pt, Pd, or Ni at ambient or elevated temperatures.
  • Wurtz reaction couples two alkyl halides using sodium metal in dry ether to produce symmetrical higher alkanes, though it fails for odd-carbon asymmetric coupling.
  • Decarboxylation of carboxylic acids involves heating sodium or potassium salts with soda lime (NaOH + CaO) to eliminate carbon dioxide and yield lower alkanes.

Chemical Reactivity and Halogenation

  • Alkanes are chemically inert under normal conditions due to strong non-polar C-C and C-H bonds, earning the historic designation paraffins.
  • Free radical halogenation proceeds via chain initiation, propagation, and termination steps, with bromination being significantly more selective than chlorination.
  • Thermal cracking or pyrolysis at high temperatures in the absence of air breaks higher alkanes into smaller alkanes, alkenes, and hydrogen.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Wurtz reaction produces symmetrical alkanes efficiently.

Observation: Wurtz reaction fails to yield pure single alkanes when two different alkyl halides are mixed. — A statistical mixture of three different hydrocarbons (R-R, R-R', R'-R') is formed due to random radical coupling.

Exception First member of homologous series of alkenes and alkynes starts with 2 carbons.

Observation: The first member of the alkane series is methane (CH4) with 1 carbon atom. — The general formula CnH2n+2 allows n=1, whereas alkenes require n ≥ 2 due to the double bond requirement.

Exception Fluorination of alkanes is a standard preparative halogenation method.

Observation: Direct fluorination of alkanes is violently explosive and uncontrolled. — The C-F bond formation is extremely exothermic, causing cleavage of the carbon-carbon backbone.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Methane is also known as marsh gas and is the primary component of natural gas.
  • Liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) consists primarily of a mixture of propane and butane.
  • Octane number is a measure of antiknock quality of petrol, where 2,2,4-trimethylpentane (isooctane) is assigned a value of 100.
  • Heptane is assigned an octane rating of 0 due to its severe knocking tendency.
  • Aromatization of n-hexane over Cr2O3/Al2O3 at 773 K produces benzene.
  • Conformations of ethane include staggered and eclipsed forms, interconverted by rotation around the C-C σ-bond.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming Wurtz reaction can successfully synthesize methane; methane cannot be prepared by Wurtz coupling since minimum two carbons are required.
  • Confusing the stability order of free radicals (3° > 2° > 1°) with carbocation stability principles.
  • Forgetting that iodination of alkanes is reversible and requires an oxidizing agent like HIO3 or HNO3 to drive the reaction forward.
  • Mistaking conformational isomers for structural constitutional isomers; conformers rapidly interconvert at room temperature.

⚡ Quick Revision: Alkanes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Homologous Series and Nomenclature

Alkanes form a homologous series differing by a -CH2- unit, named according to IUPAC rules based on the longest carbon chain.

Preparation Strategies

Synthesized via hydrogenation of unsaturated hydrocarbons, reduction of alkyl halides, decarboxylation, and organometallic coupling reactions.

Conformational Analysis

Ethane and higher alkanes exhibit rotational isomerism resulting in distinct energy profiles corresponding to torsional and steric strains.

Chemical Inertness and Reactions

Due to high bond dissociation energies of C-C and C-H bonds, they undergo free radical substitution and combustion reactions rather than ionic additions.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Paraffins (Parum + affinis)
Historical name for alkanes signifying low chemical affinity.
Wurtz Reaction (2 R-X + 2 Na)
Coupling reaction of alkyl halides using sodium metal in dry ether.
Decarboxylation (R-COONa + NaOH/CaO)
Elimination of carbon dioxide from carboxylic acid salts.
Pyrolysis (Thermal Cracking)
Thermal decomposition of higher alkanes into smaller fragments.
Octane Number (Antiknock rating)
Scale measuring resistance to engine knocking in internal combustion engines.
Torsional Strain (Eclipsing strain)
Increased potential energy resulting from interaction of eclipsed bonds.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • General formula: CnH2n+2
  • Hybridization of carbon: sp3 with 109.5° bond angle
  • Reactivity order of free radical halogenation: 3° > 2° > 1°
  • Soda lime composition: NaOH and CaO in 3:1 ratio
  • Conformational stability: Staggered > Eclipsed

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons containing only single covalent bonds.
  • Physical properties like boiling point depend on molecular weight and branching.
  • Principal chemical reactions involve free radical substitution and combustion.
  • Conformational analysis explains spatial arrangements arising from sigma bond rotation.

⚖️ Key Differences

Chain Isomerism VS Conformational Isomerism

Chain isomers are distinct constitutional compounds requiring bond cleavage to interconvert; conformers interconvert freely via single bond rotation.

Question 1 of 18

What is the hybridization of carbon atoms in an alkane molecule?

Explanation: All carbon atoms in saturated alkanes form four single sigma bonds, corresponding to tetrahedral sp3 hybridization.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following alkanes cannot be prepared in good yield by the Wurtz reaction?

Explanation: Propane requires coupling of methyl and ethyl halides, which yields a mixture of ethane, propane, and butane.

Question 3 of 18

What is the primary product formed when sodium propanoate is heated with soda lime?

Explanation: Decarboxylation of sodium propanoate eliminates CO2 and reduces the carbon chain length by one, yielding ethane.

Question 4 of 18

Which conformer of ethane possesses maximum torsional strain?

Explanation: In the eclipsed conformation, C-H bonds directly overlap and face each other, maximizing torsional strain.

Question 5 of 18

What is the IUPAC name of the alkane with formula C5H12 that has the highest boiling point?

Explanation: Straight-chain pentane has the maximum surface area and stronger van der Waals forces, leading to the highest boiling point among its isomers.

Question 6 of 18

Which reagent is used for the aromatization of n-hexane to benzene?

Explanation: Passing n-hexane over heated Cr2O3, V2O5, or Mo2O3 supported on alumina cyclizes and dehydrogenates it to benzene.

Question 7 of 18

Halogenation of alkanes proceeds through which reactive intermediate?

Explanation: Thermal or photochemical homolytic cleavage of halogen molecules generates halogen free radicals that propagate substitution.

Question 8 of 18

Why is direct fluorination of alkanes not used for preparing alkyl fluorides?

Explanation: The extreme exothermicity of fluorine addition breaks carbon-carbon bonds, resulting in explosive decomposition.

Question 9 of 18

What is the IUPAC name of neo-pentane?

Explanation: Neo-pentane features a central carbon bonded to four methyl groups, designated as 2,2-dimethylpropane.

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following gases is the major constituent of Compressed Natural Gas (CNG)?

Explanation: Compressed Natural Gas consists predominantly of methane (CH4).

Question 11 of 18

In the free-radical chlorination of methane, which step represents chain propagation?

Explanation: Propagation involves a radical reacting with a neutral molecule to form a new radical and neutral product.

Question 12 of 18

What type of isomerism is exhibited by 2-methylpropane and butane?

Explanation: They possess the same molecular formula C4H10 but differ in carbon skeleton branching, defining chain isomerism.

Question 13 of 18

What is the compound assigned an octane number of zero?

Explanation: n-Heptane causes severe knocking and is assigned an octane rating of 0.

Question 14 of 18

Kolbe electrolytic decarboxylation of potassium acetate yields which gas at the anode?

Explanation: Kolbe electrolysis of acetate produces ethane and carbon dioxide at the anode, while hydrogen gas is evolved at the cathode.

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following has the lowest boiling point?

Explanation: Neo-pentane is the most compact and spherical isomer, resulting in minimal surface contact and the lowest boiling point.

Question 16 of 18

What is the general formula for cycloalkanes?

Explanation: Cycloalkanes contain one ring, introducing one degree of unsaturation and matching the formula CnH2n.

Question 17 of 18

Which alkyl halide reacts fastest with sodium in dry ether during a Wurtz reaction?

Explanation: Iodides have the weakest C-I bond strength among alkyl halides, making them the most reactive in nucleophilic and radical coupling.

Question 18 of 18

What is the bond length of a C-C single bond in alkanes?

Explanation: The standard C-C sp3-sp3 single bond length in alkanes is 1.54 Å.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following alkanes can be synthesized in high purity via Wurtz reaction without forming mixed byproducts?

Solution: Ethane is formed by coupling two methyl radicals from methyl chloride, ensuring a single pure symmetrical product.

Question 2 of 10
What is the principal organic product formed during the Kolbe electrolysis of potassium succinate?

Solution: Electrolysis of potassium succinate (a salt of a dicarboxylic acid) eliminates two carboxylate groups as CO2, forming ethene.

Question 3 of 10
In the free radical bromination of isobutane, what is the major monobromo product?

Solution: Bromination is extremely selective for tertiary hydrogen atoms, yielding 2-bromo-2-methylpropane as the principal product.

Question 4 of 10
What is the coordination number and geometry of each carbon atom in methane?

Solution: Methane carbon forms four sigma bonds arranged in a tetrahedral geometry around the central atom.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following exhibits the highest boiling point?

Solution: Boiling point increases regularly with molecular weight and molecular surface area in straight-chain alkanes; n-heptane has the highest molecular mass among the choices.

Question 6 of 10
Which catalyst is employed for the industrial catalytic cracking of higher petroleum fractions?

Solution: Silica-alumina (SiO2-Al2O3) acid catalysts are widely used in catalytic cracking processes.

Question 7 of 10
What volume of oxygen gas is required for the complete combustion of 1 mole of propane at STP?

Solution: Combustion equation: C3H8 + 5 O2 → 3 CO2 + 4 H2O. One mole of propane requires 5 moles of oxygen, corresponding to 5 × 22.4 L = 112 L at STP.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following has a zero dipole moment?

Solution: 2,2-Dimethylpropane is symmetrically tetrahedral, causing bond dipole moments to completely cancel out, resulting in a net zero dipole moment.

Question 9 of 10
The conversion of n-heptane to toluene by heating with Cr2O3/Al2O3 is an example of:

Solution: Cyclization and dehydrogenation of alkanes containing six or more carbons to form aromatic rings is termed aromatization or reforming.

Question 10 of 10
How many chain isomers exist for the molecular formula C5H12?

Solution: Pentane has three structural isomers: n-pentane, isopentane (2-methylbutane), and neo-pentane (2,2-dimethylpropane).
