Preparation and Properties of Alkanes

Alkanes are saturated aliphatic hydrocarbons characterized by sp3 hybridized carbon atoms linked exclusively by single covalent sigma bonds. Their general molecular formula is CnH2n+2, representing the maximum possible hydrogen content for a given carbon skeleton.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthesis Methods of Alkanes

  • Catalytic hydrogenation converts alkenes and alkynes to alkanes using finely divided Ni, Pt, or Pd catalysts via syn addition.
  • Wurtz reaction couples two alkyl halides using sodium metal in dry ether to synthesize symmetrical higher alkanes.
  • Decarboxylation of carboxylic acids involves heating sodium salts of fatty acids with soda lime (NaOH + CaO), releasing carbon dioxide.
  • Clemmensen reduction and Wolff-Kishner reduction selectively reduce aldehyde and ketone carbonyl groups to methylene (-CH2-) groups.

Physical Properties and Conformational Analysis

  • Boiling point increases regularly with increasing molecular weight due to stronger London dispersion forces in larger molecules.
  • Branched-chain isomers possess lower boiling points than straight-chain isomers because branching decreases the effective surface area of contact.
  • Melting point depends on crystal packing efficiency; symmetrical molecules exhibit higher melting points due to favorable lattice energy.
  • Conformational isomers of ethane exhibit varying potential energy profiles, with the staggered conformation being most stable and the eclipsed conformation possessing maximum torsional strain.

Chemical Reactions and Halogenation Mechanism

  • Alkanes undergo free radical substitution reactions with halogens in the presence of ultraviolet light or high temperature.
  • The reactivity order of halogens is F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2, with iodination being reversible unless an oxidizing agent like HIO3 is added.
  • Nitration of higher alkanes occurs via free radical mechanisms at high temperatures (400-500°C) with nitric acid vapor, yielding nitroalkane mixtures.
  • Catalytic oxidation using specialized catalysts yields alcohols, aldehydes, or carboxylic acids depending on the chosen metal oxide catalyst and reaction conditions.

Units: R = alkyl group, X = halogen (Cl, Br, I)

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Wurtz reaction yields symmetrical alkanes with an even number of carbon atoms.

Observation: Mixtures of two different alkyl halides yield a statistical mixture containing odd carbon alkanes. — Free radical coupling is indiscriminate, producing cross-coupled and homo-coupled products that are difficult to separate.

Exception Fluorination of alkanes is slow and controllable.

Observation: Fluorination is violently explosive and uncontrollable under standard laboratory conditions. — The reaction is extremely exothermic with a very low activation energy barrier.

Exception Iodination of alkanes proceeds readily to form iodoalkanes.

Observation: Iodination is a reversible equilibrium process that strongly favors reactants. — Hydrogen iodide (HI) formed as a byproduct is a strong reducing agent that reduces iodoalkanes back to alkanes.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Wurtz Odd Carbon Rule
R-X + 2Na + X-R' → R-R + R-R' + R'-R'

Application: Remember that mixing two different alkyl halides in Wurtz synthesis always gives a three-component statistical mixture.

Combustion Oxygen Stoichiometry
O2 required = (3n + 1) / 2

Application: Quickly balance any alkane complete combustion equation by plugging the carbon number 'n' into this stoichiometric numerator.

Free Radical Halogenation Selectivity
3° C-H > 2° C-H > 1° C-H

Application: Directly predict the major monohalogenation product by replacing the hydrogen on the most substituted carbon atom.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Methane is known as marsh gas and is the primary component of natural gas.
  • Decarboxylation using soda lime decreases the carbon chain length by one carbon atom.
  • Kolbe electrolytic decarboxylation of potassium acetate yields ethane at the anode.
  • Conformational isomers are interconverted exclusively by rotation around carbon-carbon single sigma bonds.
  • Staggered conformation of ethane possesses zero torsional strain.
  • Chlorination of methane is a free radical substitution reaction proceeding via chain initiation, propagation, and termination steps.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming Wurtz reaction can cleanly prepare pure odd-carbon alkanes from single mixed reactants.
  • Confusing the staggered and eclipsed conformations regarding stability and torsional strain.
  • Forgetting that iodination requires an oxidizing agent to remove hydroiodic acid.
  • Mistaking Corey-House synthesis for Wurtz reaction when attempting to synthesize unsymmetrical alkanes with good yields.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation and Properties of Alkanes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

General Methods of Preparation

Alkanes are prepared via reduction of alkyl halides, hydrogenation of unsaturated hydrocarbons, decarboxylation of carboxylic acid salts, and Wurtz coupling.

Physical Characteristics

Boiling points increase with molecular mass and decrease with molecular branching due to alterations in intermolecular van der Waals surface contact.

Halogenation Mechanism

Free radical halogenation follows an exothermic chain propagation sequence exhibiting selectivity trends ordered as tertiary > secondary > primary.

Conformational Analysis

Rotation about carbon-carbon sigma bonds generates conformational isomers, with staggered rotamers representing energy minima.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Alkanes (CnH2n+2)
Saturated open-chain hydrocarbons containing only carbon-carbon single bonds.
Wurtz Reaction (R-X + 2Na)
Coupling of alkyl halides using sodium metal in dry ether to form higher alkanes.
Soda Lime (NaOH + CaO)
Reagent mixture used for decarboxylation of carboxylic acid salts.
Conformation (Rotamer)
Different spatial arrangements of atoms resulting from bond rotation.
Torsional Strain (Eclipsing strain)
Repulsive interaction between electron clouds of bonds on adjacent carbon atoms.
Kolbe Electrolysis (Anodic oxidation)
Electrolytic decarboxylation of carboxylate salts yielding symmetrical alkanes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • General formula: CnH2n+2
  • Reactivity order in halogenation: F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2
  • Stability of free radicals: 3° > 2° > 1°
  • Soda lime consists of NaOH and CaO in a 3:1 ratio
  • Staggered conformation is energetically more stable than eclipsed conformation

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkanes are chemically inert under normal conditions, earning the historical designation paraffin.
  • Synthesis methods include reduction, hydrogenation, organometallic coupling, and decarboxylation.
  • Halogenation proceeds via free radical mechanisms with predictable regioselectivity.
  • Physical properties correlate directly with molecular weight and branching.

⚖️ Key Differences

Clemmensen Reduction VS Wolff-Kishner Reduction

Clemmensen uses zinc-amalgam and concentrated HCl (acidic), whereas Wolff-Kishner uses hydrazine and base (basic).

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following reagents is used in the Clemmensen reduction of carbonyl compounds to alkanes?

Explanation: Clemmensen reduction specifically employs zinc amalgam and concentrated hydrochloric acid to reduce aldehydes and ketones to alkanes.

Question 2 of 19

What is the primary product formed during the decarboxylation of sodium propionate with soda lime?

Explanation: Decarboxylation removes one molecule of CO2 as Na2CO3, converting a 3-carbon carboxylate salt into a 2-carbon alkane (ethane).

Question 3 of 19

Which conformation of ethane has maximum torsional strain?

Explanation: The eclipsed conformation places C-H bonds directly aligned with each other, maximizing electron cloud repulsion and torsional strain.

Question 4 of 19

Wurtz reaction is best suited for the preparation of:

Explanation: Coupling identical alkyl halide molecules yields symmetrical alkanes containing an even number of carbon atoms.

Question 5 of 19

What is the role of CaO in soda lime used for decarboxylation?

Explanation: Calcium oxide does not react directly but keeps sodium hydroxide dry, high-melting, and easy to handle in powdered form.

Question 6 of 19

In free radical chlorination of propane, which free radical intermediate is more stable?

Explanation: Propane has both primary and secondary C-H bonds; abstraction of secondary hydrogen yields a more stable secondary free radical.

Question 7 of 19

Which halogen undergoes substitution with alkanes with explosive violence?

Explanation: Fluorination is violently exothermic due to the low bond dissociation energy of F2 and high bond energy of H-F.

Question 8 of 19

Kolbe electrolysis of potassium succinate yields:

Explanation: Electrolysis of a dicarboxylic acid salt like succinate yields ethene along with carbon dioxide at the anode.

Question 9 of 19

The boiling point of isomeric pentanes follows the order:

Explanation: Straight-chain n-pentane has maximum surface area and intermolecular forces, giving it the highest boiling point compared to branched isomers.

Question 10 of 19

Which catalyst is used in the catalytic hydrogenation of alkenes to alkanes at room temperature?

Explanation: Platinum and palladium catalyze hydrogenation effectively at room temperature, whereas nickel requires elevated temperatures.

Question 11 of 19

What is the general formula of cycloalkanes?

Explanation: Cycloalkanes possess one ring constraint, removing two hydrogen atoms compared to open-chain alkanes, resulting in CnH2n.

Question 12 of 19

Which of the following compounds has the highest melting point?

Explanation: Neopentane is highly symmetrical and packs tightly into a crystal lattice, giving it an abnormally high melting point.

Question 13 of 19

In the mechanism of free radical halogenation, which step consumes a radical and produces a non-radical molecule?

Explanation: Chain termination involves the combination of two free radicals to form a covalent molecule, ending the chain cycle.

Question 14 of 19

What byproduct is formed when alkyl halides react with metallic sodium in dry ether?

Explanation: The Wurtz reaction produces sodium halide (NaX) as an inorganic byproduct alongside the coupled alkane.

Question 15 of 19

Which reducing agent is used in the Wolff-Kishner reduction?

Explanation: Wolff-Kishner reduction uses hydrazine (NH2NH2) followed by a strong base like potassium hydroxide in a high-boiling alcohol solvent.

Question 16 of 19

Why is iodination of alkanes carried out in the presence of iodic acid (HIO3)?

Explanation: Iodic acid consumes the byproduct hydroiodic acid (HI) by oxidizing it back to iodine, driving the equilibrium forward.

Question 17 of 19

What is the hybridization of carbon atoms in alkanes?

Explanation: All carbon atoms in saturated alkanes form four single sigma bonds and are sp3 hybridized with tetrahedral geometry.

Question 18 of 19

Which of the following alkanes cannot be prepared in good yield by Wurtz reaction?

Explanation: Preparing propane via Wurtz reaction requires methyl and ethyl halides, producing a mixture of ethane, propane, and butane.

Question 19 of 19

Pyrolysis of higher alkanes to yield lower alkanes and alkenes is known as:

Explanation: Thermal cracking (pyrolysis) breaks down high-molecular-weight hydrocarbons into smaller alkanes and alkenes at high temperatures.

Question 1 of 10
What is the major product formed when 2-methylpropane is subjected to monochlorination in the presence of sunlight?

Solution: Monochlorination proceeds via abstraction of the tertiary hydrogen atom, forming 2-chloro-2-methylpropane as the major product.

Question 2 of 10
Which alkane cannot be synthesized by Wurtz reaction?

Solution: Propane requires mixing methyl and ethyl halides, leading to an inseparable mixture of ethane, propane, and butane.

Question 3 of 10
In alkane halogenation, the relative reactivity of primary, secondary, and tertiary hydrogen atoms toward chlorination is approximately:

Solution: Under standard free radical chlorination conditions, the relative reactivity per hydrogen for 1° : 2° : 3° is approximately 1 : 3.8 : 5.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following compounds has a zero dipole moment?

Solution: 2,2-dimethylpropane (neopentane) is tetrahedral and symmetrical, causing individual bond dipole moments to cancel out entirely.

Question 5 of 10
The C-C bond length in ethane is:

Solution: The carbon-carbon single bond length in sp3 hybridized alkanes such as ethane is 1.54 Å.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following reactions is used to decrease the length of a carbon chain?

Solution: Soda lime decarboxylation eliminates one carbon atom as carbon dioxide, reducing the chain length.

Question 7 of 10
During the combustion of 1 mole of butane, how many moles of water are produced?

Solution: The formula for complete combustion of C4H10 yields (n+1) moles of H2O, where n = 4, giving 5 moles.

Question 8 of 10
Which conformation of butane is the most stable?

Solution: The anti conformation places bulky methyl groups 180 degrees apart, minimizing steric hindrance and maximizing stability.

Question 9 of 10
What is formed when ethyl iodide is reduced with Zn and HCl?

Solution: Reduction of an alkyl halide with zinc and hydrochloric acid replaces the halogen atom with hydrogen, yielding an alkane (ethane).

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following methods yields alkanes with an odd number of carbon atoms?

Solution: Decarboxylation of an even-carbon sodium carboxylate removes one carbon, yielding an alkane with an odd number of carbon atoms.
