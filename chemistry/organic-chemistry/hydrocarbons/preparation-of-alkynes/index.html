Preparation of Alkynes

Alkynes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing a carbon-carbon triple bond with the general formula CnH2n-2. They are synthesized via elimination reactions of dihalides or alkylation of metal acetylides.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Dehydrohalogenation of Dihalides

  • Treatment of vicinal dihalides with alcoholic KOH yields a vinylic halide, which requires stronger bases like NaNH2 to form an alkyne.
  • Geminal dihalides undergo two consecutive dehydrohalogenation steps using sodamide in liquid ammonia to generate the triple bond.
  • Terminal alkynes formed via these methods react with NaNH2 to form sodium acetylides, requiring subsequent protonation.

Dehalogenation of Tetrahalides

  • Heating vicinal tetrahalides with reactive metals such as zinc dust in anhydrous alcohol results in the elimination of four halogen atoms.
  • The reaction proceeds via the intermediate formation of dihalides, eventually yielding the corresponding alkyne.
  • This method is primarily utilized for the laboratory purification or synthesis of alkynes from corresponding alkenes via halogenation.

Acetide Alkylation and C-C Bond Formation

  • Sodium acetylide reacts readily with primary alkyl halides via an SN2 mechanism to extend the carbon chain and form higher alkynes.
  • Secondary and tertiary alkyl halides fail in this synthesis due to competing E2 elimination pathways favored under strongly basic conditions.
  • Acetylene itself can be prepared industrially by the hydrolysis of calcium carbide (CaC2).
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Dehydrohalogenation of Geminal Dihalides
R-CH2-CH(X)2 + 2 NaNH2 → R-C≡CH + 2 NaX + 2 NH3
Conditions: Sodamide in liquid ammonia at elevated temperatures
Units: Reaction yields alkyne, sodium halide, and ammonia
Kolbe's Electrolytic Synthesis
R-CH=C(COOK)-COOK + 2 H2O → R-C≡CH + 2 CO2 + H2 + 2 KOH
Conditions: Electrolysis of aqueous potassium maleate or fumarate salts
Units: Alkyne evolved at anode along with carbon dioxide
Alkylation of Sodium Acetylide
R-C≡C- Na+ + R'-X → R-C≡C-R' + NaX
Conditions: Primary alkyl halide in liquid ammonia
Units: Higher substituted alkyne and sodium halide
Dehydrohalogenation of Vicinal Dihalides
R-CH(X)-CH2-X + 2 KOH(alc) → R-C≡CH + 2 KX + 2 H2O
Conditions: Alcoholic KOH followed by strong base like NaNH2 for terminal alkynes
Units: Reaction yields alkyne, potassium halide, and water
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Base-promoted dehydrohalogenation yields internal and terminal alkynes equally.

Observation: Terminal alkynes require an excess of strong base like NaNH2. — The acidic proton of the terminal alkyne is abstracted by the base, forming a stable sodium acetylide salt that shifts the equilibrium.

Exception Alkylation of metal acetylides works for all alkyl halides.

Observation: Secondary and tertiary alkyl halides yield alkenes instead of alkynes. — Strongly basic acetylide anions act as Brønsted-Lowry bases rather than nucleophiles with hindered substrates.

Exception Dehalogenation of tetrahalides uses standard reducing agents.

Observation: Zinc dust in boiling alcohol is specifically required. — Mild reducing agents are insufficient to cleave all four carbon-halogen bonds simultaneously.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
The Base Ladder Rule
Alc. KOH (weak) → NaNH2 (strong)

Application: Use alc. KOH for the first HX elimination and NaNH2 for the stubborn second vinylic HX elimination.

Chain Elongation Check
R-C≡C- + R'-X → R-C≡C-R'

Application: Always ensure the alkyl halide (R'-X) is primary; secondary or tertiary alkyl halides give elimination products.

Calcium Carbide Hydrolysis
CaC2 + 2 H2O → Ca(OH)2 + C2H2

Application: Direct source for preparing acetylene (ethyne) industrially without halogen intermediates.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Calcium carbide reacts with water to produce acetylene gas.
  • Vicinal dihalides undergo double elimination with alcoholic KOH and NaNH2 to form alkynes.
  • Sodium acetylide undergoes SN2 alkylation exclusively with primary alkyl halides.
  • Secondary and tertiary alkyl halides yield alkenes instead of alkynes when treated with sodium acetylide.
  • Dehalogenation of 1,1,2,2-tetrabromoethane with zinc dust yields ethyne.
  • Terminal alkynes possess acidic protons that react with strong bases like NaNH2.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Using secondary or tertiary alkyl halides for acetylide alkylation expecting substitution products.
  • Forgetting that terminal alkynes require an extra equivalent of base because of proton transfer.
  • Confusing vicinal and geminal dihalide structures during dehydrohalogenation equations.
  • Assuming mild metals can perform dehalogenation of tetrahalides efficiently.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation of Alkynes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Elimination Reactions

Alkynes are synthesized by eliminating two molecules of hydrogen halide from dihalides using strong bases.

Carbon-Carbon Coupling

Metal acetylides act as strong nucleophiles in SN2 attacks on primary alkyl halides to build larger carbon skeletons.

Industrial Preparation

Ethyne is produced commercially via the hydrolysis of calcium carbide derived from lime and coke at high temperatures.

Dehalogenation Pathways

Tetrahalides react with active metals like zinc to eliminate four halogen atoms and form a triple bond.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Vicinal Dihalide (R-CH(X)-CH2X)
Compound with halogens on adjacent carbons.
Geminal Dihalide (R-CH2-CH(X)2)
Compound with two halogens on the same carbon.
Sodium Acetylide (RC≡CNa)
Strongly nucleophilic acetylide salt used for chain elongation.
Calcium Carbide (CaC2)
Industrial precursor yielding ethyne upon hydrolysis.
Dehydrohalogenation (-HX Elimination)
Removal of hydrogen and halogen atoms to form unsaturation.
SN2 Reaction (Bimolecular Nucleophilic Substitution)
Conconcerted pathway for alkylating metal acetylides.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Alc. KOH alone only converts vicinal dihalides to vinylic halides; NaNH2 is needed for alkynes.
  • Primary alkyl halides yield substituted alkynes; 2° and 3° alkyl halides give alkenes.
  • CaC2 + 2 H2O → HC≡CH + Ca(OH)2
  • Tetrahalides + Zn dust → Alkyne + 2 ZnX2
  • Terminal alkynes are acidic due to sp hybridization of carbon.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkynes are prepared mainly through elimination and alkylation reactions.
  • Strong bases like NaNH2 are essential for second-step eliminations.
  • Chain elongation is achieved via metal acetylide chemistry.
  • Industrial scale production relies on calcium carbide hydrolysis.

⚖️ Key Differences

Vicinal Dihalide Elimination VS Geminal Dihalide Elimination

Vicinal requires two distinct steps (alc. KOH then NaNH2), whereas geminal dihalides undergo double elimination directly with strong NaNH2.

Question 1 of 18

Which reagent is required to convert 1,2-dibromopropane into propyne?

Explanation: Conversion of a vicinal dibromide to an alkyne requires alcoholic KOH for the first elimination and sodamide (NaNH2) for the second vinylic elimination.

Question 2 of 18

What is the product of the reaction between sodium acetylide and 2-bromopropane?

Explanation: Secondary alkyl halides undergo E2 elimination instead of SN2 substitution when treated with strongly basic sodium acetylide, yielding alkenes.

Question 3 of 18

Calcium carbide upon reaction with water gives:

Explanation: Hydrolysis of calcium carbide (CaC2) yields ethyne (acetylene) gas and calcium hydroxide.

Question 4 of 18

Which compound is formed when 1,1,2,2-tetrabromobutane is heated with zinc dust?

Explanation: Dehalogenation of 1,1,2,2-tetrabromobutane with zinc dust eliminates all four bromine atoms to form 2-butyne.

Question 5 of 18

Why is NaNH2 preferred over alcoholic KOH for the elimination of halogen from vinylic halides?

Explanation: Vinylic halides are unreactive toward alcoholic KOH because the carbon-halogen bond has partial double-bond character; a stronger base like NaNH2 is required.

Question 6 of 18

Which of the following alkyl halides will successfully undergo alkylation with sodium acetylide?

Explanation: Only primary alkyl halides like ethyl chloride undergo SN2 substitution successfully with sodium acetylide without major elimination.

Question 7 of 18

The hybridization of carbon atoms in ethyne is:

Explanation: Ethyne contains a triple bond, meaning each carbon atom is sp hybridized with a linear geometry.

Question 8 of 18

Which of the following reactions is used for chain lengthening in organic synthesis?

Explanation: Alkylation of metal acetylides forms new carbon-carbon bonds, effectively lengthening the carbon chain.

Question 9 of 18

In the preparation of alkynes from geminal dihalides, the intermediate or final base utilized is:

Explanation: Sodamide (NaNH2) in liquid ammonia is strong enough to abstract protons and drive off two molecules of HX from geminal dihalides.

Question 10 of 18

Kolbe's electrolysis of potassium salt of fumaric acid yields:

Explanation: Electrolysis of potassium maleate or fumarate results in the formation of ethyne at the anode along with carbon dioxide.

Question 11 of 18

What is the role of alcohol in alcoholic KOH during dehydrohalogenation?

Explanation: Alcohol acts as a medium and provides alkoxide ions which facilitate base-promoted elimination.

Question 12 of 18

Which alkyne cannot be prepared by the alkylation of sodium acetylide?

Explanation: 2-Butyne is an internal alkyne and cannot be prepared by direct mono-alkylation of terminal sodium acetylide.

Question 13 of 18

The C≡C bond length in ethyne is approximately:

Explanation: Due to increased s-character and triple bonding, the carbon-carbon triple bond length in ethyne is 1.20 Å.

Question 14 of 18

Which reagent converts 1,1-dibromoethane to acetylene?

Explanation: Geminal dihalides like 1,1-dibromoethane require sodamide in liquid ammonia to undergo double elimination to ethyne.

Question 15 of 18

Terminal alkynes can be distinguished from internal alkynes using:

Explanation: Ammoniacal silver nitrate reacts with terminal alkynes to form insoluble white/silver acetylide precipitates.

Question 16 of 18

During the preparation of alkynes from tetrahalides, the metal used is:

Explanation: Zinc dust is standard for dehalogenating vicinal tetrahalides to yield alkynes.

Question 17 of 18

Which of the following processes does NOT produce an alkyne?

Explanation: Hydrogenation of ethene produces ethane, which is an alkane, not an alkyne.

Question 18 of 18

What byproduct is formed during the dehydrohalogenation of dihalides using NaNH2?

Explanation: NaNH2 reacts with released HX to form sodium halide and ammonia gas.

Question 1 of 10
Which method is best suited for preparing 3-hexyne from 1-butyne?

Solution: Converting 1-butyne to its sodium salt and reacting it with ethyl chloride extends the chain via SN2 substitution to form 3-hexyne.

Question 2 of 10
What happens when 1,1-dichlorobutane is treated with excess sodamide?

Solution: Geminal dihalides undergo double dehydrohalogenation with excess NaNH2 to form terminal alkynes like 1-butyne.

Question 3 of 10
Which reaction sequence correctly converts ethene into ethyne?

Solution: Ethene is brominated to 1,2-dibromoethane (vicinal dihalide), which is then dehydrohalogenated using NaNH2 to form ethyne.

Question 4 of 10
In the Kolbe's electrolysis of potassium maleate, what gas is liberated at the cathode?

Solution: Reduction of water at the cathode during electrolysis produces hydrogen gas.

Question 5 of 10
Why do secondary and tertiary alkyl halides fail to yield alkynes with sodium acetylide?

Solution: Steric hindrance in secondary and tertiary halides favors the elimination pathway (E2) over the SN2 substitution pathway.

Question 6 of 10
What is the geometry around each carbon atom in an alkyne molecule?

Solution: Triple-bonded carbons are sp hybridized, imparting a linear geometry with a 180° bond angle.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following metal acetylides is explosive when dry?

Solution: Silver and copper acetylides are shock-sensitive and explosive when dry.

Question 8 of 10
The starting material for preparing ethyne via the industrial route is:

Solution: Limestone (CaCO3) is heated to give quicklime, which reacts with coke to form calcium carbide; its hydrolysis yields ethyne.

Question 9 of 10
How many moles of NaNH2 are required for complete dehydrohalogenation of one mole of a vicinal dibromide to an alkyne?

Solution: Two moles of base are required to eliminate two moles of HX per mole of dihalide.

Question 10 of 10
Which test confirms the presence of a terminal alkyne?

Solution: Ammoniacal silver nitrate (Tollens' reagent) reacts with terminal alkynes to form silver acetylide precipitates.
