Preparation and Properties of Alkynes

Alkynes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-carbon triple bond with a general formula of CnH2n-2. They undergo characteristic electrophilic addition, nucleophilic addition, and metal substitution reactions due to their sp-hybridized carbon atoms.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthesis Methods of Alkynes

  • Dehydrohalogenation of vicinal or geminal dihalides using molten alcoholic KOH followed by sodamide (NaNH2) yields terminal or internal alkynes.
  • Action of water on calcium carbide (CaC2) produces acetylene industrially via hydrolysis at room temperature.
  • Alkylation of sodium acetylides with primary alkyl halides via SN2 mechanism allows step-up synthesis to higher alkynes: R-C≡C- Na+ + R'-X → R-C≡C-R'.

Structure and Acidity of Terminal Alkynes

  • The carbon-carbon triple bond consists of one σ-bond and two π-bonds formed by overlapping sp-hybridized atomic orbitals.
  • Terminal alkynes exhibit appreciable acidity (pKa ≈ 25) because the 50% s-character of sp orbitals holds electrons closer to the nucleus.
  • Terminal alkynes react with tollens' reagent [Ag(NH3)2]+ OH- and cuprous chloride [Cu(NH3)2]+ to form insoluble metal acetylide precipitates.

Chemical Reactivity and Addition Reactions

  • Electrophilic addition across the triple bond follows Markovnikov's rule, proceeding through a resonance-stabilized vinyl cation intermediate.
  • Hydration of alkynes catalyzed by HgSO4 and dilute H2SO4 yields enols which rapidly tautomerize to carbonyl compounds (ketones or acetaldehyde).
  • Ozonolysis of alkynes followed by reductive workup with zinc and water yields α-diketones or carboxylic acids depending on substitution.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Addition of HX to alkynes follows Markovnikov's rule strictly

Observation: Addition of HBr to terminal alkynes in the presence of peroxides yields anti-Markovnikov vinylic bromides — Peroxides shift the mechanism from ionic electrophilic addition to a free radical chain mechanism.

Exception Alkynes undergo electrophilic addition more rapidly than alkenes due to high electron density

Observation: Hydration and halogenation of alkynes are significantly slower than corresponding alkenes — The vinyl cation intermediate formed during electrophilic addition to alkynes is less stable than the corresponding alkyl carbocation.

Exception Internal alkynes undergo catalytic hydrogenation to alkanes indiscriminately

Observation: Use of Lindlar's catalyst stops hydrogenation specifically at the cis-alkene stage — Poisoning the palladium surface with quinoline or lead acetate deactivates the catalyst toward further alkene reduction.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Lindlar vs Birch Reduction Mnemonic
LIN-CIS, BIR-TRANS

Application: Lindlar catalyst gives cis-alkenes; Birch reduction (Na/liquid NH3) gives trans-alkenes.

Hydration Product Shortcut
Acetylene → Aldehyde; Higher Alkynes → Ketones

Application: Directly predict the carbonyl product: ethyne gives ethanal (acetaldehyde), while propyne or higher alkynes give methyl ketones.

Silver/Copper Acetylide Test Rule
Terminal = Test Positive

Application: Only terminal alkynes have acidic protons to react with Ag(I) or Cu(I) ammonia complexes; internal alkynes give negative tests.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Ethyne (acetylene) molecule is linear with a C≡C bond length of 120 pm and a C-H bond length of 106 pm.
  • Acidity order for hydrocarbons: Ethyne > Ethene > Ethane due to increasing s-character of carbon hybrid orbitals.
  • Cuprous acetylide is red, whereas silver acetylide is white; both are explosive when dry.
  • Ozonolysis of 2-butyne yields biacetyl (2,3-butanedione) upon reductive workup.
  • Polymerization of acetylene at high temperature through copper tube gives benzene.
  • Terminal alkynes react with Grignard reagents to liberate corresponding alkanes and form alkynylmagnesium halides (R-C≡C-MgX).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the hydration products: assuming propyne gives propionaldehyde instead of acetone.
  • Forgetting that internal alkynes do not react with ammoniacal silver nitrate or cuprous chloride solutions.
  • Assuming Birch reduction produces cis-alkenes instead of thermodynamically stable trans-alkenes.
  • Writing incorrect stoichiometry for complete catalytic hydrogenation where 2 moles of H2 are required per mole of alkyne to reach the alkane.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation and Properties of Alkynes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Preparation via Elimination

Vicinal or geminal dihalides undergo double dehydrohalogenation with strong bases like sodamide to form carbon-carbon triple bonds.

Acidity of Terminal Alkynes

Due to 50% s-character, terminal alkynes exhibit acidic behavior, reacting with strong bases and transition metal ions to form stable acetylides.

Stereoselective Reductions

Catalytic hydrogenation with Lindlar catalyst yields cis-alkenes, whereas dissolving metal reduction with sodium in liquid ammonia yields trans-alkenes.

Electrophilic Addition and Tautomerism

Addition of water across the triple bond yields unstable enols that instantly tautomerize into stable carbonyl compounds.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Ethyne (C2H2)
Simplest alkyne, commonly known as acetylene.
Lindlar Catalyst (Pd/BaSO4 + Quinoline)
Heterogeneous catalyst used for cis-selective alkyne hydrogenation.
Birch Reduction (Na + Liquid NH3)
Dissolving metal reduction method yielding trans-alkenes.
Tautomerism (Keto-Enol Equilibrium)
Rapid interconversion between constitutional isomers via proton shift.
Metal Acetylide (R-C≡C-M)
Organometallic derivative formed by replacing terminal alkyne proton.
Vicinal Dihalide (R-CH(X)-CH2X)
Compound with halogen atoms on adjacent carbon atoms.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Acetylene gives benzene on cyclic trimerization over red-hot iron tube at 873 K.
  • Terminal alkynes give white precipitate with [Ag(NH3)2]+.
  • Terminal alkynes give red precipitate with [Cu(NH3)2]+.
  • Hydration of propyne yields acetone (propan-2-one).
  • Hydration of ethyne yields ethanal (acetaldehyde).

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkynes contain sp-hybridized carbons with linear geometry and high electron density.
  • Preparation involves elimination of HX from dihalides or alkylation of metal acetylides.
  • Chemical properties are dominated by electrophilic additions and terminal C-H acidity.
  • Stereoselective reductions permit controlled synthesis of cis- and trans-alkenes.

⚖️ Key Differences

Terminal Alkyne VS Internal Alkyne

Terminal alkynes possess acidic protons and react with Tollen's/CuCl reagents, whereas internal alkynes do not.

What hybridization describes the carbon atoms sharing the triple bond in alkynes?

Explanation: Cyclic trimerization of three molecules of acetylene over hot iron tube yields benzene.

Which catalyst is used in Lindlar's reduction?

Explanation: Lindlar catalyst consists of finely divided palladium on calcium carbonate or barium sulfate poisoned with quinoline.

Question 7 of 18

What is the general formula for non-cyclic alkynes?

Explanation: Acyclic alkynes containing one triple bond conform to the general molecular formula CnH2n-2.

Question 8 of 18

Terminal alkynes are acidic because:

Explanation: The 50% s-character in sp-hybridized orbitals pulls bonding electrons closer to carbon, enhancing proton dissociation.

Question 9 of 18

Which reagent is used for dehydrohalogenation of vicinal dihalides to form alkynes?

Explanation: Strong base such as sodamide (NaNH2) in liquid ammonia achieves double dehydrohalogenation.

Question 10 of 18

Ozonolysis of 2-butyne followed by Zn/H2O workup produces:

Explanation: Cleavage of the carbon-carbon triple bond in 2-butyne by ozone gives two molecules of a diketone, biacetyl.

Question 11 of 18

What is the IUPAC name of acetylene?

Explanation: Acetylene is the trivial name; its IUPAC systematic name is ethyne.

Question 12 of 18

Which test distinguishes between 1-butyne and 2-butyne?

Explanation: Ammoniacal silver nitrate reacts only with terminal alkynes (1-butyne) to give a white precipitate.

Question 13 of 18

How many σ and π bonds are present in a carbon-carbon triple bond?

Explanation: A triple bond consists of one strong sigma bond and two orthogonal pi bonds.

Question 14 of 18

Action of water on calcium carbide yields:

Explanation: Hydrolysis of calcium carbide (CaC2) is a classic industrial route for producing acetylene.

Question 15 of 18

When 1-butyne reacts with excess HBr, the major product is:

Explanation: Electrophilic addition of excess HBr across the triple bond follows Markovnikov's rule twice, yielding 2,2-dibromobutane (geminal dihalide).

Question 16 of 18

Why is terminal alkyne proton acidic?

Explanation: The resulting carbanion (acetylide ion) has its negative charge in an orbital with high s-character, making it relatively stable.

Question 17 of 18

Which of the following compounds has the shortest C≡C bond length?

Explanation: Ethyne has the least steric crowding and highest % s-character effect, resulting in the shortest C≡C bond length (120 pm).

Question 18 of 18

What happens when ethyne is passed through red-hot copper tube?

Explanation: Passing ethyne over red-hot copper or iron tubes results in cyclic trimerization to form benzene.
