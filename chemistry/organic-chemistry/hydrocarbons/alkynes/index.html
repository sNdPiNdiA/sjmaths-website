Organic Chemistry

Alkynes

Alkynes are unsaturated hydrocarbons characterized by a carbon-carbon triple bond with the general molecular formula CnH2n-2. The carbon atoms forming the triple bond undergo sp hybridization, resulting in a linear geometry with a bond angle of 180°.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structure, Bonding, and Acidity of Alkynes

  • The carbon-carbon triple bond consists of one σ-bond and two π-bonds formed by the lateral overlap of unhybridized p-orbitals.
  • Terminal alkynes exhibit appreciable acidity (pKa ≈ 25) due to the 50% s-character of the sp-hybridized carbon atoms.
  • Terminal alkynes react with strong bases like NaNH2 or Grignard reagents to form metal acetylides, whereas internal alkynes do not.
  • Silver nitrate in ammonia ([Ag(NH3)2]+) and copper(I) chloride in ammonia ([Cu(NH3)2]+) yield white and red/yellow precipitates respectively with terminal alkynes.

Preparation Methods of Alkynes

  • Dehydrohalogenation of vicinal or geminal dihalides using alcoholic KOH followed by strong base NaNH2 yields alkynes.
  • Reaction of sodium acetylide with primary alkyl halides provides a standard synthetic route for ascending the alkyne series.
  • Kolbe's electrolytic electrolysis of sodium or potassium salts of maleic or fumaric acid yields alkynes through decarboxylation.
  • Action of water on calcium carbide (CaC2) produces ethyne (acetylene) industrially.

Chemical Reactions and Stereoselectivity

  • Catalytic hydrogenation of alkynes over Lindlar's catalyst (palladized calcium carbonate deactivated with lead acetate) yields cis-alkenes stereospecifically.
  • Reduction of alkynes with sodium in liquid ammonia (Birch reduction) yields trans-alkenes via a radical-anion intermediate mechanism.
  • Electrophilic addition of halogen acids (HX) follows Markovnikov's rule, yielding geminal dihalides through intermediate vinyl cations.
  • Hydration of alkynes in the presence of HgSO4 and dilute H2SO4 yields carbonyl compounds via unstable enol tautomers.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Addition of HBr to unsymmetrical alkenes/alkynes follows Markovnikov's rule in the absence of peroxides.

Observation: Addition of HBr to alkynes in the presence of organic peroxides does not give anti-Markovnikov product reliably compared to alkenes. — The intermediate vinyl radical formed during anti-Markovnikov addition in alkynes is less stable and readily undergoes chain transfer or polymerization side reactions.

Exception Terminal alkynes react with Grignard reagents to form alkanes and magnesium acetylides.

Observation: Internal alkynes fail to react with Grignard reagents or sodium amide. — Internal alkynes lack the acidic sp-C-H proton necessary for proton abstraction by strong bases.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Ethyne has a linear structure with a C-C bond length of 120 pm and C-H bond length of 106 pm.
  • Terminal alkynes give characteristic red precipitate with ammoniacal cuprous chloride solution.
  • Ketal/Enol tautomerism converts vinyl alcohol intermediates into stable aldehydes or ketones.
  • Ozonolysis of alkynes yields vicinal dicarbonyl compounds which undergo further oxidation to carboxylic acids.
  • Polymerization of ethyne at high temperature over red-hot iron tube gives benzene.
  • Alkynes are less reactive than alkenes toward electrophilic addition due to tighter holding of π-electron cloud by sp carbons.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming hydration of all alkynes gives aldehydes; only ethyne yields acetaldehyde while all other terminal and internal alkynes yield ketones.
  • Confusing the stereochemical outcome of Lindlar reduction (cis) with Birch reduction (trans).
  • Forgetting that internal alkynes do not react with ammoniacal AgNO3 or CuCl.
  • Failing to account for the acidic nature of sp C-H hydrogen atoms in terminal alkynes.

⚡ Quick Revision: Alkynes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Acidity of Terminal Alkynes

Terminal alkynes possess acidic hydrogen atoms due to the high electronegativity of sp-hybridized carbon atoms, allowing displacement by metal ions.

Electrophilic Addition Reactions

Alkynes undergo electrophilic addition reactions in two steps: first to vinyl derivatives, then to saturated geminal derivatives, generally following Markovnikov orientation.

Stereoselective Reductions

Partial hydrogenation controls the stereochemical outcome, giving cis-alkenes with Lindlar catalyst and trans-alkenes with sodium in liquid ammonia.

Oxidative Cleavage

Reaction with strong oxidizing agents or ozonolysis cleaves the triple bond entirely to form carboxylic acids or carbon dioxide.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Lindlar Catalyst (Pd/CaCO3 + Pb(OAc)2)
Poisoned palladium catalyst used for cis-hydrogenation of alkynes.
Birch Reduction (Na/liq. NH3)
Dissolving metal reduction system yielding trans-alkenes.
Acetylide (RC≡C- M+)
Metal derivative of terminal alkyne formed via proton abstraction.
Tautomerism (Keto-Enol Equilibrium)
Rapid interconversion between constitutional isomers via proton shift.
Vicinal Dihalide (R-CH(X)-CH(X)-R)
Dihalogen derivative with halogens on adjacent carbon atoms.
Geminal Dihalide (R-CX2-CH2-R)
Dihalogen derivative with two halogens on the same carbon atom.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Terminal alkynes have sp-CH acidity (pKa ≈ 25).
  • Hydration of ethyne yields acetaldehyde; propyne yields acetone.
  • Lindlar reduction gives cis-alkenes; Birch reduction gives trans-alkenes.
  • CuCl in NH3 gives red ppt with terminal alkynes.
  • Polymerization of acetylene over Fe tube at 873 K yields benzene.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkynes contain a triple bond with one σ and two π bonds.
  • Acidity of terminal alkynes distinguishes them from alkenes and alkanes.
  • Electrophilic addition proceeds via vinyl cation intermediates.
  • Controlled hydrogenation yields specific geometric isomers of alkenes.

⚖️ Key Differences

Terminal Alkyne VS Internal Alkyne

Terminal alkynes react with ammoniacal AgNO3/CuCl and active metals, while internal alkynes do not.

Which reagent is used to convert propyne to propan-2-one?

Explanation: Hydration of propyne with dilute sulfuric acid and mercuric sulfate catalyst gives acetone (propan-2-one) via Markovnikov addition and subsequent tautomerism.

Question 2 of 19

What is the product of the reaction between ethyne and ammoniacal silver nitrate?

Explanation: Terminal alkynes like ethyne react with ammoniacal silver nitrate to form a white precipitate of silver acetylide.

Question 3 of 19

Which of the following catalysts brings about cis-hydrogenation of alkynes?

Explanation: Lindlar's catalyst (palladized calcium carbonate deactivated with lead acetate) selectively hydrogenates alkynes to cis-alkenes with syn-addition.

Question 4 of 19

The hybridization of carbon atoms in an ethyne molecule is:

Explanation: Each carbon in ethyne is bonded to two other atoms with no lone pairs, resulting in sp hybridization and a linear geometry.

Question 5 of 19

Which alkyne is most acidic?

Explanation: Ethyne has two terminal acidic hydrogens and less positive inductive effect from alkyl groups compared to substituted alkynes, making it the most acidic.

Question 6 of 19

Birch reduction of alkynes yields:

Explanation: Sodium in liquid ammonia reduces alkynes to trans-alkenes via a radical-anion mechanism.

Question 7 of 19

When ethyne is passed through a red-hot iron tube at 873 K, the product formed is:

Explanation: Cyclic polymerization of three molecules of ethyne over a red-hot iron tube yields benzene.

Question 8 of 19

The C≡C bond length in ethyne is approximately:

Explanation: The carbon-carbon triple bond length in ethyne is about 120 pm due to high s-character pulling electron density closer.

Question 9 of 19

Reaction of 1-butyne with sodium amide in liquid ammonia gives:

Explanation: Sodium amide is a strong base that deprotonates terminal 1-butyne to form sodium 1-butynide and ammonia.

Question 10 of 19

What is formed when 2-butyne is oxidized with hot alkaline KMnO4?

Explanation: Oxidative cleavage of 2-butyne splits the triple bond symmetrically, yielding two molecules of acetic acid.

Question 11 of 19

Which reagent distinguishes 1-butyne from 2-butyne?

Explanation: Ammoniacal CuCl reacts only with terminal alkynes (1-butyne) to give a red precipitate, whereas internal alkynes (2-butyne) do not react.

Question 12 of 19

In the addition of HX to propyne, the first step involves:

Explanation: Electrophilic addition begins with the attack of the alkyne π-electron cloud on the electrophilic proton (H+).

Question 13 of 19

The number of σ and π bonds in ethyne is:

Explanation: Ethyne (H-C≡C-H) has two C-H σ-bonds, one C-C σ-bond (total 3 σ), and two C-C π-bonds (total 2 π).

Question 14 of 19

Which of the following compounds gives a red precipitate with ammoniacal Cu2Cl2?

Explanation: Pent-1-yne is a terminal alkyne and forms a red copper(I) acetylide precipitate with ammoniacal cuprous chloride.

Question 15 of 19

Dehydrohalogenation of 1,2-dibromopropane with alcoholic KOH followed by sodamide yields:

Explanation: Successive elimination of two molecules of HBr from a vicinal dibromide yields an alkyne (propyne).

Question 16 of 19

The pKa value of acetylene is closest to:

Explanation: Acetylene is significantly more acidic than alkenes and alkanes with a pKa value around 25.

Question 17 of 19

Ozonolysis of an alkyne followed by zinc hydrolysis gives diketones. The alkyne is:

Explanation: Ozonolysis of internal alkynes yields vicinal dicarbonyl (diketone) compounds.

Question 18 of 19

Which of the following reactions is used for chain lengthening in alkynes?

Explanation: Nucleophilic substitution of alkyl halides by sodium acetylide adds carbon atoms to the chain, enabling alkyne synthesis with higher carbon numbers.

Question 19 of 19

What is the intermediate formed during the acid-catalyzed hydration of alkynes?

Explanation: Protonation of the alkyne triple bond by acid forms an sp2-hybridized vinyl cation intermediate.

Question 1 of 10
What is the product when 1-pentyne is treated with excess hydrogen chloride?

Solution: Addition of two molecules of HCl across the triple bond of 1-pentyne follows Markovnikov's rule twice on the same carbon, yielding 2,2-dichloropentane.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following alkynes will give a white precipitate with ammoniacal silver nitrate?

Solution: Hex-1-yne is a terminal alkyne and forms silver hex-1-ynide (white precipitate) with ammoniacal silver nitrate.

Question 3 of 10
Conversion of acetylene to acetaldehyde is an example of:

Solution: Hydration of acetylene forms vinyl alcohol which immediately tautomerizes to acetaldehyde.

Question 4 of 10
Why are alkynes less reactive than alkenes towards electrophilic addition?

Solution: The 50% s-character in sp carbons holds the π-electrons more tightly to the nucleus, making them less available for electrophilic attack compared to sp2 carbons in alkenes.

Question 5 of 10
The reaction of calcium carbide with water produces:

Solution: Hydrolysis of calcium carbide (CaC2) is the standard industrial method for producing acetylene (ethyne).

Question 6 of 10
What is formed when acetylene is bubbled through aqueous alkaline KMnO4?

Solution: Mild oxidation of acetylene with cold dilute alkaline KMnO4 yields oxalacetic acid or directly oxalic acid through cleavage and further oxidation.

Question 7 of 10
Which alkyne yields propanoic acid and carbon dioxide upon ozonolysis?

Solution: Ozonolysis of pent-1-yne cleaves the terminal triple bond to give propanoic acid (CH3CH2COOH) and CO2 (from the terminal CH).

Question 8 of 10
Which reagent converts 3-hexyne to trans-3-hexene?

Solution: Dissolving metal reduction using sodium in liquid ammonia stereoselectively reduces internal alkynes like 3-hexyne to trans-3-hexene.

Question 9 of 10
The number of possible alkynes with molecular formula C5H8 is:

Solution: C5H8 has three isomeric alkynes: pent-1-yne, pent-2-yne, and 3-methylbut-1-yne.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following compounds has the highest boiling point?

Solution: Alkynes possess linear geometry and higher polarizability, leading to stronger intermolecular forces and higher boiling points compared to corresponding alkenes and alkanes.
