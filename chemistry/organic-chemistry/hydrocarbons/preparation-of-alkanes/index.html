Organic Chemistry

Preparation of Alkanes

Alkanes are saturated aliphatic hydrocarbons prepared through various reduction, decarboxylation, organometallic, and electrolytic methods. These synthetic routes allow the conversion of unsaturated hydrocarbons, alkyl halides, carboxylic acids, and carbonyl compounds into stable C-C single-bonded species.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Reduction and Hydrogenation Methods

  • Catalytic hydrogenation involves the addition of H2 across carbon-carbon multiple bonds using heterogeneous catalysts like Pt, Pd, or Ni (Sabatier-Senderens reduction).
  • Alkyl halides undergo reduction to alkanes using Zn/HCl, LiAlH4, or HI in the presence of red phosphorus.
  • Grignard reagents (R-MgX) react with compounds containing active hydrogen such as water, alcohols, or amines to yield alkanes quantitatively.

Coupling and Organometallic Reactions

  • The Wurtz reaction couples two molecules of alkyl halide using sodium metal in dry ether to form symmetrical higher alkanes.
  • The Corey-House synthesis involves converting an alkyl halide into a lithium dialkylcopper reagent (R2CuLi), which then couples with another alkyl halide to synthesize unsymmetrical alkanes.
  • Frankland reaction uses zinc metal instead of sodium with alkyl halides to prepare lower alkanes.

Decarboxylation and Electrolytic Methods

  • Decarboxylation of sodium salts of carboxylic acids with sodalime (NaOH + CaO at 673 K) eliminates carbon dioxide to form alkanes.
  • Kolbe's electrolysis of sodium or potassium salts of monocarboxylic acids yields symmetrical alkanes at the anode via free radical intermediates.
  • Methane cannot be prepared successfully via Wurtz or Kolbe coupling reactions due to radical disproportionation pathways yielding multiple products.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Decarboxylation of Carboxylic Acids
R-COONa + NaOH + CaO → R-H + Na2CO3 (Δ)
Conditions: Sodalite (NaOH + CaO mixture) heating
Units: Yields alkane with one carbon less than parent acid
Kolbe Electrolytic Synthesis
2R-COOK + 2H2O → R-R + 2CO2 + H2 + 2KOH
Conditions: Electrolysis of concentrated aqueous sodium or potassium carboxylate
Units: Anodic coupling yielding symmetrical alkanes
Clemmensen Reduction
>C=O + 4[H] → >CH2 + H2O
Conditions: Zn-Hg amalgam and concentrated HCl
Units: Reduction of aldehydes and ketones to alkanes
Wolff-Kishner Reduction
>C=O + NH2NH2 → >C=NNH2 → >CH2 + N2
Conditions: Hydrazine followed by base (KOH) and glycol at high temperature
Units: Base-stable reduction of carbonyl compounds
Wurtz Reaction
2R-X + 2Na → R-R + 2NaX (in dry ether)
Conditions: Dry ether solvent, primary alkyl halides
Units: Yield of symmetrical alkane with even carbon number
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Wurtz reaction yields symmetrical alkanes with an even number of carbon atoms.

Observation: Attempting to synthesize methane is impossible, and using a mixture of two different alkyl halides yields a statistical mixture of three different alkanes. — Free radical intermediates combine randomly, resulting in difficult separation due to similar boiling points.

Exception Reduction of alkyl halides gives standard alkanes.

Observation: Tertiary alkyl halides treated with active metals or specific reducing agents frequently undergo elimination (alkene formation) rather than substitution/reduction. — Steric hindrance heavily favors the E2 elimination pathway over SN2 or direct reduction.

Exception Clemmensen reduction reduces carbonyl groups to methylene groups.

Observation: Acid-sensitive substrates (such as acid-labile functional groups or complex polysaccharides) decompose under the harsh acidic conditions of Zn-Hg/HCl. — Concentrated hydrochloric acid causes unwanted side reactions, necessitating the use of base-catalyzed Wolff-Kishner reduction instead.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Sabatier-Senderens reduction uses nickel catalyst at 523-573 K to convert alkenes and alkynes into alkanes.
  • Methane is prepared in the laboratory by heating sodium acetate with sodalime.
  • Wurtz reaction fails to produce pure propane because a mixture of ethane, propane, and butane is formed.
  • Kolbe electrolysis takes place in an electrolytic cell where alkanes and carbon dioxide evolve at the anode.
  • Clemmensen reduction utilizes amalgamated zinc and hydrochloric acid.
  • Wolff-Kishner reduction uses hydrazine followed by potassium hydroxide in ethylene glycol.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming Wurtz reaction can produce pure odd-carbon alkanes from a single alkyl halide.
  • Confusing the role of CaO in sodalime (CaO keeps NaOH dry and acts as a high-melting diluent, not a catalyst).
  • Forgetting that methane cannot be prepared by Kolbe electrolysis because acetic acid gives ethane.
  • Overlooking the requirement of dry ether in Wurtz and Grignard reactions due to moisture reactivity.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation of Alkanes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Hydrogenation of Unsaturated Hydrocarbons

Alkenes and alkynes add molecular hydrogen in the presence of finely divided transition metal catalysts (Pt, Pd, Ni) to yield saturated alkanes.

Halide Reduction and Organometallics

Alkyl halides are reduced via chemical reducing agents, HI/red P, or converted into Grignard reagents followed by hydrolysis to form parent alkanes.

Decarboxylation and Kolbe Electrolysis

Carboxylic acid derivatives lose CO2 either thermally via sodalime or anodically via electrolysis to generate hydrocarbon chains.

Carbonyl Reductions

Aldehydes and ketones are selectively converted to methylene groups using either acidic Clemmensen or basic Wolff-Kishner conditions.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Sodalime (NaOH + CaO)
A granular mixture used for decarboxylation of carboxylic acids.
Wurtz Reaction (2R-X + 2Na)
Coupling reaction of alkyl halides using sodium metal in dry ether.
Kolbe Electrolysis (Electrolytic Decarboxylation)
Anodic oxidation of carboxylate salts to yield symmetrical alkanes.
Clemmensen Reduction (Zn-Hg / HCl)
Reduction of carbonyl groups to alkanes under acidic conditions.
Wolff-Kishner (NH2NH2 / KOH)
Reduction of carbonyl groups to alkanes under basic conditions.
Gilman Reagent (R2CuLi)
Lithium dialkylcopper used in Corey-House synthesis.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Methane cannot be prepared by Wurtz reaction.
  • Kolbe electrolysis produces CO2 gas at the anode.
  • Sodalime is a mixture of NaOH and CaO.
  • Grignard reagents react with acidic hydrogen to form alkanes.
  • Clemmensen reduction employs Zn-Hg and concentrated HCl.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons prepared via reduction, coupling, and decarboxylation.
  • Coupling methods like Wurtz and Kolbe are restricted to symmetrical alkanes for high yields.
  • Carbonyl compounds are converted to alkanes via Clemmensen or Wolff-Kishner reductions.
  • Grignard reagents provide versatile synthetic routes from alkyl halides.

⚖️ Key Differences

Clemmensen Reduction VS Wolff-Kishner Reduction

Clemmensen operates under acidic conditions (Zn-Hg/HCl), while Wolff-Kishner operates under basic conditions (NH2NH2/KOH).

Question 1 of 20

Which of the following reagents is used in the Clemmensen reduction?

Explanation: Clemmensen reduction specifically uses zinc-mercury amalgam and concentrated hydrochloric acid to reduce carbonyl groups to methylene groups.

Question 2 of 20

What is the product formed when methyl iodide reacts with sodium in dry ether?

Explanation: Methyl iodide (CH3I) undergoes Wurtz reaction with sodium in dry ether to form ethane (CH3-CH3).

Question 3 of 20

Which alkane cannot be prepared in good yield by the Wurtz reaction?

Explanation: Propane requires a mixture of methyl and ethyl halides, yielding a mixture of ethane, propane, and butane due to radical scrambling.

Question 4 of 20

Sodalime is a mixture of:

Explanation: Sodalime is a solid mixture of sodium hydroxide (NaOH) and calcium oxide (CaO).

Question 5 of 20

In Kolbe's electrolytic method, where is carbon dioxide evolved?

Explanation: During Kolbe electrolysis of sodium carboxylates, CO2 gas is liberated at the anode along with the alkane.

Question 6 of 20

Which of the following represents the Corey-House synthesis reagent?

Explanation: Corey-House synthesis utilizes lithium dialkylcopper (R2CuLi), known as Gilman's reagent.

Question 7 of 20

Decarboxylation of sodium propanoate with sodalime yields:

Explanation: Decarboxylation removes one carbon atom as Na2CO3, converting sodium propanoate (3 carbons) into ethane (2 carbons).

Question 8 of 20

Which reducing agent is best suited for reducing an acid-sensitive carbonyl compound to an alkane?

Explanation: Wolff-Kishner reduction uses basic conditions, making it ideal for substrates that decompose in the acidic medium of Clemmensen reduction.

Question 9 of 20

What is the catalyst used in Sabatier-Senderens reduction of alkenes?

Explanation: Sabatier-Senderens reduction involves catalytic hydrogenation of alkenes and alkynes over finely divided nickel at 523-573 K.

Question 10 of 20

Grignard reagent reacts with water to form:

Explanation: Grignard reagents (R-MgX) are strong nucleophiles and bases; reaction with active hydrogen compounds like water yields alkanes (R-H).

Question 11 of 20

Which of the following methods yields an alkane with an odd number of carbon atoms?

Explanation: Decarboxylation of a sodium salt of an even-carbon fatty acid yields an odd-carbon alkane. Wurtz and Kolbe give even-carbon alkanes when starting from single alkyl halides/salts.

Question 12 of 20

What happens when ethyl chloride is treated with Zn/HCl?

Explanation: Reduction of alkyl halides with Zn/HCl replaces the halogen with hydrogen, yielding the corresponding alkane (ethane).

Question 13 of 20

In the reduction of alkyl halides with HI and red P, red phosphorus acts as:

Explanation: Red phosphorus reacts with the I2 formed during the reaction to prevent the reversible oxidation of alkane back to alkyl iodide.

Question 14 of 20

Which gas is evolved at the cathode during Kolbe electrolysis of sodium acetate?

Explanation: Reduction of water takes place at the cathode during electrolysis, releasing hydrogen gas (H2).

Question 15 of 20

The conversion of acetone to propane can be achieved by:

Explanation: Acetone is a ketone, and its conversion to propane requires the reduction of the carbonyl group, achieved via Clemmensen or Wolff-Kishner reduction.

Question 16 of 20

Which alkyl halide gives the highest yield of alkane in the Wurtz reaction?

Explanation: Primary alkyl halides predominantly undergo coupling via Wurtz reaction, whereas secondary and tertiary halides predominantly undergo elimination.

Question 17 of 20

What is the intermediate formed in the Wurtz reaction proposed by modern mechanism?

Explanation: Modern mechanistic studies indicate that Wurtz reaction proceeds through free radical and organometallic (sodium-alkyl) intermediates.

Question 18 of 20

Which of the following compounds reacts with methylmagnesium bromide to give methane?

Explanation: Methanol contains active hydrogen (O-H), which reacts with methylmagnesium bromide to yield methane gas.

Question 19 of 20

Frankland reaction is similar to Wurtz reaction, but it uses:

Explanation: The Frankland reaction replaces sodium metal with zinc metal in reaction with alkyl halides to prepare alkanes.

Question 20 of 20

Which of the following is true for the preparation of methane?

Explanation: Reduction of methyl iodide with Zn/HCl or HI/red P successfully yields methane, whereas Wurtz and Kolbe methods cannot yield methane.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following compounds gives butane upon Wurtz reaction?

Solution: Ethyl chloride (2 carbons) undergoes Wurtz coupling to double the carbon chain, yielding butane (4 carbons).

Question 2 of 10
What is the role of calcium oxide in sodalime?

Solution: CaO keeps sodium hydroxide dry, prevents the deliquescent NaOH from attacking glass apparatus, and acts as a high-melting diluent.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following methods is suitable for synthesizing an odd-carbon alkane like propane?

Solution: Corey-House synthesis couples an organocuprate with an alkyl halide, allowing controlled synthesis of odd-carbon alkanes.

Question 4 of 10
Reduction of alkyl halide with HI in the presence of red phosphorus is an example of:

Solution: Replacing halogen with hydrogen is a reduction reaction.

Question 5 of 10
In Wolff-Kishner reduction, the hydrazine derivative formed initially is:

Solution: Reaction of carbonyl with hydrazine yields hydrazone, which is subsequently decomposed by base into an alkane and nitrogen gas.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following cannot be used to prepare methane?

Solution: Methyl iodide with sodium in dry ether undergoes Wurtz coupling to give ethane, not methane.

Question 7 of 10
During Kolbe electrolysis of sodium acetate, what is observed at the anode?

Solution: Kolbe electrolysis of sodium acetate yields ethane and carbon dioxide at the anode.

Question 8 of 10
Which reagent converts aldehydes and ketones directly into alkanes under basic conditions?

Solution: Wolff-Kishner reduction uses hydrazine and base (KOH) under basic conditions.

Question 9 of 10
What is the product when methylmagnesium iodide reacts with heavy water (D2O)?

Solution: Reaction of Grignard reagent with D2O replaces the metal halide bond with deuterium, yielding CH3D (deuterated methane).

Question 10 of 10
Why does the Wurtz reaction fail for tertiary alkyl halides?

Solution: Tertiary alkyl halides undergo rapid base/metal-induced elimination (E2) rather than coupling due to steric crowding.
