Organic Chemistry

Organic Chemistry Fundamentals and Reaction Mechanisms

Organic chemistry encompasses the study of carbon compounds, focusing on their structure, properties, composition, reactions, and synthesis. Chemical behavior in organic systems is fundamentally governed by electronic effects, steric factors, and the stability of reaction intermediates.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electronic Displacement Effects

  • Inductive effect (-I/+I) is a permanent polarization of a σ-bond transmitted through carbon chains, decreasing rapidly beyond three bonds.
  • Resonance effect (+R/-R) involves the delocalization of π-electrons through a conjugated system, altering electron density permanently.
  • Hyperconjugation is the delocalization of σ-electrons of a C-H bond into an adjacent empty or partially filled p-orbital or π-orbital.
  • Electromeric effect (+E/-E) is a temporary polarization induced entirely by the demand of an attacking reagent at a multiple bond.

Reaction Intermediates and Stability

  • Carbocations are electron-deficient species with a sp2 hybridized central carbon, stabilized by +I, +R, and hyperconjugation.
  • Carbanions possess a negative charge and an unshared pair of electrons, stabilized primarily by -I and -R electron-withdrawing groups.
  • Free radicals are neutral species containing an unpaired electron, stabilized in the order tertiary > secondary > primary.
  • Carbenes are divalent carbon intermediates existing in singlet (bent, paired electrons) and triplet (linear/bent, parallel spins) states.

Stereochemistry and Isomerism

  • Geometrical isomerism arises from restricted rotation about carbon-carbon double bonds or ring systems, designated by cis/trans or E/Z notation.
  • Optical isomerism occurs in molecules lacking a plane, center, or alternating axis of symmetry, resulting in non-superimposable mirror images.
  • R/S configuration is assigned using the Cahn-Ingold-Prelog (CIP) priority rules based on atomic number.
  • Conformational analysis evaluates energy barriers of acyclic and cyclic molecules, where chair conformation of cyclohexane is the most stable.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Arrhenius Equation
k = A e-Ea / RT
Conditions: Valid for elementary thermal reactions over moderate temperature ranges
Units: k in s-1 or mol-1L s-1; Ea in J mol-1
Hammett Equation
log(k / k0) = ρ σ
Conditions: Applied to meta- and para-substituted benzene derivatives
Units: Dimensionless parameters (σ substituent constant, ρ reaction constant)
Coulomb's Law for Electrostatic Interactions
E = (q1 q2) / (4 π ε0 εr r)
Conditions: Applicable to point charges in a medium of dielectric constant εr
Units: E in Joules; q in Coulombs; r in meters
Degree of Unsaturation (Double Bond Equivalent)
DBE = C + 1 - (H/2) + (N/2) - (X/2)
Conditions: Applicable to molecular formulas of the type CcHhNnOoXx
Units: Dimensionless integer
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Higher alkyl substitution generally increases alkene stability via hyperconjugation.

Observation: Trans-cycloalkenes smaller than eight carbons are highly unstable or non-existent due to severe ring strain. — Geometric constraints prevent proper p-orbital overlap in small-ring trans-cycloalkenes.

Exception Electron-withdrawing groups increase acidity of carboxylic acids.

Observation: Ortho-substituted benzoic acids are almost always stronger acids than benzoic acid regardless of whether the substituent is +I or -I. — Ortho-effect involves steric inhibition of resonance and proximity stabilization of the carboxylate anion.

Exception Primary alkyl halides undergo SN2 reactions faster than tertiary alkyl halides.

Observation: Bridgehead halides are completely unreactive toward both SN1 and SN2 mechanisms. — Inability to attain planar sp2 geometry at the bridgehead carbon prevents carbocation formation and backside attack.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
CARBOCAT Mnemonic for Stability
3° > 2° > 1° > CH3+

Application: Use hyperconjugation count (alpha-H) to quickly determine carbocation and alkene stability.

CIP Priority Rule Shortcut
Atomic Number First → Mass Number Second

Application: Compare atoms directly attached to the stereocenter by atomic number; higher atomic number gets priority 1.

E/Z Designation Mnemonic
Entgegen = Opposite (Trans-like), Zusammen = Together (Cis-like)

Application: Assign priorities on each carbon of the double bond; if high-priority groups are on opposite sides, it is (E).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The stability of carbanions follows the reverse order of carbocations: 1° > 2° > 3°.
  • Aromaticity requires cyclic planar conjugation adhering to Hückel's rule of (4n + 2) π-electrons.
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation can undergo carbocation rearrangement, whereas acylation does not.
  • Dehydration of alcohols follows Zaitsev's rule, yielding the more substituted alkene as the major product.
  • Lucas reagent (anhydrous ZnCl2 + conc. HCl) differentiates 1°, 2°, and 3° alcohols based on turbidity time.
  • Cannizzaro reaction occurs in aldehydes lacking alpha-hydrogens when treated with concentrated alkali.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the direction of inductive effect with electromeric effect; remember inductive is permanent, electromeric is temporary.
  • Assuming all cyclic conjugated systems with 4n π-electrons are antiaromatic without verifying planarity and ring closure.
  • Forgetting that SN1 reactions can yield rearranged products due to carbocation shifts.
  • Misidentifying meso compounds as optically active simply because they contain chiral centers.

⚡ Quick Revision: Organic Chemistry Fundamentals and Reaction Mechanisms

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electronic Effects

Permanent and temporary electronic shifts determine bond polarity, acidity, basicity, and reactivity sites in organic molecules.

Reaction Intermediates

Carbocations, carbanions, free radicals, and carbenes dictate the mechanistic pathways of organic transformations.

Stereochemistry

Three-dimensional spatial arrangements give rise to optical and geometrical isomerism, influencing physiological and chemical properties.

Aliphatic and Aromatic Substitution

Nucleophilic substitution and electrophilic aromatic substitution form the core synthetic routes for functional group interconversions.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Chirality (Asymmetry)
Property of a molecule being non-superimposable on its mirror image.
Racemic Mixture ((±) Mix)
An equimolar mixture of enantiomers showing zero optical rotation.
Tautomerism (Dynamic Equilibrium)
Structural isomerism involving interconversion via proton transfer.
Electrophile (E+)
An electron-deficient species seeking electron pairs.
Nucleophile (Nu-)
An electron-rich species donating electron pairs.
Mesomeric Effect (R Effect)
Electron delocalization through conjugated π-systems.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Hückel's Rule: (4n + 2) π-electrons for aromaticity.
  • Markovnikov's Rule: Addition of HX gives the more stable carbocation intermediate.
  • Zaitsev's Rule: Elimination yields the most substituted alkene.
  • Walden Inversion: Characteristic of SN2 backside attack.
  • Peroxide Effect: Anti-Markovnikov addition of HBr in presence of peroxides.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Inductive and resonance effects dictate electron density distribution.
  • Carbocation stability governs elimination and substitution pathways.
  • Chiral centers determine optical activity and stereospecific outcomes.
  • Aromatic stability dictates resistance to addition and propensity for substitution.

⚖️ Key Differences

Enantiomers VS Diastereomers

Enantiomers are non-superimposable mirror images; diastereomers are non-mirror image stereoisomers.

Which species is an electrophile?

Explanation: AlCl3 has an incomplete octet (6 electrons), making it a Lewis acid and electrophile.

Question 6 of 20

Which of the following is aromatic according to Hückel's rule?

Explanation: Benzene is a planar cyclic conjugated system containing 6 π-electrons (n=1, 4n+2 = 6).

Question 7 of 20

The hyperconjugative stability of alkenes depends on the presence of:

Explanation: Hyperconjugation involves the overlap of C-H sigma bonds of alpha-carbon with adjacent p or pi orbitals.

Question 8 of 20

Which reagent is used to distinguish between 1°, 2°, and 3° alcohols?

Explanation: Lucas reagent reacts at different rates: 3° instantly, 2° in 5 minutes, 1° requires heating.

Question 9 of 20

The reaction of propene with HBr in the presence of organic peroxide follows:

Explanation: Presence of peroxide induces a free radical chain mechanism leading to anti-Markovnikov addition of HBr.

Question 10 of 20

Which of the following compounds will give a positive Cannizzaro reaction?

Explanation: Formaldehyde lacks alpha-hydrogens, which is a requirement for undergoing self-oxidation-reduction (Cannizzaro).

Question 11 of 20

What is the hybridization of the central carbon in a methyl free radical?

Explanation: A methyl free radical is planar with sp2 hybridization and an odd electron residing in an unhybridized p-orbital.

Question 12 of 20

Which functional group shows the strongest -R effect?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is a strong electron-withdrawing group exerting both -I and -R effects.

Question 13 of 20

Geometrical isomerism is exhibited by:

Explanation: 2-butene has restricted rotation across the double bond and different groups attached to each carbon of the double bond.

Question 14 of 20

Which of the following carbocations undergoes rapid rearrangement?

Explanation: Neopentyl carbocation (1°) rearranges via a 1,2-methyl shift to form a stable tertiary carbocation.

Question 15 of 20

The correct order of boiling points for isomeric pentanes is:

Explanation: Straight-chain n-pentane has larger surface area, leading to higher van der Waals forces and higher boiling point than branched isomers.

Question 16 of 20

Which species acts as a nucleophile?

Explanation: Cyanide ion (CN-) has a lone pair of electrons and a negative charge, making it a strong nucleophile.

Question 17 of 20

meso-Tartaric acid is optically inactive due to the presence of:

Explanation: Meso compounds contain chiral centers but possess an internal plane or center of symmetry, causing internal compensation.

Question 18 of 20

Friedel-Crafts acylation of benzene with acetyl chloride in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3 yields:

Explanation: Acylation introduces an acyl group (-COR), producing acetophenone.

Question 19 of 20

Which of the following is the strongest acid?

Explanation: Acetic acid forms a resonance-stabilized carboxylate anion, making it significantly stronger than alcohols, water, or phenol.

Question 20 of 20

In Kjeldahl's method, nitrogen in an organic compound is quantitatively converted to:

Explanation: Digestive treatment with concentrated sulfuric acid converts organic nitrogen into ammonium sulfate.

Solution: Intramolecular dehydration of ethanol at 443 K using concentrated sulfuric acid produces ethene.

Question 2 of 10
Which effect accounts for the enhanced acidity of picric acid compared to phenol?

Solution: Three electron-withdrawing nitro groups at ortho and para positions exert strong -I and -R effects, stabilizing the phenoxide ion.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following alkyl halides reacts most rapidly via SN1 mechanism?

Solution: 2-chloro-2-methylpropane is a tertiary halide, forming a stable tertiary carbocation required for rapid SN1 substitution.

Question 4 of 10
Which compound exhibits keto-enol tautomerism?

Solution: Acetone possesses alpha-hydrogens adjacent to the carbonyl carbon, allowing keto-enol tautomerism.

Question 5 of 10
What is the product of ozonolysis of 2-methylpropene followed by zinc and water reduction?

Solution: Ozonolysis of (CH3)2C=CH2 cleaves the double bond to yield acetone and formaldehyde.

Question 6 of 10
Which reagent converts a primary alcohol directly into an aldehyde without over-oxidation to carboxylic acid?

Solution: PCC is a mild anhydrous oxidizing agent that selectively oxidizes primary alcohols to aldehydes.

Question 7 of 10
In electrophilic aromatic substitution, a nitro group directs incoming substituents to which position?

Solution: The nitro group is a strong deactivating meta-directing substituent due to its -R and -I effects.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following compounds gives iodoform test?

Solution: Ethanol contains the CH3-CH(OH)- group, which undergoes oxidation and halogenation to precipitate yellow iodoform.

Question 9 of 10
The reaction of ethyl chloride with aqueous KOH proceeds via which mechanism?

Solution: Primary alkyl halides like ethyl chloride undergo nucleophilic substitution via a bimolecular SN2 pathway with strong base.

Question 10 of 10
Which species acts as the attacking reagent in sulfonation of benzene?

Solution: Sulfuration proceeds via electrophilic attack of sulfur trioxide (SO3), which may be neutral or protonated depending on conditions.
