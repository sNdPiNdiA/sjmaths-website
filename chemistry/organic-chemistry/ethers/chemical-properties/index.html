Chemical Properties of Ethers

Ethers are relatively unreactive compounds due to the stability of the ether linkage and the strength of the C-O bond. Their chemical behavior is predominantly governed by the basicity of ether oxygen and the cleavage of the C-O bond under acidic conditions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Cleavage of C-O Bonds in Ethers

  • Ethers undergo cleavage of C-O bonds only under harsher conditions using strong acids like HI or HBr at elevated temperatures.
  • With mixed alkyl ethers containing a primary and a tertiary alkyl group, the reaction follows an SN1 mechanism yielding tertiary alkyl halide and primary alcohol.
  • With aryl alkyl ethers (e.g., anisole), cleavage invariably yields phenol and alkyl iodide because the phenyl C-O bond has partial double-bond character.

Electrophilic Substitution in Aromatic Ethers

  • The alkoxy group (-OR) in aromatic ethers is strongly ortho- and para-directing and activates the aromatic ring toward electrophilic substitution due to +R resonance.
  • Halogenation of anisole with bromine in ethanoic acid yields a mixture of 4-bromoanisole and 2-bromoanisole without requiring a Lewis acid catalyst.
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation and acylation proceed smoothly with anisole in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3 as catalyst.

Auto-oxidation and Complex Formation

  • Ethers react slowly with atmospheric oxygen in the presence of light to form unstable, explosive hydroperoxides and dialkyl peroxides.
  • Due to the presence of two lone pairs of electrons on the oxygen atom, ethers act as Lewis bases and dissolve in strong acids to form oxonium salts.
  • Ethers react with anhydrous boron trifluoride or Grignard reagents to form stable etherates, which explains their widespread utility as inert polar solvents.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Autoxidation of Ethers
R-CH2-O-R' + O2 → R-CH(OOH)-O-R'
Conditions: Exposure to atmospheric oxygen and light over time
Units: Forms explosive organic hydroperoxides
Zeisel Method for Alkoxyl Determination
R-OCH3 + HI → CH3I + R-OH (CH3I + AgNO3 → AgI↓)
Conditions: Refluxing with concentrated HI, followed by silver nitrate precipitation
Units: Mass of AgI precipitated determines alkoxy percentage
Formation of Oxonium Salts
R-O-R' + H+ → [R-OH+-R']
Conditions: Reaction with cold concentrated mineral acids (H2SO4 or HCl)
Units: Forms stable soluble oxonium salts
Acidic Cleavage with Excess HX
R-O-R' + 2HX → RX + R'X + H2O
Conditions: Concentrated HX (HI or HBr) at high temperatures
Units: Reaction stoichiometry: 1 mole ether consumes 2 moles HX
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl aryl ethers react with HX to form alkyl halide and phenol.

Observation: Anisole with HI yields iodomethane and phenol, never iodobenzene. — The sp2 hybridized carbon of the benzene ring imparts partial double bond character to the aromatic C-O bond, preventing cleavage of the aryl-oxygen bond.

Exception Primary-tertiary mixed ethers cleave to give tertiary halide via SN1.

Observation: Reaction with anhydrous HI at low temperature can isolate intermediate alcohol. — Protonation forms the oxonium ion, but steric hindrance and carbocation stability dictate the rate of leaving group departure.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Ethers are completely inert toward bases, oxidizing agents, and reducing agents under normal conditions.
  • Reaction of diethyl ether with cold concentrated H2SO4 yields soluble diethyl oxonium hydrogen sulfate.
  • Cleavage of alkyl aryl ethers (anisole) with hot conc. HI always yields phenol and alkyl iodide.
  • Ethers must be tested and freed from peroxides before distillation to prevent violent explosions.
  • Zeisel's method quantitatively estimates the number of methoxy (-OCH3) groups in an ether.
  • Ethoxybenzene (phenetole) undergoes bromination to give 4-ethoxyphenol as the major product.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming aryl-oxygen bond breaks in alkyl aryl ethers with HI; remember the aryl-O bond is remarkably stable.
  • Forgetting that ethers form explosive peroxides upon prolonged exposure to air and light.
  • Writing substitution reactions of ethers without considering the role of strong acids as catalysts.
  • Confusing the regiochemistry of ether cleavage involving tertiary versus primary alkyl groups.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Properties of Ethers

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Acidic Cleavage Mechanism

Ethers react with concentrated hydrohalic acids (HI, HBr) via protonation of the ether oxygen followed by nucleophilic attack of halide ion on the resulting oxonium species.

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution

Alkoxy groups activate benzene rings toward electrophilic attack, directing incoming groups predominantly to the ortho and para positions due to resonance stabilization.

Auto-oxidation Behavior

Ether molecules readily abstract hydrogen adjacent to the oxygen atom in the presence of light and O2, yielding dangerous hydroperoxides.

Lewis Base Character

The lone pairs on ether oxygen enable coordination with Lewis acids like BF3 and metal ions, forming stable complexes and rendering them excellent solvents.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Cleavage of alkyl aryl ethers with HI yields phenol + alkyl iodide.
  • Tertiary alkyl ethers cleave via SN1 to give tertiary halide.
  • Ethers form explosive peroxides with O2 and light.
  • Alkoxy group is ortho/para directing in electrophilic aromatic substitution.
  • Ethers act as Lewis bases due to oxygen lone pairs.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Ethers exhibit low chemical reactivity owing to strong C-O single bonds.
  • Acidic cleavage with HI/HBr is the principal reaction mode for breaking the ether linkage.
  • Aromatic ethers undergo rapid electrophilic substitution due to +R activation.
  • Safety precautions regarding peroxide formation are essential during ether handling.

⚖️ Key Differences

Cleavage of Aliphatic Ethers VS Cleavage of Aromatic Ethers

Aliphatic ethers cleave to form two alkyl derivatives, whereas aromatic ethers always yield phenol and an alkyl halide.

Question 1 of 18

What are the products formed when ethyl methyl ether reacts with hot concentrated HI?

Explanation: With excess hot HI, mixed ethers give a mixture of corresponding alkyl iodides and water, while initial cleavage yields iodoethane and methanol.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following compounds is formed when anisole reacts with Br2 in ethanoic acid?

Explanation: Bromination of anisole in the absence of a catalyst gives a mixture of ortho- and para-bromoanisole, with para being major.

Question 3 of 18

Why do ethers possess dipole moments even if their geometry is bent?

Explanation: The two polar C-O bonds inclined at an angle do not cancel each other out, resulting in a net dipole moment (e.g., 1.15 D for diethyl ether).

Question 4 of 18

Which reagent is used in the Zeisel method to determine the number of methoxy groups?

Explanation: Concentrated hydriodic acid (HI) cleaves alkoxy groups to form volatile alkyl iodides in the Zeisel method.

Question 5 of 18

The cleavage of tert-butyl methyl ether with HI proceeds via which mechanism?

Explanation: The formation of a stable tertiary carbocation favors the SN1 mechanism, yielding tert-butyl iodide and methanol.

Question 6 of 18

What happens when diethyl ether is exposed to air and sunlight for a long time?

Explanation: Ethers undergo autoxidation in the presence of air and light to produce hazardous, explosive hydroperoxides.

Question 7 of 18

Anisole undergoes nitration with a mixture of conc. HNO3 and conc. H2SO4 to give:

Explanation: The -OCH3 group is activating and ortho/para-directing, yielding ortho- and para-nitroanisole.

Question 8 of 18

Ethers can be regarded as dialkyl derivatives of:

Explanation: Replacing both hydrogen atoms of water (H-O-H) with alkyl or aryl groups yields ethers (R-O-R').

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following ethers reacts most rapidly with cold concentrated HI?

Explanation: Tert-butyl methyl ether forms a highly stable tertiary carbocation readily, making its cleavage exceptionally fast.

Question 10 of 18

Why are ethers used as solvents for Grignard reagents?

Explanation: Ethers provide unshared electron pairs on oxygen to coordinate with and stabilize the organomagnesium halide.

Question 11 of 18

The C-O-C bond angle in ethers is approximately:

Explanation: Due to steric repulsion between bulky alkyl groups, the C-O-C bond angle is greater than the tetrahedral angle, around 111.7°.

Question 12 of 18

Which compound is formed when methyl phenyl ether is treated with excess HI?

Explanation: Due to partial double bond character of the C(aryl)-O bond, cleavage occurs to give phenol and iodomethane.

Question 13 of 18

What is the hybridisation of oxygen in aliphatic ethers?

Explanation: Ether oxygen uses sp3 hybrid orbitals, accommodating two lone pairs and two sigma bonds.

Question 14 of 18

Friedel-Crafts acylation of anisole with acetyl chloride and anhydrous AlCl3 gives:

Explanation: Friedel-Crafts acylation yields both ortho and para substituted methoxyacetophenones, with para as the major product.

Question 15 of 18

Which test is used to detect the presence of ether peroxides?

Explanation: Peroxides oxidize Fe2+ to Fe3+, which reacts with thiocyanate (KSCN) to form a deep red blood-colored complex.

Question 16 of 18

When diethyl ether is heated with excess concentrated HI, the products are:

Explanation: Reaction with excess HI converts diethyl ether completely into ethyl iodide and water.

Question 17 of 18

Ethers exhibit which type of isomerism with monohydric alcohols?

Explanation: Ethers and monohydric alcohols share the same general molecular formula (CnH2n+2O) and represent functional group isomers.

Question 18 of 18

Which of the following cannot be prepared by Williamson synthesis?

Explanation: Tertiary alkyl halides undergo elimination rather than substitution when treated with alkoxides, preventing di-tert-butyl ether formation.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary product when isopropyl methyl ether is treated with concentrated HI?

Solution: Secondary alkyl group forms a secondary carbocation or undergoes SN2, but in acidic conditions, iodide attacks the less hindered or secondary carbon forming isopropyl iodide and methanol.

Question 2 of 10
Why are ethers generally unreactive towards bases and reducing agents?

Solution: Ethers lack acidic hydrogens and active functional groups, making them stable toward nucleophiles, bases, and reducing agents.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following catalysts is used during the halogenation of aromatic ethers like anisole?

Solution: The activating +R effect of the methoxy group makes the ring sufficiently reactive for halogenation without Lewis acid catalysts.

Question 4 of 10
In the Zeisel determination of methoxy groups, the evolved alkyl iodide is passed into an alcoholic solution of:

Solution: Silver nitrate reacts with alkyl iodide to precipitate silver iodide (AgI), which is weighed gravimetrically.

Question 5 of 10
What is the byproduct formed when ethers undergo auto-oxidation?

Solution: Auto-oxidation of ethers produces unstable and explosive alpha-hydroperoxides.

Question 6 of 10
Which ether cannot be cleaved by cold HI to give an alcohol and alkyl iodide?

Solution: Diphenyl ether is extremely resistant to cleavage by hydrogen halides even under harsh conditions due to strong aryl-oxygen bonds.

Question 7 of 10
Which electronic effect explains the ortho/para directing nature of alkoxy groups in aromatic rings?

Solution: The lone pairs on oxygen participate in resonance (+R), increasing electron density at ortho and para positions.

Question 8 of 10
What happens when diethyl ether reacts with cold concentrated sulfuric acid?

Solution: Cold concentrated H2SO4 protonates the ether oxygen to form a soluble oxonium hydrogen sulfate salt.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following statements is correct regarding the physical properties of ethers?

Solution: Ethers cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds among themselves, resulting in much lower boiling points than corresponding isomeric alcohols.

Question 10 of 10
In the reaction of benzyl methyl ether with HI, the products are:

Solution: Benzyl carbocation is extremely stable due to resonance, so iodide attacks the methyl group (SN2) while benzyl group forms benzyl alcohol via carbocation or direct substitution.
