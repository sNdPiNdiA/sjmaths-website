Ethers

Ethers are organic compounds characterized by an oxygen atom bonded to two alkyl or aryl groups with the general formula R-O-R'. They exhibit tetrahedral geometry around the oxygen atom with C-O-C bond angles significantly wider than regular tetrahedral angles due to steric repulsion.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation Methods of Ethers

  • Williamson Ether Synthesis involves the nucleophilic substitution of an alkyl halide by an alkoxide ion, proceeding via an SN2 mechanism.
  • Intermolecular Dehydration of Alcohols occurs in the presence of protic acids like concentrated H2SO4 at 413 K to yield symmetrical ethers.
  • Secondary and tertiary alkyl halides cannot be used in Williamson synthesis because they predominantly undergo elimination (E2) rather than substitution.
  • Alkoxymercuration-demercuration of alkenes involves reaction with alcohol in the presence of mercury(II) acetate followed by sodium borohydride reduction, yielding unsymmetrical ethers via Markovnikov orientation.

Chemical Properties and Cleavage Reactions

  • Ethers react with concentrated HI or HBr at elevated temperatures to undergo cleavage of the C-O bond, forming alkyl halides and alcohols.
  • When an alkyl-aryl ether like anisole is cleaved with HI, the reaction consistently yields phenol and an alkyl iodide because the aryl-oxygen bond possesses partial double-bond character.
  • Mixed ethers containing a primary and a tertiary alkyl group yield a tertiary alkyl halide and a primary alcohol upon reaction with hydrogen halides due to the formation of stable tertiary carbocations.
  • Ethers act as Lewis bases, reacting with Lewis acids like BF3 or Mg to form stable coordination complexes known as oxonium or etherates.

Physical Properties and Structure

  • The C-O-C bond angle in dimethyl ether is approximately 111.7°, which is wider than tetrahedral due to repulsive interactions between bulky alkyl groups.
  • Ethers possess a small net dipole moment, resulting in weak dipole-dipole attractions and boiling points significantly lower than isomeric alcohols of comparable molecular mass.
  • Lower ethers show appreciable solubility in water because oxygen atoms can form hydrogen bonds with water molecules.
  • Ether vapors form highly explosive mixtures with air and easily undergo autoxidation to form dangerous, shock-sensitive hydroperoxides.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Primary alkyl halides are typically used in Williamson synthesis for high yields.

Observation: Tertiary alkyl halides yield alkenes exclusively. — Strong basicity of alkoxide ions promotes E2 elimination over SN2 substitution when encountering sterically hindered substrates.

Exception Ether cleavage by HI yields two alkyl units based on size.

Observation: Alkyl aryl ethers yield phenol and alkyl iodide, never aryl iodide and alcohol. — Resonance stabilization of the aromatic ring gives the phenyl-oxygen bond partial double-bond character, preventing cleavage of the sp2 C-O bond.

Exception Mixed ethers with primary and secondary groups cleave to give primary halide.

Observation: Mixed ethers with tertiary groups yield tertiary halide. — Reaction proceeds via an SN1 mechanism involving a highly stable tertiary carbocation intermediate.

Williamson Substrate Rule

Application: Always choose a primary alkyl halide and a tertiary alkoxide when designing a Williamson synthesis to prevent elimination side reactions.

Application: In unsymmetrical alkyl ethers with 3° or 2° centers, the alkyl group forming the more stable carbocation or transition state forms the iodide.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Dimethyl ether and ethanol are functional isomers sharing the molecular formula C2H6O.
  • The boiling point of diethyl ether (34.6 °C) is much lower than that of n-butanol (117.7 °C) due to absence of self-association via hydrogen bonds.
  • Williamson synthesis follows strict second-order kinetics (rate = k[R-X][R'O−]).
  • Ethers must be stored in dark, tightly sealed containers with reducing agents or iron to prevent hazardous peroxide accumulation.
  • Anisole undergoes electrophilic aromatic substitution predominantly at ortho and para positions due to the +R directing effect of the -OCH3 group.
  • Reaction of an ether with hot concentrated HI is used quantitatively in the Zeisel method for estimating methoxy or ethoxy groups.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming tertiary alkyl halides can successfully form ethers in Williamson synthesis; they yield alkenes instead.
  • Confusing the cleavage products of alkyl aryl ethers: the aromatic portion always yields phenol, not iodobenzene.
  • Neglecting the safety hazard of ether peroxides during laboratory distillation or evaporation.
  • Confusing structural isomers between ethers and alcohols having identical molecular formulas.

⚡ Quick Revision: Ethers

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Nomenclature and Classification

Ethers are classified as symmetrical (simple) or unsymmetrical (mixed) depending on whether the alkyl or aryl groups attached to oxygen are identical or different. IUPAC nomenclature designates them as alkoxyalkanes.

Synthetic Routes

The principal industrial and laboratory methods include intermolecular dehydration of alcohols with acid catalysts and the Williamson ether synthesis utilizing alkoxides and primary alkyl halides.

Chemical Reactivity

Ethers are generally inert towards bases, oxidizing agents, and reducing agents under normal conditions. Their main chemical reactions involve cleavage of the C-O bond by strong acids and electrophilic substitution on aromatic rings.

Safety and Handling

Due to their high volatility, low flash points, and tendency to form explosive peroxides upon prolonged exposure to air and light, ethers require careful storage and handling protocols.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Williamson Synthesis (R-O-Na+ + R'-X)
Method for preparing symmetrical and unsymmetrical ethers via SN2 mechanism.
Alkoxymercuration (Hg(OAc)2, ROH)
Electrophilic addition method to synthesize unsymmetrical ethers from alkenes.
Zeisel Method (Quantitative HI Cleavage)
Analytical procedure to determine the number of alkoxy groups in an ether.
Autoxidation (R-CH(OOH)-O-R)
Spontaneous atmospheric oxidation of ethers yielding explosive hydroperoxides.
Anisole (C6H5OCH3)
Methoxybenzene, a common aromatic ether exhibiting activating ortho/para-directing properties.
Oxonium Ion (R2OH+)
Protonated ether species formed in acidic media acting as a reactive intermediate.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Intermolecular dehydration of ethanol with conc. H2SO4 at 413 K yields diethyl ether.
  • Tertiary alkyl halides undergo E2 elimination instead of SN2 substitution with alkoxide ions.
  • Cleavage of alkyl aryl ethers with HI always yields phenol and an alkyl iodide.
  • Ethers have boiling points lower than corresponding isomeric alcohols due to lack of intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
  • Ethers react with atmospheric oxygen over time to form dangerous explosive peroxides.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Ethers are neutral, relatively inert oxygen-containing organic compounds.
  • Williamson synthesis is the premier route for unsymmetrical ether preparation using 1° halides.
  • C-O bond cleavage requires harsh reagents like concentrated HI or HBr with heat.
  • Aromatic ethers direct electrophilic substitution exclusively to ortho and para positions.

⚖️ Key Differences

Williamson Ether Synthesis VS Alcohol Dehydration

Williamson uses alkoxide + alkyl halide (SN2), whereas dehydration uses alcohol + acid catalyst (molecular condensation).

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following reagents is best suited for the preparation of tert-butyl methyl ether via Williamson synthesis?

Explanation: Tert-butyl methyl ether requires a tertiary alkoxide (sodium tert-butoxide) and a primary alkyl halide (methyl chloride) to prevent E2 elimination.

Question 2 of 18

What are the products formed when ethyl methyl ether is heated with excess concentrated hydroiodic acid (HI)?

Explanation: Excess hot concentrated HI cleaves both C-O bonds of mixed ethers, producing corresponding alkyl iodides (iodoethane and iodomethane) along with water.

Question 3 of 18

Why do ethers possess lower boiling points compared to isomeric alcohols?

Explanation: Unlike alcohols, ether molecules lack O-H bonds, preventing intermolecular hydrogen bonding and resulting in much lower boiling points.

Question 4 of 18

The cleavage of anisole with hot concentrated HI yields which of the following principal products?

Explanation: Due to resonance stabilization of the aryl-oxygen bond, the O-CH3 bond breaks to yield phenol and iodomethane.

Question 5 of 18

Which compound is formed when ethanol is heated with concentrated sulfuric acid at 413 K?

Explanation: Intermolecular dehydration of ethanol at 413 K in the presence of concentrated H2SO4 yields diethyl ether.

Question 6 of 18

What is the primary danger associated with storing ethers for extended periods in unsealed containers?

Explanation: Ethers undergo autoxidation upon exposure to air and light, generating unstable, shock-sensitive hydroperoxides.

Question 7 of 18

Which hybridization is assigned to the oxygen atom in dimethyl ether?

Explanation: The oxygen atom in ethers forms two single sigma bonds and holds two lone pairs, resulting in sp3 hybridization and bent geometry.

Question 8 of 18

In the mechanism of Williamson ether synthesis, what type of reaction is primarily involved?

Explanation: Williamson synthesis proceeds via an SN2 nucleophilic attack of the alkoxide ion on the alkyl halide.

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following ethers has the highest boiling point?

Explanation: Boiling point increases with molecular weight and van der Waals surface area; dipropyl ether is the largest among the choices.

Question 10 of 18

What is the IUPAC name of the compound commonly known as methyl phenyl ether?

Explanation: According to IUPAC rules, methyl phenyl ether is named methoxybenzene (anisole).

Question 11 of 18

Reaction of propene with methanol in the presence of mercury(II) acetate followed by sodium borohydride yields:

Explanation: Alkoxymercuration-demercuration adds alcohol across the double bond following Markovnikov's rule, yielding isopropyl methyl ether.

Question 12 of 18

Why cannot tertiary alkyl halides be used to prepare ethers via Williamson synthesis?

Explanation: Steric hindrance in tertiary halides promotes elimination (E2) rather than SN2 substitution when treated with basic alkoxides.

Question 13 of 18

Ethers act as which type of chemical species when reacting with Lewis acids like BF3?

Explanation: The unshared electron pairs on the oxygen atom allow ethers to act as Lewis bases, forming oxonium complexes.

Question 14 of 18

What happens when diethyl ether is treated with cold concentrated HI?

Explanation: With cold HI, symmetric ethers cleave to form one mole of alcohol and one mole of alkyl halide.

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following functional groups is an isomer of ethers containing two carbon atoms?

Explanation: Ethers and alcohols exhibit functional isomerism; ethanol and dimethyl ether share the formula C2H6O.

Question 16 of 18

Which group directs incoming electrophiles to ortho and para positions in aromatic ethers like anisole?

Explanation: The methoxy group (-OCH3) exerts a strong +R (mesomeric) effect, activating the benzene ring toward ortho/para substitution.

Question 17 of 18

What is the approximate C-O-C bond angle in dimethyl ether?

Explanation: Steric repulsion between bulky alkyl groups widens the C-O-C bond angle to approximately 111.7°.

Question 18 of 18

Which reagent is commonly used to detect the presence of peroxides in old ether samples?

Explanation: Peroxides oxidize Fe2+ to Fe3+, which reacts with thiocyanate to form a deep red complex.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following pairs represents functional isomers?

Solution: Dimethyl ether and ethanol both share the molecular formula C2H6O but belong to different homologous series.

Question 2 of 10
What is the product when methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE) is treated with hydriodic acid?

Solution: Cleavage of MTBE yields methyl iodide and tert-butanol because the tertiary carbocation formed is rapidly trapped by iodide.

Question 3 of 10
Williamson synthesis is classified as which type of organic reaction?

Solution: It is a bimolecular nucleophilic substitution (SN2) reaction where an alkoxide ion displaces a halide ion.

Question 4 of 10
Why is sodium metal stored under kerosene, while ethers must be tested for peroxides before distillation?

Solution: Accumulated ether peroxides have low volatility and can detonate violently when heated during distillation.

Question 5 of 10
Which catalyst is used for the industrial preparation of symmetrical ethers from alcohols?

Solution: Acid-catalyzed intermolecular dehydration of primary alcohols at 413 K yields symmetrical ethers.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following functional groups has the highest priority when naming mixed compounds containing ethers?

Solution: Alcohols have higher IUPAC suffix priority than ethers, which are always treated as alkoxy substituents.

Question 7 of 10
What is observed when ether is shaken with aqueous potassium iodide and starch?

Solution: Peroxides oxidize I− to I2, which forms a deep blue complex with starch.

Question 8 of 10
Which ether is commonly used as an anesthetic in medicine?

Solution: Diethyl ether has historically served as a general anesthetic due to its depressant effect on the central nervous system.

Question 9 of 10
In electrophilic substitution of anisole, the directing influence of the methoxy group is:

Solution: The -OCH3 group is electron-donating via resonance (+R effect), directing incoming electrophiles to ortho and para positions.

Question 10 of 10
What happens when ethyl phenyl ether is treated with bromine in ethanoic acid?

Solution: Bromination of phenetole (ethyl phenyl ether) yields 4-bromoethoxybenzene as the major para isomer due to activating resonance effects.
