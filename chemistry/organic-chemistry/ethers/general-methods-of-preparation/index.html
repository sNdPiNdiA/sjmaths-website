General Methods of Preparation of Ethers

Ethers are organic compounds containing an oxygen atom bonded to two alkyl or aryl groups, represented by the general formula R-O-R'. They are synthesized primarily via nucleophilic substitution pathways such as the Williamson ether synthesis and acid-catalyzed dehydration of alcohols.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Williamson Ether Synthesis

  • Involves the reaction of an alkyl halide with a sodium alkoxide to yield symmetrical or unsymmetrical ethers.
  • Proceeds via an SN2 mechanism, requiring primary alkyl halides for optimal yields.
  • Secondary and tertiary alkyl halides undergo competing elimination (E2) reactions to yield alkenes instead of ethers.

Dehydration of Alcohols

  • Treatment of primary alcohols with concentrated H2SO4 at 413 K yields symmetrical ethers.
  • At higher temperatures (443 K), the reaction pathway shifts to favor alkene formation via intramolecular dehydration.
  • The mechanism proceeds through the protonation of the alcohol followed by bimolecular nucleophilic displacement of water by a second alcohol molecule.

Alternative Synthetic Routes

  • Alkoxymercuration-demercuration of alkenes adds alcohols across double bonds in accordance with Markovnikov's rule without skeletal rearrangement.
  • Reaction of diazoalkanes with alcohols in the presence of HBF4 or BF3 catalyst forms methyl ethers efficiently.
  • Treating haloalkanes with dry Ag2O provides a direct laboratory route for synthesizing symmetrical ethers.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Williamson synthesis forms ethers from alkyl halides and alkoxides.

Observation: Tertiary alkyl halides fail to yield ethers. — Steric hindrance induces the E2 elimination pathway, exclusively producing alkenes.

Exception Acid-catalyzed dehydration of alcohols always produces symmetrical ethers.

Observation: Mixtures of different alcohols yield complex statistical mixtures of three different ethers. — Intermolecular attack occurs indiscriminately between protonated and unprotonated alcohol species present in the reaction medium.

Exception Alkenes react with water to form alcohols via oxymercuration.

Observation: Alkenes reacting with alcohols instead of water form ethers. — The alcohol acts as the nucleophile attacking the mercurinium intermediate instead of water.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Williamson Halide Rule
1° Halide + Alkoxide → Ether; 3° Halide + Alkoxide → Alkene

Application: Always select the alkyl halide component as primary and the alkoxide as tertiary when synthesizing hindered unsymmetrical ethers.

Dehydration Temperature Key
413 K = Ether; 443 K = Alkene

Application: Remember the 30-degree difference rule for ethanol dehydration products in numerical and conceptual MCQs.

Markovnikov Ether Addition
Alkene + Alcohol → Ether (H goes to less substituted carbon)

Application: Use Markovnikov's regioselectivity rule during alkoxymercuration-demercuration problems.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Williamson ether synthesis follows an SN2 mechanism.
  • Tertiary alkyl halides undergo elimination rather than substitution when treated with alkoxides.
  • Ethanol yields diethyl ether with concentrated H2SO4 at 413 K.
  • Ethanol yields ethene with concentrated H2SO4 at 443 K.
  • Alkoxymercuration-demercuration yields ethers via Markovnikov addition.
  • Reaction of alkyl halides with dry Ag2O produces symmetrical ethers.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Using tertiary alkyl halides in Williamson synthesis expecting high ether yields, ignoring the dominant E2 elimination.
  • Confusing the temperature limits for alcohol dehydration (413 K vs 443 K).
  • Assuming acid-catalyzed dehydration is suitable for preparing unsymmetrical ethers from two different alcohols.
  • Neglecting the role of sterics in determining the success of nucleophilic attack in ether formation.

⚡ Quick Revision: General Methods of Preparation of Ethers

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Williamson Synthesis Mechanism

Nucleophilic attack of alkoxide ion on primary alkyl halide via concerted SN2 pathway forming ether and halide salt.

Dehydration Pathway

Protonation of alcohol hydroxyl group followed by nucleophilic displacement by a second alcohol molecule at 413 K.

Oxymercuration-Demercuration

Addition of alkoxy groups across alkene double bonds using mercuric acetate and alcohol, followed by sodium borohydride reduction.

Silver Oxide Method

Direct conversion of alkyl halides to symmetrical ethers using dry silver oxide via precipitation of silver halide.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Williamson Synthesis (R-X + R'-ONa)
Method for preparing symmetrical and unsymmetrical ethers.
Alkoxide (R-O-)
Strong nucleophile derived from deprotonation of alcohols.
Intermolecular Dehydration (2 ROH → ROR + H2O)
Acid-catalyzed condensation of two alcohol molecules.
Markovnikov Addition (Regioselective addition)
Addition where electrophile attaches to carbon with more hydrogens.
Diazoalkane (R-CHN2)
Compound used to synthesize methyl ethers with alcohol.
SN2 Reaction (Bimolecular substitution)
Concerted nucleophilic substitution step with inversion of configuration.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Williamson synthesis requires 1° alkyl halides.
  • Dehydration temperature for ethers is 413 K.
  • Dehydration temperature for alkenes is 443 K.
  • Alkoxymercuration follows Markovnikov's rule.
  • Ag2O converts alkyl halides to symmetrical ethers.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Ethers are prepared via substitution and dehydration reactions.
  • Williamson synthesis is versatile for unsymmetrical ethers if 1° halides are used.
  • Acid-catalyzed dehydration works best for symmetrical primary ethers.
  • Alkene functionalization provides alternate access routes.

⚖️ Key Differences

Williamson Synthesis with 1° Halide VS Williamson Synthesis with 3° Halide

1° halide yields ether via SN2; 3° halide yields alkene via E2.

Explanation: The initial step in acid-catalyzed dehydration is the rapid protonation of the oxygen atom of the alcohol.

Question 12 of 19

Which type of alkyl halide gives the highest yield of ether in Williamson synthesis?

Explanation: Primary alkyl halides offer minimal steric hindrance, facilitating smooth SN2 attack by alkoxide ions.

Question 13 of 19

Explanation: Sulfuric acid acts as a source of protons to activate the alcohol and dehydrates the system.

Question 14 of 19

Explanation: Sodium borohydride (NaBH4) reduces the organomercury intermediate to yield the ether product.

Question 16 of 19

Which of the following ethers cannot be prepared efficiently by Williamson synthesis?

Explanation: Di-tert-butyl ether cannot be prepared because both potential reactant paths involve tertiary alkyl groups which undergo elimination.

Question 17 of 19

Question 19 of 19

Explanation: Secondary alkyl halides like 2-bromopropane undergo E2 elimination with strong nucleophile/bases like sodium methoxide to yield propene.
