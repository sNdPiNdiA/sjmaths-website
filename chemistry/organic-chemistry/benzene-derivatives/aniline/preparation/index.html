Preparation of Aniline

Aniline is synthesized industrially and in the laboratory primarily through the catalytic or chemical reduction of nitrobenzene. The fundamental pathway involves the sequential transfer of electrons and protons to convert the nitro group into a primary aromatic amine.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Reduction Methods for Nitrobenzene

  • Béchamp reduction uses scrap iron and dilute HCl, generating catalytic FeCl2 in situ which minimizes acid consumption.
  • Catalytic hydrogenation utilizes hydrogen gas over Raney nickel, palladium, or platinum catalysts under pressure.
  • Chemical reduction using Sn/HCl or Zn/HCl yields an anilinium salt, which is subsequently neutralized with alkali to release free aniline.

Degradation and Nucleophilic Substitution Routes

  • Hofmann bromamide degradation converts benzamide to aniline with the loss of one carbonyl carbon atom, yielding a primary amine.
  • Ammonolysis of chlorobenzene requires harsh conditions (473 K and 60 atm) due to the partial double bond character of the C-Cl bond.
  • Reduction by ammonium sulfide selectively reduces one nitro group in dinitrobenzenes, yielding meta-nitroaniline.

Physical and Chemical Isolation Parameters

  • Pure aniline is a colourless oily liquid, but darkens on exposure to air and light due to oxidative resinification.
  • Aniline is sparingly soluble in water but readily soluble in organic solvents and dissolves in mineral acids to form water-soluble salts.
  • Steam distillation is employed to isolate aniline from reaction mixtures due to its immiscibility with water and high boiling point.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Catalytic Hydrogenation
C6H5NO2 + 3H2 →[Pd/C or Ni] C6H5NH2 + 2H2O
Conditions: High pressure and temperature over metal catalysts
Units: Reaction yield in percentage
Ammonolysis of Chlorobenzene
C6H5Cl + 2NH3 →[Cu2O, 473K, 60 atm] C6H5NH2 + NH4Cl
Conditions: High temperature, high pressure, copper(I) oxide catalyst
Units: Temperature in Kelvin, Pressure in atm
Hofmann Degradation of Benzamide
C6H5CONH2 + Br2 + 4NaOH → C6H5NH2 + Na2CO3 + 2NaBr + 2H2O
Conditions: Treatment of primary amide with aqueous or alcoholic NaOH and bromine
Units: Stoichiometric proportions
Béchamp Reduction
C6H5NO2 + 3Fe + 6HCl → C6H5NH2 + 3FeCl2 + 2H2O
Conditions: Refluxing nitrobenzene with scrap iron and hydrochloric acid
Units: Stoichiometric mole ratios
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aliphatic halides undergo easy nucleophilic substitution with ammonia to form amines.

Observation: Chlorobenzene does not react easily with aqueous ammonia under normal laboratory conditions. — Resonance stabilization of the C-Cl bond confers partial double bond character, increasing bond strength and decreasing reactivity.

Exception Direct nitration of aniline yields exclusively ortho- and para-substituted derivatives.

Observation: Direct nitration using concentrated HNO3 and H2SO4 yields a substantial amount of meta-nitroaniline. — Under strongly acidic nitration conditions, aniline is protonated to form the meta-directing anilinium ion.

Exception Primary amines react with nitrous acid to form stable aliphatic diazonium salts.

Observation: Primary aromatic amines yield stable diazonium salts only at 0 - 5 °C; higher temperatures yield phenols. — Lower temperatures prevent the thermal hydrolysis of the aromatic diazonium cation to form phenol and nitrogen gas.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Aniline has a molecular formula of C6H5NH2 and a molar mass of 93.13 g/mol.
  • Béchamp reduction uses catalytic amounts of FeCl2 because iron reacts with HCl to form FeCl2 and H2}.
  • Steam distillation is used to separate aniline from reaction mixtures because it is insoluble in water and boils at 457 K.
  • The industrial synthesis of aniline primarily relies on catalytic hydrogenation of nitrobenzene over copper or nickel catalysts.
  • Anilinium ion (C6H5NH3+) is meta-directing during electrophilic aromatic substitution.
  • Aniline turns brown upon storage due to auto-oxidation forming complex conjugated polymeric impurities.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming direct nitration of aniline gives high yields of ortho- and para-nitroaniline without protecting the amino group.
  • Forgetting that chlorobenzene requires high pressure (60 atm) and temperature (473 K) for conversion to aniline via ammonolysis.
  • Confusing the role of iron in Béchamp reduction as a stoichiometric reagent rather than a primary reductant coupled with catalytic HCl regeneration.
  • Neglecting the temperature range (0-5 °C) during diazotization, leading to unwanted phenol formation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation of Aniline

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Reduction Pathways

Nitrobenzene is reduced to aniline via multiple pathways including Béchamp reduction, catalytic hydrogenation, and metal-acid systems.

Degradation Reactions

Benzamide undergoes Hofmann bromamide degradation to yield aniline with loss of the carbonyl carbon atom.

Nucleophilic Substitution

Chlorobenzene reacts with aqueous ammonia under harsh thermodynamic conditions (Cu2O, 473 K, 60 atm) to produce aniline.

Isolation and Stability

Aniline is isolated via steam distillation and requires protection from light and air to prevent oxidative darkening.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Béchamp stoichiometry: C6H5NO2 + 3Fe + 6HCl → C6H5NH2 + 3FeCl2 + 2H2O
  • Catalytic hydrogenation uses H2 with Ni, Pd, or Pt catalysts.
  • Aniline is isolated via steam distillation.
  • Diazotization requires 0-5 °C temperature.
  • Direct nitration of aniline yields meta-nitroaniline due to protonation in acidic media.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Aniline is predominantly prepared by reducing nitrobenzene.
  • Iron and hydrochloric acid form the basis of the classic Béchamp reduction.
  • Catalytic hydrogenation provides a cleaner industrial route.
  • Hofmann degradation and ammonolysis offer alternative synthesis pathways.

⚖️ Key Differences

Béchamp Reduction VS Catalytic Hydrogenation

Béchamp uses Fe/HCl generating iron sludge, whereas catalytic hydrogenation uses H2 gas and metal catalysts without hazardous solid waste.

Which reagent system is used in the classic Béchamp reduction of nitrobenzene to aniline?

Explanation: Béchamp reduction specifically employs scrap iron and hydrochloric acid to reduce nitrobenzene to aniline.

Question 2 of 18

What is the primary reason for purifying aniline by steam distillation?

Explanation: Aniline is steam volatile, insoluble in water, and boils at a high temperature (457 K), making steam distillation ideal for separation.

Question 3 of 18

In the industrial ammonolysis of chlorobenzene to aniline, what catalyst and conditions are required?

Explanation: Chlorobenzene requires harsh conditions including copper(I) oxide, 473 K, and 60 atm pressure to react with ammonia.

Question 4 of 18

Which reaction achieves the conversion of benzamide into aniline with the loss of one carbon atom?

Explanation: Hofmann degradation treats an amide with bromine and sodium hydroxide, yielding a primary amine with one fewer carbon atom.

Question 5 of 18

Why does direct nitration of aniline with conc. HNO3/H2SO4 yield a significant amount of meta-nitroaniline?

Explanation: In strongly acidic nitration media, aniline gets protonated to form the meta-directing anilinium ion (C6H5NH3+).

Question 6 of 18

What is the colour change observed when pure aniline is exposed to air and light for a prolonged period?

Explanation: Aniline undergoes auto-oxidation upon exposure to air and light, producing coloured resinous oxidation products.

Question 7 of 18

Which metal-acid combination reduces nitrobenzene to phenylhydroxylamine as an intermediate before forming aniline?

Explanation: Neutral reduction of nitrobenzene using zinc dust and aqueous ammonium chloride stops at the phenylhydroxylamine stage.

Question 8 of 18

What is the correct stoichiometry of iron required per mole of nitrobenzene in the Béchamp reduction?

Explanation: The balanced Béchamp equation shows 3 moles of Fe are consumed per mole of nitrobenzene reduced.

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following methods yields primary aromatic amines without altering the carbon skeleton?

Explanation: Catalytic hydrogenation of nitrobenzene retains the exact carbon skeleton of the aromatic ring.

Question 10 of 18

What is the product formed when aniline reacts with sodium nitrite and hydrochloric acid at 0-5 °C?

Explanation: Primary aromatic amines undergo diazotization with HNO2 at 0-5 °C to form stable benzenediazonium salts.

Question 11 of 18

Why is chlorobenzene less reactive towards nucleophilic substitution compared to nitrochlorobenzene?

Explanation: Electron-withdrawing nitro groups activate the ring toward nucleophilic attack by stabilizing the carbanion intermediate.

Question 12 of 18

Which reagent selectively reduces one nitro group in 1,3-dinitrobenzene?

Explanation: Selective reduction of dinitrobenzenes to nitroanilines is achieved using sodium sulfide or ammonium sulfide.

Question 13 of 18

In the reduction of nitrobenzene using Sn and HCl, what is the immediate salt obtained before base treatment?

Explanation: Reduction with Sn/HCl produces anilinium chloride complex, which requires treatment with alkali to liberate free aniline.

Question 14 of 18

What is the role of FeCl2 generated during Béchamp reduction?

Explanation: FeCl2 hydrolyzes to release HCl, accelerating the dissolution of iron and sustaining the reduction cycle.

Question 15 of 18

Which functional group is converted to an amino group during the Hofmann degradation of amides?

Explanation: Hofmann degradation specifically converts primary carboxamides (-CONH2) into primary amines (-NH2).

Question 16 of 18

Which catalyst is commonly used in industrial vapour-phase hydrogenation of nitrobenzene?

Explanation: Vapour phase catalytic hydrogenation of nitrobenzene is efficiently carried out over supported copper or nickel catalysts.

Question 17 of 18

What is the physical state of pure aniline at room temperature and pressure?

Explanation: Pure aniline exists as a colourless, high-boiling oily liquid.

Question 18 of 18

Which of the following compounds is the starting material for preparing aniline via the Béchamp process?

Explanation: Béchamp reduction uses nitrobenzene as the starting nitroaromatic substrate.
