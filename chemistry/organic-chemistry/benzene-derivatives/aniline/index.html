Aniline

Aniline (benzenamine) is an aromatic primary amine consisting of a benzene ring substituted with an amino group. It exhibits basic character due to the lone pair on the nitrogen atom and undergoes rapid electrophilic aromatic substitution reactions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation and Reduction Methods

  • Aniline is industrially prepared by the catalytic hydrogenation of nitrobenzene over a nickel or copper catalyst.
  • Laboratory reduction of nitrobenzene is carried out using Sn and concentrated HCl to yield aniline hydrochloride, followed by treatment with alkali.
  • Electrolytic reduction of nitrobenzene in acidic medium yields p-aminophenol as a major rearrangement product (Bamberger rearrangement).

Chemical Reactivity and Electrophilic Substitution

  • The amino group in aniline is strongly activating and ortho/para-directing due to +R resonance effect of the nitrogen lone pair.
  • Direct halogenation (bromination) of aniline with bromine water yields a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline due to high electron density.
  • To obtain mono-brominated aniline, the amino group must be protected via acetylation with acetic anhydride prior to bromination.
  • Nitration of aniline in strong acid yields a significant amount of m-nitroaniline (nearly 47%) due to the formation of the anilinium ion which is meta-directing.

Distinctive Diagnostic Reactions

  • Primary aromatic amines react with nitrous acid at 0 - 5 °C to form stable arenediazonium salts.
  • The carbylamine test (isocyanide test) is specific for primary amines, yielding a foul-smelling phenyl isocyanide upon heating with chloroform and alcoholic KOH.
  • Azo coupling reactions of benzene diazonium chloride with phenol or naphthols yield brightly colored azo dyes in alkaline medium.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aliphatic amines are stronger bases than aromatic amines due to alkyl electron-donating inductive effects.

Observation: Aniline is a significantly weaker base than ammonia and aliphatic amines. — The lone pair on the nitrogen atom of aniline is delocalized into the benzene ring via resonance, reducing its availability for protonation, and the anilinium ion is less resonance-stabilized relative to neutral aniline.

Exception Friedel-Crafts alkylation and acylation are standard methods to introduce alkyl and acyl groups into aromatic rings.

Observation: Aniline fails to undergo Friedel-Crafts reactions. — Aniline forms a stable salt with the Lewis acid catalyst (AlCl3), rendering the nitrogen positively charged and strongly deactivating the aromatic ring.

Exception Nitration of activating aromatic compounds yields predominantly ortho and para isomers.

Observation: Direct nitration of aniline produces substantial meta-nitroaniline. — Under strongly acidic nitration conditions, aniline is protonated to the meta-directing anilinium ion (-NH3+).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Aniline reacts with nitrous acid at 0-5 °C to form benzenediazonium chloride.
  • Bromination of aniline without protection yields 2,4,6-tribromoaniline.
  • Acetanilide preparation uses acetic anhydride to moderate the activating effect of the amino group.
  • Carbylamine test detects primary amines by producing isocyanides with chloroform and KOH.
  • The pKb value of aniline is approximately 9.38, indicating weak basicity.
  • Coupling of benzenediazonium chloride with phenol yields an orange-red azo dye in alkaline medium.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming aniline undergoes Friedel-Crafts alkylation easily; remember that AlCl3 coordinates with the amino group.
  • Confusing direct nitration products of aniline; direct nitration yields meta-nitroaniline due to salt formation in acid.
  • Forgetting temperature control (0-5 °C) during diazotization, which leads to phenol formation via hydrolysis.
  • Overlooking the requirement of protecting the amino group during mono-halogenation or mono-nitration.

⚡ Quick Revision: Aniline

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Synthesis of Aniline

Aniline is predominantly produced via catalytic reduction of nitrobenzene using hydrogen gas over transition metal catalysts or via chemical reduction using Sn/HCl.

Basicity and Electronic Effects

The lone pair on nitrogen is delocalized into the aromatic ring via resonance, decreasing basicity compared to aliphatic amines.

Electrophilic Substitution Control

Due to extreme ring activation, targeted mono-substitution requires acetylation of the amino group to decrease electron density.

Diazonium Chemistry

Benzenediazonium salts serve as versatile synthetic intermediates for introducing diverse functional groups (halogens, cyano, hydroxyl, hydrogen) via replacement reactions.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Diazotization temperature: 0 - 5 °C
  • Aniline pKb: 9.38
  • Reagents for carbylamine test: CHCl3 + alc. KOH
  • Product of direct bromination: 2,4,6-tribromoaniline
  • Reagent for protecting amino group: (CH3CO)2O

📌 Quick Recall

  • Aniline is a weak base due to resonance delocalization of the nitrogen lone pair.
  • Direct electrophilic substitution results in trisubstituted products unless protected.
  • Diazonium salts derived from aniline undergo substitution and coupling reactions.
  • Diagnostic tests include the carbylamine test and dye test via diazotization.

⚖️ Key Differences

Direct Nitration of Aniline VS Nitration of Acetanilide

Direct nitration yields meta-nitroaniline (via anilinium ion), while nitration of acetanilide yields p-nitroacetanilide followed by hydrolysis to p-nitroaniline.

Why does aniline not undergo Friedel-Crafts reaction?

Explanation: The amino group reacts with Lewis acid AlCl3 to form a coordination complex, deactivating the ring.

Question 5 of 18

Explanation: Strongly acidic nitration conditions protonate aniline to the anilinium ion, which is meta-directing and deactivating.

Explanation: Acetic anhydride converts aniline to acetanilide, lowering the activation of the amino group.

Explanation: Resonance delocalization of the nitrogen lone pair into the aromatic ring reduces electron density on nitrogen.

Question 8 of 18

Explanation: Azo coupling between benzenediazonium chloride and phenol in basic medium yields p-hydroxyazobenzene (an azo dye).

Question 9 of 18

Explanation: Tin and concentrated hydrochloric acid reduce nitrobenzene directly to aniline hydrochloride, yielding aniline on basification.

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following compounds does NOT give the carbylamine test?

Explanation: N-methylaniline is a secondary amine, and the carbylamine test is exclusively given by primary amines.

Question 11 of 18

What is the IUPAC name of aniline?

Explanation: Benzenamine is the official IUPAC name, though phenylamine and aminobenzene are also widely accepted.

Question 12 of 18

Question 13 of 18

Explanation: Bromination of aniline yields 2,4,6-tribromoaniline as a characteristic white precipitate.

Question 14 of 18

Acetanilide on hydrolysis with dilute acid yields:

Explanation: Acid or base hydrolysis of acetanilide cleaves the amide bond to regenerate aniline and acetic acid.

Question 15 of 18

Which test distinguishes primary aromatic amines from secondary aromatic amines?

Explanation: Primary amines give the carbylamine test, whereas secondary and tertiary amines do not.

Question 16 of 18

What is the hybridization of nitrogen in aniline?

Explanation: The nitrogen atom in aniline is pyramidal with partial sp2 character due to resonance stabilization of the lone pair.

Question 17 of 18

Which method is used to convert nitrobenzene to aniline on an industrial scale?

Explanation: Catalytic hydrogenation using hydrogen gas over nickel or copper is the preferred industrial route.

Question 18 of 18

Benzenediazonium chloride reacts with hypophosphorous acid (H3PO2) to give:

Explanation: Reduction of diazonium salts with H3PO2 or ethanol replaces the diazo group with hydrogen, yielding benzene.

Which reagent replaces the diazonium group with -I?

Solution: Treatment of benzenediazonium chloride with potassium iodide (KI) directly yields iodobenzene.

Solution: Schotten-Baumann reaction of aniline with benzoyl chloride yields benzanilide.

Question 4 of 10
What is the hybridization state of the nitrogen atom in phenyl isocyanide formed in the carbylamine test?

Solution: Isocyanides contain a carbon-nitrogen triple bond where nitrogen is sp hybridized.

Question 5 of 10
Oxidation of aniline with acidified sodium dichromate yields:

Solution: Strong oxidation of aniline breaks down the ring system to yield p-benzoquinone as the principal colored product.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following is the weakest base?

Solution: Resonance stabilization of the unprotonated amine makes aniline a weaker base than ammonia and aliphatic amines.

Question 7 of 10
What product is obtained when benzenediazonium chloride is treated with CuCN/KCN?

Solution: Sandmeyer cyanation using CuCN yields benzonitrile.

Question 8 of 10
Which amine does not react with nitrous acid to form diazonium salts?

Solution: Aliphatic primary amines form unstable aliphatic diazonium salts that rapidly lose nitrogen to give carbocations and alcohols.

Question 9 of 10
In the Hinsberg test, aniline reacts with benzenesulfonyl chloride to form a product that is:

Solution: Primary amines form N-substituted sulfonamides containing an acidic hydrogen, making them soluble in aqueous alkali.
