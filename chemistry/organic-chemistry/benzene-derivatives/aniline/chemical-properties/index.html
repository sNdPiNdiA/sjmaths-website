Chemical Properties of Aniline

Aniline is an aromatic amine possessing a benzene ring directly attached to an amino group, exhibiting pronounced basic and nucleophilic character. The presence of the strongly activating -NH2 group directs electrophilic aromatic substitution predominantly to the ortho and para positions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Basic Character and Salt Formation

  • Aniline is a weaker base than aliphatic amines due to resonance delocalization of the lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom into the benzene ring.
  • Protonation of aniline yields the anilinium ion, which lacks resonance stabilization of the lone pair, making the free amine more stable than its conjugate acid.
  • Aniline reacts with strong mineral acids such as HCl to form stable anilinium chloride salts.
  • Electron-withdrawing substituents on the ring decrease basicity, whereas electron-donating groups increase the basic strength of aniline derivatives.

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution Reactions

  • The amino group is strongly activating and ortho/para-directing due to +M resonance effect, often yielding trisubstituted products (2,4,6-tribromoaniline) upon bromination with bromine water.
  • Direct nitration of aniline yields a complex mixture of tarry oxidation products alongside meta-nitroaniline due to protonation in strongly acidic medium forming the meta-directing anilinium ion.
  • To obtain mono-substituted derivatives like p-nitroaniline, the amino group is first protected via acetylation with acetic anhydride.
  • Sulfonation of aniline with concentrated sulfuric acid at high temperature yields sulfanilic acid, which exists predominantly as a internal salt known as a zwitterion.

Diagnostic Tests and Coupling Reactions

  • The carbylamine test involves heating primary amines like aniline with chloroform and alcoholic KOH to yield foul-smelling phenyl isocyanide.
  • Diazonium salts derived from aniline undergo azo coupling reactions with phenols and aromatic amines in mild alkaline or acidic media to yield brightly colored azo dyes.
  • Oxidation of aniline with acidified potassium dichromate produces aniline black, a polymeric oxidation product.
  • Diazotization of aniline requires precise temperature control between 0 °C and 5 °C to prevent decomposition of the resulting benzenediazonium chloride.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Primary amines react with nitrous acid to yield stable aliphatic diazonium salts.

Observation: Aniline yields benzenediazonium chloride which decomposes above 5 °C. — Aliphatic diazonium ions undergo rapid loss of nitrogen to form stable carbocations, whereas aromatic diazonium ions derive temporary stability from the resonance interaction with the benzene ring.

Exception Direct nitration of aromatic rings yields predominantly ortho and para isomers.

Observation: Direct nitration of aniline yields a significant amount of m-nitroaniline. — The strongly acidic nitric-sulfuric acid mixture protonates the -NH2 group to form the -NH3+ ion, which acts as a strongly electron-withdrawing meta-director via -I effect.

Exception Friedel-Crafts alkylation and acylation are standard methods for ring substitution on aromatic amines.

Observation: Aniline fails to undergo Friedel-Crafts reactions. — The Lewis acid catalyst (AlCl3) coordinates with the lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom of aniline, rendering the nitrogen positively charged and strongly deactivating the ring.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Aniline has a pKb value of approximately 9.4, making it a weaker base than ammonia (pKb = 4.75).
  • Acetylation of aniline protects the amino group and lowers its activating power during electrophilic substitution.
  • Reaction of aniline with bromine water yields a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline instantly without a catalyst.
  • Diazotization of aniline must be carried out strictly between 273 K and 278 K to prevent side-product formation.
  • Sulfanilic acid exists predominantly as a zwitterion (internal salt) due to proton transfer from the sulfonic acid group to the amino group.
  • Coupling of benzenediazonium chloride with phenol occurs in mildly alkaline medium to yield p-hydroxyazobenzene.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming aniline undergoes Friedel-Crafts alkylation smoothly; the AlCl3 catalyst coordinates with the nitrogen lone pair.
  • Forgetting that direct nitration of aniline yields meta-nitroaniline as a major byproduct due to anilinium ion formation.
  • Failing to maintain temperatures below 5 °C during diazotization, which results in phenol formation via hydrolysis.
  • Confusing the basicity order of amines in aqueous phase where steric and hydration effects alter the expected inductive trend.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Properties of Aniline

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Resonance and Basicity

The lone pair on the nitrogen atom of aniline participates in resonance with the aromatic ring, reducing electron density on nitrogen and decreasing basicity compared to aliphatic amines.

Electrophilic Substitution Control

Due to extreme activation by the -NH2 group, electrophilic substitution reactions require temporary deactivation via acetylation to control regioselectivity.

Diazonium Chemistry

Treatment with nitrous acid at 0-5 °C converts aniline to benzenediazonium chloride, a versatile intermediate for synthesizing diverse aryl derivatives via replacement and coupling.

Characteristic Identification

Primary aromatic amines are positively identified through the carbylamine test with chloroform/KOH and azo dye formation tests.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Zwitterion (Internal Salt)
Dipolar ion with both positive and negative localized charges, characteristic of sulfanilic acid.

Carbylamine (R-NC)
Isocyanide compound formed during reaction of primary amine with chloroform and base.
Acetylation (R-NHCOCH3)
Introduction of an acetyl group to protect amino functionalities.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Aniline is a weaker base than aliphatic amines due to resonance delocalization.
  • Diazotization requires 273-278 K; higher temperatures destroy the diazonium salt.
  • Friedel-Crafts reactions fail with aniline because AlCl3 coordinates with nitrogen.
  • Direct bromination of aniline yields 2,4,6-tribromoaniline instantly.
  • Acetylation reduces the activating effect of the amino group during electrophilic substitution.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Aniline exhibits both basic properties and high susceptibility to electrophilic aromatic substitution.
  • Substituents on the benzene ring significantly alter both base strength and substitution orientation.
  • Diazonium chemistry serves as a cornerstone for synthesizing substituted aromatic compounds.
  • Protection strategies are mandatory for controlled monosubstitution reactions.

⚖️ Key Differences

Direct Nitration of Aniline VS Nitration of Acetanilide

Direct nitration yields tar and m-nitroaniline due to salt formation, whereas acetanilide yields pure p-nitroacetanilide followed by hydrolysis.

Question 1 of 18

Why is aniline a weaker base than methylamine?

Explanation: Resonance delocalization of the lone pair onto the ortho and para positions of the benzene ring renders the nitrogen electron pair less available for protonation in aniline.

Question 2 of 18

What is the product formed when aniline reacts with sodium nitrite and hydrochloric acid at 0-5 °C?

Explanation: Primary aromatic amines react with nitrous acid at 0-5 °C to form stable benzenediazonium salts.

Question 3 of 18

Which reagent is used to convert aniline into acetanilide?

Explanation: Acetylation of aniline is typically carried out using acetic anhydride or acetyl chloride in the presence of a mild base.

Question 4 of 18

Why does aniline fail to undergo Friedel-Crafts reaction?

Explanation: Aluminum chloride, being a Lewis acid, coordinates with the lone pair of electrons on the aniline nitrogen, placing a positive charge on nitrogen which deactivates the ring.

Question 5 of 18

What is the main product of the reaction between aniline and bromine water at room temperature?

Explanation: The activating effect of the -NH2 group is so strong that reaction with bromine water yields the 2,4,6-tribrominated derivative instantaneously.

Question 6 of 18

Which test distinguishes primary amines from secondary and tertiary amines?

Explanation: Primary amines react with chloroform and alcoholic KOH to produce offensive-smelling isocyanides (carbylamine test).

Question 7 of 18

What is the physical state and appearance of freshly prepared pure aniline?

Explanation: Pure aniline is a colorless liquid, though it darkens upon exposure to air and light due to oxidation.

Question 8 of 18

What is formed when sulfanilic acid is dissolved in water?

Explanation: Sulfanilic acid has both an acidic (-SO3H) and a basic (-NH2) group, allowing proton transfer to form an internal zwitterion.

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following compounds is the strongest base in aqueous medium?

Explanation: Alkyl groups attached to nitrogen exert a +I inductive effect, increasing basicity. Tertiary and secondary aromatic amines often show modified basicities depending on steric and solvation factors.

Question 10 of 18

In the azo coupling reaction of benzenediazonium chloride with phenol, what is the color of the dye formed?

Explanation: Azo coupling yields extended conjugated systems known as azo dyes, with hydroxyazobenzene typically exhibiting an orange-yellow color.

Question 11 of 18

What is the primary reason for protecting the amino group of aniline prior to nitration?

Explanation: Protection via acetylation prevents the formation of the anilinium ion in acidic nitration medium, thereby avoiding meta-substitution and tar formation.

Question 12 of 18

What happens when aniline is treated with acidified potassium dichromate?

Explanation: Oxidation of aniline with strong oxidizing agents like acidic K2Cr2O7 yields a complex polymeric mixture known as aniline black.

Question 13 of 18

Which species acts as the electrophile in the diazotization of aniline?

Explanation: The active nitrosating agent in the reaction of NaNO2 and HCl is the nitrosonium (or nitrosyl) cation, NO+.

Question 14 of 18

What is the effect of an electron-donating group (such as -CH3) at the para position of aniline on its basicity?

Explanation: Electron-donating groups increase electron density on the nitrogen atom via inductive and hyperconjugative effects, thereby increasing basicity.

Question 15 of 18

Why is benzenediazonium chloride kept cold during chemical synthesis?

Explanation: Aryl diazonium salts are thermally unstable and readily decompose with the evolution of nitrogen gas above 5 °C.

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following describes the correct IUPAC name for aniline?

Explanation: Both benzenamine and phenylamine/aminobenzene are recognized by IUPAC nomenclature rules for aniline.

Question 17 of 18

What is the product when benzenediazonium chloride is treated with hypophosphorous acid (H3PO2)?

Explanation: Reduction of benzenediazonium chloride with H3PO2 or ethanol replaces the diazonium group with a hydrogen atom, yielding benzene.

Question 18 of 18

How does aniline react with benzoyl chloride in the presence of sodium hydroxide?

Explanation: Reaction of primary amines with benzoyl chloride in alkaline medium is known as the Schotten-Baumann reaction, yielding N-phenylbenzamide (benzanilide).

Question 1 of 10
Which functional group in aniline is responsible for its ortho- and para-directing nature in electrophilic substitution?

Solution: The amino group (-NH2) donates electrons through resonance (+M effect), activating the ortho and para positions of the benzene ring.

Question 2 of 10
What is the product when aniline is treated with benzenediazonium chloride in weakly acidic medium?

Solution: Azo coupling of benzenediazonium chloride with aniline in weak acid yields p-aminoazobenzene.

Question 3 of 10
Why does aniline not undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation?

Solution: Lewis acid catalysts like AlCl3 bind to the lone pair of nitrogen, creating a deactivating positive charge.

Question 4 of 10
What is observed when aniline reacts with bromine water?

Solution: Bromine water reacts rapidly with aniline to form an insoluble white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following correctly orders the basicity of ammonia, methylamine, and aniline in aqueous solution?

Solution: Alkyl groups increase basicity via +I effect, whereas resonance in aniline decreases basicity, making methylamine the strongest and aniline the weakest.

Question 6 of 10
What is the reagent required to replace the diazonium group of benzenediazonium chloride with a hydroxyl group (-OH)?

Solution: Heating benzenediazonium chloride in water (warm water hydrolysis) replaces the diazonium group with a hydroxyl group to form phenol.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following compounds is formed during the acetylation of aniline?

Solution: Acetylation of aniline with acetic anhydride yields N-phenylacetamide, commonly known as acetanilide.

Question 8 of 10
What is the hybridization of the nitrogen atom in aniline?

Solution: The nitrogen atom in aniline is sp2 hybridized due to the participation of its lone pair in resonance with the aromatic ring, making the amino group pyramidal with significant planar character.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following reactions is used to prepare fluorobenzene from aniline?

Solution: The Balz-Schiemann reaction involves treating a diazonium fluoroborate salt with heat to yield aryl fluorides such as fluorobenzene.

Question 10 of 10
What happens when aniline is exposed to air for a prolonged period?

Solution: Aniline is readily oxidized by atmospheric oxygen, causing stored samples to darken over time into red-brown or black liquids.
