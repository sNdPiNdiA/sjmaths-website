Preparation of Anisole

Anisole (methoxybenzene) is an aromatic ether synthesized predominantly via the methylation of sodium phenoxide using dimethyl sulfate or methyl iodide. It undergoes facile electrophilic aromatic substitution due to the strong +M activating effect of the methoxy group.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Williamson Synthesis Mechanism

  • Phenol is treated with aqueous sodium hydroxide to generate sodium phenoxide, which acts as a strong nucleophile.
  • The phenoxide ion attacks methyl iodide via an SN2 bimolecular nucleophilic substitution mechanism.
  • Polar aprotic solvents or anhydrous conditions prevent competing elimination reactions and hydrolysis of alkyl halides.

Methylation Using Dimethyl Sulfate

  • Phenol is reacted with dimethyl sulfate in the presence of an aqueous alkali (NaOH) under phase-transfer or Schotten-Baumann conditions.
  • Dimethyl sulfate acts as an efficient methylating agent transferring the methyl group via an alkyl-oxygen cleavage pathway.
  • This method is industrially favored due to higher atom economy and cost-effectiveness compared to alkyl iodides.

Properties and Direct Ring Activation

  • Anisole possesses a boiling point of 154 °C and exhibits distinct aromatic ether stability.
  • The -OCH3 group strongly activates the benzene ring toward electrophilic aromatic substitution via resonance stabilization.
  • Substitution predominantly occurs at the ortho and para positions due to lone pair delocalization from the ethereal oxygen.

Units: Stoichiometric proportions

Conditions: Catalytic presence of BF3 or anhydrous ether

Williamson Ether Synthesis for Anisole
C6H5O-Na+ + CH3I → C6H5OCH3 + NaI
Conditions: Reflux in polar aprotic solvent or dry ether
Units: Stoichiometric proportions (1:1 molar ratio)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl halides generally undergo E2 elimination with strong bases.

Observation: Methyl halides (like methyl iodide) undergo exclusively SN2 substitution without competing E2 elimination. — Methyl halides possess no beta-hydrogens, making elimination structurally impossible.

Exception Phenols react directly with alcohols to form ethers readily under mild conditions.

Observation: Direct condensation of phenol and methanol requires harsh acid catalysis and high thermal energy. — The phenolic C-O bond possesses partial double bond character due to resonance, making it resistant to cleavage.

Exception Ethers are generally inert towards dilute aqueous acids.

Observation: Anisole undergoes cleavage of the methyl-oxygen bond when heated with concentrated hydroiodic acid (HI). — Protonation of the ether oxygen followed by nucleophilic attack of I- yields iodomethane and phenol.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Williamson Reagent Match
Phenoxide + 1° Alkyl Halide

Application: Always select a primary alkyl halide (like CH3I) to synthesize unsymmetrical or simple aromatic ethers to prevent alkene formation.

Direct Methylation Memory
Phenol + (CH3)2SO4 / NaOH

Application: Recall that industrial preparation avoids expensive alkyl iodides in favor of dimethyl sulfate and alkali.

HI Cleavage Rule
Ar-O-CH3 + HI → Ar-OH + CH3I

Application: Cleavage of anisole with HI always yields iodomethane and phenol because phenyl-oxygen bond cleavage is unfavorable due to resonance stabilization.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Anisole is chemically known as methoxybenzene with formula C6H5OCH3.
  • Williamson ether synthesis involves the reaction of sodium phenoxide with methyl iodide.
  • The methoxy group (-OCH3) is ortho/para-directing and strongly activating in electrophilic aromatic substitution.
  • Cleavage of anisole with hot concentrated HI yields phenol and iodomethane.
  • Dimethyl sulfate in aqueous NaOH provides an economical industrial route for anisole synthesis.
  • Anisole does not react with metallic sodium to evolve hydrogen gas, distinguishing it from unreacted phenol.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Using tertiary alkyl halides in Williamson synthesis, which leads predominantly to alkene formation via E2 elimination instead of ethers.
  • Assuming phenyl halides can react with sodium methoxide under mild conditions (phenols/aryl halides lack reactivity in standard SN2 without activating groups).
  • Confusing the cleavage products of anisole with HI; remember that iodomethane is formed, not iodobenzene, due to stronger Ar-O bond energy.
  • Forgetting that sodium phenoxide must be prepared first by reacting phenol with NaOH before adding the alkyl halide.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation of Anisole

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Williamson Ether Synthesis Principle

Alkoxides or phenoxides react with primary alkyl halides via an SN2 pathway to form aryl alkyl ethers like anisole.

Industrial Methylation Agents

Dimethyl sulfate serves as a cost-effective methylating agent for converting sodium phenoxide into anisole in alkaline media.

Electronic Effects of Methoxy Group

The -OCH3 group exerts a strong +M effect, activating the aromatic ring toward electrophilic substitution at ortho and para positions.

Ether Cleavage Reactions

Reaction of anisole with concentrated hydroiodic acid breaks the alkyl-oxygen bond to release iodomethane and regenerate phenol.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Anisole (C6H5OCH3)
Aromatic ether formed by methylation of phenol, colorless liquid with a pleasant aroma.
Sodium Phenoxide (C6H5ONa)
Nucleophilic salt formed by treating phenol with sodium hydroxide.
Williamson Synthesis (SN2 Ether Formation)
Laboratory method for preparing ethers from alkoxides/phenoxides and primary alkyl halides.
Dimethyl Sulfate ((CH3O)2SO2)
Powerful methylating agent used in industrial preparation of anisole.
+M Effect (Positive Mesomeric Effect)
Electron-donating resonance effect exerted by substituents possessing lone pairs.
HI Cleavage (Ether Hydrolysis)
Reaction of ethers with hydroiodic acid yielding alkyl halides and alcohols/phenols.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Anisole formula: C6H5OCH3
  • Williamson synthesis follows an SN2 mechanism.
  • Primary alkyl halides are mandatory for high ether yields.
  • Cleavage of anisole with HI yields phenol and CH3I.
  • Methoxy group activates the benzene ring towards electrophilic substitution.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Anisole is synthesized by reacting sodium phenoxide with methylating agents like CH3I or (CH3O)2SO2.
  • The reaction proceeds via nucleophilic substitution where phenoxide attacks the methyl carbon.
  • Anisole undergoes facile electrophilic substitution reactions due to resonance activation by the methoxy group.
  • Ether cleavage with HI selectively produces iodomethane due to thermodynamic stability of the aryl-oxygen bond.

⚖️ Key Differences

Williamson Synthesis VS Esterification

Williamson synthesis couples phenoxide with alkyl halides to form ethers, whereas esterification couples acids with alcohols.

Which reagent combination is used in the Williamson synthesis of anisole?

Explanation: Williamson synthesis involves the nucleophilic attack of sodium phenoxide on primary alkyl halides like methyl iodide to yield anisole.

Question 2 of 19

What is the primary mechanism of the reaction between sodium phenoxide and methyl iodide?

Explanation: Methyl iodide is a primary alkyl halide and reacts with the strong nucleophile (phenoxide) via a bimolecular nucleophilic substitution (SN2) mechanism.

Question 3 of 19

Which compound is formed when anisole is heated with concentrated hydroiodic acid (HI)?

Explanation: Due to resonance stabilization of the aryl-oxygen bond, cleavage occurs at the alkyl-oxygen bond yielding phenol and iodomethane.

Question 4 of 19

What is the electronic effect of the methoxy group (-OCH3) in anisole on electrophilic aromatic substitution?

Explanation: The methoxy group exhibits a strong positive resonance (+M) effect and electron-donating inductive (+I) effect, activating the ring at ortho and para positions.

Question 5 of 19

Which industrial reagent is commonly substituted for methyl iodide in the large-scale preparation of anisole?

Explanation: Dimethyl sulfate is economically favored over alkyl iodides for industrial methylation of phenol in alkaline media.

Question 6 of 19

Why can tertiary alkyl halides not be used successfully in Williamson synthesis to form ethers?

Explanation: Steric hindrance in tertiary alkyl halides promotes elimination (E2) over substitution (SN2) in the presence of strong bases like alkoxides/phenoxides.

Question 7 of 19

What is the IUPAC name of anisole?

Explanation: According to IUPAC nomenclature, anisole is systematically named methoxybenzene.

Question 8 of 19

In the preparation of sodium phenoxide prior to Williamson synthesis, which base is typically used?

Explanation: Phenol is weakly acidic and reacts with aqueous sodium hydroxide to form water-soluble sodium phenoxide.

Question 9 of 19

What is the byproduct when phenol is methylated using dimethyl sulfate and sodium hydroxide?

Explanation: The reaction of phenol, dimethyl sulfate, and NaOH yields anisole, sodium methyl sulfate, and water as byproducts.

Question 10 of 19

Which of the following describes the nature of the C-O bond between the benzene ring and oxygen in anisole?

Explanation: Delocalization of lone pairs from oxygen into the aromatic ring imparts partial double bond character to the aryl-oxygen bond.

Question 11 of 19

What happens when anisole is treated with Br2 in ethanoic acid?

Explanation: Due to the activating ortho/para-directing methoxy group, bromination yields a mixture of 2-bromoanisole and 4-bromoanisole.

Question 12 of 19

Diazomethane reacts with phenol to yield anisole along with the evolution of which gas?

Explanation: Reaction of phenol with diazomethane (CH2N2) cleanly produces anisole and nitrogen gas (N2).

Question 13 of 19

Why does direct condensation of phenol and methanol require strong acid catalysts?

Explanation: The partial double bond character of the C-OH bond in phenol resists direct nucleophilic displacement, requiring acid catalysis and energy.

Question 14 of 19

Which analytical test can distinguish between phenol and anisole?

Explanation: Phenol gives a violet coloration with neutral FeCl3 and evolves H2 gas with sodium metal, whereas anisole does neither.

Question 15 of 19

What is the physical state and appearance of pure anisole at room temperature?

Explanation: Anisole is a colorless liquid possessing a characteristic pleasant, aromatic odor.

Question 16 of 19

Which solvent system is most appropriate for Williamson ether synthesis to maximize SN2 rates?

Explanation: Polar aprotic solvents or dry ether do not solvate the nucleophile (phenoxide) as strongly as protic solvents, thereby enhancing its SN2 nucleophilicity.

Question 17 of 19

Friedel-Crafts acylation of anisole with acetyl chloride in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3 yields predominantly:

Explanation: Friedel-Crafts acylation of anisole introduces the acyl group at the activated ortho and para positions.

Question 18 of 19

In the cleavage of anisole by HI, why is iodomethane formed instead of iodobenzene?

Explanation: The resonance stabilization of the Ar-O bond prevents cleavage on the phenyl side, directing attack at the methyl carbon.

Question 19 of 19

Which functional group class does anisole belong to?

Explanation: Anisole features an oxygen atom bonded to an aromatic phenyl ring and an aliphatic methyl group, classifying it as an aromatic ether.
