Anisole

Anisole (methoxybenzene, C6H5OCH3) is an aromatic ether characterized by an electron-donating methoxy substituent attached to a benzene ring. Its chemical reactivity is dominated by the strong +M effect of the -OCH3 group, which activates the ring toward electrophilic aromatic substitution.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation and Physical Properties

  • Anisole is prepared via the Williamson ether synthesis by reacting sodium phenoxide with methyl iodide.
  • It is a colorless liquid with a characteristic pleasant odor, insoluble in water but miscible with organic solvents like ethanol and ether.
  • The boiling point of anisole (155 °C) is significantly lower than that of phenol due to the absence of intermolecular hydrogen bonding.

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution

  • The +M effect of the methoxy group increases electron density predominantly at the ortho and para positions of the aromatic ring.
  • Nitration of anisole yields a mixture of o-nitroanisole and p-nitroanisole, with the para isomer predominating.
  • Bromination occurs readily even in the absence of a Lewis acid catalyst like FeBr3, yielding p-bromoanisole as the major product.

Cleavage of Ether Linkage

  • Reaction of anisole with hot concentrated HI yields phenol and methyl iodide because of the preferential protonation of oxygen and formation of stable oxonium species.
  • Methyl iodide is formed rather than iodobenzene because the C(sp2)-O bond in the phenyl group has partial double bond character and is stronger than the C(sp3)-O bond.
  • Reaction with excess HI at high temperatures converts phenol further into iodobenzene via a multi-step pathway.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Cleavage by Hydrogen Iodide
C6H5OCH3 + HI → C6H5OH + CH3I
Conditions: Heated with concentrated hydriodic acid
Units: Temperature dependent (reflux)
Nitration of Anisole
C6H5OCH3 + HNO3 + H2SO4 → o-/p-C6H4(OCH3)(NO2) + H2O
Conditions: Low temperature using concentrated nitric and sulfuric acids
Units: Product ratio favored at para position
Friedel-Crafts Acetylation
C6H5OCH3 + CH3COCl → CH3COC6H4OCH3 + HCl
Conditions: Presence of anhydrous Lewis acid catalyst (AlCl3) in CS2 solvent
Units: Molar equivalents
Williamson Ether Synthesis
C6H5O- Na+ + CH3I → C6H5OCH3 + NaI
Conditions: Reaction of sodium phenoxide with methyl iodide in anhydrous medium
Units: Stoichiometric proportions (1:1 molar ratio)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Ethers generally undergo acid-catalyzed cleavage to yield two alkyl halides.

Observation: Anisole yields phenol and an alkyl halide (methyl iodide) upon cleavage with HI. — The partial double bond character between the aromatic ring and oxygen prevents the cleavage of the phenyl-oxygen bond, leaving the phenol moiety intact.

Exception Electrophilic aromatic substitution of benzene rings requires strong Lewis acid catalysts (e.g., FeBr3).

Observation: Bromination of anisole proceeds readily with bromine water in the absence of a catalyst. — The strong +M activation by the methoxy group increases the nucleophilicity of the benzene ring sufficiently to react with weak electrophiles.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Ortho/Para Directing Rule for Anisole
+M group → o/p directing

Application: Always predict major electrophilic substitution products at the para position due to lower steric hindrance compared to the ortho position.

HI Cleavage Product Rule
Ar-O-R + HI → Ar-OH + R-I

Application: The alkyl group (R) forms the alkyl iodide, while the aryl group (Ar) retains the oxygen to form phenol.

Friedel-Crafts Catalyst Choice
Anisole + RCOCl / Anhydrous AlCl3

Application: Anisole easily undergoes Friedel-Crafts reactions without requiring harsh conditions because the ring is already strongly activated.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Anisole IUPAC name is methoxybenzene with formula C6H5OCH3.
  • Prepared via Williamson synthesis using sodium phenoxide and methyl iodide.
  • Cleavage by HI produces phenol and iodomethane (methyl iodide).
  • Methoxy group exerts a strong +M effect, activating ortho and para positions.
  • Nitration gives a mixture of ortho-nitroanisole and para-nitroanisole.
  • Friedel-Crafts acylation of anisole yields 4-methoxyacetophenone as the major product.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the cleavage products of anisole with HI, mistakenly producing iodobenzene and methanol instead of phenol and iodomethane.
  • Forgetting that anisole does not form intermolecular hydrogen bonds despite containing oxygen.
  • Assuming Friedel-Crafts alkylation or acylation fails on anisole due to catalyst complexation; while complexation occurs, the ring remains activated.
  • Predicting meta-substitution for anisole instead of ortho/para substitution.

⚡ Quick Revision: Anisole

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Synthesis of Anisole

Synthesized efficiently via Williamson ether synthesis by reacting sodium phenoxide with methyl halides under standard conditions.

Electronic Effects

The methoxy group exhibits a strong +M effect and a weak -I effect, leading to overall ring activation and ortho/para orientation.

Ether Cleavage Reactions

Reaction with HI selectively cleaves the alkyl-oxygen bond due to partial double bond character of the aryl-oxygen bond, yielding phenol.

Electrophilic Substitution

Undergoes rapid nitration, halogenation, sulfonation, and Friedel-Crafts reactions without requiring extreme conditions.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Anisole (C6H5OCH3)

+M Effect (Mesomeric Effect)
Electron-donating resonance effect that activates the benzene ring.
Williamson Synthesis (R-O- + R'-X)
Standard method for ether formation using alkoxide and alkyl halide.
Cleavage (C-O Bond Breaking)
Reaction with HI breaking the ether linkage into phenol and alkyl iodide.
Friedel-Crafts Acylation (ArH + RCOCl → ArCOR)
Introduction of an acyl group into the aromatic ring.
Methoxy Group (-OCH3)
Activating substituent directing incoming groups to ortho/para positions.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Anisole formula: C6H5OCH3
  • Cleavage product with HI: Phenol + CH3I
  • Activating group nature: +M effect directs ortho/para
  • Boiling point: 155 °C
  • Williamson synthesis reagent: Sodium phenoxide + CH3I

📌 Quick Recall

  • Anisole is an aromatic ether with a high electron density at ortho and para positions.
  • It undergoes facile electrophilic substitution reactions compared to benzene.
  • Cleavage with HI selectively produces methyl iodide and phenol.
  • It lacks intermolecular hydrogen bonding, resulting in a lower boiling point than phenol.

⚖️ Key Differences

Phenol VS Anisole

Phenol exhibits strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding and acidic character, whereas anisole is neutral and lacks hydrogen bonding.
