Chemical Properties of Phenol

Phenol exhibits distinct chemical properties due to the presence of a hydroxyl group directly attached to an sp2 hybridized carbon of an aromatic ring. The resonance stabilization of the phenoxide ion makes phenol significantly more acidic than aliphatic alcohols.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Acidity of Phenol and Salt Formation

  • Phenol reacts with active metals like sodium to evolve hydrogen gas and form sodium phenoxide.
  • The phenoxide ion is stabilized by resonance, dispersing the negative charge over the ortho and para positions of the benzene ring.
  • Phenols dissolve in sodium hydroxide solutions, whereas aliphatic alcohols generally do not due to weaker acidity.
  • Electron-withdrawing substituents at ortho and para positions increase acidity, while electron-donating groups decrease it.

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution Reactions

  • The hydroxyl group is strongly ortho/para-directing and activates the aromatic ring toward electrophilic substitution.
  • Bromination with bromine water yields 2,4,6-tribromophenol as a white precipitate without needing a Lewis acid catalyst.
  • Nitration with dilute nitric acid at room temperature yields a mixture of ortho-nitrophenol and para-nitrophenol.
  • Sulphonation yields ortho-phenolsulphonic acid at low temperatures and para-phenolsulphonic acid at higher temperatures.

Name Reactions and Oxidative Cleavage

  • The Reimer-Tiemann reaction introduces an aldehyde group at the ortho position using chloroform and aqueous NaOH.
  • The Kolbe reaction converts sodium phenoxide into salicylic acid using carbon dioxide under pressure.
  • Oxidation of phenol with chromic anhydride (CrO3) yields benzoquinone as a conjugated diketone.
  • Distillation of phenol with zinc dust reduces the hydroxyl group, yielding benzene and zinc oxide.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alcohols react with aqueous NaOH to form sodium alkoxides

Observation: Aliphatic alcohols do not react with aqueous NaOH — Alcohols are weaker acids than water, shifting the equilibrium backward, whereas phenol is a stronger acid than bicarbonate.

Exception Phenol undergoes mononitration easily under harsh conditions

Observation: Phenol undergoes nitration with dilute HNO3 at 298 K — The hydroxyl group strongly activates the benzene ring, making it reactive toward weak electrophiles.

Exception Friedel-Crafts alkylation and acylation work efficiently on activated aromatic rings

Observation: Phenols fail to undergo standard Friedel-Crafts reactions — The phenolic oxygen forms a stable complex with the anhydrous AlCl3 catalyst, deactivating the ring.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Ortho vs Para Separation Rule
Steam Volatility Rule

Application: Use steam distillation to separate ortho-nitrophenol (steam volatile due to intramolecular hydrogen bonding) from para-nitrophenol (intermolecular hydrogen bonding, less volatile).

Electrophilic Power Mnemonic
O-P-A-D (Ortho-Para Activating Directing)

Application: Remember that -OH, -OR, -NH2 direct incoming electrophiles exclusively to ortho and para positions due to +M effect.

Zinc Dust Reduction
Phenol + Zn → Benzene + ZnO

Application: Whenever a conversion requires stripping oxygen off an aromatic ring, use zinc dust distillation.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Phenol turns neutral FeCl3 solution intense violet due to formation of a coordination complex.
  • The Reimer-Tiemann reaction proceeds via the generation of dichlorocarbene (:CCl2) intermediate.
  • Intramolecular hydrogen bonding in o-nitrophenol lowers its boiling point compared to p-nitrophenol.
  • Kolbe's reaction synthesizes 2-hydroxybenzoic acid (salicylic acid) from sodium phenoxide and CO2.
  • Oxidation of phenol with acidified sodium dichromate yields conjugated 1,4-benzoquinone.
  • Coupling reaction of phenol with benzenediazobenzene chloride in mild alkaline medium yields p-hydroxyazobenzene (orange dye).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the role of anhydrous AlCl3 in Friedel-Crafts reactions with phenol, where it coordinates with oxygen instead of acting as a pure catalyst.
  • Assuming phenol liberates CO2 from sodium bicarbonate; phenol is weaker than carbonic acid and does not react with NaHCO3.
  • Mistaking the mechanism of Reimer-Tiemann reaction as a standard carbocation electrophilic substitution instead of carbene insertion/attack.
  • Forgetting that bromination of phenol with bromine water yields tribromophenol without needing a Lewis acid catalyst.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Properties of Phenol

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Acidity and Resonance

Phenols are acidic because the phenoxide ion is stabilized by delocalization of the negative charge over the benzene ring.

Electrophilic Substitution

The hydroxyl group activates ortho and para positions through resonance (+M effect) and inductive withdrawal (-I effect).

Named Reactions

Kolbe-Schmitt and Reimer-Tiemann reactions introduce carboxylic acid and formyl groups respectively into the phenolic ring.

Oxidation and Reduction

Phenols oxidize to quinones easily and reduce back to benzene upon distillation with zinc dust.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Phenol gives violet coloration with neutral FeCl3.
  • pKa of phenol is approximately 9.95.
  • Reimer-Tiemann uses CHCl3 + NaOH to form ortho-hydroxybenzaldehyde.
  • Kolbe reaction uses CO2 + NaOH to form salicylic acid.
  • Zinc dust converts phenol to benzene.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Phenols are stronger acids than alcohols due to resonance stabilization of phenoxide ion.
  • The -OH group is strongly activating and ortho/para-directing.
  • Electrophilic substitutions occur readily under mild conditions.
  • Oxidative and reductive pathways yield quinones and benzene respectively.

⚖️ Key Differences

Alcohol acidity VS Phenol acidity

Phenols are much more acidic than aliphatic alcohols due to sp2 carbon attachment and resonance stabilization.

Explanation: Distillation of phenol with zinc dust reduces the phenolic OH group to a hydrogen atom, yielding benzene and zinc oxide.

Question 2 of 19

Phenol reacts with bromine water to give:

Explanation: The strong activating effect of the -OH group directs bromine into all available ortho and para positions, yielding 2,4,6-tribromophenol.

Question 3 of 19

The intermediate formed in the Reimer-Tiemann reaction of phenol is:

Explanation: Chloroform reacts with aqueous base to generate dichlorocarbene (:CCl2), which acts as the electrophile.

Question 4 of 19

Salicylic acid is prepared from phenol by:

Explanation: Kolbe-Schmitt reaction involves the carboxylation of sodium phenoxide with carbon dioxide to yield salicylic acid.

Question 5 of 19

Why is phenol more acidic than ethanol?

Explanation: The negative charge on the oxygen of the phenoxide ion is delocalized over the benzene ring via resonance, increasing its stability.

Question 6 of 19

Which compound gives a violet color with neutral FeCl3 solution?

Explanation: Phenols react with neutral iron(III) chloride to form colored coordination complexes, typically violet or purple.

Question 7 of 19

Nitration of phenol with dilute nitric acid at 298 K yields primarily:

Explanation: Mild nitration using dilute nitric acid yields a mixture of ortho-nitrophenol and para-nitrophenol.

Question 8 of 19

Which group increases the acidity of phenol most effectively when placed at the para position?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is a strong electron-withdrawing group via both -I and -M effects, stabilizing the phenoxide ion.

Question 9 of 19

Oxidation of phenol with chromic anhydride yields:

Explanation: Chromic anhydride oxidizes phenol directly to conjugated 1,4-benzoquinone.

Question 10 of 19

Which of the following does not react with phenol?

Explanation: Phenol is a weaker acid than carbonic acid and therefore does not liberate carbon dioxide from sodium bicarbonate.

Question 11 of 19

The boiling point of o-nitrophenol is lower than p-nitrophenol because of:

Explanation: o-Nitrophenol forms intramolecular hydrogen bonds, preventing association between molecules and causing higher volatility.

Question 12 of 19

Coupling reaction of phenol with benzene diazonium chloride occurs in which medium?

Explanation: The coupling reaction requires a mildly alkaline medium to convert phenol into phenoxide ion, which is more reactive toward diazonium cations.

Question 13 of 19

The hybridization of oxygen in phenol is approximately:

Explanation: The phenolic oxygen atom is surrounded by two lone pairs and two single bonds, indicating sp3 hybridization with partial double bond character due to resonance.

Question 14 of 19

Fries rearrangement involves the conversion of:

Explanation: Fries rearrangement is the Lewis acid-catalyzed rearrangement of phenolic esters to ortho and para hydroxyketones.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following compounds is also known as carbolic acid?

Explanation: Phenol was historically isolated from coal tar and named carbolic acid due to its acidic and antiseptic nature.

Question 16 of 19

What is the product when phenol is treated with excess concentrated HNO3 and concentrated H2SO4?

Explanation: Treatment of phenol with concentrated nitric and sulfuric acid results in exhaustive nitration to form 2,4,6-trinitrophenol, known as picric acid.

Question 17 of 19

Why do phenols fail to undergo standard Friedel-Crafts alkylation?

Explanation: The Lewis acid catalyst (AlCl3) coordinates with the oxygen lone pair of phenol, deactivating the catalyst and preventing substitution.

Question 18 of 19

Which reagent converts phenol to phenyl acetate?

Explanation: Phenol reacts with acid chlorides like acetyl chloride in the presence of an aqueous base (Schotten-Baumann reaction) to form esters.

Question 19 of 19

The color of the dye formed during the coupling reaction of phenol with diazonium salts is typically:

Explanation: Azo dyes formed by coupling phenols with diazonium compounds typically exhibit intense orange or red coloration due to extended conjugation.
