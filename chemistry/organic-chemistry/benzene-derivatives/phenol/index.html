Organic Chemistry

Phenol

Phenol is an aromatic organic compound containing a hydroxyl group directly attached to a benzene ring, possessing the chemical formula C6H5OH. It exhibits significant acidity compared to aliphatic alcohols due to the resonance stabilization of the resulting phenoxide ion.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Acidity of Phenol and Resonance Effects

  • Phenol is a weaker acid than mineral acids but significantly stronger acid than aliphatic alcohols.
  • The phenoxide ion (C6H5O-) is stabilized by delocalization of the negative charge into the benzene ring via resonance.
  • Electron-withdrawing substituents (e.g., -NO2) at ortho and para positions increase acidity, while electron-donating groups (e.g., -CH3) decrease acidity.
  • The pKa value of phenol is approximately 9.95, whereas ethanol has a pKa of about 15.9.

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution Reactions

  • The hydroxyl group is strongly activating and ortho/para-directing due to +R resonance effect.
  • Reaction with dilute nitric acid at 298 K yields a mixture of ortho-nitrophenol and para-nitrophenol.
  • Treatment with bromine water without catalyst immediately yields 2,4,6-tribromophenol as a white precipitate.
  • Sulfonation yields ortho-phenolsulfonic acid at low temperatures (293 K) and para-phenolsulfonic acid at higher temperatures (373 K).

Name Reactions and Distinctive Identifications

  • Kolbe's Reaction: Carboxylation of sodium phenoxide using carbon dioxide produces salicylic acid.
  • Reimer-Tiemann Reaction: Formylation of phenol using chloroform and sodium hydroxide yields salicylaldehyde via a dichlorocarbene intermediate.
  • Coupling Reaction: Phenol reacts with benzenediazonium chloride in mild alkaline medium to form p-hydroxyazobenzene dye.
  • Neutral FeCl3 Test: Phenol reacts with neutral iron(III) chloride to form a characteristic violet, purple, or green colored complex.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alcohols react with hydrogen halides via C-O bond cleavage to form alkyl halides.

Observation: Phenols do not readily undergo substitution of the -OH group with halogen acids under normal conditions. — Partial double bond character of the C-O bond in phenol arises from resonance interaction with the aromatic ring.

Exception Friedel-Crafts alkylation and acylation of phenols proceed smoothly with Lewis acid catalysts.

Observation: Phenols fail to give standard yields in Friedel-Crafts reactions. — The Lewis acid catalyst (e.g., AlCl3) coordinates strongly with the oxygen lone pair of phenol, deactivating the catalyst.

Exception Esterification of alcohols uses acid catalysts directly.

Observation: Phenols cannot be efficiently esterified using carboxylic acids directly. — Low nucleophilicity of phenolic oxygen requires conversion of phenol to phenoxide or use of more reactive acid chlorides/anhydrides.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Directing Group Mnemonic
O-P Activating Rule

Application: Remember that groups with lone pairs on the atom attached directly to the ring (-OH, -OR, -NH2) are ortho/para directors and strong activators.

Reimer-Tiemann vs Kolbe Product Trick
CHCl3 = Aldehyde (-CHO), CO2 = Acid (-COOH)

Application: Chloroform adds one carbon with oxygen (aldehyde), whereas carbon dioxide adds a carboxyl group (acid).

Acidity Comparison Shortcut
NO2 > Halogen > H > CH3

Application: Electron withdrawing groups increase acidity (lower pKa), electron releasing groups decrease acidity (higher pKa).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Phenol is also known as carbolic acid due to its weak acidic nature.
  • Cumene oxidation is the industrial method for synthesizing phenol along with acetone.
  • Bromination of phenol in CS2 at low temperature yields a mixture of o-bromophenol and p-bromophenol.
  • Reduction of phenol with zinc dust yields benzene.
  • Oxidation of phenol with chromic acid (Na2Cr2O7 / H2SO4) produces benzoquinone.
  • The C-O bond length in phenol is 136 pm, shorter than 142 pm in methanol due to resonance.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming phenol turns blue litmus red strongly like mineral acids; it only turns blue litmus red weakly.
  • Confusing the electrophile in Reimer-Tiemann reaction as CCl3+ instead of dichlorocarbene (:CCl2).
  • Writing mono-bromination of phenol using bromine water; bromine water always yields 2,4,6-tribromophenol.
  • Believing phenols undergo easy substitution of the hydroxyl group with HCl like tertiary alcohols.

⚡ Quick Revision: Phenol

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

The carbon-oxygen bond in phenol involves sp2 hybridization of the aromatic carbon, imparting partial double bond character and restricted bond rotation.

Acidity of Phenols

Phenols dissociate in water to form resonance-stabilized phenoxide ions, making them significantly more acidic than aliphatic alcohols but less acidic than carboxylic acids.

Electrophilic Substitution

The activating and ortho/para-directing nature of the phenolic -OH group facilitates rapid halogenation, nitration, sulfonation, and Friedel-Crafts-like reactions.

Key Name Reactions

Important synthetic transformations include the Reimer-Tiemann reaction for formylation, Kolbe-Schmitt reaction for carboxylation, and coupling reactions for azo dyes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Phenol gives violet color with neutral FeCl3.
  • Bromine water gives 2,4,6-tribromophenol white precipitate.
  • Zinc dust reduction of phenol gives benzene.
  • Cumene process yields phenol and acetone.
  • Electron withdrawing groups increase phenol acidity.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Phenols are aromatic hydroxyl compounds with enhanced acidity due to resonance.
  • The -OH group strongly activates the benzene ring towards electrophilic substitution at ortho and para positions.
  • Industrial preparation is dominated by the cumene hydroperoxide method.
  • Distinctive chemical tests include FeCl3 complexation and bromine water precipitation.

⚖️ Key Differences

Alcohol acidity VS Phenol acidity

Phenols are much more acidic than alcohols because the phenoxide ion is resonance stabilized whereas the alkoxide ion is not.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following is most acidic?

Explanation: p-Nitrophenol contains an electron-withdrawing nitro group at the para position, which strongly stabilizes the phenoxide ion via -R and -I effects, making it more acidic than phenol.

Question 2 of 18

What is the product formed when phenol is treated with zinc dust?

Explanation: Heating phenol with zinc dust causes reduction, where the oxygen atom is removed by zinc to form benzene and zinc oxide.

Question 3 of 18

The intermediate formed in the Reimer-Tiemann reaction of phenol is:

Explanation: Reaction of chloroform with base generates dichlorocarbene (:CCl2), which acts as the electrophile in the Reimer-Tiemann reaction.

Question 4 of 18

Phenol reacts with neutral FeCl3 to give:

Explanation: Phenols form iron(III) complexes with neutral FeCl3 resulting in a characteristic violet, purple, blue, or green coloration.

Question 5 of 18

Which reagent converts phenol to benzoquinone?

Explanation: Oxidation of phenol with chromic acid yields conjugated 1,4-benzoquinone.

Question 6 of 18

In the Kolbe-Schmitt reaction, sodium phenoxide is treated with:

Explanation: Kolbe-Schmitt reaction involves carboxylation of sodium phenoxide using carbon dioxide under pressure to yield salicylic acid.

Question 7 of 18

Bromination of phenol with bromine water gives:

Explanation: Due to strong activation by the -OH group, aqueous bromine reacts instantly to substitute all available ortho and para positions, yielding 2,4,6-tribromophenol.

Question 8 of 18

Which of the following groups decreases the acidity of phenol when placed at the ortho position?

Explanation: Methyl (-CH3) is an electron-donating group via hyperconjugation and +I effect, which destabilizes the phenoxide ion and decreases acidity.

Question 9 of 18

The industrial preparation of phenol uses cumene. Cumene is chemically:

Explanation: Cumene is isopropylbenzene, which is oxidized to cumene hydroperoxide and subsequently cleaved by acid to phenol and acetone.

Question 10 of 18

Phenolphthalein is prepared by condensing phenol with:

Explanation: Phenolphthalein is synthesized by the condensation of two moles of phenol with one mole of phthalic anhydride in the presence of concentrated sulfuric acid.

Question 11 of 18

Why is the C-O bond in phenol shorter than in methanol?

Explanation: Resonance structures of phenol show a partial double bond character between the carbon of the benzene ring and oxygen, shortening the bond length.

Question 12 of 18

Nitration of phenol with dilute nitric acid at low temperature mainly yields:

Explanation: Nitration with dilute nitric acid at 298 K yields a mixture of ortho-nitrophenol and para-nitrophenol due to the directing effect of the -OH group.

Question 13 of 18

Ortho-nitrophenol is more volatile than para-nitrophenol due to:

Explanation: Ortho-nitrophenol forms intramolecular hydrogen bonding, reducing association between molecules and lowering boiling point, making it more volatile.

Question 14 of 18

Coupling reaction of phenol with benzenediazonium chloride occurs in which medium?

Explanation: The coupling reaction requires a mildly alkaline medium to convert phenol into phenoxide ion, which is a stronger nucleophile for electrophilic attack by diazonium cations.

Question 15 of 18

What is the primary product of the reaction between phenol and concentrated sulfuric acid at 373 K?

Explanation: Sulfonation of phenol is reversible; at higher temperatures (373 K), the thermodynamically more stable para-isomer (phenol-4-sulfonic acid) predominates.

Question 16 of 18

Which catalyst deactivates phenols in Friedel-Crafts reactions due to complex formation?

Explanation: Anhydrous AlCl3 coordinates strongly with the oxygen lone pair of phenol, preventing successful Friedel-Crafts alkylation or acylation.

Question 17 of 18

Picric acid is chemically known as:

Explanation: Picric acid is 2,4,6-trinitrophenol, a strong acid synthesized by nitration of phenol.

Question 18 of 18

Ether formation from phenol using alkyl halides in the presence of base is known as:

Explanation: Phenoxide ions react with alkyl halides via SN2 mechanism to form alkoxybenzenes (anisole derivatives), representing Williamson ether synthesis.

Question 1 of 10
Which reagent is used to convert cumene into cumene hydroperoxide in the industrial synthesis of phenol?

Solution: Cumene oxidation utilizes atmospheric oxygen (air) in an auto-oxidation process to form cumene hydroperoxide.

Question 2 of 10
Why is o-nitrophenol steam volatile while p-nitrophenol is not?

Solution: Intramolecular hydrogen bonding in ortho-nitrophenol prevents intermolecular association, resulting in higher volatility and steam distillability.

Question 3 of 10
The conversion of phenyl allyl ether to 2-allylphenol on heating is an example of:

Solution: Thermal rearrangement of allyl aryl ethers to ortho-allylphenols is known as the Claisen rearrangement.

Question 4 of 10
Fries rearrangement involves the conversion of:

Solution: Fries rearrangement is the Lewis acid-catalyzed conversion of phenolic esters into ortho- and para-hydroxyaryl ketones.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following compounds does not evolve hydrogen gas with sodium metal?

Solution: Methoxybenzene (anisole) lacks an acidic hydrogen attached to an electronegative oxygen atom and does not react with sodium metal to evolve hydrogen.

Question 6 of 10
The electrophile in the Kolbe-Schmitt reaction is:

Solution: Carbon dioxide acts as a weak electrophile attacking the strongly nucleophilic phenoxide ion ring.

Question 7 of 10
Phenol can be distinguished from ethanol using:

Solution: Neutral FeCl3 gives a colored complex with phenol but shows no reaction with ethanol.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following substituents increases the pKa value of phenol most?

Solution: An increase in pKa implies a decrease in acidity. Electron-donating methyl groups decrease acidity and thus increase pKa.

Question 9 of 10
When phenol is reacted with acetyl chloride in the presence of pyridine, the product is:

Solution: Acetylation of phenol with acetyl chloride in base yields phenyl acetate ester (Schotten-Baumann reaction principle).

Question 10 of 10
What is the hybridization of the oxygen atom in phenol?

Solution: The oxygen atom in phenol is bonded to carbon via a single bond and has lone pairs involved in resonance, adopting sp2 hybridization geometry.
