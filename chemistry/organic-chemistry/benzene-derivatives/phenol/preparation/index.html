Preparation of Phenol

Phenol is synthesized industrially and in the laboratory via methods including the alkaline fusion of sodium benzenesulfonate, hydrolysis of diazonium salts, and the cumene oxidation process. These pathways convert aromatic hydrocarbon derivatives into hydroxybenzenes through nucleophilic substitution and rearrangement mechanisms.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Industrial Syntheses of Phenol

  • The Cumene process generates both phenol and acetone simultaneously via a hydroperoxide intermediate under catalytic air oxidation.
  • The Dow process utilizes chlorobenzene reacting with aqueous sodium hydroxide under extreme conditions of 623 K and 300 atm via a benzyne intermediate.
  • Alkali fusion of sodium benzenesulfonate involves high-temperature reaction with molten sodium hydroxide to yield sodium phenoxide.

Laboratory Preparations from Amines and Acids

  • Primary aromatic amines react with nitrous acid (HNO2 at 273-278 K) to form stable benzenediazonium chloride.
  • Hydrolysis of benzenediazonium chloride by warm water yields phenol along with the evolution of nitrogen gas.
  • Decarboxylation of salicylic acid with sodalime (NaOH + CaO) produces phenol through thermal elimination of carbon dioxide.

Mechanistic Features and Intermediates

  • The Dow process proceeds via an elimination-addition mechanism involving a highly reactive benzyne (cyclohexa-1,5-diene) intermediate.
  • Cumene oxidation proceeds via free-radical propagation where oxygen inserts into the tertiary benzylic C-H bond.
  • Acidification of sodium phenoxide using mineral acids like HCl or H2SO4 liberates free phenol.

Dow Process
C6H5Cl + NaOH → C6H5ONa + NaCl (followed by H+ → C6H5OH)
Conditions: High temperature (623 K) and high pressure (300 atm)
Units: Reaction parameters in Kelvin and atmospheres
Diazonium Salt Hydrolysis

Cumene Process
C6H5CH(CH3)2 + O2 → C6H5C(CH3)2OOH → C6H5OH + CH3COCH3
Conditions: Air oxidation followed by dilute acid hydrolysis (H2SO4)
Units: Yield stoichiometric coefficients in moles
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aliphatic halides undergo nucleophilic substitution readily under mild aqueous alkali conditions.

Observation: Chlorobenzene is extremely unreactive toward nucleophilic substitution under normal conditions. — Partial double bond character of the C-Cl bond due to resonance and sp2 hybridization of the carbon atom.

Exception Diazonium salts decompose explosively when heated dry.

Observation: Diazonium salt hydrolysis to phenol is conducted safely in an aqueous acidic medium. — Dilute aqueous solution prevents local overheating and uncontrolled explosive decomposition of the dry salt.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Cumene is isopropylbenzene, which upon air oxidation gives cumene hydroperoxide.
  • Acetone is obtained as a valuable by-product in the manufacture of phenol via cumene.
  • Chlorobenzene requires 623 K and 300 atm pressure to react with aqueous NaOH in the Dow process.
  • Benzenediazonium chloride is prepared by treating aniline with NaNO2 and HCl at 273-278 K.
  • Decarboxylation of sodium salicylate with sodalime yields phenol.
  • Hydrolysis of benzene sulfonic acid with superheated steam also produces phenol.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting that the Dow process requires both high temperature and high pressure.
  • Confusing the role of acetone as a reactant instead of a co-product in the cumene process.
  • Failing to acidify sodium phenoxide at the final step to isolate free phenol.
  • Exceeding 5 °C during diazotization, which destroys the diazonium salt.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation of Phenol

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Cumene Oxidation Pathway

Oxidation of isopropylbenzene yields cumene hydroperoxide, which rearranges in acid to phenol and acetone.

Nucleophilic Aromatic Substitution

Chlorobenzene reacts with NaOH under forcing conditions via a benzyne intermediate to form sodium phenoxide.

Diazotization and Hydrolysis

Aniline is converted to benzenediazonium chloride at 0-5 °C, which hydrolyzes in warm water to phenol.

Alkali Fusion of Sulfonates

Sodium benzenesulfonate treated with molten sodium hydroxide gives sodium phenoxide and sodium sulfite.

Benzyne (C6H4)
Highly reactive transient intermediate in nucleophilic aromatic substitution.

High-temperature reaction of sodium benzenesulfonate with base.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Cumene process yields phenol and acetone.
  • Dow process conditions: 623 K and 300 atm.
  • Diazotization temperature: 273-278 K.
  • Chlorobenzene C-Cl bond has partial double-bond character.
  • Acidification of sodium phenoxide yields phenol.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Industrial methods include cumene oxidation and Dow process.
  • Laboratory methods include diazonium hydrolysis and alkali fusion.
  • Intermediates include hydroperoxides, benzyne, and diazonium salts.
  • Mineral acids are required in final steps to release free phenol from salts.

⚖️ Key Differences

Cumene Process VS Dow Process

Cumene uses air oxidation of isopropylbenzene; Dow uses harsh alkaline substitution of chlorobenzene.

What is the intermediate formed during the Dow process of phenol preparation?

Explanation: Nucleophilic aromatic substitution under basic high-temperature conditions proceeds via an elimination-addition mechanism involving a benzyne intermediate.

Question 4 of 18

Benzenediazonium chloride on warming with water gives:

Explanation: Hydrolysis of benzenediazonium salts with warm water replaces the diazonium group with a hydroxyl group, yielding phenol.

Question 5 of 18

What is the optimum temperature range for the diazotization of aniline?

Explanation: Diazotization must be performed at 273-278 K (0-5 °C) to prevent decomposition of the diazonium salt.

Question 6 of 18

Which reagent is used for alkali fusion of sodium benzenesulfonate to prepare phenol?

Explanation: Sodium benzenesulfonate is fused with molten NaOH at 573-623 K to form sodium phenoxide, which is then acidified.

Question 7 of 18

Which co-product is obtained during the industrial synthesis of phenol via the cumene process?

Explanation: The cleavage of cumene hydroperoxide yields one molecule of phenol and one molecule of acetone.

Question 8 of 18

Why is chlorobenzene less reactive towards nucleophilic substitution compared to alkyl chlorides?

Explanation: Resonance stabilization in chlorobenzene confers partial double bond character to the C-Cl bond, making cleavage difficult.

Question 9 of 18

Treatment of aniline with nitrous acid at 273-278 K produces:

Explanation: Primary aromatic amines react with HNO2 at 0-5 °C to form stable diazonium salts.

Question 10 of 18

Decarboxylation of sodium salicylate with sodalime gives:

Explanation: Heating sodium salicylate with sodalime (NaOH + CaO) results in the elimination of CO2 and formation of phenol.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following processes does NOT yield phenol directly or via its salt?

Explanation: The Wurtz reaction is used for the coupling of alkyl halides to form higher alkanes, not for phenol preparation.

Question 12 of 18

Cumene is chemically known as:

Explanation: Cumene is the common name for isopropylbenzene (C6H5CH(CH3)2).

Question 13 of 18

In the laboratory preparation of phenol from benzene, benzene is first sulfonated with oleum to give:

Explanation: Sulfonation of benzene with oleum (H2SO4 + SO3) yields benzenesulfonic acid.

Question 14 of 18

Hydrolysis of cumene hydroperoxide is carried out using:

Explanation: Cumene hydroperoxide undergoes acid-catalyzed rearrangement (using dilute H2SO4) to form phenol and acetone.

Question 15 of 18

Which starting material is reacted with nitrous acid to prepare benzenediazonium chloride?

Explanation: Aniline is a primary aromatic amine that undergoes diazotization upon treatment with HNO2.

Question 16 of 18

What is the final step in both the Dow process and alkali fusion of sulfonates to obtain phenol?

Explanation: Both processes initially yield sodium phenoxide, which must be treated with an acid to liberate free phenol.

Question 17 of 18

Which of the following methods is most widely used for the commercial production of phenol today?

Explanation: The cumene process is currently the dominant industrial method for manufacturing phenol.

Question 18 of 18

What gas is evolved during the hydrolysis of benzenediazonium chloride to phenol?

Explanation: The reaction releases nitrogen gas (N2) as a stable leaving group.
