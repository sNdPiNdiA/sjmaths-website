Chemical properties of benzaldehyde

Benzaldehyde is the simplest aromatic aldehyde containing a formyl group attached directly to a phenyl ring, exhibiting characteristic nucleophilic addition and oxidation reactions. Its chemical behavior is influenced by the electron-withdrawing resonance effect of the benzene ring combined with the absence of alpha-hydrogens.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Oxidation and Reduction Characteristics

  • Benzaldehyde readily undergoes aerial oxidation upon standing in air to form crystalline benzoic acid.
  • It reduces Tollens' reagent (ammoniacal silver nitrate) to deposit a silver mirror, confirming its aldehydic nature.
  • Unlike aliphatic aldehydes, it does not reduce Fehling's solution or Benedict's solution due to the strong stability of the phenyl-carbonyl conjugation.
  • Reduction with Zn/HCl (Clemmensen reduction) or NH2NH2/OH- (Wolff-Kishner reduction) converts the carbonyl group into a methylene group (toluene).

Nucleophilic Addition and Condensation Reactions

  • Benzaldehyde reacts with hydrocyanic acid (HCN) to form benzaldehyde cyanohydrin via nucleophilic attack.
  • It undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction in the presence of concentrated alkali, yielding benzyl alcohol and sodium benzoate via intermolecular hydride transfer.
  • Reaction with primary amines yields stable Schiff bases (azomethines, C6H5CH=NR).
  • Reaction with hydroxylhydrazine or phenylhydrazine produces corresponding crystalline oximes and phenylhydrazones useful for characterization.

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution and Name Reactions

  • The formyl group is meta-directing and strongly deactivating due to -R and -I effects during electrophilic substitution.
  • Nitration with concentrated HNO3/H2SO4 yields predominantly m-nitrobenzaldehyde.
  • Under the Benzoin condensation, two molecules of benzaldehyde couple in the presence of cyanide ions to form an α-hydroxy ketone.
  • The Perkin reaction involves condensation with aliphatic acid anhydrides in the presence of sodium salts to yield cinnamic acid derivatives.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aldehydes reduce Fehling's solution.

Observation: Benzaldehyde does not reduce Fehling's solution. — The aromatic ring stabilizes the carbonyl group, making it resistant to mild oxidizing agents like Cu(II) tartrate complexes.

Exception Aldehydes with α-hydrogens undergo base-catalyzed aldol condensation.

Observation: Benzaldehyde undergoes Cannizzaro reaction instead of self-aldol condensation. — Benzaldehyde completely lacks α-hydrogens, preventing the formation of an enolate ion required for aldol addition.

Exception Electrophilic substitution in substituted benzenes with -R groups occurs at ortho/para positions.

Observation: Nitration and bromination of benzaldehyde direct the incoming group to the meta position. — The electron-withdrawing resonance and inductive effects of the -CHO group deplete electron density specifically at the ortho and para positions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Benzaldehyde is commonly known as oil of bitter almonds.
  • Industrial preparation involves the catalytic oxidation of toluene using air over V2O5.
  • Rosenmund reduction converts benzoyl chloride to benzaldehyde using H2 over Pd/BaSO4 poisoned with quinoline.
  • Etard reaction uses chromyl chloride (CrO2Cl2) to oxidize toluene directly to benzaldehyde via a chromium complex intermediate.
  • Gattermann-Koch reaction uses carbon monoxide and HCl in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3/CuCl to form benzaldehyde from benzene.
  • Schiff base formation involves condensation of benzaldehyde with primary amines yielding an imine linkage.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the Etard reaction reagent (CrO2Cl2) with standard chromic acid oxidation which yields benzoic acid.
  • Expecting benzaldehyde to undergo aldol condensation with itself without realizing the absence of alpha-hydrogens.
  • Assuming aromatic aldehydes reduce Fehling's solution just like aliphatic aldehydes.
  • Forgetting that benzaldehyde easily undergoes aerial oxidation to benzoic acid during prolonged storage.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical properties of benzaldehyde

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Synthesis Methods

Benzaldehyde is synthesized via Etard reaction (toluene + CrO2Cl2), Gattermann-Koch formylation (benzene + CO + HCl), and Rosenmund reduction (benzoyl chloride + H2/Pd-BaSO4).

Oxidative and Reductive Behavior

It undergoes facile oxidation to benzoic acid and reduces Tollens' reagent. Reduction via Clemmensen or Wolff-Kishner conditions yields toluene, while catalytic hydrogenation yields benzyl alcohol.

Disproportionation and Condensation

Lacking alpha-hydrogens, it undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction in strong base and participates in benzoin condensation in the presence of cyanide ions.

Electrophilic Substitution

The formyl group acts as a deactivating meta-directing substituent, directing incoming electrophiles like nitro groups predominantly to the meta position.

Selective hydrogenation of acyl chlorides to aldehydes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Benzaldehyde has no alpha-hydrogens, hence no aldol condensation.
  • Etard reaction uses chromyl chloride to form a brown chromium complex intermediate.
  • Cannizzaro reaction yields equimolar amounts of alcohol and carboxylate salt.
  • Benzaldehyde reduces Tollens' reagent but fails with Fehling's solution.
  • Perkin reaction yields alpha,beta-unsaturated aromatic acids like cinnamic acid.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Benzaldehyde exhibits dual chemical personality through aromatic ring substitution and carbonyl group reactions.
  • Nucleophilic additions proceed smoothly with HCN and bisulfite.
  • Oxidation readily affords benzoic acid, necessitating storage precautions.
  • Name reactions like Cannizzaro, Perkin, and Benzoin are foundational for UP PGT examinations.

⚖️ Key Differences

Etard Reaction VS Gattermann-Koch Reaction

Etard oxidizes toluene using chromyl chloride, whereas Gattermann-Koch formylates benzene using CO and HCl.

Question 1 of 19

What is the product formed when benzaldehyde reacts with concentrated NaOH?

Explanation: In the absence of alpha-hydrogens, benzaldehyde undergoes the Cannizzaro disproportionation reaction with concentrated alkali to form equimolar quantities of benzyl alcohol and sodium benzoate.

Question 2 of 19

Which reagent is used in the Etard reaction for converting toluene to benzaldehyde?

Explanation: The Etard reaction specifically utilizes chromyl chloride (CrO2Cl2) to oxidize the methyl group of toluene to a chromium complex, which on hydrolysis yields benzaldehyde.

Question 3 of 19

Benzaldehyde does not give a positive test with which of the following reagents?

Explanation: Aromatic aldehydes like benzaldehyde are resistant to mild oxidation by Cu(II) complexes and therefore fail to reduce Fehling's solution, unlike most aliphatic aldehydes.

Question 4 of 19

What is the catalyst used in the Benzoin condensation of benzaldehyde?

Explanation: Potassium cyanide (KCN) acts as a specific nucleophilic catalyst in the benzoin condensation, facilitating the coupling of two benzaldehyde molecules.

Question 5 of 19

The directional influence of the formyl group (-CHO) in electrophilic aromatic substitution of benzaldehyde is:

Explanation: The formyl group is electron-withdrawing via resonance (-R) and inductive (-I) effects, which deactivates the ring and directs incoming electrophiles to the meta position.

Question 6 of 19

Which reaction produces cinnamic acid from benzaldehyde?

Explanation: The Perkin reaction involves heating benzaldehyde with an aliphatic acid anhydride (such as acetic anhydride) in the presence of a sodium salt of the acid to yield cinnamic acid.

Question 7 of 19

Rosenmund reduction converts benzoyl chloride into benzaldehyde using:

Explanation: Rosenmund reduction employs hydrogen gas in the presence of palladium supported on barium sulfate, poisoned with quinoline, to selectively reduce acyl chlorides to aldehydes.

Question 8 of 19

What is the intermediate formed during the Etard reaction of toluene?

Explanation: Toluene reacts with chromyl chloride to form a brown solid chromium complex (Etard complex), which upon acid hydrolysis gives benzaldehyde.

Question 9 of 19

When benzaldehyde is treated with primary amines, the resulting product is a:

Explanation: Condensation of benzaldehyde with primary amines (R-NH2) yields imines, commonly known as Schiff bases (C6H5CH=NR).

Question 10 of 19

Which of the following compounds will undergo Cannizzaro reaction?

Explanation: Only aldehydes lacking alpha-hydrogens, such as benzaldehyde and formaldehyde, undergo the base-catalyzed Cannizzaro disproportionation reaction.

Question 11 of 19

Prolonged exposure of benzaldehyde to air results in the formation of:

Explanation: Benzaldehyde readily undergoes aerial oxidation upon standing in air, converting into crystalline benzoic acid.

Question 12 of 19

The reaction of benzaldehyde with hydroxylamine (NH2OH) yields:

Explanation: Reaction of aldehydes with hydroxylamine produces oximes accompanied by the elimination of a water molecule.

Question 13 of 19

Gattermann-Koch reaction uses which gas mixture to convert benzene into benzaldehyde?

Explanation: The Gattermann-Koch formylation uses carbon monoxide (CO) and hydrogen chloride (HCl) in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3 and CuCl.

Question 14 of 19

Which reducing agent converts benzaldehyde into toluene?

Explanation: Clemmensen reduction (Zn/HCl) or Wolff-Kishner reduction completely reduces the carbonyl oxygen of benzaldehyde to a methylene group, yielding toluene.

Question 15 of 19

What is the IUPAC name of benzaldehyde?

Explanation: According to IUPAC nomenclature, benzaldehyde is systematically named benzenecarbaldehyde.

Question 16 of 19

In the Cannizzaro reaction of benzaldehyde, the reducing agent and oxidizing agent are:

Explanation: Cannizzaro is a disproportionation reaction where one molecule of benzaldehyde is oxidized and another is reduced simultaneously.

Question 17 of 19

Which type of addition reaction is characteristic of the carbonyl group in benzaldehyde?

Explanation: Due to the polarization of the carbon-oxygen double bond, carbonyl compounds characteristically undergo nucleophilic addition reactions.

Question 18 of 19

What color is the precipitate formed when benzaldehyde reduces Tollens' reagent?

Explanation: Reduction of ammoniacal silver nitrate (Tollens' reagent) by benzaldehyde results in the deposition of metallic silver on the inner walls of the test tube, forming a silver mirror.

Question 19 of 19

Which compound is formed when benzaldehyde reacts with hydrogen cyanide (HCN)?

Explanation: Nucleophilic addition of hydrogen cyanide across the carbonyl double bond of benzaldehyde produces benzaldehyde cyanohydrin.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary product of the reaction between benzaldehyde and active hydrogen in the presence of Pd/BaSO4?

Solution: Catalytic hydrogenation of the carbonyl group in benzaldehyde over standard metal catalysts yields benzyl alcohol.

Question 2 of 10
Which catalyst is essential for the Gattermann-Koch formylation of benzene to benzaldehyde?

Solution: Anhydrous aluminum chloride coupled with cuprous chloride (CuCl) serves as the catalyst system in Gattermann-Koch formylation.

Question 3 of 10
Why is benzaldehyde less reactive than aliphatic aldehydes towards nucleophilic addition?

Solution: Delocalization of the carbonyl π-electrons into the benzene ring (+R effect) reduces the positive charge on the carbonyl carbon, lowering its electrophilicity.

Question 4 of 10
Which name reaction involves the self-condensation of benzaldehyde in the presence of cyanide ion?

Solution: Cyanide ion acts as a nucleophilic catalyst to facilitate the benzoin condensation of two benzaldehyde molecules.

Question 5 of 10
Nitration of benzaldehyde yields primarily:

Solution: The -CHO group is a meta-directing deactivator, so nitration yields predominantly meta-nitrobenzaldehyde.

Question 6 of 10
What type of isomerism is exhibited by benzaldehyde oxime?

Solution: Due to restricted rotation about the C=N double bond and the presence of different groups on carbon and nitrogen, benzaldehyde oxime exhibits geometrical (syn and anti) isomerism.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following compounds is produced when benzaldehyde is treated with chlorine in the presence of light?

Solution: Chlorination of benzaldehyde under radical conditions or oxidation of its derivatives yields benzoyl chloride (C6H5COCl).

Question 8 of 10
Which chemical test confirms the presence of the carbonyl group in benzaldehyde without distinguishing between aldehyde and ketone?

Solution: 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine (2,4-DNP) reacts with both aldehydes and ketones to form colored crystalline hydrazones.

Question 9 of 10
What is the role of quinoline in the Rosenmund reduction used for benzaldehyde synthesis?

Solution: Quinoline acts as a poison for the palladium catalyst to prevent over-reduction of benzaldehyde into benzyl alcohol.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following reactions demonstrates the electrophilic substitution on the aromatic ring of benzaldehyde?

Solution: Nitration is a classic electrophilic aromatic substitution occurring on the benzene ring of benzaldehyde.
