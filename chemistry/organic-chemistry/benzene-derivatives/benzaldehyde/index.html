Benzaldehyde

Benzaldehyde (C6H5CHO) is the simplest aromatic aldehyde, consisting of a formyl group attached to a phenyl ring. It exhibits typical aldehyde reactivity alongside specific aromatic substitution and condensation pathways.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation Methods of Benzaldehyde

  • Etard Reaction converts toluene to benzaldehyde using chromyl chloride (CrO2Cl2) in CS2 via a chromium complex intermediate.
  • Gattermann-Koch Reaction introduces a formyl group into benzene using carbon monoxide and hydrogen chloride in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3 and CuCl.
  • Rosenmund Reduction reduces benzoyl chloride to benzaldehyde using hydrogen over poisoned palladium catalyst (Pd-BaSO4).

Chemical Properties and Reactions

  • Benzaldehyde undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction upon treatment with concentrated alkali due to the complete absence of α-hydrogen atoms.
  • Nucleophilic addition of HCN yields mandelonitrile, a chiral cyanohydrin formed as a racemic mixture.
  • Electrophilic aromatic substitution occurs predominantly at the meta-position because the formyl group is a strong deactivating and meta-directing substituent.

Condensation and Oxidation Reactions

  • Reaction with primary aromatic amines yields stable Schiff bases, known as benzylideneanilines.
  • Oxidation with nitric acid, KMnO4, or atmospheric oxygen converts benzaldehyde readily into benzoic acid.
  • Benzaldehyde fails to reduce Fehling's solution despite being an aldehyde, but readily reduces Tollens' reagent.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aliphatic and most aromatic aldehydes reduce Fehling's solution.

Observation: Benzaldehyde does not reduce Fehling's solution. — The aromatic ring stabilizes the carbonyl carbon and increases steric hindrance, lowering the reducing power toward mild complexed metal ions.

Exception Aldehydes with α-hydrogens undergo aldol condensation in dilute alkali.

Observation: Benzaldehyde cannot undergo self-aldol condensation. — It completely lacks α-hydrogen atoms required for enolate formation under basic conditions.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Etard Reagent Mnemonic
CrO2Cl2 / CS2

Application: Instantly identify toluene to benzaldehyde oxidation without over-oxidizing to benzoic acid.

Meta-Directing Group Rule
-CHO = Electron Withdrawing (EWG)

Application: Any incoming electrophile in benzaldehyde will attach to the meta position due to the -I and -R effects of the carbonyl group.

Cannizzaro vs Aldol Check
No α-H → Cannizzaro; Has α-H → Aldol

Application: Instantly determine product pathways by counting hydrogens on the carbon adjacent to the carbonyl.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Benzaldehyde is also known as oil of bitter almonds.
  • The boiling point of benzaldehyde is 452 K.
  • Etard reaction uses chromyl chloride to form a brown chromium complex intermediate.
  • Benzaldehyde gives a positive Tollens' test, precipitating elemental silver.
  • Perkin reaction of benzaldehyde with acetic anhydride yields cinnamic acid.
  • Benzoin condensation requires cyanide ion catalysis.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Gattermann reaction (using HCN/HCl) with Gattermann-Koch reaction (using CO/HCl).
  • Assuming benzaldehyde reduces Fehling's solution because it is an aldehyde.
  • Forgetting that benzaldehyde undergoes meta-substitution during electrophilic aromatic substitution.
  • Writing aldol condensation products for benzaldehyde alone without a coreactant possessing α-hydrogens.

⚡ Quick Revision: Benzaldehyde

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

Benzaldehyde features an sp2 hybridized carbonyl carbon conjugated with the aromatic π-electron cloud.

Oxidation and Reduction

It oxidizes to benzoic acid and reduces to benzyl alcohol via catalytic hydrogenation or Clemmensen reduction to toluene.

Named Reactions

Key transformations include Cannizzaro, Benzoin, Perkin, and Claisen-Schmidt condensations.

Ring Substitutions

The deactivating formyl group directs incoming electrophiles to the meta position.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Cannizzaro Reaction (Disproportionation)
Self-redox reaction of aldehydes without alpha-hydrogens in concentrated alkali.
Etard Reaction (CrO2Cl2 oxidation)
Selective oxidation of methylarenes to benzaldehydes.
Rosenmund Reduction (H2 / Pd-BaSO4)
Catalytic hydrogenation of acyl chlorides to aldehydes.
Benzoin (C6H5CH(OH)COC6H5)
Dimerization product of benzaldehyde formed via cyanide catalysis.
Schiff Base (Imine derivative)
Condensation product of benzaldehyde with primary amines.
Cinnamic Acid (C6H5CH=CHCOOH)
Product of the Perkin reaction between benzaldehyde and acetic anhydride.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Benzaldehyde lacks alpha-hydrogens.
  • It undergoes disproportionation in conc. NaOH.
  • It is prepared via Etard, Gattermann-Koch, and Rosenmund methods.
  • It gives negative test with Fehling's solution.
  • Electrophilic substitution occurs at the meta position.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Aromatic aldehyde with strong resonance stabilization.
  • Exhibits nucleophilic addition and condensation reactions.
  • Acts as a meta-directing group in aromatic substitution.
  • Readily oxidized to benzoic acid.

⚖️ Key Differences

Gattermann Reaction VS Gattermann-Koch Reaction

Gattermann uses HCN/HCl, while Gattermann-Koch uses CO/HCl with CuCl.

Question 1 of 18

Which reagent is used in the Etard reaction for converting toluene to benzaldehyde?

Explanation: Chromyl chloride (CrO2Cl2) in CS2 selectively oxidizes the methyl group of toluene to a chromium complex, which hydrolyzes to benzaldehyde.

Question 2 of 18

Benzaldehyde does not undergo:

Explanation: Aldol condensation requires alpha-hydrogen atoms, which benzaldehyde lacks.

Question 3 of 18

The catalyst used in the benzoin condensation of benzaldehyde is:

Explanation: Potassium cyanide (KCN) acts as a specific nucleophilic catalyst for benzoin condensation.

Question 4 of 18

Rosenmund reduction converts benzoyl chloride to:

Explanation: Rosenmund reduction is the catalytic hydrogenation of acyl chlorides to aldehydes using poisoned palladium.

Question 5 of 18

What is the product formed when benzaldehyde reacts with concentrated NaOH?

Explanation: In the absence of alpha-hydrogens, benzaldehyde undergoes disproportionation (Cannizzaro reaction) to form an alcohol and a salt of carboxylic acid.

Question 6 of 18

The directing nature of the formyl group in benzaldehyde for electrophilic aromatic substitution is:

Explanation: The formyl group is electron-withdrawing via -I and -R effects, making it deactivating and meta-directing.

Question 7 of 18

Which test distinguishes benzaldehyde from acetaldehyde?

Explanation: Acetaldehyde reduces Fehling's solution, whereas benzaldehyde fails to give a positive Fehling's test.

Question 8 of 18

In the Perkin reaction, benzaldehyde is heated with an aliphatic acid anhydride in the presence of:

Explanation: The Perkin reaction utilizes the sodium salt of the acid corresponding to the anhydride (e.g., sodium acetate with acetic anhydride).

Question 9 of 18

What is the intermediate formed during the Etard reaction of toluene?

Explanation: Toluene reacts with chromyl chloride to form a brown chromium complex, which subsequently hydrolyzes to benzaldehyde.

Question 10 of 18

Reaction of benzaldehyde with primary amines yields:

Explanation: Condensation of aldehydes with primary amines forms imines, commonly referred to as Schiff bases.

Question 11 of 18

Gattermann-Koch formylation of benzene uses which gaseous mixture?

Explanation: Gattermann-Koch reaction employs carbon monoxide and hydrogen chloride in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3/CuCl.

Question 12 of 18

Which of the following compounds is formed when benzaldehyde reacts with HCN?

Explanation: Nucleophilic addition of hydrogen cyanide to benzaldehyde produces mandelonitrile.

Question 13 of 18

Reduction of benzaldehyde with Zn-Hg and concentrated HCl is known as:

Explanation: Clemmensen reduction utilizes amalgamated zinc and hydrochloric acid to convert carbonyl groups to methylene groups.

Question 14 of 18

Benzaldehyde on oxidation yields:

Explanation: Mild or strong oxidizing agents convert the formyl group of benzaldehyde into a carboxylic acid group.

Question 15 of 18

What is the commercial name of benzaldehyde?

Explanation: Benzaldehyde is naturally found in bitter almonds and is historically known as oil of bitter almonds.

Question 16 of 18

Which reaction converts benzaldehyde into cinnamic acid?

Explanation: The Perkin reaction condenses aromatic aldehydes with acid anhydrides to yield α,β-unsaturated aromatic acids like cinnamic acid.

Question 17 of 18

What happens when benzaldehyde is treated with hydroxylamine?

Explanation: Reaction with hydroxylamine (NH2OH) yields benzaldoxime.

Question 18 of 18

Which reagent reduces benzaldehyde to benzyl alcohol?

Explanation: Complex metal hydrides like sodium borohydride (NaBH4) reduce aldehydes to primary alcohols.

Which of the following reagents converts benzaldehyde directly to benzene?

Solution: Decarboxylation of benzoic acid (derived from benzaldehyde) with soda lime yields benzene.

Question 2 of 10
What is the intermediate in the Cannizzaro reaction of benzaldehyde?

Solution: The Cannizzaro reaction proceeds via nucleophilic addition of hydroxide followed by a rapid intramolecular hydride transfer.

Question 3 of 10
Which compound is formed when benzaldehyde is treated with chlorine in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3?

Solution: The formyl group is meta-directing, so chlorination yields m-chlorobenzaldehyde.

Question 4 of 10
Benzaldehyde reacts with phenylhydrazine to give:

Solution: Reaction with phenylhydrazine forms benzaldehyde phenylhydrazone.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following methods cannot be used to prepare benzaldehyde?

Solution: The Wurtz reaction is used for the coupling of alkyl halides to form alkanes, not for preparing benzaldehyde.

Question 6 of 10
The conversion of benzaldehyde to benzyl alcohol and potassium benzoate involves:

Solution: Simultaneous oxidation and reduction of the same reactant in a Cannizzaro reaction is termed disproportionation.

Question 7 of 10
When benzaldehyde is boiled with alcoholic KCN, the product is:

Solution: Cyanide ion catalyzes the condensation of two molecules of benzaldehyde into benzoin.

Question 8 of 10
Which property of benzaldehyde accounts for its stability compared to aliphatic aldehydes?

Solution: Conjugation of the carbonyl group with the benzene ring provides resonance stabilization.

Question 9 of 10
What is the product of nitration of benzaldehyde with a mixture of conc. HNO3 and conc. H2SO4?

Solution: Nitration introduces the nitro group at the meta-position due to the deactivating meta-directing nature of the -CHO group.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following compounds gives benzoic acid upon oxidation with acidic KMnO4?

Solution: Benzaldehyde, toluene, and benzyl alcohol can all be oxidized to benzoic acid using strong oxidizing agents.
