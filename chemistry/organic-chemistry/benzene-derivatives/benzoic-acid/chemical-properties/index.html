Chemical properties of benzoic acid

Benzoic acid is a crystalline aromatic carboxylic acid that undergoes characteristic reactions involving the acidic proton, the carbonyl group, and the benzene ring. Its chemical behavior is dictated by the electron-withdrawing nature of the carboxyl group, which directs electrophilic substitution to the meta position.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Reactions Involving the Carboxylic Acid Group

  • Benzoic acid reacts with aqueous sodium bicarbonate to yield sodium benzoate, water, and effervescence of carbon dioxide gas.
  • Reaction with alcohols in the presence of an acid catalyst produces aromatic esters through a Fischer esterification mechanism.
  • Treatment with phosphorus pentachloride (PCl5) or thionyl chloride (SOCl2) converts benzoic acid into benzoyl chloride.
  • Reduction of benzoic acid using strong reducing agents like lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4) yields benzyl alcohol.

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution on the Benzene Ring

  • The carboxyl group is strongly electron-withdrawing via inductive (-I) and resonance (-M) effects, making benzoic acid deactivated toward electrophilic attack.
  • Nitration of benzoic acid using a nitrating mixture (concentrated HNO3 and H2SO4) predominantly yields m-nitrobenzoic acid (approx. 80%).
  • Halogenation, such as bromination using Br2 in the presence of FeBr3, gives m-bromobenzoic acid as the major product.
  • Sulfonation of benzoic acid with fuming sulfuric acid yields m-sulfobenzoic acid as the primary product.

Special Reactions and Decarboxylation

  • Heating the sodium salt of benzoic acid with soda-lime (NaOH + CaO) undergoes decarboxylation to yield benzene.
  • Benzoic acid does not undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation or acylation because the carboxyl group coordinates with the Lewis acid catalyst (AlCl3), causing catalyst deactivation.
  • Schmidt reaction of benzoic acid with hydrazoic acid (HN3) in the presence of concentrated H2SO4 produces aniline via phenyl isocyanate.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Carboxylic acids undergo Friedel-Crafts reactions to form substituted aromatic ketones.

Observation: Benzoic acid fails to undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation or acylation. — The lone pairs on the oxygen atom of the carboxyl group coordinate with the Lewis acid catalyst (AlCl3), forming an unreactive complex that poisons the catalyst.

Exception Electron-withdrawing groups direct incoming electrophiles to the meta position.

Observation: During nitration or halogenation of benzoic acid, a small amount of ortho and para isomers are still formed alongside the major meta product. — Deactivation is not absolute; local electronic fluctuations and steric factors allow minor pathways.

Exception Reduction of carboxylic acids typically stops at the aldehyde stage using mild reducing agents.

Observation: Direct partial reduction of benzoic acid to benzaldehyde using catalytic hydrogenation is not feasible. — Carboxylic acids are extremely stable and require strong reducing agents like LiAlH4 which reduce them directly to primary alcohols, or Rosenmund reduction of the corresponding acid chloride.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
META Mnemonic for Directed Substitution
M-E-T-A: Meta Electron-Withdrawing Type Activator

Application: Remember that groups with a double or triple bond connected to the ring (like -COOH, -NO2, -CN) are meta-directing deactivators.

Decarboxylation Soda-Lime Shortcut
R-COOH + Soda-Lime → R-H + Na2CO3

Application: Drop the -COOH group entirely and replace it with an -H atom to find the main alkane or arene product.

Acidity Comparison Trick
pKa value inversely proportional to Acidity Strength

Application: Lower pKa means stronger acid. Electron-withdrawing substituents on the ortho or para positions generally increase benzoic acid strength.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Benzoic acid turns blue litmus red due to its acidic nature.
  • The pKa of benzoic acid is 4.20, making it stronger than acetic acid (pKa = 4.76).
  • Reaction of benzoic acid with NaHCO3 distinguishes it from phenols and alcohols.
  • Bromination of benzoic acid yields m-bromobenzoic acid as the major product.
  • Soda-lime decarboxylation of benzoic acid produces benzene.
  • Esterification of benzoic acid with methanol in acid gives methyl benzoate.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming benzoic acid undergoes Friedel-Crafts reactions readily with AlCl3.
  • Confusing the ortho/para-directing nature of activating groups with the meta-directing nature of the -COOH group.
  • Forgetting that sodium bicarbonate reacts with benzoic acid to release CO2 gas.
  • Confusing the reduction product of benzoic acid with LiAlH4 as benzaldehyde instead of benzyl alcohol.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical properties of benzoic acid

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Acidic Character

Benzoic acid ionizes in water to form the resonance-stabilized benzoate ion and hydronium ion, showing greater acidity than aliphatic carboxylic acids due to sp2 hybridization and resonance stabilization.

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution

The electron-withdrawing -COOH group deactivates the benzene ring and directs incoming electrophiles specifically to the meta-position during nitration, sulfonation, and halogenation.

Derivative Formation

The hydroxyl portion of the carboxyl group undergoes nucleophilic acyl substitution to form acid chlorides, esters, amides, and acid anhydrides under appropriate reagent conditions.

Ring Decarboxylation

Thermal treatment of sodium benzoate with soda-lime eliminates carbon dioxide, serving as a standard synthetic route for the preparation of pure benzene.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • pKa of benzoic acid = 4.20
  • Benzoic acid + NaHCO3 → CO2 gas evolution
  • Ring substitution directs to meta position
  • Friedel-Crafts reaction fails due to catalyst complexation
  • Soda-lime decarboxylation yields benzene

📌 Quick Recall

  • Benzoic acid exhibits typical carboxylic acid reactions and aromatic ring reactions.
  • The carboxyl group acts as a deactivating, meta-directing substituent.
  • It reacts with NaHCO3 releasing CO2, distinguishing it from phenols.
  • Reduction yields benzyl alcohol, while decarboxylation yields benzene.

⚖️ Key Differences

Phenol Acidity VS Benzoic Acid Acidity

Benzoic acid (pKa 4.2) is significantly stronger than phenol (pKa 10.0) and reacts with NaHCO3.
