Organic Chemistry

Benzoic Acid

Benzoic acid is the simplest aromatic carboxylic acid with the chemical formula C6H5COOH. It exists as a crystalline, colorless solid and serves as a vital precursor for the industrial synthesis of numerous organic compounds.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation Methods

  • Oxidation of alkylbenzenes using alkaline or acidic KMnO4 yields benzoic acid regardless of alkyl chain length.
  • Hydrolysis of benzotrichloride with aqueous calcium hydroxide or sodium hydroxide produces benzoic acid.
  • Carbonation of phenylmagnesium bromide (Grignard reagent) with solid carbon dioxide followed by acid hydrolysis forms benzoic acid.
  • Hydrolysis of benzonitrile in the presence of acid or base catalysts yields benzoic acid.

Chemical Properties and Reactions

  • Reaction with PCl5, PCl3, or SOCl2 converts benzoic acid into benzoyl chloride.
  • Nitration of benzoic acid using a nitrating mixture gives predominantly 3-nitrobenzoic acid due to the meta-directing nature of the carboxy group.
  • Reduction of benzoic acid with lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4) selectively yields benzyl alcohol.
  • Benzoic acid undergoes decarboxylation when heated with soda lime (NaOH + CaO) to yield benzene.

Acidity and Substituent Effects

  • Benzoic acid is a stronger acid than acetic acid due to electron-withdrawing resonance and inductive effects of the phenyl ring.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups (EWGs) like -NO2, -CN, and -halogens at the ortho and para positions increase the acidity of benzoic acid.
  • Electron-donating groups (EDGs) like -CH3 and -OCH3 decrease the acidity of benzoic acid.
  • The ortho effect states that almost any ortho-substituted benzoic acid is a stronger acid than benzoic acid itself due to steric inhibition of resonance.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Electron-donating groups decrease acidity when placed on the benzene ring.

Observation: Ortho-toluic acid is more acidic than benzoic acid. — The ortho effect creates steric hindrance that forces the carboxyl group out of coplanarity with the ring, relieving destabilizing electronic interactions and stabilizing the carboxylate anion.

Exception Meta and para substituents follow pure inductive and resonance Hammett parameter trends.

Observation: Ortho-substituted benzoic acids frequently show unpredictable pKa variations. — Steric hindrance, intramolecular hydrogen bonding, and proximity effects dominate over electronic polar effects at the ortho position.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Benzoic acid has a melting point of 395 K and is sparingly soluble in cold water but soluble in hot water.
  • The pKa value of benzoic acid is approximately 4.20.
  • Oxidation of toluene to benzoic acid is a standard industrial synthesis route using KMnO4 or catalytic air oxidation.
  • The carboxyl group (-COOH) in benzoic acid is deactivating and meta-directing for electrophilic aromatic substitution.
  • Reaction of benzoic acid with ethanol in the presence of concentrated sulfuric acid yields ethyl benzoate, a fragrant ester.
  • Treatment of benzoic acid with ammonia followed by strong heating yields benzamide and ultimately benzonitrile.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming tertiary alkylbenzenes can be oxidized to benzoic acid by KMnO4; they cannot because they lack benzylic hydrogens.
  • Confusing the directing group property of -COOH (meta-directing) with activating groups like -OH and -NH2.
  • Believing that ortho-methoxybenzoic acid is a weaker acid than benzoic acid due to the electron-donating methoxy group; the ortho effect overrides electronic effects, making it stronger.
  • Forgetting that soda lime decarboxylation removes the carboxyl group entirely to form benzene.

⚡ Quick Revision: Benzoic Acid

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

Benzoic acid features an sp2 hybridized carbonyl carbon bonded to the aromatic ipso carbon. The planarity of the molecule facilitates extensive resonance stabilization of the carboxylate anion.

Acidity Fundamentals

The greater acidity of benzoic acid relative to aliphatic acids stems from the electron-withdrawing sp2 hybridized carbon of the benzene ring, which stabilizes the negative charge on the conjugate base.

Synthetic Routes

Key synthetic pathways include side-chain oxidation of alkylbenzenes, hydrolysis of nitriles or trihalomethylbenzenes, and carboxylation of Grignard reagents.

Reactivity Profiles

Reactions involve both the acidic proton of the carboxyl group and substitution/addition reactions on the aromatic ring, dictated by the meta-directing influence of the -COOH group.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Benzoylation (C6H5CO- transfer)
Introduction of a benzoyl group into an organic molecule containing an active H atom.
Ortho Effect (Steric/Electronic anomaly)
Increased acidity of ortho-substituted benzoic acids compared to their isomers.
Decarboxylation (-CO2 elimination)
Removal of a carboxyl group as carbon dioxide gas.
Soda Lime (NaOH + CaO)
Reagent mixture used for laboratory decarboxylation of carboxylic acids.
Benzotrichloride (C6H5CCl3)
Trichloromethylbenzene used to prepare benzoic acid via hydrolysis.
Schotten-Baumann Reaction (Acylation in aq. base)
Reaction of amines or phenols with benzoyl chloride in aqueous sodium hydroxide.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • pKa of benzoic acid = 4.20
  • Carboxyl group is meta-directing in electrophilic aromatic substitution
  • Ortho-substituted benzoic acids are generally stronger acids than benzoic acid
  • Reduction of benzoic acid with LiAlH4 produces benzyl alcohol
  • Reaction with soda lime produces benzene and sodium carbonate

📌 Quick Recall

  • Benzoic acid is an aromatic carboxylic acid widely prepared via oxidation of toluene.
  • It is more acidic than acetic acid due to electron-withdrawing resonance of the phenyl ring.
  • Substituents at ortho positions increase acidity regardless of electronic character due to the ortho effect.
  • It undergoes typical carboxylic acid reactions including esterification, salt formation, and acid chloride synthesis.

⚖️ Key Differences

Phenol Acidity VS Benzoic Acid Acidity

Benzoic acid (pKa ~ 4.2) is significantly stronger than phenol (pKa ~ 10) because the carboxylate anion has its negative charge delocalized over two equivalent electronegative oxygen atoms.

Explanation: Schotten-Baumann reaction uses benzoyl chloride in the presence of aqueous sodium hydroxide for benzoylation.

Question 10 of 19

Explanation: Benzoic acid (pKa 4.20) is stronger than acetic acid (pKa 4.76) due to the electron-withdrawing phenyl ring.

Question 11 of 19

Which substituent at the para position increases the acidity of benzoic acid the most?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is a strong electron-withdrawing group via -I and -M effects, significantly enhancing acidity.

Question 12 of 19

Explanation: Carbonation of Grignard reagents adds one carbon atom, yielding benzoic acid from phenylmagnesium bromide.

Question 13 of 19

What is the hybridization of the carbonyl carbon in benzoic acid?

Explanation: The carbonyl carbon forms three sigma bonds and one pi bond, making it sp2 hybridized.

Question 14 of 19

When benzoic acid is nitrated, the major product is:

Explanation: Due to the meta-directing nature of the carboxy group, nitration yields primarily 3-nitrobenzoic acid.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following alkylbenzenes cannot be oxidized to benzoic acid by KMnO4?

Explanation: Tert-butylbenzene lacks benzylic hydrogen atoms, which are required for the initial oxidation step by KMnO4.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary organic product formed when phenylmagnesium bromide reacts with carbon dioxide, followed by treatment with dilute HCl?

Solution: Grignard reagent addition to CO2 forms a magnesium salt that hydrolyzes to benzoic acid.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following substituent groups increases the acidity of benzoic acid when placed at the para position?

Solution: The nitro group (-NO2) is electron-withdrawing via resonance and induction, stabilizing the conjugate base and increasing acidity.

Question 3 of 10
In the reduction of benzoic acid to benzyl alcohol, which of the following reagents is most suitable?

Solution: Carboxylic acids are resistant to catalytic hydrogenation and dissolving metal reductions, requiring strong reducing agents like LiAlH4.

Question 4 of 10
Why is ortho-hydroxybenzoic acid (salicylic acid) a stronger acid than benzoic acid?

Solution: Salicylic acid forms a stable intramolecular hydrogen bond in the conjugate base anion, facilitating proton release.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following methods cannot be used to prepare benzoic acid?

Solution: Reduction of benzaldehyde with Zn/HCl (Clemmensen reduction) yields toluene, not benzoic acid.

Question 6 of 10
What happens when benzoic acid is heated with ammonia and the intermediate is strongly heated?

Solution: Heating benzoic acid with ammonia forms ammonium benzoate, which upon strong heating dehydrates to benzamide and further dehydrates to benzonitrile.

Question 7 of 10
The carboxyl group in benzoic acid exhibits which electronic effect on the aromatic ring?

Solution: The carbonyl group withdraws electrons through both resonance (-M) and inductive (-I) effects.

Question 8 of 10
Which gas is evolved when sodium bicarbonate is added to an aqueous solution of benzoic acid?

Solution: Reaction of benzoic acid with sodium bicarbonate releases carbon dioxide gas with effervescence.

Question 9 of 10
What is the end product of side-chain chlorination of toluene in the presence of sunlight, followed by aqueous hydrolysis?

Solution: Chlorination of toluene gives benzal chloride (C6H5CHCl2), which upon hydrolysis yields benzaldehyde.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following compounds has the lowest pKa value?

Solution: p-Nitrobenzoic acid has a nitro group that strongly withdraws electrons, increasing acidity and thus lowering its pKa value below the others.
