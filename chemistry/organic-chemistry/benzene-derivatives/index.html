Organic Chemistry

Benzene Derivatives

Benzene derivatives comprise monosubstituted and polysubstituted aromatic compounds whose chemical reactivity is heavily governed by the electronic and steric effects of the substituent group. Electrophilic aromatic substitution on these rings proceeds via resonance and inductive polarization, determining meta or ortho/para directing outcomes.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Directive Influence and Electronic Effects

  • Activating groups such as -OH, -NH2, and -R increase the electron density of the benzene ring through +R or +I effects, making it more reactive toward electrophiles.
  • Deactivating groups such as -NO2, -CN, and -COOH withdraw electron density via -R or -I effects, retarding electrophilic aromatic substitution.
  • Ortho/para directors comprise all activating groups and halogens, whereas meta directors consist entirely of deactivating groups bearing a positive or partial positive charge on the atom directly attached to the ring.

Reactivity in Electrophilic Substitution

  • Halogens are deactivating yet ortho/para-directing due to the opposing actions of their strong -I effect (deactivating) and weaker +R lone-pair donation (directing).
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation and acylation fail with strongly deactivated benzene rings bearing groups like -NO2, -SO3H, or amino groups that form complexes with the AlCl3 catalyst.
  • The rate-determining step in standard electrophilic aromatic substitution is the formation of the resonance-stabilized carbocation intermediate, known as the sigma complex or Wheland intermediate.

Reactions of Ring Substituents

  • Side-chain oxidation of alkylbenzenes with hot acidic KMnO4 always converts alkyl groups with a benzylic hydrogen into a benzoic acid group regardless of chain length.
  • Reduction of nitrobenzene yields distinct intermediates depending on the reducing agent, producing nitrosobenzene, phenylhydroxylamine, or aniline.
  • Sandmeyer reaction converts primary aromatic amines into aryl halides using Cu(I) salts via aryl radical intermediates generated from diazonium salts.

Hammett Equation
log(k / k0) = ρσ
Conditions: Applied to side-chain reactions of meta- and para-substituted benzene derivatives
Units: k and k0 are rate constants (dimensionless ratio), ρ is reaction constant, σ is substituent constant
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Halogens are typically deactivating and meta-directing like other deactivators.

Observation: Halogens are deactivating but ortho/para-directing. — Resonance stabilization by lone pair donation (+R effect) outweighs the inductive electron withdrawal (-I effect) at the ortho and para positions.

Exception Friedel-Crafts alkylation yields monoalkylated products predominantly.

Observation: Friedel-Crafts alkylation undergoes polyalkylation. — The alkyl group introduced is an activating group, making the newly formed alkylbenzene more reactive than the starting benzene.

Exception Primary alkyl halides undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation without rearrangement.

Observation: Propylbenzene and higher straight-chain alkyl groups rearrange during alkylation. — Formation of more stable secondary or tertiary carbocations via hydride shift drives the rearrangement.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Directing Group Mnemonic
All activating = o/p; Halogens = o/p (deactivators); All other deactivators = meta

Application: Instantly categorize any substituent in electrophilic substitution questions by checking if it has a lone pair (+R) or a multiple bond to an electronegative atom (-R).

Benzylic Oxidation Shortcut
Any alkyl side chain + KMnO4 / Δ → Benzoic Acid

Application: Ignore the chain length of alkylbenzenes during oxidation problems; the final product is always benzoic acid as long as at least one benzylic hydrogen exists.

Dipole Moment Symmetry Trick
p-Disubstituted (symmetrical) μ = 0; o-Disubstituted > m-Disubstituted

Application: Quickly rank dipole moments of disubstituted benzenes without calculation by examining vector cancellation angles (θ = 180° for para gives zero).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Toluidine reacts with nitrous acid at 0-5°C to form a stable diazonium salt.
  • Phenol undergoes bromination with aqueous Br2 to yield 2,4,6-tribromophenol instantly without a catalyst.
  • Nitrobenzene cannot undergo Friedel-Crafts reactions due to catalyst coordination with the nitro group.
  • Chlorobenzene is less reactive than benzene toward nucleophilic aromatic substitution unless strong electron-withdrawing groups are ortho/para to the chlorine.
  • Birch reduction of benzene yields 1,4-cyclohexadiene using sodium in liquid ammonia and an alcohol.
  • Gattermann-Koch reaction uses CO, HCl, and an anhydrous AlCl3/CuCl catalyst to convert benzene into benzaldehyde.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming halogens are activating because they are ortho/para directors.
  • Forgetting that Friedel-Crafts alkylation of propyl chloride yields isopropylbenzene due to carbocation rearrangement.
  • Applying KMnO4 oxidation to tert-butylbenzene and expecting benzoic acid (tert-butylbenzene lacks a benzylic hydrogen and does not react).
  • Writing meta-directed products for aniline in strong acid media where it exists as the anilinium ion (-NH3+), which is meta-directing.

⚡ Quick Revision: Benzene Derivatives

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Resonance Effects in Monosubstituted Benzenes

Substituents donate or withdraw electrons through resonance, placing negative charge at ortho and para positions for +R groups and positive charge for -R groups.

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution Mechanism

The mechanism proceeds via electrophile addition to form a non-aromatic sigma complex, followed by proton elimination to restore aromaticity.

Side-Chain Halogenation vs Ring Halogenation

Ring halogenation requires Lewis acid catalysts like FeBr3, whereas side-chain halogenation of alkylbenzenes proceeds via free radical mechanisms under UV light or heat.

Synthetic Utility of Diazonium Salts

Aromatic diazonium salts serve as versatile intermediates for introducing -F, -Cl, -Br, -I, -CN, -OH, and -H groups onto the benzene ring via replacement reactions.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Wheland Intermediate (Sigma Complex)
A non-aromatic carbocation intermediate formed during electrophilic aromatic substitution.
Hammett Constant (σ)
A quantitative measure of polar substituent effects in meta and para positions.
Birch Reduction (Na / NH3 (liq))
Partial reduction of benzene rings to 1,4-cyclohexadienes.
Sandmeyer Reaction (ArN2+ + CuX)
Transformation of diazonium salts to aryl halides using copper(I) salts.
Gattermann Reaction (ArH + HCN + HCl)
Formylation of aromatic rings using copper powder catalyst.
Benzyne (C6H4)
Highly reactive dehydrobenzene intermediate involved in nucleophilic aromatic substitution.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Activating groups direct incoming electrophiles to ortho and para positions.
  • Deactivating groups except halogens direct incoming electrophiles to the meta position.
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation is prone to carbocation rearrangements.
  • Side-chain oxidation of alkylbenzenes with KMnO4 always yields benzoic acid if a benzylic hydrogen is present.
  • Halogens are deactivating but ortho/para directing due to competing -I and +R effects.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Substituent effects dictate both the rate and regioselectivity of further substitution.
  • Resonance and inductive effects determine whether a group is activating or deactivating.
  • Aromatic side chains undergo specific free-radical halogenation and vigorous oxidation.
  • Diazonium chemistry bridges nitroaromatics to diverse functionalized benzene derivatives.

⚖️ Key Differences

Friedel-Crafts Alkylation VS Friedel-Crafts Acylation

Alkylation introduces an alkyl group and causes polyalkylation/rearrangement; acylation introduces an acyl group, deactivates the ring, and prevents over-reaction.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following groups is ortho/para-directing and deactivating?

Explanation: Halogens like -Cl exhibit a strong -I effect (deactivating) that outweighs their weaker +R lone-pair donation, making them ortho/para-directing deactivators.

Question 2 of 19

What is the primary product when propylbenzene is oxidized with hot alkaline KMnO4?

Explanation: Side-chain oxidation of any alkylbenzene with at least one benzylic hydrogen using KMnO4 invariably yields benzoic acid.

Question 3 of 19

In the nitration of toluene, the major product is:

Explanation: The methyl group in toluene is an activating ortho/para-director, directing the incoming nitro group to the ortho and para positions.

Question 4 of 19

Which reagent is used in the Gattermann-Koch reaction to convert benzene into benzaldehyde?

Explanation: Gattermann-Koch reaction utilizes carbon monoxide and hydrogen chloride in the presence of anhydrous aluminum chloride and cuprous chloride to form benzaldehyde.

Question 5 of 19

The intermediate formed during electrophilic aromatic substitution is known as:

Explanation: Electrophilic aromatic substitution proceeds via a resonance-stabilized carbocation intermediate termed the sigma complex or Wheland intermediate.

Question 6 of 19

Which of the following compounds has a dipole moment of zero?

Explanation: p-Dichlorobenzene is completely symmetrical, so the individual dipole moments of the C-Cl bonds point in opposite directions and cancel each other out completely.

Question 7 of 19

What is the product when phenol is treated with bromine water?

Explanation: The strongly activating -OH group activates all ortho and para positions, resulting in immediate precipitation of 2,4,6-tribromophenol upon treatment with aqueous bromine.

Question 8 of 19

Friedel-Crafts acylation of benzene with acetyl chloride in the presence of AlCl3 yields:

Explanation: Friedel-Crafts acylation introduces an acyl group (CH3CO-) onto the benzene ring, producing acetophenone.

Question 9 of 19

Why does aniline fail to undergo Friedel-Crafts reaction?

Explanation: The nitrogen lone pair on aniline coordinates with the Lewis acid catalyst (AlCl3), deactivating the ring and halting the Friedel-Crafts reaction.

Question 10 of 19

Which of the following is a meta-directing group?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is a strong electron-withdrawing group via -R and -I effects, directing incoming electrophiles to the meta position.

Question 11 of 19

Reduction of nitrobenzene with Zn and NaOH yields:

Explanation: Reduction of nitrobenzene using zinc dust and aqueous sodium hydroxide yields hydrazobenzene (1,2-diphenylhydrazine).

Question 12 of 19

Which of the following alkylbenzenes undergoes oxidation to benzoic acid most readily?

Explanation: All alkylbenzenes containing at least one benzylic hydrogen are oxidized by acidic KMnO4 to benzoic acid as the final aromatic product.

Question 13 of 19

In nucleophilic aromatic substitution via a benzyne intermediate, what is the hybridization of the two carbons sharing the triple bond?

Explanation: The triple bond in benzyne lies outside the aromatic ring system, and the participating carbons remain sp2 hybridized with the in-plane π bond formed by overlap of sp2 orbitals.

Question 14 of 19

Which directive effect dominates in halobenzenes?

Explanation: Although inductive withdrawal is strong, the resonance effect (+R) dictates the ortho/para regioselectivity in halobenzenes.

Question 15 of 19

What product is obtained when benzene is treated with excess chlorine in the presence of sunlight (UV light)?

Explanation: In the presence of UV light, chlorine adds across the benzene double bonds to form benzene hexachloride (1,2,3,4,5,6-hexachlorocyclohexane).

Question 16 of 19

Which catalyst is typically employed for chlorination of benzene ring?

Explanation: Electrophilic ring chlorination requires a Lewis acid catalyst such as FeCl3 or iron metal to generate the chloronium ion equivalent.

Question 17 of 19

The conversion of benzenediazonium chloride to chlorobenzene using CuCl/HCl is called:

Explanation: The reaction of diazonium salts with cuprous halides to form aryl halides is the Sandmeyer reaction.

Question 18 of 19

Which group exhibits a positive resonance effect (+R)?

Explanation: The hydroxyl group (-OH) donates electrons via resonance due to the presence of lone pairs on the oxygen atom, exhibiting a +R effect.

Question 19 of 19

What happens to the rate of nitration of benzene when a methoxy group is attached to the ring?

Explanation: The methoxy group (-OCH3) is strongly activating via +R electron donation, significantly increasing the rate of electrophilic nitration.

Question 1 of 10
Identify the major product formed when phenol is treated with concentrated HNO3 and concentrated H2SO4.

Solution: Treatment of phenol with concentrated nitric acid in the presence of concentrated sulfuric acid nitrates all available ortho and para positions, yielding 2,4,6-trinitrophenol.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following species is the electrophile in aromatic sulfonation?

Solution: Sulfuric acid generates sulfur trioxide (SO3), which acts as the potent neutral electrophile in aromatic sulfonation.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following disubstituted benzenes has the highest dipole moment?

Solution: The vector sum of dipole moments depends on the angle between substituents (θ). Ortho (θ = 60°) has the largest vector sum compared to meta (120°) and para (180°).

Question 4 of 10
In electrophilic aromatic substitution, the rate-determining step is:

Solution: The formation of the non-aromatic sigma complex by electrophilic attack disrupts aromaticity and represents the highest energy transition state, making it rate-determining.

Question 5 of 10
Which reaction does NOT yield benzene or a substituted benzene derivative?

Solution: Chlorobenzene is unreactive toward simple hydride reduction by LiAlH4 under normal conditions because of partial double bond character in the C-Cl bond.

Question 6 of 10
What is formed when toluene is treated with chlorine in the presence of sunlight?

Solution: Sunlight initiates free-radical chlorination of the benzylic side chain of toluene, leading successively to benzyl chloride, benzal chloride, and finally benzotrichloride.

Question 7 of 10
Which directive influence is exhibited by the -NHCOCH3 group on a benzene ring?

Solution: The acetamido group (-NHCOCH3) has its nitrogen lone pair delocalized toward the carbonyl group, making it a moderately activating ortho/para-director.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following compounds undergoes nucleophilic aromatic substitution most readily?

Solution: The presence of three strong electron-withdrawing nitro groups at ortho and para positions heavily depletes ring electron density, facilitating nucleophilic aromatic substitution via Meisenheimer intermediates.

Question 9 of 10
What is the reagent used in the Etard reaction to convert toluene into benzaldehyde?

Solution: The Etard reaction specifically converts methylbenzenes into benzaldehydes using chromyl chloride (CrO2Cl2) as the oxidizing agent.

Question 10 of 10
Which intermediate is trapped during the nucleophilic aromatic substitution of aryl halides via elimination-addition mechanism?

Solution: Strong bases react with unactivated aryl halides via an elimination-addition mechanism that proceeds through a highly reactive benzyne intermediate.
