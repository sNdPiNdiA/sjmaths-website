Organic Chemistry

Reaction Mechanism

Organic reaction mechanisms describe the step-by-step sequence of elementary reactions by which overall chemical changes occur. They involve bond cleavage, bond formation, and the generation of reactive intermediates.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Bond Fission and Reactive Intermediates

  • Homolytic cleavage results in the formation of free radicals, where each bonded atom retains one electron.
  • Heterolytic cleavage produces carbocations or carbanions due to unequal distribution of bonding electrons.
  • Carbocation stability follows the order: 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl, driven by hyperconjugation and inductive effects.
  • Carbon free radicals exhibit the same stability order (3° > 2° > 1°) stabilized by hyperconjugation.

Electronic Displacement Effects

  • The inductive effect (-I or +I) operates through sigma bonds and decreases rapidly with distance.
  • Resonance (mesomeric) effect involves delocalization of π-electrons through conjugation, altering electron density across the system.
  • Hyperconjugation involves the delocalization of σ(ψ) electrons of a C-H bond into an adjacent empty or partially filled p-orbital.
  • Electromeric effect (Ε) is a temporary polarization of a multiple bond in the presence of an attacking reagent.

Nucleophiles and Electrophiles

  • Nucleophiles are electron-rich species acting as Lewis bases, donating electron pairs to form new bonds.
  • Electrophiles are electron-deficient species acting as Lewis acids, accepting electron pairs.
  • Ambident nucleophiles contain two different nucleophilic centers, such as the cyanide ion (CN-) or nitrite ion (NO2-).
  • Steric hindrance significantly impacts the rate of bimolecular reactions by blocking the approach trajectory of the reagent.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception 1° carbocations are less stable than 2° carbocations

Observation: Cyclopropylmethyl carbocation exhibits exceptional stability — Bisected conformation allows optimal orbital overlap and charge delocalization into the cyclopropane ring.

Exception E2 eliminations proceed via anti-periplanar geometry

Observation: Syn-elimination occurs in rigid cyclic systems where anti-periplanar geometry is geometrically impossible — Pyrolytic eliminations such as acetate pyrolysis proceed via concerted syn-elimination through a cyclic transition state.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
CARB Mnemonic for Carbocation Stability
3° > 2° > 1° > CH3+

Application: Remember that more alkyl substituents provide more hyperconjugative structures and +I stabilization.

SN1 vs SN2 Conditions
SN1 = Polar Protic Solvents + Weak Nucleophile; SN2 = Polar Aprotic Solvents + Strong Nucleophile

Application: Quickly identify mechanism type based on solvent polarity and nucleophile strength in exam questions.

Markownikoff's Rule Shortcut
H goes to more hydrogens

Application: Predicts the major carbocation intermediate and final addition product in electrophilic additions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Carbocations are sp2 hybridized with a planar geometry and an empty unhybridized p-orbital.
  • Carbanions are sp3 hybridized with a pyramidal geometry and possess a lone pair of electrons.
  • Free radicals are planar or shallow pyramidal with sp2 hybridization of the radical carbon.
  • The stability of carbanions follows the reverse order of carbocations: 1° > 2° > 3° due to +I alkyl group electron-donating destabilization.
  • Hyperconjugation is also known as no-bond resonance.
  • Primary alkyl halides predominantly react via the SN2 pathway due to minimal steric hindrance.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the role of polar protic and polar aprotic solvents in nucleophilic substitution reactions.
  • Assuming all elimination reactions follow the Zaitsev rule without checking for bulky bases giving Hofmann products.
  • Forgetting that carbocation rearrangements can occur via 1,2-hydride or 1,2-methyl shifts to form more stable carbocations.
  • Confusing electrophilicity with acidity or nucleophilicity with basicity.

⚡ Quick Revision: Reaction Mechanism

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Bond Cleavage Types

Bonds undergo homolytic cleavage to form free radicals or heterolytic cleavage to form ions.

Electronic Effects

Inductive, resonance, and hyperconjugative effects dictate electron density distribution and molecular stability.

Reaction Intermediates

Carbocations, carbanions, free radicals, and carbenes act as short-lived high-energy species during reactions.

Substitution and Elimination

Substrate structure, reagent strength, and solvent dictate whether SN1, SN2, E1, or E2 pathways predominate.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Carbocation (R3C+)
Positively charged carbon species with six valence electrons.
Carbanion (R3C-)
Negatively charged carbon species with eight valence electrons.
Free Radical (R3C•)
Neutral species containing an unpaired electron.
Nucleophile (Nu-)
Electron-rich reagent that donates an electron pair.
Electrophile (E+)
Electron-deficient reagent that accepts an electron pair.
Transition State (‡)
Maximum energy point along the reaction coordinate.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carbocation stability order: 3° > 2° > 1°
  • Carbanion stability order: 1° > 2° > 3°
  • SN2 proceeds with 100% inversion of configuration.
  • SN1 proceeds with racemization.
  • Zaitsev's rule predicts the more substituted alkene as the major elimination product.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Reaction mechanisms track bond breaking and making events.
  • Intermediates determine stereochemical outcomes.
  • Solvents and reagents dictate mechanistic pathways.
  • Electronic effects rationalize stability and reactivity trends.

⚖️ Key Differences

Inductive Effect VS Resonance Effect

Inductive operates through sigma bonds and drops off with distance; resonance operates through pi bond conjugation and does not depend on distance.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following carbocations is the most stable?

Explanation: Tertiary carbocations are stabilized by maximum hyperconjugative structures and inductive electron donation.

Question 2 of 18

What is the hybridization of the central carbon in a carbocation?

Explanation: Carbocations have three bond pairs and no lone pairs, resulting in trigonal planar geometry with sp2 hybridization.

Question 3 of 18

Which reaction intermediate possesses a sextet of electrons?

Explanation: A carbocation has a positive charge and six electrons in its valence shell.

Question 4 of 18

Walden inversion is a characteristic feature of which mechanism?

Explanation: SN2 reactions proceed via backside attack, leading to complete inversion of configuration (Walden inversion).

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following is a polar aprotic solvent?

Explanation: Acetone is a polar aprotic solvent because it lacks acidic hydrogen atoms capable of forming hydrogen bonds.

Question 6 of 18

Which species acts as an electrophile?

Explanation: AlCl3 has an incomplete octet on aluminum, acting as a Lewis acid and electrophile.

Question 7 of 18

The rate determining step in an SN1 reaction involves:

Explanation: The slow, rate-determining step of SN1 is the ionization of the substrate to form a carbocation.

Question 8 of 18

Which effect operates through space or sigma bonds and is distance-dependent?

Explanation: The inductive effect is transmitted through sigma bonds and diminishes rapidly along the carbon chain.

Question 9 of 18

What is the geometry of a carbanion?

Explanation: Carbanions have three bond pairs and one lone pair, leading to a pyramidal geometry (sp3 hybridized).

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following compounds will react fastest in an SN2 reaction?

Explanation: Methyl chloride has the least steric hindrance, facilitating rapid backside attack in SN2 reactions.

Question 11 of 18

Homolytic bond cleavage produces:

Explanation: Homolytic cleavage results in equal sharing of bonding electrons, yielding free radicals.

Question 12 of 18

Which stability order is correct for free radicals?

Explanation: Alkyl radicals are stabilized by hyperconjugation, following the order 3° > 2° > 1°.

Question 13 of 18

Which intermediate is involved in the addition of HBr to alkenes in the presence of peroxides?

Explanation: Peroxide effect (anti-Markownikoff addition) proceeds via a free radical chain mechanism.

Question 14 of 18

Which reagent is an ambident nucleophile?

Explanation: Cyanide ion can attack through carbon or nitrogen, making it an ambident nucleophile.

Question 15 of 18

Hyperconjugation involves overlap between which orbitals?

Explanation: Hyperconjugation involves the interaction between a σ bond (C-H) and a neighboring p or π orbital.

Question 16 of 18

Which elimination pathway is concerted and lacks an intermediate?

Explanation: E2 is a concerted, single-step elimination reaction where proton abstraction and leaving group departure occur simultaneously.

Question 17 of 18

Which compound exhibits the strongest -I effect?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is a strongly electron-withdrawing group exerting a significant -I effect.

Question 18 of 18

In the reaction of tert-butyl chloride with water, the mechanism is predominantly:

Explanation: Tertiary alkyl halides undergo substitution via the SN1 mechanism due to stable carbocation formation in polar protic solvents.

Question 1 of 10
What is the order of stability of carbanions: (CH3)3C-, (CH3)2CH-, CH3CH2-, CH3-?

Solution: Alkyl groups donate electrons via +I effect, destabilizing carbanions. Hence, methyl carbanion is most stable.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following species is aromatic?

Solution: Cyclopentadienyl anion has 6 π-electrons, satisfying Hückel's rule (4n + 2 where n = 1).

Question 3 of 10
Which type of cleavage results in the generation of free radicals?

Solution: Homolytic fission involves symmetrical breaking of a covalent bond to form free radicals.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following represents an electrophilic addition reaction?

Solution: Addition of bromine to alkenes proceeds via an electrophilic cyclic bromonium ion intermediate.

Question 5 of 10
Which solvent best promotes an SN2 reaction?

Solution: DMSO is a polar aprotic solvent that enhances nucleophile reactivity by not solvating anions strongly.

Question 6 of 10
Which molecule exhibits optical isomerism due to restricted rotation?

Solution: Certain substituted biphenyls exhibit atropisomerism due to restricted rotation around the central C-C bond.

Question 7 of 10
Which effect is permanent and operates through π-system delocalization?

Solution: Resonance is a permanent electronic effect involving delocalization of π-electrons.

Question 8 of 10
In E1 elimination, the base:

Solution: E1 is unimolecular; the rate-determining step is ionization of substrate, independent of base concentration.

Question 9 of 10
Which compound is most reactive toward electrophilic aromatic substitution?

Solution: Anisole contains a strongly activating -OCH3 group via resonance, increasing electron density on the ring.

Question 10 of 10
What is the primary product when 2-bromobutane is treated with alcoholic KOH?

Solution: Elimination follows Zaitsev's rule, yielding 2-butene as the major stable substituted alkene product.
