Reaction Mechanism (Organic Chemistry)

Reaction mechanisms describe the step-by-step sequence of elementary reactions by which overall chemical changes occur. They involve bond cleavage, intermediate formation, and bond creation dictated by electronic and steric factors.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Bond Fission and Reactive Intermediates

  • Homolytic cleavage results in the formation of free radicals possessing an unpaired electron.
  • Heterolytic cleavage yields ionic species, specifically carbocations (sp2 hybridized, sextet of electrons) and carbanions (sp3 hybridized, octet with a lone pair).
  • Carbocation stability follows the order: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3+ due to hyperconjugation and inductive effects.
  • Carbon free radical stability parallels carbocations: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3•.

Electronic Effects and Displacement

  • The inductive effect (-I/+I) operates through σ-bonds and drops off rapidly with distance.
  • Resonance (Mesomeric) effect involves the delocalization of π-electrons through conjugation, providing significant stabilization.
  • Electromeric effect (+E/-E) is a temporary polarization of a multiple bond in the presence of an attacking reagent.
  • Hyperconjugation involves the interaction of σ-electrons of a C-H bond with an adjacent empty or partially filled p-orbital (σ-π conjugation).

Types of Organic Reactions and Kinetics

  • SN1 reactions proceed via a two-step mechanism through a planar carbocation intermediate with first-order kinetics.
  • SN2 reactions proceed via a concerted single-step mechanism with Walden inversion and second-order kinetics.
  • E1 elimination proceeds via a carbocation intermediate, whereas E2 elimination requires anti-periplanar geometry in a concerted step.
  • Electrophilic aromatic substitution (EAS) proceeds via a resonance-stabilized σ-complex (arenium ion).
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Hammett Equation for Linear Free Energy Relationships
log(k / k0) = ρ σ
Conditions: Applied to meta- and para-substituted benzene derivatives
Units: k and k0 are rate constants, ρ is reaction constant, σ is substituent constant
Kinetic Isotope Effect (KIE)
KIE = kH / kD
Conditions: Primary KIE when bond to the isotopically labeled atom is broken in the rate-determining step
Units: Dimensionless ratio of rate constants
Reaction Enthalpy from Bond Dissociation Energies
ΔH° = ∑ BDE(bonds broken) - ∑ BDE(bonds formed)
Conditions: Gas phase homolytic bond cleavage approximations
Units: ΔH° and BDE in kJ mol-1
Arrhenius Equation for Reaction Rates
k = A e-Ea / RT
Conditions: Applicable to elementary steps and temperature-dependent reaction rates
Units: k in s-1 or M-1s-1, Ea in J mol-1, R in J K-1mol-1, T in K
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Tertiary alkyl halides undergo SN1 substitution.

Observation: Bridgehead tertiary halides (e.g., 1-bromobicyclo[2.2.1]heptane) are unreactive toward SN1. — Carbocations cannot achieve the required sp2 planar geometry at the bridgehead position due to ring strain.

Exception E2 elimination yields Zaitsev's rule product (more substituted alkene) as the major product.

Observation: Bulky bases (e.g., potassium tert-butoxide) yield Hoffman's product (less substituted alkene). — Steric hindrance prevents the bulky base from abstracting the internal hindered proton, favoring terminal proton removal.

Exception Electron-donating groups (e.g., -CH3) direct incoming electrophiles to ortho/para positions in benzene.

Observation: Nitrobenzene undergoes meta-substitution exclusively. — The strong -M and -I effects of the nitro group strongly deplete electron density at the ortho and para positions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Carbocations are sp2 hybridized with a vacant p-orbital and trigonal planar geometry.
  • Free radicals are sp2 hybridized with an odd electron residing in an unhybridized p-orbital.
  • Carbanions are sp3 hybridized with pyramidal geometry and a lone pair of electrons.
  • SN2 reactions proceed with complete inversion of configuration at the chiral center.
  • E2 eliminations require anti-periplanar geometry of the hydrogen and leaving group.
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation can suffer from carbocation rearrangement, whereas acylation does not.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing carbocation rearrangement in SN1/E1 reactions with concerted SN2/E2 pathways.
  • Assuming primary carbocations form readily without rearrangement or hydride/methide shift.
  • Forgetting that halogens are deactivating yet ortho/para directing in aromatic substitution.
  • Neglecting steric hindrance when predicting SN2 versus E2 reaction pathways.

⚡ Quick Revision: Reaction Mechanism

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Bond Cleavage and Intermediates

Homolytic fission generates free radicals, while heterolytic fission generates carbocations and carbanions. Stability is governed by inductive, resonance, and hyperconjugative effects.

Nucleophilic Substitution

SN1 is a two-step process via a carbocation leading to racemization, while SN2 is a concerted single-step displacement resulting in inversion of configuration.

Elimination Reactions

E1 proceeds via a carbocation and gives Zaitsev's product, while E2 is concerted, requiring anti-periplanar orientation and yielding Zaitsev or Hofmann products based on base bulkiness.

Aromatic Electrophilic Substitution

Involves attack of an electrophile on the aromatic ring to form a non-aromatic σ-complex (arenium ion), followed by proton loss to restore aromaticity.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Carbocation (R3C+)
A positively charged carbon species with six valence electrons.
Carbanion (R3C-)
A negatively charged carbon species with eight valence electrons.
Free Radical (R3C•)
A neutral species containing an unpaired electron.
Electrophile (E+)
An electron-deficient species seeking electron-rich centers.
Nucleophile (Nu-)
An electron-rich species donating an electron pair.
Transition State (TS)
A high-energy state at the maximum of a reaction coordinate profile.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carbocation stability: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3+
  • SN2 rate depends on both substrate and nucleophile concentrations.
  • E2 mechanism requires anti-periplanar geometry.
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation is prone to carbocation rearrangement.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups deactivate the benzene ring toward electrophilic attack.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Reaction mechanisms track bond-making and bond-breaking steps.
  • Intermediates like carbocations determine substitution and elimination pathways.
  • Electronic effects (inductive, resonance, hyperconjugation) explain stability trends.
  • Stereochemical outcomes differentiate concerted from stepwise mechanisms.

⚖️ Key Differences

SN1 Reaction VS SN2 Reaction

SN1 is unimolecular with a carbocation intermediate and racemization; SN2 is bimolecular, concerted, and inverts configuration.

Which group is meta-directing in electrophilic aromatic substitution?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is strongly electron-withdrawing via resonance and inductive effects, directing incoming groups to the meta position.

Question 7 of 18

In the dehydration of alcohols to alkenes via E1 mechanism, the rate-determining step is:

Explanation: The formation of the carbocation by loss of the protonated hydroxyl group is the slow, rate-determining step in E1.

Which of the following has the highest stability?

Explanation: Tertiary carbocations are stabilized by maximum hyperconjugative structures and inductive electron donation from three alkyl groups.

Question 9 of 18

Which solvent favors SN2 reactions?

Explanation: Acetone is a polar aprotic solvent, which solvates cations but leaves nucleophiles un-solvated and highly reactive, favoring SN2.

Question 10 of 18

Explanation: Hydroxide ion (OH-) acts as a nucleophile substituting the halide ion in a nucleophilic substitution reaction.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following compounds will give a positive test with alcoholic silver nitrate?

Explanation: Tert-butyl chloride readily ionizes to form a stable tertiary carbocation and precipitates AgCl instantly with ethanolic AgNO3.

Question 12 of 18

The geometry of a carbanion is typically:

Explanation: Carbanions possess three bond pairs and one lone pair, leading to a pyramidal geometry similar to ammonia.

Question 13 of 18

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following effects is permanent?

Explanation: The inductive effect is a permanent polarization present in molecules with electronegative atoms, whereas the electromeric effect is temporary.

Question 16 of 18

Explanation: The attack of an electrophile on benzene forms a resonance-stabilized carbocation known as the arenium ion or σ-complex.

Question 17 of 18

Question 18 of 18

What is the primary role of anhydrous AlCl3 in Friedel-Crafts reactions?

Solution: Anhydrous AlCl3 is a Lewis acid that coordinates with alkyl/acyl halides to generate the reactive carbocation or acylium ion electrophile.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following carbocations is most stable?

Solution: Tertiary butyl carbocation (CH3)3C+ is stabilized by hyperconjugation across nine α-hydrogens.

Question 3 of 10
In the SN1 solvolysis of optically active 2-bromobutane, the product formed is:

Solution: SN1 proceeds via a planar achiral carbocation, which is attacked equally from both faces, yielding a racemic mixture.

Question 4 of 10
Which species is ambident nucleophile?

Solution: Cyanide ion (CN-) can attack through carbon or nitrogen, making it an ambident nucleophile.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following has the strongest +I effect?

Solution: Inductive electron-donating (+I) power increases with alkyl substitution branching: tert-butyl > isopropyl > ethyl > methyl.

Question 6 of 10
What is the geometry of a carbocation?

Solution: Carbocations are sp2 hybridized with three bonding pairs and an empty p-orbital, forming a trigonal planar arrangement.

Question 7 of 10
Which compound does not undergo nucleophilic substitution easily?

Solution: Chlorobenzene (C6H5Cl) has partial double-bond character in the C-Cl bond due to resonance, making it unreactive toward nucleophilic substitution.

Question 8 of 10
Which rule governs regioselectivity in elimination reactions yielding the more substituted alkene?

Solution: Saytzeff's rule states that elimination of hydrogen halide from an alkyl halide yields the more substituted, more stable alkene as the major product.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following intermediates is electron-deficient and acts as an electrophile?

Solution: Carbenes possess six valence electrons and act as electron-deficient electrophilic species.

Question 10 of 10
The rate expression for an SN1 reaction is:

Solution: SN1 is unimolecular; the rate-determining step depends solely on the concentration of the substrate.
