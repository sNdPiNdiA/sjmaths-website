Organic Chemistry

Simple Addition Reactions

Simple addition reactions involve the combination of two or more molecules to form a single product, characteristic of unsaturated systems containing double or triple bonds. These processes typically proceed via electrophilic, nucleophilic, or free-radical pathways depending on the electronic nature of the substrate and reagent.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrophilic Addition to Alkenes and Alkynes

  • Electrophilic addition proceeds in two steps: slow electrophile attack forming a carbocation or cyclic intermediate, followed by fast nucleophile capture.
  • The reaction rate is dependent on the stability of the intermediate carbocation, where tertiary carbocations form faster than secondary and primary.
  • Markovnikov's rule states that the electrophilic portion (proton) adds to the carbon with a greater number of hydrogen atoms to yield the more stable carbocation.
  • Halogenation of alkenes proceeds through a cyclic halonium ion, forcing anti-addition stereochemistry to yield trans-dihalides.

Free Radical Addition and Stereochemistry

  • The peroxide effect reverses the regioselectivity of HBr addition to alkenes through a homolytic radical chain mechanism.
  • Free radical addition is not observed with HCl or HI because the propagation steps are thermodynamically unfavorable.
  • Catalytic hydrogenation of alkenes exclusively yields syn-addition products due to the simultaneous delivery of hydrogen atoms from the metal catalyst surface.
  • Epoxidation of alkenes with peroxycarboxylic acids (Prilezhajev reaction) yields syn-oxiranes via a concerted mechanism.

Nucleophilic Addition to Carbonyl Compounds

  • Aldehydes and ketones undergo nucleophilic addition across the polar carbon-oxygen double bond due to the electrophilic nature of the carbonyl carbon.
  • Steric hindrance and electronic factors (inductive and resonance effects of substituents) dictate the relative reactivity of carbonyl compounds.
  • Cyanohydrin formation involves the attack of cyanide ion on the carbonyl carbon followed by protonation to form an α-hydroxynitrile.
  • Addition of Grignard reagents to aldehydes and ketones yields metal alkoxides, which upon acidic hydrolysis produce alcohols.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Anti-Markovnikov's Rule (Peroxide Effect)
R−CH=CH2 + HBr &xrightarrow[(RCOO)2]{} R−CH2−CH2Br
Conditions: Free radical addition in the presence of organic peroxides
Units: Regioselective product distribution via radical stability
Hydrogenation of Alkenes
R−CH=CH−R + H2 &xrightarrow[Pt/Pd/Ni]{} R−CH2−CH2−R
Conditions: Heterogeneous catalytic hydrogenation with metal surface adsorption
Units: Syn-addition yielding saturated hydrocarbons
Halogenation of Alkenes
R−CH=CH−R + X2 → R−CH(X)−CH(X)−R
Conditions: Electrophilic addition via cyclic halonium ion intermediate
Units: Anti-addition stereospecificity
Hydration of Alkynes
R−C≡CH + H2O &xrightarrow[HgSO4]{H2SO4} R−C(=O)−CH3
Conditions: Electrophilic addition followed by keto-enol tautomerism
Units: Yields carbonyl compounds (aldehydes or ketones)
Markovnikov's Rule Orientation
R−CH=CH2 + HBr → R−CH(Br)−CH3
Conditions: Polar addition of unsymmetrical reagents to unsymmetrical alkenes
Units: Regioselective product distribution based on carbocation stability
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Addition of hydrogen halides to alkenes follows Markovnikov's rule

Observation: Addition of HBr in the presence of organic peroxides yields Anti-Markovnikov products — Presence of peroxides initiates a free-radical chain mechanism rather than an ionic electrophilic pathway

Exception All hydrogen halides (HCl, HBr, HI) exhibit the peroxide effect

Observation: HCl and HI do not exhibit the peroxide effect with terminal alkenes — Propagation step involving HCl is endothermic due to strong H-Cl bond, while HI addition is reversible because the C-I radical easily cleaves

Exception Addition of halogens to alkenes produces racemic mixtures via cyclic halonium ions

Observation: Addition of chlorine to acetylene yields trans-1,2-dichloroethene predominantly as a kinetic product — Alkynes possess lower nucleophilicity than alkenes, altering the lifetime and stability of cyclic chloronium intermediates

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Carbocation stability order: 3° > 2° > 1° > Methyl.
  • Syn-addition occurs exclusively during catalytic hydrogenation over Pt, Pd, or Ni.
  • Addition of water to alkynes in the presence of HgSO4 and H2SO4 produces ketones except for acetylene which yields acetaldehyde.
  • Epoxidation of alkenes occurs via a concerted stereospecific syn-addition mechanism.
  • Bisulfite addition complexes are water-soluble crystalline solids used to separate and purify carbonyl compounds.
  • Grignard reagents react with formaldehyde to give primary alcohols, other aldehydes to give secondary alcohols, and ketones to give tertiary alcohols.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming HCl and HI undergo radical addition in the presence of peroxides just like HBr.
  • Confusing the regioselectivity of acid-catalyzed hydration (Markovnikov) with hydroboration-oxidation (Anti-Markovnikov).
  • Forgetting that carbocation rearrangements (1,2-hydride or 1,2-methyl shifts) can occur during electrophilic addition reactions.
  • Ignoring stereochemical constraints such as syn vs. anti addition pathways in cyclic systems.

⚡ Quick Revision: Simple Addition Reactions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrophilic Addition Fundamentals

Electrophilic addition reactions target π-electron clouds of alkenes and alkynes. The regioselectivity is strictly governed by carbocation stability according to Markovnikov's rule.

Stereochemical Control

Halogenation proceeds with anti-stereospecificity via cyclic halonium ions, whereas catalytic hydrogenation proceeds with syn-stereospecificity via metal surface adsorption.

Radical Addition Pathways

In the presence of peroxides, HBr adds via a free-radical chain mechanism resulting in anti-Markovnikov regioselectivity, which is unique to HBr among hydrogen halides.

Nucleophilic Addition to Carbonyls

Carbonyl groups undergo nucleophilic addition where the nucleophile attacks the electrophilic sp2 carbon, forming a tetrahedral intermediate followed by protonation.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Markovnikov's Rule (Regioselective addition)
Addition rule where the electrophile bonds to the carbon with more hydrogen atoms.
Halonium Ion (Three-membered cyclic intermediate)
Bridged cationic intermediate formed during electrophilic halogenation of alkenes.
Syn Addition (Same-side addition)
Addition of two groups to the same face of a double or triple bond.
Anti Addition (Opposite-side addition)
Addition of two groups to opposite faces of a double or triple bond.
Peroxide Effect (Kharasch effect)
Reversal of Markovnikov addition for HBr in the presence of peroxides.
Tautomerism (Keto-enol equilibrium)
Rapid interconversion of structural isomers through proton transfer and double bond shift.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carbocation stability order: 3° > 2° > 1°
  • Halogenation gives anti-addition products
  • Catalytic hydrogenation gives syn-addition products
  • HBr with peroxide gives anti-Markovnikov addition
  • Hydration of alkynes yields carbonyl compounds via tautomerism

📌 Quick Recall

  • Addition reactions convert unsaturated hydrocarbons into saturated derivatives.
  • Electrophilic additions proceed via carbocation or bridged intermediates.
  • Radical pathways override ionic rules when peroxides are present.
  • Carbonyl additions involve nucleophilic attack on the polarized C=O bond.

⚖️ Key Differences

Acid-Catalyzed Hydration VS Hydroboration-Oxidation

Acid-catalyzed hydration follows Markovnikov's rule with carbocation rearrangements; hydroboration-oxidation gives anti-Markovnikov syn-hydration without rearrangements.

What is the primary intermediate formed during the electrophilic addition of HX to an alkene?

Explanation: Electrophilic addition begins with protonation of the alkene to form a carbocation intermediate.

Question 2 of 19

Which reagent exhibits the peroxide effect during addition to propene?

Explanation: Only HBr undergoes free-radical addition in the presence of peroxides due to favorable thermodynamics of propagation steps.

Question 3 of 19

What is the stereochemical outcome of halogenation of alkenes?

Explanation: Halogenation proceeds via a cyclic halonium ion intermediate, resulting in anti-addition.

Question 4 of 19

Catalytic hydrogenation of alkenes is an example of:

Explanation: Hydrogen atoms are delivered simultaneously from the metal catalyst surface, leading to syn-addition.

Question 5 of 19

What is the product of hydration of acetylene in the presence of HgSO4 and H2SO4?

Explanation: Hydration of acetylene yields vinyl alcohol which tautomerizes to acetaldehyde.

Question 6 of 19

Which of the following carbocations is the most stable?

Explanation: Tertiary carbocations are stabilized by hyperconjugation and inductive effects of three alkyl groups.

Question 7 of 19

In the addition of HBr to 2-methylpropene without peroxides, the major product is:

Explanation: Markovnikov addition places the bromine atom on the more substituted tertiary carbon to form 2-bromo-2-methylpropane.

Question 8 of 19

Which reaction does NOT involve a carbocation intermediate?

Explanation: Hydroboration-oxidation proceeds via a concerted syn-addition mechanism without forming a carbocation.

Question 9 of 19

What functional group is formed when a Grignard reagent reacts with formaldehyde followed by acidification?

Explanation: Formaldehyde reacts with Grignard reagents to yield primary alcohols after acid hydrolysis.

Question 10 of 19

Which species acts as the electrophile in the nitration or halogenation of alkenes?

Explanation: Polarized halogen molecules or cyclic halonium ions act as electrophiles attacking the pi bond.

Question 11 of 19

What is the role of HgSO4 in the hydration of alkynes?

Explanation: Mercury(II) coordinates with the alkyne triple bond to facilitate nucleophilic attack by water.

Question 12 of 19

Which of the following compounds is most reactive toward nucleophilic addition?

Explanation: Formaldehyde is the least sterically hindered and most electronically deficient carbonyl compound, making it the most reactive.

Question 13 of 19

Addition of bromine to trans-2-butene yields primarily:

Explanation: Anti-addition of bromine to trans-2-butene yields the meso diastereomer.

Question 14 of 19

What is the intermediate formed during the oxymercuration-demercuration of alkenes?

Explanation: Oxymercuration proceeds via a cyclic mercurinium ion, preventing carbocation rearrangements.

Question 15 of 19

Which reagent converts an alkene into a 1,2-diol with syn-stereospecificity?

Explanation: Cold dilute alkaline KMnO4 (Baeyer's reagent) hydroxylates alkenes with syn-stereospecificity.

Question 16 of 19

Why is HCl addition to alkenes not affected by peroxides?

Explanation: The propagation step involving chlorine radical addition is endothermic, preventing the radical chain reaction.

Question 17 of 19

What is the final product when propyne undergoes acid-catalyzed hydration?

Explanation: Hydration of propyne yields an enol that tautomerizes to propanone (acetone).

Question 18 of 19

Which of the following represents an example of nucleophilic addition-elimination?

Explanation: Reaction of carbonyls with ammonia derivatives (like 2,4-DNP) involves nucleophilic addition followed by elimination of water.

Question 19 of 19

In electrophilic addition to unsymmetrical alkenes, rearrangement can occur when:

Explanation: Carbocation intermediates undergo 1,2-shifts if a more stable carbocation can be generated.

Question 1 of 10
What is the major product formed when 2-methyl-2-butene reacts with HCl?

Solution: Protonation yields a tertiary carbocation at C2, which is captured by chloride ion to form 2-chloro-2-methylbutane.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following compounds will not show geometrical isomerism in addition products formed by halogenation?

Solution: 1-butene is a terminal alkene and its halogenation product does not exhibit geometrical isomerism.

Question 3 of 10
What is the primary product of hydroboration-oxidation of 1-methylcyclohexene?

Solution: Hydroboration-oxidation proceeds via syn-addition of water in an anti-Markovnikov fashion, yielding cis-2-methylcyclohexanol.

Question 4 of 10
In the reaction of carbonyl compounds with sodium bisulfite, the crystalline precipitate is formed due to:

Solution: Bisulfite forms a water-soluble crystalline addition complex via nucleophilic attack on the carbonyl carbon.

Question 5 of 10
Which alkene reacts fastest with electrophilic HBr?

Solution: 2-methylpropene forms a tertiary carbocation, which is the most stable and forms at the fastest rate.

Question 6 of 10
What is the stereochemistry of the diol formed by treating cis-2-butene with cold alkaline KMnO4?

Solution: Syn-hydroxylation of cis-2-butene yields the meso-2,3-butanediol diastereomer.

Question 7 of 10
Why does HI fail to undergo peroxide-catalyzed anti-Markovnikov addition?

Solution: The addition of an iodine radical to an alkene is endothermic and readily reversible, preventing the propagation chain.

Question 8 of 10
What intermediate is trapped when alkenes are treated with peroxyacids?

Solution: Peroxyacids transfer an oxygen atom to the alkene in a concerted reaction, yielding an epoxide.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following reagents converts an alkyne to a cis-alkene?

Solution: Lindlar's catalyst (poisoned palladium) reduces alkynes to cis-alkenes via syn-addition of hydrogen.

Question 10 of 10
What is the product when methyl magnesium bromide reacts with acetaldehyde followed by hydrolysis?

Solution: Reaction of acetaldehyde (a 2-carbon aldehyde) with methyl Grignard (1-carbon) yields propan-2-ol, a secondary alcohol.
