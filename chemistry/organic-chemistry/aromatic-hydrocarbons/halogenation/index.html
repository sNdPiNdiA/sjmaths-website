Halogenation

Aromatic halogenation involves the electrophilic substitution of an aromatic ring hydrogen by a halogen atom using a Lewis acid catalyst. The reaction proceeds via a positively charged sigma-complex intermediate followed by proton elimination to regenerate aromaticity.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution Mechanism

  • The Lewis acid catalyst (e.g., FeCl3) interacts with the halogen molecule to generate a strong electrophile (Cl+ or Br+).
  • Attack of the pi-electron cloud of the benzene ring on the electrophile forms a non-aromatic carbocation intermediate known as the sigma-complex or arenium ion.
  • Loss of a proton from the sp3 hybridized carbon of the sigma-complex restores the stable aromatic sextet.
  • Halogens are deactivating yet ortho/para-directing groups in electrophilic aromatic substitution due to the balance between inductive electron withdrawal and resonance electron donation.

Thermodynamics and Specific Halogen Reactivity

  • Fluorination is violently exothermic and uncontrollable, requiring specialized reagents and conditions to yield monofluoro derivatives.
  • Chlorination and bromination proceed smoothly in the presence of appropriate Lewis acid catalysts with moderate activation energies.
  • Iodination is thermodynamically unfavorable and reversible because the byproduct HI acts as a strong reducing agent converting aryl iodide back to benzene.
  • Addition of an oxidizing agent like iodic acid (HIO3) or nitric acid is necessary during iodination to consume HI and drive the equilibrium forward.

Side-Chain vs Ring Halogenation

  • Nuclear halogenation occurs on the aromatic ring using Lewis acid catalysts in the dark via an ionic electrophilic pathway.
  • Side-chain halogenation of alkylbenzenes occurs via a free-radical mechanism requiring ultraviolet light or high temperature.
  • Free-radical halogenation of toluene yields benzyl chloride, benzal chloride, or benzotrichloride sequentially depending on the molar ratio of halogen.
  • Radical stability dictates that abstraction of a benzylic hydrogen is much faster than abstraction of an aromatic ring hydrogen.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Halogens are generally deactivating groups in electrophilic aromatic substitution.

Observation: Halogens direct incoming electrophiles predominantly to the ortho and para positions. — Resonance stabilization involving lone pairs on the halogen atom outweighs the inductive electron-withdrawing effect at the ortho and para positions.

Exception Electrophilic halogenation of benzene requires a Lewis acid catalyst.

Observation: Phenol and aniline undergo rapid halogenation (e.g., bromination with bromine water) without any catalyst. — The strongly activating -OH and -NH2 groups drastically increase the electron density of the aromatic ring.

Exception Halogens add across carbon-carbon double bonds via ionic electrophilic addition.

Observation: Allylic or benzylic substitution occurs instead of addition when alkenes react with halogen at high temperatures or low concentrations. — High temperature or photochemical conditions favor the homolytic cleavage of halogen molecules into free radicals over ionic addition.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Catalyst Pairing Mnemonic
Cl2/AlCl3 and Br2/FeBr3

Application: Match the halogen with its corresponding metal halide catalyst; usually, the metal matches the halogen anion used.

Directing Group Rule
All activating groups + Halogens = ortho/para directors

Application: Remember that despite being deactivators, halogens direct incoming groups to ortho and para positions unlike nitro or carbonyl groups.

Light vs Dark Condition Trick
Dark + Acid = Ring; Light + Heat = Side Chain

Application: Quickly identify whether the reaction takes place on the aromatic ring or the aliphatic side-chain based on reaction conditions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Fluorine reacts explosively with benzene and cannot be used directly for standard aromatic fluorination.
  • Aromatic iodination requires an oxidizing agent like HNO3 or HIO3 to oxidize the formed HI.
  • Bromination of phenol with aqueous bromine gives 2,4,6-tribromophenol as a white precipitate without a catalyst.
  • In side-chain chlorination of toluene under UV light, benzyl chloride is formed via a free-radical chain mechanism.
  • The sigma-complex intermediate in electrophilic aromatic substitution is sp3 hybridized at the attacked carbon.
  • Halogens deactivate the benzene ring because their inductive electron withdrawal (-I) exceeds their resonance donation (+R) in pulling electron density.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming halogens are meta-directing because they are deactivating groups; halogens are ortho/para-directing.
  • Using a Lewis acid catalyst for the photochemical side-chain halogenation of alkylbenzenes.
  • Forgetting to include an oxidizing agent when writing the reaction for direct aromatic iodination.
  • Confusing the substitution of benzene rings with addition reactions across alkenes under similar reagent conditions.

⚡ Quick Revision: Halogenation

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrophilic Substitution Pathway

Aromatic rings undergo electrophilic halogenation via generation of a halonium electrophile using Lewis acid catalysts like FeCl3 or AlCl3.

Anomalous Halogen Orientation

Halogens deactivate the aromatic ring inductively yet direct incoming electrophiles to the ortho and para positions due to resonance stabilization of the intermediate.

Thermodynamics of Iodination

Direct iodination is reversible because HI is a strong reducing agent; an oxidizing agent must be added to consume HI.

Photochemical Side-Chain Reaction

Alkylbenzenes undergo free-chain radical substitution on the side-chain rather than the aromatic ring when exposed to UV light or heat.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Arenium Ion (Sigma-complex)
A non-aromatic cationic intermediate formed during electrophilic aromatic substitution.
Lewis Acid (Electron Pair Acceptor)
Catalysts like AlCl3 or FeCl3 used to polarize halogen molecules.
Deactivating Group (Electron Withdrawing)
Substituents that decrease the reactivity of the aromatic ring relative to benzene.
Ortho-Para Director (Positional Selectivity)
Groups that direct the incoming electrophile to positions 2 and 4 relative to themselves.
Benzylic Radical (C6H5CH2•)
A resonance-stabilized carbon radical centered on the carbon adjacent to the benzene ring.
Halogenation (R-H + X2 → R-X + HX)
The replacement of one or more hydrogen atoms in an organic compound by halogen atoms.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Halogens are deactivating yet ortho/para-directing.
  • Iodination requires an oxidizing acid (HIO3) to remove byproduct HI.
  • Side-chain halogenation proceeds via free radicals under UV light.
  • Nuclear halogenation proceeds via electrophilic aromatic substitution with Lewis acids.
  • Phenol undergoes rapid bromination without a catalyst due to strong activation.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Halogenation of aromatic rings is a cornerstone electrophilic substitution reaction.
  • Catalyst choice dictates whether ring or side-chain substitution predominates.
  • Reactivity order follows F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2.
  • Regioselectivity is controlled by resonance and inductive effects of existing substituents.

Explanation: FeCl3 coordinates with Cl2 to generate the chloronium electrophile (Cl+).

Side-chain halogenation of alkylbenzenes follows which mechanism?

Explanation: UV light homolytically cleaves halogen molecules into radicals, initiating a free-radical chain reaction on the side-chain.

Question 10 of 18

Which group exhibits an inductive withdrawal greater than its resonance donation?

Explanation: Halogens have strong electronegativity resulting in an -I effect that outweighs their +R effect overall.

Question 11 of 18

In the chlorination of toluene with Cl2 in dark and cold conditions with FeCl3, the major product is:

Explanation: Lewis acid catalyst in the dark promotes nuclear substitution yielding ortho and para chlorotoluene.

Explanation: Phenol has a strongly activating -OH group which increases the electron density on the ring.

Question 13 of 18

The hybridization of the carbon atom in benzene that is attacked by an electrophile changes from:

Explanation: The attacked carbon forms a sigma bond with the electrophile, changing its hybridization from sp2 to sp3.

Question 14 of 18

Why is direct fluorination of benzene impractical?

Explanation: The extreme exothermicity of fluorine addition causes degradation and explosive ring cleavage.

Question 15 of 18

Which species acts as the Lewis acid in bromination of benzene with FeBr3?

Explanation: FeBr3 accepts an electron pair from Br2 to polarize and cleave the bromine-bromine bond.

Explanation: Excess chlorine and UV light replace all three benzylic hydrogen atoms to form benzotrichloride.

Question 17 of 18

Explanation: The sigma complex loses aromaticity temporarily and carries a positive charge distributed over the ring.

Explanation: The formation of the non-aromatic carbocation intermediate (sigma complex) is endothermic and constitutes the rate-limiting step.

What happens when phenol reacts with bromine water?

Solution: Phenol is strongly activated by the hydroxyl group, leading to rapid tribromination at ortho and para positions without a catalyst.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following alkylbenzenes undergoes side-chain chlorination most rapidly under UV light?

Solution: Cumene forms a tertiary benzylic free radical which is the most stable among the choices, speeding up hydrogen abstraction.

Question 3 of 10
In the Friedel-Crafts or halogenation of benzene, what is the exact role of the Lewis acid?

Solution: Lewis acids interact with halogen molecules to polarize the bond and facilitate the formation of the electrophilic species.

Question 4 of 10
Which halogen can be added directly to benzene without a catalyst under mild conditions?

Solution: None of the halogens add directly without catalysts or specific radical initiators; fluorine is too violent, while Cl2 and Br2 need Lewis acids for ring substitution.

Question 5 of 10
What is the product when ethylbenzene is treated with Cl2 in the presence of UV light?

Solution: Free radical substitution occurs preferentially at the benzylic carbon (C-1) because the resulting radical is resonance-stabilized.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following statements concerning aromatic halogenation is incorrect?

Solution: Benzene does not react with bromine water without a catalyst; only activated rings like phenol react with bromine water.

Question 7 of 10
The directing nature of halogen atoms in benzene substitution is explained by:

Solution: While inductive withdrawal deactivates the ring, resonance stabilization of the intermediate places negative charge density at ortho and para positions.

Question 8 of 10
During side-chain chlorination of toluene, the initiation step involves:

Solution: Ultraviolet light supplies energy for the homolytic cleavage of chlorine molecules into chlorine free radicals.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following reagents converts toluene to benzaldehyde via a chlorinated intermediate?

Solution: Though Etard uses chromyl chloride, controlled side-chain chlorination (benzal chloride) followed by hydrolysis also yields benzaldehyde.

Question 10 of 10
Why is the second step of electrophilic aromatic substitution (loss of proton) very fast?

Solution: The loss of a proton from the sp3 carbon restores the stable aromatic sextet, making this step energetically favorable and rapid.
