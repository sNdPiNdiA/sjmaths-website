Sulphonation

Sulphonation is the electrophilic aromatic substitution reaction that introduces a sulphonic acid group (-SO3H) into an aromatic ring. The active electrophile in concentrated or fuming sulphuric acid is sulphur trioxide (SO3).

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrophilic Substitution Mechanism

  • The attacking electrophile is sulphur trioxide (SO3), acting as a strong Lewis acid due to electronegative oxygen atoms.
  • The rate-determining step is the formation of the resonance-stabilized sigma complex (arenium ion).
  • Deprotonation of the sigma complex restores aromaticity, yielding the benzene sulphonate anion which is subsequently protonated.

Reversibility and Thermodynamic Control

  • Sulphonation is uniquely a reversible electrophilic aromatic substitution process among standard electrophilic substitutions.
  • High temperatures favor thermodynamic control, often leading to the formation of the more stable isomer (e.g., naphthalene-2-sulphonic acid).
  • Low temperatures favor kinetic control, preferentially yielding the kinetically accessible isomer (e.g., naphthalene-1-sulphonic acid).

Kinetic Isotope Effect (KIE)

  • A primary kinetic isotope effect (kH/kD > 1) is frequently observed in sulphonation.
  • Unlike nitration or halogenation where proton loss is fast, proton abstraction from the sigma complex can be partially rate-determining in sulphonation.
  • Reversibility of the initial addition step makes the overall isotope dependence complex depending on relative microscopic rate constants.

Overall Sulphonation Reaction of Benzene
C6H6 + H2SO4 (SO3) → C6H5SO3H + H2O
Conditions: Elevated temperatures or fuming sulphuric acid
Units: Stoichiometric molar ratio
Desulphonation Equilibrium
ArSO3H + H2O + H+ ⇌ ArH + H3SO4+
Conditions: Dilute aqueous acid with steam distillation
Units: Temperature dependent equilibrium
Rate Law for Sulphonation
Rate = k[ArH][SO3]
Conditions: First-order with respect to both aromatic substrate and sulphur trioxide
Units: Rate in mol L-1 s-1, k in L mol-1 s-1
Electrophile Generation (Fuming H2SO4)
2 H2SO4 ⇌ H3SO4+ + HSO4- ⇌ SO3 + H3O+ + HSO4-
Conditions: Concentrated or fuming H2SO4 containing free SO3
Units: Equilibrium constant dimensionless
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Most electrophilic aromatic substitutions are irreversible under standard conditions.

Observation: Sulphonation is readily reversible. — The sulphonic acid group can be easily displaced by a proton in the presence of dilute acid and steam (desulphonation).

Exception Kinetic products dominate at higher temperatures.

Observation: Thermodynamic products dominate at higher temperatures in naphthalene sulphonation. — Higher thermal energy overcomes the activation barrier for the reversible thermodynamic pathway, yielding the less sterically hindered and more stable 2-isomer.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Naphthalene Isomer Rule
1-Alpha at Low, 2-Beta at High

Application: Remember that 80 °C gives 1-naphthalenesulphonic acid (kinetic), while 160 °C gives 2-naphthalenesulphonic acid (thermodynamic).

Electrophile Identification
Oleum = H2SO4 + SO3

Application: Always identify free SO3 as the true electrophile in concentrated or fuming sulphuric acid systems.

Blocking Group Strategy
SO3H as a Temporary Mask

Application: Use sulphonation to temporarily block a para or ortho position, perform subsequent transformations, and remove the group via desulphonation.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Sulphur trioxide (SO3) is the active electrophile in aromatic sulphonation using concentrated H2SO4.
  • Sulphonation is a reversible reaction, allowing synthetic utility as a blocking or directing group.
  • Desulphonation is achieved by heating aromatic sulphonic acids with dilute aqueous acid under steam distillation.
  • Sulphonation of naphthalene at 80 °C yields predominantly the 1-isomer, whereas 160 °C yields the 2-isomer.
  • Benzene sulphonic acid on fusion with molten NaOH followed by acid workup yields phenol.
  • The kinetic isotope effect in sulphonation indicates that proton removal can be partially rate-limiting.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming SO42- or HSO4- acts as the primary electrophile instead of neutral SO3.
  • Forgetting that sulphonation is reversible, leading to errors in predicting high-temperature product distributions.
  • Confusing kinetic and thermodynamic temperature thresholds for naphthalene sulphonation.
  • Neglecting the role of water in shifting the sulphonation-desulphonation equilibrium.

⚡ Quick Revision: Sulphonation

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrophilic Character of SO3

Sulphur trioxide is a powerful electrophile due to high electronegativity of bonded oxygen atoms leaving sulphur electron-deficient.

Reversibility & Equilibrium

The reaction proceeds via a sigma complex and is completely reversible, governed by Le Chatelier's principle with water and acid concentrations.

Synthetic Applications

Aromatic sulphonic acids serve as vital intermediates for manufacturing phenols, dyes, and drugs via alkali fusion.

Temperature Dependence

Kinetic versus thermodynamic product distribution is prominently demonstrated in polycyclic aromatic systems like naphthalene.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Sulphonation (Ar-H → Ar-SO3H)
Introduction of a sulphonic acid group into an organic molecule.
Desulphonation (Ar-SO3H → Ar-H)
Removal of the sulphonic acid group using hot dilute acid.
Oleum (H2S2O7)
Fuming sulphuric acid containing dissolved sulphur trioxide.
Sigma Complex (Arenium Ion)
Resonance-stabilized carbocation intermediate in electrophilic aromatic substitution.
Alkali Fusion (ArSO3Na + NaOH → ArONa)
Industrial route to phenols from sulphonic acid salts.
Kinetic Isotope Effect (KIE)
Ratio of reaction rates using isotopically distinct reactants.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Electrophile in sulphonation is neutral SO3.
  • Sulphonation is completely reversible.
  • Low temp favors 1-naphthalenesulphonic acid.
  • High temp favors 2-naphthalenesulphonic acid.
  • Alkali fusion converts sulphonates to phenols.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Sulphonation introduces the -SO3H group using H2SO4 or oleum.
  • The process proceeds through electrophilic aromatic substitution via a sigma complex.
  • Reversibility enables selective synthesis and blocking group methodologies.
  • Temperature tuning controls kinetic versus thermodynamic isomer formation in polycyclic systems.

⚖️ Key Differences

Kinetic Isomer VS Thermodynamic Isomer

Kinetic isomer forms faster at lower temperatures due to lower activation energy, while thermodynamic isomer is more stable and forms at higher temperatures.

Question 9 of 19

Why is the sulphonation reaction reversible?

Explanation: The sulphonate group is a good leaving group under acidic aqueous conditions, allowing microscopic reversibility.

Question 10 of 19

In the rate law for aromatic sulphonation (Rate = k[ArH][SO3]), what is the overall reaction order?

Explanation: It is first order with respect to substrate and first order with respect to SO3, making it second order overall.

Question 11 of 19

Which of the following describes the electronic nature of the SO3 molecule in sulphonation?

Explanation: SO3 acts as a strong Lewis acid and electrophile due to highly electronegative oxygen atoms withdrawing electron density from sulphur.

Question 12 of 19

What is observed regarding the kinetic isotope effect (KIE) in many sulphonation reactions?

Explanation: A primary kinetic isotope effect is often observed because proton loss can be partially rate-determining.

Question 13 of 19

Why is oleum preferred over dilute sulphuric acid for sulphonating unreactive rings?

Explanation: Oleum supplies high concentrations of free SO3, driving the electrophilic substitution forward.

Question 14 of 19

What is the role of sulphonic acid groups in synthetic organic chemistry when used temporarily?

Explanation: The -SO3H group is often used as a temporary blocking group to direct substitution to specific positions before being removed.

Question 15 of 19

Which isomer of naphthalenesulphonic acid is thermodynamically more stable?

Explanation: 2-Naphthalenesulphonic acid is thermodynamically more stable due to less steric hindrance at the beta position.

Question 16 of 19

How does water affect the sulphonation equilibrium?

Explanation: Water participates in desulphonation; high water concentration shifts the equilibrium back towards the starting aromatic compound.

Question 17 of 19

What type of directing group is the sulphonic acid group (-SO3H) for subsequent electrophilic substitution?

Explanation: The sulphonic acid group is electron-withdrawing by resonance and induction, making it a meta-directing and deactivating group.

Question 18 of 19

Which industrial chemical process utilizes benzene sulphonic acid as a key precursor?

Explanation: Alkali fusion of sodium benzene sulphonate is a classic industrial route for producing phenol.

Question 19 of 19

In the mechanism of sulphonation, what is abstracted to restore aromaticity?

Explanation: Loss of a proton from the sigma complex restores the aromatic stabilization energy.
