Friedel–Crafts Acylation

Friedel–Crafts acylation involves the electrophilic substitution of an aromatic ring with an acyl group using a Lewis acid catalyst. This reaction introduces an aryl ketone moiety without the skeletal rearrangement typically observed in Friedel–Crafts alkylation.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mechanism of Electrophilic Acylation

  • The active electrophile is the acylium ion (resonance-stabilized oxonium ion).
  • Reaction rate depends on the formation of the Lewis acid-acyl chloride complex RCOCl·AlCl3.
  • The intermediate is a resonance-stabilized non-aromatic carbocation known as the sigma-complex or arenium ion.
  • Unlike alkylation, acylation does not undergo carbocation rearrangement due to resonance stabilization of the acylium ion.

Catalyst Requirements and Stoichiometry

  • A stoichiometric amount of Lewis acid (typically AlCl3) is required because the product ketone forms a stable complex with the catalyst.
  • Common alternative catalysts include FeCl3, ZnCl2, and BF3.
  • Proton acids such as HF or polyphosphoric acid (PPA) can also catalyze acylation when using carboxylic acids or anhydrides.
  • Moisture destroys the catalyst by hydrolysis, necessitating strictly anhydrous conditions.

Scope, Limitations, and Regioselectivity

  • The reaction yields aromatic ketones via electrophilic aromatic substitution.
  • Deactivating groups such as -NO2, -CN, or -SO3H prevent the reaction from proceeding.
  • Anilines and alkylamines fail to undergo the reaction because the amino group forms a stable complex with the Lewis acid catalyst.
  • The newly introduced acyl group is deactivating and meta-directing, preventing polyacylation.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Friedel-Crafts reactions occur readily with all substituted benzenes.

Observation: Nitrobenzene and strongly deactivated benzenes fail to undergo acylation. — The electron deficiency of the ring lowers the HOMO energy, preventing electrophilic attack by the weak acylium ion.

Exception Lewis acid catalysts are required in catalytic amounts.

Observation: Stoichiometric amounts (more than 1 equivalent) of AlCl3 are mandatory for acylation. — The carbonyl oxygen of the product ketone binds strongly to AlCl3, deactivating the catalyst until aqueous workup.

Exception Primary alkyl groups undergo rearrangement during Friedel-Crafts alkylation.

Observation: Primary acyl groups do not undergo carbon skeleton rearrangement during acylation. — Acylium ions are resonance-stabilized oxonium species that do not rearrange to secondary or tertiary carbocations.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
No Rearrangement Rule
Acylium = No Rearrangement

Application: When asked to synthesize straight-chain alkyl phenyl ketones (e.g., propiophenone), always use acylation followed by Clemmensen or Wolff-Kishner reduction instead of direct alkylation.

Stoichiometry Check
Moles(AlCl3) ≥ Moles(Ketone Product)

Application: Remember that you need at least 1 equivalent of AlCl3 for the acyl chloride and extra equivalents if other Lewis basic functional groups are present on the ring.

Deactivation Mnemonic
KAG: Ketone Always Guards (Meta)

Application: The acyl group introduced is a deactivating meta-director, which ensures the reaction stops cleanly at the monoacylated stage without poly-substitution.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The electrophile in Friedel-Crafts acylation is the acylium ion, [R-C+=O].
  • Anhydrous AlCl3 is the most common Lewis acid catalyst used.
  • Acylation does not suffer from carbocation skeletal rearrangements.
  • The reaction product requires a hydrolytic workup to release the ketone from the AlCl3 complex.
  • Friedel-Crafts acylation of benzene with acetyl chloride yields acetophenone.
  • Deactivating groups like nitro or cyano groups prevent the reaction from occurring.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming AlCl3 is required in catalytic quantities like in alkylation.
  • Forgetting that aniline and N-substituted aniline derivatives fail to react due to Lewis acid-base adduct formation at the nitrogen atom.
  • Expecting polyacylation to occur readily on the aromatic ring.
  • Confusing the mechanism with nucleophilic acyl substitution at the carbonyl carbon.

⚡ Quick Revision: Friedel–Crafts Acylation

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrophile Generation

Reaction of an acyl chloride or anhydride with AlCl3 generates the resonance-stabilized acylium ion electrophile.

Attack and Deprotonation

The acylium ion attacks the benzene ring to form a sigma-complex, followed by loss of a proton to restore aromaticity.

Complexation and Workup

The ketone product forms a stable complex with AlCl3, requiring aqueous hydrolysis to liberate the free aryl ketone.

Synthetic Utility

Combined with reduction methods, acylation provides a clean route to prepare straight-chain alkylbenzenes without rearrangement.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Acylium Ion (R-C+=O)

Lewis Acid (AlCl3)
Electron-pair acceptor catalyst used to generate the electrophile.
Sigma Complex (Arenium Ion)
Non-aromatic intermediate formed during electrophilic addition.
Aryl Ketone (Ar-COR)
Primary product of Friedel-Crafts acylation.
Clemmensen Reduction (Zn(Hg)/HCl)
Method to reduce aryl ketones to methylene groups.
Wolff-Kishner Reduction (NH2NH2/OH-)
Base-catalyzed reduction of aryl ketones to hydrocarbons.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Acylium ions do not undergo skeletal rearrangement.
  • Stoichiometric AlCl3 is consumed during workup.
  • The acyl product is less reactive than the starting material.
  • Anhydrous conditions are strictly mandatory.
  • Carboxylic acid anhydrides can replace acyl chlorides.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Friedel-Crafts acylation introduces acyl groups onto aromatic rings.
  • The active electrophile is the acylium ion.
  • No carbocation rearrangements take place.
  • Product inhibition requires stoichiometric catalyst usage.

⚖️ Key Differences

Friedel-Crafts Alkylation VS Friedel-Crafts Acylation

Alkylation uses alkyl halides and causes rearrangements with poly-substitution; acylation uses acyl halides, avoids rearrangements, and stops at mono-substitution.

Why is a stoichiometric amount of AlCl3 required in Friedel-Crafts acylation?

Explanation: The carbonyl oxygen of the ketone coordinates strongly with AlCl3, tying up the catalyst until water is added.

Question 3 of 18

Which of the following compounds fails to undergo Friedel-Crafts acylation?

Explanation: Nitrobenzene contains a strongly deactivating nitro group, rendering the ring unreactive toward weak electrophiles like acylium ions.

Question 4 of 18

Which reagent combination is used to convert an acyl chloride and benzene into an aryl ketone?

Explanation: Anhydrous AlCl3 acts as the Lewis acid catalyst to generate the acylium ion from the acyl chloride.

Question 5 of 18

What is the primary advantage of Friedel-Crafts acylation over alkylation for synthesizing n-propylbenzene?

Explanation: Direct alkylation with 1-chloropropane yields a mixture containing isopropylbenzene due to rearrangement; acylation followed by reduction avoids this.

Question 6 of 18

What is the directing nature of the acyl group in the resulting phenyl ketone?

Explanation: The electron-withdrawing acyl group is a deactivating group and directs incoming electrophiles to the meta position.

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following catalysts can be used in Friedel-Crafts acylation besides AlCl3?

Explanation: Other Lewis acids such as FeCl3, ZnCl2, and BF3 can also catalyze Friedel-Crafts reactions.

Question 8 of 18

What happens when water is added to the reaction mixture at the end of Friedel-Crafts acylation?

Explanation: Aqueous workup hydrolyzes the ketone-AlCl3 complex, releasing the free ketone and hydrated aluminium salts.

Question 9 of 18

Why do primary alkyl groups rearrange in alkylation but acyl groups do not in acylation?

Explanation: The positive charge on the acylium ion is delocalized onto the oxygen atom (R-C+=O ↔ R-C≡O+), conferring high stability.

Question 10 of 18

Which starting material is unsuitable for Friedel-Crafts acylation due to catalyst poisoning?

Explanation: Aniline possesses a basic amino group that coordinates irreversibly with the Lewis acid catalyst, deactivating it.

Question 11 of 18

What type of reaction is Friedel-Crafts acylation classified as?

Explanation: The aromatic ring acts as a nucleophile attacking the electrophilic acylium ion, representing electrophilic aromatic substitution.

Question 12 of 18

Which derivative can be used in place of an acyl chloride in Friedel-Crafts acylation?

Explanation: Carboxylic acid anhydrides readily react with Lewis acids to generate the same acylium ion intermediate.

Question 13 of 18

How many moles of AlCl3 are theoretically consumed per mole of benzoyl chloride reacting with benzene?

Explanation: At least 1 mole of AlCl3 complexes with the resulting ketone and is tied up until aqueous workup.

Question 14 of 18

What is the product of the reaction between benzene, acetyl chloride, and AlCl3 followed by H2O workup?

Explanation: Acetyl chloride introduces an acetyl group into benzene, forming acetophenone.

Question 15 of 18

Which reduction method can convert acetophenone into ethylbenzene?

Explanation: Clemmensen reduction (Zn(Hg)/HCl) reduces the carbonyl group of an aryl ketone to a methylene group.

Question 16 of 18

Which factor prevents polyacylation in Friedel-Crafts acylation?

Explanation: The introduced acyl group withdraws electron density, making the ring less reactive than the starting material.

Question 17 of 18

What is the role of the Lewis acid in the first step of Friedel-Crafts acylation?

Explanation: The Lewis acid pulls the chloride ion from the acyl chloride to generate the electrophilic acylium ion.

Question 18 of 18

Which of the following is true regarding Friedel-Crafts acylation of phenol?

Explanation: Phenols and other oxygen/nitrogen-containing substrates require additional moles of Lewis acid due to complexation at the heteroatom.

Question 1 of 10
What is the resonance structure that accounts for the stability of the acylium ion?

Solution: The acylium ion is stabilized by resonance between R-C+=O and the triple-bonded oxonium form R-C≡O+.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following reagents can effect the reduction of acetophenone to ethylbenzene?

Solution: Clemmensen reduction using zinc amalgam and concentrated hydrochloric acid reduces aryl ketones to methylene groups.

Question 3 of 10
What is the primary reason why aniline cannot be used directly in Friedel-Crafts acylation?

Solution: The lone pair on the amino nitrogen reacts with the Lewis acid catalyst to form a complex, deactivating the ring and poisoning the catalyst.

Question 4 of 10
In the mechanism of Friedel-Crafts acylation, the removal of a proton from the sigma complex is facilitated by:

Solution: The [AlCl4]- counterion or other species acts as a weak base to abstract the proton, restoring aromaticity.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following compounds will undergo Friedel-Crafts acylation most rapidly?

Solution: Anisole contains an activating methoxy group (-OCH3) that donates electron density to the ring via resonance, accelerating electrophilic substitution.

Question 6 of 10
What byproduct is formed during the reaction of an acyl chloride with an aromatic ring in the presence of AlCl3 before workup?

Solution: HCl gas is evolved during the electrophilic substitution step, and the ketone remains complexed with AlCl3.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following is true for the stereochemistry and regiochemistry of Friedel-Crafts acylation?

Solution: Steric hindrance and electronic factors favor substitution predominantly at the para position when mono-substituted benzenes are used.

Question 8 of 10
Which acid derivative cannot be used for Friedel-Crafts acylation?

Solution: Acyl chlorides, anhydrides, and even carboxylic acids (with strong protic acid catalysts like HF) can effect acylation.

Question 9 of 10
Why is polyacylation generally not observed in Friedel-Crafts acylation?

Solution: The newly introduced acyl group is electron-withdrawing, making the aromatic ring less reactive than the precursor.

Question 10 of 10
What is the role of anhydrous conditions in Friedel-Crafts acylation?

Solution: Water reacts violently with AlCl3 to form inactive hydrated species, destroying its Lewis acidity.
