Friedel–Crafts Alkylation

Friedel-Crafts alkylation involves the electrophilic substitution of an aromatic ring with an alkyl halide in the presence of a Lewis acid catalyst. The reaction proceeds via the generation of a reactive carbocation electrophile that attacks the aromatic π-system.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mechanism and Electrophile Generation

  • The active electrophile is a carbocation, an ion pair, or a polarized complex formed between the alkyl halide and the Lewis acid.
  • Anhydrous AlCl3, FeCl3, and BF3 act as effective Lewis acid catalysts by polarizing or cleaving the C-halogen bond.
  • The attack of the electrophile on the benzene ring forms a resonance-stabilized intermediate known as the arenium ion or sigma complex.
  • Loss of a proton from the sigma complex restores aromaticity, yielding the alkylbenzene and regenerating the Lewis acid catalyst.

Carbocation Rearrangements and Limitations

  • Primary carbocations frequently undergo 1,2-hydride shift or 1,2-alkyl shift to generate more stable secondary or tertiary carbocations.
  • Direct synthesis of propylbenzene from 1-chloropropane yields a mixture containing primarily isopropylbenzene (cumene) due to rearrangement.
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation fails when aromatic rings are deactivated by strongly electron-withdrawing groups such as -NO2, -CN, or -SO3H.
  • Aryl halides and vinyl halides cannot be used because they fail to form stable carbocations under standard reaction conditions.

Polyalkylation and Directing Effects

  • Newly introduced alkyl groups are ortho/para-directing and activating toward further electrophilic aromatic substitution.
  • The activation caused by the alkyl substituent leads to polyalkylation, making it difficult to stop the reaction at the monoalkylation stage.
  • Excess aromatic substrate is typically used in the reaction mixture to minimize excessive polyalkylation.
  • Sterically hindered alkyl halides yield lower yields due to increased activation energy and competing elimination (E1/E2) side reactions.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Primary alkyl halides yield straight-chain alkylbenzenes

Observation: Rearrangement to branched alkylbenzenes occurs — Greater thermodynamic stability of secondary and tertiary carbocations formed via 1,2-shifts.

Exception Friedel-Crafts alkylation proceeds efficiently with any benzene derivative

Observation: Fails with strongly deactivated rings like nitrobenzene or aniline — Aniline coordinates with the Lewis acid catalyst to form an unreactive meta-directing ammonium salt complex; nitrobenzenes are too electron-deficient.

Exception Lewis acid is consumed stoichiometrically in Friedel-Crafts

Observation: Complexation with basic aromatic products requires more than catalytic amounts — Strong alkylbenzene-AlCl3 complexes tie up the catalyst, requiring stoichiometric or excess quantities.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Carbocation Stability First
3° > 2° > 1° rearrangement rule

Application: Always draw the most stable carbocation before attaching it to the benzene ring to predict the correct major product.

Avoid Polyalkylation Shortcut
Use Acylation + Reduction instead

Application: To prepare straight-chain alkylbenzenes without rearrangement, perform Friedel-Crafts acylation followed by Clemmensen or Wolff-Kishner reduction.

Deactivation Check
No -NO2, -NR2, -COOH allowed

Application: Quickly eliminate choices where the aromatic ring bears strong electron-withdrawing groups or basic amino groups.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The electrophile in Friedel-Crafts alkylation is a carbocation or carbocation-Lewis acid complex.
  • Anhydrous AlCl3 is the most common Lewis acid catalyst used to generate the electrophile.
  • Primary alkyl halides like ethyl chloride yield straight-chain products without major rearrangement.
  • 1-Chloropropane yields 60-70% isopropylbenzene due to carbocation rearrangement.
  • Friedel-Crafts reactions fail with aniline because AlCl3 complexes with the nitrogen lone pair.
  • Alkyl groups are activating and ortho/para-directing, leading inevitably to polyalkylation.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming primary alkyl halides never rearrange; they always form carbocations prone to 1,2-shifts.
  • Using hydrated AlCl3 instead of anhydrous, which destroys the catalytic activity.
  • Forgetting that aniline and substituted anilines do not undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation.
  • Stating that Friedel-Crafts alkylation can be used to synthesize propylbenzene directly from 1-chloropropane without isomers.

⚡ Quick Revision: Friedel–Crafts Alkylation

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution Mechanism

Alkylation of benzene proceeds through electrophilic attack of a carbocation to form a resonance-stabilized cyclohexadienyl cation intermediate, followed by proton elimination.

Carbocation Rearrangement Pathways

Less stable primary carbocations undergo intramolecular hydride or alkyl shifts to yield thermodynamically favored secondary or tertiary carbocation intermediates.

Catalytic Role of Lewis Acids

Anhydrous metal halides like AlCl3 polarize the alkyl halide bond to facilitate carbocation generation and stabilize the leaving counter-anion.

Synthetic Limitations

The reaction fails with strongly deactivated aromatic rings and is plagued by polyalkylation due to ring activation by the newly introduced alkyl group.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Arenium Ion (Sigma complex)
Resonance-stabilized cationic intermediate in electrophilic aromatic substitution.
Lewis Acid (AlCl3, FeCl3)
Electron-pair acceptor used to generate electrophiles.
Polyalkylation (Multiple substitution)
Addition of more than one alkyl group due to ring activation.
Rearrangement (1,2-Hydride/Alkyl Shift)
Migration of a group to form a more stable carbocation.
Cumene (Isopropylbenzene)
Product of benzene alkylation with propene or 2-chloropropane.
Acylation (R-CO-Cl + AlCl3)
Alternative pathway avoiding rearrangements to prepare alkylbenzenes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carbocation stability order: 3° > 2° > 1°
  • Anhydrous conditions are strictly required for AlCl3 activation.
  • Friedel-Crafts fails with aromatic rings bearing -NO2, -CN, or -NR2.
  • Alkyl groups activate the benzene ring toward further substitution.
  • Primary propyl halides yield isopropylbenzene as the major product.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation attaches alkyl groups to aromatic rings via carbocation intermediates.
  • Carbocation rearrangements limit the synthesis of straight-chain alkylbenzenes.
  • Polyalkylation is a major side reaction mitigated by using excess aromatic substrate.
  • Strongly deactivated aromatic substrates fail to react under standard conditions.

⚖️ Key Differences

Friedel-Crafts Alkylation VS Friedel-Crafts Acylation

Alkylation uses alkyl halides and suffers from rearrangements and polyalkylation; acylation uses acyl chlorides, avoids rearrangements, and stops at monoacylation.

What is the major product formed when benzene reacts with 1-chloropropane in the presence of AlCl3?

Why does Friedel-Crafts alkylation fail with nitrobenzene?

Question 4 of 18

Which intermediate is formed during the reaction of benzene with an alkyl halide and AlCl3?

Explanation: The electrophilic attack on the benzene ring generates a resonance-stabilized arenium ion or sigma complex.

Question 5 of 18

What is the primary reason for polyalkylation in Friedel-Crafts alkylation?

Explanation: Alkyl groups are electron-donating via hyperconjugation and inductive effects, activating the ring toward further substitution.

Question 6 of 18

Which alkyl halide does NOT undergo carbocation rearrangement during Friedel-Crafts alkylation?

Explanation: Tert-butyl chloride forms a tertiary carbocation directly, which is already maximally stable and does not rearrange.

Question 7 of 18

Aniline fails to undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation because:

Explanation: The nitrogen lone pair on aniline coordinates with the Lewis acid AlCl3, converting it into a strongly deactivating meta-directing ammonium salt.

Question 8 of 18

Which reaction sequence can be used to synthesize straight-chain propylbenzene without rearrangement?

Explanation: Friedel-Crafts acylation does not involve carbocation rearrangements, and subsequent reduction yields the straight-chain alkylbenzene.

Question 9 of 18

What is the active electrophile in the reaction of methyl chloride with benzene and AlCl3?

Explanation: Methyl chloride reacts with AlCl3 to generate the methyl cation (or strongly polarized methyl-AlCl3 complex acting as a methyl cation equivalent).

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following functional groups on benzene prevents Friedel-Crafts alkylation?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is strongly electron-withdrawing and deactivates the ring, halting Friedel-Crafts reactions.

Question 11 of 18

In Friedel-Crafts alkylation, what is the role of AlCl3?

Explanation: AlCl3 has an incomplete octet of electrons, allowing it to act as a Lewis acid by accepting an electron pair from the alkyl halide.

Question 12 of 18

Which isomer is the major product when benzene reacts with 2-chlorobutane in the presence of AlCl3?

Explanation: 2-Chlorobutane forms a secondary sec-butyl carbocation, which attacks benzene to yield 2-phenylbutane without further rearrangement to a less stable primary ion.

Question 13 of 18

Why are vinyl and aryl halides generally unreactive in Friedel-Crafts alkylation?

Explanation: Vinylic and phenyl carbocations are extremely unstable energetically, preventing their formation under standard Friedel-Crafts conditions.

Question 14 of 18

What type of electronic effect do alkyl substituents exert on an aromatic ring?

Explanation: Alkyl groups donate electron density to the aromatic ring through inductive effect and hyperconjugation, activating it.

Question 15 of 18

How can polyalkylation be minimized during Friedel-Crafts alkylation?

Explanation: Using a large excess of the aromatic hydrocarbon ensures that alkyl halide molecules preferentially collide with unsubstituted benzene rather than alkylbenzene.

Question 16 of 18

What is the geometry of the carbocation intermediate in Friedel-Crafts alkylation?

Explanation: Carbocations possess sp2 hybridization at the central carbon, resulting in a trigonal planar geometry.

Question 17 of 18

Which of the following compounds cannot undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation?

Explanation: Nitrobenzene is strongly deactivated due to the electron-withdrawing nitro group, making Friedel-Crafts alkylation ineffective.

Question 18 of 18

The step in Friedel-Crafts alkylation that restores aromaticity is:

Explanation: Deprotonation of the arenium ion (sigma complex) restores the stable aromatic sextet of electrons.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary organic product when benzene reacts with 2-methyl-1-chloropropane in the presence of AlCl3?

Solution: The primary isobutyl cation rearranges via a 1,2-methide shift to form the tertiary tert-butyl carbocation, yielding tert-butylbenzene.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following catalysts is NOT typically used in Friedel-Crafts reactions?

Solution: NaCl is an ionic salt and does not possess Lewis acid properties required to generate carbocations from alkyl halides.

Question 3 of 10
In the mechanism of Friedel-Crafts alkylation, the sigma complex is:

Solution: The sigma complex (arenium ion) loses aromaticity temporarily but is stabilized by resonance across multiple canonical forms.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following benzene derivatives undergoes Friedel-Crafts alkylation most rapidly?

Solution: Toluene bears a methyl group, which is an activating substituent that accelerates electrophilic aromatic substitution compared to deactivated rings.

Question 5 of 10
What limits the synthetic utility of Friedel-Crafts alkylation for preparing monoalkylbenzenes?

Solution: Rearrangements alter the intended alkyl chain structure, and ring activation causes further substitution (polyalkylation).

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following compounds gives a single mono-alkylated product without rearrangement when reacted with benzene and AlCl3?

Solution: Tert-butyl chloride forms a tertiary carbocation that cannot rearrange further, yielding only tert-butylbenzene.

Question 7 of 10
During Friedel-Crafts alkylation, why is anhydrous AlCl3 mandatory?

Solution: Water reacts violently with AlCl3 to form hydrochloric acid and aluminum hydroxide hydrates, destroying its Lewis acidity.

Question 8 of 10
What is the role of the hydrogen atom removed during the final step of Friedel-Crafts alkylation?

Solution: The proton removed from the sigma complex combines with the [AlCl4]- counter-ion to release HCl and regenerate the AlCl3 catalyst.

Question 9 of 10
Which reactant pair will fail to undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation under standard conditions?

Solution: Aryl halides like chlorobenzene do not form carbocations and fail to react in Friedel-Crafts alkylation.

Question 10 of 10
Which alternative method is preferred to synthesize straight-chain alkylbenzenes avoiding rearrangements?

Solution: Friedel-Crafts acylation proceeds without carbocation rearrangements, and the resulting ketone can be reduced to the desired alkylbenzene.
