Preparation of Benzene

Benzene is an aromatic hydrocarbon synthesized via thermal aromatization, decarboxylation, reduction, and catalytic cyclotrimerization. Industrial and laboratory routes utilize specific precursors such as sodium benzoate, phenol, chlorobenzene, and acetylene.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Decarboxylation and Reduction Routes

  • Decarboxylation of sodium benzoate with soda lime (NaOH + CaO) yields benzene and sodium carbonate.
  • Calcium oxide (CaO) keeps the sodium hydroxide dry and acts as a fusible medium during heating.
  • Reduction of phenol is executed by heating phenolic vapours with zinc dust, where zinc acts as a deoxygenating agent.
  • Chlorobenzene undergoes reduction via Ni-Al alloy in the presence of aqueous NaOH (catalytic hydrogenation/reduction).

Cyclotrimerization and Aromatization of Aliphatic Precursors

  • Cyclotrimerization of acetylene (ethyne) takes place over a red-hot iron tube at 873 K to form benzene.
  • Catalytic reforming (aromatization) of n-hexane over Cr2O3/V2O5/Mo2O3 at 773 K under 10-20 atm pressure produces benzene.
  • Heptane similarly undergoes aromatization to yield toluene under identical catalytic conditions.

Hydrolysis of Grignard Reagents and Diazotization Routes

  • Phenylmagnesium bromide (Grignard reagent) reacts with water or dilute acids to yield benzene via protonolysis.
  • Aniline undergoes diazotization with HNO2 (NaNO2 + HCl) at 273-278 K to form benzenediazonium chloride.
  • Reduction of benzenediazonium chloride with H3PO2 (hypophosphorous acid) or ethanol/Cu+ yields benzene.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkanes normally do not form cyclic aromatic rings easily under mild conditions.

Observation: n-Hexane cyclizes to benzene. — Presence of specific transition metal oxide catalysts (Cr2O3 supported on alumina) at high temperatures facilitates simultaneous dehydrogenation and cyclization.

Exception Haloarenes are extremely unreactive towards nucleophilic substitution.

Observation: Chlorobenzene can be converted to benzene. — Requires strong reducing agents like Ni-Al alloy in alkaline medium or catalytic hydrogenation rather than standard aqueous substitution.

Exception Phenols are acidic and do not readily lose their hydroxyl group as water.

Observation: Distillation with zinc dust removes the hydroxyl oxygen. — Thermodynamically stable ZnO is formed as a byproduct, driving the reduction of phenol to benzene.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Cyclotrimerization of ethyne requires a red-hot iron or copper tube at 873 K.
  • Soda-lime is a mixture of sodium hydroxide (NaOH) and calcium oxide (CaO) in a 3:1 ratio.
  • Reduction of phenol with zinc dust yields benzene and zinc oxide.
  • Catalytic reforming of n-hexane over Cr2O3-Al2O3 produces benzene.
  • Benzenediazonium chloride reduction using hypophosphorous acid (H3PO2) gives benzene and phosphorous acid.
  • Decarboxylation of benzoic acid derivatives proceeds via carbanion intermediates.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the role of CaO with NaOH in soda-lime; CaO is used to keep NaOH dry and prevent attack on glass apparatus, not just as a reactant.
  • Forgetting the exact temperature (873 K) for acetylene cyclotrimerization.
  • Assuming zinc dust reduction of phenol yields an intermediate ether instead of direct benzene.
  • Misidentifying the dehydrogenation catalyst in the aromatization of n-hexane as nickel instead of chromium/vanadium oxides.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation of Benzene

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Decarboxylation Methods

Sodium salts of aromatic acids heated with soda-lime eliminate carbon dioxide as sodium carbonate, yielding benzene rings.

Reduction and Deoxygenation

Phenolic compounds lose oxygen when heated with active metals like zinc dust, forming stable zinc oxide and aromatic hydrocarbons.

Catalytic Aromatization

Aliphatic straight-chain alkanes having six or more carbons undergo simultaneous cyclization and dehydrogenation over metal oxides.

Polymerization Routes

Aliphatic alkynes like ethyne undergo cyclic polymerization over red-hot metal catalysts to construct symmetrical aromatic rings.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Soda-lime (NaOH + CaO)
A solid mixture used for decarboxylation reactions.
Aromatization (Catalytic Dehydrocyclization)
Conversion of aliphatic alkanes into aromatic hydrocarbons.
Cyclotrimerization (3 C2H2 → C6H6)
Synthesis of a benzene ring from three molecules of acetylene.
Diazotization (Ar-NH2 → Ar-N2+Cl-)
Conversion of primary aromatic amines into diazonium salts using HNO2.
Reduction (Addition of H / Removal of O)
Chemical process converting functional groups into hydrocarbons using metals.
Grignard Reagent (R-Mg-X)
Organometallic halide yielding hydrocarbons upon protonolysis.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ethyne trimerization occurs at 873 K over Fe tube.
  • Phenol reduction uses Zn dust.
  • Sodium benzoate decarboxylation uses NaOH + CaO.
  • n-Hexane aromatization uses Cr2O3 / Al2O3.
  • Diazonium salt reduction to benzene uses H3PO2.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Benzene is prepared from both aliphatic and aromatic precursors.
  • Thermal and catalytic methods are central to industrial production.
  • Functional group removal is achieved via decarboxylation and deoxygenation.
  • Cyclization links linear chains to hexagonal aromatic systems.

⚖️ Key Differences

Soda-lime Decarboxylation VS Kolbe Electrolysis

Soda-lime uses thermal reaction with metal oxides, whereas Kolbe electrolysis uses electrical current on carboxylate salts.

Which of the following reagents reduces benzenediazonium chloride to benzene?

Question 6 of 18

The role of calcium oxide (CaO) in soda-lime is to:

Explanation: CaO is hygroscopic and keeps NaOH dry, while also preventing molten NaOH from corroding the glass reaction vessel.

Question 7 of 18

Benzene can be prepared industrially from petroleum via:

Explanation: Catalytic reforming (dehydrocyclization) of six-carbon or higher alkanes present in petroleum fractions yields benzene.

Question 8 of 18

What is the product of the reaction between phenylmagnesium bromide and water?

Explanation: Protonolysis of Grignard reagents by water yields the corresponding parent hydrocarbon, which is benzene for phenylmagnesium bromide.

Question 9 of 18

During the catalytic reforming of n-hexane to benzene, the reaction involves:

Explanation: The conversion of n-hexane to benzene involves loss of hydrogen (dehydrogenation) and ring closure (cyclization).

Question 10 of 18

Which starting material is required to synthesize benzene in a single step using zinc dust?

Explanation: Phenol distills with zinc dust to yield benzene directly.

Question 11 of 18

What byproduct is formed along with benzene during the decarboxylation of sodium benzoate with soda-lime?

Explanation: C6H5COONa + NaOH → C6H6 + Na2CO3.

Question 12 of 18

The conversion of aniline to benzenediazonium chloride is known as:

Explanation: Reaction of primary aromatic amines with nitrous acid at 273-278 K is termed diazotization.

Question 13 of 18

Which of the following compounds gives toluene upon catalytic aromatization?

Explanation: n-Heptane contains seven carbon atoms and undergoes aromatization to form methylbenzene (toluene).

Question 14 of 18

In the laboratory preparation of benzene from benzoic acid, benzoic acid is first converted to sodium salt using:

Explanation: Benzoic acid reacts with sodium hydroxide to form sodium benzoate before soda-lime decarboxylation.

Question 15 of 18

What is the hybridization change of carbon atoms when acetylene trimerizes to form benzene?

Explanation: Acetylene carbons are sp hybridized, and in benzene they become sp2 hybridized.

Question 16 of 18

Which metal oxide is commonly used as a catalyst in the aromatization of alkanes?

Explanation: Cr2O3 supported on alumina is the standard catalyst for dehydrocyclization of alkanes.

Question 17 of 18

Reduction of chlorobenzene to benzene can be accomplished using:

Explanation: Chlorobenzene is reduced to benzene using a Nickel-Aluminum alloy in aqueous sodium hydroxide.

Question 18 of 18

How many moles of acetylene are required to produce one mole of benzene during cyclic polymerization?

Explanation: Three molecules of ethyne (C2H2) trimerize to form one molecule of benzene (C6H6).

Which of the following reactions does NOT yield benzene?

Solution: Hydration of ethylene produces ethanol, not benzene.

Question 2 of 10
What is the primary organic product when chlorobenzene reacts with Ni-Al alloy in aqueous NaOH?

Solution: Ni-Al alloy in NaOH acts as a reducing agent that replaces the chlorine atom with hydrogen, forming benzene.

Question 3 of 10
During the preparation of benzene from sodium benzoate, soda-lime is preferred over pure NaOH because:

Solution: Calcium oxide raises the melting point and keeps NaOH dry, preventing it from attacking the glass reaction flask.

Question 4 of 10
Which hydrocarbon will yield toluene upon catalytic aromatization?

Solution: n-Heptane has seven carbons and cyclizes/dehydrogenates to methylbenzene (toluene).

Question 5 of 10
The reduction of benzenediazonium chloride to benzene using ethanol involves ethanol acting as a:

Solution: Ethanol reduces the diazonium salt to benzene while being oxidized to acetaldehyde.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following compounds has sp hybridized carbon atoms that convert to sp2 during benzene synthesis?

Solution: Ethyne (acetylene) has sp hybridized carbons which become sp2 upon cyclotrimerization.

Question 7 of 10
What happens when phenol vapour is passed over heated zinc dust?

Solution: Zinc abstracts oxygen from phenol to form zinc oxide and benzene.

Question 8 of 10
Which reagent combination successfully converts benzene sulfonic acid into benzene?

Solution: Desulfonation of benzene sulfonic acid is achieved by heating with superheated steam in the presence of an acid catalyst.

Question 9 of 10
The conversion of n-hexane to benzene is an example of:

Solution: Simultaneous removal of hydrogen and ring closure of an alkane is termed dehydrocyclization or aromatization.

Question 10 of 10

Solution: Catalytic reforming of petroleum naphtha fractions is the primary industrial source of benzene.
