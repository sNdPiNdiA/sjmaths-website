Properties of Benzene

Benzene is a planar aromatic hydrocarbon with a molecular formula C6H6, characterized by a resonance-stabilized delocalized π-electron system. It exhibits unique chemical stability, undergoing electrophilic aromatic substitution rather than typical alkene addition reactions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Physical and Structural Properties

  • Benzene is a colorless liquid with a distinct aromatic odor, highly insoluble in water but completely miscible with organic solvents like ethanol and ether.
  • X-ray diffraction confirms that benzene is a regular planar hexagon with all C-C bond lengths equal to 139 pm, perfectly intermediate between single (154 pm) and double (134 pm) carbon-carbon bonds.
  • Each carbon atom in benzene is sp2 hybridized, forming three σ-bonds in the plane with a bond angle of 120°.
  • The unhybridized p-orbitals overlap side-on to form a continuous, cyclic delocalized π-cloud above and below the molecular plane containing 6 π-electrons.

Chemical Reactivity and Substitution Mechanisms

  • Benzene predominantly undergoes electrophilic aromatic substitution (EAS), preserving its stable aromatic sextet rather than undergoing addition reactions.
  • The mechanism of EAS proceeds in two steps: slow generation of the electrophile followed by addition to form a resonance-stabilized arenium ion (σ-complex), and fast loss of a proton to restore aromaticity.
  • Common electrophilic substitutions include nitration (HNO3/H2SO4), halogenation (Cl2/FeCl3), sulfonation (fuming H2SO4), and Friedel-Crafts alkylation/acylation.
  • Friedel-Crafts reactions can suffer from carbocation rearrangements and polyalkylation unless controlled with excess aromatic reactant or acyl chloride derivatives.

Aromaticity and Stability Criteria

  • Huckel's Rule states that monocyclic, planar systems with (4n + 2) π-electrons (where n = 0, 1, 2...) possess exceptional thermodynamic stability known as aromaticity.
  • Benzene exhibits a massive resonance energy of approximately 150 kJ mol-1, making it significantly more stable than predicted for a hypothetical localized 1,3,5-cyclohexatriene.
  • The ring current generated by the delocalized π-electrons leads to a characteristic downfield shift in 1H-NMR spectroscopy, appearing at δ 7.27 ppm.
  • Under extreme conditions like high temperature, high pressure, or ultraviolet irradiation, benzene can undergo addition reactions such as hydrogenation to cyclohexane or chlorination to benzene hexachloride (Lindane).

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Benzene undergoes electrophilic substitution rather than addition

Observation: Addition under drastic UV irradiation or high pressure — Addition destroys the stable aromatic sextet and requires input energy exceeding the resonance stabilization energy unless forced by extreme conditions.

Exception Friedel-Crafts alkylation yields straight-chain alkylbenzenes directly from primary alkyl halides

Observation: Rearrangement to more stable secondary or tertiary carbocations occurs — Primary carbocations rearrange via 1,2-hydride or 1,2-methyl shifts to form more stable carbocation intermediates before attacking the benzene ring.

Exception Friedel-Crafts reactions proceed smoothly with deactivated aromatic rings

Observation: No reaction with strongly deactivated rings like nitrobenzene — Strongly electron-withdrawing groups deactivate the ring toward electrophilic attack and form stable complexes with the Lewis acid catalyst (AlCl3).

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Huckel's Rule Fast Check
Total π e- = 2, 6, 10, 14...

Application: Subtract 2 from the total π electrons and divide by 4. If 'n' is a whole integer (0, 1, 2, 3...), the species is aromatic.

Directing Group Memory Aid
All meta-directing groups have a multiple bond to an electronegative atom (e.g., -NO2, -CN, -COOH)

Application: Quickly identify meta-directors in synthesis questions by checking if the atom directly attached to the ring has a multiple bond to oxygen, nitrogen, or sulfur.

Friedel-Crafts Reagent Pair
Electrophile source + Lewis acid catalyst (R-X + AlCl3)

Application: Never use vinyl or aryl halides in standard Friedel-Crafts reactions because their carbocations are extremely unstable.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Benzene carbon-carbon bond length is 139 pm, intermediate between single (154 pm) and double (134 pm) bonds.
  • The resonance energy of benzene is approximately 150 kJ mol-1.
  • Benzene undergoes electrophilic aromatic substitution via a two-step mechanism involving a σ-complex intermediate.
  • Nitrogen dioxide ion (NO2+) is the active electrophile in benzene nitration using HNO3 and H2SO4.
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation uses an alkyl halide and an anhydrous Lewis acid catalyst such as AlCl3.
  • Huxel's rule requires planar, cyclic, fully conjugated systems with (4n + 2) π-electrons for aromaticity.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming benzene has alternating single and double bonds with localized π-electrons; all six bonds are identical in length and bond order.
  • Confusing Friedel-Crafts alkylation with acylation regarding carbocation rearrangement; acylation does not rearrange because the acylium ion is resonance-stabilized.
  • Forgetting that strongly deactivated rings (e.g., nitrobenzene) do not undergo Friedel-Crafts reactions.
  • Stating that benzene decolorizes cold dilute alkaline KMnO4; benzene is resistant to mild oxidation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Properties of Benzene

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Aromaticity and Structure

Benzene is a planar, cyclic hexagonal molecule with sp2 hybridized carbons and delocalized π-molecular orbitals obeying Huckel's 4n+2 rule.

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution

Reactions proceed via electrophile generation, addition to form the arenium ion, and elimination of a proton to regenerate the aromatic system.

Directing Effects and Activating Groups

Electron-donating groups direct incoming electrophiles to ortho/para positions and activate the ring, while electron-withdrawing groups direct meta and deactivate.

Friedel-Crafts Reactions

Alkylation and acylation introduce alkyl and acyl groups onto the benzene ring using Lewis acid catalysts, subject to carbocation rearrangements in alkylation.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Aromaticity (Cyclic delocalization)
Special stability associated with cyclic, planar, fully conjugated systems having (4n + 2) π-electrons.
Arenium Ion (σ-complex)
Resonance-stabilized carbocation intermediate formed during electrophilic aromatic substitution.
Resonance Energy (ΔHres)
Energy difference between the actual delocalized molecule and its most stable localized contributor.
Electrophilic Substitution (EAS)
Reaction where an electrophile replaces a hydrogen atom on an aromatic ring.
Friedel-Crafts Alkylation (R-Alkyl substitution)
Introduction of an alkyl group into a benzene ring using an alkyl halide and Lewis acid.
Directing Group (Regioselectivity)
Substituent already on the ring that dictates the position of subsequent substitution.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Benzene bond length: 139 pm
  • Resonance energy: ~150 kJ/mol
  • Huckel's Rule: (4n + 2) π-electrons
  • Active electrophile in nitration: NO2+
  • Catalyst for Friedel-Crafts: Anhydrous AlCl3

📌 Quick Recall

  • Benzene is a stable planar molecule with a delocalized π-electron cloud.
  • It undergoes substitution rather than addition to preserve aromatic stability.
  • Substituents on the ring control both reactivity rate and incoming group orientation.
  • Electrophilic substitutions form the cornerstone of aromatic organic synthesis.

⚖️ Key Differences

Electrophilic Addition VS Electrophilic Substitution

Addition breaks the π-system (typical for alkenes); substitution preserves the π-system (typical for benzene).

Question 1 of 10
What is the hybridization of carbon and the bond angle in a benzene molecule?

Solution: Each carbon in benzene is sp2 hybridized with trigonal planar geometry and bond angles of 120°.

Question 2 of 10
Which compound gives ortho and para products upon nitration?

Solution: Halogens like chlorine are moderately deactivating but direct ortho/para due to lone-pair resonance donation.

Question 3 of 10
What is the role of anhydrous AlCl3 in Friedel-Crafts reactions?

Solution: AlCl3 acts as a Lewis acid, accepting a halide ion from alkyl or acyl halides to form reactive carbocations or acylium ions.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following is NOT a characteristic property of benzene?

Solution: Benzene does not react with bromine water under normal conditions unless a Lewis acid catalyst is present, avoiding alkene-like addition.

Question 5 of 10
How many nodal planes are present in the delocalized π-system of benzene?

Solution: The entire cyclic π-system has a nodal plane coinciding with the molecular plane of the carbon ring.

Question 6 of 10
Which species is electrophilic in aromatic sulfonation?

Solution: Neutral sulfur trioxide (SO3) is a potent electrophile due to electron deficiency at the sulfur atom.

Question 7 of 10
What happens when toluene is treated with alkaline KMnO4?

Solution: Alkaline KMnO4 oxidizes the alkyl side chain of toluene completely to a carboxylic acid group, yielding benzoic acid.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following is an activating group for electrophilic aromatic substitution?

Solution: The acetamido group (-NHCOCH3) donates electrons via nitrogen lone pair resonance, activating the ring for ortho/para substitution.

Question 9 of 10
What is the primary product of the chlorination of benzene in the presence of anhydrous FeCl3?

Solution: Chlorination of benzene using Cl2 and FeCl3 yields chlorobenzene via electrophilic aromatic substitution.

Question 10 of 10
According to Huckel's rule, which total number of π-electrons cannot form an aromatic monocyclic ring?

Solution: 8 π-electrons do not fit the (4n + 2) formula (for n=1.5), making 8-electron systems antiaromatic or non-aromatic.
