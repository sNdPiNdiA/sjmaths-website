Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution

Electrophilic aromatic substitution is a chemical process where an electrophile replaces a proton on an aromatic ring via a carbocation intermediate known as a sigma complex or Wheland intermediate. The aromatic system temporarily loses its resonance stabilization before regaining it through the loss of a proton.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mechanism of Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution

  • The first step involves the attack of the π-electron cloud of the aromatic ring on the electrophile to generate a resonance-stabilized arenium ion (Wheland intermediate).
  • The second and rapid step involves the abstraction of a proton by a weak base, restoring aromaticity and completing the substitution pathway.
  • The rate-determining step is almost invariably the formation of the sigma complex, which destroys the aromatic stabilization energy.
  • Kinetic isotope effects (KIE) are typically near unity ($k_H/k_D \approx 1$) because proton abstraction occurs rapidly after the rate-limiting step.

Directing and Activating Effects of Substituents

  • Activating groups (such as -OH, -NH2, -R) donate electron density via resonance or inductive effects, increasing the rate of electrophilic substitution.
  • Deactivating groups (such as -NO2, -CN, -COR) withdraw electron density, raising the activation energy and slowing down substitution relative to benzene.
  • Except for halogens, all activating groups are ortho/para-directing due to the stabilization of the positive charge in the intermediate during attack at these positions.
  • Halogens are deactivating yet ortho/para-directing due to the opposing action of strong inductive electron withdrawal and weak resonance electron donation.

Friedel-Crafts Alkylation and Acylation

  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation introduces an alkyl group using an alkyl halide and a Lewis acid catalyst like AlCl3, but suffers from carbocation rearrangement and polyalkylation.
  • Friedel-Crafts acylation uses an acyl chloride and a Lewis acid to form an unreactive acylium ion complex, preventing polyalkylation and rearrangements.
  • Friedel-Crafts reactions fail with strongly deactivated rings such as nitrobenzene or compounds containing basic amino groups that coordinate irreversibly with the catalyst.
  • Alkylbenzenes undergo rearrangement via hydride or methide shifts to form more stable secondary or tertiary carbocation intermediates prior to ring attack.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Halogens are electron-withdrawing and typically direct electrophiles to the meta position.

Observation: Halogens are ortho/para-directing. — Lone pair resonance donation stabilizes the ortho and para carbocation intermediates more effectively than the inductive withdrawal deactivates the ring overall.

Exception Friedel-Crafts alkylation proceeds cleanly with primary alkyl halides via direct substitution.

Observation: Primary alkyl halides undergo rearrangement to more stable secondary or tertiary carbocations. — The thermodynamic drive to form a more stable carbocation intermediate dictates the final alkyl substitution product rather than the straight-chain skeleton.

Exception Aniline undergoes rapid electrophilic substitution reactions such as nitration and halogenation.

Observation: Direct nitration yields extensive oxidation and tarry byproducts, and Friedel-Crafts reactions fail completely. — The basic nitrogen atom coordinates with the Lewis acid catalyst (AlCl3), rendering the ring strongly deactivated with a permanent positive charge (-NR3+).

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
OPD-M Rule for Substituents
Lone Pair on Atom Attached = Ortho/Para Director

Application: If the atom attached directly to the benzene ring possesses a lone pair of electrons (e.g., -OH, -OR, -NHR, -Cl), the group is ortho/para directing.

Multiple Bond Deactivator Trick
Multiple Bond to Heteroatom = Meta Director

Application: If the attached atom is doubly or triply bonded to a more electronegative atom (e.g., -NO2, -CN, -CHO, -COOH), the group is meta-directing and deactivating.

Friedel-Crafts Acylation Bypass
Acylation + Clemmensen/Wolff-Kishner Reduction = Clean Alkylation

Application: To avoid carbocation rearrangements in alkylation, perform Friedel-Crafts acylation followed by reduction of the carbonyl group to a methylene group.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The electrophile in sulfonation of benzene is sulfur trioxide (SO3) or protonated sulfuric acid.
  • Gattermann-Koch reaction uses carbon monoxide and hydrogen chloride in the presence of AlCl3 and CuCl to form benzaldehyde.
  • Chlorination of benzene requires an anhydrous Lewis acid catalyst like FeCl3 or AlCl3.
  • The nitrating mixture consists of concentrated nitric acid and concentrated sulfuric acid in a 1:1 or specific volumetric ratio.
  • Desulfonation occurs when aromatic sulfonic acids are heated with dilute aqueous acid under steam distillation conditions.
  • Direct iodination of benzene is an equilibrium process that requires an oxidizing agent like HNO3 or HIO3 to consume the generated HI.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming Friedel-Crafts alkylation works well with aniline or nitrobenzene without catalyst complexation issues.
  • Forgetting that Friedel-Crafts acylation produces a complex with the ketone product, requiring stoichiometric rather than catalytic Lewis acid.
  • Confusing the role of sulfuric acid in nitration as a simple solvent rather than a dehydrating catalyst generating the nitronium ion.
  • Neglecting carbocation rearrangements in primary alkyl halides during Friedel-Crafts alkylation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Mechanism of Electrophilic Substitution

Arromatic substitution proceeds via electrophilic attack forming a resonance-stabilized sigma complex followed by rapid deprotonation to restore aromaticity.

Directing Effects

Activating electron-donating groups direct incoming electrophiles to ortho and para positions, whereas deactivating electron-withdrawing groups direct to the meta position.

Friedel-Crafts Reactions

Alkylation and acylation introduce carbon chains onto the aromatic ring via carbocation or acylium ion intermediates using Lewis acid catalysts.

Synthetic Utility

Strategic sequencing of directing groups allows the targeted synthesis of complex multi-substituted benzene derivatives.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Wheland Intermediate (σ-complex)
Resonance-stabilized carbocation intermediate formed during electrophilic aromatic substitution.
Nitronium Ion (NO2+)
Active electrophile generated in mixed acid nitration systems.
Acylium Ion (R-C+=O)
Resonance-stabilized electrophile in Friedel-Crafts acylation.
Activating Group (Electron-Donating Group)
Substituent that increases the rate of electrophilic aromatic substitution.
Deactivating Group (Electron-Withdrawing Group)
Substituent that decreases the rate of electrophilic aromatic substitution.
Kinetic Isotope Effect (KIE)
Ratio of rate constants for isotopically labeled substrates ($k_H/k_D$).

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Rate = k[ArH][E+] for typical electrophilic aromatic substitution
  • Halogens are deactivating but ortho/para directing
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation causes carbocation rearrangements
  • Friedel-Crafts acylation requires stoichiometric Lewis acid
  • Sulfonation is a reversible equilibrium reaction

📌 Quick Recall

  • Electrophilic aromatic substitution involves two main steps: addition of the electrophile and loss of a proton.
  • Substituent electronic effects dictate both the reaction rate and the regiochemical outcome.
  • Friedel-Crafts reactions provide versatile pathways for attaching carbon frameworks to aromatic rings.
  • Anomalous directing behaviors can be rationalized by resonance and inductive contributions.

⚖️ Key Differences

Friedel-Crafts Alkylation VS Friedel-Crafts Acylation

Alkylation introduces alkyl groups and undergoes rearrangement/polyalkylation; acylation introduces acyl groups, does not rearrange, and stops at monosubstitution.

Question 1 of 19

What is the active electrophile in the nitration of benzene using mixed acid?

Explanation: Nitric acid is protonated by sulfuric acid and loses water to generate the nitronium ion (NO2+), which serves as the active electrophile.

Question 2 of 19

Which of the following groups is meta-directing in electrophilic aromatic substitution?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is a strong electron-withdrawing group via resonance and induction, directing incoming electrophiles to the meta position.

Question 3 of 19

Why are halogens ortho/para-directing despite being deactivating?

Explanation: Although halogens withdraw electron density inductively, their lone pairs can be donated via resonance, specifically stabilizing the positive charge at the ortho and para positions.

Question 4 of 19

Which reaction is best suited to prepare n-propylbenzene from benzene without rearrangement?

Explanation: Friedel-Crafts acylation does not rearrange, giving a propanoyl derivative which can then be reduced via Clemmensen or Wolff-Kishner reduction to n-propylbenzene.

Question 5 of 19

What is the rate-determining step in standard electrophilic aromatic substitution of benzene?

Explanation: The formation of the sigma complex (Wheland intermediate) breaks aromaticity and represents the highest activation energy barrier, making it rate-determining.

Question 6 of 19

Which of the following catalysts is commonly used for Friedel-Crafts alkylation?

Explanation: Anhydrous aluminum chloride (AlCl3) is a classic Lewis acid catalyst used to generate carbocations or polar complexes in Friedel-Crafts reactions.

Question 7 of 19

Sulfonation of benzene is reversible. Which reagent drives the reverse reaction (desulfonation)?

Explanation: Heating aromatic sulfonic acids with dilute aqueous acid under steam distillation conditions hydrolyzes the sulfonic acid group off the ring.

Question 8 of 19

Which of the following is an activating group that strongly directs ortho/para?

Explanation: The secondary amino group (-NHCH3) has an unshared pair of electrons on nitrogen that donates electron density into the ring via resonance, strongly activating it.

Question 9 of 19

In the chlorination of benzene using Cl2 and FeCl3, what is the role of FeCl3?

Explanation: FeCl3 acts as a Lewis acid, coordinating with molecular chlorine to polarize the Cl-Cl bond and facilitate electrophilic attack.

Question 10 of 19

What is the primary byproduct formed during Friedel-Crafts acylation using an acyl chloride and AlCl3?

Explanation: The reaction of an acyl chloride with an aromatic ring releases hydrogen chloride (HCl) as a byproduct.

Question 11 of 19

Direct iodination of benzene requires the presence of an oxidizing agent because:

Explanation: Iodination produces HI as a byproduct, which can reduce the iodobenzene back to benzene; an oxidizing agent like nitric acid is added to consume the HI.

Question 12 of 19

Which intermediate is common to all electrophilic aromatic substitution mechanisms?

Explanation: All standard electrophilic aromatic substitutions proceed through a resonance-stabilized carbocation intermediate known as the arenium ion or sigma complex.

Question 13 of 19

Why does aniline fail to undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation or acylation?

Explanation: The basic nitrogen lone pair reacts with the Lewis acid catalyst (e.g., AlCl3), converting the activating -NH2 into a strongly deactivating -NH2+-AlCl3- group.

Question 14 of 19

Which alkyl halide undergoes Friedel-Crafts alkylation without rearrangement?

Explanation: Chloromethane (CH3Cl) cannot form a carbocation that can rearrange, as methyl carbocation is primary and small, though ethyl chloride also cannot rearrange since its cation is primary.

Question 15 of 19

What is the product when benzene is treated with excess chloromethane in the presence of AlCl3?

Explanation: Alkyl groups are activating substituents, so the toluene initially formed is more reactive than benzene, leading to polyalkylation.

Question 16 of 19

Which of the following substituent groups exhibits both -I and +R effects, yet is overall deactivating?

Explanation: Chlorine is more electronegative than carbon, exerting an electron-withdrawing inductive effect (-I), while its lone pairs provide a weak resonance donation (+R); overall, the -I effect dominates, making it deactivating.

Question 17 of 19

In electrophilic aromatic substitution, what happens to the kinetic isotope effect when substituting deuterium instead of protium?

Explanation: Since proton abstraction occurs rapidly after the rate-limiting formation of the sigma complex, the rate does not depend on whether C-H or C-D is broken, yielding a KIE near unity.

Question 18 of 19

Which reagent combination generates the electrophile for formylation of benzene via the Gattermann reaction?

Explanation: The Gattermann reaction utilizes hydrogen cyanide (HCN) and hydrogen chloride in the presence of a Lewis acid catalyst to form benzaldehyde derivatives.

Question 19 of 19

Which of the following compounds is most reactive towards electrophilic nitration?

Explanation: Toluene possesses an electron-donating methyl group via hyperconjugation and induction, which activates the aromatic ring towards electrophilic attack compared to benzene and deactivated rings.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary role of sulfuric acid in the nitration of benzene?

Solution: Sulfuric acid is a stronger acid than nitric acid and protonates it, leading to the loss of water and generation of the active nitronium electrophile.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following substituents deactivates the benzene ring yet is ortho/para-directing?

Solution: Chlorine (-Cl) withdraws electron density inductively (-I) while donating via resonance (+R); the net effect is deactivation with ortho/para-direction.

Question 3 of 10
Which Friedel-Crafts reaction does not experience carbocation rearrangement?

Solution: Friedel-Crafts acylation forms an acylium ion that does not undergo alkyl-type carbocation rearrangements.

Question 4 of 10
What is the active electrophile in Friedel-Crafts alkylation using alkyl chloride and AlCl3?

Solution: The reaction generates a carbocation or a polarized electrophilic complex (R+...AlCl4-) that attacks the aromatic ring.

Question 5 of 10
Why does nitration of aniline under acidic conditions still yield some meta product?

Solution: In strongly acidic nitration media, aniline is protonated to form the anilinium ion (-NH3+), which is strongly electron-withdrawing and meta-directing.

Question 6 of 10
In the sulfonation of benzene, what makes the reaction reversible?

Solution: Sulfonation is a reversible equilibrium process where adding water drives desulfonation and adding excess SO3 drives forward sulfonation.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following compounds reacts most sluggishly in Friedel-Crafts alkylation?

Solution: Nitrobenzene possesses a strongly deactivating, electron-withdrawing nitro group that prevents successful Friedel-Crafts alkylation or acylation reactions.

Question 8 of 10
What is the product of the reaction between benzene and excess methyl chloride in the presence of AlCl3?

Solution: Because alkyl groups activate the ring, multiple alkylations occur successively until steric hindrance prevents further substitution, ultimately forming heavily methylated derivatives.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following directing groups relies primarily on hyperconjugation for its activating and directing properties?

Solution: The methyl group (-CH3) exerts electron-donating effects through inductive release and hyperconjugation, activating the ring towards ortho and para substitution.

Question 10 of 10
What is the kinetic isotope effect ($k_H/k_D$) typically observed for typical electrophilic aromatic substitution reactions?

Solution: Because carbon-hydrogen bond cleavage occurs rapidly after the rate-determining formation of the sigma complex, substituting deuterium results in a KIE near 1.
