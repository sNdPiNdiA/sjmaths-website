Organic Chemistry

Biomolecules

Biomolecules are complex organic molecules that form the structural and functional basis of living organisms, categorized primarily into carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids. They govern metabolic pathways, genetic information storage, and enzymatic catalysis within cellular systems.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Carbohydrate Classification and Reactions

  • Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones or substances that yield them on hydrolysis, classified as monosaccharides, oligosaccharides, and polysaccharides.
  • D- and L-configuration refers to the orientation of the -OH group on the chiral carbon furthest from the carbonyl carbon, mirroring glyceraldehyde.
  • Glucose reacts with hydroxylamine to form an oxime, with hydrogen cyanide to form a cyanohydrin, and is oxidized by bromine water to gluconic acid.
  • Osazone formation involves three molecules of phenylhydrazine reacting with aldoses or ketoses, consuming C-1 and C-2 to yield characteristic yellow crystalline derivatives.

Amino Acid and Protein Architecture

  • Amino acids contain both an amino group (-NH2) and a carboxyl group (-COOH), existing predominantly as zwitterions in aqueous solution at physiological pH.
  • Proteins are linear polymers of L-(α)-amino acids linked together by stable peptide bonds (-CO-NH-).
  • Secondary structure includes the α-helix and β-pleated sheet stabilized exclusively by intermolecular and intramolecular hydrogen bonding between peptide backbone atoms.
  • Denaturation of proteins disrupts secondary, tertiary, and quaternary structures through heat, pH changes, or heavy metal ions without altering the primary amino acid sequence.

Nucleic Acids and Enzyme Catalysis

  • Nucleic acids are polymers of nucleotides composed of a pentose sugar, a nitrogenous base (purine or pyrimidine), and a phosphate group.
  • RNA contains ribose sugar and uracil, whereas DNA contains 2-deoxyribose sugar and thymine with a double-helix structure.
  • Enzymes are biological catalysts that lower the activation energy of biochemical reactions without altering the thermodynamic equilibrium constant.
  • Vitamins are organic compounds required in small amounts for normal health; fat-soluble vitamins include A, D, E, and K, while water-soluble vitamins include the B-complex and C.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception All naturally occurring amino acids are optically active

Observation: Glycine — Glycine has two hydrogen atoms attached to the alpha-carbon, making it achiral and optically inactive.

Exception Carbohydrates follow the general formula C_x(H_2O)_y

Observation: Deoxyribose (C_5H_10O_4) and Rhamnose (C_6H_12O_5) — They do not conform strictly to the stoichiometric ratio of water to carbon despite being classified chemically as sugars.

Exception Aldehydes reduce Tollens' and Fehling's reagents

Observation: Sucrose — Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar because both anomeric carbons (C-1 of glucose and C-2 of fructose) are involved in glycosidic bond formation.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Glucose and fructose are functional isomers containing aldehyde and ketone groups respectively.
  • The linkage between two monosaccharide units in a disaccharide is termed a glycosidic linkage.
  • In alpha-D-glucose, the C-1 -OH group is oriented below the plane of the glucose ring.
  • Ascorbic acid is chemically recognized as Vitamin C and prevents scurvy.
  • Insulin is a peptide hormone that regulates blood glucose homeostasis in humans.
  • Denaturation of egg albumin upon boiling involves the disruption of hydrogen bonds and tertiary folding.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming all carbohydrates are sweet tasting; polysaccharides like starch and cellulose are tasteless.
  • Confusing the alpha-linkage of starch (amylose) with the beta-linkage of cellulose.
  • Forgetting that RNA contains uracil instead of thymine found in DNA.
  • Classifying sucrose as a reducing sugar due to incorrect assumptions about glycosidic linkages.

⚡ Quick Revision: Biomolecules

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Monosaccharides and Mutarotation

Monosaccharides exhibit optical isomerism and undergo mutarotation where specific rotation changes until equilibrium is reached between alpha and beta anomers.

Glycosidic and Peptide Linkages

Biomacromolecules are formed by condensation reactions eliminating water, creating ether linkages (glycosidic) in carbohydrates and amide linkages (peptide) in proteins.

Nucleotide Polymerization

Nucleotides link via phosphodiester bonds between the 3'-OH of one pentose and the 5'-phosphate of the next to form polynucleotide backbones.

Enzymatic Kinetics

Enzymes provide alternative reaction pathways with lower activation energies, exhibiting high substrate specificity and pH/temperature sensitivity.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Anomers (C-1 Epimers)
Diastereomers differing in configuration at the C-1 carbon (hemiacetal carbon).
Zwitterion (Dipolar Ion)
Internal salt of an amino acid containing both positive and negative charges.
Glycosidic Bond (C-O-C Linkage)
Ether linkage joining two monosaccharide units.
Peptide Bond (-CO-NH-)
Amide bond linking amino acid residues in proteins.
Nucleoside (Base + Sugar)
Combination of a nitrogenous base and a pentose sugar without phosphate.
Nucleotide (Base + Sugar + Phosphate)
Phosphorylated nucleoside serving as the monomer of nucleic acids.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Cellulose is a linear polymer of beta-D-glucose with beta(1→4) glycosidic linkages.
  • Amylose is water-soluble with alpha(1→4) linkages, while amylopectin is insoluble with alpha(1→6) branching.
  • Vitamin B12 contains cobalt metal coordinated in a corrin ring.
  • Denatured proteins retain their primary structure intact.
  • DNA base pairing is stabilized by hydrogen bonding between adenine-thymine and guanine-cytosine.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carbohydrates store energy and provide structural support in plants and animals.
  • Proteins serve structural, enzymatic, transport, and hormonal roles.
  • Nucleic acids preserve and transmit hereditary traits across generations.
  • Lipids provide insulation, long-term energy storage, and cellular membrane integrity.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nucleoside VS Nucleotide

A nucleoside contains only base and sugar, whereas a nucleotide includes a phosphate group.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following carbohydrates is a non-reducing sugar?

Explanation: Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar because its glycosidic bond involves the reducing centers (anomeric carbons) of both glucose and fructose.

Question 2 of 18

Which amino acid is optically inactive?

Explanation: Glycine has two hydrogen atoms on the alpha-carbon, possessing a plane of symmetry and lacking a chiral center.

Question 3 of 18

The base found exclusively in RNA but not in DNA is:

Explanation: Uracil replaces thymine in RNA molecules, pairing with adenine.

Question 4 of 18

The linkage present in proteins is:

Explanation: Amino acids are connected via peptide bonds formed by the condensation of carboxylic and amino groups.

Question 5 of 18

Vitamin C is chemically known as:

Explanation: Vitamin C is ascorbic acid, a water-soluble vitamin essential for collagen synthesis.

Question 6 of 18

Alpha-helix structure in proteins is stabilized by:

Explanation: Intramolecular hydrogen bonding between carbonyl oxygen and amide hydrogen stabilizes the alpha-helix.

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following is a fat-soluble vitamin?

Explanation: Vitamins A, D, E, and K are fat-soluble, whereas B-complex and C are water-soluble.

Question 8 of 18

Hydrolysis of starch yields exclusively:

Explanation: Starch is a polysaccharide composed entirely of D-glucose monomer units.

Question 9 of 18

The term anomer of glucose refers to isomers that differ in configuration at:

Explanation: Anomers are diastereomers that differ specifically at the hemiacetal or anomeric carbon (C-1 in aldoses).

Question 10 of 18

Denaturation of proteins does NOT affect which of the following?

Explanation: Denaturation disrupts higher-order structures (2nd, 3rd, 4th) but leaves the covalent peptide bonds of the primary structure intact.

Question 11 of 18

Cellulose differs from amylose in having:

Explanation: Cellulose consists of beta-D-glucose units connected by beta(1→4) glycosidic bonds.

Question 12 of 18

Which reagent is used to test for reducing sugars by forming a red precipitate?

Explanation: Reducing sugars reduce Cu2+ in Fehling's solution to red insoluble cuprous oxide (Cu2O).

Question 13 of 18

Nucleosides are composed of:

Explanation: A nucleoside consists solely of a nitrogenous base attached to a pentose sugar.

Question 14 of 18

Which metal ion is present in Vitamin B12?

Explanation: Vitamin B12 (cyanocobalamin) contains a centrally coordinated cobalt ion.

Question 15 of 18

The isoelectric point of an amino acid is the pH at which:

Explanation: At the isoelectric point, the concentration of the zwitterion is maximal and the net electrical charge is zero.

Question 16 of 18

Which sugar is present in milk?

Explanation: Lactose is a disaccharide composed of galactose and glucose found in milk.

Question 17 of 18

The double helical model of DNA was proposed by:

Explanation: James Watson and Francis Crick proposed the double helix structure of DNA in 1953.

Question 18 of 18

Which of the following is classified as a derived lipid?

Explanation: Cholesterol is a sterol and a derived lipid obtained upon hydrolysis of complex lipids.
