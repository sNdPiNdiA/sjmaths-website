Classification of Vitamins

Vitamins are organic compounds required in minute quantities for various physiological functions and are classified based on their solubility in water or lipids. This solubility profile dictates their absorption, transport, storage, and excretion pathways within the human body.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Fat-Soluble Vitamins (A, D, E, K)

  • These vitamins possess hydrophobic hydrocarbon chains or ring systems, making them insoluble in water but soluble in lipids and organic solvents.
  • They are absorbed along with dietary fats in the intestine utilizing micelles and bile salts, subsequently entering the lymphatic system.
  • Excess intake of fat-soluble vitamins leads to accumulation in the liver and adipose tissue, potentially causing hypervitaminosis.
  • They typically function as structural components, steroid hormone precursors, or cellular antioxidants rather than direct coenzymes.

Water-Soluble Vitamins (B-Complex and C)

  • These compounds contain numerous polar functional groups such as hydroxyl, amino, or carboxyl groups that confer high aqueous solubility.
  • They are readily absorbed directly into the bloodstream from the gastrointestinal tract and are excreted rapidly via urine.
  • Due to negligible bodily storage, regular daily dietary intake is required to prevent deficiency syndromes like scurvy or beri-beri.
  • Most water-soluble vitamins function as essential precursors for coenzymes involved in intermediary cellular metabolism.

Structural and Functional Sub-classification

  • The B-complex family is subdivided into energy-releasing vitamins (e.g., B1, B2, B3) and hematopoietic vitamins (e.g., B9, B12).
  • Vitamin C acts as a potent biological reducing agent and electron donor due to its easily oxidizable enediol moiety.
  • Vitamin K exists in multiple forms including phylloquinone (K1) and menaquinone (K2), essential for post-translational carboxylation of clotting factors.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Water-soluble vitamins are not stored in the body and are excreted daily.

Observation: Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin) — It contains a cobalt metal center and a complex corrin ring, allowing significant hepatic storage lasting several years.

Exception Vitamins must be obtained exclusively from external dietary sources.

Observation: Vitamin D and Vitamin K — Vitamin D is synthesized in the skin upon UV exposure, and Vitamin K is biosynthesized by commensal intestinal microflora.

Exception Vitamins function strictly as organic cofactors or antioxidants.

Observation: Vitamin D (Calcitriol) — It undergoes sequential hydroxylations in the liver and kidney to function as a classic steroid hormone regulating calcium homeostasis.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Vitamin A deficiency causes night blindness (nyctalopia) and xerophthalmia.
  • Vitamin D deficiency leads to rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults.
  • Vitamin E protects polyunsaturated fatty acids from lipid peroxidation.
  • Vitamin K is strictly required for the gamma-carboxylation of glutamate residues in prothrombin.
  • Vitamin B1 deficiency results in beriberi, characterized by neurological and cardiovascular disorders.
  • Vitamin B12 contains a corrin ring with a centrally coordinated cobalt ion.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Vitamin C as a lipid-soluble antioxidant; it is strictly water-soluble.
  • Assuming all B-complex vitamins are stored in the liver like Vitamin B12.
  • Forgetting that Vitamin D acts biologically as a steroid hormone rather than a traditional coenzyme.
  • Overlooking that Vitamin K can be synthesized endogenously by gut bacteria.

⚡ Quick Revision: Classification of Vitamins

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Solubility Basis of Classification

Vitamins are broadly partitioned into lipophilic (A, D, E, K) and hydrophilic (B-complex, C) categories which dictate their pharmacokinetic behavior.

Lipid-Solubility Characteristics

Fat-soluble vitamins require dietary lipids and bile acids for intestinal absorption and accumulate within lipid droplets and hepatic tissue.

Aqueous-Solubility Characteristics

Water-soluble vitamins dissolve freely in aqueous body fluids, act predominantly as coenzymes, and require continuous dietary repletion.

Metabolic and Physiological Roles

Vitamins serve vital catalytic, hormonal, and protective functions despite being required in microgram to milligram quantities.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Hypervitaminosis (Pathological Condition)
Toxic condition resulting from excessive accumulation of fat-soluble vitamins.
Coenzyme (Cofactor)
Non-protein organic molecule bound to an enzyme essential for catalytic activity.
Nyctalopia (Night Blindness)
Inability to see clearly in dim light due to Vitamin A deficiency.
Antioxidant (Free Radical Scavenger)
Substance that inhibits oxidation and protects cells from reactive oxygen species.
Corrin Ring (Macrocyclic Ligand)
Tetrapyrrole-derived chelating ring system found in Vitamin B12.
Secysteroid (Modified Steroid)
Steroid derivative where one of the B-ring carbon-carbon bonds is cleaved.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Fat-soluble: A, D, E, K
  • Water-soluble: B-complex, C
  • Vitamin B12 stores in liver despite being water-soluble
  • Vitamin C is ascorbic acid, acting as a reducing agent
  • Vitamin K is essential for blood coagulation

📌 Quick Recall

  • Vitamins are classified purely by physical solubility in lipids versus water.
  • Lipid solubility governs storage risk and bile salt dependency.
  • Water solubility dictates rapid renal clearance and frequent dietary requirement.
  • Biochemical roles diverge between coenzyme activity (B, C) and structural/hormonal roles (A, D, E, K).

⚖️ Key Differences

Vitamin D3 VS Vitamin D2

D3 (cholecalciferol) is animal-derived via UV skin synthesis, while D2 (ergocalciferol) is plant-derived.

Which of the following vitamins is classified as water-soluble?

Question 2 of 18

Fat-soluble vitamins are absorbed along with fats in the presence of:

Explanation: Bile salts emulsify dietary fats and fat-soluble vitamins into micelles for efficient intestinal mucosal absorption.

Question 3 of 18

Which vitamin is stored in significant quantities in the human liver despite being water-soluble?

Explanation: Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) binds to specific carrier proteins and can be stored in hepatic tissue for years.

Question 4 of 18

Excessive accumulation of which vitamin group leads to hypervitaminosis?

Explanation: Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) are stored in adipose tissue and the liver, causing toxicity when overconsumed.

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following is chemically a secysteroid?

Explanation: Vitamin D possesses a broken B-ring steroidal skeleton, defining it chemically as a secysteroid.

Question 6 of 18

Thiamine (Vitamin B1) contains which heterocyclic ring systems?

Explanation: Thiamine consists of a substituted pyrimidine ring bridged to a thiazole ring.

Question 7 of 18

The primary physiological role of Vitamin E is:

Explanation: Vitamin E (α-tocopherol) acts as a premier lipid-soluble chain-breaking antioxidant protecting polyunsaturated fatty acids.

Question 8 of 18

Which vitamin deficiency causes scurvy?

Explanation: Vitamin C deficiency impairs collagen hydroxylation, resulting in the connective tissue breakdown known as scurvy.

Question 9 of 18

Phylloquinone is another chemical name for:

Explanation: Phylloquinone is plant-derived Vitamin K1.

Question 10 of 18

Which vitamin functions biologically as a steroid hormone regulating calcium and phosphate metabolism?

Explanation: Active calcitriol (Vitamin D) binds nuclear receptors to regulate intestinal calcium absorption.

Question 11 of 18

Niacin (Vitamin B3) is a derivative of which nitrogenous heterocycle?

Explanation: Niacin is pyridine-3-carboxylic acid.

Question 12 of 18

Riboflavin (Vitamin B2) is a constituent of which coenzymes?

Explanation: Riboflavin forms the reactive isoalloxazine ring system in Flavin Mononucleotide (FMN) and Flavin Adenine Dinucleotide (FAD).

Question 13 of 18

Which metal ion is coordinated within the macrocyclic corrin ring of Vitamin B12?

Explanation: Cobalamin contains a central cobalt atom coordinated to four nitrogens of the corrin ring.

Question 14 of 18

Beta-carotene serves as a biochemical precursor (provitamin) for:

Explanation: Oxidative cleavage of β-carotene in the intestinal mucosa yields retinal, the active form of Vitamin A.

Question 15 of 18

What is the primary excretion route for excess water-soluble vitamins?

Explanation: Water-soluble vitamins are filtered by the kidneys and excreted rapidly in urine when intake exceeds bodily saturation.

Question 16 of 18

Pyridoxine is the chemical designation for:

Explanation: Vitamin B6 exists in forms including pyridoxine, pyridoxal, and pyridoxamine.

Question 17 of 18

Which vitamin is synthesized endogenously by human intestinal bacterial flora?

Explanation: Menaquinones (Vitamin K2) are synthesized by commensal bacteria in the large intestine.

Question 18 of 18

The structural classification of vitamins into fat-soluble and water-soluble groups was primarily based on:

Explanation: The fundamental dichotomy rests strictly on whether the compound dissolves in organic lipid phases or aqueous phases.

Which of the following vitamins acts as a cofactor for carboxylation reactions involving blood clotting proteins?

Solution: Vitamin K carboxylates glutamate residues on prothrombin and other coagulation factors.

Question 5 of 10
Tocopherols represent the chemical family for:

Solution: Vitamin E refers to a group of methylated phenols known as tocopherols and tocotrienols.

Question 6 of 10
Which vitamin deficiency is directly linked to prolonged blood clotting times?

Solution: Vitamin K deficiency prevents proper gamma-carboxylation of clotting factors II, VII, IX, and X.

Retinol, retinal, and retinoic acid are different forms of:

Solution: These are the active retinoids comprising Vitamin A activity.

What structural feature distinguishes water-soluble vitamins from fat-soluble vitamins?

Solution: Water-soluble vitamins contain abundant hydrogen-bonding polar functional groups conferring high aqueous solubility.

Question 9 of 10

Solution: Vitamin C is heat-sensitive and readily oxidized in aqueous food preparation.

Question 10 of 10
