Organic Chemistry

Vitamin Deficiency Diseases

Vitamins are essential organic micronutrients required in trace amounts for normal metabolic functions and physiological maintenance. Dietary deprivation or malabsorption of specific vitamins leads to defined deficiency syndromes and metabolic pathologies.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Fat-Soluble Vitamin Pathologies

  • Vitamin A (Retinol) deficiency manifests as xerophthalmia, corneal keratinization, and night blindness (nyctalopia) due to impaired rhodopsin regeneration.
  • Vitamin D (Cholecalciferol) deficiency impairs intestinal calcium and phosphate absorption, causing rickets in growing children and osteomalacia in adults.
  • Vitamin E (Tocopherol) deficiency leads to increased erythrocyte hemolysis and neurological dysfunction due to loss of cellular antioxidant protection.
  • Vitamin K (Phylloquinone/Menaquinone) deficiency prevents the gamma-carboxylation of glutamic acid residues in clotting factors II, VII, IX, and X, resulting in hemorrhagic disorders.

Water-Soluble Vitamin Syndromes

  • Vitamin B1 (Thiamine) deficiency causes beriberi (affecting cardiovascular and peripheral nervous systems) and Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome in chronic alcoholism.
  • Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin) deficiency produces cheilosis, angular stomatitis, glossitis, and vascularization of the cornea.
  • Vitamin B3 (Niacin) deficiency results in pellagra, classically characterized by the four Ds: dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia, and death.
  • Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin) deficiency causes pernicious anemia and subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord due to impaired DNA synthesis and myelin maintenance.
  • Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid) deficiency leads to scurvy, characterized by defective collagen hydroxylation (proline/lysine), fragile capillaries, and bleeding gums.

Biochemical Coenzyme Roles and Deficiencies

  • Thiamine Pyrophosphate (TPP) acts as a coenzyme for pyruvate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase; deficiency leads to cellular energy metabolism failure.
  • Pyridoxal Phosphate (PLP, Vitamin B6) deficiency impairs transamination, decarboxylation, and glycogen phosphorylase, causing sideroblastic anemia and peripheral neuropathy.
  • Folic Acid (Vitamin B9) deficiency disrupts one-carbon transfer reactions, causing megaloblastic anemia and neural tube defects (e.g., spina bifida) during embryogenesis.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Water-soluble vitamins are not stored in the body and must be replenished daily.

Observation: Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin) — It is stored in the hepatic parenchyma in large quantities, meaning deficiency symptoms take years to manifest after cessation of intake.

Exception Vitamins are exclusively obtained from exogenous dietary sources.

Observation: Vitamin D and Vitamin K — Vitamin D is synthesized photochemically in the skin from 7-dehydrocholesterol, and Vitamin K is synthesized by commensal intestinal microflora.

Exception Deficiencies of water-soluble vitamins cause immediate clinical symptoms due to rapid clearance.

Observation: Folic Acid and Vitamin B12 — Both have substantial tissue pools and enterohepatic circulation, delaying the onset of megaloblastic anemia.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Vitamin A deficiency causes night blindness (nyctalopia) and xerophthalmia.
  • Vitamin B1 (Thiamine) deficiency causes beriberi and is linked to high carbohydrate diets.
  • Vitamin B3 (Niacin) deficiency leads to pellagra, characterized by dermatitis, diarrhea, and dementia.
  • Vitamin B12 deficiency results in megaloblastic anemia coupled with neurological degeneration.
  • Vitamin C deficiency impairs proline hydroxylation in collagen, producing scurvy.
  • Vitamin D deficiency causes rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Vitamin B1 deficiency (beriberi) with Vitamin B3 deficiency (pellagra).
  • Assuming water-soluble vitamins cannot accumulate to toxic levels; high doses of niacin cause flushing and liver damage, and vitamin B6 causes neuropathy.
  • Forgetting that Vitamin D acts functionally as a steroid prohormone rather than a classical dietary catalyst.
  • Overlooking that fat-soluble vitamin deficiencies occur readily in patients with pancreatic or biliary tract disorders.

⚡ Quick Revision: Vitamin Deficiency Diseases

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Fat-Soluble Vitamin Deficiencies

Vitamins A, D, E, and K require lipid absorption mechanisms. Deficiencies lead to ocular, skeletal, antioxidant, and coagulation pathologies respectively.

B-Complex Coenzyme Deficiencies

B vitamins serve primarily as enzymatic cofactors. Their absence cripples intermediary metabolism, energy production, and amino acid processing.

Ascorbic Acid and Hydroxylation

Vitamin C maintains metal ions in reduced states (e.g., Fe2+) required by prolyl and lysyl hydroxylases for stable collagen triple helix formation.

Clinical Diagnosis of Avitaminosis

Specific physical stigmata such as Bitot's spots (Vitamin A), cheilosis (Vitamin B2), and petechiae (Vitamin C) serve as hallmark diagnostic indicators.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Retinol (Vitamin A)
Fat-soluble isoprenoid essential for vision and epithelial maintenance.
Thiamine (Vitamin B1)
Coenzyme in decarboxylation reactions; deficiency causes beriberi.
Niacin (Vitamin B3)
Precursor for NAD+ and NADP+; deficiency causes pellagra.
Cobalamin (Vitamin B12)
Corrinoid ring complex containing cobalt; deficiency causes pernicious anemia.
Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C)
Strong biological reducing agent; deficiency causes scurvy.
Cholecalciferol (Vitamin D3)
Steroid derivative regulating calcium and phosphate homeostasis.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Vitamin A deficiency causes night blindness and Bitot's spots.
  • Vitamin B1 deficiency is termed beriberi.
  • Vitamin B3 deficiency causes pellagra (4 Ds).
  • Vitamin C deficiency causes scurvy due to defective collagen synthesis.
  • Vitamin D deficiency causes rickets in children.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Vitamins are vital organic micronutrients categorized into fat-soluble and water-soluble groups.
  • Deficiencies disrupt specific enzymatic, structural, or signaling pathways.
  • Fat-soluble vitamin deficiencies take longer to appear due to hepatic storage.
  • Water-soluble deficiencies manifest rapidly due to lack of significant bodily reserves.

⚖️ Key Differences

Osteomalacia VS Osteoporosis

Osteomalacia is caused by Vitamin D deficiency leading to defective bone mineralization, whereas osteoporosis is a reduction in total bone mass and matrix.

Question 1 of 18

Night blindness (nyctalopia) is caused by the deficiency of which vitamin?

Explanation: Vitamin A (retinol) is a precursor for retinal, the chromophore of rhodopsin required for low-light vision; its deficiency directly causes night blindness.

Question 2 of 18

Pellagra is associated with the deficiency of:

Explanation: Niacin (Vitamin B3) deficiency causes pellagra, characterized by dermatitis, diarrhea, and dementia.

Question 3 of 18

Which of the following vitamins is water-soluble?

Explanation: Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) and the B-complex vitamins are water-soluble, whereas A, D, E, and K are fat-soluble.

Question 4 of 18

Scurvy is characterized by bleeding gums and poor wound healing due to the deficiency of:

Explanation: Ascorbic acid (Vitamin C) deficiency impairs the hydroxylation of proline and lysine in collagen, causing fragile connective tissue and scurvy.

Question 5 of 18

Rickets in children is caused by a deficiency of:

Explanation: Vitamin D deficiency leads to impaired calcium absorption, resulting in defective bone mineralization known as rickets in children.

Question 6 of 18

Pernicious anemia is caused by the deficiency of:

Explanation: Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) deficiency, often due to a lack of intrinsic factor, causes megaloblastic pernicious anemia.

Question 7 of 18

Beriberi is caused by the deficiency of:

Explanation: Thiamine (Vitamin B1) deficiency causes beriberi, which affects the peripheral nervous and cardiovascular systems.

Question 8 of 18

Which vitamin deficiency leads to prolonged blood clotting times?

Explanation: Vitamin K is a necessary cofactor for the gamma-glutamyl carboxylase that activates blood clotting factors II, VII, IX, and X.

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following is also known as Ascorbic Acid?

Explanation: Ascorbic acid is the chemical name for Vitamin C.

Question 10 of 18

Cheilosis and angular stomatitis are deficiency symptoms of:

Explanation: Riboflavin deficiency characteristically manifests as inflammation of the lips (cheilosis) and cracks at the corners of the mouth (angular stomatitis).

Question 11 of 18

Which vitamin can be synthesized by human skin upon exposure to ultraviolet light?

Explanation: Vitamin D3 is synthesized in the skin when 7-dehydrocholesterol reacts with UVB radiation.

Question 12 of 18

Which B vitamin is crucial during pregnancy to prevent neural tube defects such as spina bifida?

Explanation: Folic acid is essential for DNA synthesis and closure of the neural tube during early embryonic development.

Question 13 of 18

A patient presenting with peripheral neuropathy, confusion, and ophthalmoplegia due to chronic alcoholism likely has:

Explanation: Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome is a severe neurological manifestation of thiamine (Vitamin B1) deficiency common in chronic alcoholism.

Question 14 of 18

Which vitamin acts as a primary lipid-soluble cellular antioxidant protecting cell membranes?

Explanation: Vitamin E (tocopherol) functions as a free radical scavenger, protecting polyunsaturated fatty acids in cell membranes from lipid peroxidation.

Question 15 of 18

Excessive intake of which type of vitamin is most likely to cause hypervitaminosis and toxicity?

Explanation: Fat-soluble vitamins are stored in bodily tissues and are not readily excreted, leading to potential toxicity upon excessive supplementation.

Question 16 of 18

Bitot's spots on the conjunctiva are a clinical sign of:

Explanation: Bitot's spots are triangular, foamy accumulations of keratinized epithelial debris on the conjunctiva, characteristic of severe Vitamin A deficiency.

Question 17 of 18

Which amino acid serves as a metabolic precursor for the endogenous synthesis of niacin (Vitamin B3)?

Explanation: The human body can synthesize niacin from the essential amino acid tryptophan at a conversion rate of approximately 60 mg tryptophan to 1 mg niacin.

Question 18 of 18

Coenzyme A contains which water-soluble vitamin as a structural component?

Explanation: Pantothenic acid (Vitamin B5) is a core component of Coenzyme A (CoA), which is vital for acyl group transfer in cellular respiration.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following coenzyme forms is derived from niacin (Vitamin B3)?

Solution: Niacin is the functional precursor for nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) and its phosphorylated derivative (NADP+).

Question 2 of 10
A patient with prolonged fat malabsorption due to celiac disease is most likely to develop deficiencies in:

Solution: Fat malabsorption impairs the uptake of all lipid-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, and K).

Question 3 of 10
Which vitamin deficiency is associated with glossitis, angular stomatitis, and corneal vascularization?

Solution: Riboflavin deficiency typically presents with mucosal lesions of the mouth and tongue, alongside ocular vascularization.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following minerals or factors is required alongside Vitamin B12 for its proper absorption in the terminal ileum?

Solution: Gastric parietal cells secrete Intrinsic Factor, which binds to Vitamin B12 and enables its receptor-mediated endocytosis in the ileum.

Question 5 of 10
Hypervitaminosis D leads to:

Solution: Excessive Vitamin D causes extreme intestinal calcium absorption and bone resorption, leading to hypercalcemia and pathological calcification of soft tissues.

Question 6 of 10
Which vitamin is synthesized by human large intestine flora in nutritionally significant quantities?

Solution: Commensal bacteria in the colon synthesize menaquinones (Vitamin K2), contributing to human dietary requirements.

Question 7 of 10
In collagen synthesis, Vitamin C acts as a reducing agent to keep which metal ion in its reduced, active state?

Solution: Prolyl and lysyl hydroxylases require iron in the ferrous state (Fe2+); Vitamin C reduces oxidized ferric iron (Fe3+) back to Fe2+.

Question 8 of 10
Which deficiency causes megaloblastic anemia that is clinically indistinguishable from folate deficiency?

Solution: Both folate and Vitamin B12 deficiencies disrupt purine and pyrimidine synthesis, resulting in megaloblastic anemia, though B12 also causes neurological symptoms.

Question 9 of 10
Which condition is caused by severe protein-energy malnutrition accompanied by micronutrient deficiencies, distinct from pure vitamin deficiency?

Solution: Kwashiorkor and marasmus are primary protein-energy malnutrition syndromes, whereas beriberi, pellagra, and scurvy are specific avitaminoses.

Question 10 of 10
Which vitamin is a derivative of naphthoquinone and plays a key role in post-translational modification of proteins?

Solution: Vitamin K is a 2-methyl-1,4-naphthoquinone derivative essential for the gamma-carboxylation of specific glutamate residues in blood clotting and bone proteins.
