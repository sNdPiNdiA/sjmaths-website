Organic Chemistry

Vitamins

Vitamins are organic compounds required in small amounts for normal metabolic functions and cannot be synthesized in adequate quantities by the human body. They are classified into water-soluble and fat-soluble categories based on their solubility profiles.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification and Solubility Profiles

  • Vitamins are broadly classified into fat-soluble (A, D, E, K) and water-soluble (B-complex and C) groups based on polarity and storage characteristics.
  • Fat-soluble vitamins possess long aliphatic hydrocarbon chains or fused ring systems, allowing them to accumulate in liver and adipose tissues.
  • Water-soluble vitamins contain numerous polar hydroxyl or amine groups, making them readily excretable via urine and necessitating daily dietary intake.
  • Excessive accumulation of fat-soluble vitamins can lead to hypervitaminosis, whereas excess water-soluble vitamins are typically flushed out.

Structural Features and Coenzyme Roles

  • Vitamin B1 (Thiamine) functions as thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP) in oxidative decarboxylation of α-keto acids.
  • Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin) serves as a precursor for FAD and FMN, acting as electron carriers in redox reactions.
  • Vitamin B3 (Niacin) forms the active sites of NAD+ and NADP+ involved in hydride transfer.
  • Vitamin B12 (Cyanocobalamin) contains a corrin ring system with a centrally coordinated cobalt(III) ion.
  • Vitamin C acts as a potent antioxidant by undergoing reversible oxidation to dehydroascorbic acid.

Deficiency Pathologies and Dietary Sources

  • Deficiency of Vitamin A causes xerophthalmia and night blindness due to impaired rhodopsin regeneration in the retina.
  • Deficiency of Vitamin D causes rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults due to defective calcium and phosphorus mineralization.
  • Deficiency of Vitamin C leads to scurvy, characterized by defective collagen synthesis and fragile capillary blood vessels.
  • Deficiency of Vitamin B1 results in beri-beri, marked by neurological and cardiovascular complications.
  • Deficiency of Vitamin B12 causes pernicious anemia linked to impaired DNA synthesis and megaloblastic erythrocyte maturation.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception All vitamins must be acquired entirely from exogenous dietary sources.

Observation: Vitamin D and Vitamin K — Vitamin D is synthesized endogenously in skin upon UV exposure from 7-dehydrocholesterol, and Vitamin K is synthesized by gut commensal bacteria.

Exception Water-soluble vitamins are not stored in the body and must be replenished daily.

Observation: Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin) — Vitamin B12 is stored in significant amounts in the liver, meaning deficiency symptoms can take years to manifest after dietary cessation.

Exception Vitamins act solely as organic cofactors and coenzymes.

Observation: Vitamin D and Vitamin A (Retinoic acid) — These function as steroid and hormone-like regulators that bind to nuclear receptors to modulate gene transcription.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Vitamin A (Retinol) is a diterpene containing a β-ionone ring and an isoprenoid side chain.
  • Vitamin C is a strong reducing agent due to its enediol functional group capable of donating two electrons.
  • Vitamin B12 is unique among vitamins for containing a transition metal ion, specifically cobalt coordinated within a corrin macrocycle.
  • Excess intake of Vitamin D leads to hypercalcemia and soft tissue calcification.
  • Provitamin A carotenoids like β-carotene undergo cleavage in the intestinal mucosa to yield retinal.
  • Tocopherols (Vitamin E) protect polyunsaturated fatty acids in cell membranes from lipid peroxidation.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Vitamin B3 (Niacin) deficiency with scurvy; Niacin deficiency causes pellagra (the 4 Ds: dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia, death).
  • Assuming all B-complex vitamins contain nitrogen atoms arranged in pyridine rings; structures vary widely from thiazole rings to pteridine rings.
  • Assuming water-soluble vitamins cannot accumulate to toxic levels; high doses of synthetic Vitamin B6 can cause peripheral neuropathy.
  • Forgetting that calciferol requires dual hydroxylations in the liver and kidneys to become the active hormone calcitriol.

⚡ Quick Revision: Vitamins

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Chemical Classification of Vitamins

Vitamins are categorized into fat-soluble and water-soluble compounds, dictating their absorption, transport, storage, and excretion pathways in human physiology.

Coenzymatic Role of B-Vitamins

Water-soluble B vitamins function primarily as precursors to essential coenzymes such as TPP, NAD+, FAD, and CoA involved in cellular energy metabolism.

Lipid-Soluble Regulatory Functions

Vitamins A and D function beyond simple cofactors, acting as hormone-like transcriptional regulators that modulate gene expression and cellular differentiation.

Deficiency Disorders and Pathologies

Inadequate dietary intake or malabsorption of specific vitamins leads to distinct clinical syndromes such as scurvy, rickets, beriberi, and night blindness.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Retinol (Vitamin A1)
Essential fat-soluble isoprenoid alcohol involved in vision and epithelial maintenance.
Thiamine (Vitamin B1)
Water-soluble coenzyme precursor containing thiazole and pyrimidine rings.
Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C)
Lactone antioxidant acting as a reducing agent in collagen hydroxylation.
Cholecalciferol (Vitamin D3)
Secosteroid hormone regulating calcium and phosphate homeostasis.
α-Tocopherol (Vitamin E)
Lipophilic chain-breaking antioxidant protecting cell membrane lipids.
Cyanocobalamin (Vitamin B12)
Cobalt-containing corrinoid compound required for erythropoiesis and nervous system function.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Fat-soluble vitamins: A, D, E, K (stored in liver).
  • Water-soluble vitamins: B-complex and C (excreted in urine).
  • Vitamin C deficiency causes scurvy.
  • Vitamin D deficiency causes rickets in children.
  • Vitamin B12 contains a cobalt ion in a corrin ring.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Vitamins are essential micronutrients acting as coenzymes or regulatory agents.
  • Deficiencies result in specific metabolic and structural pathologies.
  • Fat-soluble vitamins pose higher risks of hypervitaminosis due to storage capacity.
  • Structure-function relationships determine whether a vitamin acts as an antioxidant, coenzyme, or hormone.

⚖️ Key Differences

Niacin (Vitamin B3) VS Inositol

Niacin is an essential B-vitamin forming NAD+, whereas inositol is a carbocyclic sugar involved in cell signaling, not classified strictly as a vitamin.

Which of the following vitamins is water-soluble?

Explanation: Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) is water-soluble, whereas vitamins A, D, and K are fat-soluble.

Question 2 of 18

Night blindness is caused by the deficiency of which vitamin?

Explanation: Vitamin A (retinol) is essential for the formation of rhodopsin in retinal rod cells; its deficiency leads to night blindness.

Question 3 of 18

Which vitamin contains a cobalt atom coordinated in a corrin ring system?

Explanation: Cyanocobalamin (Vitamin B12) contains a central cobalt atom bonded to a corrin macrocycle.

Question 4 of 18

Scurvy is caused by the deficiency of:

Explanation: Ascorbic acid (Vitamin C) deficiency impairs collagen hydroxylation, resulting in scurvy.

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following is also known as Calciferol?

Explanation: Vitamin D is a group of secosteroids known generically as calciferols.

Question 6 of 18

Beriberi is caused by the deficiency of:

Explanation: Vitamin B1 (thiamine) deficiency results in the neurological and cardiovascular disorder known as beriberi.

Question 7 of 18

Which vitamin acts as a powerful biological chain-breaking antioxidant in lipid membranes?

Explanation: α-Tocopherol (Vitamin E) is a lipid-soluble antioxidant that protects cell membranes from radical-mediated peroxidation.

Question 8 of 18

Pellagra is associated with the deficiency of:

Explanation: Niacin deficiency causes pellagra, characterized by dermatitis, diarrhea, and dementia.

Question 9 of 18

Which vitamin is synthesized by intestinal bacteria in humans?

Explanation: Vitamin K is synthesized by commensal bacteria residing in the human large intestine.

Question 10 of 18

What is the primary function of Vitamin K in blood coagulation?

Explanation: Vitamin K acts as a cofactor for γ-glutamyl carboxylase, which carboxylates glutamate residues on prothrombin and other clotting factors.

Question 11 of 18

Which vitamin derivative functions as a coenzyme in transamination and decarboxylation reactions of amino acids?

Explanation: Pyridoxal phosphate (PLP), the active form of Vitamin B6, is the primary coenzyme for amino acid metabolism.

Question 12 of 18

Excessive intake of which vitamin class is most likely to result in hypervitaminosis and toxicity?

Explanation: Fat-soluble vitamins accumulate in the liver and adipose tissue, increasing toxicity risks upon excessive intake.

Question 13 of 18

Which of the following is a symptom of Vitamin D deficiency in children?

Explanation: Rickets involves impaired bone mineralization in growing children due to insufficient Vitamin D.

Question 14 of 18

Riboflavin (Vitamin B2) is a precursor for which coenzymes?

Explanation: Riboflavin is a structural component of flavin mononucleotide (FMN) and flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD).

Question 15 of 18

Which compound is provitamin A?

Explanation: β-Carotene is a carotenoid cleaved in the body to produce molecules of Vitamin A (retinol).

Question 16 of 18

Pernicious anemia is linked to the malabsorption of:

Explanation: Pernicious anemia results from an autoimmune destruction or lack of intrinsic factor required for Vitamin B12 absorption.

Question 17 of 18

Which functional group in ascorbic acid is primarily responsible for its reducing property?

Explanation: The enediol moiety in ascorbic acid readily donates electrons to become dehydroascorbic acid.

Question 18 of 18

Which vitamin is also known as Folic Acid?

Explanation: Vitamin B9 is commonly designated as folic acid, vital for one-carbon transfer reactions and DNA synthesis.

Solution: Water-soluble vitamins are polar and excess amounts are filtered and excreted by the kidneys.

Solution: Dietary β-carotene and retinyl esters are cleaved and hydrolyzed to yield active retinal and retinol.
