Functions of Vitamins

Vitamins are essential organic micronutrients required in trace amounts for regulating various metabolic processes, cellular growth, and overall physiological maintenance. They act predominantly as coenzymes, prosthetic groups, or potent biological antioxidants.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Water-Soluble Vitamins and Coenzyme Roles

  • Vitamin B1 (Thiamine) functions as thiamine pyrophosphate in oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate and α-ketoglutarate.
  • Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin) forms the prosthetic groups FAD and FMN involved in electron transport chains.
  • Vitamin B3 (Niacin) is the precursor for NAD+ and NADP+, acting as hydride acceptors in dehydrogenation.
  • Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine) converts to pyridoxal phosphate, catalyzing transamination, decarboxylation, and racemization of amino acids.
  • Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin) contains a central Co3+ ion coordinated to a corrin ring and catalyzes intramolecular rearrangements.

Fat-Soluble Vitamins and Physiological Functions

  • Vitamin A (Retinol/Retinal) forms 11-cis-retinal which binds to opsin to form rhodopsin for visual phototransduction.
  • Vitamin D3 (Cholecalciferol) is hydroxylated in liver and kidneys to 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol to promote bone mineralization.
  • Vitamin E (α-Tocopherol) functions as a chain-breaking antioxidant protecting polyunsaturated fatty acids from lipid peroxidation.
  • Vitamin K (Phylloquinone/Menaquinone) acts as a cofactor for γ-glutamyl carboxylase, essential for blood clotting factors II, VII, IX, and X.

Deficiency Pathologies and Metabolic Signatures

  • Thiamine deficiency results in Beriberi, characterized by neurological disorders and cardiovascular impairments due to halted TCA cycle flux.
  • Niacin deficiency causes Pellagra, classically identified by the four Ds: dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia, and death.
  • Ascorbic acid absence prevents proline hydroxylation leading to unstable collagen fibrils, manifesting clinically as Scurvy.
  • Vitamin D deficiency in children causes Rickets, characterized by defective bone mineralization and skeletal deformities.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Most vitamins cannot be synthesized by higher animals and must be obtained from diet.

Observation: Vitamin D can be synthesized in human skin via UV photolysis of 7-dehydrocholesterol, and Vitamin K is synthesized by gut microflora. — Endogenous biosynthetic pathways exist either partially via photochemical reactions or microbial symbiosis.

Exception Water-soluble vitamins are readily excreted in urine and do not accumulate to toxic levels.

Observation: Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine) in excessive megadoses can cause irreversible sensory neuropathy. — High concentrations bind to sensory nerve root ganglia causing axonal degeneration.

Exception Vitamins function exclusively as organic coenzymes or antioxidants.

Observation: Active Vitamin D (Calcitriol) acts classically as a steroid hormone regulating gene transcription. — It binds to intracellular nuclear receptors regulating calcium-binding protein synthesis.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Vitamin A exists in three main active forms: retinol, retinal, and retinoic acid.
  • Thiamine pyrophosphate contains a reactive thiazole ring responsible for carbanion generation.
  • Riboflavin contains an isoalloxazine ring system attached to a D-ribitol side chain.
  • Vitamin C serves as a specific reducing agent keeping iron ions in the active Fe2+ state for hydroxylases.
  • Pellagra is specifically linked to a dietary deficiency of Niacin (Vitamin B3) or its precursor tryptophan.
  • Vitamin K dependent carboxylation converts glutamate residues to γ-carboxyglutamate for calcium binding.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the active coenzyme forms: Niacin forms NAD+/NADP+, Riboflavin forms FAD/FMN.
  • Assuming Vitamin C is a coenzyme for transamination instead of collagen proline hydroxylation.
  • Treating all B vitamins as fat-soluble; remember B12 is stored in the liver despite being water-soluble.
  • Forgetting that excessive intake of fat-soluble vitamins leads to toxic bioaccumulation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Functions of Vitamins

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Coenzyme Functions

Water-soluble vitamins act as essential components of coenzymes facilitating group-transfer reactions in cellular metabolism.

Visual Cycle Mechanisms

Vitamin A derivatives undergo photoisomerization driving electrical impulse transmission in retinal rod cells.

Calcium Homeostasis

Vitamin D functions hormonally to enhance intestinal absorption and renal reabsorption of calcium ions.

Antioxidant Defense

Vitamin E scavenges lipid peroxyl radicals protecting cellular membrane integrity from oxidative stress.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Vitamin B1 deficiency causes Beriberi.
  • Vitamin B3 deficiency causes Pellagra.
  • Vitamin C deficiency causes Scurvy.
  • Vitamin D deficiency causes Rickets in children and Osteomalacia in adults.
  • Vitamin K is required for post-translational modification of clotting proteins.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Vitamins are classified into water-soluble and fat-soluble categories.
  • Most water-soluble vitamins function directly as coenzymes in intermediary metabolism.
  • Fat-soluble vitamins regulate specialized physiological functions including vision, bone mineralization, and coagulation.
  • Deficiency in specific micronutrients leads to well-characterized metabolic and clinical disorders.

⚖️ Key Differences

FAD / FMN VS NAD+ / NADP+

FAD/FMN are derived from Vitamin B2 and accept two electrons and two protons, whereas NAD+/NADP+ are derived from Vitamin B3 and accept a hydride ion (two electrons and one proton).

Question 1 of 19

Which vitamin derivative functions as a coenzyme in transamination reactions?

Explanation: Pyridoxal phosphate (PLP), derived from Vitamin B6, is the primary coenzyme for transamination, decarboxylation, and racemization of amino acids.

Question 2 of 19

Night blindness is caused by the deficiency of which vitamin?

Explanation: Vitamin A is essential for the formation of rhodopsin in retinal rod cells; its deficiency impairs dark adaptation leading to night blindness (nyctalopia).

Question 3 of 19

Which vitamin acts as a coenzyme in carboxylation reactions essential for blood clotting?

Explanation: Vitamin K acts as a cofactor for γ-glutamyl carboxylase, which converts glutamate residues in prothrombin and other clotting factors to γ-carboxyglutamate.

Question 4 of 19

Pellagra is caused by the deficiency of:

Explanation: Pellagra is the deficiency disease associated with Niacin (Vitamin B3) or its metabolic precursor amino acid tryptophan.

Question 5 of 19

Which vitamin functions as a potent lipid-soluble antioxidant in cell membranes?

Explanation: α-Tocopherol (Vitamin E) is a major chain-breaking antioxidant that protects polyunsaturated fatty acids from free radical peroxyl attack.

Question 6 of 19

Scurvy is characterized by defective synthesis of which protein due to vitamin C deficiency?

Explanation: Vitamin C is required as a reducing cofactor for prolyl and lysyl hydroxylases, enzymes necessary for stabilizing the triple-helix structure of collagen.

Question 7 of 19

Which of the following B-complex vitamins contains a cobalt atom coordinated in a corrin ring?

Explanation: Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin) features a central cobalt ion coordinated with a substituted corrin macrocycle ring.

Question 8 of 19

Active Vitamin D functions primarily as a:

Explanation: Calcitriol (active Vitamin D3) binds to intracellular nuclear receptors, functioning as a steroid hormone to control calcium and phosphate absorption.

Question 9 of 19

Thiamine pyrophosphate is the active coenzyme form of:

Explanation: Thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP) is derived from Vitamin B1 (thiamine) and is crucial for α-keto acid decarboxylation.

Question 10 of 19

Which water-soluble vitamin is stored in significant amounts in the human liver?

Explanation: Unlike most water-soluble vitamins, Vitamin B12 can be stored in the liver in sufficient quantities to last for several years.

Question 11 of 19

FMN and FAD are active coenzyme derivatives of:

Explanation: Flavin mononucleotide (FMN) and Flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD) are synthesized from Riboflavin and act as redox coenzymes.

Question 12 of 19

Which vitamin deficiency causes beriberi?

Explanation: Beriberi results from a deficiency of Vitamin B1 (thiamine), impairing carbohydrate catabolism and nerve conduction.

Question 13 of 19

NAD+ and NADP+ are coenzymes containing which vitamin component?

Explanation: Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) and its phosphorylated form (NADP+) contain nicotinamide derived from Niacin (Vitamin B3).

Question 14 of 19

Which of the following is NOT a fat-soluble vitamin?

Explanation: Vitamin C is ascorbic acid, which is water-soluble. Vitamins A, D, E, and K are fat-soluble.

Question 15 of 19

Biotin (Vitamin B7) serves as a biochemical carrier for which group?

Explanation: Biotin acts as a covalently bound prosthetic group for carboxylases, serving as a carrier for activated bicarbonate/carboxyl groups.

Question 16 of 19

Rickets in children is caused by a deficiency of:

Explanation: Vitamin D deficiency impairs calcium and phosphorus deposition in growing bones, resulting in rickets.

Question 17 of 19

Which metal ion is kept in its reduced ferrous (Fe2+) state by ascorbic acid during collagen synthesis?

Explanation: Ascorbic acid reduces ferric iron (Fe3+) back to ferrous iron (Fe2+), keeping prolyl and lysyl hydroxylases active.

Question 18 of 19

Coenzyme A (CoA) is structurally synthesized using which vitamin?

Explanation: Pantothenic acid (Vitamin B5) is a core structural component of Coenzyme A, vital for acyl-group transfer in the TCA cycle.

Question 19 of 19

Which vitamin derivative forms rhodopsin when bound to the protein opsin?

Explanation: 11-cis-Retinal is the active aldehyde form of Vitamin A that binds to opsin to form rhodopsin pigment in rod cells.

Question 1 of 10
Which vitamin is synthesized by human skin upon exposure to ultraviolet light?

Solution: 7-Dehydrocholesterol in the skin is photolysed by UV radiation to form cholecalciferol (Vitamin D3).

Question 2 of 10
The structural feature common to all active forms of Vitamin E is:

Solution: Tocopherols and tocotrienols possess a substituted chromanol ring attached to a 16-carbon phytyl side chain.

Question 3 of 10
Which coenzyme is directly involved in transferring single carbon units at the oxidation level of formyl or methenyl?

Solution: Tetrahydrofolate (derived from Vitamin B9 / Folic acid) is the primary carrier of 1-carbon units in various oxidation states.

Question 4 of 10
Excess intake of which vitamin class is most likely to result in toxic hypervitaminosis?

Solution: Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) accumulate in adipose tissue and liver, posing a significant risk of toxicity at high doses.

Question 5 of 10
Which deficiency disease is correctly matched with its vitamin?

Solution: Pellagra is caused by Niacin (Vitamin B3) deficiency. Beriberi is B1, Scurvy is C, and Rickets is D.

Question 6 of 10
What is the primary role of Vitamin K in post-translational protein modification?

Solution: Vitamin K acts as a cofactor for glutamate carboxylation to form γ-carboxyglutamate, essential for calcium binding in blood clotting proteins.

Question 7 of 10
Which vitamin contains a planar isoalloxazine ring?

Solution: Riboflavin contains a heterocyclic isoalloxazine ring linked to ribitol.

Question 8 of 10
In carbohydrate metabolism, pyruvate dehydrogenase complex requires how many distinct vitamin-derived cofactors?

Solution: The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex requires five cofactors: TPP (B1), Lipoamide, CoA (B5), FAD (B2), and NAD+ (B3).

Question 9 of 10
Which vitamin deficiency leads to megaloblastic anemia similar to folate deficiency?

Solution: Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin) deficiency causes megaloblastic anemia by trapping folate in an unusable methyl-tetrahydrofolate form.

Question 10 of 10
Which chemical form of Vitamin A is directly involved in the visual cycle upon photo-excitation?

Solution: 11-cis-Retinal undergoes photoisomerization to all-trans-retinal when struck by light in rod and cone cells.
