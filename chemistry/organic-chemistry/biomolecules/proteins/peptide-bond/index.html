Organic Chemistry

Peptide Bond

A peptide bond is a substituted amide linkage formed by the condensation reaction between the α-carboxyl group of one amino acid and the α-amino group of another. This covalent bond exhibits partial double-bond character due to resonance, imparting rigidity and planarity to the peptide backbone.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Chemical Structure and Resonance

  • The peptide bond (-CO-NH-) is formed via the elimination of a water molecule between an α-amino group and an α-carboxyl group.
  • Resonance structures show that the C-N bond possesses about 40% double-bond character, preventing free rotation around the axis.
  • The carbonyl oxygen carries a partial negative charge while the amide nitrogen carries a partial positive charge, creating a permanent dipole moment.

Stereochemical and Conformational Properties

  • The six atoms comprising the peptide group (-CO-NH-) lie in a single planar configuration with fixed bond angles.
  • The torsional angle ω (omega) for the peptide bond is strictly held near 180° (trans conformation) in almost all peptide linkages to minimize steric hindrance.
  • Proline forms a cis or trans peptide bond with nearly equal energy barriers due to its cyclic side chain involving the nitrogen atom.

Hydrolysis and Stability

  • Despite its thermodynamic stability, the peptide bond undergoes slow acid- or base-catalyzed hydrolysis under extreme conditions yielding free amino acids.
  • Biological cleavage requires specific catalysts known as proteases or peptidases which lower the activation energy of hydrolysis.
  • The high kinetic stability of the peptide bond at neutral pH and physiological temperature is essential for maintaining protein structure and genetic integrity.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Peptide bonds exclusively adopt the trans configuration to minimize steric hindrance between adjacent side chains.

Observation: X-Pro peptide bonds (where X is any amino acid followed by proline) — The cyclic structure of proline results in similar steric energy values for both cis and trans forms, allowing approximately 10% of X-Pro bonds to exist in the cis conformation in native proteins.

Exception Amide nitrogens in peptides are weak bases and uncharged at physiological pH.

Observation: N-terminal amino groups — The terminal amino group possesses a lower pKa (approx. 8.0) compared to side-chain amines and exists predominantly in the protonated (-NH3+) form at physiological pH.

Exception Peptide bonds are completely stable in aqueous neutral solutions indefinitely.

Observation: Aspartyl-prolyl peptide bonds — These specific linkages show high lability to dilute acid due to neighboring group participation by the aspartate carboxyl side chain, causing rapid cleavage.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Peptide Bond Count Rule
Number of peptide bonds = n - 1

Application: For any linear peptide containing n amino acid residues, subtract one to find the total number of peptide bonds formed.

Water Molecule Release Mnemonic
Water released = n - 1

Application: Use this relation to quickly calculate the molecular weight of a synthesized peptide: Molecular Weight = Sum of (amino acid residue weights) - 18 × (n - 1).

Backbone Atom Planarity Identifier
Cα - C - O - N - H - Cα

Application: Remember that exactly 6 atoms lie in the exact same plane for every single peptide bond unit along the polypeptide chain.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The peptide bond length is 1.32 Å, which is shorter than a standard C-N single bond (1.49 Å) due to resonance stabilization.
  • All six atoms of the peptide group lie strictly in the same plane, enforcing a rigid structural backbone.
  • The peptide bond dipole moment is approximately 3.5 Debye, oriented with the negative end at the carbonyl oxygen.
  • Proteins and polypeptides are directional polymers possessing an N-terminus and a C-terminus.
  • Proline is the only standard amino acid that can readily form a cis peptide bond inside polypeptide chains.
  • Enzymatic cleavage of peptide bonds involves nucleophilic attack by water or serine hydroxyl groups on the carbonyl carbon.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the number of amino acids with the number of peptide bonds in cyclic versus linear peptides (cyclic peptides have n bonds for n residues).
  • Assuming free rotation is possible around the C-N bond of the peptide linkage (rotation is restricted by the partial double bond).
  • Forgetting that the C-terminus and N-terminus contribute charged ionic groups that change net peptide charge across pH titration curves.
  • Mistaking the torsional angle ω for φ or ψ (ω governs the peptide bond itself, while φ and ψ govern single bonds to the α-carbon).

⚡ Quick Revision: Peptide Bond

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Condensation and Formation

Peptide bonds form via elimination of water between carboxyl and amino groups, producing an amide linkage with directional properties from N- to C-terminus.

Resonance and Geometry

Partial double-bond character restricts rotation, forcing the six atoms of the peptide group into a rigid planar geometry predominantly in the trans conformation.

Stability and Reactivity

High activation energy prevents spontaneous hydrolysis in neutral water, requiring specific enzymes or harsh conditions to cleave the amide backbone.

Conformational Angles

Backbone flexibility is entirely dictated by rotation around the single bonds flanking the α-carbon, quantified by Ramachandran angles φ and ψ.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Peptide bond length = 1.32 Å
  • Number of peptide bonds in linear peptide = n - 1
  • Peptide group is strictly planar with 6 atoms
  • Trans conformation is thermodynamically favored over cis
  • Partial double bond character prevents free rotation

📌 Quick Recall

  • Formed by dehydration synthesis between amino acids.
  • Exhibits resonance giving it partial double bond character.
  • Rigid and planar structure restricts protein backbone conformations.
  • Stable under physiological conditions but hydrolyzes via acid/base catalysis or enzymes.

⚖️ Key Differences

Peptide Bond VS Glycosidic Bond

Peptide bonds link amino acids via amide groups, whereas glycosidic bonds link monosaccharides via ether/acetal linkages.

Question 1 of 18

What is the primary reason for the restricted rotation around the peptide C-N bond?

Explanation: The lone pair on the amide nitrogen delocalizes into the carbonyl carbon-oxygen π-system, imparting partial double-bond character and restricting rotation.

Question 2 of 18

How many peptide bonds are present in a linear pentapeptide?

Explanation: A linear peptide with n amino acid residues contains n - 1 peptide bonds. For a pentapeptide (n = 5), the number of bonds is 5 - 1 = 4.

Question 3 of 18

Which dihedral angle defines rotation around the peptide bond itself (-CO-NH-)?

Explanation: The torsional angle ω specifically describes the peptide bond rotation, while φ and ψ describe rotations around the N-Cα and Cα-C bonds.

Question 4 of 18

What is the predominant geometrical configuration of peptide bonds in proteins?

Explanation: To minimize steric clashes between adjacent α-carbon side chains, virtually all peptide bonds in proteins adopt the trans configuration (ω = 180°).

Question 5 of 18

Which amino acid is most commonly found in the cis conformation of a peptide bond?

Explanation: Proline's cyclic side chain reduces the energy difference between cis and trans forms, allowing a significant fraction of X-Pro bonds to be cis.

Question 6 of 18

How many atoms lie in the same plane for a single peptide bond group?

Explanation: The six atoms—Cα, C, O, N, H, and Cα—lie strictly in a single plane due to the rigidity of the peptide bond.

Question 7 of 18

What type of chemical reaction forms a peptide bond?

Explanation: A peptide bond forms by the elimination of a water molecule (dehydration condensation) between an amine and a carboxylic acid.

Question 8 of 18

What is the approximate length of a peptide C-N bond compared to a standard C-N single bond?

Explanation: Due to partial double-bond character, the peptide C-N bond is shorter (1.32 Å) than a standard single bond (1.49 Å).

Question 9 of 18

Which functional group is generated at one end of a peptide chain during formation?

Explanation: A polypeptide chain possesses a free amino group at the N-terminus and a free carboxyl group at the C-terminus.

Question 10 of 18

Why are peptide bonds kinetically stable under physiological conditions?

Explanation: The uncatalyzed hydrolysis of peptide bonds has a very high activation energy, making them extremely stable at neutral pH in the absence of enzymes.

Question 11 of 18

What is the direction convention used when writing peptide and protein sequences?

Explanation: By universal biochemical convention, peptide sequences are always written and read from the N-terminal residue to the C-terminal residue.

Question 12 of 18

Which of the following bonds is NOT part of the protein backbone?

Explanation: The Cα-Cβ bond leads into the amino acid side chain, whereas the other three bonds form the repeating polypeptide backbone.

Question 13 of 18

What causes the dipole moment of a peptide bond?

Explanation: The electronegative oxygen pulls electron density away from nitrogen, creating a permanent dipole moment aligned with the bond axis.

Question 14 of 18

Which enzyme class is responsible for the biological cleavage of peptide bonds?

Explanation: Proteases (or peptidases) catalyze the hydrolysis of peptide bonds in biological systems.

Question 15 of 18

What is the approximate value of the torsional angle ω for a trans peptide bond?

Explanation: The trans conformation corresponds to an omega (ω) dihedral angle of approximately 180 degrees.

Question 16 of 18

In a cyclic hexapeptide, how many peptide bonds are present?

Explanation: In a cyclic peptide, the chain loops back on itself, meaning the number of peptide bonds equals the number of amino acid residues (6 bonds for 6 residues).

Question 17 of 18

Which resonance contributor gives the peptide bond its rigidity?

Explanation: The dipolar resonance contributor has a double bond between carbon and nitrogen (-C=N+-), restricting rotation.

Question 18 of 18

What is released when two amino acids condense to form a dipeptide?

Explanation: The condensation reaction between an amino group and a carboxyl group eliminates one molecule of water (H2O).
