α-Amino Acids

α-Amino acids are organic bifunctional compounds containing both a basic amino group and an acidic carboxyl group attached to the same carbon atom. They serve as the fundamental monomeric building blocks of peptides and proteins.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Stereochemistry and Configuration

  • All naturally occurring α-amino acids except glycine contain at least one chiral center and exhibit optical activity.
  • Almost all proteinogenic amino acids possess the L-configuration based on the Fischer projection correlation with L-glyceraldehyde.
  • Amino acids exist predominantly as zwitterions (dipolar ions) in aqueous solutions across a specific pH range.
  • Glycine is the only proteinogenic amino acid lacking optical isomerism due to a hydrogen atom serving as its R-group.

Acid-Base Properties and Isoelectric Point

  • In strongly acidic media, amino acids exist as cations and migrate toward the cathode during electrolysis.
  • In strongly alkaline media, amino acids exist as anions and migrate toward the anode.
  • The isoelectric point (pI) is the specific pH at which the net electrical charge of the amino acid is zero and migration in an electric field ceases.
  • Amino acids with acidic side chains (e.g., aspartic acid) possess lower pI values, while basic side chains (e.g., lysine) yield higher pI values.

Characteristic Chemical Reactions

  • Reaction with ninhydrin yields a characteristic purple-blue complex (Ruhemann's purple), utilized for quantitative and qualitative colorimetric detection.
  • Treatment with nitrous acid (HNO2) converts the primary amino group to a hydroxyl group with the evolution of nitrogen gas (Van Slyke method).
  • Esterification of the carboxyl group with alcohols in the presence of dry HCl gas produces amino acid esters.
  • Peptide bond formation proceeds via condensation between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of another with the elimination of water.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception All α-amino acids are optically active.

Observation: Glycine — Glycine has two identical hydrogen atoms attached to the α-carbon, making it achiral.

Exception All proteinogenic α-amino acids have primary amino groups (-NH2).

Observation: Proline — Proline contains a secondary cyclic imino group incorporated into a five-membered pyrrolidine ring.

Exception All proteinogenic amino acids possess L-configuration.

Observation: D-amino acids — Found occasionally in bacterial cell walls and peptide antibiotics, distinct from mammalian proteins.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Glycine is optically inactive because the α-carbon is bonded to two identical hydrogen atoms.
  • Proteins yield exclusively L-amino acids upon complete acid or enzymatic hydrolysis.
  • The zwitterionic form gives amino acids high melting points and high water solubility due to strong electrostatic interactions.
  • Ninhydrin test detects primary amines down to microgram quantities, producing a purple color.
  • Sanger's reagent (1-fluoro-2,4-dinitrobenzene) is used for N-terminal amino acid analysis in peptides.
  • Isoelectric precipitation is widely used for isolating and purifying specific amino acids and proteins.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming all amino acids rotate plane-polarized light in the dextrorotatory direction.
  • Confusing the D/L sugar/amino acid descriptor system with the optical rotation (+/-) or d/l configuration system.
  • Forgetting that proline contains a secondary amine rather than a primary amine.
  • Calculating the isoelectric point of acidic or basic amino acids using only pKa1 and pKa2.

⚡ Quick Revision: α-Amino Acids

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Classification and Structure

Amino acids are classified by their side chain R-group polarity, acid-base properties, and metabolic fate into essential and non-essential categories.

Zwitterionic Character

Internal proton transfer between the carboxyl and amino groups creates a dipolar ion with amphoteric properties functioning as both acid and base.

Isoelectric Point

The characteristic pH where ion migration ceases depends directly on the dissociation constants of the ionizable functional groups present.

Peptide Linkage

Polymerization occurs via amide bond formation between the carboxyl carbon of one unit and the amino nitrogen of the adjacent unit.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Zwitterion (Dipolar Ion)
A neutral molecule with positive and negative electrical charges at different locations.
Isoelectric Point (pI)
The pH at which net charge is zero.
Essential Amino Acid (Dietary requirement)
Amino acids that cannot be synthesized de novo by the human organism.
Ninhydrin (Triketohydrindene hydrate)
Reagent for sensitive amino acid detection.
Peptide Bond (−CO−NH−)
Amide linkage joining successive amino acid residues.
Chiral Center (Asymmetric carbon)
Carbon atom bonded to four different chemical groups.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Glycine is achiral.
  • Proline contains a secondary amine.
  • All proteinogenic amino acids are L-isomers.
  • Ninhydrin gives a purple color with primary amines.
  • Zwitterions exist at the isoelectric point.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Amino acids are bifunctional building blocks of proteins.
  • Amphoteric nature allows buffering action in biological systems.
  • Optical activity is a key feature of all amino acids except glycine.
  • Specific color reactions like the ninhydrin test permit analytical identification.

⚖️ Key Differences

D/L Configuration VS d/l Optical Rotation

D/L refers to absolute configuration relative to glyceraldehyde; d/l (+/-) refers to direction of rotation of plane-polarized light.

Which of the following amino acids is optically inactive?

Question 2 of 19

The zwitterionic form of an amino acid possesses:

Explanation: In a zwitterion, the protonated amino group (+1) and deprotonated carboxyl group (-1) balance out, resulting in a net zero charge.

Which amino acid contains a secondary amino group?

Explanation: Proline has its nitrogen atom incorporated into a five-membered pyrrolidine ring, forming a secondary amine.

Question 4 of 19

What color is produced when amino acids react with ninhydrin?

Explanation: Reaction of primary amino acids with ninhydrin yields Ruhemann's purple-blue colored complex.

Question 5 of 19

The configuration of naturally occurring proteinogenic amino acids is predominantly:

Explanation: Nearly all amino acids derived from proteins possess the L-configuration.

Question 6 of 19

Which of the following is an acidic amino acid?

Explanation: Aspartic acid contains a side chain with an extra carboxyl group, rendering it acidic.

Question 7 of 19

At pH values significantly lower than its isoelectric point, an amino acid exists primarily as a:

Explanation: In acidic media (low pH), protons bind to the carboxylate group, converting the zwitterion into a net positively charged cation.

Question 8 of 19

Which reagent is commonly used to identify N-terminal residues of peptides via Edman degradation?

Explanation: Phenyl isothiocyanate (Edman's reagent) reacts with the N-terminal amino group to form a thiazolinone derivative.

Question 9 of 19

Explanation: The pI is the arithmetic mean of the two pKa values associated with the zwitterionic equilibrium.

Question 10 of 19

Explanation: Cysteine features a reactive side chain containing a thiol (-SH) functional group.

Question 11 of 19

Explanation: Peptide bonds (-CO-NH-) link successive amino acid units in proteins.

Question 12 of 19

Which of the following is classified as an essential amino acid for humans?

Explanation: Valine cannot be synthesized by the human body and must be obtained from the diet.

Question 14 of 19

Proline reacts with ninhydrin to give a:

Explanation: Proline is a secondary amine and forms a yellow adduct with ninhydrin.

Question 15 of 19

Which amino acid possesses an aromatic ring?

Explanation: Phenylalanine contains a benzyl side chain, classifying it as an aromatic amino acid.

Question 16 of 19

The amphoteric nature of amino acids is due to the presence of:

Explanation: The simultaneous presence of a carboxyl group and an amino group confers amphoteric behavior.

Question 17 of 19

What happens to an amino acid at its isoelectric point during electrophoresis?

Explanation: At the pI, the net charge is zero, so the amino acid does not migrate toward either electrode.

Question 18 of 19

Which sulfur-containing amino acid does not contain a thiol group?

Explanation: Methionine contains a thioether linkage (-S-CH3) rather than a free thiol group.

Question 19 of 19

Explanation: Threonine contains two chiral centers (at the α-carbon and β-carbon).
