Lipids and Hormones

Lipids are a diverse group of hydrophobic organic compounds composed mainly of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen, functioning as structural components of cell membranes and energy stores. Hormones are biochemical messengers synthesized by endocrine glands that regulate physiological activity and metabolic pathways.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification and Chemical Structure of Lipids

  • Simple lipids are esters of fatty acids with various alcohols, exemplified by fats, oils, and waxes.
  • Compound lipids contain additional functional groups such as phosphoric acid, nitrogen bases, or carbohydrates, forming phospholipids and glycolipids.
  • Derived lipids include hydrolysis products like steroids, terpenes, and prostaglandins that retain hydrophobic characteristics.
  • Fatty acids possess a long hydrocarbon tail and a terminal carboxyl group (-COOH), existing as saturated or unsaturated chains.

Classification and Biosynthesis of Hormones

  • Steroid hormones are synthesized from cholesterol in the adrenal cortex, testes, ovaries, and placenta, containing the cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene core.
  • Amino acid derivatives include thyroid hormones (thyroxine) and catecholamines (epinephrine) derived from tyrosine and phenylalanine.
  • Peptide and protein hormones like insulin and glucagon are encoded by specific genes and undergo post-translational proteolytic cleavage.
  • Eicosanoids such as prostaglandins and leukotrienes are derived from arachidonic acid and act locally as autacoids.

Physiological Reactions and Analytical Constants

  • Hydrogenation converts liquid unsaturated vegetable oils into solid fats by catalytic addition of hydrogen across double bonds.
  • Rancidity involves oxidative cleavage of double bonds in unsaturated fatty acids, yielding volatile aldehydes and carboxylic acids.
  • The saponification number is inversely proportional to the average molecular weight of the constituent fatty acids in a triglyceride.
  • The iodine number measures the degree of unsaturation in fats and oils by quantifying halogen addition across π-bonds.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Most naturally occurring unsaturated fatty acids possess cis-conformation.

Observation: Trans-fatty acids — Industrial hydrogenation or microbial action isomerizes the natural cis double bonds into the more stable trans configuration.

Exception Steroids are strictly classified as non-saponifiable lipids.

Observation: Cholesterol esters — When esterified with long-chain fatty acids, cholesterol can undergo alkaline hydrolysis (saponification) similar to simple triglycerides.

Exception Hormones are transported exclusively through the bloodstream to distant target organs.

Observation: Autocrine and paracrine hormones — Certain eicosanoids and local mediators act on the same cell that secreted them or on immediately adjacent cells via interstitial fluid.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Cholesterol serves as the universal precursor for all steroid hormones, including sex hormones and corticosteroids.
  • The iodine value of tristearin is zero because it lacks carbon-carbon double bonds.
  • Prostaglandins are derived from 20-carbon polyunsaturated essential fatty acids, specifically arachidonic acid.
  • Phosphatidylcholine (lecithin) is a major phospholipid containing choline linked to phosphate.
  • Waxes are high molecular weight esters formed from long-chain fatty acids and long-chain monohydric alcohols.
  • Insulin contains two polypeptide chains linked together by inter-chain disulfide bonds.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the saponification value with the acid value; saponification measures total esters and free acids, while acid value measures only free acids.
  • Assuming all lipids yield glycerol upon alkaline hydrolysis; sphingolipids yield sphingosine instead of glycerol.
  • Believing that cholesterol is exclusively pathological; it is an essential structural component of animal cell membranes and bile acid precursor.
  • Mistaking peptide hormones for steroid hormones regarding cell membrane permeability; peptide hormones bind to extracellular receptors.

⚡ Quick Revision: Lipids and Hormones

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Lipid Classification and Structure

Lipids are hydrophobic biomolecules categorized into simple, compound, and derived types based on structural hydrolysis products and functional groups.

Saponification and Iodine Metrics

Analytical constants like saponification number and iodine number quantify molecular weight distribution and the degree of unsaturation in fats and oils.

Hormonal Classification Pathways

Hormones are categorized into steroid, amino-acid derived, peptide, and eicosanoid classes based on their chemical origin and biosynthetic route.

Steroid Core and Derivatives

The cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene tetracyclic ring framework constitutes the structural foundation for cholesterol, bile acids, and steroid hormones.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Triglycerides (Triacylglycerols)
Esters of glycerol with three fatty acid molecules.
Phospholipids (Phosphoacylglycerols)
Amphipathic lipids containing a phosphate head group.
Steroids (Cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene derivatives)
Lipids characterized by a fused four-ring carbon skeleton.
Iodine Value (g I2 / 100g fat)
Mass of iodine consumed by 100 grams of a chemical substance.
Saponification Value (mg KOH / g fat)
Milligrams of potassium hydroxide required to saponify 1g of fat.
Prostaglandins (Eicosanoids)
Signalling molecules derived from unsaturated 20-carbon fatty acids.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Steroid ring system consists of 17 carbon atoms in 4 fused rings (3 cyclohexane, 1 cyclopentane).
  • Saponification value is inversely related to average molecular mass of fatty acids.
  • Arachidonic acid is a 20-carbon polyunsaturated fatty acid with 4 cis double bonds.
  • Cholesterol formula is C27H46O.
  • Triglycerides function as neutral storage lipids in adipose tissue.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Lipids provide energy storage, structural support, and insulation.
  • Hormones act as chemical messengers regulating metabolic and physiological processes.
  • Unsaturated fatty acid levels dictate melting points and iodine numbers.
  • Endocrine signaling relies on specific receptor binding for steroid and peptide hormones.

⚖️ Key Differences

Simple Lipids VS Compound Lipids

Simple lipids yield only fatty acids and alcohols upon hydrolysis, whereas compound lipids yield additional groups like phosphoric acid or sugars.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following analytical constants indicates the average molecular weight of fatty acids in a fat sample?

Explanation: Saponification value measures the milligrams of KOH required to saponify 1g of fat, which is inversely proportional to the average molecular weight of the fatty acids.

Question 2 of 18

The tetracyclic ring system present in cholesterol and steroid hormones is known as:

Explanation: Steroids possess a core structure consisting of three cyclohexane rings and one cyclopentane ring, collectively called cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene.

Question 3 of 18

Which fatty acid is the biological precursor for the biosynthesis of prostaglandins?

Explanation: Arachidonic acid, a 20-carbon polyunsaturated fatty acid, serves as the direct precursor for eicosanoids including prostaglandins.

Question 4 of 18

Tristearin is subjected to complete hydrogenation. The iodine value of the resulting product will be:

Explanation: Tristearin is a fully saturated triglyceride containing no carbon-carbon double bonds, hence its iodine value is zero.

Question 5 of 18

Which lipid class is primarily responsible for the bilayer structure of cell membranes?

Explanation: Phospholipids are amphipathic molecules containing polar heads and non-polar tails, enabling spontaneous bilayer formation in aqueous media.

Question 6 of 18

What is the total number of carbon atoms present in the standard cholesterol molecule?

Explanation: Cholesterol is a sterol containing 27 carbon atoms arranged in its characteristic tetracyclic ring and side chain.

Question 7 of 18

The conversion of liquid vegetable oils into solid fats by catalytic hydrogenation involves:

Explanation: Catalytic hydrogenation adds hydrogen across unsaturated carbon-carbon double bonds (π-bonds), converting liquid oils into saturated solid fats.

Question 8 of 18

Which hormone is classified as an amino acid derivative rather than a steroid or peptide?

Explanation: Thyroxine is synthesized by iodination and coupling of tyrosine residues in thyroglobulin, making it an amino acid derivative.

Question 9 of 18

The acid value of a fat or oil measures:

Explanation: Acid value represents the milligrams of potassium hydroxide required to neutralize free fatty acids present in 1 gram of the substance.

Question 10 of 18

Waxes are chemical esters composed of:

Explanation: Waxes are simple lipids formed by the esterification of long-chain fatty acids with long-chain monohydric alcohols.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following lipids does NOT contain glycerol as its backbone alcohol?

Explanation: Sphingolipids contain sphingosine, an 18-carbon amino alcohol, instead of glycerol as their structural backbone.

Question 12 of 18

Progesterone is a steroid hormone that contains how many carbon atoms?

Explanation: Progestogens like progesterone are C21 steroids containing a pregnane skeleton.

Question 13 of 18

Rancidity in edible oils stored improperly is primarily caused by:

Explanation: Oxidative rancidity occurs when atmospheric oxygen attacks the double bonds of unsaturated fatty acids, generating foul-smelling aldehydes and acids.

Question 14 of 18

Which steroid hormone possesses an aromatic ring A with a phenolic hydroxyl group?

Explanation: Estrogens such as estradiol are C18 steroids characterized by an aromatic A ring with a phenolic -OH group and no C19 methyl group.

Question 15 of 18

The primary physiological role of triacylglycerols in animals is:

Explanation: Triacylglycerols are highly reduced molecules stored in adipose tissue, serving as the body's primary long-term energy reserve.

Question 16 of 18

Androgens (such as testosterone) are steroid hormones characterized by possessing how many carbon atoms?

Explanation: Androgens are C19 steroids based on the androstane skeleton.

Question 17 of 18

Which reagent is utilized in the determination of the iodine value of fats?

Explanation: Wijs solution containing iodine monochloride (ICl) in glacial acetic acid is standard for halogenation during iodine value titration.

Question 18 of 18

Corticosteroids secreted by the adrenal cortex are synthesized starting from:

Explanation: All steroid hormones, including glucocorticoids and mineralocorticoids, are biosynthesized from cholesterol precursor.

Question 1 of 10
High saponification value of a fat indicates that the fat contains:

Solution: Since saponification value is inversely proportional to molecular weight, a high value signifies lower molecular weight fatty acids with shorter chains.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following vitamins is structurally related to sterols and lipids?

Solution: Vitamin D (calciferol) is a secosteroid derived from cholesterol photolysis, classifying it among lipid-soluble vitamins.

Question 3 of 10
The hormone responsible for lowering blood glucose levels by promoting cellular uptake is:

Solution: Insulin is an anabolic peptide hormone secreted by pancreatic beta cells that lowers blood glucose levels.

Question 4 of 10
Phosphatidylinositol acts as a precursor for second messengers in signal transduction. It is a:

Solution: Phosphatidylinositol is a glycerophospholipid containing a phosphorylated inositol head group.

Question 5 of 10
In the structure of steroids, numbering of carbon atoms begins at which ring?

Solution: IUPAC convention for numbering the steroid cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene nucleus starts at ring A and proceeds sequentially through rings B, C, and D.

Question 6 of 10
Which fatty acid is considered essential in human nutrition?

Solution: Linoleic acid contains multiple unsaturations and cannot be synthesized de novo by humans, making it an essential fatty acid.

Question 7 of 10
What happens to the melting point of a fatty acid chain when the number of cis double bonds increases?

Solution: Cis double bonds introduce kinks in the hydrocarbon chain, preventing tight packing and lowering the melting point.

Question 8 of 10
Sphingomyelin differs from glycerophospholipids because it contains:

Solution: Sphingolipids like sphingomyelin utilize sphingosine as their amino alcohol backbone rather than glycerol.

Question 9 of 10
Which category of hormones binds to intracellular receptors and alters gene transcription directly?

Solution: Lipophilic steroid hormones diffuse freely across plasma membranes to bind intracellular receptors and regulate gene expression.

Question 10 of 10
The Reichert-Meissl number of fat measures:

Solution: Reichert-Meissl number quantifies the number of milligrams of 0.1M KOH required to neutralize water-soluble volatile fatty acids in 5g of fat.
