Classification of Steroid Hormones

Steroid hormones are tetracyclic lipids possessing the characteristic cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene ring system. They are classified based on their physiological roles and the number of carbon atoms into groups such as glucocorticoids, mineralocorticoids, sex hormones, and secosteroids.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structural Framework and Carbon Numbering

  • The steroid nucleus consists of three fused cyclohexane rings (A, B, C) and one cyclopentane ring (D).
  • Carbon atoms are numbered sequentially from 1 to 27 following a standardized IUPAC numbering pathway starting in ring A.
  • Stereochemistry at ring junctions (cis/trans) dictates overall 3D shape, with the 5α- and 5β-series differing at C-5.
  • Methyl groups are typically located at C-10 (angular C-19) and C-13 (angular C-18).

Classification by Carbon Count and Function

  • Estrogens (C18) lack the C-19 angular methyl group and possess a fully aromatic A-ring with a phenolic hydroxyl group at C-3.
  • Androgens (C19) retain the C-19 methyl group at C-10 and typically feature keto or hydroxyl functionalities at C-3 and C-17.
  • Progestogens and Corticosteroids (C21) contain a two-carbon side chain attached at the C-17 position of the D-ring.
  • Vitamin D (Secosteroids) are formed by the photolytic cleavage of the B-ring of sterols, breaking the 9,10 carbon-carbon bond.

Corticosteroid Subclasses and Functional Groups

  • Glucocorticoids (e.g., cortisol) regulate carbohydrate metabolism and possess an α-ketol side chain at C-17 and oxygenation at C-11.
  • Mineralocorticoids (e.g., aldosterone) control electrolyte balance and feature an aldehyde group at the C-18 angular position.
  • Functional alterations on the core rings modify receptor binding affinity, shifting activity from mineralocorticoid to glucocorticoid.
  • Conjugation reactions (glucuronidation or sulfation) at polar hydroxyl groups enhance water solubility for renal excretion.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception All steroid hormones retain the standard C-19 angular methyl group at C-10.

Observation: Estrogens (C18 series) — Metabolic aromatization of ring A involves the enzymatic loss of the C-19 angular methyl group and subsequent aromatization of the six-membered ring.

Exception Steroids possess a fully saturated, alicyclic tetracyclic carbon skeleton.

Observation: Estrogens and Vitamin D derivatives — Estrogens contain an aromatic benzene-like A ring, while secosteroids (Vitamin D) feature a broken B-ring system.

Exception Naturally occurring cholesterol and sterols have a 27-carbon skeleton.

Observation: Bile acids and certain plant sterols — Oxidative cleavage of the side chain yields C-24 bile acids (e.g., cholic acid), while phytosterols contain extra alkyl branching (C-28 or C-29).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The fundamental parent ring system of all steroids is cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene, also known as sterane.
  • Estrogens are C18 steroids characterized by an aromatic A-ring and the absence of the C-19 angular methyl group.
  • Androgens are C19 steroids possessing both C-10 and C-13 angular methyl groups.
  • Progestogens and corticosteroids contain 21 carbon atoms with a characteristic two-carbon side chain at C-17.
  • Aldosterone possesses a unique aldehyde group at the C-18 position which forms a hemiacetal equilibrium structure.
  • Vitamin D is classified as a secosteroid because one of the rings (Ring B) is cleaved photochemically.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing C18 (estrogens) and C19 (androgens) carbon counts; remember estrogens lose one carbon during A-ring aromatization.
  • Assuming all rings in a steroid are saturated; estrogens have an aromatic A-ring.
  • Forgetting that corticosteroids possess a 2-carbon side chain attached to C-17, unlike androgens which only have a keto or hydroxyl group at C-17.
  • Misidentifying secosteroids as non-steroids simply because a ring bond is broken.

⚡ Quick Revision: Classification of Steroid Hormones

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Steroid Ring Framework

The core structural unit is the 17-carbon cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene system, comprising three six-membered rings and one five-membered ring.

Sex Hormones Classification

Divided into C18 estrogens (aromatic A-ring) and C19 androgens (testosterone derivatives retaining the C-19 methyl group).

Adrenocortical Hormones

C21 steroids encompassing glucocorticoids (sugar metabolism) and mineralocorticoids (salt balance), characterized by a C-17 side chain.

Secosteroids and Derivatives

Molecules such as calciferol (Vitamin D) derived from sterols by ring-opening reactions while maintaining the fundamental steroid heritage.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Sterane (Cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene)
The parent saturated tetracyclic hydrocarbon ring system of all steroids, C17H28.
Estrane (C18H30)
Parent hydrocarbon of estrogens.
Androstane (C19H32)
Parent hydrocarbon of androgens.
Pregnane (C21H36)
Parent hydrocarbon of progestins and corticosteroids.
Secosteroid (Ring-cleaved steroid)
Steroid derivative in which one of the ring bonds has been cleaved (e.g., Vitamin D).
Corticosteroid (C21 hormone)
Hormone secreted by the adrenal cortex regulating metabolism and electrolytes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Estrogens = C18, Aromatic A-ring, No C-19 methyl
  • Androgens = C19, C-10 and C-13 methyl groups present
  • Corticosteroids = C21, C-17 side chain present
  • Cholesterol = C27 sterol precursor
  • Parent ring system = Cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene

📌 Quick Recall

  • Steroid hormones are classified by carbon count and physiological function.
  • Aromatization of Ring A defines the estrogen class.
  • C-17 side chain modifications separate C21 corticosteroids from C19 androgens.
  • Stereochemistry at ring junctions creates diverse receptor-specific biological activities.

⚖️ Key Differences

Androstane (C19) VS Estrane (C18)

Androstane retains the C-10 angular methyl group, whereas estrane lacks it due to aromatic ring formation.

Which angular methyl group is missing in estrane derivatives compared to androstane derivatives?

Explanation: The C-19 angular methyl group attached to C-10 is lost during the biosynthetic aromatization of ring A in estrogens.

Question 7 of 19

Aldosterone is unique among corticosteroids because it contains which functional group at C-18?

Explanation: Aldosterone features an aldehyde group at the C-18 angular position, which can form a cyclic hemiacetal with the C-11 hydroxyl group.

Question 8 of 19

Cholesterol, the precursor to steroid hormones, contains how many carbon atoms?

Explanation: Cholesterol is a 27-carbon sterol (cholestane derivative) containing a branched aliphatic side chain at C-17.

Question 9 of 19

What is the parent hydrocarbon name for progesterone and cortisol?

Explanation: Pregnane (C21H36) is the parent hydrocarbon for both progestogens and corticosteroid hormones.

Question 10 of 19

Which ring in cholesterol is cleaved to produce Vitamin D3 under UV irradiation?

Explanation: UV photolysis cleaves the 9,10 carbon-carbon bond of Ring B in 7-dehydrocholesterol to form previtamin D3.

Question 11 of 19

Testosterone is classified under which steroid category?

Explanation: Testosterone is a C19 androgen hormone responsible for male secondary sex characteristics.

Question 12 of 19

Cortisol is a primary example of which hormone class?

Explanation: Cortisol is a glucocorticoid synthesized in the adrenal cortex, regulating glucose metabolism and stress response.

Question 13 of 19

The stereochemical descriptor '5α-' implies what orientation of the hydrogen atom at C-5 relative to the angular methyl at C-10?

Explanation: In the 5α-series (allo series), the hydrogen at C-5 is trans to the C-10 methyl group (pointing away, dashed bond).

Question 14 of 19

Bile acids differ from cholesterol primarily in having:

Explanation: Bile acids are C24 sterols formed by the oxidative cleavage of the cholesterol side chain, ending in a carboxylic acid group.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following hormones is a C18 steroid?

Explanation: Estradiol is a primary estrogen and contains 18 carbon atoms.

Question 16 of 19

The IUPAC numbering of the steroidal carbon skeleton proceeds in which general direction?

Explanation: Numbering starts at C-1 in ring A, moves through rings B and C, finishes ring D, and then numbers any side chains.

Question 17 of 19

Which functional group is typically found at C-3 in biologically active androgens and progestogens?

Explanation: Active androgens and progestogens typically feature a 3-keto-Δ4 structure (conjugated enone system in ring A).

Question 18 of 19

What structural modification distinguishes corticosterone from cortisol?

Explanation: Corticosterone lacks the 11β-hydroxyl group found in cortisol, shifting its potency towards mineralocorticoid activity.

Question 19 of 19

Which class of steroids acts primarily on the distal tubules of the kidney to promote sodium reabsorption?

Explanation: Mineralocorticoids such as aldosterone regulate electrolyte and fluid balance by acting on renal epithelial cells.
