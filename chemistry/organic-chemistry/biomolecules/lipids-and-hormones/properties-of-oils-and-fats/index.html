Properties of oils and fats

Oils and fats are triesters of glycerol with higher fatty acids, structurally classified as triglycerides. They undergo characteristic chemical reactions including saponification, hydrogenation, and halogenation.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Physical Properties and Melting Point Trends

  • Fats are solids at room temperature due to a high proportion of saturated fatty acid residues allowing tight molecular packing.
  • Oils are liquids at room temperature owing to the presence of cis-double bonds that create kinks in the hydrocarbon chain.
  • Melting points increase with increasing chain length and decrease with a higher degree of unsaturation.
  • Pure triglycerides are colorless, odorless, and tasteless; distinct colors and odors in natural lipids are due to dissolved pigments and impurities.

Chemical Reactions of Triglycerides

  • Hydrogenation of liquid vegetable oils converts unsaturated double bonds into saturated single bonds, producing solid fats like vanaspati ghee.
  • Saponification involves alkaline hydrolysis of fats using aqueous NaOH or KOH to yield glycerol and alkali salts of fatty acids (soaps).
  • Rancidity occurs via hydrolytic cleavage yielding volatile foul-smelling short-chain acids or oxidative cleavage of double bonds producing peroxides and aldehydes.
  • Transesterification exchanges alkoxy groups between an ester and an alcohol, widely utilized in biodiesel production from waste cooking oils.

Analytical Constants for Characterization

  • Iodine value measures the degree of unsaturation; higher iodine values indicate a greater number of double bonds per molecule.
  • Saponification value is inversely proportional to the average molecular weight of the fatty acid residues in the fat.
  • Reichert-Meissl (RM) value quantifies water-soluble steam-volatile fatty acids, serving as a key parameter to detect butter adulteration.
  • Acid value determines the amount of free fatty acids present, serving as an indicator of lipid degradation and age.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Saponification Value (SV)
SV = (56.1 × (b - a) × N) / W
Conditions: b = mL of HCl for blank, a = mL of HCl for sample, N = normality, W = weight of fat in g
Units: mg of KOH required to saponify 1 g of fat
Iodine Value (IV)
IV = (12.7 × (b - a) × N) / W
Conditions: Wijs method using iodine monochloride (ICl)
Units: g of I2 absorbed by 100 g of fat
Acid Value
Acid Value = (56.1 × V × N) / W
Conditions: V = mL of KOH, N = normality of KOH, W = weight of fat in g
Units: mg of KOH required to neutralize free fatty acids in 1 g of fat
Saponification Equivalent
SE = (Wt of fat in mg) / (Moles of KOH consumed)
Conditions: Equivalent weight of the fat or mean molecular weight of fatty acids divided by 3
Units: g/equiv
Triglyceride Structure
C3H5(OOCR)3 + 3H2O → C3H5(OH)3 + 3RCOOH
Conditions: Acid or base catalyzed hydrolysis
Units: R represents long-chain alkyl or alkenyl groups
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Unsaturated fats are liquids at room temperature with low melting points.

Observation: Trans-fatty acids and high-molecular-weight trans-unsaturated triglycerides remain solid. — Trans-configuration permits linear chain alignment and stronger intermolecular Van der Waals forces compared to cis-isomers.

Exception Higher molecular weight triglycerides have higher saponification values.

Observation: Saponification value is inversely proportional to molecular weight. — Larger molecular weight means fewer moles of fat per gram, hence fewer ester linkages cleaved per gram of sample.

Exception Fats and oils are completely insoluble in water.

Observation: Short-chain triglycerides and lower fatty acid salts (soaps) show partial or colloidal solubility. — Presence of shorter hydrophobic tails and hydrophilic carboxylate groups increases polarity.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Iodine Value Rule
IV ∝ Number of double bonds per molecule

Application: Quickly rank oils by unsaturation: drying oils (IV > 130) > non-drying oils (IV < 100) > fats (IV < 70).

Saponification Value Rule
SV ∝ 1 / (Molecular Weight of Fat)

Application: Coconut oil and butter have low molecular weight fatty acids (lauric, butyric), hence unusually high saponification values.

Rancidity Identification Mnemonic
O-A-H (Oxidation → Aldehydes/Peroxides; Hydrolysis → Acids)

Application: Differentiate oxidative rancidity (double bond attack by O2) from hydrolytic rancidity (ester cleavage by moisture/lipase).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Triglycerides are triesters formed by the condensation of one glycerol molecule with three fatty acid molecules.
  • The catalytic hydrogenation of oils uses nickel (Ni) catalyst at elevated temperature and pressure.
  • An iodine value greater than 130 classifies a vegetable oil as a drying oil used in paints and varnishes.
  • Butter has a high Reichert-Meissl value due to the presence of water-soluble volatile butyric acid.
  • Hard soaps are sodium salts of long-chain fatty acids, while soft soaps are potassium salts.
  • Oxidative rancidity involves free radical chain reactions at allylic methylene carbons adjacent to double bonds.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing iodine value with acid value; iodine value measures double bonds, while acid value measures free carboxylic acids.
  • Assuming higher molecular weight fats have higher saponification values; the relationship is strictly inverse.
  • Assuming all oils become solid upon hydrogenation; partial hydrogenation yields semi-solid margarines and shortenings.
  • Forgetting that natural fats are heterogeneous mixtures of mixed triglycerides rather than single pure compounds.

⚡ Quick Revision: Properties of oils and fats

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Triglyceride Composition

Natural lipids consist of mixed triglycerides containing diverse saturated and unsaturated fatty acid chains esterified to glycerol.

Saponification and Soaps

Base hydrolysis yields glycerol and carboxylate salts possessing surfactant properties critical for cleansing action.

Analytical Constants

Iodine, saponification, and acid values provide definitive quantitative metrics for structural characterization and purity.

Rancidity Mechanisms

Lipid degradation occurs via microbial or chemical hydrolysis and atmospheric oxidation, altering taste, odor, and safety.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Triglyceride (C3H5(OOCR)3)
Triester of glycerol and fatty acids serving as the primary constituent of fats and oils.
Iodine Value (IV)
Grams of iodine absorbed by 100 g of fat, measuring unsaturation degree.
Saponification Value (SV)
Milligrams of KOH required to saponify 1 g of fat.
Acid Value (AV)
Milligrams of KOH needed to neutralize free fatty acids in 1 g of fat.
Rancidification (Oxidative/Hydrolytic Cleavage)
Spoilage process producing foul-smelling aldehydes, ketones, and short-chain acids.
Reichert-Meissl Value (RM Value)
Milligrams of 0.1 N KOH required to neutralize volatile water-soluble fatty acids from 5 g of fat.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Iodine value > 130 = Drying oil
  • Saponification value ∝ 1 / Molecular weight
  • Hydrogenation converts liquid oils to solid fats using Ni catalyst
  • Rancidity involves oxidation at allylic positions and hydrolysis of ester linkages
  • Butter shows high Reichert-Meissl value due to butyric acid

📌 Quick Recall

  • Fats and oils are lipid esters of glycerol and fatty acids.
  • Unsaturation dictates melting point, physical state, and iodine value.
  • Chemical constants (SV, IV, AV) characterize lipid purity and composition.
  • Hydrogenation and saponification are key industrial transformations.

⚖️ Key Differences

Iodine Value VS Acid Value

Iodine value assesses carbon-carbon double bonds, whereas acid value measures free unesterified carboxylic acid groups.

Explanation: Rancidity occurs via oxidation of double bonds by atmospheric oxygen and hydrolytic cleavage of ester bonds.

Question 9 of 18

Cis-double bonds in natural unsaturated fatty acids cause:

Explanation: Cis-configuration introduces structural kinks preventing tight crystal packing, resulting in lower melting points (liquid state).

Question 10 of 18

Soaps are chemically defined as:

Explanation: Soaps are alkali metal salts of long-chain monocarboxylic fatty acids produced by saponification.

Question 11 of 18

Which reagent is used in Wijs method for determining the Iodine Value?

Explanation: Wijs solution consists of iodine monochloride in glacial acetic acid, used to halogenate double bonds.

Question 12 of 18

What happens to the iodine value of an oil during hydrogenation?

Explanation: Hydrogenation saturates double bonds, thereby consuming fewer halogen equivalents and decreasing the iodine value.

Question 13 of 18

Castor oil is unique because it contains a high percentage of:

Explanation: Castor oil is rich in ricinoleic acid, a hydroxy fatty acid that imparts unusual viscosity and solubility properties.

Question 14 of 18

A high acid value in a commercial oil sample indicates:

Explanation: An elevated acid value indicates accumulation of free fatty acids due to hydrolytic rancidity or improper storage.

Question 15 of 18

Which lipid class serves as the most concentrated form of metabolic energy storage in animals?

Explanation: Triglycerides are highly reduced and anhydrous, yielding over twice the energy per gram compared to carbohydrates.

Question 16 of 18

The saponification equivalent of a pure triglyceride is equal to:

Explanation: Since each triglyceride molecule contains three ester linkages, one equivalent weight corresponds to one-third of the molecular weight.

Question 17 of 18

Which of the following oils is classified as a non-drying oil?

Explanation: Olive oil has a low iodine value (below 100) and does not form a hard film upon exposure to air, making it non-drying.

Question 18 of 18

Hydrolytic rancidity is accelerated by the enzyme:

Explanation: Lipase enzymes catalyze the hydrolysis of ester bonds in triglycerides, releasing free fatty acids and glycerol.

Question 2 of 10
Which parameter indicates the presence of lower volatile fatty acids in butter?

Solution: Reichert-Meissl (RM) value specifically measures steam-volatile, water-soluble fatty acids like butyric and caproic acids.

Question 3 of 10
Why do oils have lower melting points than fats?

Solution: Cis-double bonds create angular bends in hydrocarbon chains, disrupting crystal lattice formation and lowering melting points.

Question 4 of 10
In the determination of Iodine Value, excess iodine reacts with potassium iodide to form:

Solution: Iodine combines with excess KI to form soluble triiodide ions (I3-) in solution.

Question 5 of 10
Which fatty acid possesses one double bond at C-9 and C-10 position?

Solution: Oleic acid is a monounsaturated C18 fatty acid with a cis-double bond between carbons 9 and 10.

Question 6 of 10
What is the primary byproduct formed during the saponification of fats?

Solution: Saponification of triglycerides yields glycerol (propane-1,2,3-triol) alongside soap molecules.

Question 7 of 10
An oil sample shows an acid value of 10. This signifies that:

Solution: Acid value is defined numerically as milligrams of KOH required to neutralize free fatty acids present in 1 gram of fat.

Question 8 of 10
Which class of lipids yields soaps and glycerol upon alkaline hydrolysis?

Solution: Triglycerides (fats and oils) are saponifiable lipids that undergo alkaline hydrolysis to produce soaps and glycerol.

Question 9 of 10
During the hardening of oils, which catalyst is typically employed?

Solution: Finely divided nickel (Ni) is universally used as a heterogeneous catalyst for industrial hydrogenation of oils.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following statements regarding drying oils is correct?

Solution: Drying oils possess high polyunsaturation, allowing them to undergo oxidative polymerization into durable protective films.
