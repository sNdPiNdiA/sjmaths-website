Organic Chemistry

Lipids

Lipids are a diverse group of naturally occurring organic compounds characterized by their solubility in non-polar organic solvents and insolubility in water. They function primarily as structural components of cell membranes, energy storage reserves, and signaling molecules.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification and Structure of Fatty Acids

  • Fatty acids are monocarboxylic acids with long aliphatic hydrocarbon chains, usually containing an even number of carbon atoms from 14 to 22.
  • Saturated fatty acids contain no double bonds (e.g., palmitic acid C15H31COOH, stearic acid C17H35COOH) and pack tightly via London dispersion forces.
  • Unsaturated fatty acids contain one or more double bonds (e.g., oleic acid, linoleic acid) almost exclusively in the cis-configuration, introducing kinks that lower melting points.
  • Essential fatty acids such as linoleic acid and linolenic acid cannot be synthesized de novo by mammals and must be obtained from diet.

Simple and Complex Lipids

  • Triglycerides (Triacylglycerols) are triesters of glycerol and three fatty acid molecules, serving as the major form of energy storage in adipose tissue.
  • Waxes are simple lipids formed by the esterification of long-chain fatty acids with long-chain monohydric alcohols.
  • Phospholipids contain a glycerol backbone, two fatty acids, a phosphate group, and an amino alcohol (e.g., choline, ethanolamine), forming bilayers in cell membranes.
  • Sphingolipids contain a sphingosine amino-alcohol backbone instead of glycerol; an example is sphingomyelin, abundant in the myelin sheath of nerve fibers.

Derivatives, Steroids, and Lipid Analysis

  • Steroids are nonsaponifiable lipids characterized by the tetracyclic cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene ring system containing 17 carbon atoms.
  • Cholesterol is the most common animal sterol, serving as a precursor for bile acids, steroid hormones, and vitamin D synthesis.
  • The iodine value determines the degree of unsaturation; higher iodine values indicate a greater number of double bonds per molecule.
  • Saponification value is inversely proportional to the average molecular weight of the fatty acids present in the fat or oil.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Most naturally occurring unsaturated fatty acids possess cis double bonds.

Observation: Trans fatty acids possess trans double bonds. — Produced via industrial hydrogenation or naturally in ruminants, resulting in higher melting points similar to saturated fats.

Exception Lipids are strictly non-polar and hydrophobic.

Observation: Phospholipids and glycolipids are amphipathic. — Presence of polar head groups (phosphate/sugars) and non-polar tails (fatty acids) allows formation of micelles and bilayers.

Exception Saturated fats are solids at room temperature due to linear tight packing.

Observation: Short-chain saturated fatty acids (e.g., butyric acid) are liquids. — Low molecular mass outweighs the packing effect, resulting in lower boiling and melting points.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Cholesterol contains 27 carbon atoms and a characteristic 3β-hydroxy group on ring A.
  • Prostaglandins are derivatives of 20-carbon eicosanoic polyunsaturated fatty acids like arachidonic acid.
  • Rancidity of fats is caused by oxidative cleavage of double bonds forming volatile aldehydes and carboxylic acids.
  • Waxes are completely insoluble in water and serve as protective coatings on leaves, feathers, and fruits.
  • Lecithin (phosphatidylcholine) is a zwitterionic phospholipid found extensively in cellular membranes.
  • Terpenes are derived from 5-carbon isoprene units (2-methyl-1,3-butadiene) and classified by number of units.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing saponification value with acid value; saponification measures total esters and free acids, whereas acid value measures only free acids.
  • Assuming all fatty acids are synthesized by the human body; essential fatty acids must be supplied via diet.
  • Believing steroids contain ester bonds like true lipids; steroids are tetracyclic alcohols or ketones.
  • Forgetting that natural unsaturated fatty acids have cis geometry which prevents tight crystallization.

⚡ Quick Revision: Lipids

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Fatty Acid Composition

Fatty acids are aliphatic monocarboxylic acids divided into saturated and unsaturated classes, dictating melting behavior and nutritional properties.

Complex Membrane Lipids

Phospholipids and sphingolipids form lipid bilayers due to their amphipathic nature, critical for compartmentalization in biological systems.

Steroids and Terpenes

Non-saponifiable lipids including cholesterol, steroid hormones, and fat-soluble vitamins execute regulatory and structural tasks.

Analytical Constants

Parameters like iodine number, saponification value, and acid number quantitatively characterize purity, unsaturation, and molecular weight.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Triglyceride (Triacylglycerol)
Ester of glycerol and three fatty acid chains.
Phospholipid (Glycerophospholipid)
Lipid containing a phosphate group and polar head.
Sphingolipid (Ceramide derivative)
Lipid possessing a sphingosine backbone instead of glycerol.
Sterol (Steroid alcohol)
Steroid containing a hydroxyl group at C3.
Iodine Number (IN)
Grams of I2 absorbed by 100g of fat.
Saponification Value (SV)
Milligrams of KOH required to hydrolyze 1g of fat.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Cholesterol core consists of 17 carbons in a 4-ring fused system plus side chains.
  • Essential fatty acids: linoleic, linolenic, and arachidonic acids.
  • Iodine value measures unsaturation degree.
  • Saponification value is inversely proportional to fatty acid chain length.
  • Phospholipids are amphipathic molecules forming lipid bilayers.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Lipids serve as energy storage, structural membrane units, and signaling precursors.
  • Classification splits into simple lipids, complex lipids, and derived lipids.
  • Double bonds in natural lipids are predominantly cis-configured.
  • Analytical values provide precise chemical identity for fats and oils.

⚖️ Key Differences

Simple Lipids VS Complex Lipids

Simple lipids yield only fatty acids and alcohols upon hydrolysis; complex lipids yield additional groups like phosphate, sugars, or nitrogen bases.

Which vitamin is synthesized from cholesterol precursors?

Question 16 of 19

Question 17 of 19

Lipids are extracted from biological tissues using:

Explanation: Non-polar organic solvent systems like chloroform and methanol effectively dissolve and extract lipids.

Question 18 of 19

Explanation: Sphingomyelin is a principal sphingolipid enriched in the myelin sheath surrounding neurons.
