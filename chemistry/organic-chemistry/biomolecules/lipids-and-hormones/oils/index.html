Organic Chemistry

Oils

Oils are triesters of glycerol and fatty acids that exist in a liquid state at room temperature due to a high proportion of unsaturated hydrocarbon chains. They undergo characteristic chemical reactions such as hydrogenation, saponification, and rancidification.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Chemical Constitution and Fatty Acid Composition

  • Oils are liquid lipids primarily composed of triglycerides derived from glycerol and predominantly unsaturated fatty acids.
  • Common unsaturated fatty acids found in natural oils include oleic acid (C18H34O2), linoleic acid (C18H32O2), and linolenic acid (C18H30O2).
  • The presence of cis-double bonds introduces kinks in the hydrocarbon chains, preventing tight molecular packing and resulting in lower melting points.
  • Plant-derived oils are rich in polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), whereas animal fats contain predominantly saturated chains.

Characteristic Reactions of Oils

  • Hydrogenation converts liquid oils into semi-solid fats (vegetable ghee) by catalytic addition of hydrogen across carbon-carbon double bonds using a nickel catalyst.
  • Saponification involves base-catalyzed hydrolysis of triglycerides using sodium or potassium hydroxide to yield glycerol and alkali salts of fatty acids (soaps).
  • Rancidification occurs via hydrolytic cleavage or oxidative cleavage of double bonds by atmospheric oxygen, producing volatile foul-smelling aldehydes and carboxylic acids.
  • Halogenation (addition of iodine or Wijs solution) measures the degree of unsaturation, quantified as the iodine number.

Analytical Constants for Oil Characterization

  • Saponification value is inversely proportional to the average molecular weight of the fatty acid residues present in the oil.
  • Iodine value directly measures the degree of unsaturation; higher iodine values indicate a greater number of double bonds per triglyceride molecule.
  • Acid value defines the number of milligrams of KOH required to neutralize the free fatty acids present in 1 g of oil, serving as an indicator of freshness.
  • Reichert-Meissl (RM) value measures the water-soluble volatile fatty acids (butyric and caproic acids) obtained by hydrolysis of 5 g of fat or oil.

Saponification Reaction
C3H5(OOCR)3 + 3NaOH → C3H5(OH)3 + 3RCOONa
Conditions: Reflux with strong aqueous alkali (NaOH or KOH)
Units: Stoichiometric molar ratio 1:3
Iodine Number Calculation
Iodine Value = (126.9 × c × v) / w × 100
Conditions: c = concentration of Na2S2O3, v = volume consumed, w = weight of oil in grams
Units: g I2 absorbed per 100 g of oil
Saponification Value
Saponification Value = (56.1 × (b - a) × M) / W
Conditions: b = blank titre, a = sample titre, M = molarity of HCl, W = weight of oil
Units: mg KOH required to saponify 1 g of oil

CH2(OOCR1)-CH(OOCR2)-CH2(OOCR3)
Conditions: R1, R2, R3 are long-chain alkyl groups containing one or more double bonds
Units: Molecular weight in g/mol
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Oils are liquid at room temperature due to unsaturated fatty acid chains.

Observation: Castor oil is a liquid oil that is rich in a hydroxylated fatty acid (ricinoleic acid). — Presence of the polar -OH group creates intermolecular hydrogen bonding, yet the overall chain geometry and structural bulk still prevent crystallization at room temperature.

Exception Triglycerides are neutral lipids lacking free acidic or basic functional groups.

Observation: Rancid oils exhibit acidic properties. — Oxidative and hydrolytic degradation releases free fatty acids into the matrix, elevating the acid value.

Exception Unsaturated fatty acids in natural plant oils possess cis-configurations.

Observation: Partially hydrogenated oils contain trans-fatty acids. — Catalytic hydrogenation isomerization side-reactions convert natural cis-double bonds into thermodynamically more stable trans-configurations.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Oils are triesters of glycerol and unsaturated long-chain monocarboxylic acids.
  • The catalytic hydrogenation of oils uses finely divided Nickel (Ni) at elevated temperatures (approx 150-200°C).
  • Iodine value is expressed as grams of iodine absorbed by 100 grams of oil.
  • The rancidity of butter and oils involves the formation of peroxides followed by cleavage into low molecular weight aldehydes and acids.
  • Saponification value of butter or coconut oil is exceptionally high due to the presence of short-chain fatty acid residues.
  • Linseed oil is a classic example of a drying oil because it undergoes rapid atmospheric oxidation and polymerization to form a tough film.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing iodine value with acid value; iodine value measures unsaturation, whereas acid value measures free fatty acids.
  • Assuming all plant-derived lipids are saturated; plant oils are rich in unsaturated bonds.
  • Believing that saponification yields free fatty acids instead of alkali carboxylate salts (soaps).
  • Neglecting temperature effects on the physical classification of lipids as oils versus fats.

⚡ Quick Revision: Oils

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Composition

Oils consist of mixed triglycerides containing unsaturated fatty acid residues with cis-double bonds.

Hydrogenation and Hardening

Addition of hydrogen across carbon-carbon double bonds converts liquid oils into solid fats using nickel catalysts.

Analytical Indices

Saponification value, iodine value, and acid value serve as quantitative metrics for purity and structural characterization.

Rancidity

Oxidative and enzymatic breakdown processes generate volatile degradation products responsible for off-flavors and odors.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Oils have high unsaturation and exist as liquids at room temperature.
  • Hydrogenation converts vegetable oils to fats using a Ni catalyst.
  • Iodine value measures the degree of unsaturation in an oil.
  • Saponification value is inversely proportional to molecular weight.
  • Rancidity is caused by oxidation and hydrolysis of ester bonds.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Natural oils are glycerol triesters of unsaturated fatty acids.
  • Key analytical parameters include iodine value, saponification value, and acid value.
  • Chemical modification includes hydrogenation and base hydrolysis.
  • Spoilage occurs through oxidative rancidity pathways.

⚖️ Key Differences

Iodine Value VS Acid Value

Iodine value measures double bonds, while acid value measures unesterified free fatty acids.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following describes the chemical nature of oils?

Explanation: Oils are triesters formed by the condensation of glycerol with three molecules of fatty acids, predominantly unsaturated ones.

Question 2 of 19

What is the catalyst used in the commercial hydrogenation of oils?

Explanation: Finely divided nickel is universally employed as a heterogeneous catalyst for hydrogenating double bonds in vegetable oils.

Question 3 of 19

The iodine value of an oil is a measure of:

Explanation: Iodine value measures the mass of iodine in grams consumed by 100 grams of a chemical substance, directly indicating double bond count.

Question 4 of 19

A high saponification value indicates that the oil contains:

Explanation: Since saponification value is inversely proportional to average molecular weight, smaller fatty acid chains require more KOH per gram of oil.

Question 5 of 19

Which of the following is classified as a drying oil?

Explanation: Linseed oil contains high levels of linolenic and linoleic acids, making it an excellent drying oil due to rapid oxidative polymerization.

Question 6 of 19

What products are formed during the alkaline hydrolysis (saponification) of an oil?

Explanation: Saponification of triglycerides with NaOH or KOH yields glycerol and sodium or potassium salts of fatty acids (soaps).

Question 7 of 19

Oxidative rancidity in oils is primarily caused by:

Explanation: Atmospheric oxygen attacks unsaturated double bonds, forming unstable peroxides that cleave into foul-smelling aldehydes and carboxylic acids.

Question 8 of 19

The presence of cis-double bonds in the fatty acid chains of oils causes:

Explanation: Cis-configurations introduce structural bends that prevent close alignment of molecules, suppressing crystallization.

Question 9 of 19

Acid value of an oil is expressed as:

Explanation: Acid value is specifically defined as the mg of KOH required to neutralize the free fatty acids in 1 gram of the sample.

Question 10 of 19

Which fatty acid possesses three double bonds in its C18 chain?

Explanation: Linolenic acid is an octadecatrienoic acid (C18:3), possessing three non-conjugated double bonds.

Question 11 of 19

Vegetable ghee is manufactured from vegetable oils by:

Explanation: Hydrogenation saturates the double bonds of liquid vegetable oils, turning them into solid fats like vegetable ghee.

Question 12 of 19

Which constant is useful in detecting adulteration with mineral oils?

Explanation: Mineral oils cannot be saponified; hence, adulteration significantly alters (lowers) the saponification value of the oil.

Question 13 of 19

Castor oil is unique among natural plant oils because it contains:

Explanation: Castor oil is rich in ricinoleic acid, which features a hydroxyl group on the 12th carbon of the oleic acid chain.

Question 14 of 19

What happens to the iodine value of an oil upon complete hydrogenation?

Explanation: Hydrogenation eliminates double bonds, meaning no iodine can be absorbed, driving the iodine value close to zero.

Question 15 of 19

Which test or analytical value confirms the presence of water-soluble volatile fatty acids in butter or oil?

Explanation: The Reichert-Meissl value quantifies water-soluble steam-volatile fatty acids, particularly useful for testing butter purity.

Question 16 of 19

In the structure of a triglyceride, the three hydroxyl groups of glycerol are esterified with:

Explanation: Triglycerides are formed by esterifying all three hydroxyl groups of glycerol with long-chain carboxylic (fatty) acids.

Question 17 of 19

Which type of chemical bond is primarily cleaved during the rancidification of oils?

Explanation: Oxidative rancidification specifically attacks the reactive carbon-carbon double bonds of unsaturated fatty acids.

Question 18 of 19

Why are oils liquid while fats are solid at room temperature?

Explanation: Unsaturated chains in oils create kinks that prevent efficient intermolecular packing, resulting in a liquid state.

Question 19 of 19

Which functional group is present in triglycerides?

Explanation: Triglycerides contain ester functional groups (-COO-) linking glycerol and fatty acid moieties.

Question 2 of 10
Which fatty acid contains one double bond at C9-C10?

Solution: Oleic acid is a monounsaturated C18 fatty acid with a cis-double bond between carbon atoms 9 and 10.

Question 3 of 10
During the rancidification of oils, unpleasant odors are primarily caused by the formation of:

Solution: Oxidative cleavage of double bonds breaks down long chains into volatile short-chain aldehydes and acids.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following oils is non-drying?

Solution: Olive oil is a non-drying oil with a low iodine value (<85), containing mostly oleic acid.

Question 5 of 10
What is the primary role of nickel during oil hardening?

Solution: Nickel acts as a heterogeneous catalyst to lower activation energy for the addition of hydrogen across double bonds.

Question 6 of 10
An oil sample shows an exceptionally high acid value. This indicates:

Solution: A high acid value reveals that cleavage of ester linkages has released substantial amounts of free fatty acids.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following statements about natural oils is correct?

Solution: Cis-unsaturation creates bends in the chains, keeping natural oils liquid at room temperature.

Question 8 of 10
The unit of Iodine Value is:

Solution: Iodine value is conventionally expressed as grams of iodine absorbed per 100 grams of the sample.

Question 9 of 10
Which reagent is used in the Wijs method for determining iodine value?

Solution: Wijs solution consists of iodine monochloride dissolved in glacial acetic acid, used to halogenate double bonds.

Question 10 of 10
Lipids that yield glycerol and fatty acids upon hydrolysis are classified as:

Solution: Fats and oils are simple lipids representing esters of fatty acids with glycerol.
