Difference between DNA and RNA

Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) and ribonucleic acid (RNA) are nucleic acids responsible for the storage and expression of genetic information. They differ structurally in their sugar components, pyrimidine bases, strand conformation, and biochemical stability.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structural Variations in Sugar and Base Moieties

  • DNA contains 2-deoxy-D-ribose lacking a hydroxyl group at the C-2' position, whereas RNA contains D-ribose with a hydroxyl group at C-2'.
  • DNA incorporates the pyrimidine base thymine (5-methyluracil), while RNA contains uracil instead of thymine.
  • Both DNA and RNA share the purine bases adenine (A) and guanine (G), and the pyrimidine base cytosine (C).
  • The presence of the C-2' hydroxyl group in RNA makes it chemically more reactive and susceptible to alkaline hydrolysis.

Conformational Architecture and Strands

  • DNA predominantly exists as a double-stranded helix (B-form) stabilized by inter-strand hydrogen bonding and base stacking interactions.
  • RNA typically functions as a single-stranded polymer that folds into complex tertiary structures through internal base-pairing loops.
  • Double-stranded DNA strictly obeys Chargaff's rules where adenine pairs with thymine via two hydrogen bonds and guanine pairs with cytosine via three hydrogen bonds.
  • Single-stranded RNA does not obey strict 1:1 purine-to-pyrimidine ratios as bases pair intramolecularly rather than intermolecularly.

Biological Function and Cellular Stability

  • DNA functions as the primary genetic repository, permanently storing hereditary information within the nucleus of eukaryotic cells.
  • RNA acts primarily as an intermediary and catalyst, translating genetic instructions into functional proteins via mRNA, tRNA, and rRNA.
  • DNA exhibits high chemical stability due to the absence of the reactive C-2' hydroxyl group, protecting genetic data over long cellular lifespans.
  • RNA exhibits transient stability and undergoes rapid enzymatic or chemical degradation once its protein synthesis task is completed.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception DNA is exclusively double-stranded and RNA is single-stranded

Observation: Certain viral genomes — Some bacteriophages and viruses (e.g., Parvovirus) contain single-stranded DNA, while some viruses (e.g., Reovirus) contain double-stranded RNA.

Exception DNA does not contain uracil and RNA does not contain thymine

Observation: Transfer RNA (tRNA) — tRNA molecules undergo post-transcriptional methylation and modification, frequently incorporating thymine residues (e.g., TΨC loop).

Exception Nucleic acids are strictly informational polymers

Observation: Ribozymes — Specific functional RNA molecules possess catalytic activity capable of cleaving and splicing nucleic acid phosphodiester bonds.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • DNA contains 2-deoxyribose sugar lacking an oxygen atom at the C-2' position.
  • RNA contains ribose sugar with a reactive -OH group at the C-2' carbon.
  • Thymine is 5-methyluracil, present in DNA while uracil is present in RNA.
  • Alkaline hydrolysis readily cleaves RNA due to intramolecular nucleophilic attack by the 2'-OH group on the phosphodiester linkage.
  • Chargaff's rule ([A] + [G] = [T] + [C]) applies strictly to double-stranded DNA.
  • DNA is more stable against ultraviolet and chemical degradation than RNA.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the C-2' position of the pentose sugar; remember DNA lacks the -OH group at C-2', not C-3'.
  • Assuming RNA never contains thymine; modified tRNAs frequently contain thymine residues.
  • Applying Chargaff's rule ([A]=[T], [G]=[C]) to single-stranded DNA or single-stranded RNA molecules.
  • Confusing purines (Adenine, Guanine) with pyrimidines (Cytosine, Thymine, Uracil).

⚡ Quick Revision: Difference between DNA and RNA

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Nucleotide Building Blocks

Nucleotides consist of a nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar, and a phosphate group linked by phosphoester bonds.

Sugar-Phosphate Backbone

The backbone is formed by alternating pentose sugar and phosphate groups connected via 3',5'-phosphodiester linkages.

Hydrogen Bonding and Stability

Complementary base pairing via hydrogen bonds holds double-stranded nucleic acids together, with G-C pairs providing higher thermal stability than A-T pairs.

Enzymatic and Chemical Lability

The C-2' hydroxyl group in RNA acts as an intramolecular nucleophile, rendering RNA alkali-labile compared to DNA.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Nucleoside (Base + Sugar)
A purine or pyrimidine base linked to C-1' of a pentose sugar via N-glycosidic bond.
Nucleotide (Nucleoside + Phosphate)
The monomeric unit of nucleic acids consisting of a nucleoside esterified with phosphoric acid.
Chargaff's Rule ([A]=[T], [G]=[C])
Equimolar ratio of purines to pyrimidines in double-stranded DNA.
Phosphodiester Bond (3'-5' Linkage)
Covalent linkage joining the 3' carbon of one sugar to the 5' carbon of another via a phosphate group.
Ribozyme (Catalytic RNA)
An RNA molecule possessing enzymatic activity capable of chemical reactions.
Tautomerization (Keto-Enol Shift)
Dynamic proton shifts in nitrogenous bases influencing base-pairing specificity.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • DNA sugar: C5H10O4; RNA sugar: C5H10O5
  • DNA bases: A, T, G, C; RNA bases: A, U, G, C
  • DNA is double-stranded (B-form); RNA is single-stranded
  • RNA is alkali-labile; DNA is alkali-stable
  • Chargaff's equivalence holds for dsDNA only

📌 Quick Recall

  • DNA stores permanent genetic data with high chemical stability.
  • RNA translates genetic data and exhibits structural and catalytic diversity.
  • Pentose sugar differences dictate chemical reactivity toward hydrolysis.
  • Pyrimidine substitution of Thymine (DNA) with Uracil (RNA) is a key biochemical differentiator.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nucleoside VS Nucleotide

A nucleoside lacks the phosphate group, whereas a nucleotide contains one or more phosphate groups.

Which of the following components is present in RNA but absent in DNA?

Why is RNA more susceptible to alkaline hydrolysis than DNA?

Explanation: The 2'-hydroxyl group on the ribose sugar of RNA acts as an intramolecular nucleophile, facilitating cleavage of the phosphodiester bond in alkaline conditions.

Question 4 of 19

Chargaff's rule states that in double-stranded DNA:

Explanation: According to Chargaff's rules, the amount of purines ([A] + [G]) equals the amount of pyrimidines ([T] + [C]) in double-stranded DNA.

Question 5 of 19

Which base pairing involves three hydrogen bonds?

Explanation: Guanine and Cytosine form three hydrogen bonds, making G-C rich regions more thermally stable than A-T regions.

Question 6 of 19

Chemical formula of 2-deoxy-D-ribose sugar is:

Explanation: Deoxyribose lacks one oxygen atom compared to ribose, giving it the molecular formula C5H10O4.

Question 7 of 19

Thymine can also be chemically designated as:

Explanation: Thymine has a methyl group at the C-5 position of the pyrimidine ring, making it 5-methyluracil.

Question 8 of 19

Which of the following nucleic acids can possess catalytic activity (ribozyme)?

Explanation: Certain RNA molecules, termed ribozymes, possess catalytic activity capable of performing biochemical reactions such as RNA splicing.

Question 9 of 19

The linkage between the pentose sugar and the nitrogenous base is called:

Explanation: A nitrogenous base is attached to the C-1' carbon of the pentose sugar via an N-glycosidic bond.

Question 10 of 19

What is the primary cellular function of DNA?

Explanation: DNA serves as the primary repository of genetic information in living organisms.

Question 11 of 19

In a double-stranded DNA molecule, if the percentage of Adenine is 30%, what is the percentage of Cytosine?

Explanation: If A = 30%, then T = 30% (total 60%). Remaining 40% is G + C, so C = 20%.

Question 12 of 19

Which structural form of DNA is most common under physiological cellular conditions?

Explanation: The B-form is the standard right-handed double helix conformation adopted by DNA under physiological conditions.

Question 13 of 19

Which of the following is classified as a purine base?

Explanation: Guanine and Adenine are purines (double-ring structures), while Cytosine, Thymine, and Uracil are pyrimidines.

Question 14 of 19

What type of bond connects successive nucleotides in the nucleic acid backbone?

Explanation: Successive nucleotides are joined by 3',5'-phosphodiester bonds between the sugar hydroxyl groups and phosphate.

Question 15 of 19

Which RNA type carries genetic instructions from the nucleus to the ribosome?

Explanation: Messenger RNA (mRNA) acts as the transcript carrying genetic codes from genomic DNA to ribosomes for translation.

Question 16 of 19

Viral genetic material can exceptionally be:

Explanation: Certain viruses deviate from standard dogma, possessing single-stranded DNA or double-stranded RNA genomes.

Question 17 of 19

Which chemical test differentiates ribose from 2-deoxyribose sugar?

Explanation: Bial's Orcinol test is used to distinguish pentoses; ribose yields a green color faster than deoxyribose due to structural differences.

Question 18 of 19

The number of hydrogen bonds between Adenine and Thymine base pair is:

Explanation: Adenine and Thymine pair via two hydrogen bonds.

Question 19 of 19

Which enzyme is responsible for synthesizing RNA from a DNA template during transcription?

Explanation: RNA polymerase catalyzes the transcription of RNA from a DNA template.

Question 1 of 10
What is the structural difference between thymine and uracil?

Solution: Thymine is 5-methyluracil; it differs from uracil by the presence of a methyl group at the C-5 carbon of the pyrimidine ring.

Question 2 of 10
If a double-stranded DNA has 20% Guanine, calculate the percentage of Adenine.

Solution: G = 20%, so C = 20% (Total G+C = 40%). Remaining A+T = 60%, hence Adenine = 30%.

Question 3 of 10
Which nucleic acid conformation is typically single-stranded with extensive intramolecular folding?

Solution: RNA is synthesized as a single strand and folds back on itself to form complex secondary and tertiary structures.

Question 4 of 10
Which functional group makes DNA more chemically stable than RNA?

Solution: The absence of the C-2' hydroxyl group in 2-deoxyribose prevents neighboring group participation in hydrolysis, conferring high stability to DNA.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following pairs represents purine bases?

Solution: Adenine and Guanine are double-ring purine bases found in both DNA and RNA.

Question 6 of 10
In RNA, uracil pairs with which base?

Solution: Uracil in RNA forms complementary base pairing with Adenine through two hydrogen bonds.

Question 7 of 10
The molecular formula for the pentose sugar in DNA is:

Solution: 2-Deoxyribose in DNA has the molecular formula C5H10O4.

Question 8 of 10
Which type of RNA is most abundant in a typical mammalian cell?

Solution: Ribosomal RNA (rRNA) constitutes approximately 80% of total cellular RNA.

Question 9 of 10
What is the primary reason Chargaff's rule fails for single-stranded nucleic acids?

Solution: Chargaff's rule depends on inter-strand pairing ([A]=[T], [G]=[C]). Single-stranded molecules lack a complementary template strand.

Question 10 of 10
Which chemical bond connects the 5'-phosphate of one nucleotide to the 3'-hydroxyl of another?

Solution: Phosphodiester bonds link the 3' carbon of one pentose sugar to the 5' carbon of the next via a phosphate group.
