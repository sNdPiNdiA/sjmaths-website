DNA Fingerprinting

DNA fingerprinting is a laboratory technique used to determine the nucleotide sequence characteristics of specific DNA regions unique to individual genomes. It relies on the analysis of highly polymorphic repetitive DNA sequences known as variable number tandem repeats (VNTRs) or short tandem repeats (STRs).

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Restriction Fragment Length Polymorphism (RFLP) and Southern Blotting

  • Genomic DNA is digested using specific restriction endonucleases that cleave at palindromic recognition sequences.
  • DNA fragments are separated by agarose gel electrophoresis based on molecular size and charge.
  • Separated fragments are transferred to a nitrocellulose or nylon membrane via Southern blotting capillary action.
  • Radioactively or fluorescently labeled DNA probes hybridize with complementary target sequences on the membrane.

Variable Number Tandem Repeats (VNTRs) and Minisatellites

  • VNTRs consist of short nucleotide sequences (9-60 base pairs) repeated head-to-tail in non-coding regions of DNA.
  • The number of repeats varies extensively among individuals, generating high genetic polymorphism.
  • Jeffreys probes, such as 33.15 and 33.6, target core sequences common to many hypervariable minisatellites.
  • Multilocus profiling generates complex banding patterns where each band represents a specific restriction fragment containing the repeat.

Short Tandem Repeats (STRs) and PCR Amplification

  • Modern forensic profiling relies on STRs (microsatellites) consisting of 2-7 base pair repeat units.
  • Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) selectively amplifies minute DNA quantities using sequence-specific flanking primers.
  • Multiplex PCR enables simultaneous amplification of 13 or more core CODIS loci labeled with distinct fluorescent dyes.
  • Capillary electrophoresis with laser detection resolves PCR products differing by as little as a single base pair.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Hardy-Weinberg Allele Frequency Relation
p2 + 2pq + q2 = 1
Conditions: For a biallelic VNTR locus with alleles p and q in a large random mating population
Units: Fractional allele/genotype frequencies
Electrophoretic Migration Rate
v = μ E
Conditions: Where v is velocity, μ is electrophoretic mobility, and E is electric field strength
Units: v in m s-1, E in V m-1
Melting Temperature of DNA Duplex
Tm = 81.5 + 16.6(log10[Na+]) + 0.41(%G+C) - (500/L)
Conditions: Where L is the length of the duplex in base pairs and [Na+] is sodium ion concentration
Units: Tm in °C
Probability of Matching Genotypes (Product Rule)
P = p1 × p2 × p3 × ... × pn
Conditions: Assuming independent assortment and Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium across loci
Units: Dimensionless probability (0 to 1)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Offspring inherit identical genetic markers from both biological parents.

Observation: De novo mutations in repeat units — Slippage during DNA replication can alter the number of repeat units, leading to allele size discrepancies between parent and child.

Exception Unrelated individuals exhibit completely different RFLP and STR profiles.

Observation: Monozygotic (identical) twins — Monozygotic twins originate from a single fertilized zygote and share an identical nuclear genome, yielding indistinguishable profiles.

Exception Restriction endonucleases cleave target DNA consistently.

Observation: CpG island methylation — Methylation of cytosine bases within restriction sites (e.g., HpaII) inhibits enzymatic cleavage by specific restriction endonucleases.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Paternity Index Rule
PI = X / Y (where X = probability given alleged father, Y = probability given random man)

Application: Combined Paternity Index (CPI) is the product of individual locus PIs to calculate overall paternity probability.

Electrophoretic Migration Mnemonic
Smaller & Shorter = Faster & Further

Application: Smaller DNA fragments migrate faster through the agarose or polyacrylamide gel matrix toward the anode.

Southern Blotting Sequence
SNoW DRoP (Southern = DNA, Northern = RNA, Western = Protein)

Application: Quickly recall which blotting technique applies to which macromolecule.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Alec Jeffreys discovered DNA fingerprinting in 1984 using minisatellite DNA probes.
  • VNTR stands for Variable Number Tandem Repeats, which are fundamental to classical RFLP profiling.
  • STR analysis uses loci with 2-7 base pair repeating units, forming the basis of the FBI CODIS database.
  • Ethidium bromide or fluorescent dyes like SYBR Safe intercalate into DNA base pairs for visualization under UV light.
  • DNA carries a net negative charge due to phosphodiester backbones and migrates toward the positive anode during electrophoresis.
  • Southern blotting utilizes nitrocellulose or nylon membranes to immobilize single-stranded DNA fragments after alkaline denaturation.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Southern blotting (DNA) with Northern blotting (RNA) or Western blotting (protein).
  • Assuming restriction endonucleases cleave single-stranded DNA instead of double-stranded DNA recognition sequences.
  • Forgetting that restriction enzymes require specific divalent metal ions like Mg2+ for catalytic activity.
  • Neglecting that identical twins cannot be differentiated using standard nuclear DNA profiling markers.

⚡ Quick Revision: DNA Fingerprinting

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Restriction Digestion

Specific endonucleases recognize palindromic sequences and cleave double-stranded DNA to generate reproducible fragment lengths.

Electrophoretic Separation

Negatively charged DNA fragments migrate through a porous gel matrix under an electric field, separating inversely proportional to their molecular size.

Hybridization and Detection

Radioactive or fluorescent single-stranded probes bind specifically to complementary target sequences transferred onto blotting membranes.

PCR and STR Profiling

Enzymatic amplification of specific short tandem repeat loci allows forensic identification from trace biological samples.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

VNTR (Variable Number Tandem Repeats)
Minisatellite DNA sequences with 9-60 bp repeat units.
STR (Short Tandem Repeat)
Microsatellite DNA sequences with 2-7 bp repeat units.
RFLP (Restriction Fragment Length Polymorphism)
Variation in DNA fragment sizes produced by restriction digestion.
CODIS (Combined DNA Index System)
FBI database of standardized STR DNA profiles.
Probe (Labeled DNA/RNA sequence)
Single-stranded nucleic acid tagged to detect complementary sequences.
Palindromic Sequence (Symmetric recognition site)
DNA sequence reading the same forward and backward on opposite strands.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • DNA carries a net negative charge due to phosphate groups.
  • Alec Jeffreys developed the original RFLP-based DNA fingerprinting.
  • STR markers use 2-7 base pair repeats amplified via PCR.
  • Southern blotting transfers DNA from agarose gels to nylon membranes.
  • Monozygotic twins share identical nuclear DNA profiles.

📌 Quick Recall

  • DNA profiling exploits hypervariable non-coding regions.
  • RFLP requires large, high-molecular-weight DNA samples.
  • STR analysis is sensitive and compatible with degraded forensic samples.
  • Statistical probability calculations confirm identity or relatedness.

⚖️ Key Differences

Minisatellite (VNTR) VS Microsatellite (STR)

Minisatellites are 9-60 bp repeats analyzed via RFLP, whereas microsatellites are 2-7 bp repeats analyzed via PCR.

Who is credited with the discovery of DNA fingerprinting?

What type of chemical bond is cleaved by restriction endonucleases?

During agarose gel electrophoresis, toward which electrode do DNA molecules migrate?

Which enzyme is essential for amplifying trace amounts of DNA in modern forensic profiling?

Explanation: Taq polymerase is a thermostable enzyme used in PCR to synthesize complementary DNA strands.

Can monozygotic twins be distinguished using standard nuclear STR DNA fingerprinting?

What is the primary function of a DNA probe in hybridization?

What is the primary advantage of STR analysis over RFLP analysis?

Which chemical agent is commonly used to visualize DNA bands under UV light in agarose gels?

Explanation: Ethidium bromide intercalates between DNA base pairs and fluoresces under UV illumination.

What defines the recognition sites of most restriction endonucleases?

What does CODIS stand for in forensic DNA databases?

In Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, what term represents the frequency of a homozygous recessive genotype for a biallelic locus?

Question 15 of 19

What causes the high polymorphism of VNTR loci among individuals?

Explanation: Polymorphism arises from unequal crossing over and replication slippage altering repeat numbers.

Question 16 of 19

What is the purpose of the denaturation step in Southern blotting?

Explanation: Alkaline denaturation converts double-stranded DNA into single strands so probes can hybridize.

Question 17 of 19

Which database contains DNA profiles contributed by law enforcement agencies in the United States?

Explanation: CODIS is the national database utilized by law enforcement for forensic DNA matching.

Question 18 of 19

What property determines the migration rate of DNA fragments in gel electrophoresis?

Explanation: Smaller fragments move faster and further through the gel matrix compared to larger fragments.

Question 19 of 19

Why are fluorescent dyes preferred over radioactive isotopes in modern STR analysis?

Explanation: Fluorescent labeling avoids radioactive waste and permits simultaneous detection of multiple loci (multiplexing).

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary role of dideoxynucleotides in DNA sequencing methods derived from fingerprinting technologies?

Solution: Dideoxynucleotides lack a 3'-OH group, causing chain termination upon incorporation by DNA polymerase.

Question 2 of 10
In DNA profiling, what does a stutter product during PCR amplification refer to?

Solution: Slippage of the DNA polymerase during PCR of tandem repeats produces minor stutter peaks.

Question 3 of 10
Which parameter is most critical when selecting a restriction enzyme for RFLP analysis of genomic DNA?

Solution: The frequency and specificity of the restriction enzyme recognition site determine fragment distribution.

Question 4 of 10
What is the function of EDTA in DNA extraction buffer solutions?

Solution: EDTA binds Mg2+ ions required by endogenous nucleases, protecting DNA from enzymatic degradation.

Question 5 of 10
Why is sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) often used in cell lysis buffers prior to DNA fingerprinting?

Solution: SDS is an anionic detergent that disrupts lipid bilayers and denatures contaminating proteins.

Question 6 of 10
What is the purpose of stringency washes following DNA hybridization on a Southern blot?

Solution: High-temperature and low-salt stringency washes remove improperly matched, non-specific probe hybridizations.

Question 7 of 10
In capillary electrophoresis for STR analysis, how are different loci with overlapping size ranges distinguished?

Solution: Multiplex PCR utilizes distinct fluorescent tags so overlapping fragment sizes can be resolved by color.

Question 8 of 10
Which type of DNA is inherited exclusively down the maternal line and used in complementary forensic investigations?

Solution: Mitochondrial DNA is maternally inherited and valuable for analyzing highly degraded or ancient samples.

Question 9 of 10
What is the primary reason for multiplexing in forensic PCR amplification?

Solution: Multiplexing conserves precious forensic samples by analyzing numerous loci in a single PCR tube.

Question 10 of 10
If a child inherits an allele not present in the mother's profile, where must that allele have originated?

Solution: Mendelian inheritance dictates that paternal alleles missing in the maternal profile must originate from the biological father.
