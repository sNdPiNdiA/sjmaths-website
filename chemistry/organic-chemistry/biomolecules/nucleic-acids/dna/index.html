Deoxyribonucleic Acid

Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) is a biopolymer composed of repeating nucleotide monomers linked by phosphodiester bonds. It serves as the primary repository of genetic information in living organisms.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Primary Structure and Nucleotide Architecture

  • Each nucleotide consists of a 2'-deoxy-D-ribose sugar, a nitrogenous base, and a phosphate group.
  • Nitrogenous bases are classified as purines (Adenine, Guanine) and pyrimidines (Cytosine, Thymine).
  • Nucleotides are linked together via 3',5'-phosphodiester bridges forming the sugar-phosphate backbone.
  • The polynucleotide chain possesses directional polarity, running strictly from the 5' end to the 3' end.

Secondary Structure and Double Helix Geometry

  • The DNA double helix consists of two polynucleotide chains wound around a common axis in a right-handed spiral (B-form).
  • The two strands are antiparallel; one runs 5'→3' while the complementary strand runs 3'→5'.
  • Base pairing is highly specific: Adenine pairs with Thymine via two hydrogen bonds, while Guanine pairs with Cytosine via three hydrogen bonds.
  • The exterior hydrophilic sugar-phosphate backbone protects the hydrophobic interior stacked aromatic nitrogenous bases.

Physicochemical Properties and Denaturation

  • Aqueous solutions of DNA exhibit high viscosity due to their extended polymeric rigidity and high molecular weight.
  • Thermal denaturation or melting disrupts hydrogen bonds and base stacking, converting double-stranded DNA into single-stranded coils.
  • Higher G+C content increases the melting temperature (Tm) due to the presence of three stabilizing hydrogen bonds per base pair.
  • Hyperchromicity is observed during denaturation, where the UV absorbance at 260 nm increases as base unstacking occurs.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Double-stranded DNA always obeys Chargaff's rule where [A] = [T] and [G] = [C].

Observation: Single-stranded DNA (ss-DNA) viral genomes — Single-stranded nucleic acids lack a complementary strand, failing to maintain equal stoichiometric purine-pyrimidine pairing ratios.

Exception DNA forms a right-handed B-form double helix under physiological conditions.

Observation: Z-DNA conformation — High salt concentrations or alternating purine-pyrimidine sequences (like alternating GC repeats) induce a left-handed helical geometry.

Exception Nucleic acids contain Thymine as the standard pyrimidine base paired with Adenine.

Observation: RNA molecules — RNA incorporates Uracil instead of Thymine due to metabolic energy efficiency and prevention of cytosine deamination mutations.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • DNA stands for 2'-deoxyribonucleic acid, lacking a hydroxyl group at the C2' position.
  • The distance between two adjacent base pairs along the helical axis is 0.34 nm (3.4 Å).
  • One complete turn of the B-DNA double helix measures 3.4 nm and contains 10 base pairs.
  • The diameter of the B-DNA double helix is approximately 2.0 nm (20 Å).
  • Phosphodiester linkages involve esterification between phosphoric acid and the 3' and 5' hydroxyl groups of adjacent pentose sugars.
  • Hyperchromic effect refers to the increase in UV absorbance at 260 nm upon DNA melting.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the 5' and 3' ends; remember synthesis and reading always proceed exclusively from 5' to 3'.
  • Assuming single-stranded DNA obeys Chargaff's equivalence rules; it only applies to double-stranded helical DNA.
  • Forgetting that RNA contains Uracil while DNA contains Thymine (5-methyluracil).
  • Confusing nucleosides (base + sugar) with nucleotides (base + sugar + phosphate).

⚡ Quick Revision: Deoxyribonucleic Acid

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Nucleoside and Nucleotide Building Blocks

Nucleosides consist of a nitrogenous base attached to C1' of a pentose sugar via a N-glycosidic linkage. Addition of a phosphate group at the C5' hydroxyl forms a nucleotide.

Watson-Crick Double Helix Model

The DNA model features two antiparallel polynucleotide chains wound into a right-handed double helix stabilized by hydrophobic stacking and inter-strand hydrogen bonds.

Chargaff's Base Composition Laws

In native double-stranded DNA, the mole percentage of adenine equals thymine, and guanine equals cytosine, establishing the structural basis for base complementarity.

Denaturation and Renaturation Kinetics

Thermal or chemical disruption breaks hydrogen bonds causing strand separation, a process characterized by a cooperative melting temperature (Tm) dependent on GC content.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Nucleoside (Base + Sugar)
Glycosylamine compound lacking a phosphate group.
Nucleotide (Base + Sugar + Phosphate)
The monomeric repeating unit of nucleic acids.
Purine (Bicyclic Nitrogenous Base)
Aromatic heteroaromatic system containing fused pyrimidine and imidazole rings.
Pyrimidine (Monocyclic Nitrogenous Base)
Six-membered heterocyclic aromatic ring containing two nitrogen atoms.
Melting Temperature (Tm)
Temperature at which 50% of double-stranded DNA is denatured into single strands.
Hyperchromicity (ΔA260)
Increase in UV absorption capacity upon unwinding of base-stacked DNA.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • [A] = [T] and [G] = [C] in double-stranded DNA
  • B-form pitch = 3.4 nm with 10 base pairs per turn
  • A-T base pairs form 2 hydrogen bonds
  • G-C base pairs form 3 hydrogen bonds
  • DNA polarity runs strictly from 5' to 3'

📌 Quick Recall

  • DNA is a polymer of deoxynucleotides joined by 3',5'-phosphodiester bonds.
  • The secondary structure is an antiparallel, right-handed double helix.
  • Base pairing specificity relies on complementary hydrogen bonding rules.
  • Thermal stability is directly proportional to the relative guanine-cytosine content.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nucleoside VS Nucleotide

A nucleoside contains only a base and sugar; a nucleotide contains a base, sugar, and one or more phosphate groups.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following nitrogenous bases is NOT present in DNA?

Explanation: Uracil is found primarily in RNA, whereas DNA contains Thymine in its place.

Question 2 of 18

What type of linkage connects nucleotide monomers in a DNA strand?

Explanation: Nucleotides in a single strand are linked together by 3',5'-phosphodiester bonds.

Question 3 of 18

According to Chargaff's rules, if a double-stranded DNA sample contains 20% Adenine, what is the percentage of Cytosine?

Explanation: If Adenine is 20%, Thymine is also 20% (Total AT = 40%). The remaining 60% is split equally between G and C, making Cytosine 30%.

Question 4 of 18

How many hydrogen bonds are formed between a Guanine-Cytosine base pair?

Explanation: Guanine and Cytosine form three hydrogen bonds, making G-C rich regions more thermally stable.

Question 5 of 18

What is the pitch (length of one complete turn) of a B-DNA double helix?

Explanation: One complete turn of B-DNA spans 3.4 nm and contains 10 base pairs.

Question 6 of 18

The linkage between the nitrogenous base and the C1' of the pentose sugar in a nucleoside is:

Explanation: An N-glycosidic bond connects N1 of pyrimidines or N9 of purines to the C1' carbon of the sugar.

Question 7 of 18

Which structural form of DNA is left-handed?

Explanation: Z-DNA is a left-handed double helical structure often found in alternating purine-pyrimidine sequences.

Question 8 of 18

What causes hyperchromicity in DNA solutions upon heating?

Explanation: Melting disrupts base stacking and hydrogen bonds, increasing UV absorption at 260 nm (hyperchromic effect).

Question 9 of 18

The polarity of a single DNA strand is defined by:

Explanation: Strand polarity runs from the 5' phosphate group to the free 3' hydroxyl group of the terminal sugar.

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following bases belongs to the purine class?

Explanation: Adenine and Guanine are purines with a bicyclic ring structure.

Question 11 of 18

What is the primary factor contributing to the stability of the DNA double helix along its axis?

Explanation: Base stacking interactions (hydrophobic and π-π interactions) provide the major thermodynamic stability to the DNA helix.

Question 12 of 18

If a single-stranded DNA has the sequence 5'-ATGC-3', what is the sequence of its complementary strand written in standard 5' to 3' direction?

Explanation: The complementary sequence is 3'-TACG-5', which when written in standard 5' to 3' direction becomes 5'-CGTA-3'.

Question 13 of 18

Which enzyme or condition is capable of causing DNA denaturation?

Explanation: High pH (alkaline conditions) ionizes keto groups of bases and disrupts hydrogen bonding, causing strand separation.

Question 14 of 18

What is the approximate diameter of the B-DNA double helix?

Explanation: The diameter of standard B-form DNA is approximately 2.0 nm (20 Å).

Question 15 of 18

Which chemical component distinguishes deoxyribose from ribose?

Explanation: Deoxyribose lacks an oxygen atom at the C2' position of the pentose ring compared to ribose.

Question 16 of 18

In a nucleic acid, the phosphate group is esterified to which carbon atoms of two adjacent sugar rings?

Explanation: Phosphodiester linkages connect the 3' carbon of one sugar to the 5' carbon of the next via phosphate ester bonds.

Question 17 of 18

Which of the following statements regarding Chargaff's rule is correct?

Explanation: Chargaff's rule states that [A] = [T], hence the ratio [A] / [T] equals 1 in double-stranded DNA.

Question 18 of 18

What is the biological significance of the complementary nature of DNA strands?

Explanation: Base complementarity ensures that each strand can serve as a template for exact replication of the genetic code.

Question 4 of 10
What is the effect of increased G+C content on the melting temperature (T_m) of DNA?

Solution: G-C base pairs have three hydrogen bonds compared to two in A-T pairs, requiring more thermal energy to dissociate, thereby increasing T_m.

Question 5 of 10
A nucleotide differs from a nucleoside by possessing:

Solution: A nucleotide is a nucleoside esterified with one or more phosphate groups.

In the Watson-Crick model, the two strands of DNA are:

Question 7 of 10
What is responsible for the negative charge of the DNA backbone at physiological pH?

Solution: The hydroxyl groups of the phosphate backbone dissociate to form negatively charged oxygen species at physiological pH.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following represents the correct pyrimidine bases found in DNA?
