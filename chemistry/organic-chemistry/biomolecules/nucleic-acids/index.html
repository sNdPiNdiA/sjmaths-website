Organic Chemistry

Nucleic Acids

Nucleic acids are high-molecular-weight biopolymers composed of nucleotide monomers linked by phosphodiester bonds. They function as the primary informational carriers directing protein synthesis and genetic inheritance in living organisms.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Chemical Structure of Nucleotides and Nucleosides

  • A nucleoside consists of a purine or pyrimidine base linked to C-1′ of a β-D-ribose or 2′-deoxy-β-D-ribose via a glycosidic bond.
  • The phosphate group is attached via an ester linkage to the C-5′ hydroxyl group of the pentose sugar to form a nucleotide.
  • Purine bases include adenine (A) and guanine (G), characterized by a fused bicyclic ring system.
  • Pyrimidine bases include cytosine (C), thymine (T), and uracil (U), possessing a single heterocyclic ring.

Primary and Secondary Structure of DNA

  • The primary structure of DNA is defined by the linear sequence of nucleotides connected by 3′-5′ phosphodiester linkages.
  • The Watson-Crick double helix model features two antiparallel polynucleotide chains coiled around a common axis in a right-handed helix.
  • Base pairing occurs strictly through hydrogen bonding: adenine pairs with thymine via two H-bonds, and guanine pairs with cytosine via three H-bonds.
  • The hydrophilic sugar-phosphate backbone faces outward, while the hydrophobic aromatic bases stack vertically on the interior.

Ribonucleic Acid (RNA) and Structural Variations

  • RNA contains β-D-ribose instead of 2′-deoxyribose and replaces thymine with uracil.
  • Messenger RNA (mRNA) carries genetic instructions from nuclear DNA to the ribosomes for protein translation.
  • Transfer RNA (tRNA) adopts a cloverleaf secondary structure containing an anticodon loop to interpret mRNA codons.
  • Ribosomal RNA (rRNA) associates with proteins to form ribosomes, acting as the structural and catalytic core for peptide bond formation.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Chargaff's rule stating [A] = [T] and [G] = [C]

Observation: Single-stranded DNA and RNA viral genomes — Single-stranded nucleic acids lack complementary pairing partners, leading to non-equivalent base ratios.

Exception DNA contains thymine and RNA contains uracil

Observation: Certain tRNA molecules and viral RNA genomes — Post-transcriptional methylation and modification pathways yield minor bases, including thymine residues within specific tRNA loops.

Exception Nucleic acids exist purely as linear duplexes or single strands

Observation: Ribozymes and functional RNAs — Intramolecular base pairing allows RNA to fold into complex tertiary structures with catalytic enzymatic activity.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The linkage connecting nucleotides in a nucleic acid chain is a 3′-5′ phosphodiester bond.
  • Adenine always binds to Thymine via two hydrogen bonds in standard DNA Watson-Crick base pairing.
  • RNA undergoes alkaline hydrolysis easily due to the presence of the 2′-hydroxyl group on the ribose ring, which is absent in DNA.
  • Guanine pairs with Cytosine through three hydrogen bonds, conferring higher thermal stability to regions rich in G-C pairs.
  • Nucleosides lack the phosphate group entirely, consisting only of a nitrogenous base and a pentose sugar.
  • Synthetic or modified nucleotides lacking the 3′-OH group act as chain terminators in DNA sequencing methods.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing nucleosides with nucleotides; always verify the presence of the phosphate group for nucleotides.
  • Assuming RNA contains thymine; RNA universally incorporates uracil instead of thymine under standard physiological conditions.
  • Forgetting that Chargaff's rule applies exclusively to double-stranded DNA, not single-stranded nucleic acids.
  • Overlooking the directionality of nucleic acid strands; sequences are conventionally written and synthesized from the 5′ end to the 3′ end.

⚡ Quick Revision: Nucleic Acids

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Monomeric Units

Nucleotides serve as the fundamental building blocks, constructed from a nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar, and one or more phosphate groups.

Backbone Architecture

The polynucleotide backbone alternates phosphate and sugar units linked by stable covalent phosphodiester bonds.

Double Helical Stability

DNA maintains its double helical structure via hydrogen bonding between complementary base pairs and hydrophobic base stacking interactions.

Genetic Information Flow

Biological information flows from DNA to RNA via transcription, and subsequently to proteins via translation.

RNA molecule possessing enzymatic cleavage or ligation activity.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • DNA: Deoxyribose sugar, A, T, G, C bases, double-stranded.
  • RNA: Ribose sugar, A, U, G, C bases, single-stranded.
  • A pairs with T (2 H-bonds); G pairs with C (3 H-bonds).
  • Polarity of strands is always antiparallel (5'→3' paired with 3'→5').
  • Alkaline hydrolysis cleaves RNA due to intramolecular nucleophilic attack by the 2'-OH group.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nucleic acids are essential biopolymers responsible for heredity and protein synthesis.
  • Monomers are nucleotides containing a base, sugar, and phosphate.
  • DNA stores genetic information securely in a double-helix architecture.
  • RNA translates genetic data into functional enzymes and structural proteins.

⚖️ Key Differences

DNA Hydrolysis in Alkali VS RNA Hydrolysis in Alkali

RNA is rapidly hydrolyzed by alkali due to the 2'-OH group forming a cyclic intermediate, whereas DNA resists alkaline hydrolysis.

Which of the following components constitutes a nucleoside?

Explanation: A nucleoside is composed exclusively of a nitrogenous base linked to a pentose sugar via a β-N-glycosidic bond.

Question 2 of 18

The linkage holding successive nucleotides together in a DNA strand is:

Explanation: Successive nucleotides in nucleic acids are joined by 3′-5′ phosphodiester bonds.

Question 3 of 18

Which base is present in RNA but absent in DNA under normal physiological conditions?

Explanation: Uracil replaces thymine as the standard pyrimidine base in RNA molecules.

Question 4 of 18

According to Chargaff's rules for double-stranded DNA, if a sample contains 20% Adenine, what is the percentage of Cytosine?

Explanation: If Adenine = 20%, Thymine = 20% (total 40%). The remaining 60% is split equally between G and C, meaning Cytosine = 30%.

Question 5 of 18

How many hydrogen bonds are formed between Guanine and Cytosine in DNA base pairing?

Explanation: Guanine and cytosine interact through three specific hydrogen bonds, enhancing structural stability.

Question 6 of 18

Which of the following structural features is unique to RNA compared to DNA?

Explanation: RNA differs from DNA by being typically single-stranded, containing ribose (with a 2'-OH group), and incorporating uracil.

Question 7 of 18

The type of bond connecting the nitrogenous base to the C-1' carbon of the pentose sugar is:

Explanation: The base is attached to the sugar through a β-N-glycosidic bond.

Question 8 of 18

Which nucleic acid acts as the adaptor molecule during protein translation?

Explanation: Transfer RNA (tRNA) acts as an adaptor, matching specific mRNA codons with their respective amino acids.

Question 9 of 18

Why is RNA susceptible to alkaline hydrolysis while DNA is stable under alkaline conditions?

Explanation: The 2'-OH group in RNA acts as an intramolecular nucleophile attacking the adjacent phosphodiester bond under alkaline conditions.

Question 10 of 18

The two strands of a DNA double helix run in opposite chemical directions, known as:

Explanation: DNA strands run in opposite directions (one 5'→3' and the other 3'→5'), making them antiparallel.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following bases is classified as a purine derivative?

Explanation: Guanine and Adenine are purines with a bicyclic ring system.

Question 12 of 18

What is the primary function of mRNA?

Explanation: Messenger RNA carries genetic transcripts from genomic DNA to ribosomes for translation into polypeptides.

Question 13 of 18

Which chemical component distinguishes deoxyribose from ribose?

Explanation: 2'-Deoxyribose lacks an oxygen atom at the C-2' position compared to ribose.

Question 14 of 18

In a DNA molecule, the twisting of the strands creates:

Explanation: The geometry of the Watson-Crick double helix generates alternating major and minor grooves along the outer surface.

Question 15 of 18

Which enzyme is responsible for synthesizing RNA from a DNA template during transcription?

Explanation: RNA polymerase catalyzes the transcription of DNA into RNA transcripts.

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following is a pyrimidine base found in both DNA and RNA?

Explanation: Cytosine is a pyrimidine base common to both DNA and RNA.

Question 17 of 18

What type of forces stabilizes the vertical stacking of base pairs inside the DNA double helix?

Explanation: Hydrophobic interactions and aromatic π-π stacking between adjacent planar bases stabilize the interior stack of the helix.

Question 18 of 18

The melting temperature (T_m) of DNA increases with an increase in which parameter?

Explanation: Higher G-C content increases the number of three-hydrogen-bond pairs, raising the thermal denaturation threshold (T_m).

Question 1 of 10
Which nucleotide component differentiates adenosine from deoxyadenosine?

Solution: Adenosine contains ribose, whereas deoxyadenosine contains 2'-deoxyribose.

Question 2 of 10
What is the structural role of major and minor grooves in the DNA double helix?

Solution: Grooves expose the hydrogen bond donors and acceptors of base pairs, allowing sequence-specific transcription factor binding.

Question 3 of 10
During nucleic acid hydrolysis, complete acid hydrolysis yields:

Solution: Complete acid hydrolysis breaks both glycosidic and ester bonds, yielding free bases, sugars, and phosphoric acid.

Question 4 of 10
Which base pairing holds true for RNA secondary structures where non-Watson-Crick pairing occurs?

Solution: G-U wobble base pairing commonly occurs in RNA secondary structures such as tRNA anticodon loops.

Question 5 of 10
The directionality of a single DNA strand is designated by:

Solution: A strand has polarity defined by its free 5'-phosphate group at one end and 3'-hydroxyl group at the other.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following structural forms of DNA is a right-handed helix with 10.4 base pairs per turn?

Solution: B-DNA is the standard physiological right-handed helical conformation with approximately 10.4 base pairs per turn.

Question 7 of 10
What happens to the absorbance at 260 nm (hyperchromic effect) when double-stranded DNA is thermally denatured?

Solution: Melting dsDNA disrupts base stacking, increasing UV absorption at 260 nm, known as the hyperchromic effect.

Question 8 of 10
Which chemical reagent is commonly used to precipitate nucleic acids out of aqueous solution?

Solution: Monovalent cations neutralize negative charges on the phosphate backbone, and ethanol decreases hydration, precipitating nucleic acids.

Question 9 of 10
Which class of RNA represents the largest fraction of total cellular RNA?

Solution: Ribosomal RNA constitutes approximately 80% of total cellular RNA in typical cells.

Question 10 of 10
What is the primary function of DNA polymerase during replication?

Solution: DNA polymerases catalyze the extension of DNA strands by adding deoxynucleotides to the 3'-OH terminus.
