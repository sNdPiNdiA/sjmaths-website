Organic Chemistry

RNA

Ribonucleic acid (RNA) is a polymeric molecule essential in various biological roles in coding, decoding, regulation, and expression of genes. It contains D-ribose sugar, a phosphate backbone, and nitrogenous bases adenine, guanine, cytosine, and uracil.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structural Components of RNA

  • RNA contains β-D-ribose sugar, characterized by the presence of a hydroxyl group at the C-2' position.
  • The pyrimidine bases in RNA are cytosine (C) and uracil (U), replacing thymine found in DNA.
  • Purine bases consist of adenine (A) and guanine (G), forming N-glycosidic linkages with the C-1' of the ribose ring.

Primary Classes and Functions of RNA

  • Messenger RNA (mRNA) carries genetic information transcribed from DNA to the ribosome for polypeptide synthesis.
  • Transfer RNA (tRNA) acts as an adaptor molecule, matching specific amino acids to corresponding mRNA codons via its anticodon loop.
  • Ribosomal RNA (rRNA) associates with proteins to form ribosomes, serving both structural and catalytic roles during peptide bond formation.

Chemical Stability and Hydrolysis

  • The 2'-OH group on the ribose sugar makes RNA susceptible to alkaline hydrolysis via internal nucleophilic attack, forming a 2',3'-cyclic monophosphate intermediate.
  • RNA is chemically unstable in basic solutions compared to DNA due to this base-catalyzed cleavage mechanism.
  • Secondary structures in RNA involve Watson-Crick base pairing (A-U and G-C) alongside non-canonical base pairs such as G-U wobbling.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Chargaff's equivalence rules ([A] = [T] and [G] = [C])

Observation: Does not apply to single-stranded RNA molecules — Single-stranded RNA lacks a complementary double-helical partner strand, leading to unequal base ratios.

Exception Nucleic acids contain thymine as a standard pyrimidine base

Observation: RNA predominantly contains uracil instead of thymine — Uracil is energetically less expensive to produce, though methylation to thymine in DNA provides added resistance against spontaneous cytosine deamination mutations.

Exception Nucleic acids are stable under mild alkaline conditions

Observation: RNA undergoes rapid strand cleavage in dilute alkali — The neighboring 2'-hydroxyl group acts as an intramolecular nucleophile attacking the adjacent phosphodiester bond.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • RNA contains D-ribose sugar with a reactive 2'-hydroxyl group.
  • Uracil replaces thymine as the principal pyrimidine base paired with adenine in RNA.
  • Alkaline hydrolysis of RNA proceeds via a cyclic 2',3'-monophosphate intermediate.
  • Transfer RNA (tRNA) exhibits a cloverleaf secondary structure containing modified bases like dihydrouridine and pseudouridine.
  • Ribosomal RNA (rRNA) constitutes the major fraction of total cellular RNA (approximately 80%).
  • Messenger RNA (mRNA) has a 5'-cap (7-methylguanosine) and a 3'-poly(A) tail in eukaryotes.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the 2'-OH group presence in RNA with DNA, leading to incorrect assumptions regarding alkaline stability.
  • Assuming Chargaff's rule holds true for all RNA forms, ignoring single-stranded exceptions.
  • Forgetting that uracil lacks the 5-methyl group present in thymine.
  • Mistaking nucleosides for nucleotides by omitting the phosphate group.

⚡ Quick Revision: RNA

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Chemical Composition

RNA consists of alternating phosphate and D-ribose units attached to nitrogenous bases A, U, G, and C.

Hydrolysis Mechanism

The 2'-OH group facilitates intramolecular transesterification under alkaline conditions, cleaving the phosphodiester backbone.

Functional Diversification

Cellular RNA is divided into mRNA (template), tRNA (adaptor), and rRNA (catalytic/structural) classes.

Secondary Structure

RNA folds into complex hairpins and stem-loop structures stabilized by non-canonical and Watson-Crick hydrogen bonding.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • RNA contains ribose; DNA contains deoxyribose.
  • Uracil is present in RNA instead of thymine.
  • Alkali causes rapid hydrolysis of RNA via 2'-OH attack.
  • mRNA, tRNA, and rRNA are the three principal functional types.
  • tRNA possesses characteristic modified bases like pseudouridine.

📌 Quick Recall

  • RNA is vital for protein synthesis and gene expression regulation.
  • Chemical instability toward alkali distinguishes RNA from DNA.
  • Hydrogen bonding patterns dictate complex RNA secondary folding.
  • Enzymatic polymerization proceeds in the 5' to 3' direction.

Nucleoside VS Nucleotide

Question 1 of 19

Which sugar is present in RNA?

Explanation: RNA contains D-ribose sugar, distinguished by a hydroxyl group at the C-2' position.

Question 2 of 19

Which pyrimidine base is unique to RNA compared to DNA?

Explanation: Uracil is found in RNA, whereas thymine (5-methyluracil) is found in DNA.

Question 3 of 19

What is the primary reason for the instability of RNA in alkaline solutions?

Explanation: The 2'-hydroxyl group acts as an intramolecular nucleophile in alkaline medium, cleaving the phosphodiester bond.

Question 4 of 19

Which type of RNA accounts for the highest percentage of total cellular RNA?

Explanation: Ribosomal RNA (rRNA) constitutes approximately 80% of total cellular RNA.

Question 5 of 19

The bond linking the base to the sugar in a ribonucleoside is:

Explanation: An N-glycosidic bond connects the nitrogenous base to C-1' of the ribose sugar.

Question 6 of 19

Which molecule contains the anticodon loop?

Explanation: Transfer RNA (tRNA) contains the anticodon triplet that pairs with the codon on mRNA.

Question 7 of 19

During alkaline hydrolysis of RNA, what intermediate is formed?

Explanation: Attack by the 2'-O- anion on the phosphorus atom forms a 2',3'-cyclic monophosphate intermediate.

Question 8 of 19

Which structural feature is characteristic of eukaryotic mRNA?

Explanation: Eukaryotic mRNAs undergo post-transcriptional modification adding a 5'-7-methylguanosine cap and a 3'-poly(A) tail.

Question 9 of 19

Which base pairing rule applies to double-stranded regions of RNA?

Explanation: Adenine pairs with uracil (A-U) and guanine pairs with cytosine (G-C) via hydrogen bonding.

Question 10 of 19

What is the chemical difference between uracil and thymine?

Explanation: Thymine is 5-methyluracil; it possesses a methyl group at the C-5 position of the pyrimidine ring where uracil has a hydrogen atom.

Question 11 of 19

What does a nucleoside consist of?

Explanation: A nucleoside is composed of a nitrogenous base bonded to a pentose sugar without any phosphate group.

Question 12 of 19

Which enzyme catalyzes the synthesis of RNA from a DNA template?

Explanation: RNA polymerase synthesizes RNA transcripts using a DNA template strand during transcription.

Question 13 of 19

What type of bond links nucleotides together in the RNA polymer backbone?

Explanation: Adjacent nucleotides are joined by 3'-5' phosphodiester linkages.

Question 14 of 19

Which modified base is commonly found in tRNA molecules?

Explanation: Pseudouridine and dihydrouridine are typical modified nucleosides abundant in tRNA.

Question 15 of 19

Why is RNA generally single-stranded while DNA is double-stranded?

Explanation: The steric hindrance and electrostatic repulsion caused by the bulky 2'-OH group prevent stable B-form double helix formation in RNA.

Question 16 of 19

Which RNA fraction has the smallest molecular size and sedimentation coefficient?

Explanation: tRNA molecules are small, typically around 75-95 nucleotides, with a sedimentation coefficient of about 4S.

Question 17 of 19

What is the function of mRNA?

Explanation: mRNA carries the genetic code transcribed from DNA to ribosomes for translation into proteins.

Question 18 of 19

Which statement regarding Chargaff's rules and RNA is correct?

Explanation: Chargaff's base equivalence rules apply strictly to double-stranded nucleic acids, including double-stranded RNA viruses.

Question 19 of 19

The linkage between the phosphate group and the sugar hydroxyl in RNA is an:

Explanation: Phosphoric acid reacts with alcohol hydroxyl groups on the sugar to form phosphoester (phosphodiester) linkages.

Question 1 of 10
Which chemical feature differentiates ribose from 2-deoxyribose?

Solution: Ribose has a hydroxyl (-OH) group at the C-2' position, while 2-deoxyribose has only a hydrogen (-H) atom.

Question 2 of 10
What is the primary structural difference between uracil and thymine?

Solution: Thymine (5-methyluracil) contains a methyl group at C-5, which uracil lacks.

Question 3 of 10
Why does RNA undergo hydrolysis in dilute NaOH while DNA remains stable?

Solution: The 2'-OH group in ribose attacks the adjacent phosphodiester bond under alkaline conditions to form a cyclic phosphate.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following is classified as a nucleoside?

Solution: Adenosine is a nucleoside (adenine + ribose). The others contain phosphate groups.

Question 5 of 10
What is the approximate percentage of rRNA in total cellular RNA?

Solution: Ribosomal RNA makes up approximately 80% of total RNA in a cell.

Question 6 of 10
Which bond connects the 5'-carbon of one pentose sugar to the 3'-carbon of the next in RNA?

Solution: Nucleotides are linked together in RNA by 3'-5' phosphodiester bonds.

Question 7 of 10
Which base is NOT found in normal RNA transcripts?

Solution: Thymine is normally found in DNA, while uracil is its RNA counterpart.

Question 8 of 10
In the cloverleaf model of tRNA, which loop interacts directly with the mRNA codon?

Solution: The anticodon loop contains the 3-nucleotide sequence complementary to the mRNA codon.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following statements about RNA is incorrect?

Solution: Single-stranded RNA does not obey Chargaff's rules because bases are not paired in equal complementary ratios.

Question 10 of 10
What product is obtained upon complete acid hydrolysis of RNA?

Solution: Complete acid hydrolysis yields D-ribose sugar, phosphoric acid, and the nitrogenous bases (A, G, C, U).
