Preparation of Fructose

Fructose is a laevorotatory ketohexose commonly obtained via the hydrolysis of sucrose or inulin. It serves as an important structural isomer of glucose with a functional ketose group at C-2.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Hydrolysis of Sucrose for Fructose Production

  • Sucrose undergoes invert sugar production when treated with dilute mineral acids or the enzyme invertase.
  • The reaction yields an equimolar mixture of D-(+)-glucose and D-(-)-fructose.
  • Separation of fructose from glucose is achieved by forming insoluble calcium fructunate precipitate using calcium hydroxide.

Polymer Hydrolysis via Inulin

  • Inulin is a naturally occurring polysaccharide found in the roots of dahlias and chicory, composed entirely of fructose units.
  • Acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of inulin provides a high-purity source of D-fructose without the competing presence of glucose.
  • This industrial pathway avoids complex separation procedures required when starting from sucrose inversion mixtures.

Structural Characterization and Properties

  • Fructose exists predominantly as a furanose ring structure in combined states and crystalline forms, contrasting with the pyranose form of glucose.
  • It acts as a reducing sugar despite being a ketose because of isomerization to aldoses via enediol intermediates in basic media.
  • Specific rotation of natural D-fructose is -92.4°, rendering it strongly laevorotatory.

Conditions: Acetic anhydride, pyridine catalyst
Units: Pentaacetate derivative formation
Hydrolysis of Sucrose
C12H22O11 + H2O → C6H12O6 (Glucose) + C6H12O6 (Fructose)
Conditions: Dilute HCl or H2SO4, alcoholic medium
Units: Stoichiometric molar ratios (1:1)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Ketoses are generally non-reducing sugars due to the absence of a free aldehyde group.

Observation: Fructose is a reducing sugar that reduces Tollens' and Fehling's reagents. — In alkaline solution, fructose undergoes tautomerization via an enediol intermediate to form glucose and mannose, exposing a reactive aldehyde group.

Exception Monosaccharides with different configuration at C-1 and C-2 form distinct osazones.

Observation: Glucose, fructose, and mannose yield the exact same osazone derivative. — The reaction involves C-1 and C-2 carbons where structural differences are neutralized during the formation of phenylhydrazone linkages.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Fructose is a laevorotatory ketohexose with the molecular formula C6H12O6.
  • Hydrolysis of sucrose with dilute acid gives an equimolar mixture of glucose and fructose known as invert sugar.
  • Calcium hydroxide is used to separate fructose from invert sugar by precipitating calcium fructunate.
  • Inulin serves as the main polysaccharide raw material for the industrial production of pure fructose.
  • Fructose forms a pentaacetate derivative upon treatment with acetic anhydride, confirming the presence of five hydroxyl groups.
  • Fructose gives a positive Tollens' test because it tautomerizes to an aldose under alkaline test conditions.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming fructose is a non-reducing sugar because it contains a ketone group instead of an aldehyde group.
  • Confusing the specific rotation sign of fructose (-92.4°) with that of glucose (+52.5°).
  • Forgetting that glucose, fructose, and mannose produce the identical osazone crystal structure.
  • Mistaking the furanose ring form in combined fructose for the pyranose form predominantly found in free crystal glucose.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation of Fructose

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Sucrose Hydrolysis and Invert Sugar

Sucrose is hydrolyzed into D-glucose and D-fructose. The resulting mixture shows a net inversion of optical rotation from dextro to laevo.

Industrial Inulin Pathway

Polymeric inulin undergoes complete cleavage via acid or enzymatic hydrolysis to yield pure D-fructose without glucose contamination.

Isolation via Calcium Fructunate

Selective precipitation using calcium hydroxide isolates fructose from invert sugar solutions, followed by acidification with carbon dioxide.

Structural Features

Fructose possesses a C-2 keto group, five hydroxyl groups, and exhibits mutarotation between alpha and beta cyclic forms.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Invert Sugar (C12H22O11 hydrolysis product)
Equimolar mixture of D-glucose and D-fructose.
Inulin ((C6H10O5)n)
Poly-fructose reserve carbohydrate found in chicory roots.
Furanose (5-membered ring)
Cyclic hemiacetal/hemiketal structure containing four carbons and one oxygen.
Mutarotation (Δ[α])
Spontaneous change in specific optical rotation of an aqueous solution over time.
Osazone (Bis-phenylhydrazone derivative)
Yellow crystalline derivative formed by reaction with excess phenylhydrazine.
Calcium Fructunate (Complex salt)
Insoluble calcium-fructose complex used for sugar separation.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Fructose formula: C6H12O6 with a C-2 keto group.
  • Specific rotation of D-fructose is -92.4°.
  • Invert sugar rotation changes from +66.5° to negative values.
  • Glucose, fructose, and mannose give the same osazone.
  • Fructose reduces Fehling's solution via enediol rearrangement.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Fructose is prepared commercially by hydrolyzing sucrose or inulin.
  • Separation relies on calcium fructunate precipitation.
  • It is a ketohexose that exhibits reducing properties in alkaline conditions.
  • It forms a 5-membered furanose ring structure in combined states.

⚖️ Key Differences

Glucose Ring Size VS Fructose Ring Size

Glucose forms a 6-membered pyranose ring, whereas combined fructose forms a 5-membered furanose ring.

Question 1 of 18

Which reagent is used to separate fructose from an invert sugar mixture?

Explanation: Calcium hydroxide reacts with fructose to form an insoluble calcium fructunate precipitate, enabling separation from glucose.

Question 2 of 18

What is the optical rotation nature of naturally occurring D-fructose?

Explanation: Natural D-fructose rotates plane-polarized light to the left and is designated as laevorotatory with [α] = -92.4°.

Question 3 of 18

Hydrolysis of inulin yields exclusively which monosaccharide?

Explanation: Inulin is a fructose polymer (fructan); hence, its complete hydrolysis yields only D-fructose units.

Question 4 of 18

Why does fructose give a positive test with Tollens' reagent despite being a ketone?

Explanation: In alkaline Tollens' solution, fructose tautomerizes via an enediol intermediate to form glucose and mannose, exposing a reactive aldehyde group.

Question 5 of 18

The term 'invert sugar' refers to:

Explanation: Hydrolysis of sucrose inverts the optical rotation from positive to negative, producing an equimolar mixture called invert sugar.

Question 6 of 18

How many moles of phenylhydrazine are consumed per mole of fructose to form osazone?

Explanation: Three moles of phenylhydrazine are consumed to oxidize C-1/C-2 and form the stable osazone crystal structure.

Question 7 of 18

Fructose reacts with acetic anhydride to form a pentaacetate. This indicates the presence of how many hydroxyl groups?

Explanation: Formation of a pentaacetate derivative confirms the presence of five hydroxyl (-OH) groups in the fructose molecule.

Question 8 of 18

What ring structure does free crystalline fructose predominantly adopt?

Explanation: In its free crystalline state, fructose predominantly exists as a 6-membered pyranose ring, though it adopts a furanose ring in combined states like sucrose.

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following carbohydrates is a ketohexose?

Explanation: Fructose is a 6-carbon monosaccharide containing a ketonic carbonyl group at C-2.

Question 10 of 18

The chemical formula for fructose is:

Explanation: Fructose is a hexose monosaccharide with the molecular formula C6H12O6.

Question 11 of 18

Which enzyme converts sucrose into invert sugar?

Explanation: Invertase (or sucrase) specifically catalyzes the hydrolysis of sucrose into glucose and fructose.

Question 12 of 18

Which carbohydrate yields the same osazone as D-fructose?

Explanation: D-glucose, D-fructose, and D-mannose all form identical osazone crystals because structural differences at C-1 and C-2 are eliminated during the reaction.

Question 13 of 18

What is the primary commercial source for laboratory preparation of fructose on a small scale?

Explanation: Sucrose is readily available and hydrolyzed to produce an invert sugar mixture containing fructose.

Question 14 of 18

The functional group characteristic of fructose in its open-chain form is:

Explanation: Fructose is a ketose sugar containing a carbonyl group located at the C-2 position.

Question 15 of 18

What happens when calcium fructunate is treated with carbon dioxide?

Explanation: Passing carbon dioxide through calcium fructunate suspension precipitates insoluble calcium carbonate and releases free D-fructose into solution.

Question 16 of 18

Which property distinguishes D-glucose from D-fructose?

Explanation: Glucose is an aldohexose and dextrorotatory, whereas fructose is a ketohexose and laevorotatory.

Question 17 of 18

Which carbon atom carries the carbonyl oxygen in open-chain D-fructose?

Explanation: By definition of a 2-ketohexose, the ketonic carbonyl group is situated at C-2.

Question 18 of 18

The specific rotation of pure sucrose is +66.5°. After complete acid hydrolysis, the optical rotation becomes:

Explanation: Since D-fructose (-92.4°) has a higher absolute specific rotation than D-glucose (+52.5°), the net rotation of the invert mixture becomes negative.
