Classification of Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or polyhydroxy ketones, or substances that yield these on hydrolysis, classified based on their behavior towards hydrolysis and functional groups. They serve as primary energy sources and structural components in biological systems.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification Based on Hydrolysis Behavior

  • Monosaccharides cannot be hydrolyzed into simpler polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones; examples include glucose, fructose, and galactose.
  • Oligosaccharides yield 2 to 10 monosaccharide molecules upon hydrolysis, with disaccharides (e.g., sucrose, lactose, maltose) being the most common.
  • Polysaccharides are high molecular weight polymers that yield a large number of monosaccharide units on hydrolysis, such as starch, cellulose, and glycogen.

Classification Based on Functional Groups and Carbon Atoms

  • Carbohydrates containing an aldehyde group (-CHO) are classified as aldoses, while those containing a keto group (>C=O) are classified as ketoses.
  • Monosaccharides are further sub-classified based on the total number of carbon atoms into trioses (C3), tetroses (C4), pentoses (C5), hexoses (C6), and heptoses (C7).
  • Combination nomenclature explicitly defines both features, such as aldohexose (e.g., glucose) or ketohexose (e.g., fructose).

Classification Based on Reducing Property

  • Reducing sugars possess free aldehyde or ketone groups capable of reducing Tollens' reagent, Fehling's solution, or Benedict's solution; all monosaccharides and most disaccharides (except sucrose) fall here.
  • Non-reducing sugars lack free reducing groups because their anomeric carbon atoms are involved in glycosidic bond formation; sucrose is a prime example.
  • Reducing disaccharides feature a hemiacetal or hemiketal hydroxyl group free on at least one monomer unit, permitting mutarotation.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
General Formula of Disaccharides
Cn(H2O)n-1
Conditions: Hydration state resulting from the condensation of two monosaccharides with loss of one water molecule
Units: n = total number of carbon atoms
General Formula of Polysaccharides
(C6H10O5)n
Conditions: Polymerization of hexose units with the elimination of water
Units: n = degree of polymerization
Optical Isomerism Limit
N = 2n
Conditions: For aldoses with different chiral centers
Units: N = maximum number of optically active isomers, n = number of asymmetric carbon atoms
General Formula of Monosaccharides
Cn(H2O)n
Conditions: Valid for most simple aldoses and ketoses where n ≥ 3
Units: n = integer representing number of carbon atoms
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception All carbohydrates strictly follow the stoichiometric formula Cn(H2O)n.

Observation: Deoxyribose (C5H10O4) and rhamnose (C6H12O5) — They contain fewer oxygen atoms than the standard hydrate ratio due to reduction of specific hydroxyl groups.

Exception All compounds matching Cn(H2O)n are carbohydrates.

Observation: Formaldehyde (CH2O), acetic acid (C2H4O2), and lactic acid (C3H6O3) — They do not possess the polyhydroxy aldehyde or polyhydroxy ketone structural requirement of carbohydrates.

Exception All disaccharides are reducing sugars due to the presence of potential free carbonyl groups.

Observation: Sucrose — In sucrose, the reducing groups of both glucose (C1) and fructose (C2) are involved in glycosidic bond formation (alpha-D-glucopyranosyl-(1→2)-beta-D-fructofuranoside).

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Aldose vs Ketose Carbon Count Mnemonic
Glyceraldehyde (3) → Erythrose (4) → Ribose (5) → Glucose (6)

Application: Remember the sequence of carbon lengths to quickly determine molecular weights and isomer counts using N = 2n.

Reducing Sugar Identification Rule
Free Anomeric Carbon = Reducing Sugar

Application: Check if C1 (aldose) or C2 (ketose) is free or linked in a glycosidic bond to immediately determine reducing capability.

Disaccharide Hydrolysis Component Trick
Sucrose → Glu + Fru | Lactose → Glu + Gal | Maltose → Glu + Glu

Application: Use the acronym SLM = G-F, G-Gal, G-G for quick hydrolysis product identification in exams.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Glucose is an aldohexose containing one aldehyde group and five hydroxyl groups.
  • Fructose is a ketohexose containing one ketose group at C2 and five hydroxyl groups.
  • Cellulose is a linear polysaccharide of β-D-glucose units linked by β-(1→4) glycosidic bonds.
  • Starch consists of two fractions: water-soluble amylose (15-20%) and water-insoluble amylopectin (80-85%).
  • Amylose is a linear polymer of α-D-glucose with α-(1→4) linkages, whereas amylopectin is branched with α-(1→6) branch points.
  • Glycogen is structurally similar to amylopectin but is more highly branched (every 8-12 glucose units).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming all sugars ending in '-ose' are reducing; sucrose is non-reducing.
  • Confusing the glycosidic linkage configuration (α vs β) in starch versus cellulose.
  • Classifying formaldehyde as a carbohydrate simply because it fits the Cn(H2O)n empirical formula.
  • Neglecting the difference between structural polysaccharides (cellulose) and storage polysaccharides (starch, glycogen).

⚡ Quick Revision: Classification of Carbohydrates

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Definition and Structural Basis

Carbohydrates are classified chemically as polyhydroxy aldehydes or polyhydroxy ketones. Their classification depends primarily on hydrolysis products into monosaccharides, oligosaccharides, and polysaccharides.

Monosaccharide Classification

Categorized by functional group into aldoses and ketoses, and by carbon content from trioses to heptoses. They serve as the fundamental building blocks of all higher carbohydrates.

Oligosaccharides and Glycosidic Linkages

Disaccharides like sucrose, maltose, and lactose are formed by condensation reactions between monosaccharide units joined through covalent ether linkages known as glycosidic bonds.

Polysaccharides and Biological Roles

High molecular weight polymers including starch, glycogen, and cellulose fulfill critical roles in energy storage and structural integrity across plant and animal kingdoms.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Monosaccharide (CnH2nOn)
Simplest carbohydrate unit that cannot be hydrolyzed further.
Oligosaccharide (Cn(H2O)n-1)
Carbohydrate yielding 2 to 10 monosaccharide units on hydrolysis.
Polysaccharide ((C6H10O5)n)
Polymeric carbohydrate yielding numerous monosaccharides upon hydrolysis.
Reducing Sugar (Free -CHO or >C=O)
Sugar capable of reducing metal ions in Tollens' or Fehling's reagents.
Glycosidic Linkage (C-O-C bridge)
Ether bond joining two monosaccharide units together.
Mutarotation ([α]D change)
Spontaneous change in specific rotation of an optically active solution.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • General monosaccharide formula: Cn(H2O)n
  • Sucrose is a non-reducing disaccharide composed of glucose and fructose.
  • Cellulose contains β-(1→4) glycosidic linkages.
  • Starch is a mixture of amylose and amylopectin.
  • Deoxyribose violates the exact Cn(H2O)n stoichiometric ratio.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carbohydrates are classified by hydrolysis behavior into mono-, oligo-, and polysaccharides.
  • Functional group classification divides them into aldoses and ketoses.
  • Reducing property depends on the presence of free hemiacetal or hemiketal groups.
  • Glycosidic bonds link individual monosaccharide units into larger chains.

⚖️ Key Differences

Amylose VS Amylopectin

Amylose is linear with α-(1→4) bonds; amylopectin is branched with additional α-(1→6) linkages.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following compounds is classified as a non-reducing sugar?

Explanation: Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar because both anomeric carbons of glucose and fructose are involved in the glycosidic bond, leaving no free aldehyde or ketone group.

Question 2 of 18

What are the hydrolysis products of lactose?

Explanation: Lactose is a disaccharide that upon hydrolysis yields one molecule of D-glucose and one molecule of D-galactose.

Question 3 of 18

Which carbohydrate does not correspond to the general formula Cn(H2O)n?

Explanation: 2-Deoxyribose has the molecular formula C5H10O4, lacking one oxygen atom compared to the standard Cn(H2O)n formula.

Question 4 of 18

Starch is a polymer composed of:

Explanation: Starch is composed of amylose and amylopectin, both of which are polymers made entirely of α-D-glucose units.

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following is a ketohexose?

Explanation: Fructose contains a ketonic carbonyl group at the C-2 position and has six carbon atoms, making it a ketohexose.

Question 6 of 18

Cellulose differs structurally from starch in that cellulose contains:

Explanation: Cellulose consists of linear chains of β-D-glucose units linked exclusively by β-(1→4)-glycosidic bonds.

Question 7 of 18

How many optical isomers are theoretically possible for an aldohexose with 4 asymmetric carbon atoms?

Explanation: Using the formula 2n where n = 4, the number of optical isomers is 24 = 16.

Question 8 of 18

Which carbohydrate is also known as blood sugar?

Explanation: Glucose is the primary circulating sugar in human blood, commonly referred to as blood sugar.

Question 9 of 18

Which reagent is used to distinguish between reducing and non-reducing sugars?

Explanation: Fehling's solution reacts with reducing sugars to form a red precipitate of cuprous oxide, remaining unreactive with non-reducing sugars.

Question 10 of 18

Amylopectin is a polymer containing:

Explanation: Amylopectin is branched; the main chains are formed by α-(1→4) linkages, while branch points are formed by α-(1→6) linkages.

Question 11 of 18

Oligosaccharides on hydrolysis yield how many monosaccharide units?

Explanation: By definition, oligosaccharides yield between 2 and 10 monosaccharide molecules upon complete hydrolysis.

Question 12 of 18

Which sugar is sweetest among natural sugars?

Explanation: Fructose is a monosaccharide found in fruits and honey that exhibits the highest relative sweetness among natural sugars.

Question 13 of 18

In polysaccharides, monosaccharide units are joined together by which type of bond?

Explanation: Monosaccharide units in di-, oligo-, and polysaccharides are linked via covalent ether bonds called glycosidic linkages.

Question 14 of 18

Glycogen is stored primarily in which organs of animals?

Explanation: Animals store excess glucose in the form of glycogen primarily within the liver and skeletal muscle tissues.

Question 15 of 18

Which functional group is characteristic of an aldose?

Explanation: Aldoses contain a terminal aldehyde group (-CHO).

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following is a disaccharide?

Explanation: Maltose is a disaccharide composed of two α-D-glucose units.

Question 17 of 18

Which component of starch is soluble in water?

Explanation: Amylose is the linear, water-soluble fraction of starch, whereas amylopectin is insoluble.

Question 18 of 18

Which carbohydrate is indigestible by humans due to the lack of specific enzymes?

Explanation: Humans lack the enzyme required to hydrolyze β-(1→4) glycosidic linkages present in cellulose.

Question 1 of 10
Which functional group distinction separates aldoses from ketoses?

Solution: Aldoses contain an aldehyde (-CHO) functional group, whereas ketoses contain a ketone (>C=O) functional group.

Question 2 of 10
What is the primary structural difference between maltose and cellobiose?

Solution: Both are disaccharides of glucose, but maltose features an α-(1→4) glycosidic bond while cellobiose features a β-(1→4) glycosidic bond.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following is classified as a pentose monosaccharide?

Solution: Ribose contains five carbon atoms, making it a pentose sugar.

Question 4 of 10
Why is sucrose a non-reducing sugar while maltose is a reducing sugar?

Solution: In sucrose, both C1 of glucose and C2 of fructose are linked in the glycosidic bond, whereas maltose has a free anomeric carbon at C1 of the second glucose unit.

Question 5 of 10
Which polysaccharide serves as the primary energy reserve in animals?

Solution: Glycogen is the principal storage carbohydrate in animals, functioning analogously to starch in plants.

Question 6 of 10
How many chiral carbon atoms are present in an open-chain aldohexose like D-glucose?

Solution: In open-chain D-glucose (CH2OH-CH(OH)-CH(OH)-CH(OH)-CH(OH)-CHO), carbons 2, 3, 4, and 5 are asymmetric (chiral), giving a total of 4.

Question 7 of 10
Which test confirms the presence of reducing sugars?

Solution: Fehling's test produces a red precipitate of Cu2O when heated with reducing sugars.

Question 8 of 10
Which carbohydrate is composed of repeating units of D-fructose and is used to test kidney function?

Solution: Inulin is a polymer of fructose units (fructan) used clinically to measure glomerular filtration rate.

Question 9 of 10
What type of linkage connects monomer units in amylose?

Solution: Amylose is a linear polymer where D-glucose units are joined exclusively by α-(1→4)-glycosidic linkages.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following represents a triose carbohydrate?

Solution: Glyceraldehyde contains three carbon atoms, making it the simplest aldotriose.
