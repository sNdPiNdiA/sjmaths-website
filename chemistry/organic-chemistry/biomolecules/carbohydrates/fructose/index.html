Organic Chemistry

Fructose

Fructose is a naturally occurring ketohexose with the molecular formula C6H12O6, existing predominantly in cyclic furanose conformations in combined forms. It functions as a reducing sugar due to keto-enol tautomerism allowing isomerization to glucose and mannose in alkaline media.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Stereochemistry and Cyclic Structure of Fructose

  • D-Fructose contains a ketone group at C-2 and five hydroxyl groups, classifying it structurally as a 2-ketohexose.
  • In aqueous solution, it predominantly forms a five-membered furanose ring (alpha-D-fructofuranose and beta-D-fructofuranose).
  • The cyclic hemiacetal (technically hemiketal) formation involves the nucleophilic attack of the C-5 hydroxyl group on the C-2 carbonyl carbon.
  • Fructose exhibits mutarotation, where the specific rotation of freshly prepared solution shifts from -133.5° to an equilibrium value of -92.4°.

Chemical Reactions and Reducing Property

  • Despite containing a ketone group, fructose acts as a reducing sugar because it undergoes Lobry de Bruyn-Alberda van Ekenstein transformation into glucose and mannose under basic conditions.
  • Fructose reacts with Tollens' and Fehling's reagents, reducing them due to the intermediate formation of aldose structures in alkaline medium.
  • Treatment with excess phenylhydrazine yields glucosazone, identical to the osazone formed from D-glucose and D-mannose due to identical configurations at C-3, C-4, and C-5.
  • Catalytic hydrogenation or reduction with sodium borohydride (NaBH4) yields a mixture of two alditols: D-sorbitol (D-glucitol) and D-mannitol.

Derivatives and Polysaccharide Constitution

  • Fructose forms crystalline osazones with melting points around 205°C, serving as a diagnostic derivative for identification.
  • Methylation followed by hydrolysis yields 1,3,4,6-tetra-O-methyl-D-fructose, confirming its furanose ring structure in sucrose.
  • Polymers of fructose linked by β-(2→1) glycosidic bonds are known as inulins, found in roots of dahlias and chicory.
  • Sucrose is a non-reducing disaccharide composed of α-D-glucopyranose and β-D-fructofuranose linked via an α-1,2β-glycosidic bond.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Ketones typically do not reduce Tollens' or Fehling's reagents.

Observation: Fructose is a ketose that actively reduces Tollens' and Fehling's solutions. — In alkaline test media, fructose undergoes rapid tautomerization into glucose and mannose via an enediol intermediate, exposing a reactive aldehyde group.

Exception Monosaccharides predominantly form six-membered pyranose rings in the free crystalline state.

Observation: Crystalline D-fructose exists predominantly as a furanose ring. — The thermodynamic stability of crystalline D-fructofuranose overrides pyranose formation in the solid state, though both forms coexist in solution.

Exception Different starting hexoses yield different osazone crystals with distinct stereochemical compositions.

Observation: D-glucose, D-fructose, and D-mannose yield the exact same osazone crystals. — The reaction involves oxidation and derivatization of C-1 and C-2, eliminating the stereochemical differences at these two centers while leaving C-3, C-4, and C-5 unchanged.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Fructose is commonly known as levulose due to its strong laevorotatory property ([α]D = -92.4°).
  • It is the sweetest naturally occurring carbohydrate among all common dietary sugars.
  • Seliwanoff's test uses resorcinol in concentrated HCl to distinguish ketohexoses (rapid red color) from aldohexoses.
  • Reduction of fructose with NaBH4 yields a mixture of two epimeric alditols: D-sorbitol and D-mannitol.
  • Sucrose is hydrolyzed by the enzyme invertase to equimolar mixtures of D-glucose and D-fructose.
  • Fructose contains one primary alcohol group at C-1 and another primary-like environment at C-6, with secondary alcohols at C-3, C-4, and C-5.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming fructose is a non-reducing sugar because it contains a ketone functional group.
  • Confusing the furanose ring structure of crystalline fructose with the pyranose structure predominant in aqueous equilibrium.
  • Failing to account for mutarotation changes when calculating specific rotation values in freshly prepared solutions.
  • Forgetting that osazone formation consumes three moles of phenylhydrazine and releases one mole of aniline and ammonia.

⚡ Quick Revision: Fructose

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Classification

D-Fructose is a 2-ketohexose containing six carbon atoms, classified structurally as a monosaccharide with formula C6H12O6.

Cyclic Forms and Mutarotation

Fructose forms alpha and beta anomers predominantly in the furanose form in crystals and exhibits mutarotation in aqueous solutions.

Reducing Characteristics

Fructose reduces Tollens' and Fehling's reagents via base-catalyzed isomerization to reactive aldoses.

Analytical Identification

Seliwanoff's test and phenylhydrazine osazone precipitation serve as standard identification techniques for fructose.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Levulose (D-Fructose)
Historical name for fructose reflecting its laevorotatory optical activity.
Furanose (5-membered ring)
Cyclic hemiacetal/hemiketal structure containing four carbons and one oxygen.
Mutarotation ([α] shift)
Spontaneous change in optical rotation when a pure anomer dissolves in water.
Inulin ((C6H10O5)n)
Polymer of D-fructofuranose units linked by β-(2→1) bonds.
Osazone (Bis-phenylhydrazone)
Crystalline derivative formed by reaction of sugars with excess phenylhydrazine.
Epimer (Stereoisomer)
Diastereomers that differ in configuration at only one chiral carbon center.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Molecular formula: C6H12O6
  • Specific rotation: -92.4°
  • Functional group: Ketone at C-2
  • Test reagent for ketoses: Seliwanoff's Reagent
  • Reduction products: D-sorbitol and D-mannitol

📌 Quick Recall

  • Fructose is a ketohexose exhibiting reducing properties due to enediol tautomerism.
  • It forms furanose rings in crystals and mutarotates in solution.
  • It yields the same osazone as glucose and mannose.
  • It forms the fructose unit in sucrose linked via C-2.

⚖️ Key Differences

Glucose VS Fructose

Glucose is an aldohexose with a pyranose ring preference, while fructose is a ketohexose with a furanose crystal structure preference.

Question 1 of 19

What is the structural classification of D-fructose?

Explanation: D-Fructose contains six carbon atoms and a ketone carbonyl group at carbon-2, making it a ketohexose.

Question 2 of 19

Why does fructose give a positive Tollens' test despite being a ketone?

Explanation: In the alkaline Tollens' reagent, fructose undergoes Lobry de Bruyn-Alberda van Ekenstein transformation into glucose and mannose, which possess aldehyde groups.

Question 3 of 19

What is the primary ring structure of crystalline D-fructose?

Explanation: In the solid crystalline state, D-fructose adopts a five-membered furanose ring structure (beta-D-fructofuranose).

Question 4 of 19

Which reagent is specifically used to distinguish ketohexoses from aldohexoses?

Explanation: Seliwanoff's test utilizes resorcinol and HCl; ketohexoses dehydrate rapidly to form a deep red complex faster than aldohexoses.

Question 5 of 19

What is the product formed upon reduction of D-fructose with sodium borohydride?

Explanation: Reduction of the C-2 carbonyl group creates a new chiral center, yielding a mixture of two epimeric alditols: D-sorbitol (D-glucitol) and D-mannitol.

Question 6 of 19

Which sugars yield the exact same osazone derivative upon reaction with excess phenylhydrazine?

Explanation: Glucose, fructose, and mannose differ only in configuration at C-1 and C-2, which are the carbons consumed during osazone formation.

Question 7 of 19

What is the specific rotation value of D-fructose at equilibrium in water?

Explanation: Equilibrium aqueous solution of D-fructose displays a specific optical rotation of -92.4°, giving it the historical name levulose.

Question 8 of 19

What type of glycosidic linkage connects glucose and fructose units in sucrose?

Explanation: Sucrose links C-1 of α-D-glucopyranose with C-2 of β-D-fructofuranose via an α-1,2β-glycosidic bond.

Question 9 of 19

What is inulin?

Explanation: Inulin is a naturally occurring storage polysaccharide composed of linear chains of β-(2→1) linked D-fructofuranose units.

Question 10 of 19

How many chiral centers are present in an open-chain molecule of D-fructose?

Explanation: Open-chain D-fructose has chiral carbons at C-3, C-4, and C-5, totaling 3 chiral centers (C-1 and C-6 are primary carbons, C-2 is a carbonyl carbon).

Question 11 of 19

What color is observed in a positive Seliwanoff's test for fructose?

Explanation: Seliwanoff's test produces a characteristic deep red color within minutes for ketohexoses due to hydroxymethylfurfural condensation.

Question 12 of 19

Which carbon atom in D-fructose contains the ketone functional group in the open-chain form?

Explanation: By definition, 2-ketohexoses feature the carbonyl ketone functionality located at the second carbon atom (C-2).

Question 13 of 19

What term describes the change in optical rotation of a freshly dissolved fructose solution over time?

Explanation: Mutarotation is the spontaneous shift in optical rotation as an anomer interconverts to an equilibrium mixture in solution.

Question 14 of 19

Which enzyme catalyzes the hydrolysis of sucrose into glucose and fructose?

Explanation: Invertase specifically hydrolyzes sucrose, a reaction historically called the inversion of cane sugar.

Question 15 of 19

What is the molecular formula of fructose?

Explanation: Fructose is a hexose monosaccharide with the standard hexose molecular formula C6H12O6.

Question 16 of 19

Which structural feature differentiates D-fructose from D-glucose in an open-chain Fischer projection?

Explanation: Glucose features an aldehyde carbonyl at C-1, whereas fructose features a ketone carbonyl at C-2.

Question 17 of 19

What is formed when fructose is treated with concentrated mineral acids upon heating?

Explanation: Dehydration of ketohexoses like fructose with concentrated acids leads to the formation of hydroxymethylfurfural, which further hydrolyzes to levulinic acid and formic acid.

Question 18 of 19

Why is fructose classified as a monosaccharide?

Explanation: Monosaccharides are simple carbohydrates that cannot be hydrolyzed into smaller polyhydroxy aldehyde or ketone units.

Question 19 of 19

What is the primary commercial source of fructose?

Explanation: Fructose occurs widely along with glucose in honey, tree fruits, and berries, and is produced commercially as high-fructose corn syrup.

Question 1 of 10
Which statement is incorrect regarding D-fructose?

Solution: Fructose is a reducing sugar, not a non-reducing sugar.

Question 2 of 10
The conversion of D-fructose into D-glucose and D-mannose in alkaline solution is known as:

Solution: The base-catalyzed interconversion of monosaccharides via an enediol intermediate is the Lobry de Bruyn-Alberda van Ekenstein transformation.

Question 3 of 10
How many moles of phenylhydrazine are consumed per mole of fructose to form osazone?

Solution: Osazone formation requires 3 moles of phenylhydrazine: one for oxidation at C-1/C-2 and two for hydrazone formation.

Question 4 of 10
Catalytic reduction of D-fructose produces:

Solution: Reduction of the C-2 ketone group creates a new chiral center, generating an epimeric pair of sugar alcohols: sorbitol and mannitol.

Question 5 of 10
Which ring structure is predominantly present in sucrose?

Solution: Sucrose is composed of an α-D-glucopyranose unit linked to a β-D-fructofuranose unit.

Question 6 of 10
What is the chemical nature of Seliwanoff's reagent?

Solution: Seliwanoff's reagent consists of resorcinol dissolved in concentrated aqueous hydrochloric acid.

Question 7 of 10
Why does sucrose fail to reduce Fehling's solution?

Solution: Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar because its glycosidic bond ties up both anomeric carbons, preventing exposure of free aldehyde or ketone groups.

Question 8 of 10
Which functional group is absent in open-chain D-fructose?

Solution: D-fructose is a ketose and contains a ketone group at C-2, lacking an aldehyde group in its open-chain structure.

Question 9 of 10
What is the product of complete methylation of D-fructose followed by acid hydrolysis?

Solution: Methylation of furanose fructose leaves hydroxyls at C-1, C-3, C-4, and C-6 free after hydrolysis, yielding 1,3,4,6-tetra-O-methyl-D-fructose.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following is true regarding mutarotation of fructose?

Solution: Mutarotation is the equilibrium interconversion of alpha and beta ring isomers passing through the open-chain carbonyl form in solution.
