Organic Chemistry

Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or substances that yield such compounds on hydrolysis, classified as monosaccharides, oligosaccharides, and polysaccharides. They serve as primary energy sources and structural components in living systems.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification and Stereochemistry of Monosaccharides

  • Monosaccharides are classified based on the number of carbon atoms (triose, tetrose, pentose, hexose) and the functional group (aldose or ketose).
  • The D- and L-configurations are assigned relative to glyceraldehyde, based on the stereogenic center furthest from the principal carbonyl group.
  • Anomers are diastereomers that differ in configuration only at the C-1 carbon (hemiacetal carbon in aldoses) known as the anomeric carbon.
  • Cyclic pyranose and furanose ring structures exist in equilibrium via mutarotation, exhibiting characteristic specific rotation changes in aqueous solution.

Chemical Reactions and Functional Group Transformations

  • Aldoses and ketoses reduce Tollens' reagent, Fehling's solution, and Benedict's solution due to tautomerization to enediols, acting as reducing sugars.
  • Reaction with hydroxylamine forms oximes, and further reduction with sodium amalgam yields corresponding polyalcohols (alditols).
  • Bromine water selectively oxidizes the aldehyde group of aldoses to aldonic acids, whereas nitric acid oxidizes both terminal groups to dicarboxylic glucaric acids.
  • Phenylhydrazine reacts with monosaccharides to form crystalline derivatives called osazones, consuming three moles of reagent per mole of sugar.

Oligosaccharides and Polysaccharides

  • Disaccharides like sucrose, maltose, and lactose are linked via glycosidic linkages formed by the elimination of a water molecule between two monosaccharide units.
  • Sucrose is a non-reducing disaccharide (α-D-glucopyranosyl-(1→2)-β-D-fructofuranoside) due to the involvement of both anomeric carbons in the glycosidic bond.
  • Starch is composed of two fractions: water-soluble amylose (linear α-1,4-linkages) and water-insoluble amylopectin (branched α-1,4 and α-1,6 linkages).
  • Cellulose is a structural polysaccharide consisting of linear chains of β-D-glucose units joined by β-1,4-glycosidic linkages, rendering it indigestible by humans.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception All monosaccharides containing a free aldehyde or ketone group act as reducing sugars.

Observation: Sucrose is a disaccharide containing glucose and fructose units, yet it is a non-reducing sugar. — The glycosidic linkage involves the anomeric carbons of both glucose (C-1) and fructose (C-2), leaving no free hemiacetal or hemiketal groups to tautomerize.

Exception Carbohydrates are generally defined by the empirical formula Cx(H2O)y.

Observation: Deoxyribose (C5H10O4) and rhamnose (C6H12O5) are carbohydrates that do not strictly fit this stoichiometric ratio. — They contain fewer oxygen atoms than hydrogen-to-oxygen water equivalents due to deoxygenation reactions during biosynthesis.

Exception Cellulose and starch are both polymers built exclusively from D-glucose monomer units.

Observation: Cellulose provides rigid structural support in plants, whereas starch serves as an energy reserve. — The stereochemistry of the glycosidic linkage (β-1,4 in cellulose vs α-1,4 in starch) causes drastically different supramolecular hydrogen bonding and chain conformations.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
COR (Carbonyl Oxidation Rule)
Br2 water → Aldonic Acid; HNO3 → Aldaric Acid

Application: Quickly identify oxidation products of aldoses based on the strength of the oxidizing agent.

Osazone Carbon Identity Trick
C-1 and C-2 are involved in osazone crystal formation

Application: D-glucose, D-mannose, and D-fructose yield the exact same osazone because configurations at C-3, C-4, C-5, and C-6 remain unaltered.

Mutarotation Indicator
Specific Rotation Changes over time until equilibrium

Application: Any sugar showing mutarotation must have a free or accessible hemiacetal/hemiketal group (anomeric carbon with -OH).

🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming ketoses do not give positive tests with Tollens' or Fehling's reagents; ketoses undergo keto-enol tautomerization in alkaline medium to form reactive aldoses.
  • Confusing anomers with epimers; anomers differ strictly at the anomeric carbon (C-1 for aldoses), while epimers can differ at any chiral center.
  • Forgetting that sucrose undergoes inversion of optical rotation upon hydrolysis, turning from dextrorotatory to laevorotatory.
  • Misidentifying the glycosidic bond orientation in cellulose as alpha instead of beta.

⚡ Quick Revision: Carbohydrates

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Classification and Structure

Carbohydrates are classified as monosaccharides, oligosaccharides, and polysaccharides based on hydrolysis behavior. Monosaccharides contain active hydroxyl and carbonyl groups capable of forming stable cyclic hemiacetals.

Stereochemical Nomenclature

Stereogenic centers dictate D/L configuration based on the highest-numbered chiral carbon. Cyclic forms introduce anomerism (α and β forms) characterized by mutarotation.

Chemical Reactions

Characteristic reactions include reduction to alditols, oxidation to aldonic or aldaric acids, osazone formation, and acylation/alkylation of hydroxyl groups.

Structural Polysaccharides

Starch (amylose and amylopectin) and glycogen serve as energy storage reserves, whereas cellulose provides structural rigidity through extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Aldose (R-CHO)
A monosaccharide containing an aldehyde functional group.
Ketose (R-CO-R)
A monosaccharide containing a ketone functional group.
Anomer (C-1 epimer)
Stereoisomers differing in configuration at the hemiacetal anomeric carbon.
Mutarotation ([α]D change)
Spontaneous change in optical rotation of a freshly prepared sugar solution.
Glycosidic Linkage (C-O-C bond)
Ether linkage joining monosaccharide units in di-, oligo-, and polysaccharides.
Invert Sugar (Equimolar glucose + fructose)
Hydrolysis product of sucrose showing inversion of optical rotation.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Glucose formula: C6H12O6
  • Sucrose formula: C12H22O11
  • Cellulose linkage: β-1,4-glycosidic bond
  • Starch amylose linkage: α-1,4-glycosidic bond
  • Tollens' reagent: Ammoniacal silver nitrate solution

📌 Quick Recall

  • Monosaccharides exhibit optical isomerism and mutarotation.
  • Reducing sugars contain free or masked hemiacetal groups.
  • Disaccharides consist of two monosaccharides linked by glycosidic bonds.
  • Polysaccharides function as energy reserves or structural supports.

⚖️ Key Differences

Amylose VS Amylopectin

Amylose is linear with α-1,4 bonds; amylopectin is branched with both α-1,4 and α-1,6 bonds.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following is a non-reducing sugar?

Explanation: Sucrose has its anomeric carbons of glucose and fructose involved in glycosidic bond formation, preventing free aldehyde or ketone generation.

Question 2 of 18

D-Glucose and D-Mannose are classified as:

Explanation: D-Glucose and D-Mannose differ in stereochemical configuration exclusively at carbon-2 (C-2), making them epimers.

Question 3 of 18

The number of chiral carbons in an open-chain aldohexose is:

Explanation: An aldohexose has 6 carbons, of which C-1 is an aldehyde carbon and C-6 is a primary alcohol carbon; the remaining 4 carbons (C-2, C-3, C-4, C-5) are chiral.

Question 4 of 18

Mutarotation involves changes in which property of a sugar solution?

Explanation: Mutarotation is the spontaneous change in optical rotation as the alpha and beta cyclic forms interconvert via the open-chain form.

Question 5 of 18

Which reagent converts glucose to gluconic acid?

Explanation: Bromine water is a mild oxidizing agent that selectively oxidizes the aldehyde group of aldoses to carboxylic acid without affecting primary alcohol groups.

Question 6 of 18

What is the product formed when D-glucose is reduced with sodium amalgam and water?

Explanation: Reduction of the carbonyl group in D-glucose yields sorbitol, a hexahydric alcohol.

Question 7 of 18

Cellulose differs structurally from starch because cellulose contains:

Explanation: Cellulose consists of D-glucose units connected by β-1,4-glycosidic linkages, whereas starch features α-1,4 linkages.

Question 8 of 18

Which carbohydrate gives a positive test with Tollen's reagent?

Explanation: Maltose is a reducing disaccharide with a free hemiacetal hydroxyl group, allowing it to reduce Tollens' reagent.

Question 9 of 18

The hydrolysis of sucrose yields an equimolar mixture of:

Explanation: Sucrose on hydrolysis breaks down into one molecule of α-D-glucose and one molecule of β-D-fructose.

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following is a ketohexose?

Explanation: Fructose is a ketohexose containing six carbon atoms with a ketonic carbonyl group at C-2.

Question 11 of 18

How many moles of phenylhydrazine are consumed per mole of glucose to form glucosazone?

Explanation: Three moles of phenylhydrazine are consumed: one to form the phenylhydrazone, one to oxidize C-2, and one to form the bis-phenylhydrazone (osazone).

Question 12 of 18

Which disaccharide contains a β-1,4 glycosidic linkage?

Explanation: Lactose is composed of β-D-galactose and β-D-glucose linked via a β-1,4 glycosidic bond.

Question 13 of 18

The repeating units in glycogen are linked by:

Explanation: Glycogen has a structure similar to amylopectin with main chains linked by α-1,4 bonds and branch points by α-1,6 bonds.

Question 14 of 18

Kiliani-Fischer synthesis is used for:

Explanation: The Kiliani-Fischer synthesis increases the carbon chain length of an aldose by one carbon atom via cyanohydrin formation and hydrolysis.

Question 15 of 18

Which test is specifically used to distinguish between reducing monosaccharides and disaccharides?

Explanation: Barfoed's test uses copper acetate in dilute acetic acid; monosaccharides react faster than reducing disaccharides.

Question 16 of 18

Molisch test is a general test for:

Explanation: The Molisch test involves treatment with alpha-naphthol and concentrated sulfuric acid, producing a purple ring for carbohydrates.

Question 17 of 18

Which acid is obtained when D-glucose is oxidized with concentrated nitric acid?

Explanation: Concentrated nitric acid oxidizes both the terminal aldehyde and primary alcohol groups of glucose to yield dicarboxylic glucaric acid.

Question 18 of 18

In a pyranose ring, the heterocyclic oxygen atom is incorporated into a:

Explanation: Pyranose refers to a cyclic monosaccharide containing a six-membered ring comprising five carbon atoms and one oxygen atom.

Question 1 of 10
What is the total number of stereoisomers possible for an aldoheptose with 5 asymmetric carbon atoms?

Solution: The number of stereoisomers is given by 2n, where n is the number of chiral centers. For n = 5, 25 = 32 stereoisomers.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following sugars does not exhibit mutarotation?

Solution: Methyl α-D-glucoside is a glycoside where the anomeric -OH is converted to an -OCH3 group, locking the ring and preventing mutarotation.

Question 3 of 10
Ruff degradation is used for:

Solution: Ruff degradation is a step-down method that oxidatively shortens an aldose chain by removing one carbon atom as carbon dioxide.

Question 4 of 10
Which chemical test confirms the presence of a five- or six-membered cyclic ring in glucose?

Solution: Formation of methyl glucosides via reaction with methanol in the presence of dry HCl establishes the cyclic acetal/ketal structure.

Question 5 of 10
The specific rotation of freshly prepared α-D-glucose is +112°, which changes at equilibrium to:

Solution: Mutarotation of α-D-glucose causes the specific rotation to shift from +112° to an equilibrium value of +52.7°.

Question 6 of 10
Which functional group is absent in glucose?

Solution: An aldohexose like glucose contains an aldehyde group and alcohol groups, but it lacks a ketonic carbonyl group.

Question 7 of 10
In the structure of starch, amylose constitutes approximately what percentage of total starch?

Solution: Natural starch typically consists of 15-20% water-soluble amylose and 80-85% water-insoluble amylopectin.

Question 8 of 10
Which reagent is used to convert an aldose into the corresponding alditol?

Solution: Reduction of the carbonyl group of an aldose with sodium borohydride (NaBH4) or catalytic hydrogenation yields the corresponding alditol.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following compounds is a disaccharide containing two glucose units linked by an α-1,4 glycosidic bond?

Solution: Maltose is a disaccharide composed of two α-D-glucose units joined by an α-1,4-glycosidic linkage.

Question 10 of 10
Which monosaccharide has all hydroxyl groups on the right side in its Fischer projection formula of D-form?

Solution: D-Ribose is an aldopentose where all hydroxyl groups on chiral carbons (C-2, C-3, C-4) are on the right side in the Fischer projection.
