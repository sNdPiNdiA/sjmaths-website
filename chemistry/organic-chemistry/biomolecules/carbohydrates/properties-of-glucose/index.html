Properties of Glucose

Glucose is an aldohexose with the molecular formula C6H12O6, containing one aldehyde group and five hydroxyl groups. It exhibits extensive structural isomerism, including open-chain and cyclic pyranose forms linked to mutarotation.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structural Elucidation and Functional Groups

  • Presence of a carbonyl group (aldehyde) is confirmed by the formation of an oxime with hydroxylamine and a cyanohydrin with hydrogen cyanide.
  • Reduction of glucose with excess hydroiodic acid (HI) and red phosphorus at high temperature yields n-hexane, confirming a straight chain of six carbon atoms.
  • Acetylation of glucose with acetic anhydride yields a pentaacetate, proving the presence of exactly five hydroxyl (-OH) groups.
  • Oxidation of glucose with nitric acid (HNO3) yields a dicarboxylic acid called saccharic acid (glucaric acid), confirming a primary alcohol group at C-6.

Stereochemistry and Cyclic Hemiacetal Formation

  • Glucose exists predominantly in cyclic hemiacetal structures where the C-1 aldehyde carbon reacts intramolecularly with the C-5 hydroxyl group to form a six-membered pyranose ring.
  • The cyclic structure creates a new asymmetric center at C-1, generating two diastereomers designated as α-D-glucose and β-D-glucose, known as anomers.
  • Mutarotation is the spontaneous change in specific rotation of an optically active compound in solution, observed when freshly prepared α-D-glucose ([α]D = +112°) or β-D-glucose ([α]D = +19°) reaches an equilibrium value of +52.5°.
  • Fischer projection formulas map the spatial orientation of groups, where D-configuration is assigned based on the right-hand orientation of the OH group at the penultimate carbon (C-5).

Chemical Reactions and Oxidative Behavior

  • Glucose acts as a reducing sugar because it reduces Tollen's reagent (silver mirror) and Fehling's solution (red precipitate of Cu2O) due to the free aldehyde group in open-chain equilibrium.
  • Mild oxidation using bromine water selectively oxidizes the aldehyde group to a carboxylic acid, producing gluconic acid without attacking the secondary alcohols.
  • Reaction of glucose with phenylhydrazine in a 1:3 molar ratio forms glucosazone, characterized by a distinct needle-like crystalline structure involving carbons C-1 and C-2.
  • Action of concentrated alkalies on glucose leads to rearrangement into fructose and mannose via the Lobry de Bruyn-Alberda van Ekenstein transformation through an enediol intermediate.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aldehydes typically form bisulfite addition compounds and give positive Schiff's test.

Observation: Glucose does not form a bisulfite addition compound and gives a negative Schiff's test. — The aldehyde group is predominantly masked in the stable cyclic hemiacetal (pyranose) form, leaving very little free open-chain aldehyde in solution.

Exception Pentaacetate of glucose should react with hydroxylamine to form an oxime if free carbonyl is present.

Observation: Glucose pentaacetate does not react with hydroxylamine. — Acetylation blocks all five hydroxyl groups and permanently locks the sugar in the cyclic acetal form, destroying the free hemiacetal equilibrium.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Anomer vs Epimer Identification
Anomer = C-1 configuration change; Epimer = C-2, C-3, or C-4 configuration change

Application: Use 'C1-Anomer' mnemonic to quickly distinguish α and β forms from structural epimers like mannose or galactose.

Oxidation Product Selector
Br2 water → Gluconic Acid; HNO3 → Saccharic Acid; Tollen's → Gluconate + Ag

Application: Match the oxidant strength: mild (Br2) oxidizes only C-1; strong (HNO3) oxidizes both C-1 and C-6.

Sorbitol/Mannitol Isomerism
Reduction of C=O → -CHOH-

Application: Remember that reduction of D-glucose yields D-glucitol (sorbitol), whereas reduction of D-mannose yields D-mannitol.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Glucose contains one primary alcoholic group (-CH2OH) and four secondary alcoholic groups.
  • The molecular weight of glucose is 180.16 g/mol, determined via cryoscopic and ebullioscopic methods.
  • Glucosazone formation consumes 3 moles of phenylhydrazine and releases 1 mole of aniline and 1 mole of ammonia.
  • Epimers are stereoisomers that differ in configuration at only one specific asymmetric carbon atom (e.g., glucose and mannose at C-2).
  • Bromine water test differentiates aldoses from ketoses because ketoses are not oxidized by Br2/H2O.
  • Mutarotation involves ring opening from cyclic pyranose to open-chain form and re-closing in the opposite configuration.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing anomers with enantiomers; anomers are diastereomers differing specifically at the anomeric carbon (C-1).
  • Assuming glucose has a free open-chain aldehyde group in all reactions; cyclic hemiacetal is the dominant species.
  • Confusing gluconic acid (monocarboxylic formed via Br2 oxidation) with saccharic acid (dicarboxylic formed via HNO3 oxidation).
  • Neglecting the role of water in mutarotation kinetics, which is base- and acid-catalyzed.

⚡ Quick Revision: Properties of Glucose

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Elemental Composition and Molecular Structure

Glucose is an aldohexose established by elemental analysis, molecular weight determination, and reduction to n-hexane via HI.

Functional Group Identification

Acetylation confirms five -OH groups, oxime formation confirms a carbonyl group, and nitric acid oxidation confirms a terminal primary alcohol.

Cyclic Hemiacetal and Anomerism

Intramolecular nucleophilic attack of C-5 OH on C-1 carbonyl forms a six-membered pyranose ring with α and β anomers.

Mutarotation and Optical Activity

Mutarotation is the equilibration of optical rotations between α and β anomers through the transient open-chain aldehyde form.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Anomer (C-1 Isomer)
Diastereomers differing in configuration at the hemiacetal carbon.
Mutarotation (Spontaneous Rotation Change)
Change in specific rotation of a sugar solution until equilibrium is attained.
Glucosazone (Osazone Derivative)
Yellow crystalline derivative formed by reaction with phenylhydrazine.
Epimer (Configurational Isomer)
Stereoisomers differing at only one chiral center.
Pyranose (Six-membered Ring)
Cyclic hemiacetal structure analogous to pyran.
Reducing Sugar (Ag+ / Cu2+ Reducer)
Carbohydrate possessing a free or potentially free aldehyde or ketone group.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Equilibrium specific rotation of D-glucose is +52.5°
  • Glucose pentaacetate proves five -OH groups exist
  • HNO3 oxidation yields saccharic acid (glucaric acid)
  • Br2 water oxidation yields gluconic acid
  • n-Hexane is formed upon reduction with HI / red P

📌 Quick Recall

  • Glucose is an aldohexose with five hydroxyl groups and one aldehyde group.
  • It forms stable cyclic pyranose structures with alpha and beta anomeric forms.
  • It undergoes mutarotation in aqueous solutions to reach equilibrium.
  • It acts as a reducing sugar reacting positively with Tollen's and Fehling's reagents.

⚖️ Key Differences

Gluconic Acid VS Glucaric Acid (Saccharic Acid)

Gluconic acid is monocarboxylic (C-1 oxidized), while glucaric acid is dicarboxylic (C-1 and C-6 oxidized).

Question 1 of 18

Which reagent is used to confirm the presence of a straight-chain six-carbon skeleton in glucose?

Explanation: Reduction of glucose with conc. HI and red phosphorus at 373 K yields n-hexane, confirming all six carbons are linked in a straight chain.

Question 2 of 18

What is the product formed when glucose is oxidized with bromine water?

Explanation: Bromine water is a mild oxidizing agent that selectively oxidizes the aldehyde group of glucose to a carboxylic acid, forming gluconic acid.

Question 3 of 18

Mutarotation of glucose involves:

Explanation: Mutarotation is the spontaneous change in specific rotation caused by the interconversion between alpha and beta pyranose forms through the open-chain aldehyde.

Question 4 of 18

How many moles of phenylhydrazine are consumed per mole of glucose to form glucosazone?

Explanation: Formation of glucosazone consumes 3 moles of phenylhydrazine, with 1 mole reacting at C-1/C-2 and 2 moles acting as oxidizing/condensing agents.

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following compounds is an epimer of D-glucose at C-2?

Explanation: D-Mannose differs from D-glucose only in the stereochemical configuration at carbon-2 (C-2), making it a C-2 epimer.

Question 6 of 18

Glucose pentaacetate formation proves the presence of how many hydroxyl groups?

Explanation: Reaction of glucose with acetic anhydride yields a pentaacetate derivative, indicating the presence of five hydroxyl groups.

Question 7 of 18

Why does glucose fail to give the Schiff's test?

Explanation: In aqueous solution, glucose exists predominantly in cyclic hemiacetal form, leaving insufficient free aldehyde to restore the magenta color in Schiff's test.

Question 8 of 18

Oxidation of glucose with concentrated nitric acid yields:

Explanation: Concentrated nitric acid is a strong oxidizing agent that oxidizes both the terminal aldehyde (C-1) and primary alcohol (C-6) to carboxylic acid groups, forming saccharic (glucaric) acid.

Question 9 of 18

What is the specific rotation of freshly prepared alpha-D-glucose in water?

Explanation: Freshly prepared alpha-D-glucose has a specific rotation of +112°, which mutarotates to an equilibrium value of +52.5°.

Question 10 of 18

Which functional group is present in open-chain D-glucose?

Explanation: D-Glucose is an aldohexose, meaning its open-chain structure contains one terminal aldehyde group and five hydroxyl groups.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following is a non-reducing sugar?

Explanation: Sucrose is a non-reducing disaccharide because both anomeric carbons (C-1 of glucose and C-2 of fructose) are involved in glycosidic bond formation.

Question 12 of 18

The Lobry de Bruyn-Alberda van Ekenstein transformation converts glucose into:

Explanation: Alkaline treatment of glucose leads to isomerization into fructose and mannose via an enediol intermediate.

Question 13 of 18

Which carbon atom is the anomeric carbon in D-glucopyranose?

Explanation: Carbon-1 (C-1) is the carbonyl carbon in the open-chain form, which becomes the hemiacetal carbon (anomeric carbon) upon ring closure.

Question 14 of 18

Catalytic hydrogenation or sodium borohydride reduction of glucose yields:

Explanation: Reduction of the carbonyl group of glucose using NaBH4 or catalytic hydrogenation produces sorbitol (glucitol), a hexahydric alcohol.

Question 15 of 18

Which sugar derivative is formed when glucose reacts with hydroxylamine?

Explanation: Reaction of glucose with hydroxylamine (NH2OH) forms glucose oxime via condensation of the aldehyde group.

Question 16 of 18

Anomers of glucose differ in configuration specifically at:

Explanation: Anomers (α and β) are diastereomers that differ specifically in configuration at the anomeric carbon (C-1).

Question 17 of 18

What characterizes a reducing sugar?

Explanation: Reducing sugars reduce Tollen's reagent (Ag+) and Fehling's solution (Cu2+) due to free or accessible aldehyde/ketone groups.

Question 18 of 18

What is the relationship between D-glucose and L-glucose?

Explanation: D-Glucose and L-glucose are non-superimposable mirror images of each other, making them enantiomers.

Question 1 of 10
What is the hybridisation of the anomeric carbon (C-1) in cyclic D-glucopyranose?

Solution: In cyclic D-glucopyranose, C-1 is part of a tetrahedral hemiacetal carbon bonded to four different groups, giving it sp3 hybridisation.

Question 2 of 10
Which test distinguishes between an aldose and a ketose?

Solution: Bromine water oxidizes aldoses (to gluconic acid) but does not oxidize ketoses, serving as a specific distinction test.

Question 3 of 10
How many chiral carbon atoms are present in an open-chain D-glucose molecule?

Solution: Open-chain D-glucose has 5 chiral carbon atoms (C-2, C-3, C-4, C-5, and C-6 has none, C-1 is not chiral).

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following pairs represents epimers?

Solution: D-Glucose and D-Galactose are C-4 epimers, differing only in the stereochemical configuration at carbon-4.

Question 5 of 10
What product is obtained when glucose is treated with excess phenylhydrazine?

Solution: Reaction with excess phenylhydrazine yields glucosazone, a characteristic osazone crystal.

Question 6 of 10
The equilibrium mixture of D-glucose in water contains approximately what percentage of beta-D-glucose?

Solution: At equilibrium in water at 298 K, glucose consists of about 64% beta-D-glucose, 36% alpha-D-glucose, and traces of open-chain form.

Question 7 of 10
Which carbon atom of glucose is involved in the formation of the pyranose six-membered ring?

Solution: The pyranose ring is formed by the intramolecular hemiacetal formation between the C-1 aldehyde carbon and the C-5 hydroxyl oxygen.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following acids is formed when glucose is oxidized by Tollen's reagent?

Solution: Oxidation of glucose by Tollen's reagent oxidizes the aldehyde group to gluconate while reducing Ag+ to metallic silver.

Question 9 of 10
What is the optical activity behavior of a freshly prepared aqueous solution of alpha-D-glucose over time?

Solution: Fresh alpha-D-glucose starts at +112° and undergoes mutarotation until it equilibrates at +52.5°.

Question 10 of 10
Which chemical proof confirms that all five hydroxyl groups in glucose are present as secondary or primary alcohols rather than tertiary?

Solution: Acetylation forming a pentaacetate with five acetyl groups confirms five reactive -OH groups, none of which exhibit tertiary steric hindrance.
