Hydroboration

Hydroboration involves the addition of a boron-hydrogen bond across an alkene or alkyne double or triple bond to form organoboranes. Subsequent oxidation yields anti-Markovnikov alcohols with syn stereochemistry.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mechanism and Stereochemistry of Hydroboration

  • Hydroboration proceeds via a concerted, four-center transition state without the formation of carbocation intermediates.
  • The addition of both boron and hydrogen occurs exclusively from the same face of the alkene, resulting in syn addition.
  • The boron atom adds preferentially to the less sterically hindered and more electron-rich carbon atom.

Regioselectivity and Anti-Markovnikov Hydration

  • The overall transformation of terminal alkenes to primary alcohols yields anti-Markovnikov regioselectivity.
  • Steric factors dominate the regiochemical outcome, directing the larger electrophilic boron atom to the less substituted carbon.
  • Electronic factors also contribute, as the partial negative charge develops on the more substituted carbon bearing more stable partial positive charge in the transition state.

Synthetic Utility of Organoboranes

  • Reaction of trialkylboranes with alkaline hydrogen peroxide yields alcohols with retention of configuration at the carbon center.
  • Treatment with alkaline silver nitrate or iodine permits carbon-carbon bond formation or halogenation.
  • Hydroboration of alkynes using hindered dialkylboranes stops at the vinylic borane stage, which upon oxidation produces aldehydes or ketones.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Addition of electrophiles to alkenes yields Markovnikov products via carbocation intermediates.

Observation: Hydroboration-oxidation yields anti-Markovnikov alcohol products. — The reaction proceeds via a concerted synchronous transition state rather than a discrete carbocation, controlled by steric approach of the bulky borane.

Exception Simple borane (BH3) adds three times to terminal alkynes to form complex trialkylborane derivatives.

Observation: Terminal alkynes react with hindered dialkylboranes (e.g., 9-BBN or disiamylborane) to give mono-hydroboration products. — Steric hindrance prevents subsequent additions, allowing controlled synthesis of aldehydes from terminal alkynes.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Hydroboration of alkenes is a concerted, stereospecific syn addition reaction.
  • Boron acts as the electrophile and adds to the less hindered carbon atom.
  • Oxidation of organoboranes with alkaline H2O2 proceeds with retention of configuration.
  • The reaction does not involve carbocation intermediates, thereby preventing molecular rearrangements.
  • Hydroboration of terminal alkynes using hindered boranes yields aldehydes upon oxidation.
  • Diborane (B2H6) exists as a dimer and is typically used in situ generated in ether solvents like THF.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming carbocation formation occurs; hydroboration is a concerted, single-step addition per B-H bond.
  • Confusing the regiochemistry of acid-catalyzed hydration (Markovnikov) with hydroboration-oxidation (anti-Markovnikov).
  • Forgetting that oxidation of the C-B bond proceeds with retention of configuration, not inversion.
  • Applying simple BH3 to alkynes when a mono-addition product is desired, leading to over-reaction.

⚡ Quick Revision: Hydroboration

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Nature of Diborane and Solvents

Diborane exists as a dimer (B2H6) held by banana bonds (3-center 2-electron bonds) and is handled safely as a THF complex (BH3·THF).

Stereospecific Syn Addition

The simultaneous delivery of boron and hydrogen to the same face of the alkene ensures syn stereochemical addition across the double bond.

Anti-Markovnikov Regioselectivity

Steric hindrance directs the boron atom to the less substituted carbon, creating an overall anti-Markovnikov addition pattern after oxidation.

Oxidation Mechanism

Hydroperoxide ion attacks the electron-deficient boron, triggering a 1,2-alkyl migration from boron to oxygen with retention of configuration.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Hydroboration (Addition of B-H)
Addition of a boron-hydride bond across an unsaturated carbon-carbon bond.
Organoborane (R3B)
An organometallic compound containing carbon-boron covalent bonds.
Syn Addition (Same-face addition)
Addition of two substituents to the same face of a double or triple bond.
Anti-Markovnikov (Regiochemical outcome)
Addition where the hydrogen adds to the more substituted carbon and functional group to the less substituted carbon.
Disiamylborane (Sia2BH)
A sterically hindered dialkylborane used for selective hydroboration of alkynes.
9-BBN (9-Borabicyclo[3.3.1]nonane)
A highly selective hydroborating reagent utilized for terminal alkenes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Reagents: BH3·THF followed by H2O2, NaOH.
  • Regiochemistry: Anti-Markovnikov alcohol formation.
  • Stereochemistry: Concerted syn addition.
  • Absence of carbocation rearrangement.
  • Oxidation proceeds with retention of configuration.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Hydroboration-oxidation converts alkenes into alcohols with anti-Markovnikov regioselectivity.
  • The addition step is concerted and gives syn stereochemistry.
  • No carbocation rearrangements occur during the reaction sequence.
  • Alkynes can be converted to aldehydes or ketones depending on the reagent stoichiometry.

⚖️ Key Differences

Oxymercuration-Reduction VS Hydroboration-Oxidation

Oxymercuration gives Markovnikov alcohols with potential rearrangements, whereas hydroboration gives anti-Markovnikov alcohols with syn addition and no rearrangements.

What is the primary regiochemical outcome of the hydroboration-oxidation of terminal alkenes?

What type of stereochemical addition is observed in the hydroboration step?

Explanation: Hydroboration proceeds via a concerted four-center transition state, resulting in strict syn addition of H and BH2.

Question 3 of 18

Which reagents are used in the second step of hydroboration-oxidation?

Explanation: Alkaline hydrogen peroxide (H2O2, NaOH) oxidizes the carbon-boron bond to a carbon-oxygen bond.

Question 4 of 18

Does hydroboration of alkenes involve carbocation intermediates?

Explanation: Hydroboration is a concerted reaction proceeding through a single four-center transition state without carbocation intermediates.

Question 5 of 18

What is the product when 1-hexene is subjected to hydroboration-oxidation?

Explanation: Terminal alkenes yield primary alcohols via anti-Markovnikov addition, converting 1-hexene to hexan-1-ol.

Question 6 of 18

Which reagent is typically used as a source of BH3 in organic synthesis?

Explanation: Borane is commonly utilized as a complex with tetrahydrofuran (BH3.THF) to stabilize diborane in solution.

Question 7 of 18

What is the stereochemical outcome at the carbon center when a C-B bond is oxidized to a C-OH bond?

Explanation: The oxidation of organoboranes by alkaline hydrogen peroxide proceeds with complete retention of configuration.

Question 8 of 18

Why do molecular rearrangements not occur during hydroboration?

Explanation: Carbocation rearrangements do not occur because no free carbocation intermediates are formed during concerted hydroboration.

Question 9 of 18

What is the structure of diborane (B2H6) characterized by?

Explanation: Diborane features bridging hydrogen atoms forming three-center two-electron (3c-2e) bonds, often called banana bonds.

Question 10 of 18

What product is obtained when terminal alkynes are treated with a hindered dialkylborane followed by H2O2/OH-?

Explanation: Hydroboration of terminal alkynes with hindered dialkylboranes yields vinylic boranes that oxidize to aldehydes.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following describes the electronic factor in hydroboration?

Explanation: Boron acts as the electrophile and attacks the pi bond, directing itself to the carbon with higher electron density/less steric hindrance.

Question 12 of 18

What is 9-BBN commonly used for in organic synthesis?

Explanation: 9-BBN (9-borabicyclo[3.3.1]nonane) is a bulky, highly selective hydroborating reagent for alkenes.

Question 13 of 18

How many moles of alkene react with one mole of BH3 in complete hydroboration?

Explanation: Each BH3 molecule contains three B-H bonds and can react with three moles of alkene to form a trialkylborane.

Question 14 of 18

What happens when an organoborane is treated with acetic acid (CH3COOH) under heat?

Explanation: Protonolysis (protonodegeneration) of trialkylboranes with carboxylic acids replaces the carbon-boron bond with a carbon-hydrogen bond, yielding an alkane.

Question 15 of 18

In the mechanism of hydroboration, the transition state is best described as:

Explanation: The transition state involves simultaneous bonding of boron and hydrogen to the two carbons of the alkene in a four-membered cyclic array.

Question 16 of 18

Which alkene would undergo the fastest hydroboration?

Explanation: Alkenes with appropriate substitution react rapidly; however, terminal and moderately substituted alkenes react quickly before extreme steric crowding inhibits the approach.

Question 17 of 18

What is the role of NaOH in the oxidation step of hydroboration?

Explanation: Sodium hydroxide deprotonates hydrogen peroxide to generate the reactive hydroperoxide nucleophile (HOO-).

Question 18 of 18

Which of the following functional groups is compatible with hydroboration without being reduced?

Explanation: Hydroboration targets unsaturated carbon-carbon bonds selectively while leaving many other reducible functional groups intact depending on exact reagent choice.

Question 1 of 10
What stereochemical relationship exists between the hydrogen and the hydroxyl group added across an alkene in hydroboration-oxidation?

Solution: Hydroboration introduces H and B with syn stereochemistry, and subsequent oxidation retains configuration, resulting in overall syn addition of H and OH.

Question 2 of 10
Why does 3,3-dimethylbut-1-ene undergo hydroboration-oxidation without rearrangement, unlike acid-catalyzed hydration?

Solution: Concerted addition avoids forming carbocation intermediates, thus eliminating the driving force for hydride or methyl shifts.

Question 3 of 10
What is the active hydroborating species when BH3 is dissolved in tetrahydrofuran?

Solution: Borane dissolves in THF to form a stable monomeric Lewis acid-base complex, BH3.THF.

Question 4 of 10
In the mechanism of organoborane oxidation, the migrating group moves from:

Solution: Nucleophilic attack of hydroperoxide on boron leads to migration of an alkyl group from boron to the adjacent oxygen atom.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following reagents is a hindered dialkylborane used to stop alkyne hydroboration at the alkene stage?

Solution: Disiamylborane (bis(3-methyl-2-butyl)borane) is sterically hindered and reacts selectively with terminal alkynes to form vinylic boranes.

Question 6 of 10
What is the final product when an internal alkyne undergoes hydroboration-oxidation?

Solution: Internal alkynes yield enols upon oxidation which tautomerize to ketones.

Question 7 of 10
Which factor primarily controls the regioselectivity of hydroboration?

Solution: Steric hindrance dictates that the larger boron atom attaches to the less substituted, less crowded carbon.

Question 8 of 10
What happens to the optical activity of a chiral alkene during hydroboration-oxidation if a new chiral center is created?

Solution: Stereospecific syn addition creates specific diastereomeric relationships based on the facial approach to the chiral alkene.

Question 9 of 10
How many moles of hydrogen gas are typically liberated per mole of BH3 when trialkylboranes react with carboxylic acids?

Solution: Protonolysis of trialkylboranes with carboxylic acids yields alkanes and triacyl borates without liberating hydrogen gas.

Question 10 of 10
Which statement is incorrect regarding hydroboration-oxidation?

Solution: Hydroboration-oxidation does not involve carbocation rearrangements, making that statement incorrect.
