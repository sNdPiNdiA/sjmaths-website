Named Reactions in Organic Synthesis

Named reactions constitute fundamental carbon-carbon and carbon-heteroatom bond-forming transformations utilized in complex organic synthesis. They proceed via distinct mechanistic pathways involving specific catalytic systems, intermediates, and stereochemical outcomes.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Carbonyl Condensation Reactions

  • The Aldol condensation requires aldehydes or ketones possessing at least one α-hydrogen to form an enolate ion intermediate.
  • Crossed aldol condensations are synthetically useful only when one reactant lacks α-hydrogens, such as benzaldehyde or formaldehyde.
  • The Cannizzaro reaction occurs with aldehydes lacking α-hydrogens under strongly basic conditions, undergoing a simultaneous oxidation-reduction (disproportionation) pathway.
  • Perkin reaction involves the condensation of an aromatic aldehyde with an acid anhydride in the presence of an alkali salt of the acid to yield α,β-unsaturated aromatic acids.

Rearrangement and Coupling Pathways

  • Beckmann rearrangement converts ketoximes into N-substituted amides in the presence of acidic catalysts like H2SO4, PCl5, or SOCl2.
  • The migrating group in the Beckmann rearrangement is always anti to the departing hydroxyl group due to stereoelectronic constraints.
  • Claisen rearrangement is a [3,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement of allyl aryl ethers yielding ortho-allylphenols via a cyclic transition state.
  • Pinacol-Pinacolone rearrangement involves the acid-catalyzed dehydration of a 1,2-diol to a ketone, driven by the formation of a stable carbocation followed by methyl or aryl migration.

Oxidation, Reduction, and Halogenation

  • Clemmensen reduction utilizes zinc-amalgam (Zn-Hg) and concentrated hydrochloric acid to reduce aldehydes and ketones to corresponding alkanes.
  • Wolf-Kishner reduction accomplishes the same overall deoxygenation using hydrazine (NH2NH2) and a strong base like KOH under anhydrous high-temperature conditions.
  • Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction halogenates the α-carbon of carboxylic acids using red phosphorus and bromine or chlorine.
  • Reimer-Tiemann reaction introduces a formyl group into a phenol ring using chloroform (CHCl3) and aqueous NaOH via a dichlorocarbene intermediate.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Cannizzaro Reaction
2 Ar-CHO + OH- → Ar-COO- + Ar-CH2OH
Conditions: Concentrated alkali (50% NaOH), absence of α-hydrogens
Units: Disproportionation reaction yielding equal moles of alcohol and carboxylate salt
Claisen Condensation
R-CH2-COOC2H5 + R'-COOC2H5 → R'-CO-CH(R)-COOC2H5 + C2H5OH
Conditions: Strong base (NaOEt), ester containing α-hydrogens
Units: Formation of β-keto ester
Diels-Alder Reaction
Diene + Dienophile → Cyclohexene derivative (Δ)
Conditions: Thermal pericyclic [4+2] cycloaddition
Units: Concerted stereospecific addition forming a six-membered ring
Grignard Reaction
R-MgX + R'-CO-R'' → R-C(R')(R'')-OMgX → (H3O+) R-C(R')(R'')-OH
Conditions: Dry ether solvent, moisture-free conditions
Units: Nucleophilic addition of organometallic reagent to carbonyl compounds
Aldol Condensation
2 R-CH2-CHO → R-CH2-CH(OH)-CH(R)-CHO → R-CH2-CH=C(R)-CHO + H2O
Conditions: Dilute aqueous base (NaOH, KOH) or acid
Units: Reaction yields β-hydroxy aldehyde/ketone followed by α,β-unsaturated carbonyl
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aldehydes with α-hydrogens undergo base-catalyzed aldol condensation.

Observation: Formaldehyde and Benzaldehyde fail to undergo self-aldol condensation. — Complete absence of α-hydrogens prevents enolate anion formation.

Exception Ketones normally resist oxidation by mild oxidizing agents like Tollen's reagent.

Observation: α-Hydroxy ketones (Aloins/Keto-ols) reduce Tollen's and Fehling's reagents. — Rapid tautomerization to enediols allows oxidation to α-diketones.

Exception Reduction of carbonyl groups to hydrocarbons is achieved via Clemmensen reduction.

Observation: Acid-sensitive substrates decompose under Clemmensen reduction conditions (concentrated HCl/Zn-Hg). — Harsh mineral acid conditions cleave acid-labile protective groups, requiring the alternative basic Wolff-Kishner reduction.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Reduction Reagent Mnemonic
Clemmensen = Acid (Cl-A); Wolff-Kishner = Base (W-K-B)

Application: Instantly select Zn-Hg/HCl for acid-stable substrates and NH2NH2/OH- for base-stable substrates during carbonyl deoxygenation.

Carbene Generation Shortcut
CHCl3 + OH- → :CCl2 + Cl- + H2O

Application: Use this intermediate stoichiometry for Reimer-Tiemann and Carbylamine test questions.

Diels-Alder Stereospecificity Rule
Cis-dienophile → Cis-cyclohexene adduct

Application: Retain relative stereochemistry of the starting dienophile in the product ring since the reaction is a concerted syn-addition.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Cannizzaro reaction is a disproportionation reaction yielding one mole of alcohol and one mole of carboxylate salt per two moles of aldehyde.
  • Diels-Alder reaction requires an electron-rich diene (s-cis conformation) and an electron-deficient dienophile.
  • Reimer-Tiemann reaction proceeds via dichlorocarbene (:CCl2) electrophilic aromatic substitution to yield salicylaldehyde.
  • HVZ reaction specifically halogenates carboxylic acids at the α-position using catalytic phosphorus and halogen.
  • Crossed Cannizzaro reaction involving formaldehyde yields sodium formate and the corresponding alcohol selectively.
  • Kolbe's electrolytic synthesis of sodium salts of carboxylic acids yields symmetrical alkanes at the anode via free radical decarboxylation.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the roles of base in Aldol (generates enolate) versus Cannizzaro (attacks carbonyl carbon directly).
  • Applying Clemmensen reduction to acid-sensitive molecules without checking the pH requirement (concentrated HCl).
  • Forgetting that internal alkynes do not undergo terminal substitution in Sonogashira or Heck coupling without terminal protons.
  • Assuming Diels-Alder dienes can react in the s-trans conformation rather than the required s-cis conformation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Named Reactions in Organic Synthesis

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Enolate Chemistry and Aldol Additions

Carbonyl compounds containing α-hydrogens form nucleophilic enolates under basic conditions, attacking other carbonyl electrophiles to create carbon-carbon bonds.

Pericyclic Cycloadditions

Thermal [4+2] cycloadditions (Diels-Alder) proceed through a concerted, cyclic transition state with retention of stereochemistry from the dienophile.

Deoxygenation Protocols

Ketones and aldehydes are converted to alkanes using Clemmensen (acidic) or Wolff-Kishner (basic) reduction pathways depending on substrate acid-base stability.

Formylation and Halogenation

Electrophilic substitutions on aromatic rings (Reimer-Tiemann) and α-functionalizations of acids (HVZ) introduce specific functional groups via discrete reactive intermediates.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Enolate (R-CH=C(O-)-R)
Ambident nucleophile formed by abstraction of an α-hydrogen.
Carbene (:CR2)
Neutral divalent carbon species acting as electrophile or nucleophile.
Hydrazone (R2C=NNH2)
Intermediate formed in Wolff-Kishner reduction from carbonyl and hydrazine.
Dienophile (Alkenes/Alkynes with EWG)
Unsaturated reactant accepting diene electrons in Diels-Alder reaction.
Zwitterion (R+-X-)
Dipole molecule with positive and negative formal charges.
Disproportionation (Simultaneous oxidation & reduction)
Redox process where single species is simultaneously oxidized and reduced.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Cannizzaro applies strictly to aldehydes without α-hydrogens.
  • Clemmensen uses Zn-Hg / HCl; Wolff-Kishner uses NH2NH2 / KOH.
  • Diels-Alder requires s-cis diene conformation.
  • Reimer-Tiemann intermediate is :CCl2.
  • HVZ reaction uses Red P + X2 for α-halogenation.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Named reactions provide predictable routes for carbon skeleton construction.
  • Reaction conditions dictate whether acidic or basic intermediates form.
  • Stereospecificity in pericyclic reactions depends on orbital symmetry conservation.
  • Carbene and free radical intermediates govern halogenation and coupling steps.

⚖️ Key Differences

Aldol Condensation VS Cannizzaro Reaction

Aldol requires α-hydrogens and forms β-hydroxy carbonyls; Cannizzaro requires absence of α-hydrogens and causes disproportionation.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following compounds undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction?

Explanation: Formaldehyde lacks α-hydrogens and readily undergoes a Cannizzaro disproportionation reaction in concentrated alkali.

Question 2 of 18

What is the reactive intermediate in the Reimer-Tiemann reaction?

Explanation: Reaction of chloroform with base generates dichlorocarbene (:CCl2) which attacks the phenoxide ion.

Question 3 of 18

Clemmensen reduction converts a carbonyl group into:

Explanation: Clemmensen reduction uses zinc-amalgam and hydrochloric acid to completely deoxygenate aldehydes and ketones to alkanes.

Question 4 of 18

Which reagent is used in the Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction?

Explanation: HVZ reaction selectively halogenates the α-position of carboxylic acids using red phosphorus and a halogen.

Question 5 of 18

In a Diels-Alder reaction, the diene must adopt which conformation?

Explanation: The diene must be in the s-cis conformation so that the terminal carbon atoms are close enough to overlap with the dienophile π-system.

Question 6 of 18

Beckmann rearrangement converts ketoximes into:

Explanation: Acid-catalyzed Beckmann rearrangement transforms ketoximes into corresponding substituted amides.

Question 7 of 18

Which named reaction involves the reaction of an ester with a base to form a β-keto ester?

Explanation: Claisen condensation involves base-promoted condensation of two ester molecules containing α-hydrogens.

Question 8 of 18

Perkin reaction is used to prepare:

Explanation: Perkin reaction condenses aromatic aldehydes with acid anhydrides to yield α,β-unsaturated aromatic acids such as cinnamic acid.

Question 9 of 18

The conversion of benzaldehyde to benzoin is achieved via:

Explanation: Benzoin condensation is catalyzed by cyanide ions and converts aromatic aldehydes into α-hydroxy ketones (benzoins).

Question 10 of 18

Which reduction method uses hydrazine and potassium hydroxide in ethylene glycol?

Explanation: Wolff-Kishner reduction employs hydrazine and strong base at elevated temperatures to reduce carbonyls to hydrocarbons.

Question 11 of 18

Pinacol-Pinacolone rearrangement proceeds via the formation of:

Explanation: Protonation of a vicinal diol followed by water loss generates a carbocation intermediate which undergoes rearrangement.

Question 12 of 18

Kolbe electrolytic decarboxylation of potassium propionate yields:

Explanation: Electrolysis of potassium propionate yields butane via dimerization of ethyl radicals generated at the anode.

Question 13 of 18

Which reagent combination effects allylic or benzylic bromination selectively?

Explanation: N-bromosuccinimide (NBS) in carbon tetrachloride selectively brominates allylic and benzylic positions via radical mechanisms.

Question 14 of 18

Gattermann-Koch reaction is used to introduce which group into benzene?

Explanation: Gattermann-Koch formylation introduces a -CHO group into benzene rings using CO and HCl in the presence of AlCl3/CuCl.

Question 15 of 18

Which reaction converts primary amines into isocyanides (carbylamines)?

Explanation: Heating primary amines with chloroform and alcoholic KOH yields foul-smelling isocyanides (carbylamine test).

Question 16 of 18

Hofmann bromamide degradation of amides results in:

Explanation: Reaction of primary amides with Br2 and NaOH produces primary amines containing one carbon atom less than the parent amide.

Question 17 of 18

Gabriel phthalimide synthesis is specifically used for the preparation of:

Explanation: Gabriel synthesis yields pure primary aliphatic amines without contamination by secondary or tertiary amines.

Question 18 of 18

Sandmeyer reaction involves the replacement of diazonium groups using cuprous salts to yield:

Explanation: Sandmeyer reaction converts aromatic diazonium salts into aryl halides or nitriles using CuCl, CuBr, or CuCN.

Question 1 of 10
Which named reaction involves the conversion of acid amides to primary amines with loss of one carbon atom using Br2/NaOH?

Solution: Hofmann bromamide degradation converts primary amides into primary amines with loss of the carbonyl carbon as carbonate.

Question 2 of 10
The [3,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement of an allyl phenyl ether is known as:

Solution: Claisen rearrangement thermally converts allyl aryl ethers into ortho-allylphenols via a concerted cyclic transition state.

Question 3 of 10
Which reagent is employed in the Etard reaction to convert toluene to benzaldehyde?

Solution: Etard reaction uses chromyl chloride (CrO2Cl2) in CS2 to oxidize methyl groups on aromatic rings to aldehyde complexes.

Question 4 of 10
Which reaction is used for chain elongation of alkyl halides using sodium acetylide or Grignard reagents?

Solution: Corey-House synthesis couples lithium dialkylcopper reagents with alkyl halides to form unsymmetrical alkanes.

Question 5 of 10
Gattermann aldehyde synthesis differs from Formylation via Gattermann-Koch by using:

Solution: Gattermann reaction uses hydrogen cyanide (HCN) and HCl with Lewis acid catalysts, whereas Gattermann-Koch uses carbon monoxide (CO).

Question 6 of 10
Which reaction involves the condensation of active methylene compounds with carbonyl compounds catalyzed by bases?

Solution: Knoevenagel condensation is a nucleophilic addition of an active methylene compound to a carbonyl group followed by dehydration.

Question 7 of 10
Darzens glycidic ester condensation yields which functional group intermediate?

Solution: Reaction of an α-halo ester with a carbonyl compound in the presence of a base yields α,β-epoxy esters (glycidic esters).

Question 8 of 10
Which catalyst is used in the Rosenmund reduction for converting acyl chlorides to aldehydes?

Solution: Rosenmund reduction employs palladium on barium sulfate poisoned with sulfur or quinoline to prevent over-reduction to alcohols.

Question 9 of 10
Simmons-Smith reaction is used for the stereospecific synthesis of:

Solution: Simmons-Smith reaction reacts alkenes with diiodomethane and zinc-copper couple (CH2I2-Zn(Cu)) to generate cyclopropanes.

Question 10 of 10
Which reaction converts an aromatic primary amine into an aryl iodide using potassium iodide?

Solution: Diazonium salt solution treated with aqueous potassium iodide readily yields aryl iodides without requiring copper catalysts.
