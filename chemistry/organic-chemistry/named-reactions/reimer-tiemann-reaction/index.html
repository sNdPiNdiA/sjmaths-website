Reimer-Tiemann Reaction

The Reimer-Tiemann reaction is an example of electrophilic aromatic substitution used for the ortho-formylation of phenols and heterocyclic compounds. It involves the reaction of phenol with chloroform in the presence of an aqueous base to form hydroxybenzaldehydes.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Reaction Mechanism and Active Electrophile

  • The active electrophile is dichlorocarbene (:CCl2), generated via the alpha-elimination of hydrogen chloride from chloroform.
  • Hydroxide ion abstracts a proton from chloroform to form the trichloromethide carbanion (CCl3-), which subsequently loses a chloride ion.
  • Phenoxide ion, formed in the alkaline medium, undergoes rapid electrophilic attack by the electron-deficient dichlorocarbene at the ortho-position.

Regioselectivity and Intermediates

  • The reaction predominantly yields ortho-hydroxybenzaldehyde (salicylaldehyde) due to favorable coordination of the electrophile with the adjacent phenoxide oxygen.
  • The primary intermediate is a benzal chloride derivative (phenoxydichloromethane), which upon alkaline hydrolysis yields the corresponding aldehyde.
  • Para-isomer is formed only in minor quantities unless the ortho-position is blocked by a substituent.

Substrate Scope and Limitations

  • Phenols react smoothly under Reimer-Tiemann conditions; pyrroles and indoles also undergo formylation via a similar carbene insertion pathway.
  • Aliphatic alcohols do not yield aldehydes under these conditions because they lack the aromatic ring stabilization and activating oxyanion effect.
  • Strong electron-withdrawing groups on the phenol ring reduce the nucleophilicity of the phenoxide ion, significantly lowering reaction yields.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Phenols react with CCl4 and NaOH to give ortho-hydroxybenzaldehydes.

Observation: Reaction with carbon tetrachloride (CCl4) yields hydroxybenzoic acids instead of hydroxybenzaldehydes. — Reaction via trichloromethyl carbocation intermediate yields a trichloromethyl ether that hydrolyzes completely to a carboxylic acid group (-COOH).

Exception Electrophilic aromatic substitution on phenols occurs predominantly at the para position due to steric hindrance.

Observation: Reimer-Tiemann formylation occurs predominantly at the ortho position. — Intramolecular coordination between the phenoxide oxygen and the incoming electrophilic carbene stabilizes the ortho-transition state.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The active species generated from chloroform in alkaline medium is dichlorocarbene.
  • Salicylaldehyde is the primary product formed during the formylation of phenol.
  • The reaction proceeds through an electrophilic substitution mechanism on the phenoxide ion.
  • Intramolecular hydrogen bonding in salicylaldehyde increases its volatility relative to the para-isomer.
  • Heterocyclic compounds such as pyrrole undergo formylation at the C-2 position.
  • Substitution of chloroform with carbon tetrachloride results in the formation of salicylic acid.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming the electrophile is a carbocation instead of a neutral, electron-deficient carbene.
  • Confusing the Reimer-Tiemann product (aldehyde) with the Kolbe-Schmitt product (carboxylic acid).
  • Forgetting that the phenoxide ion is much more reactive toward electrophilic attack than neutral phenol.
  • Overlooking the role of aqueous alkali in both generating the carbene and forming the reactive phenoxide.

⚡ Quick Revision: Reimer-Tiemann Reaction

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Carbene Generation

Chloroform undergoes alpha-elimination in the presence of strong aqueous base to produce electron-deficient dichlorocarbene, the active electrophile.

Substrate Activation

Phenol is converted into a phenoxide ion by the base, which dramatically increases the electron density on the aromatic ring, facilitating electrophilic attack.

Regioselective Attack

The dichlorocarbene attacks the ortho-position of the phenoxide ring, guided by coordination with the adjacent oxygen atom.

Hydrolysis and Workup

The resulting dichloromethyl ether intermediate undergoes base-catalyzed hydrolysis followed by acidification to yield ortho-hydroxybenzaldehyde.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Dichlorocarbene (:CCl2)

Salicylaldehyde (2-Hydroxybenzaldehyde)
The primary ortho-formylation product of phenol.
Phenoxide Ion (C6H5O-)
The activated nucleophilic aromatic substrate.
Alpha-Elimination (1,1-Elimination)
Loss of atoms or groups from the same carbon atom.
Kolbe-Schmitt Reaction (Carboxylation)
Reaction of phenoxide with CO2 to form hydroxybenzoic acid.
Intramolecular Hydrogen Bonding (Chelation)
Hydrogen bond formation within the same molecule.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Electrophile: :CCl2
  • Base: Aqueous NaOH or KOH
  • Primary product: Salicylaldehyde
  • Substrate: Phenol or activated heterocycles
  • Byproduct: NaCl and H2O

📌 Quick Recall

  • Reimer-Tiemann reaction introduces a -CHO group into phenols.
  • Reaction utilizes chloroform and aqueous sodium hydroxide.
  • Proceeds via dichlorocarbene electrophilic addition.
  • Favors ortho-substitution due to directed coordination.

⚖️ Key Differences

Reimer-Tiemann Reaction VS Kolbe-Schmitt Reaction

Reimer-Tiemann uses CHCl3 to form an aldehyde, whereas Kolbe-Schmitt uses CO2 to form a carboxylic acid.

Question 1 of 19

What is the active electrophile in the Reimer-Tiemann reaction?

Explanation: Chloroform in the presence of base undergoes alpha-elimination to generate neutral dichlorocarbene, which acts as the electrophile.

Question 2 of 19

Which of the following compounds is the principal organic product when phenol reacts with chloroform and aqueous NaOH?

Explanation: The Reimer-Tiemann reaction of phenol yields 2-hydroxybenzaldehyde, commonly known as salicylaldehyde.

Question 3 of 19

What type of reaction mechanism is operative in the Reimer-Tiemann reaction?

Explanation: The reaction involves the attack of an electron-deficient carbene on the electron-rich aromatic ring of the phenoxide ion, which is an electrophilic aromatic substitution.

Question 4 of 19

In the generation of the active electrophile, chloroform undergoes which type of elimination?

Explanation: Both the proton and the chloride ion are lost from the same carbon atom (C-1), representing an alpha-elimination.

Question 5 of 19

What is the role of sodium hydroxide in the Reimer-Tiemann reaction?

Explanation: Sodium hydroxide abstracts a proton from phenol to form phenoxide and also abstracts a proton from chloroform to generate dichlorocarbene.

Question 6 of 19

Which reagent combination gives hydroxybenzoic acid instead of hydroxybenzaldehyde with phenol?

Explanation: Using carbon tetrachloride (CCl4) instead of chloroform leads to the introduction of a -COOH group, yielding salicylic acid.

Question 7 of 19

Why is the ortho-isomer predominantly formed in the Reimer-Tiemann reaction of phenol?

Explanation: Coordination between the phenoxide oxygen and the incoming carbene directs the electrophile specifically to the ortho-position.

Question 8 of 19

Which of the following heterocyclic compounds undergoes formylation under Reimer-Tiemann conditions?

Explanation: Electron-rich heterocycles like pyrrole undergo Reimer-Tiemann formylation similarly to phenols.

Question 9 of 19

What is the intermediate formed immediately after carbene addition to the phenoxide ring?

Explanation: The addition of dichlorocarbene to the phenoxide ring forms a substituted dichloromethyl-cyclohexa-2,4-dienone intermediate.

Question 10 of 19

Which functional group is introduced into the aromatic ring during the Reimer-Tiemann reaction?

Explanation: The Reimer-Tiemann reaction specifically introduces a formyl (-CHO) group.

Question 11 of 19

What is the physical state of salicylaldehyde at room temperature?

Explanation: Salicylaldehyde is a colorless to pale yellow oily liquid with a bitter almond-like odor.

Question 12 of 19

Aliphatic alcohols fail to undergo the Reimer-Tiemann reaction because:

Explanation: The reaction requires an activated aromatic system (like phenol or pyrrole) to stabilize the carbene addition and subsequent steps.

Question 13 of 19

Which type of hydrogen bonding is present in salicylaldehyde?

Explanation: Salicylaldehyde exhibits strong intramolecular hydrogen bonding between the phenolic -OH group and the adjacent -CHO oxygen.

Question 14 of 19

What byproduct is eliminated when dichlorocarbene is formed from chloroform?

Explanation: The generation of dichlorocarbene involves the formal elimination of HCl from chloroform.

Question 15 of 19

How many moles of NaOH are consumed per mole of phenol in the standard stoichiometric Reimer-Tiemann reaction with chloroform?

Explanation: Three moles of NaOH are typically consumed: one to form the phenoxide ion and two to neutralize the HCl equivalents generated during carbene formation and hydrolysis.

Question 16 of 19

What is the color change or observable feature of the Reimer-Tiemann reaction mixture upon adding chloroform to alkaline phenol?

Explanation: The reaction mixture typically develops a pronounced color (often red or yellow) due to the formation of colored intermediates and phenoxide complexes.

Question 17 of 19

Which of the following describes the nature of dichlorocarbene?

Explanation: Dichlorocarbene has an incomplete octet (6 valence electrons) on carbon, making it a neutral electrophile.

Question 18 of 19

If the ortho-positions of phenol are blocked by alkyl groups, where does the Reimer-Tiemann formylation occur?

Explanation: If the ortho-positions are blocked, formylation takes place at the available para-position.

Question 19 of 19

Which IUPAC name correctly represents the primary product of the Reimer-Tiemann reaction on phenol?

Explanation: The primary product is salicylaldehyde, whose IUPAC name is 2-hydroxybenzaldehyde.

Question 1 of 10
What is the hybridization of the neutral carbon atom in dichlorocarbene?

Solution: The central carbon in dichlorocarbene has three electron domains (two single bonds with chlorine atoms and one lone pair), making its hybridization sp2.

Question 2 of 10
Which intermediate species directly precedes the formation of salicylaldehyde after dichlorocarbene attack on phenoxide?

Solution: Carbene addition forms a dichloromethyl-substituted cyclohexadienone intermediate that subsequently hydrolyzes to the aldehyde.

Question 3 of 10
In the mechanism of the Reimer-Tiemann reaction, proton abstraction from chloroform by hydroxide is classified as:

Solution: The loss of a proton and a chloride ion from the same carbon atom of chloroform is an alpha-elimination.

Question 4 of 10
Why is aqueous sodium hydroxide preferred over an alcoholic base for generating dichlorocarbene in this reaction?

Solution: Aqueous sodium hydroxide effectively deprotonates both chloroform and phenol in a biphasic or homogeneous aqueous-organic setup.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following statements is incorrect regarding the Reimer-Tiemann reaction?

Solution: The reaction proceeds via a neutral carbene intermediate, not a carbocation.

Question 6 of 10
What happens when 4-methylphenol (p-cresol) undergoes the Reimer-Tiemann reaction?

Solution: Since the para-position is blocked by a methyl group, formylation occurs at the available ortho-position.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following factors contributes to the stability of salicylaldehyde compared to its para-isomer?

Solution: Intramolecular hydrogen bonding between the phenolic OH and the formyl oxygen stabilizes the ortho-isomer (salicylaldehyde).

Question 8 of 10
What is the primary role of acidification during the workup stage of the Reimer-Tiemann reaction?

Solution: Acidification neutralizes the phenoxide salt and completes the hydrolysis to yield the neutral hydroxybenzaldehyde product.

Question 9 of 10
Which substrate will react fastest under Reimer-Tiemann conditions?

Solution: Phenol forms a highly reactive phenoxide ion in alkali, whereas nitrophenol is deactivated and anisole lacks the acidic hydroxyl group.

Question 10 of 10
The Reimer-Tiemann reaction is classified as a named reaction primarily used for:

Solution: The Reimer-Tiemann reaction is a classic method for the formylation of phenols and related aromatic substrates.
