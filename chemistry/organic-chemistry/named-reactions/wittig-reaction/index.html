Organic Chemistry

Wittig Reaction

The Wittig reaction is a chemical reaction of an aldehyde or ketone with a phosphonium ylide to yield an alkene and triphenylphosphine oxide. This method specifically constructs carbon-carbon double bonds at desired locations without ambiguity in regioisomer position.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Ylide Preparation and Structure

  • Phosphonium ylides, also known as phosphoranes, act as nucleophilic carbon species due to resonance stabilization between carbanionic and ylenic forms.
  • Primary and secondary alkyl halides undergo smooth SN2 attack with triphenylphosphine, while tertiary halides fail due to steric hindrance.
  • The choice of base depends on the acidity of the α-protons on the phosphonium salt, with strong bases like n-butyllithium required for simple alkyl triphenylphosphonium salts.

Reaction Mechanism and Intermediates

  • The reaction proceeds via nucleophilic addition of the ylide carbon to the carbonyl carbon, forming a dipolar intermediate called a betaine or directly a four-membered ring called an oxaphosphetane.
  • Oxaphosphetane is the proven key intermediate whose thermal decomposition dictates the stereochemical outcome of the alkene product.
  • The thermodynamic driving force of the reaction is the formation of the extremely stable triphenylphosphine oxide (Ph3P=O) byproduct.

Stereochemical Control in Wittig Olefination

  • Unstabilized ylides lack electron-withdrawing groups and typically yield Z-alkenes (cis-alkenes) as kinetic products via non-reversible erythro-betaine intermediates.
  • Stabilized ylides possess adjacent electron-withdrawing groups (e.g., esters, ketones) and yield thermodynamically favored E-alkenes (trans-alkenes).
  • The Schlosser modification of the Wittig reaction permits the stereoselective isomerization of kinetic Z-alkenes into thermodynamic E-alkenes.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl halides readily form phosphonium salts via SN2 mechanism.

Observation: Tertiary alkyl halides and aryl halides do not form phosphonium salts. — Steric hindrance prevents backside SN2 attack on tertiary centers, and aryl halides lack leaving group reactivity.

Exception Wittig reaction yields alkenes with aldehydes and ketones.

Observation: Sterically hindered ketones (e.g., di-tert-butyl ketone) fail to react. — Extreme steric congestion blocks the nucleophilic attack of the ylide carbon onto the carbonyl carbon.

Exception Unstabilized ylides yield exclusively Z-alkenes.

Observation: Horner-Wadsworth-Emmons modification (using phosphonate esters) yields E-alkenes predominantly. — Phosphonate carbanions form reversible intermediates that equilibrate to the thermodynamically most stable E-isomer.

Z/E Prediction Rule
Unstabilized = Z (cis), Stabilized = E (trans)

Application: Quickly identify whether the ylide has an electron-withdrawing group to instantly predict the stereochemistry of the resulting alkene.

Byproduct Driving Force

Application: Use the extremely strong phosphorus-oxygen double bond (∼ 543 kJ/mol) to justify the irreversibility of the cycloreversion step in exam mechanisms.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The Wittig reaction converts aldehydes and ketones into alkenes using phosphonium ylides.
  • Triphenylphosphine (Ph3P) reacts with alkyl halides in an SN2 fashion to form phosphonium salts.
  • Strong bases like butyl-lithium (BuLi) deprotonate phosphonium salts to generate reactive ylides.
  • The reaction proceeds through a four-membered cyclic intermediate termed an oxaphosphetane.
  • Triphenylphosphine oxide is formed as a stable, crystalline byproduct driving the reaction equilibrium.
  • Unstabilized ylides predominantly give Z-alkenes, whereas stabilized ylides give E-alkenes.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming tertiary alkyl halides can form phosphonium salts; they undergo elimination instead.
  • Forgetting that moisture must be strictly excluded during ylide generation because ylides are strong bases that react with water.
  • Confusing the stereochemical outcome of unstabilized ylides (cis/Z) with stabilized ylides (trans/E).
  • Failing to account for the formation of Ph3P=O as the thermodynamic driving force.

⚡ Quick Revision: Wittig Reaction

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Phosphonium Salt Synthesis

Alkyl halides react with triphenylphosphine via an SN2 mechanism to produce phosphonium salts, limited strictly by steric hindrance at the reaction center.

Ylide Generation

Strong bases remove the acidic proton adjacent to the phosphonium center, generating a resonance-stabilized ylide (phosphorane) with nucleophilic carbon character.

Oxaphosphetane Intermediate

Addition of the ylide to aldehydes or ketones forms a transient betaine that collapses into a four-membered oxaphosphetane ring.

Stereochemical Control

Unstabilized ylides deliver Z-alkenes via kinetic control, while stabilized ylides afford E-alkenes through reversible thermodynamic pathways.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Ylide (R2C--PPh3+)
A neutral dipolar molecule with adjacent positively charged heteroatom and negatively charged carbon.
Phosphonium salt ([R4P]+X-)
Quaternary phosphorus salt formed from alkyl halide and triphenylphosphine.
Oxaphosphetane (Cyclic C-O-P-C intermediate)
Four-membered cyclic intermediate in the Wittig reaction.
Betaine (Dipolar intermediate)
Open-chain dipolar species formed prior to oxaphosphetane ring closure.
Schlosser Modification (Modified Wittig conditions)
Method to convert kinetic Z-alkenes into thermodynamic E-alkenes using phenyllithium.
HWE Reaction (Phosphonate modification)
Wittig variant using phosphonate esters to yield E-alkenes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Reagents: Alkyl halide + Ph3P → Salt + Base → Ylide + Carbonyl → Alkene
  • Driven by formation of extremely stable Ph3P=O
  • Unstabilized ylide → Z-alkene
  • Stabilized ylide → E-alkene
  • Steric hindrance prevents reaction with di-tert-butyl ketone

📌 Quick Recall

  • Wittig reaction constructs C=C bonds precisely without double bond migration.
  • Requires phosphonium salt generation followed by deprotonation with strong base.
  • Proceeds via oxaphosphetane intermediate cycloreversion.
  • Stereoselectivity is dictated by ylide stabilization.

⚖️ Key Differences

Unstabilized Ylide VS Stabilized Ylide

Unstabilized ylides lack electron-withdrawing groups and yield Z-alkenes; stabilized ylides have electron-withdrawing groups and yield E-alkenes.

Which reagent is used to prepare the phosphonium salt in the first step of the Wittig reaction?

Explanation: Triphenylphosphine (Ph3P) acts as a nucleophile in an SN2 reaction with alkyl halides to form phosphonium salts.

Question 2 of 19

What is the primary thermodynamic driving force of the Wittig reaction?

Explanation: The extremely strong P=O double bond in triphenylphosphine oxide provides the thermodynamic sink driving the reaction.

Question 3 of 19

Which intermediate is formed when a phosphonium ylide reacts with a ketone?

Explanation: The reaction proceeds via nucleophilic addition to form a betaine, which rapidly closes to a four-membered oxaphosphetane ring.

Question 4 of 19

Unstabilized phosphonium ylides predominantly yield which alkene stereoisomer?

Explanation: Unstabilized ylides react under kinetic control to predominantly give Z-alkenes.

Question 5 of 19

What type of base is typically required to deprotonate simple alkyl triphenylphosphonium salts?

Explanation: Protons on the carbon adjacent to the phosphonium center have moderate acidity, requiring strong non-nucleophilic bases like n-BuLi.

Question 6 of 19

Which alkyl halide is completely unreactive toward phosphonium salt formation?

Explanation: Tertiary alkyl halides undergo rapid elimination rather than SN2 substitution due to severe steric hindrance.

Question 7 of 19

Stabilized ylides containing electron-withdrawing groups preferentially yield which alkene isomer?

Explanation: Stabilized ylides form reversible intermediates, leading predominantly to the thermodynamically more stable E-alkene.

Question 8 of 19

What is the byproduct of the Wittig reaction besides the alkene?

Explanation: Triphenylphosphine oxide (Ph3P=O) is the stoichiometric phosphorus-containing byproduct.

Question 9 of 19

In the Wittig reaction, the ylide carbon acts as a:

Explanation: Due to resonance stabilization, the carbon bonded to phosphorus carries partial negative charge and acts as a nucleophile toward carbonyl carbons.

Question 10 of 19

Which modification uses phosphonate esters instead of phosphonium salts to yield E-alkenes?

Explanation: The Horner-Wadsworth-Emmons (HWE) reaction utilizes phosphonate carbanions for stereoselective E-alkene synthesis.

Question 11 of 19

Why must water be strictly excluded during ylide generation?

Explanation: Phosphonium ylides are strong carbanionic bases that are immediately protonated and destroyed by protic solvents like water.

Question 12 of 19

What is the oxidation state of phosphorus in triphenylphosphine oxide?

Explanation: Phosphorus forms five bonds (counting the double bond as two) and exhibits a +5 oxidation state.

Question 13 of 19

Which functional group cannot be successfully converted to an alkene via standard Wittig conditions due to steric hindrance?

Explanation: Di-tert-butyl ketone is too sterically hindered for the nucleophilic ylide to attack the carbonyl carbon.

Question 14 of 19

Which scientist shared the 1979 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the development of the Wittig reaction?

Explanation: Georg Wittig received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1979 for developing the use of phosphorus-containing compounds in organic synthesis.

Question 15 of 19

What is the geometry of the oxaphosphetane intermediate?

Explanation: Oxaphosphetane is a four-membered heterocyclic ring containing carbon, oxygen, and phosphorus atoms.

Question 16 of 19

Which reaction condition favors the Schlosser modification of the Wittig reaction?

Explanation: Schlosser modification involves betaine formation, metalation, and controlled protonation to force E-alkene formation.

Question 17 of 19

What role does the alkyl halide play in the preparation of phosphonium salts?

Explanation: The alkyl halide undergoes nucleophilic substitution by triphenylphosphine acting as an electrophile.

Question 18 of 19

Which statement is correct regarding the regioselectivity of the Wittig reaction?

Explanation: The Wittig reaction is regiospecific; the alkene double bond is formed exactly at the carbon atom originally possessing the carbonyl oxygen.

Question 19 of 19

What is the visual appearance of triphenylphosphine oxide byproduct in many organic workups?

Explanation: Triphenylphosphine oxide is a crystalline solid that often requires precipitation with hexane or chromatography for complete removal.

What is the primary product of the reaction between cyclohexanone and methyltriphenylphosphonium iodide in the presence of n-BuLi?

Solution: Cyclohexanone reacts with methylenetriphenylphosphorane to yield methylenecyclohexane.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following ylides is classified as stabilized?

Solution: Ylides adjacent to electron-withdrawing ester groups are stabilized.

Question 3 of 10
In the Horner-Wadsworth-Emmons modification, phosphonate carbanions are used instead of phosphonium ylides. What is the major stereochemical outcome?

Solution: Phosphonate carbanions favor thermodynamic equilibration leading to E-alkenes.

Question 4 of 10
Why do tertiary alkyl halides fail to form phosphonium salts?

Solution: Steric hindrance at tertiary centers blocks SN2 attack, promoting base-induced elimination.

Question 5 of 10
What is the hybridization change of the carbonyl carbon during a Wittig reaction?

Solution: Carbonyl carbon starts as sp2, becomes part of an sp3 intermediate ring (oxaphosphetane), and ends up as sp2 in the alkene.

Question 6 of 10
Which solvent system is typically employed for the generation of phosphonium ylides?

Solution: Anhydrous ethereal solvents like THF prevent premature protonation of the moisture-sensitive ylide.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following describes the nature of phosphorus in a phosphonium ylide?

Solution: The phosphorus atom is positively charged and electrophilic, while the adjacent carbon bears carbanionic character.

Question 8 of 10
What happens when benzaldehyde reacts with ethoxycarbonylmethylenetriphenylphosphorane?

Solution: Stabilized ylides react with benzaldehyde to give alpha,beta-unsaturated esters with E-stereoselectivity.

Question 9 of 10
Which named reaction is formally analogous to the Wittig reaction but utilizes silanes?

Solution: The Peterson olefination uses α-silylated carbanions analogously to phosphonium ylides.

Question 10 of 10
What is the stoichiometric ratio of alkyl halide to triphenylphosphine in phosphonium salt synthesis?

Solution: One molecule of triphenylphosphine reacts with one molecule of alkyl halide in a 1:1 ratio.
