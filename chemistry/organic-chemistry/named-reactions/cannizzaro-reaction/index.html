Cannizzaro Reaction

The Cannizzaro reaction is a base-induced disproportionation of aldehydes lacking alpha-hydrogens, yielding a primary alcohol and a carboxylate salt. This self-oxidation-reduction process proceeds via nucleophilic addition followed by a rapid hydride transfer step.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Reaction Mechanism and Hydride Transfer

  • The hydroxide ion attacks the carbonyl carbon of the aldehyde lacking α-hydrogens to form a tetrahedral dianionic intermediate.
  • A hydride ion (H-) is transferred from the dianion to a second molecule of aldehyde in the rate-determining step.
  • The products formed are a carboxylate anion and a alkoxide ion, which subsequently abstracts a proton from the solvent to yield the primary alcohol.

Substrate Scope and Structural Requirements

  • Aldehydes lacking α-hydrogens such as formaldehyde (HCHO), benzaldehyde (C6H5CHO), and trimethylacetaldehyde ((CH3)3CCHO) undergo the Cannizzaro reaction.
  • Presence of α-hydrogens leads to competing aldol condensation reactions due to rapid enolate formation under basic conditions.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups on aromatic rings accelerate the nucleophilic addition step, increasing the overall reaction rate.

Crossed Cannizzaro Reaction Dynamics

  • When a mixture of formaldehyde and another non-enilizable aldehyde is treated with alkali, formaldehyde is oxidized to formate while the other aldehyde is reduced to alcohol.
  • Formaldehyde possesses a higher reduction potential and acts as the preferred hydride donor, ensuring high selectivity for the reduction of the co-reactant.
  • Intramolecular Cannizzaro reactions occur in substrates like glyoxal (OHC-CHO), yielding glycolic acid derivatives within a single molecular framework.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Rate Law for Uncrossed Cannizzaro Reaction
Rate = k[RCHO]2[OH-]2
Conditions: High concentrations of strong base for non-aromatic and certain aromatic aldehydes
Units: Rate constant k in L3 mol-3 s-1
Intramolecular Rate Expression
Rate = k[Glyoxal][OH-]
Conditions: Glyoxal or dialdehydes without α-hydrogens undergoing internal disproportionation
Units: Rate constant k in L mol-1 s-1
Crossed Cannizzaro Reaction Yield with Formaldehyde
ArCHO + HCHO + OH- → ArCH2OH + HCOO-
Conditions: Formaldehyde acts exclusively as the sacrificial reductant (hydride donor)
Units: Molar conversion efficiency
Overall Stoichiometry of Cannizzaro Reaction
2 RCHO + OH- → RCH2OH + RCOO-
Conditions: Concentrated aqueous or alcoholic strong base (NaOH or KOH), absence of α-hydrogens
Units: Stoichiometric molar ratio: 2 moles aldehyde : 1 mole base
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aldehydes with alpha-hydrogens undergo Cannizzaro reaction with strong base

Observation: Aldol condensation occurs instead — Base abstracts the acidic α-proton faster than it adds to the carbonyl carbon, forming enolate ions.

Exception Aromatic aldehydes with strong electron-donating groups react normally

Observation: Reaction rate is significantly retarded or fails completely — Electron-donating groups (e.g., -OCH3, -NR2) decrease the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon toward hydroxide attack.

Exception Crossed Cannizzaro yields a statistical 50:50 mixture of two alcohols and two acids

Observation: Nearly 100% yield of one specific alcohol and acid is obtained — Using formaldehyde as one reactant selectively reduces it to formate while exclusively reducing the second aldehyde to alcohol.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Alpha-Hydrogen Check Mnemonic
Count carbons adjacent to -CHO

Application: If adjacent carbon has zero hydrogens, immediately consider Cannizzaro reaction instead of Aldol condensation.

Formaldehyde Reductant Rule
HCHO + ArCHO + OH- → HCOO- + ArCH2OH

Application: In crossed Cannizzaro with formaldehyde, formaldehyde always becomes the acid (oxidized) and the other aldehyde becomes the alcohol (reduced).

Disproportionation Stoichiometry
2 Equivalent Aldehyde = 1 Equivalent Alcohol + 1 Equivalent Carboxylate

Application: Use the 2:1 stoichiometric ratio when calculating limiting reagents and theoretical yields in numerical problems.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Cannizzaro reaction is a classical example of intermolecular oxidation-reduction (disproportionation).
  • Formaldehyde and benzaldehyde are classic substrates that lack alpha-hydrogens.
  • The rate-determining step in the standard mechanism is the transfer of the hydride ion.
  • Crossed Cannizzaro using formaldehyde as a sacrificial agent yields specific reduction products.
  • Intramolecular Cannizzaro occurs with compounds like glyoxal and dialdehydes.
  • Strong bases such as 50% NaOH or KOH are mandatory for driving the reaction.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming aliphatic aldehydes with alpha-hydrogens can undergo the Cannizzaro reaction.
  • Forgetting that the reaction consumes 1 mole of base per 2 moles of aldehyde.
  • Confusing the role of formaldehyde in crossed Cannizzaro reactions as the substrate being reduced instead of oxidized.
  • Omitting the requirement for concentrated base conditions.

⚡ Quick Revision: Cannizzaro Reaction

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Redox Nature

One molecule of aldehyde is oxidized to a carboxylic acid salt while another is reduced to an alcohol simultaneously.

Substrate Specificity

Restricted strictly to aldehydes lacking alpha-hydrogens to prevent competing aldol condensation pathways.

Hydride Transfer Mechanism

Involves nucleophilic addition of OH- followed by direct hydride transfer from the intermediate dianion.

Synthetic Utility

Provides a direct route to produce primary alcohols and carboxylates from non-enolizable aldehydes.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Disproportionation (Redox Process)
A chemical reaction where a single substance is simultaneously oxidized and reduced.
Alpha-Hydrogen (C-H on α-carbon)
Hydrogen atoms attached to the carbon atom directly adjacent to the carbonyl carbon.
Crossed Cannizzaro (Mixed Reaction)
Reaction between two different non-enolizable aldehydes, typically with formaldehyde.
Hydride Shift (H- Transfer)
Movement of a hydrogen atom with both bonding electrons between carbon centers.
Dianionic Intermediate (Gem-diol alkoxide)
Species possessing two negative charges formed after initial hydroxide addition.
Non-enolizable (No α-H)
Compounds incapable of tautomerizing to enol forms due to absence of alpha-hydrogens.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • 2 RCHO + OH- → RCH2OH + RCOO-
  • Requires concentrated base and zero alpha-hydrogens
  • Formaldehyde acts as a universal reductant in crossed Cannizzaro
  • Rate law is third or fourth order overall depending on conditions
  • Intramolecular version occurs in 1,2-dialdehydes

📌 Quick Recall

  • Cannizzaro is a base-catalyzed disproportionation of non-enolizable aldehydes.
  • Produces equimolar amounts of alcohol and carboxylate salt in simple systems.
  • Hydride transfer represents the key mechanistic step.
  • Formaldehyde is exceptionally useful in crossed Cannizzaro reactions.

⚖️ Key Differences

Cannizzaro Reaction VS Aldol Condensation

Cannizzaro requires no alpha-hydrogens and gives redox products; Aldol requires alpha-hydrogens and forms carbon-carbon bonds.

What are the products formed when benzaldehyde is treated with concentrated NaOH?

The rate-determining step in the Cannizzaro reaction is:

Explanation: The transfer of a hydride ion from the dianionic tetrahedral intermediate to another molecule of aldehyde is the rate-determining step.

Question 4 of 19

Why do aldehydes with alpha-hydrogens fail to give Cannizzaro reaction under normal conditions?

Explanation: Base abstracts acidic alpha-hydrogens to form enolates, favoring aldol condensation over nucleophilic addition to the carbonyl.

Question 5 of 19

In a crossed Cannizzaro reaction between formaldehyde and benzaldehyde, the major oxidation product is:

Explanation: Formaldehyde is more easily oxidized and acts as the reducing agent, converting into sodium formate while benzaldehyde is reduced to benzyl alcohol.

Question 6 of 19

Which reagent is typically employed to effect the Cannizzaro reaction?

Explanation: Concentrated aqueous or alcoholic sodium hydroxide (typically 50%) is required to drive the disproportionation.

Question 7 of 19

Intramolecular Cannizzaro reaction is exhibited by:

Explanation: Glyoxal (OHC-CHO) has two adjacent aldehyde groups with no alpha-hydrogens, undergoing internal disproportionation.

Question 8 of 19

The stoichiometry of aldehyde to base in a simple Cannizzaro reaction is:

Explanation: Two moles of aldehyde react with one mole of hydroxide base to form one mole of alcohol and one mole of carboxylate.

Question 9 of 19

What type of reaction is the Cannizzaro reaction classified as?

Explanation: It is a disproportionation reaction where one molecule is oxidized and another is reduced.

Question 10 of 19

Which of the following does not possess alpha-hydrogens?

Explanation: Formaldehyde (HCHO) has no carbon attached to the carbonyl carbon, hence zero alpha-hydrogens.

Question 11 of 19

The intermediate formed immediately after hydroxide ion attack on the carbonyl carbon is:

Explanation: Nucleophilic attack of OH- on the carbonyl carbon generates a tetrahedral alkoxide species (dianion in strong base).

Question 12 of 19

What is the oxidation state change for the carbon undergoing reduction in benzaldehyde during Cannizzaro reaction?

Explanation: Carbonyl carbon in benzaldehyde is in +1 oxidation state (approximate formal oxidation state of carbonyl carbon in aldehyde), changing to 0 in benzyl alcohol (-1 oxidation state of CH2OH carbon relative to aromatic ring).

Question 13 of 19

Can ketones undergo the Cannizzaro reaction?

Explanation: Ketones lack the formyl hydrogen necessary for the hydride transfer and typically undergo aldol or self-condensation reactions.

Question 14 of 19

In the mechanism of Cannizzaro reaction, the hydride is transferred from:

Explanation: Hydride transfer originates from the tetrahedral addition intermediate formed by the first aldehyde molecule and hydroxide.

Question 15 of 19

Which aromatic aldehyde will react fastest in a Cannizzaro reaction?

Explanation: p-Nitrobenzaldehyde contains a strong electron-withdrawing nitro group, which increases the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon and accelerates attack.

Question 16 of 19

What is the final organic product derived from the oxidized portion of benzaldehyde in a Cannizzaro reaction using KOH?

Explanation: Oxidation of benzaldehyde yields benzoic acid, which exists as potassium benzoate in the presence of excess KOH.

Question 17 of 19

Trichloroacetaldehyde (chloral) undergoes Cannizzaro reaction with base rather than aldol condensation because:

Explanation: Chloral (CCl3CHO) has no alpha-hydrogens on the adjacent carbon, forcing it to undergo Cannizzaro reaction.

Question 18 of 19

Which solvent system is most commonly used when studying Cannizzaro kinetics?

Explanation: Aqueous or aqueous-alcoholic solutions of strong bases provide the necessary hydroxide concentration and polarity.

Question 19 of 19

The driving force for the irreversible nature of many Cannizzaro reactions is:

Explanation: Formation of stable carboxylate anions and neutral alcohols makes the overall sequence thermodynamically favorable and practically irreversible.

Electron-withdrawing substituents on the benzene ring of benzaldehyde in a Cannizzaro reaction:

Question 7 of 10
Why is formaldehyde selected as the reductant in crossed Cannizzaro reactions?

Solution: Formaldehyde lacks alpha-hydrogens and readily acts as a sacrificial hydride donor, ensuring high yield of the desired alcohol from the co-reactant.
