Organic Chemistry

Acid Derivatives

Carboxylic acid derivatives are compounds where the hydroxyl group of a carboxylic acid is replaced by a heteroatom such as halogen, alkoxy, amino, or acyloxy group. Their general formula is RCOZ, encompassing acyl halides, anhydrides, esters, and amides.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structure and Relative Reactivity of Acid Derivatives

  • The carbonyl carbon in acid derivatives bears a partial positive charge, rendering it susceptible to nucleophilic attack.
  • Reactivity is inversely proportional to the basicity of the leaving group and directly proportional to the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon.
  • Acyl chlorides are the most reactive due to the poor resonance donation of chlorine and its high electronegativity.
  • Amides are the least reactive derivatives because the lone pair on nitrogen is strongly delocalized into the carbonyl π-system via resonance.

Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution Mechanism

  • Reactions proceed via a two-step addition-elimination mechanism involving a tetrahedral intermediate.
  • The first step is rate-determining, involving the addition of a nucleophile to the electrophilic carbonyl carbon.
  • The second step involves the collapse of the tetrahedral intermediate with the expulsion of the leaving group.
  • Stronger nucleophiles like alkoxides, amines, and hydroxide ions readily react without catalysts, whereas weaker nucleophiles require acid or base activation.

Interconversion and Specific Chemical Reactions

  • A more reactive acid derivative can be converted into a less reactive derivative using appropriate nucleophiles.
  • Esters undergo acid-catalyzed (reversible) or base-promoted (irreversible saponification) hydrolysis.
  • Amides undergo dehydration upon heating with phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5) to yield nitriles.
  • Reduction of esters and acid chlorides with LiAlH4 yields primary alcohols, whereas reduction with DIBAH at low temperatures stops at the aldehyde stage.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Less reactive acid derivatives cannot directly convert into more reactive ones via simple nucleophilic substitution.

Observation: Amides or esters cannot be directly converted to acyl chlorides using HCl. — Thermodynamic unfavorability; stronger leaving groups cannot be displaced by weaker bases without specialized reagents like SOCl2 on acids.

Exception Reduction of acid derivatives with LiAlH4 yields primary alcohols.

Observation: Tertiary amides yield tertiary amines upon reduction with LiAlH4. — The oxygen of the tertiary amide is completely removed as an aluminate complex, leaving the carbon-nitrogen single bond intact.

Exception Esters generally react with Grignard reagents to form tertiary alcohols after workup.

Observation: Reaction of ethyl formate with excess Grignard reagent yields a secondary alcohol. — Formate esters contain a hydrogen atom attached to the carbonyl carbon instead of an alkyl group, yielding an aldehyde intermediate that reacts further.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Reactivity Memory Mnemonic
Cl - An - Es - Am

Application: Chloride > Anhydride > Ester > Amide. Remember: 'Clowns Always Eat Apples' (Chloride, Anhydride, Ester, Amide) for descending order of reactivity.

Leaving Group Basicity Rule
pKa of Conjugate Acid ← Reactivity

Application: The lower the pKa of the leaving group's conjugate acid, the better the leaving group and the more reactive the derivative.

Reduction Product Shortcut
RCO-Z → RCH2OH + ZH

Application: With LiAlH4, replace the entire -Z group (except in amides where oxygen is removed) and reduce the carbonyl to -CH2OH.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Acyl chlorides are synthesized from carboxylic acids using SOCl2, PCl3, or PCl5.
  • Acid anhydrides are formed by the dehydration of two carboxylic acid molecules with heating or acetic anhydride reaction.
  • Transesterification is an equilibrium process catalyzed by either acid or base where one ester is converted into another.
  • Hofmann bromamide degradation converts primary amides into primary amines with one less carbon atom using Br2 and KOH.
  • Claisen condensation involves the self-condensation of two moles of an ester possessing α-hydrogens in the presence of a strong base like NaOEt.
  • Amides exhibit high melting and boiling points compared to other derivatives of comparable molecular weight due to intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming Grignard reagents yield ketones when reacted with esters; they react twice to yield tertiary alcohols.
  • Confusing the role of pyridine in acylation reactions; it acts as a mild base to scavenge HCl, not as a nucleophilic catalyst.
  • Forgetting that acid-catalyzed esterification is reversible and requires removal of water or excess reagent to drive equilibrium forward.
  • Confusing Hofmann degradation (removes carbonyl carbon) with reduction (retains carbon skeleton).

⚡ Quick Revision: Acid Derivatives

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Synthesis of Derivatives

Carboxylic acids serve as the central precursor for all acid derivatives via activation of the hydroxyl group.

Reactivity Trends

Basicity of the leaving group dictates nucleophilic acyl substitution rates, following the order: Cl- > RCOO- > RO- > R2N-.

Hydrolysis Pathways

Esters undergo both acidic and basic hydrolysis, where basic hydrolysis (saponification) proceeds irreversibly to yield carboxylate salts.

Nitrogenous Derivatives

Amides display strong resonance stabilization, lowering their electrophilicity and increasing their stability toward nucleophilic attack.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Acyl Chloride (RCOCl)
Derivative containing a carbonyl bonded to a chlorine atom.
Acid Anhydride ((RCO)2O)
Derivative formed by condensation of two acid molecules with loss of water.
Ester (RCOOR')
Derivative containing an alkoxy group bonded to a carbonyl carbon.
Amide (RCONH2)
Derivative containing an amino group bonded to a carbonyl carbon.
Saponification (Base Hydrolysis)
Base-catalyzed hydrolysis of esters yielding carboxylate and alcohol.
Transesterification (Ester Interconversion)
Exchange of the alkoxy group of an ester with an incoming alcohol.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Reactivity order: Acyl Chloride > Anhydride > Ester > Amide
  • LiAlH4 reduces esters and acyl chlorides to primary alcohols.
  • DIBAH at -78°C reduces esters selectively to aldehydes.
  • P2O5 dehydrates amides to form nitriles.
  • Hofmann degradation shortens carbon chain by one carbon.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nucleophilic acyl substitution is the signature reaction of acid derivatives.
  • Leaving group ability correlates directly with the acidity of its conjugate acid.
  • Resonance stabilization in amides explains their low reactivity and high melting points.
  • Grignard reagents react with esters twice to form tertiary alcohols.

⚖️ Key Differences

Acyl Chloride Synthesis via SOCl2 VS Alkyl Chloride Synthesis via SOCl2

Acyl chlorides retain the carbonyl group (RCOCl), whereas alkyl chlorides replace the -OH entirely (RCl).

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following acid derivatives is the most reactive toward nucleophilic acyl substitution?

Explanation: Acetyl chloride is the most reactive because chlorine is a poor resonance donor and a weak base, making it an excellent leaving group.

Question 2 of 18

What is the product when an ester is treated with two equivalents of a Grignard reagent followed by acidic workup?

Explanation: Esters react with Grignard reagents to form a ketone intermediate, which immediately reacts with a second equivalent of Grignard reagent to yield a tertiary alcohol.

Question 3 of 18

Which reagent selectively reduces an ester to an aldehyde at low temperatures (-78°C)?

Explanation: Diisobutylaluminium hydride (DIBAH) at -78°C stops at the aldehyde stage by forming a stable tetrahedral aluminum intermediate.

Question 4 of 18

In the alkaline hydrolysis of an ester (saponification), the reaction is:

Explanation: Saponification is irreversible because the resulting carboxylate anion is resonance-stabilized and does not react with the alcohol; it follows second-order kinetics (Rate = k[Ester][OH-]).

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following derivatives exhibits the highest carbonyl stretching frequency in IR spectroscopy?

Explanation: Propanoyl chloride has the highest carbonyl stretching frequency (~1810 cm-1) due to the strong -I effect of chlorine decreasing electron density in the π-bond.

Question 6 of 18

What product is formed when acetamide is heated with phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5)?

Explanation: P2O5 is a dehydrating agent that removes water from primary amides to yield nitriles (acetonitrile).

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following amines will react with acetyl chloride to form an N-substituted amide?

Explanation: Both primary and secondary amines possess replaceable N-H protons and can form stable amides upon reacting with acyl chlorides.

Question 8 of 18

Hofmann bromamide degradation of benzamide yields:

Explanation: Hofmann degradation converts a primary amide into a primary amine with the loss of the carbonyl carbon, transforming benzamide into aniline.

Question 9 of 18

What is the role of pyridine in the reaction of an acid chloride with an alcohol?

Explanation: Pyridine acts as a weak organic base to neutralize the HCl byproduct generated during esterification, preventing side reactions.

Question 10 of 18

Which acid derivative does not undergo hydrolysis easily under mild acidic conditions?

Explanation: Amides are the most stable toward hydrolysis due to strong resonance stabilization of the C-N bond, requiring harsh acidic or basic conditions.

Question 11 of 18

Transesterification involves:

Explanation: Transesterification is an equilibrium process where an ester reacts with an alcohol to exchange its alkoxy group.

Question 12 of 18

Which of the following compounds is formed when acetic acid reacts with thionyl chloride?

Explanation: Thionyl chloride (SOCl2) converts carboxylic acids into acyl chlorides with gaseous SO2 and HCl as byproducts.

Question 13 of 18

The order of leaving group ability in nucleophilic acyl substitution is:

Explanation: Leaving group ability is directly related to the stability of the anion, which is inversely related to basicity: Cl- is the weakest base and best leaving group.

Question 14 of 18

Reduction of an amide with LiAlH4 yields:

Explanation: Reduction of a primary amide with LiAlH4 replaces the carbonyl oxygen with two hydrogens, yielding a primary amine.

Question 15 of 18

Claisen condensation requires an ester possessing:

Explanation: Claisen condensation proceeds via deprotonation at the α-carbon to form an enolate ion, requiring at least one α-hydrogen.

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following reagents converts an acid anhydride into two equivalents of a carboxylic acid?

Explanation: Hydrolysis of acid anhydrides with water yields two molecules of the corresponding carboxylic acid.

Question 17 of 18

Why are amides less reactive than esters?

Explanation: Strong resonance donation from nitrogen into the carbonyl group reduces the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon in amides.

Question 18 of 18

What is the byproduct formed when an acid chloride reacts with an alcohol?

Explanation: The reaction of an acyl chloride with an alcohol yields an ester and hydrochloric acid (HCl) as a byproduct.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following derivatives undergoes transesterification most readily?

Solution: Transesterification is the exchange of the alkoxy group of an ester with another alcohol.

Question 2 of 10
What is the intermediate formed during nucleophilic acyl substitution of acid derivatives?

Solution: Nucleophilic attack on the carbonyl carbon forms a sp3-hybridized tetrahedral intermediate before elimination occurs.

Question 3 of 10
Which reagent reduces an acid chloride selectively to an aldehyde without reducing it further to an alcohol?

Solution: The Rosenmund reduction uses poisoned palladium on barium sulfate to stop the reduction of acyl chlorides at the aldehyde stage.

Question 4 of 10
The reaction of methylmagnesium iodide with ethyl acetate yields ultimately:

Solution: Grignard reagents react twice with esters, first forming a ketone and then reacting further to yield a tertiary alcohol.

Question 5 of 10
Which derivative is formed when benzoic anhydride reacts with excess methanol?

Solution: Reaction of an acid anhydride with an alcohol yields one equivalent of an ester and one equivalent of a carboxylic acid.

Question 6 of 10
In the Claisen condensation of ethyl propanoate in the presence of sodium ethoxide, the base removes a proton from:

Solution: The base abstracts an acidic α-proton from the ester to generate a resonance-stabilized enolate ion.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following compounds is insoluble in cold dilute HCl but soluble in aqueous NaOH?

Solution: Acetamide is neutral/very weakly basic and does not dissolve in HCl, but it undergoes hydrolysis in aqueous NaOH or exhibits weak acidic properties on nitrogen.

Question 8 of 10
What is the product of the reaction between acetyl chloride and dimethylamine?

Solution: Reaction of acetyl chloride with a secondary amine (dimethylamine) yields a tertiary amide, N,N-dimethylacetamide.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following statements is incorrect regarding acid derivatives?

Solution: Esters undergo rapid and irreversible hydrolysis in basic medium (saponification).

Question 10 of 10
During Hofmann bromamide degradation, the migrating group shifts from the carbonyl carbon to:

Solution: In the Hofmann rearrangement, an alkyl or aryl group migrates from the carbonyl carbon to the electron-deficient nitrogen atom of the intermediate nitrene/isocyanate.
