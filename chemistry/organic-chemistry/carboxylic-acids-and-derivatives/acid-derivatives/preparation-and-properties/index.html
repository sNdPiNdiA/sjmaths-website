Preparation and Properties of Carboxylic Acid Derivatives

Carboxylic acid derivatives including acid chlorides, anhydrides, esters, and amides are synthesized primarily through nucleophilic acyl substitution reactions. Their chemical reactivity is governed by the basicity of the leaving group and the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthesis of Acid Derivatives

  • Acid chlorides are prepared by reacting carboxylic acids with thionyl chloride (SOCl2), phosphorus trichloride (PCl3), or phosphorus pentachloride (PCl5).
  • Anhydrides are synthesized via the dehydration of two carboxylic acid molecules using P2O5 or by reacting an acid chloride with a carboxylate salt.
  • Esters are classically obtained through Fischer esterification of carboxylic acids with alcohols in the presence of concentrated H2SO4.
  • Amides are formed by the thermal dehydration of ammonium carboxylate salts or via the ammonolysis of acid chlorides and esters.

Reactivity and Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution

  • The general reactivity order toward nucleophilic acyl substitution is: Acid chloride > Acid anhydride > Ester > Amide.
  • Reactivity is inversely proportional to the stability of the leaving group, which correlates directly with the basicity of the conjugate acid.
  • Acid chlorides react rapidly with water, alcohols, and amines due to the high electronegativity and poor resonance donation of the chlorine atom.
  • Amides are the least reactive derivatives because the lone pair on nitrogen is delocalized into the carbonyl π-system via resonance.

Chemical Properties and Interconversions

  • Hydrolysis of all carboxylic acid derivatives yields the parent carboxylic acid under acidic conditions or carboxylate salts under basic conditions.
  • Reaction of acid derivatives with Grignard reagents (R'MgX) leads to tertiary alcohols, consuming two equivalents of the reagent.
  • Reduction using LiAlH4 converts esters, acid chlorides, and anhydrides into primary alcohols, while amides are reduced to amines.
  • Claisen condensation involves the self-condensation of esters containing α-hydrogens in the presence of a strong base like NaOEt to form β-keto esters.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Esters generally require strong acid catalysis for hydrolysis

Observation: Base-catalyzed saponification is irreversible — The reaction goes to completion because the carboxylate anion formed is resonance-stabilized and electrostatically repels the attacking nucleophile.

Exception Grignard reagents convert acid derivatives to tertiary alcohols

Observation: Reaction with cadmium reagents (R2Cd) stops at ketones — Organocadmium compounds are less nucleophilic than Grignard reagents and do not react further with ketones.

Exception Acyl chlorides react vigorously with water to form acids

Observation: Sterically hindered acyl chlorides show sluggish hydrolysis — Bulky alkyl or aryl groups block the approach of the water molecule to the electrophilic carbonyl carbon.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Leaving Group Basicity Rule
Reactivity ∝ 1 / Basicity of Leaving Group

Application: Weaker bases (Cl-, RCOO-) make better leaving groups, rendering the derivative more reactive.

Grignard Consumption Count
Acid Chloride / Ester = 2 Equivalents; Amide = 2 Equivalents

Application: Always remember that esters and acid chlorides consume 2 moles of Grignard reagent to yield tertiary alcohols.

Reduction Product Mapping
Derivative + LiAlH4 → Alcohols (except Amide → Amine)

Application: Esters, anhydrides, and acid chlorides yield primary alcohols, whereas amides uniquely yield amines upon reduction.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Acid chlorides react with alcohols to form esters in the presence of pyridine as an HCl scavenger.
  • Amides undergo Hofmann bromamide degradation with Br2 and NaOH to yield primary amines with one less carbon atom.
  • Esters undergo saponification upon heating with aqueous alkali to yield soap and glycerol.
  • Anhydrides are prepared by heating dicarboxylic acids like succinic or phthalic acid.
  • Lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4) reduces esters to two distinct primary alcohol fragments.
  • The carbonyl stretch in infrared spectra shifts to higher wavenumbers in the order: Amide < Ester < Anhydride < Acid Chloride.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming amides can be easily hydrolyzed in neutral water without acid or base catalysis.
  • Forgetting that Grignard reagents react with esters to form tertiary alcohols rather than ketones.
  • Confusing the products of ester reduction using DIBAL-H at low temperature (aldehydes) versus LiAlH4 (alcohols).
  • Overlooking the role of pyridine in acylation reactions using acid chlorides.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation and Properties of Carboxylic Acid Derivatives

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution Mechanism

The standard mechanism proceeds via addition of the nucleophile to form a tetrahedral intermediate followed by elimination of the leaving group.

Preparation of Acid Halides

Utilizes SOCl2 or PCl5 with carboxylic acids, yielding gaseous byproducts (SO2, HCl) that drive the reaction forward.

Interconversion of Derivatives

More reactive acid derivatives can be easily converted into less reactive derivatives using appropriate nucleophilic reagents.

Reduction Pathways

Strong reducing agents like LiAlH4 cleave esters and reduce acid derivatives, whereas mild reagents or catalytic hydrogenation provide specific alternate routes.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Fischer Esterification (RCOOH + R'OH → RCOOR')
Acid-catalyzed condensation of carboxylic acid and alcohol.
Saponification (Base Hydrolysis of Ester)
Alkaline hydrolysis of fats or esters yielding carboxylate salts.
Ammonolysis (RCOCl + NH3)
Reaction of acid derivatives with ammonia to form amides.
Transesterification (RCOOR' + R''OH)
Exchange of alkoxy group between an ester and an alcohol.
Hofmann Degradation (RCONH2 + Br2 + NaOH)
Conversion of primary amide to primary amine with loss of CO.
Claisen Condensation (Ester self-condensation)
Base-promoted condensation of two ester molecules into a β-keto ester.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Reactivity order: RCOCl > (RCO)2O > RCOOR' > RCONH2
  • Grignard reagent with ester yields tertiary alcohol
  • SOCl2 is preferred for acid chloride synthesis as byproducts are gases
  • Amides undergo reduction to form amines, unlike other derivatives
  • Pyridine is used as a base catalyst in acylation reactions

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carboxylic acid derivatives react via nucleophilic acyl substitution.
  • Leaving group ability dictates the overall reaction rate.
  • Interconversions allow synthesis of less reactive derivatives from more reactive ones.
  • Reduction pathways vary depending on the specific reducing agent and derivative.

⚖️ Key Differences

Fischer Esterification VS Saponification

Fischer esterification is reversible acid-catalyzed ester formation, while saponification is irreversible base-catalyzed ester cleavage.

Which reagent is best suited for converting a carboxylic acid to an acid chloride without forming solid inorganic byproducts?

Explanation: Reaction with SOCl2 produces gaseous SO2 and HCl, leaving a pure acid chloride product.

Question 4 of 20

In the reduction of an amide with LiAlH4, the final product obtained is a:

Explanation: Unlike other acid derivatives that yield alcohols upon reduction with LiAlH4, amides yield amines.

Question 5 of 20

What is the function of sulfuric acid in Fischer esterification?

Explanation: Concentrated H2SO4 protonates the carbonyl oxygen, increasing the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon.

Question 6 of 20

Which of the following derivatives undergoes ammonolysis most readily?

Explanation: Acid chlorides are the most reactive and readily undergo ammonolysis even under mild conditions.

Question 7 of 20

Saponification of ethyl benzoate with aqueous NaOH yields:

Explanation: Base hydrolysis of an ester yields a carboxylate salt (sodium benzoate) and an alcohol (ethanol).

Question 8 of 20

Which compound is formed when acetamide is heated with P2O5?

Explanation: P2O5 is a dehydrating agent that converts amides into nitriles.

Question 9 of 20

The order of leaving group ability in acyl substitution is:

Explanation: Leaving group ability is inversely related to basicity; chloride is the weakest base and best leaving group.

Question 10 of 20

What is the product of the reaction between acetyl chloride and dimethylamine?

Explanation: Reaction of an acid chloride with a secondary amine yields a tertiary amide (N,N-dimethylacetamide).

Question 11 of 20

Transesterification is best described as:

Explanation: Transesterification involves swapping the alcohol portion of an ester with a different alcohol.

Question 12 of 20

Which of the following reagents converts an ester directly to an aldehyde at low temperature?

Explanation: Diisobutylaluminum hydride (DIBAL-H) at -78 °C reduces esters selectively to aldehydes.

Question 13 of 20

Acetic anhydride is prepared by heating sodium acetate with:

Explanation: Reaction of a carboxylate salt with an acid chloride yields an acid anhydride.

Question 14 of 20

Why are amides less reactive than esters towards nucleophiles?

Explanation: Strong resonance donation from nitrogen's lone pair decreases the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon in amides.

Question 15 of 20

What is the role of pyridine in the reaction of an acid chloride with an alcohol?

Explanation: Pyridine acts as a weak base to scavenge HCl formed during esterification, preventing side reactions.

Question 16 of 20

Which acid derivative does not undergo Friedel-Crafts acylation readily?

Explanation: Amides coordinate with Lewis acid catalysts (like AlCl3), deactivating the catalyst and preventing the reaction.

Question 17 of 20

Hydrolysis of an acid anhydride yields:

Explanation: Anhydrides react with water to split symmetrically into two carboxylic acid molecules.

Question 18 of 20

Which of the following compounds will give a positive test with neutral FeCl3 solution?

Explanation: Carboxylic acid derivatives form hydroxamic acids with hydroxylamine, which give colored complexes with FeCl3.

Question 19 of 20

The carbonyl stretching frequency (cm-1) is highest for:

Explanation: Acid chlorides exhibit the highest C=O stretching frequency (~1800 cm-1) due to strong inductive electron withdrawal by chlorine.

Question 20 of 20

Methyl benzoate on reaction with HNO2/H2SO4 gives:

Explanation: The ester group is a deactivating, meta-directing group, yielding methyl m-nitrobenzoate upon nitration.

Question 1 of 10
Which acid derivative reacts with Grignard reagents to yield a ketone instead of a tertiary alcohol?

Solution: Organocadmium reagents react with acid chlorides to stop cleanly at ketones without further reacting to form tertiary alcohols.

Question 2 of 10
What is the product when propionyl chloride reacts with excess lithium aluminium deuteride (LiAlD4)?

Solution: Reduction with LiAlD4 introduces deuterium at the methylene position and deuterated hydroxyl group upon aqueous workup.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following compounds gives benzoic acid upon hydrolysis?

Solution: All carboxylic acid derivatives yield benzoic acid upon complete hydrolysis.

Question 4 of 10
In transesterification, the use of excess alcohol shifts the equilibrium in accordance with:

Solution: Adding excess reactant drives reversible equilibria forward based on Le Chatelier's principle.

Question 5 of 10
Which derivative exhibits the lowest carbonyl stretching frequency in IR spectroscopy?

Solution: Amides show the lowest C=O frequency (~1650 cm-1) due to strong nitrogen lone pair resonance weakening the C=O bond.

Question 6 of 10
What byproduct is formed when a carboxylic acid reacts with PCl5?

Solution: RCOOH + PCl5 → RCOCl + POCl3 + HCl.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following is true regarding base-catalyzed ester hydrolysis?

Solution: Saponification consumes one equivalent of base and is essentially irreversible due to resonance stabilization of the carboxylate anion.

Question 8 of 10
Treatment of methyl benzoate with cold aqueous NaOH yields:

Solution: Base hydrolysis cleaves methyl benzoate to sodium benzoate and methanol.

Question 9 of 10
Which reagent selectively reduces acid chlorides to aldehydes without reducing them further to alcohols?

Solution: Rosenmund reduction uses poisoned palladium catalyst (BaSO4) to stop the reduction of acid chlorides at the aldehyde stage.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following nitrogenous derivatives does NOT react with nitrous acid (HNO2) to give N2 gas?

Solution: Primary amides react with HNO2 to form carboxylic acids and nitrogen gas, whereas primary aromatic/aliphatic and secondary amines exhibit different specific pathways.
