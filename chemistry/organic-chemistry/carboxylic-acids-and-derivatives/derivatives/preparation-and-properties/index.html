Preparation and Properties of Carboxylic Acid Derivatives

Carboxylic acid derivatives including acyl halides, anhydrides, esters, and amides are synthesized primarily via nucleophilic acyl substitution. Their chemical reactivity is governed by the leaving group ability and electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthesis of Carboxylic Acid Derivatives

  • Acyl chlorides are prepared by reacting carboxylic acids with thionyl chloride (SOCl2), phosphorus pentachloride (PCl5), or phosphorus trichloride (PCl3).
  • Acid anhydrides are synthesized by the dehydration of two carboxylic acid molecules using strong dehydrating agents like P2O5 under heating.
  • Esters are produced via Fischer esterification where carboxylic acids react with alcohols in the presence of concentrated H2SO4.
  • Amides are formed by thermal dehydration of ammonium carboxylates or via the nucleophilic attack of ammonia on acyl chlorides and anhydrides.

Reactivity and Mechanism of Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution

  • The relative reactivity order of derivatives toward nucleophilic acyl substitution is RCOCl > (RCO)2O > RCOOR' > RCONH2.
  • The reaction mechanism proceeds via a tetrahedral intermediate followed by the elimination of the leaving group (alkoxide, halide, or carboxylate).
  • Basic hydrolysis of esters is irreversible (saponification) because the resulting carboxylate anion is resonance-stabilized and unreactive toward the alkoxide nucleophile.
  • Steric hindrance around the acyl carbon significantly decreases the reaction rate in both esterification and hydrolysis pathways.

Chemical Properties and Reduction Pathways

  • Reduction of esters with LiAlH4 yields primary alcohols, whereas reduction with DIBAL-H at low temperatures stops at the aldehyde stage.
  • Amides undergo Hofmann bromamide degradation using Br2/NaOH to yield primary amines with the loss of one carbon atom.
  • Esters react with Grignard reagents (R'MgX) to form tertiary alcohols after aqueous workup.
  • Acid chlorides undergo Rosenmund reduction with H2 over Pd-BaSO4 poisoned with quinoline to yield aldehydes exclusively.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Esters generally form alcohols upon reduction with metal hydrides.

Observation: Bouveault-Blanc reduction utilizes metallic sodium in absolute ethanol to reduce esters to primary alcohols via single-electron transfer steps. — Sodium acts as a single-electron reducing agent providing radical-anion intermediates rather than direct hydride transfer.

Exception Amides are the least reactive acid derivatives toward nucleophilic attack.

Observation: Imides exhibit significantly higher acidity and reactivity toward nucleophiles due to the electron-withdrawing effect of two adjacent carbonyl groups. — Strong resonance stabilization of the conjugate base anion distributes negative charge across both carbonyl oxygen atoms.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Leaving Group Basicity Mnemonic
Weaker Base = Better Leaving Group = Higher Derivative Reactivity

Application: Chloride (Cl-) is the weakest base among leaving groups, hence acyl chlorides are the most reactive derivatives.

RCONH2 + Br2 + 4NaOH → RNH2 + Na2CO3 + 2NaBr + 2H2O

Application: Directly drop the carbonyl carbon (C=O) to instantly write the primary amine product formula.

Grignard Addition to Esters
1 Mole Ester + 2 Moles R'MgX → Tertiary Alcohol

Application: Remember that esters consume two equivalents of Grignard reagent, unlike aldehydes and ketones which consume one.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Acyl chlorides react with secondary amines to form tertiary amides with the elimination of HCl.
  • Rosenmund reduction stops at the aldehyde stage because palladium catalyst is poisoned by BaSO4 and sulfur compounds.
  • Transesterification is an equilibrium process catalyzed by either strong acids or alkoxide bases.
  • Amides show amphoteric character, acting very weakly as acids and extremely weakly as bases.
  • Lithium aluminum hydride reduces amides directly to primary amines without isolating intermediate aldehydes.
  • Saponification of triglycerides yields glycerol and soaps (sodium or potassium salts of fatty acids).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the role of pyridine in acylation reactions; pyridine neutralizes HCl and prevents reverse reactions.
  • Assuming Grignard reagents yield aldehydes when reacted with esters; they always yield tertiary alcohols.
  • Forgetting that carbamates and ureas are nitrogen derivatives of carbonic acid, exhibiting distinct reactivity profiles.
  • Overlooking the necessity of dry conditions when preparing or reacting acyl chlorides.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation and Properties of Carboxylic Acid Derivatives

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution

Derivatives react via addition-elimination mechanisms where the tetrahedral intermediate collapses by ejecting the conjugate base of the acid.

Preparation Routes

Thionyl chloride and phosphorus halides convert acids to reactive acyl chlorides, serving as starting materials for other derivatives.

Reduction and Degradation

Strong reducing agents like LiAlH4 convert derivatives to alcohols or amines, while Hofmann degradation achieves chain shortening.

Basicity of Leaving Groups

Reactivity directly correlates with the pKa of the conjugate acid of the leaving group; lower pKa implies better leaving ability.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Acylation (RCO- introduction)
Introduction of an acyl group into a molecule containing an active hydrogen.
Saponification (Base ester hydrolysis)
Alkaline hydrolysis of esters yielding carboxylate salts and alcohols.
Transesterification (Ester interchange)
Exchange of the alkoxy group of an ester with an external alcohol.
Rosenmund Reduction (RCOCl → RCHO)
Catalytic hydrogenation of acyl chlorides to aldehydes.
Hofmann Degradation (RCONH2 → RNH2)
Conversion of primary amides to primary amines with loss of CO.
Tetrahedral Intermediate (sp3 intermediate)
Transient species formed during nucleophilic addition to carbonyl groups.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Reactivity order: RCOCl > (RCO)2O > RCOOR' > RCONH2
  • Rosenmund reagent: H2 / Pd-BaSO4
  • Hofmann degradation reagents: Br2 and aqueous NaOH
  • Fischer esterification uses concentrated H2SO4 catalyst
  • Grignard reagent with ester yields tertiary alcohol

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carboxylic acid derivatives undergo nucleophilic acyl substitution.
  • Leaving group ability dictates overall derivative reactivity.
  • Reduction pathways depend strongly on the choice of reducing agent.
  • Degradation reactions enable carbon skeleton manipulation.

⚖️ Key Differences

Acyl Chloride VS Alkyl Chloride

Acyl chloride contains a carbonyl group adjacent to chlorine, making it highly reactive toward nucleophiles, unlike alkyl chlorides.

Solution: Two equivalents of ammonia are required; one reacts to form the amide, and the second neutralizes the HCl byproduct.

Which compound is obtained when acetamide is heated with P2O5?

Solution: Heating primary amides with strong dehydrating agents like P2O5 results in dehydration to nitriles.

Question 4 of 10
In transesterification, the use of excess alcohol shifts the equilibrium to the:

Solution: According to Le Chatelier's principle, excess reactant pushes the equilibrium toward the formation of products.

Question 5 of 10
Which acid derivative exhibits the lowest boiling point due to lack of hydrogen bonding?

Solution: Acyl chlorides lack N-H or O-H bonds, preventing intermolecular hydrogen bonding and resulting in lower boiling points.

Question 6 of 10
What is the primary organic product of the reaction between benzoyl chloride and sodium benzoate?

Solution: An acyl chloride reacts with a carboxylate salt to yield an acid anhydride.

Question 7 of 10
Which chemical test distinguishes an ester from a carboxylic acid?

Solution: Carboxylic acids evolve CO2 gas with NaHCO3, whereas neutral esters show no effervescence.

Question 8 of 10
The reduction of amides with LiAlH4 yields:

Solution: LiAlH4 reduces amides completely to primary amines containing the same number of carbon atoms.

Question 9 of 10
Which group is the best leaving group in acyl substitution?

Solution: Chloride ion is the weakest base among the options, making it the superior leaving group.

Question 10 of 10
What is the role of pyridine in the Schotten-Baumann reaction?

Solution: Pyridine acts as a base to neutralize the HCl produced during acylation reactions, preventing side reactions.
