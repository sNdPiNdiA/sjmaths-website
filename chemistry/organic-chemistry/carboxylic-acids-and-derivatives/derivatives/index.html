Carboxylic Acid Derivatives

Carboxylic acid derivatives are compounds where the hydroxyl group of a carboxylic acid is replaced by heteroatom substituents such as -Cl, -OR, -NH2, or -OCOR. These compounds undergo nucleophilic acyl substitution reactions via tetrahedral intermediates.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Reactivity Order of Carboxylic Acid Derivatives

  • The general reactivity order toward nucleophilic acyl substitution is: Acid Halides > Acid Anhydrides > Esters > Amides.
  • Reactivity is governed by the leaving group ability, basicity of Z-, and positive charge density on the carbonyl carbon.
  • Weaker bases (like Cl-) are better leaving groups, rendering acyl chlorides the most reactive derivatives.
  • Amides possess strong resonance stabilization (-C=O ↔ -C-O-=NH2+), reducing carbonyl electrophilicity.

Preparation and Synthesis Interconversions

  • Carboxylic acids react with SOCl2, PCl3, or PCl5 to yield acid chlorides.
  • Esters are synthesized via Fischer esterification by refluxing carboxylic acids with alcohols in the presence of concentrated H2SO4.
  • Amides are formed by the thermal dehydration of ammonium carboxylate salts or by reacting acid chlorides with ammonia or amines.
  • Acid anhydrides are prepared by the dehydration of two carboxylic acid molecules using P2O5 or heat.

Characteristic Chemical Reactions

  • All derivatives undergo nucleophilic acyl substitution via a tetrahedral addition-elimination intermediate.
  • Grignard reagents (R'MgX) react with acid derivatives to form tertiary alcohols (via ketone intermediates for esters and acid chlorides).
  • Amides undergo the Hofmann bromamide degradation using Br2 and NaOH to yield primary amines with one less carbon atom.
  • Esters undergo Claisen condensation in the presence of a strong base like NaOEt to yield β-keto esters.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Esters generally hydrolyze slowly in neutral water.

Observation: Oxalate and malonate esters hydrolyze significantly faster under neutral conditions. — Neighboring group participation and internal hydrogen bonding stabilize the transition state.

Exception Acid chlorides react violently with water to form acids.

Observation: Sterically hindered acid chlorides like 2,4,6-trimethylbenzoyl chloride hydrolyze extremely slowly. — Severe steric hindrance prevents the approach of water molecules to the carbonyl carbon.

Exception Amides are poor leaving groups and resist direct displacement.

Observation: N-nitroamides and N-acylimidazoles undergo rapid substitution reactions. — The electron-withdrawing substituents stabilize the negative charge on the departing nitrogen atom.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Leaving Group Basicity Rule
Reactivity ∝ 1 / Basicity of Leaving Group

Application: Remember that weaker bases make better leaving groups. pKa of conjugate acid (H-Z): HCl (-7) > RCOOH (4.8) > ROH (16) > RNH2 (38), reversing to give reactivity order.

Grignard Product Counter
Acid Chloride / Ester + 2 R'MgX → 3° Alcohol

Application: Quickly identify that acid chlorides and esters add two alkyl groups from Grignard reagents, whereas acid anhydrides and amides require stoichiometric control.

Hofmann Degradation Carbon Loss
R-CONH2 + Br2 + 4NaOH → R-NH2 + Na2CO3 + 2NaBr + 2H2O

Application: Directly drop the carbonyl carbon (-CO-) to find the primary amine product formula in one step.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Acid halides are the most reactive derivatives due to the poor resonance donation of chlorine and its high electronegativity.
  • Fischer esterification is an equilibrium process; yields are maximized by using an excess of alcohol or removing water.
  • Lithium aluminium hydride (LiAlH4) reduces esters to primary alcohols, whereas DIBAL-H stops at the aldehyde stage at low temperatures.
  • Amides are neutral because the lone pair on nitrogen is delocalized into the carbonyl π-system.
  • Transesterification involves converting one ester into another using an alcohol catalyst under acidic or basic conditions.
  • Urea is a diamide of carbonic acid and decomposes upon heating to form biuret.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the oxidation state of acid derivatives with aldehydes and ketones.
  • Assuming amides are basic because of the nitrogen atom; resonance makes them extremely weak bases.
  • Forgetting that Grignard reagents react with esters to form tertiary alcohols, not ketones.
  • Overlooking that Hofmann bromamide degradation results in the loss of one carbon atom.

⚡ Quick Revision: Carboxylic Acid Derivatives

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Reactivity

Carboxylic acid derivatives contain an acyl group bonded to an electronegative substituent. Reactivity depends on carbonyl electrophilicity and leaving group ability.

Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution

All interconversions proceed via addition-elimination mechanisms forming a tetrahedral intermediate, contrasting with SN1/SN2 mechanisms of alkyl halides.

Reduction Pathways

Strong reducing agents like LiAlH4 convert acid derivatives to primary alcohols or amines, while milder reagents provide selectivity.

Special Reactions

Named reactions such as Hofmann degradation and Claisen condensation are vital for carbon-chain manipulation and functional group transformation.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Acyl Group (R-CO-)
Functional group derived by removal of OH from carboxylic acid.
Transesterification (RCOOR' + R''OH → RCOOR'' + R'OH)
Exchange of alkoxy group between an ester and an alcohol.
Saponification (Base hydrolysis of ester)
Alkaline hydrolysis yielding carboxylate salt and alcohol.
Tetrahedral Intermediate (sp3 carbon adduct)
Unstable intermediate formed during nucleophilic acyl substitution.
Hofmann Degradation (RCONH2 → RNH2)
Conversion of amide to primary amine with loss of CO.
Imide ((RCO)2NH)
Compound containing two acyl groups bonded to the same nitrogen.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Reactivity order: Acid Chloride > Anhydride > Ester > Amide
  • Grignard reagent with ester yields 3° alcohol
  • Hofmann bromamide degradation shortens the carbon chain by one carbon
  • LiAlH4 reduces all derivatives to primary alcohols (or amines for amides)
  • Amides are planar due to restricted rotation from resonance C-N partial double bond character

📌 Quick Recall

  • Acyl substitution proceeds via addition-elimination mechanism.
  • Leaving group basicity dictates relative reaction rates.
  • Esters undergo acid or base catalyzed hydrolysis.
  • Derivatives serve as key synthetic intermediates in organic synthesis.

⚖️ Key Differences

Acyl Chloride VS Alkyl Chloride

Acyl chlorides undergo substitution via addition-elimination, whereas alkyl chlorides undergo SN1/SN2 substitution.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following derivatives is most reactive toward nucleophilic acyl substitution?

Explanation: Chloride is the weakest base among the leaving groups, making acetyl chloride the most reactive derivative.

Question 2 of 19

What is the product when ethyl acetate reacts with excess methylmagnesium bromide followed by acid workup?

Explanation: Esters react with two equivalents of Grignard reagents to yield tertiary alcohols (tert-butanol derivative).

Question 3 of 19

In Hofmann bromamide degradation, benzamide is converted to:

Explanation: Hofmann degradation converts an amide to a primary amine with one fewer carbon atom, turning benzamide into aniline.

Question 4 of 19

Which reagent converts carboxylic acids directly to acid chlorides?

Explanation: Thionyl chloride (SOCl2) converts carboxylic acids to acid chlorides with gaseous byproducts (SO2 and HCl).

Question 5 of 19

The intermediate formed during nucleophilic acyl substitution is:

Explanation: Nucleophilic attack on the sp2 carbonyl carbon temporarily forms an sp3 tetrahedral intermediate.

Question 6 of 19

Fischer esterification is an example of:

Explanation: Alcohol acts as a nucleophile attacking the protonated carbonyl carbon in an acyl substitution process.

Question 7 of 19

Which acid derivative exhibits restricted rotation around the C-N bond due to resonance?

Explanation: Amides show partial double bond character between C and N due to resonance, restricting rotation.

Question 8 of 19

What is the product of the reduction of an ester with DIBAL-H at -78 °C followed by hydrolysis?

Explanation: DIBAL-H at low temperature reduces esters specifically to aldehydes.

Question 9 of 19

Which of the following compounds does not react with Grignard reagents to form a tertiary alcohol?

Explanation: Formate esters yield secondary alcohols because they possess only one alkyl group attached to the carbonyl carbon.

Question 10 of 19

Transesterification is catalyzed by:

Explanation: Transesterification can be catalyzed by both acid (protonating carbonyl) or base (deprotonating alcohol).

Question 11 of 19

The IUPAC name of acetic anhydride is:

Explanation: Symmetric acid anhydrides are named by replacing 'acid' with 'anhydride' after the corresponding alkanoic acid name.

Question 12 of 19

Which derivative is least susceptible to nucleophilic attack?

Explanation: Amides are the least reactive because the -NH2 group is a poor leaving group and a strong electron donor.

Question 13 of 19

When acetamide is heated with P2O5, the product formed is:

Explanation: Phosphorus pentoxide is a dehydrating agent that converts primary amides into nitriles.

Question 14 of 19

Which of the following has the highest boiling point?

Explanation: Carboxylic acids form stronger intermolecular hydrogen bonds (dimers) than primary amides, giving them higher boiling points.

Question 15 of 19

What is the role of pyridine in the Schotten-Baumann reaction (acylation of amines)?

Explanation: Pyridine acts as a base to scavenge the HCl byproduct and prevent protonation of the amine reactant.

Question 16 of 19

Hydrolysis of an ester in the presence of sodium hydroxide is known as:

Explanation: Base-catalyzed ester hydrolysis yields soap (carboxylate salts), hence termed saponification.

Question 17 of 19

Which derivative cannot be prepared directly from carboxylic acids by reaction with alcohols?

Explanation: Acid chlorides cannot be prepared directly from carboxylic acids using alcohols; chlorinating agents like SOCl2 are required.

Question 18 of 19

What is the byproduct of the reaction between an acid chloride and an alcohol?

Explanation: The reaction of an acid chloride with an alcohol yields an ester and hydrochloric acid (HCl).

Question 19 of 19

Which of the following compounds gives positive test with neutral FeCl3 solution?

Explanation: Certain esters and acetoacetic esters that exhibit keto-enol tautomerism give colored complexes with neutral FeCl3.

Question 1 of 10
What is the principal organic product formed when benzoyl chloride reacts with dimethylamine?

Solution: Acyl chlorides react with secondary amines to form tertiary amides such as N,N-dimethylbenzamide.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following compounds produces a primary alcohol upon reduction with LiAlH4?

Solution: Acid chlorides, esters, and acid anhydrides are all fully reduced by LiAlH4 to primary alcohols.

Question 3 of 10
Why are amides less reactive than acid chlorides toward nucleophiles?

Solution: Nitrogen's lone pair is delocalized into the carbonyl group, reducing positive charge on the carbonyl carbon.

Question 4 of 10
In the alkaline hydrolysis of an ester, the oxygen-18 label from labeled water (H2O18) ends up in:

Solution: Alkaline hydrolysis via acyl-oxygen cleavage incorporates the oxygen-18 label into the formed carboxylate salt.

Question 5 of 10
Which acid derivative reacts with Grignard reagents to yield a secondary alcohol?

Solution: Formate esters have a hydrogen instead of an alkyl group, yielding secondary alcohols after addition of two moles of Grignard reagent.

Question 6 of 10
What happens when urea is heated strongly?

Solution: Heating urea results in the condensation of two molecules to form biuret along with the evolution of ammonia gas.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following reagents converts an acid anhydride to a carboxylic acid and an ester?

Solution: Reaction of an acid anhydride with an alcohol yields one molecule of carboxylic acid and one molecule of ester.

Question 8 of 10
The conversion of RCOOH to RCOOR' using alcohol and acid is classified as:

Solution: Direct acid-catalyzed esterification of carboxylic acids with alcohols is known as Fischer esterification.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following represents the correct order of leaving group ability?

Solution: Weaker bases are better leaving groups. Chloride is the weakest base and best leaving group among the set.

Question 10 of 10
What is the main product of the reaction between methyl benzoate and excess ammonia?

Solution: Esters react with ammonia via aminolysis to yield amides and alcohols (Benzamide and Methanol).
