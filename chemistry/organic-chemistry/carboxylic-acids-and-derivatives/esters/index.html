Organic Chemistry

Esters

Esters are carboxylic acid derivatives characterized by the general formula RCOOR' where the hydroxyl group is replaced by an alkoxy group. They are widely synthesized via Fischer esterification and undergo nucleophilic acyl substitution reactions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthesis and Preparation Methods

  • Fischer esterification involves treating a carboxylic acid with an alcohol in the presence of concentrated H2SO4.
  • Reaction of acyl chlorides or acid anhydrides with alcohols yields esters rapidly and without reversible equilibrium limitations.
  • Transesterification converts one ester into another by reacting it with a different alcohol under acid or base catalysis.
  • Reaction of silver carboxylates with alkyl halides produces esters via nucleophilic substitution.

Chemical Reactivity and Mechanisms

  • Esters undergo nucleophilic acyl substitution via a tetrahedral addition intermediate due to electrophilic carbonyl carbon polarization.
  • Acid-catalyzed hydrolysis (AAC2 mechanism) proceeds through protonation, water attack, proton transfer, and elimination.
  • Base-promoted hydrolysis (BAC2 mechanism) is irreversible, yielding a carboxylate salt and an alcohol.
  • Reduction of esters with lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4) yields two distinct primary alcohols.

Physical Properties and Spectroscopic Identification

  • Lower esters exhibit characteristic fruity or sweet odors and possess lower boiling points than corresponding acids due to lack of intermolecular O−H hydrogen bonding.
  • In IR spectroscopy, esters display a strong, sharp carbonyl (C=O) stretching band between 1735–1750 cm−1.
  • 1H-NMR spectra show characteristic downfield shifting of the protons on the carbon directly attached to the ester oxygen (−CH2O− around δ 3.6–4.1 ppm).
  • Esters with α-hydrogens undergo Claisen condensation in the presence of strong alkoxide bases to form β-keto esters.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Esters undergo base-promoted hydrolysis via BAC2 mechanism (alkyl-oxygen bond cleavage is extremely rare).

Observation: Alkyl-oxygen cleavage (BAL1) occurs when the alcohol part is tert-butyl or benzyl. — Stable carbocation formation facilitates unimolecular alkyl-oxygen bond fission instead of acyl-oxygen cleavage.

Exception Esters are less reactive toward nucleophilic addition than aldehydes and ketones.

Observation: Methyl trifluoroacetate reacts extremely rapidly with nucleophiles. — Strong inductive electron withdrawal by the trifluoromethyl group drastically increases the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon.

Exception Reduction of esters with NaBH4 is ineffective under normal conditions.

Observation: Esters with heavily electron-withdrawing substituents or specific cyclic structures show slight reduction with NaBH4 in polar protic solvents. — Enhanced electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon lowers the activation energy for hydride transfer from NaBH4.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Fischer esterification is a reversible equilibrium reaction driven forward by removing water or using excess alcohol.
  • Saponification of esters is an irreversible base-catalyzed reaction producing carboxylate soaps and alcohols.
  • Esters react with Grignard reagents (R"MgX) to form tertiary alcohols containing two identical groups derived from the Grignard reagent.
  • Reduction of esters with LiAlH4 yields two moles of alcohol per mole of ester cleaved.
  • Ammonolysis of esters yields primary amides and alcohols (RCOOR' + NH3 → RCONH2 + R'OH).
  • Lactones are cyclic esters formed via intramolecular esterification of hydroxy carboxylic acids (typically γ- or δ-hydroxy acids).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming ester hydrolysis in base is reversible; saponification is irreversible due to resonance stabilization of the carboxylate anion.
  • Confusing the cleavage site in ester hydrolysis; standard mechanism involves acyl-oxygen cleavage, not alkyl-oxygen cleavage.
  • Using NaBH4 expecting complete reduction of simple aliphatic esters to alcohols under standard conditions.
  • Forgetting that transesterification requires catalytic amounts of acid or base to proceed at a practical rate.

⚡ Quick Revision: Esters

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Nomenclature

Esters are named as alkyl alkanoates, where the alkyl group comes from the alcohol component and the alkanoate comes from the carboxylic acid.

Synthesis Pathways

Principal synthetic methods include Fischer esterification, reaction of acyl chlorides/anhydrides with alcohols, and alcoholysis of nitriles.

Mechanisms of Hydrolysis

Hydrolysis proceeds via tetrahedral intermediates under both acid (AAC2) and base (BAC2) catalysis conditions.

Synthetic Utility

Esters serve as protecting groups for carboxylic acids and are key starting materials in Claisen condensations and Grignard syntheses.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Fischer Esterification (Acid-catalyzed ester formation)
Direct equilibrium reaction between carboxylic acid and alcohol.
Saponification (Base hydrolysis of esters)
Alkaline cleavage yielding carboxylate salt and alcohol.
Transesterification (Alcohol exchange)
Conversion of one ester into another via alcoholysis.
Lactone (Cyclic ester)
Intramolecular ester formed from hydroxy acids.
BAC2 Mechanism (Base-catalyzed acyl-oxygen cleavage)
Second-order bimolecular base hydrolysis.
Claisen Condensation (Ester self-condensation)
Base-catalyzed condensation forming β-keto esters.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • IR absorption for ester C=O stretch is 1735–1750 cm−1.
  • Esters react with 2 equivalents of Grignard reagent to form tertiary alcohols.
  • LiAlH4 reduces esters to two primary alcohols.
  • Saponification is base-promoted and irreversible.
  • Lower esters possess distinct fruity odors.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Esters contain the acyl-alkoxy moiety RCOOR'.
  • They undergo nucleophilic acyl substitution readily.
  • Hydrolysis can be acid or base catalyzed.
  • Widely found in natural fats, oils, and artificial flavorings.

⚖️ Key Differences

Acyl-oxygen cleavage VS Alkyl-oxygen cleavage

Acyl-oxygen cleavage breaks the C=O bond from oxygen (standard), whereas alkyl-oxygen cleavage breaks the oxygen-alkyl bond (tert-butyl/acidic conditions).

Question 1 of 19

What is the primary product of the reaction between an ethyl ester and excess methylmagnesium bromide followed by acidic workup?

Explanation: Esters react with two equivalents of Grignard reagents to yield tertiary alcohols after protonation of the alkoxide intermediate.

Question 2 of 19

Which reagent effectively reduces an ester to two primary alcohols?

Explanation: LiAlH4 is a powerful reducing agent capable of reducing unreactive esters to primary alcohols, whereas NaBH4 is generally ineffective.

Question 3 of 19

What is the typical infrared (IR) absorption range for the carbonyl stretching frequency in saturated aliphatic esters?

Explanation: Saturated aliphatic esters exhibit a strong C=O stretching band in the region of 1735–1750 cm−1 due to inductive withdrawal by the alkoxy oxygen.

Question 4 of 19

Which mechanism describes the base-promoted hydrolysis of methyl acetate?

Explanation: Base-promoted ester hydrolysis proceeds via the BAC2 mechanism (Base-catalyzed, Acyl-oxygen cleavage, Bimolecular).

Question 5 of 19

What type of cyclic compound is formed when γ-hydroxybutyric acid is heated?

Explanation: γ-Hydroxy acids undergo intramolecular esterification to form stable 5-membered cyclic esters known as γ-lactones.

Question 6 of 19

In Fischer esterification, what is the role of concentrated sulfuric acid?

Explanation: H2SO4 protonates the carbonyl oxygen to increase electrophilicity and acts as a dehydrating agent to shift equilibrium toward the ester.

Question 7 of 19

Which of the following derivatives is the most reactive toward nucleophilic acyl substitution?

Explanation: Acid chlorides possess the best leaving group (Cl−) and exhibit the highest electrophilicity among carboxylic acid derivatives.

Question 8 of 19

What is produced when ethyl acetate is treated with sodium ethoxide (NaOEt) followed by acid workup?

Explanation: Two molecules of ethyl acetate undergo Claisen condensation in the presence of sodium ethoxide to form ethyl acetoacetate (β-keto ester).

Question 9 of 19

Transesterification of methyl propanoate with excess ethanol in the presence of acid catalyst yields primarily:

Explanation: Transesterification exchanges the alkoxy group; using excess ethanol shifts equilibrium to form ethyl propanoate.

Question 10 of 19

Which ester undergoes alkyl-oxygen cleavage (BAL1) rather than acyl-oxygen cleavage during acid hydrolysis?

Explanation: tert-Butyl esters undergo alkyl-oxygen cleavage because the resulting tert-butyl carbocation is exceptionally stable.

Question 11 of 19

What is the byproduct formed during the saponification of fats (triglycerides) using sodium hydroxide?

Explanation: Saponification of triglycerides yields sodium salts of fatty acids (soaps) and glycerol (propane-1,2,3-triol).

Question 12 of 19

Why do esters have lower boiling points than isomeric carboxylic acids?

Explanation: Carboxylic acids form stable intermolecular hydrogen-bonded dimers, whereas esters cannot form hydrogen bonds with themselves, leading to lower boiling points.

Question 13 of 19

Reaction of an ester with ammonia (ammonolysis) produces:

Explanation: Ammonolysis of an ester replaces the alkoxy group with an amino group, producing a primary amide and an alcohol.

Question 14 of 19

In the 1H-NMR spectrum of ethyl acetate (CH3COOCH2CH3), the protons of the −CH2− group appear as a:

Explanation: The methylene protons (−CH2−) are split by the adjacent three methyl protons into a quartet according to the n+1 rule.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following reagents can selectively reduce an ester to an aldehyde at low temperatures?

Explanation: Diisobutylaluminum hydride (DIBAL-H) at −78°C cleanly reduces esters to aldehydes without over-reducing to alcohols.

Question 16 of 19

What is the IUPAC name for CH3CH2COOCH3?

Explanation: The acid portion has 3 carbons (propanoate) and the alkyl group attached to oxygen is a methyl group, giving methyl propanoate.

Question 17 of 19

Which factor decreases the rate of Fischer esterification?

Explanation: Steric hindrance caused by bulky substituents near the reaction center significantly slows down both esterification and hydrolysis.

Question 18 of 19

What is the product of the reaction between phenyl acetate and aluminum chloride?

Explanation: Aryl esters undergo Fries rearrangement in the presence of Lewis acids like AlCl3 to yield hydroxyaryl ketones.

Question 19 of 19

Which functional group isomer is constitutional to methyl propanoate?

Explanation: Methyl propanoate (C4H8O2) is a functional isomer of butanoic acid and other carboxylic acids with four carbons.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary organic product formed when methyl benzoate is treated with aqueous NaOH under reflux?

Solution: Base hydrolysis (saponification) of methyl benzoate yields sodium benzoate and methanol.

Question 2 of 10
Which compound does not undergo Claisen condensation?

Solution: Ethyl 2,2-dimethylpropanoate (ethyl pivalate) lacks α-hydrogens and therefore cannot undergo Claisen condensation.

Question 3 of 10
What is the major product of the reaction between an acid chloride and an alcohol?

Solution: Reaction of acyl chlorides with alcohols is a swift, non-equilibrium method producing esters and hydrogen chloride.

Question 4 of 10
During the acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of an ester, what is the nature of the intermediate formed after water attacks the protonated carbonyl?

Solution: Nucleophilic acyl substitution mechanisms proceed through a tetrahedral addition intermediate (species with four groups bonded to the acyl carbon).

Question 5 of 10
Which ester is most resistant to alkaline hydrolysis due to steric hindrance?

Solution: Bulky tert-butyl groups adjacent to the carbonyl carbon heavily hinder nucleophilic attack by hydroxide ions, slowing hydrolysis.

Question 6 of 10
What happens when a δ-hydroxy acid is treated with an acid catalyst?

Solution: δ-Hydroxy acids readily undergo intramolecular esterification to form stable 6-membered cyclic esters (δ-lactones).

Question 7 of 10
Which reagent is used to convert a carboxylic acid directly into an ester without isolating the intermediate acid chloride?

Solution: Fischer esterification directly converts carboxylic acids to esters using an alcohol and a mineral acid catalyst.

Question 8 of 10
In transesterification, the reaction is typically driven to completion by:

Solution: Transesterification is an equilibrium process; continuously distilling off the more volatile alcohol product shifts the equilibrium forward.

Question 9 of 10
What is the expected 1H-NMR multiplicity for the −CH3 protons of the acid portion in methyl propanoate (CH3CH2COOCH3)?

Solution: The terminal methyl protons (CH3CH2−) are coupled to the adjacent two methylene protons, appearing as a triplet.

Question 10 of 10
Which spectroscopic method is definitive for distinguishing an ester from an isomeric carboxylic acid?

Solution: Carboxylic acids exhibit a very broad O−H stretch from 2500–3500 cm−1, which is absent in esters.
