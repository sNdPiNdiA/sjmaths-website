Chemical Properties of Carboxylic Acids and Derivatives

Carboxylic acids and their derivatives undergo nucleophilic acyl substitution, decarboxylation, and reduction reactions governed by the polarity of the carbonyl carbon and the leaving group ability. Their acidity is driven by resonance stabilization of the resultant carboxylate anion.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Acidity and Electronic Effects

  • Carboxylic acids are stronger acids than alcohols and phenols due to extensive resonance stabilization of the carboxylate anion.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups (EWG) such as -NO2, -CN, and halogens increase acidity via inductive and field effects (-I effect).
  • Electron-donating groups (EDG) such as alkyl groups decrease acidity by intensifying negative charge on the carboxylate oxygen via +I effect.
  • Ortho-effect in benzoic acid derivatives generally causes enhanced acidity regardless of whether the substituent is electron-donating or withdrawing.

Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution and Derivatives

  • Carboxylic acid derivatives follow the reactivity order: Acyl halides > Acid anhydrides > Esters > Amides.
  • Reaction proceeds via a tetrahedral intermediate formed by nucleophilic addition to the carbonyl carbon followed by elimination of the leaving group.
  • Acid chlorides react with alcohols, amines, and water to yield esters, amides, and carboxylic acids, respectively, releasing HCl.
  • Amides undergo Hofmann bromamide degradation with Br2 and KOH to form primary amines with the loss of one carbon atom.

Decarboxylation and Reduction Pathways

  • Sodium salts of carboxylic acids undergo decarboxylation upon heating with soda-lime (NaOH + CaO) to yield alkanes.
  • Carboxylic acids are resistant to catalytic hydrogenation but are reduced to primary alcohols by lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4).
  • The Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction selectively halogenates the α-carbon of carboxylic acids in the presence of red phosphorus.
  • Reduction with diborane (B2H6) selectively reduces carboxylic acids to primary alcohols without affecting ester, nitro, or double bonds.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Formic acid (HCOOH) does not contain an alkyl group attached to the carbonyl carbon.

Observation: It exhibits reducing properties, reacting with Tollen's reagent and Fehling's solution. — Presence of an aldehyde-like structural unit (H-C=O) within the molecule.

Exception Ortho-substituted benzoic acids are generally stronger acids than benzoic acid.

Observation: Ortho-methoxybenzoic acid is less acidic than expected in certain non-polar media due to intramolecular hydrogen bonding. — Chelation stabilizes the neutral acid form relative to the dissociated anion.

Exception Aliphatic carboxylic acids undergo esterification easily with primary and secondary alcohols.

Observation: Tertiary alcohols undergo elimination rather than esterification under acidic conditions. — Formation of a stable tertiary carbocation leads exclusively to alkene formation via E1 pathway.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
EWG Acidity Rule
pKa decreases as -I/-M strength increases

Application: More halogens or closer proximity to the carboxylic carbon drastically lowers pKa and increases acid strength.

Derivative Reactivity Order
Cl > Anhydride > Ester > Amide

Application: Always predict that a less reactive derivative can be synthesized from a more reactive derivative, but not vice versa.

HVZ Carbon Alpha Rule
Only α-H containing acids react

Application: If a carboxylic acid lacks α-hydrogens (e.g., trimethylacetic acid or benzoic acid), it will fail the HVZ halogenation test.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Formic acid reduces Tollens' reagent to metallic silver, acting as both an acid and an aldehyde.
  • Decarboxylation of sodium acetate with soda-lime yields methane gas.
  • Esters undergo saponification when treated with aqueous NaOH, yielding carboxylate salts and alcohols.
  • Amides are dehydrated to nitriles when treated with phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5).
  • Benzoic acid is less acidic than oxalic acid due to the absence of adjacent electron-withdrawing carboxyl groups.
  • Acetic anhydride reacts with salicylic acid to produce aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the role of soda-lime in decarboxylation with conc. H2SO4 used in dehydration.
  • Assuming tertiary alcohols form esters smoothly with carboxylic acids under standard Fisher esterification conditions.
  • Forgetting that alpha-halogenation in the HVZ reaction requires red phosphorus, not just elemental halogen.
  • Assuming aliphatic carboxylic acids undergo oxidation with standard KMnO4; they are stable to mild oxidizing agents.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Properties of Carboxylic Acids and Derivatives

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Acidity of Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids ionize in water to form carboxylate anions stabilized by resonance delocalizing the negative charge equally over two electronegative oxygen atoms.

Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution

Nucleophiles attack the electrophilic carbonyl carbon, producing a tetrahedral intermediate followed by expulsion of the leaving group.

Reduction and Decarboxylation

Strong reducing agents like LiAlH4 convert acids to primary alcohols, while soda-lime removes the carboxyl group entirely as sodium carbonate.

Derivative Interconversion

Acid chlorides serve as versatile synthetic intermediates to prepare anhydrides, esters, and amides efficiently under mild conditions.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Carboxylate Anion (RCOO-)
Resonance-stabilized anion formed by deprotonation of a carboxylic acid.
Acyl Chloride (RCOCl)
Most reactive carboxylic acid derivative containing a carbon-chlorine bond.
Saponification (Base Hydrolysis of Ester)
Alkaline hydrolysis of esters yielding soap and alcohol.
HVZ Reaction (Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky)
Alpha-halogenation of carboxylic acids using red phosphorus and X2.
Hofmann Degradation (RCONH2 + Br2 + KOH)
Conversion of an amide to a primary amine with one fewer carbon atom.
Soda-Lime (NaOH + CaO)
Reagent used for thermal decarboxylation of carboxylic acid salts.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Order of acid strength: RCOOH > H2O > ROH > HC≡CH > NH3 > RH
  • Reactivity order of derivatives: RCOCl > (RCO)2O > RCOOR' > RCONH2
  • Formic acid contains both acid and aldehyde functionalities
  • Ortho-effect increases acidity of benzoic acid derivatives irrespective of +I or -I substituent
  • LiAlH4 reduces acids to primary alcohols whereas NaBH4 does not react with carboxylic acids

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carboxylic acids are weak acids exhibiting strong resonance stabilization in their conjugate bases.
  • Electrophilic attack at the carbonyl carbon dictates nucleophilic acyl substitution mechanisms.
  • Acid derivatives vary widely in reactivity depending on the basicity of the leaving group.
  • Specialized reactions like HVZ, Hunsdiecker, and Hofmann degradation provide targeted synthetic routes.

⚖️ Key Differences

Fischer Esterification VS Saponification

Fischer esterification is acid-catalyzed and reversible (Acid + Alcohol → Ester), whereas saponification is base-promoted and irreversible (Ester + Base → Salt + Alcohol).

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following compounds is the strongest acid?

Explanation: Trichloroacetic acid has three electron-withdrawing chlorine atoms exerting a strong -I effect, stabilizing the carboxylate anion and increasing acidity.

Question 2 of 18

The reagent used in the Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky reaction is:

Explanation: The HVZ reaction requires halogen (like Br2) in the presence of catalytic red phosphorus to halogenate the alpha-carbon.

Question 3 of 18

Which carboxylic acid derivative is the most reactive towards nucleophilic acyl substitution?

Explanation: Acyl chlorides contain the chloride ion, which is a very weak base and an excellent leaving group, making acyl chlorides the most reactive derivatives.

Question 4 of 18

Decarboxylation of sodium acetate with soda-lime yields:

Explanation: Heating sodium acetate with soda-lime (NaOH + CaO) results in the loss of CO2 as Na2CO3, yielding methane.

Question 5 of 18

Formic acid differs from acetic acid because formic acid:

Explanation: Formic acid contains a formyl group (H-C=O) which gives it reducing properties, allowing it to reduce Tollens' reagent and Fehling's solution.

Question 6 of 18

What product is formed when an amide is treated with Br2 and aqueous KOH?

Explanation: Hofmann bromamide degradation converts an amide into a primary amine containing one carbon atom less than the parent amide.

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following represents the correct order of acidity?

Explanation: Electron-withdrawing nitro group increases acidity, while electron-donating methoxy group decreases acidity relative to benzoic acid.

Question 8 of 18

Which reagent reduces a carboxylic acid directly to a primary alcohol?

Explanation: Lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4) is a powerful reducing agent capable of reducing carboxylic acids to primary alcohols.

Question 9 of 18

Salicylic acid reacts with acetic anhydride to produce:

Explanation: Acetylation of the phenolic -OH group of salicylic acid with acetic anhydride yields acetylsalicylic acid, commonly known as aspirin.

Question 10 of 18

In the esterification of carboxylic acids with alcohols, the oxygen atom of the formed water molecule typically originates from:

Explanation: Isotopic labeling studies prove that the -OH group is lost from the carboxylic acid and the -H is lost from the alcohol during Fischer esterification.

Question 11 of 18

Which compound does not undergo the Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky reaction?

Explanation: Trimethylacetic acid (2,2-dimethylpropanoic acid) lacks alpha-hydrogens and therefore cannot undergo alpha-halogenation via the HVZ reaction.

Question 12 of 18

The hydrolysis of an ester in the presence of an alkali is known as:

Explanation: Alkaline hydrolysis of esters produces carboxylate salts (soaps) and is termed saponification.

Question 13 of 18

Which of the following acids has the lowest pKa value?

Explanation: Fluorine is the most electronegative halogen, exerting the strongest -I effect, making fluoroacetic acid the strongest acid (lowest pKa).

Question 14 of 18

Reaction of acid chlorides with dialkylcadmium (R2Cd) yields:

Explanation: Reaction of acyl chlorides with dialkylcadmium reagents provides a selective method for synthesizing ketones without further reacting to tertiary alcohols.

Question 15 of 18

What is the primary product of the reaction between benzoic acid and phosphorus pentachloride (PCl5)?

Explanation: Carboxylic acids react with PCl5, PCl3, or SOCl2 to form acyl chlorides (benzoyl chloride in this case).

Question 16 of 18

Amides are generally less reactive than esters towards nucleophilic attack because:

Explanation: The lone pair on the nitrogen atom in amides delocalizes into the carbonyl group via resonance, reducing the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon.

Question 17 of 18

Which acid does not show optical isomerism?

Explanation: Succinic acid (butanedioic acid) lacks a chiral carbon center and therefore does not exhibit optical activity.

Question 18 of 18

Thermal decomposition of ammonium acetate yields:

Explanation: Heating ammonium acetate causes dehydration, yielding acetamide and water.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following acid derivatives cannot be prepared directly by treating the carboxylic acid with the corresponding nucleophile?

Solution: Acyl chlorides cannot be prepared directly by reacting carboxylic acids with Cl-; instead, reagents like SOCl2, PCl3, or PCl5 must be used.

Question 2 of 10
The correct order of leaving group ability in nucleophilic acyl substitution is:

Solution: Leaving group ability parallels the strength of the conjugate acid: HCl is a strong acid, making Cl- an excellent leaving group.

Question 3 of 10
In the HVZ reaction, red phosphorus reacts with halogen to form:

Solution: Red phosphorus reacts with halogen to generate phosphorus trihalide (PX3), which converts the carboxylic acid into an acyl halide reactive intermediate.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following pairs shows correct relationship of ortho-effect in benzoic acid?

Solution: Virtually all ortho-substituents (whether EDG or EWG) increase the acidity of benzoic acid due to steric inhibition of resonance, forcing the carboxyl group out of coplanarity.

Question 5 of 10
What happens when calcium acetate is dry distilled?

Solution: Dry distillation of calcium acetate yields acetone along with calcium carbonate.

Question 6 of 10
Which reagent selectively reduces esters to aldehydes without reducing them all the way to alcohols?

Solution: Diisobutylaluminum hydride (DIBAL-H) at low temperatures (-78 °C) selectively reduces esters to aldehydes.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following compounds gives a positive iodoform test?

Solution: Pyruvic acid contains a methyl ketone/alpha-keto acid unit (CH3-C=O) which reacts with iodine and base to give a positive iodoform test.

Question 8 of 10
When propanoic acid is treated with bromine in the presence of red phosphorus, the product is:

Solution: HVZ reaction specifically halogenates the alpha-carbon, yielding 2-bromopropanoic acid.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following possesses the highest boiling point?

Solution: Carboxylic acids form robust cyclic dimers via intermolecular hydrogen bonding, resulting in significantly higher boiling points than alkanes, alcohols, or esters of comparable molecular mass.

Question 10 of 10
The hydrolysis of acetonitrile in acidic medium yields:

Solution: Complete acid- or base-catalyzed hydrolysis of nitriles (cyanides) yields carboxylic acids via an intermediate amide.
