Acid Anhydrides

Acid anhydrides are functional derivatives of carboxylic acids formed by the condensation of two carboxyl groups with the elimination of water, possessing the general formula (RCO)2O. They act as potent acylating agents in organic synthesis due to the strong electron-withdrawing inductive effect of the two adjacent carbonyl groups.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation and Synthesis Methods

  • Acid anhydrides are prepared by the reaction of acyl chlorides with sodium carboxylate salts (RCOONa + R'COCl → (RCO)2O + NaCl).
  • Dicarboxylic acids with 5 or 6 carbon chains (such as succinic and glutaric acid) undergo thermal dehydration to yield cyclic anhydrides.
  • Phosphorus pentoxide (P4O10) is the standard laboratory dehydrating agent used to convert aliphatic carboxylic acids into open-chain anhydrides.

Chemical Reactivity and Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution

  • Acid anhydrides undergo nucleophilic acyl substitution faster than esters but slower than acyl chlorides.
  • Reaction with primary and secondary amines yields amides and one equivalent of a carboxylic acid byproduct.
  • Friedel-Crafts acylation using phthalic anhydride or acetic anhydride in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3 introduces an acyl group into aromatic rings.

Physical Properties and Spectral Characteristics

  • Lower aliphatic anhydrides are pungent, colorless liquids, whereas higher anhydrides exist as crystalline solids.
  • IR spectra of anhydrides exhibit two characteristic carbonyl stretching absorption bands near 1820 cm-1 and 1750 cm-1 due to symmetric and asymmetric coupling.
  • Boiling points of acid anhydrides are significantly higher than corresponding carboxylic acids of comparable molecular mass due to increased molecular surface area and dipole-dipole interactions.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Carboxylic acids readily yield simple open-chain anhydrides upon heating.

Observation: Formic acid (HCOOH) does not form a stable anhydride. — Formic anhydride decomposes spontaneously into carbon monoxide and water upon formation.

Exception All dicarboxylic acids readily form cyclic anhydrides upon heating.

Observation: Oxalic and malonic acids do not form cyclic anhydrides on simple heating. — Heating oxalic acid yields CO, CO2, and H2O, while malonic acid undergoes decarboxylation to acetic acid.

Exception Acid anhydrides react with water to yield carboxylic acids slowly.

Observation: Ketene (CH2=C=O), the internal anhydride of acetic acid, reacts violently with water. — Ketene possesses a highly reactive cumulated heterocumulene-like system making it extremely susceptible to nucleophilic attack.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Acetic anhydride is manufactured industrially by the catalytic cracking of acetic acid to ketene followed by reaction with additional acetic acid.
  • Phthalic anhydride is synthesized commercially by the catalytic vapor-phase oxidation of naphthalene or o-xylene over V2O5.
  • Acid anhydrides react with alcohols in the presence of pyridine to form esters and a carboxylic acid.
  • The reaction of acetic anhydride with salicylic acid yields aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) and acetic acid.
  • Anhydrides do not form hydrogen bonds with themselves as efficiently as carboxylic acids, yet they have higher boiling points than hydrocarbons of similar weight.
  • Ammonia reacts with acetic anhydride to form acetamide and ammonium acetate.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the single carbonyl stretch of esters with the characteristic twin carbonyl stretches of acid anhydrides in IR spectroscopy.
  • Assuming formic anhydride can be isolated at room temperature, forgetting its spontaneous decomposition to carbon monoxide and water.
  • Neglecting the formation of a stoichiometric equivalent of carboxylic acid as a byproduct during acylation reactions with anhydrides.
  • Applying thermal dehydration indiscriminately to oxalic and malonic acids to yield cyclic anhydrides.

⚡ Quick Revision: Acid Anhydrides

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Synthesis of Anhydrides

Acid anhydrides are synthesized via dehydration of carboxylic acids using P4O10 or via nucleophilic acyl substitution of acid chlorides with carboxylate salts.

Reactivity Profile

Due to electron withdrawal by two carbonyl groups, anhydrides readily undergo acyl transfer with water, alcohols, and amines to form acids, esters, and amides.

Cyclic Anhydrides

Dicarboxylic acids with appropriate chain lengths (succinic and glutaric acids) form stable 5- and 6-membered cyclic anhydrides upon thermal elimination of water.

Spectroscopic Identification

Anhydrides are identified reliably via their characteristic pair of strong carbonyl stretching absorption bands in the infrared region near 1820 and 1750 cm-1.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Acid Anhydride ((RCO)2O)
Compound containing two acyl groups bonded to a common oxygen atom.
Mixed Anhydride (RC(=O)OC(=O)R')
Anhydride derived from two different carboxylic acids.
Ketene (CH2=C=O)
Internal anhydride and reactive intermediate used to produce acetic anhydride.
Acetylation (CH3CO- transfer)
Introduction of an acetyl group into an organic molecule using acetic anhydride.
Phthalic Anhydride (C8H4O3)
Cyclic anhydride derived from phthalic acid, used in polymer synthesis.
Acyl Transfer (Nucleophilic Substitution)
Reaction mechanism where an acyl group is transferred to a nucleophile.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Anhydrides exhibit two C=O stretching bands in IR at ~1820 and ~1750 cm-1.
  • Reaction of acetic anhydride with salicylic acid yields aspirin.
  • P4O10 is the standard reagent for dehydrating monocarboxylic acids.
  • Formic anhydride does not exist stably at room temperature.
  • Succinic acid yields a 5-membered cyclic anhydride upon heating.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Anhydrides are reactive carboxylic acid derivatives serving as powerful acylating agents.
  • They react with water, alcohols, and amines to produce acids, esters, and amides respectively.
  • Cyclic anhydrides are readily formed when ring strain is minimal (5- and 6-membered rings).
  • They are distinguished spectroscopically by characteristic double carbonyl stretching frequencies.

⚖️ Key Differences

Acid Chloride Acyl Reactivity VS Acid Anhydride Acyl Reactivity

Acid chlorides are more reactive toward nucleophiles than acid anhydrides due to the superior leaving group ability of chloride over carboxylate.

Which reagent is commonly used to convert acetic acid into acetic anhydride?

Explanation: Phosphorus pentoxide (P4O10) is a powerful dehydrating agent that removes water from carboxylic acids to form acid anhydrides.

What is the characteristic IR absorption pattern for the carbonyl groups in an acyclic acid anhydride?

Explanation: Acid anhydrides exhibit characteristic symmetric and asymmetric carbonyl stretching vibrations resulting in a distinct doublet near 1820 and 1750 cm-1.

Question 3 of 19

Reaction of salicylic acid with acetic anhydride produces:

Explanation: Acetylation of the phenolic hydroxyl group of salicylic acid by acetic anhydride yields acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin) along with acetic acid.

Question 4 of 19

Why is formic anhydride (HCO)2O not isolated under normal conditions?

Explanation: Formic anhydride is thermodynamically unstable and decomposes spontaneously into carbon monoxide and water upon formation.

Question 5 of 19

Which of the following dicarboxylic acids readily forms a cyclic anhydride upon heating?

Explanation: Succinic and glutaric acids form stable 5- and 6-membered cyclic anhydrides upon thermal dehydration.

Question 6 of 19

In the nucleophilic acyl substitution of acid anhydrides, what is the leaving group?

Explanation: During nucleophilic attack on an anhydride, a carboxylate ion departs as the leaving group.

Question 7 of 19

Which derivative is most reactive toward nucleophilic acyl substitution?

Explanation: Acid chlorides possess the best leaving group (Cl-) and highest carbonyl electrophilicity, making them more reactive than anhydrides.

Question 8 of 19

What product is obtained when acetic anhydride reacts with excess ammonia?

Explanation: Ammonia reacts with acetic anhydride to yield acetamide and ammonium acetate.

Question 9 of 19

Phthalic anhydride is commercially prepared by the catalytic oxidation of:

Explanation: Industrial synthesis of phthalic anhydride proceeds via vapor-phase oxidation of naphthalene or o-xylene using vanadium pentoxide (V2O5).

Question 10 of 19

What is the general formula of acid anhydrides?

Explanation: Acid anhydrides have the general formula (RCO)2O, representing two acyl groups linked by an oxygen atom.

Question 11 of 19

Which catalyst is typically used to accelerate esterification using acid anhydrides and alcohols?

Explanation: Pyridine acts as a mild organic base and catalyst that activates the anhydride toward nucleophilic attack by the alcohol.

Question 12 of 19

The boiling points of acid anhydrides are higher than those of corresponding:

Explanation: Due to larger molecular size and strong dipole-dipole interactions, anhydrides boil significantly higher than alkanes of comparable mass.

Question 13 of 19

What happens when an acid anhydride is hydrolyzed with water?

Explanation: Hydrolysis of an acid anhydride cleaves the central C-O-C bond to yield two molecules of carboxylic acid.

Question 14 of 19

Mixed anhydrides are formed from:

Explanation: Mixed anhydrides contain two distinct acyl groups derived from two different carboxylic acids.

Question 15 of 19

Ketene reacts with acetic acid to produce:

Explanation: Addition of acetic acid across the carbon-carbon double bond of ketene yields acetic anhydride.

Question 16 of 19

Which cyclic anhydride has a 5-membered ring?

Explanation: Succinic anhydride contains a 5-membered ring (including the two carbonyl carbons, two methylene carbons, and the ring oxygen).

Question 17 of 19

When an acid anhydride reacts with a primary amine, the product is a/an:

Explanation: Reaction of a primary amine with an acid anhydride yields an N-substituted amide and a carboxylic acid.

Question 18 of 19

Which functional group is absent in acid anhydrides?

Explanation: Acid anhydrides do not contain free hydroxyl (-OH) groups, unlike parent carboxylic acids.

Question 19 of 19

Friedel-Crafts acylation using succinic anhydride and benzene yields:

Explanation: Friedel-Crafts reaction of benzene with succinic anhydride in the presence of AlCl3 yields beta-benzoylpropionic acid.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following describes the correct order of reactivity towards nucleophilic acyl substitution?

Solution: Reactivity decreases as leaving group basicity increases and electrophilicity drops: Acid Chlorides > Acid Anhydrides > Esters > Amides.

Question 2 of 10
What is the product of the reaction between acetic anhydride and excess methylamine?

Solution: Reaction of acetic anhydride with a primary amine (methylamine) yields N-methylacetamide and acetic acid.

Question 3 of 10
Which acid does NOT form a stable anhydride?

Solution: Formic anhydride is unstable and decomposes into carbon monoxide and water upon formation.

Question 4 of 10
What is formed when maleic acid is heated gently?

Solution: Maleic acid (cis-1,2-ethenedicarboxylic acid) readily loses water upon heating to form maleic anhydride due to favorable cis-geometry.

Question 5 of 10
In the synthesis of aspirin, acetic anhydride acts as a/an:

Solution: Acetic anhydride transfers an acetyl group to the hydroxyl group of salicylic acid, functioning as an acylating agent.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following statements concerning acid anhydrides is correct?

Solution: Acid anhydrides possess the characteristic bridging oxygen structure connecting two acyl groups (-CO-O-CO-).

Question 7 of 10
What is the main organic product when acetic anhydride reacts with benzene in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3?

Solution: Friedel-Crafts acylation of benzene with acetic anhydride yields acetophenone and acetic acid.

Question 8 of 10
Why do cyclic anhydrides like succinic anhydride form more readily than acyclic anhydrides?

Solution: Intramolecular dehydration to form 5-membered cyclic anhydrides is favored entropically and enthalpically due to low ring strain.

Question 9 of 10
Which compound is produced by the catalytic cracking of acetic acid at high temperature in industrial manufacturing of acetic anhydride?

Solution: Industrial preparation of acetic anhydride proceeds via thermal cracking of acetic acid to form ketene (CH2=C=O).

Question 10 of 10
What happens to the melting point as the aliphatic chain length increases in straight-chain symmetrical acid anhydrides?

Solution: Symmetrical straight-chain acid anhydrides exhibit melting point alternation (even-odd effect) similar to dicarboxylic acids and alkanes.
