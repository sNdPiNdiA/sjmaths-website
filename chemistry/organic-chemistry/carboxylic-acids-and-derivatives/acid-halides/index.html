Acid Halides

Acid halides are reactive carboxylic acid derivatives containing the -COX functional group where the hydroxyl group is replaced by a halogen atom. They serve as versatile intermediates in organic synthesis due to the strong electron-withdrawing nature of the halogen and carbonyl group.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation Methods of Acid Halides

  • Carboxylic acids react with thionyl chloride (SOCl2), phosphorus pentachloride (PCl5), or phosphorus trichloride (PCl3) to yield acyl chlorides.
  • The reaction using SOCl2 is preferred because the byproducts (SO2 and HCl) are gases that escape cleanly, leaving pure product.
  • Oxalyl chloride ((COCl)2) is also widely used for mild and high-yielding conversion of carboxylic acids to acid chlorides.

Chemical Reactivity and Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution

  • Acid halides are the most reactive carboxylic acid derivatives due to the strong -I and -R effects of the halogen atom enhancing the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon.
  • They readily undergo nucleophilic acyl addition-elimination reactions with water, alcohols, ammonia, and amines to form acids, esters, and amides respectively.
  • Steric hindrance in the R group decreases the rate of nucleophilic attack at the carbonyl carbon.

Reduction and Organometallic Reactions

  • Catalytic hydrogenation of acid chlorides over palladium on barium sulfate poisoned with quinoline yields aldehydes via the Rosenmund reduction.
  • Reaction of acid halides with dialkylcadmium or modified organocuprates yields ketones without over-reduction to tertiary alcohols.
  • Reaction with excess Grignard reagents (R'MgX) converts acid halides directly into tertiary alcohols via intermediate ketones.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Acid halides react readily with nucleophiles under mild conditions.

Observation: Fluorides (R-COF) are significantly less reactive than chlorides, bromides, and iodides. — Strong carbon-fluorine bond energy and strong pπ-pπ overlap between fluorine and carbonyl carbon reduce electrophilicity.

Exception Reduction of acid chlorides with LiAlH4 yields primary alcohols.

Observation: Use of bulky reducing agents like lithium tri-tert-butoxyaluminum hydride (LiAlH(O-t-Bu)3) stops the reaction at the aldehyde stage. — Steric hindrance and deactivated reagent reactivity prevent further reduction of the intermediate aldehyde.

Exception Friedel-Crafts acylation of benzene uses acyl halides with Lewis acid catalysts like AlCl3.

Observation: Aryl halides and strongly deactivated benzene rings do not undergo Friedel-Crafts acylation. — Insufficient electron density on the aromatic ring prevents electrophilic attack by the acylium ion complex.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Acid halides have the general IUPAC suffix 'oyl chloride/bromide'.
  • Thionyl chloride (SOCl2) is the most convenient reagent for preparing acyl chlorides because all byproducts are gaseous.
  • Rosenmund reduction converts acid chlorides to aldehydes using Pd/BaSO4.
  • Acid halides react with primary and secondary amines to form substituted amides via the Schotten-Baumann reaction conditions.
  • The carbonyl carbon of an acid halide is $sp^2$ hybridized with a trigonal planar geometry.
  • Acyl halides react with diazomethane to form diazoketones, which undergo the Wolff rearrangement.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the reactivity order of carboxylic acid derivatives; remember that acid halides are the most reactive, not amides.
  • Forgetting that reduction of acid chlorides with standard LiAlH4 gives alcohols, whereas Rosenmund reduction stops at aldehydes.
  • Assuming acid halides can be stored indefinitely in moist air; they rapidly hydrolyze, fuming in moist air due to HCl formation.
  • Using unpoisoned Pd catalyst in Rosenmund reduction, which leads to over-reduction to hydrocarbons or primary alcohols.

⚡ Quick Revision: Acid Halides

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Synthesis and Structure

Acid halides contain a carbonyl group bonded to a halogen atom, synthesized efficiently using thionyl chloride or phosphorus pentachloride.

Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution

High electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon driven by halogen -I and -R effects makes acid halides exceptionally reactive toward diverse nucleophiles.

Reduction Pathways

Catalytic hydrogenation with poisoned palladium catalysts selectively reduces acid halides to aldehydes, while strong hydrides yield alcohols.

Carbon-Carbon Bond Formation

Reactions with organometallic compounds like dialkylcadmium or Grignard reagents yield ketones or tertiary alcohols respectively.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Reactivity order: Acid halides > Anhydrides > Esters > Amides
  • Rosenmund catalyst: Pd/BaSO4 poisoned with quinoline/S
  • SOCl2 leaves clean gaseous byproducts (SO2, HCl)
  • Grignard reagent with acid chloride yields 3° alcohol
  • Acyl halides fume in moist air due to HCl evolution

📌 Quick Recall

  • Acid halides are highly reactive acid derivatives.
  • Halogen electronegativity increases carbonyl carbon electrophilicity.
  • They undergo nucleophilic acyl substitution readily.
  • Selective reductions provide access to aldehydes and alcohols.

⚖️ Key Differences

Rosenmund Reduction VS Stephen Reaction

Rosenmund uses acid chloride with H2/Pd-BaSO4; Stephen uses alkyl cyanide with SnCl2/HCl.

Question 1 of 18

Which reagent is best suited for converting a carboxylic acid to an acid chloride with gaseous byproducts?

Explanation: Thionyl chloride (SOCl2) produces SO2 and HCl gases, leaving pure acid chloride.

Question 2 of 18

What is the product of the reaction between acetyl chloride and excess methylmagnesium bromide followed by hydrolysis?

Explanation: Acetyl chloride reacts with 2 equivalents of Grignard reagent to yield a tertiary alcohol (2-methylpropan-2-ol).

Question 3 of 18

In the Rosenmund reduction, what is the role of barium sulfate (BaSO4)?

Explanation: BaSO4 acts as a surface area moderator and poison to prevent over-reduction of the aldehyde to an alcohol.

Question 4 of 18

Which of the following carboxylic acid derivatives is the most reactive toward nucleophilic acyl substitution?

Explanation: Acid chlorides possess the best leaving group (Cl-) and highest carbonyl electrophilicity.

Question 5 of 18

What is formed when benzoyl chloride reacts with benzene in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3?

Explanation: Friedel-Crafts acylation of benzene with benzoyl chloride yields benzophenone.

Question 6 of 18

Why do acid halides fume in moist air?

Explanation: Moisture hydrolyzes acid halides instantly, releasing hydrogen chloride gas which forms fumes.

Question 7 of 18

Which reducing agent can selectively convert an acid chloride to an aldehyde without reducing it further?

Explanation: Lithium tri-tert-butoxyaluminum hydride is a sterically hindered mild reducing agent that stops at the aldehyde stage.

Question 8 of 18

What is the hybridization of the carbonyl carbon in an acyl chloride?

Explanation: The carbonyl carbon forms three sigma bonds and one pi bond, resulting in sp2 hybridization and trigonal planar geometry.

Question 9 of 18

Reaction of acetyl chloride with sodium acetate yields:

Explanation: Nucleophilic attack of acetate ion on acetyl chloride forms acetic anhydride.

Question 10 of 18

What is the IUPAC name of CH3CH2COCl?

Explanation: A three-carbon acyl chloride is named propanoyl chloride.

Question 11 of 18

Which compound is formed when an acid chloride reacts with a secondary amine?

Explanation: Reaction of an acid chloride with a secondary amine yields a tertiary amide (disubstituted amide).

Question 12 of 18

The high reactivity of acid halides is attributed to:

Explanation: Halogens withdraw electron density via induction, intensifying the positive charge on the carbonyl carbon.

Question 13 of 18

Which metal catalyst is used in Rosenmund reduction?

Explanation: Palladium supported on barium sulfate (Pd/BaSO4) is the standard Rosenmund catalyst.

Question 14 of 18

Acyl chlorides react with diazomethane to form:

Explanation: Acyl chlorides react with diazomethane to give diazoketones, precursors for Arndt-Eistert synthesis.

Question 15 of 18

Which reagent converts acid chlorides into alkanes directly?

Explanation: Total reduction of acid chloride to alkane requires reduction to alcohol, conversion to halide/tosylate, and hydrogenolysis.

Question 16 of 18

What is the byproduct when carboxylic acid reacts with PCl5 to form acid chloride?

Explanation: R-COOH + PCl5 → R-COCl + POCl3 + HCl.

Question 17 of 18

What happens when acetyl chloride is treated with water?

Explanation: Acid halides undergo rapid exothermic hydrolysis with water to form the corresponding carboxylic acid and hydrogen chloride.

Question 18 of 18

Which derivative among the following has the lowest boiling point compared to others of comparable molecular weight?

Explanation: Acid halides lack intermolecular hydrogen bonding (unlike acids, amides, and alcohols), resulting in lower boiling points.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary product when propanoyl chloride reacts with lithium di-tert-butoxychromate or poisoned Pd?

Solution: Controlled reduction of propanoyl chloride yields propanal (propionaldehyde).

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following acid derivatives does not undergo Friedel-Crafts acylation?

Solution: Amides do not act as effective acylating agents in Friedel-Crafts reactions due to strong nitrogen lone pair delocalization.

Question 3 of 10
In the conversion of RCOOH to RCOCl using PCl3, how many moles of carboxylic acid react per mole of PCl3?

Solution: One mole of PCl3 reacts with three moles of RCOOH to yield three moles of RCOCl and one mole of H3PO3.

Question 4 of 10
What is formed when acetyl chloride reacts with dimethylcadmium?

Solution: Dialkylcadmium reagents react cleanly with acid chlorides to form ketones without reacting further.

Question 5 of 10
The dipole moment of acid chlorides is generally high because of:

Solution: Polarization of the C=O bond coupled with the electronegative halogen creates a significant dipole moment.

Question 6 of 10
Which catalyst poisoning agent is classically used in the Rosenmund reduction?

Solution: Quinoline or sulfur is used to poison the palladium catalyst and stop reduction at the aldehyde stage.

Question 7 of 10
Reaction of an acid chloride with alcohol in the presence of pyridine yields:

Solution: Alcoholysis of acid halides yields esters, with pyridine serving as a base to scavenge HCl.

Question 8 of 10
Why can't hydrogen iodide be used to prepare acid iodides from carboxylic acids directly?

Solution: Hydrogen iodide is a strong reducing agent and thermally unstable, making acyl iodides difficult to prepare by direct halogenation.

Question 9 of 10
What is the geometry around the carbonyl carbon in acid halides?

Solution: The sp2 hybridized carbonyl carbon exhibits a trigonal planar geometry.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following compounds reacts most vigorously with water?

Solution: Acetyl chloride is the most reactive derivative, undergoing violent and exothermic hydrolysis with water.
