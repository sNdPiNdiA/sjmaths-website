Properties of Acid Derivatives

Carboxylic acid derivatives include acyl chlorides, acid anhydrides, esters, and amides, characterized by a common acyl group attached to a leaving group. Their chemical behavior is dominated by nucleophilic acyl substitution reactions, where the reactivity decreases with the basicity of the leaving group.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution Mechanism

  • Carboxylic acid derivatives undergo addition-elimination via a tetrahedral intermediate, unlike aldehydes and ketones which undergo simple nucleophilic addition.
  • The reaction rate is primarily controlled by the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon and the leaving group ability.
  • Stronger electron-withdrawing groups on the acyl moiety enhance carbonyl electrophilicity, accelerating the substitution rate.
  • Leaving group ability follows the conjugate acid strength: Cl- > RCOO- > RO- > NH2-.

Interconversion and Chemical Properties

  • Acid chlorides are the most reactive derivatives and can be easily converted to anhydrides, esters, and amides using appropriate nucleophiles.
  • Esters undergo acid-catalyzed esterification which is fully reversible, as well as base-catalyzed irreversible saponification.
  • Amides are the least reactive acid derivatives due to strong resonance stabilization involving lone pair delocalization from nitrogen into the carbonyl π-system.
  • Reduction of acid derivatives with LiAlH4 yields primary alcohols from esters and chlorides, and primary amines from amides.

Spectroscopic and Physical Characteristics

  • Infrared (IR) spectra of acid derivatives show strong carbonyl stretching bands (νC=O) varying with inductive and resonance effects: acyl chlorides (~1800 cm-1), anhydrides (~1820 and 1750 cm-1), esters (~1735 cm-1), and amides (~1680 cm-1).
  • Primary and secondary amides exhibit characteristic N-H stretching absorption bands in the 3300-3500 cm-1 region.
  • Boiling points of acid derivatives depend heavily on polarity and hydrogen bonding, with primary amides showing exceptionally high boiling points due to intermolecular N-H···O bonding.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Acid derivatives generally react rapidly with water to form carboxylic acids.

Observation: Certain hindered amides and specialized esters are remarkably stable to aqueous hydrolysis. — Steric hindrance shields the carbonyl carbon from nucleophilic attack by water molecules.

Exception Acyl chlorides react with all amines to yield amides.

Observation: Tertiary amines do not form stable amides upon reaction with acyl chlorides. — Tertiary amines lack an acidic N-H proton required for elimination after the initial nucleophilic attack, leading instead to quaternary ammonium salts.

Exception Reduction of esters with NaBH4 is ineffective.

Observation: Esters with strongly electron-withdrawing substituents on the alcohol or acid portion can occasionally undergo slow reduction with NaBH4 in polar solvents like methanol. — Enhanced electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon lowers the activation energy for hydride transfer from the weaker reductant NaBH4.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Leaving Group Basicity Rule
Reactivity ∝ 1 / (Basicity of Leaving Group)

Application: Quickly rank derivative reactivity by looking at the pKa of the conjugate acid of the leaving group (HCl: -7, RCOOH: 4.8, ROH: 16, RNH2: 38).

IR Carbonyl Frequency Trend
νC=O: Cl > Anhydride > Ester > Amide

Application: Remember 'CAN-EA' (Chloride, Anhydride, Ester, Amide) in decreasing order of carbonyl stretching frequency.

Hofmann Degradation Product Shortcut
RCONH2 + Br2 + 4NaOH → RNH2 + Na2CO3 + 2NaBr + 2H2O

Application: The amine formed has one carbon atom less than the starting unsubstituted amide.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Acid chlorides are synthesized from carboxylic acids using SOCl2, PCl3, or PCl5.
  • Esters undergo transesterification when treated with an alcohol in the presence of acid or base catalysts.
  • Amides undergo dehydration with P4O10 to form alkyl cyanides (nitriles).
  • Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction halogenates the α-position of carboxylic acids, not their derivatives directly.
  • Grignard reagents react with acyl chlorides and esters to yield tertiary alcohols after protonation.
  • Rosenmund reduction selectively converts acyl chlorides into aldehydes using poisoned palladium catalyst (Pd/BaSO4).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the mechanism of aldehydes/ketones (nucleophilic addition) with acid derivatives (nucleophilic acyl substitution).
  • Assuming NaBH4 can reduce esters under standard conditions; LiAlH4 is required.
  • Forgetting that primary and secondary amides form hydrogen bonds, causing abnormally high boiling points compared to esters of similar molecular weight.
  • Neglecting the formation of HCl during acyl chloride reactions with alcohols or amines, which requires a base scavenger like pyridine.

⚡ Quick Revision: Properties of Acid Derivatives

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Acyl Derivative Structure and Reactivity

Carboxylic acid derivatives share an acyl group bonded to an electronegative heteroatom leaving group. Their reactivity is governed by inductive electron withdrawal and resonance donation.

Nucleophilic Substitution Pathway

Reactions proceed via a tetrahedral intermediate followed by elimination of the leaving group. Leaving group ability dictates the overall reaction kinetics.

Interconversions and Reductions

More reactive derivatives can be converted into less reactive ones (e.g., acyl chloride to ester or amide). Strong reducing agents like LiAlH4 reduce derivatives to alcohols or amines.

Spectroscopic Identification

IR spectroscopy distinguishes derivatives by their characteristic carbonyl stretching frequencies, which correlate inversely with the resonance stabilization of the acyl group.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Acyl Chloride (RCOCl)
Derivative containing a carbonyl carbon bonded to a chlorine atom; highly reactive.
Ester (RCOOR')
Derivative containing an alkoxy group bonded to the acyl carbon; pleasant fruit-like odors.
Amide (RCONH2)
Derivative containing an amino group bonded to the acyl carbon; highly stable due to resonance.
Saponification (Base Hydrolysis)
Base-promoted hydrolysis of an ester yielding carboxylate salt and alcohol.
Transesterification (RCOOR' + R''OH → RCOOR'' + R'OH)
Exchange of the alkoxy group of an ester with another alcohol.
Rosenmund Reduction (RCOCl + H2 → RCHO)
Selective catalytic hydrogenation of acid chlorides to aldehydes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Reactivity order: Acyl Chloride > Anhydride > Ester > Amide.
  • Leaving group ability follows basicity: weaker base is a better leaving group.
  • Primary amides have high melting and boiling points due to intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
  • Esters yield primary alcohols upon reduction with LiAlH4.
  • Acyl chlorides react with Grignard reagents to form tertiary alcohols.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nucleophilic acyl substitution is the defining reaction of acid derivatives.
  • Resonance stabilization in amides reduces carbonyl electrophilicity significantly.
  • Acid derivatives can be interconverted from more reactive to less reactive forms.
  • Spectroscopy provides clear structural identification via unique C=O stretching frequencies.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nucleophilic Addition VS Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution

Aldehydes/ketones undergo addition without leaving group loss, whereas acid derivatives expel a leaving group after tetrahedral intermediate formation.

Question 1 of 19

What is the correct order of reactivity of carboxylic acid derivatives towards nucleophilic substitution?

Explanation: Reactivity decreases as the basicity of the leaving group increases: Cl⁻ < RCOO⁻ < RO⁻ < NH₂⁻.

Question 2 of 19

Which of the following acid derivatives has the highest carbonyl stretching frequency in the IR spectrum?

Explanation: Acyl chlorides (RCOCl) exhibit the highest carbonyl stretching frequency (~1800 cm⁻¹) due to strong inductive electron withdrawal by chlorine.

Question 3 of 19

Which reagent is used in the Rosenmund reduction to convert an acyl chloride to an aldehyde?

Explanation: H₂ over poisoned palladium on barium sulfate (Pd/BaSO₄) selectively reduces acid chlorides to aldehydes without reducing them further to alcohols.

Question 4 of 19

What are the products formed during the saponification of an ethyl acetate with sodium hydroxide?

Explanation: Base hydrolysis (saponification) of an ester yields a carboxylate salt (sodium acetate) and an alcohol (ethanol).

Question 5 of 19

Why are amides the least reactive among carboxylic acid derivatives toward nucleophilic attack?

Explanation: The nitrogen lone pair is delocalized into the carbonyl π-system (R-C(O⁻)=N⁺H₂), reducing the positive character and electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon.

Question 6 of 19

What product is obtained when an ester reacts with excess Grignard reagent followed by acid hydrolysis?

Explanation: Esters react with two equivalents of Grignard reagents to yield tertiary alcohols after workup.

Question 7 of 19

Which of the following reducing agents can selectively reduce an ester to a primary alcohol?

Explanation: LiAlH₄ is a powerful reducing agent capable of reducing esters to primary alcohols, whereas NaBH₄ is generally too weak for esters.

Question 8 of 19

In the reaction of an acyl chloride with an amine, why is an excess of amine or a base like pyridine added?

Explanation: The reaction produces HCl as a byproduct, which can protonate the amine reactant and halt the reaction; a base scavenges this HCl.

Question 9 of 19

What is formed when an amide is heated with phosphorus pentoxide (P4O10)?

Explanation: P₄O₁₀ is a dehydrating agent that converts primary amides into nitriles by removing a molecule of water.

Question 10 of 19

Which leaving group is the best in nucleophilic acyl substitution reactions?

Explanation: Chloride ion (Cl⁻) is the conjugate base of a strong acid (HCl), making it a weak base and an excellent leaving group.

Question 11 of 19

Transesterification involves the reaction of an ester with:

Explanation: Transesterification is the exchange of the alkoxy group of an ester with the alkoxy group of an incoming alcohol.

Question 12 of 19

What is the characteristic infrared absorption range for the carbonyl stretch of an aliphatic ester?

Explanation: Esters typically show strong carbonyl absorption bands around 1735 cm⁻¹.

Question 13 of 19

Reaction of a carboxylic acid with thionyl chloride (SOCl2) yields an acyl chloride along with which gaseous byproducts?

Explanation: RCOOH + SOCl₂ → RCOCl + SO₂ (gas) + HCl (gas). The gaseous byproducts escape, leaving pure acyl chloride.

Question 14 of 19

Which of the following compounds has the lowest boiling point?

Explanation: Esters lack intermolecular hydrogen bonding involving O-H or N-H bonds (unlike alcohols, acids, and primary amides), resulting in lower boiling points.

Question 15 of 19

What happens when acetamide is treated with bromine and aqueous sodium hydroxide?

Explanation: Hofmann bromamide degradation converts primary amides into primary amines with one less carbon atom.

Question 16 of 19

Which derivative can be prepared directly by reacting a carboxylic acid with an alcohol in the presence of concentrated H2SO4?

Explanation: Fischer esterification reacts a carboxylic acid with an alcohol under acidic catalysis to form an ester.

Question 17 of 19

Why do tertiary amides fail to form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with one another?

Explanation: Tertiary amides have two alkyl groups attached to nitrogen and no N-H bonds, preventing N-H···O hydrogen bonding.

Question 18 of 19

What is the intermediate formed during the nucleophilic substitution of an acid derivative?

Explanation: Nucleophilic attack on the sp² carbonyl carbon forms an sp³ tetrahedral intermediate before the leaving group is expelled.

Question 19 of 19

Which acid derivative reacts most vigorously with water?

Explanation: Acyl chlorides react violently and exothermically with water to form carboxylic acids and HCl due to extreme electrophilicity and a great leaving group.

Question 1 of 10
Which reagent converts a carboxylic acid directly into an acid anhydride?

Solution: Heating carboxylic acids with a strong dehydrating agent like P₄O₁₀ removes water to form acid anhydrides.

Question 2 of 10
What is the primary organic product formed when ethyl benzoate reacts with aqueous NaOH upon heating?

Solution: Base hydrolysis of ethyl benzoate yields sodium benzoate and ethanol.

Question 3 of 10
Which derivative is resistant to hydrolysis under mild acidic conditions but reacts readily under vigorous basic conditions?

Solution: Esters require catalysts (acid or base) and heating for hydrolysis compared to highly reactive acyl chlorides.

Question 4 of 10
During transesterification, the reaction is driven to completion by:

Solution: Transesterification is an equilibrium process; Le Chatelier's principle is applied by using excess alcohol or distilling off the product alcohol.

Question 5 of 10
What is the product of the reaction between acetyl chloride and excess methylmagnesium bromide after acidic workup?

Solution: Acyl chlorides react with two equivalents of Grignard reagents to form tertiary alcohols; methylmagnesium bromide yields 2-methylpropan-2-ol (tert-butyl alcohol).

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following compounds will give a positive test with neutral FeCl3 solution?

Solution: Compounds with enolizable hydrogen atoms like ethyl acetoacetate form colored coordination complexes with neutral FeCl₃.

Question 7 of 10
Why is it impossible to prepare an acid anhydride by reacting an acyl chloride with a tertiary amine?

Solution: Acyl chlorides react with carboxylate salts to form anhydrides; tertiary amines react to give quaternary ammonium salts.

Question 8 of 10
In the mechanism of nucleophilic acyl substitution, the tetrahedral intermediate collapses to expel the leaving group because:

Solution: The reformation of the strong C=O double bond is energetically favorable and drives the elimination of the leaving group.

Question 9 of 10
Which compound is formed when propanoic acid is heated with ammonia and the resulting product is strongly heated?

Solution: Heating propanoic acid with ammonia forms ammonium propanoate, which upon thermal dehydration yields propanamide.

Question 10 of 10
What is the IUPAC name of the compound formed when benzoyl chloride reacts with methylamine?

Solution: Reaction of benzoyl chloride with primary amine methylamine yields N-methylbenzamide.
