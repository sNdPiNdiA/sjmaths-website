Acidic Nature of Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids exhibit acidity due to the resonance stabilization of their conjugate base, the carboxylate anion. The presence of electron-withdrawing substituents enhances this acidity, whereas electron-donating groups decrease it.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Resonance Stabilization of Carboxylate Anion

  • Carboxylic acids ionize to release a proton, forming a carboxylate anion (RCOO-).
  • The negative charge in the carboxylate ion is delocalized equally over two electronegative oxygen atoms through resonance.
  • Neutral carboxylic acid possesses non-equivalent resonance structures, making the conjugate base significantly more stable than the unionized acid.

Electronic Effects of Substituents

  • Electron-withdrawing groups (EWGs) such as -NO2, -CN, and halogens increase acidity via -I and -M effects by dispersing negative charge.
  • Electron-donating groups (EDGs) such as -CH3 and -OCH3 decrease acidity via +I effects by intensifying negative charge on the carboxylate oxygen.
  • The acidic strength follows the inductive order: F > Cl > Br > I for halogen-substituted acetic acids.

Comparative Acidity with Alcohols and Phenols

  • Carboxylic acids are stronger acids than alcohols because alkoxide anions lack resonance stabilization.
  • Carboxylic acids are generally stronger acids than phenols because the negative charge is distributed over two oxygen atoms in carboxylate, compared to carbon atoms in the phenoxide ring.
  • The pKa value of typical aliphatic carboxylic acids ranges from 4.0 to 5.0, whereas phenols range around 10.0.

Negative Logarithm of Ka
pKa = -log10(Ka)
Conditions: Standard state conditions (298 K, 1 bar)
Units: Dimensionless
Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation
pH = pKa + log([RCOO-] / [RCOOH])
Conditions: Buffer solutions containing carboxylic acid and its salt
Units: Dimensionless
Hammett Equation for Substituted Benzoic Acids
log(K / K0) = ρσ
Conditions: Meta and para-substituted benzoic acids in water at 298 K
Units: Dimensionless
Acid Dissociation Constant
Ka = [RCOO-][H3O+] / [RCOOH]
Conditions: Dilute aqueous solution at constant temperature
Units: Dimensionless for thermodynamic Ka, mol dm-3 for concentration ratio
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Ortho-substituted benzoic acids are expected to be less acidic due to electron-donating steric effects.

Observation: Ortho-substituted benzoic acids (regardless of +I or -I groups) are almost always stronger acids than benzoic acid itself. — This is known as the ortho-effect, attributed to steric inhibition of resonance, forcing the carboxyl group out of the plane of the benzene ring and relieving electrostatic repulsion upon ionization.

Exception Formic acid (HCOOH) is expected to be a weaker acid than acetic acid (CH3COOH) due to alkyl inductive effects.

Observation: Formic acid (pKa = 3.77) is a stronger acid than acetic acid (pKa = 4.76). — The methyl group in acetic acid exerts a +I effect which destabilizes the acetate anion relative to the formate anion.

Exception Electron-donating groups consistently decrease acidity when attached to the aromatic ring.

Observation: p-Methoxybenzoic acid is slightly stronger than benzoic acid in certain non-polar media, though generally weaker in water. — Solvent-solute interactions and specific hydrogen bonding can sometimes invert expected electronic substituent parameters.

Cl3CCOOH > Cl2CHCOOH > ClCH2COOH

Application: More electron-withdrawing atoms and closer proximity to the -COOH group exponentially increase the acidity.

Ortho-Effect Shortcut
Ortho > Para ≈ Meta (mostly)

Application: In substituted benzoic acids, almost any ortho-substituent (whether EWG or EDG) makes the acid stronger than benzoic acid.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Carboxylic acids react with sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3) to liberate carbon dioxide gas with effervescence.
  • The pKa value is inversely proportional to acid strength; lower pKa denotes a stronger acid.
  • Trifluoroacetic acid (CF3COOH) is one of the strongest organic acids due to the extreme -I and -F electron withdrawal.
  • Benzoic acid is a stronger acid than aliphatic carboxylic acids like acetic acid because the sp2 carbon of the benzene ring is more electronegative than sp3.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups in the alpha position enhance acidity by facilitating proton release.
  • Salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid) exhibits intramolecular hydrogen bonding in its conjugate base, increasing stability.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing pKa with Ka, assuming a higher numerical pKa means a stronger acid.
  • Assuming ortho-substituents always decrease acidity via steric hindrance, forgetting the ortho-effect.
  • Believing alcohols can dissolve in NaHCO3 solution just like carboxylic acids.
  • Neglecting the number of electronegative substituents when ranking haloacetic acid strengths.

⚡ Quick Revision: Acidic Nature of Carboxylic Acids

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Ionization Equilibrium

Carboxylic acids dissociate in water to establish an equilibrium between the unionized acid and the carboxylate anion, quantified by Ka.

Resonance Stabilization

Equivalent resonance structures in the carboxylate ion spread negative charge equally, driving the equilibrium forward and enhancing acidity.

Substituent Effects

Inductive and resonance effects of ring or chain substituents modify electron density at the carboxyl carbon, altering acidic strength predictably.

Diagnostic Tests

Reaction with aqueous sodium bicarbonate distinguishes carboxylic acids from phenols and alcohols through CO2 effervescence.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Carboxylate Anion (RCOO-)
Conjugate base of a carboxylic acid stabilized by resonance.
Acid Dissociation Constant (Ka)
Equilibrium constant for the dissociation of an acid in water (mol dm-3).
pKa (-log(Ka))
Logarithmic measure of acid strength, dimensionless.
Inductive Effect (-I / +I)
Polarization of sigma bonds through carbon chains caused by electronegativity differences.
Ortho Effect (Steric/Electronic)
Enhanced acidity of ortho-substituted benzoic acids.
Hammett Constant (σ)
Quantitative measure of polar substituent effects on benzene rings.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carboxylic acids react with NaHCO3 to give CO2 gas.
  • pKa of acetic acid is 4.76.
  • pKa of benzoic acid is 4.20.
  • EWGs increase acidity; EDGs decrease acidity.
  • Trifluoroacetic acid is stronger than trichloroacetic acid.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carboxylic acids are acidic due to resonance-stabilized carboxylate ions.
  • Substituents alter acidity via inductive and resonance mechanisms.
  • Ortho-substituted benzoic acids are unusually strong.
  • Carboxylic acids are stronger acids than phenols and alcohols.

⚖️ Key Differences

pKa VS pH

pKa is an intrinsic constant for a specific acid, whereas pH varies with concentration and solution conditions.

Question 1 of 20

Which of the following acids has the lowest pKa value?

Explanation: Trichloroacetic acid has three electron-withdrawing chlorine atoms which exert a strong -I effect, stabilizing the carboxylate ion and making it the strongest acid (lowest pKa).

Question 2 of 20

The conjugate base of a carboxylic acid is stabilized by:

Explanation: The carboxylate anion is stabilized by equivalent resonance structures that spread the negative charge evenly over two oxygen atoms.

Question 3 of 20

Which compound evolves CO2 when treated with aqueous NaHCO3?

Explanation: Acetic acid is sufficiently strong to decompose sodium bicarbonate, releasing carbon dioxide gas, whereas alcohols and phenols are too weak.

Question 4 of 20

Among the following, which exhibits the greatest acid strength?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is a strong electron-withdrawing group via both -I and -M effects, significantly enhancing the acidity of p-nitrobenzoic acid.

Question 5 of 20

What is the primary reason for the ortho-effect in substituted benzoic acids?

Explanation: Ortho substituents force the carboxyl group out of the coplanarity with the benzene ring, inhibiting resonance in the unionized acid and stabilizing the anion.

Question 6 of 20

Formic acid is stronger than acetic acid because:

Explanation: The electron-donating methyl group in acetic acid destabilizes the acetate anion by intensifying negative charge, making it weaker than formic acid.

Question 7 of 20

Which halogen-substituted acetic acid is the strongest?

Explanation: Fluorine is the most electronegative halogen, exerting the strongest inductive electron-withdrawal (-I) effect, making fluoroacetic acid the strongest among haloacetic acids.

Question 8 of 20

Phenol is a weaker acid than benzoic acid because:

Explanation: In the carboxylate ion, the negative charge is shared between two electronegative oxygen atoms, whereas in the phenoxide ion, it is delocalized onto less electronegative carbon atoms.

Question 9 of 20

What happens to the acidity of propanoic acid when a chlorine atom is placed at the α-position compared to the β-position?

Explanation: The inductive effect diminishes rapidly with distance; thus, closer proximity of the electronegative chlorine atom at the α-position results in greater acidity.

Question 10 of 20

Which of the following functional groups decreases the acidic strength of benzoic acid when present at the para position?

Explanation: The methoxy group (-OCH3) acts as an electron-donating group via resonance (+M), which increases electron density on the ring and decreases acidity.

Question 11 of 20

The pKa of acetic acid is approximately:

Explanation: Acetic acid is a weak organic acid with a standard pKa value of approximately 4.76 in water at 25 °C.

Question 12 of 20

Which of the following is correct regarding the acidity order of mono-substituted benzoic acids?

Explanation: Both nitro groups are electron-withdrawing, with para exerting both -I and strong -M effects, making it more acidic than meta, which operates primarily via -I.

Question 13 of 20

Alcohols are weaker acids than carboxylic acids primarily because:

Explanation: The alkoxide ion retains a localized negative charge on a single oxygen atom without any resonance stabilization.

Question 14 of 20

Which acid has the highest Ka value?

Explanation: Methanoic acid (formic acid) has the highest Ka (lowest pKa) among simple unsubstituted aliphatic carboxylic acids.

Question 15 of 20

An electron-withdrawing substituent at the meta position of benzoic acid affects acidity primarily through:

Explanation: Resonance effects do not operate from the meta position in benzene derivatives; therefore, substituent effects at meta operate almost exclusively via the inductive (-I) effect.

Question 16 of 20

Which reagent is most suitable to distinguish between benzoic acid and phenol?

Explanation: Aqueous sodium bicarbonate liberates CO2 with benzoic acid but does not react with phenol, serving as a specific diagnostic test.

Question 17 of 20

The acidity of chloroacetic acid is greater than acetic acid due to:

Explanation: Chlorine is electronegative and pulls electron density away through sigma bonds (-I effect), stabilizing the conjugate base.

Question 18 of 20

Which compound is the strongest acid among the following?

Explanation: Acetic acid (pKa ~ 4.76) is significantly stronger than phenol (pKa ~ 10), water (pKa ~ 15.7), and ethanol (pKa ~ 16).

Question 19 of 20

What is the effect of increasing the length of the alkyl chain on the acidity of simple carboxylic acids?

Explanation: Longer alkyl chains exhibit a stronger cumulative +I effect, which destabilizes the carboxylate anion and decreases acid strength.

Question 20 of 20

In the Hammett equation log(K/K0) = ρσ, the reaction constant ρ for the ionization of benzoic acids in water is defined as:

Explanation: By definition, the reference reaction of benzoic acid ionization in water at 25 °C sets the reaction constant ρ equal to 1.0.
