Organic Chemistry

Carboxylic Acids and Derivatives

Carboxylic acids and their derivatives contain the acyl group bonded to electronegative heteroatoms that dictate their reactivity toward nucleophilic acyl substitution. Their physical and chemical profiles are heavily governed by intermolecular hydrogen bonding and resonance stabilization.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Acidity and Electronic Effects in Carboxylic Acids

  • Carboxylic acids are significantly stronger acids than alcohols and phenols due to extensive resonance stabilization of the carboxylate anion (RCOO-).
  • Electron-withdrawing substituents (-NO2, -X, -CN) increase acidity via -I and -M effects, stabilizing the resulting negative charge.
  • Electron-donating alkyl groups (-R) decrease acidity through +I effect, destabilizing the carboxylate anion by intensifying negative charge density.
  • Ortho-effect in benzoic acid derivatives causes increased acidity regardless of electronic nature due to steric inhibition of resonance.

Preparation and Synthetic Routes of Carboxylic Acids

  • Oxidation of primary alcohols and aldehydes using strong oxidizing agents like KMnO4 or Jones reagent yields carboxylic acids.
  • Hydrolysis of nitriles (cyanides) under acidic or basic conditions provides a robust two-step route starting from alkyl halides.
  • Carboxylation of Grignard reagents with solid carbon dioxide (dry ice) followed by acid hydrolysis increases the carbon chain length by one unit.
  • Oxidation of alkylbenzenes with alkaline KMnO4 selectively converts any alkyl side chain possessing a benzylic hydrogen directly into a benzoic acid derivative.

Reactivity and Interconversion of Carboxylic Acid Derivatives

  • Carboxylic acid derivatives follow the reactivity order: Acid Chlorides > Acid Anhydrides > Esters > Amides.
  • Nucleophilic acyl substitution proceeds via a tetrahedral addition-elimination intermediate where the leaving group departs in the second step.
  • Reduction of esters using LiAlH4 yields two moles of primary alcohols, whereas selective reduction with DIBAL-H stops at the aldehyde stage at low temperatures.
  • Amides undergo Hofmann bromamide degradation with Br2 and aqueous KOH to yield a primary amine with the loss of one carbon atom.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl groups generally decrease acidity via electron-releasing inductive effect (+I).

Observation: Formic acid (HCOOH) is stronger than acetic acid (CH3COOH). — Acetic acid possesses an electron-donating methyl group that destabilizes the carboxylate anion, whereas formic acid lacks this group.

Exception Ortho-substituted benzoic acids are generally more acidic than benzoic acid.

Observation: Ortho-methoxybenzoic acid is less acidic than expected in specific conformations. — Intramolecular hydrogen bonding or steric hindrance can alter the optimal coplanarity required for resonance stabilization.

Exception Carboxylic acids do not typically undergo typical nucleophilic addition reactions like aldehydes and ketones.

Observation: Formic acid undergoes self-oxidation-reduction and reduces Tollens' reagent. — Formic acid contains a structural aldehyde equivalent (-CHO group attached to -OH) enabling reducing properties.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Carboxylic acids exist predominantly as cyclic dimers in vapor phase and non-polar solvents due to strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
  • Boiling points of carboxylic acids are higher than alcohols of comparable molecular mass because of stable two-molecule hydrogen-bonded dimers.
  • Lower aliphatic carboxylic acids (C1 to C4) are completely miscible with water due to hydrogen bonding with water molecules.
  • Formic acid reacts with concentrated H2SO4 to produce carbon monoxide and water.
  • Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky reaction halogenates the α-carbon of carboxylic acids using chlorine or bromine in the presence of red phosphorus.
  • Rosenmund reduction converts acid chlorides into aldehydes using poisoned palladium catalyst (Pd/BaSO4).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the acidity order of substituted benzoic acids where ortho-isomers almost always exhibit the ortho-effect, rendering them more acidic than meta- and para-isomers.
  • Assuming esters undergo rapid spontaneous hydrolysis in neutral water without realizing that acid or base catalysis is required.
  • Forgetting that HVZ halogenation strictly requires at least one α-hydrogen atom on the substrate acid.
  • Mistaking the mechanism of esterification where the oxygen atom in the resulting ester originates from the alcohol, not the carboxylic acid (proven via isotopic labeling).

⚡ Quick Revision: Carboxylic Acids and Derivatives

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Acidity

Carboxylic acids owe their acidity to resonance stabilization of the carboxylate ion, where the negative charge is delocalized equally over two electronegative oxygen atoms.

Synthetic Pathways

Acids are prepared via oxidation of primary alcohols/aldehydes, hydrolysis of cyanides, or carbonation of Grignard reagents with dry ice followed by acidification.

Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution

Derivatives react via addition-elimination mechanisms, with reaction rates dictated by the basicity of the departing leaving group.

Name Reactions and Degradations

Important transformations include HVZ halogenation, Hofmann bromamide degradation, and decarboxylation using soda lime.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Carboxyl Group (-COOH)
Functional group comprising a carbonyl and a hydroxyl group.
Acyl Group (R-C(=O)-)
Functional moiety derived by removing the hydroxyl group from an oxo acid.
Zwitterion (Dipolar Ion)
Neutral molecule possessing both positive and negative ionic centers.
Ortho Effect (Steric/Electronic Effect)
Anomalous high acidity of ortho-substituted benzoic acids.
Saponification (Base Hydrolysis of Ester)
Alkaline hydrolysis of esters to yield carboxylate salts and alcohols.
Transesterification (Alcoholysis)
Exchange of the alkoxy group of an ester with another alcohol.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carboxylic acids form stable hydrogen-bonded dimers doubling their apparent molecular mass in non-polar media.
  • Formic acid reduces Tollens' reagent and KMnO4 due to its inherent aldehydic hydrogen.
  • Acid chlorides are the most reactive carboxylic acid derivatives.
  • Hofmann bromamide degradation shortens the carbon chain by one carbon atom.
  • Esterification is an equilibrium process driven forward by removing water or using excess alcohol.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carboxylic acids exhibit high boiling points due to intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups enhance acidity while electron-donating groups reduce it.
  • Derivatives interconvert via nucleophilic acyl substitution following the leaving group ability scale.
  • Key diagnostic tests include effervescence with NaHCO3 releasing CO2 gas.

⚖️ Key Differences

Alcohol Acidity VS Carboxylic Acid Acidity

Alcohols yield alkoxides with poor resonance stabilization, whereas carboxylic acids yield highly resonance-stabilized carboxylate ions.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following acids is the strongest due to inductive effect?

Explanation: Trichloroacetic acid has three chlorine atoms exerting a strong -I effect, stabilizing the carboxylate anion significantly more than one or two chlorines.

Question 2 of 19

What is the product when acetic acid is treated with red P and Cl2?

Explanation: This is the Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction which selectively halogenates the alpha-carbon to form monochloroacetic acid.

Question 3 of 19

Which derivative of carboxylic acids is most reactive towards nucleophilic substitution?

Explanation: Acid chlorides possess the best leaving group (Cl-), making them the most reactive acyl derivatives.

Question 4 of 19

Propanoic acid on treatment with soda lime undergoes which reaction type?

Explanation: Heating sodium propanoate with soda lime (NaOH + CaO) results in the loss of CO2 (decarboxylation) yielding ethane.

Question 5 of 19

In the esterification of carboxylic acids with alcohols, the oxygen atom of the water molecule originates from:

Explanation: Mechanistic studies using isotopic 18O labeling show that the -OH group is lost from the carboxylic acid and the hydrogen comes from the alcohol.

Question 6 of 19

Which reagent converts a carboxylic acid directly into an acid chloride?

Explanation: Thionyl chloride (SOCl2) converts carboxylic acids to acid chlorides with gaseous by-products (SO2 and HCl) that easily escape.

Question 7 of 19

Hofmann bromamide degradation of benzamide yields:

Explanation: Benzamide (C6H5CONH2) undergoes Hofmann degradation to form aniline (C6H5NH2) with the loss of the carbonyl carbon as Na2CO3.

Question 8 of 19

Which of the following compounds gives brisk effervescence with NaHCO3 solution?

Explanation: Carboxylic acids are sufficiently acidic to decompose sodium bicarbonate, releasing carbon dioxide gas with effervescence.

Question 9 of 19

Reduction of an ester with LiAlH4 yields:

Explanation: LiAlH4 cleaves the ester bond completely, reducing the acyl portion to a primary alcohol and releasing the alkoxy portion as a second alcohol molecule.

Question 10 of 19

Formic acid differs from other carboxylic acids because it:

Explanation: Due to its formyl group (-CHO structure), formic acid acts as a reducing agent and reduces Tollens' reagent to metallic silver.

Question 11 of 19

What is the correct order of acid strength for fluoroacetic, chloroacetic, bromoacetic, and iodoacetic acids?

Explanation: Acidity parallels electronegativity of the halogen atom; fluorine is the most electronegative, imparting the strongest -I effect.

Question 12 of 19

Hydrolysis of an amide under acidic conditions yields:

Explanation: Acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of amides produces a carboxylic acid and an ammonium salt (protonated amine).

Question 13 of 19

Which acid exhibits the highest boiling point?

Explanation: Boiling point increases with molecular mass due to increasing van der Waals forces alongside intermolecular hydrogen bonding.

Question 14 of 19

The conversion of RCOOH to RCH2OH can be efficiently achieved using:

Explanation: Lithium aluminium hydride (LiAlH4) is a powerful reducing agent capable of reducing carboxylic acids to primary alcohols.

Question 15 of 19

Which compound is formed when calcium acetate is dry distilled?

Explanation: Dry distillation of calcium acetate yields acetone along with calcium carbonate.

Question 16 of 19

Amides are amphoteric in nature because they can act as:

Explanation: Amides exhibit very weak basicity due to nitrogen lone pair resonance with the carbonyl, and very weak acidity due to N-H polarization.

Question 17 of 19

Which carboxylic acid cannot be prepared by the oxidation of corresponding primary alcohol?

Explanation: Pivalic acid corresponds to a tertiary carbon skeleton (2,2-dimethylpropanoic acid) which lacks a primary alcohol precursor with matching carbon skeleton.

Question 18 of 19

The IUPAC name of lactic acid is:

Explanation: Lactic acid has a three-carbon backbone with a hydroxyl group on carbon 2, making it 2-hydroxypropanoic acid.

Question 19 of 19

What happens when benzoic acid is heated with sodalime?

Explanation: Decarboxylation of sodium benzoate with soda lime eliminates CO2 to yield benzene.

In the Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky reaction, the catalyst used is:

Question 1 of 10
What is the principal organic product formed when acetamide is treated with Br2 and aqueous KOH?

Solution: Hofmann bromamide degradation of acetamide (CH3CONH2) yields methylamine (CH3NH2) along with K2CO3, KBr, and water.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following acids gives a positive test with Tollen's reagent?

Solution: Formic acid contains a formyl hydrogen atom which acts as a reducing agent, reducing ammoniacal silver nitrate (Tollen's reagent) to metallic silver.

Question 3 of 10
The correct order of boiling points for acetic acid, ethanol, and acetaldehyde is:

Solution: Carboxylic acids form stable double hydrogen-bonded dimers (highest bp), alcohols form extensive single hydrogen bonds, and aldehydes possess dipole-dipole interactions only (lowest bp).

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following compounds is formed when calcium formate is dry distilled?

Solution: Dry distillation of calcium formate yields formaldehyde (methanal) and calcium carbonate.

Question 5 of 10
What is the product of the reaction between benzoyl chloride and benzene in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3?

Solution: This is a Friedel-Crafts acylation reaction producing benzophenone (diphenyl ketone).

Question 6 of 10
Which carboxylic acid has the lowest pKa value?

Solution: Lowest pKa corresponds to the strongest acid. Trichloroacetic acid is the strongest acid among the choices due to maximum electron-withdrawing chlorine atoms.

Question 7 of 10
When propanoic acid reacts with methanol in the presence of concentrated H2SO4, the ester formed is:

Solution: Propanoic acid (3 carbons) combined with methanol (1 carbon) produces methyl propanoate.

Question 8 of 10
Which reagent can convert acetic anhydride into acetic acid?

Solution: Hydrolysis of acid anhydrides with water yields two molecules of corresponding carboxylic acids.

Question 9 of 10
Alpha-bromination of propanoic acid requires:

Solution: Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) halogenation requires halogen (Br2) and red phosphorus to convert alpha-hydrogens into bromo derivatives.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following functional groups has the lowest priority in IUPAC nomenclature?

Solution: Carboxylic acids and their derivatives rank higher in IUPAC principal functional group precedence compared to aldehydes (-CHO).
