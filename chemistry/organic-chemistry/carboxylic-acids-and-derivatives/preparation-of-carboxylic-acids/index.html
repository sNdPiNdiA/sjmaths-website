Organic Chemistry

Preparation of Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids are synthesized through the oxidation of primary alcohols and aldehydes, or the hydrolysis of nitriles, esters, and acid derivatives. Organometallic reagents like Grignard reagents react with carbon dioxide to yield carboxylic acids upon acidification.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Oxidative Methods from Hydrocarbons and Alcohols

  • Primary alcohols and aldehydes are readily oxidized to carboxylic acids using strong oxidizing agents such as alkaline KMnO4 or Jones reagent (CrO3 in aqueous H2SO4).
  • Alkylbenzenes undergo oxidation with alkaline KMnO4 to yield benzoic acid derivatives, regardless of the alkyl chain length, provided at least one benzylic hydrogen is present.
  • Tertiary alkylbenzenes do not undergo side-chain oxidation due to the absence of benzylic hydrogen atoms.

Synthesis via Organometallic and Nitrile Intermediates

  • Grignard reagents react with solid carbon dioxide (dry ice) to form magnesium salts of carboxylic acids, which subsequently yield the corresponding carboxylic acid on acidification.
  • Nitriles are hydrolyzed to amides and further to carboxylic acids under acidic or basic conditions.
  • Alkyl halides can be converted to nitriles using NaCN in a nucleophilic substitution, extending the carbon chain by one carbon atom prior to hydrolysis.

Hydrolysis of Acid Derivatives and Specialized Reactions

  • Carboxylic acid derivatives including esters, acyl halides, anhydrides, and amides yield carboxylic acids upon hydrolysis.
  • Esters undergo base-catalyzed hydrolysis known as saponification, yielding carboxylate salts that yield free acids upon acidification.
  • Methyl ketones undergo the haloform reaction with sodium hypohalite to produce carboxylic acids with the loss of a haloform molecule.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Oxidation of alkylbenzenes yields benzoic acid regardless of alkyl chain length.

Observation: Tertiary alkylbenzenes (e.g., tert-butylbenzene) are unreactive toward KMnO4 oxidation. — Absence of benzylic hydrogen atoms prevents the initial hydrogen abstraction step required for oxidation.

Exception Grignard reagents add to carbonyl compounds to form alcohols.

Observation: Reaction with carbon dioxide yields carboxylic acids. — The intermediate magnesium carboxylate is stabilized and does not undergo further nucleophilic addition under standard dry conditions.

Exception Alcohols are oxidized to aldehydes and then acids using standard oxidants.

Observation: Primary alcohols can be selectively stopped at the aldehyde stage using mild reagents like PCC. — Pyridinium chlorochromate (PCC) lacks water, preventing the formation of the hydrate intermediate necessary for further oxidation to the acid.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Chain Extension Mnemonic
R-X → R-CN → R-COOH

Application: Use when an exam question asks to increase the carbon chain length by one carbon atom.

Benzylic Oxidation Rule
Ar-CH(R)2 or Ar-CH2-R → Ar-COOH

Application: Any alkyl side chain on a benzene ring regardless of length is oxidized down to a single -COOH group, provided a benzylic C-H exists.

Grignard CO2 Carbon Count
R-MgX + CO2 = R+1 Carbons

Application: The resulting carboxylic acid always contains one more carbon atom than the starting alkyl halide used to form the Grignard reagent.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Oxidation of toluene with acidic or alkaline KMnO4 produces benzoic acid.
  • Grignard reagents react with dry ice to form carboxylic acids containing one more carbon atom.
  • Hydrolysis of alkyl cyanides gives carboxylic acids via intermediate amides.
  • PCC oxidizes primary alcohols only up to aldehydes, not carboxylic acids.
  • Esters undergo alkaline hydrolysis to yield carboxylate salts, termed saponification.
  • Methyl ketones react with halogen and alkali to give carboxylic acids via the haloform reaction.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting that tertiary alkylbenzenes do not react with alkaline KMnO4.
  • Assuming Grignard reagents can be carbonated in wet ether or aqueous media.
  • Confusing the role of PCC versus KMnO4 in alcohol oxidation.
  • Failing to account for the carbon atom contributed by CO2 in Grignard synthesis.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation of Carboxylic Acids

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Oxidative Cleavage and Synthesis

Primary alcohols and aldehydes are converted to carboxylic acids via strong oxidizing agents like KMnO4 or K2Cr2O7.

Organometallic Carbonation

Grignard reagents react with carbon dioxide to insert a carboxyl group, extending the carbon chain by one unit upon acidification.

Hydrolysis of Derivatives

Nitriles, esters, amides, and acid halides yield carboxylic acids upon acid- or base-catalyzed hydrolysis.

Side-Chain Oxidation

Alkylbenzenes with at least one benzylic hydrogen are oxidized directly to benzoic acid by alkaline potassium permanganate.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Saponification (Base Hydrolysis of Ester)
Alkaline hydrolysis of esters yielding carboxylate salts and alcohols.
Benzylic Hydrogen (C-H on alpha carbon to ring)
Hydrogen atom attached to the carbon directly bonded to a benzene ring.
Jones Reagent (CrO3 / H2SO4)
Strong oxidizing agent used to convert primary alcohols directly to carboxylic acids.
Grignard Reagent (R-MgX)
Organometallic halide used in carbon chain extension reactions.
Haloform Reaction (R-CO-CH3 + X2/OH-)
Oxidative cleavage of methyl ketones to carboxylic acids.
Dry Ice (Solid CO2)
Carbon source used in Grignard carbonation reactions under anhydrous conditions.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • KMnO4 oxidizes any alkyl side chain on a benzene ring to a -COOH group, provided a benzylic hydrogen is present.
  • Grignard reagents add to CO2 to form carboxylic acids with +1 carbon.
  • Nitrile hydrolysis requires either strong acid or strong base with heat.
  • PCC stops at the aldehyde stage and does not yield carboxylic acids.
  • Esters hydrolyze in base to form carboxylate salts requiring acid workup.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carboxylic acids are prepared via oxidation of alcohols and alkylbenzenes.
  • Carbon chain extension is achieved via nitriles and Grignard reagents.
  • Acid derivatives readily hydrolyze to give parent carboxylic acids.
  • Anhydrous conditions are mandatory for Grignard carbonation.

⚖️ Key Differences

PCC Oxidation VS KMnO4 Oxidation

PCC stops at the aldehyde stage for primary alcohols, whereas KMnO4 oxidizes them all the way to carboxylic acids.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following reagents is used to convert toluene to benzoic acid?

Explanation: Alkaline KMnO4 oxidizes the methyl group of toluene directly to a carboxyl group, forming potassium benzoate which yields benzoic acid upon acidification.

Question 2 of 18

What is the product formed when methylmagnesium bromide reacts with carbon dioxide followed by acid hydrolysis?

Explanation: CH3MgBr reacting with CO2 gives magnesium acetate, which on acid hydrolysis yields acetic acid (ethanoic acid).

Question 3 of 18

Which alkylbenzene is resistant to oxidation by alkaline KMnO4?

Explanation: Tert-butylbenzene lacks benzylic hydrogen atoms, making it resistant to side-chain oxidation by KMnO4.

Question 4 of 18

Hydrolysis of acetonitrile with aqueous acid yields:

Explanation: Acid-catalyzed complete hydrolysis of acetonitrile (CH3CN) yields acetic acid along with ammonium ions.

Question 5 of 18

Which reagent will convert a primary alcohol directly to a carboxylic acid?

Explanation: Jones reagent (CrO3 in aqueous H2SO4) is a strong oxidant that converts primary alcohols directly to carboxylic acids.

Question 6 of 18

What is the role of dry ether in the preparation of carboxylic acids using Grignard reagents?

Explanation: Dry ether provides an inert, anhydrous medium necessary to stabilize the Grignard reagent and prevent premature protonation.

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following compounds gives a carboxylic acid on treatment with NaOH followed by H+?

Explanation: Esters like ethyl acetate undergo alkaline hydrolysis (saponification) to yield carboxylate salts, which give carboxylic acids upon acidification.

Question 8 of 18

The haloform reaction of a methyl ketone with halogen and alkali produces:

Explanation: Methyl ketones react with halogen and base to yield a carboxylate salt and a haloform (e.g., CHI3).

Question 9 of 18

In the laboratory preparation of benzoic acid from an alkylbenzene, what is the intermediate species before acidification?

Explanation: Oxidation with alkaline KMnO4 produces soluble potassium benzoate, which precipitates as benzoic acid upon addition of mineral acid.

Question 10 of 18

Which method increases the carbon chain length by one carbon atom when preparing a carboxylic acid from an alkyl halide?

Explanation: Converting an alkyl halide to a nitrile (using KCN) introduces an extra carbon atom, which upon hydrolysis yields a carboxylic acid with n+1 carbons.

Question 11 of 18

What happens when benzamide is treated with aqueous acid under reflux?

Explanation: Amides undergo acid-catalyzed hydrolysis to yield carboxylic acids and ammonium salts.

Question 12 of 18

Which of the following functional groups cannot be oxidized directly to a carboxylic acid by Jones reagent?

Explanation: Ketones do not undergo standard oxidation to carboxylic acids under Jones conditions without C-C bond cleavage.

Question 13 of 18

Which reagent is selective for stopping the oxidation of a primary alcohol at the aldehyde stage?

Explanation: Pyridinium chlorochromate (PCC) is a mild, anhydrous oxidant that oxidizes primary alcohols to aldehydes without proceeding to carboxylic acids.

Question 14 of 18

What is the byproduct formed alongside the carboxylate salt in the haloform reaction of acetone?

Explanation: The haloform reaction cleaves methyl ketones to give a carboxylate salt and a haloform (CHI3, CHCl3, or CHBr3).

Question 15 of 18

Which reaction sequence correctly converts bromomethane to acetic acid?

Explanation: Nucleophilic substitution of bromomethane with cyanide gives acetonitrile, which upon acid hydrolysis yields acetic acid.

Question 16 of 18

Why must carbon dioxide used in Grignard carbonation be in solid form (dry ice)?

Explanation: Solid carbon dioxide (dry ice) is used to ensure an anhydrous source of CO2, avoiding moisture that would destroy the Grignard reagent.

Question 17 of 18

Which derivative of carboxylic acids is most resistant to hydrolysis?

Explanation: Amides are the most stable and least reactive acid derivatives toward hydrolysis due to resonance stabilization of the C-N bond.

Question 18 of 18

Oxidation of 1-phenylethane with alkaline KMnO4 gives:

Explanation: The benzylic carbon of 1-phenylethane is oxidized to a carboxyl group, yielding benzoic acid and releasing CO2.
