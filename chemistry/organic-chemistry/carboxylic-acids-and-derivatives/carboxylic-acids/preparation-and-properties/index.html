Preparation and Properties of Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids are organic compounds containing the carboxyl functional group (-COOH) characterized by high polarity and strong hydrogen bonding. They are prepared via oxidation of primary alcohols, hydrolysis of nitriles, or carbonation of Grignard reagents.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthetic Routes to Carboxylic Acids

  • Primary alcohols and aldehydes undergo vigorous oxidation with KMnO4 or K2Cr2O7 to yield carboxylic acids.
  • Alkyl halides are converted to nitriles using NaCN, followed by acid-catalyzed hydrolysis to extend the carbon chain by one carbon atom.
  • Grignard reagents react with solid carbon dioxide (dry ice) to form magnesium salts, which yield acids upon acidification.
  • Alkylbenzenes undergo side-chain oxidation with alkaline KMnO4 regardless of chain length, yielding benzoic acid.

Acidic Strength and Substituent Effects

  • Carboxylic acids are stronger acids than alcohols and phenols due to resonance stabilization of the carboxylate anion.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups (EWGs) disperse negative charge on the carboxylate oxygen, increasing acidity (pKa decreases).
  • Electron-donating groups (EDGs) intensify negative charge and destabilize the carboxylate ion, decreasing acid strength.
  • In substituted benzoic acids, ortho-effect causes ortho-substituted isomers to be stronger acids than their meta and para counterparts due to steric and electronic stabilization.

Chemical Properties and Functional Group Transformations

  • Reaction with PCl5, PCl3, or SOCl2 converts carboxylic acids into highly reactive acid chlorides.
  • Heating carboxylic acids with ammonia forms ammonium salts, which on strong heating dehydrate to form amides.
  • Decarboxylation occurs when sodium salts of carboxylic acids are heated with soda-lime (NaOH + CaO), releasing CO2 and yielding alkanes.
  • Alpha-hydrogen atoms in carboxylic acids are halogenated via the HVZ reaction using red phosphorus and chlorine or bromine.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Formic acid lacks an alkyl group and behaves only as a reducing agent.

Observation: Formic acid reduces Tollens' reagent and Fehling's solution. — Presence of an aldehyde-like structural unit (H-C(=O)OH) in its molecule.

Exception Ortho-substituted benzoic acids are generally stronger acids than benzoic acid.

Observation: Ortho-methoxybenzoic acid is less acidic than expected in specific conformations. — Steric inhibition of resonance and intramolecular hydrogen bonding alters charge dispersal.

Exception Dicarboxylic acids eliminate water upon heating to form cyclic anhydrides.

Observation: Malonic acid and oxalic acid undergo decarboxylation rather than simple anhydride formation. — Oxalic acid eliminates CO and CO2, while malonic acid loses CO2 due to stable six-membered cyclic transition states.

EWG ↑ ⇒ Acid Strength ↑ (pKa ↓) | EDG ↑ ⇒ Acid Strength ↓ (pKa ↑)

Application: Quickly rank acidity by identifying -I and -M effects of substituents on the aromatic ring or aliphatic chain.

Application: Always remember that soda-lime decarboxylation removes one carbon atom from the parent acid chain.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Carboxylic acids form stable dimers in vapor phase and non-polar solvents due to intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
  • Boiling points of carboxylic acids are significantly higher than alcohols of comparable molecular mass due to extensive hydrogen bonding.
  • Formic acid (HCOOH) gives a positive Tollens' test because it contains both acid and aldehyde functionalities.
  • Esterification is a reversible equilibrium reaction catalyzed by mineral acids like concentrated H2SO4.
  • The reaction of Grignard reagent with carbon dioxide extends the carbon chain by exactly one carbon atom.
  • Ortho-toluic acid is a stronger acid than benzoic acid due to the combined inductive and steric ortho-effect.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the acidity of carboxylic acids with alcohols; carboxylic acids are much stronger due to resonance stabilization of the carboxylate anion.
  • Assuming all carboxylic acids undergo the HVZ reaction; alpha-hydrogen-free acids like benzoic acid or trimethylacetic acid do not react.
  • Forgetting that soda-lime decarboxylation eliminates CO2, shortening the alkane product chain by one carbon relative to the starting acid.
  • Confusing the products of reduction with LiAlH4 (which yields primary alcohols) versus milder reducing agents.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation and Properties of Carboxylic Acids

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

Carboxyl carbon is sp2 hybridized with trigonal planar geometry and bond angles close to 120°. The carbonyl oxygen withdraws electron density, polarizing the O-H bond for facile proton release.

Methods of Preparation

Synthesized via oxidation of primary alcohols or alkylbenzenes, hydrolysis of nitriles and esters, and carbonation of organometallic Grignard reagents with carbon dioxide.

Acidity and Substituent Effects

Acidity is governed by the stability of the carboxylate anion. Electron-withdrawing groups enhance acidity through inductive and resonance electron withdrawal.

Chemical Reactions

Undergo nucleophilic acyl substitution, decarboxylation, reduction to primary alcohols, and alpha-halogenation via the Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky reaction.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Carboxyl Group (-COOH)
Functional group comprising a carbonyl and hydroxyl group attached to the same carbon atom.
Zwitterion (Dip dipolar ion)
Neutral molecule with positive and negative electrical charges at different locations.
HVZ Reaction (Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky)
Alpha-halogenation of carboxylic acids using halogen and red phosphorus.
Soda-Lime (NaOH + CaO)
Reagent mixture used for the decarboxylation of carboxylic acid sodium salts.
Esterification (RCOOR')
Acid-catalyzed condensation reaction between a carboxylic acid and an alcohol.
Ortho Effect (Steric/Electronic)
Enhanced acidity of ortho-substituted benzoic acids regardless of substituent electronic nature.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carboxylic acids are stronger acids than phenols and alcohols.
  • HCOOH reduces Tollens' reagent and Fehling's solution.
  • Grignard + CO2 → Carboxylic acid (Chain extension by 1 carbon).
  • HVZ reaction requires alpha-hydrogen atoms.
  • Decarboxylation with soda-lime removes one carbon atom.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carboxylic acids possess high boiling points due to intermolecular hydrogen bonding dimers.
  • Preparation involves oxidation, hydrolysis, and Grignard carbonation.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups increase acidity; electron-donating groups decrease it.
  • Reactions include salt formation, esterification, acid halide formation, and decarboxylation.

⚖️ Key Differences

Formic Acid VS Acetic Acid

Formic acid reduces Tollens' reagent due to its formyl hydrogen, whereas acetic acid does not.

Which of the following reagents is used to convert an alkyl cyanide into a carboxylic acid?

Explanation: Acid or base-catalyzed hydrolysis of nitriles yields carboxylic acids.

Question 2 of 18

What is the product when acetic acid reacts with red phosphorus and chlorine?

Explanation: The HVZ reaction selectively halogenates the alpha-carbon to yield monochloroacetic acid.

Question 3 of 18

Which acid is the strongest among the following?

Explanation: Trichloroacetic acid has three electron-withdrawing chlorine atoms, which significantly stabilize the carboxylate anion through the inductive effect.

Question 4 of 18

Carboxylic acids have higher boiling points than alcohols of comparable molecular mass because:

Explanation: Carboxylic acids form robust cyclic dimers via two intermolecular hydrogen bonds, requiring higher thermal energy to break.

Question 5 of 18

Which gas is evolved when a carboxylic acid reacts with sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3)?

Explanation: Carboxylic acids are strong enough acids to decompose sodium bicarbonate, liberating carbon dioxide gas with effervescence.

Question 6 of 18

Reaction of methylmagnesium bromide with carbon dioxide followed by acid hydrolysis yields:

Explanation: Grignard reagent CH3MgBr reacting with CO2 gives CH3COOMgBr, which upon hydrolysis yields acetic acid.

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following compounds does not undergo the Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky reaction?

Explanation: Trimethylacetic acid (2,2-dimethylpropanoic acid) lacks alpha-hydrogen atoms, making HVZ halogenation impossible.

Question 8 of 18

Formic acid gives a positive test with:

Explanation: Formic acid acts as a reducing agent and reduces Tollens' reagent due to its unique aldehyde-like hydrogen.

Question 9 of 18

When sodium acetate is heated with soda-lime, the product formed is:

Explanation: Decarboxylation of sodium acetate with soda-lime yields methane and sodium carbonate.

Question 10 of 18

Which derivative of carboxylic acids is most reactive towards nucleophilic acyl substitution?

Explanation: Acid chlorides possess the best leaving group (Cl-) and high carbonyl polarization, making them the most reactive acyl derivatives.

Question 11 of 18

What is the pKa range of typical aliphatic monocarboxylic acids?

Explanation: Aliphatic carboxylic acids have pKa values typically ranging between 4.7 and 5.0.

Question 12 of 18

Which of the following acids is naturally found in stinging ants?

Explanation: Formic acid (methanoic acid) is secreted by ants, deriving its name from the Latin word 'formica'.

Question 13 of 18

Reduction of carboxylic acids to primary alcohols is efficiently achieved by using:

Explanation: Lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4) is a strong reducing agent capable of reducing carboxylic acids directly to primary alcohols.

Question 14 of 18

Benzoic acid treated with bromine in the presence of FeBr3 predominantly yields:

Explanation: The carboxyl group is a deactivating meta-directing group in electrophilic aromatic substitution.

Question 15 of 18

What is formed when a carboxylic acid is heated with phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5)?

Explanation: Dehydration of carboxylic acids with powerful dehydrating agents like P2O5 yields acid anhydrides.

Question 16 of 18

Which substituent on benzoic acid increases its acidity the most?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is a strong electron-withdrawing group via both -I and -M effects, greatly increasing acidity.

Question 17 of 18

Hydrolysis of an ester in the presence of an alkali is known as:

Explanation: Base-catalyzed hydrolysis of esters yields carboxylate salts and alcohols, a process termed saponification.

Question 18 of 18

The dimerization of carboxylic acids in benzene solvent can be detected by:

Explanation: Measurement of depression in freezing point (cryoscopy) shows double the expected molecular mass due to hydrogen-bonded dimers.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following acids has the lowest pKa value?

Solution: Formic acid has no electron-donating alkyl group, making it the strongest acid among simple aliphatic carboxylic acids (lowest pKa).

Question 2 of 10
In the esterification of carboxylic acids, the oxygen atom of the eliminated water molecule originates from:

Solution: Isotopic labeling studies confirm that the -OH group is lost from the carboxylic acid, while the -H comes from the alcohol.

Question 3 of 10
Which acid undergoes decarboxylation most readily upon heating?

Solution: Beta-keto acids undergo thermal decarboxylation very easily via a cyclic six-membered transition state.

Question 4 of 10
Reaction of benzoic acid with hydriodic acid and red phosphorus at high temperature yields:

Solution: HI with red phosphorus is a strong reducing agent that reduces carboxylic acids completely to the corresponding hydrocarbons.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following represents the correct order of boiling points?

Solution: Carboxylic acids form stable dimers through stronger hydrogen bonds than alcohols, giving them higher boiling points.

Question 6 of 10
What is the intermediate formed during the conversion of an acid chloride to an amide with ammonia?

Solution: Nucleophilic addition of ammonia to the carbonyl carbon of an acid chloride forms a tetrahedral intermediate before chloride elimination.

Question 7 of 10
Which compound is obtained when calcium acetate is dry distilled?

Solution: Dry distillation of calcium acetate yields acetone along with calcium carbonate.

Question 8 of 10
The electronegativity difference and resonance in the carboxyl group make the carbonyl carbon:

Solution: The withdrawal of electron density by oxygen atoms renders the carbonyl carbon electron-deficient and strongly electrophilic.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following acids does not show optical isomerism?

Solution: Succinic acid (butanedioic acid) lacks a chiral carbon center and therefore does not exhibit optical isomerism.

Question 10 of 10
What is the principal product when phthalic acid is heated strongly with ammonia?

Solution: Phthalic acid reacts with ammonia to form ammonium phthalate, which upon heating loses water to yield phthalimide.
