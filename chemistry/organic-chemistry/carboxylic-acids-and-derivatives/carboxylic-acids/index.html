Organic Chemistry

Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids are organic compounds containing the functional group -COOH, characterized by strong acidity relative to alcohols and phenols due to resonance stabilization of the carboxylate anion. They undergo nucleophilic acyl substitution, decarboxylation, and reduction reactions to form diverse functional derivatives.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Acidity and Electronic Effects

  • Carboxylic acids exhibit higher acidity than alcohols due to resonance stabilization of the resulting carboxylate anion.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups (-I, -M effects) increase acidity by dispersing the negative charge on the carboxylate oxygen atoms.
  • Electron-donating groups (+I, +M effects) decrease acidity by intensifying negative charge density on the carboxylate ion.
  • The acidity order for halogen-substituted acetic acids follows inductive strength: CF3COOH > CCl3COOH > CHCl2COOH > CH2ClCOOH > CH3COOH.

Preparation Methods of Carboxylic Acids

  • Oxidation of primary alcohols and aldehydes using acidic KMnO4 or K2Cr2O7 yields carboxylic acids.
  • Alkylbenzenes undergo vigorous oxidation with alkaline or acidic KMnO4 to yield benzoic acid, regardless of the side chain length.
  • Hydrolysis of nitriles (cyanides) with aqueous acid or base yields carboxylic acids via amide intermediates.
  • Grignard reagents react with carbon dioxide (CO2) followed by acid hydrolysis to yield carboxylic acids containing one more carbon atom.

Chemical Reactions and Properties

  • Reaction with strong reducing agents like LiAlH4 reduces carboxylic acids directly to primary alcohols.
  • Decarboxylation of sodium salts of carboxylic acids with soda lime (NaOH + CaO) yields alkanes with the elimination of Na2CO3.
  • Carboxylic acids react with PCl5, PCl3, or SOCl2 to form corresponding acyl chlorides.
  • Heating dicarboxylic acids leads to cyclic anhydrides (for succinic and glutaric acids) or decarboxylation (for malonic and adipic acids).

Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation
pH = pKa + log([RCOO-] / [RCOOH])
Conditions: Buffer solutions containing carboxylic acid and its salt
Units: Dimensionless
Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) Reaction
RCH2COOH + X2 → RCH(X)COOH + HX
Conditions: Catalytic amounts of red phosphorus and halogen (X2 = Cl2, Br2)
Units: Molar stoichiometry
Esterification Reaction
RCOOH + R'OH ⇄ RCOOR' + H2O
Conditions: Acid catalyst (concentrated H2SO4 or HCl gas)
Units: Molar stoichiometry
Acid Dissociation Constant (Ka)
Ka = [RCOO-][H3O+] / [RCOOH]
Conditions: Dilute aqueous solution at 298 K
Units: Dimensionless (often expressed as pKa)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aliphatic carboxylic acids are weaker acids than mineral acids but stronger than alcohols.

Observation: Formic acid (HCOOH) is stronger than acetic acid and most other simple aliphatic monocarboxylic acids. — The absence of an electron-donating methyl group in formic acid results in less negative charge density accumulation on the carbonyl carbon, facilitating proton release.

Exception Ortho-substituted benzoic acids are generally expected to be less acidic due to steric hindrance inhibiting resonance.

Observation: Most ortho-substituted benzoic acids (except ortho-alkyl substituents) are stronger than benzoic acid itself. — This phenomenon is known as the ortho-effect, combining steric inhibition of resonance, field effects, and intramolecular hydrogen bonding in the anion.

Exception Carboxylic acids do not readily undergo typical carbonyl nucleophilic addition reactions.

Observation: Formic acid acts as both an acid and a reducing agent, reducing Tollens' reagent and failing to form stable tertiary alcohols with excess Grignard reagent without reduction. — The presence of the formyl hydrogen atom (H-C=O) within the carboxylic structure allows oxidation-reduction behavior atypical of higher homologues.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Ortho Effect Rule
o-Substituted Benzoic Acid > Benzoic Acid

Application: Almost every ortho substituent (whether electron-withdrawing or electron-donating, except alkyl groups) increases the acidity of benzoic acid due to the ortho-effect.

Decarboxylation Shortcut
R-COOH + Soda Lime + Heat → R-H + Na2CO3

Application: Use this reaction to step down the carbon chain length by one carbon atom in synthesis problems.

Acidity Trend Mnemonic
F > Cl > Br > I (Inductive Effect)

Application: For halogenated acetic acids, electronegativity dictates acidity: fluorine exerts the strongest -I effect, making fluoroacetic acid the strongest acid among halogen derivatives.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Carboxylic acids form stable dimers in vapor phase and non-polar solvents due to intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
  • Boiling points of carboxylic acids are higher than corresponding alcohols due to extensive association via two hydrogen bonds per dimer unit.
  • Formic acid gives a silver mirror test with Tollens' reagent because it contains an aldehyde structural unit.
  • HVZ reaction specifically alpha-halogenates carboxylic acids containing at least one alpha-hydrogen atom.
  • Kolbe's electrolytic synthesis of sodium or potassium salts of carboxylic acids yields symmetrical alkanes at the anode.
  • Benzene ring in benzoic acid undergoes electrophilic aromatic substitution at the meta-position due to the deactivating -M effect of the -COOH group.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the acidity order of substituted benzoic acids where ortho-isomers are often considered weakest due to steric hindrance, ignoring the ortho-effect.
  • Assuming carboxylic acids react with NaHCO3 to give alcohols instead of generating carbon dioxide gas effervescence.
  • Forgetting that reduction of carboxylic acids with NaBH4 does not occur; LiAlH4 is required.
  • Misidentifying HVZ reaction requirements: failing to include red phosphorus alongside the halogen.

⚡ Quick Revision: Carboxylic Acids

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

The carboxyl carbon is sp2 hybridized with trigonal planar geometry, forming a resonance-stabilized carboxylate ion upon deprotonation.

Acidity Constants

Acid strength is quantified via Ka and pKa values, where electron-withdrawing substituents lower pKa and enhance acidity.

Synthetic Routes

Synthesized via oxidation of primary alcohols and aldehydes, carbonation of Grignard reagents, and hydrolysis of nitriles or esters.

Derivatives and Reactions

Undergoes nucleophilic acyl substitution to form esters, amides, and acid halides, alongside specialized reactions like HVZ and decarboxylation.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Carboxyl Group (-COOH)
Functional group combining carbonyl and hydroxyl groups.
Carboxylate Anion (RCOO-)
Resonance-stabilized anion formed by loss of a proton from a carboxylic acid.
HVZ Reaction (Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky)
Alpha-halogenation of carboxylic acids using halogen and red phosphorus.
Ortho Effect (Steric/Electronic Effect)
Anomalous high acidity of ortho-substituted benzoic acids.
Soda Lime (NaOH + CaO)
Reagent used for decarboxylation of carboxylic acids to form alkanes.
Dimerization ((RCOOH)2)
Association of two acid molecules via hydrogen bonding in non-polar media.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carboxylic acids are stronger acids than phenols and alcohols.
  • Formic acid reduces Tollens' reagent and Fehling's solution.
  • Ortho-substituted benzoic acids are generally stronger acids than benzoic acid.
  • Decarboxylation with soda lime removes the -COOH group as Na2CO3.
  • Esterification is a reversible acid-catalyzed nucleophilic acyl substitution.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carboxylic acids feature a resonance-stabilized carboxylate anion conferring high relative acidity.
  • Preparation methods include oxidation of primary alcohols/aldehydes, nitrile hydrolysis, and Grignard carbonation.
  • Reactions include salt formation, esterification, reduction to primary alcohols, and alpha-halogenation.
  • Substituent electronic effects profoundly influence acid strength following inductive and resonance principles.

⚖️ Key Differences

Phenol Acidity VS Carboxylic Acid Acidity

Carboxylic acids are stronger because their conjugate base anion has negative charge shared equally between two equivalent oxygen atoms, whereas phenoxide anion charge is delocalized onto carbon atoms of the benzene ring.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following compounds is the strongest acid?

Explanation: Trichloroacetic acid (CCl3COOH) is the strongest acid among the choices due to the maximum electron-withdrawing inductive (-I) effect exerted by three chlorine atoms, which stabilizes the resulting carboxylate anion.

Question 2 of 18

Carboxylic acids react with NaHCO3 to liberate which gas?

Explanation: Carboxylic acids are strong enough acids to decompose sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3), liberating carbon dioxide (CO2) gas with brisk effervescence.

Question 3 of 18

The conversion of acetic acid to bromoacetic acid using Br2 and red phosphorus is known as:

Explanation: The Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction involves alpha-halogenation of carboxylic acids having an alpha-hydrogen in the presence of red phosphorus and halogen.

Question 4 of 18

Formic acid differs from acetic acid because formic acid:

Explanation: Formic acid contains an aldehyde group (H-C=O) in its structure, enabling it to act as a reducing agent and reduce Tollens' reagent, unlike acetic acid.

Question 5 of 18

Which reagent reduces a carboxylic acid directly to a primary alcohol?

Explanation: Lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4) is a powerful reducing agent capable of reducing carboxylic acids to primary alcohols, whereas NaBH4 is unreactive toward carboxylic acids.

Question 6 of 18

Heating a mixture of sodium benzoate and soda lime gives:

Explanation: Decarboxylation of sodium benzoate with soda lime (NaOH + CaO) yields benzene and sodium carbonate.

Question 7 of 18

The boiling points of carboxylic acids are higher than alcohols of comparable molecular mass due to:

Explanation: Carboxylic acids form stable cyclic dimers in vapor phase and non-polar solvents via two intermolecular hydrogen bonds, resulting in unusually high boiling points.

Question 8 of 18

Which of the following derivatives of carboxylic acids cannot be prepared directly by treating the acid with SOCl2?

Explanation: Acid anhydrides are typically prepared by dehydrating carboxylic acids with P2O5 or by reacting acid chlorides with carboxylate salts, not directly via SOCl2.

Question 9 of 18

What is the hybridisation of the carboxyl carbon atom in carboxylic acids?

Explanation: The carboxyl carbon atom forms three sigma bonds and one pi bond, adopting trigonal planar geometry with sp2 hybridization.

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following acids is expected to be the weakest?

Explanation: Acetic acid lacks electron-withdrawing halogen substituents. Halogen substituents exert -I effects that stabilize the carboxylate anion, making halogenated acetic acids stronger than acetic acid itself.

Question 11 of 18

Hydrolysis of alkyl cyanides with aqueous acid yields:

Explanation: Complete acid- or base-catalyzed hydrolysis of nitriles (cyanides) yields carboxylic acids through intermediate amides.

Question 12 of 18

In the esterification of carboxylic acids with alcohols, the function of concentrated H2SO4 is to:

Explanation: Concentrated H2SO4 protonates the carbonyl oxygen of the carboxylic acid to enhance electrophilicity and acts as a dehydrating agent to shift equilibrium toward ester formation.

Question 13 of 18

Which substituent on the benzoic acid ring decreases its acidity relative to benzoic acid?

Explanation: The methyl group (-CH3) exerts an electron-donating (+I) effect, intensifying negative charge on the carboxylate ion and decreasing the acidity of toluic acid relative to benzoic acid.

Question 14 of 18

The reaction of a Grignard reagent with solid carbon dioxide (dry ice) followed by acid hydrolysis yields:

Explanation: Grignard reagents react as nucleophiles with electrophilic carbon dioxide to form magnesium carboxylate salts, which upon acidification yield carboxylic acids with one additional carbon atom.

Question 15 of 18

Which dicarboxylic acid on heating undergoes decarboxylation most readily?

Explanation: Oxalic acid (HOOC-COOH) and malonic acid derivatives undergo decarboxylation very readily upon heating because the resulting carbanion-like transition state is stabilized by adjacent electron-withdrawing carbonyl groups.

Question 16 of 18

The correct order of boiling point for ethanol, acetic acid, and acetaldehyde is:

Explanation: Acetic acid forms stronger and more extensive dimeric hydrogen bonds than ethanol, while acetaldehyde lacks hydrogen bonding and has the lowest boiling point.

Question 17 of 18

Esterification of carboxylic acids is an example of:

Explanation: Esterification proceeds via nucleophilic acyl substitution where the alcohol oxygen attacks the carbonyl carbon of the protonated carboxylic acid.

Question 18 of 18

Which of the following compounds does not undergo HVZ reaction?

Explanation: Trimethylacetic acid (2,2-dimethylpropanoic acid) lacks alpha-hydrogen atoms, which are strictly required for the enolization step in the Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky reaction.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary product formed when benzoic acid reacts with bromine in the presence of FeBr3?

Solution: The carboxyl group is deactivating and meta-directing in electrophilic aromatic substitution, yielding 3-bromobenzoic acid as the major product.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following is correct regarding the dimerization of carboxylic acids?

Solution: Carboxylic acids form stable dimers via intermolecular hydrogen bonding in vapor phase and non-polar solvents, raising their boiling points.

Question 3 of 10
What is formed when calcium acetate is heated dry?

Solution: Dry distillation of calcium salts of carboxylic acids (other than calcium formate) yields ketones; calcium acetate yields acetone (propanone).

Question 4 of 10
Which acid is the strongest among the following fluoro-substituted acetic acids?

Solution: Trifluoroacetic acid (CF3COOH) has three strongly electronegative fluorine atoms providing maximal -I stabilization to the conjugate base, making it the strongest acid.

Question 5 of 10
In the reduction of RCOOH to RCH2OH using LiAlH4, what is the intermediate species?

Solution: Reduction of carboxylic acids proceeds through an aldehyde intermediate, which is rapidly reduced further to the primary alcohol by LiAlH4.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following represents the correct order of acid strength?

Solution: Acid strength decreases with the lengthening and increasing +I effect of alkyl chains attached to the carboxyl group: Formic > Acetic > Propanoic.

Question 7 of 10
Reaction of benzoic acid with hydriodic acid (HI) in the presence of red phosphorus at high temperature yields:

Solution: Reduction of benzoic acid with HI and red phosphorus completely reduces the carboxyl group to a methyl group, yielding toluene.

Question 8 of 10
Which functional derivative of carboxylic acids is most reactive toward nucleophilic acyl substitution?

Solution: Acyl chlorides are the most reactive carboxylic acid derivatives because the chlorine atom is a weak base and excellent leaving group.

Question 9 of 10
Ammonium acetate when heated strongly produces:

Solution: Thermal dehydration of ammonium acetate yields acetamide (CH3CONH2) and water.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following compounds will not give a positive test with NaHCO3?

Solution: Phenol is a weaker acid than carbonic acid (H2CO3) and cannot liberate CO2 from NaHCO3, whereas carboxylic acids and picric acid are sufficiently strong.
