Physical Properties of Carboxylic Acids and Derivatives

Carboxylic acids exhibit exceptionally high boiling points compared to aldehydes, ketones, and alcohols of comparable molecular mass due to strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding forming stable cyclic dimers. Solubility in water decreases progressively with the increase in the length of the hydrophobic alkyl chain.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Intermolecular Hydrogen Bonding and Dimerization

  • Carboxylic acids exist predominantly as cyclic dimers in the vapor phase and in non-polar solvents due to strong two-point hydrogen bonding.
  • The apparent molecular mass determined colligatively in non-polar solvents is nearly double the actual formula mass.
  • Polar carbonyl (C=O) and hydroxyl (O–H) groups impart high polarity and substantial dipole moments to the carboxyl function.
  • Higher boiling points of carboxylic acids relative to corresponding alcohols are a direct consequence of more effective intermolecular association.

Solubility Trends in Polar and Non-Polar Media

  • Lower aliphatic carboxylic acids (methanoic to butanoic acid) are completely miscible with water due to extensive hydrogen bonding with water molecules.
  • Solubility in water drops sharply from pentanoic acid onwards as the hydrophobic alkyl chain dominates the molecular surface area.
  • Aromatic carboxylic acids such as benzoic acid are nearly insoluble in cold water but dissolve readily in hot water and alkaline solutions.
  • Carboxylic acids are readily soluble in less polar organic solvents such as ether, benzene, and chloroform.

Physical States and Melting Point Alternation

  • The first three members (formic, acetic, and propanoic acids) are pungent-smelling, colorless liquids at room temperature.
  • Aliphatic acids with ten or more carbon atoms are wax-like solids with very low volatility.
  • Dicarboxylic acids and aliphatic acids exhibit melting point alternation (even-odd effect) owing to differences in crystal packing efficiency.
  • Even-carbon acids pack more densely in the crystal lattice, leading to higher lattice energies and melting points than their odd-carbon neighbors.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Boiling points increase strictly with molecular weight across all homologous series.

Observation: Formic acid (HCOOH) and acetic acid (CH3COOH) boil at unexpectedly high temperatures relative to hydrocarbons of similar mass. — Formation of remarkably stable, persistent cyclic hydrogen-bonded dimers in the liquid and vapor phases.

Exception Solubility in water decreases uniformly with increasing molecular mass.

Observation: Branched-chain isomers of carboxylic acids show higher water solubility than their straight-chain structural isomers. — Branching decreases the effective hydrophobic surface area, allowing more effective hydration of the polar carboxyl group.

Exception Melting points increase monotonically with chain length in homologous series.

Observation: Carboxylic acids with an even number of carbon atoms melt at higher temperatures than the immediately succeeding odd-carbon acid. — Differential orientation of terminal carboxyl groups in even versus odd carbon chains yields tighter crystal packing.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Carboxylic acids form exceptionally stable cyclic dimers in vapor phase, doubling their apparent molecular weight.
  • Lower carboxylic acids (C1–C4) are miscible with water due to intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
  • Formic acid possesses both a carbonyl and a formyl-like hydrogen, reducing its melting point compared to acetic acid.
  • Melting points of straight-chain monocarboxylic acids alternate between even and odd carbon numbers due to crystal packing.
  • Boiling points of carboxylic acids are higher than alcohols of comparable molecular mass due to extensive two-point hydrogen bonding.
  • Solubility in water decreases rapidly as the hydrophobic alkyl chain length exceeds four carbon atoms.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming carboxylic acids exist as single monomers in non-polar solvents like benzene.
  • Confusing the boiling point order of carboxylic acids with alcohols of similar molecular weight.
  • Overlooking the even-odd alternation phenomenon when comparing melting points of higher fatty acids.
  • Treating aromatic carboxylic acids as water-soluble at room temperature.

⚡ Quick Revision: Physical Properties of Carboxylic Acids and Derivatives

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Hydrogen Bonding and Dimerization

Carboxylic acids exhibit strong intermolecular association through dual hydrogen bonds, forming cyclic dimeric structures that elevate boiling points.

Solubility Behavior

Lower homologues dissolve completely in water via hydrogen bond formation, while higher homologues form immiscible oily layers due to hydrophobic alkyl chains.

Physical States and Melting Trends

Lower members are pungent liquids, intermediate ones are oils, and higher members are odorless solids displaying crystal lattice alternation.

Volatility and Vapor Phase Properties

Vapor phase dissociation of cyclic dimers occurs at elevated temperatures, resulting in vapor density anomalies inconsistent with simple monomer formulas.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Dimerization (2 RCOOH → (RCOOH)2)
Association of two monomer units via dual hydrogen bonds.
Partition Coefficient (log P)
Logarithmic ratio of solute concentrations in immiscible solvents.
Dipole Moment (μ)
Measure of net molecular polarity measured in Debye units.
Melting Alternation (Even-Odd Effect)
Fluctuation in melting points due to crystal lattice packing differences.
Hydrophobic Effect (Non-polar repulsion)
Tendency of non-polar hydrocarbon chains to exclude water molecules.
Miscibility (Infinite solubility)
Ability of liquids to dissolve completely in each other in all proportions.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carboxylic acids form cyclic dimers in benzene, doubling apparent molecular mass.
  • Boiling point sequence: Acid > Alcohol > Ketone > Aldehyde > Alkane.
  • Water solubility limit for monocarboxylic acids is four carbon atoms.
  • Even-carbon fatty acids melt higher than adjacent odd-carbon acids.
  • Formic acid and acetic acid are pungent liquids miscible with water.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Strong hydrogen bonding dictates the high boiling points of carboxylic acids.
  • Hydrophobic alkyl chain length controls water solubility in homologous series.
  • Vapor density measurements in non-polar solvents confirm dimerization.
  • Crystal packing explains the even-odd alternation in melting points.

⚖️ Key Differences

Alcohol Hydrogen Bonding VS Carboxylic Acid Hydrogen Bonding

Alcohols form open linear chains, whereas carboxylic acids form closed cyclic dimers involving two hydrogen bonds per pair.

Question 1 of 20

Which of the following has the highest boiling point?

Explanation: Acetic acid forms stable cyclic dimers through strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding, resulting in a higher boiling point than alcohols, ketones, or alkanes of comparable molecular weight.

Question 2 of 20

The apparent molecular mass of acetic acid in benzene solution as determined by freezing point depression is approximately:

Explanation: In non-polar solvents like benzene, acetic acid dimerizes completely, doubling the apparent molecular mass from 60 to approximately 120 g/mol.

Question 3 of 20

Which carboxylic acid is completely miscible with water?

Explanation: Butanoic acid is soluble (miscible to a large extent, with lower members like formic, acetic, and propanoic being completely miscible), whereas higher acids have very low solubility.

Question 4 of 20

The phenomenon of alternation in melting points of fatty acids is due to:

Explanation: Even-carbon fatty acids pack more efficiently in the crystal lattice, resulting in higher lattice energy and melting points compared to odd-carbon acids.

Question 5 of 20

What is the physical state of straight-chain monocarboxylic acids containing more than ten carbon atoms at room temperature?

Explanation: Higher straight-chain aliphatic carboxylic acids (C10 and above) are wax-like solids due to increased van der Waals dispersion forces.

Question 6 of 20

Why do carboxylic acids have higher boiling points than alcohols of comparable molecular mass?

Explanation: The cyclic dimer of carboxylic acids involves two simultaneous hydrogen bonds, requiring more thermal energy to break than the single hydrogen bonds in alcohols.

Question 7 of 20

Which solvent dissolves benzoic acid most effectively at room temperature?

Explanation: Benzoic acid reacts with sodium bicarbonate to form water-soluble sodium benzoate, dissolving readily despite low solubility in neutral cold water.

Question 8 of 20

The dipole moment of aliphatic carboxylic acids is primarily due to:

Explanation: The electronegativity difference in the polar carbonyl (C=O) and hydroxyl (O–H) groups generates a substantial net dipole moment.

Question 9 of 20

Which factor decreases the water solubility of carboxylic acids?

Explanation: Increasing the length of the hydrophobic alkyl chain decreases water solubility by increasing non-polar character.

Question 10 of 20

In the vapor phase at low pressure, acetic acid exists primarily as:

Explanation: Hydrogen-bonded cyclic dimers persist even in the vapor phase at moderate temperatures and pressures.

Question 11 of 20

Formic acid differs from higher carboxylic acids in that it:

Explanation: Formic acid (HCOOH) features a formyl proton attached directly to the carbonyl carbon, giving it reducing properties alongside acid characteristics.

Question 12 of 20

What is the trend in boiling point across the straight-chain homologous series of carboxylic acids?

Explanation: Boiling points increase smoothly with increasing molecular mass due to increasing van der Waals dispersion forces.

Question 13 of 20

Which of the following acids has the lowest boiling point?

Explanation: Formic acid has the lowest molecular mass and smallest hydrocarbon chain in the series, yielding the lowest boiling point among straight-chain aliphatic acids.

Question 14 of 20

Solubility of benzoic acid in water increases upon heating because:

Explanation: Dissolution of most solid organic acids in water is endothermic, so increasing temperature enhances solubility.

Question 15 of 20

Which functional group derivative of carboxylic acids has the lowest boiling point compared to the parent acid?

Explanation: Carboxylic acid derivatives lack the extensive two-point cyclic hydrogen bonding characteristic of parent carboxylic acids, resulting in lower boiling points.

Question 16 of 20

The pungent smell characteristic of lower carboxylic acids is most pronounced in:

Explanation: Lower volatile carboxylic acids such as acetic, propanoic, and butyric acids possess strong, pungent, or rancid odors.

Question 17 of 20

Why are dicarboxylic acids generally less soluble in water than monocarboxylic acids of similar carbon count?

Explanation: Dicarboxylic acids possess higher crystal lattice energies due to symmetrical dual hydrogen bonding networks in the solid state, lowering water solubility.

Question 18 of 20

Which compound does not form hydrogen bonds?

Explanation: Esters like methyl acetate lack a hydrogen atom bonded to an electronegative oxygen atom, preventing self-association via hydrogen bonding.

Question 19 of 20

The boiling point of acetic acid is higher than that of ethanol despite similar molecular mass because:

Explanation: Two hydrogen bonds per dimer in acetic acid provide stronger intermolecular attraction than the single hydrogen bonds in ethanol.

Question 20 of 20

What happens to the density of straight-chain aliphatic carboxylic acids as the carbon chain lengthens?

Explanation: Density approaches a limiting value of approximately 1.0 g/cm3 as the contribution of the heavy carboxyl group becomes diluted by the polymethylene chain.

Question 1 of 10
Which carboxylic acid exhibits the highest solubility in water?

Solution: Propanoic acid has a shorter hydrophobic chain than butanoic, pentanoic, or hexanoic acids, resulting in higher water solubility.

Question 2 of 10
The alternating melting points observed in dicarboxylic acids are attributed to:

Solution: Even-numbered carbon chains pack more compactly in the crystal lattice than odd-numbered chains, causing higher melting points.

Question 3 of 10
In the absence of hydrogen bonding, the boiling point of acetic acid would be closest to that of:

Solution: Without hydrogen bonding, intermolecular forces would rely solely on van der Waals interactions, comparable to hydrocarbons of similar mass such as propane.

Question 4 of 10
What is the primary reason for the pungent odor of lower aliphatic carboxylic acids?

Solution: Low molecular weight acids have sufficient vapor pressure at room temperature to reach olfactory receptors, producing a pungent smell.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following compounds has the lowest vapor pressure at 25°C?

Solution: Decanoic acid has the highest molecular mass and strongest dispersion forces, yielding the lowest volatility and lowest vapor pressure.

Question 6 of 10
Why does branching in the alkyl chain of a carboxylic acid increase its solubility in water relative to its straight-chain isomer?

Solution: Branching makes the molecule more compact, reducing the hydrophobic surface area exposed to water molecules.

Question 7 of 10
The boiling point of formic acid is 100.5°C, which is close to water because:

Solution: Formic acid forms extensive intermolecular hydrogen-bonded chains and dimers, giving it a boiling point very close to water.

Question 8 of 10
When benzoic acid is dissolved in a non-polar solvent, colligative property measurements indicate a van 't Hoff factor (i) close to:

Solution: Since two molecules associate into one dimer, the number of effective particles in solution is halved, making the van 't Hoff factor i ≈ 0.5.

Question 9 of 10
Which solvent is most suitable for recrystallizing benzoic acid?

Solution: Benzoic acid is sparingly soluble in cold water but dissolves readily in hot water, making hot water ideal for recrystallization upon cooling.

Question 10 of 10
The physical state of methanoic acid at room temperature is:

Solution: Methanoic acid (formic acid) is a pungent, colorless liquid boiling at 100.5°C.
