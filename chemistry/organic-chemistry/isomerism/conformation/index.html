Organic Chemistry

Conformation

Conformations are the various spatial arrangements of atoms that result from rotation about single covalent bonds. These distinct non-superimposable orientations interconvert rapidly at room temperature via thermal energy.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Acyclic Systems and Ethane Conformations

  • Ethane exhibits infinite conformations represented via Sawhorse and Newman projections.
  • The staggered conformation (dihedral angle 60°) is the energy minimum due to maximum separation of C-H bonds.
  • The eclipsed conformation (dihedral angle 0°) corresponds to an energy maximum with a torsional barrier of approximately 12.0 kJ/mol.
  • Propane features a slightly higher torsional barrier in its eclipsed form (14.0 kJ/mol) due to steric repulsion of the methyl group.

Butane Stereochemistry and Steric Strain

  • Butane has distinct staggered conformations: anti (θ = 180°) and gauche (θ = 60°).
  • The anti conformation is the most stable state due to minimal steric hindrance between methyl groups.
  • The gauche conformation is destabilized by van der Waals repulsion between methyl groups, creating a 3.8 kJ/mol energy penalty.
  • Fully eclipsed (syn-periplanar) conformation represents the highest energy state for butane due to simultaneous methyl-methyl and eclipsing interactions.

Cycloalkane Conformations and Ring Strain

  • Cyclohexane adopts a chair conformation which is virtually free of both angle strain and torsional strain.
  • Axial bonds are oriented parallel to the C3 axis, while equatorial bonds project outward along the periphery.
  • Ring inversion (chair-chair flip) converts all axial positions to equatorial and vice versa.
  • Monosubstituted cyclohexanes strongly favor the equatorial position to minimize 1,3-diaxial steric interactions.

Conditions: At constant temperature and pressure
Units: ΔG° in kJ/mol, Keq is dimensionless

Keq = [Conformer B] / [Conformer A]
Conditions: Applicable for equilibrium between two states
Units: Concentrations in mol/L
Torsional Energy Barrier
ΔE = V0(1 + cos(nθ)) / 2
Conditions: Rotational barrier for alkanes
Units: ΔE in kJ/mol, θ is dihedral angle
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Gauche conformation is always less stable than anti in butane.

Observation: In molecules like ethylene glycol (ethane-1,2-diol), the gauche conformation is more stable than the anti conformation. — Formation of an intramolecular hydrogen bond between the two adjacent hydroxyl groups stabilizes the gauche form.

Exception Equatorial substituents are always preferred in monosubstituted cyclohexanes.

Observation: Fluorocyclohexane or hydroxycyclohexane in specific non-polar solvents can show reduced conformational preference or reversal. — Dipole-dipole interactions and solvent polarity effects can occasionally offset pure steric bulk considerations.

Exception Planar rings have zero strain according to Baeyer strain theory.

Observation: Cyclopropane and cyclobutane deviate significantly from planar geometries or possess inherent strain. — Cyclopropane adopts a bent-bond (banana bond) structure to minimize angle strain, while cyclobutane adopts a puckered conformation.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Newman Projection Conversion
Front carbon = point (3 lines), Back carbon = circle (3 lines touching periphery)

Application: Quickly identify dihedral angles by sighting down the C-C bond axis in exam questions.

Chair Flip Rule
Up stays Up, Down stays Down; Axial becomes Equatorial and vice-versa

Application: Instantly draw flipped cyclohexane rings without re-calculating stereocenters.

A-Value Stability Ranking
Higher A-value = Stronger preference for equatorial position

Application: Rank stability of substituted cyclohexanes directly using standard thermodynamic A-values.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The rotational barrier of ethane is 12.0 kJ/mol and is purely torsional in origin.
  • Cyclohexane chair conformation has zero angle strain and zero torsional strain.
  • Gauche butane is higher in energy than anti butane by 3.8 kJ/mol due to steric strain.
  • 1,3-Diaxial interactions in cyclohexane destabilize axial substituents.
  • The twist-boat conformation is an energy minimum on the cyclohexane potential energy surface, lower than the transition state boat form.
  • cis-1,4-Di-tert-butylcyclohexane adopts a twist-boat conformation to avoid extreme steric crowding.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing configuration with conformation; remember conformations interconvert without breaking bonds.
  • Assuming boat conformation is a transition state; it is an unstable local maximum, while twist-boat is a local minimum.
  • Forgetting that equatorial substituents can still experience steric crowding if bulky groups are adjacent.
  • Inverting the 'up/down' configuration of substituents during a chair flip.

⚡ Quick Revision: Conformation

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Torsional Strain

Strain resulting from eclipsing of bonds on adjacent atoms, overcome by bond rotation.

Steric Strain

Repulsive interactions between non-bonded atoms forced closer than their van der Waals radii.

Angle Strain

Strain resulting from bond angles deviating from the ideal tetrahedral angle of 109.5°.

Conformational Isomerism

Stereoisomers interconvertible by rotation about single bonds, obeying Boltzmann distribution.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Conformation (Spatial arrangement)
Non-superimposable orientations generated by single bond rotation.
Dihedral Angle (Torsion angle (θ))
Angle between two intersecting planes defined by three bonds.
Staggered (Energy minimum)
Conformation where bonds on adjacent carbons bisect each other.
Eclipsed (Energy maximum)
Conformation where bonds on adjacent carbons directly overlap.
A-Value (Conformational free energy)
Free energy difference between equatorial and axial monosubstituted cyclohexanes.
Flagpole Interaction (Transannular strain)
Steric repulsion between C1 and C4 hydrogens in boat cyclohexane.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ethane barriers: 12.0 kJ/mol (eclipsed vs staggered)
  • Butane anti-gauche energy difference: 3.8 kJ/mol
  • Cyclohexane chair conformation bond angles: 109.5°
  • Boat cyclohexane energy penalty relative to chair: ~29 kJ/mol
  • Equatorial position preference is dictated by A-values

📌 Quick Recall

  • Conformations interconvert rapidly at room temperature.
  • Staggered forms are lower in energy than eclipsed forms.
  • Cyclohexane chair conformation provides the most strain-free geometry.
  • Substituents on cyclohexane strongly favor equatorial orientations.

⚖️ Key Differences

Anti Conformation VS Gauche Conformation

Anti has dihedral angle of 180°; gauche has dihedral angle of 60°.

Question 1 of 19

What is the dihedral angle between the methyl groups in the anti conformation of butane?

Explanation: In the anti conformation, the two bulky methyl groups are positioned at opposite sides with a dihedral angle of 180°.

Question 2 of 19

Which conformation of ethane possesses maximum torsional strain?

Explanation: The eclipsed conformation has maximum torsional strain because the C-H bonds directly overlap.

Question 3 of 19

What is the primary source of ring strain in cyclopropane?

Explanation: Cyclopropane has a bond angle of 60°, drastically deviating from the tetrahedral angle of 109.5°, causing severe angle strain.

Question 4 of 19

Which cyclohexane conformation represents the absolute global energy minimum?

Explanation: The chair conformation is completely free of angle and torsional strain, making it the most stable energy minimum.

Question 5 of 19

In monosubstituted cyclohexane, bulky groups predominantly occupy which position?

Explanation: Equatorial positions minimize 1,3-diaxial steric interactions, making them thermodynamically favored for bulky substituents.

Question 6 of 19

What is the energy difference between the chair and boat conformations of cyclohexane approximately?

Explanation: The boat conformation is destabilized by flagpole interactions and eclipsing strain, resting about 29 kJ/mol higher in energy than the chair.

Question 7 of 19

Which factor stabilizes the gauche conformation of ethylene glycol?

Explanation: Intramolecular hydrogen bonding between the two adjacent hydroxyl groups stabilizes the gauche conformer.

Question 8 of 19

How many total staggered conformations exist for butane when rotating 360 degrees?

Explanation: Butane has three staggered conformations: one anti and two gauche forms.

Question 9 of 19

What happens to an axial substituent during a chair-chair ring inversion?

Explanation: During chair-chair interconversion, all axial bonds become equatorial and all equatorial bonds become axial.

Question 10 of 19

Which of the following has the highest torsional barrier?

Explanation: Butane has larger methyl-methyl and methyl-hydrogen eclipsing interactions, giving it a higher rotational barrier than ethane or propane.

Question 11 of 19

What type of strain is predominantly present in planar cyclobutane?

Explanation: Planar cyclobutane suffers from significant angle strain (90° vs 109.5°) and complete eclipsing torsional strain.

Question 12 of 19

What is the dihedral angle in the eclipsed conformation of ethane?

Explanation: The eclipsed conformation occurs when the C-H bonds on adjacent carbons align, giving a dihedral angle of 0°.

Question 13 of 19

Which conformer of cyclohexane possesses C2 symmetry?

Explanation: The twist-boat conformation lacks a plane of symmetry and possesses C2 symmetry.

Question 14 of 19

What is the value of the torsional barrier in ethane?

Explanation: Experimental and quantum chemical calculations place the torsional barrier of ethane at approximately 12.0 kJ/mol.

Question 15 of 19

In cis-1,3-dimethylcyclohexane, the most stable chair conformation has:

Explanation: cis-1,3-Dimethylcyclohexane places both methyl groups in equatorial positions (both pointing 'up'), giving a trans-diaxial-free stable chair.

Question 16 of 19

What term describes the non-bonded interactions between substituents on C1 and C3 in a cyclohexane ring?

Explanation: Axial substituents at C1 and C3 experience steric crowding known as 1,3-diaxial interaction.

Question 17 of 19

Which conformation of butane corresponds to the highest energy point on the potential energy profile?

Explanation: When the two methyl groups are directly eclipsing each other (syn-periplanar), the steric and torsional repulsion creates the absolute energy maximum.

Question 18 of 19

Why is cyclooctane conformationally complex?

Explanation: Medium rings like cyclooctane exhibit crown and boat-chair conformations with low barriers to interconversion.

Question 19 of 19

What is the conformational preference of trans-1,4-di-tert-butylcyclohexane?

Explanation: To avoid extreme steric hindrance, both bulky tert-butyl groups occupy equatorial positions in a diequatorial chair conformation.

Which conformer of cyclohexane has the highest potential energy?

Solution: The half-chair conformation is the highest energy transition state during chair-boat interconversion.

Question 2 of 10
What is the rotational barrier around the C-C bond in ethane in kcal/mol?

Solution: The torsional barrier of ethane is approximately 12.0 kJ/mol, which is equivalent to 2.9 kcal/mol.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following molecules has a stable gauche conformation due to hydrogen bonding?

Solution: Ethylene glycol forms an intramolecular hydrogen bond in its gauche conformation, making it more stable than the anti form.

Question 4 of 10
In cyclohexane, what is the geometric relationship between an axial bond and the adjacent equatorial bond on the same carbon?

Solution: Axial bonds are parallel to the ring axis, while equatorial bonds project outward, intersecting at approximately 90° relative to the axial vector.

Question 5 of 10
What is the primary factor responsible for the energy barrier in propane rotation?

Solution: In propane, eclipsing the bulky methyl group with hydrogen introduces both torsional and steric strain.

Question 6 of 10
Which conformer of butane is higher in energy than the gauche conformer but lower than the fully eclipsed conformer?

Solution: Partially eclipsed (syn-clinal) has methyl-hydrogen eclipsing interactions, placing it higher than gauche but lower than fully eclipsed methyl-methyl interactions.

Question 7 of 10
What is the predominant conformation of trans-1,2-dimethylcyclohexane in the gas phase?

Solution: trans-1,2-Dimethylcyclohexane prefers the diequatorial chair conformation to minimize 1,3-diaxial interactions.

Question 8 of 10
How many conformational isomers of cyclohexane are considered energy minima?

Solution: Chair and twist-boat are the two stable conformers that represent local energy minima on the potential energy surface.

Question 9 of 10
What is the approximate free energy difference (ΔG°) for methylcyclohexane chair inversion favoring the equatorial methyl?

Solution: The A-value for a methyl group on cyclohexane is approximately 7.3 kJ/mol (1.74 kcal/mol).

Question 10 of 10
Which cyclic system is considered virtually free of all ring strain?

Solution: Cyclohexane in the chair conformation has negligible angle strain and staggered torsional bonds, resulting in zero net ring strain.
