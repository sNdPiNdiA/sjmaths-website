Geometrical Isomerism

Geometrical isomerism is a form of stereoisomerism arising from restricted rotation about carbon-carbon double bonds, cyclic structures, or carbon-nitrogen double bonds. It results in differing spatial arrangements of substituents, designated as cis-trans or E-Z configurations.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Stereochemical Basis and Restricted Rotation

  • Geometrical isomerism arises due to the presence of a restricted rotation axis, typically a π-bond in alkenes or a C-C single bond in cycloalkanes.
  • The π-bond prevents free rotation because twisting requires breaking or significantly weakening the lateral overlap of p-orbitals.
  • To exhibit geometrical isomerism, each doubly bonded carbon atom must be attached to two different groups (a ≠ b and c ≠ d).

Cis-Trans and E-Z Nomenclature Systems

  • The cis isomer places identical or high-priority groups on the same side of the reference plane, while the trans isomer places them on opposite sides.
  • The E-Z system (Entgegen-Zusammen) based on Cahn-Ingold-Prelog (CIP) priority rules must be used when all four substituents on the double bond are different.
  • Priority is assigned based on atomic number (higher atomic number gets higher priority); 'Z' denotes higher priority groups on the same side, and 'E' on opposite sides.

Physicochemical Properties of Isomers

  • Cis-isomers generally possess a higher dipole moment due to vector addition of bond dipoles, whereas symmetrical trans-isomers often have a net dipole moment of zero.
  • Trans-isomers exhibit higher melting points and crystal lattice stability due to better packing efficiency in the solid state.
  • Cis-isomers typically have lower boiling points and higher solubility in polar solvents compared to their trans counterparts.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Number of Geometrical Isomers (Symmetrical Polyene, n is even)
N = 2n-1 + 2(n/2)-1
Conditions: Symmetrical polyenes with an even number (n) of double bonds
Units: N = Total number of geometrical isomers (dimensionless)
Number of Geometrical Isomers (Symmetrical Polyene, n is odd)
N = 2n-1 + 2(n-1)/2
Conditions: Symmetrical polyenes with an odd number (n) of double bonds
Units: N = Total number of geometrical isomers (dimensionless)
Dipole Moment Calculation
μ = q × d
Conditions: Applied to calculate the net dipole moment of cis and trans isomers
Units: μ in Debye (D), q in Coulomb, d in meters
Number of Geometrical Isomers (Unsymmetrical Polyene)
N = 2n
Conditions: Compounds with n distinct double bonds and unsymmetrical ends
Units: N = Total number of geometrical isomers (dimensionless)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkenes with two different substituents on each carbon always show cis-trans isomerism.

Observation: Cycloalkenes with ring sizes smaller than eight carbons (e.g., cyclohexene) cannot accommodate a trans double bond. — Ring strain and geometric constraints make trans-cycloalkenes too unstable or geometrically impossible to exist at room temperature.

Exception Trans isomers always have lower dipole moments than cis isomers.

Observation: Unsymmetrical 1,2-disubstituted alkenes where electronegative substituents have specific orientations can show non-zero dipole moments for trans forms. — Vector cancellation is incomplete when substituents are structurally non-identical or possess large localized group moments.

Exception Compounds with restricted rotation require carbon-carbon double bonds.

Observation: Oximes (C=N-OH), diazo compounds (-N=N-), and alicyclic rings also exhibit geometrical isomerism. — The lone pair on nitrogen or the rigid cyclic framework similarly restricts rotational freedom around the bond axis.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
CIP Priority Mnemonic
Atomic Number → Mass Number → Lone Pair

Application: Always determine priority by highest atomic number first; if atoms are identical, move down the chain to the next atom with the highest atomic number.

Dipole Moment Shortcut
μcis > μtrans (for symmetric 1,2-disubstituted alkenes)

Application: Quickly identify polarity questions by checking symmetry; trans-1,2-dichloroethene has μ = 0 D.

Isomer Count Formula Selector
Unsymmetric: 2n | Symmetric (even n): 2n-1 + 2(n/2)-1

Application: Check if the terminal groups of the polyene are identical before selecting the formula for calculating total geometrical isomers.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Restricted rotation around the C=C bond is essential for geometrical isomerism.
  • Cycloalkanes like 1,2-dimethylcyclopropane show cis-trans isomerism due to restricted ring rotation.
  • Fumaric acid is the trans isomer of maleic acid and has a higher melting point.
  • Maleic acid readily forms a cyclic anhydride on heating, whereas fumaric acid does not.
  • Oximes show geometrical isomerism where syn and anti terms replace cis and trans.
  • E-Z nomenclature relies strictly on CIP priority rules assigned to substituents.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming all alkenes with two substituents show geometrical isomerism (both substituents on the same carbon must be different).
  • Confusing optical isomerism with geometrical isomerism; geometrical isomers are achiral unless an alternative chiral center is present.
  • Forgetting that cycloalkanes with two substituents on the same carbon atom cannot show cis-trans isomerism.
  • Applying cis-trans nomenclature to systems with three identical groups on the double bond carbons.

⚡ Quick Revision: Geometrical Isomerism

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Restricted Rotation

Geometrical isomerism is restricted to systems where rotation about a bond axis is prevented by pi-bonding or cyclic constraints.

CIP Priority Rules

Assignment of E and Z descriptors requires rigorous atomic number ranking of groups attached to each doubly-bonded carbon.

Physical Properties

Cis isomers exhibit higher dipole moments and boiling points, while trans isomers exhibit higher melting points and stability.

Non-Alkenyl Systems

Cycloalkanes, oximes, and azo compounds also display geometrical isomerism following similar spatial hindrance rules.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Cis (Same side)
Configuration with similar groups on the same side of the reference plane.
Trans (Opposite side)
Configuration with similar groups on opposite sides of the reference plane.
Entgegen ((E))
Descriptor indicating higher priority groups on opposite sides.
Zusammen ((Z))
Descriptor indicating higher priority groups on the same side.
Maleic Acid (cis-Butenedioic acid)
Cis isomer that forms an anhydride on heating.
Fumaric Acid (trans-Butenedioic acid)
Trans isomer with higher thermal stability and melting point.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • N = 2n for unsymmetrical polyenes with n double bonds
  • Cis isomers have higher dipole moments than trans isomers
  • Trans isomers have higher melting points due to crystal lattice packing
  • Restricted rotation is mandatory for geometrical isomerism
  • CIP rules govern E-Z nomenclature

📌 Quick Recall

  • Geometrical isomers are stereoisomers that are not mirror images.
  • Carbon-carbon double bonds and cycloalkane rings restrict rotation.
  • CIP priority rules determine E and Z designations.
  • Physical and chemical properties differ significantly between cis and trans pairs.

⚖️ Key Differences

Cis-trans nomenclature VS E-Z nomenclature

Cis-trans is used for simple disubstituted alkenes, while E-Z is universal and based on CIP priority rules.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following compounds will show geometrical isomerism?

Explanation: 2-butene has two different groups attached to each of the doubly bonded carbon atoms (H and CH3), fulfilling the requirement for geometrical isomerism.

Question 2 of 18

The total number of geometrical isomers for 2,4-hexadiene is:

Explanation: 2,4-hexadiene is a symmetrical diene with n=2. Using the formula for a symmetrical polyene with even n: N = 2^(2-1) + 2^((2/2)-1) = 2^1 + 2^0 = 2 + 1 = 3.

Question 3 of 18

Which isomer of 1,2-dichloroethene has a zero dipole moment?

Explanation: In trans-1,2-dichloroethene, the C-Cl bond dipoles are equal and point in exactly opposite directions, cancelling each other out completely.

Question 4 of 18

Maleic acid and fumaric acid are:

Explanation: Maleic acid is the cis-isomer and fumaric acid is the trans-isomer of butenedioic acid, making them geometrical isomers.

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following has the highest melting point?

Explanation: Trans-2-butene has a symmetrical structure that packs more efficiently into a crystal lattice, resulting in a higher melting point than the cis isomer.

Question 6 of 18

What is the E/Z designation for an alkene where the higher priority groups are on the same side?

Explanation: Z (Zusammen) indicates that the groups of higher priority are located on the same side of the double bond.

Question 7 of 18

Cycloalkenes with ring size less than how many carbon atoms are unstable in the trans-configuration?

Explanation: Trans-cycloalkenes are strained and unstable for ring sizes smaller than eight carbons due to severe ring geometry constraints.

Question 8 of 18

Which of the following compounds exhibits geometrical isomerism?

Explanation: 1,2-dichloroethene has different groups on each of the doubly bonded carbons, allowing for cis and trans configurations.

Question 9 of 18

According to CIP rules, which group has the highest priority?

Explanation: Chlorine (-Cl) has the highest atomic number (17) among the given options, giving it top priority.

Question 10 of 18

Why does maleic acid readily form an anhydride on heating while fumaric acid does not?

Explanation: In maleic acid, the two -COOH groups are close to each other on the same side (cis), facilitating intramolecular dehydration to form a cyclic anhydride.

Question 11 of 18

The restricted rotation causing geometrical isomerism in alkenes is due to:

Explanation: The pi bond formed by lateral overlap of p-orbitals prevents free rotation about the carbon-carbon double bond axis.

Question 12 of 18

Which property is typically greater in a cis-isomer compared to its trans-isomer?

Explanation: Cis-isomers have a vector sum of bond dipoles greater than zero, resulting in a higher dipole moment.

Question 13 of 18

How many geometrical isomers are possible for an unsymmetrical alkene with 3 distinct double bonds (n=3)?

Explanation: For an unsymmetrical polyene with n distinct double bonds, the number of geometrical isomers is 2^n = 2^3 = 8.

Question 14 of 18

Which of the following nitrogen-containing compounds shows geometrical isomerism?

Explanation: Oximes (R1R2C=N-OH) exhibit syn-anti geometrical isomerism due to the restricted rotation and the lone pair on the nitrogen atom.

Question 15 of 18

If two groups on the same carbon of an alkene are identical, the number of geometrical isomers is:

Explanation: If a carbon atom in a double bond is attached to two identical groups, swapping them does not produce a new isomer, so geometrical isomerism is impossible.

Question 16 of 18

Which priority order is correct according to CIP rules for halogen atoms?

Explanation: Priority increases with atomic number: Iodine (53) > Bromine (35) > Chlorine (17) > Fluorine (9).

Question 17 of 18

Which of the following cycloalkanes can show geometrical isomerism?

Explanation: 1,2-dimethylcyclopropane has different substituents on adjacent ring carbons, existing in cis and trans forms.

Question 18 of 18

What is the configuration of an alkene where high-priority groups are on opposite sides?

Explanation: E (Entgegen) signifies that the groups of higher priority are on opposite sides of the double bond.

Question 2 of 10
Which statement is correct regarding cis and trans isomers?

Solution: Trans isomers are more stable due to reduced steric hindrance between bulky groups located on opposite sides of the double bond.

Question 3 of 10
Oximes exhibit geometrical isomerism due to:

Solution: Oximes contain a carbon-nitrogen double bond and a lone pair on the nitrogen atom, which restricts rotation and creates syn/anti isomers.

Question 4 of 10

Solution: 1-bromo-2-chloroethene has four different substituents attached to the doubly bonded carbons, requiring the E-Z nomenclature system.

Question 5 of 10
Between maleic acid and fumaric acid, which has the lower boiling point?

Solution: Maleic acid (cis) has intramolecular hydrogen bonding which reduces intermolecular association, leading to a lower boiling point compared to fumaric acid.

Question 6 of 10
What is the maximum number of geometrical isomers for an unsymmetrical compound with 4 double bonds?

Solution: For an unsymmetrical polyene with n=4 double bonds, the number of geometrical isomers is 2^4 = 16.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following has a net dipole moment of zero?

Solution: trans-1,2-dichloroethene is completely symmetrical with opposing bond dipoles that cancel out, resulting in a zero net dipole moment.

Question 8 of 10
Which set of substituents has the highest CIP priority on a carbon atom?

Solution: Oxygen has an atomic number of 8, which is higher than nitrogen (7), carbon (6), and hydrogen (1), giving -OH top priority.

Question 9 of 10
Why is trans-cycloheptene unstable at room temperature?

Solution: Small and medium rings (like 7-membered rings) cannot span the trans double bond without extreme angular distortion and ring strain.

Question 10 of 10

Solution: 1-butene has a terminal CH2 group with two identical hydrogen atoms, making geometrical isomerism impossible.
