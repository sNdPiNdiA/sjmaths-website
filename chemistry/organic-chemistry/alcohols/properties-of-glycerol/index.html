Properties of Glycerol

Glycerol is a trihydric alcohol systematically named propane-1,2,3-triol with the molecular formula C3H8O3. It exhibits high viscosity, hygroscopic nature, and high boiling point due to extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structure and Physical Properties

  • Glycerol contains three hydroxyl groups attached to different sp3 hybridized carbon atoms.
  • It is a colorless, odorless, viscous, sweet-tasting liquid that is completely miscible with water and ethanol.
  • The high viscosity and boiling point (290 °C) arise from an extensive three-dimensional intermolecular hydrogen-bonding network.
  • It is strongly hygroscopic, absorbing moisture from the atmosphere and finding wide use as a humectant in cosmetics.

Chemical Reactivity and Esterification

  • Reaction with excess hydrogen iodide (HI) converts glycerol into 2-iodopropane via initial substitution and subsequent reduction steps.
  • Esterification with fatty acids yields triglycerides, which are foundational structural components of animal fats and vegetable oils.
  • Nitration using a mixture of concentrated HNO3 and H2SO4 produces glyceryl trinitrate, an explosive liquid used in dynamite manufacture.
  • Acetylation with acetic anhydride yields triacetylglycerol, confirming the presence of three hydroxyl groups.

Oxidation and Dehydration Reactions

  • Controlled oxidation using Fenton's reagent (H2O2 + Fe2+) or dilute HNO3 yields a mixture of glyceraldehyde and dihydroxyacetone.
  • Dehydration with powerful dehydrating agents like KHSO4 eliminates two water molecules to yield acrolein (acrylaldehyde), identifiable by its acrid, irritating odor.
  • Reaction with anhydrous oxalic acid at 110 °C yields allyl alcohol upon decarboxylation.
  • Oxidative cleavage by Periodic acid (HIO4) cleaves adjacent vicinal diol units, consuming two moles of HIO4 per mole of glycerol to yield two moles of formaldehyde and one mole of formic acid.

Units: Product formed: Glyceryl trinitrate (Nitroglycerin)

Units: Product formed: Prop-2-en-1-ol (Allyl alcohol)

Conditions: Aqueous solution treated with periodic acid
Units: Products: Formaldehyde and Formic acid

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Primary alcohols on oxidation normally yield carboxylic acids directly.

Observation: Glycerol oxidation with mild agents yields a mixture of aldehydes and ketones. — Presence of multiple adjacent hydroxyl groups leads to competitive oxidation of both primary and secondary centers without complete degradation.

Exception Dehydration of alcohols with acid typically yields symmetrical ethers or simple alkenes.

Observation: Dehydration of glycerol yields acrolein with an intensely pungent, choking odor. — Simultaneous loss of two water molecules from adjacent carbons stabilizes the conjugated alpha,beta-unsaturated aldehyde system.

Exception Polyhydroxy compounds are generally solids at room temperature due to strong lattice energies.

Observation: Glycerol is a dense, highly viscous liquid. — Intramolecular conformational flexibility and dense intermolecular hydrogen bonding prevent regular crystalline packing at room temperature.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Periodic Acid Consumption Rule
Moles of HIO4 = n - 1 (for adjacent OH groups)

Application: For glycerol, which has three adjacent carbons with OH groups, exactly 2 moles of HIO4 are consumed.

Acrolein Test Mnemonic
Glycerol + KHSO4 → Burnt Fat Odor

Application: Use this specific test to detect the presence of glycerol or fats by noting the characteristic choking smell of acrolein.

Nitration Product Count
3 OH groups + 3 HNO3 → Trinitrate + 3 H2O

Application: Remember that all three hydroxyl groups undergo esterification with nitric acid simultaneously to form nitroglycerin.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Glycerol is systematically classified as propane-1,2,3-triol.
  • Reaction with hot concentrated HI produces 2-iodopropane as the major product.
  • Acrolein test is the standard qualitative test for detecting glycerol and fats.
  • Nitroglycerin is an explosive ester formed by the nitration of glycerol.
  • Periodic acid cleavage of glycerol yields two moles of HCHO and one mole of HCOOH.
  • Reaction of glycerol with oxalic acid at 110 °C yields allyl alcohol and carbon dioxide.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the number of moles of periodic acid required; glycerol requires 2 moles, not 3.
  • Assuming glycerol is a solid at room temperature due to multiple hydrogen bonds; it is a viscous liquid.
  • Forgetting that dehydration of glycerol requires a potent dehydrating agent like KHSO4 or concentrated H2SO4 to yield acrolein.
  • Incorrectly identifying the product of glycerol oxidation with dilute HNO3 as purely glyceric acid without considering dihydroxyacetone.

⚡ Quick Revision: Properties of Glycerol

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

Glycerol possesses three hydrophilic hydroxyl groups on a short carbon chain, imparting high boiling point, water solubility, and exceptional viscosity.

Dehydration Reactions

Thermal treatment with KHSO4 eliminates water to form acrolein, an unsaturated aldehyde characterized by a pungent, choking smell.

Oxidative Cleavage

Periodic acid oxidation breaks carbon-carbon bonds between adjacent hydroxyl-bearing carbons, producing formaldehyde and formic acid.

Industrial Derivatives

Esterification with nitric acid yields nitroglycerin, an important explosive and vasodilator used in medicine.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Glycerol (C3H8O3)
A viscous, sweet-tasting trihydric alcohol acting as a humectant.
Acrolein (CH2=CH-CHO)
Pungent aldehyde formed by dehydration of glycerol.
Nitroglycerin (C3H5(ONO2)3)
Trinitrate ester of glycerol used as an explosive and medicine.
Allyl Alcohol (CH2=CH-CH2OH)
Unsaturated monohydric alcohol produced via oxalic acid reaction.
Humectant (Hygroscopic Agent)
A substance that absorbs or retains moisture from the atmosphere.
Periodic Acid (HIO4)
Oxidizing agent used to cleave vicinal diols and triols.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Glycerol formula: C3H8O3
  • Boiling point: 290 °C with decomposition
  • Dehydration product: Acrolein
  • Periodic acid moles consumed per mole of glycerol: 2
  • Nitration product: Glyceryl trinitrate

📌 Quick Recall

  • Glycerol is a versatile triol exhibiting strong hydrogen bonding.
  • It undergoes typical alcohol reactions multiplied across three hydroxyl sites.
  • Dehydration yields acrolein as a diagnostic test.
  • Oxidative cleavage with periodic acid provides structural confirmation.

⚖️ Key Differences

Reaction with Oxalic Acid at 110 °C VS Reaction with Oxalic Acid at 260 °C

At 110 °C, glycerol yields allyl alcohol, whereas at 260 °C, it yields formic acid and glycerol monoformate.
