Chemical Properties of Alcohols

Alcohols exhibit diverse chemical reactivity due to the polarization of the O−H and C−O bonds, undergoing cleavage at either the O−H bond or the C−O bond. They act as both weak acids and nucleophiles, and readily participate in oxidation, substitution, and elimination reactions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Cleavage of O−H Bond: Acidity and Metal Reactions

  • Alcohols act as weak Brønsted acids reacting with active metals like sodium, potassium, and aluminum to yield alkoxides and hydrogen gas.
  • The acidity order of alcohols follows primary > secondary > tertiary due to electron-releasing +I inductive effect of alkyl groups increasing electron density on oxygen.
  • Alcohols are weaker acids than water and carboxylic acids, meaning alkoxides are strong bases that can be protonated by water.
  • Reaction with Grignard reagents results in the formation of alkanes via proton transfer from the alcohol O−H group to the carbanion.

Cleavage of C−O Bond: Substitution and Elimination

  • Reaction of alcohols with hydrogen halides (HX) proceeds via cleavage of the C−O bond, following the reactivity order tertiary > secondary > primary.
  • Tertiary alcohols react rapidly via an SN1 mechanism involving stable carbocation intermediates, whereas primary alcohols react via SN2.
  • Dehydration of alcohols involves loss of a water molecule to form alkenes, following Saytzeff's rule where the major product is the more substituted, stable alkene.
  • Phosphorus trihalides (PX3) and thionyl chloride (SOCl2) convert alcohols to alkyl halides with retention or inversion configurations depending on conditions.

Oxidation and Catalytic Dehydrogenation

  • Primary alcohols undergo oxidation to aldehydes using mild reagents like pyridinium chlorochromate (PCC), or further to carboxylic acids using Jones reagent (CrO3/H2SO4).
  • Secondary alcohols are selectively oxidized to ketones by both mild and strong oxidizing agents without bond cleavage.
  • Tertiary alcohols are resistant to oxidation under normal conditions because they lack α-hydrogens, undergoing elimination or strong C−C cleavage only under extreme conditions.
  • Catalytic dehydrogenation over heated copper at 573 K converts primary alcohols to aldehydes, secondary alcohols to ketones, and tertiary alcohols exclusively to alkenes via elimination.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alcohols generally undergo dehydration via E1/E2 mechanisms yielding Saytzeff alkenes.

Observation: Pinacol-pinacolone rearrangement involves 1,2-methyl/aryl shift via a carbocation intermediate. — Formation of a more stable, resonance-stabilized or tertiary oxonium/carbocation species drives the rearrangement.

Exception Primary alcohols oxidize directly to carboxylic acids with Jones reagent.

Observation: Oxidation with PCC stops strictly at the aldehyde stage. — PCC is used in an anhydrous organic solvent (dichloromethane), lacking the water required to form the aldehyde hydrate intermediate needed for further oxidation.

Exception Order of dehydration reactivity is 3° > 2° > 1° via stable carbocations.

Observation: Neopentyl alcohol undergoes rapid rearrangement during dehydration. — Primary carbocation immediately undergoes a 1,2-methyl shift to generate a stable tertiary carbocation.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Methanol is historically known as wood spirit and is prepared industrially by catalytic hydrogenation of carbon monoxide at 573 K and 200-300 atm over ZnO-Cr2O3 catalyst.
  • Ethanol is manufactured by fermentation of sugars using invertase and zymase enzymes present in yeast.
  • Denatured alcohol is commercial ethanol rendered unfit for drinking by the addition of poisonous substances like methanol and pyridine.
  • The C−O−H bond angle in methanol is approximately 108.9°, slightly distorted from tetrahedral due to lone pair repulsions on oxygen.
  • Victor Meyer test distinguishes 1°, 2°, and 3° alcohols by sequential treatment with HI, AgNO2, and HNO2 yielding red, blue, and colorless solutions respectively.
  • Glycerol (propane-1,2,3-triol) is a trihydric alcohol that reacts with oxalic acid at 383 K to form formic acid and allyl alcohol.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the acidity order of alcohols (1° > 2° > 3°) with the basicity order of their corresponding alkoxides (3° > 2° > 1°).
  • Assuming tertiary alcohols can be oxidized by KMnO4 or K2Cr2O7 under mild conditions without bond breakage.
  • Forgetting that Lucas reagent requires anhydrous zinc chloride; aqueous ZnCl2 fails to yield the test turbidity.
  • Overlooking rearrangements during carbocation-mediated eliminations and substitutions in bulky aliphatic alcohols.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Properties of Alcohols

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Acidity of Alcohols

Alcohols act as weak acids, donating a proton to form alkoxide ions. Alkyl groups decrease acidity via electron-releasing inductive effects (+I).

Nucleophilic Substitution

C−O bond cleavage occurs via SN1 for tertiary alcohols and SN2 for primary alcohols when reacting with hydrogen halides or phosphorus halides.

Oxidation Pathways

Primary alcohols oxidize to aldehydes or carboxylic acids, secondary alcohols oxidize to ketones, and tertiary alcohols are inert to standard oxidation.

Dehydration Elimination

Acid-catalyzed dehydration removes water via E1 mechanism to produce alkenes following Saytzeff's rule.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Alkoxide (R−O−)
Conjugate base of an alcohol formed by loss of a proton.
Carbinol Carbon (C−OH)
Carbon atom directly bonded to the hydroxyl group.
Lucas Reagent (ZnCl2 / HCl)
Anhydrous zinc chloride dissolved in concentrated hydrochloric acid.
PCC (C5H5NH[ClCrO3])
Pyridinium chlorochromate used for selective oxidation of 1° alcohols to aldehydes.
Saytzeff Rule (Elimination Rule)
States that the major alkene product has the greater number of alkyl substituents on the double bond carbons.
Esterification (R−COOR')
Condensation reaction between an alcohol and a carboxylic acid forming an ester.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Acidity order: R−OH > H2O > Terminal Alkyne > NH3 > Alkane
  • Dehydration reactivity: 3° > 2° > 1° alcohol
  • Lucas test speed: Tertiary > Secondary > Primary
  • PCC stops at aldehyde for primary alcohols without over-oxidation to carboxylic acid
  • Ethanol fermentation produces a maximum concentration of about 14% alcohol in wash

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alcohols undergo reactions involving cleavage of either O−H or C−O bonds.
  • Acidity decreases with increased alkyl substitution due to +I effect.
  • Oxidation state of carbinol carbon increases during oxidation reactions.
  • Dehydration yields alkenes predominantly via stable carbocation intermediates.

⚖️ Key Differences

PCC Oxidation VS Jones Oxidation

PCC stops at aldehyde stage for primary alcohols, whereas Jones reagent oxidizes primary alcohols all the way to carboxylic acids.

What is the product formed when ethanol is heated with concentrated sulfuric acid at 443 K?

Question 3 of 18

Which reagent is best suited to convert a primary alcohol directly to an aldehyde?

Explanation: PCC is a mild, anhydrous oxidizing agent that selectively converts primary alcohols to aldehydes without over-oxidizing to carboxylic acids.

Question 4 of 18

The acidic strength of alcohols follows which of the following orders?

Explanation: Electron-releasing alkyl groups destabilize the alkoxide ion by intensifying negative charge; hence acidity decreases with increased branching: 1° > 2° > 3°.

Question 5 of 18

Catalytic dehydrogenation of a secondary alcohol yields:

Explanation: Dehydrogenation of secondary alcohols over heated copper at 573 K removes hydrogen to form ketones.

Question 6 of 18

Which compound is obtained when glycerol reacts with oxalic acid at 383 K?

Explanation: Reaction of glycerol with oxalic acid at 383 K yields formic acid and carbon dioxide as primary products.

Question 7 of 18

In the dehydration of 2-butanol, the major product formed according to Saytzeff's rule is:

Explanation: Saytzeff's rule predicts that the major alkene is the most substituted one, which is trans/cis-but-2-ene.

Question 8 of 18

Victor Meyer test gives a red color for which type of alcohol?

Explanation: Primary alcohols give a red coloration with the Victor Meyer test sequence, whereas secondary give blue and tertiary give no color.

Question 9 of 18

The C−O−H bond angle in methanol is approximately:

Explanation: Due to lone pair repulsion on the oxygen atom, the C−O−H bond angle in alcohols is compressed from tetrahedral to roughly 108.9°.

Question 10 of 18

Which reagent converts phenol to benzene?

Explanation: Distillation of phenol with zinc dust reduces the phenolic hydroxyl group to hydrogen, yielding benzene.

Question 11 of 18

What happens when tertiary butyl alcohol is treated with copper at 573 K?

Explanation: Tertiary alcohols lack α-hydrogens necessary for dehydrogenation and instead undergo acid- or metal-catalyzed dehydration to form alkenes.

Question 12 of 18

The functional isomer of an aliphatic alcohol with the same general molecular formula is an:

Explanation: Monohydric alcohols and ethers share the general formula CnH2n+2O, making them functional isomers.

Question 13 of 18

Reaction of an alcohol with a carboxylic acid in the presence of concentrated HnSO4 is called:

Explanation: Fischer esterification involves the acid-catalyzed condensation of an alcohol and carboxylic acid to form an ester and water.

Question 14 of 18

Which of the following is used to denature industrial ethanol?

Explanation: Toxic additives like methanol and pyridine are added to industrial alcohol to prevent human consumption.

Question 15 of 18

What is the intermediate formed during the acid-catalyzed dehydration of ethanol?

Explanation: Protonation of the hydroxyl group forms an oxonium ion which leaves as water, generating a carbocation intermediate.

Question 16 of 18

Explanation: Glycerol (propane-1,2,3-triol) contains three hydroxyl groups, classifying it as a trihydric alcohol.

Question 17 of 18

Explanation: Active metals react with alcohols to liberate hydrogen gas and form metal alkoxides.

Solution: Strong oxidizing agents like acidified K2Cr2O7 oxidize primary alcohols directly to carboxylic acids.

Solution: Formaldehyde reacts with Grignard reagents to produce primary alcohols after hydrolysis.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following compounds has the highest boiling point?

Solution: Alcohols form strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds, resulting in significantly higher boiling points than corresponding alkanes, ethers, or aldehydes of comparable molecular mass.

Question 5 of 10
Reaction of phenol with bromine water yields:

Solution: The activating effect of the −OH group in phenol directs electrophilic bromination to yield a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol rapidly.

Question 6 of 10
Esterification of an alcohol with a carboxylic acid is a:

Solution: Fischer esterification is an equilibrium-controlled, reversible reaction driven forward by removing water or using excess reagent.

Solution: Tertiary alcohols undergo catalytic dehydration over heated copper to produce alkenes because they lack α-hydrogens.

Question 8 of 10
Which chemical test differentiates methanol from ethanol?

Solution: Ethanol contains the CH3CH(OH)− group and gives a positive iodoform test (yellow precipitate with I2/NaOH), whereas methanol does not.

Solution: Williamson ether synthesis involves the nucleophilic substitution of an alkyl halide by a sodium alkoxide to form an ether.
