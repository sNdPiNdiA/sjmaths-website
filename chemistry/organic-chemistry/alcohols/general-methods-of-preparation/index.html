Organic Chemistry

General Methods of Preparation of Alcohols

Alcohols are synthesized via hydration, reduction, and organometallic addition reactions targeting carbonyl and alkene precursors. These methods establish carbon-oxygen single bonds through nucleophilic addition and substitution pathways.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Reduction of Carbonyl Compounds

  • Aldehydes reduce with sodium borohydride (NaBH4) or lithium aluminium hydride (LiAlH4) to yield primary (1°) alcohols.
  • Ketones yield secondary (2°) alcohols upon reduction with metal hydrides or catalytic hydrogenation (H2/Ni).
  • Carboxylic acids and esters require strong reducing agents like LiAlH4 to form primary alcohols; NaBH4 is ineffective.
  • Ester cleavage using LiAlH4 produces two alcohol molecules corresponding to the acid and alcohol fragments.

Organometallic Synthesis via Grignard Reagents

  • Formaldehyde (methanal) reacts with Grignard reagents (RMgX) followed by acid hydrolysis to produce primary (1°) alcohols.
  • Higher aldehydes react with RMgX to yield secondary (2°) alcohols through nucleophilic addition at the carbonyl carbon.
  • Ketones react with Grignard reagents to produce tertiary (3°) alcohols with a newly formed carbon-carbon bond.
  • Esters react with two equivalents of Grignard reagents to yield tertiary alcohols containing two identical alkyl groups from the reagent.

Hydration and Hydroboration of Alkenes

  • Acid-catalyzed hydration proceeds via carbocation intermediates, following Markovnikov's rule with potential carbocation rearrangement.
  • Oxymercuration-demercuration yields Markovnikov alcohols without skeletal rearrangements, using Hg(OAc)2 followed by NaBH4.
  • Hydroboration-oxidation provides anti-Markovnikov alcohols via a concerted syn-addition mechanism involving trialkylboranes.
  • Alkene precursors must be pure to avoid isomeric alcohol mixtures during electrophilic hydration procedures.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Reduction of Aldehydes and Ketones
RCHO + H2 →[Ni/Pt or LiAlH4] RCH2OH
Conditions: Catalytic hydrogenation or metal hydride reduction in dry aprotic media
Units: Molar conversion efficiency
Reduction of Carboxylic Acids and Esters
RCOOH + 4[H] →[LiAlH4] RCH2OH + H2O
Conditions: Lithium aluminium hydride in anhydrous diethyl ether or THF
Units: Molar consumption of hydride reducing agent
Hydroboration-Oxidation of Alkenes
3R-CH=CH2 + BH3 → (RCH2CH2)3B →[H2O2, OH-] 3RCH2CH2OH
Conditions: THF solvent, anti-Markovnikov stereospecific syn-addition
Units: Regioselective yield percentage
Acid-Catalyzed Hydration of Alkenes
R-CH=CH2 + H2O →[H+] R-CH(OH)-CH3
Conditions: Dilute mineral acid catalyst (H2SO4), Markovnikov addition
Units: Thermodynamic equilibrium yield
Grignard Synthesis of Alcohols
RMgX + R'CHO → R-CH(OH)-R' →(H3O+) 2° Alcohol
Conditions: Anhydrous ether solvent, followed by acid hydrolysis
Units: Stoichiometric molar ratios (1:1 reactant ratio)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception NaBH4 reduces all carbonyl compounds to alcohols

Observation: NaBH4 does not reduce carboxylic acids, esters, or amides — Lower nucleophilic character of hydridoborate compared to aluminohydride due to electronegativity differences between B and Al.

Exception Acid-catalyzed hydration of alkenes gives Markovnikov product

Observation: Rearrangement of carbocation intermediates often yields rearranged constitutional isomers — Formation of more stable 2° or 3° carbocation intermediates via 1,2-hydride or 1,2-methyl shifts.

Exception Grignard reagents add to carbonyl groups to form alcohols

Observation: Grignard reagents act as strong bases with active hydrogen compounds (water, alcohols, amines) — Immediate proton transfer yields unreactive hydrocarbon alkanes instead of alcohol addition products.

Hydration Regiochemistry Mnemonic
OH-Mark vs OH-Anti

Application: Direct acid hydration puts OH on the more substituted carbon (Markovnikov); Hydroboration puts OH on the less substituted carbon (Anti-Markovnikov).

Reducing Agent Hierarchy
LiAlH4 reduces everything; NaBH4 stops at aldehydes/ketones

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Formaldehyde with RMgX yields primary alcohols; higher aldehydes yield secondary alcohols.
  • Ketones react with Grignard reagents to exclusively form tertiary alcohols.
  • Lithium aluminium hydride (LiAlH4) reduces carboxylic acids directly to primary alcohols.
  • Hydroboration-oxidation of propene yields propan-1-ol via anti-Markovnikov addition.
  • Oxymercuration-demercuration prevents carbocation rearrangements during alkene hydration.
  • Reduction of esters with LiAlH4 cleaves the ester bond to produce two distinct alcohol molecules.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Using moist ether in Grignard reactions, destroying the reagent via protonation to form an alkane.
  • Forgetting that NaBH4 cannot reduce carboxylic acids or esters.
  • Assuming acid-catalyzed hydration of 3-methylbut-1-ene yields 3-methylbutan-2-ol without considering carbocation rearrangement to 2-methylbutan-2-ol.
  • Confusing the regiochemistry of oxymercuration (Markovnikov) with hydroboration (anti-Markovnikov).

⚡ Quick Revision: General Methods of Preparation of Alcohols

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Reduction Pathways

Carbonyl compounds undergo nucleophilic hydride transfer from NaBH4 or LiAlH4 to generate hydroxyl groups.

Organometallic Additions

Grignard reagents supply nucleophilic alkyl carbanions to carbonyl electrophiles, lengthening carbon chains to form substituted alcohols.

Alkene Hydration Methods

Alkenes incorporate water across pi bonds via direct acid catalysis, oxymercuration, or hydroboration-oxidation protocols.

Functional Group Transformations

Carboxylic acid derivatives and esters require powerful metal hydrides for complete reduction to primary alcohols.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Grignard Reagent (RMgX)
Organomagnesium halide acting as a strong nucleophile and base.
Hydroboration (BH3 • THF)
Addition of B-H across alkene double bonds with anti-Markovnikov regiochemistry.
Markovnikov Rule (Regioselective addition)
Addition of protic acid where hydrogen attaches to carbon with more hydrogens.
Reduction ([H])
Addition of hydrogen or removal of oxygen, lowering oxidation state.
Oxymercuration (Hg(OAc)2)
Electrophilic addition of mercury(II) to alkenes without rearrangements.
Lithium Aluminium Hydride (LiAlH4)
Powerful reducing agent converting polar multiple bonds to alcohols.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Formaldehyde + RMgX → 1° Alcohol
  • Aldehyde + RMgX → 2° Alcohol
  • Ketone + RMgX → 3° Alcohol
  • Carboxylic Acid + LiAlH4 → 1° Alcohol
  • Alkene + BH3 / H2O2, OH- → Anti-Markovnikov Alcohol

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carbonyl reduction provides direct access to 1° and 2° alcohols.
  • Grignard reagents build molecular complexity by forming C-C bonds in alcohol synthesis.
  • Alkene hydration offers complementary Markovnikov and anti-Markovnikov pathways.
  • Anhydrous conditions are mandatory when handling organometallic reagents.

⚖️ Key Differences

NaBH4 VS LiAlH4

NaBH4 is selective for aldehydes/ketones; LiAlH4 reduces acids, esters, amides, and nitriles.

Question 4 of 19

What is the intermediate formed during the acid-catalyzed hydration of alkenes?

Explanation: Acid-catalyzed hydration proceeds via electrophilic addition of a proton to form a stable carbocation intermediate.

Question 5 of 19

Which reducing agent is selective for reducing aldehydes and ketones in the presence of ester groups?

Explanation: Sodium borohydride (NaBH4) is a mild reducing agent that reduces aldehydes and ketones but leaves esters and carboxylic acids unaffected.

Question 6 of 19

Reaction of formaldehyde with ethylmagnesium chloride followed by hydrolysis yields:

Explanation: Formaldehyde (1 carbon) plus ethylmagnesium chloride (2 carbons) produces propan-1-ol (3 carbons).

Question 7 of 19

Which alkene on acid-catalyzed hydration gives 2-methylbutan-2-ol as the major product?

Explanation: Both 2-methylbut-1-ene and 3-methylbut-1-ene form the tertiary 2-methyl-2-butyl carbocation intermediate upon protonation, yielding 2-methylbutan-2-ol.

Question 8 of 19

What is the stereochemical outcome of hydroboration-oxidation of an unsymmetrical alkene?

Explanation: Hydroboration proceeds via concerted syn-addition of B-H across the alkene double bond, delivering anti-Markovnikov regiochemistry.

Question 9 of 19

Which of the following cannot be prepared by reduction of carbonyl compounds?

Explanation: Tertiary alcohols cannot be prepared by reducing aldehydes or ketones; they require organometallic addition to ketones or esters.

Question 10 of 19

Oxymercuration-demercuration of alkenes results in:

Explanation: Oxymercuration-demercuration hydrates alkenes following Markovnikov's rule without skeletal rearrangement.

Question 11 of 19

What happens when methyl acetate reacts with 2 equivalents of phenylmagnesium bromide followed by hydrolysis?

Explanation: Esters react with two equivalents of Grignard reagents to form tertiary alcohols containing two alkyl/aryl groups from the Grignard reagent.

Question 12 of 19

Which reagent converts carboxylic acids directly to primary alcohols?

Explanation: Carboxylic acids are resistant to mild reducing agents like NaBH4 but are readily reduced to primary alcohols by LiAlH4.

Question 13 of 19

Hydrolysis of an ester with aqueous alkali yields a salt of carboxylic acid and:

Explanation: Base-catalyzed ester hydrolysis (saponification) yields carboxylate salts and alcohols.

Question 14 of 19

Which Grignard reagent combined with oxirane (ethylene oxide) yields propan-1-ol?

Explanation: Oxirane (2 carbons) reacts with methylmagnesium bromide (1 carbon) to yield propan-1-ol after ring opening and hydrolysis.

Question 15 of 19

Reduction of benzaldehyde with NaBH4 gives:

Explanation: Reduction of benzaldehyde (an aromatic aldehyde) using NaBH4 yields benzyl alcohol (C6H5CH2OH).

Question 16 of 19

In the preparation of alcohols from alkenes using concentrated H2SO4 and water, the rate-determining step is:

Explanation: The formation of the carbocation intermediate via electrophilic attack of H+ on the alkene is the slow, rate-determining step.

Question 17 of 19

Which of the following compounds yields a secondary alcohol upon reaction with Grignard reagent followed by hydrolysis?

Explanation: Acetaldehyde is a primary/higher aldehyde which reacts with Grignard reagents to form secondary alcohols.

Question 18 of 19

What is the role of dry ether in the preparation of Grignard reagents?

Explanation: Ether solvent molecules coordinate with and stabilize the magnesium center of the Grignard reagent through lone pair donation.

Question 19 of 19

Catalytic hydrogenation of ketones using H2 over Raney Nickel produces:

Explanation: Ketones contain a carbonyl carbon attached to two alkyl groups, yielding secondary alcohols upon catalytic hydrogenation.
