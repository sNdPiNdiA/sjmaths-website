Glycerol

Glycerol is a trihydric alcohol with the IUPAC name propane-1,2,3-triol, containing two primary and one secondary hydroxyl groups. It is a viscous, sweet-tasting, hygroscopic liquid obtained as a byproduct in the saponification of fats and oils.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structure, Bonding, and Physical Properties

  • Glycerol contains three hydrophilic -OH groups, imparting extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
  • It is a highly viscous, hygroscopic liquid that is miscible with water in all proportions but insoluble in ether and chloroform.
  • The presence of two primary and one secondary alcohol group dictates its regioselective esterification and etherification behavior.
  • Its high boiling point (290 °C with decomposition) is a direct consequence of a dense three-dimensional intermolecular hydrogen bond network.

Chemical Reactions and Functional Derivatives

  • Reaction with PCl5 replaces all three hydroxyl groups to yield 1,2,3-trichloropropane.
  • Esterification with long-chain fatty acids yields triglycerides, which constitute animal fats and vegetable oils.
  • Oxidation with periodic acid (HIO4) cleaves adjacent vicinal diols, producing two moles of formaldehyde and one mole of formic acid.
  • Reaction with dry HCl gas yields a mixture of α-monochlorohydrin and β-monochlorohydrin.

Industrial Preparation and Specialized Uses

  • It is manufactured industrially as a byproduct of soap manufacture (saponification of fats) and fat splitting.
  • Catalytic hydrogenolysis of carbohydrates provides an alternative modern route for large-scale glycerol synthesis.
  • Nitration of glycerol yields nitroglycerin, an explosive liquid used in the manufacture of dynamite and as a vasodilator medicine.
  • It is extensively used as a humectant in cosmetics, pharmaceuticals, tobacco, and food products to retain moisture.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Dehydration with Potassium Bisulfate
CH2OH-CHOH-CH2OH → CH2=CH-CHO + 2H2O
Conditions: Heating with dehydrating agent KHSO4 or conc. H2SO4
Units: Yields acrolein (acrylaldehyde) with characteristic foul odor
Reaction with Nitric Acid
C3H5(OH)3 + 3HNO3 → C3H5(ONO2)3 + 3H2O
Conditions: Presence of concentrated H2SO4 at low temperature
Units: Produces glyceryl trinitrate (nitroglycerin)
Oxidation with Dilute Nitric Acid
CH2OH-CHOH-CH2OH + [O] → CH2OH-CHOH-COOH (Glyceric acid)
Conditions: Mild oxidizing agent (dilute HNO3)
Units: Selectively oxidizes primary alcohol to carboxylic acid
Reaction with Oxalic Acid at 110 °C
C3H8O3 + HOOC-COOH → CH2OH-CHOH-CH2-O-CO-COOH → CH2OH-CHOH-CH2OH + CO2 + HCHO
Conditions: Heating glycerol with oxalic acid at 110 °C followed by distillation
Units: Laboratory method for the preparation of pure formic acid
Molecular Formula
C3H8O3
Conditions: Standard ambient temperature and pressure
Units: Molar mass: 92.09 g mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Vicinal diols on oxidation with periodic acid typically yield aldehydes or ketones.

Observation: Glycerol yields both formaldehyde and formic acid upon periodate cleavage. — The central carbon oxidizes completely to formic acid due to the presence of three adjacent hydroxyl-bearing carbons undergoing sequential oxidative cleavage.

Exception Dehydration of secondary alcohols with acid yields the most stable substituted alkene.

Observation: Dehydration of glycerol with KHSO4 yields acrolein, an unsaturated aldehyde. — Elimination of two water molecules occurs simultaneously accompanied by rearrangement of the intermediate enol to form the stable aldehyde functionality.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Acrolein Test Mnemonic
Glycerol + KHSO4 → Burnt Fat Odor (Acrolein)

Application: Use this specific pungent, irritating odor test to instantly identify the presence of glycerol or fats.

Periodate Consumption Rule
Moles of HIO4 consumed = n - 1 (where n is adjacent -OH groups)

Application: For glycerol, 3 adjacent carbons with -OH groups consume exactly 2 moles of HIO4.

Nitration Product Stoichiometry
1 Glycerol + 3 HNO3 → 1 Trinitrate + 3 H2O

Application: Remember that complete esterification of all three hydroxyl groups requires a 1:3 molar ratio.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Glycerol is a trihydric alcohol with IUPAC name propane-1,2,3-triol.
  • Acrolein test involves heating glycerol with potassium bisulfate (KHSO4) to produce an irritating pungent odor.
  • Nitroglycerin is an explosive liquid formed by the complete nitration of glycerol using a mixture of concentrated nitric and sulfuric acids.
  • Glycerol is hygroscopic due to extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding with water molecules.
  • Oxidation of glycerol with periodic acid yields 2 moles of HCHO and 1 mole of HCOOH.
  • Reaction of glycerol with oxalic acid at 110 °C serves as a laboratory preparation method for pure formic acid.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the products of nitric acid oxidation: dilute HNO3 gives glyceric acid, whereas bismuth nitrate yields dihydroxyacetone.
  • Assuming glycerol is a monohydric alcohol; always account for all three hydroxyl groups in stoichiometry calculations.
  • Forgetting that acrolein is an aldehyde (CH2=CH-CHO) and gives positive tests with Tollens' reagent.
  • Incorrectly stating that glycerol is insoluble in water; it is completely miscible in all proportions due to hydrogen bonding.

⚡ Quick Revision: Glycerol

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Physical Properties

Glycerol is a viscous, hygroscopic triol whose high boiling point and water solubility stem from intermolecular hydrogen bonding across its three hydroxyl groups.

Synthetic Routes and Derivatives

It is obtained via fat saponification and converted into important derivatives such as nitroglycerin explosives, triglycerides, and chlorohydrins.

Dehydration Reactions

Acid-catalyzed dehydration with KHSO4 eliminates two water molecules to produce acrolein, characterized by a pungent, acrid odor.

Oxidative Cleavage

Oxidation with periodic acid cleanly cleaves the vicinal diol system, producing formaldehyde and formic acid in predictable stoichiometric ratios.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • IUPAC Name: Propane-1,2,3-triol
  • Dehydration product: Acrolein (CH2=CH-CHO)
  • Nitration product: Glyceryl trinitrate (explosive)
  • Oxidation with HIO4: 2 HCHO + 1 HCOOH
  • Byproduct of soap industry: Glycerol

📌 Quick Recall

  • Glycerol is a key trihydric alcohol with diverse industrial applications.
  • It undergoes typical alcohol reactions with high regioselectivity due to two primary and one secondary -OH group.
  • Dehydration gives acrolein, serving as a confirmatory test.
  • Nitration yields nitroglycerin, a powerful explosive.

⚖️ Key Differences

Glyceric acid VS Tartronic acid

Glyceric acid is formed by oxidation of one primary -OH, while tartronic acid involves oxidation of both primary -OH groups to carboxylic acids.

What is the IUPAC name of glycerol?

Explanation: Glycerol contains a three-carbon chain with hydroxyl groups at positions 1, 2, and 3, designated as propane-1,2,3-triol.

Question 2 of 19

Which compound is formed when glycerol is heated with potassium bisulfate (KHSO4)?

Explanation: Heating glycerol with KHSO4 causes dehydration, eliminating two water molecules to form acrolein (acrylaldehyde).

Question 3 of 19

How many moles of periodic acid (HIO4) are consumed per mole of glycerol during oxidation?

Explanation: Glycerol has three adjacent hydroxyl-bearing carbons (two adjacent glycol units), consuming exactly 2 moles of HIO4.

Question 4 of 19

What is the primary commercial source of glycerol?

Explanation: Glycerol is obtained industrially as a major byproduct during the saponification of fats and oils in soap manufacture.

Question 5 of 19

Nitroglycerin is chemically classified as:

Explanation: Nitroglycerin is glyceryl trinitrate, an organic nitrate ester formed by the reaction of glycerol with nitric acid.

Question 6 of 19

Which of the following describes the physical property of glycerol?

Explanation: Glycerol is a clear, colorless, odorless, viscous, hygroscopic liquid with a sweet taste.

Question 7 of 19

Reaction of glycerol with oxalic acid at 110 °C yields:

Explanation: Heating glycerol with oxalic acid at 110 °C produces glycerol monooxalate which decomposes to yield pure formic acid.

Question 8 of 19

What is the product formed when glycerol is oxidized with dilute nitric acid?

Explanation: Mild oxidation of glycerol with dilute nitric acid selectively oxidizes one primary alcohol group to form glyceric acid.

Question 9 of 19

Why is glycerol more viscous than ethanol?

Explanation: Three hydroxyl groups per molecule allow for extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding networks, increasing resistance to flow (viscosity).

Question 10 of 19

What happens when glycerol reacts with excess hydrogen iodide (HI)?

Explanation: Reaction with HI initially forms triiodide which is unstable and loses iodine to yield allyl iodide, which further reacts to give isopropyl iodide.

Question 11 of 19

Which reagent converts glycerol into dihydroxyacetone?

Explanation: Bismuth nitrate specifically oxidizes the secondary hydroxyl group of glycerol to a carbonyl group, yielding dihydroxyacetone.

Question 12 of 19

Glycerol is classed as a:

Explanation: Glycerol contains three hydroxyl (-OH) groups, making it a trihydric alcohol.

Question 13 of 19

The characteristic odor of acrolein produced during the dehydration of glycerol resembles:

Explanation: Acrolein has a very characteristic, highly irritating, pungent odor resembling burning fat.

Question 14 of 19

Which of the following functional groups is NOT present in glycerol?

Explanation: Glycerol contains two primary and one secondary alcohol groups (-OH), but no aldehyde carbonyl group in its normal structure.

Question 15 of 19

In the manufacture of dynamite, nitroglycerin is absorbed on:

Explanation: Alfred Nobel stabilized liquid nitroglycerin by absorbing it on kieselguhr (diatomaceous earth) to produce dynamite.

Question 16 of 19

What is the action of dry HCl gas on glycerol at room temperature?

Explanation: Dry HCl gas reacts with glycerol to yield a mixture of α- and β-monochlorohydrins.

Question 17 of 19

Which test is used to detect the presence of glycerol or fats?

Explanation: The acrolein test with KHSO4 is the standard qualitative test for detecting glycerol.

Question 18 of 19

Glycerol is widely used in cosmetics primarily as a:

Explanation: Due to its high hygroscopic nature, glycerol acts as an effective humectant to retain moisture in cosmetic products.

Question 19 of 19

When glycerol is treated with phosphorus trichloride (PCl3), the product is:

Explanation: Reaction with PCl3 replaces all three -OH groups with chlorine atoms, yielding 1,2,3-trichloropropane.

Solution: Bismuth nitrate selectively oxidizes the secondary alcohol group of glycerol into dihydroxyacetone.

Question 2 of 10
Which property of glycerol makes it suitable for use in automobile radiator antifreezes?

Solution: Aqueous solutions of glycerol show significant freezing point depression, making them effective antifreezes.

Question 3 of 10
How many chiral centers are present in a glycerol molecule?

Solution: Glycerol has a plane of symmetry and no asymmetric carbon atoms (chiral centers), making it optically inactive.

Question 4 of 10
Reaction of glycerol with excess acetic anhydride gives:

Solution: Reaction with excess acetic anhydride acetylates all three hydroxyl groups, yielding glycerol triacetate.

Question 5 of 10
What is the state of hybridization of carbon atoms in glycerol?

Solution: All three carbon atoms in glycerol are bonded via single bonds, hence they are all sp3 hybridized.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following statements regarding glycerol is correct?

Solution: Glycerol is a thick, sweet-tasting, viscous liquid that is miscible with ethanol and water.

Question 7 of 10
Upon treatment with dry HCl, glycerol primarily yields which chlorohydrin isomer in greater proportion?

Solution: α-monochlorohydrin (3-chloropropane-1,2-diol) is formed in higher proportion due to statistical preference at the primary positions.

Question 8 of 10
Which test confirms the presence of vicinal hydroxyl groups in glycerol?

Solution: Periodic acid specifically reacts with compounds containing vicinal diols, cleaving the carbon-carbon bonds.

Question 9 of 10
What is formed when glycerol is heated with concentrated HI?

Solution: Reaction with conc. HI yields triiodide which decomposes to allyl iodide, further reacting with excess HI to form isopropyl iodide.

Question 10 of 10
Glycerol reacts with sodium metal to evolve:

Solution: Active metals like sodium react with the alcoholic hydroxyl groups of glycerol to form glyceroxides and evolve hydrogen gas.
