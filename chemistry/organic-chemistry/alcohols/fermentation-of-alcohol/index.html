Fermentation of Alcohol

Fermentation of alcohol is a biochemical process where complex carbohydrates are converted into ethanol and carbon dioxide by the action of specific enzymes. The process is strictly anaerobic and proceeds through successive enzymatic breakdown steps.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Enzymatic Catalysis in Carbohydrate Breakdown

  • Starch is converted into soluble maltose using the enzyme diastase derived from germinated barley or malt.
  • Maltose is subsequently hydrolyzed into glucose molecules via the action of the enzyme maltase.
  • Sucrose derived from molasses undergoes hydrolysis into glucose and fructose via the enzyme invertase.
  • All enzymatic reactions require specific optimum temperature ranges between 25 °C and 37 °C for maximum catalytic turnover.

Glycolysis and Decarboxylation Pathway

  • Glucose enters the glycolytic pathway (Embden-Meyerhof-Parnas pathway) to yield pyruvic acid under anaerobic conditions.
  • Pyruvic acid undergoes decarboxylation catalyzed by pyruvate decarboxylase to yield acetaldehyde and carbon dioxide gas.
  • Acetaldehyde is subsequently reduced to ethanol by alcohol dehydrogenase using NADH as the reducing equivalent.
  • The complete conversion sequence from glucose to ethanol is mediated by the multi-enzyme complex zymase found in yeast.

Environmental Parameters and Constraints

  • Fermentation processes require complete exclusion of oxygen to prevent aerobic oxidation of ethanol to acetic acid.
  • The optimum pH range for yeast activity during alcoholic fermentation lies between 4.5 and 6.5.
  • High concentrations of ethanol (above 14-18%) inhibit yeast cell viability, halting the fermentation process entirely.
  • Addition of antiseptics like ammonium fluoride or sulfuric acid prevents bacterial contamination during industrial fermentation.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Enzymes operate efficiently across wide temperature ranges

Observation: Denaturation above 40-50 °C — Enzymes are proteinaceous catalysts whose tertiary structures disrupt at elevated temperatures, leading to permanent loss of catalytic activity.

Exception Yeast can ferment solutions of arbitrarily high sugar concentration

Observation: Inhibition at high sugar concentrations (>10%) — High osmotic pressure causes dehydration of yeast cells, inhibiting enzyme secretion and halting metabolic pathways.

Exception Fermentation yields exclusively ethanol as the primary liquid product

Observation: Formation of by-products like glycerol and fusel oils — Secondary metabolic pathways in yeast produce small quantities of higher alcohols and glycerol, especially under alkaline pH conditions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Zymase is a complex of enzymes present in yeast responsible for converting glucose to ethanol and carbon dioxide.
  • Invertase converts sucrose into an equimolar mixture of D-glucose and D-fructose, known as invert sugar.
  • Power alcohol is an anhydrous mixture of 80% petrol and 20% absolute alcohol used as motor fuel.
  • Denatured alcohol (methylated spirit) is rendered unfit for drinking by adding 10% methanol and pyridine.
  • The maximum concentration of alcohol obtained by natural fermentation of sugar is about 14 to 18%.
  • Pasteur's studies proved that fermentation is fundamentally a physiological process associated with the life processes of yeast.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the roles of invertase and diastase; invertase acts on disaccharides (sucrose), while diastase acts on polysaccharides (starch).
  • Assuming fermentation requires atmospheric oxygen; it is strictly an anaerobic metabolic pathway.
  • Failing to account for enzyme denaturation when temperatures exceed 50 °C in industrial reactors.
  • Believing that pure 100% ethanol can be obtained directly by fractional distillation of wash, ignoring the constant-boiling azeotrope at 95.6%.

⚡ Quick Revision: Fermentation of Alcohol

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Carbohydrate Raw Materials

Fermentation utilizes starchy grains and sugary molasses as economical carbohydrate feedstocks for large-scale industrial ethanol production.

Enzymatic Hydrolysis

Complex polysaccharides and disaccharides are broken down into fermentable monosaccharides through specific hydrolytic enzymes like diastase and invertase.

Anaerobic Glycolysis

Yeast enzymes convert monosaccharides into pyruvic acid, followed by decarboxylation and reduction to yield ethanol and carbon dioxide.

Purification and Rectification

Dilute fermented wash is subjected to fractional distillation to produce rectified spirit (95.6% ethanol), which is further dehydrated to absolute alcohol.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Zymase (Enzyme Complex)
Catalytic protein complex in yeast converting glucose to ethanol.
Wash (Fermented Solution)
Dilute aqueous solution of ethanol (8-10%) obtained after fermentation.
Rectified Spirit (C2H5OH (95.6%))
Azeotropic mixture of ethanol and water obtained by fractional distillation.
Absolute Alcohol (100% C2H5OH)
Water-free ethanol prepared by distilling rectified spirit over quicklime.
Power Alcohol (Fuel Blend)
Blend of absolute alcohol and petrol used as internal combustion engine fuel.
Invert Sugar (Glucose + Fructose)
Equimolar mixture obtained by the hydrolysis of sucrose.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Optimum temperature for yeast fermentation is 25-37 °C.
  • Yeast is inactivated at temperatures above 50 °C.
  • Maximum ethanol concentration by direct fermentation is 14-18%.
  • Rectified spirit contains 95.6% ethanol and 4.4% water.
  • Pyridine and methanol are added to ethanol to produce methylated spirit.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Fermentation converts sugars into ethanol anaerobically using yeast enzymes.
  • Starch requires diastase and maltase prior to zymase action.
  • Sucrose requires invertase prior to zymase action.
  • Fractional distillation of wash yields rectified spirit.

⚖️ Key Differences

Rectified Spirit VS Absolute Alcohol

Rectified spirit contains 4.4% water as an azeotrope, whereas absolute alcohol is 100% pure ethanol.

Question 12 of 18

Explanation: Rectified spirit is refluxed over quicklime (CaO), which absorbs water, followed by distillation to yield absolute alcohol.

Which of the following conditions is mandatory for alcoholic fermentation?

Explanation: Alcoholic fermentation is strictly an anaerobic process carried out in the absence of molecular oxygen.

Question 14 of 18

The chemical formula of starch is:

Explanation: Starch is a polysaccharide with the general formula (C6H10O5)n.

Question 15 of 18

What is the optimum pH range for yeast fermentation?

Explanation: Yeast enzymes function most effectively in a weakly acidic medium with a pH range of 4.5 to 6.5.

Question 16 of 18

Who established that fermentation is a biological process caused by yeast?

Explanation: Louis Pasteur conclusively proved that fermentation is a vital physiological process associated with living yeast cells.

Question 17 of 18

The conversion of molasses into ethanol involves which initial enzyme?

Explanation: Molasses contains sucrose, which is first hydrolyzed by invertase into glucose and fructose.

Question 18 of 18

Why is absolute alcohol not prepared by direct fractional distillation of rectified spirit?

Explanation: Water and ethanol form a minimum-boiling azeotrope at 95.6% composition, preventing further separation by simple distillation.
