Organic Chemistry

Alcohols

Alcohols are organic compounds containing one or more hydroxyl (-OH) groups attached directly to carbon atoms of aliphatic systems. They act both as weak acids and weak nucleophiles due to the polarization of the O-H and C-O bonds.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structure, Bonding, and Physical Properties

  • The C-O-H bond angle in alcohols is approximately 108.9°, slightly compressed from the tetrahedral angle due to lone pair repulsions on oxygen.
  • Alcohols exhibit high boiling points compared to alkanes and haloalkanes of comparable molecular mass due to intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
  • Lower aliphatic alcohols are completely miscible with water because they form hydrogen bonds with water molecules.

Preparation Methods of Alcohols

  • Hydration of alkenes occurs via Markovnikov's rule using dilute H2SO4, proceeding through a carbocation intermediate with potential rearrangement.
  • Reduction of aldehydes and ketones using LiAlH4 or NaBH4 yields primary and secondary alcohols respectively.
  • Reaction of Grignard reagents (RMgX) with formaldehyde yields primary alcohols, with other aldehydes yields secondary alcohols, and with ketones yields tertiary alcohols.

Chemical Reactions and Oxidation Behavior

  • Primary alcohols on oxidation with PCC (Pyridinium Chlorochromate) stop at the aldehyde stage, whereas KMnO4 or CrO3 oxidizes them directly to carboxylic acids.
  • Secondary alcohols are oxidized to ketones by Jones reagent (CrO3 in aqueous H2SO4), while tertiary alcohols are generally resistant to oxidation under mild conditions.
  • Dehydration of alcohols to form alkenes follows Saytsev's rule via an E1 mechanism for tertiary and E2 mechanism for primary alcohols using concentrated H2SO4 at elevated temperatures.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alcohols react with hydrogen halides to form alkyl halides.

Observation: Tertiary alcohols react instantaneously at room temperature via SN1, whereas primary alcohols require Lucas reagent (ZnCl2/HCl) and heating or proceed via SN2. — Stability of the intermediate carbocation dictates the mechanism; 3° carbocations are highly stable, while 1° carbocations are extremely unstable.

Exception Primary alcohols are oxidized to carboxylic acids by strong oxidizing agents.

Observation: Primary alcohols yield aldehydes without over-oxidation when treated with anhydrous reagents like PCC or Dess-Martin periodinane. — Anhydrous conditions prevent the formation of the gem-diol intermediate required for further oxidation to the carboxylic acid.

Exception Lower molecular weight alcohols are miscible with water in all proportions.

Observation: Branching in isomeric alcohols increases aqueous solubility compared to straight-chain isomers. — Branching decreases the hydrophobic surface area of the hydrocarbon chain relative to the hydrophilic hydroxyl group.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Methanol is historically known as wood spirit and is produced by the catalytic hydrogenation of carbon monoxide at 573-673 K under 200-300 atm pressure over a ZnO-Cr2O3 catalyst.
  • Ethanol forms an azeotropic mixture with water at 95.6% composition by mass, making absolute alcohol impossible to obtain by simple fractional distillation.
  • Denatured alcohol consists of industrial ethanol mixed with poisonous substances like methanol (pyridine) to render it unfit for human consumption.
  • The acidic strength of alcohols follows the order: Primary > Secondary > Tertiary due to the electron-releasing (+I effect) alkyl groups intensifying negative charge on oxygen.
  • Phenols are significantly more acidic than aliphatic alcohols because the phenoxide ion is stabilized by resonance, unlike the alkoxide ion.
  • Esterification of alcohols with carboxylic acids is reversible and is driven forward by removing water or using an excess of the alcohol or acid.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the oxidation products: writing that tertiary alcohols yield ketones or carboxylic acids upon oxidation with mild reagents (they do not react under mild conditions).
  • Assuming Lucas test works via an SN2 mechanism for all alcohols; tertiary alcohols strictly proceed via stable carbocation intermediates (SN1).
  • Forgetting that reduction of carbonyl compounds with NaBH4 does not reduce carboxylic acids or esters, unlike LiAlH4.
  • Overlooking rearrangements (carbocation shifts) during the acid-catalyzed dehydration of primary or secondary alcohols.

⚡ Quick Revision: Alcohols

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Nomenclature and Classification

Alcohols are classified as primary, secondary, or tertiary based on the number of carbon atoms attached directly to the carbinol carbon. IUPAC nomenclature replaces the terminal '-e' of the corresponding alkane with '-ol'.

Acidity and Hydrogen Bonding

The hydroxyl group imparts polarity, allowing extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding which elevates boiling points. Alcohols act as weak Brøsted acids, reacting with active metals like sodium to form alkoxides and hydrogen gas.

Nucleophilic Substitution and Cleavage

Reactions involving cleavage of the C-O bond occur via the conversion of the -OH group into a good leaving group (protonation or tosylation), followed by nucleophilic attack by halides or other nucleophiles.

Oxidation and Dehydration Pathways

Oxidation state changes depend on the number of hydrogen atoms on the carbinol carbon. Dehydration reactions yield alkenes via acid catalysis, adhering to Zaitsev's rule for regioselectivity.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Order of acidity: Primary > Secondary > Tertiary > Water.
  • Lucas reagent composition: Anhydrous ZnCl2 + concentrated HCl.
  • Jones reagent composition: CrO3 + aqueous H2SO4 in acetone.
  • PCC selectively oxidizes 1° alcohols to aldehydes without advancing to carboxylic acids.
  • Glycerol is a trihydric alcohol containing three hydroxyl groups on adjacent carbons.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alcohols form strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds, resulting in anomalously high boiling points.
  • Preparation involves hydration of alkenes, reduction of carbonyls, and Grignard synthesis.
  • Reactions involve either O-H bond cleavage (acidity, esterification) or C-O bond cleavage (substitution, dehydration).
  • Oxidation behavior effectively distinguishes between primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols.

⚖️ Key Differences

Hydration via Acid Catalysis VS Hydroboration-Oxidation

Acid-catalyzed hydration proceeds via carbocations (Markovnikov product with rearrangements), whereas hydroboration-oxidation is a concerted syn-addition yielding anti-Markovnikov products without rearrangements.

What is the principal organic product formed when propene is treated with diborane followed by alkaline hydrogen peroxide?

Explanation: Hydroboration-oxidation of alkenes results in anti-Markovnikov hydration, converting propene into propan-1-ol.

Question 4 of 18

Absolute alcohol is obtained from rectified spirit by:

Explanation: Rectified spirit (95.6% ethanol) forms an azeotrope. Adding benzene allows ternary azeotropic distillation to remove the remaining water, yielding absolute alcohol.

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following compounds will give a positive iodoform test?

Explanation: Butan-2-ol is a secondary alcohol containing the CH3-CH(OH)- group, which reacts with I2 and NaOH to yield yellow iodoform precipitate.

Question 6 of 18

The acidic strength of aliphatic alcohols decreases in the order:

Explanation: Alkyl groups exert electron-releasing (+I) effects. More alkyl groups in tertiary alcohols intensify negative charge on oxygen, destabilizing the alkoxide ion and decreasing acidity.

Question 7 of 18

Reaction of methylmagnesium bromide with formaldehyde followed by acid hydrolysis yields:

Explanation: Formaldehyde (HCHO) reacting with a Grignard reagent (CH3MgBr) followed by hydrolysis yields a primary alcohol with two carbons, which is ethanol.

Question 8 of 18

The boiling point of ethanol is much higher than that of methoxymethane (dimethyl ether) because:

Explanation: Ethanol molecules are held together by strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds due to the presence of the polar O-H group, whereas ethers lack O-H bonds.

Question 9 of 18

Dehydration of 2-methylpropan-2-ol with concentrated H2SO4 primarily proceeds via:

Explanation: Tertiary alcohols undergo acid-catalyzed dehydration via a stable tertiary carbocation intermediate, following the E1 mechanism.

Question 10 of 18

Phenol is more acidic than ethanol because:

Explanation: The negative charge on the oxygen atom in the phenoxide ion is delocalized into the benzene ring via resonance, stabilizing the conjugate base, whereas the ethoxide ion lacks resonance stabilization.

Question 11 of 18

Which reagent converts ethyl alcohol into ethylene?

Explanation: Concentrated H2SO4 at 443 K abstracts water from ethanol to produce ethene (ethylene) via intramolecular dehydration.

Question 12 of 18

The conversion of an alcohol to an ester using a carboxylic acid is known as:

Explanation: Fischer esterification involves heating a carboxylic acid with an alcohol in the presence of an acid catalyst to form an ester and water.

Question 13 of 18

Which of the following is a trihydric alcohol?

Explanation: Glycerol (propane-1,2,3-triol) contains three hydroxyl groups, classifying it as a trihydric alcohol.

Question 14 of 18

The IUPAC name of tertiary butyl alcohol is:

Explanation: (CH3)3COH has a longest carbon chain of three with a methyl group and a hydroxyl group at position 2, giving 2-methylpropan-2-ol.

Question 15 of 18

When vapours of a secondary alcohol are passed over heated copper at 573 K, the product is a:

Explanation: Catalytic dehydrogenation of secondary alcohols over copper at 573 K removes hydrogen to form ketones.

Question 16 of 18

Which alcohol does not undergo oxidation?

Explanation: 2-Methylpropan-2-ol is a tertiary alcohol lacking alpha-hydrogen atoms, making it resistant to mild oxidizing agents.

Question 17 of 18

In the esterification of alcohols, the oxygen atom of the formed ester originates from:

Explanation: Isotopic labeling studies confirm that the oxygen of the alcohol is retained in the ester molecule while the acid supplies the acyl carbon.

Question 18 of 18

Which of the following represents wood spirit?

Explanation: Methanol was historically obtained by the destructive distillation of wood, hence the common name wood spirit.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary product when 2-methylpropan-2-ol is treated with concentrated sulfuric acid at 413 K?

Solution: Acid-catalyzed dehydration of tertiary alcohols at high temperatures primarily yields alkenes via E1 elimination.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following describes the correct acidity order of alcohols?

Solution: Electron-donating alkyl groups increase electron density on the oxygen atom, destabilizing the alkoxide ion. Thus, primary alcohols are the most acidic among aliphatic alcohols.

Question 3 of 10
Hydroboration of alkenes followed by oxidation with alkaline H2O2 yields:

Solution: Hydroboration-oxidation adds water across the double bond in an anti-Markovnikov fashion with syn stereochemistry.

Question 4 of 10
Which test distinguishes between methanol and ethanol?

Solution: Ethanol gives a positive iodoform test due to the presence of the alpha-methyl group, whereas methanol lacks this group and does not react.

Question 5 of 10
Oxidation of a secondary alcohol with Jones reagent yields:

Solution: Secondary alcohols possess one hydrogen on the carbinol carbon, which upon oxidation yields a ketone.

Question 6 of 10
Absolute alcohol cannot be prepared by simple fractional distillation of rectified spirit because:

Solution: Rectified spirit forms a constant-boiling azeotrope at 95.6% ethanol and 4.4% water, preventing separation by simple distillation.

Question 7 of 10
Reaction of phenylmagnesium bromide with acetone followed by acidic hydrolysis gives:

Solution: Grignard reagent addition to acetone (a ketone) forms a tertiary alcohol, specifically 2-phenylpropan-2-ol after workup.

Question 8 of 10
The C-O bond length in alcohols is approximately:

Solution: The single covalent bond between carbon and oxygen in alcohols measures approximately 142 pm.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following compounds is a dihydric alcohol?

Solution: Ethylene glycol (ethane-1,2-diol) contains two hydroxyl groups, making it a dihydric alcohol.

Question 10 of 10
What is the byproduct formed when an alcohol reacts with metallic sodium?

Solution: Alcohols react with active metals like sodium to form sodium alkoxides and liberate hydrogen gas.
