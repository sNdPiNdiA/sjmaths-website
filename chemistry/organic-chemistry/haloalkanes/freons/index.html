Organic Chemistry

Freons

Freons are stable, non-toxic, and non-flammable chlorofluorocarbon (CFC) derivatives of methane and ethane used primarily as refrigerants and propellants. They undergo homolytic cleavage in the stratosphere via ultraviolet radiation to release chlorine free radicals that catalytically destroy ozone.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthesis and Nomenclature of Freons

  • Freons are manufactured by the Swarts reaction, treating polyhalogenated alkanes with anhydrous antimony trifluoride in the presence of antimony pentachloride.
  • The general formula Freon-C is derived using the convention (x-1)(y+1)(w) where x is carbon atoms, y is hydrogen atoms, and w is fluorine atoms.
  • Freon-12 (CF2Cl2) contains one carbon atom, zero hydrogen atoms, and two fluorine atoms.
  • They are thermally stable, chemically inert under normal conditions, odorless, and liquefy easily under pressure.

Atmospheric Impact and Ozone Depletion Mechanism

  • Due to high chemical inertness, Freons do not react in the troposphere and slowly diffuse into the stratosphere without degradation.
  • In the stratosphere, high-energy UV radiation breaks C-Cl bonds homolytically to release reactive chlorine free radicals.
  • A single chlorine free radical can destroy up to 100,000 ozone molecules via a self-sustaining catalytic propagation cycle.
  • The formation of the ozone hole over Antarctica is heavily accelerated by these heterogeneous reactions on polar stratospheric cloud surfaces.

Environmental Regulation and Alternatives

  • The Montreal Protocol mandated the phased-out production and consumption of ozone-depleting chlorofluorocarbons globally.
  • Hydrochlorofluorocarbons (HCFCs) such as HCFC-22 contain C-H bonds that undergo premature degradation by tropospheric hydroxyl radicals.
  • Hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs) contain no chlorine atoms, completely eliminating ozone depletion potential (ODP), though many retain high global warming potential (GWP).
  • Modern refrigeration systems increasingly adopt natural refrigerants such as ammonia, hydrocarbons, and carbon dioxide to replace synthetic halocarbons.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aliphatic halogen derivatives are generally reactive toward nucleophiles and bases.

Observation: Freons are remarkably unreactive toward acids, bases, and oxidizing agents under normal tropospheric conditions. — The extremely high strength of multiple C-F and C-Cl bonds and the steric shielding by fluorine atoms prevent standard nucleophilic substitution.

Exception Lower haloalkanes readily dissolve in water due to dipole-dipole interactions.

Observation: Freons are practically insoluble in water. — Their symmetrical non-polar or weakly polar molecular geometry prevents effective hydrogen bonding with polar water molecules.

Exception Halogenated compounds containing hydrogen are flammable.

Observation: Freons such as CF2Cl2 are completely non-flammable. — High fluorine content and lack of active hydrogen atoms inhibit combustion chain reactions.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Freon Number to Formula Conversion
Add 90 to the Freon number to get XYZ (X=C, Y=H, Z=F); remaining valencies filled by Cl.

Application: For Freon-12: 12 + 90 = 102 → 1 Carbon, 0 Hydrogen, 2 Fluorine → C1H0F2Cl2 (CF2Cl2).

Formula to Freon Number Calculation
For CxHyClzFw, use (x-1)(y+1)(w).

Application: For CFCl3 (x=1, y=0, w=1): (1-1)(0+1)(1) = 011 → Freon-11.

Ozone Depletion Catalytic Rule
1 Chlorine atom = 105 Ozone molecules destroyed.

Application: Useful for quantitative reasoning questions regarding radical longevity in the stratosphere.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Freons are chemically designated as chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).
  • Freon-12 is chemically known as dichlorodifluoromethane (CF2Cl2).
  • The Swarts reaction utilizes anhydrous SbF3 to replace chlorine with fluorine.
  • Freons undergo homolytic bond cleavage in the stratosphere upon absorbing UV radiation.
  • Chlorine free radicals act as homogeneous catalysts in ozone destruction.
  • The Montreal Protocol regulates the phase-out of ozone-depleting substances.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Freon nomenclature digits; remember the formula is (C-1)(H+1)(F), not just direct atomic counts.
  • Assuming Freons destroy ozone in the troposphere; destruction occurs exclusively in the stratosphere.
  • Believing HCFCs have zero ozone depletion potential; they possess low ODP, not zero ODP.
  • Forgetting that HFCs contain no chlorine atoms and therefore have zero ODP.

⚡ Quick Revision: Freons

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Synthesis via Swarts Reaction

Alkyl chlorides or bromides are treated with metallic fluorides like SbF3 or CoF3 to yield stable chlorofluorocarbons.

Physical Properties

Extremely stable, non-toxic, non-corrosive, and easily liquefiable gases used extensively in cooling systems.

Stratospheric Photolysis

UV photolysis of Freons releases active Cl• radicals which initiate the catalytic breakdown of ozone.

Alternative Refrigerants

Transitioning to HCFCs, HFCs, and natural refrigerants to comply with international environmental protection treaties.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

CFC (Chlorofluorocarbon)
Synthetic alkane derivative containing chlorine and fluorine.
HCFC (Hydrochlorofluorocarbon)
Transition compound containing hydrogen, chlorine, fluorine, and carbon.
HFC (Hydrofluorocarbon)
Compound containing hydrogen, fluorine, and carbon with zero ODP.
Swarts Reaction (Halogen Exchange)
Method for preparing alkyl fluorides using metal fluorides.
ODP (Ozone Depletion Potential)
Relative measure of ozone destruction ability compared to CFC-11.
Stratosphere (Atmospheric Layer)
Atmospheric layer between 10 km and 50 km altitude where ozone layer resides.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Freon-12 formula is CF2Cl2.
  • Freon-11 formula is CFCl3.
  • CFCs are inert in the troposphere but active in the stratosphere.
  • Chlorine free radical is regenerated in the ozone destruction cycle.
  • Montreal Protocol deals with ozone-depleting substances.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Freons are stable CFC refrigerants synthesized by the Swarts reaction.
  • Their accumulation in the stratosphere leads to catalytic destruction of ozone via chlorine radicals.
  • International protocols restrict CFC use in favor of HCFCs and HFCs.
  • Freon nomenclature follows the (x-1)(y+1)(w) numerical code system.

⚖️ Key Differences

CFCs VS HFCs

CFCs contain chlorine and deplete ozone (high ODP), whereas HFCs contain no chlorine and have zero ODP.
