Chemical Properties of Haloalkanes

Haloalkanes undergo nucleophilic substitution, elimination, and metal-mediated reactions due to the polarization of the carbon-halogen bond. The presence of an electronegative halogen atom renders the adjacent carbon electrophilic, facilitating various substitution pathways.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Nucleophilic Substitution Reactions (SN1 and SN2)

  • SN2 mechanism proceeds via a single concerted step involving a pentavalent transition state with complete inversion of configuration (Walden inversion).
  • SN1 mechanism proceeds via a two-step carbocation intermediate, leading to racemization due to planar geometry of the carbocation.
  • Reactivity order for SN2 is Primary > Secondary > Tertiary due to steric hindrance.
  • Reactivity order for SN1 is Tertiary > Secondary > Primary due to carbocation stability provided by hyperconjugation and inductive effects.

Elimination Reactions (E1 and E2)

  • E2 mechanism is a concerted bimolecular elimination requiring anti-periplanar geometry of the proton and the leaving group.
  • E1 mechanism involves unimolecular formation of a carbocation followed by rapid proton abstraction by a weak base.
  • Zaitsev's rule dictates that elimination predominantly yields the most stable, highly substituted and thermodynamically favored alkene.
  • Bulky bases such as potassium tert-butoxide yield Hofmann (less substituted) alkenes due to steric hindrance preventing abstraction of internal protons.

Reaction with Metals and Organometallic Synthesis

  • Reaction of haloalkanes with magnesium metal in dry ether yields Grignard reagents (R-Mg-X), which act as strong carbon nucleophiles and bases.
  • Wurtz reaction couples two molecules of alkyl halide using sodium metal in dry ether to form symmetrical higher alkanes.
  • Haloalkanes react with metallic lithium to produce organolithium compounds (R-Li), utilized in Corey-House synthesis.
  • Finkelstein reaction converts alkyl chlorides or bromides into alkyl iodides using sodium iodide in acetone via an SN2 process.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl halides generally undergo nucleophilic substitution readily.

Observation: Vinylic and aryl halides are extremely unreactive toward SN1 and SN2 substitutions. — Partial double bond character due to resonance and sp2 hybridization of carbon-halogen bond increase bond strength.

Exception Tertiary alkyl halides exclusively undergo SN1 and E1 reactions.

Observation: Tertiary alkyl halides undergo rapid SN2 substitution with strong nucleophiles under high pressure or specific gas-phase conditions. — Absence of solvent stabilization can occasionally bypass carbocation formation.

Exception Finkelstein reaction uses NaI in acetone.

Observation: Fluorination of haloalkanes cannot be achieved directly by Finkelstein reaction. — Swarts reaction using metal fluorides like AgF or Hg2F2 is required due to high lattice energy of NaF.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
SN Order Inversion Mnemonic
SN2 = 1>2>3 (Steric 2 fast); SN1 = 3>2>1 (Stable 1 carbocation)

Application: Quickly identify reaction rates by remembering that SN2 loves 1° (less crowded) while SN1 loves 3° (stable carbocation).

Ambient vs. Nitrite Ambident Nucleophiles
K-CN → R-CN (C-bonded); Ag-CN → R-NC (N-bonded)

Application: Ionic KCN gives cyanides due to carbon attack, whereas covalent AgCN gives isocyanides due to silver-halogen affinity driving nitrogen attack.

Elimination vs Substitution Predictor
Strong base/heat = Elimination (E2/E1); Weak base/cold = Substitution (SN2/SN1)

Application: Look at reagent conditions: OH-/heat favors alkene formation, while OH-/cold favors alcohol formation.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The C-X bond length increases in the order: C-F < C-Cl < C-Br < C-I.
  • The C-X bond dipole moment decreases from C-F to C-I due to decreasing electronegativity.
  • Aqueous KOH converts alkyl halides to alcohols via nucleophilic substitution.
  • Alcoholic KOH promotes dehydrohalogenation to yield alkenes.
  • Grignard reagents must be prepared under strictly anhydrous conditions to prevent protonolysis.
  • Allylic and benzylic halides show enhanced reactivity toward both SN1 and SN2 reactions due to resonance stabilization.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the role of aqueous KOH (substitution) with alcoholic KOH (elimination).
  • Assuming alkyl fluorides can be prepared by standard halogen exchange methods like Finkelstein reaction.
  • Forgetting that carbocation rearrangements (1,2-hydride or 1,2-methyl shifts) occur during SN1 and E1 reactions.
  • Writing retention of configuration for SN2 mechanisms instead of complete inversion.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Properties of Haloalkanes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Nucleophilic Substitution Pathways

Alkyl halides undergo substitution via SN1 or SN2 pathways depending on substrate structure, nucleophile strength, and solvent polarity.

Elimination Competitions

Dehydrohalogenation follows Zaitsev's rule under E2 or E1 conditions, producing substituted alkenes as primary products.

Organometallic Derivatives

Reaction with magnesium yields reactive Grignard reagents, serving as vital intermediates for carbon-carbon bond formation.

Stereochemical Outcomes

SN2 reactions cause inversion of configuration, whereas SN1 reactions lead to racemic mixtures through planar carbocation intermediates.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Walden Inversion (Stereochemical inversion)
Spatial inversion of chiral carbon center during SN2 attack.
Zaitsev Rule (Elimination rule)
Major alkene has maximum alkyl substituents on double-bonded carbons.
Ambident Nucleophile (Dual-site nucleophile)
Nucleophile with two reactive sites (e.g., CN-, NO2-).
Grignard Reagent (R-Mg-X)
Organomagnesium halide used for nucleophilic alkyl additions.
Racemization (Equal enantiomer mix)
Formation of equal amounts of d and l isomers resulting in optical inactivity.
Finkelstein Reaction (Halogen exchange)
Conversion of R-Cl/R-Br to R-I using NaI in acetone.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • SN2 rate depends on steric hindrance: 1° > 2° > 3°.
  • SN1 rate depends on carbocation stability: 3° > 2° > 1°.
  • Polar aprotic solvents accelerate SN2 reactions.
  • Polar protic solvents stabilize carbocations, accelerating SN1 reactions.
  • Grignard reagents react with active hydrogen compounds to form alkanes.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Haloalkanes possess polarized C-X bonds driving substitution and elimination.
  • SN2 is concerted and stereospecific with inversion.
  • SN1 proceeds via carbocation with racemization.
  • Elimination yields alkenes following Zaitsev's rule.

⚖️ Key Differences

SN1 Mechanism VS SN2 Mechanism

SN1 is unimolecular with carbocation intermediate; SN2 is bimolecular and concerted.

Question 8 of 18

Explanation: Dry ether is used as a solvent in the Wurtz reaction to prevent violent reaction of sodium metal with water.

Question 9 of 18

What stereochemical outcome is observed in SN2 reactions at a chiral center?

Explanation: Nucleophilic attack from the backside relative to the leaving group results in Walden inversion.

Question 10 of 18

Which reagent is used in the Swarts reaction?

Explanation: Swarts reaction specifically uses metallic fluorides like AgF, Hg2F2, or CoF2 to synthesize alkyl fluorides.

Question 11 of 18

Why are aryl halides less reactive towards nucleophilic substitution compared to alkyl halides?

Explanation: Resonance delocalization of lone pairs from the halogen into the benzene ring imparts partial double bond character to the C-X bond.

Question 12 of 18

Reaction of alkyl halide with silver nitrite (AgNO2) primarily yields:

Explanation: AgNO2 is predominantly covalent, leading to attack via nitrogen to form nitroalkanes as major products.

Question 13 of 18

Which alkyl halide reacts most rapidly with aqueous NaOH?

Explanation: Carbon-iodine bond is the weakest due to large size and low bond dissociation enthalpy, making iodide the best leaving group.

Question 14 of 18

The order of reactivities of haloalkanes in SN2 reactions is:

Explanation: Steric hindrance around the reaction center progressively increases from primary to tertiary alkyl halides, slowing down SN2 attack.

Question 15 of 18

Explanation: Finkelstein reaction replaces chlorine or bromine with iodine via an SN2 nucleophilic substitution pathway.

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following compounds gives a racemic mixture on hydrolysis with aqueous NaOH?

Explanation: Chiral secondary alkyl halides like 2-chlorobutane undergo SN1 hydrolysis via a planar carbocation, resulting in racemization.

Question 17 of 18

What is formed when chloroform is treated with primary amine and alcoholic KOH?

Explanation: Carbylamine test involves reaction of primary amine with chloroform and base to yield foul-smelling alkyl isocyanides.

Question 18 of 18

Which catalyst is used in the Friedel-Crafts alkylation of benzene using alkyl chloride?

Explanation: Anhydrous aluminum chloride acts as a Lewis acid catalyst to generate electrophilic carbocation species from alkyl halides.
