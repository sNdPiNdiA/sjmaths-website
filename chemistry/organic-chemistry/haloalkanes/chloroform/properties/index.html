Properties of Chloroform

Chloroform (CHCl3) is a dense, colorless, sweet-smelling trihalomethane synthesized via chlorination of methane or ethanol. It undergoes base-catalyzed hydrolysis and radical oxidation to yield toxic phosgene gas in the presence of oxygen.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Physical Properties and Stabilization

  • Chloroform is a dense, heavy liquid with a boiling point of 334 K and a characteristic sweet smell.
  • It is sparingly soluble in water but completely miscible with organic solvents like ethanol and diethyl ether.
  • To prevent oxidation to toxic phosgene gas, 1% ethanol is added as a stabilizer which acts as a radical scavenger.
  • It must be stored in dark, tightly closed amber bottles filled to the brim to exclude air and sunlight.

Chemical Reactions and Electrophilic Addition

  • In the presence of a strong base, chloroform undergoes alpha-elimination to generate the electrophilic intermediate dichlorocarbene (:CCl2).
  • The carbylamine reaction converts primary amines into foul-smelling isocyanides using chloroform and alcoholic KOH.
  • The Reimer-Tiemann reaction introduces a formyl group (-CHO) ortho to the hydroxyl group in phenols via dichlorocarbene attack.
  • Nitration with concentrated nitric acid yields chloropicrin (trichloronitromethane), which is used as an insecticide and tear gas.

Reactivity and Acidic Nature

  • The presence of three electronegative chlorine atoms makes the C-H bond in chloroform weakly acidic.
  • Reaction with aqueous sodium hydroxide converts chloroform to sodium formate via an unstable gem-triol intermediate.
  • Reduction with zinc and hydrochloric acid yields dichloromethane, while reduction with zinc and aqueous alkali yields methane.
  • Heating with primary aromatic amines in basic medium forms isocyanides, serving as a specific functional test for primary amines.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl halides do not typically undergo base-induced alpha-elimination easily.

Observation: Chloroform readily loses HCl under basic conditions to form dichlorocarbene. — Strong -I effect of three chlorine atoms stabilizes the negative charge on the conjugate base carbon, facilitating proton abstraction.

Exception Gem-diols and triols are generally unstable and eliminate water immediately.

Observation: The intermediate formed during alkaline hydrolysis of chloroform is a trichloromethanol derivative. — Stabilizing polar interactions or subsequent rapid elimination leads directly to formic acid derivatives without isolating the stable hydrate.

Exception Primary amines react with nitrous acid or alkyl halides via substitution.

Observation: Primary amines react with chloroform and base to yield foul-smelling isocyanides. — Generated dichlorocarbene acts as an electrophile attacking the nitrogen center followed by base-promoted elimination.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Chloroform has a density greater than water (approx. 1.49 g/cm3).
  • It is stabilized against oxidation by the addition of 1% ethanol.
  • Oxidation of chloroform produces highly toxic carbonyl chloride (phosgene).
  • It reacts with primary amines and alcoholic KOH to give a foul-smelling isocyanide test.
  • The electrophile in the Reimer-Tiemann reaction involving chloroform is dichlorocarbene.
  • Nitration of chloroform yields chloropicrin (CCl3NO2).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting that ethanol is added as a preservative, not as a reactant in synthesis.
  • Confusing the electrophile in Reimer-Tiemann reaction as a carbocation instead of dichlorocarbene.
  • Assuming chloroform is soluble and lighter than water.
  • Neglecting the role of sunlight in initiating the oxidation of chloroform to phosgene.

⚡ Quick Revision: Properties of Chloroform

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

Chloroform features an sp3 hybridized carbon bonded to one hydrogen and three chlorine atoms, imparting a tetrahedral geometry and a dipole moment of 1.01 D.

Stability and Storage

Susceptible to photo-oxidation by atmospheric oxygen to form phosgene gas, mitigated by storing in amber bottles with ethanol stabilizer.

Carbene Chemistry

Acts as a precursor for dichlorocarbene in strong base, driving synthetic transformations like the Reimer-Tiemann and carbylamine reactions.

Acidity of C-H Bond

Strong electron-withdrawing inductive effects of three chlorine atoms polarize the C-H bond, making it weakly acidic towards strong bases.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Formula: CHCl3
  • Stabilizer: 1% Ethanol
  • Oxidation product: Phosgene (COCl2)
  • Reactive intermediate: Dichlorocarbene (:CCl2)
  • Test reaction: Carbylamine reaction for 1° amines

📌 Quick Recall

  • Chloroform is a heavy, sweet-smelling liquid trihalomethane.
  • It undergoes rapid oxidation to phosgene in the presence of light and air.
  • Base treatment generates dichlorocarbene for formylation and carbylamine syntheses.
  • It exhibits weak carbon acidity due to the electron-withdrawing chlorine atoms.

⚖️ Key Differences

Reimer-Tiemann Reaction VS Kolbe's Reaction

Reimer-Tiemann uses chloroform and alkali to form hydroxybenzaldehyde, whereas Kolbe's uses CO2 and sodium phenoxide to form salicylic acid.

Question 1 of 19

What is added to chloroform as a preservative to prevent the formation of phosgene?

Explanation: 1% ethanol is added to chloroform to act as a negative catalyst and radical scavenger, converting any formed phosgene into harmless diethyl carbonate.

Question 2 of 19

Which reactive intermediate is formed when chloroform reacts with sodium hydroxide?

Explanation: Base-induced alpha-elimination of HCl from chloroform yields neutral, electrophilic dichlorocarbene (:CCl2).

Question 3 of 19

The oxidation of chloroform in the presence of sunlight and air yields:

Explanation: Chloroform is oxidized by atmospheric oxygen under UV light to produce highly toxic carbonyl chloride (phosgene).

Question 4 of 19

The carbylamine test is used for the detection of:

Explanation: Primary amines react with chloroform and alcoholic KOH to form foul-smelling isocyanides, which is characteristic of the carbylamine test.

Question 5 of 19

What is the product formed when phenol reacts with chloroform in the presence of aqueous NaOH?

Explanation: This is the Reimer-Tiemann reaction, which introduces an -CHO group ortho to the hydroxyl group in phenol, yielding salicylaldehyde.

Question 6 of 19

What is the IUPAC name of chloropicrin?

Explanation: Chloropicrin is formed by the nitration of chloroform and is chemically known as trichloronitromethane (CCl3NO2).

Question 7 of 19

Chloroform is heavier than water because:

Explanation: The presence of three heavy chlorine atoms gives chloroform a high molecular weight and density (1.49 g/cm3), making it heavier than water.

Question 8 of 19

Which reducing agent converts chloroform to methane?

Explanation: Reduction of chloroform with zinc dust and aqueous sodium hydroxide or water yields methane, whereas Zn/HCl yields dichloromethane.

Question 9 of 19

What is the geometry of the chloroform molecule?

Explanation: The central carbon atom is sp3 hybridized, resulting in a tetrahedral geometry with bond angles close to 109.5°.

Question 10 of 19

Why is chloroform stored in dark amber bottles?

Explanation: Dark amber bottles cut off light rays that catalyze the oxidation of chloroform into toxic phosgene gas.

Question 11 of 19

Which of the following describes the acidic character of chloroform?

Explanation: The strong electron-withdrawing inductive effect (-I) of three chlorine atoms makes the C-H bond weakly acidic, allowing proton abstraction by strong bases.

Question 12 of 19

Reaction of chloroform with aqueous KOH yields primarily:

Explanation: Alkaline hydrolysis of chloroform replaces chlorines with hydroxyl groups to form an unstable intermediate that eliminates water to yield sodium formate.

Question 13 of 19

What is the electrophile in the Reimer-Tiemann reaction?

Explanation: Dichlorocarbene (:CCl2) acts as the electron-deficient electrophile attacking the phenoxide ion in the Reimer-Tiemann reaction.

Question 14 of 19

Which halogen derivative is formed when chloroform is treated with chlorine gas in sunlight?

Explanation: Free radical chlorination of chloroform replaces the remaining hydrogen atom, yielding carbon tetrachloride (CCl4).

Question 15 of 19

What is the physical state of chloroform at room temperature?

Explanation: Chloroform exists as a volatile, dense, colorless liquid at standard temperature and pressure.

Question 16 of 19

Which compound is formed when chloroform reacts with primary amine and alcoholic KOH?

Explanation: The carbylamine reaction yields alkyl or aryl isocyanides (carbylamines), identifiable by their intensely foul odor.

Question 17 of 19

What is the role of ethanol when mixed with chloroform?

Explanation: Ethanol acts as an antioxidant and radical scavenger, reacting with phosgene to form harmless ethyl carbonate.

Question 18 of 19

Which test confirms the presence of chloroform when warmed with primary amine and alkali?

Explanation: The carbylamine reaction produces a characteristically foul-smelling carbylamine (isocyanide).

Question 19 of 19

In the mechanism of chloroform's base-catalyzed halogenation or elimination, the first step is:

Explanation: The strong base abstracts the acidic proton from chloroform to form a trichloromethyl carbanion, which then expels a chloride ion to form dichlorocarbene.
