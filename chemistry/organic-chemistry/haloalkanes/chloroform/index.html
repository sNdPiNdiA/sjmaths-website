Organic Chemistry

Chloroform

Chloroform (trichloromethane, CHCl3) is a dense, colorless haloalkane synthesized via the halogenation of methane or ethanol with chlorine. It undergoes base hydrolysis, oxidation to phosgene, and carbonylation reactions under specific conditions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation and Physical Properties

  • Chloroform is industrially prepared by the reduction of carbon tetrachloride or by the chlorination of methane or ethanol.
  • It is a dense, sweet-smelling, non-flammable liquid with a boiling point of 61.2°C.
  • It is sparingly soluble in water but miscible with organic solvents like ethanol and diethyl ether.
  • Due to the presence of three electron-withdrawing chlorine atoms, the alpha-hydrogen of chloroform is distinctly acidic (pKa ≈ 15.7).

Chemical Reactions and Synthetic Applications

  • Reaction with aqueous sodium hydroxide converts chloroform to sodium formate via intermediate dichlorocarbene formation.
  • The Carbylamine reaction is used as a specific chemical test for primary aliphatic and aromatic amines, yielding foul-smelling isocyanides.
  • The Reimer-Tiemann reaction introduces a formyl group (-CHO) at the ortho position of phenols to yield phenolic aldehydes.
  • Nitration of chloroform with concentrated nitric acid yields chloropicrin (CCl3NO2), a tear gas and insecticide.

Oxidation and Storage Precautions

  • Chloroform slowly undergoes auto-oxidation by atmospheric oxygen in the presence of UV light to form extremely toxic carbonyl chloride (phosgene).
  • To prevent phosgene toxicity, chloroform is stored in dark, amber-colored bottles filled to the brim.
  • 1% to 2% ethanol is routinely added as a stabilizer to chloroform to react with any formed phosgene, converting it into harmless diethyl carbonate.
  • Phosgene presence is tested using potassium carbonate paper or aniline, which gives characteristic precipitation or color changes.

Conditions: Zn/HCl or Zn/H2O reduction
Units: Converts to methane

Conditions: Tetrahedral geometry around carbon atom
Units: Molar Mass: 119.38 g/mol
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Haloalkanes generally undergo straightforward nucleophilic substitution with aqueous bases.

Observation: Chloroform does not form a stable alcohol (trichloromethanol) upon treatment with aqueous alkali. — Elimination of a water molecule from unstable trichloromethanol instantly yields reactive dichlorocarbene (:CCl2).

Exception Alkyl halides do not typically exhibit marked C-H acidity.

Observation: Chloroform exhibits pronounced C-H bond acidity. — Strong inductive (-I) effect of three chlorine atoms stabilizes the resulting carbanion intermediate.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Carbylamine Carbon Source Mnemonic
CHCl3 = C1 + RNH2 → RNC

Application: Remember that the central carbon of the isocyanide (-NC) in the carbylamine test originates entirely from chloroform.

Phosgene Stabilizer Trick
Ethanol (EtOH) + Phosgene (COCl2) → Diethyl Carbonate

Application: Always select ethanol as the correct preservative option in multiple-choice questions regarding chloroform storage.

Reimer-Tiemann Electrophile
CHCl3 + OH- → :CCl2 + H2O + Cl-

Application: Identify the actual electrophile in the Reimer-Tiemann reaction as dichlorocarbene, not the chloroform molecule directly.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Chloroform chemical formula is CHCl3 with a molecular weight of 119.38 g/mol.
  • Ethanol is added as a preservative to chloroform to scavenge toxic phosgene.
  • Dichlorocarbene (:CCl2) acts as the key intermediate in both Carbylamine and Reimer-Tiemann reactions.
  • Chloropicrin (CCl3NO2) is synthesized by the nitration of chloroform.
  • Reaction of chloroform with primary amines yields foul-smelling alkyl isocyanides.
  • Chloroform vapors act as a powerful anesthetic historically, but its hepatotoxicity restricted its medical utility.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming chloroform gives a simple substitution product with NaOH instead of forming dichlorocarbene.
  • Forgetting that secondary and tertiary amines do not give a positive Carbylamine test.
  • Overlooking the necessity of dark-colored bottles and stabilizing agents for safe chloroform storage.
  • Confusing chloropicrin (CCl3NO2) with chloroprene or chloral hydrate.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chloroform

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Acidity

Chloroform is a haloalkane with a tetrahedral geometry where three electronegative chlorine atoms induce significant C-H polarization and acidity.

Carbene Generation

Base treatment abstracts the acidic proton, prompting chloride expulsion to generate the electrophilic divalent carbon species, dichlorocarbene.

Name Reactions

Chloroform serves as the source of carbon in synthetic name reactions including the Reimer-Tiemann and Carbylamine reactions.

Oxidation and Stabilization

Atmospheric oxygen and light oxidize chloroform to phosgene, necessitating the addition of ethanol stabilizer during storage.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Formula: CHCl3
  • Oxidation product: COCl2
  • Stabilizer: Ethanol
  • Electrophile: :CCl2
  • Nitration product: CCl3NO2

📌 Quick Recall

  • Chloroform is a halogenated methane derivative with acidic alpha-hydrogens.
  • It reacts with primary amines to produce foul-smelling isocyanides.
  • It reacts with phenol in basic medium to yield salicylaldehyde.
  • It requires strict storage conditions with ethanol to prevent phosgene poisoning.

⚖️ Key Differences

Chloral Hydrate VS Chloroform

Chloral hydrate is CCl3CH(OH)2 used as a sedative, whereas chloroform is CHCl3.

Question 1 of 18

What is the IUPAC name of chloroform?

Explanation: Chloroform contains three chlorine atoms attached to a single carbon, making its IUPAC name trichloromethane.

Question 2 of 18

Which substance is added to chloroform as a stabilizer to prevent phosgene formation?

Explanation: 1-2% ethanol is added to chloroform to convert any generated phosgene into non-toxic diethyl carbonate.

Question 3 of 18

The intermediate formed when chloroform reacts with aqueous base is:

Explanation: Base abstraction of the proton followed by chloride ion loss yields dichlorocarbene (:CCl2).

Question 4 of 18

Which reaction is used to distinguish primary amines from secondary and tertiary amines?

Explanation: Primary amines react with chloroform and KOH to form foul-smelling isocyanides in the Carbylamine reaction.

Question 5 of 18

What is the product of the reaction between phenol, chloroform, and sodium hydroxide?

Explanation: The Reimer-Tiemann reaction introduces an aldehyde group ortho to the hydroxyl group in phenol, forming salicylaldehyde.

Question 6 of 18

What toxic gas is produced when chloroform is exposed to air and sunlight?

Explanation: Oxidation of chloroform by oxygen in the presence of light yields carbonyl chloride, commonly known as phosgene.

Question 7 of 18

What is formed when chloroform is treated with concentrated nitric acid?

Explanation: Nitration of chloroform yields trichloronitromethane, known as chloropicrin, which functions as a tear gas.

Question 8 of 18

Why does chloroform exhibit acidity at its C-H bond?

Explanation: The three chlorine atoms exert a strong inductive (-I) effect, withdrawing electrons and stabilizing the conjugate base carbanion.

Question 9 of 18

Reduction of chloroform with zinc and hydrochloric acid yields:

Explanation: Complete reduction of chloroform by Zn/HCl replaces all chlorine atoms with hydrogen, yielding methane.

Question 10 of 18

What is the geometry around the central carbon atom in chloroform?

Explanation: With four single covalent bonds (one C-H and three C-Cl), chloroform adopts a tetrahedral geometry with sp3 hybridization.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following statements regarding chloroform is incorrect?

Explanation: Chloroform is only sparingly soluble in water; it is not completely miscible.

Question 12 of 18

In the Carbylamine reaction, what is the functional group of the final product?

Explanation: Primary amines react to form alkyl or aryl isocyanides (carbylamines), characterized by an extremely foul odor.

Question 13 of 18

How should chloroform be stored in a laboratory?

Explanation: Dark amber bottles filled to the brim prevent exposure to UV light and oxygen, stopping phosgene formation.

Question 14 of 18

What is the chemical formula of phosgene?

Explanation: Phosgene is carbonyl chloride with the chemical formula COCl2.

Question 15 of 18

Which test confirms the presence of phosgene in aged chloroform?

Explanation: Aniline reacts with phosgene to form diphenylurea, serving as a confirmatory test.

Question 16 of 18

What is the state of hybridization of the central carbon in dichlorocarbene?

Explanation: Dichlorocarbene (:CCl2) has three electron domains around carbon (two sigma bonds and one lone pair), corresponding to sp2 hybridization.

Question 17 of 18

Which reagent converts chloroform into carbon tetrachloride?

Explanation: Free radical chlorination of chloroform with Cl2 in the presence of sunlight replaces the remaining hydrogen with chlorine.

Question 18 of 18

What is the approximate boiling point of pure chloroform?

Explanation: Pure chloroform boils at approximately 61.2°C under normal atmospheric pressure.
