Preparation and Properties of Alkyl Halides

Alkyl halides are aliphatic halogen derivatives of alkanes characterized by a polar carbon-halogen bond. Their preparation involves substitution and addition reactions, while their chemical reactivity is dominated by nucleophilic substitution and elimination pathways.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Methods of Preparation

  • Halogenation of Alkanes: Free radical chlorination or bromination of alkanes yields a mixture of isomeric haloalkanes.
  • Alcohol Conversion: Alcohols react with halogen acids (HX), phosphorus halides (PX3, PX5), or thionyl chloride (SOCl2) to form haloalkanes, with SOCl2 being preferred due to gaseous byproducts.
  • Hunsdiecker Reaction: Silver salts of carboxylic acids react with bromine in carbon tetrachloride to yield alkyl bromides with the loss of carbon dioxide.
  • Finkelstein and Swarts Reactions: Halogen exchange methods where alkyl iodides are prepared using NaI in acetone (Finkelstein), and alkyl fluorides using metal fluorides like AgF or Hg2F2 (Swarts).

Nucleophilic Substitution Reactions

  • SN2 Mechanism: Proceeds via a concerted single-step pathway with complete inversion of configuration (Walden inversion) and follows second-order kinetics.
  • SN1 Mechanism: Proceeds through a planar carbocation intermediate via a two-step pathway, resulting in racemization and following first-order kinetics.
  • Substrate Reactivity Order: For SN2, reactivity decreases as 3° < 2° < 1° due to steric hindrance; for SN1, reactivity follows 3° > 2° > 1° due to carbocation stability.
  • Leaving Group Ability: Reactivity scales inversely with basicity of the leaving group: I- > Br- > Cl- >> F-.

Elimination and Metal Reactions

  • β-Elimination (Dehydrohalogenation): Treatment of alkyl halides with alcoholic KOH yields alkenes predominantly via the Saytzeff rule, forming the more substituted and stable alkene.
  • Grignard Reagents: Alkyl halides react with magnesium metal in dry ether to form organometallic compounds which act as strong nucleophiles and bases.
  • Reduction Reactions: Haloalkanes are reduced to alkanes using catalytic hydrogenation, metal-acid combinations (Zn/HCl), or complex metal hydrides (LiAlH4).

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Tertiary alkyl halides undergo SN1 reactions exclusively.

Observation: Tertiary alkyl halides with bridgehead structures (e.g., 1-bromoadamantane) fail to undergo SN1 reactions. — Bridgehead carbocations cannot attain the required planar sp2 hybridization due to rigid ring strain.

Exception Vinyl and aryl halides are highly reactive towards nucleophilic substitution.

Observation: Vinyl and aryl halides are extremely unreactive toward SN1 and SN2 reactions under normal conditions. — Partial double bond character due to resonance stabilization and sp2 hybridized carbon-halogen bond.

Exception Alkyl fluorides are readily prepared by direct halogenation of alkanes.

Observation: Direct fluorination of alkanes is violently explosive and uncontrollable. — Fluorination is highly exothermic, requiring indirect methods like the Swarts reaction.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Thionyl chloride (SOCl2) is the preferred reagent for converting alcohols to alkyl chlorides because gaseous byproducts (SO2 and HCl) escape, leaving pure alkyl chloride.
  • The order of leaving group ability in nucleophilic substitution is I- > Br- > Cl- > F-.
  • Allylic and benzylic halides show enhanced reactivity toward both SN1 and SN2 reactions due to resonance stabilization of the carbocation and transition state, respectively.
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation involves alkyl halides and anhydrous AlCl3 to introduce alkyl groups onto an aromatic ring.
  • Wurtz reaction fails for the synthesis of asymmetrical alkanes in good yields due to the formation of a statistical mixture of products.
  • AgF, Hg2F2, and CoF3 are specific reagents used in the Swarts reaction to synthesize alkyl fluorides.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the role of aqueous KOH (substitution to form alcohol) with alcoholic KOH (elimination to form alkene).
  • Assuming tertiary alkyl halides can undergo SN2 reactions under standard nucleophilic conditions.
  • Forgetting that Grignard reagent formation requires strictly anhydrous conditions to prevent protonolysis by moisture.
  • Overlooking carbocation rearrangements (hydride or methyl shifts) during SN1 reactions involving secondary or primary substrates capable of forming more stable carbocations.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation and Properties of Alkyl Halides

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Synthesis Pathways

Alkyl halides are prepared primarily from alcohols using HX, PX3, PX5, or SOCl2, and from hydrocarbons via free radical halogenation or addition of hydrogen halides.

Substitution Dynamics

Nucleophilic substitution proceeds via SN1 (via carbocation, racemization, tertiary favored) or SN2 (concerted, inversion, primary favored) pathways depending on substrate and solvent.

Elimination Reactions

Dehydrohalogenation via E2 or E1 pathways yields alkenes following the Saytzeff rule, competing directly with substitution under basic conditions.

Organometallic Derivatives

Reaction with active metals such as magnesium yields Grignard reagents, which are versatile nucleophiles used extensively in carbon-carbon bond formation.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Haloalkane (R-X)
Aliphatic organic compound containing a carbon-halogen bond.
Grignard Reagent (R-Mg-X)
Organomagnesium halide acting as a strong carbon nucleophile.
Walden Inversion (Inversion of Configuration)
Spatial inversion of chiral carbon during SN2 attack.
Saytzeff Rule (Elimination Rule)
Empirical rule stating more substituted alkene is the major elimination product.
Ambident Nucleophile (Dual Nucleophile)
Nucleophile with two reactive nucleophilic sites (e.g., CN-, NO2-).
Finkelstein Reaction (Halogen Exchange)
Conversion of alkyl chloride/bromide to alkyl iodide using NaI in acetone.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • RateSn1 = k[R-X]
  • RateSn2 = k[R-X][Nu-]
  • Order of R-X reactivity in SN1: 3° > 2° > 1°
  • Order of R-X reactivity in SN2: 1° > 2° > 3°
  • Leaving group order: I- > Br- > Cl- > F-

📌 Quick Recall

  • Preparation methods include alcohol halogenation and hydrocarbon substitution.
  • Substitution proceeds via SN1 or SN2 depending on steric and electronic factors.
  • Elimination yields alkenes via base-promoted pathways.
  • Organometallic reactions establish carbon-carbon bonds.

⚖️ Key Differences

Aqueous KOH VS Alcoholic KOH

Aqueous KOH promotes nucleophilic substitution (alcohol formation), whereas alcoholic KOH promotes β-elimination (alkene formation).
