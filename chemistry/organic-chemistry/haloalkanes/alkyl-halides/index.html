Alkyl Halides

Alkyl halides, or haloalkanes, are aliphatic hydrocarbons where one or more hydrogen atoms are replaced by halogen atoms. They exhibit high polarity due to the electronegativity difference between carbon and halogen atoms, dictating their nucleophilic substitution and elimination reactivity.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Nucleophilic Substitution Reactions (SN1 and SN2)

  • The SN2 mechanism is a single-step concerted process characterized by backside attack, resulting in complete Walden inversion.
  • Tertiary alkyl halides undergo substitution predominantly via the SN1 mechanism due to the high stability of the intermediate tertiary carbocation.
  • Polar protic solvents favor SN1 reactions by stabilizing the ions through solvation, whereas polar aprotic solvents accelerate SN2 reactions.

Elimination Reactions (E1 and E2)

  • The E2 mechanism is a concerted elimination requiring anti-periplanar geometry of the departing proton and the halide ion.
  • Sterically hindered bulky bases (e.g., potassium tert-butoxide) direct elimination toward the less substituted Hofmann alkene product.
  • Primary alkyl halides favor substitution under mild conditions, whereas secondary and tertiary halides predominantly undergo elimination at elevated temperatures.

Preparation and Organometallic Reactions

  • Alkyl chlorides are synthesized via the Darzens process using thionyl chloride (SOCl2) in pyridine, yielding clean gaseous byproducts.
  • Alkyl halides react with magnesium metal in dry ether to form Grignard reagents (R-Mg-X), which act as strong carbon nucleophiles.
  • The Finkelstein reaction converts alkyl chlorides or bromides into alkyl iodides using sodium iodide in dry acetone.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Tertiary alkyl halides undergo substitution via SN1 and elimination via E1.

Observation: In the presence of strong nucleophiles/bases at high concentrations, certain secondary/tertiary substrates can undergo atypical pathways. — Solvent polarity and base strength alter the competitive energetic balance between SN1/SN2 and E1/E2 pathways.

Exception Vinyl and aryl halides readily undergo nucleophilic substitution.

Observation: Vinyl and aryl halides are extremely unreactive toward SN1 and SN2 mechanisms. — Partial double bond character due to resonance stabilization and sp2 hybridization makes carbon-halogen bond cleavage energetically unfavorable.

Exception Primary halides strictly follow SN2 kinetics.

Observation: Neopentyl halides (e.g., neopentyl chloride) are extremely unreactive toward SN2 substitution. — Severe steric hindrance around the reaction center prevents backside attack by the incoming nucleophile.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Carbon-halogen bond length increases down the halogen group: C-F < C-Cl < C-Br < C-I.
  • Carbon-halogen bond strength decreases down the group, making alkyl iodides the most reactive in substitution.
  • Aqueous KOH converts alkyl halides into alcohols via substitution.
  • Alcoholic KOH converts alkyl halides into alkenes via dehydrohalogenation.
  • Swarts reaction is used to synthesize alkyl fluorides using AgF or Hg2F2.
  • Wurtz reaction couples two alkyl halide molecules using sodium in dry ether to form higher alkanes.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing SN1 reactivity order with SN2 reactivity order.
  • Assuming alkyl fluorides react rapidly in standard nucleophilic substitution without considering poor leaving group ability.
  • Forgetting that aqueous vs alcoholic medium completely changes the reaction pathway from substitution to elimination.
  • Failing to account for carbocation rearrangement (hydride or methyl shift) during SN1 or E1 reactions.

⚡ Quick Revision: Alkyl Halides

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

Alkyl halides possess sp3 hybridized carbon atoms bonded to electronegative halogens, creating a permanent dipole moment and making the carbon electrophilic.

Nucleophilic Substitution

Nucleophilic substitution proceeds via either a concerted bimolecular pathway (SN2) with inversion or a stepwise unimolecular pathway (SN1) via a carbocation.

Elimination Reactions

Base-promoted elimination yields alkenes, following Zaitsev's rule for stable products or Hofmann's rule when using bulky bases.

Synthetic Utility

Alkyl halides serve as versatile synthetic intermediates for producing alcohols, ethers, amines, nitriles, and organometallic reagents.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Haloalkane (R-X)
Aliphatic compound containing a carbon-halogen bond.
Nucleophile (Nu-)
Electron-rich species seeking a positive center.
Leaving Group (X-)
Stable anion departing with electron pair.
Carbocation (R+)
Trigonal planar electron-deficient carbon intermediate.
Walden Inversion (Stereochemical inversion)
Spatial inversion of chiral center during SN2.
Grignard Reagent (RMgX)
Organomagnesium halide used for carbon-carbon bond formation.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • SN2 is favored by primary halides and polar aprotic solvents.
  • SN1 is favored by tertiary halides and polar protic solvents.
  • Zaitsev rule yields the most substituted alkene.
  • Finkelstein reaction prepares alkyl iodides.
  • Swarts reaction prepares alkyl fluorides.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkyl halides undergo substitution and elimination.
  • Reactivity depends heavily on steric and electronic factors.
  • Carbocation stability dictates SN1/E1 rates.
  • Grignard reagents require anhydrous conditions.

⚖️ Key Differences

SN1 VS SN2

SN1 involves racemization and carbocation intermediates; SN2 involves inversion and a concerted single-step transition state.
