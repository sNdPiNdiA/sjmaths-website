Haloalkanes

Haloalkanes are aliphatic hydrocarbons in which one or more hydrogen atoms are replaced by halogen atoms. They exhibit high reactivity due to the polarization of the carbon-halogen bond.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation and Physical Properties

  • Haloalkanes are prepared by the reaction of alcohols with halogen acids, phosphorus halides, or thionyl chloride (SOCl2).
  • The boiling points of haloalkanes increase with an increase in the size of the halogen atom and molecular weight due to van der Waals forces.
  • Branching in alkyl chains decreases the boiling point due to a decrease in surface area and spherical shape approximation.
  • Despite being polar, haloalkanes are immiscible in water because they cannot form hydrogen bonds with water molecules.

Nucleophilic Substitution Reactions

  • The SN2 mechanism proceeds via a concerted single-step pathway with complete Walden inversion of configuration.
  • The SN1 mechanism proceeds via a planar carbocation intermediate, resulting in racemization of the chiral center.
  • Reactivity order for alkyl halides in SN2 reactions is Primary > Secondary > Tertiary due to steric hindrance.
  • Reactivity order for alkyl halides in SN1 reactions is Tertiary > Secondary > Primary due to carbocation stability.

Elimination and Organometallic Reactions

  • Dehydrohalogenation of haloalkanes with alcoholic KOH follows Saytzeff's rule, yielding the more substituted alkene as the major product.
  • Alkyl halides react with metallic magnesium in dry ether to form Grignard reagents (R-Mg-X), which act as strong carbon nucleophiles.
  • Finkelstein reaction involves the halogen exchange of alkyl chlorides or bromides with sodium iodide in dry acetone to form alkyl iodides.
  • Swarts reaction converts alkyl chlorides or bromides to fluorides using metallic fluorides such as AgF, Hg2F2, or CoF2.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Vinyl and aryl halides undergo rapid nucleophilic substitution

Observation: Vinyl and aryl halides are extremely unreactive toward nucleophilic substitution — Partial double bond character due to resonance and sp2 hybridization of carbon bonded to halogen.

Exception Tertiary alkyl halides undergo SN2 reactions smoothly

Observation: Tertiary alkyl halides do not undergo SN2 reactions — Severe steric hindrance around the electrophilic carbon blocks the backside attack of the incoming nucleophile.

Exception Primary alkyl halides undergo SN1 substitution

Observation: Primary alkyl halides rarely undergo SN1 substitution — Primary carbocation intermediates are extremely unstable and high in energy.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
SN1 vs SN2 Reactivity Order
123 for SN2, 321 for SN1

Application: Remember 1°>2°>3° for SN2 (less steric hindrance) and 3°>2°>1° for SN1 (more stable carbocation).

Finkelstein vs Swarts Mnemonic
FIN-kelstein uses NaI; SW-arts uses Metal Fluorides

Application: Use NaI in acetone for iodine exchange (Finkelstein) and AgF/Hg2F2 for fluorine exchange (Swarts).

Saytzeff Elimination Rule
More alkyl groups = More stable alkene

Application: In base-promoted elimination, eliminate hydrogen from the carbon with fewer hydrogens to form the Zaitsev product.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Thionyl chloride (SOCl2) is preferred for preparing alkyl chlorides from alcohols because byproducts (SO2 and HCl) are gases and escape easily.
  • Allylic and benzylic halides show high reactivity toward both SN1 and SN2 mechanisms due to resonance stabilization of intermediates or transition states.
  • Grignard reagents must be prepared in anhydrous conditions because traces of moisture decompose them into corresponding alkanes.
  • Iodoalkanes develop a brown color on standing due to the liberation of free iodine upon photochemical decomposition.
  • Chloroform (CHCl3) is stored in dark-colored bottles completely filled to prevent its oxidation to poisonous phosgene gas (COCl2).
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation of benzene with haloalkanes requires anhydrous AlCl3 as a Lewis acid catalyst.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming primary halides can undergo SN1 reactions without rearrangement or special conditions.
  • Forgetting that aqueous KOH causes substitution (alcohol formation), whereas alcoholic KOH causes elimination (alkene formation).
  • Writing Grignard reagent formula incorrectly without mentioning dry ether as the mandatory reaction medium.
  • Confusing the stereochemical outcome of SN1 (racemic mixture) with SN2 (inversion).

⚡ Quick Revision: Haloalkanes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Classification and Nomenclature

Haloalkanes are classified based on the number of halogen atoms (mono, di, poly) and the hybridization of the carbon atom bonded to the halogen (sp3 carbon).

Nucleophilic Substitution Reactions

Nucleophilic substitution proceeds via SN1 (two-step, carbocation, racemization) or SN2 (concerted, transition state, inversion) pathways depending on substrate structure and solvent.

Elimination Reactions

Beta-elimination (dehydrohalogenation) removes a hydrogen and halogen to form alkenes, following Saytzeff's rule under standard conditions.

Organometallic Derivatives

Reaction with active metals such as magnesium yields Grignard reagents, which serve as versatile synthetic intermediates for carbon-carbon bond formation.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • SN1 rate depends only on [R-X].
  • SN2 rate depends on [R-X] and [Nu-].
  • Tertiary halides follow SN1; primary halides follow SN2.
  • Alcoholic KOH gives elimination; aqueous KOH gives substitution.
  • Grignard reagents react violently with protic sources like water and alcohols.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Haloalkanes feature polar C-X bonds rendering them reactive toward nucleophiles and bases.
  • Substitution mechanisms divide into SN1 and SN2 based on kinetics and stereochemistry.
  • Elimination reactions compete with substitution and follow Saytzeff's rule.
  • Synthetic utility is enhanced by organometallic coupling and halogen exchange.

⚖️ Key Differences

Alcoholic KOH VS Aqueous KOH

Alcoholic KOH produces alkenes via elimination, whereas aqueous KOH produces alcohols via substitution.

What is the primary product formed when primary alcohol is treated with red P and I2?

Solution: Red phosphorus and iodine react in situ to form PI3, converting primary alcohols into alkyl iodides.

Question 2 of 10
Which reaction is used to lengthen carbon chain using alkyl halides?

Solution: Wurtz reaction couples two alkyl halide molecules with sodium to form a higher alkane with an increased carbon chain.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following is an example of a vicinal dihalide?

Solution: Vicinal dihalides have halogen atoms on adjacent carbon atoms, such as 1,2-dibromoethane.

Question 4 of 10
Which alkyl halide is least reactive towards nucleophilic substitution?

Solution: C-F bond is extremely strong due to high bond dissociation enthalpy, making methyl fluoride the least reactive.

Question 5 of 10
In the elimination of 2-bromobutane, the Zaitsev product is:

Solution: trans-2-butene is more stable than cis-2-butene due to less steric hindrance between methyl groups, making it the major Zaitsev product.

Question 6 of 10
Grignard reagent reacts with carbon dioxide followed by acid hydrolysis to yield:

Solution: Nucleophilic addition of Grignard reagent to CO2 gives a magnesium salt, which upon acidification yields carboxylic acids.

Question 7 of 10
Which species acts as the electrophile in SN1 mechanism?

Solution: SN1 mechanism generates a carbocation intermediate in the rate-determining step.

Question 8 of 10
What is the IUPAC name of CH3CH(Cl)CH2CH3?

Solution: Numbering from the end closer to the substituent gives 2-chlorobutane.

Question 9 of 10
Which compound does not form a Grignard reagent?

Solution: CH3OH contains an acidic proton (-OH) which decomposes Grignard reagents instantly upon contact.

Question 10 of 10
The dipole moment of chloromethane is higher than that of fluoromethane because:

Solution: Dipole moment is product of charge and distance; although C-F bond is more polar, the much longer C-Cl bond distance results in a higher dipole moment.
