General Methods of Preparation of Haloalkanes

Haloalkanes are synthesized via nucleophilic substitution or electrophilic addition reactions utilizing aliphatic hydrocarbons or alcohols as precursors. Reagents such as halogen acids, phosphorus halides, and thionyl chloride selectively convert hydroxyl groups into carbon-halogen bonds.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation from Alcohols

  • Alcohols react with HX in the order of reactivity: 3° > 2° > 1° due to carbocation stability.
  • Phosphorus pentachloride (PCl5) reacts with alcohols to yield alkyl chloride, phosphoryl chloride, and hydrochloric acid.
  • Thionyl chloride (SOCl2) is the preferred laboratory method because gaseous byproducts escape cleanly.
  • Phosphorus tribromide and triiodide are prepared in situ using red phosphorus and respective halogens.

Halogenation of Hydrocarbons

  • Free radical chlorination of alkanes yields complex mixtures of isomeric haloalkanes due to low selectivity.
  • Free radical bromination is highly selective, yielding predominantly tertiary haloalkanes over primary ones.
  • Direct iodination is reversible; an oxidizing agent like HIO3 or HNO3 is added to consume HI.
  • Alkene hydrohalogenation follows Markovnikov's rule via a carbocation intermediate, yielding more substituted halides.

Halogen Exchange Reactions

  • Finkelstein reaction converts alkyl chlorides or bromides to alkyl iodides using NaI in dry acetone.
  • The driving force for the Finkelstein reaction is the precipitation of NaCl or NaBr in acetone.
  • Swarts reaction produces alkyl fluorides by treating alkyl chlorides or bromides with metallic fluorides like AgF, Hg2F2, or CoF2.
  • Halogen exchange allows access to pure alkyl iodides and fluorides that are difficult to prepare via direct halogenation.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Action of HX on Alcohols
R-OH + HX → R-X + H2O (catalyst ZnCl2 for HCl)
Conditions: Lucas reagent (anhydrous ZnCl2 + concentrated HCl) for tertiary, secondary, primary alcohols
Units: R-OH concentration in mol L-1
Phosphorus Halides with Alcohols
3R-OH + PX3 → 3R-X + H3PO3 (X = Cl, Br)
Conditions: Anhydrous conditions; PX3 often generated in situ using red P and X2
Units: Stoichiometric proportions
Darbons Synthesis (Thionyl Chloride)
R-OH + SOCl2 → R-Cl + SO2(g) + HCl(g)
Conditions: Pyridine base medium to trap HCl byproduct
Units: Gaseous byproducts evolve cleanly leaving pure alkyl chloride
Hunsdiecker Reaction
R-COOAg + Br2 → R-Br + CO2 + AgBr
Conditions: Refluxing silver salt of carboxylic acid in anhydrous CCl4
Units: Chain shortening by one carbon atom
Halogenation of Alkanes
R-H + X2 → R-X + HX (in presence of hν or Δ)
Conditions: Free radical substitution via UV light or thermal initiation
Units: X2 = Cl2, Br2; yield dependent on statistical probability
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alcohols react with halogen acids via SN2 mechanism

Observation: Tertiary alcohols react via SN1 mechanism — Tertiary carbocation formation is energetically favored due to hyperconjugation and +I effect of three alkyl groups.

Exception Addition of HBr to alkenes follows Markovnikov's rule

Observation: Anti-Markovnikov addition occurs for HBr in the presence of peroxides — Presence of organic peroxides shifts the reaction mechanism from electrophilic ionic addition to a free-radical chain mechanism.

Exception Alkyl fluorides are easily prepared by direct fluorination of alkanes

Observation: Direct fluorination is violently explosive and uncontrollable — Fluorine has an extremely low F-F bond dissociation energy and the reaction is excessively exothermic.

Application: Remember 'Finkelstein for Iodine' and 'Swarts for Fluorine' to instantly identify halogen exchange reagents.

Application: Use Lucas reagent time test: 3° is immediate turbidity, 2° takes 5 minutes, 1° requires heating.

Application: Whenever a synthesis requires decreasing the carbon chain length by one, invoke the Hunsdiecker reaction.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Thionyl chloride (SOCl2) is the best reagent for preparing alkyl chlorides because organic-soluble products are absent and byproducts are gases.
  • Finkelstein reaction employs sodium iodide in dry acetone to synthesize alkyl iodides.
  • Swarts reaction uses silver fluoride (AgF) or mercury(I) fluoride (Hg2F2) to prepare alkyl fluorides.
  • Peroxide effect (anti-Markovnikov addition) is exclusively observed with HBr, not with HCl or HI.
  • Allylic and benzylic halides can be selectively prepared using N-bromosuccinimide (NBS) in carbon tetrachloride.
  • Direct iodination of alkanes requires an oxidizing agent such as iodic acid (HIO3) to remove hydrogen iodide formed.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming peroxide effect works with HCl or HI; it only works with HBr because HCl bond is too strong to cleave homolytically and HI radical rapidly recombines.
  • Confusing Finkelstein and Swarts reagents; NaI is for iodination, whereas AgF is for fluorination.
  • Forgetting that Lucas reagent requires anhydrous ZnCl2; aqueous acid will not give the differential turbidity rates.
  • Assuming direct fluorination of alkanes yields pure alkyl fluoride without explosion.

⚡ Quick Revision: General Methods of Preparation of Haloalkanes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Alcohol Conversion Pathways

Alcohols are converted to haloalkanes using HX, PX3, PCl5, or SOCl2, with SOCl2 providing the purest alkyl chlorides due to gaseous byproducts.

Hydrocarbon Halogenation

Alkanes undergo free-radical halogenation, while alkenes undergo electrophilic addition governed by Markovnikov's rule or anti-Markovnikov rule in the presence of peroxides.

Halogen Exchange Protocols

Finkelstein and Swarts reactions enable the synthesis of alkyl iodides and fluorides respectively, overcoming limitations of direct halogenation.

Carboxylation Degradation

The Hunsdiecker reaction decarboxylates silver salts of carboxylic acids with bromine to form lower alkyl bromides.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Lucas Reagent (Conc. HCl + Anhyd. ZnCl2)
Reagent used to distinguish between 1°, 2°, and 3° alcohols.
Finkelstein Reaction (R-X + NaI → R-I + NaX)
Halogen exchange method to synthesize alkyl iodides in dry acetone.
Swarts Reaction (R-X + AgF → R-F + AgX)
Method for preparing alkyl fluorides using heavy metal fluorides.
Hunsdiecker Reaction (RCOOAg + Br2 → RBr + CO2 + AgBr)
Decarboxylative bromination yielding lower alkyl bromides.
Markovnikov's Rule (Regioselective addition)
Negative part of addendum adds to carbon with fewer hydrogens.
Anti-Markovnikov (Peroxide effect)
Addition of HBr against Markovnikov's rule via free radicals.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • SOCl2 + R-OH → R-Cl + SO2 + HCl
  • NaI in dry acetone drives Finkelstein reaction via precipitation
  • Peroxide effect is unique to HBr among hydrogen halides
  • Lucas reagent reactivity: 3° > 2° > 1°
  • Swarts reaction employs AgF or Hg2F2 for fluorination

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alcohols are the most versatile precursors for haloalkanes.
  • Hydrocarbon halogenation relies on free-radical or electrophilic addition pathways.
  • Halogen exchange is essential for alkyl iodides and fluorides.
  • Reaction conditions dictate whether substitution, elimination, or rearrangement occurs.

⚖️ Key Differences

Finkelstein Reaction VS Swarts Reaction

Finkelstein uses NaI in acetone for iodides; Swarts uses AgF/Hg2F2 for fluorides.

Explanation: NaCl or NaBr is insoluble in dry acetone and precipitates out, driving the equilibrium forward according to Le Chatelier's principle.

Question 17 of 18

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary byproduct formed when an alcohol reacts with phosphorus pentachloride (PCl5)?

Solution: Reaction of alcohol with PCl5 yields alkyl chloride, phosphoryl chloride (POCl3), and hydrochloric acid (HCl).

Question 2 of 10
Which reagent is used in the Swarts reaction to introduce fluorine into an organic halide?

Solution: Silver fluoride (AgF) or mercurous fluoride is utilized in the Swarts reaction for safe fluorination.

Question 3 of 10
During the halogenation of alkanes, why is direct iodination carried out in the presence of HIO3?

Solution: HIO3 oxidizes the reducing HI byproduct back to iodine (I2), driving the reversible iodination forward.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following alcohols reacts slowest with Lucas reagent?

Solution: Butan-1-ol is a primary alcohol, which reacts slowest (requires heating and takes considerable time) with Lucas reagent.

Question 5 of 10
The peroxide effect operates via which of the following intermediates?

Solution: Peroxides undergo homolytic cleavage to generate free radicals, initiating a radical chain addition for HBr.

Question 6 of 10
Which reaction produces an alkyl halide with one less carbon atom than the parent carboxylic acid?

Solution: Hunsdiecker reaction decarboxylates silver carboxylates, leading to loss of one carbon atom as CO2.

Question 7 of 10
Why does dry acetone favor the Finkelstein reaction?

Solution: NaCl and NaBr are insoluble in dry acetone and precipitate out, shifting the equilibrium toward alkyl iodide formation.

Question 8 of 10
Which alkyl halide cannot be prepared in good yield by direct free-radical halogenation of alkanes?

Solution: Alkyl iodides cannot be prepared in good yield via direct iodination because the reaction is reversible and unfavorable.

Question 9 of 10
What is the role of pyridine when SOCl2 is used to convert alcohols to alkyl chlorides?

Solution: Pyridine neutralizes the acidic HCl byproduct, preventing side reactions like acid-catalyzed ether formation or elimination.

Question 10 of 10
Which compound is the main product when isobutylene reacts with HCl?

Solution: Addition follows Markovnikov's rule through a tertiary carbocation intermediate, yielding 2-chloro-2-methylpropane.
