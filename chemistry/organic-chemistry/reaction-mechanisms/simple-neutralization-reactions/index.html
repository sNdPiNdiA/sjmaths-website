Simple Neutralization Reactions

Neutralization is a chemical reaction in which an acid and a base quantitatively react to form salt and water. The fundamental driving force of aqueous neutralization between strong acids and strong bases is the combination of hydronium and hydroxide ions to form water.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Thermodynamics of Acid-Base Neutralization

  • The standard enthalpy of neutralization for any strong acid and strong base is constant at approximately -57.1 kJ/mol because the net ionic reaction is simply H+(aq) + OH-(aq) → H2O(l).
  • When a weak acid or weak base is involved, part of the heat evolved is absorbed in the dissociation of the weak electrolyte, resulting in a lower heat of neutralization value.
  • Neutralization reactions are inherently exothermic due to the formation of the covalent O-H bond in water from aqueous ions.

Stoichiometry and Quantitative Titrations

  • Equivalence points in neutralization titrations depend on the relative strengths of the reacting acids and bases, resulting in acidic, basic, or neutral salts.
  • Indicators used in neutralization titrations (such as phenolphthalein or methyl orange) undergo structural changes over specific pH ranges corresponding to the steep inflection point of the titration curve.
  • Polyprotic acids undergo stepwise neutralization, yielding multiple inflection points on a titration curve corresponding to each successive dissociation constant (Ka1, Ka2).

Salt Hydrolysis and Solution pH

  • Salts derived from strong acids and strong bases (e.g., NaCl) do not undergo hydrolysis and maintain a neutral pH of 7.0 at 298 K.
  • Salts derived from weak acids and strong bases (e.g., CH3COONa) undergo anionic hydrolysis, producing an alkaline solution with pH > 7.
  • Salts derived from weak bases and strong acids (e.g., NH4Cl) undergo cationic hydrolysis, producing an acidic solution with pH < 7.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Heat of neutralization for strong acids and bases is always -57.1 kJ/mol

Observation: HF and HCN show different (often higher or lower) heats of neutralization — HF is a weak acid and forms strong hydrogen bonds with fluoride ion, and lattice/hydration energies alter the net enthalpy.

Exception Salts of strong acids and strong bases give neutral solutions

Observation: Certain metal aquo ions like [Al(H2O)6]3+ in strong acid salts create acidic solutions — High charge density polarizes coordinated water molecules, promoting proton release (hydrolysis).

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Heat of Neutralization Rule
Weak acid/base factor

Application: Whenever a weak acid or base is neutralized, the measured heat of neutralization is numerically less than -57.1 kJ/mol because energy is consumed in ionization.

Salt pH Shortcut
S-S (Neutral), W-S (Weakly Basic), S-W (Weakly Acidic)

Application: Quickly identify salt nature by checking parent components: Strong-Strong = Neutral, Weak Acid-Strong Base = Basic, Strong Acid-Weak Base = Acidic.

Normality Equation
N1V1 = N2V2

Application: Always convert molarity to normality by multiplying by the n-factor (basicity for acids, acidity for bases) before applying.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The net ionic equation for the neutralization of any strong acid by a strong base is H+ + OH- → H2O.
  • The standard enthalpy of neutralization of a strong acid with a strong base is -57.1 kJ/mol.
  • Phenolphthalein is a suitable indicator for strong acid-strong base and weak acid-strong base titrations.
  • Methyl orange is suitable for strong acid-weak base titrations.
  • Hydrolysis constant (Kh) is related to ionic product of water (Kw) via Kh = Kw / (Ka × Kb) for weak acid-weak base salts.
  • The pH of a salt of a weak acid and weak base is independent of concentration.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming all neutralization reactions release -57.1 kJ/mol of heat, forgetting weak acid ionization enthalpy.
  • Confusing the n-factor of H3PO4 when calculating normality in partial neutralization reactions.
  • Failing to account for temperature dependence of Kw when calculating pH of neutral water at elevated temperatures.
  • Assuming salts of weak acids and weak bases are always strictly neutral (pH = 7.0).

⚡ Quick Revision: Simple Neutralization Reactions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Fundamental Neutralization Principle

Neutralization involves the combination of hydrogen and hydroxide ions to form undissociated water molecules, accompanied by salt formation.

Enthalpy Dynamics

Strong acid-base reactions exhibit constant exothermic enthalpy values due to identical net ionic processes.

Salt Hydrolysis Mechanics

Cations and anions derived from weak electrolytes react with water molecules, altering the pH of the resulting equivalence point solution.

Titration Curve Behavior

Titration curves feature sharp inflection regions near equivalence points, dictating the appropriate choice of acid-base indicators.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Neutralization (Acid + Base → Salt + Water)
Quantitative reaction between acid and base species.
Enthalpy of Neutralization (ΔHneut)
Heat released when one gram equivalent of an acid is neutralized by a base.
Hydrolysis (Kh)
Reaction of salt ions with water molecules to alter solution pH.
Equivalence Point (Stoichiometric point)
Point where moles of added titrant chemically equate to analyte moles.
Indicator (pH transition agent)
Weak organic acid or base changing color across specific pH intervals.
Buffer Solution (pH-stable mixture)
Solution resisting pH change upon addition of small amounts of acid or base.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Strong acid + Strong base → Neutral salt + H2O (ΔH = -57.1 kJ/mol)
  • Weak acid + Strong base → Basic salt (pH > 7)
  • Strong acid + Weak base → Acidic salt (pH < 7)
  • Kw = [H+][OH-] = 10-14 at 298 K
  • N1V1 = N2V2 neutralization formula

📌 Quick Recall

  • Neutralization is fundamentally proton transfer from acid to base.
  • Enthalpy changes vary depending on electrolyte dissociation strength.
  • Hydrolysis determines residual solution acidity or alkalinity.
  • Indicator selection relies on matching the steep pH transition region of titration curves.

⚖️ Key Differences

End point VS Equivalence point

End point is the experimental color change observation, whereas equivalence point is the exact stoichiometric completion point.

What is the standard enthalpy of neutralization of a strong acid with a strong base in dilute aqueous solution?

Explanation: The standard enthalpy of neutralization for strong acids and bases is constant at -57.1 kJ/mol due to the formation of one mole of water from H+ and OH-.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following salts produces an acidic aqueous solution upon dissolution?

Explanation: NH4Cl is derived from a strong acid (HCl) and a weak base (NH4OH), resulting in cationic hydrolysis and an acidic solution.

Question 3 of 18

What is the pH of an aqueous solution of ammonium acetate (CH3COONH4) if Ka = Kb?

Explanation: When the dissociation constants of the weak acid and weak base are equal, the salt solution remains neutral with pH = 7.

Question 4 of 18

Which indicator is most suitable for titrating acetic acid with sodium hydroxide?

Explanation: Phenolphthalein changes color in the basic pH range (8.2-10.0), matching the equivalence point of a weak acid-strong base titration.

Question 5 of 18

The heat of neutralization of a weak acid with a strong base is numerically less than -57.1 kJ/mol because:

Explanation: Part of the heat evolved is consumed in the dissociation of the unionized weak acid molecules.

Question 6 of 18

What is the normality of a 0.5 M H2SO4 solution used in neutralization?

Explanation: Normality = Molarity × n-factor (basicity = 2 for H2SO4). Thus, 0.5 M × 2 = 1.0 N.

Question 7 of 18

Which salt does not undergo hydrolysis in water?

Explanation: NaCl is formed from a strong acid (HCl) and a strong base (NaOH), neither of whose ions undergo hydrolysis.

Question 8 of 18

The hydrolysis constant (Kh) for the salt of a weak acid and strong base is expressed as:

Explanation: For an anionic hydrolysis of a weak acid salt, Kh = Kw / Ka.

Question 9 of 18

What happens to the pH when ammonium chloride is added to ammonium hydroxide solution?

Explanation: Adding NH4Cl introduces a common ion (NH4+), suppressing the ionization of NH4OH and lowering the pH (common ion effect in buffers).

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following represents a neutralisation reaction?

Explanation: Reaction between HCl (acid) and NaOH (base) yielding salt and water is a classic neutralization reaction.

Question 11 of 18

The degree of hydrolysis (h) increases with:

Explanation: Degree of hydrolysis increases upon dilution and with temperature rise for endothermic hydrolysis processes.

Question 12 of 18

What is the pH range of phenolphthalein color change?

Explanation: Phenolphthalein transitions from colorless to pink in the pH range of 8.2 to 10.0.

Question 13 of 18

When 100 mL of 0.1 M HCl is mixed with 100 mL of 0.1 M NaOH, the resulting solution is:

Explanation: Equal moles of strong acid and strong base completely neutralize each other to form a neutral salt solution (pH = 7).

Question 14 of 18

Which acid has the highest heat of neutralization when reacted with NaOH?

Explanation: HCl is a strong acid and does not require energy for ionization, releasing the maximum standard heat of neutralization (-57.1 kJ/mol).

Question 15 of 18

The expression for pH of a weak acid-strong base salt solution is:

Explanation: The standard derived formula for the pH of a salt of a weak acid and strong base is pH = 7 + 0.5pKw + 0.5pKa + 0.5log(c).

Question 16 of 18

What is the basicity of phosphoric acid (H3PO4)?

Explanation: H3PO4 contains three replaceable hydrogen atoms bonded to oxygen, giving it a basicity of 3.

Question 17 of 18

In a titration of a strong acid against a weak base, the pH at the equivalence point is:

Explanation: The salt formed undergoes cationic hydrolysis, making the equivalence point solution acidic (pH < 7).

Question 18 of 18

Which of the following is a Lewis acid-base neutralization example?

Explanation: The reaction between BF3 and NH3 forms a coordinate covalent bond, representing a classical Lewis acid-base neutralization.

Question 2 of 10

What is the hydrolysis constant (Kh) expression for ammonium chloride (NH4Cl)?

Question 4 of 10
When 50 mL of 0.2 M NaOH is mixed with 50 mL of 0.1 M HCl, the resulting solution is:

Solution: NaOH is in excess (10 mmol NaOH vs 5 mmol HCl), leaving 5 mmol of excess OH- ions, making the solution basic.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following compounds can act both as a Bronsted-Lowry acid and a Bronsted-Lowry base in neutralization contexts?

Solution: HCO3- can accept a proton to form H2CO3 or donate a proton to form CO32- (amphiprotic).

Question 6 of 10
What is the pH of a 0.01 M NaOH solution assuming complete dissociation?

Solution: [OH-] = 10-2 M, pOH = 2, therefore pH = 14 - 2 = 12.

Question 7 of 10
In the titration of sodium carbonate with HCl using methyl orange indicator, the color change occurs at:

Solution: Methyl orange changes color at the end of the second stage of titration where Na2CO3 is completely converted to H2CO3 / CO2.

Question 8 of 10
What is the unit of the hydrolysis constant (Kh)?

Solution: Hydrolysis constants can be expressed with concentration units depending on the reaction, but often treated as dimensionless ratios like equilibrium constants depending on formulation.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following pairs will form a buffer solution upon mixing in appropriate molar amounts?

Solution: A weak acid (CH3COOH) and its salt with a strong base (CH3COONa) constitute an acidic buffer solution.

Question 10 of 10
Calculate the volume of 0.1 M NaOH required to completely neutralize 25 mL of 0.2 M H2SO4}.

Solution: Using N1V1 = N2V2 (0.4 N × 25 mL = 0.1 N × V2), V2 = 100 mL.
