Organic Chemistry

Simple Addition Reactions

Simple addition reactions involve the combination of two or more molecules to form a single adduct without the loss of atoms or small molecules. These processes predominantly occur across unsaturated functional groups containing carbon-carbon or carbon-heteroatom multiple bonds.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrophilic Addition to Alkenes and Alkynes

  • Electrophilic addition proceeds via a two-step mechanism involving a carbocation intermediate or a cycliconium ion.
  • The addition of unsymmetrical reagents to unsymmetrical alkenes follows Markovnikov's rule, yielding the more stable carbocation.
  • Halogenation of alkenes proceeds via a cyclic halonium ion intermediate, ensuring strict anti-stereospecific addition.
  • Hydration of alkenes using dilute H2SO4 yields alcohols via carbocation formation, susceptible to rearrangement.

Nucleophilic Addition to Carbonyl Compounds

  • Aldehydes and ketones undergo nucleophilic addition via an sp2 to sp3 hybridization change at the carbonyl carbon.
  • Steric hindrance and electronic factors dictate reactivity; aliphatic aldehydes are more reactive than ketones due to +I effect and steric crowding.
  • Cyanohydrin formation involves the attack of cyanide ion on the carbonyl carbon followed by protonation.
  • Grignard reagents add to carbonyl compounds to yield alcohols, functioning as potent carbon nucleophiles.

Free Radical Addition and Stereochemical Outcomes

  • Addition of HBr in the presence of peroxides follows the Kharasch effect (anti-Markovnikov addition) via a free radical chain mechanism.
  • Radical addition does not occur with HCl or HI because the propagation steps are either endothermic or unfavorable.
  • Catalytic hydrogenation of alkynes yields cis-alkenes using Lindlar's catalyst via a concerted syn-addition pathway.
  • Dissolving metal reduction (Na/liquid NH3) of alkynes yields trans-alkenes through a radical anion intermediate.

Rate = k[Carbonyl][Nucleophile]
Conditions: Base or acid-catalyzed addition to carbonyl compounds
Units: Rate in mol dm-3 s-1, k in dm3 mol-1 s-1

Conditions: Bimolecular electrophilic addition (AdE2)

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Addition of HBr to alkenes follows Markovnikov's rule.

Observation: Anti-Markovnikov addition occurs in the presence of organic peroxides. — Peroxides shift the mechanism from ionic electrophilic addition to a free radical chain pathway where Br• attacks first to form the more stable carbon radical.

Exception Halogenation of alkenes yields vicinal dihalides via a cyclic halonium ion.

Observation: Addition of iodine (I2) is practically reversible and generally does not form stable vicinal diiodides. — Vicinal diiodides are thermodynamically unstable and readily eliminate iodine to revert to the alkene.

Exception Nucleophilic addition to carbonyl groups is faster for aldehydes than ketones.

Observation: Formaldehyde reacts faster than higher aliphatic aldehydes. — Absence of alkyl groups provides minimal steric hindrance and maximum positive charge density on the carbonyl carbon.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Markovnikov Mnemonic
The rich get richer (in hydrogen).

Application: In addition of HX to an alkene, the hydrogen atom attaches to the carbon already possessing more hydrogens.

Carbonyl Reactivity Order
HCHO > RCHO > R2CO

Application: Reactivity in nucleophilic addition decreases as alkyl substitution increases due to combined steric and inductive effects.

Lindlar vs Birch Reduction
Lindlar = Cis, Birch = Trans

Application: Use H2/Lindlar catalyst for cis-alkene synthesis from alkynes, and Na/NH3 for trans-alkene synthesis.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Electrophilic addition to alkenes is initiated by attack on the electrophile to form a carbocation intermediate.
  • Carbocation rearrangements (1,2-hydride or 1,2-methyl shifts) can occur during electrophilic addition if a more stable carbocation can form.
  • Addition of bromine to trans-2-butene yields racemic 2,3-dibromobutane via anti-addition.
  • Bisulfite addition compounds are crystalline solids used for the isolation and purification of carbonyl compounds.
  • Grignard reagents must be handled in anhydrous ether solvents to prevent protonolysis by water.
  • Addition of water to alkynes in the presence of HgSO4 and H2SO4 yields carbonyl compounds via enol tautomerization.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting that carbocation rearrangements can alter the expected carbon skeleton in alkene additions.
  • Assuming anti-Markovnikov addition applies to HCl and HI in the presence of peroxides.
  • Neglecting stereochemical outcomes in cyclic halonium or mercurinium ion ring-opening steps.
  • Failing to account for intramolecular hydrogen bonding in specific carbonyl hydrate or hemiacetal equilibria.

⚡ Quick Revision: Simple Addition Reactions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Electrophilic Addition Mechanism

Involves π-bond polarization and attack by an electrophile to generate a carbocation, followed by nucleophilic capture.

Regioselectivity & Markovnikov Rule

Regioselectivity is governed by the relative stability of the intermediate carbocation or radical species formed in the rate-determining step.

Nucleophilic Addition to Carbonyls

Driven by the polarity of the C=O bond, leading to nucleophilic attack at the electrophilic carbonyl carbon followed by protonation.

Stereospecificity

Halogenation and hydroboration-oxidation exhibit high stereospecificity, leading to specific syn or anti addition products.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Markovnikov Addition (Regioselective addition)
Addition where the electrophile adds to the carbon with more hydrogens.
Kharasch Effect (Peroxide effect)
Anti-Markovnikov addition of HBr in the presence of peroxides.
Halonium Ion (Cyclic intermediate)
Three-membered positively charged intermediate formed during halogen addition.
Cyanohydrin (RCH(OH)CN)
Adduct formed by nucleophilic addition of HCN to carbonyl compounds.
Lindlar Catalyst (Pd/BaSO4, quinoline)
Poisoned palladium catalyst for cis-hydrogenation of alkynes.
Birch Reduction (Na in liquid NH3)
Dissolving metal reduction yielding trans-alkenes from alkynes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Electrophilic addition rate increases with electron-donating groups on the alkene.
  • Carbonyl nucleophilic addition is catalyzed by both acids and bases.
  • Peroxides only reverse the addition of HBr, not HCl or HI.
  • Hydration of alkynes follows Markovnikov's rule to give ketones (except acetylene which gives acetaldehyde).
  • Grignard reagents add to aldehydes to give secondary alcohols and to ketones to give tertiary alcohols.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Addition reactions convert unsaturated systems into saturated systems.
  • Mechanism type depends strictly on the electronic nature of the substrate and reagent.
  • Carbocation stability dictates regiochemistry in ionic electrophilic additions.
  • Steric and electronic factors control nucleophilic addition rates at carbonyl centers.

⚖️ Key Differences

Markovnikov Addition VS Anti-Markovnikov Addition

Markovnikov places the hydrogen on the carbon with more hydrogens (ionic); Anti-Markovnikov places the bromine on that carbon (radical).

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following intermediates is responsible for the anti-addition of bromine to alkenes?

Explanation: Bromine addition proceeds via a cyclic bromonium ion intermediate, which is then opened by bromide ion via an SN2 pathway leading to anti stereochemistry.

Question 2 of 18

The addition of HBr to propene in the presence of benzoyl peroxide yields:

Explanation: In the presence of peroxides, HBr adds via a free radical mechanism following anti-Markovnikov's rule to give 1-bromopropane.

Question 3 of 18

Which carbonyl compound is most reactive toward nucleophilic addition?

Explanation: Formaldehyde has no alkyl groups to exert +I effect or steric hindrance, making its carbonyl carbon most electropositive and reactive.

Question 4 of 18

What is the product of hydration of 2-methylpropene with dilute H2SO4?

Explanation: Protonation yields a tertiary carbocation, attack of water followed by deprotonation yields 2-methyl-2-propanol.

Question 5 of 18

Lindlar's catalyst is used to convert alkynes to:

Explanation: Lindlar catalyst is a poisoned palladium catalyst that hydrogenates alkynes specifically to cis-alkenes via syn addition.

Question 6 of 18

Why does HCl not undergo anti-Markovnikov addition in the presence of peroxides?

Explanation: The propagation step where a chlorine radical adds to the double bond is endothermic, making the chain propagation energetically unfavorable.

Question 7 of 18

In the base-catalyzed hydration of carbonyl compounds, the attacking species is:

Explanation: Base catalysis generates hydroxide ion (OH-), a strong nucleophile that directly attacks the carbonyl carbon.

Question 8 of 18

Reaction of 2-butyne with sodium in liquid ammonia yields:

Explanation: Dissolving metal reduction using Na/NH3 reduces alkynes to trans-alkenes via a radical anion intermediate.

Question 9 of 18

Which alkene reacts fastest with electrophilic HBr?

Explanation: 2-methylpropene yields a tertiary carbocation upon protonation, which is the most stable and forms at the fastest rate.

Question 10 of 18

Addition of HCN to carbonyl compounds is an example of:

Explanation: Cyanide ion acts as a nucleophile attacking the electrophilic carbonyl carbon.

Question 11 of 18

What is the intermediate formed during oxymercuration-demercuration of alkenes?

Explanation: Oxymercuration proceeds through a cyclic mercurinium ion, preventing carbocation rearrangements.

Question 12 of 18

Hydroboration-oxidation of alkenes results in:

Explanation: Hydroboration-oxidation proceeds via concerted syn addition leading to anti-Markovnikov hydration without carbocation rearrangement.

Question 13 of 18

Grignard reagent reacting with formaldehyde produces:

Explanation: Formaldehyde reacts with Grignard reagents to yield primary alcohols after hydrolysis.

Question 14 of 18

Which of the following reagents converts an alkene to a vicinal diol with syn-stereochemistry?

Explanation: Cold dilute alkaline KMnO4 or OsO4 effects syn-dihydroxylation of alkenes.

Question 15 of 18

In nucleophilic addition to aldehydes and ketones, the hybridization of the carbonyl carbon changes from:

Explanation: The planar trigonal sp2 carbonyl carbon becomes tetrahedral sp3 upon nucleophilic attack.

Question 16 of 18

Which alkyl halide is formed when propene reacts with HI in the dark?

Explanation: Addition of HI follows Markovnikov's rule to give 2-iodopropane as the major product.

Question 17 of 18

The driving force for the addition of bisulfite to aldehydes is:

Explanation: Bisulfite forms a crystalline solid adduct which precipitates out, driving the equilibrium forward.

Question 18 of 18

Addition of bromine water to an alkene results in the formation of a:

Explanation: In aqueous bromine medium, water acts as a better nucleophile than bromide ion, yielding a bromohydrin.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following represents the correct order of electrophilic addition reactivity for alkenes?

Solution: Alkyl groups donate electron density via +I and hyperconjugation, stabilizing the intermediate carbocation. Thus, 2-methylpropene > propene > ethylene.

Question 2 of 10
What is the main product when propene is treated with Cl2 in water?

Solution: Chlorine addition in water forms a chloronium ion which is opened by H2O at the more substituted carbon, yielding 1-chloropropan-2-ol.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following compounds does not undergo nucleophilic addition easily?

Solution: Diethyl ketone has two bulky ethyl groups causing significant steric hindrance and electron-donating inductive effect, lowering carbonyl reactivity.

Question 4 of 10
Oxymercuration-demercuration of 3-methyl-1-butene gives predominantly:

Solution: Oxymercuration proceeds via a mercurinium ion without carbocation rearrangement, yielding 3-methyl-2-butanol according to Markovnikov orientation.

Question 5 of 10
In the reaction of Grignard reagent with carbon dioxide followed by acidification, the product is:

Solution: Nucleophilic addition of Grignard to CO2 gives a carboxylate salt, which on acidification yields a carboxylic acid.

Question 6 of 10
Which species acts as the electrophile in the nitration of alkenes or associated addition pathways?

Solution: Nitronium ion (NO2+) functions as the active electrophile.

Question 7 of 10
Addition of bromine to trans-2-butene gives:

Solution: Anti-addition of bromine to trans-2-butene yields the meso form due to molecular symmetry.

Question 8 of 10
Which reaction sequence converts an alkene to an aldehyde with one carbon atom less?

Solution: Ozonolysis of alkenes cleaves the carbon-carbon double bond. Reductive workup with Zn/H2O yields aldehydes or ketones.

Question 9 of 10
What is the product when phenylmagnesium bromide reacts with acetaldehyde followed by hydrolysis?

Solution: Grignard addition to acetaldehyde yields a secondary alcohol, specifically 1-phenylethanol.

Question 10 of 10
The stereochemical outcome of catalytic hydrogenation of alkenes is generally:

Solution: Catalytic hydrogenation on metal surfaces occurs via simultaneous delivery of hydrogen atoms from the metal surface, resulting in syn-addition.
