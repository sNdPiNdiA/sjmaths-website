Electrophilic Addition of Alkenes

Electrophilic addition reactions of alkenes proceed via the initial attack of an electrophile on the electron-rich pi bond to form a carbocation or cyclic intermediate. The subsequent nucleophilic attack yields saturated addition products under various stereochemical outcomes.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Carbocation Intermediates and Markovnikov Regioselectivity

  • The pi bond of the alkene acts as a nucleophile, attacking the electrophilic proton to generate the most stable carbocation intermediate.
  • Carbocation stability follows the order: 3° > 2° > 1°, dictating the primary regiochemical outcome according to Markovnikov's rule.
  • Rearrangements via 1,2-hydride shift or 1,2-methyl shift frequently occur if a less stable carbocation can convert into a more stable tertiary or resonance-stabilized carbocation.

Halogenation and Halonium Ion Stereospecificity

  • Reaction with halogens (Cl2, Br2) proceeds through a three-membered cyclic halonium ion intermediate rather than an open carbocation.
  • The subsequent backside attack by the halide nucleophile results in anti-addition, yielding trans-dihalide stereoisomers from cyclic or acyclic alkenes.
  • In the presence of water, halohydrin formation occurs preferentially where water acts as the nucleophile attacking the more substituted carbon of the halonium bridge.

Radical-Mediated Addition and Hydroboration-Oxidation

  • HBr addition in the presence of peroxides proceeds via a free radical chain mechanism, resulting in anti-Markovnikov product formation.
  • Hydroboration-oxidation of alkenes yields anti-Markovnikov alcohols with syn-stereochemistry via a concerted, four-center transition state.
  • Oxymercuration-demercuration provides Markovnikov alcohols without skeletal rearrangements because the intermediate is a mercurinium ion, preventing carbocation formation.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Addition of hydrogen halides follows Markovnikov regioselectivity exclusively.

Observation: Addition of HBr in the presence of organic peroxides yields anti-Markovnikov products. — The reaction mechanism shifts from an ionic electrophilic addition (carbocation intermediate) to a free-radical chain mechanism.

Exception Halogenation of alkenes gives exclusively anti-addition products via halonium ions.

Observation: Certain alkenes with bulky substituents or specific stabilizing groups can form open carbocations, leading to mixtures of syn and anti addition. — Steric hindrance or strong carbocation stabilization lowers the activation energy for open carbocation formation over cyclic halonium ions.

Exception Hydration of alkenes requires concentrated acid catalysts and proceeds through carbocations.

Observation: Hydroboration-oxidation avoids carbocation intermediates entirely, yielding anti-Markovnikov syn-hydration. — The concerted addition of boron and hydrogen across the pi bond bypasses ionic intermediates and carbocation rearrangements.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Electrophilic addition is initiated by the attack of an electrophile on the electron-rich alkene pi bond.
  • Carbocation stability dictates Markovnikov orientation: 3° > 2° > 1°.
  • Halogenation of alkenes involves a cyclic halonium ion leading exclusively to anti-addition.
  • Peroxide effect is strictly limited to HBr; HCl and HI do not undergo radical anti-Markovnikov addition.
  • Hydroboration-oxidation gives syn-addition and anti-Markovnikov hydration.
  • Ozonolysis with reductive workup (Zn/H2O) converts alkenes into aldehydes and/or ketones.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming HCl and HI undergo anti-Markovnikov addition in the presence of peroxides due to unfavorable thermodynamics of propagation steps.
  • Forgetting to check for carbocation rearrangements (hydride or methyl shifts) in acid-catalyzed additions.
  • Confusing the regiochemistry of acid-catalyzed hydration (Markovnikov) with hydroboration-oxidation (anti-Markovnikov).
  • Failing to account for stereochemistry in cyclic alkene halogenation (e.g., forming trans products from cyclohexene).

⚡ Quick Revision: Electrophilic Addition of Alkenes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Mechanism of Electrophilic Addition

Electrophilic addition begins with the formation of a pi-complex followed by a sigma-complex (carbocation or cyclic ion). The rate-determining step is the formation of this electrophilic intermediate.

Regioselectivity and Rearrangements

Unsymmetrical alkenes yield products dictated by carbocation stability. Rearrangements occur via 1,2-shifts to generate more stable tertiary carbocations whenever possible.

Stereochemical Outcomes

Cyclic intermediates like halonium and mercurinium ions enforce anti-addition. Concerted pericyclic-like pathways such as hydroboration and catalytic hydrogenation enforce syn-addition.

Oxidative Cleavage

Ozonolysis cleaves both sigma and pi bonds of alkenes to form carbonyl compounds, serving as a vital diagnostic tool for determining double bond positions in unknown structures.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Markovnikov's Rule (Regioselective principle)
Electrophile adds to the sp2 carbon bonded to more hydrogen atoms.
Halonium Ion (Cyclic intermediate)
Three-membered ring intermediate formed during halogen addition.
Peroxide Effect (Kharasch effect)
Anti-Markovnikov addition of HBr in the presence of peroxides.
Syn Addition (Same-side addition)
Addition of two groups to the same face of the double bond.
Anti Addition (Opposite-side addition)
Addition of two groups to opposite faces of the double bond.
Ozonolysis (Oxidative cleavage)
Reaction of alkenes with ozone followed by reduction to form carbonyls.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carbocation stability order: 3° > 2° > 1° > Methyl
  • Halogenation involves cyclic halonium intermediate and anti-addition
  • Peroxide effect applies only to HBr addition
  • Hydroboration-oxidation yields anti-Markovnikov syn-alcohols
  • Oxymercuration-demercuration prevents carbocation rearrangements

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkenes act as nucleophiles toward electrophiles due to high electron density in pi bonds.
  • Regioselectivity is governed by intermediate stability (carbocation vs radical).
  • Stereochemistry is dictated by the cyclic or concerted nature of reaction intermediates.
  • Various reagents allow fine control over regiochemical and stereochemical outcomes.

⚖️ Key Differences

Acid-catalyzed hydration VS Hydroboration-oxidation

Hydration is Markovnikov with possible rearrangements; hydroboration is anti-Markovnikov syn-addition without rearrangements.

Question 1 of 18

What is the primary intermediate formed during the electrophilic addition of HCl to propene?

Explanation: Protonation of propene yields a more stable secondary carbocation (isopropyl cation) rather than a primary carbocation.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following reagents shows anti-Markovnikov addition to propene in the presence of peroxides?

Explanation: Only HBr undergoes radical-mediated anti-Markovnikov addition in the presence of peroxides; HCl and HI propagation steps are thermodynamically unfavorable.

Question 3 of 18

The addition of bromine (Br2) to trans-2-butene yields which stereoisomer as the major product?

Explanation: Anti-addition of Br2 to trans-2-butene via a cyclic bromonium ion yields the meso stereoisomer.

Question 4 of 18

What is the key advantage of oxymercuration-demercuration over acid-catalyzed hydration?

Explanation: Oxymercuration-demercuration proceeds via a mercurinium ion intermediate, avoiding carbocation formation and eliminating skeletal rearrangements.

Question 5 of 18

Hydroboration-oxidation of 1-methylcyclopentene yields predominantly which alcohol?

Explanation: Hydroboration-oxidation results in syn-addition of H and OH across the double bond, giving cis-2-methylcyclopentanol.

Question 6 of 18

In ozonolysis of 2-methyl-2-butene followed by reductive workup (Zn/H2O), what products are formed?

Explanation: (CH3)2C=CHCH3 cleaved at the double bond yields acetone (CH3)2C=O and acetaldehyde CH3CHO.

Question 7 of 18

Which carbocation is the most stable among the following?

Explanation: Tertiary butyl cation ((CH3)3C+) is stabilized by hyperconjugation and inductive effects from three methyl groups.

Question 8 of 18

What is the intermediate formed during the reaction of alkenes with cold dilute alkaline KMnO4?

Explanation: Syn-hydroxylation with cold KMnO4 proceeds through a cyclic manganate ester intermediate.

Question 9 of 18

Addition of HBr to 3,3-dimethyl-1-butene yields 2-bromo-2,3-dimethylbutane as the major product. This occurs via:

Explanation: Initial 2° carbocation undergoes a 1,2-methyl shift to form a more stable tertiary carbocation before bromide attack.

Question 10 of 18

What type of reagent is an alkene in electrophilic addition reactions?

Explanation: The pi bond of an alkene is electron-rich, allowing it to donate electron pairs and act as a nucleophile.

Question 11 of 18

Which alkene reacts fastest with electrophilic HBr?

Explanation: 2-Methylpropene has two methyl electron-donating groups attached to the sp2 carbons, stabilizing the transition state leading to the tertiary carbocation.

Question 12 of 18

Catalytic hydrogenation of alkenes is an example of:

Explanation: Both hydrogen atoms adsorb and transfer from the metal catalyst surface to the same face of the alkene, resulting in syn-addition.

Question 13 of 18

What is the product when cyclohexene reacts with Br2 in water (bromohydrin formation)?

Explanation: Bromonium ion opening by water occurs via anti-addition, yielding trans-2-bromocyclohexanol.

Question 14 of 18

Which of the following compounds has the highest heat of hydrogenation per mole?

Explanation: 1-Butene is the least stable alkene among the isomers, so it releases the highest amount of energy upon hydrogenation.

Question 15 of 18

In the ionic addition of HX to an alkene, what is the rate-determining step?

Explanation: The formation of the high-energy carbocation intermediate from the pi complex is the slowest, rate-determining step.

Question 16 of 18

Epoxidation of alkenes using peroxycarboxylic acids (RCO3H) proceeds with:

Explanation: Peracid epoxidation is a concerted reaction delivering both oxygen bonds to the same face of the alkene (syn-addition).

Question 17 of 18

What is formed when propene reacts with concentrated sulfuric acid followed by water hydrolysis?

Explanation: Acid-catalyzed hydration of propene follows Markovnikov's rule to yield 2-propanol.

Question 18 of 18

Which species acts as the electrophile in bromination of alkenes?

Explanation: Polarization of the Br-Br bond by the pi cloud induces an electrophilic bromine species forming a cyclic bromonium ion.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following alkenes reacts most rapidly with electrophilic addition of HCl?

Solution: 2-Methylpropene forms a tertiary carbocation upon protonation, which has the lowest activation energy.

Question 2 of 10
What is the major product of the reaction between 2-methyl-2-butene and HI?

Solution: Markovnikov addition places the iodide on the tertiary carbon, yielding 2-iodo-2-methylbutane.

Question 3 of 10
Which reagent pair is used for hydroboration-oxidation of alkenes?

Solution: Hydroboration uses diborane (or BH3-THF) followed by alkaline hydrogen peroxide oxidation.

Question 4 of 10
In the halogenation of cis-2-butene with Cl2, the stereochemical outcome is:

Solution: Anti-addition of Cl2 to cis-2-butene yields the dl-pair (enantiomers) of 2,3-dichlorobutane.

Question 5 of 10
What causes the anti-Markovnikov addition of HBr in the presence of peroxides?

Solution: Peroxides generate bromine radicals which add to the alkene to form the more stable carbon radical, reversing regioselectivity.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following undergoes acid-catalyzed hydration with a rearrangement of the carbon skeleton?

Solution: Protonation of 3,3-dimethyl-1-butene forms a 2° carbocation that rearranges via 1,2-methyl shift to a more stable 3° carbocation.

Question 7 of 10
What is the oxidation state change for carbon when an alkene is converted to a diol using cold KMnO4?

Solution: Each sp2 carbon bonded to H and C is oxidized to a C-OH carbon, increasing oxidation state by 1.

Question 8 of 10
Which intermediate is common in the addition of bromine to alkenes in non-polar solvents?

Solution: Bromination proceeds via a bridged cyclic bromonium ion intermediate due to participation of bromine lone pairs.

Question 9 of 10
What products are obtained from the ozonolysis of 1-butene followed by Zn/H2O workup?

Solution: CH2=CHCH2CH3 cleaves to form HCHO (formaldehyde) and CH3CH2CHO (propionaldehyde).

Question 10 of 10
Which reaction does NOT involve a carbocation intermediate?

Solution: Hydroboration is a concerted reaction proceeding through a four-center transition state without carbocation intermediates.
