Organic Chemistry

Concept of Reaction Mechanism

A reaction mechanism details the sequential step-by-step sequence of elementary reactions by which overall chemical change occurs. It accounts for bond cleavage, bond formation, intermediates, and transition states.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Bond Fission and Reactive Intermediates

  • Homolytic cleavage results in the formation of free radicals possessing an unpaired electron, favored by UV light, peroxides, or high heat.
  • Heterolytic cleavage generates carbocations and carbanions, favored by polar solvents and heteroatom electronegativity differences.
  • Carbocations are sp2 hybridized with a vacant p-orbital, exhibiting planar geometry and stability order 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl via hyperconjugation and inductive effects.

Types of Reagents and Electronic Effects

  • Electrophiles are electron-deficient species (Lewis acids) that attack regions of high electron density, while nucleophiles are electron-rich species (Lewis bases).
  • The inductive effect (-I/+I) operates through sigma bonds and decreases rapidly with increasing distance from the functional group.
  • Resonance effect (-R/+R) involves the delocalization of π-electrons through conjugated systems, significantly altering electron density distributions.

Methods for Determining Reaction Mechanisms

  • Isotope labeling using deuterium (D) or 14C identifies specific bond-breaking events and rearrangement pathways.
  • Kinetic isotope effects (KIE), particularly kH/kD values, determine whether a rate-determining step involves C-H bond cleavage.
  • The detection and trapping of transient intermediates using radical scavengers or spectroscopic methods confirm proposed pathway steps.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Rate Law Expression
Rate = k[A]x[B]y
Conditions: Elementary or overall step rate dependence on reactant concentrations
Units: Rate in mol L-1 s-1, k in concentration dependent units
Hammett Equation
log(k/k0) = ρσ
Conditions: Linear free energy relationship for aromatic side-chain reactions
Units: ρ is reaction constant, σ is substituent constant (dimensionless)
Eyring-Polanyi Equation
k = (kBT/h) e-ΔG‡/RT
Conditions: Transition state theory calculation of reaction rates
Units: kB is Boltzmann constant, h is Planck constant, ΔG‡ in J mol-1
Arrhenius Equation
k = A e-Ea/RT
Conditions: Temperature dependence of reaction rate constants
Units: k in s-1 or mol L-1 s-1, Ea in J mol-1, R in J K-1 mol-1, T in K
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception 3° Carbocations are universally more stable than 1° carbocations.

Observation: Bridgehead carbocations in small bicyclic systems (e.g., 1-bromobicyclo[2.2.1]heptane derivatives) resist formation. — Bredt's rule prohibits sp2 hybridization at bridgehead carbons due to severe ring strain and angular distortion.

Exception Electron-withdrawing groups decrease electrophilic aromatic substitution reaction rates.

Observation: Halogens are ortho/para-directing despite being electron-withdrawing via the inductive effect. — Strong +R resonance donation from lone pairs outweighs the -I electron withdrawal during the intermediate sigma-complex stabilization.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Homolytic bond dissociation energy determines radical reaction initiation feasibility.
  • Carbanions are sp3 hybridized with a pyramidal geometry, stabilized by -I and -R groups.
  • Hyperconjugation involves the overlap of σC-H electrons with an empty p-orbital.
  • The Hammett reaction constant (ρ) measures susceptibility to substituent electronic effects; positive ρ indicates accumulation of negative charge in transition state.
  • Kinetic isotope effect values exceeding 2 for C-H versus C-D bond cleavage confirm primary rate-determining C-H bond breaking.
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation can undergo carbocation rearrangement to form thermodynamically more stable products.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the order of a reaction with its molecularity; molecularity refers strictly to elementary steps.
  • Assuming all carbocations undergo rearrangement; rearrangements occur only if stability increases (e.g., 2° to 3°).
  • Neglecting solvent effects when predicting SN1 versus SN2 pathways.
  • Failing to account for stereochemical inversion in SN2 reactions when assigning product configurations.

⚡ Quick Revision: Concept of Reaction Mechanism

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Bond Fission Dynamics

Homolytic fission generates neutral radicals, whereas heterolytic fission generates charged ions depending on electronegativity differences and solvent stabilization.

Reactive Intermediates

Carbocations, carbanions, free radicals, carbenes, and arynes serve as short-lived high-energy chemical species dictating organic reaction pathways.

Electronic Effects

Inductive, resonance, hyperconjugation, and steric effects systematically direct bond polarization, intermediate stability, and site-selectivity.

Mechanistic Elucidation

Reaction mechanisms are established via kinetic analysis, isolation of intermediates, isotopic labeling experiments, and stereochemical studies.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Electrophile (E+)
Electron-deficient species seeking electron pairs.
Nucleophile (Nu-)
Electron-rich species donating electron pairs.
Carbocation (R3C+)
Carbon species bearing a positive charge and sextet of electrons.
Carbanion (R3C-)
Carbon species bearing a negative charge and an unshared electron pair.
Free Radical (R•)
Neutral species possessing an unpaired electron.
Transition State ([TS]‡)
Energy maximum along the reaction coordinate with partial bonds.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • SN2 proceeds via 100% inversion of configuration.
  • SN1 proceeds via racemization through a planar carbocation.
  • E1 and E2 mechanisms compete directly with substitution reactions under basic or acidic conditions.
  • Carbocation stability order: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3+.
  • Carbanion stability order: CH3- > 1° > 2° > 3°.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Reaction mechanisms track bond breaking and formation steps.
  • Intermediates represent energy minima, while transition states represent energy maxima.
  • Electronic effects dictate the regioselectivity of electrophilic and nucleophilic attacks.
  • Isotope labeling provides unequivocal proof of specific bond cleavage timing.

⚖️ Key Differences

Inductive Effect VS Resonance Effect

Inductive operates through sigma bonds and drops with distance; resonance operates through conjugated pi systems over entire chains.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following reactive intermediates has an octet of electrons around the central carbon?

Explanation: Carbanions possess eight electrons in their valence shell (a sextet plus a lone pair), whereas carbocations have six and free radicals have seven.

Question 2 of 19

What is the hybridization of the central carbon in a simple carbocation?

Explanation: Carbocations have three bond pairs and zero lone pairs around the central carbon, resulting in trigonal planar geometry with sp2 hybridization.

Question 3 of 19

Which solvent best promotes an SN2 reaction mechanism?

Explanation: Polar aprotic solvents like DMSO dissolve reactants without strongly solvating nucleophiles, leaving them highly reactive for SN2 attacks.

Question 4 of 19

What type of cleavage results in the formation of free radicals?

Explanation: Homolytic cleavage involves symmetrical bond breaking where each bonded atom retains one electron of the shared pair.

Question 5 of 19

Which effect is responsible for the stability of substituted alkenes?

Explanation: Hyperconjugation between C-H sigma bonds of alkyl groups and pi bonds of alkenes stabilizes substituted alkenes (Saytzeff's rule).

Question 6 of 19

In the Hammett equation log(k/k0) = ρσ, what does a positive value of ρ indicate?

Explanation: A positive ρ value indicates that electron-withdrawing groups accelerate the reaction because negative charge builds up in the rate-determining transition state.

Question 7 of 19

Walden inversion is a characteristic feature of which mechanism?

Explanation: SN2 reactions proceed via backside attack, causing complete inversion of configuration known as Walden inversion.

Question 8 of 19

Which of the following is an ambient nucleophile?

Explanation: Cyanide ion can attack through carbon or nitrogen, making it an ambident nucleophile.

Question 9 of 19

What is the primary intermediate in an E1 elimination reaction?

Explanation: E1 reactions proceed in two steps: ionization to form a carbocation, followed by proton abstraction by a base.

Question 10 of 19

Which carbocation is the most stable?

Explanation: Tertiary butyl cation ((CH3)3C+) is 3° and stabilized by nine hyperconjugative alpha-hydrogens.

Question 11 of 19

Which of the following is electrophilic in nature?

Explanation: Aluminum trichloride (AlCl3) is an electron-deficient Lewis acid and acts as an electrophile.

Question 12 of 19

What is the kinetic order of an SN1 reaction?

Explanation: SN1 is unimolecular; the rate depends only on the substrate concentration, making it first order overall.

Question 13 of 19

Which electronic displacement effect is permanent?

Explanation: Both inductive and resonance effects are permanent polarization effects in molecules, whereas the electromeric effect is temporary.

Question 14 of 19

Deuterium isotope effect is used primarily to study:

Explanation: Kinetic isotope effects measure changes in reaction rates when hydrogen is replaced by deuterium, identifying if C-H bond cleavage occurs in the rate-determining step.

Question 15 of 19

Which species acts as a Lewis base?

Explanation: Methylamine has a lone pair on nitrogen and acts as an electron-pair donor (Lewis base/nucleophile).

Question 16 of 19

What intermediate is formed during the nitration of benzene?

Explanation: Electrophilic aromatic substitution proceeds through a resonance-stabilized sigma complex or arenium ion intermediate.

Question 17 of 19

Which of the following compounds has the highest boiling point due to intermolecular hydrogen bonding?

Explanation: Ethanol possesses O-H bonding capable of strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding.

Question 18 of 19

The order of stability of free radicals is:

Explanation: Free radicals follow the same stability order as carbocations (3° > 2° > 1° > Methyl) due to hyperconjugation.

Question 19 of 19

Which reagent causes the conversion of alkenes to vicinal diols with syn-stereochemistry?

Explanation: Cold dilute alkaline KMnO4 (Baeyer's reagent) hydroxylates alkenes with syn-addition.

What is the primary role of Lewis acids in electrophilic aromatic substitution reactions?

Solution: Lewis acids (like FeCl3 or AlCl3) polarize or react with reagents to generate strong electrophilic species.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following compounds exhibits optical activity?

Solution: D-lactic acid contains a chiral center without a plane of symmetry, making it optically active.

Question 3 of 10
In elimination reactions, Saytzeff's rule predicts that the major product is:

Solution: Saytzeff's rule states that elimination yields the most substituted, thermodynamically stable alkene.

Question 4 of 10
Which species is formed when benzene reacts with chlorine in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3?

Solution: Electrophilic aromatic substitution of benzene with chlorine and AlCl3 yields chlorobenzene.

Question 5 of 10
What is the stereochemical outcome of an SN1 reaction at a single chiral center?

Solution: SN1 goes through a planar carbocation intermediate attacked equally from both faces, resulting in a racemic mixture.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following is a nucleophile?

Solution: Cyanide ion (CN-) has a negative charge and lone pair, serving as a classic nucleophile.

Question 7 of 10
Which compound does not undergo Friedel-Crafts reaction?

Solution: Nitrobenzene possesses a strong deactivating nitro group (-R/-I) that halts Friedel-Crafts alkylation and acylation.

Question 8 of 10
The temporary displacement of pi electrons under the influence of an attacking reagent is called:

Solution: The electromeric effect is a temporary polarization occurring only in the presence of an attacking reagent.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following carbocations is least stable?

Solution: Methyl cation (CH3+) has no alpha-hydrogens for hyperconjugation and lacks alkyl inductive stabilization.

Question 10 of 10
What is the role of Zn dust in the ozonolysis reductive workup?

Solution: Zinc destroys hydrogen peroxide formed during reductive workup, preventing carboxylic acid formation from aldehydes.
