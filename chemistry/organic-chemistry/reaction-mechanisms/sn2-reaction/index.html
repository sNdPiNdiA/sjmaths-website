SN2 Reaction

The SN2 reaction is a bimolecular nucleophilic substitution process that proceeds via a concerted mechanism with complete inversion of configuration. The reaction rate is strictly dependent on the concentration of both the substrate and the nucleophile, following second-order kinetics.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Stereochemical Consequences and Walden Inversion

  • The reaction proceeds exclusively with backside attack of the nucleophile relative to the leaving group.
  • This spatial approach causes a complete Walden inversion of the tetrahedral configuration, akin to an umbrella turning inside out.
  • Optically active substrates undergo racemization accompanied by complete inversion to yield a single enantiomer product of opposite configuration.

Transition State Geometry and Energetics

  • The SN2 pathway occurs in a single concerted step without the formation of any discrete carbocation intermediate.
  • The transition state possesses a pentacoordinate carbon where both the incoming nucleophile and leaving group share partial bonds.
  • The central carbon in the transition state adopts an sp2 hybridization with the unbonded p-orbital perpendicular to the attacking substituents.

Substrate Structure and Solvent Effects

  • Steric hindrance drastically retards the rate, establishing the reactivity order: Methyl > 1° > 2° >> 3° alkyl halides.
  • Polar aprotic solvents like acetone, DMSO, DMF, or acetonitrile accelerate the reaction by failing to solvate the nucleophile effectively.
  • Strong, negatively charged nucleophiles such as CN-, I-, RS-, and OH- favor the bimolecular pathway.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Tertiary alkyl halides do not undergo SN2 substitution due to extreme steric congestion.

Observation: Certain bridgehead tertiary halides or specific strained ring systems can occasionally exhibit modified reactivity profiles under extreme conditions. — Extreme geometric constraints prevent the formation of the planar transition state required for backside attack.

Exception Vinyl and aryl halides are completely unreactive toward SN2 substitution.

Observation: Activation by strong electron-withdrawing groups ortho/para to the leaving group can shift the mechanism toward SNAR addition-elimination. — The sp2 hybridized carbon-halogen bond possesses higher bond energy and electron density repulsion prevents nucleophilic approach.

Exception Neopentyl halides are primary alkyl halides and should theoretically react rapidly via SN2.

Observation: Neopentyl halides react extremely slowly or not at all under standard SN2 conditions. — The bulky methyl groups on the adjacent β-carbon create insurmountable steric hindrance blocking the backside attack trajectory.

Solvent Polarity Rule
PROTIC = SN1, APROTIC = SN2

Application: Identify solvent type instantly to determine the preferred substitution pathway; protic solvents hydrogen-bond nucleophiles, while aprotic solvents free them up.

Leaving Group Ability Scale
I- > Br- > Cl- >> F-

Application: Correlate leaving group ability directly with weak basicity and strong acid strength (conjugate bases of strong acids leave best).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • SN2 reactions follow second-order kinetics overall, first order with respect to substrate and first order with respect to nucleophile.
  • The stereochemical outcome of an SN2 reaction is 100% inversion of configuration (Walden inversion).
  • Reactivity of alkyl halides follows the sequence: R-I > R-Br > R-Cl >> R-F based on carbon-halogen bond strength and leaving group ability.
  • Polar aprotic solvents (such as DMSO, acetone, DMF) enhance SN2 reaction rates by leaving the nucleophile unsolvated and highly reactive.
  • Tertiary alkyl halides undergo negligible SN2 substitution due to prohibitive steric hindrance around the reaction center.
  • Ambident nucleophiles like cyanide (CN-) and nitrite (NO2-) can attack through multiple atoms depending on conditions.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming retention of configuration; remember that SN2 always causes inversion (Walden inversion).
  • Confusing the solvent effects: polar protic solvents stabilize carbocations for SN1, whereas polar aprotic solvents promote SN2.
  • Treating neopentyl halides as normal primary halides; their massive steric hindrance makes them virtually unreactive to SN2.
  • Writing fractional or zero-order rate laws for a bimolecular concerted displacement step.

⚡ Quick Revision: SN2 Reaction

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Concerted Bimolecular Kinetics

The SN2 mechanism is a single-step concerted pathway where bond breaking and bond formation occur simultaneously. The reaction rate depends on the concentration of both reactants, yielding a second-order rate equation.

Steric Control and Substrate Effects

Steric hindrance around the electrophilic carbon is the primary factor dictating SN2 feasibility. Methyl and primary substrates react rapidly, while secondary substrates react moderately and tertiary substrates are inert.

Stereochemical Inversion

Due to backside attack requirements, the nucleophile approaches from the face opposite the leaving group. This results in the complete inversion of the tetrahedral stereocenter configuration.

Nucleophile and Solvent Influence

Strong, non-bulky, anionic nucleophiles combined with polar aprotic solvents maximize the nucleophilic attack rate by minimizing transition-state energy barriers.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Walden Inversion (Stereochemical Inversion)
The inversion of a chiral center during a bimolecular nucleophilic substitution reaction.
Bimolecular (Second-Order Kinetics)
Rate law dependent on the concentrations of two reacting species.
Backside Attack (Trajectory)
The approach of a nucleophile at 180 degrees relative to the leaving group bond.
Polar Aprotic (Solvent Class)
Solvents lacking acidic hydrogen atoms that do not solvate anions strongly (e.g., acetone, DMSO).
Leaving Group (Nucleofuge)
An atom or group that detaches with an electron pair during heterolytic bond cleavage.
Transition State (Activated Complex)
The highest energy state along the reaction coordinate with a partially formed and partially broken bond.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Rate = k [Substrate] [Nucleophile]
  • Reactivity order: Methyl > 1° > 2° >> 3°
  • Stereochemistry: 100% Inversion (Walden inversion)
  • Preferred solvents: Polar aprotic (DMSO, DMF, Acetone)
  • Leaving group sequence: I- > Br- > Cl- >> F-

📌 Quick Recall

  • SN2 reactions occur in a single concerted step with no intermediates.
  • Steric hindrance is the main barrier to SN2 reactivity.
  • Backside attack forces a complete inversion of stereochemical configuration.
  • Strong nucleophiles and polar aprotic solvents are essential for optimal reaction rates.

⚖️ Key Differences

SN1 Reaction VS SN2 Reaction

SN1 proceeds via a carbocation intermediate with racemization; SN2 is a concerted single-step process with inversion.

Which solvent is most suitable for promoting an SN2 reaction?

What is the hybridization of the central carbon in the transition state of an SN2 reaction?

Explanation: In the pentacoordinate transition state, the central carbon adopts sp2 hybridization with the remaining p-orbital overlapping the incoming and leaving groups.

Question 7 of 19

Why do tertiary alkyl halides fail to undergo SN2 reactions?

Explanation: The three bulky alkyl groups on a tertiary carbon block the backside attack trajectory of the nucleophile entirely.

Question 8 of 19

Which of the following nucleophiles is considered weakest in polar aprotic solvents?

Explanation: Fluoride is small, highly charge-dense, and strongly hydrogen-bonded or tightly held in aprotic media compared to larger polarizable nucleophiles.

Question 9 of 19

How does doubling the concentration of the nucleophile affect the rate of an SN2 reaction?

Explanation: Because the reaction is first-order with respect to the nucleophile, doubling its concentration doubles the reaction rate.

Question 10 of 19

Which substrate is classified as a neopentyl halide and is unreactive toward SN2?

Explanation: 1-bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane (neopentyl bromide) has a primary carbon attached to a bulky quaternary beta-carbon, causing extreme steric hindrance.

Question 11 of 19

In an SN2 displacement of (R)-2-bromobutane with sodium hydroxide, what is the configuration of the major product?

Explanation: The backside attack causes an inversion of configuration, converting (R)-2-bromobutane into (S)-butan-2-ol.

Question 12 of 19

Which of the following conditions favors SN2 over SN1?

Explanation: High concentrations of strong nucleophiles and polar aprotic solvents favor the bimolecular SN2 pathway.

Question 13 of 19

What type of intermediate is formed during an SN2 reaction?

Explanation: The SN2 reaction is concerted and proceeds through a single transition state without forming any stable intermediates.

Question 14 of 19

Which halogen atom leaves fastest from an alkyl halide in substitution reactions?

Explanation: The carbon-iodine bond is the weakest and longest, and iodide is the best leaving group.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following compounds will react most rapidly with potassium iodide in acetone?

Explanation: 1-bromopropane is a primary halide with a good leaving group (Br-), making it very reactive in the Finkelstein reaction (SN2).

Question 16 of 19

What is the effect of increasing solvent polarity from hexane to DMSO on an SN2 reaction involving a neutral nucleophile and alkyl halide?

Explanation: Polar aprotic solvents like DMSO stabilize the transition state and increase the ground-state energy of anionic or polar nucleophiles, accelerating SN2 reactions.

Question 17 of 19

Which reaction coordinate profile corresponds to an SN2 mechanism?

Explanation: A concerted single-step reaction like SN2 features a single energy barrier (transition state) and no intermediate valley.

Question 18 of 19

Which species is a poor nucleophile but a strong base capable of causing elimination instead of SN2?

Explanation: Bulky bases like tert-butoxide are sterically hindered, making proton abstraction (E2) much faster than backside SN2 attack.

Question 19 of 19

In the reaction of ethyl bromide with aqueous NaOH, what happens to the rate if the concentration of both reactants is tripled?

Explanation: Rate = k [Substrate][Nucleophile]. Tripling both terms results in 3 × 3 = 9 times the original rate.

What is the order of reactivity of alkyl chlorides in an SN2 reaction?

Solution: Steric hindrance dictates the SN2 reactivity order: Methyl (CH3Cl) > Primary > Secondary > Tertiary.

Question 2 of 10
Which nucleophile will react slowest in an SN2 reaction in a polar aprotic solvent?

Solution: Fluoride is the smallest, most strongly solvated, and least polarizable halide, making it the slowest SN2 nucleophile among the choices.

Question 3 of 10
When (S)-2-bromobutane reacts with sodium cyanide via an SN2 mechanism, what is the configuration of the nitrile product?

Solution: SN2 proceeds with complete inversion. The (S) precursor inverts to yield the (R) nitrile product.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following is a polar aprotic solvent?

Solution: DMSO is a polar aprotic solvent because it lacks O-H or N-H bonds capable of hydrogen bonding to nucleophiles.

Question 5 of 10
What is the effect of branching at the β-carbon on SN2 reaction velocity?

Solution: Branching at the β-carbon (such as in neopentyl halides) increases steric congestion around the reaction center, decreasing SN2 velocity.

Question 6 of 10
Which rate equation correctly represents an SN2 process?

Solution: The bimolecular rate law depends on the concentrations of both the substrate and the nucleophile.

Question 7 of 10
Why is sodium iodide in acetone used to convert alkyl chlorides to alkyl iodides (Finkelstein reaction)?

Solution: Sodium chloride is insoluble in acetone and precipitates, shifting the SN2 equilibrium toward the alkyl iodide product.

Question 8 of 10
Which substrate is completely unreactive toward SN2 substitution under standard conditions?

Solution: Tert-butyl chloride is a tertiary alkyl halide whose severe steric hindrance completely blocks the SN2 pathway.

Question 9 of 10
In terms of leaving group ability, which of the following is correct?

Solution: Neutral water (H2O) is a weak base and an excellent leaving group compared to hydroxide ion (OH-), which is a strong base and poor leaving group.

Question 10 of 10
What geometry does the transition state approximate during an SN2 displacement?

Solution: The pentacoordinate carbon in the transition state has three equatorial substituents in a plane and two axial bonds connected to the nucleophile and leaving group, resembling a trigonal bipyramid.
