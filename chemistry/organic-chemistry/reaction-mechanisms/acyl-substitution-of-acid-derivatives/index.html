Acyl Substitution of Acid Derivatives

Carboxylic acid derivatives undergo nucleophilic acyl substitution via a tetrahedral addition-elimination mechanism. The overall reactivity of these derivatives is dictated by the basicity of the leaving group and resonance stabilization of the starting carbonyl compound.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mechanism of Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution

  • The reaction proceeds via a tetrahedral intermediate formed by nucleophilic attack on the sp2 hybridized carbonyl carbon.
  • The collapse of the tetrahedral intermediate expels the leaving group, regenerating the carbon-oxygen double bond.
  • Unlike aldehydes and ketones which undergo nucleophilic addition, acid derivatives undergo addition-elimination.
  • The rate-determining step for basic hydrolysis is typically the formation of the tetrahedral intermediate.

Reactivity Factors and Leaving Group Basicity

  • Leaving group ability is inversely proportional to its basicity and directly proportional to the acidity of its conjugate acid.
  • Acyl chlorides are the most reactive due to the poor basicity and weak solvation requirement of the chloride ion (Cl-).
  • Amides are the least reactive because the amino group (-NH2) is a strong base and a poor leaving group.
  • Resonance donation from heteroatom lone pairs (O, N) reduces the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon, lowering reactivity.

Interconversion and Functional Group Transformations

  • A more reactive acid derivative can be easily converted into a less reactive derivative via nucleophilic acyl substitution.
  • Conversion of carboxylic acids to acyl chlorides requires activating reagents such as SOCl2, PCl3, or PCl5.
  • Esters react with primary and secondary amines in aminolysis to yield amides and alcohols.
  • Acid anhydrides react with alcohols under mild conditions to form esters and carboxylic acids without strong acid catalysts.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Saponification Rate Equation
Rate = k[RCOOR'][OH-]
Conditions: Base-catalyzed ester hydrolysis (Second-order overall)
Units: k in mol-1 dm3 s-1
Acid-Catalyzed Ester Hydrolysis Rate
Rate = k[RCOOR'][H+][H2O]
Conditions: Low pH aqueous medium, pseudo-first order under excess water
Units: k in mol-2 dm6 s-1
Schotten-Baumann Reaction Rate
Rate = k[RCOCl][R'OH][OH-]
Conditions: Aqueous alkaline medium trapping liberated HCl
Units: k in mol-2 dm6 s-1
Relative Reactivity Order of Acid Derivatives
RCOCl > (RCO)2O > RCOOR' > RCONH2
Conditions: Based on leaving group ability (pKa of conjugate acid: HCl(-7) < RCOOH(4.8) < ROH(16) < NH3(38))
Units: Dimensionless reactivity scale
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Less reactive acid derivatives cannot directly convert to more reactive derivatives.

Observation: Direct synthesis of anhydrides from carboxylate salts and acyl chlorides. — The driving force is the creation of a thermodynamic sink via coupling highly reactive acyl intermediates with good nucleophiles.

Exception Steric hindrance always decreases nucleophilic attack rates.

Observation: Tertiary alcohols can form esters with acyl chlorides in pyridine (Schotten-Baumann variant). — Pyridine acts as a mild base and catalyst, forming an active N-acylpyridinium intermediate that increases electrophilicity.

Exception Esters undergo base-catalyzed hydrolysis irreversibly.

Observation: Saponification with bulky bases (e.g., lithium diisopropylamide) can yield alternative condensation products. — Steric bulk suppresses standard acyl substitution and favors proton abstraction (enolization).

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Leaving Group Basicity Rule
Reactivity ∝ 1 / Basicity of Leaving Group

Application: Compare the pKa of the conjugate acid of the leaving group; lower pKa means better leaving group and higher acyl derivative reactivity.

Interconversion Hierarchy Flow
Cl- > RCOO- > RO- > NH2-

Application: Always point reaction arrows from left to right in the hierarchy; converting right to left requires forcing conditions or coupling reagents (e.g., DCC).

Tetrahedral Intermediate Stability
EDG on Acyl Group → Unstable Intermediate → Slow Substitution

Application: Identify electron-donating groups (amides, esters) which increase electron density on the carbonyl carbon via resonance, reducing attack rates.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Acyl chlorides react violently with water to form carboxylic acids and hydrochloric acid.
  • Esters undergo transesterification in the presence of acid (H+) or base (OR-) catalysts.
  • Amides undergo Hofmann bromamide degradation with Br2 and KOH to form primary amines with one less carbon.
  • Lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4) reduces esters, acid chlorides, and anhydrides to primary alcohols.
  • Diisobutylaluminum hydride (DIBAL-H) at low temperatures (-78 °C) reduces esters selectively to aldehydes.
  • N,N-Dicyclohexylcarbodiimide (DCC) is a common dehydrating agent used to couple carboxylic acids with amines to form amides.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing nucleophilic addition in aldehydes/ketones with nucleophilic acyl substitution in acid derivatives.
  • Assuming amides can be hydrolyzed in neutral water without harsh acidic or basic catalysts and high temperatures.
  • Neglecting the reversibility of acid-catalyzed esterification and hydrolysis reactions.
  • Forgetting that Grignard reagents add twice to esters and acid chlorides to form tertiary alcohols, but only once to nitriles or via specific modified reagents to other derivatives.

⚡ Quick Revision: Acyl Substitution of Acid Derivatives

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Mechanism Framework

Acyl substitution proceeds through a two-step addition-elimination pathway involving a high-energy tetrahedral intermediate.

Leaving Group Basicity

Reactivity is directly governed by the basicity of the departing group; weaker bases serve as better leaving groups.

Interconversion Rules

Derivatives can be transformed downward in the reactivity series spontaneously, while upward conversions require specialized reagents.

Reduction Pathways

Different hydride donors (LiAlH4, NaBH4, DIBAL-H) exhibit varying chemoselectivity toward acid derivatives.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Acyl Group (R-C(=O)-)
Functional group derived by the removal of hydroxyl group from an oxoacid.
Tetrahedral Intermediate ([R-C(O-)(Nu)(LG)])
High-energy sp3 intermediate formed during nucleophilic attack.
Transesterification (RCOOR' + ROH → RCOOR'' + ROH)
Exchange of the alkoxy group of an ester with an external alcohol.
Aminolysis (RCOCl + 2R'NH2 → RCONHR' + R'NH3+Cl-)
Reaction of an acid derivative with an amine to yield an amide.
Saponification (RCOOR' + OH- → RCOO- + R'OH)
Base-promoted hydrolysis of an ester yielding a carboxylate salt and alcohol.
Schotten-Baumann (RCOCl + R'OH + NaOH)
Acylation of alcohols or amines in aqueous alkaline medium.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Reactivity order: RCOCl > (RCO)2O > RCOOR' > RCONH2
  • Acid derivatives undergo addition-elimination, not simple substitution or direct addition.
  • Leaving group ability follows: Cl- > RCOO- > RO- > NH2-
  • Grignard reagents react with esters and acyl chlorides to yield tertiary alcohols.
  • DIBAL-H at -78 °C reduces esters to aldehydes.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Acyl substitution requires a tetrahedral intermediate and a suitable leaving group.
  • Stronger basic leaving groups make the parent derivative less reactive toward nucleophiles.
  • Interconversions allow synthesis of esters and amides from acyl chlorides and anhydrides.
  • Reduction reactions convert acid derivatives into alcohols, aldehydes, or amines depending on the reagent.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nucleophilic Addition VS Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution

Addition occurs in aldehydes/ketones without regeneration of C=O; substitution occurs in acid derivatives via elimination of a leaving group, restoring the C=O bond.

Question 1 of 20

Which of the following acid derivatives is the most reactive toward nucleophilic acyl substitution?

Explanation: Acetyl chloride possesses chloride ion as a leaving group, which is the weakest base among the options, making it the most reactive derivative.

Question 2 of 20

What is the intermediate formed during the nucleophilic acyl substitution of an ester?

Explanation: The attack of a nucleophile on the sp2 carbonyl carbon generates an sp3 hybridized tetrahedral intermediate.

Question 3 of 20

In the base-catalyzed hydrolysis of esters, what is the nature of the reaction overall?

Explanation: Saponification follows second-order kinetics, being first order with respect to both the ester and the hydroxide ion: Rate = k[Ester][OH-].

Question 4 of 20

Which reagent selectively reduces an ester to an aldehyde at -78 °C?

Explanation: Diisobutylaluminum hydride (DIBAL-H) at low temperatures (-78 °C) halts reduction at the aldehyde stage by forming a stable intermediate complex.

Question 5 of 20

What is the conjugate acid pKa value associated with the leaving group of an amide?

Explanation: The conjugate acid of the amide leaving group (-NH2) is ammonia (NH3), which has a high pKa of approximately 38.

Question 6 of 20

Which reaction describes the conversion of a carboxylic acid to an acid chloride using thionyl chloride?

Explanation: Thionyl chloride (SOCl2) reacts with carboxylic acids to yield acyl chlorides with gaseous byproducts (SO2 and HCl), leaving a clean product.

Question 7 of 20

Why are amides the least reactive carboxylic acid derivatives?

Explanation: The nitrogen atom donates its lone pair via resonance (+M), reducing carbonyl electrophilicity, and the amino group is a strong base (poor leaving group).

Question 8 of 20

What happens when an ester reacts with excess Grignard reagent followed by aqueous workup?

Explanation: Esters react with Grignard reagents to form ketones, which immediately react with a second equivalent of Grignard reagent to yield tertiary alcohols.

Question 9 of 20

Which of the following compounds does not undergo nucleophilic acyl substitution readily?

Explanation: Acetamide has the least reactive acyl group due to the strongly basic amide leaving group.

Question 10 of 20

Transesterification is best promoted by:

Explanation: Transesterification requires either acid catalysis (protonating carbonyl to enhance electrophilicity) or base catalysis (generating alkoxide nucleophile).

Question 11 of 20

In the Schotten-Baumann reaction, what is the role of aqueous NaOH?

Explanation: NaOH neutralizes the HCl byproduct generated during the acylation of alcohols or amines by acyl chlorides, preventing protonation of the amine nucleophile.

Question 12 of 20

Which acid derivative yields a nitrile upon dehydration with P2O5?

Explanation: Primary amides undergo dehydration when treated with powerful dehydrating agents like P2O5 or SOCl2 to form nitriles.

Question 13 of 20

Which of the following reducing agents reduces acid chlorides specifically to aldehydes without over-reduction?

Explanation: The Rosenmund reduction uses poisoned palladium on barium sulfate to reduce acyl chlorides to aldehydes safely.

Question 14 of 20

What is the product of the reaction between an acid anhydride and a primary amine?

Explanation: Aminolysis of acid anhydrides yields one equivalent of an amide and one equivalent of a carboxylic acid salt or acid.

Question 15 of 20

Why can acetic acid not be directly converted into ethyl acetate without a catalyst?

Explanation: Esterification is an equilibrium process requiring acid catalysis to increase carbonyl electrophilicity and removal of water to drive the reaction forward.

Question 16 of 20

What is the intermediate formed during the Hofmann bromamide degradation of amides?

Explanation: Hofmann degradation proceeds through an alkyl isocyanate intermediate formed via rearrangement of an N-bromoamide.

Question 17 of 20

Which solvent is commonly used in reactions involving sensitive acyl chlorides to avoid side reactions with water?

Explanation: Anhydrous aprotic solvents such as dichloromethane or diethyl ether prevent premature hydrolysis of highly reactive acyl chlorides.

Question 18 of 20

Which derivative has the lowest leaving group pKa for its conjugate acid?

Explanation: The conjugate acid of Cl- is HCl, which has a pKa of -7, the lowest among all common leaving groups in acid derivatives.

Question 19 of 20

What type of stereochemical outcome is observed when a chiral center is attached to the carbonyl carbon during nucleophilic substitution?

Explanation: Since the planar sp2 carbonyl carbon allows attack from both top and bottom faces of the tetrahedral intermediate, racemization occurs if the chiral center is directly at the acyl carbon.

Question 20 of 20

Which of the following compounds yields acetic acid upon aqueous acidic hydrolysis?

Explanation: Esters, acyl chlorides, and acid anhydrides all hydrolyze to yield acetic acid when treated with water under appropriate conditions.

Question 1 of 10
Which acid derivative reacts slowest with water?

Solution: Acetamide is the least reactive due to strong resonance stabilization and the poor leaving group ability of the amino group.

Question 2 of 10
What is the organic product formed when ethyl acetate is treated with excess methylmagnesium bromide followed by acid workup?

Solution: Ethyl acetate reacts with two equivalents of Grignard reagent to yield a tertiary alcohol, specifically 2-methylpropan-2-ol.

Question 3 of 10
Which catalyst is typically used in Fischer esterification?

Solution: Concentrated sulfuric acid protonates the carbonyl oxygen, increasing its electrophilicity toward alcohol attack in Fischer esterification.

Question 4 of 10
During the reduction of an ester with LiAlH4, how many moles of hydride equivalents are delivered per mole of ester?

Solution: Reduction of an ester to two moles of alcohols (one from acyl portion, one from alkoxy portion) consumes 4 moles of hydride equivalents (typically 1 mole of LiAlH4).

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following compounds will give a positive test with neutral FeCl3 solution?

Solution: Beta-keto esters exist partially in enol forms which form colored coordination complexes with Fe3+ ions.

Question 6 of 10
What is the role of pyridine in the acylation of alcohols using acyl chlorides?

Solution: Pyridine acts as a mild base that neutralizes liberated HCl and can form an active acylpyridinium intermediate.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following acid derivatives has the highest carbonyl stretching frequency (νmax) in IR spectroscopy?

Solution: Acetyl chloride has the highest carbonyl stretching frequency (~1800 cm-1) due to strong inductive electron withdrawal by chlorine and minimal resonance donation.

Question 8 of 10
In transesterification, the use of an excess of the incoming alcohol shifts the equilibrium in accordance with:

Solution: Transesterification is an equilibrium process; adding excess reactant shifts position toward the desired ester product based on Le Chatelier's principle.

Question 9 of 10
Which reagent converts an acid amide to a primary amine having the same number of carbon atoms?

Solution: LiAlH4 reduces amides directly to primary amines without loss of carbon atoms, unlike the Hofmann degradation which removes one carbon.

Question 10 of 10
Why is acyl substitution faster in polar aprotic solvents compared to polar protic solvents for neutral nucleophiles?

Solution: Polar protic solvents solvate neutral or anionic nucleophiles strongly through hydrogen bonding, reducing their availability and nucleophilic strength.
