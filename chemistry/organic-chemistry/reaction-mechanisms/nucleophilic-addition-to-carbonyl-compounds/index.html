Organic Chemistry

Nucleophilic Addition to Carbonyl Compounds

Carbonyl compounds undergo nucleophilic addition due to the polarization of the carbon-oxygen double bond, creating an electrophilic carbonyl carbon. The reaction rate is primarily governed by both electronic inductive/resonance effects and steric hindrance around the carbonyl center.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electronic and Steric Effects on Reactivity

  • The carbonyl carbon carries a significant partial positive charge (δ+) due to the high electronegativity of oxygen, facilitating attack by electron-rich species.
  • Steric hindrance significantly decreases reaction rates; thus, formaldehyde reacts faster than aliphatic aldehydes, which in turn react faster than ketones.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups (EWG) increase the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon and accelerate nucleophilic addition.
  • Electron-donating groups (EDG) decrease electrophilicity through +I or +R effects, reducing the reaction rate.

General Mechanism and Stereochemical Outcomes

  • Nucleophilic addition proceeds via a two-step mechanism involving nucleophilic attack to form a tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate followed by protonation.
  • Under acidic conditions, protonation of the carbonyl oxygen occurs first, enhancing the electrophilicity of the carbon center for weak nucleophiles.
  • Addition of achiral nucleophiles to unsymmetrical planar carbonyl compounds yields a racemic mixture (50:50 enantiomers) due to face-selective attack.
  • Chiral carbonyl substrates yield diastereomeric mixtures whose ratio is dictated by stereoelectronic control models such as Felkin-Anh.

Classic Addition Reactions and Derivatization

  • HCN addition produces cyanohydrins; the optimal pH is maintained around 8-9 to balance sufficient cyanide ion concentration with undissociated hydrogen cyanide.
  • Bisulfite addition yields crystalline adducts used for the purification and isolation of volatile or liquid aldehydes and methyl ketones.
  • Ammonia derivatives (2,4-DNP, hydroxylamine, semicarbazide) undergo addition-elimination (condensation) yielding crystalline derivatives with characteristic melting points.
  • Grignard reagents and organolithium compounds act as strong carbon nucleophiles, adding to carbonyls to form alcohols after acidic workup.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aliphatic aldehydes are more reactive than ketones towards nucleophilic addition.

Observation: Formaldehyde reacts faster than all higher aldehydes, but chloral (trichloroacetaldehyde) exhibits exceptionally high reactivity. — The presence of three strongly electron-withdrawing chlorine atoms exerts a massive -I effect, making the carbonyl carbon extremely electrophilic and favoring stable hydrate formation.

Exception Sterically hindered ketones undergo slow nucleophilic addition.

Observation: Di-tert-butyl ketone and highly hindered ketones react extremely sluggishly or fail to form cyanohydrins and bisulfite addition products. — Severe steric crowding prevents the approach of the nucleophile and destabilizes the crowded sp3 tetrahedral intermediate relative to the sp2 reactant.

Exception Aromatic aldehydes are generally less reactive than aliphatic aldehydes.

Observation: Nitro-substituted benzaldehydes (e.g., 4-nitrobenzaldehyde) exhibit unusually rapid reaction rates with nucleophiles. — Strong resonance stabilization from the nitro group withdraws electron density directly from the aromatic ring and carbonyl carbon, drastically increasing electrophilicity.

Formaldehyde > Aldehyde > Ketone > Sterically Hindered Ketone

Application: Instantly arrange any set of carbonyl compounds for nucleophilic addition tests by counting alkyl groups attached to the carbonyl carbon.

pH Sweet Spot for Cyanohydrin Synthesis
Keep pH between 8.0 and 9.0

Application: At lower pH, equilibrium shifts because CN- is protonated to HCN; at higher pH, insufficient H+ prevents protonation of the intermediate alkoxide.

Bisulfite Adduct Filter
Only Aldehydes and Methyl Ketones

Application: Bulky ketones larger than methyl ketones do not form bisulfite addition compounds due to steric inhibition, serving as a quick separation trick.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Formaldehyde is the most reactive aldehyde toward nucleophilic addition due to minimal steric hindrance and absence of electron-donating alkyl groups.
  • Addition of Grignard reagents to methanal gives primary alcohols; other aldehydes yield secondary alcohols; ketones yield tertiary alcohols.
  • Bisulfite addition compound formation is a reversible reaction that can regenerate the pure carbonyl compound upon treatment with dilute acid or base.
  • Cyanohydrin formation involves attack by cyanide ion generated in situ from potassium cyanide and mineral acid.
  • 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine (2,4-DNP) test yields yellow, orange, or red crystalline precipitates specific for aldehydes and ketones.
  • Reduction of carbonyl compounds with LiAlH4 or NaBH4 proceeds via nucleophilic hydride (H-) transfer to the carbonyl carbon.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the nucleophilic addition step with electrophilic aromatic substitution; carbonyl carbons are electron-deficient and attract nucleophiles.
  • Assuming HCN addition works best in strongly acidic media; high acid protonates cyanide into unreactive HCN gas.
  • Forgetting that bulky ketones fail to form bisulfite addition products due to extreme steric congestion.
  • Treating reduction by NaBH4 as a radical mechanism instead of polar nucleophilic hydride delivery.

⚡ Quick Revision: Nucleophilic Addition to Carbonyl Compounds

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Polarization and Electrophilicity

The carbon-oxygen double bond is strongly polarized with oxygen acquiring a negative charge and carbon a positive charge. This polarization governs the attack of various oxygen, nitrogen, carbon, and sulfur nucleophiles.

Acid and Base Catalysis

Base catalysis increases nucleophile potency by generating stronger nucleophiles, whereas acid catalysis activates the carbonyl oxygen via protonation to make the carbonyl carbon more electrophilic.

Equilibrium and Reversibility

Many nucleophilic additions such as hydrate and cyanohydrin formations are equilibria. Electron-withdrawing groups drive the equilibrium toward the addition product, while bulky groups favor the reactant side.

Derivative Formation

Condensation reactions with derivatives of ammonia involve nucleophilic addition followed by elimination of a water molecule, yielding stable hydrazones, oximes, or semicarbazones.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Nucleophile (Nu-)
An electron-rich species that donates an electron pair to form a covalent bond with the carbonyl carbon.
Cyanohydrin (RCH(OH)CN)
An organic compound featuring a hydroxyl group and a cyano group attached to the same carbon atom.
Hemiacetal (RCH(OH)OR')
An unstable intermediate formed by the addition of one molecule of alcohol to an aldehyde.
Acetal (RCH(OR')2)
A gem-diether formed from the reaction of an aldehyde with two equivalents of an alcohol under acid catalysis.
Felkin-Anh Model (Stereoelectronic control)
A predictive model for stereoselective nucleophilic addition to chiral carbonyl compounds based on torsional strain and orbital alignment.
Hydrate (Gem-diol)
A compound containing two hydroxyl groups attached to the same carbon atom resulting from water addition.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Reactivity order: HCHO > RCHO > RCOR'
  • Optimum pH for cyanohydrin formation is 8-9.
  • Bisulfite addition is exclusive to aldehydes and methyl ketones.
  • Addition of RMgX followed by hydrolysis yields alcohols.
  • Carbonyl carbon hybridization changes from sp2 to sp3 upon nucleophilic attack.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carbonyl compounds undergo nucleophilic addition due to bond polarization.
  • Steric and electronic factors dictate the rate and extent of addition.
  • Acids and bases catalyze different phases of the nucleophilic addition pathway.
  • Addition derivatives serve essential roles in identification and purification.

⚖️ Key Differences

Hemiacetal VS Acetal

Hemiacetals contain one -OH and one -OR group on the carbon, whereas acetals contain two -OR groups.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following compounds has the highest reactivity toward nucleophilic addition?

Explanation: Formaldehyde has no electron-donating alkyl groups and minimal steric hindrance, making its carbonyl carbon the most electrophilic.

Question 2 of 19

What is the optimal pH range for the formation of cyanohydrins from carbonyl compounds?

Explanation: A pH of 8-9 ensures an optimal balance between maintaining sufficient free cyanide ions and preventing protonation of the carbonyl oxygen or excessive hydroxide concentration.

Question 3 of 19

Which carbonyl compound forms a crystalline bisulfite addition product most readily?

Explanation: Bisulfite addition is restricted to aldehydes and methyl ketones due to steric constraints; ethanal is an unhindered aldehyde.

Question 4 of 19

During nucleophilic addition to a carbonyl carbon, the hybridization of the carbonyl carbon changes from:

Explanation: The planar trigonal carbonyl carbon (sp2) is converted into a tetrahedral intermediate (sp3) upon nucleophile attack.

Question 5 of 19

What type of product is formed when an aldehyde reacts with two equivalents of an alcohol in the presence of dry HCl gas?

Explanation: Reaction with one equivalent gives a hemiacetal, whereas two equivalents yield a stable acetal.

Question 6 of 19

Which reagent is used to distinguish between aldehydes and ketones?

Explanation: Tollens' reagent (ammoniacal silver nitrate) oxidizes aldehydes to carboxylates with a silver mirror test, while ketones do not react.

Question 7 of 19

The addition of HCN to carbonyl compounds is an example of:

Explanation: The cyanide ion acts as a nucleophile attacking the electrophilic carbonyl carbon.

Question 8 of 19

Which substituent on benzaldehyde decreases its reactivity toward nucleophilic addition?

Explanation: The methoxy group (-OCH3) is an electron-donating group via resonance (+R), which decreases the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon.

Question 9 of 19

What is the intermediate formed during base-catalyzed nucleophilic addition to a carbonyl compound?

Explanation: Nucleophilic attack on the carbonyl group yields a negatively charged alkoxide tetrahedral intermediate.

Question 10 of 19

Which of the following compounds will not react with NaHSO3?

Explanation: Di-isopropyl ketone is extremely sterically hindered, preventing the formation of the bulky bisulfite addition compound.

Question 11 of 19

Reaction of a ketone with hydroxylamine (NH2OH) yields:

Explanation: Hydroxylamine condenses with carbonyl compounds to form oximes along with the elimination of water.

Question 12 of 19

Which of the following factors increases the rate of nucleophilic addition?

Explanation: Electron-withdrawing groups increase the partial positive charge on the carbonyl carbon, facilitating nucleophilic attack.

Question 13 of 19

What is the product when formaldehyde reacts with concentrated NaOH?

Explanation: Formaldehyde lacks alpha-hydrogens and undergoes disproportionation (Cannizzaro reaction) via hydride transfer.

Question 14 of 19

Which nucleophile adds to carbonyl compounds reversibly?

Explanation: HCN addition is a reversible equilibrium, unlike Grignard or hydride reductions which are irreversible.

Question 15 of 19

The reaction of aldehydes with 2,4-DNP produces a derivative useful for:

Explanation: 2,4-DNP derivatives are colored crystalline solids with sharp melting points used for qualitative identification.

Question 16 of 19

Which organic compound does not undergo aldol condensation?

Explanation: Benzaldehyde lacks alpha-hydrogens, thus failing to undergo self-aldol condensation.

Question 17 of 19

In the mechanism of acid-catalyzed nucleophilic addition, the first step is:

Explanation: Acid catalysis begins with protonation of the carbonyl oxygen to increase the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon.

Question 18 of 19

What is the stereochemical outcome when an achiral aldehyde reacts with an achiral nucleophile?

Explanation: Attack from both faces of the planar carbonyl group occurs with equal probability, yielding a racemic mixture.

Question 19 of 19

Which of the following is a condensation reaction involving nucleophilic addition followed by elimination?

Explanation: Oxime formation involves addition of NH2OH to form a carbinolamine followed by elimination of H2O.

Solution: Severe steric hindrance around the carbonyl carbon in di-tert-butyl ketone prevents nucleophilic attack by cyanide.

Question 2 of 10
What is the role of acid in acid-catalyzed nucleophilic addition to carbonyl compounds?

Solution: Protonation of the carbonyl oxygen polarizes the C=O bond further, making the carbon more susceptible to weak nucleophiles.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following statements concerning bisulfite addition compounds is correct?

Solution: Bisulfite addition is reversible; treatment with acid or base regenerates the original carbonyl compound.

Question 4 of 10
When benzaldehyde reacts with methylamine, the product formed is a:

Solution: Primary amines condense with aldehydes to form imines (Schiff bases) with elimination of water.

Question 5 of 10
Which electronic effect explains why aliphatic ketones are less reactive than aldehydes?

Solution: Two alkyl groups in ketones donate electron density via inductive (+I) and hyperconjugative effects, decreasing carbonyl electrophilicity.

Question 6 of 10
What is the product of the reaction between an aldehyde and a primary alcohol in the presence of dry HCl?

Solution: Reaction with one equivalent of alcohol yields a hemiacetal; two equivalents are required for acetal formation.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following reagents undergoes addition without subsequent elimination when reacting with a ketone?

Solution: Grignard reagents undergo direct nucleophilic addition followed by protonation to form an alcohol without water elimination.

Question 8 of 10
Why is the optimal pH for cyanohydrin formation maintained between 8 and 9?

Solution: Too low pH decreases CN- concentration by forming HCN; too high pH depletes protons required to protonate the alkoxide intermediate.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following species acts as the attacking nucleophile in NaBH4 reduction of a ketone?

Solution: Sodium borohydride delivers a hydride ion (H-), which functions as a carbon nucleophile attacking the carbonyl carbon.

Question 10 of 10
What is the stereochemical nature of the product when propan-2-one reacts with methylmagnesium bromide followed by hydrolysis?

Solution: The product is tert-butanol, which is a symmetrical tertiary alcohol and is achiral (optically inactive).
