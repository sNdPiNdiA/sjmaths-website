Acyl Substitution

Nucleophilic acyl substitution is a reaction mechanism where a nucleophile replaces a leaving group attached to an acyl carbon, proceeding via a tetrahedral intermediate. The reactivity of acyl derivatives is dictated by the basicity of the leaving group and electronic and steric factors around the carbonyl carbon.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mechanism of Nucleophilic Addition-Elimination

  • The reaction proceeds via a two-step addition-elimination pathway involving a sp3-hybridized tetrahedral intermediate.
  • The first step is rate-determining, involving the nucleophile attacking the electrophilic carbonyl carbon.
  • The expulsion of the leaving group from the tetrahedral intermediate regenerates the carbonyl double bond.
  • Stronger nucleophiles increase the rate of the initial addition step.

Reactivity Order and Leaving Group Ability

  • Acyl chlorides are the most reactive due to poor orbital overlap of chlorine's 3p lone pairs with carbon's 2p orbital and the strong -I effect of chlorine.
  • Amides are the least reactive derivatives because the amino group is a poor leaving group and exhibits strong resonance donation to the carbonyl.
  • A poorer leaving group corresponds to a higher electron density on the carbonyl oxygen and a more stable tetrahedral intermediate.
  • Acyl derivatives can be converted to less reactive derivatives directly, but more reactive derivatives require harsh reagents.

Esterification and Hydrolysis Pathways

  • Acid-catalyzed ester hydrolysis (AAC2 mechanism) involves protonation of the carbonyl oxygen followed by water attack.
  • Base-catalyzed ester hydrolysis (BAC2 mechanism) is irreversible due to resonance stabilization of the resulting carboxylate anion.
  • Steric hindrance in the alcohol or acid component significantly decreases the rate of both esterification and hydrolysis.
  • Acyl-oxygen fission is the most common bond cleavage pathway during standard ester hydrolysis reactions.

Base-Catalyzed Ester Hydrolysis Rate Law
Rate = k[Ester][OH-]
Conditions: Saponification in aqueous alkaline medium
Units: L mol-1 s-1
Acid-Catalyzed Ester Hydrolysis Rate Law
Rate = k[Ester][H+][H2O]
Conditions: Fischer esterification or acidic hydrolysis
Units: L2 mol-2 s-1
Schotten-Baumann Reaction Rate Expression
Rate = k[RCOCl][ArNH2] or [ROH] (in presence of NaOH)
Conditions: Aqueous sodium hydroxide biphasic system
Units: L mol-1 s-1
Relative Reactivity Order of Acyl Derivatives
RCOCl > (RCO)2O > RCOOR' > RCONH2
Conditions: Standard nucleophilic attack conditions
Units: Dimensionless rate/reactivity sequence
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Less reactive acyl derivatives cannot be directly converted to more reactive ones.

Observation: Carboxylic acids can be converted directly to acyl chlorides using SOCl2, PCl3, or PCl5. — Formation of gaseous byproducts (SO2 and HCl) drives the equilibrium forward via entropy.

Exception Sterically hindered esters undergo rapid alkaline hydrolysis.

Observation: Bulky esters like methyl 2,4,6-trimethylbenzoate resist alkaline hydrolysis under standard conditions. — Extreme steric hindrance prevents the hydroxide ion from attacking the carbonyl carbon to form the tetrahedral intermediate.

Exception Amines act as nucleophiles in acylation reactions.

Observation: Tertiary amines act as catalysts (e.g., pyridine or DMAP) rather than stable acylation products. — Tertiary amines lack an N-H proton to eliminate after initial nucleophilic attack, forming an unstable acylammonium intermediate.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Leaving Group Basicity Mnemonic
Weaker Base = Better Leaving Group = Faster Reaction

Application: Use pKa of conjugate acids: HCl (-7) > RCOOH (4-5) > ROH (16) > RNH2 (38) to instantly predict acyl reactivity.

Ester Cleavage Identification
AAC2 vs AAL1

Application: If the alcohol component is tertiary, acid-catalyzed hydrolysis switches from acyl-oxygen cleavage to alkyl-oxygen cleavage via carbocation (AAL1).

Schotten-Baumann Shortcut
RCOCl + R'OH + NaOH → RCOOR' + NaCl + H2O

Application: Always neutralize the generated HCl with aqueous base to prevent reversible side reactions and drive ester/amide formation to completion.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Acyl chlorides react most rapidly with nucleophiles due to the strong electron-withdrawing inductive effect of chlorine.
  • Amides are the most stable and least reactive acyl derivatives toward nucleophilic substitution.
  • Saponification of esters is an irreversible reaction because the carboxylate ion is stabilized by resonance and repels nucleophiles.
  • Thionyl chloride (SOCl2) is the preferred reagent for converting carboxylic acids to acyl chlorides because all byproducts are gases.
  • Steric hindrance around the acyl carbon decreases the rate of nucleophilic acyl substitution.
  • Pyridine acts as a nucleophilic catalyst in acylation reactions by forming a reactive acylpyridinium intermediate.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming amides can be hydrolyzed as easily as esters without harsh acidic or basic conditions and heating.
  • Forgetting that the tetrahedral intermediate expels the best leaving group, not necessarily the one that added first.
  • Confusing nucleophilic acyl substitution (addition-elimination) with aliphatic nucleophilic substitution (SN1/SN2).
  • Neglecting the role of aqueous NaOH in removing acidic protons during Schotten-Baumann amine acylation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Acyl Substitution

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Tetrahedral Intermediate

Nucleophilic addition to acyl derivatives forms a charged or neutral sp3 tetrahedral intermediate before leaving group departure.

Leaving Group Basicity

The reactivity of acyl compounds inversely correlates with the basicity of their leaving groups (Cl- > RCOO- > RO- > RNH2-).

Ester Hydrolysis Mechanisms

Esters undergo hydrolysis via acyl-oxygen cleavage under standard acidic or basic conditions, yielding carboxylic acids or carboxylate salts.

Derivative Interconversion

More reactive acyl derivatives can be converted into less reactive derivatives through direct nucleophilic substitution with appropriate nucleophiles.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Acyl Group (R-C(=O)-)
A functional group derived by the removal of the hydroxyl group from an oxoacid.
Tetrahedral Intermediate (sp3 Carbon species)
Transient reaction intermediate formed after nucleophilic attack on a carbonyl carbon.
Saponification (Base-promoted ester hydrolysis)
Alkaline hydrolysis of esters yielding carboxylate salts and alcohols.
Schotten-Baumann Reaction (Acylic benzoylation)
Reaction of amines or alcohols with acyl chlorides in the presence of aqueous base.
Leaving Group (X-)
An atom or group that detaches from the substrate with an electron pair during substitution.
Fischer Esterification (Acid-catalyzed ester synthesis)
Equilibrium reaction between carboxylic acid and alcohol yielding ester and water.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Reactivity sequence: Acyl chloride > Anhydride > Ester > Amide
  • Base-catalyzed ester hydrolysis is irreversible.
  • SOCl2 yields gaseous byproducts SO2 and HCl.
  • Tertiary amines act as catalysts, not final acylated products.
  • Steric hindrance lowers nucleophilic attack rates significantly.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nucleophilic acyl substitution proceeds via addition-elimination.
  • Leaving group ability dictates overall acyl derivative reactivity.
  • Acids and bases catalyze ester hydrolysis through distinct kinetic pathways.
  • Interconversion follows the rule of moving from more reactive to less reactive derivatives.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution VS Nucleophilic Aliphatic Substitution

Acyl substitution proceeds via a tetrahedral intermediate addition-elimination pathway, whereas aliphatic substitution proceeds via concerted SN2 or carbocation SN1 pathways.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following acyl derivatives is the most reactive toward nucleophilic attack?

Explanation: Acetyl chloride is the most reactive due to the strong -I effect and poor orbital overlap of the chloride leaving group.

Question 2 of 19

What is the key intermediate formed during nucleophilic acyl substitution?

Explanation: Nucleophilic addition to the carbonyl carbon forms an sp3-hybridized tetrahedral intermediate.

Question 3 of 19

Why is base-catalyzed ester hydrolysis irreversible?

Explanation: The resulting carboxylate anion is negatively charged, making it unreactive toward alkoxide or alcohol nucleophiles.

Question 4 of 19

Which reagent is best suited to convert a carboxylic acid directly to an acyl chloride without leaving acidic aqueous residues?

Explanation: Thionyl chloride (SOCl2) yields gaseous SO2 and HCl, leaving pure acyl chloride behind.

Question 5 of 19

In the Schotten-Baumann reaction, what is the role of aqueous sodium hydroxide?

Explanation: NaOH neutralizes the generated HCl, preventing protonation of the amine reactant and pushing equilibrium toward products.

Question 6 of 19

Which functional group has the lowest reactivity toward nucleophilic substitution?

Explanation: Amides are the least reactive because the nitrogen lone pair strongly donates into the carbonyl carbon via resonance.

Question 7 of 19

What is the molecularity of the rate-determining step in base-catalyzed ester hydrolysis?

Explanation: The attack of OH- on the ester is a bimolecular step involving both species.

Question 8 of 19

Which acyl derivative can be prepared directly from all other three main derivatives?

Explanation: Amides are the least reactive and can be prepared by reacting acyl chlorides, anhydrides, or esters with ammonia or amines.

Question 9 of 19

What type of cleavage occurs predominantly in acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of primary alkyl esters?

Explanation: Standard acid-catalyzed ester hydrolysis (AAC2) proceeds via acyl-oxygen bond cleavage.

Question 10 of 19

Which factor primarily explains the high reactivity of acid anhydrides compared to esters?

Explanation: Carboxylate ions are weaker bases than alkoxides, making them better leaving groups.

Question 11 of 19

What is the role of pyridine in the acylation of alcohols using acid chlorides?

Explanation: Pyridine neutralizes generated HCl and forms a reactive acylpyridinium intermediate.

Question 12 of 19

Which species acts as the nucleophile in the saponification of an ester?

Explanation: Hydroxide ion (OH-) is the strong nucleophile that attacks the ester carbonyl carbon.

Question 13 of 19

What is the product of the reaction between an acid chloride and a secondary amine?

Explanation: A secondary amine reacts with an acyl chloride to form a tertiary amide and HCl.

Question 14 of 19

Under what condition does ester hydrolysis switch to alkyl-oxygen cleavage (AAL1)?

Explanation: Tertiary alkyl esters can undergo stable carbocation formation, leading to AAL1 cleavage.

Question 15 of 19

Which compound is formed when an acid anhydride reacts with excess ammonia?

Explanation: Reaction of an anhydride with ammonia yields one molecule of an amide and one molecule of a carboxylate salt (or carboxylic acid).

Question 16 of 19

What is the hybridization change of the carbonyl carbon during nucleophilic acyl substitution?

Explanation: The planar sp2 carbonyl carbon becomes tetrahedral (sp3) upon nucleophilic addition, then reverts to sp2 after leaving group departure.

Question 17 of 19

Which of the following reagents will convert an ester directly into an amide?

Explanation: Ammonolysis of esters using excess ammonia or amine yields an amide and an alcohol.

Question 18 of 19

Why are tertiary aliphatic amines unable to form stable amides upon reacting directly with acyl chlorides?

Explanation: Tertiary amines lack an N-H proton, preventing the elimination step needed to form a stable neutral amide.

Question 19 of 19

In the transesterification reaction, what is exchanged?

Explanation: Transesterification involves exchanging the alkoxy group of an ester with that of an added alcohol.

Solution: Protonation of the carbonyl oxygen increases electrophilicity, facilitating nucleophilic attack by water.

Which of the following statements about transesterification is correct?

Solution: Transesterification is an equilibrium reaction between an ester and an alcohol, catalyzed by acids or bases.

Question 5 of 10
Why is acetyl chloride stored in tightly sealed containers away from moisture?

Solution: Acyl chlorides hydrolyze rapidly upon exposure to moisture in the air.

Question 6 of 10
Which leaving group has the highest pKa of its conjugate acid among the common acyl derivatives?

Solution: Amides have the strongest conjugate acid pKa (~38 for amines), indicating the poorest leaving group ability.

What is the product when acetic anhydride reacts with ethanol?

Solution: Reaction of an anhydride with an alcohol yields one equivalent of ester and one equivalent of carboxylic acid.

Which of the following is true for the tetrahedral intermediate in acyl substitution?

Solution: The central carbon in the tetrahedral intermediate is sp3 hybridized with four single bonds.

Question 9 of 10

Solution: Base-catalyzed (saponification) hydrolysis consumes exactly one equivalent of hydroxide per mole of ester.
