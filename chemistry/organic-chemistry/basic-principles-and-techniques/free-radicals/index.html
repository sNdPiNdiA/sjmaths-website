Organic Chemistry

Free Radicals

Free radicals are reactive chemical species possessing an odd number of electrons, typically featuring an unpaired electron in an unhybridized p-orbital. They are generated via homolytic bond cleavage driven by thermal energy, ultraviolet radiation, or radical initiators.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Generation and Structure of Free Radicals

  • Free radicals are generated through homolytic fission where shared bonding electrons are distributed equally between the separating fragments.
  • The central carbon atom in alkyl radicals adopts an sp2 hybrid state with planar geometry, leaving the unpaired electron in a pure, unhybridized perpendicular p-orbital.
  • Thermal or photochemical homolysis of peroxides (such as benzoyl peroxide or di-tert-butyl peroxide) readily generates initiating radicals due to weak oxygen-oxygen single bonds.

Stability and Electronic Effects

  • Free radical stability follows the order: 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl radical, stabilized primarily through hyperconjugation and inductive electron donation from adjacent alkyl groups.
  • Allylic and benzylic radicals exhibit enhanced stability due to resonance delocalization of the unpaired electron over neighboring π-systems or aromatic rings.
  • Bridgehead free radicals are extremely unstable due to the inability of the rigid cage carbon to achieve the required planar sp2 hybridization.

Radical Halogenation Kinetics and Selectivity

  • Free radical chlorination is rapid, exothermic, and less selective because the transition state for hydrogen abstraction occurs early along the reaction coordinate.
  • Free radical bromination is endothermic, highly selective, and follows the Hammond postulate where the transition state closely resembles the free radical product.
  • Autoxidation processes proceed via radical chain mechanisms involving alkyl radicals reacting with molecular oxygen to form peroxy radicals (ROO•).

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl radicals show increasing stability with methyl substitution.

Observation: Bridgehead free radicals (e.g., 1-adamantyl radical) resist formation despite being tertiary. — Insolubility of the radical center in adopting a planar geometry prevents sufficient p-orbital overlap.

Exception Halogenation of alkanes proceeds with standard statistical distribution.

Observation: Fluorination of alkanes is violently explosive and uncontrollable. — The hydrogen abstraction step is extremely exothermic, leading to extensive chain branching and carbon-carbon bond cleavage.

Exception Free radical additions to unsymmetrical alkenes follow anti-Markovnikov regioselectivity.

Observation: Radical additions in the absence of peroxides or in the presence of strong inhibitors fail to yield anti-Markovnikov products. — Peroxide initiators are strictly required to generate the halogen radical species that adds first to the least substituted carbon.

Hammond Selectivity Rule
Endo = Selective, Exo = Non-selective

Application: Bromination is endothermic (selective for 3°), chlorination is exothermic (less selective).

Peroxide Effect Direction
HBr + Peroxide = Anti-Markovnikov

Application: Remember that HCl and HI do not give anti-Markovnikov radical addition due to unfavorable thermodynamics of propagation steps.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Alkyl free radicals have an odd number of electrons and possess an unpaired electron in an unhybridized p-orbital.
  • The stability order of free radicals is 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3•.
  • Free radical halogenation of alkanes involves three distinct stages: initiation, propagation, and termination.
  • Free radical bromination is more selective than chlorination due to an endothermic transition state.
  • Allylic and benzylic radicals derive extra stability from resonance delocalization.
  • Anti-Markovnikov addition of hydrogen bromide to alkenes operates via a free radical chain mechanism in the presence of peroxides.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming free radicals readily undergo carbocation-like Wagner-Meerwein rearrangements; radical rearrangements are exceedingly rare.
  • Confusing the selectivity of radical bromination with radical chlorination; chlorination yields a nearly statistical mixture of isomers.
  • Applying the peroxide effect to HCl and HI; HCl addition is thermodynamically unfavorable and HI addition is reversible.
  • Treating bridgehead carbon radicals as stable tertiary systems without considering geometric constraints.

⚡ Quick Revision: Free Radicals

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Homolytic Fission and Initiation

Homolytic bond cleavage yields neutral radical species possessing unpaired electrons. Thermal or photochemical activation of radical initiators starts chain pathways.

Propagation and Chain Reactions

Propagation steps consume reactants and regenerate radical species, driving efficient chemical transformations like halogenation and polymerization.

Radical Stability and Resonance

Hyperconjugation, inductive effects, and resonance delocalization stabilize free radicals, dictating the regiochemical outcome of reactions.

Termination Pathways

Radical termination occurs via radical combination or disproportionation, ending radical chain sequences.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Homolytic Fission (Bond cleavage)
Equal splitting of a covalent bond yielding two radicals.
Initiator (R-O-O-R)
A labile compound that generates radicals upon heating.
Hyperconjugation (σ-π overlap)
Delocalization of electrons from C-H σ bond to the radical p-orbital.
Hammond Postulate (Transition state theory)
Transition state resembles the adjacent species closest in energy.
Peroxide Effect (Kharasch effect)
Anti-Markovnikov addition of HBr to alkenes via radicals.
Disproportionation (Redox termination)
Transfer of a hydrogen atom between two radicals to form an alkane and an alkene.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Stability order: 3° > 2° > 1° > Methyl radical
  • Hybridization of carbon radical center: sp2
  • Propagation steps are thermochemically determining for overall chain length
  • Bromination is highly selective; chlorination is non-selective
  • Peroxides only reverse addition for HBr, not HCl or HI

📌 Quick Recall

  • Free radicals are electron-deficient, reactive odd-electron species.
  • Stability is governed by hyperconjugation and resonance.
  • Halogenation proceeds via radical chain mechanisms.
  • Rearrangements in free radicals are extremely uncommon.

⚖️ Key Differences

Free Radical VS Carbocation

Free radicals possess 7 valence electrons and neutral charge, whereas carbocations possess 6 valence electrons and a positive charge.

Question 1 of 18

What is the hybridization of the radical carbon atom in a simple alkyl free radical?

Explanation: The carbon atom bearing the radical adopts a planar sp2 hybridization with the unpaired electron residing in the unhybridized p-orbital.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following free radicals is the most stable?

Explanation: Tertiary alkyl radicals are the most stable due to maximum hyperconjugative stabilization from adjacent alkyl groups.

Question 3 of 18

What type of bond cleavage generates free radicals?

Explanation: Homolytic cleavage involves symmetrical breaking of a covalent bond where each bonded atom retains one electron.

Question 4 of 18

Why is free radical bromination more selective than chlorination?

Explanation: According to the Hammond postulate, endothermic hydrogen abstraction in bromination has a transition state that closely resembles the product radical, resulting in high selectivity.

Question 5 of 18

Which compound is commonly used as a radical initiator?

Explanation: Benzoyl peroxide readily undergoes thermal homolysis to yield phenyl radicals, serving as an efficient radical initiator.

Question 6 of 18

What is the geometry of a methyl radical (CH3•)?

Explanation: The central carbon in a methyl radical is sp2 hybridized, resulting in a planar trigonal geometry.

Question 7 of 18

In the free radical chlorination of propane, which position yields the major monochloro product?

Explanation: C-2 is secondary and forms a more stable secondary radical intermediate, leading to 2-chloropropane as the major product.

Question 8 of 18

Which hydrogen halide exhibits the peroxide effect (anti-Markovnikov addition to alkenes)?

Explanation: Only HBr undergoes anti-Markovnikov addition in the presence of peroxides because both propagation steps are energetically favorable.

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following does NOT typically undergo radical rearrangement?

Explanation: Alkyl free radicals rarely undergo 1,2-shifts or skeletal rearrangements because the activation energy is too high.

Question 10 of 18

What termination step involves two radicals combining to form a single covalent bond?

Explanation: Radical combination or coupling involves two radicals joining their unpaired electrons to form a covalent bond.

Question 11 of 18

Allylic radicals are exceptionally stable due to:

Explanation: Allylic radicals enjoy resonance stabilization where the odd electron is shared over multiple carbon atoms.

Question 12 of 18

Which of the following radicals is stabilized by aromatic resonance?

Explanation: The benzyl radical benefits from resonance stabilization involving the aromatic ring π-system.

Question 13 of 18

In radical chain chlorination of methane, what is the propagation step yielding methyl chloride?

Explanation: The reaction of methyl radical with molecular chlorine forms methyl chloride and regenerates a chlorine radical, representing a propagation step.

Question 14 of 18

Which species is formed during radical disproportionation?

Explanation: Disproportionation involves hydrogen atom transfer from one radical to another, producing one saturated molecule (alkane) and one unsaturated molecule (alkene).

Question 15 of 18

Bridgehead radicals fail to form readily because:

Explanation: Rigid polycyclic bridgehead systems cannot easily flatten out to achieve the required sp2 hybridization.

Question 16 of 18

Which radical is stabilized by both inductive and hyperconjugative effects?

Explanation: The tertiary butyl radical has nine adjacent alpha-C-H bonds contributing to extensive hyperconjugation.

Question 17 of 18

What is the primary role of radical inhibitors (scavengers)?

Explanation: Inhibitors react rapidly with propagating radicals to form stable, non-radical products, thus stopping radical chain reactions.

Question 18 of 18

Ultraviolet light promotes halogenation of alkanes primarily by:

Explanation: UV light provides the specific energy quanta required to homolytically cleave halogen molecules into reactive halogen radicals.

Which factor contributes most to the stability of the allyl radical?

Solution: Allyl radicals are stabilized by resonance delocalization of the odd electron over adjacent p-orbitals.

Question 3 of 10
In free radical halogenation, propagation steps are:

Solution: Propagation steps continually consume and regenerate radicals, forming a self-sustaining cycle.

Question 4 of 10
Why does fluorination of alkanes lack synthetic utility?

Solution: Fluorination is violently exothermic and uncontrolled, leading to fragmentation and carbonization.

Question 5 of 10
Which radical species possesses an sp hybridized center?

Solution: Radicals centered on sp-hybridized carbons (such as alkynyl radicals) feature linear geometry around the radical center.

Question 6 of 10
What is the role of light in photochemical halogenation?

Solution: Light supplies the quantum energy needed to break halogen molecules homolytically into radicals.

Question 7 of 10
Which intermediate is NOT involved in free radical addition of HBr to alkenes?

Solution: Carbocations are intermediates in ionic Markovnikov additions, not in free radical anti-Markovnikov additions.

Question 8 of 10
The term 'homolytic fission' implies:

Solution: Homolytic fission splits a bond evenly, with each atom retaining one bonding electron.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following is a termination step in radical halogenation?

Solution: Combining two radical species to form a neutral molecule removes radicals from the system, terminating the chain.

Question 10 of 10
What is the primary reason bridgehead radicals are exceptionally unreactive and unstable?

Solution: Bridgehead carbons are locked in rigid ring geometries that prevent them from flattening into the required sp2 planar configuration.
