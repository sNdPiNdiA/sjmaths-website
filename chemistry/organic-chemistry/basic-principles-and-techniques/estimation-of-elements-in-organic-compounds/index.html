Organic Chemistry

Estimation of elements in organic compounds

Quantitative elemental analysis determines the percentage composition of carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, halogens, sulfur, and phosphorus in organic compounds. Standard analytical methods include combustion analysis, Kjeldahl's method, Carius method, and gravimetric precipitation.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Liebig's Combustion Method for Carbon and Hydrogen

  • A known mass of the organic compound is heated with anhydrous copper(II) oxide in a hard glass tube.
  • Carbon is quantitatively converted to carbon dioxide, which is absorbed in a U-tube containing concentrated potassium hydroxide (KOH).
  • Hydrogen is quantitatively converted to water vapor, which is absorbed in a U-tube containing anhydrous calcium chloride (CaCl2).

Nitrogen Estimation via Kjeldahl and Dumas Methods

  • Kjeldahl's method involves digesting the organic compound with concentrated H2SO4 and K2SO4 catalyst to convert nitrogen into ammonium sulfate.
  • The resulting ammonium sulfate is heated with excess NaOH, and the liberated ammonia gas is trapped in a known excess of standard acid solution.
  • Dumas method involves heating the organic compound with copper oxide in an atmosphere of carbon dioxide, yielding free nitrogen gas collected over an aqueous KOH solution.

Halogen, Sulfur, and Phosphorus Estimation

  • The Carius method uses fuming HNO3 and AgNO3 to precipitate halogens as silver halides (AgX).
  • Sulfur is oxidized to sulfuric acid by fuming HNO3 and precipitated gravimetrically as barium sulfate (BaSO4 using BaCl2).
  • Phosphorus is oxidized to phosphoric acid and precipitated as ammonium phosphomolybdate, (NH4)3PO4•12MoO3, or ignited to magnesium pyrophosphate (Mg2P2O7).
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Percentage of Hydrogen (Liebig's Method)
% H = (Mass of H2O produced / Mass of organic compound) × (2 / 18) × 100
Conditions: Complete combustion in presence of excess oxygen and copper(II) oxide
Units: Mass in grams
Percentage of Nitrogen (Kjeldahl's Method)
% N = (1.4 × Molarity of acid × Volume of acid neutralized) / Mass of organic compound
Conditions: Conversion of organic nitrogen to (NH4)2SO4 via digestion with concentrated H2SO4
Units: Molarity in mol/L, Volume in mL, Mass in grams
Percentage of Halogen (Carius Method)
% Halogen = (Atomic mass of halogen × Mass of AgX precipitate / Molecular mass of AgX × Mass of organic compound) × 100
Conditions: Heating with fuming nitric acid and silver nitrate in a sealed Carius tube
Units: Mass in grams
Percentage of Sulfur (Carius Method)
% S = (32 × Mass of BaSO4 / 233 × Mass of organic compound) × 100
Conditions: Oxidation of sulfur to sulfate, followed by precipitation with barium chloride
Units: Mass in grams
Percentage of Carbon (Liebig's Method)
% C = (Mass of CO2 produced / Mass of organic compound) × (12 / 44) × 100
Conditions: Complete combustion in presence of excess oxygen and copper(II) oxide
Units: Mass in grams
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Kjeldahl's method is universally applicable for nitrogen estimation in organic compounds.

Observation: Fails for compounds containing nitrogen in nitro groups, azo groups, or ring systems such as pyridine and nitrobenzene. — These nitrogen-containing functional groups are not quantitatively converted to ammonium sulfate under standard Kjeldahl digestion conditions.

Exception Halogens in all organic compounds can be estimated using the Carius method.

Observation: Fluorine is not estimated accurately by the Carius method. — Silver fluoride (AgF) is highly soluble in water and does not form a quantitative precipitate.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Carbon and Hydrogen Stoichiometric Shortcuts
% C = (12 / 44) × (WCO2 / W) • 100 and % H = (2 / 18) × (WH2O / W) • 100

Application: Remember the fraction multipliers 12/44 for CO2 to C and 2/18 for H2O to H to save calculation time in numericals.

Kjeldahl Acid-Base Back Titration Rule
Milliequivalents of acid neutralized = (Normality × Volume)acid - (Normality × Volume)base

Application: Use milliequivalents directly to find the effective acid consumed by ammonia without converting everything to moles.

Halogen Atomic Mass Reference
Cl = 35.5, Br = 80, I = 127

Application: Memorize molar masses of AgCl (143.5), AgBr (188), and AgI (235) to rapidly solve Carius method percentage calculations.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Liebig's method uses copper(II) oxide for the complete combustion of carbon and hydrogen.
  • Concentrated KOH is used to absorb carbon dioxide gas in quantitative organic estimation.
  • Anhydrous calcium chloride absorbs water vapor produced during the combustion of organic compounds.
  • Kjeldahl's method involves the digestion of organic nitrogen with concentrated sulfuric acid to form ammonium sulfate.
  • Copper sulfate and potassium sulfate are added in Kjeldahl digestion as catalysts and to raise the boiling point of sulfuric acid.
  • The Carius method utilizes fuming nitric acid in a sealed glass tube for halogen, sulfur, and phosphorus estimation.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Using hydrated calcium chloride instead of anhydrous CaCl2 for water absorption, which introduces significant errors in hydrogen estimation.
  • Assuming Kjeldahl's method works for nitro and diazo compounds without prior reduction steps.
  • Forgetting to multiply the volume of acid by its normality (or molarity × n-factor) in back-titration calculations.
  • Confusing the gravimetric conversion factor for sulfur (32/233) with that of barium (137/233).

⚡ Quick Revision: Estimation of elements in organic compounds

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Combustion Analysis Principles

Carbon and hydrogen are estimated simultaneously by burning a known mass of organic compound over copper oxide, capturing the resulting CO2 and H2O in specific absorbent weights.

Nitrogen Estimation Methods

Nitrogen is determined either by gas volumetric measurement via Dumas combustion or through acidimetric titration of ammonia generated via Kjeldahl digestion.

Halogen Estimation via Carius Method

Halogens react with silver nitrate in the presence of fuming nitric acid inside a sealed Carius tube to yield insoluble silver halide precipitates for gravimetric determination.

Sulfur and Phosphorus Quantification

Sulfur and phosphorus are oxidized to their respective mineral acids using nitric acid and subsequently precipitated and weighed as barium sulfate or magnesium pyrophosphate.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Liebig's Method (Combustion Analysis)
Analytical method for estimating carbon and hydrogen percentages via CuO combustion.
Kjeldahl's Method (Digestion & Titration)
Method for estimating organic nitrogen via acid digestion and ammonia titration.
Carius Method (Sealed Tube Oxidation)
Method for estimating halogens, sulfur, and phosphorus using fuming nitric acid.
Dumas Method (Gas Volumetry)
Method for determining nitrogen by collecting liberated N2 gas over KOH.
Gravimetric Analysis (Precipitation Method)
Quantitative determination based on the mass of a solid precipitate.
Back Titration (Excess Reagent Titration)
Titration technique where unreacted excess acid is neutralized with standard base.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carbon factor: 12/44 for CO2 conversion
  • Hydrogen factor: 2/18 for H2O conversion
  • Kjeldahl nitrogen formula: % N = (1.4 × M × V) / W
  • Sulfur factor: 32/233 for BaSO4 conversion
  • Carius method uses fuming HNO3 and AgNO3

📌 Quick Recall

  • Quantitative elemental analysis establishes empirical and molecular formulas of organic compounds.
  • Combustion analysis measures C and H as CO2 and H2O.
  • Kjeldahl and Dumas methods quantify nitrogen content with specific chemical limitations.
  • Carius method handles halogen, sulfur, and phosphorus through acid digestion and precipitation.

⚖️ Key Differences

Dumas Method VS Kjeldahl Method

Dumas is a volumetric method applicable to all nitrogen compounds, whereas Kjeldahl is a titrimetric method failing for heterocyclic and nitro compounds.

Question 1 of 19

In Liebig's method for estimation of carbon and hydrogen, the absorbent used for carbon dioxide is:

Explanation: Concentrated KOH solution absorbs carbon dioxide efficiently to form potassium carbonate.

Question 2 of 19

Which of the following compounds fails to give nitrogen estimation by Kjeldahl's method?

Explanation: Nitrobenzene contains nitrogen in a nitro group which is not completely converted to ammonium sulfate during Kjeldahl digestion.

Question 3 of 19

In the Carius method, halogen estimation is carried out by heating the organic compound with:

Explanation: Fuming nitric acid oxidizes carbon and hydrogen, and silver nitrate precipitates the halogen as silver halide.

Question 4 of 19

During sulfur estimation by Carius method, sulfur is converted to:

Explanation: Sulfur is oxidized by fuming nitric acid to sulfuric acid, which is then precipitated as barium sulfate.

Question 5 of 19

What is the gravimetric factor used for calculating percentage of sulfur when BaSO4 is weighed?

Explanation: The percentage of sulfur is calculated as (32 / 233) × (Mass of BaSO4 / Mass of compound) × 100, where 32 is the atomic mass of S and 233 is the molecular mass of BaSO4.

Question 6 of 19

In Dumas method, nitrogen gas is collected over an aqueous solution of:

Explanation: Aqueous KOH is used to absorb carbon dioxide gas produced during combustion, leaving pure nitrogen gas to be collected.

Question 7 of 19

Fluorine cannot be estimated by the Carius method because:

Explanation: Silver fluoride (AgF) is highly soluble in water and does not precipitate out of solution.

Question 8 of 19

In Kjeldahl's method, potassium sulfate is added to sulfuric acid to:

Explanation: Potassium sulfate raises the boiling point of H2SO4, ensuring more rapid and complete digestion of the organic compound.

Question 9 of 19

A 0.2 g organic compound containing halogen gives 0.188 g of AgBr in Carius method. The percentage of bromine in the compound is (Atomic weight: Ag = 108, Br = 80):

Explanation: % Br = (80 / 188) × (0.188 / 0.2) × 100 = 40%.

Question 10 of 19

Phosphorus in an organic compound can be estimated by converting it into:

Explanation: Phosphorus is precipitated as ammonium phosphomolybdate and then ignited to magnesium pyrophosphate (Mg2P2O7) for weighing.

Question 11 of 19

Which catalyst is commonly used along with potassium sulfate in Kjeldahl digestion?

Explanation: Anhydrous copper sulfate or mercury is used as a catalyst in Kjeldahl digestion.

Question 12 of 19

In combustion analysis, anhydrous calcium chloride is packed in a U-tube to absorb:

Explanation: Anhydrous calcium chloride is a powerful desiccant specifically used to absorb water vapor.

Question 13 of 19

The percentage of carbon in 0.24 g of an organic compound that gives 0.88 g of CO2 on combustion is:

Explanation: % C = (12 / 44) × (0.88 / 0.24) × 100 = (12/44) × (3.67) × 100 = 50%.

Question 14 of 19

Which of the following nitrogenous functional groups yields nitrogen gas directly when heated with CuO in carbon dioxide atmosphere?

Explanation: In the Dumas method, all organic nitrogen compounds release free N2 gas when heated with copper(II) oxide.

Question 15 of 19

In the estimation of nitrogen by Kjeldahl method, 0.5 g of compound required 10 mL of 1 M H2SO4 for neutralization of ammonia. The percentage of nitrogen is:

Explanation: % N = (1.4 × 2 × 10) / 0.5 = 28 / 0.5 = 56%. (Note: 1 M H2SO4 = 2 N).

Question 16 of 19

Sulfur estimation using BaCl2 involves which type of analysis?

Explanation: Gravimetric analysis involves weighing the isolated insoluble precipitate of barium sulfate.

Question 17 of 19

Which reagent is used to test for the presence of halogens in Lassaigne's extract before quantitative estimation?

Explanation: Silver nitrate acidified with dilute nitric acid is standard for detecting halide ions.

Question 18 of 19

During Carius halogen estimation, nitric acid is added to:

Explanation: Fuming nitric acid oxidizes interfering carbon and hydrogen atoms to CO2 and H2O.

Question 19 of 19

What is the purpose of adding sodium fusion (Lassaigne's extract) in qualitative tests prior to quantitative estimation?

Explanation: Sodium fusion converts covalently bound N, S, and halogens into water-soluble ionic species like NaCN, Na2S, and NaX.

Question 1 of 10
In Dumas method, 0.25 g of an organic compound gave 40 mL of nitrogen gas collected at 300 K and 715 mm Hg pressure. (Aqueous tension at 300 K = 15 mm Hg). The percentage of nitrogen is:

Solution: Correct application of P1V1/T1 = P2V2/T2 at STP followed by % N calculation yields 16.4%.

Question 2 of 10
Which absorbent is placed closest to the combustion tube in Liebig's apparatus?

Solution: Anhydrous CaCl2 tube is connected directly to the combustion tube to absorb water first, followed by the KOH tube for CO2.

Question 3 of 10
Why is fuming nitric acid used in the Carius method instead of dilute nitric acid?

Solution: Fuming nitric acid provides strong oxidizing action to completely oxidize sulfur and phosphorus to their respective acids.

Question 4 of 10
In a Kjeldahl estimation, the ammonia evolved from 0.75 g of compound neutralized 30 mL of 0.25 N H2SO4. The percentage of nitrogen is:

Solution: % N = (1.4 × Normality × Volume) / Mass = (1.4 × 0.25 × 30) / 0.75 = 10.5 / 0.75 = 14% ... wait, recalculation: 1.4 × 0.25 × 30 = 10.5; 10.5 / 0.75 = 14%. Correct option is 14% (Option B).

Question 5 of 10
Phosphorus estimation involves conversion into magnesium pyrophosphate (Mg2P2O7). What is the stoichiometric ratio of P to Mg2P2O7?

Solution: One molecule of Mg2P2O7 contains two phosphorus atoms, establishing a 2:1 atom ratio.

Question 6 of 10
Which halogen gives a yellow precipitate soluble in ammonium hydroxide with difficulty during Carius estimation?

Solution: Silver iodide (AgI) is a yellow precipitate that is sparingly soluble in ammonium hydroxide.

Question 7 of 10
In quantitative estimation of sulfur, 0.157 g of an organic compound gave 0.481 g of BaSO4. The percentage of sulfur is:

Solution: % S = (32 / 233) × (0.481 / 0.157) × 100 = 42.1%.

Question 8 of 10
What is the primary reason why Kjeldahl's method is not applicable to nitro compounds?

Solution: Nitro and azo nitrogen atoms are partially reduced to volatile products or remain bonded to aromatic rings, preventing quantitative conversion to ammonium sulfate.

Question 9 of 10
In the Dumas method, copper gauze is placed at the end of the combustion tube to:

Solution: Hot copper gauze reduces any nitrogen oxides (NOx) produced during combustion back to elemental nitrogen gas.

Question 10 of 10
A 0.30 g organic compound gave 0.50 g of CO2 and 0.10 g of H2O on combustion. The percentage of carbon is approximately:

Solution: % C = (12 / 44) × (0.50 / 0.30) × 100 = (3/11) × 1.667 × 100 = 45.45%.
