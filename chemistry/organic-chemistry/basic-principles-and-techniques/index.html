Organic Chemistry

Basic Principles and Techniques in Organic Chemistry

Organic chemistry encompasses the study of covalent carbon compounds, their structural isomerism, electronic displacement effects, and reaction intermediates. Quantitative and qualitative analytical methods establish empirical and molecular formulas of these compounds.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structural Representation and Isomerism

  • Organic molecules are represented using Lewis structures, condensed formulas, and bond-line formulas where lines represent carbon-carbon bonds and terminal ends represent methyl groups.
  • Structural isomerism includes chain, position, functional group, metamerism, and tautomerism arising from different connectivity of atoms.
  • Stereoisomerism involves identical constitution but different spatial arrangements, categorized into geometrical (cis-trans) and optical isomerism.
  • The IUPAC nomenclature system uses root word, primary suffix (-ane, -ene, -yne), and secondary suffix (functional group priority) to assign unique systematic names.

Electronic Displacements in Covalent Bonds

  • The Inductive effect (-I or +I) is a permanent polarization of a σ bond caused by electronegativity differences, decaying rapidly beyond three carbon atoms.
  • The Resonance (Mesomeric) effect involves delocalization of π electrons or lone pairs through a conjugated system, denoted as +R or -R.
  • The Electromeric effect (E) is a temporary polarization occurring in π-bond systems in the presence of an attacking reagent.
  • Hyperconjugation involves the interaction of σ electrons of a C-H bond of an alkyl group with an adjacent unfilled or partially filled p-orbital or π-orbital (no-bond resonance).

Reaction Intermediates and Purification Techniques

  • Carbocations are electron-deficient species with sp2 hybridization and a planar geometry, stabilized by +I, +R, and hyperconjugation effects.
  • Carbanions possess a negative charge and an unshared electron pair with pyrimidal geometry, stabilized by -I and -R effects.
  • Free radicals are neutral species possessing an odd electron, generated via homolytic cleavage and stabilized by alkyl substitution.
  • Purification techniques include sublimation, crystallization, distillation, differential extraction, and chromatography (TLC, column, HPLC).
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Percentage of Hydrogen (Liebig's Method)
% H = (2 / 18) × (Mass of H2O produced / Mass of organic compound) × 100
Conditions: Combustion of organic compound forming water vapor absorbed in anhydrous CaCl2
Units: Mass in grams
Percentage of Nitrogen (Kjeldahl's Method)
% N = (1.4 × Molarity of acid × Volume of acid neutralized) / Mass of organic compound
Conditions: Digestion with concentrated H2SO4 followed by distillation with NaOH
Units: Molarity in mol L-1, Volume in mL, Mass in grams
Percentage of Halogen (Carius Method)
% Halogen = (Atomic mass of halogen × Mass of AgX formed) / (Molecular mass of AgX × Mass of organic compound) × 100
Conditions: Heating with fuming HNO3 and AgNO3 in a Carius tube
Units: Mass in grams
Inductive Effect Magnitude
F ∝ q / r2
Conditions: Transmitted strictly through sigma (σ) bonds along a carbon chain
Units: Distance r in meters, Charge q in Coulombs
Percentage of Carbon (Liebig's Method)
% C = (12 / 44) × (Mass of CO2 produced / Mass of organic compound) × 100
Conditions: Combustion of organic compound in presence of excess oxygen and copper(II) oxide
Units: Mass in grams
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl groups attached to an unsaturated system generally release electrons via hyperconjugation.

Observation: The tert-butyl group exhibits greater electron-releasing power than the methyl group primarily due to an increased number of α-hydrogens. — Greater number of available C-H σ-bonds for overlap with adjacent vacant or π-orbitals increases stability.

Exception Kjeldahl's method is universally applicable for nitrogen estimation in all nitrogenous organic compounds.

Observation: Compounds containing nitrogen in a ring (e.g., pyridine) or nitro and azo groups do not give quantitative conversion to ammonium sulfate. — Nitrogen in these heterocyclic systems or oxidation states resists direct reduction by boiling sulfuric acid unless modified.

Exception Halogens present in all organic compounds precipitate quantitatively with AgNO3 directly without digestion.

Observation: Fluorine does not form a stable silver salt (AgF is soluble in water), hence Carius method fails for organofluorine compounds. — High lattice energy and high solubility prevent precipitation of silver fluoride.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Stability Order of Carbocations and Free Radicals
3° > 2° > 1° > CH3+

Application: Directly count α-hydrogens (hyperconjugative structures = n + 1) to determine relative stability of alkyl carbocations and radicals.

Kjeldahl Rapid Calculation Mnemonic
% N = (1.4 × N × V) / W

Application: Use N as normality or molarity multiplied by n-factor of the acid directly with volume in mL and weight in grams.

IUPAC Priority Order for Functional Groups
COOH > SO3H > anhydride > ester > acid halide > amide > cyanide > aldehyde > ketone > alcohol > amine

Application: Memorize the prefix/suffix hierarchy to determine the principal functional group serving as the primary suffix.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The inductive effect operates exclusively through sigma bonds and its magnitude drops sharply after three carbon atoms.
  • Hyperconjugation is also known as no-bond resonance or Baker-Nathan effect.
  • Kjeldahl's method cannot be used for nitro compounds, azo compounds, and pyridine derivatives.
  • The Carius method uses fuming nitric acid to oxidize sulfur and halogens to sulfuric acid and silver halides respectively.
  • In paper chromatography, the stationary phase is the moisture held in the pores of filter paper and the mobile phase is a liquid solvent.
  • Homolytic fission involves symmetrical cleavage of covalent bonds resulting in neutral free radicals with unpaired electrons.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the electromeric effect with the inductive effect; remember that electromeric effect is a temporary effect occurring only in the presence of a reagent.
  • Assuming Kjeldahl's method is applicable to all nitrogen-containing organic compounds without exception.
  • Forgetting that fluorine cannot be estimated by the standard Carius halogen method because silver fluoride is water-soluble.
  • Miscounting the number of alpha hydrogens when evaluating hyperconjugation in branched carbocations.

⚡ Quick Revision: Basic Principles and Techniques in Organic Chemistry

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structural Representation and Nomenclature

Organic structures are simplified using bond-line notation, and IUPAC nomenclature strictly follows functional group priority hierarchies.

Electronic Effects

Permanent (inductive, resonance) and temporary (electromeric) electronic shifts govern the electron density distribution in molecules.

Reaction Intermediates

Carbocations, carbanions, and free radicals act as transient reactive intermediates dictating organic reaction pathways.

Quantitative Analysis

Elemental composition is determined through stoichiometric combustion (Liebig), acid-base titration (Kjeldahl), and precipitation (Carius).

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Inductive Effect (-I / +I)
Permanent displacement of sigma electrons along a carbon chain due to electronegativity.
Resonance Effect (+R / -R)
Delocalization of pi electrons or lone pairs across a conjugated pi system.
Hyperconjugation (Baker-Nathan Effect)
Interaction of sigma electrons of C-H bond with adjacent empty or partially filled p-orbital.
Homolytic Cleavage (Symmetrical breaking)
Bond breaking where each bonded atom retains one electron to form free radicals.
Heterolytic Cleavage (Unsymmetrical breaking)
Bond breaking where one atom retains both electrons to form ions.
Chromatography (Partition/Adsorption)
Technique for separating mixture components between stationary and mobile phases.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Inductive effect decreases rapidly with distance and becomes negligible after C-3.
  • Stability of carbocations: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3+.
  • Stability of carbanions: CH3- > 1° > 2° > 3°.
  • Nitrogen percentage formula in Kjeldahl: % N = (1.4 × N × V) / W.
  • Hyperconjugation requires at least one alpha-hydrogen on a saturated carbon adjacent to an unsaturated system.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Structural representations dictate 2D and 3D geometries of carbon skeletons.
  • Electronic displacement effects explain relative acidity, basicity, and stability.
  • Reaction intermediates determine whether a mechanism is ionic or free radical.
  • Analytical techniques provide empirical and molecular formulas of organic compounds.

⚖️ Key Differences

Inductive Effect VS Resonance Effect

Inductive effect involves sigma electrons and acts through bonds with distance decay; resonance involves pi/lone-pair electrons and is distance-independent across conjugation.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following molecules shows the maximum +I effect?

Explanation: Tert-butyl group (-C(CH3)3) has the maximum number of electron-releasing methyl groups attached to the central carbon, exerting the highest +I effect.

Question 2 of 19

The number of sigma and pi bonds in pent-2-en-4-yne is:

Explanation: Pent-2-en-4-yne (HC≡C-CH=CH-CH3) contains 10 sigma bonds and 3 pi bonds (one in double bond, two in triple bond).

Question 3 of 19

Which species is electrophilic in nature?

Explanation: AlCl3 is an electron-deficient Lewis acid with an incomplete octet, making it a strong electrophile.

Question 4 of 19

The IUPAC name of CH3-CH(OH)-CH2-CN is:

Explanation: The longest carbon chain contains 4 carbons with nitrile carbon as C-1, making it 3-hydroxybutanenitrile.

Question 5 of 19

Which intermediate has an octet of electrons around the central carbon?

Explanation: A carbanion possesses a negative charge and a lone pair, giving the central carbon 8 electrons (complete octet).

Question 6 of 19

Hyperconjugation involves the delocalization of:

Explanation: Hyperconjugation involves overlap between sigma-electrons of a C-H bond and an adjacent vacant or partially filled p-orbital.

Question 7 of 19

Which compound does not give Lassaigne's test for nitrogen?

Explanation: Benzene contains no nitrogen atoms, hence it will not give a positive Lassaigne's test for nitrogen.

Question 8 of 19

In Kjeldahl's method, copper sulfate acts as a:

Explanation: Anhydrous copper sulfate acts as a catalyst to speed up the digestion of organic nitrogenous compounds with sulfuric acid.

Question 9 of 19

Which of the following carbocations is most stable?

Explanation: Tert-butyl carbocation (3°) has 9 alpha hydrogens providing maximum hyperconjugative stabilization.

Question 10 of 19

The state of hybridization of carbon atoms in allenes (CH2=C=CH2) is:

Explanation: The terminal carbons are sp2 hybridized, while the central carbon is sp hybridized due to two orthogonal double bonds.

Question 11 of 19

Which effect is temporary and operates only in the presence of a reagent?

Explanation: The electromeric effect is a temporary polarization of pi-electrons induced by the approach of an attacking reagent.

Question 12 of 19

Which of the following is an example of metamerism?

Explanation: Ethoxyethane and 1-methoxypropane are metamers as they have the same functional group (ether) but different alkyl chains attached to the oxygen atom.

Question 13 of 19

In paper chromatography, separation is based on:

Explanation: Paper chromatography is a type of partition chromatography where components partition between stationary liquid phase and mobile liquid phase.

Question 14 of 19

Which of the following groups exerts a -R effect?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) withdraws electrons via resonance, exerting a -R effect.

Question 15 of 19

The correct stability order of free radicals is:

Explanation: Alkyl free radicals follow the stability order 3° > 2° > 1° due to hyperconjugation and inductive stabilization.

Question 16 of 19

Which halogen does not form a precipitate with silver nitrate in Carius method?

Explanation: Silver fluoride (AgF) is soluble in water, hence fluorine cannot be estimated by the Carius method using silver nitrate.

Question 17 of 19

Tautomerism is exhibited by:

Explanation: Acetone (CH3COCH3) possesses alpha-hydrogens, enabling keto-enol tautomerism.

Question 18 of 19

Which bond undergoes homolytic cleavage most easily under UV light?

Explanation: The Cl-Cl bond has a relatively low bond dissociation enthalpy and readily undergoes homolytic fission upon absorbing UV photons.

Question 19 of 19

The IUPAC suffix for a secondary amine is:

Explanation: Primary, secondary, and tertiary amines all use '-amine' as the primary suffix in IUPAC nomenclature.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following species is aromatic according to Huckel's rule?

Solution: Cyclopentadienyl anion has 6 pi electrons (4n + 2 where n = 1), a planar ring, and complete delocalization, making it aromatic.

Question 2 of 10
The IUPAC name of the compound CH3-CH=CH-C≡CH is:

Solution: Numbering from the end closer to multiple bonds gives lowest locants: pent-3-en-1-yne (lower locant given to double bond in case of a tie).

Question 3 of 10
Which group exhibits a +R effect?

Solution: The amino group (-NH2) donates electrons through resonance via its lone pair, exerting a +R effect.

Question 4 of 10
What is the hybridization of carbon atoms in acetonitrile (CH3CN)?

Solution: The methyl carbon is sp3 hybridized while the cyanide carbon and nitrogen are sp hybridized.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following compounds gives a blood-red coloration in Lassaigne's test for nitrogen and sulfur together?

Solution: Presence of both N and S yields sodium thiocyanate (NaCNS), which reacts with Fe3+ to form ferric thiocyanate having a blood-red color.

Question 6 of 10
The hyperconjugative structures involving a methyl group attached to an alkene have no bond between:

Solution: Hyperconjugation results in a 'no-bond resonance' structure where the proton (H+) remains in the vicinity without a covalent bond.

Question 7 of 10
Which method is best suited for the separation of a mixture of ortho- and para-nitrophenols?

Solution: Ortho-nitrophenol is steam volatile due to intramolecular hydrogen bonding, whereas para-nitrophenol forms intermolecular hydrogen bonds and is less volatile.

Question 8 of 10
The C-C bond length in benzene is:

Solution: Due to resonance delocalization, the carbon-carbon bond length in benzene is intermediate between single (1.54 Å) and double bonds (1.34 Å), measuring 1.39 Å.

Question 9 of 10
Which carbocation is stabilized by resonance?

Solution: The benzyl carbocation benefits from extensive resonance delocalization of the positive charge into the benzene ring.

Question 10 of 10
In Duma's method for nitrogen estimation, the gas collected in the nitrometer is:

Solution: Duma's method involves passing nitrogen oxides over heated copper gauze to reduce them to elemental nitrogen gas (N2).
