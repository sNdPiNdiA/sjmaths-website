Organic Chemistry

IUPAC Nomenclature

IUPAC nomenclature provides a systematic method of naming organic chemical compounds to ensure each structure has a unique and unambiguous name. The system relies on identifying the principal functional group, the longest carbon chain, and assigning locants using the lowest possible numbers.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Selection of Principal Chain and Unsaturation

  • The principal chain must be selected based on the maximum number of functional groups, followed by maximum multiple bonds (double then triple), maximum carbon atoms, and maximum substituents.
  • A double bond takes precedence over a triple bond in numbering only when there is a choice between equivalent locants (e.g., pent-3-en-1-yne vs pent-2-en-4-yne).
  • If a chain contains both a ring and a chain, the one with the greater number of carbon atoms usually forms the base component unless rules prioritize the cyclic system.
  • Unsaturated systems must incorporate maximum multiple bonds into the parent root even if a slightly longer aliphatic chain exists without them.

Functional Group Suffixes and Prefixes

  • The principal functional group is designated by the secondary suffix at the very end of the IUPAC name.
  • All other functional groups and alkyl substituents are designated as prefixes and arranged in alphabetical order (ignoring numerical multiplying prefixes like di-, tri-, tetra-).
  • Terminal functional groups like -CHO, -CN, and -COOH include the carbon atom in the principal chain unless directed otherwise by specific cyclic nomenclature rules.
  • When terminal functional groups cannot be included in the chain, special replacement suffixes such as -carbaldehyde, -carbonitrile, and -carboxylic acid are used.

Stereochemical Descriptors in Nomenclature

  • Geometrical isomerism is denoted using (E) (entgegen, opposite) and (Z) (zusammen, together) based on Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priority rules.
  • Optical activity and absolute configuration are specified using (R) (rectus) and (S) (sinister) prefixes assigned to chiral centers.
  • Relative stereochemistry in cyclic systems is indicated via cis and trans or syn and anti descriptors.
  • Chirality descriptors and locants are separated by hyphens and enclosed in parentheses at the beginning of the IUPAC name.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Locant Numbering Rule
Lowest locant set: (a, b, c...) < (a', b', c'...)
Conditions: When comparing two different numbering directions point-by-point in order of increasing numbers.
Units: Dimensionless integer indices
Degree of Unsaturation (Double Bond Equivalent)
DBE = C + 1 - (H / 2) + (N / 2) - (X / 2)
Conditions: Where C = carbons, H = hydrogens, N = nitrogens, X = halogens.
Units: Dimensionless count of rings and pi bonds
Priority Sequence of Functional Groups
-COOH > -SO3H > -COOR > -COX > -CONH2 > -CN > -CHO > >C=O > -OH > -NH2 > -C=C- > -C≡C-
Conditions: Used to determine the principal functional group serving as the secondary suffix.
Units: Ordinal hierarchy rank
General IUPAC Structure
Name = Prefix + Root Word + Primary Suffix + Secondary Suffix
Conditions: Applicable to all organic compounds containing substituents, principal chains, unsaturation, and functional groups.
Units: Not applicable
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl substituents are generally arranged in alphabetical order including multiplying prefixes.

Observation: Multiplying prefixes (di-, tri-, tetra-, sec-, tert-) are ignored in alphabetization, except that iso- and neo- are included. — Standard IUPAC rule treats 'iso' and 'neo' as integral parts of the alkyl group name, whereas 'sec-' and 'tert-' prefixes are hyphenated and ignored.

Exception Numbering starts from the end closest to the substituent or functional group.

Observation: Lowest locant rule takes absolute precedence over alphabetical order when numbering from both ends yields different sets. — The complete locant set comparison (e.g., 2,5 vs 3,6) governs the lowest set independent of which substituent appears first alphabetically.

Exception Principal functional groups provide the secondary suffix.

Observation: Ethers, halides, and nitro groups are never cited as suffixes; they are strictly treated as prefixes. — These functional groups lack official IUPAC secondary suffix designations and are classified exclusively as substituents.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Priority Sequence Mnemonic
Car S Car Car Con Cyan Ald Ket OH Am Amin

Application: Remember the order: Carboxylic acid > Sulfonic acid > Ester > Acid halide > Amide > Cyanide > Aldehyde > Ketone > Alcohol > Amine.

Alphabetical Rule Trick
Ignore 'di, tri, tetra, sec, tert'; Keep 'iso, neo, cyclo'

Application: Quickly check substituent sorting by crossing out hyphenated prefixes and multiplying factors before alphabetizing.

DBE Shortcut
DBE = C - (H/2) + (N/2) + 1

Application: Quickly find degrees of unsaturation by ignoring monovalent halogens (replace H with H+X) and oxygen/sulfur atoms.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The IUPAC name for acetone is propan-2-one.
  • Tertiary butyl alcohol IUPAC name is 2-methylpropan-2-ol.
  • Acetonitrile IUPAC name is ethanenitrile.
  • Formaldehyde IUPAC name is methanal.
  • Acetic acid IUPAC name is ethanoic acid.
  • Formic acid IUPAC name is methanoic acid.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Failing to include the carbon of the functional group (like -COOH or -CN) into the principal chain when it should be counted.
  • Alphabetizing based on prefixes like 'di-' or 'tri-' instead of the core substituent name.
  • Assigning lower locants based on alphabetical priority instead of the strict lowest locant set rule.
  • Treating ethers as suffixes instead of alkoxy prefixes.

⚡ Quick Revision: IUPAC Nomenclature

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Root Word and Carbon Count

The root word designates the number of carbon atoms in the principal continuous chain (e.g., meth-, eth-, prop-, but-, pent-).

Suffixes for Unsaturation

Primary suffixes (-ane, -ene, -yne) indicate the presence of single, double, or triple carbon-carbon bonds respectively.

Principal Functional Group Suffix

The secondary suffix defines the highest priority functional group present in the molecule, dictating the principal class of the compound.

Substituent Prefixes and Locants

Substituents and lower-priority functional groups are denoted as alphabetical prefixes preceded by exact numerical locants.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Locant (Numerical index)
Number indicating the position of a substituent or unsaturation.
Principal Chain (Parent hydrocarbon)
Longest continuous carbon chain complying with priority rules.
Functional Group (Characteristic moiety)
Specific atom or group of atoms responsible for chemical reactions.
Homologous Series (Family of compounds)
Series of compounds differing by a -CH2- unit.
Degree of Unsaturation (DBE)
Sum of rings and pi bonds in a molecule.
Zwitterion (Internal salt)
Neutral molecule with positive and negative electrical charges.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carboxylic acids have the highest general IUPAC functional group priority.
  • Numbering always gives lowest locants to multiple bonds over alkyl substituents.
  • Prefixes are arranged alphabetically, ignoring hyphenated prefixes like sec- and tert-.
  • Terminal carbon atoms of -CN and -COOH are carbon number 1 in the principal chain.
  • Ethers are always named as alkoxy substituents.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Systematic nomenclature prevents ambiguity across chemical literature.
  • Priority tables dictate secondary suffixes.
  • Lowest locant rule overrides alphabetical order during numbering.
  • Stereochemical descriptors define 3D spatial arrangements.

⚖️ Key Differences

Acyl halide vs Acid anhydride VS -COX vs -(CO)2O

Acyl halides use 'oyl halide' suffix, while anhydrides use 'anhydride' with corresponding acid names.

What is the correct IUPAC name for (CH3)3CCl?

Explanation: The longest carbon chain contains 3 carbons (propane). At carbon-2, there is a chloro and a methyl group. Thus, 2-chloro-2-methylpropane.

Question 2 of 18

Which functional group has the highest priority according to IUPAC rules?

Explanation: Carboxylic acid (-COOH) sits at the top of the standard IUPAC functional group priority table.

Question 3 of 18

What is the IUPAC name of CH3CH2OCH2CH3?

Explanation: Ethers are named as alkoxyalkanes; here, an ethoxy group is attached to an ethane chain, yielding ethoxyethane.

Question 4 of 18

What is the IUPAC name for CH2=CH-CH2-OH?

Explanation: Numbering starts from the carbon closest to the -OH group, giving prop-2-en-1-ol.

Question 5 of 18

What is the correct IUPAC name for CH3-CH(CH3)-CHO?

Explanation: The aldehyde carbon is C-1, and a methyl group is at C-2 on a 3-carbon chain (propanal), yielding 2-methylpropanal.

Question 6 of 18

Determine the degree of unsaturation for a compound with formula C6H10.

Explanation: DBE = 6 + 1 - (10/2) = 7 - 5 = 2. This corresponds to two double bonds or one triple bond or a ring plus a double bond.

Question 7 of 18

What is the IUPAC name of acetyl chloride (CH3COCl)?

Explanation: Acyl chlorides use the suffix -oyl chloride on a 2-carbon chain, giving ethanoyl chloride.

Question 8 of 18

What is the IUPAC name for CH3COCH3?

Explanation: Propanone and propan-2-one are both accepted IUPAC names for acetone.

Question 9 of 18

What is the IUPAC name of CH3-C≡CH?

Explanation: Propyne and prop-1-yne are both correct IUPAC names for methylacetylene.

Question 10 of 18

How is the -NH2 group designated when a higher priority functional group is present?

Explanation: When an amine is subordinate to a higher priority group like a carboxylic acid, it is named using the prefix 'amino-'.

Question 11 of 18

What is the IUPAC name of CH3CH(NH2)COOH?

Explanation: The principal group is -COOH (C-1), with an amino group at C-2 on a 3-carbon chain, yielding 2-aminopropanoic acid.

Question 12 of 18

What is the IUPAC name for NC-CH2-CN?

Explanation: A 3-carbon chain with two nitrile groups at the ends is named propanedinitrile.

Question 13 of 18

What is the correct IUPAC name for benzene-1,2-dicarboxylic acid?

Explanation: Benzene-1,2-dicarboxylic acid is commonly known as phthalic acid.

Question 14 of 18

What is the IUPAC name for CH2(OH)-CH(OH)-CH2(OH)?

Explanation: The systematic IUPAC name for glycerol is propane-1,2,3-triol.

Question 15 of 18

What is the IUPAC name for CH3-COO-CH3?

Explanation: Esters are named as alkyl alkanoates; the alkyl group is methyl and the acid part is ethanoate, giving methyl ethanoate.

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following describes the prefix order for 3-ethyl-5-methylheptane?

Explanation: Substituents are always cited in alphabetical order ('ethyl' before 'methyl').

Question 17 of 18

What is the IUPAC name for p-cresol?

Explanation: Both 4-methylphenol and 1-hydroxy-4-methylbenzene are accepted IUPAC names for p-cresol.

Question 18 of 18

What is the IUPAC name for CH3CONH2?

Explanation: A two-carbon amide is named ethanamide according to systematic IUPAC rules.
