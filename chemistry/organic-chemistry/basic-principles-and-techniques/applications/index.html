Organic Chemistry

Applications of Electronic Displacements and Reactive Intermediates

Electronic displacement effects including inductive, resonance, hyperconjugation, and electromeric effects dictate the stability of reactive intermediates and acidic/basic strengths of organic compounds. These permanent and temporary polarization phenomena govern reaction pathways, regioselectivity, and stereochemical outcomes in organic transformations.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Stability of Carbocations, Free Radicals, and Carbanions

  • Carbocation stability increases with the number of hyperconjugative structures and +I groups, following the order: 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl.
  • Free radical stability follows the same order as carbocations (3° > 2° > 1°) due to hyperconjugation and spin delocalization.
  • Carbanion stability is inversely proportional to carbocations, increasing with −I and −R groups: 1° > 2° > 3° due to steric hindrance and charge intensification.
  • Allylic and benzylic carbocations and radicals exhibit exceptional stability due to extensive π-electron resonance delocalization.

Acidic and Basic Strength of Organic Compounds

  • Carboxylic acids are stronger acids than alcohols because the carboxylate anion is stabilized by equivalent resonance structures.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups (−I, −R) enhance acidity by dispersing negative charge, whereas electron-donating groups (+I, +R) decrease acidity.
  • Aliphatic amine basicity in aqueous solution is determined by a combination of +I effect, steric hindrance, and solvation energy, yielding the order (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > (CH3)3N for methylamines.
  • Aromatic amines like aniline are weaker bases than aliphatic amines because the lone pair on nitrogen is delocalized into the benzene ring via resonance.

Heat of Hydrogenation and Alkene Stability

  • Alkene stability is directly proportional to the number of α-hydrogens available for hyperconjugation and inversely proportional to its heat of hydrogenation.
  • Trans-alkenes are generally more stable than cis-alkenes due to minimized steric repulsion between bulky alkyl groups.
  • Tetrasubstituted alkenes possess lower heats of hydrogenation compared to disubstituted or monosubstituted isomers, indicating higher thermodynamic stability.
  • Bredt's rule states that a double bond cannot be placed at the bridgehead of a bridged ring system if the ring contains fewer than eight carbon atoms due to angle strain and pyramidalization.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception 3° Aliphatic amines are strongest bases due to maximum +I effect.

Observation: Trimethylamine is less basic than dimethylamine in aqueous solution. — Steric hindrance to hydrogen bonding and effective solvation of the protonated cation override the +I inductive effect.

Exception Electron-withdrawing groups always increase acidity at any position.

Observation: Ortho-substituted benzoic acids are stronger acids than benzoic acid regardless of whether the substituent is +I or −I (ortho effect). — Steric inhibition of resonance forces the carboxyl group out of the coplanar alignment with the benzene ring, destabilizing the neutral acid and favoring ionization.

Exception Halogens exert a −I effect and decrease basicity of aliphatic amines.

Observation: Haloamines show complex basicity trends dependent on solvent and inductive-field interplay. — Combined field effects and polarizability counteract the simple sigma inductive attenuation over longer carbon chains.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Picric acid (2,4,6-trinitrophenol) is a stronger acid than acetic acid due to intense −R and −I effects of three nitro groups stabilizing the phenoxide ion.
  • Formic acid is stronger than acetic acid because the methyl group in acetic acid exerts a +I electron-donating effect that destabilizes the carboxylate anion.
  • Aniline is a weaker base than ammonia because the lone pair on nitrogen is delocalized into the benzene ring.
  • Pyrrole is a very weak base because its nitrogen lone pair is part of the aromatic 6π-electron sextet (Huckel's rule).
  • Triphenylmethyl carbocation is exceptionally stable due to extensive resonance delocalization across three phenyl rings.
  • The dipole moment of chlorobenzene is less than that of methyl chloride despite chlorine's high electronegativity due to opposing resonance and inductive vectors.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the temporary electromeric effect (+E/−E) with permanent inductive or resonance effects; remember E-effect operates only in the presence of an attacking reagent.
  • Assuming hyperconjugation involves only C-H bonds; C-D and C-F bonds can also participate, exhibiting an isotope effect.
  • Forgetting that meta-directing groups deactivate the benzene ring for electrophilic aromatic substitution via both −I and −R effects.
  • Neglecting steric inhibition of resonance in ortho-substituted benzoic acid derivatives when evaluating pKa values.

⚡ Quick Revision: Applications of Electronic Displacements and Reactive Intermediates

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Inductive Effect

Permanent shifting of σ-electrons along a carbon chain induced by electronegativity differences, categorized into electron-withdrawing (−I) and electron-donating (+I) groups.

Resonance and Mesomeric Effect

Delocalization of π-electrons or lone pairs across conjugated systems, significantly influencing bond lengths, dipole moments, and chemical reactivity.

Hyperconjugation (No-Bond Resonance)

Interaction between σ-electrons of C−H bond of an alkyl group and an adjacent empty or partially filled p-orbital (σ−p conjugation).

Reactive Intermediates

High-energy, short-lived chemical species including carbocations, carbanions, free radicals, carbenes, and nitrenes whose stabilities dictate reaction mechanisms.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Inductive Effect (-I / +I Effect)
Permanent polarization of a σ-bond due to electronegativity difference.
Resonance Energy (Eres)
Energy difference between the most stable canonical structure and the resonance hybrid.
Hyperconjugation (Baker-Nathan Effect)
Delocalization of σ-electrons into adjacent unsaturated systems.
Electromeric Effect (+E / -E Effect)
Temporary complete transfer of π-electrons under the influence of a reagent.
Carbocation (R3C+)
Species containing a sp2-hybridized carbon with a sextet of electrons.
Carbanion (R3C−)
Species containing a sp3-hybridized carbon with an octet and a negative charge.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Order of carbocation stability: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3+
  • Order of carbanion stability: CH3− > 1° > 2° > 3°
  • Order of free radical stability: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3•
  • Strongest aliphatic amine base in gas phase: Tertiary amine (3° > 2° > 1° > NH3)
  • Carboxylic acids are stronger acids than phenols; phenols are stronger than alcohols.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Inductive effect decreases rapidly with distance and is negligible beyond three carbon atoms.
  • Resonance structures must have identical positions of atomic nuclei and the same number of paired electrons.
  • Hyperconjugation explains the relative stability of substituted alkenes and alkyl radicals.
  • Ortho-effect causes anomalous acidity in benzoic acid derivatives and basicity in aniline derivatives.

⚖️ Key Differences

Inductive Effect VS Electromeric Effect

Inductive effect is permanent and operates through σ-bonds; electromeric effect is temporary and operates through π-bonds upon reagent approach.

Question 3 of 20

The correct order of basicity of aliphatic amines in aqueous solution is:

Explanation: In aqueous medium, basicity depends on +I effect, steric hindrance, and solvation, resulting in secondary > tertiary > primary for methylamines.

Which of the following alkenes has the lowest heat of hydrogenation?

Explanation: trans-But-2-ene is the most thermodynamically stable alkene among the options, hence it has the lowest heat of hydrogenation.

Question 5 of 20

The hyperconjugative stability of alkyl radicals is due to overlap between:

Explanation: Hyperconjugation in free radicals involves delocalization of σ-electrons of C−H bond with the single electron in the half-filled p-orbital.

Question 6 of 20

Which group exerts a +R (plus mesomeric) effect?

Explanation: The hydroxyl group (−OH) has lone pairs on oxygen that can be donated into the conjugated system, exhibiting a +R effect.

Question 7 of 20

Which of the following compounds is most acidic?

Explanation: Phenol forms a resonance-stabilized phenoxide ion, making it significantly more acidic than ethanol, water, or ethyne.

Question 8 of 20

Which radical is stabilized by both resonance and hyperconjugation?

Explanation: The benzyl radical benefits from resonance delocalization into the aromatic ring as well as hyperconjugation from the methylene C−H bonds.

Question 9 of 20

The number of hyperconjugative structures for an isopropyl carbocation is:

Explanation: An isopropyl carbocation has 6 α-hydrogen atoms (three on each adjacent methyl group), yielding 6 hyperconjugative structures.

Question 10 of 20

Which of the following molecules has zero dipole moment?

Explanation: In 1,4-dichlorobenzene, the individual C−Cl bond dipole moments are equal in magnitude and directly oppose each other, canceling out to zero.

Question 11 of 20

Decreasing order of −I effect for the groups (−NO2, −F, −CN, −OH) is:

Explanation: Standard electronegativity and electron-withdrawing capacity place nitro and cyano groups ahead of halogens and hydroxyl in inductive electron withdrawal.

Question 12 of 20

Which species acts as an electrophile?

Explanation: AlCl3 has an incomplete octet (sextet of electrons on aluminum), making it a Lewis acid and a strong electrophile.

Question 13 of 20

The stability order of carbanions (CH3−, 1°, 2°, 3°) is:

Explanation: +I effect of alkyl groups intensifies negative charge on carbanion carbon, destabilizing 3° carbanions. Thus, CH3− > 1° > 2° > 3°.

Question 14 of 20

Which of the following is an example of a nucleophile?

Explanation: The cyanide ion (CN−) possesses a lone pair of electrons and a negative charge, acting as a classic carbon nucleophile.

Question 15 of 20

The compound with the highest pKa value among the following is:

Explanation: A higher pKa indicates a weaker acid. Ethanol is the weakest acid among the choices, hence has the highest pKa.

Question 16 of 20

In electrophilic aromatic substitution, the group −NHCOCH3 is:

Explanation: The acetamido group (−NHCOCH3) donates electrons via resonance but experiences steric and inductive moderation from the carbonyl, making it moderately activating.

Question 17 of 20

Which of the following exhibits tautomerism?

Explanation: Acetaldehyde (CH3CHO) possesses α-hydrogens adjacent to the carbonyl group, enabling keto-enol tautomerism.

Question 18 of 20

Heterolytic bond cleavage of a carbon-chlorine bond produces:

Explanation: Heterolytic fission results in unequal sharing of bonding electrons, yielding a carbocation (R+) and a halide anion (Cl−).

Question 19 of 20

Which isomer of diaminobenzene is most basic?

Explanation: Ortho-phenylenediamine experiences intramolecular hydrogen bonding between the two amino groups, stabilizing the conjugate acid and increasing basicity.

Question 20 of 20

The dipole moment is highest for:

Explanation: In cis-1,2-dichloroethylene, the individual bond dipoles reinforce each other at an angle, giving a substantial net dipole moment unlike the trans isomer.

Which carbocation is stabilized solely by hyperconjugation and inductive effect without resonance?

Solution: Tert-butyl carbocation lacks conjugated π-systems, hence its stability stems entirely from hyperconjugation and +I effect of methyl groups.

Question 2 of 10
What is the primary reason for the high acidity of picric acid compared to phenol?

Solution: Three nitro groups at ortho and para positions exert strong −R and −I effects, efficiently delocalizing and dispersing the negative charge of the phenoxide ion.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following molecules has a permanent dipole moment different from zero?

Solution: cis-1,2-Dichloroethene is unsymmetrical in terms of vector addition of bond dipoles, giving a net dipole moment of 1.90 D.

Question 4 of 10
In the reaction of propene with HBr, the formation of 2-bromopropane proceeds via which intermediate?

Solution: Markovnikov addition proceeds via the more stable secondary carbocation intermediate.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following statements regarding resonance is correct?

Solution: The resonance hybrid is more stable than any individual canonical structure by an amount equal to the resonance energy.

Question 6 of 10
The correct order of acidity for halogenoacetic acids is:

Solution: Acidity parallels the electronegativity of the halogen atom due to the −I effect: F > Cl > Br > I.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following compounds does not exhibit keto-enol tautomerism?

Solution: Benzophenone (C6H5COC6H5) lacks α-hydrogen atoms adjacent to the carbonyl carbon, preventing enolization.

Question 8 of 10
The −I effect is strongest in which of the following groups?

Solution: Fluorine has the highest electronegativity among the options, hence exerts the strongest −I inductive effect.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following intermediates is electron-deficient and acts as an electrophile?

Solution: Divalent carbenes (e.g., :CH2) possess a sextet of electrons and act as electrophiles.

Question 10 of 10
What is the hybridization of the central carbon atom in a carbocation?

Solution: A carbocation carbon forms three sigma bonds and possesses an empty unhybridized p-orbital, corresponding to sp2 hybridization and trigonal planar geometry.
