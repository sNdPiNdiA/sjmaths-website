Organic Chemistry

Types of Organic Reactions

Organic reactions are classified based on the nature of bond cleavage, reagent type, and the overall transformation of functional groups. The primary categories include substitution, addition, elimination, and rearrangement reactions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Substitution Reactions

  • Nucleophilic substitution proceeds via two primary pathways: SN1 (two-step with carbocation intermediate) and SN2 (concerted single-step with inversion).
  • Electrophilic aromatic substitution (EAS) involves attack by an electrophile on the π-electron cloud of an aromatic ring, followed by proton loss to restore aromaticity.
  • Free radical substitution occurs in alkanes via initiation, propagation, and termination steps in the presence of UV light or peroxides.

Addition and Elimination Reactions

  • Electrophilic addition is characteristic of alkenes and alkynes, governed by Markovnikov's rule where the electrophile adds to yield the more stable carbocation.
  • Elimination reactions (E1, E2, E1cB) convert saturated compounds into unsaturated ones, typically following Zaitsev's rule for regioselectivity.
  • Nucleophilic addition dominates carbonyl chemistry, where aldehydes and ketones react with nucleophiles via a tetrahedral intermediate.

Rearrangement and Pericyclic Reactions

  • Rearrangement reactions involve the migration of a group from one atom to an adjacent atom, driven by the formation of a more stable carbocation or intermediate.
  • Wagner-Meerwein and Pinacol-Pinacolone rearrangements are classic examples involving 1,2-shifts of alkyl or aryl groups.
  • Pericyclic reactions proceed through concerted cyclic transition states without discrete intermediates, governed by Woodward-Hoffmann rules.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Tertiary alkyl halides undergo SN1 substitution.

Observation: Bridgehead tertiary halides (e.g., 1-bromobicyclo[2.2.2]octane) are completely inert to SN1 and SN2 reactions. — Carbocation cannot achieve planar sp2 hybridization at the bridgehead position due to ring strain.

Exception Addition of HBr to alkenes follows Markovnikov's rule.

Observation: In the presence of peroxides (ROOR), HBr addition to unsymmetrical alkenes follows anti-Markovnikov's rule (Kharasch effect). — The reaction shifts from an ionic electrophilic addition mechanism to a free radical chain mechanism.

Exception Elimination of alkyl halides yields Zaitsev's more substituted alkene.

Observation: Bulky bases (e.g., potassium tert-butoxide) with secondary/tertiary halides yield the less substituted Hofmann alkene. — Steric hindrance prevents the base from abstracting the more hindered internal proton, favoring the accessible terminal proton.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • SN2 reactions proceed with complete inversion of configuration (Walden inversion).
  • SN1 reactions proceed with racemization due to attack from both faces of the planar carbocation.
  • Electrophilic addition to alkenes forms a cyclic halonium ion intermediate for halogens like Br2.
  • E2 elimination requires anti-periplanar geometry between the leaving group and the β-hydrogen.
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation suffers from carbocation rearrangement and polyalkylation complications.
  • Dehydration of alcohols follows an E1 mechanism via a carbocation intermediate with possible rearrangements.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming all primary alkyl halides undergo SN2 exclusively without considering rearrangement or E2 competition with strong bases.
  • Forgetting that carbocations can undergo 1,2-hydride or 1,2-methyl shifts to form more stable tertiary carbocations.
  • Confusing the role of peroxides in hydrohalogenation (peroxide effect applies strictly to HBr, not HCl or HI).
  • Neglecting stereochemical outcomes like inversion in SN2 and retention/inversion mixtures in SN1.

⚡ Quick Revision: Types of Organic Reactions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Bond Cleavage and Intermediates

Homolytic cleavage yields free radicals, whereas heterolytic cleavage yields carbocations and carbanions acting as reactive intermediates.

Substitution Pathways

SN1 is favored by tertiary substrates and polar protic solvents, while SN2 is favored by primary substrates and strong nucleophiles in polar aprotic solvents.

Addition Reactions

Electrophilic addition to alkenes adheres to Markovnikov's rule via stable carbocation intermediates, reversed only by radical initiators with HBr.

Elimination Pathways

E1 proceeds via carbocation formation and Zaitsev's rule, whereas E2 is a concerted, single-step process requiring anti-periplanar alignment.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Nucleophile (Nu-)
Electron-rich species that donates an electron pair to form a chemical bond.
Electrophile (E+)
Electron-deficient species that accepts an electron pair.
Carbocation (R3C+)
Carbon species bearing a positive charge and six valence electrons (sp2).
Free Radical (R•)
Neutral species containing an unpaired electron.
Zaitsev's Rule (Elimination Rule)
Predicts that the major alkene product has the greater number of alkyl substituents on the double bond.
Markovnikov's Rule (Addition Rule)
States that the electrophile adds to the carbon with more hydrogen atoms in unsymmetrical alkenes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • SN2 rate = k[Substrate][Nucleophile]
  • SN1 rate = k[Substrate]
  • Carbocation stability: 3° > 2° > 1°
  • Free radical stability: 3° > 2° > 1°
  • E2 requires anti-periplanar geometry

📌 Quick Recall

  • Organic reactions are classified into substitution, addition, elimination, and rearrangement.
  • Reaction mechanisms depend on substrate structure, reagent type, solvent, and temperature.
  • Intermediates like carbocations, carbanions, and free radicals dictate stereochemical outcomes.
  • Regioselectivity and stereoselectivity are core principles in predicting major organic products.

⚖️ Key Differences

SN1 Mechanism VS SN2 Mechanism

SN1 is unimolecular, stepwise with a carbocation and racemization; SN2 is bimolecular, concerted with inversion.

Which of the following alkyl halides undergoes SN1 reaction most rapidly?

Explanation: Tertiary butyl chloride forms a stable tertiary carbocation, making it the most reactive toward SN1 substitution.

Question 2 of 18

In an SN2 reaction involving a chiral substrate, the stereochemical outcome is:

Explanation: SN2 is a concerted backside attack mechanism resulting in complete inversion of configuration.

Question 3 of 18

Which rule governs the regioselectivity of electrophilic addition to unsymmetrical alkenes?

Explanation: Markovnikov's rule predicts the major product based on the formation of the more stable carbocation intermediate.

Question 4 of 18

Which reagent causes anti-Markovnikov addition of HBr to propene?

Explanation: In the presence of organic peroxides, HBr adds via a free radical mechanism yielding the anti-Markovnikov product.

Question 5 of 18

What is the hybridization of the central carbon in a carbocation?

Explanation: Carbocations possess three sigma bonds and an empty p-orbital, resulting in trigonal planar geometry with sp2 hybridization.

Question 6 of 18

Which of the following is a polar aprotic solvent favorable for SN2 reactions?

Explanation: Acetone is a polar aprotic solvent that does not solvate nucleophiles strongly, enhancing their reactivity in SN2 reactions.

Question 7 of 18

The intermediate formed in the chlorination of methane via free radical substitution is:

Explanation: Homolytic cleavage of chlorine molecules by UV light generates chlorine radicals, initiating a radical chain mechanism.

Question 8 of 18

Which elimination mechanism proceeds through a concerted, single-step pathway?

Explanation: E2 is a bimolecular, concerted elimination where proton abstraction and leaving group departure occur simultaneously.

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following compounds is most reactive towards electrophilic aromatic substitution?

Explanation: Phenol has an strongly activating -OH group via resonance, increasing electron density on the benzene ring for electrophilic attack.

Question 10 of 18

What geometry is required for the leaving group and β-hydrogen in an E2 elimination?

Explanation: E2 elimination requires an anti-periplanar (180°) conformation to achieve optimal orbital overlap during transition.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following species acts as an electrophile?

Explanation: AlCl3 has an incomplete octet (Lewis acid), making it an electron-deficient electrophile.

Question 12 of 18

During the dehydration of 2-butanol with concentrated H2SO4, the major alkene formed is:

Explanation: Zaitsev's rule dictates that 2-butene (disubstituted/trisubstituted stable alkene) is the major product over 1-butene.

Question 13 of 18

Which reaction intermediate is electron-deficient and possesses a sextet of electrons?

Explanation: Carbocations have six valence electrons around the positively charged carbon atom.

Question 14 of 18

Which alkyl halide reacts fastest in an SN2 displacement with sodium iodide in acetone?

Explanation: 1-Bromobutane provides a good balance of primary carbon reactivity and a stable leaving group (Br-) for SN2.

Question 15 of 18

Friedel-Crafts acylation of benzene uses an acyl chloride in the presence of:

Explanation: Anhydrous AlCl3 acts as a Lewis acid catalyst to generate the acylium ion electrophile.

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following groups is meta-directing in electrophilic aromatic substitution?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is strongly electron-withdrawing via -I and -M effects, directing incoming electrophiles to the meta position.

Question 17 of 18

What is the primary role of the catalyst in a chemical reaction?

Explanation: Catalysts lower activation energy (ΔG‡) without altering thermodynamics or equilibrium positions.

Question 18 of 18

Which of the following reactions involves the conversion of a ketone to a hydrocarbon using Zn-Hg and concentrated HCl?

Explanation: Clemmensen reduction specifically uses zinc amalgam and HCl to reduce aldehydes and ketones to alkanes.

Which of the following alkyl halides reacts exclusively via the SN1 mechanism?

Solution: Tertiary butyl bromide ((CH3)3CBr) reacts exclusively via SN1 due to steric hindrance and stable tertiary carbocation formation.

Question 2 of 10
What is the primary stereochemical result of an SN1 reaction at a stereogenic center?

Solution: SN1 reactions proceed through a planar carbocation intermediate attacked equally from both faces, yielding a racemic mixture.

Question 3 of 10
Which compound is least reactive in nucleophilic addition reactions of carbonyl compounds?

Solution: Acetone (CH3COCH3) has two electron-donating methyl groups that reduce the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon and cause steric hindrance.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following catalysts is used in Friedel-Crafts alkylation?

Solution: Anhydrous aluminum chloride (AlCl3) is a classic Lewis acid catalyst for generating carbocation electrophiles in Friedel-Crafts alkylation.

Question 5 of 10
Which base favors Hofmann elimination over Zaitsev elimination?

Solution: Potassium tert-butoxide is a bulky base that abstracts the sterically accessible terminal proton, yielding the Hofmann alkene.

Question 6 of 10
Which reactive intermediate has carbon bonded to two groups and possesses two non-bonding electrons?

Solution: Carbenes are neutral reactive intermediates containing a divalent carbon atom with six valence electrons.

Question 7 of 10
In the nitration of benzene, the active electrophile is:

Solution: Reaction between nitric acid and sulfuric acid generates the nitronium ion (NO2+) as the active electrophile.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following solvents decreases the rate of an SN2 reaction?

Solution: Water is a polar protic solvent that hydrogen-bonds strongly to nucleophiles, lowering their reactivity in SN2 pathways.
