Properties of Nitro Compounds

Nitro compounds are organic derivatives containing the functional group -NO2 bonded to a carbon skeleton, exhibiting pronounced polarity due to high electronegativity of oxygen atoms. They participate in diverse reduction reactions and display distinct acid-base behavior stemming from electron-withdrawing mesomeric and inductive effects.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Physical Properties and Polarity

  • Aliphatic nitro compounds are colorless liquids (except nitromethane which is a solid at very low temperatures) with pleasant fruity odors.
  • Aromatic nitro compounds are typically yellow crystalline solids or high-boiling liquids with bitter almond-like odors.
  • Due to the intense −I and −M effects of the −NO2 group, nitroalkanes possess high dipole moments ranging from 3.5 to 3.9 D.
  • Boiling points of nitroalkanes are significantly higher than isomeric alkyl nitrites and corresponding hydrocarbons due to strong electrostatic intermolecular attractions.

Acidity and Tautomerism

  • Primary and secondary nitroalkanes possess α-hydrogen atoms which exhibit acidic character due to the electron-withdrawing nature of the nitro group.
  • The conjugate base of a nitroalkane is stabilized by resonance, delocalizing the negative charge over both oxygen atoms and the nitrogen atom.
  • Nitroalkanes exhibit keto-enol tautomerism, existing predominantly in the true nitro form (pseudo-acid) and converting into the aci-form (isonitro form in the presence of strong bases.
  • Tertiary nitroalkanes lack α-hydrogens and therefore do not show aci-nitro tautomerism or dissolve in aqueous alkali.

Chemical Reduction and Ring Substitutions

  • Reduction of nitrobenzene yields different products depending on the reagent and pH: acidic medium gives aniline, neutral medium gives N-phenylhydroxylamine, and alkaline medium gives hydrazobenzene.
  • Catalytic hydrogenation using Raney Ni or Pt/C reduces nitro groups directly to primary amines without affecting isolated double bonds.
  • The nitro group is a strong meta-directing group and deactivates the aromatic ring toward electrophilic aromatic substitution reactions.
  • Nef reaction converts primary and secondary nitroalkanes into corresponding aldehydes or ketones via their aci-nitro sodium salts upon treatment with mineral acids.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aliphatic nitro compounds with α-hydrogens readily dissolve in aqueous sodium hydroxide.

Observation: Tertiary nitroalkanes do not dissolve in aqueous NaOH. — Tertiary nitroalkanes lack α-hydrogen atoms required to form the soluble aci-nitro salt.

Exception Reduction of nitrobenzene with metals in acidic medium yields primary aromatic amines.

Observation: Reduction with Zn dust and aqueous NaOH yields azobenzene or hydrazobenzene instead of simple amines. — Alkaline reduction proceeds through bimolecular condensation pathways involving nitrosobenzene and N-phenylhydroxylamine intermediates.

Exception Alkyl nitrites (RONO) and nitroalkanes (RNO2) are functional isomers with similar boiling points.

Observation: Alkyl nitrites have significantly lower boiling points than nitroalkanes. — Alkyl nitrites lack the permanent high dipole moment and strong electrostatic intermolecular associations characteristic of the nitro group.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Nitrobenzene is known as Oil of Mirbane and is prepared by nitrating benzene with a nitrating mixture at 310-320 K.
  • The nitro group exhibits both −I effect and −M effect, making it a powerful deactivating group in aromatic substitution.
  • Tertiary nitroalkanes do not react with nitrous acid (HNO2) because they lack α-hydrogen atoms.
  • Reduction of nitrobenzene with Zn/NaOH gives hydrazobenzene, which undergoes rearrangement to benzidine in acidic medium.
  • The boiling points of nitroalkanes are higher than those of corresponding alkyl halides of comparable molecular mass due to dipole-dipole interactions.
  • Aliphatic nitro compounds show aci-nitro tautomerism, where the aci-form is strongly acidic and turns litmus red.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming tertiary nitroalkanes can exhibit tautomerism; remember they lack α-hydrogens.
  • Confusing the reduction products of nitrobenzene in neutral medium with those in acidic medium.
  • Neglecting the role of sodium hydroxide in converting nitroalkanes to their soluble aci-salts.
  • Confusing nitroalkanes (R-NO2) with alkyl nitrites (R-O-N=O) in terms of hydrolysis behavior.

⚡ Quick Revision: Properties of Nitro Compounds

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Preparation and Structure

Nitro compounds contain the C-N bond where nitrogen is sp3 hybridized in nitroalkanes and sp2 in aromatic rings, stabilized by resonance hybrids.

Acid-Base Behavior

Primary and secondary nitroalkanes function as pseudo-acids due to stabilization of the carbanion conjugate base by the electron-withdrawing nitro group.

Reduction Pathways

Selective reduction controls the transformation of nitro groups into amines, hydroxylamines, or azo compounds depending on pH and catalysts.

Synthetic Utility

Nef reaction and condensation with aldehydes (Henry reaction) establish nitro compounds as versatile synthetic intermediates in organic synthesis.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Pseudo-acid (RCH2NO2)
A neutral organic compound that forms a salt with bases through slow tautomeric conversion.
Aci-nitro form (RCH=N(O)OH)
The acidic tautomeric form of nitroalkanes containing an OH group attached to nitrogen.
Oil of Mirbane (C6H5NO2)
Trivial name for nitrobenzene used in perfumery.
Nef Reaction (RCH=NOONa → RCHO)
Conversion of nitroalkanes to carbonyl compounds via aci-salts.
Henry Reaction (Nitroaldol reaction)
Base-catalyzed condensation of nitroalkanes with aldehydes or ketones.
Victor Meyer Test (Differentiation test)
Chemical test to distinguish 1°, 2°, and 3° nitroalkanes based on color reactions.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Nitro group is strongly electron-withdrawing via both −I and −M mechanisms.
  • Primary nitroalkanes give blood-red color in Victor Meyer test.
  • Secondary nitroalkanes give blue color in Victor Meyer test.
  • Tertiary nitroalkanes give no color (remain colorless) in Victor Meyer test.
  • Reduction in neutral medium (Zn/NH4Cl) yields N-phenylhydroxylamine.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nitro compounds exhibit high dipole moments and elevated boiling points.
  • Presence of α-hydrogens confers pseudo-acidity and tautomerism.
  • Reduction pathways depend critically on the nature of the reagents and reaction medium.
  • Nef reaction provides a direct route from nitroalkanes to aldehydes and ketones.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nitroalkanes VS Alkyl Nitrites

Nitroalkanes have direct C-N bonds and are stable to hydrolysis; alkyl nitrites have C-O-N bonds and hydrolyze easily.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following nitroalkanes does not show tautomerism?

Explanation: Tertiary nitroalkanes like (CH3)3CNO2 do not possess α-hydrogen atoms, which are essential for aci-nitro tautomerism.

Question 2 of 19

The reduction of nitrobenzene in neutral medium using Zn dust and NH4Cl yields:

Explanation: Neutral reduction using Zn/NH4Cl stops at the intermediate stage, yielding N-phenylhydroxylamine.

Question 3 of 19

What is the product of the Nef reaction applied to nitroethane?

Explanation: The Nef reaction converts primary nitroalkanes into corresponding aldehydes upon treatment with base followed by acid hydrolysis; nitroethane yields acetaldehyde.

Question 4 of 19

Primary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid (HNO2) to form:

Explanation: Primary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid to form nitrolic acids, which dissolve in NaOH to give blood-red solutions.

Question 5 of 19

The dipole moment of nitroalkanes is unusually high because of:

Explanation: The semi-polar coordinate N-O bond carries significant charge separation, resulting in high dipole moments (3.5 - 3.9 D).

Question 6 of 19

Which reagent is best suited for selective reduction of a nitro group in the presence of an isolated alkene?

Explanation: Fe/HCl or Fe/CH3COOH selectively reduces aromatic nitro groups without hydrogenating aliphatic double bonds.

Question 7 of 19

Secondary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid to form:

Explanation: Secondary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid to form pseudrols (nitroso-nitro derivatives) which dissolve in NaOH to give blue solutions.

Question 8 of 19

Aromatic nitro compounds are generally:

Explanation: Most aromatic nitro compounds are yellow-colored crystalline solids or high-boiling liquids.

Question 9 of 19

What color is observed when secondary nitroalkane treated with HNO2 is made alkaline with NaOH?

Explanation: Pseudrols derived from secondary nitroalkanes give a characteristic blue color in alkaline solution.

Question 10 of 19

The boiling points of nitroalkanes are higher than isomeric nitrites due to:

Explanation: The permanent high dipole moment of nitroalkanes leads to strong intermolecular dipole-dipole interactions.

Question 11 of 19

Which of the following compounds is known as Oil of Mirbane?

Explanation: Nitrobenzene possesses an odor similar to bitter almonds and is commercially known as Oil of Mirbane.

Question 12 of 19

In alkaline medium, reduction of nitrobenzene with zinc and sodium hydroxide yields:

Explanation: Alkaline reduction of nitrobenzene produces hydrazobenzene through bimolecular condensation steps.

Question 13 of 19

Tertiary nitroalkanes give what color result in the Victor Meyer test?

Explanation: Tertiary nitroalkanes do not react with nitrous acid because they lack alpha-hydrogens, thus remaining colorless.

Question 14 of 19

The aci-form of nitroalkanes differs from the nitro form by having:

Explanation: The aci-form (isonitro form) contains a hydroxyl group bonded to nitrogen (RCH=N(O)OH).

Question 15 of 19

Which effect predominates when the nitro group is attached to a benzene ring?

Explanation: The nitro group exerts both negative inductive (-I) and negative mesomeric (-M) effects.

Question 16 of 19

What is formed when nitromethane is treated with excess formaldehyde in the presence of a base?

Explanation: Nitromethane condenses with three moles of formaldehyde via the Henry reaction to yield tris(hydroxymethyl)nitromethane.

Question 17 of 19

Alkyl nitrites can be distinguished from nitroalkanes by reaction with:

Explanation: Alkyl nitrites undergo easy acid-catalyzed hydrolysis to alcohols and nitrous acid, whereas nitroalkanes are resistant to dilute acid hydrolysis.

Question 18 of 19

What is the hybridization of nitrogen in the nitro group of nitroalkanes?

Explanation: Nitrogen in the nitro group forms three sigma bonds and one pi system, making it sp2 hybridized.

Question 19 of 19

Reduction of nitrobenzene with Sn and concentrated HCl gives:

Explanation: Sn/HCl reduction in acidic medium yields aniline, which forms aniline hydrochloride under acidic conditions.

Question 1 of 10
What is the principal reason for the acidity of alpha-hydrogens in nitroalkanes?

Solution: The conjugate base (carbanion/aci-anion) is stabilized by delocalization of negative charge over the electronegative oxygen atoms of the nitro group.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following describes the correct Victor Meyer color sequence for 1°, 2°, and 3° nitroalkanes?

Solution: Primary nitroalkanes give red, secondary give blue, and tertiary give no color (colorless).

Question 3 of 10
Alkyl nitrites and nitroalkanes are examples of:

Solution: They possess the same molecular formula but different functional groups (R-NO2 vs R-O-N=O).

Question 4 of 10
Which chemical transformation represents the Nef reaction?

Solution: The Nef reaction is the conversion of nitroalkanes into aldehydes or ketones via aci-nitro salts.

Question 5 of 10
What is the state and appearance of nitromethane at room temperature?

Solution: Nitromethane is a polar, colorless liquid at room temperature.

Question 6 of 10
What happens when nitrobenzene is treated with Br2 in the presence of FeBr3?

Solution: The nitro group is meta-directing, so bromination yields meta-bromonitrobenzene as the major product.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following reagents converts nitrobenzene directly to azobenzene?

Solution: Reduction of nitrobenzene with zinc dust and sodium hydroxide yields azobenzene.

Question 8 of 10
Why do tertiary nitroalkanes fail to dissolve in aqueous NaOH?

Solution: Dissolution in alkali requires abstraction of an alpha-hydrogen to form the soluble aci-salt.

Question 9 of 10
In the reduction of nitrobenzene to aniline using Sn/HCl, the active reducing agent is:

Solution: The reaction between tin and hydrochloric acid generates nascent hydrogen which reduces the nitro group.

Question 10 of 10
Which nitro compound has the highest boiling point among C3H7NO2 isomers?

Solution: 1-Nitropropane has a more linear structure and higher surface area leading to stronger intermolecular dipole interactions than branched isomers.
