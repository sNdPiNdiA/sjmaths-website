Alpha-Hydrogen Reactions of Nitro Compounds

Aliphatic nitro compounds containing at least one alpha-hydrogen exhibit acidic character due to strong electron-withdrawing inductive and resonance effects of the nitro group. This acidity allows them to undergo pseudo-nitromer tautomerism, halogenation, and Henry aldol-type condensation reactions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Acidity of Alpha-Hydrogens in Nitroalkanes

  • The α-hydrogens of primary and secondary nitroalkanes exhibit significant acidity due to the stabilization of the resulting carbanion by the electron-withdrawing -I and -R effects of the -NO2 group.
  • Primary nitroalkanes (e.g., nitroethane) possess two α-hydrogens, secondary nitroalkanes possess one, and tertiary nitroalkanes lack α-hydrogens, rendering them inert to pseudo-nitromer tautomerism and related condensations.
  • The conjugate base formed by proton abstraction is a resonance-stabilized nitronate ion (aci-nitro anion), delocalizing negative charge over both oxygen atoms.
  • Nitroalkanes function as pseudo-acids because their ionization occurs measurably slower than typical mineral acids due to structural reorganization during tautomerization.

Tautomerism and Halogenation Reactions

  • Nitroalkanes exhibit keto-enol type tautomerism known as nitro-aci tautomerism, yielding a relatively stable acidic form containing an oxime-like hydroxyl group (RCH=N+(O-)OH).
  • Reaction of primary and secondary nitroalkanes with halogens (Cl2, Br2, or I2) in the presence of a base yields halogenated nitroalkanes.
  • Primary nitroalkanes react with excess halogen and base to form trihalonitromethanes (such as chloropicrin, CCl3NO2), which act as strong tear gases and fumigants.
  • The halogenation proceeds via rapid formation of the stabilized nitronate carbanion, followed by electrophilic attack by the halogen molecule.

Henry Reaction and Condensations

  • The Henry reaction (nitro-aldol reaction) involves the base-catalyzed nucleophilic addition of an α-hydrogen containing nitroalkane to aldehydes or ketones to form β-nitro alcohols.
  • Subsequent dehydration of β-nitro alcohols yields nitroalkenes, which serve as versatile synthetic intermediates for amines, oximes, and carbonyl compounds via the Nef reaction.
  • Formaldehyde reacts with primary nitroalkanes in the presence of excess base to undergo sequential hydroxymethylation via aldol-type additions until all α-hydrogens are substituted.
  • Tertiary nitroalkanes fail to undergo the Henry reaction or halogenation due to the complete absence of ionizable α-hydrogens.

aci-Nitro Tautomerism Equilibrium
RCH2NO2 ⇌ RCH=N(OH)(O)
Conditions: Base or acid catalysis in polar solvents
Units: Equilibrium constant KT is dimensionless
Henry Reaction (Nitro-Aldol)
RCH2NO2 + R'CHO → R'CH(OH)-CH(R)NO2
Conditions: Catalytic amounts of base (NaOH, KOH, or amine)
Units: Stoichiometric reaction yield in percentage
Halogenation of Nitroalkanes
RCH2NO2 + 3X2 + 3NaOH → RC(X)3NO2 + 3NaX + 3H2O
Conditions: Presence of excess halogen and strong base
Units: Molar stoichiometry 1:3 for nitroalkane to halogen
Pseudo-acid Dissociation Rate
v = k1[RCH2NO2][OH-] - k-1[RCH=NO2-][H2O]
Conditions: Base-catalyzed proton abstraction step
Units: Rate in mol L-1 s-1
Acidity Constant of Nitroalkanes
Ka = [RCH=NO2-][H+] / [RCH2NO2]
Conditions: Aqueous solution at 298 K
Units: Ka in mol L-1, pKa is dimensionless
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aldehydes and ketones undergo standard self-aldol condensation under basic conditions.

Observation: Nitroalkanes undergo Henry condensation and nitronate salt formation instead of standard self-aldol condensation. — The strongly electronegative nitro group enhances α-proton acidity far beyond typical carbonyl α-hydrogens, favoring stable nitronate salt formation.

Exception All nitroalkanes should react with nitrous acid (HNO2) identically based on alkyl substitution.

Observation: Tertiary nitroalkanes do not react with nitrous acid, primary nitroalkanes yield red-colored aci-nitro nitrolic acids, and secondary nitroalkanes yield blue pseudrols. — The reaction mechanism requires the presence of an α-hydrogen to eliminate water and form nitrolic acids or pseudrols.

Exception Compounds without α-hydrogens cannot exhibit tautomerism or condensation reactions.

Observation: Nitromethane (CH3NO2) with three α-hydrogens can undergo condensation with formaldehyde up to three times to form tris(hydroxymethyl)nitromethane. — The accumulation of electron-withdrawing hydroxymethyl groups increases the acidity of remaining α-hydrogens sequentially.

Application: Remember that nitromethane consumes exactly 3 moles of halogen and base to substitute all three α-hydrogens, analogous to the haloform reaction.

Nitrous Acid Color Test Mnemonic

Application: Use the 'PR-SB-TN' rule (Primary-Red, Secondary-Blue, Tertiary-None) for rapid identification in analytical organic chemistry questions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Primary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid to form nitrolic acids which dissolve in NaOH to give red solutions.
  • Secondary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid to form pseudrols which dissolve in NaOH to give blue solutions.
  • Tertiary nitroalkanes do not react with nitrous acid due to the absence of alpha-hydrogens.
  • Chloropicric (CCl3NO2) is prepared by the action of chlorine on nitromethane in the presence of sodium hydroxide.
  • The acidity of nitroalkanes is attributed to the electron-withdrawing inductive (-I) and resonance (-R) effects of the nitro group.
  • Nitroalkanes behave as pseudo-acids because their rate of proton abstraction is slower than instantaneous mineral acid dissociation.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing tertiary nitroalkanes with tertiary alcohols; tertiary nitroalkanes completely lack α-hydrogens and do not undergo base-catalyzed reactions.
  • Forgetting that nitromethane has three reactive α-hydrogens and can react with three equivalents of benzaldehyde or formaldehyde.
  • Assuming nitro-aci tautomerism requires extreme heat; it readily occurs in dilute aqueous alkali at room temperature.
  • Misidentifying chloropicrin as trichloromethane instead of trichloronitromethane (CCl3NO2).

⚡ Quick Revision: Alpha-Hydrogen Reactions of Nitro Compounds

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Acidity and Resonance Stabilization

Alpha-hydrogens in nitroalkanes are acidic because the conjugate base (nitronate ion) delocalizes negative charge across the nitro group oxygens via resonance.

Pseudo-Nitromer Tautomerism

Primary and secondary nitroalkanes tautomerize from the nitro form (RCH2NO2) to the more acidic aci-nitro form (RCH=N+(O-)OH).

Henry Reaction (Nitro-Aldol)

Base-catalyzed nucleophilic addition of nitroalkanes to carbonyl compounds yields beta-nitro alcohols, which dehydrate to form useful nitroalkene derivatives.

Halogenation and Nitrolic Acids

Alpha-hydrogen substitution with halogens in basic media produces halo-nitroalkanes, while reaction with nitrous acid distinguishes primary, secondary, and tertiary nitroalkanes.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Pseudo-acid (RCH2NO2)
A compound that ionizes measurably slower than typical mineral acids due to structural tautomerism.
Nitronate Ion (RCH=NO2-)
Resonance-stabilized conjugate base formed upon alpha-hydrogen abstraction.
Henry Reaction (Nitro-aldol addition)
Base-catalyzed addition of nitroalkanes to aldehydes or ketones forming beta-nitro alcohols.
Chloropicrin (CCl3NO2)
Trihalonitromethane produced by exhaustive halogenation of nitromethane.
Nitrolic Acid (RC(=NOH)NO2)
Red-colored product formed from primary nitroalkanes and nitrous acid.
Pseudrol (R2C(NO2)(NO))
Blue-colored product formed from secondary nitroalkanes and nitrous acid.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Tertiary nitroalkanes lack alpha-hydrogens and show no reaction with bases or nitrous acid.
  • Primary nitroalkanes give red nitrolic acid test with HNO2.
  • Secondary nitroalkanes give blue pseudrol test with HNO2.
  • Nitromethane reacts with 3 moles of Br2/NaOH to give tribromonitromethane.
  • Henry reaction yields beta-nitro alcohols in the presence of base catalysts.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alpha-hydrogens in nitro compounds are acidic due to -I and -R effects of -NO2.
  • Nitronate anions exhibit strong resonance stabilization.
  • Tautomerism produces aci-nitro forms which react readily with electrophiles.
  • Halogenation and condensation reactions are restricted to primary and secondary nitroalkanes.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nitrolic Acid VS Pseudrol

Nitrolic acid is formed from primary nitroalkanes (red in NaOH), whereas pseudrol is formed from secondary nitroalkanes (blue in NaOH).

Primary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid (HNO2) to form:

Explanation: Primary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid to yield nitrolic acids, which dissolve in aqueous NaOH to form red-colored solutions.

Question 3 of 19

What is the product formed when nitromethane reacts with excess chlorine in the presence of sodium hydroxide?

The Henry reaction is an example of which type of organic reaction?

Explanation: The Henry reaction involves the nucleophilic addition of a nitronate carbanion to the carbonyl carbon of an aldehyde or ketone.

Question 5 of 19

Secondary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid to form compounds that dissolve in alkali to give which color?

Explanation: Secondary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid to form pseudrols, which yield a characteristic blue coloration in alkaline solution.

Question 6 of 19

Why do nitroalkanes behave as pseudo-acids?

Explanation: Nitroalkanes are termed pseudo-acids because proton abstraction is coupled with a relatively slow structural conversion from nitro to aci-form.

Question 7 of 19

Which of the following compounds will give a positive test with sodium hydroxide and iodine (iodoform test)?

Explanation: 1-Nitroethane contains a CH3-CH(NO2)- group which reacts with halogen and base to yield haloform-type cleavage.

Question 8 of 19

How many alpha-hydrogens are present in 2-methyl-2-nitropropane?

Explanation: The central carbon bonded to the nitro group is attached to three methyl groups and possesses zero hydrogen atoms.

Question 9 of 19

The conjugate base of a nitroalkane is called a:

Explanation: Deprotonation of the alpha-hydrogen of a nitroalkane produces a resonance-stabilized nitronate ion (aci-nitro anion).

Question 10 of 19

Which catalyst is typically employed in the Henry reaction?

Explanation: A base catalyst is required to abstract the acidic alpha-hydrogen and generate the active nitronate nucleophile.

Question 11 of 19

Tertiary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid to yield:

Explanation: Due to the total lack of alpha-hydrogens, tertiary nitroalkanes cannot react with nitrous acid.

Question 12 of 19

What is the intermediate formed during the base-catalyzed bromination of nitroethane?

Explanation: The base abstracts an alpha-proton to generate a stabilized nitronate carbanion, which then attacks bromine.

Question 13 of 19

Which group exerts both -I and -R effects that increase alpha-hydrogen acidity?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is strongly electron-withdrawing via both inductive (-I) and resonance (-R) mechanisms.

Question 14 of 19

Dehydration of beta-nitro alcohols obtained from the Henry reaction yields:

Explanation: Beta-nitro alcohols readily undergo elimination of water under appropriate conditions to form conjugated nitroalkenes.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following has the most acidic alpha-hydrogens?

Explanation: The strong electron-withdrawing nitro group makes the alpha-hydrogens of nitroethane significantly more acidic than those of aldehydes or alcohols.

Question 16 of 19

In nitro-aci tautomerism, the aci-form contains which functional group combination?

Explanation: The aci-tautomer possesses an acidic hydroxyl group attached to nitrogen bonded to oxygen (RCH=N+(O-)OH).

Question 17 of 19

Nitromethane can react with a maximum of how many molecules of formaldehyde in the presence of base?

Explanation: Nitromethane possesses three alpha-hydrogens and can undergo hydroxymethylation three times to yield tris(hydroxymethyl)nitromethane.

Question 18 of 19

What happens when nitroethane is treated with aqueous sodium hydroxide?

Explanation: Primary and secondary nitroalkanes dissolve in aqueous sodium hydroxide by forming water-soluble sodium nitronate salts.

Question 19 of 19

Which test is used to distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary nitroalkanes?

Explanation: The nitrous acid (HNO2) test gives distinctive red (primary), blue (secondary), and no reaction (tertiary) results.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following nitroalkanes is incapable of undergoing the Henry reaction?

Solution: Tertiary nitroalkanes lack alpha-hydrogens required to form the nitronate nucleophile necessary for the Henry reaction.

Question 2 of 10
The product formed when nitroethane is treated with nitrous acid and subsequently with NaOH is:

Solution: Nitroethane is a primary nitroalkane; its reaction with nitrous acid yields nitrolic acid, which dissolves in NaOH to form a red solution.

Question 3 of 10
In the tautomeric equilibrium between nitro and aci-forms, which form is generally present in a higher percentage in pure liquid aliphatic nitro compounds?

Solution: The nitro form is thermodynamically much more stable and predominates overwhelmingly in pure liquid nitroalkanes at equilibrium.

Question 4 of 10
What is the hybridization of the carbon atom bearing the nitro group in the nitronate ion?

Solution: Upon deprotonation, the alpha-carbon rehybridizes from sp3 to sp2 to accommodate delocalization into the pi system of the nitro group.

Question 5 of 10
Which reagent converts a primary nitroalkane directly into a carboxylic acid or aldehyde?

Solution: The Nef reaction converts nitroalkanes into corresponding carbonyl compounds using base followed by acid hydrolysis.

Question 6 of 10
How many alpha-hydrogens are present in 2-nitrobutane?

Solution: 2-Nitrobutane is a secondary nitroalkane with one alpha-hydrogen on C-2 and two on C-3, totaling 3 alpha-hydrogens.

Question 7 of 10
Which nitro compound gives no reaction with aqueous sodium hydroxide?

Solution: 2-Methyl-2-nitropropane is a tertiary nitroalkane lacking acidic alpha-hydrogens, hence it does not form water-soluble nitronate salts with NaOH.

Question 8 of 10
The conversion of nitroethane into chloropicrin requires how many moles of chlorine per mole of nitroethane?

Solution: Nitroethane has three alpha-hydrogens, requiring 3 moles of halogen (Cl2) for complete substitution.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following best explains why nitroalkanes are pseudo-acids?

Solution: Pseudo-acids ionize slowly because proton abstraction is coupled with a slow structural rearrangement to the aci-form.

Question 10 of 10
In the reaction of secondary nitroalkanes with nitrous acid, the intermediate compound that produces the blue color is a:

Solution: Secondary nitroalkanes react with HNO2 to form pseudrols (gem-nitro nitroso compounds), which impart a blue color in alkaline solution.
