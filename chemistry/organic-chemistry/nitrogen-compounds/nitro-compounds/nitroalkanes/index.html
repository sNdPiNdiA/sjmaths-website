Nitroalkanes

Nitroalkanes are organic compounds containing the functional group -NO2 attached to an alkyl carbon. They exhibit pronounced tautomerism and strong carbon acidity due to the electron-withdrawing inductive and resonance effects of the nitro group.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation Methods of Nitroalkanes

  • Primary and secondary nitroalkanes are synthesized by reacting alkyl halides with silver nitrite (AgNO2) in absolute ethanol, proceeding via an SN2 mechanism.
  • Vapor phase nitration of lower alkanes with nitric acid at 400-475 °C yields a complex mixture of nitroalkanes due to free radical cleavage of carbon-carbon bonds.
  • Aliphatic nitro compounds are also prepared by the oxidation of oximes or primary amines using trifluoroperacetic acid.

Acidity and Tautomerism

  • Primary and secondary nitroalkanes exhibit pseudo-acid character and undergo keto-enol type tautomerism known as aci-nitro tautomerism (nitro form ⇄ aci-form).
  • The alpha-hydrogens of nitroalkanes are remarkably acidic (pKa ≈ 8-10) because the resulting carbanion is stabilized by the delocalization of negative charge over the two electronegative oxygen atoms of the nitro group.
  • Tertiary nitroalkanes lack alpha-hydrogens and therefore do not exhibit aci-nitro tautomerism or form salts with aqueous alkalies.

Chemical Reactions of Nitroalkanes

  • Primary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid (HNO2) to form nitrolic acids, which dissolve in aqueous NaOH to yield a characteristic blood-red coloration.
  • Secondary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid to form pseudonitroles, which yield a blue color upon dissolution in alkaline medium, while tertiary nitroalkanes do not react.
  • Catalytic hydrogenation or reduction of nitroalkanes using Fe/HCl or Zn/HCl converts them cleanly into corresponding primary aliphatic amines.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl halides reacting with alkali metal nitrites (NaNO2/KNO2) predominantly yield alkyl nitrites (RONO).

Observation: Reaction with silver nitrite (AgNO2) yields nitroalkanes (RNO2) as the major product. — AgNO2 is predominantly covalent, making the unshared electron pair on nitrogen a better nucleophile than oxygen in polar aprotic/alcoholic media.

Exception Nitro groups are generally meta-directing and strongly deactivating in electrophilic aromatic substitution of benzene rings.

Observation: Aliphatic nitro compounds do not undergo classical electrophilic aromatic substitution, but their alpha-carbons readily act as carbon nucleophiles in basic conditions. — The strong -I and -M effects of the aliphatic nitro group polarize the adjacent C-H bond, facilitating proton abstraction rather than ring substitution.

Exception Carbonyl compounds are typically synthesized by oxidation of alcohols or ozonolysis of alkenes.

Observation: Nitroalkanes provide an alternative route to carbonyl compounds via the Nef reaction using strong acids. — Protonation of the aci-nitro salt leads to an iminium-like species that rapidly hydrolyzes to aldehydes or ketones.

Alpha-Hydrogen Count Rule for Tautomerism

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Silver nitrite (AgNO2) reacts with alkyl halides to form nitroalkanes via a nucleophilic substitution mechanism.
  • Primary and secondary nitroalkanes are soluble in aqueous NaOH due to the formation of water-soluble aci-nitro salts.
  • Tertiary nitroalkanes lack alpha-hydrogens, making them unreactive toward nitrous acid in the Victor Meyer test.
  • The Henry reaction involves the condensation of nitroalkanes with aldehydes or ketones in the presence of a base.
  • Reduction of nitroalkanes with Sn/HCl or Fe/HCl yields primary aliphatic amines.
  • The Nef reaction converts primary and secondary nitroalkanes into aldehydes and ketones respectively, using concentrated mineral acids.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the products of NaNO2 (alkyl nitrites) and AgNO2 (nitroalkanes) with alkyl halides.
  • Assuming tertiary nitroalkanes can form salts with aqueous sodium hydroxide despite lacking alpha-hydrogens.
  • Forgetting that aci-nitro tautomers require at least one alpha-hydrogen adjacent to the nitro functionality.
  • Incorrectly identifying the Nef reaction as an oxidation process instead of a hydrolysis of an aci-nitro salt.

⚡ Quick Revision: Nitroalkanes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Synthesis and Structure

Nitroalkanes possess a highly polar -NO2 group bonded to an sp3 hybridized carbon atom. They are prepared via nucleophilic displacement using silver nitrite or via vapor-phase thermal nitration.

Acidity and Tautomerism

The presence of electron-withdrawing nitro groups confers significant acidity to alpha-hydrogens. This enables rapid tautomerization between the nitro (CH-NO2) and aci-nitro (C=N(OH)O) forms.

Name Reactions

Key transformations include the Henry reaction for carbon-carbon bond formation and the Nef reaction for converting nitroalkanes into carbonyl compounds.

Analytical Distinction

Victor Meyer's test provides a reliable chemical method to differentiate between primary, secondary, and tertiary nitroalkanes through distinct color reactions with nitrous acid and alkali.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Nitroalkane (R-NO2)
Aliphatic compound containing a nitro functional group.
Aci-Nitro Form (R-CH=NO(OH))
Acidic tautomer of primary and secondary nitroalkanes.
Nef Reaction (Acid Hydrolysis)
Conversion of nitroalkanes to carbonyl compounds via aci-salt.
Henry Reaction (Nitroaldol Addition)
Base-catalyzed addition of nitroalkane to a carbonyl compound.
Pseudonitrole (R2C(NO)(NO2))
Product formed from secondary nitroalkanes with nitrous acid.
Nitrolic Acid (RC(=NOH)(NO2))
Product formed from primary nitroalkanes with nitrous acid.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • AgNO2 + R-X → R-NO2 + AgX
  • Primary nitro + HNO2 → Nitrolic acid → Blood red in NaOH
  • Secondary nitro + HNO2 → Pseudonitrole → Blue in NaOH
  • Tertiary nitro + HNO2 → No reaction (Colorless)
  • Nef reaction converts RCH2NO2 to RCHO

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nitroalkanes show strong carbon acidity due to resonance stabilization of the conjugate base.
  • Tautomerism is restricted to primary and secondary nitroalkanes containing alpha-hydrogens.
  • Reduction gives primary amines, offering a direct synthetic route.
  • Analytical distinction is achieved via Victor Meyer and nitrous acid tests.

⚖️ Key Differences

Alkyl Nitrite (RONO) VS Nitroalkane (RNO2)

Alkyl nitrites contain an R-O-N=O linkage and are formed with NaNO2, whereas nitroalkanes contain a C-N bond and are formed with AgNO2.

Why are alpha-hydrogens of nitroalkanes unusually acidic?

Explanation: The electron-withdrawing inductive and resonance effects of the nitro group stabilize the resulting carbanion (aci-nitro anion).

Question 12 of 19

Explanation: The Henry reaction requires a base (such as NaOH, KOH, or an amine) to abstract an alpha-proton from the nitroalkane and generate the nucleophile.

Question 13 of 19

Explanation: Hydrolysis of nitromethane with strong acid yields formic acid and hydroxylamine.

Question 14 of 19

Explanation: Vapor phase nitration at high temperatures involves free radical C-C bond cleavage, yielding a complex mixture of various nitroalkanes.

Question 15 of 19

The C-N-O bond angle in the nitro group is approximately:

Explanation: The nitrogen atom in the nitro group is sp2 hybridized with a trigonal planar geometry, yielding bond angles close to 120°.

Question 16 of 19

Which alkyl halide gives maximum yield of nitroalkane with AgNO2?

Explanation: Primary alkyl halides undergo SN2 substitution smoothly with AgNO2 to produce high yields of nitroalkanes.

Question 17 of 19

Which compound gives a blue color in the Victor Meyer test?

Explanation: Secondary nitroalkanes (like 2-nitropropane) react with HNO2 to form pseudonitroles, which give a blue color in NaOH.

Question 18 of 19

What functional groups are present in an aci-nitro tautomer?

Explanation: The aci-nitro tautomer contains a carbon-nitrogen double bond with a hydroxyl group attached to the nitrogen atom (-C=N(OH)O).

Explanation: All three alpha-hydrogens of nitromethane are replaced by bromine atoms in basic medium to form bromopicrin (CBr3NO2).
