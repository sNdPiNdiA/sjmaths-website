Organic Chemistry

Preparation of Nitro Compounds

Nitro compounds are organic derivatives containing the functional group -NO2 bonded to a carbon atom. They are synthesized primarily via electrophilic substitution, nucleophilic displacement, and oxidation methods.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Nitration of Aliphatic Hydrocarbons

  • Aliphatic hydrocarbons undergo vapor phase nitration at 400-475 °C with nitric acid vapor.
  • The mechanism proceeds through free-radical intermediates, leading to C-C bond cleavage and a mixture of lower nitroalkanes.
  • Tertiary alkanes react faster than secondary, which react faster than primary alkanes due to radical stability.

Nucleophilic Substitution of Alkyl Halides

  • Reaction of alkyl halides with potassium nitrite (KNO2) yields mainly alkyl nitrites (R-O-N=O) due to ionic bonding.
  • Reaction with silver nitrite (AgNO2) yields mainly nitroalkanes (R-NO2) due to the covalent character of Ag-O bond.
  • Primary and secondary alkyl halides yield corresponding nitroalkanes via an SN2 mechanism.

Electrophilic Aromatic Nitration

  • Benzenoid rings react with a nitrating mixture containing H2SO4 and HNO3 to produce nitroarenes.
  • Sulfuric acid protonates nitric acid to generate the active electrophile, the nitronium ion (NO2+).
  • The rate-determining step is the formation of the resonance-stabilized arenium ion (sigma complex).
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Nucleophilic Substitution (Kolbe Nitration)
R-X + AgNO2 → R-NO2 + AgX
Conditions: Alkyl halide with silver nitrite in polar aprotic solvent
Units: R-X in moles, major product is nitroalkane due to covalent nature of AgNO2
Aromatic Nitration
Ar-H + HNO3 + H2SO4 → Ar-NO2 + H2O
Conditions: Concentrated HNO3 and H2SO4 at 50-60 °C
Units: Electrophilic substitution generating the nitronium ion (NO2+)
Oxidation of Primary Amines
R-CH2-NH2 + 2[O] → R-CH2-NO2
Conditions: Using Caro's acid (H2SO5) or trifluoroperacetic acid
Units: Converts primary aliphatic amines directly to nitroalkanes
Vapor Phase Nitration of Alkanes
R-H + HNO3 (g) → R-NO2 + H2O
Conditions: High temperature (400-475 °C)
Units: Temperature in °C, Yields a complex mixture of nitroalkanes via free-radical mechanism
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl halides with NaNO2 or KNO2 form nitroalkanes.

Observation: Alkyl nitrites (R-O-N=O) are formed as major products. — KNO2 is ionic and provides ambient nitrite ion acting as an ambident nucleophile attacking via oxygen.

Exception Nitration of aniline gives ortho and para nitroanilines directly.

Observation: Direct nitration yields a large amount of m-nitroaniline along with tarry oxidation products. — Strongly acidic medium protonates the amino group to form the meta-directing anilinium ion (-NH3+).

Exception Direct iodination of benzene requires an oxidizing agent.

Observation: Direct nitration proceeds readily without any external catalyst for activated rings like phenol. — The nitronium ion is a very strong electrophile capable of attacking activated aromatic systems spontaneously.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Silver vs Potassium Nitrite Mnemonic
Ag = Nitro (Covalent), K = Nitrite (Ionic)

Application: Use AgNO2 when aiming for carbon-nitrogen bond formation (nitroalkane), and KNO2 for oxygen-nitrogen bond (alkyl nitrite).

Nitrating Mixture Generation
HNO3 + 2H2SO4 → NO2+ + H3O+ + 2HSO4-

Application: Remember that sulfuric acid acts as a stronger acid than nitric acid, protonating it to release the NO2+ electrophile.

Protection of Amino Group in Nitration
Aniline → Acetanilide → p-Nitroacetanilide → p-Nitroaniline

Application: To avoid oxidation and meta-directing salt formation during aniline nitration, first acetylate the amino group.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Silver nitrite reacts with haloalkanes to form nitroalkanes due to covalent Ag-O bond character.
  • The electrophile in aromatic nitration is the nitronium ion (NO2+).
  • Vapor phase nitration of propane yields 1-nitropropane, 2-nitropropane, nitroethane, and nitromethane.
  • Oxidation of tertiary alkyl amines with KMnO4 yields tertiary nitro compounds.
  • Direct nitration of aniline is preceded by acetylation to prevent oxidation and meta-substitution.
  • Chloropicrin (trichloronitromethane) is prepared by the action of nitric acid on chloral or steam distillation of picric acid with bleaching powder.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the products of KNO2 (alkyl nitrite) and AgNO2 (nitroalkane) reaction with alkyl halides.
  • Assuming direct nitration of aniline yields high yields of p-nitroaniline without protection.
  • Neglecting the formation of cleavage products during high-temperature vapor phase nitration of higher alkanes.
  • Forgetting that H2SO4 acts as an acid in the nitrating mixture, not a dehydrating agent alone.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation of Nitro Compounds

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Nucleophilic Substitution Route

Alkyl halides react with silver nitrite in polar aprotic solvents to yield nitroalkanes via an SN2 pathway.

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution

Arene rings react with a mixture of concentrated nitric and sulfuric acids generating the NO2+ electrophile.

Vapor Phase Nitration

Alkanes react with nitric acid vapor at elevated temperatures through homolytic cleavage of carbon-hydrogen and carbon-carbon bonds.

Oxidation Methods

Primary aliphatic amines are oxidized using peroxy acids or Caro's acid to directly yield primary nitroalkanes.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Nitration (-NO2 introduction)
Process of introducing a nitro group into an organic molecule.
Nitronium Ion (NO2+)
Electrophile active in aromatic electrophilic nitration.
Ambident Nucleophile (NO2-)
Nucleophile that can react through two different atoms (N or O).
Chloropicrin (CCl3NO2)
Trichloronitromethane used as an insecticide and tear gas.
Acetanilide (C6H5NHCOCH3)
Protected derivative of aniline used to direct ortho/para nitration.
Arenium Ion (σ-complex)
Resonance-stabilized carbocation intermediate in electrophilic aromatic substitution.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • AgNO2 gives nitroalkane; KNO2 gives alkyl nitrite.
  • Nitrating mixture consists of conc. HNO3 + conc. H2SO4.
  • Vapor phase nitration occurs at 400-475 °C via free radicals.
  • Aniline requires acetylation prior to nitration to prevent tar formation.
  • Chloropicrin is formed by nitration of chloroform or chloral.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nitro compounds are synthesized via substitution, electrophilic addition, and oxidation.
  • Reagents dictate whether a true nitro compound or an alkyl nitrite forms.
  • Aromatic systems require generation of the nitronium ion via acid catalysis.
  • Aliphatic systems require high energy or radical conditions for nitration.

⚖️ Key Differences

Alkyl Nitrite VS Nitroalkane

Alkyl nitrites contain R-O-N=O linkages whereas nitroalkanes contain R-C-NO2 linkages.

Which reagent is used to convert an alkyl halide into a nitroalkane predominantly?

Explanation: Silver nitrite (AgNO2) is covalent, favoring attack through nitrogen to yield nitroalkanes.

Question 2 of 19

What is the active electrophile in the nitration of benzene using a nitrating mixture?

Explanation: Concentrated sulfuric acid protonates nitric acid, losing water to form the nitronium ion (NO2+).

Question 3 of 19

Vapor phase nitration of propane with nitric acid vapor at 400 °C yields:

Explanation: High temperature vapor phase nitration proceeds via free radicals, causing C-C bond cleavage and producing a mixture of nitro compounds.

Question 4 of 19

Why is aniline acetylated before carrying out its nitration in the laboratory?

Explanation: Acetylation moderates the activating effect of the -NH2 group and prevents the formation of tarry oxidation products and meta-salts.

Question 5 of 19

Reaction of bromoethane with potassium nitrite (KNO2) yields mainly:

Explanation: KNO2 is ionic and provides nitrite ions acting through oxygen, yielding alkyl nitrites as major products.

Question 6 of 19

Which of the following compounds is known as chloropicrin?

Explanation: Trichloronitromethane (CCl3NO2) is commonly known as chloropicrin.

Question 7 of 19

Direct oxidation of primary aliphatic amines to primary nitroalkanes is achieved using:

Explanation: Caro's acid (peroxymonosulfuric acid) oxidizes primary amines directly to nitroalkanes.

Question 8 of 19

In aromatic electrophilic substitution, the rate-determining step involves the formation of:

Explanation: The slow, rate-determining step is the attack of the electrophile forming the resonance-stabilized arenium ion.

Question 9 of 19

What is the role of concentrated sulfuric acid in the nitrating mixture?

Explanation: H2SO4 protonates HNO3, causing the elimination of water and generation of the nitronium ion.

Question 10 of 19

Which alkyl halide undergoes substitution with AgNO2 most readily to form nitroalkanes?

Explanation: Primary alkyl halides undergo SN2 substitution smoothly with AgNO2.

Question 11 of 19

The functional group present in nitro compounds is:

Explanation: Nitro compounds contain the carbon-nitrogen bonded -NO2 group.

Question 12 of 19

Which nitro compound cannot be prepared by direct nitration of the corresponding alkane?

Explanation: Tertiary alkanes undergo extensive decomposition and oxidation during vapor phase nitration rather than clean substitution.

Question 13 of 19

Nitration of toluene occurs at which positions predominantly?

Explanation: The methyl group in toluene is ortho/para-directing and activating towards electrophilic aromatic substitution.

Question 14 of 19

What color is pure nitrobenzene?

Explanation: Nitrobenzene is a pale yellow to yellowish oily liquid with an odor resembling bitter almonds.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following methods yields nitroalkanes from silver salts of fatty acids?

Explanation: Treatment of silver salts of carboxylic acids with bromine and NO2 yields nitroalkanes.

Question 16 of 19

The hybridization of nitrogen in the nitro group (-NO2) is:

Explanation: The central nitrogen atom in a nitro group is sp2 hybridized due to double bonding with one oxygen and coordinate/single bond with another.

Question 17 of 19

Nitromethane can be prepared in the laboratory by heating an aqueous solution of sodium chloroacetate with:

Explanation: Heating sodium chloroacetate with sodium nitrite yields sodium nitroacetate which decarboxylates to give nitromethane.

Question 18 of 19

Which acid is liberated when nitric acid is protonated by sulfuric acid during nitration?

Explanation: HNO3 + 2H2SO4 → NO2+ + H3O+ + 2HSO4-.

Question 19 of 19

What is the chief product when benzene is treated with excess nitric acid and sulfuric acid at high temperature?

Explanation: Further nitration of nitrobenzene under vigorous conditions gives m-dinitrobenzene as the nitro group is meta-directing.
