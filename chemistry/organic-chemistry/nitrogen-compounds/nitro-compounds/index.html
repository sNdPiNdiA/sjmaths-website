Organic Chemistry

Nitro Compounds

Nitro compounds are organic derivatives of hydrocarbons containing one or more nitro groups (-NO2) bonded to carbon atoms. They exhibit strong -I and -R effects due to the electronegativity of oxygen and the positive formal charge on nitrogen.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation Methods of Nitro Compounds

  • Vapour phase nitration of alkanes involves reacting lower alkanes with nitric acid at 400-500°C, yielding a complex mixture of nitroalkanes via free radical mechanisms.
  • Nucleophilic substitution of alkyl halides using silver nitrite (AgNO2) predominantly yields nitroalkanes due to the covalent nature of Ag-O bond.
  • Victor Meyer reaction converts alkyl iodides to primary nitroalkanes via silver nitrite followed by treatment with nitrous acid (HNO2).
  • Aromatic nitration is an electrophilic aromatic substitution utilizing an electrophile, the nitronium ion (NO2+), generated from concentrated HNO3 and H2SO4.

Chemical Properties and Reduction Reactions

  • Reduction in acidic medium (Sn/HCl or Fe/HCl) converts aromatic nitro compounds directly to primary aromatic amines.
  • Reduction in neutral medium (Zn dust and NH4Cl) yields phenylhydroxylamines (C6H5NHOH).
  • Reduction in alkaline medium (such as sodium arsenite or zinc/NaOH) produces bimolecular reduction products including azoxybenzene, azobenzene, and hydrazobenzene.
  • Electrophilic substitution in nitrobenzene occurs exclusively at the meta-position because the -NO2 group is a powerful electron-withdrawing deactivating group (-I and -R effects).

Acidity and Condensation Reactions

  • Primary and secondary nitroalkanes exhibit pronounced C-H acidity because the resulting carbanion is stabilized by resonance with the nitro group.
  • Nitroalkanes possessing α-hydrogens exhibit tautomerism, existing in equilibrium between nitro form and aci-form (nitronic acid).
  • Nef carbonyl synthesis converts primary and secondary nitroalkanes into corresponding aldehydes and ketones respectively upon treatment with base followed by acid hydrolysis.
  • Aldol-type condensation occurs between nitroalkanes containing α-hydrogens and aldehydes in the presence of a base to yield β-nitroalkanols.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl halides react with alkali metal nitrites (NaNO2 or KNO2) to form nitroalkanes.

Observation: Mainly alkyl nitrites (RONO) are formed alongside minor nitroalkanes. — NaNO2 is ionic and furnishes ambident nitrite ion (O=N-O-), which reacts predominantly through the more nucleophilic oxygen atom.

Exception Nitrobenzene undergoes standard Friedel-Crafts alkylation and acylation.

Observation: Friedel-Crafts reaction fails completely for nitrobenzene. — The strongly electron-withdrawing nitro group coordinates with the Lewis acid catalyst (AlCl3), deactivating the aromatic ring.

Exception Primary, secondary, and tertiary nitroalkanes all react with nitrous acid.

Observation: Tertiary nitroalkanes do not react with nitrous acid (HNO2). — Tertiary nitroalkanes lack an α-hydrogen atom required for the formation of nitrolic acids or pseudonitrols.

Victor Meyer Colour Test Mnemonic
1° = Red, 2° = Blue, 3° = Colourless

AgNO2 vs NaNO2 Product Rule
AgNO2 → C-NO2 (Covalent), NaNO2 → C-O-N=O (Ionic)

Application: Predicting major products in nucleophilic substitution of alkyl halides.

meta-Directing Power Rule
All groups with positive charge on atom attached to ring are meta-directing

Application: Instantly identifying directing behavior of -NO2, -NR3+, -CN, and -CHO.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Nitrobenzene is commonly known as oil of mirbane due to its bitter almond-like odor.
  • The dipole moment of nitrobenzene (3.95 D) is remarkably high due to electron displacement toward the oxygen atoms.
  • Reduction of nitrobenzene in neutral medium using Zn/NH4Cl gives N-phenylhydroxylamine.
  • Tertiary nitroalkanes lack α-hydrogen atoms, hence they do not show aci-nitro tautomerism.
  • Electrophilic substitution in nitrobenzene takes place at meta-position with severe deactivation.
  • Silver nitrite favors carbon-alkylation giving nitroalkanes, whereas potassium nitrite favors oxygen-alkylation giving alkyl nitrites.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the products of AgNO2 and NaNO2 reactions with alkyl halides.
  • Assuming tertiary nitroalkanes give colored solutions in the Victor Meyer test.
  • Forgetting that Friedel-Crafts catalysts form inactive complexes with nitroarenes.
  • Confusing aci-nitro tautomerism with keto-enol tautomerism in mechanism details.

⚡ Quick Revision: Nitro Compounds

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Synthesis and Structure

Nitro compounds contain the functional group -NO2 exhibiting resonance stabilization and strong electron-withdrawing inductive and resonance effects. They are synthesized via nitration of alkanes, substitution of alkyl halides, or nitration of aromatic rings.

Acidity and Tautomerism

Primary and secondary nitroalkanes display remarkable C-H acidity and undergo tautomerism to form aci-nitro forms, which react with bases to form stable salts useful in organic synthesis like Nef reaction.

Reduction Pathways

Nitro compounds undergo stepwise reduction depending on the pH of the reaction medium, producing hydroxylamines, azo compounds, hydrazobenzenes, or primary amines.

Aromatic Substitution and Directing Effects

The nitro group is strongly deactivating and meta-directing in electrophilic aromatic substitution reactions, rendering Friedel-Crafts reactions unviable for nitrobenzene.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • AgNO2 gives nitroalkanes; KNO2 gives alkyl nitrites.
  • 1° nitroalkane + HNO2 → Nitrolic acid (Red with NaOH).
  • 2° nitroalkane + HNO2 → Pseudonitrol (Blue with NaOH).
  • 3° nitroalkane + HNO2 → No reaction (Colourless with NaOH).
  • Nitrobenzene + Sn/HCl → Aniline.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nitro group exerts strong -I and -R effects.
  • Primary and secondary nitroalkanes exhibit pseudo-acidity.
  • Reduction pathways depend critically on reaction medium conditions.
  • Aromatic nitro groups strongly deactivate rings toward electrophilic attack.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nitroalkanes VS Alkyl Nitrites

Nitroalkanes have C-N linkage and are stable to alkali; alkyl nitrites have C-O-N linkage and hydrolyze easily.

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is electron-withdrawing via -I and -R effects, directing incoming electrophiles to the meta-position.

What is the hybridization of nitrogen in the nitro group?

Question 3 of 10
In the reduction of nitrobenzene, which intermediate is formed in neutral medium?

Solution: Neutral reduction using Zn/NH4Cl arrests the reduction at the N-phenylhydroxylamine stage.

Question 4 of 10
Which reagent is most suitable for converting nitroalkanes to primary amines?

Solution: Catalytic hydrogenation or metal-acid reduction (Sn/HCl) efficiently reduces nitroalkanes to primary amines.

Question 5 of 10
What is the chief product of the reaction between 2-nitropropane and nitrous acid?

Solution: Secondary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid to form pseudonitrols, which turn blue in alkaline solution.

Question 6 of 10
Why is nitromethane a weaker acid than trinitromethane?

Solution: Trinitromethane has three electron-withdrawing nitro groups that stabilize the carbanion much more effectively than the single nitro group in nitromethane.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following nitro compounds does not undergo the Nef carbonyl reaction?

Solution: Tertiary nitroalkanes lack alpha-hydrogens and cannot form the aci-salt required for the Nef reaction.

Question 8 of 10
Which species acts as the active electrophile in aromatic nitration?

Solution: The nitronium ion (NO2+) generated from HNO3 and H2SO4 is the active electrophile.

Solution: Nitroalkanes (R-NO2) and alkyl nitrites (R-O-N=O) share the same molecular formula and are functional isomers.
