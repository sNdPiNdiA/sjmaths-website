Secondary Nitroalkanes

Secondary nitroalkanes are organic compounds containing a nitro group (-NO2) attached to an sp3 hybridized carbon atom that is further bonded to two other carbon atoms. Due to the presence of an alpha-hydrogen atom, secondary nitroalkanes exhibit characteristic tautomerism and acidic properties.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Acid-Base Properties and Tautomerism

  • Secondary nitroalkanes possess one alpha-hydrogen atom, rendering them weakly acidic.
  • Removal of the alpha-proton by a base yields a resonance-stabilized nitronate anion.
  • They exhibit keto-enol type tautomerism, existing predominantly in the true nitro form under neutral conditions.
  • The aci-form (nitronic acid form) is formed upon treatment with aqueous alkali and gives a characteristic coloration with FeCl3.

Chemical Reactions and Characteristic Tests

  • Reaction with nitrous acid (HONO) converts secondary nitroalkanes into pseudo-nitrols.
  • Pseudo-nitrols are typically blue-colored solids or liquids that dissolve in alkali to yield a blue solution.
  • Unlike primary nitroalkanes, secondary nitroalkanes do not react with nitrous acid to form red aci-nitro salts.
  • Reduction of secondary nitroalkanes with catalytic hydrogenation or metal-acid systems yields secondary amines.

Synthetic Utility: The Nef Reaction

  • Conversion of secondary nitroalkanes to corresponding ketones is achieved via the Nef reaction.
  • The reaction proceeds through the generation of a nitronate salt using a strong base followed by acid hydrolysis.
  • Intermediate species include N-alkylidenhydroxylamine derivatives or directly hydrolyzable oxonium species.
  • It serves as a reliable synthetic route for transforming carbon-nitro bonds into carbonyl groups (C=O).

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Nitroalkanes generally act as weak carbon acids due to electron-withdrawing nitro groups.

Observation: Tertiary nitroalkanes lack alpha-hydrogens and show no C-H acidity or tautomerism. — Absence of alpha-hydrogen prevents the formation of the resonance-stabilized nitronate conjugate base.

Exception Primary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid to form red-colored nitrolic acids soluble in NaOH.

Observation: Secondary nitroalkanes yield blue pseudo-nitrols which are neutral or insoluble-to-soluble differently. — Presence of two alkyl groups restricts substitution to a single nitroso group addition instead of forming oximes of nitrolic acids.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Secondary nitroalkanes contain one alpha hydrogen attached to the carbon bearing the nitro group.
  • The Victor Meyer test differentiates secondary nitroalkanes by producing a blue color with nitrous acid.
  • Pseudo-nitrols derived from secondary nitroalkanes are nitroso-nitroalkanes (R2C(NO2)(NO)).
  • Secondary nitroalkanes undergo the Nef reaction to yield ketones upon treatment with acid.
  • Nitronate anions derived from secondary nitroalkanes act as ambident nucleophiles.
  • Reduction of secondary nitroalkanes yields secondary amines via hydroxylamine intermediates.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the Victor Meyer test colors: remember primary is red, secondary is blue.
  • Forgetting that tertiary nitroalkanes do not react with nitrous acid due to the complete lack of alpha hydrogens.
  • Assuming the Nef reaction yields aldehydes instead of ketones from secondary nitroalkanes.
  • Neglecting the role of strong base in generating the nitronate intermediate before acid hydrolysis in the Nef reaction.

⚡ Quick Revision: Secondary Nitroalkanes

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Acidity and Structure

Secondary nitroalkanes are moderately acidic because the conjugate base (nitronate ion) is stabilized by resonance across the nitro group.

Tautomerism

They exhibit tautomerism between the nitro form (R2CHNO2) and the aci-form (R2C=NOOH).

Victor Meyer Differentiation

Reaction with HNO2 followed by alkali converts secondary nitroalkanes into blue pseudo-nitrol solutions.

Nef Carbonyl Synthesis

Acid hydrolysis of secondary nitronate salts successfully converts the nitroalkane functionality into a ketone.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Pseudo-nitrol (R2C(NO2)(NO))
Blue-colored product formed from secondary nitroalkanes and HONO.
Nitronate Anion ([R2C=NO2]−)
Resonance-stabilized conjugate base of a nitroalkane.
Aci-nitro form (R2C=N(OH)(O))
Nitronic acid tautomer of secondary nitroalkanes.
Nef Reaction (Synthesis of Ketones)
Conversion of nitroalkanes to carbonyl compounds via acid hydrolysis of salts.
Victor Meyer Test (Classification Test)
Chemical test using HNO2 and NaOH to distinguish primary, secondary, and tertiary nitroalkanes.
Alpha Hydrogen (C−H on C−NO2)
Acidic hydrogen attached to the carbon bearing the nitro group.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Secondary nitroalkanes have exactly one alpha hydrogen.
  • Pseudo-nitrols are blue in color.
  • Nef reaction converts secondary nitroalkanes to ketones.
  • Nitronate anions are ambident nucleophiles.
  • Tertiary nitroalkanes give no color in the Victor Meyer test.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Secondary nitroalkanes exhibit tautomerism due to alpha hydrogens.
  • They form pseudo-nitrols with nitrous acid.
  • Base treatment yields nitronate salts.
  • Hydrolysis yields corresponding ketones.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nitrolic Acid VS Pseudo-nitrol

Nitrolic acids are formed from primary nitroalkanes (red in base), while pseudo-nitrols are formed from secondary nitroalkanes (blue in base).

Identify the number of alpha-hydrogen atoms in 3-nitropentane.

Solution: 3-Nitropentane is CH3CH2CH(NO2)CH2CH3, where the carbon bearing the nitro group has 2 adjacent CH2 groups, meaning there are 2 alpha-hydrogens total (one on C3).

Secondary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid because they possess:

Question 5 of 10
Which reagent combination converts a secondary nitroalkane into a ketone directly via the Nef reaction?

Solution: The standard Nef reaction conditions involve forming the sodium nitronate salt with NaOH followed by treatment with sulfuric acid.

Question 6 of 10
What is the structural characteristic of a pseudo-nitrol?

Solution: Pseudo-nitrols contain both a nitro group and a nitroso group attached to the same carbon atom (−C(NO)(NO2)−).

Question 7 of 10
Why are secondary nitroalkanes soluble in aqueous sodium hydroxide?

Solution: Deprotonation by NaOH yields ionic sodium nitronate salts which dissolve readily in water.

Question 8 of 10

Solution: The Victor Meyer test produces red solutions for primary and blue solutions for secondary nitroalkanes.

Question 9 of 10

Solution: Keto-enol or nitro-aci tautomerism involves the shift of an alpha hydrogen to an oxygen atom of the nitro group.
