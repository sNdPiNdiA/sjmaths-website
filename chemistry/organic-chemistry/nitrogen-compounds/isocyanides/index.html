Isocyanides

Isocyanides, also known as carbylamines, are organic compounds characterized by the functional group -N+≡C- attached to an alkyl or aryl group. They possess a coordinate covalent bond between nitrogen and carbon, resulting in a divalent carbon atom with a lone pair.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structure, Bonding, and Nomenclature

  • The functional group is represented as -N+≡C- or -NC, displaying ambidentate character.
  • The central carbon is sp-hybridized, containing one unshared lone pair and behaving like a carbene analogue.
  • IUPAC nomenclature designates them as alkane isocyanides or isocyanoalkanes.
  • Due to resonance contributors, the carbon atom carries a formal negative charge and the nitrogen carries a formal positive charge.

Synthetic Routes and Preparative Methods

  • The Carbylamine Test involves treating a primary amine with chloroform and alcoholic potassium hydroxide to yield foul-smelling isocyanides.
  • Reaction of alkyl iodides with silver cyanide (AgCN) predominantly yields isocyanides due to the covalent nature of AgCN.
  • Dehydration of N-substituted formamides using phosphoryl chloride (POCl3) in the presence of pyridine produces isocyanides.
  • Unlike alkyl halides reacting with KCN to form nitriles, reaction with AgCN drives the formation of isocyanides because silver forms a strong ionic precipitate with halides.

Chemical Properties and Reactions

  • Isocyanides undergo hydrolysis in acidic media to form primary amines and formic acid, unlike nitriles which yield carboxylic acids.
  • Reduction with hydrogen and a catalyst yields secondary amines (N-methylalkylamines), distinguishing them from cyanides which yield primary amines.
  • They undergo addition reactions with sulfur and halogens to form isothiocyanates (R-N=C=S) and imidoyl halides, respectively.
  • Isocyanides readily react with transition metals to form stable coordinate complexes due to strong π-backbonding capability.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Reaction of alkyl halides with metal cyanides normally yields nitriles (R-CN).

Observation: Reaction with silver cyanide (AgCN) yields isocyanides (R-NC). — AgCN is predominantly covalent, making the lone pair on nitrogen more nucleophilic than the carbon atom.

Exception Reduction of organic nitrogen compounds generally yields primary amines.

Observation: Catalytic reduction of isocyanides yields secondary amines (methylalkylamines). — The carbon atom of the isocyanide group already bears the alkyl group, and hydrogenation adds methyl-like substitution across the triple bond.

Exception Hydrolysis of acid derivatives yields carboxylic acids and ammonia/amines.

Observation: Acid hydrolysis of isocyanides yields a primary amine salt and formic acid. — The carbon atom of the isocyanide is at a lower oxidation state corresponding to formic acid upon oxidation/hydrolysis.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Isocyanides are also known as carbylamines or isonitriles with the general formula R-N≡C.
  • The carbylamine reaction is a specific test for primary aliphatic and aromatic amines.
  • Alkyl halides react with KCN to form cyanides, whereas reaction with AgCN yields isocyanides.
  • Acid hydrolysis of isocyanides yields primary amines and formic acid.
  • Reduction of isocyanides gives secondary amines containing a methyl group attached to nitrogen.
  • Isocyanides possess a characteristic intensely foul and obnoxious odor.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the reduction products of nitriles (primary amines) with isocyanides (secondary amines).
  • Assuming KCN and AgCN yield the same major product in substitution reactions with alkyl halides.
  • Forgetting that secondary and tertiary amines do not give the carbylamine test.
  • Writing carboxylic acid instead of formic acid as the carbon-containing product during isocyanide hydrolysis.

⚡ Quick Revision: Isocyanides

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

Isocyanides contain a divalent carbon bonded to nitrogen via a coordinate covalent bond, generating significant dipole moments and carbene-like reactivity.

Methods of Preparation

Synthesized primarily via the carbylamine reaction of primary amines with chloroform and base, or through the reaction of alkyl halides with silver cyanide.

Chemical Reactivity

Characterized by susceptibility to acid hydrolysis yielding primary amines and formic acid, and catalytic hydrogenation forming secondary amines.

Analytical Identification

Identified by their remarkably foul stench and positive carbylamine test, making them useful analytical markers for primary amines.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • General formula: R-N≡C
  • Carbylamine test reagent: CHCl3 + alc. KOH
  • AgCN yields isocyanides; KCN yields nitriles
  • Acid hydrolysis gives primary amine + HCOOH
  • Reduction gives secondary amine

📌 Quick Recall

  • Isocyanides are isomers of nitriles with distinct physical and chemical properties.
  • They possess a coordinate bond and exhibit carbene-like chemical behavior.
  • Preparation involves carbylamine reaction or alkyl halide reaction with AgCN.
  • Reactions are dominated by additions at the divalent carbon atom and hydrolysis.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nitrile (R-CN) VS Isocyanide (R-NC)

Nitriles bond through carbon and yield carboxylic acids on hydrolysis; isocyanides bond through nitrogen and yield primary amines.
