Preparation of Isocyanides

Isocyanides, also known as isonitriles or carbylamines, are organic compounds containing the functional group -N+≡C-. Their primary synthetic routes involve the reaction of primary amines with chloroform and base, or the nucleophilic substitution of alkyl halides with silver cyanide.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthesis via Carbylamine Reaction

  • Primary amines react with chloroform and alcoholic potassium hydroxide to yield foul-smelling isocyanides.
  • The reactive intermediate generated in situ is dichlorocarbene (:CCl2) formed via α-elimination from chloroform.
  • Secondary and tertiary amines do not undergo the carbylamine test, making this a specific test for primary amines.
  • The reaction mechanism proceeds via nucleophilic attack of the primary amine on the electrophilic dichlorocarbene.

Nucleophilic Substitution with Silver Cyanide

  • Alkyl halides react with silver cyanide (AgCN) to predominantly form isocyanides (RNC) due to the covalent nature of AgCN.
  • In contrast, the use of ionic potassium cyanide (KCN) yields nitriles (RCN) due to ambidentate cyanide nucleophile attack via the carbon atom.
  • The silver ion acts as a carbocation stabilizer, assisting the removal of halide and directing the attack through the nitrogen atom.
  • Reaction yields are optimal with primary alkyl halides subject to SN2 mechanisms.

Dehydration of N-Formyl Derivatives

  • Primary amines are first converted to formamides by heating with formic acid.
  • Dehydration of the resulting formamide using phosphorus oxychloride (POCl3) and a tertiary amine base yields isocyanides.
  • This method is particularly useful for preparing sterically hindered isocyanides that fail via other routes.
  • Reaction conditions require anhydrous media to prevent hydrolysis of the intermediate formamide and final isocyanide.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Cyanide ion (CN-) acts as an ambident nucleophile attacking via carbon to form nitriles.

Observation: Silver cyanide (AgCN) reacts with alkyl halides to form isocyanides instead of nitriles. — AgCN has high covalent character due to pseudo-noble gas configuration of Ag+, leaving nitrogen's lone pair more reactive for initial attack.

Exception Primary, secondary, and tertiary amines undergo standard alkylation and substitution reactions.

Observation: Only primary amines respond to the carbylamine reaction with CHCl3 and KOH. — Carbene insertion and subsequent base-induced elimination require two replaceable protons on the nitrogen atom.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Carbylamine test is specific for primary amines, both aliphatic and aromatic.
  • The intermediate in the carbylamine reaction is dichlorocarbene (:CCl2).
  • AgCN yields isocyanides because silver has high polarizing power and covalent affinity for halides.
  • Isocyanides hydrolyze in acidic media to give primary amines and formic acid.
  • Reduction of isocyanides with catalytic hydrogenation or sodium/alcohol yields secondary amines (RNHCH3).
  • The IUPAC nomenclature prefix for the -NC group is isonitrile or carbyl.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the products of KCN and AgCN with alkyl halides; KCN gives RCN while AgCN gives RNC.
  • Assuming secondary and tertiary amines give a positive carbylamine test.
  • Forgetting that chloroform acts as the carbon source in the carbylamine reaction.
  • Overlooking the requirement of anhydrous conditions during formamide dehydration with POCl3.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation of Isocyanides

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Carbylamine Synthesis Mechanism

Primary amines react with chloroform in the presence of base via a dichlorocarbene intermediate to form foul-smelling isocyanides.

Ambident Nucleophile Ambiguity

The cyanide ion acts through carbon with ionic salts (KCN) and through nitrogen with covalent silver salts (AgCN).

Formamide Dehydration Route

Formic acid condensation with primary amines followed by POCl3 dehydration provides a clean route to hindered isocyanides.

Chemical Reactivity and Odor

Isocyanides are characterized by an intensely offensive odor, susceptibility to acid hydrolysis, and reduction to secondary methylamines.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Carbylamine (RNC)
Alternative name for isocyanides containing the -N+≡C- group.
Dichlorocarbene (:CCl2)
Electrophilic intermediate generated from chloroform and base.
Ambident Nucleophile ([:C≡N:]-)
Nucleophile that can react through two different atoms (C or N).
Formamide (RNHCHO)
Intermediate formed by treating primary amines with formic acid.
Dehydration (-H2O)
Removal of water molecule using reagents like POCl3.
Isomerism (Functional Isomerism)
Nitriles (RCN) and isocyanides (RNC) are functional isomers of each other.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carbylamine test detects primary amines using CHCl3 + KOH.
  • AgCN produces isocyanides; KCN produces nitriles.
  • Intermediate in carbylamine reaction is :CCl2.
  • Acid hydrolysis of isocyanides yields primary amine and formic acid.
  • Reduction of isocyanides yields secondary N-methylamines.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Isocyanides contain the isonitrile functional group with a coordinate covalent bond.
  • Carbylamine reaction is the premier synthesis for primary amines.
  • Metal salt choice (AgCN vs KCN) dictates nitrogen vs carbon alkylation.
  • Dehydration of formamides provides alternative entry for hindered systems.

⚖️ Key Differences

KCN + Alkyl Halide VS AgCN + Alkyl Halide

KCN gives alkyl cyanide (RCN), whereas AgCN gives alkyl isocyanide (RNC).

Which of the following reagents is used in the carbylamine test?

What is the reactive intermediate in the carbylamine reaction?

Explanation: Chloroform reacts with base via alpha-elimination to generate dichlorocarbene (:CCl2).

Question 3 of 19

Reaction of ethyl chloride with AgCN yields primarily:

Explanation: Silver cyanide is covalent, leading to attack by nitrogen to form ethyl isocyanide.

Question 4 of 19

Which type of amine responds to the carbylamine test?

Explanation: Only primary amines possess two hydrogens on nitrogen necessary to form isocyanides via carbene insertion.

Question 5 of 19

Acid hydrolysis of an isocyanide yields:

Explanation: Hydrolysis of RNC in acidic medium breaks the carbon-nitrogen bond to yield primary amine and formic acid.

Question 6 of 19

What is the product of the reduction of methyl isocyanide with H2/Ni or LiAlH4?

Explanation: Reduction of isocyanides adds hydrogen across the multiple bond to yield secondary amines, specifically N-methylamines (RNHCH3).

Question 7 of 19

Why does AgCN give isocyanides while KCN gives nitriles?

Explanation: The ionic nature of KCN makes the carbon more nucleophilic, whereas the covalent nature of AgCN makes nitrogen more reactive.

Question 8 of 19

Formamides can be dehydrated to isocyanides using which reagent combination?

Explanation: Phosphorus oxychloride in the presence of a tertiary amine base dehydrates formamides to isocyanides.

Question 9 of 19

Which compound exhibits an extremely foul and offensive odor?

Explanation: Aliphatic isocyanides are notorious for their intensely foul, characteristic odors.

Question 10 of 19

The functional group isomer of an alkyl cyanide (RCN) is:

Explanation: Alkyl cyanides and alkyl isocyanides have the same molecular formula CnH2n+1N, making them functional isomers.

Question 11 of 19

In the structure of isocyanide (-N+≡C-), the bond between nitrogen and carbon consists of:

Explanation: A triple bond (like in cyanides and isocyanides) consists of one sigma bond and two pi bonds.

Question 12 of 19

Which amine will fail the carbylamine test?

Explanation: Diethylamine is a secondary amine and does not give the carbylamine test.

Question 13 of 19

What is the role of KOH in the carbylamine reaction?

Explanation: KOH acts as a base to abstract a proton from chloroform generating dichlorocarbene and neutralizing byproduct HCl.

Question 14 of 19

Treatment of primary amine with formic acid gives:

Explanation: Reaction of primary amine with HCOOH yields an N-formyl derivative (formamide).

Question 15 of 19

Which reagent converts alkyl halides to nitriles rather than isocyanides?

Explanation: Potassium cyanide (KCN) is predominantly ionic and yields alkyl cyanides.

Question 16 of 19

What color or physical property is typically associated with lower isocyanides?

Explanation: Lower aliphatic isocyanides are colorless liquids characterized by extremely repulsive odors.

Question 17 of 19

Which of the following is an ambident nucleophile?

Explanation: The cyanide ion can attack through either its carbon or nitrogen atom, classifying it as ambident.

Question 18 of 19

What is the IUPAC suffix for isocyanides?

Explanation: IUPAC nomenclature designates the -N+≡C- group with the suffix -isonitrile.

Question 19 of 19

Which reactant is necessary alongside primary amine and chloroform for the carbylamine reaction?

Explanation: Alcoholic potassium hydroxide is required for the generation of the carbene intermediate.
