Aliphatic Amines

Aliphatic amines are organic derivatives of ammonia where one or more hydrogen atoms are replaced by alkyl groups. They exhibit basic character due to the presence of a lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation Methods of Aliphatic Amines

  • Reduction of nitriles using catalytic hydrogenation or LiAlH4 yields primary amines with an extended carbon chain.
  • Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis converts potassium phthalimide into primary aliphatic amines via nucleophilic substitution followed by alkaline hydrolysis.
  • Hofmann Bromamide Degradation treats a primary acid amide with bromine and aqueous NaOH to yield a primary amine with one carbon atom less.
  • Reduction of amides and oximes using LiAlH4 provides a direct route to primary amines.

Basicity and Electronic Effects

  • The lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom allows aliphatic amines to act as Lewis bases and Brønsted-Lowry bases.
  • Inductive effect (+I) of alkyl groups increases electron density on the nitrogen atom, enhancing base strength relative to ammonia.
  • Solvation effect stabilizes substituted ammonium cations through hydrogen bonding, opposing the inductive trend in aqueous media.
  • Steric hindrance by bulky alkyl groups decreases the stability of the conjugate acid, reducing basicity in tertiary amines.

Chemical Reactions and Distinguishing Tests

  • Carbylamine test identifies primary amines by producing foul-smelling isocyanides upon heating with chloroform and alcoholic KOH.
  • Hinsberg test uses benzenesulfonyl chloride to distinguish between primary (soluble in alkali), secondary (insoluble, alkali-stable), and tertiary amines (unreactive).
  • Nitrous acid reaction converts primary aliphatic amines into unstable aliphatic diazonium salts which decompose to yield alcohols and nitrogen gas.
  • Acylation reactions involve the treatment of primary and secondary amines with acid chlorides or anhydrides to form N-substituted amides.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Base strength should continuously increase with the number of alkyl groups (+I effect: Tertiary > Secondary > Primary > Ammonia).

Observation: In aqueous solution, secondary amines are stronger bases than tertiary amines. — Combined steric hindrance and decreased hydration stability of tertiary ammonium cations outweigh the positive inductive effect.

Exception Primary aliphatic amines react with nitrous acid (HNO2) to form stable diazonium salts.

Observation: Aliphatic primary amines form highly unstable diazonium salts that instantly decompose to carbocations and nitrogen gas. — Aliphatic diazonium ions lack resonance stabilization, unlike aromatic diazonium counterparts.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Aliphatic amines are more basic than ammonia due to the electron-donating inductive effect (+I) of alkyl groups.
  • The aqueous basicity order for methylamines is (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > (CH3)3N.
  • Gabriel phthalimide synthesis is exclusively used for preparing primary aliphatic amines without contamination of secondary or tertiary amines.
  • Aliphatic primary amines react with nitrous acid (NaNO2 + HCl) at 0-5°C to liberate nitrogen gas quantitatively.
  • Carbylamine test is a specific test for primary amines and gives offensive-smelling alkyl isocyanides.
  • Hinsberg reagent is benzenesulfonyl chloride (C6H5SO2Cl).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming tertiary amines are the strongest bases in water due to having three +I alkyl groups, ignoring solvation and steric factors.
  • Confusing Gabriel phthalimide synthesis as a viable method for preparing primary aromatic amines (aryl amines fail due to unreactivity of aryl halides towards nucleophilic substitution).
  • Forgetting that nitrous acid reaction with aliphatic primary amines yields nitrogen gas, unlike aromatic amines which form stable salts.
  • Misidentifying Hinsberg reagent as benzoyl chloride instead of benzenesulfonyl chloride.

⚡ Quick Revision: Aliphatic Amines

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

Nitrogen is sp3 hybridized with a pyramidal geometry and a lone pair occupying the fourth orbital. This lone pair confers nucleophilic and basic properties.

Basicity Parameters

Base strength is quantified by Kb or pKb values. Higher Kb or lower pKb indicates a stronger base in aqueous solutions.

Synthetic Routes

Key synthetic pathways include reduction of nitriles, amides, and nitro compounds, alongside nucleophilic substitution methods like Gabriel synthesis.

Analytical Identification

Chemical differentiation relies on reactions with nitrous acid, Hinsberg reagent, and chloroform with alkali to establish amine class.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Primary Amine (R-NH2)
Organic compound with one alkyl group attached to nitrogen.
Secondary Amine (R2NH)
Organic compound with two alkyl groups attached to nitrogen.
Tertiary Amine (R3N)
Organic compound with three alkyl groups attached to nitrogen.
Hinsberg Reagent (C6H5SO2Cl)
Benzenesulfonyl chloride used to separate amine mixtures.
Carbylamine (R-NC)
Alkyl isocyanide with an extremely foul odor.
Zwitterion (Dipolar Ion)
Molecule with both positive and negative electrical charges.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Aliphatic amines are stronger bases than ammonia.
  • Secondary methylamine is more basic than primary methylamine in water.
  • Gabriel synthesis yields pure primary aliphatic amines.
  • Carbylamine test detects primary amines exclusively.
  • Nitrous acid converts primary aliphatic amines to nitrogen gas.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Aliphatic amines function as Lewis bases through nitrogen lone pairs.
  • Basicity is governed by inductive, steric, and solvation factors.
  • Synthetic strategies involve reduction and degradation reactions.
  • Distinction is achieved via Hinsberg and carbylamine tests.

⚖️ Key Differences

Gas phase basicity order VS Aqueous phase basicity order

Gas phase follows pure inductive effect (3° > 2° > 1° > NH3), whereas aqueous phase incorporates solvation and steric effects (2° > 1° > 3° or similar).

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following represents the correct order of basicity of methylamines in aqueous solution?

Explanation: In aqueous solution, combined steric hindrance, inductive effect, and hydration energy result in secondary methylamine being the strongest base: (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > (CH3)3N.

Question 2 of 19

Which reagent is used in the Carbylamine test?

Explanation: Primary amines react with chloroform and alcoholic potassium hydroxide to form foul-smelling carbylamines (isocyanides).

Question 3 of 19

Gabriel phthalimide synthesis is used for the preparation of:

Explanation: Gabriel synthesis exclusively produces primary aliphatic amines via nucleophilic substitution of potassium phthalimide with alkyl halides.

Question 4 of 19

What is the Hinsberg reagent?

Explanation: Hinsberg reagent is benzenesulfonyl chloride (C6H5SO2Cl), utilized for classifying primary, secondary, and tertiary amines.

Question 5 of 19

When ethylamine reacts with nitrous acid (NaNO2 + HCl) at 0-5°C, the principal product is:

Explanation: Aliphatic primary amines react with HNO2 to form unstable diazonium salts that decompose immediately to yield ethanol, nitrogen gas, and HCl.

Question 6 of 19

Which amine does not react with benzenesulfonyl chloride?

Explanation: Tertiary amines lack replaceable hydrogen atoms on the nitrogen atom and do not react with benzenesulfonyl chloride.

Question 7 of 19

In the Hofmann bromamide degradation reaction, acetamide is converted into:

Explanation: Hofmann bromamide degradation removes the carbonyl carbon, converting acetamide (CH3CONH2) into methylamine (CH3NH2).

Question 8 of 19

The hybridization of nitrogen in aliphatic amines is:

Explanation: The nitrogen atom in aliphatic amines forms three sigma bonds and holds one lone pair, corresponding to sp3 hybridization.

Question 9 of 19

Which of the following compounds has the highest boiling point?

Explanation: Primary amines like propan-1-amine have intermolecular hydrogen bonding that is more extensive than in tertiary amines, leading to higher boiling points.

Question 10 of 19

Reduction of alkyl cyanides (nitriles) with Na/EtOH yields:

Explanation: Reduction of alkyl cyanides (Mendius reduction) adds hydrogen across the triple bond to yield a primary amine (RCH2NH2).

Question 11 of 19

What happens when methylamine is treated with excess methyl iodide?

Explanation: Exhaustive methylation of a primary amine with excess alkyl halide yields a quaternary ammonium salt.

Question 12 of 19

Which functional group isomerism is exhibited by primary, secondary, and tertiary amines having the same molecular formula?

Explanation: Primary, secondary, and tertiary amines with the same molecular formula belong to different subclasses of amines, exhibiting functional group isomerism.

Question 13 of 19

Which method is best suited for preparing aniline?

Explanation: Reduction of nitrobenzene (e.g., using Sn/HCl) is the standard method for preparing aniline.

Question 14 of 19

The basicity of amines is attributed to:

Explanation: The unshared electron pair (lone pair) on the nitrogen atom enables amines to accept protons, conferring basicity.

Question 15 of 19

Which amine is insoluble in alkali after reacting with benzenesulfonyl chloride?

Explanation: Secondary amines form sulfonamides lacking acidic protons, making them insoluble in alkaline solutions.

Question 16 of 19

Ammonolysis of alkyl halides generally yields:

Explanation: Ammonolysis yields a complex mixture of primary, secondary, tertiary amines and quaternary ammonium salts due to successive alkylation.

Question 17 of 19

Which of the following compounds is most basic in gas phase?

Explanation: In the gas phase without solvation effects, increasing the number of electron-donating alkyl groups increases basicity: (CH3)3N > (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > NH3.

Question 18 of 19

Reaction of primary amine with acid chloride yields:

Explanation: Acylation of a primary amine with an acid chloride produces an N-substituted amide and HCl.

Question 19 of 19

Which reagent converts an amide directly into an amine with one less carbon atom?

Explanation: Hofmann bromamide degradation uses bromine and sodium hydroxide to convert amides to primary amines with degradation of the carbon chain.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following compounds is most basic in aqueous solution?

Solution: Secondary ethylamine ((C2H5)2NH) exhibits the optimal balance of inductive effect, hydration stability, and steric constraints in water, making it the strongest base among ethylamines.

Question 2 of 10
What is the product formed when methylamine reacts with nitrous acid at 0-5°C?

Solution: Aliphatic primary amines react with HNO2 to form unstable diazonium ions that rapidly decompose to yield methanol and nitrogen gas.

Question 3 of 10
Which reagent can distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary amines?

Solution: Hinsberg reagent (benzenesulfonyl chloride) distinguishes amine classes based on the solubility of their corresponding sulfonamides in alkali.

Question 4 of 10
Ammonolysis of propyl chloride predominantly yields:

Solution: Direct ammonolysis of alkyl halides leads to successive alkylation, resulting in a mixture of all four types of amine products.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following amines has the lowest boiling point?

Solution: Trimethylamine is a tertiary amine incapable of intermolecular hydrogen bonding, resulting in a lower boiling point than primary and secondary isomers.

Question 6 of 10
Which reaction produces an amine with an increased carbon chain length?

Solution: Reduction of nitriles (R-CN → R-CH2NH2) extends the carbon chain by one carbon atom.

Question 7 of 10
What is the hybrid state of carbon in the isocyanide group formed during the carbylamine test?

Solution: In isocyanides (R-N≡C), the carbon atom is sp hybridized, forming a triple bond with nitrogen.

Question 8 of 10
Which compound is formed when secondary amine reacts with nitrous acid?

Solution: Secondary amines react with nitrous acid to form oily yellow N-nitrosamines (R2N-N=O).

Question 9 of 10
Why are aliphatic amines stronger bases than ammonia?

Solution: Alkyl groups donate electron density via the positive inductive (+I) effect, increasing the electron availability on the nitrogen atom.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following describes the shape of aliphatic amines?

Solution: Due to three bond pairs and one lone pair around the sp3 hybridized nitrogen atom, aliphatic amines adopt a trigonal pyramidal geometry.
