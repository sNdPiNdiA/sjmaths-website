Substituted Anilines

Substituted anilines are derivatives of aniline where substituents replace aromatic ring hydrogens or amino group hydrogens, significantly altering base strength and reactivity. The presence of electron-releasing or electron-withdrawing groups modulates the electron density on the nitrogen atom through inductive and resonance effects.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electronic Effects on Basicity

  • Electron-donating groups (EDGs) such as -CH3 and -OCH3 increase aniline basicity when present at the para-position by stabilizing the free amine through +I and +R effects.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups (EWGs) such as -NO2, -CN, and -X decrease aniline basicity via -I and -R effects by dispersing electron density away from the nitrogen atom.
  • The basicity of substituted anilines follows the general order: p-anisidine > aniline > p-nitroaniline.
  • Ortho-substituted anilines often exhibit anomalous basicity due to the ortho-effect, causing steric inhibition of resonance regardless of electronic character.

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution Reactions

  • The amino group is a strongly activating and ortho/para-directing group due to the lone pair on nitrogen participating in resonance with the benzene ring.
  • Direct nitration of aniline yields a complex mixture containing tarry oxidation products alongside ortho and para isomers because HNO3 acts as an oxidizing agent.
  • To synthesize monobromoaniline (specifically 4-bromoaniline), the amino group is first protected via acetylation with acetic anhydride before bromination.
  • Sulphonation of aniline yields sulfanilic acid, which predominantly exists as a zwitterion (dipolar ion) in aqueous medium.

Diazotization and Synthetic Transformations

  • Primary substituted aromatic amines react with nitrous acid (NaNO2 + HCl) at 0-5 °C to form stable arenediazonium salts.
  • Arenediazonium chlorides undergo Sandmeyer reaction using Cu(I) halides to yield chloro-, bromo-, or cyano-substituted benzenes.
  • Gattermann reaction substitutes the diazonium group using copper powder in place of copper(I) salts.
  • Coupling reactions of diazonium salts with phenols and tertiary aromatic amines yield azo dyes through electrophilic substitution at the para-position.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Electron-withdrawing groups always decrease basicity

Observation: Ortho-nitroaniline and ortho-haloanilines display unexpected pKb variations — Steric inhibition of resonance and intramolecular hydrogen bonding alter the solvation energy and proton availability.

Exception Aliphatic amines are weaker bases than aromatic amines

Observation: Cyclohexylamine and aliphatic secondary/tertiary amines are far stronger bases than aniline — Delocalization of the nitrogen lone pair into the aromatic π-system in aniline makes the lone pair less available for protonation.

Exception Direct halogenation of aniline yields 2,4,6-tribromoaniline

Observation: Mono-halogenation is achieved via prior conversion to acetanilide — Acetylation reduces the activating power of the nitrogen atom, preventing exhaustive substitution at all ortho/para positions.

Ortho Effect Rule
Ortho-Substituted Anilines Are Weaker Bases

Application: Irrespective of whether an ortho substituent is electron-releasing or withdrawing, it usually lowers basicity compared to the para isomer due to steric hindrance to protonation.

Diazonium Temperature Window
0 - 5 °C

Application: Always select the low-temperature option for diazotization reactions to prevent decomposition of the diazonium salt into phenol.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Aniline is a weaker base than ammonia because the resonance structures of aniline show positive charge on nitrogen.
  • The pKb of aniline is 9.38, while that of aliphatic amines like methylamine is around 3.36.
  • Nitration of aniline directly gives tar, so amino protection via acetylation is mandatory for pure mono-nitro derivatives.
  • Sulfanilic acid exists as a zwitterion (H3N+-C6H4-COO-) giving it high melting point and insolubility in water.
  • Carbylamine test (isocyanide test) identifies primary substituted anilines by producing foul-smelling phenyl isocyanide with CHCl3 and KOH.
  • Coupling of benzenediazonium chloride with N,N-dimethylaniline occurs at the para-position in weakly acidic medium.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the basicity order of aliphatic and aromatic amines; remember aromatic amines are weaker.
  • Assuming direct bromination of aniline yields monobromoaniline instead of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline.
  • Forgetting that ortho-substituted anilines do not follow standard Hammett substituent constant rules due to steric hindrance.
  • Writing incorrect formulas for diazonium salts by omitting the positive charge on the terminal nitrogen.

⚡ Quick Revision: Substituted Anilines

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Resonance and Basicity

The lone pair on nitrogen is delocalized over the aromatic ring, reducing electron density on nitrogen and decreasing basicity compared to aliphatic amines.

Substituent Effects

Electron-releasing groups enhance basicity by increasing electron density, whereas electron-withdrawing groups diminish basicity via inductive and resonance withdrawal.

Synthetic Utility of Diazonium Salts

Arenediazonium salts serve as versatile intermediates for introducing -Cl, -Br, -CN, -I, -OH, and -H groups into the aromatic ring via replacement reactions.

Protection Strategies

Acetylation of the amino group reduces excessive activation during electrophilic substitution, allowing controlled mono-halogenation or mono-nitration.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Anilinium Ion (C6H5NH3+)
Conjugate acid of aniline formed upon protonation.
Diazotization (ArNH2 → ArN2+Cl-)
Conversion of primary aromatic amine into diazonium salt using HNO2.
Zwitterion (Dipolar Ion)
Internal salt form where acidic and basic centers neutralize each other.
Sandmeyer Reaction (ArN2+ + CuX → ArX)
Replacement of diazonium group by halogen or cyano group.
Coupling Reaction (ArN2+ + Ar'H → Ar-N=N-Ar')
Electrophilic substitution producing colored azo compounds.
Ortho Effect (Steric Hindrance)
Abnormal decrease in basicity of ortho-substituted anilines.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Aniline pKb = 9.38
  • Diazotization temperature = 0 - 5 °C
  • Direct bromination yields 2,4,6-tribromoaniline
  • Sulfanilic acid exists as a internal zwitterion
  • Acetylation protects the amino group for mono-substitution

📌 Quick Recall

  • Substituted anilines exhibit modified basicity based on substituent electronic effects.
  • EDGs increase basicity; EWGs decrease basicity.
  • Diazonium chemistry enables introduction of diverse functional groups.
  • Ortho-substituents disrupt resonance and basicity trends.

⚖️ Key Differences

Sandmeyer Reaction VS Gattermann Reaction

Sandmeyer uses Cu(I) salts (CuCl/HCl), while Gattermann uses copper powder with corresponding acid.

Which of the following substituted anilines is the strongest base in aqueous solution?

Question 2 of 18

What is the product of the reaction between aniline and acetyl chloride?

Explanation: Aniline reacts with acetyl chloride or acetic anhydride to form acetanilide via nucleophilic acyl substitution.

Question 3 of 18

In the diazotization of aniline with NaNO2 and HCl, what is the optimum temperature range?

Explanation: Diazonium salts are unstable at higher temperatures and decompose to phenols; hence 0-5 °C is maintained.

Question 4 of 18

Direct bromination of aniline with bromine water yields:

Explanation: The strong activating effect of the -NH2 group directs bromine to all available ortho and para positions, yielding 2,4,6-tribromoaniline.

Question 5 of 18

Sulfanilic acid exists predominantly as a:

Explanation: Internal proton transfer from the sulfonic acid group to the amino group creates a dipolar zwitterion.

Question 6 of 18

Which group decreases the basicity of aniline the most?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is a strong electron-withdrawing group via both -I and -R effects, heavily reducing nitrogen electron density.

Question 7 of 18

Carbylamine test is given by:

Explanation: Primary amines react with chloroform and alcoholic KOH to form foul-smelling isocyanides.

Question 8 of 18

Sandmeyer reaction involves the use of:

Explanation: Sandmeyer reaction specifically uses copper(I) salts like Cu2Cl2 or Cu2Br2 with corresponding halogen acids.

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following compounds does not couple with benzenediazonium chloride?

Explanation: Benzene lacks strong activating groups like -OH or -NR2 and does not undergo azo coupling with diazonium salts.

Question 10 of 18

The hybridization of nitrogen in aniline is approximately:

Explanation: Due to resonance interaction with the aromatic ring, the nitrogen atom adopts a planar geometry with sp2 hybridization.

Question 11 of 18

What happens when benzenediazonium chloride is treated with hypophosphorous acid (H3PO2)?

Explanation: H3PO2 reduces the diazonium group to hydrogen, effectively removing the amino group from the benzene ring.

Question 12 of 18

Ortho-substituted anilines are generally weaker bases than para-substituted anilines due to:

Explanation: Steric hindrance by the ortho substituent forces the amino group out of the plane of the ring, decreasing resonance stabilization of the free base.

Question 13 of 18

Which reagent converts nitrobenzene to aniline in acidic medium?

Explanation: Tin and hydrochloric acid selectively reduce nitro groups to primary aromatic amines.

Question 14 of 18

In the coupling reaction of benzenediazonium chloride with phenol, the reaction is carried out in:

Explanation: Mildly alkaline medium converts phenol into phenoxide ion, which is a stronger nucleophile and accelerates electrophilic aromatic substitution.

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following is a liquid substituted aniline used in dye manufacturing?

Explanation: N,N-dimethylaniline is an important liquid tertiary aromatic amine widely used in dye synthesis.

Question 16 of 18

Reaction of aniline with benzoyl chloride in the presence of NaOH is known as:

Explanation: Benzoylation of amines using benzoyl chloride and aqueous base is termed the Schotten-Baumann reaction.

Question 17 of 18

When acetanilide is nitrated, the major product is:

Explanation: Steric hindrance at the ortho position makes 4-nitroacetanilide the major product during nitration.

Question 18 of 18

The color of azo dyes formed by coupling reaction is typically:

Explanation: Extended conjugation in azo compounds (Ar-N=N-Ar') imparts characteristic yellow, orange, or red colors.

Question 1 of 10
What is the correct decreasing order of basicity for aniline, p-toluidine, and p-nitroaniline?

Solution: Electron-donating methyl group in p-toluidine increases basicity, while electron-withdrawing nitro group in p-nitroaniline decreases basicity.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following reagents replaces the diazonium group with a hydrogen atom?

Solution: Hypophosphorous acid (H3PO2) or ethanol acts as a reducing agent to replace the diazonium group with hydrogen (deamination).

Question 3 of 10
The ortho-effect in substituted anilines typically results in:

Solution: Steric hindrance from ortho substituents inhibits resonance stabilization of the free amine, lowering basicity regardless of electronic nature.

Question 4 of 10
In the Schotten-Baumann reaction, aniline reacts with:

Solution: Schotten-Baumann reaction involves benzoylation of amines using benzoyl chloride in the presence of aqueous sodium hydroxide.

Question 5 of 10
Which amine does not react with nitrous acid to form diazonium salts?

Solution: N-methylaniline is a secondary amine and forms an N-nitrosoamine instead of a stable arenediazonium salt.

Question 6 of 10
Coupling of benzenediazonium chloride with phenol takes place at which position?

Solution: Azo coupling predominantly occurs at the para position relative to the phenolic -OH group due to lower steric hindrance.

Question 7 of 10
What is the end product when nitrobenzene is reduced with Zn and NaOH?

Solution: Reduction of nitrobenzene with zinc dust and alkali yields hydrazobenzene (1,2-diphenylhydrazine).

Question 8 of 10
Why is aniline not successfully nitrated directly with concentrated HNO3 and H2SO4?

Solution: Direct nitrated mixture oxidizes the reactive aniline ring into undesirable black oxidation products.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following substituents exhibits both -I and +R effects when attached to aniline?

Solution: Methoxy group (-OCH3) withdraws electrons inductively (-I) but donates through resonance (+R), with +R dominating at the para position.

Question 10 of 10
Identification of primary aromatic amines is reliably done using:

Solution: The carbylamine reaction generates foul-smelling isocyanides exclusively with primary amines.
