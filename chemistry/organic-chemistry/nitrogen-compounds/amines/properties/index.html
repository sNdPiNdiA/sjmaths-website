Properties of Amines

Amines are derivatives of ammonia where one or more hydrogen atoms are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups, exhibiting characteristic basicity and nucleophilicity due to a lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom. Their physical and chemical behaviors are heavily dictated by hydrogen bonding, inductive effects, and steric hindrance.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Physical Properties and Hydrogen Bonding

  • Primary and secondary amines form intermolecular hydrogen bonds, leading to higher boiling points than comparable hydrocarbons and ethers but lower than corresponding alcohols.
  • Tertiary amines cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds among themselves due to the absence of N-H bonds, resulting in lower boiling points than primary and secondary structural isomers.
  • Lower aliphatic amines are soluble in water because they form hydrogen bonds with water molecules, but solubility decreases as the hydrocarbon chain length increases.

Basicity of Aliphatic and Aromatic Amines

  • The basicity of amines depends on the availability of the nitrogen lone pair to accept a proton, measured quantitatively via pKb values.
  • In the gas phase, basicity strictly follows the inductive order: tertiary > secondary > primary > ammonia due to cumulative +I electron-donating alkyl groups.
  • In aqueous solution, basicity is a balance of +I effect, solvation enthalpy, and steric hindrance, reversing the gas-phase order for secondary and tertiary systems.

Chemical Reactions and Characteristic Tests

  • Primary amines react with chloroform and alcoholic potassium hydroxide to form foul-smelling isocyanides (carbylamines), serving as a specific test for primary amines.
  • Aromatic primary amines react with nitrous acid (NaNO2 + HCl) at 273-278 K to form stable diazonium salts.
  • Hinsberg reagent (benzene sulfonyl chloride) distinguishes between primary, secondary, and tertiary amines based on the solubility of the resulting sulfonamides in alkali.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aliphatic amine aqueous basicity should follow gas phase trend (3° > 2° > 1°).

Observation: In aqueous solution, 2° amines are often stronger bases than 3° amines (e.g., dimethylamine > trimethylamine). — Steric hindrance of three bulky alkyl groups in tertiary ammonium cations prevents effective solvation by water molecules, destabilizing the conjugate acid.

Exception Aniline is expected to be more basic than ammonia due to the +I effect of the phenyl ring.

Observation: Aniline is considerably less basic than ammonia (pKb ~ 9.38 vs 4.75). — Resonance stabilization of aniline distributes the nitrogen lone pair into the aromatic ring, making it less available for protonation.

Exception Electrophilic substitution in aniline yields ortho and para substituted derivatives exclusively.

Observation: Direct nitration of aniline yields a significant amount (47%) of m-nitroaniline alongside p-nitroaniline and o-nitroaniline. — In strongly acidic nitration media, the aniline molecule is protonated to form the anilinium ion, which exerts a strong -I electron-withdrawing meta-directing effect.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Aqueous Basicity Order Mnemonic
For Methyl groups: 2° > 1° > 3° > NH3; For Ethyl groups: 2° > 3° > 1° > NH3

Application: Immediately select the correct basicity sequence in aqueous medium based on whether the substituent is methyl or ethyl.

Hinsberg Test Solubility Rule
1° Amine = Soluble in alkali; 2° Amine = Insoluble in alkali; 3° Amine = No reaction

Application: Quickly identify amine class during qualitative analysis based on alkali treatment of the sulfonyl chloride derivative.

Diazonium Salt Stability
ArN2+X- stable at 0-5 °C; Aliphatic diazonium salts decompose instantly to carbocations.

Application: Use temperature criteria to distinguish aromatic vs aliphatic amine reactions with nitrous acid.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Aniline does not undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation or acylation due to salt formation with the Lewis acid catalyst AlCl3.
  • The boiling point order for isomeric amines is Primary > Secondary > Tertiary.
  • Aliphatic amines are stronger bases than ammonia due to the electron-releasing inductive (+I) effect of alkyl groups.
  • Direct halogenation (bromination) of aniline with bromine water yields 2,4,6-tribromoaniline as a white precipitate.
  • To direct electrophilic substitution of aniline exclusively to p-acetanilide, amino group acetylation is performed first.
  • Quaternary ammonium salts (R4N+X-) are completely ionized solids structurally analogous to ammonium halides.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing gas-phase basicity order with aqueous-phase basicity order.
  • Assuming tertiary amines can form intermolecular hydrogen bonds because they possess a nitrogen atom with a lone pair.
  • Forgetting that aniline forms a complex with anhydrous AlCl3, failing Friedel-Crafts reactions.
  • Confusing carbylamine test reagents (CHCl3 + KOH) with iodoform test reagents.

⚡ Quick Revision: Properties of Amines

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

Amines feature a pyramidal geometry around the nitrogen atom with sp3 hybridization, where the lone pair occupies the fourth orbital.

Basicity Determinants

Basicity is quantified via Kb and pKb values, reflecting electron availability on nitrogen governed by inductive, resonance, and solvation factors.

Acylation and Protection

Primary and secondary amines react with acid chlorides or anhydrides to form stable amides, useful for reducing reactivity in directed synthesis.

Diazotization Chemistry

Aromatic primary amines yield stable diazonium salts at 273-278 K, serving as versatile intermediates for Sandmeyer and Gattermann reactions.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Carbylamine Reaction (Isonitrile synthesis)
Reaction of primary amine with chloroform and alcoholic KOH to yield foul-smelling isocyanide.
Hinsberg Reagent (C6H5SO2Cl)
Benzene sulfonyl chloride used to separate amine mixtures.
Diazotization (ArNH2 → ArN2+)
Conversion of aromatic primary amine to diazonium salt using HNO2 at 0-5 °C.
Coupling Reaction (Azo dye formation)
Electrophilic substitution between diazonium salt and activated aromatic rings.
Ammonolysis (RX + NH3)
Nucleophilic substitution of alkyl halides by ammonia yielding amine mixtures.
Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis (Primary amine route)
Method for synthesizing primary aliphatic amines avoiding polyalkylation.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Gas phase basicity: 3° > 2° > 1° > NH3
  • Aqueous methyl amine basicity: 2° > 1° > 3° > NH3
  • Hinsberg primary amine derivative is soluble in alkali.
  • Aniline does not undergo Friedel-Crafts reaction.
  • Bromination of aniline yields 2,4,6-tribromoaniline without catalyst.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Boiling points follow 1° > 2° > 3° due to hydrogen bonding extent.
  • Basicity is controlled by +I effect, steric hindrance, and hydration energy.
  • Hinsberg reagent differentiates primary, secondary, and tertiary amines.
  • Aromatic amines undergo facile diazotization to form versatile synthetic intermediates.

⚖️ Key Differences

Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis VS Hofmann Bromamide Degradation

Gabriel synthesis is used to prepare pure primary aliphatic amines from alkyl halides; Hofmann degradation converts amides to primary amines with one less carbon atom.

Which of the following amines does not react with nitrous acid to form nitrogen gas?

Explanation: Secondary amines like dimethylamine react with nitrous acid to form yellow oily N-nitrosamines without evolving nitrogen gas.

Question 2 of 18

The correct order of basic strength of methylamines in aqueous solution is:

Explanation: In aqueous solution, methyl substitution basicity follows the order secondary > primary > tertiary due to combined inductive, solvation, and steric effects.

Question 3 of 18

Which reagent is used in the Hinsberg test to distinguish amine classes?

Explanation: Benzene sulfonyl chloride (Hinsberg reagent) reacts differently with primary, secondary, and tertiary amines and distinguishes their solubility in alkali.

Question 4 of 18

Aniline is less basic than ammonia because:

Explanation: Resonance stabilization of aniline delocalizes the nitrogen lone pair into the aromatic ring, reducing proton affinity.

Question 5 of 18

Gabriel phthalimide synthesis is specifically used for the preparation of:

Explanation: Gabriel synthesis yields pure primary aliphatic amines through nucleophilic substitution of potassium phthalimide on alkyl halides.

Question 6 of 18

Carbylamine test is given by:

Explanation: Only primary amines (both aliphatic and aromatic) react with chloroform and alcoholic KOH to yield foul-smelling carbylamines.

Question 7 of 18

The boiling point of trimethylamine is lower than that of propylamine because:

Explanation: Propylamine has N-H bonds allowing intermolecular hydrogen bonding, whereas tertiary trimethylamine lacks N-H bonds.

Question 8 of 18

Why does aniline fail to undergo Friedel-Crafts reaction?

Explanation: Basic aniline reacts with Lewis acid AlCl3 to form a complex salt, rendering the ring strongly deactivated.

Question 9 of 18

What is the product when aniline is treated with bromine water at room temperature?

Explanation: Direct bromination of aniline with bromine water yields a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline due to strong ring activation by -NH2.

Question 10 of 18

In the gas phase, the basicity order of amines is:

Explanation: In the gas phase without solvent effects, increasing alkyl groups increase electron density via +I effect, giving 3° > 2° > 1° > NH3.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following compounds gives a foul smell when heated with chloroform and alcoholic KOH?

Explanation: Ethylamine is a primary amine, which undergoes the carbylamine reaction yielding ethyl isocyanide with a foul smell.

Question 12 of 18

To prepare p-nitroaniline from aniline, the standard protective step involves:

Explanation: Acetylation moderates the activating effect of the amino group, ensuring para-substitution predominates during nitration before deacetylation.

Question 13 of 18

What is formed when benzenediazonium chloride is treated with hypophosphorous acid (H3PO2)?

Explanation: Reduction of benzenediazonium chloride with H3PO2 (or ethanol) replaces the diazonium group with hydrogen, yielding benzene.

Question 14 of 18

Primary aliphatic amines react with nitrous acid at 0-5 °C to yield:

Explanation: Aliphatic diazonium salts are extremely unstable and decompose immediately to carbocations, yielding alcohols and nitrogen gas.

Question 15 of 18

Which amine is a gas at room temperature?

Explanation: Lower aliphatic amines like methylamine and dimethylamine are gases at room temperature due to low molecular mass.

Question 16 of 18

The hybridization of nitrogen in aliphatic amines is:

Explanation: Nitrogen in amines uses three sp3 hybrid orbitals for sigma bonds and one for the lone pair.

Question 17 of 18

Which of the following is the strongest base in aqueous solution?

Explanation: Dimethylamine is a secondary aliphatic amine possessing optimal balance of inductive effect and solvation, making it a stronger base than ammonia, aniline, or trimethylamine.

Question 18 of 18

Reaction of an amide with Br2 and KOH yields a primary amine with:

Explanation: Hofmann bromamide degradation removes the carbonyl carbon as carbonate, yielding a primary amine with one carbon atom fewer than the parent amide.

Solution: Primary amine sulfonamides possess an acidic hydrogen, making them soluble in alkali to form clear solutions that reprecipitate upon acid addition.

Question 3 of 10

Solution: Hofmann bromamide degradation converts amides to primary amines containing one fewer carbon atom using Br2 and KOH.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following compounds is most basic in aqueous solution?

Solution: Benzylamine is an aliphatic amine where the amino group is separated from the aromatic ring by a methylene group, avoiding resonance delocalization and retaining high basicity.

Question 5 of 10
Sandmeyer reaction uses cuprous halides to convert diazonium salts into:

Solution: Sandmeyer reaction involves copper(I) chloride or bromide to introduce chlorine or bromine into the aromatic ring from diazonium salts.

Question 6 of 10
What is the hybridization and geometry of the nitrogen atom in trimethylamine?

Solution: Trimethylamine features sp3 hybridization with a pyramidal geometry distorted by the lone pair.

Question 7 of 10
Why is aniline acetylated prior to monobromination?

Solution: Acetylation withdraws electron density from nitrogen via resonance, preventing polybromination and ensuring mono-substitution.

Question 8 of 10
Which reagent converts benzenediazonium chloride into iodobenzene?

Solution: Treatment of benzenediazonium chloride with potassium iodide (KI) directly yields iodobenzene without requiring copper catalysts.

Question 9 of 10
Which test uniquely identifies primary aromatic amines?

Solution: Azo dye test formed by coupling diazonium salts with phenols or naphthols specifically identifies aromatic primary amines.

Question 10 of 10
The decreasing order of boiling points for isomeric butylamines (1°, 2°, 3°) is:

Solution: Primary butylamines form extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonds, secondary form fewer, and tertiary form none, giving the boiling point order 1° > 2° > 3°.
