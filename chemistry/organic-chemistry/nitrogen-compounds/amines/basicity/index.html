Basicity of Amines

Amines act as Lewis bases due to the presence of a lone pair of electrons on the trivalent nitrogen atom. The basic strength of amines is quantified by their dissociation constant Kb or pKb values.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electronic and Inductive Effects on Basicity

  • The +I effect of alkyl groups increases electron density on the nitrogen atom, enhancing basicity.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups (e.g., -NO2, -X, -CN) decrease electron density on nitrogen, lowering basicity.
  • Aliphatic amines are generally stronger bases than ammonia due to the electron-donating inductive effect of alkyl substituents.

Steric Hindrance and Solvation Effects in Solution

  • Protonated amines are stabilized by hydrogen bonding with water molecules, where primary ammonium ions form more hydrogen bonds than tertiary ones.
  • Steric hindrance from bulky alkyl groups impedes approach of solvent molecules, destabilizing the hydrated ammonium cation.
  • In the gas phase, basicity strictly follows the inductive order: Tertiary > Secondary > Primary > Ammonia.

Aromatic and Heterocyclic Amine Basicity

  • Aniline is a weaker base than aliphatic amines because the lone pair on nitrogen is delocalized into the benzene ring via resonance.
  • Protonation of aniline destroys resonance stabilization of the free amine, yielding a less stable anilinium ion.
  • Electron-donating groups on the ring increase aniline basicity, while electron-withdrawing groups at ortho and para positions decrease basicity.

Conditions: Applicable to buffer solutions containing amine bases and their salts

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Basicity order in aqueous methyl-substituted amines follows inductive plus solvation trend as (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > (CH3)3N > NH3.

Observation: Tertiary methylamine shows lower basicity than secondary methylamine in water. — Severe steric hindrance to hydration in trimethylammonium ion outweighs its +I stabilization.

Exception Basicity order in aqueous ethyl-substituted amines follows standard inductive trend.

Observation: Diethylamine > Triethylamine > Ethylamine > Ammonia. — Combined steric and solvation factors alter the expected pure inductive hierarchy for ethyl groups.

Exception Aromatic amines are weak bases due to resonance delocalization.

Observation: p-Nitroaniline is significantly less basic than aniline, but o-substituted anilines show anomalous pKb values. — Ortho-effect creates steric inhibition of resonance, altering expected electronic trends.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Aliphatic amines are stronger bases than ammonia due to electron-releasing inductive (+I) effect of alkyl groups.
  • Gas-phase basicity order of amines strictly follows inductive order: (CH3)3N > (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > NH3.
  • In aqueous solution, secondary methylamine ((CH3)2NH) is the strongest base due to balanced +I, steric, and hydration effects.
  • Aniline is a weaker base than cyclohexylamine because aniline lone pair participates in resonance with the benzene ring.
  • Presence of electron-withdrawing groups like -NO2 at the para-position of aniline decreases its basicity.
  • Quaternary ammonium salts ([R4N]+X-) do not possess basic properties as they lack a lone pair on nitrogen.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming tertiary amines are always the strongest bases in aqueous media without accounting for steric hindrance to hydration.
  • Confusing Kb with pKb such that a high Kb value is incorrectly associated with a weak base.
  • Neglecting resonance effects when comparing the basicity of aliphatic cyclic amines (e.g., piperidine) with aromatic amines (e.g., pyridine).
  • Forgetting that protonation of aniline yields the anilinium ion, which cannot participate in resonance through the nitrogen lone pair.

⚡ Quick Revision: Basicity of Amines

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Lewis Base Character

Amines possess a localized sp3 hybridized lone pair on nitrogen, allowing coordination with protons or Lewis acids.

Inductive and Solvation Equilibrium

Basicity is dictated by the net balance of electron-releasing inductive effects and hydrogen-bonding stabilization of the conjugate acid in water.

Resonance Suppression

Aromatic resonance withdraws electron density from the nitrogen atom, rendering aromatic amines significantly weaker bases than alkylamines.

Substituent Perturbations

Ring substituents modulate basicity via resonance (-M/+M) and inductive (-I/+I) pathways, profoundly altering pKa values.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Base Dissociation Constant (Kb)
Equilibrium constant measuring amine basicity strength in water.
pKb (pKb)
Negative logarithm of Kb; smaller values indicate stronger bases.
Inductive Effect (+I / -I)
Permanent polarization of σ-bonds caused by electronegativity differences.
Resonance Effect (+M / -M)
Delocalization of π-electrons across conjugated systems.
Hydration Energy (ΔHhyd)
Energy released when ions are solvated by water molecules.
Anilinium Ion (C6H5NH3+)
Conjugate acid of aniline formed upon protonation.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • pKa + pKb = 14 at 298 K
  • Gas phase basicity: 3° > 2° > 1° > NH3
  • Aqueous methylamine basicity: 2° > 1° > 3° > NH3
  • Aqueous ethylamine basicity: 2° > 3° > 1° > NH3
  • Aniline is a weaker base than ammonia due to resonance delocalization

📌 Quick Recall

  • Alkyl substitution increases basicity via +I effect relative to ammonia.
  • Solvation stabilization heavily dictates aqueous basicity order, penalizing bulky tertiary groups.
  • Resonance delocalization of the nitrogen lone pair drastically reduces aromatic amine basicity.
  • Substituents on aromatic rings alter basicity predictably via electronic effects.

⚖️ Key Differences

Gas Phase Basicity VS Aqueous Phase Basicity

Gas phase depends entirely on inductive electron donation (3° > 2° > 1°), whereas aqueous phase incorporates steric hindrance and solvation effects.

Which of the following represents the correct order of basicity of methylamines in aqueous solution?

Explanation: In aqueous solution, secondary methylamine is most basic due to balanced inductive, solvation, and steric effects.

Question 2 of 18

What is the gas-phase basicity order for ethylamines?

Explanation: In the gas phase without solvation, basicity increases purely with the number of electron-donating alkyl groups: 3° > 2° > 1°.

Question 3 of 18

Why is aniline a weaker base than ammonia?

Explanation: Resonance delocalization of the nitrogen lone pair into the aromatic ring reduces its availability for protonation.

Question 4 of 18

Which of the following compounds has the lowest pKb value?

Explanation: Lower pKb corresponds to a stronger base. Aliphatic amines like methylamine are much stronger bases than aromatic ones.

Question 5 of 18

What effect does a nitro group at the para position have on the basicity of aniline?

Explanation: The -NO2 group exerts -M and -I effects, withdrawing electron density from the nitrogen and decreasing basicity.

Question 6 of 18

Which amine does not react with nitrous acid to form diazonium salts below 5°C?

Explanation: Primary aliphatic amines react with HNO2 to yield unstable aliphatic diazonium ions which decompose immediately to alcohols and N2.

Question 7 of 18

Between pyridine and piperidine, which is more basic?

Explanation: In piperidine, the nitrogen lone pair resides in an sp3 orbital, whereas in pyridine it is in an sp2 orbital. sp3 lone pairs are held less tightly to the nucleus, making piperidine a stronger base.

Question 8 of 18

What is the relationship between pKa of conjugate acid and pKb of base at 298 K?

Explanation: For any conjugate acid-base pair in aqueous solution, pKa + pKb = pKw = 14 at 298 K.

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following substituted anilines is the strongest base?

Explanation: p-Anisidine contains a -OCH3 group which exhibits a +M effect, increasing electron density on the ring and enhancing basicity.

Question 10 of 18

What is the hybridization of the nitrogen atom in trimethylamine?

Explanation: Trimethylamine nitrogen is bonded to three methyl groups and holds one lone pair, totaling four electron domains, corresponding to sp3 hybridization.

Question 11 of 18

Which factor primarily accounts for the high basicity of aliphatic amines compared to ammonia?

Explanation: Alkyl groups donate electron density toward nitrogen via the inductive effect, increasing electron availability for protons.

Question 12 of 18

Consider aqueous ethylamines. The correct basicity order is:

Explanation: For ethylamines in water, diethylamine is most basic, followed by triethylamine, then monoethylamine.

Question 13 of 18

Why does quaternary ammonium hydroxide ([R4N]+OH-) act as a strong base comparable to NaOH?

Explanation: Quaternary ammonium hydroxides are fully ionized salts in aqueous solution, releasing hydroxide ions freely.

Question 14 of 18

Which of the following compounds is the weakest base?

Explanation: Triphenylamine features three phenyl groups drawing electron density away from nitrogen via resonance, making it an extremely weak base.

Question 15 of 18

What happens to the basicity of a primary amine when an acyl group (-COR) is introduced to form an amide?

Explanation: In amides, the nitrogen lone pair is delocalized into the adjacent carbonyl group via resonance, rendering amides essentially neutral or extremely weak bases.

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following is true for pyrrole regarding basicity?

Explanation: The lone pair on nitrogen in pyrrole is involved in aromaticity (Hückel's rule), so protonation destroys the aromatic system.

Question 17 of 18

How does steric hindrance affect the basicity of amines in aqueous solution?

Explanation: Bulky alkyl groups sterically hinder water molecules from solvating and stabilizing the protonated ammonium ion.

Question 18 of 18

Which nitrogen-containing compound is most basic in aqueous medium?

Explanation: Dimethylamine strikes the optimal balance between +I inductive electron release and steric hindrance to hydration.
