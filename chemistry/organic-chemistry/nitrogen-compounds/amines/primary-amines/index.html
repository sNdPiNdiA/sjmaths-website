Organic Chemistry

Primary Amines

Primary amines are organic derivatives of ammonia wherein one hydrogen atom is replaced by an alkyl or aryl group, possessing the general formula RNH2. They act as both weak bases and nucleophiles due to the presence of a lone pair of electrons on the trivalent nitrogen atom.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthesis Methods of Primary Amines

  • Reduction of nitro compounds using catalytic hydrogenation (H2/Pd, Pt, or Ni) or active metals with acid (Fe/HCl, Sn/HCl) yields primary aliphatic and aromatic amines.
  • Reduction of nitriles (R−CN) with Na/EtOH (Bouveault–Blanc reduction) or LiAlH4 produces primary amines with an extension of one carbon atom.
  • Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis involves the reaction of potassium phthalimide with alkyl halides followed by alkaline hydrolysis to yield pure primary aliphatic amines without secondary or tertiary amine contamination.
  • Hofmann Bromamide Degradation converts primary acid amides into primary amines containing one carbon atom less than the starting material.

Chemical Properties and Reactions

  • Primary amines react with nitrous acid (HNO2 in situ from NaNO2 + HCl) at 0–5°C to yield stable diazonium salts in aromatic systems, or unstable aliphatic diazonium species that decompose to alcohols.
  • Carbylamine reaction (isocyanide test) is a specific diagnostic test for primary amines, yielding foul-smelling alkyl/aryl isocyanides upon heating with chloroform and caustic potash.
  • Benzoylation or acetylation of primary amines yields amides via nucleophilic acyl substitution, a reaction known as the Schotten–Baumann reaction when using benzoyl chloride.
  • Primary amines react with carbonyl compounds (aldehydes and ketones) to form stable condensation products known as Schiff bases (imines).

Basicity and Electronic Effects

  • The basicity of primary aliphatic amines is governed by the +I effect of alkyl groups, which increases electron density on the nitrogen atom and stabilizes the conjugate acid.
  • In the gas phase, the basicity order of aliphatic amines follows the inductive trend: Tertiary > Secondary > Primary > Ammonia.
  • In aqueous solution, hydration effects modify the base strength, resulting in the typical aliphatic basicity order: Secondary > Primary > Tertiary for methyl substituents.
  • Aromatic primary amines such as aniline are considerably weaker bases than aliphatic primary amines due to resonance delocalization of the lone pair into the benzene ring.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aliphatic primary amines react with nitrous acid (HNO2) to form stable diazonium salts.

Observation: Aliphatic primary amines yield unstable diazonium ions that immediately decompose to give alcohols, nitrogen gas, and alkenes. — Aliphatic diazonium cations lack the resonance stabilization available to aromatic diazonium counterparts.

Exception Primary aromatic amines undergo direct halogenation (e.g., bromination with Br2/H2O) to yield mono-substituted products.

Observation: Aniline reacts with aqueous bromine instantaneously to yield 2,4,6-tribromoaniline as a white precipitate. — The strongly activating −NH2 group increases electron density at ortho and para positions to an extreme degree.

Exception Amines with higher alkyl substitution are stronger bases due to cumulative inductive electron release.

Observation: Triethylamine is a weaker base than diethylamine in aqueous solution. — Steric hindrance by three bulky alkyl groups prevents effective solvation and stabilization of the corresponding substituted ammonium cation by water molecules.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Gabriel Phthalimide Limitation Mnemonic
Gabriel = Alkyl Halide Only

Application: Remember that aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with potassium phthalimide under mild conditions, meaning aromatic primary amines (like aniline) cannot be prepared via this route.

Hofmann Carbon Loss Rule
R−CO−NH2 → R−NH2 (n − 1 carbons)

Application: Instantly identify that Hofmann bromamide degradation steps down the carbon chain by exactly one carbon atom, useful for ascending/descending carbon series problems.

Aqueous Basicity Trend Mnemonic
213 for Methyl, 231 for Ethyl

Application: For methyl substituents in water, order is 2° > 1° > 3°; for ethyl substituents, order is 2° > 3° > 1°.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Primary amines can be distinguished from secondary and tertiary amines using the Carbylamine test, which produces foul-smelling isocyanides.
  • Hofmann bromamide degradation reaction involves the migration of an alkyl or aryl group from the carbonyl carbon to the adjacent nitrogen atom.
  • Aniline on reaction with benzenediazonium chloride in weakly acidic medium forms p-aminoazobenzene, a bright yellow dye.
  • To prepare a primary aromatic amine without unwanted poly-halogenation, aniline is first acetylated using acetic anhydride to protect the amino group.
  • Hinsberg test (benzenesulfonyl chloride) reacts with primary amines to form sulfonamides that are soluble in aqueous alkali due to acidic hydrogen on nitrogen.
  • Reduction of nitrobenzene with Fe/HCl is preferred over catalytic hydrogenation on an industrial scale because FeCl2 formed hydrolyzes to generate HCl, acting catalytically.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the Hinsberg test solubility behavior: primary amine sulfonamides are soluble in alkali, whereas secondary amine sulfonamides are insoluble.
  • Assuming Gabriel phthalimide synthesis is suitable for synthesizing aniline; aryl halides fail to react via S_N2 mechanism.
  • Forgetting the strict low-temperature requirement (0–5°C) for diazotization of aromatic amines, above which decomposition to phenols occurs.
  • Misapplying the gas-phase basicity order to aqueous solutions where solvation energy plays a dominant role.

⚡ Quick Revision: Primary Amines

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Methods of Preparation

Primary amines are synthesized via reduction of nitro compounds, nitriles, and amides, or through specialized name reactions like Gabriel phthalimide and Hofmann degradation.

Basicity and Structure

The lone pair on nitrogen confers basic and nucleophilic character, modulated by inductive effects of alkyl groups and resonance delocalization in aromatic systems.

Diagnostic Tests

Carbylamine reaction and Hinsberg reagent reactions serve as classical laboratory tests to unambiguously identify and separate primary amines from secondary and tertiary homologs.

Aromatic Reactivity

Aromatic primary amines undergo facile ring substitution and diazotization, serving as versatile synthetic intermediates for diverse azo dyes and substituted benzenes.

Degradation of primary amide to primary amine with loss of one carbon.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carbylamine test is exclusively given by primary amines (both aliphatic and aromatic).
  • Aromatic diazonium salts are stable only between 0°C and 5°C.
  • Aniline cannot be prepared by Gabriel phthalimide synthesis.
  • Hofmann bromamide degradation shortens the carbon chain by one carbon atom.
  • Secondary amine sulfonamides are insoluble in alkali due to the absence of acidic protons on nitrogen.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Primary amines contain the −NH2 functional group attached to carbon.
  • Basicity in water depends on inductive effect, steric hindrance, and hydration.
  • Chemical reactions include alkylation, acylation, carbylamine test, and diazotization.
  • Protecting groups like acetyl groups are used during directed electrophilic substitution of aniline.

⚖️ Key Differences

Carbylamine Test VS Azo Dye Test

Carbylamine test identifies primary amines (aliphatic/aromatic) via foul isocyanide smell; Azo dye test identifies aromatic primary amines via coupling with β-naphthol.

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following reagents can be used to distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary amines?

Explanation: Hinsberg reagent (benzenesulfonyl chloride) reacts differently with primary, secondary, and tertiary amines, allowing their separation and identification.

Question 2 of 19

The product formed when primary amine reacts with chloroform and alcoholic KOH is:

Explanation: The carbylamine reaction yields foul-smelling alkyl or aryl isocyanides (carbylamines).

Question 3 of 19

Which compound does not undergo Hofmann bromamide degradation?

Explanation: N-methylethanamide is a secondary amide and lacks the required two unsubstituted hydrogens on the amide nitrogen for Hofmann degradation.

Question 4 of 19

In the Gabriel phthalimide synthesis, potassium phthalimide is treated with:

Explanation: Potassium phthalimide undergoes nucleophilic substitution with alkyl halides to yield N-alkylphthalimides.

Question 5 of 19

What is the correct order of basicity of methylamines in aqueous solution?

Explanation: For methyl-substituted amines in water, the combined effect of inductive stabilization, steric hindrance, and hydration results in secondary > primary > tertiary basicity order.

Question 6 of 19

Aniline is usually prepared by the reduction of nitrobenzene using:

Explanation: Iron scrap and hydrochloric acid (Fe/HCl) is the standard industrial reducing agent for large-scale production of aniline from nitrobenzene.

Question 7 of 19

Which of the following amines gives a clear solution in alkali after reacting with Hinsberg reagent?

Explanation: Primary amine reacts to form an N-alkylbenzenesulfonamide containing an acidic hydrogen, making it soluble in aqueous alkali.

Question 8 of 19

Why is aniline a weaker base than cyclohexylamine?

Explanation: In aniline, the nitrogen lone pair is delocalized into the benzene ring via resonance, making it less available for protonation.

Question 9 of 19

The conversion of an amide to a primary amine with one carbon less is known as:

Explanation: Hofmann bromamide degradation degrades primary amides into primary amines with the elimination of the carbonyl carbon as carbonate.

Question 10 of 19

What happens when ethylamine reacts with nitrous acid at room temperature?

Explanation: Aliphatic primary amines react with HNO2 to form unstable diazonium salts that decompose immediately into ethanol and nitrogen gas.

Question 11 of 19

Direct bromination of aniline with aqueous bromine yields:

Explanation: The activating effect of the −NH2 group is so strong that aqueous bromine rapidly converts aniline into 2,4,6-tribromoaniline precipitate.

Question 12 of 19

To prepare mono-bromo derivative of aniline, the amino group is first protected by:

Explanation: Acetylation with acetic anhydride converts aniline to acetanilide, moderating the activating effect of the amino group.

Question 13 of 19

Which reagent reduces nitrobenzene directly to phenylhydroxylamine in neutral medium?

Explanation: Neutral reducing agents like zinc dust and ammonium chloride reduce nitrobenzene selectively to N-phenylhydroxylamine.

Question 14 of 19

Primary aliphatic amines can be converted into isonitriles by heating with:

Explanation: The carbylamine reaction uses chloroform and potassium hydroxide to convert primary amines to isonitriles.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following compounds is most basic?

Explanation: Benzylamine (C6H5CH2NH2) has the amino group isolated from the benzene ring by a methylene group, preventing resonance delocalization and retaining high basicity.

Question 16 of 19

Reduction of alkyl cyanides with sodium and alcohol gives:

Explanation: Bouveault–Blanc reduction (Na/EtOH) of nitriles yields primary amines with an expanded carbon chain.

Question 17 of 19

Aromatic diazonium salts are usually prepared at what temperature range?

Explanation: Low temperatures (0–5°C) prevent the thermal decomposition of aromatic diazonium salts into phenols.

Question 18 of 19

Schiff base is formed by the condensation of a primary amine with:

Explanation: Primary amines condense with aldehydes or ketones to form imines, commonly referred to as Schiff bases.

Question 19 of 19

Which of the following is amphoteric in nature?

Explanation: Amino acids contain both basic amino groups and acidic carboxyl groups, making them amphoteric (zwitterionic).

Which nitrogen compound is most resistant to reduction to a primary amine?

Solution: Quaternary ammonium salts lack reducible functional groups and do not yield primary amines upon reduction.

When acetamide is treated with Br2 and KOH, the product is:

Solution: Acetamide (CH3CONH2) undergoes Hofmann degradation to form methylamine (CH3NH2) with loss of one carbon.

Question 6 of 10
The basic strength of amines is expressed correctly by which quantity?

Solution: Base dissociation constant pK_b is the standard metric; lower pK_b signifies higher basic strength.

Question 7 of 10
Why is aniline acetylated before nitration in mixed acid?

Solution: Acetylation protects and moderates the extremely activating amino group, preventing undesired multi-substitution and oxidative destruction.

Solution: Reaction of aliphatic primary amines with HNO2 releases nitrogen gas quantitatively.

Question 9 of 10

Solution: Benzylamine consists of a methylamine core substituted with a phenyl group, designated as phenylmethanamine.
