Tertiary Amines

Tertiary amines are organic nitrogen compounds in which all three hydrogen atoms of ammonia are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups, possessing a general formula R3N. Due to the presence of a lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom, they act as Lewis bases and nucleophiles.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structure and Hybridization

  • The nitrogen atom in tertiary amines is sp3 hybridized with a pyramidal geometry distorted by the lone pair.
  • The C-N-C bond angle ranges between 101° and 108°, depending on steric hindrance of the alkyl groups.
  • Tertiary amines with three different alkyl groups (chiral nitrogen) cannot be resolved into optical isomers due to rapid nitrogen inversion through a planar transition state.

Basicity and Nucleophilicity

  • In the gas phase, the basicity order of amines follows inductive effect: tertiary > secondary > primary > ammonia.
  • In aqueous solution, basicity is modulated by steric hindrance, inductive effect, and hydration energy of the resulting ammonium cation.
  • Tertiary amines lack N-H bonds, preventing them from forming intermolecular hydrogen bonds with each other, resulting in lower boiling points compared to primary and secondary amines of comparable molecular mass.

Chemical Reactions and Identification

  • Tertiary amines do not react with nitrous acid (HNO2) to form diazonium salts; aliphatic tertiary amines form water-soluble nitrites, while aromatic tertiary amines undergo C-nitrosation.
  • Treatment with alkyl halides yields quaternary ammonium salts via nucleophilic substitution (SN2).
  • Hinsberg test (benzene sulfonyl chloride) classifies amines: tertiary amines do not react and remain insoluble in alkali.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl inductive effect predicts tertiary amines to be the strongest bases in water.

Observation: Trimethylamine is often less basic in water than dimethylamine. — Steric hindrance to hydration of the protonated cation outweighs the electron-donating inductive effect in aqueous media.

Exception Amines with three different groups attached to nitrogen should exhibit optical activity.

Observation: Chiral tertiary amines undergo spontaneous racemization at room temperature. — The activation energy barrier for nitrogen inversion is very low (~25-42 kJ mol-1), causing rapid interconversion of enantiomers.

Exception Primary, secondary, and tertiary amines all react with benzenesulfonyl chloride (Hinsberg reagent).

Observation: Tertiary amines show no apparent reaction with benzenesulfonyl chloride. — Tertiary amines lack a replaceable hydrogen on the nitrogen atom to form a stable sulfonamide.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Tertiary amines lack N-H bonds and therefore cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with each other.
  • The hybridization of nitrogen in trimethylamine is sp3 with a pyramidal structure.
  • Aromatic tertiary amines such as N,N-dimethylaniline undergo electrophilic substitution (nitrosation) at the para position.
  • Quaternary ammonium salts formed by tertiary amines and alkyl halides are completely ionized in water.
  • Aliphatic tertiary amines react with nitrous acid to yield soluble nitrite salts.
  • Tertiary amines do not form stable adducts in the Hinsberg test due to the absence of replaceable N-H protons.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming tertiary amines have the highest basicity in aqueous solution just because they have three electron-donating alkyl groups.
  • Confusing gas-phase basicity order (3° > 2° > 1°) with aqueous basicity order.
  • Predicting optical activity for chiral tertiary amines without accounting for rapid nitrogen inversion.
  • Believing that tertiary amines react with Hinsberg reagent to form soluble products.

⚡ Quick Revision: Tertiary Amines

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Molecular Architecture

Tertiary amines feature a pyramidal sp3 nitrogen center bonded to three organic groups. Rapid umbrella-like nitrogen inversion prevents optical isolation of chiral tertiary amines.

Acid-Base Behavior

Basicity depends on inductive stabilization, steric inhibition of solvation, and solvent effects. In water, secondary amines often surpass tertiary amines in base strength.

Synthetic Utility

Tertiary amines serve as phase-transfer catalysts, nucleophilic catalysts in acylation reactions, and precursors for quaternary ammonium hydroxides via exhaustive alkylation.

Identification Tests

Distinguished from primary and secondary amines by failure to react with benzenesulfonyl chloride and nitrous acid under standard conditions.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Gas phase basicity: (CH3)3N > (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > NH3
  • Aqueous basicity (methyl series): (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > (CH3)3N > NH3
  • Hinsberg test result for tertiary amine: No reaction, insoluble layer
  • Nitrous acid reaction: Forms soluble nitrite salt, no N2 gas evolution
  • Boiling point trend: Primary > Secondary > Tertiary for isomeric amines

📌 Quick Recall

  • Tertiary amines lack N-H bonds, leading to lower boiling points.
  • Solvation effects dictate aqueous basicity anomalies.
  • They react with alkyl halides to form quaternary ammonium salts.
  • Chiral tertiary amines cannot be resolved due to fast nitrogen inversion.

⚖️ Key Differences

Gas phase basicity VS Aqueous solution basicity

Gas phase relies solely on +I inductive effect (3° max); aqueous phase includes solvation and steric factors.
