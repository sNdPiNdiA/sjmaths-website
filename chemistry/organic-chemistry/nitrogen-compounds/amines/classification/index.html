Classification of Amines

Amines are organic derivatives of ammonia characterized by the replacement of one or more hydrogen atoms by alkyl or aryl groups. They are systematically classified based on the number of organic substituents attached directly to the nitrogen atom and the structural nature of the substituent groups.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification Based on Substitution Degree

  • Primary (1°) amines possess the functional grouping −NH2 bonded to a single carbon atom.
  • Secondary (2°) amines feature a central nitrogen atom bound to two distinct carbon chains via >NH.
  • Tertiary (3°) amines contain a nitrogen atom fully substituted by three carbon chains as ≡N.
  • Degree of substitution refers directly to the number of carbon atoms bonded to the nitrogen atom, not the carbon attached to the chain.

Aliphatic versus Aromatic Amines

  • Aliphatic amines contain nitrogen bonded exclusively to sp3 hybridized alkyl carbon atoms.
  • Aromatic amines (arylamines) feature the nitrogen atom bonded directly to an sp2 hybridized carbon of an aromatic ring.
  • Benzylamines are classified distinctly because the nitrogen is attached to a side-chain sp3 carbon, exhibiting aliphatic chemical properties.
  • Resonance delocalization of the lone pair into the aromatic ring profoundly alters the basicity of aromatic amines compared to aliphatic counterparts.

Simple versus Mixed Amines

  • Simple amines contain identical alkyl or aryl groups attached to the nitrogen atom (e.g., trimethylamine).
  • Mixed amines feature two or three different alkyl or aryl substituents bonded to the same nitrogen atom (e.g., N-methylethanamine).
  • IUPAC nomenclature dictates that mixed secondary and tertiary amines are named as N-substituted derivatives of primary amines.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Tertiary amines have higher boiling points than alkanes of comparable molecular mass due to polarity.

Observation: Tertiary amines lack N−H bonds and cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds. — Absence of hydrogen bonding causes tertiary amines to have significantly lower boiling points than isomeric primary and secondary amines.

Exception Aliphatic amines are stronger bases than ammonia due to the electron-releasing inductive effect (+I) of alkyl groups.

Observation: In aqueous solution, secondary amines are sometimes more basic than tertiary amines. — Steric hindrance to hydration and destabilization of the protonated cation by steric crowding outweigh the electronic +I effect.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Degree Identification Rule
Count C-N bonds

Application: Count the number of direct carbon-nitrogen single bonds attached to the central N atom to immediately determine 1°, 2°, or 3° classification.

Hinsberg Reagent Separation Mnemonic
1° dissolves in alkali, 2° insoluble, 3° unreactive

Application: Use benzenesulfonyl chloride (Hinsberg reagent) to rapidly distinguish and separate primary, secondary, and tertiary amine mixtures.

IUPAC N-Prefix Rule
Largest group = parent; others = N-alkyl

Application: In secondary and tertiary mixed amines, assign the longest carbon chain as the parent alkane/amine and designate smaller chains with the N- prefix.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Primary amines contain the −NH2 functional group bonded to one alkyl or aryl group.
  • Secondary amines contain the >NH group bonded to two alkyl or aryl groups.
  • Tertiary amines contain the ≡N group bonded to three alkyl or aryl groups.
  • Quaternary ammonium salts contain four alkyl groups and carry a permanent positive charge on nitrogen.
  • Benzylamine is classified as an aliphatic amine because the nitrogen is attached to an sp3 carbon.
  • Aniline is the simplest aromatic amine where nitrogen is directly attached to the benzene ring.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing benzylamine with aniline; remember benzylamine is aliphatic while aniline is aromatic.
  • Counting total carbon atoms in the molecule instead of carbon atoms directly attached to nitrogen when determining amine degree.
  • Assuming tertiary amines can form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with themselves.
  • Neglecting the steric effect of bulky alkyl groups on amine basicity in aqueous media.

⚡ Quick Revision: Classification of Amines

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structural Classification

Amines are classified as primary, secondary, or tertiary based on the replacement of one, two, or three hydrogen atoms of ammonia by organic groups.

Aliphatic versus Aromatic

Aliphatic amines feature nitrogen bonded to sp3 carbons, whereas aromatic amines have nitrogen bonded directly to sp2 carbons of an aromatic ring.

Nomenclature Principles

Secondary and tertiary mixed amines are named using N-alkyl prefixes to designate smaller groups attached to the nitrogen atom.

Physical Property Variations

Intermolecular hydrogen bonding is strongest in primary amines, moderate in secondary amines, and absent in tertiary amines, affecting boiling points accordingly.

Tertiary Amine (R3N)
Compound with three alkyl/aryl groups attached to nitrogen.
Quaternary Ammonium Salt ([R4N]+X−)
Ionic compound with four alkyl groups on nitrogen.
Arylamine (Ar−NH2)
Aromatic amine with nitrogen directly attached to an aryl ring.
Hinsberg Reagent (C6H5SO2Cl)
Benzenesulfonyl chloride used to distinguish amine classes.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Degree of amine depends on C−N bonds to nitrogen, not the nature of alkyl groups.
  • Primary amines form intermolecular hydrogen bonds, raising their boiling points.
  • Tertiary amines cannot form hydrogen bonds with each other.
  • Benzylamine is an aliphatic amine, not an aromatic amine.
  • Quaternary ammonium hydroxides are strong bases comparable to sodium hydroxide.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Amines derive from ammonia via substitution.
  • Classification splits into 1°, 2°, and 3° categories.
  • Aliphatic and aromatic amines show different chemical behaviors.
  • Hinsberg test separates amine classes effectively.

⚖️ Key Differences

Benzylamine VS Aniline

Benzylamine has −CH2− between ring and N (aliphatic); Aniline has N directly on the ring (aromatic).

Question 1 of 19

What is the structural classification of isopropylamine?

Explanation: Isopropylamine has the formula (CH3)2CH−NH2, where nitrogen is attached to one carbon atom, making it a primary amine.

Question 2 of 19

Which of the following is a secondary amine?

Explanation: Diethylamine has two ethyl groups attached to the nitrogen atom (CH3CH2)2NH, classifying it as a secondary amine.

Question 3 of 19

Benzylamine is classified as which type of amine?

Explanation: In benzylamine, the nitrogen atom is bonded to an sp3 hybridized methylene carbon (−CH2−), classifying it as an aliphatic amine.

Question 4 of 19

What is the general formula of a quaternary ammonium salt?

Explanation: Quaternary ammonium salts contain four alkyl groups bonded to nitrogen, creating a permanent positive charge balanced by an anion X−.

Question 5 of 19

Which amine does not react with Hinsberg's reagent?

Explanation: Tertiary amines lack a hydrogen atom attached to nitrogen, so they cannot form sulfonamides and do not react with benzenesulfonyl chloride.

Question 6 of 19

Aniline is an example of which type of amine?

Explanation: Aniline (C6H5NH2) has a primary amino group directly attached to an aromatic sp2 carbon, making it an aromatic primary amine.

Question 7 of 19

What is the IUPAC name of CH3−NH−CH2CH3?

Explanation: The longer chain is ethane (ethanamine) and the smaller group on nitrogen is methyl, giving N-methylethanamine.

Question 8 of 19

Which of the following amines has the highest boiling point among isomers of molecular formula C3H9N?

Explanation: Propan-1-amine is a primary amine capable of extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding compared to secondary or tertiary isomers.

Question 9 of 19

What reagent is used to distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary amines?

Explanation: Hinsberg reagent (benzenesulfonyl chloride) reacts differently with 1°, 2°, and 3° amines, allowing their separation and identification.

Question 10 of 19

In the Hinsberg test, the derivative formed from a secondary amine is:

Explanation: Secondary amine sulfonamides lack acidic protons on nitrogen, making them insoluble in aqueous alkali.

Question 11 of 19

Which of the following is a heterocyclic amine?

Explanation: Pyridine contains a nitrogen atom as part of a ring structure, classifying it as a heterocyclic amine.

Question 12 of 19

How many alkyl groups are attached to the nitrogen atom in a tertiary amine?

Explanation: A tertiary amine has three alkyl or aryl groups attached directly to the nitrogen atom.

Question 13 of 19

Which compound is a mixed secondary amine?

Explanation: N-methylpropan-1-amine contains two different alkyl groups (methyl and propyl) attached to nitrogen.

Question 14 of 19

What is the hybridization of the nitrogen atom in alkylamines?

Explanation: Aliphatic amine nitrogen atoms are sp3 hybridized with a pyramidal geometry.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following classes of amines reacts with nitrous acid to form stable diazonium salts at low temperatures (0-5°C)?

Explanation: Aromatic primary amines react with HNO2 at 0-5°C to form stable aryldiazonium salts.

Question 16 of 19

What is the product formed when a primary aliphatic amine reacts with nitrous acid?

Explanation: Aliphatic primary amines react with HNO2 to form unstable aliphatic diazonium salts which decompose to yield alcohols and nitrogen gas.

Question 17 of 19

Secondary amines react with nitrous acid to form:

Explanation: Secondary amines react with nitrous acid to form yellow oily N-nitrosamines.

Question 18 of 19

Which functional group represents a primary amine?

Explanation: The −NH2 group is the characteristic functional group of primary amines.

Question 19 of 19

Which of the following is classified as a tertiary amine?

Explanation: N,N-dimethylmethanamine (trimethylamine) has three methyl groups attached to the nitrogen atom.
