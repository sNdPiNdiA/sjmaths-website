Distinction between Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary Amines

Primary, secondary, and tertiary amines are classified based on the number of alkyl or aryl groups attached to the nitrogen atom. Chemical distinction relies on differential reactivity toward specific reagents such as benzenesulfonyl chloride, nitrous acid, and oxalic ester.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Hinsberg Test for Amine Separation

  • Benzenesulfonyl chloride (C6H5SO2Cl) reacts with primary amines to form N-alkylbenzenesulfonamide, which dissolves in alkali due to the acidic hydrogen on nitrogen.
  • Secondary amines react with Hinsberg reagent to form N,N-dialkylbenzenesulfonamides, which lack acidic hydrogen and are insoluble in alkali.
  • Tertiary amines do not react with benzenesulfonyl chloride under standard conditions because they lack an N−H bond.

Action of Nitrous Acid (HNO2)

  • Primary aliphatic amines react with cold HNO2 (HNO2 generated in situ from NaNO2 + HCl at 0–5°C) to yield unstable diazonium salts that rapidly decompose to evolve nitrogen gas (N2) and form alcohols.
  • Primary aromatic amines form stable diazonium salts (Ar−N2+Cl−) at 0–5°C.
  • Secondary amines (both aliphatic and aromatic) react with HNO2 to form yellow, oily N-nitrosamines (R2N−N=O).
  • Tertiary aliphatic amines dissolve in HNO2 to form water-soluble tertiary amine salts without gas evolution or nitrosation.

Hoffmann's Mustard Oil Reaction and Acetylation

  • Primary amines react with carbon disulfide (CS2) and mercuric chloride (HgCl2) to form alkyl isothiocyanates, which possess the characteristic smell of mustard.
  • Secondary amines give a dithiocarbamate salt with CS2, but do not yield isothiocyanates upon treatment with HgCl2.
  • Tertiary amines do not react with carbon disulfide.
  • Acetylation using acetic anhydride or acetyl chloride converts primary and secondary amines into solid amides, while tertiary amines show no reaction.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Tertiary amines do not react with Hinsberg reagent.

Observation: Tertiary aliphatic amines undergo unexpected cleavage or side reactions if impurities like secondary amines are present. — Commercial samples of tertiary amines frequently contain primary and secondary amine impurities that give false-positive reactions.

Exception Primary aromatic amines form stable diazonium salts at 0–5°C.

Observation: Primary aliphatic amines do not form isolable diazonium salts; they eliminate N2 instantly. — Aliphatic diazonium ions are extremely unstable due to rapid loss of nitrogen gas to form highly reactive primary carbocations.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Hinsberg Solubility Mnemonic
1° = Soluble in alkali, 2° = Insoluble in alkali, 3° = No reaction

Application: Use this sequence during qualitative analysis test flow charts: add Hinsberg reagent, test alkali solubility of precipitate.

Nitrous Acid Gas Evolution
1° amine + HNO2 → N2 gas bubble

Application: Evolution of nitrogen gas upon adding NaNO2/HCl at 0°C instantly confirms a primary aliphatic amine.

N-Nitrosamine Color Tag
2° amine + HNO2 → Yellow oily layer

Application: Formation of a distinct yellow oily or liquid layer floating on the aqueous phase points directly to a secondary amine.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Carbylamine test is exclusively given by primary amines (both aliphatic and aromatic) yielding foul-smelling isocyanides.
  • Hinsberg reagent is benzenesulfonyl chloride, C6H5SO2Cl.
  • Secondary amines form N-nitrosamines with nitrous acid, which turn red with Liebermann's nitroso test.
  • Tertiary aliphatic amines form quaternary ammonium salts when treated with alkyl halides (exhaustive alkylation).
  • Acetylation of secondary amines yields N,N-dialkylacetamides which lack acidic protons.
  • Primary aliphatic amines evolve nitrogen gas quantitatively with nitrous acid, useful for measuring amino acid content (Van Slyke method).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the solubility of Hinsberg derivatives: 1° derivative is soluble in KOH, 2° derivative is insoluble.
  • Assuming tertiary amines react with nitrous acid to form N-nitrosamines; only secondary amines form nitrosamines.
  • Forgetting that aromatic tertiary amines undergo ring nitrosation or electrophilic substitution instead of reacting at the nitrogen atom.
  • Confusing carbylamine test as a general test for all amines; it only detects primary amines.

⚡ Quick Revision: Distinction between Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary Amines

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Classification and Structure

Amines are classified as 1°, 2°, or 3° depending on replacement of one, two, or three hydrogen atoms of ammonia by organic groups.

Hinsberg Separation Protocol

Benzenesulfonyl chloride acts as a chemical screening agent separating amine mixtures based on alkali solubility of the resulting sulfonamides.

Nitrous Acid Probes

Reaction with HNO2 distinguishes amine types through evolution of N2 gas (1°), formation of yellow oily nitrosamines (2°), or simple salt formation (3°).

Carbylamine Diagnostic Test

Treatment with chloroform and alcoholic KOH produces foul-smelling isocyanides specifically for primary amines.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • 1° amine + Hinsberg reagent → Alkali-soluble sulfonamide
  • 2° amine + Hinsberg reagent → Alkali-insoluble sulfonamide
  • 3° amine + Hinsberg reagent → No reaction
  • 1° amine + CHCl3 + KOH → Foul isocyanide
  • 2° amine + HNO2 → Yellow oily N-nitrosamine

📌 Quick Recall

  • Primary amines react with Hinsberg reagent to form alkali-soluble derivatives.
  • Secondary amines form alkali-insoluble sulfonamides and yellow oils with nitrous acid.
  • Tertiary amines lack N−H bonds, failing Hinsberg and carbylamine tests.
  • Carbylamine test is specific for primary amines.

⚖️ Key Differences

Hinsberg Reagent VS Lucas Reagent

Hinsberg reagent (C6H5SO2Cl) distinguishes amines; Lucas reagent (conc. HCl / anhydrous ZnCl2) distinguishes alcohols.
