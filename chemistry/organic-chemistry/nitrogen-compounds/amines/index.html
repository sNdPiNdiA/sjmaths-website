Organic Chemistry

Amines

Amines are derivatives of ammonia in which one or more hydrogen atoms are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups. They act as both weak bases and nucleophiles due to the presence of a lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Basicity of Amines

  • The basicity of amines depends on the ease of availability of the lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom.
  • In the gas phase, the basicity order follows inductive effect: 3° > 2° > 1° > NH3.
  • In aqueous solution, basicity is governed by a combination of inductive effect, solvation effect, and steric hindrance.
  • For aliphatic methylamines in water, the order is 2° > 1° > 3° > NH3, while for ethylamines it is 2° > 3° > 1° > NH3.

Preparation Methods

  • Reduction of nitro compounds using Fe/HCl or Sn/HCl yields primary aromatic amines efficiently.
  • Ammonolysis of alkyl halides leads to a mixture of primary, secondary, tertiary amines, and quaternary ammonium salts.
  • Gabriel Phthalimide synthesis specifically yields pure primary aliphatic amines without contamination by secondary or tertiary amines.
  • Reduction of nitriles with LiAlH4 or catalytic hydrogenation produces primary amines with an extended carbon chain.

Chemical Reactions and Distinguishing Tests

  • Primary amines react with chloroform and alcoholic KOH to form foul-smelling carbylamines (isocyanides).
  • Hinsberg test uses benzenesulfonyl chloride; primary amine derivatives are soluble in alkali, secondary are insoluble, and tertiary do not react.
  • Aromatic primary amines undergo diazotization with nitrous acid at 0 – 5°C to form stable diazonium salts.
  • Electrophilic aromatic substitution in aniline yields 2,4,6-tribromoaniline instantly upon treatment with bromine water due to strong +R activation.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aliphatic amines are generally stronger bases than ammonia due to +I effect.

Observation: Aromatic amines (such as aniline) are significantly weaker bases than ammonia. — The lone pair on nitrogen in aniline is delocalized into the benzene ring via resonance, making it less available for protonation.

Exception Primary amines react with nitrous acid (HNO2) to form stable aliphatic diazonium salts.

Observation: Aliphatic primary amines form unstable diazonium salts that immediately decompose to yield carbocations and nitrogen gas. — Aliphatic diazonium ions lack resonance stabilization and rapidly lose N2.

Exception Aniline undergoes standard nitration with concentrated HNO3 and H2SO4 to give ortho and para nitro derivatives.

Observation: Direct nitration of aniline also yields a substantial amount of m-nitroaniline (47%). — In strongly acidic medium, aniline is protonated to form the anilinium ion, which is meta-directing due to -I effect.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Aniline on acetylation with acetic anhydride gives acetanilide, which protects the amino group during ring halogenation or nitration.
  • Carbylamine test is exclusively given by primary amines (both aliphatic and aromatic).
  • Quaternary ammonium salts contain four alkyl groups attached to nitrogen and possess a permanent positive charge.
  • Reduction of nitrobenzene in neutral medium (Zn/NH4Cl) gives N-phenylhydroxylamine.
  • Hoffmann bromamide degradation results in the loss of the carbonyl carbon as carbonate, shortening the carbon chain by one.
  • Coupling reactions of diazonium salts with phenol or aniline proceed via electrophilic aromatic substitution at the para position.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the basicity order of aliphatic amines in gas phase versus aqueous solution.
  • Assuming tertiary amines react with Hinsberg reagent to form alkali-soluble sulfonamides.
  • Forgetting that aniline undergoes oxidation upon standing in air, turning dark brown/black.
  • Using direct halogenation of aniline without acetylation, which yields 2,4,6-tribromoaniline uncontrollably.

⚡ Quick Revision: Amines

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Classification and Nomenclature

Amines are classified as primary, secondary, or tertiary based on the number of organic substituents attached to the nitrogen atom. IUPAC nomenclature designates them as alkanamines with appropriate locants.

Basicity and Electronic Effects

The availability of the unshared electron pair dictates basic strength. Electron-releasing groups increase basicity while electron-withdrawing groups decrease it.

Synthetic Routes

Key synthetic pathways include reduction of nitro compounds, nitriles, and amides, alongside nucleophilic substitution methods such as Gabriel phthalimide synthesis and Hoffmann degradation.

Diazonium Chemistry

Aromatic primary amines form diazonium salts which act as versatile synthetic intermediates for introducing diverse functional groups into aromatic rings via Sandmeyer and coupling reactions.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Primary Amine (R-NH2)
An organic compound containing the -NH2 group attached to an alkyl or aryl group.
Secondary Amine (R2NH)
An amine with two organic substituents bonded to nitrogen.
Tertiary Amine (R3N)
An amine with three organic substituents bonded to nitrogen.
Diazonium Salt (Ar-N2+X-)
Compound containing the characteristic -N2+ group.
Hinsberg Reagent (C6H5SO2Cl)
Benzenesulfonyl chloride used to distinguish amine classes.
Carbylamine (R-NC)
Isocyanide compound characterized by an extremely foul odor.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Gas phase basicity: 3° > 2° > 1° > NH3
  • Aqueous methylamine basicity: 2° > 1° > 3° > NH3
  • Diazotization temperature: 0 – 5°C
  • Gabriel phthalimide product: Pure 1° aliphatic amine
  • Hinsberg 1° amine derivative is alkali soluble

📌 Quick Recall

  • Amines function as Lewis bases and nucleophiles.
  • Solvation and steric factors modify the inductive basicity trend in water.
  • Hinsberg reagent effectively separates 1°, 2°, and 3° amines.
  • Aromatic diazonium salts enable nucleophilic substitution on benzene rings.

⚖️ Key Differences

Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis VS Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation

Gabriel synthesis prepares primary amines without carbon loss, whereas Hoffmann degradation shortens the carbon chain by one carbon.

Aromatic amines are weaker bases than ammonia because:

Explanation: Resonance stabilization in aniline involves delocalization of the nitrogen lone pair into the aromatic ring, reducing its availability for protonation.

Question 13 of 19

Explanation: Gabriel phthalimide synthesis avoids the formation of secondary and tertiary amines, yielding pure primary aliphatic amines.

Explanation: Aliphatic primary amines react with HNO2 to form unstable diazonium salts which decompose to yield alcohols and nitrogen gas.

Question 15 of 19

Which reagent is best suited to convert propanamide to ethanamine?

What is the primary product formed when nitrobenzene is reduced with Sn and concentrated HCl?

Solution: Reduction of nitrobenzene using active metal and acid (Sn/HCl) yields aniline.

Question 2 of 10
Which of the following amines is most basic in the gas phase?

Solution: In the gas phase, electron-donating alkyl groups stabilize the positive ammonium ion by inductive effect, making tertiary amines the strongest bases.

Question 3 of 10
During the diazotization of aniline with NaNO2 and HCl, what is the purpose of maintaining low temperature (0 – 5°C)?

Solution: Aromatic diazonium salts are thermally unstable and react with water at higher temperatures to form phenol.

Question 4 of 10
Which amine reacts with nitrous acid to form an N-nitrosamine which appears as a yellow oily layer?

Solution: Secondary amines react with HNO2 to form N-nitrosamines, which are insoluble yellow oily liquids.

Question 5 of 10
The conversion of acetamide to methanamine involves which named reaction?

Solution: Conversion of an amide to a primary amine with loss of one carbon is Hoffmann bromamide degradation.

Question 6 of 10
What is the product of coupling reaction between benzenediazonium chloride and aniline in acidic medium?

Solution: Coupling of benzenediazonium chloride with aniline yields p-aminoazobenzene as a bright yellow-orange dye.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following is least basic among ethylamines in aqueous solution?

Solution: Ammonia is less basic than all ethylamines in aqueous solution because ethyl groups exert a strong electron-releasing inductive effect.

Question 8 of 10
In the Hinsberg test, the sulfonamide derivative formed from a secondary amine is:

Solution: Secondary amine sulfonamides lack an acidic hydrogen on the nitrogen atom, rendering them insoluble in aqueous KOH.

Question 9 of 10
Which reaction is used to convert benzene diazonium chloride to chlorobenzene?

Solution: Both Sandmeyer reaction (CuCl/HCl) and Gattermann reaction (Cu/HCl) convert benzenediazonium chloride to chlorobenzene.

Question 10 of 10
What is the hybridization of the nitrogen atom in trimethylamine?

Solution: The nitrogen atom in trimethylamine forms three sigma bonds and holds one lone pair, resulting in tetrahedral geometry with sp3 hybridization.
