Organic Chemistry

Nitrogen Compounds

Organic nitrogen compounds comprise amines, diazonium salts, cyanides, and nitro compounds containing trivalent nitrogen bonded to carbon and hydrogen. Their chemical behavior is dictated by the presence of a lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom and variations in electronegativity.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification and Basicity of Amines

  • Amines are classified as primary (1°), secondary (2°), or tertiary (3°) depending on the number of alkyl or aryl groups attached to the nitrogen atom.
  • The basicity of amines in aqueous solution is governed by a combination of inductive effect, steric hindrance, and hydration enthalpy of the substituted ammonium cation.
  • For aliphatic methylamines in water, the basicity order is (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > (CH3)3N, whereas for ethylamines it is (C2H5)2NH > (C2H5)3N > C2H5NH2.
  • Aromatic amines like aniline are significantly weaker bases than aliphatic amines due to the resonance stabilization of the free amine where the lone pair is delocalized into the benzene ring.

Synthetic Routes and Chemical Reactions of Amines

  • Reduction of nitro compounds using catalytic hydrogenation (H2/Ni, Pd, or Pt) or metal-acid systems (Fe/HCl or Sn/HCl) yields primary aromatic amines.
  • Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis is exclusively used for the preparation of pure aliphatic primary amines by reacting potassium phthalimide with alkyl halides followed by alkaline hydrolysis.
  • Hinsberg's reagent (benzenesulfonyl chloride) reacts with 1° amines to form soluble sulfonamides, with 2° amines to form insoluble sulfonamides, and does not react with 3° amines.
  • Primary aliphatic amines react with nitrous acid (HNO2) at 0-5°C to form unstable diazonium salts that rapidly decompose to yield alcohols and N2 gas.

Diazonium Salts and Cyanides

  • Arenediazonium salts are stabilized by resonance within the aromatic ring and are generally stable at temperatures between 0°C and 5°C.
  • Sandmeyer reaction involves the conversion of aryldiazonium salts to aryl chlorides, bromides, or cyanides using corresponding cuprous halide (CuX) or CuCN catalysts.
  • Coupling reactions of diazonium salts with phenols and aromatic amines yield brightly colored azo dyes through electrophilic aromatic substitution at the para position.
  • Alkyl cyanides (nitriles) undergo complete acid or base catalyzed hydrolysis to yield carboxylic acids, and reduction with Na/EtOH yields primary amines (mendius reduction).

Carbylamine Reaction
R-NH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH → R-NC + 3KCl + 3H2O
Conditions: Aliphatic or aromatic primary amine heated with chloroform and alcoholic KOH
Units: Qualitative test yielding foul-smelling isocyanide
Diazotization Reaction
C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2HCl → [C6H5N2]+Cl- + NaCl + 2H2O
Conditions: Primary aromatic amine at 0 - 5 °C
Units: Synthesis of benzenediazonium chloride
Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation
R-CONH2 + Br2 + 4NaOH → R-NH2 + Na2CO3 + 2NaBr + 2H2O
Conditions: Amide heated with bromine and caustic alkali
Units: Step-down reaction yielding a primary amine with one fewer carbon atom
Hinsberg Reagent Test Reaction
R-NH2 + C6H5SO2Cl → C6H5SO2NH-R + HCl
Conditions: Primary amine with benzene sulfonyl chloride in aqueous alkali
Units: Stoichiometric reaction yielding alkali-soluble sulfonamide
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aliphatic amines are stronger bases than aromatic amines due to electron-donating alkyl groups.

Observation: Aniline is a much weaker base (pK_b = 9.38) than ammonia (pK_b = 4.75). — Resonance delocalization of the nitrogen lone pair into the aromatic ring decreases its availability for protonation, and the anilinium ion is less resonance-stabilized than neutral aniline.

Exception Tertiary amines should have the highest basicity due to cumulative +I effect of three alkyl groups.

Observation: Tertiary amines often show lower or comparable basicity to secondary amines in aqueous media. — Steric hindrance prevents effective solvation of the bulky tertiary ammonium cation by water molecules, destabilizing the conjugate acid.

Exception Primary alkyl halides react with ammonia to yield exclusively primary amines.

Observation: Ammonolysis of alkyl halides yields a complex mixture of primary, secondary, tertiary amines, and quaternary ammonium salts. — The newly formed primary amine also acts as a nucleophile and attacks remaining alkyl halide molecules.

Application: Exclusively detects primary amines (both aliphatic and aromatic). Secondary and tertiary amines do not give this test.

Application: Use this reaction immediately when a question asks to convert an amide to an amine with one less carbon atom in the chain.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Aniline undergoes electrophilic substitution reactions like bromination and nitration vigorously; protection of the amino group via acetylation is required to control monosubstitution.
  • The reaction of aniline with bromine water at room temperature rapidly yields 2,4,6-tribromoaniline as a white precipitate.
  • Gabriel Phthalimide synthesis cannot be used to prepare aromatic primary amines because aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with potassium phthalimide under mild conditions.
  • Mendius reduction converts alkyl cyanides to primary amines using sodium and ethanol (Na/C2H5OH).
  • Diazonium salts react with HBF4 followed by heating (Schieme reaction) to selectively introduce fluorine into an aromatic ring.
  • Nitrobenzene undergoes reduction in neutral medium with Zn dust and NH4Cl to form N-phenylhydroxylamine.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the basicity order of methylamines and ethylamines in aqueous solution.
  • Assuming aromatic primary amines can be prepared cleanly by reacting aryl halides with ammonia without high temperature and copper catalysts.
  • Forgetting that tertiary amines do not react with Hinsberg's reagent or nitrous acid to form stable derivatives.
  • Ignoring the necessity of low temperature (0-5°C) during diazotization, leading to the hydrolysis of diazonium salt to phenol.

⚡ Quick Revision: Nitrogen Compounds

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Classification and Nomenclature

Amines are derivatives of ammonia where one or more hydrogen atoms are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups. IUPAC naming suffixes use 'amine' with positional locants.

Basicity Parameters

Basicity depends on electron-donating inductive effects, steric hindrance to solvation, and resonance stabilization in aromatic systems. K_b and pK_b values quantify this strength.

Preparation and Degradation

Synthesized via nitro reduction, nitrile reduction, ammonolysis, and Hofmann degradation. Hofmann degradation results in carbon chain shortening by one carbon.

Diazonium Chemistry

Arenediazonium salts undergo substitution (Sandmeyer, Gattermann, Schiemann) and coupling reactions, serving as versatile intermediates for diverse aromatic derivatives.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Primary Amine (R-NH2)
Organic compound containing one alkyl/aryl group attached to nitrogen.
Hinsberg Reagent (C6H5SO2Cl)
Benzenesulfonyl chloride used to distinguish 1°, 2°, and 3° amines.
Diazotization (Ar-NH2 → Ar-N2+Cl-)
Conversion of aromatic primary amine to diazonium salt using HNO2 at 0-5°C.
Carbylamine (R-NC)
Isonitrile compound characterized by an extremely foul smell.
Coupling Reaction (Ar-N2+ + Ar'-H → Ar-N=N-Ar')
Electrophilic substitution forming brightly colored azo dyes.
Gabriel Synthesis (Phthalimide route)
Method to synthesize pure aliphatic primary amines.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Aliphatic amines are stronger bases than ammonia; aromatic amines are weaker bases.
  • Diazonium salt synthesis must be performed strictly at 0 to 5 °C.
  • Hinsberg test: 1° soluble in alkali, 2° insoluble, 3° no reaction.
  • Carbylamine test is specific for primary amines.
  • Hofmann bromamide degradation decreases the carbon chain length by one.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Amines exhibit basic properties due to the nitrogen lone pair.
  • Basicity is a delicate balance of inductive, steric, and solvation effects.
  • Diazonium salts bridge aliphatic and aromatic synthetic pathways.
  • Distinguishing tests like Hinsberg and Carbylamine are crucial for identification.

⚖️ Key Differences

Sandmeyer Reaction VS Gattermann Reaction

Sandmeyer uses Cu(I) salts (CuCl/CuBr) with HX, whereas Gattermann uses copper powder with HX.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following represents the correct basicity order of methylamines in aqueous solution?

Explanation: In aqueous medium, secondary methylamine is most basic due to balanced inductive effect and solvation stabilization, followed by primary and tertiary.

Question 2 of 18

Which reagent is used in the Carbylamine test?

Explanation: The Carbylamine test requires heating a primary amine with chloroform and alcoholic potassium hydroxide to produce a foul-smelling isocyanide.

Question 3 of 18

Gabriel phthalimide synthesis is used for the preparation of:

Explanation: Gabriel synthesis yields pure primary aliphatic amines via nucleophilic substitution of alkyl halides by potassium phthalimide.

Question 4 of 18

What is the product formed when aniline reacts with bromine water at room temperature?

Explanation: The activating effect of the -NH2 group is so strong that bromine water instantly precipitates 2,4,6-tribromoaniline.

Question 5 of 18

Which amine does not react with Hinsberg reagent?

Explanation: Tertiary amines lack a replaceable hydrogen on the nitrogen atom and therefore do not react with benzenesulfonyl chloride.

Question 6 of 18

In the Hofmann bromamide degradation reaction, acetamide is converted into:

Explanation: Hofmann bromamide degradation removes the carbonyl carbon as carbonate, converting acetamide (2 carbons) into methylamine (1 carbon).

Question 7 of 18

Benzenediazonium chloride reacts with phenol in alkaline medium to give:

Explanation: Coupling reaction of benzenediazonium chloride with phenol in alkaline medium yields p-hydroxyazobenzene, an orange-yellow azo dye.

Question 8 of 18

Which of the following compounds has the lowest boiling point?

Explanation: Tertiary amines cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with one another because they lack N-H bonds, resulting in lower boiling points compared to 1° and 2° amines.

Question 9 of 18

Reduction of alkyl cyanide with sodium and ethanol yields:

Explanation: Mendius reduction reduces alkyl cyanides (R-CN) to primary amines (R-CH2-NH2).

Question 10 of 18

Which catalyst is used in the Sandmeyer reaction for preparing chlorobenzene from benzenediazonium chloride?

Explanation: The Sandmeyer reaction utilizes cuprous chloride (Cu2Cl2) in hydrochloric acid to convert diazonium salt to chlorobenzene.

Question 11 of 18

The correct IUPAC name of CH3-NH-CH(CH3)2 is:

Explanation: The longest carbon chain contains 3 carbons (propane), with an N-methyl substituent attached to carbon 2, giving N-methylpropan-2-amine.

Question 12 of 18

Which reaction can be used to introduce a fluorine atom into an aromatic ring?

Explanation: The Balz-Schiemann reaction involves treating a diazonium salt with fluoroboric acid (HBF4) followed by thermal decomposition to form aryl fluoride.

Question 13 of 18

Aniline is usually prepared by the reduction of nitrobenzene using:

Explanation: Both metal-acid systems (Sn/HCl or Fe/HCl) effectively reduce nitrobenzene to aniline, though Fe/HCl is preferred industrially as FeCl2 formed can be hydrolyzed.

Question 14 of 18

Which of the following nitro compounds does not show tautomerism?

Explanation: 2-methyl-2-nitropropane (tert-nitrobutane) lacks alpha-hydrogen atoms, making aci-nitro tautomerism impossible.

Question 15 of 18

Primary amines react with nitrous acid to form:

Explanation: Reaction with HNO2 at 0-5°C forms unstable aliphatic diazonium salts that rapidly lose N2 to yield alcohols, while aromatic ones form stable salts.

Question 16 of 18

Nitrobenzene on reduction with Zn dust and aqueous NaOH gives:

Explanation: Reduction of nitrobenzene with zinc and sodium hydroxide yields azoxybenzene as an intermediate product.

Question 17 of 18

The hybridization of nitrogen in aliphatic primary amines is:

Explanation: The nitrogen atom in primary amines forms three sigma bonds and holds one lone pair, exhibiting tetrahedral geometry with sp3 hybridization.

Question 18 of 18

What is the end product when acetonitrile is hydrolyzed completely with aqueous acid?

Explanation: Complete acid hydrolysis of acetonitrile yields acetic acid and ammonium salts.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following amines is most basic in the gas phase?

Solution: In the gas phase, basicity increases with the number of electron-donating alkyl groups due to cumulative +I effect, making tertiary amines most basic.

Question 2 of 10
What is the product when nitrobenzene is reduced with alkaline stannous chloride or zinc/NaOH?

Solution: Alkaline reduction or bimolecular reduction of nitrobenzene leads to azobenzene formation.

Question 3 of 10
The conversion of benzene diazonium chloride to chlorobenzene using Cu/HCl is known as:

Solution: Using copper powder (Cu) with HCl instead of copper(I) chloride defines the Gattermann reaction.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following molecules has a pyramidal geometry similar to ammonia?

Solution: Trimethylamine is sp3 hybridized with a lone pair on nitrogen, giving it a pyramidal geometry.

Question 5 of 10
During nitration of aniline, large amounts of m-nitroaniline are formed because:

Solution: Strong acid protonates aniline to form the anilinium ion, carrying a positive charge on nitrogen that exerts a strong -I effect, directing incoming electrophiles to the meta position.

Question 6 of 10
Which compound is obtained when acetamide is treated with LiAlH4?

Solution: Reduction of an amide with lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4) reduces the carbonyl oxygen to CH2 without changing carbon chain length, yielding ethylamine.

Question 7 of 10
Carbylamine test is positive for:

Solution: The carbylamine test is a qualitative test characteristic for all primary amines, both aliphatic and aromatic.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following does not react with nitrous acid to yield nitrogen gas?

Solution: Diethylamine is a secondary amine and reacts with nitrous acid to form N-nitrosamines, without releasing nitrogen gas.

Question 9 of 10
What happens when nitroethane is treated with nitrous acid (HNO2)?

Solution: Primary nitroalkanes react with nitrous acid to form nitrolic acids which dissolve in NaOH to give red solutions.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following functional groups has the highest priority in IUPAC nomenclature when naming amines?

Solution: Carboxylic acid has higher priority than amino, alcohol, and halogen groups, leading to amino acids where -NH2 acts as a substituent.
