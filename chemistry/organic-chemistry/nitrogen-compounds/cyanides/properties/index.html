Properties of Cyanides

Cyanides are organonitrogen compounds containing the functional group -C≡N, exhibiting polarity due to the high electronegativity of nitrogen. They undergo hydrolysis, reduction, and nucleophilic addition reactions characteristic of both nitriles and associated cyanate species.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Physical Properties and Bonding in Nitriles

  • The nitrile carbon and nitrogen are sp hybridized, creating a linear C-C≡N geometry with a bond angle of 180°.
  • Nitriles possess a high dipole moment (approx. 3.5 to 4.0 D) due to the large electronegativity difference between carbon and nitrogen.
  • Lower aliphatic nitriles are water-soluble due to hydrogen bonding with water molecules, but solubility decreases sharply as the alkyl chain lengthens.
  • Boiling points of nitriles are significantly higher than corresponding alkanes and alkyl halides of comparable molecular mass due to dipole-dipole attractions.

Hydrolysis and Solvolysis Reactions

  • Complete acid or base-catalyzed hydrolysis of nitriles yields carboxylic acids along with an ammonium salt as a byproduct.
  • Partial hydrolysis stops at the amide stage when treated with alkaline hydrogen peroxide or cold concentrated hydrochloric acid.
  • Alcoholysis of nitriles in the presence of dry HCl gas produces imino ether hydrochlorides, which react with water to yield esters (Pinner synthesis).

Reduction and Organometallic Addition Reactions

  • Catalytic hydrogenation or reduction with LiAlH4 converts nitriles directly into primary amines.
  • The Stephen reaction uses stannous chloride and hydrochloric acid to reduce nitriles selectively to aldimines, which hydrolyze to aldehydes.
  • Reaction of nitriles with Grignard reagents followed by hydrolysis forms ketones via an intermediate ketimine complex.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl halides react with KCN to form predominantly covalent alkyl cyanides (nitriles).

Observation: Reaction of alkyl halides with AgCN yields predominantly isocyanides (carbylamines). — AgCN is predominantly covalent with silver showing high affinity for carbon, leaving the nitrogen lone pair available for nucleophilic attack.

Exception Reduction of nitriles with standard mild reducing agents yields primary amines.

Observation: Reduction using DIBAL-H at low temperatures followed by hydrolysis yields aldehydes instead of amines. — DIBAL-H delivers a single hydride ion to form a stable intermediate aluminum complex that hydrolyzes exclusively to the aldehyde stage.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Nitriles contain sp hybridized carbon and nitrogen atoms with a linear geometry.
  • The Stephen reaction converts alkyl cyanides into aldehydes using SnCl2 and HCl.
  • Partial hydrolysis of cyanides yields amides, whereas complete hydrolysis yields carboxylic acids.
  • Reduction of nitriles with LiAlH4 or catalytic hydrogenation produces primary amines.
  • Reaction with Grignard reagents followed by acid hydrolysis yields ketones.
  • Alkyl cyanides undergo alkaline hydrolysis much faster than corresponding amides under specific conditions.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the products of AgCN and KCN reactions with alkyl halides; AgCN gives isocyanides while KCN gives nitriles.
  • Forgetting that Stephen reaction requires subsequent aqueous hydrolysis to liberate the final aldehyde.
  • Assuming DIBAL-H reduction always yields amines; at low temperatures with nitriles, it halts at the aldehyde stage.
  • Neglecting to count the nitrile carbon atom when determining total carbon atoms in IUPAC nomenclature.

⚡ Quick Revision: Properties of Cyanides

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

Nitriles feature a polar carbon-nitrogen triple bond with sp hybridization, resulting in high boiling points and strong dipole moments.

Hydrolysis Pathways

Acid or base-catalyzed hydrolysis converts nitriles through amides into carboxylic acids, serving as a key chain-functionalization route.

Reduction Chemistry

Depending on the reagent (SnCl2, LiAlH4, or DIBAL-H), reductions yield either primary amines, aldehydes, or intermediate imines.

Nucleophilic Additions

Grignard reagents add across the polar C≡N triple bond to form ketimines, which readily hydrolyze to yield ketones.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Nitrile (RCN)
An organic compound containing the -C≡N functional group.
Isonitrile (RNC)
An isomer of nitrile containing the -N≡C functional group.
Stephen Reaction (RCN + SnCl2 + HCl)
Reduction of nitriles to aldimines leading to aldehydes.
Mendius Reduction (RCN + Na/C2H5OH)
Reduction of nitriles to primary amines.
Pinner Synthesis (RCN + R'OH + HCl)
Reaction of nitriles with alcohols to form esters via imino ethers.
Cyanide Ion (CN-)
An ambidentate nucleophile capable of bonding through carbon or nitrogen.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Nitriles have sp hybridization and linear geometry.
  • KCN gives nitriles with alkyl halides, while AgCN gives isocyanides.
  • Stephen reduction converts RCN to RCHO.
  • Complete hydrolysis of RCN yields RCOOH.
  • Grignard reagent addition to RCN yields ketones (RCOR').

📌 Quick Recall

  • Cyanides exhibit strong polarity and high boiling points relative to alkanes.
  • Chemical reactivity is dominated by nucleophilic addition to the triple bond and hydrolysis.
  • Reduction reactions provide direct access to amines and aldehydes.
  • Ambidentate nature of the cyanide ion dictates regioselectivity with metal counterions.

⚖️ Key Differences

KCN reaction with RX VS AgCN reaction with RX

KCN forms predominantly covalent nitriles (RCN), whereas AgCN forms isocyanides (RNC).

Question 1 of 18

What is the hybridization of carbon and nitrogen in the cyanide functional group (-C≡N)?

Explanation: Both the carbon and nitrogen atoms in the nitrile triple bond are sp hybridized, resulting in a linear geometry.

Question 2 of 18

Which reagent is used in the Stephen reaction to convert a nitrile into an aldehyde?

Explanation: The Stephen reaction uses stannous chloride (SnCl2) and hydrochloric acid (HCl) to reduce nitriles to aldimines, which hydrolyze to aldehydes.

Question 3 of 18

Partial hydrolysis of an alkyl cyanide typically yields which functional group?

Explanation: Partial hydrolysis stops at the amide stage (RCONH2) under mild acidic or alkaline conditions.

Question 4 of 18

Reaction of alkyl halides with silver cyanide (AgCN) predominantly forms:

Explanation: AgCN is covalent with silver tightly bound to carbon, directing the alkyl group to attack at the nitrogen atom, forming isocyanides.

Question 5 of 18

Reduction of acetonitrile (CH3CN) with Na and C2H5OH (Mendius reduction) yields:

Explanation: Mendius reduction reduces nitriles to primary amines; CH3CN yields ethylamine (CH3CH2NH2).

Question 6 of 18

What is the product when methyl cyanide reacts with methylmagnesium bromide followed by acid hydrolysis?

Explanation: Grignard reagent addition to CH3CN followed by hydrolysis forms acetone (CH3COCH3).

Question 7 of 18

Why do lower nitriles have high solubility in water?

Explanation: Lower nitriles form hydrogen bonds with water molecules through the lone pair on the nitrogen atom.

Question 8 of 18

Which reducing agent can selectively reduce a nitrile to an aldehyde at low temperatures?

Explanation: Diisobutylaluminum hydride (DIBAL-H) at low temperatures (-78°C) reduces nitriles to aldehydes after hydrolysis.

Question 9 of 18

What is the end product of complete acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of benzonitrile?

Explanation: Complete hydrolysis of any nitrile yields the corresponding carboxylic acid; benzonitrile yields benzoic acid.

Question 10 of 18

The C≡N bond in nitriles consists of:

Explanation: A triple bond always consists of one strong sigma bond and two orthogonal pi bonds.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following compounds has a remarkably foul and nauseating smell?

Explanation: Alkyl isocyanides (carbylamines) are notorious for their extremely foul, offensive odours.

Question 12 of 18

In the Pinner synthesis, treatment of a nitrile with an alcohol and dry HCl gas forms:

Explanation: The intermediate formed in the Pinner reaction before aqueous workup is an imino ether hydrochloride.

Question 13 of 18

Which reagent converts ethyl cyanide to propanoic acid?

Explanation: Complete hydrolysis using aqueous acid with heating converts ethyl cyanide (propanenitrile) to propanoic acid.

Question 14 of 18

The dipole moment of nitriles is relatively high because:

Explanation: The higher electronegativity of nitrogen polarizes the C≡N triple bond significantly, generating a high dipole moment.

Question 15 of 18

What type of isomerism is exhibited by nitriles and isocyanides?

Explanation: Nitriles (-C≡N) and isocyanides (-N≡C) belong to different functional group classes, thus exhibiting functional isomerism.

Question 16 of 18

Reaction of potassium cyanide with chloromethane yields primarily:

Explanation: KCN is ionic and reacts with chloromethane via SN2 attack by carbon to yield acetonitrile (ethanenitrile).

Question 17 of 18

What is the oxidation state of the carbon atom in the cyanide group (-CN)?

Explanation: In the -C≡N group, carbon shares three bonds with nitrogen and one with the alkyl group, corresponding to an oxidation state of +3.

Question 18 of 18

Catalytic hydrogenation of alkyl cyanides over Raney nickel primarily produces:

Explanation: Catalytic hydrogenation adds four hydrogen atoms across the triple bond to yield primary amines.

Question 1 of 10
Which reaction sequence correctly converts acetic acid into propanoic acid using a cyanide intermediate?

Solution: Reduction of acetic acid to ethanol, conversion to chloroethane, nucleophilic substitution with KCN to propanenitrile, followed by hydrolysis gives propanoic acid (chain extension).

Question 2 of 10
What is the principal organic product formed when propanenitrile is reduced with LiAlH4?

Solution: LiAlH4 reduces propanenitrile completely to 1-propanamine (propylamine).

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following compounds has the highest boiling point?

Solution: Propanenitrile possesses a very high dipole moment resulting in strong dipole-dipole interactions, giving it a higher boiling point than the other listed species.

Question 4 of 10
The ambidentate nature of the cyanide ion is best demonstrated by its reaction with:

Solution: Reaction of alkyl halides with KCN yields nitriles (C-bonded), while AgCN yields isocyanides (N-bonded), illustrating ambidentate behavior.

Question 5 of 10
What intermediate is formed during the Stephen reduction of benzonitrile before acid hydrolysis?

Solution: The Stephen reduction yields an aldimine hydrochloride intermediate (PhCH=NH · HCl) prior to aqueous workup.

Question 6 of 10
Which reagent will convert propanamide into propanenitrile?

Solution: Dehydration of amides using phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5) or thionyl chloride yields nitriles.

Question 7 of 10
In terms of IUPAC nomenclature, what is the suffix used for nitriles?

Solution: The IUPAC suffix for compounds containing the -C≡N group as principal functional group is '-nitrile'.

Question 8 of 10
What is the role of hydrochloric acid in the Pinner reaction?

Solution: Dry HCl gas protonates the nitrile nitrogen, facilitating nucleophilic attack by the alcohol to form the imino ether hydrochloride.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following statements regarding isocyanides compared to nitriles is correct?

Solution: Hydrolysis of isocyanides yields primary amines and formic acid, unlike nitriles which yield carboxylic acids and ammonia.

Question 10 of 10
When phenyl cyanide (benzonitrile) is treated with phenylmagnesium bromide followed by hydrolysis, the product is:

Solution: Benzonitrile reacting with phenylmagnesium bromide yields benzophenone (diphenyl ketone) after hydrolysis.
