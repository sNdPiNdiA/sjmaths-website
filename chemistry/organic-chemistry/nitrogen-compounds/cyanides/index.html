Organic Chemistry

Cyanides

Cyanides are organic compounds containing the functional group -C≡N, featuring sp-hybridized carbon bonded to nitrogen via a triple bond. They function as ambident nucleophiles and serve as versatile intermediates in organic synthesis.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structure and Bonding in Cyanides

  • The nitrile carbon is sp-hybridized, imparting a linear geometry with a bond angle of 180° around the C≡N axis.
  • The carbon-nitrogen triple bond consists of one strong σ-bond and two orthogonal π-bonds, resulting in a high bond dissociation energy.
  • A significant dipole moment exists due to the high electronegativity of nitrogen, drawing electron density away from the carbon atom.

Methods of Preparation

  • Alkyl halides react with potassium cyanide (KCN) in aqueous ethanolic solution to yield alkyl cyanides via SN2 substitution.
  • Dehydration of primary amides using dehydrating agents such as phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5) or thionyl chloride yields nitriles.
  • Dehydration of aldoximes with acetic anhydride directly forms the corresponding nitriles.

Chemical Reactions and Transformations

  • Partial hydrolysis of cyanides in the presence of cold concentrated HCl yields amides.
  • Grignard reagents add across the carbon-nitrogen triple bond to form complexes that upon hydrolysis yield ketones.
  • Alkaline hydrolysis using hydrogen peroxide converts nitriles directly to amides without progressing to carboxylic acids.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl halides react with KCN to form predominantly alkyl cyanides (nitriles).

Observation: Reaction of alkyl halides with AgCN forms predominantly isocyanides (carbylamines). — KCN is an ionic compound releasing free cyanide ions favoring carbon attack, whereas AgCN is predominantly covalent, making nitrogen a more reactive nucleophile.

Exception Nitriles are readily reduced to primary amines by catalytic hydrogenation or LiAlH4.

Observation: Reduction with SnCl2 and HCl stops cleanly at the imine stage (Stephen reduction) giving aldehydes upon hydrolysis. — Controlled reduction limits hydrogen addition before complete saturation to amine occurs.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Hydrolysis Carbon Count Rule
R-CN → R-COOH

Application: The resulting carboxylic acid contains one more carbon atom than the original alkyl group R. Always add one carbon when converting a halide to an acid via cyanide.

Reduction Product Selector
SnCl2/HCl = Aldehyde | Na/EtOH = Amine | LiAlH4 = Amine

Application: Use Stephen reduction reagents (SnCl2/HCl) when an aldehyde is desired from a nitrile; use strong reducing agents (LiAlH4 or Na/EtOH) for primary amines.

Grignard Addition Mnemonic
R-CN + R'MgX → R-CO-R'

Application: Addition of a Grignard reagent to a nitrile followed by acidic hydrolysis always yields a ketone, never an aldehyde (except when reacting with HCN).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Alkyl cyanides undergo reduction with sodium and alcohol (Mendius reduction) to form primary amines.
  • Cyanides are polar molecules with high boiling points compared to alkyl halides of comparable molecular mass.
  • The carbon atom of the cyanide group is electrophilic due to inductive and resonance electron withdrawal by nitrogen.
  • Reaction of hydrogen cyanide with aldehydes and ketones produces cyanohydrins.
  • Cyanides act as weak bases because the lone pair on nitrogen is held tightly in an sp-hybrid orbital.
  • Thallium cyanide or silver cyanide yields isocyanides due to the covalent nature of the metal-nitrogen bond.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the carbon count in nitrile synthesis: failing to include the cyanide carbon atom in IUPAC naming.
  • Assuming Grignard addition to hydrogen cyanide (HCN) yields a ketone instead of a primary alcohol.
  • Forgetting that acidic hydrolysis of cyanides yields ammonium salts rather than free ammonia.
  • Confusing the reduction products of Stephen reduction with catalytic hydrogenation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Cyanides

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Synthesis Pathways

Cyanides are synthesized primarily through nucleophilic substitution of alkyl halides using KCN or by dehydration of primary amides with P2O5.

Hydrolysis Reactions

Complete hydrolysis yields carboxylic acids with an increased carbon chain length, whereas partial hydrolysis stops at the amide stage.

Reduction Pathways

Catalytic hydrogenation and metal hydride reductions yield primary amines, while Stephen reduction selectively produces aldehydes.

Nucleophilic Additions

Grignard reagents react with nitriles to form imine intermediates that hydrolyze smoothly to give ketones.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Nitrile (R-CN)
Organic compound containing the cyano functional group.
Isocyanide (R-NC)
Compound containing the isonitrile functional group with coordinate bond.
Stephen Reaction (SnCl2 + HCl)
Reduction of nitriles to aldimines yielding aldehydes.
Mendius Reduction (Na + C2H5OH)
Reduction of nitriles to primary amines.
Cyanohydrin (R-CH(OH)CN)
Addition product of HCN across carbonyl groups.
Ambident Nucleophile ([:C≡N:]-)
Nucleophile with two reactive sites capable of attacking via carbon or nitrogen.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • KCN gives cyanides; AgCN gives isocyanides.
  • Hydrolysis of cyanides increases carbon chain length by one.
  • Stephen reduction converts RCN to RCHO.
  • Grignard reagent + RCN → Ketone.
  • Cyanide carbon is sp-hybridized.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Cyanides contain the C≡N triple bond with linear geometry.
  • They are prepared via KCN substitution or amide dehydration.
  • Key reactions include hydrolysis, reduction, and Grignard addition.
  • They exhibit distinct differences from isomeric isocyanides.

⚖️ Key Differences

KCN substitution VS AgCN substitution

KCN forms alkyl cyanides (nitriles), while AgCN forms alkyl isocyanides (carbylamines).

Question 1 of 18

What is the hybridization of carbon and nitrogen in the cyano group (-C≡N)?

Explanation: Both the carbon and nitrogen atoms in the cyano group are sp-hybridized, forming a linear geometry with one sigma and two pi bonds.

Question 2 of 18

Which reagent is used in the Stephen reaction for converting nitriles to aldehydes?

Explanation: The Stephen reaction utilizes stannous chloride (SnCl2) and hydrochloric acid (HCl) to reduce nitriles to aldimines, which hydrolyze to aldehydes.

Question 3 of 18

Reaction of ethyl chloride with potassium cyanide gives:

Explanation: Ethyl chloride reacts with ionic KCN via SN2 substitution to form propionitrile (propanenitrile), extending the carbon chain.

Question 4 of 18

When methyl cyanide is hydrolyzed completely with aqueous acid, the product is:

Explanation: Complete hydrolysis of acetonitrile (methyl cyanide) yields acetic acid along with an ammonium salt.

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following compounds gives a primary amine upon reduction with Na/EtOH?

Explanation: Mendius reduction of methyl cyanide with sodium and ethanol produces ethylamine, a primary amine.

Question 6 of 18

Silver cyanide reacts with alkyl halides to form primarily:

Explanation: Due to the covalent character of AgCN, nitrogen acts as the nucleophile, yielding alkyl isocyanides.

Question 7 of 18

What is the intermediate formed during the Stephen reduction of cyanides?

Explanation: The reduction produces an aldimine hydrochloride salt intermediate, which precipitates before subsequent hydrolysis.

Question 8 of 18

Grignard reagents react with nitriles to yield after hydrolysis:

Explanation: Nucleophilic addition of Grignard reagent to R-CN yields an imine salt that hydrolyzes to form a ketone.

Question 9 of 18

Partial hydrolysis of nitriles with cold concentrated HCl yields:

Explanation: Partial hydrolysis stops at the amide stage due to the milder reaction conditions.

Question 10 of 18

What is the geometry of the C≡N functional group?

Explanation: The sp hybridization of both carbon and nitrogen results in a linear bond angle of 180°.

Question 11 of 18

Dehydration of primary amides with phosphorus pentoxide yields:

Explanation: P2O5 is a powerful dehydrating agent that removes water from primary amides to form cyanides (nitriles).

Question 12 of 18

Which characteristic property is typical of alkyl isocyanides compared to cyanides?

Explanation: Alkyl isocyanides possess a notoriously foul, offensive odour, unlike the ethereal odours of nitriles.

Question 13 of 18

Reduction of acetonitrile with lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4) produces:

Explanation: LiAlH4 is a strong reducing agent that completely reduces nitriles to primary amines (ethylamine).

Question 14 of 18

Cyanides function as ambident nucleophiles because:

Explanation: Ambident nucleophiles can react through two different nucleophilic centres, either carbon or nitrogen.

Question 15 of 18

What product is obtained when hydrogen cyanide adds to acetone?

Explanation: Nucleophilic addition of HCN to acetone gives acetone cyanohydrin [ (CH3)2C(OH)CN ].

Question 16 of 18

Which method is best suited for stepping up the carbon chain by one carbon atom?

Explanation: Introducing a cyanide group introduces an extra carbon atom, effectively stepping up the homologous series.

Question 17 of 18

Hydrolysis of isocyanides yields which of the following products?

Explanation: Acid hydrolysis of isocyanides cleaves the carbon-nitrogen bond to give a primary amine and formic acid.

Question 18 of 18

What is the IUPAC name of CH3CH2CN?

Explanation: Propane-3-nitrile or propanenitrile is the systematic IUPAC name for CH3CH2CN, counting three carbon atoms.

Question 1 of 10
Which functional isomerism is exhibited by cyanides and isocyanides?

Solution: Cyanides (-CN) and isocyanides (-NC) belong to different functional groups while having the same molecular formula, representing functional isomerism.

Question 2 of 10
In the reduction of R-CN to R-CH2NH2 using Na/EtOH, the active reducing species is:

Solution: Sodium reacting with ethanol generates nascent hydrogen which reduces the triple bond of the nitrile group.

Question 3 of 10
What is the correct IUPAC name for CH3-C(CH3)2-CN?

Solution: The longest carbon chain containing the nitrile carbon has three carbons, with two methyl substituents at C-2, giving 2,2-dimethylpropanenitrile.

Question 4 of 10
Which of the following represents an ambident nucleophile?

Solution: The cyanide ion can form bonds through either its carbon or nitrogen atom, classifying it as an ambident nucleophile.

Question 5 of 10
Which reaction produces a carboxylic acid with the same number of carbon atoms as the starting alkyl halide?

Solution: Since cyanide introduction inherently adds one carbon atom to the chain, the resulting carboxylic acid always has one more carbon.

Question 6 of 10
What happens when acetamide is heated with phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5)?

Solution: P2O5 dehydrates acetamide by removing a molecule of water to yield acetonitrile.

Question 7 of 10
Alkyl cyanides are generally:

Solution: Lower aliphatic nitriles are colourless liquids with pleasant ethereal odours and are soluble in water and organic solvents.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following compounds will not undergo Stephen reduction?

Solution: Stephen reduction is specific to nitriles (cyanides) and does not apply to isocyanides.

Question 9 of 10
The boiling points of cyanides are higher than those of isomeric alkyl halides because:

Solution: The strong dipole moment of the C≡N bond leads to stronger dipole-dipole interactions, resulting in higher boiling points than alkyl halides.

Question 10 of 10
What is the final product when benzonitrile is subjected to complete acidic hydrolysis?

Solution: Complete hydrolysis of benzonitrile converts the -CN group into a -COOH group, yielding benzoic acid.
