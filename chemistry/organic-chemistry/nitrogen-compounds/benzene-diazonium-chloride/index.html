Benzene diazonium chloride

Benzene diazonium chloride is an organic compound with the chemical formula C6H5N2Cl, classified as an aromatic diazonium salt. It is synthesized via the diazotization of aniline using nitrous acid at low temperatures (0–5 °C).

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthesis and Stability

  • Prepared by treating aniline with nitrous acid (generated in situ from NaNO2 and HCl) at 0–5 °C.
  • Low temperature is mandatory to prevent the hydrolysis of the diazonium salt to phenol.
  • The stability of benzene diazonium chloride arises from resonance stabilization of the diazonium cation.
  • It is typically handled in aqueous solution and is explosive in the dry crystalline state.

Replacement Reactions Involving Loss of Nitrogen

  • In Sandmeyer reactions, the diazonium group is replaced by -Cl, -Br, or -CN using cuprous salts.
  • Gattermann reaction utilizes copper powder in the presence of halogen acids instead of cuprous salts.
  • Iodoarene synthesis is achieved simply by treating the diazonium salt with aqueous potassium iodide (KI) without a catalyst.
  • Fluorobenzene is prepared via the Balz-Schiemann reaction using fluoroboric acid followed by thermal decomposition.

Coupling and Reduction Reactions

  • Azo coupling with phenols and aromatic amines yields brightly colored azo dyes through electrophilic aromatic substitution.
  • Coupling occurs predominantly at the para-position relative to the activating hydroxyl or amino group.
  • Reduction with stannous chloride and hydrochloric acid converts the diazonium salt to phenylhydrazine.
  • Mild reduction using hypophosphorous acid (H3PO2) or ethanol replaces the diazonium group with a hydrogen atom (deamination).
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Sandmeyer Reaction (Chloro/Bromo)
C6H5N2+Cl- + Cu2Cl2 → C6H5Cl + N2 + CuCl
Conditions: Presence of corresponding cuprous halide in HX
Units: Yield in percentage (%)
Gattermann Reaction
C6H5N2+Cl- + Cu/HCl → C6H5Cl + N2 + CuCl
Conditions: Freshly prepared copper powder with corresponding halogen acid
Units: Yield in percentage (%)
Balz-Schiemann Reaction
C6H5N2+Cl- + HBF4 → C6H5N2+BF4- → C6H5F + N2 + BF3
Conditions: Thermal decomposition of fluoroborate salt
Units: Temperature in Kelvin (K)
Azo Coupling Reaction
C6H5N2+Cl- + C6H5OH → C6H5-N=N-C6H4-OH + HCl
Conditions: Mild alkaline medium (pH 9-10) for phenol; mild acidic for amines
Units: Wavelength in nanometers (nm)
Diazotization Reaction
C6H5NH2 + HNO2 + HCl → C6H5N2+Cl- + 2H2O
Conditions: Temperature maintained at 0–5 °C
Units: Stoichiometric molar amounts
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Most diazonium salts are prepared using standard halogen acids like HCl.

Observation: Fluorobenzene cannot be prepared by direct halogenation or standard Sandmeyer reaction. — Direct fluorination is violently explosive, and CuF does not promote the Sandmeyer reaction, requiring the Balz-Schiemann route.

Exception Iodination of aromatic rings generally requires Lewis acid catalysts.

Observation: Iodobenzene is prepared directly from benzene diazonium chloride using KI without a catalyst. — The diazonium group is an exceptionally good leaving group (driven by stable N2 gas formation), requiring no external activation.

Exception Electrophilic aromatic substitution typically requires strong electrophiles and harsh conditions.

Observation: Azo coupling proceeds readily under mild conditions (pH 9-10 for phenols). — The diazonium cation is a sufficiently strong electrophile to attack strongly activated rings like phenols and tertiary aromatic amines.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Sandmeyer vs Gattermann Reagents
Sandmeyer = Cu2X2/HX; Gattermann = Cu/HX

Application: Remember that Gattermann uses elemental copper powder, whereas Sandmeyer uses cuprous halide.

Deamination Mnemonic
H3PO2 or CH3CH2OH turns -N2Cl into -H

Application: Use hypophosphorous acid or ethanol when a synthetic route requires removing an amino group via diazotization.

Azo Dye Color Rule
Phenol + Diazonium (Basic pH) → p-Hydroxyazobenzene (Orange/Red dye)

Application: Identify coupling medium pH: basic for phenols, weakly acidic for primary/secondary/tertiary amines.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Diazotization temperature must strictly be maintained between 0–5 °C to prevent decomposition to phenol.
  • Benzene diazonium chloride reacts with KI to form iodobenzene without needing a copper catalyst.
  • Reduction of benzene diazonium chloride with SnCl2/HCl yields phenylhydrazine hydrochloride.
  • Azo dyes are characterized by the presence of the chromophoric -N=N- linkage.
  • The Balz-Schiemann reaction involves thermal decomposition of benzene diazonium fluoroborate to yield fluorobenzene.
  • Reaction with hypophosphorous acid (H3PO2) replaces the diazonium group with hydrogen (deamination).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Forgetting the strict temperature range (0–5 °C) during diazotization, leading to hydrolysis.
  • Confusing Sandmeyer catalyst (Cu2X2) with Gattermann catalyst (Cu powder).
  • Failing to maintain basic medium (pH 9-10) during azo coupling with phenols.
  • Assuming fluorobenzene can be prepared directly via standard Sandmeyer chlorination/bromination conditions.

⚡ Quick Revision: Benzene diazonium chloride

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Synthesis via Diazotization

Aniline reacts with nitrous acid at 0–5 °C to form soluble benzene diazonium chloride.

Replacement of Diazonium Group

The -N2Cl group acts as an excellent leaving group, being replaced by Cl, Br, CN, I, F, OH, or H.

Coupling Reactions

Diazonium salts act as weak electrophiles reacting with activated aromatic rings to form colored azo dyes.

Synthetic Utility in Organic Synthesis

Enables introduction of groups into specific aromatic ring positions that cannot be introduced via direct electrophilic substitution.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Diazotization (Conversion to diazonium)
Reaction of primary aromatic amine with HNO2 at 0–5 °C.
Sandmeyer Reaction (Cu2X2 catalysis)
Replacement of -N2Cl by halogen or cyano group using cuprous salts.
Gattermann Reaction (Cu powder catalysis)
Replacement of -N2Cl using copper powder and halogen acid.
Balz-Schiemann Reaction (HBF4 thermal decomposition)
Synthesis of fluorobenzene from diazonium fluoroborate.
Azo Coupling (-N=N- linkage formation)
Electrophilic substitution with phenols or amines yielding colored dyes.
Deamination (Replacement by -H)
Removal of amino group via H3PO2 reduction.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Temperature range: 0–5 °C
  • Sandmeyer: Cu2Cl2 / Cu2Br2
  • Gattermann: Cu / HX
  • Balz-Schiemann: HBF4 → Δ
  • Iodobenzene synthesis: KI without catalyst

📌 Quick Recall

  • Benzene diazonium chloride is stable only at low temperatures.
  • It undergoes substitution reactions releasing nitrogen gas.
  • Coupling reactions produce stable, colored azo compounds.
  • It serves as a versatile intermediate for directing otherwise difficult substitutions.

⚖️ Key Differences

Sandmeyer Reaction VS Gattermann Reaction

Sandmeyer uses cuprous halide (Cu2X2), whereas Gattermann uses elemental copper powder (Cu).

Question 16 of 19

Explanation: Hydrolysis of benzene diazonium chloride upon warming with water yields phenol and hydrochloric acid.

Question 17 of 19

Explanation: The benzene diazonium cation (C6H5N2+) acts as the weak electrophile attacking the activated ring.

Question 18 of 19

Which aniline derivative cannot be successfully diazotized directly?

Explanation: Extremely weak basicity of heavily substituted nitroanilines like 2,4,6-trinitroaniline impedes normal diazotization.

Question 19 of 19

What is the oxidation state of nitrogen bonded to the benzene ring in benzene diazonium chloride?

Explanation: The positively charged diazonium nitrogen carries a formal charge of +1.
