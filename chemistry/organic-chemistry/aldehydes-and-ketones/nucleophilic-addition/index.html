Organic Chemistry

Nucleophilic Addition

Aldehydes and ketones undergo nucleophilic addition reactions where a nucleophile attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon. The reaction proceeds via an sp2 to sp3 hybridization change at the carbonyl carbon.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mechanistic Pathway and Stereochemistry

  • The carbonyl carbon is sp2 hybridized with a trigonal planar geometry and a partial positive charge.
  • Nucleophiles attack from a trajectory of approximately 107° to the C=O bond, known as the Bürgi-Dunitz angle.
  • Attack of the nucleophile generates a tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate that subsequently abstracts a proton from the solvent.
  • Reaction of an unsymmetrical ketone or aldehyde with an achiral nucleophile yields a racemic mixture due to planar symmetry.

Electronic and Steric Effects on Reactivity

  • Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones toward nucleophilic addition due to lesser steric hindrance and fewer electron-donating alkyl groups.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups (EWG) increase electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon by stabilizing the developing negative charge on oxygen.
  • Electron-donating groups (EDG) decrease reactivity through positive inductive effects (+I) that destabilize the intermediate alkoxide.
  • Bulky substituents around the carbonyl carbon raise the energy of activation by hindering the approach of the nucleophile.

Classic Addition Reactions of Carbonyl Compounds

  • Addition of HCN produces cyanohydrins, serving as a key route for extending carbon chains by one unit.
  • Reaction with alcohols in the presence of dry HCl gas yields acetals and ketals via hemiacetal and hemiketal intermediates.
  • Derivatives of ammonia such as hydroxylamine, hydrazine, and 2,4-DNP undergo addition followed by elimination (condensation) to form crystalline solids.
  • Grignard reagents and organolithium compounds act as carbanion equivalents, delivering carbon nucleophiles to form alcohols.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Aldehydes are generally more reactive than ketones in nucleophilic addition.

Observation: Formaldehyde reacts slower than certain activated cyclic ketones or chloral. — Chloral (CCl3CHO) possesses three strong -I inductive effect groups that drastically increase the positive charge on the carbonyl carbon.

Exception Bisulfite addition compounds form readily with all aldehydes and methyl ketones.

Observation: Bulky ketones such as di-tert-butyl ketone and acetophenone derivatives fail to form bisulfite addition products. — Severe steric congestion around the sp3 hybridized tetrahedral carbon prevents the addition of the bulky SO3H- nucleophile.

Exception Grignard reagents add to aldehydes and ketones to yield alcohols.

Observation: Sterically hindered ketones react via reduction or enolization instead of nucleophilic addition. — Bulky Grignard reagents (e.g., isopropylmagnesium bromide) act as reducing agents via a cyclic six-membered transition state (Meerwein-Ponndorf-Verley type transfer).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Formaldehyde is the most reactive aldehyde toward nucleophilic addition due to the absence of electron-donating alkyl groups.
  • Addition of sodium hydrogen sulfite (NaHSO3) is reversible and used for the separation and purification of aldehydes.
  • Grignard reagents (RMgX) add to formaldehyde to give primary alcohols, other aldehydes give secondary alcohols, and ketones give tertiary alcohols.
  • Ammonia derivatives react with carbonyl compounds via nucleophilic addition followed by dehydration (condensation).
  • 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine (2,4-DNP) test is specifically used for the characterization of carbonyl compounds.
  • Hydration of carbonyl compounds forms gem-diols, which are stable only when strongly electron-withdrawing groups are present (e.g., chloral hydrate).
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing nucleophilic addition-elimination with direct nucleophilic substitution.
  • Assuming steric hindrance affects only the nucleophile and not the stability of the transition state.
  • Forgetting that acidic conditions protonate the carbonyl oxygen first, enhancing electrophilicity.
  • Failing to account for hyperconjugation and inductive effects in substituted aromatic aldehydes.

⚡ Quick Revision: Nucleophilic Addition

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Carbonyl Electrophilicity

The polarization of the carbon-oxygen double bond creates an electrophilic center at carbon, facilitating attack by electron-rich nucleophiles.

Acid and Base Catalysis

Acid catalysis protonates the carbonyl oxygen to increase carbon electrophilicity, while base catalysis generates a more potent nucleophile.

Equilibrium Control in Additions

Reactions like cyanohydrin formation and acetalization are equilibrium-controlled and sensitive to reaction pH and reactant concentrations.

Nitrogen Nucleophiles

Reactions with primary amines proceed through unstable carbinolamine intermediates that eliminate water to form stable imines (Schiff bases).

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Nucleophile (Nu-)
An electron-rich species that donates an electron pair to form a chemical bond.
Electrophile (E+)
An electron-deficient species that accepts an electron pair.
Cyanohydrin (RCH(OH)CN)
A class of organic compounds containing a hydroxyl and a cyano group on the same carbon.
Hemiacetal (RCH(OH)(OR'))
An addition product of an aldehyde and an alcohol.
Acetal (RCH(OR')2)
A gem-diether formed by substitution of the hydroxyl group in a hemiacetal.
Schiff Base (R2C=NR')
An imine formed by condensation of a carbonyl compound with a primary amine.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones in nucleophilic addition.
  • Bürgi-Dunitz trajectory angle is approximately 107 degrees.
  • Bisulfite addition is restricted to aldehydes and methyl ketones.
  • Grignard reagent addition requires anhydrous conditions.
  • Acid catalysis protonates carbonyl oxygen to enhance electrophilicity.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nucleophilic addition involves sp2 to sp3 hybridization change at the carbonyl carbon.
  • Reactivity depends on steric factors and inductive electronic effects.
  • Addition of nitrogen nucleophiles involves subsequent elimination of water.
  • Carbon-carbon bond formation is achieved via reagents like HCN and Grignard reagents.

⚖️ Key Differences

Hemiacetal VS Hemiketal

Hemiacetal is derived from an aldehyde, whereas hemiketal is derived from a ketone.

Which of the following compounds is most reactive towards nucleophilic addition?

Explanation: Trigonal planar sp2 hybridized carbonyl carbon converts into tetrahedral sp3 hybridized intermediate.

Which reagent is used to convert aldehydes into cyanohydrins?

Explanation: HCN catalyzed by a base generates cyanide ion, which acts as the nucleophile adding to the carbonyl carbon.

Question 4 of 18

The Bürgi-Dunitz angle for nucleophilic attack on a carbonyl group is approximately:

Explanation: Nucleophiles attack the carbonyl carbon at an optimum trajectory angle of approximately 107°.

Question 5 of 18

Which of the following does not form a bisulfite addition compound?

Explanation: Benzophenone has two bulky phenyl groups causing extreme steric hindrance, preventing bisulfite addition.

Question 6 of 18

Reaction of an aldehyde with an alcohol in the presence of dry HCl yields:

Explanation: Excess alcohol in dry HCl converts aldehydes fully into acetals via hemiacetal intermediates.

Question 7 of 18

Which derivative is formed when carbonyl compounds react with 2,4-DNP?

Explanation: Reaction with 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine yields colored crystalline 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazones.

Question 8 of 18

Why are aldehydes generally more reactive than ketones in nucleophilic addition?

Explanation: Aldehydes possess one alkyl group compared to two in ketones, resulting in lower steric hindrance and higher electrophilicity.

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following catalysts increases the rate of nucleophilic addition by protonating the carbonyl oxygen?

Explanation: Acids like HCl protonate the carbonyl oxygen, increasing the positive charge and electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon.

Question 10 of 18

What is the product of the reaction between formaldehyde and a Grignard reagent followed by hydrolysis?

Explanation: Formaldehyde reacts with Grignard reagents to produce primary alcohols after acid workup.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following is true regarding nucleophilic addition of HCN to carbonyl compounds?

Explanation: Base catalysis provides sufficient concentration of cyanide ions by shifting the dissociation equilibrium of HCN.

Question 12 of 18

Chloral hydrate is stable due to:

Explanation: The strong electron-withdrawing inductive effect of the trichloromethyl group stabilizes the gem-diol structure.

Question 13 of 18

Reaction of a ketone with hydroxylamine (NH2OH) gives:

Explanation: Ketones react with hydroxylamine to form oximes with the elimination of a water molecule.

Question 14 of 18

Which of the following reagents reduces a carbonyl group to a hydrocarbon?

Explanation: Clemmensen reduction (Zn-Hg / conc. HCl) reduces aldehydes and ketones to corresponding alkanes.

Question 15 of 18

In the addition of Grignard reagents to carbonyl compounds, the nucleophile is:

Explanation: Grignard reagents dissociate to provide a carbanion equivalent (R-) as the nucleophile.

Question 16 of 18

Which carbonyl compound gives a positive Schiff's test?

Explanation: Aliphatic aldehydes like acetaldehyde restore the magenta color of Schiff's reagent, while ketones do not.

Question 17 of 18

Addition of sodium bisulfite to aldehydes forms a crystalline precipitate because:

Explanation: Bisulfite addition products form crystalline solids due to strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding.

Question 18 of 18

What happens to the rate of nucleophilic addition when electron-withdrawing groups are attached to the carbonyl carbon?

Explanation: Electron-withdrawing groups increase the partial positive charge on the carbonyl carbon, increasing its electrophilicity and reaction rate.

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following factors decreases the rate of nucleophilic addition to a carbonyl compound?

Solution: Electron-donating groups increase electron density on the carbonyl carbon and destabilize the developing negative charge in the transition state.

Question 2 of 10
When acetone reacts with methylmagnesium bromide followed by hydrolysis, the product is:

Solution: Acetone (a ketone) reacts with a Grignard reagent to yield a tertiary alcohol, 2-methyl-2-propanol (tert-butanol).

Question 3 of 10
What is the role of ZnCl2 (anhydrous) in the reaction of alcohols with aldehydes to form acetals?

Solution: Anhydrous ZnCl2 acts as a Lewis acid, coordinating with the carbonyl oxygen to enhance electrophilicity.

Question 4 of 10
Which nitrogen derivative of carbonyl compounds gives a crystalline derivative known as a semicarbazone?

Solution: Reaction with semicarbazide yields semicarbazones.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following compounds fails to undergo nucleophilic addition with sodium bisulfite?

Solution: Acetophenone possesses a bulky phenyl group that creates steric hindrance, preventing bisulfite addition.

Question 6 of 10
In the base-catalyzed addition of HCN to a carbonyl compound, the active nucleophile is:

Solution: The base abstracts a proton from HCN to generate the cyanide ion (CN-), which is the active nucleophile.

Question 7 of 10
Which of the following statements is correct regarding the stereochemistry of nucleophilic addition to unhindered unsymmetrical ketones?

Solution: Attack of an achiral nucleophile from top or bottom faces of a planar carbonyl group occurs with equal probability, yielding a racemic mixture.

Question 8 of 10
Hydration of aliphatic aldehydes yields gem-diols. These are stable only when:

Solution: Gem-diols are generally unstable in solution unless stabilized by strong -I groups like trichloromethyl in chloral hydrate.

Question 9 of 10
Which reagent converts a carbonyl compound into an imine (Schiff base)?

Solution: Primary amines react with aldehydes and ketones via nucleophilic addition and dehydration to form stable imines.

Question 10 of 10
What is the hybridization of the carbonyl carbon in aldehydes and ketones?

Solution: The carbonyl carbon forms three sigma bonds and one pi bond, adopting trigonal planar geometry with sp2 hybridization.
