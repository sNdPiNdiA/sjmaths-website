Organic Chemistry

Preparation and Properties of Ketones

Ketones are organic carbonyl compounds characterized by a divalent >C=O group bonded to two carbon atoms. They are prepared via oxidation of secondary alcohols, ozonolysis of alkenes, and Friedel-Crafts acylation.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthesis Methods of Ketones

  • Secondary alcohols undergo catalytic oxidation using Jones reagent (CrO3/H2SO4) or PCC to yield ketones.
  • Friedel-Crafts acylation of aromatic rings with acyl chlorides in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3 yields aryl ketones.
  • Hydration of alkynes other than acetylene in the presence of HgSO4 and H2SO4 follows Markovnikov addition to form ketones.
  • Reaction of organolithium reagents or Gilman reagents ((R2CuLi)) with acyl chlorides provides an efficient synthesis route for ketones.

Chemical Properties and Reactions

  • Ketones undergo nucleophilic addition reactions at the carbonyl carbon, though less reactive than aldehydes due to steric and electronic factors.
  • Base-catalyzed halogenation of methyl ketones in the presence of halogen and NaOH produces carboxylate salts and haloform (Haloform Reaction).
  • Reduction of ketones with amalgamated zinc and concentrated HCl yields hydrocarbons via the Clemmensen reduction.
  • Reduction of ketones with hydrazine and KOH in high-boiling alcohol yields hydrocarbons via the Wolff-Kishner reduction.

Spectroscopic and Physical Characteristics

  • The carbonyl stretching frequency (νC=O) in the IR spectrum typically appears as a strong absorption band around 1715 cm-1.
  • In 1H-NMR spectroscopy, protons adjacent to the carbonyl carbon (α-protons) typically resonate in the 2.1–2.6 ppm region.
  • Lower aliphatic ketones are completely miscible with water due to hydrogen bonding between the carbonyl oxygen and water molecules.
  • Ketones possess higher boiling points than corresponding alkanes and ethers due to dipole-dipole interactions.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Ketones generally do not undergo oxidation easily.

Observation: Strong oxidizing agents (HNO3, KMnO4) cleave the carbon-carbon bond adjacent to the carbonyl group. — Harsh conditions break the α-C—C bond, yielding a mixture of carboxylic acids with fewer carbon atoms (Popoff's rule).

Exception Carbonyl compounds undergo nucleophilic addition readily.

Observation: Sterically hindered ketones (e.g., di-tert-butyl ketone) fail to react with sodium bisulfite. — Extreme steric congestion prevents the attack of the nucleophile and destabilizes the tetrahedral intermediate.

Exception Alkynes hydrate to form ketones via Markovnikov addition.

Observation: Ethyne (acetylene) hydrates to form acetaldehyde (an aldehyde), not a ketone. — Terminal alkyne with only two carbons yields a primary carbonyl derivative (acetaldehyde) upon tautomerization of vinyl alcohol.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Ketones are less reactive than aldehydes toward nucleophilic addition due to electron-donating inductive effects of two alkyl groups.
  • Acetone reacts with Grignard reagents followed by hydrolysis to give tertiary (3°) alcohols.
  • The iodoform test is positive for methyl ketones and secondary alcohols containing the CH3CH(OH)— group.
  • Benzophenone does not undergo aldol condensation because it lacks α-hydrogen atoms.
  • Reduction of ketones with sodium borohydride (NaBH4) yields secondary alcohols.
  • Ozonolysis of 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene yields two molecules of acetone.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the reagent for Clemmensen reduction (Zn-Hg/HCl) with Wolff-Kishner reduction (NH2NH2/OH-).
  • Assuming aromatic ketones undergo haloform reaction without considering ring substitution effects.
  • Forgetting that formaldehyde yields primary alcohol and other aldehydes yield secondary alcohols with Grignard reagents, whereas ketones strictly yield tertiary alcohols.
  • Neglecting Popoff's rule when predicting oxidation products of unsymmetrical dialkyl ketones.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation and Properties of Ketones

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Preparation Pathways

Ketones are synthesized primarily through secondary alcohol oxidation, Friedel-Crafts acylation, and hydration of non-terminal alkynes using metal catalysts.

Nucleophilic Addition

The sp2 hybridized carbonyl carbon is susceptible to nucleophilic attack, though steric hindrance by two alkyl groups decreases ketone reactivity relative to aldehydes.

Reduction Reactions

Ketones are reduced to secondary alcohols via hydride donors (NaBH4, LiAlH4) or directly to alkanes via Clemmensen or Wolff-Kishner reductions.

Name Reactions

Key transformations include the haloform reaction for methyl ketones and Friedel-Crafts acylation for aromatic ketones using Lewis acid catalysts.

Introduction of an acyl group into an organic molecule via Friedel-Crafts reaction.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ketones give tertiary alcohols with Grignard reagents.
  • Methyl ketones specifically respond to the Iodoform test.
  • Ketones do not reduce Tollens' or Fehling's reagents.
  • Clemmensen reduction uses acidic Zn-Hg/HCl.
  • Wolff-Kishner reduction uses basic hydrazine/KOH.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Ketones contain a carbonyl carbon bonded to two carbon groups.
  • Synthesized via alcohol oxidation, alkyne hydration, and acylation.
  • Undergo nucleophilic addition and reduction to alkanes or secondary alcohols.
  • Distinguished from aldehydes by their resistance to mild oxidizing agents.

⚖️ Key Differences

Aldehydes VS Ketones

Aldehydes have at least one H bonded to carbonyl carbon and reduce Tollens' reagent; ketones have two carbon groups and do not.

Which reagent is used in the Clemmensen reduction of ketones?

Explanation: Clemmensen reduction employs zinc-amalgam and concentrated hydrochloric acid to reduce carbonyl groups to methylene groups.

What is the product formed when acetone reacts with methylmagnesium bromide followed by acid hydrolysis?

Explanation: Acetone (a ketone) reacts with Grignard reagent to form a tertiary alcohol, specifically 2-methylpropan-2-ol (tert-butyl alcohol).

Question 3 of 18

Which of the following compounds will give a positive iodoform test?

Explanation: Propan-2-one is a methyl ketone (contains CH3C=O group) and thus responds positively to the iodoform test.

Question 4 of 18

In the Wolff-Kishner reduction, the carbonyl group of a ketone is converted into:

Explanation: Wolff-Kishner reduction reduces the carbonyl group (>C=O) to a methylene group (>CH2).

Question 5 of 18

Benzophenone reacts with phenylmagnesium bromide to yield:

Explanation: Reaction of benzophenone (a ketone) with a Grignard reagent yields triphenylmethanol, a tertiary alcohol.

Question 6 of 18

Which catalyst is used in Friedel-Crafts acylation of benzene to prepare acetophenone?

Explanation: Anhydrous aluminum chloride (AlCl3) is the standard Lewis acid catalyst for Friedel-Crafts acylation.

Question 7 of 18

What is formed when propyne is hydrated in the presence of HgSO4 and dilute H2SO4?

Explanation: Hydration of propyne follows Markovnikov's rule to form an unstable enol that tautomerizes to propanone (acetone).

Question 8 of 18

Ketones are generally less reactive than aldehydes toward nucleophilic addition because of:

Explanation: Two alkyl groups attached to the carbonyl carbon in ketones exert both steric hindrance and electron-donating (+I) effects, decreasing electrophilicity.

Question 9 of 18

Popoff's rule is used to predict the products of:

Explanation: Popoff's rule governs the cleavage pattern during the vigorous oxidation of unsymmetrical aliphatic ketones.

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following does not undergo aldol condensation?

Explanation: Benzaldehyde lacks α-hydrogen atoms, so it cannot undergo self-aldol condensation.

Question 11 of 18

The C=O stretching frequency in IR spectroscopy for open-chain saturated ketones appears near:

Explanation: A standard saturated aliphatic ketone exhibits a strong carbonyl stretch around 1715 cm-1.

Question 12 of 18

What is the product of the reaction between a ketone and hydrazine in strongly basic medium?

Explanation: Hydrazine in a basic medium (Wolff-Kishner reduction) converts ketones directly to alkanes via hydrazone intermediates.

Question 13 of 18

Which oxidant readily converts secondary alcohols into ketones without affecting double bonds?

Explanation: PCC is a mild, selective anhydrous reagent used to oxidize secondary alcohols to ketones safely.

Question 14 of 18

What is formed when calcium acetate is dry distilled?

Explanation: Dry distillation of calcium acetate yields propanone (acetone) alongside calcium carbonate.

Question 15 of 18

Why can aldehydes be distinguished from ketones using Tollens' reagent?

Explanation: Aldehydes are easily oxidized by ammoniacal silver nitrate (Tollens' reagent), depositing elemental silver as a mirror, whereas ketones resist mild oxidation.

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following represents the general formula for aliphatic ketones?

Explanation: Aliphatic ketones and aldehydes share the general molecular formula CnH2nO, making them functional isomers.

Question 17 of 18

Which compound is formed when diethyl cadmium reacts with acetyl chloride?

Explanation: Dialkyl cadmium reagents react with acyl chlorides to yield ketones; acetyl chloride and diethyl cadmium produce butan-2-one.

Question 18 of 18

The alpha-hydrogens of ketones are acidic primarily due to:

Explanation: Removal of an α-hydrogen yields an enolate ion whose negative charge is resonance-stabilized by the adjacent carbonyl oxygen.

Which reagent converts a secondary alcohol directly into a ketone?

Solution: Jones reagent or acidic dichromate oxidizes secondary alcohols directly to ketones.

Solution: Ketones react with Grignard reagents to yield tertiary alcohols after acidic workup.

Question 3 of 10
During the Clemmensen reduction of a ketone, the carbonyl oxygen is replaced by:

Solution: Clemmensen reduction replaces the carbonyl oxygen atom with two hydrogen atoms, forming a methylene (>CH2) group.

Question 4 of 10
Which ketone is expected to have the highest boiling point?

Solution: Boiling points increase with molecular weight due to increased van der Waals dispersion forces; pentan-2-one is the heaviest.

Question 5 of 10
What product is formed when calcium benzoate is dry distilled with calcium formate?

Solution: Dry distillation of calcium formate with calcium salt of another acid yields an aldehyde (benzaldehyde in this case).

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following catalysts is utilized in the Wolff-Kishner reduction?

Solution: Wolff-Kishner reduction uses hydrazine followed by a strong base like KOH in a high-boiling solvent such as ethylene glycol.

Question 7 of 10
Why do ketones fail to undergo Cannizzaro reaction?

Solution: Cannizzaro reaction requires aldehydes lacking α-hydrogens; ketones possess α-hydrogens and undergo enolization instead.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following tests can distinguish between pentan-2-one and pentan-3-one?

Solution: Pentan-2-one is a methyl ketone and gives a positive iodoform test, whereas pentan-3-one does not.

Question 9 of 10
What is the intermediate formed when a ketone reacts with 2,4-DNP?

Solution: Reaction of ketones with 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine yields colored 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone precipitates.

Question 10 of 10
In nucleophilic addition to ketones, the attacking species at the carbonyl carbon is:

Solution: The electrophilic carbonyl carbon is attacked by electron-rich nucleophiles in the first step of nucleophilic addition.
