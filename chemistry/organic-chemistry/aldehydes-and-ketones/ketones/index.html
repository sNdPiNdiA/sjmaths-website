Organic Chemistry

Ketones

Ketones are organic compounds characterized by a carbonyl group bonded to two carbon atoms, represented by the general formula R−CO−R'. They undergo nucleophilic addition reactions and exhibit keto-enol tautomerism.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation and Synthesis of Ketones

  • Oxidation of secondary alcohols using reagents such as Jones reagent (CrO3/H2SO4) or PCC yields ketones efficiently.
  • Ozonolysis of alkenes with internal double bonds produces ketones depending on the substitution pattern of the alkene carbons.
  • Friedel-Crafts acylation of aromatic rings with acyl chlorides in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3 yields aryl ketones.
  • Reaction of dialkylcadmium or organolithium reagents with acyl chlorides provides a versatile route to ketones without over-addition.

Chemical Reactivity and Nucleophilic Addition

  • Ketones are less reactive than aldehydes toward nucleophilic addition due to steric hindrance and +I electron-donating inductive effects of two alkyl groups.
  • Reaction with Grignard reagents followed by hydrolysis yields tertiary alcohols.
  • Addition of sodium hydrogensulfite (NaHSO3) forms a crystalline bisulfite addition product, useful for isolation and purification.
  • Reaction with monohydric alcohols in the presence of dry HCl gas forms ketals.

Reduction, Oxidation, and Characteristic Tests

  • Clemmensen reduction and Wolff-Kishner reduction both convert the carbonyl oxygen into a methylene group (CH2).
  • Ketones resist oxidation by mild oxidizing agents (Tollen's, Fehling's, Benedict's reagents), distinguishing them from aldehydes.
  • Iodoform test is positive for methyl ketones, yielding a yellow precipitate of CHI3 upon treatment with I2 and NaOH.
  • Baeyer-Villiger oxidation converts ketones to esters using peroxy acids like m-CPBA.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Ketones do not generally reduce Tollen's reagent

Observation: α-Hydroxy ketones (ketols) give a positive Tollen's test — They undergo tautomerism to form enediols which tautomerize to reducing sugars/aldehydes.

Exception Ketones are less reactive than aldehydes toward nucleophilic addition

Observation: Cyclic ketones with small ring sizes (e.g., cyclobutanone, cyclopropanone) are extremely reactive — Ring strain is relieved upon sp3 rehybridization of the carbonyl carbon during nucleophilic attack.

Exception Friedel-Crafts alkylation and acylation proceed smoothly on activated rings

Observation: Ketones containing strongly deactivating amino groups fail in Friedel-Crafts reactions — The amino group coordinates with the Lewis acid catalyst (AlCl3), rendering the ring permanently deactivated.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Iodoform Methyl Rule
CH3-C=O + 3I2 + 4NaOH → CHI3 (↓ yellow)

Application: Quickly identify any compound containing a CH3-CO- group or a secondary alcohol that oxidizes to it.

Grignard Alcohol Product Predictor
HCHO → 1° | RCHO → 2° | RCOR' → 3°

Application: Instantly determine the degree of alcohol formed when reacting RMgX with carbonyl compounds.

Reduction Reagent Mapping
Zn(Hg)/HCl (Acidic) vs NH2NH2/KOH (Basic)

Application: Choose Clemmensen for acid-stable substrates and Wolff-Kishner for base-stable, acid-sensitive substrates.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Ketones have a sp2 hybridized carbonyl carbon with a bond angle of approximately 120°.
  • Acetone (Propanone) is industrially prepared by the catalytic dehydrogenation of isopropyl alcohol.
  • Cyclopropanone exists almost entirely in the hydrate form in aqueous solution due to high ring strain.
  • Clemmensen reduction uses amalgamated zinc and hydrochloric acid.
  • Wolff-Kishner reduction utilizes hydrazine followed by potassium hydroxide in ethylene glycol.
  • Methyl ketones react with sodium bisulfite to form crystalline addition compounds.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming ketones reduce Fehling's solution; standard acyclic ketones do not react.
  • Forgetting that acidic conditions in Clemmensen reduction can hydrolyze acid-sensitive functional groups.
  • Confusing the role of hydrazine in Wolff-Kishner reduction with standard hydrazone formation without base.
  • Misidentifying non-methyl ketones as positive for the iodoform test.

⚡ Quick Revision: Ketones

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

The carbonyl carbon is sp2 hybridized, creating a strong dipole moment due to electronegativity difference between carbon and oxygen.

Nucleophilic Addition Mechanism

Nucleophiles attack the electrophilic carbonyl carbon from a trajectory perpendicular to the plane of the carbonyl group, forming a tetrahedral intermediate.

Reduction Pathways

Carbonyl groups can be reduced to secondary alcohols using NaBH4 or LiAlH4, or completely reduced to methylene groups via Clemmensen or Wolff-Kishner conditions.

Distinguishing Tests

2,4-DNP test confirms the presence of any carbonyl compound, while iodoform test specifically identifies methyl ketones.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Carbonyl Group (>C=O)
Functional group consisting of a carbon atom double-bonded to an oxygen atom.
Ketyl Radical (R2C•−O-)
Intermediate formed during bimolecular reduction of ketones.
Ketal (R2C(OR')2)
Geminal diether formed by reaction of ketone with two equivalents of alcohol.
Enolate ([RCH−C(O−)R'])
Anionic species formed by abstraction of an α-proton from a carbonyl compound.
Baeyer-Villiger Oxidation (RC(=O)R' → RCOOR')
Oxidation of a ketone to an ester using a peroxy acid.
Clemmensen Reduction (C=O → CH2)
Deoxygenation of carbonyl using zinc amalgam and concentrated HCl.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ketones are less reactive than aldehydes in nucleophilic addition.
  • Methyl ketones give a positive iodoform test with I2/NaOH.
  • Clemmensen uses acidic conditions; Wolff-Kishner uses basic conditions.
  • Grignard reagent + Ketone → Tertiary Alcohol.
  • 2,4-DNP derivative forms colored crystalline precipitates with ketones.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Ketones possess two carbon groups attached to the carbonyl carbon.
  • Synthesis involves secondary alcohol oxidation or Friedel-Crafts acylation.
  • Reactions are dominated by nucleophilic addition and α-substitution.
  • They resist mild oxidation unlike aldehydes.

⚖️ Key Differences

Clemmensen Reduction VS Wolff-Kishner Reduction

Clemmensen uses acidic Zn(Hg)/HCl, whereas Wolff-Kishner uses basic NH2NH2/KOH.

Question 1 of 18

Which of the following reagents is best used to convert a ketone into a methylene group?

Explanation: Zn(Hg) / HCl is used in the Clemmensen reduction to reduce the carbonyl group to a CH2 group.

Question 2 of 18

Acetone reacts with Iodine in the presence of sodium hydroxide to give:

Explanation: Acetone is a methyl ketone and undergoes the haloform reaction to produce iodoform (CHI3).

Question 3 of 18

What is the hybridization of the carbonyl carbon in ketones?

Explanation: The carbonyl carbon forms three sigma bonds and one pi bond, making it sp2 hybridized.

Question 4 of 18

Which compound will give a positive iodoform test?

Explanation: Propan-2-one is a methyl ketone (CH3-CO-CH3), which gives a positive iodoform test.

Question 5 of 18

Reaction of a ketone with a Grignard reagent followed by hydrolysis yields:

Explanation: Ketones react with Grignard reagents to form tertiary alcohol addition products after hydrolysis.

Question 6 of 18

Ketones are less reactive than aldehydes toward nucleophilic addition because:

Explanation: Two alkyl groups donate electrons (+I effect) and create steric hindrance, reducing electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon.

Question 7 of 18

Which test can distinguish between an aldehyde and a ketone?

Explanation: Tollen's reagent oxidizes aldehydes but not typical ketones, providing a clear distinction.

Question 8 of 18

The product of ozonolysis of 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene is:

Explanation: Ozonolysis of (CH3)2C=C(CH3)2 cleaves the double bond to yield two molecules of acetone.

Question 9 of 18

In the Wolff-Kishner reduction, the reagent used along with hydrazine is:

Explanation: Wolff-Kishner reduction requires hydrazine followed by strong base like KOH in a high-boiling solvent.

Question 10 of 18

Ketals are formed when ketones react with:

Explanation: Ketones react with two equivalents of monohydric alcohols in the presence of acid catalyst to form ketals.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following compounds does not react with NaHSO3?

Explanation: Steric hindrance in bulky ketones like acetophenone prevents the formation of the bisulfite addition compound.

Question 12 of 18

Baeyer-Villiger oxidation of a ketone yields:

Explanation: Baeyer-Villiger oxidation inserts an oxygen atom adjacent to the carbonyl carbon, converting a ketone into an ester.

Question 13 of 18

Which ketone is extremely reactive in nucleophilic addition due to ring strain?

Explanation: Cyclopropanone has severe ring strain which is relieved upon nucleophilic addition to the carbonyl carbon.

Question 14 of 18

Friedel-Crafts acylation of benzene with acetyl chloride in presence of AlCl3 gives:

Explanation: Acetyl chloride introduces an acyl group (CH3CO-) onto the benzene ring, forming acetophenone.

Question 15 of 18

What is formed when calcium acetate is dry distilled?

Explanation: Dry distillation of calcium acetate yields acetone along with calcium carbonate.

Question 16 of 18

The enol form of acetone has how many sigma and pi bonds?

Explanation: Enol of acetone (CH2=C(OH)CH3) contains 9 sigma bonds and 2 pi bonds.

Question 17 of 18

Which reagent converts secondary alcohols to ketones?

Explanation: Pyridinium chlorochromate (PCC) is a mild oxidizing agent that selectively converts secondary alcohols to ketones.

Question 18 of 18

The carbon-oxygen double bond of a ketone is:

Explanation: Oxygen is more electronegative than carbon, polarizing the carbonyl bond and placing a partial positive charge on carbon.

Question 1 of 10
What is the primary organic product when 2-methylpropan-2-ol is subjected to oxidation?

Solution: Tertiary alcohols like 2-methylpropan-2-ol do not have alpha hydrogens and resist oxidation under standard conditions.

Question 2 of 10
Which compound does not undergo aldol condensation?

Solution: Benzophenone lacks alpha hydrogens, making it incapable of undergoing aldol condensation.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following has the highest boiling point?

Solution: Propan-1-ol can form intermolecular hydrogen bonds, giving it a higher boiling point than ketones, aldehydes, and alkanes of comparable molecular weight.

Question 4 of 10
What is the product when acetone is treated with dilute NaOH?

Solution: Two molecules of acetone undergo base-catalyzed aldol addition to form diacetone alcohol.

Question 5 of 10
Which catalyst is used in the industrial preparation of acetone from isopropyl alcohol?

Solution: Dehydrogenation of isopropyl alcohol over heated copper catalyst at 573 K yields acetone.

Question 6 of 10
The keto and enol forms of acetone are examples of:

Solution: Keto and enol forms are constitutional isomers that exist in rapid dynamic equilibrium, known as tautomers.

Question 7 of 10
Which ketone gives a cyclic trimer known as trioxane upon treatment with acid?

Solution: Formaldehyde forms trioxane (though formaldehyde is an aldehyde, this tests carbonyl trimerization principles).

Question 8 of 10
When phenylmagnesium bromide reacts with carbon dioxide followed by acidification, the product is benzoic acid. What is formed if it reacts with acetone instead?

Solution: Reaction of phenylmagnesium bromide with acetone yields 2-phenylpropan-2-ol, a tertiary alcohol.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following reagents reduces ketones specifically to secondary alcohols without affecting double bonds?

Solution: Sodium borohydride (NaBH4) selectively reduces carbonyl groups to secondary alcohols while leaving isolated carbon-carbon double bonds intact.

Question 10 of 10
In the reaction of acetone with HCN, the attacking species is:

Solution: Cyanide ion (CN-) acts as the active nucleophile in the base-catalyzed nucleophilic addition of HCN to ketones.
