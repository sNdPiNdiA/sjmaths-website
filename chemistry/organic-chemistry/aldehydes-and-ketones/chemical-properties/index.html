Chemical Properties of Aldehydes and Ketones

Aldehydes and ketones undergo nucleophilic addition reactions at the electrophilic carbonyl carbon due to significant bond polarization. They also exhibit characteristic oxidation, reduction, and α-hydrogen acidity reactions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Nucleophilic Addition Reactions

  • The carbonyl carbon is sp2 hybridized with trigonal planar geometry, rendering it susceptible to nucleophilic attack from both faces.
  • Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones toward nucleophilic addition due to both electronic (smaller +I effect) and steric factors.
  • Derivatives like 2,4-DNP, oximes, and semicarbazones form via nucleophilic addition followed by dehydration (elimination).
  • Bisulfite addition compound formation is reversible and used for the purification and separation of aldehydes.

Oxidation and Reduction Pathways

  • Aldehydes are easily oxidized to carboxylic acids using mild oxidizing agents like Tollens' reagent [Ag(NH3)2]+ and Fehling's solution.
  • Ketones resist mild oxidation and require vigorous conditions (HNO3, KMnO4) with C-C bond cleavage yielding lower carboxylic acids.
  • Meerwein-Ponndorf-Verley (MPV) reduction converts carbonyls to alcohols using aluminum isopropoxide in isopropanol.
  • Oppenauer oxidation is the microscopic reverse of MPV reduction, converting secondary alcohols to ketones using aluminum t-butoxide.

Reactions Involving Alpha-Hydrogens

  • Carbonyl compounds possess acidic α-hydrogens due to the strong electron-withdrawing inductive and resonance effects of the carbonyl group.
  • Aldehydes and ketones with α-hydrogens undergo aldol condensation in the presence of dilute bases to form β-hydroxy carbonyl compounds.
  • Compounds lacking α-hydrogens undergo disproportionation via the Cannizzaro reaction when treated with concentrated alkali.
  • The haloform reaction occurs when methyl ketones are treated with halogen and base, yielding a carboxylate salt and a haloform (CHX3).

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Formaldehyde lacks alpha-hydrogens and undergoes Cannizzaro reaction.

Observation: Formaldehyde undergoes rapid nucleophilic addition with ammonia to form hexamethylenetetramine (urotropine). — Extreme lack of steric hindrance allows six molecules of formaldehyde and ammonia to condense symmetrically.

Exception Ketones do not reduce Tollens' reagent or Fehling's solution.

Observation: α-hydroxy ketones (e.g., fructose, benzoin) give positive Tollens' and Fehling's tests. — They undergo tautomerization to form enediols which readily isomerize to aldehydes in alkaline medium.

Exception Nucleophilic addition to carbonyl compounds is acid-catalyzed.

Observation: Strong nucleophiles like Grignard reagents and hydrides do not require acid catalysis. — They are powerful enough to attack the neutral, unprotonated carbonyl carbon directly.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Tollens' vs Fehling's Test Scope
Tollens = Aliphatic + Aromatic; Fehling = Aliphatic only

Application: Use Tollens' reagent to distinguish benzaldehyde from aliphatic aldehydes if needed, noting both react; use Fehling's specifically to distinguish aliphatic aldehydes from aromatic aldehydes (benzaldehyde fails Fehling's).

Clemmensen vs Wolff-Kishner Choice
Acid-sensitive = Wolff-Kishner (Base); Base-sensitive = Clemmensen (Acid)

Application: Select the reduction method based on the stability of other functional groups present in the organic molecule towards acid or base.

Haloform Test Identifier
CH3-CO- or CH3-CH(OH)- grouping

Application: Quickly identify any alcohol or carbonyl compound that will yield a yellow precipitate of iodoform with I2/NaOH.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Formaldehyde reacts with ammonia to produce urotropine (hexamethylenetetramine), used as a urinary antiseptic.
  • Benzaldehyde undergoes Cannizzaro reaction because it possesses no alpha-hydrogens.
  • Crossed aldol condensation gives a mixture of four products unless one reactant lacks alpha-hydrogens.
  • Clemmensen reduction employs Zinc-Amalgam and concentrated hydrochloric acid.
  • Wolff-Kishner reduction uses hydrazine followed by potassium hydroxide in ethylene glycol.
  • Bisulfite addition compound is crystalline and reverts to the original carbonyl compound upon treatment with dilute acid or alkali.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming benzaldehyde responds positively to Fehling's solution; aromatic aldehydes fail Fehling's test.
  • Confusing the role of dilute NaOH (Aldol) versus concentrated NaOH (Cannizzaro).
  • Forgetting that ketones with alpha-hydrogens can undergo self-condensation under vigorous basic conditions.
  • Overlooking that alpha-hydroxy ketones give positive tests with Tollens' and Fehling's reagents due to tautomerization.

⚡ Quick Revision: Chemical Properties of Aldehydes and Ketones

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Carbonyl Polarization

The carbon-oxygen double bond is polar with oxygen acquiring a partial negative charge and carbon a partial positive charge, driving nucleophilic addition.

Acidity of Alpha-Hydrogens

The electron-withdrawing nature of the carbonyl group activates alpha-hydrogens, enabling carbanion formation, enolization, and condensation reactions.

Oxidation Disparity

Aldehydes contain an easily oxidized formyl hydrogen, whereas ketones require C-C bond cleavage for oxidation, making them significantly more resistant.

Reduction Pathways

Carbonyl groups are reduced to alcohols by metal hydrides (NaBH4, LiAlH4) and completely to hydrocarbons via Clemmensen or Wolff-Kishner methods.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Tollens' Reagent ([Ag(NH3)2]+)
Ammoniacal silver nitrate solution used to oxidize aldehydes to carboxylate ions with silver mirror formation.
Fehling's Solution (Cu2+ + Rochelle salt + NaOH)
Alkaline copper(II) solution used to detect aliphatic aldehydes, forming a red precipitate of Cu2O.
Schiff's Reagent (Rosaniline hydrochloride decolorized with SO2)
Reagent restored to pink/magenta color by aldehydes.
Urotropine ((CH2)6N4)
Hexamethylenetetramine formed from formaldehyde and ammonia.
Hemiacetal (RCH(OH)(OR'))
Unstable addition product of aldehyde and one alcohol molecule.
Cyanohydrin (RCH(OH)CN)
Addition product of hydrogen cyanide across the carbonyl group.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Aldehydes > Ketones in nucleophilic addition reactivity.
  • Benzaldehyde undergoes Cannizzaro reaction due to lack of alpha-hydrogens.
  • Clemmensen reduction uses Zn-Hg / HCl; Wolff-Kishner uses NH2NH2 / KOH.
  • Iodoform test is positive for compounds containing CH3CO- or CH3CH(OH)- groups.
  • Formaldehyde with conc. NaOH undergoes disproportionation (Cannizzaro).

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carbonyl carbon undergoes nucleophilic addition readily.
  • Alpha-hydrogens confer acidic character leading to aldol reactions.
  • Mild oxidants oxidize aldehydes but not ketones.
  • Reduction yields either alcohols or hydrocarbons depending on reagents.

⚖️ Key Differences

Aldol Condensation VS Cannizzaro Reaction

Aldol requires alpha-hydrogens and dilute base; Cannizzaro requires absence of alpha-hydrogens and concentrated base.

Explanation: Clemmensen reduction utilizes zinc-amalgam and concentrated hydrochloric acid to reduce carbonyls to methylene groups.

Explanation: Self-aldol condensation of acetone yields diacetone alcohol as the primary addition product.

Explanation: Aldehydes reduce Tollens' reagent to elemental silver, forming a silver mirror on the test tube wall.
