Aldehydes and Ketones

Aldehydes and ketones are carbonyl compounds containing the functional group >C=O, characterized by sp2 hybridization and a planar geometry. They undergo rapid nucleophilic addition reactions due to the polarization of the carbon-oxygen double bond.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structure and Physical Properties

  • The carbonyl carbon is sp2 hybridized with a bond angle of approximately 120° and a strongly polar C=O bond.
  • Due to dipole-dipole interactions, aldehydes and ketones have higher boiling points than non-polar alkanes of comparable molecular mass but lower than alcohols.
  • Lower aliphatic aldehydes and ketones are miscible with water due to effective hydrogen bonding between the carbonyl oxygen and water molecules.
  • Solubility rapidly decreases as the hydrophobic alkyl chain length exceeds four carbon atoms.

Nucleophilic Addition and Acidity

  • Nucleophilic attack occurs perpendicular to the planar carbonyl carbon, proceeding through an sp3 hybridized tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate.
  • Aldehydes are generally more reactive than ketones toward nucleophilic addition due to lesser steric hindrance and stronger positive inductive effect (larger +I of two alkyl groups in ketones).
  • Alpha-hydrogens of aldehydes and ketones are weakly acidic due to the strong electron-withdrawing inductive and resonance effect of the carbonyl group.
  • Acidity permits facile keto-enol tautomerism, enabling characteristic substitution and condensation reactions.

Name Reactions and Oxidative Distinction

  • Aldehydes containing alpha-hydrogens undergo Aldol condensation in dilute alkali to yield beta-hydroxy carbonyl compounds.
  • Aldehydes lacking alpha-hydrogens undergo disproportionation via the Cannizzaro reaction when treated with concentrated strong base.
  • Tollens' reagent (ammoniacal silver nitrate) oxidizes aldehydes to carboxylate anions, producing a characteristic silver mirror.
  • Fehling's solution reacts with aliphatic aldehydes to form a red-brown precipitate of copper(I) oxide, while aromatic aldehydes give a negative test.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Formaldehyde is an aldehyde and should follow standard primary aldehyde reactivity trends.

Observation: Formaldehyde is exceptionally reactive and undergoes Cannizzaro reaction despite lacking alpha-hydrogens. — Lack of alkyl groups minimizes steric hindrance and maximizes electrophilicity at the carbonyl carbon.

Exception Aliphatic aldehydes reduce both Tollens' and Fehling's reagents.

Observation: Benzaldehyde reduces Tollens' reagent but fails to reduce Fehling's solution. — The aromatic ring stabilizes the benzaldehyde molecule, preventing facile oxidation by the milder copper(II) tartrate complex.

Exception Ketones are resistant to mild oxidizing agents.

Observation: Alpha-hydroxy ketones (ketols) readily reduce Tollens' and Fehling's reagents. — They undergo easy keto-enol tautomerism to form alpha-diketones or reducing enediol intermediates.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Alpha-Hydrogen Counting for Aldol
Count H atoms on C directly attached to >C=O

Application: If ≥ 1 alpha-H, compound undergoes Aldol condensation in dilute base; if 0 alpha-H, it undergoes Cannizzaro in conc. base.

Clemmensen vs Wolff-Kishner Reagent Mnemonic
Clemmensen = Acid (HCl), Wolff-Kishner = Base (NH2NH2 / KOH)

Application: Use Clemmensen for acid-sensitive substrates and Wolff-Kishner for base-sensitive substrates during carbonyl reduction to hydrocarbons.

Tollens Test Target
All RCHO + [Ag(NH3)2]+ → Ag(s) mirror

Application: Distinguishes any aldehyde (aliphatic or aromatic) from ketones instantly.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Formaldehyde is a gas at room temperature, whereas higher aldehydes and ketones are liquids or solids.
  • Grignard reagents react with formaldehyde to form primary alcohols, with other aldehydes to form secondary alcohols, and with ketones to form tertiary alcohols.
  • Addition of sodium hydrogensulfite (NaHSO3) is specific to aldehydes and methyl ketones, forming a crystalline bisulfite addition product.
  • Hydrazine derivatives (2,4-DNP) yield intensely colored crystalline orange or red precipitates used for identifying carbonyl compounds.
  • Rosenmund reduction converts acyl chlorides to aldehydes using catalytic palladium poisoned with barium sulfate.
  • Stephen reaction reduces alkyl cyanides to imines using stannous chloride and hydrochloric acid, which hydrolyze to aldehydes.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the role of conc. NaOH (causes Cannizzaro for aldehydes without alpha-H) vs dilute NaOH (causes Aldol condensation for aldehydes with alpha-H).
  • Assuming aromatic ketones reduce Fehling's solution or Tollens' reagent; only aromatic aldehydes reduce Tollens' reagent.
  • Forgetting that formaldehyde exists mainly as an aqueous polymer (formalin or paraformaldehyde) in solution.
  • Misidentifying the hybridization change during nucleophilic addition from sp2 to sp3.

⚡ Quick Revision: Aldehydes and Ketones

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Carbonyl Polarization and Reactivity

The carbon-oxygen double bond is strongly polarized with partial positive charge on carbon, dictating high susceptibility to nucleophilic attack.

Nucleophilic Addition Mechanisms

Additions of HCN, alcohols, and Grignard reagents proceed through nucleophilic attack on the sp2 carbon to generate tetrahedral intermediates.

Alpha-Carbon Acidity and Reactions

Presence of electron-withdrawing carbonyl groups activates alpha-hydrogens, driving Aldol condensations, Claisen condensations, and halogenations.

Redox Transformations

Aldehydes are selectively oxidized by Tollens' and Fehling's reagents and can be reduced to hydrocarbons via Clemmensen or Wolff-Kishner protocols.

Dynamic equilibrium between keto and enol forms dependent on alpha-hydrogens.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones towards nucleophilic addition.
  • Cannizzaro reaction requires aldehydes without alpha-hydrogens and concentrated alkali.
  • Aldol condensation requires aldehydes or ketones possessing at least one alpha-hydrogen.
  • Clemmensen reduction uses Zn-Hg/HCl while Wolff-Kishner uses NH2NH2/KOH.
  • Formaldehyde gives silver mirror test with Tollens' reagent.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Aldehydes and ketones feature an sp2 hybridized planar carbonyl carbon.
  • Nucleophilic addition is the primary reaction pathway.
  • Alpha-hydrogens confer acidic properties enabling carbon-carbon bond forming condensations.
  • Specific analytical tests separate aldehydes from ketones.

⚖️ Key Differences

Aldol Condensation VS Cannizzaro Reaction

Aldol requires alpha-hydrogens and dilute base; Cannizzaro requires absence of alpha-hydrogens and concentrated base.

What is the hybridization of the carbonyl carbon in ketones?

In Clemmensen reduction, carbonyl groups are converted to:

Explanation: Clemmensen reduction uses zinc-amalgam and concentrated hydrochloric acid to reduce aldehydes and ketones directly to methylene (-CH2-) groups.

Question 5 of 19

Which of the following has the highest reactivity towards nucleophilic addition?

Explanation: Formaldehyde has no alkyl groups to exert steric hindrance or electron-donating +I effect, maximizing electrophilicity.

Question 6 of 19

The intermediate formed during nucleophilic addition to carbonyl compounds is:

Explanation: Nucleophilic attack on the sp2 carbonyl carbon generates an sp3 hybridized tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate.

Question 7 of 19

Rosenmund reduction is used for the preparation of:

Explanation: Rosenmund reduction selectively hydrogenates acyl chlorides to aldehydes using Pd/BaSO4 poisoned with sulfur or quinoline.

Question 8 of 19

Which compound gives a positive iodoform test?

Explanation: Ethanol (and methyl ketones or secondary alcohols with a CH3-CH(OH)- group) undergoes oxidation and halogenation to yield yellow iodoform precipitate.

Question 9 of 19

Cyanohydrin formation from acetone and HCN is an example of:

Explanation: Cyanide ion acts as a nucleophile attacking the carbonyl carbon in nucleophilic addition.

Question 10 of 19

What is the end product of the reaction between a ketone and a Grignard reagent followed by hydrolysis?

Explanation: Ketones react with Grignard reagents to yield tertiary alcohols upon acid hydrolysis.

Question 11 of 19

Schiff's reagent gives a pink/magenta color with:

Explanation: Schiff's reagent is a specific test that restores magenta color in the presence of free aldehyde groups.

Question 12 of 19

Which reagent is used in Wolff-Kishner reduction?

Explanation: Wolff-Kishner reduction employs hydrazine (NH2NH2) followed by base (KOH/glycol) to reduce carbonyls to hydrocarbons.

Question 13 of 19

Alpha-hydrogens of aldehydes and ketones are acidic due to:

Explanation: The electron-withdrawing carbonyl group stabilizes the resulting carbanion or enolate ion through resonance.

Question 14 of 19

Addition of sodium hydrogensulfite to carbonyl compounds forms a:

Explanation: Bisulfite addition forms a crystalline solid, useful for separating and purifying aldehydes and methyl ketones.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following catalysts is used in Aldol condensation?

Explanation: Dilute aqueous alkali (NaOH or KOH) generates the enolate ion required to initiate Aldol condensation.

Question 16 of 19

Ketones are generally less reactive than aldehydes because:

Explanation: Two alkyl groups in ketones exert a stronger +I electron-donating effect and steric hindrance, reducing electrophilicity.

Question 17 of 19

Reduction of aldehydes with NaBH4 yields:

Explanation: Aldehydes are reduced by sodium borohydride to primary alcohols.

Question 18 of 19

The product of cross-aldol condensation between formaldehyde and acetaldehyde is:

Explanation: Formaldehyde lacks alpha-hydrogens, so acetaldehyde supplies the enolate, resulting in condensation products such as aldol intermediates leading to polyalcohols.

Question 19 of 19

Which functional isomerism is exhibited by aldehydes and ketones?

Explanation: Aldehydes and ketones with the same molecular formula belong to different homologous series, representing functional isomerism.
