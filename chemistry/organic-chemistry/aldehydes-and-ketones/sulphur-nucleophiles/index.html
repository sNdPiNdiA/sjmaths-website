Sulphur Nucleophiles in Carbonyl Addition

Sulphur nucleophiles such as thiols, bisulphites, and thioacetals undergo nucleophilic addition reactions with aldehydes and ketones. These reactions serve as crucial methods for carbonyl protection, synthesis of functionalized derivatives, and selective reduction pathways.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Bisulphite Addition Reactions

  • Sodium bisulphite forms crystalline bisulphite addition products with aldehydes and methyl ketones.
  • The reaction is reversible; treatment with dilute acid or base regenerates the free carbonyl compound.
  • Steric hindrance heavily disfavours bisulphite addition, meaning bulky ketones fail to react.

Thioacetals and Thioketals

  • Aldehydes and ketones react with thiols in the presence of Lewis acid catalysts to form thioacetals and thioketals.
  • Cyclic dithiols derived from ethane-1,2-dithiol yield stable five-membered cyclic thioketals.
  • Thioacetals serve as stable protecting groups for carbonyl functions as they resist basic, acidic, and oxidizing environments.

Synthetic Applications and Reductive Cleavage

  • Desulfurization of thioacetals using Raney Ni / H2 converts a carbonyl group directly into a methylene group (RCHO → RCH3).
  • Sulphur nucleophiles exhibit higher nucleophilicity toward soft electrophilic carbonyl carbon than corresponding oxygen nucleophiles.
  • Thioacetal anions can be alkylated at the carbon formerly belonging to the carbonyl group, enabling carbon chain extension.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Thioacetal Formation
RCHO + 2 R'SH → RCH(SR')2 + H2O
Conditions: Acid catalysis (e.g., BF3·OEt2 or dry HCl)
Units: Stoichiometric 1 aldehyde : 2 thiol
Thioketal Formation
R2CO + HSCH2CH2SH → R2C(SCH2CH2S) + H2O
Conditions: Dithiol with anhydrous acid catalyst in organic solvent
Units: Stoichiometric 1 ketone : 1 dithiol
Mozingo Reduction (Thioacetal Desulfurization)
RCH(SR')2 + 2 H2 → RCH3 + 2 R'SH
Conditions: Raney Nickel, ethanol, reflux
Units: Catalytic hydrogenation conditions
Bisulphite Addition Compound Formation
RCHO + NaHSO3 → RCH(OH)SO3Na
Conditions: Saturated aqueous sodium bisulphite solution, room temperature
Units: Stoichiometric 1:1 molar ratio
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Most aliphatic ketones react readily with sodium bisulphite to form solid addition complexes.

Observation: Di-isopropyl ketone, acetophenone, and bulkier aromatic ketones do not form bisulphite addition compounds. — Severe steric crowding around the carbonyl carbon prevents the approach and binding of the bulky bisulphite anion.

Exception Carbonyl protecting groups generally cleave easily under aqueous acidic hydrolysis.

Observation: Cyclic thioacetals and thioketals are completely stable toward standard aqueous acid and base hydrolysis. — The carbon-sulphur bond is less polarized and resistant to simple protonation-driven hydrolysis compared to oxygen acetals.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Raney Nickel Shortcut
C=O + HS-R-SH → Cyclic Thioketal → [Raney Ni / H2] → -CH2-

Application: Directly convert any aldehyde or ketone into an alkane without going through Clemmensen or Wolff-Kishner conditions.

Bisulphite Precipitation Rule
Aldehydes + Methyl Ketones + Cyclic Ketones (< 8 rings) = Positive Bisulphite Test

Application: Use sodium bisulphite precipitation to separate and purify low molecular weight aldehydes and methyl ketones from complex mixtures.

Soft-Soft Interaction Principle
Sulphur (Soft nucleophile) → Attacks C=O (Soft carbon end)

Application: Remember that sulphur reagents prefer soft electrophilic centers, making them selective over hard oxygen centers in complex multi-functional molecules.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Sodium bisulphite addition to carbonyl compounds is a reversible reaction used for separation and purification.
  • Ethan-1,2-dithiol reacts with ketones to form stable cyclic thioketals under acidic conditions.
  • Raney Nickel mediated hydrogenolysis of thioacetals provides an alternative to Clemmensen and Wolff-Kishner reductions.
  • Steric hindrance in bulky ketones prevents bisulphite crystal formation.
  • Thioacetals are resistant to aqueous mineral acids but cleaved by mercuric chloride (HgCl2) or silver nitrate.
  • Thioacetal carbon atoms can be deprotonated by strong bases (like alkyl lithium) to generate nucleophilic carbanions for C-C bond formation.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming di-tert-butyl ketone will form a bisulphite addition product; steric hindrance completely blocks the reaction.
  • Forgetting that oxygen acetals hydrolyze in aqueous acid whereas thioacetals require metal ion activation (Hg2+) or Raney Ni.
  • Confusing the role of Raney Nickel as a hydrogenation catalyst that specifically removes sulphur atoms as nickel sulphide.
  • Expecting cyclic dithioacetals to form without an acid catalyst; Lewis acids like BF3 etherate are mandatory.

⚡ Quick Revision: Sulphur Nucleophiles in Carbonyl Addition

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Bisulphite Addition Complex

Aldehydes and methyl ketones form solid, water-soluble bisulphite addition compounds via nucleophilic attack of SO3H-.

Thioacetalization Protocol

Reaction of carbonyl compounds with thiols or dithiols produces thioacetals and thioketals catalysed by anhydrous acids.

Carbonyl Protection Strategy

Cyclic thioacetals serve as robust protecting groups against aggressive bases, nucleophiles, and oxidising agents during multi-step organic synthesis.

Reductive Methylene Conversion

Treatment of thioacetals with Raney Nickel and hydrogen gas reduces the carbonyl carbon completely to a methylene (-CH2-) unit.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Thiol (R-SH)
Organosulphur compound acting as a soft sulphur nucleophile.
Thioacetal (RCH(SR')2)
Derivative formed from aldehyde and two thiol equivalents.
Thioketal (R2C(SR')2)
Derivative formed from ketone and thiol equivalents.
Bisulphite Compound (RCH(OH)SO3Na)
Crystalline addition product used for carbonyl isolation.
Raney Nickel (Ra-Ni)
Finely divided nickel-aluminium alloy catalyst used for desulfurization.
Carbonyl Protection (Masking Group)
Temporary transformation of carbonyl to prevent unwanted reactions.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Aldehydes and methyl ketones form solid bisulphite addition products.
  • Ethane-1,2-dithiol + Ketone → Cyclic thioketal + H2O.
  • Thioacetal + Raney Ni / H2 → Methylene group (-CH2-).
  • Thioacetals resist acidic hydrolysis but cleave with Hg2+ salts.
  • Sulphur nucleophiles are soft and react preferentially at soft carbon centres.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Sulphur nucleophiles attack carbonyl carbons via nucleophilic addition.
  • Bisulphite adducts provide a purification handle for volatile or liquid carbonyls.
  • Thioacetals are superior protecting groups compared to oxygen acetals due to acid stability.
  • Raney Ni desulfurization achieves complete reduction of carbonyl to alkane.

⚖️ Key Differences

Oxygen Acetal Hydrolysis VS Thioacetal Hydrolysis

Oxygen acetals hydrolyze easily in dilute aqueous acid, whereas thioacetals require thiophilic metal ions like Hg(II) or Ag(I).

Question 1 of 19

Which of the following compounds forms a bisulphite addition compound most readily?

Explanation: Acetone is a small methyl ketone with minimal steric hindrance, allowing rapid formation of the bisulphite addition complex.

Question 2 of 19

What is the primary product formed when benzaldehyde reacts with two equivalents of ethanethiol in the presence of dry HCl?

Explanation: An aldehyde reacting with two equivalents of a thiol forms a thioacetal under acid catalysis.

Question 3 of 19

Which reagent is used for the desulfurization of thioacetals to yield hydrocarbons?

Explanation: Raney Nickel in the presence of hydrogen gas cleanly reduces thioacetals to corresponding alkanes (Mozingo reduction).

Question 4 of 19

Why do bulky ketones fail to form sodium bisulphite addition complexes?

Explanation: Large alkyl groups create steric congestion around the carbonyl carbon, blocking the approach of the bulky bisulphite anion.

Question 5 of 19

Cyclic thioketals are typically prepared from ketones using which of the following reagents?

Explanation: Ethane-1,2-dithiol reacts with ketones to form stable five-membered cyclic thioketals.

Question 6 of 19

Which property makes thioacetals superior to oxygen acetals as protecting groups?

Explanation: Thioacetals are stable under both acidic and basic conditions where normal oxygen acetals would readily hydrolyze.

Question 7 of 19

What is the oxidation state change when a thioacetal is converted to an alkane via Raney Ni?

Explanation: Converting a carbonyl-derived carbon (oxidation state +2) into a methylene group (oxidation state -2) is a formal reduction.

Question 8 of 19

Which metal salt is commonly used to hydrolyze thioacetals back to carbonyl compounds?

Explanation: Mercuric chloride (HgCl2) acts as a thiophilic metal ion, precipitating mercury sulphide and facilitating thioacetal hydrolysis.

Question 9 of 19

In terms of Pearson's HSAB theory, sulphur in thiols is classified as:

Explanation: Sulphur atoms are large, polarizable, and possess lower charge density, classifying them as soft nucleophiles.

Question 10 of 19

Which carbonyl compound does NOT form a crystalline bisulphite addition compound?

Explanation: Acetophenone contains a bulky phenyl ring attached to the carbonyl carbon, causing steric hindrance that prevents bisulphite addition.

Question 11 of 19

The addition of sodium bisulphite to a carbonyl compound is an example of:

Explanation: The sulphonate nucleophile (SO3H-) attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon, making it a nucleophilic addition.

Question 12 of 19

What is the IUPAC class of the product obtained when a ketone reacts with a thiol?

Explanation: Ketones reacting with thiols yield thioketals (dithioacetals).

Question 13 of 19

Which reagent regenerates a free aldehyde from its bisulphite addition compound?

Explanation: Adding dilute acid or base shifts the reversible equilibrium back toward the free aldehyde and bisulphite ion.

Question 14 of 19

Which of the following describes the nature of the C-S bonds in thioacetals?

Explanation: C-S bonds have lower polarity compared to C-O bonds, rendering them stable toward acidic hydrolysis conditions.

Question 15 of 19

Why is Mozingo reduction advantageous over Clemmensen reduction?

Explanation: Mozingo reduction avoids harsh acidic (Clemmensen) or basic (Wolff-Kishner) conditions, making it safe for acid- or base-sensitive groups.

Question 16 of 19

When ethanal reacts with sodium bisulphite, what is the structure of the functional group formed in the adduct?

Explanation: The bisulphite adduct features a hydroxyl group and a sodium sulphonate group attached to the same aliphatic carbon.

Question 17 of 19

Which type of carbonyl compound is most reactive toward sulphur nucleophiles like bisulphite?

Explanation: Formaldehyde is the least sterically hindered and most electronically deficient aldehyde, showing highest reactivity.

Question 18 of 19

What role does boron trifluoride etherate (BF3·OEt2) play in thioacetalization?

Explanation: BF3·OEt2 coordinates to the carbonyl oxygen, increasing the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon for thiol attack.

Question 19 of 19

If an organic chemist wants to protect a ketone in a strongly basic medium, which derivative is best?

Explanation: Cyclic thioketals are exceptionally stable in both strong basic and acidic environments, serving as ideal protecting groups.
