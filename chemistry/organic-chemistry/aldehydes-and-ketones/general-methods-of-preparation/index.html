General Methods of Preparation of Aldehydes and Ketones

Aldehydes and ketones are synthesized via the oxidation of alcohols, dehydrogenation, or hydration of alkynes, alongside specialized reduction and acylation pathways. These carbonyl compounds serve as fundamental intermediates for organic synthesis in nucleophilic addition and condensation reactions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation from Hydrocarbons and Alkynes

  • Terminal alkynes other than acetylene undergo acid-catalyzed hydration in the presence of HgSO4 and H2SO4 to yield methyl ketones via keto-enol tautomerism.
  • Acetylene yields acetaldehyde upon hydration under identical acidic mercuric sulfate conditions.
  • Ozonolysis of symmetrical and unsymmetrical alkenes cleaves the carbon-carbon double bond to form aldehydes or ketones depending on the substitution pattern of the alkene carbons.
  • Etard reaction utilizes chromyl chloride (CrO2Cl2) to selectively oxidize toluene derivatives into chromium complexes that hydrolyze to benzaldehyde.

Oxidation and Dehydrogenation of Alcohols

  • Primary alcohols yield aldehydes when treated with mild oxidizing agents such as PCC or PDC, avoiding over-oxidation to carboxylic acids.
  • Secondary alcohols undergo oxidation using Jones reagent (CrO3/H2SO4) or copper catalysts to produce ketones exclusively.
  • Catalytic dehydrogenation of primary and secondary alcohol vapours over heated copper at 573 K yields aldehydes and ketones respectively along with hydrogen gas.

Acylation and Reduction of Acid Derivatives

  • Rosenmund reduction converts acyl chlorides into aldehydes using hydrogen gas over palladized barium sulfate poisoned with quinoline or sulfur.
  • Stephen reaction reduces alkyl or aryl cyanides using stannous chloride and hydrochloric acid to form imines, which on subsequent acid hydrolysis yield aldehydes.
  • Friedel-Crafts acylation of aromatic rings with acyl chlorides or acid anhydrides in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3 produces aromatic ketones.

Conditions: Anhydrous conditions, Pyridinium chlorochromate (PCC) or Collin's reagent
Units: Molar stoichiometry (1:1)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Primary alcohols oxidize to carboxylic acids with strong oxidizing agents.

Observation: PCC and PDC stop the oxidation of primary alcohols strictly at the aldehyde stage. — These reagents lack water and operate in non-aqueous organic solvents, preventing the formation of the aldehyde hydrate intermediate required for further oxidation.

Exception Reduction of acid chlorides via catalytic hydrogenation yields alkanes.

Observation: Rosenmund reduction stops precisely at the aldehyde stage. — The catalyst surface of palladium is intentionally poisoned by barium sulfate and sulfur/quinoline, rendering it inactive for the subsequent reduction of aldehydes to alcohols.

Exception Friedel-Crafts acylation can be performed on any substituted benzene ring.

Observation: Strongly deactivated aromatic rings such as nitrobenzene or sulfonic acids do not undergo Friedel-Crafts acylation. — The Lewis acid catalyst AlCl3 forms a stable coordinate bond with the strongly basic heteroatom substituents, deactivating the catalyst and rendering the ring inert.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • PCC stands for Pyridinium chlorochromate and is used for selective oxidation of primary alcohols to aldehydes.
  • Rosenmund reduction employs Pd/BaSO4 to prevent over-reduction of acyl chlorides to alcohols.
  • Stephen reaction reduces nitriles to aldimines using SnCl2 and HCl.
  • Gattermann-Koch reaction introduces a formyl group into benzene using CO, HCl, and anhydrous AlCl3/CuCl.
  • Hydration of acetylene yields acetaldehyde, while all higher terminal alkynes yield methyl ketones.
  • Dry distillation of calcium salts of carboxylic acids yields aldehydes when calcium formate is mixed with the calcium salt of another acid.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Using aqueous KMnO4 for oxidizing primary alcohols to aldehydes, which leads directly to carboxylic acids.
  • Forgetting that formaldehyde cannot be prepared via Friedel-Crafts acylation because formyl chloride is unstable at room temperature.
  • Confusing Stephen reaction (SnCl2/HCl) with Stephen-like reductions involving DIBAL-H.
  • Assuming hydration of terminal alkynes follows Markovnikov addition without considering the formation of unstable enols that tautomerize.

⚡ Quick Revision: General Methods of Preparation of Aldehydes and Ketones

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Oxidative Methods

Alcohols are converted to carbonyl compounds via catalytic dehydrogenation or chemical oxidation using chromium-based reagents like PCC, PDC, and Jones reagent.

Reduction of Acid Derivatives

Acyl chlorides yield aldehydes via Rosenmund reduction, and nitriles yield aldehydes via Stephen reduction, utilizing poisoned catalysts and stannous chloride respectively.

Hydrocarbon Cleavage

Alkynes undergo acid-catalyzed hydration to form carbonyl compounds, while alkenes undergo reductive ozonolysis to cleave double bonds into specific aldehyde or ketone fragments.

Aromatic Electrophilic Acylation

Arenes react with acyl halides and anhydrides in the presence of Lewis acids like AlCl3 to form aryl ketones via Friedel-Crafts acylation.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • PCC stops at aldehyde for primary alcohols.
  • Rosenmund catalyst uses BaSO4 as a poison.
  • Acetylene hydration gives acetaldehyde.
  • Friedel-Crafts acylation requires anhydrous AlCl3.
  • Etard reaction uses chromyl chloride.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carbonyl synthesis spans oxidation, reduction, and hydrocarbon functionalization.
  • Milder oxidizing agents prevent over-oxidation to acids.
  • Poisoned catalysts are essential for selective reductions.
  • Hydration and ozonolysis offer routes from unsaturated systems.

⚖️ Key Differences

Rosenmund Reduction VS Clemmensen Reduction

Rosenmund converts acid chlorides to aldehydes; Clemmensen converts aldehydes/ketones to alkanes using Zn-Hg/HCl.

Question 1 of 18

Which reagent is best suited for converting a primary alcohol directly to an aldehyde without forming a carboxylic acid?

Explanation: PCC is an anhydrous mild oxidizing agent that selectively oxidizes primary alcohols to aldehydes and stops there.

Question 2 of 18

In the Rosenmund reduction, what is the role of barium sulfate (BaSO4)?

Explanation: Barium sulfate acts as a poison for the palladium catalyst, reducing its activity so that the aldehyde formed is not further reduced to an alcohol.

Question 3 of 18

The Stephen reaction involves the reduction of which functional group?

Explanation: Stephen reaction reduces nitriles using stannous chloride and hydrochloric acid to form aldimines.

Question 4 of 18

Which hydrocarbon on acidic hydration in the presence of HgSO4 gives acetaldehyde?

Explanation: Acetylene (ethyne) is the only alkyne that yields an aldehyde (acetaldehyde) upon hydration; all other alkynes yield ketones.

Question 5 of 18

The Etard reaction is used to convert toluene to:

Explanation: Etard reaction uses chromyl chloride to oxidize the methyl group of toluene to a chromium complex, which hydrolyzes to benzaldehyde.

Question 6 of 18

Which compound cannot be prepared via Friedel-Crafts acylation?

Explanation: Benzaldehyde cannot be prepared by Friedel-Crafts acylation because formyl chloride (HCOCl) is unstable and decomposes.

Question 7 of 18

Ozonolysis of 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene followed by zinc dust and water yields:

Explanation: Cleavage of 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene yields two molecules of acetone (propan-2-one).

Question 8 of 18

Catalytic dehydrogenation of a secondary alcohol over copper at 573 K gives:

Explanation: Secondary alcohols undergo dehydrogenation to yield ketones, whereas primary alcohols yield aldehydes.

Question 9 of 18

Which reagent is employed in the Gattermann-Koch reaction to synthesize benzaldehyde from benzene?

Explanation: Gattermann-Koch reaction treats benzene with carbon monoxide and hydrogen chloride in the presence of anhydrous aluminium chloride and cuprous chloride.

Question 10 of 18

What is the intermediate formed during the Stephen reaction before hydrolysis?

Explanation: Nitriles are reduced by SnCl2/HCl to aldimine hydrochlorides, which subsequently undergo hydrolysis to form aldehydes.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following compounds yields propan-2-one upon acidic hydration?

Explanation: Propyne undergoes hydration following Markovnikov's rule to form an unstable enol that tautomerizes to propan-2-one (acetone).

Question 12 of 18

Dry distillation of a mixture of calcium formate and calcium acetate yields:

Explanation: Dry distillation of calcium formate gives formaldehyde, calcium acetate gives acetone, and a mixture of calcium formate and calcium acetate yields acetaldehyde.

Question 13 of 18

Which metal catalyst is used in Rosenmund reduction?

Explanation: Palladium supported on barium sulfate is the standard catalyst for the Rosenmund reduction.

Question 14 of 18

In ozonolysis, the function of zinc dust in the reductive workup is to:

Explanation: Zinc dust destroys the hydrogen peroxide formed during ozonolysis, preventing unwanted further oxidation of aldehydes to carboxylic acids.

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following represents the general formula for aliphatic ketones?

Explanation: Aldehydes and ketones are functional isomers sharing the general molecular formula CnH2nO.

Question 16 of 18

Oxidation of toluene with acidic KMnO4 leads to:

Explanation: Strong oxidizing agents like acidic KMnO4 oxidize toluene completely to benzoic acid, whereas milder reagents like CrO2Cl2 stop at benzaldehyde.

Question 17 of 18

Which reaction produces benzaldehyde from benzene using HCN and HCl in the presence of AlCl3?

Explanation: The Gattermann reaction (formylation) uses hydrogen cyanide and hydrogen chloride with Lewis acid catalysts to produce benzaldehyde.

Question 18 of 18

Which functional group is reduced to an aldehyde in the Rosenmund reduction?

Explanation: Rosenmund reduction specifically converts acyl chlorides (acid chlorides) into aldehydes.
